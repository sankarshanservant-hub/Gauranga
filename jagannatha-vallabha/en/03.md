# Act Three. Bhava-prakasha (The Revealing of Feeling)

*Radha despairs at Krishna's feigned refusal; her friends console her; Madhavi brings from Krishna a picture with Radha's portrait and a verse, a sign of His love; Radha sends Madanika to Krishna.*

*(Enter Ashoka-manjari.)*

**Ashoka-manjari** *(Prakrit)*. Ah, I have heard that my dear friend, with Madanika, the Forest-goddess and Shashimukhi, is sitting near the bower of madhavi creepers, plotting some secret. I'll take a look and go. *(Looking ahead and approaching.)* Ah, they are whispering something very softly. It would not be right for me to go in there. *(Exit.)*

*(Enter Radha, being admonished by Shashimukhi and Madanika.)*

**Radha** *(sighing long and hot, Prakrit)*. So Madhava has really rejected me!

*Song 9 — in raga Sama-gurjari.*

> **3.** The conduct that women of good family keep, I, losing my reason, counted as a straw.
> O Shiva, Shiva, what an unworthy thing I have done! Say, Fate: who now will win Him over? *(Refrain.)*
> I am still a girl, yet I am full of feelings like a grown woman; who else, like me, has thrown off shame?
> For the joy of Gajapati Rudra this song of the poet Ramananda Raya has been sung.[^1]

**Shashimukhi** *(Prakrit)*. I have told you everything as it happened; so judge for yourself.

**Radha** *(using Sanskrit)*.

> **5.** Hearing again and again the sound of His flute, the Parabrahman in harmony with the fair chants of the Sama Veda; seeing again and again the essence of the beauty of the finest youth of the three worlds, skilled in play; meditating again and again on Him, radiant as the rising sun and the moon — the beloved of Shri — I feel my mind burning me like a smouldering fire of chaff.[^2]

**Shashimukhi** *(using Sanskrit)*. Friend, give up this misplaced insistence.

> **7.** Whatever I told Krishna for your sake, He warded it all off by making a show of being a mere child. Enough! Let that poison be — the thought of Krishna, steeped in the honey dripping from the flowers of your longing; turn your desire elsewhere, fair-faced one.[^3]

*Song 10 — in raga Suhayi.*

> **8.** A woman serves her husband even if he is unworthy; does a doe choose a lion?
> Radhika, give up your love for Madhava! *(Refrain.)*
> And when the moon wanes, does the forest of night-lilies cease to love it?[^4]
> May Ramananda Raya's song ever delight King Gajapati Rudra.

**Radha** *(in tears)*. Lady Madanika, what is this?

> **9.** Hari does not know the pain of love broken off; love does not know what is its place and what is not; Madana does not understand that we are weak; no one knows another's pain in full; our life is not in our power; and this youth lasts but two or three days. Alas, alas, what a course Fate takes![^5]

**Madanika.** Why do you torment yourself so? For:

> **10.** If a bee, drawn from afar by the rich fragrance of a ketaki flower, has come to it — then even if, circling and circling, she finds little nectar in its pollen, once she has reached it she does not leave it.[^6]

**Radha** *(taking courage)*. "He has rejected me already" — having said this only half-way, in fear and trembling: Lady, this is not my fault. For

> **11.** when by the will of fate the Enemy of Madhu came into the path of my eyes, then that wretch Madana stole my heart.
> *(Pausing a moment and sighing long and hot.)*
> If He comes again even for a moment into the path of my eyes, I shall set every moment of that time with jewels.[^7]

**Madanika** *(aside)*. Her love has passed all bounds. Let me divert her mind with the most welcome news. *(Aloud.)* Child, look, look:

> **13.** The young mango tree that you, lovely one, raised by watering its roots from the lotus of your hand — its crown is now bristling with buds; I think that is why the bees are speaking sweet words to it.[^8]

**Radha** *(in fear and trembling)*. Friend Shashimukhi, remember me![^9]

**Madanika** *(aside)*. Ah, what a train of misfortunes has come upon us! *(Aloud.)* Child, do not lose heart so: I have noticed that His heart is full of love.

*Song 11 — in raga Deshaga.*

> **15.** Look: how is it that His lotus face constantly bristles with gooseflesh at sweet tales? How does He, leaving thousands of games, smile so charmingly with a fresh smile?
> Innocent one, give up your excessive fears! *(Refrain.)*
> How He speaks again and again, with respect and tenderness, to your friend about the heart of the matter, fair-faced one! Can your mind not reason about this?[^10]
> May these words of the poet Ramananda Raya long dwell in the heart of Gajapati Rudra, lord of men, which is the very essence of rasa and versed in the knowledge of play.

**Radha.** Lady,

> **16.** water is only guessed at in the cloud, while the flames of the forest fire have already seized the body. The body is so tender and she is still a child — O Shiva, Shiva, how will the doe survive?[^11]

**Madanika.** Child, to find this out I sent Madhavi with a picture bearing your likeness.

*(Enter Madhavi with a picture in her hand.)*

**Madhavi.** Lady, I bow to you.

**Madanika.** Welcome, child. Have you too learnt the secret?

**Madhavi.** Of course.

**Madanika.** Then tell it.

**Madhavi** *(shows the picture)*.

**Radha** *(bashfully asks for the picture)*.

**Madhavi.** First give me a reward for good news!

**Madanika** *(aside)*. Surely Mukunda, clearly understanding her heart, has fallen in love too.

> *(Unnumbered.)* "For if the sprout of love should ever break through carelessness, it cannot be joined again."

*(Aloud.)* Child, hand over the picture.

**Madhavi** *(showing it a little, covers it with the edge of her garment)*.

**Shashimukhi** *(seizing it by force, looks at it. Prakrit)*. Ah, what are these letters? *(Reads.)*

> **27.** "Do not fear, fair-faced one, that he has turned away from you: whom would a lotus opening for the first time not delight? And though the youth loses his composure at the very first breath of its fragrance — how could he remain indifferent even for a moment?"[^12]

**Madhavi** *(Prakrit)*. Friend, may you flourish in your beloved's love!

**Radha** *(sighing long and hot, Prakrit)*. Ah, friend, where would we find such good fortune? *(To Madanika.)* What does this mean?

**Madanika.** This: clearly understanding your heart, Mukunda too has fallen in love. "For if the sprout of love should ever break through carelessness, it cannot be joined again." So, child, do not be distressed: the tree of our desires has borne fruit.

**Radha** *(Prakrit)*. Even now I do not believe it. You alone are my refuge here.

**Madanika.** Here I go then; give me leave.

**Radha** *(bowing, using Sanskrit)*. Revered lady,

> **33.** this grove is thick with swarms of humming bees; the jewel of the day has probably gone behind the peak of the western mountain; the breeze gently, gently stirs the bees on the jasmine; what more is there to say — the moon too is about to rise.[^13]

*Song 12 — in raga Karnata.*

> **34.** The grove where the bees hum so sweetly is now so terrible; the gentle fragrant breeze does me harm.
> All this I have told you. And more: because of the heavy five arrows my life is wavering. *(Refrain.)*
> The forest, where maddened cuckoos give pain, is the highest torment; even the body, made for the joy of union, has become a vessel of great fear.
> May this radiant song, made by the poet Raya whose name is "Rama", soon fill King Rudra with happiness.[^14]

**Madanika.** Child, look for me at the foot of the vakula tree. *(Exit.)*

*(Exeunt the others too.)*

*End of the Third Act of the drama "Sri Jagannatha-vallabha", called "Bhava-prakasha".*

[^1]: Radha believes herself rejected and reproaches herself for having disregarded convention, not knowing that Krishna's refusal was only a test (Act 2). For Gaudiya Vaishnavas her readiness to sacrifice everything, even her good name, is a sign of the highest selfless love (CC Adi 4.167–169: for Krishna's sake the gopis gave up everything — worldly custom and Vedic duty alike).

[^2]: *Sragdharā* metre. The sound of Krishna's flute is called the Parabrahman in harmony with the chants of the Sama Veda: the Absolute itself is in the sound of His flute. "Beloved of Shri" (*Śrīkānta*) is Krishna. The end of the fourth line is corrupt in the edition and is rendered by the sense and the edition's Hindi translation. A fire of chaff (*kukūla*) smoulders slowly and long — an image of the unquenchable pain of separation.

[^3]: Shashimukhi, like Madanika, tests the firmness of Radha's love. The beginning of the verse is unclear in the edition and is rendered by the sense.

[^4]: Shashimukhi says that a wife stays faithful to her husband (the doe does not choose the lion) — but at once refutes herself: the lilies love the moon even when it wanes, that is, true love does not depend on the beloved's merits. A proverb spoken in consolation may mean the very opposite.

[^5]: *Śārdūla-vikrīḍita* metre. The last words of the third line are unclear in the edition and are rendered by the sense and the edition's Hindi translation.

[^6]: The first line is corrupt in the edition; the sense is restored from the Hindi translation: a bee once drawn to a flower does not leave it. Madanika consoles Radha: Krishna, having once seen her, will not abandon her.

[^7]: "Set with jewels" — that is, count every moment a treasure. The fourth line is one syllable short in the print and has been corrected by the metre.

[^8]: An allegory: the young tree Radha has raised is Krishna's love, nurtured by her; the bees that cling to its buds are a sign that love is about to blossom. Madanika hints at good news.

[^9]: Radha fears she will die of separation and asks her friend to remember her.

[^10]: Madanika recounts how Krishna listened to her stories about Radha: His feelings betray His love. A line of the song is partly unclear in the edition.

[^11]: *Āryā* metre. The cloud is Krishna, the water the hope of meeting, the fire the heat of separation; the doe is Radha herself. The last word is corrupt in the print and is read as "भविता".

[^12]: *Mandākrāntā* metre. This is Krishna's verse on the picture with Radha's portrait: the lotus opening for the first time is Radha, the youth who breathes its fragrance is Krishna Himself. His earlier refusal (Act 2) was only feigned. The beginning of the fourth line is unclear in the edition.

[^13]: *Śikhariṇī* metre. Nightfall and moonrise intensify the pain of separation; Radha asks Madanika to hurry.

[^14]: "The poet Raya whose name is 'Rama'" is a play on the name of Ramananda Raya.
