# Act One. Purva-raga (First Attraction)

*Benediction and prologue: at the command of Gajapati Prataparudra the stage-director produces the drama of Ramananda Raya; Krishna enters Vrindavana with His jester-friend; hearing the flute, Radha comes to the grove with her friends; the first meeting of glances.*

> **1.** May the dance of Murari ever bestow happiness on the three worlds — accompanied by the music of the vina adorned with notes, the muraja drum and the flute; abounding in charming laughter as the creeper of His body bends in three places; resplendent with the clapping of His friends and the jingling of ankle-bells.[^1]

*Moreover:*

> **2.** "Is it a smile? No — it is the white-rayed moon. A tremulous eye? No — a lotus. An ear? No — the bowstring with which the Mind-born conquers the world." May this illusion, born in the corners of the eyes of the cowherd maidens bewildered with eager joy at the orb of Mukunda's face, serve you for a hundred blessings.[^2]

*Moreover:*

> **3.** Raising to the full the ocean of desire of the doe-eyed, making the night-lily forests that are the hearts of the self-controlled blossom utterly, and rendering the flocks of cakravaka birds that are the demons entirely stricken with grief — may the moon that appears in the guise of the face of Madhu's Enemy spread your bliss.[^3]

*Song 1 — in raga Natana.*

> **4.** Worship the Enemy of Madhu, whose locks are stirred by the gentle Malaya breeze, encircled by the peacock crest; in whom eagerness has been awakened by the beauties with sidelong tremulous glances, their hearts filled with the darts of Manmatha;
> whose mind delights in love-play — worship the Enemy of Madhu, who longs for the moon-lovely faces of the cowherd girls. *(Refrain.)*
> Him, with neck lightly swaying, lovely with a fresh smile, who day by day dwells in the heart of Gajapati Prataparudra —
> thus charmingly, full of rasa, does Ramananda Raya sing.[^4]

*(At the end of the benediction.)*

**Stage-director.** Enough of prolixity! My dear, this way, this way!

*(Enter the actress.)*

**Actress** *(Prakrit)*. Here I am, sir. To gladden with a gracious glance the heart of the maidservant fallen at your feet — that rests with my husband alone.[^5]

**Stage-director** *(joyfully)*. May you long enjoy, lady, the pleasures of youth, dressed as befits a connoisseur.

**Actress** *(Prakrit)*. For what reason has my lord called me?

**Stage-director.** My dear, do you not know this news of favour?

**Actress** *(Prakrit)*. Now my heart is bursting with curiosity to hear it.

**Stage-director.** Listen, dear. Today, at the close of a spring day — when the breeze that caressed the serpents, the braids lying on the Malaya mountain, the breast of the lady of the southern quarter forsaken by the young sun, had made the separated women faint, and the consoling words of their friends, the very elixir of life for the separated, were flowing; when in the clear forest of the sky the flower of cool rays, the moon, had blossomed amid the star-buds that open ever anew like undrilled pearls, poised at the tips of the creepers that are the corners of the restless eyes of separated women gazing at it in deep envy — [I was commanded] by him whose mind, like the Milk Ocean, is fit to be the abode of Lakshmi's Beloved, who covets its matchless lustre of beauty;[^6] a male cuckoo skilled in tasting the juice of the mango-buds of rasa ripened from vibhava and the rest;[^7] whose heart is adorned with the pearls of virtues that accompany the necklace on the neck of Shri. What more need be said?

> **10.** Hearing even his name, Sekandhara hides in a cave; the ornament of the land of Kalavarga looks about with tears; the king of Gurjara has come to regard his own capital as an old forest; the lord of Gauda knows himself to be as one aboard a ship on a storm-tossed ocean.[^8]

> **11.** Is it the play of self-multiplication of the Lord's mountain, a double of the nectar-rayed moon, the extract of the Snow Mountain, a twin of the Milk Ocean, the essence of an autumn cloud? — The mass of his fame, sharing sovereignty with the waters of the heavenly river, ceaselessly purifies the world.

> **12.** Rejoicing at the embrace of the rivers formed by the streams of water poured with his gifts,[^9] the ocean praises him under the pretext of the roar of its rolling, towering waves; and by the sacrifices he constantly performs the minds of the gods are so stitched to him that, in the form of sacred images (pratimā), they never leave his courtyard.

By him — the Rudra of the fire of world's end to the hosts of hostile kings — by the illustrious Prataparudra I have been commanded to stage some composition concerning the feet of Sri Hari. As it was said:

> **14.** "O best of actors, stage some composition resplendent with the play of the feet of Madhu's Enemy, rich in those very qualities of His, a source of complete delight to the hearts of the sensitive — a new work, not composed in another's shadow."

**Actress.** Then tell me.

**Stage-director.** How is that treasure-house of learning to be honoured? For even the lord of speech, wishing to speak of him, would be at a loss. *(Reflecting a moment.)* Ah, I remember!

**Actress** *(Prakrit)*. What is it, then?

**Stage-director.** The poet Sri Ramananda Raya — son of Sri Bhavananda Raya, an ocean of jewel-virtues such as serenity, whose counsels, steeped in the many maxims of policy laid down by the preceptor of the gods, made the king upright and virtuous;[^10] a poet whose mind is adorned by the feet of Sri Hari — has composed and offered the musical drama of Ramananda called *Sri Jagannatha-vallabha*, dear to Gajapati Prataparudra and adorned with all these qualities. That is what I shall stage. And the poet humbly said this:

> **18.** "Let there be not even a whiff of merit in this so-called composition — still, it is our glorification of the lotus feet of Madhu's Enemy. That will surely be the cause of a flood of bliss for the connoisseur's heart; therefore this effort is not fruitless."

So let the musicians be told to take up their vinas.

**Actress** *(using Sanskrit)*. As my lord commands. *(Looking ahead.)* Look, look!

> **19.** On the lake whose ripples the gentle Malaya breeze has smoothed, this bee, yellowed with fresh pollen and drunk with the honey of every lotus, sleeps at evening from fatigue in a lotus cup, its limbs motionless.[^11]

**Stage-director** *(joyfully)*. Excellent, excellent, my dear! You have cast my mind into the whirlpool of an ocean of curiosity — for you have reminded me of Narayana in yellow garments, languid with the fatigue of play full of drinking the honey of the lips of hundreds of cowherd maidens, sleeping somewhere on the couch of a heart adorned with the pillow of a mature young wife's breast.[^12]

*Behind the scenes:*

> **21.** Hari, Lord of the God of gods, endowed with the thirty-two marks, has gone with the cowherd boys to the forest by the Yamuna.

*Song 2 — in raga Kedara.*

> **22.** Behold Him — His peacock crest wreathed by a creeper whose shoots the softest breeze sets swaying; His tilaka mocking the crescent moon reflected on an emerald surface;
> in a dress that captivates young women — behold Him, as if the moon had come down to earth in a special form. *(Refrain.)*
> His face shines lovely with the lustre of jewelled earrings swinging in play; His sweet eyes, playfully darting, awaken longing in the young cowherd wives.
> May this noble form of Madhu's Enemy, sung by the poet Ramananda Raya, again and again give birth to joy in the heart of Gajapati Rudra, lord of men.

**Stage-director** *(with a start)*. My dear, my younger brother announces Sri Krishna's going to Vrindavana.[^13] Let us also go and prepare our costumes.

*(Exeunt both.)*

*End of the prologue (prastavana).*

*(Enter Krishna as described.)*

**Krishna.** Friend Ratikandala, look, look how lovely Vrindavana is! For:

> **26.** These creepers and trees touch one another with moving hands, rows of shoots of boundless lustre; their flower-eyes are lined with collyrium, the quivering bees that embrace them; their buds are swelling as if with longing, and the beauty of their crowns sways; and as if intoxicated with the honey of spring, on every side they hold with one another a conversation full of rasa — in the calls of the maddened cuckoos.[^14]

**Jester** *(Prakrit)*. Hey friend, to you this Vrindavana is charming; to me it is just a dining hall — here some sweet curd, there some spiced drink, there fragrant ghee, there rice pudding.[^15]

**Krishna.** Friend —

*Song 3 — in raga Vasanta.*

> **28.** Ah, look: at this unheard-of form of yours, with its antics fit for the heavens, Vrindavana has long been laughing beyond all bounds — as it were with lovely clusters of opening flowers.[^16]
> Behold, friend, the very essence of the earth — this Vrindavana of Mine, as if filled with rasa again and again at your coming. *(Refrain.)*
> As if with hosts of trembling leaf-hands struck by the gentle breeze, this delightful place seems ever to be teaching you to dance to your heart's content.
> May this utterance, charming to the heart of Gajapati Rudra, well-arranged, composed by the poet Ramananda Raya, delight the connoisseurs day by day.

Friend, how very sweet is the cooing of the cuckoos!

**Jester** *(Prakrit)*. Hey friend, the sound of your flute is sweeter than that, and our voice is sweeter still. So you play the flute, and I'll sing.

**Krishna.** As my friend pleases. *(Plays the flute.)*

**Jester** *(Prakrit)*. Hey, your flute has been heard; now let my voice be heard too! *(Pulling a face, he bellows harshly. Looking at the treetops.)* Hey, we've won! At your flute these sons of a slave-girl, the cuckoos, only fell silent, but at my singing they've flown off who knows where. So, friend, don't be proud!

**Krishna.** Friend, look, look: some pitiless person has broken the fresh ashoka shoots — it grieves the heart.

**Jester** *(Prakrit)*. Hey friend, I've heard that those slave-girl's daughters, the cowherd girls, come here to gather flowers. *(Mockingly.)* That's exactly why you won't leave this Vrindavana.

*Behind the scenes:*

> **36.** Having drunk to the full with the cups of her ears the sound of the flute of Madhusudana sporting in Vrindavana, Radha — her deep shyness loosened by awakening love — eagerly entered the circle of her friends.[^17]

*Song 4 — in raga Gaundakiri.*

> **37.** She turns her glance in every direction, like a lotus stirred by a gentle breeze —
> Radha enters the grove of play, love's pain rising in her at every step. *(Refrain.)*
> Placing her soft, slow feet, she seems to imitate an elephant's gait.
> May these words of the poet Ramananda Raya bring joy to Rudra, lord of elephants.

**Jester** *(listening, Prakrit)*. Hey, I've understood, I've understood!

**Krishna.** What?

**Jester** *(Prakrit)*. You're asking *me*?[^18]

*(Enter Radhika, followed by her friends, Madanika and the Forest-goddess.)*[^19]

**Jester** *(looking ahead, Prakrit)*. Hey friend, look, look: some conjurer has set a troop of golden dolls in motion — they're coming this way! I'll grab one and run off; one is enough to set a poor little brahmin like me up for life. *(Creeps forward slowly to seize one.)*

**Krishna.** Fie, fool! That is not a troop of golden dolls but a band of cowherd girls.

**Jester** *(looking closely, laughing, Prakrit)*. Well guessed! So your coming to Vrindavana has borne fruit.

**Krishna.** Fie, fool! What fruit has My coming to Vrindavana?

**Jester** *(Prakrit)*. I mean: protecting the fresh shoots of Vrindavana from these slave-girl's daughters.

**Radha** *(looking ahead, Prakrit)*. Noble Madanika, who is this — soft in lustre as a blue lotus petal, in garments like a heap of gold — who, His neck slightly bent, plays the flute so sweetly, so sweetly?

**Madanika.** Friend, don't you know? He of whom I told you:

> **44.** This is that youth — the tree on whose branches the birds that are young women's hearts alight — Mukunda, shining as if the Five-arrowed himself had appeared: no sooner does He come into the path of beautiful women's eyes than the knot of their garment loosens of itself.[^20]

**Krishna** *(glancing briefly, aside)*. Ah, at what auspicious moment was this being born! For:

> **46.** Though neither the lotus nor the moon is ever a likeness of this doe-eyed one's face, yet comparison never sets foot anywhere else but on them.[^21]

**Jester** *(Prakrit)*. I see: your heart is longing for those slave-girl's daughters, the cowherd girls. So come, let's get out of their sight and comfort ourselves with sweet curd and spiced drink. Look — it's midday.

**Krishna.** Friend, you have observed rightly. For:

> **49.** "How is it, horses, that you are so tired from travelling merely the sky that here your speed has failed?" — as if to reproach his horses thus, the sun, having reached the middle of the sky, stretches out his ray-hands, as though measuring the heavens.[^22]

**Jester** *(peering a long while with screwed-up eyes, Prakrit)*. Friend, I too must describe the disc of the sun. Vishvakarma once mounted the sun on a lathe and spun it; I reckon the sun's disc is still spinning today from that momentum.[^23]

**Madanika.** Friend, you are tired from your long walk; come, let us go.

*(Exeunt omnes.)*

*End of the First Act, called "Purva-raga".*

[^1]: Verses 1–3 and Song 4 form the *nandi*, the benediction opening a drama. *Tribhaṅga*: Krishna stands with His body bent in three places (neck, waist and knee). Above the Hindi translation of this page the 1901 editor printed "Śrī Gaurāṅga-candro vijayate" ("May Sri Gauranga-chandra be victorious"); these words are not part of the Sanskrit text of the drama.

[^2]: The figure *apahnuti* ("denial"): the thing named is denied and replaced by the object of comparison (smile → moon, eye → lotus, ear → Kama's bowstring). In their joyous bewilderment the gopis can hardly believe they are seeing Krishna's face and not the moon and lotuses.

[^3]: Here and throughout, the "desire" (*kāma*) of the gopis is their pure love (prema), merely called *kāma*. Krishnadasa Kaviraja explains in CC Adi 4.163–171 and Madhya 8.215 that the gopis' love is called "lust" although it aims only at Krishna's pleasure, and differs from worldly lust as gold from iron. The moon of Krishna's face gladdens the gopis and the ascetics (night-lilies open at moonrise) but grieves the demons, as moonrise grieves the cakravaka birds, which by tradition are parted at night.

[^4]: The closing line of the song (*bhaṇitā*) names the author and dedicates the song to the king. Krishna "dwelling in the heart" of Prataparudra points to the king's devotion. The phrase about "sidelong tremulous glances" is referred by the edition's footnote gloss to the beauties, not to Krishna, and is so translated.

[^5]: Prakrit: by the convention of Sanskrit drama, women, servants and the jester speak Prakrit. These speeches are translated from the Prakrit with the help of the Sanskrit *chāyā* (rendering) printed in the 1901 edition; they are marked *(Prakrit)*.

[^6]: The king's mind is compared to the Milk Ocean, the abode of Vishnu, Lakshmi's Beloved. This long clause has no main verb of its own; the verb "I was commanded" comes in the speech after verse 12. The right edge of page 6 of the edition is torn; three or four letters have been restored from the sense.

[^7]: Vibhava, anubhava, etc. — the components of rasa in poetics; the king is called a connoisseur of rasa.

[^8]: A eulogy of the king (*praśasti*). Sekandhara is probably Sultan Sikandar (Lodi) of Delhi, with a pun on *kandara*, "cave". Kalavarga is Gulbarga (the Bahmani sultanate), Gurjara is Gujarat, Gauda is Bengal. The beginning of the second line is corrupt in the edition (one syllable too many for the metre) and is rendered by the sense.

[^9]: "Waters of his gifts": by rite a gift is accompanied by a libation of water.

[^10]: Bhavananda Raya was Ramananda's father; in CC Madhya 10 Mahaprabhu receives him and his sons in Puri. The Sanskrit is partly corrupt here (printed "…*mantrā ṛvīkṛta*…") and is rendered by the sense. The compound also admits another reading, in which Bhavananda himself is called "lord of the earth" (*pṛthvīśvara*), that is, a ruler.

[^11]: The verse is printed without a number and belongs to passage 19. The bee wearied with the honey of all the lotuses is a veiled image of Krishna wearied by His play with the gopis; so the stage-director understands it in his next speech.

[^12]: Narayana is here a name of Krishna. The images of Krishna's love-play with the gopis in this drama describe the transcendental (*aprākṛta*) rasa, the eternal play of the Lord with His own pleasure-potency, which cannot be compared to worldly lust (cf. CC Madhya 8.137: in Vrindavana Krishna is the "transcendental ever-fresh Cupid").

[^13]: By stage convention the stage-director calls the actor playing Krishna his "younger brother".

[^14]: The creepers and trees are pictured as pairs of lovers: shoots are hands, flowers are eyes, bees are collyrium. *Utkalikā* means both "bud" and "longing".

[^15]: The jester (*vidūṣaka*, a brahmin buffoon, the hero's friend) is a glutton and a simpleton, as the convention of Sanskrit drama requires. His name here is Ratikandala. *Śikhariṇī* is spiced sweet curd, *rasālā* a spiced drink.

[^16]: The sense of *divocita-khelam* is uncertain ("with antics fit for heaven" or "fit for the day"); Krishna teases His jester friend, at whose appearance the flowers seem to laugh.

[^17]: "Love" is *manoja* (literally "mind-born", a name of Kama). For Radha it is prema, the highest love for Krishna, not worldly passion (CC Adi 4.163–171).

[^18]: That is, "you know it yourself".

[^19]: Madanika is a friend of Radha. The Forest-goddess (*Vana-devatā*) is, in the Gaudiya Vaishnava tradition, a name for Vrindadevi.

[^20]: The Five-arrowed is Kama (Cupid). In the Gaudiya Vaishnava understanding Krishna is the "transcendental Cupid" (CC Madhya 8.137), and what is described are the symptoms of loving agitation (*sāttvika-bhāva*) in His eternal beloveds. This is not worldly passion.

[^21]: Radha's face is incomparable: the lotus and the moon are not its equals, and yet poets have nothing else to compare it with.

[^22]: The sun, halted at the zenith, seems to scold the horses of its chariot. *Kara* means both "ray" and "hand". At midday the cowherd girls go home.

[^23]: The jester's joke draws on the Puranic story that Vishvakarma trimmed the sun on a lathe to lessen its heat.
