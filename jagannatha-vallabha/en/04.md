# Act Four. Radha-abhisara (Radha's Tryst)

*Krishna, suffering in separation, repents of His pretence; Madanika tells Him of Radha's sufferings and at His request goes to bring her; Radha comes to the appointed grove; Krishna waits for her anxiously as the moon rises; the meeting.*

*(Enter Madanika.)*

**Madanika.** Ah, I heard from Madana-manjari that Mukunda is sitting with the little brahmin at the foot of the vakula tree; I'll go there. *(Looking ahead.)* Ah, here is Mukunda, consulting dejectedly about something with the brahmin. Surely the Flower-arrowed One has been at play here. Let me hide behind the madhavi bush and listen. *(She hides.)*

*(Enter Krishna, acting the state of one in love, talking with the jester.)*

**Madanika** *(aside)*.

*Song 13 — in raga Malava.*

> **2.** This face, sweet as the moon's orb — alas, it has long been faded, like a lotus struck by the moon's ray, wounded day and night by the arrows of the Bodiless One.
> The great pain of Madhava's body splits my heart in two, into a hundred pieces. *(Refrain.)*
> His broad chest, having cast off its necklace, has turned grey with separation — ah, is it an emerald rock struck by a moonbeam?
> May the lovely song spoken by the poet Ramananda Raya delight Gajapati Rudra, ocean of good deeds — cooler than moonlight.[^1]

**Krishna.**

> **3.** If that lotus-eyed one revealed to Me her boundless love through her friend's lips, and I contrived this mockery — alas, alas, I have thrown away a great jewel, thinking it a shell! If only by fate she might come again within sight of My eyes, through My countless merits![^2]

**Jester** *(Prakrit)*. Hey friend, didn't I tell you not to reject this loving girl? Why do you pine now? When the appetite is gone, what's the use of laddus and sweets? So here I'm the only remedy.

**Krishna.** How so?

**Jester** *(Prakrit)*. I'm a brahmin: I'll keep repeating a mantra over and over and draw her here![^3]

**Krishna.** I know your brahminhood. Look rather — here is Madanika.

**Madanika** *(coming forward)*. Blessings on you, child.

**Krishna** *(looking ahead)*. What, Madanika? *(Respectfully.)* Lady, welcome.

**Madanika.** Noble one, to see the moon of your face is all the welfare I need.

**Jester** *(Prakrit)*. Our dear friend is tormented by the Flower-arrowed One; so bring that very cowherd girl!

**Krishna** *(embarrassed)*. Fie, fool, don't talk like that!

**Jester** *(Prakrit)*. We brahmins are simple folk — we speak plainly.

**Madanika** *(smiling)*. Child, you are truthful indeed.

**Jester** *(Prakrit)*. Of course! Look, look — here are lotus leaves! *(He shows dry, rustling leaves. Then, using Sanskrit:)*[^4]

*Song 14 — in raga Duhkhi-varadi.*

> **15.** In the lotus grove not a flower, not a petal is left, for the sake of Vanamali; and in Vrindavana you see only lovely opening shoots.
> Simple girl, don't you see Krishna? He has placed all His hope in you, forgotten His play — like a chataka bird thirsting for the cloud. *(Refrain.)*
> Looking at the moon, He keeps saying: "Bring Rahu quickly!" — tell me, how could He speak so if Madana were not dwelling in His heart, staging his games?[^5]
> May this fine song of Ramananda Raya, full of separation from Hari, who is secretly vanquished by the arrows of the Mind-born, increase the joy of Gajapati Rudra.

**Madanika.** Is that all?

**Jester** *(Prakrit)*. You've become our dear friend's friend, yet you don't understand even what's been said. Very well, I'll go myself and bring her — I too am an envoy with full powers! *(He starts to go.)*

**Krishna** *(catches him by his upper cloth)*.

**Madanika.** Child Krishna, why hide anything from me?

**Krishna.** Lady, I want to ask you something.

**Madanika.** Speak freely.

**Krishna.**

> **22.** Ever since you told Me that she scorns the lustre of the moon's face — let the moon torment Me! But the Malaya breeze, which I prized so much because it touches her body — how can it too toy with My life?[^6]

**Madanika** *(aside)*. Radhika's wish has been fulfilled together with ours. Let me tell Him of her pain of separation too. *(Aloud.)* Child, of that lovely one too only her beauty remains. For:

> **24.** This slender one, grinding sandal and cool moon on a golden stone slab, seeks something to anoint her body. Pausing a moment — alas, alas! — she rose from her bed of juicy lotus leaves before they could catch fire and start to rustle.[^7]

*Song 15 — in raga Sama-todi.*

> **25.** Emaciated, she falls in the mire endlessly made by the tears of her eyes, and her feet slip.
> Madhava, the pain of love is heavy! Hari, Hari, Radha is barely alive. *(Refrain.)*
> How do You dwell in her heart — and yet torment it? O Shiva, Shiva, and still You do not quench her longing![^8]
> May Ramananda's flawless song delight King Gajapati Rudra.

**Jester** *(Prakrit)*. Lady, I reckon cowherd girls are reckless: they go looking for ointment of moon and sandal. But our dear friend, seeing the moon, hides his body somewhere and sits with his eyes shut, like an owl before the sun; and if he catches even a breath of sandal-wind, he darts this way and that like a snake at a potent spell.[^9]

**Krishna** *(aside)*. Well said. *(Aloud.)* Fie, fool, don't chatter so!

**Madanika.** How many rules of duty were not preached to her to test her heart!

**Krishna** *(aside, anxiously)*. Can she have turned away from her longing for Me?

**Madanika.** So then:

> **30.** when she disregards the rebukes of her elders, when she takes no pleasure in her friends' jests full of rasa, when she sees sandal as poison and the moon as fire — then I came here to tell You of her state.[^10]

**Krishna** *(with a sigh of relief)*. Since you, a stranger to deceit, in causeless affection would draw Me out even from the ocean of Smara — then win her over and bring her to the bower-house by the keshara tree. Show your skill![^11]

**Madanika.** Child, it shall be so.

**Jester** *(Prakrit)*. Simple lady, this is true indeed — I, a brahmin, stand surety for it.

**Krishna.** Enough of doubts — accomplish My cause.

**Madanika.** Here I go. Blessings on you, child. *(Exit.)*

*(Enter Radhika, dressed for the tryst.)*

**Radha** *(Prakrit)*. Friend Madhavi, you have deceived me!

*Song 16 — in raga Ramakeli.*

> **37.** The path is hidden in darkness; mountains and caves — all have become level ground.
> Why, friend, does the lady delay? Does even Fate not wish me well? *(Refrain.)*
> Have I crossed this fearful, boundless, dense forest in vain?
> May this, made by Ramananda Raya, ever delight Rudra, lord of elephants.[^12]

**Madhavi.** Friend, enough of doubts: take it that the lady has already come.

*(Enter Madanika.)*

**Madanika.** Child, fortune smiles on you!

**Radha** *(with a joyful sigh, Prakrit)*. Lady, what news from there?

**Madanika.** Such as there is in a violent fever of love.

**Radha** *(Prakrit)*. How so?

**Madanika.**

> **43.** He reviles the moon, scatters the sandal, throws off His long necklace, shrinks from the dew, and now does not even speak to His dear companions. What does Govinda, afflicted by separation from you, not do? So, Radha, gladden Him — He thinks only of preparing a bed deep in the bower.[^13]

*(Then, in the bower, Krishna.)*

**Krishna.** Friend, why does Madanika delay so? *(Anxiously.)*

> **44.** This slender one walks slowly, weighed down by her full breasts and hips; and this bower I have appointed for our tryst is far away. The girl is timid by nature, and the forest lies in dreadful blinding darkness; how is she to come to Me? Here love alone is the refuge.[^14]

*(Acting a moment's reflection, sighing long and hot.)*

> **45.** Has she taken Me for a stranger to her feeling and turned away? Did she not trust her friend's words? Or has she lost her way in this forest so full of darkness — or, struck by Smara's arrows, is the tender one unable to walk?

*(Looking ahead.)* Ah, it seems the moon is about to rise! For:

> **46.** As the plaintive cries of the cakravakas spread, as the night-lilies all around open wider and wider, as at every step the lotus beds close — so, I fear, the moon is already strolling on the paths of the eastern mountain.[^15]

*(Sadly.)*

> **47.** Somehow trusting her friend's words, she may already, in a dress suited to the darkness, have come half-way along the path — and now this wretched moon has defiled the eastern quarter. Bewildered, she can neither come on nor go back today — what will the clever one do?[^16]

*(Folding His hands respectfully.)*

> **48.** O eastern mountain, my friend! Be kind and stretch your hundreds of peaks up high: if the moon comes into view, it will be an obstacle to the doe-eyed one's journey and to My very life.

**Jester** *(listening, Prakrit)*. Hey, listen — what's that going "runu-runu"?

**Krishna** *(listening towards the wings)*.

> **50.** "Is it the sound of her anklets? Or the humming of a swarm of bees? Or the tinkling of her girdle? Or the cry of sarasa cranes in love?" — while Govinda was thus wondering, Radhika, leaning on her friend's hand, soon became the ornament of His bower-house of play.[^17]

*Song 17 — in raga Malava-shri.*

> **51.** The flowers in her wave-like tresses are like foam; with a movement of her right eye she seems to bid the Bodiless One dance.
> For play with Madhava, Radha goes to Hari with slow steps, her necklace gently swaying. *(Refrain.)*
> With the corner of her sweet glance, trembling with apprehension, shyness and the fullness of rasa, she lovingly offers the Slayer of Madhu a garland of blue lilies.[^18]
> May this, spoken by the poet Ramananda Raya, sweetly delight with floods of rasa Gajapati Rudra, lord of men, the new Madana of our time.

**Jester** *(looking ahead, Prakrit)*. Hey friend, we've won! It looks as though the lady is coming over there.

*(Enter Madanika.)*

**Madanika.** Children, at long last the wish of your friends is fulfilled. So give me leave to go elsewhere.

**Jester** *(Prakrit)*. And me — to another bower!

*(Exeunt omnes.)*

*End of the Fourth Act, "Radha-abhisara".*

[^1]: Madanika sees Krishna suffering in separation. His face has faded like a lotus under the moon (the lotus closes at night), and His dark chest is compared to an emerald rock. The first word of the song is printed "madana" ("Kama"); by the sense and the Hindi translation we read "vadana" ("face"). The last line is unclear.

[^2]: *Śārdūla-vikrīḍita* metre. Krishna repents of His feigned refusal (Act 2). "Thrown away a jewel, thinking it a shell" is a proverbial image. There is no stage direction here in the Sanskrit text.

[^3]: The jester jokes that as a brahmin he will "draw" Radha by an attraction-spell (*ākarṣaṇa*).

[^4]: The jester shows Madanika dry leaves: everything fresh in the forest has already been gathered for cooling beds for the suffering Krishna. The direction "using Sanskrit" refers to the following song (sung by the jester or Madanika; the edition does not name the character).

[^5]: Rahu is the demon who swallows the moon at an eclipse. In separation Krishna cannot bear the moonlight and calls for Rahu to "swallow" the moon. The first line of the song is corrupt in the print and is rendered by the sense and the edition's Hindi translation.

[^6]: *Śikhariṇī* metre. The beginning of the verse is corrupt in the print. Krishna loved the Malaya breeze because it touches Radha, but now, in separation, the breeze torments Him too.

[^7]: Signs of the "fever of love" (*madana-jvara*): Radha seeks cooling with sandal, "cool moon" (camphor) and lotus leaves, but the heat of her body is such that the leaves wither and rustle.

[^8]: "Torment" — printed *vāmam*, "perversely, hostilely" (Hindi: "being crooked"). Krishna dwells in Radha's heart and yet does not quench her longing — a sign of the highest love in separation (*vipralambha*).

[^9]: The jester compares Radha's and Krishna's sufferings. The owl cannot bear the sun and the snake flees from the charmer's spell; to Krishna in separation the moon and sandal are unbearable.

[^10]: *Śikhariṇī* metre. Madanika relates that Radha was tested with intimidation and reminders of duty (cf. Act 3, Shashimukhi), but her love did not waver — a sign of *anurāga*, unwavering love that knows no obstacles.

[^11]: *Vasanta-tilakā* metre (printed as prose in the edition). "Causeless" (*akāraṇa-vatsalā*) — the friend's selfless love for both lovers, as Rupa Gosvami describes the sakhis, who are happy only in the union of Radha and Krishna.

[^12]: Radha has come to the place of the tryst (*abhisāra*) at night through the dark forest — in poetics an *abhisārikā*, a heroine going to her lover. In the Gaudiya Vaishnava tradition her journey through darkness and danger is an image of all-conquering prema, which takes no account of any obstacle.

[^13]: *Śārdūla-vikrīḍita* metre. The print has *prāleyāṁtra sati*, read as *prāleyāt trasati*, "shrinks from the dew".

[^14]: *Śikhariṇī* metre. "Here love alone is the refuge" (*kāmo 'tra śaraṇam*): *kāma* here is the same prema (CC Adi 4.163).

[^15]: At night the cakravakas are parted and cry, night-lilies open and day-lotuses close — all signs of moonrise. The eastern mountain (Udayagiri) is the mountain behind which, by tradition, the sun and moon rise.

[^16]: The moonlight will betray Radha, dressed in dark clothes for a night tryst (*tamisrābhisārikā*). "Eastern quarter" — literally "the quarter of Shakra", that is, of Indra.

[^17]: *Śārdūla-vikrīḍita* metre. A charming scene of uncertainty (*sandeha*): Krishna at first mistakes the sound of Radha's anklets for other sounds.

[^18]: Madhu-mathana — "Slayer of Madhu", Krishna. The garland is a sign of choosing the beloved. In the last line King Prataparudra is called "the new Madana of our time" — courtly praise of his beauty. The words "bid the Bodiless One dance" are unclear in the print (*nartitumatanumanāmaṁ*).
