# Act Five. Radha-sangama (Union with Radha)

*Dawn after the night in the grove; Madanika tells Shashimukhi of the meeting of Radha and Madhava; Radha, abashed, returns in the light of morning; an alarm behind the scenes — the bull-demon Arishtasura has burst into Vraja; Krishna slays him; Madanika again unites Radha with Krishna; the closing benediction.*

*(Enter Shashimukhi.)*

**Shashimukhi** *(Prakrit)*. Ah, I don't know what happened today in the grove with those two intent on bliss. I'll follow the lady and find out. *(Looking ahead.)* Ah, here she comes, slowly, slowly, her eyes closed with sleep. *(Using Sanskrit.)*

> **2.** Slowly, slowly, somehow lifting her eyes with their almost motionless pupils, her shoulders drooping and her arms hanging loose, stepping so languidly that her feet falter and the jingling of her anklets falters too — the lady, her body wholly overcome by sleep, shows her joy.[^1]

*Song 18 — in raga Sukha-sindhura.*

> **3.** Her eyes half-closed and reddish, her face here seeming to have lost its glow — like a red lotus at dawn reflected in the water beside the moon.
> What is this? As she enters, my mind sees in her a woman leaving after love-play. *(Refrain.)*
> Her arms are slack, and again and again her golden, jewelled bangles softly tinkle; setting her feet unevenly, she stills the jingling of her anklets, their lovely play.
> May these words of the poet Ramananda Raya bring joy to the heart of Gajapati Rudra, lord of men, and sport among the connoisseurs.[^2]

*(Enter Madanika, dressed as described. Rubbing her eyes, looking ahead.)*

**Madanika.** Ah, how lovely is the close of a spring night! For:

> **5.** Here gently, gently blows a breeze from the lotus beds; there is the chatter of cuckoos delighted by the taste of mango; somewhere, the humming of bees about a blossoming creeper; somewhere, the soft, sweet, joyful murmur of the cakravakas.[^3]

*(Walking a few steps, showing joy.)*

> **6.** The young couples who, through mutual love and acquaintance with the skill of ardent Smara, stayed awake all this night, are now asleep in their houses; and as if to vie with their breathing, the wind from the sandal paths plays over the rows of opening lotuses.

*(Looking ahead, in wonder.)*

> **7.** Here startled, startled, there unceasingly joyful, here lowering and there raising her eyes — so behaves this female cakravaka, that even clever young wives might become her pupils.[^4]

*(Going aside a moment, in amazement.)* Ah, here is something lovelier still! For:

> **8.** Seeing her own reflection in an opening lotus full of honey and taking it for her mate, the female bee eagerly darts to it; then, seeing her true companion arriving, abashed, the clever one can neither stay nor go, and long keeps circling.[^5]

**Shashimukhi.** She is so captivated by the loveliness of the morning that she does not see me. *(Approaching with the words "I bow to you.")* Lady, I bow to you.

**Madanika.** What, Shashimukhi, child! Lost in thought, I did not notice you for a long while.

**Shashimukhi.** Lady, why do you seem to me as if overcome by sleep?

**Madanika.** Child, why "as if"? I am.

**Shashimukhi.** But why?

**Madanika.**

> **14.** Today Radha and Madhava were in the grove; watching their wondrous play, I passed this night without sleep.

**Shashimukhi.** And what happened there?

**Madanika.** Listen. *(Wiping her eyes.)* Child, you know what happened until they entered the grove.

**Shashimukhi.** Of course.

**Madanika.** After that —

> **18.** the stupor that seized the Enemy of Mura made even her mind, full of love and shyness, fear that He was indifferent; and had not His trembling, stirred by the wind of the feathers of Kama's arrows, reassured her, the doe-eyed one would not have been comforted.[^6]

**Shashimukhi.** Joy, joy! I am fulfilled.

**Madanika.** And from here on the friends are fulfilled still more.

**Shashimukhi.** Did the lady see anything else?

**Madanika.** Everything.

**Shashimukhi.** And then?

**Madanika.** Child,

> **24.** with apprehension and with love, with laughter, with shyness, with pride, with jealous pique, with captivating words, with tenderness, with longing — such was then in the grove the love-play of Radha and Madhusudana that Madana himself was filled with wonder.[^7]

*Song 19 — in raga Ahira.*

> **25.** To the soft jingling of her anklets she came near the couch; and the Enemy of Madhu too took a few steps towards her, in keeping with her.
> Shashimukhi, what shall I tell you? I have seen a wonder — the fullness of the play of Radha and Madhava! *(Refrain.)*
> And so, it is said, their two bodies were united so that no difference remained; friend, as if pierced by the arrows of the Odd-arrowed One, they forgot their long sorrow.
> And though wounded by nails and teeth, they sighed deeply as if in fear. May this fine song of Ramananda Raya increase the joy of Rudra, lord of elephants.[^8]

**Shashimukhi.** Lady, this seems to me almost incongruous.

**Madanika.** How so?

**Shashimukhi.** Where did they get such skill in love?

**Madanika.** Ah, simple girl!

> **29.** A teacher instructs with great effort, and the teaching ripens in time — so it is with all the sciences; but the arts of love arise of themselves.[^9]

> **30.** Meanwhile the moon, having long, it seems, been schooled in those arts of love-play, now, as if to put its training into practice, devotedly clung to the western quarter.[^10]

**Shashimukhi.** And now my heart longs to see the most lovely bodies of those two blessed ones, adorned with the marks of nails and teeth, content with the long-awaited bliss of their desired meeting.

*(Enter Radhika in haste; Krishna at some distance.)*

**Radha** *(looking ahead, Prakrit)*. Ah, the quarters are growing light! How shall I go without covering my body? *(Hurrying two or three steps, she looks back, turning her neck.)*

**Krishna** *(gazing a moment)*. Ah, how fear and love are mingled in the doe-eyed one!

> **33.** Two or three steps she goes quickly, two or three again slowly; trembling with fear, she gazes meaningfully about the quarters; and my beloved, though I am near, at every step thinks me far away, as though I were not even within sight of her eyes.[^11]

**Radha** *(hurries on again)*.

**Madanika.** Child, look, look: ahead is Radhika, and a little way off, Madhava. For she

> **35.** fears a snake in front of her less than a stump far off; is less alarmed by an elephant's roar than by the cawing of a flock of crows; is less bewildered in darkness than in bright light; and so, I think, she is less distressed in separation than in union with her beloved.[^12]

*Song 20 — in raga Lalita.*

> **36.** In the special battle of the Lord of Rati, befitting the deep cherished desire, where the heart is stolen by knowing both victory and defeat and longing grows —
> she, with her graceful, captivating body, by the gentle movement of her lotus feet, most skilful, tells what has passed. *(Refrain.)*
> Where the enchanting sounds are like the twang of the many arrows of the flower-bow; where the marks of nails and teeth are devised as a sequence of merits —
> make your hearts free of fault as you hear this, spoken by the poet Ramananda Raya, known to Gajapati Rudra, lord of men, and giving joy to the connoisseurs.[^13]

**Madanika.** The girl is timid with great fear. Let us go and reassure her. *(Approaching.)* Child, welcome.

**Radha** *(looking in confusion, Prakrit)*. Ah, it is the lady! *(Bows bashfully.)*

*An uproar behind the scenes:* "Disaster! Disaster!" *(All listen.)*

*Again behind the scenes:*

> **40.** Gouging the earth with his horns and hooves, splitting the quarters with a roar like the thunder of clouds at the end of the world, rolling in fury his eyes that blaze like meteors, challenging to combat — by fate Arishta has appeared before us, plunging Vraja into calamity.

*(All hide in the depths of the grove and watch.)*

**Krishna** *(advancing boldly)*. Fear not, dwellers of the cowherd village! *(Raising His arm proudly.)*

> **42.** While this arm of Krishna is awake — the great prop of the earth with its ring of mountains crumbled by arrogant danavas, the towering sacrificial post at the rite of appeasement for Shakra harassed by enemies — fear will never touch even the cowherds whose sole refuge it is. Ah, this bull is toying with My life! *(He strides forth boldly.)*[^14]

*Behind the scenes:*

> **43.** Alas, alas! Those horns with which he split the mountains, unable to bear their pride in their peaks, he has now aimed at Mukunda, the boy whose body is tender as a blue lotus!

**Madanika** *(watching, in tears)*.

> **44.** Today, Earth, bear an immeasurable burden; gods, whence your hope of victory? Goddess Shri, take up the vow; people of Vraja, where now is even the news of joy for you? Mother Devaki, what will become of you? Nanda and the rest, you are lost; Radhika, the world has become empty for you today. Alas, alas, we are undone![^15]

**Radha** *(listening, in fear, Prakrit)*. Woe is me, unfortunate! So this is how evil fate has played!

**Shashimukhi.** Friend, take heart, take heart — this is Mukunda!

*Behind the scenes:*

> **47.** He at whose opening of the eyes the three worlds closed theirs, at whose rising they bowed down, before whose whirling in the sky even the winds hardly dared to whirl — that Arishta Sriman Mukunda, as if playing with a ball, has hurled far from Vrindavana and slain, and has made the world free of calamity.[^16]

*(Enter Krishna.)*

**All** *(gaze at Him eagerly)*.

**Madanika.** Ah, how lovely is the child adorned with victory! For:

> **49.** His creeper-like locks dishevelled and matted with drops of sweat, the sandal on His forehead washed away, the peacock feather of His crest gradually slipping, the lovely colour of His body set off by the dust raised by His steps — so appearing, Madhava, after long waiting, delights the eyes.

*(Approaching.)* What good fortune, child, that we see you chosen by Victory herself![^17]

**Krishna** *(seeing her, joyfully)*. Lady, welcome.

**Madanika.** Now that we see the child adorned with victory, all is truly well. So rest a while here, child, on the path by the vakula tree.

**Krishna.** As the lady wishes. *(Sits down.)*

**Madanika** *(touching Him affectionately)*. Child, you have done a hard deed — I wish to give you a reward.

**Krishna.** As the lady wishes.

**Madanika** *(goes out and returns with Radha)*. Child,

> **56.** she has not yet recovered from the new meeting; her eyes are wide with fear; honour her with Your sweet glance, as if after a long separation.

**Krishna** *(gazes eagerly)*.

**Madanika.** Daughter,

> **58.** fan your beloved, beaded with drops of sweat from the fierce fight, with the hem of your garment, and gladden him with a loving word.

**Radha** *(fans Him lovingly)*.

**Madanika.** What more can I do to please You?

**Krishna.** Lady, is there anything more pleasing than this?

> **61.** When the arrows of the Five-arrowed One were piercing Me on every side, the doe-eyed one, sole source of My bliss, was won for Me by your grace; and again I have gained a stream of nectar for My eyes. What, lady, can there be in the world more pleasing still for such as Me?

*Song 21 — in raga Mangala-gurjari.*

> **62.** The moon-faced one, like the full autumn moon, filled with great love, has joined Me, and her hand is in Mine.
> Lady, what else here could be desired by Me? The fruit of many good deeds has come of itself. *(Refrain.)*
> The cuckoos, the moon, spring and the swarms of bees — all now fill Me with happiness.
> May this, spoken by Ramananda — the play of Hari — bring King Rudra happiness and immortality.[^18]

And still, let this be so:

> **63.** Whoever, his mind bound by faith, eagerly serves day by day this incomparable secret, the nectar of the play of Me, Gopala — on him, whose mind is given to Me, may you always look with a gracious eye, so that he may attain the perfection he desires in the forests of Vraja.[^19]

**Madanika.** So be it.

*(Exeunt omnes.)*

*End of the Fifth Act, "Radha-sangama".*

*Thus ends the drama "Sri Jagannatha-vallabha", composed by Ramananda Raya.*

[^1]: *Mandākrāntā* metre. Shashimukhi sees Madanika (the Forest-goddess), who has not slept all night. "Her feet falter" — the print reads *maṁdayāsa*, corrected in the edition's errata to *mandanyāsa* ("slow step").

[^2]: Madanika, sleepless all night, resembles a woman after love-play. This is a hint, explained later: she spent the whole night watching the play of Radha and Krishna (verse 14). The first line of the song is unclear in places. For Gaudiya Vaishnavas the joy of the sakhis lies in arranging and beholding the meeting of Radha and Krishna, not in enjoying Krishna themselves (cf. CC Madhya 8.206–208).

[^3]: *Śikhariṇī* metre. A description of dawn (*prabhāta*), obligatory in Sanskrit drama. The end of the third line is unclear in the print.

[^4]: The last line is unclear. The edition's Hindi translation reads: "what clever woman does not imitate this cakravaka?" In the morning the female cakravaka is reunited with her mate after the night's separation.

[^5]: An allegory: the female bee who took a reflection for her beloved and then sees the real one is an image of the mingling of conflicting emotions (*bhāva-śābalya*).

[^6]: *Śārdūla-vikrīḍita* metre. Stupor (*stambha*) and trembling (*kampa*) are *sāttvika-bhāvas*, bodily signs of ecstatic love: Radha at first took Krishna's stupor for indifference and only by His trembling understood His love. The beginning of the third line is unclear in the print.

[^7]: Here and further on (verses 24–36) Ramananda describes the highest stage of madhura-rasa, *sambhoga*, the union of Radha and Krishna, in the language of classical poetics. Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati stresses that these pastimes (*aprākṛta-līlā*) have nothing in common with worldly relationships. Their participants are Bhagavan Himself and His own pleasure-potency (*hlādinī-śakti*). Such descriptions are meant only for devotees whose hearts are purified, and they must not be understood in a worldly way or imitated (cf. CC Adi 4.164–171: kama and prema differ as iron and gold). Ramananda himself calls them a "secret" in his closing verse 63, meant for those who serve Gopala's pastimes with faith.

[^8]: The print has *niśvasitāyatabhītaṁ*, whose sense is unclear. The edition's Hindi translation reads "began to breathe heavily in fear": the agitation came not from pain but from the fullness of love. Cf. note 7.

[^9]: *Puṣpitāgrā* metre. Shashimukhi is surprised, since Radha and Krishna are still young. Madanika replies that love needs no teaching. For Gaudiya Vaishnavas this reflects the eternal, self-manifest nature of their love: it is not acquired but abides eternally in Radha and Krishna.

[^10]: *Vasanta-tilakā* metre. The moon "clung to the western quarter", that is, it set and dawn came — a play on the image of the moon as a lover.

[^11]: *Śārdūla-vikrīḍita* metre. Returning in the morning, Radha fears being seen. Lines 3–4 are partly unclear in the print and are rendered by the sense and the edition's Hindi translation.

[^12]: *Śārdūla-vikrīḍita* metre. Radha fears the harmless (a stump, a crow, light) more than real danger; likewise, meeting her beloved, she is more anxious than in separation, from fear of being seen and from the fullness of feeling.

[^13]: The song continues the description of *sambhoga* (see note 7). The sense of "by the gentle movement of her lotus feet" (printed *mṛdupadakamalalavehā*) is unclear. The edition's Hindi translation reads: "it is at once understood from the gentle movement of her lotus feet". The closing line in the original is an appeal to the audience: "make your hearts free of fault". The connoisseurs are to hear this with a pure heart.

[^14]: The slaying of Arishtasura is told in *Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 10.36. "Prop of the earth" alludes to the lifting of Govardhana Hill. "Sacrificial post of the appeasement of Shakra" means Krishna's arm as the support that averts Indra's wrath. Ramananda joins this pastime to the dawn after the night in the grove. In the *Bhāgavata* Arishta comes in the evening, but as a dramatist Ramananda is free in ordering the events.

[^15]: *Śārdūla-vikrīḍita* metre. Madanika despairs, thinking Krishna in danger. "Mother Devaki": according to the edition's gloss, this is Yashoda's second name ("Nanda's wife has two names, Yashoda and Devaki"), as she is also called in some Puranas. "Shri, take up the vow" means "take the vow of widowhood".

[^16]: *Śārdūla-vikrīḍita* metre. Krishna hurled Arishta "like a ball": in the *Bhāgavata* (10.36) Krishna, seizing the bull by the horns, threw it down and killed it.

[^17]: "Chosen by Victory herself" — literally "embraced by the goddess of Victory at her *svayaṁvara*", the ceremony at which a bride chooses her husband.

[^18]: The last song of the drama, sung by Krishna. The twenty-one songs of the drama are passages 1.4, 1.22, 1.28, 1.37, 2.20, 2.24, 2.35, 2.46, 3.3, 3.8, 3.15, 3.34, 4.2, 4.15, 4.25, 4.37, 4.51, 5.3, 5.25, 5.36 and 5.62 of the edition. They are written in the style of Jayadeva's *Gīta-govinda*, each with a raga, a refrain (*dhruva*) and a closing line naming the author and dedicated to Prataparudra.

[^19]: *Śārdūla-vikrīḍita* metre. The closing benediction (*bharata-vākya*). Krishna asks the Forest-goddess (Madanika) to look graciously on those who with faith "serve" this "secret, the nectar of Gopala's play" (hearing, singing or staging it), so that they may attain perfection in Vraja. For Gaudiya Vaishnavas this points to the path of *rāgānugā-bhakti* under the guidance of Radha's companions. According to the CC, Mahaprabhu sang and heard the "songs of Raya's drama" day and night with Svarupa Damodara and Ramananda Raya (Madhya 2.77) and relished its verses in His ecstatic moods (Antya 20.67).
