## Translator's Preface


The *Śrī Jagannātha-vallabha-nāṭaka*, "The Drama of the Beloved of Jagannatha", is a Sanskrit musical drama (*saṅgīta-nāṭaka*) in five acts. Its author is Ramananda Raya, one of the closest associates of Sri Chaitanya Mahaprabhu. The drama tells of the love of Radha and Krishna in Vrindavana, from the first meeting of their glances to their union. It contains 21 songs (*gītas*) in the style of Jayadeva's *Gīta-govinda*. Each song has a named raga and a refrain (*dhruva*), and in its last line the poet names himself and dedicates the song to the king of Orissa, Gajapati Prataparudra.

### Ramananda Raya according to the *Chaitanya-charitamrita*

Ramananda Raya, son of Bhavananda Raya, governed a province on the Godavari (Vidyanagara) under Prataparudra. Krishnadasa Kaviraja tells in the *Chaitanya-charitamrita* (CC Madhya 8) how Mahaprabhu, setting out on His pilgrimage to the South, sought him out on the bank of the Godavari on the advice of Sarvabhauma Bhattacharya. Mahaprabhu questioned him about the goal and the path of spiritual life, and Ramananda unfolded, step by step, the stages of bhakti. He began with the performance of one's own duty (CC Madhya 8.57) and rose to the pure love of the gopis and the supreme love of Srimati Radharani. The chapter closes with Krishnadasa's words that through Ramananda's mouth the Lord revealed rasa (Madhya 8.310–311). Later Ramananda left government service and settled in Puri, remaining at Mahaprabhu's side to the end of His pastimes (on the meeting with Bhavananda Raya and his sons, see CC Madhya 10).

CC Antya 5 relates how Ramananda, in a secluded garden, taught two young temple dancers (*devadāsīs*) to dance and sing "the songs of his own drama" (*nija-nāṭaka-gīta*, Antya 5.13–14). He taught them to express all the feelings of divine love with their faces, glances and movements, and then to perform before Jagannatha (Antya 5.24). Krishnadasa stresses that touching the young dancers did not disturb Ramananda's mind or body in the least, because he felt himself a maidservant of the gopis of Vraja (Antya 5.19–20). Such is the power of his devotion. This is the *Jagannātha-vallabha*: the drama was written for the temple of Jagannatha and was performed before Him.

### Mahaprabhu heard these songs

During His last twelve years Mahaprabhu lived in Puri, in the Gambhira, absorbed in separation from Krishna (CC Madhya 2.3, 2.7). Krishnadasa testifies that in those days He "constantly recited the verses of Raya's drama" (*rāyera nāṭaka-śloka*, Madhya 2.17). Together with Svarupa Damodara and Ramananda Raya, He "sang and heard day and night", with the highest bliss, the songs of Chandidasa and Vidyapati, "the songs of Raya's drama", the *Karṇāmṛta* and the *Gīta-govinda* (Madhya 2.77). The last chapter of the CC says that Mahaprabhu recited the verses of Jayadeva, of the *Bhāgavata*, of "Raya's drama" and of the *Karṇāmṛta* according to His mood and relished them in ecstasy (Antya 20.67). Gaudiya Vaishnavas therefore honour this drama as one of the books Mahaprabhu Himself relished.

Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati, whom we follow in interpretation, stresses that the pastimes of Radha and Krishna described here in the language of classical poetics are transcendental (*aprākṛta*) līlā. Its participants are Bhagavan Himself and His own pleasure-potency. The love of the gopis is prema, not worldly passion, even though poets call it *kāma* (CC Adi 4.163–171). *Parakīyā*, love for Krishna as for "another's" beloved, exists only in Vraja (CC Adi 4.47) and is arranged by Yogamaya for the fullness of rasa. Such descriptions are meant for those whose hearts are purified, and they must not be understood in a worldly way or imitated. In his closing verse Ramananda himself calls the drama a "secret" for those who serve the pastimes of Gopala with faith (5.63). These passages are marked in the notes.

### The 1901 edition

The translation follows the edition *Śrī Jagannātha-vallabha-nāṭakam. Śrī Rādhā-Kṛṣṇa-rāsa-krīḍā-viṣayam. Śrīyuta kavivara Rāmānanda Rāya praṇītam*. It was edited by Nityasvarupa Brahmachari, a disciple of Svami Prakashananda Sarasvati and recipient of the grace of the siddha Sri Radharamana-charana Dasa. The text was corrected by Pandit Lakshmanacharya of Vrindavan, and Sri Balakrishna Gosvami rendered the Prakrit speeches into Sanskrit (*chāyā*). The book was printed at the Devakinandana Press, Vrindavan, in Samvat 1958 (1901). It has 108 printed pages and a list of errata. An IA scan (collection of Dr Urmila Sharma, Varanasi, CC0) is kept in the repository: `sources/south-india/ramananda-jagannatha-vallabha/`.

According to the editor's Sanskrit introduction (*bhūmikā*), the drama had earlier been printed in Bengali script and is here reissued in Devanagari. The edition contains:

- the text of the drama (Sanskrit and Prakrit);
- a Sanskrit *chāyā* of the Prakrit speeches;
- brief Sanskrit glosses by the editor on individual words and figures of speech (e.g. "here the figure is *apahnuti*");
- a Hindi translation by the editor.

There is no old Sanskrit commentary (*ṭīkā*) in the edition.

**What has been translated.** Only the text of Ramananda Raya's drama has been translated: verses, songs, prose speeches and stage directions. The editor's introduction, glosses and Hindi translation have not been translated or paraphrased. In difficult places they helped with the sense, and this is then stated in a note. The Prakrit speeches (spoken by the women, the jester and some attendants) are translated from the Prakrit with the help of the edition's Sanskrit *chāyā* and are marked *(Prakrit)*. Modern translations of the drama have not been used.

**Numbering.** Within each act the edition numbers all passages consecutively — verses, songs, prose speeches and stage directions. We keep the edition's numbers only for the verses and songs; prose speeches are given without numbers, with the speaker's name. The verse numbers therefore run with gaps. A few verses are printed without a number or within a prose speech (e.g. 1.19, 1.26, 3.25a); this is noted.

**The text.** The scan is damaged in places (torn page edges, worn print), and the edition has a fair number of misprints. Corrupt passages are restored from the sense, the metre and the editor's Hindi translation, and are noted. Obvious misprints are corrected, taking the edition's errata into account. A working transcription made from the page images is in `src/`; the corrected Sanskrit text of the verses and songs with a word-for-word translation is in `skt/`.

**Two versions.** The light version contains the translation only. The full version gives, for each verse and song, the Sanskrit (Prakrit) text in Devanagari, an IAST transliteration, a word-for-word translation and the translation; prose is given in translation only.

### Characters

- **Krishna** (Madhava, Mukunda, Hari, the Enemy of Madhu, etc.).
- **Radha** (Radhika).
- **Madanika** — Radha's elder friend and confidante. She is addressed as "lady" (*devī*), and Krishna treats her with respect. She is probably the same as the Forest-goddess (*Vana-devatā*) of Vrindavana (cf. Vrindadevi of the Gaudiya Vaishnava tradition).
- **Shashimukhi, Ashoka-manjari, Madhavi, Madana-manjari** — Radha's friends.
- **Ratikandala** — the jester (*vidūṣaka*), a brahmin buffoon, Krishna's friend.
- **The stage-director** (*sūtradhāra*) and **the actress** (*naṭī*, his wife) — in the prologue.

### The acts

1. **Purva-raga** — first attraction.
2. **Bhava-pariksha** — the testing of feeling.
3. **Bhava-prakasha** — the revealing of feeling.
4. **Radha-abhisara** — Radha's tryst.
5. **Radha-sangama** — union with Radha.

**Stage-director** *(joyfully)*. May you long enjoy, lady, the pleasures of youth, dressed as befits a connoisseur.

[^01-1]: Verses 1–3 and Song 4 form the *nandi*, the benediction opening a drama. *Tribhaṅga*: Krishna stands with His body bent in three places (neck, waist and knee). Above the Hindi translation of this page the 1901 editor printed "Śrī Gaurāṅga-candro vijayate" ("May Sri Gauranga-chandra be victorious"); these words are not part of the Sanskrit text of the drama.

[^01-2]: The figure *apahnuti* ("denial"): the thing named is denied and replaced by the object of comparison (smile → moon, eye → lotus, ear → Kama's bowstring). In their joyous bewilderment the gopis can hardly believe they are seeing Krishna's face and not the moon and lotuses.

[^01-3]: Here and throughout, the "desire" (*kāma*) of the gopis is their pure love (prema), merely called *kāma*. Krishnadasa Kaviraja explains in CC Adi 4.163–171 and Madhya 8.215 that the gopis' love is called "lust" although it aims only at Krishna's pleasure, and differs from worldly lust as gold from iron. The moon of Krishna's face gladdens the gopis and the ascetics (night-lilies open at moonrise) but grieves the demons, as moonrise grieves the cakravaka birds, which by tradition are parted at night.

[^01-4]: The closing line of the song (*bhaṇitā*) names the author and dedicates the song to the king. Krishna "dwelling in the heart" of Prataparudra points to the king's devotion. The phrase about "sidelong tremulous glances" is referred by the edition's footnote gloss to the beauties, not to Krishna, and is so translated.

[^01-5]: Prakrit: by the convention of Sanskrit drama, women, servants and the jester speak Prakrit. These speeches are translated from the Prakrit with the help of the Sanskrit *chāyā* (rendering) printed in the 1901 edition; they are marked *(Prakrit)*.

[^01-6]: The king's mind is compared to the Milk Ocean, the abode of Vishnu, Lakshmi's Beloved. This long clause has no main verb of its own; the verb "I was commanded" comes in the speech after verse 12. The right edge of page 6 of the edition is torn; three or four letters have been restored from the sense.

[^01-7]: Vibhava, anubhava, etc. — the components of rasa in poetics; the king is called a connoisseur of rasa.

[^01-8]: A eulogy of the king (*praśasti*). Sekandhara is probably Sultan Sikandar (Lodi) of Delhi, with a pun on *kandara*, "cave". Kalavarga is Gulbarga (the Bahmani sultanate), Gurjara is Gujarat, Gauda is Bengal. The beginning of the second line is corrupt in the edition (one syllable too many for the metre) and is rendered by the sense.

[^01-9]: "Waters of his gifts": by rite a gift is accompanied by a libation of water.

[^01-10]: Bhavananda Raya was Ramananda's father; in CC Madhya 10 Mahaprabhu receives him and his sons in Puri. The Sanskrit is partly corrupt here (printed "…*mantrā ṛvīkṛta*…") and is rendered by the sense.

[^01-11]: The verse is printed without a number and belongs to passage 19. The bee wearied with the honey of all the lotuses is a veiled image of Krishna wearied by His play with the gopis; so the stage-director understands it in his next speech.

[^01-12]: Narayana is here a name of Krishna. The images of Krishna's love-play with the gopis in this drama describe the transcendental (*aprākṛta*) rasa, the eternal play of the Lord with His own pleasure-potency, which cannot be compared to worldly lust (cf. CC Madhya 8.137: in Vrindavana Krishna is the "transcendental ever-fresh Cupid").

[^01-13]: By stage convention the stage-director calls the actor playing Krishna his "younger brother".

[^01-14]: The creepers and trees are pictured as pairs of lovers: shoots are hands, flowers are eyes, bees are collyrium. *Utkalikā* means both "bud" and "longing".

[^01-15]: The jester (*vidūṣaka*, a brahmin buffoon, the hero's friend) is a glutton and a simpleton, as the convention of Sanskrit drama requires. His name here is Ratikandala. *Śikhariṇī* is spiced sweet curd, *rasālā* a spiced drink.

[^01-16]: The sense of *divocita-khelam* is uncertain ("with antics fit for heaven" or "fit for the day"); Krishna teases His jester friend, at whose appearance the flowers seem to laugh.

[^01-17]: "Love" is *manoja* (literally "mind-born", a name of Kama). For Radha it is prema, the highest love for Krishna, not worldly passion (CC Adi 4.163–171).

[^01-18]: That is, "you know it yourself".

[^01-19]: Madanika is a friend of Radha. The Forest-goddess (*Vana-devatā*) is, in the Gaudiya Vaishnava tradition, a name for Vrindadevi.

[^01-20]: The Five-arrowed is Kama (Cupid). In the Gaudiya Vaishnava understanding Krishna is the "transcendental Cupid" (CC Madhya 8.137), and what is described are the symptoms of loving agitation (*sāttvika-bhāva*) in His eternal beloveds. This is not worldly passion.

[^01-21]: Radha's face is incomparable: the lotus and the moon are not its equals, and yet poets have nothing else to compare it with.

[^01-22]: The sun, halted at the zenith, seems to scold the horses of its chariot. *Kara* means both "ray" and "hand". At midday the cowherd girls go home.

[^01-23]: The jester's joke draws on the Puranic story that Vishvakarma trimmed the sun on a lathe to lessen its heat.

[^02-1]: The print reads *tvayā locanātithikṛtaḥ* — "became the guest of your eyes"; by the sense and the edition's Hindi translation, "of her (Radha's) eyes".

[^02-2]: The Flower-weaponed One (Kusumayudha) is Kama, the god of love; in the drama his names (Madana, Smara, Manmatha, Ananga etc.) stand for Radha's love, which Gaudiya Vaishnavas understand as prema, not worldly passion (CC Adi 4.163–171).

[^02-3]: The Odd-arrowed One (Vishamabana) is Kama with his five (an odd number of) arrows. *Śilīmukha* means both "arrow" and "bee".

[^02-4]: Signs of hidden love: the moon, the cuckoos and the spring breeze intensify the pain of separation (*viraha*), so Radha avoids them.

[^02-5]: "O Shiva, Shiva!" is an exclamation of pity ("alas!"). The closing line calls the audience "devoted to the feet of Hari": Ramananda writes for Vaishnava connoisseurs.

[^02-6]: A bed of lotus petals, bracelets of lotus stalks and sandal are cooling remedies for the "fever" of love-separation. The printed "*viṣa*" is to be read "*bisa*", lotus stalk. The word order of the song is unclear in places and is rendered by the sense.

[^02-7]: The device *ākāśa-bhāṣita* ("speech to the air"): a character converses with an unseen interlocutor (here the parrots) and repeats the answers herself.

[^02-8]: *Viṣkambhaka*: an interlude in which minor characters report what has happened or will happen.

[^02-9]: The letter is in Prakrit, in the *āryā* metre. Radha reproaches Krishna: He wounds the heart, but the blame falls on Madana (Kama); she sees Krishna everywhere, not Kama — a sign of the highest love, in which the beloved is seen everywhere. The edition's Sanskrit *chāyā* has the verb in the third person (*vidhyati*); the translation follows the Prakrit (*vijjhasi*, second person).

[^02-10]: The key to the whole act: Krishna only feigns indifference in order to test the firmness of Radha's love (*bhāva-parīkṣā*, "testing of feeling").

[^02-11]: Krishna deliberately takes "Madana" literally, as a man's name, and offers to deal with him as with the demons Kamsa used to send.

[^02-12]: The jester takes up the game: "Madana" is both Kama and, so he claims, his own name — and a brahmin may not be punished.

[^02-13]: *Nisṛṣṭārtha-dūtī*: in poetics, a "messenger with full authority", who herself decides what to say and how to arrange the meeting.

[^02-14]: "Mountain-stronghold of her family" — that is, one faithful to the custom of her family. Krishna continues His feigned test (see note 10).

[^02-15]: Prakrit, *āryā* metre. An allegory: Radha (the swan), driven by the heat of love, seeks refuge with Krishna (the lotus cluster), but He, "swaying" (feigning hesitation), pushes her away. The end of the line is rendered approximately from the edition's *chāyā*.

[^02-16]: Madanika sees that Krishna loves Radha too and is only hiding it.

[^02-17]: Here and in Song 8 Krishna appeals to worldly morality. But this is only a test, as He Himself says aside twice (before verse 30 and after verse 44) — just as in the *Bhāgavata* (10.29) Krishna first advises the gopis to go back to their husbands. According to Gaudiya Vaishnava teaching the gopis are Krishna's eternal associates, His own pleasure-potency (*hlādinī*). Their "marriage" to others is arranged by Yogamaya for the fullness of rasa, and the *parakīyā* love (love as for "another's" beloved) exists only in Vraja (CC Adi 4.47). Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati stresses that this transcendental *parakīyā* cannot be measured by worldly morality and must not be imitated in this world.

[^02-18]: The jester, the hero's constant companion, jokes that he is with Krishna even in His dreams.

[^02-19]: The line is unclear (printed *kimiti vayaṁ kalayāma na citram*). The edition's Hindi translation takes it differently: "…why should we abandon our own bounds?"

# Act Three. Bhava-prakasha (The Revealing of Feeling)

[^03-1]: Radha believes herself rejected and reproaches herself for having disregarded convention, not knowing that Krishna's refusal was only a test (Act 2). For Gaudiya Vaishnavas her readiness to sacrifice everything, even her good name, is a sign of the highest selfless love (CC Adi 4.167–169: for Krishna's sake the gopis gave up everything — worldly custom and Vedic duty alike).

[^03-2]: *Sragdharā* metre. The sound of Krishna's flute is called the Parabrahman in harmony with the chants of the Sama Veda: the Absolute itself is in the sound of His flute. "Beloved of Shri" (*Śrīkānta*) is Krishna. The end of the fourth line is corrupt in the edition and is rendered by the sense and the edition's Hindi translation. A fire of chaff (*kukūla*) smoulders slowly and long — an image of the unquenchable pain of separation.

[^03-3]: Shashimukhi, like Madanika, tests the firmness of Radha's love. The beginning of the verse is unclear in the edition and is rendered by the sense.

[^03-4]: Shashimukhi says that a wife stays faithful to her husband (the doe does not choose the lion) — but at once refutes herself: the lilies love the moon even when it wanes, that is, true love does not depend on the beloved's merits. A proverb spoken in consolation may mean the very opposite.

[^03-5]: *Śārdūla-vikrīḍita* metre. The last words of the third line are unclear in the edition and are rendered by the sense and the edition's Hindi translation.

[^03-6]: The first line is corrupt in the edition; the sense is restored from the Hindi translation: a bee once drawn to a flower does not leave it. Madanika consoles Radha: Krishna, having once seen her, will not abandon her.

[^03-7]: "Set with jewels" — that is, count every moment a treasure. The fourth line is one syllable short in the print and has been corrected by the metre.

[^03-8]: An allegory: the young tree Radha has raised is Krishna's love, nurtured by her; the bees that cling to its buds are a sign that love is about to blossom. Madanika hints at good news.

[^03-9]: Radha fears she will die of separation and asks her friend to remember her.

[^03-10]: Madanika recounts how Krishna listened to her stories about Radha: His feelings betray His love. A line of the song is partly unclear in the edition.

[^03-11]: *Āryā* metre. The cloud is Krishna, the water the hope of meeting, the fire the heat of separation; the doe is Radha herself. The last word is corrupt in the print and is read as *bhavitā*.

[^03-12]: *Mandākrāntā* metre. This is Krishna's verse on the picture with Radha's portrait: the lotus opening for the first time is Radha, the youth who breathes its fragrance is Krishna Himself. His earlier refusal (Act 2) was only feigned. The beginning of the fourth line is unclear in the edition.

[^03-13]: *Śikhariṇī* metre. Nightfall and moonrise intensify the pain of separation; Radha asks Madanika to hurry.

[^03-14]: "The poet Raya whose name is 'Rama'" is a play on the name of Ramananda Raya.

[^04-1]: Madanika sees Krishna suffering in separation. His face has faded like a lotus under the moon (the lotus closes at night), and His dark chest is compared to an emerald rock. The first word of the song is printed "madana" ("Kama"); by the sense and the Hindi translation we read "vadana" ("face"). The last line is unclear.

[^04-2]: *Śārdūla-vikrīḍita* metre. Krishna repents of His feigned refusal (Act 2). "Thrown away a jewel, thinking it a shell" is a proverbial image. There is no stage direction here in the Sanskrit text.

[^04-3]: The jester jokes that as a brahmin he will "draw" Radha by an attraction-spell (*ākarṣaṇa*).

[^04-4]: The jester shows Madanika dry leaves: everything fresh in the forest has already been gathered for cooling beds for the suffering Krishna. The direction "using Sanskrit" refers to the following song (sung by the jester or Madanika; the edition does not name the character).

[^04-5]: Rahu is the demon who swallows the moon at an eclipse. In separation Krishna cannot bear the moonlight and calls for Rahu to "swallow" the moon. The first line of the song is corrupt in the print and is rendered by the sense and the edition's Hindi translation.

[^04-6]: *Śikhariṇī* metre. The beginning of the verse is corrupt in the print. Krishna loved the Malaya breeze because it touches Radha, but now, in separation, the breeze torments Him too.

[^04-7]: Signs of the "fever of love" (*madana-jvara*): Radha seeks cooling with sandal, "cool moon" (camphor) and lotus leaves, but the heat of her body is such that the leaves wither and rustle.

[^04-8]: "Torment" — printed *vāmam*, "perversely, hostilely" (Hindi: "being crooked"). Krishna dwells in Radha's heart and yet does not quench her longing — a sign of the highest love in separation (*vipralambha*).

[^04-9]: The jester compares Radha's and Krishna's sufferings. The owl cannot bear the sun and the snake flees from the charmer's spell; to Krishna in separation the moon and sandal are unbearable.

[^04-10]: *Śikhariṇī* metre. Madanika relates that Radha was tested with intimidation and reminders of duty (cf. Act 3, Shashimukhi), but her love did not waver — a sign of *anurāga*, unwavering love that knows no obstacles.

[^04-11]: *Vasanta-tilakā* metre (printed as prose in the edition). "Causeless" (*akāraṇa-vatsalā*) — the friend's selfless love for both lovers, as Rupa Gosvami describes the sakhis, who are happy only in the union of Radha and Krishna.

[^04-12]: Radha has come to the place of the tryst (*abhisāra*) at night through the dark forest — in poetics an *abhisārikā*, a heroine going to her lover. In the Gaudiya Vaishnava tradition her journey through darkness and danger is an image of all-conquering prema, which takes no account of any obstacle.

[^04-13]: *Śārdūla-vikrīḍita* metre. The print has *prāleyāṁtra sati*, read as *prāleyāt trasati*, "shrinks from the dew".

[^04-14]: *Śikhariṇī* metre. "Here love alone is the refuge" (*kāmo 'tra śaraṇam*): *kāma* here is the same prema (CC Adi 4.163).

[^04-15]: At night the cakravakas are parted and cry, night-lilies open and day-lotuses close — all signs of moonrise. The eastern mountain (Udayagiri) is the mountain behind which, by tradition, the sun and moon rise.

[^04-16]: The moonlight will betray Radha, dressed in dark clothes for a night tryst (*tamisrābhisārikā*). "Eastern quarter" — literally "the quarter of Shakra", that is, of Indra.

[^04-17]: *Śārdūla-vikrīḍita* metre. A charming scene of uncertainty (*sandeha*): Krishna at first mistakes the sound of Radha's anklets for other sounds.

[^04-18]: Madhu-mathana — "Slayer of Madhu", Krishna. The garland is a sign of choosing the beloved. In the last line King Prataparudra is called "the new Madana of our time" — courtly praise of his beauty. The words "bid the Bodiless One dance" are unclear in the print (*nartitumatanumanāmaṁ*).

# Act Five. Radha-sangama (Union with Radha)

*Dawn after the night in the grove; Madanika tells Shashimukhi of the meeting of Radha and Madhava; Radha, abashed, returns in the light of morning; an alarm behind the scenes — the bull-demon Arishtasura has burst into Vraja; Krishna slays him; Madanika again unites Radha with Krishna; the closing benediction.*

*vinyasyantī śithilitabhujadvandvasaṁnāmitāṁsā |*

रचयति मामधुना सुखभरितम् ॥
प्रणयतु रुद्रनृपे सुखममृतं
रामानन्दभणितहरिरमितम् ॥ ६२ ॥

*pariṇataśāradaśaśadharavadanā*
*militā pāṇitale gurumadanā ||*
*devi kimiha paramasti madiṣṭaṁ*
*bahutarasukṛtaphalitamanudiṣṭam || dhru ||*
*pikavidhumadhumadhupāvalicaritaṁ*
*racayati māmadhunā sukhabharitam ||*
*praṇayatu rudranṛpe sukhamamṛtaṁ*
*rāmānandabhaṇitahariramitam || 62 ||*

*Word for word:* pariṇata-śārada-śaśadhara-vadanā — with a face like the full autumn moon; militā — has united; pāṇi-tale — hand in hand; guru-madanā — filled with great love; devi — O lady; kim — what; iha — here; param — further; asti — is; mad-iṣṭam — desired by Me; bahu-tara-sukṛta-phalitam — the fruit of many good deeds; anudiṣṭam — has come unbidden; pika-vidhu-madhu-madhupa-āvali-caritam — the doings of cuckoos, moon, spring and swarms of bees; racayati — make; mām — Me; adhunā — now; sukha-bharitam — filled with happiness; praṇayatu — may it bring; rudra-nṛpe — to King Rudra; sukham — happiness; amṛtam — immortality; rāmānanda-bhaṇita-hari-ramitam — the play of Hari spoken by Ramananda.

> **62.** The moon-faced one, like the full autumn moon, filled with great love, has joined Me, and her hand is in Mine.
> Lady, what else here could be desired by Me? The fruit of many good deeds has come of itself. *(Refrain.)*
> The cuckoos, the moon, spring and the swarms of bees — all now fill Me with happiness.
> May this, spoken by Ramananda — the play of Hari — bring King Rudra happiness and immortality.[^05-18]

And still, let this be so:

### Verse 63

श्रद्धाबद्धमतिर्मम प्रतिदिनं गोपाललीलस्य यः
संसेवेत रहस्यमेतदतुलं लीलामृतं लोलधीः ।
तस्मिन्मद्गतमानसे किल कृपादृष्ट्या भवत्या सदा
भाव्यं येन निजेप्सितां व्रजवने सिद्धिं समाप्नोति सः ॥ ६३ ॥

*śraddhābaddhamatirmama pratidinaṁ gopālalīlasya yaḥ*
*saṁseveta rahasyametadatulaṁ līlāmṛtaṁ loladhīḥ |*
*tasminmadgatamānase kila kṛpādṛṣṭyā bhavatyā sadā*
*bhāvyaṁ yena nijepsitāṁ vrajavane siddhiṁ samāpnoti saḥ || 63 ||*

*Word for word:* śraddhā-baddha-matiḥ — his mind bound by faith; mama — My; pratidinam — every day; gopāla-līlasya — of Gopala at play; yaḥ — who; saṁseveta — serves; rahasyam — secret; etat — this; atulam — incomparable; līlā-amṛtam — nectar of pastimes; lola-dhīḥ — eager; tasmin — on him; mad-gata-mānase — whose mind is given to Me; kila — indeed; kṛpā-dṛṣṭyā — with a gracious glance; bhavatyā — by you; sadā — always; bhāvyam — it should be; yena — so that; nija-īpsitām — desired; vraja-vane — in the forests of Vraja; siddhim — perfection; samāpnoti — attains; saḥ — he.

> **63.** Whoever, his mind bound by faith, eagerly serves day by day this incomparable secret, the nectar of the play of Me, Gopala — on him, whose mind is given to Me, may you always look with a gracious eye, so that he may attain the perfection he desires in the forests of Vraja.[^05-19]

**Madanika.** So be it.

*(Exeunt omnes.)*

*End of the Fifth Act, "Radha-sangama".*

*Thus ends the drama "Sri Jagannatha-vallabha", composed by Ramananda Raya.*

[^05-1]: *Mandākrāntā* metre. Shashimukhi sees Madanika (the Forest-goddess), who has not slept all night. "Her feet falter" — the print reads *maṁdayāsa*, corrected in the edition's errata to *mandanyāsa* ("slow step").

[^05-2]: Madanika, sleepless all night, resembles a woman after love-play. This is a hint, explained later: she spent the whole night watching the play of Radha and Krishna (verse 14). The first line of the song is unclear in places. For Gaudiya Vaishnavas the joy of the sakhis lies in arranging and beholding the meeting of Radha and Krishna, not in enjoying Krishna themselves (cf. CC Madhya 8.206–208).

[^05-3]: *Śikhariṇī* metre. A description of dawn (*prabhāta*), obligatory in Sanskrit drama. The end of the third line is unclear in the print.

[^05-4]: The last line is unclear. The edition's Hindi translation reads: "what clever woman does not imitate this cakravaka?" In the morning the female cakravaka is reunited with her mate after the night's separation.

[^05-5]: An allegory: the female bee who took a reflection for her beloved and then sees the real one is an image of the mingling of conflicting emotions (*bhāva-śābalya*).

[^05-6]: *Śārdūla-vikrīḍita* metre. Stupor (*stambha*) and trembling (*kampa*) are *sāttvika-bhāvas*, bodily signs of ecstatic love: Radha at first took Krishna's stupor for indifference and only by His trembling understood His love. The beginning of the third line is unclear in the print.

[^05-7]: Here and further on (verses 24–36) Ramananda describes the highest stage of madhura-rasa, *sambhoga*, the union of Radha and Krishna, in the language of classical poetics. Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati stresses that these pastimes (*aprākṛta-līlā*) have nothing in common with worldly relationships. Their participants are Bhagavan Himself and His own pleasure-potency (*hlādinī-śakti*). Such descriptions are meant only for devotees whose hearts are purified, and they must not be understood in a worldly way or imitated (cf. CC Adi 4.164–171: kama and prema differ as iron and gold). Ramananda himself calls them a "secret" in his closing verse 63, meant for those who serve Gopala's pastimes with faith.

[^05-8]: The print has *niśvasitāyatabhītaṁ*, whose sense is unclear. The edition's Hindi translation reads "began to breathe heavily in fear": the agitation came not from pain but from the fullness of love. Cf. note 7.

[^05-9]: *Puṣpitāgrā* metre. Shashimukhi is surprised, since Radha and Krishna are still young. Madanika replies that love needs no teaching. For Gaudiya Vaishnavas this reflects the eternal, self-manifest nature of their love: it is not acquired but abides eternally in Radha and Krishna.

[^05-10]: *Vasanta-tilakā* metre. The moon "clung to the western quarter", that is, it set and dawn came — a play on the image of the moon as a lover.

[^05-11]: *Śārdūla-vikrīḍita* metre. Returning in the morning, Radha fears being seen. Lines 3–4 are partly unclear in the print and are rendered by the sense and the edition's Hindi translation.

[^05-12]: *Śārdūla-vikrīḍita* metre. Radha fears the harmless (a stump, a crow, light) more than real danger; likewise, meeting her beloved, she is more anxious than in separation, from fear of being seen and from the fullness of feeling.

[^05-13]: The song continues the description of *sambhoga* (see note 7). The sense of "by the gentle movement of her lotus feet" (printed *mṛdupadakamalalavehā*) is unclear. The edition's Hindi translation reads: "it is at once understood from the gentle movement of her lotus feet". The closing line in the original is an appeal to the audience: "make your hearts free of fault". The connoisseurs are to hear this with a pure heart.

[^05-14]: The slaying of Arishtasura is told in *Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 10.36. "Prop of the earth" alludes to the lifting of Govardhana Hill. "Sacrificial post of the appeasement of Shakra" means Krishna's arm as the support that averts Indra's wrath. Ramananda joins this pastime to the dawn after the night in the grove. In the *Bhāgavata* Arishta comes in the evening, but as a dramatist Ramananda is free in ordering the events.

[^05-15]: *Śārdūla-vikrīḍita* metre. Madanika despairs, thinking Krishna in danger. "Mother Devaki": according to the edition's gloss, this is Yashoda's second name ("Nanda's wife has two names, Yashoda and Devaki"), as she is also called in some Puranas. "Shri, take up the vow" means "take the vow of widowhood".

[^05-16]: *Śārdūla-vikrīḍita* metre. Krishna hurled Arishta "like a ball": in the *Bhāgavata* (10.36) Krishna, seizing the bull by the horns, threw it down and killed it.

[^05-17]: "Chosen by Victory herself" — literally "embraced by the goddess of Victory at her *svayaṁvara*", the ceremony at which a bride chooses her husband.

[^05-18]: The last song of the drama, sung by Krishna. The twenty-one songs of the drama are passages 1.4, 1.22, 1.28, 1.37, 2.20, 2.24, 2.35, 2.46, 3.3, 3.8, 3.15, 3.34, 4.2, 4.15, 4.25, 4.37, 4.51, 5.3, 5.25, 5.36 and 5.62 of the edition. They are written in the style of Jayadeva's *Gīta-govinda*, each with a raga, a refrain (*dhruva*) and a closing line naming the author and dedicated to Prataparudra.

[^05-19]: *Śārdūla-vikrīḍita* metre. The closing benediction (*bharata-vākya*). Krishna asks the Forest-goddess (Madanika) to look graciously on those who with faith "serve" this "secret, the nectar of Gopala's play" (hearing, singing or staging it), so that they may attain perfection in Vraja. For Gaudiya Vaishnavas this points to the path of *rāgānugā-bhakti* under the guidance of Radha's companions. According to the CC, Mahaprabhu sang and heard the "songs of Raya's drama" day and night with Svarupa Damodara and Ramananda Raya (Madhya 2.77) and relished its verses in His ecstatic moods (Antya 20.67).
