# Kavi Karnapura. Sri Chaitanya-charita-mahakavya
## Sarga Twenty — verses 1–49 (printed pp. 572–588)

**Title of the sarga:** none — the colophon of the edition gives no title for this sarga (*iti śrī-kṛṣṇa-caitanya-caritāmṛte mahākāvye śrīla-karṇapūra-kṛtau viṁśatitamaḥ sargaḥ*, "Thus [ends] the twentieth sarga in the great poem Sri Krishna-Chaitanya-charitamrita, the work of Srila Karnapura"; it goes on: "This great poem Sri Krishna-Chaitanya-charitamrita is complete. Number of verses: 1911").
**Metre:**
- 1–36 and 41 — *śālinī* (*ma-ta-ta-ga-ga*: — — — — | — ⏑ — — ⏑ — —; 11 syllables per pada, caesura after the fourth). The edition says: "up to verse 36 — *śālinī*".
- 37 and 47 — *śārdūla-vikrīḍita* (*ma-sa-ja-sa-ta-ta-ga*: ———⏑⏑—⏑—⏑⏑⏑———⏑——⏑—; 19 syllables per pada).
- 38–39 and 48 — *mandākrāntā* (*ma-bha-na-ta-ta-ga-ga*: ————⏑⏑⏑⏑⏑——⏑——⏑——; 17 syllables per pada).
- 40 — *śikhariṇī* (*ya-ma-na-sa-bha-la-ga*: ⏑—————⏑⏑⏑⏑⏑——⏑⏑⏑—; 17 syllables per pada).
- 42–45 and 49 — *vasantatilakā* (*ta-bha-ja-ja-ga-ga*: ——⏑—⏑⏑⏑—⏑⏑—⏑——; 14 syllables per pada).
- 46 — *mālinī* (*na-na-ma-ya-ya*: ⏑⏑⏑⏑⏑⏑———⏑——⏑——; 15 syllables per pada, caesura after the eighth).

All 49 verses scan in the metres stated, provided that hiatus is respected (*śeta ity* in 31, *tata ito* in 40, *śrutaya indur* in 49: the vowels do not coalesce). Irregularities of the printed text: in 1 and 12 the second pada has only ten syllables (one of the opening heavy syllables is missing); in 19 the fourth syllable of the fourth pada is light (*gāḍham ardya-*); in 23 the printed *trailokya-nām* gives a light third syllable, and the metre requires *trailokyānām*, as in the transliteration; in 35 the second pada has only nine syllables (*kañcit kālaṁ bhūyo 'dhvanaiva*: two syllables are missing); in 41 the edition has a syllable too many in the fourth pada (*svaṁ svaṁ*), and the text is given after the Gosvami edition (GGM); in 44 the second pada has thirteen syllables (*manasā ca* restores the metre).

**Total verses:** 49. The publisher gives the total number of verses of the poem as 1911 (in the colophon and in his note after verse 49). The printed numbering of the twenty sargas runs without gaps or repetitions and adds up to 1912 (29 + 122 + 144 + 77 + 129 + 123 + 107 + 63 + 95 + 80 + 89 + 134 + 148 + 135 + 110 + 49 + 66 + 63 + 100 + 49); the difference of one is not explained by the numbering itself and is presumably a slip in the publisher's count (his other figures in the same note are also inexact; see the note on 49). The unnumbered quotations in Sargas 6 and 7 are not included in either figure.

**Word-for-word conventions:** compound words are divided into their members, each with its own meaning; case is rendered by an English preposition ("of", "with", "in", "by", "to", "from"); the vocative is given with "O". Square brackets enclose a word that is understood (for example, [whose], [like]).

**Contents of the passage:** the journey through Bengal, the return, the Lord's departure; the author's conclusion.
- 1–10 — the Lord dismisses the ministers; by Prataparudra's order houses are built for Him everywhere; crowds of people; the Lord and Puri point each other out (7); Ramananda turns back from Bhadra (9).
- 11–19 — Gauda: the abode of Raghava (12); the house of Srivasa and the crowds; the secret departure by boat at night; Srivasa and the others search for Him.
- 20–25 — Shantipura: the meeting with Advaita; **Mother Shachi** (23); six days; Advaita regales the people.
- 26–30 — a place across the Ganga, west of Navadvipa (Kuliya?); crowds; the Lord hides Himself.
- 31–36 — an obscure place, "Gopinatha" (31); the way towards the Yamuna, the return to the Ganga, then to Puri (32–34); the second departure for the Yamuna (35); Ramananda gives up his life in separation (36 — at variance with the Chaitanya-charitamrita).
- 37–41 — summary: twenty years of festivals in Puri; the Lord's departure to His own abode; chronology: sannyasa in His twenty-fourth year, three years of wandering, twenty years in Puri, forty-seven years in all (40–41).
- 42–49 — the author's conclusion:
  - the source — the records of **Murari** (Gupta), which "the child saw" (42–43);
  - a blessing on the poem; the author's humility (44–45, 48);
  - the signature: the youngest son of Shivananda Sena (46);
  - the hero of the poem is *dhīrodātta* (47);
  - the date of completion: Shaka 1464 (1542 CE), the month of Ashadha, a Monday, the second tithi of the dark fortnight (49).

**Textual cruxes:** 1 (*pāṇinā thodiyāya*; short pada), 12 (short pada), 17 (*pāyāt*), 19 (metre), 21 (*āsīn naiṣāṁ*), 22 (*yogam*), 23 (*trailokya-nām*), 25 (*yāmo*), 31, 35 (short pada), 41 (extra syllable), 42 (*keścin*), 44 (short pada) — see the notes.

Format and conventions as in Sarga One.

### 1

**1.**

```
रात्रिर्याता नाथ तल्पं जहीही-
त्याकर्ण्य पक्षिणां कूजितानि ।
नेत्रे निद्रामुद्रिते जागृहीति
द्रागाक्षिप्यन् पाणिनाथोदियाय ॥१॥
```
*rātrir yātā nātha talpaṁ jahīhīty
ākarṇya pakṣiṇāṁ kūjitāni |
netre nidrā-mudrite jāgṛhīti
drāg ākṣipyan pāṇinā thodiyāya (pāṇinātha udiyāya) ||1||*

**Word for word:** *rātriḥ* — the night; *yātā* — has gone; *nātha* — O Master; *talpam* — the bed; *jahīhi* — leave; *iti* — thus; *ākarṇya* — having heard; *pakṣiṇām* — of the birds; *kūjitāni* — the warbling; *netre* — [His] two eyes; *nidrā* — by sleep; *mudrite* — sealed; *jāgṛhi* — wake up; *iti* — [saying] thus; *drāk* — quickly; *ākṣipyan* — rubbing; *pāṇinā* — with [His] hand; *atha* — then; *udiyāya* — rose.

**Translation.** Hearing the warbling of the birds — "The night has gone, O Master; leave Your bed!" — He quickly rubbed with His hand His eyes, sealed by sleep, [saying to them,] "Wake up!", and then rose.

**Note.** The metre is *śālinī* (so the edition: "up to verse 36 — *śālinī*"). As printed, the second pada has only ten syllables (*ākarṇya pakṣiṇāṁ kūjitāni*: — — ⏑ — ⏑ — — ⏑ — ⏑): of the four heavy syllables of the opening only three are present, and *-ṇya* is light; something has dropped out. The printed run *pāṇināthodiyāya* is to be divided *pāṇinā atha udiyāya*, "with the hand; then [He] rose"; the division *pāṇinā thodiyāya* in the transliteration line is merely mechanical.

### 2

**2.**

```
निर्माल्यान्नं तत्र सद्यः समेतं
दृष्ट्वा हर्षादाह्निकान्यारभेत ।
अन्नं पानं पिष्टकादि प्रकामं
तैस्तैर्भुक्त्वा प्रीतिमांश्च प्रतस्थे ॥२॥
```
*nirmālyānnaṁ tatra sadyaḥ sametaṁ
dṛṣṭvā harṣād āhnikāny ārabheta |
annaṁ pānaṁ piṣṭakādi prakāmaṁ
tais tair bhuktvā prītimāṁś ca pratasthe ||2||*

**Word for word:** *nirmālya* — sanctified; *annam* — food; *tatra* — there; *sadyaḥ* — just; *sametam* — arrived; *dṛṣṭvā* — having seen; *harṣāt* — joyfully; *āhnikāni* — the daily rites; *ārabheta* — began; *annam* — rice; *pānam* — drink; *piṣṭaka-ādi* — cakes and the rest; *prakāmam* — to [His] heart's content; *taiḥ taiḥ* — with all of them; *bhuktvā* — having eaten; *prītimān* — pleased; *ca* — and; *pratasthe* — set out.

**Translation.** Seeing the sanctified food that had just arrived there, He joyfully began His daily rites; and having eaten to His heart's content, with all of them, rice, drink, cakes and the rest, He set out, pleased.

**Note.** The optative *ārabheta* is used in the sense of the past (for the metre).

### 3

**3.**

```
तत्रामात्यौ तेन सम्यग्विसृष्टौ
ताभ्यां भूयो नेत्रपाथोजपाथः ।
तेने क्षामे तत्तनू हन्त ताभ्या-
मुत्साहोऽयं कः प्रकारो विधातुः ॥३॥
```
*tatrāmātyau tena samyag visṛṣṭau
tābhyāṁ bhūyo netra-pāthoja-pāthaḥ |
tene kṣāme tat-tanū hanta tābhyām
utsāho 'yaṁ kaḥ prakāro vidhātuḥ ||3||*

**Word for word:** *tatra* — there; *amātyau* — the two ministers; *tena* — by Him; *samyak* — duly; *visṛṣṭau* — [were] dismissed; *tābhyām* — by the two; *bhūyaḥ* — abundantly; *netra* — of the eyes; *pāthoja* — of the lotuses; *pāthaḥ* — the water; *tene* — was shed; *kṣāme* — [became] wasted; *tat* — their; *tanū* — two bodies; *hanta* — alas; *tābhyām* — by the two [had been shown]; *utsāhaḥ* — zeal; *ayam* — this; *kaḥ* — what; *prakāraḥ* — [is] the way; *vidhātuḥ* — of the Creator.

**Translation.** There the two ministers were duly dismissed by Him. They shed abundant water from the lotuses of their eyes, and their bodies — alas! — wasted away; and such zeal [had been shown] by them! What a way the Creator has!

**Note.** The ministers are Mangaraja and Harichandana (19.94).

### 4

**4.**

```
देशं देशं प्रत्युपेयुः समन्ता-
दाज्ञा राज्ञो लेखपूर्वाः समस्ताः ।
स्थाने स्थाने नव्यनव्यं निशान्तं
सामग्रीभिः कर्त्तुमग्रे पवित्रम् ॥४॥
```
*deśaṁ deśaṁ pratyupeyuḥ samantād
ājñā rājño lekha-pūrvāḥ samastāḥ |
sthāne sthāne navya-navyaṁ niśāntaṁ
sāmagrībhiḥ karttum agre pavitram ||4||*

**Word for word:** *deśam deśam* — to land after land; *pratyupeyuḥ* — came; *samantāt* — on every side; *ājñāḥ* — the orders; *rājñaḥ* — of the king; *lekha-pūrvāḥ* — written; *samastāḥ* — all; *sthāne sthāne* — at every place; *navya-navyam* — brand-new; *niśāntam* — a dwelling; *sāmagrībhiḥ* — with provisions; *karttum* — to make; *agre* — in advance; *pavitram* — clean.

**Translation.** To land after land, on every side, came all the king's written orders: at every place a brand-new, clean dwelling was to be made ready in advance, with provisions.

### 5

**5.**

```
उद्यन्नासीद्यत्र तैरेष नाथो
हर्षोत्कर्षाल्लक्षसंख्यैर्मनुष्यैः ।
निष्प्रत्यूहं तत्र तत्रेक्षणाब्जैः
काकुप्रोक्तैः पूजितः संस्तुतश्च ॥५॥
```
*udyann āsīd yatra tair eṣa nātho
harṣotkarṣāl lakṣa-saṅkhyair manuṣyaiḥ |
niṣpratyūhaṁ tatra tatrekṣaṇābjaiḥ
kāku-proktaiḥ pūjitaḥ saṁstutaś ca ||5||*

**Word for word:** *udyan* — appearing; *āsīt* — was; *yatra* — wherever; *taiḥ* — by those; *eṣaḥ* — this; *nāthaḥ* — Master; *harṣa* — of joy; *utkarṣāt* — from the excess; *lakṣa* — in hundreds of thousands; *saṅkhyaiḥ* — numbering; *manuṣyaiḥ* — by people; *niṣpratyūham* — without hindrance; *tatra tatra* — there; *īkṣaṇa* — of the eyes; *abjaiḥ* — with the lotuses; *kāku* — plaintive; *proktaiḥ* — with words; *pūjitaḥ* — [was] worshipped; *saṁstutaḥ* — praised; *ca* — and.

**Translation.** Wherever this Master appeared, there, out of the excess of their joy, people numbering hundreds of thousands worshipped Him without hindrance with the lotuses of their eyes and praised Him with plaintive words.

### 6

**6.**

```
अद्य श्वो वा नूनमत्रैष्यतीति
प्रोच्चैरासीदग्रतो हर्षनादः ।
पश्चादायातीति तस्मादुपेतो
भो भोः पश्चादेव सर्वत्र भूयः ॥६॥
```
*adya śvo vā nūnam atraiṣyatīti
proccair āsīd agrato harṣa-nādaḥ |
paścād āyātīti tasmād upeto
bho bhoḥ paścād eva sarvatra bhūyaḥ ||6||*

**Word for word:** *adya* — today; *śvaḥ* — tomorrow; *vā* — or; *nūnam* — surely; *atra* — here; *eṣyati* — He will come; *iti* — thus; *pra-uccaiḥ* — very loudly; *āsīt* — there was; *agrataḥ* — ahead; *harṣa* — of joy; *nādaḥ* — a cry; *paścāt* — afterwards; *āyāti* — He is coming; *iti* — thus; *tasmāt* — then; *upetaḥ* — He has come; *bho bhoḥ* — ho, ho!; *paścāt eva* — afterwards; *sarvatra* — everywhere; *bhūyaḥ* — again.

**Translation.** "Today or tomorrow He will surely come here!" — such was the cry of joy that rang out very loudly ahead [of Him]; afterwards, "He is coming!"; then, "Ho, ho! He has come!" — and so again everywhere afterwards.

### 7

**7.**

```
केचित् केचित्तत्र पप्रच्छुरार्य्याः
क्वासौ क्वासौ कृष्णचैतन्यचन्द्रः ।
इत्थं नाथं पूरिणं तं प्रभुं तं
तावन्योन्यं दर्शयामासतुस्तान् ॥७॥
```
*kecit kecit tatra papracchur āryāḥ
kvāsau kvāsau kṛṣṇa-caitanya-candraḥ |
itthaṁ nāthaṁ pūriṇaṁ taṁ prabhuṁ taṁ
tāv anyonyaṁ darśayāmāsatus tān ||7||*

**Word for word:** *kecit kecit* — some; *tatra* — there; *papracchuḥ* — asked; *āryāḥ* — worthy [people]; *kva asau* — where is He; *kṛṣṇa-caitanya-candraḥ* — Krishna-Chaitanya-chandra; *ittham* — thus; *nātham* — the Master; *pūriṇam* — Puri; *tam* — that; *prabhum* — Lord; *tam* — that; *tau* — the two; *anyonyam* — each other; *darśayāmāsatuḥ* — pointed out; *tān* — to them.

**Translation.** Some worthy people there asked: "Where is He, where is He — Krishna-Chaitanya-chandra?" Thereupon those two pointed each other out to them: [Puri showed them] the Master, that Lord, [and the Lord showed them] that Puri.

**Note.** A playful scene: each of the two points to the other. Puri is presumably Paramananda Puri, the Lord's companion on this journey (19.6, 12). There is no such scene in the Chaitanya-charitamrita (Madhya 16).

### 8

**8.**

```
वासं वासं प्रत्युपेते प्रभाते
राज्ञामाज्ञायन्त्रिताः सर्व एव ।
देशे देशे शुद्धगेहानि कृत्वा
सामग्रीश्च प्रोन्मदा आनयन्ति ॥८॥
```
*vāsaṁ vāsaṁ pratyupete prabhāte
rājñām ājñā-yantritāḥ sarva eva |
deśe deśe śuddha-gehāni kṛtvā
sāmagrīś ca pronmadā ānayanti ||8||*

**Word for word:** *vāsam vāsam* — at halting place after halting place; *pratyupete* — when [He] had arrived; *prabhāte* — at dawn; *rājñām* — of the kings; *ājñā* — by the order; *yantritāḥ* — constrained; *sarve eva* — all; *deśe deśe* — in every land; *śuddha* — clean; *gehāni* — houses; *kṛtvā* — having built; *sāmagrīḥ* — provisions; *ca* — and; *pronmadāḥ* — in rapture; *ānayanti* — bring.

**Translation.** Whenever He arrived at dawn at one halting place after another, all [the people] in every land, bound by the order of the kings, built clean houses and in rapture brought provisions.

**Note.** *Rājñām* is plural, "of the kings"; perhaps it stands for *rājño*, "of the king".

### 9

**9.**

```
रामानन्दो भद्रपर्यन्तमेत्य
प्रत्यावृत्तस्तेन सम्यग्विसृष्टः ।
विच्छेदार्त्तः क्षेत्रमेव प्रतस्थे
गौराङ्गोऽयं सोऽप्युपेयादुदीचीम् ॥९॥
```
*rāmānando bhadra-paryantam etya
pratyāvṛttas tena samyag visṛṣṭaḥ |
vicchedārttaḥ kṣetram eva pratasthe
gaurāṅgo 'yaṁ so 'py upeyād udīcīm ||9||*

**Word for word:** *rāmānandaḥ* — Ramananda; *bhadra-paryantam* — as far as Bhadra; *etya* — having come; *pratyāvṛttaḥ* — turned back; *tena* — by Him; *samyak* — duly; *visṛṣṭaḥ* — dismissed; *viccheda* — by separation; *ārttaḥ* — afflicted; *kṣetram eva* — to [Sri] Kshetra; *pratasthe* — set out; *gaurāṅgaḥ ayam* — and this Gauranga; *saḥ api* — He for His part; *upeyāt* — went; *udīcīm* — to the north.

**Translation.** Ramananda, having come as far as Bhadra and been duly dismissed by Him, turned back and, afflicted by separation, set out for [Sri] Kshetra; and Gauranga for His part went on to the north.

**Note.** Bhadra is Bhadraka. According to the Chaitanya-charitamrita (Madhya 16.151–155), Ramananda accompanies the Lord as far as Remuna, and there, taking leave, falls unconscious.

### 10

**10.**

```
ओड्रं यावद्भूपतेर्लेखयुक्ता
आसंस्तावत्तावदेवं धुरीणैः ।
प्रातर्धूपान्तर्गतं राजयोग्यं
निर्माल्यं चानीतमेव प्रकामम् ॥१०॥
```
*oḍraṁ yāvad bhūpater lekha-yuktā
āsaṁs tāvat tāvad evaṁ dhurīṇaiḥ |
prātar-dhūpāntargataṁ rāja-yogyaṁ
nirmālyaṁ cānītam eva prakāmam ||10||*

**Word for word:** *oḍram yāvat* — as far as Odra [extended]; *bhūpateḥ* — of the king; *lekha* — with the letters; *yuktāḥ* — provided; *āsan* — [the places] were; *tāvat tāvat* — so far, all the way; *evam* — thus; *dhurīṇaiḥ* — by the officials in charge; *prātaḥ* — of the morning; *dhūpa* — *dhupa* [offering]; *antargatam* — forming part; *rāja-yogyam* — fit for a king; *nirmālyam* — the sanctified remnants; *ca* — and; *ānītam eva* — were brought; *prakāmam* — in abundance.

**Translation.** As far as Odra [extended], [the places] were provided with the king's letters; and all that way the officials in charge thus brought in abundance the sanctified remnants of the morning *dhupa* offering, fit for a king.

**Note.** Odra is Orissa.

### 11

**11.**

```
श्रीमान् गौडं देशमासाद्य गङ्गा
द्रष्टव्येति प्रेमवैह्वल्यनुन्नः ।
तत्संसृष्टिस्निग्धमुग्धान्तरात्मा
तत्तत्स्थानाप्यायिताङ्गः स रेजे ॥११॥
```
*śrīmān gauḍaṁ deśam āsādya gaṅgā
draṣṭavyeti prema-vaihvalya-nunnaḥ |
tat-saṁsṛṣṭi-snigdha-mugdhāntarātmā
tat-tat-sthānāpyāyitāṅgaḥ sa reje ||11||*

**Word for word:** *śrīmān* — the beautiful [Lord]; *gauḍam* — of Gauda; *deśam* — the land; *āsādya* — having reached; *gaṅgā* — the Ganga; *draṣṭavyā* — must be seen; *iti* — thus; *prema* — of love; *vaihvalya* — by the agitation; *nunnaḥ* — impelled; *tat* — with her; *saṁsṛṣṭi* — by contact; *snigdha* — softened; *mugdha* — [and] charmed; *antara-ātmā* — [whose] inner self [was]; *tat-tat* — of those various; *sthāna* — places; *āpyāyita* — refreshed; *aṅgaḥ* — [whose] limbs [were]; *saḥ* — He; *reje* — shone.

**Translation.** Having reached the land of Gauda, the beautiful [Lord], impelled by the agitation of love — "The Ganga must be seen!" — shone, His inner self softened and charmed by contact with her, His limbs refreshed by those various places.

**Note.** Gauda is Bengal. The Gosvami edition (GGM) has the same text.

### 12

**12.**

```
आगत्य श्रीराघवस्याश्रमान्तः
श्रीगौरश्चन्द्रवत् पूर्वशैलम् ।
गन्धैर्माल्यैः पुष्पधूपोपहारैः
प्रेमाविष्टः कौतुकी संममाद ॥१२॥
```
*āgatya śrī-rāghavasyāśramāntaḥ
śrī-gauraś candravat pūrva-śailam |
gandhair mālyaiḥ puṣpa-dhūpopahāraiḥ
premāviṣṭaḥ kautukī saṁmamāda ||12||*

**Word for word:** *āgatya* — having come; *śrī-rāghavasya* — of Sri Raghava; *āśrama* — the abode; *antaḥ* — into; *śrī-gauraḥ* — Sri Gaura; *candra-vat* — like the moon; *pūrva-śailam* — [to] the Eastern Mountain; *gandhaiḥ* — with fragrances; *mālyaiḥ* — with garlands; *puṣpa* — flowers; *dhūpa* — incense; *upahāraiḥ* — [and] offerings; *prema* — by love; *āviṣṭaḥ* — possessed; *kautukī* — delighted; *saṁmamāda* — rejoiced.

**Translation.** Having come into the abode of Sri Raghava, as the moon [comes] to the Eastern Mountain, Sri Gaura, possessed by love and delighted, rejoiced amid fragrances, garlands, flowers, incense and offerings.

**Note.** Raghava Pandita of Panihati (cf. the Chaitanya-charitamrita, Madhya 16.202–205: the Lord arrives at Panihati by boat, and Raghava Pandita leads Him with difficulty through the crowd to his house). As printed, the second pada has only ten syllables (*śrī-gauraś candravat pūrva-śailam*: — — — — ⏑ — — ⏑ — ⏑): of the five heavy syllables of the opening only four are present.

### 13

**13.**

```
तत्र स्थित्वा राघवस्याश्रमेऽसौ
नीत्वा नाथः पञ्चषान् वासरान् सः ।
ज्येष्ठं तावच्छ्रीनवद्वीपभूमा-

jyeṣṭhaṁ tāvac chrī-navadvīpa-bhūmāv

**Word for word:** *tatra* — there; *sthitvā* — having stayed; *rāghavasya* — of Raghava; *āśrame* — in the abode; *asau* — He; *nītvā* — having spent; *nāthaḥ* — the Master; *pañcaṣān* — five or six; *vāsarān* — days; *saḥ* — He; *jyeṣṭham* — the senior [companion]; *tāvat* — meanwhile; *śrī-navadvīpa* — of Sri Navadvipa; *bhūmau* — to the land; *agre* — ahead; *prītyā* — lovingly; *preṣayāmāsa* — sent; *hṛṣṭaḥ* — joyful.

**Note.** "The senior" is apparently Paramananda Puri. The Gosvami edition (GGM) has the same text.

### 14

**14.**

```
तस्मिन् याते गौरचन्द्रः समेतः
श्रीवासस्य प्रेमपात्रस्य गेहम् ।

**15.**

```
द्वित्रैरस्मिन् वासरैर्लक्षसंख्या
भूयो भूयो हर्षपाथोधिमग्नाः ।

**Word for word:** *dvi-traiḥ* — in two or three; *asmin* — here; *vāsaraiḥ* — days; *lakṣa-saṅkhyāḥ* — hundreds of thousands; *bhūyaḥ bhūyaḥ* — again and again; *harṣa* — of joy; *pāthodhi* — in the ocean; *magnāḥ* — immersed; *yāta-āyātam* — going and coming; *sarvataḥ* — from all sides; *cakruḥ* — made; *atra* — here; *chidram* — a gap; *na āsīt* — there was not; *ca* — and; *evam* — such [is]; *asya* — His; *anubhāvaḥ* — power.

**Translation.** In two or three days here hundreds of thousands [of people], immersed again and again in an ocean of joy, came and went from all sides, and there was not [even] a gap here. Such is His power!

### 16

**16.**

```
रथ्यास्वोकःद्वारि केचिद्द्रुमेषु
प्राचीरेषु प्रायशोऽन्ये मनुष्याः ।
आसन् लीलाभित्तिचित्रप्रतीका
नोत्कण्ठानां पारमीयुः कदाचित् ॥१६॥
```
*rathyāsv okaḥ-dvāri kecid drumeṣu
prācīreṣu prāyaśo 'nye manuṣyāḥ |
āsan līlā-bhitti-citra-pratīkā
notkaṇṭhānāṁ pāram īyuḥ kadācit ||16||*

**Word for word:** *rathyāsu* — in the streets; *okaḥ* — of houses; *dvāri* — at the doors; *kecit* — some; *drumeṣu* — in the trees; *prācīreṣu* — on the walls; *prāyaśaḥ* — mostly; *anye* — others; *manuṣyāḥ* — people; *āsan* — were; *līlā* — of a pleasure-[house]; *bhitti* — on the wall; *citra* — pictures; *pratīkāḥ* — like; *na* — not; *utkaṇṭhānām* — of [their] longing; *pāram* — the end; *īyuḥ* — reached; *kadācit* — ever.

**Translation.** Some people were in the streets, at the doors of houses, in the trees; others, for the most part, on the walls — [standing] like pictures on the wall of a pleasure-house; and they never reached the end of their longing.

**Note.** *Okaḥ-dvāri* keeps the visarga before *d* against the rules of sandhi (regularly *oko-dvāri*); the metre does not require it, since *-ko-* would be heavy as well.

### 17

**17.**

```
रात्रावेकोऽपह्नुतो नौकयासौ
तत्तद्ग्रामस्योत्तरेणान्यदेशम् ।
आयातः श्रीवासुदेवस्य गेहं
गत्वा पायात् श्रीशिवानन्दगेहम् ॥१७॥
```
*rātrāv eko 'pahnuto naukayāsau
tat-tad-grāmasyottareṇānya-deśam |
āyātaḥ śrī-vāsudevasya gehaṁ
gatvā pāyāt (prāyāt?) śrī-śivānanda-geham ||17||*

**Word for word:** *rātrau* — at night; *ekaḥ* — alone; *apahnutaḥ* — concealed; *naukayā* — by boat; *asau* — He; *tat-tat* — that; *grāmasya* — of the village; *uttareṇa* — to the north; *anya* — another; *deśam* — to a place; *āyātaḥ* — having come; *śrī-vāsudevasya* — of Sri Vasudeva; *geham* — to the house; *gatvā* — having gone; *prāyāt* (?) — set out; *śrī-śivānanda* — of Sri Shivananda; *geham* — for the house.

**Translation.** At night, alone and concealed, He came by boat to another place, north of that village; having gone to the house of Sri Vasudeva, He [then] set out for the house of Sri Shivananda.

**Note.** *Pāyāt* is probably a misprint for *prāyāt*, "set out"; one could also divide *gatvā 'pāyāt* (*apāyāt*, "went away"). From the context (18–19: Srivasa and the others search for Him and cannot find Him) the subject is the Lord Himself, who has secretly left the crowd. Shivananda Sena was of Kumarahatta (Kanchanapalli); Vasudeva is Vasudeva Datta: the Chaitanya-charitamrita (Madhya 16.206) names the two together. The Gosvami edition (GGM) has the same text.

### 18

**18.**

```
अस्मिन् गेहे रात्रिमेकां तु नीत्वा
भिक्षां चक्रे देश एवोत्तरे सः ।
तत्तल्लोकैर्लक्षसंख्यैः समेतो

```
*asmin gehe rātrim ekāṁ tu nītvā
bhikṣāṁ cakre deśa evottare saḥ |

**Word for word:** *asmin* — in this; *gehe* — house; *rātrim* — night; *ekām* — one; *tu* — and; *nītvā* — having spent; *bhikṣām* — [His] meal; *cakre* — took; *deśe eva uttare* — in that same northern quarter; *saḥ* — He; *tat-tat* — of those parts; *lokaiḥ* — by the people; *lakṣa-saṅkhyaiḥ* — numbering hundreds of thousands; *sametaḥ* — accompanied; *nauka-ārūḍhaḥ* — having boarded a boat; *śāntipuryām* — to Shantipura; *jagāma* — went.

**Translation.** Having spent one night in this house, He took His meal in that same northern quarter; and, accompanied by hundreds of thousands of the people of those parts, He boarded a boat and went to Shantipura.

### 19

**19.**

```
श्रीवासाद्यैस्तैरथालोक्य नैनं
प्रत्युद्विग्नैः सर्वतोऽन्विष्य भूयः ।
यावन्नैषोऽदर्शि तावत् सुदुःखै-
र्गाढं गाढमर्द्यमानैरभावि ॥१९॥
```
*śrīvāsādyais tair athālokya nainaṁ
pratyudvignaiḥ sarvato 'nviṣya bhūyaḥ |
yāvan naiṣo 'darśi tāvat suduḥkhair
gāḍhaṁ gāḍham ardyamānair abhāvi ||19||*

**Word for word:** *śrīvāsa-ādyaiḥ* — by Srivasa and the others; *taiḥ* — by those; *atha* — then; *ālokya* — seeing; *na* — not; *enam* — Him; *pratyudvignaiḥ* — alarmed; *sarvataḥ* — everywhere; *anviṣya* — having searched; *bhūyaḥ* — at length; *yāvat* — as long as; *na* — not; *eṣaḥ* — He; *adarśi* — was found; *tāvat* — so long; *su-duḥkhaiḥ* — in great grief; *gāḍham gāḍham* — sorely, sorely; *ardyamānaiḥ* — tormented; *abhāvi* — [they] remained.

**Translation.** Then Srivasa and the others, not seeing Him, were alarmed and searched everywhere at length; and as long as He was not found, they remained in great grief, sorely, sorely tormented.

**Note.** *Abhāvi* is an impersonal passive ("it was [so] with them"). As printed, the fourth syllable of the fourth pada is light (*gāḍhaṁ gāḍham ardya-*: — — — ⏑ — ⏑ …), where the *śālinī* requires a heavy one.

**20.**

```
नावा गच्छन् स्वर्धुनीमध्यभूमौ
नाम्नां गाथां लोलचित्तः प्रकाश्य ।
अद्वैतस्य ग्राममासाद्य नाथः

```
*nāvā gacchan svardhunī-madhya-bhūmau
nāmnāṁ gāthāṁ lola-cittaḥ prakāśya |

**Word for word:** *nāvā* — by boat; *gacchan* — going; *svardhunī* — of the Heavenly River (the Ganga); *madhya-bhūmau* — in the midst; *nāmnām* — of the Names; *gāthām* — the song; *lola* — tremulous; *cittaḥ* — [whose] heart [was]; *prakāśya* — having sung out; *advaitasya* — of Advaita; *grāmam* — the village; *āsādya* — having reached; *nāthaḥ* — the Master; *premnā* — out of love; *uttasthau* — rose; *gantum* — to go; *atyantam* — extremely; *utkaḥ* — eager.

**Translation.** Going by boat in the midst of the Heavenly River and, His heart tremulous, singing out the song of the Names, the Master reached Advaita's village and rose out of love, extremely eager to go [to Him].

### 21

**21.**

```
मध्येद्वारं तेन सार्द्धं महार्हः
सङ्गस्तस्याश्लेषकोलाहलेन ।
आसीन्नैषां प्राणिनां भाग्यभाजां
चक्षुःश्रोत्रद्वन्द्वतृप्त्यै बभूव ॥२१॥
```
*madhye-dvāraṁ tena sārdhaṁ mahārhaḥ
saṅgas tasyāśleṣa-kolāhalena |
āsīn naiṣāṁ (āsīd eṣāṁ?) prāṇināṁ bhāgya-bhājāṁ
cakṣuḥ-śrotra-dvandva-tṛptyai babhūva ||21||*

**Word for word:** *madhye-dvāram* — in the doorway; *tena sārdham* — with Him [Advaita]; *mahā-arhaḥ* — most precious; *saṅgaḥ* — the meeting; *tasya* — His; *āśleṣa* — of embraces; *kolāhalena* — with the clamour; *āsīt* — there was; *na* — not; *eṣām* — of these; *prāṇinām* — living beings; *bhāgya-bhājām* — fortunate; *cakṣuḥ* — of eyes; *śrotra* — [and] ears; *dvandva* — of the pairs; *tṛptyai* — for the satisfaction; *babhūva* — was.

**Translation.** In the doorway there took place His most precious meeting with Him, amid the clamour of embraces; [yet] it was not enough to satisfy the eyes and ears of these fortunate beings.

**Note.** The printed *āsīn naiṣāṁ* is to be divided *āsīt* | *na eṣām*: *āsīt* completes the first sentence ("there was the meeting"), and *na … tṛptyai babhūva* says that what they saw and heard did not sate the eyes and ears of the fortunate onlookers — they could not have enough of it. So the printed text gives good sense, and an emendation to *āsīd eṣām* ("it became the satisfaction of the eyes and ears of these fortunate beings") is not necessary. The Gosvami edition (GGM) has the same text.

**22.**

```

### 23

**23.**

```

### 24

**24.**

```
तत्रैवासीत् षड्दिनानि क्रमेण

**25.**

### 26

**26.**

```
अन्येद्युः स श्रीनवद्वीपभूमेः
पारेगङ्गं पश्चिमे क्वापि देशे ।

pāre-gaṅgaṁ paścime kvāpi deśe |

netrānandaṁ samyag āgatya tene ||26||*

**Word for word:** *anyedyuḥ* — on another day; *saḥ* — He; *śrī-navadvīpa-bhūmeḥ* — of the land of Sri Navadvipa; *pāre-gaṅgam* — beyond the Ganga; *paścime* — in the west; *kva api* — in some; *deśe* — place; *śrīmān* — the beautiful [Lord]; *sarva* — of all; *prāṇinām* — living beings; *tat-tat* — by each of His; *aṅgaiḥ* — limbs; *netra* — for the eyes; *ānandam* — joy; *samyak* — fully; *āgatya* — having come; *tene* — spread.

**Translation.** On another day the beautiful [Lord], having come to a certain place beyond the Ganga, to the west of the land of Sri Navadvipa, fully spread joy for the eyes of all living beings by each of His limbs.

**Note.** According to the Chaitanya-charitamrita (Madhya 16.207–209), [He stayed] at the house of Vidya-Vachaspati, and then for a week at the house of Madhava Dasa, where hundreds of thousands of people flocked (by tradition, in Kuliya).

**30.**

```

नेत्रानन्दं सर्वलोकस्य तन्वं-

### 31

**31.**

```
कश्चिद्गोपीनाथशीतिप्रसिद्धं
गोपीनाथे शेत इत्यन्वयेन ।
तस्मिन् देशे क्वापि गौराङ्गचन्द्रः
प्रेमाविष्टो वीक्ष्य शश्वन्ननन्द ॥३१॥
```
*kaścid gopīnātha-śīti-prasiddhaṁ
gopīnāthe śeta ity anvayena |
tasmin deśe kvāpi gaurāṅga-candraḥ
premāviṣṭo vīkṣya śaśvan nananda ||31||*

**Word for word:** *kaścit* — a certain [one]; *gopīnātha-śīti* (= *gopīnātha-śī iti*? ⟨?⟩) — as "Gopinatha-shi"; *prasiddham* — renowned; *gopīnāthe* — at Gopinatha; *śete* — lies (sleeps); *iti* — thus; *anvayena* — by the derivation; *tasmin* — in that; *deśe* — region; *kva api* — somewhere; *gaurāṅga-candraḥ* — Gaurangachandra; *prema* — by love; *āviṣṭaḥ* — overcome; *vīkṣya* — having seen; *śaśvat* — continually; *nananda* — rejoiced.

**Translation.** Somewhere in that region — renowned as "Gopinatha[-shi]" ⟨?⟩ by the derivation "he lies at Gopinatha" — Gaurangachandra, having seen a certain [person], was overcome by love and rejoiced continually.

**Note.** An obscure verse ⟨?⟩. The syntax does not resolve cleanly: *kaścit* is nominative, *prasiddham* accusative or neuter. The translation takes *prasiddham* with the region and *kaścit* as the person the Lord saw. *-śīti* is probably *śī iti*: the name "Gopinatha-shi" (*śī*, "lying") explained by the derivation *gopīnāthe śete*, "he lies at Gopinatha". If *kaścit* is a misprint for *kañcit* (accusative), the sense would be "having seen a certain [one] renowned as 'Gopinatha-shi'". Perhaps a place where Gopinatha "sleeps" is meant (cf. Remuna with Kshirachora Gopinatha?); it cannot be identified from our sources. The Gosvami edition (GGM) has the same text.

### 32

**32.**

```

गाढोत्कण्ठः पश्चिमे क्वापि गत्वा ।
प्रत्यावृत्तो भूय एष स्वचित्ते

pratyāvṛtto bhūya eṣa sva-citte
kiṁvālokya svardhunī-tīram āyāt ||32||*

**Word for word:** *kālindīye* — of the Kalindi; *tīre eva* — to the very bank; *prayātum* — to go; *gāḍha* — deeply; *utkaṇṭhaḥ* — longing; *paścime* — to the west; *kva api* — somewhere; *gatvā* — having gone; *pratyāvṛttaḥ* — turned back; *bhūyaḥ* — again; *eṣaḥ* — He; *sva-citte* — in His own heart; *kiṁvā* — something; *ālokya* — having considered; *svardhunī* — of the Heavenly River; *tīram* — to the bank; *āyāt* — came.

**Translation.** Deeply longing to go to the very bank of the Kalindi, He went somewhere to the west; but, having considered something in His own heart, He turned back and came again to the bank of the Heavenly River.

**Note.** The Kalindi is the Yamuna; the Heavenly River (*svardhunī*) is the Ganga. According to the Chaitanya-charitamrita (Madhya 16.211, 265–274), the Lord, having reached Kanai Natashala, turns back, remembering Sanatana's words that one does not go to Vrindavana with a crowd.

**34.**

यत्र श्रीमांश्चित्रमेवावतीर्णः ।

**Word for word:** *tat-tat* — by this or that; *vyājāt* — pretext; *svardhunī* — of the Heavenly River; *tīram* — to the bank; *āyāt* — came; *yatra* — where; *śrīmān* — the beautiful [Lord]; *citram eva* — wondrously; *avatīrṇaḥ* — had descended; *netra* — for the eyes; *ānandam* — joy; *sarva-lokasya* — of all people; *kṛtvā* — having given; *nīlādri* — on Niladri; *stha* — of Him who dwells; *prītaye* — for the delight; *bhūyaḥ* — again; *āsīt* — was [there].

**Note.** *Nīlādri-stha*, "He who dwells on Niladri", is Jagannatha.

**35.**

kañcit kālaṁ bhūyo 'dhvanaiva (tenaiva?) |

According to the Chaitanya-charitamrita (Madhya 17), the second journey to Vrindavana goes through the forest of Jharikhanda, not through Bengal; Karnapura compresses the events. The Gosvami edition (GGM) has the same text.

**36.**

```

"Went to His own abode" — the disappearance of the Lord from the world. "With some of them" — together with those associates who departed after Him.

**Word for word:** *prema* — of love; *ambhodhau* — in the ocean; *jagat* — the world; *atiśaye* — boundless; *majjayitvā* — having immersed; *saḥ* — He; *bhūyaḥ* — then; *viccheda* — of separation; *agnau* — in the fire; *api ca* — also; *vidadhe* — made; *magnam* — sunk; *atyanta-durge* — utterly impassable; *citram citram* — wonderful, wonderful; *tat api* — even so; *satatam* — always; *prema* — of love; *sindhuḥ* — the ocean; *balīyān* — stronger; *āsīt* — was; *kaḥ ayam* — what is this; *śiva śiva* — Heaven forbid; *mahān* — great; *gauracandra* — of Gaurachandra; *anubhāvaḥ* — power.

**39.**

```
नानादेशान्निजनिजजनानेवमेकत्र कृत्वा

तैस्तैः सार्द्धं बत विलसितो हन्त गौडोत्कलेषु

```
*nānā-deśān nija-nija-janān evam ekatra kṛtvā
tān anyonyaṁ praṇaya-nibiḍān kārayitvā prakāmam |
tais taiḥ sārdhaṁ bata vilasito hanta gauḍotkaleṣu
svaṁ dhāmāsmin gatavati gatā bhūr viyogāgni-sindhau ||39||*

**Word for word:** *nānā* — from various; *deśāt* — lands; *nija-nija* — His own; *janān* — people; *evam* — thus; *ekatra* — in one place; *kṛtvā* — having gathered; *tān* — them; *anyonyam* — to one another; *praṇaya* — by love; *nibiḍān* — closely bound; *kārayitvā* — having made; *prakāmam* — fully; *taiḥ taiḥ sārdham* — together with all of them; *bata* — ah; *vilasitaḥ* — having sported; *hanta* — alas; *gauḍa* — in Gauda; *utkaleṣu* — [and] in Utkala; *svam* — to His own; *dhāma* — abode; *asmin* — He; *gatavati* — when [He] had gone; *gatā* — went (sank); *bhūḥ* — the earth; *viyoga* — of separation; *agni* — of fire; *sindhau* — into the ocean.

**Translation.** Having thus gathered His own people from various lands in one place and made them fully bound to one another by close love, He — ah! — sported with all of them in Gauda and Utkala; [and] when He — alas! — had gone to His own abode, the earth sank into an ocean of the fire of separation.

**Note.** Metre — *mandākrāntā*; the verse scans correctly. Utkala is Orissa.

```

prakāmaṁ sannyāsaṁ samakṛta navadvīpa-talataḥ |

**Word for word:** *caturviṁśe* — in the twenty-fourth [year]; *tāvat* — first; *prakaṭita* — made manifest; *nija* — by His own; *prema* — love; *vivaśaḥ* — overpowered; *prakāmam* — fully; *sannyāsam* — sannyasa; *samakṛta* — accepted; *navadvīpa-talataḥ* — from Navadvipa; *tri-varṣaḥ* — three years; *ca* — and; *kṣetrāt* — from the Kshetra; *api* — even; *tataḥ itaḥ* — hither and thither; *yan* — going; *agamayat* — spent; *tathā* — thus; *dṛṣṭvā* — beholding; *yātrāḥ* — the festivals; *vyanayat* — passed; *akhilāḥ* — all; *viṁśati* — twenty; *samāḥ* — years.

**Translation.** In the twenty-fourth [year], overpowered by His own love, which He had made manifest, He first fully accepted sannyasa, [leaving] from Navadvipa; three years He spent going hither and thither, [setting out] even from the Kshetra; and thus, beholding the festivals, He passed all of twenty years.

**Note.** Metre — *śikhariṇī* (⏑—————⏑⏑⏑⏑⏑——⏑⏑⏑—; 17 syllables per pada); the verse scans correctly. *Tri-varṣaḥ* is nominative; the sense requires "three years" as the object of *agamayat* ⟨?⟩. Karnapura's chronology: sannyasa at twenty-four, three years of wandering, twenty years in Puri — forty-seven years of life in all (cf. verse 41). In the Chaitanya-charitamrita (Madhya 1.15–21): twenty-four years in Navadvipa and twenty-four years after sannyasa — six years of travel and eighteen years in Puri.

**42.**

```

यद्यद्विलासललितं समलेखि तज्ज्ञै-

*āśaiśavaṁ prabhu-caritra-vilāsa-vijñaiḥ
keścin (kaiścin) murārir iti maṅgala-nāmadheyaiḥ |

**Word for word:** *ā-śaiśavam* — from childhood; *prabhu* — of the Lord; *caritra* — of the deeds; *vilāsa* — [and] pastimes; *vijñaiḥ* — by [one] who knew; *kaiścit* — by a certain [person]; *murāriḥ iti* — "Murari"; *maṅgala* — auspicious; *nāmadheyaiḥ* — bearing the name; *yat yat* — whatever; *vilāsa* — of the pastimes; *lalitam* — charm; *samalekhi* — was written down; *tat-jñaiḥ* — by [one] who knew it; *tat tat* — all that; *vilokya* — having seen; *vililekha* — wrote; *śiśuḥ* — child; *saḥ eṣaḥ* — this very.

**Translation.** Whatever charm of the pastimes was written down by a certain [person] bearing the auspicious name "Murari" — one who from childhood knew the deeds and pastimes of the Lord, who knew these things — having seen all that, this very child wrote [it].

"The child" is the author himself. *Ā-śaiśavam* may also mean "from [the Lord's] childhood on". The plural instrumentals (*kaiścit … vijñaiḥ … nāmadheyaiḥ … taj-jñaiḥ*) are honorific. In the edition *keścin* is a misprint for *kaiścin*.

*baddhāñjaliḥ śirasi nirbhara-kāku-vādair

```

```

**Word for word:** *dhīra-udātta* — "steadfast and exalted" (*dhīrodātta*); *mahattamaḥ* — supremely great; *guṇa* — of virtues; *nidhiḥ* — a treasury; *yasmin* — in which; *asau* — this one; *nāyakaḥ* — the hero; *yatra* — in which; *amūḥ* — these; *lipayaḥ* — writings; *nirantara* — unceasingly; *balat* — growing strong; *prema* — love; *prakāśa* — manifesting; *akṣarāḥ* — [whose] syllables; *yatra* — in which; *aneka* — of many; *mahā-mahā-uttama* — the very greatest; *dhiyām* — of minds; *cāritram* — the conduct; *antargatam* — is contained; *tat* — that; *caitanya* — of Chaitanya; *caritra* — of the deeds; *varṇanam* — description; *idam* — this; *jīyāt* — may triumph; *ajasram* — forever; *bhuvi* — on earth.

### 49

**49.**

```

शाके तथा खलु शुचौ शुभगे च मासि ।
वारे सुधाकिरणनाम्न्यसितद्वितीया-
तिथ्यन्तरे परिसमाप्तिरभूदमुष्य ॥४९॥
```
*vedā rasāḥ śrutaya indur iti prasiddhe
śāke tathā khalu śucau śubhage ca māsi |
vāre sudhā-kiraṇa-nāmny asita-dvitīyā-
tithy-antare parisamāptir abhūd amuṣya ||49||*

**Word for word:** *vedāḥ* — the Vedas (4); *rasāḥ* — the rasas (6); *śrutayaḥ* — the shrutis (4); *induḥ* — the moon (1); *iti* — thus; *prasiddhe* — known; *śāke* — in the Shaka year; *tathā* — and; *khalu* — indeed; *śucau* — in Shuchi (Ashadha); *śubhage* — auspicious; *ca* — and; *māsi* — in the month; *vāre* — on the day; *sudhā-kiraṇa* — of the Nectar-rayed (the moon); *nāmni* — bearing the name; *asita* — of the dark [fortnight]; *dvitīyā* — second; *tithi* — lunar day; *antare* — within; *parisamāptiḥ* — the completion; *abhūt* — took place; *amuṣya* — of this [work].

**Translation.** In the Shaka year known [as] "the Vedas, the rasas, the shrutis, the moon", and indeed in the auspicious month of Shuchi, on the day bearing the name of the Nectar-rayed [moon], within the second lunar day of the dark [fortnight], the completion of this [work] took place.

**Note.** Metre — *vasantatilakā*; the verse scans correctly. A chronogram (*bhūta-saṅkhyā*): the Vedas = 4, the rasas = 6, the shrutis = 4, the moon = 1; the digits are read from right to left — Shaka 1464, i.e. 1542 CE; the month of Ashadha (June–July), a Monday, the second tithi of the dark fortnight. The publisher adds: the Lord appeared in Shaka 1407 and lived forty-seven years; the poem was written nine years after His disappearance; "it is now Shaka 1905, that is, the poem is 498 years old; it has 1911 verses". The publisher's reckoning is inexact: 1407 + 47 = 1454, and from 1454 to 1464 is ten years (according to the Chaitanya-charitamrita, the Lord's disappearance was in Shaka 1455, 1533 CE); 498 = 1905 − 1407, the years since the Lord's appearance, whereas from Shaka 1464 to 1905 is 441 years ⟨?⟩.
