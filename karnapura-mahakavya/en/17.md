# Kavi Karnapura. Sri Chaitanya-charita-mahakavya
## Sarga Seventeen — verses 1–66 (printed pp. 511–531)

**Title of the sarga:** *Śrī-puruṣottama-vihāra-varṇanam upavana-vilāsaś ca*, "The Description of the Pastimes in Sri Purushottama, and the Pastimes in the Garden" (from the colophon).
**Metre:** in its second half the sarga is a "display of metres": from verse 43 almost every verse is in a metre of its own, and in 34 and 56 the name of a metre is woven into the text.
- 1–14, 16–24 — *mañjubhāṣiṇī* (*sa-ja-sa-ja-ga*: ⏑⏑—⏑—⏑⏑⏑—⏑—⏑—; 13 syllables per pada). In 5 the third pada has fourteen syllables (*tad-anusaṅgi-*; *tanu-saṅgi-* would scan); in 8 the third pada has a light syllable (*-va* of *iva*) in the ninth place (*ivāyayuḥ* would scan); in 19 the third pada, which by the metre ends with *vāsaso*, is one syllable short.
- 15 — *anuṣṭubh* (a quotation).
- 25–29 — *mandākinī* (*na-na-ra-ra*: ⏑⏑⏑⏑⏑⏑—⏑——⏑—; 12 syllables per pada).
- 30–42 — *candravartma* (*ra-na-bha-sa*: —⏑—⏑⏑⏑—⏑⏑⏑⏑—; 12 syllables per pada); in 34 the name of the metre, *candra-vartma* ("the path of the moon"), opens the verse. In 33 the fourth pada has thirteen syllables (*tāṇḍavākulita-*; *tāṇḍavākula-* would scan); in 40 the first pada has a heavy syllable (*-lī-* of *paṭalīkayā*) in the tenth place, where a light one is required; in 41 the fourth pada has a heavy syllable (*vas-* of *vastv*) in the sixth place, where a light one is required.
- 43 — *mattamayūra* (*ma-ta-ya-sa-ga*: —————⏑⏑——⏑⏑——; 13 syllables per pada). The third pada has twelve syllables (a light syllable is missing in the eleventh place, before *ramyaḥ*); the fourth has fourteen (*taṁ* is superfluous to the metre).
- 44 — *na-na-sa-sa-ga* (⏑⏑⏑⏑⏑⏑⏑⏑—⏑⏑——; 13 syllables per pada).
- 45 — *kalahaṁsa* (*sa-ja-sa-sa-ga*: ⏑⏑—⏑—⏑⏑⏑—⏑⏑——; 13 syllables per pada). The third pada has fourteen syllables (*mūrdhani*, where the metre requires *mūrdhni*).
- 46 — *bhramara-vilasita* (*ma-bha-na-la-ga*: ————⏑⏑⏑⏑⏑⏑—; 11 syllables per pada).
- 47 — *dodhaka* (*bha-bha-bha-ga-ga*: —⏑⏑—⏑⏑—⏑⏑——; 11 syllables per pada).
- 48–49 — *śālinī* (*ma-ta-ta-ga-ga*: —————⏑——⏑——; 11 syllables per pada).
- 50 — *upajāti* (padas of *indravajrā* ——⏑——⏑⏑—⏑—— and *upendravajrā* ⏑—⏑——⏑⏑—⏑——).
- 51–52 — *rathoddhatā* (*ra-na-ra-la-ga*: —⏑—⏑⏑⏑—⏑—⏑—; 11 syllables per pada).
- 53 — *vasanta-tilakā* (——⏑—⏑⏑⏑—⏑⏑—⏑——; 14 syllables per pada).
- 54 — *śaśikalā* (fourteen light syllables and one heavy: ⏑⏑⏑⏑⏑⏑⏑⏑⏑⏑⏑⏑⏑⏑—; 15 syllables per pada). The first pada has only fourteen syllables.
- 55 — a nine-syllable metre (*ma-ta-ya*: —————⏑⏑——).
- 56 — *līlākhela* (fifteen heavy syllables per pada); the name *vidyun-mālā* (a metre of eight heavy syllables) is woven into the text.
- 57–62 — *līlā* (*ma-sa-ma-bha-ga-ga*: ———⏑⏑—————⏑⏑——; 14 syllables per pada). In 57 the second pada has fifteen syllables (*cumbavad*, where the metre requires *cumbad*); in 58 the second pada has twelve (two heavy syllables are missing at the beginning); in 60 the third pada has a light syllable (*-pu-* of *kaśipunā*) in the thirteenth place, where a heavy one is required.
- 63–66 — *śārdūla-vikrīḍita* (———⏑⏑—⏑—⏑⏑⏑———⏑——⏑—; 19 syllables per pada). In 66 the printed text is faulty in the third and fourth padas; the readings of the Gosvami edition, adopted here, restore the metre.

**Total verses:** 66.

**Word-for-word conventions:** compound words are divided into their members, each with its own meaning; case is rendered by an English preposition ("of", "with", "in", "by", "to", "from"); the vocative is given with "O". Square brackets enclose a word that is understood (for example, [whose], [like]).

**Contents of the passage:** the Ratha-yatra (continued): rest in the garden, the meeting with Rupa, Sanatana and Anupama, dances in the garden, bathing, the return to the chariot.
- 1–2 — Gaura after His dance before the chariot; the images of Jagannatha along the road.
- 3–7 — the Lord enters a garden dear to Jagannatha (a *yugmaka*, 3–4); the breeze and the goddesses of the forest serve Him.
- 8–24 — **Rupa, Sanatana and Anupama** ("three oceans of rasa", 9):
  - they praise the Lord with the hymns of Brahma; concealing His feelings, He directs them to Jagannatha (12);
  - He recites the verse *na me 'bhaktaś catur-vedī* (15) and bows down to them;
  - with straws between their teeth they recite the words of the gopis;
  - the Lord has mahaprasada served from three pots called *ghasa*; the brothers withdraw.
- 25–29 — an impetuous dance in the garden; a secluded dance with Vakreshvara.
- 30–39 — the songs of Vasudeva (apparently Vasudeva Datta); the eight sattvika-bhavas; "the path of the moon" eclipsed (34).
- 40–42 — the pond; rest on its bank.
- 43–50 — the bathing:
  - Krishnadasa, a servant of Nityananda, pours water from the pond over the Lord;
  - Advaita and the Lord pour water over each other;
  - Govinda brings fresh cloth.
- 51–52 — mahaprasada with the devotees; a stroll through the garden.
- 53–58 — the return to the chariot: the Lord pushes it (53), claps His hands and holds the ropes; His companions sing of His own pastimes.
- 59–62 — sunset; the chariot halts; Jagannatha is carried into the temple over cushions (60); the Lord, with Govinda, beholds Him again.
- 63–66 — the close of the day: people grow motionless at the sight of Jagannatha and are stunned at the sight of Gaura; the Lord sports thus every year.

**Textual cruxes:** 2 (verse number ॥६॥), 8 (*prabho*), 11 (*sādhvasā*), 15 (*na me bhaktaḥ* without avagraha), 16 (*mattayā*), 23 (*ghasa*), 40 (grammar), 49 (*ratyotkaṇṭhāt*), 50 (*udgamanāya*), 53 and 60 (*kṣipyan*), 57 (*cumbavad*), 58 (a short pada), 59 (*-sthita-miśraṁ*, *tarke*), 60 (*ānaya*), 65 (*madāti*), 66 (third and fourth padas) — see the notes. Other metrical irregularities of the printed text: 5, 8, 19, 33, 40, 41, 43, 45, 54, 60 (see above).

Format and conventions as in Sarga One.

### 1

**1.**

```
नटनान्तरेऽथ घनघर्मवारिणा
विलसत्तनुर्वरविलोहितांशुकः ।
पुरतोऽसिताचलपतेर्मुहुर्बभौ
कनकाचलो रुचिरधातुनिर्झरः ॥१॥
```
*naṭanāntare 'tha ghana-gharma-vāriṇā
vilasat-tanur vara-vilohitāṁśukaḥ |
purato 'sitācala-pater muhur babhau
kanakācalo rucira-dhātu-nirjharaḥ ||1||*

**Word for word:** *naṭana* — of the dance; *antare* — at the close; *atha* — then; *ghana* — abundant; *gharma* — of perspiration; *vāriṇā* — with the water; *vilasat* — shining; *tanuḥ* — [whose] body [was]; *vara* — beautiful; *vilohita* — crimson; *aṁśukaḥ* — [whose] cloth [was]; *purataḥ* — before; *asita* — of the Dark; *acala* — Mountain; *pateḥ* — the Lord; *muhuḥ* — again and again; *babhau* — shone; *kanaka* — golden; *acalaḥ* — a mountain; *rucira* — with lovely; *dhātu* — of minerals; *nirjharaḥ* — [with] streams.

**Translation.** Then, at the close of the dance, His body shining with the water of abundant perspiration, wearing a beautiful crimson cloth, He shone again and again before the Lord of the Dark Mountain like a golden mountain with lovely streams of [red] minerals.

**Note.** Metre — *mañjubhāṣiṇī* (*sa-ja-sa-ja-ga*: ⏑⏑—⏑—⏑⏑⏑—⏑—⏑—); verses 1–24, except 15. The crimson cloth answers to the streams of red ore, the perspiration to the mountain torrents.

### 2

**2.**

```
अभितोऽभितः पथि रथान्तरान्तरे
प्रतिमास्तथास्य जगतांपतेर्मुहुः ।
अवलोक्य तेन कनकाद्रिकान्तिना
किमिवेशितृत्वमिह ताभ्य आदधे ॥२॥
```
*abhito 'bhitaḥ pathi rathāntarāntare
pratimās tathāsya jagatāṁ-pater muhuḥ |
avalokya tena kanakādri-kāntinā
kim iveśitṛtvam iha tābhya ādadhe ||2||*

**Word for word:** *abhitaḥ abhitaḥ* — on both sides; *pathi* — on the road; *ratha* — of the chariots; *antara-antare* — in the spaces between; *pratimāḥ* — the images; *tathā* — so; *asya* — of this; *jagatām-pateḥ* — Lord of the worlds; *muhuḥ* — again and again; *avalokya* — having looked upon; *tena* — by Him; *kanaka-adri* — of a golden mountain; *kāntinā* — [whose] lustre [is that]; *kim* — whether; *iva* — as it were; *īśitṛtvam* — lordship; *iha* — here; *tābhyaḥ* — on them; *ādadhe* — was conferred.

**Translation.** Again and again looking upon the images of this Lord of the worlds on both sides of the road, in the spaces between the chariots — did He, whose lustre is that of a golden mountain, thereby confer lordship on them here, as it were?

**Note.** In the edition this verse is numbered ॥६॥, a misprint for ॥२॥. Apparently the images of Jagannatha along the road are meant. The Gosvami edition (GGM) has the same text.

### 3–4

**3.**

```
सुचिरं विलस्य पुरतो रथस्य स
प्रविवेश शीतलतलद्रुमावहम् ।
असिताद्रिमौलितिलकस्य वल्लभं
श्रमशान्तये ह्युपवनं मनोरमम् ॥३॥
```
*suciraṁ vilasya purato rathasya sa
praviveśa śītala-tala-drumāvaham |
asitādri-mauli-tilakasya vallabhaṁ
śrama-śāntaye hy upavanaṁ manoramam ||3||*

**Word for word:** *suciram* — for a very long time; *vilasya* — having sported; *purataḥ* — before; *rathasya* — the chariot; *saḥ* — He; *praviveśa* — entered; *śītala* — cool; *tala* — [with] the ground beneath (i.e. with shade); *druma* — trees; *āvaham* — bearing; *asita-adri* — of the Dark Mountain; *mauli* — of the crown; *tilakasya* — of the tilaka (ornament); *vallabham* — beloved; *śrama* — of fatigue; *śāntaye* — for the relief; *hi* — indeed; *upavanam* — a garden; *manoramam* — charming.

**4.**

```
नवजातिकुन्दकरवीरयूथिका-
नवमालिकाललितमाधवीचयैः ।
वकुलै रसालशिशुभिश्च चम्पकैः
परितः समावृतममन्दविभ्रमम् ॥४॥ (युग्मकम्)
```
*nava-jāti-kunda-karavīra-yūthikā-
navamālikā-lalita-mādhavī-cayaiḥ |
vakulai rasāla-śiśubhiś ca campakaiḥ
paritaḥ samāvṛtam amanda-vibhramam ||4|| (yugmakam)*

**Word for word:** *nava* — fresh; *jāti* — of jati; *kunda* — of kunda; *karavīra* — of oleander; *yūthikā* — of yuthika; *navamālikā* — of navamallika; *lalita* — of lovely; *mādhavī* — madhavi; *cayaiḥ* — by clusters; *vakulaiḥ* — by bakulas; *rasāla* — mango; *śiśubhiḥ* — by young [trees]; *ca* — and; *campakaiḥ* — by champakas; *paritaḥ* — on all sides; *samāvṛtam* — surrounded; *amanda* — no small; *vibhramam* — [having] charm.

**Translation (3–4).** Having sported for a very long time before the chariot, He, to relieve His fatigue, entered a charming garden, beloved of the Tilaka on the crown of the Dark Mountain, with trees that gave cool shade — a garden of no small charm, surrounded on all sides by clusters of fresh jati, kunda, oleander, yuthika, navamallika and lovely madhavi, by bakulas, young mango trees and champakas.

**Note.** A *yugmaka* (so marked in the edition). *Jāti*, *yūthikā*, *navamālikā*, *mādhavī* are various kinds of jasmine and flowering creepers; *karavīra* is the oleander; *bakula* — *Mimusops elengi*. On the garden where the Lord rests during the Ratha-yatra, cf. Chaitanya-charitamrita, Madhya 13.194–204: at Balagandi, where food is offered to Jagannatha, the Lord enters a beautiful garden and lies down there to rest.

### 5

**5.**

```
परितः प्रसूनभरमाश्लिषंस्तथा
सरसां वहन् सरसशीकरोत्करम् ।
तदनुसङ्गिघर्मकणिकाः समाहर-
न्नभजत् प्रभुं लघु लघु क्षणं मरुत् ॥५॥
```
*paritaḥ prasūna-bharam āśliṣaṁs tathā
sarasāṁ vahan sarasa-śīkarotkaram |
tad-anusaṅgi-gharma-kaṇikāḥ samāharann
abhajat prabhuṁ laghu laghu kṣaṇaṁ marut ||5||*

**Word for word:** *paritaḥ* — all around; *prasūna* — of flowers; *bharam* — the abundance; *āśliṣan* — embracing; *tathā* — likewise; *sarasām* — of the lakes; *vahan* — carrying; *sarasa* — fresh; *śīkara* — of spray; *utkaram* — a multitude; *tat* — to Him; *anusaṅgi* — clinging; *gharma* — of perspiration; *kaṇikāḥ* — the drops; *samāharan* — gathering up; *abhajat* — served; *prabhum* — the Lord; *laghu laghu* — very gently; *kṣaṇam* — for a while; *marut* — the breeze.

**Translation.** The breeze, embracing the abundance of flowers all around and carrying a multitude of fresh spray from the lakes, gathering up the drops of perspiration that clung to Him, served the Lord very gently for a while.

**Note.** The breeze has the three classical virtues: it is fragrant (with the flowers), cool (with the spray) and gentle. The third pada has fourteen syllables instead of thirteen: *tad-anusaṅgi-* gives ⏑⏑⏑—⏑ where the metre requires ⏑⏑—⏑—; the reading *tanu-saṅgi-* ("clinging to the body") would scan correctly ⟨?⟩. The Sanskrit is given as printed.

### 6

**6.**

```
वनदेवताभिरनिशं मनोरमै-
र्नवपल्लवैर्नवशिरीषचामरैः ।
लघुवीज्यमानतनुरुत्सुकात्मभिः
सदृशं बभौ विहितगौरविग्रहः ॥६॥
```
*vana-devatābhir aniśaṁ manoramair
nava-pallavair nava-śirīṣa-cāmaraiḥ |
laghu vījyamāna-tanur utsukātmabhiḥ
sadṛśaṁ babhau vihita-gaura-vigrahaḥ ||6||*

**Word for word:** *vana* — of the forest; *devatābhiḥ* — by the goddesses; *aniśam* — unceasingly; *manoramaiḥ* — with charming; *nava* — fresh; *pallavaiḥ* — with shoots; *nava* — of fresh; *śirīṣa* — shirisha flowers; *cāmaraiḥ* — with chamaras; *laghu* — gently; *vījyamāna* — being fanned; *tanuḥ* — [whose] body [was]; *utsuka* — eager; *ātmabhiḥ* — [whose] hearts [were]; *sadṛśam* — befittingly; *babhau* — shone; *vihita* — who had assumed; *gaura* — a golden; *vigrahaḥ* — form.

**Translation.** He who had assumed a golden form, His body gently and unceasingly fanned by the eager-hearted goddesses of the forest with charming fresh shoots and with chamaras of fresh shirisha flowers, shone as befitted [Him].

**Note.** The shirisha (*Albizia lebbeck*) has fluffy flowers resembling a chamara, the yak-tail whisk.

### 7

**7.**

```
मधुरोल्लसद्वदनदीधितिच्छटा-
मृतधारया स्नपयतीव किं जगत् ।
त्रिविधैश्च तापतपनैर्दुरासदै-
र्नहि बाध्यतामिति स गौरचन्द्रमाः ॥७॥
```
*madhurollasad-vadana-dīdhiti-cchaṭā-
mṛta-dhārayā snapayatīva kiṁ jagat |
trividhaiś ca tāpa-tapanair durāsadair
nahi bādhyatām iti sa gauracandramāḥ ||7||*

**Word for word:** *madhura* — sweetly; *ullasat* — radiant; *vadana* — of the face; *dīdhiti* — of the rays; *chaṭā* — of the streams; *amṛta* — of the nectar; *dhārayā* — with a current; *snapayati* — bathes; *iva* — as it were; *kim* — does [He] not; *jagat* — the world; *trividhaiḥ* — by the threefold; *ca* — and; *tāpa* — of miseries; *tapanaiḥ* — by the scorching heats; *durāsadaiḥ* — unbearable; *na hi* — not; *bādhyatām* — may be oppressed; *iti* — so that; *saḥ* — He; *gaura-candramāḥ* — Gaurachandra (the golden moon).

**Translation.** Is not Gaurachandra, as it were, bathing the world with a current of the nectar of the streams of rays from His sweetly radiant face, so that it may not be oppressed by the unbearable scorching heats of the threefold miseries?

**Note.** The threefold miseries are those arising from one's own body and mind (*ādhyātmika*), from the higher powers (*ādhidaivika*) and from other beings (*ādhibhautika*).

### 8

**8.**

```
अथ केचनास्य जगतां पतेः प्रियाः
परमप्रभावभरभूरिभूषिताः ।
रससारसिन्धव इव ययुः प्रभो-
पदपङ्कजद्वयमवेक्षितुं तदा ॥८॥
```
*atha kecanāsya jagatāṁ pateḥ priyāḥ
parama-prabhāva-bhara-bhūri-bhūṣitāḥ |
rasa-sāra-sindhava iva yayuḥ prabhoḥ
pada-paṅkaja-dvayam avekṣituṁ tadā ||8||*

**Word for word:** *atha* — then; *kecana* — certain; *asya* — of this; *jagatām pateḥ* — Lord of the worlds; *priyāḥ* — beloved ones; *parama* — of supreme; *prabhāva* — power; *bhara* — with the fullness; *bhūri* — richly; *bhūṣitāḥ* — adorned; *rasa* — of rasa; *sāra* — of the essence; *sindhavaḥ* — oceans; *iva* — like; *yayuḥ* — came; *prabhoḥ* — of the Lord; *pada* — of the feet; *paṅkaja* — of the lotuses; *dvayam* — the pair; *avekṣitum* — to behold; *tadā* — then.

**Translation.** Then certain beloved ones of this Lord of the worlds, richly adorned with the fullness of supreme power, came like oceans of the essence of rasa to behold the pair of the Lord's lotus feet.

**Note.** "The Lord of the worlds" may also be referred to Jagannatha ("certain dear devotees of Jagannatha"). In the edition *prabho* at the end of the third pada lacks the visarga; we read *prabhoḥ*. In the third pada the ninth syllable (*-va* of *iva*) is light where the metre requires a heavy one; the reading *ivāyayuḥ* (*iva āyayuḥ*, "came") would scan correctly ⟨?⟩.

### 9

**9.**

```
ससनातनानुपमरूपरूपिणः
स्वपदाब्जभक्तिरससागरत्रयान् ।
प्रददर्श विस्फुरितभाववीचिभि-
र्जगदाप्लुतं विदधतः कृपानिधिः ॥९॥
```
*sa-sanātanānupama-rūpa-rūpiṇaḥ
sva-padābja-bhakti-rasa-sāgara-trayān |
pradadarśa visphurita-bhāva-vīcibhir
jagad āplutaṁ vidadhataḥ kṛpānidhiḥ ||9||*

**Word for word:** *sa-sanātana* — with Sanatana; *anupama* — of Anupama; *rūpa* — [and] of Rupa; *rūpiṇaḥ* — having the forms (also: "of incomparable beauty"); *sva* — to His own; *pada* — feet; *abja* — lotus; *bhakti* — of devotion; *rasa* — of the rasa; *sāgara* — of oceans; *trayān* — the three; *pradadarśa* — saw; *visphurita* — with sparkling; *bhāva* — of feeling; *vīcibhiḥ* — with the waves; *jagat* — the world; *āplutam* — flooded; *vidadhataḥ* — making; *kṛpā-nidhiḥ* — the treasure-house of mercy.

**Translation.** The treasure-house of mercy saw the three oceans of the rasa of devotion to His own lotus feet, which had taken the forms of Sanatana, Anupama and Rupa and were flooding the world with the sparkling waves of their feeling.

**Note.** A play on words: *sa-sanātanānupama-rūpa-rūpiṇaḥ* may also be read "possessed of an eternal (*sanātana*), incomparable (*anupama*) beauty (*rūpa*)". According to the Chaitanya-charitamrita the brothers came to Puri separately: Anupama (Vallabha) passed away suddenly in Bengal, and Rupa, delayed, came to Puri alone (Antya 1.37–39), while Sanatana came later (Antya 4). In Karnapura all three come together.

### 10

**10.**

```
अथ ते समेत्य निकटं महाप्रभो-
रनुभावसोदरतमा इव त्रयः ।
प्रियसोदरा विहितकाकुभाषितं
भृशमस्तुवन् जलजजन्मनस्तवैः ॥१०॥
```
*atha te sametya nikaṭaṁ mahāprabhor
anubhāva-sodaratamā iva trayaḥ |
priya-sodarā vihita-kāku-bhāṣitaṁ
bhṛśam astuvan jalaja-janmana(ḥ) stavaiḥ ||10||*

**Word for word:** *atha* — then; *te* — they; *sametya* — having approached; *nikaṭam* — near; *mahāprabhoḥ* — to Mahaprabhu; *anubhāva* — to [His] ecstatic manifestations; *sodaratamāḥ* — most closely akin; *iva* — as if; *trayaḥ* — the three; *priya* — dear; *sodarāḥ* — brothers; *vihita* — made; *kāku* — plaintive; *bhāṣitam* — [with] speech; *bhṛśam* — fervently; *astuvan* — praised; *jalaja* — the Lotus-; *janmanaḥ* — born; *stavaiḥ* — with the hymns.

**Translation.** Then those three dear brothers, as if most closely akin to His ecstatic manifestations, came near to Mahaprabhu and, speaking plaintively, fervently praised Him with the hymns of the Lotus-born.

**Note.** The hymns of the Lotus-born are the prayers of Brahma (Srimad-Bhagavatam 10.14). *Anubhāva* — the outward signs of ecstatic feeling; *anubhāva-sodaratamāḥ*, literally "most closely related to [His] anubhāvas", seems to mean that the brothers share His ecstasy ⟨?⟩.

### 11

**11.**

```
अथ भूयशो गलितनेत्रवारिभिः
पुलकोत्करैर्मृदुतया च चेतसः ।
विवशा महाप्रभुसमीपमास्थिताः
स्तवनं प्रचक्रुरथ वीतसाध्वसा(ः) ॥११॥
```
*atha bhūyaśo galita-netra-vāribhiḥ
pulakotkarair mṛdutayā ca cetasaḥ |
vivaśā mahāprabhu-samīpam āsthitāḥ
stavanaṁ pracakrur atha vīta-sādhvasāḥ ||11||*

**Word for word:** *atha* — then; *bhūyaśaḥ* — again; *galita* — streaming; *netra* — of the eyes; *vāribhiḥ* — by the water; *pulaka* — of hairs standing on end; *utkaraiḥ* — by the multitude; *mṛdutayā* — by the softness; *ca* — and; *cetasaḥ* — of heart; *vivaśāḥ* — overpowered; *mahāprabhu* — Mahaprabhu; *samīpam* — near; *āsthitāḥ* — standing; *stavanam* — praise; *pracakruḥ* — offered; *atha* — then; *vīta* — cast off; *sādhvasāḥ* — [whose] fear [was].

**Translation.** Then again, overpowered by the tears streaming from their eyes, by the multitude of their hairs standing on end and by the softness of their hearts, standing near Mahaprabhu, they cast off their fear and offered praise.

**Note.** In the edition the final visarga of *sādhvasāḥ* is missing; it is restored in brackets.

---

### 12

**12.**

```
स निशम्य तत्तदवहित्थया प्रभु-
र्निजगाद भूयश इदं कृपानिधिः ।
अयमेष नीलगिरिमौलिचन्द्रमाः
पुरतः समेत्य कुरुत स्तवं न किम् ॥१२॥
```
*sa niśamya tat tad avahitthayā prabhur
nijagāda bhūyaśa idaṁ kṛpānidhiḥ |
ayam eṣa nīla-giri-mauli-candramāḥ
purataḥ sametya kuruta stavaṁ na kim ||12||*

**Word for word:** *saḥ* — He; *niśamya* — having heard; *tat tat* — all this; *avahitthayā* — concealing His feelings; *prabhuḥ* — the Lord; *nijagāda* — said; *bhūyaśaḥ* — again; *idam* — this; *kṛpā-nidhiḥ* — the treasure-house of mercy; *ayam eṣaḥ* — here is; *nīla-giri* — of the Blue Mountain; *mauli* — on the crown; *candramāḥ* — the Moon; *purataḥ* — in front; *sametya* — having approached; *kuruta* — offer (pl.); *stavam* — praise; *na kim* — why not.

**Translation.** Having heard all this, the Lord, the treasure-house of mercy, concealing His feelings, again said this: "Here before you is the Moon on the crown of the Blue Mountain. Why do you not go up to Him and offer your praise?"

**Note.** *Avahitthā* — the concealment of one's feelings, one of the *vyabhicāri-bhāvas*. *Kuruta* is an imperative plural ("offer!"): the Lord redirects the praise to Jagannatha.

### 13

**13.**

```
निविडानुरागपटलीबलत्तर-
द्रढिमान एत इति यान्तु वा कथम् ।
श्लथतां ततोऽधिकमभिप्रयत्नतः
स्तवनं प्रचक्रुरपि वीतसाध्वसाः ॥१३॥
```
*nibiḍānurāga-paṭalī-balat-tara-
dradhimāna eta iti yāntu vā katham |
ślathatāṁ tato 'dhikam abhiprayatnataḥ
stavanaṁ pracakrur api vīta-sādhvasāḥ ||13||*

**Word for word:** *nibiḍa* — of dense; *anurāga* — attachment; *paṭalī* — by the layers; *balat-tara* — ever-strengthening; *draḍhimānaḥ* — [whose] firmness [was]; *ete* — they; *iti* — therefore; *yāntu* — could go; *vā katham* — how indeed; *ślathatām* — to slackness; *tataḥ* — therefore; *adhikam* — still more; *abhiprayatnataḥ* — with effort; *stavanam* — praise; *pracakruḥ* — offered; *api* — and; *vīta* — cast off; *sādhvasāḥ* — [whose] fear [was].

**Translation.** Since their firmness grew ever stronger through the layers of dense attachment, how could they slacken? Therefore, casting off fear, they offered praise with still greater effort.

### 14

**14.**

```
विविधप्रकारमपनीय साहसं
न शशाक वारयितुमेष तान् यदा ।
अतिहर्षवारिनिधिपूरसञ्चयै-
रवगाहिता विदधिरे तदैव ते ॥१४॥
```
*vividha-prakāram apanīya sāhasaṁ
na śaśāka vārayitum eṣa tān yadā |
ati-harṣa-vāri-nidhi-pūra-sañcayair
avagāhitā vidadhire tadaiva te ||14||*

**Word for word:** *vividha* — in various; *prakāram* — ways; *apanīya* — turning aside; *sāhasam* — [their] boldness; *na śaśāka* — could not; *vārayitum* — restrain; *eṣaḥ* — He; *tān* — them; *yadā* — when; *ati* — of excessive; *harṣa* — joy; *vāri-nidhi* — of the ocean; *pūra* — of the floods; *sañcayaiḥ* — by the masses; *avagāhitāḥ* — immersed; *vidadhire* — were made; *tadā eva* — then indeed; *te* — they.

**Translation.** When He, trying in various ways to turn aside their boldness, could not restrain them, then they were plunged in the massed floods of an ocean of excessive joy.

**Note.** *Avagāhitā vidadhire* may also be understood as "[He] made them plunge".

---

### 15

**15.**

```
न मे भक्तश्चतुर्वेदी मद्भक्तः श्वपचः प्रियः ।
तस्मै देयं ततो ग्राह्यं स च पूज्यो यथा ह्यहम् ॥१५॥
```
*na me ('bhaktaś) bhaktaś catur-vedī mad-bhaktaḥ śvapacaḥ priyaḥ |
tasmai deyaṁ tato grāhyaṁ sa ca pūjyo yathā hy aham ||15||*

**Word for word:** *na* — not; *me* — to Me; *abhaktaḥ* — one without devotion; *catuḥ-vedī* — a knower of the four Vedas; *mat-bhaktaḥ* — My devotee; *śva-pacaḥ* — a dog-eater; *priyaḥ* — dear; *tasmai* — to him; *deyam* — [one] should give; *tataḥ* — from him; *grāhyam* — [one] should accept; *saḥ* — he; *ca* — and; *pūjyaḥ* — is to be worshipped; *yathā* — as; *hi* — indeed; *aham* — I.

**Translation.** "A knower of the four Vedas without devotion is not dear to Me; My devotee, though a dog-eater, is dear. To him one should give, from him one should accept; he is to be worshipped as I am."

**Note.** Metre — *anuṣṭubh*. A well-known verse (quoted in the Chaitanya-charitamrita, Madhya 19.50, and in the Hari-bhakti-vilasa with reference to the Itihasa-samuchchaya). The edition prints नमे भक्तः without the avagraha; the standard reading is *na me 'bhaktaś catur-vedī*. Read as printed, the first quarter would mean "a knower of the four Vedas is not My devotee". The Lord recites this verse to the brothers, who considered themselves low because of their service at the Muslim court.

---

### 16

**16.**

```
इति संनिपठ्य मधुरं महाप्रभुः
प्रणनाम भूमिषु निपत्य दण्डवत् ।
तदतिप्रगल्भमनसो न ते ततो
भयमाययुः प्रबलभक्तिमत्तया ॥१६॥
```
*iti saṁnipaṭhya madhuraṁ mahāprabhuḥ
praṇanāma bhūmiṣu nipatya daṇḍavat |
tad ati-pragalbha-manaso na te tato
bhayam āyayuḥ prabala-bhakti-mattayā ||16||*

**Word for word:** *iti* — thus; *saṁnipaṭhya* — having recited; *madhuram* — sweetly; *mahāprabhuḥ* — Mahaprabhu; *praṇanāma* — bowed down; *bhūmiṣu* — on the ground; *nipatya* — having fallen; *daṇḍa-vat* — like a rod; *tat* — thereupon; *ati* — very; *pragalbha* — bold; *manasaḥ* — [whose] hearts [were]; *na* — not; *te* — they; *tataḥ* — from that; *bhayam* — fear; *āyayuḥ* — came to; *prabala* — of mighty; *bhakti* — devotion; *mattayā* — by the intoxication.

**Translation.** Having thus sweetly recited [this verse], Mahaprabhu fell to the ground like a rod and bowed down [to them]. Thereupon they, their hearts made very bold by the intoxication of mighty devotion, were not frightened by this.

**Note.** *Mattayā* stands for the expected *mattatayā* (the metre does not allow the extra syllable).

### 17

**17.**

```
मधुरोल्लसद्वद वदेति भूयशो
वचनं यदाविरभवन्महाप्रभोः ।
ददृशुस्तदाभिमतरूपमुत्तमं
शतचन्द्रसान्द्रकिरणप्रकाशवत् ॥१७॥
```
*madhurollasad vada vadeti bhūyaśo
vacanaṁ yadāvirabhavan mahāprabhoḥ |
dadṛśus tadābhimata-rūpam uttamaṁ
śata-candra-sāndra-kiraṇa-prakāśavat ||17||*

**Word for word:** *madhura* — sweetly; *ullasat* — radiant; *vada vada* — "Speak, speak!"; *iti* — thus; *bhūyaśaḥ* — again and again; *vacanam* — the word; *yadā* — when; *āvirabhavat* — came forth; *mahāprabhoḥ* — from Mahaprabhu; *dadṛśuḥ* — [they] saw; *tadā* — then; *abhimata* — the desired; *rūpam* — form; *uttamam* — supreme; *śata* — of a hundred; *candra* — moons; *sāndra* — of the dense; *kiraṇa* — rays; *prakāśa-vat* — resembling the radiance.

**Translation.** When the sweetly radiant words "Speak, speak!" came forth again and again from Mahaprabhu, they then saw His supreme form, the form they desired, resembling the radiance of the dense rays of a hundred moons.

### 18

**18.**

```
स तु गौरचन्द्र इति निर्भरोत्सुको
द्विगुणप्रकाशमधुमाधुरीमयः ।
अवदन्मुहुर्वद वदेति निर्भरं
स्मितदीधितिस्नपितभूमिमण्डलः ॥१८॥
```
*sa tu gauracandra iti nirbharotsuko
dviguṇa-prakāśa-madhu-mādhurī-mayaḥ |
avadan muhur vada vadeti nirbharaṁ
smita-dīdhiti-snapita-bhūmi-maṇḍalaḥ ||18||*

**Word for word:** *saḥ tu* — and He; *gauracandraḥ* — Gaurachandra; *iti* — thus; *nirbhara* — intensely; *utsukaḥ* — eager; *dviguṇa* — of doubled; *prakāśa* — radiance; *madhu* — of the honeyed; *mādhurī* — sweetness; *mayaḥ* — full; *avadat* — said; *muhuḥ* — again and again; *vada vada* — "Speak, speak!"; *iti* — thus; *nirbharam* — fervently; *smita* — of [His] smile; *dīdhiti* — with the rays; *snapita* — bathing; *bhūmi* — of the earth; *maṇḍalaḥ* — [by whom] the circle [was].

**Translation.** And Gaurachandra, intensely eager, full of the honeyed sweetness of a doubled radiance, again and again fervently said "Speak, speak!", bathing the circle of the earth with the rays of His smile.

### 19

**19.**

```
अथ ते विहाय जलजोद्भवस्तवं
तृणसञ्चयं च परिगृह्य दन्तकैः ।
अधिकण्ठमभिनिबध्य वाससोऽञ्चल-
मुत्सुका विदधिरेतरां स्तुतिम् ॥१९॥
```
*atha te vihāya jalajodbhava-stavaṁ
tṛṇa-sañcayaṁ ca parigṛhya dantakaiḥ |
adhikaṇṭham abhinibadhya vāsaso 'ñcalam
utsukā vidadhiretarāṁ stutim ||19||*

**Word for word:** *atha* — then; *te* — they; *vihāya* — having abandoned; *jalaja-udbhava* — of the Lotus-born; *stavam* — the hymn; *tṛṇa* — of straws; *sañcayam* — a bunch; *ca* — and; *parigṛhya* — having taken; *dantakaiḥ* — with [their] teeth; *adhikaṇṭham* — about the neck; *abhinibadhya* — having tied; *vāsasaḥ* — of the cloth; *añcalam* — the edge; *utsukāḥ* — full of longing; *vidadhiretarām* — offered still more [fervently]; *stutim* — praise.

**Translation.** Then, abandoning the hymn of the Lotus-born, taking a bunch of straws between their teeth and tying the edge of their cloth about their necks, they, full of longing, offered praise still more fervently.

**Note.** A straw between the teeth and the cloth about the neck are gestures of utmost humility; cf. Rupa and Sanatana before the Lord at Ramakeli, "holding straws between their teeth and binding their cloth about their necks" (Chaitanya-charitamrita, Madhya 1.185). *Vidadhiretarām* is a verb with the intensive suffix *-tarām*. Metrically the third pada ends with *vāsaso*: *'ñcalam utsukā vidadhiretarāṁ stutim* forms a regular fourth pada. The third pada then has only twelve syllables, and its opening *adhikaṇṭham abhi-* (⏑⏑—⏑⏑⏑) does not fit the metre (⏑⏑—⏑—⏑⏑): a syllable seems to have dropped out ⟨?⟩. The Sanskrit is given as printed.

### 20

**20.**

```
स्वमनोऽनुकूलमभिवाञ्छितप्रदं
विनिपठ्य गोपरमणीजनोदितम् ।
विदधुः स्तवं नयननीरभूषिताः
सुखसागरे परिममज्जुरप्यमी ॥२०॥
```
*sva-mano-'nukūlam abhivāñchita-pradaṁ
vinipaṭhya gopa-ramaṇī-janoditam |
vidadhuḥ stavaṁ nayana-nīra-bhūṣitāḥ
sukha-sāgare parimamajjur apy amī ||20||*

**Word for word:** *sva* — to their own; *manaḥ* — hearts; *anukūlam* — agreeable; *abhivāñchita* — what is desired; *pradam* — bestowing; *vinipaṭhya* — having recited; *gopa* — cowherd; *ramaṇī* — of beautiful women; *jana* — by the folk; *uditam* — what was spoken; *vidadhuḥ* — offered; *stavam* — praise; *nayana* — of the eyes; *nīra* — with the water; *bhūṣitāḥ* — adorned; *sukha* — of happiness; *sāgare* — in the ocean; *parimamajjuḥ* — were immersed; *api* — too; *amī* — they.

**Translation.** Having recited what had been spoken by the beautiful cowherd women — words agreeable to their own hearts and bestowing what is desired — they, adorned with the water of their eyes, offered praise; and they too were immersed in an ocean of happiness.

**Note.** Apparently the Gopi-gita (Srimad-Bhagavatam 10.31) or other prayers of the gopis ⟨?⟩. The Gosvami edition (GGM) has the same text.

### 21

**21.**

```
इति निर्भरं परमकाकुभाषितै-
र्मधुरं सुधामयमिवाकलय्य सः ।
भृशमानयानय विधीयतां द्रुतं
सुमहाप्रसाद इति सस्पृहोऽभवत् ॥२१॥
```
*iti nirbharaṁ parama-kāku-bhāṣitair
madhuraṁ sudhāmayam ivākalayya saḥ |
bhṛśam ānayānaya vidhīyatāṁ drutaṁ
sumahāprasāda iti saspṛho 'bhavat ||21||*

**Word for word:** *iti* — thus; *nirbharam* — fully; *parama* — most; *kāku* — plaintive; *bhāṣitaiḥ* — by the speeches; *madhuram* — sweet; *sudhā-mayam* — made of nectar; *iva* — as if; *ākalayya* — having perceived; *saḥ* — He; *bhṛśam* — intensely; *ānaya ānaya* — "Bring, bring!"; *vidhīyatām* — let [it] be arranged; *drutam* — quickly; *su-mahā-prasādaḥ* — the great mahaprasada; *iti* — thus; *sa-spṛhaḥ* — eager; *abhavat* — became.

**Translation.** Having thus fully taken in their most plaintive speeches as something sweet, as if made of nectar, He became intensely eager: "Bring it, bring it! Let the great mahaprasada be served quickly!"

### 22

**22.**

```
अथ ते पदाम्बुजयुगस्य सन्निधौ
क्षितिमूलमध्यतिशयप्रवेपिताः ।
निपतन्त एव नयनाम्बुनिर्झरैः

```
*atha te padāmbuja-yugasya sannidhau

nipatanta eva nayanāmbu-nirjharaiḥ
paridhauta-sarva-tanavaḥ samāsata ||22||*

**Word for word:** *atha* — then; *te* — they; *pada* — of the feet; *ambuja* — lotus; *yugasya* — of the pair; *sannidhau* — near; *kṣiti* — of the earth; *mūlam adhi* — on the surface; *atiśaya* — violently; *pravepitāḥ* — trembling; *nipatantaḥ eva* — all but collapsing; *nayana* — of the eyes; *ambu* — of the water; *nirjharaiḥ* — by the torrents; *paridhauta* — washed; *sarva* — whole; *tanavaḥ* — [whose] bodies [were]; *samāsata* — sat down.

**Translation.** Then they, trembling violently and all but collapsing, their whole bodies washed by torrents of tears, sat down on the ground near the pair of His lotus feet.

### 23

**23.**

```
अथ संप्रसादितमहाप्रसादको
ललितैर्घसाभिधघटैस्त्रिभिस्ततः ।
मधुरोल्लसद्वदनचन्द्रसुन्दरो
```

lalitair ghasābhidha-ghaṭais tribhis tataḥ |
madhurollasad-vadana-candra-sundaro
ruruce vibhur nija-jana-priyaṅkaraḥ ||23||*

**Word for word:** *atha* — then; *saṁprasādita* — who had graciously distributed; *mahā-prasādakaḥ* — mahaprasada; *lalitaiḥ* — from lovely; *ghasa* — ghasa; *abhidha* — called; *ghaṭaiḥ* — from pots; *tribhiḥ* — from three; *tataḥ* — then; *madhura* — sweetly; *ullasat* — radiant; *vadana* — of the face; *candra* — with the moon; *sundaraḥ* — beautiful; *ruruce* — shone; *vibhuḥ* — the almighty Lord; *nija* — to His own; *jana* — people; *priyaṅkaraḥ* — giving joy.

**Translation.** Then the almighty Lord, who gives joy to His own people, having graciously distributed mahaprasada from three lovely pots called *ghasa*, shone, beautiful with the moon of His sweetly radiant face.

**Note.** *Ghasa* is apparently a local (Oriya) name for an earthen pot of temple food ⟨?⟩. The Gosvami edition (GGM) has the same text.

### 24

**24.**

```
अथ तेऽपि निर्वृतहृदो मनोरथा-
नुमतप्रकाशरुचिदर्शनोत्सुकाः ।
विगलद्विलोचनझराप्लुताङ्गका-
श्चलिता बभूवुरतिभाग्यराशयः ॥२४॥
```
*atha te 'pi nirvṛta-hṛdo manorathā-
numata-prakāśa-ruci-darśanotsukāḥ |
vigalad-vilocana-jharāplutāṅgakāś
calitā babhūvur ati-bhāgya-rāśayaḥ ||24||*

**Word for word:** *atha* — then; *te api* — they too; *nirvṛta* — at peace; *hṛdaḥ* — [whose] hearts [were]; *manoratha* — with [their] heart's desire; *anumata* — in accord; *prakāśa* — of the manifestation; *ruci* — the splendour; *darśana* — to behold; *utsukāḥ* — eager; *vigalat* — flowing; *vilocana* — from the eyes; *jhara* — by the streams; *āpluta* — bathed; *aṅgakāḥ* — [whose] bodies [were]; *calitāḥ* — departed; *babhūvuḥ* — became; *ati* — of great; *bhāgya* — good fortune; *rāśayaḥ* — treasuries.

**Translation.** Then they too — treasuries of great good fortune — their hearts at peace, eager to behold the splendour of that manifestation [of the Lord] which accorded with their heart's desire, their bodies bathed in the streams flowing from their eyes, departed.

**Note.** The last verse in *mañjubhāṣiṇī*.

### 25

**25.**

```
उपवनमधि हर्षवारांनिधि-
र्नटनरभसलोलचित्तस्तदा ।
अथ मधुमधुरं चकारोद्भटं
नटनमभिरसं समं तद्विधैः ॥२५॥
```
*upavanam adhi harṣa-vārāṁnidhir
naṭana-rabhasa-lola-cittas tadā |
atha madhu-madhuraṁ cakāroddhaṭaṁ
naṭanam abhirasaṁ samaṁ tad-vidhaiḥ ||25||*

**Word for word:** *upavanam adhi* — in the garden; *harṣa* — of joy; *vārāṁnidhiḥ* — the ocean; *naṭana* — to dance; *rabhasa* — by the impulse; *lola* — stirred; *cittaḥ* — [whose] heart [was]; *tadā* — then; *atha* — and so; *madhu-madhuram* — honey-sweet; *cakāra* — performed; *udbhaṭam* — impetuous; *naṭanam* — a dance; *abhirasam* — full of rasa; *samam* — together; *tat-vidhaiḥ* — with those like Him.

**Translation.** Then in the garden the Ocean of joy, His heart stirred by an impulse to dance, performed a honey-sweet, impetuous dance full of rasa together with those like Him.

**Note.** Metre of verses 25–29 — *mandākinī* (*na-na-ra-ra*: ⏑⏑⏑⏑⏑⏑—⏑——⏑—).

### 26

**26.**

```
सरभसमपि तत्र वक्रेश्वर-
द्विजकुलशशिना समं प्रेमवान् ।
मधुमधुररुचिच्छटासुन्दरः
सततमिह ततान लीलायितम् ॥२६॥
```
*sarabhasam api tatra vakreśvara-
dvija-kula-śaśinā samaṁ premavān |
madhu-madhura-ruci-cchaṭā-sundaraḥ

**Word for word:** *sa-rabhasam* — ardently; *api* — and; *tatra* — there; *vakreśvara* — Vakreshvara; *dvija* — of the twice-born; *kula* — of the race; *śaśinā* — with the moon; *samam* — together with; *premavān* — full of love; *madhu-madhura* — of honey-sweet; *ruci* — radiance; *chaṭā* — with streams; *sundaraḥ* — beautiful; *satatam* — unceasingly; *iha* — here; *tatāna* — unfolded; *līlāyitam* — play.

**Translation.** And there, full of love, beautiful with streams of honey-sweet radiance, He ardently and unceasingly unfolded His play here together with Vakreshvara, the moon of the race of the twice-born.

**Note.** Vakreshvara Pandita, an associate of the Lord, was famed for his dancing; cf. Chaitanya-charitamrita, Madhya 14.98–101: in the garden the Lord dances beneath every tree while Vasudeva Datta sings, and then bids Vakreshvara dance and Himself sings with Svarupa Damodara.

**27.**

```
क्षणमपि परिरभ्य वक्रेश्वरं

क्षणमपि लघु विन्यसन् राजते
```

### 28

**28.**

```
क्षणमपि परितो मुहुर्विभ्रमं
स च परिरभतेऽथ तं भूयशः ।
लघु लघु मधुरं कलं गायति
स्मितरुचिररुचा क्षणं दीपयन् ॥२८॥
```
*kṣaṇam api parito muhur vibhramaṁ
sa ca parirabhate 'tha taṁ bhūyaśaḥ |
laghu laghu madhuraṁ kalaṁ gāyati

**Word for word:** *kṣaṇam api* — now; *paritaḥ* — all around; *muhuḥ* — again and again; *vibhramam* — [with] graceful whirling; *saḥ ca* — and He; *parirabhate* — embraces; *atha* — then; *tam* — him; *bhūyaśaḥ* — once more; *laghu laghu* — very softly; *madhuram* — sweetly; *kalam* — tenderly; *gāyati* — sings; *smita* — of [His] smile; *rucira* — with the charming; *rucā* — lustre; *kṣaṇam* — now; *dīpayan* — illuminating.

**Translation.** Now, whirling gracefully all around again and again, He embraces him once more; now, illuminating [all] with the charming lustre of His smile, He sings very softly, sweetly and tenderly.

**Note.** *Sa ca parirabhate … tam* may also be understood as "and he [Vakreshvara] in turn embraces Him" ⟨?⟩.

### 29

**29.**

```
इति निभृतमनेन वक्रेश्वर-
द्विजकुलशशिनाथ सम्पादयन् ।
नटनमभिरसं रसाम्भोनिधि-

```
*iti nibhṛtam anena vakreśvara-

naṭanam abhirasaṁ rasāmbhonidhir

**Word for word:** *iti* — thus; *nibhṛtam* — in seclusion; *anena* — with this; *vakreśvara* — Vakreshvara; *dvija-kula* — of the race of the twice-born; *śaśinā* — the moon; *atha* — then; *sampādayan* — performing; *naṭanam* — a dance; *abhirasam* — full of rasa; *rasa* — of rasa; *ambhonidhiḥ* — the ocean; *nyadhita* — set down; *saḥ* — He; *paritaḥ* — all around; *pada* — [His] foot; *ambhoruham* — lotus.

**Translation.** Thus, performing in seclusion with Vakreshvara, the moon of the race of the twice-born, a dance full of rasa, the Ocean of rasa then set His lotus foot down all around.

### 30

**30.**

```
तत्तथैव रभसादुपवनतो
वासुदेव इति निर्भरमधुरः ।
गानकौतुकरसैर्निजदयितं
रञ्जयन् कलपदं रहसि जगौ ॥३०॥
```
*tat-tathaiva rabhasād upavanato
vāsudeva iti nirbhara-madhuraḥ |
gāna-kautuka-rasair nija-dayitaṁ
rañjayan kala-padaṁ rahasi jagau ||30||*

**Word for word:** *tat-tathā eva* — at that very time; *rabhasāt* — eagerly; *upavanataḥ* — in the garden; *vāsudevaḥ* — Vasudeva; *iti* — [named]; *nirbhara* — deeply; *madhuraḥ* — sweet; *gāna* — of singing; *kautuka* — of the delight; *rasaiḥ* — with the rasas; *nija* — his own; *dayitam* — beloved; *rañjayan* — delighting; *kala-padam* — with sweet words; *rahasi* — in seclusion; *jagau* — sang.

**Translation.** At that very time, in the garden, the deeply sweet [singer] named Vasudeva, delighting his beloved [Lord] with the rasas of the delight of song, eagerly sang sweet songs in seclusion.

**Note.** Metre — *candravartma* (*ra-na-bha-sa*: —⏑—⏑⏑⏑—⏑⏑⏑⏑—); verses 30–42. Vasudeva is the singer; in verse 32 he is called "the best of physicians" (*bhiṣag-ṛṣabha*), that is, he was of the vaidya caste: Vasudeva Datta, the brother of Mukunda Datta. So too in the Chaitanya-charitamrita, Madhya 14.98–99, where Vasudeva Datta sings in the garden and the Lord dances.

### 31

**31.**

```
एककः सुमधुरं कलनिनदो
गीतमुत्तमतमं मधुमधुरम् ।
यज्जगौ कथमयं तमतिरसो
नो विकारमिह जात्वहह किमु ॥३१॥
```
*ekakaḥ sumadhuraṁ kala-ninado
gītam uttamatamaṁ madhu-madhuram |
yaj jagau katham ayaṁ tam atiraso
no vikāram iha jātv ahaha kim u ||31||*

**Word for word:** *ekakaḥ* — alone; *su-madhuram* — very sweet; *kala* — soft; *ninadaḥ* — [whose] voice [was]; *gītam* — a song; *uttamatamam* — most excellent; *madhu-madhuram* — honey-sweet; *yat* — since; *jagau* — [he] sang; *katham* — how; *ayam* — this [Lord]; *tam* — that; *ati-rasaḥ* — overflowing with rasa; *no* — not; *vikāram* — transformation; *iha* — here; *jātu* — ever; *ahaha* — ah; *kim u* — what then [to say].

**Translation.** Alone, in a soft voice, he sang a very sweet, most excellent, honey-sweet song: how then could this [Lord], overflowing with rasa, ever fail to [undergo] that transformation here? Ah, what more [need be said]?

**Note.** The sentence is elliptical: a verb such as "would undergo" is understood with *vikāram* ⟨?⟩. *Vikāra* — the transformation of the body and mind in ecstasy.

### 32

**32.**

```
गायतीह मधुरं भिषगृषभे
वासुदेव इति निर्भरमधुरे ।
आननर्त्त रभसादवशतनु-
र्भावभावितनतनुद्युतिमधुरः ॥३२॥
```
*gāyatīha madhuraṁ bhiṣag-ṛṣabhe
vāsudeva iti nirbhara-madhure |
ānanartta rabhasād avaśa-tanur
bhāva-bhāvita-tanu-dyuti-madhuraḥ ||32||*

**Word for word:** *gāyati* — as [he] was singing; *iha* — here; *madhuram* — sweetly; *bhiṣak* — of physicians; *ṛṣabhe* — the bull (i.e. the best); *vāsudeve* — Vasudeva; *iti* — [named]; *nirbhara* — deeply; *madhure* — sweet; *ānanartta* — broke into dance; *rabhasāt* — impetuously; *avaśa* — helpless; *tanuḥ* — [whose] body [was]; *bhāva* — with ecstatic feeling; *bhāvita* — suffused; *tanu* — of the body; *dyuti* — with the lustre; *madhuraḥ* — sweet.

**Translation.** As that best of physicians named Vasudeva, deeply sweet, was sweetly singing here, [Gaura], His body helpless, sweet with the lustre of His body suffused with ecstatic feeling, impetuously broke into dance.

**Note.** *Gāyati* is the locative of the present participle (*gāyat*), in an absolute construction with *bhiṣag-ṛṣabhe … vāsudeve* (*vāsudeva iti* = *vāsudeve iti* by sandhi).

### 33

**33.**

```
अश्रुभिः सुबहलैः पुलकघटा-
पूरितैरवयवैरतिमधुरैः ।
स्तम्भघर्महसितादिभिरनिशं
ताण्डवाकुलिततनुः स विजयते ॥३३॥
```
*aśrubhiḥ subahalaiḥ pulaka-ghaṭā-
pūritair avayavair ati-madhuraiḥ |
stambha-gharma-hasitādibhir aniśaṁ
tāṇḍavākulita-tanuḥ sa vijayate ||33||*

**Word for word:** *aśrubhiḥ* — with tears; *su-bahalaiḥ* — abundant; *pulaka* — of hairs standing on end; *ghaṭā* — with masses; *pūritaiḥ* — filled; *avayavaiḥ* — with limbs; *ati-madhuraiḥ* — exceedingly sweet; *stambha* — with stupor; *gharma* — perspiration; *hasita* — laughter; *ādibhiḥ* — and the rest; *aniśam* — unceasingly; *tāṇḍava* — by the tandava; *ākulita* — agitated; *tanuḥ* — [whose] body [is]; *saḥ* — He; *vijayate* — is glorious.

**Translation.** Glorious is He — with abundant tears, with His exceedingly sweet limbs covered with masses of hairs standing on end, with stupor, perspiration, laughter and the rest, His body unceasingly agitated by the tandava!

**Note.** The fourth pada has thirteen syllables instead of twelve: *tāṇḍavākulita-* gives an extra light syllable; the reading *tāṇḍavākula-tanuḥ* would scan correctly ⟨?⟩. The Sanskrit is given as printed.

### 34

**34.**

```
चन्द्रवर्त्म पिहितं वदनरुचा
मेरुरेष विजितोऽपघनरुचा ।
निन्दितं नु कमलं पदकमलै-
र्नृत्यतोऽस्य मधुरं मधुररुचः ॥३४॥
```
*candra-vartma pihitaṁ vadana-rucā
merur eṣa vijito 'paghana-rucā |
ninditaṁ nu kamalaṁ pada-kamalair
nṛtyato 'sya madhuraṁ madhura-rucaḥ ||34||*

**Word for word:** *candra* — of the moon; *vartma* — the path; *pihitam* — [was] eclipsed; *vadana* — of the face; *rucā* — by the radiance; *meruḥ* — Meru; *eṣaḥ* — this; *vijitaḥ* — [was] conquered; *apaghana* — of the body; *rucā* — by the lustre; *ninditam* — [was] put to shame; *nu* — indeed; *kamalam* — the lotus; *pada* — of the feet; *kamalaiḥ* — by the lotuses; *nṛtyataḥ* — dancing; *asya* — of Him; *madhuram* — sweetly; *madhura* — sweet; *rucaḥ* — [whose] radiance [is].

**Translation.** As He whose radiance is sweet danced sweetly, the path of the moon was eclipsed by the radiance of His face, this Meru was conquered by the lustre of His body, and the lotus was indeed put to shame by His lotus feet.

**Note.** Metre: *candravartma* (*ra-na-bha-sa*: —⏑—⏑⏑⏑—⏑⏑⏑⏑—; 12 syllables per pada), as throughout 30–42. The name of the metre is woven into the verse itself (a *mudrā*): its very first words, *candra-vartma*, "the path of the moon" (that is, the sky), are both the image — the radiance of His face eclipses the moon's path — and the name of the metre in which the verse is written. *Apaghana* — "body, limb".

**35.**

```

```

**Word for word:** *yat tu* — whatever; *gāyati* — he sings; *mahā* — with great; *rasa* — rasa; *balitam* — filled; *tatra* — in that; *yat yat* — whatever; *iha* — here; *na asti* — is not; *ati-lalitam* — very graceful; *bhāva* — with feeling; *bhāvitam* — imbued; *asau* — He; *nija* — from His own; *dayite* — beloved (lit. "in"); *tat* — that; *tataḥ* — therefore; *dviguṇitam* — doubled; *samakalayat* — perceived.

**Translation.** Whatever that [singer] sang, filled with great rasa — even whatever in it was not very graceful — He, [hearing it] from His own beloved, perceived as doubly imbued with feeling.

### 36

**36.**

```
अष्टभावबलितं स तु युगप-
च्छ्रीमदङ्गतलतः परिकलयन् ।
आननर्त्त रभसादवशतनु-
```

chrīmad-aṅga-talataḥ parikalayan |
ānanartta rabhasād avaśa-tanur

**Word for word:** *aṣṭa* — with the eight; *bhāva* — feelings; *balitam* — filled; *saḥ tu* — and He; *yugapat* — all at once; *śrīmat* — of the beautiful; *aṅga* — body; *talataḥ* — on the surface; *parikalayan* — displaying; *ānanartta* — danced; *rabhasāt* — impetuously; *avaśa* — helpless; *tanuḥ* — [whose] body; *gāyataḥ* — for the singing; *asya* — one (lit. **37.**

```
तत्तथोपवनमध्यतिमधुरः
श्रीशचीजठरवारिधिशशभृत् ।
```

**Word for word:** *tat-tathā* — thus; *upavanam adhi* — in the garden; *ati-madhuraḥ* — the most sweet; *śrī-śacī* — of Sri Shachi; *jaṭhara* — of the womb; *vāridhi* — from the ocean; *śaśabhṛt* — the moon; *ramya* — of the lovely; *tāṇḍava* — tandava; *rasa* — with the rasa; *sphurita* — quivering; *tanuḥ* — [whose] body; *sarvataḥ* — on every side; *atanuta* — spread out; *nirbhara* — the fullness; *lalitam* — of charm.

**Translation.** Thus in the garden the most sweet Moon from the ocean of Sri Shachi's womb, His body quivering with the rasa of the lovely tandava, spread out on every side the fullness of [His] charm.

**Note.** The moon was born from the ocean at its churning; Gaura is the moon born from the "ocean" of Shachi's womb.

**38.**

```
यो विलोकयति तस्य तु हृदयं
तत्क्षणेन चुलुकीकृतमभवत् ।
```

**Word for word:** *yaḥ* — who; *vilokayati* — beholds [Him]; *tasya tu* — of him; *hṛdayam* — the heart; *tat-kṣaṇena* — at that very moment; *culukī-kṛtam* — drunk up in one sip from the hollow of the hand; *abhavat* — became; *kintu* — but; *tasya* — his; *nayanam* — eye; *gata* — gone; *nimiṣam* — [whose] blinking; *tatra tatra* — now here, now there; *subhṛśam* — firmly; *parimilati* — clings.

**Note.** *Culukī-kṛ* — "to make into a palmful (*culuka*)", that is, to drink up without remainder (cf. Agastya, who drank up the ocean).

**40.**

```
फुल्लपङ्कजरजःपटलीकया

दीर्घिकारुचिरशीकरनिकरै-
```

vāyunā paridhutaṁ prabhum abhajat ||40||*

**Word for word:** *phulla* — of blossoming; *paṅkaja* — lotuses; *rajaḥ* — of the pollen; *paṭalīkayā* — with a covering; *kurvatā* — making; *sita* — white; *ruciṁ* — in lustre; *bhramara* — of bees; *kulam* — the swarm; *dīrghikā* — the pond; *rucira* — lovely; *śīkara* — of spray; *nikaraiḥ* — with masses; *vāyunā* — by the wind; *paridhutam* — fanned; *prabhum* — the Lord; *abhajat* — served.

**Translation.** With masses of lovely spray the pond served the Lord, who was fanned by the wind — [a wind] that, with a covering of pollen from the blossoming lotuses, made the swarm of bees white in lustre.

**Note.** The subject of *abhajat* is *dīrghikā*, "the pond" (nominative): the pond served with its spray the Lord (*prabhum*, accusative), who was fanned (*paridhutam*) by the wind. In the IAST line *dīrghikā* is joined by a hyphen to *rucira-śīkara-nikaraiḥ*, but it must be taken as a separate word (the Devanagari does not divide the words). The Gosvami edition (GGM) has the same text. Metre: in the first pada the tenth syllable (*-lī-* of *paṭalīkayā*) is heavy where the metre requires a light one (*paṭalikayā* would be regular).

### 41

**41.**

```
तत्र शीतलतटे प्रसृमरया

आदधे सपदि विश्रमणविधिं
```

*tatra śītala-taṭe prasṛmarayā

ādadhe sapadi viśramaṇa-vidhiṁ

**Word for word:** *tatra* — there; *śītala* — cool; *taṭe* — on the bank; *prasṛmarayā* — widely spreading; *chāyayā* — by the shade; *su-madhure* — most sweet; *madhura* — sweet; *mukhaḥ* — [whose] face; *ādadhe* — performed; *sapadi* — at once; *viśramaṇa* — of rest; *vidhim* — the rite; *kam* — whom; *na harṣati* — does not delight; *vastu* — a thing; *ati-ati-lalitam* — most exquisitely charming.

**42.**


### 43

**43.**

```
श्रीमन्नित्यानन्दपदाब्जप्रतिपन्न-
स्तत्तन्मध्ये कोऽपि महात्मा बहुभाग्यः ।
कृष्णाद्यो दासः स धरित्रीषु रम्यः
श्रीगौराङ्गं तं तत्र विलोक्याभिननन्द ॥४३॥
```
*śrīman-nityānanda-padābja-pratipannas
tat-tan-madhye ko 'pi mahātmā bahu-bhāgyaḥ |
kṛṣṇādyo dāsaḥ sa dharitrīṣu ramyaḥ
śrī-gaurāṅgaṁ taṁ tatra vilokyābhinananda ||43||*

**Word for word:** *śrīmat-nityānanda* — of Sriman Nityananda; *pada* — of the feet; *abja* — at the lotuses; *pratipannaḥ* — who had taken refuge; *tat-tat* — them; *madhye* — among; *kaḥ api* — a certain; *mahā-ātmā* — great soul; *bahu* — with much; *bhāgyaḥ* — [endowed with] good fortune; *kṛṣṇa-ādyaḥ* — beginning with "Krishna"; *dāsaḥ* — "dasa"; *saḥ* — he; *dharitrīṣu* — on earth; *ramyaḥ* — charming; *śrī-gaurāṅgam* — Sri Gauranga; *tam* — Him; *tatra* — there; *vilokya* — having seen; *abhinananda* — rejoiced.

**Translation.** Among them a certain great soul, most fortunate, who had taken refuge at the lotus feet of Sriman Nityananda — "dasa" [whose name] begins with "Krishna", charming on earth — rejoiced on seeing Sri Gauranga there.

**Note.** Metre: *mattamayūra* (*ma-ta-ya-sa-ga*: —————⏑⏑——⏑⏑——; 13 syllables per pada). From 43 onwards almost every verse is written in a metre of its own. The third pada has only twelve syllables (a light syllable is missing before *ramyaḥ*), and the fourth has fourteen (one heavy syllable too many in the opening: without *taṁ* the pada would be regular). The name Krishnadasa is split up for the sake of the metre. In 44 he is called "the best of brahmanas" (*kṣiti-sura-vara*). Which Krishnadasa is meant is unclear: the episode is not in the Chaitanya-charitamrita, and there are several Krishnadasas among the associates.

### 44

**44.**

```
तमथ मधुरमुखचन्द्रमवेक्ष्य
क्षितिसुरवर इह गौरसुधांशोः ।
नटनरभसभरघर्मजलाक्तं
स्नपयितुमतनुत चेतसि चेष्टाम् ॥४४॥
```

*tam atha madhura-mukha-candram avekṣya
kṣiti-sura-vara iha gaura-sudhāṁśoḥ |
naṭana-rabhasa-bhara-gharma-jalāktaṁ
snapayitum atanuta cetasi ceṣṭām ||44||*

**Word for word:** *tam* — that; *atha* — then; *madhura* — sweet; *mukha* — of the face; *candram* — moon; *avekṣya* — having seen; *kṣiti-sura* — of the "gods on earth" (brahmanas); *varaḥ* — the best; *iha* — here; *gaura-sudhāṁśoḥ* — of the moon Gaura; *naṭana* — of the dance; *rabhasa* — of the impetuosity; *bhara* — from the abundance; *gharma* — of perspiration; *jala* — with the water; *āktam* — moistened; *snapayitum* — to bathe [Him]; *atanuta* — conceived; *cetasi* — in [his] heart; *ceṣṭām* — the desire.

**Translation.** Then, seeing here that sweet moon of the face of the moon Gaura, wet with the water of perspiration from the abundance of His impetuous dance, the best of brahmanas conceived in his heart the desire to bathe Him.

**Note.** Metre: ⏑⏑⏑⏑⏑⏑⏑⏑—⏑⏑—— (*na-na-sa-sa-ga*, 13 syllables per pada); all four padas are regular (in the second, with the hiatus *vara iha*).

### 45

**45.**

```
स कुतश्चिदात्तघट एव महात्मा
लघुदीर्घिकाजलचयेन सतृष्णम् ।
प्रभुमूर्द्धनि नेत्रसलिलाप्लुतदेहः
पुलकावलीविलसितोऽथ सिषेच ॥४५॥
```

*sa kutaścid ātta-ghaṭa eva mahātmā

prabhu-mūrdhani netra-salilāpluta-dehaḥ
pulakāvalī-vilasito 'tha siṣeca ||45||*

**Word for word:** *saḥ* — he; *kutaścit* — from somewhere; *ātta* — having taken; *ghaṭaḥ* — a pot; *eva* — indeed; *mahā-ātmā* — the great soul; *laghu* — quickly; *dīrghikā* — of the pond; *jala* — of water; *cayena* — with a mass; *sa-tṛṣṇam* — eagerly; *prabhu* — of the Lord; *mūrdhani* — on the head; *netra* — of the eyes; *salila* — with the water; *āpluta* — bathed; *dehaḥ* — [whose] body; *pulaka* — of hairs standing on end; *āvalī* — with rows; *vilasitaḥ* — resplendent; *atha* — then; *siṣeca* — poured.

**47.**

```
तं परिलोच्य मनोरमदेहो
```

### 48

**48.**

```
अद्वैतोऽयं तत्तथैवोपविष्टः
स्नानार्थं श्रीगौरचन्द्रस्य सङ्गे ।
```

### 49

**49.**

```
भूयोभूयस्तैः पयोभिः सुशीतै-
रत्योत्कण्ठात् सेचयामास विप्रः ।
नेत्राम्भोभिः सोऽपि तत्राभिषिक्त-
```

*bhūyo-bhūyas taiḥ payobhiḥ suśītair
atyotkaṇṭhāt (aty-utkaṇṭhāt?) secayāmāsa vipraḥ |

**Word for word:** *bhūyaḥ-bhūyaḥ* — again and again; *taiḥ* — with those; *payobhiḥ* — waters; *su-śītaiḥ* — very cool; *ati-utkaṇṭhāt* — out of intense longing; *secayāmāsa* — poured; *vipraḥ* — the brahmana; *netra* — of the eyes; *ambhobhiḥ* — with the waters; *saḥ api* — He Himself; *tatra* — there; *abhiṣiktaḥ* — bathed; *citram citram* — a wonder, a wonder; *gauracandra* — of Gaurachandra; *anubhāvaḥ* — the power.


**51.**


### 54

**54.**

```
क्षणमपि करकमलयुगकलित-
ध्वनि जय जय जय जय जय जय भोः ।
इति निरवधि रथपरिसरपृथिवी-
```

*kṣaṇam api kara-kamala-yuga-kalita-

iti niravadhi ratha-parisara-pṛthivīm
abhi kala-padam ayam ati-rahasi jagau ||54||*

**Word for word:** *kṣaṇam api* — now; *kara* — of hands; *kamala* — lotus; *yuga* — by the pair; *kalita* — produced; *dhvani* — [with] a sound (clapping); *jaya jaya…* — "Glory, glory…"; *bhoḥ* — oh!; *iti* — thus; *niravadhi* — ceaselessly; *ratha* — of the chariot; *parisara* — surrounding; *pṛthivīm abhi* — on the ground; *kala-padam* — in sweet words; *ayam* — He; *ati-rahasi* — quite softly (?); *jagau* — sang.

**Translation.** Now, clapping with His pair of lotus hands, "Glory, glory, glory, glory, glory, glory — oh!" — thus He sang ceaselessly in sweet words, quite softly, on the ground around the chariot.

**Note.** Metre: *śaśikalā* (fourteen light syllables and one heavy: ⏑⏑⏑⏑⏑⏑⏑⏑⏑⏑⏑⏑⏑⏑—; 15 syllables per pada). The first pada has only fourteen syllables (the compound *-kalita-dhvani* runs on into the second pada): one syllable is missing ⟨?⟩. *Ati-rahasi* — literally "in great privacy"; in a crowded procession this probably means "to Himself, under His breath" ⟨?⟩. The Gosvami edition (GGM) has the same text.

**55.**

### 56

**56.**

```

```
*ullāsair harṣotkarṣai romāñcālī-rājad-deho
gāyadbhis tais taiḥ svīyaiḥ svīyāṁ līlā-gāthām eva |

Here too the name of a metre is woven into the verse: *vidyun-mālā*, "a garland of lightning" (third pada), is at once the image of His radiance and the name of the metre (*vidyunmālā*) from which this one is built. All four padas are regular (the final syllable of the second and third padas is free). His companions sing of His own pastimes.

### 57

**57.**

```
उच्चैरुच्छ्रितचूडाकुम्भग्रस्तपताका-
चुम्बवद्भास्करबिम्बः श्रीमान् स्यन्दनमुख्यः ।
सोऽयं नीलमहीध्रश्रीमन्मौलिसुधांशो-
```

### 58

**58.**

```
इत्येवं पथि दृष्ट्वा दृष्ट्वा कौतुकचेष्टा-
मात्रविलासो लास्योद्दामसुमूर्त्तिः ।
श्रीमत्स्यन्दनयात्रां त्रैलोक्याद्भुतरूपां
गौराङ्गोऽतिकृपालुर्नेत्राभ्यामपिबत् सः ॥५८॥
```
*ity evaṁ pathi dṛṣṭvā dṛṣṭvā kautuka-ceṣṭā-
mātra-vilāso lāsyoddāma-sumūrttiḥ |
śrīmat-syandana-yātrāṁ trailokyādbhuta-rūpāṁ
gaurāṅgo 'ti-kṛpālur netrābhyām apibat saḥ ||58||*

**Word for word:** *iti evam* — thus; *pathi* — on the road; *dṛṣṭvā dṛṣṭvā* — gazing and gazing; *kautuka* — of playfulness; *ceṣṭā* — the movement; *mātra* — merely; *vilāsaḥ* — [whose] pastime; *lāsya* — in dance; *uddāma* — unrestrained; *su-mūrttiḥ* — [whose] beautiful form; *śrīmat* — beautiful; *syandana* — of the chariot; *yātrām* — the procession; *trailokya* — to the three worlds; *adbhuta* — wondrous; *rūpām* — in appearance; *gaurāṅgaḥ* — Gauranga; *ati-kṛpāluḥ* — the most merciful; *netrābhyām* — with [His] eyes; *apibat* — drank; *saḥ* — He.

**Translation.** Thus on the road, gazing and gazing, the most merciful Gauranga — for whom any playful movement is already a pastime, and whose beautiful form was unrestrained in the dance — drank with His eyes the beautiful procession of the chariot, wondrous in appearance to the three worlds.

### 59

**59.**

```

त्रैलोक्यस्थतमिस्रं भूयोभूय उदस्य ।
अर्के स्यन्दनमुख्यः श्रीनीलाद्रिसुधांशो-
स्तर्के तत्र निषण्णो नोत्साहो मनुजानाम् ॥५९॥
```
*astādri-stha-vanālīṁ viśrāmārtham upaiti
trailokya-stha-tamisraṁ bhūyo-bhūya udasya |
arke syandana-mukhyaḥ śrī-nīlādri-sudhāṁśos
tarke tatra niṣaṇṇo notsāho manujānām ||59||*

**Word for word:** *asta-adri* — on the Mountain of Sunset; *stha* — lying; *vana* — of forests; *ālīm* — to the line; *viśrāma* — of rest; *artham* — for the sake; *upaiti* — goes; *trailokya* — over the three worlds; *stha* — lying; *tamisram* — the darkness; *bhūyaḥ-bhūyaḥ* — again and again; *udasya* — having dispelled; *arke* — [when] the sun; *syandana* — of chariots; *mukhyaḥ* — the foremost; *śrī-nīlādri* — of the beautiful Blue Mountain; *sudhāṁśoḥ* — of the moon; *tarke* — I suppose; *tatra* — there; *niṣaṇṇaḥ* — came to a halt; *na* — no; *utsāhaḥ* — zeal [to go on]; *manujānām* — of the people.

**Translation.** When the sun, having again and again dispelled the darkness lying over the three worlds, goes away to rest to the line of forests on the Mountain of Sunset, the foremost chariot of the Moon of the beautiful Blue Mountain, I suppose, came to a halt there: the people had no [more] zeal [to go on].

**Note.** Metre: *līlā*, as in 57–58. The edition has *-sthita-miśraṁ*, which makes no sense; the Gosvami edition (GGM) reads *trailokya-stha-tamisraṁ*, "the darkness lying over the three worlds", and so we read. *Tarke*, "I suppose", is also in the Gosvami edition. *Upaiti* stands where a locative of the participle would be expected (in agreement with *arke*). *Notsāho manujānām* is literally "[there was] no zeal of the people"; it may also be understood as "[but] the people's zeal did not [abate]" ⟨?⟩.

### 60

**60.**

```
आगत्यानय कच्छे तत्रत्यान् सुखसिन्धौ
क्षिप्यन् सायमकार्षीच्छ्रीनीलाद्रिसुधांशुः ।
वर्त्मन्येव समन्तात् सञ्चार्यैः कशिपुना
क्रामन् पादविहारैरुद्धस्तत्र निवेशम् ॥६०॥
```
*āgatyānaya (ālaya-?) kacche tatratyān sukha-sindhau
kṣipyan sāyam akārṣīc chrī-nīlādri-sudhāṁśuḥ |
vartmany eva samantāt sañcāryaiḥ kaśipunā
krāman pāda-vihārai ruddhas tatra niveśam ||60||*

**Word for word:** *āgatya* — having come; *ālaya* (?) — of the abode; *kacche* — to the vicinity; *tatratyān* — the people there; *sukha* — of happiness; *sindhau* — into the ocean; *kṣipyan* — casting; *sāyam* — in the evening; *akārṣīt* — made; *śrī-nīlādri* — of the beautiful Blue Mountain; *sudhāṁśuḥ* — the moon; *vartmani eva* — right along the road; *samantāt* — from all sides; *sañcāryaiḥ* — being moved along; *kaśipunā* — on cushions; *krāman* — advancing; *pāda* — with steps; *vihāraiḥ* — playful; *ruddhaḥ* — held back; *tatra* — there; *niveśam* — entrance.

**Translation.** Having come to the vicinity of [His] abode and casting the people there into an ocean of happiness, in the evening the Moon of the beautiful Blue Mountain, advancing right along the road on cushions that were moved along from all sides, and held back by playful steps, made His entrance there.

**Note.** Evidently the *pahaṇḍi* is described: Jagannatha is carried, swaying, over cotton cushions (*kaśipu*) laid down before Him. The edition has *ānaya*; by sense we read *ālaya-* ⟨?⟩. *Kṣipyan* — the same irregular form as in 53. *Pāda-vihārai ruddhaḥ* — *-vihāraiḥ* with the visarga dropped before *r*. *Sañcāryaiḥ* (plural) does not agree with *kaśipunā* (singular); *kaśipu* is evidently meant collectively. Metre: in the third pada the thirteenth syllable (*-pu-* of *kaśipunā*) is light where the metre requires a heavy one. The Gosvami edition (GGM) has the same text.

### 65

**65.**

```
शक्त्या चेन्नयनं नयत्यतितरां नीलाद्रिरत्ने जन-
स्तत्स्वान्तं पुनरत्र चित्रलिखितप्रायं शचीनन्दने ।
चेत्तत्रैव ददाति लोचनयुगं चित्रं चरित्रं ततो-
ऽकस्माद्वा जडिमा विमोहनकरोऽकस्मान्मुहुर्जायते ॥६५॥
```
*śaktyā cen nayanaṁ nayaty atitarāṁ nīlādri-ratne janas
tat svāntaṁ punar atra citra-likhita-prāyaṁ śacīnandane |
cet tatraiva dadāti locana-yugaṁ citraṁ caritraṁ tato
'kasmād vā jaḍimā vimohana-karo 'kasmān muhur jāyate ||65||*

**Word for word:** *śaktyā* — with effort; *cet* — if; *nayanam* — the gaze; *nayati* — directs; *atitarām* — intently; *nīlādri* — of the Blue Mountain; *ratne* — on the Jewel; *janaḥ* — a person; *tat* — then; *svāntam* — [his] heart; *punaḥ* — however; *atra* — here; *citra* — in a picture; *likhita* — painted; *prāyam* — almost; *śacī-nandane* — on the son of Shachi; *cet* — if; *tatra eva* — on Him; *dadāti* — fixes; *locana* — of eyes; *yugam* — the pair; *citram* — wondrous; *caritram* — the behaviour; *tataḥ* — then; *akasmāt* — suddenly; *vā* — indeed; *jaḍimā* — stupor; *vimohana* — bewilderment; *karaḥ* — causing; *akasmāt* — without cause; *muhuḥ* — again and again; *jāyate* — arises.

**Translation.** If a person with effort directs his gaze intently on the Jewel of the Blue Mountain, then his heart here, on the son of Shachi, becomes as though painted in a picture; and if he fixes the pair of his eyes on Him Himself — wondrous is this behaviour! — then suddenly, without cause, a bewildering stupor arises again and again.

**Note.** The edition has मदाति (*madāti*), which makes no sense; by sense we read *dadāti*, as does the Gosvami edition (GGM).

### 66

**66.**

```
इत्येवं रथयात्रया सरभसं स्वैः स्वैः स्वकीयैर्गुणं
सङ्कीर्त्य स्वमवेक्ष्य तत्र मुदितः प्रत्यब्दमाक्रीडति ।
तत्तल्लास्यविलासकौतुककथा कैर्वा समुद्गीयतां
ब्रह्मादेरपि नास्ति नास्ति नितरां शक्तिस्तथा तादृशी ॥६६॥
```
*ity evaṁ ratha-yātrayā sarabhasaṁ svaiḥ svaiḥ svakīyair guṇaṁ
saṅkīrtya svam avekṣya tatra muditaḥ pratyabdam ākrīḍati |
tat-tal-lāsya-vilāsa-kautuka-kathā kair vā samudgīyatāṁ
brahmāder api nāsti nāsti nitarāṁ śaktis tathā tādṛśī ||66||*

**Word for word:** *iti evam* — thus; *ratha-yātrayā* — during the Ratha-yatra; *sa-rabhasam* — ardently; *svaiḥ svaiḥ svakīyaiḥ* — with all His own [people]; *guṇam* — the qualities; *saṅkīrtya* — having glorified; *svam* — Himself; *avekṣya* — beholding; *tatra* — there; *muditaḥ* — joyful; *pratyabdam* — every year; *ākrīḍati* — sports; *tat-tat* — of all those; *lāsya* — of dance; *vilāsa* — of the pastimes; *kautuka* — of the wonders; *kathā* — the tale; *kaiḥ* — by whom; *vā* — indeed; *samudgīyatām* — can be sung; *brahma-ādeḥ* — of Brahma and the others; *api* — even; *na asti na asti* — there is not, there is not; *nitarām* — at all; *śaktiḥ* — ability; *tathā* — so; *tādṛśī* — such.

**Translation.** Thus every year He sports there joyfully during the Ratha-yatra: together with all His own people He ardently glorifies [His] qualities, beholding Himself. By whom indeed can the tale of the wonders of all those pastimes of dance be sung? Even Brahma and the others have not, have not at all, such ability.

**Note.** *Svam avekṣya*, "beholding Himself": in Jagannatha Gaura beholds His own self, Krishna. *Pratyabdam*, "every year": the Lord came to the Ratha-yatra every year of the eighteen years of His life in Puri. In the edition the third pada has only eighteen syllables (*tal-lāsya-vilāsa*), and in the fourth *śakti-kathā* breaks the metre of *śārdūla-vikrīḍita*. The Gosvami edition (GGM) reads *tat-tal-lāsya-…* and *śaktis tathā tādṛśī*, which restores the metre in both padas; this text is adopted.
