# Kavi Karnapura. Sri Chaitanya-charita-mahakavya
## Sarga Nineteen — verses 1–100 (printed pp. 546–571)

**Title of the sarga:** *Śrī-vṛndāvana-yātrā*, "The Journey to Sri Vrindavana" (from the colophon: *iti śrī-kṛṣṇa-caitanya-caritāmṛte mahākāvye śrī-vṛndāvana-yātrāyām ūnaviṁśaḥ sargaḥ*, "Thus [ends] the nineteenth sarga, [in the account of] the journey to Sri Vrindavana, in the great poem Sri Krishna-Chaitanya-charitamrita").
**Metre:**
- 1–99 — *anuṣṭubh* (*śloka*: 8 syllables per pada; in the even padas the fifth to seventh syllables are ⏑—⏑; in the odd padas, besides the normal ⏑——, the permitted variants (*vipulā*) are used freely). Irregularities of the printed text: in 6 the third pada (*paramānanda-purī ca*) has ⏑⏑— in the fifth to seventh places, which is neither the normal form nor a *vipulā* (a licence with a proper name); in 22 the second and third padas of the edition are each a syllable short, and in 25 the second line has 13 syllables instead of 16; in both the text is restored after the Gosvami edition (GGM); in 37 the second pada has seven syllables (*nānā nūnanū nanu*); in 49 the first pada (*avapayo 'tivimalam*) has both the second and the third syllables light; 70 has six padas; in 81 the fourth pada has nine syllables (*dṛṣṭaye tatra cakāra saḥ*). In 45 and 53 (palindromes) each printed line is a single pada.
- 100 — *mandākrāntā* (*ma-bha-na-ta-ta-ga-ga*: ————⏑⏑⏑⏑⏑——⏑——⏑——; 17 syllables per pada).

**Total verses:** 100.

**Word-for-word conventions:** compound words are divided into their members, each with its own meaning; case is rendered by an English preposition ("of", "with", "in", "by", "to", "from"); the vocative is given with "O". Square brackets enclose a word that is understood (for example, [whose], [like]). In the *citra-kāvya* verses the division of words follows the word division (*padaccheda*) and the prose order (*anvaya*) of the edition's Sanskrit commentary.

**Sources:** for the *citra-kāvya* verses the edition gives a Sanskrit commentary (*ṭīkā*), and for some of them a word division (*padaccheda*) and the prose order (*anvaya*); the translations of these verses are based on it.

**A feature of the sarga — *citra-kāvya*** ("picture poetry", verse puzzles). The commentary at verse 17 announces that from here on there follow verses on one, two, three and more consonants. The types:
- verses on a restricted set of sounds:
  - on two consonants (*dvyakṣara*) — 17 (*bh, v*), 41 (*l, n*);
  - on a single consonant (*ekākṣara*) — 25 (*l*), 37 (*n*);
  - without consonant clusters (*asaṁyoga*) — 61;
  - without labial sounds (*niroṣṭhya*) — 65;
  - without diphthongs (*asandhyakṣara*) — 89;
- figured verses (*bandha*), whose syllables, written out in a grid, form a pattern — 21 (*gomūtrikā*, "the zigzag of a cow's urine trail"), 29 (*muraja*, "the drum"), 93 (*padma*, "the lotus");
- palindromes — 45 (each pada), 53 (the whole verse);
- others:
  - *yamaka* (the repetition of a group of syllables with a different meaning) — 5, 33, 75 and elsewhere;
  - a verse with two readings (*ślokāvṛtti*) — 49.

**Contents of the passage:** the beginning of the journey to Vrindavana (by way of Bengal).
- 1–5 — the decision to go to Mathura by way of the bank of the Ganga:
  - 2–3 — an insertion made by the publishers from another edition (footnote);
  - Ramananda holds the Lord back; the play on "Vijaya-dashami / the tenth state" (5).
- 6–12 — the companions: Govinda, Jagadananda, Damodara Pandita, Paramananda Puri; mahaprasada for the Vaishnavas of Bengal; on the advice of Puri the sanctified cloth received from Prataparudra is to be sent to the Lord's mother (9–12).
- 13–19 — Damodara is late for the first service; the Lord, "the Ocean of love, out of love", strikes him with a little book (18–19).
- 20–34 — the farewell to Jagannatha and to Puri:
  - the Lord sends back those who follow Him, headed by Kashi Mishra (22);
  - Ramananda follows Him in a palanquin (26); the loving dispute between them (31–34).
- 35–51 — mahaprasada from Vaninatha; autumn; the Lord embraces the trees as if they were the trees of Vrindavana (43–48); Bhuvaneshvara.
- 52–80 — a village with a house built by Ramananda; Kataka (Cuttack): the temple of Gopinatha; the brahmana Svapneshvara; a garden with a bakula tree.
- 81–92 — **Prataparudra** comes with an army of four divisions (drawn up in rings, 87–88) and falls at the Lord's feet.
- 93–100 — the king orders Mangaraja and Harichandana to escort the Lord beyond the Mahanadi and to raise memorial pillars; the night's halt at Chaturdvara.

**Textual cruxes:** 2–3 (insertion), 6 (metre), 21 (*samūt*), 22 (short padas), 25 (short line), 29 (*natatrātrā*), 32 (*tyaktā*), 34 (variant *kariṣyati*), 37 (seven-syllable pada), 41 (*nalāla*), 48 (*paśyā*), 49, 53, 70 (six padas), 77 (*randhona-*), 78, 79, 81 (nine-syllable pada), 94 (*bhavānena*), 99 (*āropyo*) — see the notes.

Format and conventions as in Sarga One.

**3.**

```
{ प्रयातुं यमुनातीरं गङ्गातीरे मनो दधे ।
यत्तटे सोऽवतीर्णोऽस्ति तस्यां प्रीतिर्महीयसी ॥२॥
दक्षिणादागतो यावत्तावत्तत्र महाप्रभुः ।
मथुरायां चलत्येव रामानन्दोऽत्र बाधते ॥३॥ }**
```
*prayātuṁ yamunā-tīraṁ gaṅgā-tīre mano dadhe |
yat-taṭe so 'vatīrṇo 'sti tasyāṁ prītir mahīyasī ||2||
dakṣiṇād āgato yāvat tāvat tatra mahāprabhuḥ |
mathurāyāṁ calaty eva rāmānando 'tra bādhate ||3||*

**Word for word (2):** *prayātum* — to set out; *yamunā* — of the Yamuna; *tīram* — for the bank; *gaṅgā* — of the Ganga; *tīre* — on the bank; *manaḥ* — [His] mind; *dadhe* — He set; *yat* — on whose; *taṭe* — bank; *saḥ* — He; *avatīrṇaḥ asti* — has descended; *tasyām* — for her; *prītiḥ* — love; *mahīyasī* — very great.

**Word for word (3):** *dakṣiṇāt* — from the south; *āgataḥ* — returned; *yāvat … tāvat* — no sooner … than; *tatra* — then; *mahāprabhuḥ* — Mahaprabhu; *mathurāyām* — for Mathura; *calati eva* — is already setting off; *rāmānandaḥ* — Ramananda; *atra* — at this; *bādhate* — hinders (holds [Him] back).

**Translation (2–3).** Wishing to set out for the bank of the Yamuna, He set His mind [first] on the bank of the Ganga: for it is on her bank that He has descended, and His love for her is very great. No sooner had Mahaprabhu returned from the south than He was already setting off for Mathura — but at this Ramananda holds Him back.

**Note.** The edition's footnote (in Sanskrit): "In this book two verses were illegible because of a printing defect; therefore, after consulting another book, they were typeset by computer." Verses 2–3 are enclosed in braces; that is, we have a later reprint with an insertion. *Bādhate* means rather "holds back"; cf. the Chaitanya-charitamrita, Madhya 16.4–8, 85–92: for years Sarvabhauma and Ramananda dissuade the Lord from going to Vrindavana, until He reproaches them.

### 5

**5.**

```
दशम्यां विजयायां तु गमनं भविता प्रभोः ।
दशम्यां विजयायां तु दशायामहमग्रतः ॥५॥
```
*daśamyāṁ vijayāyāṁ tu gamanaṁ bhavitā prabhoḥ |
daśamyāṁ vijayāyāṁ tu daśāyām aham agrataḥ ||5||*

**Word for word:** *daśamyām* — on the tenth [day]; *vijayāyām* — of Vijaya; *tu* — indeed; *gamanam* — the departure; *bhavitā* — will take place; *prabhoḥ* — of the Lord; *daśamyām* — in the tenth; *vijayāyām* — all-conquering; *tu* — but; *daśāyām* — in the state; *aham* — I [shall be]; *agrataḥ* — before [that].

**Translation.** "On Vijaya-dashami the Lord's departure will take place — but I shall be in the tenth, all-conquering state before [that]."

**Note.** A play on words (*yamaka*: the first half of each line is the same), explained in the edition's Sanskrit commentary: *vijayā daśamī* is the festival of Vijaya-dashami (in autumn); in the second line *vijayā* is "she who conquers especially" (*viśeṣeṇa jayatīti*), and *daśamī daśā* is the tenth of the ten states of one in love, that is, the end of life. The commentary gives the list: "attraction of the eyes, attachment of the mind, constant thought, sleeplessness, emaciation, aversion to [other] objects, loss of shame, madness, swooning, dying — these are the states in order." The sense: "The Lord will depart on the day of Vijaya, but I shall die even before."

*bhāvābhāvābhibhāvābhibhava-bhāve babhau bhavaḥ |
vibhāv evam-bhāva-bhāve babhūva bhuvi vaibhavam ||17||
(dvyakṣaraḥ)*

**Word for word** (following the word division of the edition's commentary: *bhāva abhāva abhibhāva abhibhava bhāve babhau bhavaḥ | vibhau evambhāva bhāve babhūva bhuvi vaibhavam*): *bhāva* — of the presence; *abhāva* — from the absence; *abhibhāva* — [by] the all-pervading state [of separation]; *abhibhava* — of being overcome; *bhāve* — [when there was] the state; *babhau* — shone forth; *bhavaḥ* — the birth [of Damodara]; *vibhau* — [when] the Master [was]; *evam-bhāva* — such; *bhāve* — in a state; *babhūva* — there arose; *bhuvi* — on earth; *vaibhavam* — glory.

**Translation.** When [the Lord] was in the state of being overcome by the all-pervading state [of separation] arising from the absence [of Damodara], the birth [of Damodara] shone forth; when the Master was in such a state, [his] glory arose on earth.

**Note.** *Dvyakṣara*, a "two-consonant" verse: the whole verse uses only the consonants *bh* and *v* (a kind of *citra-kāvya*). The translation follows the edition's Sanskrit commentary: "*bhāva* is existence; its *abhāva* is the absence of Sri Damodara; *abhi-bhāva* is the state of separation on all sides; *abhibhava* is being overcome by it; in this state *bhavaḥ*, the birth [of Damodara], shone forth, and on earth there was *vaibhavam*, honour. The sense: he in separation from whom the Lord becomes agitated — his birth is fruitful, and honour is his." The commentary adds: "From here on there follows one verse each on one, two, three and more consonants."

### 25

**25.**

```
ललल्लीलो ललल्लीलो लोलो लोलो ललल्ललः ।
लीलालोलोऽलिलीलालीं लीलालीं लोललां ललुः ॥२५॥
(एकाक्षरः)
```
*lalal-līlo lalal-līlo lolo lolo lalal-lalaḥ |
līlā-lolo 'li-līlālīṁ līlālīṁ lolalāṁ laluḥ ||25||
(ekākṣaraḥ)*

**Word for word** (following the edition's commentary): *lalat* — shining; *līlaḥ* — [whose] pastime [of going to Vraja is]; *lalat* (= *laḍat*) — cast aside; *līlaḥ* — [whose] pastime [of living at Nilachala is]; *lolaḥ* — restless; *lolaḥ* — eager [to go to Vraja]; *lalat* — wishing; *lalaḥ* (= *laḍaḥ*) — [to send all the people away]; *līlā* — in play; *lolaḥ* — unsettled; *ali* — of bees; *līlā-ālīm* — [like] a series of sports; *lola* — the restless one; *lām* — holding back; *[ceṣṭā-]ālīm* — a series of [efforts] ⟨?⟩; *laluḥ* — [the devotees] made.

**Translation.** [The Lord], whose pastime [of going to Vraja] shone forth, who had cast aside the pastime [of living at Nilachala], restless, eager, wishing to send [everyone] away, was unsettled in His play; [and the devotees], like bees at their sport, made a series of efforts to hold back the restless One.

**Note.** *Ekākṣara*, a "one-consonant" verse: the whole verse uses only the consonant *l* (*ḍ* and *l* being treated as identical). The sense follows the edition's Sanskrit commentary: as bees do not abandon a flower swaying in the wind but try to settle on it, so Govinda, Damodara and the others did not leave the departing Shachinandana but tried to hold Him back. *Laluḥ* is the perfect of *lā*, "to take, to hold". The second *līlālīm* is taken as "a series of [efforts]" (the commentary's *ceṣṭā-ālīm*, "a series of actions") ⟨?⟩. In the edition the second line has 13 syllables instead of 16; the Gosvami edition (GGM) reads *līlālolo 'li-līlālīṁ līlālīṁ lolalāṁ laluḥ*, and the repeated *līlālīṁ* is restored accordingly.

**29.**

```

**Word for word** (following the edition's commentary): *saḥ* — He; *tatra* — then; *gamana* — of the journey; *ārambhe* — at the beginning; *nata* — of the humble; *trātṛ* — of the protectors; *trāḥ* — the protector; *na na ābabau* — did not not go (= did go); *pavitra* — pure; *aṅghri-jana* — to the servants of [His] feet; *ānandam* — joy [giving]; *bhavitra* — auspicious; *āgamana* — return; *ānanam* — [whose] face [promised].

**33.**

```

### 37

**37.**

```

नाना नूने नाननान्नोने नो नान ननुन्ननु ॥३७॥
(पुनरेकाक्षरः)
```
*nānānā nuni nānene nānā nūnanū nanu |
nānā nūne nānanānnone no nāna nanunnanu ||37||
(punar ekākṣaraḥ)*

**The edition's word division and prose order:** *nanu nānānā nuni ā-aṇūn nānā-inenān anaṇūn anu | nānānūne na ānana-anna-ūne no nānā nut na nanu.*

**Word for word** (following the commentary): *nanu* — oh!; *nānānā* — a certain man among many; *nuni* — humbly (?); *ā-aṇūn* — quite small [offerings]; *nānā* — manifold; *ina* — to the Lord; *inān* — equal (= the Lord's prasada); *anaṇūn anu* — considering [them] abundant; *nānā-anūne* — in regard to variety and abundance; *ānana* — of the mouth; *anna* — of the food (= the nectar of the lips); *ūne* — in a [supposed] deficiency; *no* — not; *nānā* — "[it is] little"; *nut* — saying; *na* — was not.

**Translation.** Oh! A certain man among many, seeing the manifold [gifts of prasada], equal to the Lord, considered them abundant, though they were quite small: neither as to their variety and quantity, nor on the ground that they fell short of the nectar of His lips, did he say, "[It is] little."

**Note.** Again an *ekākṣara*: the whole verse uses only the consonant *n* (*ṇ* being treated as equal to *n*). The sense follows the edition's Sanskrit commentary: the Lord's prasada is equal to the Lord Himself, and so even a small quantity of it was honoured by all as abundant. The commentary quotes the Brihad-bhagavatamrita: "What is offered to the Lord of the world — food, drink and the rest — is unchanging like Brahman: as Vishnu is, so is it." *Ina* — "lord, master"; *inena tulyaḥ inaḥ* — "[the prasada,] equal to the Lord". The translation is tentative ⟨?⟩. Metre: the second pada (*nānā nūnanū nanu*) has only seven syllables ⟨?⟩. The Gosvami edition (GGM) has the same text.

**41.**

(द्व्यक्षरः)
```
*līlā-lolāli-lalanā lalan nalina-lālanaiḥ |
nalāla (? calāla) lalanālīnāṁ līlāṁ lān anilo lalan ||41||
(dvyakṣaraḥ)*

**Word for word** (following the edition's commentary): *līlā* — in play; *lola* — restless; *ali* — of the bees; *lalanāḥ* — the female companions; *lalan* — desiring; *nalina* — of the lotuses; *lālanaiḥ* — by the swaying; *lalanā-ālīnām* — of those clinging to [their] companions; *līlām* — the play; *lān* — taking; *anilaḥ* — the wind; *lalan* — desiring [= well pleased]; *nalāla* (?) — moved.

**Translation.** The wind, swaying the lotuses, desiring the female companions of the bees, restless in their play, and taking on the play proper to those who cling to their companions, moved along, well pleased ⟨?⟩.

(pratilomānuloma-ślokaḥ)*

**Word for word:** *sārasā* — full of sarasa cranes; *sa-rasā* — full of water; *ram* (?) — ⟨?⟩; *rasā* — the earth; *nūtana-nūtanā* — new and ever new; *na-atanū-atanū* — of [things] embodied and unembodied (?); *sāra* — the essence; *raṁsā* (?) — bestowing; *sā* — she (autumn); *rasa* — with juices; *sārasā* — filled.

**Translation.** That [autumn] — a fresh, ever-fresh earth, full of water and of sarasa cranes, bestowing the essence on all things embodied and unembodied, filled with juices — [shone] ⟨?⟩.

**Note.** A *pratilomānuloma-śloka*: the whole verse, written out syllable by syllable, reads the same from the beginning and from the end. The word division is partly conjectural ⟨?⟩. As printed, the palindrome is imperfect: the first pada has only seven syllables (सा र सा स र सा रं), whereas the mirror image of the fourth (रं सा सा र स सा र सा) requires eight — सा र सा स र सा सा रं; and the fifth syllable of the second pada (*na* in *nūtana*) does not mirror the fourth syllable of the third (*ta*). Metre: apart from the short first pada, the verse is regular. The Gosvami edition (GGM) has the same text.

### 65

**65.**

```
आयाति करुणासिन्धुरिति श्रुत्वा गजेश्वरः ।
आज्ञया सकलं तीर्थं चकार करलालितम् ॥६५॥
(निरोष्ठ्यः)
```
*āyāti karuṇā-sindhur iti śrutvā gajeśvaraḥ |
ājñayā sakalaṁ tīrthaṁ cakāra kara-lālitam ||65||
(niroṣṭhyaḥ)*

**Word for word:** *āyāti* — is coming; *karuṇā* — of mercy; *sindhuḥ* — the Ocean; *iti* — thus; *śrutvā* — having heard; *gaja-īśvaraḥ* — the lord of elephants (the Gajapati); *ājñayā* — by [his] command; *sakalam* — every; *tīrtham* — landing-place; *cakāra* — made; *kara* — by hands; *lālitam* — tended.

**Translation.** Hearing, "The Ocean of Mercy is coming," the lord of elephants by his command had every landing-place tended by [his people's] hands.

**Note.** A *niroṣṭhya*: a verse without labial consonants (*p, ph, b, bh, m*; the anusvara is not counted). The semivowel *v* (in *śrutvā*, *gajeśvaraḥ*), being dento-labial, is evidently not reckoned as labial, nor is the vowel *u* (in *karuṇā*, *sindhuḥ*). The "lord of elephants" is the Gajapati, the title of the kings of Orissa — here Prataparudra. *Tīrtha* — a landing-place or ford for crossing a river. Cf. Chaitanya-charitamrita, Madhya 16.112–116: the king orders houses and new boats to be made ready for the Lord at the crossings, and memorial pillars to be set up.

### 66

**66.**

```

```
*sarvāṅgīnair alaṅkārair mādhuryaujaḥ-prasādavān |
gopīnātho rarājāsau vāg-vilāsaḥ kaver iva ||66||*

**Word for word:** *sarva-aṅgīnaiḥ* — covering all the limbs; *alaṅkāraiḥ* — with ornaments; *mādhurya* — sweetness; *ojaḥ* — vigour; *prasāda* — [and] clarity; *vān* — possessing; *gopīnāthaḥ* — Gopinatha; *rarāja* — shone; *asau* — He; *vāk* — of words; *vilāsaḥ* — the play; *kaveḥ* — of a poet; *iva* — like.

**Translation.** Gopinatha, with ornaments covering all His limbs, possessing sweetness, vigour and clarity, shone like a poet's play of words.

**Note.** A double meaning: *alaṅkāra* is both "ornament" and "figure of speech", and *mādhurya*, *ojas* and *prasāda* are the three excellences (*guṇa*) of poetic diction. The edition's Sanskrit commentary explains: *mādhurya* is the delight that melts the heart; *ojas* is the brilliance that expands the heart (in the heroic, the loathsome and the furious rasas); *prasāda* is that which pervades the heart at once, as fire seizes dry wood.

**Word for word:** *atha* — then; *svapneśvaraḥ nāma* — named Svapneshvara; *saḥ ayam* — this same; *dharaṇi* — of the earth; *daivatam* — god (a brahmana); *bhikṣā-artham* — for a meal; *avṛṇot* — invited; *tatra* — there; *gṛhe* — to [his] house; *api ca* — and also; *samānayat* — brought.

**Translation.** Then this same god on earth, the brahmana named Svapneshvara, invited [the Lord] there for a meal and also brought [Him] to his house.

**Note.** *So 'yam*, "this same", presumably points to the brahmana of verse 70. In the Chaitanya-charitamrita (Madhya 16.100) the brahmana Svapneshvara invites the Lord to a meal at Kataka, and afterwards the Lord rests beneath a bakula tree (16.102).

### 75

**75.**

```

**Word for word** (following the edition's commentary). First line: *āgāra* — for the house; *āhitya* — [of] being beneficial; *sukha-de* — giving the pleasure; *manorāme* — charming. Second line: *āga* (= *aga*, "mountain") — of the mountains; *arāhitya* — inseparable [pleasure], i.e. the pleasure of hill country (*pārvatya-sukha*); *sukha-de* — giving; *sadā-ārāme* — to a fine garden; *tadā* — then; *ānayat* — brought; *saḥ* — he; *tān* — them; *atha* — then.

**Translation.** Then he brought them at that time to a fine garden — charming, giving the pleasure of being beneficial to the household, and giving the unfailing pleasure of hill country ⟨?⟩.

**Note.** *Yamaka*: the two lines open with the same sequence of syllables (*āgārāhitya-sukhade*), which is divided and understood differently in each. The analysis of the edition's Sanskrit commentary: *hitaṁ karotīti hityam* ("that which does good", with the suffix *yat*); *na gacchantīti agāḥ*, "those that do not move", i.e. mountains, with the suffix *aṇ* giving *āgāḥ*; *sadārāma* is an excellent garden near a house. The translation is tentative ⟨?⟩. The Gosvami edition (GGM) has the same text.

siṣeca tasya sarvāṅgaṁ sarvāṅgīnam ivāśliṣan ||91||*

**Word for word:** *bahudhā* — in many ways; *gauracandraḥ api* — Gaurachandra too; *premṇā* — with love; *ābhāṣya* — having spoken; *vacaḥ* — of words; *amṛtaiḥ* — with the nectar; *siṣeca* — sprinkled; *tasya* — his; *sarva-aṅgam* — whole body; *sarva-aṅgīnam* — [with an embrace] covering the whole body; *iva* — as if; *āśliṣan* — embracing.

**Translation.** Gaurachandra too, speaking to him lovingly in many ways, sprinkled his whole body with the nectar of His words, as if embracing him from head to foot.

### 92

**92.**

```
आज्ञायाज्ञां प्रसादं च कृतकृत्यः स निर्ययौ ।
अमात्यनिचयाः सर्वे ततो द्रष्टुं ययुर्द्रुतम् ॥९२॥
```
*ājñāyājñāṁ prasādaṁ ca kṛta-kṛtyaḥ sa niryayau |
amātya-nicayāḥ sarve tato draṣṭuṁ yayur drutam ||92||*

**Word for word:** *ājñāya* — having received; *ājñām* — the command; *prasādam* — the favour; *ca* — and; *kṛta-kṛtyaḥ* — having accomplished [his] purpose; *saḥ* — he; *niryayau* — departed; *amātya* — of ministers; *nicayāḥ* — the throngs; *sarve* — all; *tataḥ* — then; *draṣṭum* — to see [the Lord]; *yayuḥ* — went; *drutam* — quickly.

**Translation.** Having received [the Lord's] command and favour, his purpose accomplished, he departed; then all the throngs of ministers quickly went to see [the Lord].

**Note.** A play on sounds: *ājñāyājñām*.

---

### 93

**93.**

```
पारेचित्रोत्पलं सोऽकूपारे चित्रोत्पलं यथा ।
यियासोः स्वमतं ज्ञात्वा भूपः सत्पात्रमब्रवीत् ॥९३॥
(पद्मभेदः)
```
*pāre-citrotpalaṁ so 'kūpāre citrotpalaṁ yathā |
yiyāsoḥ svamataṁ jñātvā bhūpaḥ sat-pātram abravīt ||93||
(padma-bhedaḥ)*

**Word for word:** *pāre-citrotpalam* — beyond the Chitrotpala; *saḥ* — he; *akūpāre* — in the ocean; *citra-utpalam yathā* — as [one would go] for many-coloured lotuses; *yiyāsoḥ* — of [the Lord], who wished to go; *svamatam* — the intention; *jñātvā* — having learnt; *bhūpaḥ* — the king; *sat-pātram* — to a worthy person; *abravīt* — said.

**Translation.** Having learnt the intention of [the Lord], who wished to go beyond the Chitrotpala — as [one would go] into the ocean for many-coloured lotuses ⟨?⟩ — he, the king, said to a worthy [servant]:

**Note.** *Padma-bandha* (the edition's subscript: *padma-bhedaḥ*), "the lotus pattern": a figured verse whose syllables, written out in a diagram, form the shape of a lotus (the arrangement itself is not explained ⟨?⟩). The first line also contains the near-repetition *pāre-citrotpalam / akūpāre citrotpalam* (*yamaka*). The Chitrotpala is a river (a branch of the Mahanadi; cf. 94). The edition's Sanskrit commentary: *pāre-citrotpalam* is an *avyayībhāva* compound under the rule "*pāre* and *madhye* [are compounded] with a genitive"; *akūpāra* means "ocean" (according to the Amarakosha). The point of the simile is not clear ⟨?⟩.

### 94

**94.**

```
मङ्गराज भवानेव हरिचन्दनसंगतः ।
पारेमहानदि महाप्रभुमन्वेतु सत्वरम् ॥९४॥
```
*maṅgarāja bhavān eva haricandana-saṁgataḥ |
pāre-mahānadi mahāprabhum anvetu satvaram ||94||*

**Word for word:** *maṅgarāja* — O Mangaraja; *bhavān* — your honour; *eva* — indeed; *haricandana* — Harichandana; *saṁgataḥ* — together with; *pāre-mahānadi* — beyond the Mahanadi; *mahāprabhum* — Mahaprabhu; *anvetu* — should escort; *satvaram* — quickly.

**Translation.** "O Mangaraja, you yourself, together with Harichandana, should quickly escort Mahaprabhu beyond the Mahanadi."

**Note.** The edition has *bhavānena*; the Gosvami edition (GGM) reads *bhavān eva*, and this reading is adopted. Mangaraja and Harichandana are officers of the king; cf. the Chaitanya-charitamrita, Madhya 16.113, 126: the king entrusts Harichandana and Mangaraja with the service of the Lord, and they follow Him together with Ramananda.

### 95

**95.**

```
तदाज्ञयाथ ते सोऽपि श्रीरामानन्दरायकः ।
नौकाः सुमहतीश्चक्रे प्रभुं चाथ समानयन् ॥९५॥
```
*tad-ājñayātha te so 'pi śrī-rāmānanda-rāyakaḥ |
naukāḥ sumahatīś cakre prabhuṁ cātha samānayan ||95||*

**Word for word:** *tat-ājñayā* — by his command; *atha* — then; *te* — they; *saḥ api* — he too; *śrī-rāmānanda-rāyakaḥ* — Sri Ramananda Raya; *naukāḥ* — boats; *su-mahatīḥ* — very large; *cakre* — prepared; *prabhum* — the Lord; *ca* — and; *atha* — then; *samānayan* — brought across.

**Translation.** By his command they — and Sri Ramananda Raya too — made ready very large boats and then brought the Lord across.

**Note.** *Cakre*, "prepared", is singular (with Ramananda), while *samānayan*, "brought", is plural.

### 96

**96.**

```
उदियाय तदा पूर्णो भगवान् मृगलाञ्छनः ।
करैः सम्मार्जयामास पन्थानमखिलं ततः ॥९६॥
```
*udiyāya tadā pūrṇo bhagavān mṛga-lāñchanaḥ |
karaiḥ sammārjayāmāsa panthānam akhilaṁ tataḥ ||96||*

**Word for word:** *udiyāya* — rose; *tadā* — then; *pūrṇaḥ* — full; *bhagavān* — the divine; *mṛga* — with a deer; *lāñchanaḥ* — marked (the moon); *karaiḥ* — with [his] rays (= hands); *sammārjayāmāsa* — swept clean; *panthānam* — the road; *akhilam* — the whole; *tataḥ* — then.

**Translation.** Then the divine full moon, marked with a deer, rose, and with his rays — his hands — swept the whole road clean.

**Note.** *Kara* means both "ray" and "hand".

### 97

**97.**

```
ततो गच्छति गौराङ्गे राजकीयस्तदागतः ।
तत्रत्यांस्तत्र निर्णीय तदाज्ञां निजगाद सः ॥९७॥
```
*tato gacchati gaurāṅge rājakīyas tadāgataḥ |
tatratyāṁs tatra nirṇīya tad-ājñāṁ nijagāda saḥ ||97||*

**Word for word:** *tataḥ* — then; *gacchati* — as [He] was going; *gaurāṅge* — Gauranga; *rājakīyaḥ* — a royal [officer]; *tadā* — then; *āgataḥ* — having come; *tatratyān* — to the local [people]; *tatra* — there; *nirṇīya* — having determined [the place] ⟨?⟩; *tat* — his (the king's); *ājñām* — command; *nijagāda* — announced; *saḥ* — he.

**Translation.** Then, as Gauranga was going on, a royal officer who had come at that time determined [the place] there ⟨?⟩ and announced the king's command to the local people:

### 98

**98.**

```
आज्ञापयति देवो यच्छ्रूयतां तन्महोत्तमाः ।
आरोप्योऽत्र स्तम्भ एको येन तीर्थं भवेदिदम् ॥९८॥
```
*ājñāpayati devo yac chrūyatāṁ tan mahottamāḥ |
āropyo 'tra stambha eko yena tīrthaṁ bhaved idam ||98||*

**Word for word:** *ājñāpayati* — commands; *devaḥ* — His Majesty; *yat* — what; *śrūyatām* — let it be heard; *tat* — that; *mahā-uttamāḥ* — O most excellent ones; *āropyaḥ* — is to be erected; *atra* — here; *stambhaḥ* — a pillar; *ekaḥ* — one; *yena* — by which; *tīrtham* — a holy place; *bhavet* — may become; *idam* — this.

**Translation.** "Hear, O most excellent ones, what His Majesty commands: a pillar is to be erected here, so that this [place] may become a holy place."

**Note.** Cf. the Chaitanya-charitamrita, Madhya 16.115: Prataparudra orders a memorial column to be set up at every place where the Lord bathes or crosses a river.

### 99

**99.**

```
इति श्रुत्वा नृपाज्ञां ते स्तम्भमारोप्य तत्र च ।
नौकामारोप्यो मुदिताः प्रभुं हर्षादुपासत ॥९९॥
```
*iti śrutvā nṛpājñāṁ te stambham āropya tatra ca |
naukām āropyo (āropya) muditāḥ prabhuṁ harṣād upāsata ||99||*

**Word for word:** *iti* — thus; *śrutvā* — having heard; *nṛpa* — of the king; *ājñām* — the command; *te* — they; *stambham* — a pillar; *āropya* — having erected; *tatra* — there; *ca* — and; *naukām* — onto a boat; *āropyo* (read *āropya*) — having placed [Him]; *muditāḥ* — glad; *prabhum* — the Lord; *harṣāt* — with joy; *upāsata* — served.

**Translation.** Hearing the king's command thus, they erected a pillar there and, placing [the Lord] in a boat, gladly served the Lord with joy.

**Note.** The edition has *āropyo*, a misprint for *āropya*. The play *āropya* — "having erected" (the pillar) / "having placed" (the Lord in the boat).

### 100

**100.**

```
इत्थं पारेनदि स तु चतुर्द्वारमागत्य तैस्तै-
रात्रौ चन्द्रातपमधुरिमव्यावृतायां समन्तात् ।
स्वापं चक्रे प्रभुरथ जगन्नाथसन्मण्डपान्त-
र्लोकैर्लक्षावधिभिरपि तु स्थानमेवात्र नापे ॥१००॥
```
*itthaṁ pāre-nadi sa tu catur-dvāram āgatya tais tai
rātrau candrātapa-madhurima-vyāvṛtāyāṁ samantāt |
svāpaṁ cakre prabhur atha jagannātha-san-maṇḍapāntar
lokair lakṣāvadhibhir api tu sthānam evātra nāpe ||100||*

**Word for word:** *ittham* — thus; *pāre-nadi* — beyond the river; *saḥ tu* — He; *catur-dvāram* — to Chaturdvara; *āgatya* — having come; *taiḥ taiḥ* — with all those [companions]; *rātrau* — in the night; *candra* — of the moon; *ātapa* — of the light; *madhurima* — by the sweetness; *vyāvṛtāyām* — enveloped; *samantāt* — on all sides; *svāpam* — sleep; *cakre* — took; *prabhuḥ* — the Lord; *atha* — then; *jagannātha* — of Jagannatha; *sat* — of the fine; *maṇḍapa* — pavilion; *antaḥ* — inside; *lokaiḥ* — by the people; *lakṣa* — a hundred thousand; *avadhibhiḥ* — numbering up to; *api tu* — but; *sthānam eva* — even a place; *atra* — there; *na āpe* — was not found.

**Translation.** Thus, having crossed the river and come to Chaturdvara with all those [companions], the Lord then slept, in a night enveloped on all sides by the sweetness of the moonlight, inside the fine pavilion of Jagannatha; but the people, up to a hundred thousand [in number], could not find even a place there.

**Note.** Metre — *mandākrāntā* (————⏑⏑⏑⏑⏑——⏑——⏑——; 17 syllables per pada); the verse scans correctly. Chaturdvara is a village on the northern bank of the Mahanadi opposite Kataka (cf. the Chaitanya-charitamrita, Madhya 16.116, 122: the king has new houses built there, and on the night of the full moon the Lord crosses to Chaturdvara in a new boat).
