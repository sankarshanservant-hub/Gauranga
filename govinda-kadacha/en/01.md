# Part 1

*Navadvipa. Govinda comes to Mahaprabhu; the decision to take sannyasa; the night of departure (pp. 1–20)*

*[p. 1]*

**1.** My home is in Kanchananagar, in Burdwan.
My father's name is Shyamadasa; my name is Govinda.

**2.** By caste I am a smith: I forge weapons, ladles and fetters.
My mother's name was Madhavi.

**3.** My wife's name is Shashimukhi.
One day, quarrelling, she spoke harshly to me —

**4.** she abused me as a worthless fool.
Stung by that insult, I left home at dawn.

**5.** I set out in the Shaka year 1430,[^1]
seething with wounded pride, and never looked back.

**6.** Step by step I reached Katwa,
and there I heard the name of Sri Chaitanya.

**7.** Everyone was praising Chaitanya,
and hearing it I hurried off to see him.

*[p. 2]*

**8.** All day I walked across the fields,
and at dawn I crossed over and came to the landing of Nadia.

**9.** Below Nadia, on the Ganga, is a landing called Mishraghat.
My joy grew as I beheld the capital of Nadia.[^2]

**10.** On the right the river Vagdevi murmurs along,
as if singing for the joy of all.

**11.** Above the landing is the courtyard of Srivasa,
and near it a huge tank.

**12.** Close by stands the palace of King Ballala;
it lies in ruins, yet its traces still remain.

**13.** I sat on the landing, lost in thought for a long while,
and just then Sri Chaitanya came to bathe.

**14.** A gamchha[^3] tied at his waist, his frame finely built,
and with him a joyful Avadhuta.

**15.** Three or four more companions came along, dancing;
the Lord went down into the bosom of the Ganga to bathe.

**16.** The heroic Avadhuta leapt in from the bank
and began to sport in the water, swimming.

**17.** Behind them came Srivasa Thakura and Damodara,
Siddha Haridasa, and on the left Gadadhara.

**18.** Last of all came Advaita Gosai.
Never had I seen anyone so majestic.

*[p. 3]*

**19.** White hair and grey beard, wonderfully winning;
his beard reached below his chest.

**20.** The old man swam, shouting the name of Hari,
while the Avadhuta swam back and forth across the river.

**21.** One by one they all plunged into the Ganga
and, swimming, began all sorts of games.

**22.** But I gazed at the Lord's wondrous form,
enchanted by its radiance.

**23.** Having bathed, Gora-chand[^4] came up onto the bank,
his curling locks tumbling down his back.

**24.** His body was the colour of pure gold,
his eyes long like the petals of a blue lotus.

**25.** Lovely cheeks, a broad forehead;
even his ordinary walk looked like a dancer's step.

**26.** His charming thighs outshine plantain stems,
and his two lovely brows seem drawn with a brush.

**27.** His two feet look as though painted with alta;[^5]
whoever sees them, even a sage's eyes are entranced.

**28.** His body full of love, "Hari bol!" on his lips,
he embraces whomever he favours with mercy.

**29.** My Gora says "Hari" and sheds tears;
the tears spurt out like jets from a squirt-gun.

*[p. 4]*

**30.** Then my Gora Raya set off,
and the Avadhuta Nityananda ran after him.

**31.** A fisherman told me who they were,
and one by one I came to know them all.

**32.** As I watched their water-play with my own eyes,
an ocean of feeling surged up in my heart.

**33.** People said God had come to Shachi's house,
and that is why my heart was so eager to see him.

**34.** By good fortune the quarrel at home was only a pretext;
that is why I hastened to Navadvipa.

**35.** Sitting on the landing, I watched this lila,
and who knows what feeling arose in me.

**36.** My body bristled like a kadamba flower,
and I began to tremble all over.

**37.** I broke into a sweat and my cloth grew wet;
I longed to wash his feet with my tears.

**38.** With curling locks down his back and a smiling face,
the Lord came on, surrounded by his companions.

**39.** As luck would have it, the Lord noticed me
and glanced at me sidelong again and again.

**40.** And when he came slowly nearer,
I fell to the ground and clasped his feet.

*[p. 5]*

**41.** I rolled at his feet,
and merciful Nimai took my hand and sat me up.

**42.** I sat before him with folded hands, weeping,
and smiling and laughing he asked me a word or two.

**43.** Nectar flowed unceasingly in his smile,
and the fragrance of his body enchanted my heart.

**44.** Ever intoxicated with the Name of Hari, very slender;
so many devotees were rolling at his feet.

**45.** How can I describe what that feeling was?
It seemed that millions of gods came to fall at his feet.

**46.** When the Lord stands in a dark room,
the lustre of his body dispels the darkness.

**47.** What creator, sitting in solitude,
must have moulded him by kneading the moon with streams of nectar?

**48.** Whoever sees this form with his own eyes,
detachment from worldly things follows at his heels.

**49.** Smiling, he talked with me
and kindly asked my name.

**50.** The Lord said: "What is your caste, what is your name,
what trade do you follow, where is your home?"

**51.** Hearing the Lord's words, I folded my hands
and, forgetting myself, began to speak.

*[p. 6]*

**52.** "O merciful one, why such kindness to me?
The name of this lowly one is Govinda Dasa.

**53.** I was a householder, working at many things,
but now I have become a beggar on the road.

**54.** I have left worldly life to see the Lord;
now give me a place at your crimson feet.

**55.** My home is in Kanchananagar, in Burdwan;
my father's name is Shyamadasa Karmakar."

**56.** Hearing this, the Lord said to me:
"Stay, Govinda, in my house.

**57.** In my house you will be looked after,
and every day you will joyfully sing the sankirtana of the Name.

**58.** Every day you will happily receive Krishna's prasada,
and every wish of your heart will be fulfilled.

**59.** You will always be engaged in service:
bring Ganga water and tulasi.

**60.** Every day you will eat prasada to your fill —
juicy greens, shukta, and ghanta of banana flower."[^6]

**61.** Saying this, the Lord wanted to take me with him,
and at once I went into the Lord's household.

**62.** The house stands above the Ganga, most charming:
five handsome thatched huts.

*[p. 7]*

**63.** The Lord lives at the southern edge of the town,
intoxicated with the Name of Hari, always rejoicing.

**64.** Near it is a huge tank,
which some call the Ballala-sagara.

**65.** Let me now name, one by one,
the devotees who are always with the Lord:

**66.** Advaita Acharya, and also Svarupa and Srivasa;
the Acharya's two sons, Achyuta and Krishnadasa;[^7]

**67.** Mukunda, Murari Gupta and Gadadhara;
Narahari, Vidyanidhi, Shekhara, Shridhara;

**68.** and two or four more intimate devotees
with whom he worships in secret.

**69.** The Avadhuta Nityananda is like a madman,
rolling on the ground and shedding tears without end.

**70.** Shachi-devi is gentleness itself, very short of stature;
she is forever calling out, "Nimai! Nimai!"

**71.** Vishnupriya-devi is the Lord's wife,
busy day and night with his service.

**72.** Modest, humble, soft and gentle of speech;
I became the servant of her feet.

**73.** So I lived as a servant in Shachi's house,
finishing every task before I was asked.

*[p. 8]*

**74.** I am an expert eater and eat with delight;
after doing the Lord's work I go about with him.

**75.** Every day bhoga is offered in the Vishnu temple:
so many fruits and roots, curd-cheese, butter, cream and thickened milk!

**76.** Greens, dal, yogurt, shukta, modakas, payasa,
vadas, laddus and sweets of every kind — delicious to eat.

**77.** Every day Mother Shachi does the cooking,
and everyone joyfully honours the prasada.

**78.** I, this servant, am the crown-jewel of gluttons;
all year round I eat from the merciful Lord's plate.

**79.** What can I say? Prasada has no equal;
a single grain of it is like nectar.

**80.** Thus I stayed in the Lord's house,
and everyone was kind to me as Chaitanya's servant.

**81.** My Lord is Chaitanya Gosai;
wherever and whenever he goes, I go with him.

**82.** His heart is always restless with love for Krishna;
when he hears Krishna's name, tears stream down.

**83.** If anyone calls out "Radhe!" aloud,
at once his tears pour down in torrents.

**84.** If someone happens to call "Pranakrishna!",[^8]
he runs to him and embraces him.

*[p. 9]*

**85.** One evening, in Srivasa's courtyard,
the Lord was sitting with his devotees.

**86.** Just then my Avadhuta Raya
began to cry out again and again, "Yamuna!

**87.** Here is the site of the rasa, the landing of the Yamuna!
Where are the hundreds of gopis, where is that dance?"

**88.** Hearing Nitai's words, the Lord rushed off
and leapt into the Ballala-sagara.

**89.** Overwhelmed with feeling, the Lord swam,
while all the devotees stood on the bank watching.

**90.** Thus his longing grew day by day,
and under the weight of prema his body grew thin.

**91.** But merciful Chaitanya was not satisfied with this.
He said, "I will take sannyasa and teach the living beings.

**92.** With a straw between my teeth[^9] I will go round all the villages
and deliver every living being by giving the Name of Hari.

**93.** Abandoning household life, I will go to the town of Katwa;
my guru Keshava Bharati will set me free.

**94.** I will not stay at home; I will take sannyasa —
otherwise how shall I deliver all beings?"

**95.** The Lord wished to take sannyasa;
hearing it, I felt a great pain in my heart.

*[p. 10]*

**96.** The Lord called the Avadhuta and said:
"I shall take sannyasa — do not stop me.

**97.** At the holy time, in the month of Magha, on the sun's northern course,[^10]
I shall take sannyasa. Keep this secret.

**98.** Tell this to Mukunda and Gadadhara,
but do not proclaim it everywhere.

**99.** Tell my mother only by a hint,
and do not announce it among the devotees."

**100.** I, the servant who was with him, heard it all,
and my heart seemed to split in two.

**101.** Alas, alas! This grief cannot be borne —
my Lord Gora Raya will take sannyasa!

**102.** Gora will go away to take sannyasa!
Let my heart burst into a hundred pieces!

**103.** I am lighter than a straw; what does it matter to me?
And yet let a hundred thunderbolts fall on my head!

**104.** How shall I bear the pain of separation from the Lord?
How shall I pass my days without Chaitanya?

**105.** After that the Lord himself rose
and went to Mukunda.

**106.** I followed him weeping,
unable to see the road for my tears.

*[p. 11]*

**107.** Calling Mukunda, he said:
"I shall accept the staff and the water-pot.[^11]

**108.** Giving up my shikha and sacred thread, I shall take sannyasa;
if I do not, how shall I deliver the living beings?"

**109.** Hearing these words, the worthy Mukunda
flooded his broad chest with tears.

**110.** Mukunda collapsed to the ground,
and the Lord took him by the hand, raised him and sat him down.

**111.** "My life is leaving me — what have you told me, O merciful one?
Hearing these words, this wretch's heart is breaking.

**112.** Go on distributing the Name of Hari a while longer,
and then, Lord, renounce the world and take sannyasa."

**113.** Hearing this, the Lord hurried to Gadadhara
and told him everything openly.

**114.** Hearing the words, Gadadhara cried out,
as though the sky had fallen on his head.

**115.** Gadadhara rolled on the ground,
and his body turned red with streams of blood.

**116.** "What have you told me?" said Gadadhara, rising and sitting up.
"Your … [illegible] heart.

**117.** Bring me poison, I will drink it at once!
Oh, how shall I bear separation from the Lord?

*[p. 12]*

**118.** If millions of scorpions were to sting me,
that pain would be trifling compared with this.

**119.** If my beloved Nimai renounces everything,
I shall follow him in love.

**120.** When Murari and the other devotees hear of this,
they will fall senseless wherever they are.

**121.** If Chaitanya leaves us, what use is the body?
Let a thunderbolt fall on our heads this very moment!"

**122.** Then Gadadhara clasped his feet
and, shedding tears, began to say:

**123.** "When your mother hears this,
how will she keep the life in her body?"

**124.** Afterwards, as the news passed from ear to ear,
alas, the very life of the Vaishnavas seemed to fly away.

**125.** One said, "Millions of scorpions are stinging me!"
Another said, "My life is burning in fire!"

**126.** Some fell like felled trees,
others, with clenched teeth, collapsed senseless.

**127.** Hearing all this, my Vishvambhara
began to reason with everyone at length.

**128.** The Lord hastened to the Vaishnavas
and consoled them all with sweet words.

*[p. 13]*

**129.** Then Shachi-devi heard this news
and, sensing disaster, fell unconscious.

**130.** Beating her breast, Shachi wept aloud,
her tears falling on her breast.

**131.** "Oh Nimai, where will you go, my son?"
Hearing her lament, even beasts and birds wept.

**132.** Then the Avadhuta came into the Lord's courtyard
and told this to Shachi.

**133.** Like a thunderbolt the words pierced Shachi's heart,
and at once she fell crashing to the ground.

**134.** Striking her breast she wept loudly,
and the earth turned to mud with the stream of her tears.

**135.** Vishnupriya, hearing the whispered news,
sat down at once, her hands on her head.

**136.** Tears streamed down over her breast,
as if an ocean of grief had surged up.

**137.** But Gora paid no heed to her
and went off to Srivasa's courtyard.

**138.** There, in Srivasa's house, the Lord began
a great sankirtana and lost outer consciousness.

**139.** Intoxicated with kirtana, Gora began to dance,
and his cloth slipped off.

*[p. 14]*

**140.** His body became like a kadamba flower;[^12]
my hero of dharma danced, unconscious of himself.

**141.** Streams of blood oozed from his pores,
his body bruised from his falls.

**142.** As he danced he cried, "O Vanamali!"
and the devotees danced with him, clapping their hands.

**143.** On the sankranti of Pausha,[^13] at dusk,
the Lord came back to his own home.

**144.** Many people kept coming and going,
and Mother Shachi's grief welled up in waves.

**145.** He consoled his mother with sweet words
and then showed himself in the kitchen.

**146.** The second watch of the night passed;
the Lord ate and lay down to sleep.

**147.** I too went to my own place and lay down,
but at the Lord's order I stayed awake.

**148.** In the last part of the night the merciful Lord
came out, called me and said:

**149.** "Be ready and wait here;
I will go and take leave at my mother's feet."

**150.** So saying he went into the inner apartments,
then came out again and called me.

*[p. 15]*

**151.** Urgently he said, "Come with me.
Let us go to Katwa to cut the bonds.

**152.** Do not tell this to anyone and everyone:
I will take sannyasa and deliver the living beings.

**153.** Selfish and wicked, they drink wine and eat meat —
tell me, what will become of the people of Kali?

**154.** Devoted to belly and genitals, without any discipline,
they lie incessantly for the sake of money.

**155.** Worms of lust, they lap the spittle of women's mouths
and throw away the stream of the nectar of bhakti.

**156.** They relish the food of harlots and obey harlots,
absorbed in gold, women, arts and amorous play.

**157.** Therefore I will give up my shikha and thread
and go from door to door giving the Name of Hari.

**158.** The great mantra of the Name requires no initiation;
I will preach that Name on roads and at landings.

**159.** Chandalas, youths, householders, children, the old and women,
drunk with the Name, will stand in rows.

**160.** Boys will say "Hari", and girls will say it too;
even the godless and the Aghorapanthis will be drunk with the Name.

**161.** The banner of the Name will fly, piercing the sky;
king and subjects together will roll on the ground.

*[p. 16]*

**162.** If I do not take sannyasa and put on the kaupina,
how shall I deliver the sinful, the afflicted and the wretched?

**163.** Seeing the miserable state of the people of Kali,
I can stay no longer; my heart trembles.

**164.** Taking water-pot and kaupina I will become a sannyasi
and deliver everyone by giving the names of Radha and Krishna.

**165.** For those who are the worst sinners and sufferers
my heart is always weeping in anguish."

**166.** As he was speaking with me thus,
Shachi-devi appeared.

**167.** Shachi-devi came hurrying out
and stood before him with arms outstretched.

**168.** Then, clasping his mother's feet,
the Lord took leave and set off.

**169.** His mother stood in the doorway weeping,
and I followed behind, carrying his wooden sandals.

**170.** Shachi stood like a wooden doll,
tears pouring down in torrents.

**171.** Then, going out through the door,
my hero of dharma crossed the Ganga.

**172.** Having crossed, the Lord went on to Kantaka-nagara,[^14]
and I followed behind to serve him.

*[p. 17]*

**173.** All the wonderful lilas I am able to witness
I record in my karcha as far as my powers allow.[^15]

**174.** At dusk we reached Kantaka-nagara;
in every house gongs, conches and bells were sounding.

**175.** Then, during the night, Mukunda, Shekhara,
the Avadhuta, Brahmananda and Gadadhara,

**176.** the teacher Gangadasa and the singer Shivai —
one after another they all appeared.

**177.** In the depth of the night Gora, crying "Hari!",
began to dance, mad with love.

**178.** Hundreds of thousands of people came to see him,
and all were amazed at his devotion to Krishna.

**179.** Some threw flowers at him, some offered garlands;
the radiance of the Lord's body lit up every direction.

**180.** Millions of Cupids cannot compare with his beauty;
his waist is slimmer than the middle of a damaru drum.

**181.** Wherever his large eyes are turned,
blue lotuses seem to rain down there.

**182.** Arms reaching to his knees, very tall;
with a straw in his teeth, Gora begs for servitude to God.

**183.** Thus the night passed in dance and song.
Seeing the crowds, Gora began to speak:

*[p. 18]*

**184.** "Brothers, listen attentively to my words:
there is no difference at all between Krishna and Krishna's name.

**185.** Worship Krishna, think of Krishna, say Krishna's name;
by the power of the Name, brothers, you will reach the eternal abode.

**186.** All that you see here is false,
a mere shadow of prakriti — so say the Vedas.

**187.** Your cherished body will be left lying
when Yama comes and squeezes your throat.

**188.** There is no difference between a bedstead and a pallet on the ground;
those who see difference are the ones who suffer.

**189.** Whoever grows proud on getting wealth
is a worm of hell — such is the verdict of scripture.

**190.** Between king and pauper there is no difference at all;
it is ignorance, brothers, that creates the sense of difference.

**191.** A handful of rice fills a king's belly,
and with that same handful the poor man is content.

**192.** The penniless man sleeps happily on the bare ground,
while the king has no sleep on his priceless bed.

**193.** The king does not eat gold, diamonds, emeralds or pearls,
yet, drunk with wealth, he does not think of the Lord of all.

**194.** As the poor man will die at the hour of death,
so will the landlord go to the house of Yama.

*[p. 19]*

**195.** Over the king's eyes the blinkers of maya are tied;
he is always blind, like an oil-mill bullock.

**196.** The oil-mill bullock walks round and round in one place;
in millions of years its road never ends.

**197.** When the soul-bird flies away, the body will remain inert,
rattling like a broken cage.

**198.** The pampered body will rot and decay,
and jackals and dogs will eat their fill of it.

**199.** Drunk with pride and sunk in worldly life,
man dies brooding, crying "my property, my property".

**200.** Daughters, sons, mansions, tanks and gardens,
women, gold and the like rob him of his senses.

**201.** Whose daughter, whose son, whose husband?
Give up all inert things and fix your mind on Krishna.

**202.** False are sons, false are daughters, false are wealth and grain;
the one true reality is Chaitanya.[^16]

**203.** What more can I say about the rotten life of the householder?
He is worn to shreds, sunk in sons, daughters and riches.

**204.** As his property grows, so do his schemes,
and he suffers endless torment like a worm in filth.

**205.** Everywhere Krishna's form is shining,
but only one whose eyes are pure can see it.

*[p. 20]*

**206.** The fool attached to worldly things sees with fleshly eyes;
the eternally liberated devotee sees with the eye of divine knowledge.

**207.** How can one whose eyes are blinded by the dust of worldliness
manage to see subtle truth?

**208.** Everyone talks of love, love — but who knows what love is?
Is the essence of love the service of a woman?

**209.** When you know that man and woman are not different,
then the truth of love will surely dawn on you.

**210.** If love exists for the sake of offspring,
then there is no truth of love in it at all.

**211.** But if one feels in one's heart, 'I exist for God',
that the great souls certainly call love.

**212.** Hear attentively the truth of pure love,
whose slightest wave soothes a scorched heart.

**213.** Like the longing of a young woman on seeing a young man —
think as I may, I see no other longing like it.

**214.** That is why devotees worship the Lord of the Yadus,
fixing their minds on him in the mood of a wife.

**215.** But in one who longs for Atmarama,[^17]
can lust remain in the heart?

**216.** As darkness cannot remain near light,
so lust is burnt to ashes near Krishna."

### Translator's notes

[^1]: Shaka 1430 corresponds to 1508–09 CE. The traditional date of Chaitanya's sannyasa is the month of Magha, Shaka 1431 (January–February 1510).
[^2]: *Pat* is an old word for a capital (*raj-pat*). Mishraghat, the river Vagdevi and Ballala's tank (Ballala-sagara, Ballal Dighi) are local Navadvipa place-names.
[^3]: A *gamchha* is a thin cotton towel worn as a wrap.
[^4]: *Gora-chand*, "the golden moon", an affectionate name of Chaitanya.
[^5]: *Alta*: the red dye with which Bengali women paint the edges of their feet.
[^6]: *Shukta* is a slightly bitter Bengali vegetable stew; *ghanta* (*ghonto*) is a mixed dish, here made of banana flowers.
[^7]: Achyuta (Achyutananda) and Krishnadasa (Krishna Mishra) were sons of Advaita Acharya; they are central figures of the *Advaita-prakasha*.
[^8]: "Pranakrishna" means "Krishna, my life"; here apparently an ordinary personal name to which Chaitanya responds in ecstasy.
[^9]: Holding a straw between the teeth is a gesture of utmost humility.
[^10]: *Uttarayana*, the "northern course" of the sun after the winter solstice, an auspicious period.
[^11]: The *danda* (staff) and *kamandalu* (water-pot) are the emblems of a sannyasi; the *shikha* (tuft of hair) and sacred thread are given up on taking sannyasa.
[^12]: The kadamba flower is a fluffy ball; the image is of hair standing on end, one of the bodily signs of ecstasy.
[^13]: The sankranti of Pausha (Makara-sankranti) is the last day of the month of Pausha, in mid-January.
[^14]: Kantaka-nagara is the old name of Katwa.
[^15]: A key statement for the whole book: the *karcha* (*kadacha*) is a set of notes, a diary, which the author claims to keep as events unfold.
[^16]: A play on words: *chaitanya* means "consciousness, spirit", and is also Chaitanya's name.
[^17]: *Atmarama*, "he who delights in himself", an epithet of Krishna.
