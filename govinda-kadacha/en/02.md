# Part 2

*Katwa: the sannyasa. Shantipur. Burdwan and Shashimukhi. On through Medinipur and Narayangarh (pp. 21–40)*

*[p. 21]*

**217.** Only the longing of love remains —
thus I have shown you where love is to be found.

**218.** Now strive with all your heart for love:
you will be fulfilled, and the torments of worldly life will go.

**219.** Through quarrels, disputes, hatred and lies
worldly life becomes a hell — think on this.

**220.** For money the householder tells lies,
cheats, and even kills wherever he can.

**221.** What more can I say of the householder's rotten life?
He is worn out by sons, daughters, possessions and wealth.

**222.** You never think whose you are and who is yours;
for the sake of an inert lump of flesh you are busy earning.

**223.** Death will surely come, there are no two ways about it,
yet you keep thinking you will live forever.

**224.** However many shastras you have read, combing every detail,
by reading you have all become nothing but great fools.

**225.** The more learning and cleverness, the more selfishness;
the more you read, the dirtier the heart becomes.

**226.** With your lips you call another's wife "mother",
but find her alone and at once you are blinded by lust.

**227.** Lust and anger are enemies when it comes to others,
but when it comes to yourself they are your friends.

*[p. 22]*

**228.** All these are the boundless torments of hell;
think about it in your heart, even once.

**229.** Until devotion to God arises,
so long will you remain in such a hell.

**230.** A small self-interest you can give up,
but millions of coins can lead you astray.

**231.** For the people of Kali the essence is the Name of Hari alone;
take that Name and go to the eternal abode.

**232.** Always say "Hari bol!" with thrilling joy;
why make such a commotion in the bazaar of Kali?

**233.** What difference is there between a mansion and a hut?
Go and ask the learned, brothers.

**234.** As a king sleeps on a bedstead in his palace,
so the poor man sleeps in his hut.

**235.** The king drinks water from a golden cup,
the hut-dweller drinks from an earthen pot.

**236.** The aim of both is the same — to quench thirst;
the king's golden cup is mere illusion.

**237.** Does the king eat dal of pearls and curry of gems?
Does he cook his rice out of diamonds?

**238.** Drunk with pride, the king cannot see it,
and so he comes and goes again and again."

*[p. 23]*

**239.** Thus Chaitanya Gosai taught,
and great crowds gathered in one place.

**240.** Seated beneath a bilva tree in Kantaka-nagara,
he gave many teachings in a loud voice.

**241.** The words of his holy mouth are the essence of Vedanta;
whoever hears them is freed from worldly existence.

**242.** Thus a day and a night went by.
The next morning the Lord bathed.

**243.** The women wept, pressing the ends of their saris to their eyes,
their tears pouring down in torrents.

**244.** One said: "May every misfortune that threatens such beauty fall on me![^18]
How will his mother stay alive?

**245.** Here the pride of millions of Cupids is humbled;
never have I seen such beautiful hair.

**246.** How lovely is his spotless chin!
His lotus eyes outshine the blue lotus.

**247.** Never have I seen such a wondrous form.
How will Nimai bear the kaupina and the staff?

**248.** Keshava Thakura[^19] must be made of stone —
how can he shave off his hair? How cruel!

**249.** Ah, how beautiful is the garland of forest flowers at his throat!
The radiance of his face has lit up every direction."

*[p. 24]*

**250.** So the women said many things.
Meanwhile the Lord quietly called me.

**251.** The Lord said, "Quickly bring all the things that are needed;
I shall shave my head to take sannyasa.

**252.** I will no longer stay at home in bondage;
at home there is the torment of hell at every turn."

**253.** Hearing this, pure-hearted Gadadhara,
the Avadhuta Nityananda and Chandrashekhara

**254.** brought all the requisites fit for sannyasa
and filled the sannyasi's store.

**255.** They called a barber named Deva,
and the barber sat down beneath the bilva tree.

**256.** Then Chaitanya Gosai said to the barber,
"Shave me, Deva — I am going to Vraja."

**257.** Receiving Bharati's order, the barber
sat down close by to shave him.

**258.** When at last the barber put the razor to his hair,
the women all cried out at once.

**259.** The women said, "Barber, do not do this!
Do not shave off such locks of hair!"

**260.** So saying, the women burst into tears,
and then Deva began to shave.

*[p. 25]*

**261.** Thousands upon thousands of people
came flocking to Kantaka-nagara to see the sannyasa.

**262.** At the close of day, his head shaved,
Nimai put on the dress of a sannyasi.

**263.** Staff and water-pot in hand, he put on the kaupina
and covered it with saffron cloth.

**264.** Gosai stood before Bharati,
lighting up every quarter with his beauty — I would give my life for him!

**265.** The Avadhuta, Gadadhara and Gangadasa
one by one stood beside the sannyasi.

**266.** Seeing the Lord's wondrous form, Bharati
bowed inwardly at his lotus feet.

**267.** Inwardly he said, "Gosai, you are God himself;
the whole universe, moving and unmoving, is under you.

**268.** To teach the people you have put on the kaupina;
to show the path of bhakti you have made yourself subject to the lowly."

**269.** In the afternoon the Lord became a sannyasi,
and the women raised the ulu-cry.[^20]

**270.** Creepers, leaves, branches and trees floated in love;
beasts, birds and insects seemed to break into dance.

**271.** Hundreds of thousands showered flowers,
and Kantaka-nagara became the Nandana garden.[^21]

*[p. 26]*

**272.** Bharati gave him the name Sri Krishna Chaitanya.
Hundreds of thousands came and went.

**273.** The respectable married women, filling their palms,
showered puffed rice on the Lord's head.

**274.** The cry of "Hari!" rose, piercing the sky,
and all rolled on the ground, steeped in devotion.

**275.** The Name, bursting through the heavens, wandered the skies,
and all the gods listened, drunk with joy.

**276.** That night my Lord stayed awake
and passed the night dancing with the Name of Hari.

**277.** In the morning the Lord said to Shekhara:
"All of you go home to Nadia.

**278.** Go with Brahmananda to my mother
and tell her that Nimai has taken sannyasa.

**279.** If my mother weeps,
comfort her with words of reassurance."

**280.** Then, with Nityananda and Gadadhara,
he took Bharati and set out in high spirits.

**281.** I followed behind, carrying the wooden sandals.
Chaitanya Gosai was drunk with the wine of the Name.

**282.** Hundreds of thousands followed the Lord,
and many pandits went along to see him.

*[p. 27]*

**283.** Rudradeva, Ramaratna, Jagai Pandita,
Gangadasa, Shambhuchandra, famed in the world,

**284.** Ishana, Shankara, Balarama, Gadadhara,
Chanda-Chandeshvara, crest-jewel of pandits,

**285.** Kashishvara, Nyayaratna and Siddheshvara,
Panchanana the Vedantist and Ratnakara —

**286.** all these great pandits went with him,
while Sri Krishna Chaitanya, drunk with love, went on in delight.

**287.** Absorbed in dance, the Lord ran ahead —
now running, now leaping, now falling to the ground.

**288.** Tears streamed from his eyes,
and Bharati Gosai wept, tasting that love.

**289.** Then, in ecstasy, he went eastwards
and hurried, drunk with feeling, to the Acharya's house.[^22]

**290.** He stayed some time in the Acharya's house,
and meanwhile Mother Shachi came there too.

**291.** Sri Krishna Chaitanya Prabhu bowed
at his mother's feet and spoke to her tenderly.

**292.** After a few words, cutting through the bonds of affection,
he set out southwards, leaving everyone.

**293.** Ishana, Pratapa, Gangadasa, Gadadhara
and Baneshvara went along with the sannyasi.

*[p. 28]*

**294.** When we all reached Burdwan,
I began to wonder what fate had in store for me.

**295.** Looking at me, the Lord began to speak,
as if streams of nectar were flowing out.

**296.** Clapping me on the back, the Lord said,
"Come, Govinda, let us go to your house."

**297.** Hearing this, I started,
and the Lord, laughing, walked on with a swaying gait.

**298.** "When the Lord took sannyasa, I put on the kaupina,
gave up pride and became utterly humble.

**299.** I have no more desire to live a household life;
I will go with the Lord, dancing."

**300.** As we walked along, I folded my hands
and said a few words in reply:

**301.** "I will not go back to Kanchananagar, Lord;
I have given up vile household life like excrement."

**302.** While I was saying this, my wife,
having somehow heard of it, came there in haste.

**303.** Tears were streaming from her eyes,
and she threw herself down at my feet.

**304.** With tears she began to say:
"Come back home, I will go with you.

*[p. 29]*

**305.** Over a trifle you have abandoned the household —
tell me, what provision have you made for your maidservant?

**306.** To whose door shall I go, where shall I beg?
Who will give me alms out of kindness?

**307.** What is in store for me? At whose door
shall I go begging for my belly's sake?"

**308.** Hearing her words, I hung my head
and inwardly kept saying, "Hari, Hari."

**309.** Remembering Hari cuts all bonds;
that is why I fixed my mind on Hari's feet.

**310.** Then merciful Chaitanya, seeing this,
began to speak to her sweet words.

**311.** Hearing the Lord's words, the unhappy woman
began to wet the earth with her tears.

**312.** Distraught with weeping, she looked about her on all sides,
while the Lord tried to make her understand, speaking of the highest truth.

**313.** Hearing those words of the Lord so suddenly,
she covered her eyes with the end of her sari and wept.

**314.** Her weeping moved the Lord to compassion,
and at once he turned to me and said:

**315.** "Govinda, you stay at home;
I will go to Puri with another servant."

*[p. 30]*

**316.** At these words tears poured from my eyes,
and at once I fell, distraught, clasping his feet.

**317.** I washed his two feet with my tears,
and at once the Lord turned and went on.

**318.** Then my neighbours gathered together
and began to talk to me, trying to win me over.

**319.** But I began to speak of the poison of worldly life
and to expound the hidden nature of woman:

**320.** "Listen, brothers, to what I say of woman:
the woman and her lover are one and the same life.

**321.** If now you all turn your gaze to the portion of the Self,
you will see that woman and lover are all one.

**322.** Those who, thinking it sweeter than nectar,
drink a woman's spittle with their eyes closed,

**323.** forgetting eternal bliss and delighting in that —
shame on such a wretch; his birth is wasted.

**324.** My Gauranga is the complete Brahman, the eternal;
shall I abandon his company and take up household life?"

**325.** Saying this, we crossed the Damodar
and went on to the house of Kashi Mitra.[^23]

**326.** Kashi Mitra is a virtuous man,
and the Lord went and stayed in his house.

*[p. 31]*

**327.** For the offering Mitra gave good rice,
and the Lord, seeing the rice, praised it.

**328.** The Lord said, "This rice is very fine.
By what name do people call it?"

**329.** Mitra said, "Its name is Jagannatha-bhoga;
when it is offered, every wish is fulfilled."

**330.** Hearing "Jagannatha-bhoga", the Lord started,
and at once streams of love began to flow.

**331.** Weeping, he said, "Ah, Jagannatha!
Quickly draw me to you!"

**332.** Having cooked greens, dal and various dishes,
the Lord, rejoicing, put them all together.

**333.** The smell of the betho greens[^24] filled the air,
and before the offering was even made my mind was restless.

**334.** The Lord said, "Quickly bring tulasi;
we will offer the bhoga and I will give you prasada to your heart's content.

**335.** You are very hungry, my child;
that is why you keep looking this way and that."

**336.** I was deeply ashamed at the Lord's words
and stood there with my head hung down.

**337.** He made the offering and distributed the prasada,
and my heart rejoiced at the gravy of the shukta.

*[p. 32]*

**338.** I happily ate half a fried bitter gourd,
lifting big mouthfuls into my mouth.

**339.** Sour chutney[^25] with molasses — like nectar;
how much I ate, my face beaming with joy!

**340.** In the afternoon Gora-chand left Mitra's house
and hastened southwards — a snare of love.

**341.** In due course we reached the village of Hajipur,
and there the Lord intoxicated everyone with the Name of Hari.

**342.** Outside the village is a huge banyan tree,
and there we slowly settled down.

**343.** In the evening the Lord began sankirtana,
and the Name rose up into the sky, piercing the heavens.

**344.** The Lord began to dance and intoxicated the whole countryside —
where were his kaupina and waist-string? His dress in disarray.

**345.** Now he falls crashing to the ground,
now, foaming at the mouth, rolls this way and that.

**346.** Hundreds of people gathered there,
and all became intoxicated with the sankirtana of the Name.

**347.** All the men and women together grew drunk with the Name.
Blessed is the power of the Name — I would give my life for it!

**348.** Boys and girls, old men, young men and young women
clapped their hands and danced with devotion.

*[p. 33]*

**349.** Half the night passed in this way,
and then Hari cooked the rice received as alms.

**350.** A village devotee brought ghee,
and with the ghee my Lord fried bitter gourd.

**351.** Shukta with neem, ghee and fried bitter gourd —
my king of Nadia offered them as bhoga.

**352.** Gora-Hari took only a little prasada,
and then I sat down with a leaf-plate.

**353.** I ate the prasada puffing and panting,
*[the second line of the couplet is missing in the edition]*

**354.** and when my belly swelled up,
I went and took refuge at the Lord's feet.

**355.** Then the Lord passed his hand over my belly,
and at once it went back to normal.

**356.** Then I raised the cry "Hari, Hari!",
and the devotees started up in surprise.

**357.** Next morning beautiful Gauranga rose,
called the devotees and spoke with them at length.

**358.** Having reasoned with the devotees, he took his leave
and set off quickly with his companions.

**359.** When he drew near Medinipur,
people heard the news and came running.

*[p. 34]*

**360.** Among them a rich man came up
and was struck dumb on seeing the Lord's form.

**361.** His name was Keshava Samanta, a very wealthy man.
With much guile the rich man talked of many things.

**362.** Now he would say with a laugh, "O best of sannyasis,
take some money, as much as your heart desires.

**363.** Throw off the kaupina and put on proper clothes —
why should a young man take sannyasa?

**364.** Enjoy yourself, yogi, by serving your senses;
why die a slave to renunciation?"

**365.** Hearing the rich man's words, the Lord smiled slightly
and patiently set about teaching him.

**366.** The Lord said, "Money, cowries, gold and emeralds
are all transformations of clay, as the shastras say.

**367.** All that is a transformation of clay will in time become clay.
Why then do you all burst with pride?

**368.** Do not set your feet in the snare of the Lord's maya,
or you will die again and again.

**369.** Whoever wants to die again and again,
the bonds of maya will never let him go.

**370.** Give up everything and worship Him with devotion;
then supreme bliss will arise in your heart.

*[p. 35]*

**371.** You wander about saying 'mine, mine',
not knowing you have already entered the jaws of Time.

**372.** When it grinds you between its teeth,
where will your beautiful women be then?

**373.** Where will your gold, your silver and your grain be?
Where will your thickened milk, cream and curd-cheese be?

**374.** This cherished body will burn to ashes,
and if it does not burn, jackals will eat it.

**375.** Your head will roll in the filth by the leather-worker's door.[^26]
Worship Krishna, say 'Krishna' — time is passing in vain.

**376.** What are you proud of? Everything is transient.
The eternal reality is Krishna, the place where one finds peace.

**377.** O best of rich men, listen to my words:
do you eat diamonds, pearls and emeralds?

**378.** A handful of rice is enough to stop hunger;
why then are you proud all the time?"

**379.** Having taught the rich man thus,
the Lord looked at me and said a few words:

**380.** "[Let us go to Narayangarh,][^27] let us go;
perhaps we shall find some joy there."

**381.** Saying just this, he rose quickly,
and at once I lifted the sandals onto my shoulder.

*[p. 36]*

**382.** While my Gora walked happily along the road,
in the evening we reached that place.

**383.** In Narayangarh there is a Shiva, Dhaleshvara,
and he hurried to see him.

**384.** He is the village deity of Narayangarh.
The Lord began to weep, his tears flowing in streams.

**385.** Crying aloud, "Hara, Hara!",
the Lord fell crashing to the ground.

**386.** Choked with love, he rolled on the ground,
and his cloth and water-pot fell who knows where.

**387.** The great sattvika ecstasies[^28] arose in him,
and in his love blood spurted from his pores.

**388.** His outer cloth and kaupina slipped off who knows where;
how many ascetics came there to see that state!

**389.** Vanaprasthas, brahmacharis, so many renunciants
came there to see the Lord of Nadia.

**390.** Seeing his love and devotion, all were amazed
and fell to the ground, crying, "He is a god!"

**391.** Raising the cry of "Hari!", the Lord began to dance,
and seeing that tremendous state, all shuddered.

**392.** Thus everyone was dancing beneath the tree.
*[the second line of the couplet is missing in the edition]*

*[p. 37]*

**393.** I, a sinner and the lowest of men, kept eyeing the laddus,[^29]
and was seized by a craving to eat my fill of them.

**394.** The all-knowing Lord understood me by a hint,
and, making the laddus into prasada, gave them to me to eat.

**395.** I ate five laddus and filled my belly,
and a brahmana brought me cool water.

**396.** In time the villagers heard the news
and one by one gathered at that place.

**397.** The Lord made the offering and distributed the prasada,
and all of them together received it there.

**398.** Seeing their devotion to the prasada, I thought hard:
I, the glutton, had filled my belly before everyone else.

**399.** Thinking this and repenting inwardly,
to destroy my sin I clasped the Lord's feet.

**400.** He reasoned with me in many words and placed his foot on my head,
and at once the confusion left my mind.

**401.** Then in ecstasy he began to dance,
and all grew drunk on the wine of the nectar of Hari.

**402.** Some danced, some rolled on the ground,
some ran towards the trees crying "Krishna!"

**403.** Gradually a great crowd gathered at that place.
*[the second line of the couplet is missing in the edition]*

*[p. 38]*

**404.** Hearing of the new sannyasi,
everyone came to that place one after another.

**405.** Vireshvara Sena and Bhavani Shankara
came before the Lord with many people.

**406.** With palanquins, elephants, horses and many carriages
they came into the Lord's presence.

**407.** Bhavani Shankara is a very rich man;
he came with hundreds of people.

**408.** Kettle-drums on camels' backs, colourful banners,
four silver howdahs going on ahead.

**409.** All of them worms of worldliness, drunk with pride;
seeing them, the Lord's heart filled with compassion.

**410.** Seeing their condition, merciful Chaitanya
blessed them by giving them devotion.

**411.** Teaching devotion, the Lord intoxicated them all,
and more than a hundred thousand listened, still as puppets.

**412.** With a straw in his teeth and folded hands, the Lord said,
"All of you, listen to my humble words."

**413.** The Lord said, "Listen, all you wealthy gentlemen:
this world is like a juggler's trick.

**414.** When, overcome by sleep, you mount a throne,
then the thought 'I am king' arises in your mind.

*[p. 39]*

**415.** Hundreds of ministers and friends deliberate,
hundreds of thousands of subjects come with gifts.

**416.** But you never reflect on what all this is.
Fool, understand: it is the shadow of a reflection.

**417.** The inert world is a reflection of the truth of Krishna,
and dream is a reflection of that — so say the Vedas.

**418.** Think it over: both of them are dreams;
the only difference is sleeping or waking.

**419.** All the king's kingdom is a waking dream.
Consider what is true and what is false by the word of the Vedas.

**420.** Gold, silver, gems and pearls are transformations of clay;
the precious thing is Krishna — this is the essence.

**421.** The eternal reality is Bhagavan, so say the Vedas,
and all else that you see is false.

**422.** One who stays submerged under water —
how can he see the things on dry land?

**423.** If you lift him out of the water onto the bank,
then he will see the things of the land close by.

**424.** So it is with one who is sunk in worldliness —
how can he see Radha and Krishna?

**425.** He over whose eyes the blinkers of maya are tied
is always blind, like the oil-mill bullock.

*[p. 40]*

**426.** Who knows what lies inside a mountain cave?
How can one know its truth from outside?

**427.** Just so the subtle nature of the inert world —
who can perceive it by gross means?

**428.** The endless universes are the Lord's form;
he alone understands this who gives up the path of ritual works.

**429.** When you give up inertness and become full of consciousness,
then you will be able to see Krishna's form.

**430.** Listen: by Krishna's will power has been given to matter,
but only one who has devotion can see it.

**431.** A knot has formed between matter and consciousness;
only one whose rajas and tamas are gone can untie it.[^30]

**432.** Who can understand the nature of the inert world?
People keep going round like the oil-presser's bullock.

**433.** The oil-presser's bullock walks a short track, it is true,
but, caught in that plight, it never finds the end.

**434.** Blinkered, it goes round one path until it dies;
just so the soul goes round within worldly life.

**435.** Wearing the blinkers of maya, the soul goes round till it dies,
and therefore it cannot see the subtle truth.

**436.** Coveting others' property and others' wives,
how can one then practise the worship of Krishna?

### Translator's notes

[^18]: "To take on the misfortunes (*balai*)" is a Bengali formula of admiration: "may every evil that threatens this beauty fall on me".
[^19]: Keshava Bharati, Chaitanya's sannyasa guru.
[^20]: *Ulu-dhvani*, the ritual ululation of Bengali women at solemn moments.
[^21]: Nandana is Indra's heavenly garden.
[^22]: Advaita Acharya's house at Shantipur. Govinda passes very briefly over an episode known from other biographies: after his sannyasa Chaitanya spent several days at Shantipur, where Shachi came to see him.
[^23]: Kashi Mitra, not to be confused with Kashi Mishra of Puri. The Damodar river flows near Burdwan.
[^24]: *Beto shak*: bathua or white goosefoot, a popular Bengali leafy green.
[^25]: *Chukra/chukai*: a sour dish (a broth of sour leaves or fruit), sweetened with molasses.
[^26]: A harsh image from street speech: the dead body thrown out where the leather-workers (*muchi*) work.
[^27]: The line is corrupt in the edition ("…na chala mora jai"). From the context (the next page) Narayangarh is meant.
[^28]: The *sattvika bhavas* are the eight bodily signs of ecstasy: stupor, perspiration, hair standing on end, faltering voice, trembling, change of colour, tears and fainting.
[^29]: The lines before this, explaining where the laddus came from, are missing in the edition.
[^30]: *Rajas* and *tamas*, the gunas of passion and ignorance.
