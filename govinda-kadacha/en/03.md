# Part 3

*Through Orissa to Puri. Departure for the South. Meeting with Ramananda Raya. The Buddhists of Trimandal, Dhundhiram, the rich man Tirtharam (pp. 41–60)*

*[p. 41]*

**437.** Krishna is the truth beyond change, so say the Vedas;
he cannot be grasped by a changeful mind."

**438.** Thus, having blessed many lands,
merciful Chaitanya hastened to Jaleshvar.

**439.** At Jaleshvar there is a Shiva named Billeshvara;
seeing him, devotion welled up in the Lord's heart.

**440.** A sannyasi lived in Shiva's temple,
and the Lord went slowly up to him.

**441.** Coming before the sannyasi, he bowed down,
and the sannyasi, seeing the Lord, started.

**442.** The sannyasi said, "Who are you? You are no ordinary man.
Why do you bow before me?

**443.** By the fruit of what merit have I seen you today?
The moment I saw you, my bonds were cut.

**444.** You are the fruit of my austerities, O merciful one;
seeing you, all my sins have been destroyed."

**445.** Thus the excellent sannyasi, gazing at the Lord,
choked with love and burst into tears.

**446.** At once my Lord, to hide his identity,
raised his arms and began to dance, crying "Hari!"

**447.** Now crying "Krishna!" he leaps and runs,
now falls to the ground and rolls about.

*[p. 42]*

**448.** A great crowd gathered for the sankirtana of the Name,
and my Chaitanya passed the night awake.

**449.** Next day, coming to the bank of the Suvarnarekha,
he was thrilled to see Raghunatha Dasa.[^31]

**450.** Then we went on to Hariharpur,
and there Nimai became intoxicated with the Name of Hari.

**451.** He fell crashing to the ground,
*[the second line of the couplet is missing in the edition]*

**452.** and so that day passed,
the Lord weeping, drunk with bliss.

**453.** The next day we went to Baleshvar;
seeing Gopala, the Lord was in great bliss.

**454.** Next morning we went to Nilagarh,
and at Nilagarh Nimai became drunk with the Name.

**455.** As he danced, he gradually lost consciousness
and fell crashing to the ground.

**456.** Thus that day passed;
countless onlookers came and sat around him.

**457.** As he sang the Name his bliss increased,
and the Lord fell senseless to the ground.

**458.** Thus the devotees gathered together
and became intoxicated, tasting supreme bliss.

**459.** Next day, coming to the bank of the river Vaitarani,
he burst into tears, crying, "Krishna, take me across!"[^32]

*[p. 43]*

**460.** His body choked with love, always as if crazed,
he walks on saying "Hari", looking neither right nor left.

**461.** The next day we crossed the Mahanadi,
and on the way we saw the deity Gopinatha.

**462.** We all received Gopinatha's maha-prasada,
and with that prasada joy surged in our hearts.

**463.** Then, all filled with love,
we set off to see Sakshi-Gopala.[^33]

**464.** Raising his arms and crying "Hari!", he began to run,
and his falling tears turned the earth to mud.

**465.** Seeing Sakshi-Gopala from afar,
he sprawled on the ground, choked with love.

**466.** Seeing Gopala, it was as if he remembered something,
and at once, gazing at his face, he began to weep.

**467.** "Gopala, Gopala!" he called again and again.
Who can describe the force of his love?

**468.** Then, going to the temple of Lingaraja,
the Lord burst into tears in some mood unknown.[^34]

**469.** Leaving Lingaraja we came to Atharanala;[^35]
seeing the temple flag, my Lord fell to the ground.

**470.** Never had I seen such a flood of tears:
*[p. 44]*
the Lord turned the earth to mud with the stream of his tears.

**471.** Crying, "Ah, ah, Lord Jagannatha!", Sri Hari
flooded the ground, shedding tears.

**472.** He falls and flails and weeps aloud;
whoever he sees before him he clasps in his arms.

**473.** "Look, there is my Krishna dancing as a cowherd boy!
Ah, how beautiful his hair is!"

**474.** Seeing the Lord's temple he weeps aloud,
now and then falling crashing to the ground.

**475.** He rushed to the Lord's door and rolled in the dust,
but for the stream of his tears he could not see Vishnu's form.

**476.** Falling, flailing, crying out, rolling on the ground —
a hundred thousand people came to see that state.

**477.** With great difficulty the Lord checked the flow of his love,
but on seeing Mahavishnu he burst into tears again.

**478.** The devotees were startled by the sound of his weeping,
and he ran and embraced Gadadhara.

**479.** He went to the Garuda pillar and clung to it;
his forehead was cut and blood began to flow.

**480.** Seeing this, [illegible][^36] with his upper cloth
wiped the Lord's blood away.

**481.** Having had darshan, he went to Mishra's house,[^37]
and the devotees began to arrive in procession.

*[p. 45]*

**482.** Thus, staying some time in Puri,
we enjoyed ever new delights each day.

**483.** The Avadhuta, Krishnadasa and Haridasa
stayed by the Lord, relishing supreme bliss.

**484.** Puri was filled with the sound of the Name all eight watches,[^38]
and everyone rolled on the ground.

**485.** Some string garlands, some grind sandalwood,
some prepare the bhoga offering.

**486.** Gradually all the companions gathered,
and Puri's splendour surpassed Vaikuntha.

**487.** The brahmana Krishnadasa and pot-bellied Shyamadasa
guard the Lord on either side.

**488.** Now he falls, drunk with love,
now he leaps into the sea.

**489.** Premadasa, Gopadasa and the brahmana mahanta
shower nectar, reading the Bhagavata.

**490.** Raghunatha Dasa and Acharya Shekhara,
Damodara, Narahari and Gadadhara —

**491.** every day all of them go together to the holy temple,
taking my Lord along in their midst.

**492.** Sweet mridangas play, sometimes kartals too;
he is always drunk with the Name, with no thought of time.

*[p. 46]*

**493.** Thus my Lord, in Mishra's house,
was always rejoicing with the devotees.

**494.** Every day Kashi Mishra brings prasada,[^39]
its fragrance stealing the heart, its taste sweet.

**495.** All kinds of fried and roasted dishes — how many!
How much prasada I stuffed into my belly!

**496.** Fried chickpeas, crunchy mixtures, mung, beans,
sesame, linseed, wheat and barley — I would die for them!

**497.** Hundreds of fruits and roots, scraped coconut —
every day the Gora of Nadia gives them to me with his own hands.

**498.** Sugar crystals, dates, laddus and gaja,
adhasa, pitha, puli, juicy gaja,

**499.** rice soaked in ghee, bhuta-ghonto, nota greens —
after eating such prasada I am lost for words.

**500.** Speechless, every day I eat my fill;
whatever I get goes straight into my belly.

**501.** Thus the days went by,
and gradually all the devotees began to arrive.

**502.** Shankara Bharati and Paramananda Puri,
Damodara Svami, Pradyumna Brahmachari,

**503.** Chidananda Giri, Premananda Sarasvati
came and went to the Lord every day.

*[p. 47]*

**504.** Many devotees gathered together at Nilachala
and worshipped with great delight.

**505.** At this time Sarvabhauma appeared,
and with him many devotees came and joined them.

**506.** Seeing Mahavishnu, the Lord was filled with longing
and bowed again and again in devotion.

**507.** Seeing Govinda,[^40] the Lord fainted
and lay there like a corpse.

**508.** Sarvabhauma Bhattacharya was there;
he lifted him in his arms and carried him to his own house.

**509.** How much service he rendered the Lord!
The rasa of devotion overflowed in Sarvabhauma.

**510.** Then, having given devotion to Sarvabhauma,
the Lord made ready to set out on his southern journey.

**511.** For three months my Chaitanya Gosai
stayed in Puri together with Nitai.

**512.** Then, on the seventh day of Vaishakha,
he set out for the South, floating in the nectar of love.

**513.** At the time of departure Nitai grew anxious
and, humbled by devotion, began to speak:

**514.** "Do not go alone," said Nityananda Raya;
"let all of us go along with you."

*[p. 48]*

**515.** Very eager to go was Gadadhara, dear as his own life,
and Premananda Sarasvati and Shankara Bharati.

**516.** Hearing this, my Lord smiled a little
and said, "I shall go alone, without companions."

**517.** The Avadhuta Nityananda, hearing these words,
began to speak, shedding tears:

**518.** "You are going far away to the South.
Let the brahmana Krishnadasa Thakura go with you.

**519.** The brahmana is pure and will do
whatever you tell him to do."

**520.** "How can we stay here without you?
That is why we all say: let us go with you."

**521.** Hearing this, Mahaprabhu smiled a little
and, giving them instructions, dissuaded them all.

**522.** Hearing that, they all said,
"Then let your servant Govinda go with you."

**523.** Hearing this, my Lord said, smiling,
"I am very fond of Govinda's company.

**524.** Whoever goes or does not go, Govinda will go;
whatever work I have, Govinda will do."[^41]

**525.** Saying this, Sri Chaitanya took his leave
and set off southwards, all the devotees running after him.

*[p. 49]*

**526.** Little by little, very slowly,
we all reached the temple of Alalanatha.

**527.** Seeing Alalanatha, his feelings overflowed,
and he soaked the earth of that place with his tears.

**528.** Dancing, the Lord lost consciousness
and fell crashing to the ground.

**529.** Next morning, taking leave of everyone,
we three set out on our southern journey.[^42]

**530.** At this time Sarvabhauma said softly:
"On the bank of the Godavari you will meet Raya.

**531.** Ramananda Raya is the best of devotees who know rasa;
at Krishna's name his eyes are always wet with tears.

**532.** Rama Raya enjoys pure bliss;
at the Name of Hari his heart rejoices."

**533.** Hearing this, he hastened to the bank of the Godavari,
and there Ramananda came and met him.

**534.** Seeing the young sannyasi, devotion arose in him;
Rama Raya clasped his feet and began to weep.

**535.** Ramananda Raya said, "You are God himself.
I am most fortunate to have seen you."

**536.** The Lord said, "Raya, speak about Krishna;
by your conclusions the ache of my heart will go."

*[p. 50]*

**537.** Raya said, "Lord, I know nothing;
unless you make me speak, no words will come."

**538.** "Dwelling in the heart, you teach everything;
by your mercy you make the dumb eloquent."

**539.** The Lord asked, "By what truth is the mind purified?"
Raya said, "That truth is meeting with the saints."

**540.** "I want from you a truth subtler still."
Raya said, "There is no truth beyond renunciation."

**541.** The Lord said, "The subtle truth is attachment to God."
Raya said, "Higher even than that is prema-bhakti."

**542.** The Lord said, "Tell me a deeper essence still, O wise one."
Raya said, "The essence of all is Rai, full of rasa."[^43]

**543.** Rama Raya wanted to tell a deeper essence still,
but Gora Raya at once covered his mouth.

**544.** The Lord said, "Butter lies hidden in milk;
he who churns will get its taste."

**545.** The Lord said, "Raya, I know nothing.
Speak, speak of Krishna; let me hear it from your mouth.

**546.** You, Rama Raya, are a Vaishnava detached from the world.
Speak, speak the truth of Krishna, let my heart be soothed."

**547.** Hearing the Lord's words, Ramananda Raya
folded his hands in humility and said:

*[p. 51]*

**548.** "Why do you play tricks again and again, Lord of the universe?
Be merciful and make this servant your follower.

**549.** You have spread the rasa of devotion all over the land
and in your mercy sanctified this earth.

**550.** Have mercy on this wretched one, Jagannatha;
give detachment to my heart and take me with you."

**551.** Hearing this, the Lord embraced Raya,
and Rama Raya, clasping his knees, wept.

**552.** Ramananda's breast was flooded with tears,
and seeing this the Lord said in a choked voice:

**553.** "You, Rama Raya, are the crest-jewel of Vaishnavas."
Ramananda, his face lowered, kept saying "Rama, Rama".

**554.** The Lord said, "Raya, you are most fortunate;
your devotion is beyond description."

**555.** Raya said, "I am the lowest of sinners;
you have been defiled by touching me.

**556.** Mercifully forgive me that offence,
and, seated in my heart, make me taste devotion."

**557.** That night Ramananda passed
in such conversation with Gora-chand.

**558.** Next day Raya clasped the Lord's feet,
*[p. 52]*
took his leave and went off to his own duties.

**559.** The Lord said, "Ramananda, now I shall go on;
come to Nilachala and stay with me.

**560.** You, I and the Bhatta[^44] will live in seclusion
and, discussing the truth of Krishna, find peace in life."

**561.** So saying, the Lord sent Raya away,
and Ramananda bowed and went home.

**562.** Of all that Raya said with the Lord,
not a hundredth part has remained in this book.

**563.** Thus Ramananda came for ten days
and rejoiced to see the sannyasi of Nadia.

**564.** The Lord felt great affection on seeing Ramananda,
and Raya lost consciousness on seeing the Lord.

**565.** Taking leave of Raya,
the Lord entered the town of Trimandal.[^45]

**566.** Many Buddhists live in the town of Trimandal;
they all came to meet beautiful Gauranga.

**567.** The Lord debated with the Buddhists,
and the king of Trimandal came to act as arbiter.

**568.** The Buddhists admitted defeat in the debate,
and all the pandits and onlookers burst out laughing.

**569.** All said, "This sannyasi is no man!
The way he debated is beyond words."

*[p. 53]*

**570.** The chief of the Buddhists, Ramagiri Raya,
bowed and said, "Show me the way.

**571.** You are no man, young sannyasi;
I long to stay with you.

**572.** In the world I was the crest-jewel of unbelievers;
mercifully show me the path of devotion."

**573.** Smiling, Lord Chaitanya said kindly:
"You are my master, Ramagiri Raya.

**574.** One who thrills with joy saying 'Hari'
is my master — that is the practice."

**575.** Hearing the Lord's words, Ramagiri Raya
at once fell crashing to the ground.

**576.** Lying at his feet, Ramagiri said,
"What have you said to this lowest of men, O merciful one?

**577.** Dwelling in all beings, you see everything.
Mercifully give me a place at your crimson feet."

**578.** Devotion arose in Ramagiri the unbeliever,
and seeing this my Lord was filled with joy.

**579.** All the Buddhists, crest-jewels of pandits,
followed Ramagiri's path.

**580.** The young sannyasi left the disputants without hope;
seeing this, Ramananda[^46] looked all around.

*[p. 54]*

**581.** At last, eager to debate,
came Dhundhiram Tirtha, who lived by the Tungabhadra.

**582.** Ever drunk with pride, vain of his learning,
he did not understand the path of devotion — learned only in dry logic.

**583.** Dhundhiram was indeed a great pandit,
but he was very much afraid of debating.

**584.** Svami Dhundhiram went to debate,
but the Lord, shedding tears, rolled on the ground again and again.

**585.** The Lord said, "Listen, listen, Svami Dhundhiram:
I have lost the argument with you.

**586.** I will write you a certificate of victory in secret:
'Chaitanya has now lost to you.'

**587.** By the grace of Vani[^47] you are a learned Gosai;
who could beat you in logic and scripture?

**588.** Nyaya, Sankhya, Patanjala and Vedanta —
you are expert in every shastra, good sir.

**589.** I am an ignorant sannyasi and know nothing;
again and again I admit defeat before you.

**590.** You are more learned than the Dhundhi of old;[^48]
your learning is famous throughout the world."

**591.** So saying, he sent Dhundhiram away,
but Dhundhi did not want to go and looked all around.

*[p. 55]*

**592.** After looking this way and that, Dhundhi
fell at the Lord's feet with a heart made wholly pure.

**593.** Having given devotion to the unbeliever Dhundhi,
the Lord set out for Pantha-guha, remembering Sri Hari.[^49]

**594.** Dhundhiram became known by the name Haridasa,
and the unbelievers whispered and said all sorts of things.

**595.** Smiling, the Lord called me,
and I lifted the two pairs of sandals onto my shoulders.

**596.** The sandals, water-pot and whatever belongings we had,
and also what Raya had given, I gathered up and took.

**597.** Not far away there was a banyan called Akshaya;
at dusk the Lord arrived at that place.

**598.** A Shiva named Vateshvara is there,
and Gora-chand hastened there with devotion.

**599.** With devotion the Lord bowed to Vateshvara
and spent the night there without food.

**600.** In the morning the Lord went to bathe,
and I went from door to door begging alms.

**601.** I came back with the alms at midday;
my Gora Raya cooked and made the offering.

**602.** I received prasada like nectar,
and just then the wealthy Tirtha[^50] arrived.

*[p. 56]*

**603.** Two harlots came with him to look,
to test the sannyasi's vaunted firmness.

**604.** The harlots named Satyabai and Lakshmibai
came up to the Lord and talked of many things.

**605.** Coached by Tirtha, those two harlots
made many attempts to see through the Lord.

**606.** Tirtharam said all sorts of things to himself:
"Now by a trick I will rob this sannyasi of his power."

**607.** Lakshmi played all manner of tricks, and Satyabala laughed;
Satyabala sat down beside the Lord with a smile.

**608.** Satya undid her bodice and bared her breasts,
and the Lord addressed Satya as "Mother".

**609.** At the Lord's words Satya trembled all over,
and seeing this Lakshmi was much afraid.

**610.** There was not the slightest agitation in the Lord's mind.
Satyabala ran and fell at his feet.

**611.** "Why do you make me an offender, Mother?"
Saying only this, the Lord fell to the ground.

**612.** His matted hair came loose, he was grey with dust,
his body trembling all over with longing.

**613.** Everything about my Lord was in disarray;
he no longer saw where Lakshmi was, where Satya was.

*[p. 57]*

**614.** Saying "Hari, Hari!" the Lord began to dance,
his hair standing on end, tears streaming down.

**615.** His kaupina had slipped off; where was his outer cloth?
He danced naked, breathing hard and fast.

**616.** He falls, heedless of thorns and splinters;
the bunch of garlands at his neck has broken.

**617.** From fasting he is nothing but skin and bone;
streams of blood flow over his thin limbs.

**618.** Drunk with the Name of Hari, Gora Raya dances,
and a wondrous radiance comes forth from his body.

**619.** Seeing this, that rich man was shaken within;
he fell at his feet and took refuge.

**620.** The Lord, unaware of the outer world, tramples him underfoot;
crying "Hari!" with arms upraised he dances onwards.

**621.** Clasping Satya in his arms he says, "Say 'Hari'!
Say 'Hari', lord of life, Mukunda, Murari!"

**622.** Where is the Lord, where is Mukunda-Murari?
Seeing such a state, all lost consciousness.

**623.** Drunk with the Name of Hari, the Lord has no outer awareness;
his head droops, his soul in turmoil.

**624.** Froth at his mouth, dust on his limbs, no cloth,
his hair bristling, his eyes closed.

*[p. 58]*

**625.** Seeing this state, all the Buddhists cry, "Hari, Hari!"
Hearing it, tears flow from Gora's eyes.

**626.** The tears began to spurt like a squirt-gun;
seeing this, Tirtharam burst into tears.

**627.** "I am a great unbeliever," says Tirtharam;
"be merciful, Lord, and give me the Name of Hari."

**628.** Embracing the unbeliever Tirtharam,
the Lord says, "Tirtharam, you are a holy man.

**629.** I have been purified by touching you.
'You are a foremost devotee,'" he says again and again.

**630.** Then the rich Tirtharam fell at his feet
and wept long in great distress.

**631.** When, through his weeping, devotion arose,
the Lord at once took his hand and embraced him.

**632.** The Lord said, "Count riches as straw,
and then you will gain the priceless jewel of devotion.

**633.** Throw your clothes and ornaments far away;
leave transient wealth and worship the eternal treasure.

**634.** Coming and going again and again you will suffer —
this is the counsel for those free from desire.

**635.** This cherished body, covered with skin,
will rot in a few days' time.

*[p. 59]*

**636.** When the bird of life flies from the body,
it becomes either worms, or ashes, or excrement.

**637.** In the three worlds there is nothing to be proud of;
the only glory lies in worshipping God.

**638.** Knowing luxury and wealth to be transient,
throw them away one by one.

**639.** Faith in God brings God to you;
no other proof can be put into words.

**640.** Countless worlds are proof of it,
yet the learned gosai wants proof.

**641.** There is no need for much debate and wrangling;
Krishna comes and meets the seeker through faith.

**642.** What need is there to discuss many shastras?
Have faith and worship Krishna.

**643.** One who boasts of wealth again and again
suffers unceasing sorrow day by day.

**644.** One who prides himself for the sake of honour —
tell me, who will relieve his sorrow?

**645.** 'This is mine, I am his' — so everyone says,
but when the eyes close, no one belongs to anyone.

**646.** All people pretend to kinship for nothing;
mourning a dead body is like mourning a broken doll.

*[p. 60]*

**647.** The son is born of the father, everyone knows,
but the Vedas do not teach that the two minds are one.

**648.** When life leaves the son's body,
that does not mean the father dies.

**649.** The son takes birth from the mother's body,
but know for certain that the two are not one.

**650.** No one belongs to anyone — this is the course of what is to be known,
and it cannot be established by proofs.

**651.** God is the object of knowledge, and the proof of him
is present within the human heart.

**652.** Fools think him farther than the far;
the wise accept that he is very near.

**653.** I have told you the essence of truth, the teaching of the Vedas;
foolish people do not seek it.

**654.** Whoever knows all these true principles
does not die again and again."

**655.** Hearing all this from the Lord's lips, Tirtharam
gave up attachment to worldly things and chanted the Name of Hari.

**656.** The Lord became intoxicated with the sankirtana of Hari,
and gradually his companions came and gathered round.[^51]

**657.** "The rich Tirtharam has fallen into trouble" —
so the unbelievers said of him, gossiping in many ways.

### Translator's notes

[^31]: According to the *Chaitanya-charitamrita*, Raghunatha Dasa (Gosvami) of Saptagram fled to Chaitanya at Puri only some years later. A meeting at the Suvarnarekha in 1510 is one of the details of the *Karcha* that critics regard as anachronistic.
[^32]: The Vaitarani is a real river in Orissa and also the mythical river at the border of Yama's realm which must be "crossed"; hence the double sense of the cry.
[^33]: Sakshi-Gopala, "Gopala the witness", is a deity near Puri. Gopinatha, mentioned just before, is on the traditional route at Remuna (near Baleshvar), not after the Mahanadi; Govinda may have confused the order.
[^34]: Lingaraja is the chief Shiva temple of Bhubaneswar.
[^35]: Atharanala is the eighteen-arched stone bridge at the entrance to Puri, from which the flag of Jagannatha's temple is first seen.
[^36]: The word is corrupt in the edition (*dhyanapura*); perhaps the name of one of the companions.
[^37]: Kashi Mishra, in whose house Chaitanya later lived in Puri.
[^38]: The eight watches (*at-pahar*): round the clock.
[^39]: The beginning of this line is partly lost in the edition and is restored from the sense.
[^40]: Probably Jagannatha, whom Govinda here calls Govinda; possibly a separate deity of the temple complex.
[^41]: A key passage for the controversy. According to the *Chaitanya-charitamrita* (Madhya 7) only the brahmana Krishnadasa went south with Chaitanya. The *Karcha* acknowledges Krishnadasa's candidacy but asserts that Govinda went too.
[^42]: "We three": Chaitanya, Govinda and apparently Krishnadasa, who is hardly mentioned afterwards.
[^43]: Rai is Radha. A compressed version of the famous dialogue of Chaitanya and Ramananda (CC Madhya 8), structured quite differently from the *Charitamrita*.
[^44]: Sarvabhauma Bhattacharya.
[^45]: Trimandal (*Trimanda*) is a place near the Godavari, perhaps Trimalgiri or Tirumalla in Sen's reading; the identification is uncertain.
[^46]: So in the edition; perhaps Ramagiri is meant.
[^47]: Vani is Sarasvati, goddess of learning.
[^48]: An allusion to some famous Dhundhi of the past, probably a commentator.
[^49]: Pantha-guha, "the traveller's cave", is a place-name; cf. the robber Pantha Bhil in the next part.
[^50]: Tirtharam, the rich man of the following episode.
[^51]: The line is unclear; perhaps Tirtharam's companions are meant.
