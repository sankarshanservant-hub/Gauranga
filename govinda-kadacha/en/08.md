# Part 8

*Indira Bai. The robber Naroji in the Chorananadi forest. Khandala, Nasik, Panchavati. Ashtabhuja and the sermon against animal sacrifice. The Tapti, Bharuch, Baroda, the death of Naroji. Ahmedabad (pp. 143–162)*

*[p. 143]*

**1560.** "I have grown old doing wicked deeds;
deliver me by giving me the dust of your feet."

**1561.** Saying this, Indira rolled in the dust,
and the Lord delivered Indira by giving her the Name.

**1562.** Having received the Name of Hari, the beautiful Indira
left her home, renouncing everything.

**1563.** From that day all Khandoba's wives
became drunk with the Name of Hari, tears flowing from their eyes.

**1564.** Never have I seen so merciful a Lord;
how many sinners he delivered — there is no counting them.

**1565.** Having seen the muraris' devotion with his own eyes,
in the morning he wished to go to the Chorananadi forest.

**1566.** The village people said, "What business have you there?
The Chorananadi forest is a mine of sin.

**1567.** Many robbers live in the Chorananadi forest;
why do you wish to go there?"

**1568.** The Lord said, "I will go to the Chorananadi forest;
seeing Chorananadi, my purpose will be fulfilled."

**1569.** The village people said, "Do not go there, sannyasi;
we do not like holy men going there.

**1570.** Great thieves and forest robbers live in that place;
if you go there your life will be in danger."

*[p. 144]*

**1571.** The Lord said, "What can the robbers take from me?
I will go there right now."

**1572.** Rama Svami said, "Lord, the Chorananadi forest
is no tirtha; what business have you there?

**1573.** If the robbers do you some harm,
people will die of separation from you."

**1574.** The Lord said, "Have no fear, Rama Svami;
I will make the robbers drunk with the Name of Hari."

**1575.** Saying this, the Lord went to Chorananadi,
and arriving at Chorananadi he sat beneath a tree.

**1576.** Here many wicked men have their den
and live by robbery.

**1577.** A man came along saying "khai mai";
I could not understand anything he said.

**1578.** The Lord, understanding all his speech,
answered him in "khai mai" and explained everything.

**1579.** The man stood staring for a moment,
then, glancing this way and that, went back into the forest.

**1580.** A very powerful man named Naroji
came forward with his weapons and arms.

**1581.** Gradually two or four men appeared;
seeing the sannyasi, they all bowed down.

*[p. 145]*

**1582.** Naroji said, "Come to my place;
you shall spend tonight there."

**1583.** Hearing Naroji's words, the Lord said,
"I will pass tonight here beneath the tree."

**1584.** Hearing the Lord's words, Naroji
told two or four men to bring alms.

**1585.** Hearing Naroji's words, they all ran off,
and Nimai sat absorbed in the Name of Hari.

**1586.** Some bring firewood and sugar, some rice,
some milk, some ghee, some fruits and roots.[^114]

**1587.** They brought heaps of food,
and seeing so much food my greed grew.

**1588.** I had wandered through many lands with the Lord,
but nowhere had I seen so much food.

**1589.** Having supplied all kinds of things and surrounding him on all sides,
Naroji's men stood in rows.

**1590.** As he chanted the Name of Hari, my Lord
was then lost in love for Krishna.

**1591.** Where was the milk and sugar, where the rice?
At the touch of his feet the fruits and roots were scattered.

**1592.** Two or four men said, "What kind of sannyasi is this?
He spoils heaps of food on purpose."

*[p. 146]*

**1593.** Naroji said, "I have never seen anything like it.
Why does my heart weep on seeing this sannyasi?

**1594.** Who can tell how many sins I have committed?
Why today do I wish to put on a kaupina?

**1595.** Why today does my heart weep?
Have I set my foot in the sannyasi's snare?

**1596.** All the things are spoilt — have no fear;
I will bring all these things again."

**1597.** Standing to one side, Naroji himself
gazed fixedly at Gora, the jewel of virtues.

**1598.** Tears flowed from the Lord's eyes,
and everyone stood there like puppets.

**1599.** Hearing of this, gradually the band of robbers
came out of the forest one by one.

**1600.** In the afternoon my Gora, the jewel of virtues,
fainted with love and fell to the ground.

**1601.** His body choked with love and grey with dust,
tears streaming down onto his breast.

**1602.** Weeping, Naroji said, "Listen, sannyasi,
tell me plainly: what mantra have you recited?

**1603.** Seeing your state, I think in my heart:
I will sin no more, living in this forest.

*[p. 147]*

**1604.** I am sixty years old;
I will do no more sinful deeds, I will leave worldly life.

**1605.** I, a brahmana's son, am a great villain;
do not hesitate to give me the dust of your feet.

**1606.** I have no children and no household —
why then should I go on doing sinful deeds?

**1607.** My belly will be fed if people give me alms;
why then should I stay with the robbers?

**1608.** I have come to loathe evil deeds;
I will be chief of the robbers no longer."

**1609.** Saying this, Naroji looked at his band
and at that moment threw down his weapons and arms.

**1610.** The Lord said, "Naroji, listen to what I say;
how often must I tell you again and again?

**1611.** Put on a kaupina and cover your nakedness;
feed yourself by begging and asking.

**1612.** For whom do you hoard wealth?
Father, mother, brother, friend — no one belongs to anyone.

**1613.** If a handful of rice keeps the body alive,
why hoard wealth sinfully?

**1614.** Drink spring water from the cup of your hands;
what is the use of collecting many vessels?

*[p. 148]*

**1615.** All the rich men equal to Kubera
will one day go to the city of the dead.

**1616.** The road by which the poor man goes when he leaves this body
is the very road the emperor too must take.

**1617.** Why die in vain, saying 'mine, mine'?
With love and devotion, brother, chant the Name of Hari."

**1618.** Hearing this teaching, the brahmana Naroji
wished to come along with us.

**1619.** Naroji said, "I will show you all the holy places;
from tirtha to tirtha I will follow you.

**1620.** Until now my eyes were blinded by the smoke of delusion;
from today I have thrown my weapons to the ground.

**1621.** With these hands I have committed so many murders,
with this mouth I have spoken so many harsh words.

**1622.** I will be robber chief no longer.
What a path you have shown me, refuge of the helpless!

**1623.** I always lived hidden in the jungle;
my body is riddled with sin, and I never gave it a thought."

**1624.** Saying this, the robber chief gave up everything
and, putting on a kaupina, went with the Lord.

**1625.** The robbers went off who knows where,
and Naroji came along with us.

*[p. 149]*

**1626.** Then from the Chorananadi forest
the Lord set out to see Khandala.

**1627.** The Mula river flows here, very swift;
arriving at Khandala, the Lord said to me:

**1628.** "This river is a holy tirtha;
bathing here, sins are destroyed."

**1629.** At the Lord's command I bathed
and went into the town to beg.

**1630.** Naroji Thakura follows behind me;
having begged, I came back late in the day.

**1631.** Gradually two or four people of Khandala
came and showed themselves to the Lord.

**1632.** With pure hearts they all sat round about;
one said, "Come, Lord, to my estate."

**1633.** All the people of Khandala are very hospitable;
they all tugged at him, eager to take him away.

**1634.** At last they all started a quarrel
and were ready to come to blows.

**1635.** One said, "I saw him first",
another said, "I brought the alms."

**1636.** Thus they quarrelled with one another,
and seeing this my Lord laughed inwardly.

*[p. 150]*

**1637.** A rich man said, "In my garden
I will give you alms today; go and stay there.

**1638.** You have no cloth to wear — what a shame!
I wish to give you a piece of cloth.

**1639.** If you want some money for the road,
whatever you ask, brother, I will bring at once."

**1640.** Smiling, the Lord said, "Listen, maharaja,
I have no use for luxury or wealth.

**1641.** I count even the torn cloth I wear as plenty;
I know I have no need of money.

**1642.** Along with wealth, pride increases,
and with that pride the burden of sin grows.

**1643.** This whole universe that you see with your eyes —
think where it will go.

**1644.** All will become [illegible], all will be destroyed;
only the Lord of Brahma will remain.[^115]

**1645.** My two companions have brought alms;
what need is there of more alms?

**1646.** The body must somehow be kept alive,
so on some days one has to take alms.

**1647.** But tell me, what will come of a lot of food?
Give it to the poor and wretched and make up their want.

*[p. 151]*

**1648.** Say 'Hari' with love, sitting beneath a tree,
and then you will all be able to cut your bonds.

**1649.** There is no happiness in the bonds of maya;
with love and devotion say 'Hari' with your lips, brothers.

**1650.** Love and devotion to God are made of rasa;
whoever knows them merges in the nectar of eternity.[^116]

**1651.** One who worships Krishna enjoys heaven forever;
for one who abandons Krishna there is hell."

**1652.** Saying this, my Lord spoke no more
and, closing his eyes, began to sway.

**1653.** Under the weight of his thrills his matted hair came loose,
his outer cloth slipped off and he began to dance.

**1654.** Lost in love, his body grey with dust —
what can I say of love? To tell it would be a long story.

**1655.** Chanting the Name of Hari, he passed the night sitting up,
and Naroji, sitting beside him, wiped away his sweat.

**1656.** Rising in the morning, the Lord went to the town of Nasik
to make pilgrimage with a pure heart.

**1657.** Here Thakura Lakshmana
cut off the nose of the rakshasi Shurpanakha.

**1658.** To the west of it, near Tryambak,
is the site of Rama's hut.

*[p. 152]*

**1659.** Going there, Mahaprabhu
offered hymns and praises and at last began kirtana.

**1660.** "Rama's footprints are here" —
hearing this, he ran off into the forest.

**1661.** In the depths of the dense forest, beside a spring,
two footprints stand out on a rock.

**1662.** Touching the footprints,
he was overcome by a still deeper surge of love.

**1663.** With his thrills, the matted locks on his head seemed to dance,
and his frail body seemed to swell.

**1664.** The Lord said, "Where is Rama, lord of my life?
Show yourself lovingly and soothe my heart."

**1665.** At last, clinging to my neck,
he burst into tears, saying, "Where is my Rama?"

**1666.** The fragrance of lotus comes from the Lord's body,
and a breeze began to blow softly.

**1667.** What can I say of love — I am afraid to speak of it;
never have I seen such a wondrous state.

**1668.** At every turn he calls out, "O Krishna!"
and sometimes, like a madman, looks this way and that.

**1669.** Who knows whom he calls, gazing at the sky;
sometimes he starts as though seeing something.

*[p. 153]*

**1670.** A day or two pass in fasting;
from not eating, his body has grown thin.

**1671.** Then, entering Panchavati,
he saw the Ganesha installed by Lakshmana.

**1672.** One day, in a cave in the Panchavati forest,
coming back from begging, I secretly saw:

**1673.** that deserted forest is utterly silent;
only now and then do two or four people live there.

**1674.** The forest is ringing with stillness;
with closed eyes beautiful Gauranga is contemplating something.

**1675.** A mass of radiance is coming forth from his body;
my young sannyasi is absorbed in meditation.

**1676.** Seeing this state, my eyes were dazzled,
and I crept close to watch.

**1677.** Naroji had gone somewhere to bring fruit;
I stood to one side.

**1678.** Hearing footsteps, the Lord seemed suddenly,
before my very eyes, to restrain all his feelings.

**1679.** Naroji, having gathered fruits and roots from somewhere,
stood before him with folded hands.

**1680.** After making the offering and eating a little, Gora Raya
spent the whole night sitting up.

*[p. 154]*

**1681.** Leaving Panchavati, my Gaura-Hari
went in the morning to the town of Daman.

**1682.** He did not stay even a day in the town of Daman;
leaving Daman, the Lord went north.

**1683.** Then for a fortnight, wandering and wandering,
Gora, the delight of hearts, spent his time on the road.

**1684.** Gradually he came into the kingdom of Suratha[^117]
and, seeing Ashtabhuja, lay rolling on the ground.

**1685.** Having seen the eight-armed Bhagavati with his own eyes,
the Lord stayed there three days.

**1686.** Ashtabhuja was installed by King Suratha;
seeing Bhagavati, he felt boundless joy.

**1687.** In the goddess's temple there was a sannyasi;
he wished to ask the Lord something.

**1688.** The sannyasi said, "Come, come, sannyasi gosai;
never have I seen a holy man like you.

**1689.** Seeing you, devotion arises in my heart;
tell me, how shall I cross the ocean of worldly existence?

**1690.** How should one worship the Supreme Lord?
Tell me this and remove my anxiety."

**1691.** The Lord said, "I know no essential truth;
Bhavani will dispel all the darkness of your mind.

*[p. 155]*

**1692.** As an ordinary heroine, seeing a handsome hero,
looks at him with her heart full of passion,[^118]

**1693.** in that same mood call on Krishna again and again,
and the darkness of your mind will vanish of itself."

**1694.** While he was speaking, a brahmana
came to the goddess to sacrifice a goat.

**1695.** The Lord said, "You make the sacrifice in order to eat it,
and do not know what your fate will be afterwards.

**1696.** The goddess is a pure form — so the shastras say;
how could she eat what is forbidden?

**1697.** King Suratha offered a hundred thousand sacrifices;
in the city of the dead a hundred thousand swords fall upon him.

**1698.** You do not study the words of the shastras,
yet you practise religion by killing animals.

**1699.** It was the meat-eating rakshasas who, in order to eat,
laid down the rule of killing animals.

**1700.** Non-violence is the highest dharma, say all the shastras;
be kind to living beings and joy will arise.

**1701.** Maya has bound you tightly;
how will you cut the bonds without a sword?

**1702.** You crave tamasic food, and that is why
you are so eager to slaughter sheep and goats before the goddess.

*[p. 156]*

**1703.** You think you will be saved by killing an animal,
and that is why you have come to make a sacrifice.

**1704.** You can drive the soul out of the body,
but can you put the soul back into a dead body?

**1705.** If someone before the goddess, with devotion,
were to cut off your head as a human sacrifice —

**1706.** tell me, brother, how would your heart feel?
Let the animal go; I want to see it with my own eyes.

**1707.** The eight-armed Bhagavati would eat wine and meat?
Hearing such a thing, a holy man would laugh it away.

**1708.** Turn your minds to the eternal dharma
and, following the shastras, give up evil practices.

**1709.** The goddess is a supreme Vaishnavi and does not eat meat;
why then do you try to beguile her with sacrifices?

**1710.** If killing living beings were dharma,
why are robbers not called holy men?

**1711.** The fisherman kills many fish every day —
why should I not call him righteous?

**1712.** Killing men and killing animals are great sins;
if you commit this sin, the threefold misery will increase."

**1713.** Going to see the eight-armed Bhagavati,
he gave this teaching, examining the shastras.

*[p. 157]*

**1714.** The man who had come to worship Durga
listened to the Lord's words with devotion.

**1715.** Hearing the Lord's words, he became detached,
and the brahmana let the sacrificial goat go.

**1716.** Worshipping with flowers and bilva leaves, the excellent brahmana
went back home happily.

**1717.** The Lord sat down firmly before the goddess
and with folded hands began a hymn to Bhavani.

**1718.** Having offered praise and obeisance to Bhavani, Gora Raya
hurried towards the river Tapti, a great tirtha.

**1719.** Bathing three times a day in the waters of the Tapti,
he went to see the image of Vamana.

**1720.** On an open plain near the Tapti
an image of Lord Vamana is installed.

**1721.** King Bali installed this image;
for this reason the Tapti became a tirtha.

**1722.** Vamana bathed in the waters of the Tapti,
and that is why the Tapti is called a great tirtha.

**1723.** Having bowed at the feet of Lord Vamana,
Gaura-Hari went to see the sacrificial pit.

**1724.** To see the sacrificial pit in the town of Bharoch,
he left the Tapti and went to the bank of the Narmada.

*[p. 158]*

**1725.** In Bharoch King Bali performed sacrifice at a sacrificial pit;
he goes with a joyful heart to see it.

**1726.** Seeing with his own eyes the huge hollow of the pit,
boundless joy arose in Chaitanya's heart.

**1727.** Having bathed in the Narmada, a great tirtha,
Gora, the delight of hearts, went to the town of Baroda.

**1728.** East of Baroda is Thakura Dakorji;[^119]
the Lord wished to see Dakorji.

**1729.** In Dakorji's courtyard there is a huge tamala tree,
and beneath it the darling of Shachi stood.

**1730.** Having seen Dakorji, the Lord offered obeisance and praise
and came back again to the town of Baroda.

**1731.** The king of Baroda is very virtuous;
the worthy king is devoted to the service of Govinda.

**1732.** With his own hands he sweeps Govinda's temple;
people say to one another that the king is like Ambarisha.

**1733.** The maharaja is always busy for Govinda's sake;
the king is always full of love for Govinda's service.

**1734.** Gathering tulasi with his own hands,
the king offers it at Govinda's lotus feet with devotion.

**1735.** In the evening Gora goes to Govinda's house
and, seeing Govinda, rolls on the ground in love.

*[p. 159]*

**1736.** A single torn outer cloth, the look of a madman;
the Lord is always intoxicated, absorbed in Krishna.

**1737.** His whole body smeared with dust, his eyes closed;
seeing Govinda, he sheds tears.

**1738.** After three days a calamity occurred here:
Naroji died of fever.

**1739.** At the time of death Gora Raya sat before him
and stroked Naroji's body with his lotus hand.

**1740.** When Naroji closed his eyes,
he himself gave Krishna's name into his ear from his holy lips.

**1741.** Naroji Thakura is very fortunate:
Bhagavan gave Krishna's name into his ear.

**1742.** At the time of death Naroji, with folded hands,
looking towards the Lord, said "Hari, Hari".

**1743.** Taking Naroji in his arms, Lord Vishvambhara
moved him from beneath the tamala tree.

**1744.** Having begged [what was needed], Naroji was buried,
and the Lord performed kirtana circling the grave.

**1745.** The maharaja, hearing of this by word of mouth,
came himself with a crowd to see the sannyasi.

**1746.** Seeing the Lord, the king bowed down,
and the Lord said nothing, good or bad.

*[p. 160]*

**1747.** Respectfully the king invited him to take alms.
The Lord said, "I take alms at householders' doors.

**1748.** I have no need of alms of luxury;
that is why I do not ask alms at your door."

**1749.** With folded hands the king asked him to accept alms,
and, having no choice, he told me to accept them.

**1750.** At the Lord's sign, from the king,
like an ordinary man, I begged a handful of alms.

**1751.** Having given alms, the maharaja went away,
and Gora-chand completed his daily rites.

**1752.** Rising in the morning, we go westwards
and, going a little way, reach the Mahanadi.[^120]

**1753.** A very swift river, beautiful to see,
with many stones rushing along in it.

**1754.** Crossing the river, my Gora, the delight of hearts,
reached the vicinity of Ahmedabad.

**1755.** Wondrous Ahmedabad, a splendid city:
how many gardens, how many charming houses!

**1756.** Huge mansions stand in splendour in its midst;
the people of the country are always devoted to serving guests.

**1757.** The village people regard a guest as equal to a god
and serve guests with all their hearts.

*[p. 161]*

**1758.** Enchanted by the Lord's beauty, people
came with devotion and stood all around.

**1759.** Some said, "Listen, listen, young sannyasi,
come to my house; I will give you alms and serve you."

**1760.** The Lord said, "I will not go to a householder's home;
tonight I will stay by the Nandani."

**1761.** There is a beautiful garden called Nandani;
at its edge Lord Vishvambhara settled down.

**1762.** Seeing this, the village people brought alms,
and at night Gora-chand made the offering.

**1763.** Many people came and surrounded the Lord
and, seeing the sannyasi, spoke with devotion.

**1764.** A pandit came and showed himself
and began to recite verses of the Srimad-Bhagavata.

**1765.** The Lord said, "Sing Krishna's glories well;
I wish to forget everything while hearing the verses.

**1766.** You study the Bhagavata every day;
seeing you, the torment of worldly life goes away.

**1767.** Every day you sing the glories of Krishna;
blessed, blessed are you, brahmana, you are most fortunate."

**1768.** Having talked with the Lord, the brahmana
called all the people and said:

*[p. 162]*

**1769.** "All of you serve this sannyasi well;
this sannyasi is no ordinary man — he must be some god.

**1770.** Seeing him, detachment arises;
know for certain he is no ordinary man."

**1771.** I cannot understand all the speech of the people;
what I can, I write down from gestures and hints.

**1772.** Having long travelled the tirthas of this country,
Shachi's darling understands everyone's speech.

**1773.** Sometimes, asking the Lord about a word or two,
I write it down in my karcha after thinking it over.

**1774.** The lila that I have seen with my own eyes
I record in my karcha very secretly.[^121]

**1775.** Always intoxicated, the Lord, in an ecstasy of love for Krishna,
wanders from tirtha to tirtha, from land to land.

**1776.** In Ahmedabad many people gathered;
they all come in crowds to see the Lord.

**1777.** Seeing many people all around, Gora Raya,
drunk with joy, distributes the Name to all.

**1778.** The Lord said, "With devotion all of you chant the Name;
all suffering will go far away and no sorrow will remain.

**1779.** Do not despise anyone out of pride;
free from pride, say 'Hare Krishna Hare'."

### Translator's notes

[^114]: D. C. Sen quotes this couplet in his introduction as evidence in the dispute over whether Chaitanya ate rice (*ata-chuna*).
[^115]: The line is badly corrupted in the edition; the translation is approximate.
[^116]: The line is partly corrupted; translated from the sense.
[^117]: The edition reads "*Harather rajya*", apparently "the kingdom of Suratha" (cf. below, "Suratha installed Ashtabhuja").
[^118]: *Ragatmika*, "full of passion (*raga*)", a term of the Gaudiya Vaishnava theory of spontaneous devotion.
[^119]: Dakorji is Ranchhodrai at Dakor (Gujarat), a famous deity of Krishna.
[^120]: Probably the river Mahi (Gujarat).
[^121]: Another statement by the author, key for the whole book, about how he kept his notes.
