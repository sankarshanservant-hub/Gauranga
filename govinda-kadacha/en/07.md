# Part 7

*The poor brahmana and his Gopala. Nilagiri, Gurjari, Agastya-kunda. The pandit Arjuna. Sahya-kulachala, Pune: "Krishna is in the pond". Bholeshvar, Devaleshvar, Jejuri and Khandoba's "muraris" (pp. 123–142)*

*[p. 123]*

**1340.** "By the power of the Name of Hari no fear of Yama remains;
call 'Krishna, Krishna' and have no doubt."[^99]

**1341.** Hearing these words, my strength revived,
and my bodily strength seemed to double.

**1342.** Walking on, we came near a small hamlet,
hoping for shelter.

**1343.** It is a very small hamlet, all its people poor;
there the sannyasi Nimai went and sat down.

**1344.** The hamlet, ringed by hills, is lovely to see;
I went into the village to beg.

**1345.** There was a very poor brahmana there,
who somehow lived by begging.

**1346.** I went to his house to beg alms,
and the brahmana said, "Wait a little, brother.

**1347.** Sit a while; don't go away empty-handed —
if a guest goes away unfed, hell will be my lot.

**1348.** I will go and beg alms and bring them to you;
wait here a little while, brother."

**1349.** Saying this, the brahmana went off to beg
and brought two coconuts and gave them to me.

**1350.** Bringing the alms, I offered them to the Lord beneath the tree,
and the Lord joyfully offered the fruits as bhoga.

*[p. 124]*

**1351.** Hearing about the brahmana, my Gora Raya
went to see him at dusk.

**1352.** A brahmana and his wife live there together;
for the sake of serving Gopala they beg alms.

**1353.** Seeing the Lord in his own house,
the brahmana came and stood before him with folded hands.

**1354.** The brahmana said, "With what shall I honour my guest?
How can I say to you, Lord, 'Go away'?

**1355.** We live here for the service of Gopala;
we both beg alms and serve.

**1356.** I have no seat — what can I give you to sit on?"
The brahmana's wife said, "Brahmana, offer your head!

**1357.** Look, lightning is playing over the guest's body!
Bring tulasi and place it at the guest's feet."

**1358.** Hurriedly the brahmana brought tulasi
and rushed to place it at the Lord's feet.

**1359.** Then Chaitanya, taking the brahmana by the hand, explained:
"Offer the tulasi at Gopala's feet."

**1360.** Hearing this, the brahmana began to weep,
and at once the merciful Lord embraced him.

**1361.** The Lord said, "You, brahmana, are most fortunate:
Bhagavan himself dwells in your house.

*[p. 125]*

**1362.** What can I say of your good fortune, thakura!
Gopala sports in your house.

**1363.** Your wife is Kamala[^100] herself;
think it over and see for yourself."

**1364.** The brahmana said, "I count your mercy my good fortune;
you are no ordinary man, O merciful one.

**1365.** Why else does lightning play over your body?
I feel the fragrance of lotus coming from your body.

**1366.** If you are not Bhagavan, O merciful one,
why does the scent of lotus come from your body?

**1367.** Place your foot on my head" —
saying this, the brahmana bowed his head down.

**1368.** At these words, biting his tongue,
merciful Chaitanyadeva drew back.

**1369.** The brahmana and his wife, distraught,
ran and bowed their heads at his feet.

**1370.** Spreading his arms, the Lord raised the brahmana,
and then began to sing with devotion.

**1371.** The brahmana's house seemed to become Vrindavana,
and the village folk came to hear the Name of Hari:

**1372.** "Hare Krishna, Hare Krishna, Krishna Krishna, Hare Hare,
Hare Rama, Hare Rama, Rama Rama, Hare Hare."[^101]

*[p. 126]*

**1373.** Merciful Chaitanya began this song,
and with it all the listeners grew excited.

**1374.** Hearing the Name from the Lord's lips, the village people
all rolled at the Lord's feet.

**1375.** Singing on and on, the night passed,
and in the morning my Lord took his leave.

**1376.** When the Lord had taken leave and gone out,
the people stood gazing after him like puppets.

**1377.** He signalled me, saying "Govinda",
and at once I lifted the load onto my shoulder.

**1378.** Near the land of Kandar the Nilagiri stands in splendour;
in the afternoon we slowly came there.

**1379.** Ah, how beautiful is that king of mountains, the Nilagiri!
He stands like a great being absorbed in meditation.

**1380.** At its foot hundreds of caves appear,
and a wondrous sight crowns its summit.

**1381.** Huge trees, climbing onto its head,
fan it like chowries, swaying in the wind.

**1382.** With a splashing sound the water of the springs falls;
seeing it, my curiosity grew.

**1383.** We wander about at the foot of the mountain
and see ever new beauties.

*[p. 127]*

**1384.** How many creepers have twined round the trees,
lovingly displaying the union of husband and wife!

**1385.** Peacocks sit on the branches and cry,
and birds of many kinds sing with sweet voices.

**1386.** Flowers of every kind have bloomed and lit the place up,
as if a garland were swinging at nature's neck.

**1387.** At night how many creepers glow brightly,
and fireflies shine in swarms on tree after tree.

**1388.** A little river flows with a murmuring sound;
sitting on its bank, the Lord performs his evening worship.

**1389.** At night we sat down beneath a tree,
and the Lord said to me, "Tonight we stay here."

**1390.** Saying just this, Gora closed his eyes
and passed the night in the Name of Hari.

**1391.** By the Lord's grace I feel neither hunger nor thirst,
and so I lie down wherever we happen to be.

**1392.** On the day the Lord tells me to beg,
on that day I go to the householders' doors.

**1393.** In the morning we went to the town of Gurjari;
many people live here happily.

**1394.** Here many fine houses stand in splendour;
Gora Raya went and sat at the edge of the town.

*[p. 128]*

**1395.** Here there is a tank called Agastya-kunda;
bathing in the tank, we were filled with joy.

**1396.** Gora Raya, having bathed in Agastya-kunda,
sat on the bank and sang the glories of Hari.

**1397.** Gradually two or four people appeared;
a brahmana brought milk and sugar and set them by him.

**1398.** Some said, "Guest, come to my house";
others said, "Say Krishna's name once more.

**1399.** The Name of Hari is very sweet in your mouth;
hearing the Name, all my sorrow and pain have gone.

**1400.** Krishna's name in your mouth is like nectar;
speak, speak of Krishna, let my soul be soothed."

**1401.** He listened to no one's words and did not reply;
how many wise men came to see the Lord!

**1402.** Closing his eyes, Gora-chand began to sway,
and tears burst forth from his eyes.

**1403.** My Gora Raya does not see the people;
crying "O Krishna!" he weeps and wets the earth.

**1404.** The Lord began to weep, sobbing;
his cloth came open and his matted hair spread over his back.

**1405.** His hair on end, distraught with weeping,
dishevelled, the Lord says all sorts of confused things.

*[p. 129]*

**1406.** Sometimes the Lord, drunk, rolls on the ground,
sometimes he flails and falls to the earth.

**1407.** "Here is my dear friend Mukunda-Murari!" —
saying this, Chaitanya the beggar rushes forward.

**1408.** Sometimes he says, "Come, Narahari, my life!
Hearing Krishna's name, I embrace you!"

**1409.** In this way Gora Raya says many things
and, drunk with feeling, runs about.

**1410.** Hearing of his wondrous power, all the worthy men
come into the Lord's presence.

**1411.** A great pandit named Arjuna
argued with the Lord, citing the proofs of scripture.

**1412.** Arjuna said, "I do not accept the doctrine of the jiva;
I know the doctrine of the Self and the doctrine of the jiva to be one."

**1413.** The Lord said, "You are a worthy pandit;
hear the proof of scripture and decide.

**1414.** If you know the meaning of the shruti 'Two birds',[^102]
why do you take two truths to be one?"

**1415.** Raising the subtle points of Vedanta, Gora Raya
explained everything to Arjuna in minute detail:

**1416.** "The jiva, the Self and the Supreme Self abide thus:
the Self is a great tree, and the jiva is its leaf.

*[p. 130]*

**1417.** What lesson have you learned, pandit thakura?
Put aside all this idle speculation.

**1418.** Maya is the shadow of God; he is not tainted by it.
By his will the jiva becomes subject to maya.

**1419.** One who can give up maya by the power of the Name
is a great sage in this world.

**1420.** Behind the curtain of maya there is Someone;
lift the curtain and behold Him."

**1421.** Saying this, he called out, "O Krishna!" —
and the place seemed to fall silent at once.

**1422.** How many times I had heard the Name from the Lord's lips,
but today my body thrilled.

**1423.** The Lord began to call, "Rama, Rama!" —
and the place then seemed to become Vaikuntha.

**1424.** A favourable breeze began to blow,
and the villagers began to appear in crowds.

**1425.** Hundreds of people standing all round
listened [to the Name] in silence.

**1426.** As if the gods of heaven, to hear the Name,
had come down above our heads and were listening.

**1427.** A bewitching scent of lotus spread,
and Gaura-Hari, unconscious, chants the Name.

*[p. 131]*

**1428.** Everyone's eyes are on the Lord's face,
tears falling unceasingly.

**1429.** Great Marathas[^103] come in crowds
and all together listen to the Name.

**1430.** Looking back behind me, I see
hundreds of respectable married women standing there.

**1431.** All listen to the Name of Hari with devotion,
the women wiping their tears with the ends of their saris.

**1432.** Countless Vaishnavas, Shaivas and sannyasis gather
and listen to the Name of Hari with closed eyes.

**1433.** Thus the Lord made this country drunk;
I have never seen such power.

**1434.** Sometimes Gora Raya recites [from the Bhagavata],
sometimes he sings songs and enraptures the listeners.

**1435.** Thus, chanting the Name of Hari,
the Lord, beside himself, began to dance.

**1436.** His matted locks came loose, his kaupina slipped off,
his body grey with dust, like the poorest of the poor.

**1437.** Dancing, the Lord lost consciousness
and then fell crashing to the ground.

**1438.** The Lord lay there like an inert thing,
and seeing this all the people came forward.

*[p. 132]*

**1439.** Some sprinkled water on the Lord's face,
some very carefully lifted him up.

**1440.** After two dandas the Lord rose and sat up,
and all, amazed, raised the cry of "Hari".

**1441.** In the afternoon a brahmana brought alms,
and my Lord made the offering beneath the tree.

**1442.** Leaving the town of Gurjari, my Gora Raya
wished to go to the town of Purna.[^104]

**1443.** For seven days he never held any conversation,
and went straight up the mountain at Vijapur.

**1444.** His only provision for the road is the Name of Hari;
climbing the mountain, the Lord rested.

**1445.** Here, climbing to the summit of the mountain,
he rejoiced to behold Hara and Gauri.

**1446.** Coming down from the mountain, Chaitanya Gosai
went north, and I followed behind.

**1447.** At once the Sahya-kulachala came into view;
seeing the kulachala, the Lord was overwhelmed with joy.

**1448.** I have seen the Mahendra and Malaya mountains with my own eyes,
but the beauty of Sahyagiri — ah, it cannot be told.

**1449.** From afar a blue line can be seen;
there my Lord hurries to see it.

*[p. 133]*

**1450.** Majestically the mountain stands;
seeing the mountain, my heart seemed to dance.

**1451.** The Lord said, "This mountain is an abode of joy."
Saying "abode of joy", he bowed down.

**1452.** Having seen the Sahya-kulachala, he went on,
and Lord Vishvambhara seemed filled with thrills.

**1453.** Leaving Sahyagiri, he went north,
saying "Hari, Hari" in boundless joy.

**1454.** He has no desires and goes in the poorest garb,
yet the Lord has flooded many lands with the rasa of devotion.

**1455.** A kaupina for clothing, dust smeared over his whole body;
seeing him, one would think: surely a madman.

**1456.** In time Gora-chand came to the town of Purna,
and many pandits came there in crowds.

**1457.** Many people here study the shastras,
and gradually many people came to see him.

**1458.** There is a pool called Achchhapara;
my Nimai went and sat beside it.

**1459.** A spreading bakula tree stands above it,
and my Lord settled down beneath it.

**1460.** Hundreds of pandits dwell here,
and day and night the pandits expound various shastras.

*[p. 134]*

**1461.** Hundreds of students come here
and study various shastras with their gurus.

**1462.** Here many people are skilled in learning,
and hundreds of Sanskrit schools stand in their midst.

**1463.** One who studies the Bhagavata
is accepted by everyone as a pandit.

**1464.** One who does not know the Gita and the Bhagavata
no one accepts as a pandit.

**1465.** A pandit is expounding the Bhagavata;
hearing it, tears flow from the Lord's eyes.

**1466.** A brahmavadin pandit came,
and the Lord refuted all his arguments.

**1467.** Many Vaishnava sadhus, gathering together,
were awakened on seeing the Lord's devotion.

**1468.** Closing his eyes, the Lord says, "Krishna, Krishna",
tears flowing from his eyes onto his breast.

**1469.** The Lord said, "My life, Mukunda-Murari,
come and rise in my heart.

**1470.** Radha-Krishna is all-powerful, the support of the universe;
who but Krishna bears the burden of this world?

**1471.** In whose pores are millions of universes —
how shall I see that Krishna, my life?

*[p. 135]*

**1472.** He ate earth and, on his mother's lap, opened his mouth,
and at once his mother saw the universe in his mouth.

**1473.** For that Krishna my heart is restless;
without Krishna my soul is in torment."

**1474.** A pandit came up and said,
"Your Krishna is in this pond."

**1475.** Hearing these words, the Lord started
and, his body thrilling, stood up.

**1476.** Never have I seen such a flood of tears;
Nimai, weeping in separation from Krishna, was distraught.

**1477.** Crying "Krishna!" he began to sob,
saying, "Without Krishna life is in vain."

**1478.** He soaked the lap of the earth with tears
and, weeping, said "Hari bol".

**1479.** Once he said to me, "What torment is this?
Without Krishna my soul can bear the pain no longer."

**1480.** Again that man said mockingly,
"Sannyasi, your Krishna is hiding in the water."

**1481.** This time Mahaprabhu, hearing his words,
at once leapt into the water in an ecstasy of love.

**1482.** Many people jumped into the pond,
pulled the Lord out onto the bank and lamented in many ways.

*[p. 136]*

**1483.** The man who had said Krishna was in the water —
all the pandits reproached him.

**1484.** The Lord said, "Why rebuke the maharaja for nothing?
Krishna always abides in water, on land and in the sky.

**1485.** Krishna around, Krishna beside, the world is full of Krishna,
but only one who is a devotee can see him.

**1486.** Devotion is the supreme truth in this world;
the devotee kicks even liberation on the head.

**1487.** One who cannot understand the wheel of maya
is most unfortunate in this world.

**1488.** False is homeland, false the homestead, false house and home;
for the sake of food the mind is distraught.

**1489.** Who is kin, who a stranger, who father and mother?
Tell me, whose neck will you put your arm round and call brother?

**1490.** There is no difference between man and woman — the difference is of the skin;
this the Vedas say again and again.

**1491.** In the darkness of delusion the soul forgets itself
and does not once say 'Hari' with its lips.

**1492.** Do not take false pride in riches, brothers;
always pass your time saying 'Hare Krishna'.

**1493.** This world is covered in the darkness of sin;
apart from Hari nothing in the world is true.

*[p. 137]*

**1494.** On the day the two birds leave the tree of the body,[^105]
on that day the inert body will be left lying.

**1495.** Why do you go on dreaming while awake, brothers?
No one will live forever; all will die.

**1496.** Come, brothers, let us all together raise the cry of 'Hari';
hearing the Name, Kritanta[^106] himself will tremble.

**1497.** However mighty a king of kings or emperor may be,
one day his royal market will surely break up.

**1498.** The maharaja rules by his might —
why then is his heart scorched by the three miseries?

**1499.** Priceless gems and pearls will not go with you;
understand by experience that wealth is empty and transient.

**1500.** With devotion say 'Hare Krishna', brothers,
and all of you will live in the world in perpetual bliss.

**1501.** Enchanted by maya, forgetting everything,
what are you all so proud of?

**1502.** In time even the seven kulachalas[^107] will vanish;
nothing in the inert world will remain.

**1503.** With devotion meditate on that True and Eternal One,
and hold fast to his feet.

**1504.** Lord Gadadhara[^108] will take away all suffering,
and this whole world will become like Vaikuntha.

*[p. 138]*

**1505.** One who is entangled in worldly wealth
cannot cut those cruel bonds.

**1506.** One who falls into bondage by his own wish
everyone calls a terrible fool.

**1507.** With the sword of the Name of Hari cut the bonds of maya,
and you will go without effort to the eternal abode.

**1508.** There will be no more birth, death or old age again and again;
call 'Krishna, Krishna' and the darkness will vanish.

**1509.** Work out your prarabdha[^109] in utter humility,
and then grief, pain and sorrow will go far away."

**1510.** Crowds of people came to see the Lord,
and countless pandits came to debate.

**1511.** Some say, "This sannyasi is no man";
some say, "This is a great soul."

**1512.** The Lord does not reply to anyone's words;
at the Name of Hari streams of love flow from both his eyes.

**1513.** Closing both eyes, the Lord chants the Name of Hari,
rolling over and over on the ground.

**1514.** The Lord said, "Which holy place shall I go to next?
Show me the way — where is Bholeshvar?"

**1515.** Near the village of Patas is Gora-ghat;
there is the great shrine called Bholeshvar.

*[p. 139]*

**1516.** "In Bholeshvar Mahadeva dwells" —
this was the advice given by the worthy Tunnu.

**1517.** The excellent brahmana named Tunnu is a very learned pandit;
it was from his words that the Lord learned of it.

**1518.** Tunnu said, "Bholeshvar is there."
Hearing this, the Lord set off to Shiva's presence.

**1519.** At Bholeshvar there is a fair every year;
hearing this, the Lord's heart danced.

**1520.** Looking at me, the Lord smiled a little
and said, "Let us turn back and go to Bholeshvar."

**1521.** Walking many roads over the mountains,
we came to Bholeshvar and saw a splendid tirtha.

**1522.** On the mountain stands a huge temple,
and inside it we saw Lord Bholeshvar.

**1523.** Here there is a Siddha-kupa;[^110]
drawing water from it, the Lord bathed.

**1524.** Seeing Bholeshvar, love arose in the Lord,
and with folded hands he sang many hymns.

**1525.** Losing consciousness, Gora fell to the ground
and rolled over and over many times.

**1526.** Having seen Bholeshvar, Gora Raya
hurried to see nearby Devaleshvar.

*[p. 140]*

**1527.** Seeing Devaleshvar, the Lord, jewel of virtues,
bowed down and lay rolling on the earth.

**1528.** Choked with love, he sang many hymns,
and people came with devotion to see the Lord.

**1529.** Devaleshvar dwells on a mountain,
and far from it the town of Jijuri[^111] stands in splendour.

**1530.** In Jijuri there is a god named Khandoba;
with the Lord we go to see Khandoba.

**1531.** A woman who for various reasons cannot be married
is married by the grace of Khandoba.

**1532.** Father and mother bring their daughter to Khandoba
and with much devotion give the daughter to Khandoba.

**1533.** The daughters of poor fathers live here
and serve Khandoba with affection.

**1534.** Hundreds of women, regarding Khandoba as their husband,
have gradually become beggars on the road.

**1535.** Deceived at Khandoba's shrine,
many women here live as prostitutes.

**1536.** Calling themselves Khandoba's wives, they commit sin,
and afterwards a very hard fate befalls them.

**1537.** Many people come here on pilgrimage,
and by guile they drag them down into hell.

*[p. 141]*

**1538.** All the poor maidens come here;
when they are married they say, "We are Khandoba's wives."

**1539.** Hearing this, the Lord, to see these women,
went there very secretly.

**1540.** People call them "muraris";[^112]
seeing the muraris, the Lord was moved to compassion.

**1541.** If one hears of the sorrows of the muraris,
compassion arises even in the cruellest heart.

**1542.** I cannot say how cruel a father is
who makes his own daughter a murari!

**1543.** Hearing this, the Lord went to the fine courtyard
to deliver all the women.

**1544.** I said, "There is no need to go to that place,"
but Chaitanya Gosai did not listen to my words.

**1545.** My Lord went into the muraris' quarter
and purified them all by giving them the Name of Hari.

**1546.** Unable to bear the women's sorrow,
he wants to deliver all the muraris.

**1547.** Hearing of the Lord's wondrous state with their own ears,
gradually many women came to this place.

**1548.** The Lord said to the women, "Chant the Name of Hari;
by the power of the Name you will surely gain the eternal abode.

*[p. 142]*

**1549.** Hari is most merciful, the refuge of the helpless;
all of you think of him as your own husband.

**1550.** All the gopis, to gain Krishna as their husband,
performed the Katyayani vow[^113] with pure hearts.

**1551.** If Krishna becomes your husband, no fear of worldly existence will remain;
know for certain that Krishna is the husband of all.

**1552.** Always call 'Krishna, Krishna' with devotion;
always say with your lips, 'Hare Krishna, Hare'."

**1553.** Saying this, my Lord began to chant the Name,
and at once his body was filled with thrills.

**1554.** Seeing the Lord's state, all the women
began to worship the Lord's feet.

**1555.** The Lord said, "I beg alms at the doors of householders;
I am utterly untouchable — do not touch me.

**1556.** Say 'Hari' with devotion and your sufferings will end;
by the power of the Name everyone's sins will be burnt to ashes.

**1557.** One who has sunk into sin without understanding
is freed from sin by the power of the Name of Hari."

**1558.** Hearing the teaching, all of Khandoba's wives
stood in rows before the Lord.

**1559.** Indira Bai came forward and said with folded hands,
"Have mercy on me, worthy sannyasi."

### Translator's notes

[^99]: In the edition the beginning of p. 123 partly repeats the end of p. 122 (the tiger episode), apparently through overlapping lines in the OCR. The repetition is omitted; the tiger episode is given in Part 6.
[^100]: Kamala is Lakshmi.
[^101]: The maha-mantra is given in the order "Hare Krishna… Hare Rama…", as it is usually sung in Bengal.
[^102]: "Two birds" (*dvā suparṇā*) is the famous mantra of the Mundaka Upanishad (3.1.1) and the Rigveda about two birds on one tree, one eating the fruit, the other only watching. It is the traditional basis for distinguishing the jiva from the Paramatma.
[^103]: *Maharahi* in the edition apparently means Maharashtrians (Marathas).
[^104]: Purna-nagar is Pune.
[^105]: A reference to the same mantra of the two birds.
[^106]: Kritanta, "he who makes an end", is Yama.
[^107]: The *kulachalas* are the seven principal mountain ranges of India in Puranic geography.
[^108]: Gadadhara, "bearer of the mace", is Vishnu.
[^109]: *Prarabdha*: karma that has already begun to bear fruit.
[^110]: *Siddha-kupa*: "well of perfection".
[^111]: Jijuri is Jejuri near Pune, the chief temple of Khandoba.
[^112]: *Murari* (*murali*): girls dedicated to Khandoba, comparable to devadasis. This episode is one of the best-known passages of the *Karcha*.
[^113]: The Katyayani vow was undertaken by the gopis of Vraja to obtain Krishna (*Bhagavata Purana* 10.22).
