# Part 5

*Sandhitirtha, the Kaveri, Nagar. Tanjore. Padmakot and the healing of the blind man. Tripatra and the Shaiva Bharga. The great forest. Srirangam and the brahmana who reads the Gita (pp. 81–100)*

*[p. 81]*

**878.** There the two rivers Nanda and Bhadra meet;
going there, he bathed at their confluence.

**879.** The master of that tirtha is Sadananda Puri;
he turned out to be a very learned pandit.

**880.** Sadananda Puri raised the doctrine of advaita,
but with a single argument the Lord broke all Puri's pride.

**881.** In the end Sadananda, amazed,
fell rolling at the Lord's feet in a surge of devotion.

**882.** Having given him the truth of devotion, my sannyasi
went to Chaipalla-tirtha to see its ways.

**883.** The dwellers of that tirtha are very virtuous;
there the sannyasi, son of Shachi, arrived.

**884.** There was a beautiful bhairavi[^71] named Siddheshvari,
radiant, a great ascetic, like Maheshvari herself.

**885.** From austerities she was nothing but skin and bone;
she sat at the foot of a bilva tree, repeating her mantra.

**886.** She sat motionless, absorbed in meditation;
seeing her, a sinner gains much knowledge.

**887.** She had reached a hundred years of age,
yet no one could tell it from her appearance.

**888.** There was another, a bhairavi named Shrigali;
she lived on the bank of the river.

*[p. 82]*

**889.** Having reverently seen Shrigali,
the son of Shachi went on to the bank of the Kaveri.

**890.** Having bathed in the Kaveri, young Gauranga
became lost in drinking the nectar of the Name of Hari.

**891.** In the afternoon he told me to go begging,
and I went into the town for alms.

**892.** Collecting a little parched grain and flour,
I brought them and set them before the Lord.

**893.** The Lord made rotis and offered the bhoga,
and on receiving the prasada I was refreshed.

**894.** My merciful Lord, in the town of Nagar,
rose in the morning and went on, full of love for Krishna.

**895.** Smeared with dust, hair matted, with no other talk,
Nimai goes along the road saying "Krishna, Krishna".

**896.** In the town of Nagar there are Sri Rama and Lakshmana;
going there, the Lord bowed to them.

**897.** Many people live in Nagar,
and there he made the Name of Hari manifest.

**898.** Seeing the movement of the Lord's love, the townsfolk,
from children to women, all turned from worldly things.

**899.** For three days he sang and danced there,
and the news of it spread from town to town.

*[p. 83]*

**900.** People gathered from ten kroshas around,
and one by one the Lord gave them all the Name of Hari.

**901.** Never have I seen so merciful a Lord:
Chaitanya Gosai gives the Name from house to house.

**902.** There was a wicked brahmana there;
calling the Lord a fraud, he tried to drive him away.

**903.** That brahmana thakura with his gang
shouted at the merciful Lord, "Away, away, away!"

**904.** The brahmana thakura said, "You cheat!
Dressed up as a false sannyasi, you're making trouble!

**905.** On the pretext of religious teaching you are ruining the village people.
I'll expose you and drive you out this very minute!"

**906.** He came before the Lord and heaped abuse on him,
and the Lord laughed his harsh words away.

**907.** At last Chaitanya Gosai called the brahmana
and said, "Beat me, brother, but say 'Hari'."

**908.** All the other people who were around him,
seeing the brahmana's behaviour, rushed to beat him.

**909.** Merciful Chaitanyadeva, reflecting,
began to speak to the brahmana, addressing him:

**910.** "Listen, merciful brahmana thakura:
say 'Hari, Hari' and you will find great happiness.

*[p. 84]*

**911.** In this transient body there is no happiness;
sink yourself in the Name of Hari and rejoice, brother.

**912.** This body is an inert lump; at the time of death
no one will go with you — this is certain.

**913.** Brothers, friends, wife, sons — no one belongs to anyone;
all of them are clothes, ornaments and heaps of money.

**914.** Jackals and dogs will eat the transient body;
it will rot and decay — be sure of this.

**915.** Raise your arms, say 'Hari' and dance with me,
and you will never again have to go to Yama's abode.

**916.** Wife or son — it is a juggler's game,
a gathering in the world for a couple of days.

**917.** The family plays tricks for the sake of food.
Think, brother: who is who to you?

**918.** Throwing the noose of love round his neck, the woman pulls hard,
and by that pull the foolish master of the house dies.

**919.** Sweet words on her lips and poison in her heart;
when she gets no money, she grinds her teeth.[^72]

**920.** She never lets him take the path of truth;
she binds him in bonds and destroys all his aspirations.

**921.** A woman's love is like poison,
yet the fool drinks it, calling it nectar.

*[p. 85]*

**922.** At the hour of death sons and daughters come near
and say, 'Father, what have you left for us?'

**923.** Think of all this, good brother brahmana,
and say 'Hari' with devotion — this is the alms I beg.

**924.** Strike me — that is no sorrow;
say 'Hari' with all your heart — this is the alms I beg.

**925.** Say 'Hari' with devotion; the Name will go with you,
and through it you will gain eternal happiness for endless time."

**926.** All the people who stood around
grew excited at the Lord's words.

**927.** Crying "Hari bol!", they all began to dance,
and the heart of the unbelieving brahmana was purified.

**928.** By the Lord's grace the brahmana grew drunk with the Name of Hari
and fell to the ground at the Lord's feet.

**929.** Thus having blessed the brahmana,
Chaitanyadeva left Nagar.

**930.** As he set out, the best of sannyasis
signalled me to get up quickly.

**931.** I tied the two pairs of sandals onto my head
and took the two loads on my two shoulders.

**932.** How many respectable women ran to see the Lord!
He went on to the town of Tanjore, seven kroshas away.

*[p. 86]*

**933.** In Tanjore lives a Vaishnava brahmana
named Dhaleshvara, who serves Krishna.

**934.** In his temple there are images of Radha and Krishna,
and there my Gora went slowly.

**935.** In the middle of the brahmana Dhaleshvara's courtyard
stands a huge bakula tree in its splendour.

**936.** Many Vaishnava sannyasis live there;
whoever sees that place, even a householder, becomes detached.

**937.** To its left is the Shiva Gosamaja;
the Lord had darshan of Shiva with love.

**938.** Nearby was a delightful lake,
and the brahmana Dhaleshvara showed him the way.

**939.** The lake is at Kumbhakarna-kapara;[^73]
seeing it, the Lord was amazed.

**940.** Near it is a hill called Chandalu,
standing as if painted on a canvas.

**941.** All round it are many caves,
where many sannyasis live to practise austerities.

**942.** How many sannyasi gosais, absorbed in meditation,
sit with closed eyes and ash-smeared bodies!

**943.** There an excellent brahmana named Bhatta
took the Lord to his own house.

*[p. 87]*

**944.** Hearing the name of Krishna, the brahmana went mad,
and merciful Chaitanya bestowed his grace on him.

**945.** The worthy Bhatta is always drunk with the Name of Hari;
when he takes Krishna's name, tears fall.

**946.** Seeing his ecstasy of love, beautiful Gauranga
said, "Brahmana, you are the best of sadhus.

**947.** Seeing you, even great sins are destroyed.
You, worthy brahmana, are my master;

**948.** seeing you, no sorrow or suffering remains."
At the praise the brahmana was deeply ashamed,

**949.** and he ran and fell at the Lord's feet.
He said, "Lord, why do you embarrass me so?

**950.** Words of praise only increase this wretch's pain.
I am a worm of hell, the crest-jewel of sinners;

**951.** you yourself have saved me by your grace.
Whoever touches me goes to hell;

**952.** today by your grace my sins are destroyed."
Seeing the brahmana's humility, the son of Shachi

**953.** said, "Brahmana, you are blessed, you are a holy man."
In an ecstasy of love the Lord dances in the brahmana's house,

**954.** and seeing it the brahmana rejoices at heart.[^74]

*[p. 88]*

**955.** There was a chief sannyasi named Sureshvara
who served Hari unceasingly.

**956.** Six others were under him;
all of them, detached, worship in the forest.

**957.** Huge trees stand in splendour all around;
the wondrous beauty of that forest is beyond words.

**958.** In the middle of that forest is a little river,
most charming, flowing with a murmuring sound.

**959.** The waters of the springs all join together
and become a river cutting through the forest.

**960.** There they all live and go nowhere,
and the village people bring them alms there.

**961.** That lovely place is a great holy land,
and there Mahaprabhu went on.

**962.** Seeing the Lord, that detached sannyasi
thrilled with joy, floating in bliss.

**963.** Gora Raya stayed there for some days,
and, drunk with bliss, sang the glories of Hari.

**964.** Then the sannyasi Sureshvara, amazed,
danced with the Lord, floating in the waters of love.

**965.** That place is in the kingdom of King Jaysingh;
the king takes no tax from sannyasis.

*[p. 89]*

**966.** That place is like the abode of Vaikuntha;
when one enters it, the heart is soothed.

**967.** Leaving that forest, the son of Shachi
went on to see the tirtha of Padmakot.

**968.** The goddess of Padmakot is the eight-armed Bhagavati;
going there, the Lord bowed down to her.

**969.** My Gora Raya sang many hymns,
and hundreds of people ran to see him.

**970.** Sitting there, the Lord gave teachings,
and hundreds of people gathered there.

**971.** The Lord said, "Brothers, all of you chant the Name of Hari;
by the power of the Name, brothers, you will all reach the eternal abode.

**972.** Tell me, what need is there of the inert body?
When you die, jackals and crows will eat it.

**973.** All of you have fallen into the net of maya;
tear the net, brothers, by chanting the Name of Hari.

**974.** Whose daughter, whose son — all a false pretence;
saying 'mine, mine', everyone has lost his senses.

**975.** Whose are you, who is yours, who is kin and who a stranger?
Maya is playing like a juggler.

**976.** Those who harbour worldly desires in this world

*[p. 90]*

suffer much torment, coming and going.

**977.** In the womb they live among excrement
and satisfy their cravings by eating faeces and urine.

**978.** Those who take the inert body to be consciousness —
how will they get out of hell?

**979.** Those who take the part for the whole
dwell forever in hell.

**980.** Not knowing that worldly life is a cruel trap,
people fall into that trap and suffer much grief.

**981.** The soul has no death; the sinful body dies.
Yet, deluded and enchanted by maya, the creature loves the body."

**982.** At this teaching everyone was amazed,
and the eight-armed goddess seemed to tremble.

**983.** As soon as the cry of "Hari" came from Lord Chaitanya's lips,
at once an echo rang out all around.

**984.** Boys, girls and young men went wild,
and the eight-armed goddess seemed to sway.

**985.** The fragrance of lotus began to waft all around,
and suddenly a rain of flowers fell there.

**986.** All the women threw flowers,
playing with flowers out of devotion.

**987.** There was a blind holy man there;
with devotion he clasped the Lord's feet.

*[p. 91]*

**988.** The Lord said, "Let me go, best of sadhus."
The blind man said, "Have mercy, Lord of the world."

**989.** The Lord said, "The Lady of the world is here."[^75]
The blind man said, "Have mercy on the wretched, Hari.

**990.** Have mercy on me, merciful Lord;
my heart weeps because I cannot see your form.

**991.** I am a blind wretch; I cannot see.
Show me your form, Chaitanya Gosai."

**992.** The Lord said, "You have no fleshly eyes,
but with the eye of knowledge you see everyone's heart.

**993.** The ignorant see with their eyes;
the wise see everything with eyes closed.

**994.** You are that wise one, worthy blind man;
I believe you see everything within."

**995.** The blind man said, "Why do you play tricks, treasury of compassion?
Have mercy on me as a blind man; you are Bhagavan.

**996.** For many years I have been lying in the temple,
and in a dream Bhagavati made it clear to me.

**997.** You are that Bhagavan, the refuge of the helpless —
so Bhagavati told me in a dream.

**998.** Then I shall know you, merciful one,
if you show your form to the blind.

*[p. 92]*

**999.** The feet of an ant uproot mountains,
the lame cross the Himalayas, remembering you.

**1000.** By your grace Agastya drank up the ocean,
and Prahlada did not die when he drank poison.

**1001.** In the form of cloth you saved Draupadi's honour;
you gave sight to the blind Bilvamangala."

**1002.** Hearing the blind man's words, Chaitanya Gosai
said, "Brother, why do you make me an offender?

**1003.** Hari dwells in every heart;
ask her and see — Bhagavati will tell you.

**1004.** What you have said is not to be heard.
Why make me an offender for nothing, brother?

**1005.** I am an ordinary man, a lowly sinner;
your heart has fallen into the well of illusion."

**1006.** The blind man said, "There is no more use in words;
show me your form — this is the alms I beg."

**1007.** The blind man wept in distress for the Lord,
and the Lord went over to the blind man.

**1008.** Seeing the blind man's devotion, beautiful Gauranga
slowly took his hand.

**1009.** Spreading his arms, Gora embraced the blind man,
and at the Lord's touch the blind man shuddered.

*[p. 93]*

**1010.** Opening his eyes as quick as lightning,
the blind man saw the Lord and was fulfilled.

**1011.** The very moment he saw my hero of dharma,
the blind man fell and gave up his body.

**1012.** Crying "Hari bol!" and circling the blind man,
the Lord began to dance, mad with love.

**1013.** Having buried the blind man's body[^76] in that very courtyard,
Gauranga left Padmakot.

**1014.** Leaving Padmakot, the Lord, in the town of Tripatra,[^77]
had darshan of Shiva Chandeshvara.

**1015.** If one cries "Bom!" in his temple,
the echo of the sound goes round for a whole danda.[^78]

**1016.** In that courtyard is a huge bilva tree;
everyone calls it the perfected bilva tree.

**1017.** Many Shaivas live there,
and their leader is the most learned Bhargadeva.

**1018.** Bhargadeva is a great scholar of philosophy;
every day he worships Hara with joy.

**1019.** There my Lord, the son of Shachi,
closed his eyes and sang hymns with devotion.

**1020.** Old Bhargadeva, seeing the son of Shachi,
called all the renunciants and said:

*[p. 94]*

**1021.** "You have all heard of a wonderful sannyasi
who is wandering this country in quest of holy places.

**1022.** Everyone tells of his marvellous greatness;
this is that sannyasi, the son of Shachi.

**1023.** In his eyes there is always the shambhavi mudra;[^79]
seeing him, no sinner or sufferer remains.

**1024.** By the gift of the nectar of the Name he has flooded the land
and made children, women and the old drunk with the Name.

**1025.** I have heard that he has left his own country
and come to deliver the unbelievers by giving them the Name of Hari.

**1026.** Look, brothers, here is that young sannyasi;
this is the one everyone calls Chaitanya Gosai.

**1027.** As I heard of him, so today I have seen him.
Ah, what a form! I have never seen anything like it.

**1028.** This best of sannyasis is not a man;
otherwise why should my heart melt at the sight of him?

**1029.** This person is an incarnation of God;
all of you bow down, clasping his feet."

**1030.** Saying this, Bharga bowed down,
and the Lord bit his tongue and drew back.

**1031.** The Lord said, "For shame, Bharga, what have you said!
My birthplace is the town of Nadia.

*[p. 95]*

**1032.** I am an ordinary man, that is certain.
Why do you frighten me for nothing by calling me an avatara?

**1033.** Why do you all again and again call me
an incarnation of God and make me an offender?

**1034.** I have come to you on pilgrimage.
Raise your arms, brothers, say 'Hari' and dance, all of you.

**1035.** Why make a fuss, calling me an avatara?
Come, let us all together say 'Hari, Hari bol'.

**1036.** Never call me an incarnation of God.
You yourself are Shankara in person, lord of the world."

**1037.** The Lord returned the obeisance with folded palms,
and Bhargadeva started up in fright.

**1038.** Bharga rolled at his feet,
fallen on the ground, grey with dust.

**1039.** Bharga said, "Listen, listen, Chaitanya Gosai:
have mercy on me, an old man — this is the alms I beg.

**1040.** I know nothing of worship or spiritual practice;
I am always proud of being a detached sannyasi.

**1041.** Go and play your tricks, Lord,
on someone who has not seen through the cunning of your lila.

**1042.** Go to someone who has not recognised you,
and there hide your form by your stratagems.

*[p. 96]*

**1043.** I am old, and because of the defect of my eyes my sight is dim;
that is why I see you as a dark-complexioned youth.[^80]

**1044.** People say your colour is like gold,
but this unfortunate one sees you dark, as the fruit of his fate.

**1045.** Just once, be merciful, Chaitanya Gosai,
and show your form, if I may be granted to see it.

**1046.** Be merciful, Lord, and give me the gift of sight;
be kind and make me fortunate.

**1047.** If in your mercy you have shown yourself to this lowly one,
lift your foot and place it on my head."

**1048.** Hearing the old man's words, Shachi's son
said, "Why do you make me an offender again and again?

**1049.** I came here to see a holy man;
never mind merit, I have become a partaker of sin."

**1050.** Hearing this, Bharga folded his hands
and said, "Take your meal here today — that is my request."

**1051.** The Lord stayed a week in the town of Tripatra,
and many people gathered there.

**1052.** For seven days the Lord performed Hari-sankirtana,
and everyone became drunk with the Name of Hari.

**1053.** Many people there became Vaishnavas,
and many swung tulasi garlands round their necks.

*[p. 97]*

**1054.** What more can I say about my Lord?
Wondrous is his power, marvellous his form.

**1055.** At the end of the day Gora Raya eats a little food;
from not eating, his body is as thin as a stick.

**1056.** He is nothing but skin and bone,
and yet the radiance of his body is like fire.

**1057.** All are enchanted by the beauty of his body;
without any effort, the fragrance of lotus always comes from it.

**1058.** Whoever opens his eyes and looks at him
is dazzled by the power of his effulgence.

**1059.** After seven days, having bestowed his grace on Bharga,
my sannyasi left Tripatra.

**1060.** Bharga follows behind as a companion;
taking Bhargadeva by the hand, he sent him back.

**1061.** Hundreds of thousands come to see the Lord,
and the Lord never tires of giving Krishna's name.

**1062.** No one speaks of anything but the Name of Hari;
seeing so many devotees of Krishna, all are beside themselves.

**1063.** Everyone calls the Lord "the mad Hari-bola",[^81]
and many cry "Hari bol!" to tease him.

**1064.** How many people run after him crying "Hari"!
Hearing the Name, my Lord smears his body with dust.

*[p. 98]*

**1065.** Gone mad with the Name of Hari, Gora-chand
now and then falls in the dust and rolls about.

**1066.** When the Lord sent Bhargadeva away,
just then many children came to that place.

**1067.** Someone said, "Hey, brothers, there goes that madman!
All of you shout 'Hari, Hari' and drive him wild!"

**1068.** All the children began to tease him,
and the Lord, the son of Shachi, danced along with them.

**1069.** Sometimes he laughs, sometimes he weeps,
sometimes he falls and drops to the ground.

**1070.** Gradually all the people went away who knows where,
and in our path lay a vast forest.

**1071.** There is no end to it — thickets for fifty yojanas,
and into it the son of Shachi went.

**1072.** With no fear in my heart I walk along the footpath;
ahead goes my Chaitanya Gosai.

**1073.** We stay under trees; there is no one there.
We satisfy our hunger by eating the fruits of the trees.

**1074.** How can I tell how many wondrous fruits there were!
I carefully eat those fruits that surpass nectar.

**1075.** After three days we came upon a band of sannyasis,
and I was greatly delighted.

*[p. 99]*

**1076.** Joining them, we went on slowly,
and after a fortnight we came out of the forest.

**1077.** Beyond the forest lies the pure Ranga-dhama;[^82]
going there, the Lord gives the Name of Hari.

**1078.** In Ranga-dhama is the image of Lord Narasimha;
seeing it, devotion arises even in an unbeliever's heart.

**1079.** Prahlada stands before him with joined palms,
while the Lord slays the king of the demons.

**1080.** Never have I seen such an image.
Seeing it, Chaitanya Gosai went mad.

**1081.** The son of Shachi now falls, now rises,
now the Lord is sunk in meditation with eyes closed.

**1082.** Seeing Nrisimha, the ocean of love surges up,
and now and then he falls crashing to the ground.

**1083.** Sometimes the Lord, like a madman, babbles incoherently,
and foam comes from his mouth in gushes.

**1084.** Sometimes his upper cloth is soaked with tears,
sometimes he falls to the ground, trembling.

**1085.** Some say, "Where has this madman come from?"
Some come and fall at the Lord's feet.

**1086.** There was a practising brahmana named Yudhishthira,
the crest-jewel of Vaishnavas, saintly in conduct.

*[p. 100]*

**1087.** The brahmana worships and serves in this holy place,
and every day by reading the Gita frees himself from the fever of the world.[^83]

**1088.** Everyone laughs at the unlettered brahmana reading the Gita,
but the brahmana pays no heed, and that is why he was dear to the Lord.

**1089.** He heeds no one as he studies the Gita;
setting his heart on it, he reads in solitude.

**1090.** As long as he reads the Gita, the brahmana weeps;
seeing his tears, the Lord's heart melted.

**1091.** The Lord said, "Why do you weep, brahmana thakura?"
The brahmana said, "When I read the Gita, I am full of joy.

**1092.** I see Krishna on Arjuna's chariot;
for the delight of that I read the Gita, sannyasi gosai."

**1093.** The Lord said, "You get to see Krishna —
then be kind and embrace me.

**1094.** I have never seen a holy man like you;
by serving you I shall get to see Krishna."

**1095.** The brahmana gazed fixedly at the Lord
and fell prostrate at the Lord's feet.

**1096.** The Lord said, "Listen, listen, worthy brahmana:
do not tell this to anyone and everyone.

**1097.** You are most fortunate, crest-jewel of saints;
Krishna himself shows himself to you every day."

### Translator's notes

[^71]: A *bhairavi* is a female ascetic of the Shaiva or Tantric tradition.
[^72]: Literally "makes *khish-mish*", an expression of grumbling discontent.
[^73]: Kumbhakarna-kapara ("Kumbhakarna's skull") is a local name; the index of the 1926 edition lists *Kumbhakarna-karpara*.
[^74]: The division of lines into couplets seems to be disturbed in the edition here; the translation follows the order of the lines.
[^75]: Chaitanya hints that mercy should be asked of the goddess of the temple.
[^76]: *Samadhi*, the burial of a holy man (the bodies of sadhus are buried, not cremated).
[^77]: Tripatra is perhaps Tiruvattur or Tiruppattur; the identification is uncertain.
[^78]: A *danda* is a measure of time, 24 minutes.
[^79]: The *shambhavi mudra* is the yogic "gaze of Shambhu", in which the open eyes do not see the outer world.
[^80]: Govinda alludes to the traditional idea of Chaitanya as Krishna concealed by Radha's golden colour: Bharga sees him dark, like Krishna.
[^81]: *Khepa Hari-bola*, "the madman who shouts 'Hari bol'".
[^82]: Ranga-dhama is Srirangam.
[^83]: Compare the famous episode of the *Chaitanya-charitamrita* (Madhya 9) about the illiterate brahmana at Srirangam who weeps over the Gita because he sees Krishna on Arjuna's chariot. In the *Karcha* this brahmana is named Yudhishthira.
