# Part 12

*Puri: the festival of the Name, Prataparudra in the procession. Govinda is sent to Shantipur for Advaita. The manuscript breaks off (pp. 223–224 and the ending from the 1926 edition)*

*[p. 223]*

**2441.** Shantacharya, Vipradasa, Rupa and Sanatana[^152]
came in a crowd to see him.

**2442.** All, drunk with joy, chant the Name of Hari,
and merciful Chaitanya gives devotion from house to house.

**2443.** "Who will take the Name of Hari? Come, my brothers!" —
saying this, Nimai distributes the Name of Hari.

**2444.** By the Lord's mercy no sinner or sufferer remained;
the Lord gives the Name of Hari wherever he may be.

**2445.** The great tirtha of Puri became an abode of joy;
children, women and the old all chant the Name of Hari.

**2446.** Beasts and birds dance on hearing the Name;
before him the sea dances with arms outstretched.

**2447.** The old dance, the young dance, the children dance;
respectable women come out onto the road to see him.

**2448.** On one side the lord of rivers[^153] began to dance,
on the other the ocean of love surged up.

**2449.** As if the trees and creepers, drunk with love,
were shedding tears in the guise of dew.

**2450.** Every day there are new joys in Puri;
whoever has seen it knows — to whom shall I tell it?

**2451.** Mridangas, kettle-drums and kartals are playing,
and in their midst my darling of Shachi dances.

*[p. 224]*

**2452.** Ramadasa is a great master at beating the kettle-drum;
that is why he comes to the kirtana every day.

**2453.** The great devotee Ramadasa, full of loving attachment,
goes ahead of the kirtana, beating the drum.

**2454.** Prataparudra, joyfully leaving his royal throne,
comes every day to Mishra's house to watch the dance.

**2455.** When Mahaprabhu goes out in nagara-kirtana,
the maharaja, dressed as a pauper, runs behind.[^154]

**2456.** With both arms raised high and his limbs loosened,
the Lord goes dancing along, drunk with love.

**2457.** With half-closed eyes he gazes upwards,
now and then falling crashing to the ground.

**2458.** All are drunk with the Name of Hari, men and women alike;
drunk, the respectable women run along in rows.

**2459.** Thousands upon thousands go on all sides,
and ahead the Lord goes, dancing.

**2460.** Thus, chanting the Name, he passes his days,
and spends his nights in seclusion with Raya.[^155]

**2461.** One day Mahaprabhu, in his love for Krishna,
rushed forward to embrace Mahavishnu.

**2462.** Heeding no obstacle, he runs in his longing
and falls to the ground before the barrier.[^156]

*[The ending, from the 1926 edition; in the base edition the text breaks off here]*

**2463.** From that day the Lord does not enter the temple,
but every day has darshan from a distance.

**2464.** Standing at the door of the Bhoga-mandira,
he gazes fixedly at Mahavishnu.

**2465.** Resting his left hand on the Garuda pillar,
the Lord has darshan, drunk with love.

**2466.** Thus, staying in Puri for some time,
he gazed at Jagannatha with love.

**2467.** One day my Lord, in Mishra's house,
was singing Krishna's glories with the devotees.

**2468.** Calling me, "Govinda!",
he told me to go to the Acharya.[^157]

**2469.** As soon as I received the order, I took leave of the Lord,
bowed down and set out for Shantipur.

**2470.** The Lord patted me on the back and blessed me,
and tears flowed from my eyes in a hundred streams.

**2471.** The Lord said, "Do not weep, Govinda, my dear;
bring the Acharya here and rejoice."

**2472.** Hearing these words, tears flowed from my eyes;
my heart cannot bear the arrow of separation from the Lord.

**2473.** How shall I bear the force of separation from the Lord?
A cruel anguish has arisen in my heart…

*(The manuscript is incomplete.)*[^158]

### Translator's notes

[^152]: Shantacharya and Vipradasa are associates of Chaitanya. The mention of Rupa and Sanatana at Puri in 1512 is an obvious anachronism: according to all other sources they first met Chaitanya at Ramakeli in 1514 and came to Puri later still. This is one of the passages relied on by those who deny the *Karcha*'s authenticity.
[^153]: The lord of rivers is the ocean.
[^154]: Compare the famous episode of the *Chaitanya-charitamrita* (Madhya 13) in which King Prataparudra sweeps the road before Jagannatha's chariot.
[^155]: With Ramananda Raya.
[^156]: Compare CC Madhya 1: when Chaitanya first saw Jagannatha he rushed forward to embrace him and fell unconscious; later he viewed the Lord from the Garuda pillar, holding it with his left hand. The *Karcha* places this episode after the return from the South.
[^157]: To Advaita Acharya at Shantipur, to announce Mahaprabhu's return.
[^158]: In the 1926 edition these lines are followed by the editor's mark "(*khandita*)", "incomplete", and a note: "Something of what happened after this is to be found in the *Chaitanya-chandrodaya*, but much more in Prema Dasa's *Chaitanya-chandrodaya-kaumudi*." The manuscript on which the edition was based thus broke off at this point.
