# Part 9

*The Sabarmati and two Bengalis. Ghogha and the courtesan Barmukhi. Ruined Somnath. Junagadh and Girnar: Dattatreya's footprints and the story of Krishna's footprints (pp. 163–182)*

*[p. 163]*

**1780.** "He is not a pandit who prides himself on learning,
if his heart has not been purified by the rasa of devotion.

**1781.** One who counts millions of universes as straw
and is drunk with love — him I accept as a devotee.

**1782.** Love and devotion are the essence of truth, so the shrutis say;
the devotee of Hari, drunk with love, does not want liberation.

**1783.** One for whom love and devotion are the ornament of his throat
is an eternal associate of Krishna.

**1784.** One who tastes the sweetness[^122] of love for Krishna —
him I will serve at his feet without dispute.

**1785.** One who, in this very body, has cut his bonds
and is drunk with love for Krishna — he is a thakura.

**1786.** Mahamaya, throwing dust into the eye of knowledge,
has tied a knot between consciousness and matter.

**1787.** That is why the foolish in this world,
deluded, identify the self with the inert body.

**1788.** One who gives up identification with the inert body
is my master — so the Vedas say.

**1789.** The supreme Vaishnava, immersed in love for Krishna,
does not dissemble by making a great noise.

**1790.** One who does not know the primary meaning of Vedanta
regards the jiva and Brahman as one."

*[p. 164]*

**1791.** Saying this, the next day Gora, the delight of hearts,
left the town and went west.

**1792.** Going a little way, we saw the river Shubhramati,[^123]
singing with a murmuring voice, full of rasa.

**1793.** Crossing the river, we saw two or four people
going to Dvaraka on pilgrimage.

**1794.** Among them I saw two Bengalis —
great devotees, Ramananda and Govinda Charana.

**1795.** Seeing people from Gauda after so long,
my heart seemed to dance with joy.

**1796.** I asked Ramananda, "Where is your home?"
Ramananda said, "Brother, in Kulina-gram."[^124]

**1797.** The Lord is bathing in the river Shubhramati,
and meanwhile Ramananda is talking with me.

**1798.** Ramananda said, "Where are you going?"
I said, "I am going with the Lord to Dvaraka."

**1799.** Ramananda, hearing Chaitanyadeva's name,
lit up in the face like a flash of lightning.

**1800.** Ramananda ran and bowed down,
and Chaitanya asked him a few questions.

**1801.** Ramananda Dasa is a supreme Vaishnava;
the Lord gave Ramananda Dasa encouragement.

*[p. 165]*

**1802.** The Lord said, "Ramananda, seeing you,
the feeling of Gauda has awakened in my heart.

**1803.** How long ago did you leave home?
How long ago did you come to this holy land?

**1804.** Come then, let us go together to Dvaraka,
and all of us joyfully behold the Lord of Dvaraka."

**1805.** Hearing this from the Lord's lips, Ramananda Dasa
rejoiced to stay with the Lord.

**1806.** Having bathed, the Lord walks slowly on
and arrives at a village called Ghogha.

**1807.** A courtesan named Barmukhi[^125] lives here;
there is no telling of her treasures.

**1808.** By her trade she has amassed great wealth;
her clothes and ornaments are very costly.

**1809.** Barmukhi lives in a huge house
and goes about by every trick to rob the rich of their wealth.

**1810.** Wearing [fine clothes], she looks about languidly;
how many libertines go to her house!

**1811.** She lives here with many servants and maids;
everyone knows of her pomp and fame.

**1812.** There is a huge garden called Piyar-kanan;[^126]
the Lord went to the edge of that garden.

*[p. 166]*

**1813.** A very large neem tree stands here;
who knows why, but the Lord went and sat there.

**1814.** At his command I go to householders' doors
to beg for fruits, roots and the like.

**1815.** Having begged, I came back at midday,
and the Lord made the offering with a joyful heart.

**1816.** Then the three of us received the prasada —
I, Ramananda and Govinda Charana.

**1817.** Laughing, I called Govinda my "mite".[^127]
The Lord said, "Why do you leave Ramananda out?

**1818.** If Govinda has become your mite,
then Ramananda shall be my mite."

**1819.** Laughing, he called Ramananda his mite,
and began the Name, clapping his hands.

**1820.** Ramananda, hearing this from the Lord's lips,
stood to one side, rubbing his hands in embarrassment.

**1821.** Many people gathered to hear the Name,
and tears flowed from the Lord's eyes in a hundred streams.

**1822.** The tears began to spurt like a squirt-gun,
and seeing this the people of Ghogha were amazed.

**1823.** Seeing that Hari-sankirtana of the Lord,
two or four people became drunk with love.

*[p. 167]*

**1824.** Tears flow from the eyes of the village people;
many people came and stood in rows.

**1825.** What a mood of devotion it was cannot be described;
without blinking they all gaze at the Lord's face.

**1826.** Sometimes the Lord laughs, sometimes he weeps,
sometimes, raising his arms, he dances and sings.

**1827.** Sometimes he trembles all over and sweat flows,
sometimes in an ecstasy of love he remains silent.

**1828.** Sometimes he totters, his hair on end,
sometimes he calls aloud, "Krishna, my life!"

**1829.** "The young sannyasi is drunk with love of God" —
so the people of Ghogha whisper to one another.

**1830.** As he says "Hari, Hari", streams of bliss flow;
all stand there like puppets.

**1831.** His eyes half-closed, his matted hair loosened,
his body smeared with dust and grime.

**1832.** "Where is Krishna, my life?" he calls,
sometimes raising his hands and gazing upwards.

**1833.** "Govinda, where is Krishna? Bring him to me!
Where is Krishna, my life? Show him to me!"

**1834.** Once, saying this, he ran off
and, spreading his arms, embraced the neem tree.

*[p. 168]*

**1835.** Nimai became drunk with love of God;
never have I seen such madness.

**1836.** For many days I have been with him, wandering through many lands,
but never have I seen such ecstasy.

**1837.** Ramananda and Govinda Charana on either side
clapped their hands and raised the cry of "Hari" again and again.

**1838.** At the roadside there was a huge pit,
and in his ecstasy he rolled down into it.

**1839.** A wicked man came along, bustling about,
and said to the Lord, "Why are you deceiving people?

**1840.** You will fool the village people and take their money;
that is why you go about shouting 'Hari'.

**1841.** I have come to test the sannyasi;
I have seen hundreds of false sannyasis."

**1842.** When that unbeliever said this,
the village people wanted to thrash him.

**1843.** The Lord said, "Brothers, whom will you beat?
Make him drink the nectar of the Name of Hari.

**1844.** His throat has dried up with thirst;
pour a stream of nectar into his mouth.

**1845.** Without devotion his heart has withered;
give him the Name and destroy his fear of Yama.

*[p. 169]*

**1846.** The unbeliever's heart is like a desert;
give it the power to bear fruit.

**1847.** Come to me, holy man, I will give you the Name of Hari;
I will lift the burden of your sins from you.

**1848.** By the power of this mantra all suffering will go;
reciting the mantra of the Name of Hari bears fruit at once.

**1849.** One who recites this great mantra —
that sinner never goes to hell.

**1850.** When there is such an easy mantra in the world,
why should the sinner wander uselessly on evil paths?"

**1851.** Saying this, Mahaprabhu went up to him
and poured the nectar of the Name of Hari into his ears.

**1852.** Merciful Chaitanya, to deliver living beings,
wanders here and there, undisturbed.

**1853.** Seeing all this from her window,
Barmukhi pondered within herself.

**1854.** Having seen the Lord's wondrous mercy with her own eyes,
sitting alone, she reproached herself.

**1855.** Barmukhi said, "For shame, for shame! For the sake of money
I sell my body a hundred times a day.

**1856.** By sinful trade, uniting with other men —
for shame! — every day I indulge in amorous play.

*[p. 170]*

**1857.** This sannyasi I see is equal to God;
I will leave everything and go to him.

**1858.** The sannyasi has no money or cowries with him —
why then am I happy when I see him?

**1859.** Why should I suffer hell sitting at home?
Will Hari not have mercy on me too?

**1860.** What mantra did this sannyasi recite into the ear of Balaji the villain,[^128]
that he delivered him?

**1861.** I will go to him and destroy my sins;
I will go up to him and clasp his feet tightly."

**1862.** So Barmukhi spoke from the window,
and everyone was glad to hear her words.

**1863.** The people all around, taking up the news,
made a great commotion, laughing.

**1864.** A moment later the courtesan came down,
and her maid named Mira went behind her.

**1865.** Then Barmukhi said humbly to Mira,
"From today I give all my wealth to you.

**1866.** I have much money — I count it all as nothing;
from today I have become a beggar on the road."

**1867.** Barmukhi, the maidservant,[^129] let down her hair,
like a bank of clouds beside motionless lightning.

*[p. 171]*

**1868.** The long net of her hair falls below her hips,
while Shachi's darling sits with his eyes closed.

**1869.** All, seeing the wondrous radiance of her beauty,
stand gazing at her face.

**1870.** Barmukhi, with folded hands, says again and again,
"Cut my bonds, my sannyasi.

**1871.** I am a great sinner, a worm of hell;
if you do not have mercy, I shall go to perdition.

**1872.** Tell your maidservant by what means I shall be saved,
how, after death, I shall escape the fear of Yama.

**1873.** What further use have I for this sinful body?"
Saying this, she cut off her long hair.

**1874.** Putting on simple clothes, she covered her nakedness
and stood before the Lord with folded hands.

**1875.** The Lord said, "Barmukhi, I will tell you
a few words — carry them out in every respect.

**1876.** Make a tulasi grove here,
and living in its midst, practise the worship of Krishna."

**1877.** "You are Krishna, you are Hari," said Barmukhi,
and saying just this she fell at the Lord's feet.

**1878.** When Barmukhi fell at his feet,
the Lord at once drew back three or four steps.

*[p. 172]*

**1879.** And all the other people standing near
cried, "Blessed, blessed!" on seeing the courtesan.

**1880.** The maid Mirabai wept bitterly,
and Barmukhi said to her with a smile:

**1881.** "Listen carefully, Mira, to my words:
I have given you all the wealth I have.

**1882.** Serve guests well when they come;
in solitude, set your mind on the Name of Hari.

**1883.** Do no sinful deeds — I adjure you;
worship Sri Radha-Krishna with loving attachment.

**1884.** To love is good indeed, but not with a rogue;
make your love with Krishna, Mira.

**1885.** Give body, mind and life — everything — to Krishna;
then you will gain Krishna, the eternal treasure.

**1886.** Listen carefully to what I say, Mira:
keep company with no one but Krishna.

**1887.** Krishna will surely be merciful to you;
never, at any cost, give up the treasure that is Krishna.

**1888.** By the Lord's mercy my bonds are cut;
from today my dwelling is the tulasi grove."

**1889.** Saying this, Barmukhi took her chanting beads
and made a tulasi grove, forgetting all her pain.

*[p. 173]*

**1890.** Having given devotion to the courtesan Barmukhi,
the Lord hurried off to see Somnath.

**1891.** The Lord goes in the direction of Jafarabad,
and with great difficulty arrives there in three days.

**1892.** The people of Jafarabad are very poor,
yet they show great honour to guests.

**1893.** Many villagers brought alms,
and my Lord made rotis and offered them.

**1894.** Going into a gardener's garden,
we spent the night there happily.

**1895.** Rising in the morning, we go to Somnath
and, after six days, arrive there.

**1896.** There is none of its former beauty, nor that temple;
seeing its sorry state, tears flowed from my eyes.

**1897.** Heaps of rubble and broken remains are there;
seeing them, I felt a great pain in my heart.

**1898.** The beauty of the temple buildings has gone;
seeing this, my Lord weeps in distress.

**1899.** Weeping, my Lord began to say,
"What has the wicked Yavana[^130] done here!

**1900.** Where have you hidden, Lord, for fear of the Yavanas?
Show yourself once and soothe my heart."

*[p. 174]*

**1901.** Alas, alas, this sorrow is beyond words;
Gora Raya weeps, calling on Somnath.

**1902.** The Lord said, "Who carried off such splendour?
For money's sake the villain brought it to this.

**1903.** O Lord Somnath, to see you
my heart is in turmoil — I cannot bear it.

**1904.** I can bear separation from you no longer;
calling on you, my heart is breaking.

**1905.** Alas, alas, what sin did the devotees commit,
for what sin do they no longer see you, O god?

**1906.** In separation from you hundreds of pandas[^131]
are sunk in an ocean of sorrow.

**1907.** Did you go to Kailasa for fear of the Yavanas
and now forget your dear devotees?

**1908.** Seeing all this my heart is bursting,
and tears stream down onto my breast.

**1909.** Ah, only ruins are left lying here —
how can sinful eyes bear it?

**1910.** Come, Lord Somnath, into my heart;
in my heart I shall behold your form.

**1911.** Where have you hidden, Lord? I do not see you.
To whom shall I tell how my soul feels?

*[p. 175]*

**1912.** Alas, alas, Gangadhara! To see you
people will no longer come from foreign lands.

**1913.** Pilgrims used to come to see you with pride,
but now that glory has been wiped away.

**1914.** The Yavanas in their hatred, committing outrages,
have carried off gems, pearls and other riches.

**1915.** Alas, Lord Smarahara,[^132] where have you stayed?
In your mercy you do not show yourself to your devotees."

**1916.** Thus my Lord lamented,
and just then a storm arose in the sky.

**1917.** Dust flew up and darkened all around,
and the pandas shut the doors of their huts.

**1918.** We all sat by the outer door,
and the Lord, the Hari-bola, came and sat in our midst.

**1919.** Just then an avadhuta sannyasi came
and looked again and again at Gora-chand.

**1920.** His whole body smeared with ash, no cloth,
his matted hair tied up high, of wondrous form.

**1921.** His two eyes are red,
"Hara, Hara" on his lips, his heart pure.

**1922.** Two languid eyes, beautiful to see;
he came and blessed him with raised hand.[^133]

*[p. 176]*

**1923.** Seeing him, my Lord rose,
and he, saying something, vanished.

**1924.** Where the avadhuta went, he was seen no more.
Then, with a slight smile, my Chaitanya

**1925.** walked three times around Somnath.
I, Ramananda and Govinda Charana

**1926.** sing Hari-sankirtana with the Lord;
for the pleasure of Thakura Somnath

**1927.** the Lord sings kirtana, melting with love.
Two or four pandas came and joined us

**1928.** and began asking us for something.
Smiling, the Lord said, "A sannyasi

**1929.** has no money, cowries, food or cloth to give."
Hearing these words, Govinda Charana

**1930.** placed two coins in the panda's hand.
The panda took us to the edge of the holy tank,

**1931.** and my Lord took water and poured it on his head.
Leaving Somnath, we go to Junagadh.[^134]

**1932.** A large place indeed, but there is no tirtha;
many mansions stand in splendour all around.

**1933.** In Junagadh Gora Raya spent two days.

*[p. 177]*

**1934.** In one place there is the worship of Ranchhodji;
in the evening we go there for darshan.

**1935.** An excellent brahmana named Miraji performs the service;
we go and arrive at his house.

**1936.** Miraji received us with devotion,
and the Lord spent the night at his house.

**1937.** The brahmana brings milk, sugar and flour,
and the Lord spends the night joyfully.

**1938.** Nearby is the most charming Girnar hill;[^135]
Lord Vishvambhara goes towards it.

**1939.** Miraji, coming up with devotion,
asks the Lord again and again to stay.

**1940.** Humbly the Lord says to the brahmana,
"We shall all go to Girnar hill.

**1941.** There I shall see the feet of Guru Dattatreya;
let me go — that is the alms I ask of you."

**1942.** Hearing the Lord's words, the worthy brahmana
said nothing more, good or bad.

**1943.** Chaitanya Gosai sets out,
and we follow behind him like shadows.

**1944.** A band of sannyasis had come to this place
and were sitting with gloomy faces.

*[p. 178]*

**1945.** Their leader, named Bhargadeva,
had fallen ill and was staying there.

**1946.** Bhargadeva is tossing about beneath a tree;
the Lord came there quickly.

**1947.** Seeing Bhargadeva ill, Gora Raya
orders me to look after him.

**1948.** I, Ramananda and Govinda Charana —
the three of us set to nursing the sick man.

**1949.** The Lord tells us to give him neem juice to drink,
and we make neem juice and give it to him.

**1950.** Freed from his illness, Bhargadeva
bows countless times at the Lord's feet.

**1951.** Bhargadeva rises and praises the Lord;
rubbing his hands, Bharga says with devotion:

**1952.** "Have mercy on me, Lord, you are merciful;
my heart is eager for you.

**1953.** You have freed this lowly one from disease;
in your mercy cut the noose of maya too.

**1954.** Whom are you deceiving, young sannyasi?
I long to attain your feet.

**1955.** If you have no mercy on the lowly,
why should I call you merciful?

*[p. 179]*

**1956.** I am old, and my eyes deceive me:
in your heart I see a golden doll.

**1957.** Everyone says your colour is golden,
but my eyes see you dark, like Krishna.

**1958.** That is why I say my eyes are faulty;
in your mercy deliver this sinner."[^136]

**1959.** In his mercy the Lord infused power into Bharga,
and at once devotion welled up in his heart.

**1960.** What the Lord said to Bhargadeva with a wink is not known,
but at once tears flowed from his eyes.

**1961.** The sannyasi's disciples did not grasp the subtle truth,
while Bharga went with the Lord to Girnar.

**1962.** Girnar hill is very high;
the two feet of Guru Dattatreya are there.

**1963.** On the high summit of Girnar are the two feet;
the band of sannyasis goes to see the feet.

**1964.** In the morning we set out to see the feet,
and in the afternoon we reach the feet.

**1965.** Two footprints stand out on a rock;
seeing the feet, the Lord bowed down.

**1966.** Flag, thunderbolt and goad adorn the soles;[^137]
seeing the lotus feet, the Lord says "Hari, Hari".

*[p. 180]*

**1967.** A panda lives here permanently;
Vishvambhara asks him about the feet.

**1968.** The panda said, "When the Yadus perished,
then Baladeva came here.

**1969.** Baladeva came here for austerity;
the Lord took his yogic seat and began his austerities.

**1970.** Baladeva sat in austerity in his yogic posture,
while at Prabhasa the Yadavas began to fight.

**1971.** Drunk on mead, all the heroes
fought one another, and blood flowed.

**1972.** Satyaki and all the other heroes
one by one went to the abode of Yama.

**1973.** By Krishna's will all the Yadus perished,
and at last Krishna appeared on the mountain.

**1974.** Here, seeing Baladeva, the Lord of the Yadus
began to speak of his own departure.

**1975.** Krishna says to Baladeva, 'I shall go to Goloka;
my work is accomplished, I shall stay no longer.

**1976.** The earth has been filled with the sins of the Yadavas,
and that is why the Yadus have been destroyed.

**1977.** If the sons of Pandu weep for me,
you will relieve their grief.

*[p. 181]*

**1978.** Drupada's daughter is dearer to me than life;
I beg you, comfort her first.'

**1979.** Hearing this, Baladeva burst into tears
and humbly said these words:

**1980.** 'Vidura, Uddhava and all the other devotees —
what shall I tell them when you are gone?

**1981.** Leave some sign for them,
seeing which they will be filled with love.

**1982.** You are their very life — knowing and understanding this,
how can you go to Goloka?

**1983.** They know nothing but Krishna;
how can you abandon them and go to your own abode?

**1984.** When Panchali raises her cry of grief,
what shall I say to console her? Consider it yourself.'

**1985.** Hearing this, Krishna set his feet here,
and at once the footprints remained here."

**1986.** When the panda had explained this,
love at once arose in the Lord's heart.

**1987.** Gora, the abode of bliss, the home of love,
gazes steadily at the footprints.

**1988.** As he gazed at the prints, a waterfall of love
suddenly overflowed in his heart.

*[p. 182]*

**1989.** Choked with feeling, the Lord says slowly,
"Brother panda, you are a holy man; what a jewel you have shown!

**1990.** Every day you gain happiness from seeing them;
I see no one as virtuous as you.

**1991.** If this print were to fall on a heart of stone,
the bliss of Brahman would arise in it every day."

**1992.** Laying his head on the footprint, Gora, the delight of hearts,
falls prostrate on it again and again.

**1993.** His body, thin as a cane,
swelled with love, finding its chance.

**1994.** Touching the feet, the Lord closed his eyes,
and tears began to flow down his breast.

**1995.** He circles the feet, clapping his hands,
and his waist-cloth and the knot of his hair slipped loose.

**1996.** Seeing this state, Ramananda lost consciousness,
and Govinda Charana fell rolling on the ground.

**1997.** Coming down from the mountain, my Gora Raya
spends the night on the bank of the river called Bhadra.

**1998.** Rising in the morning, we all cross the river
and gradually come in sight of the Dhanvidhara thickets.

**1999.** The Dhanvidhara thickets are very extensive;
seeing those thickets, I was afraid.

### Translator's notes

[^122]: *Shikharini* is a sweet dish of yogurt with sugar; a metaphor of sweetness.
[^123]: Shubhramati is the Sabarmati.
[^124]: Kulina-gram is a village in Bardhaman, home of Ramananda Vasu and other associates of Chaitanya.
[^125]: The episode of the courtesan Barmukhi (Bar-mukhi) is one of the best known in the *Karcha*.
[^126]: *Piyar-kanan*, literally "garden of love".
[^127]: *Mite* (*mita*): a sworn friend, a bosom companion. A joking play on names: Govinda is the author's namesake, and Ramananda is the namesake of Ramananda Raya, Chaitanya's friend.
[^128]: Apparently the name of the villain of the preceding episode, Balaji.
[^129]: "Maidservant" here in the sense of "maidservant of the Lord": Barmukhi already calls herself so.
[^130]: *Yavana*: a foreigner, a Muslim. Somnath was destroyed by Mahmud of Ghazni in 1025 and repeatedly thereafter.
[^131]: Pandas are temple priests and pilgrim guides.
[^132]: Smarahara, "destroyer of Kama", is an epithet of Shiva. Gangadhara, "bearer of the Ganga", is also an epithet of Shiva.
[^133]: A passage is repeated here in the edition (pp. 175–176); the repetition is omitted.
[^134]: From this point the lines in the edition appear to be out of order (the couplets are broken); the translation follows the order of the lines.
[^135]: Girnar (Gunagarh) is the sacred mountain at Junagadh, a place of worship of Dattatreya.
[^136]: Compare the similar episode with another Bharga in Part 5.
[^137]: Flag, thunderbolt (*vajra*) and goad (*ankusha*) are auspicious marks on the feet of God.
