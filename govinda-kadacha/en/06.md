# Part 6

*Rishabha-parvata, Ramnad, Rameshvaram. The silent ascetic of Madhavi-vana. The Tamraparni and Kanyakumari. Travancore and King Rudrapati. Shringeri, the source of the Kaveri, Chandapur: the debate with Ishvara Bharati (pp. 101–122)*

*[p. 101]*

**1098.** The brahmana said, "You are Krishna; you have fulfilled me,"
and saying this he clasped both his feet tightly.

**1099.** On leaving, the Lord told the brahmana [to stay],
but the brahmana left everything and ran after the Lord.

**1100.** Sending the brahmana back, the son of Shachi
then went on to the Rishabha mountain.

**1101.** On the Rishabha mountain lives Paramananda Puri;
the Lord hastened to see him.

**1102.** He spoke much of Krishna with Puri,
and then came to the town of Ramnath.

**1103.** In the town of Ramnath are Rama's footprints;
seeing them, the Lord shed tears.

**1104.** His body filled with thrills and began to tremble,
and the Lord fell senseless to the ground.

**1105.** Touching the lotus feet, my merciful one
shudders again and again, breathing hard.

**1106.** Having seen the lotus feet, the son of Shachi
went on to see other holy places.

**1107.** Going to the tirtha of Rameshvara and bathing there,
my Gaura-Hari had darshan of Shiva.

**1108.** The Shiva named Rameshvara is of wondrous form;
seeing Shiva, Mahaprabhu bowed to him.

*[p. 102]*

**1109.** Many sadhus always live there,
and then all the sadhus came one by one.

**1110.** Seeing the Lord, a learned renunciant
became very eager to debate.

**1111.** The Lord said, "I do not wish to debate;
I admit I have been defeated by you in argument."

**1112.** Seeing his wondrous humility with his own eyes,
the sannyasi was dumbfounded and pondered inwardly.

**1113.** The Lord said, "What are you thinking, sannyasi thakura?
Put away all this idle speculation.[^84]

**1114.** Putting idle speculation aside, with devotion
sing the glories of Krishna, brother, with a pure heart.

**1115.** Worship Krishna, speak of Krishna, take Krishna's name;
taking Krishna's name, go to the eternal abode.

**1116.** There is no way but Krishna — this is my counsel;
coming and going again and again, you will suffer.

**1117.** What is the use of pride, good man?
What need is there to become a pandit in debate?

**1118.** A sinful pandit builds his house in hell —
so everyone says, so the shastras have written.

**1119.** One who knows many shastras yet lives by his whims —
what will that fool achieve by debating?

*[p. 103]*

**1120.** One who cheats for the sake of money —
that unbeliever does not understand the words of scripture.

**1121.** For one whose mind is busy with women and gold,
the study of the Vedas is a mockery.

**1122.** One in whose heart envy is always at play
is punished by the lord of the ancestors[^85] with his own hand.

**1123.** One whose heart melts at the Name of Hari —
he, in my view, is the great pandit.

**1124.** One from whose eyes streams of bliss flow
when he chants the Name of Hari — he is called truly learned.

**1125.** One who, however much he reads and hears, has no taste for Krishna —
he, brother, is a fool and always impure."

**1126.** Hearing these words from the Lord's lips,
the yogi stayed silent for some time.

**1127.** All the detached sannyasis, surrounding the Lord,
listened to his words, lost to themselves.

**1128.** At last Gora-chand raised both his arms
and, drunk with the Name of Hari, toppled over.

**1129.** Lord Chaitanya fell crashing down,
and the blow of a stone cut his chin.

**1130.** Blood began to flow in streams,
and the excellent pandit carefully wiped it away.

*[p. 104]*

**1131.** After three days of kirtana at Setubandha,
he went to the left to see Madhavi-vana.

**1132.** In Madhavi-vana lives an ascetic under a vow of silence;
my beggar[^86] went to see him.

**1133.** That sannyasi has a wondrous radiant form,
his white beard covering his chest.

**1134.** His long nails have curled over;
he sits in silence, naked.

**1135.** No cloth, no staff, no water-pot beside him.
Chaitanya Gosai gazed at him steadily.

**1136.** He is utterly serene, his eyes closed;
the tree is his house, the sky his clothing.

**1137.** He has no desires and is absorbed in austerity;
my Lord stood before him with folded hands.

**1138.** Chaitanya spoke many humble words and praises,
yet the excellent sannyasi did not even look round.

**1139.** Every three days all the renunciants
bring him alms of fruits and roots.

**1140.** Every three days that great yogi
takes food and so sustains his life.

**1141.** Breaking his meditation, the excellent yogi looked round,
and at that moment the Lord began to speak.

*[p. 105]*

**1142.** The yogi understood nothing of the Lord's words,
and then he conversed with him in Sanskrit.

**1143.** The worthy yogi listened calmly to his words
and spoke a few words with the Lord.

**1144.** Having spoken a few words, the great yogi
cried "Chambani shiori!"[^87] and laughed.

**1145.** Crying "Chambani shiori!", with a wholly pure heart
he bowed with a smile at the Lord's feet.

**1146.** My Gora Raya bowed in return
and, floating in joy, sang the glories of Krishna.

**1147.** Seeing the excellent yogi bow down,
all the sannyasis then clasped the Lord's feet.

**1148.** Having spent time there in spiritual conversation,[^88] Gora Raya
left that place after seven days.

**1149.** There is a tirtha called Tattvakundi[^89] there;
the Lord went there to bathe.

**1150.** Then the river Tamraparni came into view,
and the Lord went there to bathe.

**1151.** On the full-moon day of Magha, on the bank of the Tamraparni,
many pilgrims come to bathe.

**1152.** Waiting there a fortnight,
on the full moon of Magha we went and bathed.

*[p. 106]*

**1153.** Crossing the Tamraparni, the Lord
went along the seashore to see Kanyakumari.

**1154.** There are no hills, forests or villages there;
only the sound of the sea can be heard.

**1155.** Huge waves, rolling in there,
seem to be singing the praises of God consciously.

**1156.** Seeing that, the heart is gladdened;
with the rising of feeling, his body thrilled.

**1157.** The sand, heaped up like mountains,
seems to proclaim the glories of God.

**1158.** The sea roars unceasingly, "hoo, hoo";
what more can I say — everything there is beautiful.

**1159.** There is nothing to see, yet it is lovely;
there beauty is seen by one whose mind is pure.

**1160.** Calling me, "Govinda!",
the Lord, with a slight smile, told me to bathe.

**1161.** Waves like mountains come rushing in;
there he bathed with devotion.

**1162.** After bathing, my Lord weeps, crying "Hari";
the love of his heart seemed to overflow.

**1163.** His hair stood on end, his brow broke into sweat,
and his emaciated body was filled with thrills.

*[p. 107]*

**1164.** After bathing, Gora Raya pondered
and, calling me, said, "Which way will you go?"

**1165.** I said, "Whichever way the Lord goes,
that way the servant will go, to serve him."

**1166.** After bathing, a large band of sannyasis
turned back towards Mount Sitala.

**1167.** Nimai joined them and went along,
and I followed behind like a shadow.

**1168.** After going fifteen kroshas we reached Sitala,
and there the band of sannyasis halted.

**1169.** Chaitanya Gosai went beneath a tree,
and I could not think what alms to beg or where.

**1170.** Understanding my mind, with a slight smile
the Lord said, "What are you worrying about?

**1171.** We shall pass the night drinking the nectar of the Name,
and in the morning we shall get up and go wherever we like."

**1172.** Saying this, Gora-chand closed his eyes
and sat motionless, leaning against the tree.

**1173.** All the sannyasi thakuras, playing their khanjanis,[^90]
began to sing, very sweet to hear.

**1174.** Just then a merchant came there
and, giving alms to all, went away.

*[p. 108]*

**1175.** Whole fruits and roots, milk and sugar —
he gave them as alms to everyone with devotion.

**1176.** Receiving the alms, my heart was thrilled,
and the Lord took the milk and sugar and offered them.

**1177.** All the sannyasi thakuras rose in the morning
and set off for the land of Trivinku,[^91] crossing the mountains.

**1178.** The king of the land of Trivinku is very virtuous;
he cares for his subjects as for his own sons.

**1179.** All the townspeople are skilled in hospitality;
they make a great commotion over guests.

**1180.** Everyone tugs at the guests, vying for them,
and serves the guests with great affection.

**1181.** The king here — his name is Rudrapati —
is father and mother to the poor, refuge of the helpless.

**1182.** In this king's realm the subjects are very happy,
and all their hearts are full of love for the king.

**1183.** How many elephants and horses are tied at the king's gate!
There is no shortage of food in his storehouses.

**1184.** In three places in the town there are food-kitchens;
guests and travellers come and stay at them.

**1185.** Each stays there as long as he likes;
all praise him, saying, "Blessed, blessed is the king!"

*[p. 109]*

**1186.** In the evening we came to the town of Trivinku,
and the Lord sat beneath a tree with a joyful heart.

**1187.** A villager brought parched grain,
and the Lord passed the night beneath the tree.

**1188.** Next day the news spread,
and gradually the townspeople gathered.

**1189.** Seeing Gora's wondrous state, everyone
came and stood there with folded hands.

**1190.** Gora chants the Name of Hari with closed eyes,
and all, standing, praise him with pure hearts.

**1191.** The Lord sits there, not a limb moving,
tears flowing from the corners of his eyes.

**1192.** His hair on end, thrilled at heart;
seeing his state, the village people offer so many praises.

**1193.** Some say, "Come to my house, sannyasi";
others say, "I love to look at you."

**1194.** Some bring and offer fruits and roots,
but my Lord does not open his eyes to look.

**1195.** Some say, "This sannyasi is no man.
Why else should such devotion arise on seeing him?

**1196.** Seeing him, I want to give up worldly things;
my mind no longer wishes to live a household life."

*[p. 110]*

**1197.** Some say, "Today the night passed happily;
seeing the sannyasi, my heart has been purified."

**1198.** An old man came and said with devotion,
"Where is the sannyasi? Show him to me."

**1199.** Seeing his eagerness, my Gora Raya
quickly got up and went over to him.

**1200.** The old man bowed before the Lord
and brought him fruits, roots and parched grain.

**1201.** Everyone whispered about this,
and hundreds of learned men came wishing to see him.

**1202.** A brahmavadin came up
and raised the doctrine of advaita, and Chaitanya smiled.

**1203.** Citing the Vedas, Vedanta and the proofs of scripture,
he explained it to him, and hearing it the man lost himself.

**1204.** The Lord said, "Listen, listen, worthy sage:
the perfection of all practice is the love of Radha.

**1205.** Radhika's subtle love is like a mountain;
without devotion no one can find the way to it.

**1206.** Giving up her own happiness, beautiful Radhika,
mad for Krishna, renounced everything.

**1207.** One who understands Sri Radha's deep love
does not die again and again.

*[p. 111]*

**1208.** For one who is always floating on the doctrine of maya,
the truth of devotion does not dawn.

**1209.** The choicest essence of love is mahabhava,
and Sri Radha is surely full of mahabhava.

**1210.** Whoever understands this truth, worthy elder,
is not born and does not die again and again."

**1211.** Then the Lord's glory spread through the land,
and gradually all sorts of people began to gather.

**1212.** The king of this country, with great eagerness,
sent men to fetch the Lord.

**1213.** The Lord said, "I have no business there;
I do not go to worldly men."

**1214.** The royal messenger said, "Listen, sannyasi thakura,
why not go? You will get plenty of wealth.

**1215.** Clothes, ornaments and whatever you want —
there you will get such riches without effort."

**1216.** Understanding from the messenger's words what his intention was,
the Lord began to reason with him.

**1217.** With a slight smile the Lord said:
"Listen, royal messenger, I have no need of wealth.

**1218.** Into the company of those who are worms of worldliness
I never go. What use are riches to me?

*[p. 112]*

**1219.** Worms of worldliness crave wealth;
wealth is the root of evil — this is my conviction.

**1220.** Those who are drunk with wealth and forget the truth
always remain in the hell of worldliness.

**1221.** The rich man does not know that the body is transient,
and regards wealth as the purpose of life."

**1222.** Hearing this, the messenger grew angry
and went off to the king's gate to get his revenge.

**1223.** Hearing the report from the messenger's mouth, King Rudrapati
was not at all angry with the sannyasi.

**1224.** Hearing it word for word from the messenger,
he himself wished to see the sannyasi.

**1225.** King Rudrapati set off to see the sannyasi;
full of devotion, he came out in haste.

**1226.** Leaving his elephants and horses far behind,
he came to the sannyasi in very humble dress.

**1227.** With two or four ministers, the worthy king
came near the Lord and said with devotion —

**1228.** with folded hands Rudrapati said again and again:
"Be merciful and forgive my offence.

**1229.** Without understanding, I summoned you;
forgive me that offence this time.

*[p. 113]*

**1230.** Give me teaching in knowledge, saviour of the fallen:
why do living beings suffer grief and sorrow?"

**1231.** The king is very learned in many shastras;
everyone says he is a great scholar of the Bhagavata.

**1232.** Two or four learned gosais
came with him to receive instruction.

**1233.** The Lord said, "King, you are most fortunate.
You know the Bhagavata — what more can I say?

**1234.** You are well versed in many shastras, a great sage,
while I know nothing but Radha and Krishna."

**1235.** As soon as he took Krishna's name, love welled up,
and tears began to stream down.

**1236.** Drunk with love for Krishna, the Lord rose at once
and began to dance with arms outstretched.

**1237.** Crying "Hari bol!", Gora lost consciousness
and, dancing, fell crashing to the ground.

**1238.** Then the king rushed forward and lifted the Lord,
and at the same moment the maharaja grew drunk.

**1239.** Crying "Hari!", the maharaja began to dance,
and his chest was flooded with tears.

**1240.** His hair stood on end, he was filled with thrills;
falling in the dust, his body turned grey.

*[p. 114]*

**1241.** Seeing the king's devotion, my Nimai
embraced the king and said, "Come, brother.

**1242.** One whose eyes shed tears at the Name of Hari
is the apple of my eye.

**1243.** Seeing your devotion, worthy king,
know for certain that my heart is soothed."

**1244.** Saying this and sending the maharaja away,
the Lord went off to bathe.

**1245.** The king sent many fruits and roots,
and the Lord, after his daily rites, made the offering.

**1246.** Leaving people to serve the Lord,
the king went back to his capital with a happy heart.

**1247.** Some bring fruits and roots, some bring flour,
some bring parched grain to the guest's lodging.

**1248.** For Vishvambhara's sake people bustle about,
and now and then many people come and sit down.

**1249.** Each brings and offers whatever he likes,
but Gora Raya says nothing, good or bad.

**1250.** The land, ringed by mountains, is beautiful to see;
the spring waters flow most charmingly.

**1251.** All around are huge neem trees;
their wondrous beauty is beyond words.

*[p. 115]*

**1252.** There is a mountain there called Ramagiri;[^92]
everyone extols its wondrous greatness.

**1253.** All say that on its summit Ramachandra
stayed three days with Sita.

**1254.** Having won the battle of Lanka, Rama, abode of virtues,
climbed this peak and rested.

**1255.** Ramachandra with Sita and Thakura Lakshmana —
all three rested here.

**1256.** Hearing this, the Lord was filled with longing
and climbed the mountain to see the place.

**1257.** Where Rama and Sita had rested,
there my Gora went and bowed down.

**1258.** Hundreds of people climbed with the Lord
to gaze with devotion at that Ramagiri.

**1259.** This country is very extensive in breadth and length;
a fortnight passed while we crossed it.

**1260.** Then he entered the town of Payoshni[^93]
and was delighted to see Shiva-Narayana.

**1261.** In the matha of Shringari[^94] live the disciples of Shankara;
going there, the Lord met with them.

**1262.** All the disciples of Shankara, gathering together,
sat down to debate, examining the truth.

*[pp. 116–117 — from the 1926 edition; these pages are missing in the base edition]*

**1263.** In the debate all the disciples admitted defeat,
and from the matha he went to see Matsya-tirtha.

**1264.** Having visited Matsya-tirtha, the Lord came to Kachad,
and at Kachad had darshan of Bhagavati.

**1265.** Here flows the river named Bhadra, daughter of the Krishna;[^95]
having bathed, he went on to Naga-panchapadi.

**1266.** All the people here are devotees of Rama;
here the Lord told me to beg alms.

**1267.** I went round three houses begging,
and they all gave me plenty of flour.

**1268.** Staying here three nights, my Lord
went on with everyone to Chitol,[^96] crossing the mountains.

**1269.** Leaving Chitol, he went again to the bank of the Tungabhadra,
walking slowly, to bathe.

**1270.** Having bathed on the bank of the river Tungabhadra,
my Gora, delighting all hearts, sings Krishna's glories.

**1271.** The birthplace of the Kaveri is Kotigiri;[^97]
he arrived there after wandering through many lands.

**1272.** Having bathed at the source of the Kaveri,
the Lord Bhagavan went to the village of Chandapur.

**1273.** On the left a mountain called Satya stands in splendour —
Satyagiri; I cannot describe its beauty.

**1274.** From afar a blue line can be seen;
looking at its beauty soothes the eyes.

**1275.** Seeing Satyagiri, the Lord bowed down
and, keeping Satyagiri on the left, went to the right.

**1276.** Coming near the town of Chandapur,
he went and sat beneath a banyan tree.

**1277.** In Chandapur lives a detached gosai;
hearing of him from people, Nimai went to meet him.

**1278.** The gosai is indeed learned and knows many shastras;
a golden earring swings in one of his ears.

**1279.** Gradually the gosai began quoting scripture
and started a conversation full of pride.

**1280.** The sannyasi's name is Ishvara Bharati;
people say this gosai is an abode of all virtues.

**1281.** Perceiving the sannyasi's pride in his heart,
the Lord smiled a little and turned his face away.

**1282.** Lord Vishvambhara says nothing, good or bad,
and at last, irritated, the excellent sannyasi

**1283.** says to the Lord, "You don't say a word;
I do not accept you as a learned pandit.

**1284.** Everyone says you are very learned,
but I see in you not a trace of knowledge.

*[p. 118]*

**1285.** You have made the whole country shout 'Hari bol',
but I see not the slightest merit in you.

**1286.** I have heard you are learned in the shastras, yet you have nothing to say;
you wander about begging wherever you can.

**1287.** You have neither learning nor knowledge to debate with.
Why then are the foolish so suddenly beguiled?

**1288.** Who knows by what trick and stratagem
you show the subtle truth to everyone?

**1289.** How you will get away after making the fools of this country shout 'Hari bol' —
I shall see through your cunning.

**1290.** If you have the power, then debate;
this time I shall find out your wits and understanding."

**1291.** Saying this, Bharati Gosai hurried off
and came back again and sat down with three companions.

**1292.** The four of them sat on the four sides of the Lord,
and seeing this comedy the Lord began to laugh.

**1293.** Bharati said, "You laugh it off;
but consider carefully what I say.

**1294.** Tell me, which deity is to be worshipped?"
The Lord said, "What is there in the world but Krishna?"

**1295.** Bharati said, "Hear the proof of scripture:
the one Brahman is the lord of all — so the Vedas teach.

*[p. 119]*

**1296.** Wherever I look, I see everything as Brahman.
Tell me, how can this doctrine be refuted?"

**1297.** The Lord said, "I don't know how to debate;
I admit that you are learned in every truth.

**1298.** You are a great pandit in debate, gosai;
Nimai has been defeated by you.

**1299.** If you like, I can write you a certificate of victory:
today I have admitted defeat in your debate."

**1300.** Hearing this, the yogi grumbled and muttered,
and the Lord said, "Practise devotion — logic is far away.

**1301.** Devotion brings Krishna — that is the conclusion.
Reduce the doctrines of the Vedas and Vedanta to dust.

**1302.** Tell me, what is the fruit of discussing many shastras?
Without Krishna there is nowhere to stand."

**1303.** Saying this, my Lord closed his eyes;
his hair stood on end and devotion overflowed.

**1304.** Tears began to flow down his breast,
and the knot of his kaupina gradually came loose.

**1305.** Trembling all over, his heart quaking, his body sweating,
he called out "Krishna!" and began to sway.

**1306.** "O Krishna, where are you, merciful Lord?
Bestow devotion and purify my heart."

*[p. 120]*

**1307.** Saying this, the Lord began to weep,
and the surge of his heart seemed to double.

**1308.** Lord Vishvambhara hears nothing, good or bad,
and sobs and weeps unceasingly.

**1309.** Seeing a tamala tree in front of him,
he ran, crying "Krishna!", and threw his arms around it.[^98]

**1310.** Seeing this state with his own eyes,
the yogi then clasped the Lord's feet.

**1311.** The yogi said, "I beg you, let us not debate;
now my longing for Krishna is growing.

**1312.** Seeing your state, young sannyasi,
I no longer have any wish to debate.

**1313.** Give my heart the incomparable jewel of devotion;
this I humbly beg at your feet."

**1314.** But the Lord does not hear these words of the yogi;
my Lord is soaking the earth with his tears.

**1315.** In the surge of great ecstasy his body grew rigid,
and his body, the equal of gold, fell in the dust.

**1316.** Crying "Krishna!", the Lord rolls on the ground,
his body grey with dust and pierced by thorns.

**1317.** The yogi, sitting before him, began to weep,
and at once compassion for him arose in the Lord.

*[p. 121]*

**1318.** Seeing Bharati's devotion, he put his hand on his back,
and with his hand on his back said a few words.

**1319.** At the Lord's touch devotion arose in the yogi,
and his mind was immersed in the rasa of devotion to Krishna.

**1320.** How great the Lord's mercy is cannot be told;
drunk with love, the yogi rolls in the dust.

**1321.** The yogi said, "You are my Krishna.
When, Lord, will you come again and show yourself to me?"

**1322.** The Lord said, "Do not say such words again.
Krishna is the lord of Vrindavana — that is the conclusion.

**1323.** Without devotion the truth of Krishna does not dawn;
know for certain that Krishna is bound by the rope of devotion."

**1324.** The yogi said, "I have seen through your trick.
Why do you throw dust in my eyes with your cunning?

**1325.** Today I shall bind you with the rope of devotion;
today I shall snatch away both your sandals."

**1326.** Saying this, Ishvara Bharati
seized the sandals and began to tug at them hard.

**1327.** The Lord said, "Put your faith in Krishna;
from today your name is Krishnadasa."

**1328.** Saying this, the Lord left Chandapur,
and the excellent yogi came with him a long way.

*[p. 122]*

**1329.** Smiling, the Lord sent the yogi back,
and the excellent yogi bowed and went away.

**1330.** Two days and nights we went on, crossing the mountains,
and in that time, search as we might, we found no village or town.

**1331.** The path is very hard; I could not walk.
Only kadamba trees could be seen, row upon row.

**1332.** Seeing the kadamba trees, Gora said to me,
"My Krishna plays beneath these trees."

**1333.** Saying this, my Lord burst into distraught weeping
and walked on swaying, drunk with love for Krishna.

**1334.** There I saw a tiger and was afraid.
*[the second line of the couplet is missing in the edition]*

**1335.** I pointed out the tiger to the Lord with a sign,
but heard nothing from the Lord's lips, good or bad.

**1336.** The tiger was drinking water at that place,
and I crept carefully along beside the Lord.

**1337.** Gora went to the right, keeping the tiger on the left,
in ecstasy, his body drunk with the Name of Hari.

**1338.** The tiger did not even look round at us,
but I kept looking back and walked quickly on.

**1339.** Seeing how I behaved, with a slight smile
the Lord said, "What are you afraid of?"

### Translator's notes

[^84]: *Atal-patal*, literally "abysses and nether worlds", i.e. empty, far-fetched speculation.
[^85]: The lord of the ancestors (*Pitripati*) is Yama.
[^86]: "My beggar" is an affectionate name for Chaitanya the sannyasi.
[^87]: An obscure exclamation (*chambani shiori*), apparently words in a local language, which Govinda records by ear.
[^88]: *Ishta-goshthi*: conversation about the beloved deity, spiritual discussion.
[^89]: The name is unclear in the edition (*Tattvakundi*).
[^90]: The *khanjani* is a small tambourine.
[^91]: Trivinku is Travancore (Tiruvitankur). The name of King Rudrapati also occurs in the *Chaitanya-charitamrita* (Madhya 9) as the king of Travancore.
[^92]: Compare "Ramagiri-ashramas" in Kalidasa's *Meghaduta*, to which the 1926 edition points in a footnote.
[^93]: Payoshni (Payasvini) is a river and district in Kerala; in CC Madhya 9 Chaitanya sees Shankara-Narayana there.
[^94]: Shringeri is the chief matha of the Shankara school.
[^95]: The river Bhadra, "daughter of the Krishna", is presumably the Tunga-Bhadra in the Krishna river system.
[^96]: Chitol is probably Chitradurga.
[^97]: Kotigiri is the source of the Kaveri (Talakaveri in the Coorg hills).
[^98]: The tamala is a dark-barked tree whose colour recalls Krishna.
