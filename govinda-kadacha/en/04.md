# Part 4

*Vateshvara, Munna, Venkata. The robber Pantha Bhil. Girishvara and the silent ascetic. Tripadi, Pana-Narasimha, Vishnukanchi, Pakshitirtha (pp. 61–80)*

*[p. 61]*

**658.** Tirtharam gave up worldly things like a straw,
and, crying "Hari!", danced with both arms outstretched.

**659.** He marked his whole body with tilaka and wore a kaupina;
devotion made him utterly humble and meek.

**660.** Hearing of this, his wife
came running at once, weeping.

**661.** Clasping Tirtha's feet she wept,
but Tirtharam paid no heed to her words.

**662.** Her name was Kamala Kumari, and she was very beautiful;
her beauty lit up everything around.

**663.** Tirtha took Kamala by the hand and said,
"I have given all my property to you.

**664.** I have escaped from hell with my life;
you enjoy all the wealth and luxury."

**665.** Hearing these words, Kamala Kumari
fell crashing to the ground.

**666.** Seeing the net of maya that Kamala was casting,
Tirtharam smiled a little and said, "Chant the Name of Hari."

**667.** Weeping, Kamala Kumari
went back home, and Tirtha became a beggar on the road.

**668.** Having delivered Tirtharam, beautiful Gauranga
then left the holy place of Vateshvara.

*[p. 62]*

**669.** How many people brought how many clothes!
But the Lord did not touch a single piece.

**670.** At last the Lord called out "Govinda!"
and gave me a clap on the back:

**671.** "We have spent seven days at this Vateshvara.
Let us go to Nandishvara to see him."

**672.** Hearing this, I took the sandals on my shoulder
and, leaving Vateshvara, went with the Lord.

**673.** On the way we came to a vast jungle,
and seeing it my heart failed me.

**674.** That jungle stretched for ten kroshas,[^52]
and I grew anxious: how would we get through?

**675.** My all-knowing Lord smiled slightly
and went on ahead, while I fell behind.

**676.** I followed the Lord along the footpath,
and by his will I felt no fear.

**677.** How many beasts had their lairs there,
yet not one showed itself before or behind us!

**678.** Having crossed the jungle, near the town of Munna
he sat beneath a tree to rest.

**679.** Two householders of Munna came
and carefully brought some flour.

*[p. 63]*

**680.** The Lord said nothing to them, good or bad,
and gradually the two sat down close by.

**681.** Gazing at the young sannyasi, the two of them
looked at him fixedly, without blinking.

**682.** Gradually towards evening a great hubbub arose:
the townspeople came in droves to see him.

**683.** A fiery radiance flowed from the Lord's body,
and seeing it all the people stood stock-still.

**684.** Gradually the people of Munna came forward
and one by one bowed down to him.

**685.** With devotion all the people began to say,
"Come into the town, leave this tree."

**686.** My Lord, absorbed in love, did not listen to their words;
inwardly saying "Hari", he was weeping all the while.

**687.** Gradually feeling arose within him,
and at once the Lord got up and began to dance.

**688.** Crying "Hari, Hari!", he falls to the ground,
and with him the village people grew excited.

**689.** All of them, clapping their hands, began to dance,
and seeing this my Lord burst into tears.

**690.** Any unbeliever who saw that state with his own eyes
felt devotion overflowing into his heart.

*[p. 64]*

**691.** Thus half the night went by,
with all the people dancing and crying "Hari, Hari".

**692.** At last the respectable married women, leaving their homes,
came to that place to see Gauranga.

**693.** Having seen beautiful Gauranga with their own eyes,
the women could not go back home.

**694.** Looking at one another, one said to the next,
"Seeing the sannyasi, my heart is in a turmoil.

**695.** Sister, never have I seen anyone so handsome!
This is the one everybody calls Chaitanya Gosai.

**696.** Ah, from fasting he is nothing but skin and bone!
Why at such an age has he bound up a load of matted hair?"

**697.** Saying this, all the women of Munna
were overcome with weeping, tears flowing from their eyes.

**698.** So the night passed, and no one slept.
In the morning my Lord rose and set off southwards.

**699.** The people of Munna begged him in crowds to stay,
but my Lord would not listen to any entreaty.

**700.** There was a very wretched woman there,
weeping beneath that tree, tears streaming from her eyes.

**701.** When the Lord made ready to depart,
that old woman, weeping, begged for food and clothing.

*[p. 65]*

**702.** She wore torn clothes and had no food in her belly.
Seeing her, Chaitanya Gosai stopped.

**703.** Seeing her condition and understanding it in his heart,
the Lord, smiling a little, began begging alms all around.

**704.** He said, "Give me alms, brothers of Munna;
when I have alms of food and clothing, then I shall go."

**705.** The men and women of Munna, floating in joy,
brought heaps upon heaps of food and clothing.

**706.** All said, "He wants provisions for the road,"
and so they brought them in heaps.

**707.** Everyone was eager to put clothing into the Lord's hands,
and seeing the commotion the Lord began to laugh.

**708.** All said, "Clothing is a trifle, worth nothing;
let Chaitanya Gosai take my cloth first!"

**709.** No one knew what was in the Lord's mind,
and so they all hurried to bring food and clothing.

**710.** The Lord said, "Listen, listen, people of Munna:
I have accepted your alms.

**711.** Here beneath the tree sits this wretched woman;
give all this food and clothing to her."

**712.** All the people were amazed at his compassion.
Someone said, "He begged alms for the old woman's sake."

*[p. 66]*

**713.** Saying this, my Lord [adjusted] his outer cloth
and set off, saying "Hari, Hari".

**714.** The Lord looked at me and made a sign,
and I followed behind with the water-pot and sandals.

**715.** Many people began to walk along with him,
but the Lord did not once look back.

**716.** One by one all the people turned back,
but Ramananda Svami would not leave him.

**717.** Ramananda Svami is a man of great virtue;
privately I asked him what was in his heart.

**718.** Ramananda said, "Brother, on seeing the Lord
my hard heart has melted.

**719.** If the Lord does not make me his disciple,
I shall give up my life at once and not remain in the world."

**720.** Then my Lord came to the town of Venkata,
arriving there at midday.

**721.** There lived a learned gosai there,
a great Vedantic scholar without an equal.

**722.** The foremost of pandits wanted to debate,
and the Lord replied, "I have lost."

**723.** Even so the svami would not give up the debate,
and the Lord, his face beaming, began to laugh.

*[p. 67]*

**724.** However much the svami argued for advaita-vada,
Chaitanya explained to him the doctrine of dvaita-advaita.[^53]

**725.** At last a fierce debate broke out,
and gradually the danda-svami admitted defeat.

**726.** His name was Ramananda, and he was a great pandit;
Ramananda was initiated into the Name of Hari.

**727.** The Lord poured the nectar of the Name into his ears,
and devotion overflowed in the svami's heart.

**728.** Then Ramananda Svami bowed down
and at the Lord's command went back to his matha.

**729.** The svami gave the Name of Hari to all his disciples,
and his mind grew drunk with the rasa of devotion.

**730.** Staying three days in the town of Venkata,
the Lord gave the Name of Hari guilelessly from house to house.

**731.** Men and women alike — all grew drunk,
and with them danced my Chaitanya Gosai.

**732.** Town and villages, boys and girls grew drunk;
who could keep count of how many came and went?

**733.** He gave everyone teachings on the truth of devotion,
and lifelong fools rolled at his feet.

**734.** When the Lord saw an unbeliever, he would embrace him first,
and having embraced him, say to him, "Say 'Hari, Hari'."

*[p. 68]*

**735.** There was a robber there named Pantha Bhil.
Hearing of him, the Lord went off towards him.

**736.** Everyone said, "Holy man, do not go there!
What if Pantha Bhil kills you?

**737.** Pantha Bhil is a villain with no sense at all;
finding you, he may do you some harm."

**738.** Heeding no one's words, Chaitanya Gosai
hurried towards Bagula, to Pantha Bhil.

**739.** Pantha Bhil lives in a forest called Bagula;
whenever he finds a traveller he brings him to ruin.

**740.** Ignoring every warning, into the fearful forest
the Lord went, to see the sport.[^54]

**741.** I followed behind with the water-pot,
and Chaitanya Gosai said nothing to me.

**742.** Finding the Lord, Pantha received him as a guest,
and Mahaprabhu stayed there three nights.

**743.** The Lord said, "Pantha, you are a worthy holy man;
seeing you, all my sins are destroyed.

**744.** You do not live in a house like a householder;
you are a supreme sadhu, a detached sannyasi.

**745.** You are no worm of worldliness like a householder;
I see your heart is content with whatever comes.

*[p. 69]*

**746.** You have no son, no daughter, no wife;
you are not drunk with worldly things and have no attachment.

**747.** Blessed are you, King Pantha, crest-jewel of sadhus!
Seeing you, my heart has become glad.

**748.** You regard worldly wealth as straw
and could give up all you have this very moment.

**749.** You do not live with a woman —
that is why I came here, to fulfil my wish.

**750.** You are always surrounded by disciples,
and on seeing you the heart thrills with joy.

**751.** Noble one, you are not bound by maya and delusion;
I think you are the best of sadhus."[^55]

**752.** Silently hearing the Lord's words,
he bowed down at once with devotion.

**753.** Hearing the Name of Hari from the Lord's lips again and again,
an ocean of devotion surged up in his heart.

**754.** The Bhil fell rolling at the Lord's feet,
and the Lord embraced him and let him hear the Name.

**755.** All the robbers, drunk with the Name of Hari,
turned that forest into a garden of joy.

**756.** From that day Pantha put on the kaupina
and became the best of sadhus, mature in knowledge.

*[p. 70]*

**757.** By the Lord's grace Pantha gave up his sinful deeds
and always went about dancing and chanting the Name of Hari.

**758.** As soon as he took Hari's name, tears would come.
The young sannyasi was drunk with joy.

**759.** All the robbers there were in the forest
gave up their wicked ways and together cried "Hari, Hari".

**760.** All together they grew drunk with joy in that forest;
for the Lord's sake they all gave up their sinful deeds.

**761.** Having thus purified Pantha Bhil,
my hero of dharma went on, floating in joy.

**762.** His body wasted by fasting, he can hardly walk,
yet still the Lord gives the Name of Hari from house to house.

**763.** The people of that country say "khai, mai",[^56]
yet still Chaitanya Gosai distributes the Name.

**764.** My Lord has no desires whatever,
yet wherever and whenever he goes, there is plenty of everything.

**765.** Whoever sees the Lord even once
no longer has the strength to go away.

**766.** Such is the Lord's power — what more can I say?
He has broken the dam of the ocean of devotion once again.

**767.** Surging up, the ocean of devotion flooded the land;
some became sannyasis, some dervishes.

*[p. 71]*

**768.** Some became detached Vaishnavas there,
some, turned aul and baul,[^57] dance in the courtyards.

**769.** Thus my Lord, drunk with the Name,
rolls on the ground, lost in ecstasy.

**770.** Sometimes he is like inert matter, with no outer awareness,
his body bristling like a kadamba flower.

**771.** His eyes half-closed, like a corpse;
no one has ever seen such a wondrous state.

**772.** Heedless of thorns and splinters, he falls to the ground;
think as I may, I cannot tell what mood he is in.

**773.** Three nights passed beneath a tree;
he fasted and ate nothing.

**774.** Tears streamed down over his breast;
call him a hundred times and not a word, like a madman.

**775.** Sometimes he rolls on the ground, naked,
and I carefully take him up in my arms.

**776.** On the fourth day a woman came
and showed hospitality, giving flour and parched grain.[^58]

**777.** Another old woman brought milk;
the Lord mixed the flour with the milk and made an offering.

**778.** Three kroshas from there is a temple
where a linga called Girishvara was installed by the Creator.

*[p. 72]*

**779.** People say that Vishvakarma built the temple
and the Grandsire[^59] worshipped Shiva with his own hands.

**780.** There stands a huge bilva tree,
its branches spreading over a quarter of a krosha.

**781.** I had heard that this tree bears no fruit;
going there, I saw that wonder for myself.

**782.** On three sides the temple is surrounded by hills,
and on the south side the bilva tree stands in splendour.

**783.** Gathering bilva leaves with his own hands, my Lord,
lost in love, offered them to Shiva by the handful.

**784.** Then, drunk with love, Gora Raya
flailed about and fell crashing to the ground.

**785.** Now laughing, now weeping, like a madman,
tears streaming unceasingly down his breast.

**786.** His hair standing on end, almost inert;
the wondrous state of love is beyond words.

**787.** The Lord has no desire; he is drunk with the Name of Hari.
Some two days passed in that abode of Shiva.

**788.** On the third day a sannyasi with matted hair
came down from the mountain top and showed himself.

**789.** That excellent sannyasi, keeping a vow of silence,
performed his worship and went back to the mountain top.

*[p. 73]*

**790.** That solitary sannyasi had nothing on his body;
whoever sees him, even a worldly man becomes detached.

**791.** When the Lord regained consciousness,
I told him everything about the sannyasi, one thing after another.

**792.** Hearing about the sannyasi, my Gora Raya
hurried up the mountain to see him.

**793.** I ran behind him in wonder,
and gradually we reached that place.

**794.** Climbing the mountain, we saw
that sannyasi gosai beneath a tree.

**795.** No cloth, no bowl — nothing beside him.
I stood behind Chaitanya.

**796.** The excellent sannyasi, sunk in meditation, had no outer awareness;
whoever saw him became virtuous.

**797.** However humbly Gora Raya spoke to him,
the sannyasi's meditation did not break.

**798.** Then the Lord, with folded hands, began a hymn of praise,
and at that the excellent sannyasi began to look at him.

**799.** Seeing the Lord, that sannyasi thakura
smiled, and great joy welled up in his heart.

**800.** Who knows why the sannyasi smiled;
a moment later the Lord sat down beside him.

*[p. 74]*

**801.** Two other detached sannyasis came there,
and the sannyasi wished to offer hospitality.

**802.** He brought fruits called parata,
and the Lord took two of them.

**803.** He gave me four fruits to eat.
"Unless it is prasada, I will not accept it," I said.

**804.** Hearing this, my Lord Chaitanya Gosai
made the fruits into prasada and gave them to me.

**805.** Parata fruits are very sweet, like nectar;
having eaten them, my mind grew restless.

**806.** These sinful eyes of mine, out of greed,
kept glancing again and again at the Lord's fruits.

**807.** Beautiful Gauranga smiled slightly
and held out two neem fruits[^60] to me.

**808.** "How shall I eat these fruits?" I was afraid,
and at once I remembered the son of Anjana.[^61]

**809.** Seeing me hesitate and not eat the fruits,
Chaitanya Gosai said with a smile:

**810.** "The stone won't stick in your throat, Govinda.
Don't play tricks when you are given prasada.

**811.** Your desire to eat the fruit has grown strong,
but you are beside yourself for fear of a stone getting stuck."

*[p. 75]*

**812.** When Gosai spoke out what was in my mind,
I at once put the fruit down and fell at his feet.

**813.** In the end, at the Lord's order, I had to eat them,
and the sannyasi came and brought two more fruits.

**814.** After eating, cupping my hands at the spring,
I drank water with a joyful heart.

**815.** The cool, crystal-clear water of the spring —
drinking it, my whole body was refreshed.

**816.** Drunk with the Name of Hari, the Lord was filled with love,
and his body bristled like a kadamba flower.

**817.** In the surge of love the knot of his matted hair came loose,
his feet tangled together and he fell.

**818.** His forehead was cut by a blow from a stone,
and streams of blood fell to the ground.

**819.** Saliva flows from his mouth, water from his nose;
Gora Raya lies like a lifeless thing.

**820.** Seeing this, devotion arose in the sannyasi;
he fell at the Lord's feet and began to weep.

**821.** His body like a charred log, no cloth on his limbs;
his matted hair came loose and he breathed heavily.

**822.** Tears streamed down through his beard,
and in love that charred log seemed to swell into blossom.

*[p. 76]*

**823.** Then, regaining consciousness, my excellent Lord
rose and sat up, grey with dust.

**824.** The excellent sannyasi was in a fever of agitation
and, gazing at the Lord, said, "You are God."

**825.** At the sannyasi's words the Lord put his hands over his ears
and said again and again, "Sannyasi, give up such talk."

**826.** The sannyasi said, "You are by no means a man."
The Lord said, "Sannyasi, you are my God.

**827.** Wondrous is your love for God;
seeing you, even the unbeliever remembers him.

**828.** No cloth, no bowl, no craving for wealth —
millions of obeisances to your feet.

**829.** You are not subject to earthly pleasures;
beside you even heaven seems paltry."

**830.** Then the Lord went to the town of Tripadi[^62]
and, seeing the image of Sri Rama, fell in the dust.

**831.** Many Ramat Vaishnavas[^63] live there,
and they go about every which way looking for debate.

**832.** A Ramat pandit named Mathura
was known in the town as a great disputant.

**833.** He came before the Lord and demanded a debate,
and the Lord, with folded hands and shrinking, replied:

*[p. 77]*

**834.** "Mathura Thakura, I do not know how to debate;
a hundred times I admit defeat before you.

**835.** You are a devotee of Sri Rama, a Vaishnava gosai;
by serving you, how many truths I shall learn!

**836.** You are a detached Ramat, yet ruled by the lust for victory —
why smear soot with both hands over your spotless name?[^64]

**837.** Tell us something of the truth, so that, hearing it,
the people may be purified by your words.

**838.** I hear you are very skilled in logic,
but there is no merit at all in dry logic.

**839.** The truth of God, the truth of the soul, the doctrine of maya —
explain them and let us taste the nectar.

**840.** Explain what makes living beings fulfilled,
and tell us that, worthy sir.

**841.** There is no need for much debate and wrangling;
kindly tell me the subtle truth."

**842.** As he was speaking, the Lord cried "Hari bol!"
and, growing excited, became drunk with the Name.

**843.** Where was his cloth, where his upper wrap?
His hair standing on end, he breathed fast.

**844.** Then he fell crashing to the ground
and lost consciousness, the Lord becoming almost inert.

*[p. 78]*

**845.** All the Ramats, seeing that state,
surrounded the Lord and began to dance.

**846.** Some said, "This sannyasi is no man";
some fell at his feet and rolled on the ground.

**847.** After that he left the place,
and Mathura ran after him a long way.

**848.** Smiling, he sent Mathuranatha away,
and the Lord hurried on to see Pana-Narasimha.[^65]

**849.** Lord Nrisimha is offered a sugar drink as bhoga,
and so everyone calls him Pana-Narasimha.

**850.** The merciful Lord sang a hymn to Nrisimha,
and all the people were amazed to see it.

**851.** Nrisimha's priest is Madhavendra Bhuiyan;[^66]
every day he comes and worships Lord Nrisimha.

**852.** He brought a tulasi garland and put it round the Lord's neck,
and wearing the garland, my Lord said "Hari, Hari".

**853.** The pujari quickly brought some prasada,
and the Lord took just a particle in his hand.

**854.** Holding the prasada in his hand, he sang its praises,
and as he took the prasada tears flowed from both his eyes.

**855.** He had sugar drink brought for me,
and I drank and drank until my belly was full.

*[p. 79]*

**856.** Nrisimha's drink is like nectar;
whoever sees Lord Nrisimha realises the state of Brahman.

**857.** With closed eyes, the Lord says the Name of Hari;
gradually we arrived at the abode of Vishnukanchi.

**858.** At Vishnukanchi a sheth[^67] named Bhavabhuti
serves Lakshmi-Narayana with great care.

**859.** The sheth is a great devotee, a crest-jewel of saints;
his very life is given to Lakshmi-Narayana.

**860.** Every day the worthy sheth serves with devotion
and spends much money on that service.

**861.** His wife washes the temple every day;
that crest-jewel of saints is always busy with service.

**862.** Every day sweet rice is made from two maunds of thickened milk,
and how many wandering ascetics go to receive the prasada!

**863.** Seeing Lakshmi-Narayana, beautiful Gauranga
bowed down and sang long hymns of praise.

**864.** Six kroshas from Lakshmi-Narayana
stands Shiva Trikaleshvara in the open plain.

**865.** His gauripatta[^68] measures four cubits;
seeing that Shiva, the Lord was astonished.

**866.** From there Pakshigiri[^69] can be seen;
below it is Pakshitirtha, where the river Bhadra flows.

*[p. 80]*

**867.** Beautiful Gauranga bathed in that place
and ate the champi fruits that I obtained by begging.

**868.** We lay down to sleep beneath a tree,
and in the night a tiger attacked us.

**869.** Seeing its snarling and roaring, my Gora-chand
smiled and spread the snare of the Name of Hari.

**870.** Hearing the cry of "Hari!", the tiger tucked in its tail
and with one bound retreated into the forest.

**871.** Having seen this wondrous power with my own eyes,
I took up the dust of his feet and placed it on my head.

**872.** Five kroshas from the bank of the Bhadra
is a holy place called Kalatirtha.

**873.** There is an image of Lord Varaha of wondrous workmanship,
seeing which the sages and rishis are enchanted.

**874.** Having had darshan, the Lord bowed down,
and a pujari with a happy face brought him a garland.

**875.** Receiving the sanctified garland, the Lord was thrilled at heart;
his body began to tremble and his eyes to stream.

**876.** The tears spurted out like a squirt-gun,
and, sobbing, the Lord was quite overcome.

**877.** Five kroshas to the south is Sandhitirtha;[^70]
the Lord set off, and I followed behind.

### Translator's notes

[^52]: A krosha (krosh) is a measure of distance of about 3–3.5 km.
[^53]: *Dvaita-advaita*, the doctrine of "unity in difference", as opposed to the strict monism (*advaita-vada*) of the Shankara school.
[^54]: "To see the sport": Govinda stresses Chaitanya's fearless playfulness.
[^55]: The whole speech is ironic: Chaitanya praises the robber for "virtues" that are really his vices (he has no family and is indifferent to others' property), and so disarms him.
[^56]: "Khai, mai" imitates an unfamiliar language (Telugu or Kannada) that a Bengali could not understand.
[^57]: Auls and bauls are Bengali wandering ecstatic singers and ascetics; Govinda applies to the South the categories familiar to him.
[^58]: *Chuna*: probably parched or crushed grain.
[^59]: The Grandsire (*Pitamaha*) is Brahma.
[^60]: Neem (*nimba*) is a bitter fruit; Govinda is apparently afraid of its hard stone.
[^61]: The son of Anjana is Hanuman, whom Govinda invokes in his fright.
[^62]: Tripadi is Tirupati.
[^63]: The Ramats (Ramanandis) are Vaishnavas who worship Rama.
[^64]: Literally, "why smear ink with both hands over your whiteness?"
[^65]: Pana-Narasimha is the Nrisimha temple at Mangalagiri (Andhra Pradesh), where the deity is offered the sweet drink *pana*.
[^66]: *Bhuiyan* (*bhuñā*) is probably a surname or a landholder's title.
[^67]: A *sheth* (*shethi*) is a merchant.
[^68]: The *gauripatta* is the base (yoni) of the Shiva-linga.
[^69]: Pakshigiri and Pakshitirtha are Tirukkalukundram, the "hill of birds" near Chengalpattu, famous for the legend that two eagles come there every day to be fed.
[^70]: The name is corrupt in the edition (*sahita ghaṁ*); probably the next tirtha listed in the index of the 1926 edition.
