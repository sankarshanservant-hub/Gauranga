# Mahabhava-prakasha

*“The Manifestation of Mahabhava”*

*A poem by Kanai Khuntia (Odia, 16th century)*

*Translated from Odia into English*

---

## Translator's note

The *Mahabhava-prakasha* is an Odia poem by Kanai (Kanhai) Khuntia, a servant of the Jagannatha temple in Puri from among the hereditary *khuntia* temple servants. He was a contemporary of Sri Chaitanya and himself saw what he describes: the Ratha-yatra, Mahaprabhu's dance before the chariot, His kirtana and His mahabhava. In the *Vaishnava-lilamrita* Madhava Pattanayaka mentions Kanhai among those who held rahasa and sankirtana in Puri in the Lord's time.

Three chapters survive. In the first the author tells of the Ratha-yatra and of how Mahaprabhu manifested mahabhava — hence the poem's title. In the second the Lord answers the questions of Ramananda Raya and Svarupa Damodara about the tattva of Radha and the gopis and tells of His own search for a guru; here too are Ramananda's family and his leaving royal service. The third is a praise of Prataparudra — his campaigns and the raid on Puri, the king's meeting with Sri Chaitanya and the secret of Jagannatha, which the Lord begins to reveal through the story of Krishna in Dvaraka longing for Vraja. The manuscript breaks off in the middle of this story.

**Source.** The only edition is Baripada, 1965 (from a single incomplete manuscript), read line by line from the
page images. **Only Kanai's text** has been translated; the editor's preface and notes were neither translated nor
paraphrased.

**How the translation was made.** There was no native speaker of Odia among the translators. Every couplet was read
from the scan, transliterated and translated word for word (this is in the full edition, to be published
separately); the literary translation rests on that work. Doubtful places are marked "(?)", unclear ones "(??)";
words added for the sense are in square brackets.

**Numbering.** The couplets are not numbered in the edition; the "chapter.verse" numbers are the translators', and so
are the section headings. There are 583 couplets in all.

**Kanai and the Gaudiya Vaishnavas.** Kanai writes as an Odia Vaishnava: for him Jagannatha is Krishna Himself, and
Sri Chaitanya is Krishna who has revealed Radha's bhava. In this he is close to the *Chaitanya-charitamrita*. But in
his accounts of Radha, the gopis and the Lord's guru there are Odia traditions unknown to CC and CB, and images
unfamiliar to Gaudiya Vaishnavas (for example, Radha as Yogamaya). The translation renders the author exactly,
softening nothing; at such places a note briefly gives the Gaudiya Vaishnava position according to Srila
Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati.

[^01-2]: Kanai (Kanhai) Khuntia was a servant of Jagannatha in Puri from among the *khuntia* temple servants (one of the hereditary groups of the temple's sevakas), a contemporary and companion of Sri Chaitanya. In Madhava's *Vaishnava-lilamrita* (VL 2.25–27) Kanhai builds the rahasa mandapa near Bata-Ganesha and holds sankirtana there together with Gopala Guru. The text follows the 1965 edition (Baripada), p. 1; the edition gives the poem incomplete — it breaks off in the middle. The chapter opens with a mangalacharana: praise of Jagannatha as Krishna-Vishnu (1.1–1.15) and of Sri Gaurasundara (from 1.16).
[^01-1]: Nilachala (Niladri, "the Blue Mountain") is Puri; its Lord is Jagannatha. Meru is the world-mountain, the axis of the universe.

# Kanai Khuntia. *Mahabhava-prakasha*, Chapter 2
## Ramananda Raya's family; Ramananda leaves royal service and comes to Sri Chaitanya; Prataparudra at Kashi Mishra's house


*The second vritta (?) [section]*[^02-1]

**2.1.** In this book, the *Mahabhava-prakasha*, is the essence that came forth from the lotus mouth of Sri Gauranga.  
**2.2.** How can we, worthless ones, describe it? Know the words of Prabhu's holy mouth to be a firm secret.  
**2.3.** The best [place] of the Panchakroshi of Purushottama-kshetra — its name is Bentapur, most charming.[^02-2]  
**2.4.** In that village dwell the foremost (?) Karanas (?); all of them serve with the king as panjias.[^02-3]  
**2.5.** In their family there was one named Bhavananda; he, a Pattanayaka, versed in the shastras (?), held (?) to dharma.[^02-4]

# Kanai Khuntia. *Mahabhava-prakasha*, Chapter 3
## In praise of King Prataparudra


*The third vritta (?) [section]*[^03-1]

**3.1.** Sri Prataparudra is born in the Solar dynasty; he is a wondrous (?) avatara, a portion (?) of Vishnu.[^03-2]  
**3.2.** By serving Jaya-Durga [his parents] obtained him; since the mercy of the Supreme Lord was there, … (??).[^03-3]  
**3.3.** By his name the Gajapati dynasty is adorned in the realm (?); at his name the three worlds tremble.[^03-4]  
**3.4.** His company is the devotees of Vishnu, [like] wish-fulfilling trees; his mind abides in service to Vishnu's feet.  
**3.5.** Noble, the best of men, the best among the jewels of kings — the Supreme Lord promptly (?) protects him.[^03-5]  
**3.6.** That King Purushottama is the refuge (?); he is his son, [the best] among kings (?).[^03-6]  
**3.7.** He is the sovereign of the earth of … (??)-khanda; the best of kings (?) made Southern Kataka his city.[^03-7]  
**3.8.** His boundless strength resides in the might of his arms; for the afflicted he becomes a support.  
**3.9.** The treasures shankha-nidhi and padma-nidhi, it is told (?), are in his hand; and he himself serves Maharudra.[^03-8]  
**3.10.** By the power of Maharudra he is most mighty (?); by Rudra's grace all that is desired is fulfilled.[^03-9]  

## Prataparudra's southern campaigns; Ghazi Ismail's raid on Puri; the Deity is hidden in Chadhei-guha


**3.11.** That King Prataparudra was himself truly great (?): in the three worlds there remained not a single such (?) king.[^03-10]  
**3.12.** That king duly performed the golden bath; by [the rite of] the scales (?) he generously distributed (?) [gifts] to the people.[^03-11]  
**3.13.** How great (?) was his might in benta-bhiana! All the people (?) under the king knew no affliction.[^03-12]  
**3.14.** His army of seven akshauhinis was countless (?); from the arrows of his battle kings flee, routed (?).[^03-13]  
**3.15.** As he … (??) the land of Gauda, such a fate he dealt to Udayagiri [and] Chandragiri.[^03-14]  
**3.16.** That king thought of the great posts in the south, but of the Supreme Lord he did not think (?) at all.[^03-15]  
**3.17.** With thousands (?) of warriors, with a … (??) army (?) he went south; of the Supreme Lord he did not remember at all.[^03-16]  
**3.18.** At this time Ghazi Ismail, from … (??), marched to attack (?) and came to the kshetra (??) of Niladri.[^03-17]  
**3.19.** Mustering an army against Niladri, he marched; he said that he would take the Supreme Lord away.[^03-18]  
**3.20.** "We are the clan of servitors, the lowly servants of the Lord; we well know (?) this crooked design.[^03-19]  
**3.21.** … (??) in alarm (?) we all became … (??); we did not hand the Supreme Lord over to him.[^03-20]  
**3.22.** Having said so, we all took counsel together and resolved to open the secret southern door.  
**3.23.** Towards the south, by the … (??) way, ran a secret passage; there we opened the bronze door.[^03-21]  
**3.24.** Seven little bells hung there on the door; when we opened [it], the vault (??) trembled at that sound.[^03-22]  
**3.25.** But (?) the Yavana could not make it out: we carried the Supreme Lord away, taking the seat of the brahma (?).[^03-23]  
**3.26.** By the secret southern door we went out; at …-natha (??) we placed the brahma."[^03-24]

The same opening is found in the *Vaishnava-lilamrita* 4.1–4.2: the "Solar dynasty" (Surya-vamsha) is the self-designation of Kapilendra's dynasty, and "a portion of Vishnu" is courtly praise of the king. For Gaudiya Vaishnavas Prataparudra is a great devotee who swept the road before Jagannatha's chariot (CC, Madhya 13), but not an avatara in the theological sense. In the word ≈ ଅଂଶ (*aṁśa*, "portion") an extra sign resembling ନ is printed before ଶ (cf. 3.2, 3.3); possibly ଅଂଶେ ("as a portion"). The word ≈ ଅଦ୍ଭୁତ (*adbhuta*, "wondrous") is printed indistinctly.
[^03-3]: Jaya-Durga is the goddess Durga ("Victorious Durga"). The subject of "obtained" is not named — apparently Prataparudra's parents (cf. 3.6). There is no account of Prataparudra's birth by the goddess's grace in the *Chaitanya-charitamrita* or the *Chaitanya-bhagavata*. Printed ଜୟ ଦୁର୍ଗ ଙ୍କୁ with spaces and without ା; ଦୁର୍ଗାଙ୍କୁ is given. The last word is not identified: a T-shaped letter (ଦ or ହ), a sign resembling ନ, and ଲ; the rhyme with ଲଭିଥିଲେ requires the ending ‑ଲେ.
[^03-4]: Gajapati ("lord of elephants") is the title of the kings of Orissa. Before the last letter of the word a sign resembling ନ is printed (as in 3.1 and 3.2); understood as ମଣ୍ଡଳେ (*maṇḍaḷe*, "in the mandala, in the realm").
[^03-5]: The last word is printed indistinctly (≈ ସତ୍ତ୍ୱର); understood as ସତ୍ୱର (*satwara*, "promptly, without delay"), rhyming with ସାର (*sāra*).
[^03-6]: Purushottama is apparently the Gajapati Purushottama Deva (reigned c. 1467–1497), Prataparudra's father; "he is his son" is said of Prataparudra (the honorific ଅଟନ୍ତି, as in 3.10). Another understanding is possible: Purushottama is Jagannatha, who in Puri is honoured as the true king of Orissa, the Gajapatis being His servants; then "His sons are these kings". The words ≈ ଶରଣ (*śaraṇa*, "refuge") and ≈ ନୃପଗଣ (*nṛpagaṇa*, "kings") are read with doubt.
[^03-7]: The word ≈ କୁରମ (*kurama*) is not identified; it may be connected with Kurma (the tortoise, an avatara of Vishnu) and name a region, but the interpretation is unclear. Kataka (Cuttack) was the Gajapati capital; *kaṭaka* also means "royal camp". "Southern Kataka" may be Prataparudra's camp during his southern campaigns (cf. *Vaishnava-lilamrita* 4.6–4.7). ନଗ୍ର (*nagra*) = ନଗର (*nagara*, "city"). The last word is printed ≈ ନହୁବର; understood as ନୃପବର (*nṛpabara*, "best of kings").
[^03-8]: Shankha-nidhi and padma-nidhi are two of the nine treasures (nidhis) of Kubera, the god of wealth: the king is generous and rich. Maharudra is Shiva. The word ≈ ବଖାଣ (*bakhāṇa*, "as is told, renowned") is read with doubt: the second letter is a conjunct with a slanting stroke (ଖ or ନ୍ଧ; cf. 2.209).
[^03-9]: Prataparudra bears the name of Rudra. Gaudiya Vaishnavas honour Shiva (Rudra) as the greatest Vaishnava (*Srimad-Bhagavatam* 12.13.16), but regard worship of the demigods for the fulfilment of desires as a lower path (*Bhagavad-gita* 7.20–23); in the *Chaitanya-charitamrita* Prataparudra is a devotee of Jagannatha and Sri Chaitanya (Madhya 11–14). The last word of the first half-line is printed ≈ ଗଷିଷ୍ଟ: in this font the sign ରି resembles ଷ; ଗରିଷ୍ଠ (*gariṣṭha*, "most mighty") is given, ଷ୍ଟ being printed, apparently, for the rhyme with ଇଷ୍ଟ.
[^03-10]: From this couplet Kanai tells of Prataparudra's southern wars and of the Bengali army's raid on Puri, when the Deity of Jagannatha was carried away and hidden on Lake Chilika. The same story, largely in the same words, is in Madhava's *Vaishnava-lilamrita*, 4.4–4.23 (notes to VL 4.4–4.23). The *Chaitanya-charitamrita* and the *Chaitanya-bhagavata* do not describe this episode; cf. the note to VL 4.9 (*Chaitanya-bhagavata*, Antya 4: Hussain Shah, the sultan of Gauda, as the destroyer of the temples and murtis of Orissa). ବଡ଼ତ (*baṛata*), printed run together, is understood as ବଡ଼ ତ, "truly great". The word ≈ ତେହ୍ନ (*tehna*, "such") is read uncertainly.
[^03-11]: The "golden bath" (*kanaka snāna*) and apparently the *tulā-puruṣa* (weighing the king on scales against gold, which is then given to brahmanas) are royal "great gifts" (*mahādāna*). The first word of the second half is read uncertainly; ସମ୍ପିଲେ (*sampile*, "handed over, distributed"; possibly ସମର୍ପିଲେ) is printed with a dot above the line.
[^03-12]: Benta-bhiana is the clearing of forests and making of roads for an army; here, campaigns of conquest (cf. VL 4.4 and the note to it). The word ≈ କେଡୁ (*keḍu*) is understood as କେଡ଼େ ("how great"). ଜଣଇ (*jaṇaï*) is understood as "people" (ଜଣ, "person"); possibly ଜାଣଇ ("knows"). The sense of the second half is uncertain.
[^03-13]: An akshauhini is a complete battle division (in the *Mahabharata*, 21,870 chariots, as many elephants, etc.); "seven akshauhinis" is an epic hyperbole. ଅକ୍ଷଉଣୀ (*akṣaüṇī*) is a popular form. On p. 15 the sign େ is in places given as a small hook before the consonant (ବାଣେ, ନୃପେ here; also in 3.21, 3.22, 3.31, 3.32 and elsewhere). The words ≈ ଗଜଗଜ (*gajagaja*, "countless, teeming") and ≈ ଭଜା (*bhajā*, "flee"; possibly ଭଙ୍ଗା, "routed") are read uncertainly.
[^03-14]: Gauda is Bengal, the realm of Sultan Hussain Shah. Udayagiri and Chandragiri are fortresses in the south, the frontier of the Gajapatis' wars with Vijayanagara (cf. VL 4.7 and the note to it); ଉଦେଗିରି (*udegiri*) is a popular form. The first word (≈ ସାନ୍ତବେଣ୍ଟୀ) is not identified; its relation to ବେଣ୍ଟ (3.13) is unclear.
[^03-15]: Kanai reproaches the king: on his campaigns he forgot Jagannatha (cf. 3.17), and then the raid took place (3.18). ଠାଣ (*ṭhāṇa*) is a fortified camp or post. The last word ≈ ଚିନ୍ତିଲ (*cintila*, "thought of") is read uncertainly.
[^03-16]: Cf. VL 4.6: "with thousands of warriors, with a campaigning (?) army he again set out south" (ସହସ୍ରେ ବୀର ଦଣ୍ଡ (?) ସେନ). Here the print has ≈ ସସ୍ରେ (given as ସ[ହ]ସ୍ରେ), ≈ ଘଣ୍ଟ (not identified; VL has ଦଣ୍ଡ (?), "campaign, punishment", in this place) and ≈ ସେନ ("army").
[^03-17]: Ghazi Ismail was a general of Sultan Alauddin Hussain Shah of Bengal; around 1509–1510, while Prataparudra was at war in the south, he raided Orissa and Puri (cf. VL 4.9 and the note to it). ଗାଜ ଇସିମିଲ (*gāja isimila*) is "Ghazi Ismail". The word ≈ ଶୂର (*śūra*) is not identified (VL 4.9 has "from the north" in this place). The first word of the second half is printed as three signs (≈ ର/ଚ, ଡ, ଢ଼ି); understood as ଚଢ଼ି (*caṛhi*, "marching to attack"; cf. ଚଢ଼ଟି in VL 4.9). In the last word ≈ କ୍ଷେତ୍ରମେଳ the "kshetra" (Sri Kshetra, Puri) is clear; the end of the word is not identified.
[^03-18]: Niladri (Nilachala) is Puri. *Kaṭaka* means "army, camp".
[^03-19]: From 3.20 to 3.26 the speakers are apparently the temple servitors (sevakas) themselves, in the first person plural; to whom they are speaking is not said (possibly this is the officer's report to the king, cf. 3.34–3.37). ତ (*ta*, "indeed") may stand in place of ନ ("not"): "we do not know such crookedness".
[^03-20]: The first half is read only in part: the first word ≈ ଅଢ଼ (possibly ଅତି, "very"), ≈ ଆରତେ (*ārate*, "in distress, in alarm"), ≈ କାକରଥ (not identified; by the sense one would expect "alarmed", ≈ କାତର).
[^03-21]: ଗୋପତ (*gopata*, 3.22) means "secret". The word ≈ ରେଷବାଟେ is not identified (ବାଟେ — "by the way"). The bronze door is of bell-metal (*kaṁsāra*).
[^03-22]: The word ≈ ଗୁମ୍ଫଟି is read by the sense as ଗୁମ୍ଫ (*gumpha*, "cave, vault") + ‑ṭi; it is printed ≈ ଗ, ମ with a subscript sign, and ଟି.
[^03-23]: The "brahma" (*brahma*, 3.25–3.26) is, in Odia temple tradition, the hidden sacred substance (*brahma-padārtha*) within the Deity of Jagannatha, which is transferred into the new body when the Deity's body is renewed (nava-kalevara); apparently it is this that the servitors carried away, deceiving the Yavana. Gaudiya Vaishnavas, following Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati, worship the Deity of Jagannatha as the Lord Himself, non-different from His form, not as a casing that holds some substance (cf. *Chaitanya-charitamrita*, Madhya 5: "You are not a pratima — You are the son of the king of Vraja Himself," said of Sakshi-gopala). ବ୍ରହ୍ମସ୍ଥଳ (*brahmasthaḷa*, "the seat of the brahma") is understood uncertainly. ମାତ୍ରକ (*mātraka*) is understood as "but".
[^03-24]: The word ≈ ବାଳିଲେକ is not identified; possibly ବାଳି (*bāḷi*, "sand") and ଲୋକ[ନାଥ] — Lokanatha, a temple of Shiva in Puri — but this is only a guess.
[^03-25]: Cf. VL 4.12: "Seating the Deities on elephants, Nrisimha Uparaya (?) leads [Them]." ମାତ୍ରରେ (*mātrare*) is printed with the same sign ତ୍ର as in ମାତ୍ର (3.16) and ପାତ୍ର (3.37); understood as "as soon as"; by the sense ମାଝରେ ("in the middle [of the night]") is also possible. The first letter of the word ≈ ତାକୁ (*tāku*, "Him") resembles ନ.
[^03-26]: Narasimha (3.27) and Nrisimha Mahapatra are the same person; in VL 4.12 he is called Nrisimha Uparaya (cf. the note to VL 4.12). Mahapatra is a title of rank. Printed ଅ, a space, ନ୍ତି; given as ଅ[ଟ]ନ୍ତି (*aṭanti*, "is", hon.; cf. 3.6, 3.10).
[^03-27]: Pattajoshi (*paṭṭayośī*; printed ≈ ପଠ, a broken sign, ଯୋଶୀ) is the office of the chief administrator of Jagannatha's temple (Pattajoshi Mahapatra). The word ≈ ପଦେଲ is understood as ପଦେ ("in the office"); the last letter is unclear. ବାହି (*bāhi*, "rowing"): the Deity was taken by boat (across Chilika, cf. 3.32–3.33).
[^03-28]: Gada-Kenkal (the fort of Kenkal) — cf. VL 4.11 and 4.19, where the servitors come with the Deities to the fort of Kenkal. The first two words are not identified (≈ ବୁଡ଼ି, ≈ ଯ + a small hook + ନ; given as ଯ[ବ]ନ, "Yavana"), so it is unclear who entered the fort and bathed — the Yavana or the servitors. The word ≈ ସ୍ନାହାନ is understood as ସ୍ନାନ (*snāna*, "bath"); possibly ସ୍ନାନ ଦାନ ("bath and giving of gifts").
[^03-29]: Printed ପ, a space, ତ୍ର; given as ପ[ପା]ତ୍ର (*pātra*, "officers"). The second half is read only in part: ମିଳି ଲ (possibly ମିଳିଲେ, "joined"), ≈ ଗଡ଼ଆମାନେ (not identified; possibly ଗଡ଼ୁଆମାନେ, "the people of the fort"), ଗହଳେ (*gahaḷe*, "together, in the company").
[^03-30]: ହରଦ (*harada*) = ହ୍ରଦ (*hrada*, "lake"); cf. VL 4.13: "in the midst of Lake Chilika, inside … (??) Chadhei". The word ≈ ଚୁମ୍ପିକ is not identified; possibly it is the same unclear word as ≈ କୁଁମାକ in VL 4.13 (understood there as "cave, shelter"). Printed ≈ ଉପୟରେ; given as ଉପାୟରେ (*upāẏare*, "in a way").
[^03-31]: Chilika is a large lagoon south-west of Puri (cf. the note to VL 4.13). The line is read only in part: ≈ ତଟ (*taṭa*, "shore"), ≈ ଅଟେ ("is"), after ସେ a gap in the print (possibly a word is missing), ≈ ଭିଆଣ ("arrangement"?); in the second half ≈ ନୃହ is not identified, and the end of the line (≈ ରା + fragments of signs) is broken.
[^03-32]: Cf. VL 4.14–4.15: "a Pana (?) set out to the south [and] on the bank of the Krishnaveni gives the king the news." Here the print has ≈ ପା + େ + ତ୍ର + କ; understood as ପାତ୍ରେକ (*pātreka* = ପାତ୍ର ଏକ, "a certain officer"); VL has ପାଣେକ (?), "a Pana". The Krishnaveni is the river Krishna in the south, where the king stood with his army.
[^03-33]: The second half is not understood: ≈ ଗୋଟାକ ସାତଟା ହେଲ ("one became seven"? — possibly an idiom for agitation or anger; cf. VL 4.16: "in anger he spoke"), then ≈ ଶୁ + a small sign, ବଂଶ ("dynasty"), ≈ ରା + fragments of signs (the end of the line is broken). Printed ବଖ, a space, ଣ; given as ବଖ[ଖା]ଣ (*bakhāṇa*, "tidings").
[^03-34]: Cf. VL 4.17: "While I live — what deed (?) is being done: the Lord has been given (?) to the Yavanas!" Printed ଥ, a space, ଉଁ; given as ଥ[ଥା]ଉଁ (*thāu̐*, "while I am"). The king apparently understood the news as meaning that the Lord had been handed over to the Yavanas.
[^03-35]: Chadhei-guha (the "Bird Cave") is a hill-island on Chilika where, according to Puri tradition, the Deities were hidden from raids (cf. VL 4.13, 4.18 and the notes to them).
[^03-36]: Cf. VL 4.21: "I will slay the Yavanas — what are they, wretched (?)!" Printed ମ, a dot, ରିଣ; given as ମ[ମା]ରିଣ (*māriṇa*, "having slain"); possibly ମରଣ (*maraṇa*, "doom [for them]").
[^03-37]: Printed ଗଢ଼; corrected to ଗାଢ଼ (*gāṛha*, "firm, strong") after the edition's list of errata ("୧୫।୨୯": page 15, line 29; by our count this is the 29th line of the page, so the number matches). In VL 4.19 the king contemplates the Deities in the fort of Kenkal.
[^03-38]: Parabrahma is a usual name of Jagannatha in Odia tradition. Gaudiya Vaishnavas, following the *Brahma-samhita* (5.1) and the *Chaitanya-charitamrita* (Adi 2), worship Krishna as the Supreme Personality of Godhead and regard the impersonal Brahman as His effulgence; for them Jagannatha is Krishna Himself.
[^03-39]: The account of p. 15 continues: in the Chadhei cave the king beheld the Parabrahman (3.40), and Jagannatha — "the Supreme Krishna" — speaks to him; by 3.43 this was in a dream. The Lord's speech (3.41–3.42) is understood only in part. "These" (*emāna*) are apparently the servitors who saved the Deity (3.20–3.37), whom the Lord bids to enjoy the bhoga (3.42); possibly "these" are the yavanas. The word ≈ ରହିଛ is not identified: printed ≈ ର, a sign like ନ୍ତ or ହି, and ଛ; tentatively understood as "you abide." ପଦ (*pada*) is "feet" or "rank, position" (the king's).
[^03-40]: The word ≈ ଛେଜ (*cheja*) is not identified; possibly a vernacular form of ସେଜ (*seja*, "couch"): "couch and throne." ଘେନଣ (*ghenaṇa*) = ଘେନିଣ ("taking"), as in 1.61. Bhoga is the food offered to Jagannatha; once offered it becomes prasad (cf. 3.61).
[^03-41]: The tradition that Prataparudra accepted Vaishnava initiation after a dream is not found in the *Chaitanya-charitamrita* or the *Chaitanya-bhagavata*. In the *Chaitanya-charitamrita* Prataparudra is a great devotee of Jagannatha who sweeps the road before the chariot (Madhya 13) and receives Sri Chaitanya's mercy (Madhya 14). The word ≈ ଦୀକ୍ଷା (*dīkṣā*, "initiation") is printed indistinctly (≈ ଦୀ + ଷା).
[^03-42]: Cf. *Vaishnava-lilamrita* 4.22: "Making the name of Jagannatha his japa beads, the king swiftly (?) sets out." ଧୁର (*dhura*) is "yoke, burden; foremost, chief"; understood as "mainstay, bearer" of the name; the first letter is ଧ or ବ. The word ≈ ଧେଇଣ is not identified (printed େ, ଧ, a dot, ଇଣ; possibly ଧାଇଁଣ, "rushing"). ସୁମର (*sumara*) is understood as ସମର (*samara*, "battle"); possibly ସୁମରି ("remembering"). ହୋଇଲକ (*hoilaka*) = ହୋଇଲେକ ("became").
[^03-43]: Madara (ମଦାର) is apparently Mandaragada, Garh Mandaran, a fort on the Orissa–Bengal border (cf. *Vaishnava-lilamrita* 4.23 and its note: "he drove off the mlecchas and reached the [place] called Mandaragada"). [^03-44]: The word ≈ ଧାରି is not identified; possibly ଧାଡ଼ି (*dhāṛi*, "raid, onslaught"). [^03-47]: The year 918 is in one of the Odia "san" eras (Amli, Vilayati) or the Bengali san, which lag behind the Common Era by about 592–593 years, i.e. ≈ 1510–1511. [^03-175]: Chintamani is the wish-fulfilling gem, a name of Krishna (cf. the note on 3.161). Printed ଶୁଣ (without ି); given as ଶୁଣି. "My mouth" is *āmbhara* (the respectful "Our"). Krishna seems to deny having uttered Radha's name and sends the queens to Vrindavati.
[^03-176]: Vrindavati is Vrinda (Vrinda-devi); cf. 2.179 and the note there. For Gaudiya Vaishnavas, Vrinda-devi is the goddess of the Vrindavana forest who arranges the meetings of Radha and Krishna (cf. the note on 2.179).

[^03-186]: ≈ କହ (printed କ + ହ, the same ହ as in ତାହା) — the form is unclear: "tell" (imper.) or, by the sense, "[if I] tell". Vrinda fears that at the story of Radha Krishna Himself will come here (cf. 3.208).
[^03-187]: ଉକାଅ is corrected to ଡକାଅ ("call") from the edition's errata ("୨୧।୨୯"). ≈ ବର ("[you] wish, choose"?) is read uncertainly. Kanai calls Subhadra Yogamaya; in the Odia tradition Subhadra is venerated as Yogamaya, the Adya-shakti (cf. 1.55 and the note there); for Gaudiya Vaishnavas Subhadra is Krishna's sister. The story apparently leads to a tradition well known in Puri: the queens of Dvaraka listen to an account of the Vraja pastimes while Subhadra stands at the door, and Krishna and Balarama, coming and hearing it, assume in ecstasy the forms in which They are worshipped in Puri as Jagannatha and Baladeva. This account is not given in the *Chaitanya-charitamrita* or the *Chaitanya-bhagavata*; the story continues on p. 22.
