# Kanai Khuntia. *Mahabhava-prakasha*, Chapter 3
## In praise of King Prataparudra

*Translated from Odia without a native speaker; doubtful places are marked (?) and (??). The original, transliteration and word-for-word translation are in `full/03.md`.*

*The third vritta (?) [section]*[^vritta-1]

**3.1.** Sri Prataparudra is born in the Solar dynasty; he is a wondrous (?) avatara, a portion (?) of Vishnu.[^prataparudra-1]  
**3.2.** By serving Jaya-Durga [his parents] obtained him; since the mercy of the Supreme Lord was there, … (??).[^jayadurga-2]  
**3.3.** By his name the Gajapati dynasty is adorned in the realm (?); at his name the three worlds tremble.[^mandala-3]  
**3.4.** His company is the devotees of Vishnu, [like] wish-fulfilling trees; his mind abides in service to Vishnu's feet.  
**3.5.** Noble, the best of men, the best among the jewels of kings — the Supreme Lord promptly (?) protects him.[^satvara-5]  
**3.6.** That King Purushottama is the refuge (?); he is his son, [the best] among kings (?).[^purushottama-6]  
**3.7.** He is the sovereign of the earth of … (??)-khanda; the best of kings (?) made Southern Kataka his city.[^kurma-7]  
**3.8.** His boundless strength resides in the might of his arms; for the afflicted he becomes a support.  
**3.9.** The treasures shankha-nidhi and padma-nidhi, it is told (?), are in his hand; and he himself serves Maharudra.[^nidhi-9]  
**3.10.** By the power of Maharudra he is most mighty (?); by Rudra's grace all that is desired is fulfilled.[^rudra-10]  

## Prataparudra's southern campaigns; Ghazi Ismail's raid on Puri; the Deity is hidden in Chadhei-guha


**3.11.** That King Prataparudra was himself truly great (?): in the three worlds there remained not a single such (?) king.[^episode-11]  
**3.12.** That king duly performed the golden bath; by [the rite of] the scales (?) he generously distributed (?) [gifts] to the people.[^kanaka-12]  
**3.13.** How great (?) was his might in benta-bhiana! All the people (?) under the king knew no affliction.[^bhiana-13]  
**3.14.** His army of seven akshauhinis was countless (?); from the arrows of his battle kings flee, routed (?).[^akshauhini-14]  
**3.15.** As he … (??) the land of Gauda, such a fate he dealt to Udayagiri [and] Chandragiri.[^gauda-15]  
**3.16.** That king thought of the great posts in the south, but of the Supreme Lord he did not think (?) at all.[^thana-16]  
**3.17.** With thousands (?) of warriors, with a … (??) army (?) he went south; of the Supreme Lord he did not remember at all.[^sahasra-17]  
**3.18.** At this time Ghazi Ismail, from … (??), marched to attack (?) and came to the kshetra (??) of Niladri.[^ismail-18]  
**3.19.** Mustering an army against Niladri, he marched; he said that he would take the Supreme Lord away.[^kataka-19]  
**3.20.** "We are the clan of servitors, the lowly servants of the Lord; we well know (?) this crooked design.[^sevaka-20]  
**3.21.** … (??) in alarm (?) we all became … (??); we did not hand the Supreme Lord over to him.[^kakaratha-21]  
**3.22.** Having said so, we all took counsel together and resolved to open the secret southern door.  
**3.23.** Towards the south, by the … (??) way, ran a secret passage; there we opened the bronze door.[^gopata-23]  
**3.24.** Seven little bells hung there on the door; when we opened [it], the vault (??) trembled at that sound.[^ghanti-24]  
**3.25.** But (?) the Yavana could not make it out: we carried the Supreme Lord away, taking the seat of the brahma (?).[^brahma-25]  
**3.26.** By the secret southern door we went out; at …-natha (??) we placed the brahma."[^lokanatha-26]  
**3.27.** As soon as (?) the night grew deep, Narasimha seated Him (?) on an elephant and carried [Him] away.[^hasti-27]  
**3.28.** That Nrisimha Mahapatra is a wise man (?), the head of the clan of servitors, highly honoured,[^nrisimha-28]  
**3.29.** who is adorned with the office (??) of Pattajoshi; he carried the Supreme Lord away, rowing to the south.[^pattajoshi-29]  
**3.30.** … (??) the Yavana (??) entered the fort of Kenkal (?); having bathed (?) in the lake, he obtained satisfaction.[^kenkal-30]  
**3.31.** From that place, taking [Him], all the officers went; … (??) joined (?) [them], together with the Lord.[^gaduamane-31]  
**3.32.** In this way they carried the Supreme Lord away and placed [Him in] … (??), which was in the midst of the lake.[^harada-32]  
**3.33.** That is the shore (?) of Chilika; there … (??). In that place the Lord stayed; … (??) the king … (??).[^chilika-33]  
**3.34.** A certain officer (?), seeing this, set out to the south and met the king on the bank of the river Krishnaveni.[^pana-34]  
**3.35.** When that king heard such tidings, … (??).[^satata-35]  
**3.36.** He said: "While I live (?), you report this to me as good news — that [someone] has given the Lord of our clan to the Yavana!"[^thau-36]  
**3.37.** Then that officer, with folded hands, reported: "O king, we have safely hidden [Him] in Chadhei-guha."[^chadhei-37]  
**3.38.** Hearing these words, the king became calm; he said: "This very day I will slay (?) [them] — what is a wretched Yavana [to me]!"[^marina-38]  
**3.39.** Having spoken such firm words, the king, deeply moved, entered Chadhei-guha.[^corr-39]  
**3.40.** In that place he beheld the Parabrahma and, exclaiming "Hari, Hari!", offered obeisance.[^parabrahma-40]  

## Prataparudra's dream and initiation; the siege of Madara; Sri Chaitanya comes to Puri; the king learns of the young sannyasi


**3.41.** The Supreme Krishna said: "Listen, O king: you … (??) the feet (?); these would have stayed (?) before (?).[^dream-41]  
**3.42.** We would keep (?) … (??) [and] the throne in this place; taking all the bhoga, let them enjoy [it]."[^cheja-42]  
**3.43.** Such words he heard in a dream; from that day he accepted Vaishnava initiation (?).[^diksha-43]  
**3.44.** He became a mainstay (?) of the syllables of the name "Hare Krishna"; … (??) together with elephants he went to battle (?).[^dhura-44]  
**3.45.** … (??) having come to Madara, … (?) halted (?); the news arose that Prataparudra would come.[^madara-45]  
**3.46.** All the troops did not make … (??); for a month they could not breach the fort of Madara.[^dhari-46]  
**3.47.** Having returned from there, the king showed his greatness (?); as a great Vaishnava he served the Lord in the city (?).[^badapana-47]  
**3.48.** For some days (?) he served the Lord … (??); [then] the king swiftly went to the southern quarter.[^chapura-48]  
**3.49.** The nine hundred (?) and eighteenth year (sal) began; thus, by good fortune, … (??) — when [He] entered.[^sal-49]  
**3.50.** In Chaitra (?), the best spring month (?), at the time of … (??); at a youthful age … (??), at the time when He entered.[^chaitra-50]  
**3.51.** With Him, in countless crowds, the Vaishnava fellowship; from the youthful face … (??) flow (?) [tears?].[^jyota-51]  
**3.52.** With a joyful face, with the name of Hari on His lips, He — the Moon of Shachi (?) — entered the Kshetra.[^shachi-52]  
**3.53.** Whose form is the full moon (?), [whose] bhava is guileless; staff and kamandalu are His natural adornment.[^danda-53]  
**3.54.** Like a youth, [He shines with] a sweet smile (?); crying out the name "Radha, Radha," He sings most sweetly.[^radha-54]  
**3.55.** [He], the moon of Nadia, came on the twelfth lunar day; the day … (?) was a Thursday, … (??).[^dvadashi-55]  
**3.56.** All went to see the young [sannyasi]; by the sight [of Him] life, eyes and mind are filled.  
**3.57.** Step by step (?) [He] entered the Sri Mandir; at the cries of "Hari bol!" the universe shook.  
**3.58.** That very Lord Gauranga granted darshan; whoever … (??) [this darshan] — for him the pious deeds of this birth bore fruit.[^gauranga-58]  
**3.59.** Describing the hidden lilas with His lips, Gaurachandra went on, hearing [the chanting of] the names.[^aprakata-59]  
**3.60.** All heard such words with their ears; [they] deemed themselves blessed, [their] birth greatly (?) [fruitful].[^jataka-60]  
**3.61.** From the Bhoga-mandapa building [they] received prasad; in the [temple] enclosure He, the embodiment of yoga (?), deeply [absorbed], … (??).[^bhogamandapa-61]

**3.63.** Thus some days passed; … (??) the Kshetra, … (??), [He] went to the south.[^south-63]  
**3.64.** Thus, when the month of Mithuna began, the king with his retinue (?) went to the service (?) of the Lord.[^mithuna-64]  
**3.65.** [He] remains guarding the door of the anasara, … (??); in this service … (??) no one equals [him].[^anasara-65]  
**3.66.** This service, rare [even] for the gods, that king performs; for it he came, bringing … (?) [festive gifts].[^badhei-66]  
**3.67.** All the servitors took … (??); … (??) became pratiharis (door-keepers).[^pratihari-67]  
**3.68.** All this he heard — of the doings (?) of the sannyasi whose mind always abides in Krishna's rasa.[^ganta-68]  
**3.69.** The whole story of Him Lenka narrated: "A young sannyasi-deva has come here."[^lenka-69]  

## Tadhau reads the panjis; the praise of Gauranga; Ramananda Raya


**3.70.** [The king] brought Tadhau, and [he], taking out the panjis, read: "A certain young vairagi has come (?).[^tadhau-70]  
**3.71.** A circle of devotees came with Him; having seen the Supreme Lord, He went south (?).[^dakshina-71]  
**3.72.** That Prabhu made the Kunja-matha His abode. Concerning this, O great one (?), be pleased to hear."[^kunja-72]  
**3.73.** Hearing [this], the king was truly (?) astonished: "We have heard of Him from … (?)."[^heard-73]  
**3.74.** He is Sri Gauranga, the supreme devotee, the foremost … of Jagannatha …[^ellipsis-74]  
**3.75.** Gauranga, [my] life, is mother Shachi's darling child; Gauranga, [my] life, is the child of Jagannatha [Mishra].[^prana-75]  
**3.76.** Gauranga, [my] life, is a sweet form; in the body of Gauranga, [my] life, is the light of the world.  
**3.77.** In the feet of Gauranga, [my] life, is the poise of Natavara; on the body of Gauranga, [my] life, are the marks of the vajra (??).[^natavara-77]  
**3.78.** Gauranga, [my] life, is the avatara of a new ascetic (?); Gauranga, [my] life, is tender — [in Him is] the portion of Radha.[^yati-78]  
**3.79.** On the neck of Gauranga, [my] life, are beads of tulasi; Gauranga, [my] life, is unhindered in various [fields of] knowledge (?).[^jnana-79]  
**3.80.** Gauranga, [my] life, is the treasury of all knowledge; Gauranga, [my] life, is the moon of Gauda …[^gaudachandra-80]  
**3.81.** Gauranga, [my] life, is the husband of Lakshmi-devi; Gauranga, [my] life, is the understanding of all the scriptures.[^lakshmi-81]  
**3.82.** On the face of Gauranga, [my] life, is ever a line of smile; Gauranga, [my] life, proclaims (?) the explanation of the Vedanta.[^vedanta-82]  
**3.83.** In Gauranga, [my] life, all knowledge is gathered; in Gauranga, [my] life, the primal root abides merged (?).[^adimula-83]  
**3.84.** Gauranga, [my] life, saw Gangadhara (?); Gauranga, [my] life, gazed with a sweet smile (?).[^gangadhara-84]  
**3.85.** The face of Gauranga, [my] life, is like the full moon (?); seeing Gauranga, [my] life, the three worlds are happy.[^corr-85]  
**3.86.** Whoever … (??) beholds these [forms] even once, his sorrow of birth after birth is extinguished.[^janena-86]  
**3.87.** Such is the glory of that Chaitanya Thakura: He is the lord of yogis of the whole earth.  
**3.88.** The king had heard [of Him] from the mouth of Raya Ramananda, and resolved to meet the young ascetic at least once.[^ramananda-88]  
**3.89.** By Providence that ascetic entered Sri Kshetra; there was a [great] gathering [of people] at the festival of Sri Gundicha.[^gundicha-89]  
**3.90.** Here the name of Sri Gauranga became renowned; of this Sri Raya Ramananda heard (?).  
**3.91.** He is the son of Bhavananda, the Karana (?); the best of villages, Bentapur, is his estate.[^bhavananda-91]  
**3.92.** He is the Pattajoshi (?) of Prataparudra; … (??) he is a devotee, unceasingly [full of] bhava.[^pattayasha-92]  
**3.93.** His mind is ever intoxicated with Krishna-rasa; ever in love for child Krishna, he is a devotee [whose devotion is] mixed with knowledge.[^jnanamishra-93]  
**3.94.** He meditates on the Supreme Krishna; his consciousness is ever service in the mood of a servant.  
**3.95.** His younger brother is Gopinatha; he is not … (??); with him … (??) the king (??); [his] mind is ever on Krishna.[^gopinatha-95]  
**3.96.** … (??) little by little [he] grasped everything; his royal duties went away, and Shachi's Lord became [his] all.[^shachisai-96]  

## Ramananda brings the king to Sri Chaitanya; the king questions the Guru about the tattva of Jagannatha


**3.97.** [Ramananda] became a servant at the feet of Shachi's darling child; to introduce [Him] to the king he took great pains (?).[^bhetaiba-97]  
**3.98.** Before King Pratapa … did (?), led (?) …[^lacuna-98]  
**3.99.** Describing the glory of Sri Chaitanya Thakura, he brought (?) the king … (??).[^bedharuni-99]  
**3.100.** That king became enraptured (?) in devotion (?); his mind was always [turned] to Chaitanya Thakura.[^bhola-100]  
**3.101.** He is always firmly immersed in the name of Hari; [his mind] stays neither on the royal throne (?) nor in any other abode.[^takata-101]  
**3.102.** When that bhava became manifest and arose, when Sri Gauranga Prabhu showed [him] mercy —  
**3.103.** at that time that king was in our (?) company; joining his lotus (?) palms, he spoke before the Guru.[^guru-103]  
**3.104.** He said: "O supreme Guru, hear (?) my words: you are indeed the crest-jewel of yogis.  
**3.105.** To you indeed (?) [my] obeisance, most perfect … (??); you yourself are the complete … (??) of Brahman.[^brahmabani-105]  
**3.106.** You yourself are indeed an avatara, [a part] of Radha's body; you are indeed the essence of the treasury of all knowledge.[^radhaanga-106]  
**3.107.** You have indeed seen everything with your own eyes; you indeed know the glory of Jagannatha.  
**3.108.** You indeed, then, in the Jagamohana — you indeed began (?) [it] at the time of the great dance;[^jagamohana-108]  
**3.109.** you indeed, rapt in the dance, carved (?) the stone and had the print of Sripada's lotus feet preserved there.[^footprint-109]  
**3.110.** At Sripada's lotus feet the Vaishnava's mind always rests; at Sripada's lotus feet is all (?) my entreaty.  
**3.111.** You are indeed all-knowing, a seer of the three times; you indeed know [all] — [you are] a brahmarshi (??) and one who speaks of Brahman (??).[^brahmarshi-111]  
**3.112.** Have mercy, O Prabhu, … (??) my doubt; may Mahaprabhu dispel [it] — let my doubt go.[^corr-112]

**3.114.** My countless sins will not (?) be destroyed — how then (?) will this offence be expiated (?)?"[^aparadha-114]

**3.116.** "Dispel, O Prabhu, the doubt in my mind, O Antaryami! I am not a holy man … (??).[^antaryami-116]  
**3.117.** He in Whose service Brahma and Shankara toil, Whose feet are served by … (??),[^dulani-117]  
**3.118.** Whose qualities Vani-devi is engaged in singing, Whose name Narada constantly remembers —

**3.120.** I am not a worthy vessel for this matter; but you have seen His face.

**3.122.** The blue and the white forms became one body; there the supreme light of the Pair shone forth.  
**3.123.** You yourself are indeed a divine part (?); who would not accept those words … (??)?[^angi-123]

[^vritta-1]: The chapter heading is printed ତୃତୀୟ ବୃନ୍ତ (*tṛtīẏa bṛnta*); on the word ବୃନ୍ତ cf. the note to 2.1 (in `../ch02/MP-02-1-28-en.md`). The heading stands on p. 14 after the colophon of Chapter 2 (after 2.210); Chapter 3 begins with it, and the numbering of the couplets starts again from 1.
[^prataparudra-1]: Prataparudra was the Gajapati of Orissa (reigned c. 1497–1540) and Mahaprabhu's patron in Puri (cf. the note to 1.32; *Chaitanya-charitamrita*, Madhya 11–14). The same opening is found in the *Vaishnava-lilamrita* 4.1–4.2: the "Solar dynasty" (Surya-vamsha) is the self-designation of Kapilendra's dynasty, and "a portion of Vishnu" is courtly praise of the king. For Gaudiya Vaishnavas Prataparudra is a great devotee who swept the road before Jagannatha's chariot (CC, Madhya 13), but not an avatara in the theological sense. In the word ≈ ଅଂଶ (*aṁśa*, "portion") an extra sign resembling ନ is printed before ଶ (cf. 3.2, 3.3); possibly ଅଂଶେ ("as a portion"). The word ≈ ଅଦ୍ଭୁତ (*adbhuta*, "wondrous") is printed indistinctly.
[^jayadurga-2]: Jaya-Durga is the goddess Durga ("Victorious Durga"). The subject of "obtained" is not named — apparently Prataparudra's parents (cf. 3.6). There is no account of Prataparudra's birth by the goddess's grace in the *Chaitanya-charitamrita* or the *Chaitanya-bhagavata*. Printed ଜୟ ଦୁର୍ଗ ଙ୍କୁ with spaces and without ା; ଦୁର୍ଗାଙ୍କୁ is given. The last word is not identified: a T-shaped letter (ଦ or ହ), a sign resembling ନ, and ଲ; the rhyme with ଲଭିଥିଲେ requires the ending ‑ଲେ.
[^mandala-3]: Gajapati ("lord of elephants") is the title of the kings of Orissa. Before the last letter of the word a sign resembling ନ is printed (as in 3.1 and 3.2); understood as ମଣ୍ଡଳେ (*maṇḍaḷe*, "in the mandala, in the realm").
[^satvara-5]: The last word is printed indistinctly (≈ ସତ୍ତ୍ୱର); understood as ସତ୍ୱର (*satwara*, "promptly, without delay"), rhyming with ସାର (*sāra*).
[^purushottama-6]: Purushottama is apparently the Gajapati Purushottama Deva (reigned c. 1467–1497), Prataparudra's father; "he is his son" is said of Prataparudra (the honorific ଅଟନ୍ତି, as in 3.10). Another understanding is possible: Purushottama is Jagannatha, who in Puri is honoured as the true king of Orissa, the Gajapatis being His servants; then "His sons are these kings". The words ≈ ଶରଣ (*śaraṇa*, "refuge") and ≈ ନୃପଗଣ (*nṛpagaṇa*, "kings") are read with doubt.
[^kurma-7]: The word ≈ କୁରମ (*kurama*) is not identified; it may be connected with Kurma (the tortoise, an avatara of Vishnu) and name a region, but the interpretation is unclear. Kataka (Cuttack) was the Gajapati capital; *kaṭaka* also means "royal camp". "Southern Kataka" may be Prataparudra's camp during his southern campaigns (cf. *Vaishnava-lilamrita* 4.6–4.7). ନଗ୍ର (*nagra*) = ନଗର (*nagara*, "city"). The last word is printed ≈ ନହୁବର; understood as ନୃପବର (*nṛpabara*, "best of kings").
[^nidhi-9]: Shankha-nidhi and padma-nidhi are two of the nine treasures (nidhis) of Kubera, the god of wealth: the king is generous and rich. Maharudra is Shiva. The word ≈ ବଖାଣ (*bakhāṇa*, "as is told, renowned") is read with doubt: the second letter is a conjunct with a slanting stroke (ଖ or ନ୍ଧ; cf. 2.209).
[^rudra-10]: Prataparudra bears the name of Rudra. Gaudiya Vaishnavas honour Shiva (Rudra) as the greatest Vaishnava (*Srimad-Bhagavatam* 12.13.16), but regard worship of the demigods for the fulfilment of desires as a lower path (*Bhagavad-gita* 7.20–23); in the *Chaitanya-charitamrita* Prataparudra is a devotee of Jagannatha and Sri Chaitanya (Madhya 11–14). The last word of the first half-line is printed ≈ ଗଷିଷ୍ଟ: in this font the sign ରି resembles ଷ; ଗରିଷ୍ଠ (*gariṣṭha*, "most mighty") is given, ଷ୍ଟ being printed, apparently, for the rhyme with ଇଷ୍ଟ.
[^episode-11]: From this couplet Kanai tells of Prataparudra's southern wars and of the Bengali army's raid on Puri, when the Deity of Jagannatha was carried away and hidden on Lake Chilika. The same story, largely in the same words, is in Madhava's *Vaishnava-lilamrita*, 4.4–4.23 (notes to VL 4.4–4.23). The *Chaitanya-charitamrita* and the *Chaitanya-bhagavata* do not describe this episode; cf. the note to VL 4.9 (*Chaitanya-bhagavata*, Antya 4: Hussain Shah, the sultan of Gauda, as the destroyer of the temples and murtis of Orissa). ବଡ଼ତ (*baṛata*), printed run together, is understood as ବଡ଼ ତ, "truly great". The word ≈ ତେହ୍ନ (*tehna*, "such") is read uncertainly.
[^kanaka-12]: The "golden bath" (*kanaka snāna*) and apparently the *tulā-puruṣa* (weighing the king on scales against gold, which is then given to brahmanas) are royal "great gifts" (*mahādāna*). The first word of the second half is read uncertainly; ସମ୍ପିଲେ (*sampile*, "handed over, distributed"; possibly ସମର୍ପିଲେ) is printed with a dot above the line.
[^bhiana-13]: Benta-bhiana is the clearing of forests and making of roads for an army; here, campaigns of conquest (cf. VL 4.4 and the note to it). The word ≈ କେଡୁ (*keḍu*) is understood as କେଡ଼େ ("how great"). ଜଣଇ (*jaṇaï*) is understood as "people" (ଜଣ, "person"); possibly ଜାଣଇ ("knows"). The sense of the second half is uncertain.
[^akshauhini-14]: An akshauhini is a complete battle division (in the *Mahabharata*, 21,870 chariots, as many elephants, etc.); "seven akshauhinis" is an epic hyperbole. ଅକ୍ଷଉଣୀ (*akṣaüṇī*) is a popular form. On p. 15 the sign େ is in places given as a small hook before the consonant (ବାଣେ, ନୃପେ here; also in 3.21, 3.22, 3.31, 3.32 and elsewhere). The words ≈ ଗଜଗଜ (*gajagaja*, "countless, teeming") and ≈ ଭଜା (*bhajā*, "flee"; possibly ଭଙ୍ଗା, "routed") are read uncertainly.
[^gauda-15]: Gauda is Bengal, the realm of Sultan Hussain Shah. Udayagiri and Chandragiri are fortresses in the south, the frontier of the Gajapatis' wars with Vijayanagara (cf. VL 4.7 and the note to it); ଉଦେଗିରି (*udegiri*) is a popular form. The first word (≈ ସାନ୍ତବେଣ୍ଟୀ) is not identified; its relation to ବେଣ୍ଟ (3.13) is unclear.
[^thana-16]: Kanai reproaches the king: on his campaigns he forgot Jagannatha (cf. 3.17), and then the raid took place (3.18). ଠାଣ (*ṭhāṇa*) is a fortified camp or post. The last word ≈ ଚିନ୍ତିଲ (*cintila*, "thought of") is read uncertainly.
