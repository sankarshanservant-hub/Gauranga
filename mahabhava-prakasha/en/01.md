# Kanai Khuntia. *Mahabhava-prakasha*, Chapter 1
## Mangalacharana: glory to Jagannatha-Krishna and to Sri Gaurasundara

*Translated from Odia without a native speaker; doubtful places are marked (?) and (??). The original, transliteration and word-for-word translation are in `full/01.md`.*[^author-1]

*Obeisance, obeisance to Sri Guru!*  
*Sri Mahabhava-prakasha*

**1.1.** Glory to the all-auspicious wish-fulfilling tree! Glory to the Lord of Nilachala, glory to the great Meru![^niladri-1]  
**1.2.** Glory to Krishna, the chintamani, the Consort of Kamala! How many (?) millions of universes You bring forth from [the pores of Your] hair (?)![^loma-2]  
**1.3.** You dwell in the midst of the ocean, O Govinda — what bliss You display (?), O Hari (?), in yogic repose (??)![^yoga-3]  
**1.4.** In the form of the great Ananta [You are] the form of Shesha; in a single moment (?) he spews forth the halahala poison.[^shesha-4]  
**1.5.** O Father of the universe! Your body is dark as a rain-cloud; not for a moment does it slip (?) from the minds of the yogis.  
**1.6.** From Your navel-lotus this lotus was born, upon which Brahma appears (?).[^brahma-6]  
**1.7.** On the hips — yellow garments; armlets, a girdle of little bells; tassels (?) … (??) captivate the minds of the devotees.[^jhoba-7]  
**1.8.** With crossed feet, O Lord, on that blissful couch You abide, O Supreme One, with a smiling face.  
**1.9.** Kamala ceaselessly serves [Your] lotus feet; pressing the feet to her heart, she considers herself fulfilled.  
**1.10.** Vani, the holder of the vina (?), praises [Your] qualities; Indra sprinkles [You] with sandal … (??) with zeal (?).[^indra-10]  
**1.11.** Those lotus feet on which the wise reflect — those lotus feet the meditators see.  
**1.12.** Those lotus feet (?) are filled with auspicious marks: the flag and the others — the triangle (?), the lotus, the weapon (??) — marked (?) with lines.[^samudrika-12]  
**1.13.** On Your lotus feet the sages ceaselessly reflect; the Supreme Person they see on the path of knowledge.[^purushottama-13]  
**1.14.** [Hearts] long for the service of those lotus feet; the hearts of the devotees are ever enchanted by Your qualities.  
**1.15.** How many great souls long for [Your] darshan! The yogis are ever immersed in yoga.  
**1.16.** He who is on this path of bhakti is Gaurasundara, the son of Shachi, composed, with a most tender body.[^gaura-16]  
**1.17.** His (?) golden glow is like the sun; a young sannyasi, dazzling in [His] radiance.  
**1.18.** A kala of the Parabrahman in thirteen (?) kalas; in the Dvapara age … amid … (??)[^kala-18]  
**1.19.** He who is born of a portion … (??) is the son of Shachi; born of Radha's kala … the young son of a brahmin.[^amsha-19]  
**1.20.** The younger brother of Nityananda, the treasure of the devotees' hearts; at His feet the wise Vaishnavas serve.[^nityananda-20]  
**1.21.** Saviour of the distressed, Prabhu, the life of the devotees — the cause of the [new] life of Jagai and Madhai.[^jagai-21]  
**1.22.** By His mercy sinners cross over [the ocean of existence]; [even] a man born a chandala obtains (?) liberation.[^chandala-22]

**1.39.** My father is Niranjana Khuntia, wise [and] humble (?); thanks to his good deeds my birth became fruitful (?).[^niranjana-39]  
**1.40.** Near Nila-shaila, surrounded by … (??), is the goddess Harachandi, filled with glory.[^harachandi-40]

## The Ratha-yatra: the procession of the Deities; Mahaprabhu's dance; the author's ecstasy


**1.51.** They moved ahead in haste (?), … (??) … (??); with the sound of the instruments land and water (?) [resounded] — [there played] the Telangi instruments.[^telangi-51]  
**1.52.** With a rumbling sound … (??) [the procession] began (?): the radiant, supreme Sudarshana came forth (?).[^sudarshana-52]  
**1.53.** Sri Bada … (??) Prabhu, the incarnation of Shesha, who has neither beginning nor end — it cannot (?) be told.[^bada-53]  
**1.54.** He at whose feet Brahma [and] Ananta (?) toil — the daitapatis brought Him out in procession.[^ananta-54]  
**1.55.** Subhadra, the primordial Shakti, came forth on … (?); among the servants (?) I too, that fool, was toiling.[^subhadra-55]  
**1.56.** In the form of Vamana Mahaprabhu proceeds; surrounded by servants, Prabhu makes His ceremonial progress.[^vamana-56]  
**1.57.** He in whose service the thirty-six (?) niyogas toil — the crest-jewel of the gods Himself goes forth in procession.[^niyoga-57]  
**1.58.** Kashi Mishra, Mukunda and the others (?) — all the devotees — stand surrounding Gauranga.[^mukunda-58]  
**1.59.** [There was] the Gajapati, and with him eighteen karanas; among the karanas there is (?) one more in the retinue —[^karana-59]  
**1.60.** Lakshmidhara by name, he is the sole minister; and Harichandana [stands] by the [royal] umbrellas (?).[^harichandana-60]  
**1.61.** The crest-jewel of kings, having performed … (??) [and] taking (?) …, at the time of the procession … (??) … (??) taking (?).[^yodabedha-61]  
**1.62.** Crying (?) "The procession of glory!" … (??), in eager longing Sri Prabhu broke (??) into dance.[^mahima-62]  
**1.63.** Shrinivasa at that time was watching [Him], absorbed in dance, … (??) Prabhu, having lost external awareness.[^shrinivasa-63]  
**1.64.** At that time Harichandana the karana pushed him with his hand and held him back.[^barana-64]  
**1.65.** [He] had not (??) attained (?) Sri Prabhu's ecstatic feeling (?); the greatness (?) of the dance, though he understood, he did not understand … (??).[^bhara-65]  
**1.66.** As many dance-plays of Sri Prabhu as I saw — birth after birth (??) … (??) I was immersed [in them].[^janama-66]  
**1.67.** When at that time I saw the Lord of the three worlds (?) — … (??) of radiant light in the middle of the eyes.[^jyoti-67]  
**1.68.** Beholding that radiant halo in Gaura's face, I saw the power of a glimmer (?) of mahabhava.[^mahabhava-68]  
**1.69.** My life became blessed, blessed is my birth; what (?) greater wealth (?), what honour should I seek to obtain?  
**1.70.** Clasping [His] feet, I entered into the shelter of Prabhu; I considered that I had attained [a place] equal to a servant of servants.[^dasanudasa-70]  
**1.71.** Sattvika transformations, and again the wild dance; setting [it] aside (?), [He] embraced the devotees.[^uddanda-71]  
**1.72.** [My body], bristling with gooseflesh [like the stalk of] a lotus (??), attained the knot of prema … (??); trembling (??) [and] boundless pulaka seized me.[^salila-72]  
**1.73.** With a grinding of teeth I stood (?) trembling (?); over my body sweat flowed (?) in streams (?)…[^baha-73]  
**1.74.** Tears flowed (?), my cheeks grew wet; what fortune is mine — Prabhu showed [me] mercy!  
**1.75.** All this was [from] the Lord's ecstatic feeling; … (??) the hair (?) rolled on the ground.[^chula-75]  
**1.76.** … Prabhu rolled (?) on the ground — He, [my] life and body (??).[^half-76]  
**1.77.** Sri Gaura's throat was parched, [He was] wholly unconscious; … (??) foam flowed (??) in bubbles.[^phena-77]  
**1.78.** All the devotees became distraught; at the Lord's feet they fell prostrate (??) on the ground.[^ashtanga-78]  

[^author-1]: Kanai (Kanhai) Khuntia was a servant of Jagannatha in Puri from among the *khuntia* temple servants (one of the hereditary groups of the temple's sevakas), a contemporary and companion of Sri Chaitanya. In Madhava's *Vaishnava-lilamrita* (VL 2.25–27) Kanhai builds the rahasa mandapa near Bata-Ganesha and holds sankirtana there together with Gopala Guru. The text follows the 1965 edition (Baripada), p. 1; the edition gives the poem incomplete — it breaks off in the middle. The chapter opens with a mangalacharana: praise of Jagannatha as Krishna-Vishnu (1.1–1.15) and of Sri Gaurasundara (from 1.16).
[^niladri-1]: Nilachala (Niladri, "the Blue Mountain") is Puri; its Lord is Jagannatha. Meru is the world-mountain, the axis of the universe.
[^loma-2]: A picture drawn from the *Bhagavata* (10.14.11): Brahma says that countless universes pass like specks of dust through the pores of the hair on the Lord's body (cf. *Chaitanya-charitamrita*, Adi 5, on Maha-Vishnu). Two words are read tentatively: କେବଣ (*kebaṇa*, archaic "which, what") and ଲୋମୁ (*lomu*, "from the hair"; the ā-stroke of ୋ is not visible).
[^yoga-3]: The second half-line is read and understood with uncertainty: ହର (*hara*) is perhaps "Hari" with a lost "ି" (or Hara?); ଯୋଗି ଅଙ୍କ ଛନ୍ଦ (*yogi aṅka chanda*) — "on the lap of yoga"? "in yogic guise"? The translation follows the sense: Vishnu reclining on the waters of the Causal Ocean in yoga-nidra.
[^shesha-4]: Shesha (Ananta) is the thousand-headed serpent, the couch of Vishnu. Printed ମର୍ତ୍ତି (*martti*); we read it as ମୁର୍ତ୍ତି (*murtti*, "form"), our correction. The fire and poison that Ananta-Sankarshana spews forth at the destruction of the worlds are described in the *Bhagavata* (Canto 3, ch. 11). The word ମୁହୂର୍ତ୍ତିକେ (*muhūrttike*, "in a single moment"?) is printed with a break; the reading is tentative.
[^brahma-6]: Brahma is born on the lotus that grows from the navel of Vishnu (Garbhodakashayi). The pair ଆତ ଯାତ (*āta yāta*) is not identified — perhaps an echo-word meaning "is born, appears" (*yāta* = *jāta*).
[^jhoba-7]: The keyura is an armlet; *kiṅkiṇī* (here କଙ୍କିଣୀ) is a girdle of little bells. ଝୋବା (*jhobā*) apparently means "tassels, bunches" (ornaments); the next word, ମଡ଼ିଆଳ (*maṛiāḷa*?), is not identified.
[^indra-10]: Vani is Sarasvati, the goddess of speech. The word ବୀଣାଧର (*bīṇādhara*, "holder of the vina") is read by sense. In the second half-line the word printed ≈ କେଷ (*keṣa*; "hair"? "keshara", saffron?) is not identified; ନିଠାଇ (*niṭhāi*) is understood tentatively — from ନିଠା (= ନିଷ୍ଠା, "firmness, zeal").
[^samudrika-12]: Samudrika is the science of auspicious bodily marks. Tradition sees on Krishna's feet the flag, lotus, goad, thunderbolt, barley-grain, triangle and other marks. The word after "lotus" is printed unclearly (≈ ଅସ୍ତ୍ର୍ୟ, *astrya*: "weapon"? with a superfluous ya-phala sign); ରେଖାରେଣ is an archaic instrumental, "with lines". The first word of the line (ପାଦପଦ୍ମଟି, "lotus feet") is read by comparing its letters with *padma* in this line and the previous one.
[^purushottama-13]: "The Supreme Person" — *uttama puruṣa*, i.e. Purushottama: a name of the Lord and of Puri (Purushottama-kshetra), where He abides as Jagannatha.
[^gaura-16]: Here the author passes from the praise of Jagannatha-Vishnu to Sri Chaitanya — Gaurasundara, the son of Shachi. According to Gaudiya Vaishnava teaching (*Chaitanya-charitamrita*, Adi 1–4), Sri Chaitanya is Krishna Himself, who has assumed the mood and golden complexion of Sri Radha.
[^kala-18]: The couplet is damaged: the edition prints dots in place of the lost text. The reading ତେର (*tera*, "thirteen"?) and the word before "vana" (≈ ସଧ୍ୟବନ) are uncertain. Kanai apparently calls Gaura a "kala", a portion of the Parabrahman — an Odia formula. Gaudiya Vaishnavas, following Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati, teach otherwise: Krishna is the Supreme Lord Himself, the source of all avataras (*Bhagavata* 1.3.28), and Sri Chaitanya is Krishna Himself, not His portion (*Chaitanya-charitamrita*, Adi 1–2).
[^amsha-19]: The word before "portion" (≈ ନନ୍ଦଚଳା, *nandacaḷā*?) is not identified; it may be connected with Nanda (i.e. with Krishna, Nanda's son). The edition has a lacuna (dots). "Born of a portion (*aṁśa*) … and of Radha's kala" is an Odia conception. According to the *Chaitanya-charitamrita* (Adi 1.5; Adi 4), Sri Chaitanya is Radha and Krishna united in one form, not an offspring of their portions. "The son of a brahmin" is Sri Chaitanya, son of Jagannatha Mishra.
[^nityananda-20]: For Gaudiya Vaishnavas Nityananda is Balarama, the elder brother of Krishna (*Chaitanya-charitamrita*, Adi 5); hence Gaura is His "younger brother".
[^jagai-21]: Jagai and Madhai were brahmin brothers of Navadvipa, drunkards and robbers, who were saved by Nityananda and Sri Chaitanya (*Chaitanya-bhagavata*, Madhya 13).
[^chandala-22]: A chandala is a person of the lowest, "untouchable" birth. The word ଲଭ (*labha*, "obtains"?) is read with uncertainty.
[^tora-23]: "Your" refers to Jagannatha, to whom the mangalacharana (1.1–1.15) is addressed. The second half-line is not read: the print has approximately ନମସ୍କ୍ରିଲେ କଲା ଯେ ସାକ୍ଷାତ ଦେବୀ ସେଯେ (*namaskrile kalā ye sākṣāta debī seye?*). The first word is probably a distorted ନମସ୍କରିଲେ ("bowed"); the rest is unidentified. The sense of the first half-line: Sri Gaura in Puri bows to Jagannatha (cf. *Chaitanya-charitamrita*, Madhya 6: the first darshan of Jagannatha).
[^daine-24]: ଦଇନେ (*daïne*) is taken as "humbly" (from *dainya*); the first letter is ଦ or ତ. "The city" is apparently Puri. On the preaching of the holy name in Puri: *Chaitanya-charitamrita*, Madhya 10–11.
[^srivasa-26]: Shrivasa (Shrivasa Thakura) is Mahaprabhu's associate in Navadvipa, in whose house the night-long kirtans took place (*Chaitanya-charitamrita*, Adi 10, 17); with the other Bengali devotees he came to Puri every year for the Ratha-yatra. The line is printed as a single half-line: the second half of the couplet is missing in the edition (an editorial note mark stands in its place). The last sign of the first word is printed unusually (≈ ଦ); we take it as "Shrivasadi", "Shrivasa and the others".
[^sarvabhauma-27]: Sarvabhauma Bhattacharya, the Vedantist scholar at Prataparudra's court, disputed with Mahaprabhu on Vedanta and was defeated and converted to bhakti (*Chaitanya-charitamrita*, Madhya 6). The last word is printed approximately ତଜ୍ଞ (*tajña*, Skt. *tajjña*, "knower of That [Truth]"); a different first letter (ବିଜ୍ଞ, "learned") is also possible.
[^tribhanga-28]: Tribhanga is the "threefold-bent" stance of Krishna-Gopala with the flute. According to Gaudiya Vaishnava teaching, Mahaprabhu is Krishna Himself, having taken the mood and golden complexion of Sri Radha (*Chaitanya-charitamrita*, Adi 4); He revealed His form as Krishna to chosen devotees (to Ramananda Raya, Madhya 8; to Sarvabhauma, the six-armed form, Madhya 6).
[^atapata-29]: The word ଆତପାତ (*ātapāta*) is unidentified; it recurs in 1.33. Cf. ଆତ ଯାତ (*āta yāta*) in 1.6, perhaps the same echo-word (the third letter is ପ or ଯ). The sense expected is "filled, overwhelmed, stirred".
[^kalpataru-30]: The figure of Mahaprabhu as a wish-fulfilling tree distributing the fruits of prema: *Chaitanya-charitamrita*, Adi 9. The verb is printed ସେବ (*seba*, "serves") without a vowel sign.
[^kashi-31]: Kashi Mishra is the brahmana and temple servant of Puri in whose house (the Gambhira) Mahaprabhu lived (*Chaitanya-charitamrita*, Madhya 10). After his conversion Sarvabhauma became one of the Lord's closest devotees in Puri.
[^prataparudra-32]: Prataparudra-deva (the Gajapati, reigned c. 1497–1540) was the king of Odisha and Mahaprabhu's patron in Puri; his meeting with the Lord and his receiving of mercy are described in the *Chaitanya-charitamrita*, Madhya 11–14.
[^gajare-33]: The second half-line is read with uncertainty: ମହିମ (*mahima*, "glory", with ହି of a special form), ଗଜରେ (*gajare*, "in the elephant"? perhaps transposed letters for ଜଗରେ, "in the world"), ଆତପାତ (see 1.29). The first word of the line is printed approximately ଜଗତ୍‌ନାଥ / ଜଗନ୍ନାଥ; "Jagannatha" either way.
[^dhauta-34]: The reading and syntax are unclear: ପରଶିରେ (*paraśire*, "by the touch"?), ଧଉତ (*dhaüta*, "washed, purified"?), ନୃପ… (*nṛpatraṇa?*), whose third sign is unidentified. Apparently the king was purified by touching Mahaprabhu's feet (cf. *Chaitanya-charitamrita*, Madhya 14: the Lord embraces the king during the Ratha-yatra).
[^gavaksha-35]: ଧୀଟ (*dhīṭa*) is taken as "firm, steadfast"; ଗବାକ୍ଷରେ (*gabākṣare*, "in the window") is read clearly, but its sense is unclear (the king looking out of a palace window?); ସଜ (*saja*, "adorned"?): the first letter is unclear.
[^dvija-36]: "That crest-jewel of kings" is Prataparudra, longing for the [Lord's] feet. In the second half-line Kanai begins to speak of himself: "I, the worthless son of a twice-born (dvija)"; the last two words (≈ ଭବିଁକ ପରଭତ) are unidentified. In the first half-line the last word is probably ଲୋଭ (*lobha*, "longing"), with the ା merged into ଲ.
[^niranjana-39]: The name of the author's father is Niranjana Khuntia (the first sign is ନି of a special form). The khuntias are a hereditary group of servants of the Jagannatha temple. The last words of the half-lines, ନତ (*nata*, "humble", "bowed"?) and ସାଥ (*sātha*, = *sārtha* "fruitful"?), are read with uncertainty.
[^harachandi-40]: Nila-shaila ("the Blue Mountain") is Nilachala, the Jagannatha temple in Puri. Harachandi is a goddess whose shrine is in Puri (the Harachandi Sahi quarter is named after her); verses 1.40–1.43 apparently describe the place where the author lived. The word ବାଳେଲ (*bāḷela*?) is unidentified, perhaps from *bāḷi* "sand" (the sandy shore). The first word of the line is printed with a ନୀ ligature resembling ମା (cf. 1.44 and the corrigenda).
[^shankha-41]: According to Puranic tradition Puri is Shankha-kshetra, a holy land shaped like a conch turned towards the ocean, with the Jagannatha temple at its "navel". The nine khandas are the nine divisions of the earth (Bharata and others).
[^devikshetra-42]: "The holy place of the Goddess" is apparently the shrine of Harachandi (1.40), standing on the edge of the "conch", by the sea. ଆଶ୍ରୟେ (*āśraẏe*, "in the shelter") and ରଞ୍ଜି (*rañji*, "delightfully"?) are read with uncertainty. ଧରିଅଛୁ / ଅଛୁ are archaic forms in ‑achu.
[^niladri-44]: The print has ନୀଳାଦ୍ରିଶ୍ୱର (*nīḷādriśwara*; the ା after ଳ is barely visible); the edition's corrigenda give ନୀଳାଦ୍ରୀଶ୍ୱର, "the Lord of Niladri" (Jagannatha). ଶିସ (*śisa*, "head, crest"?) is read with uncertainty; the second half-line (≈ ସେବାନେ ନିପୁନକ ଅନ୍ତି ନିରନ୍ତର) is understood only in part: "… constantly in service".
[^shachisuta-45]: Shachi's son is Sri Chaitanya. ଭନେ (*bhane*) is taken as *bhaṇe*, "says, announces" (the poet's usual formula); the first letter is unclear.
[^gundicha-46]: Gundicha-ghosha (Ghosha-yatra) is the Ratha-yatra: Jagannatha, Baladeva and Subhadra ride on chariots to the Gundicha temple. Its description with Mahaprabhu's participation: *Chaitanya-charitamrita*, Madhya 13–14. The line is printed as a single half-line: the second half of the couplet is missing in the edition. ବଇଠେ (*baïṭhe*, "being seated"?): the first letter is smudged; ଗୁଣ୍ଡିଚ is printed with a subscript sign (= ଗୁଣ୍ଡିଚା).
[^pahandi-47]: The print has ମହିଁ (*mahi̐*); the edition's corrigenda give ମୁହିଁ (*muhi̐*, "I"): the author says that he himself was there as a servant. The last word of the first half-line (≈ ଶ୍ରୀଙ୍କାମୁରେ) is unidentified. Pahandi (ପହଣ୍ଡି, here ପହୁଣ୍ଡି) is the ceremonial carrying of the Deities to the chariots; "Prabhu" here, judging by the context, is Jagannatha Himself. The *Chaitanya-charitamrita*, Madhya 13, calls this rite *pāṇḍu-vijaya*.
[^mangala-48]: The line describes the rites before the procession, but most of the words are unidentified: ମଙ୍ଗଳ ଆରୋପଣ (*maṅgaḷa āropaṇa*), "placing of auspicious [items]", apparently the mangalarpana; ହଡ଼ପ (*haṛapa*), the betel box?; ବଡ଼ି (*baṛi*), ଡୋସ (*ḍosa*), ଲଗିବଡ଼ି (*lagibaṛi*) are unclear. Only "golden garland" is clear.
[^daitapati-49]: The daitapatis (daitas) are hereditary servants of Jagannatha who carry the Deities to the chariots. The *Chaitanya-charitamrita*, Madhya 13, describes how the mighty daitas carry Jagannatha, swaying Him.
[^chhera-50]: Apparently the chhera-panhara rite is described: the Gajapati king sweeps the chariots and the road before Jagannatha with a gold-handled broom. In the *Chaitanya-charitamrita* (Madhya 13), Mahaprabhu, seeing Prataparudra sweep the road, was pleased with his humble service. ଝଡ଼ଣ (*jhaṛaṇa*, "broom"?, cf. ଝାଡ଼ୁ), ଉଣ୍ଡି (*uṇḍi*) and ତହପ (*tahapa*) are read with uncertainty or unidentified.
[^telangi-51]: The description of the Ratha-yatra continues (from 1.46): the pahandi, the ceremonial procession of the Deities to the chariots (see 1.47). Telangi baja (*telaṅgī bādya*, lit. "Telinga instruments") is an ensemble of drums and trumpets that plays in the Puri temple during processions. The first half-line is only partly read: ଆଗତୁରା (*āgaturā*, "ahead, in haste"? or "trumpets ahead"?), ନଡ଼ମ (*naṛama*) and ଛନଝିଣ (*chanajhiṇa*, onomatopoeia?) are not identified; "land and water" (ସ୍ଥଳ ଜଳ) is read with uncertainty.
[^sudarshana-52]: Sudarshana is Vishnu's disc, worshipped in Puri as the fourth Deity on the altar beside Jagannatha, Baladeva and Subhadra; in the pahandi he is brought to the chariots first. The word before "began" (≈ ଯାହା, first letter smudged) and ଆରମ୍ଭିଲ (*ārambhila*, printed ≈ ଆଲମ୍ଭିଲ) are read with uncertainty; ବିଜେ ହେଲ (*bije hela*, "came forth") follows the sense.
[^bada-53]: "Sri Bada" is apparently Baladeva (in Puri He is called Bada-thakura, "the Elder Lord"); the next word (ବାଡ଼) is not identified. Kanai calls Him an incarnation of Shesha. According to Gaudiya Vaishnava teaching (*Chaitanya-charitamrita*, Adi 5), Balarama is the source of Sankarshana and Shesha, not their incarnation; Shesha is Balarama's expansion. The second half-line (କହୁ ମାତର) is understood tentatively: "it cannot be told".
[^ananta-54]: Printed ଅନ୍ତ (*anta*, "end"); by the sense probably ଅନନ୍ତ (Ananta = Shesha), who serves the Lord together with Brahma. The daitapatis carry the Deities to the chariots (see 1.49).
[^subhadra-55]: "The primordial Shakti" (*ādyā-śakti*) reflects the Odia worship of Subhadra; for Gaudiya Vaishnavas Subhadra is Krishna's sister. The word ଚାପରେ (*cāpare*) is read with uncertainty: perhaps "on a chapa" — a litter or boat-shaped platform for the Deity (?). In the second half-line the author, a khuntia, says that he himself served in this procession; ମଧ୍ୟେ (*madhye*, "among") is read with uncertainty.
[^vamana-56]: "Mahaprabhu" here, in Odia usage, is Jagannatha Himself (Sri Chaitanya in these lines is "Gauranga", "Sri Prabhu"). The form of Vamana, the dwarf avatara of Vishnu, is a traditional name for Jagannatha on the chariot; a well-known verse says: "One who sees Vamana on the chariot is not born again."
[^niyoga-57]: Chhatisa niyoga ("the thirty-six niyogas") is the traditional organisation of the hereditary servants of the Jagannatha temple, divided into groups by type of service. The first letter (ଛ) is read by the shape of the sign.
[^mukunda-58]: Kashi Mishra — see 1.31. Mukunda is apparently Mukunda Datta, the singer and companion of Mahaprabhu; according to the *Chaitanya-charitamrita* (Madhya 13), at the Ratha-yatra Mahaprabhu divided the devotees into seven kirtana groups, and Mukunda led the singing in one of them. The ି of ମୁକୁନ୍ଦାଦି is not visible.
[^karana-59]: The karanas are the Odia caste of scribes and officials. "Eighteen karanas" are apparently royal officers; nothing more is known of them from this line. The syntax of the second half-line is unclear; ଅଛନ୍ତି (*achanti*, "there are") is read with uncertainty.
[^harichandana-60]: Lakshmidhara here is a royal minister (*sacība*); whether he is the Lakshmidhara known as a pandit of Puri is unclear. Harichandana is an attendant of Prataparudra; he is also mentioned in the *Chaitanya-charitamrita* (Madhya 13) in the episode with Shrivasa (see 1.63–1.64). ଛତ୍ରଙ୍କ (*chatraṅka*, "by the umbrellas"?) is read with uncertainty; in procession the king is accompanied by umbrellas and fans.
[^yodabedha-61]: "The crest-jewel of kings" is Prataparudra (see 1.32, 1.50). The couplet is almost unread: ଷଇଠ (*ṣaïṭha*), ଘେନଣ (*ghenaṇa*) and ମୂର୍ଚ୍ଛି (*mūrcchi*, "swooning"? or ମୂର୍ତ୍ତି, "the Deity"?) are not identified. Printed ଯାଡ଼ାବେଢ (*yāṛābeḍha*); the edition's list of errata corrects it to ଯୋଡ଼ାବେଢ (*yoṛābeḍha*); the meaning is not established (*yoṛā* "pair", *beṛha* "circuit, enclosure"?). The word is preceded by an opening quotation mark with no closing one; we do not reproduce it.
[^mahima-62]: "The procession of glory" (*mahimā bijaẏa*) is in quotation marks in the edition: apparently a cry or the name of the ceremonial progress. ଦରଷେ (*daraṣe*; perhaps ହରଷେ, "joyfully"?) and the last word of the line (≈ କରେଷିଲ) are not identified; "broke into dance" follows the sense. Mahaprabhu's dance before Jagannatha's chariot is described in the *Chaitanya-charitamrita*, Madhya 13.
[^shrinivasa-63]: Shrinivasa is Shrivasa (Shrivasa Thakura), see 1.26. The word after "Prabhu" (≈ ସ୍ୱବେଚ) is not identified; one expects "at the sight (?)", "in the mood (?)".
[^barana-64]: Cf. *Chaitanya-charitamrita*, Madhya 13: Shrivasa, absorbed in watching the Lord's dance, stood in front of Prataparudra and blocked his view; Harichandana touched him to make him move aside, and Shrivasa angrily slapped him; the king restrained Harichandana, saying that he had been blessed by a devotee's touch. On this page Kanai says only that Harichandana pushed Shrivasa aside.
[^bhara-65]: The subject is apparently Harichandana, who did not understand the greatness of Mahaprabhu's dance. ଭାବାବେଶ (*bhābābeśa*) is read by comparison with the same word in 1.75; ନୋହି (*nohi*, "not") and the last word (≈ ଭାର; first letter ଭ, ବ or ସ) are read with uncertainty.
[^janama-66]: From this couplet Kanai speaks of himself as an eyewitness. Printed ଜଧମ (*jadhama*) — probably a misprint for ଜନମ (*janama*, "birth"): "birth after birth". The word ≈ କ୍ଷିପ୍ତା (*kṣiptā*) is not identified.
[^jyoti-67]: Light "in the middle of the eyes" is a figure of contemplation close to the language of the Odia Panchasakha poets (contemplation of light, of the Shunya). According to Gaudiya Vaishnava teaching, the effulgence of Brahman is only the radiance of Krishna's body (*Chaitanya-charitamrita*, Adi 2), and the highest is to see the Lord Himself in His form; in the next line Kanai indeed speaks of the radiance in Gaura's face. ତ୍ରୈଲୋକ୍ୟଈଶ୍ୱରେ (*trailokya-īśware*) is a tentative reading; ନାମୁ (*nāmu*) is not identified.
[^mahabhava-68]: Mahabhava is the highest stage of prema, which gives the poem its name. According to Gaudiya Vaishnava teaching, mahabhava belongs to Sri Radha, and Sri Chaitanya, having accepted Radha's mood, displayed it (*Chaitanya-charitamrita*, Adi 4). ଆଭାସ (*ābhāsa*, "glimmer") is read with uncertainty.
[^dasanudasa-70]: "Servant of servants" (*dāsānudāsa*) is the traditional self-designation of a Vaishnava (cf. Sri Chaitanya's words, "I am the servant of the servants of the servants of the Lord of the gopis", *Padyavali*, quoted in the *Chaitanya-charitamrita*, Madhya 13).
[^uddanda-71]: The sattvika transformations (*sāttvika vikāra*) are the eight bodily signs of ecstasy: stupor, perspiration, hair standing on end, faltering voice, trembling, change of colour, tears and fainting. Uddanda-nritya is a wild dance with high leaps; so Mahaprabhu danced before the chariot (*Chaitanya-charitamrita*, Madhya 13). ନ୍ୟାସ (*nyāsa*, "setting aside"?) is read with uncertainty; ଭକତ ବଦନ is lit. "the faces of the devotees".
[^salila-72]: The couplet is printed on two lines: the right half-line stands on a separate indented line. After the word ସଲଳ (*salaḷa*) the edition places a note sign and, in parentheses, the reading ସଲିଳଜ (*saliḷaja*, "water-born", i.e. a lotus); the translation follows this reading, "like the stalk of" being added for sense (the figure of a thorny lotus stalk for hair standing on end). ପ୍ରେମଗ୍ରନ୍ଥି (*prema-granthi*, "knot of prema"), ତାର (*tāra*; ତ or ଚ?) and ଚମକଣ (*camakaṇa*, "trembling"?) are read with uncertainty. Pulaka is the hair standing on end in ecstasy.
[^baha-73]: At the end of the line the edition prints ବାହ… (a lacuna) and, in parentheses, ବାହା — a completion ("flowed"). ଛିଆ (*chiā*, "standing") and ବୃନ୍ଦେ ଦେହୁ (*bṛnde dehu*, "in profusion — from the body") are read with uncertainty.
[^chula-75]: The second half-line is not identified: ≈ ମଳ ଭରମାନ ଚୂଳ (*maḷa bharamāna cūḷa*) — "the dust-covered (?) hair (?)"; it is also unclear whom it concerns — the author or Mahaprabhu (cf. 1.76).
[^half-76]: The line is printed as the right half-line only: the first half of the couplet is missing in the edition. ଲୁଟିଲେ (*luṭile*, "rolled", hon.) and ପ୍ରାଣଅଙ୍ଗ (*prāṇa-aṅga*) are read with uncertainty.
[^phena-77]: The *Chaitanya-charitamrita* also speaks of foam on Mahaprabhu's lips in ecstasy (Madhya 13, the signs during the dance before the chariot). The first word of the second half-line (≈ ଧାମାଦ୍ୱାରେ) is not identified — "from the mouth" is expected; the last (≈ ଫେନସ୍ରାବ, "flow of foam") is read by sense and by the rhyme with ସର୍ବ.
[^ashtanga-78]: The word before "fell prostrate" (≈ ସଷ୍ଟାକେ) is not identified; perhaps a corrupt ସାଷ୍ଟାଙ୍ଗେ (*sāṣṭāṅge*, "prostrate with the whole body").
