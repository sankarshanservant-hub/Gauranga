# Kanai Khuntia. *Mahabhava-prakasha*, Chapter 2
## Ramananda Raya's family; Ramananda leaves royal service and comes to Sri Chaitanya; Prataparudra at Kashi Mishra's house

*Translated from Odia without a native speaker; doubtful places are marked (?) and (??). The original, transliteration and word-for-word translation are in `full/02.md`.*

*The second vritta (?) [section]*[^vritta-1]

**2.1.** In this book, the *Mahabhava-prakasha*, is the essence that came forth from the lotus mouth of Sri Gauranga.  
**2.2.** How can we, worthless ones, describe it? Know the words of Prabhu's holy mouth to be a firm secret.  
**2.3.** The best [place] of the Panchakroshi of Purushottama-kshetra — its name is Bentapur, most charming.[^bentapur-3]  
**2.4.** In that village dwell the foremost (?) Karanas (?); all of them serve with the king as panjias.[^karana-4]  
**2.5.** In their family there was one named Bhavananda; he, a Pattanayaka, versed in the shastras (?), held (?) to dharma.[^bhavananda-5]  
**2.6.** His three sons are archers (?); because they served the king, … (??) skilful.[^sons-6]  
**2.7.** Versed in all the shastras, they became intimate (?) with Prataparudra.[^atayata-7]  
**2.8.** The eldest son, Dharanidhara, is a great yogi; he kept all the affairs of the village … (??).[^dharanidhara-8]  
**2.9.** His brother, Gopinatha Pattanayaka, served wonderfully in the science of government.[^gopinatha-9]  
**2.10.** Know likewise his youngest (?) brother: his name is Ramananda Pattanayaka.[^sabusana-10]  
**2.11.** He, gathering a retinue, took leave (?) and came to govern Vidyanagara.[^vidyanagara-11]  
**2.12.** Thus he made arrangements (??) with the king, and thus with dignity he managed a part of the kingdom.[^bhuana-12]  
**2.13.** Ramananda obtained the rank of Raya; he is the king's dearest friend.[^raya-13]  
**2.14.** His mind is always immersed in the rasa of Krishna; his mind is always attached to Krishna's feet.  
**2.15.** Thus he became mad with the rasa of Krishna and could not manage the affairs (?) of the kingdom.[^gahana-15]  
**2.16.** To a brahmana named Bira (??) from the village of Rajyamandri he entrusted his domain.[^rajyamandri-16]  
**2.17.** Taking the brahmana Rajiva from the fort of Khallikot, there, on the Godavari (?), he made him his friend (?).[^khallikot-17]  
**2.18.** In that place Rajiva kept him company (?); he gave words [of instruction] (?) to the pilgrims.[^tirthala-18]  
**2.19.** Thus Sri Ramananda, intoxicated with the rasa of Krishna, utterly forgot the king's affairs.  
**2.20.** Such a best of devotees could not stay [there]: he heard of Gauranga's Radha-rasa in Sri Kshetra.[^shrikshetra-20]  
**2.21.** He ceaselessly chanted the name "Radha, Radha", and handed over (?) [his charge] to King Prataparudra.[^samujhai-21]  
**2.22.** He left the king's company and joined Chaitanya, immersing his mind in the feet of Guru-Govinda.[^gurugovinda-22]  
**2.23.** King Prataparudra, trusting in him, dwells (?) with him, having come to the eternal abode (?).[^nityadhama-23]  
**2.24.** They entered Kashi Mishra's house; the king came and prostrated himself before Prabhu.[^kashimishra-24]  
**2.25.** He said: "We shall hear the command from the holy (??) mouth" …[^adesha-25]  
**2.26.** At the root of an amalaki (?) tree Chaitanya Thakura, seated, at once (?) manifested bhava.[^aunla-26]  
**2.27.** "Rise, rise, O king!" [He] cried out; Ramananda Raya lifted the king and held him up.[^uttha-27]  
**2.28.** All the devotees became one-pointed, all fixing their meditation on the feet of Chaitanya Thakura.  

## Ramananda Raya asks about the tattva of Radha; Mahaprabhu explains the hidden tattva: bijas, sakhis, fire and water; the Goloka story begins


**2.29.** In their souls the maha-mantra of Krishna, in their mouths the great name; with the corners of their eyes [they] behold the feet of Sri Prabhu.  
**2.30.** Thus some time passed in silence; Raya Ramananda, together (?) with the king (?), came before [Prabhu].[^nripati-30]  
**2.31.** Joining his hands, raising high (?) … (??), he said: "Please explain [to us] the light of the universe.[^jyoti-31]  
**2.32.** That Radha who is … (??) of Vrishabhanu, the beloved (?) of Krishna, who is the great shakti flooding (?) the world —[^vrishabhanu-32]  
**2.33.** please explain to us (??) the tattva of that Radha. Hearing this mahabhava, may [our] lives be blessed."[^amapu-33]  
**2.34.** Hearing this, Prabhu smiled; Prabhu cried aloud [about] the tattva (?) of Radha and Krishna.[^tanta-34]  
**2.35.** He uttered the names "Krishna, Krishna, Radha, Radha" and, half-closing His eyes, revealed the great tattva:  
**2.36.** "Listen (?), O devotees, to what you have asked: you have asked about the deepest secret of all the Vaishnava shastras.[^shunasi-36]  
**2.37.** Know, then: this is a hidden tattva; know, O devotees, this is the unmanifest lila.[^aprakata-37]  
**2.38.** The fifty (?) letters, which are light — in the same way (?) they, comprising the tattvas of all the mantras, are of infinite measure.[^varna-38]  
**2.39.** Among them are the bijas of all the gods; 'ram', O servant (?), is the bija of Radhika.[^bija-39]  
**2.40.** The letter 'ra' is the embodiment of the great radiance; the letter 'ra', know, is the supreme … …[^lacuna-40]  
**2.41.** Radhika is the form of the radiance of fire; by the bija 'ram' prema … becoming … (??) to Krishna.[^taka-41]  
**2.42.** In the essential structure (?) of the body all abide; by the eight letters the eight sakhis surround [Her].[^sakhi-42]  
**2.43.** The bija 'lam' (?) is fire by nature, its place is in the heart; know: [She] is the ruler of the heart's … (??), the form of prema.[^lam-43]  
**2.44.** She is the ruler of the pingala channel; She, full of prema, showers the nectar of prema.[^pingala-44]  
**2.45.** Lalita, Vishakha, Chandralekha and the other sakhis — see — have surrounded the bijas … (??).[^lalita-45]  
**2.46.** … (??) — Radha is like fire; know: She is not born of a womb.[^ayoni-46]  
**2.47.** Radhika is fire, and the Yamuna is water; She is the heat of fire, ever longing.[^yamuna-47]  
**2.48.** Ever restless with the radiance of fire, [They] keep sporting in the waters of the Yamuna.[^krida-48]  
**2.49.** The secret of the tattva of kama they know without knowing; the tattvas of play they contemplate in the mind.[^kama-49]  
**2.50.** She abides in all places; [She] whose treasure (?) is the jewel (?) abiding in the circle of the Void.[^shunya-50]  
**2.51.** As a lotus leaf, though in water, [remains] to the water … (?): [the drops] roll about, … (??) does not stick … (??);[^padmapatra-51]  
**2.52.** as pure gold melts in fire, [while] a jewel remains [in water] — and even deep within (?) the water does not pass into it;[^manika-52]  
**2.53.** as the bee kisses the madhavi flower — having kissed the flower, the bee flies off [taking (?)] the honey —[^madhavi-53]  
**2.54.** so too Radha's body has no touch: in union with the radiance of 'ram' Krishna joyfully sports.[^sparsha-54]  
**2.55.** Such is Prabhu's account. …[^half-55]  
**2.56.** In Goloka Sri Radha became angry on account of Sudama; the son of Nanda Himself … [^sudama-56]  

## Radha's anger and Kishora's distress; Radha as Yogamaya and Bhavamayi; the secret of bhava; the search for a guru and the meeting with Keshava Bharati


**2.57.** Seeing the anger of the supreme Shakti, Kishora, the all-auspicious, fell into great distress.[^kishora-57]  
**2.58.** They saw the supreme light — Prabhu, the primeval root; all the devotees, in remembrance (?), say:[^surata-58]  
**2.59.** "Since Radha is Yogamaya in the form of a woman, therefore She wears yellow garments.[^yogamaya-59]  
**2.60.** Among proud beauties She is the proud one; among those who walk like an intoxicated [elephant] (?), She is called the one of the finest gait.[^manini-60]  
**2.61.** Among beautiful women She is the most beautiful of all, enchanting Krishna with Her beauty, having assumed a form of radiance.  
**2.62.** Bodiless, She eternally enjoys in a body (?); with the youth of Vraja She sports.[^sharira-62]  
**2.63.** Sometimes in a body (?) She bears a body; and again, becoming invisible, She sports with Krishna.  
**2.64.** Radha is a beauty [made] of bhava, the mine of bhava; therefore Her name is Bhavamayi.  
**2.65.** The bhava of the heart, or … (??) — so all call Her; know: this is Radha; hence She bears a secret name.[^keli-65]  
**2.66.** Whoever serves Krishna absorbed in bhava gains [a place] within Radha's body.[^radhanga-66]  
**2.67.** When, immersed (?) in bhava, one worships Krishna, that is concentration in the mood of Radha's bhava.[^majana-67]  
**2.68.** This bhava is secret; this is an unmanifest bhava; these matters will never become manifest.  
**2.69.** Know these to be secret, esoteric tattvas …"[^half-69]  
**2.70.** We have expounded the words of our guru; we ourselves have spoken the supreme knowledge.[^speaker-70]  
**2.71.** Know this to be the supreme secret teaching; here is the proof: we heard this tattva from the guru's mouth.  
**2.72.** So we shall tell you what is secret, and remove all doubts from your hearts.  
**2.73.** When we came to the Kshetra, [our] body fell in supreme absorption in Krishna.[^kshetra-73]  
**2.74.** Without a guru this body fell into distress; without a guru the body became futile.  
**2.75.** Without the guru's grace this (?) soul is futile; when there is the guru's grace, the soul gains its purpose (?).[^ehu-75]  
**2.76.** Not having obtained such grace (??) of a guru, I grieved; [then] I met a certain Vaishnava on the bank of a lake.[^kripa-76]  
**2.77.** His name was Keshava Bharati, a supreme Vaishnava; his bhava ever abides in the rasa of Hari.[^keshava-77]  
**2.78.** Seeing my despair, he could not bear it; "We are not worthy (?)," he let me know.[^nuhu-78]  
**2.79.** I fell, firmly clasping his feet, and said: "Liberate my soul!"  
**2.80.** Thus, seeing me, he became distressed; he lifted me from the ground and seated me (?).[^asana-80]  
**2.81.** Embracing me, he then spoke with sweet speech: …[^half-81]  
**2.82.** He said: "We indeed know your story; we know: your body is Krishna's secret.[^bheda-82]  
**2.83.** We know your mind is always turned to Krishna; thinking (?) of this, we are greatly happy at heart.[^sada-83]  
**2.84.** Supreme service to Krishna is very … …[^lacuna-84]  
**2.85.** We know only how to receive the guru's grace; I have no command from my guru to give [it] to you."[^grahana-85]  

## Keshava Bharati on Madhavendra Puri; the search for the sadguru among the dunes; initiation; "bhava is the form of Radha"


**2.86.** Our guru is Madhavendra Puri; of all the shastras … …[^madhavendra-86]

**2.88.** Bathing in the water of the tears from his eyes, he makes the dust of the devotees' feet his tilaka.  
**2.89.** He is ever immersed in the service (?) of Vishnu (?), the Kaivalya; in bhava he obtains the darshan of the Supreme Krishna.[^kaivalya-89]

**2.91.** My ashrama is beside Gupta-Vrindavana, near the [Jagannatha-]vallabha, to the east [of it].[^vallabha-91]

**2.104.** Thus we asked about the tattva of Radha; we asked about the tattva of the Divine Couple, Radha and Krishna.  
**2.105.** The guru's grace came, great merit became mine: [I,] the lowly one, heard words from the guru's holy mouth.  
**2.106.** My guru, … (??), the all-auspicious, said: "We shall give you initiation (diksha)."[^sadajnama-106]  
**2.107.** Thus the supreme guru rejoiced; giving [me] the great mantras, he gave [me] satisfaction.  
**2.108.** He is my diksha-guru, the trust of the world; the mantra of Radha [and] the tattva of Radha [he gave me] into the ear (?), in secret.[^diksha-108]  
**2.109.** Such secret words he spoke; he declared plainly: "Bhava is the form of Radha.[^bhava-109]  
**2.110.** The manifestation of bhava is the sight of Radha; absorption in bhava is the union of the Couple."  
**2.111.** "What is bhava?" I asked the guru; "I [wish to] hear the secret tattvas," I said.[^kisa-111]  
**2.112.** My guru, firm (?) in absorption in bhava, said: "We shall bring [you] to Radha (lit. 'let [you] meet in Radha's company')."[^tana-112]

[^vritta-1]: The chapter heading is printed ଦ୍ୱିତୀୟ ବୃନ୍ତ (*dwitīẏa bṛnta*). As in the colophon at the end of Chapter 1 (after 1.158), the word ବୃନ୍ତ (literally "stalk") is understood as "vritta, section" (perhaps for ବୃତ୍ତ, *bṛtta*). Chapter 2 begins with this heading, and the numbering of the couplets starts again from 1.
[^bentapur-3]: Purushottama-kshetra is Puri. The Panchakroshi is the sacred district of five kroshas around the holy place. Bentapur (ବେଣ୍ଟପୁର, *beṇṭapura*) is a village near Puri; Kanai places Ramananda Raya's family here (2.4–2.10).
[^karana-4]: Karana (*karaṇa*) is the Odia caste of scribes and officials; *karaṇam* (in southern Odisha and among the Telugu) is a village accountant-scribe. Panjias (*pāñjiā*) are keepers of records (*pāñji*, "register, chronicle"). Printed ≈ କରଣମ ନେ (*karaṇama ne*) with a space; apparently the sign ା failed to print, and the reading should be କରଣମାନେ (*karaṇamāne*, "the Karanas", plural: cf. "in their family" in 2.5). The word ଚରମ (*carama*, "foremost"? "last"?) is read with doubt.
[^bhavananda-5]: In the *Chaitanya-charitamrita* Bhavananda is Ramananda Raya's father, Bhavananda Raya (Madhya 10; Antya 9); here he is given the title Pattanayaka. Printed ≈ ଶାତ୍ରୀ (*śātrī*), understood as ଶାସ୍ତ୍ରୀ (*śāstrī*, "versed in the shastras"); in ଘେନିଣ (*gheniṇa*, "holding, having taken") the ି is unclear.
[^sons-6]: In Kanai, Bhavananda has three sons: Dharanidhara, Gopinatha and Ramananda (2.8–2.10). In the *Chaitanya-charitamrita* (Madhya 10) Bhavananda Raya presents five sons to Mahaprabhu: Ramananda Raya, Gopinatha Pattanayaka, Kalanidhi, Sudhanidhi and Vaninatha. The word ଅଟେ (*aṭe*, "are") is faintly printed; ଅଭାବ (*abhāba*; "need"?) is not identified, and the sense of the second half-line is unclear.
[^atayata-7]: The word ଆତଯାତ (*ātayāta*) is understood as "coming and going", i.e. intimate, having access [to the king]; the meaning is uncertain. Whether the couplet refers to all the sons or to one of them is unclear.
[^dharanidhara-8]: Dharanidhara is not mentioned in the *Chaitanya-charitamrita*. The last word (≈ ବହଗି, *bahagi*) is not identified; "affairs" (*bheda*, also "secrets") of the village is understood from the context.
[^gopinatha-9]: Gopinatha Pattanayaka is Ramananda Raya's brother; the *Chaitanya-charitamrita* (Antya 9) tells how, when he was in debt to the treasury, Prataparudra pardoned him by Mahaprabhu's mercy. The word ଚମତ୍କ (*camatka*) is understood as ଚମତ୍କାର (*camatkāra*, "wonderfully"), shortened for the rhyme.
[^sabusana-10]: Printed ≈ ସବୁସାନ (*sabusāna*, "youngest of all"); the reading is uncertain. In the *Chaitanya-charitamrita* (Madhya 10) Ramananda is named first among Bhavananda's sons. Pattanayaka is an Odia title (see 2.5, 2.9).
[^vidyanagara-11]: Vidyanagara is a town on the Godavari; cf. *Chaitanya-charitamrita*, Madhya 8: there Ramananda Raya, governing on behalf of Prataparudra, met Mahaprabhu during His journey through South India. The first half-line is understood with doubt: ଗହଣ (*gahaṇa*, "retinue, company") and ମେଲଣ (*melaṇa*; = ମେଲାଣି, "leave-taking"?).
[^bhuana-12]: The word ≈ ଭୁଆଣ (*bhuāṇa*) is not identified; perhaps ଭିଆଣ (*bhiāṇa*, "arrangement, agreement").
[^raya-13]: Raya (*rāẏa*) is a title; Kanai explains that Ramananda received it as a rank. Cf. *Chaitanya-charitamrita*, Madhya 11 and 14: Ramananda Raya is Prataparudra's trusted man.
[^gahana-15]: ଗହଣ (*gahaṇa*) literally means "company, retinue"; here and in 2.19, 2.22 it is understood as "affairs, service" (at the king's court).
[^rajyamandri-16]: Rajyamandri (ରାଜ୍ୟମାନ୍ଦ୍ରୀ, *rājyamāndrī*) is apparently Rajamahendri (Rajahmundry) on the Godavari. The editor's note mark at this word is not carried into the translation. The brahmana's name (≈ ବିର, *bira*) is read with doubt.
[^khallikot-17]: Khallikot is a fort (*gaṛa*) in southern Odisha. Rajiva is not mentioned in the *Chaitanya-charitamrita*. ଗୋଦାବରିରେ (*godābarire*, "on the Godavari"), ବନ୍ଧୁ (*bandhu*, "friend"; perhaps ବନ୍ଧ, *bandha*, "dam, embankment") and ସେହି (*sehi*) are read with doubt; who made whom a friend is unclear.
[^tirthala-18]: Who "gave words to the pilgrims" — Rajiva or Ramananda — is unclear; *bākyamāna* literally means "words".
[^shrikshetra-20]: In Kanai, Ramananda leaves his post on hearing in Vidyanagara of Gauranga in Sri Kshetra (Puri). The *Chaitanya-charitamrita* tells it differently: Mahaprabhu Himself met Ramananda on the bank of the Godavari during His South Indian journey and asked him to leave his post and come to Nilachala (Madhya 8); Ramananda returned to Puri with the king's permission (Madhya 11). "Radha-rasa": according to Gaudiya Vaishnava teaching, Sri Chaitanya is Krishna Himself, having accepted the mood and complexion of Radha (*Chaitanya-charitamrita*, Adi 4).
[^samujhai-21]: Printed ≈ ସମୁଝାଇ (*samujhāi*; ମ with a subscript sign), understood as "handed over [his charge]"; the reading is uncertain.
[^gurugovinda-22]: "Guru-Govinda" is apparently Sri Chaitanya as guru and Govinda (Krishna); "the feet of the guru [and] Govinda" is also possible. According to Gaudiya Vaishnava teaching, Sri Chaitanya is Krishna (Govinda) Himself.
[^nityadhama-23]: "The eternal abode" (*nityadhāma*) is apparently Puri (Nilachala). The ି on ନ is unclear. In whom the king trusts and with whom he "dwells" (*bihare*) — Ramananda or Chaitanya — is unclear.
[^kashimishra-24]: Kashi Mishra's house is Mahaprabhu's residence in Puri (*Chaitanya-charitamrita*, Madhya 10). According to the *Chaitanya-charitamrita*, Mahaprabhu for a long time refused to see the king; Prataparudra received His embrace in a garden during the Ratha-yatra (Madhya 11–14). In Kanai the king comes to Prabhu in Kashi Mishra's house and prostrates himself before Him.
[^adesha-25]: Only the first half-line is printed (the line ends with a single bar); the second half of the couplet is missing in the edition. The second word is printed unclearly (≈ ଦ୍ରେ); it is read as ଶ୍ରୀ (*śrī*, "holy [mouth]"). Who speaks — the king or Ramananda — is not stated.
[^aunla-26]: Printed ≈ ଅଏଁଳା (*ae̐ḷā*), understood as ଅଁଳା (*a̐ḷā*, the amalaki, "Indian gooseberry" tree). The last word is printed ≈ ତପୂର, understood as ତତ୍ପର (*tatpara*, "at once, eagerly").
[^uttha-27]: Cf. *Chaitanya-charitamrita*, Madhya 14: Mahaprabhu embraces Prataparudra in the garden when the king, dressed as a simple Vaishnava, massages His feet.
[^nripati-30]: "The king" is apparently Prataparudra, who has come to Prabhu together with Ramananda (2.24–2.27). The first letter of ନୃପତି (*nṛpati*, "king") is printed unclearly; ଏକ (*eka*, "one") is taken as "together". The word ନୀରବେ (*nīrabe*, "in silence") is set with a ନୀ ligature resembling ମା (likewise ନୀରର in 2.48).
[^jyoti-31]: The first half-line is read only in part: ଉଚ୍ଚେ (*ucce*, "high"; an unclear mark above it) and a word ≈ ବେଦଙ୍କର (*bedaṅkara*, "of the Vedas"?; the third letter not identified) with ମାନ (*māna*, "honour"? "measure"?) — the connection is unclear. "The light of the universe" (*brahmāṇḍa jyoti*) — cf. 1.67 and its note: in Gaudiya Vaishnava teaching the effulgence of Brahman is only the light of Krishna's body (*Chaitanya-charitamrita*, Adi 2); Ramananda's question at once turns to the tattva of Radha (2.32–2.33).
[^vrishabhanu-32]: Vrishabhanu is the father of Sri Radha. After the name a second ≈ ଭାନୁ (*bhānu*, "sun") is printed; a word for "daughter" (cf. ସୁତା, *sutā*) would be expected; the reading is unclear. ଭାବନୀ (*bhābanī*) is taken as ଭାବିନୀ (*bhābinī*, "beloved"); the last word ≈ ପ୍ଲାବନୀ (*plābanī*, "flooding [with prema]") — a subscript sign is visible under ପ; perhaps ପାବନୀ (*pābanī*, "purifying"). In Gaudiya Vaishnava teaching Radha is the hladini-shakti, Krishna's internal energy of bliss (*Chaitanya-charitamrita*, Adi 4).
[^amapu-33]: Printed ≈ ଆମାପୁ (*āmāpu*); taken as "to us" (ଆମାକୁ / ଆମ୍ଭକୁ, *āmāku / āmbhaku*, would be expected). The reading is unclear.
[^tanta-34]: Printed ≈ ତନ୍ତ (*tanta*) without ୍ୱ; taken as ତତ୍ତ୍ୱ (*tattwa*, "tattva"), cf. 2.33, 2.35; perhaps ତନ୍ତ୍ର (*tantra*). The word ଶତ୍କାର (*śatkāra*) is corrected to ଚିତ୍କାର (*citkāra*, "a loud cry") per the edition's corrigenda (entry "୮।୭": on p. 8 the word stands in line 6 — the line number in the list is off by one; cf. the same correction in 1.124).
[^shunasi-36]: ଶୁଣସି (*śuṇasi*) is apparently an archaic form "listen" (or "you hear"); the reading is uncertain. Printed ନଗୂଢ଼ (*nagūṛha*) without ି; taken as ନିଗୂଢ଼ (*nigūṛha*, "the hidden secret").
[^aprakata-37]: The unmanifest (*aprakaṭa*) lila is the Lord's eternal pastimes in His abode, invisible in this world; the Gaudiya Vaishnavas also distinguish manifest (*prakaṭa*) and unmanifest lila.
[^varna-38]: "The fifty letters" are apparently the fifty sounds of the Sanskrit alphabet (*mātṛkā*) of which, in tantric teaching, all mantras consist. The first word (≈ ପଞ୍ଚାଶ, *pañcāśa*) is read with uncertainty (the first letter and the last — ଶ or ଣ — are unclear); ପରକାରେ (*parakāre*) is taken as ପ୍ରକାରେ (*prakāre*, "in [this] way").
[^bija-39]: A bija is the "seed" syllable of a mantra; 'ram' is the bija of fire in tantric tradition. From here (2.39–2.54) Kanai sets out the tattva of Radha in the language of the Odia yoga of the body (close to the Panchasakha poets): Radha is identified with the letter 'ra', with fire and radiance (*tejas*), the sakhis with letters, Her place is in the heart and in the pingala channel. The translation renders the text exactly. In Gaudiya Vaishnava teaching (after Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati) Radha is an eternal person, Krishna's hladini-shakti, Her body is spiritual (sat-cit-ananda), and the sakhis are Her expansions (*Chaitanya-charitamrita*, Adi 4); Radha is not identified with syllables, elements or channels of the yogic body. The word ଦାସ (*dāsa*, "servant") is taken as an address; it may belong to something else.
[^lacuna-40]: Here and in 2.41, 2.56 the edition prints dots — apparently a gap in the manuscript; the translation gives "…".
[^taka-41]: Within the second half-line are the edition's dots (a gap); the last word (≈ ଟକା, *ṭakā*) is not identified. The sense of the half-line is unclear.
[^sakhi-42]: The eight sakhis (*aṣṭa-sakhī*) are Radha's principal friends (cf. 2.45), here linked to eight letters. The word ≈ ବନ୍ଧରେ (*bandhare*, "in the structure, in the bond"?) is read with uncertainty.
[^lam-43]: The first word is printed ≈ ଲଂ (*laṁ*); the letter looks like ଲ, not ର, although the sense (fire, cf. 2.39, 2.41, 2.54) would lead one to expect ରଂ (*raṁ*). The word ≈ ହୃଦପୃ (*hṛdapṛ*) is not identified — perhaps a truncated ହୃଦପୃଷ୍ଠ (*hṛdapṛṣṭha*, cf. 2.46). ଅଧିକାରଣୀ (*adhikāraṇī*) = ଅଧିକାରିଣୀ (*adhikāriṇī*, "ruler, mistress").
[^pingala-44]: Pingala is, in hatha-yoga teaching, the right, "solar" (fiery) channel of the subtle body; hence the link with fire in 2.41–2.47. On the Gaudiya Vaishnava view see the note to 2.39.
[^lalita-45]: Lalita, Vishakha, Chandralekha are Radha's sakhis. The names are printed without ି: ଲଳତା, ବଶାଖା; the corrections ଲଳିତା, ବିଶାଖା are given. The word ≈ ଲବତ (*labata*; perhaps a list of letters "la, ba, ta") is not identified.
[^ayoni-46]: The first word (≈ ହୃଦପୃଷ୍ଠଶୁଷ, *hṛdapṛṣṭhaśuṣa*; "drying the surface of the heart"?) is not identified. "Not born of a womb" (*ayonisambhūtā*) is how Kanai speaks of Radha's appearance; the Gaudiya Vaishnavas likewise hold Radha's appearance in Vrishabhanu's house to be transcendental, not an ordinary birth.
[^yamuna-47]: The figure: Radha is fire, the Yamuna water; "the heat of fire" apparently means the longing of separation and prema.
[^krida-48]: The subject is not named; taken as Radha and Krishna (cf. 2.54). ନୀରର (*nīrara*) is "of the waters" (gen.), taken as "in the waters". The verb form କରୁଣଥାଅନ୍ତି (*karuṇathāanti*) is taken as a continuous "keep doing".
[^kama-49]: "Kama" here is the love of Radha and Krishna. In Gaudiya Vaishnava teaching the love of the gopis is only called kama: kama is the desire to please oneself, prema the desire to please Krishna (*Chaitanya-charitamrita*, Adi 4). The subject ("they", *sehu*) is not named — perhaps the sakhis; the sense of "know without knowing" is unclear.
[^shunya-50]: Shunya ("the Void") is, among the Odia Panchasakha poets, a name of the Supreme; "the circle of the Void" is the highest point of yogic contemplation; cf. *Vaishnava-lilamrita* 1.13. In Gaudiya Vaishnava teaching (after Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati) the Absolute is the personal Krishna, not an impersonal Void. Printed ≈ ଥ ନ୍ତି; per the edition's corrigenda ("୮।୨୨") — ଥାନ୍ତ[ି] (*thānti*, "abides"). The word ≈ ମାଣିକ (*māṇika*, "jewel") is read with uncertainty; the last word is ≈ ଧନ (*dhana*, "treasure"), perhaps ଧ୍ୟାନ (*dhyāna*, "meditation").
[^padmapatra-51]: A lotus leaf that water does not wet is an ancient figure of detachment (cf. *Bhagavad-gita* 5.10). The words ≈ ମନେଷ (*maneṣa*) and ≈ ଠଳ (*ṭhaḷa*) are not identified; the connection "ଜଳକୁ ଜଳ" ("to the water — water"?) is unclear. 2.51–2.53 are three comparisons leading to 2.54.
[^manika-52]: Understood thus: gold melts in fire, whereas a jewel in water stays impervious. The word ତଳେହେଁ (*taḷehe̐*, "even below, even deep within"?) is read with uncertainty.
[^madhavi-53]: Madhavi is a spring creeper with fragrant flowers. Per the edition's corrigenda ("୮।୨୫") ଯଏ is corrected to ଯାଏ (*yāe*, "goes away"); ଚମ୍ବି (*cambi*) is printed without ୁ and taken as ଚୁମ୍ବି (*cumbi*, "having kissed"). The word ଯେନ (*yena*) is understood with uncertainty (perhaps ଘେନି, *gheni*, "taking"). The editor's note mark at the end of the line is not carried over.
[^sparsha-54]: "No touch" — Kanai apparently says that Krishna's play with Radha (like the bee's with the flower, 2.53) leaves no trace and does not affect Her — in the spirit of the yogic reading of 2.39–2.50. In Gaudiya Vaishnava teaching the pastimes of Radha and Krishna are an eternal reality in spiritual bodies, not an allegory. Per the edition's corrigenda ("୮।୨୬") ସଙ୍ଗଲେ is corrected to ସଙ୍ଗରେ (*saṅgare*, "together with").
[^half-55]: Only the first half-line is printed (the line ends with a single stroke); the second half of the couplet is missing in the edition. The second letter of the first word (≈ ପ୍ରଭୁଙ୍କର, *prabhuṅkara*, "of Prabhu") is printed unclearly.
[^sudama-56]: Radha's quarrel with Sudama (Shridama) in Goloka and his curse by which Radha descends to earth are known from the *Brahma-vaivarta Purana*; the story is not in the *Chaitanya-charitamrita* or the *Chaitanya-bhagavata*. The second half-line breaks off in the edition's dots (a gap); on p. 9 the text resumes with a new couplet.
[^kishora-57]: This continues 2.56: in Goloka Sri Radha became angry on account of Sudama. "The supreme Shakti" is Radha; Kishora ("the youth") and "the all-auspicious" are Krishna. The first letter of କିଶୋର (*kiśora*) is blotted; the rhyme of the half-lines (*kiśora* / *maṅgaḷa*) is imperfect.
[^surata-58]: Printed ≈ ସୁରତ (*surata*); taken as "remembrance" (cf. Hindi *surat*); possibly a misprint for ସ୍ତୁତି (*stuti*, "praise"). Who "saw the supreme light" — Kishora or the devotees — is not said; cf. "the light of the universe" in 2.31. Where the devotees' speech ends is not marked; the quotation marks (2.59–2.69) follow the sense.
[^yogamaya-59]: Kanai calls Radha Yogamaya in the form of a woman and explains Her yellow garments by this (yellow garments, *pītāmbara*, are usually ascribed to Krishna). According to Gaudiya Vaishnava teaching, Radha is the hladini-shakti, Krishna's energy of bliss, the embodiment of mahabhava (*Chaitanya-charitamrita*, Adi 4), while Yogamaya is the Lord's internal energy that arranges His lilas; in Gaudiya Vaishnava descriptions Radha's garments are usually blue.
[^manini-60]: A manini is a heroine filled with mana, the proud anger of jealousy (cf. Radha's anger in 2.56–2.57). A gait "like an intoxicated elephant" is a traditional figure for a beauty's stately walk. The second letter of ମତଗାମିନୀ (*matagāminī*, ≈ *matta-gāminī*) is read as ତ with uncertainty (possibly ମଦ, *mada*, "intoxication").
[^sharira-62]: In 2.62 and 2.63 the third letter of the word read as ଶରୀରରେ (*śarīrare*, "in a body") resembles ଇ (≈ ଶରୀଇରେ); the reading ଶରୀରରେ is supported by sense and metre. "Bodiless — in a body": apparently Radha abides as bhava in the bodies of devotees (cf. 2.65–2.67), in the spirit of the yogic interpretation of 2.39–2.54. According to Gaudiya Vaishnava teaching, Radha's form is eternal and spiritual (*sac-cid-ānanda*); see also the note on 2.39.
[^keli-65]: The first word is read as ହୃଦୟର (*hṛdaẏara*, "of the heart"; the ୟ resembles ପୁ). The word ≈ କେଳ (*keḷa*) is not identified — possibly କେଳି (*keli*, "play"); the editor's note mark on it is omitted.
[^radhanga-66]: Kanai's idea that a devotee "gains [a place] within Radha's body" is close to the Odia yogic tradition (cf. 2.39–2.54). According to Gaudiya Vaishnava teaching, the practitioner does not become Radha or enter Her: he serves Radha and Krishna following the mood of Her sakhis, Her girlfriends (*Chaitanya-charitamrita*, Madhya 8 — Sri Chaitanya's conversation with Ramananda Raya).
[^majana-67]: Printed ମଜଣ (*majaṇa*), taken as "immersion" (= ମଜ୍ଜନ, *majjana*?). On the practitioner and Radha's mood, see the note on 2.66.
[^half-69]: Only the first half-line is printed (the line ends with a single danda); the second half of the couplet is missing in the edition.
[^speaker-70]: From 2.70 the speaker, using "we" of himself, tells of his guru, and from 2.73 of how he came to the Kshetra and sought a guru. Apparently Prabhu continues His discourse to the devotees (cf. 2.36 and 2.55; the whole book is "the words of Prabhu's holy mouth", 2.2); but the speaker is not named, and it may be the author himself.
[^kshetra-73]: The Kshetra is Sri Kshetra, Puri. If Prabhu is speaking, cf. *Chaitanya-charitamrita*, Madhya 6: on first seeing Jagannatha, Mahaprabhu fell unconscious in the ecstasy of love.
[^ehu-75]: The word after "without grace" is blotted; read as ଏହୁ (*ehu*, "this"), possibly ଏଥି (*ethi*, "here"). *Akāraṇa* / *kāraṇa* is wordplay: "futile, without cause" / "effective, having a purpose"; the understanding of the second half-line is uncertain.
[^kripa-76]: Printed ≈ କୃମ କୁ (*kṛma ku*): apparently a misprint for କୃପାକୁ (*kṛpāku*, "grace", acc.; the letters ପ and ମ are similar) or for କ୍ରମକୁ (*kramaku*, "order, succession"). ଚିନ୍ତିଲି, ଭେଟିଲି (*cintili*, *bheṭili*) are past forms of the 1st person singular ("I grieved", "I met"); ସର (*sara*) is taken as "lake".
[^keshava-77]: Keshava Bharati is the sannyasi from whom Sri Chaitanya accepted sannyasa at Katwa (*Chaitanya-bhagavata*, Madhya-khanda 28; *Chaitanya-charitamrita*, Madhya 3), before coming to Puri; Mahaprabhu received initiation (diksha) from Ishvara Puri at Gaya (*Chaitanya-bhagavata*, Adi-khanda 17; *Chaitanya-charitamrita*, Adi 17). If Prabhu is the speaker in 2.73–2.85, Kanai's account differs: the meeting with Keshava Bharati takes place after the arrival in the Kshetra, on the bank of a lake. According to Gaudiya Vaishnava teaching, Sri Chaitanya is Krishna Himself; He accepts a guru only to teach the world.
[^nuhu-78]: Printed ≈ ନ ୂହୁ, read as ନୁହୁ (*nuhu*, "[we] are not", 1st person plural, cf. ଜାଣୁ, *jāṇu*, "we know" in 2.82): Keshava Bharati apparently says that he is not worthy [to be the guru] (cf. 2.85); the sense "[you] are not ready" is also possible. ଜଣଇଲେ is corrected to ଜଣାଇଲେ (*jaṇāile*, "let know") per the edition's list of corrigenda.
[^asana-80]: Printed ଆସକୁ (*āsaku*); taken as ଆସନକୁ (*āsanaku*, "to the seat"), which also completes the metre.
[^half-81]: Only the first half-line is printed; the second half of the couplet is missing in the edition.
[^bheda-82]: Understood thus: Keshava Bharati knows that Krishna is hidden in the speaker's body. If Prabhu is speaking, this agrees with Gaudiya Vaishnava teaching: Sri Chaitanya is Krishna Himself, who has taken the mood and complexion of Radha (*Chaitanya-charitamrita*, Adi 4).
[^sada-83]: ସଦ (*sada*) is taken as ସଦା (*sadā*, "always"); ଭାବ (*bhāba*) as "thinking" (= ଭାବି, *bhābi*?).
[^lacuna-84]: After ଅତି (*ati*, "very") the edition prints dots — apparently a gap in the manuscript; the second half-line is missing.
[^grahana-85]: The editor's note mark on ଗ୍ରହଣ (*grahaṇa*, "receiving") is omitted. Keshava Bharati apparently declines to give initiation without his own guru's command; the account continues on p. 10.
[^madhavendra-86]: Madhavendra Puri is the sannyasi whom the Gaudiya Vaishnavas honour as the "first sprout" of the tree of prema (*Chaitanya-charitamrita*, Adi 9), the guru of Ishvara Puri; on him see *Chaitanya-charitamrita*, Madhya 4 (Gopala and Kshirachora-Gopinatha at Remuna) and Antya 8. The second half-line breaks off in the edition's dots (lacuna); the final «॥» is not printed.
[^kaivalya-89]: Kaivalya ("aloneness") is liberation in Indian philosophy, and among the Odia poets of the Panchasakha circle also a name of the Supreme; here it is taken as an apposition to "Vishnu". The first word is read with doubt: a letter with a spur on the left, as in ବିଶ୍ୱାସ (*biśwāsa*, 2.108), with the subscript worn away; possibly ବସ୍ତୁ (*bastu*, "the Reality"). ସେବାୟେ (*sebāẏe*) is taken as a locative, "in service". The Gaudiya Vaishnavas, following the *Srimad-Bhagavatam*, place pure service to Krishna above kaivalya-liberation (cf. the note to 2.50).
[^meru-90]: Meru is the world-mountain, a figure of greatness. Bharati is one of the ten monastic names (dashanami) of the sannyasis of Shankara's tradition; Keshava Bharati belonged to it (cf. the note to 2.77). Kanai calls Madhavendra Puri the guru of "our Bharati ashrama"; this is not found in the *Chaitanya-charitamrita* or the *Chaitanya-bhagavata*.
[^vallabha-91]: Jagannatha-vallabha is a garden in Puri where Mahaprabhu rested with the devotees during the Ratha-yatra (*Chaitanya-charitamrita*, Madhya 14).
