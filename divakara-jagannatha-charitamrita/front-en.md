# Jagannatha-charitamrita

*"The Nectar of the Life of Jagannatha"*

*A poem by Divakara Dasa (Odia, 17th century). Chapters 1–7 and 23*

*Translated from Odia into English*

---

## Translator's note

The *Sri Jagannatha-charitamrita* (in the running heads of the edition, *Jagannathamrita-gita*) is an Odia poem by Divakara Dasa (Divakara Mishra), a poet of the 17th century. He calls himself "the brahmana Divakara", son of Janadeva and Champa, of the Bharadvaja gotra. Divakara belongs to the Odia line of Jagannatha Dasa — author of the Odia *Bhagavata* and one of the Panchasakha — and stands fifth in the succession of disciples after him. The poem is a life of Jagannatha Dasa, "Ati Bada Gosain"; Chapters 1–7 tell of him in connection with Sri Chaitanya in Puri, and Chapter 23 speaks of the glory of Jagannatha's festivals and closes the book. Only these eight chapters are translated here; Chapters 8–22 do not speak of Sri Chaitanya.

What the chapters contain. Chapter 1: the mangalacharana, the sixteen disciples of Jagannatha Dasa, the tradition of his birth "from Radha's laughter", his childhood and studies. Chapter 2: Mahaprabhu's meeting with Jagannatha Dasa reading the *Bhagavata* under the banyan at Bata-Ganesha, the displeasure of the Bengali Vaishnavas, the teaching on diksha and the guru, and Jagannatha Dasa's initiation by Balarama Dasa. Chapter 3: the name "Atibada" and the Gaudiyas' departure from Puri — to Yajapur, Vishnupur and Vrindavana. Chapters 4–7: Mahaprabhu's teachings to Sarvabhauma, Nityananda and the Vaishnavas on maha-prasada, the Name, the murti of Jagannatha and the Harinama, the account of Prataparudra, and the Lord's departure into the body of Jagannatha. Chapter 23: the glory of beholding the Snana-yatra, the chariot and Gundicha, and the glory of Purushottama-kshetra.

**Source.** The edition: Cuttack, 1927 (corrected by Pandit Janardan Kar), from the archive.org scan; the text was read line by line from the page images. **Only Divakara's text** has been translated; the 1963 edition and modern translations were not used.

**How the translation was made.** There was no native speaker of Odia among the translators. Every couplet was read from the scan, transliterated and translated word for word (this is in the full edition, to be published separately); the literary translation rests on that work. Doubtful places are marked "(?)", unclear ones "(??)"; words added for the sense are in square brackets; Sanskrit shlokas are marked "[Skt.]". Questions for an Odia specialist are collected separately.

**Numbering.** The couplets are not numbered in the edition; the "chapter.verse" numbers are the translators', and so are the section headings.

**Divakara and the Gaudiya Vaishnavas.** The line of Jagannatha Dasa ("ativadi", "atibadi") is counted by the Gaudiya Vaishnava acharyas, Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati among them, among the apasampradayas — currents that have departed from Sri Chaitanya's teaching. Divakara consistently exalts Jagannatha Dasa: he is "born from Radha's laughter", the Lord calls him "Atibada" and holds him above His Bengali associates; the murti of Jagannatha is the source of all avatars, Krishna above Krishna; the mahamantra is in the Odia order "Hare Rama Krishna"; Mahaprabhu's departure is His entering the body of Jagannatha. Neither the *Chaitanya-charitamrita* nor the *Chaitanya-bhagavata* mentions Jagannatha Dasa. The translation renders the author exactly, softening nothing; at each such place a note briefly gives the Gaudiya Vaishnava position according to Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati: Sri Chaitanya is Krishna Himself, who has accepted Radha's mood and radiance; Jagannatha is Krishna Himself; His body is sat-cit-ananda, and His departure is a disappearance by His own will. The book is valuable as a testimony of how Sri Chaitanya lived on in the memory of the Odia Vaishnavas of the 17th century and as a source of Odia traditions about Puri — not as an authority in theology.
