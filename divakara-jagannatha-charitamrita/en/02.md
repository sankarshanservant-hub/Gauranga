# Divakara Dasa. *Jagannatha-charitamrita*, Chapter 2
## Sri Chaitanya in Purushottama; Jagannatha Dasa Reads the *Bhagavata* under the Banyan

*Translated from Odia without a native speaker; doubtful places are marked (?) and (??). The original, transliteration and word-for-word translation are in `full/02.md`.*[^intro-2-1]

*Chapter Two*

**2.1.** Listen attentively, good people, to the nectarean narrative [that] you have heard (?).[^shunicha-2-1]  
**2.2.** When that Chaitanya Gosain had appeared (lit. "was born") from Shachi's womb,  
**2.3.** He completed His family duties in [worldly] life (?) and then took initiation into sannyasa.[^sannyasa-2-3]  
**2.4.** Taking a host of Vaishnavas with Him, He wandered much over the earth.  
**2.5.** He visited many holy places and wandered through Vrindavana and other [places].  
**2.6.** Not finding the rasa of loving devotion, He entered Purushottama.[^prema-2-6]  
**2.7.** Having come, He stayed in a matha and went to see Jagannatha.[^matha-2-7]  
**2.8.** Thus some days passed. Hear of the lila of Sri Chaitanya.  
**2.9.** Sri Ati Bada Jagannatha [Dasa] — the Tenth Canto of the *Bhagavata*  
**2.10.** at the root (?) of the sacred tree he expounds in detail before Ganesha.[^vriksha-2-10]  
**2.11.** "Those who reject Krishna's shelter (?) and churn (?) the words of the Vedas[^tusha-2-11]  
**2.12.** [bear] toil only for gain: in the end they do not obtain liberation (??).  
**2.13.** As in pounding … (??) no rice grains are found in it,  
**2.14.** so one fallen (?) from Krishna, [given to] other worship, dies [and becomes] a bhuta."  
**2.15.** At this time Sri Chaitanya, taking His companions along,  
**2.16.** graciously came under the banyan; hearing the Purana, He was pleased.[^vata-2-16]  

## Sri Chaitanya Honours Jagannatha Dasa; the Vaishnavas Murmur; a Dispute about Initiation


**2.17.** In the fullness of prema Sri Chaitanya embraced the Dasa.[^embrace-2-17][^intro-2-17]  
**2.18.** For two and a half days the two remained in the bonds (??) of prema.  
**2.19.** Concealing His prema and restraining Himself, Gosai blessed [him].  
**2.20.** Having walked around the temple, [He] paid honour to Jagannatha.  
**2.21.** A twelvefold (?) full prostration he made before the Prabhu.[^dandavat-2-21]  
**2.22.** To the brahmana Sri Chaitanya said: "You are not different from the Sri Murti."[^murti-2-22]  
**2.23.** [This is] the nectar of Chaitanya's deeds: this devotee is Chaitanya's [devotee].[^charitamrita-2-23]  
**2.24.** Thus Lord Chaitanya spoke in the brahmana's presence:  
**2.25.** "To call Jagannatha 'Brahman' does not come from My mouth.[^brahma-2-25]  
**2.26.** Brahman dwells in all bodies (lit. 'vessels'): [to call him] equal [to all] I fear.  
**2.27.** To say [that I wish] to become his servant — [that too] I fear to utter.  
**2.28.** He is certainly a Dasa (lit. 'servant'); what am I to do, [his] state being such (?)?  
**2.29.** His own feet (?) are a great abode of prema.  
**2.30.** Placing them in the lotus (?) of My heart, I shall meditate [on them] without selfish desire (?)."  
**2.31.** Having said this, Sri Chaitanya honours [him] again and again.  
**2.32.** The host of Vaishnavas who were [there], seeing this, lamented (?):[^vaishnavas-2-32]  
**2.33.** "With an Odia brahmana He has unceasingly increased … (??).[^shiva-2-33]  
**2.34.** He will stay in this kingdom and observe Odia rites.[^karma-2-34]  
**2.35.** We shall draw Him away from this association." Thus lamenting (?), they began [to speak].  
**2.36.** They said: "The Utkala brahmanas, moreover, have received no instruction (initiation).[^upadeshia-2-36]  
**2.37.** Do not touch (?) them: the shastras forbid it.[^sparsha-2-37]  
**2.38.** Do not desire association with the worldly (?); do not worship various gods.  
**2.39.** A man who has not received initiation — do not touch his body.  
**2.40.** Like a tiger, a snake, a crocodile (?), shun (?) them, [even if they are] friendly (?).  
**2.41.** A person who has not received initiation is equal to an untouchable (?)."  
**2.42.** Hearing [this], Sri Chaitanya gave His command: "This is not a good word.  

*Shloka*  
**2.43.** *[Skt.]* 'Even now in Dvaraka, day after day, the proclamation is heard (?): whether unlearned or learned, a brahmana is My body.'[^dvaraka-2-43]  
**2.44.** All the gods are in the brahmana's body; in the brahmana's body Vishnu dwells.  
**2.45.** Though he has not received instruction (initiation), he is nonetheless a body of bhakti.[^anga-2-45]  

*Shloka*  
**2.46.** *[Skt.]* 'In whose body Krishna dwells, whose body is the form of bhakti — between that Krishna and the brahmana one should make no distinction (?).'"[^bhinna-2-46]  
**2.47.** Since such a command had been given, all then fell silent.  
**2.48.** The Dasa received this news and went for the Prabhu's darshan.  
**2.49.** Taking two [pots of] abhada in his hands, he came and presented himself [before Him] at the matha.[^abhada-2-49]  
**2.50.** Having washed his hands, he at once made a full prostration to the Prabhu.  
**2.51.** Sri Chaitanya commanded: "Come, O Dasa, full of bhakti!"  
**2.52.** The Dasa, with joined palms, said: "I (lit. 'we') have no initiation (lit. 'instruction')."[^diksha-2-52]  

*Shloka*  
**2.53.** *[Skt.]* 'Vows, charity, austerity, ablution, purity, shraddha and sacrifice — all dharmas perish [if they are] … (?) without instruction (initiation).'[^yatha-2-53]  
**2.54.** Thus Jagannatha Dasa spoke before the Prabhu.  

*Shloka*  
**2.55.** *[Skt.]* 'Sin committed in millions of births, knowingly and unknowingly, is put to flight (?) by merely accepting diksha — of this there is no doubt.'[^koti-2-55]  
**2.56.** Though there be sins of millions of births, [committed] knowingly and unknowingly, they depart (?):  
**2.57.** by merely accepting diksha that [person] becomes sinless and is delivered.  

## The fruits of initiation; what a guru must be, and from whom initiation is not to be taken


*Shloka*  
**2.58.** *[Skt.]* "Then comes the fulfilment of what is desired (?): wealth, dharma and good bhakti; and at the end [the initiate] goes to the divine abode of Vishnu, where servants serve [Him].[^phala-2-58][^intro-2-58]  

*Shloka*  
**2.59.** *[Skt.]* The killing of a brahmana, drinking liquor, theft of gold and the other (?) [great] sins, and hundreds of thousands of lesser sins, [a person] destroys by the power of diksha."  
**2.60.** The killing of a brahmana, drinking liquor, or whoever steals gold:  
**2.61.** these and other sins, hundreds of thousands, diksha manifestly takes away.  
**2.62.** A person who has received initiation (lit. 'instruction') obtains whatever he desires.  
**2.63.** [The initiated] obtain wealth, dharma and bhakti; in sadhana they gain all fruits.  
**2.64.** Listen (?), O sadhus, to the rule for examining (??) a guru.[^pariksha-2-64]  

*Shloka*  
**2.65.** *[Skt.]* "Though born in a great family, versed in all the shastras, and having studied [Vedic branches] a thousand in number (?), a non-Vaishnava cannot be a guru."[^avaishnava-2-65]  
**2.66.** Though he was born in the best family and knows all the shastras,  
**2.67.** though he studied the Vedas in a thousand branches and became expert,  
**2.68.** if he is not (?) a Vaishnava, he is not fit for the position of guru.  

*Shloka*  
**2.69.** *[Skt.]* "Brahmanas, Vaishnavas, devotees, truthful, sattvic and steady: in taking diksha from them [comes] good understanding, [and one delivers] one's family (??)."[^varna-2-69]  
**2.70.** Being a brahmana, [he must be] a Vaishnava: the disposition of his mind will be in bhakti;  
**2.71.** he will always speak the truth and by his sattvic nature be steady.  
**2.72.** Whoever takes initiation from such a one delivers a hundred [generations] of his family.  
**2.73.** From [a guru of] which (??) caste initiation is received, such is the dwelling place [of the initiate].  

*Shloka*  
**2.74.** *[Skt.]* "[One initiated] by a brahmana [goes] to Brahmaloka, by a kshatriya to Indraloka (?), by a vaishya to [the world] of Prajapati, and one initiated by a shudra to [the world] of the Gandharvas."[^loka-2-74]  
**2.75.** [For one who received] initiation from a brahmana, his dwelling is in Brahmaloka.  
**2.76.** Whoever takes initiation from a kshatriya dwells together with the gods.  
**2.77.** Having received the mantra from a vaishya, one goes and dwells in Pitriloka.  
**2.78.** [For one who received] initiation from a shudra, his dwelling is in the world of the Gandharvas.  
**2.79.** Such is the rule for non-Vaishnavas; but from a Vaishnava one obtains every perfection.  

*Shloka*  
**2.80.** *[Skt.]* "One without a son (?), one who has lost his children, one with a diseased scrotum (?), a dwarf, … (??); one with bad nails, with dark teeth, a leper, and one with the look of a rogue;[^unfit-2-80]  
**2.81.** *[Skt.]* one lacking a limb, one suffering from a grievous disease (?), one without family roots, and also the unsteady (?): [from them] there is no diksha; such are to be avoided.  
**2.82.** *[Skt.]* The untouchable (?), … (??), and one who has sided with enemies; a boaster, one fallen from [his own] guru, the unrighteous: such a one is to be abandoned.[^lectio-2-82]  
**2.83.** *[Skt.]* The blind, the deaf (?), the dumb, the dull, and also the lame; one fallen from purity and without propriety: such a guru is to be avoided.  
**2.84.** *[Skt.]* The cheat, the miser, the fool, the fickle (?), one devoid of bhakti, or an adulterer, one of crooked mind: [such] are to be avoided.  
**2.85.** *[Skt.]* Horse-traders (?) and cattle-traders (?), village priests, physicians (??), weapon-makers (?), cooks, those who bow [to no one]."  
**2.86.** These [people] with bad marks (?), having become gurus, bear guilt.[^kulakshana-2-86]  
**2.87.** If initiation is received from them, it destroys wealth, dharma and fame.  
**2.88.** In the practice of the mantra there is no perfection; whence, then, will he find bhakti?  
**2.89.** Therefore whoever is wise will quickly abandon such a guru.  
**2.90.** A guru who has no son: from him there is no initiation;  
**2.91.** one with a swollen scrotum (??) or a dwarf: from him do not accept [initiation];  
**2.92.** one whose nails have withered, whose teeth (?) will be … (??)[^danta-2-92]  

## From Whom Not to Accept the Name; a Guru of Any Varna; Jagannatha Dasa Asks for the Name


**2.93.** a hunchback, a rogue, an ugly person — do not accept the Name from [such].[^defects-2-93][^intro-2-93]  
**2.94.** One-eyed, sick with phlegm, … (??), [suffering from] elephantiasis, one whose body is lame;  
**2.95.** one who has no root of lineage — [he] is not fit to be a guru.  
**2.96.** A guru who does the work of a purohita — abandon him as guru, away with him (?)![^purohita-2-96]  
**2.97.** One whom it is improper to touch; one who is younger in age;  
**2.98.** a guru who is on the enemy's side — from him there is no initiation.  
**2.99.** One who quarrels with everyone — do not touch him.  
**2.100.** One who abandons his own dharma — from him there is no initiation.  
**2.101.** A guru who is blind or deaf — abandon him from afar.  
**2.102.** A person who is a slanderer (?), palsied, or subject to phlegm disease;  
**2.103.** one who does not keep his own purity — such a guru is not fit to be touched.  
**2.104.** A rogue, a miser, a fool — they are not worthy of worship (?) as gurus.  
**2.105.** [One who] falsely calls himself a Vaishnava [but] does nothing for the sake (?) of bhakti;  
**2.106.** one who consumes intoxicating substances, one who drinks wine;  
**2.107.** [one who] casts his eye on another's wife (printed 'on another's servant') — such a guru is not fit to be touched.[^paradara-2-107]  
**2.108.** One who speaks with guile; one who is impotent (a eunuch);  
**2.109.** one who trades in horses; one who kills animals with his own hand;  
**2.110.** one whose position is that of a village priest — reject his Name from afar.  
**2.111.** One who practises medicine (?); one who, serving, carries out [others'] orders;[^ajna-2-111]  
**2.112.** or one who serves out … (??) [food] — they are not worthy of obeisance.  
**2.113.** Having understood thus the signs of a guru, receive the mantra into the ear.  
**2.114.** Perhaps [someone] will say thus: "He is surely absorbed in the Name;[^objection-2-114]  
**2.115.** they know many things and are intent on bhakti —  
**2.116.** why (?) then are these not [counted as] Vaishnavas and not fit (?) for the position of guru?"  
**2.117.** — They had faults even before, and now they are acquiring [them] still more.  
**2.118.** Until they give up these faults, how can [they] be called gurus?  
**2.119.** As if [someone], taking a good seed (?), sows it in scorched ground —[^bija-2-119]  
**2.120.** however much effort he makes there, a tree will never grow —  
**2.121.** so the mantra is the good seed, [and] the highest bhakti is the instrument (?) [for] that seed.  
**2.122.** If this mantra comes into the hand of [such] a guru, it will perhaps become futile.  
**2.123.** As [when] an excellent brahmana, skilled in the Vedas and Puranas,  
**2.124.** has eaten in a chandala's house — so many [of his] virtues have become futile.  
**2.125.** A Vaishnava who is such (?) — never make him your guru.  
**2.126.** Practise sadhana with the highest bhakti — then there will be the highest bhakti.  
**2.127.** But one who is free from desire (?) and detached has given up these faults.[^varna-2-127]  
**2.128.** One who worships Hari single-mindedly is entitled [to be guru] for all varnas.  
**2.129.** Brahmana, kshatriya, shudra, vaishya, the various castes, ascetic, sannyasi;  
**2.130.** be it a shudra or a woman — whoever knows the twelve [kinds of] prema,[^dvadasha-2-130]  
**2.131.** the bhava of the prema of the Couple — Kishori and Kishora — together,  
**2.132.** the devotee who knows these qualities is himself the form of the body of prema.  
**2.133.** He is blessed among Vaishnavas; he is fit [to be] a guru.  
**2.134.** One who has received into his ear the Name from such a guru —  
**2.135.** the two kings of mantras beginning with [the name] Rama (?), this is the essence of all mantras —[^ramadi-2-135]  
**2.136.** one who serves the lotus (?) [feet] of that guru — blessed is his discipleship.  
**2.137.** Thus spoke Jagannatha Dasa before Sri Chaitanya.[^speaker-2-137]  
**2.138.** "O Lord, ocean of devotees, give [me] the command [to chant] the Name of Sri Hari!"  
**2.139.** Hearing [this], the Prabhu was delighted and said to Matta Balarama:[^balarama-2-139]  
**2.140.** "Give him initiation; have him perform the kirtan that begins with [the name] Rama."  

*Shloka*  
**2.141.** *[Skt.]* "The Name of Hari, the Name of Hari, the Name alone is the supreme refuge. In [the age of] Kali there is no, no, no other way."[^harinama-2-141]  

## Matta Balarama Initiates Jagannatha Dasa; the End of the Chapter


**2.142.** The one who (?) [is called] Matta Balarama is the best among the Vaishnavas,[^matta-2-142][^intro-2-142]  
**2.143.** truthful, sattvic, most steadfast, an ocean of mercy (?) in the rasa of prema.  
**2.144.** Sitting, lying, eating, and also while bathing and drinking water,  
**2.145.** they know neither night nor day: they remain intoxicated with the Name.  
**2.146.** Relishing the nectar of the Name, in bliss [they] thrill with prema (?).  
**2.147.** Seeing his prema and bhakti, the Merciful One showed mercy.[^kripakara-2-147]  
**2.148.** That lord, Matta Balarama, having performed siddhi (?), sat down on the asana.  
**2.149.** Making [a mark] in the shape of Hari's foot, he applied tilaka to the forehead:  
**2.150.** it is called "Hari-mandira"; from the middle of the nose up to the hair it shines (?).[^tilaka-2-150]  
**2.151.** He put malas of lotus [seeds], tulasi and dhatri (?) on Dasa's neck.  
**2.152.** On the chest, a lotus-shaped tilaka; conch and disc on both arms.  
**2.153.** Placing these mudras on the crown of the head (?), applying [them] at the sides of the … (??);  
**2.154.** the navel, the waist (?), the four sides; Srivatsa and moon, in fourteen [places].[^mudra-2-154]  
**2.155.** He had the seat purified; [he spoke] the words of the sankalpa, [naming] place and time.  
**2.156.** He began (?) [the rite with] the string and kaupina; seated, he performed [the invocation of] the rishi and chandas,[^nyasa-2-156]  
**2.157.** kara-nyasa and anga-nyasa, and meditation on the form of Sri Jagannatha.  
**2.158.** In the midst of the assembly of Vaishnavas, in the presence of Sri Mahaprabhu,  
**2.159.** he gave the instruction in the Name: the sweet (?) kirtan beginning with the Name.  
**2.160.** Then he imparted the king of mantras, and also gave the sixteen [names] of prema.[^mantra-2-160]  
**2.161.** In the rasa of the prema of Radha and Krishna, he imparted the Gayatri of the Couple.  
**2.162.** He imparted the mantra of Thakura and Thakurani, which is the essence [of all].  
**2.163.** The five names of Sri Jagannatha the excellent Sri Guru imparted.  
**2.164.** The mantra called "beginning with [the name] Krishna" no one knew at that time:[^krishnadi-2-164]  
**2.165.** [people] do not abandon the word of the Veda; all honour Brahma's command.  
**2.166.** Thus, by the rule of initiation, Dasa obtained all perfection (?).  
**2.167.** In such [people] Divakara Dasa places his hope (?).[^asha-2-167]  
**2.168.** Whoever accepts [initiation] thus is delivered from samsara.  
**2.169.** This is the very mantra of [our] own line. Whoever, showing the mantra of another line,[^kula-2-169]  
**2.170.** will deceive a person and lead [him] away — do not go [to such a one], even if it costs your life.  
**2.171.** Association [with him] will be like (??) [association with] a young woman; worshipping the king of mantras, [a person] will cross over [samsara].  

*Thus [ends], in the *Sri Jagannathamrita*, [the life of] Sri Ati Bada Jagannatha Gosai — the second chapter, called "Anukramani" (?).*[^colophon-2-171]

[^intro-2-1]: Chapter 2 begins on PDF page 7 (printed p. 6), after the colophon of Chapter 1. On Divakara Dasa, the Atibadi line and the Gaudiya Vaishnava position see Chapter 1, the note to 1.1. On features of the type (ି as an inner stroke, ଅ/ଆ, ଭ/ଭା) see Chapter 1, the note to 1.72. The chapter tells of Sri Chaitanya in Puri and of Jagannatha Dasa; the episode begins with his meeting with Mahaprabhu under the banyan at Bata-Ganesha (cf. VL 4).
[^shunicha-2-1]: ଶୁଣିଛ (*śuṇicha*) is formally a perfect of the 2nd person ("you have heard"); perhaps "listen — [here is] the nectarean narrative" is meant.
[^sannyasa-2-3]: ରାଜ୍ୟେ (*rājye*, lit. "in the kingdom") is taken as "in worldly life, at home". According to the CC, Mahaprabhu lived as a householder in Navadvipa until the age of twenty-four and then took sannyasa in Katwa (CC, Adi 17; Madhya 3). The print has ସନ୍ୟାସ (*sanyāsa*).
[^prema-2-6]: According to Divakara, Mahaprabhu wandered through holy places, Vrindavana among them, and, not finding the rasa of loving devotion [there], came to Purushottama (Puri). This prepares the account of His finding it with Jagannatha Dasa. According to the CC, after sannyasa Mahaprabhu went straight to Puri (Madhya 3–6), then travelled in the South (Madhya 7–9), and went to Vrindavana only later (Madhya 17–19). For Gaudiya Vaishnavas Sri Chaitanya is Krishna Himself in the mood of Radha, the source and giver of prema (CC, Adi 3–4). He does not seek prema from others. Cf. VL 4 (Mahaprabhu's arrival in Puri and meeting with Jagannatha Dasa at Bata-Ganesha) and the notes there. The print has ଅଦି (*adi*) for ଆଦି (2.5).
[^matha-2-7]: A "matha" is a monastic residence. According to the CC, in Puri Mahaprabhu first stayed with Sarvabhauma and then lived in the house of Kashi Mishra (Madhya 6; Madhya 10).
[^vriksha-2-10]: "Sri Ati Bada Jagannatha" in 2.9 is Jagannatha Dasa (cf. 1.20, 1.68). The "sacred tree" is the banyan in the temple precincts at whose root Bata-Ganesha stands (cf. 1.94; VL 2.22). ମୂଳର (*mūḷara*, "of the root") is an uncertain reading: the second sign is bold. The print has ଅଗର (*agara*) for ଆଗର, "before".
[^tusha-2-11]: 2.11–2.14 are apparently what Jagannatha Dasa expounds (or the author's own words). They render *Bhagavata Purana* 10.14.4: "Those who give up bhakti, the path to the highest good, and labour only for knowledge, gain nothing but trouble, like those who thresh empty husks". The unclear places are understood accordingly. ଅଶ୍ରେ (*aśre*) is taken as *āśre* / *āśraẏa*, "shelter" (possibly ଅଂଶେ). In ମନ୍ଥନ୍ତି (*manthanti*, "churn") the printed sign looks like ଣ୍ଟ ("maṇṭanti"); after the *Bhagavata* verse one would expect "only for knowledge". In 2.12 the signs are unclear: େ, a damaged sign, ା and a sign like ସ with a subscript. The word is taken as ମୋକ୍ଷ (*mokṣa*), "liberation". ରପୁ (*rapu*) in 2.13 is not identified: after the verse "husk" (*tuṣa*) would be expected. ଚ୍ୟୁତ (*cyuta*, "fallen") in 2.14 is read by the rhyme with ଭୂତ: the first sign is bold. In 2.13 the first sign of «ଯେମନ୍ତ» looks like ସ, and in 2.14 the first sign of «ସେହି» looks like ଯ (the ସ/ଯ trap); both are read from the sense (as… so…). ପ୍ରତେଜନ୍ତି (*pratejanti*, "reject") — cf. Chapter 1, 1.14.
[^vata-2-16]: Mahaprabhu comes under the banyan to hear Jagannatha Dasa's reading. This is an episode of Odia tradition; cf. VL 4 (the meeting at Bata-Ganesha). It is not in the CC or the CB: according to the CC, in Puri Mahaprabhu hears the *Bhagavata* from Gadadhara Pandita (Antya 7). "The Purana" here is the *Bhagavata*. *Sakhā-gaṇa* means "companions, friends" (not *sakhī*).
[^intro-2-17]: Continuation of Chapter 2: PDF pages 8–9 (printed pp. 7–8). On Divakara Dasa, the Atibadi line and the Gaudiya Vaishnava position see Ch. 1, note to 1.1; on the beginning of the episode (Mahaprabhu hears Jagannatha Dasa's *Bhagavata* under the banyan) see the notes to 2.1 and 2.16. Features of the type are as in Ch. 1 (note to 1.72): the sign ି is often printed as a short stroke inside the letter (ବିପ୍ରେ, ଦିବସ, ଦିନେ, ବିଧ, ବିସୂରି); the ligature ରା resembles ସ୍ୱ (ରାଜ୍ୟରେ 2.34). On these pages ଆ is a separate sign resembling ଥ without a serif (ଆଲିଙ୍ଗନ, ଆସେ, ଓଡ଼ିଆ, ଆଚରିବେ, ଆମ୍ଭେ, ଆଜ୍ଞା, ଆସ), while ଅ has a serif (ଅଢ଼ାଇ, ଅଗ୍ରତ, ଅଟେ, ଅଣ, ଅଛଇ, ଅଙ୍ଗ, ଅଜ୍ଞାନେ). [^bija-2-119]: The simile of the seed: the mantra is a good seed (*drabya*, "a valuable substance"). Cast into "scorched ground" (an unworthy guru), it will not sprout. The second half of 2.121 (*uttama bhakti drabya yantra*) is understood tentatively. ବୀଜ (*bīja*) in 2.119 is printed with a broken ୀ. ବଅର୍ଥ (2.122, 2.124) is the printed spelling of *byartha*, "futile". The example of the brahmana who ate at a chandala's house (2.123–2.124) expresses the caste rule of purity in food. Gaudiya Vaishnavas measure purity by bhakti, not by birth: cf. the note to 2.43 and CC, Madhya 8.128. In 2.125 there is a dot over ଯେ (ଂ/ଁ?).
[^varna-2-127]: Here the author (through Jagannatha Dasa) says that a guru may be a person of any varna, ashrama or sex, if he worships Hari single-mindedly and knows the prema of Radha and Krishna. This is close to the Gaudiya Vaishnava rule: "Whether one is a brahmana, a sannyasi or a shudra — whoever knows the science of Krishna is a guru" (CC, Madhya 8.128). But it contradicts 2.69–2.70 (the guru must be a brahmana-Vaishnava) and the scheme of 2.73–2.79 ("the guru's caste → the disciple's world"). ନିସ୍ପୃହେ (*nispṛhe*, "free from desire") is read tentatively: the sign ୃ stands under ହ. ସ୍ତିରୀ (*stirī*, 2.130) = *strī*, "woman".
[^dvadasha-2-130]: "The twelve [kinds of] prema" are apparently the twelve rasas (flavours of relationship with Krishna): five primary (shanta, dasya, sakhya, vatsalya, madhurya) and seven secondary (*Bhakti-rasamrita-sindhu*, parts 2–4; CC, Madhya 19). But this is only a guess: in the Odia tradition of the Panchasakha the count may differ. "Kishori and Kishora" are Radha and Krishna; "the Couple" (*yugaḷa*) is the Divine Couple.
[^ramadi-2-135]: "Beginning with [the name] Rama" (*rāmādi*): apparently the mahamantra in the Odia order — "Hare Rama Hare Rama Rama Rama Hare Hare / Hare Krishna Hare Krishna Krishna Krishna Hare Hare". "The two kings of mantras" are probably its two halves (or the names of Rama and Krishna). Cf. VL 6.1–6.18: the dispute "Hare Rama" or "Hare Krishna" and Mahaprabhu's decision "Hare Rama for Nilachala" (notes to VL 6.1 and 6.15). Gaudiya Vaishnavas, following Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati, chant the mahamantra beginning with "Hare Krishna". The word ରାମାଦି is read from the sign ରା, which looks like ସ୍ୱ; likewise in 2.140.
[^speaker-2-137]: 2.137 shows that the whole discourse on diksha and the guru (from 2.52–2.53) is Jagannatha Dasa's speech before Mahaprabhu; cf. the notes to 2.52 and 2.58. In 2.138 he asks Mahaprabhu for the Name; cf. 2.52 ("I have no initiation"). ପଦ୍ମର (*padmara*, 2.136) is printed as ପ + ଦ with a subscript sign; perhaps ପଦର (*padara*), "of the feet".
[^balarama-2-139]: "Matta Balarama" is Balarama Dasa, the eldest of the Panchasakha, called Matta ("intoxicated [with prema]"), author of the Odia *Jagamohana Ramayana*; cf. VL 5.131 and its note. According to the tradition Divakara gives (the guru-pranali 1.42–1.45; Chapter 23), Jagannatha Dasa was initiated by Balarama Dasa. Here Mahaprabhu Himself entrusts this to him. Grammatically ବଳରାମେ (*baḷarāme*) could also be an agentive ("Balarama said"), but by the sense of 2.140 the speaker is the Prabhu. The episode is not in the CC or CB, and neither Balarama Dasa nor Jagannatha Dasa is mentioned there. In the Gaudiya Vaishnava view Mahaprabhu is Krishna Himself, the giver of the Name (CC, Adi 3.77–3.78; CC, Adi 7). On His own initiation from Ishvara Puri see the note to 2.45.
[^harinama-2-141]: The famous shloka of the *Brihan-naradiya Purana* (38.126), quoted in CC, Adi 17.21. The usual reading of the first line is *harer nāmaiva kevalam*, "the Name of Hari alone". Here it reads *nāmaiva śaraṇaṁ param*, "the Name alone is the supreme refuge". Continued on the next page.
[^intro-2-142]: End of Chapter 2: PDF pages 14–15 (printed pp. 13–14). Just before this (2.137–2.141), Jagannatha Dasa asks Sri Chaitanya for the Name, and Mahaprabhu tells Matta Balarama to initiate him (notes to 2.137 and 2.139). Here the rite itself is described. On p. 15, after the colophon, Chapter 3 begins (`ch03/DD-03-1-16-*`). At the foot of p. 14 there is a printer's signature, "୨ ଜଗନ୍ନାଥାମୃତ ଗୀତା"; it is not part of the poem. In the right margin by 2.150 there is a sign resembling a proof-reader's mark. Features of the type are as in the notes to 2.17 and 2.58. The sign ି is often printed as a stroke inside the letter (ରାତି, ଦିବସ, କରି, ହରି, ତିଳକ, ବେନି, ନାମାଦି, ବିଧି). The ligature ରା resembles ସ୍ୱ (ରାତି, ବଳରାମେ, କରାଇଲେ, ମନ୍ତ୍ରରାଜ, ରାଧା, ଠାକୁରାଣୀ). ସଂ is printed as a sign resembling ଫ (ସଂସାରୁ). On p. 14, ଆ is a sign with a hook at the top left, resembling ଥ (ଆସନ, ଆସ୍ୱାଦ, ଆନନ୍ଦେ, ଆସନେ, ଆରମ୍ଭିଲେ; likewise ଆବର in 2.144). ଅ has a flat, "square" top (ଅଙ୍ଗନ୍ୟାସ). For this reason ଅଜ୍ଞା in 2.165 is given with the correction ଆଜ୍ଞା, as in 2.138. On p. 15 both signs are often filled with ink, and the distinction is unreliable. In 2.166 the signs ଦା, େ and ସ of ଦାସେ are shifted upward, and after ସିଦ୍ଧ there is a smudged sign (taken as ି).
[^matta-2-142]: On Balarama Dasa and the epithet "Matta" see the note to 2.139. The first word is printed ଯେ + ହୁ + the sign ଟ with the stroke ି (ଯେହୁଟି, "the one who"; cf. ଯେହୁ in 2.164). The same ଟ appears in କଟି (2.154). ଦୟା ("of mercy", 2.143) is a tentative reading: the print looks like ଦବା. The plural in 2.144–2.146 ("they know", "they remain") is honorific and refers to Balarama. In 2.146 the print has ସେମ; it is taken as ପ୍ରେମ ("prema").
[^kripakara-2-147]: Whose prema and bhakti "the Merciful One" sees is not stated. Perhaps Balarama sees the bhakti of Jagannatha Dasa. Another reading is also possible: Mahaprabhu sees Balarama's bhakti and therefore entrusts the initiation to him. ସିଦ୍ଧି କରି (2.148, "having performed siddhi") is understood tentatively; perhaps a preliminary rite is meant. The sign ସି differs from ଶୁ in ଶୁଦ୍ଧି (2.155).
[^tilaka-2-150]: 2.149–2.154 describe the Vaishnava marks with which the guru endows the disciple. They are the "Hari-mandira" tilaka ("temple of Hari") from the middle of the nose up to the hair, tilaka on the chest and on other parts of the body, kanthi beads of lotus seeds, tulasi and dhatri (amalaki), and stamped marks of conch and disc. Gaudiya Vaishnavas know the same marks: the twelve urdhva-pundra tilakas, the "temple of Hari" on the forehead, tulasi beads, and the mudras of conch and disc (*Hari-bhakti-vilasa*, ch. 4). The last word of 2.150 is printed ଶୋ + ହ with a subscript (like ହୁ); it is taken as ଶୋହଇ ("shines"). ଧାତ୍ରୀ (2.151) is read from the sense: the second sign resembles ତ୍ରୀ/ଙ୍ଗୀ.
[^mudra-2-154]: The reading of 2.153–2.154 is unclear in places. ତାଲୁରେ is printed ତା + ଲୁ (the ଲ was checked against ପୁଲକିତ, 2.146; it differs from ହୁ in ବାହୁର, 2.152). It is taken as ତାଳୁରେ, "on the crown of the head (?)"; possibly a misprint for ବାହୁରେ, "on the arms". The word କଠାଗର is unidentified. Possible readings are କଣ୍ଠାଗ୍ର, "the front of the throat", or ଜଠର, "the belly". The translation leaves it blank. The "fourteen [places]" (2.154) differ from the usual twelve places of tilaka (*Hari-bhakti-vilasa*, ch. 4: forehead, belly, chest, throat, sides, arms, back, nape). Srivatsa and the moon as marks on the body are not mentioned by Gaudiyas.
[^nyasa-2-156]: 2.155–2.157 describe the rite of diksha: purifying the seat, the sankalpa (a vow naming place and time), invoking the rishi and chandas of the mantra, kara-nyasa and anga-nyasa (placing the syllables of the mantra on the hands and limbs), and meditation. Diksha in the *Hari-bhakti-vilasa* (ch. 2) is arranged the same way. But the meditation here is on the form of Sri Jagannatha. The string and kaupina (*ḍora-kaupīna*) are the dress of a renunciant. It seems that Jagannatha Dasa also receives the renunciant's dress (*bheka*). This is not stated plainly: ଆରମ୍ଭିଲେ ("began, commenced") is understood tentatively. The last sign of କୌପିନ is printed as ନ with a dark subscript. Madhava also tells of Jagannatha Dasa's initiation by Balarama Dasa at Mahaprabhu's order (VL 7.1–7.18: "Take diksha — go to Balarama"). But there the rite is not described, and Svami (Jagannatha Dasa) goes to Balarama himself; he does not receive diksha "in the presence of Mahaprabhu". The episode is not in the CC or CB (see the note to 2.139).
[^mantra-2-160]: What Jagannatha Dasa receives (2.159–2.163): instruction in the Name (*nāma upadeśa*), the "king of mantras", the "sixteen [names] of prema", the "Gayatri of the Couple" Radha and Krishna, the mantra of Thakura and Thakurani (Radha and Krishna), and the "five names of Sri Jagannatha". The "sixteen [names]" are apparently the maha-mantra of sixteen names (*ṣoḍaśakaṁ nāmnām* of the *Kali-santarana Upanishad*). On the "king of mantras" cf. 2.135 and the note to it. Gaudiya Vaishnavas give the Gopala mantra and the kama-gayatri at diksha, and the Name at harinama initiation (*Hari-bhakti-vilasa*, ch. 2). "Five names of Jagannatha" are unknown in their practice; what these names are is unclear. ନାମାଦି (2.159, "beginning with the Name") is printed with a clear ନା, not ରା as in ରାମାଦି in 2.140. ସୁରସ (2.159) may be ସ୍ୱରସ.
[^krishnadi-2-164]: The sense of 2.164–2.165 seems to be this. At that time no one knew the maha-mantra beginning with the name of Krishna ("Hare Krishna…"): all followed the Veda and Brahma's command. In the *Kali-santarana Upanishad*, Brahma gives Narada the maha-mantra beginning with "Hare Rama". Cf. 2.135, 2.140 ("beginning with [the name] Rama") and VL 6.1–6.18. Gaudiya Vaishnavas chant the maha-mantra beginning with "Hare Krishna", as Mahaprabhu Himself gave it (CB, Madhya 23). For more, see the note to 2.135.
[^asha-2-167]: The author's signature (*bhaṇitā*). The first sign of ଆଶ has the hook of ଆ; it is taken as ଆଶ, "hope, reliance". ଅଂଶ ("share") is also possible: "in such ones Divakara Dasa has a share". Cf. ଆଶେ / ଅଂଶେ in 2.105.
[^kula-2-169]: *Kuḷa-mantra* is the mantra of [one's own] line (lineage). 2.169–2.171 are a warning: this mantra is one's own, and one should not go to someone who shows the mantra of another line and leads a person away. Against whom the warning is aimed is not said. If the "other" mantra is the "Hare Krishna" order (2.164), the warning may be aimed at the Gaudiyas. Gaudiya Vaishnavas recognise one line of Mahaprabhu, the Brahma-Madhva-Gaudiya sampradaya, and consider the "ativadi" an apasampradaya (Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati; see Ch. 1, the note to 1.1). The sense of 2.171a is unclear. Perhaps it means "association [with such a one] is like [dangerous] association with a young woman". Another reading is also possible: "[the mantra] should be kept close, like a young [wife]". ପ୍ରାୟେ ("like") is read clearly; the syntax is what is unclear.
[^colophon-2-171]: The colophon is set in two lines, as at the end of Chapter 1 (see the note to the colophon of Ch. 1). Chapter 2 bears the same name as Chapter 1, "Anukramani"; the print has ଅନୁକର୍ମଣି, taken as ଅନୁକ୍ରମଣି. Instead of "Jagannatha Dasa" (Ch. 1), it has "Jagannatha Gosai". Below the colophon there is a printer's ornament.
