# Divakara Dasa. *Jagannatha-charitamrita*, Chapter 1
## Introduction: Obeisances to the Guru, the Vaishnavas and the Gods; Ati Bada Gosain and the Sixteen Disciples

*Translated from Odia without a native speaker; doubtful places are marked (?) and (??). The original, transliteration and word-for-word translation are in `full/01.md`.*[^intro-1]

*Jagannathamrita-gita*[^title-1]

*Chapter One*

*Shloka*  
**1.1.** *[Skt.]* Full of all perfections, [granting] knowledge, devotion and liberation as befits each, he speaks (?) all perfection … (??). Obeisance to Sri Guru![^guru-shloka-1]  
**1.2.** First of all I, the brahmana Divakara, bow to the guru.[^divakara-2]  
**1.3.** He delivered [me] from terrible darkness and showed [me] the lamp of bhakti.  
**1.4.** The feet of all the Vaishnavas are the refuge of the brahmana Divakara.  
**1.5.** The baseness that was in me vanished by the guru's word.  
**1.6.** Whatever doubts [I] may have will go by your command.  
**1.7.** You will show the path of bhakti and take [me] along as your companion.

*Shloka*  
**1.8.** *[Skt.]* Lakshmi, Sarasvati, and also the divine sages (?) and Gajanana, Brahma, Sri Vishnu and Rudra — these the brahmana Divakara should worship.[^devata-8]  
**1.9.** That the book [be written] without obstacles, I serve your feet.  
**1.10.** You have made the whole world; [you] are perceived in countless (?) books.[^granthai-10]  
**1.11.** The composition I have begun — O (?) gods, make it free of obstacles.  
**1.12.** Bring the book to completion without hindrance: the brahmana Divakara [seeks your] refuge.  
**1.13.** If the book turns out to have faults, the saints will never blame it:  
**1.14.** they take its merits and cast (?) the faults far away.  
**1.15.** Even if it be utterly flawless, the wicked one blames it without restraint (?).[^khala-15]

*Shloka*  
**1.16.** *[Skt.]* Slanderers and pigs Hari has made useful: slanderers purify the saint, pigs purify the village.[^nindaka-16]  
**1.17.** There is no sinner in the universe equal to a person [given to] slander.  
**1.18.** Sinful and base people do not understand — and they blame an excellent book.  
**1.19.** If he does not slander (?), he will suffer [the torments of the hell] Raurava (?).[^raurava-19]  
**1.20.** Now — to Ati Bada Gosain: before him I prostrate.[^atibada-20]  
**1.21.** His sixteen disciples — before them I prostrate.  
**1.22.** [The disciples] of Sri Ati Bada Gosain — these are the names of the sixteen disciples:

*Shloka*  
**1.23.** *[Skt.]* Uddhava (?), Ramachandra, Gopinatha, Sri Hari, Vamana, Nandana, Gaura (?), Gopala, Khandana (?),[^sixteen-23]  
**1.24.** *[Skt.]* Janardana Pati (?), Krishnadasa and Vanamali, and also Govardhana, Kanhu Khuntia (?) and Karmi (?) — so they should be named.[^kanhu-24]  
**1.25.** *[Skt.]* And then the disciple of Jagannatha Dasa … (??). Thus the sixteen disciples; to them, blessed in the [age of] Kali (?), I bow.[^mritavan-25]  
**1.26.** Kirtana in ecstatic (?) [love] for Hari, worship in the mood of a sakhi.[^umada-26]  
**1.27.** Sri Ati Bada Gosain was born from a portion of Radha.[^radha-27]  
**1.28.** His sixteen disciples are portions of the sixteen sakhis.  
**1.29.** For the joy of serving their Lord they left the eternal [abode] and were born together [with Him].  
**1.30.** Of the sixty-four crores of sakhis, they are the chief in eternal service.[^sakhi-30]  
**1.31.** To perform the lila they came and gathered in the world of mortals.  
**1.32.** Whoever shows ecstatic (?) love — know that one to be an eternal sakhi.  
**1.33.** [He] whom they call Purushottama is eternal; here there is no "difference-and-non-difference" (?).[^bhinna-33]  
**1.34.** There are proofs of this; I shall tell [them] — listen, O saints.

## Signs of the Devotees; the Succession of Gurus; Fidelity to One's Own Path


**1.35.** [Those who bear] the marks of conch and disc by nature (?) — know them as Vaishnavas of Dvaraka.[^lakshana-35][^intro-35]  
**1.36.** One whose eyes do not blink — know him [as one] of the host of the gods.  
**1.37.** As a physician does not know the medicine that revives the dead (?),[^sanjivani-37]  
**1.38.** as a man does not know [how] to cross beyond dying, —  
**1.39.** [so,] as long as the body is not a woman's (?), one does not know the bhava of ecstasy (?).[^umada-39]  
**1.40.** Only by staying outside ecstasy (?) does one fail to attain prema in bhakti.  
**1.41.** Now I am writing down (?) in the book the succession of gurus.[^pranali-41]  

*Shloka*  
**1.42.** *[Skt.]* First, the brahmana Divakara; his guru, Navinakeshara (?); his [guru], the all-merciful (?) Kola (?) Krishna;[^pranali-42]  
**1.43.** *[Skt.]* his disciple (?), Vanamali; and Ati Bada Jagannatha Dasa; the glorious Balarama, and then Hridayananda;  
**1.44.** *[Skt.]* Gauridasa Pandita and Chaitanya Mahaprabhu; Advaita (??), Madhavacharya (?), Nrihari, … (??);[^names-44]  
**1.45.** *[Skt.]* Padmanabha, a shadow-portion (?), and also a portion of Madhava; Vyasa and Narada, Brahma and Narayana with Vishnu (??) — [thus] the name, the bliss of Parabrahman, was proclaimed with effort.  
**1.46.** All the gurus of this succession I have heard of from the mouths of sadhus.  
**1.47.** Honour all the Vaishnavas, but do not abandon your own [object of] worship (?).[^puja-47]  
**1.48.** One who serves another's [object of] worship (?) is called a ruin of the family —  
**1.49.** he is like a … (??) young woman who leaves her husband and loves another.[^batapi-49]  

*In the Vishnu Purana:*  
**1.50.** *[Skt.]* "Shruti and smriti are My command. Whoever, a fool (?), transgresses it is a breaker of My command, My enemy: even if he is My devotee, he is not a Vaishnava."[^ajna-50]  
**1.51.** Bhagavan says to Narada: "The Vedas and Puranas are My words.[^narada-51]  
**1.52.** The fools who abandon them and worship Me by another path  
**1.53.** obtain prosperity and siddhis by their sadhana, yet they do not attain bhakti.  
**1.54.** Since they break (?) My command, they are My enemies.  
**1.55.** However much sadhana they perform — where will they attain Me?"  

*In the Bhagavad-gita:*  
**1.56.** "What the Veda has enjoined on each — by doing (?) that he will cross over [the ocean of existence].[^gita-56]  
**1.57.** If a shudra does a brahmana's work, it is not good: he will perish in improper work.  
**1.58.** Doing the work of their own caste, let people not fear, even if it turns out poor."  
**1.59.** Whatever dharma one follows, let him not go over to another's, even if there be gain (?) [in it].  

## Ati Bada Gosain and Sri Chaitanya — from the Laughter of Radha and Krishna

**1.60.** Sri Ati Bada Gosain — who can tell his deeds?  
**1.61.** The charming laughter of Sri Radha — from that he was born.[^hasa-61]  
**1.62.** Sri Vaikuntha is the eternal abode; Sri Radha and Krishna [sit] on one seat.[^vaikuntha-62]  
**1.63.** They abide in one bhava; [Their] mind never wavers.  
**1.64.** They were in happiness, in the bliss of rasa; with tenderness [He] looked at [Her] face.  
**1.65.** Seeing Radha's face, like nectar, Krishna obtained happiness.  
**1.66.** That laughter is sweet nectar; from it Chaitanya appeared (lit. "was born").[^caitanya-66]  
**1.67.** Since the Two [abide] in oneness (?), Radha, looking at Krishna, laughed.[^ekatva-67]  
**1.68.** From that laughter at once was born he who [is] Ati Bada Jagannatha.  
**1.69.** The eternal Lord Sri said: "We two shall appear (lit. 'be born') in the world of mortals."  
**1.70.** Looking into Sri Chaitanya's face, [He said]: "Taking twelve sakhis along,[^dvadasha-70]  
**1.71.** appear (lit. 'be born') in the town of Nadiya and perform kirtana of the name of Hari."  

[^intro-1]: Divakara Dasa (Divakara Mishra; he calls himself "the brahmana Divakara", 1.2), a seventeenth-century Odia poet, stands fifth in the line of disciples from Atibadi Jagannatha Dasa (author of the Odia *Bhagavata*, one of the Panchasakha). His *Sri Jagannatha-charitamrita* (in the edition's running heads, *Jagannathamrita-gita*) is a life of Jagannatha Dasa; chapters 1–7 tell of him in connection with Sri Chaitanya in Puri, and chapter 23 of his initiation by Balarama Dasa, closing the poem. The translation follows the 1927 edition (Cuttack, revised by Pandit Janardana Kara), PDF pages 2–3; chapters 1–7 and 23 are translated. Divakara writes from within the Atibadi line and consistently exalts Jagannatha Dasa and his disciples; such passages are translated exactly, and the notes briefly give the Gaudiya Vaishnava position. The Atibadi line ("ativadi") is named in the list of *apasampradayas* — currents that deviate from Sri Chaitanya's teaching — cited by the Gaudiya acharyas, including Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati; Gaudiya Vaishnavas do not accept its teaching that Jagannatha Dasa is a manifestation of Radha (see 1.27) or that he stands above Mahaprabhu's associates. Neither the *Chaitanya-charitamrita* nor the *Chaitanya-bhagavata* mentions Jagannatha Dasa; cf. VL 4 (meeting with Mahaprabhu) and VL 5.119 (the title "Atibada").
[^title-1]: The title on the first page of the edition and in its running heads; the title page calls the book *Sri Jagannatha-charitamrita*.
[^guru-shloka-1]: The source of the shloka has not been identified. The reading is uncertain: printed ବଦତି (*vadati*, "speaks"); the sense would lead one to expect ଦଦାତି (*dadāti*, "grants"). The word ସ୍ୟାଭଭତ୍ (*syābhabhat?*) is not identified — perhaps a corruption of *syād bhavet* ("may it be"); the metre (anushtubh) is kept either way.
[^divakara-2]: "The brahmana Divakara" is the author's signature, repeated in the mangalacharana (1.4, 1.8, 1.12). The "guru" here is his own teacher in the Atibadi line.
[^devata-8]: The list of deities of the mangalacharana. The reading ସୁରର୍ଷୀଂଶ୍ଚ (*surarṣīṁś ca*, "and the divine sages") is conjectural: the second sign looks like ସ. Printed ଦିବାକରେ (*divākare*); grammar would require *divākaro* (nom.). Verses 1.9–1.12 address these deities ("you").
[^granthai-10]: The form ଗ୍ରନ୍ଥଇଁ (*granthaï̐*) is unclear; taken as "in / through books".
[^khala-15]: The print has no divider "।" between the half-lines. ଖଳତା (*khaḷatā*) is taken as "the wicked one [blames] it" (*khaḷa tā*); "wickedness" is also possible. ଅବଧା (*abadhā*) is taken as "without restraint" — a guess.
[^nindaka-16]: The shloka seems to be a current saying: the slanderer "cleanses" the saint as a pig cleans the village of filth. The grammar of the first line is faulty (*saphalo nirmito hariḥ*, lit. "Hari was made useful"); it is translated by sense. *Śuddhyanti* ("are purified") stands for "purify".
[^raurava-19]: Raurava is one of the hells (*Bhagavata Purana* 5.26). The couplet is printed with a negative ("if he does not slander") and is translated literally, but so it contradicts the context (1.17–1.18). The likely sense is "if he slanders, he will suffer Raurava": the negative is either a misprint or part of an idiom not understood. The word ଭୁଞ୍ଜି (*bhuñji*, "having suffered") is read with doubt.
[^atibada-20]: Ati Bada (Odia *ati baṛa*, "very great") is Jagannatha Dasa, founder of the line to which the author belongs; hence the name of his followers, the Atibadi. According to Odia tradition Mahaprabhu gave him this title (Divakara narrates the episode below); it is absent from the *Chaitanya-charitamrita* and the *Chaitanya-bhagavata*. Cf. VL 5.119 and its note. *Gosāi̐* is the honorific "Gosvami".
[^sixteen-23]: The sixteen disciples of Jagannatha Dasa (1.21–1.22). The division into names is conjectural: the print is unclear at the start of the line (ଉଦ୍ଧବୋ, *Uddhava*?), in ଗୌରେ (*gaure*, *gauro* expected), and in "Gopala-Khandana" (one name or two?). One counts 15–16 names depending on the division.
[^kanhu-24]: "Kanhu Khuntia" (*kāhnu-khuṇṭikāś ca*) is a conjectural reading. A disciple of Jagannatha Dasa; whether he is the same as Kanai (Kanhai) Khuntia, Mahaprabhu's associate and author of the *Mahabhava-prakasha* (cf. VL 2.25–27), is not established. "Janardana Pati" and "Karmi" are likewise conjectural (Pati is an Odia family title); *vadet* — "one should name".
[^mritavan-25]: The words ମୃତବାନଥ (*mṛtavān atha?*) are not understood (perhaps *smṛtavān atha*, "then remembered"?); କଳଧନ୍ୟା (*kaḷadhanyā*) is taken as "blessed in the [age of] Kali" — a guess. Who is called "the disciple of Jagannatha Dasa" in the first line is unclear.
[^umada-26]: The word is printed as ସ/ଉ + ମା + ଦ; taken as ଉମାଦ (*umāda*) = *unmāda*, "ecstasy, madness [of love]"; likewise in 1.32. The final "॥" is damaged in the print.
[^radha-27]: An Atibadi teaching: Jagannatha Dasa is a portion (*amsha*) of Sri Radha, and his sixteen disciples are portions of sixteen sakhis (1.28). Gaudiya Vaishnavas do not accept this. According to the *Chaitanya-charitamrita* (Adi 4), the sakhis and gopis are expansions of Sri Radha Herself, and Sri Chaitanya is Krishna Himself in the mood and complexion of Radha (Adi 1.5). Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati counts the "ativadi" among the apasampradayas precisely for such claims. Cf. VL 5.119 (note on the identification of Jagannatha Dasa with Radha).
[^sakhi-30]: "Sixty-four crores of sakhis" is a number from the Odia tradition; it does not occur in Gaudiya Vaishnava texts (cf. Radha's eight principal sakhis, *Chaitanya-charitamrita*, Adi 4; *Ujjvala-nilamani*).
[^bhinna-33]: The translation of the second half-line is conjectural. *Bhinnābhinna* means "different-and-non-different"; perhaps the point is that the sakhis (the disciples) and Purushottama are eternally inseparable. Cf. Sri Chaitanya's teaching of *achintya-bhedabheda*, the inconceivable oneness and difference of the Lord and His energies (*Chaitanya-charitamrita*, Adi 2; Madhya 20); Divakara shows no direct link to it.
[^intro-35]: Continuation of Chapter 1: PDF pages 4–5 (printed pp. 3–4). On Divakara Dasa, the Atibadi line and the Gaudiya Vaishnava position, see the note to 1.1. Couplets 1.35–1.40 continue the account of "signs" promised in 1.34.
[^lakshana-35]: "Signs" (cf. 1.34): according to Divakara, the different classes of beings are recognised by distinguishing marks. Conch and disc are Vishnu's emblems. The reading ସ୍ୱଭାବ (*swabhāba*, "by nature, from birth") is uncertain, and the ି in ଚିହ୍ନ (*cihna*) is unclear in print. Unblinking eyes are the traditional sign of the gods (*animiṣa*).
[^sanjivani-37]: *Mrita-sanjivani* is the remedy (or knowledge) that brings the dead back to life. The print seems to have ମୃତୁ (*mṛtu*), taken as *mṛta*. Couplets 1.37–1.38 are similes for 1.39–1.40.
[^umada-39]: The word is read as ଉମାଦ (*umāda* = *unmāda*, "ecstasy [of love]"), as the translator of 1.26 and 1.32 also took it (see the note to 1.26). The first sign resembles ସ୍ୱ/ଉ and the second ମା. The reading "of Radha" (ରାଧାଙ୍କ) is rejected: in Radha's name on these pages the second sign is a clear ଧା (cf. 1.61, 1.65). ସ୍ତିରୀ (*stirī*) is taken as colloquial "woman" (= *strī*); the second sign is unclear. The author seems to say that knowing the sakhi's bhava requires a female body or a female self-identification. Gaudiya Vaishnavas understand service in the mood of a sakhi (*manjari*) as internal — in the spiritual body (*siddha-deha*) contemplated within the mind (CC, Madhya 22.156–157), not as a change of outward appearance. Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati's list of apasampradayas includes, besides the "ativadi", the "sakhibheki", who outwardly imitate the sakhis.
[^pranali-41]: *Guru-pranali* is the list of gurus in a succession. The form ଲେଖୁଣ (*lekhuṇa*) is unclear; taken as "I am writing down".
[^pranali-42]: The shlokas 1.42–1.45 give Divakara's guru-pranali, ascending from the author to Narayana. His guru is Navinakeshara (?); then comes Kola (?) Krishna, then Vanamali (apparently the same as in the list of the sixteen disciples, 1.24) and Ati Bada Jagannatha Dasa. This makes Divakara fifth in succession from Jagannatha Dasa (cf. the note to 1.1). Above Jagannatha Dasa stands Balarama (Dasa; Chapter 23 tells of Jagannatha Dasa's initiation by Balarama Dasa), then Hridayananda and Gauridasa Pandita, and above them Sri Chaitanya. Gauridasa Pandita is a companion of Sri Chaitanya and Nityananda from Ambika-Kalna (CC, Adi 11). Hridayananda is apparently Hridaya-Chaitanya, Gauridasa's disciple and Shyamananda's guru (according to the Gaudiya hagiographies). Balarama Dasa's initiation by Hridayananda is not mentioned in Gaudiya sources. The upper part of the list (Narayana, Brahma, Narada, Vyasa, … Padmanabha, Nrihari, Madhava) resembles the Brahma-Madhva parampara accepted by Gaudiya Vaishnavas (Kavi Karnapura's *Gaura-ganoddesha-dipika*, Baladeva Vidyabhushana's *Prameya-ratnavali*). In that parampara, however, Sri Chaitanya takes initiation from Ishvara Puri, disciple of Madhavendra Puri (CB, Adi 17; CC, Adi 9), while Divakara puts Advaita (?) and Madhavacharya (?) in that place. Gaudiya Vaishnavas do not recognise the branch "Sri Chaitanya — Gauridasa — Hridayananda — Balarama Dasa — Jagannatha Dasa". Many readings in the shlokas are uncertain (see 1.44), and the Sanskrit is irregular (e.g., *divākaro* before *tad-*).
[^names-44]: Uncertain readings in 1.44–1.45. ଅଦ୍ୱତି (*advati*) is taken as "Advaita" (printed ଅ + a sign like ଦ + ତି); ମାଧବାଚାର୍ଯ୍ୟୋ (*mādhavācāryo*) is read from context. Madhvacharya or Madhavendra Puri may be meant. ନୃହର[ନୃହରି] is Nrihari (Nrihari-tirtha of the Madhva parampara?); ଭବାନ ହି (*bhavāna hi*) is not understood. In 1.45, ଛାୟାଂଶକୋ (*chāyāṁśako*, "shadow-portion"?) and ସବିଷ୍ଣୁନା (*saviṣṇunā*, "with Vishnu"?) are tentative readings, and the syntax of the shloka is unclear. Shloka 1.45 takes three lines in print.
[^puja-47]: ପୂଜ (*pūja*) is taken as "worship, object of worship" (= *pūjā* or *pūjya*). The sense of 1.47–1.49: honour all Vaishnavas, but do not betray one's own succession and one's own Deity. କୁଳବୁଡ଼ା (*kuḷabuṛā*), literally "one who drowns the family", is an abusive term: "ruin, disgrace of the family".
[^batapi-49]: The word ବଟପୀ (*baṭapī*?) is not identified; by context it means "unfaithful, wanton". The first sign is ବ or କ.
[^ajna-50]: A widely quoted verse, usually given as *śruti-smṛtī mamaivājñe yas te ullaṅghya vartate | ājñā-cchedī mama drohī mad-bhakto 'pi na vaiṣṇavaḥ* and ascribed to Vishnu. Divakara names the *Vishnu Purana* as its source; this could not be verified. The print has ଆଜ୍ଞାଚ୍ଛେଦା (*ājñācchedā*) where ଆଜ୍ଞାଚ୍ଛେଦୀ is expected. ମୂଢ଼ାସ୍ତୁ (*mūḍhās tu*, "but a fool"?) is an uncertain reading.
[^narada-51]: The source of Bhagavan's words to Narada (1.51–1.55) is not established. Cf. the verse of the *Brahma-yamala* quoted in *Bhakti-rasamrita-sindhu* (1.2.101): devotion to Hari without [following] shruti, smriti, the Puranas and the Pancharatra leads only to disturbance. ରୁଦ୍ଧି (*ruddhi*) is the Odia spelling of *ṛddhi*, "prosperity"; ଭାଜନ୍ତି (*bhājanti*, "break, violate"?) is understood from context.
[^gita-56]: 1.56–1.58 are Odia verses (not Sanskrit), which Divakara quotes as words of the *Bhagavad-gita*. In sense they are a free rendering of BG 3.35 and 18.47 ("better one's own dharma, though imperfect…"). The Odia source is not established. କରଣ (*karaṇa*) is taken as "doing" (= *kariṇa*?). Following Sri Chaitanya's conversation with Ramananda Raya (CC, Madhya 8), Gaudiya Vaishnavas regard varnashrama duty as "external" compared with pure bhakti (cf. BG 18.66).
[^hasa-61]: The Atibadi teaching: Jagannatha Dasa was born from Radha's laughter (1.61, 1.67–1.68), and Sri Chaitanya from Krishna's (1.66). Cf. VL 5.116–5.117, where Jagannatha Dasa himself tells this story, and the note there. Gaudiya Vaishnavas do not accept this: Sri Chaitanya is Krishna Himself, who has taken Radha's bhava and complexion (CC, Adi 1.5; Adi 4). Jagannatha Dasa is not mentioned in the CC or the CB. See also the note to 1.27.
[^vaikuntha-62]: In Divakara, Radha and Krishna abide in Vaikuntha. For Gaudiya Vaishnavas the eternal abode of Radha and Krishna is Goloka-Vrindavana, which is above Vaikuntha (CC, Adi 5).
[^caitanya-66]: Whose laughter it is is not said directly; by the context (1.64–1.65) and by VL 5.116–5.117, it is Krishna's. Of Sri Chaitanya's appearance the text says "was born" (*jāta*), translated "appeared". On the Gaudiya Vaishnava position, see the note to 1.61.
[^ekatva-67]: ଏକତ୍ୱ (*ekatwa*, "oneness") is an uncertain reading (the second sign looks like ତ/ଦ with a subscript).
[^dvadasha-70]: Who speaks to whom in 1.69–1.71 is not said directly. Understood thus: the eternal Lord (Krishna) addresses Sri Chaitanya (or the future Sri Chaitanya). "We two" in 1.69 apparently means Sri Chaitanya and Jagannatha Dasa (or Radha and Krishna). Nadiya is Navadvipa. Gaudiya Vaishnavas have no such list of "twelve sakhis" accompanying Sri Chaitanya; cf. the twelve gopalas, companions of Nityananda (*Gaura-ganoddesha-dipika*). The first sign of the word ସଖୀ is unclear in print.
