# Divakara Dasa. *Jagannatha-charitamrita*, Chapter 7
## Mahaprabhu with His Associates in the Jagamohana; the Gundicha Yatra and Bahuda

*Translated from Odia without a native speaker; doubtful places are marked (?) and (??). The original, transliteration and word-for-word translation are in `full/07.md`.*[^intro-7-1]

*Chapter Seven*

**7.1.** The 'Chaitanya-charitamrita': spoken from the mouth of Sri Chaitanya.[^charitamrita-7-1]  
**7.2.** This book the brahmin Divakara tells for the sake (?) of the saints.  
**7.3.** One day Lord Chaitanya graced the Sri jagamohana [with His presence].  
**7.4.** Gauridasa Pandita [was there] together with Matta Balarama.[^gauridas-7-4]  
**7.5.** The disciple Rupa (?), who is a Gosai, [and] Sri Sanatana Prabhu are here;  
**7.6.** Sri Raghunatha Bhatta is present; Sri Jiva (?) Svami is here;  
**7.7.** Sri Gopala Dasa is here; Sri Raghunatha Dasa is here.  
**7.8.** These six Gosais are here; [here too is] the Odia Kashi Mishra.[^six-7-5]  
**7.9.** In front is Sarvabhauma; Gopinatha Acharya is [also] here.  
**7.10.** Jagadananda Chakravarti; and Mukunda Datta is here.  
**7.11.** Sri Damodara Chakravarti; Sri Ramananda, who is [also] here.  
**7.12.** Sri Nityananda [and] Sri Advaita: both Prabhus are together [with Him].[^associates-7-9]  
**7.13.** Mahaprabhu Sri Chaitanya [takes] darshan of Sri Jagannatha:  
**7.14.** four circumambulations, twelve full prostrations.[^pradakshina-7-14]  
**7.15.** The Vaishnavas from the land of Gauda came before (??) the Gundicha.  
**7.16.** Having completed the nine-day yatra, they served (?) in the Gundicha procession.  
**7.17.** Bahuda, [the return] of the Sri Gundicha yatra: Jagannatha is [again] in Nila-kandara.  
**7.18.** On the Ekadashi of the bright fortnight of Ashadha all the Vaishnavas gathered,  
**7.19.** and all prostrated themselves near (?) Sri Prabhu Chaitanya.[^gundicha-7-15]  

## Mahaprabhu Explains Harinama: the Names "Hari", "Rama" and "Krishna"


**7.20.** Sri Ati Bada Jagannatha [Dasa] too made his prostration.[^intro-7-20]  
**7.21.** Nityananda and all the others gathered before Prabhu.  
**7.22.** They asked (?) about Harinama; Prabhu declared thus.[^harinama-7-22]  
**7.23.** First of all, then, in a great (??) shloka Prabhu established the virtues of the Name.  
**7.24.** The first is "Hari", two syllables: such is the command of Sri Chaitanya.  
**7.25.** O saints, listen: chant the name of Hari in bliss!  
**7.26.** Insects, birds and all other forms, all the unmoving and moving [beings] —  
**7.27.** in all of these, filling [them], abides the word "Hari".  
**7.28.** Whoever is by nature (??) a devotee becomes a Vaishnava by remembrance (?).[^smarana-7-28]  
**7.29.** Mere remembrance destroys the sin of the devotees;  
**7.30.** it destroys the ocean of sorrow: [one] easily attains the feet of Vishnu.  

*Shloka*  
**7.31.** *[Skt.]* "The essence of skill (??) in all (?), the embodied deity of beauty (?), who plays with Radha (?) in the rasa dance — He is called Rama (?)."[^rama-7-31]  
**7.32.** *[Skt.]* "The yogis delight in the Infinite, in the Truth-bliss, in the Spirit; therefore by the word 'Rama' the Supreme Brahman is called."[^ramante-7-32]  
**7.33.** Inaccessible to Hara and Viranchi (?) — such is the name of Rama.  
**7.34.** It is very (?) lovely and tender, the best of all.  
**7.35.** It is the source of all bhavas, melting in the bhava of the bliss of prema.  
**7.36.** In all the Vedas and the Vedanta the name of Sri Rama is the essence of all.  
**7.37.** Since He [dances] the rasa with Radha, therefore Krishna is called Rama.  
**7.38.** Eternally in the hearts of the yogis: in meditation they eternally contemplate (?) Rama (?).  
**7.39.** He whom we call Rama is the form of sat-chit-ananda.  
**7.40.** Therefore this is the formless Brahman; the name of Sri Rama is the root support.[^brahman-7-40]  

*Shloka*  
**7.41.** *[Skt.]* "He whose sole happiness is bliss, possessed of Sri, dark, lotus-eyed, Gokulananda-nandana — He is called Krishna."[^krishna-7-41]  
**7.42.** Sri Lord Chaitanya gave the explanation of the word "Krishna".  
**7.43.** The supreme bliss and happiness of all living beings is the name of Krishna.  
**7.44.** In bliss He is Bliss; in the form of Brahman He is chid-ananda.  
**7.45.** From the body of Radha Lakshmi is born; therefore He is called Shriman (?).[^lakshmi-7-45]  
**7.46.** He is with Radha (?): the Couple abide together.  
**7.47.** His body is dark and beautiful, like a blue sapphire.  
**7.48.** His eyes are more beautiful than the lotus (?); the Name is the essence that delivers the earth.  
**7.49.** Dark as durva grass, He in the form of Brahman is the primeval root.  
**7.50.** All the hosts of rishis in the universe perform the sankirtana of [the name of] Krishna.  
**7.51.** The word "go" — … (??); the word "kula" — [refers] to the gods.[^gokula-7-51]  
**7.52.** The bliss of the devotees' minds — by this word [He is] "Gokulananda".  
**7.53.** Since this is born of bliss, therefore the name is "Ananda-ananda".  
**7.54.** So it is written (?) in the *Brahma-vaivarta Purana*.[^bvp-7-54]  
**7.55.** To all the devotees there are, [He is] bliss for the eyes (?).  
**7.56.** The word "Nandanandana" — when we take (?) it and write it in the book —[^nandanandana-7-56]  
**7.57.** [means]: He who appeared (lit. 'was born') to Nanda in the Dvapara-yuga; from this the name will become famous.  
**7.58.** Then this word in the Vedas and the Vedanta is uttered by the rishis, Mahesha and Brahma;  
**7.59.** in Satya and the other yugas, for millions upon millions [of years], they contemplate that word (?): "Krishna".  
**7.60.** Since He appears (lit. "is born") from bliss, He is called "Ananda-nandana".  
**7.61.** As many words as speech utters, as many more as are in the *Nirukta* (??),[^nirukta-7-61]  
**7.62.** as many as you hear in the Vedas and the Vedanta — know all this to be the name of Krishna.  
**7.63.** As many forms as the eye sees — know this to be Harinama.  

[^intro-7-1]: Beginning of Chapter 7: PDF page 43 (printed p. 42), after the colophon of Chapter 6. The heading 'ସପ୍ତମ ଅଧ୍ୟାୟ' stands below an ornament; 7.1 is set with an indent. 19 couplets, no shlokas. This portion ends with 7.19 at the foot of p. 43; the text continues on p. 44. There is no printer's signature. Features of the type: ି is often invisible (ଦିନେକ, ବିଜୟେ, ଓଡ଼ିଆ, ନବଦିନ, ସାରିଲେ); such ି are given without brackets. ରା/ରୀ look like ସ୍ୱ (ଗଉରୀ, ରାମାନନ୍ଦେ).
[^charitamrita-7-1]: 'Chaitanya-charitamrita' ('the nectar of Chaitanya's deeds') is what Divakara calls his narrative (or this part of it) here: 'spoken from the mouth of Sri Chaitanya'. Cf. 6.88–6.96, where Ati Bada records Mahaprabhu's teaching. This is not Krishnadasa Kaviraja's *Sri Chaitanya-charitamrita* (CC), to which we refer. In the colophons the poem is called *Sri Jagannathamrita*. ଅର୍ଥର (7.2) is taken as 'for the sake of' (gen. of *artha*, 'purpose, benefit'); perhaps 'the meaning [of the book]'.
[^gauridas-7-4]: Gauridasa Pandita and Matta Balarama (Balarama Dasa) are links in Divakara's guru-pranali: Gauridasa, Hridayananda, Balarama Dasa, Jagannatha Dasa (see the notes to 1.42 and 2.139). According to the CC (Adi 11), Gauridasa Pandita is an associate of Nityananda from Ambika-Kalna. Balarama Dasa is not mentioned in the CC or CB.
[^six-7-5]: 7.5–7.8: the six Gosais are Rupa, Sanatana, Raghunatha Bhatta, Jiva, Gopala Dasa (presumably Gopala Bhatta) and Raghunatha Dasa. Here the list agrees with the Gaudiya one. In 3.170–3.172 Nityananda is named instead of Raghunatha Bhatta (see the note to 3.170). But according to the CC, the six were never all together with Mahaprabhu in Puri. Rupa and Sanatana came to Puri (Antya 1; Antya 4), Raghunatha Bhatta twice (Antya 13). Raghunatha Dasa lived in Puri for many years (Antya 6). Mahaprabhu met Gopala Bhatta in the south, according to Gaudiya tradition. Jiva Gosvami, by the same tradition, never saw Mahaprabhu (see the note to 3.170). ରୂପକ, ଜୀବକ are names ending in -ka. This may be a suffix on the name or a genitive ('Rupa's disciple'?); but the six Gosais add up only with Rupa. Kashi Mishra is called 'the Odia' (*oṛiā*); Mahaprabhu lived in his house in Puri (see the note to 2.7).
[^associates-7-9]: Gopinatha Acharya is Sarvabhauma's brother-in-law (CC, Madhya 6). On Jagadananda Chakravarti see the note to 3.83. Mukunda Datta is a singer, an associate of Mahaprabhu since the Navadvipa days (CC, Adi 10). 'Damodara Chakravarti' has not been identified. He may be Svarupa Damodara or Damodara Pandita (CC, Adi 10); the title 'Chakravarti', as with Jagadananda, does not occur in the CC. Nityananda and Advaita, according to the CC, came to Puri with the Gaudiyas for the Ratha-yatra (Madhya 11; Madhya 16).
[^pradakshina-7-14]: Four circumambulations and twelve prostrations are the order of darshan in Divakara; we do not know his source. According to the CC (Antya 14), Mahaprabhu usually beheld Jagannatha from the Garuda-stambha in the natamandira, in front of the jagamohana.
[^gundicha-7-15]: 7.15–7.19 follow the yearly cycle of the Ratha-yatra. The Gaudiyas came to Puri before the Ratha-yatra and stayed for the four months of chaturmasya (CC, Madhya 11; Madhya 15; cf. the notes to 3.167 and 3.175). The 'nine-day yatra' (*nabadina yātrā*) lasts from the Ratha-yatra (the second lunar day of the bright fortnight of Ashadha) to Bahuda, the return procession on the tenth day. Nila-kandara is Puri (see the note to 3.137). The Ekadashi after Bahuda is Hari-shayana Ekadashi, the beginning of chaturmasya; on this day in Puri the Deities on the chariots are adorned with gold (Suna Besha). Uncertain readings. ସମୂର୍ବେ (7.15, ??): printed ସ + ମୂ + ର୍ବେ, an unidentified word; by sense 'before' (ସପୂର୍ବେ? ପୂର୍ବେ?), since the Gaudiyas came before the Ratha-yatra. ଗୁଣ୍ଡିଚା is printed ≈ ଗୁଣ୍ଡିଚ୍ଚ. ଖଟିଲେ (7.16, ?) is taken as 'served, laboured' (*khaṭibā*). Who the subject of 7.16 is, the Gaudiyas or Jagannatha, is unclear. କୂଳେ (7.19, ?) is taken as 'near' (lit. 'on the bank'). The scene continues on p. 44.
[^intro-7-20]: Continuation of Chapter 7: PDF pages 44–45 (printed pp. 43–44). 41 Odia couplets and three Sanskrit shlokas (7.31, 7.32, 7.41); before the shlokas stand unnumbered "ଶ୍ଲୋକଃ" lines (I1, I2). Lines 7.33 and 7.42 are indented. The scene continues 7.15–7.19: after the Bahuda and the Ekadashi, Ati Bada (Jagannatha Dasa; see the note to 1.20), Nityananda and the others bow to Mahaprabhu and ask about Harinama. Mahaprabhu explains the names: "Hari" (7.24–7.30), "Rama" (7.31–7.40), "Krishna" (7.41–7.63). Page 45 ends with 7.63; the chapter continues on p. 46. Features of the type. The sign ି is often invisible (ହରି, ମିଳିଲେ, ନିତ୍ୟେ, ଚିଦାନନ୍ଦ); such ି are given without brackets. ରା looks like ସ୍ୱ (ରାଧା, ରାମ, ପୁରାଣ, ନିରାକାର). ହ with a bold dot below is read as ହୁ (ସେହୁ, ଏହୁ, କହୁ), as in 6.42. The sign like ଫ in 7.50 is a fused ସଂ, as in 6.47 and 6.71.
[^harinama-7-22]: 7.22–7.63 explain the mahamantra word by word: "Hari", "Rama", "Krishna". The order is the Odia one, "Hare Rama … Hare Krishna" (see the notes to 2.135 and 3.147). Divakara takes the first word as the name "Hari", "two syllables" (7.24): Hari fills all forms, moving and unmoving (7.26–7.27). For the Gaudiya Vaishnavas, "Hare" in the mahamantra is the vocative of "Hara" (Harā), a name of Sri Radha; so in the Gaudiya explanations of the mahamantra attributed to Jiva Gosvami and Gopala Guru Gosvami. Mahaprabhu Himself gave the mahamantra to the residents of Navadvipa (CC, Adi 17). On the power of remembrance and of the shadow of the Name (7.28–7.30) see the note to 5.85. Unclear readings. ପଚାରିଲେ (7.22, ?): printed ପ + a sign like ସ୍ୱ/ଚ with a hook + ର + େଲ; possibly ପସାରିଲେ, 'spread'. ମହାଶ୍ଲୋକେ (7.23, ??): between ମ and ଶ୍ଲୋକେ there is a solid blot; possibly ମନ୍ତ୍ରଶ୍ଲୋକେ. Which shloka 7.23 means is unclear; the shlokas come below (7.31, 7.32, 7.41).
[^smarana-7-28]: ସ୍ୱରୂପଟି (7.28, ??): after ସ୍ୱରୂପ stands a sign like ଟ with a ring below and a loop above. It seems to be the same unidentified sign as in 6.61, 6.66 and 6.67 (see the note to 6.65). It closely resembles ଟି in the word କୋଟି (7.59): there ି is a hook over ଟ; here it seems fused into a loop. If so, Chapter 6 may read ଶଯ୍ୟାଟି, ପ୍ରବଟି etc., with the Odia particle ‑ଟି. This is our conjecture. Here understood as 'by nature' (cf. *svarūpataḥ*); possibly 'he who is the very form of a devotee'. ସ୍ମରଣେ (?): the first sign is like ଦୂ/ହୂ with a bold blot below; cf. ସ୍ମରଣ in 7.29.
[^rama-7-31]: Shloka 7.31 is a variant of a verse from Gaudiya explanations of the mahamantra: *vaidagdhya-sāra-sarvasvaṁ mūrta-līlādhidaivatam / rādhikāṁ ramayan nityaṁ rāma ity abhidhīyate* ("The whole wealth and essence of skill, the embodied presiding deity of the lilas, He ever delights Radhika — therefore He is called Rama"). Its source is unknown to us. The print has other words (*sarveṣu*, *lāvaṇya-devatā*, *rādhayā rāsa-keliko*); we translate what is printed. The conclusion in 7.37: Rama is Krishna Himself, who dances the rasa with Radha. This agrees with the Gaudiya reading of "Rama" in the mahamantra as Radha-ramana. Unclear readings: ବଧେଗ୍ଧ (printed ≈ ବ + ଧ + େ + a sign like ଭ/ଷ with a tail) is taken as *vaidagdhya*; ସ·ର as *sāra*; ଲାବଣ୍ୟ (?); ରାଧୟା (?), printed ≈ ସ୍ୱ + ଧ + ୟା. The last word is printed ସ୍ୱସ (= ରାସ), like the word before it; by sense and by 7.37 it is ରାମ.
[^ramante-7-32]: Shloka 7.32 is from the *Padma Purana*; Mahaprabhu quotes it in CC (Madhya 9.29), followed by the explanation of the name "Krishna" (*kṛṣir bhū-vācakaḥ…*, Madhya 9.30). There Mahaprabhu also says that one name of Krishna equals three names of Rama (Madhya 9.32–9.33). In Divakara the name of Rama is "the essence of all the Vedas" (7.36), Brahman and "the root support" (7.40). 7.33: "inaccessible to Hara (Shiva) and Viranchi (Brahma)" — printed ≈ ବ/ର + ର + ଞ୍ଚି + ଙ୍କ, OCR ବରଞ୍ଚ…; ଅଗମ is printed with a sign like ଃ at the end (ଅଗମ୍ୟ?). 7.34 ଅତି (?): ତି is blotted. 7.38: ଭାବନ୍ତି (?) — after ଭାବ there is a small faint sign; ରାମ (?) is printed ≈ ଘମ.
[^brahman-7-40]: 7.39–7.40: Rama is the form of sat-chit-ananda; "the formless Brahman", whose "root support" is the name of Sri Rama. The syntax of 7.40 is unclear; possibly: "therefore this Brahman is formless, [and] the name of Rama is the root support". For the Gaudiya Vaishnavas the impersonal Brahman is the effulgence of Krishna's body (*Brahma-samhita* 5.40; CC, Adi 2), and the Name is non-different from the Lord Himself and has form.
[^krishna-7-41]: The source of shloka 7.41 is unknown to us. Divakara explains it word by word: "bliss" (7.43–7.44); "Shriman" (7.45–7.46); "dark" and "lotus-eyed" (7.47–7.49); "Gokulananda" (7.51–7.52); "Ananda-ananda" (7.53); "Nandanandana" (7.56–7.57); "Ananda-nandana" (7.60). The compound *gokulānanda-nandanaḥ* ("the joy of Gokula, the son [of Nanda]"? "the son of the bliss of Gokula"?) is left untranslated: Divakara derives several names from it. The usual Gaudiya explanation of the name "Krishna" is different: *kṛṣi*, "being", and *ṇa*, "bliss" (CC, Madhya 9.30). 7.44 is understood tentatively.
[^lakshmi-7-45]: 7.45: Lakshmi is born from Radha's body, therefore Krishna is "Shriman", "possessor of Sri". This is close to Gaudiya teaching: the Lakshmis are expansions of Sri Radha (CC, Adi 4). ଶ୍ରୀମତ (?): printed ଶ୍ରୀ + a sign like ମ/ଦ with a hook + ତ; taken as ଶ୍ରୀମନ୍ତ. 7.46 ସହିତ (?): printed ≈ ସଖୀତ; if read ସଖୀ, 'friend', the sense is perhaps: "She (Lakshmi), having become Radha's friend…". 7.48 ପଦ୍ମଠୁଁ (?): printed ପ + ଦ with a blot (୍ମ) + a sign like ଠୁ/ଷୁ + ଁ; taken as a comparison, "[more beautiful] than the lotus".
[^gokula-7-51]: 7.51–7.53 explain "Gokulananda". The word after "go" is unidentified: printed ମ + a sign like ସ୍ତା + a bold sign like ଙ୍କ/କ୍ଷ + ର; possibly ମନ୍ତ୍ରାକ୍ଷର, "mantra syllable". In Sanskrit *go* means "cow", also "the senses", "speech, the Veda"; which sense Divakara intends is unclear. "Ananda-ananda" (7.53) may be a misprint for "Ananda-nandana" (cf. 7.60).
[^bvp-7-54]: The *Brahma-vaivarta Purana* (Sri Krishna-janma-khanda) contains syllable-by-syllable explanations of the name "Krishna". We have not checked shloka 7.41 against it. ଲେଖିଛି (?): printed ଲେଖି + ଛ with a subscript sign; possibly ଲେଖିଛନ୍ତି.
[^nandanandana-7-56]: 7.56–7.57 are understood tentatively: apparently the name "Nandanandana" ("son of Nanda") became famous when Krishna appeared to Nanda in the Dvapara-yuga. Yet in 7.58–7.59 Divakara says that the rishis, Shiva and Brahma contemplate the name of Krishna already in the Satya-yuga. For the Gaudiya Vaishnavas the Name is eternal and non-different from Krishna (*nāma cintāmaṇiḥ kṛṣṇaḥ*, *Padma Purana*; CC, Madhya 17.133). ନେତ୍ରକୁ (7.55, ?): printed େନ + a triangular sign ≈ ତ୍ର + କୁ. ଆଣି (7.56, ?): printed ଅ + a bold sign ≈ ଣି/ଶି; rhymes with ବାଣୀ. ବଚନ (7.59, ?): the first sign is like ଢ/ଵ.
[^nirukta-7-61]: 7.61–7.63: all words and all visible forms are the name of Krishna, Harinama. Cf. 7.27: the word "Hari" fills everything. For the Gaudiya Vaishnavas everything that exists is Krishna's energy, but the Name is not just any sound: it is transcendental (*aprakrita*) and non-different from Krishna (CC, Madhya 17.131–133). ନିରୁକ୍ତେ (7.61, ??): printed ନ + a sign like ରୁ/ବୃ + ତ୍ତ୍ରେ, OCR ଲଦ୍ଧନ୍ତେ; taken as "in the *Nirukta*" (Yaska's etymological work) or "in etymologies".
