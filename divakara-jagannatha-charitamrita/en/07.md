# Divakara Dasa. *Jagannatha-charitamrita*, Chapter 7
## Mahaprabhu with His Associates in the Jagamohana; the Gundicha Yatra and Bahuda

*Translated from Odia without a native speaker; doubtful places are marked (?) and (??). The original, transliteration and word-for-word translation are in `full/07.md`.*[^intro-7-1]

*Chapter Seven*

**7.1.** The 'Chaitanya-charitamrita': spoken from the mouth of Sri Chaitanya.[^charitamrita-7-1]  
**7.2.** This book the brahmin Divakara tells for the sake (?) of the saints.  
**7.3.** One day Lord Chaitanya graced the Sri jagamohana [with His presence].  
**7.4.** Gauridasa Pandita [was there] together with Matta Balarama.[^gauridas-7-4]  
**7.5.** The disciple Rupa (?), who is a Gosain, [and] Sri Sanatana Prabhu are here;  
**7.6.** Sri Raghunatha Bhatta is present; Sri Jiva (?) Svami is here;  
**7.7.** Sri Gopala Dasa is here; Sri Raghunatha Dasa is here.  
**7.8.** These six Gosains are here; [here too is] the Odia Kashi Mishra.[^six-7-5]  
**7.9.** In front is Sarvabhauma; Gopinatha Acharya is [also] here.  
**7.10.** Jagadananda Chakravarti; and Mukunda Datta is here.  
**7.11.** Sri Damodara Chakravarti; Sri Ramananda, who is [also] here.  
**7.12.** Sri Nityananda [and] Sri Advaita: both Prabhus are together [with Him].[^associates-7-9]  
**7.13.** Mahaprabhu Sri Chaitanya [takes] darshan of Sri Jagannatha:  
**7.14.** four circumambulations, twelve full prostrations.[^pradakshina-7-14]  
**7.15.** The Vaishnavas from the land of Gauda came before (??) the Gundicha.  
**7.16.** Having completed the nine-day yatra, they served (?) in the Gundicha procession.  
**7.17.** Bahuda, [the return] of the Sri Gundicha yatra: Jagannatha is [again] in Nila-kandara.  
**7.18.** On the Ekadashi of the bright fortnight of Ashadha all the Vaishnavas gathered,  
**7.19.** and all prostrated themselves near (?) Sri Prabhu Chaitanya.[^gundicha-7-15]  

## Mahaprabhu Explains Harinama: the Names "Hari", "Rama" and "Krishna"


**7.20.** Sri Ati Bada Jagannatha [Dasa] too made his prostration.[^intro-7-20]  
**7.21.** Nityananda and all the others gathered before Prabhu.  
**7.22.** They asked (?) about Harinama; Prabhu declared thus.[^harinama-7-22]  
**7.23.** First of all, then, in a great (??) shloka Prabhu established the virtues of the Name.  
**7.24.** The first is "Hari", two syllables: such is the command of Sri Chaitanya.  
**7.25.** O saints, listen: chant the name of Hari in bliss!  
**7.26.** Insects, birds and all other forms, all the unmoving and moving [beings] —  
**7.27.** in all of these, filling [them], abides the word "Hari".  
**7.28.** Whoever is by nature (??) a devotee becomes a Vaishnava by remembrance (?).[^smarana-7-28]  
**7.29.** Mere remembrance destroys the sin of the devotees;  
**7.30.** it destroys the ocean of sorrow: [one] easily attains the feet of Vishnu.  

*Shloka*  
**7.31.** *[Skt.]* "The essence of skill (??) in all (?), the embodied deity of beauty (?), who plays with Radha (?) in the rasa dance — He is called Rama (?)."[^rama-7-31]  
**7.32.** *[Skt.]* "The yogis delight in the Infinite, in the Truth-bliss, in the Spirit; therefore by the word 'Rama' the Supreme Brahman is called."[^ramante-7-32]  
**7.33.** Inaccessible to Hara and Viranchi (?) — such is the name of Rama.  
**7.34.** It is very (?) lovely and tender, the best of all.  
**7.35.** It is the source of all bhavas, melting in the bhava of the bliss of prema.  
**7.36.** In all the Vedas and the Vedanta the name of Sri Rama is the essence of all.  
**7.37.** Since He [dances] the rasa with Radha, therefore Krishna is called Rama.  
**7.38.** Eternally in the hearts of the yogis: in meditation they eternally contemplate (?) Rama (?).  
**7.39.** He whom we call Rama is the form of sat-chit-ananda.  
**7.40.** Therefore this is the formless Brahman; the name of Sri Rama is the root support.[^brahman-7-40]  

*Shloka*  
**7.41.** *[Skt.]* "He whose sole happiness is bliss, possessed of Sri, dark, lotus-eyed, Gokulananda-nandana — He is called Krishna."[^krishna-7-41]  
**7.42.** Sri Lord Chaitanya gave the explanation of the word "Krishna".  
**7.43.** The supreme bliss and happiness of all living beings is the name of Krishna.  
**7.44.** In bliss He is Bliss; in the form of Brahman He is chid-ananda.  
**7.45.** From the body of Radha Lakshmi is born; therefore He is called Shriman (?).[^lakshmi-7-45]  
**7.46.** He is with Radha (?): the Couple abide together.  
**7.47.** His body is dark and beautiful, like a blue sapphire.  
**7.48.** His eyes are more beautiful than the lotus (?); the Name is the essence that delivers the earth.  
**7.49.** Dark as durva grass, He in the form of Brahman is the primeval root.  
**7.50.** All the hosts of rishis in the universe perform the sankirtana of [the name of] Krishna.  
**7.51.** The word "go" — … (??); the word "kula" — [refers] to the gods.[^gokula-7-51]  
**7.52.** The bliss of the devotees' minds — by this word [He is] "Gokulananda".  
**7.53.** Since this is born of bliss, therefore the name is "Ananda-ananda".  
**7.54.** So it is written (?) in the *Brahma-vaivarta Purana*.[^bvp-7-54]  
**7.55.** To all the devotees there are, [He is] bliss for the eyes (?).  
**7.56.** The word "Nandanandana" — when we take (?) it and write it in the book —[^nandanandana-7-56]  
**7.57.** [means]: He who appeared (lit. 'was born') to Nanda in the Dvapara-yuga; from this the name will become famous.  
**7.58.** Then this word in the Vedas and the Vedanta is uttered by the rishis, Mahesha and Brahma;  
**7.59.** in Satya and the other yugas, for millions upon millions [of years], they contemplate that word (?): "Krishna".  
**7.60.** Since He appears (lit. "is born") from bliss, He is called "Ananda-nandana".  
**7.61.** As many words as speech utters, as many more as are in the *Nirukta* (??),[^nirukta-7-61]  
**7.62.** as many as you hear in the Vedas and the Vedanta — know all this to be the name of Krishna.  
**7.63.** As many forms as the eye sees — know this to be Harinama.  

## The Name "Hare Rama Krishna"; Prataparudra; Mahaprabhu Enters Jagannatha


**7.64.** From the Vedas to jest (?), all that is heard in this world,[^intro-7-64]  
**7.65.** true or untrue (?) speech: know that this is Harinama.[^harinama-7-64]  

*Shloka*

**7.66.** *[Skt.]* Let people with joy in their hearts remember the One worthy of praise (?), the cause of the destruction of ignorance and its effects, the beloved of His own Radha (?), whose form is a dark youth; let the great remember the One worthy of praise with greatness (?).[^shloka-7-66]  
**7.67.** And Prabhu Chaitanya further spoke, [when] they asked (?) about the name (??):  
**7.68.** "Whoever is joyful (??), great [and] saintly (?), remember the Name in this way (lit. 'perform the bhava of the Name').[^readings-7-67]  
**7.69.** Remember 'Hare Rama Krishna': [it] will destroy the season of ignorance.  
**7.70.** Avidya, births [and] karmas are destroyed by remembrance.  
**7.71.** In bliss, together with Radha, He eternally performs the rahasa (?):  
**7.72.** Sri Krishna enjoys with Radha; therefore He is called by the name 'Rama'.[^rama-7-71]  
**7.73.** The name of Sri Krishna is the form of sat-cit-ananda:  
**7.74.** a youthful, dark form; in rasa, an ocean (?) of beauty [and] love.  
**7.75.** The rasika devotees (?), in the fullness of rasa, will float in this ocean of rasa.  
**7.76.** This should be shown to rasika disciples; it should not be told to the disciples of others.[^rasika-7-76]  
**7.77.** The Vaishnava who has conquered his senses and taken refuge in the mind-delighting (?) Name,  
**7.78.** such ones know this rasa; others are not capable of it (?)."  
**7.79.** Having said this, Gaurahari departed to [His] residence.  
**7.80.** Thus Lord Chaitanya spoke in the Sri jagamohana.  
**7.81.** This account of the five shlokas came forth from the mouth of Sri Chaitanya.[^pancha-7-81]  
**7.82.** Thus the glory of Chaitanya, the limit of [all] virtues, became manifest:  
**7.83.** like the rays of the sun, it lit up the earth.  
**7.84.** Prataparudra, the best of kings, the lord of Puri, [possessed of] a perfected body (siddha-anga),  
**7.85.** was born in the world of mortals for the sake of accomplishing bhakti.[^prataparudra-7-84]  
**7.86.** He asked many Vaishnavas, but did not thereby gain an understanding of bhakti.  
**7.87.** From [the time of] the darshan (?), giving [Him] his mind, he served Lord Chaitanya.  
**7.88.** He said: "Instruct me in Hari-bhakti"; [so] the king [asked] from his heart (?).  
**7.89.** Chaitanya Gosain said: "In the rasa of a vishayi (?) there is no bhakti."[^vishayi-7-89]  
**7.90.** The best of kings reflected: "Right here I will attain bhakti."  
**7.91.** He served in many ways, [but] there was no permission from Mahaprabhu.  
**7.92.** One day, in disguise, the king went into the jagamohana;  
**7.93.** taking a broom in his hand, he swept, hidden (?) in [another's] garb.[^pahanra-7-93]  
**7.94.** At the morning (?) interval [between the services] Mahaprabhu arrived.  
**7.95.** Bowing down full length to Jagannatha, He joined both hands  
**7.96.** and, saying "Hare Rama Krishna", sang kirtan loudly.  
**7.97.** The king, who was nearby, said "Hare Rama Krishna."  
**7.98.** Hearing this, Sri Chaitanya was displeased: "A vishayi is touching the Name!  
**7.99.** This is not proper: by this bhakti grows weak."  
**7.100.** Hearing this, the king hurried and fell at Prabhu's feet:  
**7.101.** "O Lord! I heard [the Name] from Your holy mouth and took refuge (?) in the nectar of the Name.  
**7.102.** What bhava is in this Name, that it leads (?) into the body of Jagannatha?  
**7.103.** … (??) joining with me, deign (?) to give [me] the command with a loving heart."[^debaka-7-103]  
**7.104.** Hearing this, Chaitanya was pleased [and] looked (?) at Jagannatha Dasa:  
**7.105.** "O Ati Bada Jagannatha! Tell the king the truth of prema."[^atibada-7-105]  
**7.106.** Having said this, Sri Chaitanya entered (lit. "merged into") the body of Sri Jagannatha:  
**7.107.** whence He had come before, there, returning, He entered.[^lina-7-106]  
**7.108.** To establish bhakti in non-devotees, He showed bhakti to the devotees;  
**7.109.** leaving the virtue (?) of bhakti in the world, He departed to His own abode.[^end-7-109]  

## End of Chapter 7: Jagannatha Dasa's Bhakti; Divakara's Signature


**7.110.** In such (?) a bhava Jagannatha Dasa joyfully served that bhakti.[^intro-7-110]  
**7.111.** At that time [people] did not know bhakti; they said, "I am Brahman."[^brahma-7-111]  
**7.112.** They did not know the name of Sri Hari; they lived in the mood of rajas and tamas.  
**7.113.** [Then] Krishna appeared (lit. "was born") in the form of Chaitanya: giving up everything, worship (?) [Him] with bhakti.[^bhajata-7-113]  
**7.114.** Those feet of Krishna-Chaitanya are the refuge of the brahmana Divakara.  

*Thus [ends] in the Sri Jagannathamrita the seventh chapter, [the life] of Sri Ati Bada Jagannatha Dasa Gosain, called "Anukramani" (?).*[^colophon-7-114]

[^charitamrita-7-1]: 'Chaitanya-charitamrita' ('the nectar of Chaitanya's deeds') is what Divakara calls his narrative (or this part of it) here: 'spoken from the mouth of Sri Chaitanya'. Cf. 6.88–6.96, where Ati Bada records Mahaprabhu's teaching. This is not Krishnadasa Kaviraja's *Sri Chaitanya-charitamrita* (CC), to which we refer. In the colophons the poem is called *Sri Jagannathamrita*. ଅର୍ଥର (7.2) is taken as 'for the sake of' (gen. of *artha*, 'purpose, benefit'); perhaps 'the meaning [of the book]'.
[^gauridas-7-4]: Gauridasa Pandita and Matta Balarama (Balarama Dasa) are links in Divakara's guru-pranali: Gauridasa, Hridayananda, Balarama Dasa, Jagannatha Dasa (see the notes to 1.42 and 2.139). According to the CC (Adi 11), Gauridasa Pandita is an associate of Nityananda from Ambika-Kalna. Balarama Dasa is not mentioned in the CC or CB.
[^six-7-5]: 7.5–7.8: the six Gosains are Rupa, Sanatana, Raghunatha Bhatta, Jiva, Gopala Dasa (presumably Gopala Bhatta) and Raghunatha Dasa. Here the list agrees with the Gaudiya one. In 3.170–3.172 Nityananda is named instead of Raghunatha Bhatta (see the note to 3.170). But according to the CC, the six were never all together with Mahaprabhu in Puri. Rupa and Sanatana came to Puri (Antya 1; Antya 4), Raghunatha Bhatta twice (Antya 13). Raghunatha Dasa lived in Puri for many years (Antya 6). Mahaprabhu met Gopala Bhatta in the south, according to Gaudiya tradition. Jiva Gosvami, by the same tradition, never saw Mahaprabhu (see the note to 3.170). ରୂପକ, ଜୀବକ are names ending in -ka. This may be a suffix on the name or a genitive ('Rupa's disciple'?); but the six Gosains add up only with Rupa. Kashi Mishra is called 'the Odia' (*oṛiā*); Mahaprabhu lived in his house in Puri (see the note to 2.7).
[^associates-7-9]: Gopinatha Acharya is Sarvabhauma's brother-in-law (CC, Madhya 6). On Jagadananda Chakravarti see the note to 3.83. Mukunda Datta is a singer, an associate of Mahaprabhu since the Navadvipa days (CC, Adi 10). 'Damodara Chakravarti' has not been identified. He may be Svarupa Damodara or Damodara Pandita (CC, Adi 10); the title 'Chakravarti', as with Jagadananda, does not occur in the CC. Nityananda and Advaita, according to the CC, came to Puri with the Gaudiyas for the Ratha-yatra (Madhya 11; Madhya 16).
[^pradakshina-7-14]: Four circumambulations and twelve prostrations are the order of darshan in Divakara; we do not know his source. According to the CC (Antya 14), Mahaprabhu usually beheld Jagannatha from the Garuda-stambha in the natamandira, in front of the jagamohana.
[^gundicha-7-15]: 7.15–7.19 follow the yearly cycle of the Ratha-yatra. The Gaudiyas came to Puri before the Ratha-yatra and stayed for the four months of chaturmasya (CC, Madhya 11; Madhya 15; cf. the notes to 3.167 and 3.175). The 'nine-day yatra' (*nabadina yātrā*) lasts from the Ratha-yatra (the second lunar day of the bright fortnight of Ashadha) to Bahuda, the return procession on the tenth day. Nila-kandara is Puri (see the note to 3.137). The Ekadashi after Bahuda is Hari-shayana Ekadashi, the beginning of chaturmasya; on this day in Puri the Deities on the chariots are adorned with gold (Suna Besha). Who the subject of 7.16 is, the Gaudiyas or Jagannatha, is unclear. କୂଳେ (7.19, ?) is taken as 'near' (lit. 'on the bank'). The scene continues on p. 44.

[^rama-7-31]: Shloka 7.31 is a variant of a verse from Gaudiya explanations of the mahamantra: *vaidagdhya-sāra-sarvasvaṁ mūrta-līlādhidaivatam / rādhikāṁ ramayan nityaṁ rāma ity abhidhīyate* ("The whole wealth and essence of skill, the embodied presiding deity of the lilas, He ever delights Radhika — therefore He is called Rama"). Its source is unknown to us. The print has other words (*sarveṣu*, *lāvaṇya-devatā*, *rādhayā rāsa-keliko*); we translate what is printed. The conclusion in 7.37: Rama is Krishna Himself, who dances the rasa with Radha. This agrees with the Gaudiya reading of "Rama" in the mahamantra as Radha-ramana. The last word is printed ସ୍ୱସ (= ରାସ), like the word before it; by sense and by 7.37 it is ରାମ.
[^ramante-7-32]: Shloka 7.32 is from the *Padma Purana*; Mahaprabhu quotes it in CC (Madhya 9.29), followed by the explanation of the name "Krishna" (*kṛṣir bhū-vācakaḥ…*, Madhya 9.30). There Mahaprabhu also says that one name of Krishna equals three names of Rama (Madhya 9.32–9.33). In Divakara the name of Rama is "the essence of all the Vedas" (7.36), Brahman and "the root support" (7.40). 7.33: "inaccessible to Hara (Shiva) and Viranchi (Brahma)" — printed ≈ ବ/ର + ର + ଞ୍ଚି + ଙ୍କ, OCR ବରଞ୍ଚ…; ଅଗମ is printed with a sign like ଃ at the end (ଅଗମ୍ୟ?). 7.34 ଅତି (?): ତି is blotted. 7.38: ଭାବନ୍ତି (?) — after ଭାବ there is a small faint sign; ରାମ (?) is printed ≈ ଘମ.
[^brahman-7-40]: 7.39–7.40: Rama is the form of sat-chit-ananda; "the formless Brahman", whose "root support" is the name of Sri Rama. The syntax of 7.40 is unclear; possibly: "therefore this Brahman is formless, [and] the name of Rama is the root support". For the Gaudiya Vaishnavas the impersonal Brahman is the effulgence of Krishna's body (*Brahma-samhita* 5.40; CC, Adi 2), and the Name is non-different from the Lord Himself and has form.
[^krishna-7-41]: The source of shloka 7.41 is unknown to us. Divakara explains it word by word: "bliss" (7.43–7.44); "Shriman" (7.45–7.46); "dark" and "lotus-eyed" (7.47–7.49); "Gokulananda" (7.51–7.52); "Ananda-ananda" (7.53); "Nandanandana" (7.56–7.57); "Ananda-nandana" (7.60). The compound *gokulānanda-nandanaḥ* ("the joy of Gokula, the son [of Nanda]"? "the son of the bliss of Gokula"?) is left untranslated: Divakara derives several names from it. The usual Gaudiya explanation of the name "Krishna" is different: *kṛṣi*, "being", and *ṇa*, "bliss" (CC, Madhya 9.30). 7.44 is understood tentatively.
[^lakshmi-7-45]: 7.45: Lakshmi is born from Radha's body, therefore Krishna is "Shriman", "possessor of Sri". This is close to Gaudiya teaching: the Lakshmis are expansions of Sri Radha (CC, Adi 4). ଶ୍ରୀମତ (?): printed ଶ୍ରୀ + a sign like ମ/ଦ with a hook + ତ; taken as ଶ୍ରୀମନ୍ତ. 7.46 ସହିତ (?): printed ≈ ସଖୀତ; if read ସଖୀ, 'friend', the sense is perhaps: "She (Lakshmi), having become Radha's friend…". 7.48 ପଦ୍ମଠୁଁ (?): printed ପ + ଦ with a blot (୍ମ) + a sign like ଠୁ/ଷୁ + ଁ; taken as a comparison, "[more beautiful] than the lotus".
[^gokula-7-51]: 7.51–7.53 explain "Gokulananda". The word after "go" is unidentified: printed ମ + a sign like ସ୍ତା + a bold sign like ଙ୍କ/କ୍ଷ + ର; possibly ମନ୍ତ୍ରାକ୍ଷର, "mantra syllable". In Sanskrit *go* means "cow", also "the senses", "speech, the Veda"; which sense Divakara intends is unclear. "Ananda-ananda" (7.53) may be a misprint for "Ananda-nandana" (cf. 7.60).
[^bvp-7-54]: The *Brahma-vaivarta Purana* (Sri Krishna-janma-khanda) contains syllable-by-syllable explanations of the name "Krishna". We have not checked shloka 7.41 against it. ଲେଖିଛି (?): printed ଲେଖି + ଛ with a subscript sign; possibly ଲେଖିଛନ୍ତି.
[^nandanandana-7-56]: 7.56–7.57 are understood tentatively: apparently the name "Nandanandana" ("son of Nanda") became famous when Krishna appeared to Nanda in the Dvapara-yuga. Yet in 7.58–7.59 Divakara says that the rishis, Shiva and Brahma contemplate the name of Krishna already in the Satya-yuga. For the Gaudiya Vaishnavas the Name is eternal and non-different from Krishna (*nāma cintāmaṇiḥ kṛṣṇaḥ*, *Padma Purana*; CC, Madhya 17.133). ନେତ୍ରକୁ (7.55, ?): printed େନ + a triangular sign ≈ ତ୍ର + କୁ. ଆଣି (7.56, ?): printed ଅ + a bold sign ≈ ଣି/ଶି; rhymes with ବାଣୀ. ବଚନ (7.59, ?): the first sign is like ଢ/ଵ.
[^nirukta-7-61]: 7.61–7.63: all words and all visible forms are the name of Krishna, Harinama. Cf. 7.27: the word "Hari" fills everything. For the Gaudiya Vaishnavas everything that exists is Krishna's energy, but the Name is not just any sound: it is transcendental (*aprakrita*) and non-different from Krishna (CC, Madhya 17.131–133). ନିରୁକ୍ତେ (7.61, ??): printed ନ + a sign like ରୁ/ବୃ + ତ୍ତ୍ରେ, OCR ଲଦ୍ଧନ୍ତେ; taken as "in the *Nirukta*" (Yaska's etymological work) or "in etymologies".
[^intro-7-64]: PDF pages 46–47 (printed pp. 45–46). Continuation of 7.20–7.63: on pp. 44–45 Mahaprabhu in the jagamohana explains the names "Hari", "Rama" and "Krishna". Here the explanation ends (7.64–7.78) and Mahaprabhu goes to His residence (7.79). Then come the account of Prataparudra (7.84–7.105) and of how Mahaprabhu enters Jagannatha (7.106–7.109). Chapter 7 ends on printed p. 47 (PDF 48). There is one shloka (7.66); the unnumbered line is I3 ("Shloka"). Features of the type: ି is often invisible (ସ୍ମରିବ, ଅବିଦ୍ୟା, ନିତ୍ୟେ, ମୂରତି, ନିକଟରେ, ଦିନେକ, etc.); such ି are given without brackets. ରା resembles ସ୍ୱ (ରାଧା, ରାଜା, ରାହାସ, ମନୋଭିରାମ, ପହଁରା).
[^harinama-7-64]: 7.64–7.65 complete the thought of 7.62–7.63: all that the eye sees and the ear hears is Harinama. Gaudiya Vaishnavas teach otherwise: the name of Krishna is non-different from Krishna Himself (CC, Madhya 17.133). But not every sound in the world is Harinama. There is a distinction between the pure name, namabhasa and nama-aparadha (CC, Antya 3). Unclear readings. ହାସ୍ୟ (7.64, ?): the first letter resembles both ହ and ଦ; ଦାସ୍ୟ ('service') does not fit the sense. ଅସଦ୍ (7.65, ?): a stroke is printed over ଦ.
[^shloka-7-66]: The shloka is set in four lines (dandas after the 2nd and 4th). The metre is upajati (the eleven-syllable indravajra and upendravajra). The metre helps the reading. The second line is indravajra, so its 3rd syllable is short: *sva-rādhā-ramaṇaṁ* (printed ≈ ସ୍ୱସ୍ୱଧାରମଣଂ; ରା resembles ସ୍ୱ). The word *īḍyaṁ* (printed ≈ ଈ + ତ/ଡ୍ୟ + ଂ, twice) gives the required two long syllables. Corrections: ହେତୁଃ → *hetum* (acc., agreeing with *īḍyaṁ*); ପୁଂମାସଃ → *pumāṁsaḥ*. ମହତା is perhaps = *mahatāṁ* ('by the great'). "The cause of the destruction of ignorance" is probably an explanation of the name "Hari" (He who "takes away", *harati*, ignorance; cf. 7.24 and the note to 7.22). Cf. the explanations of "Rama" (7.31–7.32) and "Krishna" (7.41). We have not identified the source of the shloka.
[^readings-7-67]: 7.67–7.68 are poorly read. ଜିନାମ (??): printed ଜି + ନାମ ('name'); perhaps a corruption of ନିଜ ନାମ, 'one's own name'. ପୁଛିଲ (?) is taken as 'asked'. ଯେହୁଷ୍ଟ (??): printed ଯେ + ହୁ/ଦୁ + ଷ୍ଟ. Variants: ଯେ ହୃଷ୍ଟ, 'who are joyful', or ଯେ ଦୁଷ୍ଟ, 'whoever [is] wicked'. The metre (9 syllables) allows both. ସାଧବ is taken as 'sadhus' (?). 'Will accomplish' (fut.) is also possible; the usual meaning of the Odia word is 'merchant'.
[^rama-7-71]: "Rama" is "He who enjoys (*ramate*) with Radha". Cf. 7.37 and the note to 7.31: this agrees with the Gaudiya explanation of "Rama" in the mahamantra as Radha-ramana. But the order "Hare Rama Krishna" (7.69, 7.96) is an Odia custom; Gaudiya Vaishnavas, following Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati, begin the mahamantra with "Hare Krishna" (see the notes to 3.147 and 7.22). *Rāhāsa* (?) is taken as *rahasa*, 'the secret pastimes' (printed ≈ ସ୍ୱହାସ). Perhaps it is ରାସ, 'the rasa-lila', as in 7.37.
[^rasika-7-76]: An instruction to keep the teaching secret. It is shown only to "rasika disciples", not to "the disciples of others". This is how the Atibadi line speaks, setting itself apart from other Vaishnavas (cf. 6.100: the book is "the wealth of the svakiya Vaishnavas"). Gaudiya Vaishnavas also enjoin hearing of the pastimes of Radha and Krishna from a pure devotee and not from the unqualified (cf. CC, Madhya 8). But for them Mahaprabhu's teaching is open to all: He ordered the Name to be preached in every town and village (CC, Adi 9; CB, Antya 4). Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati counts the "ativadi" among the deviant communities (apasampradayas). *Pāraku nuhanti* (7.78, ?) means 'are not capable' (from ପାରକ).
[^pancha-7-81]: "The account of the five shlokas": so Divakara calls Mahaprabhu's teaching in the jagamohana (7.20–7.80). There are four shlokas set in the chapter: 7.31, 7.32, 7.41 and 7.66. How Divakara counts five is unclear to us; perhaps he also counts the shloka mentioned in 7.23 (see the note to 7.22). Cf. the title "Chaitanya-charitamrita, spoken from the mouth of Sri Chaitanya" (7.1).
[^prataparudra-7-84]: Prataparudra was the Gajapati of Orissa, a contemporary of Mahaprabhu. Divakara calls him "the lord of Puri" and "[possessed of] a perfected body", come into the world for the sake of bhakti. According to the CC, Prataparudra was a great devotee who received Mahaprabhu's mercy (Madhya 11–14). What follows (7.86–7.105) is an Odia version of the CC account, with divergences (see below). Cf. VL 2.121, 5.7, 9.119, 9.149. *Darśana hantu* (7.87, ?) is taken as 'from [the time of] the darshan'. ହନ୍ତୁ is perhaps a Middle Odia postposition 'from'. ନୃପ ଚିତ୍ତେ (7.88, ?): printed ≈ ନୃପ + ଚ + ତ୍ତେ; cf. ଚିତ୍ତେ in 7.103.
[^vishayi-7-89]: According to the CC (Madhya 11.3–11), Mahaprabhu would not agree to see the king: for a renunciant, meeting a *viṣayī* ('a man engaged in worldly affairs') is worse than drinking poison (Madhya 11.8). Here Mahaprabhu says the same. The reading ବିଷୟୀ (?) is supported by ବିଷୟୀରେ in 7.98; in 7.89 the last sign is printed unclearly (ୀ or ୁ). If ବିଷୟୁ, then 'in the rasa of sense objects there is no bhakti'.
[^pahanra-7-93]: *Pahãrā* is a broom, sweeping. Cf. the chhera-panhara, a rite of the Gajapati: at the Ratha-yatra he sweeps the chariots with a golden broom (VL 2.121, 5.7; CC, Madhya 13.15–16). According to the CC, Mahaprabhu was pleased by this service of the king. Later the king, in the simple dress of a Vaishnava, came to Mahaprabhu in a garden near Gundicha, began to recite the "Gopi-gita" and was embraced (Madhya 14). In Divakara it is different: the king in disguise sweeps the jagamohana, Mahaprabhu comes there and sings "Hare Rama Krishna", and the king repeats after Him. ଲୁଚିତ (?) is taken as 'hidden' (from ଲୁଚିବା, 'to hide'). ପ୍ରଭାତ (7.94, ?): printed ପ୍ରଭ + ତ, ା unclear.
[^debaka-7-103]: Unclear readings in 7.101–7.104. ଅଶ୍ରେ (7.101, ?) is taken as ଆଶ୍ରେ, 'refuge'. ଭେଦାଇ (7.102, ?) is taken as 'leads into' (from ଭେଦିବା, 'to penetrate'). [^atibada-7-105]: Mahaprabhu charges Ati Bada Jagannatha (Jagannatha Dasa) with teaching the king "the truth of prema". Thus Divakara makes Jagannatha Dasa the successor of Mahaprabhu's teaching; cf. 6.88–6.94 and the note to 6.90. According to the CC, the king was brought to Mahaprabhu by Sarvabhauma and Ramananda Raya (Madhya 11–14), while Mahaprabhu Himself heard the teaching on prema from Ramananda and revealed it to Svarupa Damodara and to Rupa and Sanatana. Jagannatha Dasa is not mentioned in the CC or CB. For the position of the Gaudiya Vaishnavas (Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati; the "ativadi" are in the list of apasampradayas), see the note to 6.90. ଅତେବଡ → ଅତିବଡ଼, as in 6.99.
[^lina-7-106]: Mahaprabhu's departure in Divakara: having spoken His last words, He "merged (*līna*) into the body of Sri Jagannatha" and returned whence He had come. This is an Odia tradition; cf. VL 9.38, 9.80 and 9.120 (in 9.120 these are the words Rama Raya proposes to announce to the people; Madhava himself tells of the departure differently, VL 9.42–9.128). The position of the Gaudiya Vaishnavas (Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati): the Lord's body is sat-cit-ananda, and His departure is a disappearance (antardhana) by His own will (cf. BG 4.9; the notes to VL 9.42 and 9.90). The CC does not describe the departure: its narrative ends with the "Shikshashtaka" (Antya 20). According to Lochana Dasa's *Chaitanya-mangala*, Mahaprabhu entered the Deity of Jagannatha; according to another tradition, Tota-Gopinatha. For Gaudiya Vaishnavas Sri Chaitanya is Krishna Himself (CC, Adi 1), non-different from Jagannatha. But they do not accept Divakara's teaching on the tattva of Jagannatha and Chaitanya (cf. Ch. 6).

[^brahma-7-111]: "I am Brahman" (*muhi̐ brahma*, cf. Skt. *ahaṁ brahmāsmi*) is for Divakara a mark of the age before Sri Chaitanya's appearance: the impersonalist (Mayavada) self-identification with Brahman. Gaudiya Vaishnavas, following Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati, likewise see Mahaprabhu's preaching as a refutation of Mayavada (cf. the dialogue with Sarvabhauma, CC, Madhya 6, and with Prakashananda, CC, Adi 7); here Divakara agrees with them.
[^bhajata-7-113]: *Bhajata* is formally an imperative plural ("worship"), but may be indicative ("they worship"); then: "all, giving up [the former ways], worship with bhakti." *Kṛṣṇa caitanya rūpe jāta* is taken as "Krishna appeared in the form of Chaitanya"; for Gaudiya Vaishnavas Sri Chaitanya is Krishna Himself in the mood of Radha (CC, Adi 1.5; 4).
[^colophon-7-114]: The colophon is set in two lines, as at the end of chapters 1–6 (cf. the notes on the colophons of chs. 5 and 6). Printed ଅତବଡ଼ (‑i not printed; cf. ଅତେବଡ in 6.99, 7.105) is taken as ଅତିବଡ଼; ଅନୁକର୍ମଣି as ଅନୁକ୍ରମଣି ("Anukramani", as in the previous chapters).
