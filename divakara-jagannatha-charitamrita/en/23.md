# Divakara Dasa. *Jagannatha-charitamrita*, Chapter 23
## The Glory of Beholding the Four Deities: the Snana-yatra, the Chariot and Gundicha

*Translated from Odia without a native speaker; doubtful places are marked (?) and (??). The original, transliteration and word-for-word translation are in `full/23.md`.*[^intro-23-1]

*Chapter Twenty-Three*

**23.1.** The brahmana Divakara says: O good people, listen [to this account], full of rasa.  
**23.2.** That Sri Ati Bada Gosain again told his disciples this:[^atibada-23-2]  
**23.3.** "Chaitanya Mahaprabhu was [here]. He too cherished this joy.  
**23.4.** The supreme, infinite form that (?) abides in the shape of wood (daru) —  
**23.5.** in Sri Baladeva, in Subhadra, in Jagannatha, in Sudarshana.  
**23.6.** Whoever takes darshan of the fourfold form, blessed is his life.[^chaturdha-23-6]  
**23.7.** [Their] darshan purifies the fallen, and also [their] wives and sons.  
**23.8.** The Lotus-eyed One who is Tripura (?) is eternally called by the name Lalita.[^tripura-23-8]  
**23.9.** If you serve her, you will be able to abide (??) in eternal service.  

*(In the Vishnu Purana.)*[^vishnu-23-10]

**23.10.** Brahma told the rishis about Prabhu's Snana-yatra:[^snana-23-10]  
**23.11.** whoever, saying 'Jaya, Rama, Krishna!', offers praise with his mouth,  
**23.12.** he dwells in Brahmaloka and sees not a trace (?) of sorrow.  
**23.13.** Whoever joyfully beholds the bathing of the three Deities,  
**23.14.** he, taking twenty-one generations of his family [with him], dwells happily in the eternal abode.  
**23.15.** If by fate a person takes darshan of the fourfold form,  
**23.16.** that being, taking a hundred generations [of his forefathers], [attains] what is desired in the eternal.  
**23.17.** When Bhagavan, having finished His bath, goes forth in the form [turned] to the south (?) —[^dakshina-23-17]  
**23.18.** whatever beings see this attain sayujya liberation.[^sayujya-23-18]  
**23.19.** As much [fruit] as a man has who has circumambulated the seven earths three times,  
**23.20.** that much fruit he attains who sees the form [turned] to the south.  
**23.21.** The darshan of Badari-kshetra, the plunge (?) at Ganga-sagara,  
**23.22.** as much merit as [comes] from giving gifts at the time (?) of a lunar and a solar eclipse,  
**23.23.** the fruit of austerity, homa and japa — [all this] that being obtains at once.  
**23.24.** (??) The beings who behold the great (?) Gundicha there (?),[^gundicha-23-24]  
**23.25.** who see [it] even once in a month (?), are delivered from the fever of sufferings,  
**23.26.** obtain the happiness of supreme bliss and (?) attain ninefold bhakti.[^navadha-23-26]  
**23.27.** The man who serves seven days at the house of Gundicha —  
**23.28.** those, serving and serving, attain ninefold bhakti.  
**23.29.** Brahma told the rishis the glory of the Gundicha-yatra.  
**23.30.** [Even] the Primal One (?) does not know its virtues — how then can they be told in a composition?[^adye-23-30]  
**23.31.** The being who utters (??) the three syllables 'Gun-di-cha' —[^kalyati-23-31]  
**23.32.** however many sins there are in his family line, they are destroyed by saying [it] but once.  
**23.33.** Whoever beholds [the Lord] mounted on the chariot is blessed among the Vaishnavas.  
**23.34.** Following the chariot even for a moment, one attains bhakti of eighty-one kinds (?).[^ekashi-23-34]  
**23.35.** On whose body the dust of the chariot flies up and falls — as many [specks] as fall, if one counts them,  
**23.36.** for that many yugas he serves in the company of Krishna.  
**23.37.** Whoever is crushed (?) beneath (?) the chariot, he, in [one] breath out and in,[^ratha-23-37]  
**23.38.** attains the fruit of the ashvamedha; who can tell what he gains (?)?  
**23.39.** If his forefathers are in heaven (?), seeing him they are delivered from hell;[^pitri-23-39]  
**23.40.** pushing aside Yama's messengers, they go to the world of Vishnu.  

*(In the Skanda Purana.)*[^skanda-23-41]

**23.41.** Bhagavan spoke these words to Indradyumna:  
**23.42.** 'Twenty-four (?) crores of tirthas [and] Pushkara are [here], O Indradyumna.[^caubashi-23-42]  
**23.43.** Whoever beholds My Gundicha there for nine days,  
**23.44.** that being at once attains [the fruit of bathing in] the waters of twenty-four (?) crores of tirthas.  
**23.45.** [Whoever] sees Me in the house of Gundicha casts off (??) all sorrows.[^upekhe-23-45]  
**23.46.** As much fruit as comes in a day — (??) one attains tenfold.  

[^intro-23-1]: The beginning of Chapter 23: PDF pages 140–141 (printed pp. 139–140). On p. 140 there is only the running head above the chapter title. The end of Chapter 22 with its colophon is on the preceding page (PDF 139) and is not included here. The portion has 46 couplets and no Sanskrit shlokas. Two unnumbered lines name sources: "(In the *Vishnu Purana*.)" before 23.10 and "(In the *Skanda Purana*.)" before 23.41. The last quotation continues on p. 141 (PDF 142). Contrary to expectation, these pages do not tell of Jagannatha Dasa's initiation. Here Ati Bada Gosain (Jagannatha Dasa) instructs his disciples on the glory of beholding the four Deities of Puri. Then follow Puranic *mahatmyas* of the Snana-yatra, the Ratha-yatra and Gundicha. Features of the type are as in earlier chapters. ି is often a stroke or invisible; such ି are given without brackets. ତୁ resembles ଭୁ (ଚତୁର୍ଦ୍ଧା 23.6, 23.15). ରା/ରୀ resemble ସ୍ୱ (ତ୍ରିପୁରା 23.8, ବଦରୀ 23.21). ତ resembles ନ (ତପସ୍ୟା 23.23). The subscript ୍ର is often detached and printed as a stroke under the line (ବ୍ରହ୍ମା, ଚନ୍ଦ୍ର, ଇନ୍ଦ୍ର). «॥» sometimes looks like a bold dot.
[^atibada-23-2]: Ati Bada Gosain is Jagannatha Dasa (see the note to 1.20). From 23.3 he is the speaker; where his speech ends is not visible on these pages. In 23.3 Divakara links the worship of Jagannatha in Puri with Mahaprabhu. This agrees with the CC: Mahaprabhu beheld Jagannatha daily and took part in the Snana-yatra, the Gundicha-marjana and the Ratha-yatra (Madhya 11–14).
[^chaturdha-23-6]: On the Chaturdha-murti, the four Deities of the altar (Balabhadra, Subhadra, Jagannatha, Sudarshana), see the note to 4.25.
[^tripura-23-8]: Judging by her place in the list, the "Lotus-eyed One" is apparently Subhadra. Divakara identifies her with Tripura and Lalita: Lalita-Tripurasundari is a name of the Goddess in the Shakta tradition. This is a Shakta feature of the Odia temple worship of Subhadra. For the Gaudiya Vaishnavas, Subhadra is Krishna's sister, a manifestation of His internal energy; Lalita is the foremost of Sri Radha's principal sakhis, not Tripurasundari. The reading ତ୍ରିପୁରା: printed ତ with a subscript ୍ର and the arc ି, then ପୁ and a sign like ସ୍ୱ (= ରା). In 23.9 the word before ପାର is unread. It is taken as ତିଷ୍ଠି, 'to abide'; perhaps ଚିହ୍ନି ('to recognize') or ତରି ('to cross over').
[^vishnu-23-10]: The references "In the *Vishnu Purana*" and "In the *Skanda Purana*" (before 23.41) are the author's; we have not checked them. The *mahatmyas* of the Snana-yatra, the Ratha-yatra and Gundicha are told at length in the *Purushottama-kshetra-mahatmya* of the *Skanda Purana*. We know of no such accounts in the *Vishnu Purana*.
[^snana-23-10]: The Snana-yatra is the bathing of the Deities on the full moon of the month of Jyeshtha (cf. VL 8.83 and its note). ଋଷିଙ୍କି (*ṛṣiṅki*, also 23.29) is an archaic dative form, 'to the rishis'. "Jaya, Rama, Krishna" (23.11) is an exclamation on beholding the bathing.
[^dakshina-23-17]: "The form [turned] to the south" (*dakṣiṇa mūrati*, also 23.20) is a tentative interpretation. By the sense, it is some procession of the Lord after the Snana-yatra. Perhaps it is the turning of the chariots to the south on the way back from Gundicha ("Dakshina-moda" before the Bahuda-yatra). Or perhaps something else, such as the Lord in anavasara. To be checked with a specialist.
[^sayujya-23-18]: Divakara promises sayujya, liberation by merging with the Lord, as the fruit of darshan. The Gaudiya Vaishnavas reject it: see the note to 5.26 and the *Bhagavata* 3.29.13. In 23.26 and 23.28, by contrast, the fruit of beholding is said to be bhakti.
[^gundicha-23-24]: Gundicha is the temple north-east of the main temple of Puri, to which the Deities ride on their chariots; the chariot festival is also called the "Gundicha-yatra" (see the note to 7.15; cf. VL 5.75 and its note). According to the CC (Madhya 12–14), Mahaprabhu and the devotees cleansed the Gundicha temple before the Ratha-yatra, and He danced before the chariot. The first two words of 23.24 are blotted. Printed ସ + a blot + ା and ମ + a blot + ା; tentatively taken as ସଦା (?) ମହା, 'always (?) the great'. The last word (ତହିଁ, 'there') is also doubtful. In 23.25 ମାସକ (?) is taken as 'in a month'.
[^navadha-23-26]: Ninefold bhakti means hearing, chanting and the other forms (*Bhagavata* 7.5.23–24); see the note to 6.81. In 23.28 ଭକତ (*bhakata*) is taken as *bhakti*: 'ninefold bhakti'. Before ଲଭନ୍ତି in 23.26 stands a bold sign, taken as ସେ ('they').
[^adye-23-30]: ଅଦ୍ୟେ (ଅ = ଆ, *ādye*) is taken as '[even] the Primal One': Brahma (or the Lord) does not know all the virtues of Gundicha. 'First of all' is also possible; cf. ଅଦ୍ୟେ in 7.23–7.24.
[^kalyati-23-31]: The word before ଉଚ୍ଚାର is unread. Printed କ + ଲ with a subscript (≈ ୍ୟ) + a sign like '8' with a hook. The same sign is in ଲେଶଟି (23.12, 'not a trace'). It is apparently the unidentified sign of 6.61–6.67 and 7.28 (note to 7.28); here it probably reads ଟି. The sense of the half-line is clear: 'whoever utters [the word] "Gundicha"'.
[^ekashi-23-34]: ଏକାଶୀ (*ekāśī*) means 'eighty-one'. Perhaps the nine kinds of bhakti are meant, each in nine varieties (9 × 9). ମତ (*mata*) is taken as 'kind, sort'. In Gaudiya teaching, the *Bhakti-rasamrita-sindhu* (1.2) lists 64 angas of bhakti.
[^ratha-23-37]: The reading is uncertain. ତଳରେ ('beneath') is printed as a bold blot + ଳ + ରେ. ଚମ୍ପଇ ('is crushed, pressed down'?) is printed ଚ + ମ with a subscript + ଇ. If the reading is right, Divakara praises one who perishes under the chariot. Cf. VL 5.76, where Sanatana throws himself under the chariot. According to the CC (Antya 4), Mahaprabhu forbade Sanatana to give up his body in this way: it is an act in the mode of ignorance, and Krishna is not attained so, but only by bhajana. ଉଃଶ୍ୱାସ (23.37) is a spelling of *ucchwāsa*, 'breathing in'.
[^pitri-23-39]: Literally: "if his forefathers abide in heaven (*swarge*), seeing him they are delivered from hell". The logic is unclear: one expects "if his forefathers are in hell". Perhaps ସ୍ୱର୍ଗେ is used broadly here for 'in the other world'. The reading ସ୍ୱର୍ଗେ is certain. ଯମଲୋକଙ୍କୁ (23.40) means 'the people of Yama', that is, his messengers (yamadutas).
[^skanda-23-41]: Indradyumna is the king who, according to the Puranic legend, established the worship of Jagannatha (see the note to 4.91). According to the tradition of the *Skanda Purana*, the Lord promised Indradyumna to come every year to the site of his sacrifices, at Gundicha. "Nine days" (23.43) is the time the Deities stay in the Gundicha temple, from the Ratha-yatra to the Bahuda-yatra (the return procession).
[^caubashi-23-42]: Printed ଚୌବଶୀ (*caubaśī*; in 23.44 the sign after ଚୌ is blotted). Taken as ଚଉବିଶି, 'twenty-four', with an invisible ି. It may be ଚଉରାଶୀ, 'eighty-four', but the sign clearly resembles ବ, not ସ୍ୱ (= ରା). Pushkara is a famous tirtha (a lake near Ajmer). According to Divakara, all the tirthas and Pushkara abide in Puri (cf. VL 1.2: "You made all the tirthas one").
[^upekhe-23-45]: The last words of 23.45 and 23.46 are unread. In 23.45 it is printed ଉ + a sign like ତ୍ + ପ + ଖେ (rhyming with ଦେଖେ). It is taken as ଉପେଖେ, 'disregards, casts off'; perhaps ଉତ୍ପାଟେ ('uproots'). In 23.46, before ଦଶଗୁଣ stands ସ + େ + a sign like ହ (ସହେ?). By the sense one expects 'there' or 'at night' (ରାତ୍ରେ?): "as much fruit as comes in a day [elsewhere], here one attains tenfold". Continued on p. 141 (PDF 142).
