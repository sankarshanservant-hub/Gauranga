# Divakara Dasa. *Jagannatha-charitamrita*, Chapter 6
## The Murti of Jagannatha, the Lord above the Lord: the Lotus of the *Brahma-samhita*, Goloka in the Puri Temple, Mantra, Yantra and Tantra

*Translated from Odia without a native speaker; doubtful places are marked (?) and (??). The original, transliteration and word-for-word translation are in `full/06.md`.*[^intro-6-1]

*Chapter Six*

**6.1.** Listen, good people, to the teaching of Sri Chaitanya.  
**6.2.** At the darshan of Sri Jagannatha, Prabhu abides with a contented heart.  
**6.3.** All the Vaishnavas put a question to Prabhu:  
**6.4.** "Here is the murti of Jagannatha: by what feeling (?) does one obtain prema-bhakti [in Him]?"  
**6.5.** Hearing these words, Gosain said: "Listen attentively.  

*Shloka*  
**6.6.** *[Skt.]* The supreme Lord is Krishna; His form is eternal being, knowledge and bliss. He is beginningless and Himself the origin of all; He is Govinda, the cause of all causes.[^ishvara-6-6]  
**6.7.** The Lord above the Lord, beyond the supreme, the Highest;  
**6.8.** this is Krishna above Krishna, Brahman above Brahman;  
**6.9.** the Beginningless above the beginningless, the cause of all, the supreme perfection (?).  
**6.10.** Sri Jagannatha, who [is] this murti: from Him indeed (?) is the origin (?) of all.[^upare-6-7]  

*Shloka*  
**6.11.** *[Skt.]* In the whorl (?) [of the lotus] is the central yantra, hexagonal, with a diamond pivot; the place of the six-limbed, six-footed (??) [mantra], with prakriti and purusha. In the midst of the yantra (?) of the great lotus one should practise the hexagonal yantra.  
**6.12.** The place of six limbs and six feet: Radha and Krishna on one seat.  
**6.13.** Here, in the earthly world (?), They abide; … (??) having divided (?): of that I speak.  

*Shloka*  
**6.14.** *[Skt.]* The world (?) abides in the rasa of the bliss of prema and the great bliss, joined with the mantra (?) in the form of light, with the kama-bija.[^samhita-6-11]  
**6.15.** The bliss of prema and the great bliss become bliss through rasa.  
**6.16.** These two, being in the form of light, abide in the kama-bija.  
**6.17.** The beginning and end (?) of this shloka, that 'surrounded' (??), I shall tell further on.[^vrita-6-17]  

*Shloka*  
**6.18.** *[Skt.]* Let the placing of its bija (?) be fourfold (??); there are the petals, [the abodes] of the Shris (?). They are the four murtis, fourfold (??), inconceivable (??), supreme, 'pluta'.[^bija-6-18]  
**6.19.** Amid the filaments of that lotus, however many petals are in it,  
**6.20.** in the four quarters (?) are dharma, moksha [and the other goals]: these They grant visibly.  
**6.21.** Thus the Chaturdha-murti: the four abide here.[^chaturdha-6-21]  
**6.22.** Goloka is nitya, [then] maha-nitya; in parama-nitya the four (?) abide.  
**6.23.** The Bada-danda is called Goloka; it is also the twenty-two steps (?).  
**6.24.** Maha-nitya is the jagamohana; parama-nitya is the throne.[^baisi-6-23]  
**6.25.** That which is called 'fourfold prolonged' (chatuh-pluta) is (??) in the svaras of the Veda (?).[^pluta-6-25]  
**6.26.** These words of prema … (??) Sri Chaitanya has spoken.[^vani-6-26]  

*Shloka*  
**6.27.** *[Skt.]* The essence of mantra, the essence of yantra, the essence of tantra, the abode; … (??) and the essence of prema, and the brahma-vidya Shodashi.[^shodashi-6-27]  
**6.28.** In the mantra the essence is the bija of the mantra; in the yantra, the Sri Murti in full (?).  
**6.29.** In the tantra is Sri Jagannatha: such is the place declared (??) by all.  
**6.30.** However many deeds one does with the body, however many vows (?) one keeps on earth,  
**6.31.** the aim of deeds at the end of the body: this is therefore (?) called tattva (?).  
**6.32.** Whoever meditates on this Jagannatha thereby (?) obtains the tantra fully.[^tantra-6-28]  
**6.33.** In this murti is prema itself; in Him is the play of rasa.  
**6.34.** This is Brahman manifest, [the true] Brahman; He bears the sixteen kalas.  
**6.35.** Taking all these kalas, They form the array (?) of the body (?).[^kala-6-34]  

*Shloka*  
**6.36.** *[Skt.]* The yantra and tantra of the bliss of prema, adorned with the mandala of prema; in the tasting of prema the Devi … (??) Vipureshvara (??).[^prema-6-36]  

## The murti of Jagannatha as the source of avataras, tirthas and Vedas; Omkara, Shesha and Subhadra; Radha and Krishna in the daru-murti


**6.37.** Radha, the bliss of prema, shines; this is the yantra and the tantra, infinite (?).[^radha-6-37][^intro-6-37]  
**6.38.** Her radiance (?) has adorned this whole mandala of prema: [such is] the tantra.  
**6.39.** Filled (?) with bliss in prema is the rasika-deva Jagannatha.  

*Shloka*  
**6.40.** *[Skt.]* "The imperishable is the supreme Brahman, the perishable is all this world; in the form of the perishable and the imperishable abides Purushottama."[^akshara-6-40]  
**6.41.** That form which is not a perishable (??) creation is called Parabrahman.  
**6.42.** The world in which (?) birth takes place (?) is called perishable in form.  
**6.43.** This murti of Jagannatha is the essence, imperishable kalpa after kalpa.  

*Shloka*  
**6.44.** *[Skt.]* "The abode (??) of all avataras, worshipped first among the most excellent, abiding (?); the perishable and the imperishable, Tara-brahma, the Supreme Self (?), Purushottama."[^avatara-6-44]  
**6.45.** As many avataras of Vishnu as there are, all come forth from this murti.  
**6.46.** As many kshetras as there are on earth (?), all are born from this kshetra.  
**6.47.** As many tirthas as there are in this world (?), all come forth from these feet.  
**6.48.** As many things (?) as there are in the world, all are born from this womb.  
**6.49.** As many books of the Vedas and shastras as there are, all are born (?) from this word (?).  
**6.50.** As many moving and unmoving [beings] as there are, all are born from this body.  
**6.51.** This Jagannatha is the Supreme: from Him are born all the Brahmas (?).  
**6.52.** He is the highest among the purushas of the Veda; He (??) is incomparable in this body.  

*Shloka*  
**6.53.** *[Skt.]* "Omkara is the seed-measure; in the field is the circle of the knower of the field. The syllable 'a' is the middle measure, and brahma-vidya is not (?) established [in it]."[^omkara-6-53]  
**6.54.** Born from Omkara, the Vedas [and] Puranas (?) multiplied,  
**6.55.** all mantras, the essence of yantras, various kinds of knowledge, the expansion (?) of Brahman.  
**6.56.** If one examines and understands the matter, all is born from Omkara alone.  
**6.57.** Some (?), not understanding (?) Omkara, sit practising brahma-jnana.  
**6.58.** Born (?) in Purushottama, the kshetras and tirthas spread over the earth (?).  
**6.59.** From this (??) murti came forth countless avataras;  
**6.60.** by various feats they won fame and became the greatest in the world.  
**6.61.** The seeds (?) of the moving and unmoving … (??): all are born from this kshetra.  

*Shloka*  
**6.62.** *[Skt.]* "The syllable 'ma' is the full measure; one should remember the bindu (??) as Purna-brahman. Beyond it, with the mark (?) of Shesha, abides … (??) of the moon."[^shesha-6-62]  
**6.63.** 'A', 'e' [= 'u'] (?), 'ma': the Supreme Self, the essence of Brahman.  
**6.64.** On the head of Shesha-deva is the couch-bed: there He abides (?).  

*Shloka*  
**6.65.** *[Skt.]* "Beyond it is [the place] of Subhadra: on the bed of prema and of happiness (?); in her lap (?) is the kama-bija; one should remember it as fivefold (?)."[^subhadra-6-65]  
**6.66.** Subhadra, exceedingly sweet, on (??) the bed of prema, full of happiness,  
**6.67.** unites the kama-bija: that is the fivefold … (??).  

*Shloka*  
**6.68.** *[Skt.]* "Having given the rasa of the bliss of rasa, having enjoyed (?) here before, the chief … (??), immersed in madness, is the contemplation of rasa."[^darumurti-6-68]  
**6.69.** Sri Radha gives rasa, Sri Krishna relishes the rasa.  
**6.70.** Immersed in the highest rasa, the two abide in two bhavas.  
**6.71.** They know that happiness in the world (?); who else is there besides Them?  
**6.72.** In the bliss of prema, in the bhava of prema, They are immersed, full of happiness.  
**6.73.** Intoxicated by the embrace of prema, Jagannatha with Radha  
**6.74.** abide, obtaining great happiness: the two in this daru-murti.  

## End of Chapter 6: the Path of Raga to the Murti of Jagannatha; Ati Bada Records Mahaprabhu's Teaching


**6.75.** [Both] abide in the happiness of supreme bliss, intoxicated with the happiness of great bliss.[^ananda-6-75][^intro-6-75]  
**6.76.** Since the two blisses are one, therefore [this] is the form of sat-cit-ananda.  
**6.77.** That which the shastras call (?) prema: this feeling of prema one obtains here.  
**6.78.** The raga [sought] in worship by the path of raga is obtained right here.  
**6.79.** Whatever (?) bhakti there is with the feeling of prema, it is in the murti of Jagannatha.  
**6.80.** Worship Him by the path of raga; cast the service of others (?) far away.[^raga-6-77]  
**6.81.** Bhakti is of nine kinds; mukti is obtained in four kinds.  
**6.82.** If one sets the mind on extensive injunctions, the eighteen siddhis arise.  
**6.83.** Of eighty-one (?) kinds of bhakti the saints have written in the shastras.  
**6.84.** In many ways there is disagreement: [so] are bhajana [and] sadhana arranged.[^navadha-6-81]  
**6.85.** Whoever meditates by whatever doctrine, all of them (?) will come here.  
**6.86.** At the darshan (?) [of Him] they will obtain bhakti [and] mukti, all perfections according to their wish (?).  
**6.87.** This Jagannatha is the fulfilment of desires; and in desirelessness, all perfection (?).[^darshana-6-85]  
**6.88.** Thus spoke the words that Prabhu, Krishna Chaitanya.  
**6.89.** All the Vaishnavas, as many as were [there], heard and were pleased.  
**6.90.** Ati Bada Gosain was [there]: he heard the command of Prabhu.  
**6.91.** He kept this discourse in mind [and] wrote [it] down in shlokas (?) [and] chaupadis.  
**6.92.** Moreover (?), that book of 'fruit' (??) he compiled, making a grantha.  
**6.93.** There he set [it] out in detail, as Prabhu's command was.  
**6.94.** All this discourse he reads in the jagamohana hall.[^atibada-6-90]  
**6.95.** Thus Chaitanya Hari, proceeding, went to [His] residence.  
**6.96.** This fivefold (?) word of prema Prabhu Chaitanya spoke.[^pancatmika-6-96]  
**6.97.** Says the brahmin Divakara: at the feet of that Prabhu is my head.  
**6.98.** This book is an exposition of bhakti, the practice of svakiya bhakti.  
**6.99.** Sri Ati Bada Gosain: at his feet I abide.  
**6.100.** For the svakiya Vaishnavas this book is their wealth.[^svakiya-6-98]  

*Thus [ends], in the Sri Jagannathamrita, [the life of] Ati Bada Gosain, the sixth chapter, called 'Anukramani' (?).*[^colophon-6-100]

[^ishvara-6-6]: Shloka 6.6 is *Brahma-samhita* 5.1, verbatim. Krishnadasa Kaviraja also quotes it (CC, Adi 2.107). Mahaprabhu found the *Brahma-samhita* in the South and brought it to Puri (CC, Madhya 9); Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati published it with his commentary. The reading is secure.
[^upare-6-7]: 6.7–6.10 interpret shloka 6.6 in the spirit of the Atibadi line. The shloka calls Krishna the supreme Lord, while the author makes the murti of Jagannatha 'Krishna above Krishna' and 'Brahman above Brahman', from whom all originates. Cf. the notes to 4.52 (Jagannatha as Parabrahman and source of the avatars) and 2.22. For the Gaudiya Vaishnavas Jagannatha is Krishna Himself (CC, Madhya 13), not a stage above Krishna. There is nothing above Krishna, Svayam Bhagavan: *kṛṣṇas tu bhagavān svayam* (*Bhagavata* 1.3.28; CC, Adi 2). Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati counts the 'ativadis' among the apasampradayas, for such teachings among others. Unclear readings: ପରହିଦ୍ଧି (6.9): the third sign is ହି, not ସି; taken as ପରସିଦ୍ଧି, 'supreme perfection' (or *prasiddhi*, 'renowned'). ଏଥୁହି (6.10): after ଏଥୁ stands a sign that in 6.34 clearly reads ହି (ଏହି, rhyming with ବହନ୍ତି); taken as *ethu̐ hi̐*, 'from Him indeed'. ଉତପତ୍ତି: the third sign looks like ε; taken from the sense.
[^samhita-6-11]: Shlokas 6.11 and 6.14 together are *Brahma-samhita* 5.3, with differences. The *Brahma-samhita* reads: *karṇikāraṁ mahad yantraṁ ṣaṭ-koṇaṁ vajra-kīlakam / ṣaḍ-aṅga-ṣaṭ-padī-sthānaṁ prakṛtyā puruṣeṇa ca / premānanda-mahānanda-rasenāvasthitaṁ hi yat / jyotī-rūpeṇa manunā kāma-bījena saṅgatam* ('the whorl [of the lotus of Gokula] is a great hexagonal yantra with a diamond pivot; the place of the six-limbed, six-footed [eighteen-syllable] mantra, with prakriti and purusha; abiding in the rasa of the bliss of prema and great bliss, joined with the mantra of light, the kama-bija'). Here is printed *karṇikāre* (?) *madhyaṁ* for *karṇikāraṁ mahad*, and *jagat* for *hi yat*. The third line of 6.11 ('in the midst of the yantra of the great lotus one should practise the hexagonal yantra') is absent from the *Brahma-samhita*; it is a precept of tantric sadhana. ଷଡ୍ବାସ୍ପ (6.11): printed ଷଡ଼ଙ୍ଗଷଡ୍ + ବା + a sign like ସ୍ପ; corrected after the *Brahma-samhita* to ଷଟ୍ପଦୀ. ମନୁନା (6.14): the last sign looks like ଜା/ନା. In 6.12 'the place of six limbs and six feet' is identified with the seat where Radha and Krishna sit together. 6.13: ମର୍ତ୍ତ୍ୟରେ: a sign with an arc ≈ ର୍ତ୍ତ/ଣ୍ଡି; taken as 'in the earthly world' (or ମଣ୍ଡିରେ?). ଦେବା ଭେଦାଇ is not understood: 'God, having divided [into two]'? or 'to give, having distinguished'?
[^vrita-6-17]: 6.17 is unclear. ଅଦ୍ୟପ୍ରାନ୍ତ is taken as *ādya-prānta*, 'beginning and end'. ବୃତ may be *vṛta*, 'surrounded', as in *Brahma-samhita* 5.5 of the square around Gokula (*caturbhir hetubhir vṛtam*), or *vṛtta*, 'account'. The author apparently promises to give the continuation of the shloka; 6.18 is indeed close to *Brahma-samhita* 5.4–5.5.
[^bija-6-18]: Shloka 6.18 is badly corrupted and only partly understood. The second half of the first line is *Brahma-samhita* 5.4 (*tat-patrāṇi śriyām api*, 'its petals are [the abodes] of the Shris', i.e. the gopis). 'The four murtis': cf. 5.5 (*catur-mūrteś catur-dhāma*). ତଦ୍ବୀଜନ୍ୟାସଂ: the dark sign is taken as ବୀ, as in କାମବୀଜେନ (6.14); the *Brahma-samhita* has here *tat-kiñjalkaṁ tad-aṁśānām* ('its filaments are [the abodes] of His portions'). ଚତସ୍ୟାତ୍ / ଚତସ୍ୟାଦଚିନ୍ତ୍ୟଃ: the second sign looks like ଛ; taken as *catuḥ syāt*, 'let it be fourfold', and *catuḥ syād acintyaḥ*, 'fourfold, inconceivable'. ଶ୍ରିୟାମପି: the ending is printed like ମତି. 'Pluta' is a prolonged (three-mora) vowel; cf. 6.25. There is no '॥' at the end.
[^chaturdha-6-21]: The Chaturdha-murti are the four Deities of the Puri altar (Balabhadra, Subhadra, Jagannatha, Sudarshana); see the note to 4.25. Here they are identified with the 'four murtis' of shloka 6.18, i.e. placed in the lotus of Gokula. In 6.20 ଚତୁଷ୍ପାଦ, 'four parts (quarters)', apparently means the four goals of life (dharma, artha, kama, moksha; cf. *Brahma-samhita* 5.5, *caturbhiḥ puruṣārthaiś ca*). The Gaudiya Vaishnavas see the highest goal beyond all four, in prema (CC, Adi 7.84).
[^baisi-6-23]: 6.22–6.24 transfer the abodes of Goloka onto the Puri temple. Goloka is the Bada-danda, the grand road of Puri (cf. VL 5.144, 5.163), and the Baisi Pahacha, the twenty-two steps leading from the Lion Gate to the main temple. Maha-nitya is the jagamohana, the hall before the sanctum. Parama-nitya is the throne, the ratna-vedi, where the four abide (6.22). ପାବଚ୍ଛ: printed ପାବ + a sign like ଛ/ଚ୍ଛ; taken as 'steps' (*pāhāca*). These names of abodes (nitya, maha-nitya, parama-nitya) do not occur in CC/CB. For the Gaudiyas too Puri (Nilachala) is a transcendental abode; Mahaprabhu saw in Jagannatha the Krishna of Vrindavana (CC, Madhya 13), and in the route of the Ratha-yatra the way from Dvaraka to Vrindavana.
[^pluta-6-25]: 6.25 is unclear. In Vedic phonetics 'pluta' is a vowel three morae long; 'chatuh-pluta', 'fourfold pluta', is apparently linked here with the four murtis (cf. 6.18 *paramaḥ plutaḥ*). The first word of the second half-verse: printed a dark ସ with a tail + ର; taken as ସ୍ୱର, 'svara', tone (or ସ୍ମୃତି?). Then a sign like ε + ଛି under an ି arc; taken as ଅଛି, 'is' (the metre gives 9 syllables).
[^vani-6-26]: ଦଶାହ୍ନିକ is not identified: printed ଦ + a dark sign like ଶା/ଷା + ହ୍ନି + କ. 6.26 is the narrator's; apparently the end of the first part of Mahaprabhu's answer.
[^shodashi-6-27]: The source of shloka 6.27 has not been found. Shodashi ('the sixteenfold') is, in the Shakta tantras, a name of the goddess Tripurasundari and her sixteen-syllable vidya. Brahma-vidya is knowledge of Brahman. Here all these are epithets of Jagannatha as the 'essence' of mantra, yantra, tantra and prema; cf. 6.34 (sixteen kalas). ବିତତ୍: printed ବି + ତ + a sign like ତ୍ତ୍/ଭ୍ with virama; not identified (perhaps *viddhi*, 'know'). The first word is printed like ମରସାରଂ; taken as ମନ୍ତ୍ରସାରଂ after the series yantra-, tantra-sara.
[^tantra-6-28]: 6.28–6.32 present Jagannatha as mantra, yantra and tantra; cf. the notes to 4.67 (Jagannatha in the form of the mantra) and 4.29. The teaching of Jagannatha as tantra belongs to the Odia tantric tradition; it is not in CC/CB. The Gaudiya Vaishnavas, following Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati, approach Jagannatha not by tantric sadhana but by pure bhakti, by hearing and chanting the Name (CC, Madhya 22; Antya 4.70–4.71). Unclear readings: ବିସ୍ତର (6.28): printed ବ + a sign like ସ୍ତୁ + ର. ଉକତ (6.29): printed ଉ + a dark sign like କ + ତ, taken as *ukta*, 'declared'; ଏତ, 'such' (the rhyme with ଜଗନ୍ନାଥ is imperfect, as in 4.53). ଭ୍ରତ (6.30) is corrected to ବ୍ରତ, 'vow'. ତେନ (6.31, 6.32): printed େ + ତ + ନ; taken as Skt. *tena*, 'therefore, thereby'. ତତ୍ତ୍ୱ (6.31): the last sign resembles ତନ୍ତ୍ର in 6.29; ତତ୍ତ୍ୱ is taken for the rhyme with ଅର୍ଥ.
[^kala-6-34]: 'Sixteen kalas' are sixteen digits of fullness (like the sixteen digits of the moon); in the tantras, a mark of the complete Deity (cf. 'sixteen-syllable' in 4.67 and Shodashi in 6.27). The Gaudiya Vaishnavas, after *Bhagavata* 1.3.28, call Krishna the source of all avatars rather than the possessor of a certain number of portions. 6.35 is unclear. ଦେହର: printed ଦେ + a sign like ସ୍ୱ/ହ + ର. ଅବଳୀ is taken as *āvalī*, 'row, array'. 'They' are apparently the Four (6.21–6.22).
[^prema-6-36]: The source of shloka 6.36 has not been found. The second line is poorly understood. କଥନ୍ତୁ: printed କ + ଥ + a sign like ନ୍ତୁ/ୟୁ. ବିପୁରେଶ୍ୱର: printed ବି + ପୁ + ରେ + ଶ୍ୱ + ର; perhaps ତ୍ରିପୁରେଶ୍ୱରୀ, 'the Mistress of Tripura' (cf. Shodashi in 6.27). [^radha-6-37]: 6.37–6.39 paraphrase shloka 6.36: its "Devi" who relishes prema is Radha (cf. the note to 6.36). Jagannatha is called "rasika-deva", the relisher of rasa. [^akshara-6-40]: The source of shloka 6.40 has not been found. The idea is close to *Bhagavad-gita* 15.16–15.18: all beings are the perishable (*kṣara*), the unchanging is the imperishable (*akṣara*), and Purushottama is beyond both. Here, however, Purushottama abides "in the form of both the perishable and the imperishable". Purushottama is also a name of Puri (Purushottama-kshetra) and of Jagannatha; cf. 6.58. In 6.41 the key word is not identified (printed କ୍ଷ + a sign like ସୃ + ଷ୍ଟି); taken as "perishable creation" (କ୍ଷର-ସୃଷ୍ଟି?). In 6.42 the sign like ହୁ (in ହୁଏ and ଯେହୁ) is ହ with a bold dot below.
[^avatara-6-44]: 6.44–6.61: the murti of Jagannatha is the source of all the avataras of Vishnu, of kshetras and tirthas, of the Vedas and shastras, of all beings and of "all the Brahmas" (?). Cf. the notes to 4.52 (Jagannatha as Parabrahman and source of the avataras) and to 6.7 (Jagannatha as "Krishna above Krishna"). For the Gaudiya Vaishnavas the source of all avataras is Krishna (*ete cāṁśa-kalāḥ puṁsaḥ kṛṣṇas tu bhagavān svayam*, *Bhagavata* 1.3.28; CC, Adi 2), and Jagannatha is Krishna Himself. They can therefore refer what is said here about the avataras to Jagannatha as Krishna; the divergence is that the author places the murti above Krishna (6.8). "Tara-brahma" (6.44) is probably *tāraka-brahma*, "the Brahman that carries [one] across [samsara]"; the Upanishads give this name to Om and to the mantra of Rama. Shloka 6.44 is poorly read: *kṣayāṇi* is taken as Vedic *kṣaya*, "abode"; ପରତ୍ମା as *parātmā* (the metre wants two syllables). ସର୍ବବ୍ରହ୍ମ (6.51) is either "all the Brahmas" (the creators of the universes) or "all Brahman". 6.52 ("the highest among the purushas of the Veda") plays on the name Purushottama; the lone sign like ଷ before ଏହି is not identified (ସେ?).
[^omkara-6-53]: 6.53–6.64 concern Omkara and its measures (*mātrā*): 'a', 'u' (printed ଏକାର, 'e'; ଉକାର is expected), 'm' and apparently the bindu (6.62). From Omkara are born the Vedas, the Puranas, the mantras and all else (6.54–6.56). Here Divakara is close to the CC: Mahaprabhu tells Prakashananda that the pranava is the maha-vakya and the form of the Lord, and from the pranava come all the Vedas and the world (CC, Adi 7). 6.57 reproaches those who, not understanding Omkara, "practise brahma-jnana". Shlokas 6.53 and 6.62 are understood only in part. The syllable 'a' is called the "middle measure" (usually it is the first). The second line of 6.53 is unclear (*brahma-vidyā na saṁsthitā*?). ବଲ୍ଲଂ (6.62) is taken as *binduṁ* from sense and metre. ଚନ୍ଦ୍ରଚନ୍ଦ୍ରମସ୍ୟଂ may refer to the crescent (*ardha-candra*) above the bindu in the written Om; this is our conjecture.
[^shesha-6-62]: 6.62–6.64: the measure 'm' is linked with Shesha: "on the head of Shesha-deva is the couch". Shesha here is apparently Balabhadra, as in 4.29–4.30 and 4.67 (see the notes there); next comes Subhadra (6.65–6.67), then Radha and Krishna-Jagannatha (6.68–6.74). The measures of Om thus seem to be matched with the Deities of the altar; this is our conjecture. For the Gaudiya Vaishnavas Shesha serves the Lord as His bed, seat, umbrella and so on (CC, Adi 5), and Baladeva is the source of Shesha. ବର୍ତ୍ତିରେ (6.64): printed ବ + a sign like ର୍ତ୍ତି + େର; taken as "abides".
[^subhadra-6-65]: Subhadra on the "bed of prema" unites the kama-bija (*klīṁ*, the bija of Krishna; cf. the note to 4.29, where Subhadra is given the bija "hrim"). What "fivefold" (*pañcātmika*) means, the author does not explain. The CC/CB know no such interpretation of Subhadra. Shloka 6.65 and couplets 6.66–6.67 are poorly read. ତତ୍କ୍ରୋଡ଼େ: printed ତ + ତ୍କ + େକ୍ରା + a sign like ଢ; taken as *tat-kroḍe*, "in her lap". In 6.61, 6.66 and 6.67 the same unidentified sign occurs (like ଟ with a ring below): ଚରାଚରବାଟ, ଶଯ୍ୟାଟ, ପ୍ରବଟ (perhaps ପ୍ରକଟ, "manifest").
[^darumurti-6-68]: 6.69–6.74: Radha gives rasa and Sri Krishna relishes it; Jagannatha with Radha in the embrace of prema are "the two in this daru-murti", the wooden murti of Jagannatha. For the Gaudiya Vaishnavas Radha and Krishna united in one form in Sri Chaitanya: "Radha and Krishna are one soul in two bodies; now They have become one again, in Chaitanya" (Svarupa Damodara's verse, CC, Adi 1.5; cf. Adi 4). For them Jagannatha is Krishna, and Radha's mood in Puri is shown by Mahaprabhu (CC, Madhya 13). [^navadha-6-81]: 6.81–6.84. The nine kinds of bhakti are hearing, chanting and the rest (*Bhagavata*, 7.5.23–24). The four kinds of mukti are presumably salokya, samipya, sarupya and sayujya. The *Bhagavata* (3.29.13) names five kinds and says that a pure devotee does not accept them even when they are offered. Krishna lists the eighteen siddhis to Uddhava (*Bhagavata*, 11.15). The author sets the path of vidhi (it gives siddhis) against the path of raga (it leads to prema in Jagannatha). Gaudiya Vaishnavas, following the CC (Madhya 19), call those who desire bhukti, mukti and siddhi 'restless': pure bhakti is free from these desires (*Bhakti-rasamrita-sindhu*, 1.1.11). Printed ଏକାଣି: ଣି looks like ଶି. 6.84 is understood uncertainly. *Duimata* means 'a twofold opinion, disagreement'; *racita* means 'arranged, composed'.
[^darshana-6-85]: 6.85–6.87: all doctrines lead to Jagannatha. His darshan gives bhakti, mukti and all siddhis. [^svakiya-6-98]: 'Svakiya' (*svakīyā*, 'one's own'). In Gaudiya rasa-shastra this is the love of a lawful wife, as distinct from parakiya, the love of the gopis outside marriage. According to Gaudiya Vaishnava teaching, the highest prema is the parakiya-bhava of the gopis of Vraja (CC, Adi 4; Rupa Gosvami's *Ujjvala-nilamani*). Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati upheld this teaching and rejected its worldly imitation by the sahajiyas. If Divakara means 'svakiya' in this sense, he departs from the Gaudiyas. But the word may also simply mean 'one's own', that is, 'the bhakti of our [line]' and 'the Vaishnavas of our [line]' (6.100), the followers of Ati Bada. We translate it as a term without choosing between these readings.
