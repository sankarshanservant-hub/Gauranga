# Divakara Dasa. *Jagannatha-charitamrita*, Chapter 5
## The Author on Himself and His Book; Nityananda's Question and the Kavacha of the Mantra

*Translated from Odia without a native speaker; doubtful places are marked (?) and (??). The original, transliteration and word-for-word translation are in `full/05.md`.*[^intro-5-1]

*Chapter Five*

**5.1.** Listen attentively (?), good people: I tell the *Jagannathamrita*.  
**5.2.** For fear of wicked people I write a little [and] in a veiled way (?).[^khala-5-2]  
**5.3.** Wicked people who expound (?) learning (?) will revile (?) [this] book.  
**5.4.** My name is the brahmin Divakara; my father is Janadeva (?).[^vamsha-5-4]

**5.6.** His father is Baidhara (?); my mother was named Champa.  
**5.7.** My gotra is Bharadvaja, the pravara Brihaspati [and] Angiras (?).  
**5.8.** Apart from (?) Jagannatha alone, nothing else delights my mind.  

*Shloka*  
**5.9.** *[Skt.]* "Not Vrindavana, not the cowherds' town (?) with its groves and dwellings, not Ayodhya, [not] Dvaraka, not … (??), [not] Kashi [and] Madhupuri; not Kuru… (?), not … (??), not … (??) or [another] kshetra (??): [only] Jagannatha's abode gladdens (?) my heart (?) with love (??)."[^kshetra-5-9]

**5.11.** I wrote books — the Vedas; in them my mind finds no delight.[^veda-5-11]  
**5.12.** Jagannatha alone I worship (?) with all my heart.  
**5.13.** I took refuge at [His] holy lotus (?) [feet] — that is all my worship.

**5.17.** Whatever instructions the Gosain formerly gave, wherever [he gave them],  
**5.18.** that book I wrote, making it exact, following [their] sense.  
**5.19.** I myself am not so (?) worthy (?): Sri Jagannatha is pleased to speak [it].[^method-5-14]  
**5.20.** His Name is a wish-fulfilling tree, the Mount Meru of the devotees' hopes.[^nama-5-20]  
**5.21.** Receiving His command, I set out (??) into the wealth of bhakti (?).  
**5.22.** He who expounded bhakti to Sarvabhauma — Chaitanya Gosain —

**5.25.** One day Nityananda joyfully asked Prabhu.[^nityananda-5-25]

*Shloka*

## The kavacha of the Name: guardians of the quarters, colours of the syllables, its fruits; end of Chapter 5


**5.62.** The eastern quarter [is guarded by] Stoka-Krishna, taking the syllable 'shna' as companion.[^intro-5-62]  
**5.63.** Let Mahabahu abide in the west and take the syllable 'kri' as companion.  
**5.64.** In the south — … (??) [his] place (?); taking the syllable 'kha' (?) [= 'shna'] as companion.[^name-5-64]  
**5.65.** Vishala will guard the south-west; two (?) syllables will be with him.  
**5.66.** Mahabala will be in the north, taking the syllable 'shna' (?) as companion.  
**5.67.** Vrishabha will guard the north-west; two (?) syllables will be with him.  
**5.68.** Let Devaprastha abide in the north (?), taking the syllable 're' as companion.  
**5.69.** Ujjvala will guard the north-east; the syllable 'ha' will be [his] companion.  
**5.70.** Let Mahabahu guard the upper [quarter]; let the syllable 're' be his companion.  
**5.71.** Ananta will guard the lower [quarter], taking the syllable 'Om' (?) as companion.[^dik-5-71]  
**5.72.** The wise (?) Shyama will guard the eight (??) [quarters]; He in all beings is … (??) love (??).[^shyama-5-72]  
**5.73.** May that Krishna, beloved of the gopis (?), abide in [my] intellect [and] in my whole body.  
**5.74.** The syllable 'ha' (?) is white in colour, the best among all letters.[^varna-5-74]  
**5.75.** However many sins there are in this body, on remembering [it] it quickly removes [them].  
**5.76.** The syllable 're' is red in colour; it pervades (??) … (??) in a person's body.  
**5.77.** However many sins of former births there are, the syllable 're' removes them when it is uttered.  
**5.78.** The syllable 'ra' is golden in colour, [like] Radha's (?) incomparable body.[^radha-5-78]  
**5.79.** However many sins arise at an eclipse (?), they are destroyed when 'ra' is uttered.  
**5.80.** The syllable 'ma' is of the form of light: it is directly Niranjana.  
**5.81.** However many sins of false speech there are, on remembering [it] it quickly removes [them].  
**5.82.** The syllable 'kri' is like collyrium: it removes the lesser sins.  
**5.83.** Sins of betrayal of trust (?) it removes at once when it is chanted.  
**5.84.** The syllable 'shna' appears white, like [the light] of the sun and moon (?).  
**5.85.** Grave faults and great sins are destroyed by mere chanting.[^papa-5-85]  
**5.86.** The person who, saying (?) 'Hare Rama Krishna', holds to this Name —  
**5.87.** in him bhakti arises (?), and he attains it flawlessly (?).  

*Shloka*  
**5.88.** *[Skt.]* "To the hypocrite, the non-devotee, … (??) one who has not subdued the senses, the babbler (?), the proud, the fool, one averse to love for Krishna [this should not be given]."[^shloka-5-88]  
**5.89.** The hypocritical, non-devoted person, and also the disciple of another path;  
**5.90.** one who is in the grip (?) of his own greatness, talkative (?) [and] proud;  
**5.91.** one who does not know love for Krishna — let this book not be told before him.  
**5.92.** People of demonic mind, hearing [it], laugh, clapping (?) their hands.  
**5.93.** The man who reviles (?) this scripture is an asura of Lanka:  
**5.94.** he had heard the name of Sri Rama, and therefore obtained a human body.[^lanka-5-93]  
**5.95.** Whoever puts on this kavacha of the Name of Hari as armour on the body,  
**5.96.** in him bhakti arises for Krishna's feet, and he has no fear of Yama's punishment.  
**5.97.** Afflictions from the planets, bhutas [and] pretas, dakinis, pishachis — all of them;  
**5.98.** king, tiger, crocodile, thief (??) — of all these he becomes fearless (?).  
**5.99.** Enemies, on seeing [him], are pacified; wealth and grain are obtained in abundance.  
**5.100.** Whether high-born (?) [or] wicked (?), whoever sees [it] attains bhakti near Krishna.[^phala-5-96]  
**5.101.** Hearing the words of the kavacha of the Name of Hari, cross over samsara (?), O beings!"  
**5.102.** Before Sri Nityananda Prabhu, Sri Chaitanya lovingly  
**5.103.** expounded the whole rasa of bhakti. Brahmins were nearby;  
**5.104.** all the Vaishnavas who were [there] learned this knowledge.  
**5.105.** Sri Ati Bada Jagannatha heard [it] together with the Vaishnavas,  
**5.106.** cast off all worldliness (?) and held this bhakti in his heart.  
**5.107.** Let all the people under his shelter (?) practise contemplation of this bhakti.[^atibada-5-105]  
**5.108.** Says the brahmin Divakara: my head is at the feet of Sri Guru.

[^vamsha-5-4]: The author's genealogy: Divakara, son of Janadeva (?) and Champa; grandfather Jagu Dasa, great-grandfather Nanda Dasa, great-great-grandfather Baidhara (?). The names are read uncertainly. ଜନଦେବ: printed a sign like ଜ + a sign like ନ, then ଦେବ (OCR ଛମମ ଦେଵ୍ଵ). ବାଇଧର: printed a sign like ବ/କ + ା + a dark ଇ/ଳ + ଧ + ର. These names have not been checked against other sources. Divakara is called fifth in succession from Jagannatha Dasa in the spiritual line, not by family (see Ch. 1, note to 1.1). The pravara of the Bharadvaja gotra is traditionally three rishis: Angiras, Brihaspati (Barhaspatya) and Bharadvaja. Printed ବୃହସ୍ପତ୍ୟଙ୍ଗିସ: ର and ା are not printed; the correction [ବୃହସ୍ପତ୍ୟାଙ୍ଗିରସ] is by sense. ବିନେ (5.8): printed ବ with a hook ି + େନ (OCR ବଚନେ); taken as 'apart from'.
[^kshetra-5-9]: The source of the shloka has not been found. The metre is shikharini (17 syllables); it is clearly seen in the first and fourth lines of the shloka. The second and third lines are poorly printed. ଗୋ-ନଗରୀ: printed େଗା + dots + ନଗରୀ; the metre wants a light syllable here (ନ ଚ?). Third line: ନ କୁରୁ (Kurukshetra?) + ପୁ + a sign like େ/ଟ + ନ/ମ + କୁ (Pushkara?); ନ + a sign like ଗ/ଦ + a sign like ଙ୍ଗ + dots + a sign like ରା (Ganga?); ବା କ୍ଷେତ୍ରମ… (printed େକ୍ଷ + ତ୍ର + ମ + ୃ + େ + a sign like ମ/ସ; 'another kshetra'?). Fourth line: ହୃଦୟ (printed ହୃଦ + a sign like ୟ), ପ୍ରେମ୍ନାପି (printed a sign like ପ୍ର + େ + ମ୍ନ + ା + a sign like ପି), ନନ୍ଦତି (printed a sign like ନ + ନ୍ଦ/ନ୍ଧ + ତି) are guesses. The sense is clear from the paraphrase 5.10. 'The kshetras described before' are apparently 4.131–4.134, where Mathura, the Dvarakas, Ayodhya, Kashi and Vrindavana are said to abide in Purushottama. Madhupuri is Mathura. For Gaudiya Vaishnavas the highest abode is Vrindavana, the place of Krishna's lilas with the gopis (see the note to 4.131).
[^veda-5-11]: *Puthi lekhili bedamāna* — 'I wrote (copied) books — the Vedas'; the author apparently speaks of his former occupation as a brahmin scribe. Gaudiya Vaishnavas accept the Vedas as the highest authority (shabda-pramana). For them the aim of the Vedas is Krishna: 'By all the Vedas I am to be known' (BG 15.15). So Mahaprabhu explained the Vedanta to Sarvabhauma and Prakashananda (CC, Madhya 6; Adi 7). Giving up the Vedas for 'Jagannatha alone' is foreign to the Gaudiyas. But they too place bhakti above book learning (CC, Madhya 8).
[^method-5-14]: 5.14–5.19 are the author on his method: Sanskrit shlokas 'from the Vedas (?)' with an Odia verse paraphrase. Chapters 4–5 are built this way. The readings are uncertain. ଛନ୍ଦକୁ (5.14): printed a dark ଛ/ଜ + a sign like ନ୍ଦ + କୁ; possibly ଜନକୁ, 'for people'. ତୋଳାକର (5.14; OCR ତୋଳାକର): taken as ତୋଳା କରି, 'gathering'; this is a guess. ରଚନା (5.15): printed a sign like ସ (= ର) + a sign like ୟ (= ଚ?) + ନା + a separate sign like ଁ/ି. ବେଦନ୍ତୁଁ (5.15): printed େବ + a sign like ଦ/ଳ + ନ୍ତୁଁ; taken as 'from the Vedas', possibly 'from the Vedanta'. The shlokas of Chapters 4–5 are not Vedic texts; their source has not been found (see the note to 4.73). The 'Gosain' (5.17) is not named. The words 'Jagannatha Himself speaks the book' are the author's claim. Cf. the humility of Krishnadasa Kaviraja, who wrote the CC by the order of Madana-Gopala (CC, Adi 8).

[^nityananda-5-25]: ନିତ୍ୟାନନ୍ଦେ: printed ନ with a hook ି + ତ୍ୟା + ନ + େ + ନ୍ଦ (OCR ଛତ୍ଯୟାଳନହେ). In Divakara, Nityananda is among the Gaudiyas (see the note to 3.170). [^sayujya-5-26]: The source of the shloka has not been found. 'Hare Rama…' is the maha-mantra in the Odia order (see the notes to 3.147 and 4.103). The 'fruit of five' (*pañca*) is unclear; perhaps the five 'states' of 4.117–4.123 or the five kinds of liberation. Gaudiya Vaishnavas reject sayujya (merging with the Lord): pure devotees do not accept any of the five kinds of mukti even if the Lord Himself offers them (*Bhagavata* 3.29.13; cf. CC, Madhya 6 — Mahaprabhu's talk with Sarvabhauma on mukti). A kavacha (5.27) is an 'armour': protecting the parts of the body with the syllables of a mantra (nyasa); the list apparently continues on p. 34. Gaudiya Vaishnavas teach that the Name does not depend on diksha, purashcharana or other rites (CC, Madhya 15.108). କରୁଥେ: printed a dark ଭ/କ + ରୁ + େ + ଥ/ଯ (OCR ଭର୍ସୁସ); taken as 'they make, put on'.

Gaudiya Vaishnavas do not seek 'all power' (*sarvaiśvarya*) as the fruit of bhakti: pure bhakti is free from the desire for mystic powers (CC, Madhya 19.149; see the note to 4.126). Mahaprabhu, according to the CC, taught that the Name needs neither diksha nor rituals (CC, Madhya 15.108; see the note to 5.26). The CC and CB say nothing of a nyasa or kavacha of the mantra.
[^hara-5-34]: 5.34–5.35 are unclear. The sense seems to be: Brahma, Shiva, Indra and the guardians of the quarters have power because they put the name 'Hara' in front. The connection of the half-verses is unclear; ମୁଳେ (printed ମ + a subscript hook + େଳ) and ଅଘୋର (printed ଅ + େ + a sign like ଦ/ଚ/ଘ + a stroke + ର) are read with doubt. 'Hara' here is apparently 'Hare', the first word of the mantra. In Gaudiya explanations of the mahamantra 'Hare' is the vocative of 'Hara' (Radha, the energy of Krishna). The order with 'Hare Rama' before 'Hare Krishna' is the Odia one (see the notes to 2.135 and 5.26).
[^nyasa-5-32]: 5.32–5.38 are a nyasa: the six different syllables of the mantra 'Hare Rama Hare Krishna' (ha, re, ra, ma, kri, shna) are placed on parts of the body. In the shloka (5.32–5.33) 'ha' is placed on the nose, in the Odia rendering (5.36) on the head; the rest agrees. The syllable 'shna' (*ṣṇa*) is printed with the letter ଖ (*kha*): in Odia pronunciation ଷ sounds like 'kh', and old manuscripts often write ଖ for ଷ. Likewise in 5.58. ରାକାର (5.32) and ରାକ୍ଷର (5.37): printed a sign like ସ/ଷ, taken as the ସ୍ୱ-like ରା; this is a guess based on the syllables of the mantra (otherwise 'sha'). ମଧ୍ୟେଣ (5.32) is not identified. କୃକାରୋ (5.33): printed a bold sign like ଖ + a subscript ୃ; accepted from the Odia rendering in 5.38 (କୃକାର).
[^sakhi-5-39]: Shloka 5.39 is a kavacha made of the names of gopis and cowherd boys. The first eight are Radha's eight principal sakhis, as with the Gaudiya Vaishnavas: Lalita, Vishakha, Champakalata, Chitra, Rangadevi, Sudevi, Tungavidya, Indulekha (Rupa Gosvami, *Radha-Krishna-ganoddesha-dipika*). Next come Shashirekha, Vimala, Palika, Manjari (in 5.51, Ananga-manjari; for the Gaudiyas, Radha's younger sister), Shyamala, Madhumati, Dhanya, Mangala. Then Krishna's friends: Sridama, Sudama, Vasudama, Arjuna, Subala, Kinkini. The last word of the shloka (printed ବରୁଥ… ≈ ନା/ଳା) is apparently the name of Varuthapa, one of Krishna's friends (it occurs in the Gaudiya lists of sakhas). According to the OCR, p. 36 goes on to name Vrishabha, Devaprastha, Vishala. Radha and Krishna Himself are not named in the list (apart from the unclear 'Krishna' in the last line). Unclear places: ପାଳଶଙ୍ଗଂ (printed ପା + a sign like ଳ + ଶ + ଙ୍ଗଂ; likewise 5.44; perhaps *pārśvāṅgam*, 'the side'). ମୂର୍ଦ୍ଧନିଂ — 'the crown of the head'. ଭାଲଂ: printed a sign like ଭ/ଗ + ଲଂ (or ଗଳଂ, 'the neck'). ମଙ୍ଗଳାର୍ଦ୍ଧଂ ଉଦାବସ୍ଥା: by 5.55, apparently 'the belly'. ପାଦବାଚୈକ: printed ପାଦବା + େ + a sign like ଚ with the ୈ flag + କ; by 5.61, 'the left foot'. The word before କୃଷ୍ଣଃ (printed ଦ + a sign like ଶ/ଝ + a sign like ଟ + a sign like ସ୍ତ୍ରା + ≈ କ) is not identified; perhaps '[in] the eastern [and other] quarters'. Gaudiya Vaishnavas honour the sakhis and manjaris and serve them in raga-bhakti under the guidance of the guru. But a kavacha made of their names and the syllables of the mantra is not described in the CC or CB; it is an Odia practice.
[^raksha-5-40]: 5.40–5.61 are the Odia rendering of shloka 5.39. Each gopi or cowherd boy is given a syllable of the mantra. The syllables follow the order of the mahamantra 'Hare Rama Hare Rama Rama Rama Hare Hare / Hare Krishna Hare Krishna…': ha (Lalita), re (Vishakha), ra (Champakalata), ma (Chitra); ha-re-ra (Rangadevi with Sudevi), ma (Tungavidya); ra (Indulekha), ma (Shashirekha), ra (Vimala), ma (Palika); ha (Ananga-manjari), re (5.52), ha (Madhumati), re (Dhanya); ha (Mangala), re (Sudama), kri (Sridama), shna (Sudama), ha (Arjuna), re (Subala), kri (Kinkini). The list continues on p. 36. Unclear places: ଚତା[ଚିତ୍ରା] (5.43, ି and ୍ର not visible); ମଆକୁ (5.43): printed ମ + a sign like ଥ/ଅ + ା + କୁ, possibly ମଥାକୁ, 'the head'. ହରେରା ଏ ତିନି (5.45): printed ହରେ + a sign like ସ/ଷ; then a sign like େ/ତ + ନ; taken as 'these three [syllables] ha-re-ra' from the sequence of syllables. ଭାଲରେ (5.50): printed a sign like ଭ/ଢ + ଲ + ରେ. ରହୁ (5.50): possibly ରକ୍ଷୁ. [^dik-5-71]: 5.62–5.71 give the guardians of the ten directions: the eight quarters, above and below. They are Krishna's friends from Vraja: Stoka-Krishna (east, as in the shloka on p. 35: *prācīṁ … stokakṛṣṇaḥ*), Mahabahu (west, and again above, 5.70), an unidentified name (south), Vishala (south-west), Mahabala (north), Vrishabha (north-west), Devaprastha (5.68, printed 'in the north', although the north was already named in 5.66; the south-east is not named and may be meant), Ujjvala (north-east). [^varna-5-74]: 5.74–5.85 give the colour and power of each syllable: 'ha' — white, the best of all letters (5.74); 're' — red (5.76); 'ra' — golden (5.78); 'ma' — of the form of light, Niranjana (5.80); 'kri' — black like collyrium (5.82); 'shna' — white like the light of the sun and moon (5.84). Each syllable removes its own kind of sin. ହୁଅ (5.74): printed ହ with a tail + a sign like ଅ without its right stroke; taken as ହଅ, '[the syllable] "ha"' (cf. ହଅକୁ 5.51, ହୁଅ 5.55; likewise ରାଅ 5.79). 5.76 is unclear: the ends of both half-lines (printed like ଯା + େ + ସ୍ତ/ପ୍ତ and like ବ/କ + ର୍ଣ୍ଣ + େ + ସ୍ତ/ପ୍ତ) are not identified; the first is taken as ବ୍ୟାପ୍ତେ, 'pervades'. *Grahaṇe* (5.79) — 'at an eclipse'; sins committed during an eclipse are held in the smriti to be especially grave; it may also mean 'in accepting [what is another's]'. *Nirañjana* (5.80) — 'stainless', in the Odia Panchasakha tradition a name of the Supreme (cf. the teaching on Shunya; see the note to 1.1). ଘାତ (5.83): printed ଜ + a broken ା + ତ; the correction 'betrayal of trust' is by sense. ଚନ୍ଦ୍ରମାଙ୍କ (5.84): the last sign is like ଙ୍କ. The colours of the syllables of the maha-mantra are not described in the CC, CB, VL or MP.
[^radha-5-78]: The syllable 'ra' is compared to Radha's golden body. Printed a dot + ଧା + ଙ୍କ: ରା did not print. For Gaudiya Vaishnavas 'Hare' in the maha-mantra is the vocative of 'Hara', a name of Radha as Krishna's energy (see the note to 5.34); 'Rama' they understand as Krishna, 'Radha-ramana' (or as Balarama).
[^papa-5-85]: According to the Gaudiya Vaishnavas, even the semblance of the Name (*namabhasa*) destroys all sins and gives liberation. So Haridasa Thakura explained in his dispute with a brahmin (CC, Antya 3; the example of Ajamila — *Bhagavata* 6.2). But the aim of chanting the Name is not freedom from sin but prema; sins depart incidentally, as darkness does at the first glimmer of the rising sun (CC, Antya 3). This is also the point of 5.86–5.87: bhakti is born of the Name.
[^shloka-5-88]: The source of the shloka is not found. The Sanskrit is irregular: dative and locative are mixed, and there is no verb ('should not be given' is implied). ପରେଷୋଽଜିତେନ୍ଦ୍ରିୟଃ: printed ପର + େଷା + avagraha + ଜ + େତ + ନ୍ଦ୍ର + ୟଃ, ି not visible; *pareṣo* is not identified, possibly *paradveṣo*, 'hating others'. ବାଚାଳେ: printed ବା + a sign like ଚ୍ଳ + େଳ. Cf. *Bhagavad-gita* 18.67: 'This should never be spoken to one who is not austere, not devoted…'. For Gaudiya Vaishnavas, instructing the faithless and averse in the Name is the tenth offence against the Name (*nama-aparadha*) according to the *Padma Purana* (quoted in *Hari-bhakti-vilasa* 11). 5.89–5.91 are the Odia rendering of the shloka; 'the disciple of another path' (5.89) is an addition of the rendering.
[^lanka-5-93]: The revilers of this book are called asuras of Lanka: they heard Rama's name (in the battle with Rama) and for this obtained human birth. This legend is not found in this form in the *Ramayana*. It is polemic: who the revilers are the author does not say (cf. the note to 5.2). Gaudiya Vaishnavas also teach that even hostile hearing of the Name purifies (thus Shishupala, who reviled Krishna all his life, attained liberation; *Bhagavata* 7.1). But they do not connect this with accepting a book of the Atibadi line.
[^phala-5-96]: 5.95–5.101 give the fruits (*phala-shruti*) of the kavacha: bhakti for Krishna's feet, fearlessness of Yama, protection from planets, spirits, king, beasts and enemies, wealth and grain. Gaudiya Vaishnavas do not seek such fruits: pure bhakti is free from other desires (Rupa Gosvami, *Bhakti-rasamrita-sindhu* 1.1.11). Mahaprabhu prays in the *Shikshashtaka*: 'I do not ask for wealth, followers…' (CC, Antya 20.29). ଉତ୍ତମ ଜାତ ଦୁଷ୍ଟ (5.100) is read and understood uncertainly. ଚୋର (5.98): printed େ + a sign like ସ୍ତ/ସ୍ୱ + ର, no ା visible; 'thief' by the series 'king, tiger, crocodile'. ନିର୍ଭୟ: printed a dark ନ with ି + ର୍ଭ + ୟ. ସଂସାରୁ (5.101): printed a sign like ଯ/ସ + ସା + ରୁ, ଂ not visible.
[^atibada-5-105]: According to Divakara, Mahaprabhu expounds the kavacha to Nityananda in the presence of brahmins and Vaishnavas; Jagannatha Dasa ('Ati Bada') hears it together with the Vaishnavas and accepts this bhakti, and the people under his shelter are to practise it. This is a claim of the Atibadi line: the teaching of the kavacha passes from Mahaprabhu through Jagannatha Dasa to his followers. The CC and CB do not know this episode and do not mention Jagannatha Dasa. In the VL Jagannatha Dasa meets Mahaprabhu at Bata-Ganesha (VL 4). Gaudiya Vaishnavas accept the succession of Mahaprabhu's teaching through Svarupa Damodara and Rupa and Sanatana Gosvamis, while Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati names the Atibadi line ('ativadi') in the list of apasampradayas (see the note to 1.1). On Nityananda in Puri see the note to 5.25. ଭବ (5.106): printed a sign like ଭ/ଗ୍ୱ + ବ; possibly ଗର୍ବ, 'pride'. ଆଶ୍ରୟେ (5.107): printed ଅ + a sign like ଷ/ଶ୍ର + a sign like େ + ୟ.
