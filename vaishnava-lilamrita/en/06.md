# Madhava Pattanayak. *Vaishnava-lilamrita*, Chapter 6
## The Dispute over the Beginning of the Mahamantra; Shivananda, Kanhai and the Pilgrims; Madhavi Dasi

*Translated from Odia without a native speaker; doubtful places are marked (?) and (??). The original, transliteration and word-for-word translation are in `full/06.md`.*

**6.1.** Listen, sadhus, to this sweet account — [of] what the secret of the mahamantra is.[^mahamantra-1]  
**6.2.** "Hare Rama" or "Hare Krishna"? — over this a quarrel arose (?).  
**6.3.** Shivananda says: "Hare Krishna"; Kanhai says: "Hare Rama."[^disputants-3]  
**6.4.** "What should stand at the beginning of the mantra?" — over this a dispute arises.  
**6.5.** They wrangled at length — and went to Svarupa.[^svarupa-5]  
**6.6.** Svarupa says: "Come, let us go and learn from Svami."[^svami-6]  
**6.7.** Svami said: "Very well," — and sent word to call Prabhu.  
**6.8.** Prabhu summons Rama Raya, Sarvabhauma, Kashi Mishra.[^ramaray-8]  
**6.9.** In the northern (??) side mandapa all gathered:[^mandapa-9]  
**6.10.** Ananta, Achyuta, Kanhai, Shivananda and Govinda (?).[^govinda-10]  
**6.11.** They made Prabhu head of the assembly: "Do judge, [O Lord] (?)," they said.  
**6.12.** Sarvabhauma — [for] "Hare Rama"; Gadadhara — [for] "Hare Krishna."[^sides-12]  
**6.13.** Kashi Mishra says: "In the Kshetra the 'Hare Rama' mantra is current."  
**6.14.** There was much reasoned (?) debate; at last Prabhu proclaimed:  
**6.15.** "The 'Hare Rama' mantra is the essence; in Nilachala it is this that is in use.[^decision-15]  
**6.16.** What else should we do about this? Give up (?) the initial 'Hare Krishna'?"[^chhadiba-16]  
**6.17.** Shivananda says: "In Gauda [the mantra] beginning with 'Hare Krishna' is current."  
**6.18.** Prabhu said: "You speak rightly: in Gauda [let it] be 'Hare Krishna.'"[^gauda-18]  
**6.19.** Meanwhile — listen to the account: Shivananda used to bring pilgrims (?) —[^shivananda-19]  
**6.20.** to bring those who come for darshan to the Ratha [festival] and to stay a month in the Kshetra.  
**6.21.** With pilgrims from everywhere (?) he did business for cowries (?).[^kauri-21]  
**6.22.** Kanhai did not tolerate this; he says: "[He] took the pilgrims and went off (?)."[^kanhai-22]  
**6.23.** Kanhai brought about a hundred [people] and struck fear (?) into the pilgrims.[^tasa-23]  
**6.24.** Because of this (?) a quarrel began; Kashi Mishra becomes the mediator.[^heta-24]  
**6.25.** Kashi Mishra restrained (?) [them]: "Make no trouble — this way [it is] well."[^sambarile-25]  
**6.26.** The sister of Shikhi Mahanti — her name was Madhavi Dasi.[^madhavi-26]  

## Madhavi Dasi in Mahaprabhu's Gathering; Junior Haridasa


**6.27.** Great was her devotion; her heart [was set] on sankirtana.[^madhavi-27]  
**6.28.** In the Name she lost herself; she chanted the Name unceasingly.[^bhola-28]  
**6.29.** She composed many songs, measureless in the taste [rasa] of Krishna.  
**6.30.** Before Svami and Prabhu she sings songs, lost in bhava.[^svami-30]  
**6.31.** Prabhu showed her mercy: "Sit in the goshthi," [He] said.[^goshthi-31]  
**6.32.** [Then] the devotees left the ishta-goshthi: "We shall come no more," they said.  
**6.33.** "A woman is beside Him; even if she is a devotee — [what] trust (?) [is there]?"[^vishvasa-33]  
**6.34.** "We (?) shall not be here," so they declared in Prabhu's presence.[^amhanta-34]  
**6.35.** Svami said: "… (??): Madhavi Dasi is your equal.[^svami-35]  
**6.36.** Is there anywhere (?) another such devotee? And you say: let her go away!"  
**6.37.** [But] Vaishnavas, carried away by feeling, become [like] beasts (?) near a woman.[^speaker-37]  
**6.38.** Even a woman's figure of wood or stone Vaishnavas [should] not look at.  
**6.39.** And Madhavi is a body of blood and flesh: that is the true point.[^prasanga-39]  
**6.40.** This word is sure proof (?): her coming [must be] forbidden.  
**6.41.** Madhavi came no more; she gave up the company of the ishta-goshthi.  
**6.42.** Among Prabhu's companions are Gaudiyas, [but] not all [of them] are Chaitanya.[^chaitanya-42]  
**6.43.** Not in the navel of every musk deer, indeed, is (?) musk found,[^proverb-43]  
**6.44.** not in every forest [grows] sandalwood — know this for certain;  
**6.45.** [so too] not all Gaudiya Vaishnavas are equal to Svarupa.[^svarupa-45]  
**6.46.** [There was] one called Junior Haridasa; he was a companion (?) [of Prabhu] in Puri-dhama.[^haridasa-46]  
**6.47.** His intimacy with Madhavi grew day by day.  
**6.48.** He goes to Madhavi, [making up] many idle pretexts.  
**6.49.** For the sake of rice and salt he hastens (?) to meet her at her place.[^rice-49]  
**6.50.** Shikhi Mahanti forbade [him], yet he did not stop going.[^shikhi-50]  
**6.51.** The neighbours (?) grew suspicious: "This is not proper," they said.[^saibhai-51]  
**6.52.** One day, at night-time, seeing [him], spotting Junior Haridasa,  
**6.53.** Shikhi Mahanti beat [him]: foolish Haridasa got a beating from his hand.[^beating-53]  
**6.54.** The Gaudiyas gathered and went to report to Prabhu.  
**6.55.** Prabhu said: "[He] did well. Has (??) Haridasa [behaved] well?[^prabhu-55]  
**6.56.** It is not fitting to look at his face," — and He rebuked him much.  

## Haridasa's Departure; the Gaudiyas Leave Nilachala; Svami's Eight Shlokas; the Gaudiyas in Vrindavana


**6.57.** Haridasa [felt] great grief; in a watch of the night he went straight away.[^haridasa-57]  
**6.58.** He did not stay in Nilachala — he went and threw himself into the Ganga.[^ganga-58]  
**6.59.** On receiving the news the Gaudiyas gathered and lamented (?):[^bisuri-59]  
**6.60.** "Shall we go or stay in this place? Prabhu's heart has become estranged [from us].  
**6.61.** Since (?) Prabhu so extols Jaga Dasa[^jaga-61]  
**6.62.** and keeps silent (?) with us — we are leaving: [our] honour (??) is lost.[^mauna-62]  
**6.63.** Jagadasa, who has received no instruction, He proclaims by the name 'Svami.'[^upadesha-63]  
**6.64.** He has neglected us — come, let us all go from here."  
**6.65.** In a watch of the night they all went away — and did not come back.[^leave-65]  
**6.66.** Svami, Svarupa Damodara, Shivananda and Gadadhara —  
**6.67.** these sat in Prabhu's presence and deliberated over the trouble (?).[^upadhi-67]  
**6.68.** Shivananda says, calling out: "Your dealing ought not to be like this!  
**6.69.** For Your sake (?) they lived here — and now they have all gone.[^tu-69]  
**6.70.** This is not good for You: bring them back to the Kshetra (?)."[^kshetra-70]  
**6.71.** Prabhu looked (?) at Svami's face — Svami turned away.  
**6.72.** Prabhu said: "Will you not look [at Me] (??)? If you go along with Me (?), [then] we shall go."[^speakers-72]  
**6.73.** [Svami:] "A fool does not go with You (?). If You call, they will come; not (??) done…"[^murkha-73]  
**6.74.** Prabhu said: "Write a letter — they will turn back and return."  
**6.75.** Svami wrote eight shlokas — all of Prabhu's bhava, as much as there is.[^ashtaka-75]  
**6.76.** He recited the shlokas to Prabhu; in joy Prabhu embraced [him].  
**6.77.** Shivananda took [the letter] and went; "Prabhu wrote this," he said.[^lekhichu-77]  
**6.78.** As far as Yajanagara — there, in that place, they all were.[^jajnagar-78]  
**6.79.** No one came back — Shivananda along [with them].[^shivananda-79]  
**6.80.** All went by the road to Gauda; from there a large part [of them]  
**6.81.** went to Vrindavana — they met Rupa and Sanatana.[^rupa-81]  
**6.82.** Rupa settled [them] … (??) as far as Mount Govardhana.[^seyananta-82]  
**6.83.** Vallabha's disciples were [there]; on seeing (?) the Gaudiyas, at once —[^vallabha-83]  
**6.84.** a most fierce quarrel begins; the crowd of Gaudiyas is beaten.[^madia-84]  
**6.85.** The Gaudiyas said: "All the income from the temple (?) we shall eat."[^arajana-85]  

## The Fray on Govardhana and Prabhu's Word; "The Swami's Eight Shlokas"


**6.86.** They did not get to enjoy (?) the service of Gopala — and so a fierce quarrel broke out.[^gopala-86]  
**6.87.** The servants of Sri Vallabha gave some of them a beating.[^sevaka-87]  
**6.88.** Some of the Gaudiyas survived (?), and some departed from the body (??).[^deha-88]  
**6.89.** Some (?) remained in that place, near Rupa Gosai.[^rupa-89]  
**6.90.** By the [festival of the] Ratha (?) we heard the news: Kanhai came and told it.[^ratha-90]  
**6.91.** Kanhai reported to Prabhu. Prabhu said: "It has turned out well.[^prabhu-91]  
**6.92.** Whoever is 'beaten' by nature will be beaten constantly.[^mardia-92]  
**6.93.** And still sense will not enter them: as each one's nature is, [so he is]."  

*The Swami's Eight Shlokas*[^ashtaka-sh1]

**6.Sh1.** *[Skt.]* Supremely victorious is the sankirtana of Sri Krishna: it cleanses the mirror of the heart, extinguishes the great forest fire of worldly existence, sheds moonlight on the white lily of welfare, and is the life of the bride, knowledge; it swells the ocean of bliss, gives at every step a taste of full nectar, and bathes the whole self.  
**6.Sh1a.** In glorifying Krishna's qualities the mirror of the mind is cleansed.  
**6.Sh1b.** The stain of the soul goes away, and an exalted bhava streams forth.  
**6.Sh1c.** Sankirtana is the giver of teaching; it awakens bhakti.  
**6.Sh1d.** Pure prema grows; [sankirtana] gives a taste of the honey of prema for Krishna.  
**6.Sh1e.** All the senses are satisfied — such is the sankirtana of Krishna's name.  
**6.Sh2.** *[Skt.]* You have manifested many names and placed all Your potencies in them, and set no [special] time for remembering [them]. Such is Your mercy, O Lord — and such is my misfortune that no attachment [to them] has arisen in me.  
**6.Sh2a.** Your names are endless; many names You bear.  
**6.Sh2b.** In all the names is Your power; it is manifestly seen (??), [the sages] say.[^vadanti-sh2b]  
**6.Sh2c.** In remembering and chanting the Name there is no distinction of proper or improper time.  
**6.Sh2d.** O Prabhu! Such is Your mercy, but (?) I am unfortunate.  
**6.Sh2e.** In my (?) heart there is no faith, and love (?) for the Name does not arise (?).[^adara-sh2e]  
**6.Sh3.** *[Skt.]* Humbler than a blade of grass, more tolerant than a tree, seeking no honour and giving honour [to others] — [thus] should Hari always be glorified.  
**6.Sh3a.** Deem [yourself] lower than grass; be more tolerant than a tree.  
**6.Sh3b.** Giving up your own happiness and sorrow, pour out love upon others.  
**6.Sh3c.** Always repeat the name of Hari — there is no other refuge than this.  
**6.Sh4.** *[Skt.]* Neither wealth, nor followers, nor a beautiful woman, nor poetry do I desire, O Lord of the world. May there be in every birth of mine causeless devotion to You, O Ishvara.  
**6.Sh4a.** I want neither wealth nor followers, nor the jewel of a beautiful wife.  
**6.Sh4b.** [Nor] to become a poet by composing (?) verses. O Prabhu! Why should I desire [these]?  
**6.Sh4c.** Piteously I pray: O Prabhu! Birth after birth,  
**6.Sh4d.** becoming Your follower, may I go on performing bhakti.  
**6.Sh5.** *[Skt.]* O son of Nanda! Me, Your servant, fallen into the terrible ocean of existence, consider in Your mercy a particle of dust at Your lotus feet.[^padapankaja-sh5]  
**6.Sh5a.** O Krishna, son of the cowherd Nanda! Have mercy, O Gopinatha!  
**6.Sh5b.** A fallen fool in this world, I am sinking and perishing in the ocean of existence.  
**6.Sh5c.** At Your lotus feet hold [me] as a speck of dust.  

## "The Swami's Eight Shlokas" (conclusion); the End of Chapter Six


**6.Sh6.** *[Skt.]* When, as I take Your name, will my eyes [run] with streams of tears, my mouth [falter] with a voice choked with emotion, and my body be covered with hairs standing on end?[^shloka-sh6]  
**6.Sh6a.** O Jagannatha, round-eyed One! When will that day come[^chakaakhi-sh6a]  
**6.Sh6b.** when, as Your name is uttered, [my] eyes will shed tears,  
**6.Sh6c.** when, as soon as I utter Your name, my throat will be choked entirely,  
**6.Sh6d.** and my whole body will tremble with joy, its hairs standing on end?  
**6.Sh7.** *[Skt.]* A moment has become for me like a yuga, my eyes like the rainy season, the whole world has become empty — in separation from Govinda.[^yuga-sh7]  
**6.Sh7a.** In separation from Hari my heart burns beyond measure;  
**6.Sh7b.** a moment is like a yuga, water streams from my eyes in torrents;  
**6.Sh7c.** the whole world (?) seems empty — heavy is the pain of separation from Hari.  
**6.Sh8.** *[Skt.]* Let Him embrace [me], who am devoted to His feet, or trample me; or let Him wound my heart by not showing Himself — let that rake do as He likes: still the Lord of my life is He, and no other.[^shloka-sh8]  
**6.Sh8a.** O Krishna! Give me pain — or employ [me in service] at Your feet;[^khata-sh8a]  
**6.Sh8b.** embrace me — or do not give [me] darshana;  
**6.Sh8c.** [though] You sport, absorbed in love and intoxicated with it, … (??) —[^unclear-sh8c]  
**6.Sh8d.** still You are my life: apart from You [I] have no other refuge.  
**6.Sh8e.** All this bhava of Radha (?) the Swami described in eight shlokas.[^radha-sh8e]  

*Thus ends the sixth chapter.*

[^mahamantra-1]: The mahamantra is "Hare Krishna Hare Krishna Krishna Krishna Hare Hare / Hare Rama Hare Rama Rama Rama Hare Hare" (*Kali-santarana Upanishad*). In the common recensions of the Upanishad the mantra begins with "Hare Rama"; Gaudiya Vaishnavas chant it beginning with "Hare Krishna," as Mahaprabhu Himself taught (CB, Madhya 23). The dispute over the order of the two halves of the mantra and Mahaprabhu's judgement (6.2–18) are an Odia tradition; they are not in the CC/CB. Printed ସାଧବେ is taken as a vocative plural, "O sadhus."
[^disputants-3]: Shivananda is Shivananda Sena, the Bengali devotee who every year led the Gaudiyas to Puri for the Ratha-yatra (CC, Madhya 16; Antya 1, 12). Kanhai is apparently Kanai Khuntia, an Odia Vaishnava of the Panchasakha circle (cf. 5.11).
[^svarupa-5]: Svarupa Damodara, Mahaprabhu's closest companion in Nilachala; according to the CC (Antya 5), every composition or question meant for Mahaprabhu first passed through him.
[^svami-6]: Svami is Jagannatha Dasa (cf. 5.101, 5.119–124). In Madhava the dispute is settled through him, with Svarupa's mediation; there is no such episode in the CC/CB.
[^ramaray-8]: Rama Raya is Ramananda Raya; Sarvabhauma is Sarvabhauma Bhattacharya; Kashi Mishra is the one in whose house Mahaprabhu lived in Puri (CC, Madhya 10).
[^mandapa-9]: The first word is printed unclearly (≈ ଭ + a blotted sign) and is read as "northern" only from the sense and the metre (2 syllables, ≈ ଉତ୍ତ). *Pāruśa maṇḍapa* is a "side mandapa" (a pillared hall); which building is meant is not said.
[^govinda-10]: Ananta and Achyuta (Achyutananda) belong to the Panchasakha. "Govindai" is probably Govinda, Mahaprabhu's servant (CC, Madhya 10), with the affectionate ending ‑ai; the reading is uncertain.
[^sides-12]: Gadadhara is Gadadhara Pandita, Madhava's teacher. In Madhava the Bengalis (Shivananda, Gadadhara) stand for "Hare Krishna," the Odias and residents of Puri (Kanhai, Kashi Mishra, and also Sarvabhauma, though he came from Navadvipa) for "Hare Rama."
[^decision-15]: Mahaprabhu's decision — "Hare Rama" first for Nilachala, "Hare Krishna" for Gauda (6.15–18) — is an Odia tradition. Gaudiya Vaishnavas, following Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati, chant the mahamantra beginning with "Hare Krishna" (cf. the note to 6.1).
[^chhadiba-16]: The last word (printed ≈ ଗ୍ର/ଛ + ଡ଼ + ବା) is read as ଛାଡ଼ିବା "shall we give up" — a guess; if it is ଗଢ଼ିବା "shall we set," the sense is: "What else should we do — put 'Hare Krishna' at the beginning?" Printed ଏଥକୁ, କ, କଶବା are normalised as ଏଥିକୁ, କି, କରିବା.
[^gauda-18]: Gauda is Bengal. *Kahu bhala* is taken as "you speak rightly" (addressed to Shivananda).
[^shivananda-19]: According to the CC (Madhya 16; Antya 12), Shivananda Sena every year led the Bengali devotees to Puri for the Ratha-yatra, bore the travel expenses and tolls, and arranged their lodging. The reading "pilgrims" (printed ≈ ଯତ୍ରି) is uncertain.
[^kauri-21]: Cowries are shells used as small coin. If the reading is right, Madhava speaks of income from bringing pilgrims. In the CC, by contrast, Shivananda Sena himself maintains the pilgrims (Madhya 16; Antya 12), and Gaudiya Vaishnavas honour him as an eternal associate of the Lord. The word ଅଖିଳର ("from everywhere, of all") is printed ≈ ଆଖିଳର.
[^kanhai-22]: Who "took the pilgrims" is not said; understood as Kanhai's complaint against Shivananda. ଘେନ ପଳା — "taking [them], went off" (?).
[^tasa-23]: Whom Kanhai brought ("about a hundred," *śaheka*) — his own pilgrims or his own men — is not said. ତାସ is taken as *trāsa* "fear" — uncertain.
[^heta-24]: Printed ≈ ହେତ — taken as ହେତୁ "because of [this]" (rhyming with ମଧ୍ୟସ୍ଥ).
[^sambarile-25]: Printed ≈ ସମ୍ବାଶଲେ ("ri" as ଶ) — read ସମ୍ବାରିଲେ "restrained, calmed" (cf. ସମ୍ବରିବା); possibly ସମ୍ଭାଳିଲେ "settled."
[^madhavi-26]: Madhavi is Madhavi-devi, the sister of Shikhi Mahiti; in the CC (Antya 2) Mahaprabhu counts among Radha's associates "three and a half" persons in the world — Svarupa Damodara, Ramananda Raya, Shikhi Mahiti and, as the "half," his sister Madhavi. In Madhava the brother is called ମହାନ୍ତର ("Mahanti"). The account of Madhavi continues on sheet 88 (6.27 ff.).
[^madhavi-27]: Madhavi Dasi (cf. 6.26) — in the CC (Antya 2), Madhavi-devi, the sister of Shikhi Mahiti: an elderly, strict ascetic whom Mahaprabhu counted among the associates of Srimati Radharani. The CC says nothing of her songs or of her taking part in Mahaprabhu's gatherings; this is Odia tradition. *Bhagata* is taken as "devotion" (= *bhakti*); "[she was] a great devotee" is also possible.
[^bhola-28]: *Bhoḷa* — "lost, enraptured"; in this font ଭା is set as a ligature, so ଭୋଳ, ଭାବ, ଭାବେ (6.28, 6.30, 6.37, 6.47) look like ଭେଳ, ଭବ, ଭେବ. Cf. "ମତ ଭୋଳେ" (3.53), "ଭୋଳ ହୋନ୍ତି" (1.44).
[^svami-30]: Svami — Jagannatha Dasa (cf. 6.6–7); Prabhu — Mahaprabhu.
[^goshthi-31]: Goshthi, ishta-goshthi — a gathering of devotees for talk about the Lord (cf. the note to 5.108). Gaudiya Vaishnavas, following the CC (Antya 2), stress that Mahaprabhu as a sannyasi most strictly avoided the company of women and required the same of His renounced companions; the account of His seating Madhavi in the gathering is Odia tradition, not found in the CC/CB.
[^vishvasa-33]: The last word (printed ≈ ବଶ୍ୱ:ସ) is read as ବିଶ୍ୱାସ "trust, faith"; the understanding of the half-line — "even if she is a devotee, [what] trust [is there]?" — is uncertain. "Even if she is a devotee [worthy of] trust" is also possible. Printed ଅଛ is taken as ଅଛି "is".
[^amhanta-34]: The first word (printed ≈ ଆମ୍ଭ + a sign like ନ୍ତ with a tail) is taken as "we" (cf. ଆମ୍ଭେ); the form is not identified. *Jaṇāi* — "making known, declaring".
[^svami-35]: The end of the first half-line (printed ≈ ମଟ ସଲ / ମଡ ସଲ) is not identified. Svami (Jagannatha Dasa) here defends Madhavi; the understanding of 6.36a ("is there anywhere another [such] devotee?", printed ≈ କାହିଁଛ କ) is from the sense.
[^speaker-37]: The speaker of 6.37–40 is not marked: it may be Svami continuing (having acknowledged Madhavi's devotion, he nevertheless grants the concern) or the devotees' reply; the conclusion in 6.40 ("her coming [must be] forbidden") and 6.41 show that this argument prevailed. Cf. in the CC (Madhya 11) the saying cited by Mahaprabhu: a renunciant should be wary even of the form of a woman, as even the form of a snake disturbs the mind; in the CC (Antya 2), the *Bhagavata* verse that the senses drag away even the learned. *Paśu yāi* is taken as "become [like] beasts" — uncertain (printed ≈ ପବୁ/ପଶୁ ଯାଇ); possibly ପଶି ଯାଇ "having come (lit. entered) near a woman".
[^prasanga-39]: *Parasaṅga* (= *prasaṅga*) — "matter, point [of the argument]" or "contact, association"; possibly "with Madhavi [ours is] real contact".
[^chaitanya-42]: Probably a pun: "not all are Chaitanya", i.e. not all of Mahaprabhu's companions are like Him, or "not all are awakened" (*caitanya* — "consciousness, awakening"). Madhava turns to the story of Junior Haridasa.
[^proverb-43]: 6.43–45 echo a well-known Sanskrit saying (attributed to Chanakya): not every mountain has gems, not every elephant a pearl, not every forest sandalwood, nor are sadhus found everywhere. Printed ≈ ଥାଇଟି is read as ଥାଇନ୍ତି "is found".
[^svarupa-45]: Svarupa — Svarupa Damodara, himself a Bengali and the model Gaudiya Vaishnava; the understanding "not all Gaudiya Vaishnavas are alike in nature (*swarūpa*)" is also possible.
[^haridasa-46]: Junior Haridasa (*sāna* "junior, little"; in the CC, Chhota Haridasa), a kirtan singer of Mahaprabhu. According to the CC (Antya 2), at Bhagavan Acharya's request he begged fine rice for Mahaprabhu from Madhavi-devi; learning that a renunciant had spoken with a woman, Mahaprabhu forbade him admittance; a year later Haridasa went to Prayaga and gave up his body at the Triveni, after which, according to the CC, he continued to sing for Mahaprabhu in a spiritual body. Gaudiya Vaishnavas see in this a lesson for renunciants, not a fall of Haridasa. Madhava's account (6.47–53: growing intimacy, pretexts, the neighbours' suspicion, the beating) is absent from the CC/CB. The reading *saṅgī* "companion" (printed ≈ ମଙ୍ଗୀ/ସଙ୍ଗୀ) is uncertain.
[^rice-49]: In the CC (Antya 2) Haridasa asks Madhavi for rice to feed Mahaprabhu, not for himself. *Turite* "hastily" (printed ≈ ତୁଶତେ, "ri" as ଶ) is an uncertain reading; lit. "for the sake of rice and salt, at her place [he] quickly meets / obtains".
[^shikhi-50]: Shikhi Mahanti — in the CC, Shikhi Mahiti, Madhavi's brother (cf. 6.26). Printed ମହାନ୍ତ (here and in 6.53) — probably ମହାନ୍ତି with the ‑i lost.
[^saibhai-51]: *Sāibhāi* — "neighbours, people of the quarter" (*sāi* — ward, street); printed ≈ ସାଇ + ligature ଭା (?) + ଇ.
[^beating-53]: There is no beating by Shikhi Mahiti in the CC.
[^prabhu-55]: The second half-line is not read with confidence: printed ≈ ବ + େ + a dark sign (ଞ୍ଚ/ଷ/ଝ?) — ବଞ୍ଚେ "lives, survives"?; the translation "has Haridasa [behaved] well?" is from the sense. "[He] did well" is taken as approval of Shikhi Mahanti. In the CC (Antya 2) Mahaprabhu Himself, with no complaint from the Gaudiyas, tells Govinda not to admit Haridasa, saying that He cannot look at the face of a renunciant who converses with a woman.
[^haridasa-57]: Haridasa is "Sana Haridasa," Junior Haridasa (cf. 6.46–56). According to the CC (Antya 2), Mahaprabhu rejected him because, as a renunciant, he had begged rice from a woman, Madhavi-devi; after a year Haridasa went to Prayaga and entered the waters of the Triveni, after which he attained a spiritual body and went on singing for Mahaprabhu unseen. Gaudiya Vaishnavas see in this a lesson of strictness for sannyasis and the Lord's mercy to Haridasa. In Madhava the cause of his leaving is different (a beating from Shikhi Mahanti, cf. 6.53), and how it all ended is not stated outright — only "threw himself into the Ganga" (6.58). *Rāti praharu* — "in [one of] the night watches"; a watch (prahara) is about three hours.
[^ganga-58]: In the CC (Antya 2) it is the Triveni at Prayaga, the confluence of the Ganga and the Yamuna; in Madhava simply "the Ganga."
[^bisuri-59]: The last word is printed ≈ ବସୁଶଥା; read as ବିସୁରିଥା from *bisuribā* "to grieve, lament" ("ri" as ଶ, ‑i lost); the form in ‑ithā is unclear. The words of 6.60–64 are taken as the Gaudiyas' speech.
[^jaga-61]: Jaga Dasa, Jagadasa (6.63) is Jagannatha Dasa, called "Svami" by Madhava (cf. 6.6). The first unclear word (printed ≈ ଯ + ଢ଼ି with a dot) is read as ଯହିଁ "since" — a guess.
[^mauna-62]: Both marked words are unclear. The first (printed ≈ a sign like ଅ/ଝ/ମ + ଉ + ନ) is read as ମଉନ (= *mauna*) "silence": "He keeps silent with us," i.e. pays us no heed. The second (printed ≈ blotted ନ/ଚ + ା + କ) is read as ନାକ "nose," in the idiom "the nose is gone" = "honour is lost"; this reading is very uncertain.
[^upadesha-63]: *Aṇa-upadeśiā* — "one who has received no instruction (upadesha)," i.e. no initiation from a guru; this is the Gaudiyas' reproach against Jagannatha Dasa. In Madhava, Prabhu nevertheless calls him "Svami"; Jagannatha Dasa is not mentioned in the CC/CB.
[^leave-65]: The Gaudiyas' departure from Nilachala in resentment against Mahaprabhu, their halt at Yajanagara and their return to Gauda (6.59–80) are an Odia tradition; there is nothing of this in the CC/CB. According to the CC, the Gaudiyas headed by Shivananda Sena came to Mahaprabhu every year for the Ratha-yatra and spent the chaturmasya with Him (Madhya 16; Antya 10, 12).
[^upadhi-67]: Printed ≈ ଉ + ପ/ଦ with a subscript + ାଧି; read as ଉପାଧି "trouble, difficulty" — uncertain.
[^tu-69]: In 6.68–70 Shivananda addresses Prabhu with the familiar "thou" (*tohara, tora, tote*); rendered "You." The first word of 6.69b (printed ≈ ନମିତ୍ତ, first sign blotted) is read as ନିମିତ୍ତ "for the sake of."
[^kshetra-70]: The last word is printed ≈ େ + କ୍ଷ + ତ/ତ୍ର + ର; read as କ୍ଷେତ୍ରର "of the Kshetra" (genitive meaning "to the Kshetra"?) — uncertain.
[^speakers-72]: The speakers in 6.71–73 are not marked. Understood thus: Prabhu wants Svami to go with Him after those who left; Svami turns away (*bimukha*) and declines. The word in 6.72a (printed ≈ ଚ/ବୁ + ହାଁ; cf. 6.71a ≈ ଚ + ହିଁ + ଲ) is read as ଚାହାଁ "look" (from *cāhi̐bā*); ବୁଝା "understand" ("Do you not understand?") is also possible. ମୋ ତହୁଁ (?) is taken as "with Me / from Me."
[^murkha-73]: "A fool" — apparently Svami speaking of himself (self-abasement) or of those who left. The end of the line (printed ≈ ଡାକିଲେ ଆସିବେ + a blotted ନ/ମ + କଲେ) is unclear: "if You call, they will come; [but if You] do not do [so]…" or "…they did not do [it]."
[^ashtaka-75]: The "eight shlokas" are apparently the *Shikshashtaka* (cf. 6.77: Shivananda passes them off as written by Prabhu; after 6.93 the edition gives these verses under the heading "Svami's eight shlokas"). Madhava ascribes them to Jagannatha Dasa, who expressed Prabhu's bhava in them — an Odia tradition. Gaudiya Vaishnavas, following Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati, revere the *Shikshashtaka* as the composition of Sri Chaitanya Himself: according to the CC (Antya 20), Mahaprabhu Himself recited and relished these verses with Svarupa Damodara and Ramananda Raya in the last years of His lila; a number of these verses are given in Rupa Gosvami's *Padyavali* as the words of the Lord Himself.
[^lekhichu-77]: *Lekhichu* is taken as an old perfect form "has written" (cf. ‑achu); the words "Prabhu wrote this" are apparently what Shivananda told those who had left.
[^jajnagar-78]: Yajanagara is Jajpur (Yajapura) on the Vaitarani river, on the road from Puri to Bengal. *Pariyantare* — "as far as" (= *paryanta*).
[^shivananda-79]: According to the CC, Shivananda Sena is a close companion of Mahaprabhu who brought the Gaudiyas to Puri every year (Madhya 16; Antya 12); that he left with the offended party and did not return is not in the CC/CB.
[^rupa-81]: Rupa and Sanatana Gosvami lived in Vrindavana by Mahaprabhu's order, restoring the holy places and the Deity service (CC, Madhya 19–20, 23).
[^seyananta-82]: The third word of 6.82a (printed ≈ ସେ + an incomplete sign like ଯ/ବ + ାନନ୍ତ) is not identified; the sense of the line is a guess. *Yābanta* — "as far as."
[^vallabha-83]: Vallabha is Vallabhacharya, founder of the Pushti-marga Vaishnava sampradaya; according to the CC (Antya 7), he met Mahaprabhu in Puri. The Deity of Gopala on Govardhana was manifested by Madhavendra Puri (CC, Madhya 4); later His service passed to Vallabha's followers (Shrinathji). The quarrel of the Gaudiyas with Vallabha's disciples at Govardhana (6.83–86) is Madhava's account; it is not in the CC. *Cāṭa* — "disciple." The word in 6.83b (printed ≈ ନରେଟି) is read as ନିରେଖି "on seeing" — uncertain.
[^madia-84]: *Māṛiā* — "beaten, having received a beating" (cf. 6.92 *māṛiā sbabhāba* "the nature of one who gets beaten").
[^arajana-85]: *Arajana* (= *arjana*) — "what is acquired, income," i.e. the temple offerings. The first unclear word (printed ≈ େ + ଦ/ଢ + ଉ + ଳୁ) is read as ଦେଉଳୁ "from the temple"; the rhyme in *‑u* (*deuḷu / khāibu*) supports this.
[^gopala-86]: Gopala is the Deity revealed by Madhavendra Puri on Govardhana (CC, Madhya 4); later His service passed to the followers of Vallabha (cf. the note to 6.83). According to Madhava, the Gaudiyas wanted to take over the service and the temple income (6.85). The key word (printed ≈ ଭୁଞ୍ଜଲେ, first sign smudged) is read as ଭୁଞ୍ଜିଲେ "enjoyed, obtained the use of" — uncertain. The quarrel between the Gaudiyas and Vallabha's disciples is not in the CC/CB.
[^sevaka-87]: Sri Vallabha — Vallabhacharya (cf. 6.83). *Māile māṛa* — "gave a beating" (*māile* = ମାରିଲେ).
[^deha-88]: The reading is uncertain. 6.88a: printed ≈ ବଞ୍ଚଲେ, taken as ବଞ୍ଚିଲେ "survived". 6.88b: printed ≈ ଛୁଡ଼ି ଗଲେକ ଦେଥୁ ଥୋକେ; ଦେଥୁ is corrected to ଦେହୁ "from the body" — "some departed from the body", i.e. perished; ଦେଶୁ "from the country" — "some fled the land" — is also possible. The contrast with "survived" supports the first sense, but it remains doubtful. The CC/CB say nothing of this.
[^rupa-89]: Rupa Gosai — Rupa Gosvami (cf. 6.81). Printed ≈ କହୁ with an inner stroke in କ (as in କିପାଁ, 6.Sh4b) is read as କିଛୁ "some".
[^ratha-90]: *Rathaku* is taken as "for the Ratha [festival]", i.e. the Ratha-yatra, when pilgrims gather in Puri (the first printed letter is ର; the reading ଏଥକୁ is less likely). *Śuṇilu* — "we heard": Madhava speaks as an eyewitness. Kanhai is the same as in 6.3–22; possibly Kanai Khutiya, an Odia associate of Mahaprabhu (in the CC, Madhya 15, he appears at the Nandotsava festival dressed as Nanda Maharaja) — the identification needs checking.
[^prabhu-91]: The speaker in 6.91b–93 is Prabhu. The account of the Gaudiyas leaving Puri, of the fray in Vrindavana and of such a remark by Mahaprabhu is not in the CC/CB. According to the CC (Madhya 11, 16), the Bengali devotees, led by Shivananda Sena, came to Puri every year for the Ratha-yatra and returned to Bengal by Mahaprabhu's will; Gaudiya Vaishnavas see in His dealings with them unfailing love.
[^mardia-92]: *Māṛiā swabhāba* — lit. "the nature of one beaten" (cf. 6.84): one who by nature invites a beating; "a quarrelsome nature" is also possible.
[^ashtaka-sh1]: The heading "ସ୍ୱାମୀର ଅଷ୍ଟଶ୍ଲୋକ" ("The Swami's Eight Shlokas") stands in the edition; whether it belongs to the manuscript is unknown. Sanskrit shlokas 1–5 (sheets 92–93; shlokas 6–8 are on sheet 94) coincide with the *Śikṣāṣṭaka*; each is followed by an Odia paraphrase in couplets (whether by Madhava or by the Swami is not stated). Madhava ascribes the shlokas to the Swami, i.e. Jagannatha Dasa: in 6.75 "the Swami wrote eight shlokas — all the bhavas of Prabhu there are" — as a letter calling the Gaudiyas back; in 6.77 Shivananda presents them as written by Prabhu; on sheet 94 (outside this portion) the closing couplet again says that the Swami set forth the eight shlokas. This is Odia tradition. Gaudiya Vaishnavas, following Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati, honour the *Śikṣāṣṭaka* as the composition of Sri Chaitanya Himself: according to the CC (Antya 20), Mahaprabhu Himself composed the eight shlokas to instruct people and in the last years of His lila relished them with Svarupa Damodara and Ramananda Raya; several of these shlokas are cited in Rupa Gosvami's *Padyavali* as the words of the Lord Himself. The Sanskrit text is written in its usual spelling where the edition's type loses ‑i/‑ī; printed deviations are noted in the word-for-word.
[^vadanti-sh2b]: The word before *badanti* (printed ≈ ଦ + ଶ with the signs ି and ୁ/୍ର + େ + ଛ with a dot) is not read with confidence; ଦିଶୁଛେ "is seen, appears" is adopted. *Badanti* is Sanskrit for "they say".
[^adara-sh2e]: Printed ≈ ମୋହ — taken as "my" (= ମୋହର?); *moha* "delusion [in the heart]" is also possible. The last words (printed ≈ ?ଦର େହଇ) are read as ଆଦର ହେଇ "love arises"; the negation is understood from the first half, following the sense of the Sanskrit shloka (*nānurāgaḥ* "no attachment").
[^padapankaja-sh5]: In print the line ends with ପାଦପଙ୍କଜମ and the next begins with ସ୍ଥିତ; in the usual text of the shloka this is a single compound, *pāda-paṅkaja-sthita-dhūlī-sadṛśam* "like a particle of dust resting at the lotus feet". The final ‑ମ is probably a misprint.
[^shloka-sh6]: Shlokas 6–8 of "The Swami's Eight Shlokas" coincide with the sixth to eighth verses of the *Shikshashtaka* (CC, Antya 20.36, 20.39, 20.47); on Madhava's ascription of them to the Swami (Jagannatha Dasa) and on the position of the Gaudiya Vaishnavas, who honour the *Shikshashtaka* as the words of Sri Chaitanya Himself, see the notes to 6.75 and 6.Sh1. In the printed Sanskrit text ‑i is lost in places; *gadgada* is printed ≈ ଗଦନଦ (the sign after the first ଦ looks like ନ). The translation follows the usual text of the shloka.
[^chakaakhi-sh6a]: *Cakā-ākhi* — "round-eyed," lit. "with wheel- (disc-) eyes": a favourite Odia epithet of Jagannatha, whose round eyes are wide open. The Sanskrit shloka does not name the Lord; the Odia rendering addresses Jagannatha. The last word (printed ≈ ଆସିବକ) is read as ଆସିବ କି "will [it] come?"; the rhyme *ākhi / ki* supports this.
[^yuga-sh7]: A yuga is a world age lasting thousands of years; "the rainy season" stands for ceaseless tears. According to the CC (Antya 20), Mahaprabhu utters this shloka in the mood of Sri Radha's separation from Krishna.
[^shloka-sh8]: The print has ମା instead of *mām* "me" and ମଦର୍ଶନାନ୍… instead of *adarśanān…* "by not showing Himself" (the first sign is ମ, not ଅ); probably misprints — the translation follows the usual text of the shloka. *Pāda-ratām* is feminine: the speaker is a devoted gopi. According to the CC (Antya 20), Mahaprabhu explains this shloka in the mood of Sri Radha: whatever Krishna does, He is the Lord of Her life.
[^khata-sh8a]: *Khaṭāa* is the imperative of *khaṭāibā* "to set to work, make serve": "employ [me] at Your feet," i.e. make me Your maidservant. In 6.Sh8a–d the Lord is addressed with the familiar "thou" (*to, tu*); the translation has "You."
[^unclear-sh8c]: The second half (printed ≈ ଯାପନ ଗୃହି ଉନ୍ମଥଳ) is not understood. Possible meanings of the words: *yāpana* "spending [time]," *gṛhi* "in a house / householder," *unmatha-* "agitating, maddened"; from the sense of the Sanskrit shloka (*yathā tathā vā vidadhātu lampaṭaḥ* "let the rake do as He likes") one might expect "[You] spend Your time in others' houses" — but the printed text does not yield such a reading. *Mata* in the first half is taken as *matta* "intoxicated."
[^radha-sh8e]: The first word is printed ≈ ରଧାର (the ‑ā after ର is not visible); read as ରାଧାର "Radha's" (the OCR reads the same). *Bhāba* is printed ଭବ (the ଭା ligature). The couplet closes the "Eight Shlokas": the Swami (Jagannatha Dasa) expressed in them the bhava of Radha; cf. 6.75, where the same shlokas are "the bhavas of Prabhu." For Gaudiya Vaishnavas there is no contradiction here: according to the CC (Antya 20), Mahaprabhu, Krishna Himself in the mood and radiance of Radha, Himself composed the *Shikshashtaka* and relished it in Radha's bhava; the Swami's authorship is an Odia tradition (cf. the note to 6.Sh1).
