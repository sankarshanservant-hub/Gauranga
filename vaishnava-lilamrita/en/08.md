# Madhava Pattanayak. *Vaishnava-lilamrita*, Chapter 8
## Nagara-kirtana at Dawn; the Child Who Fell from the Pinda, and the Mother's Anger

*Translated from Odia without a native speaker; doubtful places are marked (?) and (??). The original, transliteration and word-for-word translation are in `full/08.md`.*

*Chapter Eight*

**8.1.** Listen attentively, O sadhus, to the description of the nagara-kirtana.[^nagara-1]  
**8.2.** The mridanga came to be called "khol" — this is how it came about (?).[^khol-2]  
**8.3.** In the rasa of many kirtanas they go out into the town from dawn (?).[^prabhatu-3]  
**8.4.** Achyuta, Ananta, Yashovanta, Balarama and Jagannatha.[^panchasakha-4]  
**8.5.** With them are two mridanga players, surrounding (??) [them].[^berhaye-5]  
**8.6.** Prabhu is the great dancer: in the rasa of kirtana He dances.  
**8.7.** The kirtana — a booming sound, the sounds of jhanj, mridanga and talas.[^jhanja-7]  
**8.8.** At every door the children (?), hearing the sound of the kirtana, [wake up].[^balute-8]  
**8.9.** And in every house the mothers set about (?) the morning chores.[^basi-9]  
**8.10.** One child got up: "I will go and see the kirtana," he said.  
**8.11.** Getting up from his bed he runs headlong to see the kirtana at the door.  
**8.12.** He rolled (?) down from the pinda — from his head [poured] a heavy (?) stream of blood.[^pinda-12]  
**8.13.** The mother, busy with household work, runs [to him] wailing (??).[^krandane-13]  
**8.14.** She lifted the child into her arms and became angry with Chaitanya.[^chaitana-14]  
**8.15.** All the child's filth — she ties (??) [it] in a rag of thin [cloth].[^bandhe-15]  
**8.16.** Raising (?) [it] in her hand, she headed (?) for the street door.[^boligale-16]  
**8.17.** Reaching the threshold — [and there] the kirtana, a booming sound —  
**8.18.** the mother scolded at length and cried out in a loud voice.  
**8.19.** All the soiled rags she throws onto the kirtana.[^kirtan-19]  

## How the mridanga came to be called khol; Prabhu and the Panchasakha travel with kirtana: Alarnath, Bhubaneswar, Niali, Kenduli, Chandrabhaga


**8.20.** [The mother], in disgust, rages and shouts without end.[^mata-20]  
**8.21.** Prabhu caught up that rag and placed it on the mridanga.  
**8.22.** His body trembling with ecstatic thrill, He speaks in a faltering voice.  
**8.23.** He said: "We have obtained a treasure — the bliss of the rasa of kirtana!  
**8.24.** This is not a child's excrement" — He calls it real sandal paste.[^chandana-24]  
**8.25.** "Smear this sandal on your heads — [it is] the veneration of the rasa of kirtana."[^bola-25]  
**8.26.** Gathering the soiled rags, He laid [them] on the mridanga  
**8.27.** and says: "From today it shall be so: the mridanga shall be covered with this.  
**8.28.** In memory (?) of the angered (?) mother, make the infant's rag a covering.[^smarane-28]  
**8.29.** If this covering is not on the mridanga, the kirtana does not go on."  
**8.30.** From that day [the mridanga] was covered with cloth, and the mridanga came to be called khol.[^khol-30]  
**8.31.** Listen, good people, to your heart's delight, to the particulars of the rasa of kirtana.  
**8.32.** One day Prabhu summoned the Panchasakha and says: "Come!  
**8.33.** Do come, Swami, Balarama! Come, Achyuta and Ananta![^swami-33]  
**8.34.** Yashovanta, you come too! Swami, sit beside Me.  
**8.35.** Let us perform abundant (?) kirtana and wander from town to town.[^prachura-35]  
**8.36.** On the days of anavasara we shall go to Alarnath."[^anavasara-36]  
**8.37.** From the first lunar day [of the dark half] of Ashadha [they are away], and by the new moon they return.[^ashadha-37]  
**8.38.** Kirtana at Alarnath; [there] they taste the khiri-bhoga.[^khiri-38]  
**8.39.** Rama Raya, delighted, honours the Vaishnavas in every way.[^ramaray-39]  
**8.40.** Beside the murtis of the five Pandavas they perform kirtana without end.[^pandava-40]  
**8.41.** Thus every year they set out for Alarnath.  
**8.42.** On the full moon of Pausha they gather at Ekamra-kshetra.[^ekamra-42]  
**8.43.** At Ananta-Vasudeva's they manifest the rasa of kirtana.[^vasudeva-43]  
**8.44.** Krittivasa is that Lingaraja who manifests the form of Harihara.[^harihara-44]  
**8.45.** Offering tulasi and bilva leaves, they worship Lingaraja there.  
**8.46.** Bathing in the Gauri-kunda (?), worship of Kedareshvara,[^kedara-46]  
**8.47.** the bhoga of Ananta-Vasudeva, [a bath] with love in the Bindusagara.[^bindu-47]  
**8.48.** Without end they perform kirtana together with the Vaishnava devotees.  
**8.49.** [Then] the journey to Madhava at Niali, bathing in the Prachi.[^niali-49]  
**8.50.** Devotion (?) is offered to Madhava; they spread the rasa of kirtana.[^bhagata-50]  
**8.51.** A multitude of people gathers — they celebrate the Makara-mela.[^makara-51]  
**8.52.** In the rasa of dancing to the *Gita-Govinda*, they glorify Jayadeva.[^jayadeva-52]  
**8.53.** In the midst of the village of Kenduli they spread the rasa of kirtana.  
**8.54.** From there they go to the Chandrabhaga, where (??) [the river] falls into the sea at its mouth (?), lovely (??).[^chandrabhaga-54]  
**8.55.** Taking a dip [in the sea] on Chala-saptami (?), they perform kirtana, loudly proclaiming [the Name] (?).[^saptami-55]  
**8.56.** They worship Aditya-Vishnu and render service to the Sun.[^aditya-56]  
**8.57.** At the rising of the red sun Prabhu becomes intoxicated and forgets Himself.[^aruna-57]  

## Prabhu's yearly round of travels with the Panchasakha: Chandrabhaga, Kakatpur, the Dola in Cuttack, Jajpur — the home of Prabhu's ancestors, Tulasi-kshetra; the Panchasakha in kirtana


**8.58.** [Prabhu] manifests mahabhava and dances in many ways (?).[^mahabhava-58]  
**8.59.** Together with the Panchasakha, in the circle of devotees [absorbed] in kirtana,  
**8.60.** they taste the bhoga of khiri and laddus and perform the worship of Vishnu.[^laddu-60]  
**8.61.** On the way — worship of Mangala; Prabhu is seized by "Radha-bhrama" (?).[^mangala-61]  
**8.62.** By the road through the village of Kakatpur they arrive at the borders of the Kshetra.[^kshetra-62]  
**8.63.** For the Dola they go to Cuttack, to the festival at the royal palace.[^dola-63]  
**8.64.** The Gajapati Rudradeva shows honour to the Vaishnavas.[^rudradeva-64]  
**8.65.** The queens (?) greet [them] and perform the worship of the Vaishnavas.[^rani-65]  
**8.66.** [The Vaishnavas] manifest the rasa of kirtana; seeing Prabhu's mahabhava,  
**8.67.** the king becomes beside himself and is immersed (?) in the rasa of Krishna.  
**8.68.** From there they set out for Viraja (?), [take] their bath in the Vaitarani,[^viraja-68]  
**8.69.** halt at the Dashashvamedha Ghat; the Vaishnavas perform kirtana.  
**8.70.** This is Prabhu's ancestral place; it is called Yajanagara (?).[^yajanagara-70]  
**8.71.** [Prabhu's] grandfather was named Upendra; [he was] of the Vatsasa, a samanta, a kulina.[^vatsasa-71]  
**8.72.** He bore the title of Mishra, a Vedic [brahmana], the holder of a sasana,  
**8.73.** a pandit in the six darsanas, versed in Nyaya and Vedanta.[^darshana-73]  
**8.74.** Maharaja Kapileshvara threatened [him] with many quarrels (?).[^kapilendra-74]  
**8.75.** Out of fear of the king he fled, went to Shrihatta and settled there.[^shrihatta-75]  
**8.76.** His son was Jagannatha, and from him Prabhu appeared (lit. "was born").  
**8.77.** When they came to Jajpur, in joy, in the circle of kirtana,  
**8.78.** [His kinsmen], learning who Prabhu was, bow down to His feet.[^paricaya-78]  
**8.79.** Plastering doors and houses with cow-dung, they set out lamps and full pitchers.[^gomaya-79]  
**8.80.** Lovingly (?) they honoured Prabhu, and He manifested mahabhava.[^sarage-80]  
**8.81.** After staying there some days (?), they go on again to Tulasi-kshetra.[^tulasi-81]  
**8.82.** There they have darshan of Baladeva and partake of prasada.  
**8.83.** By the time of the bathing of the Deities they return to the Kshetra.[^snana-83]  
**8.84.** Thus, with the Panchasakha, Prabhu travels in the joy of dance.  
**8.85.** Year after year this is the custom: the Panchasakha accompany [Him].  
**8.86.** Prabhu, golden-fair in radiance, is the forgetting of sorrow.[^kanaka-86]  
**8.87.** Achyuta … (??) fathomless (??) — the narrative of the rasa-lila.[^achyuta-87]  
**8.88.** Swami is the guru (?), a great Meru of the rasa of kirtana.[^guru-88]  

## The roles of the Panchasakha in kirtana; Gadadhara and Svarupa remain in Puri; the fruits of kirtana; the Swami's *Bhagavata* in the villages; a song of the Swami


**8.89.** Balarama [Das] is sattvika, the one who sets bhava-rasa going.[^balarama-89]  
**8.90.** Ananta and Yashovanta, know, are connoisseurs of the rasa of kirtana.[^ananta-90]  
**8.91.** Guru Gadadhara performs worship; his abode is Tota-Gopinatha.[^gadadhara-91]  
**8.92.** He cannot go anywhere; he listens to [the narrations of] Krishna's lila.[^kshetra-92]  
**8.93.** Svarupa is frail of body; in [his] heart there is not this (?) maya (?).[^svarupa-93]  
**8.94.** He cannot go anywhere: he constantly endures suffering.  
**8.95.** The Panchasakha perform kirtana so as to give sons to the sonless.[^putra-95]  
**8.96.** From the jaws of wild beasts kirtana rescues living beings.[^beasts-96]  
**8.97.** Wherever nature turns hostile, people arrange the rasa (?) [of kirtana].[^prakriti-97]  
**8.98.** The Swami's *Bhagavata* manuscripts they worship as (??) a Deity.[^pothi-98]  
**8.99.** As many manuscripts of the *Sri Bhagavata* as there are — many have them copied and take them.  
**8.100.** In the villages the *Bhagavata* is read; they place it on a seat and worship it.[^asana-100]  
**8.101.** There they perform the rasa-lila and dance — the play of bhava and rasa.[^rasalila-101]  
**8.102.** Singing the Swami's pada songs, they dance, playing the khol and the tal.[^pada-102]  

*A song [of the Swami]*[^song-p0]

**8.P0.** [My] mind clings to Your feet! Giridhara! [My] mind clings to Your feet!  
**8.P1.** Best of dancers, nagara, thief of the gopis' garments — [my] mind clings to Your feet![^vasa-p1]  
**8.P2.** The Lotus-born is entangled (?) by You, [while You are] bound by the bhava of devotees — [my] mind clings to Your feet![^padmaja-p2]  
**8.P3.** Stealer of the gopis' hearts, thief of Radha's heart — [my] mind clings to Your feet!  
**8.P4.** Jaga Das says, abiding in the mood of a servant: the mind longs (?) for the treasure of prema — [my] mind clings to Your feet![^jaga-p4]  

## The invitation to Jhankad: the rasa before Sarala Chandi; wanderings with kirtana


**8.103.** Guru Achyuta gave instruction — [and] his disciple Ramachandra came (?).[^ramachandra-103]  
**8.104.** He says to the Swami: "Come, you (?), to Jhankadpur Patana!"[^jhankad-104]  
**8.105.** That (?) place where the rasa was arranged is truly a dhama (?).[^rasa-105]  
**8.106.** The sakhas gathered together, and Prabhu was with them.  
**8.107.** In that place they arranged the rasa — in the full measure of the rasa of kirtana.  
**8.108.** "This is not [merely] Sarala Chandi; [this] is truly Radha's … (??)."[^sarala-108]  
**8.109.** She is the limit of mahabhava, manifestly Radha's pratima.[^pratima-109]  
**8.110.** Prabhu plays the rasa-lila; intoxicated (?) with bhava, He dances.[^matti-110]  
**8.111.** Very lovely is this place: in it mahabhava arises (?) again.[^suramya-111]  
**8.112.** Thus a good while passes, with minds given to the rasa of kirtana.  
**8.113.** They wander through many towns and villages, spreading the rasa of kirtana.[^bhramanti-113]  
**8.114.** Says Madhava the cowherd (?): they go about intoxicated with the rasa of kirtana.[^guala-114]  

[^nagara-1]: Nagara-kirtana is a procession singing the holy names through the streets of a town. In the CC/CB nagara-kirtana is connected above all with Navadvipa (CC, Adi 17; CB, Madhya 23); Madhava describes the morning kirtana of Mahaprabhu and the Panchasakha in Puri. *Sādhabe* is the vocative plural "O sadhus."
[^khol-2]: Khol (*khoḷa*) is the Bengali and Odia name of the clay drum, the mridanga. According to Madhava, the name is connected with Odia *khoḷa* "cover, sheath": further on (8.20–30) it is told how the child's rag was laid on the mridanga and how since then the mridanga has been covered with cloth. The word *tahi̐ka* (printed ≈ ତ + ହିଁ + କ; OCR: କହିକ) is taken as "of that [event]"; the syntax of the half-line is uncertain.
[^prabhatu-3]: *Prabhātu* "from dawn" is printed ≈ ପ୍ରଭା + ତୁ in the second form, resembling ରୂ.
[^panchasakha-4]: The Panchasakha: Achyuta (Achyutananda), Ananta, Yashovanta, Balarama Dasa and Jagannatha Dasa (cf. 7.102, 7.144).
[^berhaye-5]: The last word is printed ≈ େ + ବ + ହା/ଢ଼ା + େ + ୟ (OCR: ବେଢ଼ାସ୍ତେ); tentatively read as *beṛhāẏe* "surrounding" (from *beṛhibā* "to surround"). Another word is possible (cf. *behāẏā* "unabashed"). The mridanga players are not named.
[^jhanja-7]: Jhanj are large paired cymbals; talas (*tāḷa*) are small hand cymbals (kartals). *Gaha gaha* imitates a booming, rolling sound (likewise in 8.17).
[^balute-8]: The word in 8.8a is printed ≈ ବା + a sign like ଜୁ + େ + an ink-filled ତ — the same signs as *bāḷuta* "child" in 8.10; read as *bāḷute* "children." The predicate of 8.8 is not expressed; by the sense the children wake at the sound of the kirtana (cf. 8.10–11).
[^basi-9]: *Bāsipāiṭi* — the morning household work (clearing up after the night: *bāsi* "of yesterday," *pāiṭi* "work"). The last word is printed ≈ ଉ + ଭ/ଭା + େ + ର (OCR: ଭଦ୍ର); read as *ubhāre* "take up, begin" (?) (cf. ଉଭାରିଛି 7.74).
[^pinda-12]: The pinda is the raised earthen platform-veranda in front of an Odia house. *Gaṛilara* (printed ≈ ଗଡ଼ଲର) has an unusual past ending ‑ilara, apparently for the rhyme with *dhāra*. *Bahu* (printed ≈ ବହୁ) is taken as "heavy, abundant"; possibly a verb "flowed."
[^krandane-13]: The first word of 8.13b is printed indistinctly: a sign with a loop on top and a flag at lower left (subscript ର?) + a round sign with an inner stroke (ନ୍ଦ?) + େ + ନ (OCR illegible). Tentatively *krandane* "wailing, with a cry." *Yāanti* is printed ≈ ଯାଅନ୍ତ (‑i lost).
[^chaitana-14]: *Caïtana* is a colloquial form of Chaitanya's name. The episode of the mother's anger is not in the CC/CB; it is Madhava's Odia tradition.
[^bandhe-15]: The last word of 8.15b is printed ≈ a sign like ସ/ବ + େ + a sign like ଷ/ଧ (OCR: ଘ୍ଷେ); tentatively *bandhe* "ties up." *Pataḷara* is taken as "of thin [cloth]" (*pataḷā* "thin"). The sense of the half-line is uncertain; cf. 8.26 (she "gathered" the rag, ଗୋଟାଇ).
[^boligale-16]: *Uñcāiṇa* "raising" is printed ≈ ଉ + a sign like ଞ୍ଚା + a sign like ନି/ଟି + ଣ. *Boligale* (printed ≈ ବୋଲିଗଲେ, lit. "saying, went") is taken by the sense as "headed for"; *dāṇḍa duāra* is the front door opening onto the street (*dāṇḍa* "street"). 8.17 continues syntactically in 8.18.
[^kirtan-19]: *Kīrttana upare* — "onto the kirtana," i.e. onto the kirtana party. *Sabunta* "all" has the ending ‑nta, apparently for the rhyme with *phiṅganti* (printed ≈ ଫିଙ୍ଗନ୍ତ). The story continues on leaf 106 (8.20 ff.).
[^mata-20]: This continues the story on leaf 105 (8.10–19): a child jumped out of bed and ran to see the kirtana, fell into a drain and cut his head; in her anger the mother threw the child's soiled rags into the kirtana. *Karanti* is an honorific plural; the subject is the mother (perhaps the mothers).
[^chandana-24]: Prabhu sees sandal paste in the child's excrement. This episode is found neither in the CC nor in the CB; it is an Odia tradition. Cf. CC, Antya 4: Mahaprabhu embraces Sanatana Gosvami, whose body is covered with oozing sores.
[^bola-25]: Printed ବୋଲ; by the sense we read ବୋଳ, "smear, apply" (the edition does not clearly distinguish ଲ and ଳ). The reading "say" (*bola*) is less likely. *Bandana* means "veneration, worship".
[^smarane-28]: The first word is printed ≈ ସ with a subscript sign and ୂ + ରଣେ: we read ସ୍ମରଣେ, "in memory". The third word ≈ କୁ + a sign like ସ/ମ/ପ with ‑i + ଲର: we read କୁପିଲର, "who was angered" (?). *Bāḷuta* means "infant"; *khoḷa*, "covering, sheath".
[^khol-30]: Khol is the Odia and Bengali name of the clay mridanga drum used in kirtana. Madhava derives the name from ଖୋଳ, "covering, sheath" (printed ଖୋଲ). The CC and CB often call the mridanga khol, but they give no such explanation of the name.
[^swami-33]: In Madhava "the Swami" is usually Jagannatha Dasa (cf. 7.2). *Swāmī baḷarāma* in 8.33a may be one person ("Swami Balarama"; cf. 7.93) or two addresses, to the Swami and to Balarama. The latter is more likely: 8.33–34 then name all five of the Panchasakha, and in 8.34b Prabhu again addresses the Swami (Jagannatha Dasa). Cf. the note to 7.93.
[^prachura-35]: Printed ≈ ପ୍ର + ଚ with a subscript hook + ର. We read ପ୍ରଚୁର, "abundant"; perhaps ପ୍ରଚାର, "preaching, propagation". According to the CC, Mahaprabhu entrusted the preaching of the holy name in Bengal to Nityananda (CC, Madhya 15; CB, Antya 5), while He Himself spent His last years in Puri without leaving it (CC, Madhya 1). Prabhu's travels through Orissa with the Panchasakha (8.36–57) are not described in the CC/CB; this is an Odia tradition.
[^anavasara-36]: Anavasara is the period of about two weeks after the Snana-yatra when Jagannatha is not shown to pilgrims. According to the CC (Madhya 11), on these days Mahaprabhu, unable to bear the separation, would go to Alalanatha — the CC's name for Alarnath at Brahmagiri, south of Puri. Printed ଅଲ୍ଲରନାଥେଣ.
[^ashadha-37]: When months are counted from full moon to full moon, the dark half of Ashadha begins right after the full moon of Jyeshtha (the Snana-yatra). Anavasara lasts from its first day to the new moon; then come the Netrotsava and the Ratha-yatra.
[^khiri-38]: Khiri is sweet rice cooked in milk; the khiri-bhoga of Alarnath is famous in Orissa to this day. Printed ଖିଶ୍ଭୋଗ ('ri' as ଶ).
[^ramaray-39]: Rama Raya is Ramananda Ray. The village of Bentapur, which Madhava connects with his family (3.78–81), lies not far from Alarnath. *Caracaï* means "honours, entertains, serves".
[^pandava-40]: Apparently murtis of the five Pandava brothers at the Alarnath temple. In the CC (Madhya 10) Mahaprabhu called Ramananda's father, Bhavananda Ray, Pandu, and his five sons the Pandavas.
[^ekamra-42]: Ekamra-kshetra (Ekamra-kanana) is Bhubaneswar, the holy city of Shiva. The full moon of Pausha falls in December–January. The CB (Antya 2) describes how Mahaprabhu, on His way to Puri, visited Bhubaneswar and bathed in the Bindu-sarovara. Printed ପୂର୍ଣ୍ଣିମୀ (= ପୂର୍ଣ୍ଣିମା).
[^vasudeva-43]: Ananta-Vasudeva is a temple of Krishna (Vasudeva) with Balarama (Ananta) and Subhadra on the bank of the Bindusagara in Bhubaneswar.
[^harihara-44]: Krittivasa ("clad in a hide") is a name of Shiva; Lingaraja is the principal Deity of Bhubaneswar. In Odia tradition Lingaraja is worshipped as Harihara, Shiva and Vishnu in one form. Hence both bilva and tulasi are offered to Him (8.45). Gaudiya Vaishnavas honour Shiva as the best of Vaishnavas (Bhag. 12.13.16). 44b may also be understood as "the form of Harihara is manifest".
[^kedara-46]: Kedareshvara and Gauri are the Kedara-Gauri temples in Bhubaneswar, with a pond attached to them. The first word is printed ≈ ଗ + ଉ + a sign like ଷ/ଶ. We read ଗଉରି, "Gauri" ('ri' as ଶ). *Snāhāna* is *snāna*, "bathing", lengthened for the metre.
[^bindu-47]: The Bindusagara (Bindu-sarovara) is the sacred lake of Bhubaneswar. The couplet has no verb. We take "with love" (*anurāga*) with the bath.
[^niali-49]: Niali is a village on the Prachi river with a temple of Madhava (cf. 2.58). Printed ନଆଳ.
[^bhagata-50]: *Bhagata* is "devotion" (*bhagati*, with the ‑i lost) or "devotees": possibly also "the devotees make offerings to Madhava".
[^makara-51]: The Makara-mela is the festival on Makara-sankranti, when the Sun enters Capricorn (mid-January).
[^jayadeva-52]: Jayadeva is the author of the *Gita-Govinda*. Odia tradition holds that his birthplace is Kenduli-sasana on the Prachi, near Niali (cf. 2.56–58); Bengali tradition places it at Kenduli in Birbhum. Mahaprabhu relished the songs of the *Gita-Govinda* in Puri with Svarupa Damodara and Ramananda Ray (CC, Madhya 2). *Paraśaṁse* = *praśaṁse*, "they praise".
[^chandrabhaga-54]: Chandrabhaga is a holy place on the seashore near Konark, where the Chandrabhaga river used to flow into the sea. Line 54b is not understood: it is printed *paṛaï muha̐ kahi̐ raṅgā*. Perhaps "where [the river] falls [into the sea] at its mouth". *Raṅgā* may mean "lovely", "red" or "joyful". The third word is printed ≈ a sign with a filled bottom (like କ) + ହିଁ; the OCR has ମୁହିଁ ("I") for ମୁହଁ.
[^saptami-55]: Probably Achala-saptami (Magha-saptami), the seventh day of the bright half of Magha (January–February), when pilgrims bathe in the sea at Chandrabhaga and greet the sunrise. The first word is printed ≈ a filled ଚ/ଭ + ଳ; we read ଚଳ, i.e. [ଅ]ଚଳ (?). *Buṛa pāri* means "having taken a dip". The last word is printed ଉଚ୍ଚର; the rhyme with ପାରି requires ଉଚ୍ଚରି, "proclaiming".
[^aditya-56]: Aditya-Vishnu is the Sun worshipped as Vishnu (Surya-Narayana); the Sun temple of Konark stands near Chandrabhaga. According to the *Brahma-samhita* (5.52), the sun is the eye of Govinda and runs its course by His order. A visit by Mahaprabhu to Konark is not described in the CC/CB.
[^aruna-57]: The sunrise over the sea throws Prabhu into ecstasy. Cf. CC, Antya 14 and 18: in Puri the Chataka hill and the sea reminded Him of Govardhana and the Yamuna. *Mata* = *matta*, "intoxicated"; *bhoḷa*, "beside oneself" (ଭୋ is printed as a ligature resembling ଭେ). The sentence continues in 8.58 (leaf 108).
[^mahabhava-58]: This continues 8.57: at sunrise over the sea at Chandrabhaga, Prabhu enters ecstasy. Mahabhava is the highest stage of prema, proper to Sri Radha. Gaudiya Vaishnavas see Mahaprabhu as Krishna, who has taken on the feelings and complexion of Radha (CC, Adi 1 and 4). We take *anekāi* as an adverb, "in many ways".
[^laddu-60]: Khiri is sweet rice in milk, and laddus are sweet balls. Vishnu here is Aditya-Vishnu, the Sun worshipped as Vishnu at Konark (cf. 8.56). Printed ଷିଶ୍ର ('ri' looking like ଶ, cf. 8.38) and ଆସ୍ୱଦନ.
[^mangala-61]: Mangala is a goddess whose well-known temple stands at Kakatpur on the Prachi river, between Konark and Puri (8.62). The last word of 61b is printed ≈ ରଧାଭ୍ରମ; we read ରାଧାଭ୍ରମ, "Radha-bhrama" (?). This may mean "the bewilderment [of feeling] of Radha" or "Prabhu takes [the goddess] for Radha" (?). Gaudiya Vaishnavas honour Durga as the Lord's external energy, acting by His will (Brahma-samhita 5.44). A visit to Kakatpur is not described in the CC/CB.
[^kshetra-62]: The Kshetra is Sri Kshetra, Puri: from Kakatpur the road leads back to Puri.
[^dola-63]: The Dola-yatra (Dola-purnima) is the swing festival at the full moon of Phalguna (February–March). According to the CC (Adi 13), Mahaprabhu appeared on this full moon (Gaura-purnima). Cuttack was the capital of the Gajapatis. *Naara* means the royal palace or residence; *rajā* = *rājā*. Printed ଦୋଲକୁ, ମେଲାକୁ. According to the CC (Madhya 16), Mahaprabhu passed through Cuttack on His way to Bengal, but the CC/CB do not describe yearly visits to Cuttack for the Dola (cf. the note to 8.35).
[^rudradeva-64]: Rudradeva is the Gajapati Prataparudra (r. from ≈1497). According to the CC (Madhya 11–14), Mahaprabhu at first refused to see the king and received him by His mercy only during the Ratha-yatra (Madhya 14).
[^rani-65]: The first word is printed ≈ ରଣୀମାନ + a sign like ଙ୍କ/ନ୍ତ; we read ରାଣୀମାନଙ୍କ, "of the queens". *Sambhāṣaṇa* means "courteous greeting, address".
[^viraja-68]: Viraja-kshetra is Jajpur on the Vaitarani river, with the shrine of the goddess Viraja; the Dashashvamedha Ghat is a bathing place on the Vaitarani. According to the CC (Madhya 5), Mahaprabhu visited Jajpur (Yajapura) on His way to Puri. The last word of 68a is printed ≈ ବ/ଉ + ର + ଜା + ଇ; we read ବିରଜାଇ, "to Viraja" (?). The rhyme with ଅବଗାହ ("bathing") is weak; perhaps ଅବଗାହି, "having bathed".
[^yajanagara-70]: Yajanagara is Jajpur (Skt. Yayati-nagara, Yajna-pura). Printed ≈ ଯାଜନଗ୍ରଟିଏ; we take it as ଯାଜନଗର + ‑ଟିଏ ("a, one"). According to the CC (Adi 13), Mahaprabhu's grandfather Upendra Mishra lived in Shrihatta; the CC does not say that the ancestors came there from Orissa. The Odia tradition of the Mishras' origin in Jajpur is also reflected in some Bengali biographies. Mahaprabhu Himself is the Supreme Lord, Krishna (CC, Adi 1–2); the genealogy concerns only His family in His earthly lila.
[^vatsasa-71]: *Batsasa* appears to be the name of the gotra (Vatsa / Vatsasa). Samanta is an honorific title of Odia brahmanas holding sasanas. Kulina means "of noble lineage". A sasana is a village granted by the king to brahmanas.
[^darshana-73]: The six darsanas are the classical philosophical schools: Nyaya, Vaisheshika, Sankhya, Yoga, Mimamsa and Vedanta. *Sāmaratha* = *samartha*, "versed, skilled".
[^kapilendra-74]: Kapileshvara is Kapilendradeva (≈1435–1467), founder of the Solar dynasty of the Gajapatis and grandfather of Prataparudra. The second word of 74b is printed ≈ କଲ + a sign like ହିଁ; we read କଳହିଁ, "with quarrels" (?). We take *tarajā* as "threatened" (Skt. *tarjana*) (?).
[^shrihatta-75]: Shrihatta is Sylhet in eastern Bengal. According to the CC (Adi 13), Upendra Mishra's son Jagannatha Mishra moved from Shrihatta to Navadvipa, where Mahaprabhu appeared.
[^paricaya-78]: These are Prabhu's kinsmen in Jajpur. Gaudiya Vaishnavas see in the worship of Mahaprabhu a recognition of His divinity, not merely the honouring of a sannyasi relative.
[^gomaya-79]: Plastering the threshold and the house with cow-dung, lamps and the purna-kumbha (full pitcher) are the traditional signs of a solemn welcome to a holy man.
[^sarage-80]: The second word is printed ≈ ସ + ବ/ର + e + ଗ (ସରଗେ); we read ସରାଗେ, "lovingly" (?). The loss of ‑ā is common in this edition, and the metre requires nine syllables.
[^tulasi-81]: Tulasi-kshetra is Kendrapara, with its temple of Baladeva (8.82). The second word of 81a is printed ≈ କ + ହୁ/ଛୁ + ଦ + ନ; we read କିଛୁଦିନ, "some days" (?). *Bije* is an honorific "procession, arrival".
[^snana-83]: The bathing of the Deities is the Snana-yatra at the full moon of Jyeshtha. This closes the yearly round: the Snana-yatra, anavasara and Alarnath (8.36–41); the full moon of Pausha, Bhubaneswar (8.42–48); Makara-sankranti, Niali and Kenduli (8.49–53); Magha-saptami, Chandrabhaga, Konark and Kakatpur (8.54–62); the Dola, Cuttack (8.63–67); then Jajpur and Tulasi-kshetra (8.68–83). In the CC Mahaprabhu spends His last years in Puri (cf. the note to 8.35). *Snāhāna* = *snāna* (cf. 8.46).
[^kanaka-86]: Cf. CC, Adi 3: Mahaprabhu is "Gaura", of golden complexion. *Pāśora* comes from *pāśoribā*, "to forget": "[He is] the forgetting of sorrow", i.e. at the sight of Him sorrow is forgotten. *Aṭaïṭi* (also in 8.88) appears to be *aṭaï*, "is", with the particle ‑ṭi.
[^achyuta-87]: Achyuta is Achyutananda Das of the Panchasakha. The impression of 87a is faint: printed ≈ ଅଚ୍ୟୁତ + କ, ର/ଚ with ‑i, ଣ/ଖ + ଅ(?)ଥାଇ. The reading କରିଣ ଅଥାଇ is tentative. From the context (8.86–90 describe Prabhu and each of the Panchasakha in turn), the sense is probably that Achyuta composes [songs] on the rasa-lila. Printed ରସଲୀଳାର; perhaps ରାସଲୀଳାର, "of the rasa-lila". The rasa-lila is Krishna's dance with the gopis (Bhag. 10.29–33).
[^guru-88]: Swami is Jagannatha Dasa (cf. 8.33–34). The last word of 88a is printed ≈ ଗୁ/ଲୁ + ରୁ; we read ଗୁରୁ from its shape and from the rhyme with ମହାମେରୁ. He appears to be the teacher and leader in kirtana. Meru is the world mountain, a figure of greatness. In the CC, Svarupa Damodara is named as Mahaprabhu's foremost connoisseur of rasa and kirtana (Madhya 10).
[^balarama-89]: Balarama Das, one of the Panchasakha (cf. 8.33). "Sattvika" apparently means full of the sattvika bhavas, the eight bodily signs of ecstasy (stupor, perspiration, hair standing on end, faltering voice, etc.; *Bhakti-rasamrita-sindhu* 2.3). At kirtana gatherings he "sets going" the bhava-rasa.
[^ananta-90]: Ananta and Yashovanta, two of the Panchasakha (cf. 8.33–34). Couplets 8.89–90 apparently continue the list of the Panchasakha's roles in kirtana begun on leaf 109.
[^gadadhara-91]: Gadadhara Pandit, Madhava's guru. Tota-Gopinatha is the Deity of Gopinatha in the garden (*ṭoṭā*) by the sea at Puri, whom Gadadhara served. According to the CC (Madhya 16), Gadadhara took the vow of kshetra-sannyasa, never to leave Puri; according to the CC (Antya 7), Mahaprabhu heard the *Bhagavata* recited by him. Printed ପୂଜନ୍ତ, ବସତ (‑i not visible).
[^kshetra-92]: Gadadhara is bound by his service to Gopinatha (and, according to the CC, by his vow), so he does not join Prabhu's travels with the Panchasakha (cf. 8.32–57). The first word is printed with the sign that in this font stands for କ: କାହିଁ, "anywhere" (not ତାହିଁ, "there"); likewise in 8.94 and 8.97.
[^svarupa-93]: Svarupa Damodara. The second half-line is read with uncertainty: printed ≈ ହୃଦ + େ + a sign with a thickened foot (like ୟ in ମାୟା) + a sign resembling ଏ. We read ହୃଦୟେ ଏ ମାୟା, "in the heart there is not this maya" — perhaps "he is not attached [to the body or to worldly things]"; the sense is unclear. According to the CC (Antya 14–20), Svarupa Damodara stayed constantly with Mahaprabhu in the Gambhira until the end of His lila; the CC says nothing of an illness of Svarupa.
[^putra-95]: In 8.95–97 Madhava speaks of the worldly fruits of kirtana: sons for the childless, protection from beasts and calamities. For Gaudiya Vaishnavas the highest fruit of sankirtana is prema, and worldly benefits come only incidentally (cf. *Shikshashtaka* 1; CC, Antya 20). Printed apart as ଅପୁତ୍ରି କୁ.
[^beasts-96]: Cf. CC, Madhya 17: in the Jharikhanda forest tigers and elephants chanted the name of Krishna and danced together with Mahaprabhu. Printed ହଂସ୍ର (= ହିଂସ୍ର). The form *uddharu*, "rescues", is unclear.
[^prakriti-97]: "Nature turns hostile" — drought, flood, epidemic and other calamities. The third word of the second half-line is printed ≈ ର + ସ + ଟି (OCR ଗ୍ରସଝି); we read ରସଟି, "the rasa [of kirtana]" (?). *Bhiāi* — "arrange".
[^pothi-98]: The Swami is Jagannatha Dasa, author of the Odia *Bhagavata*. *Pothi* — a palm-leaf manuscript. The second word of the second half-line is printed ≈ ଦୁଆଣେ (OCR ଭୁଆଣେ); by the sense, "as a Deity", possibly "doubly more than a Deity". Honouring the *Bhagavata* as the Lord Himself agrees with the *Bhagavatam* (1.3.43): after Krishna's departure to His own abode this Purana has risen like the sun.
[^asana-100]: Cf. the Odia village custom of the "bhagavata-tungi" (bhagavata-ghara): a house where Jagannatha Dasa's *Bhagavata* is kept on a raised seat, worshipped and read aloud.
[^rasalila-101]: Rasa-lila here is a performance of Krishna's lilas with dance (cf. rahasa, 2.103). The first sign of the word ରାସଲୀଳା is printed as ର with a tail — apparently the ligature ରା.
[^pada-102]: Padas are songs in Odia; Madhava quotes one of the Swami's songs below (8.P0–P4). The khol is the mridanga (cf. 8.30); the tal — small hand cymbals (kartals). Printed ନୃତନ୍ତ (= ନୃତ୍ୟନ୍ତି), ତାଲ.
[^song-p0]: The song is printed without numbers, as a separate block after 8.102; the next verse (8.103) is on leaf 112. The labels 8.P0–8.P4 are ours. The refrain "ତୋର ଚରଣେ ମନ ଲାଗଇରେ" stands in the edition at the beginning and after each stanza; in the translation it is repeated at the end of each line. *Tora* is the familiar "Your" ("thy"): so Krishna is addressed. Giridhara is Krishna, who lifted Govardhana hill (*Bhagavatam* 10.25). Printed ≈ ଗିଶଧର («ରି» as ଶ).
[^vasa-p1]: The stealing of the gopis' garments — *Bhagavatam* 10.22. Nagara — the elegant, skilful lover.
[^padmaja-p2]: Padmaja, "the Lotus-born", is Brahma (less often the name is used of Lakshmi). The third word is printed ≈ ଛ + a sign like ଦ + ା (OCR ଚନା); we read ଛନ୍ଦା, "entangled" (?). Perhaps a hint at how Krishna bewildered Brahma (*Bhagavatam* 10.13–14). "Bound by the bhava of devotees" — cf. *Bhagavatam* 9.4.63 ("I am subordinate to My devotees") and the Damodara lila (10.9).
[^jaga-p4]: The signature (bhanita) "Jaga Das" is a short form of the name Jagannatha Dasa: the song belongs to the Swami (cf. 8.102). *Āsaïre* is printed ଆସଇ, "comes"; possibly = ଆଶଇ, "hopes, longs". In 8.P3 ଚିତ୍ତ is printed ≈ ଚତ୍ତ.
[^ramachandra-103]: Achyuta is Achyutananda, one of the Panchasakha (cf. 7.144, 8.33). Ramachandra is his disciple; Madhava gives no other information about him. The last word is printed ≈ ଅ + a sign like ଇ (or ଣ) + ଲ (OCR ଅଇଲ); we read ଅଇଲ, "came" (as in 3.52, 4.130). The first sign of ଉପଦେଶିଲ is blotted with ink.
[^jhankad-104]: Jhankad (Jhankadpur Patana) is a place in Orissa, east of Cuttack, where the goddess Sarala (Sarala Chandi) is worshipped; the poet Sarala Das (15th c.), author of the Odia *Mahabharata*, is held to have been her devotee. *Tuhi*, "you", is a familiar form of address; printed ≈ ରୁ + ହି (the first sign is the second form of ତ; OCR ରୁଦ୍ଧ); the reading is confirmed by the rhyme with ପାଟଣାହିଁ. A visit of Mahaprabhu to Jhankad is not mentioned in the CC or CB; this is Odia tradition.
[^rasa-105]: The rasa is an enactment of Krishna's lilas with dance (cf. 8.101, rahasa 2.103). Printed ରଚତ (= ରଚିତ). The third word is ≈ େ + a sign like ଏ/ସ/ଯ + ହୁ: we read ସେହୁ, "that very"; possibly ଯେହୁ, "where". The word ଅଟଇଟି is apparently ଅଟଇ "is" with the particle ‑ଟି; its nuance is unclear.
[^sarala-108]: The first word is printed ≈ ସା + େ + a ର with a tail (as in the ligature ରା) + ଲ; by sense and by the editor's note we read ସାରଳା, Sarala. The last word is unclear: ≈ ବ + a sign like କ୍ଷ + ହେ (OCR କର୍ତହେ). If it is ବକ୍ଷ, the sense would be "[Radha's] bosom"; it may be a misprint. The speaker is not named — apparently Prabhu or the sakhas at the darshan of the goddess. The identification of Sarala Chandi with Radha is Madhava's Odia tradition (cf. Mangala, 8.61). For Gaudiya Vaishnavas, Durga is the Lord's shadow, external energy, acting by His will (*Brahma-samhita* 5.44). Radha is His internal energy of bliss, the hladini (CC, Adi 4).
[^pratima-109]: *Pratimā* here means the "embodiment, likeness" of Radha (cf. 7.140), not a Deity in a temple. Printed ≈ ପ୍ରତମା. Mahabhava is the highest stage of prema, proper to Sri Radha (CC, Adi 4).
[^matti-110]: Printed ମ + the ligature ତ୍ତ with ‑i; we read it as "intoxicated" (ମାତି, gerund of ମାତିବା?; cf. ମତ୍ତ 7.131). According to the CC (Adi 4; Antya 14–20), Mahaprabhu relishes the mood of Radha. That He "plays the rasa-lila", dancing in bhava, is Madhava's own picture.
[^suramya-111]: Printed ≈ ପୂରମ୍ୟ; we read ସୁରମ୍ୟ, "lovely" (as the editor has it). The verb is printed ≈ ଉପୁଜ with a superscript mark; we read "arises" (ଉପୁଜଇ?).
[^bhramanti-113]: Printed ଭ୍ରମନ୍ତ (= ଭ୍ରମନ୍ତି) and ନଗ୍ର (= ନଗର). According to Madhava, Prabhu goes with the Panchasakha through the villages of Orissa with kirtana (cf. 8.32–57, 8.95–102). According to the CC (Madhya 16 – Antya 20), after the journey to Vrindavana Mahaprabhu lived in Puri; the CC and CB do not describe such tours.
[^guala-114]: The closing formula of the chapter with the author's name (cf. 2.131, 7.148). *Guāḷa* (printed ≈ ଗୁଆଲ) means "cowherd": probably Madhava's humble self-designation, like *dāsa* in other chapters. Another reading is possible: "the cowherds", i.e. the Panchasakha as Krishna's cowherd friends. Then: "Madhava says: the cowherds go about intoxicated with the rasa of kirtana." *Matta* is printed ମ + a sign like ଢ, which is the ligature ତ୍ତ (cf. 7.131). *Cāla* means "go about" (cf. "କୀର୍ତ୍ତନ ରସେ ମତ୍ତ ଚାଲି" 7.145). With this verse chapter 8 ends; there is no "ଇତି…" colophon. Chapter 9 ("ନବମ ଅଧ୍ୟାୟ") begins on leaf 113.
