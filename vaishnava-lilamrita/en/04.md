# Madhava Pattanayaka. *Vaishnava-lilamrita*, Chapter 4
## Gajapati Prataparudra: the Southern War and the Raid on Puri

*Translated from Odia without a native speaker; doubtful places are marked (?) and (??). The original, transliteration and word-for-word translation are in `full/04.md`.*

**4.1.** Rudradeva is that Gajapati whose might (?) [even] the gods do not know.[^rudradeva-1]  
**4.2.** He is born of a portion of Vishnu; he is of the Solar dynasty, renowned by name.[^surya-2]  
**4.3.** In all three worlds he is the one king; his greatness is beyond measure.  
**4.4.** He subdued all kings, overawing [them] with campaigns that cut roads [through the forests].[^bhiana-4]  
**4.5.** Great in might, great in valour, he rules his people and subjects in prosperity.  
**4.6.** With thousands of warriors, with a campaigning (?) army, he again marched south.[^danda-6]  
**4.7.** At Udayagiri [and] Chandragiri he valiantly made his camp.[^udayagiri-7]  
**4.8.** In the fifteenth anka the uparaya (??) fights with Krishnaraya.[^krishnaraya-8]  
**4.9.** From the north Ghazi (?) Ismail marched (?) against the Kshetra.[^ismail-9]  
**4.10.** The servitors said to the Lord: "A great peril has come."[^sevaka-10]  
**4.11.** The servitors carried off and hid [the Deities] and came to the fort of Kenkal.[^kenkal-11]  
**4.12.** Seating the Deities on elephants, Nrisimha Uparaya (?) leads [Them] away.[^uparaya-12]  
**4.13.** In the midst of Lake Chilika, inside the … (??) of Chadhei[^chadhei-13]  
**4.14.** they hid the Deities. A Pana (?) went to the south[^pana-14]  
**4.15.** [and] on the bank of the Krishnaveni tells the king the news.[^krishnaveni-15]  
**4.16.** Hearing it, Pratapa, best of kings, spoke in anger:  
**4.17.** "While I live — what deed (?) is this: the Lord handed over (?) to the Yavanas!"[^arpila-17]  
**4.18.** [The messenger] brought word: "Chadhei-guha" — and hearing it, the jewel among kings grew calm.[^guha-18]  
**4.19.** He arrives at the fort of Kenkal and, contrite, beholds [the Deities].  
**4.20.** He said: "O Lord, have mercy: this kingdom is Yours.  
**4.21.** By Your strength I am strong: I will slay the Yavanas — what are they, the wretches (?)!"[^chara-21]  
**4.22.** Making Jagannatha's name his rosary, the king marches in haste (?).[^mala-22]  
**4.23.** He drove off the mlecchas and went as far as [the place] called Mandaragada.[^mandara-23]  
**4.24.** He quickly came back; but when he went [again] to the south,  
**4.25.** a most terrible battle broke out, and the king was sorely distressed.  
**4.26.** He said: "O Lord, protect: this kingdom is Yours."[^refrain-26]  
**4.27.** He constantly remembers the Name, fixing his mind on the Lord's feet.[^bridge-27]  

## Sri Chaitanya's arrival in Puri and meeting with Ramananda Raya


**4.28.** At that time there came to the Kshetra a new, young yati, a sannyasi, together with a company of devotees.  
**4.29.** His name was Krishna Chaitanya: He was as the Lord Himself, manifest before the eyes.[^samana]  
**4.30.** Beholding Jagannatha, He falls senseless in prema.[^faint]  
**4.31.** Sarvabhauma, who saw [this], had Him carefully tended (??).[^sastama]  
**4.32.** He took Him to his own house, and the devotees too came there.  
**4.33.** They went to the temple for darshan, and He Himself partook of prasad.  
**4.34.** For a whole month in the Kshetra their discussion of the shastras grew ever richer.[^month]  
**4.35.** For about a month He stayed in the Kshetra, frequenting the temple.  
**4.36.** At the foot of Bata-Ganesha, in plain view, Jagannatha Dasa would sit.[^jdas]  
**4.37.** He had built a pavilion there for rahasa and installed the murti of Radha-Krishna.[^rahasa]  
**4.38.** He was composing the Bhagavata, and the devotees listened attentively.  
**4.39.** All the bhava of the Bhagavata he sang day after day in a sweet voice.  
**4.40.** One day, in the leisure of evening, before a host of devotees, he was in particular  
**4.41.** expounding the meaning of the Bhagavata — and Chaitanya came and joined them.  
**4.42.** Hearing the exposition of the Bhagavata, He understood the swami's bhava.  
**4.43.** Tears trickle from the eyes; boundless thrills run through the body.[^whose]  
**4.44.** Such great bhava in Jagannatha [Dasa] Chaitanya discerned.[^thaba]  
**4.45.** He said: 'You are the best of devotees; your understanding is nourished by the bhava of devotion.  
**4.46.** Where could I obtain your bhava? I will not let go of your company!'[^humility]  
**4.47.** For two and a half praharas the two remained in each other's embrace.[^prahara]  
**4.48.** Then, sitting together, they praised one another.  
**4.49.** Chaitanya called him 'Swami', and he himself called Him 'Prabhu'.[^swami]  
**4.50.** As a river merging with the ocean — the waves of bhava rose high.[^uchhul]  
**4.51.** He met with Sarvabhauma and studied the shastras with him.  
**4.52.** Sarvabhauma, a great logician, versed in nyaya, deeply learned,[^sarva]  
**4.53.** held many learned disputations with the Lord in the Kshetra.  
**4.54.** He saw the Lord's devotion: the Lord's heart was by its very nature full of prema.  
**4.55.** Whenever the talk turned to devotion, sattvika bhavas appeared on the Lord's body:[^sattvika]  
**4.56.** His lips trembled, His body sweated; there was shaking, bhava, and rapturous dance.  
**4.57.** Seeing this, Sarvabhauma said: 'Are You not the Lord Himself?[^svayam]  
**4.58.** I do not know the bhava of devotion: with a dry heart I [deal only] in logical proofs.'[^pramana]  
**4.59.** [Sarvabhauma] spent his time with the Lord. [Then] the Lord said: 'I shall go on pilgrimage.'  
**4.60.** [Sarvabhauma:] 'When You go to the southern land, seek the company of Ramaraya.[^speaker]  
**4.61.** He knows the truth of devotion; his mind is set on the path of raga.[^raga]  
**4.62.** I do not know the talk of Krishna; Rama Raya knows it all.  
**4.63.** When You go to the southern land, be sure to meet Rama Raya.'  
**4.64.** He set out on His southern journey and came to the bank of the Godavari.[^south]  
**4.65.** Having bathed and purified Himself, He had spread His seat at the foot of a tree.[^godavari]  
**4.66.** His lustre was by nature golden — one might mistake Him for the rising sun.  
**4.67.** As if fashioned of gold, His body shone fair.  
**4.68.** His arms reached to His knees; His wide eyes were lifted up (?).[^ajanu]  
**4.69.** A brahminical radiance flashed from Him; a sweet smile flowed from His lips.  
**4.70.** At this time Ramaraya came in state [to the river] and was bathing at the ghat,[^ramananda]  
**4.71.** with his retinue; herald-drums and kettledrums resounded.[^tamaka]  
**4.72.** Ramaraya rose from the ghat, and the Raya set off in his palanquin.[^dola]  
**4.73.** Rising from the foot of the tree, Chaitanya ran up to meet him:  
**4.74.** 'Are you Ramaraya? You look like a king.'[^tumi]  
**4.75.** At once they embraced — as if lightning had merged with a cloud.[^cloud]  
**4.76.** [Chaitanya:] 'I have come and met you — behold my good fortune!'  
**4.77.** Then Ramaraya said: 'Come, pray, to my home;  
**4.78.** stay here as my guest, and we shall converse on the shastras.  
**4.79.** By my good fortune You have come and granted me the company of a sadhu.'  
**4.80.** The two became absorbed in conversation and spent ten days so.[^tendays]  
**4.81.** In verses (?), joining many meanings, Ramaraya told his account.[^samvada]  

## The Conversation on the Godavari: Radha's Prema and Mahabhava


**4.82.** [He set forth] the order of the path of bhakti and the summit of the path of raga.[^vidhi-82]  
**4.83.** He expounded many truths and imparted much knowledge (?):  
**4.84.** all the bhavas of the gopi devotees — through the meaning of the ślokas of the *Bhagavata*.[^bhagavata-84]  
**4.85.** He determined the sadhya [— the highest goal] and set forth (?) bhajana and the One who is worshipped.  
**4.86.** The *Brahma-samhita*, the *Karnamrita*, Jayadeva's *Gita-govinda* —[^books-86]  
**4.87.** all the bhavas of the path of raga he expounded at once (?).  
**4.88.** "The devotee's bhava is higher (?)," he says, praising (?) prema.  
**4.89.** "Prema alone is the goal of life, the limit of the path of bhakti."[^purushartha-89]  
**4.90.** Raya says: "Kanta-prema — this path of bhajana is the highest.  
**4.91.** Worship Krishna in the mood of a beloved — this is the summit of the path of sadhana."[^kanta-91]  
**4.92.** Still Gora was not satisfied: "Tell [Me] the way further," He said.[^bahya-92]  
**4.93.** At last Ramaraya says: "The highest goal is Radha's prema."  
**4.94.** Radha's sweet bhava Raya told in full.  
**4.95.** "Krishna's energies are three: sandhini (?), sambandhini and hladini.[^shakti-95]  
**4.96.** Hladini is Krishna's svarupa-shakti: such is its name (?).  
**4.97.** Hladini is bliss: she gives bliss to Krishna.  
**4.98.** The very energy hladini is Radhika; in prema she knows no hesitation.[^ti-98]  
**4.99.** The boundless (?) hladini is Radha: she always enchants Krishna.  
**4.100.** The essence of hladini is prema; she is Krishna's life.  
**4.101.** [Radha is] the crown jewel of all beloveds, the embodiment of mahabhava.[^mahabhava-101]  
**4.102.** Krishna is within her and without; she sees Krishna in everything.  
**4.103.** Mahabhava is of two [kinds]: they are called rudha and adhirudha.[^rudha-103]  
**4.104.** The highest [stage] of adhirudha mahabhava is called (?) madana.  
**4.105.** Radha is madana: such is the definition of mahabhava.  
**4.106.** Mahabhava is the cintamani," [so he said,] describing Radha's own nature.[^cintamani-106]  
**4.107.** "The lila is one — [yet] in two parts (?); Radha's bhava is causeless.[^eka-107]  
**4.108.** Whoever becomes a follower in Radha's bhava will certainly attain Krishna.[^anugata-108]  
**4.109.** Apart from this there is no other way — so it is made manifest in the shastras (?).  
**4.110.** In separation from Krishna Radha pines; tears flow from her eyes.  
**4.111.** The torment of separation from Govinda — apart from it there is no other path.  
**4.112.** Whoever worships Krishna in that bhava certainly attains Krishna."  
**4.113.** He told this supreme truth — the limit of the path of bhakti.  
**4.114.** "Is there still any doubt in You? Ask without hesitation.[^tora-114]  
**4.115.** Are You not the Lord Himself? Come and set my heart at rest (?).  
**4.116.** You are the avatara of Radha; [here] You taste Your own nature."[^avatara-116]  
**4.117.** Prabhu says: "Speak, speak! Radha's bhava is unbearable.  
**4.118.** Hearing it from your lips, I, My doubt dispelled, come to Myself (??)."[^cetai-118]  
**4.119.** Ramaraya recites the śloka: "At first raga arose from the play of glances…"[^pahilahi-119]  
**4.120.** Prabhu's whole body trembles; tears bathe (??) His chest;  
**4.121.** from bhava — trembling, the body flushes (?), [the hairs stand on end] like kadamba filaments.  
**4.122.** Placing [His] hand on Ramaraya's lips, [Prabhu] said: "Enough, enough (?) — [do not] speak."[^mouth-122]  
**4.123.** He embraced Raya; in time [He] came to Himself (?).[^recover-123]  
**4.124.** He told the supreme truth and dispelled all doubts without remainder.  

## "You Are My Guru": The Southern Pilgrimage and Ramananda's Move to Nilachala


**4.125.** Prabhu said: "You are My guru: from your lips I have learned the truth.[^guru-125]  
**4.126.** This position of yours as guru I shall keep remembering and never for a moment let go from My mind."  
**4.127.** Raya said: "[I am] the lowest of shudras; I am [only] a repository (??) of the evidence of the shastras.[^shudra-127]  
**4.128.** Counting (?) [me] as a guru, [You are only] making fun of me (?): I am a lowly one."[^bhandi-128]  
**4.129.** Prabhu said: "You gave instruction; you taught a rare truth.  
**4.130.** I heard of your greatness, and therefore (?) I came to you.  
**4.131.** You made all truth plainly visible: the treasure of prema, that whirlpool of rasa (?),[^rasavarta-131]  
**4.132.** the teaching on the hladini energy — where [else] would I have found these?  
**4.133.** From today you are My guru. [To take] a twice-born for a shudra is an error."[^dvija-133]  
**4.134.** So [He] stayed (?) [there] ten days, and then went on to the south.[^dasadina-134]  
**4.135.** Setubandha and Rameshvara, Sri Ranga, Udupi, Shankara (?) —[^tirtha-135]  
**4.136.** how many holy places (?) there were! [At last] He came and reached Prayaga.[^prayaga-136]  
**4.137.** Wandering through many tirthas, [visiting them] one after another (?) with Hari-nama,  
**4.138.** [spreading] Krishna-katha — the essence of rasa — and the kirtana of the Name,  
**4.139.** He arrived at Rajamandri (?) and says to Ramaraya: "Come!"[^rajamandri-139]  
**4.140.** Do not stay (?) here even a moment; leave the royal service at once.[^tu-140]  
**4.141.** To spend My days in Nilachala without you, guru, is pointless."  
**4.142.** Raya said: "So be it (?)," and he said: "Go on ahead."[^heu-142]  
**4.143.** Prabhu returned (?) — He arrived in Nilachala.[^leuti-143]  
**4.144.** In the nineteenth anka of the king, Raya arrived in Nilachala.[^anka-144]  
**4.145.** The king said: "Good," and entrusted him with the charge of the temple:[^mandira-145]  
**4.146.** "Look after the loss and gain of the temple — this is your task."  
**4.147.** Ramaraya agreed: [now] he manages the affairs of the temple.[^bebhara-147]  
**4.148.** Summoning Sri Jivadeva, [the king said]: "You go to Rajamahendri (?)."[^jiva-148]  

## Jivadeva Kavi-dindima in Rajamahendri; Ramananda and the Vaishnavas in Shri Kshetra


**4.149.** ["]There manage the affairs of state and keep the army with you.["][^sethai-149]  
**4.150.** [The king] gave Jivadeva the post and named him "Bahinipati" (?).[^bahinipati-150]  
**4.151.** Jivadeva was mighty, and in the devotee's bhava great in understanding.[^jivadeva-151]  
**4.152.** He is the rajaguru; he is called "Kavi-dindima".[^dindima-152]  
**4.153.** He composed many poems and plays and displayed a poet's gift.  
**4.154.** He composed the *Bhagata-vaibhava* and the drama *Utsahavati* (?).[^works-154]  
**4.155.** [People] watched these plays — so it was. [Then] he received the king's leave to depart,[^melani-155]  
**4.156.** stayed in Rajamahendri and gathered an army.  
**4.157.** Ramaraya, living in the Kshetra, looks after (??) the sadhu Vaishnavas.[^ksetra-157]  
**4.158.** He built a mandapa near the northern side (?) [of the temple].[^mandapa-158]  
**4.159.** There the Vaishnavas gathered as a community and gave their minds to the rasa of Krishna.  
**4.160.** The days flowed by (?) in the rasa of Krishna, says Madhava joyfully.[^end-160]  

[^rudradeva-1]: Rudradeva is King Prataparudra (r. c. 1497–1540), Mahaprabhu's patron in Puri (*Chaitanya-charitamrita*, Madhya 11–14); Gajapati, 'lord of elephants', is the title of the kings of Orissa. The second half-line is taken as *pratāpa debe na jāṇanti*, 'the gods do not know [his] might', i.e. it cannot be measured; read as one word, 'Pratapa-deva', the sense is unclear.
[^surya-2]: The Gajapatis of Kapilendra's line styled themselves the Solar dynasty (Surya-vamsha). 'A portion of Vishnu' is courtly praise of the king. For Gaudiya Vaishnavas something else matters more: in the *Chaitanya-charitamrita* Prataparudra is a great devotee who swept the road before Jagannatha's chariot (Madhya 13).
[^bhiana-4]: Benta-bhiana: clearing forest and making a road for an advancing army; here, campaigns of conquest in general.
[^danda-6]: The word before *sena* ('army') is read as ଦଣ୍ଡ, *daṇḍa*, 'chastisement, military expedition'; the first sign also resembles ଘ. The rhyme *sena* / *puṇi* is imperfect.
[^udayagiri-7]: Udayagiri and Chandragiri are fortresses in the south (now Andhra Pradesh), the frontier of the Gajapati realm in the wars with Vijayanagara. 'Made his camp': lit. 'made a *kaṭaka*' (army, war camp).
[^krishnaraya-8]: Krishnaraya is Krishnadeva Raya, king of Vijayanagara (r. 1509–1529), Prataparudra's adversary in the south. Anka: the Odia count of regnal years (see 1.72); Prataparudra's fifteenth anka falls about 1509–1510. The word ଉପରାୟ (*uparāẏa*) is unclear: a title ('sub-king, deputy'? cf. 'Nrisimha Uparaya' in 4.12) or 'falling upon [him]' (ଉପରେ 'above' + ଆଏ?). Who fights whom is not entirely clear from the line.
[^ismail-9]: Ismail Ghazi was a general of the Bengal sultan Alauddin Husain Shah; about 1509–1510, while Prataparudra was at war in the south, he raided Orissa and Puri. Printed ଗ ଜ (spaced, without ା and ି), taken as ଗାଜି, 'Ghazi'. The verb ଚଢ଼ଟି (?) is taken as 'marching in force' (ଚଢ଼ି). The *Chaitanya-bhagavata* (Antya 4) calls Husain Shah the destroyer of Orissa's temples and murtis; on the hostility at the Orissa–Bengal border in Mahaprabhu's time, see *Chaitanya-charitamrita*, Madhya 16.
[^sevaka-10]: The servitors (sevakas) of Jagannatha's temple address the Lord Himself.
[^kenkal-11]: Gada-Kenkal, the fort of Kenkal: its location cannot be established from the text.
[^uparaya-12]: The Deities of Puri were taken away from the raid on elephants. Nrisimha Uparaya is apparently the official in charge of this; *uparāẏa* is probably his title (cf. 4.8).
[^chadhei-13]: Chilika is the great lagoon south-west of Puri. Chadhei (*caṛhei*, 'bird') is Chadhei-guha, 'the Bird Cave' (4.18), an island hill in Chilika where, by Puri tradition, the Deities were sheltered from raids. The word କୁଁମାକ (*ku̐māka*) is not identified: an unclear sign (ଁ? ୀ?) stands over ମ; by sense, 'of the cave, shelter'.
[^pana-14]: Printed ପାଣେକ, taken as ପାଣ ଏକ, 'one Pana' (the Pana are an Odia caste from which runners and drummers were drawn); the third sign also resembles ଫ.
[^krishnaveni-15]: The Krishnaveni is the Krishna river in the south, where the king was with his army.
[^arpila-17]: The last word is read as ଅର୍ପିଲ, 'handed over, delivered': the king apparently took the news to mean that the Lord had fallen into the Yavanas' power. The last word of the first half-line is ବେଭର (?) = ବେଭାର, 'deed, conduct'. The rhyme *bebhara* / *arpila* is imperfect; one of the words may be corrupt.
[^guha-18]: Understood thus: the messenger reported that the Deities were sheltered in Chadhei-guha. Another reading is possible: '[they] brought [the Deities] from [the place] called Chadhei-guha'; this would explain why in 4.19 the king beholds Them at the fort of Kenkal.
[^chara-21]: The last word is printed ଛ + a sign like ୁ + ର; taken as ଛାର, 'worthless, contemptible'.
[^mala-22]: That is, the king repeats Jagannatha's name unceasingly, as on a rosary. The last word is printed like ଝରତରେ, taken as ତ୍ୱରିତରେ, 'in haste'.
[^mandara-23]: Mandaragada is Garh Mandaran, a fortress on the Orissa–Bengal frontier (now Hooghly district, West Bengal). The mlecchas here are the Bengal sultan's army.
[^refrain-26]: The second half-line repeats 4.20 word for word.
[^bridge-27]: The next verse (4.28) begins the account of Sri Chaitanya's arrival in Puri, 'at this time': Madhava thus places His coming in the period of the southern war and the raid. By the *Chaitanya-charitamrita* (Madhya 6), Mahaprabhu came to Puri soon after accepting sannyasa (1510).
[^samana]: Sākṣāt īśvara samāna — lit. 'equal to Ishvara, manifest'. The author conveys how the Lord appeared to the people of Puri; below (v. 57) Sarvabhauma asks outright whether He is not the Lord Himself. According to the Gaudiya Vaishnava teaching expounded by Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati after the Chaitanya-charitamrita (Adi 1–4), Sri Krishna Chaitanya is Svayam Bhagavan Krishna Himself, who has assumed the mood and lustre of Sri Radha; samāna does not mean 'merely like' here.
[^faint]: Cf. CC Madhya 6.1–14 and CB Antya 2: on seeing Jagannatha, Mahaprabhu falls senseless in ecstasy; Sarvabhauma Bhattacharya has Him carried to his house. Madhava keeps the same order ('first Jagannatha, then Sarvabhauma's house') in his Chaitanya-vilasa (according to B. B. Majumdar).
[^sastama]: Uncertain reading: the printed ସାଷ୍ଟାମ ଯତକ (sāṣṭāma yataka) is not found in the dictionaries. From the sense and from CC (Madhya 6), Sarvabhauma took care of the unconscious sannyasi (had Him carried, revived). Possible corruptions of sāṣṭāṅga (full prostration) and yatana (care). Question for a specialist — QUESTIONS.md.
[^month]: According to CC (Madhya 7.3–5), Mahaprabhu came to Puri in Phalguna and left for the south in Vaishakha, i.e. about two months later; Madhava says 'a month'. The 'discussion of the shastras' corresponds in CC (Madhya 6) to Sarvabhauma's seven-day exposition of the Vedanta-sutra and the debate that followed.
[^jdas]: Jagannatha Dasa — Odia Vaishnava poet, author of the Odia Bhagavata, first of the 'five friends' (Panchasakha). Bata-Ganesha — the shrine of Ganesha by the Kalpa-vata banyan in the Jagannatha temple compound. The meeting of Sri Chaitanya with Jagannatha Dasa belongs to the Odia tradition (Divakara Dasa and others); the Gaudiya biographies (CC, CB) do not have it. We translate the author's text as it stands.
[^rahasa]: Rahasa (Odia rahāsa, from rāsa) — the enactment of the pastimes of Radha and Krishna in song and dance; Madhava describes the rahasa custom of Puri in ch. 2. The murti of Radha-Krishna is an installed Deity, not an image.
[^whose]: The subject is not named: the tears and thrills may belong to Chaitanya hearing the Bhagavata or to Jagannatha Dasa himself; the next couplet ('such bhava in Jagannatha Chaitanya discerned') points rather to the latter.
[^thaba]: Printed ଠାନ (ṭhāna), but the rhyme with ଭାବ (bhāba) requires ଠାବ (ṭhāba); ṭhāba karibā — 'to locate, discern' (Brooks 1874, p. 103: ṭhāba — 'a place').
[^humility]: Mahaprabhu's words that He cannot obtain Jagannatha Dasa's bhava show the humility with which the Lord, playing the part of a devotee, extols His devotees (cf. His praise of Ramananda Raya in CC Madhya 8). According to the Gaudiya teaching followed by Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati, Sri Chaitanya is Krishna Himself in the form and mood of a devotee; in exalting a devotee He teaches humility, not that anyone is above Him.
[^prahara]: A prahara is a 'watch', an eighth of a day (≈3 hours); two and a half praharas ≈ seven and a half hours. The reading କୋଲାଗ୍ରତେ is uncertain (one would expect କୋଳାଗତେ, 'in embrace').
[^swami]: That is, Chaitanya respectfully called Jagannatha Dasa 'swami', and he called Him 'Prabhu', 'Lord'. So the Odia tradition expresses their mutual regard.
[^uchhul]: The reading ଉଭରିଲ (ubharila) rests on the agreement of passes A and B and on the same verb in v. 68 (ଉଭରି). Cf. Purnachandra (vol. 1, PDF leaf 1045): ଉଭା 'standing, erect', ଉଭାରିବା 'to raise, lift; to stand up' (with a quotation from Jagannatha Dasa's Bhagavata); hence 'the waves of bhava rose up'. Not in Brooks.
[^sarva]: Vasudeva Sarvabhauma Bhattacharya — the famous Naiyayika (logician of the Nyaya school), court pandit of Prataparudra (CC Madhya 6).
[^sattvika]: Sattvika bhavas — the eight bodily signs of ecstatic love: stupor, perspiration, horripilation, faltering voice, trembling, change of colour, tears and fainting (Bhakti-rasamrita-sindhu 2.3).
[^svayam]: In CC (Madhya 6) Sarvabhauma recognises Mahaprabhu as the Lord when He shows him His six-armed form, and composes verses in His praise; in Madhava, Sarvabhauma's awakening comes from the sight of the Lord's sattvika bhavas.
[^pramana]: Paramāṇe = pramāṇe — 'in proofs': Sarvabhauma confesses that his learning is dry logic without bhakti; cf. CC Madhya 6, where before his conversion he expounds Vedanta in the spirit of advaita.
[^speaker]: The change of speaker is not marked; by sense vv. 60–63 are Sarvabhauma's reply (cf. CC Madhya 7.62–67: Sarvabhauma begs Mahaprabhu to meet Ramananda Raya on the Godavari and not to slight him as a shudra and a worldly man). The future forms yiba, labhiba, bheṭiba are rendered here as addressed to the second person.
[^raga]: Raga-marga — the path of spontaneous love (raganuga bhakti) in the mood of the residents of Vraja, as distinct from the path of injunctions (vidhi). In CC (Madhya 8) Ramananda Raya is the master of rasa through whom Mahaprabhu reveals the highest truths of bhakti.
[^south]: The southern pilgrimage — CC Madhya 7–9. Madhava passes straight to the meeting on the Godavari, omitting the way through Kurmakshetra and Jiyada-Nrisimha.
[^godavari]: The meeting with Ramananda takes place on the bank of the Godavari near Rajamahendri (Kovvur/Rajahmundry); cf. CC Madhya 8.11–23. The forms hoiachu, pārachu (ending -chu) are unusual (in literary Odia this is the 2nd person singular) — question for a specialist.
[^ajanu]: 'Arms reaching to the knees' (ājānu-lambita-bhuja) is one of the marks of a mahapurusha; Madhava describes the Lord as Ramananda sees Him, cf. the description in CC Madhya 8 (effulgence like many suns, lotus eyes). The meaning of ଉଭରି (ubhari) is from context.
[^ramananda]: Ramananda Raya — son of Bhavananda Raya, governor of Rajamahendri under Prataparudra; Madhava served under him (ch. 3). ବଜେ (baje) is taken as ବିଜେ (bije) — the honorific 'deigns to go, proceeds' (of kings and Deities). The literal sense of v. 70 is not wholly clear.
[^tamaka]: Ṭamaka — the herald's drum, niśāṇa — great kettledrums: as governor of the region Ramananda goes out with music and retinue; cf. CC Madhya 8 (he comes to bathe in a palanquin, with music and brahmanas).
[^dola]: ହାଣ୍ଡୋଳା (hāṇḍoḷā?) — the conjunct is uncertain. In Purnachandra (vol. 7, PDF leaf 1171) ହାଣ୍ଡୋଳା is 'a small burnt earthen temple for village Deities', which does not fit here; by context a palanquin or litter is expected (cf. CC Madhya 8: dolāya caḍi', 'seated in a palanquin'; Odia ଦୋଳା, ହିନ୍ଦୋଳା). The rendering 'palanquin' is provisional. ବଜେକ = ବିଜେ with the particle -କ (cf. କଲେକ, ହେଲେକ in this same text).
[^tumi]: Cf. CC Madhya 8: Mahaprabhu asks, 'Are you Raya Ramananda?' and, hearing 'yes', embraces him.
[^cloud]: Golden Gauranga and dark Ramananda — 'lightning and a cloud', a traditional image (in CC both weep with love at this meeting and fall to the ground).
[^tendays]: According to CC (Madhya 8), Mahaprabhu spent about ten days with Ramananda in talks about Krishna.
[^samvada]: There follows in Madhava the dialogue of Ramananda and Chaitanya (cf. the 'Ramananda-samvada', CC Madhya 8.57 ff., where Ramananda answers with verses of the shastras). The first word is printed ପଦ୍ମରେ (padmare, 'in the lotus'), which makes no sense; we read ପଦ୍ୟରେ (padyare, 'in verses') — question for a specialist. The pilot ends here.
[^vidhi-82]: Continuation of Ramananda's account (4.81). Vidhi-marga is the path of rules; raga-marga is the path of spontaneous love (cf. pilot, 4.62). Printed «ବଧି» (*badhi*), read ବିଧି (*bidhi*, "rule"): the ି sign, as often in this edition, did not print; the rhyme with ଅବଧି confirms.
[^bhagavata-84]: Cf. CC, Madhya 8: Ramananda answers Mahaprabhu's questions with ślokas of the *Srimad-Bhagavatam* and other shastras; the bhava of the gopis is at the centre of his answers.
[^books-86]: The sources Ramananda draws on in Madhava: the *Brahma-samhita*, Bilvamangala's *Krishna-karnamrita* and Jayadeva's *Gita-govinda* (on Jayadeva see ch. 2). According to CC (Madhya 9), Mahaprabhu found the *Brahma-samhita* and the *Krishna-karnamrita* on His southern pilgrimage and brought them to Puri; the *Gita-govinda* He relished in Puri with Ramananda and Svarupa Damodara (CC, Madhya 2). Printed «ବ୍ରହ୍ମସହିତା» (without ଂ) = *Brahma-samhita*.
[^purushartha-89]: Prema is the "fifth purushartha", the highest goal of life, surpassing dharma, artha, kama and moksha (CC, Adi 7).
[^kanta-91]: In CC (Madhya 8) Ramananda, rising from varnashrama, names the stages — dasya, sakhya, vatsalya and finally kanta-prema ("this is the limit of sadhya"), and then Radha's prema as the crown of all sadhyas. Madhava gives this ladder in brief.
[^bahya-92]: Cf. CC, Madhya 8: to each answer Mahaprabhu says, "This is external; tell Me further." Literally "Gora did not understand" — i.e. did not accept the answer as final.
[^shakti-95]: The three internal energies of the Lord — sandhini (existence), samvit (knowledge) and hladini (bliss); cf. CC, Adi 4 and Madhya 8. Printed «ସମ୍ବନ୍ଧିନୀ» (*sambandhinī*, "the connecting one") where the doctrine expects samvit; the first sign of «ସନ୍ଧିନୀ» is unclearly printed. "Hladini" is printed with an abbreviated ligature (≈ ଲଦନୀ), below as «ଆହ୍ଲାଦିନୀ».
[^ti-98]: The particle ‑ଟି (*‑ṭi*: ଶକ୍ତିଟି, ଅଟଇଟି 4.100, 4.105, ଏହୁଟି 4.109) is taken as emphatic ("the very, precisely"); a question for a specialist.
[^mahabhava-101]: Cf. CC, Madhya 8: the essence of hladini is prema, the essence of prema is bhava, the highest stage of bhava is mahabhava; Sri Radha is the embodiment of mahabhava and the crown jewel of all Krishna's beloveds.
[^rudha-103]: The division of mahabhava into rudha and adhirudha, and the highest stage of adhirudha — madana, found only in Sri Radha — follow Srila Rupa Gosvami's *Ujjvala-nilamani*. The word «ବେଭାର» (*bebhāra*, 4.96, 4.104) is taken as "usage, designation" (= *byabahāra*) — uncertain.
[^cintamani-106]: Cf. CC, Madhya 8: mahabhava is the cintamani; such is Radha's own nature.
[^eka-107]: Cf. CC, Adi 4: Radha and Krishna are one self who assumed two forms to relish the rasa of lila. "Causeless" (*ahetuka*) — unmotivated and unconditioned (cf. *Bhagavatam* 1.2.6). The reading «ବେନିଭାଗ» (*beni* "two" + *bhāga* "part"; printed ≈ ବେନଭାଗ) is uncertain.
[^anugata-108]: Cf. CC, Madhya 8: no one enters this lila without the sakhis; whoever follows the sakhis in their mood attains the service of Radha and Krishna in the kunjas.
[^tora-114]: The change of speaker is not marked. By sense 4.114–116 are Ramananda's words to Mahaprabhu; the intimate "you" (*tora, tui, tu*) is usual in Odia bhakti, where Jagannatha too is addressed so. It is possible that 4.114 is Mahaprabhu speaking to Ramananda.
[^avatara-116]: In CC (Madhya 8) Ramananda sees Mahaprabhu as a dark cowherd boy covered by a golden radiance, and Mahaprabhu reveals to him the form of Rasaraja-Mahabhava — Krishna and Radha in one. According to the Gaudiya Vaishnava teaching (CC, Adi 1 and 4; Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati), Sri Chaitanya is Krishna Himself, who accepted Radha's bhava and radiance to taste the sweetness of His own love; Madhava's words "the avatara of Radha" express this idea in his own way. The second half-line ("the tasting of [Your] own nature is Yours") is rendered close to literally.
[^cetai-118]: The last word is printed ≈ «ଚତୋଇ» (*catoi*); read as a transposition for ଚେତାଇ (*cetāi*, "regaining consciousness / awakening") — unclear. The sense of the half-line is conjectural.
[^pahilahi-119]: The opening of Ramananda's Brajabuli song "pahilehi rāga nayana-bhaṅge bhela" ("at first attraction arose from the play of glances"), CC, Madhya 8; Madhava calls it a "śloka", and it is printed with distortions («ପହିଲଇ … ଭଙ୍ଗ ଭେଲେ»).
[^mouth-122]: Cf. CC, Madhya 8: on hearing this song Mahaprabhu covered Ramananda's mouth with His own hand. The words «ହେଉ ହେଲ କହ» are understood tentatively.
[^recover-123]: The same unclear word «ସାଷ୍ଟାମ» (*sāṣṭāma*) as in 4.31 (pilot: "sāṣṭāma yataka karāile" of the unconscious Mahaprabhu). Here "in time he became *sāṣṭāma*" after ecstasy — which supports the sense "come to oneself, conscious" for 4.31 as well. Whether Prabhu or Ramananda comes to himself is not clear.
[^guru-125]: Mahaprabhu speaks. Cf. CC, Madhya 8: at the end of the conversation Ramananda confesses that the Lord Himself spoke through his mouth, while Mahaprabhu, in humility, listens to him as a disciple. According to the Gaudiya Vaishnavas (Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati), Sri Chaitanya, Krishna Himself, plays the part of a student to glorify His devotee and to show the world that the truth about Krishna is learned from a Vaishnava. The change of speaker in 4.125–133 is unmarked; the assignment of lines follows the sense.
[^shudra-127]: By birth Ramananda was a karana (a scribe-kayastha), ranked with the shudras in the varna order; in CC (Madhya 8) he likewise calls himself low-born. The second word of the second half is printed ≈ ଘର (*ghara*, "house"); taken as "repository" — uncertain. A corruption (ଧର, "I hold to [the evidence of the shastras]") is possible. The sense of the half-line — "I only repeat the shastras" — follows from the context, not from this word.
[^bhandi-128]: The readings ଗଣି (*gaṇi*, "counting") and ଭାଣ୍ଡି (*bhāṇḍi*, "deceiving, making fun") are uncertain: the second sign of the first word and the conjunct of the second are unclearly printed (possibly ଭାଙ୍ଗି).
[^rasavarta-131]: The last word is printed ≈ ରସାବର୍ତ୍ତ (*rasābartta*); taken as *rasa-āvarta*, "whirlpool of rasa". The tail of the final ligature also allows ‑ବର୍ତ୍ତି. The list in 4.131–132 (prema, rasa, hladini) repeats the topics of the conversation in 4.95–112.
[^dvija-133]: Cf. CC, Madhya 8.128: "Whether a brahmana, a sannyasi or a shudra — whoever knows the science of Krishna is a guru." In Madhava, Mahaprabhu calls Ramananda a twice-born (*dwija*) outright and his "shudra" status an error. The Gaudiya Vaishnavas, following Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati, teach that a true Vaishnava stands above all varnas and that brahminical status is determined by qualities and initiation, not by birth.
[^dasadina-134]: Ten days — as in 4.80 and in CC (Madhya 8), where after ten days of conversation with Ramananda Mahaprabhu continues south. The last word of the first half is printed ≈ ରଲ (or ରଳ); read ରହିଲ (*rahila*, "stayed"), which the metre also requires (as printed the half-line has 8 syllables).
[^tirtha-135]: Places of the southern pilgrimage — CC, Madhya 9: Sri Ranga-kshetra (where Mahaprabhu spent the four months of the rains), Setubandha-Rameshvara, Udupi (the seat of Madhvacharya) and others. Madhava's order differs from CC. "Shankara" is probably Shiva (Shiva shrines) or Shringeri, the seat of Shankaracharya, which CC (Madhya 9) mentions next to Udupi; what the author meant is unclear.
[^prayaga-136]: According to CC (Madhya 9), Mahaprabhu returned from the south to Puri by the same road, through Vidyanagara on the Godavari; He was at Prayaga later, on the way to Vrindavana and back (Madhya 17–19). In Madhava, Prayaga stands between the southern shrines and Rajamandri — a divergence from CC (or some other place called "Prayaga"?). The first word of 4.136a is printed ≈ ପୀଠମାନନ୍ତ: "pithas" + an unclear ‑ନ୍ତ (*ananta*, "countless"?).
[^rajamandri-139]: Rajamandri (Rajamahendri, modern Rajahmundry) — Ramananda's province (see 3.132). In CC (Madhya 9), on the way back Mahaprabhu again meets Ramananda at Vidyanagara and tells him to leave his service and come to Puri; Ramananda arrives later (Madhya 10–11), as in Madhava (4.142–144). The ending ‑ର in ରାଜମନ୍ଦ୍ରୀର is taken as locative ("at Rajamandri"); the subscript ୍ର is unclearly printed.
[^tu-140]: Printed ≈ ରୂ: read ତୁ (*tu*, "you") — in this edition the second form of ତ looks like ର; this gives 9 syllables and the usual imperative "do not you stay".
[^heu-142]: ହେଉ ହେଲ (*heu hela*) — lit. "let it be, it has become"; here, by sense, assent: "very well, so be it". The same phrase occurs in 4.122, where Mahaprabhu covers Ramananda's mouth; there it probably means "enough".
[^leuti-143]: The word is unclearly printed (≈ େ + a sign like ନ + ଉ? + ଟି); read ଲେଉଟି (*leuṭi*, "having returned").
[^anka-144]: Anka — the Odia count of regnal years, which skips anka 1 and the ankas ending in 0 and 6. The nineteenth anka of Prataparudra (who came to the throne ≈1497) is his fifteenth regnal year, i.e. ≈1511/12. According to CC (Madhya 9–11), Ramananda came to Puri soon after Mahaprabhu's return from the south.
[^mandira-145]: According to CC (Madhya 11), Prataparudra released Ramananda from governing his province while keeping his salary, so that he could be with Mahaprabhu; CC says nothing about entrusting him with temple affairs. Madhava, who served under Ramananda (ch. 3), reports that the king gave him charge of Jagannatha's temple. ମନ୍ଦିର is everywhere printed ≈ ମନ୍ଦର.
[^bebhara-147]: ବେଭାର (*bebhāra* = *byabahāra*) here clearly means "affairs, management"; this supports the understanding of the same word in 4.96 and 4.104 ("usage, designation").
[^jiva-148]: Jivadeva — Ramananda's successor at Rajamahendri (his appointment and title follow in 4.149–151). The name is printed ≈ ଶ୍ରୀଜବଦେବ (without ୀ); below it is ଜୀବଦେବ. The last word of 4.148b is printed ≈ ରାଜମହେନ୍ଦଶ: the final sign looks like ଶ, read as «ରି» (*rājamahendari*), which gives the rhyme with ହକାରି.
[^sethai-149]: Continues 4.148, where the king sends Jivadeva to Rajamahendri (Rajamundry on the Godavari; cf. 3.132). We take 4.149 as the king's words (the gerunds *bujhi*, *rakhi* — "managing", "keeping"); it may also be narrative: "there he managed affairs and kept the army with him."
[^bahinipati-150]: Printed ≈ ବାହନପତି; we read ବାହିନୀପତି (*bāhinīpati*), "commander of the army", a well-known Odia military title (later also a family name). The metre (9 syllables) fits both readings.
[^jivadeva-151]: In 1.77 Jivadeva is called a "great warrior" (*mahārathā*) beside Rudradeva, and in 3.12 "the best of brahmanas"; here he is a military commander, rajaguru and poet. "Mighty" renders *sāmarathā* (= *samartha*), the same word as in 1.77.
[^dindima-152]: "Kavi-dindima" ("kettledrum of poets") is an honorific title for a poet. A Sanskrit poet of Prataparudra's court, Jivadeva Acharya, is known by this title; Madhava apparently means him (to be verified). The particle ‑ଟି (*aṭaïṭi, bolaïṭi*) is the same as in 4.98–109.
[^works-154]: *Bhagata-vaibhava* means "The Glory of the Devotees"; apparently the drama known as *Bhakti-vaibhava* (*Bhakti-vaibhava-nāṭaka*) of Jivadeva Acharya, to whom the poem *Bhakti-bhagavata* is also ascribed. The title *Utsahavati* is an uncertain reading (second sign ≈ ତ୍ସ/ସ୍ତ୍ର); the work is not identified. *Rupaka* is the general term for Sanskrit drama.
[^melani-155]: The first half-line (lit. "[they] saw the plays, this became") is uncertain: probably Jivadeva's plays were staged and watched, after which he received *melāṇi* — leave to depart — and went to Rajamahendri.
[^ksetra-157]: The Kshetra is Shri Kshetra, Puri. According to 4.144–147, in the nineteenth anka (≈1511/12) Ramananda returned to Nilachala and at the king's wish took over the management of the temple's affairs. The CC (Madhya 11) likewise tells that Ramananda, leaving his post in the south, settled in Puri to be with Mahaprabhu. The verb is printed ≈ ସମଜଇ (*samajaï*) and is not identified; the sense seems to be "looks after, cares for" (cf. ସମ୍ଭାଳଇ).
[^mandapa-158]: Apparently a mandapa for gatherings (goshthi) of Vaishnavas by the northern side of Jagannatha's temple; the temple itself is not named in the couplet. ଉତ୍ତର ପାର୍ଶ୍ୱଣ ("of the northern side") is an uncertain reading (the second sign of the second word is indistinct; = *pārśweṇa*?). As far as we know, the CC does not mention such a mandapa.
[^end-160]: The closing couplet of the chapter with the author's name (cf. 3.151); sheet 75 opens with the heading "ପଞ୍ଚମ ଅଧ୍ୟାୟ" — Chapter 5. Printed ≈ "ଦନ ବହୁଲେ … ମଧବ": we read ଦିନ ବହିଲେ "the days flowed by" and ମାଧବ (lost ‑ି and ‑ା).
