# Madhava Pattanayak. *Vaishnava-lilamrita*, Chapter 7
## The Swami Takes a Mantra from Balarama Dasa; Kashi Mishra's Anger

*Translated from Odia without a native speaker; doubtful places are marked (?) and (??). The original, transliteration and word-for-word translation are in `full/07.md`.*

*Chapter Seven*

**7.1.** Listen, O Vaishnavas, to [this] account — all sins will be destroyed.  
**7.2.** The Swami said: "I shall take a mantra and worship Krishna on the path of raga;[^svami-2]  
**7.3.** I shall give up (?) the shastras (??) [and] the Puranas — what need have I of them now?"[^sahasra-3]  
**7.4.** Prabhu said: "Take diksha — go to Balarama.[^balarama-4]  
**7.5.** Balarama's word comes true — take the mantra from him.[^vachana-5]  
**7.6.** Apart from him there is no other guru: [his] devotee's bhava is the greatest."  
**7.7.** The Swami took mantra-diksha and bowed down to Balarama.  
**7.8.** Kashi Mishra, hearing this, became very angry with the Swami:[^kashi-8]  
**7.9.** "You, a brahmana, receive (?) mantra-diksha from the mouth of a shudra!  
**7.10.** There is no … (??) in this — it is the misconduct (?) of the Kali age."[^anesara-10]  
**7.11.** The Swami said: "Sin does not cling to a Vaishnava even a particle.  
**7.12.** Be it a shudra or a brahmana — all human beings are equal.[^equal-12]  
**7.13.** If a jewel lies in a low place, to take it is dharma.[^ratna-13]  
**7.14.** This is firm in my heart: [my] steadfast (?) devotion (?) is to Balarama.[^nishtha-14]  
**7.15.** Why then do you reject (?) [me] on this account? We worship on the path of raga (?)."[^teju-15]  
**7.16.** Hearing this, Sarvabhauma and also … (??) Lakshmidhara[^lakshmidhara-16]  
**7.17.** said: "Say nothing [more]: Jagannatha has a siddha's body."[^siddha-17]  
**7.18.** They brought Kashi Mishra to understanding and increased [his] reverence for the Swami.  

## The Govardhana Stone for Raghunatha Dasa; the Panchasakha and the Path of Raga; the War with Krishnadevaraya and the Famine


**7.19.** A few days after this, Prabhu, to Raghunatha Dasa —[^raghunatha-19]  
**7.20.** calling [him], says: "Take this; keep (?) this stone from Govardhana.[^sambhaa-20]  
**7.21.** This stone is a shalagrama; do not regard it as ordinary (?)."[^itara-21]  
**7.22.** Sarvabhauma and Lakshmidhara heard [of it] — and in vexation (??) they kept silent.[^bisure-22]  
**7.23.** A shudra obtained the right to worship the shalagrama stone.[^shudra-23]  
**7.24.** "Is a shudra to be called a brahmana? What could be (??) beyond this?"[^sambhai-24]  
**7.25.** An unheard-of thing had happened — Kashi Mishra became angry.[^kashi-25]  
**7.26.** Achyuta, Ananta, Yashovanta, Balarama and Jagannatha,[^panchasakha-26]  
**7.27.** together with Prabhu, tasted rasa every day; disregarding the shastras (?) and the Puranas,[^shastra-27]  
**7.28.** they became intoxicated with sankirtana and abandoned the path of rules (vaidhi).[^vaidhi-28]  
**7.29.** All the rasa of Krishna's lila — up to the bhava of the gopis —[^gopi-29]  
**7.30.** they ponder constantly in their minds, accepting the path of raga as [the highest] authority.[^guni-30]  
**7.31.** Meanwhile, hear briefly a particular account of the Gajapati.[^gajapati-31]  
**7.32.** He had a war with Krishnadeva Raya, and more than once.  
**7.33.** The Gajapati's might and valour (?) Krishnaraya brought to nothing (?).[^balavirya-33]  
**7.34.** Famine came — a torment: "Save us, O Breaker of affliction!"[^durbhiksha-34]  
**7.35.** The crops and the harvest (?) were ruined: without water everything perished.[^phasala-35]  
**7.36.** The subjects cried out in distress: "Save us, O Lord Jagannatha!"[^jagannathe-36]  

## Famine and the Panchasakha's Kirtana; the Siege and Fall of Kondavidu


**7.37.** Without water the crops perished; … (??)[^mukhu-37]  
**7.38.** So it went year after year — floods, droughts, every kind of calamity.  
**7.39.** The flood would come and wash everything away; the drought destroyed the crops.  
**7.40.** From this calamity of floods and droughts the subjects raised a cry of distress.  
**7.41.** Balarama and Jagannatha, Achyuta, Ananta, Yashovanta[^panchasakha-41]  
**7.42.** performed kirtana from village to village, spreading (?) the name of Krishna.[^kirtan-42]  
**7.43.** In this way all the Gajapati's forces broke up (?).[^bhangile-43]  
**7.44.** From Udayagiri [and] Kondukur (?) the troops, defeated in battle,[^kondukur-44]  
**7.45.** gathered in the fort of Kondavidu (?): "We shall hold this fort well!"  
**7.46.** Rudradeva, the jewel among kings, took counsel (?) with the five (?)[^rudradeva-46]  
**7.47.** and stationed Bhadradeva [there]. "Defend this fort," he said.[^bhadradeva-47]  
**7.48.** "When there is a hero equal to you, how could any misfortune occur?"  
**7.49.** He summoned the five (?) samantas: "Defend this fort!" he said.[^samanta-49]  
**7.50.** Jivadeva, the mighty (?), stayed in that place without leaving it.[^jivadeva-50]  
**7.51.** "We shall kill — or else we shall die; other than this we shall do nothing."[^maribaa-51]  
**7.52.** Valiant Bhadradeva said: "Do not even think of anything else."[^bhaba-52]  
**7.53.** Krishnaraya's troops appeared and besieged [the fort] on all four sides.[^krishnaraya-53]  
**7.54.** Bhadradeva was there with ten (?) thousand soldiers.[^dasha-54]  
**7.55.** Within one month the soldiers there were dying of hunger.  
**7.56.** They cried out for water when the eatables had run out.[^sarilare-56]  
**7.57.** From inside the fort the cry was heard: "Save us, O Jagannatha!"  
**7.58.** After a month the enemy (?) entered the fort.[^arati-58]  
**7.59.** They bound Bhadradeva and cut down Jivadeva.  
**7.60.** And those five (?) samantas — they took them all (?) away with them.[^sabunta-60]  

## Virabhadra a Captive of Krishnadevaraya: the Duel with a Common Soldier and the Prince's End; the Gajapati's Peace with Krishnadevaraya


**7.61.** Bhadradeva was taken prisoner and remained (?) at the border of Benuru.[^bhadradeva-61]  
**7.62.** Once (?) Krishnaraya came [and] addressed (?) Bhadradeva.[^kale-62]  
**7.63.** He said: "Son of the Gajapati, you truly know the art of the sword.  
**7.64.** I have heard of your fame; when we see a bout, then we shall know, shall we not?"  
**7.65.** He called a soldier (?): "Now fence with this one."[^sainya-65]  
**7.66.** In Virabhadra's hand is a sword; it flashes (??), [he himself] is fierce.[^jhaleka-66]  
**7.67.** [Virabhadra] challenged Krishna Raya: "You fight with me," he said.  
**7.68.** At this the Raya refused: "I long [to see] your bout."[^trishna-68]  
**7.69.** Then the soldier (?) stepped forward: "This one (?) will fence [with you]."[^ehu-69]  
**7.70.** Bhadradeva says: "I am of royal blood; my contest (?) is with a king.[^taraja-70]  
**7.71.** Against another this sword is not matched (?). You [yourself] shall fight — come out (?), and well and good!"[^tule-71]  
**7.72.** The Raya said, seeing [his] presumption (??): "To test (?) your skill [against me] — that shall not be (??).[^bana-72]  
**7.73.** Fight with this soldier; if you disobey, we shall punish you."  
**7.74.** The soldier came and stood up [before him]; in his hand a sword gleams.[^ubhari-74]  
**7.75.** [Bhadradeva thought:] "He is no king's son; there is no royal blood in him."[^kumara-75]  
**7.76.** Bhadradeva, his whole body shaking, trembling with anger,  
**7.77.** drew the sword from its sheath and thrusts [it] into his own chest (?).[^vaksha-77]  
**7.78.** He fell to the ground with froth at the throat (??); Krishnaraya closes his eyes.[^galagaji-78]  
**7.79.** He said: "What a deed he has done! A worthy offspring of a king is this!"  
**7.80.** Lifting Bhadradeva, [he] exalted him with royal honours.  
**7.81.** He said: "Blessed is Virabhadra, blessed are your father and mother!  
**7.82.** You did not bear a slighting word, and you kept the Gajapati's honour."[^pana-82]  
**7.83.** Having said this, he grieved greatly and sent word to the Gajapati.  
**7.84.** Champadevi, the chief queen, is the Raya's captive.[^champa-84]  
**7.85.** The Gajapati was troubled [and] meditated on the Lord in his heart:[^prabhu-85]  
**7.86.** "Save us, O Lord Jagannatha! You are all the aims of human life."[^purushartha-86]  
**7.87.** The Raya sent word: "Virabhadra has perished."  
**7.88.** Great anxiety for the Gajapati: how could he get out of this?  
**7.89.** The Raya says: "Give your daughter — and set the chief queen free."  
**7.90.** On that basis he made peace, giving [in marriage] his daughter Jagamohini.[^jagamohini-90]  
**7.91.** The lands on the southern side of the Krishna he gave as dowry.[^krishna-91]  
**7.92.** [The Gajapati] returned to the Kshetra; then (?) he had darshan [of the Lord].[^kshetra-92]  

## The King Seeks Solace from the Panchasakha and Prabhu; the Dance at the Ratha-yatra and Prabhu's Mahabhava


**7.93.** [The king] summoned Swami Balarama Dasa and brought [him] to his side.[^svami-93]  
**7.94.** He said: "Tell of the devotees. Call Prabhu here.  
**7.95.** Perform kirtana near me — for the sake of my devotion (?) and faith.[^bhakata-95]  
**7.96.** Let me hear the name of Krishna; remove the burning heat from my heart."  
**7.97.** Laying his head at the Swami's feet, laying his head at Prabhu's feet,  
**7.98.** to the Swami he says, "Give [me] the name"; to Prabhu he says, "Instruct [me]" (??).[^nama-98]  
**7.99.** "Tell all the pastimes of Krishna; remove the torment from my heart.  
**7.100.** Seeing Your kirtana, dance and rasa, I shall be filled with joy (?)."[^tora-100]  
**7.101.** The Friend of devotees (??) heard [him] and consoled [his] heart in many ways.[^bandhu-101]  
**7.102.** Balarama, Achyuta, Ananta, Yashovanta and Jagannatha —[^panchasakha-102]  
**7.103.** in their midst Chaitanya stays and dances in the kirtana.  
**7.104.** The time of the chariot came: He danced in front of the chariot.[^ratha-104]  
**7.105.** Who can describe that dance of Prabhu? He is mahabhava, He is Rasaraja.[^rasaraja-105]  
**7.106.** In a moment He grows, [in a moment] shrinks (?), in a moment becomes invisible.[^chhurai-106]  
**7.107.** [There appear] all the traits of Radha's nature: in a moment, tears in His eyes.  
**7.108.** Seeing the unrestrained dance in bhava, the jewel among kings was filled with love.  
**7.109.** He said: "This is no human being — this is Radha's bhava in person."[^radhabhava-109]  
**7.110.** Rama Ray confirmed: "Truly, this is no human being."[^ramaray-110]  
**7.111.** The divine bhava takes possession [of Him] and becomes manifest in the delight of the dance.[^daibata-111]  
**7.112.** "This is Prabhu!" — so [the king] proclaimed (?) in the crowd of people at the Ratha-yatra.[^ughoshile-112]  
**7.113.** Hearing this, all rejoiced: "Chaitanya is Prabhu, this is certain (?)."[^bishesha-113]  
**7.114.** However many mahabhavas Prabhu has — who could describe them?  
**7.115.** Sometimes He remains in mahabhava — in the Gambhira, for a moment (?).[^gambhira-115]  
**7.116.** He rises, sits down, rolls [on the ground], crying out, "O Krishna!"  
**7.117.** Tears stream from His eyes — He is full of love for Krishna.  
**7.118.** This torment of separation from Krishna became manifest in Prabhu's body.[^viraha-118]  
**7.119.** He gives up the habit of counting the names in japa on a waist-cord.[^japa-119]  
**7.120.** In frenzy He passes (?) the day; like a child (?) He shows [His] bhava.[^nie-120]  
**7.121.** Sometimes He runs to the temple — "Where is my Krishna?" — to see [Him].[^deula-121]  
**7.122.** Crying "O Krishna!", He weeps aloud, and sometimes He reviles His own form.[^ninde-122]  
**7.123.** Sometimes He runs to the sea to see the form of Krishna.  
**7.124.** Seeing a cloud rise in the sky, He is overcome with agitation.[^megha-124]  
**7.125.** Sometimes He [is] at Tota-Gopinatha, and sometimes He sits in the Kunja-matha.[^tota-125]  

## Prabhu's Mahabhava in Nilachala; the Swami Knows His Bhava; the Panchasakha with Prabhu; the Author's Signature


**7.126.** And at times He goes to Gundicha (?) and, sitting in the mandapa, [becomes absorbed] in contemplation.[^gundicha-126]  
**7.127.** In solitude, meditating on Krishna's form, He ponders, relishing bhava.  
**7.128.** Again and again He faints, and at times the hairs of His body stand on end.[^kshane-128]  
**7.129.** And at times He rolls in the dust, smearing His whole body with dust.  
**7.130.** He runs to the Narendra tank, and at times plunges into the Markandeya [tank].[^narendra-130]  
**7.131.** Intoxicated with the rasa of kirtana, He dances wildly (?).[^matta-131]  
**7.132.** He sways back and forth, and with loud cries makes leaps.  
**7.133.** And at times He abides in [His ordinary] nature (?) and converses joyfully.[^prakriti-133]  
**7.134.** He is solely intoxicated (?) with the joy of dance, full of love for the rasa of kirtana.[^matta-134]  
**7.135.** His mind does not [rest] on sleep or food — know [this].  
**7.136.** The Swami brings a plate of food and makes [Him] eat by force (?).[^svami-136]  
**7.137.** Beside Svarupa Damodara He reveals mahabhava.[^svarupa-137]  
**7.138.** Seeing [this], people are astonished; do (?) they understand Prabhu's state?[^udanta-138]  
**7.139.** The treasure of prema of Nilachala — know: this is what is called "Prabhu."[^prabhu-139]  
**7.140.** Manifestly [He] is the embodiment of prema; He is the utmost limit of the rasa of prema.[^pratima-140]  
**7.141.** No one knows His bhava — this nature [of His] the Swami knows.[^svami-141]  
**7.142.** [The Swami] says: "Prabhu, be calm; give Your mind to the rasa of kirtana.  
**7.143.** In kirtana is the embodiment of Your bhava; how can people understand (??) [it]?"[^bhara-143]  
**7.144.** Balarama and Jagannatha, Ananta, Achyuta, Yashovanta —[^panchasakha-144]  
**7.145.** these five, gathering beside Prabhu, go about intoxicated with the rasa of kirtana.  
**7.146.** Prabhu said: "So be it (?)! Stay always with Me."[^astu-146]  
**7.147.** Prabhu manifests bhava — and calls it a human lila.[^naralila-147]  
**7.148.** So says Madhava-dasa, placing his hope in Prabhu's feet.[^colophon-148]  

[^svami-2]: The Swami is Jagannatha Dasa (cf. Ch. 6). In 6.63 the Gaudiyas reproached him as "uninstructed" (*aṇa-upadeśiā*); now he resolves to take a mantra. "The path of raga" (raga-marga) is worship of Krishna moved by spontaneous love. According to the CC (Madhya 22), raganuga-bhakti is following the mood of the eternal residents of Vraja; Gaudiya Vaishnavas, following Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati, stress that it is practised under the guidance of the guru and does not cancel respect for the shastras. Jagannatha Dasa and this episode are not mentioned in the CC/CB.
[^sahasra-3]: The second word (printed ≈ ସାହାସ୍ର, likewise in 7.27) is not identified: perhaps *sahasra* "thousand" — "the thousands [of verses] of the Puranas," or a corruption of *śāstra* — "the shastras and Puranas"; as in 7.27, the translation adopts the latter. The sense of the verse is that the Swami renounces the bookish study of the Puranas for worship on the path of raga. The first word is printed ≈ ଛୁଡ଼ବ; read as ଛାଡ଼ିବ "I shall give up." *Etheṭi* — "here, in this," with the particle ‑*ṭi* (cf. 7.10, 7.11, 7.13).
[^balarama-4]: Balarama is Balarama Dasa, one of the Panchasakha (cf. 1.76, 5.126–131). In Madhava, Prabhu Himself sends Jagannatha Dasa to take diksha from Balarama Dasa; Kashi Mishra calls Balarama a shudra (7.9), i.e. he is not a brahmana by birth. This is an Odia tradition; the Panchasakha are not mentioned in the CC/CB.
[^vachana-5]: *Bacana-siddha* — "one whose word comes true," perfected in speech. *Tā tahu̐* — "from him"; in 7.6, "apart from him."
[^kashi-8]: Kashi Mishra is the brahmana in whose house in Puri Mahaprabhu lived (CC, Madhya 10). The words of 7.9–10 are his reproach to the Swami (with the familiar "thou").
[^anesara-10]: The third word of 7.10a (printed ≈ ଅ + a sign like େ + ନ + a blotted ସ/ବ + ର) is not identified; from the sense one expects "propriety, right conduct": "there is nothing proper in this." *Ethuṭi* — "in this," with the particle ‑*ṭi*. The last word is printed ≈ ଦୂରବୁର; read as ଦୁରାଚାର "misconduct."
[^equal-12]: Cf. Mahaprabhu's words in the CC (Madhya 8.128): whether a brahmana, a sannyasi or a shudra — whoever knows the science of Krishna is a guru. Gaudiya Vaishnavas, following Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati, accept a Vaishnava of any birth as guru and as brahmana by his qualities and devotion, not by birth. In Madhava the Swami puts the thought more broadly: "all human beings are equal."
[^ratna-13]: A saying close to the *Manu-smriti* (Ch. 2): good knowledge may be accepted even from a lower person, a jewel even from an impure place. *Karibāṭi* — infinitive with the particle ‑*ṭi*.
[^nishtha-14]: The first words of 7.14b (printed ≈ ନ/ଲ with a superscript sign + ଷ with a subscript sign | ଭ + a sign like ଲ୍ଲ) are read as ନିଷ୍ଠ ଭକ୍ତି "steadfast devotion" — uncertain. *Dṛṛhe* (printed ≈ ଦୃ + େ + ଢ଼) — "firmly."
[^teju-15]: *Kipā̃* "why" is printed ≈ କପାଁ (cf. 6.Sh4b). The last word of 7.15a (printed ≈ ତ + େ + ଜ with a small mark below) is read as ତେଜୁ "you reject" (pres. 2 sg.), which gives a rhyme with *bhaju*. The first word of 7.15b (printed ≈ ର + a sign like ଙ୍ଗ) is read as ରାଗ "raga" after 7.2.
[^lakshmidhara-16]: Sarvabhauma is Vasudeva Sarvabhauma, the court pandit of Prataparudra (CC, Madhya 6). Lakshmidhara is not identified by us; he does not appear in the CC/CB. The word before his name (printed ≈ େ + ମ + ର + ଦ୍ଧ/ଢ) is not read — perhaps a byname or surname.
[^siddha-17]: "Jagannatha" here is apparently Jagannatha Dasa: he has the body of a siddha, a perfected one — and so worldly rules of caste do not apply to him. The reading "[the Swami] is a perfected body of Jagannatha" is also possible.
[^raghunatha-19]: Raghunatha Dasa Gosvami came from a wealthy kayastha family of Saptagrama; having renounced the world, he lived for many years near Mahaprabhu in Puri in the care of Svarupa Damodara (CC, Antya 6). According to the CC (Antya 6), Mahaprabhu gave him a stone from Govardhana and a garland of gunja beads, which He Himself had kept for three years, and told him to worship the stone in the mode of goodness — with water and tulasi leaves — explaining that this stone is Krishna Himself. Madhava calls the stone a "shalagrama" (7.21) and Raghunatha a "shudra" (7.23). The first word of the verse is printed ≈ ଚ/ତ + ହୁଁ; read as ତହୁଁ "after this"; *kichudina* is printed ≈ କଛୁଦନ (both ‑i lost).
[^sambhaa-20]: The last word (printed ≈ ସ + ମ with subscript ଭ + ା + ଅ) is read as ସମ୍ଭାଅ and understood as an imperative "keep, look after" (cf. ସମ୍ଭାଳିବା "to keep, manage") — a guess; the rhyme *nia / sambhāa* supports it. Instead of *ethu nia* "take this (from here)" a reading *bege nia* "take it quickly" is known.
[^itara-21]: The second word of 7.21b (printed ≈ ଇ + an unclear sign like ତ/େ + ର; OCR: ଇତର) is read as ଇତର "ordinary, common"; *na maṇa* — "do not regard" (from *maṇibā*). The rhyme *śāḷagrāma / maṇa* is imperfect. Instead of *ehuṭi* "this [indeed is]" a reading *e aṭe* "this is" is known. On the Govardhana stone see the note to 7.19.
[^bisure-22]: Sarvabhauma and Lakshmidhara — cf. 7.16 and the note to it. The first word of 7.22b (printed ≈ ବ with an inner hook, i.e. ବି, cf. ବିଶେଷ 7.31, + ସ with a subscript sign + େର) is read as ବିସୁରେ "in grief, in vexation" (cf. *bisuribā* "to lament", 6.59) — very uncertain; the sense of the line is that they did not object but kept silent. In the CC Sarvabhauma is a devotee of Mahaprabhu (Madhya 6); this episode is not in the CC/CB.
[^shudra-23]: By smarta norms the worship of the shalagrama is open only to brahmanas; hence the indignation of 7.24–25. The *Hari-bhakti-vilasa* admits pious shudras (sat-shudras) to shalagrama worship as well. Gaudiya Vaishnavas, following Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati, hold that a Vaishnava who has received proper initiation is entitled to worship the shalagrama whatever his birth (cf. CC, Madhya 8: "whether a brahmana, a sannyasi or a shudra — whoever knows the science of Krishna is a guru").
[^sambhai-24]: The verse is taken as the indignation of the orthodox (cf. 7.9 and 7.25: Kashi Mishra's anger); the speaker is not indicated. The last word (printed ≈ ସ + ମ with subscript ଭ + ା + ଇ; OCR: ସ୍ତମ୍ଧାଇ) is understood as a contraction of ସମ୍ଭବଇ "becomes possible": "what could be beyond this?" It may be a form of the same verb as ସମ୍ଭାଅ in 7.20 ("who else could cope with this?"). The rhyme *bolaï / sambhāi* supports the ending ‑i.
[^kashi-25]: *Nāhi̐ nohibā kathā* — lit. "a thing that ought never to be", i.e. something unheard-of. Kashi Mishra — cf. 7.8–9, where he is angry with Svami for taking initiation from a shudra. In the CC Kashi Mishra is a devoted servant of Mahaprabhu, in whose house the Lord lived in Puri (Madhya 10); his anger at Mahaprabhu is not in the CC/CB.
[^panchasakha-26]: The Panchasakha ("five friends") are Odia Vaishnava poets: Achyutananda, Ananta, Yashovanta, Balarama Dasa and Jagannatha Dasa (in Madhava usually "Svami"). They are not mentioned in the CC/CB.
[^shastra-27]: *Prabhura tule* — "together with Prabhu" (cf. 6.73). The word ସାହାସ୍ର (the same as in 7.3) is read as a corrupt *śāstra* "the shastras"; *sahasra* "a thousand" ("thousands [of verses] of the Puranas") is also possible, see the note to 7.3. *Upekṣi* — "disregarding". Gaudiya Vaishnavas, following Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati, do not set raganuga-bhakti against the shastras: Rupa Gosvami's *Bhakti-rasamrita-sindhu* (1.2.101) cites a verse that devotion to Hari without reliance on shruti, smriti, the Puranas and the Pancharatra leads only to disturbance.
[^vaidhi-28]: *Mata hele* — "became intoxicated, mad [with ecstasy]" (*mata* = *matta*). Vaidhi-marga is the path of sadhana-bhakti impelled by the injunctions of the shastras; raga-marga (raganuga) is the path impelled by spontaneous attraction, following the mood of the residents of Vraja. According to the CC (Madhya 22), these are the two kinds of sadhana-bhakti; for Gaudiya Vaishnavas raganuga does not mean giving up the rules of sadhana. The words "abandoned the path of rules" are Madhava's Odia interpretation.

[^svarupa-137]: Svarupa Damodara was Mahaprabhu's closest companion in Puri in the last years. According to the CC (Antya 14–20), it was to him and Ramananda Raya that the Lord disclosed His feelings of separation in the Gambhira.
[^udanta-138]: *Udanta* — "tidings, account, circumstances," here Prabhu's state. The verb is printed ≈ ବୁ + ଝ with a stroke above + ନ୍ତ (‑i not visible; likewise in 7.143); read as *bujhanti* "understand." An affirmative understanding is also possible: "people… grasp Prabhu's state."
[^prabhu-139]: The syntax of 7.139b ("ପ୍ରଭୁ ଏ ବୋଲିବାଟି ଜାଣ") is taken as "know: this is what is called 'Prabhu'." *Bolibāṭi* is printed ≈ ବୋଲବାଟି (‑i lost). Cf. the proclamation of Chaitanya as "Prabhu" in 7.112.
[^pratima-140]: *Pratimā* (printed ≈ ପ୍ରତମା) here means the "embodiment, likeness" of prema, not a temple Deity.
[^svami-141]: In Madhava, the Swami (Jagannatha Dasa) alone knows Prabhu's bhava, calms Him and addresses Him with the familiar "you" (*tora*, 7.143). According to the CC (Antya 14–20), it was Svarupa Damodara who understood Mahaprabhu's inner mood and consoled Him with songs and verses in tune with His feelings; Gaudiya Vaishnavas, following Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati, follow this account. That the Swami (and not Prabhu) is speaking is inferred from the sense (cf. 7.141b); the speaker of 7.142a is not marked.
[^bhara-143]: The word after *manuṣya* is printed ≈ ଭ without the stroke of the ligature ଭା + ର (OCR illegible). Read as *bhara* (??) — meaning unclear ("the fullness [of bhava]"?); possibly a misprint for ଭାବ "state." Translated by the general sense.
[^panchasakha-144]: The Panchasakha are Balarama Dasa, Jagannatha Dasa, Ananta, Achyuta (Achyutananda) and Yashovanta (cf. 7.102, 8.4). The couplet continues syntactically in 7.145. *Cāli* "go about, move" (7.145b) — the nuance is unclear.
[^astu-146]: The word in 7.146a is printed ≈ ଅ + ସ with a subscript sign (ତ/ଥ) and ୁ (OCR: ଅସ୍ତ); read as *astu* "so be it." *Āmbha tule* — "with Us," honorific "with Me." To whom the singular *tu* "you" is addressed (with five companions in 7.145) is unclear — perhaps the Swami.
[^naralila-147]: The subject of *bolaï* "calls" is taken to be Prabhu: He manifests bhava but calls it a human lila. Cf. CC, Madhya 21: of all Krishna's pastimes the highest is *nara-lila*, the pastimes in human form; Mahaprabhu, concealing His divinity, acts as a devotee.
[^colophon-148]: The closing formula with the author's name (cf. 2.131). With this verse Chapter 7 ends: it is followed by an ornament, with no colophon "ଇତି… ସମ୍ପୂର୍ଣ୍ଣ"; Chapter 8 ("ଅଷ୍ଟମ ଅଧ୍ୟାୟ") begins on leaf 105.
