# Madhava Pattanayaka. *Vaishnava-lilamrita*, Chapter 3
## The Two *Gita-govindas*: the Test before Jagannatha and Rudradeva's Edict

*Translated from Odia without a native speaker; doubtful places are marked (?) and (??). The original, transliteration and word-for-word translation are in `full/03.md`.*

**3.1.** O sadhus, hear with faith (?) of the sweetness of the *Gita-govinda*.[^astha-1]  
**3.2.** [To] the song that Jayadeva composed, the rahasa was performed in the gumuti (?).[^gumuti-2]  
**3.3.** For a long time this dance was performed in the gumuti.  
**3.4.** [There was] Divakara, the best of brahmanas, foremost in learning.[^divakara-4]  
**3.5.** Taking his seat in the assembly (?), he composes the *Gita-govinda* anew.  
**3.6.** He is the king's favourite and chief (?) [at his court]; he composed a song-dance.[^pata-6]  
**3.7.** He put the king's name into the signature and received many rewards (?).[^bhanita-7]  
**3.8.** This dance of the *Sri Gita-govinda* is called "the New Song".[^abhinava-8]  
**3.9.** On the former *Gita-govinda* dance a royal prohibition was proclaimed:  
**3.10.** "Let the king's song-dance be performed; the former padas shall not be sung."  
**3.11.** In time that king went to heaven, and an obstacle arose for the king's song.[^purushottama-11]  
**3.12.** Jivadeva, the best of brahmanas — his mind [was given] to learned disputation —[^jivadeva-12]  
**3.13.** reported to Rudradeva: "This dance is not good."[^rudradeva-13]  
**3.14.** The temple servants said: "How so? This dance and that one are not the same" (?).[^nohanta-14]  
**3.15.** They placed the two manuscripts in the gumuti, saying: "Choose which is good" (?).[^pothi-15]  
**3.16.** Having placed the manuscripts in the holy gumuti, they bolted (?) the doors there.  
**3.17.** The next day at dawn the servants saw with their own eyes:  
**3.18.** the Bada Thakura's song-manuscript lies beneath,[^badathakura-18]  
**3.19.** and Jayadeva's song-dance lies plainly on top.  
**3.20.** The servants went and reported; Sri Rudradeva commanded:  
**3.21.** "Let both this dance and that one be performed; set neither aside (?).  
**3.22.** The Bada Thakura's song-dance is performed at the time of the Lord's bhoga (?):[^bhoga-22]  
**3.23.** [beginning] from (??) the evening dhupa, it ends at the Bada-singhara.[^singhara-23]  
**3.24.** At other times, performing song, let them chant (?) the *Sri Gita-govinda*.  
**3.25.** The troupe of the Bada Thakura, the troupe of Kapileshvara Thakura,[^samprada-25]  
**3.26.** the troupe of the old dancers, the troupe (?) of the Telangi dancers —  
**3.27.** these dancers shall sing, they shall perform the Bada Thakura's songs.  
**3.28.** Know: this dance is for the others (?); but the singing of the *Sri Gita-govinda*,[^itara-28]  
**3.29.** the sweet song of Jayadeva, the Vaishnava singers honour (?).[^vaishnava-29]  
**3.30.** They shall sing this song of rasa, and all the untrained shall learn [it].  
**3.31.** Apart from this there shall be no other song; let the Padichha (?) be vigilant."[^padichha-31]  
**3.32.** Such was the king's ordinance; and the servants proclaimed (?) it.  
**3.33.** At the Jaya-Vijaya gate (?) they had the inscription carved in stone (?):[^inscription-33]  
**3.34.** Jayadeva's song of rasa, excluding other songs from it,  
**3.35.** they shall sing in the daytime; the Vaishnava servants shall relish [its] rasa.  
**3.36.** Know: this arrangement was made in the … (??) anka of the king.[^anka-36]  
**3.37.** Kavichandra Raya Divakara quickly (?) says to the king:[^kavichandra-37]  

## Kavichandra's Departure; Madhavendra Puri and Kshirachora-Gopinatha at Remuna


**3.38.** "O (?) king, a muddle has been made here: the servants did not obey [your] word."[^bho-38]  
**3.39.** The king, learning of it, kept silent; Kavichandra [took] it as an insult.  
**3.40.** He left the king's court (?) and went away to foreign lands.[^muhansa-40]  
**3.41.** Jivadeva was greatly delighted; the servants were satisfied.[^jivadeva-41]  
**3.42.** After some time (?), Bada-thakura's *Gita-govinda* —[^badathakura-42]  
**3.43.** its manuscript was tied up with a string: no one found any taste in it.  
**3.44.** All the Vaishnava troupes [sang] the nectarean song of Jayadeva.  
**3.45.** In the temple this very dance was performed; the other song did not take hold.[^jayadeva-45]  
**3.46.** All were satisfied; the devotees rejoiced in bhava.  
**3.47.** Now hear a sweet [tale], if you long for the treasure of prema.  
**3.48.** [There was] a Vaishnava named Madhavendra; his life is rare.[^madhavendra-48]  
**3.49.** A king named Nrisimha … to (?) Govardhana … (??) —[^nrsimha-49]  
**3.50.** brought the murti of Gopala and settled [Him] at Remuna-kataka.[^remuna-50]  
**3.51.** He installed the murti of Gopala and offered abundant bhoga.  
**3.52.** In time (?) Madhavendra came, renowned among the saintly Vaishnavas.[^kale-52]  
**3.53.** Before the murti of Gopala he swooned in rapture.  
**3.54.** So lovely [was] the form — a twofold (?) [stream] of love flowed.[^yugala-54]  
**3.55.** Gazing at the murti of Gopala, he passes the night without sleep.  
**3.56.** The servants did not give [him] bhoga; but Madhava there (?) [longed] for Him.[^madhava-56]  
**3.57.** Bolting the doors, they went home; Madhava remains without food.  
**3.58.** At midnight (?) [the servant] had a dream; the servant opened the doors  
**3.59.** and saw, hidden under the hem [of Gopala's cloth], a little pot of kshira-bhoga that had not been served (?).[^kshira-59]  
**3.60.** Seeing Gopala's mercy, the servant was astonished.  
**3.61.** Humbly (?) he prostrated himself and proclaimed the glory of Gopala.  
**3.62.** He called out loudly: "Who here is the devotee?"  
**3.63.** He calls, uttering the name of Gopala; Madhava rises with love.  
**3.64.** The servant, setting the vessel down, falls at Madhava's feet:  
**3.65.** "You are dear to Gopala, best of devotees: take the bhoga.  
**3.66.** I have offended you; I have received Gopala's command.  
**3.67.** When you taste this prasada, [you will know]: it is not (?) [mere] prasada — it is nectar."[^nuhai-67]  
**3.68.** Madhava eats with a joyful heart, remembering the name of Gopala within.  
**3.69.** At dawn the servant rose and fell at Madhava's feet.  
**3.70.** All the people (?) heard of this and at once came to Madhava:  
**3.71.** "Where was such a devotee? How much merit he must have done!"  
**3.72.** For His devotee's sake Gopala [came] to be called "Thief of Kshira" (?).  
**3.73.** All gathered together and surrendered (?) their hearts at Madhava's feet.  
**3.74.** They performed kirtana there, and Madhava thrilled all over.[^karambita-74]  
**3.75.** This Gopinatha, Kshirachora, became famous among the people.[^gopinatha-75]  
**3.76.** Many years [Madhavendra] spent [there], meditating on Kshirachora.[^years-76]  
**3.77.** His disciples Raghava and Ishvara came and joined him at Remuna.[^disciples-77]  

## Madhavendra Puri at Bentapur; the Author's Own Story: the Road from Khandapada and the Meeting with Ramananda


**3.78.** Bhavananda invited [him]: "Come to my village," he said.[^bhavananda-78]  
**3.79.** Going (?) by the road to Purushottama (?), [they] halted to rest in the village of Bentapur.[^purastama-79]  
**3.80.** Madhava, Ishvara [and] Raghava — guru and disciples — all [became] guests.[^madhavendra-80]  
**3.81.** They stayed in Bhavananda's home and performed kirtan in Bentapur.  
**3.82.** Keeping the beat on mridanga and gini, they led the kirtan of Hari's name.[^gini-82]  
**3.83.** They stayed there one paksha, in the month of Phalguna of the … (??) anka.[^anka-83]  
**3.84.** Meanwhile, hear the sweetness of [another] small, particular tale.[^author-84]  
**3.85.** My village is Khandapada; I had reached twenty-one years of age.[^khandapada-85]  
**3.86.** My father and mother passed away: how was I to live decently (?)?[^bhale-86]  
**3.87.** In this world I am a poor man: I have no kin, no brothers.  
**3.88.** "What shall I do?" I pondered (?); and I placed (?) this resolve (?) in my mind:[^dei-88]  
**3.89.** I set my mind on going to the Kshetra and gathered courage in my heart.[^kshetra-89]  
**3.90.** At dawn I went out of the village and became a wayfarer (?) on the road.[^pathika-90]  
**3.91.** How many villages, hills, fields (?) and forests I then passed!  
**3.92.** Whatever anyone gave, that I ate — I refused (?) nothing.[^bare-92]  
**3.93.** In ten days I arrived [there] and halted to rest in the village of Bentapur.  
**3.94.** Evening time came; Madhava together with Raghava  
**3.95.** at (?) Bhavananda's gate joyfully (?) performs kirtan.[^dvara-95]  
**3.96.** When I saw the kirtan, boundless attachment (?) [arose in me], and my mind grew firm.  
**3.97.** At dawn I went gently and fell at the Gosai's feet.[^gosai-97]  
**3.98.** I said: "Prabhu, protect [me]: I am the servant of your feet.  
**3.99.** Take me into [your] kirtan (?); teach me Hari's name."[^tule-99]  
**3.100.** Madhava Gosai, hearing this, said: "Rise, rise."  
**3.101.** He placed his hand on my head and stroked my back with his hand.  
**3.102.** He said: "You are still young in years — what business have you with kirtan?  
**3.103.** Go to Bhavananda: he will look after you."[^udanta-103]  
**3.104.** I went to Bhavananda and prostrated myself at his feet.  
**3.105.** Bhavananda … (??) [me] and spoke to Ramananda.[^baragila-105]  
**3.106.** Ramananda, his eldest son, sits (?) with a book in his hand.[^ramananda-106]  
**3.107.** I went up and told [him] my sorrow; he looked at me with affection.  
**3.108.** He said: "Stay with me; besides, you are a karana lad.[^karana-108]  
**3.109.** Where will you go? Stay here: one can see from your face (?) that you have studied."[^muhi-109]  
**3.110.** [I replied:] "I have studied nothing." [And he] diligently (?) taught me.[^nathai-110]  

## The Departure of Madhavendra Puri; *Jagannatha-vallabha* and Ramananda Raya at Rajamandri


**3.111.** I learned a few lessons, and for a little while (?) I took up the palm leaf (?).[^author-111]  
**3.112.** [Ramananda] said: "You will stay by me and manage my affairs."  
**3.113.** I stayed, following him, [taking] him (?) as my sole refuge (??).[^ashraya-113]  
**3.114.** Madhava Puri left the Kshetra (?) and stayed at Remuna-kataka.[^kshetra-114]  
**3.115.** Near the murti of Gopinatha he performed kirtana every day.  
**3.116.** Intoxicated with prema, overwhelmed — streams of tears flowed from his eyes.  
**3.117.** In time he departed to heaven — in the month of Phalguna of the fifth anka.[^svarga-117]  
**3.118.** The king grieved greatly and summoned Bhavananda.[^bhavananda-118]  
**3.119.** He said: "Madhava Gosai has departed to heaven singing the Name.[^nama-119]  
**3.120.** Blessed is the life of this Vaishnava, blessed the fame he established (?)."  
**3.121.** He grieved much and said: "May he attain a good destiny."  
**3.122.** I shall describe (?) Rama Raya's books: in learning he was accomplished.[^pothi-122]  
**3.123.** He composed many song-dramas — [one] by the name of *Jagannatha-vallabha*.[^jagannathavallabha-123]  
**3.124.** He offered it into the king's hands, and the king praised it: "Excellent, excellent!"  
**3.125.** The king gave his consent and said: "Stage the song-drama."  
**3.126.** In the month of Phalguna of the eighth anka — the festival (?) of *Jagannatha-vallabha*:[^utsava-126]  
**3.127.** at the Dola festival, in the temple, for five days in the Nata-mandira[^dola-127]  
**3.128.** this song-drama was performed, and the king obtained joy.  
**3.129.** With affection the king gave Ramananda the title "Raya"[^raya-129]  
**3.130.** and presented a golden staff (?). The devotees enact Radha and Krishna.[^chhadi-130]  
**3.131.** Seeing Raya's drama, the servants praised it: "Excellent, excellent!"  
**3.132.** In the ninth anka [Raya] received a command (?): "Go to Rajamandri," [the king] said,[^rajamandri-132]  
**3.133.** "govern the realm of Rajamahendri: the people of that realm are discontented."  
**3.134.** Rama Raya became the ruler and managed the affairs of Rajamandri.  
**3.135.** He showed kindness to me as well: "Come with me," he said.  
**3.136.** In the king's thirteenth anka a famine brought suffering.[^anka-136]  
**3.137.** Many people perished; the soldiers were left without food.  
**3.138.** Through Rama Raya's care the people … (??) survived.[^shayale-138]  
**3.139.** Bhavananda departed to heaven; Ramananda … (?) … (??).[^bhavananda-139]  
**3.140.** Gopinatha and Vaninatha were worthy persons for affairs of state;[^brothers-140]  
**3.141.** Kalanidhi and Sudhanidhi gave their minds to the … (??) science.[^kshetra-141]  
**3.142.** Ramananda, [filled with] the mood (?) of a devotee, [lived] day and night in the joy of kirtana.[^bhava-142]  
**3.143.** Bathing at a ghat of the Godavari, he devotes himself to the bhajana of the Name.[^godavari-143]  
**3.144.** In the company of sadhus and Vaishnavas he reads songs and stages dramas.  
**3.145.** He turned over (?) the manuscript of Jayadeva's *Gita* — the ways of rasa.[^gitagovinda-145]  
**3.146.** Constantly pondering (?) on it (?), he gives his mind to the bhajana of the Name.[^ethi-146]  
**3.147.** He is immersed in the prema of Radha and Krishna and knows nothing else.  
**3.148.** The king came (?) and rebuked him: "Gather (?) the army," he said.[^war-148]  
**3.149.** "The noose has already tightened (?) round our neck, and now you talk of Krishna-rasa![^phasha-149]  
**3.150.** At such a time this is not good: equip the army and set [it] in a ring (?)."[^bedha-150]  
**3.151.** Says Madhava the servant: listen, devotees (?), to another rasa.[^end-151]  

[^astha-1]: Printed ଅସ୍ଥ (*astha*), without the long vowels; taken as ଆସ୍ଥା କରି, "with faith, attentively" (a usual formula addressing listeners). The last sign of the half-line looks like ଶ — the usual look of ରି in this font (*kari*), as the rhyme with ମାଧୁରୀ confirms.
[^gumuti-2]: Gumuti: the dome, the inner space of Jagannatha's temple (cf. 2.41–2.50: the devadasis' dance "inside the gumuti of the temple"). Here ଗୁମୁରେ is printed three times (3.2, 3.3, 3.15) and ଶ୍ରୀଗୁମୁରେ in 3.16, without ଟି; taken as ଗୁମୁଟିରେ, "in the gumuti" (?). Rahasa: the temple dance (see 2.1, 2.65).
[^divakara-4]: Divakara is Kavichandra Raya Divakara (see 3.37), a court poet of the king. The *Abhinava Gita-govinda* ("New Gita-govinda") is usually ascribed to King Purushottama-deva himself; according to Madhava, Divakara composed it and signed it with the king's name (3.7).
[^pata-6]: ପାଟ (*pāṭa*) is taken as "chief, principal" (cf. ପାଟମହାଦେଈ, "chief queen"); perhaps "the king's dear and chief [poet]". The reading is sure (rhyme with ନାଟ); the sense is doubtful.
[^bhanita-7]: The bhanita is the closing line of a song naming its author. ମେଲଣି (*melaṇi*) is taken as "rewards, gifts" (?); perhaps ମେଲାଣି, "a parting gift".
[^abhinava-8]: The *Abhinava Gita-govinda*, "New Gita-govinda": an Odishan Sanskrit poem in imitation of Jayadeva.
[^purushottama-11]: The king is Purushottama-deva (r. c. 1467–1497), father of Prataparudra. "Went to heaven" means he passed away.
[^jivadeva-12]: Jivadeva is named together with Rudradeva in 1.77 as well; who he was cannot be established from the text.
[^rudradeva-13]: Rudradeva is King Prataparudra (r. c. 1497–1540), son of Purushottama, devotee and patron of Mahaprabhu in Puri (*Chaitanya-charitamrita*, Madhya 11–14).
[^nohanta-14]: ନୋହନ୍ତା (*nohantā*) is taken as "are not [the same]"; the ‑ା in ନୋହନ୍ତା and କେମନ୍ତା is apparently for the rhyme. The sense "this dance is not [inferior to] that one" is also possible.
[^pothi-15]: Printed ଭଲ ଭେଲ ବାଛ (*bhala bhela bācha*): taken as "choose which [turned out] good" (ଭେଲ — an old form "became, was"?). The story of the two books placed before Jagannatha, of which Jayadeva's *Gita-govinda* was found on top the next morning, is known in Odisha; a similar story is also told in Priyadasa's commentary on Nabhaji's *Bhaktamala*.
[^badathakura-18]: Bada Thakura ("the elder lord") here is the late King Purushottama, author of "the New Song"; "the Bada Thakura's songs" are the *Abhinava Gita-govinda*.
[^bhoga-22]: Printed ହେଗବେଳେ; the sign looks like ହ (as in ହେବ, 3.23); taken as ଭୋଗବେଳେ, "at the time of bhoga", the food offering to Jagannatha.
[^singhara-23]: Sandhya-dhupa (ସଞ୍ଜ ଧୂପ) is the evening food offering; the Bada-singhara is Jagannatha's last, night-time dress before His rest; by temple custom the *Gita-govinda* is sung at it. The first word, ସକଳଠାରୁ (*sakaḷaṭhāru*), is unclear: "from all"? "from morning" (ସକାଳ)? — the second sign is blotted; from the context (vv. 22–24) the Bada Thakura's songs are sung from the evening dhupa until the Bada-singhara.
[^samprada-25]: Samprada (ସପରଦା, ସପ୍ରଦା): a troupe of temple dancers. Kapileshvara Thakura is King Kapilendra-deva (r. 1434–1467), founder of the Suryavamsha dynasty and father of Purushottama. The Telangi dancers came from the Telugu-speaking lands.
[^itara-28]: ଇତରକେ (*itarake*): the first sign is blotted; taken as "for the others" (ଇତର, "other") — i.e. the Bada Thakura's songs are left to the dancers, and the *Gita-govinda* to the Vaishnava singers (?).
[^vaishnava-29]: ମାନନ୍ତ (*mānanta*) is taken as "honour"; it may be a plural ending of ଗାଆଣ, "singers". Mahaprabhu Himself in Puri relished the songs of the *Gita-govinda* day and night with Svarupa Damodara and Ramananda Raya (*Chaitanya-charitamrita*, Madhya 2).
[^padichha-31]: The Padichha (ପଡ଼ିଛା) is an official who supervises the worship in Jagannatha's temple. The reading is doubtful: printed ପ + an unclear ligature + ଛା.
[^inscription-33]: The Jaya-Vijaya gate leads into the jagamohana of Jagannatha's temple. An inscription of Prataparudra (usually dated 1499) survives on it, directing the temple dancers and singers to perform Jayadeva's *Gita-govinda* and listing the same troupes as 3.25–3.26. Printed ଦ୍ୱାରସ୍ଥରେ (?) (the ligature after ର is unclear) and ପଥରେ (?) — "on stone".
[^anka-36]: Anka: the Odia count of regnal years (see 1.72). The word before ଅଙ୍କେ is unclear: ରୃ/ତୃ/ବୃ + ଶ/ଣ; possibly ବୃଷ, the solar month Vrisha (April–May), usual in Odia dates, or a numeral. The date is fixed by the gate inscription (c. 1499).
[^kavichandra-37]: Kavichandra Raya is an honorific title of Divakara (Divakara Mishra). Printed ସତ୍ତର, taken as ସତ୍ୱର, "quickly"; ଉତ୍ତର, "reply", is also possible. His speech continues on leaf 59.
[^bho-38]: The speaker is apparently Kavichandra Raya Divakara (3.37), author of Bada-thakura's "New Song": he complains that the servants are not carrying out the royal order on the songs (3.21–3.31). The first sign of the line is a separate «େ» with the ligature this font uses for ଭା; read as ଭୋ, the vocative "O!".
[^muhansa-40]: ମୁହାଁସ (*muhā̃sa*) is taken as "presence, court" (cf. ମୁହାଁ, "facing"); not checked in dictionaries.
[^jivadeva-41]: Jivadeva — the opponent of the "New Song" who asked Rudradeva to set it aside (3.12–3.13). The servants are the temple servants who sang Jayadeva's *Gita-govinda*.
[^badathakura-42]: Bada-thakura is the late King Purushottama-deva; his *Gita-govinda* is the *Abhinava Gita-govinda* (see 3.8, 3.18). To tie a manuscript with a string is to lay it aside, to stop reading it. Printed ≈ କଛୁଦନ; read as କିଛୁଦିନ, "some days".
[^jayadeva-45]: Jayadeva's *Gita-govinda* is sung in the Jagannatha temple to this day. Mahaprabhu especially loved its songs (cf. 3.29).
[^madhavendra-48]: Madhavendra Puri — a sannyasi, guru of Ishvara Puri (3.77) and thus parama-guru of Sri Chaitanya. Gaudiya Vaishnavas honour him as the first sprout of the tree of prema (*Chaitanya-charitamrita*, Adi 9). His story with Gopala and Kshirachora-Gopinatha is told by Mahaprabhu in CC, Madhya 4.
[^nrsimha-49]: Nrisimha — apparently one of the Eastern Ganga kings named Narasimha-deva (13th–14th c.). The second half-line is unclear: ତରେ (*tare*) — "to, for"?; ଚରସ୍ଥ (*carastha*) is unidentified (ଚର "messenger"? "pasture"?). From 3.50, the king brought the murti of Gopala from Govardhana. CC (Madhya 4) differs: Madhavendra himself found Gopala on Govardhana Hill and installed Him there, while the Deity of Remuna is Gopinatha; Madhava calls the Deity of Remuna both Gopala and Gopinatha (3.75).
[^remuna-50]: Remuna — a village near Balasore in northern Odisha; kataka — a fort, royal camp.
[^kale-52]: Printed କ'ଲେ: the apostrophe marks elision in this edition; read as କାଲେ / କାଳେ, "in time, once" (?).
[^yugala-54]: ଯୁଗଳ (*yugaḷa*), "pair": possibly tears of love from both of Madhavendra's eyes; or the love of the Divine Couple, Radha and Krishna (?). ଲବଣ୍ୟ is printed without the long "a" (= ଲାବଣ୍ୟ, "loveliness").
[^madhava-56]: Madhava here and below is Madhavendra (the name shortened for the metre), not the author of the poem. The second half-line is understood tentatively: ତହିଁ (?), "there"; ତା ସରାଗ, "with love for Him".
[^kshira-59]: Kshira — sweet rice cooked in milk, the famous offering to Gopinatha at Remuna. In CC (Madhya 4), Gopinatha hid a pot of kshira behind His cloth and in a dream told the pujari to give it to Madhavendra; there Madhavendra asks for nothing and waits not at bolted doors but in the empty market, chanting the holy name. The word ନବାଡ଼େ (*nabāṛe*) is unclear: "not served" (ନ ବାଢ଼େ)?
[^nuhai-67]: ନୁହଇ (*nuhaï*): the first sign resembles both ନୁ and ଲୁ; taken as "is not": "it is not [mere] prasada — it is nectar" (?).
[^karambita-74]: କରମ୍ବିତ (Skt. *karambita*, "studded") — apparently as in *pulaka-karambita*, "covered with hairs standing on end": one of the symptoms of ecstasy (sattvika-bhava).
[^gopinatha-75]: Kshirachora-Gopinatha ("Gopinatha, the Thief of Kshira") — the Deity of the temple at Remuna; He received this name from the lila just told (CC, Madhya 4). On His way from Bengal to Puri, Mahaprabhu visited Remuna and retold this story to His companions.
[^years-76]: According to Madhava, Madhavendra spent many years at Remuna. According to CC (Madhya 4), fearing fame, he left Remuna that same night and returned later, on his journey with sandalwood for Gopala; Gaudiya Vaishnavas honour his samadhi at Remuna. ଧାନ (*dhāna*) is a simplified spelling of ଧ୍ୟାନ, "meditation".
[^disciples-77]: Ishvara Puri — disciple of Madhavendra Puri and guru of Sri Chaitanya; CC (Antya 8) describes his service to Madhavendra in his teacher's last days. Raghava — apparently Raghavendra Puri, another disciple of Madhavendra (?).
[^bhavananda-78]: Bhavananda Raya — father of Ramananda Raya; Mahaprabhu received him and his sons in Puri, calling him Pandu and his sons the five Pandavas (*Chaitanya-charitamrita*, Madhya 10; cf. Antya 9). According to Madhava, his village was Bentapur. The guest is Madhavendra Puri (see 3.80): after the wonders of Gopala and Kshirachora-Gopinatha at Remuna (leaves 59–60), Bhavananda invites him home.
[^purastama-79]: Purushottama — Puri (Purushottama-kshetra). Printed ପୁରସ୍ତମ — apparently a colloquial form of ପୁରୁଷୋତ୍ତମ (?); the second word is printed ନଲ, taken as ଗଲ, "went" (possibly ନେଲ "took [the road]" or ଚଲ "walked"). Bentapur — a village in Orissa on the way to Puri.
[^madhavendra-80]: Madhava — Madhavendra Puri; Ishvara — Ishvara Puri, his disciple, who later initiated Sri Chaitanya (*Chaitanya-charitamrita*, Adi 9 and 17); Raghava — another disciple of Madhavendra, unidentified. The *Chaitanya-charitamrita* (Madhya 4) describes Madhavendra's journey to Puri via Remuna but has no visit to Bhavananda at Bentapur.
[^gini-82]: Gini — small hand cymbals keeping the beat in kirtan.
[^anka-83]: Anka — the Odia count of regnal years (see 1.72, 3.36). The word before ଅଙ୍କ is the same unclear word as in 3.36 (ରୃ/ତୃ + a sign like ଶ); if it is ତୃ‑ "third", then Phalguna (February–March) of Prataparudra's third anka falls in early 1499 or so — the same time as the edict in 3.33–3.36. A paksha is half a lunar month.
[^author-84]: From this verse the author, Madhava Pattanayaka, tells his own story: orphanhood, the road to Puri, the meeting with Madhavendra Puri, and entering the household of Ramananda Raya.
[^khandapada-85]: Khandapada (Khandapara) — a village in Orissa, Madhava's birthplace. If he was twenty-one around 1499 (see 3.83), he was born around 1478.
[^bhale-86]: "Passed away" — lit. "became [the prey] of Kala, time". ଭଲେ (*bhale*) taken as "well, decently"; possibly ଭାଳେ "[I] pondered" (printed without ା).
[^dei-88]: Printed ଭ + ଳ + ଲି + ଇଁ — taken as ଭାଳିଲିଁ, "I pondered". In the last word the signs ଦ, େ, ଇ stand in an odd order; taken as ଦେଇ, "placing [in the mind]"; possibly ବଢ଼ଇ, "[the thought] grows". The first word of the second half-line is ବୁଦ୍ଧି "resolve" or ବୁଝି "having understood".
[^kshetra-89]: The Kshetra — Sri Kshetra, Puri, the abode of Jagannatha.
[^pathika-90]: ପଥିକା (?) — printed like ପଥ୍‌କା / ପଥୁକା; taken as ପଥିକ, "wayfarer". ପ୍ରଭାତୁ (here and in 3.97) — "from dawn" (‑ତୁ = ‑ରୁ after ତ).
[^bare-92]: Printed ବାର, then the sign େ before ନ; taken as "refusal, prohibition" (= ବାରଣ?): "I refused nothing".
[^dvara-95]: Printed ଦ୍ୱାର ଭଗେ — taken as ଦ୍ୱାରଭାଗେ, "at the gate" (?), or ଦ୍ୱାର ଆଗେ, "before the gate". ସରଗେ taken as ସରାଗେ, "joyfully, lovingly".
[^gosai-97]: Gosai (gosvami) — here Madhavendra Puri.
[^tule-99]: ତୁଲେ (*tule*) taken as "along with, into [the circle of]"; the first sign is ତୁ or ରୁ. The sense is clear from the context: the youth asks to be admitted to the kirtan.
[^udanta-103]: Lit. "he will look into your circumstances" (ଉଦନ୍ତ — "situation, news").
[^baragila-105]: The verb ବରଗିଲ (*baragila*) is not identified; the second sign resembles ଋ. Possibly ବରଣିଲ, "received, welcomed", or ବର୍ଜିଲ, "sent away".
[^ramananda-106]: Ramananda — Ramananda Raya, Bhavananda's eldest son, later a governor under Prataparudra and an intimate associate of Mahaprabhu (*Chaitanya-charitamrita*, Madhya 8). "With a book in his hand" apparently means: engaged in learned work. ବସଇ, "sits", is possibly ବସିଣ, "seated".
[^karana-108]: Karana — the Odia caste of scribes and officials; both Ramananda Raya and Madhava himself belonged to it (Pattanayaka is a karana title).
[^muhi-109]: ମୁହିଁ (*muhi̐*) taken as ମୁହଁ, "face": "one can see from your face that you have studied". If read as "I", the half-line becomes Madhava's reply, but it would then contradict 3.110.
[^nathai-110]: ନଠାଇ (*naṭhāi*) taken as ନିଠାଇ / ନିଠେଇ, "diligently, attentively" (?). The change of speakers (Madhava — Ramananda) is not marked in the original.
[^author-111]: The author's account of himself (from 3.84) continues: Ramananda Raya took the young Madhava into his house and taught him (3.106–3.110). The palm leaf is the writing material; "took up the palm leaf" apparently means he learned to write. ଖେଦାଏ (*khedāe*) is not identified; understood as "a little" by parallel with ଥୋକାଏ in the first half-line. Ramananda's words in 3.112 mark the beginning of Madhava's service as Ramananda's personal assistant (cf. 1.67).
[^ashraya-113]: The second half-line is unclear: ତାୟାକୁ (*tāẏāku*) is taken as ତାହାକୁ, "him"; ଏକ ଶ୍ରଇ (*eka śraï*) is perhaps ଏକାଶ୍ରୟ, "sole refuge"; ପର (*para*), "as, like".
[^kshetra-114]: The Kshetra is Sri Kshetra, Puri (cf. 3.89). Printed ≈ ଷେ + a sign like ତ୍ର + ‑ୁ; taken as କ୍ଷେତ୍ରୁ, "from the Kshetra" (?). According to Madhava, Madhavendra Puri (the author's "Madhava Puri", "Madhava Gosai") returned from Puri to Remuna, to Kshirachora-Gopinatha (3.48–3.77).
[^svarga-117]: The fifth anka of Prataparudra is, by conversion, roughly early 1500 (Phalguna is February–March; cf. 3.83). "Departed to heaven" (*swargabāsa*, "took up abode in heaven") is Madhava's usual expression for a departure (cf. 3.11). Gaudiya Vaishnavas honour Madhavendra Puri's departure as his entrance into the Lord's eternal pastimes, not a passage to the heavenly planets; his samadhi is at Remuna, beside the temple of Kshirachora-Gopinatha.
[^bhavananda-118]: The king is Prataparudra. Bhavananda is the father of Ramananda Raya (see 3.78).
[^nama-119]: Cf. *Chaitanya-charitamrita*, Antya 8: in his last days Madhavendra Puri was served by his disciple Ishvara Puri, and he left the world reciting the verse *ayi dīna-dayārdra nātha*, full of longing for Krishna.
[^pothi-122]: ବର୍ଣ୍ଣିବ (*barṇṇiba*) is the future of the verb "to describe"; taken as the author's words, "I shall describe" (?). ପୋଥା (*pothā*) is a variant of ପୋଥି, "book, manuscript"; ସାମରଥା (*sāmarathā*) is ସାମର୍ଥ୍ୟ, "capability, power".
[^jagannathavallabha-123]: *Jagannatha-vallabha* is Ramananda Raya's Sanskrit drama about Radha and Krishna. Mahaprabhu relished its songs (*Chaitanya-charitamrita*, Madhya 2), and Ramananda himself trained two devadasis to perform it (*Chaitanya-charitamrita*, Antya 5).
[^utsava-126]: The eighth anka is, by conversion, roughly early 1502. The last word is printed ≈ ଉତ୍ସର (the last sign is like ର); taken as ଉତ୍ସବ, "festival" (cf. ଦୋଳଉତ୍ସବ in 3.127).
[^dola-127]: Dola (Dola-yatra, Dola-purnima) is the spring swing festival on the full moon of Phalguna, one of the main festivals of the year in Puri. The Nata-mandira is the hall for dance and performances in the Jagannatha temple. In ପାଞ୍ଚଦିନ and ମନ୍ଦିରେ the «ି» is not visible (printed ≈ ପାଞ୍ଚଦନ, ମନ୍ଦରେ).
[^raya-129]: "Raya" (*rāẏa*) is an honorific title; it is the name by which Ramananda is known in the *Chaitanya-charitamrita* ("Ramananda Raya", "Raya").
[^chhadi-130]: The word ଛଡ଼ି (*chaṛi*, "staff, rod") is read uncertainly (the first sign looks like ନ with flourishes); a golden staff is a mark of honour. The second half-line apparently concerns the performance: devotees play the roles of Radha and Krishna.
[^rajamandri-132]: Rajamandri / Rajamahendri (today Rajahmundry) is a town on the Godavari, then a southern province of the Gajapati realm. In the *Chaitanya-charitamrita* (Madhya 7–8) Ramananda Raya is governor at Vidyanagara on the Godavari; there Mahaprabhu met him on His way south (in Madhava, chapter 4). The ninth anka is, by conversion, about 1503. ଅଜ୍ଞା (*ajñā*) = ଆଜ୍ଞା, "command".
[^anka-136]: The thirteenth anka is, by conversion, about 1506–1507. ଦୁର୍ଭିକ୍ଷ, "famine", is printed ≈ ଦୁଭକ୍ଷ.
[^shayale-138]: The last word (printed ≈ ଶ + ୟ + ଲେ; in this font ଶ may also be ରି) is not identified; by the sense perhaps "all, everywhere" (cf. ସକଳେ?).
[^bhavananda-139]: According to Madhava, Bhavananda departed about 1507. In the *Chaitanya-charitamrita* Bhavananda Raya with his sons meets Mahaprabhu after His return from the south (Madhya 10) and is mentioned in the story of Gopinatha Pattanayaka (Antya 9), i.e. he is alive in the 1510s. The second half-line is unclear: printed ≈ ଦୁବଳ କ୍ରୟା; if ଦୁର୍ବଳ କାୟା, "wasted his body [with grief]"; if କ୍ରିୟା, "performed the [funeral] rites".
[^brothers-140]: Gopinatha (Pattanayaka), Vaninatha, Kalanidhi and Sudhanidhi are Ramananda's brothers, the sons of Bhavananda; the *Chaitanya-charitamrita* (Madhya 10) calls them, together with Ramananda, the five Pandavas. Vaninatha served Mahaprabhu in Puri; the story of Gopinatha Pattanayaka is in CC, Antya 9.
[^kshetra-141]: The first word of the second half-line is «େ» + ଷ (= କ୍ଷ) + the sign seen in 3.114 (≈ ତ୍ର); taken as କ୍ଷେତ୍ର (??). Possibly a misprint for କ୍ଷାତ୍ର / କ୍ଷତ୍ର, "martial [science]": then Kalanidhi and Sudhanidhi took up warfare, while Gopinatha and Vaninatha took up administration.
[^bhava-142]: The second word is printed with a ligature that resembles both ରା and ଭା, + ବ; taken as ଭାବ, "mood" (?).
[^godavari-143]: The Godavari is the river on whose bank Mahaprabhu met and conversed with Ramananda Raya (*Chaitanya-charitamrita*, Madhya 8; in Madhava, chapter 4). ସ୍ନାହାନ (*snāhāna*) is ସ୍ନାନ, "bathing".
[^gitagovinda-145]: Jayadeva's *Gita* is the *Gita-govinda* (cf. 3.44). The verb is printed ≈ «େ» + ଲ with a hook + ଟ + ଇ + ଲ; taken as ଲେଉଟଇଲ, "turned over, reread" (?). ରସରୀତି, "the ways (customs) of rasa", is apparently what he read.
[^ethi-146]: The first sign of the second word is broken; taken as ଏଥି, "on this" (i.e. on the *Gita-govinda*) (?). ଗୁଣଇ, "ponders, recites" (?). ନିରନ୍ତର is printed without «ି».
[^war-148]: The king is Prataparudra. Apparently the threat of war on the southern frontier is meant (perhaps with Vijayanagara, whose army occupied the southern possessions of the Gajapati in the 1510s) (?). ଭେଲାଅ (*bhelāa*) is taken as ମେଳାଅ, "gather" (?); the first sign looks like ଭ.
[^phasha-149]: The verb is printed ≈ ଲଗିଲଟି; taken as ଲଗିଲଣି (= ଲାଗିଲାଣି), "has already tightened" (?).
[^bedha-150]: ବେଢ଼ାଏ (*beṛhāe*), from ବେଢ଼ା, "enclosure, ring"; understood as "in a ring, around" (?).
[^end-151]: The closing couplet of the chapter with the author's name; chapter 4 begins on the next leaf. ଭକତେ, "devotees", is printed indistinctly (the second sign looks like ବ/ର); ଆନ ରସ, "another rasa", apparently means "another tale", i.e. the next chapter.
