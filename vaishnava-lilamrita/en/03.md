# Madhava Pattanayaka. *Vaishnava-lilamrita*, Chapter 3
## The Two *Gita-govindas*: the Test before Jagannatha and Rudradeva's Edict

*Translated from Odia without a native speaker; doubtful places are marked (?) and (??). The original, transliteration and word-for-word translation are in `full/03.md`.*

**3.1.** O sadhus, hear with faith (?) of the sweetness of the *Gita-govinda*.[^astha-1]  
**3.2.** [To] the song that Jayadeva composed, the rahasa was performed in the gumuti (?).[^gumuti-2]  
**3.3.** For a long time this dance was performed in the gumuti.  
**3.4.** [There was] Divakara, the best of brahmanas, foremost in learning.[^divakara-4]  
**3.5.** Taking his seat in the assembly (?), he composes the *Gita-govinda* anew.  
**3.6.** He is the king's favourite and chief (?) [at his court]; he composed a song-dance.[^pata-6]  
**3.7.** He put the king's name into the signature and received many rewards (?).[^bhanita-7]  
**3.8.** This dance of the *Sri Gita-govinda* is called "the New Song".[^abhinava-8]  
**3.9.** On the former *Gita-govinda* dance a royal prohibition was proclaimed:  
**3.10.** "Let the king's song-dance be performed; the former padas shall not be sung."  
**3.11.** In time that king went to heaven, and an obstacle arose for the king's song.[^purushottama-11]  
**3.12.** Jivadeva, the best of brahmanas — his mind [was given] to learned disputation —[^jivadeva-12]  
**3.13.** reported to Rudradeva: "This dance is not good."[^rudradeva-13]  
**3.14.** The temple servants said: "How so? This dance and that one are not the same" (?).[^nohanta-14]  
**3.15.** They placed the two manuscripts in the gumuti, saying: "Choose which is good" (?).[^pothi-15]  
**3.16.** Having placed the manuscripts in the holy gumuti, they bolted (?) the doors there.  
**3.17.** The next day at dawn the servants saw with their own eyes:  
**3.18.** the Bada Thakura's song-manuscript lies beneath,[^badathakura-18]  
**3.19.** and Jayadeva's song-dance lies plainly on top.  
**3.20.** The servants went and reported; Sri Rudradeva commanded:  
**3.21.** "Let both this dance and that one be performed; set neither aside (?).  
**3.22.** The Bada Thakura's song-dance is performed at the time of the Lord's bhoga (?):[^bhoga-22]  
**3.23.** [beginning] from (??) the evening dhupa, it ends at the Bada-singhara.[^singhara-23]  
**3.24.** At other times, performing song, let them chant (?) the *Sri Gita-govinda*.  
**3.25.** The troupe of the Bada Thakura, the troupe of Kapileshvara Thakura,[^samprada-25]  
**3.26.** the troupe of the old dancers, the troupe (?) of the Telangi dancers —  
**3.27.** these dancers shall sing, they shall perform the Bada Thakura's songs.  
**3.28.** Know: this dance is for the others (?); but the singing of the *Sri Gita-govinda*,[^itara-28]  
**3.29.** the sweet song of Jayadeva, the Vaishnava singers honour (?).[^vaishnava-29]  
**3.30.** They shall sing this song of rasa, and all the untrained shall learn [it].  
**3.31.** Apart from this there shall be no other song; let the Padichha (?) be vigilant."[^padichha-31]  
**3.32.** Such was the king's ordinance; and the servants proclaimed (?) it.  
**3.33.** At the Jaya-Vijaya gate (?) they had the inscription carved in stone (?):[^inscription-33]  
**3.34.** Jayadeva's song of rasa, excluding other songs from it,  
**3.35.** they shall sing in the daytime; the Vaishnava servants shall relish [its] rasa.  
**3.36.** Know: this arrangement was made in the … (??) anka of the king.[^anka-36]  
**3.37.** Kavichandra Raya Divakara quickly (?) says to the king:[^kavichandra-37]  

## Kavichandra's Departure; Madhavendra Puri and Kshirachora-Gopinatha at Remuna


**3.38.** "O (?) king, a muddle has been made here: the servants did not obey [your] word."[^bho-38]  
**3.39.** The king, learning of it, kept silent; Kavichandra [took] it as an insult.  
**3.40.** He left the king's court (?) and went away to foreign lands.[^muhansa-40]  
**3.41.** Jivadeva was greatly delighted; the servants were satisfied.[^jivadeva-41]  
**3.42.** After some time (?), Bada-thakura's *Gita-govinda* —[^badathakura-42]  
**3.43.** its manuscript was tied up with a string: no one found any taste in it.  
**3.44.** All the Vaishnava troupes [sang] the nectarean song of Jayadeva.  
**3.45.** In the temple this very dance was performed; the other song did not take hold.[^jayadeva-45]  
**3.46.** All were satisfied; the devotees rejoiced in bhava.  
**3.47.** Now hear a sweet [tale], if you long for the treasure of prema.  
**3.48.** [There was] a Vaishnava named Madhavendra; his life is rare.[^madhavendra-48]  
**3.49.** A king named Nrisimha … to (?) Govardhana … (??) —[^nrsimha-49]  
**3.50.** brought the murti of Gopala and settled [Him] at Remuna-kataka.[^remuna-50]  
**3.51.** He installed the murti of Gopala and offered abundant bhoga.  
**3.52.** In time (?) Madhavendra came, renowned among the saintly Vaishnavas.[^kale-52]  
**3.53.** Before the murti of Gopala he swooned in rapture.  
**3.54.** So lovely [was] the form — a twofold (?) [stream] of love flowed.[^yugala-54]  
**3.55.** Gazing at the murti of Gopala, he passes the night without sleep.  
**3.56.** The servants did not give [him] bhoga; but Madhava there (?) [longed] for Him.[^madhava-56]  
**3.57.** Bolting the doors, they went home; Madhava remains without food.  
**3.58.** At midnight (?) [the servant] had a dream; the servant opened the doors  
**3.59.** and saw, hidden under the hem [of Gopala's cloth], a little pot of kshira-bhoga that had not been served (?).[^kshira-59]  
**3.60.** Seeing Gopala's mercy, the servant was astonished.  
**3.61.** Humbly (?) he prostrated himself and proclaimed the glory of Gopala.  
**3.62.** He called out loudly: "Who here is the devotee?"  
**3.63.** He calls, uttering the name of Gopala; Madhava rises with love.  
**3.64.** The servant, setting the vessel down, falls at Madhava's feet:  
**3.65.** "You are dear to Gopala, best of devotees: take the bhoga.  
**3.66.** I have offended you; I have received Gopala's command.  
**3.67.** When you taste this prasada, [you will know]: it is not (?) [mere] prasada — it is nectar."[^nuhai-67]  
**3.68.** Madhava eats with a joyful heart, remembering the name of Gopala within.  
**3.69.** At dawn the servant rose and fell at Madhava's feet.  
**3.70.** All the people (?) heard of this and at once came to Madhava:  
**3.71.** "Where was such a devotee? How much merit he must have done!"  
**3.72.** For His devotee's sake Gopala [came] to be called "Thief of Kshira" (?).  
**3.73.** All gathered together and surrendered (?) their hearts at Madhava's feet.  
**3.74.** They performed kirtana there, and Madhava thrilled all over.[^karambita-74]  
**3.75.** This Gopinatha, Kshirachora, became famous among the people.[^gopinatha-75]  
**3.76.** Many years [Madhavendra] spent [there], meditating on Kshirachora.[^years-76]  
**3.77.** His disciples Raghava and Ishvara came and joined him at Remuna.[^disciples-77]  

[^astha-1]: Printed ଅସ୍ଥ (*astha*), without the long vowels; taken as ଆସ୍ଥା କରି, "with faith, attentively" (a usual formula addressing listeners). The last sign of the half-line looks like ଶ — the usual look of ରି in this font (*kari*), as the rhyme with ମାଧୁରୀ confirms.
[^gumuti-2]: Gumuti: the dome, the inner space of Jagannatha's temple (cf. 2.41–2.50: the devadasis' dance "inside the gumuti of the temple"). Here ଗୁମୁରେ is printed three times (3.2, 3.3, 3.15) and ଶ୍ରୀଗୁମୁରେ in 3.16, without ଟି; taken as ଗୁମୁଟିରେ, "in the gumuti" (?). Rahasa: the temple dance (see 2.1, 2.65).
[^divakara-4]: Divakara is Kavichandra Raya Divakara (see 3.37), a court poet of the king. The *Abhinava Gita-govinda* ("New Gita-govinda") is usually ascribed to King Purushottama-deva himself; according to Madhava, Divakara composed it and signed it with the king's name (3.7).
[^pata-6]: ପାଟ (*pāṭa*) is taken as "chief, principal" (cf. ପାଟମହାଦେଈ, "chief queen"); perhaps "the king's dear and chief [poet]". The reading is sure (rhyme with ନାଟ); the sense is doubtful.
[^bhanita-7]: The bhanita is the closing line of a song naming its author. ମେଲଣି (*melaṇi*) is taken as "rewards, gifts" (?); perhaps ମେଲାଣି, "a parting gift".
[^abhinava-8]: The *Abhinava Gita-govinda*, "New Gita-govinda": an Odishan Sanskrit poem in imitation of Jayadeva.
[^purushottama-11]: The king is Purushottama-deva (r. c. 1467–1497), father of Prataparudra. "Went to heaven" means he passed away.
[^jivadeva-12]: Jivadeva is named together with Rudradeva in 1.77 as well; who he was cannot be established from the text.
[^rudradeva-13]: Rudradeva is King Prataparudra (r. c. 1497–1540), son of Purushottama, devotee and patron of Mahaprabhu in Puri (*Chaitanya-charitamrita*, Madhya 11–14).
[^nohanta-14]: ନୋହନ୍ତା (*nohantā*) is taken as "are not [the same]"; the ‑ା in ନୋହନ୍ତା and କେମନ୍ତା is apparently for the rhyme. The sense "this dance is not [inferior to] that one" is also possible.
[^pothi-15]: Printed ଭଲ ଭେଲ ବାଛ (*bhala bhela bācha*): taken as "choose which [turned out] good" (ଭେଲ — an old form "became, was"?). The story of the two books placed before Jagannatha, of which Jayadeva's *Gita-govinda* was found on top the next morning, is known in Odisha; a similar story is also told in Priyadasa's commentary on Nabhaji's *Bhaktamala*.
[^badathakura-18]: Bada Thakura ("the elder lord") here is the late King Purushottama, author of "the New Song"; "the Bada Thakura's songs" are the *Abhinava Gita-govinda*.
[^bhoga-22]: Printed ହେଗବେଳେ; the sign looks like ହ (as in ହେବ, 3.23); taken as ଭୋଗବେଳେ, "at the time of bhoga", the food offering to Jagannatha.
[^singhara-23]: Sandhya-dhupa (ସଞ୍ଜ ଧୂପ) is the evening food offering; the Bada-singhara is Jagannatha's last, night-time dress before His rest; by temple custom the *Gita-govinda* is sung at it. The first word, ସକଳଠାରୁ (*sakaḷaṭhāru*), is unclear: "from all"? "from morning" (ସକାଳ)? — the second sign is blotted; from the context (vv. 22–24) the Bada Thakura's songs are sung from the evening dhupa until the Bada-singhara.
[^samprada-25]: Samprada (ସପରଦା, ସପ୍ରଦା): a troupe of temple dancers. Kapileshvara Thakura is King Kapilendra-deva (r. 1434–1467), founder of the Suryavamsha dynasty and father of Purushottama. The Telangi dancers came from the Telugu-speaking lands.
[^itara-28]: ଇତରକେ (*itarake*): the first sign is blotted; taken as "for the others" (ଇତର, "other") — i.e. the Bada Thakura's songs are left to the dancers, and the *Gita-govinda* to the Vaishnava singers (?).
[^vaishnava-29]: ମାନନ୍ତ (*mānanta*) is taken as "honour"; it may be a plural ending of ଗାଆଣ, "singers". Mahaprabhu Himself in Puri relished the songs of the *Gita-govinda* day and night with Svarupa Damodara and Ramananda Raya (*Chaitanya-charitamrita*, Madhya 2).
[^padichha-31]: The Padichha (ପଡ଼ିଛା) is an official who supervises the worship in Jagannatha's temple. The reading is doubtful: printed ପ + an unclear ligature + ଛା.
[^inscription-33]: The Jaya-Vijaya gate leads into the jagamohana of Jagannatha's temple. An inscription of Prataparudra (usually dated 1499) survives on it, directing the temple dancers and singers to perform Jayadeva's *Gita-govinda* and listing the same troupes as 3.25–3.26. Printed ଦ୍ୱାରସ୍ଥରେ (?) (the ligature after ର is unclear) and ପଥରେ (?) — "on stone".
[^anka-36]: Anka: the Odia count of regnal years (see 1.72). The word before ଅଙ୍କେ is unclear: ରୃ/ତୃ/ବୃ + ଶ/ଣ; possibly ବୃଷ, the solar month Vrisha (April–May), usual in Odia dates, or a numeral. The date is fixed by the gate inscription (c. 1499).
[^kavichandra-37]: Kavichandra Raya is an honorific title of Divakara (Divakara Mishra). Printed ସତ୍ତର, taken as ସତ୍ୱର, "quickly"; ଉତ୍ତର, "reply", is also possible. His speech continues on leaf 59.
[^bho-38]: The speaker is apparently Kavichandra Raya Divakara (3.37), author of Bada-thakura's "New Song": he complains that the servants are not carrying out the royal order on the songs (3.21–3.31). The first sign of the line is a separate «େ» with the ligature this font uses for ଭା; read as ଭୋ, the vocative "O!".
[^muhansa-40]: ମୁହାଁସ (*muhā̃sa*) is taken as "presence, court" (cf. ମୁହାଁ, "facing"); not checked in dictionaries.
[^jivadeva-41]: Jivadeva — the opponent of the "New Song" who asked Rudradeva to set it aside (3.12–3.13). The servants are the temple servants who sang Jayadeva's *Gita-govinda*.
[^badathakura-42]: Bada-thakura is the late King Purushottama-deva; his *Gita-govinda* is the *Abhinava Gita-govinda* (see 3.8, 3.18). To tie a manuscript with a string is to lay it aside, to stop reading it. Printed ≈ କଛୁଦନ; read as କିଛୁଦିନ, "some days".
[^jayadeva-45]: Jayadeva's *Gita-govinda* is sung in the Jagannatha temple to this day. Mahaprabhu especially loved its songs (cf. 3.29).
[^madhavendra-48]: Madhavendra Puri — a sannyasi, guru of Ishvara Puri (3.77) and thus parama-guru of Sri Chaitanya. Gaudiya Vaishnavas honour him as the first sprout of the tree of prema (*Chaitanya-charitamrita*, Adi 9). His story with Gopala and Kshirachora-Gopinatha is told by Mahaprabhu in CC, Madhya 4.
[^nrsimha-49]: Nrisimha — apparently one of the Eastern Ganga kings named Narasimha-deva (13th–14th c.). The second half-line is unclear: ତରେ (*tare*) — "to, for"?; ଚରସ୍ଥ (*carastha*) is unidentified (ଚର "messenger"? "pasture"?). From 3.50, the king brought the murti of Gopala from Govardhana. CC (Madhya 4) differs: Madhavendra himself found Gopala on Govardhana Hill and installed Him there, while the Deity of Remuna is Gopinatha; Madhava calls the Deity of Remuna both Gopala and Gopinatha (3.75).
[^remuna-50]: Remuna — a village near Balasore in northern Odisha; kataka — a fort, royal camp.
[^kale-52]: Printed କ'ଲେ: the apostrophe marks elision in this edition; read as କାଲେ / କାଳେ, "in time, once" (?).
[^yugala-54]: ଯୁଗଳ (*yugaḷa*), "pair": possibly tears of love from both of Madhavendra's eyes; or the love of the Divine Couple, Radha and Krishna (?). ଲବଣ୍ୟ is printed without the long "a" (= ଲାବଣ୍ୟ, "loveliness").
[^madhava-56]: Madhava here and below is Madhavendra (the name shortened for the metre), not the author of the poem. The second half-line is understood tentatively: ତହିଁ (?), "there"; ତା ସରାଗ, "with love for Him".
[^kshira-59]: Kshira — sweet rice cooked in milk, the famous offering to Gopinatha at Remuna. In CC (Madhya 4), Gopinatha hid a pot of kshira behind His cloth and in a dream told the pujari to give it to Madhavendra; there Madhavendra asks for nothing and waits not at bolted doors but in the empty market, chanting the holy name. The word ନବାଡ଼େ (*nabāṛe*) is unclear: "not served" (ନ ବାଢ଼େ)?
[^nuhai-67]: ନୁହଇ (*nuhaï*): the first sign resembles both ନୁ and ଲୁ; taken as "is not": "it is not [mere] prasada — it is nectar" (?).
[^karambita-74]: କରମ୍ବିତ (Skt. *karambita*, "studded") — apparently as in *pulaka-karambita*, "covered with hairs standing on end": one of the symptoms of ecstasy (sattvika-bhava).
[^gopinatha-75]: Kshirachora-Gopinatha ("Gopinatha, the Thief of Kshira") — the Deity of the temple at Remuna; He received this name from the lila just told (CC, Madhya 4). On His way from Bengal to Puri, Mahaprabhu visited Remuna and retold this story to His companions.
[^years-76]: According to Madhava, Madhavendra spent many years at Remuna. According to CC (Madhya 4), fearing fame, he left Remuna that same night and returned later, on his journey with sandalwood for Gopala; Gaudiya Vaishnavas honour his samadhi at Remuna. ଧାନ (*dhāna*) is a simplified spelling of ଧ୍ୟାନ, "meditation".
[^disciples-77]: Ishvara Puri — disciple of Madhavendra Puri and guru of Sri Chaitanya; CC (Antya 8) describes his service to Madhavendra in his teacher's last days. Raghava — apparently Raghavendra Puri, another disciple of Madhavendra (?).
