# Madhava Pattanayaka. *Vaishnava-lilamrita*, Chapter 2
## The Rahasa in Nilachala

*Translated from Odia without a native speaker; doubtful places are marked (?) and (??). The original, transliteration and word-for-word translation are in `full/02.md`.*

**2.1.** Giving my heart to Krishna's feet, I shall describe a little of the rahasa.[^rahasa-1]  
**2.2.** The gopis (?) danced — [this dance] is worthy of the name "rasa" (?).[^rasa-2]  
**2.3.** This lila of the rasa has no end; this is the rasa of the eternal abode.  
**2.4.** The eternal abode is Nilachala; here the rasa dance is performed.  
**2.5.** Radha and Krishna — in this united form; to adorn (?) Jagannatha,[^melabapu-5]  
**2.6.** the Swami arranged the gathering and gave it the name "rahasa".[^swami-6]  
**2.7.** Here are japa, austerity and yoga, the devotees' bhava and anuraga:  
**2.8.** all this he brought together and called the rahasa.  
**2.9.** The rahasa is nama-sankirtana; it is not different from the name.  
**2.10.** Here are jhanja cymbals, mridanga, gini, karatalas, and also the leuda (?).[^instruments-10]  
**2.11.** Krishna's rasa becomes song: the *Gita-govinda* and the *Karnamrita* resound (?).[^gitagovinda-11]  
**2.12.** Taking up southern padas together, they dance in rows (?) (??).[^dakshini-12]  
**2.13.** Amid the din of cymbals and mridanga they dance, circling (?) (??).[^shastale-13]  
**2.14.** Sky and earth become one; the earth melts, streams of water [flow].  
**2.15.** In the middle of the rasa ground shines a jewelled mandapa.  
**2.16.** In the middle of the mandapa is a throne; on it Radha and Krishna are seated.  
**2.17.** On all four sides (??) the sakhis stand calmly.[^karambhitare-17]  

## The Rahasa at Bata-Ganesha; King Chodaganga; the Coming of Jayadeva


**2.18.** The eight sakhis, renowned by name, are always on all sides.[^ashta-18]  
**2.19.** In the middle is the rasa grove: such is the scene of the rasa lila.  
**2.20.** From there the sound of the flute streams forth and enchants the gopis.  
**2.21.** On all sides the devotees skilfully arranged the rahasa.  
**2.22.** Near Bata-Ganesha they raised the mandapa of the rahasa.[^bataganesha-22]  
**2.23.** Day and night they performed the rasa there — a consolation for the devotees.  
**2.24.** The din of cymbals and mridanga — a deep, sweet (?) jubilation.  
**2.25.** Kanhai built it; the Swami gave initiation into the rahasa.[^kanhai-25]  
**2.26.** Balarama, Achyuta, Yashovanta and Ananta are renowned as the sakhas.[^sakha-26]  
**2.27.** Gopala Guru and Kanhai, gathering in the rasa mandapa,  
**2.28.** arrange sankirtana daily and notice neither night nor day.  
**2.29.** Here the Puranas are read; the Swami composes the *Bhagavata*.[^bhagavata-29]  
**2.30.** They hold discourses on truth — in the honey-stream of Krishna's lilas.  
**2.31.** Where did this rahasa come from? Who first arranged it?  
**2.32.** If you ask me — [I shall tell] what I heard from the mouths of sadhus.  
**2.33.** I shall expound a little here; hold no fault in your minds (?).  
**2.34.** There was formerly a king; in happiness he ruled his people.  
**2.35.** Renowned by the name Chudangadeva — brave, daring and valiant.[^chodaganga-35]  
**2.36.** He was the best of devotees and meditated on Sri Jagannatha.  
**2.37.** He renovated the dilapidated temple and left his fame on earth (??).  
**2.38.** He built the garbha-griha and spent much wealth.  
**2.39.** [Such is] the glory of Sri Jagannatha, [such] the bound of this best of kshetras.  
**2.40.** In his old age, establishing this glory, he made a vow and performed the consecration.  
**2.41.** For the time of the gumuti's consecration he makes his son crown prince:[^gumuti-41]  
**2.42.** "When the consecration of the gumuti takes place, I shall give the throne to Kamarnava."  
**2.43.** So he thinks in his mind — and he held a splendid festival.  
**2.44.** Having clay brought quickly from the bank of the Godavari river,  
**2.45.** he made mridangas from it, giving his mind to the rasa of kirtana.  
**2.46.** Jhanja and mridanga together with gini — thus he began the kirtana.  
**2.47.** The consecration of the gumuti took place; he made Kamarnava king.  
**2.48.** In kirtana, with a voice choked with feeling, he fixed his mind at Hari's feet.  
**2.49.** He appointed mahari dancers: Hari's lila became a performance.[^mahari-49]  
**2.50.** Inside the gumuti of the temple — the sound of the devadasis' dancing  
**2.51.** to the praises of Hari, to mridanga, jhanja and karatalas.  
**2.52.** The maharis danced — and this was called the rahasa dance.  
**2.53.** Seeing this, the sovereign Gangeshvara obtained satisfaction of mind.  
**2.54.** In time that king went to heaven, having established jewel-like (?) fame.  
**2.55.** Meanwhile — listen, people — according to the *Gita-govinda* (?)[^jayadeva-55]  
**2.56.** a brahmana named Jayadeva came to the best of kshetras.  
**2.57.** His village is the sasana of Kenduli (?), on the bank of the Prachi river.[^kenduli-57]  
**2.58.** Near Madhava at Niali he served with devotion in many ways.  

[^rahasa-1]: Rahasa (*rahāsa*): the Odia name for gatherings where the rasa-lila was enacted with song and dance; according to Madhava (2.9), it is nama-sankirtana. According to ch. 4 (pilot, 4.37), Jagannatha Dasa built a mandapa for the rahasa at Bata-Ganesha in Puri.
[^rasa-2]: The print has ରସ (*rasa*); OCR in vv. 2 and 4 reads ରାସ (*rāsa*, "the rasa dance"). A play on words is possible: *rāsa*, Krishna's dance with the gopis, and *rasa*, the taste of love. The form ଗୋପୀମାନନ୍ତ (*gopīmānanta*) is unclear.
[^melabapu-5]: "United form" (*meḷa bapu*): Radha and Krishna joined in one body. In the Odia tradition Jagannatha is understood in this way. For Gaudiya Vaishnavas the united form of Radha and Krishna is Sri Chaitanya (*Chaitanya-charitamrita*, Adi 1.5), while Jagannatha is Krishna Himself. The syntax of the couplet and the word ସଜିବାକୁ (*sajibāku*, "to adorn"?) are read with uncertainty.
[^swami-6]: The Swami is apparently Jagannatha Dasa: so Chaitanya calls him in ch. 4 (pilot, 4.49).
[^instruments-10]: The jhanja is a pair of large cymbals; gini are small cymbals; karatalas are hand cymbals; ଲେଉଡ଼ା (*leuṛā?*) is an instrument whose reading and meaning are unclear.
[^gitagovinda-11]: Jayadeva's *Gita-govinda* and Bilvamangala's *Sri Krishna-karnamrita*. According to the *Chaitanya-charitamrita* (Madhya 2), in Puri Mahaprabhu relished these works day and night with Svarupa Damodara and Ramananda Raya. The last word (ଚଳେଇ, *caḷei*?) is read with uncertainty.
[^dakshini-12]: "Southern padas": songs in the southern style; the *Krishna-karnamrita* comes from the south (according to the CC, Madhya 9, Mahaprabhu brought it back from His pilgrimage in South India). The end of the half-line is not read: ପଙ୍କ୍ତି ପ୍ରାସ୍ତେ (*paṅkti prāste?*).
[^shastale-13]: The last word is unidentified: the print has approximately ଶସ୍ତଳେ (*śastaḷe?*); possibly ରସସ୍ଥଳେ ("on the rasa ground", cf. 2.15).
[^karambhitare-17]: The word ≈ କରମ୍ଭିତରେ (*karambhitare?*) is unidentified; the sense of the couplet is clear: the sakhis stand around the throne.
[^ashta-18]: The eight principal sakhis (companions) of Sri Radha; cf. v. 2.17.
[^bataganesha-22]: Bata-Ganesha is Ganesha under the banyan (bata) in the precincts of the Jagannatha temple in Puri; according to ch. 4 (pilot, 4.36–37), Jagannatha Dasa sat there and the rahasa mandapa stood there.
[^kanhai-25]: Kanhai is possibly Kanai Khuntia, an Odia Vaishnava poet of the Panchasakha circle; Gopala Guru (2.27) is unidentified. The "Swami" is Jagannatha Dasa (cf. 2.6).
[^sakha-26]: The Panchasakha ("five friends"): Balarama Dasa, Jagannatha Dasa (the Swami), Achyutananda, Ananta and Yashovanta; cf. 1.76.
[^bhagavata-29]: The *Bhagavata* of Jagannatha Dasa is the Odia rendering of the *Srimad-Bhagavatam* (16th c.), the most revered work of Odia Vaishnava literature; cf. pilot, 4.38–39.
[^chodaganga-35]: Chudangadeva (Gangeshvara, 2.53) is apparently Anantavarman Chodagangadeva (reigned c. 1078–1147), the Eastern Ganga king under whom the building of the present Jagannatha temple in Puri was begun. Below is Madhava's account of how the rahasa grew out of the temple dances under this king.
[^gumuti-41]: Gumuti: the dome, the tower of the temple. Kamarnava is Chodaganga's son and heir.
[^mahari-49]: The maharis are the temple dancers (devadasis) of the Jagannatha temple, who danced before the Lord during the services; later they also performed the songs of the *Gita-govinda*.
[^jayadeva-55]: Jayadeva is the author of the *Gita-govinda* (12th c.), which Mahaprabhu relished in Puri (CC, Madhya 2). The word ପରିମାଣେ (*parimāṇe*, "by the measure, according to"?) is understood with uncertainty: perhaps "as the *Gita-govinda* attests".
[^kenduli-57]: The print has ଦେନ୍ଦୁଳି (*denduḷi*); taken as Kenduli, Jayadeva's birthplace. The Odia tradition places Kenduli Sasana on the Prachi river, near the Madhava temple at Niali; the Bengali tradition places it in Birbhum.
