# Madhava Pattanayaka. *Vaishnava-lilamrita*, Chapter 2
## The Rahasa in Nilachala

*Translated from Odia without a native speaker; doubtful places are marked (?) and (??). The original, transliteration and word-for-word translation are in `full/02.md`.*

**2.1.** Giving my heart to Krishna's feet, I shall describe a little of the rahasa.[^rahasa-1]  
**2.2.** The gopis (?) danced — [this dance] is worthy of the name "rasa" (?).[^rasa-2]  
**2.3.** This lila of the rasa has no end; this is the rasa of the eternal abode.  
**2.4.** The eternal abode is Nilachala; here the rasa dance is performed.  
**2.5.** Radha and Krishna — in this united form; to adorn (?) Jagannatha,[^melabapu-5]  
**2.6.** the Swami arranged the gathering and gave it the name "rahasa".[^swami-6]  
**2.7.** Here are japa, austerity and yoga, the devotees' bhava and anuraga:  
**2.8.** all this he brought together and called the rahasa.  
**2.9.** The rahasa is nama-sankirtana; it is not different from the name.  
**2.10.** Here are jhanja cymbals, mridanga, gini, karatalas, and also the leuda (?).[^instruments-10]  
**2.11.** Krishna's rasa becomes song: the *Gita-govinda* and the *Karnamrita* resound (?).[^gitagovinda-11]  
**2.12.** Taking up southern padas together, they dance in rows (?) (??).[^dakshini-12]  
**2.13.** Amid the din of cymbals and mridanga they dance, circling (?) (??).[^shastale-13]  
**2.14.** Sky and earth become one; the earth melts, streams of water [flow].  
**2.15.** In the middle of the rasa ground shines a jewelled mandapa.  
**2.16.** In the middle of the mandapa is a throne; on it Radha and Krishna are seated.  
**2.17.** On all four sides (??) the sakhis stand calmly.[^karambhitare-17]  

## The Rahasa at Bata-Ganesha; King Chodaganga; the Coming of Jayadeva


**2.18.** The eight sakhis, renowned by name, are always on all sides.[^ashta-18]  
**2.19.** In the middle is the rasa grove: such is the scene of the rasa lila.  
**2.20.** From there the sound of the flute streams forth and enchants the gopis.  
**2.21.** On all sides the devotees skilfully arranged the rahasa.  
**2.22.** Near Bata-Ganesha they raised the mandapa of the rahasa.[^bataganesha-22]  
**2.23.** Day and night they performed the rasa there — a consolation for the devotees.  
**2.24.** The din of cymbals and mridanga — a deep, sweet (?) jubilation.  
**2.25.** Kanhai built it; the Swami gave initiation into the rahasa.[^kanhai-25]  
**2.26.** Balarama, Achyuta, Yashovanta and Ananta are renowned as the sakhas.[^sakha-26]  
**2.27.** Gopala Guru and Kanhai, gathering in the rasa mandapa,  
**2.28.** arrange sankirtana daily and notice neither night nor day.  
**2.29.** Here the Puranas are read; the Swami composes the *Bhagavata*.[^bhagavata-29]  
**2.30.** They hold discourses on truth — in the honey-stream of Krishna's lilas.  
**2.31.** Where did this rahasa come from? Who first arranged it?  
**2.32.** If you ask me — [I shall tell] what I heard from the mouths of sadhus.  
**2.33.** I shall expound a little here; hold no fault in your minds (?).  
**2.34.** There was formerly a king; in happiness he ruled his people.  
**2.35.** Renowned by the name Chudangadeva — brave, daring and valiant.[^chodaganga-35]  
**2.36.** He was the best of devotees and meditated on Sri Jagannatha.  
**2.37.** He renovated the dilapidated temple and left his fame on earth (??).  
**2.38.** He built the garbha-griha and spent much wealth.  
**2.39.** [Such is] the glory of Sri Jagannatha, [such] the bound of this best of kshetras.  
**2.40.** In his old age, establishing this glory, he made a vow and performed the consecration.  
**2.41.** For the time of the gumuti's consecration he makes his son crown prince:[^gumuti-41]  
**2.42.** "When the consecration of the gumuti takes place, I shall give the throne to Kamarnava."  
**2.43.** So he thinks in his mind — and he held a splendid festival.  
**2.44.** Having clay brought quickly from the bank of the Godavari river,  
**2.45.** he made mridangas from it, giving his mind to the rasa of kirtana.  
**2.46.** Jhanja and mridanga together with gini — thus he began the kirtana.  
**2.47.** The consecration of the gumuti took place; he made Kamarnava king.  
**2.48.** In kirtana, with a voice choked with feeling, he fixed his mind at Hari's feet.  
**2.49.** He appointed mahari dancers: Hari's lila became a performance.[^mahari-49]  
**2.50.** Inside the gumuti of the temple — the sound of the devadasis' dancing  
**2.51.** to the praises of Hari, to mridanga, jhanja and karatalas.  
**2.52.** The maharis danced — and this was called the rahasa dance.  
**2.53.** Seeing this, the sovereign Gangeshvara obtained satisfaction of mind.  
**2.54.** In time that king went to heaven, having established jewel-like (?) fame.  
**2.55.** Meanwhile — listen, people — according to the *Gita-govinda* (?)[^jayadeva-55]  
**2.56.** a brahmana named Jayadeva came to the best of kshetras.  
**2.57.** His village is the sasana of Kenduli (?), on the bank of the Prachi river.[^kenduli-57]  
**2.58.** Near Madhava at Niali he served with devotion in many ways.  

## Jayadeva and the *Gita-govinda* in the Temple; King Bhimadeva; Sridhara Svami


**2.59.** Learned in the shastras and Puranas, his mind was on the path of bhakti.  
**2.60.** "I shall compose a song," he said, and resolved to live in the Kshetra.  
**2.61.** Near Sri Jagannatha, with a pure, sattvic mind and feeling (?),  
**2.62.** he composed the *Sri Gita-govinda* and obtained supreme bliss.[^gitagovinda-62]  
**2.63.** He sang the lila of Sri Radha and Madhava in a sweet voice.  
**2.64.** His wife Padmavati dances (?) to the rhythm of the song.[^padmavati-64]  
**2.65.** Skilfully she performed the rahasa dance — that dancer gave the performance.  
**2.66.** The devotees exclaimed, "Blessed, blessed!" — and in devotional feeling forgot themselves.  
**2.67.** "Let the dance-service of the *Gita-govinda* take place in the temple daily" —  
**2.68.** [so] that king instituted this service and supplied saris from Kenduli.[^kenduli-68]  
**2.69.** "The rasa of this *Sri Gita-govinda* is dear to Sri Jagannatha" —  
**2.70.** saying so, he made this arrangement; the devotees were content.  
**2.71.** In time that poet went to heaven: his passing took place in the best of kshetras.[^jayadeva-71]  
**2.72.** Then, for the *Gita-govinda* dance, he built a natamandira.[^natamandira-72]  
**2.73.** For the rahasa dance [the dancers] danced the *Gita-govinda*.  
**2.74.** Such is the deed (?) of Bhimadeva; in time he departed, having established [his] fame.[^bhimadeva-74]  
**2.75.** Meanwhile, hear the rasa — the rasa of the *Sri Bhagavad-gita* (?).[^gita-75]  
**2.76.** The best of brahmanas, named Sridhara — he was born in the village of Marei.[^sridhara-76]  
**2.77.** He composed the *Bhagavata-dipika*; his view is on the path of devotion.[^dipika-77]  
**2.78.** Living at Remuna-Kataka, he serves the murti of Gopinatha.[^remuna-78]  
**2.79.** He reads the *Gopala-tapani*, expounding [it] according to Paippalada (??).[^tapani-79]  
**2.80.** His bhava on the path of devotion wins over all people.  
**2.81.** Having taken initiation in Sri Kshetra, he follows the Advaita doctrine.[^advaita-81]  
**2.82.** He is a sannyasi of Shankara['s order]; [the second half is unclear] (??).[^bhabanai-82]  
**2.83.** His view concerns the meaning of the *Bhagavata*; he expounded (?) the shastra from many viewpoints.  
**2.84.** The path of devotion, karma and jnana — all these are not separate from one another.  
**2.85.** Bhakti exists even apart from (??) jnana; but without (?) bhakti, where is jnana?[^bhakti-85]  

## Sridhara Swami; the Meghanada Wall; Purushottama-deva: Chhera-panhara and the Kanchi Campaign


**2.86.** Contemplate [the Lord] in the form of the *Bhagavata*, and you will take refuge in Sri Krishna.[^bhagavata-86]  
**2.87.** Sridhara expounded the *Bhagavata*, and all the people listened to it.[^sridhara-87]  
**2.88.** The pandits of Kashi, Navadvipa and Srirangam gathered and accepted [his exposition].  
**2.89.** Uniting knowledge with bhakti, this [exposition will remain] there until … (??).[^kasa-89]  
**2.90.** The sannyasis understood its meaning and praised Sridhara: "Blessed, blessed!"  
**2.91.** They had all the manuscripts copied and took them away, together with the *Bhavartha-dipika*.  
**2.92.** He spread the mood of bhakti and bade farewell to [dry] knowledge.[^jalanjali-92]  
**2.93.** Whoever walks the path of bhakti surely attains Krishna.  
**2.94.** Without (?) Krishna [a person] falls into hell — thus the meaning of the *Bhagavata* unfolds.[^bihune-94]  
**2.95.** Rapt (?) in nama-kirtana, he spends the nights without sleep.  
**2.96.** Chanting the king of mantras of Nrisimha and serving (?) Shiva, he obtains grace.[^nrsimha-96]  
**2.97.** The Swami lived at Kapilas; he was an eminent mahant.[^kapilas-97]  
**2.98.** Head of the Brahmachari matha (??) — his name was Sridhara Swami-pada.[^matha-98]  
**2.99.** The worship of Madhava is his root; his mind rests firmly on Umadhava.[^umadhava-99]  
**2.100.** Learning of Sridhara's rare life, Pratapa Bhanudeva [honoured him]:[^bhanudeva-100]  
**2.101.** Sridhara performed divine worship; [he was given] the right of the garland and the service of the Sri Murti.[^malya-101]  
**2.102.** Listen, devotees, to another tale: I shall describe it in a little more detail.  
**2.103.** The rahasa became a performance, rapt (?) in the mood (?) of the *Gita-govinda*.[^rahasa-103]  
**2.104.** A dog appeared there; seeing it, all the people were distressed.  
**2.105.** They went and reported to the king: "A dog has entered," they said.  
**2.106.** "[The temple] is open on four sides: put up a wall quickly!"  
**2.107.** The king said, "Good, the wall will be laid" — so he said.  
**2.108.** The Meghanada wall was laid; in a year it was completed.[^meghanada-108]  
**2.109.** The drama *Parashurama-vijaya* was presented in the temple.[^parashurama-109]  
**2.110.** Seeing it, all were delighted; the king especially honoured [them] with words.  
**2.111.** "This is the glory of Kapileshvara-deva!" — they proclaimed day and night.  
**2.112.** Of his loins was born the good Purushottama-deva.[^purushottama-112]  
**2.113.** The Bhoga-mandapa is his glory; the rahasa dance is his design (?).  
**2.114.** The temple servants said: "You are great — [yet] you are the servant of the Lord.  
**2.115.** Perform service to the Lord; the great service — give up (?) [to others?].[^parihara-115]  
**2.116.** In the great service there is the broom: ply it yourself."  
**2.117.** This service at the time of the Ratha the Lord's servant performs.  
**2.118.** Beforehand (?) they sprinkled water with camphor, sandal and aguru.  
**2.119.** The king, holding a golden broom, goes about the chariots.  
**2.120.** Seeing this, the servants praised him: "Know: this is the servant of the Lord!"  
**2.121.** Its name is chhera-panhara, an institution (?) of Purushottama.[^chhera-121]  
**2.122.** He gave a troupe (?) of dancing girls: "Perform the rahasa!" he said.[^nachuni-122]  
**2.123.** Constantly he pleased the hearts of Jagannatha and Baladeva.  
**2.124.** Having become king (?), he waged war with Saluva Narasimha.[^kanchi-124]  
**2.125.** He brought back his daughter by abduction (?); the marriage (?) took place with honour.  
**2.126.** The servants cried "Blessed!" and said, "This is the Lord's victory."  
**2.127.** For Jaga and Balia both, mounted on a black and a white horse,[^jaga-127]  
**2.128.** went, it is said, to the battlefield and fought (?) as the king's soldiers.  
**2.129.** Therefore he won the battle and married the beautiful one.  
**2.130.** Would he have obtained such honours without Jagannatha's grace?  
**2.131.** So says Madhava-dasa, setting his hope on Krishna's feet.[^colophon-131]  

[^rahasa-1]: Rahasa (*rahāsa*): the Odia name for gatherings where the rasa-lila was enacted with song and dance; according to Madhava (2.9), it is nama-sankirtana. According to ch. 4 (pilot, 4.37), Jagannatha Dasa built a mandapa for the rahasa at Bata-Ganesha in Puri.
[^rasa-2]: The print has ରସ (*rasa*); OCR in vv. 2 and 4 reads ରାସ (*rāsa*, "the rasa dance"). A play on words is possible: *rāsa*, Krishna's dance with the gopis, and *rasa*, the taste of love. The form ଗୋପୀମାନନ୍ତ (*gopīmānanta*) is unclear.
[^melabapu-5]: "United form" (*meḷa bapu*): Radha and Krishna joined in one body. In the Odia tradition Jagannatha is understood in this way. For Gaudiya Vaishnavas the united form of Radha and Krishna is Sri Chaitanya (*Chaitanya-charitamrita*, Adi 1.5), while Jagannatha is Krishna Himself. The syntax of the couplet and the word ସଜିବାକୁ (*sajibāku*, "to adorn"?) are read with uncertainty.
[^swami-6]: The Swami is apparently Jagannatha Dasa: so Chaitanya calls him in ch. 4 (pilot, 4.49).
[^instruments-10]: The jhanja is a pair of large cymbals; gini are small cymbals; karatalas are hand cymbals; ଲେଉଡ଼ା (*leuṛā?*) is an instrument whose reading and meaning are unclear.
[^gitagovinda-11]: Jayadeva's *Gita-govinda* and Bilvamangala's *Sri Krishna-karnamrita*. According to the *Chaitanya-charitamrita* (Madhya 2), in Puri Mahaprabhu relished these works day and night with Svarupa Damodara and Ramananda Raya. The last word (ଚଳେଇ, *caḷei*?) is read with uncertainty.
[^dakshini-12]: "Southern padas": songs in the southern style; the *Krishna-karnamrita* comes from the south (according to the CC, Madhya 9, Mahaprabhu brought it back from His pilgrimage in South India). The end of the half-line is not read: ପଙ୍କ୍ତି ପ୍ରାସ୍ତେ (*paṅkti prāste?*).
[^shastale-13]: The last word is unidentified: the print has approximately ଶସ୍ତଳେ (*śastaḷe?*); possibly ରସସ୍ଥଳେ ("on the rasa ground", cf. 2.15).
[^karambhitare-17]: The word ≈ କରମ୍ଭିତରେ (*karambhitare?*) is unidentified; the sense of the couplet is clear: the sakhis stand around the throne.
[^ashta-18]: The eight principal sakhis (companions) of Sri Radha; cf. v. 2.17.
[^bataganesha-22]: Bata-Ganesha is Ganesha under the banyan (bata) in the precincts of the Jagannatha temple in Puri; according to ch. 4 (pilot, 4.36–37), Jagannatha Dasa sat there and the rahasa mandapa stood there.
[^kanhai-25]: Kanhai is possibly Kanai Khuntia, an Odia Vaishnava poet of the Panchasakha circle; Gopala Guru (2.27) is unidentified. The "Swami" is Jagannatha Dasa (cf. 2.6).
[^sakha-26]: The Panchasakha ("five friends"): Balarama Dasa, Jagannatha Dasa (the Swami), Achyutananda, Ananta and Yashovanta; cf. 1.76.
[^bhagavata-29]: The *Bhagavata* of Jagannatha Dasa is the Odia rendering of the *Srimad-Bhagavatam* (16th c.), the most revered work of Odia Vaishnava literature; cf. pilot, 4.38–39.
[^chodaganga-35]: Chudangadeva (Gangeshvara, 2.53) is apparently Anantavarman Chodagangadeva (reigned c. 1078–1147), the Eastern Ganga king under whom the building of the present Jagannatha temple in Puri was begun. Below is Madhava's account of how the rahasa grew out of the temple dances under this king.
[^gumuti-41]: Gumuti: the dome, the tower of the temple. Kamarnava is Chodaganga's son and heir.
[^mahari-49]: The maharis are the temple dancers (devadasis) of the Jagannatha temple, who danced before the Lord during the services; later they also performed the songs of the *Gita-govinda*.
[^jayadeva-55]: Jayadeva is the author of the *Gita-govinda* (12th c.), which Mahaprabhu relished in Puri (CC, Madhya 2). The word ପରିମାଣେ (*parimāṇe*, "by the measure, according to"?) is understood with uncertainty: perhaps "as the *Gita-govinda* attests".
[^kenduli-57]: The print has ଦେନ୍ଦୁଳି (*denduḷi*); taken as Kenduli, Jayadeva's birthplace. The Odia tradition places Kenduli Sasana on the Prachi river, near the Madhava temple at Niali; the Bengali tradition places it in Birbhum.
[^gitagovinda-62]: The *Gita-govinda* of Jayadeva is a Sanskrit poem on the loving pastimes of Radha and Krishna. In Puri Mahaprabhu relished its songs day and night with Svarupa Damodara and Ramananda Raya (CC, Madhya 2). According to Madhava, the poem was composed in Puri, near Jagannatha.
[^padmavati-64]: Padmavati is Jayadeva's wife; Jayadeva mentions her at the opening of the *Gita-govinda*. The Odia tradition regards her as a temple dancer of Jagannatha. The print has ନୃତ୍ୟତ (*nṛtyata*); the rhyme with ପଦ୍ମାବତୀ requires ନୃତ୍ୟତି (*nṛtyati*, "dances").
[^kenduli-68]: Saris (cloth) from Kenduli, Jayadeva's home village (cf. 2.57); probably connected with the later custom of offering Jagannatha cloth woven with verses of the *Gita-govinda*. Madhava apparently ascribes the institution of the daily singing and dancing of the *Gita-govinda* in the temple to King Bhimadeva (2.74).
[^jayadeva-71]: The Bengali traditions do not speak of Jayadeva's passing in Puri; this is the Odia tradition.
[^natamandira-72]: The natamandira is the dance hall of the Jagannatha temple, in front of the jagamohana.
[^bhimadeva-74]: Bhimadeva is apparently Anangabhimadeva III (reigned c. 1211–1238) of the Eastern Ganga dynasty, to whom the temple's natamandira and the introduction of the *Gita-govinda* into the temple service are ascribed. The first letter of the word ଚରିତ ("deed") is printed like ଜ.
[^gita-75]: The print has ଶ୍ରୀଭଗବତ ଗୀତ (*śrībhagabata gīta*): literally "the song of Sri Bhagavan", i.e. the *Bhagavad-gita* (Sridhara Svami also wrote a commentary on it). The reading "the *Bhagavata* [and] the *Gita*" is also possible.
[^sridhara-76]: Sridhara Svami is the author of the *Bhavartha-dipika*, the commentary on the *Srimad-Bhagavatam*. Mahaprabhu honoured his commentary as authoritative and sternly rejected Vallabha Bhatta's attempt to refute it (CC, Antya 7). Marei is, by Odia tradition, a village near Remuna.
[^dipika-77]: The *Bhagavata-dipika* is apparently the *Bhavartha-dipika*.
[^remuna-78]: Remuna (near Balasore) is the site of the temple of Kshira-chora Gopinatha, where Madhavendra Puri served and which Mahaprabhu visited (CC, Madhya 4). Kataka means "camp, town"; ‑ଣ (*‑eṇa*) is an old locative ending (cf. pilot, ଅଙ୍ଗେଣ, କୂଳେଣ).
[^tapani-79]: The *Gopala-tapani* is an Upanishad of the *Atharva-veda* on Krishna as Gopala, on which the Gaudiya Vaishnavas rely (it is quoted in CC). Paippalada is a branch of the *Atharva-veda*; the Upanishad itself opens with the questions of sages. The print has ପଇପଲ ସରି (??) (*païpala sari*); the second word is unclear (the sign looks like ଶି/ଣି, with a stroke under ସ).
[^advaita-81]: According to the Odia tradition, Sridhara was a sannyasi of Shankara's order and a follower of Advaita (the Govardhana Matha of that order is in Puri). The Gaudiya Vaishnavas, following Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati, regard Sridhara Svami as a Vaishnava associated with the Vishnusvami line; his commentary, despite some Advaita-style expressions, places bhakti above all (cf. 2.85).
[^bhabanai-82]: The print has ପୂରଣ କରେ ?ବାନଈ (*pūraṇa kare ?abānaī*); the first letter of the last word looks like ତ/ଥ/ଉ. If read ଭବାନଈ = ଭବାନୀ, the sense may be "fulfils [a vow to?] Bhavani" or "Bhavani fulfils [his wishes]" (??); unidentified.
[^bhakti-85]: The print has ଜ୍ଞାନ ବ୍ୟସିକେ (??) (*jñāna byasike*) — from the sense of the second half, "apart from, without jnana" (?); ଭକ୍ତି ବିନେ (?) — the sign of the second word looks like ରି/ଶି, taken as ବିନେ, "without". The superiority of bhakti over jnana is the main thought of Sridhara's commentary, especially valued by the Gaudiya Vaishnavas.
[^bhagavata-86]: The account of Sridhara Swami continues (from 2.76). The speaker is not marked: perhaps this sets out Sridhara's teaching. Cf. the Gaudiya Vaishnava view: the *Srimad-Bhagavatam* is Krishna's incarnation in word, a sun that rose after His disappearance (Bhag. 1.3.43).
[^sridhara-87]: Sridhara Swami is the author of the *Bhavartha-dipika* commentary on the *Srimad-Bhagavatam* (2.91). Mahaprabhu held his exposition to be authoritative (CC, Antya 7, the episode with Vallabha Bhatta). The "Swami" in this account is Sridhara, not Jagannatha Dasa, whom Madhava calls "the Swami" in 2.25 and 2.29.
[^kasa-89]: The print has ≈ କସ (*kasa*): the letter after କ resembles ସ/ଯ. Possibly କଳ୍ପ (*kaḷpa*), "until the end of the kalpa", but the print does not show this.
[^jalanjali-92]: Jalanjali is the offering of water to the departed; "to give jalanjali to knowledge" means to part with it for good. Cf. the verse *jñāne prayāsam udapāsya…* (Bhag. 10.14.3) in the talks with Ramananda Raya (CC, Madhya 8): giving up endeavour for knowledge in favour of hearing about the Lord.
[^bihune-94]: The print has ବହୁନେ (*bahune*); taken as ବିହୁନେ (*bihune*), "without" (the "ି" sign is often lost in this font). The meaning of ବାଢ଼େ ("grows", "unfolds") in the second half-line is uncertain.
[^nrsimha-96]: The *Bhavartha-dipika* opens with obeisances to Nrisimha. The service of Shiva is apparently at the shrine on Kapilas hill (2.97). The print has ସେବ (*seba*), taken as the participle ସେବି ("serving"), as the rhyme with ଜପି requires.
[^kapilas-97]: Kapilas is a hill near Dhenkanal (Odisha) with a shrine of Shiva (Chandrasekhara). A mahant is the abbot of a matha.
[^matha-98]: The first word is uncertain: after ବ୍ରହ୍ମ the print has two signs resembling ସ୍ୱା/ହ; read as ବ୍ରହ୍ମଚାରୀ (*brahmacārī*). "Pada" is an honorific added to the name.
[^umadhava-99]: Madhava is a name of Vishnu; Umadhava, "Uma's lord", is Shiva. Here, as in 2.81–82 (Sridhara follows the advaita teaching and is a sannyasi of Shankara's tradition), Madhava's Odia tradition diverges from the Gaudiya Vaishnava one: according to Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati, Sridhara Swami was a Vaishnava following Vishnusvami, not a mayavadi; Mahaprabhu accepted his exposition of the *Bhagavatam* as true (CC, Antya 7).
[^bhanudeva-100]: Pratapa Bhanudeva is apparently Bhanudeva III of the Eastern Ganga dynasty (reigned c. 1352–1378).
[^malya-101]: Malyadhikara is the right to receive a garland taken from the Lord (a prasadi garland), a high honour in the Jagannatha temple. The syntax of 2.100–101 is understood from the sense: the king granted Sridhara this right and the service of the Sri Murti.
[^rahasa-103]: The rahasa is a performance of Krishna's lilas, here in the temple, to the songs of the *Gita-govinda* (cf. 2.49–55). There is a dot above ଭାବେ (a chandrabindu or a blot); the last word is ≈ ଭୋଳା (*bhoḷā*, "rapt"), possibly ଖେଳା (*kheḷā*, "play"). A dog entering the temple is a defilement that requires purification.
[^meghanada-108]: The Meghanada pacheri is the outer wall of the Jagannatha temple in Puri; tradition ascribes it to Kapilendra-deva (Kapileshvara, 2.111; reigned c. 1435–1467), founder of the Suryavamsha (Gajapati) dynasty.
[^parashurama-109]: *Parashurama-vijaya* is a Sanskrit drama ascribed to Kapilendra-deva himself.
[^purushottama-112]: Purushottama-deva (reigned c. 1467–1497) was Kapilendra's son and the father of Prataparudra (Rudradeva); the building of the temple's Bhoga-mandapa is ascribed to him.
[^parihara-115]: The print has ପରିହର (*parihara*), "give up, reject", which contradicts 2.116 ("ply it yourself"). The sense expects ପରିଚର (*paricara*), "perform"; or else: "give up [the pride of royal greatness]".
[^chhera-121]: Chhera-panhara is the sweeping of the chariots by the king with a golden broom and their sprinkling with sandal water before the Ratha-yatra. CC (Madhya 13) describes Prataparudra performing this service and Mahaprabhu being pleased by his humility. The meaning of ଉଆଣ (*uāṇa*) here and in 2.70, 2.72 is unclear; taken as "institution".
[^nachuni-122]: Nachunis are temple dancers (maharis, cf. 2.49). ସଂପରଦା (*saṁparadā*) is taken as "troupe" or "grant" (Skt. *samprada*?).
[^kanchi-124]: The Odia legend of Purushottama-deva's campaign against Kanchi (Kanchi-Kaveri): the king of Kanchi insulted him as a "sweeper" because of the chhera-panhara; Jagannatha and Baladeva rode ahead of the army on a black and a white horse, and the king won and took the princess as his wife (Padmavati, according to the legend). Madhava names the opponent Saluva Narasimha, a ruler of Vijayanagara in the second half of the 15th century.
[^jaga-127]: Jaga and Balia are affectionate Odia names of Jagannatha and Baladeva (Balabhadra).
[^colophon-131]: The closing formula with the author's name. Chapter 2 ends with this verse; Chapter 3 ("ତୃତୀୟ ଅଧ୍ୟାୟ") begins on leaf 57.
