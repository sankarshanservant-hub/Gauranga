# Madhava Pattanayaka. *Vaishnava-lilamrita*, Chapter 1 (vv. 1–29)
## Introduction: the Glory of Jagannatha and Nilachala

*Translated from Odia without a native speaker; doubtful places are marked (?) and (??). The original, transliteration and word-for-word translation are in `VL-01-1-29-full.md`.*[^intro-1]

*Obeisance to Sri Ganesha!*  
*May there be no obstacles!*

**1.1.** O Jagannatha, sat-cit-ananda! Your name is all the goals of human life.[^purushartha-1]  
**1.2.** You made all the tirthas one; You assumed the fourfold form.[^chaturdha-2]  
**1.3.** The name "Sri Hare Rama Krishna" is hidden (?) in Your body.[^mantra-3]  
**1.4.** The universe abides in Your belly; You are the God unattainable [even] by the shastras.  
**1.5.** All the tirthas are at Your feet, all the gods are in Your name.  
**1.6.** You, O God, are the refuge of the gods; what can I tell of Your purpose (?)?[^mati-6]  
**1.7.** You are the eternal, everlasting God; in Your body is the pure pranava.[^pranava-7]  
**1.8.** You are the eternal Truth, Parabrahman; in Your body all abodes are gathered.  
**1.9.** (??) … nigama …; Your name, protecting (?) living beings, is their support.[^nigama-9]  
**1.10.** O Lord of the world, Jagannatha! Apart from You (?) there is no other path.  
**1.11.** By constantly serving Your feet, living beings cross the waters of existence.  
**1.12.** You deliver the fallen — who can describe Your limit?  
**1.13.** Infinite, changeless, imperishable! Your name is the sum, full of Shunya.[^shunya-13]  
**1.14.** From Your name the pranava arose; from Your name the Rigveda (?) was born.  
**1.15.** Your form is full of condensed rasa; it is fearlessness for the people of the world.[^rasaghana-15]  
**1.16.** Give me shelter at Your feet, so that no sorrow remains (??) …[^atramite-16]  
**1.17.** In Your body is the lila of Radha and Krishna; this is the eternal abode, this the eternal play.[^rk-17]  
**1.18.** Nilachala is the eternal abode, pure, sattvic, incomparable.[^nilachala-18]  
**1.19.** Nilachala is the eternal Goloka; here is the eternal play of Krishna.  
**1.20.** This lila is eternal and pure (?); it is manifest in Nilachala.  
**1.21.** If one takes refuge in the eternal abode, no darkness remains for a living being.  
**1.22.** Taking refuge in Nilachala, living beings have crossed over samsara.  
**1.23.** In every age the devotees of Vishnu took refuge in Your abode and were born [there] (?).[^janamale-23]  
**1.24.** The faith of the devotees is at Your feet; there their resting place is gathered.  
**1.25.** When [You] grant living beings liberation, [they] are settled in the circle (?) of Goloka.[^mukti-25]  
**1.26.** Who would ask You for liberation? You (?) are the refuge of those who have no refuge.  
**1.27.** Whoever worships on the path of devotees obtains shelter at Your feet.  
**1.28.** You are the faith of the devotees; who can tell Your beginning and end?  
**1.29.** Devotees, gathering here, worshipped Your name and went away (?).[^chali-29]

**1.30.** Whatever hosts of devotees there are — who can describe their glory?[^thoke-30]  
**1.31.** Those who lived taking refuge at Your feet, and those who came to Your feet;  
**1.32.** those who lived in Your abode with devoted minds, in the kirtan of the name;  
**1.33.** those Vaishnavas, great ascetics — who can describe their lilas?  
**1.34.** The lilas that all the Vaishnavas of Nilachala brought about —  
**1.35.** the wondrous deeds of the Vaishnavas — who is able to hear them?  
**1.36.** Who can tell the deeds of the Vaishnavas? They are truly amrita.  
**1.37.** A little shall I describe, after reflection (?), meditating on the feet of Sri Jagannatha.  
**1.38.** I shall make my life blessed; blessed [will be] my birth and my deeds.  
**1.39.** Good people, listen and gladden your hearts: rare are the deeds of the Vaishnavas.  
**1.40.** If you would cross over samsara, listen to this profound (?) account.  
**1.41.** It was not (?) revealed before; now I (?), the servant, shall describe it.  
**1.42.** The Vaishnavas gathered here, making the name of Sri Krishna their raft.[^bhela-42]  
**1.43.** They expounded the rare truth and established the devotees in the name.  
**1.44.** Fixing their minds on Sri Radha and Krishna, they utter the name and lose themselves.[^bhola-44]  
**1.45.** Maddened by the name, they know neither night nor day.  
**1.46.** Contemplating in their hearts the Lord of Radha — Sri Jagannatha, the Lord of the world —  
**1.47.** they performed here the great lila, the great play in mahabhava.[^mahabhava-47]  
**1.48.** Rare are the deeds of the Vaishnavas; my mind will describe a little.  
**1.49.** Listen, good people, attentively: [this] will establish (?) glory in the world.  
**1.50.** Rare is the company of sadhus — where can one find it now (?)?  
**1.51.** Rare are these deeds of the sadhus, manifested in Nilachala.  
**1.52.** I shall tell a little of what is dear to me: it will destroy the torments and misfortunes of existence.  
**1.53.** May the dust of the Vaishnavas' feet fall unceasingly on my head.  
**1.54.** I am the lowly, wretched Madhava; my birth is in a shudra family.[^shudra-54]  
**1.55.** My father's name is Bhagavan; my mother, know, is Hiradei (?).[^parents-55]  
**1.56.** Born in the Karana lineage, I bear the title Pattanayaka.[^karana-56]  
**1.57.** Khandapada is my former home; I set my mind on living in Sri Kshetra.[^khandapada-57]  
**1.58.** My guru is Gadadhara Pandita; he showed [me] boundless mercy.[^gadadhara-58]  
**1.59.** He gave [me] initiation in the Gopala mantra and delivered [me], a foolish soul.[^gopala-59]  
**1.60.** I am the disciple of Gadadhara; my faith is in the devotees.  
**1.61.** Constantly serving the feet of Sri Guru, I obtained knowledge and bhakti, surrendering [to him].  
**1.62.** I made service to the Vaishnavas my root, I gave my heart at the Vaishnavas' feet.  
**1.63.** My root is the company of Vaishnavas; I shall tell of the Vaishnavas.  
**1.64.** Those who are devoted in the mood of the gopis, let them keep (?) [this] in their hearts.[^gopi-64]  
**1.65.** All that I have heard of the former deeds of Vishnu's devotees I shall describe.  
**1.66.** All the sights I witnessed with my own eyes — how could I leave (??) [them]?[^tyajai-66]  
**1.67.** The Vaishnava Ramananda Raya is wise, knowledgeable and learned.[^ramananda-67]  
**1.68.** Rama Raya is my provider, a knower of the truth of the devotees' rasa.

[^intro-1]: Madhava Pattanayaka, *Vaishnava-lilamrita* (c. 1535), ch. 1, vv. 1–29; text after the edition of B. C. Acharya (Sambalpur, 1986), scan leaves 45–46. The chapter opens with a mangalacharana, a eulogy of Jagannatha and Nilachala (Puri). The author was a disciple of Gadadhara Pandita who lived in Puri in Mahaprabhu's time.
[^purushartha-1]: Purushartha: the goals of human life, namely dharma, artha, kama and moksha. According to the *Chaitanya-charitamrita* (Adi 7), the highest, "fifth" goal is prema, love for Krishna.
[^chaturdha-2]: The fourfold form: Jagannatha, Baladeva (Balarama), Subhadra and Sudarshana, worshipped together in the temple at Puri.
[^mantra-3]: Apparently the Hare Krishna mahamantra (abbreviated by the author as "Sri Hare Rama Krishna nama"). The notion of the name "hidden" in Jagannatha's body belongs to the Odia Panchasakha tradition; it is not in the *Chaitanya-charitamrita*. For Gaudiya Vaishnavas the mahamantra is the sixteen names of the *Kali-santarana Upanishad*, and Jagannatha is Krishna Himself. The reading ଗୋପ୍ୟାନ (*gopyāna*, "secretly"?) is uncertain.
[^mati-6]: *Mati*: mind, intention, purpose; perhaps "nature". Two readings are conjectural: the print has କ (*ka*), probably କି (*ki*, "what") with the ି lost; ତୁର (*tura*) is taken as an archaic "Your".
[^pranava-7]: The pranava is the sacred syllable om. Odia Vaishnavas see the written form of om in Jagannatha's very form. According to the *Chaitanya-charitamrita* (Adi 7), the pranava is the sound incarnation of the Lord and the chief utterance of the Vedas.
[^nigama-9]: The first half-line is not read. The print has ନନ୍ଦ ନିଗମ ତନ୍ଦବାହା (*nanda nigama tandabāhā?*); the first and last signs match ନ୍ଦ in ସଚ୍ଚିଦାନନ୍ଦ (1.1), but the words are unidentified. Only "nigama" is clear: the Vedas, the scriptures. In the second half-line ରଖି (*rakhi*, "protecting") is conjectural.
[^shunya-13]: Shunya ("void") is, for the Odia poets of the Panchasakha (Jagannatha Dasa, Achyutananda and others), the highest reality without attributes; they worship Jagannatha as the Shunya-purusha. Madhava here speaks the language of this Odia tradition. Gaudiya Vaishnavas, following Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati, do not accept the Shunya as the Absolute: Jagannatha is Krishna Himself, the Supreme Person with an eternal spiritual form; the Lord's being "without qualities" means only that He has no material qualities. *Ṭhuḷa* means "gathered into one, the sum".
[^rasaghana-15]: *Rasa-ghana*, "condensed rasa": the Lord as the embodiment of the taste of love; cf. "He indeed is rasa" (*Taittiriya Upanishad* 2.7).
[^atramite-16]: The last word is unidentified: the print has ଅତ୍ରମିତେ (*atramite?*), rhyming with ମୋତେ (*mote*). Possibly a corruption of ଅନ୍ତିମେ (*antime*, "at the end [of life]"). The sense of the half-line is "let no sorrow remain".
[^rk-17]: Odia Vaishnavas contemplate in Jagannatha the eternal lila of Radha and Krishna. According to the *Chaitanya-charitamrita*, Jagannatha is Krishna Himself, and before Him Mahaprabhu experienced the mood of Radha (Madhya 13, Ratha-yatra).
[^nilachala-18]: Nilachala ("the Blue Mountain") is Puri, Purushottama-kshetra. Verses 18–22 speak of Puri as the eternal spiritual abode of Krishna, Goloka.
[^janamale-23]: ଜନମଲେ (*janamale*) is taken as "were born"; possibly ଜନମିଲେ with the ି lost. The sense: devotees sought to be born in Puri or to settle there.
[^mukti-25]: Goloka is the highest abode of Krishna. That pure devotees do not accept liberation devoid of service to the Lord: *Bhagavata Purana* 3.29.13; cf. v. 26.
[^chali-29]: ଭଜିଗଲେ ଚଳି (*bhajigale caḷi*), literally "having worshipped, went away". Possibly "they departed [from the world] worshipping Your name", or "they worshipped unceasingly". The link with the next verse (leaf 47) is to be checked.
[^thoke-30]: ଥୋକେ (*thoke*): "in groups, in hosts"; below the same word (37, 52) and ଥୋକାଏ (*thokāe*, 48) mean "a little".
[^bhela-42]: The raft is a figure for crossing the ocean of samsara; cf. 1.11, 1.22, 1.40.
[^bhola-44]: ଭୋଳ ହୋନ୍ତି (*bhoḷa honti*): "they lose themselves" in the ecstasy of love, ceasing to be aware of the external.
[^mahabhava-47]: Mahabhava is the highest stage of prema. According to the *Chaitanya-charitamrita* (Adi 4), in its fullness it belongs to Sri Radha, and Mahaprabhu manifested it in Puri; here Madhava speaks of the "great play" of the Vaishnavas of Nilachala headed by the Lord.
[^shudra-54]: Madhava calls himself a "shudra" by birth (cf. 1.56: he belongs to the Karanas, the scribe caste). Cf. *Chaitanya-charitamrita*, Madhya 8: Ramananda Raya, also a Karana, calls himself a shudra, and Mahaprabhu replies that one who knows the science of Krishna is a guru, whatever his birth.
[^parents-55]: The father's name, Bhagavan, is read with confidence. The mother's name is printed unclearly: ହୀରାଦେଈ (*hīrādeī*, Hiradei)?
[^karana-56]: The Karanas are the Odia caste of scribes and officials; "Pattanayaka" is their title of honour (hence the author's name, Madhava Pattanayaka).
[^khandapada-57]: Khandapada is a place in Odisha west of Puri (now in Nayagarh district).
[^gadadhara-58]: Gadadhara Pandita is the eternal associate of Mahaprabhu who lived in Puri and served Tota-Gopinatha there. According to Gaudiya Vaishnava teaching (*Chaitanya-charitamrita*, Adi 12; *Gaura-ganoddesha-dipika*), he is the incarnation of Sri Radha's energy.
[^gopala-59]: The Gopala mantra is the mantra for worshipping Krishna as Gopala; initiation in it is given in the Gaudiya Vaishnava tradition as well.
[^gopi-64]: The mood of the gopis is the love of the cowherd girls of Vraja for Krishna. In the *Chaitanya-charitamrita* (Madhya 8) Ramananda Raya, in his conversation with Mahaprabhu, calls the love of the gopis, and above all of Sri Radha, the highest stage of bhakti. The syntax of the couplet and the last word (ଧାର, *dhāra*?) are read with uncertainty.
[^tyajai-66]: The second half-line is not read. The print has approximately ତ୍ୟଜାଇ କେମନ୍ତେ ମୁହାଁସ (*tyajāi kemante muhā̐sa?*); only କେମନ୍ତେ, "how", is clear. The translation follows the sense ("how could I not tell what I saw?").
[^ramananda-67]: Ramananda Raya (in Madhava "Rama Raya") is the great devotee, governor under King Prataparudra, Mahaprabhu's interlocutor on the Godavari (*Chaitanya-charitamrita*, Madhya 8). "Provider" (*annadātā*): apparently Madhava was in Ramananda's service and lived on his support.
