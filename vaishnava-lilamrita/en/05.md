# Madhava Pattanayak. *Vaishnava-lilamrita*, Chapter 5
## Prataparudra at the Ratha-yatra: the Chhera-panhara and Gora's Kirtan

*Translated from Odia without a native speaker; doubtful places are marked (?) and (??). The original, transliteration and word-for-word translation are in `full/05.md`.*

**5.1.** Of [the events of] the nineteenth anka hear, good people, attentively (??).[^anka-1]  
**5.2.** Prataparudra Gajapati graciously comes to the Ratha-yatra.[^gajapati-2]  
**5.3.** The story of the young ascetic (?) he hears from Ramaraya.[^yati-3]  
**5.4.** "A young sannyasi has come," Budha Lenka related.[^lenka-4]  
**5.5.** The Tadhau, bringing the panji, reads: "The young ascetic has come.[^tadhau-5]  
**5.6.** He is here, in this very place, having completed His journey through the south."[^south-6]  
**5.7.** The king said, "Very well (?)," and went to the chhera-panhara.[^chhera-7]  
**5.8.** Performing the chhera-panhara of the chariot, he saw Gora's kirtan.  
**5.9.** Seeing it, he was amazed — and met with Chaitanya (?).[^meet-9]  
**5.10.** Ramaraya, who was in the company, is beside Prabhu.  
**5.11.** Kanhai, Jagannatha Dasa, Balarama and Yashovanta,[^companions-11]  
**5.12.** Svarupa, Achyuta, Ananta, Gadadhara and Shivananda,  
**5.13.** Bhainsa, Raghu Arakshita — many are with Him.[^bhainsa-13]  

## Talk with the King, the Lodging at Kashi Mishra's; the Journeys to Ramakeli and Prayaga, Rupa's Arrival


**5.14.** The king addressed Gora, gladdened at seeing the kirtan.[^talk-14]  
**5.15.** [The king] said, "This is a good practice: the name of Hari is the essence.[^speaker-15]  
**5.16.** Keep performing kirtan here; do nothing other than this."  
**5.17.** Chaitanya said, "O king, you are a servant of Jagannatha.  
**5.18.** What could be greater than this? Seeing [it], I am blessed."  
**5.19.** The king charged (?) Raya: "Look well after the ascetic's affairs."[^baragile-19]  
**5.20.** Summoning Kashi Mishra, [he said:] "House Chaitanya with care (?)."[^kashi-20]  
**5.21.** So the king commanded — and lodged (?) [Him] in Kashi Mishra's house.[^rakhaile-21]  
**5.22.** This place is near the temple, a fitting abode for the ascetic.  
**5.23.** To relish the bhava of Radha, He appeared (lit. "took birth") in the world of mortals.[^radha-23]  
**5.24.** At His waist He ties a cord and ties knots (?) on the cord.[^knots-24]  
**5.25.** Daily (?) He chants a hundred thousand names; from dawn He performs kirtan.[^lakh-25]  
**5.26.** In bliss He lives in the rasa of Krishna, in the mandapa on the northern side.[^mandapa-26]  
**5.27.** Meanwhile — listen, people — after some days Chaitanya says:  
**5.28.** "I shall go from here to the north and, returning, come back to the Kshetra."[^north-28]  
**5.29.** Raya went with Him and, parting [from Him] at Cuttack, came back.[^cuttack-29]  
**5.30.** By way of Cuttack and Remuna (?), Chaitanya went to Ramakeli,[^remuna-30]  
**5.31.** met Rupa and Sanatana, and made them give up royal service:[^ramakeli-31]  
**5.32.** "You two brothers are learned, worthy vessels for the rasa of Krishna."  
**5.33.** From there He turned back and arrived in the Kshetra.[^return-33]  
**5.34.** All were gladdened — Rama Raya and Kashi Mishra.  
**5.35.** Some days after this, He made ready to go to Prayaga.  
**5.36.** He went by the forest road and, going on, reached Prayaga.[^prayaga-36]  
**5.37.** In that place He met Rupa and stayed [there] ten days.  
**5.38.** He said to Rupa, "You come — appear in Nilachala."[^nilachala-38]  
**5.39.** Chaitanya turned back and arrived in Nilachala.  
**5.40.** Rupa came after [some] days, by the road through Remuna and Bhadrak.[^rupa-40]  
**5.41.** In Nilachala Rupa lives; his life in Krishna (?) … (??).[^tekaste-41]  
**5.42.** His love is for the shastras; he is full of affection for sadhus and Vaishnavas.  

## Rupa with Ramananda and Svami; Kabir in Puri; Sanatana's Arrival


**5.43.** Chaitanya brought [Rupa] to Raya and said, introducing him: "This is Rupa.[^raya-43]  
**5.44.** Teach him tattva, show him the path (?) of rasa."  
**5.45.** Raya conversed with Rupa and taught him the truths of rasa:  
**5.46.** shanta, sakhya and vatsalya, dasya, madhura, the mingling of bhavas (?),[^rasa-46]  
**5.47.** mottayita (?), kilakinchita and all the other truths, as many as there are.[^alankara-47]  
**5.48.** Deep is this supreme truth — in the world it is hidden.  
**5.49.** Raya is a knower (?) of the truths of rasa, and Rupa grasped (?) all of it.  
**5.50.** Having known the inner (?), supreme (?) bhava, he became fixed (?) in a humble disposition (?).[^dina-50]  
**5.51.** For ten months Rupa stayed [in Nilachala] and heard the *Bhagavata* from Svami.[^svami-51]  
**5.52.** All Svami's bhavas — trembling, hair standing on end — [were] natural [to him].  
**5.53.** He reads the verses of the *Bhagavata* — tears stream from his eyes.  
**5.54.** Having learned from Svami the truth of bhakti, [Rupa] was supremely glad.  
**5.55.** He had all the manuscripts copied — the *Brahma-samhita* and the *Karnamrita*.[^books-55]  
**5.56.** But Kashi Mishra could not bear it and said: "Send Rupa away!"[^kashi-56]  
**5.57.** "In everything his conduct is that of a mleccha: he does not go for darshan of Jagannatha.  
**5.58.** He is a minister of a mleccha king, yet [he] is brought (?) here, called a devotee."[^mleccha-58]  
**5.59.** Chaitanya says: "Rupa, go; do not stay here any longer (?).  
**5.60.** Build a holy seat in Vrindavana; write books, firm of mind."[^vrindavana-60]  
**5.61.** Rupa set out for Vrindavana — he did not linger here even for a moment.  
**5.62.** In the king's twenty-fourth anka a sannyasi comes from the north.[^anka-62]  
**5.63.** His name is Jholaa Kabir; on his body is saffron cloth.[^kabir-63]  
**5.64.** Holding a tumbura in his hand, he sings songs in a sweet voice.[^tumbura-64]  
**5.65.** His love is for Jagannatha; his mind is great (?) and generous.  
**5.66.** Svami, hearing [his] bhajan, embraced him with love.[^svami-66]  
**5.67.** He said: "All human beings are equal in this (??) [world].[^world-67]  
**5.68.** Be it a chandala or a twice-born — know: in creation [all] are equal.[^jati-68]  
**5.69.** If the mind abides in the Name alone, Krishna is attained with ease.  
**5.70.** The desire for liberation is like hell; service to one's chosen Deity — that is the root."[^mukti-70]  
**5.71.** Kabir found satisfaction and gave his mind to Jagannatha.  
**5.72.** After a month he departed — in the king's twenty-fourth anka.  
**5.73.** From the north comes (?) Sanatana — know that he is very dear to Prabhu.[^sanatana-73]  

## Sanatana under the Chariot; the Instruction of Sanatana


**5.74.** He had been minister to the mleccha king, and in time he became a Vaishnava.[^sanatana-74]  
**5.75.** On his body were evil sores; he came (?) at the time of Gundicha (?).[^gundicha-75]  
**5.76.** He threw himself under the chariot, saying that he would give up his life.[^ratha-76]  
**5.77.** Svarupa, Tulasi, Achyuta, Ananta, Kanhai and Yashovanta[^names-77]  
**5.78.** lifted him, raised him up (?) and held him, saying: "What a deed you are doing!"  
**5.79.** In the din (?) of the kirtana no one learned of this matter.[^gaha-79]  
**5.80.** Prabhu came to know of it and reproached (?) [him]: "What is this? Is this conduct of yours good?[^rebuke-80]  
**5.81.** Where is (?) a pandit more learned than you? — and you would take (??) the life from your body!"[^pandit-81]  
**5.82.** He stayed [in Nilachala] for a year and understood many truths.[^year-82]  
**5.83.** From Svami he learned the immeasurable truths of devotion:[^svami-83]  
**5.84.** the truth of Krishna and the truth of creation, the truth of the jiva and the truth of lila.  
**5.85.** The jiva and Brahman are not one: the jiva, he says, is Krishna's servant.[^jiva-85]  
**5.86.** He expounds the truth of difference-and-non-difference (bheda-abheda), and Sanatana listens in silence.[^bhedabheda-86]  
**5.87.** Speaking of Krishna's energies, he names chit-shakti and jiva-shakti,  
**5.88.** and there is one more — maya-shakti. Svami explained much.[^shakti-88]  
**5.89.** The lila-avataras, the guna-avataras, the yuga-avataras and their workings (?);[^avatara-89]  
**5.90.** Krishna's aishvarya is one bhava (?), but the bhava of madhurya is [His] own nature.[^madhurya-90]  
**5.91.** Svami expounded bhajan in the madhura-bhava.  
**5.92.** Madhura is the sadhana, and he calls (?) it the best as well.  
**5.93.** In the highest way Krishna manifests Himself in His humanlike lila — it alone is full of rasa.[^naralila-93]  
**5.94.** "Recite the kama-gayatri mantra, making your mind firm in Krishna.[^kamagayatri-94]  
**5.95.** Abandoning (?) the vaidhi (?) path, perform bhajan by the path of raga.[^raga-95]  
**5.96.** A Vaishnava does not desire (??) liberation — he longs for service to Krishna.[^mukti-96]  
**5.97.** Thus Krishna is attained, thus the doubts of the mind are dispelled."  
**5.98.** Prabhu said: "Go from here and establish a holy seat in Vrindavana."[^vrindavana-98]  
**5.99.** And Sanatana set out after a year had passed.  

## Vallabha in Nilachala; Svami Receives the Name "Atibada"; Balarama Dasa


**5.100.** From the north came Vallabha; his devotional feeling is for Gopala.[^vallabha-100]  
**5.101.** Together (?) with Svami he has a meeting: they read the *Bhagavata*.[^svami-101]  
**5.102.** His mind is on Sri Jagannatha: at darshan he finds joy.  
**5.103.** "This [Jagannatha] is Gopijana-vallabha Himself, Who has taken the form of Radha-Krishna" —[^gopijana-103]  
**5.104.** [so] the sannyasi Vallabha understood, and the devotee made [Him his] offering, saying:  
**5.105.** "My faith is this: know that Jagannatha is Gopijana-vallabha."  
**5.106.** Having come for the Ratha [festival], Vallabha Gosai stayed [in Nilachala] two months.[^ratha-106]  
**5.107.** Seeing Prabhu's kirtana, he gains boundless joy.  
**5.108.** He came to the ishta-goshthi and grasped (?) many truths.[^goshthi-108]  
**5.109.** Meanwhile — listen with relish — in the king's twenty-seventh anka,[^anka-109]  
**5.110.** one day, sitting in seclusion, Prabhu, beside (?) Svami, [said]:  
**5.111.** "Tell Me the account of [our former] embodiment (?): from you [I] differ only in body."[^pinda-111]  
**5.112.** Svami said: "Very well." Prabhu says: "Tell it fully."  
**5.113.** Svami relates in private: "Radha was beside (?) [Krishna].  
**5.114.** Radha is not different from Krishna — She abides in wholehearted bhava.[^ekadeha-114]  
**5.115.** That form is sac-cid-ananda: Radha's svarupa [and His] are one body.  
**5.116.** Looking at Radha, [Krishna] laughed — the nectar of Krishna's laughter flowed.  
**5.117.** From Krishna's laughter is Your birth; from Radha's laughter is my birth."[^hasa-117]  
**5.118.** [Thus] he told of the former form, and this is not otherwise (?) [than it was].  
**5.119.** Prabhu embraced him with love (prema) and said: "This one is truly very great (ati-bada)!"[^atibada-119]  
**5.120.** "Yours and Mine is one life; only the body is different.  
**5.121.** You tell this very great story — from today you have become Atibada."  
**5.122.** He took the uttariya (?) from His shoulder and tied it on Svami's head.[^uttari-122]  
**5.123.** He says: "This is true greatness! Svami, you are My very life."  
**5.124.** Prabhu told Svarupa to call him Atibada.[^svarupa-124]  
**5.125.** And before the gathering (?) of all the devotees Prabhu proclaims [him] Atibada.  
**5.126.** Kashi Mishra and Rama Raya, Balarama, Achyuta, surrounding (??) [Him],[^names-126]  
**5.127.** together (?) with Ananta and Yashovanta — [before them] Prabhu proclaimed this news.  
**5.128.** All accepted this usage (?) — "Atibada."  
**5.129.** He told a very great story — from today he became Atibada.  
**5.130.** Meanwhile, another tale full of rasa: hearing it, good people, rejoice.  
**5.131.** [He who] is called (?) Balarama Dasa — he is a mahapurusha.[^balarama-131]  
**5.132.** He is the son of Somanatha; he reveals the truth of Vedanta.[^vedanta-132]  

## Balarama Dasa and the Sand Chariot; End of Chapter 5


**5.133.** He composed the song of the *Ramayana*; he is called a yoga-purusha (?).[^yogapurusha-133]  
**5.134.** [Once] he climbed onto the chariot; in his hand there was (?) a flower garland:  
**5.135.** "On the chariot I shall give [it] to Vamana; I shall offer [Him my] devotional feeling" —[^vamana-135]  
**5.136.** [saying] so, he climbed onto the chariot. The servants say: "Get away from here!"[^sevaka-136]  
**5.137.** "You have no right to offer a garland to [the Lord of] the chariot (?)."  
**5.138.** The servants reviled [him] much and said: "Get away from this place, you!"  
**5.139.** He came down from the chariot and said nothing to anyone.  
**5.140.** He went to the seashore to worship Jagannatha.  
**5.141.** Out of sand he made a chariot and called: "[O] our (?) son of Nanda (?)!"[^nandabala-141]  
**5.142.** "Come, O brother Balarama, bringing sister Subhadra!"  
**5.143.** The three Deities took Their seat [on it] and accepted worship on the sand chariot.[^pratima-143]  
**5.144.** On the Danda the chariot did not move; the crowd of people stood stunned (?).[^danda-144]  
**5.145.** The devotees stood looking on; however much the servants strove (?),  
**5.146.** [however much] the king, having come, raged (?) — still the chariot does not move.[^king-146]  
**5.147.** The day passed, night came — and yet the chariot did not move.  
**5.148.** A hundred thousand devotees gathered — no one could move [it] (??).[^ghai-148]  
**5.149.** At night the king had a dream: "I am on the seashore," [the Lord] said.[^dream-149]  
**5.150.** "Go, king, and bring Me together with the devotee Balarama."  
**5.151.** The king planned a procession (?): Prabhu and Svami — [in his] retinue (?);[^gasta-151]  
**5.152.** Kashi Mishra, Tapana Acharya (?), Kanhai, Ananta Acharya (?),[^names-152]  
**5.153.** Achyuta, Ananta, Yashovanta, Tulasi Mishra, Damodara Pandita.[^names-153]  
**5.154.** With the din of cymbals and mridangas the seashore seemed to brim over.  
**5.155.** On the sand all (?) assembled and offered dandavat prostrations.  
**5.156.** In the festivity (?) — the bhava and longing (?) [of all]; the joy of the devotees … (??).[^utsava-156]  
**5.157.** They glorified Balarama, worshipped [him] with flowers and sandal,  
**5.158.** and said: "Come, Balarama; your name is 'best of devotees'.  
**5.159.** With devotion and bhava you have bound [Him]; you have brought Prabhu under your control.[^prabhu-159]  
**5.160.** Where is there a devotee [like] you? Come quickly — we are swooning (??)."[^muruchu-160]  
**5.161.** Balarama Dasa came and appeared upon the chariot.  
**5.162.** He offered the garland to Prabhu and covered [His] body with sandal.  
**5.163.** The din of bells and mridangas — the Bada-danda then seemed to brim over.  
**5.164.** The chariot rolled (??) rumbling (?), says Madhava, falling at the feet.[^end-164]  

[^anka-1]: Prataparudra's nineteenth anka is, by the usual count (anka 1 and ankas ending in 0 and 6 are skipped), the 15th regnal year, ≈1511/12; the Ratha-yatra of this anka falls ≈ summer 1512. According to the CC (Madhya 9–11), Mahaprabhu returned to Puri from the south shortly before the Ratha-yatra, to which the Bengali devotees came. Printed ≈ ବଷୟେ — read ବିଷୟେ ("concerning, of"); the last word of the second half-line (≈ ଅୟେ) is not identified, and "attentively" follows the sense.
[^gajapati-2]: Gajapati ("lord of elephants") is the title of the Odia kings. *Bije* is the honorific verb for the movement of the king or the Deity.
[^yati-3]: Printed ≈ ସତ ("a true [story]"); we read ଯତି, "ascetic, sannyasi", as in 5.5 (ସ and ଯ are almost indistinguishable in this type). The king learns of Mahaprabhu from Ramananda Raya — cf. CC, Madhya 11, where Ramananda, on his return, tells the king about the Lord.
[^lenka-4]: Budha Lenka — "old Lenka"; *lenka* is an Odia designation of a servant or attendant. The person is not identified.
[^tadhau-5]: The Tadhau is apparently a temple scribe (karana) who keeps the records (*pañji*; cf. the *Madala-panji*, the chronicle of Jagannatha's temple). Where his words end is not marked; we take 5.5b–5.6 as what he reads out.
[^south-6]: Mahaprabhu's southern pilgrimage (≈1510–1512) — CC, Madhya 7–9; in Madhava, Chapter 4 (the meeting with Ramananda on the Godavari, 4.81–124).
[^chhera-7]: The chhera-panhara is the king's sweeping of the chariots with a golden broom (cf. 2.121). According to the CC (Madhya 13), Mahaprabhu was pleased by this humble service of Prataparudra. "ହେଉ ହେଲ" (cf. 4.122) is taken as "very well, all right" — uncertain.
[^meet-9]: CC (Madhya 13–14): during the procession the king watched Mahaprabhu's kirtan and was amazed; the meeting itself took place later, in the garden, when the chariot halted: Prataparudra, in the plain dress of a Vaishnava, recited verses of the *Gopi-gita*, and Mahaprabhu embraced him. Madhava compresses the account into one line. The word before "met" is printed ≈ ରୂଲେ/ତୁଲେ and is taken as "together with" (?).
[^companions-11]: Prabhu's companions in the kirtan according to Madhava: Kanhai (cf. 2.25); the Panchasakha — Jagannatha Dasa, Balarama Dasa, Yashovanta, Achyutananda, Ananta (cf. 1.76); and Svarupa Damodara, Gadadhara Pandita and Shivananda (Sena?). In the CC (Madhya 13) the Ratha-yatra is led by seven kirtan groups of Bengali devotees (Svarupa, Shrivasa, Mukunda, Advaita, Nityananda and others); the participation of the Odia Panchasakha is a tradition of the Odia school, not attested in the CC/CB.
[^bhainsa-13]: Bhainsa and Raghu Arakshita are not identified.
[^talk-14]: Gora (Gaura) is Mahaprabhu, as in 5.8. According to the CC (Madhya 12–14), Mahaprabhu as a sannyasi avoided meeting the king; Prataparudra's humble service at the chhera-panhara pleased Him, but the meeting took place later, in the garden, when the king came in the plain dress of a Vaishnava. In Madhava the king and Mahaprabhu converse at once, at the Ratha-yatra.
[^speaker-15]: The change of speaker is not marked. We take 5.15–16 as the king's words (the honorific verb *boile*; Chaitanya's reply begins in 5.17). "ଏ ଭଲ ବେଭାର" (*e bhala bebhāra*) — "this is a good practice [conduct]" (*bebhāra* = *byabahāra*); it could also be understood as an inquiry after His welfare.
[^baragile-19]: Raya is Ramananda Raya. The verb ବରଗିଲେ (*baragile*) is the same as the unidentified ବରଗିଲ in 3.105; there and here a superior gives an instruction, so we assume the sense "charged, enjoined" (?). "Ascetic" — printed ≈ ଯତର, read ଯତିର (‑ି lost; cf. ଯତ = *yati* in 5.5); likewise in 5.22.
[^kashi-20]: Kashi Mishra was a temple priest and the king's preceptor; in his house near the temple (later called the "Gambhira") Mahaprabhu lived in Puri. According to the CC (Madhya 10), the lodging at Kashi Mishra's was arranged, at the king's wish, by Sarvabhauma immediately after the Lord's return from the south, before the Ratha-yatra; in Madhava the king entrusts it to Ramananda and to Kashi Mishra himself after the Ratha-yatra. The form ମିଶ୍ରକଇ (*miśrakaï*) is taken as a dative-accusative (= ‑କୁ?). "With care" — printed ≈ ଯ + ନ with a subscript sign, read ଯତ୍ନ (?).
[^rakhaile-21]: The last word is printed ≈ ର + a small sign like "େ" + ା + ଇଲେ; we read ରଖାଇଲେ, "lodged, had [Him] housed" (?), from the rhyme and from 5.20 (*rakha*).
[^radha-23]: This agrees with the Gaudiya Vaishnava understanding of the confidential reason for Mahaprabhu's descent — to relish Shri Radha's love for Krishna (CC, Adi 4). The link with the preceding verse is not marked: 5.23–26 describe Mahaprabhu in Puri.
[^knots-24]: Understood as counting the names chanted by knots on a cord. The word after "knots" is printed ≈ ଆଲେ (or ଥାଲେ) and is not identified (ଗଣ୍ଠିଆଳ, "knotted"?). "Cord" — printed ≈ ସ[ୂ]ତ୍ରକୁ, "on the cord" (?).
[^lakh-25]: The first sign is blotted with ink; we read ନିତ୍ୟ, "daily", from the sense (?). A hundred thousand names is a "lakh" (*lakṣa*).
[^mandapa-26]: Cf. 4.158: Ramananda built a mandapa on the northern side, where the Vaishnavas gathered (there the unclear ପାର୍ଶ୍ୱଣ (?); here ପାରୁଶ, *pāruśa* = *pārśva*, "side"). Exactly where this mandapa stood (on the northern side of the temple?) is unclear.
[^north-28]: The journey north is the attempt to go to Vrindavan through Bengal (CC, Madhya 16; ≈1514 by the Gaudiya Vaishnava chronology). Madhava reduces the account to a few lines.
[^cuttack-29]: According to the CC (Madhya 16), Ramananda accompanied the Lord further — to the border of Odisha. Printed ≈ ଛଡ଼ଣ, read ଛଡ଼ିଣ, "having parted" (or ଛାଡ଼ିଣ, "having left [Him]").
[^remuna-30]: Remuna (near Balasore) is the place of the Deity Kshira-chora Gopinatha (CC, Madhya 4). The syntax of ରେମୁଣା କଟକେଣ ହୋଇ (*remuṇā kaṭakeṇa hoi*) is unclear: "via Remuna [and] Cuttack" or "from Cuttack via Remuna"; the same phrase occurs elsewhere in the poem.
[^ramakeli-31]: Ramakeli is a village near Gauda where Mahaprabhu met the brothers Rupa and Sanatana, ministers of Sultan Husain Shah (CC, Madhya 1 and 16). According to the CC (Madhya 19), they gave up their service later, after this meeting; Madhava compresses this into one line.
[^return-33]: According to the CC (Madhya 16), Mahaprabhu turned back from Kanai-natashala and returned to Puri.
[^prayaga-36]: According to the CC (Madhya 17–19), Mahaprabhu went through the Jharikhanda forest to Vrindavan (≈1515) and on the way back met Rupa at Prayaga and instructed him for ten days (Madhya 19). Madhava mentions neither Vrindavan nor the instruction of Sanatana in Varanasi (Madhya 20–25).
[^nilachala-38]: According to the CC (Madhya 19), Mahaprabhu sent Rupa to Vrindavan and afterwards allowed him to come to Him in Nilachala by way of Gauda; Rupa's arrival in Puri is in CC, Antya 1. *Parakāśa* (= *prakāśa*) is taken as the imperative "appear".
[^rupa-40]: Remuna and Bhadrak lie on the road from Bengal to Puri. According to the CC (Antya 1), in Puri Rupa stayed with Haridasa Thakura for about ten months and read his plays *Vidagdha-madhava* and *Lalita-madhava* to Mahaprabhu, Ramananda and Svarupa.
[^tekaste-41]: The last word is printed ≈ a sign like ଡ଼ or ଙ with a dot + ଟ + କା + େ + ସ with a subscript sign (*ṭekāste*?) and is not identified. *Kṛṣṇabartti* is taken as "a life [devoted] to Krishna" (*kṛṣṇa-bṛtti*?) or "talk of Krishna" (*kṛṣṇa-bārttā*?).
[^raya-43]: Raya is Ramananda Raya (in Madhava also "Ramaraya"). The account of Rupa's coming to Puri begins on sheet 77 (5.38–42). According to the CC (Antya 1), in Puri Rupa's dramas were examined in the presence of Ramananda Raya and Sarvabhauma, and Ramananda glorified his knowledge of rasa; the instruction in rasa-tattva itself Rupa had received earlier from Mahaprabhu at Prayaga (CC, Madhya 19). In Madhava, Chaitanya entrusts Rupa's teaching to Raya himself. In 5.43b ପ୍ରକାଶେ is taken as "openly, by way of introduction" — uncertain.
[^rasa-46]: The five principal rasas (relationships with the Lord) — shanta, dasya, sakhya, vatsalya, madhurya. *Bhāva-śābalya*, "the mingling of bhavas", is the simultaneous combination of several emotions, described in Rupa Gosvami's *Bhakti-rasamrita-sindhu*. The first sign of the word is printed unclearly; the reading ଶାବଲ୍ୟ is suggested by the rhyme with ବାତ୍ସଲ୍ୟ.
[^alankara-47]: Mottayita (the display of hidden love at the mention of the beloved) and kilakinchita (a mixture of emotions — laughter, tears, anger and others) are "ornaments" (alankaras) of Sri Radha, later described by Rupa Gosvami himself in the *Ujjvala-nilamani*. The first word is printed unclearly (≈ ମୋ + ଟ୍ଟା/ଷ୍ଠା + ୟି + ତ); in the second the ‑i signs are lost.
[^dina-50]: The couplet is understood tentatively: three words are read with uncertainty (ଭିତର? ଶ୍ରେଷ୍ଠ? ବୃତ୍ତି?), and ପରମାଣି is taken as "taking as the standard" (from *pramāṇa*). The humility of Rupa and Sanatana is glorified many times in the CC (Antya 1, 4).
[^svami-51]: Svami is Jagannatha Dasa (cf. 2.6), author of the Odia *Bhagavata*. Rupa's study of the *Bhagavata* under Jagannatha Dasa is a tradition of the Odia school; it is not mentioned in the CC/CB. According to the CC (Antya 1), in Puri Rupa lived together with Haridasa Thakura and stayed until the Dola-yatra; for Gaudiya Vaishnavas Rupa Gosvami is the acharya of rasa-tattva, who received it directly from Mahaprabhu.
[^books-55]: The *Brahma-samhita* (its fifth chapter) Mahaprabhu found in the temple of Adi-Keshava, and Bilvamangala's *Krishna-karnamrita* on the bank of the Krishna-vena river, during His southern pilgrimage; He brought both books to Puri, where the devotees copied them (CC, Madhya 9, 11). Printed ସହିତା (without anusvara) and ≈ କର୍ଣ୍ଣମୃତେ.
[^kashi-56]: Kashi Mishra was Mahaprabhu's host in Puri (the Gambhira is in his house). The CC has no such displeasure on Kashi Mishra's part: according to the CC (Antya 1, 4), Rupa and Sanatana themselves, out of humility, considering themselves defiled by their service at the Muslim court, did not approach the temple of Jagannatha, and Mahaprabhu visited them daily at Haridasa's place and praised their humility. The accusation of Rupa (5.56–58) is an account of the Odia tradition.
[^mleccha-58]: The mleccha king is Husain Shah, sultan of Bengal; Rupa (Dabir Khas) and Sanatana (Sakara Mallik) were his ministers (CC, Madhya 1, 19). Who "brings" Rupa (3rd person ଆଣଇ) is not stated.
[^vrindavana-60]: According to the CC (Antya 1), after the Dola-yatra Mahaprabhu sent Rupa to Vrindavana, charging him to write books on rasa and to restore the lost holy places; from Puri Rupa went first to Bengal and from there to Vrindavana. In the edition the numbers of 5.60 and 5.61 are printed transposed (।୬୧।, ।୬୦।); here they follow the order of the lines.
[^anka-62]: Prataparudra's twenty-fourth anka is, by the usual count (anka 1 and ankas ending in 0 and 6 are skipped), the 19th regnal year, ≈1515/16 (accession ≈1497).
[^kabir-63]: Kabir — the poet-saint of Varanasi (15th – early 16th c.), a weaver by birth. *Jholaā* is apparently an Odia rendering of *julāhā*, "weaver". Madhava calls him a sannyasi in saffron cloth. Kabir's coming to Puri and his meeting with Jagannatha Dasa are a tradition of the Odia school (the "Kabir-chaura" math is known in Puri); Kabir is not mentioned in the CC/CB.
[^tumbura-64]: The tumbura is a stringed drone instrument (cf. the tanpura).
[^svami-66]: Svami is Jagannatha Dasa. Who speaks in 5.67–70 is not marked; by the sense it is Svami instructing Kabir (in 5.71 Kabir "found satisfaction").
[^world-67]: The last word (printed ≈ ଶସ୍ତଳ) is the same unclear word as in 2.13 and 2.37, where by the sense it means "on earth, in the world".
[^jati-68]: Cf. the CC (Antya 4): for the service of Krishna low birth is no obstacle and high birth no qualification; according to the *Srimad-Bhagavatam* (3.33.7), even a dog-eater who chants the holy name is worthy of honour.
[^mukti-70]: Cf. *Srimad-Bhagavatam* 3.29.13: pure devotees do not accept any kind of liberation if it lacks service to the Lord. Gaudiya Vaishnavas likewise regard the striving for impersonal liberation as an obstacle to bhakti.
[^sanatana-73]: Sanatana Gosvami, Rupa's elder brother. According to the CC (Antya 4), he came to Puri from Vrindavana through the Jharikhanda forest when Rupa had already left; Mahaprabhu lodged him with Haridasa. The account continues on sheet 80.
[^sanatana-74]: Sanatana (Gosvami) — before his renunciation Sakara Mallik, minister of Husain Shah, the sultan of Bengal (the "mleccha king", cf. 5.58). According to the CC (Madhya 19–20), he left state service, was imprisoned by the sultan, escaped and met Mahaprabhu in Varanasi, where he received His detailed instruction (Madhya 20–24); from there Mahaprabhu sent him to Vrindavana. Madhava says it briefly: "in time he became a Vaishnava".
[^gundicha-75]: Gundicha — the chariot festival (Ratha-yatra, "Gundicha-yatra"), when the Deities ride in chariots to the Gundicha temple. The reading of the couplet is uncertain: the first word is printed ≈ ଆସତ (taken as ଆସନ୍ତ "having come"), and the last sign of the second is unclear. According to the CC (Antya 4), on the way to Puri through the Jharikhanda forest Sanatana's whole body broke out in itching sores from bad water and fasting, and he resolved to give up his body under the wheel of Jagannatha's chariot during Ratha-yatra.
[^ratha-76]: In the CC (Antya 4) Sanatana only intended this; Mahaprabhu, knowing his intention, told him that giving up the body is an act in the mode of ignorance and that Krishna is not attained that way, but only by bhajan; moreover, Sanatana's body had already been surrendered to Mahaprabhu and belonged to Him. In Madhava, Sanatana actually throws himself under the chariot and the companions lift him up (5.77–78) — an account of the Odia tradition.
[^names-77]: Svarupa — Svarupa Damodara. Tulasi — possibly Tulasi the parichha (superintendent of the Jagannatha temple) mentioned in the CC. Achyuta (Achyutananda), Ananta and Yashovanta belong to the Panchasakha; Kanhai — cf. 2.25 (possibly Kanai Khuntia).
[^gaha-79]: *Gaha gaha* is apparently onomatopoeic (a hum, the thick noise of a crowd); reading and sense are uncertain.
[^rebuke-80]: Cf. the CC (Antya 4): Mahaprabhu reproaches Sanatana — if Krishna could be obtained by giving up the body, He Himself would give up millions of bodies; Krishna is not attained by giving up the body. The first unclear word (printed ≈ ତରକଲ) is taken as *tarakila* "began to reproach" (or *tarajila* "threatened"?); "what is this" — *kisa* (printed ≈ କସ).
[^pandit-81]: The last words of both half-lines are read uncertainly (printed ≈ କାହିଁଛ and ≈ ମୂଚୁଛ); the translation is conjectural. In the CC Sanatana is glorified as a great knower of the scriptures.
[^year-82]: According to the CC (Antya 4), Sanatana lived with Mahaprabhu in Puri for about a year — from Ratha-yatra to the Dola-yatra of the next spring; then Mahaprabhu let him go to Vrindavana (cf. 5.99).
[^svami-83]: Svami — Jagannatha Dasa (cf. 2.6, 5.51). According to the CC (Madhya 20–24), all this teaching — on Krishna, on the jiva as Krishna's eternal servant, on the three energies, on the avataras, on sadhana-bhakti (vaidhi and raganuga) and on prema — was given to Sanatana by Mahaprabhu Himself, in Varanasi, over two months. Madhava ascribes the instruction of Sanatana in Puri to Jagannatha Dasa — a tradition of Odisha; for Gaudiya Vaishnavas Sanatana Gosvami is a disciple of Mahaprabhu Himself. The speaker in 5.85–97 is by sense Svami (named outright in 5.88 and 5.91).
[^jiva-85]: Cf. CC, Madhya 20.108: the constitutional nature of the jiva is to be Krishna's eternal servant. Gaudiya Vaishnavas, as here, reject the identification of the jiva with Brahman.
[^bhedabheda-86]: Gaudiya Vaishnavas call this teaching *achintya-bheda-abheda* — "inconceivable oneness and difference"; it was proclaimed by Mahaprabhu Himself (CC, Adi 7; Madhya 20) and systematically set out by Srila Jiva Gosvami. In Madhava, bheda-abheda is expounded by Jagannatha Dasa.
[^shakti-88]: Cf. CC, Madhya 20 (and *Vishnu Purana* 6.7.61): Krishna has three energies — internal (chit), marginal (jiva) and external (maya). Couplets 5.87–88 form one sentence.
[^avatara-89]: Mahaprabhu instructs Sanatana on the lila-, guna-, yuga- and other avataras in CC, Madhya 20. The last word (*bebhāra* "conduct, usage", as in 5.80b) is not fully clear here.
[^madhurya-90]: Cf. CC, Madhya 21: Krishna's sweetness (madhurya) is the essence of His nature, and His majesty (aishvarya) is subordinate to it. The last word of the first half-line is printed incompletely (≈ ଭା…) and restored by rhyme as ଭାବ.
[^naralila-93]: Cf. CC, Madhya 21.101: of all Krishna's pastimes the highest is His humanlike lila (*nara-lila*).
[^kamagayatri-94]: The kama-gayatri is the mantra for worshipping Krishna as the transcendental Cupid (Madana) of Vrindavana (cf. CC, Madhya 8); Gaudiya Vaishnavas receive it from the guru at initiation. Verses 5.94–97 are an instruction in the imperative; by sense the speaker is Svami.
[^raga-95]: Vaidhi-bhakti (following the injunctions of scripture) and raganuga-bhakti (following the spontaneous love of the residents of Vraja) are described in CC, Madhya 22, and in Rupa Gosvami's *Bhakti-rasamrita-sindhu*. Gaudiya Vaishnavas teach that raganuga awakens from genuine eagerness (*lobha*) and also follows the scriptures, rather than simply setting vaidhi-bhakti aside. Both unclear words (printed ≈ ବଇଧ…, ≈ ଛଡ଼ିଣ) are understood by the sense.
[^mukti-96]: Cf. 5.70 and the note to it. The verb of the first half-line is not identified (printed ≈ ବୁହିଁ); the sense suggests ଚାହିଁ "wants".
[^vrindavana-98]: According to the CC (Antya 4), Mahaprabhu let Sanatana go to Vrindavana after the Dola-yatra, charging him to write books on bhakti and to restore the lost holy places. "Pitha" — a holy seat; cf. 5.60 (the same charge to Rupa).
[^vallabha-100]: Vallabha is Vallabha Bhatta (Vallabhacharya, 1479–1531), founder of the Pushti-marga, a worshipper of Bala-Gopala (the child Krishna). According to the CC (Madhya 19), Mahaprabhu met him at Adaila near Prayaga — that is, north of Puri; later Vallabha Bhatta came to Puri himself, Mahaprabhu humbled his pride in his commentary on the *Bhagavata*, and afterwards, with His permission, Vallabha received the Kishora-Gopala mantra from Gadadhara Pandita (CC, Antya 7). Vallabha's meetings with Jagannatha Dasa belong to the Odia tradition; they are not in the CC/CB. Printed ≈ ଅଇଲ ("came"), ‑ā lost.
[^svami-101]: Svami is Jagannatha Dasa, author of the Odia *Bhagavata* (cf. 5.51). The word ତୁଳେଣ ("together with"?) is read and understood tentatively.
[^gopijana-103]: Gopijana-vallabha, "the beloved of the gopis," is the youthful Krishna, Kishora-Gopala. According to Madhava, Vallabha, who had worshipped Gopala, recognises in Jagannatha Gopijana-vallabha Himself, "Who has taken the form of Radha-Krishna" (an Odia conception of Jagannatha). Cf. CC, Antya 7: Vallabha Bhatta moves from the service of Bala-Gopala to that of Kishora-Gopala. According to his followers' tradition, Vallabha became a sannyasi only at the end of his life; what the devotee "offered" in 5.104 is not stated.
[^ratha-106]: Ratha-yatra is the chariot festival of Jagannatha in Puri (month of Ashadha); Mahaprabhu's kirtana and dancing before the chariot are described in CC, Madhya 13. According to the CC (Antya 7), Vallabha Bhatta spent some time in Puri and saw Mahaprabhu's kirtana.
[^goshthi-108]: Ishta-goshthi is a gathering of devotees for discourse on the chosen Deity and on spiritual truths; the expression also occurs in the CC. The last word (printed ≈ ସମଜଲେ) is taken as "grasped, understood" (cf. ସମଝିବା) — tentatively.
[^anka-109]: The twenty-seventh anka of Prataparudra is, by the usual count (anka 1 and ankas ending in 0 and 6 are skipped), the 21st regnal year, ≈1517/18 (accession ≈1497).
[^pinda-111]: The first word (printed ≈ ପିଣ୍ଡା + ଙ୍ଗ) is read as *piṇḍāṅga*, "body, embodiment"; cf. "former form" (*pūrba aṅga*) in 5.118. The idea that Mahaprabhu and Jagannatha Dasa differ "only in body" (5.111, 5.120) belongs to the Odia tradition; it is not in the CC/CB.
[^ekadeha-114]: Cf. CC (Adi 4): Radha and Krishna are one svarupa, yet for the taste of lila they eternally exist in two forms; Radha is Krishna's hladini-shakti, not different from Him.
[^hasa-117]: The account of Mahaprabhu's birth from Krishna's laughter and Jagannatha Dasa's from Radha's laughter belongs to the Odia Atibadi tradition. Gaudiya Vaishnavas, following Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati, recognise Sri Chaitanya as Krishna Himself, Who accepted the mood and golden complexion of Radha (CC, Adi 1, 4); Jagannatha Dasa's claim to origin from Radha does not occur in the CC/CB and is not accepted by Gaudiya Vaishnavas.
[^atibada-119]: "Atibada" (Odia *ati baṛa*, "very great") is the epithet of Jagannatha Dasa; according to Odia tradition it was given by Mahaprabhu Himself, hence the name of his followers, the Atibadis. This episode is not mentioned in the CC/CB. The list of apasampradayas (deviant groups) given by the Gaudiya acharyas includes the "ativadi"; Gaudiya Vaishnavas do not accept this line's teaching of Jagannatha Dasa's identity with Radha.
[^uttari-122]: The uttariya is the upper cloth worn over the shoulders. To tie on someone's head a cloth from one's own shoulder is a mark of the highest honour (cf. the siropa, a turban of honour). The reading ଉତ୍ତରୀ is uncertain (the signs are blurred).
[^svarupa-124]: Svarupa Damodara, Mahaprabhu's closest companion in Nilachala.
[^names-126]: Kashi Mishra; Rama Raya is Ramananda Raya; Balarama (Dasa), Achyuta (Achyutananda), Ananta and Yashovanta (5.127), together with Jagannatha Dasa, make up the Panchasakha (cf. 1.76). [^balarama-131]: Balarama Dasa is the eldest of the Panchasakha, author of the Odia *Jagamohana Ramayana* (*Dandi Ramayana*) and the *Lakshmi Purana*; by tradition the son of Somanatha Mahapatra, an official at Prataparudra's court. [^yogapurusha-133]: Balarama Dasa (cf. 5.131–132) is the author of the Odia *Jagamohana Ramayana* (*Dandi Ramayana*). A yoga-purusha is "a man of yoga", an ascetic endowed with yogic power. The last sign of the half-line (ତ) is taken as an emphatic particle ("indeed"); this is uncertain.
[^vamana-135]: Vamana here is Jagannatha on the chariot: in Puri the Lord seated on the chariot during the Ratha-yatra is called Vamana (Ratha-vamana). A well-known verse says that one who sees Vamana on the chariot is not born again.
[^sevaka-136]: The servants (*sebaka*) are Jagannatha's temple servants, who stay on the chariots during the Ratha-yatra and decide who may climb up. The story of Balarama Dasa, the sand chariot and Jagannatha's halted chariot (5.134–164) is a well-known Odia tradition; it is not in the CC/CB.
[^nandabala-141]: The second half-line is read uncertainly. Printed ≈ ଡାକିଲ ଆମ ନନ୍ଦବଳା: the third word ends in ମ (not ସ, as in ଆସ "come" in 5.142), hence taken as "our"; the last word is "child of Nanda" (*nanda-bāḷā*, Krishna, i.e. Jagannatha), printed without ‑ā. [ଆସ] "come" is also possible.
