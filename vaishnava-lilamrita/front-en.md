# Vaishnava-lilamrita

*"The Nectar of the Pastimes of the Vaishnavas"*

*A poem by Madhava Pattanayaka (Odia, c. 1535)*

*Translated from the Odia*

---

## Translator's note

The *Vaishnava-lilamrita* is an Odia poem in nine chapters by Madhava Pattanayaka, written, by the author's own
account, in the forty-eighth regnal year of Prataparudra (c. 1535). Madhava was a disciple of Gadadhara Pandita,
from whom he received the Gopala mantra. An orphan from Khandapada, as a youth he saw the kirtan of Madhavendra
Puri, was taken into the house of Bhavananda Raya and became the scribe of Ramananda Raya, and later lived in Puri
in the company of Mahaprabhu. Many of his accounts are therefore eyewitness testimony: of the Jagannatha temple and
its kings, of Ramananda, of the Panchasakha — the five Odia Vaishnava poets (Balarama Dasa, Jagannatha Dasa,
Achyutananda, Ananta and Yashovanta) — and of Mahaprabhu's life in Puri. Earlier Madhava had written the
*Chaitanya-vilasa*, on Sri Chaitanya before His sannyasa and arrival in Puri.

**Source.** The text of the poem follows the edition of B. Ch. Acharya (Sambalpur, 1986), read line by line from
the page images. **Only Madhava's text** is translated; the editor's introduction and notes were neither translated
nor paraphrased.

**How the translation was made.** No native speaker of Odia took part. Every couplet was read from the scan,
transliterated and translated word for word (this material will appear in the full edition, published separately);
the literary translation rests on that work. Doubtful places are marked "(?)", unclear ones "(??)"; words supplied
for the sense are in square brackets. Questions for a specialist in Odia are listed separately.

**Numbering.** Couplets are numbered as in the edition: "chapter.verse". The "Eight Verses" in chapter 6 (Sanskrit
verses with their Odia rendering) are marked 6.Sh1…6.Sh8e, and Jagannatha Dasa's song in chapter 8 8.P0…8.P4.
Section headings are the translator's (the edition has none).

**Madhava and the Gaudiya Vaishnavas.** Madhava belongs to the Odia line of Jagannatha Dasa (the Atibadi). In much
he agrees with the *Chaitanya-charitamrita* — on the Lord's descent to taste the bhava of Radha, on the conversation
with Ramananda Raya, on Mahaprabhu's mahabhava in the Gambhira — but at times he departs from Gaudiya Vaishnava
teaching: he places Jagannatha Dasa beside the Lord Himself, ascribes the *Shikshashtaka* to him, speaks of
Nilachala as the highest abode, and tells of Mahaprabhu's departure according to Odia tradition. The translation
renders the author faithfully, softening nothing; at each such place a note briefly gives the Gaudiya Vaishnava
position after Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati: Sri Chaitanya is Krishna Himself, who has taken the mood and lustre
of Radha; His body is sat-chit-ananda, and His departure is a disappearance by His own will.
