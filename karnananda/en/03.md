# Niryasa 3. The Glory of Ramachandra Kaviraja: Srinivasa's Samadhi and the Lost Ornament

*Hemalata's account: in Vishnupura Srinivasa, seated between his two wives, enters meditation and in his siddha-deha beholds the water-pastimes of Radha and Krishna in the Yamuna*

**1.** Glory, glory to Sri Krishna Chaitanya and Nityananda!
Glory to Advaita-chandra, glory to Gaura's devotees!

**2.** I shall tell another narrative — listen attentively;
I shall tell something confidential — fill your ears with it.

**3.** By hearing it bliss arises in the heart;
what can I, most unworthy, say of it?

**4.** Listen, listen, devotees, to the greatness of Ramachandra,
by singing whose virtues prema arises in the heart.

**5.** One day my mistress, Srila Hemalata,
graciously began to tell me this.

**6.** What I heard from the lips of Srimati
filled my heart with joy.

**7.** Touching with my ears the ocean of Sri Ramachandra's greatness,
I floated in bliss, in great jubilation.

**8.** Prabhu and Ramachandra are as if one body;
his heart is deep, he is a great and composed devotee.

**9.** What sweet beauty, what sweetness of character!
All the virtues I heard of are wondrous.

**10.** Sri Ramachandra Kaviraja knows Prabhu's heart —
this is manifest to the whole world.

**11.** Sri Ramachandra's deeds are renowned in the world,
his gentleness and depth are famous throughout the universe.

**12.** Of this I shall tell a little, making it known,
touching only one particle for my own purification.

**13.** One day, in the house at Vana-Vishnupura,
Prabhu was seated with a joyful heart.

**14.** The two Ishvaris[^1] sit on either side of him
and blissfully gaze on Prabhu's form.

**15.** Both greatly praised their good fortune:
"Such lotus feet of Prabhu we have obtained by great fortune!"

**16.** Then Prabhu speaks of Krishna in supreme bliss,
and hearing it, the Ishvaris' joy increased.

**17.** Thus for some time, in the rasa of talk about Krishna,
Prabhu was immersed in great jubilation of prema.

**18.** His mind, overflowing with bhava, cannot grow calm;
tears, trembling and gooseflesh pervade his body.

**19.** Now he roars and rolls on the ground,
now he cries out and calls aloud.

**20.** Saying "Sri Gauranga-chandra", he faints again and again;
helpless in transport, he cries, "Alas, alas!"

**21.** Now he calls aloud, "Sri Rupa, Sanatana!"
Saying "Sri Bhatta Gosani", he floats in the happiness of prema.

**22.** When some time had passed in this way,
in talk of other things Prabhu's mind grew calm.

**23.** Then, after a while, he bathed,
put on white cloth and sat down on his seat.

**24.** Applying tilaka to his forehead and the letters of the holy names to his body,
Prabhu recites hymns in a beautiful voice.

**25.** How the sound of his voice surpasses the kokila!
Joyful at heart, Prabhu recites the hymns.

**26.** With a joyful heart, seated on his seat,
Prabhu carefully serves Sri Vamshivadana.[^2]

**27.** Having performed the service with sandal and tulasi
and offered it, Prabhu sat down to meditate.

**28.** Fixing his mind in the siddha-deha of his cherished service,
he beholds the wondrous sweetness of the pastimes of Radha and Krishna.[^3]

**29.** He beholds the water-pastimes of Radha and Krishna,
and seeing that lila his mind is immersed in happiness.

**30.** In the Yamuna, skilfully arranging the water-sport,
They advanced against each other in a water-battle.

**31.** All the gopis surrounded Krishnachandra —
as though a host of lightning-flashes surrounded a cloud.

**32.** All the ornaments on their bodies they gave to the maidservants
and entered the water, saying, "We shall defeat Krishna!"

**33.** The sakhis devoted to service, remaining on the bank,
fill their eyes with the beauty of the forms of the Two.

**34.** Sri Rupa Manjari and Lavanga Manjari,
Sri Guna Manjari and Sri Rati Manjari,

**35.** Sri Rasa Manjari and Vilasa Manjari,
Sri Manjulali and all the other manjaris —

**36.** all of them, standing behind, watch
and calmly behold the lila.

**37.** All together they girded their garments tightly at the waist
and bound their hair very firmly.

**38.** When the battle began,
They began to speak with great boasting.

**39.** And as soon as the water-battle began,
(Radha) unnoticed splashed water in Sri Krishna's face.

**40.** What movements of Her body, what swaying of Her waist!
What movements of Her hands, what play of Her brows!

**41.** What grace of motion, what gait!
Immersed, She showers Him with water without end.

**42.** What wondrous movement of Her breast,
what sweetness in the slight turn of Her neck!

**43.** Every now and then, the play of brows and waves of words,
and waves of Her voice surpassing the ocean of nectar.

**44.** Then fair-faced Radha with Her sakhis
splashes water, aiming at Krishna's eyes.

**45.** What hundreds of endless ruses therein!
What movement of the water — the limit of expertise!

**46.** All shower Him with water, joyful at heart,
as the clouds of Shravana pour down rain.

**47.** A smile on Her lips — in it an ocean of loveliness:
the moon Krishna sank in the ocean of nectar.

**48.** Now they fight in water up to the knees, now up to the waist,
now up to the breast, now up to the neck.[^4]

**49.** Now they fight face to face, now breast to breast,
now eye to eye, now nail to nail.

**50.** Now it is a battle of words, now hand to hand;
all are spent in the play, intoxicated with bliss.

**51.** So the water-battle grew boundlessly:
with daring they drive the water at each other.

**52.** Then Krishna stole the garments of all;
in the clear water of the Yamuna their bodies are seen.

**53.** What grace of limbs, what waves of loveliness!
Bliss grows in the heart, waves of happiness.

**54.** This water-pastime is fathomless and boundless:
how can a living being of small intelligence comprehend it?

**55.** It is described at length in the *Sri Govinda-lilamrita*:
Kaviraja Gosani depicted it there.[^5]

**56.** Helpless with bliss, Radha forgot Herself,
and at that moment the ornament of Her nose slipped off.[^6]

**57.** Having finished the pastime, all came up on the bank;
the sakhis devoted to service rejoiced.

**58.** Dressing each in her own garments and ornaments,
they joyfully admire the beauty of their forms.

**59.** Then the happy Sweet-faced One with the sakhis
entered the bower-house together with Krishna.

**60.** At once all the foods prepared by Vrinda were brought;
seeing the fare, all rejoiced at heart.

**61.** Arranging various fruits,
(Radha), seeing the fare, was immersed in bliss.

**62.** How many kinds of sweets and various dishes!
Both taste them, immersed in bliss.

**63.** The sakhis devoted to service serve:
each performs her own service.

**64.** Seeing the sweetness of the forms of the Two,
the sakhis, gazing on Their beauty, forget themselves.

**65.** What essence of loveliness the Creator has made!
What sweetness — an ocean of nectar, the limit of beauty!

**66.** With what, brothers, can I compare this beauty,
this grace of form — the limit of sweetness?

**67.** I look for a comparison — there is none:
the beauty of Their holy forms is comparable only to itself.

**68.** The Creator kneaded it from the essence of nectar;
millions of moons are offered up to the beauty of Their faces.

**69.** Then, gazing at Radha's moon-like face,
they see: the nose is bare — where is the nose-ornament?

**70.** "Has it fallen into the water during the play?" —
all were troubled about the ornament.

**71.** They all took counsel with one another;
their hearts were anxious about the nose-ring.

**72.** Then Sri Rupa Manjari speaks by a sign,
casting a glance at Sri Guna Manjari.

**73.** Sri Guna Manjari, making a sign,
speaks with pleasure to Mani Manjari:[^7]

**74.** "You, dear one, are virtuous and know Radha's heart;
how many times you have brought Radha's ornaments!

**75.** Sometimes the play is in the water of the kunda, sometimes in water up to the breast,
sometimes the play is by day, sometimes at night —

**76.** so many times you have brought back ornaments.
Now find it and bring it — this is my word."

**77.** Then Mani Manjari, receiving the order,
joyfully went to search.

**78.** Coming to the bank of the Yamuna, she looked;
not finding it on the bank, she entered the water.

**79.** She gazes into the clear water of the Yamuna,
but cannot see the nose-ornament.

**80.** The water shines like a mirror,
the rays of the sun sparkle in it.

**81.** She searched a long time but could not find it,
and, not finding it, was pained at heart.

**82.** During the play in the water the Two had fought many battles —
both most expert, both most discerning.

**83.** In the Yamuna are enchanting footprints,
and among them the nose-ring had fallen.

**84.** Covered by a lotus leaf, it was not noticed;
not finding the ornament, she became anxious.

**85.** White sand and a white leaf
had covered it — that is why it was not seen.

**86.** Thus, having searched for a long time,
she reflected with a sorrowful heart.

**87.** Meanwhile the two Sri Ishvaris, looking at Prabhu,
began to speak in great anxiety.

**88.** From the first prahara of the day until evening
so much time has passed, and Prabhu's meditation does not end.

**89.** They saw that his whole body had grown stiff,
and both recalled the states of Mahaprabhu.

**90.** There is no breathing in or out, the belly does not move;
seeing this, both lost heart.

**91.** How loudly they shouted into his ears —
but he does not regain consciousness, even hearing the cries of "Hari!"

**92.** When the first prahara of the night had passed in this way,
the Ishvaris' grief grew in their hearts.

**93.** How many forebodings rose in their minds:
"Surely now the Creator has become merciless to me!"

**94.** They beat their breasts and roll on the ground;
"What have you done, Creator!" they cry, "Alas, alas!"

**95.** Now, calming themselves for a moment and taking heart,
both fan him very gently with the end of their cloth.

**96.** Hearing that Prabhu does not come out of his meditation, the king
came quickly, in great haste.

**97.** The king came to Prabhu's house with an anxious heart
and many times prostrated himself on the ground with eight limbs.

**98.** Then the king saw an even deeper state;
seeing it, the king grew sorrowful at heart.

**99.** Such an expression of bhava he had never heard of;
he holds a finger to his nose and cries, "Alas, alas!"

**100.** The king came and sat near the Thakurani
and began to question the two Srimatis.

**101.** The Thakurani says, "Listen, I shall tell you,"
and began to describe his state to him:

**102.** "He sat down to meditate in the first prahara of the day…"
From the lips of Srimati the king learned everything.

**103.** The king, in great anxiety, looks for a remedy;
sighing deeply, the king cries, "Alas, alas!"

**104.** At that moment Sri Vallavi Kaviraja came
and bowed to the Ishvari, prostrate on the ground.

**105.** Then Sri Vyasacharya, Sri Krishnavallabha,
Janaki Dasa and Prasada Dasa all came.

[^1]: Ishvari and Gauranga-priya, Srinivasa's wives.
[^2]: Sri Vamshivadana is the Deity of Krishna served by Srinivasa; according to the editor's note, this Deity is now at Budhuipara, in the temple of Radha-Madhava.
[^3]: *Siddha-deha*: the spiritual form of a maidservant (manjari) in which the raganuga devotee, by the guru's mercy, serves Radha and Krishna (cf. 1.338). Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati stressed that such descriptions of intimate pastimes are for the pure in heart and must not be understood in a mundane way: this is the lila of Bhagavan Himself and His internal energy.
[^4]: *Kanthadaghna*, "up to the neck" (the editor explains the form by the grammar of the *Mugdhabodha*).
[^5]: The *Govinda-lilamrita* of Krishnadasa Kaviraja (canto 15, the water-pastimes in Radha-kunda).
[^6]: *Beshar* (*vesar*): a pendant nose-ring; below it is also called the "nose-ornament".
[^7]: Guna Manjari is the spiritual form of Gopala Bhatta Gosvami, Mani Manjari that of Srinivasa (cf. the *Prema-vilasa* glossary). Rupa Manjari signs to Guna Manjari, and she to her follower Mani Manjari: thus the succession of service is observed.
