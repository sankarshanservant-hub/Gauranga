# Niryasa 3. The Glory of Ramachandra Kaviraja: Srinivasa's Samadhi and the Lost Ornament

*Hemalata's account: in Vishnupura Srinivasa, seated between his two wives, enters meditation and in his siddha-deha beholds the water-pastimes of Radha and Krishna in the Yamuna*

**1.** Glory, glory to Sri Krishna Chaitanya and Nityananda!
Glory to Advaita-chandra, glory to Gaura's devotees!

**2.** I shall tell another narrative — listen attentively;
I shall tell something confidential — fill your ears with it.

**3.** By hearing it bliss arises in the heart;
what can I, most unworthy, say of it?

**4.** Listen, listen, devotees, to the greatness of Ramachandra,
by singing whose virtues prema arises in the heart.

**5.** One day my mistress, Srila Hemalata,
graciously began to tell me this.

**6.** What I heard from the lips of Srimati
filled my heart with joy.

**7.** Touching with my ears the ocean of Sri Ramachandra's greatness,
I floated in bliss, in great jubilation.

**8.** Prabhu and Ramachandra are as if one body;
his heart is deep, he is a great and composed devotee.

**9.** What sweet beauty, what sweetness of character!
All the virtues I heard of are wondrous.

**10.** Sri Ramachandra Kaviraja knows Prabhu's heart —
this is manifest to the whole world.

**11.** Sri Ramachandra's deeds are renowned in the world,
his gentleness and depth are famous throughout the universe.

**12.** Of this I shall tell a little, making it known,
touching only one particle for my own purification.

**13.** One day, in the house at Vana-Vishnupura,
Prabhu was seated with a joyful heart.

**14.** The two Ishvaris[^1] sit on either side of him
and blissfully gaze on Prabhu's form.

**15.** Both greatly praised their good fortune:
"Such lotus feet of Prabhu we have obtained by great fortune!"

**16.** Then Prabhu speaks of Krishna in supreme bliss,
and hearing it, the Ishvaris' joy increased.

**17.** Thus for some time, in the rasa of talk about Krishna,
Prabhu was immersed in great jubilation of prema.

**18.** His mind, overflowing with bhava, cannot grow calm;
tears, trembling and gooseflesh pervade his body.

**19.** Now he roars and rolls on the ground,
now he cries out and calls aloud.

**20.** Saying "Sri Gauranga-chandra", he faints again and again;
helpless in transport, he cries, "Alas, alas!"

**21.** Now he calls aloud, "Sri Rupa, Sanatana!"
Saying "Sri Bhatta Gosani", he floats in the happiness of prema.

**22.** When some time had passed in this way,
in talk of other things Prabhu's mind grew calm.

**23.** Then, after a while, he bathed,
put on white cloth and sat down on his seat.

**24.** Applying tilaka to his forehead and the letters of the holy names to his body,
Prabhu recites hymns in a beautiful voice.

**25.** How the sound of his voice surpasses the kokila!
Joyful at heart, Prabhu recites the hymns.

**26.** With a joyful heart, seated on his seat,
Prabhu carefully serves Sri Vamshivadana.[^2]

**27.** Having performed the service with sandal and tulasi
and offered it, Prabhu sat down to meditate.

**28.** Fixing his mind in the siddha-deha of his cherished service,
he beholds the wondrous sweetness of the pastimes of Radha and Krishna.[^3]

**29.** He beholds the water-pastimes of Radha and Krishna,
and seeing that lila his mind is immersed in happiness.

**30.** In the Yamuna, skilfully arranging the water-sport,
They advanced against each other in a water-battle.

**31.** All the gopis surrounded Krishnachandra —
as though a host of lightning-flashes surrounded a cloud.

**32.** All the ornaments on their bodies they gave to the maidservants
and entered the water, saying, "We shall defeat Krishna!"

**33.** The sakhis devoted to service, remaining on the bank,
fill their eyes with the beauty of the forms of the Two.

**34.** Sri Rupa Manjari and Lavanga Manjari,
Sri Guna Manjari and Sri Rati Manjari,

**35.** Sri Rasa Manjari and Vilasa Manjari,
Sri Manjulali and all the other manjaris —

**36.** all of them, standing behind, watch
and calmly behold the lila.

**37.** All together they girded their garments tightly at the waist
and bound their hair very firmly.

**38.** When the battle began,
They began to speak with great boasting.

**39.** And as soon as the water-battle began,
(Radha) unnoticed splashed water in Sri Krishna's face.

**40.** What movements of Her body, what swaying of Her waist!
What movements of Her hands, what play of Her brows!

**41.** What grace of motion, what gait!
Immersed, She showers Him with water without end.

**42.** What wondrous movement of Her breast,
what sweetness in the slight turn of Her neck!

**43.** Every now and then, the play of brows and waves of words,
and waves of Her voice surpassing the ocean of nectar.

**44.** Then fair-faced Radha with Her sakhis
splashes water, aiming at Krishna's eyes.

**45.** What hundreds of endless ruses therein!
What movement of the water — the limit of expertise!

**46.** All shower Him with water, joyful at heart,
as the clouds of Shravana pour down rain.

**47.** A smile on Her lips — in it an ocean of loveliness:
the moon Krishna sank in the ocean of nectar.

**48.** Now they fight in water up to the knees, now up to the waist,
now up to the breast, now up to the neck.[^4]

**49.** Now they fight face to face, now breast to breast,
now eye to eye, now nail to nail.

**50.** Now it is a battle of words, now hand to hand;
all are spent in the play, intoxicated with bliss.

**51.** So the water-battle grew boundlessly:
with daring they drive the water at each other.

**52.** Then Krishna stole the garments of all;
in the clear water of the Yamuna their bodies are seen.

**53.** What grace of limbs, what waves of loveliness!
Bliss grows in the heart, waves of happiness.

**54.** This water-pastime is fathomless and boundless:
how can a living being of small intelligence comprehend it?

**55.** It is described at length in the *Sri Govinda-lilamrita*:
Kaviraja Gosani depicted it there.[^5]

**56.** Helpless with bliss, Radha forgot Herself,
and at that moment the ornament of Her nose slipped off.[^6]

**57.** Having finished the pastime, all came up on the bank;
the sakhis devoted to service rejoiced.

**58.** Dressing each in her own garments and ornaments,
they joyfully admire the beauty of their forms.

**59.** Then the happy Sweet-faced One with the sakhis
entered the bower-house together with Krishna.

**60.** At once all the foods prepared by Vrinda were brought;
seeing the fare, all rejoiced at heart.

**61.** Arranging various fruits,
(Radha), seeing the fare, was immersed in bliss.

**62.** How many kinds of sweets and various dishes!
Both taste them, immersed in bliss.

**63.** The sakhis devoted to service serve:
each performs her own service.

**64.** Seeing the sweetness of the forms of the Two,
the sakhis, gazing on Their beauty, forget themselves.

**65.** What essence of loveliness the Creator has made!
What sweetness — an ocean of nectar, the limit of beauty!

**66.** With what, brothers, can I compare this beauty,
this grace of form — the limit of sweetness?

**67.** I look for a comparison — there is none:
the beauty of Their holy forms is comparable only to itself.

**68.** The Creator kneaded it from the essence of nectar;
millions of moons are offered up to the beauty of Their faces.

**69.** Then, gazing at Radha's moon-like face,
they see: the nose is bare — where is the nose-ornament?

**70.** "Has it fallen into the water during the play?" —
all were troubled about the ornament.

**71.** They all took counsel with one another;
their hearts were anxious about the nose-ring.

**72.** Then Sri Rupa Manjari speaks by a sign,
casting a glance at Sri Guna Manjari.

**73.** Sri Guna Manjari, making a sign,
speaks with pleasure to Mani Manjari:[^7]

**74.** "You, dear one, are virtuous and know Radha's heart;
how many times you have brought Radha's ornaments!

**75.** Sometimes the play is in the water of the kunda, sometimes in water up to the breast,
sometimes the play is by day, sometimes at night —

**76.** so many times you have brought back ornaments.
Now find it and bring it — this is my word."

**77.** Then Mani Manjari, receiving the order,
joyfully went to search.

**78.** Coming to the bank of the Yamuna, she looked;
not finding it on the bank, she entered the water.

**79.** She gazes into the clear water of the Yamuna,
but cannot see the nose-ornament.

**80.** The water shines like a mirror,
the rays of the sun sparkle in it.

**81.** She searched a long time but could not find it,
and, not finding it, was pained at heart.

**82.** During the play in the water the Two had fought many battles —
both most expert, both most discerning.

**83.** In the Yamuna are enchanting footprints,
and among them the nose-ring had fallen.

**84.** Covered by a lotus leaf, it was not noticed;
not finding the ornament, she became anxious.

**85.** White sand and a white leaf
had covered it — that is why it was not seen.

**86.** Thus, having searched for a long time,
she reflected with a sorrowful heart.

**87.** Meanwhile the two Sri Ishvaris, looking at Prabhu,
began to speak in great anxiety.

**88.** From the first prahara of the day until evening
so much time has passed, and Prabhu's meditation does not end.

**89.** They saw that his whole body had grown stiff,
and both recalled the states of Mahaprabhu.

**90.** There is no breathing in or out, the belly does not move;
seeing this, both lost heart.

**91.** How loudly they shouted into his ears —
but he does not regain consciousness, even hearing the cries of "Hari!"

**92.** When the first prahara of the night had passed in this way,
the Ishvaris' grief grew in their hearts.

**93.** How many forebodings rose in their minds:
"Surely now the Creator has become merciless to me!"

**94.** They beat their breasts and roll on the ground;
"What have you done, Creator!" they cry, "Alas, alas!"

**95.** Now, calming themselves for a moment and taking heart,
both fan him very gently with the end of their cloth.

**96.** Hearing that Prabhu does not come out of his meditation, the king
came quickly, in great haste.

**97.** The king came to Prabhu's house with an anxious heart
and many times prostrated himself on the ground with eight limbs.

**98.** Then the king saw an even deeper state;
seeing it, the king grew sorrowful at heart.

**99.** Such an expression of bhava he had never heard of;
he holds a finger to his nose and cries, "Alas, alas!"

**100.** The king came and sat near the Thakurani
and began to question the two Srimatis.

**101.** The Thakurani says, "Listen, I shall tell you,"
and began to describe his state to him:

**102.** "He sat down to meditate in the first prahara of the day…"
From the lips of Srimati the king learned everything.

**103.** The king, in great anxiety, looks for a remedy;
sighing deeply, the king cries, "Alas, alas!"

**104.** At that moment Sri Vallavi Kaviraja came
and bowed to the Ishvari, prostrate on the ground.

**105.** Then Sri Vyasacharya, Sri Krishnavallabha,
Janaki Dasa and Prasada Dasa all came.

**106.** Seeing Prabhu, all were saddened
and, hanging their heads, began to ponder.

**107.** They tried in every way — he does not regain consciousness;
seeing that the meditation does not break, they lost heart.

**108.** The third prahara of the night passed;
crying "Alas, alas!", they wept much:

**109.** "Alas, cruel Creator, what have you done!" —
they beat their breasts, rolling on the ground.

**110.** "Now the Creator has become cruel to me!" —
crying "Alas, alas!", how they weep.

**111.** Then Prabhu's devotees gathered together
and began to speak in great anxiety:

**112.** "Listen, listen, Thakurani! Calm your heart:
our Prabhu is absorbed in bhava — he will regain consciousness."

**113.** Both grew a little calmer, restraining their grief,
and, taking heart, sat down beside Prabhu.

**114.** All together they reflect in their minds:
by no means does Prabhu's meditation break.

**115.** So the night passed and day came;
endlessly they wondered how to break the meditation.

**116.** The king and all Prabhu's devotees
reflect with sorrowful hearts.

**117.** Thus they fell into many thoughts —
the third prahara of the day began.

**118.** Still there is no movement; grief is in their hearts,
and in their minds unceasing forebodings:

**119.** "Alas, alas, what shall we do, where shall we go?
Where shall we find such a treasure of virtues?"

**120.** Pained at heart, all lament:
"Surely the cruel Creator has brought calamity."

**121.** So that day too passed,
and the third day began.

**122.** Loud wailing arose;
falling, they dash themselves on the ground.

**123.** Restraining herself and taking heart, the Thakurani
held cotton to his nose and watches.

**124.** When she saw that the cotton at his nose did not stir,
she tore her hair and fell, striking the ground.

**125.** Rolling on the ground, she cries, "Alas, alas!";
she beats her breast and weeps aloud.

**126.** Now she rises, now falls, now loses consciousness,
now wails and weeps.

**127.** Thus they began to lament;
all were distraught and lost their strength:

**128.** "Ah, ah, pitiless, cruel Creator!
Why have you taken away the limit of our happiness?

**129.** Having given the treasure of mercy, why have you taken it away?
Having given a great jewel, you have snatched it back!"

**130.** Then Srimati-jiu reflects within herself,
and as she reflects, she remembers a certain word.

**131.** Her heart brightened, her face brightened,
and she began to speak with joy.

**132.** The devotees all together entreat her:
"Tell us, tell us, Thakurani, what is in your mind?"

**133.** The king and all came closer:
"Tell us and calm us — we are in distress."

**134.** Then Srimati speaks with joy:
"Listen gladly, all you devotees.

**135.** What I heard earlier from Prabhu's lips,
all that has now come back to my mind.

**136.** Ramachandra Kaviraja knows the truth about Prabhu;
no one else knows the thoughts of Prabhu's heart.

**137.** If he comes, then there will be joy."
And she began to tell, very softly:

**138.** The Thakurani says: "Listen. One day Prabhu
was speaking of the Kaviraja's virtues:

**139.** 'He is the limit of composure, his bhajana is profound;
that great and steadfast one knows my mind.

**140.** All the movements of my heart the Kaviraja knows;
I dreamt that the Kaviraja would come today.'

**141.** This he said again and again with joy —
and just then Ramachandra arrived in supreme bliss.

**142.** Seeing Prabhu, he fell to the ground and bowed,
and with joined palms offered many praises.

**143.** Prabhu rose and embraced him
and began to ask about his welfare.

**144.** The Kaviraja says: 'Without the sight of you,
without the sight of your feet, what welfare can there be?

**145.** Now good fortune has come — I have obtained your sight;
I have attained my goal, my life is fruitful.'

**146.** Taking the Kaviraja by the hand, Prabhu
joyfully seated him beside him.

**147.** Some time passed in talk of Krishna;
at the sight of each other the joy of both increased.

**148.** Then after a while both bathed
and, reciting hymns, came back.

**149.** Now he calls aloud, 'Gaurachandra!',
now, saying 'Rupa, Sanatana!', sheds tears;

**150.** now, saying 'Sri Bhatta Gosani!', cries out;
every now and then he exclaims, 'Radha-Govinda!'

**151.** Thus Prabhu came back after bathing,
went to Sri Vamshivadana and bowed down;

**152.** he changed his cloth, applied tilaka,
and calls again and again, 'Sri Kunda! Govardhana!'

**153.** Then, joyful, performing his daily duties,
he gladly went to water Tulasi.

**154.** Then, having carefully served the shalagrama,
he offered many sweets and other things.

**155.** Offering mukhavasa,[^8] he performed arati;
coming out into the courtyard, he bowed many times.

**156.** Returning to the house, Prabhu honoured prasada
and with great mercy gave the remnants to the Kaviraja.

**157.** Then both sat in great bliss —
to whom can I tell those wondrous conversations?

**158.** Then the two of us cooked:
with joy we prepared various dishes of rice and vegetables.

**159.** When the cooking was ready, we told Prabhu;
he brought the shalagrama and offered Him the meal,

**160.** took Him back into the temple and laid Him to rest,
and then fanned Him very gently.

**161.** Then Prabhu came out into the courtyard
and bowed many times, prostrate on the ground.

**162.** With joy he looks upon the assembly of Vaishnavas
and, seeing the beauty of the Vaishnavas, rejoices at heart.

**163.** Then Prabhu addressed the Vaishnavas,
inviting them to honour prasada.

**164.** All the Vaishnavas said, "As you command."
Receiving their consent, Prabhu was boundlessly glad.

**165.** With a joyful heart he had them bathe,
and the Vaishnavas came and sat down to eat.

**166.** The Vaishnavas sat in rows;
seeing them all, Prabhu forgot himself.

**167.** Prabhu himself began to serve the food,
and I brought the platters of rice and vegetables.

**168.** The Vaishnavas ate their fill, up to the throat;
Prabhu asks, "Do you need anything more?"

**169.** "We need nothing more — hear, treasury of mercy:
we have received prasada, the limit of our good fortune."

**170.** Having finished the meal, they rinsed their mouths,
refreshed them and sat in their places.

**171.** Then Prabhu came into the house
and, seeing the Kaviraja, was immersed in bliss.

**172.** Then we two, having bathed,
laid a woollen cloth on the seat.

**173.** Prabhu came and sat down to eat,
and we two served.

**174.** I asked, 'Let the Kaviraja sit down to eat.'
Prabhu said, 'He will honour prasada afterwards.'

**175.** Saying this, Prabhu takes prasada with a joyful heart,
while Ramachandra sits and fans him.

**176.** Having finished his meal, Prabhu rose
and ordered, 'Ramachandra, now you eat.'

**177.** Rinsing his mouth, Prabhu sat down right there,
and the Kaviraja got up to eat.

**178.** On Prabhu's seat, from his plate,
from his vegetable bowls and his water-pot —

**179.** seated, he joyfully takes prasada,
accepting it on his head as Prabhu's order.

**180.** As he ate, so many bhavas arose in him:
his body was covered with gooseflesh, tears flowed from his eyes.

**181.** Thus the Kaviraja ate everything,
rinsed his mouth and sat beside Prabhu.

**182.** I went and took (Prabhu's) chewed betel.[^9]

**183.** Prabhu went to his bed
and lay down, and Ramachandra massages his feet.

**184.** Then, having lain for a while,
Prabhu rose, uttering the name of Hari.

**185.** Then we, finding Prabhu alone,
humbly addressed Prabhu's feet:

**186.** 'Prabhu, you constantly praise the Kaviraja,
but such a person and such conduct we have never seen:

**187.** the guru's seat, his eating plate,
his vegetable bowls and his water-pot —

**188.** how could he sit down and eat from them?'
So we told Prabhu the doubt in our hearts.

**189.** Prabhu said, 'Ramachandra is an ocean of virtues;
his mind is beyond your reach.

**190.** Later you will understand it — listen attentively:
you will see it with your own eyes.'

**191.** Joyfully accepting Prabhu's order on his head,
(the Kaviraja) took the chewed betel and ate it.

**192.** The next day Prabhu, together with Ramachandra,
came (into the courtyard), and both rejoiced.

**193.** Coming into the courtyard, they stroll together;
with the Kaviraja he strolls, joyful:

**194.** Prabhu walks in front, the Kaviraja behind;
holding hands, both walk about the courtyard.

**195.** In the courtyard lay a straw ring (bad),[^10]
and Prabhu spoke as if in fright.

**196.** Leaping back, Prabhu cried, 'A snake!
Look well, Kaviraja — a snake!'

**197.** The Kaviraja says, 'Prabhu, it is a snake!
I have seen it, seen it, Prabhu — it certainly is!'

**198.** Then, after walking about for a while,
(Prabhu says:) 'It is not a snake, it is a straw ring — I have looked closely.'

**199.** The Kaviraja says, 'That is true, Prabhu:
it is a ring; it was never a snake.'

**200.** And we sit and watch this;
seeing the beauty of the forms of the two, our eyes are soothed.

**201.** Thus both, rejoicing,
went into the house and sat down.

**202.** Then we two together began to reflect
and understood: Ramachandra is a treasury of virtues.

**203.** Later, when we were alone,
Prabhu came there

**204.** and, coming, spoke sweetly:
'Listen, listen, both of you, I shall explain in detail.

**205.** Now you have seen Ramachandra's virtue with your own eyes;
I shall give you an example of it — listen attentively.

**206.** Once Dronacharya, gathering all his disciples,
sat joyfully and taught them the use of weapons —

**207.** Duryodhana and all his brothers,
Yudhishthira and all five brothers.

**208.** Having taught them all the use of weapons for many days,
he said to all with a smile, "Today I shall test you."

**209.** Saying this, on a very tall tree
he placed a bird.

**210.** One by one the teacher calls them all:
"Aiming at the bird's eye, shoot your arrow:

**211.** shoot an arrow into one eye so that it comes out through the other,"
so the teacher says to all.

**212.** Duryodhana and all his brothers
came with bows and arrows, glad at heart;

**213.** one by one they all, taking bows and arrows,
came forward with drawn bows to pierce (the bird).

**214.** When they had set the arrow on the bow,
Drona calls to them: "What do you see?"

**215.** Bow and arrow in hand, the disciples say:
"I see the tree, I see the branches."

**216.** Then Drona answers in anger:
"Go and sit down and stay there with your bow and arrows!"

**217.** Thus he tested them all:
"You have not yet learned the use of the bow."

**218.** Last of all Drona called Arjuna;
the hero came at once, drawing his bow.

**219.** Bowing to his teacher, the hero took his bow
and went joyfully to pierce (the bird).

**220.** (Drona) calls to the hero Arjuna:
"What do you see? Tell me, pure-minded one."

**221.** Arjuna says: "Teacher, I see only the bird;
and now I do not see the bird — I see only the eye."

**222.** Drona says: "Draw your bow and shoot:
taking aim, pierce the bird's eye with your arrow."

**223.** Then the hero Arjuna released the arrow:
piercing one eye, the arrow came out through the other.

**224.** "Well done, well done!" Drona cries,
and, looking at all the disciples, says:

**225.** "The hero does not see the tree — he sees only the bird;
and no longer sees the bird — he sees only the eye.

**226.** What I told him, that is what he is able to see;
he will not see the tree — what concern is the tree of his?"

**227.** Then Arjuna, bowing again to his teacher,
went and sat down among the disciples.

**228.** Dronacharya's heart was filled with bliss,
and again and again he repeats:

**229.** "You are in every way equal to me;
such a wondrous deed I have seen in no one.

**230.** You are dearer to me than all my disciples —
this is certain; I have told you the very essence."

**231.** Hearing this, Duryodhana grew sad at heart:
the prince was grieved and brooded within himself.'

**232.** Saying this, Prabhu was glad at heart:
'Understand in your hearts how the virtues of Ramachandra are sung:

**233.** what I told him, that he did without hesitation:
obedient to the order in every way, he ate.

**234.** And see further: there was a straw ring in the courtyard;
I said "snake" — and he took it for a snake.

**235.** Again I said, "It is not a snake, it is a ring" —
and the Kaviraja says, "It is certainly a ring."

**236.** Both of you understand this attentively,'
so Prabhu began to speak with joy.

**237.** 'Our doubt is dispelled — now explain in detail;[^11]
by Prabhu's mercy my doubt has been resolved.

**238.** Without your mercy how could we have understood this?
Now we have grasped it with our hearts.'

**239.** Prabhu says: 'From today you are fortunate:
you have seen and heard the virtues of Ramachandra.

**240.** As Phalguni[^12] was among Dronacharya's disciples,
so, understand, is Ramachandra.

**241.** Ramachandra is an ocean of virtues, his greatness boundless;
I have told you the very essence.

**242.** Whoever in my community follows Ramachandra
will become great in my community.

**243.** Ramachandra and Narottama are a pair of eyes:
without eyes the whole body is useless.

**244.** As Ramachandra is in virtue, so is Narottama;
there is no difference between them — both are equal.

**245.** The inner heart of the two is known to Kaviraja Govinda,
and Govinda Chakravarti too knows it.

**246.** Whoever follows Ramachandra
will attain the pastimes of Radha and Krishna beyond (this world);

**247.** among the group of manjaris, on the path of parakiya,
he will surely attain the abode of Vrindavana.

**248.** Hear this with your hearts:
I have told you this with certainty.'

**249.** Speaking thus, Prabhu felt great happiness:
he sang the virtues of Ramachandra as if with five mouths.

**250.** Thus Prabhu explained much,
and we listened, pricking up both our ears."

**251.** As she was telling this to the devotees, the Thakurani
recalled another wondrous account:

**252.** "Hear it with one mind,
hear it with deep faith and care.

**253.** Hearing such a wondrous account is auspicious;
it is a most pure and spotless account.

**254.** Once, earlier, Prabhu was taking his meal,
and on his right and left two were seated:

**255.** on one side Ramachandra, on the other Narottama;
most charmingly the three take their meal.

**256.** I cannot describe the joy of that meal;
seeing it, we forgot ourselves.

**257.** Absorbed in the rasa of talk about Krishna, with joy in his heart,
(Prabhu) himself serves prasada to the two;

**258.** again and again he serves them vegetables,
while we, standing aside, watch.

**259.** 'How can the one who is served serve his servants?' —
the doubt in my heart grew.

**260.** Then, when all had finished the meal,
they rinsed their mouths in great joy.

**261.** Then the three came and sat in seclusion
and began to speak of Krishna's deeds.

**262.** As they spoke of Krishna,
all three grew limp with bliss, their bodies glowing.

**263.** Their hearts, overflowing with prema, cannot grow calm;
their bodies are covered with gooseflesh, tears flow from their eyes.

**264.** How many more expressions of prema arose in them!
I know no limit to the hundreds of bhavas in them.

**265.** Thus for some time, in talk of Krishna,
waves of happiness rolled through them.

**266.** Then, finding an opportunity,
we humbly asked Prabhu.

[^1]: Ishvari and Gauranga-priya, Srinivasa's wives.
[^2]: Sri Vamshivadana is the Deity of Krishna served by Srinivasa; according to the editor's note, this Deity is now at Budhuipara, in the temple of Radha-Madhava.
[^3]: *Siddha-deha*: the spiritual form of a maidservant (manjari) in which the raganuga devotee, by the guru's mercy, serves Radha and Krishna (cf. 1.338). Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati stressed that such descriptions of intimate pastimes are for the pure in heart and must not be understood in a mundane way: this is the lila of Bhagavan Himself and His internal energy.
[^4]: *Kanthadaghna*, "up to the neck" (the editor explains the form by the grammar of the *Mugdhabodha*).
[^5]: The *Govinda-lilamrita* of Krishnadasa Kaviraja (canto 15, the water-pastimes in Radha-kunda).
[^6]: *Beshar* (*vesar*): a pendant nose-ring; below it is also called the "nose-ornament".
[^7]: Guna Manjari is the spiritual form of Gopala Bhatta Gosvami, Mani Manjari that of Srinivasa (cf. the *Prema-vilasa* glossary). Rupa Manjari signs to Guna Manjari, and she to her follower Mani Manjari: thus the succession of service is observed.
[^8]: *Mukhavasa*: refreshing spices (betel etc.) offered to the Deity after the meal.
[^9]: The remnants of betel chewed by the guru are a special prasada; below (191) Ramachandra partakes of it. Couplet 182 is a single half-line in the edition (without a rhyming partner).
[^10]: *Bad*: according to the editor's note, a straw vessel for storing grain or a straw ring used as a stand for a water-pot.
[^11]: The words of Srinivasa's wives (the change of speaker is not marked in the original).
[^12]: Phalguni is Arjuna (as the editor also notes).
