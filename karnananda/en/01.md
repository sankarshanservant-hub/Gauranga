# Niryasa 1. Srinivasa, the Wish-fulfilling Tree, and His Branches

*Opening verses and salutations; Mahaprabhu manifests two shaktis — Sri Rupa (the books) and Srinivasa (their spreading); Srinivasa as the wish-fulfilling tree and his branches; life in Yajigrama, the Gosvamis' books and his daily round; the order to describe Srinivasa's branches; Ramachandra Kaviraja at the pond in Yajigrama*

**1.** *Glory to Sri Sri Krishna-Chaitanya-chandra!*
*May Hari, the son of Shachi, radiant with a lustre lovelier than gold, ever be manifest in the cave of your heart — He who out of mercy descended in the age of Kali to bestow what had not been bestowed for a long time: the exalted, radiant rasa of service, the wealth of devotion to Himself.*[^1]

**2.** *Sri Krishna — Krishna Chaitanya, with Sanatana and Rupa, with Gopala and the Raghunathas, the beloved of Vraja — protect me!*

**3.** *I bow to Gopala Bhatta, whose heart is flooded with eternal prema (the prema of Sanatana), who is marked by friendship with Sri Rupa, whose only life is Radha-ramana, and who grants the desire of those who worship.*

**4.** *I worship Srinivasa Prabhu, dear to Radha-ramana, who established the scriptures of rasa and leads to the goal of parakiya.*[^2]

**5.** Glory, glory to Mahaprabhu, glory to the ocean of mercy!
Glory, glory to Nityananda, glory to the friend of the fallen!

**6.** Glory, glory to Advaita-chandra, the ocean of compassion!
Glory, glory to the Lord's associates headed by Srivasa!

**7.** Glory to Sri Rupa and Sanatana, embodiments of prema!
Glory to Sri Gopala Bhatta, the well of prema-bhakti!

**8.** Glory to Srila Raghunatha Bhatta — be merciful to me!
Glory to Raghunatha Dasa on the bank of Radha-kunda!

**9.** Glory, glory to Jiva Gosani, the treasury of compassion!
Glory to Sri Acharya Prabhu, the limit of all virtues!

**10.** Glory, glory to Ramachandra Kaviraja and Govinda:
the world rejoices in the rasa of their lives!

**11.** Glory to the Vaishnava Gosanis, purifiers of the fallen!
Be merciful, my masters: I take shelter of you.

**12.** Listen, listen, devotees, with one mind:
Mahaprabhu manifested two shaktis,

**13.** to fulfil the cherished desire of His heart
and for the joy of revealing it on earth.

**14.** Empowering Sri Rupa, He manifested the books,
and His heart rejoiced as that shakti was revealed.

**15.** Thus He manifested the greatest of great treasures —
the one through whom hundreds of thousands of verses appeared.[^3]

**16.** And so that this rare treasure might be made known,
He came and manifested shakti in Srinivasa.

**17.** Having manifested the two shaktis, He rejoiced at heart;
tasting this, the living beings became free.

**18.** To make that rare treasure known,
He came and spread it through the shakti in Srinivasa.

**19.** Such is my Acharya Thakura Srinivasa:
in the shelter of this wish-fulfilling tree the living beings' sufferings were removed.

**20.** Srinivasa descended in the form of a wish-fulfilling tree
and out of mercy delivered the living beings.

**21.** Ramachandra Kaviraja is a branch of that tree:
how could I write of his endless virtues?

**22.** Charming is the form of Ramachandra Kaviraja;
his virtues are to the world what the tree is.

**23.** His younger brother is most virtuous —
his name is Sri Govinda Kaviraja.

**24.** Another branch of the tree is named Govinda Chakravarti;
these three branches are the abode of every virtue.

**25.** Beginning with them, the branches of the tree
are endless and boundless — who could write of them?

**26.** Now I shall name the sub-branches of the tree:
Sri Balarama Kaviraja and the other sub-branches.

**27.** Its branches and sub-branches spread over the world;
from the sidelong glance of compassion the leaves unfolded.

**28.** Various blessed bhavas blossomed as flowers,
and the pure bhava of parakiya filled it with fragrance.

**29.** Thus the tree became most fragrant,
and on it ripened the fruit of pure prema-bhakti.

**30.** Listen, listen, devotees — this is my request:
water the tree with the water of hearing and the other forms of service.

**31.** Casting far away karma, jnana and the rest,
all of you taste the fruit, filling yourselves to the throat.

**32.** Srinivasa was adorned as the wish-fulfilling tree:
in Gauda he made manifest hundreds of thousands of verses.[^3]

**33.** All the books Sri Rupa Gosvami composed,
all the books Sanatana Gosvami revealed,

**34.** what Sri Bhatta Gosani (Gopala Bhatta) revealed,
and Raghunatha Bhatta and Raghunatha Dasa,

**35.** the collection of books written by Sri Jiva Gosvami,
and all the rasa-filled books the Kaviraja made[^4] —

**36.** taking all these books to Gauda, Prabhu
spread them freely, with joy in his heart.

**37.** Srinivasa, like the wind, brought the clouds of books —
he who carried them with great effort.

**38.** Bringing the clouds of books from among the hills of Vraja,
he watered the field of Gauda with the water of prema.

**39.** The living beings — crops scorched by the sun of Kali —
received life from the rain of Krishna-prema.

**40.** The rain-clouds of prema filled the earth,
and the devotee-peacocks dance, intoxicated, intoxicated.

**41.** When Prabhu took up residence in Yajigrama,
Vaishnavas came and gathered round him every day.

**42.** To them all he speaks of prema in bhakti-yoga;
he cured them all of the disease of jnana and karma.

**43.** So many days pass in the bliss of prema;
floating in the rasa of Krishna-prema, he sings, full of bhava.

**44.** At the request of the Vaishnavas he married,
and some days later he married again.[^5]

**45.** He studies the *Bhakti-rasamrita-sindhu* and the *Ujjvala*,
the *Vidagdha-madhava*, the *Lalita-madhava* and the rest;

**46.** the *Hari-bhakti-vilasa* and the *Bhagavatamrita*,
the commentary on the Tenth Canto and the *Dashama-charita*;

**47.** the *Mathura-mahatmya* and many collections of hymns,
the *Hamsa-duta*, the *Uddhava-sandesha* and the rest — all of them;

**48.** the *Six Sandarbhas*, the *Toshani* on the Tenth Canto of the *Bhagavata*;
the *Gitavali* and the *Virudavali* he reads in order;

**49.** the *Mukta-charitra* and the *Krishna-karnamrita*,
the *Brahma-samhita* and others, and the *Gopi-premamrita*.

**50.** How many names do I know — and the books run to hundreds of thousands of verses!
Unceasingly he studies the *Madhava-mahotsava* and others.[^6]

**51.** He read and expounded the books to the assembly of Vaishnavas,
immersed in the nectar of prema night and day.

**52.** Counting, he chants Hari-nama for one prahara;
another prahara passes in the study of the books.

**53.** Two yamas[^7] are kirtana of Radha, Krishna and Govinda;
unceasingly he floats in the prema of remembering the pastimes.

**54.** Chandidasa, Vidyapati, the *Sri Gita-govinda*,
Raya's drama[^8] — these books he sings in supreme bliss.

**55.** At night, with the devotees, the rasa and other pastimes;
he taught songs — the jubilation of bhakti and prema.

**56.** By day, service of the shalagrama and service of Tulasi:
with supreme devotion he waters her.

**57.** Meditation on Radha and Krishna, the mantra and the names of Them both —
thus he always remembers the pastimes and keeps them in mind.

**58.** Saying "Sri Rupa, Sanatana", he lets out a great cry;
saying "Sri Gopala Bhatta", he sobs aloud.

**59.** Saying "Radha-kunda, Krishna-kunda", he faints again and again;
saying "Mount Govardhana", he cries, "Alas, alas!"

**60.** Thus day and night pass in the bliss of prema,
and tasting the nectar of prema, he rejoices at heart.

**61.** The pious think it good, the impious laugh;
yet now all those people float in bliss.

**62.** He sings the glories of Gaura and of Prabhu Nityananda;
thus day and night compassion arises.

**63.** Now I shall tell of the branches of Sri Acharya Prabhu,
by merely remembering whose names prema is awakened.

**64.** The proof of this is a verse:
*I worship the great host of branches of Srila Srinivasa Prabhu: by merely remembering their names, Krishna-prema arises.*

**65.** All the branches of Sri Acharya Prabhu
two persons have described in verse:

**66.** what Thakura Mahashaya described,
and what Karnapura Kaviraja composed.[^9]

**67.** Following the verses of these two great souls,
my Prabhu ordered me to render them in payar.[^10]

**68.** I took Prabhu's order on my head, but many days passed;
and then, through the Vaishnavas, he ordered it again.

**69.** The order is stronger than I — so I describe this;
whether it is good or bad I cannot tell.

**70.** I am worthless and dull-witted: what do I know of description?
Forgive my fault, Prabhu — I take shelter.

**71.** The words of Prabhu's order and the Vaishnavas' command —
this I have understood especially in my heart.

**72.** What more can I say — I am the foremost of fools;
the Vaishnava Gosanis will forgive me everything.

**73.** Placing on my head the dust of the feet of you all,
I shall tell a little of this in payar.

**74.** Do not count it a fault if something is told out of order;
I bow to the feet of all and rejoice.

**75.** Now I shall tell of Prabhu's branches and sub-branches;
forgiving my offences, please listen.

**76.** One day, on the western side of his house,
he sat on the bank of the pond.

**77.** Just then someone came along riding in a palanquin:
he was on the road, newly married, and musicians were playing.

**78.** Seeing a form like Manmatha's, Prabhu thinks:
"Such wondrous beauty I have never seen!

**79.** His complexion is like a golden ketaki flower,
his broad chest most lovely;

**80.** his belly is handsome, with a line of fine hair;
his feet and hands are red;

**81.** his face is beautiful as the full moon,
his nose high and his teeth beautiful;

**82.** his lips are charming, surpassing the bimba fruit,
his lotus eyes shine enchantingly;

**83.** his neck like a conch, his waist slender, his hair curling,
his thighs like inverted plantain trunks, his knees well formed."

**84.** He wore silk, a garland of flowers on his neck,
sandal paste on his body. Seeing him, Prabhu asked:

**85.** "Who is he — Kamadeva or one of the Ashvini-kumaras?
Or some god, or the son of a gandharva?"

**86.** So, gazing at his form again and again,
Prabhu began to speak, and his mercy doubled:

**87.** "If, having received such a body, he worships Krishna,
then this body will bear fruit and not perish in vain.

**88.** Tell me, you who are with him, brothers:
in which village is his home, where does he live?

**89.** What is his caste and what is his name? Tell me in detail."
Bowing, they told him everything:

**90.** "This is Ramachandra Kaviraja, a great pandita:
who can equal him? He is like Vachaspati, famed like Sarasvati.

**91.** He was born in a worthy Vaidya family, foremost in fame,
a great physician; he is called 'Conqueror of the Quarters'.[^11]

**92.** His home is in Kumaranagara; his name is renowned."
Hearing this, Prabhu joyfully returned to his house.

**93.** All that Prabhu had said the Kaviraja heard attentively,
and having heard, went joyfully to his own home.

**94.** Being most composed, he gave no answer,
but began to meditate in his heart on Prabhu's feet.

**95.** Thus he passed the day at home with difficulty,
and at night came to Prabhu's door.

**96.** He passed the night with difficulty in a brahmana's house,
and in the morning came and fell at Prabhu's feet.

**97.** Weeping and weeping, he rolls on the ground,
lying on the earth like a tree torn up by the roots.

**98.** In a choked voice he says: "Give me the shade of your feet!
Do not spurn my scorched soul!"[^12]

**99.** Prabhu rose, lifted him by his creeper-like arms,
and joyfully, out of mercy, embraced him tightly.

**100.** "May you have Krishna-bhakti," he blessed him,
and, faltering with prema, began to speak:

**101.** "Birth after birth you are my friend and helper;
merciful Providence has brought you to me."

**102.** Saying this, he gave him the mantra of Radha-Krishna
and told him again and again the pastimes of Radha-Krishna.

**103.** In a few days he read the books with him,
and at last, blessing him, gave him this instruction:

**104.** "You are in every way my own self;
may you become full of prema by the mercy of Govinda.

**105.** In Vrindavana there was one like you:
Providence gave me that treasure — his name is Narottama.

**106.** For a long time we lived together;
by giving me you, the Merciful One has given me two eyes."

**107.** Thus out of mercy he instructed him
and brought him together with Narottama Thakura.

**108.** Between Narottama and Ramachandra prema grew —
such prema that they became one soul in two bodies.

**109.** Then Prabhu showed mercy to Sri Govinda Kaviraja:
he became a disciple, and Prabhu empowered him.

**110.** He is (Ramachandra's) younger brother, a great pandita;
both are maha-bhagavatas, their hearts full of prema.

**111.** To compose songs of the pastimes of Radha-Krishna in the manner of rasa verses
(Prabhu) ordered the Kaviraja with great mercy.[^13]

**112.** He composed his own verses and songs in many styles,
and the earth floated in the nectar of prema of those songs.

**113.** To the wives of the two Kavirajas
he showed mercy with a merciful heart.

**114.** Then Prabhu showed mercy to Divyasimha:[^14]
receiving Prabhu's mercy, he became most blessed.

**115.** Then to Prabhu's two virtuous wives
Prabhu, the jewel of virtues, showed mercy.

**116.** The elder is named Srimati Ishvari Thakurani:
what can I say of her incomparable virtues?

**117.** The younger is Srimati Gauranga-priya Thakurani:
how could I describe her life?

**118.** Both are most loving and virtuous,
both are expert (vidagdha), both a treasury of rasa.

**119.** I cannot describe the summit of their bhajana;
both are most composed and sweet in character.

**120.** Most dear to Prabhu, most virtuous,
both are the limit of expertise, sweet in form.

**121.** The bhajana of both is pure raganuga, exclusive;
the bhajana of both is wholly in the bhava of parakiya.[^15]

**122.** What can I say of the steadfast bhajana of both:
of karma, jnana and the like they will not even hear.

**123.** I, lowly and worthless — how could I explain it?
Both beloveds of Prabhu are equal to Prabhu himself.

**124.** Their disciples and disciples' disciples flooded the world;
further on I shall carefully tell of this.

**125.** The eldest son is named Sri Vrindavana Acharya:
Prabhu, the abode of virtues, showed him mercy.

**126.** Prabhu's middle son is Radhakrishna Acharya:
what can I say of his virtues — everything in him is wondrous.

**127.** Prabhu, the treasury of virtues, showed him mercy:
he is the wondrous limit of virtues.

**128.** The youngest son is named Sri Gati-Govinda:
Prabhu showed him mercy with a merciful heart.

**129.** Sri Gati-Govinda Prabhu wrote down the line
of his gurus in a verse of his own, eagerly and gladly.

**130.** This is the verse:
*The bee at the lotus feet of Sri Chaitanya is Gopala Bhatta Prabhu; the glorious bee at his lotus feet is the one named Sri Srinivasa, known to all people in all lands as Acharya Prabhu; and the one whose glory is the shelter of his lotus feet is called Gati-Govinda.*

**131.** He who took shelter at the lotus feet of Sri Krishna Chaitanya
and, becoming a bee, ever delights there —

**132.** Sri Gopala Bhatta Gosani, filled with mercy,
showed boundless mercy to Sri Acharya Prabhu.

**133.** The shelter of the lotus feet of Sri Acharya Prabhu —
of this Sri Gati-Govinda speaks in his verse.

**134.** He is a great giver, great and virtuous;
his disciples and disciples' disciples flooded the world.

**135.** All this I shall tell in detail later;
now I shall briefly tell of Prabhu's branches.

**136.** Then Prabhu's own daughter named Hemalata:
to her he graciously showed mercy.

**137.** She had many disciples and disciples' disciples;
she flooded the whole earth with the nectar of prema.

**138.** Another daughter is Krishnapriya Thakurani:
he, the jewel of mercy, gave her the shelter of his feet.

**139.** Another daughter, whose name is Kanchana-latika:
to her too the merciful one gave the shelter of his feet.

**140.** Then Prabhu showed mercy to Kanchanagaria:
coming there, he showed mercy to Sri Dasa Thakura.[^16]

**141.** He is a maha-bhagavata, a great pandita,
who always remained near Prabhu.

**142.** Jayakrishna, Jagadisha and Shyama-vallabha Acharya —
his three sons, great and noble in virtue.

**143.** These three worthies are vessels of the mercy of Sri Ishvara;[^17]
they are maha-bhagavatas, abodes of prema.

**144.** There too is his eldest, Sri Gokula Dasa:
the Thakura showed him mercy with great joy.

**145.** Carrying water on his head, he serves Krishna;
no one can understand his prema and its expressions.

**146.** To his son, Sri Krishnavallabha Thakura —
seeing how beautiful he was, Prabhu showed mercy.

**147.** He received mercy as a child;
he is a maha-bhagavata and made many disciples.

**148.** There too, to Sri Narasimha Kaviraja
Prabhu showed mercy: he gave him mantra, investing him with shakti.

**149.** He is a great pandita and meditates on Prabhu;
his prema, its expressions and his virtues cannot be understood.

**150.** He had many disciples and disciples' disciples.
Then Prabhu showed mercy to Raghunatha.

**151.** Ramakrishna Chattaraja is one of Prabhu's branches:
how can I write of his greatness and virtues?

**152.** Devoted to Hari-nama, he always takes Hari-nama:
counting, he chants the name always, without rest.

**153.** His son is Gopijanavallabha Chattaraja,
who is renowned in the world.

**154.** His love for Prabhu is great, and Prabhu is merciful to him;
I cannot describe his greatness.

**155.** To him, mercifully and graciously,
Prabhu gave in marriage his daughter Srila Hemalata.[^18]

**156.** Sri Kumuda Chattaraja is a dear servant of Prabhu:
apart from Prabhu's feet he has no other concern.

**157.** His son is Sri Chaitanya Chattaraja,
a vessel of Prabhu's mercy, king of great devotees.

**158.** To him Prabhu, filled with mercy, showed mercy
and gave him in marriage his daughter Srila Krishnapriya.

**159.** Rajendra Bandyopadhyaya, the Chattaraja's son-in-law —
to him too Prabhu graciously showed mercy.

**160.** I cannot write of his countless virtues:
he is always absorbed in the pastimes of Radha and Krishna.

**161.** His love for Prabhu is great, Prabhu is his life;
he constantly, without pause, chants Hari-nama.

**162.** The Chattaraja's two daughters are both virtuous,
of gentle form, both most pure and serene.

**163.** Then Prabhu showed mercy to Sri Malati:
receiving Prabhu's mercy, she became most blessed.

**164.** To the other daughter, named Sri Phuljhi Thakurani,
Prabhu, the jewel of virtues, showed mercy.

**165.** Then the one called Kalanidhi Chattaraja:
he always chants Hari-nama — that is his occupation.

**166.** Prabhu says: "You are most dear to Chaitanya;
chant a hundred thousand names, making it your rule."

**167.** Most dear to Prabhu, foremost among servants,
is Vrindavana Chattaraja, the beloved servant, his very life.

**168.** What can I say of the bhajana of them all?
As I speak, waves of an ocean of happiness rise in my heart.

**169.** There too he showed most pure mercy to a varna-brahmana:[^19]
filled with mercy, he showed him mercy.

**170.** His name is Sri Gopala Dasa; showing him mercy,
Prabhu ordered him to deliver his own community.

**171.** In the village of Kanchanagaria Prabhu's devotees
each keep the rule of a hundred thousand names of Hari.

**172.** By day they do not chant the name, but at night, sitting down,
tying their hair with a cord to the rafter, they sit and chant the name.[^20]

**173.** They are all Prabhu's life, and Prabhu is the life of them all;
that place is most dear to him, and he never leaves it.

**174.** A disciple of Gopala Dasa Thakura, a worthy man,
is Sri Gopimohana Dasa of Mirzapur.

**175.** He is a maha-bhagavata — what is there to say of him?
His disciple is Shyamadasa, who lives in Khadgrama.

**176.** Prabhu showed mercy to one named Govinda Chakravarti,
whose bhajana from childhood was incomparable.

**177.** His body is the embodiment of prema, his name renowned:
known as "Bhavaka Chakravarti", of the village of Borakuli.

**178.** His disciples and disciples' disciples filled the world;
later I shall tell of the fame that came of it.

**179.** His wife is virtuous and wise,
a vessel of the mercy of Sri Ishvari, of excellent conduct.

**180.** She takes a hundred thousand names of Hari
and constantly tells of the deeds of Mahaprabhu.

**181.** Sri Gopala Bhatta, Sri Rupa and Sanatana,
and the feet of Acharya Prabhu are her constant meditation.

**182.** How much more can I expound the virtues of this Thakurani,
whose way of bhajana is renowned in the world?

**183.** (Govinda Chakravarti's) eldest son is named Rajavallabha Chakravarti:
what can I say of his incomparable virtues?

**184.** I cannot describe his life:
apart from Prabhu's feet there is nothing else in his heart.

**185.** The two other sons became disciples of their mother —
Radhavinoda and Kishori Dasa, devoted to bhakti.

**186.** To Karnapura Kaviraja Prabhu showed mercy —
he who was blessed in describing Prabhu's branches.[^21]

**187.** I cannot describe his boundless bhajana:
he is always absorbed in service performed in the mind.

**188.** He takes a hundred thousand names of Hari
and so lives, his mind immersed in happiness.

**189.** Then Prabhu showed mercy to Vana-Vishnupura:
many disciples of his appeared there.

**190.** Then Sri Acharya showed mercy to Vyasa
and, making him his servant, taught him much.

**191.** Those confidential virtues cannot be told:
he is most learned and full of prema.

**192.** He had many branches and sub-branches;
those maha-bhagavatas delivered the world.

**193.** Sri Vamshidasa Thakura, a worthy man,
is a dear branch of Prabhu, sweet in disposition.

**194.** Devoted to Hari-nama, he always takes Hari-nama:
counting, he chants the name always, without rest.

**195.** Sri Gopaladasa Thakura is one of Prabhu's branches,
most dear to Prabhu; his virtues cannot be written down.

**196.** His home is in Budhuipara; he is a kirtana singer of Sri Krishna,
by whose kirtana even stone melts.

**197.** Sri Rupa Ghataka is a dear servant of Prabhu:
apart from the names of Radha and Krishna he has no other concern.

**198.** Then Prabhu showed mercy to Raghunandana Dasa
and gladly gave him the title "Ghataka".[^22]

**199.** Both Ghatakas are most virtuous:
both know Prabhu's feet as their all.

**200.** Sudhakara Mandala is one of Prabhu's servants;
his wife Shyamapriya is a vessel of mercy.

**201.** Their son is the virtuous Radhavallabha Mandala:
apart from Hari-nama he has no other concern.

**202.** Then Prabhu showed mercy to Kamadeva Mandala:
receiving Prabhu's mercy, he became most blessed.

**203.** Who can tell of his hidden bhava?
The pastimes of Radha and Krishna appear in his heart.

**204.** He always takes Hari-nama,
and Prabhu's two feet shine in his heart.

**205.** Then Prabhu showed mercy to Gopala Mandala,
whose faith in Prabhu's feet is most pure.

**206.** Prabhu's two fathers-in-law are most wise;
the lives of the two cannot be described.

**207.** Both are most pure in conduct and spotless in body;
they have nothing but meditation on Mahaprabhu's feet.

**208.** Sri Gopala Chakravarti is a dear servant of Prabhu:
tears flow from his eyes without rest; in kirtana he dances.

**209.** The other father-in-law is Sri Raghunandana Chakravarti:
receiving Prabhu's mercy, he became renowned.[^23]

**210.** Of Prabhu's two brothers-in-law — listen:
both became vessels of Prabhu's mercy.

**211.** The elder is the worthy Shyamadasa Chakravarti,
a vessel of Prabhu's mercy, with a merciful heart.

**212.** He is a scholar of the *Srimad-Bhagavatam*
and is wildly intoxicated with prema at the verses of the *Bhagavata*.

**213.** His younger brother is most devoted, a worthy man;
I shall tell where his home is: he lives in Faridpur.

**214.** Ramacharana Chakravarti is Prabhu's servant;
how many of his disciples shall I name?

**215.** He chants a hundred thousand names of Hari, counting them,
and speaks of the pastimes of Radha and Krishna, relishing them.

**216.** He is passionately fond of kirtana and always dances in it;
tears always flow from his eyes, for he is full of prema.

**217.** He is the life of the Vaishnavas, like a vessel of tenderness;
who can count his countless virtues?

**218.** A vessel of Prabhu's mercy is one Chatta Krishnadasa:
he chants a hundred thousand names of Hari; all his faith is in the name.

**219.** There is no end to his servants:
all are devoted to Hari-nama, all are virtuous.

**220.** One named Vanamali Dasa, born in a Vaidya family,[^24]
a dear servant of Prabhu — who can know his heart?

**221.** Sri Mohana Dasa, born in a Vaidya family,
whose steadfast bhajana is most pure —

**222.** great and worthy, sweet in disposition,
most dear to Prabhu, with a merciful heart.

**223.** Sri Radhavallabha Dasa is Prabhu's servant,
a maha-bhagavata, rich in bhajana.

**224.** Most dear to Prabhu is Sri Mathura Dasa:
he always chants Hari-nama with great joy.

**225.** Radhakrishna Dasa is a dear servant of Prabhu:
unceasingly his tears of prema flow; in kirtana he dances.

**226.** Sri Ramana Dasa is a vessel of Prabhu's mercy,
on whose lips the nectar of Hari-nama always remains.

**227.** Another servant of Prabhu is named Ramadasa:
ever in the madness of prema he dances and takes the name of Hari.

**228.** Sri Kavivallabha is Prabhu's own servant:
in prema he sings the names of Radha and Krishna with great joy.

**229.** Many books he copied out for Prabhu:
his writing is like strings of pearls; he is a great calligrapher.

**230.** Vanamali Dasa's father, Sri Gopala Dasa,
is Prabhu's servant, most pure in speech.

**231.** Then Prabhu showed mercy to Shyamadasa Chatta:
he is a maha-bhagavata and received Prabhu's mercy.

**232.** There too is Sri Atmarama, a dear servant of Prabhu:
he always chants Hari-nama, indifferent to the world.

**233.** To Sri Nakadi Dasa he showed great mercy:
he made Prabhu's feet his all.

**234.** Sri Gopiramana Dasa is a worthy Vaidya:
boundless mercy of Prabhu came to him.

**235.** He loves Hari-nama and takes the name of Hari;
he sings of the pastimes of Radha and Krishna — an abode of great prema.

**236.** His home is in Goyasa; he is a fine connoisseur of rasa:
he always speaks of Krishna's rasa, in which prema abounds.

**237.** Sri Durgadasa is Prabhu's own servant:
he always chants Hari-nama with joy in his heart.

**238.** Then Prabhu showed mercy to Shyamadasa Kaviraja,
whose bhajana is manifest in the world.

**239.** Then Prabhu showed mercy to Raghunatha Dasa,
who, receiving Prabhu's mercy, rejoiced in his heart.

**240.** To Kumudananda Thakura Prabhu showed mercy,
who, receiving Prabhu's mercy, attained the goal of life.

**241.** Sri Ramadasa Thakura is a dear servant of Prabhu:
apart from meditation on Radha and Krishna he has no other concern.

**242.** Radhavallabha Dasa Thakura is simple and generous,
in whose heart is meditation on Prabhu's feet.

**243.** Gokulananda Dasa Chakravarti is a worthy man:
Prabhu, with a merciful heart, showed him mercy.

**244.** Another servant is Sri Gokulananda Dasa:
he always chants Hari-nama; all his faith is in the name.

**245.** Then he showed mercy to Sri Gopaladasa Thakura:
receiving Prabhu's mercy, he became most blessed.

**246.** Then Prabhu showed mercy to Shyamadasa:
he is the glory of the Chatta family, of supreme devotion.

**247.** Then Prabhu set out for the darshana of Purushottama[^25]
and went joyfully along forest roads.

**248.** One day he stayed the night in a village;
robbers, taking (his books) for jewels, seized them.

**249.** The thieves carried off the books on their way;
then Prabhu went to the king for the sake of the books.

**250.** At that time a brahmana, Vyasa Chakravarti,
was reciting the Puranas to the king with great feeling.

**251.** For reciting the Puranas the king gave him the name "Acharya":
from then on he became known in the world as Acharya.

**252.** Just then the brahmana is reading the *Bhramara-gita*;
hearing the explanation, Prabhu laughs, standing some way off.

**253.** Then Prabhu entered the assembly and sat down,
and, seated, refuted the whole explanation.

**254.** Then the king's heart rejoiced greatly:
his mind became absorbed in the wish to hear the explanation.

**255.** The king humbly requests:
"Please explain it yourself, be merciful!"

**256.** Prabhu explained the verse according to the Gosvamis;
hearing it, the king became as if mad.

**257.** Then he bowed and fell at his feet:
"Prabhu, be merciful to me, I take shelter!

**258.** Alas, alas! Such an explanation I have never heard!"
Crying aloud, he weeps, fallen on the ground.

**259.** In a choked voice he says: "Hear me, sir:
be merciful, take pity on me!"

**260.** Prabhu asks: "What is this brahmana's name?"
The king replies: "Sri Vyasa Acharya.

**261.** His name from the beginning was 'Acharya'."
Prabhu said: "The name 'Acharya' suits him well."

**262.** Then Prabhu speaks to the king:
"May Vrajendra-nandana be merciful to you."

**263.** The Malla king named Sri Vira Hambira —
to him Prabhu, merciful and profound, showed mercy.

**264.** He gained steadfast bhakti for Krishna's feet;
he entrusted all the affairs of the kingdom to Prabhu.

**265.** What can I say of the shelter of that Prabhu's feet!
The shelter of those feet destroys both desires and the craving for liberation;

**266.** whoever sees those feet, touches them, takes shelter of them and serves them
easily obtains the treasure of the nectar of prema.

**267.** In that land of Vana-Vishnupura very many
became his disciples — they cannot be counted.

**268.** I have not written this out in detail in the book —
only the little I heard from the lips of Srimati.[^26]

**269.** Born in the Karana family,[^27] most pure in conduct,
two brothers, sons of Karunakara Dasa,

**270.** always wrote the documents in Prabhu's house;
therefore the merciful one gave them the name "Bishvas".[^28]

**271.** To the elder, the worthy Sri Janakirama Dasa,
merciful Prabhu showed mercy.

**272.** To his younger brother Prasada Dasa he showed mercy:
receiving Prabhu's mercy, both became great devotees.

**273.** Formerly their surname was Majumdar;
now the surname "Bishvas", given by Prabhu, became established.

**274.** There too he showed mercy to Vallavi Kavipati:
receiving the shelter of his feet, he became pious.

**275.** He always chants Hari-nama, keeping a rule:
until he has chanted a hundred thousand names of Hari he does not eat.

**276.** He lives near Prabhu; Prabhu is his life;
he entrusted his house and household to Prabhu.

**277.** His two elder brothers are worthy men:
to the eldest, Ramadasa, Prabhu was merciful,

**278.** and to the middle one, Gopaladasa, he showed mercy:
thus Prabhu's great mercy came upon the three brothers.

**279.** To Sri Vallabha Thakura, who lived in the village of Deuli,
he showed abundant mercy.

**280.** In his house Prabhu stayed at first,
and therefore Prabhu's love for him was especially great.

**281.** When from his lips Prabhu heard the news of the books' whereabouts
and recovered the stolen books, Prabhu's soul was comforted.

**282.** Together with him he went to the king;
by his direction he obtained the great treasure, the books.[^29]

**283.** Therefore Prabhu showed him mercy
and, placing his foot on his head, said:

**284.** "May Sri Radha-ramana be merciful to you,
and Sri Govinda-jiu and Madana-mohana,

**285.** Sri Gopinatha, Rupa and Sanatana,
Sri Gopala Bhatta and the feet of Sri Jiva,

**286.** Raghunatha Bhatta and Raghunatha Dasa —
may they be merciful to you with great joy!

**287.** Sri Krishnadasa and Lokanatha Gosani —
may they all cast their merciful glance upon you!

**288.** May they all fulfil your desires:
without effort you will obtain the great treasure of prema."

**289.** Filled with mercy towards him, Prabhu stayed on
and joyfully took up residence in his house.

**290.** Taking with him Vallavi Kaviraja and others,
he went with a glad heart to the king's palace.

**291.** Seeing Prabhu, the king rose joyfully
and fell prostrate on the ground, touching it with the eight limbs.

**292.** Prabhu placed his foot on the king's head
and, pleased, sat down on the seat.

**293.** Introducing all his companions,
he greeted each fittingly, well pleased.

**294.** For some time they talked of Krishna;
hearing it, the king's mind rejoiced.

**295.** An ocean of bliss surged in the king's heart;
with cries of joy he clasped Prabhu's feet:

**296.** "My birth has become fruitful — I have obtained your sight!"
The sight of those feet fulfils all desires.

**297.** Thus, having stayed some time in the assembly,
Prabhu, pleased, returned to his lodging.

**298.** The king went to his own quarters and rested;
lying down, the king began to reflect.

**299.** He thinks, "I shall establish the service of Krishna,"
and in a dream he clearly sees (the Lord) in the form of Kalachand.[^30]

**300.** There too the form of his own Prabhu appears to the king;
seeing the beauty of both Prabhus, he reflects in his heart.

**301.** As soon as he sees it, he begins to describe the beauty of both —
so that nectar flows in every syllable.

**302.** On the two Prabhus he composed two padas,
by the tasting of which the bliss of prema thickens.

**303.** In his dream the king recites the padas, and the queen, hearing them,
passed the whole night in tears.

**304.** Hearing those wondrous padas,
the heart of the chief queen became absorbed in bhava.

**305.** Then the king woke and sat up on his bed,
meditating in his heart on the lotus feet of his Prabhu.

**306.** Saying "Rupa, Sanatana!" he cries out again and again;
saying "Sri Bhatta Gosani!" he laments.

**307.** Awake, the maharaja cannot calm his mind:
the form he had seen shines in his heart.

**308.** Now he laments, now he thinks to himself:
"The dream is broken — where has such a vision gone?"

**309.** Awake, the maharaja sees that form:
he gazes in bliss on the beauty of his own Prabhu's form.

**310.** He sees Prabhu saying: "Serve this One;
be steady, and you will see a wondrous form."

**311.** The maharaja rejoices, immersed in happiness.
Just then the chief queen fell at his feet:

**312.** "What wondrous padas you have composed, O king!
Make me blessed — let me hear them!"

**313.** The king says: "I have composed no padas."
The queen says: "O king, do not deceive me!

**314.** Do not deceive me, O king, gladden my mind,
or else life will not remain in my body."

**315.** Then the king understood: without Prabhu's mercy
how could such a wondrous bhava have arisen in her?

**316.** Then the king, pleased, said:
"Hear this pada with joy."

**317.** This is the pada:
My Prabhu Srinivasa has fulfilled my hope: apart from you I have no other refuge.
I was a worm of sense objects, everything tasted sweet to me; you rid me of my royal pride. (1)
I was drinking poison — you turned everything around and showed me a stream of nectar.
My mind cries, "Beloved, beloved!" — everything around disquiets me; such is your dealing. (2)
Radha's feet are the treasure-store of happiness: you made me a maidservant at those feet, you bound my heart to Gora's feet.
Together with Sri Radha-ramana you showed me the bower-house, you revealed to me the love of the Two. (3)
I go to the bank of the Yamuna: on the shore the sakhis run about, Radha and Kanu sport happily.
The heart of this Vira Hambira ever meditates on Vraja, where bees fly by hundreds of thousands. (4)[^31]

**318.** *(The second pada:)*
Listen, friend of my heart! The Dark One with lotus eyes — what he has done to me I myself do not know.
My heart feels so strange, everything disquiets me: by loving I have lost my life. (1)
Having heard of him I saw the Dark One — and seeing him I found a burning; there is no water to quench it.
I brought aguru and sandal, ground them and smeared my body — the fire in my heart does not go out. (2)
When I sit, he comes and makes me rise, leads me to the bank of the Yamuna.
What to do, what not to do — I am always pining to death, not for a moment can I be still. (3)
My mother-in-law and sister-in-law always take me for a thief; the master of the house does not even look at me.
The heart of this Vira Hambira, follower of Srinivasa, has drowned at the feet of Kalachand. (4)[^32]

**319.** Listening and listening, the queen's joy increased;
her body grew limp in the transport of bhava, and prema rose.

**320.** Her heart trembles continually and cannot be restrained;
"What have I heard!" the queen cries, "alas, alas!"

**321.** When the queen's mind had grown calm,
she made many entreaties to the king:

**322.** "Maharaja! Accept me under your care:
give me shelter at Srinivasa's feet."

**323.** The king understood in his heart: without Prabhu's mercy
how could such a wondrous bhava have arisen?

**324.** "The queen is fortunate," the king thinks to himself;
"it seems fate has at last become most gracious to me."

**325.** "There is no limit to this good fortune," he says again and again;
in his heart the king understood that this was Prabhu's doing.

**326.** Then the king, pleased, sent for Prabhu
and, joyful, fell and rolled on the ground.

**327.** He told Prabhu's feet everything that had happened;
hearing it, Prabhu understood it fully in his heart.

**328.** Then, when Prabhu came to the chief queen,
the queen fell at his feet and began to speak:

**329.** "Prabhu, accept me now;
forgive my offences, Prabhu, and accept me.

**330.** You have descended to deliver the fallen;
I know, Prabhu, you will deliver even such a sinner as I."

**331.** Seeing the queen's entreaty, Prabhu was greatly pleased
and, filled with joy, gave her the shade of his feet.

**332.** First he lets her hear the mantra of Hari-nama,
then he has her accept the mantra of the Divine Couple.

**333.** Giving her worship with the kama-gayatri and the kama-bija,
he explains in detail about the group of manjaris:

**334.** "This is the parakiya-lila; without the group of manjaris
the rasa of parakiya can never be attained.

**335.** Without following them all, Vraja is not reached —
this I tell you with certainty.

**336.** This bhava is pure and most spotless,
like Jambu-nadi gold, supremely radiant.

**337.** I have told you in detail what is in my heart:
worship Krishna's feet, casting karma and the like far away.

**338.** In the siddha-deha perform service in the mind,
and in the external body always hear and chant.[^33]

**339.** With a pure heart always serve the feet of the Vaishnavas,
and without effort you will attain the feet of Radha-Govinda."

**340.** Having given all this instruction in worship,
Prabhu was pleased in mind and joyful at heart.

**341.** Then Prabhu showed mercy to the king's son:
rejoicing, Prabhu gave him the shade of his feet.

**342.** The heir apparent is named Sri Dhadi Hambira;
he is a vessel of Prabhu's mercy, a king among great devotees.

**343.** Then the king established the service of Kalachand
and, seeing the beauty of His holy form, was immersed in bliss.

**344.** He joyfully gazes on the beauty of Kalachand's form;
Prabhu himself joyfully performed the abhisheka.

**345.** The king unceasingly serves the Vaishnavas.
This is all I have told of the king's life.

**346.** Hearing of the king's spiritual path, Sri Jiva Gosani
there and then gave him the name Sri Gopaladasa.[^34]

**347.** Of the mercy to Vyasacharya I have written above:
Prabhu made him his own purohita.

**348.** Then the wife of Sri Vyasa Acharya:
Prabhu, the jewel of virtues, showed her mercy.

**349.** Her name is Indumukhi Thakurani:
how can I tell of her spiritual life?

**350.** Her son is the worthy Shyamadasa Chakravarti:
merciful Prabhu showed him mercy.

**351.** Prabhu shows mercy to Bhagavan Kavivara:
he is a pandita, a connoisseur of rasa, most composed.

**352.** Then Prabhu showed mercy to Narayana Kavi:
when he took shelter, Prabhu gave him the shade of his feet.

**353.** He is the brother of Sri Nrisimha Kaviraja:
the ocean of his greatness is beyond words.

**354.** Vasudeva Kaviraja is most virtuous:
his heart is wholly and steadfastly devoted to Krishna's feet.

**355.** Filled with mercy, Prabhu showed him mercy
and, giving him the shade of his feet, brought him to the goal of life.

**356.** Then Prabhu showed mercy to Vrindavana Dasa:
his fame as a kaviraja spread through the world.

**357.** Then Prabhu showed mercy to Nimai Kaviraja,
the brother of Rupa Kaviraja, renowned in the world.

**358.** He chants a hundred thousand names of Hari, counting them,
and dances in sankirtana, immersed in happiness.

**359.** In ecstasy his body grows limp, he cries out again and again,
leaps and bounds, and every moment roars.

**360.** Streams of tears flow from his eyes unceasingly,
his body is covered with gooseflesh, sweat always flows.

**361.** Then he showed mercy to Srimanta Chakravarti:
receiving the shelter of his feet, he became renowned.

**362.** He takes a hundred thousand names of Hari and has faith in the name;
he is a fine connoisseur of rasa and indifferent to the world.

**363.** Then Prabhu showed mercy to Sri Raghunandana:
showing him mercy, Prabhu was filled with joy.

**364.** Then he showed mercy to Gauranga Dasa:
who can describe his countless virtues?

**365.** He always takes Hari-nama with a mind absorbed in bhava
and always remembers the lotus feet of his Prabhu.

**366.** He always takes Hari-nama
and always remembers the pastimes of Radha and Krishna.

**367.** Saying "Rupa, Sanatana", he cries out again and again;
as soon as he says "Bhatta Gosani", tears flow.

**368.** As soon as he says "Gauranga", his mind is absorbed in bhava,
and at once he meditates on the lotus feet of his Prabhu.

**369.** Srimanta Thakura, born in a brahmana family —
to him Prabhu showed mercy, joyful at heart.

**370.** Prabhu showed mercy to Gopijanavallabha:
he is a maha-bhagavata whose (fame) spread through the world.

**371.** His bhajana cannot be told:
he is deeply absorbed in service in the mind.

**372.** Then Prabhu showed mercy to Sri Chaitanya Dasa:
as soon as he says "Sri Krishna Chaitanya", he floats in prema.

**373.** Then Prabhu showed mercy to one named Sri Govinda:
as soon as he says "Sri Gauranga", he becomes mad with prema.

**374.** To Tulasirama Dasa, born in a weaver family,
(he showed mercy); he always remembers Prabhu's feet with great longing.

**375.** Balarama Dasa was born in the land of Utkala;
he is born in a brahmana family and indifferent to the world.

**376.** Then Prabhu showed mercy to Chaudhuri Dayarama;
both were born in brahmana families and live in the same village.

**377.** How great their mutual love was cannot be told;
he surrendered everything at his Prabhu's own feet.

**378.** One of his royal devotees is Sri Harivallabha,
known as "Sarkar" — rare in the world.

**379.** Prabhu, filled with mercy, showed him mercy;
his way of bhajana cannot be described.

**380.** Another disciple of Prabhu is Krishnavallabha Chakravarti:
receiving Prabhu's mercy, he became most wise.

**381.** To Sri Krishna Purohita, a resident of Gauda,
he showed mercy, filled with compassion.

**382.** To Shyama Bhatta of the same land he showed mercy;
the disciples and grand-disciples of the two filled the world.

[^1]: A verse of Srila Rupa Gosvami (*Vidagdha-madhava* 1.2), also quoted in the *Chaitanya-charitamrita* (Adi 1.4).
[^2]: "Leads to the goal of parakiya" attempts the rare *parakīyārtha-sārthinam*. According to Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati, parakiya-bhava is the eternal transcendental mood of the gopis in Vraja, whose taste the followers of Rupa attain in the service of the manjaris; it has nothing to do with mundane "illicit love".
[^3]: "Hundreds of thousands" (*laksha grantha*): here *grantha* is the counting unit of text (32 syllables, i.e. a "verse"), as in the traditional reckoning of the Gosvamis' works; cf. CC Madhya 1.
[^4]: Krishnadasa Kaviraja, author of the *Chaitanya-charitamrita*.
[^5]: The *Prema-vilasa* (vilasas 18 and 20) and the *Bhakti-ratnakara* (13) give the same two marriages of Srinivasa (to Draupadi-Ishvari and Padmavati-Gauranga-priya); the names are not given here.
[^6]: A list of the Gosvamis' books: the *Toshani* is the *Vaishnava-toshani* (Sanatana's commentary on the Tenth Canto); the *Dashama-charita*, *Gitavali*, *Virudavali* (part of the *Stavavali*) and *Mukta-charita* are works of Rupa, Sanatana and Raghunatha Dasa; the *Brahma-samhita* and *Krishna-karnamrita* are books Mahaprabhu found in the South; the *Gopi-premamrita* is probably the text commented on by Krishnadasa Kaviraja; the *Madhava-mahotsava* is a poem by Jiva Gosvami.
[^7]: *Yama* is the same as prahara, about three hours (as the editor also explains).
[^8]: The *Jagannatha-vallabha-nataka* of Ramananda Raya.
[^9]: Thakura Mahashaya is Narottama Dasa Thakura. Karnapura Kaviraja is a disciple of Srinivasa, author of a Sanskrit list of his branches; not to be confused with Kavi Karnapura (Paramananda Sena), son of Shivananda Sena.
[^10]: "My Prabhu" is the author's spiritual master; further on the author calls himself the servant of Hemalata Thakurani, Srinivasa's daughter (cf. the colophons). Payar is the Bengali couplet metre.
[^11]: *Digvijayi*, a title of a scholar victorious in debate. Ramachandra Kaviraja was the son of Chiranjiva Sena; in the *Prema-vilasa* and the *Bhakti-ratnakara* too he meets Srinivasa in Yajigrama while returning from his wedding — an episode common to all three sources. Here his home is given as Kumaranagara.
[^12]: Literally "do not work maya on my scorched life", i.e. do not feign indifference, do not push me away.
[^13]: The subject is not named; from the sense, Srinivasa orders Govinda Kaviraja (the poet Govindadasa) to compose padas.
[^14]: Divyasimha is the son of Govinda Kaviraja (as the editor also notes).
[^15]: On parakiya-bhava see note 2.
[^16]: Kanchanagaria (Kanchana-Nagariya) is a village near Yajigrama; in the *Bhakti-ratnakara* Sri Dasa and Gokula Dasa of Kanchanagaria are disciples of Srinivasa.
[^17]: *Sri Ishvarer*, perhaps a slip for *Sri Ishvarir* (Ishvari Thakurani, Srinivasa's wife; cf. 179), or "Ishvara" is Prabhu himself.
[^18]: According to the *Karnananda*, Hemalata is the wife of Gopijanavallabha Chattaraja, and Krishnapriya the wife of Chaitanya Chattaraja (158).
[^19]: *Varna-vipra* (*varna-brahmana*): a brahmana who serves as priest to lower castes; hence Gopala Dasa is told to "deliver his own community" (literally "his own jati").
[^20]: That is, to keep from falling asleep: if the head nods, the cord pulls the hair.
[^21]: Cf. 66: Karnapura Kaviraja is the author of the Sanskrit list of Srinivasa's branches on which Yadunandana relies.
[^22]: *Ghataka*: a marriage-broker; evidently the hereditary occupation of the family.
[^23]: In the *Prema-vilasa* Srinivasa's fathers-in-law are Gopala Dasa (Gopala Chakravarti), father of Draupadi-Ishvari, and Raghu Chakravarti, father of Padmavati-Gauranga-priya.
[^24]: Vanamali Dasa is the son of Sri Gopala Dasa (see 230). The order of names in the list, as the author himself warns (74), is not always consistent.
[^25]: Purushottama is Jagannatha in Puri. According to the *Prema-vilasa* (12–13) and the *Bhakti-ratnakara* (7), the Gosvamis' books were stolen by Vira Hambira's men while Srinivasa, Narottama and Shyamananda were carrying them from Vrindavana to Gauda; here the theft takes place during Srinivasa's pilgrimage to Puri, and Narottama and Shyamananda are not mentioned.
[^26]: According to the editor's gloss, Srimati is Hemalata Thakurani, Srinivasa's daughter and the author's guru.
[^27]: Karana: Kayasthas (scribes); the editor's long footnote on the origin of the castes is not translated.
[^28]: *Bishvas* (*vishvasa*, "trust"): the family title of a trusted scribe or steward.
[^29]: In the *Bhakti-ratnakara* (7) too, Srinivasa is brought to Vira Hambira's court by a brahmana of the village of Deuli with whom he was staying; there he is called Krishnavallabha, here Sri Vallabha Thakura.
[^30]: Kalachand ("the Dark Moon"): the Deity of Krishna served by Vira Hambira in Vishnupura (Sri Kalachand); cf. *Prema-vilasa* 13.
[^31]: A song of Vira Hambira (signed "this Vira Hambira" in the last stanza). "You made me a maidservant" (*dasi*): service in the spiritual form of a maidservant (manjari) of Sri Radha is the goal of raganuga-bhajana according to Rupa Gosvami and Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati; "Gora" is Gauranga. Cf. the songs of Vira Hambira in the *Bhakti-ratnakara* (7).
[^32]: A song in the voice of a gopi (Sri Radha) suffering separation from Krishna: the "mother-in-law and sister-in-law" and "master of the house" are images of the parakiya-rasa of Vraja. According to Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati this is transcendental prema, having nothing in common with mundane passion.
[^33]: The classic instruction of raganuga-bhakti (*Bhakti-rasamrita-sindhu* 1.2.295): in the *siddha-deha* — the spiritual form inwardly conceived according to the guru's instruction — service is performed in the mind, and with the external body one performs hearing, chanting and the other practices of sadhana-bhakti. Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati stressed that the siddha-deha is revealed by the guru's mercy as the heart is purified, not wilfully imagined; the "kama-gayatri" and "kama-bija" are mantras of service to Krishna, unrelated to mundane kama.
[^34]: According to the *Prema-vilasa* (13), Vira Hambira receives the name Haricharana Dasa; here it is "Gopaladasa", given by Sri Jiva.
