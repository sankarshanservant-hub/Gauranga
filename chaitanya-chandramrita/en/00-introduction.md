# Preface by Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati

*"Sri Sri Chaitanya-chandramritam" and "Sri Sri Navadvipa-shatakam," composed by the crest-jewel of the tridandi-gosvamis, the wandering acharya, the foremost of Gaura's associates, Srila Prabodhananda Sarasvati Gosvamipada — with the "Gaudiya-bhashya" and Bengali translation of Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati, which refute all false conclusions. Published by Sri Atindranatha Devasharma Bandyopadhyaya, Gaudiya Math (1, Ultadingi Junction Road, Calcutta). First edition; offering — one and a half rupees. Printed by Ananta Vasudeva Brahmachari, B.A., at the Gaudiya Printing Works (243/2, Upper Circular Road, Calcutta).*

## About the author

At the beginning of the year 1433 of the Shaka era Sri Gaurasundara, under the pretext of a pilgrimage, distributed mercy to the devotees in South India. Setting out from Nilachala in the province of Utkala, He first came to the confluence of the Godavari with the sea and then visited many holy places of the present Madras Presidency. On the bright *ekadashi* of the month of […] Sri Mahaprabhu arrived at […]. Seeing that […] had come, the Lord […], following the rule of the *dashanami* sannyasis, resolved to spend four months at the holy place of Sri Ranganatha. Vaishnavas […] lived there. The adherence of the *sampradaya* Vaishnavas of the South to righteous conduct […]. In the villages of the South where genuine Vaishnavas [live], the *smarta* brahmanas do not find it convenient to live. […] was at that time a holy place served exclusively by Sri Vaishnavas. Therefore Sri Mahaprabhu spent four months among righteous Vaishnavas who had taken shelter of devotion to Vishnu, and, taking *darshana* of Sri Ranganatha and preaching the topics of Krishna, He instructed the living beings. At that time three brothers — Tirumalla, Venkata and Gopala-guru — who had come from the province of Mysore, lived in Srirangam. In fact they were natives of Andhra, or the Northern province. Sri Mahaprabhu, greatly pleased with this brahmana family, spent four months in their house. The son of the middle brother, Venkata, then in his boyhood (*pauganda*), is the famous Sri Gopala Bhatta, one of the six Gosvamis.

The Vaishnavas of the Sri-sampradaya love the worship of Sri Lakshmi-Narayana. By the inner mercy of Sri Mahaprabhu this Bhatta family became expert in attaining Krishna-rasa. Although we do not know much about Sri Tirumalla, it can be understood that Sri Chaitanya was his life as well.

Sri Venkata's conversation with Sri Mahaprabhu is described in the *Sri Chaitanya-charitamrita* (Madhya-lila, ninth chapter). Sri Prabodhananda's attachment to Sri Chaitanya was incomparable. Through the influence of Sri Prabodhananda's good instruction […]'s son Sri Gopala Bhatta attained the position of acharya of the Gaudiya Vaishnavas. Among the servants of Sri Chaitanya the place of Sri Prabodhananda is very high. Sri Kavi-karnapura in his *Sri Gaura-ganoddesha-dipika* has proclaimed Sri Prabodhananda Sarasvati to be Tungavidya in the pastimes of Sri Krishna.[^1] In the *Sri Hari-bhakti-vilasa* […] it is written that Sri Gopala Bhatta, the disciple of Srila Prabodhananda, who is dear to the Lord, composed the *Sri Hari-bhakti-vilasa* to please Sri Rupa, Sri Sanatana and Sri Raghunatha Dasa.[^2] In the *Sri Bhakti-ratnakara* it is written:

> Some say: great are the virtues of Prabodhananda; […] he became renowned as "Sarasvati."

> The complete Brahman […] Bhagavan — is dear to him; apart from Him, [Prabodhananda knows] nothing else even in a dream.

> Supreme renunciation, love, […] enchanting; a great poet, matchless in song, instrumental music and dance.

> Hearing his words, everyone's joy increases; boundless is the great glory of Prabodhananda.

After Sri Mahaprabhu returned to Nilachala, within a few years Sri Prabodhananda Sarasvati, having entered deeply into the worship hidden in the heart of Sri Krishna-Chaitanya, leaving […] for the sake of the desired *bhajana* […], without delay, under the shelter of Sri Gaura's feet, settled in Kamyavana in the district of Mathura. In Sri Gopala Bhatta too the desire to live in Vraja gradually awoke; later he also followed in the footsteps of his paternal uncle.

Many people ask: if Sri Prabodhananda Sarasvati was so dear to Sri Gauranga, why did […] Kaviraja Gosvami not record his life and glories for the pleasure of the readers who are devotees of Sri Gaura? […] the writing of the *Sri Bhakti-ratnakara* seems quite sufficient. Its author, Sri Ghanashyama Sri Narahari Chakravarti, says:

> These accounts of Sri Gopala Bhatta some describe in part, others do not describe at all.

> Whoever, without understanding the heart of this, […] — in his heart the seed of offence takes root.

> The supreme *rasikas*, the earlier poets, though able to describe it, did not describe it.

> They left a description only here and there; […] the poets for this reason.

> Sri Gopala Bhatta, pleased, gave the order […], forbidding them to describe what concerned himself.

> Why did he forbid it — who can understand that? He always regards himself as most lowly.

> Kaviraja could not transgress his order.

Some say that in the words written by Sri Prabodhananda support for the doctrine of *svakiya* is seen, […], seeing the superiority of *parakiya-bhajana*, […] says little about Srila Sarasvati Gosvami Prabhu. Be that as it may, all devotees who follow […], impartial like Sri Narahari Dasa, are fortunate; so let them, like him, leaving aside […], ever relish Sri Prabodhananda's pure following of Gaura and the sweetness of *parakiya* service to Sri Vrindavaneshvari.

Sri Prabodhananda's moods are supremely clear; in his language […] and sweetness are present at the same time. All Vaishnavas who have taken shelter at the feet of Sri Chaitanya obtain incomparable joy by daily reading Sri Prabodhananda's *Sri Vrindavana-shataka*. The *Sri Navadvipa-shataka* composed by him, like the *Sri Vrindavana-shataka*, also […]. Sri Prabodhananda's poem *Sri Radha-rasa-sudhanidhi* is truly incomparable in the world. Although an ordinary reader fond of poetry may not experience […] in reading this book, it is supremely dear to devotees whose hearts have been softened by Hari-rasa. Therefore how highly [it] is valued depends on differences of taste, on the reader's *sukriti*, and […] the transcendental moods rooted in the *rasa* of Vraja […]. He also has a book called *Viveka-shataka*; it is mentioned in the work of Professor Aufrecht, and the late Ramadasa Sen Mahashaya of Baharampur has seen this book.

The book *Sri Chaitanya-chandramrita* has been published […]. Even the opponents of Sri Gaura, on reading it, will feel the purity of their hearts. And it goes without saying that the followers of Sri Gaura, reading it, will be immersed in supreme bliss in an indescribable ocean of happiness. Those for whom the Lord of Goloka Himself became the object of service for four months, and to whom He granted […] — they possess an inexhaustible treasury of […], and the little circle of living beings certainly cherishes the hope of obtaining some part of it.

Some try to identify Prabodhananda, the foremost of Vaishnavas, with Prakashananda, the *mayavadi* of Kashi; but we can in no way believe their words. For about the *mayavadi* sannyasi of Kashi named Prakashananda it is written thus in the *Sri Chaitanya-bhagavata*, […], third chapter:

> Thus in Navadvipa […] Vishvambhara floats in the bliss of *bhakti* together with all His followers.

> One day, hearing a verse about the mood of the Boar, He went roaring of His own accord to the house of Murari.

> Pleased with Gupta's words, the Lord, the Boar, angry with the Vedas, says in reply:

> "As if I had no hands, no feet, no face, no eyes — so the Vedas [describe] Me, […].

> That fellow Prakashananda who teaches in Kashi — that fellow cuts My body to pieces.

> He explains that the Vedas do not accept My form; leprosy has struck his whole body, and still he does not understand.

> […] My body, which is supremely pure, whose deeds Aja (Brahma), Bhava (Shiva) and the others sing,

> by touching which piety and purity themselves become pure — that fellow calls it 'false.' What audacity!"

This event took place between the years 1425 and 1430 of the Shaka era. Sri Mahaprabhu, auspiciously arriving at Srirangam in 1433 Shaka, saw Sri Prabodhananda-pada among the three brothers. They were then […] Vaishnavas of the Sri-sampradaya, that is, adherents of *vishishta-advaita*, servants of the eternal form of Sri Narayana; while Prakashananda was then the foremost of the servants of the *mayavada* founded by Shankara. To try to make these two persons "one," or to equate them, is sheer madness.

In the twentieth chapter of the *Sri Chaitanya-bhagavata* this too is said about Prakashananda:

> As He was speaking, the Lord was filled with the mood of Ishvara; gnashing His teeth, He says emphatically:

> "The sannyasi Prakashananda lives in Kashi; that fellow thoroughly cuts My […] to pieces.

> He teaches Vedanta and does not accept My form; […] I have struck his body with leprosy, and still he does not understand.

> The body in which countless […] rest — that fellow calls it 'false.' What audacity!

> I tell you the truth, Murari: you are My servant. Whoever does not accept My body […] goes to ruin.

> True are My pastimes and deeds, true is My abode; calling them 'false,' he cuts Me to pieces.

> The glory by hearing which ignorance is destroyed today — those pastimes the sinful teacher calls 'false.'

> Whoever disregards Him whose glory is so holy will never know […] My descent."

Sri Prakashananda was the […] of the *ekadandi* sannyasis of Shankara's *sampradaya* of that time; while Sri Prabodhananda was a Ramanujiya *tridandi-jiyar-svami*, who had come from the land of Mysore and lived in Ranga-kshetra. Prakashananda was a *mayavadi* living in Kashi, while Prabodhananda was a […] living in Kamyavana. One was a resident of Aryavarta, the other a Vaishnava of the South; one was a *nirvishesha-vadi* (an advocate of the impersonal), the other […] a *savishesha-vadi* (an advocate of the personal), and later an adherent of the doctrine of *achintya-bhedabheda*. One, an opponent of Vishnu [and the Vaishnavas], became a devotee only after being delivered; the other is an eternally perfect associate of Gaura and the guru of the Vaishnava acharya Sri Gopala Bhatta Gosvami. To call and […] the most worshipful paternal uncle and guru of Sri Gopala Bhatta Gosvami not the crest-jewel of eternally perfect devotees but a hater of Vishnu and the Vaishnavas, a *mayavadi* and [formerly] a conditioned soul is a terrible offence against a Vaishnava, leading to hell.

In the *Sri Chaitanya-charitamrita*, in the twenty-fifth chapter of the Madhya-lila and in the seventh chapter of the Adi-lila, only the *mayavadi* Prakashananda is spoken of. It is incomprehensible how a person who from 1425 to 1430 Shaka was a *mayavadi* could in 1433 Shaka be in the South […] a Ramanujiya, and then in 1435 Shaka […] become a *mayavadi* again. Therefore the attempt to identify Prakashananda with Sri Prabodhananda shows only extreme ignorance. […] Such a tendency to uproot traditions is no small sorrow. Because Sri Prabodhananda Sarasvati, moved by his […] and humility, forbade through Sri Gopala Bhatta the discussion of his personal life […], and […] Kaviraja Gosvami did not transgress his order — this calamity has now arisen. Had Srila Prabodhananda known that after his disappearance, in order to count him among the offenders against Vishnu and the Vaishnavas, this terrible whirlpool of error would arise in the future, he […] would not have forbidden Sri Kaviraja Gosvami in that way. The readers of the *Bhakti-ratnakara* will understand this. About Srila Prabodhananda it is written thus in the *Bhakti-ratnakara*:

> […], Venkata and Prabodhananda — the treasure of the life of the three brothers is Gaurachandra.

> These three brothers, worshippers of Lakshmi-Narayana, by the Lord's […] became intoxicated with the *rasa* of Radha-Krishna.

> Tirumalla, Venkata, Prabodhananda — all three reflect: "How shall we live without the Lord?

> Who will joke with us? Who will take us along to bathe in the Kaveri?"

> After four months the Lord took His leave; the three brothers weep aloud.

> The Lord, […] the three brothers, spoke to them many words of consolation.

> Some say: great are the virtues of Prabodhananda, […] he became renowned as the sannyasi "Sarasvati."

> […] Sri Krishna-Chaitanya Bhagavan — is dear to him; apart from Him, [he knows] nothing else even in a dream.

In the catalogue of the esteemed Professor Aufrecht a book called *Sri Sangita-madhava* is listed among the works of Srila Prabodhananda Sarasvati. We have obtained this book. It has been published in full in the journal *Sri Sajjana-toshani*, from issue […] to issue 12 of its 18th year.

The householder Vaishnavas of the "Sri" *sampradaya*, when leaving home, never take *ekadanda* sannyasa; they all take *tridanda* sannyasa and are known as Ramanujiya *svamis*. Some, having studied Srila Prabodhananda's *Sri Chaitanya-chandramrita*, conclude that he was a "*brahma*-sannyasi," but in the absence of definite evidence, accepting this raises many difficulties.
