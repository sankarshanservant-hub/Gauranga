# Part Two

*Censure of the non-devotees. Humility (verses 29–56)*

## Censure of those not devoted to Chaitanya

**29.** Oh, who in this world is that great fortunate one who has made visible even to one such as me the son of Shachi — He whom the crowns of the whole host of the Vedas seek! He drives out, in an indescribable way, the pride of the lords of the gods and of the sages, who think themselves great and do not know His lotus feet.

**30.** Even if a man is devoid of every means of spiritual practice, if his heart is given to Gauranga, whose glory is wonderful beyond all, he has attained every goal.

**31.** Even if someone possesses countless great merits and seeks no refuge other than Hari, my mind still considers him unfortunate if he has not worshipped Chaitanya.

**32.** Shame, shame on those attached to rituals and therefore dull-witted; shame on harsh ascetics and self-restraining yogis! Shame on those whose faces blossom at the mere words "I am Brahman"! And why should we grieve over these two-legged beasts intoxicated with the taste of sense pleasure? Alas, not one of them has received even a drop of the honey of Gaura.

**33.** However much a stone is watered with nectar, no sprout will appear; however much one straightens a dog's tail, it will never become straight; O wise ones, however high one raises one's hands, how can one hold the disc of the moon? Let there be every means of practice — but without Gaura's mercy there is no festival of *bhava* (love).

**34.** When Gaurachandra has descended and the ocean of love has spread wide, its multitude of jewels (the nine kinds of *bhakti* and the rest) shining brightly — whoever has remained poor is poor indeed.

**35.** When Gaurachandra has descended and the ocean of love has spread wide, those who do not plunge into it drown in the ocean of great misfortunes.

**36.** When Chaitanya-chandra has appeared and the ocean of the nectar *rasa* of great love has spread forth — whoever has remained poor is poor indeed.

**37.** If people, even those who know all the shastras, do not know that Chaitanya is God, they merely wander about in this world devoid of consciousness (*a-chaitanya*).

**38.** Again and again tasting the fullness of the sweetness of His own names, again and again becoming intoxicated, indescribably losing control of Himself so that His body goes limp, again and again murmuring, "Sing, sing of the qualities of the lord of Vraja!" — such is Gaura. Those who have never once seen Him — how can they have *bhakti*?

**39.** Could a sprout not appear without a seed, a blind man not see, a lame man not climb a mountain peak — if even those not devoted to Sri Chaitanya, whose wondrous wealth is made of Hari-rasa, could somehow obtain the impulse of the highest love?

**40.** He who, by an unearthly unfolding of the pastimes of the intoxicating *rasa* of love, suddenly gives the festival of great and wondrous love even to one who is not among these virtuous companions and ministers of Sri Govinda — whoever does not turn his mind to Gaura Himself is here the greatest of fools, a two-legged beast.

**41.** Countless incarnations of the Lord are named in the *shruti* and other scriptures — but who could ascribe such power to anyone other than the Supreme? And what more is there to say: how many, how many revelations have the saints had of their beloved Gaura! And yet, alas — Hari, Hari! — fools do not recognize Hari in Sri Gaura.

**42.** His playful glance gives rise in all embodied beings to the bliss of love, which by its various transformations shows the worthlessness even of liberation and the other goals. If this Gaurachandra, hidden in the Vedas, is not the Lord of the world, then the doctrine of a world without God is established of itself. Shiva, Shiva! Inscrutable art thou, O *maya* of Vishnu — obeisance to thee!

**43.** Shame on an illustrious family, shame on eloquence, shame on fame, shame on learning, on a handsome form, on youth and on wealth; shame too on birth as a twice-born, on a pure *ashrama* and all the rest — if in the age of Kali one has not come to know Gaura, the Lord of the gopis made manifest.

**44.** Oh! Even the best associates of the Lord who dwell in Vaikuntha look with thrilling awe, their hair standing on end, at His servants — Vakreshvara and the others, whose bodies are always overpowered by absorption in the wondrous *rasa* radiant with great love. How, then, will one devoid of merit come to love that Gaura?

**45.** He bestows His grace on whoever it may be, then speaks to him, and His beautiful face smiles; from afar He looks at him with a tender glance and gives the festival of great love. Those, alas, whose minds have been coarsened by false reasoning do not duly honour even Him — Hari, descended as the incarnation of complete *rasa*. They are simply wicked.

## Humility (dainya)

**46.** I am cheated, I am cheated, I am cheated — there is no doubt! The whole world has been immersed in the *rasa* of Gaura, and it has not even touched me.

**47.** Who has not attained here, even without any effort, the crown of all the goals of man, on the earth touched by the dust of Gaura's lotus feet? Alas, alas! Shame on my life, shame on my learning, shame on my *ashrama* — through my great misfortune I have not had even a scent of connection with Him!

**48.** The great ocean of Gaurachandra's mercy has overflowed its shores and flooded the whole world — yet not a single drop has fallen on me, a great unfortunate. What a wonder is this!

**49.** The time is Kali; the enemies, the senses, are strong; the sacred path of *bhakti* is here barred by millions of thorns. Alas, alas, where shall I go, distraught? What shall I do, O Chaitanya-chandra, if You do not show mercy today?

**50.** That wondrous Lord never came before my eyes, I never tasted the *rasa* of Hari's lotus feet — what is gone is gone. But let me have at least this one thing: the festival of association with those who adorn the earth, their minds fixed in the feet of Sri Chaitanya.

**51.** I am given to millions of evil deeds, firmly bound in the chains of endless, terrible evil tendencies; my mind is in torment, harassed by millions of false views. Who but Gaura will be my friend here today?

**52.** Alas, alas! On the utterly barren soil of my heart even millions of my practices go to waste. Therefore with all my soul I take refuge in the feet of Sri Gaurachandra — the wondrous seed of *bhakti*.

**53.** Alas, alas! How will the sprout of the wish-fulfilling creeper of true *bhakti* break through on the utterly barren soil of my heart? But in my heart there is one supreme consolation: one who chants the name "Chaitanya" will never be pitiable.

**54.** I have fallen into the ocean of worldly suffering, I have been swallowed by the crocodiles and sea-monsters of lust, anger and the rest, I am fettered by evil tendencies and have no refuge. O Chaitanya-chandra, give me the support of Your feet!

**55.** That wondrous path of *bhakti* which Shiva, Shuka, Uddhava, Narada and others seek is not far even from us, O Chaitanya-chandra, Lord of inconceivable wealth, if Your merciful glance falls even on me, a wretch.

**56.** O Shauri (Krishna)! Where else is such unrestrained mercy, where such wondrous majesty, where such tenderness as in Yourself in the form of Gaura?
