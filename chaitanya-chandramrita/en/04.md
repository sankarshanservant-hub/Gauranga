# Part Four

*The supremacy of Sri Chaitanya. The glory of His descent. The beauty and dance of Gaura. Lament (verses 99–142)*

## The supremacy of Sri Chaitanya (utkarsha)

**99.** With the valour of a young lion in rut, eager to flood the world with the ocean of love, tender as a wondrous, newly blossomed golden *kandali* flower — glory to the indescribable Gaura-chandra, the Moon!

**100.** In beauty — millions of Kamadevas; in delighting all people — millions of moons; in tenderness — millions of mothers; in the essence of generosity — millions of trees of heaven; in depth — millions of oceans; in sweetness — millions of nectars, milks and honeys; in the realm of the *rasa* of love He has shown millions of wonders. Glory to the Lord Gaura!

**101.** Glory to the Lord Gaura, who has revealed the supreme, wondrous glory of those who hold to the *sadhana* that leads to the matchless wave of love for His lotus feet, and who, in the transport of great love, dance indescribably as if mad, filling with great amazement even Shiva, Brahma and the others.

**102.** His roar is like the roar of millions of maddened kings of beasts, His lustre like that of millions of suns, His coolness greater than that of millions of moons; by His gait He surpasses millions of maddened elephants; by His Name He redeems millions of the wretched; He is the Lord of millions of Brahmas and others, the crest-jewel of millions of knowers of non-duality[^1] — glory to Sri Sri Shachinandana!

**103.** The path that is beyond the reach of worldly intellect and devoid of outward glitter — alas, overgrown here with mighty thorns and therefore hard to travel, leading astray with false meanings, yet at once pouring forth bliss full of *rasa* — that path He at once illumines, revealing its glory; full of tenderness, He drives the darkness out of the cave of the heart — glory to that indescribable shining lamp of Navadvipa.

**104.** Burning from afar the moths of false logic, cool as millions of moons, the abode of sprouting light, the highest sweetness, dispelling the outer and inner darkness; His divine light spreads from the wick of the heart steeped in the oil of love; shining like gold — out of mercy this wondrous lamp of Navadvipa blazes brightly here.

**105.** Filling all the directions with cries, by the waves of His peals of laughter making the sky white as with blossoming jasmines and lilies, His whole body trembling like the leaves of the *ashvattha* in the wind, intoxicated with the *rasa* of love, dancing with high leaps — Gaura-Hari shines.

**106.** Without fault (knowing no night), dancing beautifully, utterly free of impurity and crookedness, pouring forth the nectar of great love that removes the threefold miseries of all living beings without exception; risen from the milk ocean of Shachi's womb, made beautiful by the dawning of good fortune; the sweetness of His rays is tasted by the *chakora* birds — the hearts of the devotees: the indescribable moon Gauranga shines.

**107.** Again and again moistening His pale cheeks with the water of His eyes, again and again — oh! — heaving long sighs, sobbing aloud, uttering most piteous cries of "Ha! Ha!" — the indescribable Gaura shines, immersed in the mood of a maiden of Vraja in separation.

**108.** Bearing an indescribable colour — the essence of gold refined in fire — now appearing as a frolicsome cowherd boy, now assuming an indescribable divine form, and at times revealing this or that wondrous pastime — Gauranga-chandra shines, manifestly the body of Radha and Madhuripu (Krishna).

## The glory of His descent (avatara-mahima)

**109.** Unexpectedly the wave of the Lord's Name comes to embodied beings, even those surrounded by grievous sins; oh! — even men's hearts, hard as diamond, have become like fresh butter — when He descends into the world. That Gaura is my refuge.

**110.** There is no yoga, no meditation, no *japa*, no austerity, no renunciation, no vows, no Vedas, no piety — what to speak of abstaining from the forbidden! — and yet, when Chaitanya, whose heart is the very essence of compassion, descends, people here joyfully and unexpectedly carry off the highest crown of the goals of man.

**111.** When Sri Chaitanya, wondrous in His glory, descends on earth, the mind that has fallen into the torrent of great *karma* becomes steady; the mind harder than great rocks becomes melted; even the mind of great yogis, giving up its *sadhana*, dances on high.

**112.** When Chaitanya-chandra revealed the supreme path of *bhakti-yoga*, the worldly gave up talk of wife, sons and so on, the learned their disputes about the shastras, the lords of yogis the torments of breath control, the ascetics their austerity, the *sannyasis* their study of knowledge. No other *rasa* remained.

**113.** When Gaura descends into the world, in every house thunders the *sankirtana* of Hari, in every body shines a profusion of thrills and tears, and in every affection there is a state of supreme sweet excellence, far beyond even the Vedas.

**114.** Unexpectedly this whole world was flooded on every side by indescribable floods of *rasa* from the ocean of great love; unexpectedly people were overcome with wonder at transformations never seen or heard of before — when Krishna descends in a body beautiful with golden radiance.

**115.** Formerly some, unrestrainably proud, seized upon all the shastras everywhere; others, thinking themselves fortunate, held to rituals, austerity and other rules high and low; and some, with perverse intent, chanted two or three names of Hari. But now that Gaurachandra has risen, even *prema* has become common.

**116.** When the Lord named Chaitanya descended, service to whose lotus feet the gods pray for, and spread through the universe the sweetest waves of the nectar of love — who is a child, who an old man, who a dullard, who a woman, who a wretch? In all who are devoted to Hari's feet there arose an indescribable oneness of taste.

**117.** All have come here — Shankara, Narada and the others; Sri (Lakshmi) herself has appeared; the god Halayudha (Balarama) has joined Him; those very Vrishnis have been born; and more — the dwellers of Vraja too have appeared: the cowherd boys, the gopis and the others — when Sri Gaurachandra, the Lord of the *rasa* of complete love, descended to earth.

**118.** The servants, the friends, and those who partake of the sweetest, radiant and generous [*rasa*] — all of them descended beside His two lotus feet. When the golden Gaura distributes His wondrous love to the world, they obtained a wealth of the nectar of great love greater than before.

**119.** When Sri Chaitanya, the essence of wondrous glory, descended to earth — ah! — even noble housewives all around laugh loudly, loudly; even hearts made of the stones of base sense attachments melt; even the ignorant put to shame the assembly of those who know all the shastras.

**120.** Formerly even the all-knowing here had hardly any consciousness (*chaitanya*): their meagre, dull reasoning never set foot on the essence of all goals. But now, when out of mercy Gaurachandra has descended into the world — who has not entered into the *bhakti* of sweet love, the radiant *rasa* of deep and generous feelings?

**121.** The supreme purport of the *Srimad Bhagavatam*, which Sri Vaiyasaki (Shuka), even in the account of the *rasa* dance, only indicated without revealing it, because it is hard to grasp; that which is the one true vessel for tasting the *rasa* of the hero (*nagara*) of Radha's love-sports — to spread that treasure, Hari descended into the world in the form of Gaura.

**122.** Some — Uddhava and the others — attained service; others attained the praiseworthy state of Shridama and the other friends; still others attained the mood of the lotus-eyed maidens of Vraja; and others, most fortunate and wise, drink [the nectar of] Radha's lotus feet. What riches has the world not received by the mercy of Sri Chaitanya Mahaprabhu!

**123.** Formerly the all-knowing best of sages spread their various doctrines by arguments, and not one person was firmly convinced of any of them. But now, when Gauranga-chandra of matchless power has risen — who has not again become certain that the highest meaning of the *shruti* is devotion to Hari alone?

**124.** I remember a certain wondrous power of the radiance of the moonstones — the toenails of Gauranga-chandra — which immerses the whole world in the matchless ocean of the excellent nectar *rasa* of great love.

**125.** The [great ones] of old, full of the highest merit and piety, brought perhaps one person or another to fulfilment. But to immerse the whole world in the ocean of love — this no one did.

**126.** Even one who has matchless fidelity to *dharma* and the weightiest devotion to Vishnu sometimes lives here with a heart as hard as iron; while one lower than a cow-killer — oh! — floods the world with streams of tears. Ah, who can fathom the deep play of the golden Gauranga?

**127.** Sometimes, absorbed in Krishna, He dances, enacting many postures; sometimes, absorbed in Radha, He cries out in anguish: "Hari! Hari! Hari!"; sometimes He crawls like an infant; sometimes He behaves like a cowherd boy. So the great Gaura of unfathomable glory astonishes the world.

**128.** On the shore of the salt ocean immersing the world in pastimes in which shines the essence of the feeling that the young cowherd girl (Radha) has for the sweetness of Krishna, the treasury of her *rasa*; already in His childhood games, by His radiance the one enchanter of the universe — an indescribable form, as if made of molten gold, delights my heart.

**129.** Who had ever before heard of the wondrous thing called *prema*? Who knew the glory of the Names? Who entered the great sweetness of the forests of Vrindavana? Who knew Radha — the limit of the sweetness and wonder of the highest *rasa*? Chaitanya-chandra alone, out of supreme mercy, revealed all this.

## The beauty and dance of Gaura

**130.** He covers the universe with millions of reflections of the golden lustre of His body, swaying on the waves of the ocean of the nectar of the *rasa* of complete love; He is praised by the Lord (Shiva), the Creator (Brahma), Vyasa and others; revealing a form that enchants the world, barely discernible by millions of *shrutis* — oh wonder! — on the shore of the salt ocean the Supreme Brahman Himself dances.

**131.** Who is this Gaura, the foremost *nagara*, the crown of the knowers of *rasa*?[^2] His hips are adorned with silk cloth; on His arm is a bracelet, on His breast a necklace, in His ears earrings, on His feet ankle bells; He has gathered up and tied His abundant curls, crowning them with a garland of blossoming jasmine — He sports, dancing to His own names.

**132.** When Sri Gaurachandra, maddened with His own love, performed a vigorous dance on earth, the gods beat the kettledrums, the best of the Gandharvas sang, the Siddhas covered this earth with an unceasing rain of flowers, and hosts of great *rishis* lovingly drew near, offering wondrous hymns.

**133.** Now He laughs, now weeps, now faints, now rolls on the ground, now runs, now dances, now sighs, now utters loud cries of "Ha! Ha!" — intoxicated with the wine of great love, Gaura-Hari sports here.

**134.** With indescribable floods of tears turning the ground before Him to mud, resting on His palm a cheek pale as a *badari* fruit, dwelling — how wondrous! — on the shore of the salt ocean and wearing a red garment, Hari Himself, having become golden, spreads love for the lotus feet of Radha.

**135.** Making the directions resound with the stamping of His feet, turning the ground to mud with drops of tears, — oh! — making the sky bright with peals of laughter, shining like the moon, His red garment swaying on His beautiful, graceful hips — what Lord is this who dances joyfully in the flower garden on the shore of the salt ocean?

## Lament (shochaka)

**136.** Whose nature even the crest-jewels of all the Vedas cannot discern; whose sweetest path, inaccessible even to Lakshmi, Shiva, Brahma and the others, is yet indescribably beautiful and easily accessible to those on whom His merciful glance has fallen; who suddenly intoxicated this world with the wine of the *rasa* of Sri Radha and Hari — can that Sri Chaitanya-chandra be within the reach of my words or my mind?

**137.** Somewhere there is dullness in rituals, somewhere *japa*, austerity, yoga and so on, somewhere ritualistic worship of Govinda, somewhere pride of knowledge; and the sacred *bhakti* of Hari, though radiant, somewhere remains only in words. Ah, Chaitanya, where have You gone? Your path is seen nowhere!

**138.** When Hari appeared, golden as molten gold, by His glory alone the world was immersed in the *rasa* of love, and everywhere the *sankirtana* of Hari thundered loudly, loudly — ah, will that sweet time ever return?

**139.** This is the same blessed capital of Gauda, the same shore of the ocean, the same Sri Purushottama (Jagannatha), the same names of Madhupati — but, Hari, Hari! — such a festival of love is seen nowhere. Ah, Chaitanya, treasury of mercy, shall I ever see Your glory again?

**140.** If one says that Gaurachandra is like the partial incarnations — the Fish and the rest — He is not so: for they are only the manifestation of some potency and pastime. But He, whose greatness is unfathomable, revealing wondrous pastimes with the matchless fullness of all potencies, descended as the complete [Lord].

**141.** Though Mahaprabhu has a glory more wondrous even than that of the Original One, the source of Brahma and Shiva, on hearing the prattle of an innocent child He will surely be tender.

**142.** I have not seen the shastras, I have not seen gurus, the wise have not examined [my words] with their keen intellect — so however I may prattle like a child, may Gaura-Hari be pleased with me.

*Thus ends the Sri Sri Chaitanya-chandramrita in twelve sections, composed by Shripada Srimat Prabodhananda Sarasvati — the jewel in the crown of the family of the tridandi-gosvamis, the best of wandering teachers, an eminent associate of Gaura.*

[^1]: "Knowers of non-duality" (*advaita*) — the experts in non-dualism (Advaita Vedanta); this is also how Bhaktisiddhanta explains the word.

[^2]: *Nagara-vara* — according to Bhaktisiddhanta, "the crown of the highest knowers of *rasa*": Gaura is Krishna Himself, enacting His pastime on the side of the *ashraya* (tasting the feelings of Radha). It does not mean a worldly "gallant lover," nor does it support the doctrine of *gaura-nagari*, which Bhaktisiddhanta rejected.
