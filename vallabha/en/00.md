# Sri Chaitanya in the Texts of Other Traditions

*The Vallabha vartas (Braj-bhasha, 17th c.) and Yadunatha's Vallabha-digvijaya (Sanskrit, 1610); the Bhaktamals of
Nabhadas, Dhruvadas and Raghavdas (Braj-bhasha, 17th c.); the kavitts of Priyadas (1712)*

*Translated into English with notes*

---

## Translator's Preface

Sri Chaitanya Mahaprabhu and Vallabhacharya (1479–1531), founder of the Pushti-marga, were contemporaries. The
*Chaitanya-charitamrita* tells of two meetings between them: at Adaila near Prayaga, where Vallabha Bhatta received
the Lord in his home (Madhya 19), and at Puri, where the Lord humbled Vallabha's pride in his learning and then
received him with mercy (Antya 7). The Vallabha tradition has kept its own stories of Chaitanya. Collected and
translated here are **all the passages found** in which Sri Krishna-Chaitanya acts or is mentioned:

- **the vartas** — prose lives in Braj-bhasha: the *Nij-varta* (the life of Vallabha), the *Baithak-charitra* (accounts of the 84 "seats", the places where Vallabha read the Bhagavata) and the *Chaurasi Vaishnavan ki Varta* (on his 84 disciples). Their core is the oral tellings of Vallabha's grandson Gokulanatha (1551–1640), written down and reworked in the seventeenth century; translated from the edition of Govardhandas Lakshmidas (Bombay, Samvat 1959 [1902/03]);
- **Yadunatha's Vallabha-digvijaya** (by another grandson of Vallabha; Samvat 1667 [1610/11]) — a condensed Sanskrit list of events, in which the meeting with Chaitanya is named in a single phrase.

**Why category D.** This is the tradition of another school, formed more than a century after the events. The
stories are sectarian: in them Chaitanya is as a rule the junior of Vallabha, and Vallabha himself is "Sri Acharyaji
Mahaprabhu", "Purna Purushottama". The chronology is often impossible: the meeting in Vrindavana is dated Samvat 1548
(1491, when the Lord was five years old) and involves Jiva Gosvami, born about 1513; Shyamananda and the appearance
of Radha-ramana (1542) are placed in Vallabha's lifetime; Madhavendra Puri is called "a Telanga brahmin", "the guru
of Krishna-Chaitanya" and "a disciple of Vyasatirtha". Hence in the combined lila database all entries from these
texts have category D ("doubtful"). Their value lies elsewhere: they are a witness to Sri Chaitanya from outside the
Gaudiya circle. The vartas see in Him a great bhakta, Jagannatha's "dear bhakta", one who knows separation and the
holy Name; in one place Vallabha feeds Him with unoffered food, "seeing in His body the dwelling of Sri Krishna".
Some details echo the Gaudiya sources: the Bengali servants of Gopala (Shrinathji) on Govardhana and Madhavendra
Puri, Adaila on the Lord's road, Puri and the Ratha-yatra in Yadunatha.

**How to read.** The translation is faithful to the text: nothing is softened or "corrected" to fit Gaudiya theology.
Where the text diverges from the *Chaitanya-charitamrita* or from the Gaudiya position (after Srila Bhaktisiddhanta
Sarasvati: Sri Chaitanya is Krishna Himself in the mood and complexion of Radha), this is said in the notes — briefly
and without polemic. The episodes are numbered (1.1–1.12 the vartas, 2.1 Yadunatha), the paragraphs within each
episode; the headings give the section of the original and the pages of the PDF scan (`sources/vallabha/`). Where
only the connected part about Chaitanya is translated from a long story, the omission is marked "[…]" and summarized
in a note. An unqualified "Mahaprabhu" in the translation always means Vallabha (as in the text). Misprints and
obscure places in the edition are noted. A catalogue of all passages, including brief mentions and dead ends, is in
`CATALOG.md`; names are in `GLOSSARY.md`.

**Sources.** Vartas: ed. Yaduvamshiya Govardhandas Lakshmidas, Bombay, Tattva-vivechak Press, Samvat 1959 (IA
`in.ernet.dli.2015.308511`). Yadunatha: the Sanskrit text from the 1985 edition (Nathdwara; IA
`vallabhadigvijayayadunathjimaharaj1985_202003_900_R`); the Hindi translation and notes of that edition were not used.
The 1908 edition entitled *Vallabha-digvijaya* proved to be a poem by Krishnashastri (first part, up to the arrival
in Mathura) and does not contain the meeting with Chaitanya.

**Parts 3–4: the Bhaktamals and Priyadas.** The second half of the book gives testimonies of other schools of North
India. The Bhaktamal ("Garland of Devotees") is a collection of short verses of praise on the saints of all
traditions. In the Bhaktamal of Nabhadas (Ramanandi, c. 1600) a separate chhappay is devoted to Nityananda and
Krishna-Chaitanya: They are avataras who appeared in the eastern land in the form of saints and uprooted the impiety of
Gauda; this is the earliest known mention of Mahaprabhu outside Bengal. There too Rupa and Sanatana, Jiva, Gopala
Bhatta, Raghunatha Dasa, Lokanatha, Kashishvara and Prabodhananda are glorified. Dhruvadas (Radhavallabhi, Vrindavana,
1620s–1640s) opens his list of the saints of Vrindavana with the Gaudiyas: "The whole of Gauda was delivered —
Krishna-Chaitanya appeared." Raghavdas (Dadu-panthi, 1660) places Nityananda, Chaitanya and the Gosvamis in the section
of the Madhva sampradaya and directly calls Chaitanya Krishna and Nityananda Balarama. All the verses found are
translated in part 3 — with the original (the verses are short) and notes on persons and variant readings.

Part 4 gives the kavitts of Priyadas, a Gaudiya by initiation, on the Bhaktamal (1712): on Nityananda and Sri Chaitanya
(Krishna took the colour of the gopis' love — "the Son of Yashoda became the Son of Shachi"), on the young Mahaprabhu's
victory over the digvijayi Keshava Kashmiri in Nadia, and on the associates where Mahaprabhu is named. The kavitts on
Keshava Kashmiri are absent from the Naval Kishore lithographs (1883) and, as Rupkala remarks, were excluded from their
copies by the Nimbarkis; they are translated from the Bombay edition of 1914, where they survive. This is late,
largely a retelling of CC — but valuable as evidence of how Gaudiya narratives entered the common hagiography of Braj.
In the lila database all these texts are category D (`bhaktamal`).
