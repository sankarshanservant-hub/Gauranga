# 1. The Vartas (Nij-varta, Baithak-charitra, Chaurasi Vaishnavan ki Varta)

*The vartas (vārtā, "accounts") are prose lives of Vallabhacharya (1479–1531), founder of the Pushti-marga, and of
his disciples, in Braj-bhasha. Their core is the oral tellings of his grandson Gokulanatha (1551–1640), written down
and reworked in the seventeenth century (Hariraya and others). Translated from the edition of Govardhandas
Lakshmidas (Bombay, Samvat 1959 [1902/03]); headings give the section and the pages of the PDF scan
(`sources/vallabha/`). All passages found in which Sri Krishna-Chaitanya acts or is mentioned are translated (catalogue
in `CATALOG.md`). **This is a tradition of the Vallabha school — late and sectarian, with anachronisms; in the lila
database it is category D.** The translation follows the text; the Gaudiya position and divergences from the
*Chaitanya-charitamrita* (CC) are given in the notes.*

## 1.1. Jagannatha's command at Puri (Nij-varta, varta-prasang 15; PDF pp. 55–56)

*Puri, the temple of Jagannatha. Present: Sri Acharyaji (Vallabhacharya), Sri Jagannatha; Krishna-Chaitanya is mentioned.*

[…][^1]

**1.** After staying there some days, Sri Acharyaji[^2] came to Sri Jagannatharayaji to take leave. Then a loud,
terrible sound like a peal of thunder arose in the temple. As soon as they heard it, all the panda brahmins[^3] ran
out of the temple and stood outside, and the doors of the temple closed. Sri Acharyaji alone remained in the temple.

**2.** And Sri Jagannathaji commanded him: "The path of service that you have revealed is very dear to Me. Now,
through your line, spread the path of service and reveal it in its fullness.

**3.** And the book *Sri Krishna-premamrita* that you composed — give it to Our dear bhakta Krishna-Chaitanya.[^4]

**4.** And spread in your path the book *Gita-govinda* composed by Jayadeva.[^5]

**5.** And the dish of aubergines that you forbade — bring it back into use."[^6]

**6.** Then he said, "As You command," prostrated himself fully, opened the leaves of the doors, and Sri Acharyaji
came out. Everyone was greatly astonished. After that he went on from there to sanctify the earth.[^7]

## 1.2. The Bengali brahmins as servants of Shrinathji; Madhavendra Puri (Nij-varta, varta-prasang 22; PDF pp. 67–68)

*Govardhana, the temple of Sri Govardhananathaji (Shrinathji). Present: Sri Acharyaji, Saddu Pande, the Bengali
brahmins, Madhavendra Puri, Krishnadas, Kumbhandas, Avadhutdas, Sri Gusainji; Krishna-Chaitanya is mentioned.*

**1.** One day Sri Acharyaji Mahaprabhu called Saddu Pande and commanded: "The temple of Sri Govardhananathaji is
finished, but so large a temple needs many servants too. You are a brahmin — so serve Sri Govardhananathaji. For this
is the rule: it is good when the service of Bhagavan is done by brahmins."

**2.** Then Saddu Pande said to him: "Maharaj, the people of our caste have no understanding at all of the rules of
purity. Entrust the service to someone who understands something of service."

**3.** Then Sri Acharyaji thought to himself: "There are brahmins living at Shrikund — they are servants of
Krishna-Chaitanya. Let us appoint them."[^8] And he called those Bengali brahmins and gave them the command to serve.

**4.** Among them was Madhavendra Puri — an acharya of the Madhva-sampradaya, a Telanga brahmin, the guru of
Krishna-Chaitanya;[^9] with him Sri Acharyaji had studied the Vedas at Kashi, and at that time it had been said that
he would give him the service of Bhagavan.[^10] Him he made the head, and his disciples he placed in the service.

**5.** He made Krishnadasji the adhikari,[^11] gave Kumbhandas the service of kirtan, and taught them all his own
custom and order. He fixed Sri Govardhananathaji's daily *neg*: "This much food Sri Govardhananathaji will partake of
every day."[^12]

**6.** Then he said to the Bengalis: "Saddu Pande will bring you this *neg* every day; if more comes, take up more;
but do not make it less than this *neg*. And maintain yourselves on that mahaprasad." So Sri Acharyaji commanded.

**7.** And he said: "Do not miss His hours. Offer whatever comes by the will of Bhagavan, but be careful that Sri
Thakurji is not kept waiting."

**8.** And for fourteen years the Bengalis served Shrinathji. Then Shrinathji became displeased with the Bengalis'
service and told Avadhutdas and Krishnadas to drive them out, saying: "These Bengalis steal My wealth and carry it
off — drive them out."[^13]

**9.** After Sri Acharyaji they served for three more years. Then, at the command of Sri[nathji], Sri Gusainji drove
them out and placed Gujarati brahmins in the service.[^14]

## Notes


[^1]: Omitted: the beginning of prasang 15 (PDF pp. 49–55) — Vallabha's stay at Puri, his debate with Mayavadi
brahmins before the king (called "Raja Bhojadev" in the text), the note with the verses "एकं शास्त्रं
देवकीपुत्रगीतम्…" said to have been written by Jagannatha's own hand, and the punishment of a slanderous brahmin.
Chaitanya is not mentioned there.

[^2]: "Sri Acharyaji Mahaprabhu" is the vartas' constant name for Vallabhacharya; in the Pushti-marga the title
"Mahaprabhu" belongs to him. In this translation an unqualified "Mahaprabhu" always means Vallabha; Sri Chaitanya is
called in the vartas only "Krishna-Chaitanya" (कृष्णचैतन्य).

[^3]: Pandas (पंडया) — the temple priests of Puri.

[^4]: The *Sri Krishna-premamrita* (श्रीकृष्णप्रेमामृत) is a stotra of names of Krishna which the Vallabha tradition
ascribes to Vallabha; its origin is told in baithak 13 (§ 1.9), where the book is in fact given to Krishna-Chaitanya.
Jagannatha calls Chaitanya "Our dear bhakta": the vartas see him as a great devotee. For Gaudiya Vaishnavas Sri
Krishna-Chaitanya is Krishna Himself, who has taken the mood and complexion of Radha (CC Adi 1.5; 4.55–56; so too Srila
Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati teaches); His appearance as a devotee is the lila of the Lord Himself, come to taste Radha's
love and to teach bhakti. According to the CC (Antya 7) Vallabha Bhatta himself came to the Lord at Puri, and the Lord
humbled his pride in his learning; the Gaudiya sources know nothing of a book passing from Vallabha to Chaitanya.

[^5]: Jayadeva's *Gita-govinda* is the book Sri Chaitanya loved, which He relished with Svarupa Damodara and Ramananda
Raya (CC Madhya 2.77; Antya 15 etc.); its songs are sung before Jagannatha.

[^6]: "वैंताकको साक" — a dish (*shak*) of aubergines (Skt. *vṛntāka*). According to the varta Vallabha had earlier
forbidden it in the service, and Jagannatha tells him to lift the ban.

[^7]: "पृथ्वी पावन करिवेकों" — "to purify the earth": the vartas' name for Vallabha's pilgrimage circuits ("the first,
second, third circumambulation of the earth").

[^8]: "Shrikund" (श्रीकुंड) — apparently Radha-kunda by Govardhana. According to the CC (Madhya 4.22–43) the Deity of
Gopala on Govardhana was revealed by Madhavendra Puri, who set two Bengali brahmin disciples to serve Him; Gaudiyas
identify Madhavendra's Gopala with Shrinathji. The varta keeps the same pairing — Madhavendra and the Bengali
servants — but has Vallabha appoint them. The preceding prasang (21) dates the building of the temple Samvat
1556–1576 [1499–1519 CE].

[^9]: The Gaudiya sources do not call Madhavendra Puri a "Telanga brahmin"; Yadunatha's *Vallabha-digvijaya* calls him
"an Andhra" (`CATALOG.md` no. 19). Vallabha's own Telugu origin may have been transferred to him. In the Gaudiya
parampara (Kavi Karnapura, *Gaura-ganoddesha-dipika* 21–22) Madhavendra is a sannyasi in the line of Madhva, disciple
of Lakshmipati Tirtha; Sri Chaitanya's guru is his disciple Ishvara Puri (CC Adi 9.10–13; 17.8), so that Madhavendra is
the Lord's guru's guru. According to the CC (Madhya 4) Madhavendra left the world at Remuna before the Lord's sannyasa
(1510), so he could not have served in a temple finished in 1519.

[^10]: "ता समें भगवत्सेवा देवेकी कही हती" — "at that time the giving of the service of Bhagavan had been spoken of":
the subject is not named; the sense is that Vallabha then promised Madhavendra to give him a service.

[^11]: Krishnadas Adhikari — manager of Shrinathji's temple, one of the eight poets of the Ashta-chhap, as was
Kumbhandas.

[^12]: *Neg* (नेग) — a fixed share, the daily measure of foodstuffs for offering.

[^13]: The expulsion of the Bengalis is told at length in the *Ashtasakha ki varta* (the life of Krishnadas Adhikari:
the burning of the Bengalis' huts, their complaint to Rupa and Sanatana at Mathura; PDF pp. 594–600; Chaitanya is not
named there — not translated, `CATALOG.md` no. 22). The Gaudiya sources do not describe it; according to the CC (Madhya
18) Gopala, sheltering from the Muslims, stayed a month at Mathura in the house of Vitthaleshvara (Vallabha's son),
and Rupa Gosvami and his companions had His darshan there.

[^14]: Sri Gusainji — Vitthalanatha (1516–1586), Vallabha's younger son. "Gujarati brahmins" (गुर्जर ब्राह्मण) — the
service of Shrinathji (now at Nathdwara) has remained with them since.
