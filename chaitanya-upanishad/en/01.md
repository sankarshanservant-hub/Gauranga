# Sri Chaitanya Upanishad

*The Śrī-Śrī-Caitanyopaniṣat of the Atharva Veda, with the Sanskrit commentary of Srila Bhaktivinoda Thakura, “Śrī-Śrī-Caitanya-caraṇāmṛta,” and the Bengali translation “Amṛta-bindu”*[^1]

## The Commentator’s Introduction

> **Commentary “Śrī-Śrī-Caitanya-caraṇāmṛta.”** Bowing to Chaitanya, the embodiment of rasa, together with the Pañca-tattva,[^2] I compose, for my own purification, a commentary on the Chaitanya Upanishad.
>
> This all-blissful Śrī Chaitanya Upanishad, contained within the beginningless Atharva Veda, did not come to light until now, because it lay beyond the sight of people turned away [from God], whose eyes were blinded by māyā. Then, when Śrī Navadvīpa-candra[^3] descended, it was made known through the efforts of pure devotees. According to the principle of the Bhāgavata, “in Kali You are hidden” (Bhāg. 7.9.38),[^4] the śāstras concerning Bhagavān Śrī Chaitanya-deva, who is to be worshipped in the Kali age, are also hidden in form; therefore it is no wonder that those bewildered by karma and jñāna, through their misfortune, have no faith in it.

## Verse 1

अथ पिप्पलादः समित्पाणिर्भगवन्तं ब्रह्माणमुपसन्नो
भगवन्मे शुभं किमत्र चक्षस्वेति ॥ १ ॥

*atha pippalādaḥ samit-pāṇir bhagavantaṁ brahmāṇam upasanno*
*bhagavan me śubhaṁ kim atra cakṣasveti || 1 ||*

*Word for word:* atha — then; pippalādaḥ — Pippalāda; samit-pāṇiḥ — with sacrificial fuel in hand; bhagavantam — the lord; brahmāṇam — Brahmā; upasannaḥ — approaching; bhagavan — O lord; me — to me; śubham — good; kim — what; atra — here (in this world); cakṣasva — declare; iti — thus [he said]

**1.** Then Pippalāda, with sacrificial fuel in hand,[^5] approached the lord Brahmā [and said]: “O lord, declare to me: what is the good here?”

> **Commentary.** Now we follow the subject proper. “Then” — after studying many śāstras and practising many kinds of worship, the sage Pippalāda, wishing to know his own highest good, came to his guru, the four-faced [Brahmā], with sacrificial fuel in hand, and asked: “By what will my highest good come about?”
>
> **“Amṛta-bindu.”** Then Pippalāda came to his father,[^6] the lord Brahmā, with the question: “O lord! Tell me, what in this world is my highest good?”

## Verse 2

स होवाच ।
भूय एव तपसा ब्रह्मचर्येण शश्वत् रमस्व मनो वशेति ॥ २ ॥

*sa hovāca |*
*bhūya eva tapasā brahmacaryeṇa śaśvat ramasva mano vaśeti || 2 ||*

*Word for word:* saḥ — he (Brahmā); ha — indeed; uvāca — said; bhūyaḥ eva — once more; tapasā — by austerity; brahmacaryeṇa — by celibacy; śaśvat — constantly; ramasva — abide (lit. “take delight”); manaḥ — the mind; vaśa — subdue; iti — thus

**2.** He said: “Once more, by austerity and celibacy, constantly abide [in them], and subdue the mind.”[^7]

> **Commentary.** The four-faced one said to him, instructing: “For another year, by austerity — the practice of yoga — and by celibacy — pure conduct born of detachment — purify your body and mind, and then come.”
>
> **“Amṛta-bindu.”** Brahmā said: “Devote a long time to austerity and celibacy, and conquer the mind.”

## Verse 3

स तथा भूत्वा भूय एनमुपसद्याह
भगवन्कलौ पापाच्छन्नाः प्रजाः कथं मुच्येरन्निति ॥ ३ ॥

*sa tathā bhūtvā bhūya enam upasadyāha —*
*bhagavan kalau pāpācchannāḥ prajāḥ kathaṁ mucyerann iti || 3 ||*

*Word for word:* saḥ — he (Pippalāda); tathā — so; bhūtvā — having done (lit. “having become”); bhūyaḥ — again; enam — him; upasadya — approaching; āha — said; bhagavan — O lord; kalau — in [the age of] Kali; pāpa-ācchannāḥ — covered by sin; prajāḥ — living beings; katham — how; mucyeran — will be liberated; iti — thus

**3.** Having done so, he approached him again and said: “O lord, how will the living beings in Kali, covered by sin, be liberated?”

> **Commentary.** Pippalāda, having purified his body and mind by austerity and celibacy, came again into the presence of his guru and asked: “How will those in Kali, whose course is sinful by the very nature [of the age], attain liberation from matter?”
>
> **“Amṛta-bindu.”** Pippalāda, following this and having purified his heart, came again to his father and said: “O lord! How will the people of the Kali age, covered by sin, be liberated?”

## Verse 4

को वा देवता को वा मन्त्रो ब्रूहीति ॥ ४ ॥

*ko vā devatā ko vā mantro brūhīti || 4 ||*

*Word for word:* kaḥ vā — who then; devatā — the deity; kaḥ vā — what then; mantraḥ — the mantra; brūhi — tell; iti — thus

**4.** “Who is [their] Deity, and what is the mantra? Tell me.”

> **Commentary.** “O lord! Tell me: who is the Īśvara to be worshipped by the living beings whose consciousness is defiled by Kali, and what is the mantra of worship?”
>
> **“Amṛta-bindu.”** “Tell me: who is the Deity to be worshipped in the Kali age, and what is the mantra of bhajana?”

## Verse 5

स होवाच । रहस्यं ते वदिष्यामि
जाह्नवीतीरे नवद्वीपे गोलोकाख्ये धाम्नि गोविन्दो द्विभुजो
गौरः सर्वात्मा महापुरुषो महात्मा महायोगी
त्रिगुणातीतः सत्त्वरूपो भक्तिं लोके काश्यतीत ।
तदेते श्लोका भवन्ति ॥ ५ ॥

*sa hovāca | rahasyaṁ te vadiṣyāmi —*
*jāhnavī-tīre navadvīpe golokākhye dhāmni govindo dvi-bhujo*
*gauraḥ sarvātmā mahā-puruṣo mahātmā mahā-yogī*
*tri-guṇātītaḥ sattva-rūpo bhaktiṁ loke kāśyatīta |*
*tad ete ślokā bhavanti || 5 ||*

*Word for word:* saḥ ha uvāca — he said; rahasyam — a secret; te — to you; vadiṣyāmi — I shall tell; jāhnavī-tīre — on the bank of the Jāhnavī (Ganges); navadvīpe — in Navadvīpa; goloka-ākhye dhāmni — in the abode called Goloka; govindaḥ — Govinda; dvi-bhujaḥ — two-armed; gauraḥ — golden (Gaura); sarva-ātmā — the Self of all; mahā-puruṣaḥ — the Great Person; mahā-ātmā — the great soul; mahā-yogī — the great yogī (master of potencies); tri-guṇa-atītaḥ — beyond the three guṇas; sattva-rūpaḥ — whose form is [pure] sattva; bhaktim — bhakti; loke — in the world; kāśyati — will manifest; iti — thus; tat — on this; ete ślokāḥ — these verses; bhavanti — there are

**5.** He said: “I shall tell you a secret. On the bank of the Jāhnavī, in Navadvīpa, in the abode called Goloka, Govinda — two-armed, golden (Gaura), the Self of all, the Great Person, the great soul, the great Yogī, beyond the three guṇas, whose form is [pure] sattva — will manifest bhakti in the world.”[^8] On this there are these verses.

> **Commentary.** Brahmā, the knower of the secret of all the Vedas, said to him: “To you I shall tell that secret. On the bank of the Jāhnavī, in the supreme abode of Navadvīpa, called Goloka, made manifest [on earth], the Self of all — the Self of all beings — the Great Person, the great soul, the great Yogī — the possessor of the spiritual potency — beyond the three guṇas — beyond māyā — whose form is sattva — that is, whose form is pure spirit — Bhagavān, the two-armed Śrī Kṛṣṇa, having become Śrī Gaurāṅga in His form, will manifest bhakti in the world — supreme bhakti, characterized by prema.” In the Eleventh Canto, with the words “I bow, O Great Person, to Your lotus feet” (Bhāg. 11.5.33–34),[^9] it is established that Bhagavān Śrī Chaitanya-deva, the purifier of the Kali age, is the Great Person (Mahāpuruṣa).
>
> **“Amṛta-bindu.”** Brahmā said: “I shall tell you this supremely hidden truth. The Self of all, the Great Person, the Supersoul, the great Yogī, beyond the three guṇas, consisting of pure sattva, the two-armed Śyāmasundara Himself, having descended as Gaurasundara in the abode of Navadvīpa on the bank of the Jāhnavī, which is called Goloka, will manifest bhakti in the world.” On this subject these verses are spoken.

## Verse 6

एको देवः सर्वरूपी महात्मा
गौरो रक्तश्यामलश्वेतरूपः ।
चैतन्यात्मा स वै चैतन्यशक्तिर्
भक्ताकारो भक्तिदो भक्तिवेद्यः ॥ ६ ॥

*eko devaḥ sarva-rūpī mahātmā*
*gauro rakta-śyāmala-śveta-rūpaḥ |*
*caitanyātmā sa vai caitanya-śaktir*
*bhaktākāro bhakti-do bhakti-vedyaḥ || 6 ||*

*Word for word:* ekaḥ — one; devaḥ — God; sarva-rūpī — assuming all forms; mahā-ātmā — the great soul; gauraḥ — Gaura (golden); rakta — red; śyāmala — dark; śveta — white; rūpaḥ — [having] form; caitanya-ātmā — whose self is consciousness (Chaitanya); saḥ — He; vai — truly; caitanya-śaktiḥ — possessor of the spiritual potency; bhakta-ākāraḥ — in the form of a devotee; bhakti-daḥ — giver of bhakti; bhakti-vedyaḥ — known through bhakti

**6.** The one God, assuming all forms, the great soul, is Gaura, [who in other ages is] red, dark, and white in form. He is Chaitanya (Consciousness) itself;[^10] He is [the possessor of] the spiritual potency; He is in the form of a devotee, the giver of bhakti, known through bhakti.

> **Commentary.** That Great Person in the form of Gaura at times, in the three yugas beginning with Kṛta, becomes red, dark, and white in colour — this is seen in the words of Garga and others: “white, red, and likewise yellow; now He has become black” (Bhāg. 10.8.13).[^11] He alone, in Kali, is in the form of a devotee. For the swift good of the living beings of the Kali age He first sent into the world the devotees Śaṭhakopa, Rāmānuja, Viṣṇusvāmī, Madhvācārya, Nimbāditya, and others; and then, desiring to bestow prema — the supreme secret, the unfolding of madhura-rasa — He Himself, too, becoming the embodiment of bhakti whose self is Śrī Kṛṣṇa-Caitanya, descended together with His own abode Goloka upon the land of Gauḍa, which thereby became the supreme tīrtha. This is the secret. “Known through bhakti” — to be known by the living beings through the function of bhakti, not through the function of dry knowledge.
>
> **“Amṛta-bindu.”** The one supreme Deity, assuming all forms, the Great Person Gauracandra, in the other three yugas assumes white, red, and dark forms. He is Consciousness itself, the possessor of the spiritual potency; He is in the form of a devotee, the giver of bhakti, known through bhakti.

## Verse 7

नमो वेदान्तवेद्याय कृष्णाय परमात्मने ।
सर्वचैतन्यरूपाय चैतन्याय नमो नमः ॥ ७ ॥

*namo vedānta-vedyāya kṛṣṇāya paramātmane |*
*sarva-caitanya-rūpāya caitanyāya namo namaḥ || 7 ||*

*Word for word:* namaḥ — obeisance; vedānta-vedyāya — to the one known through Vedānta; kṛṣṇāya — to Kṛṣṇa; parama-ātmane — to the Supreme Self; sarva-caitanya-rūpāya — to the form of all consciousness; caitanyāya — to Chaitanya; namaḥ namaḥ — obeisance, obeisance

**7.** Obeisance to Kṛṣṇa, known through Vedānta, the Supreme Self — to Chaitanya, the form of all consciousness, obeisance, obeisance!

> **Commentary.** Even the four-faced [Brahmā], considering His boundless mercy toward his own Mādhva-sampradāya,[^12] bows down to that Chaitanya: “Obeisance to the one known through Vedānta…”
>
> **“Amṛta-bindu.”** Again and again obeisance to Śrī Chaitanya-deva — Śrī Kṛṣṇa, known through Vedānta, the Supersoul, the form of all consciousness.

## Verse 8

वेदान्तवेद्यं पुरुषं पुराणं
चैतन्यात्मानं विश्वयोनिं महान्तम् ।
तमेव विदित्वातिमृत्युमेति
नान्यः पन्था विद्यतेऽयनाय ॥ ८ ॥

*vedānta-vedyaṁ puruṣaṁ purāṇaṁ*
*caitanyātmānaṁ viśva-yoniṁ mahāntam |*
*tam eva viditvāti-mṛtyum eti*
*nānyaḥ panthā vidyate 'yanāya || 8 ||*

*Word for word:* vedānta-vedyam — known through Vedānta; puruṣam — the Person; purāṇam — primeval; caitanya-ātmānam — whose self is Chaitanya (consciousness); viśva-yonim — the source of the universe; mahāntam — the Great; tam eva — Him alone; viditvā — having known; ati-mṛtyum — beyond death; eti — goes; na — not; anyaḥ — other; panthāḥ — path; vidyate — exists; ayanāya — for going (to the goal)

**8.** Only by knowing Him — the primeval Person known through Vedānta, whose self is Chaitanya, the source of the universe, the Great — does one pass beyond death; there is no other path for going [to the goal].[^13]

> **Commentary.** Having again reflected upon all the statements of Vedānta, [Brahmā] says to Pippalāda: “Known through Vedānta…” That Chaitanya, though in the form of a devotee, is the one known through Vedānta — Śrī Kṛṣṇa Himself. For the “going” of the living beings — entering the spiritual world after leaving the material world — there is no other path than taking shelter at the feet of Śrī Chaitanya. Through the dāsya-rasa revealed by Śrī Rāmānuja and others the “going” is never complete: the course of those who serve in dāsya and [similar rasas] reaches as far as Vaikuṇṭha, as may be seen in their books.[^14] [This is so] because Chaitanya is the source of the universe and the sole teacher in the science of the rasa of Vraja.
>
> **“Amṛta-bindu.”** Only by knowing Śrī Chaitanya-deva — known through Vedānta, the primeval Person, the embodiment of consciousness, the cause of the universe, the Great — can one pass beyond death. There is no other means of crossing over māyā.

[^1]: Translated from the edition of Sri Gopinath Gaudiya Math (Mayapur, ed. Sridas Baba, Gaurabda 448 ≈ 1934), where the Sanskrit is printed in Bengali script; here it is given in Devanagari. The text of the Upanishad has been collated with the electronic text of sanskritdocuments.org; variants are noted. The commentary “Śrī-Śrī-Caitanya-caraṇāmṛta” was composed by Bhaktivinoda Thakura (in the colophon, “Kedāra”) and first published in 1887. The Bengali prose translation “Amṛta-bindu” (“A Drop of Nectar”), according to its colophon, is by Madhusudana Dasa of Sambalpur; it renders the mūla, sometimes weaving in explanations from the commentary. Both the commentary and the “Amṛta-bindu” are translated in full.
[^2]: The Pañca-tattva — Śrī Chaitanya in five aspects: the Lord Himself (Chaitanya), His expansion (Nityānanda), His incarnation (Advaita), His energy (Gadādhara), and His devotee (Śrīvāsa).
[^3]: “The moon of Navadvīpa” — Śrī Chaitanya.
[^4]: Bhāg. 7.9.38 (Prahlāda’s prayer): in the Kali age the Lord is “hidden” (channaḥ), and therefore He is called Triyuga. Gaudiya Vaishnavas see here a reference to Śrī Chaitanya, who appeared in the guise of a devotee.
[^5]: Samit-pāṇi, “fuel in hand”: the ancient custom of a disciple approaching a teacher (cf. Muṇḍaka Up. 1.2.12; Praśna Up. 1.1, where six disciples come with fuel to Pippalāda himself).
[^6]: The “Amṛta-bindu” calls Brahmā Pippalāda’s father; the commentary calls him his guru. In the Praśna Upaniṣad (Atharva Veda) Pippalāda is himself a teacher; in the Purāṇas he is the son of the ṛṣi Dadhīci. None of these traditions contradicts Brahmā, the first guru, instructing him.
[^7]: Cf. Praśna Up. 1.2, where Pippalāda tells his disciples: “Live another year in austerity, celibacy, and faith.” Hence the commentary’s gloss, “for another year.” “Ramasva” literally means “take delight, abide”; “vaśa” we take as the imperative “subdue” (vaśaya); Madhusudana has “conquer the mind.” The electronic text misprints “brahmacāryeṇa”; the 1934 edition misprints “bhuya” for “bhūya.”
[^8]: “Kāśyatīta” — so in both witnesses; we read it as kāśyati iti, “will manifest (make shine),” with the particle closing direct speech; the commentary glosses “prakāśayiṣyati,” “will manifest.” The commentary explains “mahā-yogī” as “possessor of the spiritual potency” (cit-śakti), and “sattva-rūpa” as “whose form is pure spirit” (viśuddha-sattva, not the material mode of goodness).
[^9]: Bhāg. 11.5.33–34 (“dhyeyaṁ sadā paribhava-ghnam…,” “tyaktvā su-dustyaja…”): prayers to the Mahāpuruṣa who, according to Karabhājana, is worshipped in the Kali age; Gaudiya Vaishnavas apply them to Śrī Chaitanya (Jīva Gosvāmī, Kṛṣṇadāsa Kavirāja).
[^10]: A play on words: “caitanya” means “consciousness, spirit”; “Chaitanya” is the Lord’s name. “Caitanya-ātmā” can be understood both as “whose self is consciousness” and as “He who is Chaitanya Himself.”
[^11]: Bhāg. 10.8.13: Garga’s words at Kṛṣṇa’s name-giving: “In previous yugas He assumed three colours — white, red, and yellow; now He has become black (Kṛṣṇa).” Gaudiya Vaishnavas understand “yellow” (pīta) as referring to Śrī Gaura. The mūla of the Upanishad lists red, dark, and white — the forms of the other ages; golden (Gaura) is His own form in Kali.
[^12]: Brahmā is the first ācārya of the Brahma-sampradāya, in which Madhva taught; according to Gaudiya tradition (Kavikarṇapūra, Baladeva Vidyābhūṣaṇa), Śrī Chaitanya accepted initiation in this line (through Īśvara Purī) — hence “Brahma-Madhva-Gauḍīya-sampradāya.”
[^13]: The verse almost literally coincides with Śvetāśvatara Up. 3.8 (“vedāham etaṁ puruṣaṁ mahāntam ādityavarṇaṁ tamasaḥ parastāt / tam eva viditvāti mṛtyum eti nānyaḥ panthā vidyate ’yanāya”); cf. also 3.21 and 4.18.
[^14]: This does not mean that Vaikuṇṭha is something lower or mundane: Vaikuṇṭha is the eternal spiritual abode of the Lord in His majesty (aiśvarya); but the sweetness (mādhurya) of Vraja, taught by Śrī Chaitanya, is higher. So Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati too explains, following the Chaitanya-charitamrita.
