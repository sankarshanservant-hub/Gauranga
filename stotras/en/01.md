# 1. Raghunatha Dasa Gosvami. *Shachisunv-ashtaka*

*"Eight Verses on the Son of Shachi" (in Puridasa's edition, "Sri Chaitanyashtaka"); 9 verses, shikharini metre[^1]*

**1.** Hari, having seen in Vraja His own incomparable reflection in a mirror[^2], in order to attain His own sweetness in all its fullness, as Radha, His dearest friend, attains it, was — O wonder! — born as the Lord in Gauda, taking a body golden with another's gold alone[^3]. Will the son of Shachi come again before my eyes?

**2.** He is sweet from bathing in the honey of Puri Gosai's inmost love[^4]; His feet are ceaselessly worshipped by the pure service that rises from Govinda[^5]; His face is waved with lamps by lotuses — the hundred millions of Svarupa's lives[^6]. Will the son of Shachi come again before my eyes?

**3.** He wears a kaupina, and over it an outer cloth of ruddy hue; majestic, He is served on every side by the splendour of a golden mountain[^7]; joyfully He sings aloud the garland of His own sweet names[^8]. Will the son of Shachi come again before my eyes?

**4.** The creeper of bhakti, which even the former hosts of sages skilled in devotion could not make known, hidden in the Veda, bearing as its fruit the radiant rasa of prema — that creeper the merciful Lord revealed in Gauda out of His great mercy[^9]. Will the son of Shachi come again before my eyes?

**5.** The Lord, accepting these Gaudiyas in the world as His own, instructed them like a father in this way: "O, chant 'Hare Krishna' — just so, by the rule of counting!"[^10] Will the son of Shachi come again before my eyes?

**6.** Beholding before Him the Lord of Nilachala, He bathed His tall, radiant body in streams of tears flowing from abundant prema, always standing at the place behind the loving pillar of Garuda[^11]. Will the son of Shachi come again before my eyes?

**7.** Joyfully biting with His teeth His lip, whose lustre defeats the bandhuka flower, placing His left hand on His hip and raising the other, shining, with countless thrills of love, He longed to dance[^12]. Will the son of Shachi come again before my eyes?

**8.** In a garden on the shore of a lake[^13], tormented by separation from the Moon of Gokula, He made a new river with the streams of tears from His eyes, swooned again and again — and made the whole world as though dead[^14]. Will the son of Shachi come again before my eyes?

**9.** Whoever, with a very pure mind, out of abundant humility, always recites this ashtaka on the son of Shachi, which fulfils one's desire, him Lord Chaitanya, overcome by a flood of great mercy, plunges fully into the vast ocean of prema that bestows the celebrated rasa.

---

[^1]: Base text: Puridasa's edition (1947), where this stotra opens the *Stava-vali* under the title "Sri Chaitanyashtaka"; in the Gaudiya Grantha Mandira e-text and in the tradition it is the *Shachisunv-ashtaka*. Puridasa's refrain reads *nayana-śaraṇīṁ yāsyati punaḥ* ("will He again come onto the path of [my] eyes"), the GGM text *nayana-saraṇīṁ yāsyati padam*; the sense is the same.
[^2]: Cf. Rupa's *Lalita-madhava* 8.34 and CC Adi 4: Krishna, seeing His reflection in a jewelled wall, is captivated by His own beauty and wishes to taste it as Radha tastes it. This is one of the three reasons for Gaura's advent (CC Adi 1.6).
[^3]: *apara-gauraika-tanu-bhāk*, lit. "possessing a body golden with the [lustre] of Another": Krishna appears having covered His dark beauty with Radha's golden lustre (cf. CC Adi 1.5). Puridasa reads the second line *rādhā-priyatara-sakhī-va āptum*, "in order to attain [His sweetness] as Radha, [His] dearest friend [does]".
[^4]: Puri Gosai (*purī-deva*) — Paramananda Puri, disciple of Madhavendra Puri, who lived with the Lord in Puri; the Lord honoured him as a guru. Puridasa's reading: *purī-devasyāntaḥ-praṇaya-madhuni snāna-madhuraḥ*; the GGM text has a different, corrupt reading.
[^5]: Govinda — the Lord's personal servant in Puri (CC Antya 10 etc.).
[^6]: Svarupa Damodara — the Lord's closest companion in Puri. *Prāṇārbuda-kamala-nīrājita-mukhaḥ* — "whose face is worshipped with arati (waved with lamps) by lotuses — the hundred millions of [Svarupa's] lives": Svarupa as it were offers millions of his lives to Him. The line follows Puridasa (in the GGM text the second and third lines repeat one phrase).
[^7]: The kaupina and the ruddy (*aruṇa*, saffron) cloth are the dress of a sannyasi. "The splendour of a golden mountain" (Sumeru) is His golden lustre, which as it were serves Him on all sides.
[^8]: "His own names" (*nija-madhura-nāmāvali*): the Names of Krishna are His own names.
[^9]: Cf. Rupa's *Vidagdha-madhava* 1.2 (*anarpita-carīṁ cirāt…*), quoted in CC Adi 1.4: out of mercy the Lord brought a gift long not given — the highest, radiant rasa of devotion.
[^10]: That is, repeating the Names while counting them (on beads or knots); cf. Rupa's Chaitanyashtaka I.5 (our file 04). The third line follows Puridasa (*iti prāyāṁ śikṣāṁ janaka iva tebhyaḥ paridiśan*); the GGM text has *caraṇa-madhupebhyaḥ*, "to the bees [at His] feet".
[^11]: The Garuda-stambha is the pillar bearing the image of Garuda in the temple of Jagannatha; the Lord usually beheld Jagannatha standing behind it (CC Madhya 11, Antya 14). It is called "loving" because Garuda is the Lord's devoted servant.
[^12]: A description of the posture in which the Lord began to dance in kirtana. *Agaṇita-pulakaḥ* follows Puridasa (*premṇā agaṇita-pulakaḥ*).
[^13]: *saras-tīrārāme* (GGM *sarottīrārāme*; in Puridasa the line is garbled by OCR, the sense being the same) — a garden on a lake shore; in Puri, probably the gardens by the Narendra-sarovara or the Jagannatha-vallabha garden. The author does not name the place.
[^14]: Cf. CC Antya 14–19 — the pastimes of the Lord's divine madness in separation from Krishna, which Raghunatha witnessed.
