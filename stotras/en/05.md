# 5. Rupa Gosvami. The Second *Chaitanyashtaka*

*Stava-mala, stotra 2; 9 verses, shikharini metre[^1]*

**1.** He to whom in the Kali age the wise openly offer sacrifice in rites consisting of loud kirtana — He whose body is Krishna, yet who by the abundance of His lustre is not dark[^2]; He whom they call worthy of worship for all who abide in the fourth ashrama[^3] — may that God in the form of Chaitanya be exceedingly merciful to us[^4].

**2.** Unfolding His beloved deeds, a place of joy even for sinners, with loud cries of "Jaya!" He wholly dispelled Shachi's grief; His hips are adorned with cloth that steals the lustre of the rising sun[^5] — may that God in the form of Chaitanya be exceedingly merciful to us.

**3.** He who, moved by longing, stole the boundless sweet abundance of rasa of certain ones who love Him, in order to relish it, and covered His own lustre, revealing here their lustre[^6] — may that God in the form of Chaitanya be exceedingly merciful to us.

**4.** Those long attached to the demoniac nature cannot worship Him with love; but for those in the three worlds who have taken the divine nature He is the supreme Deity[^7]; resplendent, He is ever victorious, sweet with innate bliss — may that God in the form of Chaitanya be exceedingly merciful to us.

**5.** The refuge of the Paundras[^8], who revealed the glory of Navadvipa, who by His birth adorned the world-honoured family of brahmanas versed in the Veda, He, by accepting on earth the state of a paramahamsa sannyasi, purifies that very ashrama[^9] — may that God in the form of Chaitanya be exceedingly merciful to us.

**6.** He who, having first drunk with His mouth the sweet nectar-rasa of the Name, pours it out through the gates of His eyes in the guise of copious tears — His body thrilling with joy — to reveal on earth the truth of prema[^10] — may that God in the form of Chaitanya be exceedingly merciful to us.

**7.** Revealing a body that shines like fresh gold, with a coconut water-pot, the karanga, adorning His waist, His gait as graceful as a young king of elephants, He instructs His dear ones by His love for the remnants of offerings[^11] — may that God in the form of Chaitanya be exceedingly merciful to us.

**8.** He whose smiling glance dispels the grief of the worlds around, whose very first word makes shoots of blessing sprout, and shelter at whose feet — to whom does it not bring abundance of prema? — may that God in the form of Chaitanya be exceedingly merciful to us.

**9.** Whoever with a joyful heart reads this octave of verses, dense with the fresh fragrance of the bouquets of the fame of Shachi's son, may He — the Lord of Lakshmi[^12] — make him happy at every step, without hindrance, granting him auspicious love for His lotus feet.

---

[^1]: Base text: Kāvyamālā no. 84 (1903), pp. 5–10. According to that edition's commentary, the second ashtaka describes Sri Chaitanya come from Jagannatha-kshetra to Gauda to see His mother (1514; cf. CC Madhya 16). The refrain is *sa devaś caitanyākṛtir atitarāṁ naḥ kṛpayatu*.
[^2]: Cf. Bhag. 11.5.32: *kṛṣṇa-varṇaṁ tviṣākṛṣṇaṁ sāṅgopāṅgāstra-pārṣadam / yajñaiḥ saṅkīrtana-prāyair yajanti hi sumedhasaḥ* — "In the Kali age the intelligent worship with sacrifices consisting chiefly of sankirtana Him who chants [the name] Krishna (or: who is Krishna in essence) and is not dark in lustre, together with His companions…" Rupa paraphrases this verse: He is "Krishna" in essence and golden in colour (cf. CC Adi 3).
[^3]: The fourth ashrama is sannyasa. The commentary: they call Him "the king of renunciants".
[^4]: *Caitanyākṛtiḥ* — "whose form is Chaitanya", and also "whose form is pure consciousness (*caitanya*)", i.e. spiritual.
[^5]: Cloth the colour of the rising sun is the saffron dress of a sannyasi. The commentary also unfolds a second meaning (shlesha): in the Vamana pastime the Lord, amid the gods' cries of "Jaya!", dispelled the grief of Shachi — the wife of Indra, His "dear" elder brother — by tricking and binding Bali. Of Gaura: the loud kirtana with cries of "Jaya!" in Shantipura and the visit to His mother, Shachi Devi, who pined in separation from her son.
[^6]: "Certain ones who love Him" are the gopis of Vraja headed by Radha: Krishna took their bhava and lustre in order to taste the sweetness of their love for Him (CC Adi 1.5–6; 4). The commentary gives a comparison: so too a thief hides his own appearance while stealing. *Kutukī* — "curious, moved by a longing for play".
[^7]: Cf. *Bhagavad-gita* 9.12–13 and 16.6: there are two kinds of beings in the world, the divine (devoted to Vishnu) and the demoniac. The commentary: if some, even among those who lived near the Lord, did not recognize Him as God, this does not diminish Him.
[^8]: Pundra is a region of Bengal (according to the commentary, south of Navadvipa, near Kulina-grama); the Paundras are Bengalis. "Refuge" (*gati*) means both the way and the goal.
[^9]: That is, sannyasa adds nothing to the Lord — on the contrary, by accepting it He sanctified and purified the sannyasa ashrama itself, making it devotional.
[^10]: The commentary: He teaches that prema is born of chanting the Name; "pours out" (*vamati*, lit. "vomits") is used figuratively and does not sound coarse.
[^11]: *Karaṅka* — a water-pot made of coconut shell, an attribute of a wandering sannyasi (the commentary: the Lord is on pilgrimage, *tīrthāya vrajan*). *Nirmālya* — the remnants of offerings to the Deity (garlands, prasada): by His love for them the Lord taught His companions reverence for prasada.
[^12]: "The Lord of Lakshmi" (*lakṣmīvān*, also "the beautiful one").
