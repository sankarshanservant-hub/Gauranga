# 8. Other verses of Sri Chaitanya in the *Padyavali*

*Two verses on Krishna's childhood pastimes, signed "Śrī-bhagavataḥ" in the 1911 edition[^1]*

**1.** *(Padyavali 143)* Awakened at dawn by the sound of churning curd, He entered the house of the cowherd women with soundless steps, quickly blew out the lamps with the breath of His lotus mouth, and filled His mouth with fresh butter — may Bala-Krishna protect me[^2]!

**2.** *(Padyavali 144)* Once Hari, clasping in His left hand His belt of little bells so that they should not tinkle, stooping and walking on tiptoe, softly laughing, with a movement of His eyes restraining the cowherd women who stood before Him with smiling faces, stole the fresh butter that stood behind His mother's back[^3].

---

[^1]: Base text: the *Padyavali*, 1911 edition (Berhampore). Both verses are signed "Śrī-bhagavataḥ" ("[a verse] of Bhagavan"); the commentator Virachandra Gosvami explains that Rupa here cites "two verses of Bhagavan Shachinandana", in which He, filled with avesha (absorption in the pastime), depicts Krishna's childhood pranks, and the Bengali note to the first says: "this verse was composed by Bhagavan". There are no other verses in the *Padyavali* composed by Mahaprabhu or praising Gaura Himself (see `CATALOG.md`); the attribution of these two verses follows the 1911 edition.
[^2]: Bala-Krishna is the child Krishna. The little thief needs darkness so as not to be seen.
[^3]: The commentary: with a movement of His eyes He asks the cowherd women, "Don't tell Mother!" — a display of Krishna's childish cleverness.
