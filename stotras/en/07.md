# 7. Sri Chaitanya Mahaprabhu. *Shikshashtaka*

*"Eight Verses of Instruction" — from Rupa Gosvami's Padyavali; in the order of CC Antya 20[^1]*

**1.** *(Padyavali 22)* Supremely victorious is Sri Krishna-sankirtana! It cleanses the mirror of the heart, extinguishes the great forest-fire of worldly existence, sheds moonlight on the white lotus of the highest good; it is the life of the bride, wisdom[^2]; it swells the ocean of bliss, gives at every step a taste of full nectar, and bathes the whole self.

**2.** *(Padyavali 31)* You have manifested Your Name in many names and invested them with all Your powers, and You have set no fixed times for remembering them. Such is Your mercy, O Bhagavan, and such is my misfortune that no love for them has been born in me[^3].

**3.** *(Padyavali 32)* Humbler than a blade of grass, more tolerant than a tree, seeking no honour for oneself and giving honour to others — so should one always sing of Hari.

**4.** *(Padyavali 95)* Neither wealth, nor followers, nor beautiful women, nor poetic gift do I desire, O Lord of the world. In birth after birth may I have causeless devotion to You, O Lord[^4].

**5.** *(Padyavali 71)* O son of Nanda! Me, Your servant, fallen into the ocean of worldly existence[^5], mercifully consider as a speck of dust at Your lotus feet.

**6.** *(Padyavali 94)* When will my eyes, as I repeat Your Name, be filled with flowing streams of tears, my voice break and falter, and my body be covered with thrills of joy?

**7.** *(Padyavali 328)* In separation from Govinda a moment seems to me an age, my eyes pour tears like the rainy season, and the whole world has become empty[^6].

**8.** *(Padyavali 341)* Let Him embrace me, devoted to His feet, or trample me; let Him break my heart by not appearing; let Him do as He pleases, that rake[^7] — still He alone, and no other, is the lord of my life.

---

[^1]: Base text: the *Padyavali*, ed. Ramanarayana Vidyaratna (Berhampore, 1911): all eight verses are there signed "Śrī-bhagavataḥ" — "[a verse] of Bhagavan", i.e. of Sri Chaitanya, and the edition's Bengali notes to them specify: "this verse was composed by Bhagavan Sri Krishna-Chaitanya Mahaprabhu Himself". In the anthology the verses are scattered across different sections (on the glory of the Name, on humility, on separation, etc.); the *Padyavali* numbers are given with each verse. In CC Antya 20 Krishnadasa Kaviraja quotes them together as the *Shikshashtaka*, which the Lord recited and explained in His last days, relishing each verse with Svarupa and Ramananda Raya. It is the only surviving text composed by Mahaprabhu Himself.
[^2]: "The bride, wisdom" (*vidyā-vadhū*): wisdom (vidya), the knowledge of God, is likened to a bride whose life is the chanting of the Name. Cf. Bhag. 11.5.32 on sankirtana as the sacrifice of the Kali age.
[^3]: "Many names" — Krishna, Govinda, Mukunda and so on: the Lord did not bind their remembrance to any time, place or rules of purity. Cf. CC Antya 20.
[^4]: The reading of the 1911 edition: *mama janmani janmanīśvare bhavatād bhaktir ahaitukī tvayi*.
[^5]: The 1911 edition reads *viṣaye bhavāmbudhau*; its commentary explains *viṣaya* as "abode, place" ("fallen into the ocean of existence") and mentions the reading *viṣame* ("into the terrible ocean of existence"), adopted in the CC.
[^6]: "Has become empty" (*śūnyāyitam*) — has become like a void.
[^7]: The verse is spoken in the mood of Radha (a gopi). "Rake" (*lampaṭa*) is the word of a jealous, wounded beloved; it has no worldly sense: it is the voice of mahabhava, the gopis' highest love for Krishna (CC Antya 20). "Devoted to His feet" is feminine in Sanskrit (*pāda-ratām*). The reading of the 1911 edition: *mat-prāṇa-nāthas tu sa eva nāparaḥ*.
