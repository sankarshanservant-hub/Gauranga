# 2. Raghunatha Dasa Gosvami. *Sri Gauranga-stava-kalpataru*

*"The Wish-fulfilling Tree: a Hymn to Sri Gauranga"; 12 verses, shikharini metre[^1]*

**1.** Seeing His gait, all people spit upon the best of maddened elephants, and seeing His face, upon the lovely moon[^2]; by His lustre He put to shame the golden mountain, and by the waves of His words, the intoxicating wine[^3]. Gauranga, rising in my heart, intoxicates me.

**2.** He adorned Himself as if with nine kinds of jewels — shifting pallor, stupor, faltering speech, trembling, tears, the thrill of horripilation — laughing, sweating, dancing for the full delight of the Lord of the Blue Mountain[^4]. Sri Gauranga, rising before me in my heart, intoxicates me.

**3.** In the rapture of rasa He sprinkles the people all around with slanting jets of water from His eyes, which have become like red water-sprayers[^5]; joyfully biting His sweet lip with trembling teeth, He dances. Sri Gauranga, rising in my heart, intoxicates me.

**4.** Sometimes in Mishra's abode[^6], from great separation from the son of Vraja's king — when His beautiful joints had come apart and His arms and legs had grown extraordinarily long — He rolls on the ground, with piteous entreaty, utterly distraught, weeping in a choked voice. Sri Gauranga, rising in my heart, intoxicates me.

**5.** Without opening three doors — O wonder! — leaping over three high walls, He fell among the cows of Kalinga; from great separation from Krishna His body contracted, and He lay like a tortoise[^7]. Gauranga, rising in my heart, intoxicates me.

**6.** In separation from His own Vraja, dear as millions of lives, He in madness incessantly uttered incoherent words, His mind bewildered; ceaselessly rubbing His moonlike face against the wall, He was covered with blood flowing from the wounds[^8]. Gauranga, rising in my heart, intoxicates me.

**7.** "Where is Krishna, My beloved? Quickly show Him to Me here, friend — you alone can!" — so, as if mad, He spoke to the doorkeeper; and when the man replied, "Come quickly, you will see your Beloved!" He went, holding on to his arm[^9]. Gauranga, rising in my heart, intoxicates me.

**8.** Near Niladri, catching sight of Chataka, the king of hills, He cried: "Ah! I am going from here to Vraja to see Govardhana, the lord of mountains!" — and ran as if intoxicated; and He was held back by His own companions[^10]. Gauranga, rising in my heart, intoxicates me.

**9.** At the festival of the swing-play, beneath the canopy of the beautiful pavilion, together with His Svarupa and His other own companions, Himself singing most sweetly the names of the Slayer of Mura, Gauranga, full of merriment, rising in my heart, intoxicates me[^11].

**10.** He shows mercy to Govinda, as the Lord of Lakshmi to Garuda; devotion to Puri Gosai, as the best of the Yadus to the best of gurus; affection to Svarupa, as Giridhari to the lovely Subala[^12]. Gauranga, rising in my heart, intoxicates me.

**11.** By His mercy He drew me out when I had fallen into the forest-fire of great wealth, and joyfully entrusted me, a wretch, to His own Svarupa; He gave me the gunja necklace from His breast and the Govardhana stone so dear to Him[^13]. Gauranga, rising in my heart, intoxicates me.

**12.** Such is this wish-fulfilling tree: its lovely branches are a series of verses shining with the radiance of flowers — the various true emotions that blossomed in Sri Gauranga. Whoever again and again waters it abundantly with the water of recitation, empowered by the best of healing herbs — great faith — will obtain the fruit: the sweet and exalted sight of Him[^14].

---

[^1]: Base text: Puridasa's edition (1947), stotra 2; checked against the GGM e-text. Verses 4–9 and 11 are the author's own testimony of the Lord's last years in Puri: Raghunatha lived with Him for some sixteen years under the care of Svarupa Damodara (CC Antya 6).
[^2]: "Spit upon" (*thūt-kāra*) — a gesture of utter disdain: beside Gaura's gait the elephant's gait, and beside His face the moon, seem unworthy of comparison.
[^3]: The golden mountain is Sumeru. *Śīdhu* is a sweet intoxicating drink; here it is an image of the intoxicating sweetness of His words, with no worldly sense.
[^4]: The sattvika-bhavas are listed — the involuntary bodily manifestations of ecstatic love (cf. *Bhakti-rasamrita-sindhu* 2.3); together with laughing, sweating and dancing they form as it were "nine jewels" (*nava-ratna*). The Lord of the Blue Mountain (*śiti-giri-pati*) is Jagannatha of Nilachala; the dance before Him is above all at the Ratha-yatra (CC Madhya 13).
[^5]: *Jala-yantra* — a fountain or hand-syringe for spraying water (as at the Holi festival); the Lord's eyes have turned red from weeping, and the tears spurt from them in jets.
[^6]: Mishra's abode is the house of Kashi Mishra in Puri, in whose small room, the Gambhira, the Lord lived. The lengthening of His body from separation is described in CC Antya 14.
[^7]: Cf. CC Antya 17: at night the Lord, without opening the locked doors, came to be outside three enclosures and was found by the Lion Gate among the Telinga (Kalinga) cows, His arms and legs drawn into His body like a tortoise's.
[^8]: Cf. CC Antya 19: in the madness of separation the Lord rubbed His face against the walls of the Gambhira, after which Svarupa had Shankara Pandita sleep at His feet. *Prāṇārbuda-sadṛśa-goṣṭha* — "Vraja, like millions of lives", i.e. as dear as millions of lives.
[^9]: The doorkeeper of Jagannatha's temple. *Dhṛta-tad-bhujāntaḥ* — "holding on to his arm" (lit. "having taken hold of the inside of his arm"). The episode, among the pastimes of divine madness, is also told in the Antya-lila of the CC.
[^10]: Chataka-parvata is a sand-hill by the sea in Puri; the Lord took it for Govardhana (CC Antya 14).
[^11]: The swing festival (Dola-yatra, in spring); "the beautiful pavilion" is the platform with the swing of the festival Deity. "His Svarupa" (*svarūpeṇa svena*) is Svarupa Damodara; a play on words is also possible: "in His own form".
[^12]: Parallels from Krishna's pastimes: Garuda is the servant and carrier of Vishnu (as Govinda is Gaura's servant); "the best of the Yadus" is Krishna honouring His guru Sandipani Muni (as Gaura honours Paramananda Puri); Subala is Krishna's intimate cowherd friend in Vraja (as Svarupa is Gaura's closest friend).
[^13]: CC Antya 6: the Lord gave Raghunatha a stone from Govardhana and a necklace of gunja seeds, which He had received from Shankarananda Sarasvati, saying that this stone is Krishna Himself; Raghunatha understood the gift as a sign that the Lord was entrusting him to the feet of Radharani. The fire of wealth: Raghunatha was the son of the rich landholder Govardhana Majumdar of Saptagrama, who left home for the Lord's sake.
[^14]: Phala-shruti. "Wish-fulfilling tree" (*sura-taru*, *kalpa-taru*) is the title of the stotra. *Sarasa-guru-tal-lokana-phalam* — "the fruit: the sight of Him, full of rasa and majesty" (or: "the sweet, exalted sight").
