# 4. Rupa Gosvami. The First *Chaitanyashtaka*

*Stava-mala, stotra 1; 9 verses, shikharini metre[^1]*

**1.** He who is ever worshipped by Shiva, Brahma and the other gods, who bear love for Him and have assumed human bodies[^2] — the resplendent One, instructing His devotees in the pure manner of His worship — will that Chaitanya come again before my eyes?

**2.** The fortress of the lords of the gods, the highest goal of the Upanishads, the whole wealth of the sages, the sweetness of the hosts of the surrendered, the distilled essence of the love of all the lotus-eyed cowherd maidens[^3] — will that Chaitanya come again before my eyes?

**3.** Bearing Svarupa, peerless in the world, beloved of Advaita, He in whom Shrivasa took refuge, who gave greatness to Paramananda, Hari, the deliverer of the afflicted, hastening to pour His mercy on Gajapati[^4] — will that Chaitanya come again before my eyes?

**4.** Unrestrained in rasa, His body blazing with the sweet splendour of millions of Kamadevas, the crest-jewel of sannyasis, in garments gleaming like the rays of the morning sun, surpassing the splendour of gold with the lustre of His body[^5] — will that Chaitanya come again before my eyes?

**5.** His tongue loudly vibrates "Hare Krishna", His hand shines with a lovely cord bearing rows of knots for counting the Names; His eyes are wide, His arms, long as a pair of door-bolts, are full of play[^6] — will that Chaitanya come again before my eyes?

**6.** On the shore of the ocean, at the sight of rows of flourishing gardens, He is again and again overpowered by love born of the remembrance of Vrindavana; at times His tongue cannot cease repeating "Krishna"; He is the relisher of the rasa of devotion[^7]. Will that Chaitanya come again before my eyes?

**7.** Near the Lord of Nilachala mounted on His chariot, on the road, He is beside Himself with the ecstasy of the dance stirred by the mighty waves of prema, surrounded by Vaishnavas singing joyfully[^8] — will that Chaitanya come again before my eyes?

**8.** Watering the earth with streams of tears, His limbs covered all over with thick horripilation that defeats the fresh filaments of kadamba blossoms, His body drenched with abundant sweat, He is happy in loud kirtana — will that Chaitanya come again before my eyes?

**9.** Whoever, fortunate, with a pure mind shining with trust, reads this most auspicious ashtaka — a path of remembrance of Gauranga — in him may there at once arise and spread in all its fullness a wave of prema for the pair of His pure lotus feet, which are the highest bliss[^9].

---

[^1]: Base text: Kāvyamālā no. 84 (1903), pp. 2–5. According to that edition's commentary, Rupa, living in Vrindavana, remembers the Lord abiding in Purushottama-kshetra and longs to see Him again. The refrain is *sa caitanyaḥ kiṁ me punar api dṛśor yāsyati padam*.
[^2]: According to the commentary, the gods who served Him directly in Krishna's pastimes now serve Him in the forms of Advaita Acharya, Haridasa and other companions. Cf. CC Adi 3.
[^3]: "Distilled essence" (*viniryāsa*) — Gaura is the very essence of the gopis' love for Krishna (cf. CC Adi 4 on Radha and Gaura).
[^4]: The verse is built on shlesha (double meaning). Of Gaura: "bearing [His companion] Svarupa [Damodara]; beloved of Advaita [Acharya]; refuge of Shrivasa [Pandita]; giving greatness to Paramananda [Puri]; hastening to pour mercy on Gajapati (Prataparudra, king of Orissa, CC Madhya 12–14)". Of Krishna-Vishnu: "manifesting a peerless form (*svarūpa*), beloved of non-duality (*advaita* — He is one in many forms), refuge of Shri (Lakshmi), source of the highest bliss (*paramānanda*), Hari, deliverer of the afflicted, hastening to save Gajendra, king of elephants". "Hari" also means a lion: a lion merciful to an elephant is a deliberate paradox.
[^5]: "Garments gleaming like the rays of the morning sun" — the saffron dress of a sannyasi.
[^6]: The knots on the cord are for counting the number of Names repeated (cf. *Shachisunv-ashtaka* 5, file 01). "Long as door-bolts" (*argala*) — arms reaching to the knees (*ājānu-bāhu*), a mark of a great personality.
[^7]: The gardens on the shore at Puri reminded the Lord of the forests of Vrindavana; cf. CC Madhya 14 and Antya 14–19.
[^8]: The Lord's dance before Jagannatha's chariot at the Ratha-yatra (CC Madhya 13). Rupa describes the Ratha-yatra as an eyewitness: he spent some ten months in Puri and saw the Ratha-yatra (CC Antya 1).
[^9]: Phala-shruti. The commentary: "a path of remembrance of Gauranga" — the octave helps one to meditate on Chaitanya.
