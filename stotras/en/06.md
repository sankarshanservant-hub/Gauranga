# 6. Rupa Gosvami. The Third *Chaitanyashtaka*

*Stava-mala, stotra 3; 9 verses, prithvi metre[^1]*

**1.** You, whose lotus feet are worshipped by Rudra and others who love You[^2], having reached the abode of Purushottama, shone forth there in all fullness; You are the wish-fulfilling tree that grants the desires of the whole host of those who bow to You. O son of Shachi, O Lord, O Mukunda, be merciful to me, the lowly[^3]!

**2.** Even men of vast intellect, like the greatest avataras — Sarvabhauma and others — are unable to describe You[^4]; who else could be skilled in it? Therefore — only obeisance to You. O son of Shachi, O Lord, O Mukunda, be merciful to me, the lowly!

**3.** That which even the Upanishads in the Veda in no way set down, which You Yourself did not disclose in Your other great avataras — that jewel of bhakti, O ocean of rasa, You are now scattering here upon the earth[^5]. O son of Shachi, O Lord, O Mukunda, be merciful to me, the lowly!

**4.** O You who amazed the Three-eyed One with the delight of a dance blazing up from love for Yourself[^6]; You who revealed the nectar of anuraga to those who bow; You who are beyond the understanding of the arrogant, stained by self-conceit! O son of Shachi, O Lord, O Mukunda, be merciful to me, the lowly!

**5.** Even men on earth born in low families You deliver out of abundant, lovely compassion — therefore, glad at heart, I take refuge in You. O son of Shachi, O Lord, O Mukunda, be merciful to me, the lowly!

**6.** O You whose attachment to the honeyed juice of the gentle words flowing from Your lotus mouth filled with delight all the bees — those who bow to You[^7]; O ocean of the jewels of the Names, source of every blessing for all people! O son of Shachi, O Lord, O Mukunda, be merciful to me, the lowly!

**7.** O You whose face is sweet as the moon, whose eyes are blossoming lotuses, whose lips are lovely with a cluster of smiles, whose chest is broad, whose arms surpass serpents, whose splendour is that of millions of Kamadevas! O son of Shachi, O Lord, O Mukunda, be merciful to me, the lowly!

**8.** O You golden as the flower of the golden ketaki! I am wicked upon this earth; but You do not see even a particle of fault even in one filled with every vice, and therefore with a humble mind I worship You, O You who love the wretched[^8]. O son of Shachi, O Lord, O Mukunda, be merciful to me, the lowly!

**9.** Those people who, fixing their minds on the imprints of Your feet, recite this octave of verses[^9] — O festival of the whole circle of the earth, O joy of Shachi's heart, O moon of manifest fame, O Lord — to them, O God, grant a blessing full of love for You!

---

[^1]: Base text: Kāvyamālā no. 84 (1903), pp. 10–13. According to that edition's commentary, Rupa praises Sri Chaitanya as he sees Him abiding in Purushottama-kshetra (Puri).
[^2]: According to the commentary, Rudra (Shiva) and the other gods serve Him in the forms of His companions; Rudra is Advaita Acharya (cf. verse 4).
[^3]: Refrain. The text of the 1903 edition prints *kuru mukunda mande kṣaṇam* ("grant… a moment [of attention]"?), but the commentary in the same edition explains: "be merciful (*kṛpāṁ kuru*) to me, the lowly"; we translate accordingly.
[^4]: Sarvabhauma Bhattacharya — the great logician and Vedantist of Puri, converted by the Lord (CC Madhya 6); the commentary: "like Dattatreya, Vyasa and other guru-avataras". Cf. Prabodhananda's *Sri Chaitanya-chandramrita*, which the commentary quotes here: "Where is that boundless mercy, where that wondrous majesty, where that tenderness, O Shauri, such as [are shown] in You as Gaura?"
[^5]: The commentary: the bhakti which the Veda and the Lord Himself in other avataras (Dattatreya, Vyasa) kept hidden under the cover of karma, yoga and monism, Gaura scatters over the earth like grain. Cf. *Vidagdha-madhava* 1.2.
[^6]: The Three-eyed One is Shiva; according to the commentary, Advaita Acharya, the incarnation of Shiva, amazed at the dance of the Lord filled with love for Himself (Krishna). Cf. CC Adi 4: Gaura tastes Radha's love for Krishna.
[^7]: The devotees bowing before Him are like bees intoxicated with the honey of His words.
[^8]: "Golden" (*gaura*). The ketaka (pandanus) is a flower with golden petals.
[^9]: *Padyāṣṭakam* — "an octave of verses"; the sense "an octave on the feet" (*pada-aṣṭaka*) is also possible: the reciters fix their minds on the imprints of His feet.
