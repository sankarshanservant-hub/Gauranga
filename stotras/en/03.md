# 3. Raghunatha Dasa Gosvami. Verses on Gaura in other stotras of the *Stava-vali*

*Six verses from stotras addressed to Radha and Krishna, to Govardhana, to Radha-kunda and to the guru[^1]*

## *Manah-shiksha* ("Instruction to the Mind"), 2

**1.** Do neither dharma nor adharma, of which the hosts of the shrutis speak; here in Vraja serve Radha and Krishna in all fullness. Remember the son of Shachi as the son of the lord of Nandishvara, and the best of gurus as most dear to Mukunda; remember this always, O mind[^2].

## *Sva-niyama-dashaka* ("Ten Verses on My Vows"), 1

**2.** May my highest attachment be to the guru, to the mantra, to the Name, to the feet of the best of Lords, born of Shachi's womb, to Svarupa, to Sri Rupa with his companions and to his elder brother[^3], to the king of hills, to the lake of Gandharva, to Madhupuri, to the forests of Vraja, to Vraja, to the devotee, and to the dwellers of Gokula.

## *Govardhana-vasa-prarthana-dashaka* ("Ten Verses Praying to Live at Govardhana"), 10

**3.** Sri Shachinandana, mercy without condition, Your beloved, has given me over to You, though I am a deceitful rogue; therefore, not regarding whether I am worthy or unworthy, grant me to live near You, O Govardhana[^4]!

## *Vilapa-kusumanjali* ("A Handful of Flowers of Lament"), 5

**4.** He who, an ocean of deep compassion, by nature free, at once with ropes of mercy drew me out of the waterless great well of household life, from which there is no escape and whose toil is endless, and, Himself bringing me to the edge of His feet that put the lotus to shame, gave me over to Sri Damodara[^5] — Him, Chaitanya-chandra, I worship.

## *Prarthanashraya-chaturdashaka* ("Fourteen Verses Taking Shelter of Prayer"), 4

**5.** Not seeing here Radha and Mukunda, whose bodies are fashioned of pastimes full of unrestrained, playful rasa, nor Lalita and Vishakha, nor Gauranga-chandra, nor the two Rupas[^6] — alas, how many pains am I yet to bear? Burst, then, O my forehead[^7]!

## *Abhishta-suchana* ("Declaration of What Is Desired"), 11

**6.** O Chaitanya-chandra, open the lily of my heart and make it delightful with the play of bees — the remembrance of You[^8]; and, dispelling the dense darkness of my offences, O Merciful One, give me, a wretch, the nectar of Your feet to drink.

---

[^1]: Base text: Puridasa's edition (1947), checked against the GGM e-text; the verse numbers agree in both. The verses are chosen for their direct mention of Gaura; see `CATALOG.md`.
[^2]: Gaura-tattva in a word: the son of Shachi is none other than the son of Nanda, lord of Nandishvara (Nandagrama); the guru is Krishna's dearest servant (in the Gaudiya understanding, one of Radha's attendants). "Dharma and adharma" are the path of pious rites and its opposite: both lie within worldly existence.
[^3]: Svarupa is Svarupa Damodara; Sri Rupa is Rupa Gosvami; "his elder brother" is Sanatana Gosvami. The king of hills is Govardhana; the lake of Gandharva (Radha) is Radha-kunda; Madhupuri is Mathura.
[^4]: Cf. *Gauranga-stava-kalpataru* 11 (file 02) and CC Antya 6: by giving Raghunatha the stone from Govardhana, the Lord as it were entrusted him to Govardhana itself. "[Govardhana's] beloved" — because Govardhana is the best of Hari's servants.
[^5]: Sri Damodara is Svarupa Damodara: the Lord placed Raghunatha in his care (CC Antya 6). The well of household life is his father's wealthy house, from which Raghunatha fled to the Lord.
[^6]: "The two Rupas" (*rūpa-yugam*) — probably Svarupa and Rupa (Raghunatha constantly names them together: *Manah-shiksha* 3, *Sva-niyama* 1 — verse 2 of this file); possibly Rupa and Sanatana. The verse was written in Vraja, at Radha-kunda, after the departure of the Lord and of Svarupa, and apparently of Rupa.
[^7]: "Burst, forehead" — a cry of despair: according to popular belief, one's fate is written on the forehead.
[^8]: The kumuda is the night-lily that opens under the moon; Chaitanya-*chandra* ("moon") opens it. The bees are thoughts hovering about Him.
