# Sarvabhauma-shataka

*"A Hundred Verses of Sarvabhauma" (also* Sushloka-shataka*,* Chaitanya-shataka*)*

*A Sanskrit hymn in glory of Sri Chaitanya, traditionally ascribed to Sarvabhauma Bhattacharya. 101 verses*

*Translated from the Sanskrit into English*

---

## Translator's Note

According to the *Chaitanya-bhagavata* (Antya 3), when Sri Chaitanya revealed His six-armed form to Sarvabhauma Bhattacharya, the great logician of Puri, Sarvabhauma glorified the Lord with a hundred verses, and the Lord said they would become known as the *Sarvabhauma-shataka*; the *Chaitanya-charitamrita* (Madhya 6) also speaks of "a hundred verses" and quotes two famous verses of Sarvabhauma — *vairāgya-vidyā-nija-bhakti-yoga…* and *kālān naṣṭaṁ bhakti-yogaṁ…*.

**Source.** The translation follows the Sanskrit text (101 verses — a hundred and a concluding phala-shruti) known to us only from a secondary copy taken from the modern book *Sri Gauranga Mahima* (we have not seen a printed edition); the modern English translation and word-for-word that accompanied the text were not used. The text is transmitted with errors; their corrections by metre and grammar are recorded in the full edition (Sanskrit, transliteration, metres, word-for-word), prepared separately.

**On authenticity.** The text as we have it cannot be confidently identified with the hundred verses spoken of in the CB and CC. The two verses of Sarvabhauma from CC Madhya 6 are not in it. It contains formulas and practices of the late 16th century and later (the service of Radha-Krishna "in every home," Gaura with Gadadhara as Krishna with Radha, a list of the limbs of bhakti coinciding with the *Bhakti-rasamrita-sindhu*), a Gaura with curls and jewels rather than the sannyasi whom Sarvabhauma knew, inserted cycles of verses with common refrains, repeated padas and many errors of language and metre unusual for a renowned scholar. Most probably it is a late composite shataka ascribed to Sarvabhauma on the strength of the CB and CC tradition; it may well include earlier verses. It is nonetheless an old Gaudiya Vaishnava hymn full of love for Gaura, and it is offered here as such.

**How the translation was made.** Each verse is translated as a whole, numbered as in the text; words supplied for sense are in square brackets. Where the author speaks imprecisely from the standpoint of Gaudiya Vaishnava teaching (according to Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati), the translation is kept exact and a note explains.
