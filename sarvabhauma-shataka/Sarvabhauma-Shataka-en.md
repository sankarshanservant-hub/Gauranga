# Sarvabhauma-shataka

*"A Hundred Verses of Sarvabhauma" (also* Sushloka-shataka*,* Chaitanya-shataka*)*

*A Sanskrit hymn in glory of Sri Chaitanya, traditionally ascribed to Sarvabhauma Bhattacharya. 101 verses*

*Translated from the Sanskrit into English*

---

## Translator's Note

According to the *Chaitanya-bhagavata* (Antya 3), when Sri Chaitanya revealed His six-armed form to Sarvabhauma Bhattacharya, the great logician of Puri, Sarvabhauma glorified the Lord with a hundred verses, and the Lord said they would become known as the *Sarvabhauma-shataka*; the *Chaitanya-charitamrita* (Madhya 6) also speaks of "a hundred verses" and quotes two famous verses of Sarvabhauma — *vairāgya-vidyā-nija-bhakti-yoga…* and *kālān naṣṭaṁ bhakti-yogaṁ…*.

**Source.** The translation follows the Sanskrit text (101 verses — a hundred and a concluding phala-shruti) known to us only from a secondary copy taken from the modern book *Sri Gauranga Mahima* (we have not seen a printed edition); the modern English translation and word-for-word that accompanied the text were not used. The text is transmitted with errors; their corrections by metre and grammar are recorded in the full edition (Sanskrit, transliteration, metres, word-for-word), prepared separately.

**On authenticity.** The text as we have it cannot be confidently identified with the hundred verses spoken of in the CB and CC. The two verses of Sarvabhauma from CC Madhya 6 are not in it. It contains formulas and practices of the late 16th century and later (the service of Radha-Krishna "in every home," Gaura with Gadadhara as Krishna with Radha, a list of the limbs of bhakti coinciding with the *Bhakti-rasamrita-sindhu*), a Gaura with curls and jewels rather than the sannyasi whom Sarvabhauma knew, inserted cycles of verses with common refrains, repeated padas and many errors of language and metre unusual for a renowned scholar. Most probably it is a late composite shataka ascribed to Sarvabhauma on the strength of the CB and CC tradition; it may well include earlier verses. It is nonetheless an old Gaudiya Vaishnava hymn full of love for Gaura, and it is offered here as such.

**How the translation was made.** Each verse is translated as a whole, numbered as in the text; words supplied for sense are in square brackets. Where the author speaks imprecisely from the standpoint of Gaudiya Vaishnava teaching (according to Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati), the translation is kept exact and a note explains.

---

## The Shataka

**1.** Bowing down to You, O Lord Gaura, I speak a hundred [verses] at Your feet[^1-1]. For the happiness of sadhus of good intent, show me mercy.[^1-2]

**2.** Having established in every home the service of Sri Radha and Krishna[^1-3], Gaura dances in the glorious sankirtana, overwhelmed by prema.

**3.** On the tongue, the sadhana of Hari's Name; O, from the eyes a hundred streams [of tears]; over the whole body the hairs ceaselessly stand on end and sweat shines: [thus] from Sriman Gaurahari, the giver of bold and sweet bhakti, people ever learn the eternal service that follows the beautiful women of Vraja[^1-4].

**4.** For those fallen through the filth of Kali, covered with grief and delusion, distraught with cares for their kinsfolk, for service to a master[^1-5] and for wealth — having thus considered the means of their deliverance, Gaura appeared (lit. "was born"), the merciful giver of the Name, manifesting His sweet body.

**5.** The fool who is devoid of bhakti towards Sri Sriman Krishna Chaitanya, the sole deliverer of the world, is surely tormented (lit. "cooked") in hell.

**6.** That Krishna who once enjoyed pastimes with Radha in the grove now, joined with Gadadhara[^1-6], dwells on earth as Gaura.

**7.** For those bitten by the serpent of samsara, who have fainted in the age of Kali, the remedy is the Name of Bhagavan and service to the glorious Vaishnavas.

**8.** For fools absorbed in the objects of the senses, the medicine that purifies the mind is service to the guru with trust and eating the remnants [of the food] of Vaishnavas[^1-7].

**9.** I bow down to Sri Chaitanya Mahaprabhu, the glorious ocean of mercy. Show mercy, O Jagannatha[^1-8]! Grant me service to You.

**10.** By Your mercy give, give [me] service to You, O Master, O Mahaprabhu! You are the giver of prema to the fallen — therefore I beg again and again.

**11.** Save me, the fallen one, sunk in the ocean of samsara, O Lord! You are able to uplift the wretched — therefore I have come to You for shelter.

**12.** You are the deliverer of the worlds; You are the sustainer and the giver of riches. Deliver [me], O Master! Give [me] service, O son of Shachi!

**13.** Through the fruit [of worshipping] all the avatars, which the Puranas proclaim, there is no release for me[^1-9] — therefore I have come to You for shelter.

**14.** The Lord, whose songs of wondrous, sweet syllables delight the ear, joyfully moves in the midst of the circle formed by the host of His devotees; Gaurachandra Himself, enchanting [even] the enchanting, the ornament of the three worlds, dances in Nilachala, the supreme abode[^1-10].

**15.** Seeing Purushottama, Hari, whose body is golden, joyfully [rushed] to press to the lotus of His heart Him whose radiance is like a rain-cloud — and fell to the ground, fainting from the surge of all the bhavas; and at times He shows not the slightest movement[^1-11].

**16.** In Gaura's eyes are streams [of tears], in His mouth faltering speech; His whole body is covered with bristling hairs, and in bhava He rolls on the ground.

**17.** One who has unfailing love for the lotus feet of Chaitanya will have bhakti for the Lords of Vrindavana for a hundred births[^1-12].

**18.** As for the lotus feet of Radha there arises bhakti marked by prema, just so for Krishna Chaitanya sweet rati grows.

**19.** I take refuge in Gaurachandra, the king of dancers, desired by the three worlds: His radiance is like that of a golden mirror, His lotus face is lovely, His smile is sweet as a bud[^1-13], His lips are like ripe bimba fruits, His limbs are rounded and graceful, and His neck is like a conch.

**20.** I worship Him who is golden like gold tested on the touchstone: tall, most charming, with a moon-face of sweet radiance, with eyes like full-blown lotuses, with rows of teeth like pearls, with a fine adornment of fresh sprays of lovely flowers on both ears, with gleaming jewelled bracelets[^1-14].

**21.** O friend of all the worlds, O ocean of prema! To this person, full of every deceit and devoid of knowledge, yet surrendered [to You], give, O Lord, service at Your lotus feet — for Your name "Boat of the Fallen"[^1-15] has become manifest [to all].

**22.** Raising both arms, Sri Gaurachandra Himself out of mercy commands all people: "Ho, ho, bhagavatas, say, say 'Hari'!" In prema He dances, roars aloud, cries out in agitation "Ha! Ha!", rolls on the ground, swoons, and presses His hands to His chest.

**23.** At once I bow to the form of Gaura holding the string [for counting] the Name — which bestows the singing of the Names "Hare" and "Krishna", destroys grief, delusion, greed, torment and all obstacles, and gives bhakti to the host of devotees eager for [His] lotus feet[^1-16].

**24.** [Him] whose curling locks are bound with garlands of malati and mallika[^1-17], on whose forehead tilaka shines, whose cheeks are adorned with jewelled earrings,

**25.** whose body is anointed with sandal and aguru, who is adorned with bracelets and armlets, whose feet have tinkling ankle-bells — Gaurachandra I worship.

**26.** Sweet, sweet[^1-18] — whose body shines like gold, whose fine garment, O, is a ruddy cloth; the sole good of the world, the one Supreme above all, inclined to mercy — worship Him, the Supreme!

**27.** Who, setting aside the form of Krishna[^1-19], became Gaura in the age of Kali — Him, supreme bliss, Sri Chaitanya Mahaprabhu, I praise.

**28.** Who, giving up the yellow garment, wears the red cloth[^1-20] — in that Gaura, the ocean of mercy, the shelter of the worlds, I take shelter.

**29.** Krishna has descended again — the eternal Gaurachandra — for the deliverance of those sunk in this sin [that holds] three parts[^1-21].

**30.** When Gaura descended in the age of Kali, chandalas and other low-born people, and all sinners, as many as there are — these for the most part [became] Vaishnavas.

**31.** Seeing one fallen and wretched, the Vaishnavas, purifiers of the world, in the age of Kali take him by both hands and out of mercy beg [him to accept] Hari's Name[^1-22].

**32.** As soon as Gaura began sankirtana, living beings came running to hear of [His] qualities; [even] the impure in heart — what to speak of the pure — on hearing, intoxicated, indeed broke into dance.

**33.** What a wonder, what a wonder! When the son of Shachi appeared (lit. "was born") in the age of Kali, women, children, the dull, fools and others — all [became] devoted to the Name.

**34.** Chandalas, yavanas[^1-23], fools — all perform kirtana of Hari's names and qualities, for Gaura has appeared (lit. "been born") in the age of Kali.

**35.** How wonderful are the deeds of Gaurahari! [The deeds] of His dear servants are more wonderful still: [they] spread in the world the gift of prema — the joy of sankirtana and the ardent love of people[^1-24].

---

**36.** I praise that Gaurachandra — the charming one, whose topknot is bound with rounded garlands of jewels, whose lovely soft forehead is delicately painted with sandal, in whose ears two earrings shine, on whose chest a necklace rests[^1-25].

**37.** One who always remembers, with body, mind and speech, the form, qualities and deeds of Chaitanya and His charming attire — longing for the dust of the lotuses of that person's feet, I serve him, together with [my] kinsmen and sons, for a hundred births[^1-26].

**38.** May this tongue [be engaged] in the kirtana of Your Name, my ears and mind in hearing and remembering, my eyes ever in beholding Your form, and my head in bowing to the feet of Chaitanya[^1-27].

**39.** All the great souls, whose very nature is the rasa of the bliss of sankirtana, pure in heart, indeed equal to Krishna[^1-28], carry the people across [the ocean of] samsara by their gifts of prema.

**40.** A land where there is neither family custom nor righteous conduct — even so, that land is blessed through the sankirtana of Hari's Name.

**41.** For the deliverance of all those who [follow] evil teachings[^1-29], Krishna Chaitanya, the Lord of the worlds, descended in the age of Kali.

**42.** All the avatars, so the sadhus say, are able to save the people who worship them; but Gaurachandra carried both devotees and non-devotees across [samsara] by His gifts of the nectar of Krishna's Name[^1-30].

**43.** Chaitanya, the giver of prema, by His thundering cries of bhava quickly made the people of the whole world such that their hearts were drawn by Govinda and turned away from the evil objects of the senses. But the fool who, when Sri Gaurachandra had thus appeared (lit. "been born") in the world, remained deprived — the afflicted, the sinner, the drunkard, averse to Hari and the guru — is deprived for ever.

**44.** When Gaurachandra, desired by the three worlds, descended, the fallen, the yavanas and the fools — all in this world, seized [with longing] for the sankirtana of the Name — clap [their hands, rejoicing,] in every way[^1-31]; and we too have attained our goal by taking shelter of Krishna's Name.

**45.** Sweetest of the sweet is this conduct of the Vaishnavas: they did not look upon those degraded by the filth of Kali as guilty (lit. "did not approach [them] with the thought of fault"); and to give there [, in the age of Kali,] the Name, the essence of all the Vedas, Sri Gaurachandra descended out of mighty mercy.

**46.** Having carried all people across the impassable ocean of Kali by the Name, Sri Gaurachandra of His own accord entrusted and told to every person the truth about Hari, about the Vaishnavas and about the Name[^1-32].

**47.** All the Vaishnavas in the world, at the Lord's command, for the deliverance [of people] cry aloud [the Name] in every land, in every home[^1-33].

**48.** For the deliverance of the worlds, the service of Hari — the friend of the world, the maker of the world — and His kirtana, both bringing joy, have been established everywhere[^1-34].

**49.** From the time Gauranga, prema embodied, began to give prema in the world — from that very time the sinner, the afflicted, the drunkard, the robber of the wealth of all people, the ungrateful, abandoning all their own dharmas, and the objects of the senses like poison, loudly sing [the Name of] Krishna, intoxicated, beside themselves, immersed in the ocean of prema[^1-35].

**50.** Those for whom in no [former] age of Kali[^1-36], through many births, was there deliverance — in [this] age of Kali they are all immersed in happiness by the grace of singing the Name.

**51.** By the grace of Hari's Names [even] a sinful person may be saved (lit. "cross over"); the teacher [of this] is Krishna Chaitanya Himself, the Lord of the world.

**52.** The friend of the whole world, the giver of the Name, the merciful one, of the colour of gold tested on the touchstone, full of all sweetness, with the sweetest smile, with a tender glance, shining with prema — may Gaurachandra, the king of dancers, shine forth in [my] heart[^1-37]!

**53.** Whose deeds are sweetest, whose sole mantra is Krishna's Name, who is known to the whole world as the giver of all prema — beyond measure; covered with abundant bristling of the hairs, the stealer of people's hearts — may Gaurachandra, the king of dancers, shine forth in the heart!

**54.** The essence of all the Vedas, the fullest of full avatars[^1-38], the destroyer of the filth of Kali, the revealer of prema-bhakti, sporting with playful grace in the company of His dear companions — may Gaurachandra, the king of dancers, shine forth in the heart!

**55.** Incomparably charming in the world, expert in the play of dance, in sweet attire, sending all lands into rapture (lit. "into a swoon"), a deep [ocean] of mighty qualities, by nature pure sattva[^1-39] — may Gaurachandra, the king of dancers, shine forth in the heart!

**56.** Ceaselessly shedding tears, covered with sweat, trembling, His body thrilled with bristling hairs, the abode of all beauty, stirring the hearts of a hundred Kamadevas, glorious — may Gaurachandra, the king of dancers, shine forth in the heart!

**57.** The giver of Krishna's Name — the Name that subdues Yama[^1-40] — the utmost limit of the mercy that saves the most fallen and wretched, whose lovely body shines with the secret of the forests of Vraja[^1-41] — may Gaurachandra, the king of dancers, shine forth in the heart!

**58.** Versed in all the rasas, delighting in Krishna's Name, a deep [ocean] of mighty qualities, steadfast in delivering living beings, of incomparable bodily beauty, illumining [with His radiance even] King Ananga (Kamadeva) — may Gaurachandra, the king of dancers, shine forth in the heart!

**59.** With a spotless lotus face, with lips like ripe bimba fruits, with a fine nose like a sesame flower, with a neck like a conch, tall, with rounded staff-like arms, with a deep navel — may Gaurachandra, the king of dancers, shine forth in the heart!

**60.** Whose radiance is that of gold tested on the touchstone, the embodiment of the essence of beauty, the destroyer of the filth of Kali, whose fame is supreme, the giver of prema-bhakti to all the worlds — may Gaurachandra, the king of dancers, shine forth in the heart[^1-42]!

**61.** His hair adorned with garlands of many kinds of jewels, wondrous, His forehead shining with sandal tilaka, with rows of curls, with locks swinging at both ears, with a necklace on His chest — may Gaurachandra, the king of dancers, shine forth in the heart!

**62.** From the time Hari's Name appeared on earth, from that very time people everywhere have become Vaishnavas — purified, bearing tilaka, pure beads (lit. "garlands") and the Name. Hari, Hari! Just so it came to pass in the age of Kali[^1-43].

**63.** Since Gaurahari's mercy towards living beings is full, the Lord, the sole master of the wretched, out of pity ever offers this prayer with cries of "Ha! Ha!": "O Krishna, ocean of mercy! The people scorched by the great forest fire of worldly existence — save them, save them, O Mahaprabhu! By Your own mercy give [them] bhakti for You in abundance!"[^1-44]

**64.** Seeing [people] dejected in heart and afraid of the sins of Kali, Gaura Himself gave [them] the mantra of Hari's Name and commanded the devotees: "Perform sankirtana with dancing and [playing] instruments!"[^1-45]

**65.** "A murti of Hari with beautiful limbs, sweet, bent in three places[^1-46] — install it in every home": so Gaura would say to the devotees.

**66.** O You whose eyes are like the petals of a red lotus! Whose lips, tender as shoots, are like a lovely bimba! Whose nostrils are full of grace! O Gaurachandra, obeisance to You[^1-47]!

**67.** O You in whom is the beauty of millions of Kandarpas! To You, whose face shines like millions of moons, [obeisance]! O You who shine like millions of golden flowers! O Gaurachandra, obeisance to You!

**68.** O You whose rows of teeth shine like pearls! O mine of auspiciousness [revealed] in the beauty of [Your] smile! O You with a lion's neck and a gleaming throat! O Gaurachandra, obeisance to You!

**69.** O You whose chest shines with a garland of mallika! With pearls hanging from the ears! Adorned with bracelets and armlets! O mighty-armed One[^1-48], obeisance to You!

**70.** O You whose waist is slender like a lion's[^1-49]! Whose knees surpass plantain stems in beauty! Whose feet are like a tortoise's back! O Gaurachandra, obeisance to You!

---

**71.** I take refuge in Your lotus feet, whose toes are like champaka buds. Show mercy, O Lord of kindness! O Gaurachandra, obeisance to You!

**72.** O You whose rows of [toe]nails have outshone with their splendour many ruby mirrors! I beg refuge at [Your] feet. O Gaurachandra, obeisance to You!

**73.** I have taken refuge in the lotus feet marked with the signs of the flag and the thunderbolt[^1-50] — what will Yama do to me? O Gaurachandra, obeisance to You!

**74.** You, O Lord, are the saviour of hundreds upon hundreds of the fallen; how then, for what fault, am I, who have surrendered [to You], deprived? Save me, terrified by the fear of Kali, O friend of the wretched! You are the refuge of those who come for refuge; what [more] shall I say, O Gaurachandra?

**75.** How wonderful are the deeds of Gaurahari! Through [His] teaching of the Name people take refuge in Hari; endowed with bhakti for Hari, they dance, sing, weep and cry aloud for their own [highest] good[^1-51].

**76.** When Gaura, the destroyer of the sins of Kali, had descended, people on earth, overwhelmed by bhava, ceaselessly talked with one another of Krishna and indeed uttered the Name of Hari, which bestows true bhakti.

**77.** In [the ages of] Satya, Treta and Dvapara, for whom, for whom did the fruit arise of sacrifice, meditation, austerity and vows, [performed] according to the prescriptions for auspicious acts[^1-52]?

**78.** [But] in the age of Kali, by the mercy of Sri Gaura, those who merely utter the Name alone have attained nearness to Krishna, devoted to prema-bhakti.

**79.** The garland of the Names "Hare", "Krishna", "Rama", which bestows bhakti and which Chaitanya has brought [to all that exists] — from the atom to the universe[^1-53] —

**80.** those who chant [it] — the Names of Hari — [knowing them] as consciousness and knowledge[^1-54], and who serve the Vaishnavas, go to the abode of Hari.

**81.** Those who hear the lore of the truth of the guru, diligently sing the mantra of Hari's Name, and worship the sadhu and the guru as [their] deity[^1-55] — they, in the age of Kali, [are] the devotees of Chaitanya.

**82.** Krishna Chaitanya-deva revealed the Name of Hari; whoever obtains it is blessed and purifies the world.

**83.** Even if he has love for the Vaishnava and is always greedy for kirtana, [yet] he has turned away from Gauranga-chandra, he is no bhagavata at all[^1-56].

**84.** Though he, firm in dharma, with a mind turned to nothing else, constantly serves the murti of Hari, still, if he has turned away from Gauranga-chandra, he is not blessed and does not know the truth.

**85.** What happiness could that deprived one wish to enjoy, who does not know Gaurachandra, established by all the Vedas? Hari, Hari! How can this be? Where has [his] life gone? He has fallen into Kumbhipaka[^1-57] amid the ocean of worldly existence.

**86.** At the lotus feet of the son of Shachi, the deity of [my] family[^1-58], I seek refuge alone. O Vaishnavas, always show mercy to me, who am greatly distressed — [mercy,] the receptacle of all manner of wishes. May unimpeded perfection be mine, who have bowed [my] mind [before you]!

**87.** Not wealth, not fame, not [noble] family, not austerity, not people, not prosperity, not a son, not happiness — [only] refuge at Your feet, O Gaurahari, give me as a boon in birth after birth[^1-59]!

**88.** Me, beset by many afflictions and ailments and devoid of remembrance [of You], O Lord — from the fear of worldly existence save, save, O Gaurachandra, ocean of mercy!

**89.** Wandering through many births, I have become a human being in the age of Kali; distraught in soul, [I seek] refuge at Your lotus feet — protect me, O Lord!

**90.** Save me — distressed, fallen, pitiable — O Sri Shachisuta! All are immersed in the joy of prema — do not leave me deprived, O Lord!

**91.** Another deity, too, is able to save all the sinful, but in my deliverance [only] the Lord Gaura has power, for [He] is the purifier of the fallen[^1-60].

**92.** At the feet of Sri Gaura I beg, I beg again and again: whether living or dying, may I meditate on Your form.

**93.** O Krishna, You, having assumed a dark [form] in Dvapara and the form of Gauranga in Kali, playfully give prema-bhakti to all people[^1-61].

**94.** [All] that I desire is dedicated to Gaura's lotus feet with body, mind and speech. O merciful One, grant me the fulfilment of all aims: may remembrance of You be in me ceaseless and eternal.

**95.** But the Lord, independent, Himself assuming for His play a human form[^1-62], accomplished the deliverance of the world by the Names of Hari.

**96.** O friend of the helpless, sole ocean of pity! Free me from the bondage of samsara. I wander to the holy places singing Your Names, seeing true sadhus [who show] the form of Lord Hari[^1-63].

**97.** Whatever was said, whatever was done before, whatever was heard, whatever was in the mind — forgive it all, O Gaura; may remembrance of You always be mine.

**98.** Casting off shame, I beg at [Your] feet: give me bhakti marked by prema, O Gaura, ocean of mercy! Without You there is no remover of sorrow.

**99.** To me, sinking in the ocean because of [deeds] done in many births, grant perfection, O Lord Gaurachandra, [so that] I may constantly render radiant service to Your lotus feet and the kirtana of Hari.

**100.** O Gauranga, non-different from the son of Vraja's king[^1-64]! To You I make [my] entreaty: show mercy, O Lord of kindness, [and] I [shall] render every service.

**101.** By those who with love and joy sing the *Chaitanya-shataka*, recite it and hear it, Sri Shachisuta will be attained[^1-65].

[^1-1]: According to CB (Antya 3), Sarvabhauma spoke a hundred verses when the Lord revealed His form to him; cf. CC Madhya 6, which also speaks of Sarvabhauma's hundred verses.

[^1-2]: The text follows a secondary online copy (a blog, transcribed from the book *Sri Gauranga Mahima*, publication details not given), recoded from the Balaram font; we have not seen a printed edition. Obvious misprints and recoding errors have been corrected (see the full version). The attribution to Sarvabhauma is not proven: the text has features suggesting a later origin (see the notes to verses 2, 6, 8, 20).

[^1-3]: Lit. "having established the service of Sri Radha-Krishna in home after home". In CC and CB the Lord, during His advent, spreads above all the sankirtana of the Name (cf. His order to Nityananda and Haridasa to go from house to house calling on all to chant Krishna's name — CB Madhya 13), while He entrusts the establishment of the service of Radha-Krishna in Vrindavana to the Gosvamis (CC Madhya 23 etc.). The author apparently means the service that spread by Gaura's mercy through His followers; cf. verse 65.

[^1-4]: Grammatically, lines 1–2 do not say whose signs these are — Gaurahari's own or those of the people who learn from Him; the latter is more likely. "The service that follows the women of Vraja" is raganuga-bhakti, service to Krishna in the footsteps of the gopis. *Pragalbha* ("bold, mature") is also, in poetics, the name of the mature, self-assured heroine (nayika).

[^1-5]: *Pati-sevā*, "service to a master": serving an employer or a king for one's livelihood; it may also be understood as "service to a husband".

[^1-6]: Gadadhara Pandita, the Lord's childhood friend and His companion in Puri, is according to Kavi-karnapura's *Gaura-ganoddesha-dipika* a manifestation of Radha. Hence the parallel: Krishna with Radha in Vraja — Gaura with Gadadhara on earth. Yet according to CC, Gaura Himself is Krishna who has assumed Radha's bhava and radiance (CC Adi 1.5; Adi 4): Radha is not outside Him but within Him, and Gadadhara is Her manifestation among the Lord's associates.

[^1-7]: "Service to the guru with trust" (*viśrambheṇa guroḥ sevā*) coincides word for word with one of the limbs of sadhana-bhakti in Rupa Gosvami's *Bhakti-rasamrita-sindhu* (the list is quoted in CC Madhya 22). On the potency of the remnants of the devotees' food cf. CC Antya 16.

[^1-8]: *Jagannātha*, "Lord of the world"; the same name is borne by the Deity of Puri, where, according to CC and CB, Sarvabhauma spoke these verses.

[^1-9]: That is, even the fruits that the Puranas promise for worshipping all the avatars cannot atone for the author's sins; only Gaura can save him. The sense of the first half-verse is conjectural; *niṣkṛti* means "atonement, release".

[^1-10]: *Manohara-manoharaḥ*, "enchanting [even] the enchanting"; it may also be understood as "the Enchanter of [even] the Enchanter", i.e. of Krishna. On the Lord's kirtanas in Nilachala, where He danced in the midst of the groups of devotees, see CC Madhya 11 and 13 (the kirtana before the chariot at Ratha-yatra).

[^1-11]: Purushottama is Jagannatha of Puri. Cf. CC Madhya 6: at His first darshana the Lord rushed to embrace Jagannatha and fell unconscious; Sarvabhauma saw Him, carried Him to his house, and the Lord lay motionless for a long time. The last words of the verse (*parama-dhāri saṁspandanam*) are unclear and possibly corrupt; they are translated by the sense.

[^1-12]: *Vṛndāṭavīśayoḥ*, "for the two Lords of Vrinda's forest", i.e. Radha and Krishna. *Śata-janmani*, "in a hundred births", i.e. birth after birth.

[^1-13]: The reading *madhura-mukula-hāsyam* ("whose sweet smile is like a bud") emends the printed *madhura-mukura* ("mirror"): the word is repeated from the first line and makes no sense with "smile". A neck "like a conch" has three folds, a mark of beauty.

[^1-14]: Flowers on the ears and jewelled bracelets (as also in verses 24–25: locks bound with garlands, earrings, armlets, ankle-bells) are the adornment of Nimai in Navadvipa before sannyasa, or the form of Gaura for meditation; in Puri Sarvabhauma saw the Lord as a sannyasi. The author may be describing the vision revealed to him (CC Madhya 6; CB Antya 3).

[^1-15]: *Patita-taraṇa*, lit. "the crossing (boat) for the fallen", i.e. "the Saviour of the fallen" (cf. the usual *patita-pāvana*).

[^1-16]: "The Names 'Hare' and 'Krishna'" are apparently the Hare Krishna maha-mantra. *Nāma-sūtra* ("the string of the Name") is probably a string of beads or a cord with knots for counting the Names. All the epithets in the original are feminine, agreeing with the word *mūrti* ("form"). The metre is faulty in the first line; the correct reading is unknown.

[^1-17]: Verses 24–25 form a single sentence. Malati and mallika are kinds of jasmine. The curling locks belong to the form of Nimai before sannyasa (on accepting sannyasa the Lord had His head shaved); cf. the note to verse 20.

[^1-18]: Cf. Bilvamangala's *Krishna-karnamrita*: *madhuraṁ madhuraṁ vapur asya vibhoḥ…* ("Sweet, sweet is the body of the Lord…"). The ruddy (saffron) cloth is the dress of a sannyasi.

[^1-19]: According to the teaching of CC, Krishna does not cease to be Himself: He covers His dark form with Radha's bhava and golden radiance (CC Adi 1.5; Adi 4). "Setting aside the form of Krishna" is a poetic expression: He conceals His dark beauty.

[^1-20]: The yellow garment (pitambara) is Krishna's dress; the red (saffron) cloth is the dress of a sannyasi. The verb in the printed text (*dharoti*) is not a correct form; it is translated by the sense.

[^1-21]: The sense of *tri-bhāga-pāpe 'smin* ("in this sin of three parts") is unclear. Possibly: in the age of Kali, when only a quarter of dharma remains and sin has taken three parts (cf. *Bhagavata Purana* 1.16–17, the bull of dharma standing on one leg); or the sins of body, speech and mind.

[^1-22]: Cf. CB Madhya 13: the Lord tells Nityananda and Haridasa to go from house to house and ask everyone: "Say 'Krishna', serve Krishna."

[^1-23]: Chandalas are the lowest of outcastes; yavanas are foreigners, here above all Muslims (cf. the conversion of Chand Kazi, CC Adi 17, and Haridasa Thakura, born in a Muslim family).

[^1-24]: The syntax of the second half-verse is not entirely clear: lit. "spreads in the world the gift of the joy of sankirtana, of the love of people [and] of prema". The idea that the glory of the devotees surpasses that of the Lord Himself is common in Vaishnava poetry. The first line is repeated almost word for word in verse 75.

[^1-25]: *Cūḍa* is the topknot. Jewels in the hair, earrings and a necklace belong to the form of Nimai before sannyasa, or to the form for meditation, not to the sannyasi whom Sarvabhauma knew (see the notes to verses 20 and 24; cf. verses 59, 61, 66–70).

[^1-26]: "That person's feet" are the feet of the devotee who remembers Gaura: the author places service to such a Vaishnava above every other goal. "With my kinsmen and sons": the author speaks as a householder; Sarvabhauma was a grihastha (on his family see CC Madhya 15). "For a hundred births": cf. verse 17.

[^1-27]: Cf. *Bhagavata Purana* 9.4.18–20 (King Ambarisha engages all his senses in Krishna's service) and 10.10.38 (the prayer of Nalakuvara and Manigriva: speech in recounting His qualities, ears in hearing His narrations, head in bowing down). The motif is the same; there is no verbal coincidence. The text is emended for the metre (see the full version).

[^1-28]: *Kṛṣṇa-tulya*, "equal to Krishna" in the power to save, i.e. as His representatives. According to Gaudiya Vaishnava teaching the devotee is not equal to Krishna but serves Him; cf. Vishvanatha Chakravarti's *Gurv-ashtaka*: all the shastras call the guru Hari Himself, and so the sadhus regard him, "but he is the Lord's dear [servant]" (*kintu prabhor yaḥ priya eva tasya*).

[^1-29]: *Ku-tantra*, "an evil teaching, a false scripture"; possibly a hint at the tantric cults with wine and meat that were widespread in Bengal at the time (cf. the description of Navadvipa before the Lord's advent in CB Adi 2).

[^1-30]: Cf. CC Adi 7: the Lord distributes prema to all, without considering whether they are fit or not. The verb *tatāra* (lit. "crossed over") is used in the sense of "carried across" (instead of the causative *tārayām āsa*); the form is confirmed by the metre.

[^1-31]: *Sphoṭayantaḥ*, lit. "making [something] crack or burst"; probably in the sense of *āsphoṭayantaḥ*, "clapping [their hands or slapping their arms]" for joy. *Nāma-saṅkīrtanārtāḥ*, lit. "smitten (pining) with the sankirtana of the Name". The last line is one syllable short; the sense is unaffected. "We too": the author counts himself among the delivered.

[^1-32]: The text of this verse seems corrupt: the metre is faulty in the second and third lines and the syntax is unclear; it is translated by the sense (see the full version).

[^1-33]: Cf. the Lord's command in CC Madhya 7: "Whomever you meet, instruct him about Krishna" (*yāre dekha, tāre kaha ‘kṛṣṇa’-upadeśa*), and His prediction in CB Antya 4 that His Name will be preached in all the towns and villages of the earth.

[^1-34]: The syntax is unclear: *sevā-kīrtane sthāpite sukhe* is taken as a dual ("service and kirtana, [both] joyful, have been established"). It may also be understood: "the service of Hari [is] everywhere where kirtana has been joyfully established". Cf. verses 2 and 65.

[^1-35]: Cf. *Bhagavad-gita* 18.66 (*sarva-dharmān parityajya*) and the deliverance of the drunkards Jagai and Madhai (CB Madhya 13). The construction *yad avadhi… tad avadhi* ("from the time that… from that very time") is as in verse 62 and in the well-known verse attributed to Yamunacharya, *yad-avadhi mama cetaḥ kṛṣṇa-padāravinde…*

[^1-36]: Lit. "in which age of Kali". Apparently the point is that Gaura does not appear in every age of Kali but only in the one that follows Krishna's advent (once in a day of Brahma; CC Adi 3). The text may also be corrupt: *kasmin kila yuge*, "in no age".

[^1-37]: Verses 52–61 are ten verses in the malini metre with a common refrain, "may Gaurachandra, the king of dancers, shine forth in the heart" — a separate stava, as it were, within the shataka. *Naṭendra*, "king of dancers": cf. verse 19.

[^1-38]: According to CC (Adi 2), Sri Chaitanya is Krishna Himself, Svayam Bhagavan, the source of all the avatars.

[^1-39]: *Śuddha-sattva*, "pure sattva", transcendental being unmixed with the gunas of maya (cf. *Bhagavata Purana* 4.3.23: *sattvaṁ viśuddhaṁ vasudeva-śabditam*). The line "a deep [ocean] of mighty qualities" is repeated in verse 58.

[^1-40]: *Śamana* is Yama, the lord of hell and judge of sinners; the Name frees from his punishment (cf. the story of Ajamila, *Bhagavata Purana* 6.1–2). The metre is faulty in the first line; the correct reading is unknown.

[^1-41]: "The secret of the forests of Vraja" is probably the intimate pastimes of Radha and Krishna: according to CC (Adi 1.5; Adi 4), in Gaura Radha and Krishna are one, and He manifests Radha's bhava and radiance.

[^1-42]: The text is emended: in the printed *kānteḥ* and *prema-bhaktiḥ* the endings seem to have been interchanged (*kāntiḥ* and *prema-bhakteḥ* are required), and *yasya-kīrti-variṣṭhāḥ* violates metre and grammar; *yasya kīrtir variṣṭhā* ("whose fame is supreme") is adopted.

[^1-43]: Tilaka and pure (probably tulasi) beads on the neck are the outward marks of a Vaishnava. "Hari, Hari!" is an exclamation of wonder. Cf. verses 30–35, 47.

[^1-44]: The text is partly corrupt (*pūrṇodayā* is emended to *pūrṇa-dayā* for the metre; *maivaṁ* at the beginning of the last line is unclear, perhaps *saivaṁ*, "thus He…"). It is understood that Gaura, in the role of a devotee, prays to Krishna for the living beings, and the address "Mahaprabhu" is referred to Krishna. Another understanding is possible: this is a prayer that [a devotee] offers to Gaurahari Himself.

[^1-45]: Cf. CB Madhya 23, where the Lord instructs the people of Navadvipa to chant the maha-mantra and perform kirtana (see also the note to verse 23).

[^1-46]: *Tri-bhaṅga* is the posture of Krishna bent in three places (at the neck, waist and knees), holding the flute. CC and CB record no such command of the Lord; the worship of Deities in the home is a feature of later Gaudiya practice (cf. verse 2 and the note to it).

[^1-47]: Verses 66–73 are eight verses (an ashtaka) with the refrain "O Gaurachandra, obeisance to You!", describing the Lord's form from the face to the feet; like verses 52–61, they are a separate stava, as it were, within the shataka.

[^1-48]: *Mahā-bhuja*, "long-armed, mighty-armed"; cf. *ājānu-lambita-bhujau* ("whose arms reach to the knees") in the first verse of CB (Adi 1.1), on Gaura and Nityananda.

[^1-49]: *Mṛgendra-madhya-kaṅkāla*, lit. "whose frame is [slender] in the middle like a lion's": a slender waist with a broad chest is a mark of beauty. The word *kaṅkāla* ("skeleton, frame") is unusual here; the text may be corrupt. Feet "like a tortoise's back" have a high instep; plantain stems are the usual comparison for the thighs.

[^1-50]: The flag (*dhvaja*) and the thunderbolt (*vajra*) are among the auspicious marks on the soles of Krishna's feet; tradition sees them on Gaura's feet as well. On Yama see the note to verse 57. Verse 73 concludes the ashtaka of verses 66–73 (see the note to verse 66): the description of the Lord's form proceeds from the face to the feet and ends with a prayer for refuge at His feet (71–73).

[^1-51]: The meaning of *raṭanti svārthān* ("cry aloud for their own aims") is unclear, and the metre of this line is faulty; the text may be corrupt. The first line repeats the opening of verse 35 word for word.

[^1-52]: Lit. "for whom, for whom did the fruit arise" — a rhetorical question: for very few. Verses 77–78 contrast the difficult paths of the former yugas with the easy path of the age of Kali, the chanting of the Name; cf. *Bhagavata Purana* 12.3.52: what was attained in Satya-yuga by meditating on Vishnu, in Treta by sacrifices, in Dvapara by serving [the Lord], is attained in Kali by the kirtana of Hari.

[^1-53]: Verses 79–80 form a single sentence. "The garland of the Names 'Hare', 'Krishna', 'Rama'" is the maha-mantra (cf. the note to verse 23). Lit. "gathered by Chaitanya amid the atom and the universe"; the sense seems to be that the Lord gave it to all that exists, from the smallest to the greatest (cf. the note to verse 47).

[^1-54]: *Caitanya-jñāna-rūpataḥ* is ambiguous (a shlesha): "as being by nature consciousness (*caitanya*) and knowledge" or "as the form of Chaitanya". Cf. the verse of the *Padma Purana* quoted in CC Madhya 17: the Name is Krishna Himself, "the embodiment of the rasa of consciousness" (*caitanya-rasa-vigrahaḥ*), since the Name and the Named are non-different.

[^1-55]: *Guru-devatā*, "the guru [honoured as] one's deity"; cf. *Bhagavata Purana* 11.2.37 (*guru-devatātmā*, "seeing in the guru one's deity and one's self"). It may also be read as "the guru and the Deity". On the Gaudiya Vaishnava understanding see the note to verse 39.

[^1-56]: Verses 83–85 denounce those who, while honouring Hari, turn away from Gaura. For a Gaudiya Vaishnava this follows from Gaura being Krishna Himself (CC Adi 1–4): whoever rejects Him rejects Krishna and is deprived of the prema that the Lord, as Gaura, gives to all (cf. CC Adi 8). Similarly severe words about those averse to Gaura are frequent in Prabodhananda Sarasvati's *Chaitanya-chandramrita*. *Bhāgavata*, "a devotee of Bhagavan".

[^1-57]: Kumbhipaka is the hell where sinners are cooked in boiling oil (cf. *Bhagavata Purana* 5.26). "Established by all the Vedas": cf. the scriptural testimonies about Gaura cited in CC Adi 3 (e.g. *Bhagavata Purana* 11.5.32). The metre of the last line is faulty.

[^1-58]: *Kula-daivata*, "the deity of the family", i.e. the Deity a family worships from generation to generation. The expression presupposes an already established household worship of Gaura (cf. verses 2, 65 and the notes to them).

[^1-59]: Cf. Sri Chaitanya's *Shikshashtaka* (verse 4, CC Antya 20): *na dhanaṁ na janaṁ na sundarīṁ kavitāṁ vā jagad-īśa kāmaye / mama janmani janmanīśvare bhavatād bhaktir ahaitukī tvayi* — "I desire neither wealth, nor followers, nor beautiful women, nor [the fame of] a poet, O Lord of the world; may I have causeless bhakti for You, O Lord, in birth after birth." Our verse reworks this prayer in the totaka metre.

[^1-60]: The understanding of the first half-verse is uncertain; it may also be read as "is another deity able to save all the sinful?" Cf. verse 42: all the avatars save those who worship them, while Gaura saves even non-devotees; so here the author considers himself so fallen that only Gaura can save him.

[^1-61]: Cf. *Bhagavata Purana* 10.8.13: Garga Muni says that in former yugas the Lord appeared white, red and yellow, and now has become dark (*kṛṣṇa*); the Gaudiyas refer the yellow, golden form to Gaura (CC Adi 3).

[^1-62]: *Līlā-manuja-vigraha*, "a human form [assumed] for play". According to Gaudiya Vaishnava teaching, the Lord's form only resembles a human one; it is eternal and spiritual; cf. *Bhagavad-gita* 9.11, where Krishna speaks of fools who disregard Him when He comes in a humanlike form.

[^1-63]: The sadhus "show the form of Hari" as His representatives, through whom His mercy acts; according to Gaudiya Vaishnava teaching the sadhu is not the Lord Himself but His dear servant (see the note to verse 39).

[^1-64]: The son of Vraja's king (Vrajendra-nandana) is Krishna. Gaura is non-different from Him: He is Krishna Himself in the bhava and radiance of Radha (CC Adi 1.5). Verse 100 completes the hundred verses spoken of in verse 1; its third line repeats verse 71.

[^1-65]: The phala-shruti ("on the fruit of recitation"), the hundred-and-first verse after the hundred (cf. verse 1 and the note to it). The text calls itself the *Chaitanya-shataka* ("A Hundred [Verses] on Chaitanya"); according to CB (Antya 3), the Lord said that these verses would be known as the *Sarvabhauma-shataka*.
