# Canto One. The Descent in Kali

*Benediction; the Earth before Brahma, Brahma at the Milk Ocean, the Lord's reply; Krishna's desire to taste Radha's love; who will appear as whom; Advaita calls the Lord; birth of Vishvarupa; the Lord in Shachi's womb (pp. 6–10)*

*[p. 6]*

**0.** May Hari, the son of Shachi, illumined by a splendour more beautiful than gold, ever be manifest in the cave of your heart — He who out of mercy has descended in the age of Kali to bestow what had not been given for a long time: the treasure of devotion to Himself, the most sublime and radiant rasa.[^1]

**1.** Not born among cowherds, He constantly gives bliss; by the Name alone He destroys Agha without entering it; without weapons, by His own [associates], He has removed the burden of the earth — may the wonderful Gaura-Krishna be victorious in the world![^2]

**2.** His life, which destroys the impurity of Kali, was once sung in the *Vayu Purana* by the mighty Gautama to his son, as Vishnu declared the truth to the Self-born [Brahma].[^3]

**3.** Taking it as their basis, noble great poets — Vrindavana [Dasa] and others[^4] — made their pure and harmless speech fruitful in divine poems, to delight the devotees.

**4.** Although they have already drunk the nectar of the deeds (*charita-amrita*) of Chaitanya-chandra, still there is room for me — as the chakora still drinks the moonlight, though the moon has been drunk by the gods.[^5]

**5.** And so, bowing my head with love to Sri Rupa, Vakreshvara Pandita and the other devotees — detached, yet full of love — I shall tell of His life as best I can.[^6]

**6.** When the age of Kali had set in, which steals away the conduct of the righteous, men became sinful in soul — masters of evil speech, false doctrines and empty logic, destroyers of the joy of the virtuous,

**7.** greedy for others' wealth and others' wives, lazy in following their own duty, their minds fixed on violence, falsehood and deceit, consenting to walk wrong paths.

**8.** When the Earth could no longer bear the heavy burden produced by the cruel tread of the feet of men whose hearts were void of compassion, she took the form of a cow and went to the world of the Unborn [Brahma].

**9.** Though she is the foremost of burden-bearers, she trembled as she told of the unbearable load, like a cow in terror of a hyena's sharp claws; and the Self-born consoled her,

**10.** saying: "Even when you were oppressed by the Ten-headed one, Kamsa, Keshi and the other demons, you were not like this. Your only friend will again remove your burden, O Earth."[^7]

**11.** Then, taking with him the Earth, who kept sighing in great distress, together with Mahendra and the other chief gods, the Veda-mouthed [Brahma], with Shankara, anxiously went to the Milk Ocean.

*[p. 7]*

**12.** He gazed into the waves of the Milk Ocean, rising in pure streams of white water, and, quickly alighting from his carrier, stepped onto the clean, broad shore.

**13.** And the shore, like a mother, at once refreshed that assembly of worshipful gods who had come from afar, with the soft seat of its lap and with its waters.

**14.** Then the lord of the Unborn's [world], his fatigue relieved, touching water, with a purified mind meditating on the far-distant Adhokshaja, with palms joined above his head, praised Him:

**15.** "Obeisance to You, the Supreme God, O all-pervading, eternal One, whose body is all the worlds; to You whose abode is boundless, whose glory the sages sing, the Great One, Destroyer of the world's evil, the Greatest.

**16.** The Supreme Soul, the highest reality, able to unfold the whole multitude of things, eternal, all-pervading, ever-blissful — can I establish Him by any [philosophical] category?

**17.** By His will even insentient atoms, attaining a likeness of consciousness, create the world, forming dyads and the rest. By what other means, then, shall I approach Him?[^8]

**18.** He dwells in the midst of the heart-lotus of those whose minds are purified by eightfold yoga, granting them perfections and shelter — what conduct [of mine] will satisfy Him?

**19.** Untouched by afflictions, by the fruits of action and their residues, He placed the Vedas in my mouths; entering the worlds and favouring them, He abides [in them] — under whose control can He be who controls the universe?

**20.** He displays before Himself the many-formed Prakriti, full of the gunas, like a dancing girl, while He Himself watches her as a witness — with what visible things can one please Him?

**21.** Priests, spreading out the rite of sacrifice, worship Him with Yajur mantras and oblations of ghee, and attain the heavenly kingdom with its imperishable fruit — who in the world is more worthy of worship than He?

**22.** Even those unable to describe Him say "not this, not this", calling Him the beyond; words, together with the mind, turn back from Him — who can describe Him with various words of praise?

**23.** Some with one, others with two, three, four, five, six, even eight means of knowledge try to prove You — yet they do not know Your nature.[^9]

**24.** Therefore only by those who with joy and undivided heart render causeless devotion are You seen, and to them You submit — as the ocean belongs to those who enter it, not to those who stand far off, O Boundless One."

**25.** As the self-possessed Self-born praised Him thus again and again, the Lord appeared before him and answered what was in his mind — for great souls know the intentions of others:

**26.** "I know, O lotus-born: in the world you created a way of life contrary to duty has arisen, and Vishvambhara [the Earth], exhausted by the weight of their feet, sinks lower and lower in sorrow — so I think.[^10]

**27.** In Kali I have no other incarnation who removes such a burden from the earth except Kalki, and for him the time has not yet come. Hence I am perplexed: how, O Earth, are you to be protected?

*[p. 8]*

**28.** Sinful men, feeble, short-lived, weak in zeal and resolve, will die at the mere sight of the blaze of My weapons; and if all are slain, how will the world exist?

**29.** Go, then, and be attentive; I shall somehow find a means to remove the earth's burden. By My command, take birth on earth in the bodies of My devotees."

**30.** Having said this, the Lord at once vanished into the sky, like a fresh thundering cloud, raining upon the eyes and hearts [of the gods].

**31.** Then Brahma, having pondered again and again all that he had seen and heard with his many eyes and ears, and holding it in great esteem, told it all to the chief gods, who had not seen or heard the Unborn One.

**32.** Drinking the nectar of the Self-born's command, the gods attained supreme bliss: with their minds they worshipped the Lord, with their bodies they returned to their worlds, and with their portions they took birth on earth.

**33.** Then the Lord of the gods, the all-pervading, the all-knowing, having dismissed the gods, lay in the midst of the ocean and pondered, finding no solution as to how to remove the Earth's burden.

**34.** At that very moment there arose in Purushottama, whose only abode is Vrindavana, a certain desire that had remained unfulfilled — a special one among His pastimes:

**35.** "What is My great sweetness like — Mine, who am eternally the concentrate of being, consciousness and bliss, shining with a portion of pure goodness (*vishuddha-sattva*)? Even My own heart is amazed at it.

**36.** Radha — a drop of whose beauty is coveted by countless Lakshmis, She who worships the Enemy of Keshi and the other asuras — on attaining Me at once becomes intoxicated, perspires, and Her limbs fail Her.

**37.** What is My beloved's love for Me like? What is the essence of My beauty? What happiness does She feel in tasting Me? These three things, I think, are hard to know.[^11]

**38.** Therefore I shall surely assume the form of Radhika, come to earth, and especially taste My own sweetness, proper to the pastimes with the gopis."

**39.** Having decided that this could not be achieved otherwise, Mukunda entered the courtyard of Vishnu's mind and, as if speaking with him face to face, addressed him in words worthy of the highest praise:[^12]

**40.** "O Master of all, by Your will this world is conquered; quickly bring it good. Relying on this nectar-streaming form of Yours as on a ladder, I shall descend.

**41.** Nanda is known [there] as Jagannatha, and the mother who bore Me as one named Shachi. Becoming the child of these two, the lords of the island city,[^13] I am born on earth.

**42.** You, before Me, be My elder brother Vishvarupa, the emperor of wandering mendicants; and I, named Sri Krishna Chaitanya, shall become the lord of sannyasis, enduring hardship.[^14]

**43.** By the work of singing and glorifying My Names and by charming dances in the madness of love, preaching the yoga of devotion to Myself, I shall deliver these fallen men.

**44.** Shiva […][^15] has become known as Advaita Acharya; and Bala, coming somewhere in Gauda, is Nityananda, the avadhuta full of immeasurable bliss.

*[p. 9]*

**45.** Brahma has been born under the name Haridasa; Indra as King Prataparudra; Sandipani as Keshava Bharati; and Paurnamasi as Sita, the mother of his [Advaita's] children.[^16]

**46.** Narada, who melts at talks of Me, is Srinivasa, wholly devoted to You; and after him followed Tumburu, under the name Ramananda, knower of the teaching on bhakti.[^17]

**47.** Female companions, friends, elders, other relatives, servants and attendants — marked by their qualities and natures, they will be born on earth as communities of devotees.

**48.** And those who in My various incarnations took shelter of My lotus feet with devoted hearts — some will yet be born, some are already born, and some, know this, are being born now.

**49.** Gathering together by the ocean[^18] along with Me, they — as moneylenders [seek out] debtors — by the manifest power of devotion to Me will make happy the fallen men, [eternal] debtors."

**50.** Having said this and indicated all that was to be done, [Mukunda] became the object of Vishnu's thoughts; and the Lord of the world, having considered it, rejoiced, for He wished to remove the burden of the earth.[^19]

**51.** Then, in the venerable land of teachers, there was born before [the Lord] the first of them, Advaita-deva. With his arguments he demolished the views of heretics, false logicians and Mayavadis.[^20]

**52.** With arguments from the three Vedas he taught the eternity of the three — the devotee, devotion and the Lord — and also the non-material nature[^21] of Purushottama's pastimes, body and abode.

**53.** "Not by works, not by progeny," [says] the shruti, [and so say] many smritis; he upheld the superiority of devotion, rejecting even celebrated liberation.[^22]

**54.** But, pained by the abundance of the heretics' talk and unable to overcome all that evil speech otherwise, he began to worship the Consort of Radha:

**55.** every day he worshipped the shalagrama stone marked with the chakra with tender tulasi buds, and cried out: "O Friend of the fallen, deliver these fallen ones now!"[^23]

**56.** As he cried out, the Lord appeared in his heart and said: "Do not grieve, friend; soon I shall come to the town of Navadvipa."

**57.** Then Sita's husband, knowing the Lord's command given in answer to his prayer, rejoiced, and began to seek out the devotees born in various villages and towns.

**58.** Meanwhile Sri Jagannatha Mishra, having seen eight daughters of his wife — incomparably lovely girls — born and die, grieving over this and desiring a son, worshipped Krishna on the bank of the celestial river [Ganga].[^24]

**59.** Pleased with his service, Vishnu, the destroyer of the hosts of the wicked, resolved and descended in his wife. And Vishvarupa, after his father had given him initiation, wishing to bestow grace on all mankind, became a mendicant monk, living on alms.[^25]

*[p. 10]*

**60.** He did not stay in any chosen holy place of Aryavarta frequented by ascetics under vows — wandering, as befits the noble, he disappeared from sight. Therefore the Mishra again sank into the blinding darkness of great grief, and together with his wife ceaselessly cherished the hope of the rising of the Krishna-moon.

**61.** Pleased with the couple's japa and worship, Mukunda in the month of Magha, on a certain day, entered the womb of Sri Shachi. From then on both, full of joy, became honoured by the whole world, and Lakshmi herself stayed with them constantly, like a maidservant.[^26]

**62.** In the *Sri Gaura-krishnodaya*, composed for the delight of connoisseurs by Govinda — a bee intoxicated by tasting the drops of honey that open within the lotus feet of Sri Gauranga, the bull among the lords of sannyasis — this first canto, named "The Descent in Kali," has come to its close.[^27]

*Thus ends the first canto of the* Sri Gaura-krishnodaya *composed by Sri Govinda.*

[^1]: This verse is not by Govinda-deva: it is Srila Rupa Gosvami's benedictory verse from the *Vidagdha-madhava* (1.2), quoted by Krishnadasa Kaviraja at the opening of the *Chaitanya-charitamrita* (Adi 1.4). In the edition it stands before verse 1 and is also numbered "1"; for consistent numbering we mark it **0**.
[^2]: The verse is built on a contrast with Krishna's pastimes: Krishna was born among cowherds, slew the demon Agha by entering his mouth, and removed the Earth's burden with weapons; Gaura-Krishna, not born among cowherds, destroys sin (*agha*) by the Name alone and removes the Earth's burden without weapons, through His associates. Cf. CC Adi 3: in Kali the Lord comes with His associates and the Holy Name as His weapons.
[^3]: The author refers to a Puranic prophecy about Gaura told by the sage Gautama to his son in the *Vayu Purana*. We have not found it in the commonly known editions of that Purana; such prophecies about Gauranga ascribed to the Puranas circulated in the later tradition. The simile: as Vishnu revealed the truth to Brahma (cf. Bhag. 2.9, the *chatuh-shloki*).
[^4]: *Vṛndāvanādyāḥ*, "Vrindavana and others": most probably Vrindavana Dasa Thakura, author of the *Chaitanya-bhagavata*, and the later biographers; it may also be read "the poets of Vrindavana", i.e. the Gosvamis and Krishnadasa Kaviraja.
[^5]: *Caritāmṛtam* is a pun: "the nectar of the deeds" and the title of Krishnadasa Kaviraja's *Chaitanya-charitamrita*, on which Govinda-deva relies throughout the poem. The chakora is a bird said by poets to feed on moonlight; the moon is regarded as a vessel of nectar drunk by the gods.
[^6]: Vakreshvara Pandita, an associate of Mahaprabhu, to whose family line, according to the editor (Bhaktisiddhanta), the author belonged. *Virakta* ("detached") and *rakta* ("in love", "attached") — a play on words: detached from the world, yet full of love for the Lord.
[^7]: The scene follows the pattern of Bhag. 10.1.17–23 (the Earth as a cow before Brahma prior to Krishna's appearance). The Ten-headed one is Ravana.
[^8]: An allusion to the Vaisheshika doctrine of the world composed of atoms (pairs of atoms, *dvyanuka*): according to the verse, even atoms create only by the Lord's will, endowed by Him with consciousness. Verse 16 likewise plays on the Vaisheshika term *padartha* ("category").
[^9]: The number of valid means of knowledge (*pramana*) differs among the philosophical schools, from one (the Charvakas: perception) to eight (the Pauranikas).
[^10]: *Viśvambharā*, "She who bears the universe", is a name of the Earth; at the same time a hint at Mahaprabhu's childhood name, Vishvambhara.
[^11]: Krishna's three desires are those of Svarupa Damodara's famous verse (CC Adi 1.6; discussed in Adi 4): the greatness of Radha's love, the wonderful sweetness of Krishna that Radha relishes, and the happiness She feels in relishing it. Cf. Adi 4: the external cause of the appearance is the removal of the Earth's burden and the preaching of the yuga-dharma; the internal cause is these three desires.
[^12]: Mukunda (Krishna) addresses Vishnu, who lies in the ocean. This is Govinda-deva's way of showing Vishnu's part in Krishna's descent; according to the CC (Adi 4), when Krishna appears, Vishnu the maintainer is present within Him and the yuga-dharma is preached through Him. In the poem Vishnu is then born as Vishvarupa (vv. 42, 59) — see note 14.
[^13]: *Dvāpa-pura* — probably "the city on the island", i.e. Navadvipa ("nine islands"); the text may be corrupt.
[^14]: A peculiarity of the poem: Vishnu, whom Krishna addresses, becomes Vishvarupa, Gaura's elder brother. Neither the CC nor Karnapura says so: according to the *Gaura-ganoddesha-dipika*, Vishvarupa is Sankarshana (Baladeva), and according to the tradition followed by Bhaktisiddhanta, Vishvarupa after his departure entered into Nityananda.
[^15]: The word *śilāyāsya* in the edition is unclear (probably corrupt); perhaps Advaita's connection with the shalagrama stone (v. 55) is meant. On Advaita as Shiva (Sadashiva), cf. the *Gaura-ganoddesha-dipika*, where Advaita is Maha-Vishnu and also Sadashiva (CC Adi 6: Advaita is Maha-Vishnu, "non-different from Hari").
[^16]: The list of incarnations (vv. 44–46) partly agrees with Karnapura's *Gaura-ganoddesha-dipika* (Haridasa — Brahma; Keshava Bharati — Sandipani; Sita — Yogamaya, i.e. Paurnamasi), partly differs from it: according to the GGD, Prataparudra is King Indradyumna, not Indra. *Sītā-prasūḥ* may also be read differently: "Sita and his [Advaita's] mother".
[^17]: "To You" — to Vishnu, whom Krishna is addressing. Srivasa as Narada agrees with the GGD. Tumburu is the celestial singer, Narada's companion; according to the GGD, however, Ramananda Raya is Arjuna (and the gopi Arjuniya), not Tumburu.
[^18]: *Samayā samudram*, "by the ocean", i.e. at Nilachala (Puri), where the associates will gather around the Lord.
[^19]: That is, Vishnu, the maintainer of the world, accepted Krishna's design: the external purpose (removing the Earth's burden) will be fulfilled together with the internal one.
[^20]: The edition reads *mārikānām*, evidently a misprint for *māyikānām* ("Mayavadis", followers of the doctrine of maya). On Advaita's preaching before the Lord's appearance see CC Adi 3, 13.
[^21]: The edition reads *sa prākṛtatvam* ("materiality"), but the sense requires *aprākṛtatvam*, "non-material, transcendental nature": the teaching that the Lord's pastimes, body and abode are not material is one of the foundations of Gaudiya Vaishnava theology (cf. Bhaktisiddhanta on *aprakrita*). We translate according to the sense.
[^22]: A quotation from the *Mahanarayana Upanishad* (*na karmaṇā na prajayā dhanena tyāgenaike amṛtatvam ānaśuḥ*, "not by works, not by progeny, not by wealth — by renunciation alone some attained immortality"), turned here into an argument for bhakti. The words *na sādhyayaty ādi* are corrupt.
[^23]: Cf. CC Adi 3 (closing verses): Advaita, seeing the world devoid of devotion, worships the shalagrama with Ganga water and tulasi leaves and loudly calls on Krishna. Govinda-deva adds the Lord's reply in Advaita's heart (v. 56).
[^24]: CC Adi 13: eight daughters were born to Jagannatha Mishra and Shachi and died; then Vishvarupa was born.
[^25]: Here (v. 59) Govinda-deva apparently means the birth of Vishvarupa as the descent of Vishnu (cf. v. 42). On Vishvarupa's sannyasa see CC Adi 13, 15.
[^26]: CC Adi 13: in the month of Magha, Shaka 1406, the Lord entered the bodies of Jagannatha and Shachi; their house was filled with abundance, and Mishra and Shachi perceived wonderful signs.
[^27]: The colophon stanza recurs (with variations) at the end of each canto. *Kalāvataraṇa* is ambiguous: "the descent in Kali" and "the descent of the portions" (*kala*, partial incarnations, the associates) — both senses suit the content of the canto.
