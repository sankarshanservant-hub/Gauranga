# Canto Five. Youth

*The journey to East Bengal and Tapana Mishra; Lakshmipriya's departure; the marriage with Vishnupriya; the victory over the digvijayi; Gaya and initiation from Ishvara Puri; kirtan, the universal form shown to Advaita, the abhisheka; Nityananda and the six-armed form; Jagai and Madhai; Gopala Chapala; the subduing of the Kazi; the mango tree; the astrologer; Srivasa's son (pp. 24–27)*

*[p. 24]*

**1.** Then Vishvambhara, surrounded by many disciples, knower of the Shruti, whose body is the ornament of the earth, detached, taking meritorious baths in rivers other than the Ganga too, displayed His pastimes in Vanga, like the Bodiless One [Kama].

**2.** Having conquered the local pandits there with various sciences, He then, again with humility, quickly bestowed His grace upon them and by apt arguments made them His devotees.

**3.** There lived a foremost brahmin named Tapana Mishra, who wished to determine the means [of attaining the goal] according to Kanada's doctrine. Having heard in a dream something of His greatness, he began to question Him — both from a desire to know and from a desire to win.

**4.** He Himself resolved his doubts completely and instructed him: "Best of brahmins, go to Varanasi; live in that city devoted to chanting My names until I come." And he did so.[^1]

**5.** Thus, wandering from town to town, the Best of men delivered men and women from hell; and in Vanga His path — with prints of the wheel, lotus, fish and conch — came to purify the earth like the Ganga.

**6.** Meanwhile Lakshmi, in her first youth, though long protected at home by her elders, bitten by the mighty serpent of heavy separation from her beloved, fell into a painful state and passed away.

**7.** The Omniscient, learning of this at once and of the time, though shaken at heart, kept His composure with steadiness; to soothe His mother's grief He returned home in due course, drawing after Him the hearts and eyes of the women of Vanga.[^2]

**8.** Then He, an Indra among brahmins, dispelling Shachi's delusion of "mine" and "I", revealed the truth to her; having performed for His wife the rites that give unchanging happiness, He, the skilled teacher, went on sporting with His disciples.[^3]

**9.** Shachi, worried that her son was left without a wife, thinking of the continuation of the line, took care of it and married her son to Sanatana Mishra's daughter Vishnupriya, incomparable among mortals.

**10.** Having performed the wedding rites only with an external mind and honoured His relatives with food and clothing, He rejoiced every day in singing the qualities of Mukunda, in sankirtana with hand-clapping, karatalas and the strains of the mridanga.[^4]

**11.** One moonlit night, while the Lord was teaching His disciples on the bank of the celestial river, a certain scholar came to Him — a digvijayi, "conqueror of the quarters."

**12.** Though He received him with honour, he, full of envy, the thundering-voiced poet, spoke before the Lord: "You are famed as the pandit 'Nimai' — 'without guile'; I have come here to learn about your guilelessness.[^5]

**13.** What have you studied, what remains to be studied, what do you teach these students? Having learned today of the power of your learning, gentle one, we have come to know your noble, universally beneficial nature."

**14.** Hearing his words, He, glad at heart, replied in speech full of humility and affection: "What can we say? Before you, who blaze like the scorching summer sun, we are fireflies.

**15.** We wish to hear your new poetry, which, like a beloved, holds friendship, rasa and bhava within; the poetry that praises the Jahnavi — a channel of a nectar pool, priceless, beyond words."

*[p. 25]*

**16.** Then the king of poets, hearing His friendly words — a thunderbolt among disputants, as if by the proud roar of conceit — at once praised the Bhagirathi, who dwells in the matted locks of the Lord of spirits [Shiva], in a hundred verses.

**17.** Then the Lord, choosing one verse, said: "Your verses praising the Tripathaga are victorious, astonishing the three worlds; explain to us, noble one, this one verse."

**18.** Saying this, Achyuta recited before him that verse — "mahattvam…". And he, the steadfast one, seeing that the Lord of the universe remembered at a single hearing, said with fear and amazement:

**19.** "The meaning of this verse is clear from the construction of its words — why examine its merits and faults? You know neither ornaments, nor merits, nor faults; and if you do know, child, why do you ask?"

**20.** Having said this, the best of poets fell silent. The Moon-faced One, seeing that he could not bear contradiction, spoke with a gentle turn of phrase that nevertheless shattered worldly [pride], to free the proud man of his pride:

**21.** "In this verse there are many hidden faults, and five obvious, gross ones — the chief of them *avimrishta-vidheyamsha*." Having thus examined everything, the Golden One explained it to him according to Bharata and other authorities.[^6]

**22.** The brahmin, appreciating His skill in exposition, was at once at a loss before the Lord; bereft of arguments, his great pride crushed, he addressed the Lord with a glimmer of joy in his words:

**23.** "Seeing how you, even with a child's understanding, teach all the shastras, we have faith [in you]: my poetry, swift as a gust of storm, has been overcome by one who held it in memory."

**24.** Having thus honoured Him and considering himself defeated by a child, he, robbed of his wealth of meanings, went home, fasted, and at midnight, propitiating the goddess, told her about it.

**25.** The consort of Hari [Sarasvati], laughing, said to him: "Why do you grieve at being humbled by the Lord of all? Hundreds of learned Sarasvatis serve Him; I am His maidservant; what then of you!"

**26.** Enlightened thus by the auspicious [goddess], the aged brahmin at the end of the night went to the Lord's abode and, his heart pacified, with joined palms, crying "Glory!" and "Save me!", took shelter of His feet.

**27.** Graciously instructing him in the truth about Himself, He saved him by uprooting his evil tendencies; and likewise, as a father his sons, He made all people happy by instructing them in true knowledge.[^7]

**28.** Then He, Himself the teacher of the three worlds, seeing [the need for] initiation, went to Gaya, a land of sacrifice holy as Prayaga, and there, taking shelter of Ishvara, disciple of Sri Madhavendra Puri, entered the sampradaya.

**29.** Having pleased him with service and respectful words and received his consent, the best of sages, wearing the tilaka that destroys Kali, beads of tulasi and the other marks of a Vaishnava, returned.[^8]

**30.** From then on the Lord, in His own town, with Vakreshvara and other rasikas, performed the service of devotion to Himself — sankirtana, remembrance, glorification, obeisances and the rest — expert in bestowing prema, with many [other] of its limbs.

**31.** Then Advaita-deva, an ocean of mercy, a teacher of great intellect, came, eager to see Him; and Vishvambhara showed him His universal form, as [Krishna] showed it to Pritha's son in battle.[^9]

**32.** His majesty… Srivasa and other honoured great [devotees] performed abhisheka over Gaura as the teacher of the world; and by the mercy of Chaitanya-chandra they themselves became teachers.[^10]

*[p. 26]*

**33.** Once the moon of avadhutas, Nityananda-svarupa, came, wishing to see his brother. At the moment of their meeting He [Gaura] became six-armed, raising the conch, disc, the bow Sharnga, flute, lotus and [club].

**34.** Then the Lord shone four-armed, holding in His hands flute, conch and disc; and finally, [the desire] fulfilled, He, with fragrant body, appeared dark, threefold-bent, holding a flute.

**35.** Seeing Him shining in His former form, the avadhuta himself became Balabhadra — fair, with plough and club — delighting his followers and the devotees.[^11]

**36.** There lived two brahmins, ruined by their own deeds, named Jagai and Madhai; the ocean of mercy, having mercifully delivered these wretches, raised the banner of "Purifier of the fallen."[^12]

**37.** Other devotees of the town gathered too, with the signs of prema — hair on end, faltering voice, rapt appearance; in Srivasa's house, the doors locked at night, they spent the days in sankirtana with Him.

**38.** A certain brahmin named Gopala Chapala, hearing [this kirtan] many times from outside and unable to bear it, at night unseen threw at the door things used in left-hand [Shakta] worship — wine and the like.

**39.** In the morning the great souls, seeing this filth, laughingly had it thrown away; and for this offence the slanderer, his body swarming with worms, suffered from leprosy, abandoned even by the Lord.[^13]

**40.** From then on the townspeople daily performed sankirtana secretly in their homes with their families. Then the heretics, unable to bear it, ignorant of scripture, quickly informed their Yavana ruler.

**41.** And he, angered, sent armed men: "Forbid this!" — and these men of low character, by oaths and spies, strictly forbade [kirtan] throughout the town, in every house.

**42.** Hearing this, the Lord, enraged, frightened that sinful Mleccha with the form of Nrisimha and, organising a great festive procession led by Advaita and the righteous, set out [to him].

**43.** In front went Advaita, behind the avadhuta, and in the middle Haridasa danced with arms upraised; and He Himself, clearly seen everywhere, beheld the house of the ruler of the Yavanas.

**44.** He, in terror, at once came out before Him and with his whole heart took shelter of that Lord; and He, loving even to a Mleccha among the righteous, at once casting aside his scripture, instructed him in true scripture.[^14]

**45.** Thus the Lord, performing many different deeds full of majesty, which He had wished for in various incarnations, by His mercy purified all the fallen — Mlecchas, cowherds, Shakas, Pukkasas, Kaulas and Shaktas.

**46.** Once, wearied by sankirtana, He entered the courtyard and planted a mango stone; that very moment a sprout appeared — and there was a tree with trunk, branches and foliage.

**47.** At once large fruits the size of palm fruits appeared on it, sweet and filling; a marvel: from then on, after every kirtan the host of His devotees was satisfied [with them].[^15]

**48.** Then a certain astrologer came to the courtyard; the Lord asked him: "Who was I before?" He, after reflection, said: "You are the Lord of all"; but Gaurahari laughed: "I was a cowherd boy."[^16]

**49.** Once after kirtan the son of Srinivasa, who had lived only a few years, died; the Lord, seeing him, quickly raised him, and by His power he delivered in the assembly a discourse on the truth of Brahman.[^17]

*[p. 27]*

**50.** Thus, like the moon on the full-moon day, He gave joy to creation with a rain of the nectar of prema and in His mercy saw no fault in anyone. Therefore even the townspeople of stony nature, intoxicated with the nectar of prema, singing the names of Sri Krishna, cast off their fear of Yama and of the Yavanas.

**51.** In the *Sri Gaura-krishnodaya*, composed for the delight of connoisseurs by Govinda — a bee intoxicated by tasting the drops of honey that open within the lotus feet of Sri Gauranga, the bull among the lords of sannyasis — this fifth canto, on youth, radiant by its very nature, is drawing to its close.

*Thus ends the fifth canto of the* Sri Gaura-krishnodaya *composed by Sri Govinda.*

[^1]: The journey to East Bengal and the instruction to Tapana Mishra — CC Adi 16: the Lord teaches him that the goal is prema and the means in Kali is chanting the holy name, and tells him to go to Varanasi, where they will later meet (Madhya 17). The mention of Kanada (Vaisheshika) is Govinda-deva's.
[^2]: CC Adi 16: in the Lord's absence Lakshmipriya, suffering in separation, left the world, "bitten by the snake of separation"; the Lord returned and consoled His mother. The image of the snake is the same as in the CC.
[^3]: *Mukhaja-duścyavana*, "the Indra (Dushchyavana) of those born from [Brahma's] mouth", i.e. king of brahmins; a pun: Shachi is the name of Indra's consort. CC Adi 16: the Lord consoles His mother with reflections on the inevitability of fate.
[^4]: The marriage with Vishnupriya — CC Adi 16. "With an external mind" — i.e. without worldly attachment.
[^5]: *Nirmāyi* plays on the name "Nimai": "without maya, guileless." The victory over the digvijayi (Keshava Kashmiri) — CC Adi 16.
[^6]: CC Adi 16 quotes this verse of the digvijayi ("mahattvaṁ gaṅgāyāḥ…") and the Lord's analysis: five faults (among them *avimrishta-vidheyamsha*) and five ornaments. Bharata is the author of the *Natya-shastra*, the founder of poetics.
[^7]: CC Adi 16: at night Sarasvati revealed to the digvijayi that the Lord is her Master; in the morning he took shelter of the Lord and became a devotee. "Consort of Hari": in the Vaishnava interpretation Sarasvati is the Lord's energy (consort). Verse 23 is partly unclear in the edition; the translation is approximate.
[^8]: Gaya and initiation from Ishvara Puri — CC Adi 17 (briefly), in detail CB, Adi-khanda. "Entered the sampradaya" — the line of Madhavendra Puri, which by Gaudiya Vaishnava tradition is in the succession of Madhva (Bhaktisiddhanta).
[^9]: CC Adi 17: the Lord showed Advaita the universal form (vishvarupa), as Krishna did to Arjuna.
[^10]: The beginning of the verse is unclear. The Lord's abhisheka (the mahaprakasha in Srivasa's house) — CC Adi 17; CB Madhya 9.
[^11]: CC Adi 17: the Lord showed Nityananda the six-armed form (*shad-bhuja*); in detail, CB Madhya. Govinda-deva adds that Nityananda in response revealed the form of Balarama.
[^12]: The deliverance of Jagai and Madhai — CC Adi 17; CB Madhya 13.
[^13]: CC Adi 17: Gopala Chapala left at Srivasa's door the articles for worshipping Bhavani, with wine; for this offence against a Vaishnava he was struck by leprosy, and the Lord refused to forgive him until he begged forgiveness of Srivasa.
[^14]: The Kazi and the ban on sankirtana — CC Adi 17: the Kazi broke the mridangas; the Lord came to his house with a huge kirtan procession; the Kazi admitted that Nrisimha had terrified him in a dream, and stopped the persecution. Govinda-deva's order: the Lord frightens the Kazi with Nrisimha, then goes in procession.
[^15]: CC Adi 17: the Lord planted a mango stone in Srivasa's courtyard, and at once a tree grew with fruits without stone or skin; the devotees ate them after kirtan for a whole year.
[^16]: CC Adi 17: the omniscient (*sarvajna*) astrologer, seeing the Lord, beheld Him as Krishna; the Lord told him that He had been a cowherd.
[^17]: CC Adi 17: during kirtan at Srivasa's house a son died; the Lord bade the dead boy speak, and he told of the eternity of the soul and transmigration. Govinda-deva's "discourse on Brahman" (*brahma-vada*) means a discourse on the spiritual nature of the soul, not the Mayavadi doctrine.
