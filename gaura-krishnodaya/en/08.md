# Canto Eight. The Gift of Prema to the People of Utkala

*Bhuvaneshvara; the broken staff; ecstasy before Jagannatha and Sarvabhauma; Gopinatha Acharya proves the Lord's divinity; seven days of Vedanta and the critique of Shankara's commentary; the universal form and Sarvabhauma's conversion; the Dola-yatra and festivals; prasada for Sarvabhauma; departure for the south from Alalanatha (pp. 37–40)*

*[p. 37]*

**1.** Having heard the story of the Beloved of the cowherd women, whose wealth is faithfulness to His word, and having understood all about the guilt and innocence [of the two brahmins], the Omniscient One, gazing again and again at the shining Lord, went on with love, keeping Him in His heart.

**2.** Having seen in the Ekamra forest the linga Bhuvanesha and the place of Kapoteshvara on the [river] Bhargavi, the Lord gave His staff to His brother and, as soon as He glimpsed the temple [in the distance], went on ahead in a surge of love.

**3.** Then the avadhuta, together with the devotees, broke the staff and, purified by serving Him, ran after Him. Ahead, at Atharanala, they said sorrowfully: "Master, do not go alone in such a rush!

**4.** Seeing You, Lord, with Your heart melting with love, who can help You? Let us all go together, having bowed to blue-throated Shambhu, to the face of the dark Moon."

**5.** Hearing His brother speak, the Lord asked: "And where is the staff — My helper, who bears My fatigue?" To this the lord of the earth [Nityananda] answered Him with love and fear, hiding a smile in the corners of his lips:

**6.** "When You, overcome with prema, absorbed in dancing, fell to the ground, O Destroyer of sin, I caught You — and from the weight I fell beside You, and the staff broke at that moment, today, in the first watch."[^1]

**7.** Angered over the staff, as if furious, He ran on ahead, leaving the devotees — a forest fire for the dry trees of sin; and as soon as He lovingly reached Sri Jagannatha, He fell unconscious, flooded by the waters of prema.

**8.** Seeing Him senseless on the ground, Sarvabhauma Bhattacharya, who happened to be near, said: "Who is this handsome mendicant monk who has come and fallen, shaken to the depths of his soul?"

**9.** All said: "No one knows who he is or where he comes from; on seeing the Lord he fell to the ground. He seems an enviable monk; therefore you should take him home so that he may recover."

**10.** At their word the Bhattacharya had his students carry Him on their shoulders to his house and sat beside Him; seeing that His breath was perceptible only by the trembling of a wisp of cotton, he took His body in his hands for a moment and gently rubbed it.

**11.** "How could this blazing sattvika emotion appear in this human body? Or is this God Himself, assuming a human guise to reveal devotion to Himself?"

**12.** While the Bhattacharya was thinking thus, the Lord's servants, having seen the Supreme Lord in the temple and fulfilled [their desire], and having heard from the people the news of the Lord, came there anxiously together with Nityananda.

**13.** Seeing Sri Chaitanya, the best of monks, unconscious, sheltered in the Bhattacharya's house, they, led by Nityananda, lovingly sang a song cooling to the ear, reviving.

**14.** With a roar, taking the sweetness of that song into His heart, dancing loudly, rising together with them, out of the abundance of love He endlessly made the sprouts of joy grow in the eyes of those who watched.

**15.** Seeing this, the Bhattacharya rose in delight together with Gauranga and the townspeople in the assembly and, bowing low to His lotus feet, [offered] to give alms to Him and to the devotees, weakened with hunger.[^2]

*[p. 38]*

**16.** Accepting his words, the Lord also accepted the alms — various dishes lovingly given by the god on earth [the brahmin] — and stayed with him, like a fire that has hidden its brilliance under ash.

**17.** Gopinatha, called Acharya, His countryman, also came there and bowed his head to Gaura's feet; and the Teacher of all blessed the one He knew: "May your mind, smiling with joy, rest in Krishna."[^3]

**18.** Disregarding this blessing, the Acharya [Sarvabhauma] at a suitable time said to the Lord: "Lord, if you hear from me the meaning of Vedanta, you will attain, great one, the knowledge that destroys difference."

**19.** Having said this, toward evening he showed Him the Lord [Jagannatha] with His companions, and at the end of the night again gave Him abundant alms; so he spent the night, and in the morning relieved Him of His fatigue.

**20.** He asked Gopinatha: "Who is he, what is his name, from whom did he receive initiation, this friend of yours?" To this question his sister's husband answered his brother-in-law the Bhattacharya: "He is the Lord of the world!

**21.** He was born in Navadvipa to the best of brahmins, Mishra, and received initiation from the noble one named Keshava Bharati. He cares nothing for 'senior' and 'junior' — Sri Chaitanya delivers by [His] consciousness alone.

**22.** [People say:] 'In the age of Kali there is no incarnation of Krishna' — let that opinion be, O follower of convention. He is Bhagavan Himself, established by the scriptures, possessed of the fullness of majesty, untouched by worldly enjoyments.

**23.** Of His sannyasa and golden radiance Vyasa spoke in the *Brahma[-purana]*, at the beginning of the *Bharata* and in his own words; in the *Vayu Purana* he clearly revealed His nature. Therefore, best of brahmins, do not be proud before Him."[^4]

**24.** Having heard Gopinatha's words, the best of the twice-born smiled; having bathed and served Gaurahari, he, in a secluded house, surrounded by righteous men, humbly began to expound the Vedanta to Him.

**25.** For seven days He listened to the sutras of the Shruti and, considering [the commentary] useless, did not even part His lips. This troubled the best of brahmins, and he, smiling slightly, asked: "Have you understood this or not, Sir?"

**26.** Hearing these words of the Acharya, the best of sannyasis said: "This is mastery of exposition, O well-wisher. The meaning of the sutra shines in it like the sun, but your commentary appears as a bank of clouds covering it.

**27.** What is directly expressed by the words of the Shruti — the meaning of the pranava — how does it become figurative in your hands, O [knower of] the highest meaning? Leaving aside the Tara [Om], you take the Shruti statement 'tat tvam asi' — a partial statement; this is not right.[^5]

**28.** The reality whose body is concentrated light of being and consciousness is Brahman, and the Purusha is His conscious form. Non-different from Him and eternal are [the energies] of conscious and unconscious kind — like the ocean and its waves; and this [world] is a well on the shore.

**29.** Do you accept that the jiva is a part of the Lord, or not? If it is a part of the Whole — where is it, tell me, and who is it? But if you say that reality is one and indivisible because there are no parts — then who, separately, is the one whose body is the universe?

**30.** Therefore non-difference is clear only in the statement about manifestation: if the jiva is not what is illumined [by Him], what do you take it to be? If Brahman is non-dual, then, with the [grammatical] non-difference of the genitive, who will be the Antaryami, the supreme being?

**31.** The Brahman said to be above all is Bhagavan; and what is lower He bears as His body. To assert the equality of these two, O gentle one, is your transgression, and is it not a grave one? Speak, good man!

*[p. 39]*

**32.** In the Vedanta it is not said that Brahman is without attributes: on the contrary, since none is equal to Him, He shines as possessed of attributes. God, having created the universe by this [energy], Himself unchanging, plays in it; He undergoes neither transformation nor illusory change.[^6]

**33.** 'From which, in which, by which' — know, brahmin, that these three causes [are named] in the scripture that speaks of His distinctive attributes. Why then do you call Bhagavan, who has a form of being, consciousness and bliss, formless, although He is the source?[^7]

**34.** In His portion of bliss is [the energy] named hladini; in the portion of consciousness, the one named sandhini; and in the portion of being, the one named samvit. In the three portions of Him who is Brahman, these three energies of the Lord, according to the Shruti, are eternal.[^8]

**35.** And when the Shruti says 'without hands, without feet' and so on — that is the denial in the Lord of a material [body], not [His] essence; in the formless there could be no breathing and the like. Vyasa established this in all the Puranas.

**36.** Acharya Sri Shankara, concealing this highest truth, taught otherwise in this age; this is not his fault — for at the Lord's command he, being most disciplined, [taught] this doctrine of maya."[^9]

**37.** Thus, whatever arguments he put forward, relying on all the commentaries on the sutras — in each of them his power was crushed by Gaura's words, and the god of the earth [the brahmin], though a great speaker, fell into despondency.

**38.** To him — worthy of association, reproaching himself, bowing down, intelligent — the Lord out of compassion, to save him, revealed to the astonished man the fullness of His majesty and His two feet, raining the nectar of love.

**39.** Seeing Him four-armed and then two-armed, the Bhattacharya in amazement realised the Supreme Truth and, bowing down, with a purified heart, praised the One famed in holy verse with a hundred shlokas.[^10]

**40.** When in him — his throat choked with tears and longing, his hair standing on end — there played the restless sidelong glance of Vishnu, by that contact he, formerly engaged in harsh logic, at once became a deliverer from the cycle of rebirth for himself and for others.

**41.** When the Bhattacharya began to practise bhakti, the devotees on earth readily came to worship the moon of Gaura; he became the flagstaff in the procession that crushes the hordes of heretics — skilled in the Shruti together with [the chanting of] the Name.

**42.** Thus, shedding streams of prema, He lived in his house until the end of spring; and there the Lord saw the swing pastime of the Lord of Nilachala together with Haladhara.

**43.** At the end of the month of Phalguna, preparing to celebrate the festival of coloured powder (phalgu), to play in moon-bright festive rites, standing on a chariot like one with a hundred banners, before which dancing girls sang sweetly —

**44.** Sri Govinda [the festival Deity], like the moon, dispelling darkness from afar, delighting the chakora-devotees, filling the earth with fragrance, relieving the heat, bearing disc and conch —

**45.** along the road lit by many lights, in the holy place of Vishnu — as long as the God moved, the ocean of prema of the best of Gauras kept rising.

**46.** Every day dancing with His servants in boundless joy, drawn toward the God as if by the reins of a loving thirst, like a ship on the ocean driven by a favourable wind, the son of Shachi spent five nights thus.

**47.** When on the previous night offerings had been made into the fire, auspicious for the welfare of the world, on the full moon in the morning on a high long platform the faultless swing pastime of Vishnu took place,

*[p. 40]*

**48.** [where there were] rows of pillars, waterpots, wheels and many raised banners, and above the middle a swaying canopy, waving chamaras and shining ornaments.

**49.** While the God, in the clear shady pavilion, in such play, which raises the joy of the three worlds, enjoyed divine offerings, the Lord [Gaura] with the townspeople served Him.

**50.** When clouds of coloured powder veiled the place, people gazed unblinking like the gods at Him — the Supreme Lord absorbed in dancing — and themselves became just as eager.

**51.** When the Lord fell senseless out of joy at [the sight of] the remnants of the worship of the Smiling One, there was a crush — like a host of gods intoxicated with joy — of people with bodies wet with sweat, full of praise.

**52.** Some there, somehow joining their palms above their heads and gazing at that joy, loudly proclaimed "Glory!" with the name of Achyuta, and so made fruitful the years of their long lives.

**53.** Having served the God thus, the righteous at the end of the night carried Him with the greatest reverence to His abode and, installing Him there, absorbed in bliss, went to their homes, having seen the Lord of all beings.

**54.** Then, in the month of Chaitra, seeing the festival of Rama's appearance on Rama-navami, the worship of damanaka and other celebrations, clearly radiant with fragrance, Chaitanya joyfully served the God above the gods.[^11]

**55.** One morning in the month of Madhava, having seen the Lord of Sri and taken the remnants of His offering, He lovingly ran to the learned Bhattacharya, wishing to know his heart.

**56.** When he ate that [prasada] without even washing, He considered the whole land of Utkala blessed; and then Gaura wished to deliver quickly Karnata, Dravida and other lands [still] uninstructed.[^12]

**57.** When the day at his house drew to a close, the Generous One, having taken alms and seeing the Acharya standing above all on earth in bhakti, honoured the other blessed ones and set out — on the mission of the touch of prema.

**58.** After the departing [Lord] ran the townspeople with melting hearts, crying: "Master, where are You going, leaving us, Your own?" — like the people [of Ayodhya] after Rama.

**59.** The Lord, though indifferent to this, accompanied by His own, came in the evening [to Alalanatha] and, seeing the Lord named Alalanatha, held sankirtana there with them in the assembly, with dancing.

**60.** Having spent the night there thus, the Lord humbly sent them back, saddened, to Purushottama and, at the avadhuta's word, free from desires, set out for the south together with Krishnadasa.[^13]

**61.** And they, having let Him go, who had departed on the southern path like the sun — self-controlled, by agreement — returned, powerless in the days of His [northward] course, as if parched by the hot breath [of separation].

**62.** In the *Sri Gaura-krishnodaya*, composed for the delight of connoisseurs by Govinda — a bee intoxicated by tasting the drops of honey that open within the lotus feet of Sri Gauranga, the bull among the lords of sannyasis — the eighth canto, on the gift of prema to the people of Utkala, has come to its close.

*Thus ends the eighth canto of the* Sri Gaura-krishnodaya *composed by Sri Govinda.*

[^1]: CC Madhya 5: on the way the Lord gave His staff to Nityananda, who broke it into three pieces and threw it into the river Bhargi (Bhargavi); the Lord grew angry and went on to Puri alone. Nityananda's explanation ("You fell, I caught You, and the staff broke") is as in the CC.
[^2]: CC Madhya 6: the Lord, seeing Jagannatha, lost consciousness; Sarvabhauma had Him carried to his house; His companions came and revived Him with loud kirtan. The Bhattacharya fed them with prasada.
[^3]: CC Madhya 6: Gopinatha Acharya, the Lord's countryman from Navadvipa and Sarvabhauma's brother-in-law, recognised the Lord. The blessing "may your mind be fixed on Krishna" the Lord gave to Sarvabhauma, who considered it a Vaishnava's blessing [inferior to what befits a sannyasi]. The phrasing in the edition is unclear; following the CC we refer the blessing to Sarvabhauma.
[^4]: Gopinatha's debate with Sarvabhauma and his students on the Lord's divinity — CC Madhya 6. "At the beginning of the *Bharata*" — probably the *Vishnu-sahasranama* of the *Mahabharata* (with the names "golden in colour … sannyasi"), quoted in the CC (Adi 3.49–50). The references to the *Brahma Purana* and *Vayu Purana* follow tradition; cf. 1.2.
[^5]: CC Madhya 6: the Lord says that Shankara interprets the sutras not directly (*mukhya-vritti*) but indirectly (*lakshana*); the principal statement (*maha-vakya*) of the Vedas is the pranava (Om), while "tat tvam asi" is a partial (*pradeshika*) statement. Govinda-deva follows the CC.
[^6]: A refutation of the doctrine of *vivarta* (illusory transformation) and of the *parinama* of Brahman itself; according to the CC (Madhya 6), the world is a transformation (parinama) of the Lord's energy, while the Lord Himself is unchanging. Verses 28–31 are partly obscure in the edition; the translation conveys the course of the argument.
[^7]: A reference to the *Taittiriya Upanishad* 3.1 ("from which all beings are born, by which they live, into which they enter") and to *Vedanta-sutra* 1.1.2 ("from whom is the birth and so on"): Brahman, as the cause of the world, possesses attributes.
[^8]: Govinda-deva's order differs from the CC (Adi 4.62, Madhya 6): there sandhini is the energy of being (*sat*), samvit the energy of consciousness (*chit*), hladini the energy of bliss (*ananda*). Probably parts of the verse are transposed (or the text is corrupt); we translate as printed.
[^9]: CC Madhya 6 and Adi 7: Shankara at the Lord's command concealed the truth and preached Mayavada; no blame attaches to him (Padma Purana: "In the age of Kali, in the form of a brahmin, I shall proclaim this false doctrine"). Bhaktisiddhanta consistently repeats this judgement: Shankara is an incarnation of Shiva carrying out his commission.
[^10]: CC Madhya 6: the Lord showed Sarvabhauma first a four-armed form, then the form of Krishna with the flute; Sarvabhauma composed a hundred shlokas in His honour.
[^11]: The festivals of Puri (Dola-yatra, Rama-navami, the worship of damanaka) are mentioned only briefly in the CC. Govinda-deva, an Oriya, describes the rites of Puri in detail; this is one of his independent features. According to the CC (Madhya 7), the Lord left for the south soon after Sarvabhauma's conversion, in the month of Vaishakha.
[^12]: CC Madhya 6: the Lord brought Sarvabhauma Jagannatha's prasada early in the morning, and he ate it without washing — thus transcending the Vedic rules; the Lord embraced him and said that now He was at peace.
[^13]: CC Madhya 7: the Lord went south with the brahmin Krishnadasa, given Him as a servant on Nityananda's advice; at Alalanatha He danced in kirtan and sent the devotees back.
