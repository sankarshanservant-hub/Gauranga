# Canto Two. The Power of the Lord

*Shachi's pregnancy and the thirteenth month; the Lord's appearance on the Phalguna full moon during the eclipse; the Infant refuses the breast until Advaita initiates Shachi into the mantra; Sita and the goddesses visit the Infant; the naming; the first rice; the footprints and the thirty-two marks of a great personality (pp. 11–14)*

*[p. 11]*

**1.** Then Shachi, the beloved of the best of Mishras, equal to the celestial Shachi in purity of nature, showed the signs of pregnancy and took on the beauty of the autumn sky when the moon is hidden by clouds.

**2.** As she grew thinner day and night, the pallor of her body increased; as her longing for unheard-of foods gradually grew, so did her belly.

**3.** Again and again she was sick, yet her spirit did not flag; even at night she took no pleasure in union with her husband, her love of amusement waned, and even in bed, after jesting, she could not sleep.

**4.** Darkness quickly seized the tips of her full breasts — she whose eyes were those of a timid doe — as Rahu of old seized the jar of nectar divided in two in the quarrel of gods and asuras.[^1]

**5.** After some days, she saw at night that her womb shone like the sky, and slowly told her husband of this wonder, beyond the minds of those who dwell on earth.

**6.** Hearing her words and seeing her form, which could astonish the heart, Mishra said to his virtuous wife: "Some great soul will be born."

**7.** Some more months passed, and again the month of Magha came — the time [for the birth]; yet the child did not come forth, and both of them grew anxious.

**8.** Then Nilambara Chakravarti, having considered everything, came himself and said to his well-mannered daughter: "Do not grieve, my daughter: in the thirteenth month a most beautiful son will be born to you."[^2]

**9.** And now spring arose in the world, nourishing tender shoots on the rows of trees; the quarters shone with the breezes from the Malaya hills, laden with the pollen of lotuses;

**10.** the forests grew lovely with clusters of blossoms, groves of ketaki opened, and all was filled with the song of male cuckoos intoxicated by the juice of young mango shoots;

**11.** the humming of bees drunk with nectar troubled the hearts of wives whose husbands were away — [spring became] the first teacher of the science of lament; all around was filled with the fragrance of flowers.

**12.** The tithi of the Phalguna full moon arrived; the moon, lord of the Phalguni asterism, shone with blazing rays; Leo [was rising], and the year [of the] Shaka [era] reached one thousand four hundred and seven.[^3]

**13.** At that hour, when the dwellers of the three worlds rejoiced, the Lord was born, bringing glory to His mother. Seeing that spotless moon in the courtyard, darkness swallowed the spotted moon.[^4]

**14.** The darkness of the eclipse spread everywhere outside and the darkness of ignorance within people — Gaurachandra, risen with His own radiance, dispelled them, as the sun dispels the mist.

**15.** As He lay in the courtyard and, under the pretext of sweet crying, uttered His own name — the essence of all learning — the Shrutis came to Him like women, as they came to Hiranyagarbha lying on the lotus from Hari's navel.

*[p. 12]*

**16.** Then the best of Mishras joyfully, seeing the boy with His umbilical cord, performed [the rites over] Him with the brahmins — Him who was like a blazing image of Kama cast of molten gold.

**17.** The courtyard of Mishra's house was filled with a rain of celestial flowers together with curd and kunkuma; the quarters resounded with the sound of celestial trumpets, mingled with his [earthly] music, song and dance.

**18.** Then the women, now His own kin, joyfully brought [the Infant] out, having cut the cord in the birth chamber; and people gave gifts in many ways, bathing in the waters of the Ganga on the pretext of the eclipse.[^5]

**19.** Then the Lord, displaying on earth His power and [the importance] of the supreme mantra of the name, would not drink at His mother's breast; and the women, at a loss, did not know what to say.[^6]

**20.** Then the lord Advaita, learning of the birth of Mahaprabhu, told Haridasa of it and, eager to see Him, the noble one set out himself with his wife, taking fine gifts.

**21.** And Sita — leaving behind her husband with the porters carrying the baggage — was brought by very swift palanquin-bearers to the house of the best of Mishras the next day, ahead [of him].[^7]

**22.** Seeing the women sitting dejected beside the Infant, the Acharya's wife, full of compassion, with a bewildered look, wise, said not a word.

**23.** The brahmin women bowed to her who had come to the house of the wife of the king of the twice-born and told her what fate had done; and, distressed like female chakravakas,[^8] they spent all that night without sleep.

**24.** On the third day he, knower of the hidden [Advaita], entered the house of Shachi's astonished husband, whose mind was fixed on his radiant son who had not drunk the nectar of his mother's breast.

**25.** He bowed and honoured the noble Acharya who had come to his house, and in conversation, exchanging wishes of well-being, he somehow told him of his Infant's behaviour.

**26.** Having heard Mishra's words, [Advaita] drank in with his eyes the nectar of the sweetness of his son's body, and then, with firm reverence, withdrawing into an inner room, secretly disclosed to the Lord [his] confidential [request]:

**27.** "Lord, You have descended in Kali to fulfil [the needs] of the sinful. If You do not drink the juice of Your mother's breast, how will You remain in this world among the living?

**28.** So let Shachi's austerity ripen quickly: drink her milk at once! It was You Yourself who earlier began to act as a son to obtain this — how then are You now averse to it?"

**29.** The Lord, heeding and honouring his request, full of the taste of his friendship, said to the Acharya: "O best of the twice-born, do you not know how sinful it is to take impure food?

**30.** This body — an abode of excrement and urine, like a corpse — always grows impure; without initiation from Me, without initiation into My mantra, it does not become pure by any ablutions.

**31.** Therefore, with this great mantra of initiation into Me, purify My mother; then I will drink milk." Saying this, the Lord gave the great mantra to the best of the twice-born.[^9]

*[p. 13]*

**32.** And the best of Acharyas handed to Shachi at once both the mantra and the son — both supremely great; and, receiving both at once, the goddess shone like Aditi in heaven.

**33.** Then the people, their cherished wish fulfilled, their eyes flooded with tears of joy, danced to the kirtan of the holy name of Hari, delighting even the Yavanas.[^10]

**34.** The women of the town, impelled by love, losing their ornaments in their haste, forgetting children, husbands and household tasks, calling one another — high and low alike —

**35.** agitated by the wish to see Mishra's extraordinary son, came to his house and brought some gifts — valuables and things to eat.

**36.** Arundhati, Haimavati, Sarasvati, Shachi and other chief goddesses, assuming the form of brahmins' wives, also entered his house, unsuspected by anyone.[^11]

**37.** Seeing Him — the one beauty among the sons of the world, a pleasure-house for the herds of does that were their glances — they blessed Him many times and with difficulty returned to their abodes.

**38.** When seven days had passed, [Shachi] bathed in warm water, performed the rite of ablution and adorned herself; and her relatives festively performed for mother and son the rite of coming out [of the birth chamber].

**39.** On the next day Nilambara Chakravarti too, master of astrology, came, eager to see his grandson, and told his son-in-law the wonderful fruit [of the positions] of the planets and stars at the Infant's birth.

**40.** When [the period of] impurity was over, Shachi's husband, now purified, invited all the brahmins, sadhus and Vaishnavas and with great enthusiasm and festivity performed his son's name-giving ceremony.

**41.** Seating on his lap the Infant, adorned with a priceless necklace, armlets, gold coins, tinkling bells, a belt of little bells, beautiful bracelets and the rest, Mishra came out to the assembly

**42.** and said to all assembled: "This son out of love will sustain the whole universe — so let Him bear the name Vishvambhara; and since in Him [all] consciousness (*chit*) is gathered, He is called Chaitanya.

**43.** He is also Gaura, for He shines like gold, and likewise Krishna, for He attracts every heart. Since both these [names] refer to Him alone and are in accord in Him, let Him be known as Gaura-Krishna."[^12]

**44.** Having blessed the Infant, having dined and received honours, they took leave of Mishra with fine words full of affection and went to their homes.

**45.** And the people, hearing those names, used them — names of great fruit — as weapons to destroy sins, and again and again did battle with the enemies: lust and the rest.

**46.** Then the best of Acharyas with his wife, gladly accepting the worship offered by Mishra's wife, knowing what he had to do, went home, constantly remembering the Infant, the ornament of the three worlds.

**47.** Then He, possessed of undiminishing radiance, displaying a series of His powers along with charming play, nectar to the eyes of the world, grew in body day by day like the young moon.

**48.** One day Mishra, surrounded by the best of brahmins who had performed their daily fire rites, with great festivity performed the rite of first taking outdoors the boy, who shone like Kumara.

*[p. 14]*

**49.** When He had begun to crawl slowly about on His knees and hands, on an auspicious day He delighted His parents by tasting soft rice with milk.

**50.** The son babbled half-words, laughed for joy, showing His shining little teeth, and toddled here and there, stumbling — and the couple rejoiced in Him.

**51.** The best of Mishras, free of envy, planning an annual festival on his son's birthday, invited brahmins learned in mantras and agamas and honoured them.

**52.** Then at dawn Shachi, awaking, saw in the inner rooms the beautiful footprints of her son, whose little feet were wet with turmeric, and [wondered]: "What are these unknown [prints]?"

**53.** She told Mishra, and he, deciding that they were the footprints of the Deity [of Krishna], the Enemy of Kamsa, rejoiced with an extraordinary feeling [in his heart] and sprinkled them with tears of joy.[^13]

**54.** Then Shachi eagerly took her son, who had woken, gave Him the nectar of her breast, washed Him, and, rubbing His foot, noticed marks on it.

**55.** Again she said to her husband: "What is this, ah! The [same] prints shine on our son's foot!" Hearing this, he gladly and quickly examined His tender foot, red as a japa flower —

**56.** marked with signs: the wheel-line, flag, thunderbolt, jambu fruit, goad, lotus, svastika, barleycorn, leaf, pennant, octagon, chariot, waterpot, cow's hoofprint, triangle, fish, conch, banyan, moon and bow —

**57.** a lovely, safe cage giving festival to the birds of the eyes; from frequent embraces, kisses and the like it became wet with tears of joy.[^14]

**58.** Then the maternal grandfather came and, his heart melted, said to the assembly: "I knew the greatness of this Infant from looking at His horoscope — what more is there to say?"

**59.** Seeing in the Infant's limbs fineness in five, length and redness in seven, prominence in six, and depth, shortness and breadth in three, [he said]:[^15]

**60.** "Therefore this Hari who has been born is a great personality (*mahapurusha*); there is no other deliverer for you from the sufferings of the world. Serve His lotus feet with an undoubting heart — He is your treasure here."

**61.** These words of Nilambara Chakravarti settled in their ears, and the peaceful best of Vedic scholars, confirming them by the views of the Shrutis, took shelter of Him. And Mishra, having celebrated the Lord's birthday as his means allowed, considered himself and his wife blessed to be the parents of such a son.

**62.** In the *Sri Gaura-krishnodaya*, composed for the delight of connoisseurs by Govinda — a bee intoxicated by tasting the drops of honey that open within the lotus feet of Sri Gauranga, the bull among the lords of sannyasis — the second canto, named "The Power of the Lord," has come to its close.

*Thus ends the second canto of the* Sri Gaura-krishnodaya *composed by Sri Govinda.*

[^1]: Darkening of the nipples is a sign of pregnancy. The comparison with Rahu seizing the nectar at the churning of the ocean: the nipples are like darkness seizing the two halves of the jar of nectar.
[^2]: CC Adi 13: the Lord stayed in Shachi's womb for thirteen months; Nilambara Chakravarti, Shachi's father, calculated by the stars and reassured the family.
[^3]: The year is written in word-numerals (*bhuta-sankhya*): *abdhi* "ocean" = 7 (the seven oceans), *jagat-śata* = 1400 — i.e. Shaka 1407 (February 1486 CE). CC Adi 13: the Lord appeared in Shaka 1407, on the Phalguna full moon, in the evening, in simha-lagna (the sign of Leo).
[^4]: Birth during a lunar eclipse — CC Adi 13: Rahu swallowed the moon, and during the eclipse all the people loudly chanted "Hari! Hari!" The poet plays on this: seeing the new, flawless Moon (Gaura), darkness out of shame swallowed the old, spotted moon.
[^5]: CC Adi 13: during the eclipse people bathed in the Ganga and gave alms, chanting the names of Hari.
[^6]: The beginning of a story absent from the CC: the Infant refuses the breast until Shachi receives the mantra from Advaita (vv. 19–32). This is the "legend of Shachi's initiation by Advaita" that scholars name as Govinda-deva's chief independent addition. A similar story is found in the *Advaita-prakasha* (ch. 10), where Advaita initiates Mishra and Shachi.
[^7]: CC Adi 13 tells of Sita Thakurani coming with gifts to the newborn (without Advaita); in Govinda-deva Sita comes first and Advaita follows on the third day.
[^8]: Female chakravakas, according to poetic convention, spend the night separated from their mates, pining until dawn.
[^9]: Govinda-deva puts into the Infant's mouth a teaching on the necessity of initiation (*diksha*): without it the body and food are not purified. Here the idea takes the form of a pastime: according to Gaudiya Vaishnava teaching, Shachi is an eternal associate of the Lord (Yashoda in Vraja; see 1.41) and there is no impurity in her; the Lord arranges this to instruct the world. Bhaktisiddhanta constantly stressed the importance of diksha, citing the *Hari-bhakti-vilasa* (2.12): as bell-metal becomes gold by alchemy, so a person becomes twice-born by initiation.
[^10]: *Yavanān apītavān* is apparently corrupt; we read *yavanān api*, "even the Yavanas" (Muslims). Cf. CC Adi 13: at the Lord's birth even Muslims, seeing the Hindus, jokingly repeated the name of Hari.
[^11]: CC Adi 13: the goddesses — wives of the celestials — came in the guise of brahmins' wives and, recognised by no one, saw the Infant. Haimavati is Parvati; Shachi here is Indra's consort.
[^12]: An explanation of the names and of the title of the poem. The naming Vishvambhara is in CC Adi 14 (according to the CC, the name Krishna Chaitanya was given at sannyasa; cf. 1.42). "Gaura" and "Krishna" as designations of one and the same subject (grammatical *samanadhikaranya*) give the "Gaura-Krishna" of the poem's title.
[^13]: CC Adi 14: in the house were found the prints of small feet with the marks of flag, thunderbolt, conch, wheel and fish; Jagannatha and Shachi concluded that Bala-Gopala (their Deity) was walking about the house, and later saw the same marks on their son's feet.
[^14]: The list of marks on the Lord's foot (v. 56) is partly unclear in the print and OCR; we translate according to the usual list of marks on Krishna's feet. The foot is compared to a "cage" for the "birds of the eyes": a glance once caught in it can no longer fly away.
[^15]: The thirty-two signs of a mahapurusha — CC Adi 14.15 quotes the same verse ("five long, five fine, seven red, six prominent, three short, three broad, three deep"). Govinda-deva's count differs somewhat (the print is probably corrupt here).
