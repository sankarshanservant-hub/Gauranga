# Yadunatha Dasa's *Shakha-nirnayamrita*

*Homage to the branches of Gadadhara Pandita: Dhruvananda, Shridhara Brahmachari, Ananta Acharya, Nayanananda Mishra, Gangamantri, Mamu Thakura, Kanthabharana, Achyutananda, Bhugarbha Gosvami, Bhagavata Dasa and others, Jayananda, Madhava Acharya, Madhu Pandita; then Chandrashekhara, Vakreshvara, Damodara Pandita, Yadunatha Chakravarti, Bhavananda, Mangala Vaishnava and others (verses 1–59)*

**1.** I bow to Dhruvananda, who ever delights in the pastimes of *ujjvala-rasa*,[^1] and to whom Sri Gadadhara in his mercy gave his own mood.

**2.** I bow to Sri Shridhara, the wondrous *brahmachari*, called Sudama,[^2] made wholly of the nectar of *prema*, who delights in the pastimes of Gaura.

**3.** I bow to the venerable Haridasa Brahmachari, the great soul, a mass of supreme bliss, a treasury of joy for the devotees.[^3]

**4.** I bow to the one named Ananta Acharya, in whom is a boundless and wondrous *rasa*, who is made of manifold boundless wonders, and who is truly a vessel of love for Gaura.[^4]

**5.** He whose inborn nature is endowed with the wondrous beauty of *mahabhava*,[^5] in whose heart are Radha and Krishna — to that Kavidatta I bow.

**6.** I bow to Sri Nayanananda Mishra, an ocean of the nectar of *prema*, the one vessel of the jewel of the *prema* of Gadadhara and of Gaura.

**7.** I praise Gangamantri, who delights in the happiness of service, and whom Svardhuni (the Ganga) well instructed to make manifest the Holy Name and *prema*.[^6]

**8.** He whom Gaurachandra, together with His associates, lovingly called "Mamu" in Utkala[^7] — to that Mamu Thakura I bow.

**9.** Ananta, born in the Chatta family, bears the honorary title Sri Kanthabharana. To him who is endowed with a multitude of pastimes, whose very self is the *rasa* of Radha and Krishna, who has descended as the necklace about Their neck — to Sri Kanthabharana I bow.[^8]

**10.** I bow to the one named Achyutananda, full of the bliss of the nectar of the great *rasa*, the dearest of Gadadhara, the son of Srimad Advaita.

**11.** I bow also to Bhugarbha Gosvami, renowned as "risen from the womb of the earth,"[^9] ever the great soul, the master who bestows love for Krishna.

**12.** I bow to Bhugarbha's companion, Sri Bhagavata Dasa, whose mind is ever adorned with singing of the pastimes of Radha and Krishna.

**13.** I praise the great soul Vaninatha Brahmachari, who in the assembly of devotees is called "the devotee," and in whom the host of devotees delights.[^10]

**14.** Full of love for Krishna, pure, bestower of supreme bliss — to Vallabha Chaitanya[^11] I bow, whose heart is wholly given to singing of the pastimes.

**15.** I bow to the *pandita* named Srinatha, the refuge of good qualities, by whom the whole due order of Krishna's service was carried out with great care.[^12]

**16.** He who fully bestows the wealth of *prema* even on the most wretched of people — to Srimad Uddhava Dasa, rich in virtues, I bow.

**17.** He whose holy book nourishes love for the sweetness of Krishna[^13] — to that Jitamitra, the bestower of all desired ends, I bow.

**18.** I bow to Jagannatha Dasa, known as "Kashthakata,"[^14] by whom the auspicious Holy Name was given in the land of Tripura as well.

**19.** To the excellent Haridasa Acharya,[^15] who dwells in the land of Vanga, made radiant by his own radiance (*ujjvala*) — to him I bow with supreme devotion.

**20.** I bow to Gopala Dasa, a resident of Sadipura, who flooded Bikrampur with the *rasas* of love for Radha and Krishna.[^16]

**21.** I bow to Sri Harsha Mishra, who delights in love for Krishna, whose mind is maddened by love for Gaura, a sprout of the *rasa* of great bliss.[^17]

**22.** I bow to Vraja-Lakshminatha Dasa,[^18] whose form is an abode of compassion, who is endowed with *mahabhava*, the bestower of the good fortune of Vraja.

**23.** I bow to the great soul Sri Chaitanya Dasa of Rangabati,[^19] whose body is ever adorned with tears of love, hairs standing on end, and thrills of ecstasy.

**24.** I bow to the great soul named Raghunatha, the root of *prema*: by merely hearing his name one attains the *rasa* of Vrindavana.

**25.** I bow to Shivananda, who bears the name Kumudananda,[^20] endowed with the radiant (*ujjvala*) *rasa*, pure, dwelling in the forest of Vrinda.

**26.** I bow to Jayananda, the great soul, the servant of Chaitanya, through whose efforts the *Sri Chaitanya-vilasa* was brought to light.[^21]

**27.** I bow to Amogha Pandita, whom Sri Gaura made His own — whose voice falters with love, whose body is steeped in it, and whose whole form is overcome with thrills of ecstasy.

**28.** I bow to Madhava Acharya, the abode of the *rasa* of devotion to Krishna, by whose diligence the book *Sri Krishna-mangala* was composed.[^22]

**29.** I bow to Gopala Dasa, the shelter of the *rasa* of loving devotion, who serves the two lotus feet of Sriman Madana-Gopala.[^23]

**30.** Endowed with honey-sweet affection, attached in love, the great soul who delights in dwelling in Vrindavana — to Sri Madhu Pandita I bow.[^24]

### Translator's notes

**31.** [I bow to] Sri Chandrashekhara — a vessel of Paurnamasi's abundant prema, a flood of boundless mercy, who is called "Paurnamasi."[^25]

**32.** I bow to Sri Vakreshvara Pandita, whose fame shines both in Utkala and in Tailanga, and who is filled with a flood of prema.[^26]

**33.** Endowed with good qualities without exception, his form supremely gentle, his very nature great *rasa* — I bow to Sri Damodara Pandita, whom the wise know as Svarupa because he gave up the *shikha* and the sacred thread.[^27]

**34.** Him who delights in the joy of serving Srila Govindadeva, merciful, the giver of prema, pure, an eternal embodiment of bliss — I bow to the excellent Ananta Acharya, a multitude of *mahabhava*,[^28] whose body from feet to crown is made radiant by hairs standing on end in prema.[^29]

**35.** I bow to the one named Sri Krishnadasa, whose body is intoxicated with prema, whose form is ever adorned with tears of prema, with thrills and hairs standing on end.[^30]

**36.** I bow to Paramananda Bhattacharya, the lover of *rasa*, who bestows [shelter at] the feet of Radha-Govinda, of Gauranga and of Gadadhara.[^31]

**37.** Full of great splendour, delighting in the joy of beautiful service — to him, Bhavananda Gosvami, who gives prema to those who remember him, I bow.[^32]

**38.** It was by him that Srila Gopinathadeva was carefully and well served; by the mere remembrance of him prema for Krishna is born.[^33]

**39.** I praise Yadunatha Chakravarti, an ocean of good qualities, most dear to Gadadhara, called "Lila-bhagavata"; to the root of prema, the greatly wise, the great-hearted — to him I bow with devotion.[^34]

**40.** I bow to the one named Pushpa-Gopala, who delights in prema: by his own *rasas* [the village] named Svarnagramaka burst into blossom.[^35]

**41.** I praise the brahmachari, the great-hearted Krishnadasa, whose mind is anointed with the *ujjvala-rasa*, peaceful, dwelling in the forest of Vrindavana.[^36]

**42.** He is the companion of Lokanatha-Bhatta, the abode of the happiness and bliss of prema, immersed in the *rasa* of Radha and Krishna, called Sri Champakalatika.[^37]

**43.** I bow to the excellent acharya Vidyananta,[^38] who lived on the bank of the Ganga — he by whom worship of Gaura was performed with fruits and roots.

**44.** I bow to Mangala Vaishnava, pure in heart and body, whose body is softened by the nectar of the pastimes of the Lord and Lady of Vrindavana.[^39]

**45.** I bow to Govinda Acharya, the abode of the nectar of love for Krishna, the relisher of Govinda's delight, who lives in the land of Malla.[^40]

**46.** I bow to Srimad Akrura Thakura, the giver of the bliss of *bhava*, the root of love for Gadadhara, who delights in love for Gaura.[^41]

**47.** I bow to Sanketa Acharya, who understood the hints of Sri Gaura, a vessel of Gaura's love, the master who bestows love for Krishna.[^42]

**48.** I bow to the glorious king Rudra, whose name begins with "Pratapa" — the renowned [Prataparudra], by whom Gaura together with Gadadhara was well served.[^43]

**49.** I bow to Kamalakanta Acharya, whose form is of great beauty, a mass of supreme bliss, who served Rupa.[^44]

**50.** I bow to Sri Yadava Acharya, whose body is intoxicated with prema, who possesses the full ripeness of the *rasa* of the pastimes, an ocean of good qualities.[^45]

**51.** I bow to the one named Vallabha Bhatta, who lives in Ayarol, who plunges into the ocean of the loving pastimes of Radha and Krishna.[^46]

**52.** [I bow to] Narayana Padiyari — the abode of the nectar of love for Gaura, who delights in the joy of serving Sri Gadadhara and Gauranga.[^47]

**53.** I bow to Sri Hridayananda, ever immersed in the *rasa* of prema, whose body embodies Gaura's *bhava*, the wonder of *mahabhava*.[^48]

**54.** I bow to the one named Chaitanya Vallabha, the abode of the *rasa* of prema, who longs to sing the qualities of Gadadhara and Gaura.[^49]

**55.** With supreme devotion I bow to the one named Hasti-Gopala Dasa, whose body is intoxicated with prema — the excellent one, full of love for Gaura.

**56.** [I bow] also [to] Bhagavan Acharya, whose body is full of splendour; by the mere remembrance of him love for Gaura is born.[^50]

**57.** I bow to Vaishnava Dasa, pure in heart and body, whose body is softened by the nectar of the pastimes of the Lord and Lady of Vrindavana.[^51]

**58.** With devotion I bow to Yadunatha Chakravarti, called "Lila-bhagavata," the root of prema, the greatly wise, the great-hearted.[^52]

**59.** All the associates of the Pandita, who delight in the joy of serving the feet of Srila Sri Gaura, are those whose bodies exist for *shringara* — for service in *madhurya-rasa*.[^53]

### Translator's notes

[^1]: *Ujjvala* ("radiant," "bright") is a name of *madhurya-rasa*, conjugal love for Krishna (cf. Rupa Gosvami's *Ujjvala-nilamani*). Literally *sadojjvala-vilasin*, "ever delighting in *ujjvala*."
[^2]: Sudama is a cowherd friend of Krishna in Vraja; here apparently the name of Shridhara Brahmachari in Krishna's pastimes (*sudamakhyam*, "called Sudama"). In the *Gaura-ganoddesha-dipika* (127) it is Sundarananda who is named as Sudama.
[^3]: The reading is unclear: the edition has *bhaktamudakaram*; taken as *bhaktamodakaram*, "a treasury (mine) of joy for the devotees."
[^4]: A play on words: *ananta* means "boundless." The end of the line is corrupt in the edition (*gaura-prema-nohi-bhajanam*, one syllable too long for the metre); restored as *gaura-premno hi bhajanam*, "truly a vessel of love for Gaura"; *gaura-premaika-bhajanam*, "the one vessel of love for Gaura," is also possible.
[^5]: The reading is unclear: literally *svabhavajam*, "born of his own nature"; understood to mean that the state of *mahabhava* is inborn, natural to him.
[^6]: Svardhuni ("the heavenly river") is the Ganga; the edition has *svadhunya*, restored as *svardhunya*. A play on the name: Ganga-mantri, "counsellor of the Ganga," "one instructed by the Ganga"; *sumantritah*, "well instructed (given a mantra)."
[^7]: *Mamu* (Bengali): "uncle," a mother's brother. Mamu Thakura served the Deity of Tota-Gopinatha in Puri after Gadadhara Pandita.
[^8]: A verse of three half-lines. The Chatta are a brahmana family (Chattopadhyaya). *Kanthabharana* means "neck ornament, necklace"; *upadhi*, an honorary title. This Ananta is apparently not Ananta Acharya of verse 4: in CC Adi 12 Ananta Acharya and Kanthabharana are named separately. The end (*tayoh kanthavatarakam*, literally "the descent [as the ornament] of Their neck") is unclear; taken as a play on the title: he is the incarnation of the necklace of Radha and Krishna.
[^9]: A play on words: *bhu-garbha*, "the womb (depths) of the earth"; the reading *bhugarbhottha-suvishrutam* is unclear.
[^10]: The reading is unclear: *bhakta-sanghatta-bhaktakhyam*, literally "called a devotee in the assembly (throng) of devotees"; *ranjitam* ("delighting," "beloved") may also be read *rajitam*, "shining among the host of devotees."
[^11]: In CC Adi 12: "Vallabha Chaitanya-dasa."
[^12]: In CC Adi 12: "Srinatha Chakravarti"; here he is called a *pandita*. *Paripati*: the due order, the proper rite of service.
[^13]: The book is not named. The words *krishna-madhurya* could also be taken as its title (*Krishna-madhurya*), but this is only a conjecture.
[^14]: *Kashthakata* (Bengali): "woodcutter"; the nickname is also given in CC Adi 12. Tripura (*traipura desha*) is a region to the east of Bengal.
[^15]: In CC Adi 12: "Sri Hari Acharya." Vanga is Bengal, in the narrower sense East Bengal.
[^16]: In CC Adi 12: "*saji puriya gopala*" (Puriya Gopala); here, Gopala Dasa of Sadipura (*sadipura*). Bikrampur (*vikramam puram*) is a region of East Bengal near Dhaka.
[^17]: In CC Adi 12: "Sri Harsha, Raghu Mishra" (two names in a row); here, "Sri Harsha Mishra." The edition has *gaura-premanamatta-cittam* (one syllable too long for the metre); restored as *gaura-premonmatta-cittam*, "whose mind is maddened by love for Gaura."
[^18]: Literally *vraja-lakshminatha-dasam*: possibly "Lakshminatha Dasa of Vraja," or "servant of the Lord of Vraja-Lakshmi." In CC Adi 12: "Pandita Lakshminatha."
[^19]: *Rangavati*; in CC Adi 12: "*rangavati caitanya-dasa*."
[^20]: In CC Adi 12: Shivananda Chakravarti, "who found his rest at the feet of Madana-Gopala." The name Kumudananda ("joy of the *kumuda*," the night lotus) is not explained in the text. "The forest of Vrinda" is Vrindavana.
[^21]: *Sri Chaitanya-vilasa*: so Jayananda's *Chaitanya-mangala*, a Bengali life of the Lord, is called here.
[^22]: *Sri Krishna-mangala*: Madhava Acharya's Bengali poem on Krishna based on the *Srimad-Bhagavatam*.
[^23]: The edition has *kunja-dvandva* ("two groves"); restored as *anghri-kanja-dvandva*, "the two lotuses of the feet." According to CC Adi 12, it was Shivananda Chakravarti who found his rest at the feet of Madana-Gopala (cf. verse 25); which of the Gopalas of Gadadhara's branch is named here, the text does not say.
[^24]: A play on words: *madhu*, "honey, sweetness," and the name Madhu. Madhu Pandita was the servant of the Deity of Gopinatha in Vrindavana.
[^25]: The verse has no verb; "I bow" is understood from the preceding verse. The compound *paurṇamāsī-pṛthu-prema-pātram* may also be taken as "Paurnamasi, a vessel of abundant prema"; the second line names Chandrashekhara "Paurnamasi" outright — this is his identity in Vraja. The text does not specify which Chandrashekhara is meant; CC Adi 12 does not list one among Gadadhara's branches. The project's register of persons marks Govinda Acharya as "GGD 41: Paurnamasi" (cf. verse 45) — to be checked.
[^26]: Utkala is Orissa; Tailanga is the Telugu country in the south of India.
[^27]: A verse of three half-lines. Giving up the *shikha* and the sacred thread without accepting full *sannyasa*, and the name "Svarupa," are marks of Svarupa Damodara (Purushottama Acharya; CC Madhya 10). Damodara Pandita of the CC is a different person; the author apparently means Svarupa Damodara, although he calls him "Damodara Pandita."
[^28]: *Kadambaka* means "multitude, mass"; probably also an allusion to the *kadamba* flower, to which hairs standing on end in ecstasy are compared.
[^29]: The edition gives four half-lines under one number: the first couplet (on the service of Govindadeva) ends with "॥" and no number. In Vrindavana the service of Govindadeva was carried out by Haridasa Pandita, a disciple of Ananta Acharya (CC Adi 8); so *anantācārya-varyam* might also mean "the best [of the disciples] of Ananta Acharya," with the first couplet referring to Haridasa Pandita. Ananta Acharya himself has already been praised in verse 4.
[^30]: The second line repeats word for word the second line of verse 23. The text does not specify which Krishnadasa this is; Krishnadasa Brahmachari is praised separately in verse 41.
[^31]: Probably Paramananda Bhattacharya of Vrindavana, the friend of Madhu Pandita (cf. verse 30).
[^32]: *Smṛti-prema-dam* — literally "who gives prema through remembrance [of him]"; cf. the next verse.
[^33]: Grammatically this verse continues verse 37 (on Bhavananda). Gopinatha is the Deity in Vrindavana served by Madhu Pandita (verse 30); Bhavananda was Madhu Pandita's fellow-student and a servant of Gopinatha (*Bhakti-ratnakara* 13).
[^34]: A verse of three half-lines. *Līlā-bhāgavatābhidham* — "bearing the title Lila-bhagavata," i.e. probably "a *bhagavata* (reciter, knower) of the [Lord's] pastimes"; the meaning of the title is unclear. The second and third lines are repeated almost verbatim in verse 58.
[^35]: A play on words: *puṣpa* is "flower," *puṣpitaḥ* "blossomed," and *rasa* means both "sap" and "taste, mood." *Svarṇagrāmaka*, "the Golden Village," is apparently his village; the place is not identified (Sonargaon in East Bengal, for instance, is called Suvarnagrama in Sanskrit). The reading and sense of the line are unclear.
[^36]: *Ujjvala*, "the radiant," is Rupa Gosvami's name for *madhurya-rasa* (*shringara*) (*Ujjvala-nilamani*). Cf. Krishnadasa Brahmachari, servant of Madana-mohana in Vrindavana.
[^37]: The verse has no verb and grammatically continues verse 41, i.e. it apparently describes the same Krishnadasa Brahmachari. *Lokanātha-bhaṭṭa-saṅgam* — reading unclear: "companion of Lokanatha and Bhatta" (Lokanatha Gosvami and perhaps Gopala Bhatta Gosvami in Vrindavana), or the name of one person, "Lokanatha Bhatta." Champakalatika (Champakalata) is one of the eight principal *sakhis* of Radha; this is his identity in Vraja.
[^38]: *Vidyānantācārya-varyam* — reading unclear. Possibly "Vidyananda Acharya" (ন্ত and ন্দ are easily confused in the type), "Ananta Acharya, [rich] in learning," or "the best of acharyas, boundless in learning" (*vidyā-ananta*). The person is not identified.
[^39]: *Vṛndāvaneśayoḥ* is a dual: the Lord and the Lady of Vrindavana, Radha and Krishna.
[^40]: Malla-desha is probably Mallabhum (Vana-Vishnupura); not to be confused with Mallabhumi on the Subarnarekha. Cf. Govinda Acharya, the composer of songs about Radha and Krishna (project register of persons).
[^41]: *Bhavānandadam* — reading unclear: *bhāvānanda-dam*, "giver of the bliss of *bhava*," or *bhavānanda-dam*, "giver of bliss to the world"; a name, Bhavananda, is also possible (cf. verse 37). *Ṭhakkura* is a Sanskritized "Thakura." Akrura Thakura is not named in CC Adi 12; the person is not identified.
[^42]: *Saṅketa* means "sign, hint"; the name seems to be played upon by the word *iṅgita* ("hint, sign"). Sanketa Acharya is not named in CC Adi 12; the person is not identified.
[^43]: *Pratāpādyam* — "with 'Pratapa' at the beginning," i.e. Prataparudra, the king of Orissa.
[^44]: *Rūpa-niṣeviṇam* — "who served Rupa" (Rupa Gosvami?) or "who served the form [of the Lord]"; cf. Yadava Acharya, Rupa's companion (verse 50). The text does not specify which Kamalakanta is meant.
[^45]: Probably Yadavacharya Gosani, companion of Rupa Gosvami in Vrindavana (CC Adi 8).
[^46]: *Āyarola* is Arail (Adail) near Prayaga, the village of Vallabha Bhatta (Vallabhacharya, founder of the *pushti-marga*). According to CC Antya 7, he received initiation from Gadadhara Pandita.
[^47]: The verse has no verb. *Paḍiyāri* is apparently the Oriya *paḍihārī / paḍiyārī*, "doorkeeper, temple attendant" (in the Jagannatha temple) — a title or an office. The person is not identified.
[^48]: According to the project glossary, Hridayananda is the same as Hridaya Chaitanya; not verified for this verse.
[^49]: CC Adi 12 names in Gadadhara's branch both Vallabha Chaitanya Dasa (cf. verse 14) and Chaitanya Vallabha.
[^50]: The verse has no verb. The text does not specify which Bhagavan Acharya is meant (cf. Bhagavan Acharya of Puri, CC Adi 10, Antya 2); CC Adi 12 does not name him in Gadadhara's branch.
[^51]: *Vaiṣṇavaṃ dāsam* may also be taken as "the Vaishnava servant." The verse almost exactly coincides with verse 44 (on Mangala Vaishnava); it may be the same person, or a repetition.
[^52]: An almost verbatim repetition of verse 39 (without its first line); in the edition *cakravartī* stands in the nominative.
[^53]: "The Pandita" is Gadadhara Pandita. *Śṛṅgārārtha-kalevarāḥ* — literally "whose bodies are for *shringara*": in the eternal *lila* of Vraja their service is in the conjugal (*madhurya*) *rasa*. With this verse the poem ends.
