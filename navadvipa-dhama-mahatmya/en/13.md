# Chapter 13. Śrī Vidyā Nagar and Śrī Jahnudwīp

**1.** All glory to Gaura, Nityānanda, Advaita, Gadādhar, Śrīvās, and the ocean of kīrtan, Śrī Nabadwīp Dhām!

**2.** Coming to Vidyā Nagar, Nityānanda Rāy taught Śrī Jīva the importance of the town.

**3.** “During the destruction of the universe, the eternal abode of Nabadwīp remains undisturbed as an eight-petalled lotus.

**4.** “All the Avatārs and thousands of fortunate souls stay in one part of that lotus.

**5.** “In Vidyā Nagar within Ṛtudwīp, the Lord, as Matsya, holds all the Vedas.

**6.** “The Vedas, which contain all knowledge, took shelter and stayed here. They named this place Śrī Vidyā Nagar.

**7.** “When Brahmā desired to create the world again, he saw the full devastation and became extremely afraid.

**8.** “At that time, the Lord bestowed mercy upon him. He received that mercy here and then prayed to the Lord.

**9.** “As he opened his mouths, the extremely beautiful goddess Saraswatī manifested from his tongues.

**10.** “Becoming empowered by Saraswatī, four-headed Brahmā prayed to Kṛṣṇa and felt great satisfaction.

**11.** “When creation takes place, Māyā remains on the bank of the Virajā River and envelops all directions with the three modes of material nature.

**12–13.** “The sages then reveal knowledge within that world manifested by Māyā. They take shelter in this abode of Saraswatī and conquer ignorance.

**14.** “They study the sixty-four branches of knowledge and then teach them at various places on the earth.

**15.** “The residences of all the sages who study each and every type of knowledge are present here eternally.

**16.** “Śrī Vālmīki came here by the grace of Nārad and learned the art of poetry.

**17.** “Dhanvantari came here and learned āyurveda. Viśvāmitra and others learned archery.

**18.** “Śaunaka and other sages studied Vedic mantras. The god of gods, Lord Śiva, studied tantra.

**19.** “At the request of the sages, the four Vedas manifested from the four mouths of Lord Brahmā.

**20.** “Kapila resided here and wrote about sāṅkhya, and Śrī Gautam Ṛṣi wrote texts about logic and argument.

**21.** “Kaṇāda wrote vaiśeṣika, and Pātañjali wrote texts on yoga.

**22.** “Jaiminī wrote texts about mīmāṁsā, and Vedavyās wrote the Purāṇas and other texts.

**23.** “Nārad and four other sages wrote the Pañcharātras and taught souls spiritual practices.

**24.** “In this garden, all the Upaniṣads worshipped Śrī Gaurāṅga for a long time.

**25.** “Invisibly, Śrī Gaura said to them, ‘The conception of formlessness has polluted your hearts.

**26–27.** “‘As scriptures, you will not attain Me, but when you take birth as My associates in My manifest Pastimes, you will see Me and loudly chant My glories.’

**28.** “Hearing this, the Upaniṣads became silent and secretly waited here.

**29.** “This glorious Age of Kali, in which Śrī Gaurāṅga has descended, is the best of all ages.

**30–33.** “Hearing that Gaurāṅgasundar would perform Pastimes of scholarship, Bṛhaspati and his associates took birth. As Vāsudev Sārvabhauma, Bṛhaspati endeavoured greatly to please Gaurāṅga. Knowing within his heart, ‘My Lord will perform Pastimes of scholarship in Nabadwīp’, and becoming detached, Bṛhaspati left the assembly of Indra with his associates. Joyfully, he and his associates took birth in various places.

**34.** “Bṛhaspati made a school in Vidyā Nagar and taught here as Sārvabhauma Bhaṭṭāchārya.

**35.** “Thinking, ‘Later, I may sink into the net of knowledge and forget Gaurāṅga’, he performed a trick.

**36.** “Keeping his disciples in Nadia, he went elsewhere before the birth of Gaura.

**37.** “He thought, ‘If I am Gaura’s servant, then my Lord will mercifully bring me to His side.’

**38.** “Thinking this, Sārvabhauma went to Nīlāchal and propagated texts on māyāvād there.

**39.** “Here, during His Pastimes of scholarship, Gaurachandra Prabhu defeated and joked with the disciples of Sārvabhauma.

**40.** “Sometimes the Lord came to Vidyā Nagar, posed trick questions, and defeated everyone.

**41.** “Defeated, all the teachers and students would flee.

**42.** “Ignorance leaves those who hear about Gaurāṅga’s extraordinary Pastimes of scholarship.”

**43.** Hearing this with the joy of divine love, Jīva rolled on the ground at the school in Vidyā Nagar, filled with divine love.

**44.** At the holy feet of Nityānanda, he submitted, “I have a doubt. Please cut it away.

**45.** “Knowledge of analysis and argument are inauspicious, so how do they reside in the eternal Dhām?”

**46.** Hearing this, Nityānanda Prabhu embraced Jīva and affectionately said, “Hari! Haribol!

**47.** “Nothing is inauspicious in the holy abode of the Lord. Here, argument and analysis have no power of their own.

**48.** “Here, everything is dependent on devotion and serves devotion. As a result of their sins, the wicked think the opposite.

**49.** “Here, the goddess of devotion is supreme: everyone is her servant, and everything reveals her.

**50.** “Nabadwīp is the abode of the ninefold practice of devotion. Here, action and knowledge always serve devotion.

**51.** “The scriptures give misconceptions to those who are averse, and love for Kṛṣṇa to those who are gentle.

**52.** “Prauḍhā Māyā, a servant of Gaurāṅga, is the presiding goddess here. In every age, she stays here and serves Gaurāṅga.

**53.** “Māyā blinds those who are very sinful and inimical to Vaiṣṇavas, and subjects them to various miseries.

**54.** “All sin and karma are annihilated here. In the form of knowledge, Prauḍhā Māyā destroys karma.

**55.** “If, however, someone offends a Vaiṣṇava, Prauḍhā Māyā drives them further into the miseries of karma.

**56.** “Such sinners may study in Nadia, but they never attain the wealth of divine love for the feet of Kṛṣṇa.

**57.** “They attain only ignorance of true knowledge and do not see the glory of Śrī Gaurāṅga in Nadia.

**58.** “Thus, knowledge is not inauspicious; its shadow, ignorance, is inauspicious.

**59.** “O Jīva, you will reveal all this by the mercy of Gaurāṅga. By His desire, you will write scriptures about it.

**60.** “Actually, He will write scriptures through you. Now, let us go to the residence of Jahnu Muni.”

**61.** While speaking, they went to Jān Nagar and saw the beauty of the hermitage of Jahnu Muni.

**62.** Nityānanda Prabhu said, “This charming abode is known as Jahnudwīp and Bhadravan.

**63.** “Jahnu Muni performed austerities here and saw the golden form of Gaura.

**64.** “Once, when Jahnu Muni sat here to perform his evening meditation, His copper vessel fell into the swiftly flowing River Gaṅgā.

**65.** “The copper vessel fell into the river and floated away. Jahnu Muni then drank all the water of the Gaṅgā with his palm.

**66.** “Mahārāj Bhagīrath thought, ‘Where did the Gaṅgā go?’ Bewildered, he began to think over the situation.

**67.** “Understanding that Jahnu Muni had drunk all the water of the Gaṅgā, Mahārāj Bhagīrath became concerned.

**68.** Sober Mahārāj Bhagīrath worshipped the sage for some time, and eventually the Gaṅgā broke out of the sage’s body.

**69.** “Thereafter, she became known as Jāhnavī. The whole world now calls her ‘Jāhnavī’.

**70.** “Some time later, Gaṅgā’s son, Bhīṣmadev, met his grandfather Jahnu Muni here.

**71.** “Jahnu Muni honoured Bhīṣma and kept Bhīṣma at his home for a long time.

**72.** “Bhīṣma learned profound religious principles from Jahnu Muni and later taught the essence of them to Yudhiṣṭhir Mahārāj.

**73.** “Residing in Nabadwīp, Bhīṣma attained the wealth of devotion and became recognised as a Vaiṣṇava.

**74.** “Thus, Jahnudwīp is supremely purifying. Fortunate souls reside here eternally.”

**75.** That day Nityānanda and the devotees stayed at the home of a devotee in Jahnudwīp.

**76.** The next morning, Nityānanda Prabhu took the devotees and went to Modadrumadwīp.

**77.** Bhakti Vinod, whose pride is the feet of Jāhnavā and Nitāi, chants the glories of Nadia.
