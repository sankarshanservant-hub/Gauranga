# Chapter 3. Instructions for Circumambulating the Dhām

**1.** All glory to the son of Śachī, the moon of Nabadwīp! All glory to the avadhūt Nityānanda Rāy!

**2.** All glory to the worshippable Śrī Advaita Prabhu! All glory to Gadādhar Paṇḍit and Śrīvās Paṇḍit!

**3.** All glory to Nabadwīp Dhām, the best of all abodes, the abode with which Gaurachandra appeared!

**4.** Devotees, listen! I will now describe everything within the thirty-two miles of Nabadwīp.

**5.** Sixteen rivers exist eternally within the thirty-two miles of Nabadwīp.

**6.** Four islands are on the east bank of the original Gaṅgā, and five are on the west bank.

**7.** These heavenly rivers flow throughout the islands and always beautify Nabadwīp Dhām.

**8.** Gaṅgā Devī’s original stream always flows through the centre of the Dhām; the other holy rivers flow in separate streams.

**9.** The beautiful Yamunā flows near the Gaṅgā. The Saraswatī, the carrier of knowledge, flows in another channel.

**10.** East of the Yamunā are the three long rivers: the Tāmraparṇī, Kṛtamālā, and Brahmaputra.

**11.** The Sarayū, Narmadā, Sindhu, Kāverī, Gomatī, and Godāvarī flow swiftly on the west side.

**12.** All these rivers cross each other and divide Nabadwīp into nine parts.

**13.** By the will of the Lord, these rivers sometimes go dry, and when the Lord wills it, they again fill with water.

**14.** By the will of the Lord, they sometimes submerge portions of land and then again reveal them.

**15.** The Dhām performs Pastimes eternally in this way and always appears before fortunate souls.

**16.** When intense desire arises in the heart of a devotee, all the islands and rivers in the Dhām reveal themselves.

**17.** Sometimes in dreams, sometimes in meditation, and sometimes in their vision, devotees see the Dhām through their devotion.

**18.** All the scriptures call the island at the confluence of the Gaṅgā and Yamunā Antardwīp.

**19.** Within Antardwīp is Māyāpur, where Lord Chaitanya appeared.

**20.** Devotees know Mahāvan in Goloka to be Māyāpur in Nabadwīp.

**21.** Śvetadwīp, Vaikuṇṭha, and Goloka Vṛndāvan are always present in Nabadwīp.

**22–23.** The seven great cities—Ayodhyā, Mathurā, Māyā, Kāśī, Kāñchī, Avantī, and Dvārakā—exist eternally in Nabadwīp in their respective places, by the arrangement of Gaurachandra.

**24.** The true form of the city Māyā, the gateway of the Gaṅgā, is Māyāpur, whose glories are stated repeatedly throughout the scriptures.

**25.** Those who once go to Māyāpur easily cross over material illusion.

**26.** If a soul wanders in Māyāpur, then the influence of Māyā goes away, and they do not take birth again.

**27.** North of Māyāpur is Sīmantadwīp. The sādhus and scriptures always explain the procedure for circumambulating the Dhām.

**28.** O wise devotees, visit Śrī Māyāpur in Antardwīp and then go to Sīmantadwīp.

**29.** Godrumadwīp is south of Māyāpur. Walk there and then joyfully go to Madhyadwīp.

**30.** Visit these four islands on the east bank of the Gaṅgā and then slowly cross the river.

**31.** Peacefully walk through Koladwīp and then see the beauty of Ṛtudwīp.

**32.** O best of the wise, thereafter, see the wonderful beauty of Jahnudwīp and then go to Modadrumadwīp.

**33.** O devotees, see Rudradwīp, cross the Gaṅgā again, and then visit Māyāpur once more.

**34.** There, slowly enter the abode of Śrī Jagannāth and Śachī to see the Lord.

**35.** Circumambulation is always done in this way and is a source of unlimited joy for the soul.

**36.** Everyone concurs that it is best to start on Mākarī Saptamī and end on Phālgunī Pūrṇimā.

**37–38.** Nitāi and Gaurāṅga bless the great souls who complete their circumambulation and visit Māyāpur on the birthday of the Lord. They make them qualified for devotion and give them the shade of Their feet.

**39.** I have briefly described circumambulating the Dhām. Now I will elaborate. Please listen.

**40.** Those who traverse the one hundred and sixty-eight miles of Śrī Gauḍa Maṇḍal quickly attain the wealth of divine love for Gaura.

**41.** Bhakti Vinod, whose aspiration is the shade of Jāhnavā and Nitāi’s feet, reveals the glories of Nadia.
