# Chapter 11. Śrī Śrī Koladwīp, Śrī Samudragaḍ, Śrī Champā Haṭṭa, and Śrī Jayadev

**1.** All glory to Śrī Chaitanya, Nityānanda, Advaita, Śrīvās, and all the devotees of the Lord!

**2.** All glory to the land of Gauḍa, the best of all lands, where Śrī Chaitanya descended with His Name!

**3.** Nityānanda Prabhu said, “Everyone, listen! Here, the Gaṅgā convenes with five other rivers.

**4.** “The Gaṅgā flows here with the Mandākinī and the Alakānandā. The Saraswatī is also hidden here.

**5.** “The Yamunā comes from the west with the Bhogavatī, and the swift Mānasa Gaṅgā also flows within them.

**6.** “Considering this place Mahāprayāg, sages have performed billions of sacrifices here with Lord Brahmā.

**7.** “The glories of this place of Brahmā’s sacrifices are unlimited. If a soul bathes here, they do not take birth again.

**8.** “Who can describe the glories of this place? It is not a holy place that is now simply a dry river.

**9.** “All souls who give up their life in the water, on the earth, or in the air here attain Śrī Goloka Vṛndāvan.

**10.** “This place is known as Kuliyāpāhāḍa. It is a raised land on the bank of the Gaṅgā, resembling a mountain.

**11.** “It is called Koladwīp in the scriptures. Everyone, listen to a story from Satya-yuga.

**12.** “There was a brāhmaṇ boy named Vāsudev. He regularly worshipped Varāhadev.

**13.** “He worshipped a Deity of Śrī Varāha and constantly prayed to Him.

**14.** “‘O Lord! Mercifully reveal Yourself to me so that my eyes and life may be fulfilled.’

**15.** “Praying in this way, the brāhmaṇ would roll on the ground and cry, ‘O Lord! If You do not reveal Yourself, my life is worthless.’

**16.** “Eventually, Śrī Varāha mercifully revealed Himself to Vāsudev in the form of a boar.

**17.** “His body was ornamented with various jewels and ornaments, and His feet, neck, nose, mouth, and eyes were enchanting.

**18.** “His body was as tall as a mountain. Seeing Him, the brāhmaṇ again and again felt himself to be fortunate.

**19.** “He fell to the ground, bowed at the feet of the Lord, cried fervently, and rolled on the ground.

**20.** “Seeing the devotion of the brāhmaṇ, Varāha spoke sweet words to Vāsudev.

**21.** “‘O Vāsudev! You are My devotee, and I am very pleased with your worship.

**22.** “‘When Kali-yuga arrives, I will perform Pastimes in Nabadwīp. Listen to My conclusive words.

**23.** “‘There is no abode like Nabadwīp in the three worlds; all of My most beloved abodes are hidden within it.

**24.** “‘Brahmāvarta and all other holy places that exist are present here. This is the conclusion of the scriptures.

**25–26.** “‘The holy place in which I appeared during Brahmā’s sacrifice and killed Hiraṇyākṣa, ripping him with My tusks, exists here where I have now appeared.

**27.** “‘All holy places exist here and serve Nabadwīp. By residing in Nabadwīp, you reside in all the holy places.

**28.** “‘You are fortunate: you have served Me in Nabadwīp. You will take birth here when I appear as Śrī Gaurāṅga.

**29.** “‘Easily, you will see His grand saṅkīrtan and extraordinary form.’

**30.** “Saying this, Śrī Varāha disappeared. A divine voice then arose to guide the brāhmaṇ.

**31.** “Worshippable Vāsudev was a great scholar. He studied all the scriptures and understood everything clearly.

**32.** “‘At the beginning of Kali-yuga during the reign of Vaivasvata Manu, the Pastimes of Śrī Gaurāṅga will manifest in Nadia.

**33.** “‘The sages kept this truth hidden but also hinted at it in the scriptures. The wise understand all of this.

**34.** “‘When the Lord appears, His Pastimes will be revealed, but for now they should be kept hidden.’ In this way, Vāsudev understood the purport of the scriptures.

**35.** “With great joy, the brāhmaṇ performed saṅkīrtan, chanting the Name of Gaura constantly within his heart.

**36–37.** “Having seen the mountain-like form of Varāhadev, the wise Vāsudev decided that this place would be named Koladwīp Parvat. Since then, it has been known as Parvat.

**38.** “O Jīva! Devotees know this place to be Govardhan Hill of Vṛndāvan, which is filled with the Lord’s eternal Pastimes.

**39.** “See Śrī Bahulāvan to the north. Its effulgence shines in all directions.

**40.** “O Jīva, the twelve forests are not arranged here the way they are in Vṛndāvan.

**41.** “By the will of the Lord, their order is reversed here. The whimsical Lord knows the reason for this.

**42.** “See the way they are arranged here. This extraordinary reversal increases everyone’s divine love.”

**43.** Nityānanda Prabhu went ahead and then said, “See here Samudragaḍ.

**44.** “O best of the wise, see here both Dvārakā Purī and Śrī Gaṅgā Sāgar.

**45.** “Śrī Samudra Sen was a king here. He was a great devotee of Kṛṣṇa—he knew nothing but Kṛṣṇa.

**46.** “When Bhīmasen came here with his army to conquer Bengal, he surrounded Samudragaḍ.

**47.** “The king knew Kṛṣṇa was the Pāṇḍavas’ only shelter, and that if the Pāṇḍavas faced danger, Kṛṣṇa would come.

**48.** “‘If I can cause Bhīma to show that he is afraid, Kṛṣṇa will feel compassion in response to his cries of distress.

**49.** “‘Then Kṛṣṇa will compassionately come to this servant’s land, and I will easily see His dark form with my eyes.’

**50.** “Thinking thus, the king prepared his army. Taking his elephants, horses, and foot soldiers, he went to battle.

**51.** “Remembering Śrī Kṛṣṇa, the king fired arrows. Overwhelmed by the arrows, Bhīma became extremely afraid.

**52.** “Seeing that he was in danger, Bhīma called out to Kṛṣṇa within his mind, ‘O Lord! Protect Bhīma with Your holy feet.

**53.** “‘I cannot fight with Samudra Sen, but I also cannot tolerate the great shame of retreating.

**54.** “‘O Kṛṣṇa! You are the Lord of the Pāṇḍavas. O merciful Lord! If we are defeated, it will be a great shame.’

**55.** “Hearing Bhīma’s sorrowful cry, Kṛṣṇa mercifully appeared on the battlefield.

**56.** “An extraordinary event then took place: Śrī Samudra Sen saw the Lord’s form, but no one else did.

**57.** “The Lord’s adolescent form was the colour of a fresh rain cloud, a forest garland swung on His neck, and His crown shone.

**58.** “He had extremely beautiful ornaments on all His limbs, and His extraordinary body was clothed in yellow garments.

**59.** “Seeing Kṛṣṇa’s form, the king fainted out of divine love. He then steadied himself and prayed to the Lord.

**60.** “‘O Kṛṣṇa, You are the Lord of the universe and the saviour of the fallen. Having seen this fallen soul, You have come here.

**61.** “‘The souls of this world glorify Your Pastimes, and hearing that, I desired to see You.

**62–63.** “‘My vow, however, O merciful Lord, was that You would appear in Nabadwīp: “I will see Your enchanting form here so that I do not have to leave Nabadwīp.”

**64.** “‘O merciful Lord, You have upheld my vow and appeared as Kṛṣṇa in Nabadwīp.

**65.** “‘Still, my very confidential desire is that You will appear before my eyes as Gaurāṅga.’

**66.** “The king suddenly saw before him the incomparably sweet forms and Pastimes of Rādhā and Kṛṣṇa.

**67.** “Kṛṣṇa was tending cows and playing with His friends in Śrī Kumudavan in the afternoon.

**68.** “Within a moment, these Pastimes disappeared, and the king saw the form of Śrī Gaurāṅga, filling his eyes.

**69.** “Immersed in grand saṅkīrtan, the Lord was dancing and chanting with His devotees.

**70.** “The Lord’s extremely beautiful golden lustre made the eyes of the king quiver and his heart palpitate intensely.

**71.** “Seeing the form of the Lord, the king considered himself fortunate. He then offered prayers at the Lord’s feet.

**72.** “Eventually, everything disappeared. The king became thoughtful and began to cry.

**73.** “Bhīmasen did not see this miracle and thought that the king had suddenly become afraid to fight.

**74.** “Bhīmasen showed his immense strength, and the king happily submitted his tribute.

**75.** “Bhīmasen accepted the tribute and went elsewhere. Still now, the whole world sings of Bhīma as the conqueror of all directions.

**76.** “This place, Samudragaḍ, is on the boundary of Nabadwīp. Even Brahmā does not know the glories of this place.

**77.** “The ocean came to the shelter of the Gaṅgā here and, with the mood of a devotee, served the feet of the Lord.

**78.** “The Gaṅgā said, ‘O Ocean, very soon my Lord will stay in a forest on your bank.’

**79.** “The ocean said, ‘O goddess, listen to my words! The son of Śachī never leaves Nabadwīp.

**80.** “‘Although He will stay on my bank for some time, He will remain invisibly in Nadia.

**81.** “‘The Vedas sing of the manifest and unmanifest Pastimes of the Lord here in His eternal abode of Nabadwīp.

**82.** “‘O beautiful goddess! I will stay here under your shelter and serve Śrī Gaurāṅga in Nabadwīp.’

**83.** “Saying this, the ocean stayed in Nabadwīp, meditating on the eternal Pastimes of Gaurāṅga.”

**84.** Nityānanda Prabhu then came to Champā Haṭṭa Grām and rested there in the home of Vāṇīnāth.

**85.** In the afternoon, Nityānanda toured through Champā Haṭṭa and said, “O Jīva, listen!

**86.** “Previously, there was a beautiful champak forest here that was part of Khadiravan.

**87.** “Daily, Champakalatā Sakhī would pick champak flowers here, string garlands, and serve Rādhā and Kṛṣṇa.

**88.** “When Kali-yuga started, garland-makers eagerly began to take the champak flowers from the forest.

**89.** “They established a market and resided here. They would take the flowers and sell them, and the villagers would buy them.

**90.** “As a result, this beautiful place was named Śrī Champaka Haṭṭa, and people began to call it Chā̐pāhāṭi.

**91.** “When Lakṣmaṇ Sen was the king of Nadia, Jayadev Goswāmī was one of His subjects in Nabadwīp.

**92.** “The wise Jayadev built a cottage on the bank of Ballāl Dīrghikā and resided there with his wife Padmāvatī.

**93.** “While there, he wrote the *Daśa-avatāra-stotram*, and this poem once came to the hand of Lakṣmaṇ Sen.

**94.** “The king read the poem with great joy and then asked, ‘Who wrote this?’

**95.** “Govardhan Āchārya said to the king, ‘The great poet Jayadev wrote it.’

**96.** “The king asked, ‘Where is this poet Jayadev?’ Govardhan replied, ‘He lives in Nabadwīp.’

**97.** “Hearing this, the king secretly searched for Jayadev’s home and then came there in the evening.

**98.** “The king entered the cottage in the dress of a Vaiṣṇava. He bowed to Jayadev and sat on one side.

**99.** “Jayadev understood that this man was the king and that he had come to him as a beggar in the dress of a Vaiṣṇava.

**100.** “Within a short time, the king introduced himself and requested Jayadev to go to his palace.

**101.** “The wise Jayadev was extremely detached, and he did not agree to go to the home of any materialist.

**102.** “Jayadev, the great devotee of Kṛṣṇa, then said, ‘I will leave your province and go elsewhere.

**103.** “‘Association with materialists never results in good fortune, so I will cross the Gaṅgā and go to Nīlāchal.’

**104.** “The king said, ‘O master, listen to my words. Please do not ever leave Nabadwīp.

**105.** “‘Please be merciful to me and act in such a way that your words will come true and my desire will also be fulfilled.

**106.** “‘On the other side of the Gaṅgā, there is a beautiful place known as Champā Haṭṭa. Please stay there for a couple of years.

**107.** “‘I will not go there according to my own desire; I will come to see your feet only when you desire it.’

**108.** “Hearing the words of the king, the great poet immediately replied in agreement.

**109.** “‘Although you are a materialist and this kingdom is yours, you are a devotee of Kṛṣṇa and you have no material attachment.

**110.** “‘I called you a materialist to test you, but you heard this and tolerated it.

**111.** “‘I have now understood that you are a devotee of Kṛṣṇa. You engage in material affairs but remain detached.

**112.** “‘I will stay in Champaka Haṭṭa for some time. In secret, leaving your opulence behind, you may come to see me.’

**113.** “Through one of his ministers, the king happily had a house constructed in Champaka Haṭṭa.

**114.** “The poet Jayadev stayed there for some time and served Śrī Kṛṣṇa according to the path of divine love.

**115.** “Padmāvatī Devī would bring loads of champak flowers, and Jayadev worshipped Kṛṣṇa, the son of Nanda, with them.

**116.** “Jayadev offered worship with intense love, and eventually he saw Śrī Kṛṣṇa appear before him in a form the colour of a champak flower.

**117.** “The Lord’s beautiful, golden lustre was completely enchanting, and His extremely beautiful face belittled millions of moons.

**118.** “He had wavy hair, long arms, and a beautiful flower garland around His neck. His body illumined the thatched hut of Jayadev.

**119.** “Seeing the form of Gaurāṅga, Jayadev, the best of poets, fainted in divine love as tears streamed from his eyes.

**120.** “Seeing the Lord’s form, Padmāvatī Devī fainted and fell to the ground.

**121.** “The Lord raised them both with His lotus hands and then mercifully spoke nectarean words.

**122.** “‘You are both My highly exalted devotees, and I desired to reveal Myself to you.

**123.** “‘Very soon I will take birth from the womb of Śachī Devī in Nadia.

**124.** “‘With all the devotees of all My previous Avatārs, I will distribute the wealth of divine love through Śrī Kṛṣṇa-kīrtan.

**125.** “‘At the age of twenty-four, I will take sannyās and then reside in Nīlāchal.

**126.** “‘There, immersed in intense divine love, I will deeply relish your *Śrī Gīta-govinda* with My devotees.

**127.** “‘I am telling you the truth: your *Gīta-govinda* is extremely dear to Me.

**128.** “‘Nabadwīp Dhām is spiritual and supreme; I promise you that you will come here after leaving your body.

**129.** “‘For now, you two will go to Nīlāchal. Serve Jagannāth there, and you will attain the fruit of divine love.’

**130.** “Saying this, Gaurachandra disappeared. In separation from the Lord, Jayadev and Padmāvatī fainted.

**131.** “Regaining consciousness, they began to cry profusely. Crying and crying, they offered prayers.

**132.** “‘Alas! What a form we have seen! How will we now live in separation from Him?

**133.** “‘Why did the Lord order us to leave Nadia? We understand that we must have made some offence here.

**134.** “‘Nabadwīp Dhām is spiritual and supreme, and our hearts are dismayed at the thought of leaving.

**135.** “‘It would be better to become an animal or bird and remain here forever, meditating on the Dhām.

**136.** “‘We can give up our lives, but we cannot give up the Dhām—our attachment is so intense.

**137.** “‘O Lord! Śrī Gaurāṅga! Be merciful and keep us here at Your holy feet.’

**138.** “While speaking in this way, they cried aloud, and then they heard a divine voice.

**139.** “‘Go to Nīlāchal, and do not be sad or unsteady at heart. I will tell you two things.

**140.** “‘Some time ago, you both desired to stay in Nīlāchal for a few days.

**141.** “‘Jagannāth has fulfilled this desire of yours, and He wants to see you.

**142.** “‘You two will please Jagannāth, again leave your bodies, and then reside in Nabadwīp eternally.’

**143.** “Upon hearing the divine voice, Jayadev and Padmāvatī immediately left, and after some time, they turned and looked back at Nabadwīp.

**144.** “They wept, and tears streamed from their eyes. Humbly, they spoke to the residents of Nabadwīp.

**145.** “‘Please be merciful to these two souls. Please forgive us for the offences we have committed.’

**146.** “They walked away, looking back again and again at the eight-petalled lotus of Nabadwīp.

**147.** “They went ahead and eventually did not see Nabadwīp anymore. Crying, they crossed outside the land of Gauḍa.

**148.** “Some time later, they arrived in Nīlāchal and saw Jagannāth with cheerful hearts.

**149.** “O Jīva, this is the place of Jayadev. The elders say that only this raised land is what remains of it.”

**150.** Seeing the place of Jayadev, Śrī Jīva rolled on the ground and wept in divine love.

**151.** “Glory to the poet Jayadev! Glory to Padmāvatī! Glory to *Śrī Gīta- govinda*! Glory to love for Kṛṣṇa!

**152.** “O Lord, mercifully give me a drop of the ocean of divine love that Jayadev relished.”

**153.** Saying this, Jīva tumbled to the ground and rolled at the holy feet of Nityānanda Prabhu.

**154.** That night they stayed at the house of Vāṇīnāth, and he and his family served Nityānanda Prabhu.

**155.** This poor, fallen soul, whose aspiration is the shade of Nitāi and Jāhnavā’s feet, chants the glories of Nadia.
