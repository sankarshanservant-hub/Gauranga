# Vilasa 11. Virachandra in Kheturi; the Departure of Srinivasa, Ramachandra and Narottama

*Virachandra visits Srinivasa at Yajigrama and goes with him to Kheturi; kirtana with Virachandra's dancing; Srinivasa and Ramachandra go to Vrindavana and pass from sight; Narottama's lament; his last days and his departure in the Ganga at Gambhila*

**1.** Glory to Gaura, Nityananda and Advaita with their associates!
Be merciful, Prabhu, to this wretched beggar.

**2.** Glory, glory to the listeners, oceans of compassion!
Hear what I shall now relate.

**3.** Kaviraja Thakura and Thakura Mahashaya
wrote two letters with all the news

**4.** and, together with a list of the songs composed by Sri Govinda,
sent them with great care to Vrindavana.

**5.** Hearing of the welfare there, they rejoiced
and sent all this news to Yajigrama.

**6.** In Yajigrama the Acharya with his people
rejoiced unceasingly in discussing the scriptures of bhakti.

**7.** How Sri Narottama gives bhakti to the poor and wretched,
how in his kindness he delivers robbers and pashandis —

**8.** hearing all this, what bliss swelled
in the Acharya's heart — who could tell?

**9.** As soon as he resolved to go quickly to Kheturi,
various auspicious omens appeared at once.

**10.** Someone came and said, "Virabhadra has come here."
The Acharya rejoiced to hear of his coming.

**11.** Going out, they saw that he was already near the village;
seeing him, all were overjoyed.

**12.** Prabhu Virabhadra, seeing Acharya Thakura,
quickly stepped down from his palanquin.

**13.** The Acharya with his people bowed to the ground,
and Prabhu Virabhadra embraced him with great affection.

**14.** Asking after his welfare with great joy in his heart,
he took the Acharya by the hand and walked slowly on.

**15.** The Acharya asked with great respect,
"From where have you suddenly come?"

**16.** Prabhu said, "In Khardaha I decided
to go to the village of Kheturi by way of Yajigrama.

**17.** With my people I went round Nadia and other places
and came here quickly by way of Srikhanda."

**18.** So saying, he entered the house, and at his place
Prabhu Virabhadra sat on his seat of honour.

**19.** There was great bliss at Prabhu's coming —
as if the master of the house had come home.

**20.** Draupadi Ishvari and Sri Gauranga-priya,
both wives of the Acharya, came and bowed down.

**21.** Bringing cool water, with jubilant hearts
they washed the feet of Prabhu Virabhadra.

**22.** The Acharya's eldest son, very wise,
to whom Sri Jiva Gosvami had given the name Vrindavana,

**23.** Radha-Krishna and Sri Gati-Govinda — these three
fell at the feet of Prabhu Virachandra.[^1]

**24.** Prabhu blessed the three boys
and placed his holy feet on their heads.

**25.** The Acharya's three daughters, devoted to bhakti and prema —
Hemalata, Krishnapriya and Sri Kanchanalata —

**26.** all three bowed at the feet of Prabhu Virachandra,
and Prabhu blessed them with fatherly affection.

**27.** The men and women of the village came for darshana
and all bowed at the feet of Virachandra.

**28.** Prabhu asked each one after his welfare,
and all, with soft words, surrendered themselves to him.

**29.** Having stayed there a while, Prabhu
went with his people in the highest bliss to bathe.

**30.** Meanwhile the Acharya's wife, bathing quickly,
began to cook — how, I cannot say.

**31.** She prepared greens and other dishes, boiled and fried,
sweet rice, shikharini and much else,

**32.** carefully cooked fragrant rice
and, pouring fresh ghee over it, set it on a plate.

**33.** A very wise disciple of the Acharya
offered the meal to Shalagrama-chandra.

**34.** The Govardhana-shila given by Prabhu Nityananda,
whom Prabhu Virabhadra serves, was also with him:

**35.** to Him too the meal was joyfully offered,
and the Two partook together with great love.

**36.** The two Thakuranis set out the offering —
what wondrous beauty it was, I cannot say.

**37.** The Govardhana-shila and Sri Vamshivadana[^2]
partook, and the pujari gave Them water to rinse Their mouths,

**38.** carefully offered betel
and laid Them on a wonderful bed.

**39.** Meanwhile all, having bathed and so on, went with Prabhu
and sat down to eat in a lovely courtyard.

**40.** Prabhu Virabhadra said to Sri Acharya,
"Sit down to eat and take everyone with you."

**41.** Acharya Thakura said, "That frightens me:
it is fitting that I eat last."

**42.** Hearing this, Prabhu took the Acharya's hand and laughed,
saying, "This is what is fitting: sit beside me."

**43.** Acharya Thakura could not transgress the order
and sat with everyone at Prabhu's command.

**44.** The very wise companions of Prabhu Virabhadra —
all their minds rejoiced.

**45.** How wonderfully the circle of Vaishnavas shone!
Who, seeing Prabhu Virabhadra, could remain composed?

**46.** All took lovely banana leaves,
and Prabhu ordered the food to be served.

**47.** The Acharya's two wives, embodiments of bhakti, devoted to their husband,
served with great bliss.

**48.** Sridasa, Gokulananda and Vyasa — these three
set out the various dishes on lovely plates.

**49.** Placing in rows sweet water, fried delicacies and the rest,
they sat down to honour Sri prasada.

**50.** Virabhadra, first tasting a little of this,
said with a smile, "Today it is just like Vraja!"

**51.** Then he partook of the sweetest boiled and fried dishes,
tasting the greens and the rest, in great bliss.

**52.** With great merriment all ate,
rinsed their mouths and chewed betel.

**53.** After resting a little, in rapturous bliss
they passed the day and night relishing talks of Krishna.

**54.** In the morning Sri Ramachandra and the Acharya
set out with great jubilation in their hearts.

**55.** All the dear companions of Prabhu Virabhadra
set out with joy in their hearts.

**56.** The Acharya's disciples, with blissful hearts,
went some alongside, some ahead.

**57.** By way of Kantaka-nagara they came to Budhari;
Govinda and others, hearing beforehand, came out to meet them.

**58.** On the road there was a wonderful meeting with all,
and Govinda joyfully brought them to his house.

**59.** Prabhu Virachandra, with great joy in his heart,
stayed with his people in a lovely lodging.

**60.** Acharya Thakura with his people was there too;
there was no limit to everyone's delight in one another.

**61.** What festivity of feasting and so on there was —
I cannot describe it for fear of prolixity.

**62.** For two days they stayed in the village of Budhari,
and a great many Vaishnavas came there to meet them.

**63.** Crossing the Padma with everyone and bathing,
Prabhu went on to Kheturi with a joyful heart.

**64.** Hearing beforehand of his coming, Mahashaya
had various provisions prepared:

**65.** curd, milk, chhana, mangoes and other fruits,
mango and other pickles — all was made ready.

**66.** Having the lodgings cleaned, Mahashaya
went out with his people and watched the road from afar.

**67.** As if moons had risen to dispel heat and darkness —
so, seeing them from afar, he refreshed his eyes.

**68.** Approaching with great joy in his heart,
he bowed at the feet of Prabhu Virabhadra.

**69.** Prabhu Virachandra, embracing Narottama,
lost his composure and could not restrain his heart.

**70.** Narottama, bathed in the tears from his eyes,
fell again and again at his feet, rolling on the ground.

**71.** What the meeting of all with one another was like —
not even a small part could be told with one mouth.

**72.** Acharya Thakura and Sri Thakura Mahashaya
brought Prabhu into the house of Gauranga.

**73.** Gauranga, Vallavikanta, Sri Vrajamohana,
Radha-Krishna, Radhakanta, Sri Radharamana —

**74.** Virachandra, seeing them all,
lost his composure, and tears flowed from his eyes.

**75.** He bowed again and again, falling to the ground;
what was rising in his heart — who knows?

**76.** After a while Prabhu collected himself,
and the pujari carefully gave him the prasada garland.

**77.** Acharya Thakura and Mahashaya with care
led him to the lodging where Ishvari had stayed before.

**78.** Meanwhile all the Vaishnavas, overwhelmed by the darshana,
checked their tears and grew calm.

**79.** The pujari gave everyone prasada garlands,
and with jubilant hearts they went to Prabhu.

**80.** People from Sri Kheturi and other villages
came running from all sides for darshana.

**81.** Having seen him, all went home
and said to one another in sweet words:

**82.** "The world-enchanting Nityananda-Balarama —
his son is Prabhu Virabhadra, abode of virtues."

**83.** Someone said, "Blessed is our life:
without effort we have obtained a rare darshana."

**84.** Someone said, "Thanks to Sri Thakura Mahashaya
our desires have been fulfilled in the village of Kheturi."

**85.** So the people said much in rapturous bliss;
the news of Virachandra's coming spread in all directions.

**86.** Meanwhile Prabhu Virachandra in his lovely lodging
sat down with everyone with a blissful heart.

**87.** Acharya Thakura, smiling gently,
spoke sweet words to Prabhu Virachandra:

**88.** "Today you are to eat fried delicacies here —
all was prepared beforehand as soon as your coming was heard."

**89.** Prabhu Virachandra took from his casket
the Govardhana-shila to offer Him food.

**90.** Various dishes were carefully brought to Him;
removing the offering, the shila was put back in the casket.

**91.** From the holy temple various prasada was brought;
all was ready, and he was respectfully informed.

**92.** Hearing the Acharya's words, the Gosani said,
"I am hungry; there is no need to delay."

**93.** So saying, he sat down with everyone in the courtyard;
the fortunate saw a wonderful beauty.

**94.** Harirama, Ramakrishna, Ganganarayana,
Sri Govinda Chakravarti and a few others,

**95.** carefully taking various fried delicacies,
served with the highest bliss in their hearts.

**96.** Mangoes, jackfruit, pomegranates and various fruits,
curd, milk, chhana, sugar, drinks — all

**97.** they served in order and merrily watched the beauty;
Prabhu with the Acharya and all the others ate joyfully.

**98.** Puris, pupas, laddus and other most charming things —
relishing taste after taste, the meal was abundant.

**99.** Rinsing his mouth, Prabhu sat on his seat
and joyfully chewed prasada betel.

**100.** Afterwards countless people ate;
I cannot describe all this in detail.

**101.** The Acharya and Thakura Mahashaya with their companions
were immersed in bliss beside Prabhu Virachandra.

**102.** Drinking the nectar of the deeds of Radha-Krishna and Chaitanya,
in what happiness they passed the day and night without sleep!

**103.** In the morning all performed their morning duties and bathed,
and Sri Santosha came to Prabhu Virachandra.

**104.** He dressed him in new garments of the finest cloth —
the sight of Prabhu's beauty refreshed the eyes.

**105.** Humbly entreating the Vaishnavas who were with him,
he dressed them too in new garments, his heart rejoicing.

**106.** Before Prabhu he arranged a wonderful seat;
sitting on it, Prabhu performed the service of the Govardhana-shila.

**107.** Adorning Him with flowers, tulasi and sandal paste,
he at once offered Him various foods.

**108.** Removing the offering, he bowed down many times
and distributed the prasada to everyone.

**109.** Prabhu Virachandra's cooks
very quickly prepared a wonderful meal.

**110.** It was offered to the Govardhana-shila;
removing the offering, the Shila was placed in a golden casket.

**111.** Having seen the arati of Sri Gaurachandra,
Prabhu joyfully took prasada with everyone.

**112.** Chewing betel, he rested,
and after a while sat down with everyone.

**113.** Prabhu Virachandra said to the Acharya,
"I wish to hear sankirtana."

**114.** The Acharya replied, "You fulfil all desires;
I know that now our desire too will be fulfilled."

**115.** With a jubilant heart Sri Thakura Mahashaya
said to all, "Let us not delay!"

**116.** Sri Santosha Raya had everything prepared,
and all heard that sankirtana was about to begin.

**117.** People came running from every direction
and surrounded the courtyard on all four sides.

**118.** In the afternoon Virachandra with everyone
came from his lodging to Gauranga's courtyard.

**119.** He saw the arati of the Lord's rising;
the pujari brought and gave him a garland and sandal paste.

**120.** The Acharya's heart was filled with great joy:
he drew lovely designs in sandal paste on Prabhu's body

**121.** and put various flower garlands round Prabhu's neck;
seeing his wondrous beauty, he was bathed in tears.

**122.** Mahashaya with the singers and musicians
joyfully began the sankirtana.

**123.** Gokula showered nectar singing the alapa of the raga;
Devidasa played the khol in wonderful rhythms.

**124.** The sound of khol and kartals and the turns of the alapa
pierced the sky, astonishing the world of the gods.

**125.** At Sri Mahashaya's auspicious voice
the ocean of bliss surged, and all lost their composure.

**126.** All around, a lovely circle of Vaishnavas,
and in the midst shines Prabhu Virachandra in his beauty.

**127.** His body glitters, surpassing gold,
and his sweet gait shatters Kamadeva's pride.

**128.** Absorbed in great prema he dances,
raising his long arms that reach his knees, and whirls on every side.

**129.** On his broad chest sway garlands of flowers;
waves of horripilation run over him without cease.

**130.** With his lovely lips he cries, "Hari! Hari!"
and his large eyes overflow with tears.

**131.** His lovely lotus feet move light and quick,
and the earth rejoices at their fresh touch.

**132.** He dances in the kirtana a dance that enchants the world,
and the gods in joy shower him with flowers.

**133.** Gandharvas and kinnaras, assuming human form,
watch unblinking the sweetness of his dance.

**134.** Prabhu Virachandra wished
to dance together with everyone, and looked around.

**135.** At that moment Sri Acharya and Mahashaya
began to dance with their companions, full of the bliss of prema.

**136.** What a wondrous dance, a blessing to the world!
The earth trembles under the weight of their steps.

**137.** Song, dance and music, new at every moment —
there is nothing to compare them with in the three worlds.

**138.** All forgot themselves;
on all sides rose mighty roars and shouts.

**139.** Some struck heroic poses, some leapt,
some trembled in body like lightning.

**140.** Some wept, falling at Virachandra's feet,
some rolled on the ground, unable to contain themselves.

**141.** Prabhu Virachandra was utterly overcome:
his body, grey with dust, swayed.

**142.** Again and again Prabhu let out a great lion's roar;
embracing Narottama, he could not let him go.

**143.** Taking Sri Devidasa's hands, he pressed them to his chest
and, with tears streaming from his eyes, said, "What wondrous playing!"

**144.** Stroking Gokula's face with his hand,
he said many things to him, beside himself.

**145.** Taking Sri Govinda Kaviraja by both hands,
he said, "I would die for your poetry![^3]

**146.** You indeed know the glory of Nityananda:
this is the full measure of the Acharya's grace to you."

**147.** So saying, he urged Gokula again and again,
"Sing, sing, oh, refresh my soul!"

**148.** Hearing this, Gokula sang in delight —
oh, what a wondrous song of the Kaviraja!

**149.** *Song:*
*"Glory to Nityananda, the abode and cause of the world's deliverance!*
*The name of Nityananda is the root of bliss! (Refrain.)*
*His lotus eyes, brimming with rapture, roll;*
*his gait, naturally unsteady, surpasses that of a drunkard.*
*'Brother Abhirama!' He calls out again and again,*
*and under the weight of His prema for Gaura He cannot walk."*[^4]

**150.** Sri Gokuladasa sang in wonderful rhythms,
and all the people floated in the flood of prema.

**151.** The wonders that happened in the sankirtana —
who has the power to describe them in full?

**152.** On all sides the clamour of "Hari! Hari!"
pierced the sky and filled the whole earth.

**153.** Hundreds of lamps burned, lovely to see;
in the sankirtana the third watch of the night arrived.

**154.** All sat calmly in the Lord's courtyard,
and in talks of Krishna the morning came.

**155.** Having done their morning duties and bathed,
all came to Prabhu Virachandra's lodging.

**156.** Prabhu Vira, having served the Govardhana-shila,
could not grow calm in that blissful rapture.

**157.** Again and again Prabhu asked Ramachandra,
"Tell me something of the rasa-lila."

**158.** Ramachandra's voice is a stream of nectar:
he wonderfully explained verses of the *Bhagavata*.

**159.** Hearing this, Virachandra was overjoyed
and embraced Ramachandra again and again.

**160.** After a while, collecting himself,
Prabhu Virachandra spoke sweetly to the Acharya:

**161.** "Will such rare company ever be again?" —
and as soon as he said this, tears streamed from his eyes.

**162.** The Acharya and all the others too were bathed in tears;
by Prabhu's wish all grew calm.

**163.** Sri Rupa Ghataka, Ganganarayana,
Shyamadasa, Govinda and other bhagavatas

**164.** quickly prepared, as Prabhu wished,
wonderful fried sweets, mangoes, jackfruit and the like.

**165.** These were carefully placed before the Govardhana-shila,
and Prabhu Virachandra himself made the offering.

**166.** At the proper time Prabhu removed the offering,
offered betel and placed the Shila in its casket.

**167.** Having seen Gauranga, he took prasada
with everyone with the greatest delight.

**168.** Happily chewing prasada betel,
he rested a while with everyone.

**169.** Thus the son of Prabhu Nityanandachandra
sported in great joy with his dear companions.

**170.** One day Prabhu said to the Acharya,
"I shall go to Khardaha by way of Ekachakra.

**171.** I have decided to set out tomorrow morning;
part of the way I shall go with you."

**172.** The Acharya replied, "What you have decided —
who has the power to set it aside?"

**173.** Prabhu Virachandra smiled and said softly,
"I cannot transgress your words.

**174.** I have said what came to my mind;
consider it and act as you wish."

**175.** Acharya Thakura went and told Narottama,
"Tomorrow Prabhu departs, and I with him."

**176.** Hearing this, Mahashaya was greatly distressed,
and Acharya Thakura comforted him with great care.

**177.** What else the two discussed in private —
others cannot understand.

**178.** After staying there a while, the Acharya returned to Prabhu,
and Santosha prepared everything for the journey.

**179.** All that was to go with Prabhu Virachandra
he entrusted to Thakura Kanai,

**180.** and all that Acharya Thakura needed on the way
he entrusted to Rupa Ghataka.

**181.** He quickly sent men to the village of Budhari
and had many boats kept ready on the bank of the Padmavati.

**182.** From Kheturi the news spread everywhere:
"In the morning Prabhu sets out."

**183.** People everywhere talked with one another,
and day and night there was no end of people coming and going.

**184.** Virachandra Raya, together with Srinivasa Acharya,
went to Gauranga's courtyard and took leave.

**185.** Returning to his lodging, he sat a while;
everyone gathered there.

**186.** They went quickly to the bank of the Padmavati;
no one could keep his composure.

**187.** Sri Mahashaya's disciples, like paupers,
bowed at the feet of Prabhu Virachandra

**188.** and bowed many times at the Acharya's feet;
both showed mercy to all.

**189.** Ramachandra, after saying much to Sri Mahashaya,
took leave of him for some days.

**190.** Harirama, Ramakrishna, Ganganarayana,
Sri Govinda Chakravarti, Sri Gopiramana,

**191.** Balarama Kaviraja and some others
the Acharya left with Mahashaya.

**192.** To all who had come from the village of Kheturi
he spoke many consoling words.

**193.** Acharya Thakura with Prabhu Virachandra
boarded the boat — and all lost their composure.

**194.** Ramachandra and all the others boarded the boats,
and the helmsmen quickly cast off.

**195.** A sound of weeping rose on the bank of the Padmavati —
hearing it, wood and stone would split.

**196.** The Acharya with his companions, taking Sri Virachandra,
crossed the Padma and came to the village of Budhari.

**197.** Here Mahashaya, greatly agitated,
came with everyone to Gauranga's abode.

**198.** Gauranga, Vallavikanta, Sri Vrajamohana,
Radhakanta, Radha-Krishna and Sri Radharamana —

**199.** at their darshana everyone's heart rejoiced;
very quickly all bathed and did the rest,

**200.** Mahashaya took prasada with everyone,
and day and night passed in the relish of talks of Krishna.

**201.** From that day Mahashaya became like this:
now perfectly calm, now with his heart in turmoil.

**202.** So some days passed,
and Ramachandra came back from Yajigrama.

**203.** Ramachandra's comings and goings
I have described at length in the book *Bhakti-ratnakara*.

**204.** Ramachandra's coming delighted Mahashaya,
and everyone's heart grew very glad.

**205.** In Gaurachandra's courtyard with Govinda and others
Mahashaya was day and night intoxicated with sankirtana.

**206.** King Narasimha, Chand Raya and all the rest
went mad with the relish of sankirtana.

**207.** Some days later Sri Thakura Mahashaya
said to Sri Govinda Chakravarti and all the others,

**208.** "It is long since you went home;
all of you go quickly, at least once."

**209.** Though no one wished to go,
they went nonetheless, fearing to disobey the order.

**210.** How Sri Govinda Chakravarti, Ganganarayana,
Harirama, Ramakrishna, Sri Gopiramana,

**211.** Balarama Kaviraja and the rest departed —
this I cannot describe.

**212.** Sri Thakura Mahashaya with Ramachandra
spent some days in the greatest bliss.

**213.** One day, sitting in complete seclusion,
the two took counsel together — who knows about what?

**214.** Some days later Ramachandra Kaviraja,
in great anguish, went to Yajigrama.

**215.** After a time news came from there:
Sri Acharya Thakura had gone to Vrindavana.

**216.** Ramachandra Kaviraja was constantly with him —
who can understand the hearts of these two?[^5]

**217.** One day Mahashaya could not grow calm
and, weeping, kept saying, "What has happened?"

**218.** *Tripadi:*
*"Gauranga's companions — Srivasa, Gadadhara,*
*Narahari, Mukunda, Murari,*
*Sri Svarupa Damodara, Haridasa, Vakreshvara —*
*all are worthy of prema.*
*The pastimes they performed — at hearing of them stones melt —*
*I was not granted to see.*
*I was not born then, I did not grasp their inner meaning,*
*and this arrow has remained lodged in my heart.*
*Prabhu Sanatana, Rupa, Raghunatha and both Bhattas,*
*Bhugarbha, Sri Jiva, Lokanatha —*
*all these lords together with the devotees*
*what sweet pastimes they enacted in Vrindavana!*
*The three worlds have become empty: all have passed from sight,*
*and my eyes have gone blind.*
*To whom shall I tell my grief? I cannot show my wretched face;*
*I live like a dead beast or bird.*
*Acharya Sri Srinivasa, with whom I was,*
*whose words refreshed my soul —*
*he has gone, leaving me; Ramachandra has not come back —*
*my life is restless with grief.*
*The pain of my heart — to whom shall I tell it?*
*There is no hope in this wretched life.*
*I take food and water like poison, and yet I do not die —*
*shame, shame on Narottama Dasa!"*[^6]

**219.** As soon as he said this, all heard
that Ramachandra Kaviraja had passed from sight.

**220.** Sri Thakura Mahashaya could not grow calm:
going into a lonely forest, he wept aloud:

**221.** "O Ramachandra, where have you gone, leaving me?" —
and saying this, his throat choked, he lay on the ground.

**222.** King Narasimha, Rupanarayana,
King Sri Govinda, Santosha and others,

**223.** watching from afar, were drenched in tears:
Mahashaya lay on the ground.

**224.** Surrounding him on all sides, all wept;
at last Mahashaya came to himself.

**225.** With everyone he came to Gauranga's courtyard
and, seeing the Lord, grew somewhat calm.

**226.** Having stayed thus five or seven days, Mahashaya
said to all, "I shall go to bathe in the Ganga."

**227.** Charging everyone to be careful in the Lord's service,
he came quickly to Budhari with a few companions.

**228.** From there he came to Gambhila on the bank of the Ganga,
and suddenly fever seized his body.

**229.** Giving the order, "Quickly prepare a funeral pyre!" —
Mahashaya fell silent.

**230.** His disciples were thrown into utmost distress;
Ganganarayana calmed them all.

**231.** Brahmanas and pandits come with their people —
Mahashaya only looks, speaking with no one.

**232.** So Mahashaya passed three days
and, in the eyes of people, separated from his body.[^7]

**233.** At once they bathed Mahashaya
and placed him on the pyre, on a lovely seat.

**234.** The brahmanas said to one another with pleasure,
"He made brahmanas his disciples — this is the reward!

**235.** Ganganarayana the brahmana said not a word,
and Narottama Dasa died with his speech taken away.[^8]

**236.** Ganganarayana, such a pandit,
became his disciple, abandoning his own dharma —

**237.** he has seen what became of his guru;
who knows what will become of him!"

**238.** Again and again, so that Ganganarayana would hear,
they all talked like this, laughing.

**239.** The words of the unbelievers awoke compassion in him,
and Ganganarayana came up to the pyre.

**240.** Joining his palms, he said again and again,
"Prabhu, by your own goodness you delivered unbelievers.

**241.** Now these unbelievers, not knowing the inner truth,
revile you — all are pained to hear it.

**242.** Their lot is to go to a terrible hell;
save them, looking on them with a merciful glance."

**243.** At these anguished words of Ganganarayana,
Mahashaya at that very moment returned to his body.

**244.** Saying, "Radha-Krishna! Chaitanya!" Narottama
rose from the pyre, radiant like the sun.

**245.** On all sides everyone cried the name of Hari,
and suddenly the gods showered flowers.

**246.** The reviling brahmanas, who had watched it all from afar,
were seized with great fear — none could grow calm.

**247.** They said to one another, "What have we done!
Ruining ourselves, we reviled such a person!"

**248.** Saying this, they struck their heads with their hands;
their hearts trembled, and tears fell from their eyes.

**249.** Conscious of their offence, the reviling brahmanas
fell in obeisance at Ganganarayana's feet

**250.** and said piteously, "Save us!
We have wasted our lives in the pride of being brahmanas.

**251.** We dare not go before Sri Mahashaya —
be kind and obtain his grace for us."

**252.** Hearing their anguished words, Ganganarayana
went at once to Mahashaya.

**253.** Joining his palms, he said softly,
"Prabhu, show mercy to these brahmanas."

**254.** At this the brahmanas fell to the ground,
bowed down and, joining their palms, said piteously:

**255.** "There are no brahmanas lower than we;
the blasphemy we uttered is beyond counting.

**256.** In false pride, thinking we are highest of the varnas,
we took you for an ordinary man.

**257.** All that we studied has come to nothing:
not a drop of rare bhakti ever touched us.

**258.** Be merciful, destroy our ill fate;
we have taken refuge at your feet."

**259.** Seeing their distress, Sri Thakura Mahashaya
gave them the jewel of bhakti and embraced them.

**260.** He ordered them all, "Study carefully
the books of bhakti with Ganganarayana.

**261.** In a few days all of you come to Kheturi;
today I go from here to Budhari."

**262.** So saying, he quickly bathed in the Ganga —
the fortunate saw it to their hearts' content.

**263.** The news of all this concerning Sri Mahashaya
spread everywhere, and it was a blessing to all.

**264.** From the bank of the Ganga Mahashaya with everyone
soon went to Ganganarayana's house.

**265.** There he ate various sweets with everyone
and very soon came joyfully to Budhari.

**266.** With Sri Govinda Kaviraja, Karnapura,
Gokula Kaviraja and Ballavi Majumdar

**267.** he went to the village of Kheturi
and remained constantly in talks of Krishna.

**268.** To all the service and care of the Deities
he devoted himself without pause.

**269.** Grey with the dust of Gauranga's courtyard,
he weeps, gazing at the Lord's face:

**270.** "Ha, ha, Prabhu Gauranga, Vallavikanta Krishna!
Be merciful: I thirst for worldly things.

**271.** O Prabhu Radhakanta, Sri Vrajamohana,
free me from the torments of samsara.

**272.** O Radharamana, keep me at your feet,
that I may not forget you in life or in death!"

**273.** Thus he made all kinds of entreaties,
and hearing them, beasts and birds wept.

**274.** Seeing the crowd, Prabhu[^9] would go into solitude
and in great agitation call out the names:

**275.** "O Moon of Navadvipa, beautiful Gauranga!
O Nityananda, son of Padmavati!

**276.** O Lord of Sita, merciful Sri Advaita!
O Sri Pandita Gadadhara, full of prema!

**277.** O ocean of mercy, Pandita Srivasa!
O Vakreshvara, Sri Murari, Haridasa!

**278.** O Sri Svarupa Damodara, Ramananda!
O Sri Acharya Gopinatha, Kashishvara!

**279.** O Vachaspati, Sarvabhauma Bhattacharya!
O Suryadasa, Gauridasa Pandita!

**280.** O Sri Pandita Jagadisha, Shuklambara!
O Sri Govinda Ghosha, Gadadhara Dasa!

**281.** O Pundarika Vidyanidhi Mahashaya,
Mukunda, Madhava, Vasu Ghosha, Dhananjaya!

**282.** O Sri Jagadananda, Sanjaya, Sridhara!
O Sri Mukunda, most learned Narahari!

**283.** O Srimad Rupa and Sanatana, oceans of virtue!
O Sri Bhugarbha, Lokanatha, friends of the lowly!

**284.** O Sri Gopala Bhatta, life of the fallen!
O Raghunatha Bhatta, treasury of virtues!

**285.** O Raghunatha of Svarupa, who dwells by the Kunda![^10]
O Jiva Gosvami, cast your glance on me!

**286.** O dear associates of Gaura, Nityananda and Advaita,
be merciful — I have taken refuge in you.

**287.** Seeing that I am a great sinner, do not reject me —
fulfil my desire without fail!"

**288.** Saying this, he could not grow calm
and lamented again, "Be merciful, O Lalita!

**289.** Sri Vishakha, Suchitra, Sri Champakalatika,
Rangadevi, Sudevi, endowed with the highest virtues,

**290.** Tungavidya, Indulekha, most skilful sakhis;
Sri Rupa-manjari, Rati-manjari, Kasturi,

**291.** Lavanga-manjari, Manjulali — all of you,
keep me in the service of Sri Radhika's feet!

**292.** O Radhika, Krishna is the lord of your life;
grant me ceaseless service of His lotus feet.

**293.** I shall seat You both on a jewelled throne
and gaze my fill at You surrounded by the sakhis.

**294.** At the sakhis' sign I shall happily fan You with a chamara
and offer betel to Your moon-like faces.

**295.** Will this desire of my heart be fulfilled?" —
saying this, Mahashaya heaves a long sigh.

**296.** For a while Mahashaya keeps silence:
his heart enters the Navadvipa lila.

**297.** Raising both arms, he says again and again,
"Shall I see my fill of the pastimes of Nadia?

**298.** All around, Srivasa and the Lord's other dear ones,
in front, Advaita, purifier of the world,

**299.** Nityananda on the right, Gadadhara on the left,
and in the middle shines the moon of Navadvipa.

**300.** Shall I see Gora Raya thus with His associates?" —
saying this, he floats in the streams of his tears.

**301.** Who can understand Mahashaya's ways?
Day by day his anxiety grew ever stronger.

**302.** Seeing such behaviour of Sri Mahashaya,
Sri Radhavallabha's[^11] heart is thrown into turmoil.

**303.** So all thought within themselves,
and Mahashaya took care to calm his dear ones.

**304.** Who can understand his heart? With his dear ones
he remained always immersed in the sankirtana of the name.

**305.** One day Mahashaya said to his dear ones,
"Why is Ganganarayana delayed?"

**306.** At that moment Ramakrishna and Ganganarayana came,
and with them several of those brahmanas.

**307.** They fell at Sri Mahashaya's feet;
immersed in the taste of bhakti, the brahmanas were bathed in tears.

**308.** Their devotion was boundless;
all bowed down to Sri Thakura Mahashaya.

**309.** Sri Mahashaya lovingly embraces them all;
among them was one brahmana, the foremost of all.

**310.** All told Ganganarayana their wishes,
and he gladly conveyed them to Mahashaya.

**311.** Sri Thakura Mahashaya, showing grace
and seeing their eagerness, accepted some as disciples,

**312.** and others, out of mercy, he had accepted as disciples
by his beloved Ganganarayana.

**313.** All went to Gauranga's courtyard and bowed down;
the pujari brought them mahaprasada.

**314.** Sri Govinda Kaviraja and the other learned men,
seeing the brahmanas' conduct, rejoiced at heart.

**315.** Sri Shankara Bhattacharya and all the other brahmanas
humbly bowed at their feet.

**316.** Sri Santosha, King Narasimha and all the rest
saw that the brahmanas had become true Vaishnavas.

**317.** The next day a great festival was held,
and the brahmanas went mad in the sankirtana.

**318.** All became worthy of prema-bhakti —
I would die for such grace!

**319.** The lovely deeds of Sri Mahashaya are boundless:
he fulfilled all the desires of all.

**320.** One day, very early in the morning, Mahashaya,
in great agitation, was bathed in tears.

**321.** Heaving long sighs like tongues of flame,
he lay a long while on the ground.

**322.** His lotus face withers;
in a faltering voice he says, "Alas, what has happened!

**323.** Alas, alas, fate has turned against me:
shall I ever again find such an abode of virtues?"

**324.** Saying this, Mahashaya fell silent;
hearing it, all wonder, "Who knows what will be?"

**325.** Mahashaya, knowing the hearts of his dear ones,
spoke many consoling words to all.

**326.** Coming to the Lord's courtyard, he took leave
and offered his life at the feet of the Deities.

**327.** Who can understand his heart? In great agitation
he set out for Budhari, taking Govinda and others with him.

**328.** In the village of Budhari he stayed one day;
Sri Govinda Chakravarti and others came there.

**329.** With the sweetest words he consoled them all
and passed the day and night in kirtana of the holy name.

**330.** From Budhari he went quickly to Gambhila,
bathed in the Ganga and sat in the Ganga's water.

**331.** He ordered Ramakrishna and Ganganarayana,
"Both of you, wash my body."

**332.** How could they wash him? As soon as they touched him,
he dissolved like milk into the waters of the Ganga.

**333.** Before everyone's eyes he quickly disappeared —
this is utterly unfathomable: who could understand it?[^12]

**334.** Suddenly the waves of the Ganga surged up,
and seeing this the people were filled with great wonder.

**335.** Seeing Sri Mahashaya's hidden withdrawal,
the gods from heaven showered flowers.

**336.** On all sides rose a great cry of "Hari! Hari!" —
hearing it, no one could keep his composure.

**337.** All sing the virtues of Sri Thakura Narottama:
his fame filled the world; at it stones melt.

**338.** All who had been with Mahashaya
Ganganarayana took to his home.

**339.** Harirama, Ramakrishna and all the others
upheld one another's fortitude.

**340.** With Sri Govinda Kaviraja and all the rest
they at once began preparing a great festival.

**341.** Holding a great festival in the village of Gambhila,
they hurried by way of Budhari to Kheturi.

**342.** There King Narasimha, Rupanarayana,
Krishna Simha, Chand Raya, Sri Gopiramana,

**343.** King Sri Govinda, Santosha and other dear ones
quickly prepared a great festival.

**344.** What a festival took place in the village of Kheturi —
not even with a thousand mouths could I describe it.

**345.** The wonder that occurred at the start of the sankirtana
I cannot describe for fear of lengthening the book.

**346.** Still, I shall tell a little — listen attentively:
in the Lord's courtyard the sankirtana began.

**347.** Devidasa, Gauranga, Gokula and all the others
were maddened by song and music.

**348.** Sri Govinda Chakravarti and some others
began to dance in great intoxication.

**349.** Sri Govinda Kaviraja and others, in ecstasy,
roar and shout and laugh loudly.

**350.** King Narasimha and others roll on the ground;
all around, everyone is drenched in streams of tears.

**351.** The ocean of the taste of sankirtana surged up,
and at that moment all forgot themselves.

**352.** The supernatural lila of guru, Krishna and the Vaishnavas:
all saw Narottama dancing!

**353.** Having danced a while in the bliss of sankirtana,
he passed from sight quite unnoticed.

**354.** Sri Mahashaya's dear ones, full of prema,
were utterly distraught at heart.

**355.** Then, appearing again to all in dreams,
he calmed them with consoling words.

**356.** Who else is as merciful as he?
He cannot bear anyone's sorrow, whether his own people's or others'.

**357.** Who does not pine for the virtues of Sri Thakura Mahashaya?
Hearing of them, wood and stone split.

**358.** Hear all this always with care:
Narahari tells the *Narottama-vilasa*.

*Thus ends the eleventh vilasa of the Narottama-vilasa, called "The Hidden Withdrawal of Srila Narottama".*

[^1]: Srinivasa Acharya's sons: the eldest, Vrindavana (named by Jiva Gosvami), Radha-Krishna and Gati-Govinda; his wives were Draupadi (Ishvari) and Gauranga-priya (Padmavati).
[^2]: That is, Srinivasa's Deity Sri Vamshivadana (Krishna with the flute) and Virachandra's Govardhana-shila.
[^3]: Literally, "I would take on the misfortunes of your poetry and die" — a Bengali expression of utmost love and admiration.
[^4]: A song of Govinda Kaviraja (Govinda Dasa) on Nityananda; the editions give only the first stanza with the refrain. "Brother Abhirama" is Abhirama Thakura (Ramadasa), one of the twelve gopalas, associates of Nityananda.
[^5]: The author does not speak directly of Srinivasa's departure from this world: he says only that the Acharya went to Vrindavana and that Ramachandra was constantly with him; below (11.218) Ramachandra is said to have "passed from sight". For details the author refers to the *Bhakti-ratnakara* (cf. 11.203).
[^6]: A well-known *Prarthana* of Narottama Dasa. Bhugarbha is Bhugarbha Gosvami, a disciple of Gadadhara Pandita; "Raghunatha and both Bhattas" are Raghunatha Dasa Gosvami, Raghunatha Bhatta and Gopala Bhatta Gosvami.
[^7]: "In the eyes of people" (loka-drishtye): the author stresses that the death was only apparent. An eternal associate of the Lord is not subject to death as a conditioned soul is, and he soon (11.243) returns to his body.
[^8]: An obscure line, rendered by sense. Loss of speech before death was popularly taken as a sign of an evil end — this is what the mockers hint at.
[^9]: Here "Prabhu" is Narottama himself.
[^10]: Raghunatha Dasa Gosvami, who lived at Radha-kunda, a disciple of Svarupa Damodara.
[^11]: Probably Radhavallabha Chaudhuri, one of Narottama's disciples (see 12.13); both editions read the same.
[^12]: Narottama's departure at Gambhila — "he dissolved like milk into the waters of the Ganga" — is presented by the author as a hidden withdrawal (sangopana), not an ordinary death. The episode that follows (11.352–353), Narottama's appearance in the kirtana at Kheturi after his departure, is likewise presented as a supernatural lila.
