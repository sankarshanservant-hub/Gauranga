# Vilasa 2. The Birth and Childhood of Narottama; His Departure for Vrindavana

*Narottama's birth in Kheturi; his first feeding and naming; studies and detachment; the brahmana Krishnadasa's accounts of the Lord and of Srinivasa; in a dream the Lord bids Narottama go to Vrindavana; his flight from home*

**1.** Glory to Gaura, Nityananda and Advaita with their associates!
Be merciful, Prabhu, to this wretched beggar.

**2.** Glory, glory to the listeners, oceans of mercy!
Hear what I shall now relate.

**3.** Here, after some time, by Prabhu's will,
Narottama was born to manifest bhakti.

**4.** On the full-moon day of the month of Magha, at the sixth danda,[^1]
every auspicious sign came together at the moment of his appearance.

**5.** The mother's beauty increased most wonderfully;
seeing her son, she shed tears of joy.

**6.** The lying-in chamber shone divinely,
and all who were there could not keep still for joy.

**7.** In the village of Sri Kheturi the highest good came about:
evil-mindedness vanished, people were overwhelmed with joy.

**8.** No sound but "Hari! Hari!" was on their lips,
their bodies thrilled, tears streamed from their eyes.

**9.** Bhakti-devi entered the hearts of all,
and all came running, jostling, to Krishnananda's house.

**10.** Everyone brought various gifts,
and the most discerning Datta received all with honour.

**11.** Looking at his son's face, he could not turn his eyes away —
what wondrous happiness was in Krishnananda's heart!

**12.** Sri Krishnananda's father, a most generous man,
gave away much wealth for his grandson's welfare.

**13.** How he satisfied the singers, musicians,
bards, panegyrists and heralds — who could describe?

**14.** What marvels occurred at the moment of his appearance
I cannot describe here for fear of length.

**15.** Gaura, Nityananda and Advaita with their associates
danced — Narayani saw it before her eyes.

**16.** No woman is as fortunate as Narayani,
from whose womb Thakura Narottama was born.

**17.** Day by day Narottama grew like the moon;
seeing her son's face, the mother was always beside herself.

**18.** Fortunate Krishnananda, having gained a jewel of a son,
fed the brahmanas with care every day.

**19.** Looking at his son's face soothed his eyes and heart;
on an auspicious day he performed the rite of first feeding.

**20.** The festivity there was at the first feeding —
is it possible to tell it with one mouth?

**21.** A most fortunate astrologer was there;
on seeing the child his knowledge became clear.

**22.** At the raja's bidding he examined all the auspicious marks
and said, "The name worthy of him is Narottama."

**23.** Hearing this, the brahmanas said, "So it is, so it is:
he is surely the best among men."[^2]

**24.** What the other men and women said at the naming
I cannot relate in detail.

**25.** What happened at the first feeding
I shall tell, and people will be amazed.

**26.** They carefully put rice to the child's mouth,
but he would not eat, turning his face away.

**27.** They tried in many ways, but he would not take it,
and all became greatly worried.

**28.** The astrologer said: "Do not worry about this:
he will never eat anything but food offered to Vishnu."

**29.** At once they brought rice that was Vishnu's prasada,
put it to the son's mouth, and he ate it with delight.

**30.** From that day the raja told everyone:
"Give him nothing but Krishna's prasada."

**31.** From that very day Krishnananda Datta
himself held rice-prasada of Vishnu to be supreme.

**32.** From of old they had the service of a Deity of Sri Krishna;
now his eagerness for that service grew greatly.

**33.** Thus the first feeding took place;
whoever hears of it has his desires fulfilled.

**34.** Some days later the rite of first tonsure was performed,
and they had him taught grammar and other subjects.

**35.** Whoever taught Narottama any branch of learning
had his own doubts dispelled by Narottama's grace.

**36.** Seeing Sri Narottama's conduct, the learned
spoke quietly to one another of his virtues.

**37.** Some said: "He has descended as a portion of a god —
otherwise could a human being have such power?

**38.** At such a tender age he is well trained in every task
and pleases everyone in every way."

**39.** Some said: "Looking at him for just a moment
we forget all our sorrows, and our eyes are soothed."

**40.** Others said: "The prince is so delicate!
We have never seen another so beautiful in every limb."

**41.** Saying many such things, they praised Krishnananda,
and Krishnananda was immersed in the joy of raising his son.

**42.** Seeing that his son was fit in every way,
he always pondered with great joy at heart:

**43.** "I will have my son married and give him the kingdom,
and, like my father, I shall be free of cares."

**44.** Having resolved this, he told the wise kayasthas
to search for a bride for the marriage.

**45.** Meanwhile Narottama, absorbed in prema, in secret
worshipped Krishna, tears flowing from his eyes.

**46.** A deep mood of detachment was ever in his heart;
he could not bear talk of royal pleasures and the like.

**47.** Seeing his son's detachment at every moment,
Krishnananda Raya was greatly worried.

**48.** Nothing but Narottama pleased him;
so too the mother Narayani — her life was in her son.

**49.** They kept guards by their son at all times,
and still fear dwelt constantly in their hearts.

**50.** Narottama, like a prisoner, thought within himself:
"I see no way — how am I to leave home?"

**51.** Thinking thus, he did not reveal his mind;
"What will happen? Gauranga!" he would cry and sigh.

**52.** Crying "Nitai! Advaita!" he would run in all directions,
rolling on the ground, his body grey with dust.

**53.** Raising his arms he called again and again:
"Prabhu, with Your associates deliver me!"

**54.** Thus every day, finding a very secluded place,
he would weep aloud in great distress.

**55.** The deeds of Gaurachandra had spread throughout the world;
he could not get to hear of them, yet he listened attentively.[^3]

**56.** In the village of Sri Kheturi there was an old brahmana
named Krishnadasa, devoted to Krishna.

**57.** He had fully conquered his senses; everyone stood in awe of him,
and no one dared disobey him.

**58.** He too could not do without Narottama;
having finished his service to Krishna, he would secretly go to see him.

**59.** Narottama, with great respect,
falling to the ground in obeisance, would seat him.

**60.** He would ask about Prabhu and His devotees,
and the brahmana told of each one separately.

**61.** The nectar of Chaitanya's early, middle and final pastimes
he narrated to him in order, attentively, in part.[^4]

**62.** The pastimes of Nityananda and Advaitachandra
he told in prema — hearing them, wood and stone melt.

**63.** Pandita Sri Gadadhara, Pandita Srivasa,
Vakreshvara, Svarupa, Murari, Haridasa,

**64.** Narahari Dasa, Gauridasa, Gadadhara,
Vasu Ghosha, Mukunda, Sanjaya, Damodara,

**65.** Kashishvara, Sri Paramananda Bhattacharya,
Krishnadasa Brahmachari, the excellent Lokanatha,

**66.** Sanatana, Rupa, Sri Gopala, Raghunatha,
Raghunatha Bhatta, Jiva, renowned in the world,

**67.** Subuddhi Mishra, Raghava, Krishna Pandita and others —
the accounts of all of them he told as was fitting.

**68.** In the course of it he spoke of Srinivasa Acharya —
how he was born and where.

**69.** As he spoke, tears streamed from both his eyes;
taking Narottama by the hand, the brahmana said:

**70.** "O Narottama, his life is wonderful:
in a short time he became learned in all the shastras.

**71.** An embodiment of prema-bhakti, in great eagerness
he set out for Nilachala to see Sri Chaitanya.

**72.** Some way along he heard of Sri Prabhu's disappearance;
he fell senseless, and only by His will did he remain alive."

**73.** Thus in the *Guna-lesha-suchaka* of Sri Karnapura Kaviraja:[^5]
*He appeared in the house of a foremost brahmana of the Ghanteshvara line of the Rarhi brahmanas;*
*with a pure intellect expert in many shastras he conquered his opponents while still a boy;*
*hearing that the feet of Shachi's son were manifest at Niladri, he gave up everything —*
*may he, Sri Srinivasa Prabhu, my ocean of mercy, be victorious!*

**74.** *Going to Sri Purushottama, he heard on the way of Chaitanya's disappearance;*
*falling senseless, tearing his hair, striking his head and reproaching himself,*
*he placed His feet in his heart and went on to Nilachala by himself —*
*may he, Sri Srinivasa Prabhu, my ocean of mercy, be victorious!*

**75.** "Prabhu consoled him in a dream and brought him to Nilachala;
seeing Srinivasa, all were awash in tears of prema.

**76.** Gadadhara, Vakreshvara, the Pandita and all the others
showed Srinivasa mercy, each as was fitting,

**77.** and all bade him go to Vrindavana.
Having seen Jagannatha, he set out for Gauda.

**78.** Having come to Shrikhanda and setting out again for Nilachala,
on the way he heard of the disappearance of Pandita Gosani.[^6]

**79.** Half dead, he came back to the land of Gauda;
in a dream Sri Pandita consoled him endlessly.

**80.** In the morning, distraught, he went along the road to Gauda,
and there he met people from Gauda.

**81.** Hearing from them of the disappearance of Prabhu Nityananda and Advaita,
he fell unconscious.

**82.** Coming to his senses, he kindled a fire to burn himself,
but the two Prabhus consoled him in a dream

**83.** and bade him go to Vrindavana by way of Gauda;
when the night ended he set out for Gauda.

**84.** Going to Khanda, he bowed to Narahari and Sri Raghunandana,
received their permission and set out at once."

**85.** Thus in the same *Guna-lesha-suchaka*:
*Going on his way, he came to the town called Khanda, bowed to the beloved of Chaitanya-chandra,*
*the best of Thakuras, Sri Sarakara, and received his permission;*
*then, bowing at the feet of Raghunandana, he went on, remembering him —*
*may he, Sri Srinivasa Prabhu, my ocean of mercy, be victorious!*

**86.** "Coming to Navadvipa, he saw a marvel:
the manifest pastimes of Gauranga with His associates.

**87.** Astonished, he looked again — and saw
an ocean of sorrow surging in Navadvipa.

**88.** Eagerly Srinivasa went to Prabhu's house,
and there Vishnupriya Devi showed him great mercy.

**89.** Dasa Gadadhara, Srivasa and others
favoured Srinivasa, and all were awash in tears of prema.

**90.** Then, going to Shantipura, he saw Mother Sita —
her motherly affection cannot be described.

**91.** From there, absorbed in prema, he went to Khardaha,
and there Sri Jahnava showed him great mercy.

**92.** He went to Khanakul, to Sri Abhirama,
who together with Malini showed Srinivasa mercy.

**93.** He came again to Shrikhanda, and Sri Narahari
with great affection sent him off to Vraja.

**94.** Sri Raghunandana, overwhelmed with tenderness,
told him all about the way.

**95.** Srinivasa, having consoled his mother at Yajigrama,
a few days ago went alone to Vraja."

**96.** When Narottama heard this account of Srinivasa Acharya,
prema spontaneously arose in his heart.

**97.** Eagerly Narottama thought within himself:
"Who knows after how many days I shall gain his company?"

**98.** As he pondered thus, like the current of a river
the sweetest streams of prema flowed from his eyes.

**99.** Who can understand this way of Narottama's?
Again and again he listened to the lives of Prabhu's devotees.

[^1]: A danda is twenty-four minutes; the sixth danda is about two and a half hours after sunrise. Both editions read "মাঘ পূর্ণিমা." Tradition usually gives Magha Purnima as Narottama's appearance day; the *Prema-vilasa* differs (ev-narottama-birth).
[^2]: A play on words: *nara-uttama*, "the best of men."
[^3]: That is, in the guarded house he had no way of hearing about Gaura, yet he caught every word of it.
[^4]: "Early, middle and final" follows the 1924 edition; the 2021 edition has "early and middle."
[^5]: Karnapura Kaviraja was a disciple of Srinivasa Acharya; his praise of Srinivasa is also quoted in the *Bhakti-ratnakara*. Not to be confused with Kavi Karnapura, the son of Shivananda Sena.
[^6]: Pandita Gosani is Gadadhara Pandita. Srinivasa's journey is told at length in BR 3–4; cf. *Prema-vilasa* 4–6.

**100.** He constantly reproached himself
for not having seen such an incarnation of Prabhu.

**101.** He found no peace, his heart was always overflowing;
food did not please him, he passed the nights awake.

**102.** One day, when by Prabhu's will he fell asleep,
Gaura Raya appeared to him face to face in a dream:

**103.** a form enchanting the worlds, an ocean of rasa —
lightning, kunkuma, gold: what can be compared to Him?

**104.** The knot of His curling hair rolled down His back;
even chaste wives lost their modesty on seeing Him.[^7]

**105.** Earrings in His ears, His cheeks gleaming,
tilaka on His forehead — who could keep his life?

**106.** Brows like bows, lotus eyes, the snares of Kama,
a face steeped in smiles that surpasses the full moon.

**107.** Arms reaching to the knees, a broad chest,
lovely necklaces of many gems on a conch-like neck.

**108.** A deep and shapely navel set in three folds,
a slender waist surpassing a lion's.

**109.** Thighs like inverted plantain stems, enchanting even sages,
lovely soles surpassing the lotus.

**110.** He wore an incomparable cloth tucked in three places.
Seeing such wondrous beauty, Narottama

**111.** could not blink; tears streamed from his eyes
like strings of pearls on a lotus.

**112.** His most tender body was filled with thrills,
their lustre surpassing the beauty of kadamba filaments.

**113.** In rapture he fell to the ground and clasped Prabhu's feet,
and Prabhu placed His foot on Narottama's head.

**114.** Spreading out both arms, He embraced him
and, full of affection, spoke sweet words:

**115.** "O Narottama, see, here I am before you!
My heart cannot bear your weeping.

**116.** Do not worry: you will soon go to Vrindavana
and become the disciple of My dear Lokanatha.

**117.** With great joy he will give you the mantra of initiation,
and through you much will be accomplished."

**118.** As He was saying much in this way, the sleep was broken;
without the sight of Prabhu, waves of sorrow surged.

**119.** Distraught, he fell to the ground and rolled about —
and by Prabhu's will sleep drew him in again.

**120.** In the dream he saw: in Navadvipa, on the bank of the Ganga,
Gaura, Nityananda and Advaita joyfully sporting.

**121.** Gadadhara, Srivasa, Svarupa, Narahari,
Haridasa, Vakreshvara, Mukunda, Murari,

**122.** Govinda, Madhava, Vasu Ghosha, Shuklambara,
Gauridasa, Shriman, Sanjaya, Damodara,

**123.** Mahesha, Shankara, Yadu, Acharya-nandana —
the host of devotees surrounded Prabhu and performed sankirtana.

**124.** The people of Navadvipa came running from all sides —
who would not long to see such beauty?

**125.** Brahma, Shiva and Shesha, immeasurably intoxicated with joy,
gazed on that form with unblinking eyes.

**126.** Purandara, with all the gods in heaven,
showered flowers incessantly to see that beauty.

**127.** Gandharvas and kinnaras, mingling with men,
sang of the virtues of Prabhu and His associates, danced and ran about.

**128.** That ocean of prema surged and flooded the worlds —
neither the fallen, nor the low, nor the insentient escaped it.

**129.** Hundreds of thousands of beasts and birds forgot themselves at the sight of that beauty;
those blind from birth gained sight and came running.

**130.** Narottama saw this wondrous scene,
and his eyes poured like the current of a river.

**131.** Prabhu Gaurachandra, beholding Narottama,
took him on His lap — and could not restrain His heart.

**132.** He drenched Narottama with the tears of His eyes,
and Narottama fell at Prabhu's feet.

**133.** Lifting him from the ground, Gaurahari, full of affection,
took his hand and entrusted him to Nityananda and Advaita.

**134.** Having had His dear devotees bestow their mercy on him,
He urgently bade him go to Vrindavana.

**135.** Again He said, "Be merciful to him, My dear ones,"
and saying this, sent him off to Vrindavana.

**136.** Narottama could not be still even for an instant
once he glanced at the beauty of Prabhu Nityananda.

**137.** Falling to the ground, he bowed at Prabhu's feet,
and Prabhu placed his holy feet on his head.

**138.** Spreading out his arms, he embraced him
and gave him the priceless treasure of Gauranga's prema.

**139.** He gave him permission to go to Vrindavana
and, seeing him distraught, consoled him greatly.

**140.** Seeing the great beauty of Prabhu Advaita,
Narottama fell at his feet, rolling on the ground.

**141.** Prabhu Sri Advaita could not restrain himself;
lifting him by the hand, he embraced him again and again.

**142.** Entrusting him to the lotus feet of Gauranga,
he commanded, "Go to Vrindavana."

**143.** Gadadhara, Srivasa and the others dear to Prabhu —
seeing their beauty, his eyes blossomed.

**144.** Falling to the ground he bowed at the feet of all,
and all, in prema, embraced Narottama.

**145.** Narottama bathed in the tears from everyone's eyes
and offered his mind and life at the feet of all.

**146.** Prabhu's associates consoled Narottama,
entrusted him to Prabhu's feet and sent him off.

**147.** Just as Narottama was setting out for Vrindavana,
the sleep broke, and his heart was filled with great sorrow.

**148.** Waking, he saw that the night was turning to dawn;
he performed his morning duties and consoled his heart.

**149.** At that time various auspicious omens appeared,
and Narottama in his joy was bathed in tears.

**150.** Meanwhile Narottama's father suddenly
went to Gauda on royal business with many people.

**151.** Narottama knew that this was the auspicious moment,
and on some pretext took leave of his mother.[^8]

**152.** Most prudent, he considered everything carefully
and, eluding the guards, set out in secret.

**153.** Not visiting Navadvipa and other places,
for fear of people he went to Vrindavana by forest paths.

**154.** The Mahashaya disguised himself
so that no one would recognize him if he met anyone.

**155.** Having passed a fifteen days' journey,
his anxiety eased somewhat, and he went on calmly.

**156.** How the mother and father fared here without Narottama —
who could describe it with one mouth?

**157.** Everywhere in Gauda people said to one another:
"The prince Narottama has gone to Vraja.

**158.** The one whom Prabhu drew to Himself in the village of Ramakeli —
this is that Narottama, we now know for certain.

**159.** Otherwise could another have such power,
that whoever saw him lost the fear of worldly existence?"

**160.** People said many such things and wept;
everyone spoke eagerly of Narottama.

**161.** All those dear to Nityananda, Advaita and Chaitanya
prayed constantly for Narottama's welfare.

**162.** Narottama went on unhindered along the highway;
what his displays of prema were like — who could tell?

**163.** He sang the Lord's virtues incessantly,
his eyes pouring like the current of a river.

**164.** Whoever glanced but once at Narottama
was freed from the miseries of worldly life.

**165.** In whatever village Narottama spent the night,
gladness grew in the hearts of the villagers.

**166.** Men and women stayed near Narottama
and spoke softly to one another of many things.

**167.** Some said: "Gold and champaka — let them keep their distance!
Look what a wondrous form is shining!"

**168.** Some said: "What a face, what long eyes,
what a nose, cheeks, brows, forehead, ears!"

**169.** Some said: "What arms, what a broad chest,
a navel in three folds, what a slender waist!"

**170.** Some said: "What knees, what beauty in the feet!
Of what and by whom was he fashioned — with what care!"

**171.** Some said: "This is no ordinary man —
either a god or a king's son."

**172.** Some said: "Alas, at such a tender age,
in such renunciation he wanders from land to land!"

**173.** Some said: "What more can one say —
how will his mother and father keep alive without him?"

**174.** Some said: "Perish the Creator, heartless as he is,
who drove such a boy out of his home!"

**175.** Saying many such things to one another,
they could not leave Narottama and go home.

**176.** They brought various foods and fed him a little with care,
and brought him a fine bed to sleep on.

**177.** But neither food nor sleep pleased Narottama;
in sankirtana of the name he stayed awake all night.

**178.** His body grey with dust, tears in his eyes —
seeing that state, everyone's heart wept.

**179.** At dawn he set out, taking leave of all,
and people ran after him in distress.

**180.** Whoever saw this state of his on the road —
Narottama stole the hearts of all.

**181.** Having seen all the holy places in a short time,
Narottama entered Vrindavana with gladness of heart.

**182.** First he went to Sri Mathura, to the Vishrama-ghata,
bathed in the Sri Yamuna and stayed there.

**183.** A watch of the night had passed and all was deserted,
and in prema he performed sankirtana of the holy name.

**184.** At that time a brahmana of Mathura,
a great Vaishnava, most pure in conduct,

**185.** having offered wonderful foods to Krishna,
fed Narottama, overcome with affection.

**186.** Whatever the brahmana, overwhelmed by fatherly love, asked,
Narottama, won by affection, told him.

**187.** Narottama asked for news of Vraja,
and the brahmana with an aching heart told him everything.

**188.** Hearing that Raghunatha, Kashishvara, Rupa and Sanatana
had disappeared, he wept.

**189.** Uttering the names of Sri Rupa and Sanatana,
he rolled on the ground, his body grey with dust.

**190.** "Kashishvara Pandita! Sri Bhatta Raghunatha!" —
uttering these names, he beat his head with his hands.

**191.** "Alas, alas! What has happened!" he cried again and again,
"I have not seen the holy feet of any of them!"

**192.** Saying many such things, Narottama fainted;
streams flowed from both his eyes like rivers.

**193.** Seeing that he had become as if dead, the best of brahmanas
took Narottama on his lap and wept bitterly.

**194.** After a while the very old and most steady brahmana
composed himself and calmed Narottama.

**195.** In much conversation the night had almost passed,
and by Prabhu's will sleep came over them both.

**196.** In a dream Rupa and Sanatana appeared,
with Raghunatha Bhatta and Kashishvara — all four.

**197.** Narottama, seeing their beauty, awash in tears,
fell at the feet of all, rolling on the ground.

**198.** Then they embraced Narottama with great affection
and drenched Narottama's body with tears of prema.

**199.** They spoke nectarean words of consolation,
and the fortunate brahmana heard some of them.

**200.** All of them, greatly pleased with Narottama,
showed him their mercy and vanished.

**201.** In that separation Narottama's heart lost all composure;
waking, he lamented and looked in every direction.

**202.** "Where have you gone?" he cried, tears streaming from his eyes;
seeing Narottama's state, the brahmana was astonished.

**203.** Eagerly the brahmana took Narottama on his lap
and, crying "I am purified!", was bathed in tears.

**204.** Speaking many sweet words to Narottama,
after a while the old brahmana grew calm.

**205.** Seeing that the night had turned to morning, the best of brahmanas
wanted to take Narottama to his own house.

**206.** Narottama, bowing to the brahmana,
anxiously begged leave again and again:

**207.** "Be merciful to me — I shall go
and see the feet of all the Gosvamis.

**208.** Grant that my longing be fulfilled."
The brahmana affectionately embraced him and blessed him.

**209.** He went some distance with Narottama,
but his feet would not go on — he was very tired.

**210.** Showing Narottama the way to Vrindavana,
he, full of affection, gave him a man as companion.

**211.** Narottama went on, having bowed at the brahmana's feet,
and the brahmana, pained by separation, gazed after him along the road.

**212.** As he walked, Narottama thought to himself:
"They have brought one as unworthy as I to Vrindavana!

**213.** The merciful Prabhu, Sri Gosvami Lokanatha —
will he accept one as fallen as I?

**214.** Sri Gopala Bhatta, Sri Bhugarbha Mahashaya,
Sri Jiva Gosvami and the others, abodes of prema —

**215.** shall I be able to place their lotus feet on my head?
Will they all be merciful to a helpless one like me?

**216.** Srinivasa Acharya, the embodiment of prema —
will he show love to a wretch like me?"

**217.** As soon as he said this, tears of prema flowed from his eyes;
he could not walk, his body tottered.

**218.** Meanwhile, the previous night, Srinivasa suddenly
lost his composure — his heart was pervaded by gladness.

**219.** Seeing great auspicious omens, he thought within himself:
"Surely I shall meet some friend of my life."

**220.** As prema rose of itself his eyes poured,
and for much of the night he joyfully sang the holy name.

**221.** Toward the end of the night he suddenly slept a little,
and in a dream Sri Rupa said to Srinivasa:

**222.** "O Srinivasa! When this night turns to morning,
you will meet Narottama."

[^8]: "On some pretext" (প্রকারে) — the text does not specify.
