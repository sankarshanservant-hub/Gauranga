# Vilasa 1. Lokanatha Gosvami

*Opening verses and glorifications; the life of Lokanatha: Talkhari, his going to the Lord in Navadvipa, his search for Mahaprabhu, the dream-vision and the prophecy of Narottama; Lokanatha in Vrindavana with the Gosvamis; in Ramakeli the Lord calls Narottama by name*

**1.** *O you who are dear to those surrendered to the Lord of Sri, O king of dancers,*
*uniquely skilled in bestowing the treasure of your prema,*
*O friend of the life of Sri Gaura-Vishvambhara —*
*O Lokanatha, O Prabhu, be gracious to me!*[^1]

**2.** *I bow to Sri Lokanatha, the associate of Sri Chaitanya,*
*whose one life is Sri Radha-vinoda, who is the life of the people.*

**3.** *I bow to Sri Narottama, dear to Sri Gaura,*
*a bee at the lotus feet of Lokanatha, maddened by the rasa of Radha and Krishna.*

**4.** *I worship the branches of Sri Narottama Prabhu,*
*endowed with every good quality, removers of every anartha.*

**5.** *For the delight of the Vaishnavas and to fulfil my own cherished desire,*
*I briefly relate the book called "Narottama-vilasa."*[^2]

**6.** Glory, glory to Sri Gaura-Govinda, the Lord of all,
whose body, full of prema, enchants the worlds!

**7.** Glory to the son of Shachi and Jagannatha Mishra,
glory, glory to the life of Nityananda and Advaita!

**8.** Glory to the Lord of Gadadhara Pandita's life,
glory to the Lord of Srivasa, renowned in the world!

**9.** Glory to Him who is subdued by the prema of Haridasa and Vakreshvara,
glory to Him who is expert in increasing Murari's joy!

**10.** Glory to the friend of Gauridasa and Gadadhara,
glory to the Beloved of Narahari, full of majesty!

**11.** Glory to the treasury of virtues, dear to Svarupa,
glory to the life of Sanatana, Rupa and Gopala!

**12.** Glory, glory to the Lord together with the host of His devotees!
Reveal to me a little of the pastimes of Your devotees that I long for.

**13.** O listeners, hear the words of a fool such as I;
seeing my destitution, be kind to me, all of you.

**14.** I know neither good nor bad, I have no knowledge at all;
whatever I say, it is only the will of the sadhus that is strong in it.

**15.** This book, the "Narottama-vilasa," is delightful:
correct it and relish it continually.

**16.** In the order in which I made the mangalacharana in the preceding verses,
in that order I shall now speak — listen attentively.[^3]

**17.** Glory, glory to Lokanatha, dear to Sri Chaitanya,
the lamp of a brahmana family, renowned in every respect!

**18.** Here I shall tell a little of his life —
listen: it is known throughout the world.

**19.** In the land of Jessore there is a village named Talkhari:[^4]
there he appeared, incomparable in every way.

**20.** His mother was Sita, his father Padmanabha Chakravarti;
what can I say of how the two of them shone?[^5]

**21.** Padmanabha Chakravarti is known in the world:
Prabhu Advaita had great kindness for him.

**22.** A great Vaishnava, extraordinary in all his deeds,
full of every virtue, the foremost of the brahmanas of Rarh.

**23.** Day and night he was immeasurably intoxicated with sankirtana;
who could keep composure seeing the streams from his eyes?

**24.** By the grace of Sri Advaita, with great joy in his heart,
he would constantly come to Nadia to see Gauranga.

**25.** When he returned home, nothing pleased Padmanabha:
together with his wife he always sang of Gaurachandra's virtues.

**26.** As was Padmanabha, so was his wife Sita —
a great Vaishnavi, devoted to her husband.

**27.** Having obtained such a son as Lokanatha, the virtuous mother
raised him — how, I have no power to tell.

**28.** Having dedicated her son to the feet of Gaurachandra,
she watched her son's doings with great joy.

**29.** Sri Lokanatha had great eagerness on the path of bhakti;
beautiful in every limb, he was like compassion embodied.

**30.** From a young age he was learned in all the shastras
and most diligent in serving his father and mother.

**31.** He worshipped the feet of Krishna constantly
and by the power of bhakti drew all hearts to himself.

**32.** When after some time his father and mother passed from sight,
he told his friends what was in his mind.

**33.** Leaving worldly life and its pleasures like filth,
he set out for Nadia to see Prabhu.

**34.** In Navadvipa he gave himself to Prabhu's feet,
and Prabhu graciously kept him near Himself.

**35.** Prabhu, anxious at heart about the sannyasa He was to take,
quickly sent Lokanatha to Vraja.

**36.** Who can understand Prabhu's doings? They are utterly deep:
having sent Lokanatha away, He Himself was not at ease.

**37.** Lokanatha understood what was in Prabhu's heart:
in two or four days He would leave His home.

**38.** Prabhu is the independent Lord, all is by His will:
what Lokanatha's journey was like, I cannot describe.

**39.** Tears flowed incessantly from his eyes;
a day's journey took him four or five days.

**40.** Some way along he heard that Prabhu had taken sannyasa
and, consoling His dear devotees, had gone to Nilachala.

**41.** Remembering that the hair was no longer to be seen on Prabhu's head,
he wept aloud.

**42.** Half dead, by Prabhu's order,
after some days he entered Vrindavana.

**43.** He stayed some days, gazing at the beauty of Vrindavana,
and there he heard that Prabhu had gone to the south.

**44.** Anxious at heart, Lokanatha
set off for the south, where Sri Gaurasundara was.

**45.** Some way along he learned all the news:
Prabhu had returned from the south to Nilachala.

**46.** Having set out for Vrindavana by the road through Gauda,
He had gone back to the Kshetra at the wish of the devotees.[^6]

**47.** Again Lokanatha heard that Prabhu had come to Vrindavana,
and at once he set out for Vraja.

**48.** Coming to Vrindavana, he learned all the news:
in these very days Prabhu had left for Prayaga.

**49.** Grieved, Lokanatha resolved firmly:
"At dawn I shall go to Prayaga to see Prabhu."

**50.** Remembering Prabhu's virtues, he wept;
he rolled on the ground, and none could hold him.

**51.** At the end of the night, by Prabhu's will, he fell asleep
and in a dream saw Gaurachandra in Nadia:

**52.** His body anointed with sandal, like pure gold,
flowers arranged in His lovely curling hair,

**53.** a divine tilaka on His forehead, the sacred thread on His neck —
who would not be enchanted by the play of His eyes and brows?

**54.** How sweetly He smiles and smiles with His gentle mouth,
destroying the pride of the moon and showering nectar!

**55.** What arms reaching to the knees, what a broad chest!
He wore a lovely cloth tucked in three places.

**56.** Every limb was adorned with jewelled ornaments;
His age was youthful, and in it waves of rasa.

**57.** With sweet words He said to Lokanatha:
"I always abide here together with all of you.

**58.** Here in Navadvipa My pastimes are endless;
not even Brahma and the others can reach their limit."

**59.** Having said this and more, He began to embrace Lokanatha —
and he awoke, unable to bear his grief.

**60.** By Prabhu's will sleep drew him in again,
and again Prabhu appeared before Lokanatha —

**61.** Sri Krishna Chaitanya, the crown of sannyasis —
and spoke to Lokanatha in the sweetest words:

**62.** "You have resolved to go to Prayaga —
why should you go? See, here I am before you.

**63.** O Lokanatha! I had a great longing at heart
to live together with you in Vrindavana.

**64.** That is why I quickly sent you to Vrindavana
and Myself took sannyasa from Bharati.

**65.** I was eager to see the forest of Vrinda,
but that did not come to pass; I went to Advaita's house.

**66.** All were in great sorrow at My sannyasa;
staying at Advaita's home, I consoled them all.

**67.** Knowing everyone's wish, I went to Nilachala;
having stayed there some days, I travelled through the south.

**68.** For My sake you too set out for the south;
I came to Vraja, and hearing of it you came to Vraja.

**69.** As fate would have it, we did not meet;
there is no reckoning the sorrow you suffered.

**70.** Hearing from people that I had gone to Prayaga,
you resolved to go there at dawn.

**71.** I am always near you —
do not go anywhere away from Vrindavana.

**72.** From Prayaga I shall go to Nilachala,
and you will hear all that befalls Me.

**73.** Sanatana, Rupa and others dear to Me
you will very soon see here.

**74.** Through them I shall reveal the wish of My heart,
and an ocean of happiness will surge up in Vrindavana.

**75.** On the waves of that happiness you will float always,
and all that is in your heart will be fulfilled.

**76.** After some time a king's son
will become your disciple; his name will be Narottama.

**77.** He will always float in the rasa of prema-bhakti
and will easily destroy the impurity of living beings.

**78.** He will reveal a lofty and most sweet singing,
on hearing which even wood and stone will melt."

**79.** Having said this, He embraced Lokanatha,
and Lokanatha, falling to the ground, bowed at His feet.

**80.** At that moment sleep broke and Prabhu vanished;
in his distress Lokanatha could hardly keep his life.

**81.** Remembering and remembering the virtues of Gaurangachand,
he sighed and wept with stifled sobs.

**82.** After a while he consoled himself and grew calm,
yet still streams of prema flowed from his eyes.

**83.** Seeing that morning had come, he performed his morning duties
and, sitting in solitude, chanted the holy name.

**84.** At the request of the brahmanas of Vraja, at the proper time
he ate fruits and the like, and lived beneath a tree.

**85.** He never stayed long in one place,
but wandered about the region of Vrindavana.

**86.** Seeing a wondrous beauty of the forest somewhere,
he would remain there some days in secret.

**87.** Suddenly he heard from someone
that Sri Subuddhi Mishra had come to Vrindavana.[^7]

**88.** Then Sri Rupa Gosvami came
and went back again to where Sri Gaurasundara was.

**89.** Sanatana came and went on to Nilachala.
Hearing all this, Lokanatha shed tears of prema.

**90.** Crying "Sanatana! Rupa!" he would sigh:
"When shall we live together?"

**91.** As he spoke thus, in utmost agitation,
a voice came from the sky: "They will come soon."

**92.** How Rupa and Sanatana came after some days —
all that is described at length in other books.

**93.** Sri Gopala Bhatta and others came to Vrindavana,
and Lokanatha Gosvami met with all of them.

**94.** What joy there was at their meeting —
I, a fool, cannot describe even a trace of it.

**95.** Sri Rupa Gosvami always pleased
Lokanatha Gosvami with respect in every way.

**96.** What Sanatana Gosvami's attitude to him was,
he himself made known in his book.

**97.** Thus in the *Vaishnava-toshani*:
*I bow to those dear to Vrindavana, who have taken shelter at the feet of Sri Govinda —*
*to Sri Kashishvara, Lokanatha and Sri Krishnadasa.*[^8]

**98.** With Sri Gopala Bhatta, Raghunatha Bhatta and others,
Lokanatha was ever overwhelmed with prema.

**99.** Together with them Lokanatha, absorbed in prema,
enjoyed pastimes in Vrindavana with gladness of heart.

**100.** I cannot describe his wondrous life;
well known is his friendship with Bhugarbha Gosvami:

**101.** their body and mind were one, in nothing different —
most wonderful was the love between the two.

**102.** I cannot expand here on their love;
Lokanatha was in every way after his heart.

**103.** What shall I say? Hearing of the Gosvami's renunciation,
even a heart hard as stone splits apart.

**104.** Ever independent of all, in full accord with the scriptures of bhakti,
he was devoted to the service of the Deity Sri Radha-vinoda.

**105.** How he obtained Sri Radha-vinoda
is made known in the *Bhakti-ratnakara*.[^9]

**106.** As he gazed on the sweet beauty of Sri Radha-vinoda,
he suddenly beheld the sweet beauty of Gaura.

**107.** Then he remembered Prabhu's command in the dream,
and, overwhelmed with prema, could not stop his tears.

**108.** Speaking of the lovely deeds of Gaurangachand,
he would swoon and roll on the ground.

**109.** He constantly reproached himself
for not having seen the wonderful pastimes of Gauranga.

**110.** When Krishnadasa Kaviraja asked the Gosvami
for permission to write his book,

**111.** the Gosvami gladly gave him permission,
but forbade him to describe himself in it.

**112.** When he went for permission to Sri Gopala Bhatta Gosvami,
he too, with great regret, forbade him in the same way.[^10]

**113.** These accounts I heard from the lips of the elders;
I have described a little — fortunate are those who relish it.

**114.** Lokanatha Gosvami is supremely merciful,
a vessel of Sri Chaitanya's mercy, full of the jewel of prema.

**115.** He lived ever in Vrindavana; who can fathom his intent?
With a pleased heart he bestowed his mercy on Narottama.

**116.** Thus in these verses:[^11]
*He whose only wealth is the mercy of Krishna-Chaitanya, whose heart is rich with the golden ornaments of prema for Him —*
*falling to the ground we bow to him constantly; we take shelter of him, Lokanatha Prabhu.*

**117.** *He who obtained eternal residence in Vrindavana, in whom Krishna's pastimes and rasa shine forth,*
*who follows his own good conduct ceaselessly and without rest — we take shelter of him, Lokanatha Prabhu.*

**118.** *Whose power of mercy the great sage named Narottama came to know,*
*whose detachment from worldly things is vast — we take shelter of him, Lokanatha Prabhu.*

[^1]: The verse admits a double reading: the name "Lokanatha" means "lord of the worlds," and the whole address can be taken as referring to Gaura Himself (the Consort of Sri, king of dancers in sankirtana, bestower of His own prema). In its direct sense it addresses Lokanatha Gosvami, Narottama's guru.
[^2]: The "branches" (shakha) are Narottama's disciples and followers; Vilasa 12 is devoted to them.
[^3]: The author follows the order of the opening verses: first Lokanatha (Vilasa 1), then Narottama, then his "branches."
[^4]: The text has Talgari (তালগড়ি); usually Talkhari, north of Jessore (now Bangladesh).
[^5]: "Shone" follows the 1924 edition (কান্তি); the 2021 edition has "fame" (কীর্তি).
[^6]: Following the 1924 edition ("গৌড়পথে," "by the road through Gauda"); the 2021 edition has "Lokanatha" here. This refers to Mahaprabhu's first attempt to go to Vrindavana through Gauda, when He turned back to Puri from Ramakeli (Kanai-natashala) (CC Madhya 1; below, vv. 133–169).
[^7]: Subuddhi Raya (Mishra), a former ruler of Gauda who became a Vaishnava and settled in Mathura (CC Madhya 25).
[^8]: A verse from the opening of the *Vaishnava-toshani*, Sanatana Gosvami's commentary on the tenth canto of the *Bhagavata*.
[^9]: BR 2: Radha-vinoda Himself came to Lokanatha (according to tradition, from the forest near the village of Umrao) and gave Himself to him.
[^10]: Cf. the CC: Krishnadasa Kaviraja writes almost nothing about Lokanatha and Gopala Bhatta — at their own request.
[^11]: The 1924 edition names no source; the 2021 edition ascribes these verses to Vishvanatha Chakravarti's *Stavamrita-lahari* (his octet on Lokanatha).
