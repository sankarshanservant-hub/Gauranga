# Vilasa 10. The Deliverance of the Fallen

*Jahnava's Radha beside Gopinatha; the conversion of Harirama and Ramakrishna and the defeat of their father Shivai and the scholars; Ganganarayana Chakravarti; Narottama's disciples as a potter and a betel-seller humble the proud scholars; the conversion of King Narasimha, Rupanarayana, the bandit Chand Raya and the robbers of Vanga*

**1.** Glory to Gaura, Nityananda and Advaita with their associates!
Be merciful, Prabhu, to this wretched beggar.

**2.** Glory, glory to the listeners, oceans of compassion!
Hear what I shall now relate.

**3.** The Acharya's disciples Rama and Sri Raghunandana —
the two came from Vrindavana.

**4.** Having told the Mahashaya of the well-being of Vraja,
they spoke again with great gladness:

**5.** "The Thakurani sent by Sri Jahnava Ishvari[^1] —
what wondrous beauty she has, we cannot say —

**6.** all the Gosvamis, at Gopinatha's command,
placed at the left side of Sri Gopinatha.

**7.** There was a great festival — we saw it with our eyes;
the people of Vraja and the Vaishnavas rejoiced with great love."

**8.** Hearing this account, all rejoiced,
and Ramachandra quickly sent the two to bathe.

**9.** Sri Thakura Mahashaya together with Ramachandra
went in prema, the two of them, to bathe in the Padmavati.

**10.** Along that road came two young brahmanas,
with goats, sheep and buffalo calves.[^2]

**11.** Seeing them, the Mahashaya said to Ramachandra:
"These two brahmanas are fit for the worship of Krishna."

**12.** Ramachandra, with those two brahmanas in view,
walked along, discoursing on various shastras.

**13.** The two brahmanas, who were close by,
hearing the proofs of the shastras, gained clear knowledge.

**14.** Seeing the two, they said to each other with gladness of heart:
"These are the Kaviraja and Sri Thakura Mahashaya.

**15.** From afar we heard from people of their greatness,
and today is a blessed morning — we have seen them face to face."

**16.** Saying this, they had the goats and the rest kept at a distance
and, very timidly, came near.

**17.** The two said to them in sweet words:
"What are your names, whose sons are you? Tell us who you are."

**18.** Hearing this, the brahmana said: "My name is Harirama,
and this is my younger brother, named Ramakrishna.

**19.** Our father, Shivai Acharya, is known to all;
he spends much wealth on the worship of Bhavani.

**20.** Balarama Kaviraja is a fine physician.[^3]
At our father's bidding we came to fetch the goats and the rest.

**21.** He has no fear of killing living beings:
he believes that by such acts one enjoys heaven."

**22.** Saying this, he called out to his men:
"Let the goats and the rest go, and all of you go across the Padma."

**23.** At the word of Harirama Acharya
the goats and the rest were let go right there.

**24.** All the men went across the Padmavati,
and the two spoke humbly before the two:

**25.** "We came here to buy goats at an auspicious moment:
by the sight of these feet the darkness of ignorance is gone.

**26.** Now accept these low brahmanas —
let your fame resound in the world."

**27.** Saying this, they fell prostrate on the ground,
greatly drenched with the tears of their eyes.

**28.** Seeing them distraught, the two grew compassionate;
each embraced one and calmed him.

**29.** Bathing in the Padmavati, the two, taking the two,
went joyfully to Prabhu's house.

**30.** That day, according to the shastras, was wholly auspicious;
worldly attachment was strong, yet love grew in their hearts.

**31.** Harirama Acharya, from Sri Kaviraja,
received mantra initiation with great care.

**32.** To Ramakrishna Acharya Thakura Mahashaya
gave mantra initiation with gladness of heart.

**33.** Harirama and Ramakrishna are most fortunate:
for them Ramachandra and Narottama are one.

**34.** The two fell rolling at the feet of the two,
and the two infused great power into the two.

**35.** Entrusting them to the feet of Radha-Krishna and Chaitanya,
they joyfully revealed to them the siddhanta of bhakti.

**36.** Harirama and Ramakrishna, two brothers,
were continually intoxicated with the rasa of prema-bhakti.

**37.** On the ekadashi after Vijaya-dashami
they came to Prabhu's courtyard and took leave,

**38.** and placing on their heads the dust of their gurus' feet,
they came from Kheturi to the village of Goas.

**39.** They met Balarama Kaviraja
and spent that night at his house.

**40.** Telling him all that had happened to them,
they heard all the news from him.

**41.** In the morning they met their father;
Shivai, seeing his sons, blazed up like fire.

**42.** So that all the people gathered there could hear,
he said to his son in great anger:

**43.** "You fool, tell me, in what shastra is it said
that a Vaishnava is greater than a brahmana?

**44.** Bhagavati has punished you at last:
your life is wasted without Bhagavati.

**45.** A brahmana, you became a disciple to serve some Vaishnava!
I shall have you defeated in the assembly of panditas.

**46.** By Durga's grace I shall punish you fittingly,
so that no one ever does such a thing here again."

**47.** Hearing this, Harirama in anger said again and again:
"Bring the panditas — let us see who has what power.

**48.** If you defeat me first, then I will grant it;
otherwise these words are like the croaking of frogs."

**49.** Hearing his son's words, the father lost all composure in anger
and quickly called his son into the assembly of panditas.

**50.** Harirama, like a lion, with great boldness
refuted every doctrine and set bhakti above all.

**51.** With proofs from the Vedas and others — that the Vaishnava is worshipful to all;
hearing this, all were defeated.

**52.** All the people looked at Harirama;
some said, "Where did he learn so much?"

**53.** Some said: "By the mercy of the Vaishnavas
learning comes without effort; there is no need to study.

**54.** What Narottama and Ramachandra are —
you must have heard of the virtues of the two.

**55.** These two brothers are vessels of their mercy;
they cannot be defeated anywhere."

**56.** So many spoke, seeing the assembly of panditas;
all were defeated and deeply ashamed.

**57.** Saying, "Great is the power of the Vaishnavas,"
all went humbly to their homes.

**58.** In great anger Shivai brought Murari,
a conqueror of all quarters, living in the city of Mithila.

**59.** With many people, a very learned brahmana,
intoxicated with pride, he regarded others as straw.

**60.** Balarama Kaviraja, going to him,
defeated him easily with his own words.

**61.** Defeated, the digvijayi said to all:
"It is beyond me to describe the greatness of the Vaishnavas."

**62.** Saying this, he gave away all his possessions
and out of shame never went back to his own land.

**63.** At once he took up the life of a mendicant,
and all say, "Murari is the third path."[^4]

**64.** Shivai, disgraced, became as if dead:
by hating the Vaishnavas he met great sorrow.

**65.** Bhagavati punished him fittingly,
and people became attentive to Vaishnava dharma.

**66.** All these accounts spread through every land,
and the Vaishnavas rejoiced to hear them.

**67.** Harirama and Ramakrishna Acharya, the two,
always sang the sankirtana of the name in great bliss.

**68.** Devoted to the supremely rare path of bhakti,
living amid the world, they were wholly detached.

**69.** Intoxicated day and night with Sri Krishna Chaitanya's virtues,
they always stayed with Balarama Kaviraja.

**70.** One day the two, on their own business,
came to the bank of the Suradhuni, to the village of Gambhila.

**71.** There were many learned men there, and foremost among them
was the virtuous Ganganarayana Chakravarti:

**72.** of a sattvic nature, devoted to good works,
wholly self-controlled, wise, generous in teaching.

**73.** Standing unnoticed in his own house,
he watched Harirama and Ramakrishna Acharya.

**74.** Seeing their divine radiance, he reflected:
"I have seen them before, but now I see a marvel.

**75.** The Kaviraja and Sri Thakura Mahashaya,
the two, have mercifully favoured them.

**76.** They have become Vaishnavas and captivate hearts with their beauty;
by the mercy of those two all the shastras have been revealed to them.

**77.** They defeated many panditas;
the digvijayi became a mendicant out of shame.

**78.** Their power comes from the strength of that mercy;
both are greatly fortunate, their birth is fruitful.

**79.** He who on their account reviled the Mahashaya —
Bhagavati in anger punished that pashandi.

**80.** I, a low brahmana, proud of my petty learning,
surely have also slighted that Mahashaya.

**81.** If the Mahashaya shows me mercy,
then I shall be saved from hell.

**82.** He will surely accept a sinner like me:
I have heard there is no one so merciful."

**83.** So reflecting, Ganganarayana,
deeming himself wretched, wept.

**84.** As he wept, bhakti arose in him,
and, lamenting greatly, he cried out again:

**85.** "There is no dharma higher than the Vaishnavas' dharma,
and my mind did not turn to such a dharma!

**86.** Shame, shame! What fruit is there in this wretched life?
I have passed my life in vain, without devotion to Krishna.

**87.** O Narottama, Prabhu, give me the treasure of bhakti:
I take shelter at your lotus feet."

**88.** So, in much lamentation, he passed day and night,
and toward the end of the night sleep drew him in a little.

**89.** In a dream Thakura Mahashaya appeared to him,
a form made of compassion, greatly radiant.

**90.** Smiling softly, he said to Ganganarayana:
"You are my servant — why do you lament?

**91.** All your desires will be fulfilled:
tomorrow, as you bathe in the Ganga, you will see me.

**92.** I have come here from Kheturi;
at the bathing I shall tell you everything."

**93.** Saying this, the Mahashaya vanished,
and the Chakravarti, waking from the dream, was distraught.

**94.** Morning came; quickly finishing his morning duties,
he went to the bank of the Ganga and sat in meditation.

**95.** Harirama and Ramakrishna Acharya came there,
and the Chakravarti received the two with great honour.

**96.** Most humbly he said softly:
"Stay here with me for a while.

**97.** If by my good fortune Prabhu gives me darshan,
you two make me known to him."

**98.** As they were speaking thus to one another,
the Mahashaya with everyone came to the bank of the Ganga.

**99.** Harirama Acharya said: "Look, here he is:
Prabhu has unexpectedly come to bathe in the Ganga."

**100.** Ganganarayana was overjoyed:
as he had seen in the dream, so he saw before his eyes.

**101.** The Chakravarti said to Harirama Acharya:
"What is each one's name? Tell me who they all are."

**102.** Harirama, naming them all from a distance,
went ahead and told about the Chakravarti.

**103.** The Mahashaya, smiling, said softly:
"Bring Ganganarayana to me quickly."

**104.** Harirama brought Ganganarayana,
and Ganganarayana bowed to the ground.

**105.** The Mahashaya in prema embraced him
and spoke sweet words to the Chakravarti:

**106.** "My child, seeing such conduct of yours,
what will the brahmanas here think?"

**107.** The Chakravarti said: "Prabhu, whom you favour —
does he fear brahmanas devoid of bhakti?"

**108.** Saying this, he bowed at Ramachandra's feet,
and he met with everyone as was fitting.

**109.** Seeing Ganganarayana's conduct, someone
said to another very quietly:

**110.** "I have seen him many times here in Gambhila,
but never seen such a disposition in him."

**111.** Someone said: "He who was intoxicated with learning —
how has he become so humble?"

**112.** Someone said: "It was never to be expected of him —
how did such bhakti arise in him?"

**113.** Someone said: "O brother, I have considered:
at the sight of the Mahashaya everything is possible."

**114.** Someone said: "Whomever the Mahashaya favours
attains all perfection without effort."

**115.** "Blessed, blessed is Ganganarayana in the brahmana line —
he has become a Vaishnava!" So saying, they praised him.

**116.** The Chakravarti wished to request something;
understanding this, Thakura Mahashaya said to him:

**117.** "Do not think of any of that now:
having bathed, I shall go at once to Budhari.

**118.** Tomorrow morning I shall go to Kheturi:
there is a special task at Gauranga's house.

**119.** Together with Harirama and Ramakrishna
come tomorrow and stay in the village of Budhari.

**120.** Gathering there with Karnapura and others,
rise early and come quickly to Kheturi."

**121.** Saying this, quickly bathing and so on,
the Mahashaya came with everyone to Budhari.

**122.** Ganganarayana quickly bathed in the Ganga
and brought Harirama and Ramakrishna to his home.

**123.** That day the three stayed in Gambhila,
and very early in the morning the three set out.

**124.** Going quickly to Budhari, with gladness of heart,
they stayed at the house of Sri Govinda Kaviraja.

**125.** Govinda's son is Divyasimha Kaviraja;
seeing his way of bhakti, they were amazed.

**126.** Karnapura Kaviraja and others were there;
in the morning all came quickly to Kheturi.

**127.** All went and saw Gauranga,
and everyone's mind was greatly delighted.

**128.** Ganganarayana Chakravarti, before Prabhu,
begged only for the fulfilment of his desire.

**129.** That day they spent in the bliss of sankirtana;
in the morning all performed their morning duties.

**130.** Considering it a most auspicious day,
the Mahashaya made Ganganarayana his disciple.

**131.** Giving the mantra initiation, the Mahashaya rejoiced
and entrusted him to the lotus feet of Sri Krishna Chaitanya.

**132.** Narottama Mahashaya, the incarnation of bhakti,
infused his own power into Ganganarayana.

**133.** Thus in the *Stavamrita-lahari*:
*Narottama, bhakti incarnate,*
*into whom he joyfully placed his own power —*
*may Sri Chakravarti Ganganarayana,*
*an ocean of the rasa of prema, be merciful to me!*[^5]

**134.** Ganganarayana was overwhelmed with bliss,
unable to check the tears of both his eyes.

**135.** He fell rolling at the lotus feet,
and the ocean of mercy, Narottama, embraced him.

**136.** He entrusted him to Ramachandra Kaviraja,
and he bowed at Ramachandra's feet.

**137.** Sri Govinda Kaviraja and all the others —
as he bowed, they bowed in return and embraced him.

**138.** There was bliss in the hearts of all the Vaishnavas;
the news of the mercy to Ganganarayana spread everywhere.

**139.** Ganganarayana, expert in all the shastras,
studied the books of the Gosvamis.

**140.** Ceaselessly in the ocean of the happiness of sankirtana
Ganganarayana swims in great bliss.

**141.** The Chakravarti became rich with the treasure of prema-bhakti,
his form even more radiant than before.

**142.** Ganganarayana became exclusively devoted to Krishna.
So the Mahashaya blesses brahmanas and others.

**143.** A foremost brahmana named Jagannatha Acharya
was greatly devoted to the worship of Bhagavati.

**144.** The Goddess, being pleased with him, commanded:
"Go and take shelter at Narottama's lotus feet.

**145.** Only then will your bondage to the world be cut;
you will obtain the treasure of bhakti, rare even for us.

**146.** Be exclusively devoted to that Prabhu's feet:
without worship of Krishna, life is in vain."[^6]

**147.** Receiving such a command, the brahmana at dawn
came in distress to the village of Kheturi.

**148.** Sri Thakura Mahashaya was seated;
coming before him, he bowed, falling to the ground.

**149.** With tears, distraught at heart, the brahmana,
with joined palms, said slowly:

**150.** "Bhagavati commanded me, and I have come to you;
my good and ill, Prabhu, now rest with you.

**151.** Give me mantra initiation and deliver me:
this sinner's all is these feet of yours.

**152.** My understanding is small; I do not know how to speak."
Hearing the brahmana's words, compassion arose in his heart.

**153.** Thakura Narottama made the brahmana his disciple,
and by the power of bhakti he became most excellent.

**154.** So the Mahashaya makes many his disciples;
some hear of it with joy, others are pained to hear it.

**155.** A king named Narasimha lived in a distant land;
many brahmana panditas stayed with him.

**156.** The brahmanas in anger said to the king again and again:
"Dharma is perishing, and no one looks into it.

**157.** Narottama Dasa, son of Krishnananda Datta,
taking up the Vaishnava doctrine, has ruined everything.

**158.** We do not know what sorcery he knows,
that brahmanas easily become his disciples.

**159.** If you say he is learned in the shastras —
that is only false pride before fools.

**160.** Would any words come to him before us?
Go quickly and take us with you.

**161.** You will see the fun: out of fear of me alone
he will run away from there with his bhava-kali.[^7]

**162.** Your fame will be great in every land;
through you the brahmana caste will endure.

**163.** The king is the punisher, for he is a portion of Ishvara;
otherwise many of the brahmana caste will be destroyed."

**164.** Hearing this, King Narasimha set out,
and Rupanarayana went with the king.

**165.** The teachers, taking many books,
went in great pride, rejoicing.

**166.** To Kumarapura, a village near Kheturi,
the king came with many people.

**167.** Hearing that the king had come there, the Mahashaya
said very softly to Ramachandra:

**168.** "We shall have to hold a debate with the teachers;
I thought it would interfere with bhajana."

**169.** Hearing such words of Sri Mahashaya,
Ramachandra Kaviraja said with a smile:

**170.** "Their pride will be crushed without effort,
and afterwards they will come and fall at your feet."

**171.** Saying this, Ramachandra and Ganganarayana,
the two, went to the village of Kumarapura.

**172.** On the way they became a potter and a betel-seller:
one carried betel on his head, the other pots.

**173.** Entering Kumarapura, at the market place,
the two set up a stall and sat down.

**174.** Here a student came to buy betel;
he asked the price, and this one answered in Sanskrit.

**175.** The student, proudly, spoke in Sanskrit —
and after two or four sentences he was defeated.

**176.** The betel-seller said: "Fool, what do you know?
If you are ashamed, bring your teacher."

**177.** The student went and said to his teacher:
"I have been defeated by a betel-seller and a potter.

**178.** In the village of Kheturi, where Narottama lives,
the betel-seller and the potter supply betel and pots.

**179.** What shall I say? Their learning is immense —
it seems these two may defeat even you.

**180.** If you can defeat the betel-seller and the potter,
then go to Kheturi; otherwise let us go home."

**181.** Hearing this, he became like fire and said again and again:
"Show me where this betel-seller and potter are!"

**182.** Saying this, the teacher went quickly
and debated various shastras with the betel-seller.

**183.** One by one the teachers came there,
King Narasimha and Rupanarayana.

**184.** On all sides a great crowd gathered;
what a wondrous battle of shastras took place between them!

**185.** The betel-seller and the potter, with great gladness of heart,
refuted and explained in the sweetest words.

**186.** The teachers were filled with great anger,
but could not establish their explanations against the unearthly ones.

**187.** All this cannot be told briefly:
the teachers were quickly defeated.

**188.** The king with the teachers went to their lodging;
there someone said to another, laughing:

**189.** "The teachers came like lions,
and, defeated, became like dogs.

**190.** They cannot know Sri Mahashaya even by name —
he whose disciples brahmanas are told by Parvati to become!"

**191.** So all spoke of the Mahashaya's greatness;
hearing it from people, the king became afraid.

**192.** He said softly to Rupanarayana:
"What is to be done now, brother? Tell me."

**193.** Rupanarayana said: "The essence of all
is the dharma of the Vaishnavas; there is no higher dharma.

**194.** If one always listens to blasphemy of Vaishnavas,
one surely goes to hell.

**195.** Let us go and take shelter at his feet;
only then shall we be saved — I tell you for certain."

**196.** Narasimha said: "That is what I have resolved:
there is no use in delay — let us go at once."

**197.** Rupanarayana said: "Stay here today;
tomorrow morning go with your people."

**198.** At once the news spread everywhere:
"Tomorrow the king will go with his people to Kheturi."

**199.** The teachers were in great distress:
for shame they could not appear before the king.

**200.** As if dead, they sat in their places
and said to one another, "What will happen tomorrow morning?"

**201.** Meanwhile, having defeated the teachers,
the betel-seller and the potter went to Kheturi.

**202.** Ramachandra, calling the poor, gave away the betel,
and Ganganarayana gave away the pots.

**203.** In great merriment the two came to Kheturi
and told everything to Sri Thakura Mahashaya.

**204.** Meanwhile King Narasimha thought within himself:
"Will he be merciful to such a wicked man?"

**205.** Rupanarayana, lamenting much, said:
"Without his mercy life is in vain."

**206.** Suddenly someone said from a distance:
"He will be merciful out of his own goodness."

**207.** Hearing this, they became most eager,
thinking, "How long until this night is over?"

**208.** It grew very late, and they lay down.
Meanwhile, among the teachers,

**209.** the foremost of all, extremely proud —
toward the end of the night he slept a little.

**210.** In a dream he saw the Goddess, sword in hand,
standing before him and speaking in great anger:

**211.** "In vain you studied, evil-minded one!
For reviling a Vaishnava you will fall low.

**212.** Only if I cut your head to pieces
will the grief of my heart be appeased.

**213.** Wicked asura, what lesson shall I give you?
If Narottama is merciful to you, you will be saved."

**214.** Saying many such things, glaring with blood-red eyes,
the Goddess stayed a moment and vanished.

**215.** The teacher awoke, trembling with fear,
and with a terrible cry woke everyone.

**216.** Weeping, the brahmana said to all:
"By great good fortune I have just been saved.

**217.** Because I regarded Narottama as low,
the Goddess came sword in hand to slay me.

**218.** If that Mahashaya shows mercy,
then I shall be saved from a terrible hell."

**219.** As he was saying this, the night turned to morning,
and he went and told all this before the king.

**220.** The king said: "I forbade you before — you did not listen;
you regarded the Mahashaya as an ordinary man.

**221.** What he does — is it within a man's power?
Sri Thakura Mahashaya is supremely worshipful."

**222.** Saying many such things, he calmed the teacher,
and in the morning, having bathed and so on, they made ready.

**223.** Without carriages the king with the teachers and others
went quickly to Kheturi, to Gauranga's courtyard.

**224.** At the sight of Gauranga, most humbly,
they bowed, rolling on the ground.

**225.** The very wise Ramachandra, Govinda and others who were there
received them with honour, and all were pleased.

**226.** Sri Thakura Mahashaya was in seclusion;
all were eager to see him.

**227.** At that time, having finished his regular tasks, the Mahashaya
came from a distance, and all gazed at his beauty.

**228.** King Narasimha and Rupanarayana
went forward from the courtyard to meet him.

**229.** Ramachandra said to the Mahashaya:
"This is King Narasimha, and this is Rupanarayana."

**230.** The two said: "Prabhu, how shall we introduce ourselves?
We are worldly men, low and great offenders.

**231.** We take shelter, yet we are afraid to ask:
give us mantra initiation and fulfil our desire."

**232.** Saying many such things, the two, falling to the ground,
bowed again and again, bathed in tears.

**233.** Seeing the two greatly distraught, the Mahashaya
consoled them much and embraced them both.

**234.** Narasimha and Rupanarayana, falling to the ground,
placed the Mahashaya's feet on their heads.

**235.** Sorrow went far away, bliss came into their hearts,
and bringing the teacher, they said to the Mahashaya:

**236.** "Of all the teachers he is the foremost;
his pride has gone — now deliver him."

**237.** The teacher, standing before the Mahashaya,
told in distress of the Goddess,

**238.** and said: "Forgive my offense:
I, a great evil-doer, take shelter."

**239.** Saying this, the brahmana wept, rolling on the ground,
and however he tried, could not compose himself.

**240.** Sri Thakura Mahashaya, the embodiment of compassion,
embraced the brahmana, showing him mercy.

**241.** Receiving his touch, the brahmana, with joyful heart,
taking the dust of his feet, rolled in the dust.

**242.** Ramachandra calmed the teacher,
and the teacher deemed himself blessed.

**243.** All became vessels of devotion to Krishna Chaitanya;
all this became known everywhere.

**244.** The Mahashaya, happily satisfying everyone,
came with all to Prabhu's courtyard.

**245.** Having seen the raja-bhoga arati,
everyone's heart rejoiced greatly.

**246.** Sri Santosha Raya, honouring them all,
took them to a most wonderful lodging.

**247.** He quickly had various provisions brought there
and with great care offered them for cooking.

**248.** King Narasimha, the teachers and all the others
said, "We shall partake of the Sri prasada."

**249.** Hearing this, Santosha insistently and carefully
gave foodstuffs to the people who were with them.

**250.** King Narasimha and Rupanarayana,
the teachers and some other distinguished men

**251.** all went joyfully together there,
where Sri Thakura Mahashaya was with his people.

**252.** The bliss of the meal there —
I cannot describe it for fear of the book's length.

**253.** Rupanarayana and the others ate the prasada
and passed the day and night in supreme bliss.

**254.** The next day, at a most wonderful hour,
they became disciples with great bliss at heart.

**255.** Sri Thakura Narottama showed them great mercy:
giving them mantra initiation, he entrusted them to Prabhu's feet.

**256.** For some days all stayed there
and studied the books of the Gosvamis.

**257.** The bliss day by day cannot be told:
all became eligible for prema-bhakti.

**258.** Without sankirtana no one's mind was at rest:
all became intoxicated with the bliss of sankirtana.

**259.** The holy songs composed by Sri Govinda Kaviraja
they always relished with great love.

**260.** From the holy lips of Ganganarayana Chakravarti
all heard the *Srimad-Bhagavata* with great happiness.

**261.** Day and night no one had any leisure:
all were intent on performing the limbs of bhakti.

**262.** Whoever came once to the village of Kheturi
could not leave such bliss and go away.

**263.** King Narasimha and Rupanarayana
went to their own land, and the two came back quickly.

**264.** King Narasimha's wife was Rupamala,
most devoted to her husband, modest and virtuous.

**265.** Seeing her way of bhakti, with joy at heart,
the Mahashaya gave her the holy mantra.

**266.** Bliss grew greatly in Rupamala's heart:
she took the vow of chanting a hundred thousand names.[^8]

**267.** For the feet of Radha, Krishna and Chaitanya with their associates
a deep attachment arose in her, growing day by day.

**268.** So Sri Thakura Mahashaya, out of his own goodness,
shows mercy, and all sing his virtues.

**269.** A bandit named Harishchandra Raya,
hearing of his virtues, took shelter of the Mahashaya.

**270.** Giving him mantra initiation, he delivered him,
and later he came to be called Haridasa.

**271.** He became eligible for rare bhakti
and gave up his zamindari in Jalapantha.

**272.** Seeing the mercy to the bandit, amazed,
sitting in private, people said to one another:

**273.** "Sri Thakura Mahashaya is a treasury of virtues:
he delivered the bandit without effort."

**274.** Someone said: "The chief of the bandits is Chand Raya:
people always tremble for fear of him.

**275.** If the Mahashaya is merciful to this low one,
then this land will be safe in every way."

**276.** Someone said: "O brother, do not worry:
Chand Raya will surely receive mercy.

**277.** By that mercy all this evil-mindedness will go,
and Chand Raya with his kin will become a Vaishnava."

**278.** Someone said: "This evil-doer is the last of all;
I feel he will soon be delivered."

**279.** At that moment a brahmana came joyfully and said:
"The Mahashaya has shown mercy to Chand Raya!

**280.** Making Sri Narottama's lotus feet his all,
he has been delivered from the peril of worldly life.

**281.** Before, at the sight of him there was great fear;
now, at a mere glance, bliss arises.

**282.** What shall I say? All his former evil-mindedness is gone;
he has become peaceful — what a wondrous Vaishnava!

**283.** I saw it and came: in Prabhu's courtyard
he dances in the sankirtana, grey with dust."

**284.** Hearing all this, greatly delighted,
people ran to see Chand Raya.

**285.** From afar they saw Chand Raya in prema,
fallen on the ground, bathed in tears.

**286.** Thrills all over his body, trembling again and again —
seeing this, all the people were amazed.

**287.** Someone said: "At last the fear of bandits is gone;
the Mahashaya has protected us in every way."

**288.** Saying many such things, with great bliss at heart,
all extolled Sri Chand Raya's good fortune.

**289.** At that moment some men came there,
all bearing various weapons, from distant lands.

**290.** Without making themselves known, they asked these people:
"What sort of Vaishnava has Chand Raya become, and how?"

**291.** Hearing this, they told them briefly:
"Chand Raya with his kin was a worshipper of the Devi.

**292.** Chand Raya is a mighty zamindar,
the chief of bandits, most wicked in conduct.

**293.** The Devi, greatly angered at seeing his misdeeds,
punished him fittingly through a brahma-daitya.[^9]

**294.** Then the same Devi, seeing his life in danger,
commanded: 'Take shelter at Narottama's feet.

**295.** Narottama Mahashaya is most merciful:
he will save you from hell.'

**296.** At this command in a dream Chand Raya at once
took shelter at the Mahashaya's feet.

**297.** Sri Thakura Mahashaya, seeing his great suffering,
out of his own goodness gave him the holy mantra.

**298.** His evil-mindedness was gone, he deemed himself lowly;
the yavana king seized him by force and punished him.[^10]

**299.** Chand Raya counts all those sufferings as nothing:
his mind is fixed solely on Prabhu's feet.

**300.** The yavana brought an elephant to kill Chand —
the elephant fled in fear of Chand Raya.

**301.** The king, greatly alarmed, said to everyone:
'Keep him in prison with the greatest care.'

**302.** Chand, reflecting, rejoiced:
'I did evil — this punishment is just.'

**303.** Someone said, 'Remove the trouble with the Devi's mantra.'
Chand Raya said, 'I will not touch any other mantra.'

**304.** Seeing such steadfastness, Prabhu became merciful,
and suddenly great fear came upon the yavana.

**305.** Begging pardon, he released Raya,
and he came here a few days ago."

**306.** Hearing all this, they asked everyone again:
"Where is Sri Thakura Mahashaya?"

**307.** Someone said: "Look, there under the tree
he is sitting with his dear associates."

**308.** As soon as they saw the Mahashaya from afar,
Bhakti-devi showed them mercy at once.

**309.** Throwing away their swords and other weapons,
they fell before the Mahashaya, rolling on the ground.

**310.** Seeing them all greatly distraught, the Mahashaya
said in sweet words: "Tell me who you are.

**311.** Where have you come from, and for what purpose?"
Hearing this, all said with tears:

**312.** "We are bandits of Vanga, evil brahmanas;
Chand Raya is, as it were, our chief.

**313.** We were going by boat to rob
and came to Raya to take his counsel.

**314.** From people we heard about Raya —
and as soon as we heard, our hearts turned.

**315.** When from afar we saw your lotus feet,
we did not understand what happened in our hearts.

**316.** There is no one as low as we are:
we take shelter — now deliver us."

**317.** Saying this, all wept, distraught,
and the Mahashaya consoled and calmed them all.

**318.** At that moment Chand Raya came there,
and all were overjoyed to see him.

**319.** Chand Raya, seeing them so humble,
was greatly delighted and praised them all.

**320.** Sri Thakura Mahashaya, some days later,
mercifully made them all his disciples.

**321.** All became eligible for great bhakti;
their wondrous doings I cannot relate at length.

**322.** Whoever lets all these accounts enter his ears
loses his evil-mindedness and obtains holy bhakti.

**323.** Listen to all this continually and with care:
Narahari tells the *Narottama-vilasa*.

*Thus ends the tenth vilasa of the Narottama-vilasa.*
