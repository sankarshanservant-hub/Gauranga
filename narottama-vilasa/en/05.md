# Vilasa 5. Narottama's Journey through Gauda after Nilachala

*Shrikhanda: Narahari Sarakara and Raghunandana; Yajigrama: meeting with Srinivasa, news of the disappearance of the associates in Puri; Katwa: Dasa Gadadhara and Yadunandana, the place of the Lord's sannyasa; Ekachakra: Nityananda as an old brahmana shows the places of His pastimes and appears as Balarama*

**1.** Glory to Gaura, Nityananda and Advaita with their associates!
Be merciful, Prabhu, to this wretched beggar.

**2.** Glory, glory to the listeners, oceans of mercy!
Hear what I shall now relate.

**3.** In Gauda there is a famous village named Shrikhanda;
there came Narottama, the abode of virtues.

**4.** Someone who saw Narottama on the road
was going to the house of Sri Sarakara Thakura.

**5.** Coming to the Thakura, he said softly:
"A man of wondrous beauty is coming.

**6.** What a gait of prema — now he walks, now he cannot!
Gazing toward Shrikhanda, he is bathed in tears.

**7.** It seems he has come from Nilachala;
four men are with him."

**8.** Hearing this, the Thakura said, "What more is there to say?
Narottama has come from Nilachala."

**9.** Sri Raghunandana, hearing this, went out to meet him,
and seeing Narottama from afar, he rejoiced.

**10.** Narottama, learning from people who he was,
felt a joy that cannot be told.

**11.** As he fell to the ground and bowed to Sri Raghunandana,
he ran and embraced him and could not let him go.

**12.** His throat choked, streams flowed from both his eyes;
he could not say anything of all that rose in his heart.

**13.** After a while Sri Raghunandana, growing calm,
quickly went off with Narottama.

**14.** Coming to Sri Sarakara Thakura,
Narottama bowed, rolling on the ground.

**15.** Though the Thakura was burning in the fire of separation,
still, seeing Narottama, he rejoiced at heart.

**16.** "Come, come!" he said, spreading out both arms,
and taking Narottama in his embrace, he was bathed in tears.

**17.** With what wondrous affection he seated him beside himself,
and, looking at Narottama's face, spoke gently:

**18.** "I had a great longing to see you;
it is well that you came soon — I have seen you with my eyes.

**19.** Through you Prabhu will distribute the treasure of bhakti;
many people will take shelter of you.

**20.** Prabhu in His ecstatic mood will manifest Himself in lofty song —
who will not be maddened by your kirtana?

**21.** Prabhu will fulfil all your desires;
never worry about anything.

**22.** Go quickly to Kheturi by way of Yajigrama:
Srinivasa Acharya is watching the road for you.

**23.** He came from Vishnupura a few days ago
and is always anxious about you.

**24.** When he sees you his heart will be at peace;
yesterday he came here and went back home."

**25.** Saying this, he asked for news of Sri Kshetra,
and Narottama told him the state everyone was in.

**26.** What happened to Sri Sarakara Thakura on hearing it
I could not tell with a thousand mouths.

**27.** Growing calm, he bade Sri Raghunandana:
"Take Narottama to Gauranga's courtyard."

**28.** Sri Raghunandana, taking Narottama by the hand,
led him to Gauranga's courtyard, calming him.

**29.** Narottama, seeing the Deity of Gaura-Krishna,
could not restrain his heart; streams flowed from both his eyes.

**30.** Falling to the ground he bowed again and again —
who could keep composure seeing those transformations of prema?

**31.** After a while, growing calm, he gazed his fill,
and the pujari brought and gave him a garland-prasada.

**32.** Hearing that Narottama had come, the people of Shrikhanda
all came and gathered in Gauranga's courtyard.

**33.** What happened at their meeting
could not be described even with hundreds of mouths.

**34.** All spoke to Narottama in sweet words,
calmed him and took him to a lodging.

**35.** Narottama, seated in the lodging, at once
gave Sri maha-prasada to Sri Raghunandana.

**36.** Taking the maha-prasada of Sri Jagannatha,
he quickly went and gave it to Sri Sarakara Thakura.

**37.** The Thakura ate the Sri maha-prasada with care;
remembering the past, great grief arose in him:

**38.** streams flowed from both his eyes, he could not restrain his heart;
he heaved long sighs, saying "Gaurachandra!"

**39.** After a while, growing calm, he said to Sri Raghunandana:
"Give the Sri prasada to everyone."

**40.** Sri Raghunandana gave the Sri prasada to all,
and as they ate the prasada no one's mind could be still:

**41.** remembering Prabhu's wondrous pastimes in Nilachala,
all shed tears ceaselessly.

**42.** With great effort all grew calm,
but they could not let Narottama go even for a moment.

**43.** Having passed day and night in the relish of talks about Krishna,
Narottama in the morning performed his morning duties.

**44.** Having bathed and so on and seen Gauranga,
he quickly went to the Thakura.

**45.** Sarakara Thakura, looking at Narottama's face,
said with great affection, "My eyes are soothed.

**46.** I shall not see you again," he said,
and became distraught in a way that cannot be described.

**47.** Narottama, falling to the ground again and again,
took the dust of his feet, tears flowing from his eyes.

**48.** The Thakura embraced Narottama
and, remembering Gauranga, sent him off.

**49.** Narottama set out, having taken leave
and bowed to the associates who lived in Khanda.

**50.** Sri Raghunandana went some distance with him;
unable to part, his sorrow grew greatly.

**51.** He gave him a man to go with him to Yajigrama
and consoled Narottama in many ways.

**52.** As he sent him off, his heart was breaking;
again and again he gazed at Narottama's face.

**53.** Embracing him, he stood still and composed himself,
and Narottama, bowing, was bathed in tears.

**54.** Distraught, he went along the road to Yajigrama;
whoever saw that state of his floated in tears of prema.

**55.** The wise man who had come from Khanda
pointed out the Acharya's house from afar.

**56.** Meanwhile Srinivasa Acharya, in his own house,
was teaching the shastras to his disciples.

**57.** At that moment someone came hurriedly and said:
"Sri Thakura Mahashaya has come from the Kshetra."

**58.** Someone else said: "What a marvel I have seen with my eyes:
gazing toward Yajigrama, he loses his composure."

**59.** Hearing this, Srinivasa Acharya was going out to meet him,
when Narottama came and entered the house.

**60.** Seeing each other, both were bathed in tears;
in the hearts of both the ocean of prema surged.

**61.** As Srinivasa spread his arms to embrace him,
Narottama fell to the ground in obeisance.

**62.** Who can understand the wondrous ways of these two?
It is known everywhere that only their bodies are different.

**63.** After a while both grew calm and sat down,
and told each other all that had happened.

**64.** What he had heard of the state of the devotees in the Kshetra,
he asked Narottama about again and again.

**65.** At that moment a brahmana came from the Kshetra,
a great Vaishnava, learned in all the shastras.

**66.** In the hope of studying the books of the Gosvamis
he offered himself to the Acharya.

**67.** Acharya Thakura received him courteously
and asked for news of Sri Nilachala.

**68.** Heaving a long sigh and awash in tears, the brahmana
said: "Nilachala has been emptied of its jewels.

**69.** From the day after
Sri Thakura Narottama came there, disaster struck:

**70.** one after another almost all have disappeared;
Shyamananda came — and fell into an ocean of sorrow.

**71.** The state he fell into cannot be described;
only by Prabhu's will did life remain in him.

**72.** Those who still remained consoled Shyamananda
and sent him off to his own land; I saw this and came away.

**73.** I could not stay in the Kshetra — what more can I say?
Day and night I travelled to come to Gauda."

**74.** As he spoke, the brahmana lost his composure
and wept, taking the names of the devotees of the Kshetra.

**75.** Acharya Thakura took that brahmana in his arms
and, weeping, overwhelmed, was bathed in tears.

**76.** Narottama too wept, his heart in great distress;
how much he lamented cannot be told.

**77.** Vyasa Chakravarti, Krishnavallabha and all the others[^1] —
what can I say of the state of them all?

**78.** After a while Acharya Thakura, growing calm,
consoled the brahmana and gave him a lodging.

**79.** Acharya Thakura, won over by his love,
began his studies with him that very day.

**80.** He took Sri Thakura Mahashaya aside
and told him many things — who can understand them?

**81.** They passed the night in talks about Prabhu,
and in the morning Narottama took his leave.

**82.** The state of the two at the parting —
seeing it, no one could keep his composure.

**83.** The Acharya, gazing at the road Narottama had taken,
became like one inert, streams flowing from both his eyes.

**84.** Vyasa Chakravarti and others went some distance with him,
and Narottama with effort sent them back.

**85.** Narottama went on, bathing in his tears,
and came to Kantaka-nagara, to Bharati's place.[^2]

**86.** What befell him at the sight of Dasa Gadadhara's Gauranga —
who could describe it?

**87.** Sri Gadadhara's disciple Sri Yadunandana,
known as Chakravarti, expert in all the shastras,

**88.** seeing Narottama's state, greatly moved,
came out of Prabhu's temple.

**89.** Giving Narottama the garland from Prabhu's neck,
he embraced Narottama and was bathed in tears.

**90.** His throat choked, and he said slowly:
"It is well that you came quickly to Kantaka-nagara.

**91.** For you my Prabhu Gadadhara
was so anxious — who can understand his heart?

**92.** At times, forgetting himself, he said again and again:
'Look, look, how far off is Narottama?'

**93.** O brother, what more can I say of what has happened?
Day by day the boundless ocean of sorrow grows.

**94.** When Vishnupriya Ishvari passed from sight,
he came from Navadvipa and lives here in seclusion.[^3]

**95.** Neither food nor drink pleases him, he grieves incessantly;
his golden body has grown dim and thin."

**96.** Saying many such things to Narottama,
he took him to where Dasa Gadadhara was.

**97.** He was sitting grey with dust,
eyes closed, streams flowing down his chest.

**98.** Remembering the lovely deeds of Sri Gaurachandra,
he heaved long sighs and said, "Hari, Hari."

**99.** Finding the moment, Yadunandana said:
"Narottama has come here from the Kshetra."

**100.** Hearing Narottama's name, he opened his eyes
and saw Narottama weeping, having lost his composure.

**101.** Spreading his arms, he took Narottama in his embrace
and washed Narottama's body with the tears of his eyes.

**102.** Though burnt by the fire of separation, still he rejoiced,
and holding Narottama in his arms could not let him go.

**103.** Narottama, falling at Gadadhara's feet,
washed both his feet with the tears of his eyes.

**104.** What he asked when he had calmed Narottama,
Narottama told him point by point.

**105.** What went on in his heart as he heard it —
who has the power to describe it with one mouth?

**106.** Mercifully he said to Narottama again and again:
"All your desires will be fulfilled.

**107.** Prabhu will surely dance in your kirtana
and shower prema — you will see it with your eyes.

**108.** Go quickly to the village of Kheturi
and distribute the treasure of Sri Gaurachandra's prema."

**109.** Saying many such things, he was lost in great affection
and could not check the tears of prema from his eyes.

**110.** Sri Yadunandana and others, informing him with care,
took Narottama to Bharati's place.

**111.** They said to Narottama in sweet words:
"Here Sri Keshava Bharati lived.

**112.** In this place Prabhu had His head shaved;
here, from Bharati, He took sannyasa."

**113.** As soon as they said this, everyone's throat choked,
tears flowed from their eyes like the current of a river.

**114.** Narottama floated in the tears of both his eyes
and, almost senseless, fell and rolled on the ground.

**115.** His body grey with dust — seeing that state,
who could restrain his heart?

**116.** After a while all regained outer consciousness
and saw the wondrous Gaurachandra in the temple.[^4]

**117.** Prabhu, unable to bear the grief of His dear ones,
calmed them all by the grace of His form.

**118.** Narottama stayed there that day;
what befell him cannot be told.

**119.** How he took his leave in the morning —
who could keep composure enough to describe it?

**120.** Constantly remembering Nityananda-Balarama,
he went to Rarh, to the village of Ekachakra.

**121.** As he was entering the village, merciful Nityananda,
in the guise of an old brahmana, asked Narottama:

**122.** "Tell me your name, where have you come from,
on what business, where are you going, where do you live?"

**123.** Narottama said: "My name is Narottama.
I have come from the Kshetra; I have business in this village.

**124.** It is known that Nityananda descended here,
whose mother is Padma and father Hadai Pandita.

**125.** Where He was born and the places of His pastimes —
all that I have set my heart on seeing.

**126.** Across the Padmavati is a village named Kheturi;
I live there and shall go there from here."

**127.** Hearing Narottama's sweet, gentle speech,
He laughed within Himself but did not show it.

**128.** He said to Narottama: "I know everything;
I shall show you — come with me.

**129.** Look, here Nityananda with His friends,
dressed as a cowherd boy, playfully tended the cows.

**130.** Here Nityananda, taking the plough and pestle,
went about giving everyone the boon of fearlessness.

**131.** Here Nityananda performed the rasa-lila,
here He built the bridge and entered Lanka,

**132.** slew Ravana and rescued Sita —
look, here are the endless pastimes of Ayodhya.[^5]

**133.** As Balarama sports in Shvetadvipa,
so Nityananda sports in this place.

**134.** Look, here is Hado Pandita's house:
here Nityananda Raya was born.

**135.** Crawling about the courtyard,
here He played, seizing a snake by its hood.

**136.** Look, here was His first tonsure,
here the Enchanter of the worlds put on the sacred thread.

**137.** Here He worshipped Vishnu with care —
see, this is the temple of Vishnu.

**138.** Here the sannyasi ate in supreme bliss
and begged Nityananda from Hado Ojha.

**139.** Taking Nityananda, the sannyasi went along this road,
and the villagers ran to see Nitai.

**140.** Here all wept aloud
as the sannyasi quickly led Nityananda away.

**141.** Here the mother of Nityananda-chandra
rolled on the ground, crying, 'O my son! O my son!'

**142.** Here Hado Pandita, whose life was in his son,
overwhelmed with weeping, rolled on the ground.

**143.** Here Padmavati Devi lay unconscious,
and Hadai Pandita, steadying himself, consoled her.

**144.** O Narottama, the places I have shown you
are hard even for the gods to attain — who else knows them?

**145.** In this village of Ekachakra Nityananda Raya
sports even now — the fortunate see Him."

**146.** Saying this, the brahmana vanished right there;
not seeing him, Narottama pondered in distress.

**147.** Narottama said: "I have been struck by a thunderbolt!
He was here — where has he suddenly gone?

**148.** If I do not see the brahmana again,
I shall kindle a fire and give up my life in it.

**149.** Ah, brahmana, where have you gone, leaving me?" —
and Narottama wept, raising his arms.

**150.** The ocean of mercy, Nityananda, the Holder of the plough,
in that same guise of a brahmana came before his eyes.

**151.** The brahmana became Rama of boundless sweetness:
horn and staff in hand, a crest on His head, lovely attire.

**152.** At that moment He became Balarama-Nityananda —
His beauty has no comparison in the three worlds.

**153.** Smiling, He said softly to Narottama:
"You are dear to Me; I cannot deceive you.

**154.** All your desires will soon be fulfilled.
Do not reveal that you saw Me here."

**155.** Saying this, Prabhu vanished right there,
and Narottama stood like a painted doll.

**156.** What he became in the ecstasy of that vision —
all that cannot come from my mouth.

**157.** He stayed that day in the village of Ekachakra
and in the morning went on, having seen many marvels.

**158.** "Glory to the Lord of Ekachakra, the son of Rohini!
Glory to Nityananda, the life of the poor and suffering!" —

**159.** taking the Lord's names thus, he went along the road,
his face and chest washed by the streams of both his eyes.

**160.** On the way to Kheturi he crossed the Padmavati;
the joy of the people cannot be told.

**161.** Listen to all this continually and with care:
Narahari tells the *Narottama-vilasa*.

*Thus ends the fifth vilasa of the Narottama-vilasa.*

[^1]: Vyasa Chakravarti and Krishnavallabha were disciples of Srinivasa Acharya at Yajigrama.
[^2]: Kantaka-nagara is Katwa, where the Lord took sannyasa from Keshava Bharati; there Dasa Gadadhara served the Deity of Gauranga.
[^3]: So the text: after Vishnupriya's departure Dasa Gadadhara left Navadvipa and settled in Katwa.
[^4]: That is, the Deity of Gauranga in the temple at Katwa.
[^5]: Nityananda is Balarama; in His childhood games at Ekachakra He enacted the pastimes of Krishna and Rama (CB Adi-khanda).
