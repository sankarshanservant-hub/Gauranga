# Narottama-vilasa

*"The Pastimes of Narottama"*

*A poem by Narahari Chakravarti*

*Translated from the Bengali into English*

---

## Translator's Note

The *Narottama-vilasa* is a Bengali poem by Narahari Chakravarti (Ghanashyama Dasa), the author of the
*Bhakti-ratnakara* (18th century), about Srila Narottama Dasa Thakura Mahashaya. Its twelve vilasas tell of his
guru Lokanatha Gosvami; of Narottama's birth at Kheturi, his childhood and departure for Vrindavana;
of his initiation and study under Jiva Gosvami; of the books of the Gosvamis stolen at Vishnupura; of
Narottama's pilgrimages through Gauda and to Nilachala; of the installation of the six Deities at Kheturi and
the great festival attended by Jahnava-devi, Srinivasa Acharya and the associates of Mahaprabhu; of the
deliverance of the fallen — reviling brahmanas, kings and robbers; of Virachandra's visit, the departure of
Srinivasa and Ramachandra Kaviraja and, finally, of the hidden withdrawal of Narottama himself in the waters of
the Ganga at Gambhila. The twelfth vilasa lists the "branches" — Narottama's disciples and their disciples.

**Source.** The translation is based on a public-domain edition: ed. Rakhaldas Kaviratna, 2nd ed. (Calcutta,
1924). For ease of reading a Unicode typesetting of the same text (ed. Raghunatha Dasa Shastri, 2021) was used;
every portion was checked against the 1924 edition, and where the readings differ the 1924 reading is preferred
(significant differences are noted). The few passages found only in the 2021 edition (a Sanskrit passage from
the *Sangita-madhava* and a verse of Vishvanatha Chakravarti in vilasa 12; conversely, the author's signature in
vilasa 7 is found only in the 1924 edition) are translated in their place with a note. **Only the text of the
poem** has been translated: the editors' explanations and notes have been neither translated nor paraphrased.

**Principles.** The payara couplets are translated line by line, without rhyme, and numbered consecutively
within each vilasa (the numbering is the translator's own: the 1924 edition has almost none, and that of the
2021 edition contains many errors); each tripadi, song (pada) and Sanskrit shloka is given a single number. The
Sanskrit shlokas are translated; where the author names the source, it is given. The vilasa titles are
descriptive and supplied by the translator (except those the author gives in his colophons). Names and terms
follow `GLOSSARY.md`.

**Notes** at the end of each vilasa explain realia and persons, record divergences from the
*Bhakti-ratnakara*, *Prema-vilasa*, *Chaitanya-charitamrita* and *Chaitanya-bhagavata*, and note the readings of
the editions. The interpretation follows Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati; where the author says otherwise, the
text is rendered as it stands and the difference is noted. Thus the *Prema-vilasa* tells some episodes
differently: the date of Narottama's birth, the debate with the pandita Rupanarayana, and the vision at the
first kirtana in Kheturi (in Narahari, Gauranga Himself appears in the kirtana with His associates).

In all, 3,319 couplets and songs have been translated.
