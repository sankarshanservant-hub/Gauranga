# Vilasa 4. Narottama's Journey to Nilachala

*Narottama goes to Puri along the Lord's road; the old Vaishnava brahmana; meeting with Gopinatha Acharya and the associates in Puri; darshan of Jagannatha; Tota-Gopinatha and Mamu Gosani; Haridasa's samadhi; the dream of the Lord's kirtana before the chariot and His blessing; the return through Nrisimhapura and the meeting with Shyamananda*

**1.** Glory to Gaura, Nityananda and Advaita with their associates!
Be merciful, Prabhu, to this wretched beggar.

**2.** Glory, glory to the listeners, oceans of mercy!
Hear what I shall now relate.

**3.** Sri Thakura Mahashaya went to Nilachala,
his heart stirred as he thought of Chaitanya's pastimes.

**4.** By the road on which Chaitanya-chandra went to Nilachala
he went too, praising the good fortune of that road.

**5.** Where Prabhu had rested with His devotees,
there he stayed the night, talking of it.

**6.** The people along the way who had seen Sri Chaitanya-chand —
at the sight of them his heart could hold no composure.

**7.** Praising their good fortune again and again,
he went on, bowing to them all.

**8.** Seeing Narottama, all became attached to him;
all said, "This is a devotee of Chaitanya.

**9.** Sri Krishna Chaitanya Prabhu purifies the worlds;
who but His devotee could be like this?

**10.** Ah, what beauty, what a sweet gait!
The eyes are soothed to see him — wondrous are his ways of prema."

**11.** Saying this, people ran after him,
and Narottama sent them off with kind words.

**12.** Wherever Prabhu had manifested some pastime,
he heard of it from the people and stayed there.

**13.** In the morning he went on, and people went with him;
they could not be held back — there were crowds on the road.

**14.** Where Prabhu Nityananda broke the holy staff,
he went and was greatly overwhelmed with prema.[^1]

**15.** How Prabhu's staff came to be broken —
he heard the whole account from people's lips.

**16.** Generously rewarding all those people,
he went on with a wondrous gait, tears in his eyes.

**17.** Along that road came an old brahmana,
a great Vaishnava, expert in all the shastras.

**18.** Seeing Narottama's wondrous ways of prema,
he suddenly felt great love for him.

**19.** Coming slowly up to Narottama,
he spoke gently, looking at Narottama's face:

**20.** "What is your name, my child, where have you come from?"
Hearing this, he bowed respectfully and told him.

**21.** At Narottama's words the brahmana was greatly moved;
he embraced him and drenched him with the tears of his eyes.

**22.** Taking Narottama in his arms, he could not let him go,
and again spoke softly in the sweetest words:

**23.** "For many days I have been hearing about you,
and I had a great longing to see you, my child.

**24.** Today the Creator has been very gracious to me:
coming from the Kshetra, I have seen you on the road.

**25.** The devotees of Prabhu who are manifest in Nilachala
are all very kind to me.

**26.** There all of you are talked of constantly,
and filling my ears with it I soothe my life.

**27.** I heard how you came from Vrindavana,
and how on the way the books were stolen and recovered.

**28.** At the same time I heard that you would come to the Kshetra,
and I saw everyone eagerly awaiting you.

**29.** Gopinatha Acharya and the others at Kashi Mishra's house
have been speaking of you for many days.

**30.** In the village of Ramakeli Prabhu drew you to Himself,
and joy increased in Prabhu Nityananda's heart.

**31.** The Lord's devotees were amazed,
and since then all have longed to see you.

**32.** They all keep watching the road for you;
I set out from there only today.

**33.** Do not delay — go quickly;
I shall meet you there later."

**34.** As he was saying this, his son came there,
and he introduced him to Sri Thakura Mahashaya.

**35.** The affectionate brahmana told his son everything
and with great joy gave him to Narottama as a companion.

**36.** Taking leave, the brahmana went slowly on,
and Narottama put the dust of the brahmana's feet on his head.

**37.** Arriving in the Kshetra with the brahmana's son,
Narottama stood and gazed at the beauty of Narendra lake.

**38.** Remembering Prabhu's water-pastimes,[^2]
he lost his composure; tears streamed from his eyes continually.

**39.** Quickly bathing in the Narendra,
he went eagerly to the Lion Gate.

**40.** Meanwhile Gopinatha Acharya and Prabhu's other associates
at that very moment came out onto the road to the Lion Gate.

**41.** Sri Shikhi Mahiti says to Mangaraja:
"Why has joy suddenly risen in my heart?"

**42.** Kanai Khutiya says: "I do not understand the cause,
but such auspicious signs portend a great treasure."

**43.** Gopinatha Acharya says to Vaninatha:
"Narottama will surely come here today."

**44.** At that moment the most worthy brahmana's son,
coming on ahead, brought the news of Narottama.

**45.** What each became on hearing the news of Narottama
cannot be described.

**46.** The brahmana's son went back to Narottama
and from a distance told him who they all were.

**47.** Narottama, seeing them all,
could not hold himself up; tears poured from his eyes.

**48.** He bowed again and again, falling to the ground;
seeing that state, everyone's heart wept.

**49.** Gopinatha Acharya and the others, losing their composure,
took Narottama in their arms and were bathed in tears.

**50.** What happened at the meeting with Narottama
could not be described with hundreds of thousands of mouths.

**51.** Calming Narottama in many ways,
they took him to see Jagannatha.

**52.** As he entered the Lion Gate, Narottama
saw Patita-pavana and fell prostrate on the ground.[^3]

**53.** Seeing Sri Nrisimhadeva, he shed tears;
what arose in his heart cannot be told.

**54.** At the darshan of Jagannatha he lost his composure:
streams flowed from his eyes, wondrous bhavas arose.

**55.** Jagannatha and Balarama together with Subhadra
sported on the throne, the abode of bliss.

**56.** Sri Padmalochana, the great ocean of mercy,
showed Narottama the utmost mercy.

**57.** A servant of Jagannatha, knowing the Lord's intent,
brought a garland-prasada and gave it to Narottama.

**58.** All the servants of Sri Jagannathadeva,
seeing Narottama's state, were bathed in tears.

**59.** Moment by moment Narottama lost more of his composure,
unable to check the streams from his eyes, like a river.

**60.** Consoling Narottama, Gopinatha Acharya
took him from the holy temple to his own house.

**61.** At once giving him an experienced man as companion,
he sent him to have darshan of Gopinatha and the samadhi.

**62.** He let everyone know of Narottama's coming
and had various kinds of maha-prasada brought.

**63.** Meanwhile Narottama set off quickly,
and on the way he saw several people coming.

**64.** With great pain in their hearts
they said to one another: "Alas, what has happened!

**65.** What a height of happiness we saw here!
Now the Creator has turned everything upside down in Nilachala.

**66.** The devotees of Sri Gaurachandra, purifiers of the worlds,
are passing from sight one after another.

**67.** Gopinatha Acharya and the other great Vaishnavas —
we have seen how all of them have grown frail."

**68.** Someone said: "I have come from Gopinatha;
what I saw there I cannot tell.

**69.** Unable to bear his sorrow, Sri Mamu Gosani[^4]
lies in one spot as if dead.

**70.** That beautiful body has withered;
it seems he will soon pass from sight."

**71.** Hearing this talk, Narottama, distraught,
lamented so much that I cannot describe it.

**72.** Losing his composure, his body barely holding up,
he went to Tota and saw Gopinatha.

**73.** How sweetly, how gracefully He sits!
Who could keep composure, glancing at Him even once?

**74.** His glossy dark body surpasses a fresh cloud,
the sweetness of His face enchants millions of Kandarpas.

**75.** The beauty entered Narottama's heart;
he lost his composure and was bathed in tears.

**76.** He bowed many times, falling to the ground,
and the pujari brought and gave him a garland-prasada.

**77.** To the place where Sri Pandita Gosvami's seat is
his companion took him.

**78.** Rolling on the ground near the seat,
he bowed many times in distress.

**79.** Unable to check the tears that flowed from his eyes,
he raised his arms and cried again and again:

**80.** "Ah, Prabhu, Pandita Gosani Gadadhara!
You did not come before the eyes of a sinner like me!"

**81.** Saying many such things, he wept aloud —
at that weeping wood and stone split apart.

**82.** Sri Mamu Gosani, who had lain unconscious,
heaved a long sigh and rose up, weeping.

**83.** He asked everyone, "Tell me, who is weeping?"
All said, "Narottama has come from Gauda."

**84.** Hearing the name of Narottama, weeping and weeping,
he took Narottama in his arms and could not be calm.

**85.** He threw himself down on the ground;
a tumult of weeping rose in Gopinatha's house.

**86.** By Prabhu's will, growing calm after a while,
he asked after Narottama's welfare, gazing at his face.

**87.** Though his life hung in doubt from cruel sorrow,
still, seeing Narottama, he rejoiced.

**88.** Hearing Narottama's words, he was filled with prema
and entrusted Narottama to Gopinatha's feet.

**89.** He bade him: "Go quickly to see the samadhi:
the Acharya is there, watching the road."

**90.** Hearing this, Narottama bowed sorrowfully to the ground
and went with his companion to the seashore.

**91.** Seeing the samadhi of Haridasa Thakura,
he fell to the ground and wept bitterly.

**92.** In great grief he said again and again:
"I was deprived of that happiness — such is my evil fate!"

**93.** Saying many such things, he shed tears incessantly;
whose heart would not melt to see that state?

**94.** The Vaishnavas who were there in service of the samadhi
with great effort calmed Narottama

**95.** and sent him to Gopinatha Acharya's house;
Narottama, overwhelmed, bowed and went.

**96.** The people of the Kshetra, seeing Narottama on the road,
left all their work and went along with him.

**97.** Narottama, honouring them all,
went quickly to Gopinatha Acharya's house.

**98.** Gopinatha Acharya, full of affection,
seated him beside himself and spoke sweet words:

**99.** "Everyone longs at heart to see you;
rest a moment and then go to their houses."

**100.** Meanwhile, hearing of Narottama's arrival, all
were most eager to see him.

**101.** What can I say of the state they were in, in Nilachala!
Without Prabhu they had no desire for food or water.

**102.** With great difficulty they kept their bodies alive,
always rolling on the ground, their eyes streaming.

**103.** They sighed long and often, very weak,
unable to walk, their bodies tottering.

**104.** Yet to see Narottama at Gopinatha's house
they came — love filled their bodies with strength.

**105.** At that time Narottama with his companion,
going there, saw them all coming along the road.

**106.** Narottama asked his companion
who each one was, and he told him all.

**107.** Narottama bowed at the feet of them all,
and all of them embraced Narottama.

**108.** Holding him in their arms, they went into the house
and drenched Narottama's body with the tears of their eyes.

**109.** Narottama, seeing and touching them,
could not hold himself up; streams flowed from both his eyes.

**110.** Gopinatha Acharya with the greatest care
calmed them all and seated them well.

**111.** All asked Narottama about his welfare,
and Narottama told everything from beginning to end.

**112.** What their state became on hearing it —
what can I say? Only the fortunate saw it.

**113.** Gopinatha Acharya said eagerly to all:
"Partake of Sri maha-prasada together with Narottama."

**114.** Hearing this, all, with great affection at heart,
sat down with Narottama to honour maha-prasada.

**115.** By Prabhu's will they ate a little prasada
and with most loving words fed Narottama.

**116.** After rinsing their mouths, all went to their dwellings,
and they told Narottama to rest.

**117.** Having rested, Sri Thakura Mahashaya
bathed and so on, knowing it was time for darshan.

**118.** Kanai Khutiya took
Sri Thakura Mahashaya to Jagannatha's temple.

**119.** The evening arati and everything up to the putting to rest
Narottama watched, standing apart.

**120.** With Kanai Khutiya and many others
he came to Gopinatha Acharya's house.

**121.** No one could leave Narottama and go,
but at the Acharya's word they went to their own homes.

**122.** The Acharya said to Narottama: "This place is secluded;
lie down here and sleep now."

**123.** The Acharya's fatherly love cannot be told;
when Narottama had lain down, he went to his own house.

**124.** But sleep would not come to Narottama:
he could not check his great anxiety and grief.

**125.** When by Prabhu's will he dozed a little,
in a dream he saw his Beloved before the chariot:

**126.** Krishna Chaitanya, enchanter of the worlds, Nitai,
Sri Advaita, Pandita Gadadhara Gosani,

**127.** Srivasa Pandita, Murari Gupta, Govinda,
Haridasa, Kashi Mishra, Ramananda Raya,

**128.** Vasudeva Sarvabhauma Bhattacharya,
Kashishvara, the generous Jagadisha Pandita,

**129.** Vasu Ghosha, Mukunda, Madhava, Vakreshvara,
Gauridasa, Mahesha Pandita, Damodara,

**130.** Svarupa Gosani, Shuklambara Brahmachari,
Dasa Gadadhara, Yadu, Shridhara, Kamsari,

**131.** Suryadasa, Ramai, Sundara, Dhananjaya,
Ramananda Vasu, Ghosha, Shankara, Sanjaya,

**132.** Lokanatha, Bhugarbha, Sri Rupa, Sanatana,
Sri Jiva, Gopala Bhatta, Acharya-nandana,

**133.** Krishnadasa Brahmachari, Pandita Raghava,
Paramananda Bhattacharya, Acharya Madhava,

**134.** Raghunatha Bhatta, Sri Tapana, Raghunatha,
King Sri Prataparudra, Acharya Gopinatha,

**135.** Sri Shikhi Mahiti and others renowned in the world —
all the devotees of Gauda, Vraja, Utkala, the south and other places

**136.** were sporting with Prabhu.
What can I say? The supreme bliss cannot be described.

**137.** How wonderful is the dancing before Jagannatha's chariot!
In the middle Gaurachandra, all around His dear ones.

**138.** How wondrous the beauty of Gaura with His associates!
There is nothing in the three worlds to compare with it.

**139.** At a mere sign from Prabhu His dear associates
began the lovely singing of the name.

**140.** Mridangas and other instruments played, sweet as nectar,
and on all sides "Jaya! Jaya!" resounded incessantly.

**141.** All the gandharvas and kinnaras in human guise
danced, sang and joyfully played various instruments.

**142.** The ocean of the happiness of sankirtana surged up
and flooded everything — heaven, earth and the netherworld.

**143.** Sri Krishna Chaitanya dances in sankirtana —
who in the three worlds does not long to see it?

**144.** Countless men and women run from all sides,
the gods with their consorts shower flowers.

**145.** The lame leap and strut about proudly,
those blind from birth see the sweetness of Gauranga.

**146.** He from whose mouth no word would come
calls "Gaurachandra!" again and again.

**147.** He whose eyes would shed no tear even if he were cut,
he too, hearing Gaura's virtues, is bathed in tears.

**148.** Hearing the lovely kirtana that purifies the worlds,
not even beasts and birds could keep still.

**149.** Narottama, standing to one side, watched,
overwhelmed with bliss, streams flowing from his eyes.

**150.** Seeing Narottama's state, Prabhu, in prema,
took both his hands and spoke softly:

**151.** "You will manifest unearthly song and music,
on hearing which all will rejoice.

**152.** You will see, when you perform kirtana:
just so I shall dance with everyone.

**153.** My inner longing will be revealed in song and music,
and the supreme rasika sadhus will always relish it.

**154.** Never worry about anything:
your desire will be fulfilled — I have said it.

**155.** Do not delay — go quickly to the land of Gauda
and manifest bhakti in every way.

**156.** Whoever comes and takes shelter of you
will soon gain the priceless treasure of prema.

**157.** With Ramachandra, the son of Chiranjiva Sena,
you will have a wondrous friendship.

**158.** What more shall I say to you, Narottama?
All your good and ill are Mine."[^5]

**159.** Seeing the utmost mercy shown to Narottama,
the ocean of everyone's bliss surged up.

**160.** Nityananda, Advaita, Gadadhara, Haridasa,
Sarvabhauma, Ramananda Raya, Srivasa,

**161.** Vakreshvara and all the others dear to Prabhu
embraced Narottama firmly.

**162.** Narottama floated in the tears of both his eyes
and, falling at their feet, deemed himself blessed.

**163.** Prabhu's associates, calming Narottama,
took his hand and spoke many words to him with affection.

**164.** All were agreed to send him to Gauda —
and then the sleep broke, and he was distraught with separation.

**165.** After a while Narottama, growing calm,
quickly finished his morning duties.

**166.** Gopinatha Acharya, together with Shikhi Mahiti,
quickly sent him to have darshan of Jagannatha.

**167.** Seeing the mangala-arati,
he could not hold himself up — his heart overflowed.

**168.** As soon as he thought, "How am I to go to Gauda?"
Jagannatha at once gave him a garland as His permission.

**169.** Receiving the garland-prasada, he reflected:
"Without doubt Prabhu has given me leave."

**170.** Staying a while and bowing to Jagannatha,
he went to Gopinatha Acharya's house.

**171.** Wherever Prabhu's associates were staying,
he bowed at the feet of each and came back to his lodging.

**172.** What Prabhu had said to Gopinatha in a dream,
he was eager to tell Narottama.

**173.** Composing himself, he said slowly to Narottama:
"Prabhu has directed you to go quickly to Gauda."

**174.** Having said much in this way, he fixed a day,
and the mahantas of the Kshetra gathered together.

**175.** Sending Narottama off to Gauda,
all said so much that it cannot be told.

**176.** At the parting, taking Narottama by the hand,
they spoke sweet words with great affection:

**177.** "The longing of our hearts is fulfilled — we have seen you;
but Srinivasa we shall not see again with our eyes.

**178.** We have heard that Duhkhini Krishnadasa is most worthy
and that he has recently received the name Shyamananda.

**179.** We had a great longing to see him" —
saying this, all were drenched with tears.

**180.** Narottama, seeing their state,
fell to the ground and bowed, weeping and weeping.

**181.** Calming themselves and calming Narottama,
all sent him on his way, remembering Krishna Chaitanya.

**182.** His companions, with great love,
went ahead carrying Sri maha-prasada.

**183.** What each one became after sending Narottama off
cannot be described.

**184.** Narottama went on as if dead,
and coming to the Narendra he wept bitterly.

**185.** The brahmana he had seen when coming to the Kshetra
he saw on that same road with his son.

**186.** As soon as he put the dust of the brahmana's feet on his head,
the brahmana embraced him and spoke softly:

**187.** "O Narottama, you are dearer to me than life!
I heard that you are leaving for Gauda today.

**188.** Having finished an important task, I hurried here;
by Jagannatha's will we have met on the road.

**189.** Otherwise I would have died, burning with grief at heart" —
saying this, he would not let him out of his arms.

**190.** After a while the old brahmana, distraught at heart,
gave him many blessings and let him go.

**191.** The brahmana went some distance with Narottama,
unable to part; his sorrow grew greatly.

**192.** Narottama with great effort turned him back
and went on quickly, much distressed.

**193.** Resting two days at Jajpur,
after some days he came to the village of Nrisimhapura.[^6]

**194.** Someone, coming from afar, told Shyamananda:
"Sri Thakura Mahashaya has come from the Kshetra."

**195.** As soon as he heard it, Shyamananda was overjoyed
and quickly went forward with his people to meet him.

**196.** Seeing each other, both lost their composure
and, bowing to each other, were bathed in tears.

**197.** Narottama took Shyamananda in his arms
and could not let him go — his heart overflowed with joy.

**198.** All the people, seeing this wondrous meeting,
could not check the ceaseless tears from their eyes.

**199.** Some said: "O brother, how wondrous!
Never since I was born have I seen such love."

**200.** Some said: "What I heard, that I have seen;
all the wishes of my heart — to whom shall I tell them?"

**201.** Some said: "O brother, ever since I heard of him
I had a great longing to see him once."

**202.** Some said: "Great is our good fortune —
that is why Sri Thakura Mahashaya has come here."

**203.** Some said: "Shall we have such good fortune —
shall we see Acharya Thakura even once?"

**204.** Some said: "Oh, the wish will be fulfilled:
Sri Acharya Srinivasa will give us his darshan."

**205.** Saying many such things, no one's mind was at rest;
the villagers came running in crowds.

**206.** Shyamananda gladly gave Thakura Mahashaya
a secluded lodging for fear of the crowds.

**207.** Still, to see Narottama
many people came — they could not be held back.

**208.** The merit of these people cannot be told:
they gained such a jewel by Shyamananda's mercy.

**209.** Seeing that by Shyamananda's mercy this land was blessed,
Sri Thakura Mahashaya was very happy.

**210.** Having bathed and so on and grown calm,
Narottama sat down with Shyamananda.

**211.** Finding the moment, Shyamananda carefully
told Sri Thakura Mahashaya, point by point:

**212.** "Acharya Thakura has gone from Vana-Vishnupura
to Yajigrama in these last few days.

**213.** Yesterday, in the first watch of the day,
came his gracious letter — see it, Mahashaya."

**214.** At the sight of the letter he overflowed with joy,
and as he read it his eyes were bathed in tears.

**215.** The Mahashaya read the letter with great care,
and Shyamananda, in prema, went on to tell him:

**216.** "From Sri Ambika, Prabhu has graciously[^7]
sent Sri maha-prasada together with a letter."

**217.** Reading the letter, Narottama was bathed in tears
and in prema praised Shyamananda's good fortune.

**218.** Bowing again and again to the Sri maha-prasada,
he ate it with boundless joy.

**219.** Sri Thakura Mahashaya told his companions:
"Bring the prasada here."

**220.** Taking the maha-prasada of Sri Jagannatha,
he put it into Shyamananda's mouth with great joy.

**221.** Having honoured the Sri maha-prasada with great care,
Narottama said softly to Shyamananda:

**222.** "The associates of Prabhu who live in Nilachala
are burned incessantly by the fire of separation.

**223.** Their state cannot be described:
only by Prabhu's will does life remain in them.

**224.** All of them long to see you:
do not delay — go quickly to Nilachala.

**225.** Having seen their feet there,
distribute the priceless treasure of prema in Utkala.

**226.** In some days I shall send a letter;
you will come to the village of Kheturi with your people."

**227.** Saying many such things, he stayed two days,
and all these words became known everywhere.

**228.** What the two were like at the parting
cannot be described with one mouth.

**229.** Sri Shyamananda's disciple Rasika Murari
stood to one side and wept, tears flowing from his eyes.

**230.** Sri Thakura Mahashaya, with great affection,
embraced him and showed him great mercy.

**231.** All who had taken shelter at Sri Shyamananda's feet —
how he loved them cannot be described.

**232.** Gazing and gazing at Sri Thakura Mahashaya,
all in distress fell to the ground, rolling about.

**233.** Taking the dust of his two feet on their heads,
all, with hands on their heads, sobbed and wept.

**234.** Sri Thakura Mahashaya set out for Gauda,
unable to be calm, streams flowing from both his eyes.

**235.** Here Shyamananda wept, fallen on the ground,
and however he tried, could not be calm.

**236.** How wondrous were his ways — they cannot be understood!
With a distraught heart he set out for Nilachala.

**237.** Shyamananda went in prema to Nilachala,
and Sri Thakura Mahashaya came to Gauda.

**238.** How Shyamananda went to Nilachala —
see it in detail in the book *Bhakti-ratnakara*.

**239.** Listen to all this continually and with care:
Narahari tells the *Narottama-vilasa*.

*Thus ends the fourth vilasa of the Narottama-vilasa.*

[^1]: The place where Nityananda broke the Lord's sannyasa staff (CC Madhya 5), on the Bhargavi river near Puri (Dandabhanga).
[^2]: The Lord's water-pastimes with His devotees in Narendra lake (CC Madhya 14; Antya 10).
[^3]: Patita-pavana ("Purifier of the fallen"), the form of Jagannatha at the Lion Gate of the temple.
[^4]: Following the 1924 edition, "শ্রীমামু গোসাঞি": Mamu Thakura, the servant of Tota-Gopinatha after Gadadhara Pandita (by tradition his relative and disciple); the 2021 edition has "শ্রীমান্ গোসাঞি."
[^5]: That is, "I take responsibility for all that befalls you" — a sign of complete protection.
[^6]: Nrisimhapura, a village in Utkala (Orissa) where Shyamananda was then living.
[^7]: "Prabhu" here is Shyamananda's guru, Hridaya Chaitanya of Ambika.
