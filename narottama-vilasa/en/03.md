# Vilasa 3. The Books in Vishnupura; Narottama's Journey through Gauda

*Jiva sends Srinivasa, Narottama and Shyamananda with the books to Gauda; the theft of the books by King Vira Hambira; Narottama and Shyamananda in Kheturi; Shyamananda with Hridaya Chaitanya in Ambika and in Orissa; Narottama in Navadvipa, Shantipura, Ambika and Khardaha*

**1.** Glory to Gaura, Nityananda and Advaita with their associates!
Be merciful, Prabhu, to this wretched beggar.

**2.** Glory, glory to the listeners, oceans of mercy!
Hear what I shall now relate.

**3.** Sri Jiva Gosvami together with all the mahantas
fixed an auspicious day to send the books to Gauda.

**4.** He entrusted the books to Srinivasa Acharya,
through whom Prabhu wished to distribute them.

**5.** Sri Thakura Mahashaya, in a verse of his own,
described this — it is known to all.

**6.** Thus:[^1]
*Through one power — Sri Rupa and the others — Prabhu revealed these books,*
*and through a supreme power named Sri Srinivasa He spreads them;*
*He who out of mercy manifested these two powers on earth —*
*when will Sri Chaitanya, the ocean of compassion, come before my eyes?*

**7.** Sri Jiva Gosvami, deep as millions of oceans,
was distraught at heart with separation, yet outwardly most steady.

**8.** Having had Srinivasa take leave of everyone,
he sent him off to Gauda at an auspicious moment.

**9.** Lokanatha Gosvami, full of affection,
entrusted Narottama to Srinivasa.

**10.** Again and again he told Narottama
to perform the service of the Deity, sankirtana and good conduct.

**11.** Hearing much of this kind, Narottama rejoiced;
who could describe the happiness Srinivasa felt?

**12.** Sri Jiva Gosvami, entrusting to Srinivasa
Narottama and Shyamananda, was overwhelmed with great prema.

**13.** He said to Srinivasa: "These two are yours;
in every way their care rests with you."

**14.** To Shyamananda he gave the order: "Having gone to Gauda,
you will go on to Utkala by way of Sri Ambikapuri."

**15.** I cannot describe all this here;
you will learn it in detail from the book *Bhakti-ratnakara*.[^2]

**16.** Having bowed at the feet of all the mahantas,
the three set out with the books of bhakti.

**17.** Sri Jiva Gosvami and the others, distraught at heart,
quickly went with them as far as Mathura.

**18.** They sent ahead a cart filled with books,
with eleven armed men of Vraja to guard it.

**19.** Arriving in Mathura, they all spent the night there,
and the people of Mathura were overjoyed.

**20.** What happened at the parting in the morning
not even millions upon millions of mouths could describe.

**21.** Srinivasa, Narottama and Shyamananda, the three,
in some days reached the land of Gauda.

**22.** By a forest road, near Vana-Vishnupura,
they came to a village in the midst of the forest.

**23.** There they passed much of the night on guard,
but by Prabhu's will they all fell asleep.

**24.** Someone told King Vira Hambira:
"A rich man has come with a cart full of jewels."

**25.** Hearing this, the king gleefully sent robbers at once
and without difficulty had the jewel-like books brought to him.

**26.** Without taking the books out of their chests,
the king, merely on seeing the chests, became agitated.

**27.** Again and again he bowed, falling to the ground;
the king could not understand what was happening in his heart.

**28.** The king said: "What has happened in my heart?
I do not know what jewel lies within these chests."

**29.** Saying many such things, the king shed tears;
Bhakti-devi showed him many auspicious signs.

**30.** After much deliberation within himself,
the king quickly opened the book-chests in private.

**31.** Seeing the jewel-like books in the chests,
the king spoke in great remorse, weeping:

**32.** "Alas, alas, what misfortune is mine!
What great soul have I, a wretch, caused to suffer!

**33.** If I am fortunate enough to see him,
I shall give back the jewel-like books and take shelter of him."

**34.** Thus the king spoke much, sitting in private.
Meanwhile, the books having been stolen, all awoke.

**35.** The state all fell into when the books could not be seen —
how could I, a wretch, describe it with one mouth?

**36.** They threw themselves on the ground and wept aloud;
no one could be calmed in any way.

**37.** Acharya Thakura, summoning some composure,
addressed everyone with sweet words:

**38.** "Carefully we came by a difficult road without mishap,
and here suddenly we all fell asleep.

**39.** We did not even know who stole the books or when;
surely there is some hidden purpose in this."

**40.** Sri Thakura Mahashaya said quietly:
"It seems that by this pretext mercy will come to this land."

**41.** At that moment a divine voice spoke from the sky:
"Do not worry; the books will be recovered without difficulty."

**42.** Meanwhile someone said softly to the Acharya:
"This is the king's doing — go to Vana-Vishnupura."

**43.** Hearing this, Srinivasa Acharya consoled everyone
and sent a man to Vrindavana with a letter.

**44.** With great care he urged Sri Thakura Mahashaya
again and again to go quickly to Kheturi.

**45.** To Shyamananda he said, full of prema:
"Go to Kheturi, and then quickly on to Utkala.

**46.** I shall search for the books in Vana-Vishnupura
and soon send news that they have been found.

**47.** Now do not worry about anything at all."
Saying this, he sent the two of them off.

**48.** The two did not transgress the Acharya's word
and, distraught with separation, set out.

**49.** Arriving in Sri Kheturi, Sri Thakura Mahashaya
could not let Shyamananda leave him even for a moment.

**50.** Meanwhile in Vana-Vishnupura Srinivasa Acharya
bestowed his grace on Sri Vira Hambira.

**51.** Giving back the jewel-like books, the king took shelter
and with his household became greatly devoted to bhakti.

**52.** I have told this here in brief;
I have described it at length in the *Bhakti-ratnakara*.

**53.** This news from Vana-Vishnupura
became known everywhere, and all were amazed to hear it.

**54.** Sri Acharya Thakura, with great joy at heart,
sent to Vrindavana a letter that the books had been recovered.

**55.** And to where Sri Thakura Mahashaya and Shyamananda were
he quickly sent a letter with this news.

**56.** As soon as he read the letter, Sri Thakura Mahashaya
was immersed in a joy that cannot be told.

**57.** Shyamananda, overcome with joy, for a long time
sang and danced with arms upraised.

**58.** Greatly delighted was the son of Purushottama Datta,
named Sri Santosha Datta, an abode of virtues.

**59.** He was Sri Narottama's cousin,
to whom Krishnananda Datta had given the kingdom.

**60.** So Raja Sri Santosha, in celebration,
gave many gifts to brahmanas and good people.

**61.** Sri Thakura Mahashaya was pleased with him
and quickly sent a letter to Vana-Vishnupura.

**62.** After that Shyamananda took his leave;
the sorrow of that separation — who could describe it?

**63.** The words they spoke to each other at parting —
hearing them, beasts and birds wept.

**64.** Sri Thakura Mahashaya, greatly anxious,
gave him a man to accompany him to Utkala.

**65.** Shyamananda set out with an aching heart
and by way of Navadvipa came to Ambika-nagara.

**66.** At the sight of the temple of Sri Chaitanya and Nityananda
he was overcome with prema; streams flowed from his eyes.

**67.** Seeing his state, some worthy man
went and told Sri Hridaya Chaitanya:

**68.** "Your Duhkhini Krishnadasa has come;[^3]
I have seen a wondrous manifestation of prema-bhakti.

**69.** Seeing the holy temple from afar, he falls to the ground
and bows again and again in great humility.

**70.** How he floats in the tears from both his eyes!
He is coming from a distance; I have hurried ahead."

**71.** Hearing this, Thakura, with great joy at heart,
said again and again, "Bring him quickly.

**72.** My heart is always anxious for him;
his zeal for bhakti cannot be put into words.

**73.** Having received the mantra of initiation, he stayed here some time
and won over Nitai and Chaitanya-chand with his prema.

**74.** With great care I sent him to Vrindavana;
there he studied the shastras of bhakti.

**75.** He wrote to me of his inner longings,
and seeing his yearning I gave him a corresponding command.

**76.** He devoted himself ceaselessly to service in the kunja,
found happiness, and received the name 'Shyamananda.'[^4]

**77.** In Vrindavana all showed him great mercy.
He wrote in advance that he would come here.

**78.** Nitai and Chaitanya, in their mercy, will accomplish through him
a work that will pervade the world.

**79.** I tell you: he is my dear disciple;
after many days you will see him."

**80.** Even as he said this, Shyamananda arrived
and fell reverently at his feet.

**81.** Sri Hridaya Chaitanya Thakura, with affection,
placed his holy feet on Shyamananda's head.

**82.** As he was about to embrace him, Shyamananda went and stood at a distance,
awash in tears, with great joy in his heart.

**83.** Still Thakura embraced him at once
and in prema led him into the courtyard of Prabhu's temple.

**84.** He entrusted him to the feet of Nityananda and Chaitanya,
and seeing Prabhu, Shyamananda lost his composure.

**85.** The ecstatic transformations he showed I cannot describe;
Thakura brought him along to his own quarters.

**86.** He gladly gave Shyamananda the remnants of his own meal,
and Shyamananda partook of them in supreme bliss.

**87.** Then Sri Thakura asked for news,
and Shyamananda told everything from beginning to end.

**88.** The joy he felt at the words of his very dear disciple Shyamananda
cannot be told.

**89.** For some days Shyamananda stayed with his guru
and served his guru with great gladness of heart.

**90.** One day the merciful Hridaya Chaitanya
spoke to Shyamananda in the sweetest words:

**91.** "Now do not delay in going to Utkala:
much will be accomplished through you."

**92.** Saying this, he took him before Nitai and Chaitanya,
brought a garland-prasada and gave it to Shyamananda.

**93.** Infusing great power into him, he sent him off,
and Shyamananda, distraught, wept aloud.

**94.** How Shyamananda went to Utkala
cannot be described here at length.

**95.** The pashandis and evil-doers who were in Utkala —
Shyamananda delivered them all.

**96.** He made many disciples, Sri Rasikananda and others,
and by a trace of their mercy the land was blessed.

**97.** Here I have told all this briefly;
I have described it at length in the *Bhakti-ratnakara*.

**98.** Now I shall tell how Shyamananda, with gladness of heart,
came from Sri Kheturi to the land of Sri Utkala.

**99.** The man who had come with him from Sri Kheturi
he sent back with a letter of news.

**100.** Meanwhile in Kheturi Sri Thakura Mahashaya
was greatly anxious without Shyamananda.

**101.** Receiving from him a letter with news of great good fortune,
he quickly sent it on to Vana-Vishnupura.

**102.** On reading the letter, Thakura, with great joy at heart,
sent his own letter to Shyamananda.

**103.** He sent a letter to Sri Thakura Mahashaya as well,
and reading it, the Mahashaya was greatly delighted.

**104.** The Mahashaya quickly sent a letter in return
and set out from Kheturi for Navadvipa.

**105.** In prema he went along the road like a maddened elephant,
his face and chest washed by the streams of both his eyes.

**106.** Whoever saw Sri Thakura Mahashaya but once
floated in an ocean of pure prema-bhakti.

**107.** Gazing on his beauty, people could not leave his company:
when he came to a village, everyone came running.

**108.** Saying many things, all gazed at him,
and when he left the village everyone grieved.

**109.** Thus after some days, coming near Navadvipa,
he lamented bitterly in great distress:

**110.** "O merciful Prabhu, to make me suffer
You had me born on earth at such a time!

**111.** I did not get to see these pastimes in Nadia!" —
as soon as he said this, tears flowed from his eyes.

**112.** Slowly, weeping in sorrow, he went on,
and entering Navadvipa he saw a marvel:

**113.** in every house — what joy and festivity!
An incessant clamour of "Hari! Hari!";

**114.** men and women, with great gladness of heart,
going from all sides to Prabhu's dwelling;

**115.** Gaura Raya sporting with His associates,
Nadia an ocean of the happiness of sankirtana.

**116.** Having watched this for a while, he then saw
the town of Nadia floating in an ocean of sorrow.[^5]

**117.** "What did I see! What did I see!" he said again and again,
unable to walk, tears flowing from his eyes.

**118.** After a while the Mahashaya, reflecting,
went on a little and asked for Prabhu's house.

**119.** Some, weeping, with bowed heads, said:
"Look, there is Prabhu's house; go this way."

**120.** Seeing Prabhu's house, Narottama wept;
streams flowed from both eyes like the current of a river.

**121.** Along that road came the brahmachari Shuklambara;
seeing Narottama, he was stirred at heart.

**122.** Narottama bowed, falling to the ground;
"Tell me who you are," he said, and embraced him.

**123.** When Narottama told who he was,
he spoke with the greatest affection, weeping:

**124.** "When Gaurachandra went to the village of Ramakeli,
He, mad with great prema, drew you to Himself.

**125.** Who can understand Prabhu's doings?
Already then He made your name known.

**126.** O my child Narottama, to see you
was the great longing in the hearts of all the mahantas.

**127.** In separation from Prabhu no one's mind is at peace;
some have passed from sight within a short time."

**128.** Saying this, he made himself known
and brought Narottama to meet the devotees of Prabhu.

**129.** Narottama bowed at the feet of all,
and all embraced Narottama with love.

**130.** Though distraught with the great pain of separation,
still, seeing Narottama, they were happy.

**131.** With much affection they asked for news,
and Narottama told everything from beginning to end.

**132.** Damodara Pandita and others dear to Prabhu
could not let Narottama go even for a moment.

**133.** For some days Narottama stayed in the town of Nadia
in the houses of Prabhu's dear associates.

**134.** How the Mahashaya lamented incessantly
cannot be described with one mouth.

**135.** The devotees he had not seen and wept for —
they all appeared to him in dreams.

**136.** How much mercy they showed Narottama
I have no power to tell in detail.

**137.** The mahantas who were manifest in Navadvipa
showed him great mercy, keeping him by them.

**138.** After some days, in great distress,
they bade him farewell, speaking sweet words:

**139.** "So that we might meet you, Prabhu
has kept life in our bodies through such pain.

**140.** But with Srinivasa we did not meet again."
Saying this, their throats choked and tears streamed from their eyes.

**141.** With great tenderness, gazing at Narottama's face,
all took leave of him — their hearts were breaking.

**142.** Placing the feet of all on his head, Narottama
went — the state he was in cannot be described.

**143.** Going to Prabhu's house with a troubled heart,
he saw the servants and maidservants — they were as if dead.

**144.** Seeing Narottama, all, distraught at heart,
said: "Much will be accomplished through you."

**145.** Saying this, their throats choked, streams flowing from their eyes,
they sent Narottama off with a gesture of the hand.

**146.** Narottama, distraught, wept aloud,
fell in Prabhu's courtyard and rolled in the dust.

**147.** After a while, checking his tears,
he set out along the road to Shantipura.

**148.** The marvel he saw on entering the village
I have no power to describe.

**149.** Going to the house of Prabhu Advaita,
he bowed at the feet of Sri Achyutananda.

**150.** He embraced Narottama, showed him great mercy,
asked for his news and introduced him to his dear ones.

**151.** He commanded: "Go to Nilachala and come back soon,
and spread the lovely kirtana, a treasury of rasa."

**152.** Saying this, he shed ceaseless streams from his eyes;
his withered body swayed in the wind.

**153.** Narottama, bowing his head at the feet of all,
took his leave and went on slowly.

**154.** Coming to the village of Harinadi and crossing the Ganga,
he reached Ambika and asked for the Pandita's house.[^6]

**155.** Someone said, "You have arrived — it is very close,"
and seeing Narottama, people's happiness grew greatly.

**156.** A worthy man, going ahead very quickly,
bowed and said to Sri Hridaya Chaitanya:

**157.** "I have seen a marvel — a beautiful man
whose body is filled with prema for Gaura and Nityananda.

**158.** He is coming here, asking the way;
so many streams flow from his eyes that he cannot walk."

**159.** Sri Hridaya Chaitanya, hearing this,
knew that Narottama had come here.

**160.** In prema he hurried out to the outer gate,
saw Narottama coming — and his heart was soothed.

**161.** Narottama, seeing Sri Hridaya Chaitanya,
could not hold himself up and fell at his feet.

**162.** Sri Hridaya Chaitanya, taking him by the shoulders,
embraced Narottama and sprinkled him with tears from his eyes.

**163.** He quickly led him into Prabhu's temple
and gave him darshan of Nityananda and Chaitanya.

**164.** Narottama, seeing the two Prabhus,
fell to the ground in obeisance and wept.

**165.** Hridaya Chaitanya, calming him with care,
brought a garland-prasada and gave it to him in private.

**166.** The conversation that passed between them
I have no power to relate in detail.

**167.** Sri Hridaya Chaitanya Thakura mercifully
kept Narottama with him for two or four days.

**168.** Entrusting him to the feet of Nityananda and Chaitanya,
he urgently bade him go to Nilachala.

**169.** What Sri Thakura Mahashaya became at the parting
cannot be described.

**170.** All the great devotees who were there,
seeing Narottama's state, were pained at heart.

**171.** At the thought of the virtues of Prabhu's devotees his heart overflowed;
as he walked, his helpless body would collapse in a swoon.

**172.** How wondrous was his gait in prema!
Whoever saw him but once found no peace of mind.

**173.** Others could not understand Narottama's ways:
he was most eager to go to Khardaha.

**174.** Along the way to Khardaha, where there were devotees' homes,
the Mahashaya stayed, met them and went on.

**175.** Entering Khardaha and seeing a marvel,
the most steady Narottama lost his composure.

**176.** At that moment Mahesha Pandita and others, from afar,
seeing Narottama, said softly:

**177.** "In separation from Prabhu we have become as if dead,
yet at the sight of him happiness arises in the heart.

**178.** Without Prabhu's power this would not be possible."
Saying this, they asked and learned who he was.

**179.** All said to Narottama again and again:
"Your name has long been known in the world.

**180.** How you left home and went to Vrindavana —
we heard all of it from people.

**181.** When you came by the forest road from Vrindavana,
we heard that the books were stolen and recovered.

**182.** We also heard that you had come to Navadvipa;
that is why we are still alive — to see you with our own eyes."

**183.** Saying this, all made themselves known
and, filled with prema, showed great affection.

**184.** Narottama floated in the tears of both his eyes,
falling at the feet of the devotees, rolling on the ground.

**185.** Prabhu's dear ones, embracing Narottama,
sprinkled him with tears, wholly losing their composure.

**186.** After a while, growing calm, they took Narottama
and quickly entered Prabhu's house.

**187.** Sri Vasudha and Jahnava, hearing about Narottama,
at once called him into the inner apartments.

**188.** Narottama, deeming himself blessed,
went and bowed at the feet of the two Ishvaris.

**189.** He bowed at the lotus feet of Sri Virabhadra
and on seeing him was overwhelmed with prema.

**190.** Sri Vasudha and Jahnava Devi, seeing Narottama,
lost their composure — their hearts overflowed with prema.

**191.** "The name 'Mahashaya' befits him" —
thus they praised him much and fondly to each other.

**192.** Showing Narottama the greatest mercy,
they kept him four days, unable to let him go.

**193.** They asked for all the news, item by item,
and Narottama told it in detail.

**194.** What went on in their hearts as they heard it —
who has the power to tell it with one mouth?

**195.** Sri Vasudha and Jahnava together with Virachandra
could not let Narottama go even for a moment.

**196.** The devotees who lived in the region of Khardaha
came to Khardaha and met Narottama.

**197.** Though they were sorrowful, still joy arose;
the affection they showed cannot be described.

**198.** The all-knowing Sri Jahnava Gosvamini
told Narottama something in private — I do not know what.

**199.** She quickly gave him permission to go to Nilachala
and again brought him together with all the devotees.

**200.** Mahesha Pandita and others dear to Prabhu
embraced Narottama again and again.

**201.** All bade him go to Nilachala,
and Narottama bowed at the feet of all.

**202.** Taking leave, he went off weeping and weeping,
and all the devotees wept, overwhelmed with affection.

**203.** Having gone some distance, all stopped,
calmed Narottama, and returned to their own places.

**204.** This is Sri Narottama's journey through Sri Gauda:
whoever hears of it has his desires fulfilled.

**205.** Listen to all this continually and with care:
Narahari tells the *Narottama-vilasa*.

*Thus ends the third vilasa of the Narottama-vilasa.*

[^1]: A verse by Narottama; the editions give no source (it is also quoted in the BR).
[^2]: The sending of the books and their theft in Vishnupura: BR 7; cf. *Prema-vilasa* 12–13.
[^3]: "Duhkhini (Duhkhi) Krishnadasa" was Shyamananda's former name.
[^4]: The tradition of how Radha gave Duhkhi Krishnadasa the name "Shyamananda" is told in the BR.
[^5]: Narottama first sees Navadvipa as it was in the Lord's time (the eternal lila), then in its present sorrow of separation; the same happened to Srinivasa (Vilasa 2).
[^6]: The Pandita is Gauridasa Pandita, who established the service of Gaura-Nitai in Ambika (Kalna); after him his disciple Hridaya Chaitanya.
