# Padas Dated to the Lord's Lifetime (after Majumdar)

*Padas by Sri Chaitanya's companions which Bimanbihari Majumdar (*Sri-Chaitanya-chariter upadan*, ch. 2) assigns to the
Lord's lifetime, arranged in the order of events: from Nimai's journey to East Bengal, through the Navadvipa kirtanas of
1509 and the sannyasa, to the first years of separation (the padas of the Puri period follow in the next portion). The text follows the *Padakalpataru* (PK), the *Bhakti-ratnakara* (BR) and
the *Gaura-pada-tarangini* (GPT); the Bengali original, variants and word-for-word translation are in `bn/01.md`.*

## 1. Govinda Ghosha — "gorā gelā pūrvadeśa…" (PK 1597)
*Lila: Vishvambhara in East Bengal; in Navadvipa His dear ones pine for Him. According to Majumdar it was composed while
the Lord was away, before Gaya: if so, it is the earliest surviving pada about Gaura. Raga pahida.*[^1]

**1.1.** Gora has gone to the Eastern land, and His own people, in anguish,
lament in so many ways.
Devi Lakshmipriya weeps — to hear it the heart breaks;
broad day is darkness to her.[^2]

**1.2.** *(Refrain)* Hari, Hari! Separation from Gauranga cannot be borne!
Once more we shall see Gora's face, and the sorrow will go —
if only life holds out till then.

**1.3.** Hearing Shachi's piteous weeping, every living being weeps;
Malini consoles her.[^3]
The townswomen of Nadia weep without cease;
neither clothes nor ornaments please them.

**1.4.** When, going to the bank of the Suradhuni, shall we see Gauranga on the way —
after how many days will that auspicious day come?
We shall hear the words of His moonlike face, and the burning soul will be cooled…
Govinda Ghosha's body is wasting away.

[^1]: Govinda Ghosha was the eldest of the three Ghosha brothers (Govinda, Madhava, Vasudeva), kirtana singers of the
Lord (CC Adi 10, Madhya 13). The journey to East Bengal: CB Adi 14, CC Adi 16, Murari 1.11.5–16, Lochana
(`lochana/en/20.md`, vv. 61–103). Majumdar's argument: the pada has no hint of Nimai's divinity or of kirtana — the poet
knows Him only as a youth he has seen on the bank of the Ganga; such simplicity is natural only in verses made before the
change at Gaya. In PK the pada stands as a gaura-chandrika (prelude) to the section on "distant separation" (Krishna's
departure for Mathura).
[^2]: Lakshmipriya was the Lord's first wife. According to CB (Adi 14) and Murari (1.11.17–20), while the Lord was in East
Bengal she left this world, bitten by the "serpent of separation"; the pada finds her still alive, pining for her husband.
[^3]: Malini, the wife of Shrivasa Pandita, was a close friend of Shachi.

## 2. Vasu Ghosha — "śaṅkha dundubhi-nāda…" (BR, p. 893; PK 1536)
*Lila: the abhisheka-bathing of the Lord in Shrivasa's house (the mahaprakasha), 1509. According to Majumdar, the song of
an eyewitness; raga bhupali (PK), suhai (BR).*[^4]

**2.1.** Conchs and the roll of kettledrums sound melodiously:
the companions perform the abhisheka of Gorachanda.

**2.2.** Fragrant sandal on the stone, incense, lamps alight;
the women of the town bring platters of arghya.[^5]

**2.3.** All the people of Nadia look on and rejoice;
again and again they cry "Jaya, jaya!" and sing songs.

**2.4.** All gaze upon the face of Gorachanda;
Vasu Ghosha sings the sweetness of Gora's abhisheka.

[^4]: Vasudeva (Vasu) Ghosha was the youngest of the Ghosha brothers and the most prolific of Gaura's singers. The great
bathing of the Lord in Shrivasa's house: CB Madhya 9; Murari 2.12; Lochana (`lochana/en/32.md`, vv. 93–113); Karnapura,
*Chaitanya-chandrodaya*, act 1. In the *Bhakti-ratnakara* (wave 12) Narahari Chakravarti cites precisely this pada as an
eyewitness's testimony, though he had Murari, CB and CC to hand — and on this Majumdar rests its early date. In PK it
stands as a gaura-chandrika to the snana-yatra (the bathing of Jagannatha at the full moon of Jyeshtha).
[^5]: Arghya is a respectful offering of water (with flowers, rice and durva grass). Majumdar notes that the pada keeps a
detail the biographies pass over — the part women took in the rite.

## 3. Vasu Ghosha — "taila haridrā āra kuṅkuma kastūrī…" (PK 1537)
*Lila: the same abhisheka — the anointing, bathing and dressing of the Lord. Raga varadi.*[^6]

**3.1.** Oil, turmeric, kunkuma and musk
the women, one after another, apply to Gora's body.

**3.2.** Bringing scented water, they fill the pitchers
and mix fragrant sandal and other things into it.

**3.3.** Crying "Jaya, jaya!" they pour the water over Gora's body;
wiping the holy body, someone dresses Him in garments.

**3.4.** Behold, in the bathing pavilion — Gora, king of dancers!
With joy at heart Vasudeva Ghosha sings.

[^6]: In PK (and in GPT1) this pada follows directly on the preceding one; Majumdar assigns it to the same abhisheka of
1509 (PK 1536, 1537, 1571 — the "historical" padas of Vasu Ghosha). "King of dancers" (nata-raya) is a frequent name
for Gaura, the leader of the dancers in kirtana, among the kirtana poets.

## 4. Govinda, Madhava and Vasu Ghosha — "vasilā gaurāṅgacandra ratna-siṁhāsane…" (PK 1538)
*Lila: after the abhisheka the Lord on the throne: the feast, Shachi's arati, the worship of the devotees. Signed by all
three Ghosha brothers.*[^7]

**4.1.** Gaurangachandra sat upon the jewelled throne;
Shrivasa Pandita anoints His body with sandal.

**4.2.** Gadadhara placed a garland of malati on His neck;
by the radiance of His beauty all ten directions were lit.

**4.3.** Many offerings, every kind of sweet dish
He ate, sitting together with Nityananda.

**4.4.** Having chewed tambula, He sat on the throne again;
Shachi-devi came together with Malini.

**4.5.** Lighting the five-wicked lamp, she performed arati,
waved [the flame] round Him and placed paddy and durva on His head.[^8]

**4.6.** At that moment all shower Him with flowers;
Advaita Acharya offers tulasi and sandal.

**4.7.** Gods and men come together to see it;
Nityananda, sitting at His right, watches with delight.

**4.8.** This is the matchless lila of Gora's abhisheka:
Govinda, Madhava and Vasu floated away in prema.

[^7]: Majumdar cites this pada together with the preceding ones as a description of the same abhisheka in Shrivasa's
house; from the signature "Govinda, Madhava, Vasu" (as in Vasu Ramananda) he infers that Govinda was the eldest. The
Lord sitting on Vishnu's throne in Shrivasa's house, accepting offerings and receiving the devotees' worship: CB Madhya 9;
CC Adi 17.
[^8]: Paddy (dhanya) and durva grass placed on the head are a Bengali mother's blessing.

## 5. Ramachandra — "pahu mora gaurāṅga rāya…" (BR, p. 919; PK 2186)
*Lila: the Lord in Navadvipa in the mood of Radha, with Gadadhara and Narahari beside Him. According to Majumdar, the
song of an eyewitness (Narahari Chakravarti cites it as testimony). Raga shri (PK), suhai (BR).*[^9]

**5.1.** *(Refrain)* My Lord is Gauranga Raya,
whose glories Shiva, Shuka and Brahma sing!

**5.2.** He for whom Kamala ever pines,[^10]
that very Lord raises His arms, weeps and cries out, "Hari!"

**5.3.** The body at whose sight Kamadeva became bodiless[^11]
is now ever grey with the dust of kirtana.

**5.4.** Now and again He starts up, crying, "Radha, Radha!" —
Gadadhara and Narahari stand gazing at His face.

**5.5.** His body thrills with the deep love of former days.
Says Ramachandra: who would not understand this play?

[^9]: Who this Ramachandra was is unclear: Majumdar rejects Ramachandra Puri, Ramachandra the brahmana and Ramachandra
Khan, and suggests Ramachandra Kaviraja of Nityananda's branch (not Narottama's friend, whom Narahari Chakravarti would
not have passed off as an eyewitness). In BR (wave 12) the pada stands among testimonies to the Navadvipa pastimes,
directly after Govinda Ghosha's pada on the Lord's dancing with Narahari. Narahari Sarakara Thakura (of Shrikhanda) was a
singer of Gaura's moods; Majumdar confirms his presence with the Lord in Navadvipa from the padas of other
contemporaries. Cf. CC Adi 17 (Radha's mood in Navadvipa).
[^10]: Kamala is Lakshmi, the consort of Narayana. Here she is only mentioned as longing for the Lord; cf. *Bhag.* 10.16.36
(Lakshmi longs for the dust of Krishna's feet).
[^11]: The Lord's beauty put Kama (Kamadeva, the god of love) to shame, and he was as if bereft of his body (Ananga, "the
Bodiless", is his name). The "love of former days" (v. 5.5) is Radha's love for Krishna, which the Lord tastes in the
form of Gaura. BR reads: "He gazes at the faces of Narahari and Gadadhara."

## 6. Murari Gupta — "gadādhara-aṅge pahu aṅga helāiyā…" (BR, p. 922; PK 2121)
*Lila: the Lord in Radha's mood, leaning on Gadadhara, listens to the song of Vrindavana. According to Majumdar, composed in
1509 under the immediate impression of Nimai's moods. Raga patamanjari (PK), kamoda (BR).*[^12]

**6.1.** Leaning His body against Gadadhara,
the Lord is lost in the song of Vrindavana's glories.

**6.2.** Now He laughs, now He weeps, unaware of the outer world;
in Radha's mood His heart is restless — Gokula comes to His mind.

**6.3.** The grace of His form surpasses countless Kamadevas;
millions of moons weep to see His face.

**6.4.** The three worlds melt in the rasa of these two.[^13]
I know not for what fault Murari Gupta is left without it.

[^12]: Murari Gupta, the Lord's companion from childhood, wrote the Sanskrit biography (the "Kadacha"); apart from it only
a few Bengali padas are his. Narahari Chakravarti cites this one in BR (wave 12) among eyewitness testimonies.
Majumdar's argument: the pada was written in 1509, when Nimai's moods were still new; the signature of the poet, lamenting
that he himself is "deprived" of this rasa, is an eyewitness's humility. Cf. CC Adi
17.
[^13]: "These two" are Gaura and Gadadhara. According to the *Gaura-ganoddesha-dipika* (vv. 147–153) Gadadhara Pandita is a
manifestation of Radha in Gaura's lila; the Lord, Himself in Radha's mood, remembers Gokula beside him.

## 7. Paramananda (Gupta) — "gorā-tanu dhūlāya loṭāya…" (PK 2120)
*Lila: in Navadvipa the Lord, dressed as Natavara (Krishna the dancer), calls for Radha and asks for the flute; the
companions caution the singers. According to Majumdar, an eyewitness's testimony from 1509. Raga kalyani.*[^14]

**7.1.** *(Refrain)* Gora's body rolls in the dust.
Embracing Gadadhara, He calls, "Radha, Radha!" —
and asks for the yellow garment and the flute.

**7.2.** Dressed as Natavara, His hair bound up in front
and a peacock feather shining in it,
standing thrice-bent, He cries again and again, "Hari!" —
and Gora looks about for the kadamba branch.

**7.3.** Hearing of Vrindavana's glories, His mind maddened with rasa, [He asks,]
"Where have the sakhis gone, alas?"
Not grasping His mood, all the dear companions
sing His glories, calling Him "Gauranga".

**7.4.** And someone says, "Take care! Do not sing songs of rasa:
if [prema] surges up, the earth will not hold it!"
In the joy of his heart says Paramananda:
"Who then will keep life in the body?"

[^14]: The signature is "Paramananda". S. Ray ascribed the padas bearing it to Kavi Karnapura (Paramananda Sena); Majumdar
separates six Vrajabuli padas by a certain Paramananda of Vrindavana and gives the rest, this and the next among them, to
Paramananda Gupta, in whose house Nityananda stayed (CB, Antya-khanda) — Karnapura was not yet born in 1509. His argument for
the date: only a close companion could know the detail "do not sing songs of rasa", and the Lord did not wear the
Natavara dress in Puri — hence Navadvipa. The attribution to Paramananda Gupta is Majumdar's conjecture. Cf. Murari
2.15.1 (the Lord asks for His flute).

## 8. Paramananda (Gupta) — "śacīra nandana gorācānda…" (PK 2528)
*Lila: in Navadvipa the Lord in the mood of new love (anuraga); the same mood overcomes His companions. According to
Majumdar, an eyewitness's pada.*[^15]

**8.1.** Shachi's son, Gorachand,
is the enchanting snare of all the worlds.

**8.2.** In the mood of new love He is lost to Himself:
incessantly tears flow from His lotus eyes.

**8.3.** His body is full of thrills, His speech falters;
now He steadies His heart, now He is utterly unsettled.

**8.4.** Likewise the companions with Him are overcome [by this mood].
Says Paramananda: these are waves of prema.

[^15]: Only in PK, as a gaura-chandrika to the section "anuraga". Majumdar regards this pada too as an eyewitness's: in it
the Lord's mood overcomes His companions as well. (In the catalogue the first line of this pada had been given wrongly,
from a line of a different, neighbouring song.)

## 9. Vasu Ghosha — "cāṅcara cikura cūḍā cāru bhāle…" (BR, pp. 934–935)
*Lila: Nimai in the dress of Natavara (Krishna the dancer), 1509. According to Majumdar, something seen with his own eyes.
Raga kamoda.*[^16]

**9.1.** His curling hair is bound in a topknot above the lovely brow
and wound with a garland of malati;

**9.2.** in it is set a peacock feather
and a flowering sprig with its leaves.

**9.3.** His body outshines assayed gold,
about His waist is a bright-coloured cloth;

**9.4.** a sandal tilaka shines on His brow,
a garland of forest flowers hangs to His knees.

**9.5.** Gorachand in the dress of Natavara —
what a snare for the women!

**9.6.** Seeing this, Vasudeva weeps:
my heart can find no rest.

[^16]: This and the next two padas of Vasu Ghosha are known only from the *Bhakti-ratnakara* (wave 12): Narahari
Chakravarti introduces them as songs composed at the very places in Navadvipa that are shown to Shrinivasa Acharya on
his circuit of Navadvipa ("here, under this kadamba, Gora, dressed as Natavara and standing thrice-bent, played the
flute"). Majumdar takes them as describing what was seen with one's own eyes in 1509. "A snare for the women" (9.5) is a
poetic image of the Lord's irresistible beauty (Krishna the Natavara captivates the gopis); Vrindavana Dasa stresses that
in this incarnation the Lord never so much as glanced at another man's wife, and the Gaudiya tradition does not accept
the worship of Gaura in the spirit of "Gaura-nagari".

## 10. Vasu Ghosha — "soṅari puruba-līlā tribhaṅga hailā…" (BR, p. 935)
*Lila: Gora, remembering His former lila, plays the flute on the bank of the Ganga. Raga dhanashi.*[^17]

**10.1.** Remembering His former lila, He stood thrice-bent,
and Gora raised the enchanting flute to His lips.

**10.2.** Gorachand blew into the flute's opening
and, moving His fingers, played a most lovely song.

**10.3.** All the townspeople are enchanted to hear it;
on the bank of the Suradhuni the trees and creepers thrill.

**10.4.** What can Vasudeva Ghosha say of it?
With the song of His flute Gora enchanted the world.

[^17]: Cf. Murari 2.15.1 (the Lord asks for His flute); CC Adi 17 (the Lord in Krishna's mood in Navadvipa). The pada is
also in GPT2.

## 11. Vasu Ghosha — "pūrvalīlā gorācāṅdera manete paḍila…" (BR, p. 935)
*Lila: the goshtha-lila in Navadvipa — the Lord in the mood of a cowherd boy calls the cows; with Him are Nityananda,
Ramai, Sundarananda, Gauridasa. Raga tori.*[^18]

**11.1.** His former lila came to Gorachand's mind,
and He began to call aloud: "Shangali! Dhabali!"

**11.2.** Sounding horn, venu and murali in triumph,
shouting "hai, hai!" He keeps twirling the cowherd's stick.

**11.3.** Ramai, Sundara, and Nityananda with them,
Gauridasa and all the others were filled with bliss.

**11.4.** With joy at heart Vasudeva Ghosha says:
Gorachand has revealed the goshtha-lila.

[^18]: Shangali and Dhabali are names of Krishna's cows ("Dark One", "White One"); venu and murali are kinds of flute.
Sundara is Sundarananda; Gauridasa Pandita (of Ambika-Kalna) — companions of Nityananda, cowherd boys of Vraja according
to the *Gaura-ganoddesha-dipika* (Gauridasa is Subala). "Ramai" is probably Abhirama (Ramadasa), the cowherd Shridama
according to GGD; the identification is not certain. Majumdar's argument: this pada explains why Nityananda's companions
lived in the mood of cowherds (as CB describes) — they had seen the Lord's goshtha-lila.

## 12. Vamshivadana — "śacīra nandana gorā o cāṅda-bayāne…" (PK 2564)
*Lila: the same goshtha-lila: Nityananda sounds the horn, Gauridasa and Abhirama come running. According to Majumdar, more
detailed than Vasu's and bearing "the stamp of an eyewitness"; 1509. Raga bhatiyari.*[^19]

**12.1.** Gora, the son of Shachi, with that moonlike mouth
calls again and again: "Dhabali! Shangali!"

**12.2.** Understanding where His mood is leading, Nityananda Raya
sounds a call with his mouth, imitating a horn.

**12.3.** Hearing the horn-call from Nitai-chanda's mouth,
the devotees fall senseless with prema.

**12.4.** Up ran the pandita whose name is Gauridasa,
and Abhirama runs, crying, "Brother, brother!"

**12.5.** Seeing Gauranga's form, all are seized by prema:
on His head a topknot with a peacock feather, the dress of Natavara,

**12.6.** anklets ringing on His feet, sandal on all His limbs.
Says Vamshivadana: "Come, let us go to Govardhana!"

[^19]: Vamshivadana (Vamshi) was a companion of the Lord, and after the sannyasa the guardian of Vishnupriya and Shachi in
Navadvipa. In PK the pada is a gaura-chandrika to the section "going out to the pastures" (goshtha-gamana). Majumdar
notes that the pada explains why GGD calls Gauridasa Subala and Abhirama Shridama. He dates it to 1509 (the OCR of his
book has "1507", probably a misprint).

## 13. Vasu Ghosha — "gaurāṅgacāṅdera mane ki bhāva uṭhila…" (PK 1368; BR, pp. 935–936)
*Lila: the Lord in Krishna's mood remembers the dana-lila and "collects a toll" on the roads of Nadia. According to
Majumdar, seen with his own eyes, 1509. Raga suhai (PK), kamoda (BR).*[^20]

**13.1.** What a mood arose in Gaurangachanda's heart!
In the midst of Nadia Gora set up a toll.

**13.2.** A toll of what rasa does Gora, the jewel of brahmanas, demand?
Barring the way with a cane, He holds back the young women.

**13.3.** "Pay the toll, pay the toll!" Gora calls,
and the women of the town were in a fix.

**13.4.** "In the Krishna-avatara I collected the toll" —
that mood came back to His mind, sings Vasu Ghosha.

[^20]: The dana-lila is Krishna's pastime of stopping the gopis, together with His cowherd friends, on their way to
Govardhana and demanding a "toll" for their milk and butter; it is a lila of pure prema, not of worldly passion. Here
the Lord, seized by Krishna's mood, re-enacts it: Majumdar sees in the pada the explanation of Gadadhara Dasa's mood
("who will buy Gora?"). The reading "taruṇī" ("young women", BR, PK) Majumdar holds correct, "taraṇī" ("boat", GPT) not.
Vrindavana Dasa stresses that in this incarnation the Lord never so much as glanced at another man's wife; the Gaudiya
tradition (Vrindavana Dasa, Krishnadasa Kaviraja, Bhaktisiddhanta) does not accept the worship of Gaura in the spirit
of "Gaura-nagari".

## 14. Shivananda Sena — "holi khelata gaurakiśora…" (BR, pp. 944–945)
*Lila: Holi in Navadvipa: the Lord in Krishna's mood with Gadadhara; Narahari sings of Vraja, Mukunda, Murari and Vasu
dance. According to Majumdar, apparently 1509. Raga kamoda; Vrajabuli.*[^21]

**14.1.** The youthful Gaura plays Holi;
in His embrace is Gadadhara, the beloved full of rasa.[^22]

**14.2.** Drops of sweat on His face, His body thrilling,
under the weight of the mood tears stream from His eyes.

**14.3.** They sing of the rasa of Vraja together with Narahari;
Mukunda, Murari and Vasu dance with delight.

**14.4.** Again and again He swoons in the Pandita's arms;
seeing it, the companions are lost in joy.

**14.5.** The Lord laid out a bower-temple,
falls to the ground and says: "Where is My flute?

**14.6.** Where is Govardhana, where the Yamuna's bank?
Where are the malati, yuthi and champaka flowers?"

**14.7.** Says Shivananda, hearing the Lord's words full of rasa:
where the Lord and Gadadhara are, there is a mine of rasa.

[^21]: Shivananda Sena of Kumarahatta was the father of Kavi Karnapura and later led the Gaudiya devotees to Puri every
year (CC Madhya 1, Antya 12). The pada is known from BR (wave 12) and GPT; it is not in PK. Its historical value,
according to Majumdar, is the list of the companions of the Navadvipa kirtanas; it is also confirmed by Vasu Ghosha's
pada on Holi with Gadadhara (BR, pp. 942–943). Holi (dola) is the spring festival of colours at the full moon of
Phalguna, the day of the Lord's appearance.
[^22]: "The beloved" (rasavati nari) is Gadadhara in Radha's mood (GGD 147: Gadadhara Pandita is Radha), and the Lord is
in Krishna's mood. This is the spiritual lila of Radha and Krishna remembered by the Lord and His companion, not a
worldly feeling.

## 15. Govinda Ghosha — "prāṇera mukunda he ki āji śuniluṅ ācambita…" (PK 1606)
*Lila: the first news that the Lord will leave Navadvipa; Gadadhara runs to Mukunda. According to Majumdar, late January
1510, a few days before the sannyasa. Raga pahida.*[^23]

**15.1.** *(Refrain)* O Mukunda, dear as life! What have I suddenly heard today!
To say it, life departs; it will not leave my lips:
Sri Gauranga will leave Navadvipa!

**15.2.** We knew nothing. In the morning I came to Gora —
He sits with bowed head;
tears stream from His eyes and run in rivulets down His chest,
the moon of His face has grown pale.

**15.3.** Seeing this, my heart was at once in turmoil,
but there was no chance to ask.
After a moment He came to Himself; then I asked —
and, hearing me, He gave this answer.

**15.4.** And I, overwhelmed, saying nothing to Him,
ran to you.
So I have told you; do what you can —
I have no hope of living."

**15.5.** Hearing this, Mukunda weeps, his heart finds no rest,
as he looks into Gadadhara's face.
Says this Govinda Ghosha: may this not happen!
Or else I shall die.

[^23]: In PK the pada is headed "spoken by Pandita Gosvami", i.e. by Gadadhara Pandita: in the pada he tells Mukunda Datta
of the Lord's words, and Mukunda weeps looking into his face; the signature is Govinda Ghosha's. Majumdar takes the
heading as a tradition going back to Gadadhara himself and dates the pada to the end of January 1510. In PK it opens the
section "separation to come". Cf. CB Madhya 26, Murari 2.18.17–23: the devotees learn of the Lord's decision to take
sannyasa.

## 16. Govinda Ghosha — "hede re nadīyāvāsī kāra mukha cāo…" (PK 1622)
*Lila: the day the Lord leaves Navadvipa for sannyasa. According to Majumdar, written on that very day (1510). Raga
suhai.*[^24]

**16.1.** O people of Nadia! Whose face are you looking to?
Stretch out your arms — bring Gorachand back!

**16.2.** Who else will take you all into his arms?
Who, seeing the afflicted, will offer them prema unasked?

**16.3.** What a spear in the heart, alas, what a spear in the heart!
The darling of our life is leaving Navadvipa.

**16.4.** No more shall we go to Gauranga,
no more shall we delight in kirtana.

**16.5.** The devotees weep, their hearts breaking;
only stony Govinda Ghosha does not die.

[^24]: In PK the pada stands in the section "words of the devotees of Navadvipa in separation from Gaura". The Lord's
leaving home at night at the end of Magha 1510 and crossing the Ganga towards Katwa: CB Madhya 28, CC Adi 17, Murari
3.1.6–7.

## 17. Vamshi(vadana) — "āra nā heriba prasara kapāle…" (PK 1855)
*Lila: Navadvipa's grief after the sannyasa; the weeping of Shachi and Vishnupriya. According to Majumdar, soon after the
sannyasa (1510). Raga gandhara.*[^25]

**17.1.** No more shall I see on the broad brow
the adornment of curls and tilaka,
no more see on the golden lotus [of His face]
the dance of the wagtail eyes.

**17.2.** No more will He dance in Shrivasa's house
with His chataka-bird devotees,
no more will He dance in His own house
for us to gaze at Him.

**17.3.** Will the two brothers, Nimai and Nitai,
ever dance together again?
I keep crying, "Nimai!" —
but Nimai is nowhere.

**17.4.** Pitiless Keshava Bharati came
and brought down a thunderbolt on our heads.
How, not seeing beautiful Gauranga,
shall I stay on in Nadia?

**17.5.** Who is there now to bring back
my Gaura Raya?
Hearing the weeping of mother-in-law and daughter-in-law,
Vamshi rolls in the dust.[^26]

[^25]: The signature is "Vamshi". The PK index ascribes the pada to Vamshidasa; Majumdar distinguishes Vamshidasa (a disciple
of Shrinivasa Acharya, 17th century) from Vamshivadana and assigns this pada to Vamshivadana, who looked after Shachi and
Vishnupriya — hence the poet's grief at hearing "the weeping of mother-in-law and daughter-in-law". One manuscript
(according to GPT) has a signature of Vasu Ghosha, so the attribution is not beyond doubt. Keshava Bharati was the
sannyasi who gave the Lord sannyasa at Katwa; "pitiless" is the reproach of those who love, not a condemnation (cf. CB
Madhya 28).
[^26]: The mother-in-law and daughter-in-law are Shachi and Vishnupriya, the Lord's second wife.

## 18. Ramananda (Vasu) — "pāpī māghe pahuṅ kayala sannyāsa…" (PK 1711; GPT1, p. 390)
*Lila: longing for the Lord in the spring of 1510, soon after the sannyasa. According to Majumdar, written a few days
after the sannyasa. Raga suhai; Vrajabuli.*[^27]

**18.1.** In the accursed month of Magha my Lord took sannyasa —
and then my hope of life departed too.

**18.2.** Day by day my body wastes, my eyes stream;
how many days without Gora shall I keep my life?

**18.3.** Now it is spring, and all are happy —
yet this wretched, hard life will not leave me.

**18.4.** All the love my Lord showed me! —
I remember it, and my life hangs by a thread at my throat.[^28]

**18.5.** Says Ramananda: He is the lord of my life.
When shall I see Him again beside Gadadhara?

[^27]: The signature is "Ramananda". Majumdar (who knew the pada only from GPT) argues that this is not Ramananda Raya (who
did not know the Lord before the sannyasa and could not pine for Him beside Gadadhara) but Vasu Ramananda of Kulinagrama,
a descendant of Maladhara Vasu, Gunaraja Khan (CC Madhya 15). His argument for the date: "now it is spring" — the pada was made in
Phalguna–Chaitra, some weeks after the sannyasa taken at the end of Magha (January–February 1510). The reading "মাঘে"
("in Magha") is given by PK and GPT1; Majumdar (in the OCR of his book) has "মাথে". In PK the pada is a gaura-chandrika to
the section "separation in spring".
[^28]: The reading "কণ্ঠহিঁ ডোর" follows the PK scan; in GPT the line is corrupt. The sense: life "has risen to the
throat" (is about to leave the body).

## 19. Shivananda Sena — "dayāmaya gaurahari naidyā-līlā sāṅga kari…" (GPT1, p. 382)
*Lila: from Shantipura the Lord leaves for Puri and tells the devotees of Gauda to come to Him once a year. According to
Majumdar, written right after the Lord's departure (1510). Raga mangala.*[^29]

**19.1.** Merciful Gaurahari, having ended the Nadia-lila —
alas, alas, what ill fate! —
my Lord has gone to Nilachala, leaving this servant alone:
my worldly bonds were not cut.

**19.2.** What You have commanded I shall surely carry out,
but how shall I live alone?
My sons and all my family will be like poison to me:
how shall I pass my days without You?

**19.3.** You told me to come to Nilachala
with the pilgrims of Gauda at the year's end, for darshana.
How shall I endure, how pass a whole year,
when a moment seems a hundred yugas?

**19.4.** Be merciful, Lord, grant me leave
to see Your feet every day.
If You do not command it, O Lord Vishvambhara,
Shivananda will take his own life.

[^29]: The pada is known only from GPT (signature "Shivananda"). Majumdar's argument: the words "son and family will be
like poison to me" and "how shall I live through the year until the next journey with the Gaudiyas" make sense only
directly after the Lord's departure from Shantipura for Puri; later, when the journeys had become a custom, they would
not have been written. The Lord's instruction to the devotees of Gauda to come to Puri every year: CC Madhya 3, CB Antya
1. "Will take his own life" is a hyperbole of separation, common in Bengali padas; cf. the devotees' threats to give up
their lives in separation from the Lord in CC (Madhya 3).

## 20. Narahari (Sarakara) — "sonā śatabāna jini gaurāṅga āmāra…" (PK 1729)
*Lila: the first chaturmasya after the sannyasa — the devotees do not know where the Lord is. According to Majumdar,
written during the first chaturmasya (1510). Raga suhai.*[^30]

**20.1.** My Gauranga outshines gold refined a hundred times —
what is a chamara whisk beside that thick mass of His hair?

**20.2.** Why did He shave His head, to what land has He gone?
In whose house is He spending this chaturmasya?

**20.3.** I remember and remember, and my heart breaks:
where has Gora Raya gone, dear as life itself?

**20.4.** The devotees weep and heave sighs;
Narahari Dasa cannot keep his patience.

[^30]: The signature is "Narahari Dasa". Narahari Sarakara Thakura of Shrikhanda was a companion of the Lord and a singer of
His moods; Majumdar assigns to him the padas in plain Bengali known from PK (not only from GPT). In PK the pada is a
gaura-chandrika to the section "separation in the rainy season"; Majumdar dates it to the first chaturmasya after the
sannyasa (1510), when the Lord was travelling in the South. According to CC (Madhya 9) He spent that chaturmasya at
Shrirangam, in the house of Venkata Bhatta.

## 21. Shivananda Sena — "jaya jaya paṇḍita gosāñi…" (PK 2355)
*Lila: Gadadhara Pandita gives up his vow to live in the Kshetra and his service of Gopinatha to follow the Lord to Gauda
(1514). According to Majumdar, composed at that very time. Raga patamanjari.*[^31]

**21.1.** Glory, glory to Pandita Gosani!
By the power of his mercy I sing Chaitanya's glories.

**21.2.** So does he love Gaurangachandra
that He is famed by the name "Lord of Gadadhara's life".

**21.3.** Who can understand the love of one whose life is given to Gaura,
who for His sake gave up life in the Kshetra and the service of Krishna?[^32]

**21.4.** Gadai's Gauranga, Gauranga's Gadadhara:
like Sri Rama and Janaki — one body.

**21.5.** As Radha and the Moon of Vrindavana are one soul,
so Gaura and Gadadhara are waves of one prema.

**21.6.** Says Shivananda: in love for her the Lord,
dark of body, became golden and begs for prema.[^33]

[^31]: Gadadhara Pandita (Gadai) took in Puri the vow of kshetra-sannyasa (never to leave the Kshetra) and served the
Deity of Tota-Gopinatha. When in 1514 the Lord set out for Gauda (on His way to Vrindavana), Gadadhara followed Him,
abandoning both vow and service, and only at Cuttack did the Lord persuade him to go back — CC Madhya 16. Majumdar's
argument: later, knowing from CC that the Lord sent Gadadhara back, no one would have written "for His sake he gave up
life in the Kshetra" — the pada is contemporary with the event (1514/15).
[^32]: "The service of Krishna" is the service of the Deity Gopinatha in Puri.
[^33]: "In love for her" — for Radha: Krishna (Shyama, the dark one) took on Radha's golden form to taste her love.
Majumdar sees in the last verse an echo of Svarupa Damodara's teaching (CC Adi 1.5–6, 4). Gadadhara is Radha (GGD 147).

## 22. Vasu Ghosha — "ājukāra svapana-kathā śuna lo mālinī sai…" (BR, pp. 982–983)
*Lila: Shachi tells Malini her dream: Nimai, unable to stay in Nilachala, came home. According to Majumdar, an
eyewitness's pada from the years of separation (about 1514/15). Raga kamoda.*[^34]

**22.1.** Listen, friend Malini, to the dream I had today:
Nimai came home.
Standing in the courtyard, gazing and gazing at the house,
He called me: "Mother!"

**22.2.** I was lying in the house — and, beside myself, came out,
hearing Nimai's voice.
Nimai put the dust of His mother's feet on His head,
and weeps and weeps: "Mother!"

**22.3.** "Captive to your love, I wandered about —
I could not stay in Nilachala.
To see you I have come to Nadia" —
so He says, weeping.

**22.4.** "Come to me, my child!" — I lifted Him to my breast —
and at that moment sleep left me.
Not seeing Him again, how my heart aches!
The night passed in tears.

**22.5.** Weeping and weeping, Shachi fell fainting to the ground;
Malini wails aloud.
What can I say — alas, alas! — this pain cannot be borne:
why does Vasu not die?

[^34]: The pada is known only from BR (wave 12): Narahari Chakravarti cites it in the account of Shachi's house, to which
Nityananda comes. Majumdar values this and the next pada as testimonies which the later biographers left out, as if they
"diminished" the sannyasa. Cf. CC Antya 3: the Lord says that He comes unseen to eat what Shachi cooks.

## 23. Vasu Ghosha — "āola nadīyāra loka gaurāṅga dekhite…" (GPT1, p. 413)
*Lila: the sannyasi Chaitanya comes to Nadia (1514/15); the people of Navadvipa and Mother Shachi see Him. Raga shri.*[^35]

**23.1.** The people of Nadia came to see Gauranga:
their hearts so overwhelmed with joy they cannot walk.

**23.2.** Seeing Gorachand's face after so long,
their hungry chakora-eyes are intoxicated.

**23.3.** The devotees are lost in joy, gazing at Him;
the mother runs up and embraces Gorachand.

**23.4.** As if life had returned to a dying body:
Gauranga is in Nadia, sings Vasu Ghosha.

[^35]: The pada is only in GPT (neither in BR nor in PK). Majumdar sees in it a testimony to the coming of the sannyasi Lord
to the neighbourhood of Navadvipa on His way to Gauda (1514/15), told by Murari (4.14.3–11) and Lochana (Shesha-khanda);
CC (Madhya 1) and CB (Antya 3) speak of His stay at Kuliya, opposite Navadvipa. The chakora is a bird that, in poetic
tradition, feeds on moonlight.

## 24. Krishnadasa — "ṭhākura paṇḍitera bāḍī gorā nāce phiri phiri…" (PK 2358)
*Lila: the Lord and Nityananda at Ambika with Gauridasa Pandita; Gauridasa begs Them to stay, and the Lord tells him to
serve Their images. According to Majumdar, a contemporary testimony that the worship of the Lord's Deity began in His
lifetime. Raga bhatiyari.*[^36]

**24.1.** In Thakura Pandita's house Gora dances, whirling round;
Nityananda cries, "Hari, Hari!"
Weeping, Gauridasa falls at the Lord's feet and says:
"Never leave my house!

**24.2.** Grant my request — stay in Ambika:
this is my plea at Your feet.
If You go away, I shall surely die:
looking at whom shall I live then?

**24.3.** If you two brothers stay here with me,
then everyone will be delivered.
Again I pray: do not leave me, Gaurahari —
then I shall know that You are the saviour of the fallen."

**24.4.** The Lord says: "Gauridasa, give up such a wish:
serve My image and you will see —
I am present in it, know this for certain;
keep this true word of Mine."

**24.5.** Hearing this, Gauridasa heaved a long sigh
and again weeps aloud.
Again the two brothers console him,
yet his heart finds no rest.

**24.6.** Says lowly Krishnadasa, whose hope is Chaitanya's feet:
the two brothers stayed there.
Both were taken captive by Thakura Pandita's love —
hence they are sung as "loving to Their devotees".[^37]

[^36]: Krishnadasa was the younger brother of Gauridasa Pandita, of Nityananda's branch (according to Majumdar). Gauridasa
Pandita (here "Thakura Pandita") was a companion of Nityananda, Subala according to GGD; at Ambika-Kalna he served the
Deities of Gaura-Nitai. Cf. Murari 4.14.12–14. Majumdar's argument: the pada is a contemporary's testimony that the
worship of the Lord's Deity began in His lifetime. The next pada of PK (2359) continues the story.
[^37]: "The two brothers stayed there" — the Lord and Nityananda remained at Ambika in Their Deity forms, in which, by
Their own word, They Themselves are present: the Deity is not an image but the Lord Himself, manifest through the
devotee's love.

## 25. Chandrashekhara — "kṣaṇeka rahiyā caliyā uṭhiyā paṇḍita jagadānanda…" (PK 1854)
*Lila: Jagadananda Pandita comes from Nilachala to Navadvipa, to Shachi and Vishnupriya. According to Majumdar, written
"before one's eyes", in the Lord's lifetime.*[^38]

**25.1.** After pausing a while, Pandita Jagadananda
rose and went on.
Entering the town, he sees that in every house
all the people are joyless.

**25.2.** The shops are not open, no one eats,
no face has a smile.
The women of the town weep with stifled sobs,
sitting apart, alone.

**25.3.** Having seen the town, he went
and entered the Lord's house.
As if half dead, senseless,
Ai lies on the ground.[^39]

**25.4.** And the Lord's wife, forsaken,
having lost the Lord,
lies in faded clothes,
tears flowing from her closed eyes.

**25.5.** All the servants and maids are silent;
seeing the traveller,
they ask him: "Tell us now,
where have you come from?"

**25.6.** The Pandita says: "I have come
from Nilachala.
Beautiful Gauranga sent me
to see you all."

**25.7.** Hearing these words, with tearful eyes
they went and told Shachi,
and another set off at once
running to Shrivasa's house.

**25.8.** Hearing it, Shrivasa, Malini in delight,
and all the people of Navadvipa,
who had been as if dead, came running at once —
and, coming, came back to life.

**25.9.** Malini came and gently raised
Shachi and Vishnupriya
and told them: "The Pandita has come;
Gaurahari has sent him."

**25.10.** Hearing it, Mother Shachi looked up with a start
and saw the Pandita.
She says to him: "My Nimai —
how far off is He now?"

**25.11.** Seeing the utmost of love, the greatness of a mother's tenderness,
the Pandita says, weeping:
"That jewel Gora, I know, age after age
is subdued by your love."

**25.12.** Having told everyone
of Gauranga's ways and doings,
the Pandita stayed on in the town of Nadia,
giving joy to all.

**25.13.** Chandrashekhara, no better than a beast,
is fond of the poison of sense-objects:
to Gauranga's life, the supreme nectar,
his heart does not take.

[^38]: The signature is "Chandrashekhara"; by tradition (S. Ray) this is Chandrashekhara Acharya, the Lord's maternal
uncle; the attribution is traditional but not proven. In PK the pada continues the preceding one (PK 1853, signed
"Madhavi Dasa"), in which Jagadananda walks from Puri wondering whether he will find Shachi. Jagadananda's journeys to
Shachi at the Lord's bidding: CC Antya 12. Majumdar's argument: the restraint in mentioning the Lord's wife (her name is
not given — only "the Lord's wife") is a mark of a contemporary; Vrindavana Dasa does the same.
[^39]: Ai ("mother") — so Mother Shachi was called in Navadvipa.

## 26. Narahari (Sarakara) — "rase tanu ḍhara ḍhara gaurakiśora bara…" (PK 2259)
*Theme: the truth of Gaura — Shyama became Gaura to play with Radha; the poet fears to reveal the secret. According to
Majumdar, written before Svarupa Damodara's kadacha, i.e. in the Lord's lifetime. Raga pahida (GPT).*[^40]

**26.1.** The most excellent youthful Gaura, His body brimming with rasa —
His name is Sri Krishna Chaitanya.
To speak these hidden things pains the heart:
none but the devotees know them.

**26.2.** In the Dvapara-yuga Shyama, in Kali the name Chaitanya:
so Garga's words are written in the *Bhagavata*.[^41]
I have reasoned in my heart: Shyama has become Gauranga,
and the body of Radha and Krishna bears witness to it.[^42]

**26.3.** Within, the dark body; without, the golden:
wondrous is Chaitanya's lila!
To play with Rai and to share the rasa of the bowers,
out of love He became golden.

**26.4.** This is not to be told: tell it, and who knows what may happen;
leave it untold, and the heart burns.
Reasoning in his heart and holding Gauranga in his heart,
Narahari laments.

[^40]: The signature is "Narahari"; Majumdar assigns the pada to Narahari Sarakara. In PK it stands in the section on the
sannyasa, in GPT among the opening padas (mangalacharana). Majumdar's argument: this truth (Krishna became Gaura for
Radha's sake) was later set forth openly by Svarupa Damodara in his kadacha and after him by Krishnadasa Kaviraja (CC Adi
1.5–6, 4); the poet's fear of revealing the secret would be unintelligible after that — so the pada was written in the
Lord's lifetime.
[^41]: *Bhag.* 10.8.13: Garga, naming Krishna, says that in other yugas He took white, red and yellow colours, and now has
become dark (krishna). The Gaudiya tradition sees in the "yellow" (pita) a reference to Gaura.
[^42]: The reading "সাথী" (PK, GPT) means "companion"; the sense may require "সাখী" (witness). Within He is Shyama
(Krishna), without He is clad in Radha's golden radiance (cf. CC Adi 4).
