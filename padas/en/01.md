# Padas Dated to the Lord's Lifetime (after Majumdar)

*Padas by Sri Chaitanya's companions which Bimanbihari Majumdar (*Sri-Chaitanya-chariter upadan*, ch. 2) assigns to the
Lord's lifetime, arranged in the order of events: from Nimai's journey to East Bengal, through the Navadvipa kirtanas of
1509 and the sannyasa, to the years in Puri. The text follows the *Padakalpataru* (PK), the *Bhakti-ratnakara* (BR) and
the *Gaura-pada-tarangini* (GPT); the Bengali original, variants and word-for-word translation are in `bn/01.md`.*

## 1. Govinda Ghosha — "gorā gelā pūrvadeśa…" (PK 1597)
*Lila: Vishvambhara in East Bengal; in Navadvipa His dear ones pine for Him. According to Majumdar it was composed while
the Lord was away, before Gaya: if so, it is the earliest surviving pada about Gaura. Raga pahida.*[^1]

**1.1.** Gora has gone to the Eastern land, and His own people, in anguish,
lament in so many ways.
Devi Lakshmipriya weeps — to hear it the heart breaks;
broad day is darkness to her.[^2]

**1.2.** *(Refrain)* Hari, Hari! Separation from Gauranga cannot be borne!
Once more we shall see Gora's face, and the sorrow will go —
if only life holds out till then.

**1.3.** Hearing Shachi's piteous weeping, every living being weeps;
Malini consoles her.[^3]
The townswomen of Nadia weep without cease;
neither clothes nor ornaments please them.

**1.4.** When, going to the bank of the Suradhuni, shall we see Gauranga on the way —
after how many days will that auspicious day come?
We shall hear the words of His moonlike face, and the burning soul will be cooled…
Govinda Ghosha's body is wasting away.

[^1]: Govinda Ghosha was the eldest of the three Ghosha brothers (Govinda, Madhava, Vasudeva), kirtana singers of the
Lord (CC Adi 10, Madhya 13). The journey to East Bengal: CB Adi 14, CC Adi 16, Murari 1.11.5–16, Lochana
(`lochana/en/20.md`, vv. 61–103). Majumdar's argument: the pada has no hint of Nimai's divinity or of kirtana — the poet
knows Him only as a youth he has seen on the bank of the Ganga; such simplicity is natural only in verses made before the
change at Gaya. In PK the pada stands as a gaura-chandrika (prelude) to the section on "distant separation" (Krishna's
departure for Mathura).
[^2]: Lakshmipriya was the Lord's first wife. According to CB (Adi 14) and Murari (1.11.17–20), while the Lord was in East
Bengal she left this world, bitten by the "serpent of separation"; the pada finds her still alive, pining for her husband.
[^3]: Malini, the wife of Shrivasa Pandita, was a close friend of Shachi.

## 2. Vasu Ghosha — "śaṅkha dundubhi-nāda…" (BR, p. 893; PK 1536)
*Lila: the abhisheka-bathing of the Lord in Shrivasa's house (the mahaprakasha), 1509. According to Majumdar, the song of
an eyewitness; raga bhupali (PK), suhai (BR).*[^4]

**2.1.** Conchs and the roll of kettledrums sound melodiously:
the companions perform the abhisheka of Gorachanda.

**2.2.** Fragrant sandal on the stone, incense, lamps alight;
the women of the town bring platters of arghya.[^5]

**2.3.** All the people of Nadia look on and rejoice;
again and again they cry "Jaya, jaya!" and sing songs.

**2.4.** All gaze upon the face of Gorachanda;
Vasu Ghosha sings the sweetness of Gora's abhisheka.

[^4]: Vasudeva (Vasu) Ghosha was the youngest of the Ghosha brothers and the most prolific of Gaura's singers. The great
bathing of the Lord in Shrivasa's house: CB Madhya 9; Murari 2.12; Lochana (`lochana/en/32.md`, vv. 93–113); Karnapura,
*Chaitanya-chandrodaya*, act 1. In the *Bhakti-ratnakara* (wave 12) Narahari Chakravarti cites precisely this pada as an
eyewitness's testimony, though he had Murari, CB and CC to hand — and on this Majumdar rests its early date. In PK it
stands as a gaura-chandrika to the snana-yatra (the bathing of Jagannatha at the full moon of Jyeshtha).
[^5]: Arghya is a respectful offering of water (with flowers, rice and durva grass). Majumdar notes that the pada keeps a
detail the biographies pass over — the part women took in the rite.

## 3. Vasu Ghosha — "taila haridrā āra kuṅkuma kastūrī…" (PK 1537)
*Lila: the same abhisheka — the anointing, bathing and dressing of the Lord. Raga varadi.*[^6]

**3.1.** Oil, turmeric, kunkuma and musk
the women, one after another, apply to Gora's body.

**3.2.** Bringing scented water, they fill the pitchers
and mix fragrant sandal and other things into it.

**3.3.** Crying "Jaya, jaya!" they pour the water over Gora's body;
wiping the holy body, someone dresses Him in garments.

**3.4.** Behold, in the bathing pavilion — Gora, king of dancers!
With joy at heart Vasudeva Ghosha sings.

[^6]: In PK (and in GPT1) this pada follows directly on the preceding one; Majumdar assigns it to the same abhisheka of
1509 (PK 1536, 1537, 1571 — the "historical" padas of Vasu Ghosha). "King of dancers" (nata-raya) is a frequent name
for Gaura, the leader of the dancers in kirtana, among the kirtana poets.

## 4. Govinda, Madhava and Vasu Ghosha — "vasilā gaurāṅgacandra ratna-siṁhāsane…" (PK 1538)
*Lila: after the abhisheka the Lord on the throne: the feast, Shachi's arati, the worship of the devotees. Signed by all
three Ghosha brothers.*[^7]

**4.1.** Gaurangachandra sat upon the jewelled throne;
Shrivasa Pandita anoints His body with sandal.

**4.2.** Gadadhara placed a garland of malati on His neck;
by the radiance of His beauty all ten directions were lit.

**4.3.** Many offerings, every kind of sweet dish
He ate, sitting together with Nityananda.

**4.4.** Having chewed tambula, He sat on the throne again;
Shachi-devi came together with Malini.

**4.5.** Lighting the five-wicked lamp, she performed arati,
waved [the flame] round Him and placed paddy and durva on His head.[^8]

**4.6.** At that moment all shower Him with flowers;
Advaita Acharya offers tulasi and sandal.

**4.7.** Gods and men come together to see it;
Nityananda, sitting at His right, watches with delight.

**4.8.** This is the matchless lila of Gora's abhisheka:
Govinda, Madhava and Vasu floated away in prema.

[^7]: Majumdar cites this pada together with the preceding ones as a description of the same abhisheka in Shrivasa's
house; from the signature "Govinda, Madhava, Vasu" (as in Vasu Ramananda) he infers that Govinda was the eldest. The
Lord sitting on Vishnu's throne in Shrivasa's house, accepting offerings and receiving the devotees' worship: CB Madhya 9;
CC Adi 17.
[^8]: Paddy (dhanya) and durva grass placed on the head are a Bengali mother's blessing.

## 5. Ramachandra — "pahu mora gaurāṅga rāya…" (BR, p. 919; PK 2186)
*Lila: the Lord in Navadvipa in the mood of Radha, with Gadadhara and Narahari beside Him. According to Majumdar, the
song of an eyewitness (Narahari Chakravarti cites it as testimony). Raga shri (PK), suhai (BR).*[^9]

**5.1.** *(Refrain)* My Lord is Gauranga Raya,
whose glories Shiva, Shuka and Brahma sing!

**5.2.** He for whom Kamala ever pines,[^10]
that very Lord raises His arms, weeps and cries out, "Hari!"

**5.3.** The body at whose sight Kamadeva became bodiless[^11]
is now ever grey with the dust of kirtana.

**5.4.** Now and again He starts up, crying, "Radha, Radha!" —
Gadadhara and Narahari stand gazing at His face.

**5.5.** His body thrills with the deep love of former days.
Says Ramachandra: who would not understand this play?

[^9]: Who this Ramachandra was is unclear: Majumdar rejects Ramachandra Puri, Ramachandra the brahmana and Ramachandra
Khan, and suggests Ramachandra Kaviraja of Nityananda's branch (not Narottama's friend, whom Narahari Chakravarti would
not have passed off as an eyewitness). In BR (wave 12) the pada stands among testimonies to the Navadvipa pastimes,
directly after Govinda Ghosha's pada on the Lord's dancing with Narahari. Narahari Sarakara Thakura (of Shrikhanda) was a
singer of Gaura's moods; Majumdar confirms his presence with the Lord in Navadvipa from the padas of other
contemporaries. Cf. CC Adi 17 (Radha's mood in Navadvipa).
[^10]: Kamala is Lakshmi, the consort of Narayana. Here she is only mentioned as longing for the Lord; cf. *Bhag.* 10.16.36
(Lakshmi longs for the dust of Krishna's feet).
[^11]: The Lord's beauty put Kama (Kamadeva, the god of love) to shame, and he was as if bereft of his body (Ananga, "the
Bodiless", is his name). The "love of former days" (v. 5.5) is Radha's love for Krishna, which the Lord tastes in the
form of Gaura. BR reads: "He gazes at the faces of Narahari and Gadadhara."
