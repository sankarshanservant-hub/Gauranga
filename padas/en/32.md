# Nagari Padas — VI: the "Narahari" padas of GPT (sect. 3.2, nos. 146–168): the townswomen's daydreams and the talk of the heavenly women

*On what nagari-bhava is, the position of the tradition (Vrindavana Dasa, Krishnadasa Kaviraja, Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati) and the rules of translation, see the introductory note to the section of nagari padas in portion 27 (`en/27.md`); on the two poets named Narahari (Narahari Sarakara and Narahari Chakravarti) and on the attribution labels ("probably Sarakara", "probably Chakravarti", "not established"), see the introductory note to the "Narahari" appendix in portion 17 (`en/17.md`).*

## 1. Narahari — "śuna śuna vadhū eta dine vidhi…" (GPT1 pp. 221–222, no. 146; GCC 9.2)
*Nagari-bhava (a mother-in-law's speech). An elderly woman tells her daughters-in-law: yesterday an astrologer came to their house who knows what is in
everyone's mind; when she asked how good fortune might come, he told her to honour Shachi-devi, Nimaichand's mother: the dust of her feet removes the
threefold miseries, whoever sees her face at dawn knows no sorrow, and whoever reviles her is destroyed by the goddess Ugrachanda; so every morning she
should send her daughters-in-law to her house. The mother-in-law hurries the young wives off to Shachi — and they, covering their faces, make a show of
pleading their household work. Raga "yatharaga". **Attribution: probably Chakravarti** — GCC, kirana 9, pada 2.*[^1]

**1.1.** "Listen, listen, daughters-in-law: at last the Creator has become gracious to me! Yesterday, at the time of the day-watch, an astrologer came to our house.

**1.2.** What shall I say of his virtues — such a one, truly, I have never seen here: whatever anyone asks, he tells it all — he knows what is in a person's mind.

**1.3.** 'How will good fortune come?' I said, and clasped his feet. Seeing my anxiety, he says: 'Do not worry at all, mother.

**1.4.** In your village lives Shachi-devi — do you not know her greatness? She is supremely honoured in the world; her deeds are known to all.

**1.5.** Very easily had is the dust of her feet: whoever places it on his head will have wealth and kin — but what is great in that! — at once it removes the threefold miseries.

**1.6.** Whoever rises at dawn and sees her face floats in happiness birth after birth and never knows sorrow.

**1.7.** Whoever reviles Mother Shachi burns to death in the fire of sorrow; know for certain: the goddess Ugrachanda destroys him.

**1.8.** Therefore, instructing your daughters-in-law and giving up all guile of heart, send them with care at daybreak to the house of Sri Shachi-devi.

**1.9.** She will be gracious and bless them — and the heart's wish will be fulfilled: prosperity will grow, there will always be much happiness, misfortunes will be destroyed.

**1.10.** Know for sure: Nimaichand's mother grieves at others' grief.' Saying much like this, the worthy astrologer went on to another house.[^2]

**1.11.** Hearing all this I was very glad: I felt in my heart as if all ill fortune had gone.

**1.12.** So go quickly: she is my own — she calls me 'elder sister', treats me fondly, and never regards anyone as a stranger.

**1.13.** When you get there, bow to her and speak humble words: by her grace all happiness will come — this I know for certain.

**1.14.** She will say to you all: 'Stay here this morning,' — then come back early on some pretext: for I myself shall be going there."

**1.15.** Hearing the mother-in-law's most eager words, [the young wives] are overjoyed; and, to be coaxed a little more, one says very softly, covering her face with her cloth:

**1.16.** "How can we go in the morning, leaving the household work?" Narahari says: "You will come back and do it; now there must be no delay."[^3]

[^1]: Signature — "Narahari". GPT1 — pp. 221–222, no. 146 (scan PDF pp. 480–481); GPT2 — sect. 3.2, no. 146 (catalogue: NG-117). GCC — kirana 9, pada 2 (ed. 1947,
pp. 106–107; scan PDF pp. 111–112), "vṛddhāha — yathārāga" ("the old woman said"). The ninth kirana of GCC ("Again in the morning — the outings of the nagaris of Navadvipa,
their wishes, their darshan and so on"; it opens with GCC 9.1, no. 23 of portion 31) tells how the mothers-in-law themselves send their daughters-in-law to Shachi-devi;
GPT omits GCC 9.3–9.5 — the mother-in-law coaxes the young wives again, they dress, bow to her and set out (9.3), the joyful procession of the young women (9.4, Vrajabuli,
raga lalita) and the first of their "wishes" — to bow to Shachi and steal a glance at Gauranga (9.5). Before 9.5 the author has a Sanskrit rubric, "their wishes
(manoratha) he relates in order": the following padas (nos. 2–12 of this portion) are these wishes. GCC readings: "parama ānanda" (1.11), "yāha śīghra kari" (1.12),
"āsiyā karibe" (1.16). B. Nagari-bhava — see the introductory note to the section (portion 27).
[^2]: The astrologer's speech is a praise of Shachi-devi, the Lord's mother: so the Vaishnava biographies too honour her (CB, Adi and Madhya — Shachi as the mother of the
Lord, worthy of the whole world's reverence). The "threefold miseries" (tritāpa) — those caused by one's own body and mind, by other beings and by the forces of nature.
Ugrachanda (ugracaṇḍā) is a fierce form of Durga (Chandi); the threat to those who revile Shachi is the village astrologer's word, rendered as in the original. The
promise "will have wealth and kin — but what is great in that" (1.5) the poet puts in the astrologer's mouth so that the mother-in-law, mindful of prosperity, will
gladly let the young wives go (cf. the end of no. 23 of portion 31: "there is nothing in the world but the craving for wealth").
[^3]: 1.15–1.16: the young wives, longing to see Gaura, make a show of pleading their household work "to be coaxed" — the poet's smile; rendered as in the original.
The last line can be understood either as the mother-in-law's answer or as the poet's own word ("Narahari says…"); it is left as in the original.

## 2. Narahari — "sakhīsaha sukhe śrīśacīdevīra…" (GPT1 p. 222, no. 147; GCC 9.6)
*Nagari-bhava; a daydream. On the way to Shachi's house a townswoman dreams: she will stand with her friend in Shachi-devi's courtyard and steal a
glance at Gaura; He will answer with a sly look — and she will have neither composure nor dharma left, she will tremble at Madana's arrow and strain
to embrace Gaurangachand, but her friend will hold her back by the end of her sari; the others will laugh, she will be abashed and turn away, covering
her face — and Gaura will smile. Raga "yatharaga". **Attribution: probably Chakravarti** — GCC, kirana 9, pada 6.*[^4]

**2.1.** "Gladly, with my friend, I shall go and stand in the courtyard of Sri Shachi-devi; unnoticed, I shall glance at Him once — and cool my eyes and heart.

**2.2.** He in turn will look at me — with the sly signal of His eyes; I shall have neither composure nor dharma left — I shall tremble at Madana's arrow.

**2.3.** My body will be wet with sweat, my hair will keep coming loose; my cloth will slip again and again — not a trace of shame will remain.

**2.4.** I shall be all too eager to embrace Gaurangachand — my friend will hold me back by the end of my sari; I shall heed her word.

**2.5.** Seeing me so, the others will laugh a good deal among themselves; understanding all that, I shall grow calm at heart, a little abashed.

**2.6.** Turning away, I shall stand again, covering my face with my cloth; the Lord of Narahari's life, seeing this, will smile with joy of heart."[^5]

[^4]: Signature — "Narahari". GPT1 — p. 222, no. 147 (scan PDF p. 481); GPT2 — sect. 3.2, no. 147 (catalogue: NG-118). GCC — kirana 9, pada 6 (ed. 1947, p. 109; scan
PDF p. 114), "kācicca — yathārāga" ("and another [said]"). This is the second of the "wishes" (manoratha) of the townswomen on their way to Shachi's house (note 1):
all the padas nos. 2–12 are in the future tense ("I shall stand", "I shall glance", "He will say"), i.e. not an account of what happened but what each one pictures to
herself on the way. GCC reading: "kām̐piba madana-bhare" — "I shall tremble under Madana's weight" (2.2). B. Nagari-bhava — see the introductory note to the section
(portion 27).
[^5]: 2.2–2.4: "I shall tremble at Madana's arrow", "neither composure nor dharma left", the slipping cloth, the urge to embrace Gaurangachand — a direct motif of
attraction; rendered exactly, adding nothing and softening nothing. It is the townswoman's daydream, her imagination: in the pada itself she is held back, and Gaura
only smiles. According to the biographies, in Navadvipa the Lord did not look at women or touch them (CB, Adi); the Gaudiya tradition does not accept the worship of
Gaura as a nagara (introductory note, portion 27; Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati counts "gaura-nagari" among the apasampradayas). The padas of this cycle belong to
Narahari Chakravarti (18th c.); Majumdar regards the long GPT padas signed Narahari as late, not Sarakara's.

## 3. Narahari — "saiyera samīpe dām̐ṛāiba punaḥ…" (GPT1 pp. 222–223, no. 148; GCC 9.7)
*Nagari-bhava; a daydream (feigned mana). A townswoman dreams: at her friend's sign she will stand beside her, turned away from Gaura; unable to see
her face, He will be troubled and with the corner of His eye will ask her friend: "Why such anger? Forgive — one's own are not blamed; tell her to lift
her veil just once." The friend will entreat her, she in feigned anger will go and stand far off; Gauranga will be bathed in tears, the friend will
clasp her feet — and then, smiling, she will lift her veil and glance sidelong, giving Him supreme joy. Raga "yatharaga". **Attribution: probably
Chakravarti** — GCC, kirana 9, pada 7.*[^6]

**3.1.** "At my friend's sign I shall stand beside her again; I shall not look at Gaura the nagara — I shall stay turned away.

**3.2.** Unable to see my face, He will be much troubled; glancing unnoticed at my friend, He will say to her with the corner of His eye:

**3.3.** 'I do not understand at all: why such cruel anger? Forgive — no one holds it against one's own.

**3.4.** Tell her to lift her veil just once, adjuring her by Me: let My eyes and heart be cooled by beholding the sweetness of that face.'

**3.5.** Understanding this, my friend will entreat me: 'Take the cloth from your face and stand there — let Gaurahari see.'

**3.6.** Hearing this, I shall pretend not to hear; then she will lift it with her own hand — but I shall stop her and in great anger go and stand far off.

**3.7.** Seeing this, Gauranga Raya will be bathed in tears; seeing that, my friend, distressed, will clasp my feet.

**3.8.** Then, smiling, I shall lift my veil and glance sidelong — I shall give the Lord of Narahari's life, the Friend, supreme joy."[^7]

[^6]: Signature — "Narahari". GPT1 — pp. 222–223, no. 148 (scan PDF pp. 481–482); GPT2 — sect. 3.2, no. 148 (catalogue: NG-119). GCC — kirana 9, pada 7 (ed. 1947,
pp. 109–110; scan PDF pp. 114–115). GCC readings: "keha nā laoye doṣa" (3.3), "juṛāuka nayāna hiyā" (3.4), "terachha nayāne raba" — "I shall remain [standing],
glancing sidelong" (3.8). B. Nagari-bhava — see the introductory note to the section (portion 27).
[^7]: Feigned anger (mana) and entreaty through a friend are motifs of the padas on Radha's mana, transferred to a townswoman's daydream; Gauranga's tears (3.7) and
His plea (3.3–3.4) are what she imagines; rendered as in the original. "Friend" (bandhuyā) and "the Lord of Narahari's life" are the townswoman's words and the
poet's signature about Gaura (cf. note 5 of portion 31).

## 4. Narahari — "gaura nāgara rasera sāgara…" (GPT1 p. 223, no. 149; GCC 9.8)
*Nagari-bhava; a daydream. A townswoman dreams: looking at Gaura the nagara, the ocean of rasa, she will smile and talk with her friend about rasa;
seeing her wondrous grace, He will float in joy; she will signal to Him with a curved glance, bite her lip and draw her veil, and with the aim of her
brows steal away His composure; the King of connoisseurs of rasa will long to embrace her — and Narahari will restrain Him, reminding Him of what
people would say. Raga "yatharaga". **Attribution: probably Chakravarti** — GCC, kirana 9, pada 8.*[^8]

**4.1.** "Looking at Gaura the nagara, the ocean of rasa, with a faint smile I shall tell my friend tales of rasa.

**4.2.** Seeing my wondrous grace, He will float in joy; smiling ever so slightly, I shall signal to Him with a curved glance.

**4.3.** Understanding that, I shall bite my lip and draw my veil; unnoticed, by the aim of my brows, I shall steal away the Friend's composure.

**4.4.** The King of connoisseurs of rasa will long to embrace me — and Narahari will carefully restrain Him, reminding Him of what people would say."[^9]

[^8]: Signature — "Narahari". GPT1 — p. 223, no. 149 (scan PDF p. 482); GPT2 — sect. 3.2, no. 149 (catalogue: NG-120). GCC — kirana 9, pada 8 (ed. 1947, p. 110;
scan PDF p. 115). GCC readings: "alakhita bhuru-sandhāne" (4.3), "bujhāyā" (4.4). B. Nagari-bhava — see the introductory note to the section (portion 27).
[^9]: 4.4: Gaura longing for the townswoman's embrace is again her imagination (cf. note 5); rendered exactly. The poet places himself in the heroine's daydream as
the Lord's companion who restrains Him by "shame before people" (lokera lāja). According to the biographies, the Lord in Navadvipa was strict and did not look at
women (CB, Adi; cf. nos. 19–20 of this portion, where Narahari's own heroines say the same).

## 5. Narahari — "saiyera nikaṭe dām̐ṛāba ghuṅaṭe…" (GPT1 p. 223, no. 150; GCC 9.12)
*Nagari-bhava; a daydream. A townswoman dreams: she will stand beside her friend with half her face veiled and steal glances; when the Friend looks
at her with a half-smile, she will look away; out of shyness He will lower His eyes — and then from the corner of her eye she will look at the moon of
His face; He will not be able to meet her eyes, and she will keep gazing. Raga "yatharaga". **Attribution: probably Chakravarti** — GCC, kirana 9,
pada 12.*[^10]

**5.1.** "I shall stand near my friend, half my face covered by my veil; glancing little by little, unnoticed, I shall fulfil my heart's desire.

**5.2.** When the Friend looks at me with a half-smile, I shall understand, at once turn my eyes away and keep looking elsewhere.

**5.3.** The Beloved of my life, out of shyness, will charmingly lower His eyes; seeing that, I shall again look from the corner of my eye at the moon of His face.

**5.4.** The Restless One will not be able to meet my eyes — and I, seeing that, shall keep gazing." Narahari's Lord will float in joy as eyes meet eyes.

[^10]: Signature — "Narahari". GPT1 — p. 223, no. 150 (scan PDF p. 482); GPT2 — sect. 3.2, no. 150 (catalogue: NG-350). GCC — kirana 9, pada 12 (ed. 1947, pp. 112–113;
scan PDF pp. 117–118), "anyā ca — yathārāga" ("and another"). In GCC this pada comes after GCC 9.11 (= no. 153, no. 8 of this portion); the compiler of GPT moved it.
"The Restless One" (cañcala, 5.4) refers to Gaura. The last line (signature) is the poet's word; it can also be read as continuing the townswoman's daydream ("…will
float in joy, His eyes meeting [mine]"). B. Nagari-bhava — see the introductory note to the section (portion 27).

## 6. Narahari — "āi more bahu yatana karibe…" (GPT1 p. 224, no. 151; GCC 9.9)
*Nagari-bhava; a daydream. A townswoman dreams: Ai (Shachi) will be kind to her, but she will not stay by Ai — she will stand behind her friend and
gaze her fill at Gaura while remaining unseen; He will grow anxious and ask her friend with a sign of the eyes: "Where is your companion?"; the friend
will draw her forward and lift her veil; the Crown of connoisseurs of rasa will say with the corner of His eye: "Where did you learn these tricks? How
shall I live without seeing you?" — and Narahari will make peace between them. Raga "yatharaga". **Attribution: probably Chakravarti** — GCC,
kirana 9, pada 9.*[^11]

**6.1.** "Ai will be very kind to me — but I shall not stay by Ai: quite unnoticed, I shall stand behind my friend.

**6.2.** Supremely joyful, I shall still the hunger of many days: I shall give the chakora birds of my eyes the nectar of that moon of a face to drink.

**6.3.** I shall look my fill, but He will not be able to see me; growing anxious, He will say to my friend by a sign of the eyes:

**6.4.** 'You have come alone — where has your companion stayed? Not seeing you two together, I am pained at heart.'

**6.5.** Understanding this, my friend will take me by the hand and draw me in front of her; feigning anger, smiling slightly, I shall not go forward.

**6.6.** Even so my friend will hold me in front of her and with her own hand lift my veil, teasing me no end.

**6.7.** By a sign of the eyes she will say to the Friend: 'Look at Your own,' — and the Crown of connoisseurs of rasa, looking at me, will say with the corner of His eye:

**6.8.** 'Well, well! Where did you learn these tricks? Tell Me now: how shall I live without seeing you?'

**6.9.** He will convey much like this; understanding, I shall own my fault — and yet the Crown of connoisseurs of rasa, Gora, will still be cross with me.

**6.10.** Then Narahari will make it up: he will bring [me], show the necklace on the neck — and [He], smiling slightly, will say: 'Never do so again.'"[^12]

[^11]: Signature — "Narahari". GPT1 — p. 224, no. 151 (scan PDF p. 483); GPT2 — sect. 3.2, no. 151 (catalogue: NG-351). GCC — kirana 9, pada 9 (ed. 1947, pp. 110–111;
scan PDF pp. 115–116). In GCC the second line of couplet 6.6 is omitted: in its place the edition has a row of asterisks (a mark of omission); otherwise there are minor
differences ("parama ānanda haiyā", 6.2; "antare pāiye vyathā", 6.4; "bandhu prati", 6.7). B. Nagari-bhava — see the introductory note to the section (portion 27).
[^12]: Ai ("mother") is Shachi-devi (cf. note 1); the townswoman dreams of being received kindly by Shachi. The silent conversation of the eyes (6.3–6.8) is what she
imagines (cf. note 5). 6.9–6.10 are understood conjecturally: who is "cross" and whose necklace Narahari will "show" (perhaps as a token of reconciliation) is not
clear from the text; GCC has "will make it up, yielding, will show the necklace on the neck" (mānāba māniyā dekhābe). The verb forms in -ba (niba, kariba) are used
here by the poet for the third person as well (the friend, Gora), for the sake of rhyme.

## 7. Narahari — "gaurāṅgacām̐dera pāne nirakhite…" (GPT1 pp. 224–225, no. 152; GCC 9.10)
*Nagari-bhava; a daydream. A townswoman dreams: gazing at Gaurangachand she will fall into a daze, be overcome, and her earring will roll on the
ground; she will hastily cover herself with her sari-end — and the jingle of her bangles will delight Him; with a glance He will ask: "Remove the cloth
— let Me see your hair"; her friend will tell her to turn away, and then herself throw the cloth off her head; Gora, enraptured, will gaze without
blinking, while she, abashed, will pull up her veil, scold her friend as "shameless" and step aside — to Gauranga's delight. Raga "yatharaga".
**Attribution: probably Chakravarti** — GCC, kirana 9, pada 10.*[^13]

**7.1.** "Gazing at Gaurangachand, I shall fall into a deep daze: I shall be overcome, an earring will slip and roll on the ground.

**7.2.** I shall quickly cover myself with my sari-end — with that my hand will move: hearing the jingle of the bangles on my wrist, He will be delighted.

**7.3.** With the corner of a sidelong glance Gauranga, the allure of the world, will convey: 'Remove the cloth for a moment — let Me see how beautiful your hair is.'

**7.4.** Understanding this, I shall smile slightly and cover my face with my veil; seeing me shy, He will be delighted and again convey something to my friend.

**7.5.** My clever friend will say to me: 'Stand turned away — otherwise, looking at Gauranga, you will lose all calm.'

**7.6.** At these words I shall forget myself and stand with my back to Gora — and my friend with her own hand will quickly throw the cloth off my head.

**7.7.** At that moment Gora, rapt in rasa, quite overcome, will remember nothing — He will keep gazing with unblinking eyes.

**7.8.** I, much abashed, shall hastily pull the cloth up over my head and, with outward anger, scold my friend, calling her shameless.

**7.9.** I shall go and stand a little way off from my friend — and she, owning her fault, will draw me back and hold me, swearing by my head.

**7.10.** Seeing this play of mine, Gauranga, lover of play, will again float in merriment," — and in the depths of his heart Narahari, the Friend's companion, will laugh.[^14]

[^13]: Signature — "Narahari". GPT1 — pp. 224–225, no. 152 (scan PDF pp. 483–484); GPT2 — sect. 3.2, no. 152 (catalogue: NG-121). GCC — kirana 9, pada 10 (ed. 1947,
pp. 111–112; scan PDF pp. 116–117). Two places in GPT1 are corrected after GCC (noted in VAR): 7.2 "jhām̐piba" ("I shall cover myself"; GPT1 "kām̐pila" — "trembled",
a misprint) and 7.6 "pichu kariyā" ("turning my back"; GPT1 "kichu kariyā"). B. Nagari-bhava — see the introductory note to the section (portion 27).
[^14]: Gora, enraptured and oblivious at the sight of the townswoman's uncovered head (7.7), is her daydream, as in all the padas of this cycle (note 5); rendered
exactly. A married woman's uncovered head before a strange man was by custom immodest; hence "shameless" (7.8) and the heroine's feigned indignation.

## 10. Narahari — "saiyera samīpe dām̐ṛāba nāgara…" (GPT1 p. 226, no. 155; GCC 9.14)
*Nagari-bhava; a daydream. A townswoman dreams: she will stand beside her friend, and the nagara will not look at her but only exchange smiling

**21.2.** Ah, ah! He knows nothing at all — I have never seen such a one! Utterly master of the senses, like the king of sages — His righteousness is renowned!

**21.3.** He does not listen to talk of women — this fame is known in the world; He never leaves His own house to go into anyone's house on any business!

**21.4.** So many such peerless virtues He has — how many of them will you tell? And as many as there are within — those He does not show outwardly.

**21.5.** So I say, listen: others have no power to know those virtues — they appear only in the flash of these young women's glances.

**21.6.** Your eyes, I see, like those of [some] birds, do not see the moon of the night: how should others know that Narahari's Lord is a snare that enchants women?"[^39]

pp. 126–127; scan PDF pp. 131–132); it is preceded by the short pada 10.20 (not in GPT): "these merry ones, hearing the admonition, laughed to themselves and answered

life — and that is the very notion of gaura-nagari, which the tradition does not accept (introductory note, portion 27; cf. notes 30, 35).

## 22. Narahari — "hera āisa prāṇa sajani ihāte…" (GPT1 p. 231, no. 167; GCC 10.22)
*Nagari-bhava (the dispute). The mocker's friend says to her: "Leave it — you hold these secret talks of rasa with her in vain: she is simple and does not
walk this path; one who delights in the formless will not like form. Ask her instead: if He is so master of His senses, why does He take on the guise of
a nagara in the midst of Nadia, why do women lose their shame at the fragrance of His body, why does He, seeing a man's face and mistaking it for a woman's,
fall in a swoon, why does He dance in the assembly of the virtuous taking a woman's name and repeat 'woman' in His mind, why does He gather so many young
women in His house? Let her answer." Raga "yatharaga". **Attribution: probably Chakravarti** — GCC, kirana 10, pada 22.*[^40]

**22.1.** "Come over here, dear friend: this brings no joy to the heart — in vain you hold these secret talks of rasa with her.

**22.2.** Who but a woman versed in rasa can understand the heart of a rasika? And she is very simple — she never walks this path.

**22.3.** However much you explain, she has no concern with it: one whose delight is in the formless — will form ever please her?

**22.4.** If ever she sincerely keeps your rare company, then she will understand what the play of the Moon of Nadia is.

**22.5.** Let all this be now; ask her just once: being so much master of His senses, how does He sport like this?

**22.6.** He who knows nothing [of this], in whom is no trace of pleasure — why does He take on the guise of a nagara in the midst of the town of Nadia?

**22.7.** Nowhere have I heard of this: is this the deed of a noble person? With the fragrance of His body He breaks the reserve and shame of the women-bees.

**22.8.** He who is so grave — are such deeds His, how do they please His mind? Seeing a man's face and mistaking it for a woman's face, He falls in a swoon.

**22.9.** This is very strange: one who has so much modesty — is such desire His? In the assembly of the virtuous He dances unceasingly, taking a woman's name.

**22.10.** He who never hears talk of women with His own ears — how is it that He constantly repeats "woman" in His mind?

**22.11.** He who in the world is righteous as no one else — for what reason does He gather so many young women in His own house?

**22.12.** Such are the ways of Narahari's Lord: tell her to answer this — how has such faith in such a one arisen in a generous heart?"[^41]

## 23. Narahari — "śuna śuna ogo sakala bujhinu…" (GPT1 pp. 231–233, no. 168; GCC 10.24)
*Nagari-bhava (the end of the dispute). Yet another, skilled in speech, says: "She is not at fault — you dispute in vain. Do not be sad, good and fair one:
you say Gora does not look at others' women — how can I believe it, when the nagara's play in Nadia is beyond counting and I have seen it myself? And if
you ask how this is possible in this descent — no one gives up his nature: this son of Shachi is the son of Yashoda; for the gopis' sake He herded cows and
lifted the hill, was subject to them, sought their touch, clasped a friend's feet to end a gopi's mana, carried [a gopi] on His shoulders, and their scolding
was sweeter to Him than the hymns of the Vedas; how would He give up such a nature?" Raga "yatharaga". **Attribution: probably Chakravarti** — GCC,
kirana 10, pada 24.*[^42]

**23.1.** "Listen, listen: I have understood everything — she is not at fault; it is with you all that I am angry, for your disputing.

**23.2.** If someone says something without understanding — should one laugh at that? To explain in the way she can understand — that I would think wise.

**23.3.** She is of good conduct; ah, such a one you do not know how to bring round. Be silent now and listen to what I say.

**23.4.** Come over here, good and fair one, do not be sad: hearing your words, my heart is overjoyed.

**23.5.** You say that Gora does not look at others' women even from the corner of His eye — how can I believe all this?

**23.6.** If I could see even a little of what you praise — then, swearing an oath and saying 'certainly', I would come to believe.

**23.7.** In the town of Nadia the nagara's play is beyond counting; be it as it may with others' words — this I have seen myself.

**23.8.** If you say: how is this possible in this descent? — there is a well-known reason for it; all people speak of it.

**23.9.** Whatever nature one has, no one can ever abandon it: one acts according to one's nature, and no one's prohibition avails.

**23.10.** If you think such a nature of His is seen nowhere — to that I tell you, listen: the world is witness to it.

**23.11.** This son of Shachi is the son of Yashoda: do you not know that? Need I tell you of all the secret pastimes in Vrindavana?

**23.12.** For the gopis' sake — the herding of cows, the lifting of the hill and all else; how much shall I tell of the lilas with the gopis, whatever and wherever they were!

**23.13.** Utterly subject to them all, not seeing them for a moment He feels it an aeon; with so many pretexts He goes about constantly, hoping for the touch of their bodies!

**23.14.** If some [gopi] in mana, being offended, will not turn to look at Kanu, — to end her mana He clasps her friend's feet.

**23.15.** He carries [her] on His shoulders, deeming it His supreme good fortune; hearing their scolding words gives Him more joy than the hymns of the Vedas.

**23.16.** He who for the sake of young women does not mind grave disgrace in the world — tell me now: how would He give up such a nature?

**23.17.** Know this for certain, reflecting in your hearts: by His nature He does all this — you will understand of yourselves.

**23.18.** Narahari's Lord is the Crown of connoisseurs of rasa, who has no equal: who does not know these deeds? What further doubt is there?"[^43]

## Summary of portion 32

*The portion continues the padas signed "Narahari" from section 3.2 of GPT. All 23 padas have been found in Narahari Chakravarti's "Gaura-charita-chintamani" and
checked against its scan ("probably Chakravarti" — B). No. 146 (no. 1) = GCC 9.2 — a mother-in-law, on an astrologer's word, sends her daughters-in-law to
Shachi-devi. Nos. 147–157 (nos. 2–12) = GCC 9.6–9.16 — the "wishes" (manoratha) of the townswomen on the way to Shachi's house, all in the future tense: to steal a
glance at Gaura, feigned mana, a silent conversation of the eyes, reproaches; in GCC no. 150 comes after no. 153 (9.12). Nos. 158–168 (nos. 13–23) = GCC 10.10–10.24 —
according to GCC this is the conversation and dispute of heavenly women (devaramaṇī) looking down on Nadia from the sky: some speak of the "secret play of the nagara",
others (nos. 19–20) answer with a description of the Lord's strictness — He does not look at women, does not listen to talk of them, bathes in a secluded place — and
call slander of Him an aparadha; the dispute ends with mockery and the argument "Shachi's son is Yashoda's son, a nature cannot be given up" (nos. 21–23). Section 3.2 of GPT omits GCC
9.3–9.5, 9.17–9.23, 10.1–10.9, 10.11, 10.18, 10.20, 10.23 (some of them — 9.18–9.19, 9.21–9.22, 10.7–10.9 — stand in other sections of GPT, see the concluding note of portion 33). Where GCC is more explicit than GPT (no. 9 — "the cloth over the breast" instead of "the cloth over the face";
no. 10 — "wants to embrace" instead of "wants to see your dear face"; no. 2 — "under Madana's weight"), the GPT text is translated and the GCC reading is noted in VAR
and in a note. The motifs of attraction in the townswomen's daydreams and the talk of the "nagara" are rendered exactly, without softening, with notes on the position
of the tradition (notes 5, 25, 27, 30, 41, 43).*
