# Krishnadasa, Kanu Dasa, Vasu Ghosha, Yadunandana, Nayanananda, Yadu, Ramananda, Chaitanyadasa: the rest of the "GPT only" group and finds of a full check of signatures

*This portion completes the group of padas by the other poets (apart from Narahari) that are known from the "Gaura-pada-tarangini"
(GPT1 — 1st ed. by Jagadbandhu Bhadra, 1903; GPT2 — 2nd ed., 1934), without the padas in nagari-bhava. It contains padas missed by
earlier checks (in the OCR text of GPT1 some headings "N পদ" lack the danda, and such padas merged with their neighbours) and finds
of a full manual re-check of the signatures of all contemporary poets throughout GPT1. Four of the finds turned out not to be "GPT
only": they are in the "Padakalpataru" (PK 2099 — Yadunandana, PK 2101 — Yadu, also in the "Bhakti-ratnakara", PK 2083 — unsigned,
PK 1867 — Chaitanyadasa); there the text is given from PK with the readings of GPT. The padas follow the order of the lilas: praise of
the Gaura-avatara, Advaita's call and the Lord's appearance, gostha-bhava and the kirtans of Navadvipa, Shachi's lament after the
sannyasa, the Lord's bhavas in Puri, Nityananda. The language of some padas (nos. 1, 3–5, 14) has New Bengali forms — by these they
are probably late; Majumdar in general considers GPT unreliable for attribution. Hence the attribution of padas known only from GPT is
conditional (C, for the late ones D). The Bengali original, variant readings and word-for-word translation are in `bn/15.md`.*

## 1. Krishnadasa — "āre re ninduka bhāi…" (GPT1 p. 14, no. 35)
*Rebuke of a blasphemer: Gauranga is the essence of all avataras; when drunkenness, killing of the unborn and debauchery under the
guise of tantric religion had overrun Bharata and people had begun to worship yakshas, rakshasas and Vishahari, Sri Chaitanya
appeared in Navadvipa, gave everyone the name of Hari, saved Jagai and Madhai, united brahmanas with yavanas; whoever does not
believe this has no deliverance. Raga dhanashri.*[^1]

**1.1.** "Hey, brother blasphemer, have you no understanding at all? In vain you bear a pair of eyes!
The essence of all avataras is the avatara of Sri Gauranga — and you pass Him by with disregard.

**1.2.** Drunkenness, violence, killing of the unborn, debauchery — under the guise of tantric religion they overran Bharata;
yakshas, rakshasas and Vishahari people began to worship with all kinds of offerings.[^2]

**1.3.** Seeing the misery of the living beings, my Lord Sri Chaitanya appeared in Navadvipa:
the saving name of Hari — the taraka-brahma — He Himself offered and gave to all, and removed the decline of dharma.[^3]

**1.4.** Jagai, Madhai and others who did evil without end He saved by the name of Hari;
He united brahmanas and yavanas — made them embrace: see it with your own eyes but once!

**1.5.** He made atheists into devotees, gave the lame the power to walk, granted the blind their sight."
Says lowly Krishnadasa: if you do not believe this, there is no more deliverance for you.[^4]

GPT2 — sec. 1.2, no. 35. The pada is not in PK, the "Bhakti-ratnakara" (BR), the "Kshanada-gita-chintamani" (KGC) or the
"Padamrita-samudra" (PS); it was missing from the catalogue (new row KR-022). Under the signature "Krishnadasa" PK has 22 padas by
different poets. The denunciation of "tantric religion" and words like "killing of the unborn" (ভ্রূণহত্যা) point to a late origin
(18th–19th c.); D.
[^2]: Cf. Vrindavana Dasa's description of Navadvipa before the Lord's appearance: people worship yakshas with wine and meat and honour
Vishahari (CB Adi 2). Vishahari is Manasa, the goddess of snakes. "Tantric religion" here means the rites with wine and the like that
the poet denounces.
[^3]: "Taraka-brahma" — the name that carries one across the ocean of birth and death: the maha-mantra "Hare Krishna," which the
"Kali-santarana Upanishad" calls the destroyer of the taint of Kali. "Decline of dharma" echoes Bhagavad-gita 4.7–8.
[^4]: Jagai and Madhai — CB Madhya 13; CC Adi 17. "Brahmanas and yavanas embraced": the Lord accepted all without distinction of caste —
thus Haridasa Thakura, born in a Muslim family, was one of His closest associates (CB Adi 16); the Kazi became His devotee (CC Adi 17).

## 2. Krishnadasa — "śāntipurera buḍāmālī…" (GPT1 pp. 14–15, no. 36)
*An allegory of the tree of Chaitanya: the old gardener of Shantipur (Advaita) brought a seedling from the garden of Vaikuntha and gave
it to the gardener Nitai; in Nadia grew the tree of Chaitanya and bore two fruits — the Name and prema; Nitai gives them out to all,
down to chandalas and yavanas, whether they wish it or not; the poet laments that he did not see that tree and did not know the
merciful gardener. Raga suhai.*[^5]

**2.1.** The old gardener of Shantipur, emptying the garden of Vaikuntha, brought a single seedling;
finding the gardener Nitai, He put the seedling into His hands — "Plant it with care," said Nara.[^6]

**2.2.** Nadia is the finest place: laying out a garden there, the gardener planted the tree of Chaitanya;
the tree grows day by day, its branches and leaves past counting: watering it with care, He brought it up.

**2.3.** Receiving the water of bhakti, the beautiful tree bore two fruits — the Name and prema;
in hope of these two fruits the birds — the living beings — come every day and chatter without cease.

**2.4.** The joyful gardener Nitai, a basket on his head, gives out the two fruits to all;
there is no distinction of caste — everyone's sorrow is gone: each one gets the taste of the fruit.

**2.5.** "Hold it, take it, take it!" — crying out, the joyful gardener Nitai gave out the fruits down to the chandalas;
whoever asks receives, whoever does not ask receives too: even the yavanas tasted the fruit.[^7]

**2.6.** What a turn of my karma: I did not see that tree, I did not know that merciful gardener!
Krishnadasa, full of bad desires, holding a straw between his teeth, says: shame, shame on my wretched fate!

[^5]: GPT1 — pp. 14–15, section of mangalacharana, no. 36 (suhai), scan PDF pp. 273–274; GPT2 — sec. 1.2, no. 36. The pada is not in PK,
BR, KGC or PS; it was missing from the catalogue (new row KR-023). Date of composition not established; attribution by GPT (C).
[^6]: Nara (নাড়া) is Advaita Acharya: so the Lord called Him (CB). In Krishnadasa Kaviraja the tree of bhakti is the Lord Himself, who
is also the gardener; the first sprout is Madhavendra Puri (CC Adi 9); here the allegory is different: the seedling is brought by
Advaita, who called the Lord down, and the gardener is Nityananda, who gives out the fruits.
[^7]: "Whoever does not ask receives too": Nityananda gave prema even to those who did not seek it (CC Adi 5; CB Antya 5). "Holding a straw
between the teeth" is a sign of complete humility.

## 3. Kanu Dasa — "ekadina kamalākṣa kana haridāse…" (GPT1 p. 448, no. 32)
*Advaita (Kamalaksha) says to Haridasa: many years have passed, and the hope with which He came to earth is not fulfilled; the Lord of
Vaikuntha Himself told Him that He would descend to earth for the sake of the suffering living beings and bade Him come before Him; can
the Lord's promise be in vain? The poet: the Lord never speaks untruth — He will surely appear. Raga dhanashri.*[^8]

**3.1.** One day Kamalaksha says to Haridasa:
"The wish with which I came to earth —

**3.2.** many years have passed, and the hope is unfulfilled:
My austerity is fruitless, I am in despair.

**3.3.** The Lord of Vaikuntha with His own lips told Me:
'The earth is burdened with sins, the living beings weep in sorrow;

**3.4.** to destroy the sorrow of the living beings I shall descend to earth;
You Yourself set foot there before Me.'

**3.5.** Can that promise of the Lord have proved vain?
Will the sorrow of the living beings not be removed through Me?"[^9]

**3.6.** Says Kanu: the Lord never speaks untruth —
by the good fortune of the living beings He will surely appear.

[^8]: GPT1 — p. 448, section on Advaita, no. 32 (dhanashri), scan PDF pp. 707–708; GPT2 — sec. 6.2, no. 32. The pada is not in PK, BR, KGC
or PS; it was missing from the catalogue (new row KD-012). Signature "Kanu" (কানু); Kanu Thakura, the son of Purushottama, is a poet of
the next generation, and the language of the pada (সাধনা বিফল, পাপভারাক্রান্ত) is New Bengali: the pada is probably late; D.
Kamalaksha is Advaita's childhood name.
[^9]: Advaita Acharya is Maha-Vishnu Himself; He appeared before the Lord in order to call Him down (CC Adi 3, 6; CB Adi 2). His "despair"
is not ignorance but compassion for the living beings and longing for the Lord's appearance, expressed in lila. The conversation of
the Lord with Advaita in Vaikuntha that is spoken of here is not described in CC or CB; CC (Adi 3) speaks of Advaita's resolve to call
Krishna by worship with Ganga water and tulasi. Haridasa Thakura came to Advaita in Shantipur (CB Adi 16).

## 4. Kanu Dasa — "cauddaśata sāta śāke…" (GPT1 p. 449, no. 33)
*The Lord's appearance: in the year 1407 of the Shaka era, on the full moon of Phalguna, during a lunar eclipse, Advaita Acharya was
offering tulasi leaves to the Ganga; suddenly He leapt up with a thundering cry and broke into a dance: "I have brought, I have brought
Gaura to Nadia!" — and Haridasa understood that Gauranga had been born. Raga dhanashri.*[^10]

**4.1.** In the year fourteen hundred and seven of the Shaka era, on the day of the full moon,
at the time of the lunar eclipse, in the month of Phalguna,

**4.2.** Advaita Acharya Prabhu, with a heart full of devotion,
was offering tulasi leaves to the Ganga.

**4.3.** Suddenly Nara leapt up with a thundering cry;
Haridasa was astonished, seeing His manner.

**4.4.** "I have brought, I have brought Gaura, I have brought Him to Nadia!" —
crying so, He dances, intoxicated with bliss.[^11]

**4.5.** Haridasa understood: Gauranga was born.
Grasping the hidden meaning, Kanu is beside himself with joy.

## 5. Vasu Ghosha — "abhirāma ḍāke dvārete…" (GPT1 p. 330, no. 29)
*Gostha-bhava in Navadvipa: Abhirama calls Gaura at the door to come and play — the game of Vraja was grazing the cows, the game of
Nadia is sankirtana; the time for going to the pasture is passing, but Gaura, offended, sits on mother Shachi's lap; Shachi asks
where they will go; Gorachand sets out for the pasture, and Vasu carries an umbrella for Him. Raga lalita.*[^12]

**5.1.** Abhirama calls at the door: "Hey, Gaura, will You come and play?
You are giving Yourself airs — sitting on mother Shachi's lap!

**5.2.** The game of Vraja is grazing the cows, the game of Nadia is sankirtana — the boys are mad for it;
'Ha-re-re!' — we shall see there; let us go to the bank of the Ganga.

**5.3.** The hour is past — the time for going to the pasture has gone: Gaura, tell us, will You come or not?
You have taken offence — sitting on mother Shachi's lap!"

**5.4.** Hearing Abhirama's words, mother Shachi says: "And where will you all go?"
Gorachand will go to the pasture — Vasu goes carrying the umbrella.[^13]

## 6. Yadunandana — "gaurāṅga-sundara naṭa-purandara…" (PK 2099; GPT1 p. 138, no. 80)
*Gauranga's dance: He is the lord of dancers, the manifest body of prema, like a new cloud or a golden Madana moulded of nectar; for
the sweetness of His face and smile one gives the autumn moon as a ransom; His eyes defeat the wagtail; the poet longs for His red
feet. Raga mayura.*[^14]

**6.1.** Gauranga the Beautiful, lord of dancers — the manifest body of prema:
is He a new cloud, is He a golden Madana? — as if moulded of nectar.[^15]

**6.2.** *(Refrain)* Wondrously dances Gauranga — an ocean of bliss!
For the sweetness of His face, for the cunning of His smile one gives the autumn moon as a ransom.

**6.3.** What eyes — they defeat the wagtail! How lovely the curve of His brows!
His two red feet — this Yadunandana longs for them.

## 7. Nayanananda (?) — "nāce re bhāli gaurakiśora bara-raṅgiyā…" (PK 2083 unsigned; GPT1 p. 284, no. 23)
*A dance in Radha's bhava: young Gaura dances, His golden body quivering with prema; remembering Vrindavana, Govardhana and the bank of
the Yamuna, He falls and cries: "The flute!"; in Radha's bhava He takes on Radha's colour and repeats "Radha, Radha!" — and Gadadhara,
understanding the hint, smilingly takes his place at His left. Raga turi.*[^16]

**7.1.** He dances — ah, young Gaura, finest of merry ones!
The beautiful body of Gaura, shining like gold, quivers under the weight of prema.

**7.2.** Remembering Vrindavana, Govardhana, the bank and the groves of the Yamuna, He sways and falls;
"The flute! the flute!" He cries again and again, and stands still, as if gazing at the flute at His lips.

**7.3.** In the bhava of Sri Radha, Gora took on the colour of Radha; from His lips — "Radha, Radha!";
understanding the hint, His dear Gadadhara playfully took his place at the left.[^17]

## 8. Yadu — "gorā mora baḍai raṅgiyā…" (PK 2101; BR pp. 925–926; GPT1 p. 257, no. 30)
*Kirtan on the bank of the Ganga: Gaura dances in the midst of His associates — Gadadhara and Narahari on either side, and Shrivasa,
Haridasa, Mukunda, Murari, Vasu, Ramai, Damodara and Jagadisha singing; "Hari! Hari!" is heard on all sides; seeing the moon of His
face, one forgets all torments. Raga suhini.*[^18]

**8.1.** *(Refrain)* My Gora is a great merry-maker:
on the bank of the Ganga — with new merry companions.

**8.2.** The associates sing, enchanting hearts;
in their midst dances Gaura — the jewel among brahmanas.

**8.3.** Gadadhara and Narahari — on the right and the left;
Shrivasa and Haridasa sing the name of Hari.

**8.4.** With Him are Mukunda, Murari, Vasu, Ramai;
Damodara and wise Jagadisha sing.[^19]

**8.5.** On all sides is heard: "Hari! Hari!" —
the ocean of prema has risen in waves of nectar.

**8.6.** Seeing the moon of His face, one is freed of all torments.
Says Yadu: who could forget such beauty?

## 9. Ramananda — "drāṃ drimiki drimi mādala bājata…" (GPT1 p. 335, no. 45)
*The Lord's dance in kirtan: the madala thunders, Shrivasa and all the associates sing; His arms reach to His knees, His body is as of
gold, in red garments He shines like the morning sun; now He trembles, now His hair stands on end, now, raising His arms, He cries
"Hari! Hari!", cherishing His former prema; with Him are Nityananda, Advaita and the associates; at this play even a diamond softens.
Raga gandhara.*[^20]

**9.1.** "Drang-drimiki-drimi" thunders the madala: how many talas, how many well-matched rhythms!
He dances the dance of the whole universe — Shrivasa and all the others sing.[^21]

**9.2.** His arms reach to His knees, His body is as of pure gold;
in red garments — the whole world trembles: like the morning sun.

**9.3.** Now He trembles, now His hair stands on end, now He waves His arms;
now, raising them high, He cries: "Hari! Hari!" — cherishing His former prema.

**9.4.** The avadhuta moon, Thakura Advaita, and with them all the associates.
Says Ramananda: at this play a diamond softens and wood melts.

## 10. Vasu Ghosha — "paḍiyā dharaṇītale śoke śacī…" (GPT1 p. 372, no. 21)
*Shachi's lament after the Lord's departure: fallen on the ground, she complains of cruel fate — her priceless treasure has been
stolen; Gorachand's bracelets, necklace and bed lie abandoned, Nadia is plunged in darkness; she is ready to become a yogini and go in
search of Him, to sell herself as a maid to whoever will give her back Gauranga; the poet: do not weep, Shachi — for the sake of the
living beings Nimai has become a sannyasi. Raga karuna.*[^22]

**10.1.** Fallen on the ground, Shachi weeps in grief and says: "Cruel fate has become my enemy!
I had a priceless treasure — what fate has stolen Gorachand, the darling doll of my soul?

**10.2.** The bracelets from His arms, Gorachand's necklace, the bed, the silk, the gold-woven rug —
all lie abandoned: Gaura has left me, and I go on living in vain.

**10.3.** Gauranga has gone — Nadia is plunged in darkness, my heart tosses about.
I shall become a yogini, I shall go where I can find Gauranga — I shall weep, my arms around His neck.

**10.4.** Whoever gives me back Gauranga — to him I shall sell myself without price, I shall be the maid of his servant."
Says Vasudeva Ghosha: why do you weep, Shachi? For the sake of the living beings Nimai has become a sannyasi.[^23]

## 11. Vasu Ghosha — "svarūpera mukhe śuni mānalīlā…" (GPT1 p. 290, no. 42)
*The Lord in Radha's bhava: hearing from Svarupa of the mana-lila, He is plunged into the bhava of the Loving One (Radha), dances crying
"Radha-kunda!", slowly walks to the bank of the "Yamuna," sits by the water and, His face bowed, is lost in thought; the poet: only one
who has realized rasa in the heart knows its essence. Raga dhanashri, tala dashakushi.*[^24]

**11.1.** *(Refrain)* Young Gaura is in bhavavesha:
hearing from Svarupa's lips of the mana-lila, the jewel of brahmanas was lost in the bhava of the Loving One.[^25]

**11.2.** "Radha-kunda! Radha-kunda!" — crying out, He dances with arms flung up; streams of prema flow from both His eyes.
One cannot understand where His bhava is drawing Him: slowly, slowly He walks, His gait surpassing the king of elephants.

**11.3.** Coming to the bank of the Yamuna, sitting right by the water, He is lost in thought;
seeing the waves of that bhava, I can understand nothing: He sits with His holy face bowed.

**11.4.** Says Vasudeva Ghosha: one who has realization in his heart — he, the connoisseur of rasa, knows the essence of rasa;
one who has no realization — to him it is unknowable: his life has been in vain.

## 12. Vasu Ghosha — "svarūpera kāche gaurahari…" (GPT1 p. 306, no. 24)
*The Lord in the bhava of Radha disappointed in waiting (vipralabdha): weeping before Svarupa, He says that in vain He set the snares
of love — Kala-chand did not come to the kunja, the dew is falling, the night has passed for nothing; having said this, He rolls on the

## 13. Chaitanyadasa — "ki baliba bidhātāre e dukha sahāya…" (PK 1867; GPT1 p. 281, no. 12)
*The divine madness of prema (divyonmada): the poet does not know what to say to the Creator — seeing Gora's face, how can one still
live? The Lord sits with faded face, tears pour like the heavenly Ganga onto the peak of Sumeru; now He rubs His face and head, now He
leaps up and runs, now He falls senseless; His breath stops — all weep. Raga suhini.*[^28]

**13.1.** What shall I say to the Creator, who makes us bear this pain?
Seeing Gora's face, why does life not leave me?

**13.2.** He sits with a faded face, tears pour from both His eyes —
like a stream of the heavenly Ganga onto the peak of Sumeru.

**13.3.** Now He rubs His face and head, now He leaps up and runs;
utterly spent, He falls to the ground senseless.

**13.4.** There is no breath in His nostrils — seeing this, all weep;
the heart of Chaitanyadasa cannot be calm.[^29]

## 14. Vasu Ghosha — "nitāi āmāra parama dayāla…" (GPT1 p. 424, no. 32)
*Nityananda's flood of prema: Nitai brought a flood of prema into the world, and it filled the rivers and canals; the wave carried away
sinners and sufferers alike, the devotees sport in it, some swim; Nadia and Shantipur went under — and started a boat race; Nitai
laughs, and the poet flounders in the waves. Raga sindhura.*[^30]

**14.1.** *(Refrain)* My Nitai is supremely merciful:
bringing a flood of prema, He blessed the world — filled the rivers and canals with prema.

**14.2.** The wave of prema struck — no one was left aside: sinners and sufferers were carried off by the current;
all the devotees together sport in that prema, and some go swimming.

**14.3.** Nadia went under, Shantipur is sinking in prema — the two together have started a boat race;
seeing this, Nitai laughs; all float in prema, and Vasu Ghosha flounders, gulping water.[^31]

## 15. Kanu Dasa — "āota nityānanda adabhuta cā̃da…" (GPT1 pp. 431–432, no. 59)
*Praise of Nityananda: Nityananda comes — a wondrous moon with the enchanting gait of a dancer; His face and eyes are lotuses, His lips
brighter than the dawn, His teeth pearls; with the nectar of His words He sprinkles those scorched by worldly existence, with the rays of
the name "Hare Krishna" He dispels the darkness of bad desires; around Him are His companions like stars. Raga kalyana.*[^32]

**15.1.** *(Refrain)* Nityananda comes — a wondrous moon:
natural is His gait, beautiful His dancing step — a form that enchants the people of the three worlds.

**15.2.** His face and eyes are pure, beautiful lotuses, and over them, like bees, the pair of brows;
the lustre of His red lips is brighter than the dawn, His teeth a string of pearls.[^33]

**15.3.** Those scorched by the heat of worldly existence He mercifully sprinkles with streams of the nectar of His words;
with the rays of the name "Hare Krishna" He destroys all the darkness of bad desires.

**15.4.** Around Him are His merry companions, like a host of stars: night and day this moon shines.
Hoping for the feet, tender as young shoots, of Sri Jahnava-vallabha, speaks Sri Kanu Dasa.[^34]
