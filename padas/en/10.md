# Nayanananda and Balarama Dasa: the Gaura-avatara, the Kirtanas of Navadvipa, Gaura and Gadadhara, Shantipura

*Padas of Nayanananda (Nayana Mishra), the nephew and disciple of Gadadhara Pandita, and padas signed by Balarama Dasa,
in the order of the lilas: praise of the Gaura-avatara; the kirtanas of Navadvipa, where Gaura dances beside
Gadadhara; Gauranga's form; His bhava — the Lord weeps gazing at Gadadhara's face; a spring night on the Ganga and the spring swing; Shantipura after
the sannyasa and the departure for Nilachala; Nityananda in Gauda. Majumdar counts Nayanananda among the Lord's
contemporaries: like his other padas, the pada on Gaura and Gadadhara ("premera gṛhiṇī se paṇḍita gadādhare") is cited
by Narahari Chakravarti in the *Bhakti-ratnakara* as testimony; for Balarama Dasa Majumdar accepts three padas from the
*Bhakti-ratnakara* (many padas of a later Balarama Dasa bear the same signature, so the attribution of the rest is
doubtful). The text follows the *Padakalpataru* (PK), checked against the scans, with the manuscript variants from PK's
apparatus, the *Bhakti-ratnakara* (BR), the *Gaura-pada-tarangini* (GPT1 — 1st ed., 1903; GPT2 — 2nd ed., 1934) and the
*Kshanada-gita-chintamani* (KGC); the Bengali original, variants and word-for-word translation are in the full version of the book.*

## 1. Balarama Dasa — "kaliyuga-matta-mataṅgaja-maradane…" (PK 617)
*Praise of the Gaura-avatara: the age of Kali is a mad elephant, Gaura is the king of lions who has appeared in the
mountain cave of Navadvipa; at the roar of sankirtana sins and the mystic perfections flee, and on the wretched He
hangs necklaces of the pearls of the holy name. The gaura-chandrika at the opening of PK's 3rd shakha. Raga kamoda.*[^1]

**1.1.** When the mad elephant of the Kali age was crushed,
the she-elephant of evil thinking fled far away;
on the low and the wretched He hung, as much as their necks would hold,
hundred-stranded necklaces of the pearls of the Name.[^2]

**1.2.** *(Refrain)* Wondrously shines Gaura:
in the mountain cave of the city of Sri Navadvipa
the king of lions has risen!

**1.3.** Hearing the battle roar of sankirtana,
the leopards — the sins — run away;
the does — anima and the other perfections — are frantic with fear,
abandoning the pride of their merits.[^3]

**1.4.** Renunciation, sacrifices, the vows of yama, pilgrimages, fasts —
like hares and jackals they flee as if scorched.
Balarama Dasa says: that is why in the world
only the sound of Hari's name is renowned.[^4]

[^1]: Signature: "Balarama Dasa." PK: vol. 2, PDF p. 14 (printed p. 1), opening of the 3rd shakha, section "śrīgauracandra"
(gaura-chandrika), no. 1 (label "॥১॥৬১৭॥"); raga kamoda; P-R-Sa MS no. 1091. GPT1 — p. 25, no. 1 (kamoda). The catalogue's link
to the *Bhakti-ratnakara* (BR p. 901) is mistaken: there it is a different pada signed by Narahari — "bali-kali-matta-mataṅgaja-maradana
gaurasiṃha nācata nadīyāya". The pada is in elaborate Vrajabuli; which of the Balarama Dasas wrote it is not established.
[^2]: MS "ka": "the elephant (like) Yama"; P-R-Sa: "mataṅgajamabadane" (corrupt). GPT1 in a note: "the elephant's roar." MSS "ka",
"ca": "far" (dūra). MS "ka": "pearl" (motima); so also GPT1. "Hundred-stranded necklaces" — śata-dāma, a necklace of many strands.
[^3]: Anima (the power of becoming minute) and the other eight siddhis are the mystic perfections of yoga; before
sankirtana they lose their worth, like does before a lion. The word "puṇabata" (here "merits," from puṇya) is unclear; MS
"ka" has "pumabata," "kha," "gha," "ca" "pumarata," P-R-Sa "patharata." MSS "ka," "kha," "ca," P-R-Sa: "islands" (dvīpagaṇa) instead of
"leopards" (dīpigaṇa); MSS "ka," "gha," "ca": "forest" (bana), "kha": "in the forest" instead of "battle" (raṇa).
[^4]: MS "ca": "like" (sama); MS "ka": "moon" (śaśi) instead of "hare." MSS "ka," "ca": "Hari" instead of "sound" (dhani). The
thought: nama-sankirtana is the dharma of the age of Kali (cf. CC Adi 3, 17), and all other means give way before it.

## 2. Balarama Dasa — "baṛa abatāra bhāi baṛa abatāra…" (PK 2207 = BR p. 956)
*The great incarnation: Gaura distributes the storehouse of prema to the fallen; women of noble families dance in the
sankirtana; the one whom all drove away as untouchable is now the one whose foot-dust the gods beg for; even yavanas
sing the holy name. Raga shri (BR — dhanashi).*[^5]

**2.1.** A great incarnation, brothers, a great incarnation!
To the fallen He has given away the storehouse of prema.

**2.2.** How wondrous are Gorachand's pastimes:
a king — and he carries a Vaishnava's palanquin on his shoulder![^6]

**2.3.** No likeness can be found for such an incarnation:
amid the sankirtana the wives of noble families dance.[^7]

**2.4.** The one whom everyone shunned, saying, "Do not touch!" —
from him the gods now beg the dust of his feet.

**2.5.** Even yavanas dance, sing and take the name of Hari —
yet in such an incarnation Balarama is left out.[^8]

[^5]: Signature: "Balarama." PK: vol. 3, PDF p. 271 (printed p. 260), 4th shakha, 20th pallava, section "śrīgauracandra —
prakārāntara," no. 24 (label "॥২৪॥২২০৭॥"); raga shri; P-R-Sa MS no. 2315, P-R 9.15. BR, 12th wave (1913 ed., pp. 956–957, "punardhānaśī"):
the pada is sung within the account of Navadvipa. Majumdar cites it among the BR testimonies of Balarama Dasa (the detail of
the noble wives dancing in the sankirtana is new). The catalogue had assigned it to PK 2208, which is a pada of Ananta Dasa.
GPT2 — sec. 1.3, no. 36.
[^6]: Who this "king" is, the pada does not say: perhaps the Lord Himself, the King of all, who serves His devotees, or a king
(Prataparudra) humbly serving the Vaishnavas. BR: "a Vaishnava's bag (jholā)" instead of "palanquin (dolā)"; MS P-R: "bears"
(bahe) instead of "holds."
[^7]: MS P-R: "in sankirtana people dance, having abandoned all (other) dharmas." BR: "nāpi" (corrupt), "bom̐hāri."
[^8]: BR: "all people" (saba loka) instead of "sarvva loka." A yavana is a non-Hindu (a Muslim); cf. the conversion of Chand Kazi
(CB Madhya 23; CC Adi 17).

## 3. Nayanananda — "jaya re jaya re gorā śrīśacī-nandana…" (PK 2)
*Glory to Gora, the son of Shachi, dancing His auspicious dance: Srivasa, Ramananda, Mukunda and Vasu sing; the
mridanga and the mandiras resound; one anoints Gora with sandal paste, another puts on Him a malati garland; some call
Him Rama, others the Kandarpa dear to Radha, but the poet knows only this: He is Gadadhara's life. A mangalacharana —
the second pada of PK. Raga kamoda.*[^9]

**3.1.** Glory, glory to Gora, the son of Sri Shachi,
beautiful in His auspicious dance!
In the bliss of kirtana Srivasa and Ramananda,
Mukunda and Vasu sing His virtues.[^10]

**3.2.** "Dang drimiki drimi" — the madal sounds,
the mandiras ring sweetly,
conch, karatalas and bells resound,
blending with the rhythm of His feet.[^11]

**3.3.** One anoints Gora's body with fragrant sandal paste,
another puts on Him a malati garland;
pierced to the heart by the flower-arrow of love,
the companions are lost in bhava.[^12]

**3.4.** Some say Gora is the beloved of Janaki,
others — the Five-arrowed One dear to Radha;
Nayanananda's heart knows nothing else:
He is the life of my Gadadhara.[^13]

[^9]: Signature: "Nayanananda." PK: vol. 1, PDF p. 13 (printed p. 2), 1st shakha, 1st pallava (mangalacharana), no. 2 (label
"॥২॥"); raga kamoda; P-R-Sa MS no. 1, P-R 1.3 and 38.1 (raga "kamoda-mallara," P-R "mallara"). KGC — 30th kshanada (purnima),
no. 1, gaura-chandrika (kedara); GPT1 — p. 4, no. 4 (kedara or mangala); GPT2 — sec. 1.2, no. 4. The catalogue gave the PK
number as "45" (PK 45 is a pada of Radhamohana on Radha's purvaraga).
[^10]: MS P-R: "suṭhāna re," "guṇa-gāna re" (with the refrain particle "re"); KGC lacks "re." Srivasa is the master of the
house where the night kirtanas of Navadvipa were held; Ramananda here is probably Vasu Ramananda; Mukunda is the singer
Mukunda Datta; Vasu is Vasudeva (Vasu) Ghosha.
[^11]: The syllables imitate the madal (mridanga): MSS "ka," "kha," "gha," "ca": "dāṃ drimiki drimi drimi"; P-R-Sa: "tā drimi ki
drimi"; P-R 1.3: "drimiki drimiki drimi"; P-R 38.1: "dāṃ drimi drimi," "mandirā"; KGC: "drāṃ drimiki drimi." Mandiras are
small cymbals.
[^12]: In the PK scan the epithet of the sandal paste is partly illegible; "fragrant" (sugandhi) follows KGC and GPT1. MS P-R
38.1: "One anoints Gora's body with sandal paste, (one offers) fragrant karabira," "kora re" instead of "bhora re." MSS "kha,"
"gha," "ca," P-R, P-R-Sa: "the (female) companions" (sahacari). The flower-arrow is Kamadeva's weapon; here it is the hearts pierced
by love for the Lord.
[^13]: MSS "kha," "gha," P-R, P-R-Sa: "say" (bale); "ka": "says" (kahata), so also KGC. The beloved of Janaki (Sita) is Rama. The
Five-arrowed One is Kamadeva; "the Five-arrowed One dear to Radha" is Krishna as the transcendental Madana (aprakrita-madana,
CC Madhya 8), not the worldly god of desire. Gadadhara Pandita, according to the *Gaura-ganoddesha-dipika*, is a manifestation
of Radha; Nayanananda calls Gaura the life of his Gadadhara. MSS "ka," P-R-Sa: "āmāra"; P-R: "hāmāri."

## 4. Balarama Dasa — "bhāla raṅge nāce mora śacīra dulāla…" (BR p. 837)
*Gaura dances in the kirtana of Navadvipa: sandal paste all over His body, His garland swaying, pearls on His chest,
His ankle bells ringing; the kinnaras learn singing from Him, the gandharvas contemplate His dance; the lotuses shrink
before His eyes. Raga dhanashi.*[^14]

**4.1.** How beautifully my darling of Shachi dances!
Sandal paste over all His body, His garland swaying.

**4.2.** On His broad chest is a necklace of "elephant" pearls,
to the rhythm of His feet the ankle bells ring out.[^15]

**4.3.** How many bends of the body He knows, in measure and out of measure —
in all the city of Nadia there is no such artist![^16]

**4.4.** The kinnaras learn from hearing His soft singing,
the gandharvas, seeing His tandava, sink into meditation.[^17]

**4.5.** The lotus shrinks on seeing His eyes,
when He smiles, His teeth shed the radiance of lightning.

**4.6.** His lips are redder than the bandhuli flower — and that smile!
Gazing at this form, Balarama Dasa weeps.[^18]

[^14]: Signature: "Balarama Dasa." BR, 12th wave: 1913 ed. (Murshidabad), pp. 837–838 ("gīte yathā — dhānaśī"); 1960 ed., vv.
1884–1889. The pada is not in PK or GPT1. In Narahari Chakravarti it is sung in the account of the path by which the Lord went
with kirtana from Srivasa's house to the Ganga and back; Majumdar cites it among the three BR padas of Balarama Dasa.
[^15]: Gaja-mukta is the pearl believed to be born in an elephant's head — the finest pearl. 1913 ed.: "jhañcāra," 1960 ed.: "jhaṅkāra"
(ringing).
[^16]: Chhanda-bichhanda — dancing in measure and with departures from it (syncopations).
[^17]: The kinnaras and gandharvas are celestial singers and musicians. Tandava is the vigorous male dance.
[^18]: Bandhuli (bandhuka) is a bright red flower. "oṭakhāni" — "the lips" (oṭha, oṣṭha); 1913 ed.: "…oṭakhāni hāma" (corrupt), 1960 ed.:
"oṭakhāni hāsa."

## 5. Balarama Dasa — "hari hari maṅgala bharala khiti-maṇḍala…" (PK 2065)
*Gaura dances, spreading through the world the storehouse of rasa — the kirtana of His own virtues; His form robs
Manmatha of his senses; the Name that Shiva and Brahma seek in the depths of the heart He strings into a necklace and
gives to the fallen. Raga mangala.*[^19]

**5.1.** Hari, Hari! The circle of the earth is filled with blessing —
a shop of the treasures of rasa has opened:
the kirtana of His own virtues, the jewel of prema
He ceaselessly spreads abroad.

**5.2.** *(Refrain)* The skilful dancer, youthful Gaura, dances;
His heart is ceaselessly stirred by bhava,
there is no end to the joy of prema.

**5.3.** That body, shining like pure gold —
it is the Creator who fashioned it![^20]
Manmatha has fainted: in every limb there is such
beauty that, seeing it, he has lost his wits.

**5.4.** The Name that Shiva and the Four-faced One worship,
seeking it in the depths of the heart —
of such a Name He carefully strings a necklace
and gives it to the fallen.

**5.5.** Seeing the souls sunk in a dark well,
the Lord appeared in Navadvipa;
the treasure of prema He gave away, filling the world —
only Balarama Dasa is left out.

[^19]: Signature: "Balarama Dasa." PK: vol. 3, PDF p. 217 (printed p. 206), 4th shakha, 17th pallava "śrīgaurāṅgera nṛtyādi līlā"
(Gauranga's dance and other pastimes), no. 7 (label "॥৭॥২০৬৫॥"); raga mangala; P-R-Sa MS no. 2174. GPT1 — p. 274, no. 86
(mangala); GPT2 — sec. 4.2, no. 86. Majumdar does not discuss the pada; its attribution to one of the Balarama Dasas is not
established. MS "kha": "kara" instead of "karu" (5.4).
[^20]: "The Creator fashioned" is a poetic figure of wonder: the Lord's body is eternal and spiritual (sac-cid-ananda), not
created by Brahma.

## 6. Balarama Dasa — "nācata gaura sunāgara-maṇiyā…" (PK 2066)
*Gaura's dance: a golden body, reddish garments, tears like rain, a merciful glance that drowns the world in the ocean
of prema; He embraces the fallen, and at His leaps and roars the hearts of the godless tremble. The raga is not named
("the same raga" — mangala).*[^21]

**6.1.** *(Refrain)* Gaura dances, the jewel of refined youths;
His feet, which put the wagtails to shame, delight the eye,
His ankle bells ring with a sweet "ran-ran."[^22]

**6.2.** His body has a natural golden lustre —
whoever looks, the whole world is enchanted;
millions of Madanas faint at it;
His garments are like the rays of dawn.

**6.3.** His body sways, He cannot stand steady,
His two eyes pour like clouds unceasingly;
He drowns the world in the ocean of prema,
glancing mercifully from the corners of His eyes.

**6.4.** Absorbed in that rasa, He knows no limit,
He embraces the fallen and fills the world.
Balarama says: seeing His leaps and thunderous roars,
the hearts of the godless tremble.[^23]

[^21]: Signature: "Balarama." PK: vol. 3, PDF pp. 217–218 (printed pp. 206–207), 17th pallava, no. 8 (label "॥৮॥২০৬৬॥"); P-R-Sa
MS no. 2175. GPT1 — p. 250, no. 7 ("yathārāga"); GPT2 — sec. 4.2, no. 7. Its attribution to one of the Balarama Dasas is not
established.
[^22]: Sunagara-mani, "the jewel of refined youths," is an epithet of Krishna; the poet sees Krishna Himself in the dancing
Gaura. The wagtail (khañjana) is an image of nimble, light feet or eyes.
[^23]: P-R-Sa: "on the earth" (bhūtala) instead of "in the world" (bhubana). "lakṣa" is a leap (cf. "lakṣe lakṣe" in BR). GPT1: "majāyai,"
"pāoi," "aruṇakiraṇa ambara baniyā" (without "kiye").

## 7. Balarama Dasa — "gobinda mādhaba śrīnibāsa rāmānande…" (PK 2067 = BR p. 922)
*Kirtana in Navadvipa: Govinda, Madhava, Srinivasa (Srivasa), Ramananda, Murari and Mukunda sing; hearing of His
former (Vrindavana) pastimes the Lord falls senseless and rolls in the dust; seeing Gadadhara, He weeps; He calls out,
"Shripada!" — and Nityananda, understanding the secret of His heart, weeps too. Raga mallara-kamoda (BR — dhanashi).*[^24]

**7.1.** Govinda, Madhava, Srinivasa, Ramananda,
Murari and Mukunda sing together, as their own choir.[^25]

**7.2.** Hearing of His former virtues, maddened,
in the bliss of kirtana the Lord falls senseless.

**7.3.** What a wonder — words cannot tell it:
the Lord of Goloka rolls in the dust![^26]

**7.4.** His heart is overflowing with bhava — He sees Gadadhara
and weeps in longing, His eyes brimming with tears.

**7.5.** "Shripada!" the Lord calls, falls to the ground and weeps;
understanding the secret of His heart, Nityananda weeps.[^27]

**7.6.** Seeing this, people of all three kinds weep, drunk with Gora's rasa,
but of this joy Balarama Dasa has been deprived.

mallara-kamoda; P-R-Sa MS no. 2176. BR, 12th wave (1913 ed., p. 922, "punardhānaśī"), right after Balarama's pada "…nā darabe balarāma
pāṣāṇa-hṛdaya". GPT1 — p. 275, no. 87 (shri-mallara); GPT2 — sec. 4.2, no. 87. Majumdar cites the pada as testimony of Balarama
Dasa: a kirtana with the Ghosha brothers, Srivasa, Vasu Ramananda, Murari and Mukunda.
[^25]: Govinda and Madhava are the Ghosha brothers, the singers of Navadvipa; Srinivasa is Srivasa Pandita; Ramananda is

[^26]: MSS "ka," "ca," P-R-Sa: "goloka-" (without the case ending); so also GPT1. In BR this couplet is followed by the refrain
mark (dhru).
[^27]: Shripada is the Lord's respectful way of addressing Nityananda. MSS "ka," "kha," "gha," "ca": "to the ground" (dharaṇī) instead

## 8. Nayanananda — "maṇḍalī raciyā sahacare…" (PK 2068)
*The companions have formed a circle, and in the middle Gora dances: with Him are Gadadhara and Nityananda; from house to
house He, Shyamasundara Himself, distributes love and devotion and with sankirtana offers everyone the treasure of
prema; men dance in the mood of women, women in the mood of men. The poet and his friends seek shelter at Gadadhara's
feet. Raga kedara.*[^28]

**8.1.** *(Refrain)* The companions have formed a circle,
in the middle is Gora, the best of dancers.

**8.2.** Vishvambhara dances, with Him Gadadhara,
Nityananda Raya dances;
recalling their former pastimes, they taste the joy of prema —
each grasps it according to his own nature.[^29]

**8.3.** From house to house — Shyamasundara Himself in person —
He gives away love and devotion;
He performs sankirtana and begs all to take the treasure of prema,
together with all His companions.[^30]

**8.4.** Men dance in the mood of women,
young women in the mood of men;
each having found his own bhava, his own nature,
people of every caste dance.[^31]

**8.5.** Nayanananda says: the bliss of Nadia —
the whole world is drunk with bliss.
We whose life is full of sorrow take shelter
at the feet of Madhava's son.[^32]

P-R-Sa MS nos. 2177 and 2213. GPT1 — pp. 250–251, no. 8 (kedara); GPT2 — sec. 4.2, no. 8. PK 2104 (vol. 3, PDF p. 231, no. 46) is
another recension of the same pada (after P-R-Sa no. 2213): "…nāce nityānanda bhāyā। pūraba-kautuka bhuñje prema-sukha saba sahacara laiyā,"
after which Raya refers back to PK 2068.
[^29]: "Former pastimes" are the pastimes of Vrindavana. P-R-Sa: "sabhāre" (to all) instead of "svabhābe" (by one's own nature); PK 2104
and GPT1 (in a note): "brother Nityananda" (bhāyā, bhāiyā).
[^30]: Shyamasundara, "the dark beauty," is Krishna: the poet sees in Gaura Krishna Himself going from house to house. P-R-Sa:
"merciful to all" (sabhāre sadaya haiyā) instead of "together with all His companions."
[^31]: In the ecstasy of kirtana the devotees forget the worldly distinction of sex: each dances in the bhava that has opened
within him. P-R-Sa: "dance" (nācata), "women" (prakṛti) instead of "young women" (yubatī).

P-R-Sa: "duḥkhita"; "gha": "jībajana" (souls). GPT1: "duḥkhita jībana, mādhabanandana, caraṇe śaraṇa morā."

## 9. Nayanananda — "duhum̐ duhum̐ piriti ārati nāhi ṭuṭe…" (PK 2069 = BR p. 925)
*Gaura and Gadadhara dance, drunk with each other's love: Gauranga with Gadadhara's rasa, Gadadhara with Gauranga's
pastimes; they are like Sita and Rama, like Radha and Kanu; in Nadia reigns the joy of the kirtana of prema, and the
mistress of the house of prema is Pandita Gadadhara, while Shachi's son is the touchstone of prema. Raga pathamanjari
(BR — kamoda).*[^33]

**9.1.** Their love and longing for each other never break off,
at their touch so much supreme joy arises![^34]

**9.2.** My Gauranga dances, drunk with Gadadhara's rasa,
and Gadadhara dances, drunk with Gauranga's play.

**9.3.** Prakriti and purusha — are they not Janaki and Sri Rama?
Are they not Radha with Kanu, Rati with the god Kama?[^35]

**9.4.** The grace of their limbs surpasses countless Anangas —
how can I find a likeness for the limit of their greatness?

**9.5.** What to say of the moon beside His face — every day it is born and dies;
what is the lotus beside His hands and feet — it falls apart under the weight of frost![^36]

**9.6.** The joy of the kirtana of prema is in the city of Nadia;
the mistress of the house of prema is Pandita Gadadhara.[^37]

**9.7.** The touchstone of prema is Shachi's son:
He delivered the people of the world by giving them the treasure of prema.

**9.8.** Nayanananda tells of the play of these two:
hearing of it, the heart is captivated — what is there to reason about?[^38]

[^33]: Signature: "Nayanananda." PK: vol. 3, PDF p. 219 (printed p. 208), 17th pallava, no. 11 (label "॥১১॥২০৬৯॥"); raga
pathamanjari; P-R-Sa MS nos. 2178 and 2460. BR, 12th wave (1913 ed., p. 925, "punaḥ kāmodaḥ"): Narahari Chakravarti cites the pada in
the account of Navadvipa as testimony ("ohe śrīnibāsa kichu kahila nā haya। suradhunītīre gorā raṅge bilasaya"). GPT1 — p. 251, no. 9
(pathamanjari); GPT2 — sec. 4.2, no. 9. Majumdar counts the pada among the testimonies of the closeness of Gauranga and Gadadhara
(catalogue: NN-029 = NN-015). In the catalogue the padas NS-005, BD-018 and BD-022 had been wrongly linked to PK 2069. PK 2356 (24th pallava, vol. 3, PDF p. 324) repeats the same pada.
[^34]: MS "gha," GPT1: "the heart" (marama) instead of "supreme" (parama); BR: "paraśe parama sukha jāni kata uṭhe."
[^35]: Prakriti and purusha are the female and male principles: Gadadhara, the manifestation of Radha (the pleasure energy,
hladini-shakti), and Gaura, Krishna Himself. Rati and Kama (Kamadeva) are the deities of love; the poet's comparisons are

## 26. Balarama Dasa — "anukṣaṇa aruṇa-nayana ghana ghūrata…" (BR p. 975)

**26.1.** Ceaselessly His reddish eyes roll,

**26.2.** *(Refrain)* Nitai dances, the beautiful moon,

**26.3.** On His feet are jewelled anklets,
as He moves His whole body sways;

**26.4.** He weeps, laughs, walks slowly,

there is no end of the wretched, yet He makes no distinction:
the ever unattainable treasure, the jewel of prema,
He Himself offers to the world and gives away freely.

**26.6.** When He gave away the steadfast, rare treasure of prema,
all the world's sufferings went far away;
the desires of all the poor and wretched were fulfilled,
even the women became maddened.[^105]

**26.7.** By such a merciful glance
no one was left with dark days —
yet Balarama Dasa was left out:
cruel is his heart, and hard.
