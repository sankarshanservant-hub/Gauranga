# Appendix. Padas signed "Narahari" from the Gaura-pada-tarangini — IX: Advaita

*Continuation of the appendix (the introductory note on the two Naraharis, on the arguments for attribution and on the marks in the headings
is in `17.md`). Portion 25: GPT section 6.2 "Advaita" (GPT1 pp. 439–449) — all fourteen padas signed Narahari (nos. 5–7, 9–11, 13, 15–18, 20, 21, 24):
Advaita among Gaura's companions in the morning at Navadvipa, His form, His thundering calls and His dancing in the kirtana of Gaura's glories, a
survey of His life, a praise, and His advent at Navagram. The signatures of the section were checked throughout, in the OCR and on the scan; the 12th
wave of the Bhakti-ratnakara (BR) at the relevant places, the Gaura-charita-chintamani (GCC, kirana 4) and the section on Advaita in the
Gita-chandrodaya were read continuously. Not translated (outside the appendix): nos. 8, 12, 14, 19, 22, 23 — signed Ghanashyama; no. 4 — no
signature (it ends "the Lord Sankarshana wanders behind them from door to door"); the other padas of the section are by other poets (Vrindavana Dasa,
Lochana, Shyamadasa, Vaishnava Dasa, Kanu, Trilochana, Gokulananda, Balarama Dasa). The base text is GPT1 (doubtful places by the scan), collated
with GPT2, the GCC (ed. 1947), the BR (eds. 1912 and 1960) and the Gita-chandrodaya; the PK, KGC and PS were checked. Advaita Acharya is Maha-Vishnu
Himself and Sadashiva, non-different from Hari (CC Adi 6; the Gaura-ganoddesha-dipika, 11, 76, also calls Him Sadashiva); as with Gaura and
Nityananda, the pronouns referring to Him are capitalized. The notes of the GPT editors are not reproduced.*

## 1. Narahari — "rajanī prabhāta prabhākara sama…" (GPT1 p. 440, section 6.2, no. 5; GCC)
*Morning among the companions: seeing the rising of Gaura's moon-face, Advaita, shining like the sun, beats His armpits in jubilation, walks to
and fro more intoxicated than the king of elephants; His smile is a rain of jasmine; tears flow from His eyes like strings of pearls. Raga beloyara.
**Attribution: probably Chakravarti** — the pada is in the GCC; Vrajabuli.*[^1]

**1.1.** The night has turned to dawn — and Advaita Mahashaya, like the sun, in supreme delight,
ceaselessly beats His armpits, seeing the rising of Gaura's moon-face.

**1.2.** His portly body, which has conquered the quarters, is most beautiful, and on it shines fresh horripilation;
wondrously He walks to and fro, more intoxicated than the king of elephants.

**1.3.** He laughs softly — His rows of teeth gleam, white rays issue ceaselessly,
as if wondrous jasmine flowers were raining down on every side, — and the connoisseurs of beauty are filled with longing and delight.

**1.4.** His eyes tremble, brimming with tears, and the tears flow beautifully, heeding no restraint,
glittering like strings of pearls, — and Narahari sees the pearls put to shame by the Lord.[^2]

[^1]: GPT1 — p. 440, section 6.2 "Advaita", no. 5 ("beloyāra"; scan, PDF p. 699); GPT2 — section 6.2, no. 5. **The catalogue did not know it** (new row NG-316).
GCC — kirana 4 ("the Lord among His devotees": in the morning, after waking, the companions gather round Gaura, and the poet describes each of them —
Nityananda, padas 7–11, Advaita, padas 12–15, Gadadhara, 16–17, Narahari Sarakara, 18, Srivasa, 19, and others), pada 12 ("punaḥ beloyāra"; right after
the pada on Nityananda's form — no. 29 of portion 24). Readings: 1.1 "nirakhi" ("seeing") — from the GCC, the word has dropped out in the GPT (the line is
short of the metre); 1.4 GCC "calata" ("flow"; GPT "carata"). Not in the BR, PK, KGC, PS, Gita-chandrodaya. B. Note 57 of portion 24 wrongly calls this
pada no. 4 (corrected).
[^2]: Beating the armpits (kakṣa-vādya) is a gesture of jubilation and challenge, as of a wrestler or a hero (cf. below, 9.3 and 11.2: He slaps His
arms, and Kali trembles at Advaita's roar). "Portly body": in the lila Advaita is the elder, venerable in years (cf. "the old man", buṛā, in other poets
of the same section); "which has conquered the quarters" — by its radiance and might. Advaita's tears and laughter are the ecstasy of love for Gaura,
whom He Himself called into the world. The last words (pahum̐ka parābhaba jāni, lit. "knows the defeat by the Lord") may also be understood otherwise:
"Narahari owns himself defeated by the Lord's beauty".

## 2. Narahari — "sītāpati atiśaya sukhe bhora…" (GPT1 p. 441, no. 6; GCC)
*Advaita, the Lord of Sita, is absorbed in happiness and reflects with a smile: Gaura's beauty has left Madana no trace of pride; the three worlds,
having drunk this intoxicating nectar, have forgotten everything and made Shachi's son the essence of their life; the word "sorrow" has vanished;
the sin of Kali with its retinue is cowed. Raga not specified ("yathārāga"). **Attribution: probably Chakravarti** — the pada is in the GCC;
Vrajabuli.*[^3]

**2.1.** The Lord of Sita is absorbed in boundless happiness.
Smiling gently, He reflects in His mind: "Thus not a little of Madana's pride is left!"

**2.2.** How wondrous is this excellent golden complexion — an intoxicating nectar: drinking only a little of it,
the three worlds became intoxicated, forgot everything and made Shachi's son the essence of their life.

**2.3.** The Moon with His fine companions, the abode of compassion, takes away the fierce burning and the darkness of people's hearts;
the word "sorrow" has gone from hearing on earth, in homes and in all the worlds there is great bliss.

**2.4.** [Kali's] joy has ended, everything has turned the other way: now the sin of Kali with all its retinue is cowed.
Hari, Hari! Whom has it not stripped of power, — says Narahari, — Your might, O Lord![^4]

[^3]: GPT1 — p. 441, no. 6 ("yathārāga"; scan, PDF p. 700); GPT2 — section 6.2, no. 6. **The catalogue did not know it** (new row NG-317). GCC — kirana 4,
pada 13 ("yathārāga"). Readings: 2.1 "vicāra" ("reflects") — from the GCC (GPT1 and GPT2 misprint "vicā"); 2.3 GCC "bharala bhuvanamadhi adhika ānanda"
("great bliss filled the worlds"; GPT "bhavana bhuvana madhi"). Not in the BR, PK, KGC, PS, Gita-chandrodaya. B.
[^4]: Madana (Kamadeva) is the god of love; "Madana's pride" is put to shame by the spiritual beauty of Gaura — Krishna Himself, Madana-mohana,
"the enchanter of Madana": this is praise of the Lord's all-attractive beauty, not of worldly attraction. "The Moon" is Gaura (Gaurachandra) with His
companions. Kali is the personified age of quarrel; his "joy" is his power over people, which the advent of the Lord and of His name destroys.

## 3. Narahari — "acyuta-janaka janāśraya jagamadhi…" (GPT1 p. 441, no. 7; GCC)
*The Father of Achyuta, stirred by longings, with a pauper's greed takes Gaura, still in His night attire, through His eyes into His heart,
worships Him with the flowers of His bhavas, dances unnoticed crying "Jaya!" and repeats: "I have won!"; Narahari, Haridasa and the others watch
Him in unspeakable happiness. Raga not specified. **Attribution: probably Chakravarti** — the pada is in the GCC; Vrajabuli.*[^5]

**3.1.** The Father of Achyuta, shelter of people, renowned in the world, generous, remover of the sorrow of the lowly,
forms so many longings in His mind — now He loses composure, now again restrains Himself.

**3.2.** With the unbounded greed of a pauper, without hesitation, the King of the twice-born — still in His night attire —
He took in by the path of the eyes and with great care seated on the throne of His heart.

**3.3.** He worships Him, offering kadamba flowers — His bhavas, and offers body and mind to Him in nirmanchana;
crying "Jaya, jaya!", unnoticed, He dances gently — and steals away people's hearts.

**3.4.** Again and again He says: "I have won, I have won!" — and blossoms, absorbed by Himself in the rasa of beholding.
Seeing this matchless play, Narahari, Haridasa and the others — who can measure their happiness?[^6]

[^5]: GPT1 — p. 441, no. 7 ("yathārāga"; scan, PDF p. 700); GPT2 — section 6.2, no. 7 (catalogue: NG-210). GCC — kirana 4, pada 14 ("yathārāga"); next
(pada 15) comes Ghanashyama's pada on Advaita's form (GPT no. 8). Readings: 3.2 "raṅka sama" ("like a pauper") and "rajanīka veśa" ("night attire";
cf. in the same kirana, pada 16: the Lord in the morning "in His night dress") — from the GCC; the GPT "vakṣa sama", "rajanī kareṇa" make no sense;
3.4 GCC "ko kahu ora" ("who can tell"). Not in the BR, PK, KGC, PS, Gita-chandrodaya. B.
[^6]: Achyuta is Achyutananda, Advaita's son, "the beloved of Krishna-Chaitanya" (Gaura-ganoddesha-dipika, 87); the name also means "the
Infallible" (Krishna). The King of the twice-born (dvijarāja) is both the moon and the king of brahmanas: Gaura. Nirmanchana is the rite of circular
offering (as in arati), a sign of self-surrender. "I have won!": Advaita triumphs — His worship has been fulfilled, the Lord whom He called has
appeared (cf. in the same GPT section Lochana's pada no. 30: "jitilum̐ jitilum̐… jība nistārite habe gaura abatāra"). In the last line the name
"Narahari" is both the poet's signature and, probably, the name of one of the onlookers: in the same kirana of the GCC, the pada after those on
Advaita and Gadadhara (pada 18) praises Narahari Sarakara among the Lord's companions; Haridasa is Haridasa Thakura.

## 4. Narahari — "śrī-advaitacandra bhūpa mora…" (GPT1 p. 442, no. 9; BR; GCd)
*Advaita's form in love for Gaura: tears from His reddened eyes, a radiance shaming hosts of suns, a gait surpassing the elephant's, a smile;
His roar robs the lion of its pride — Kali trembles, the wicked flee; the ocean of compassion overflows and the fallen float in the rasa of bhakti.
Raga kamoda or belavali. **Attribution: probably Chakravarti** — the pada is in the BR and the Gita-chandrodaya; Vrajabuli.*[^7]

**4.1.** Sri Advaitachandra is my sovereign!
His heart overflows with love for Gaura, tears stream ceaselessly from His reddened eyes.

**4.2.** His graceful limbs, thrilling with horripilation, shine with an excellent light that puts to shame hosts of suns;
His gait, the delight of the heart, surpasses the elephant's; He laughs — and His teeth gleam like pearls.

**4.3.** Robbing the lion of its pride, He roars again and again — Kali trembles, the wicked flee far away;
by His mighty power the threefold miseries are subdued, and the hearts of the people are filled with supreme gladness.

**4.4.** The ocean of compassion has overflowed in all directions — the base and the fallen float in the rasa of bhakti.
What will foolish Narahari understand of this play? He has revealed to the world the new deeds and qualities of Gaura.[^8]

[^7]: GPT1 — p. 442, no. 9 ("kāmoda vā belāvalī"; scan, PDF p. 701); GPT2 — section 6.2, no. 9. **The catalogue did not know it** (new row NG-318). BR 12
(ed. 1912, p. 977; ed. 1960, vv. 3767–3770, "belāvalī"): Nityananda, preaching in Gauda, stays at Saptagrama with Uddharana Datta and is about to go to
Shantipura, to Advaita's house; Ishana, telling Srinivasa of this, describes Advaita's glory ("Advaita Acharya lives in Shantipura… He who brought Shri
Krishna-Chaitanya into the world", vv. 3753–3754) and quotes one after another padas on Advaita: Narahari (vv. 3755–3760, "śrīgaura-abhinna tanu advaita āmāra" —
not in the GPT), Lochana (GPT no. 2), "Advaitachandra with His companions ever floats on the waves of the ocean of love for Gora" (v. 3766) — and this
pada; then come nos. 11, 12, 14 (Ghanashyama), 15, 16, 17, 18, 19 (Ghanashyama), 20 (nos. 6, 8–12 of this portion). Gita-chandrodaya — the section "atha
śrī-advaitacandrasya", pada 2 (there also nos. 14, 2, 11, 12, 17, 21). The Navadvipa-parikrama is the same as the BR. Readings: 4.1 BR and GCd "pahu mora"
("my Lord"); 4.2 BR and GCd "kuñjaradamana gamana" ("a gait shaming the elephant"; GPT "kuñjaragamana damana"); 4.4 BR and GCd "umaḍi calu cahu diśa"
("overflowing, flowed in all directions"). Not in the PK, KGC, PS, GCC. B.
[^8]: Advaita's roar (huṅkāra) is the thundering calls with which, worshipping with Ganga water and tulasi leaves, He called the Lord down (CC Adi 3);
here it is a jubilant roar from which Kali flees. "The threefold miseries" — those caused by one's own body and mind, by other beings and by nature.
"He has revealed Gaura's deeds to the world": Advaita was the first to proclaim and glorify Gaura as the Lord.

## 5. Narahari — "śrī-advaita guṇamaṇi sakala rasera khani…" (GPT1 p. 442, no. 10; BR)
*Advaita's life in one pada: birth from Nabha at Navagram, removal to Shantipura, pilgrimage after His parents' passing, marriage to Sita and the
austerities by which He brought Krishna and Haladhara; kirtana in Nadia, service to the Lord as sannyasi in His own house, journeys to Nilachala.
Raga kamoda. **Attribution: probably Chakravarti** — the pada is in the BR.*[^9]

**5.1.** Sri Advaita, the jewel of virtues, the mine of all rasas, took birth from Nabha's womb;
He was born at Navagram, in Vanga, played there joyfully, and after some time came to Shantipura.

**5.2.** When His father and mother passed from sight, He went on pilgrimage to the holy places, and returning, settled in Shantipura;
becoming the husband of Sri Sita, day after day He performed so many austerities — and brought [into the world] Krishna and Haladhara.

**5.3.** Seeing the pastimes in Nadia, He ever refreshed His eyes and danced in kirtana in many ways;
receiving Gorachand, the crown of sannyasis, in His own house, He served Him joyfully.

**5.4.** When the Lord stayed at Nilachala, He went there and back and intoxicated everyone with Gora's glories.
Narahari Dasa says: Sri Advaita is merciful — the three worlds proclaim this fame.[^10]

[^9]: GPT1 — p. 442, no. 10 ("kāmoda"; scan, PDF p. 701); GPT2 — section 6.2, no. 10 (catalogue: NG-211). BR 12 (ed. 1912, pp. 996–997; ed. 1960,
vv. 4024–4027, "kāmoda"): right after the pada surveying Nityananda's life (6.1 no. 65, no. 15 of portion 24) Ishana tells Srinivasa: "Sri Advaita with His
companions is ceaselessly absorbed in singing Gaura's deeds, now in Shantipura, now in Nadia; who does not sing the glory of Nabha's Son?" (vv.
4022–4023). Readings: 5.1 "vilasiyā" — from the BR (GPT "vilāsiyā"); 5.4 BR "prabhu-sthiti" ("the Lord's stay"). Not in the PK, KGC, PS, GCC,
Gita-chandrodaya. B.
[^10]: Navagram is Advaita's birthplace in Vanga (East Bengal), near Shrihatta (Sylhet); His father is Kuvera Pandita (Kuvera Mishra), His mother Nabha
(in the Advaita-prakasha, Labha; BR 12, vv. 1751–1758). Sri Sita is Advaita's wife, according to the Gaura-ganoddesha-dipika (86) Yogamaya. "Austerities"
(tapa) are His worship and His prayers for the Lord's descent (CC Adi 3); "Krishna and Haladhara" are Gaura and Nityananda. The Lord after sannyasa in
Advaita's house at Shantipura — CC Madhya 3; Advaita's yearly journeys to Puri with the Bengali devotees — CC Madhya 11 ff.

## 6. Narahari — "śāntipurapati parama sundara…" (GPT1 p. 442, no. 11; BR; GCd)
*The Lord of Shantipura in the intoxication of bhava: He roars, subduing Kali's arrogance, trembles at the name "Gaurahari" and falls into the arms of
His companions, rolls in the dust, streams pour from His lotus eyes like the rains of Shravana; seeing the wretched, He spreads mercy through the world.
Raga kamoda. **Attribution: probably Chakravarti** — the pada is in the BR and the Gita-chandrodaya; Vrajabuli.*[^11]

**6.1.** The Lord of Shantipura, most beautiful, — whose deeds are an excellent lila, —
is constantly intoxicated with the weight of bhava, His body thrilling all over.

**6.2.** Subduing the mighty arrogance of Kali, again and again He roars terribly, beside Himself;
crying "Gaurahari! Hari!", He trembles and falls into the arms of His companions.

**6.3.** Again and again He rolls on the ground, incomparable, His body grey with dust;
from His lotus eyes streams pour down like the rain-clouds of Shravana.

**6.4.** Seeing the lowly and the sorrowful, He shows mercy and spreads it through the world.
Narahari Dasa offers himself to the Lord: how supremely generous He is![^12]

[^11]: GPT1 — p. 442, no. 11 ("kāmoda"; scan, PDF p. 701); GPT2 — section 6.2, no. 11. **The catalogue did not know it** (new row NG-319). BR 12 (ed. 1912,
p. 977; ed. 1960, vv. 3771–3774, "punaḥ — kāmoda"; right after no. 9, see note 7); Gita-chandrodaya — the section on Advaita, pada 4. Readings: 6.1 GCd
"carita varaṇi na yāta" ("His deeds cannot be described"); 6.3 "kañjalocana" ("lotus eyes") — from the BR and GCd (GPT misprint "kuñja locana"); GCd
"aparūpa dhūlidhūsarita deha". Not in the PK, KGC, PS, GCC. B.
[^12]: Shravana (śāṅana) is the month of the rainy season (July–August). "Gaurahari" is the Lord's name: Advaita, Himself non-different from Hari, repeats
Gaura's name in ecstasy. "Offers himself" (balihāri) — lit. "sacrifices himself", an exclamation of admiration and devotion.

## 7. Narahari — "jaya deva deva maheśvara rūpa…" (GPT1 p. 443, no. 13; GPT only)
*Praise of Advaita, the form of Maheshvara, the king of the rasa of lila: by His call Gauranga was manifested, for His sake Gaura's lila unfolded;
He was born as Kuvera's son on the bright seventh day of the month of Magha. Raga dhanashi. **Attribution: not established** — found in no witness
(it stands among padas that are in the BR; the GPT2 index wrongly assigns it to Ghanashyama).*[^13]

**7.1.** Glory to Him who is the form of Maheshvara, the God of gods, —
to Advaita Acharya, the king of the rasa of lila!

**7.2.** By His thundering calls Gauranga was manifested,
for His sake Gaura's lila unfolded.

**7.3.** In the auspicious month of Magha, on the bright seventh lunar day,
He was born — as Kuvera's son.

**7.4.** The son of Nabha, the Lord Sri Advaita, —
may the mind of Narahari Dasa remain at His feet![^14]

[^13]: GPT1 — p. 443, no. 13 ("dhānaśī"; scan, PDF p. 702); GPT2 — section 6.2, no. 13 (catalogue: NG-212). In the GPT2 index of first lines "jaya debadeba maheśvara rūpa"
stands under Ghanashyama's name, but in the text of both editions the signature is "dāsa narahari". Readings: 7.3 GPT2 "kuvera rase" (a letter has dropped out:
GPT1 "aurase", "as the son"). Not in the BR (eds. 1912, 1913, 1960), GCC, Gita-chandrodaya, Navadvipa-parikrama, PK, KGC, PS. C.
[^14]: "Maheshvara, the God of gods" is Sadashiva: Advaita is Maha-Vishnu Himself (CC Adi 6), and the Gaura-ganoddesha-dipika (11, 76) calls Him Sadashiva.
The Sadashiva of Gaudiya theology is not the demigod Shiva who governs the mode of ignorance, but an eternal form of the Lord, non-different from Vishnu;
that is why Advaita is called "non-dual" — non-different from Hari. Kuvera is Kuvera Pandita, Advaita's father, according to the Gaura-ganoddesha-dipika
(81) Kubera, the friend of Mahadeva; Nabha is Advaita's mother. The bright seventh lunar day of the month of Magha is the day of Advaita's advent
(Advaita-saptami); likewise in Ghanashyama (GPT no. 23) and in no. 14.

## 8. Narahari — "ki bhāve vibhora mora advaita gosāñi re…" (GPT1 p. 443, no. 15; BR)
*Advaita is lost in bhava: tears, a sweet smile, the constant call "Gora! Gora!"; a body more tender than the shirisha flower, His shikha fluttering in
the wind, a garland of malati on His neck; stretching out His long arms, He embraces the fallen and gives everyone the treasure of prema, rare even for
Brahma. Raga gurjari. **Attribution: probably Chakravarti** — the pada is in the BR.*[^15]

**8.1.** In what bhava is my Advaita Gosani lost! Tears flow from His eyes;
on His moon-face is a sweet, sweet smile, and again and again He calls: "Gora! Gora!"

**8.2.** His matchless body is more tender than the shirisha flower, and abundant horripilation shines on it;
what is the elephant's gait before such beauty! By His movements He enchants the hearts of the world.

**8.3.** On His head the beautiful shikha flutters in the wind, a garland of malati sways on His neck;
stretching out His arms, which reach to His knees, He seizes the fallen and embraces them.

**8.4.** The treasure of prema and bhakti, rare even for Brahma, He Himself offers to everyone in so many ways;
and Narahari, having found so merciful a Lord, did not serve Him — and sank in the well of worldly existence.[^16]

[^15]: GPT1 — p. 443, no. 15 ("gujjarī"; scan, PDF p. 702); GPT2 — section 6.2, no. 15 (catalogue: NG-213). BR 12 (ed. 1912, pp. 978–979; ed. 1960, vv. 3782–3785,
"punaḥ gurjarī"; after Ghanashyama's pada no. 14); next Ishana says: "Sita's Lord, Advaita Gosani, is absorbed in dancing and kirtana — now in His own house,
now in the houses of His companions, now on the bank of the Ganga" (vv. 3786–3789) — and quotes no. 16. Readings: 8.3 BR "patita dhariyā"; 8.4 BR "pahu pāyā
re nā bhaji majinu bhavakūpe" ("not serving, I sank"). Not in the PK, KGC, PS, GCC, Gita-chandrodaya. B.
[^16]: The shikha is the lock of hair on the crown, the sign of a Vaishnava brahmana. "Rare even for Brahma": the prema given away by Gaura and His
companions surpasses all that the demigods attain. The poet's self-abasement in the signature ("did not serve… sank") is the usual ending of a pada: a
humble Vaishnava considers himself deprived of what is given to all.

## 9. Narahari — "nācaye advaita premarāśi…" (GPT1 pp. 443–444, no. 16; BR)
*Advaita dances, singing of Gora: He begs the Creator for thousands of mouths, slaps His arms like a wrestler — Kali trembles and knows not where to
flee; the companions sing of Gorachand's deeds, khol and karatalas resound, even the noble wives weep; the world is full of His fame. Raga dhanashi.
**Attribution: probably Chakravarti** — the pada is in the BR.*[^17]

**9.1.** Advaita dances — prema itself;
proud of Gora's glories, He knows neither day nor night.

**9.2.** What happiness it is to say "Gora! Gora!" —
He begs the Creator for hundreds of thousands of mouths.

**9.3.** Crying "Gora!", He slaps His arms like a wrestler —
Kali trembles with fear, and there is no way for him to flee.

**9.4.** What bhava is in His heart at the name of Gora!
His body, thrilling with horripilation, sways again and again.

**9.5.** His companions — His army — intoxicated with this rasa,
sing in many ways of Gorachand's deeds.

**9.6.** How the khol and the karatalas resound!
Even the wives of noble families weep, hearing that sound.

**9.7.** The world is filled with His fame;
the wretched, the fallen, the base float in prema.

**9.8.** What happiness is there in Narahari's life?
He has turned away from the feet of so merciful a Lord.[^18]

[^17]: GPT1 — pp. 443–444, no. 16 ("dhānaśī"; scan, PDF pp. 702–703); GPT2 — section 6.2, no. 16 (catalogue: NG-214). BR 12 (ed. 1912, p. 979; ed. 1960,
vv. 3790–3797, "gīte — yathā dhānaśī"; see note 15). Readings: 9.2 BR "vihire" ("of the Creator"; GPT "vihare"); 9.5 "senā" ("army") — from the BR (the GPT

## 10. Narahari — "dekha mora advaita guṇanidhi…" (GPT1 p. 444, no. 17; BR; GCd)
*Advaita's form, as if fashioned of nectar: its radiance surpasses gold, ketaki and kunkuma; His moon-face gleams as He calls "Gora, Gora!"; He
dances with raised arms, embraces the fallen and gives away the priceless treasure. Raga kamoda. **Attribution: probably Chakravarti** — the pada is
in the BR and the Gita-chandrodaya.*[^19]

**10.1.** Behold: my Advaita is a treasury of virtues!
I do not know with what love the Creator fashioned this body of nectar.

**10.2.** The radiance of His lovely form surpasses gold, ketaki and kunkuma;
overflowing with love for Gora, He is wondrously adorned with a wealth of horripilation.

**10.3.** His matchless moon-face gleams as again and again He cries: "Gora, Gora, Gora!";
streams flow ceaselessly from both His eyes, He dances with both arms raised.

**10.4.** Seizing the fallen and the base, He embraces them and Himself offers the priceless jewel;
is there anyone in the world so merciful, apart from Narahari's Lord?[^20]
