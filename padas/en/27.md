# Nagari Padas — I: Vasu Ghosha (padas from the *Padakalpataru*)

## Introductory Note to the Section of Nagari Padas

*"Nagari" (nāgarī) means a townswoman, a woman of Nadia; "nagara" (nāgara) is a townsman, a gallant, and in the language of
Vaishnava poetry the beloved, the hero of the play of love (Krishna is so called: "Shyama-nagara," "rasika-nagara").
**Nagari-bhava** is the name given to the mood of padas in which the women of Nadia, having seen the young Gaura in the
street, at the bathing ghat on the Ganga, in kirtana, tell their friends of His beauty, lose their peace, forget home,
modesty and family, pine through the nights, see Him in dreams, and after His sannyasa lament the separation. In these
songs Gaura is "Gauranga-nagara," "the nagara of Nadia." The imagery is taken from the padas on Radha and Krishna —
purvaraga, anuraga, longing in expectation, disappointed waiting, telling a friend of the meeting; that is why in the
*Padakalpataru* (PK) such padas stand as gaura-chandrikas (preludes) to the corresponding sections on Radha — marked "the
words of the nagaris" (nāgarī ukti) — or are gathered into a separate pallava, "Gauranga's form: the words of the nagaris
and others."*

*Who sang in this mood. Tradition regards Narahari Sarakara of Shrikhanda and Vasu Ghosha as the first singers of
nagari-bhava (Majumdar remarks that Vasu calls Gaura "nagara" following Narahari); poets of the Shrikhanda school wrote in
the same spirit, among them Narahari's disciple Lochana Dasa; later such padas multiply — Nayanananda, Yadunandana, and many
songs signed "Narahari" in the *Gaura-pada-tarangini* (GPT), mostly by Narahari Chakravarti (18th century). Majumdar counts
about twenty padas of Vasu Ghosha in PK as nagari-bhava; padas known only from GPT he considers unreliable (among them are
even reworkings of padas on Krishna, ascribed to Vasu to give nagari-bhava an air of antiquity).*

*The position of the tradition. The Gaudiya Vaishnava tradition does not accept the worship of Gaura in the spirit of the
nagaris.*
- *Vrindavana Dasa in the *Chaitanya-bhagavata* (Adi-khanda), telling of the young Nimai, says that the Lord, who joked and
  played pranks with everyone, in this incarnation did not look at any woman even from the corner of His eye and would not
  even hear the word "woman"; therefore the wise, though every praise befits Him, do not glorify Him as "Gauranga-nagara,"
  but sing of Him according to His nature.*
- *Krishnadasa Kaviraja in the *Chaitanya-charitamrita* (Adi 1.5–6; Adi 4) reveals the secret of this incarnation: Gaura is
  Krishna Himself, who has taken the bhava and the luster of Radha in order to taste the greatness of Her love, the
  sweetness of Himself that She tastes, and Her happiness. He came to taste Radha's love — as Radha, in the mood of a
  devotee — and not to enjoy the love of women as a hero.*
- *Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati counts "gaura-nagari" among the apasampradayas — currents that have departed from the
  teaching of Mahaprabhu: worshipping Gaura as a nagara-lover transfers to His lila in Navadvipa what belongs to Krishna's
  Vraja-lila and distorts its meaning.*

*Gaura's attractiveness for all who saw Him is a quality of Bhagavan (so Krishna too is Madana-mohana, "the enchanter of the
god of love himself"); the biographers also write that the Lord's beauty robbed the people of Nadia of their peace. But the
Lord Himself in Navadvipa was a brahmana teacher, strict in conduct, then a devotee in Radha's bhava and a sannyasi; His
lilas are not worldly love.*

*Rules of the section. (1) The nagari padas are translated for the completeness of the corpus of padas on Gaura and as a
historical document — a witness to how He was sung of in the 16th–18th centuries; they are no basis for worshipping Gaura as
a nagara. (2) The translation is faithful to the author: the text is neither softened nor heightened; where the original
speaks of a glance and of beauty, it is so translated; where it speaks openly of attraction, it is translated exactly, with
a note. (3) The heading of each pada is marked "nagari-bhava"; the notes give a brief reference to this note instead of the
full caveat. (4) Pronouns referring to Gaura are capitalized; the nagaris' words about themselves are in lower case.*

*Portion 27 contains the padas of Vasu Ghosha known from PK (the earliest and most reliable layer of nagari padas; Majumdar
includes them in his list of Vasu's padas), in the order of the lila: Gaura's form through the eyes of the townswomen, first
love and longing, a night of vain waiting, dreams and tales told to friends, the lament after the sannyasa. The text
follows PK, checked against the scans, with the manuscript variants from PK's apparatus and the *Gaura-pada-tarangini*
(GPT1 — 1st ed., 1903; GPT2 — 2nd ed., 1934); the Bengali original, variants and word-for-word translation are in
`bn/27.md`.*

## 1. Vasu Ghosha — "sajanī ai dekha śacīra nandana…" (PK 2151)
*Nagari-bhava. Gaura's form: "Friend, look — there is Shachi's son!" Birds, beasts and trees weep hearing of His virtues,
noble wives cannot hold their hearts steady; giving to all the name unattainable even for Brahma, Gora has delivered the
world. In PK it stands in the pallava "Gaura's form." According to Majumdar, a pada of nagari-bhava. Raga varadi.*[^1]

**1.1.** *(Refrain)* Friend, look — there is Shachi's son:
whoever sees Him has no peace in his heart.

**1.2.** A treasury of boundless virtues, His glory without end;
in the three worlds there is none who could set a limit to His beauty.

**1.3.** Birds, beasts, trees and creepers weep hearing of His virtues;
by His beauty and virtues noble wives cannot hold their hearts steady.[^2]

**1.4.** The name unattainable even for Brahma He has given to everyone —
says Vasudeva — so Gora has carried [all across the ocean of births].[^3]

[^1]: Signature: "Vasudeva." PK: vol. 3, PDF p. 249 (printed p. 238), 4th shakha, 18th pallava, section "atha
śrīgauracandrasya rūpa-varṇanaṃ" (description of Gaura's form), no. 43 (mark "॥৪৩॥২১৫১॥"); raga varadi; MS P-R-Sa no. 2258. GPT1 —
p. 117, no. 20 (raga varadi); GPT2 — sec. 3.1, no. 20 (catalogue: VG-183). Majumdar counts PK 2149–2155 among Vasu's nagari
padas. Here the nagari motif is only "noble wives" (1.3); the rest is praise of Gaura, the giver of the name. On nagari-bhava
see the introductory note to the section.
[^2]: "Noble wives" (kulavatī) are married women of worthy families who guard the honour of the family; "cannot hold their
hearts steady" (buka nāhi bāndhe, lit. "cannot bind the breast") — lose their peace. A common formula for describing
beauty, as in the padas on Krishna; cf. portion 23, note 7.
[^3]: Hari's name, unattainable even for Brahma, Gaura gives to all without distinction — the main theme of the padas on
Him as the yuga-avatara (cf. portion 09, no. 1). MS "gha" in 1.2: "i" for "e" ("these [three worlds]"), "rūpera" for
"rūpe" (same sense).

## 2. Vasu Ghosha — "sakhi he oi dekha gorā-kalebare…" (PK 2152)
*Nagari-bhava. Gora's form from head to foot: a face more beautiful than many moons, arms like an elephant's trunk, a
glance quicker than a wagtail, a sandal tilaka, garlands to the knees, a neck like a conch, a broad chest with necklaces,
reddish feet, jewel-like nails; "the Creator fashioned this form to be the ruin of young women." Raga kamoda.*[^4]

**2.1.** Friend, look — there is Gora's form:
His face more beautiful than many moons, His lips lovely.

**2.2.** His rounded arms surpass the trunk of the best of elephants;
the glance of Gora's eyes surpasses the wagtail.[^5]

**2.3.** On His lovely forehead is a tilaka of sandal,
wondrous garlands, ever fresh, hang down to His knees.

**2.4.** His neck is like a conch; on His broad and mighty chest,
anointed with sandal, are many jewelled necklaces.

**2.5.** His thighs are lovelier than plantain stems, His feet are reddish;
His jewel-like nails outshine the full moon and a mirror.

**2.6.** Says Vasu Ghosha: where has Gora come from?
The Creator fashioned this form to be the ruin of young women.[^6]

[^4]: Signature: "Vasu Ghosha." PK: vol. 3, PDF p. 250 (printed p. 239), 18th pallava, no. 44 (mark "॥৪৪॥২১৫২॥"); raga kamoda;
MS P-R-Sa no. 2259. GPT1 — p. 117, no. 21 (raga kamoda); GPT2 — sec. 3.1, no. 21 (catalogue: VG-009). On nagari-bhava see the
introductory note to the section.
[^5]: The wagtail (khañjana) is an image of lively, quick eyes. MS "ka": "ai," P-R-Sa: "ai" (the same "there," 2.1).
[^6]: Lit. "where was Gora not?" — an exclamation of wonder: such a form had never been anywhere. "The ruin of young women"
is a formula of the poetics of beauty (as in the padas on Krishna, "the enchanter of the god of love himself"), not a
description of the Lord's deeds; cf. portion 23, note 7. MSS "ka," "kha": "shines" (śobhe) for "adorns" (sāje, 2.3; so also
GPT1); "kha": "body" (aṅga, 2.4).

## 3. Vasu Ghosha — "dekha dekha sakhi gorā bara dvijamaṇiyā…" (PK 2153)
*Nagari-bhava. "Look, friend — Gora, the jewel of brahmanas!" With great patience the Creator made this peerless form: arms
to the knees, a hue brighter than gold — is it ketaki, a golden lotus, champaka or a gem? His speech pours nectar; millions
of moons offer themselves for His face; who, having seen Him, could walk away? Raga kanada.*[^7]

**3.1.** *(Refrain)* Look, look, friend — Gora, the finest jewel of the twice-born!
With what patience
the Creator made this peerless form![^8]

**3.2.** His two lovely arms reach to the knees,
the hue [of His body] outshines gold.
Is it ketaki, a golden lotus,
or champaka, or a gem?

**3.3.** Is its hue that of gorochana or kunkuma? —
no, the radiant body surpasses them.
With sweet words He pours down nectar,
and the mind of the three worlds is enchanted.[^9]

**3.4.** Millions of moons offer themselves for His face
and melt, falling at the moons of His nails.[^10]
Says Vasu Ghosha: who, having seen Gauranga's face,
could walk away?

[^7]: Signature: "Vasu Ghosha." PK: vol. 3, PDF p. 250 (printed p. 239), 18th pallava, no. 45 (mark "॥৪৫॥২১৫৩॥"); raga kanada;
MS P-R-Sa no. 2260 (the word "finest," bara, is absent from it). GPT1 — p. 122, no. 32 (raga kalyani); GPT2 — sec. 3.1,
no. 32 (catalogue: VG-127; row VG-126 has the same PK number). On nagari-bhava see the introductory note to the section.
[^8]: "Jewel of the twice-born" (dvijamaṇi) — the best of brahmanas. The words "kemane dhairaja dhariyā" can also be taken as
"how can one keep one's composure?"; following the structure of the sentence ("the Creator made… how, summoning patience")
we translate it as wonder at the Creator's painstaking work.
[^9]: Ketaki and champaka are golden flowers; gorochana is a yellow pigment, kunkuma is saffron: all these are customary
comparisons for Gaura's golden body.
[^10]: "Offer themselves" (nichani) — a rite in which something precious is waved around the beloved to ward off misfortune
and then given away (glossary): the moons are but a ransom for His face; "the moons of His nails" are His toenails, shining
like moons.

## 4. Vasu Ghosha — "o nā ke bala go sajani…" (PK 2154)
*Nagari-bhava. "Tell me, friend, who is this?" — a face lovelier than moons, a hue of gold and gem; the moon and the red
lotus hide in shame before His nails, palms and feet; at the beauty of His brows Madana throws away his bow; a sidelong
glance mixed with a smile strikes everyone; "beware — He has lit up the whole world." Raga (illegible in PK; in GPT1
varadi).*[^11]

**4.1.** *(Refrain)* Tell me, friend, who is this?
His lovely face is lovelier than many moons,
His hue is like gold and gem.

**4.2.** The roundness of His arms surpasses an elephant's trunk,
reaching to His knees — how lovely they are!
The moon and the red lotus, seeing His nails, palms and feet,
have hidden themselves in shame.[^12]

**4.3.** The two arches of His brows are so lovely
that Madana throws away his bow;
He casts a sidelong glance, mixing it with a smile —
and strikes everyone.[^13]

**4.4.** At His waist is a cloth the colour of dawn,
on His neck sways a garland of forest flowers.
Says Vasu Ghosha: beware —
He has lit up the whole world!

[^11]: Signature: "Vasu Ghosha." PK: vol. 3, PDF p. 250 (printed p. 239), 18th pallava, no. 46 (mark "॥৪৬॥২১৫৪॥"); MS P-R-Sa
no. 2261; the name of the raga is damaged in the scan. GPT1 — p. 122, no. 33 (raga varadi); GPT2 — sec. 3.1, no. 33
(catalogue: VG-010). On nagari-bhava see the introductory note to the section.
[^12]: Nails are compared to the moon, palms and feet to the red lotus (kokanada); here the compared is so beautiful that the
objects of comparison hide in shame. MSS "ka," "gha," "cha," P-R-Sa: "of the best elephant" (karibara); "kha": "the trunk of
the best elephant" (karibara-kara); "kha": "roundness" in the form subalani.
[^13]: Madana (Kamadeva) throws away his bow because Gaura's brows are a bow lovelier than his own; "strikes" (hānaye)
continues the same image: the glance is an arrow. Cf. note 6.

## 5. Vasu Ghosha — "dekhaha nāgara nadiyāra…" (PK 2155)
*Nagari-bhava. "Behold the nagara of Nadia!" — His gait is sweeter than the step of a royal elephant; bee-like devotees fly
to the fragrance of His lotus feet, and the whole world, drunk with honey, loses the directions; full of rasa, He smiles
slightly, and at the arrow from the corner of His eye millions of Kamadevas swoon; the world is full of cries of "Glory to
Gora!" Raga kamoda.*[^14]

**5.1.** *(Refrain)* Behold the nagara of Nadia![^15]
His gait in sweetness has surpassed the step of the best of elephants —
wondrous Gora, king of the twice-born.

**5.2.** His feet are like lotuses, and the devotees, like bees,
fly from all sides to their fragrance;
drunk with honey, the whole circle of the earth
cannot tell the directions apart.

**5.3.** Brimming with rasa, with enchanting lips,
He smiles slightly and glances again and again;
a sign from the corner of His eye, an arrow from the corner of His eyes —
and millions of Kamadevas fall senseless.

**5.4.** His ornaments are many gems, His cloth outshines the dawn,
ankle bells jingle on His reddish feet.
The world is full of cries: "Glory to Gora, the jewel of the twice-born!" —
so sings of His virtues Vasudeva Ghosha.[^16]

[^14]: Signature: "Vasudeva Ghosha." PK: vol. 3, PDF p. 251 (printed p. 240), 18th pallava, no. 47 (mark "॥৪৭॥২১৫৫॥"); raga
kamoda; MS P-R-Sa no. 2262. GPT1 — p. 122, no. 34 (raga kamoda; "nadīyāya" — "in Nadia"); GPT2 — sec. 3.1, no. 34 (catalogue:
VG-011). On nagari-bhava see the introductory note to the section.
[^15]: "Nagara" (nāgara) — a townsman, a gallant, in poetry the beloved hero; a word by which Krishna is glorified. Why the
tradition does not so call Gaura — see the introductory note. Here, apart from the name "nagara," the pada is a praise of the
Lord's form; the "bee-like devotees" at His feet and the Kamadevas losing consciousness are images of His divine
attractiveness.
[^16]: P-R-Sa: "slow" (sumanthara) for "sweet" (sumādhuri, 5.1). MS "ka" and GPT1: "the world's cry of victory" (jagata
bijaya) for "the world is full of cries" (jaga bhari jaya, 5.4).

## 6. Vasu Ghosha — "āre mora rasamaya gaurakiśora…" (PK 2211)
*Nagari-bhava. Praise of the young Gaura, full of rasa: in the three worlds there is no such nagara; noble wives are
enchanted by His beauty, trees thrill, stones melt, birds and beasts weep, the townswomen lose their peace; He stirs the
minds of gods and sages — Gora, the saviour of the fallen. In PK it stands in the pallava "Gaurachandra — another way."
Raga varadi.*[^17]

**6.1.** O my young Gaura, full of rasa!
In all three worlds there is no such nagara.

**6.2.** Chaste wives of noble families, seeing His beauty, are enchanted;
hearing of His virtues, trees and creepers thrill with joy.

**6.3.** Stones and trees melt, birds and beasts weep;
the townswomen cannot keep their hearts at rest.

**6.4.** He stirs the minds of gods, siddhas and sages.
Says Vasu Ghosha: Gora is the saviour of the fallen.[^18]

[^17]: Signature: "Vasu Ghosha." PK: vol. 3, PDF p. 272 (printed p. 261), 20th pallava, no. 28 (mark "॥২৮॥২২১১॥"); raga varadi;
MS P-R-Sa no. 2319. The neighbouring pada of the same pallava is Vasu's PK 2210 (portion 08, no. 2). GPT1 — p. 44, no. 58
(raga varadi); GPT2 — sec. 1.3 (catalogue: VG-132). Majumdar (1961 ed.) cites this pada as an example of Vasu calling Gaura
"nagara" following Narahari Sarakara. On nagari-bhava see the introductory note to the section.
[^18]: The pada joins nagari motifs (6.1, 6.3) with praise of the Lord before whom stones melt and gods and sages tremble —
Bhagavan, who saves the fallen.

## 7. Vasu Ghosha — "madanamohana gaurāṅga-badana…" (PK 2149)
*Nagari-bhava. A townswoman tells her friend: having seen Gauranga's face, which enchants Madana himself, she has lost
herself; this is the former dark Kanu become golden — otherwise how could He have stolen her heart? The young wives of
Nadia drown in love, in every house there is weeping; she will cast the rites of her family into the waters of the
Suradhuni and serve Gora. Raga tudi.*[^19]

**7.1.** Gauranga's face enchants Madana himself:
having seen this form, what has not befallen me!
His body is the colour of gold — [yet once] this was dark Kanu;
otherwise would He have stolen [so] my heart?[^20]

**7.2.** To one whose life is rasa, what is family?
Such is [He] — in the town of Nadia!
What a bitter, cruel lot: the young chaste wives have drowned,
in every house there is the weeping of love.[^21]

**7.3.** [My] lotus eyes are redder than the fresh dawn,
streams [of tears] run down my breast.
Ah, I am dying, dying, friend — to you I open my heart:
I will not live without seeing Gora.

**7.4.** The arrow of love in my heart has wounded all my body,
my soul accepts no consolation.
I will go to the bank of the Suradhuni and cast my family's rites into its waters —
I will serve Gora, the jewel of virtues.[^22]

**7.5.** All that I heard before is just so:
[He who was] dark-bodied has now become Gora.
The word of Vasudeva Ghosha: know — He is the rasika-nagara;
otherwise would He be the stealer of the gopis' hearts?[^23]

[^19]: Signature: "Vasudeva Ghosha." PK: vol. 3, PDF p. 249 (printed p. 238), 18th pallava, no. 41 (mark "॥৪১॥২১৪৯॥"); raga tudi;
MS P-R-Sa no. 2256. GPT1 — p. 160, no. 8 (raga tudi); GPT2 — sec. 3.2, no. 8 (catalogue: VG-013). A witness is the *Bhakti-ratnakara*, 12th wave (1913 ed., p. 910, scan PDF
p. 918): in the cycle of the words of the women of Nadia that Narahari Chakravarti cites while describing the places of Navadvipa (portion 29,
note 1), with Vasudeva Ghosha's signature, raga deshapala, beginning with the words "rūpa heri…"; in 7.2 it reads "rahibe" (as MSS "kha," "cha").
On nagari-bhava see the introductory note to the section.
[^20]: Gaura is Krishna (Kanu) Himself, who has taken Radha's golden luster (CC Adi 1.5); the townswoman recognizes in Him the
"stealer of hearts" of Vraja. This affirmation of the identity of Gaura and Krishna is the main theological content of the
pada (cf. 7.5).
[^21]: The sense of 7.2: before such a Gaura the bonds of family are powerless. MS "ka": "will say" (kahibe), "kha," "cha":
"will remain" (rahibe) for "will do" (karibe). P-R-Sa: "down face and breast" (mukha buka, 7.3; so also GPT1); "that" (se) for
the particle "go" (7.3).
[^22]: "I will cast my family's rites into the waters" — I will abandon the duty and honour of my family: a nagari motif
(introductory note). The words of love and service (bhajiba) are spoken by a townswoman; the translation does not soften
them, nor does it add worldly shades.
[^23]: "The stealer of the gopis' hearts" is a name of Krishna; Vasu again bears witness: the golden Gaura is the same
Krishna. "Rasika-nagara" — the connoisseur and enjoyer of rasa; in Krishna-lila this is Krishna with the gopis. The tradition
does not accept the application of this name to Gaura (introductory note).

## 8. Vasu Ghosha — "ki kahaba aparūpa gaura kiśora…" (PK 2150)
*Nagari-bhava. "What can I say of the wondrous young Gaura?" — with a sign from the corner of His eye He has stolen her
life; His sidelong glance is a calamity: the ruin of the townswomen has risen in the town; she who sees Him cannot forget,
the noble wife leaves her home; love for Gora is a pain of the heart. Raga suhini.*[^24]

**8.1.** What can I say of the wondrous young Gaura?
With a sign from the corner of His eye He has stolen my life.

**8.2.** His sidelong glance is a real calamity:
the ruin of the townswomen has risen in the town.

**8.3.** Whatever woman sees Him cannot forget Him;
the noble wife leaves her family and does not stay at home.

**8.4.** Says Vasudeva Ghosha: listen to my words —
love for Gora is a pain deep in the heart.

suhini; MS P-R-Sa no. 2257. GPT1 — p. 160, no. 9 (raga suhini); GPT2 — sec. 3.2, no. 9 (catalogue: VG-014). On nagari-bhava

## 9. Vasu Ghosha — "āra eka dina gaurāṅgasundara…" (PK 2169)
*Nagari-bhava. A townswoman tells: on another day she saw the beautiful Gauranga bathing at the ghat — a face lovelier than
millions of moons, lotus eyes, brows a bow, glances the arrows of Kama; drops of water on His curls like a string of pearls
in a cloud, on His body like pearls on gold; He wrung out His curls and put on a red cloth — "I cannot stay at home." In PK
it is Vasu's first pada in the pallava "Gauranga's form: the words of the nagaris and others." Raga varadi.*[^25]

**9.1.** On another day I saw the beautiful Gauranga —
He was bathing at the ghat.
His face lovelier than millions of moons —
seeing it, the heart breaks.

**9.2.** His body tender and radiant like tested gold,
His eyes pure lotuses;
the glances are arrows, the arches of the brows a bow:
so the archer Kama strikes.[^26]

**9.3.** His curling locks, and on them drops of water:
like a string of pearls in a cloud;
the drops on His body are like pearls on gold;
seeing this, Kama swoons.

**9.4.** He wipes His whole body, wrings out His curls,
puts on a red cloth.
Says Vasu Ghosha: so it seems to me —
I cannot stay at home.[^27]

raga varadi; MSS P-R-Sa no. 2276 and P-R no. 61. GPT1 — p. 160, no. 10 (raga varadi); GPT2 — sec. 3.2, no. 10 (catalogue:
VG-015). The beginning of 9.2 is illegible in the PK scan — restored from GPT1 ("aṅga ḍhala ḍhala"). On nagari-bhava see the

## 10. Vasu Ghosha — "eka dina ghāṭe jale giyāchilāma…" (PK 2171)
*Nagari-bhava. "One day I went to the ghat for water — and what beauty of Gora I saw!" A body like tested gold, the Lord
absorbed in the rasa of prema; a lovely face, signs from the corners of the eyes, a sandal tilaka — like the moon among the
stars; smiling, He speaks half-words; who, seeing such a nagara, can keep composure? Raga dhanashi.*[^28]

**10.1.** One day I went to the ghat for water —
what beauty of Gora I saw!
His pure body like tested gold;
the Lord was absorbed in the rasa of prema.

## 22. Vasu Ghosha — "ki kahaba re sakhi rajanika bāta…" (PK 724)
*Nagari-bhava. A townswoman tells her friend of the night: she was sleeping beside her elders; at midnight, under the full
moon and the gentle Malaya breeze, love for Gaura overwhelmed her, and weeping she cried out: "Gaura, Gaura!"; the elders
woke and heard Gaura's name — then she raised the cry: "Thief, thief!" Raga dhanashi.*[^59]

**22.1.** What shall I say, friend, of last night!
I was sleeping beside my elders.

**22.2.** At midnight, when the moon was full,
very gently the breeze from Malaya was blowing.

**22.3.** Love for Gaura filled my body;
my life in distress could find no support.

**22.4.** Weeping "Gaura, Gaura!" I rose —
the elders woke: "Who is it again?"

**22.5.** Gaura's name they all heard with their own ears;
then the elders began to suspect.

**22.6.** I raised the cry: "Thief, thief!" —
says Vasudeva Ghosha: such is the play![^60]

"॥৬১॥৭২৪॥"); raga dhanashi; MS P-R-Sa no. 1198. GPT1 — p. 204, no. 114 (raga dhanashi); GPT2 — sec. 3.2, no. 114. **Catalogue
correction**: row VG-094 counted PK 724 as "Krishna-lila" and excluded it from translation; in fact the pada is about Gaura
(the name "Gaura" in 22.3–22.5) and stands in GPT among the nagari padas. It is not in Majumdar's list (he gives the
neighbouring PK 723). On nagari-bhava see the introductory note to the section.
[^60]: A play on words: "gaura" — "caura" ("thief"): to hide whose name she was calling, the townswoman turns "Gaura" into

"Kanu, Kanu" with the word "Bhanu, Bhanu" (the sun): Vasu transfers a motif of Krishna-lila to the nagaris. MS P-R-Sa:
"canda," "manda" (22.2); "ka," "kha," "cha," P-R-Sa: "śunala" (22.5); "kha," "gha": "I rose" (uṭhalum̐), P-R-Sa: "rose"
(uṭhala) for "I raised the cry" (22.6).

## 23. Vasu Ghosha — "ki kahaba re sakhi ājuka bhāba…" (PK 249)
*Nagari-bhava. A townswoman tells her friend: she was alone, adorning herself and dressing her hair before the mirror — and at
that moment Gora, the king of dancers, appeared; her composure and her modesty gave way; at the sight of Him her body
thrilled, and He took her in His embrace. In PK it follows Radhamohana's gaura-chandrika to the section "telling of the
meeting — another way," under the heading "the words of the nagaris of Navadvipa befitting this rasa." Raga
vibhasa.*[^61]

**23.1.** What shall I say, friend, of today!
Without any effort a great fortune has come to me.

**23.2.** I was alone, adorning myself;
looking in the mirror, I was dressing my hair.

**23.3.** At that moment Gora, the king of dancers, appeared:
the composure and modesty of a noble wife gave way.

**23.4.** At the sight [of Him] my body thrilled all over —
says Vasudeva Ghosha: He took [me] in His embrace.[^62]

(the words of the nagaris of Navadvipa befitting this rasa), no. 2 (mark "॥২॥২৪৯॥"); raga vibhasa; MS P-R no. 1364. GPT1 —

section.
[^62]: The last line describes an embrace — sambhoga-nagari (cf. note 56); we translate it exactly. An important variant: in

(daraśe pūrala tanu pulakita mora, bāsudeba kahe gorā naola kiśora), without the embrace. MS P-R: "today with effort He gave
me fortune" (23.1), "I" (āmi), "there I dressed" (tahi bāndhala, 23.2), "fled" (bhāgala, 23.3); "ka," "kha": "dressed"

## 24. Vasu Ghosha — "hari hari gorā kothā gela…" (PK 1636)
*Nagari-bhava. A townswoman's lament after Gaura has gone: where has Gora gone? what cruel Creator sent this grief? Her heart
is wounded; she cannot weep aloud, like a thief's wife; she is a daughter of a noble family and does not leave the house, and
even in dreams she cannot see Him; to whom can she tell of His beauty and His pastimes? "Why does not a thunderbolt fall on
my head?" In PK it stands in the section "the lament of the devotees, and also of the nagaris of Navadvipa, in separation
from Gaurachandra." Raga suhai.*[^63]

**24.1.** *(Refrain)* Hari, Hari! Where has Gora gone?
What pitiless Creator has sent such grief?

**24.2.** My heart is wounded, my ribs are crumbling;
since life is leaving — what then is left of love?

**24.3.** I cannot weep aloud — I am like a thief's wife;
every moment I remember Gora's face.[^64]

**24.4.** I am a daughter of a noble family, I do not leave the house;
even in dreams I cannot see Him — what shall I do?

**24.5.** To whom shall I tell of that sweet beauty and those pastimes?
Without Gora, my Lord, I will enter the fire.[^65]

**24.6.** That life holds on without Gora — this is a great shame.
Says Vasu: why does not a thunderbolt fall on my head?

Gaurachandra), no. 3 (mark "॥৩॥১৬৩৬॥"); raga suhai (P-R: sindhura); MSS P-R-Sa no. 859 and P-R no. 40/111. GPT1 — p. 388,

[^64]: A proverb: a thief's wife dare not mourn her husband aloud — her grief is secret. So the townswoman cannot weep openly

## 25. Vasu Ghosha — "kaha sakhi jibana-upāya…" (PK 1669)
*Nagari-bhava. A lament after Gaura has gone: "Tell me, friend, how shall I live? Gora, the king of dancers, has left [me]";
her body has wasted away, how many days can she live in separation? where can she go to see His face? what has the Creator
written on her forehead? to whom can she tell this grief? In PK it is the gaura-chandrika to the section on Radha's lament
in external consciousness; in GPT it stands among the laments of Shachi and Vishnupriya. Raga suhai.*[^66]

**25.1.** Tell me, friend, how shall I live?
Gora, the king of dancers, has left [me].

**25.2.** Pining [for Him], my body has wasted away;
how many days shall I live in separation?

**25.3.** Gauranga's pure face —
where shall I go to see it?

**25.4.** What has the Creator written on my forehead?
I will cut it open and see what is there on my brow![^67]

**25.5.** My heart is wounded with love —
to whom shall I tell this grief?

**25.6.** Says Vasu Ghosha at the last:
without Gora life cannot be kept.[^68]
