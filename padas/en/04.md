# Gaura's Form, Navadvipa, the Sannyasa and the Return

*Padas of contemporaries which Majumdar (*Sri-Chaitanya-chariter upadan*, ch. 2) regards as eyewitness testimony, in the
order of the lilas: the six-armed form and the beauty of Gorachand in Navadvipa, Radha's mood in Gadadhara's lap, the
swing festival on the bank of the Ganga, the goshtha-lila, praise of Gaura and Nityananda; then Vasu Ghosha's cycle on the
sannyasa (the last night at home, the lament of Shachi and Vishnupriya, Katwa, Keshava Bharati, the shorn curls, the
lament of Nadia), the dance of the Lord as a sannyasi, and the Lord's return to the neighbourhood of Navadvipa (1514/15).
The text follows the *Padakalpataru* (PK), the *Bhakti-ratnakara* (BR), the *Kshanada-gita-chintamani* (KGC) and the
*Gaura-pada-tarangini* (GPT1 — 1st ed., 1903; GPT2 — 2nd ed., 1934); the Bengali original, variants and word-for-word
translation are in the full version of the book.*

## 1. Ananta Dasa — "kām̐cā se soṇāra tanu…" (PK 2167)
*Lila: Navadvipa — the Lord's six-armed form (shad-bhuja): Rama's bow and arrows, Krishna's flute, the sannyasi's staff and
waterpot. In PK the pada is headed "Then the form that revealed six arms, in Navadvipa". Majumdar mentions this pada when
speaking of Ananta Dasa, a poet of Advaita's branch and a contemporary of the Lord. Raga tirotha-dhanashi.*[^1]

**1.1.** His body is like pure gold, all of it quivering;
on His moon-face a smile — waves of nectar.

**1.2.** A forest garland hangs down to the very earth;
drawn by its fragrance, swarms of bees surround it.

**1.3.** Of the six arms the upper ones hold sharp arrows and a bow —
at the sight of them enemies tremble.[^2]

**1.4.** These arms are the colour of durva grass, their nails like moons;
bracelets and armlets of jewels shine on them.

**1.5.** Below, two arms dark as a thundercloud;
in them gleams the matchless, enchanting flute.

**1.6.** The jewel-nails outshine the moon, the palms are red;
seeing the jewelled ornaments on them, Ananga himself swoons.[^3]

**1.7.** Lower still, hands with the waterpot and the staff,
which shatter the filth of Kali and the godless.[^4]

**1.8.** At the throat and on the breast jewels and pearls sway;
the Kaustubha swings, the breast is marked with the Shrivatsa.

**1.9.** The broad chest is anointed with sandal;
the navel is deep, the waist slender as a lion's.

**1.10.** The red cloth falls to His very feet;
Ananta Dasa offers himself to the nails of His feet.[^5]

[^1]: The signature is "Dasa Ananta". In PK the pada stands last in the 18th pallava ("The form of Sri Gauranga, etc.";
vol. 3, PDF p. 256, printed p. 245; MS P-R-Sa no. 2274); in GPT1 — p. 141, no. 89; in GPT2 — sect. 3.1, twice (nos. 44 and
89), with minor variants. The Lord revealed the six-armed form to Nityananda in Navadvipa (CB Madhya 5; Murari 2.8) and
to Sarvabhauma in Puri (CC Madhya 6). Majumdar regards Ananta Dasa as a contemporary; PK has 32 padas with his signature.
[^2]: Bow and arrows are the form of Ramachandra, and the colour of durva grass (a dark greenish hue) is the colour of His
body. PK reads "uta chaya bhuja para" ("there, of the six arms…"), GPT "ubhadvaya bhujapara" ("on the two raised arms");
the sense is the same.
[^3]: The middle pair of arms holding the flute, dark as a cloud, is Krishna's form. Ananga ("the bodiless") is Kamadeva,
the god of love.
[^4]: The lowest pair of arms holds the staff (danda) and waterpot (kamandalu) of a sannyasi: the form of Gaura Himself.
For "the godless" (pāṣaṇḍa) MSS "ka", "ca" read "filth" (kaluṣa); so also GPT: "the filth, the sin of Kali".
[^5]: Nichhani (nichani) is the custom of waving something round a beloved person to ward off harm from him and then giving it
away; the poet "offers himself" in such an offering to the nails of the Lord's feet. MSS "ka", "ca", P-R-Sa read "nichani".

## 2. Govinda Ghosha — "gorācām̐da kivā tomāra vadana-maṇḍala…" (PK 1029)
*Lila: Gorachand's form — a song of the Lord's beauty in Navadvipa. In PK it opens the section "Delight in the form"
(rupa-ullasa) as a gaura-chandrika. According to Majumdar, Govinda Ghosha, the eldest of the Ghosha brothers, conveys his
own impression of Gauranga's form. Raga dhanashi.*[^6]

**2.1.** *(Refrain)* Gorachand, how wondrous is the orb of Your face!
Is it a golden lotus, is it the full autumn moon —
it shines by night and by day.

**2.2.** The colour of Your body outshines yellow haritala
and seems to surpass a motionless lightning;
is it fresh gorochana, is it gold of the finest assay —
You enchant the mind of Manmatha himself.[^7]

**2.3.** Your nose is lovelier than Garuda's beak, Your speech sweet as nectar —
there is none like You in the three worlds.
Eyes reaching to the ears are arrows, brows a drawn bow:
with a sidelong glance You pierce the hearts of women.[^8]

**2.4.** Arms reaching to the knees are anointed with sandal,
and rings and bracelets shine on them.
The waist is slenderer than a lion's, the thighs like golden plantain stems,
on the feet are anklets.

**2.5.** The gait is lovelier than a maddened elephant's and the king of swans';
seeing such a treasury of beauty,
Govinda Ghosha speaks, with joy in his heart for Gora:
"I would give myself up for Him."[^9]

[^6]: The signature is "Govinda Ghosha". In PK (vol. 2, PDF p. 225, printed p. 212; MS P-R-Sa no. 1455) the pada comes
under the heading "Now, delight in the form. Sri Gaurachandra" and opens the songs on the beauty of Radha and Krishna. It is
not in GPT. Majumdar names PK 1029 and 2146 (no. 3) among Govinda Ghosha's padas on Gaura's form.
[^7]: Haritala is orpiment, a golden-yellow paint; gorochana is a yellow pigment used for tilaka; "gold of ten van"
(daśavān) is gold refined many times, of the finest assay. Manmatha is Kamadeva: the Lord's beauty enchants the god of love
himself (cf. Krishna's name Madana-mohana). MS P-R-Sa reads "tanu" ("body") for "janu" ("as if").
[^8]: "You pierce the hearts of women" (MSS "kha", "gha", P-R-Sa: "the heart of a woman") is a stock image of the poetics
of describing beauty; we translate faithfully. Vrindavana Dasa stresses that in this incarnation the Lord never looked at
another man's wife; worship of Gaura in the spirit of "Gaura-nagari" is not accepted by the Gaudiya tradition (Vrindavana
Dasa, Krishnadasa Kaviraja, Bhaktisiddhanta).
[^9]: The words "gora mane santoṣa" also allow the reading "and Gora is pleased at heart"; our rendering is tentative.
"Give myself up" (nichani jāiye) — see note 5. For "maddened" (mayamatta) MSS "ka", "kha" read "madamatta" (same sense).

## 3. Govinda Ghosha — "kanayā kaṣila mukha-śobhā…" (PK 2146)
*Lila: Gaura's form — the causeless smile, the yellow silk, a bee flying to His feet. According to Majumdar, an
eyewitness's impression of Gauranga's form. Raga sindhura.*[^10]

**3.1.** The beauty of His face is like gold tried on the touchstone;
one glance, and the mind of the whole world is filled with longing.

**3.2.** Without any cause Gora's face smiles;
He is clad in yellow silk.[^11]

**3.3.** Drawn by the fragrance of His body,
a young bee has come flying;

**3.4.** round and round it circles at His feet,
humming sweetly.

**3.5.** In Govinda Ghosha's heart it awakens:
if I do not see Gora, everything tastes of poison.

[^10]: The signature is "Govinda Ghosha". PK: 18th pallava "The form of Sri Gauranga, etc." (vol. 3, PDF p. 248, printed
p. 237; MS P-R-Sa no. 2253); GPT1 — p. 116, no. 17; GPT2 — sect. 3.1, no. 17 (almost without variants). Majumdar quotes
from it the lines "without cause Gora's face smiles" and "if I do not see Gora, everything tastes of poison".
[^11]: "Without cause" (vini) — the smile comes not from an outward occasion but from inner joy. Yellow silk (pita-pata) is
Krishna's garment; in Navadvipa the Lord, in Krishna's mood, asked for yellow cloth (cf. PK 2122, chapter 3, no. 1). MS
P-R-Sa: "mukhe" ("on the face").

## 4. Govinda Ghosha — "śrīdāma sudāma saṅge ye rasa karilum̐ raṅge…" (PK 2128; BR, p. 919)
*Lila: Navadvipa — the Lord remembers the pastimes of Vrindavana and, crying "The flute, the flute!", falls senseless into
Gadadhara's lap; then, in Radha's mood, He repeats Her name and calls Lalita and Vishakha. Around Him are Vasu Ghosha,
Ramananda, Srivasa, Jagadananda, Narahari. According to Majumdar — an eyewitness's testimony; he cites the line about
Narahari as proof that Narahari Sarakara was with the Lord already in Navadvipa. Raga mangala (PK, GPT), dhanashi (BR).*[^12]

**4.1.** "The pastimes I enjoyed so gladly with Shridama and Sudama!.." —
saying this, the Lord cries out in agitation.
Crying "The flute, the flute!", Gaurahari loses consciousness,
and the Lord falls into Gadadhara's lap.[^13]

**4.2.** The rasa-lila, Vrindavana, the dear friends and girl-companions —
waves of prema rise.
Vasu Ghosha, Ramananda, Srivasa, Jagadananda —
with Narahari the Lord is intoxicated.[^14]

**4.3.** Absorbed in Radha's mood, He has become golden — Gora —
and ceaselessly repeats Radha's name.
Crying "Lalita, Vishakha!", the Lord rolls on the ground:
"Where is My hill Govardhana?

**4.4.** Where is the bank of the Yamuna? Where is My banyan Vamshivata?" —
saying this, He again loses consciousness.
Poor Govinda Ghosha received but a particle of this —
shame on such a life![^15]

[^12]: The signature is "Govinda Ghosha". PK: vol. 3, PDF pp. 239–240 (printed pp. 228–229), 18th pallava, right after
Shivananda Sena's pada PK 2127; MS P-R-Sa no. 2235; after it PK notes "Such is the description of absorption in bhava".
Narahari Chakravarti quotes the pada in BR (wave 12; ed. 1913, p. 919; ed. 1960, vv. 2997–3000) among the testimonies to
the Lord's moods in Navadvipa; GPT1 — pp. 281–282, no. 15; GPT2 — sect. 4.3, no. 15. Majumdar quotes the line about Vasu
Ghosha, Ramananda, Srivasa, Jagadananda and Narahari after BR. In the stage-1 catalogue the first line of the next pada
(PK 2129) was wrongly assigned to PK 2128.
[^13]: Shridama and Sudama are Krishna's cowherd friends in Vraja; BR and GPT read "Shridama and Subala". The Lord in
Krishna's mood remembers His pastimes with His friends. Gadadhara Pandita, according to the *Gaura-ganoddesha-dipika*, is a
manifestation of Radha in Gaura's lila (cf. chapter 3, no. 1).
[^14]: "Ramananda" is probably Ramananda Vasu of Kulinagrama (the Lord met Ramananda Raya only after His sannyasa). BR and
GPT read "the Lord dances with Narahari". Majumdar sees in the line proof that Narahari Sarakara was a companion of the Lord
already in Navadvipa.
[^15]: "In Radha's mood… became golden" — cf. CC Adi 4: Krishna took on Radha's lustre and mood to taste Her love.
Vamshivata is the banyan in Vrindavana under which Krishna played the flute. BR and GPT1 read "nā pāyala lavaleśe" — "did
not receive even a particle"; P-R-Sa, BR and GPT — "e chāra jīvana" — "this wretched life". The signature is the usual
formula of humility.

## 5. Vasu Ghosha — "dekhata jhulata gauracandra…" (PK 1550)
*Lila: Navadvipa — the swing festival (jhulana) in the bright fortnight of Shravana: the devotees swing Gaurachandra, the
townspeople come running at the cries of "Hari, Hari!". In PK — the gaura-chandrika opening the section on the swinging
of Radha and Krishna. According to Majumdar — a pada about an event Vasu Ghosha saw himself. Raga jayajayanti.*[^16]

**5.1.** Behold: Gaurachandra is swinging,
the wondrous jewel among the twice-born;
the utmost of the Creator's making, a matchless form
that outshines tested gold.

**5.2.** Many devotees push the swing,
surrounding Gaurachandra;
in joy the cries of "Jaya!" sound again and again,
overflowing the town in a wave.[^17]

**5.3.** Eyes like lotuses, a spotless face
that outshines the autumn moon;
the townspeople come running in a throng,
hearing the cries of "Hari, Hari!".

**5.4.** Blessed is the Kali age, blessed Gora's descent,
blessed, blessed the Suradhuni!
Nothing but Gorachand is in the heart —
so says Vasu Ghosha, having understood this.[^18]

[^16]: The signature is "Vasu Ghosha". PK: vol. 2, PDF p. 456 (printed p. 441), 30th pallava of the 3rd shakha ("The
swing lila"), under the heading "Now, the swinging game in the bright fortnight of Shravana. Jhulana. Here — Sri
Gaurachandra"; MS P-R-Sa no. 2015. In GPT (GPT1, pp. 323–324, no. 8; GPT2, section on the jhulana, no. 8) the pada is
shorter — three couplets: after the first two comes "Eyes like lotuses… outshines the autumn moon; / with Gadadhara He
swings joyfully — seeing it, Shiva-Ram is blessed", i.e. the signature of the poet Shivarama. Majumdar counts PK 1550
among Vasu Ghosha's historically valuable padas describing what the poet saw himself (the jhulana); we accept the
signature of Vasu after PK.
[^17]: The end of the couplet is damaged in the PK scan; we read "uthale nagara bhariyā" ("overflowing the town in a
wave"); GPT: "uṭhata nāgara nadīyā" ("[the din] rises in the town of Nadia").
[^18]: The Suradhuni is the Ganga, on whose bank the swing was set up. "Having understood this" — having understood that
Gaura is the Lord Himself.

## 6. Narahari — "āju racita nava ratana-him̐ḍora…" (PK 1559)
*Lila: Navadvipa — the jhulana: on the bank of the Ganga, under a tall tree, a new jewelled swing has been set up; the
companions swing Gaura, sing and dance to the drums, the people of Nadia come running, the gods shower the Lord with
flowers. In PK — a gaura-chandrika in the same section on the jhulana. Raga imana-beloyara.*[^19]

**6.1.** *(Refrain)* Today a new jewelled swing has been set up;
there, on the bank of the Suradhuni, under a tall tree, —
the young Gaura, full of rasa.

**6.2.** The skilful companions gently push the swing
and sing melodies, intoxicated with sweet rasa;
they call out the syllables of the rhythm — "khol, khol, tak, thai-thai" —
dance sweetly and play in every manner.[^20]

**6.3.** In the town of Nadia no one has stayed at home:
from every side men and women come;
the joy in their hearts grows ever greater
as they behold the Lord's smiling moon-face.

**6.4.** The gods in the sky with their retinues, in bliss,
shower flowers and cry "Jaya!";
Narahari says: the world has gone mad with joy —
who can describe this wondrous, boundless play?[^21]

[^19]: The signature is "Narahari". PK: vol. 2, PDF p. 460 (printed p. 445), 30th pallava, the third pada of the section
on the jhulana after the padas of Shivarama (PK 1556–1557, the swinging of Radha and Krishna) and Uddhava Dasa (PK 1558,
Gaura swinging with Gadadhara); MS P-R-Sa no. 2103. GPT1 — p. 326, no. 16 (raga imana); GPT2 — section on the jhulana,
no. 16. S. Ray's index of poets assigns PK 1557 to Narahari, but that is Shivarama's pada on the swinging of Radha and
Krishna; the gaura-pada signed Narahari in this section is PK 1559. The attribution to Narahari Sarakara is not proven: the
signature "Narahari" is also borne by padas of Narahari Chakravarti, and the language here is Vrajabuli, which according
to Majumdar is absent from Sarakara's genuine padas.
[^20]: The khol is a clay drum (mridanga); "tak, thai-thai" are rhythmic syllables of the dance. For "play" (vāota) MS
P-R-Sa reads "vājāyata" (same sense).
[^21]: "Has gone mad" (umatāyala) — intoxicated with the joy of prema at the sight of the Lord.

## 7. Chaitanyadasa — "gaurāṅga-cāndera mane ki bhāva uṭhila…" (PK 1169)
*Lila: Navadvipa — the goshtha-lila: seeing Gauridasa, the Lord remembers His play as a cowherd boy, asks for the milking
rope and the milk pail, calls the cows Dhavali and Shangali and His friends Shridama and Sudama; Nityananda embraces Him. In
PK — the gaura-chandrika to the goshthashtami festival. According to Majumdar — the same goshtha-lila (Gauridasa, the
milking rope) as in Vasu Ghosha. Raga bhupali.*[^22]

**7.1.** What bhava has arisen in the heart of Gaurangachand?
It seems He has remembered His former deeds.

**7.2.** Seeing Gauridasa's face, He rejoices at heart
and calls aloud: "Bring the milking rope!

**7.3.** Today is an auspicious day — let us go to the pasture:
from today I shall begin to milk the cows.

**7.4.** Where are Dhavali and Shangali? Where are Shridama and Sudama?
Give the milk pail into My hands, Rama!"[^23]

**7.5.** Beside Himself with bhava is the son of Shachi;
and at once Nityananda comes and embraces Him.

**7.6.** Chaitanyadasa says: the milking rope
Gauridasa has lost — the gopis have stolen it.[^24]

[^22]: The signature is "Chaitanyadasa". PK: vol. 2, PDF p. 286, 20th pallava of the 3rd shakha, the first pada of the
section "Goshthashtami-yatra", under the heading "Sri Gaurachandra"; MS P-R-Sa no. 1700. GPT1 — p. 331, no. 32 (raga
bhupala); GPT2 — no. 32 — with minor variants. Goshthashtami (Kartika, eighth day of the bright fortnight) is the festival
of Krishna's first going out to herd the cows. Majumdar regards Chaitanyadasa (probably of Gadadhara's branch) as a
contemporary and compares the pada with the goshtha-lila in Vasu Ghosha (BR, p. 935; chapter 1, no. 11) and Vamshivadana
(PK 2564; chapter 1, no. 12).
[^23]: Dhavali ("the white one") and Shangali (śāṅalī, "the dark one"; GPT — Shamali, the same sense) are Krishna's cows.
"Rama" is Balarama: so the Lord addresses Nityananda, who in Krishna's lila is Balarama. Shridama and Sudama are Krishna's
cowherd friends.
[^24]: A playful signature: Gauridasa Pandita in Krishna's lila is the cowherd boy Subala (according to the
*Gaura-ganoddesha-dipika*), and in Vraja the gopis hide the milking rope to delay Krishna. GPT and MS P-R-Sa read "ḍuri"
for "daṛi" (same sense).

## 8. Nayanananda — "kali-ghora-timire garāsala jaga-jana…" (PK 2204)
*Praise of Gaura: when the darkness of Kali had swallowed the world and people had abandoned dharma, the Creator granted,
without any sadhana, the chintamani gem — Gora, the Lord of great mercy; learning without worship of Gauranga is useless.
Raga shri (PK), suhai (GPT).*[^25]

**8.1.** The dense darkness of Kali has swallowed the people of the world,
dharma and righteous deeds remain far away;
without any effort the Creator brought and bestowed the chintamani gem —
Gora, the Lord of great mercy.[^26]

**8.2.** *(Refrain)* Brother, brother, Gora's virtues cannot be told:
however many hundred-mouthed ones, however many four-faced ones there are —
describing them, they find no end.[^27]

**8.3.** One who has studied the four Vedas and six darshanas
but does not worship Gauranga —
what use is his learning? As a mirror to the blind,
so is all his knowledge to him.[^28]

**8.4.** One who knows neither the Vedas nor learning
but knows that Gauranga is the essence of all —
says Nayanananda — he knows everything,
all perfections lie in the palm of his hand.

[^25]: The signature is "Nayanananda". PK: vol. 3, PDF p. 270 (printed p. 259), 20th pallava ("Sri Gaurachandra — another
manner"), raga the same as that of the preceding pada (shri); MS P-R-Sa no. 2312. GPT1 — p. 32, no. 18 (raga suhai);
GPT2 — sect. 1.3, no. 18. Nayanananda (Nayana Mishra) is the nephew and disciple of Gadadhara Pandita; Majumdar counts him
among the contemporary poets. In the stage-1 catalogue a GPT-only pada signed Narahari, "gorā vaṛa dayāra ṭhākura" (NS-009),
was wrongly linked to PK 2204: the words "Gora, the Lord of great mercy" occur here too, but PK 2204 is Nayanananda's
pada.
[^26]: Chintamani is the wish-fulfilling gem. "Without any effort" (asādhane) — without sadhana: Gaura bestows prema by
causeless mercy, without asking about qualification (cf. CC Adi 8).
[^27]: "The hundred-mouthed" — Ananta-Shesha, who ever sings of the Lord with a thousand mouths; "the four-faced" — the
Brahmas of the universes.
[^28]: PK: "as to the eyeless [a mirror] — what is a mirror to him?"; GPT: "what is the blind man to do with a mirror?". The
six darshanas are the six philosophical schools (nyaya, vaisheshika, sankhya, yoga, mimamsa, vedanta).

## 9. Raya Ananta — "preme matta nityānanda…" (KGC, 11th kshanada)
*Praise of Nityananda: intoxicated with prema, He walks swaying, rolls on the ground with the name of Hari and weeps looking
at the face of His brother, Gaura; Advaita, seeing Him, roars; the companions sing the names of Hari and Gaura. Raga
kamoda (KGC), mangala (GPT).*[^29]

**9.1.** Nityananda, intoxicated with prema, the source of natural bliss,
walks swaying, swaying;
intoxicated with His brother's mood, knowing all truths,
crying "Hari!" He rolls on the ground.[^30]

**9.2.** *(Refrain)* Nitai's body is moulded of love for Gora;
looking at His brother's face, He sinks down limply,
and streams of tears flow, watering the earth.[^31]

**9.3.** Advaita, the source of bliss, seeing Nitai's moon-face,
roars, and His body bristles with rapture;
they cry "Hari, Hari!", and again "Gaura, Gaura!" —
the dear companions sing His virtues.

**9.4.** The flood of Goloka's prema has blessed the earth —
a matchless, boundless ocean of rasa;
the world is intoxicated, filled with the names "Nitai — Chaitanya";
Raya Ananta begs but a drop.[^32]

[^29]: The signature is "Raya Ananta" (rāya ananta). The pada is known from KGC (11th kshanada — Krishna-ekadashi, no. 2,
a gaura-chandrika marked "of Sri Nityananda"; p. 83) and GPT (GPT1 — pp. 429–430, no. 52; GPT2 — sect. 6.1, no. 52); it had
not previously been found in PK (see below). B. B. Majumdar (1961) identifies the "Raya Ananta" of KGC with Ananta of Advaita's branch, a contemporary of the
Lord; the identification is not proven. The text follows KGC (checked against the scan).
Full PK check (chapter 16): the pada is also in PK — **PK 2328** (vol. 3, 22nd pallava, PDF pp. 313–314, label "॥৩৫॥২৩২৮॥", MS P-R-Sa no. 2432); PK signature "ananta nā pāila eka vindu" (MSS ka, kha: "rāya ananta māge eka vindu").
[^30]: "The source of natural bliss" (sahaje ānanda-kanda) — Nityananda ("eternal bliss") by His very nature. "Knowing all
truths" — Nityananda (Balarama) knows the hidden truth about Gaura.
[^31]: "Brother" (bhāiyā) is Gaura; for "looking at His brother's face" GPT reads "looking at Gadadhara's face".
[^32]: Goloka is Krishna's supreme abode; "the flood of Goloka's prema" is the prema that Gaura and Nitai brought to the
earth. GPT: "begs a single drop".

## 10. Vasu Ghosha — "śacīra mandire āsi duyārera pāśe vasi…" (PK 2221)
*Lila: the last night in Navadvipa — Vishnupriya wakes, does not find the Lord and comes to Shachi; Shachi and her
daughter-in-law search for Nimai with a lamp, the people of Nadia question passers-by; someone has seen Him on the road to
Kanchananagara (Katwa). According to Majumdar — a pada of Vasu Ghosha's cycle on the sannyasa ("Nimai-sannyasa"): a
historical picture with poetic exaggerations. Raga bibhasa-karuna.*[^33]

**10.1.** Coming to Shachi's rooms, sitting down by the door,
softly Vishnupriya speaks:
"He was in the bedchamber — where has He gone in the night,
bringing down a thunderbolt upon my head?

**10.2.** Gauranga is awake in my heart, there is no sleep in my eyes…" —
hearing this, Mother Shachi rose;
with dishevelled hair she runs, her cloth slipping off —
hearing her daughter-in-law's words.[^34]

**10.3.** Quickly lighting a lamp, she looked here and there,
but nowhere found a trace;
together with her daughter-in-law Vishnupriya, weeping and weeping on the road,
Shachi calls: "Nimai!"

**10.4.** Hearing this, the people of Nadia weep aloud in grief,
asking everyone for news:
if one man goes along the road, ten ask him:
"Have you seen where Gauranga went?"

**10.5.** He answers: "I saw Him on the road — no one was with Him,
He hastens along the road to Kanchananagara."
Vasu Ghosha says: such is Shachi's state —
"What if He shaves His head!"[^35]

[^33]: The signature is "Vasu Ghosha". PK: vol. 3, PDF pp. 275–276 (printed pp. 264–265), 21st pallava ("Sri Gauranga's
sannyasa, etc."), the fourth pada of the pallava — after Lochana Dasa's padas PK 2219 (before dawn the Lord goes to the

regarding Himself as utterly lowly.[^46]

**13.3.** "You are My friends; give Me this blessing,
laying your hands upon My head:

let Me attain the Lord of Vraja in Vraja!"[^47]

**13.4.** Saying this, Gaura Raya runs, His face raised to the sky,
