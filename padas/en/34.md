# Nagari Padas — VIII: Lochana Dasa (padas from GPT, sect. 3.2, nos. 62–85, 116); concluding note to the section of nagari padas

*On what nagari-bhava is, the position of the tradition (Vrindavana Dasa, Krishnadasa Kaviraja, Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati) and the rules of translation, see the introductory note to the section of nagari padas in portion 27 (`en/27.md`).*

*Portion 34 is the last of the section of nagari padas: the padas signed by Lochana Dasa in section 3.2 of the *Gaura-pada-tarangini* (GPT). There are 26
of them: nos. 61–85 (GPT1 pp. 177–189, scan PDF pp. 436–448, in one continuous block) and no. 116 (p. 204, PDF p. 463). All have been checked against the
scan of GPT1 (text and signatures), against GPT2 (same numbering, same text), the *Padakalpataru* (PK), the *Padamrita-samudra*, the
*Kshanada-gita-chintamani*, Lochana's *Chaitanya-mangala* (our translation — `../lochana/`) and the anthologies of the early twentieth century. Four
padas are not translated anew here: **no. 61** "nadīyā-nāgarī sāri sāri sāri…" = PK 2178 and **no. 66** "āra śunecha ālo sai…" = PK 2174 were translated
in portion 28 (nos. 28 and 27); **no. 80** "ānanda nadīyāpure ṭalamala premabhare…" = PK 2088 is a passage of the *Chaitanya-mangala* itself
(Madhya-khanda: the Lord in kirtana, raga sindhura, with the refrain "The town of Nadia rejoices…"; our translation — `../lochana/en/27.md`, vv. 110–118;
GPT gives vv. 110–114 and 118, without vv. 115–117), in content a praise of the Lord in kirtana, not nagari-bhava; **no. 84** "niravadhi gorārūpa mane
jāgiyāche go…" = PK 777, signed by **Vasu Ghosha** (translated in portion 27, no. 16): in GPT1 the same pada also stands as no. 17 with Vasu's
signature, so Lochana's signature in no. 84 contradicts both PK and GPT itself. The remaining 22 padas are translated below (nos. 1–22).*

*Lochana Dasa (c. 1520 — ?) is the author of the *Chaitanya-mangala*, a disciple of Narahari Sarakara Thakura of Shrikhanda, a younger contemporary of
the Lord's associates; he himself did not see the Lord. The nagari motif is present in the *Chaitanya-mangala* too (the women of Nadia, forgetting
shame, hurry to look at Gaura — see our translation, `../lochana/en/09.md`, note 9; `../lochana/en/18.md`, notes 5–6); Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati,
publishing the *Chaitanya-mangala* (1922), refuses in his preface to ascribe to it "the doctrine of gaura-nagari" (`../lochana/en/00.md`). The padas
of this portion are of two kinds: (1) padas in the literary language of the padas, in payar and tripadi metre (nos. 1–4, 15–17, 21); (2) padas in
colloquial Bengali, in short rhymed feet, with colloquial forms ("dekhalāma", "halo", "ka're") and kirtana singers' insertions in brackets (nos. 5–14,
18–20, 22) — in the manner of the playful songs traditionally connected with Lochana's name (dhāmālī, "dhamali"). The padas of the second kind are
known only from GPT (two of them — nos. 16, 17 — are also in the anthology *Prachina-vaishnava-padavali*, 1922, with Lochana's signature); their
language was probably renewed in oral transmission. None of the 22 padas is in PK or in the *Chaitanya-mangala*; Lochana's signature does not
compete with any other (except in no. 84, excluded above). The attribution is by the signature: Lochana is a recognized author (B). Where a pada
builds on someone else's lines (no. 5 begins with two couplets of Jnanadasa's pada PK 748), this is noted.*

*The translation is faithful to the author, without worldly vocabulary beyond the original and without softening: where a townswoman speaks of
attraction, embraces, a dream, it is translated exactly, with a note on the position of the tradition. The Bengali text, variants and word-for-word
translation are in `bn/34.md`. At the end — the summary of the portion and the concluding note to the whole section of nagari padas (portions 27–34).*

## 1. Lochana Dasa — "gaurāṅga-taraṅge nayana majila…" (GPT1 pp. 177–178, no. 62)
*Nagari-bhava. The townswoman's eyes have drowned in the waves of Gauranga: she is ready to take disgrace upon herself and leave home; all her
household work betrays her — she knows nothing but Gora, in anyone she meets she sees Gauranga; lying with her husband, she imagines Gaurahari coming
and taking her to Himself. Raga pahida.*[^1]

**1.1.** In the waves of Gauranga my eyes have drowned — what now shall I choose?
Lifting the basket of disgrace onto my head, I will stay at home no more.

**1.2.** *(Refrain)* Friend, what am I to do now?
Giving myself away for Gaurangachand, I will make an end of the house.[^2]

**1.3.** All my household work — it is plain to everyone now: I know nothing but Gora;
seeing another, mistaking him in my confusion, I say: "Gauranga!"

**1.4.** When I lie with my husband, Gauranga awakens in my heart:
Gaurahari, my life, comes in haste and throws my husband down on the ground;

**1.5.** He takes me, places me on His breast, presses face to face;
in ecstasy Gauranga pours out nectar — it flows down over every limb.[^3]

**1.6.** Gauranga, the jewel, I will carefully enfold in my arms;
bidding farewell to all, I have let everything float away, — says this servant Lochana.[^4]

[^1]: Signature: "e dāsa locana" ("this servant Lochana"). GPT1 — pp. 177–178, no. 62 (raga pahida; scan PDF pp. 436–437); GPT2 — sect. 3.2, no. 62
(same text). The pada is not in PK, the *Padamrita-samudra*, KGC or the *Chaitanya-mangala*; no other witnesses have been found. On Lochana Dasa see
portion 28, note 75, and the note at the beginning of this portion: a disciple of Narahari Sarakara, a younger contemporary of the Lord's associates;
attribution by the signature — B. On nagari-bhava see the introductory note to the section (portion 27).
[^2]: "Lifting the basket of disgrace onto my head" (kalaṅkera ḍāli māthāya dhariyā) — a saying: to take ill fame upon oneself openly. "Giving myself
away for Gaurangachand" (nichani laiyā) — the nichhani rite (portion 27, note 10). "I will make an end of the house" (gṛhe samādhāna di) — lit. "I will
give the house its conclusion."
[^3]: The scene in 1.4–1.5 is the speaker's imagination: "Gauranga awakens in my heart," and she goes on to picture what He would do. It is translated
as in the original, without softening. This is precisely the notion of Gaura as the lover of the townswomen ("gaura-nagari") which the Gaudiya
tradition rejects: according to the *Chaitanya-bhagavata* (Adi-khanda), the Lord in this incarnation did not look at any woman; Srila Bhaktisiddhanta
Sarasvati counts "gaura-nagari" among the apasampradayas (introductory note). According to Majumdar, nagari padas known only from GPT are an
unreliable and late layer. "Pours out nectar" (sudhā ugāraye) is an image of sweetness common in padas on Krishna.
[^4]: "Bidding farewell to all" (tilāñjali diyā) — lit. "giving the farewell handful of water with sesame" (as to the dead): leaving for ever. "I have
let float away" (bhāsānu) is in the past tense, while "I will enfold" (laiva) is in the future; so in the original.

## 2. Lochana Dasa — "śuna śuna sai āra kichu kai…" (GPT1 p. 178, no. 63)
*Nagari-bhava. "Listen, friend: Gauranga is not a man!" Ever since the townswoman saw the moon of His face, her eyes have drowned in the ocean of
beauty; at the ghat and in the field she sees Gora, the colour of a golden filament; sitting among the elders, she remembers His face; giving herself
away for Him, she will leave everything. Raga kamoda.*[^5]

**2.1.** Listen, listen, friend, I will tell you more: Gauranga is not a man!
In all the world, to what can the son of Shachi be compared?[^6]

**2.2.** Since I saw the moon of Gauranga's face, I cannot turn away [from it]:
in that ocean of beauty my eyes have drowned, the noose of love has tightened.

**2.3.** Whether I go to the ghat or the field — I see Gora all the time, the golden filament;
the honour of family, dharma and custom — all this He has made me leave.[^7]

**2.4.** Sitting among the elders, I see Him with my eyes, His face comes to my mind;
I try to hold myself back — I cannot: He has thrown my soul into turmoil.

**2.5.** Giving myself away for Gaurangachand, I will leave everything.
On Lochana's mind night and day: "I will place Him in my heart."

[^5]: Signature: "on Lochana's mind" (locanera mane). GPT1 — p. 178, no. 63 (raga kamoda; scan PDF p. 437); GPT2 — 3.2, no. 63 (same text). No other
witnesses; B. On nagari-bhava see the introductory note to the section (portion 27).
[^6]: "Gauranga is not a man" — a praise that agrees with the teaching: Gaura is Bhagavan Himself (CC Adi 1–4); the townswoman says it out of
amazement at His beauty.
[^7]: "Golden filament" (kanaka-keśara) — the colour of Gora's body is compared to the golden filaments (keshara) of a flower. Leaving "the honour of
family, dharma and custom" is a motif of the padas on the gopis (*Bhag.* 10.29) transferred to the townswomen of Nadia; it is this transfer that the
tradition rejects (introductory note; cf. portion 27, note 43).

## 3. Lochana Dasa — "hiyāra mājhāre gaurāṅga rākhiyā…" (GPT1 p. 178, no. 64)
*Nagari-bhava (only in the address to a friend). Having placed Gauranga in her heart, the townswoman will sit in solitude and look at the moon of His
face; she has heard that He who was formerly in Gokula, in Nanda's house, has now appeared in Navadvipa, in Shachi's house. Raga kamoda.*[^8]

**3.1.** Having placed Gauranga in my heart, I will sit in solitude;
to my heart's content I will keep that moon of a face before my eyes.

**3.2.** I have heard: He who formerly [was] in Gokula, in the house of Nanda, —
He has come to Navadvipa and revealed Himself in the house of Shachi.[^9]

**3.3.** The word of Lochana: listen, friend, what more shall I tell you?
I saw the face — and the mind forgot itself: I cannot forget Him.

[^8]: Signature: "the word of Lochana" (locanera vāṇī). GPT1 — p. 178, no. 64 (raga kamoda; scan PDF p. 437); GPT2 — 3.2, no. 64 (same text). No other
witnesses; B. The nagari motif is only in the address "friend": in substance this is a praise of Gaura and contemplation of Him in the heart. On
nagari-bhava see the introductory note to the section (portion 27).
[^9]: Gaura is the same Krishna, the son of Nanda, who has appeared in Shachi's house (CC Adi 1–4); in Lochana this thought runs through the whole
*Chaitanya-mangala*.

## 4. Lochana Dasa — "gaurāṅgavadane harila cetane…" (GPT1 p. 178, no. 65)
*Nagari-bhava. Gauranga's face has stolen the townswoman's reason: she wants to forget — she cannot. Refrain: "Gora has entered the heart; the
townswomen of Nadia have gone mad — I have understood it by myself." Seeing Gauranga's feet, a noble wife leaves her husband; whether it is righteous
or not — it is shameful even to say. Raga kamoda.*[^10]

**4.1.** Gauranga's face has stolen my reason — I see a great calamity has come;
I want to forget — I cannot forget; tell me, friend, what am I to do?

**4.2.** *(Refrain)* Gora has entered the heart.
The townswomen of Nadia have gone mad — I have understood it by myself.

**4.3.** As soon as I saw Gauranga's feet, He at once stole my mind;
a young noble faithful wife, whoever she may be, leaves her treasure — her husband.[^11]

**4.4.** I do not know whether by dharma or by karma — I am ashamed even to say;
Lochana Dasa is troubled in soul: now he too has understood what it is.

[^10]: Signature: "Lochana Dasa." GPT1 — p. 178, no. 65 (raga kamoda; refrain marked "dhru"; scan PDF p. 437); GPT2 — 3.2, no. 65 (same text). No other
witnesses; B. On nagari-bhava see the introductory note to the section (portion 27).
[^11]: "Leaves her treasure — her husband" (tyaje nija patidhana) is the parakiya motif of the padas on the gopis transferred to the noble wives of
Nadia (see note 7); translated exactly. "Whether by dharma or by karma" (4.4) — i.e., one cannot tell whether it is righteous or sinful. In 4.4b the poet
speaks of himself in the third person: he too has "understood by himself" the power of Gaura's form (cf. the refrain).

## 5. Lochana Dasa — "(gaurera) rūpa lāgi ām̐khi jhore…" (GPT1 pp. 179–180, no. 67)
*Nagari-bhava. The townswoman's eyes weep for Gaura's form, each of her limbs for each of His; why did she go to the Suradhuni, why did she look at
Gaura — He pierced her with the arrows of His eyes; her eyes want to go and look, her mind wants to become His maidservant; her mind slipped on the
slippery ghat of the ocean of His beauty. She will cut open her breast and hide Him there together with her life. Raga "yatharaga".*[^12]

**5.1.** For the form (of Gaura) my eyes weep, my mind is enraptured by His virtues;
for each [of His] limbs each of my limbs weeps.

**5.2.** For the touch of [His] breast my breast weeps;
my heart cannot hold the darling doll of my soul.[^13]

**5.3.** Why did I go to the Suradhuni (I went! I went!!),
why did I turn my eyes to the form of Gaura?

**5.4.** Why ever did I look at Gaura?
(Gaura) pierced me with the two arrows of His eyes.

**5.5.** My eyes say: "Let us go and look at that form";
my mind says: "I will become His maidservant."

**5.6.** He comes and goes by the path of the eyes;
He has cut through my ribs and made Himself a dwelling [there].[^14]

**5.7.** At the slippery ghat of the ocean that is Gaura's form
my mind went down, slipped and fell.

**5.8.** As if Gaura's form were not enough — it is steeped in love besides,
(and on top of that) a slight smile and a sidelong glance.

**5.9.** (Gaura has) as many forms as He has adornments;
oh! He! — He scorches me till my ribs are spent.[^15]

**5.10.** For the form (of Gaura) my eyes weep;
His virtues intoxicate my mind.

**5.11.** Not for a moment can I forget (Gaura's form);
in what an hour did I place (Gauranga's form) in my heart!

**5.12.** I will cut open this breast and hide [Him] there together with my very life;
when I wish, I will take Him out and look at the moon of His face.[^16]

**5.13.** Seeing Gaura's form, everyone's heart rejoices;
with joy in his heart says this Lochana Dasa.

[^12]: Signature: "e locana dāsa." GPT1 — pp. 179–180, no. 67 (raga "yatharaga"; scan PDF pp. 438–439); GPT2 — 3.2, no. 67 (same text). The first
two couplets (5.1–5.2) are the beginning of Jnanadasa's pada PK 748 "rūpa lāgi ām̐khi jhure guṇe mana bhora…" (PK vol. 2, 8th pallava, signature
"jñāna"; also in the *Padamrita-samudra*), where Radha weeps for Shyama; here "(gaurera)" — "of Gaura" — is added, and the whole pada is turned to
Gaura; from then on it is colloquial Bengali. Developing the opening lines of a well-known pada is a usual practice of kirtaniyas; the pada is a
composite, and its attribution to Lochana is by the signature (B). On nagari-bhava see the introductory note to the section (portion 27).
[^13]: The words in brackets — "(gaurera)", "(gaura)", "(gelāma! gelāma!!)", "(tāte āvāra)", "(gaurarūpa)", "(gaurāṅgarūpa)" — are insertions of
the kirtana singers (akhar), as GPT prints them; they are translated in brackets. "The darling doll of my soul" (parāṇa putalī) — what is dearest;
PK 748 has here "parāṇa pirīti lāgi," while "parāṇa-putalī mora" is the reading of MS P-R-Sa in the PK apparatus: GPT follows it.
[^14]: "Made a dwelling" (karala khānā): khānā is "house, dwelling" (Pers. khana) or "pieces" ("cut to pieces"); both readings are possible.
[^15]: "Scorches" (bhājite) — lit. "frying"; the sense of the line is unclear, the translation is conjectural.
[^16]: The wish to keep the Lord's form in the heart for ever; "to cut open the breast" is a hyperbole of love poetry, known also from padas on Radha.

## 6. Lochana Dasa — "uṣaḥkāle sakhī mile jala bharite yāya…" (GPT1 pp. 180–181, no. 68)
*Nagari-bhava. At dawn a young wife goes with her friends for water and meets Gora with His friends on the road; having filled her pitcher, she stands
as if stunned and gazes: who is this — a god of rasa? Brows, eyes, nose, teeth, the colour of His body, arms, necklace, white cloth, ringing anklets;
the nagara of beauty has risen — and the mind of "the nagari Lochana" is swept away. Raga "yatharaga".*[^17]

**6.1.** At dawn, together with her friends, she goes to fetch water;
on the road — a meeting: Gora-raya with His friends.

**6.2.** Dying at heart, I filled the pitcher and lifted it onto my hip;
the young wife stands as if stunned, beside herself, and gazes.[^18]

**6.3.** "Who is this — a god of rasa? there is no limit to His beauty;
what creator has gathered the treasure of rasa in one place?

**6.4.** The pair of brows — the teacher of Kama — shoots flower-arrows;
with what ink, with what brush were they made?

**6.5.** The depths of the eyes are spotless petals of a blue lotus;
the two lids, reddish, glisten moist.

**6.6.** A sesame flower — how could it compare? such is the beauty of the nose;
turned on a lathe, even — what a lustre of teeth!

**6.7.** Vermilion and orpiment were kneaded with fresh butter;
pure gold, the moon — laid on and burnished.

**6.8.** Dissolving lac-dye in milk, they moulded the hands;
they brought the moon, sifted it and sifted it — and skilfully set it there.[^19]

**6.9.** On His neck a necklace — how it shines! and what a splendour of arms! —
as if a golden elephant had come down from the sky to draw water.

**6.10.** Neatly girt, the white cloth adorns Him;
graceful, conquering the world, the anklets ring on His feet.

**6.11.** The nagara of beauty, the ocean of rasa, has come and risen [like the sun];
by this the mind of the nagari Lochana has been swept away."[^20]

[^17]: Signature: "the nagari Lochana." GPT1 — pp. 180–181, no. 68 (raga "yatharaga"; scan PDF pp. 439–440); GPT2 — 3.2, no. 68 (same text). No other
witnesses; B. The pada begins as a narrative in the third person ("goes") and passes into the first ("I filled"); so in the original. On nagari-bhava
see the introductory note to the section (portion 27).
[^18]: "The young wife" — GPT1 has "vam̐dhu" (usually "beloved, friend"); from the sense (stupor, bewilderment) it apparently stands here for vadhū,
"young wife": the speaker about herself. "Beside herself" (cauura hārā) — the meaning is conjectural. "Friends" (sakhā) — the Lord's associates.
[^19]: 6.3–6.10 describe the form from the brows to the feet, as in padas on Krishna: the brows are "the teacher of Kama" (they teach him to shoot), the
colour of the body is a mixture of vermilion, orpiment and butter burnished like pure gold; "the moon sifted and set" in the hands is apparently the
nails (cf. "the moons of the nails"). "A god of rasa" (rasera de) is conjectural (de = deva).
[^20]: "The nagari Lochana": the poet calls himself a townswoman — he sings in the bhava of a nagari, putting himself in the heroine's place. The
tradition does not accept such worship of Gaura (introductory note). "The nagara of beauty" is an epithet of Gaura in nagari-bhava (ibid.).

## 7. Lochana Dasa — "śacīra gorā kāmera koṛā…" (GPT1 p. 181, no. 69)
*Nagari-bhava. The townswoman saw Gora at the ghat: curly hair, a garland of malati round His forehead; it is distasteful even to compare pure gold
with Him. She cannot explain it to her friend: she saw Him on the way to bathe and cannot take her eyes away; the secret has stayed in her heart; awake
and asleep Gaura is in her heart. Raga "yatharaga".*[^21]

**7.1.** Shachi's Gora — the whip of Kama — I saw on the bank by the ghat:
curly hair, round the forehead a garland of fresh malati.

**7.2.** Pure gold — it is distasteful even to compare it with His beauty;
(such) a thief of hearts, a charmer of minds, is not on earth.

**7.3.** What more are you saying, friend — how shall I explain (to you)?
(From the roof?) — going to bathe with my friends, I saw Gaura.

**7.4.** Seeing (that) form, I cannot turn my two eyes away;
how I long to see Him again!

**7.5.** What more are you saying, friend — you are well off;
but my heart's secret has stayed in my heart.

**7.6.** Awake or asleep, Gaura is always in my heart;
says Lochana: whoever has seen, he knows it.

[^21]: Signature: "Lochana." GPT1 — p. 181, no. 69 (raga "yatharaga"; scan PDF p. 440); GPT2 — 3.2, no. 69 (same text). No other witnesses; B. "The whip
of Kama" (kāmera koṛā) is an unclear image: "whip," or, reading kom̐ṛā, "shoot"; in sense — the One stronger than Kama (cf. "Madana-mohana,"
introductory note). The insertion "(chāde)" in 7.3 — "on the roof" (?): the sense is unclear. On nagari-bhava see the introductory note to the section
(portion 27).

## 8. Lochana Dasa — "eka nāgarī vale didi nāite yakhana yāi…" (GPT1 pp. 181–182, no. 70)
*Nagari-bhava. A townswoman tells how, going to bathe, she put back her veil and saw Gaura; she ran home, but her eyes stayed with Him; her body
trembles; the ghat shines with His body, young women crowd; with the rope of anuraga He pulls her soul. She playfully warns her friend: look — and
you are lost; if you would keep the family honour, stay at home. Raga "yatharaga".*[^22]

**8.1.** One nagari says: "Sister, when I went to bathe,
I put back my veil, raised my face — and saw Him.

**8.2.** Seeing the form, I started — and ran home;
but my two eyes stayed tied — gazing at Gaura.

**8.3.** My body trembles, all my limbs shake;
the pendant in the nose, flashing, leaps into my very heart.[^23]

**8.4.** The ghat is lit by the radiance of Gaura's body;
to see His beauty, crowds of young women throng.

**8.5.** I went to look out of curiosity — who knew it would be like this?
With the rope of anuraga He pulls my soul.

**8.6.** My soul longs to fly away, I cannot stay at home;
not seeing Gaurachand, I do not know what is happening to my soul.

**8.7.** Look — and your eyes will stay tied: His form steals the heart;
the smiling face, the reddish eyes — here it is, the well of rasa!

**8.8.** Look — mind you, you will die gone mad, your family honour will not survive;
if you would keep family and good conduct — stay in a secluded place.

**8.9.** You will lose your family, you will go crazy, the wave of rasa will strike."
Says Lochana: only one who knows rasa can understand this.[^24]

[^22]: Signature: "Lochana." GPT1 — pp. 181–182, no. 70 (raga "yatharaga"; scan PDF pp. 440–441); GPT2 — 3.2, no. 70 (same text). No other witnesses;
B. Lines 8.3 and 8.5b recur almost word for word in no. 13 (no. 75, 13.4b, 13.6) — lines common to these songs. On nagari-bhava see the introductory
note to the section (portion 27).
[^23]: "The pendant in the nose" (nāsāra nolaka) is apparently Gaura's ornament (cf. no. 13, 13.3, 13.6: "a pendant swings in the nose… at Gora's
laughter"); in padas on Krishna — the pearl (veshara) in His nose. Translated as in the original.
[^24]: 8.7–8.9 are a playful "warning" to the friend: so great is the power of the Lord's form. "Crazy" (vauri = vāurī). The motif of "losing the
family" — see note 7.

## 9. Lochana Dasa — "gorārūpa rasera kūpa sahajei eta…" (GPT1 p. 182, no. 71)
*Nagari-bhava. Gora's form is a well of rasa just by itself — and if He were to use His art, what then! Should He arrange His curls, a noble wife
could not keep her family honour; should He glance sidelong, should He ask — who could withstand? Rati herself, leaving Kama, worships Gora's feet.
The poet: "Now I have made Gora my all." Raga "yatharaga".*[^25]

**9.1.** Gora's form — a well of rasa — is so much just by itself;
and if He uses His art, the wiles of rasa, — what will it be then!

**9.2.** If He gracefully arranges His soft curling hair,
then a faithful, noble wife cannot keep her family honour.

**9.3.** Whomever He glances at sidelong — will her pride remain?
and if He should ask — would the rasavati survive?[^26]

**9.4.** With a garland on His neck, swinging His arms, He passes by —
Rati herself, Kama's wife, leaving her husband, worships Gora's feet.[^27]

**9.5.** Our Gora fills the breast: look — and the breast is full;
as if one held Him in one's arms — joy upon joy.

**9.6.** The stream of His smile, like nectar, cools the soul;
subject to rasa, He is (everything) all my wealth, the form of my longing.[^28]

**9.7.** Listen, friend dear to my heart, what more shall I say?
Says Lochana: now I have made Gora my all.

[^25]: Signature: "Lochana." GPT1 — p. 182, no. 71 (raga "yatharaga"; scan PDF p. 441); GPT2 — 3.2, no. 71 (same text). No other witnesses; B. Couplets
9.1–9.4 recur almost word for word in no. 20 (no. 83, 20.4–20.6, 20.8) — lines common to these songs. On nagari-bhava see the introductory note to the

## Concluding Note to the Section of Nagari Padas (Portions 27–34)

*1. **How many and whose.** The section contains 183 padas in eight portions: Vasu Ghosha — 40 (portion 27 — 25 padas from PK; portion 28 — 15 padas
known from GPT); Nayanananda — 5; Yadunandana — 6 (the cycle of the 12th wave of the *Bhakti-ratnakara*, portion 29; one signed "Yadu"); Yadu — 2;
Murari — 2 (Murari Gupta by the GPT signature and Dasa Murari); Balarama Dasa — 1; Chandrashekhara — 1; Lochana Dasa — 24 (two padas from PK in
portion 28 and 22 from GPT in portion 34); "Narahari" — 102 (portions 29–33; nearly all by Narahari Chakravarti; their concluding note is at the end of
portion 33).*

*2. **Attribution** (scale — `TASK.md`). **A — 30**: 26 padas of Vasu Ghosha (25 from PK and one confirmed by the *Kshanada-gita-chintamani*) and 4
padas of Nayanananda from PK. **B — 112**: "probably Chakravarti" — 81 (75 found in the *Gaura-charita-chintamani*, 6 only in the
*Bhakti-ratnakara*), Lochana Dasa — 24, Yadunandana — 6, Vasu — 1 (GPT and the *Gita-chandrodaya*). **C — 40**: "Narahari" — 21 (padas only from GPT;
some stand in place of the lost pages of GCC), Vasu — 12 (GPT only), Nayanananda — 1, Yadu — 2, Murari — 2, Balarama — 1, Chandrashekhara — 1.
**D — 1** (a pada signed Vasu, according to Majumdar a reworking of a pada on Krishna; portion 28, no. 13). The earliest and most reliable layer is the
padas of Vasu Ghosha and Nayanananda from the *Padakalpataru*; the rest is a poetic development of the nagari motif: Lochana Dasa (a disciple of
Narahari Sarakara), Yadunandana (a disciple of Gadadhara Dasa) and, most of all, Narahari Chakravarti (first half of the eighteenth century).*

*3. **Who speaks.** In the great majority of padas the townswomen of Nadia speak — to a friend, to themselves, to the elders; in 25 "Narahari" padas
(GPT 3.2 nos. 158–180 and 3.1 nos. 129–130 = GCC, kiranas 10–11) heavenly women speak, who only look at Nadia from the sky; in a few, the poet himself,
putting himself in a townswoman's place ("the nagari Lochana," 34/6.11) or looking on from outside (34/15.11–15.13). Not nagari-bhava in content — 6
padas: Gaura's words in sleep in the words of Krishna (31/1), the heavenly women's replies praising the Lord's strictness (32/19–20, 33/3), Lochana's
praise of Gaura's form (34/17, 22); in a number of other padas the nagari motif is only the address "friend" or the frame (28/1, 34/3, 14, 16 and
others). Three padas are the townswomen's lament after the Lord's sannyasa, four are the women's words at His wedding.*

*4. **What the corpus shows.** The nagari padas speak of Gaura in the language of purvaraga and anuraga from the padas on Radha and the gopis: deceiving
the elders, "leaving family and husband," dreams of a meeting. The later the layer, the more outspoken the motif: in Vasu Ghosha (PK) it is mostly
admiration and longing; in the "Narahari" padas from GCC and in Lochana's colloquial padas — dreams with embraces and direct words of attraction. Yet
even in them Gaura in the lila itself does not respond to attraction: He passes by, dances in kirtana, leans on His associates; the "meeting" happens
only in the heroine's dream and fancy; in Narahari Chakravarti the Lord turns away and hurries off, and Lochana ends many padas with a call to "go into
your own house" and keep Gora in the heart and voices the teaching directly (34/16.4, 17.2, 22.3).*

*5. **The position of the tradition** (briefly). The Gaudiya Vaishnava tradition does not accept the worship of Gaura in the mood of the nagaris.
Vrindavana Dasa (*Chaitanya-bhagavata*, Adi-khanda) testifies that the Lord in this incarnation did not look at any woman and that the wise do not
glorify Him as "Gauranga-nagara"; Krishnadasa Kaviraja (*Chaitanya-charitamrita*, Adi 1.5–6; Adi 4) reveals that Gaura is Krishna Himself in the bhava
and radiance of Radha, who came to taste Her love as a devotee, not as an enjoying hero; Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati counts "gaura-nagari" among
the apasampradayas — and, publishing Lochana's *Chaitanya-mangala*, refuses to ascribe this doctrine to it. The nagari padas have been translated for
the completeness of the corpus of padas on Gaura and as a historical document — faithfully to the author, without worldly vocabulary beyond the
original and without softening, with notes on the position of the tradition; they are not a basis for worshipping Gaura as a nagara (introductory
note, portion 27).*
