# Nagari Padas — V: the "Narahari" padas of GPT (sect. 3.2, nos. 119, 124–145): the townswomen's dreams

*On what nagari-bhava is, the position of the tradition (Vrindavana Dasa, Krishnadasa Kaviraja, Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati) and the rules of translation, see the introductory note to the section of nagari padas in portion 27 (`en/27.md`); on the two poets named Narahari (Narahari Sarakara and Narahari Chakravarti) and on the attribution labels ("probably Sarakara", "probably Chakravarti", "not established"), see the introductory note to the "Narahari" appendix in portion 17 (`en/17.md`).*

## 1. Narahari — "ghumaka-ghore bhora śacīnandana…" (GPT1 p. 205, no. 119; GCC 2.13)
*Gaura asleep. At the end of the night Shachi's son, deep in sleep, as if immersed in a sleep of long ago in the kunja, softly says: "Wake,
crown-jewel of women, beautiful one! Without the song of your words My hearing has no joy; open your eyes, give Me a smile; rise — I Myself will
adorn you." This is not a townswoman's speech: GPT placed the pada in the section of nagari padas, but in GCC it stands in the account of the
last part of the Lord's night. Raga lalita. **Attribution: probably Chakravarti** — GCC, kirana 2, pada 13.*[^1]

**1.1.** Shachi's son, held by deep sleep — who can fathom His play of prema? — as if immersed in a sleep of long ago in the kunja, speaks thus, sweetly and softly:

**1.2.** "Wake, wake, crown-jewel of women, beautiful one — how long will you sleep at the end of the night? Without drinking the song of the nectar of your words, My restless hearing is bereft of the least joy.

**1.3.** Leave your closed eyelids: with the tremulous corners of your eyes, with a graceful movement, do Me honour. My heart is bound — I say it without fear: smile and give Me joy.

**1.4.** Understand My wish — rise and sit up: with My own hands I will adorn you." So speaks Narahari's Lord — and says it again, lost to Himself.[^2]

no. 119 (catalogue: NG-098). GCC — kirana 2, pada 13 (ed. 1947, p. 30; scan PDF p. 35), "lalita-rāga". The second kirana of GCC describes the last part
of the Lord's night: the bedchamber, the bees flying into the house, the birds that wake Him at dawn; in the next pada (GCC 2.14, not taken into GPT) these
words spoken in sleep are heard by Gadadhara Pandita, who wakes and then keeps watch by Gaura's bed (2.15–2.16). Thus in the author Gaura in sleep speaks
the words of Krishna waking Radha in the kunja at the end of the night (cf. the padas on the waking of Radha and Krishna, nishanta-lila): this is a
remembrance of Vraja, not nagari-bhava; on Gadadhara as the embodiment of Radha's bhava in Gaura-lila — GGD. The pada is in Vrajabuli (tachu, mohe, tohe,
majhu). B. The pada stands in the GPT section of nagari padas — see the introductory note to the section (portion 27).
[^2]: 1.2: "the song of the nectar of your words" (vacanāmṛta-saṅgīta) — so GPT1 and GPT2; GCC has "the nectar of your sigh" (sītkṛta, "sitkara" — a sigh from descriptions of a lovers'
meeting), a more explicit reading; the GPT text is translated. 1.3: "do Me honour" — after the GCC reading "kari kara sammāna" (GPT: "kari mana māna", sense
unclear); "My heart is bound" — GPT "mana mana bandha" (probably a misprint for GCC "mama mana bandhaka" — "My heart is in pledge [to you]"); "give Me joy"
(GPT "rabhasa") — GCC "give Me the jewel of a smile" (hāsi ratana). 1.4: "says it again, lost to Himself" (GPT "bahuri nigadata kakhana viśāri"; GCC "bahu
vinigadata kathana visāri" — "speaks much, forgetting what He says") — the sense of the last words is conjectural.

## 2. Narahari — "mora pati ati sujana sajani…" (GPT1 pp. 207–208, no. 124; GCC 7.29)
*Nagari-bhava. A townswoman tells her friend: yesterday her husband, alone with his father, said that Nimai Pandita has the power of God, has
mastered His senses, is righteous, merciful to wicked people and full of the joy of kirtana; therefore the young wives should not be forbidden
to see Him — His darshan destroys all that is inauspicious; as in Vraja Krishna was not recognised by the brahmanas but by their wives, so it is
here. Hearing this she rejoiced, and when Gaura passed along the street with Gadadhara, Nityananda and His companions, she went out and gazed
on Him to her heart's content: the other women were held back by their mothers-in-law, but no one held her back. Raga "yatharaga". **Attribution:
probably Chakravarti** — GCC, kirana 7, pada 29.*[^3]

**2.1.** "My husband, friend, is a very good man: listen to what he is like. Yesterday, sitting alone with his father, he said to him:

**2.2.** 'In the town of Nadia is Nimai Pandita: in Him is the power of God. Who created such a wonder I do not know; there is no end to His virtues.

**2.3.** One so master of His senses and so righteous I have never seen with my own eyes. To wicked people He is boundlessly merciful; He floats in the joy of kirtana.

**2.4.** Therefore I say: never, not even by oversight, forbid your daughters-in-law [to go to Him]. I know for certain: by His darshan all that is inauspicious perishes.

**2.5.** Fortunate are all our young wives — what more can I say? I cannot even express it. Therefore blessed is this birth as a woman — I take upon myself all its ills.

**2.6.** But we, drunk with false pride, live day and night like the blind: such a Wonder in the midst of Nadia — and we cannot recognise Him.

**2.7.** In Vraja the brahmanas did not know the Lord of Vraja — the brahmanas' wives attained Him. So it is here, there is no doubt of it — I have understood, having reflected.'[^4]

**2.8.** So father and son talked long in many ways; hidden nearby, I listened and listened — and my heart rejoiced.

**2.9.** I thought: if only I could see Gorachand right now — with the corners of my eyes I would tell Him all this.

**2.10.** Just then, from the direction of our quarter, joyful cries rose again and again; knowing that Gaura was coming, I hurried out and stood by the road.

**2.11.** From afar I gazed my fill — what wondrous beauty! The radiance of His body, outshining gold, blazes in every direction.

**2.12.** On His left is Gadadhara, on His right Nityananda, bliss itself; all around are His other companions: all gaze at that moon of a face.

**2.13.** With His own companions the Crown of connoisseurs of rasa comes, overflowing with rasa; who, seeing that lovely glance, can keep hold of her heart?

**2.14.** Smiling, as if in conversation, the Moon of Nadia showers streams of nectar; the grace of His form has captivated the world — like the snare of Madana.

**2.15.** Knowing that the Lord of their life was coming, the young women of our quarter came running; their harsh mothers-in-law and sisters-in-law held them back with many words.[^5]

**2.16.** But no one held me back — I fulfilled my heart's desire." Narahari says: she whose husband is so gracious — what obstacles has she?

[^3]: Signature — "Narahari". GPT1 — pp. 207–208, no. 124 (scan PDF pp. 466–467); GPT2 — sect. 3.2, no. 124 (catalogue: NG-102). GCC — kirana 7, pada 29 (ed. 1947,
pp. 89–90; scan PDF pp. 86–87; the pada begins with the first line of p. 89 — directly after the lost pp. 81–88, see n. 18 of portion 30). GPT nos. 124–126 are
the last padas of the 7th kirana of GCC (7.29–7.31). GCC readings: "seirūpa hethā", "hema jiniyā" (2.11; GPT1 and GPT2 "hena", a misprint; translated after
GCC), "āise dhāyā … nivāre aneka kaiyā" (2.15), "pāinu manera sādhā" (2.16). B. Nagari-bhava — see the introductory note to the section (portion 27).
[^4]: The husband's speech is praise of the Lord from the lips of a householder of Nadia: Nimai Pandita has "the power of God" (īśvara-śakati), has mastered the
senses (jitendriya), is merciful to the wicked — cf. in CB (Madhya) the deliverance of Jagai and Madhai and the words that the Lord's kirtana purifies even
sinners. 2.7 — the story of the wives of the sacrificing brahmanas (Bhag. 10.23): the brahmana priests did not recognise Krishna and refused Him food, while
their wives left everything, came to Him and received His mercy. So in this pada the rightness of the women who long to see Gaura is affirmed by a husband
in conversation with his father.
[^5]: "The Lord of their life" (prāṇanātha) — the townswoman's word for Gaura, as in the padas of the gopis about Krishna (cf. n. 14 of portion 30); translated
faithfully to the author. The townswoman herself in this pada only gazes at Gaura from afar, standing by the road.

## 3. Narahari — "śuna śuna sai vidhi arasika…" (GPT1 pp. 208–209, no. 125; GCC 7.30)
*Nagari-bhava. A townswoman laments that the Creator has not settled any of Gaura's companions in her street — then He would come there too;
yesterday she went with her husband's aunt to the house of Murari Gupta, where the young wives were preparing sandal paste and garlands for
Gauranga's coming, but the aunt took her away; on the road, looking back, she saw Gaura with His companions — lips, smile, brows, sandal on His
brow, necklace, gait; then her husband's elder brother appeared with his students, but he too was looking at Gora, and she, covering her face,
slipped away. The poet teases her about her shame. Raga "yatharaga". **Attribution: probably Chakravarti** — GCC, kirana 7, pada 30.*[^6]

**3.1.** "Listen, listen, friend: the Creator has no taste for rasa — I have understood how he goes about his work; otherwise why would he give me such pain day and night?

**3.2.** Had he settled one of Gaura's companions here, then the Moon of Nadia would come to our street too and dispel our grief.

**3.3.** That is why I tell you: yesterday, leaving all the housework, I went with my husband's aunt to that quarter — to the house of Murari Gupta.[^7]

**3.4.** There the young wives were rejoicing — their happiness had no bounds: for the Beloved of their life they were grinding sandal paste and stringing garlands of flowers.

**3.5.** From them I heard that Gauranga would come there; but the aunt, her business done, set off home — and I could not stay.

**3.6.** Lost in thought, having gone some way, I looked back at the road: the young crown prince of Nadia, finely dressed, was coming with His companions.

**3.7.** What wondrous beauty of the lips! The teeth — a gleam of pearls; the smile showers streams of nectar; the face — the splendour of the autumn moon.

**3.8.** And the curve of His brows, the glances of His curving eyes — in so many ways! The sandal on His brow is so lovely — at one look all young women are drowned.

**3.9.** At His throat sways a necklace of gold and gems, lighting up the world; His gait is so enchanting — who in the world, seeing it, would not forget himself?

**3.10.** In the ocean of that beauty I bathed in joy, standing by my husband's aunt; turning round, I saw: behind us, with his students, came my husband's elder brother.

**3.11.** By good luck he did not see me — he was looking at Gora; covering my face with my veil, lowering my eyes, I carefully hurried away."

**3.12.** Narahari says: your shame before your husband's elder brother — do I not know it? Were I to tell it all, you could not stay in this land.[^8]

[^6]: Signature — "Narahari". GPT1 — pp. 208–209, no. 125 (scan PDF pp. 467–468); GPT2 — sect. 3.2, no. 125 (catalogue: NG-103). GCC — kirana 7, pada 30 (ed. 1947,
pp. 90–91; scan PDF pp. 87–88). It is followed in GCC by a short concluding stanza (7.31, metre "surata-surati": the townswomen, recalling the past day, each tell
their own) and the colophon of the 7th kirana: "the morning doings… the description of the doings of the nagaris of Navadvipa"; stanza 7.31 was not taken
into GPT. GCC readings: "prāṇapriyā" (3.4), "gaurāṅga āsibe hethā", "rahite nā pālum̐ tathā" (3.5), "daśana tāraka-chaṭā" — "the teeth — a gleam of stars"
(3.7), "majaye yuvatī jāti" (3.8), "dekhilum̐" (3.10). B. Nagari-bhava — see the introductory note to the section (portion 27).
[^7]: Murari Gupta — a physician from Shrihatta, the Lord's companion from childhood, author of a Sanskrit life of Him (Murari's "Kadacha"; see `murari-gupta/`);
his house in Navadvipa is one of the places of the Lord's kirtanas (CB, Madhya).
[^8]: "The husband's elder brother" (bhāsura) — before him a young wife by custom covers her face and does not show herself. He himself, walking with his
students (paḍuyā), was also "looking at Gora". The last line is the poet's joke: the whole street would know at whom she was gazing; translated as in the
original.

## 4. Narahari — "śuna śuna sai niśira kāhinī…" (GPT1 pp. 209–210, no. 126; GCC 8.2)
*Nagari-bhava; a dream. A townswoman tells her friend about the night: in the evening, thinking of Gaura, she went with her mother-in-law to
the house of Srivasa Pandita, where Gora sat among His companions and talked smilingly with Gadadhara; for fear of her mother-in-law she
could not stay and returned home, lay down without supper and fell asleep only late at night; in her dream she came again to Srivasa's house,
anointed Gora with sandal paste, put garlands on Him — and on some pretext He embraced her. Raga "yatharaga". **Attribution: probably
Chakravarti** — GCC, kirana 8, pada 2.*[^9]

**4.1.** "Listen, listen, friend: what happened at night I will carefully tell you. At dusk, my work done, I was sitting at home.

**4.2.** Thinking and thinking of Gora's beauty and virtues — I do not know what came over me — I went with my mother-in-law to the house of Srivasa Pandita Thakura.

**4.3.** There, among His peerless companions, Gora is seated — the thief of the precious composure of young women, well-born and chaste.

**4.4.** His golden body shines, anointed with lovely sandal paste — as if Mount Sumeru had been smeared with the ground-up autumn moon.

**4.5.** A garland of malati sways at His throat — like a snare enchanting the world; hundreds upon hundreds of Madanas swoon at the sight of the shape of His face.

**4.6.** Smiling, He holds sweet converse with Gadadhara; showering nectar in streams, He soothes the ache of the ears.

**4.7.** Ah, ah! At the sight of such beauty, what becomes of the heart! What shall I say — not for one moment could I gaze my fill of Him with both my eyes.

**4.8.** Wretched me, what could I do? The Creator made woman dependent. For fear of my mother-in-law I could not stay and came home, dying at heart.

**4.9.** In grief I lay down; when my sister-in-law asked, I told her: 'I am not hungry, do not wake me — I feel somehow unwell.'

**4.10.** She, a simple soul, went away, and I lay there distraught; my body was restless, friend — sleep came only deep in the night.

**4.11.** In my dream I went to the house of Srivasa Thakura and saw Him; with all the longing of my heart I anointed Gora's body with fragrant sandal paste.

**4.12.** Carefully I put on His neck garland after fresh garland of many flowers; and the life of Narahari, the Crown of connoisseurs of rasa, on some pretext embraced [me]."[^10]

[^9]: Signature — "Narahari". GPT1 — pp. 209–210, no. 126 (scan PDF pp. 468–469); GPT2 — sect. 3.2, no. 126 (catalogue: NG-343). GCC — kirana 8, pada 2 (ed. 1947,
p. 92; scan PDF p. 89), "kācidāha — yathā[rāga]" ("one of them said"). The 8th kirana of GCC (colophon: "…the description of the tales of dreams") continues the
nagaris' "morning": it opens with pada 8.1 (raga vibhasa, not taken into GPT) — at dawn the friends gather, laughing, and each without shame tells her dream;
GPT nos. 126–144 are these tales. GCC readings: "vasiyā āchilum̐", "gorā rūpa-guṇa", "śarada śaśī" (4.4), "bhuvana-mohana-phānda", "vadana-chānda" (4.5),
"mākhālum̐ gaura gāya" (4.11). B. Nagari-bhava — see the introductory note to the section (portion 27).
[^10]: The townswoman's dream (4.11–4.12): the embrace (āliṅgana) is a direct motif of sambhoga; translated exactly, adding nothing. The dream is her imagination:
the Lord in His lila did not touch women and in Navadvipa did not look at them (CB, Adi; introductory note, portion 27), and the Gaudiya tradition does not
accept the worship of Gaura as a nagara ("gaura-nagari" is counted by Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati among the apasampradayas). Majumdar regards the long GPT padas
signed Narahari as late, not Sarakara's; these padas indeed belong to Narahari Chakravarti (18th c.). The signature merges with the townswoman's speech:
"the life of Narahari" (narahari-prāṇa) is an epithet of Gaura (cf. "Narahari's Lord"). Srivasa's house is the place of the Lord's night kirtanas (CB, Madhya;
there the doors were barred and outsiders not admitted) — here only the backdrop of the tale.

## 5. Narahari — "śuna śuna ohe parāṇa sajani…" (GPT1 pp. 210–211, no. 127; GCC 8.3)
*Nagari-bhava; a dream. A townswoman tells her dream: adorned, she sat in a kunja, pining; suddenly at the entrance there appeared a youth, a
kishora the colour of a rain-cloud — with a peacock feather on His crown, a flute at His lips, a pearl necklace; He came near, jested, beckoned
her with His glance; and as she watched, the Dark One became Gora; He sat beside her, she turned away in feigned anger, He begged forgiveness —
and the dream ends with their meeting. Raga "yatharaga". **Attribution: probably Chakravarti** — GCC, kirana 8, pada 3.*[^11]

**5.1.** "Listen, listen, friend of my heart, to you I will tell it: a dream such as tonight's I have never seen.

**5.2.** I was sitting in a kunja bower, adorned in every way; thinking and thinking, my body had wasted away, there was not a trace of joy in it.

**5.3.** With restless eyes I looked in every direction — I do not know what had come over me; and suddenly I saw: someone had come to the outer door.

**5.4.** What wondrous youth — a kishora, as if rasa itself in bodily form! What shall I say of the majesty of that nagara? His body is the colour of a rain-cloud.

**5.5.** His palms and soles outshine the dawn, His nails are a cluster of moons; the curls and the tilaka on His brow are lovely — like a snare enchanting the world.

**5.6.** His crown is gracefully tilted, a peerless peacock feather flutters; to the fragrance of its fine flowers bees come swarming — there is no counting them.

**5.7.** Holding the flute to His lower lip, He plays it again and again: who in the world, hearing it, can keep composure?

**5.8.** On His neck — a double string of pearls, like the streams of the Suradhuni; as He walks, the belt of bells on His hips tinkles, and lovely anklets on His feet.

**5.9.** With a graceful play of His brows He came up to me — and in how many ways He jested: who could understand it?

**5.10.** Smiling, looking at me, He signals with the corners of His eyes; with a trick He lifts away my veil — what is He doing! — He draws my very life to Himself.

**5.11.** And another wonder: as I watched, that Dark One became Gora. With what shall I compare Him — the thief of the life of so many chaste young wives?

**5.12.** Slowly, slowly He came near and sat beside me; with sweet, sweet words He soothes me, longing to touch.

**5.13.** In feigned anger I turned my face away — yet my joy had no bounds; 'Forgive My offence,' He said, and again caught the end of my sari.

**5.14.** At the touch of His body I grew helpless and was lost in Him." The Lord of Narahari's life, the great Connoisseur of rasa, did what was in His heart.[^12]

## 6. Narahari — "ājuka rajanī sukhamaya svapana…" (GPT1 pp. 211–212, no. 128; GCC 8.4)
*Nagari-bhava; a dream. A townswoman tells her friends a dream about themselves: adorned, they sat together and sang of Gaura half the night,
then sent a messenger to Gauranga; hearing of Him, they ran to the kunja; He seated them, jested, wounded them with His glance, embraced them. The
poet: why be ashamed to tell of yourself? Raga "yatharaga". **Attribution: probably Chakravarti** — GCC, kirana 8, pada 4.*[^13]

**6.1.** "Tonight I saw a blissful dream, friends: you are the most fortunate in the world; listen, I will tell it.

**6.2.** Each adorned in her own way, eager, you sat together in a secluded place; singing and singing songs of Gora's virtues, you passed a watch of the night.

**6.3.** Choosing the moment, you sent a messenger to where Govinda was; she went very quickly and told Gauranga everything.[^14]

**6.4.** Just as quickly she came back to you, herself agitated; hearing from her lips the news of the Beloved of your life, you all ran.

**6.5.** From afar, seeing the sweetness of Gora's form, you were enchanted; entering the kunja bower, you came up to the Lord of your life.

**6.6.** With great tenderness He seated you, taking each by the hand; smiling, how many jests He made, touching you!

**6.7.** Most clever Gora shot a fearful arrow from the corners of His eyes; by it you were overcome and could not keep the pride of youth.

**6.8.** The serpents of all your brows bit Him again and again — and the composure the Moon of Nadia had seemed at once to leave Him.

**6.9.** Opening both arms, He embraces — peerless is His love; in great joy I made a sign — and, understanding it, He grew still more intoxicated.

**6.10.** The desire that was in your hearts the King of connoisseurs of rasa fulfilled." Narahari says: why are you ashamed to tell of yourself?[^15]

p. 94; scan PDF p. 91), "anyā ca — yathārāga". GCC readings: "sukhamaya saba svapana" (6.1), "gorā-guṇagaṇa … gom̐āilā" (6.2), "karilā paraspare" — "jested with
one another" (6.6), "tā yena takhani gela" (6.8; GPT1 and GPT2 "mena"), "tomādera mane ye chila tā yena karila rasikarāja" (6.10; the OCR is uncertain). B.
Nagari-bhava — see the introductory note to the section (portion 27).
[^14]: "To where Govinda was" (govinda āchena yathā): the messenger speaks with Gauranga — Govinda here is apparently Krishna's name given to Gaura (cf. "the Moon
of Gokula", portion 29); another understanding is also possible — the house of Govinda (Ghosha or another companion) where the Lord was. The messenger (dūtī)

guise of a dream about her friends the teller is telling her own.

## 7. Narahari — "śuna śuna sai svapane dekhinu nikuñjakānane gorā…" (GPT1 p. 212, no. 129; GCC 8.5)
*Nagari-bhava; a dream. A friend tells the heroine her dream: Gora in a kunja grove was gazing at her path and weeping; learning from the friend
that she would come, He begged her to hasten; the heroine, adorned, came, and the King of connoisseurs of rasa embraced her; then on some
pretext she became manini — and He entreats her to relent; at this the teller woke. Raga "yatharaga". **Attribution: probably Chakravarti** —
GCC, kirana 8, pada 5.*[^16]

**7.1.** "Listen, listen, friend: in a dream I saw Gora in a kunja grove — gazing at your path, in anguish He was shedding tears.

**7.2.** Hearing from me that you were coming, how He pleaded with me! Seeing His state, I came in haste and told you.
