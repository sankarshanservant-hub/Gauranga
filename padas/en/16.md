# Yadu: Two Gaura-padas from the *Padakalpataru* Found in the Full Check of PK

*This chapter is the result of a full check of all four volumes of the *Padakalpataru* (PK) by the signatures of the poets
contemporary with Mahaprabhu on Majumdar's list. Only three
gaura-padas turned out to be unknown to the catalogue and to translations 01–15, all signed "Yadu" (Yadunatha): two are translated
here, the third (PK 703) is in nagari-bhava and is deferred to the section of nagari-padas. The other results of the check are PK
numbers for padas previously known only from the *Gaura-pada-tarangini* (GPT), the *Bhakti-ratnakara* or the
*Kshanada-gita-chintamani* and already translated (YD-003 = PK 2512, YD-004 = PK 1976, AN-007 = PK 2328, AN-008 = PK 2337, BD-028 =
PK 2301, BD-041 = PK 635, KR-004 = PK 1464, VG-151 = PK 2668), and a correction: Balarama's pada "saba abatāra sāra…" (chapter 13,
no. 8) is PK 2201, bearing his signature in PK itself, not Lochana Dasa's pada PK 2203; the notes of those chapters have been
supplemented. Both padas of this chapter stand in the 20th pallava of PK ("Sri Gaurachandra — other descriptions") and are also in
GPT1 (p. 37, nos. 33–34): praise of the Gaura-avatara, before whom all living beings weep, and the love of Gaura and Gadadhara. The
text follows PK with the GPT variants; the Bengali original and the word-for-word translation are in the full version of the book.*

## 1. Yadu — "jalera jība kānde dekhiyā pratibimba…" (PK 2209; GPT1 p. 37, no. 34)
*Praise of the Gaura-avatara: at the sight of Gorachand all living beings weep — the creatures of the water seeing His reflection,
the beasts and birds in the forests; trees thrill, stones melt, the blind weep aloud hearing of Him, the wives of noble families
weep, the deaf and the helpless, infants leaving the breast; there will be no such incarnation again — He saved all, only the poet
is left out. Raga mangala.*[^1]

**1.1.** The creatures of the water weep, seeing His reflection; in the forests the beasts and birds weep;
the trees thrill, the stones melt, the blind, hearing of Him, weep and cry aloud.

**1.2.** *(Refrain)* Wondrous is the body of Gorachand!
Boundless is what one feels: how can one tell it with a single mouth? What is in the heart does not come to the lips.

**1.3.** The wives of noble families — even they weep aloud; the deaf and the helpless weep in bewilderment;
the infant, leaving its mother's breast, weeps — no one knows why.[^2]

**1.4.** There will be no such incarnation again: He is an ocean of nothing but mercy.
The fallen, the foolish, the inanimate and the animate — He saved them all; only Yadu was left out.[^3]

## 2. Yadu — "gaura gadādhara duhum̐ tanu sundara…" (PK 2182; GPT1 p. 37, no. 33)
*Gaura and Gadadhara: both are beautiful and wondrously spread prema; when, rejoicing, they touch each other, nectar flows; hand in
hand, eye to eye — a sweetness beyond understanding; who, plunging into these feelings, will reach the bottom? Seeing them, people
forget body and mind. Raga kedara.*[^4]

**2.1.** Gaura and Gadadhara — both are beautiful, wondrously spreading prema;
when, rejoicing, they touch each other in their play, nectar pours without end.[^5]

**2.2.** *(Refrain)* Behold, behold: wondrous is the love of these two!
Who, plunging into these feelings, into this art full of prema, will reach the bottom?

**2.3.** Hand in hand, eye to eye — the sweetness that is between them, how could I understand it?
Seeing their wondrous beauty, the body thrills: there is none like it in all the worlds.

**2.4.** Are they dolls of nectar, or embodiments of rasa, or is it prema itself that has taken two forms?
Seeing them, the people of the world forget body and mind — will Yadu find a limit to it?[^6]

[^1]: PK — vol. 3, 20th pallava ("Śrī-Gauracandra — prakārāntara"), label "॥২৬॥২২০৯॥", raga mangala, MS P-R-Sa no. 2317 (MS P-R — 9.21),
checked against the scan (PDF p. 272, printed p. 261); GPT1 — p. 37, section of praise of the Gaura-avatara, no. 34 (mangala), checked
against the scan (PDF p. 296); GPT2 — sec. 1.3, no. 34 (p. 26). The pada is not in the *Bhakti-ratnakara* (BR), the
*Kshanada-gita-chintamani* (KGC) or the *Padamrita-samudra* (PS). It was missing from the catalogue (new row YD-019): it was found in
the full check of PK (the log of chapter 8 mentions it in passing — "PK 2209 — Yadu" — but it never entered the catalogue; the earlier
checks of GPT1 signatures missed it because the signature "yadu" is garbled in the OCR). The signature "yadu": Yadunatha Dasa
(Kavichandra), whose padas stand nearby in the same pallava (PK 2180, 2191; chapters 6 and 13), or Yadunandana — not established; C.
[^2]: They weep not from grief but from the prema that the sight of the Lord awakens — so the stones melt and the trees thrill (cf. CC
Madhya 17: in the forests of Jharikhanda beasts and birds danced and chanted the name together with the Lord). The wives of noble
families here are among all living beings: this is not the motif of townswomen captivated by Gaura's beauty (nagari-bhava) but a
picture of universal prema; cf. the wives of noble families dancing in sankirtana in Balarama's pada (PK 2207, chapter 10, no. 2).
"The helpless" (jaṛa) — the dull, unable to move or understand; MS P-R: "the dumb and the deaf" (mūka badhira).
[^3]: "The inanimate and the animate" (jaṛa ajaṛa) — the unmoving (the trees and stones of 1.1) and the moving: the Lord's mercy embraces all
that exists. "Only Yadu was left out" is the usual formula of humility in signatures (cf. Balarama in PK 2201 and 2207, chapters 13 and
10): the poet speaks of himself. MS P-R-Sa: "the fallen, the foolish people" (jana for jaṛa) and "the Deliverer" (uddhāraṇa for uddhārila).
[^4]: PK — vol. 3, 20th pallava, label "॥১৫॥২১৮২॥", raga kedara, MS P-R-Sa no. 2290, checked against the scan (PDF pp. 261–262, printed
pp. 250–251); GPT1 — p. 37, section of praise of the Gaura-avatara, no. 33 (kedara), checked against the scan (PDF p. 296); GPT2 — sec.
1.3, no. 33 (pp. 25–26). The pada is not in BR, KGC or PS; it was missing from the catalogue (new row YD-018) — found in the full check of
PK. In PK it immediately follows Nayanananda's pada on Gaura and Gadadhara (PK 2181) and stands one after Yadunatha's pada
"gaura-baraṇa tanu…" (PK 2180, chapter 13, no. 6), with which it shares theme and phrasing. The signature "yadu" is the same poet as in
no. 1 (see note 1); C.
[^5]: Gadadhara Pandita, according to Kavi Karnapura's *Gaura-ganoddesha-dipika* (vv. 147–153), is Sri Radha appearing in the Lord's
lila; his whole life is service to Gaura. The intimacy of Gaura and Gadadhara is the Lord's lila with the embodiment of His internal
energy: it is pure prema with nothing mundane in it, and therefore the poet sees in them "prema itself that has taken two forms" (2.4).
The worship of Gaura together with Gadadhara (Gaura-Gadadhara) is accepted in Gadadhara's line; the pada has nothing to do with
"gaura-nagari" (Gaura as the nagara among the women of Nadia).
[^6]: GPT1 and GPT2 read in the refrain (2.2) "who, immersed [in it], will obtain it" (majiyā pāoba seha) instead of "will reach the bottom"
(nimajiyā pāoba theha PK). "Find a limit" (pāoba pāra) — "reach the shore": admiration, not complaint; cf. the humble signature of the same poet in
no. 1.
