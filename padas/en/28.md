# Nagari Padas — II: Vasu Ghosha (padas from GPT), Nayanananda, Yadu, Murari, Balarama Dasa, Chandrashekhara, Lochana Dasa

*On what nagari-bhava is, the position of the tradition (Vrindavana Dasa, Krishnadasa Kaviraja, Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati) and the rules of translation, see the introductory note to the section of nagari padas in portion 27 (`en/27.md`).*

## 1. Vasu Ghosha — "ki herinu ogo sai vidagadha-rāja…" (KGC 5.1; GPT1 p. 162, no. 16)
*Nagari-bhava (only in the address to a friend). "What have I seen, friend — the king of those versed in rasa!" Gaura is the
wish-fulfilling tree of the devotees in the midst of Navadvipa: its branches are love, its leaves anuraga, its flowers longing,
its fruit pure prema, from which rasa flows without end; the devotees, like birds and bees, taste it, the tree's shade nourishes
all — only the poet is deprived through his own fault. In KGC it is the gaura-chandrika to the 5th kshanada. Raga suhai-deshag.*[^1]

**1.1.** What have I seen, friend! — the king of those versed in rasa,
the wish-fulfilling tree of the devotees in the midst of Navadvipa.[^2]

**1.2.** Its branches are love, its leaves are anuraga,
its flowers are longing, to enchant the world.

**1.3.** At all times the pure fruit of prema ripens on it;
from a single fruit ever-fresh rasa flows without end.[^3]

**1.4.** The devotees — chatakas, cuckoos, parrots, bees, swans —
ceaselessly delight in it, praising its rasa.

**1.5.** The unmoving and the moving, gods and men, are nourished by its shade —
only Vasudeva is deprived through the fault of his own deeds.[^4]

[^1]: Signature: "Vasudeva." KGC (Vishvanatha Chakravarti, late 17th c.; ed. 1925) — 5th kshanada ("krishna-panchami"), no. 1,
"শ্রীগৌরচন্দ্রস্য" (gaura-chandrika), raga suhai-deshag, p. 34 (scan PDF p. 66). GPT1 — p. 162, no. 16 (raga suhai or desharaga;
scan PDF p. 421); GPT2 — sect. 3.2, no. 16 (catalogue: VG-020). The pada is not in PK. The catalogue listed it as "GPT only";
the KGC witness was found in preparing this portion (attribution to Vasu by KGC and GPT, A). In GPT the pada stands in the section
of nagari padas (3.2), but the nagari motif in it is only the address "friend": in substance it praises Gaura in the image of the
wish-fulfilling tree. On nagari-bhava see the introductory note to the section (portion 27).
[^2]: "The king of those versed in rasa" (বিদগধ-রাজ): a vidagdha is a connoisseur, finely sensitive to rasa; so Krishna is
called. "Wish-fulfilling tree" (কল্পতরু) — an image known from CC (Adi 9: the Lord is both the gardener and the tree of prema itself,
giving its fruits to all). GPT1 notes a variant of the first line: "What shall I say, friend, of a wondrous thing!"
[^3]: "Ever-fresh rasa" (নব রস) can also be understood as "ninefold rasa" (all rasas in one fruit). "Longing" (আরতি) is eager
yearning, not the arati rite.
[^4]: Self-abasement in the signature is a usual device of Vaishnava poets (cf. "Vasudeva is deprived" in Vasu's padas — portion 27).
Instead of this couplet GPT1 gives a variant: "the bee-devotees seek the wish-fulfilling tree [of His feet]; says Vasu: wondrous is
this on earth"; instead of 1.4b — "many bhavas arose, but the hope was not fulfilled." KGC: "its shade nourishes" (পোষে), GPT:
"enter its shade" (পৈসে).

## 2. Vasu Ghosha — "āju mui ki dekhilum̐ gorā naṭarāya…" (GPT1 p. 164, no. 23; GCd, no. 12)
*Nagari-bhava. "What have I seen today — Gora, the king of dancers!" The Creator made Him, putting in so much rasa; His body
surpasses gold, His face a hundred moons, His eyes steal the hearts of women; on the bank of the Suradhuni Gorachand shines.
Raga dhanashi.*[^5]

**2.1.** What have I seen today — Gora, the king of dancers!
Gora's boundless glory cannot be told.

**2.2.** How did the Creator fashion Him, putting in so much rasa!
Gora's body ripples with radiance, surpassing gold.

**2.3.** His lotus face is lovelier than hundreds of moons;
the pair of His eyes steals the hearts of women.[^6]

**2.4.** Says Vasudeva Ghosha, lost in rapture:
on the bank of the Suradhuni Gorachand shines.

[^5]: Signature: "Vasudeva Ghosha." GPT1 — p. 164, no. 23 (raga dhanashi; scan PDF p. 423); GPT2 — sect. 3.2 (in the same series,
after VG-025). The catalogue had no row for it (**new row**: VG-184). Witness: the *Gita-chandrodaya* of Narahari Chakravarti (GCd;
ed. 1948, p. 72, gaura-chandrikas to Radha's purvaraga, no. 12, raga suhai) — with the same signature; there it stands among
Vasu's nagari padas known from PK (PK 2169, 28, 2172, 899, 777 — portion 27, nos. 9, 15, 11, 17, 16). The pada is not in PK or KGC; attribution to
Vasu by GPT and GCd (B). On nagari-bhava see the introductory note to the section (portion 27).
[^6]: "Steal the hearts of women" is a formula of the poetics of beauty, as in the padas on Krishna (cf. portion 27, note 6). GCd:
"how many moons," "ripples" (ঢরঢর), in 2.4 — "Gora has lit up the bank of the Suradhuni."

## 3. Vasu Ghosha — "āju mui ki pekhalum̐ gaurāṅga sundara…" (GPT1 p. 164, no. 24)
*Nagari-bhava. "What have I seen today — the beautiful Gauranga: in the three worlds there is no such nagara!" Noble wives are
enchanted by His form, trees thrill hearing of His virtues, stones melt, beasts and birds weep, the townswomen cannot hold their
hearts steady, gods, siddhas and sages are in turmoil: Gora is Madana-mohana. Raga dhanashi.*[^7]

**3.1.** What have I seen today — the beautiful Gauranga!
In these three worlds there is no such nagara.[^8]

**3.2.** All the noble wives are enchanted, seeing His form;
trees and creepers thrill, hearing of His virtues.

**3.3.** Stones melt and flow, beasts and birds weep;
all the women of the town cannot hold their hearts steady.

**3.4.** The gods, siddhas and sages are in turmoil of heart.
Says Vasudeva: Gora is Madana-mohana.[^9]

[^7]: Signature: "Vasudeva." GPT1 — p. 164, no. 24 (raga dhanashi; scan PDF p. 423); GPT2 — sect. 3.2, no. 24 (catalogue: VG-182).
The pada is not in PK, KGC or GCd; attribution to Vasu by GPT only (C). It is close to Vasu's PK 2151 (portion 27, no. 1: "birds,
beasts and trees weep hearing of His virtues; noble wives cannot hold their hearts steady"), but it is a different text. On
nagari-bhava see the introductory note to the section (portion 27).
[^8]: "Nagara" (নাগর) — townsman, gallant, in poetry the beloved (introductory note); so a townswoman speaks of the young Gaura.
[^9]: Madana-mohana — "the enchanter of the god of love himself," a name of Krishna: the Lord's beauty conquers even Kamadeva.
"Cannot hold their hearts steady" (lit. "bind the breast") is a general formula of beauty (portion 27, note 2). GPT1: "নগরী" — a
misprint for "নাগরী" (GPT2).

## 4. Vasu Ghosha — "sajani lo gorārūpa janu kām̐cā sonā…" (GPT1 pp. 162–163, no. 19)
*Nagari-bhava. "Friend, Gora's form is like pure gold: seeing it, a woman cannot stay at home." His form has fixed itself in
her eyes, wherever she looks there is Gora; He seems familiar, yet she cannot make out who He is. The poet answers: "Townswoman,
that is the stealer of the gopis' hearts!" Raga suhai.*[^10]

**4.1.** Friend, Gora's form is like pure gold:
seeing it, a woman cannot stay at home.

**4.2.** Curved brows, slanting eyes — by His glance you know Him;
give your heart to that form, friend, and no family honour remains.[^11]

**4.3.** The form has fixed itself in my eyes — I cannot forget it:
wherever I look, I see only that Gora.

**4.4.** He seems familiar, so familiar, yet I cannot make out who He is.
Says Vasu: townswoman, that is the stealer of the gopis' hearts![^12]

[^10]: Signature: "Vasu." GPT1 — pp. 162–163, no. 19 (raga suhai; scan PDF pp. 421–422); GPT2 — sect. 3.2, no. 19 (catalogue:
VG-022). The pada is not in PK, KGC, PS or GCd; attribution to Vasu by GPT only (C). The language is late and colloquial
("লেগেছে," "চিনতে," "শুধুই"), which, like the absence of early witnesses, tells against Vasu's authorship (cf. Majumdar's argument
on the unreliability of GPT — introductory note to the section). On nagari-bhava see the introductory note to the section (portion 27).
[^11]: "No family honour remains" is a nagari motif (a woman ready to disregard her family's honour for Gaura), transferred from the
padas on Radha and Krishna; translated as in the original (introductory note, rule 2).
[^12]: The poet's answer reveals what the townswoman cannot grasp: Gora is Krishna Himself, "the stealer of the gopis' hearts," and
that is why He seems familiar to her. This is a tenet of gaura-tattva (Gaura is Krishna who has taken Radha's bhava and luster;
CC Adi 1.5); it does not make Gaura the hero of a love play in Navadvipa (introductory note). GPT2: "এ" ("this") instead of "ঐ".

## 5. Vasu Ghosha — "niravadhi gorārūpa dekhi…" (GPT1 p. 164, no. 25)
*Nagari-bhava. "Ceaselessly I see Gora's form — streams flow from my eyes": night and day the townswoman knows nothing, the jewel
of brahmanas has entered her inmost heart; not seeing Gorachand's face, she cannot hold on to life. Raga dhanashi.*[^13]

**5.1.** Ceaselessly I see Gora's form —
from both my eyes it streams like a waterfall.

**5.2.** What shall I say? Where is my remedy?
My life will not stay in me.

**5.3.** Night and day I know nothing:
the jewel of brahmanas has entered my inmost heart.[^14]

**5.4.** Not seeing Gorachand's face —
says Vasu — the breast breaks.

[^13]: Signature: "Vasu." GPT1 — p. 164, no. 25 (raga dhanashi; scan PDF p. 423); GPT2 — sect. 3.2, in the same series (the number
is unclear in the OCR). The catalogue had no row for it (**new row**: VG-185). The pada is not in PK, KGC or GCd; attribution to
Vasu by GPT only (C). On nagari-bhava see the introductory note to the section (portion 27).
[^14]: "The jewel of brahmanas" (দ্বিজমণি) — the young Gaura, best of brahmanas (cf. portion 27, note 8).

## 6. Vasu Ghosha — "dekhiyā āyalum̐ gorācām̐de…" (GPT1 p. 164, no. 26)
*Nagari-bhava. "I have seen Gorachand — and since then my soul weeps": not seeing His moon-face, the townswoman finds no rest,
household work falls from her hands; Gauranga's glory has no limit. Raga dhanashi.*[^15]

**6.1.** I came back having seen Gorachand —
and since then my soul weeps.

**6.2.** My heart is restless, finds no peace,
when I do not see that moon-face.

**6.3.** My mind will not stay on household work
when I do not see Gaura's deeds.

**6.4.** Matchless is Gauranga's glory:
Vasudeva finds no limit to it.[^16]

[^15]: Signature: "Vasudeva." GPT1 — p. 164, no. 26 (raga dhanashi; scan PDF p. 423); GPT2 — sect. 3.2, in the same series. The
catalogue had no row for it (**new row**: VG-186). The pada is not in PK, KGC or GCd; attribution to Vasu by GPT only (C). On
nagari-bhava see the introductory note to the section (portion 27).
[^16]: The last couplet passes from the townswoman's words to the poet's praise: Gaura's glory has no limit (cf. portion 27, no. 1:
"in the three worlds there is none who could set a limit to His beauty").

## 7. Vasu Ghosha — "so vahuvallabha gorā jagatera manacorā…" (GPT1 pp. 163–164, no. 22)
*Nagari-bhava. "Gora is beloved of many, He steals the hearts of all the world — why then should I want Him to be mine alone?
Yet whose heart can bear the sharing!" The townswoman gives family, honour, life and youth for the treasure that is Gora; without
Him she will enter the waters of the Suradhuni. Raga vibhasa.*[^17]

**7.1.** He is Gora, beloved of many, the stealer of all the world's hearts —
why then do I want Him to be mine alone?
But say, whose heart could give such a treasure to another?
The sharing is unbearable to see.[^18]

**7.2.** *(Refrain)* Friend, I will tell you my heart's secret:
not seeing Gauranga's face, I feel my breast breaking —
who has stolen the stealer of hearts?

**7.3.** Take my family, take my honour, take my virtue, take my life,
take my life and my youth —
give me the treasure, Gora, for whom I ceaselessly long:
He is my whole wealth.

**7.4.** Or else I will enter the waters of the Suradhuni and give up my life:
Gora is the life of my life.
Says Vasudeva Ghosha: that treasure is not given away —
every moment, every instant I am bereft of it.[^19]

[^17]: Signature: "Vasudeva Ghosha." GPT1 — pp. 163–164, no. 22 (raga vibhasa; scan PDF pp. 422–423); GPT2 — sect. 3.2, no. 22
(catalogue: VG-025). The pada is not in PK, KGC or GCd; attribution to Vasu by GPT only (C). On nagari-bhava see the introductory
note to the section (portion 27).
[^18]: "Beloved of many" (বহুবল্লভ) is an epithet of Krishna, the beloved of all the gopis; the jealousy of a heroine who will not
share her beloved is a motif of the padas on Radha, transferred to a townswoman of Nadia. That Gaura belongs to all (He gave prema to
the whole world) is true; but Gaura's love for the world is mercy, not the love play of a nagara (introductory note). GPT2: "You,
beloved of many, Gora" (তো).
[^19]: The signature line is ambiguous: "that treasure is not given away" — the poet confirms that Gora cannot be yielded; "every
moment I am bereft" (হই হারা) we take as unceasing separation (cf. portion 27, note 52); it can also be read "I lose myself."
"I will enter the waters of the Suradhuni," "take my family, honour…" are nagari formulas, translated as in the original.

## 8. Vasu Ghosha — "daṇḍe daṇḍe tile tile gorācām̐da nā dekhile…" (GPT1 p. 296, no. 14)
*Nagari-bhava. "If I do not see Gorachand every moment, I live as one dead": the townswoman wants to keep His golden form always in
her heart; not seeing Him for an instant, she loses her composure; she will set her family's honour adrift on the Suradhuni, burn
her shame and gaze her fill at Gauranga. Raga suhai.*[^20]

**8.1.** If every moment, every instant I do not see Gorachand,
I live as though dead at heart.
Ceaselessly I long to keep
His golden-lustred form within my heart.

**8.2.** I do not see Him for a moment — my ribs give way,
I cannot keep my composure.
With the brush of anuraga my heart is painted within and without —
I do not know how much I owe Him.[^21]

**8.3.** I will go to the waters of the Suradhuni and set my family adrift,
I will set fire to my shame;
I will place Gauranga before me and gaze my fill —
Vasu wants no other work.[^22]

[^20]: Signature: "Vasu." GPT1 — p. 296, no. 14 (raga suhai; scan PDF p. 555), in the section of gaura-chandrikas to purvaraga and
anuraga (4.4); GPT2 — sect. 4.4, no. 14 (catalogue: VG-040). Not in PK, KGC or GCd. According to the catalogue, in the late
collection *Padamrita-madhuri* (vol. 1, pp. 469–470; an edition under copyright, its text is not used) the same pada bears the
signature of Murari Gupta and has an extra couplet: the attribution is disputed (C). On nagari-bhava see the introductory note to
the section (portion 27).
[^21]: The line is obscure: "অনুরাগের তুলি দিয়ে অন্তর বাহির হিয়ে, না জানি তার কত ধার ধারি" — lit. "with the brush of anuraga —
the inside and outside of the heart; I do not know how much debt I hold to Him." We understand: love has coloured the whole heart,
and the townswoman does not know how to repay this gift. The translation is conjectural.
[^22]: "Set my family adrift," "set fire to my shame" are formulas of renouncing family honour for love (from the padas on Radha);
translated as in the original (introductory note, rule 2).

## 9. Vasu Ghosha — "āpana jāni vanāyalum̐ veśa…" (GPT1 p. 305, no. 18)
*Nagari-bhava (vasaka-sajja — "she who has prepared for the meeting"). The townswoman, regarding Gaura as her own, adorns herself
for His coming: she binds her hair, puts on a sandal tilaka, a pearl necklace, draws designs in musk on her breast; love for Gaura
cannot be told. Raga dhanashi.*[^23]

**9.1.** Regarding Him as my own, I dressed myself:
carefully I bound my hair in a loose knot.

**9.2.** I put a sandal tilaka on my forehead,
I put a pearl necklace round my neck.

**9.3.** I drew designs in musk on my breast,
I adorned myself all over, limb by limb.[^24]

**9.4.** Love for Gaura cannot be told:
Vasudeva Ghosha finds no end to this rasa.[^25]

[^23]: Signature: "Vasudeva Ghosha." GPT1 — p. 305, no. 18 (raga dhanashi; scan PDF p. 564), in the section of gaura-chandrikas to
abhisara, rasodgara and utkanthita (4.5), next to a pada on vasaka-sajja (no. 19); GPT2 — sect. 4.5, no. 18 (catalogue: VG-168). Not in PK, KGC or GCd; attribution to Vasu by GPT only (C). On
nagari-bhava see the introductory note to the section (portion 27).
[^24]: The adornment of the vasaka-sajja (the heroine who adorns herself for her beloved's coming) is a canon of the padas on
Radha; designs in musk on the breast (কুচ) are part of that canon. Translated exactly, with nothing added. The Vrajabuli forms
"দেয়ল," "চঢ়ায়ল," "কয়ল" can also be 3rd person ("He put…"); but the pada is framed by "I dressed myself… I adorned myself"
(বনায়লুঁ), and it speaks of the heroine's own adornment.
[^25]: Lit. "Gaura's love (লেহ) cannot be told"; we understand it as the townswoman's love for Gaura. Gaura Himself, according to CC,
is in the bhava of Radha, tasting Her love; the tradition does not accept depicting Him as the beloved of the townswomen
(introductory note).

## 10. Vasu Ghosha — "niśi-parabhāte vasi āṅgināte…" (GPT1 p. 163, no. 21)
*Nagari-bhava (khandita — "the deceived"). At daybreak the townswoman sits in the courtyard with a cheerless face: "Friend, who has
done this? Gora, the Creator's finest work — another has got Him!" She had prepared the bed, did not sleep all night, went out of
the door a hundred times; when Gauranga comes she will ask: in whose house did He spend the night? Raga vibhasa-dashakushi.*[^26]

**10.1.** At daybreak I sit in the courtyard with a cheerless face:
such conduct from Gaurangachand I never knew.

**10.2.** *(Refrain)* Friend, who has done this?
Gora, the treasury of virtues, the utmost of the Creator's work — she has got Him.[^27]

**10.3.** Sprinkling musk and sandal, stringing flower garlands,
I spread the bed on a splendid couch: here Shachi's boy will lie.

**10.4.** Ah, friend, the whole night I sat awake till dawn;
three times an instant, a hundred times a danda I went out of the house.

**10.5.** Says Vasu Ghosha: when Gauranga comes I will ask Him at once:
"You did not come here — where did You spend the night, in whose house were You?"[^28]

[^26]: Signature: "Vasu Ghosha." GPT1 — p. 163, no. 21 (raga vibhasa-dashakushi; scan PDF p. 422); GPT2 — sect. 3.2, no. 21
(catalogue: VG-024). The pada is not in PK, KGC or GCd; attribution to Vasu by GPT only (C). On nagari-bhava see the introductory
note to the section (portion 27).
[^27]: "The utmost of the Creator's work" (বিধির অবধি) — the most perfect thing the Creator could make (a poetic formula; Gaura is not
created — He is the Lord Himself). "She has got Him" — another woman: the khandita heroine suspects a rival.
[^28]: The pada is wholly transferred from the canon of padas on Radha as khandita: Krishna has spent the night with another gopi and
comes at dawn bearing the marks of the meeting. Such a plot is inapplicable to Gaura: Vrindavana Dasa says plainly that in Navadvipa
the Lord did not look at women (CB, Adi-khanda; introductory note) — which is why the townswoman waits for Him all night in vain.
Translated faithfully to the author, but this is a poetic fiction of the late layer of GPT, not a lila; the tradition does not accept
such a depiction of Gaura (introductory note). Cf. the night of vain waiting in Vasu's padas from PK (portion 27, nos. 19, 20).

## 11. Vasu Ghosha — "kena māna karinu lo sai…" (GPT1 p. 313, no. 20)
*Nagari-bhava (mana — offended pride and repentance). "Why did I take offence, friend? Where has Gora gone?" The townswoman herself
pushed Gaurahari away, and now reproaches her friends for not holding Him back: home and body are empty, Gaura has turned away. The
poet consoles her: the hope will be fulfilled. Raga suhai.*[^29]

**11.1.** Why did I take offence, friend?
Where has Gora, the treasury of virtues, gone?

**11.2.** If I have abandoned my beloved,
why then does my life not leave me?

**11.3.** I did abandon Gaurahari —
but why did you not hold Him back?

**11.4.** Now home and body are both empty:
Gaura has turned away.

**11.5.** Why now these vain sighs?
Says Vasu: the hope will be fulfilled.[^30]

[^29]: Signature: "Vasu." GPT1 — p. 313, no. 20 (raga suhai; scan PDF p. 572), in the section of gaura-chandrikas to khandita and
mana (4.6); GPT2 — sect. 4.6, no. 20 (catalogue: VG-169; the first line according to the scan is "কেন মান করিনু," not "করিস").
Not in PK, KGC or GCd; attribution to Vasu by GPT only (C). On nagari-bhava see the introductory note to the section (portion 27).
[^30]: The motif of the kalahantarita — the heroine who in offended pride (mana) has pushed her beloved away and at once repents — is
taken from the padas on Radha and Krishna. "Beloved" (বঁধুয়া) is a word of the same padas. "Gaura has turned away" (বৈমুখ) — as in
Vasu's padas from PK (portion 27, no. 20: "the king of dancers has turned away from me"), here what is unwittingly reflected is that
the Lord did not respond to such feelings (introductory note).

## 12. Vasu Ghosha — "śayana-mandire hāma śutiyā āchilā…" (GPT1 p. 203, no. 113)
*Nagari-bhava (a dream). "I was lying in my bedchamber, and tonight in a dream I saw Gauranga": since then her soul burns, Gora's
form stands before her day and night, her garment is soaked with tears, her body has lost its strength. Raga bhupala.*[^31]

**12.1.** I was lying in my bedchamber —
and tonight in a dream I saw Gauranga.

**12.2.** Since then my soul burns — listen, friend:
Gora's form comes to my mind day and night.

**12.3.** I keep crying "Gora, Gora" — what has become of my heart!
My garment is soaked with my tears.

**12.4.** My body is languid and limp, it will not hold up;
recalling this feeling for Gora, Vasu Ghosha sings.[^32]

[^31]: Signature: "Vasu Ghosha." GPT1 — p. 203, no. 113 (raga bhupala; scan PDF p. 462), among the padas on the townswomen's dreams
(nos. 111–115: Vasu's PK 249 and PK 724 — portion 27, nos. 23, 22; VG-027, VG-029 — nos. 13, 14 of this portion); GPT2 — sect. 3.2,
no. 113 (catalogue: VG-028). Not in PK, KGC or GCd; attribution to Vasu by GPT only (C). On nagari-bhava see the introductory note to
the section (portion 27).
[^32]: "Feeling for Gora" (গোরা-ভাব) can also be read as "Gora's bhava," i.e. the feeling He awakens. The verb forms "আছিলা,"
"দেখিলা" are Old Bengali first-person forms ("I was lying," "I saw").
