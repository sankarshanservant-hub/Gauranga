# Praise of Gaura, the Pastimes of Navadvipa, Separation, and Nityananda in Gauda

*Padas of contemporaries which Majumdar (*Sri-Chaitanya-chariter upadan*, ch. 2) assigns to the Lord's own time, in the
order of the lilas: praise of Gaura (Paramananda); a kirtana festival in Advaita's house (Parameshvara Dasa); the Lord's
pastimes with Gadadhara and His companions in Navadvipa, which Narahari Chakravarti cites in the *Bhakti-ratnakara* as the
testimony of the eyewitness Vasu Ghosha (the battle of flowers, the game of dice, the water play, the rasa, the spring
festival of colours); the Lord's ecstatic moods (Shivananda, Chaitanyadasa); the separation of Navadvipa after the
sannyasa (Paramananda, Vasu Ghosha, Chaitanyadasa, Madhava Ghosha); and Nityananda in Gauda (Kanu Thakura). The text
follows the *Padakalpataru* (PK), the *Bhakti-ratnakara* (BR), the *Kshanada-gita-chintamani* (KGC) and the
*Gaura-pada-tarangini* (GPT1 — 1st ed., 1903; GPT2 — 2nd ed., 1934); the Bengali original, variants and word-for-word
translation are in the full version of the book.*

## 1. Paramananda (Gupta) — "gorā-abatāre yāra nā haila bhakati-sāra…" (PK 2202)
*Lila: praise of Gaura — a call to worship the feet of Gorachand, the Purifier of the fallen. In PK the pada stands in the
20th pallava among padas on Gaura's mercy; in GPT, among the mangalacharanas (songs of blessing that open a kirtana).
According to Majumdar it is one of the padas signed "Paramananda" which he ascribes to Paramananda Gupta, the Lord's
companion in Navadvipa — perhaps a fragment of his lost *Gauranga-vijaya*. Raga kamoda.*[^1]

**1.1.** One in whom the essence of devotion did not awaken in the descent of Gora —
for him I see no other means:
one whose eyes did not brighten in the rays of the sun
has been deprived by the Creator.[^2]

**1.2.** *(Refrain)* Worship the feet of Gorachand!
In the three worlds, brothers, there is no such Lord of mercy:
Gora is the great Purifier of the fallen.

**1.3.** Is He a golden cloud, or a lake of prema —
the descent, an ocean of mercy!
Whoever, having found such a descent, is not cooled —
I do not know what kind of heart he has.

**1.4.** To cross the ocean of worldly existence, making the mantra of Hari's name a raft,
Gauranga Himself ferries [all] across.
If anyone still drowns and perishes — who will save him?
This is Paramananda's plea.[^3]

[^1]: Signature: "Paramananda". PK: vol. 3, PDF p. 269 (printed p. 258), 20th pallava ("Sri Gaurachandra — another
mode"), no. 19; MS P-R-Sa no. 2310. GPT1 — p. 16, no. 40 (raga kamoda); GPT2 — sect. 1.2, no. 40. Majumdar divides the
twelve PK padas signed "Paramananda" into Vrajabuli padas of a later poet of Vrindavana and Bengali padas which he ascribes
to Paramananda Gupta — the Lord's companion in whose house Nityananda stayed (CB); he regards PK 2202, 2119, 672 and 1693
as possibly fragments of his lost *Gauranga-vijaya-gita*, mentioned by Jayananda. The attribution is conjectural (S. Ray
assigned these padas to Karnapura, Paramananda Sena).
[^2]: "Essence of devotion" (bhakati-sāra); MSS "ka", "kha", "cha" and GPT: "the taste of devotion" (bhakati-rasa). The image: Gaura
is the sun; whoever stays blind at His rising can be helped by nothing else.
[^3]: "Plea" (parihāra) — the poet's humble entreaty. The raft of the holy name is an image of the nama-sankirtana which the
Lord brought (CC Adi 3, 7).

## 2. Paramananda (Gupta) — "paraśa-maṇira sane ki diba tulanā re…" (PK 672)
*Lila: praise of Gaura: neither the touchstone, nor the moon, nor the wish-fulfilling tree can be compared with Him — He
gave away prema without waiting to be asked. In PK the pada is the gaura-chandrika to the section "rasodgara-anuraga"
(Radha recalls Her meeting with Krishna). According to Majumdar — possibly from Paramananda Gupta's *Gauranga-vijaya*.
Raga vibhasa.*[^4]

**2.1.** What can be compared with the touchstone? —
at its touch [iron] turns to gold;
but by the virtues of my Gauranga, dancing and singing,
how many people have become jewels![^5]

**2.2.** *(Refrain)* The son of Shachi, wearing the forest garland,
He to whom nothing in the three worlds can be compared —
Gora, the darling of my life.[^6]

**2.3.** Beside the form of the moon Gauranga that moon is spotted:
it cannot shine like this.
A spotless full moon has risen in the town of Nadia —
and the darkness of the heart has gone far away.

**2.4.** In this virtue neither [the cow] Surabhi nor the tree of the gods equals Him:
from them only one who asks receives;
but He, unasked, filled the whole world —
to each person, entreating, He gave the wealth of prema.[^7]

**2.5.** Gorachand can be compared only with the Lord Gorachand Himself —
consider, all of you, and you will see.
In Paramananda's heart there is a great longing:
when will Gauranga's mercy come?

[^4]: Signature: "Paramananda". PK: vol. 2, PDF pp. 61–62, 6th pallava, no. 9 ("Then — rasodgara-anuraga. Sri
Gaurachandra"); MSS P-R-Sa no. 1147, P-R 22.27. GPT1 — p. 138, no. 78 (raga vibhasa). In MS P-R the stanzas stand in
another order, and the last runs differently and is signed with the name of Nayanananda: "Gaurangachand cannot be
compared — consider in your hearts and see; in Nayanananda's heart there is great joy: ponder Gora's virtues" (see VAR).
The attribution to Paramananda (Ray's index, Majumdar) is therefore not wholly secure.
[^5]: The touchstone (parasha-mani) only turns iron into gold, but the songs of Gauranga's virtues have made "jewels" of
many people. MS P-R adds before this stanza: "How can I forget the lilas of Gaurangachand! At my Gauranga's virtues beasts
and birds weep, stones melt and crumble."
[^6]: "Darling of my life" (parāṇa-putalī, lit. "the little doll of my life") — what is dearest of all. MS P-R lacks the refrain.
[^7]: Surabhi is the wish-fulfilling cow; the tree of the gods (kalpa-vriksha) is the wish-fulfilling tree: they give only
what is asked of them. Cf. CC Adi 7 (the Lord gives prema to all without discrimination). MS P-R: "unasked, He Himself
gave".

## 3. Paramananda (Gupta) — "gorā mora dayāra abadhi guṇanidhi…" (PK 2119)
*Lila: Navadvipa — Gauranga roams the bank of the Ganga leaning on the shoulders of devotees; He weeps, repeating
"Haribol"; His body is covered with thrills, and the earth cannot hold Him. In PK — in the 18th pallava (the form of Sri
Gauranga); in KGC — the first pada of the 14th kshanada. According to Majumdar — possibly from Paramananda Gupta's
*Gauranga-vijaya*. Raga shri.*[^8]

**3.1.** My Gora is the limit of mercy, the treasury of virtues:
on the bank of the Suradhuni, in the town of Nadia,
Gauranga sports ceaselessly.

**3.2.** Placing both arms on the shoulders of devotees,
Gora cannot walk — He weeps, repeating "Haribol".

**3.3.** Both eyes brim with tears of prema —
how many rivers flow in streams!
His whole body is covered with thrills —
the earth cannot hold Him.

**3.4.** His associates go with Him ceaselessly,
crying, "Hari, Hari!"
Placing both arms on His friends' shoulders,
He walks, swaying and rocking.

**3.5.** Filling the world, He poured out prema —
His name is "Purifier of the fallen".
Hearing this, Paramananda has taken hope:
his heart accepts nothing else.[^9]

[^8]: Signature: "Paramananda". PK: vol. 3, PDF p. 237 (printed p. 226), 18th pallava, no. 11; MS P-R-Sa no. 2227. In PK
the pada stands immediately before Paramananda's pada "gorā-tanu dhūlāya loṭāya" (PK 2120; chapter 1, no. 7). GPT1 — p. 279,
no. 6 (raga shri; the first line is the refrain); GPT2 — twice (sect. 1.3, no. 16; 4.3, no. 6). KGC — 14th kshanada, no. 1
(pp. 106–107; raga dhanashi) — with minor variants: "Gora is the limit of mercy" (without "my"), "Gorachand cannot walk —
He weeps, repeating 'Hari, Hari'". Cf. CB Madhya 23 (the Lord with His devotees in kirtana along the bank of the Ganga).
[^9]: "Poured out" (ubhārila; KGC ubhārala) — lit. "made overflow". MSS "kha", "gha", P-R-Sa read in 3.4 "hilite" ("swaying").

## 4. Parameshvara Dasa — "eka dina pahum̐ hāsi advaita-mandire āsi…" (PK 23)
*Lila: the Lord comes to Advaita's house and with Nityananda and Advaita discusses a great festival; Sita Thakurani speaks
sweet words; the Lord orders that all singers and musicians be invited, that a full water-pot, banana trees and garlands
be set up, and that in the evening the "khol-mangala" be performed — the blessing of the mridanga before the kirtana. In PK
— the first pada of the section "Adhivasa of the sankirtana". According to Majumdar (following S. Ray) — an eyewitness
description. Raga dhanashi.*[^10]

**4.1.** One day the Lord, smiling, came to Advaita's house —
and the son of Shachi sat down;
with Nityananda, and with Advaita joyfully seated beside Him,
He deliberated on a great festival.

**4.2.** Hearing of this, laughing with joy, Sita Thakurani came
and spoke sweet words.
Hearing them, joyfully, about the arrangement of the festival
the son of Shachi says:

**4.3.** "Listen, mistress Sita: let us bring Vaishnavas here,
inviting them with care;
whoever sings, whoever plays — let us invite them all,
each one separately."

**4.4.** Saying this, Gora Raya commanded everyone:
"Invite the Vaishnavas;
take the khol and karatalas, anoint them with aloe-wood and sandal,
set up the full water-pot;

**4.5.** plant banana trees, tie flower garlands to them,
joyfully prepare the circle for kirtana;
with garlands, sandal, areca nuts, ghee, honey and curd,
in the evening perform the khol-mangala."[^11]

**4.6.** Hearing the Lord's words, lovingly they did all as instructed,
with various offerings and fragrances.
All cry "Hari, Hari!" and perform the khol-mangala —
so, full of rasa, sings Parameshvara Dasa.[^12]

[^10]: Signature: "Parameshvara Dasa". PK: vol. 1, PDF p. 31 (printed p. 24), 1st shakha, no. 23, under the heading "Then
— the adhivasa of the sankirtana"; MS P-R-Sa no. 23. GPT1 — p. 246, no. 26 (raga dhanashi), signed "Parameshvari Dasa";
after it in GPT1 (no. 27) comes another pada with the same signature, on the "seven groups" of the kirtana with fourteen
mridangas. Parameshvara Dasa was a companion of Nityananda (CC Adi 11; CB, Antya-khanda), and later of Jahnava-devi.
Majumdar, following S. Ray, regards the pada as an eyewitness description. Where this house of Advaita was — in Navadvipa
or in Shantipura — the pada does not say.
[^11]: The full water-pot (purna-ghata), banana trees and garlands are the usual adornment of a festival ground. The khol is
the Bengali clay mridanga; the "khol-mangala" is the rite of blessing the mridanga before a kirtana: it is anointed with
sandal, garlanded, and auspicious substances are offered to it. MSS "ga", "gha", "cha", P-R-Sa read in 4.3: "whoever plays
[instruments]" (ye bājāya).
[^12]: MS "ga" and GPT1 read in 4.1: "…seated in Advaita's house, the son of Shachi said". GPT1 ends: "Parameshvari Dasa is
immersed in rasa" (rase bhāse) instead of "sings" (lit. "tells", bhāṣe).

## 5. Vasu Ghosha — "phulabana gorācām̐da dekhiyā nayane…" (PK 1525; BR, p. 936)
*Lila: Navadvipa, 1509 — seeing a flowering grove, the Lord remembers the battle of flowers; crying "Jaya!", His
companions shower Him with flowers; Gadadhara and Nityananda are with Him. In the *Bhakti-ratnakara* the guide shows
Srinivasa Acharya this flower garden in Navadvipa and quotes the pada as testimony; in PK it is the gaura-chandrika to
the section "Phula-dola" (the spring play with flowers). According to Majumdar — seen with the poet's own eyes (1509).
Raga tudi (in BR, kamoda).*[^13]

**5.1.** Seeing with His eyes the flowering grove,
Gorachand remembered the battle of flowers.[^14]

**5.2.** Again and again crying "Jaya, jaya!", the associates
one after another throw flowers at Gora.

**5.3.** With Him are the dear Gadadhara and Nityananda;
in the battle of flowers Gora was filled with joy.

**5.4.** With Gadadhara the Lord sports —
this Vasudeva Ghosha has told.[^15]

[^13]: Signature: "Vasudeva Ghosha". PK: vol. 2, PDF p. 446, no. 101, under the heading "Then — phula-dola. Here — Sri
Mahaprabhu"; MS P-R-Sa no. 2070. BR, wave 12 (ed. 1912/1913, p. 936; ed. 1960, vv. 3224–3229): before the pada Narahari
Chakravarti writes: "One day, looking at this flower garden, [the Lord] says with a smile: 'A battle of flowers would be
good!' — and His dear companions, taking bunches of flowers, fight with flowers while all look on." GPT1 — p. 342, no. 69
(raga tudi); GPT2 — sect. 5.1, no. 69. Majumdar calls this and the following padas of BR (pp. 936–943) — the battle of
flowers, the game of dice, the water play, the holi with Gadadhara — scenes the poet saw himself; they are confirmed by
Shivananda Sena's holi pada (chapter 1, no. 14), where Vasu is named among the dancers. In the stage-1 catalogue the pada
was wrongly linked with PK 1186 (that is Vasu Ghosha's goshtha pada — another recension of the pada in chapter 1, no. 11).
[^14]: BR: "Seeing with His eyes an army of flowers, Gora said: 'A battle of flowers!'" (phula bala … balila bacane). In Navadvipa
the Lord was often absorbed in the moods of Krishna-lila (cf. chapter 1, nos. 9–13).
[^15]: Here and in the following padas Gadadhara is the chief partner in the Lord's play. In Gaudiya Vaishnava theology
Gadadhara Pandita is a manifestation of Sri Radha (*Gaura-ganoddesha-dipika* 147–153), and these pastimes reflect the lilas
of Radha and Krishna: pure prema, in which there is nothing mundane. BR: "…with Gadadhara Gora sports — Vasudeva Ghosha
reveals [this] rasa"; MS "gha": "Vasudeva Ghosha has revealed this rasa".

## 6. Vasu Ghosha — "gaurāṅgacām̐dera mane ki bhāba paṛila…" (BR, pp. 936–937)
*Lila: Navadvipa, 1509 — sitting in the flower garden, the Lord plays dice (pasha-sari) with Gadadhara; both are immersed
in the rasa of the game. The pada is known from BR and GPT. According to Majumdar — seen with the poet's own eyes. Raga
kamoda.*[^16]

**6.1.** What feeling arose in the heart of Gaurangachand! —
taking dice and pieces, Gora started a game.

**6.2.** With Gadadhara Gora plays at dice;
they began to throw the dice, saying: "I lose — I win!"

**6.3.** "Two, four!" — Gadadhara throws his turn;
"Five, three!" — Gaurangasundara casts.[^17]

**6.4.** The two were immersed in the rasa of the dice game;
crying "Jaya, jaya!", Vasudeva Ghosha sings.

[^16]: Signature: "Vasudeva Ghosha". BR, wave 12 (ed. 1912/1913, pp. 936–937; ed. 1960, vv. 3230–3234): "One day
Gaurahari with Gadadhara, sitting in this flower garden, plays at dice." GPT1 — p. 354, no. 32 (raga dhanashi), with minor
variants ("the Lord began the game"). The pada was not previously found in PK (see the end of this note). The game of dice (pasha-khela) is a well-known lila of
Radha and Krishna; on Gadadhara see note 15.
Full PK check (chapter 16): another recension of the pada is in PK — **PK 2668** (vol. 4, 30th pallava, ashtakaliya: "atha pāśā-krīṛā. tatra śrī-gauracandro yathā," PDF p. 114, label "॥১৯৫॥২৬৬৮॥"; "gaurāṅgacām̐dera mane ki bhāba haila / pāśā śārī laiyā pahu khelā ārambhila…," signature "jaya jaya diyā gāya bāsudeba ghoṣe").
[^17]: The players' cries name the throw they want. BR-1960, BR-2006 and GPT1: "'Five, three!' — calls out…" (ḍāke); GPT1
here names the Lord "Rasika-nagara" ("the connoisseur of rasa", a name of Krishna).

## 7. Vasu Ghosha — "jalakeli gorācāndera maneta paṛila…" (PK 1108; BR, p. 937)
*Lila: Navadvipa, 1509 — the Lord remembers the water play and goes down into the Ganga with His companions; all splash
one another; Gauranga splashes Gadadhara. In BR the guide shows Srinivasa that very ghat; in PK it is the gaura-chandrika
to the section "Water play". According to Majumdar — seen with the poet's own eyes. Raga saranga (in BR, mayura).*[^18]

**7.1.** The water play came to Gorachand's mind —
with His associates He went down into the water.

**7.2.** Someone splashes water on someone;
Gauranga, scooping up water, splashes Gadadhara.[^19]

**7.3.** With a joyful heart Gora sports in the water;
all shout with joy, all call to one another.

**7.4.** The lila of Gaurangachand cannot be told in words;
Vasudeva Ghosha sings of it — of Gora's virtues.

[^18]: Signature: "Vasudeva Ghosha". PK: vol. 2, PDF p. 261, 16th pallava, no. 17, under the heading "Here — water play.
Sri Gaurachandra befitting it"; MS P-R-Sa no. 1606. BR, wave 12 (ed. 1912/1913, p. 937; ed. 1960, vv. 3235–3239): "One
day at this ghat the Lord with His companions plays in the water, remembering the former [lila]." GPT1 — p. 351, no. 25
(raga tudi); GPT2 — sect. 5.2, no. 25.
[^19]: BR: "…Gadadhara splashes water on Gora's body." Krishna's water play (jala-keli) with Radha and the gopis is one of
the lilas of Vraja; on Gadadhara see note 15.

## 8. Vasu Ghosha — "bṛndābana-līlā gorāra manete paṛila…" (PK 1253; BR, p. 941)
*Lila: Navadvipa — the Lord remembers the lila of Vrindavana: the Ganga becomes for Him the Yamuna, the flowering grove
Vrindavana, His companions the gopis; bringing together khols and karatalas, He dances in the midst of the kirtana,
revealing the rasa of the rasa dance. In BR the guide tells Srinivasa: "Here Gaurachandra revealed great bliss, overcome
by the former rasa of the rasa dance"; in PK it is the gaura-chandrika to the section "The great rasa on the autumn full
moon". Raga tudi (in BR, kamoda).*[^20]

**8.1.** The lila of Vrindavana came to Gora's mind:
the Suradhuni became for Him the Yamuna.[^21]

**8.2.** He saw the flowering grove as like Vrindavana,
and took His companions for the gopis.

**8.3.** Bringing together khols and karatalas,
in their midst Gora dances amid cries of "Jaya, jaya!"

**8.4.** *(Only in BR)* Gora's body glows softly, outshining gold;
His youthful, lovely arms reach to the knees.

**8.5.** Vasudeva Ghosha delights in this:
Gorachand revealed the rasa of the rasa dance.[^22]

[^20]: Signature: "Vasudeva Ghosha". PK: vol. 2, PDF pp. 317–318, the first pada of the 24th pallava ("Then — the great
rasa on the autumn full moon. Sri Gaurachandra befitting it"); the end of the pada and its number are smudged in the scan
— by sequence it is no. 1 / 1253, which agrees with Ray's index and with Majumdar (PK 1253 is in his list of Vasu's padas
"on bhava"). BR, wave 12 (ed. 1912/1913, p. 941; ed. 1960, vv. 3291–3296). GPT1 — p. 335, no. 46 (raga tudi). Couplet 8.4
is found only in BR. Majumdar, listing the pastimes on BR pp. 936–943, does not name this pada specifically, but it
belongs to the same series.
[^21]: BR: "He took the Suradhuni for the Yamuna" (yamunāra bhāṇa, "the semblance of the Yamuna"); PK and GPT: "He turned His
feeling for the Yamuna upon the Suradhuni" (yamunāra bhāba). Suradhuni ("the river of the gods") is the Ganga.
[^22]: In these pastimes the Lord relishes the moods of Krishna-lila; in the kirtana His companions become, as it were, the
gopis — this is prema, pure and transcendental, not mundane feeling. The Gaudiya acharyas (Krishnadasa Kaviraja,
Bhaktisiddhanta) teach that Mahaprabhu is Krishna who has accepted Radha's mood and lustre to taste Her love (CC Adi 1, 4);
in Navadvipa He at times displayed the moods of Krishna Himself as well (cf. chapter 1, nos. 9–13). BR: "…Lord Gora
reveals the rasa of the rasa."

## 9. Vasu Ghosha — "dekha dekha ṛtu-rāja basanta samaya…" (PK 1425; BR, pp. 942–943)
*Lila: Navadvipa, spring — the Lord plays with coloured powder (phagu) with His companions; Srivasa leads the musicians,
Haridasa dances with upraised arms; Gadadhara is beside Him. In PK — the first pada of the section "Spring play on
Vasanta-panchami" (gaura-chandrika); in BR — among the padas on spring in Navadvipa, after padas of Narahari Chakravarti.
According to Majumdar — the holi with Gadadhara, seen with the poet's own eyes (1509). Raga vasanta.*[^23]

**9.1.** Look, look: the time of spring, the king of seasons —
Gora Raya sports with His companions.

**9.2.** Gorachand plays with coloured powder in the town of Nadia,
stealing the hearts of young women with the arrows of His eyes.[^24]

**9.3.** The companions together throw powder on Gora's body;
some run about with squirts of kunkuma.[^25]

**9.4.** Srinivasa has brought the various instruments into harmony;
with Gadadhara and others [the Lord] sports.

**9.5.** Crying "Hari!", with upraised arms, Haridasa dances;
this rasa Vasudeva Ghosha has told.

[^23]: Signature: "Vasudeva Ghosha". PK: vol. 2, PDF p. 395, no. 1, under the heading "Then — spring play on
Vasanta-panchami. Here — Sri Mahaprabhu"; MS P-R-Sa no. 1978. BR, wave 12 (ed. 1912/1913, pp. 942–943; ed. 1960,
vv. 3321–3324): the guide tells Srinivasa that Gaurachandra with His companions sported here in spring with great joy and
that "here there was a wonderful game of phagu"; in BR the pada begins with the second couplet. GPT1 — pp. 337–338, no. 54
(raga vasanta); GPT2 — sect. 5.1, no. 54. Phagu is the red powder which people throw on one another at the spring
festivals (Vasanta-panchami, dola-yatra or holi).
[^24]: The words about young women are the language of nagari-bhava (Gaura as the nagara, the beloved of the town's
women); we translate faithfully to the author. Vrindavana Dasa stresses that in this incarnation the Lord never glanced at
another man's wife; worship of Gaura in the spirit of "Gaura-nagari" is not accepted by the Gaudiya tradition (Vrindavana
Dasa, Krishnadasa Kaviraja, Bhaktisiddhanta).
[^25]: Kunkuma is a red powder (saffron); the squirt (pichaka, pichkari) is for coloured water. BR: "…run about with squirts
of sandal-water"; GPT1: "…run after [Him] with squirts [of flower-water]".

## 10. Shivananda Sena — "soṇāra baraṇa gorā prema-binodiyā…" (PK 2127)
*Lila: Navadvipa — golden Gora has flooded Nadia with prema; leaning on Govinda, He listens to the glories of Vrindavana
and, crying "Radha, Radha!", falls unconscious; Shivananda weeps, not comprehending the Lord's bhava. In PK — in the 18th
pallava, immediately after Yadunatha Dasa's pada on the Lord's bhava (PK 2126). According to Majumdar the pada conveys the
bewilderment of the devotees at the sight of Gauranga's ecstasies. Raga gauri.*[^26]

**10.1.** Golden Gora, rapt in prema,
has flooded the town of Nadia with the waters of prema.

**10.2.** Down His broad chest stream torrents of prema;
intoxicated with prema, He knows neither day nor night.

**10.3.** Leaning on Govinda,
the Lord, absorbed, listens to the glories of Vrindavana.[^27]

**10.4.** "Radha, Radha!" cries the Lord and falls unconscious;
Shivananda weeps, not comprehending the Lord's bhava.[^28]

[^26]: Signature: "Shivananda". PK: vol. 3, PDF p. 239 (printed p. 228), 18th pallava, no. 19; MS P-R-Sa no. 2234. GPT1 —
p. 281, no. 14 (raga gauri), also immediately after Yadunatha's pada "gadādhara narahari kare dhari gaurahari" (GPT1 no. 13). Shivananda
Sena of Kumarahatta, the father of Kavi-Karnapura, led the devotees of Gauda to Puri every year; Majumdar regards his padas
as written during the Lord's lifetime.
[^27]: Govinda — probably Govinda Ghosha, the kirtana singer, singing to the Lord of Vrindavana; if the pada describes Puri,
Govinda the Lord's servant. Cf. the padas of Murari Gupta "Leaning against Gadadhara…" and of Paramananda "Gora's body
rolls in the dust" (chapter 1, nos. 6–7).
[^28]: The Lord is Krishna who has accepted Radha's bhava; His cry "Radha, Radha!" and His swoon are manifestations of
mahabhava (CC Adi 4). The words "not comprehending the bhava" are the poet's humility: the depth of this bhava is beyond
the grasp even of His intimate companions.

## 11. Chaitanyadasa — "āre mora gaura kiśora…" (PK 1985)
*Lila: the Lord's bhava — youthful Gaura, rapt in the rasa of former love, says with tears of joy: "I have found the Youth
of Vraja, all My sorrow is gone"; His companions laugh with joy. In PK it is the gaura-chandrika to the section
"Samriddhiman-sambhoga" (the fullness of union after long separation). The place is not named. Majumdar compares the pada
with the Lord's words in the *Chaitanya-charitamrita*: "I have found the Lord of My life…". Raga suhai.*[^29]

**11.1.** O my youthful Gaura,
rapt in the rasa of former love!

**11.2.** In both eyes, tears of joy;
beside Himself with prema, the Lord says:

**11.3.** "I have found the Youth of Vraja —
all My sorrow has gone far away;

**11.4.** after long days I have found My life —
as if bathed in nectar."[^30]

**11.5.** Seeing this, the companions laugh with joy —
so sings Chaitanya Dasa.

[^29]: Signature: "Chaitanya Dasa". PK: vol. 3, PDF p. 188 (printed p. 177), 12th pallava, no. 17, under the heading "Then
— samriddhiman-sambhoga. Here — Sri Mahaprabhu". GPT1 — p. 413, no. 30 (raga suhai; on the same page are Vasu Ghosha's padas
nos. 31 and 33: chapter 1, no. 23; chapter 4, no. 18). Majumdar assigns Chaitanyadasa probably to Gadadhara's branch; he
compares the language of the pada with the padas of Narahari and Shivananda (cf. Narahari's pada with the same opening —
chapter 2, no. 7), and its sense with CC Madhya 1 and 13: "I have found the Lord of My life, for whom I was wasting away,
burnt by the fire of Madana" — the Lord's words at the Ratha-yatra.
[^30]: "The Youth of Vraja" (baraja-kiśora) is Krishna. These are the Lord's words in Radha's bhava as She finds Krishna after
long separation; the joy of this meeting is pure prema, not mundane feeling.

## 12. Paramananda (Gupta) — "ki karilā gorācām̐da nadiyā chāṛiyā…" (PK 1693)
*Lila: after the sannyasa — the grief of the devotees of Navadvipa: Murari, Mukunda and Srivasa will not survive the
separation, Advaita has despaired; with a straw between his teeth the poet begs the Lord to come to Nadia at least once. In
PK — the gaura-chandrika to the gopis' messages to Krishna in Mathura. According to Majumdar — probably a fragment of
Paramananda Gupta's *Gauranga-vijaya-gita*, written during the Lord's lifetime. Raga suhai.*[^31]

**12.1.** What have You done, Gorachand, leaving Nadia!
The devotees are dying, not seeing You.

**12.2.** The sports of kirtana and all the joy You gave —
remembering them again and again, everyone's heart is breaking.

**12.3.** Murari, Mukunda, Srinivasa will not live;
Advaita Acharya has despaired of life.

**12.4.** All the people of Nadia are in anguish —
their hearts writhe, not seeing You.

**12.5.** Says Paramananda, holding a straw between his teeth:
"Come to Nadia just once, Lord Gaurahari!"[^32]

[^31]: Signature: "Paramananda". PK: vol. 3, PDF p. 66 (printed p. 55), 6th pallava, no. 32 ("And again — Sri
Gaurachandra"), among the padas on the messengers to Krishna in Mathura: as the gopis call Krishna back to Vraja, so the
devotees call Gaura to Nadia; MS P-R-Sa no. 916. In the 21st pallava of PK, after Madhava Ghosha's pada PK 2277 (no. 17
below), the compiler refers to this pada again: "'What have You done, Gorachand, leaving Nadia' etc. — given above."
GPT1 — p. 385, no. 7 (raga suhai); GPT2 — sect. 5.4, no. 7. On the attribution see note 1.
[^32]: Holding a straw between the teeth is a sign of utter humility before one who is being entreated. GPT1: "This time come
to Nadia". The devotees' wish was fulfilled in 1514/15, when the Lord came to Kuliya opposite Navadvipa (CC Madhya 1, 16;
chapter 1, no. 23; chapter 4, nos. 18–19).

## 13. Vasu Ghosha — "hari hari gorā kothā gela…" (PK 1856)
*Lila: after the sannyasa — a devotee's lament: "Had I known that Gora would leave, I would have kept Him, binding His life
to mine"; how will Gadadhara and Damodara live? In PK — in the 10th pallava ("Various separations"), immediately after
Vamshi's pada "No more shall I see…" (PK 1855; chapter 1, no. 17). According to Majumdar — one of Vasu Ghosha's padas on
the sannyasa. Raga suhai.*[^33]

**13.1.** Hari, Hari! Where has Gora gone?
A spear has pierced my heart — and does not come out.

**13.2.** To whom shall I tell my grief? Words do not leave my lips;
ceaselessly I remember Gora, the jewel of virtues.

**13.3.** Had I known that Gora would leave us,
I would have kept Him, binding His life to mine.[^34]

**13.4.** How will Gadadhara and Damodara live?
Now Vasu Ghosha will die.[^35]

[^33]: Signature: "Vasu Ghosha". PK: vol. 3, PDF p. 131 (printed p. 120), 10th pallava, no. 5. In GPT1 (p. 385, no. 8, raga
suhai) the pada is signed "Radhamohana Dasa" (a poet of the early 18th c., compiler of the *Padamrita-samudra*), but the
compiler of GPT notes that in one manuscript the signature reads "Now Vasu Ghosha will die"; PK gives Vasu's signature. The
attribution is therefore not beyond doubt.
[^34]: Lit. "giving life for life, I would have kept [Him] bound". MSS "ka", "kha", "cha": "jānitāṅa" (same meaning).
[^35]: Gadadhara is Gadadhara Pandita; Damodara is probably Damodara Pandita, the Lord's companion in Navadvipa (CC Adi 10).
MSS "ka", "kha", "cha": "…how shall I live… I shall die" (bām̐ciba, mariba). The signature is a formula of grief: the poet will
not survive the separation.

## 14. Chaitanyadasa — "mohe bihi biparīta bhela…" (PK 463)
*Lila: a devotee's remorse: Gora, the darling of his life, has gone, and the poet, having found Him, did not serve Him. In
PK — the gaura-chandrika to the section "Kalahantarita" (the heroine who has rebuffed her beloved in a quarrel and
repents); the pada is composed in the voice of such a heroine. According to Majumdar — testimony of the poet's personal
closeness to Gaura. Raga suhai.*[^36]

**14.1.** The Creator has turned against me:
through my wounded pride the Lord, disregarding me, has gone.[^37]

**14.2.** What shall I do? Tell me, what way is there?
How shall I find again my Gora Raya?

**14.3.** I meant one thing — I know not what has happened:
Gora, the darling of my life, has gone, leaving me.

**14.4.** Who knew it would be so:
the treasure I was tying in the end of my sari would fall into the ocean!

**14.5.** So it happened with Chaitanyadasa:
having found Gaurangachand, he did not serve Him — and lost Him.[^38]

[^36]: Signature: "Chaitanyadasa". PK: vol. 1, PDF pp. 314–315 (printed pp. 303–304), the first pada of the 15th pallava,
under the heading "Another kind of kalahantarita. Sri Gaurachandra befitting it"; MS P-R-Sa no. 652. GPT1 — p. 313, no. 21
(raga suhai).
[^37]: The pada is composed in the voice of the kalahantarita — the heroine who in wounded pride (abhimana) has rebuffed her
beloved and now repents. The poet applies this image from Radha's lila to himself: having found Gaura, he did not serve
Him, and Gora went away (evidently the sannyasa is meant). This is the language of the gaura-chandrika, not of mundane
relations; the "wounded pride" here is the bitter self-reproach of a devotee.
[^38]: "Lost" (tejila, lit. "let go, gave up"). MS P-R-Sa: "Just so it happened…" (sei se); MSS "kha", "gha" in 14.3: "left me"
(mohe).

## 15. Vasu Ghosha — "gorā-guṇe prāṇa kānde ki buddhi kariba…" (PK 2280)
*Lila: after the sannyasa — the lament of Navadvipa: who else will show mercy to the fallen and, entreating, give away the
name of Hari? Without Gora all Nadia has become empty. In PK — in the 21st pallava, in the section "The longing of the
devotees". According to Majumdar — one of Vasu Ghosha's padas on the sannyasa. Raga suhai.*[^39]

**15.1.** For Gora's virtues my heart weeps — what can I devise?
Where shall I go to find Gauranga, the treasury of virtues?[^40]

**15.2.** Who else will show mercy, seeing the fallen?
Who, entreating, will give the rare name of Hari?

**15.3.** Who will burst into tears, seeing the destitute?
Without Gora all Nadia has become empty.[^41]

**15.4.** Vasudeva Ghosha weeps, remembering His virtues:
how is one to live, not seeing Gora?

[^39]: Signature: "Vasudeva Ghosha". PK: vol. 3, PDF p. 296 (printed p. 285), 21st pallava, no. 63, after Vasu's pada "How
many days more until I see Gorachand's face…" (PK 2279) under the common heading "Then — the longing of the devotees";
MS P-R-Sa nos. 1041 and 2384 (the pada is written in it twice); MS "kha" lacks it. GPT1 — p. 387, no. 15 (raga suhai).
Majumdar lists PK 2280 among Vasu Ghosha's padas on the sannyasa.
[^40]: MSS "gha", "cha": "the treasury of mercy" (dayāra nidhi).
[^41]: "The destitute" (akiñcana) — the humble one who has nothing but hope in the Lord. MS P-R-Sa: "Who will burst into
tears, seeing such a person? Without Gora the town of Nadia has become empty."

## 16. Madhava Ghosha — "tachu dukhe dukhī eka priya-sakhī…" (PK 2276)
*Lila: the years of separation (the Lord in Puri) — a friend of Vishnupriya, sharing her grief, as if maddened by
separation from Gaura, runs to the bank of the Ganga, to the place where the Lord used to sit, and in her raving words
seems to see Gaura in Nilachala. In PK — in the 21st pallava, after Madhava Dasa's pada on Vishnupriya's grief (PK 2275).
According to Majumdar — an imagined scene; the authorship of Madhava Ghosha for this and the next two padas is doubtful.
Raga dhanashi.*[^42]

**16.1.** A dear friend, grieving with her grief,
overwhelmed by separation from Gaura,
unable to bear it, ran off
as if gone mad.[^43]

**16.2.** In the town of Nadia, on the bank of the Suradhuni,
where the Lord used to sit —
going there, in a choking voice
she softly says something.

**16.3.** At those raving words
even a stone would melt:
as if, having gone to Nilachala,
she sees Gaura there.[^44]

**16.4.** Her eyes stream, her heart overflows —
weeping, she speaks;
Madhava Ghosha's heart is distraught:
to hear it is pain deep within.

[^42]: Signature: "Madhava Ghosha". PK: vol. 3, PDF p. 295 (printed p. 284), 21st pallava, no. 59; MS P-R-Sa no. 2380.
"Her" (tachu) refers to Vishnupriya, of whom the preceding pada in PK, by Madhava Dasa, "All my life I prided myself on
Gaura…" (PK 2275), speaks. GPT1 — p. 399, no. 32 (raga dhanashi), reading "your grief" (tuyā dukhe). Majumdar accepts Madhava
(Madhavananda) Ghosha, brother of Govinda and Vasu and the finest kirtana singer (CB Antya 5), as the Lord's contemporary,
but regards his padas on the sannyasa as late: PK 2276 is an imagined scene of the grief of Nadia's women, and PK 2278 was
probably written much later. The attribution of PK 2276–2278 to Madhava Ghosha is therefore doubtful.
[^43]: "As if gone mad" (yemata bāurī pārā) — the madness of grief in separation from Gaura, that is, of a devotee's prema.
[^44]: MSS "ka", "kha", "gha", "cha" and GPT1 read "yaichana" — "as if"; the main text of PK has "aichana" ("[Gaura] thus").

## 17. Madhava Ghosha — "abalā se biṣṇupriyā tuyā guṇa soṅariyā…" (PK 2277)
*Lila: the years of separation — the poet addresses the Lord: Vishnupriya, remembering His virtues, lies unconscious, her
friends holding cotton to her nostrils; the people of Nadia, old Shachi and the companions are barely alive — "Come to
Nadia, O best of the knowers of rasa!" According to Majumdar — Madhava Ghosha's pada on the grief of Shachi and
Vishnupriya. Raga pahida.*[^45]

**17.1.** The helpless Vishnupriya, remembering Your virtues,
fell unconscious to the ground;
her friends around, seeing this, weep,
holding a wisp of cotton to her nostrils.[^46]

**17.2.** In the fire of separation from You her heart is worn out —
life barely stays in her body.
All the dwellers of Nadia have fainted,
not seeing Your face.

**17.3.** Old Shachi is half-dead — there is no life in her body —
and You have no pity for her!
How will Your friends in Nadia keep alive?
How did You give up Your affection for them?[^47]

**17.4.** All Your companions are overwhelmed by separation:
their breath is held only by the hope of seeing You.
O best of the knowers of rasa, come to the town of Nadia! —
says the lowly Madhava Ghosha.[^48]

[^45]: Signature: "Madhava Ghosha". PK: vol. 3, PDF pp. 295–296 (printed pp. 284–285), 21st pallava, no. 60; MS P-R-Sa
no. 2381. GPT1 — p. 400, no. 33 (raga pahida); GPT2 — sect. 5.4, no. 33. Majumdar cites the pada as "PK 1277" (a misprint
for 2277); on the doubts about the attribution see note 42. Immediately after this pada the compiler of PK refers to
Paramananda's pada "What have You done, Gorachand, leaving Nadia" (no. 12).
[^46]: Cotton is held to the nostrils to see whether there is any breath. "Life barely stays in her body" (17.2) — lit.
"life has become separate from the body": a poetic exaggeration of a swoon; MS P-R-Sa: "[separation] has separated [her]
life".
[^47]: The reproach "no pity" and the familiar address (tora) are the language of grief and intimacy, not a judgement on the
Lord: in leaving His home the Lord did not abandon His own (cf. CC Madhya 3 and 16 — the meetings with Shachi in Shantipura
and Kuliya). "Affection" is in the original "maya" (māyā) in its everyday Bengali sense of "tender attachment, love", not
the illusory energy: the Lord is not subject to maya. MS P-R-Sa has "Shachi, who…" (śacīya ye) instead of "old Shachi".
[^48]: "Knower of rasa" (rasikabara) — an address to Gaura as to Krishna. MSS: "the breath flows" (vahe; P-R-Sa and GPT1), "the hope
of touching [You]" (paraśana; "gha"), "this Madhava" (P-R-Sa).

## 18. Madhava Ghosha — "gaurāṅga jhāṭa kari calaha nadīyā…" (PK 2278)
*Lila: the years of separation — the poet begs Gauranga to come quickly to Nadia: Vishnupriya, remembering His former
love, has fainted; Nadia and the companions weep in the dust. According to Majumdar — probably written long after the
sannyasa. Raga shri.*[^49]

**18.1.** Gauranga, come quickly to Nadia:
the helpless Vishnupriya is as if lifeless.[^50]

**18.2.** All Your deeds, the former love —
remembering them again and again, she has now fainted.

**18.3.** That same town of Nadia, all those companions
weep, fallen in the dust, not seeing You.

**18.4.** Says Madhava Ghosha: "Listen, Gaurahari:
delay but a moment — and I shall die first."

[^49]: Signature: "Madhava Ghosha". PK: vol. 3, PDF p. 296 (printed p. 285), 21st pallava, no. 61; MS P-R-Sa no. 2382.
GPT1 — p. 400, no. 34 (raga shri). Majumdar supposes that the pada was written long after the sannyasa: hardly anyone in
the Lord's lifetime would have called Him back like this, and the mention of Vishnupriya's "former love" seems to him
discourteous; this is only a conjecture. On the doubts about the attribution see note 42. In the stage-1 catalogue the
first line was read "gaurāṅga bāṭa kari" — the scan has "jhāṭa kari" ("quickly").
[^50]: "As if lifeless" (prāṇa-hīna) — lit. "has lost life": an exaggeration of a swoon, as in 17.2. "The former love" (18.2)
is the intimacy of the years of the Lord's household life; the love and grief of Vishnupriya, the Lord's eternal companion,
are revered by the Gaudiya tradition as pure prema.

## 19. Kanu Dasa — "āre mora pahum̐ nitāicām̐da…" (PK 2327)
*Lila: Nityananda in Gauda (after 1515) — He sings and dances, absorbed in bhava, sprinkles the suffering people with His
glance, tells of His Brother; His fair body is covered with thrills like a kadamba flower; the devotees sing around Him. In
PK — in the 22nd pallava ("Description of the virtues of Sri Nityananda"); in KGC — in the 10th kshanada. According to
Majumdar — a pada on Nityananda's preaching in Gauda. Raga deshaga.*[^51]

**19.1.** O my Lord Nitaichand!
In every house He has set the snare of prema.

**19.2.** All the people scorched by the world's heat
He has sprinkled with the corners of His eyes.[^52]

**19.3.** Boundless mercy in the land of Gauda:
He dances and wanders, absorbed in bhava.

**19.4.** In a choking voice He tells of His Brother;
His reddened eyes drown in the waters of prema.[^53]

**19.5.** His fair, beautiful body glows reddish,
the thrills like the filaments of a kadamba.

**19.6.** His body is adorned with various ornaments;
the devotees together sing joyfully.

**19.7.** Swaying — in how many ways!
His lotus feet step like a wagtail.[^54]

**19.8.** Hearing of His mercy, hope has grown:
at His feet this Kanudasa begs for prema.

[^51]: Signature: "Kanudasa". PK: vol. 3, PDF p. 313 (printed p. 302), 22nd pallava, no. 34; MS P-R-Sa no. 2431. KGC — 10th
kshanada, no. 2 (pp. 76–77; raga shri), with a different order of couplets (19.7 after 19.3) and the signature "Kanu
Dasa"; GPT1 — p. 431, no. 58 (raga barari); GPT2 — sect. 6.1, no. 58. Majumdar identifies Kanudasa and Kanurama Dasa (their
language is the same) with Kanu Thakura — son of Purushottama Dasa and grandson of Sadashiva Kaviraja, of Nityananda's
branch (CC Adi 11). Nityananda preached in Gauda at the Lord's command after 1515 (CC Madhya 15; CB Antya 5).
[^52]: The world's heat is the threefold suffering; Nityananda's glance is the cool water of mercy.
[^53]: "Brother" (bhāiyā) is Gauranga: telling of Him, Nityananda weeps. Cf. the cry "Brother, brother!" (bhāiyā re) in padas of
the same pallava of PK (2319, 2326).
[^54]: The wagtail (khanjana) is an image of a graceful, light gait. MSS "ka", "kha", "cha", P-R-Sa in 19.3: "wanders"
(vulena — honorific form); KGC and GPT1: "absorbed in prema", "absorbed in bhava".

## 20. Kanurama Dasa — "dayā kara more nitāi dayā kara more…" (PK 2321)
*Lila: a prayer to Nityananda: You gave prema to all without distinction — why then am I unhappy? You are the Lord of our
family. In PK — in the 22nd pallava ("Description of the virtues of Sri Nityananda"). According to Majumdar, "the Lord of
the family" points to three generations of the poet's family at Nityananda's feet. Raga patamanjari.*[^55]

**20.1.** Have mercy on me, Nitai, have mercy on me!
Nitai is the refuge of those without refuge, say the saints.

**20.2.** "Glory to the Giver of prema-bhakti!" proclaims Your banner;
You made no distinction at all between high and low.[^56]

**20.3.** By the gift of prema You made happy the hearts of all beings in the world;
if You are the Lord of mercy, why am I unhappy?

**20.4.** Says Kanurama Dasa: what can I say?
This is my great hope: You are the Lord of our family.[^57]

[^55]: Signature: "Kanurama Dasa". PK: vol. 3, PDF p. 311 (printed p. 300), 22nd pallava, no. 28; MS P-R-Sa no. 2425. GPT1 —
p. 431, no. 57 (raga patamanjari), immediately before PK 2327 (no. 19). In the stage-1 catalogue the first line had not been
recognized (KD-009). Majumdar: Kanurama Dasa = Kanudasa = Kanu Thakura; "the Lord of our family" — the poet's grandfather
Sadashiva Kaviraja, his father Purushottama Dasa and he himself — three generations at Nityananda's feet.
[^56]: "Banner" (patākā; MSS "ka", "kha": patakā) — the image of Nityananda as a victorious king whose banner proclaims the giving
of prema. GPT1: "…You make no distinction".
[^57]: MSS in 20.3: "the hearts of the world's people" (jaga janera — "ka"; jagat jībera — "kha"); "You are the Lord of mercy" (without
"if" — "ka"); "You, such a Lord of mercy — why am I…" ("kha", "cha").
