# Kirtanas and Gaura's Form in Navadvipa, the Lord's Ecstasy, Nityananda, Separation, and Nilachala

*Padas of contemporaries known from the *Padakalpataru* (PK), the *Bhakti-ratnakara* (BR) and the
*Kshanada-gita-chintamani* (KGC), in the order of the lilas: the Navadvipa kirtana through the eyes of Vasu Ramananda;
praise of the Gaura-avatara (Narahari, Yadunatha Dasa); Gaura's form (Shankara Ghosha, Vasudeva Datta, Ramananda); the
kirtana festival in Advaita's house (Vamshivadana); the Lord's dance in kirtana (Chaitanyadasa, Narahari, Ramananda); the
Lord's ecstatic moods (Yadunatha Dasa, Vamshidasa); pastimes on the Ganga — the boat and the swing (Ramananda);
Nityananda's dance in Srivasa's courtyard (Shankara Ghosha); Navadvipa after the sannyasa (Murari, Madhavi Dasa); kirtana
and the festival of colours in Nilachala (Ramananda, Madhavi Dasa); and the poet's prayer (Ramananda). The text follows PK, BR, KGC and the
*Gaura-pada-tarangini* (GPT1 — 1st ed., 1903; GPT2 — 2nd ed., 1934); the Bengali original, variants and word-for-word
translation are in `bn/06.md`.*

## 1. Vasu Ramananda — "caudige govinda-dhvani śuni pahuṅ hāse…" (BR, p. 952; KGC 29.1)
*Lila: a kirtana in Navadvipa: on every side the cry "Govinda!" resounds; the Lord laughs and dances together with
Nityananda; Govinda, Madhava and Vasu (the Ghosha brothers) and Mukunda sing. Narahari Chakravarti cites the pada in the
*Bhakti-ratnakara* among the testimonies to the Navadvipa kirtanas. According to Majumdar it is the pada of an eyewitness
of the Navadvipa kirtana (1509); from the order of the names "Govinda — Madhava — Vasu" he infers that Govinda Ghosha was
the eldest. Raga dhanashi (BR), mangala (KGC, GPT).*[^1]

**1.1.** On every side — "Govinda!"; hearing it, the Lord laughs;
with trembling lips Gora speaks in a faltering voice.[^2]

**1.2.** Gauranga dances, and with Him Nityananda;
the earth is flooded with prema, and bliss has swelled.

**1.3.** Govinda, Madhava, Vasu and Mukunda sing;
having found His own, He forgot Himself in the taste of kirtana.[^3]

**1.4.** The joyful companions are drunk with the nectarean taste,
and Vasu Ramananda in this is a thirsting chakora.[^4]

[^1]: Signature: "Vasu Ramananda". BR, 12th wave: 1913 ed. (Murshidabad), p. 952 ("again dhanashi"; the 1912 ed. the
same), 1960 ed. vv. 3426–3429, 2006 ed. vv. 3426–3429; in BR the pada stands in a series of padas on the Navadvipa
kirtana, near the pada of Chaitanyadasa (below, no. 10). A second recension: KGC, 29th kshanada (shukla-chaturdashi), no. 1
(pp. 229–230, raga mangala); GPT1 — p. 270, no. 73 (Majumdar's "p. 27" is evidently a misprint); GPT2 — sect. 4.2, no. 73.
In it the first and the last half-lines agree, but the middle differs (see note 3 and VAR); Majumdar considered the GPT
text corrupt. The pada is not in PK. Majumdar: Vasu Ramananda of Kulinagrama, a descendant of Maladhara Vasu, was a
contemporary of the Lord (CC Madhya 15).
[^2]: "Govinda!" (govinda-dhvani) — the name of Krishna sung in kirtana; perhaps also a hint at the lead singer Govinda
Ghosha. Gora is Gaura. "Trembling lips… faltering voice" are signs of ecstasy (sattvika-bhavas).
[^3]: "Having found His own" (pāyā = pāiyā nija-vṛnda) — His companions. In KGC and GPT, instead of 1.2–1.3: "Splendidly
Gauranga dances, and with Him Nityananda; the earth is flooded with prema, Ramananda sings. To Mukunda, Murari, Vasu He
says: 'Come here!' — for your virtues the Darling of [our] life weeps" (GPT: "Murari, Mukunda, come…"); and instead of the
first line of 1.4: "All the other devotees are drunk with bliss". The double mention of Ramananda (in 1.2 and in the
signature) argues for the BR recension.
[^4]: The chakora is a bird which, by poetic convention, feeds on moonlight: the poet greedily drinks the nectar of Gaura's
moonlike face.

## 2. Narahari — "anupāma gorā-avatāra…" (PK 2288)
*Praise of the Gaura-avatara: the Lord spread through all lands the taste of ninefold bhakti, making no distinction of
caste; in the age of Kali, where people sin, the holy name easily carries them across — what in other ages was attained
with great toil through sacrifice, japa, meditation and worship. In PK it stands in the 21st pallava, next to padas on
Gaura the sannyasi. Raga varadi.*[^5]

**2.1.** *(Refrain)* Peerless is the descent of Gora!
The taste of ninefold bhakti He spread through all lands,
making no distinction of caste.[^6]

**2.2.** Worship such a Lord, cast aside all [other] concerns,
give up all false desires.
By the virtues of Chaitanya-chanda the three worlds are lit —
effortlessly He revealed Himself.

**2.3.** Chaitanya is a wish-fulfilling tree, the guru of all living beings;
with Him is all the splendour of Goloka.
Seeing the living beings defiled, with compassionate eyes
He joyfully gave out the name of Hari.

**2.4.** Sacrifice, japa, meditation, worship — all that people did in other ages
they accomplished with very great toil.
But these people of the age of Kali, however much they sin —
taking the name, they crossed over with ease.[^7]

**2.5.** He is the essence, compassion embodied; with whom else can He be compared?
He fulfilled the hopes of the fallen.
Understanding nothing in his heart, weeping and weeping on the road,
Narahari Dasa sings His virtues.[^8]

[^5]: Signature: "Narahari Dasa". PK: vol. 3, PDF p. 299 (printed p. 288), 21st pallava, no. 71 (mark "॥৭১॥২২৮৮॥"); raga
varadi; MS P-R-Sa no. 2392. GPT1 — p. 28, no. 5 (raga varadi); GPT2 — sect. 1.3, no. 5. In Ray's index of poets it is
among the padas of Narahari. Majumdar does not discuss this pada; its simple Bengali without heaped ornament fits his
criteria for the genuine padas of Narahari Sarakara, but the attribution (Sarakara or Narahari Chakravarti) is unproven.
[^6]: The nine forms of bhakti — hearing, chanting, remembering, etc. (Bhag. 7.5.23–24). "Making no distinction of caste"
— cf. CC Adi 7 on the distribution of prema to all without discrimination. GPT: "spreading [it] with the taste of bhakti
through all lands, He made no distinction of caste".
[^7]: Cf. Bhag. 12.3.52: what was attained in Satya-yuga by meditation, in Treta by sacrifice, in Dvapara by worship, is
attained in Kali-yuga by chanting the name of Hari. GPT1 in 2.4: "but in this darkness of Kali, however much people sin —
taking the name, they cross over with ease".
[^8]: "Understanding nothing in his heart" — the poet's humility: he cannot grasp the Lord's greatness and only weeps as
he sings. MS P-R-Sa: "weeping, weeping"; "with whom can He be compared" (tāra for āra).

## 3. Narahari (?) — "pāsarā nā yāya āmāra gorācāṅdera līlā…" (PK 2344)
*Praise of Gaura: the pastimes of Gorachand cannot be forgotten — by His virtues beasts and birds weep and stones melt;
the name inaccessible to Brahma He gave out to the three worlds. In PK the pada has no signature (its last line speaks of
Nityananda); in GPT it bears the signature of Narahari Dasa. In PK it stands in the 25th pallava ("Description of the form
and virtues of Sri Chaitanya and Nityananda"). Raga vibhasa.*[^9]

**3.1.** *(Refrain)* I cannot forget the pastimes of my Gorachand!
By His virtues beasts and birds ever weep from both eyes,
and stones melt and melt.

**3.2.** For the sake of His name Mahesha became a yogi,
Virinchi meditates [on Him] incessantly.
The name inaccessible even to Brahma the Lord made easy to obtain —
and Himself, entreating, gave it to the three worlds.[^10]

**3.3.** Gauranga's body shines, and on it the hair stands on end like kadamba blossoms:
wondrous is the beauty of His holy body!
In bliss Thakura Nityananda is lost to himself,
seeing that golden radiance.[^11]

[^9]: PK: vol. 3, PDF p. 319 (printed p. 308), 25th pallava, no. 13 (mark "॥১৩॥২৩৪৪॥"), headed "again in another way"; raga
vibhasa; MS P-R-Sa no. 2448. There is no signature in PK. GPT1 — p. 28, no. 7, and GPT2 — sect. 1.3, no. 7 (both raga
vibhasa) give the last line with a signature: "In bliss Narahari Dasa is lost to himself, seeing that golden radiance".
The PK line is shorter than the tripadi metre requires (one member is missing), while the GPT line fits it; perhaps PK
preserved a line in which the poet's name was lost. The attribution to Narahari (still less to Sarakara) is unproven.
[^10]: Mahesha is Shiva; Virinchi is Brahma. "Entreating" (yāciyā) — the Lord Himself offers the name without waiting to be
asked (cf. CC Adi 3).
[^11]: Hair standing on end "like kadamba blossoms" is one of the sattvika-bhavas. MSS: "on the body of holy Gauranga"
("ka").

## 4. Yadunatha Dasa — "aparūpa cāṅda udaya nadiyāpure…" (PK 2191)
*Praise of Gaura: a wondrous Moon has risen in Nadia, and darkness has left the three worlds; the Lord whom Shesha,
Shankara, Narada and Brahma praise rolls on the ground in bliss on hearing His own virtues; from His eyes flows an ocean of
the nectar of prema. According to M-1961 it is a pada of Yadunatha Dasa (Kavichandra), a fellow countryman of Jagannatha
Mishra and a contemporary of the Lord. Raga dhanashi.*[^12]

**4.1.** A wondrous Moon has risen in the town of Nadia —
there is no more darkness in the three worlds!
On earth He destroyed the sorrow of all living beings,
bestowing the prema hidden in the Vedas.

**4.2.** *(Refrain)* O my Gaurangasundara Raya!
He made the lilies of the devotees' hearts unfold —
the salvation of the destitute.[^13]

**4.3.** Shesha, Shankara, Narada, the Four-faced One
incessantly sing His virtues;
and this incomparable Lord, hearing His own virtues,
rolls on the ground in bliss.[^14]

**4.4.** From His reddish eyes there flows, [like] an ocean,
the nectarean water of prema.
Yadunatha Dasa says: it is as if from a golden lotus
pearls were being born.[^15]

[^12]: Signature: "Yadunatha Dasa". PK: vol. 3, PDF p. 265 (printed p. 254), no. 8 (mark "॥৮॥২১৯১॥"); raga dhanashi; MS
P-R-Sa no. 2299. GPT1 — p. 35, no. 27 (raga dhanashi); GPT2 — sect. 1.3 (OCR 18989). Yadunatha Kavichandra was the youngest
son of Ratnagarbha Acharya, a fellow countryman and friend of Jagannatha Mishra (CB Adi 2; CC Adi 11 — in the branch of
Nityananda); B. B. Majumdar (*Padas of the Sixteenth Century*, 1961) considers him a contemporary who wrote of Gaura's
moods as an eyewitness, and distinguishes him from Yadunandana. Cf. his padas below (nos. 14–15).
[^13]: Gaura is the moon whose rays make the night-lilies (kumuda) — the devotees' hearts — unfold. "The destitute"
(akiñcana) — those who have nothing but devotion.
[^14]: Shesha (Ananta) is the thousand-mouthed serpent; Shankara is Shiva; the Four-faced One is Brahma. MS "gha": "the
Name" instead of "Narada". Cf. Bhag. 10.14 (Brahma's praise).
[^15]: "Ocean" (varuṇa-ālaya — "abode of Varuna"): the tears of prema flow in streams. The image of the last line: the
tears on Gaura's golden face are pearls "born" of a golden lotus. GPT1: "Yadunatha Dasa says: by the [good] fortune
(lit. the fruit of karma) of the living beings He brings forth these pearls".

## 5. Shankara Ghosha — "dekha dekha sundara śacī-nandanā…" (KGC 24.1)
*Gaura's form in Navadvipa: arms reaching to the knees, the gait of an intoxicated elephant, a garland of malati, a face
lovelier than the autumn moon, eyes full of the tears of prema's bliss; with His companions He ceaselessly plays and sings
"Hari, Hari". According to M-1961 it is a pada of Shankara Ghosha, a mridanga player and a contemporary of the Navadvipa
period. Raga beloyar.*[^16]

**5.1.** Look, look: the beautiful Son of Shachi!
His arms reach to the knees, beautifully formed.[^17]

**5.2.** His gait is like that of an intoxicated elephant;
a garland of malati sways on Gora's body.

**5.3.** His face is lovelier than the autumn moon,
His eyes are filled with the water of prema's bliss.

**5.4.** Taking His companions along, He ceaselessly plays:
in the midst of Navadvipa Gora sings "Hari, Hari!"

**5.5.** His lotus feet, which grant fearlessness, entice with their honey —
says Shankara Ghosha — He is the Deliverer of all the worlds.[^18]

[^16]: Signature: "Shankara Ghosha". KGC: 24th kshanada (shukla-navami), no. 1 ("to Sri Gaurachandra"), p. 192; raga
beloyar. The pada is not in PK or GPT; its only witness is the anthology of Vishvanatha Chakravarti (late 17th c.).
Shankara Ghosha was a mridanga player, a companion of the Lord; Devakinandana in the *Vaishnava-vandana* praises his
humility and the playing of the dampha with which he delighted the Lord. B. B. Majumdar (1961) counts him among the poets
who were companions of Nimai Pandita in Navadvipa.
[^17]: Arms reaching to the knees (ājānulambita) are one of the marks of a great personality (mahapurusha); cf. CC Adi 3 on
the Lord's marks.
[^18]: The lines of the pada are a chain of epithets in -nā (subalanā, calanā, dolanā…); the last is "the Deliverer of all
the worlds" (akhila-loka-tāraṇā). "Entice with their honey" (makaranda-lobhanā) — the image of devotees as bees thirsting
for the honey of the feet.

## 6. Vasudeva Datta (?) — "aparūpa gorā naṭarāja…" (KGC 22.1; PK 2925)
*The form of Gaura, the "king of dancers", in Navadvipa: curling locks, a sandal tilaka, arms lovelier than an elephant's
trunk, on His chest a necklace as if the Ganga were falling from the peak of Sumeru, crimson feet with moonlike nails. In
KGC it bears the signature of Vasudeva Datta, in PK and GPT that of Govinda Dasa. According to M-1961 it is the only pada
of Vasudeva Datta; Majumdar prefers the KGC attribution, since Vishvanatha Chakravarti is two or three generations older
than the compiler of PK, Vaishnavadasa. Raga kedara.*[^19]

**6.1.** *(Refrain)* Wondrous Gora, king of dancers!
The young Nagara made manifest, sporting in prema,
plays in Navadvipa.[^20]

**6.2.** His curling locks are fragrant,
on His forehead a tilaka of sandal.
Seeing [Him], chaste wives
bolt the doors of the temple of modesty.[^21]

**6.3.** His arms are lovelier than the trunk of a royal elephant,
a double necklace of elephant-pearls —
as if, rushing down from the peak of Sumeru,
the stream of the Ganga were flowing.[^22]

**6.4.** Crimson, peerless feet,
gem-like nails — radiant moons;
the devotee-bees are maddened by the fragrance —
Vasudeva Datta is enraptured.[^23]

[^19]: Signature: "Vasudeva Datta" (KGC: 22nd kshanada, shukla-saptami, no. 1, "to Sri Gaurachandra", p. 171; raga
kedara). PK 2925 (vol. 4, PDF pp. 207–208, printed pp. 198–199; 33rd pallava — "The eightfold eternal lila",
gaura-chandrika to a description of sambhoga; mark "॥১১॥২৯২৫॥"; raga kedara) has the signature "Govinda Dasa" and an extra
stanza (see VAR); so too GPT1 (p. 121, no. 31) and GPT2 (sect. 3.1, no. 31). Vasudeva Datta was the elder brother of
Mukunda Datta, a great singer (CC Adi 10; Madhya 11, 15). The attribution is disputed.
[^20]: "Nagara" — "the refined lover", a traditional epithet of Krishna; here of Gaura as Krishna Himself appearing in
Navadvipa ("young" — PK MSS "ka", "kha", "gha", "ca" and KGC: nava nāgara; the main PK text has simply nāgara).
[^21]: The image (as in padas about Krishna) speaks of the irresistibility of the Lord's beauty: at the sight of Him women
hide behind the doors of modesty. This is not a description of worldly passion, nor a basis for worshipping Gaura in the
mood of "gaura-nagari", which the Gaudiya Vaishnava tradition (Vrindavana Dasa, Krishnadasa Kaviraja, Bhaktisiddhanta
Sarasvati) does not accept: in Navadvipa, according to CB (Adi 15), the Lord did not even jest with women; He is Krishna
come in the mood of Radha, tasting Her prema.
[^22]: Elephant-pearls (gaja-mati) — according to tradition, pearls from the frontal globes of an elephant. Sumeru is the
golden mountain: Gaura's golden chest, down which the white necklace flows like the Ganga.
[^23]: In PK instead of this stanza: "I beheld the crimson feet, the nails a pair of moon-gems; maddened by the fragrance is
the swarm of bees — the mind of Govinda Dasa is enraptured".

## 7. Ramananda — "dekha dekha adabhuta sundara śacisuta…" (PK 2162)
*Gaura's form: His body, trembling with prema, outshines rays of gold; His lips sing "Hari, Hari"; drops of sandal on His
brow; His locks curl like bees round a golden lotus; tears flow from His eyes like pearls from a loosened string; He has
plunged the world into the ocean of the taste of singing His names and virtues. Signature "Ramananda" (without "Vasu"):
the attribution to Vasu Ramananda is likely but unproven. Raga gandhara.*[^24]

**7.1.** Look, look at the wondrous, beautiful Son of Shachi —
an extraordinary creation of the Creator!
The radiance of His trembling body outshines rays of gold,
His lips utter "Hari, Hari".

**7.2.** Drops of sandal gleam on His brow,
above them His locks sway;
[His face] shines brighter than a golden lotus and the moon,
and round it circles a swarm of bees.

**7.3.** His two reddish eyes put lotus petals to shame,
surpass the wagtail and the chakora;
like pearls from a loosened string,
so flow His abundant tears.[^25]

**7.4.** Into the ocean of the taste of singing His virtues and names
He has plunged the people of the world;
wretched Ramananda alone is deprived —
not even a drop has touched him.[^26]

[^24]: Signature: "Ramananda". PK: vol. 3, PDF p. 253 (printed p. 242), 18th pallava, no. 54 (mark "॥৫৪॥২১৬২॥"); raga
gandhara; MS P-R-Sa no. 2269. GPT1 — twice: p. 124, no. 40, and p. 137, no. 76 (both raga gandhara); GPT2 — sect. 3.1,
no. 76 (and no. 40). In Ray's index of poets "Ramananda" (11 padas) is kept apart from "Vasu Ramananda" (7 padas); Majumdar
ascribes to Vasu Ramananda only one pada signed "Ramananda" (PK 1711, portion 01, no. 18). The padas of this group (PK 1417,
2060, 2096, 2162, 2163, 2257, 2615, 3057) are in Vrajabuli and Bengali, like the undisputed padas of Vasu Ramananda (PK
2082, 1924); there is also the signature "Ramananda Dasa" (PK 2096, 2615). Hence authority C. In the stage-1 catalogue the
first line of PK 2160 (Shekhara Raya) had been attached to PK 2162.
[^25]: The wagtail (khañjana) and the chakora are images of lively, beautiful eyes. MSS: "trembling" (ḍagamaga — "ka"),
"string" (gāṅthala — "ka", "kha").
[^26]: MS "kha": "gave" (debe) and "not" (nāhi). GPT1, p. 137: "…how many wretched ones He saved, while Ramananda in this is
deprived — not a drop has touched him".

## 8. Ramananda — "dekhata bekata gaura adabhuta…" (PK 2163)
*Gaura's form and dance on the bank of the Ganga: He recalls the virtues of the Vraja-lila and cannot stand still; His
body thrills, tears flow; intoxicated by the devotees' singing, He dances skilfully and cries "Haribol". Signature:
"Ramananda". Raga tudi.*[^27]

**8.1.** Look: manifest is wondrous Gaura,
lighting up the bank of the Ganga!
His body is jambunada gold, His garment outshines the sun;
beautiful, well-formed, composed.[^28]

**8.2.** Recalling and recalling the virtues of the Vraja-lila,
He cannot stand still;
His body is covered with thrills, as if a kadamba had blossomed,
tears stream from His eyes.

**8.3.** Intoxicated by the taste of the devotees' ceaseless singing,
He sways His conch-like neck;
a thrill has seized the living beings; skilfully He dances
and again and again cries: "Haribol!"[^29]

**8.4.** God of gods, Lord of gods, Beloved of the people,
descent who purifies the fallen!
Ramananda, tormented by fear of the serpent of time in the age of Kali —
carry him across [the ocean of existence]!

[^27]: Signature: "Ramananda". PK: vol. 3, PDF pp. 253–254 (printed pp. 242–243), 18th pallava, no. 55; on the scan the
mark is "॥৫৫॥২১৬৪॥" — evidently a misprint (no. 55 follows PK 2162 = no. 54; in Ray's index the Ramananda pada is 2163);
raga tudi; MS P-R-Sa no. 2270. GPT1 — p. 124, no. 41 (raga tudi); GPT2 — sect. 3.1, no. 41 and no. 77. In the stage-1
catalogue the second GPT2 copy (no. 77) was wrongly ascribed to Paramananda (PA-004). On the attribution see note 24.
[^28]: Jambunada is celestial gold (from the river Jambu). MS P-R-Sa: "of beautiful, well-formed body" (śarīra for
sudhīra).
[^29]: "Conch-like neck" (kambu-kaṇṭha) — a neck with three lines, a mark of beauty. MSS "ka", "kha", "ca", P-R-Sa and
GPT1: "hearing, He dances again" (śuni puna for sunipuṇa); "ka": "the devotees (bhakata-gaṇa) are intoxicated with the
taste".

## 9. Vamshi(vadana) — "jaya jaya navadvīpa mājha…" (PK 26)
*Lila: the eve of a kirtana festival in Navadvipa: at Gauranga's bidding Advaita prepares the "khol-mangala" — the
consecration of the mridanga; having called the Vaishnavas, they perform the adhivasa of the great festival; Nitai gives
out garlands and sandal, Govinda plays the mridanga, Advaita the kartals, Haridasa sings, Srivasa holds the tune, Gora
dances. Cf. the pada of Parameshvara Dasa, PK 23 (portion 05, no. 4), on the same festival in Advaita's house. Raga
kamoda.*[^30]

**9.1.** *(Refrain)* Glory, glory [to the festival] in the midst of Navadvipa!
At Gauranga's bidding Thakura Advaita has come
and prepares the khol-mangala.[^31]

**9.2.** Having brought all the Vaishnavas, amid the clamour of "Haribol!",
they perform the adhivasa of the great festival.
Nitai Himself, [our] treasure, gives garlands and sandal,
greeting the dear Vaishnavas.[^32]

**9.3.** Govinda, taking the mridanga, plays: "ta-ta thaiya-thaiya",
nimble on the kartals is Advaita;
Haridasa sings, Srivasa holds the tune —
Gora dances at the blessing of the kirtana.[^33]

**9.4.** All round the Vaishnavas cry "Haribol!" again and again —
tomorrow there will be the great festival of kirtana.
"Today we have joyfully performed the khol-mangala," —
says Vamshi: "Cry out 'Glory!'"

[^30]: Signature: "Vamshi". PK: vol. 1, PDF p. 32 (printed p. 21), 1st pallava ("Mangalacharana"), no. 26 (mark "॥২৬॥");
raga kamoda; MS P-R-Sa no. 26. GPT1 — p. 247, no. 30 (raga kamoda); GPT2 — sect. 4.1, no. 30. In the stage-1 catalogue
the PK number was given wrongly (45; corrected in stage 1b). In PK the pada stands in a series of padas on the adhivasa of kirtana (PK 23 —
Parameshvara Dasa, 24 and 25 — Vrindavana Dasa). Majumdar ascribes the signature "Vamshi" to Vamshivadana (the guardian of
Vishnupriya), distinguishing him from the later Vamshidasa, a disciple of Srinivasa.
[^31]: The khol is the clay mridanga of Bengali kirtana; the khol-mangala is the consecration of the mridanga on the eve of
a kirtana (sandal, garlands, auspicious articles), and the adhivasa is the rite of "installation" that precedes a
festival. In PK 23 the same festival is described as arranged by Advaita and Sita Thakurani at the Lord's direction.
[^32]: MS "ga": "having given garlands and sandal".
[^33]: "Ta-ta thaiya-thaiya" imitates the rhythm of the mridanga. Govinda — evidently Govinda Ghosha or Govinda Datta (a
kirtaniya, CC Madhya 13). "Plays" (bāye) — so Ray corrects the reading of all the manuscripts, bāje ("sounds"), considering
it a scribal error.

## 10. Chaitanyadasa — "mahābhuja nācata caitanya rāya…" (PK 664; BR, p. 951)
*Lila: the Lord's dance in kirtana: His golden body is full of bhavas; hearing His own names and virtues He loses Himself in
the dance; Nadia has forgotten its sorrow; the rain of prema has watered the whole world, and only the poet is deprived.
Narahari Chakravarti cites the pada in the *Bhakti-ratnakara* among the testimonies to the Navadvipa kirtanas, next to the
pada of Vasu Ramananda (no. 1). In PK it is a gaura-chandrika to the "rasodgara". Raga vibhasa (PK), suhai (BR).*[^34]

**10.1.** *(Refrain)* Long-armed, Chaitanya Raya dances!
Who knows how many bhavas, hundreds upon hundreds,
are on Gora's golden body!

**10.2.** His body, moist with prema, pure,
is adorned with sprouts of thrill;

## 21. Madhavi Dasa — "nīlācala haite śacīre dekhite…" (PK 1853)
*Lila: Jagadananda comes from Nilachala to see Shachi; from afar Nadia seems to him like Gokula in separation: leaves fall
out of season, the sun does not shine, birds with closed eyes do not eat and weep, repeating Gorachand's name, cows stand
silent on the road — and the Pandita falls to the ground. Continued in Chandrashekhara's pada PK 1854 (portion 01, no. 25).
Signature: "Madhavi Dasa". Raga dhanashi (GPT).*[^67]

**21.1.** From Nilachala, to see Shachi,
Jagadananda comes;
stopping at a distance, he sees Nadia —
it is like Gokula.[^68]

**21.2.** *(Refrain)* Pandita Raya reflects:
"Shall I find Shachi or not?" —
with this thought he walks on.

**21.3.** Trees and creepers — hundreds and hundreds — he sees:
out of season their leaves are falling;
the sun's rays do not break through,
the clouds seem crimson.

**21.4.** Birds sitting on the branches, both eyes closed,
having given up fruit and water,
weep, crying aloud,
repeating the name of Gorachand.

**21.5.** Cows stand in herds on the road —
not a sound from any of them;
Thakura Pandita, [the master] of Madhavi Dasa,
crashed to the ground.[^69]

## 22. Ramananda — "bhāla bhāli nāce gaurāṅga raṅgiyā…" (PK 2257)
*Lila: the Lord's kirtana — evidently in Nilachala: Gauranga dances, Nitai supports Him from behind; Murari, Mukunda and Vasu
sing to the kartals and mridangas; the Lord asks: "Speak, speak, Narahari!" and looks at Gadadhara; coming up to Svarupa and
Rupa, He asks for the flute and stands in the tribhanga pose; Advaita rejoices, mighty Kashishvara supports Him. The
presence of Svarupa, Rupa and Kashishvara points to Nilachala, where the Gaudiyas came for the Ratha-yatra. Signature:
"Ramananda". Raga dhanashi.*[^70]

**22.1.** *(Refrain)* Splendidly, splendidly joyful Gauranga dances!
Intoxicated with prema, with His roar He destroys the filth of Kali;
behind Him Nitai walks, supporting [Him].

**22.2.** Kartals and mridangas sound, all sing aloud —
with Him Murari, Mukunda, Vasu;
hearing the pada, Gora Raya [dances] —
His feet do not touch the ground —
the ocean of prema surges in waves.[^71]

**22.3.** Lord Gaurahari asks: "Speak, speak, Narahari!" —
and looks at Gadadhara on His left;
blessed is dear Gadadhara, whose life is Chaitanya:
"Gadai's Gauranga," people sing.[^72]

**22.4.** Coming up to Svarupa and Rupa, He says: "Give Me the enchanting flute!" —
now He stands bent in three places;
His speech a heap of nectar; now, softly, softly laughing,
He raises both arms and cries "Hari".[^73]

**22.5.** "Glory, glory to the Jewel of the twice-born!" — an auspicious cry rose up,
Advaita's bliss increased;
mighty Kashishvara supports Advaita,
and Ramananda, seeing it, rejoices.[^74]

## 24. Ramananda — "hari hari aiche ki hoyaba hāmāra…" (PK 3057)
*The poet's prayer: "Hari, Hari! Will I have such fortune — to see Gaura's pastimes with His companions in Nadia: dance and
kirtana on the bank of the Ganga, the Lord in His place in Srivasa's house — Nityananda on the right holding the parasol,
Gadadhara on the left; someone will take me there, and I shall see His moonlike face; and when, calling His mother, He comes
home to dine…" In PK it stands among the prayer-padas (prarthana). Signature: "Ramananda". The raga is the same as that of
the preceding pada.*[^78]

**24.1.** *(Refrain)* Hari, Hari! Will I have such [fortune] —
joyfully, together with the companions, Lord Gaura's
pastimes in Nadia to behold?

**24.2.** On the bank of the Ganga, in the taste of dancing, my Lord
will sport in kirtana —
shall I see it, filling my eyes?
My long-held desire will be fulfilled.

**24.3.** When in Srivasa's house, with His own,
He sits in His place,
Nityananda on the right holding the parasol,
Pandita Gadadhara on the left —

**24.4.** then someone will take me and lead me there,
and I shall see that moonlike face;
my whole body will be covered with thrills,
and I shall attain the bliss of prema.

**24.5.** When, calling His mother, He comes home
and sits to eat and drink —
what Ramananda will behold in bliss!
Then my two eyes will not have been in vain.[^79]

bhāgya hoyaba hāmāra" — "will I have such good fortune"). On the attribution see note 24. A prayer in the form of a longing
to see the pastimes of Navadvipa is common among later poets (cf. the prarthanas of Narottama Dasa); but it may also be the
word of a contemporary yearning after the sannyasa for the days of Navadvipa. MSS "ka", "kha", "ca", P-R-Sa in 24.1: "to me"
(āmāra for hāmāra).
[^79]: "Calling His mother" (janani sambodhane) — MS "ka": "with address to the mother" (sambodhana). GPT1: "…then I shall see
