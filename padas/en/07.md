# Praise of Nitai and Chaitanya, the Appearance of Nityananda and Gaura, Nimai's Childhood, the Abhisheka, Kirtanas, the Sannyasa, Nilachala, the Deities at Ambika, Prayers

*Padas of contemporaries known from the *Padakalpataru* (PK), in the order of the lilas: praise of Nitai and Chaitanya
and of the sankirtana (Krishnadasa); Nityananda's appearance at Ekachakra (Krishnadasa); Gaura's appearance and His
childhood play in Shachi's house (Vasu Ghosha); the Govardhana of bhakti in Navadvipa (Chaitanyadasa); the abhisheka in
Srivasa's house (Krishnadasa); the dance of the Lord hearing of Vrindavana (Ramachandra); Nityananda's dance (Vasu
Ramananda); separation in autumn (Krishnadasa); the sannyasi Lord on the seashore (Ramananda); Nilachala (Paramananda);
the Deities of Gaura and Nitai in Gauridasa's house at Ambika (Krishnadasa); and Krishnadasa's prayers. The text follows
PK, checked against the scans, with the variants of the *Bhakti-ratnakara* (BR) and the *Gaura-pada-tarangini* (GPT1 —
1st ed., 1903; GPT2 — 2nd ed., 1934); the Bengali original, variants and word-for-word translation are in the full version of the book.*

## 1. Krishnadasa — "prema-sindhu gorā rāy…" (PK 2343)
*Praise of Nitai and Chaitanya: Gaura is an ocean of prema, Nitai the wave upon it; Rupa and Sanatana, diving into this
ocean, bring up jewels and string them on the thread of bhakti; the kirtana of the lilas is a blossoming lotus forest.
In PK it stands in the 23rd pallava (the form and qualities of Chaitanya and Nityananda). Raga dhanashi.*[^1]

**1.1.** An ocean of prema is Gora Raya, Nitai the wave upon it,
on all four sides — the wind of compassion.
Prema surges and spills over — the world draws breath again:
everyone's heat and thirst are gone.[^2]

**1.2.** *(Refrain)* Behold, behold the merciful Nitai and Chaitanya!
To the swans and chakravakas — the devotees — They call: "Drink, drink!"
Having found such a thing, why stay deprived?[^3]

**1.3.** Diving, Rupa and Sanatana bring up many treasures, jewels,
and carefully string a garland of them.
Making the creeper of bhakti the thread — take it, souls, adorn your necks:
the scorching heat of the sun will go.[^4]

**1.4.** The sankirtana of the taste of the lilas is a blossoming lotus forest,
whose fragrance has filled the world.
The forest of flowers has bloomed, the bees are intoxicated —
yet Krishnadasa, having found it, has stayed deprived.[^5]

[^1]: Signature: "Krishnadasa". PK: vol. 3, PDF p. 319 (printed p. 308), 23rd pallava, no. 12 (mark "॥১২॥২৩৪৩॥"); raga
dhanashi; ms. P-R-Sa no. 2447. GPT1 — pp. 32–33, no. 19 (raga dhanashi); GPT2 — sect. 1.3, no. 19. Which Krishnadasa wrote
the pada is not established: PK gathers under this signature padas of different poets (including Krishnadasa Kaviraja);
the mention of Rupa and Sanatana dates it not earlier than 1514–1516. Majumdar does not discuss it.
[^2]: "The world draws breath again" (jagata hāphān chāṛe) — like one exhausted by the heat breathing with relief; ms.
"ka" and GPT1 — hākāla ("the world cries out"). Ms. "kha": "sin" (pāpa) instead of "heat".
[^3]: Hamsa-swans and chakravakas are water birds living on the ocean of prema; the image continues the ocean metaphor.
[^4]: Rupa and Sanatana Gosvami are disciples of the Lord whose books opened the treasures of bhakti. Ms. "gha": "the
thread of a book" (grantha) instead of "creeper". "The heat of the sun" is worldly suffering; GPT1: "your burning will go".
[^5]: The humility usual with Vaishnava poets: the poet considers himself deprived of what is being given to all.

## 2. Krishnadasa — "nabadvīpe śuni siṃhanāda…" (PK 2089)
*The sankirtana in Navadvipa as a battle: Gaurachandra is the great chariot-warrior, Nityananda the general, Advaita the
vanguard; the enemies are bound with a noose of the rope of prema; the Lord, mounted on the elephant of Kali, sets up
outposts on every side. In PK it stands in the 17th pallava (the dance and pastimes of Gauranga). Raga ramakeli.*[^6]

**2.1.** *(Refrain)* In Navadvipa a lion's roar was heard!
The Vaishnavas arrayed themselves, starting the sankirtana of Hari —
the fools took it for a calamity.[^7]

**2.2.** Gaurachandra is the great chariot-warrior, Nityananda the general,
Advaita at the head of the army.
With a noose of the rope of prema they bound many foes;
unceasingly thunders the name of Hari.

**2.3.** Sri Chaitanya wages the battle, mounted on the elephant of Kali,
in the array of a hero, the crusher of godlessness.
To deliver the souls of the Kali age the Lord came to the earth
and set up outposts on all four sides.[^8]

**2.4.** High and low — all obtained the treasure of prema
by a particle of the mercy of Nitai and Chaitanya.
But Krishnadasa, seeing Yama before him, grieves deeply:
he has not found even a trace of prema.[^9]

ramakeli; ms. P-R-Sa no. 2198; after the pada a PK rubric: here the pada "kali timira ghora…" is also sung. GPT1 — p. 274,
no. 85 (raga ramakeli); GPT2 — sect. 4.2. The catalogue's former first line ("kali-jība tarāite…") is the beginning of
the third stanza. Which Krishnadasa it is has not been established.
[^7]: "A lion's roar" is the thundering kirtana; "the fools" (mūṛhamati) are the opponents of the kirtana in Navadvipa
(cf. CB Madhya 23).
[^8]: "Mounted on the elephant of Kali" (kali-gaje ārohaṇa): the Kali age, subdued, serves the Lord as His war elephant;
mss. "ka", "kha", "ca": "mounted on an elephant" (kari-gaje). GPT1: "Nityananda is the charioteer" (sārathi) instead of
"the general". The Lord crushes godlessness (pāṣaṇḍa) not with weapons but with the holy name: cf. CC Adi 17 — the
Kazi subdued by the kirtana.
[^9]: Yama is the lord of death. The self-abasement usual with Vaishnava poets.

## 3. Krishnadasa — "jaya jaya mahāprabhu jaya gauracandra…" (PK 2847)
*A nama-kirtana: glory to Mahaprabhu, His parents, Navadvipa, the Ganga, the Lord's consorts and His devotees. In PK
it is the gaura-chandrika to the morning lila (32nd pallava, the "eightfold lila"). Raga "kau".*[^10]

**3.1.** Glory, glory to Mahaprabhu, glory to Gaurachandra!
Glory to Vishvambhara, glory to the ocean of mercy!

**3.2.** Glory to the Son of Shachi, glory to Pandita Nimai!
Glory to Mishra Purandara, glory to Mother Shachi!

**3.3.** Glory, glory to Navadvipa, glory to the Suradhuni!
Glory to Lakshmi and Vishnupriya, the Lord's consorts![^11]

**3.4.** Glory, glory to the devotees who dwell in Navadvipa!
Glory, glory to the feet of Nityananda and Advaita!

**3.5.** Ever hoping for the two feet of Nityananda,
Krishnadasa has sung this nama-sankirtana.[^12]

[^10]: Signature: "Krishnadasa". PK: vol. 4, PDF pp. 179–180 (printed pp. 170–171), 32nd pallava, "Now the morning
lila. Sri Gaurachandra", no. 10 (mark "॥১০॥২৮৪৭॥"); raga "kau" (an abbreviation; so also GPT1). GPT1 — pp. 22–23,
no. 62 (without couplet 3.3); GPT2 — sect. 1.2, no. 62. The attribution is not established; the hope placed in Nityananda's feet
in the signature points rather to a poet of Nityananda's branch than to Krishnadasa Kaviraja.
[^11]: Mishra Purandara is Jagannatha Mishra, the Lord's father. Suradhuni is the Ganga. Lakshmi is Lakshmipriya, the
Lord's first consort; Vishnupriya the second.
[^12]: Nama-sankirtana — the singing of the Lord's names.

## 4. Krishnadasa — "rāṛhadeśe nāma ekacakrā grāma…" (PK 1116)
*Nityananda's appearance: in Radha-desha, in the village of Ekachakra, in the house of Hadai Pandita, on the bright
thirteenth of Magha, Haladhara was born; Hadai celebrates his son's birth, the earth sways with joy; Advaita in
Shantipura perceives that Rama, Krishna's elder brother, has appeared. In PK: "Then the appearance of Shri
Nityananda-chandra" (3rd shakha, 18th pallava, the "janma-lila"). Raga shri.*[^13]

**4.1.** In Radha-desha, in the village named Ekachakra,
in the house of Hadai Pandita,
in the auspicious month of Magha, on the bright thirteenth,
Haladhara was born.[^14]

**4.2.** Hadai Pandita, filled with joy,
holds a festival for his son.
The circle of the earth sways,
unable to contain the bliss.

**4.3.** The Lord of Shantipura, rejoicing,
divined something:
in His heart He knew: surely there is born
Rama, the elder brother of Krishna.[^15]

**4.4.** The hearts of the Vaishnavas grew serene,
they float in an ocean of bliss.
"Even this wretched sinner will be delivered,"
says the unhappy Krishnadasa.[^16]

[^13]: Signature: "Krishnadasa". PK: vol. 2, PDF p. 265 (printed p. 252), 3rd shakha, 18th pallava (the "janma-lila"),
section "tataḥ śrīnityānandacandrābirbhābaḥ", no. 5 (mark "॥৫॥১১১৬॥"); raga shri; ms. P-R-Sa no. 1614. GPT1 — p. 416, no. 7
(raga shri); GPT2 — sect. 6.1, no. 7. In the catalogue the pada was listed as PK 1117 (PK 1117 is the next pada, below,
no. 5). Which Krishnadasa it is has not been established.
[^14]: Radha-desha (Rarh) is the region west of the Ganga. Hadai Pandita (Hadai Ojha) is Nityananda's father. Haladhara
("bearer of the plough") is Balarama: Nityananda is Balarama Himself. Magha is January–February; the same date is in the
*Advaita-prakasha*. Ms. "ka" and P-R-Sa: "Radha-desh" (without the locative ending).
[^15]: The Lord of Shantipura is Advaita Acharya. Rama is Balarama, Krishna's elder brother.
[^16]: "Unhappy" (dukhī) is the poet's humble name for himself.

## 5. Krishnadasa — "bhubana-ānanda-kanda balarāma nityānanda…" (PK 1117)
*Praise of Nityananda, descended in the Kali age: He is golden like the champaka, the nails of His fingers are a row of
moons, His face eclipses the full moon, His arms reach His knees; bee-devotees hover at His lotus feet. In PK it is the
next pada of the same section. Raga suhai.*[^17]

**5.1.** The root of the world's bliss, Balarama Nityananda,
has descended in the Kali age.
All sorrows are gone: seeing that moon-face,
people float on waves of bliss.[^18]

**5.2.** *(Refrain)* Glory, glory to Nityananda-Rama!
His lustre is that of the golden champaka, on His fingers a row of moons;
by His beauty He has conquered millions of Kamadevas.[^19]

**5.3.** Seeing that face, to what can the full moon compare?
Long eyes, eyebrows like bows.
Arms reaching the knees, palms like land-lotuses,
a waist slender as a lion's.[^20]

**5.4.** At His lotus feet the bee-devotees hover;
His half-words pour out nectar.
"Now the souls of this Kali age will be delivered,"
says the humble, unhappy Krishnadasa.[^21]

[^17]: Signature: "Krishnadasa". PK: vol. 2, PDF pp. 265–266 (printed pp. 252–253), 18th pallava, no. 6 (mark
"॥৬॥১১১৭॥"); raga suhai (P-R-Sa: shri); ms. P-R-Sa no. 1615. GPT1 — p. 416, no. 8 (raga suhai); GPT2 — sect. 6.1, no. 8.
Which Krishnadasa it is has not been established.
[^18]: Mss. "ka", "kha": haila (the same form, "descended"); P-R-Sa: "in an ocean of bliss" (sāgare) instead of "on waves".
[^19]: According to Ray's apparatus, the refrain is marked only in ms. P-R-Sa; in GPT1 too it stands after the first
stanza. Kamadeva is the god of love: the Lord's beauty surpasses that of millions of Kamadevas.
[^20]: "As a lion's" — literally "as the elephant's enemy's" (kari-ari); ms. "ka": airi, "ca": vairi (same meaning).
[^21]: Ms. "kha": "bringing nectar" (āniyā). Ms. "ka": "are delivered" (haila), P-R-Sa: haiba ("will be delivered");
GPT1: "the souls of the Kali age are all delivered".

## 6. Vasu Ghosha — "jaya jaya kalaraba nadiyā nagare…" (PK 1121; BR, p. 763)
*Gaura's appearance: in Nadia cries of "Jaya!" resound; at the full moon of Phalguna, under the Phalguni nakshatra,
Gora was born to Shachi; as in the Dvapara age Krishna appeared to Yashoda, so now He has appeared in Nadia to deliver
the souls of the Kali age. Narahari Chakravarti cites the pada in the *Bhakti-ratnakara* in the account of the Lord's
appearance. According to Majumdar, Vasu Ghosha's padas on the Lord's birth and childhood are poetic invention: Vasu was
not in Navadvipa at that time. Raga tudi (BR: basanta).*[^22]

**6.1.** Glory, glory! — cries resound in the city of Nadia:
Gora has appeared from Shachi's womb.[^23]

**6.2.** At the full moon of Phalguna, under the Phalguni nakshatra,
at an auspicious moment Gora was born, the jewel of the twice-born.

**6.3.** His radiance eclipsed the full moon —
the darkness, losing hope, went far away.[^24]

**6.4.** In the Dvapara age Krishna appeared in Nanda's house —
the whole world knows of His birth from Yashoda.

**6.5.** Now He has been born to Shachi in Nadia
to deliver all the souls of the Kali age.

**6.6.** So says Vasudeva Ghosha, cherishing hope in his heart,
trusting in his mind in the two feet of Gaura.[^25]

[^22]: Signature: "Vasudeva Ghosha". PK: vol. 2, PDF p. 267 (printed p. 254), 18th pallava, no. 10 (mark "॥১০॥১১২১॥");
raga tudi; ms. P-R-Sa no. 1619. BR, 12th wave: 1912/1913 eds. — p. 763 ("tathāhi gīte basantaḥ"), 1960 ed. — vv.
895–900; in BR lines 6.3–6.5 differ (see VAR). GPT1 — p. 51, no. 2 (raga tudi or karuna); GPT2 — sect. 2.1, no. 2.
Majumdar: Vasu's padas on the birth and childhood (PK 1121, 1150, 1151, 1161 and others) are the poet's invention; we
translate them as a poetic contemplation of lilas known from the biographies (CB Adi 2–5; CC Adi 13–14).
[^23]: "From Shachi's womb" is the ordinary language of the biographies about the Lord's appearance: the Lord is not born
like a conditioned soul but manifests Himself, as Krishna appeared to Devaki (Bhag. 10.3; CC Adi 13). BR: "Gorachand was
born".
[^24]: The Lord appeared during a lunar eclipse (CC Adi 13.89–98); the poet plays on this: the moon was dimmed before His
radiance.
[^25]: Vasudeva Ghosha is Vasu Ghosha. Mss. "ka", "kha", P-R-Sa: bharasā (the same). BR: "sings Vasudeva Ghosha…
trusting in the two feet of Lord Gora".

## 7. Vasu Ghosha — "gorā-rūpe ki diba tulanā…" (PK 1137)
*Gaura's form: neither gold of the highest assay, nor lightning in a cloud, nor a garland of champakas, nor the golden
ketaki, nor gorochana can compare with Him; the fragrance of His body surpasses kunkuma. In PK it is the gaura-chandrika
to the festival of Sri Radha's birth; no raga is given.*[^26]

**7.1.** To what shall I compare the form of Gora?
Even gold of the highest assay does not compare with it.[^27]

**7.2.** Lightning in a cloud is no likeness of this form;
a garland of champakas does not compare with it.

**7.3.** The petal of the golden ketaki does not compare,
pure gorochana does not compare.[^28]

**7.4.** The fragrance of His body, surpassing kunkuma, captivates the heart.
Says Vasu: out of what did the Creator fashion Gora?[^29]

[^26]: Signature: "Vasu". PK: vol. 2, PDF p. 274 (printed p. 261), 18th pallava, "Now the festival of Sri Radhika's birth.
There — Sri Gaurachandra", no. 11 (mark "॥১১॥১১৩৭॥"); according to Ray's apparatus — ms. P-R-Sa nos. 371 and 1641 and ms.
P-R no. 21 (the pada recurs in the manuscripts). GPT1 — p. 110, no. 1 (raga shri; the beginning of the section on the
Lord's form); GPT2 — sect. 3.1, no. 1. According to Majumdar, PK 1030 = 1137 (the pada stands twice in PK); he counts it
among Vasu's padas on Gaura's form.
[^27]: "Gold of the highest assay" (kaṣila bāna sonā) — gold tested on the touchstone; P-R: another reading (kaṣida kāṃcā).
Mss. "ka", "kha", "ca" and GPT1: "likeness" (upamā) instead of "comparison".
[^28]: Ketaki is the screw-pine with golden flowers; gorochana is a bright yellow pigment. P-R-Sa and P-R, instead of
"golden": "with the form" (rūpera, rūpe).
[^29]: Kunkuma is saffron. P-R-Sa, P-R: "the form, so captivating" (rūpa ati) instead of "the fragrance of the body".

## 8. Vasu Ghosha — "eka mukhe ki kahaba gorācāndera līlā…" (PK 1141)
*The infant Gaura crawls about the courtyard: saliva trickles down His little face, His lips are redder than a ripe
bimba, on His little arms are bracelets, on His feet anklets, on His neck a tiger's claw amulet, on His back a golden
chain with a silken tassel. In PK: "Now parental love befitting infancy. Sri Gaurachandra" (the beginning of the 19th
pallava). Raga tudi.*[^30]

**8.1.** With one mouth how can I tell the lilas of Gorachand?
Shachi's Child crawls on all fours, starting all kinds of games.[^31]

**8.2.** Saliva trickles down His little face — how lovely it is!
His lips are redder than a ripe bimba fruit.[^32]

**8.3.** On His lovely little arms are armlets and bracelets;
on His feet makara-headed anklets, on His neck a tiger's claw.[^33]

**8.4.** On His back a golden chain with a silken tassel.
Says Vasudeva Ghosha: "I give my very self for Him."[^34]

bātsalyaṃ kaumārakālocitaṃ yathā. śrīgauracandraḥ", no. 1 (mark "॥১॥১১৪১॥"); raga tudi; ms. P-R-Sa no. 1648. GPT1 — p. 62,
no. 2 (raga tudi); GPT2 — sect. 2.2, no. 2. Among Vasu's childhood padas Majumdar names "PK 1140" — evidently PK 1141:
PK 1140 is a pada of Uddhava Dasa on Radha's birth. On his assessment see note 22.
[^31]: P-R-Sa instead of the second line: "crawling, He plays in all kinds of ways".
[^32]: Mss. "ka", "ca": nāle (the same, "saliva"). Mss. "ka", "kha", "gha", "ca": "lovely" (sundara) instead of "red"
(suraṅga). The bimba is a fruit with a bright red skin, a common comparison for lips.
[^33]: Armlets (aṅgada) and bracelets (balayā), makara-headed anklets (magarā khāṛu), the tiger's claw (bāghanakha) are
common ornaments and amulets of children in Bengal.
[^34]: "I give my very self" (nichani āpanā) — the nichhani rite: one waves something around a beloved one and gives it
away (here — oneself) for his welfare.

## 9. Vasu Ghosha — "kiye hāma pekhaluṃ kanaka-putaliyā…" (PK 1150; BR, p. 774)
*The boy Gaura dances in Shachi's courtyard, all covered with dust, amid naked boys, crying "Hari, Hari!"; His mother
listens to the jingling of His anklet bells. Narahari Chakravarti cites the pada in the *Bhakti-ratnakara*: Srinivasa,
in Navadvipa, is told how Vishvambhara danced in this courtyard. In PK: "Now parental love befitting childhood and
boyhood. Sri Gaurachandra". Raga mayur (BR: kamoda).*[^35]

**9.1.** What a golden doll I have seen!
In Shachi's courtyard He dances, all grey with dust.[^36]

**9.2.** On all sides naked boys surround Him,
and in their midst Gora dances, saying "Hari, Hari!"

**9.3.** On reddish lotus feet the jewel of the twice-born runs about;
His mother listens with joy to the jingling of His anklet bells.[^37]

**9.4.** Says Vasudeva Ghosha, who knows the taste of the childhood play:
"Blessed are the people of Nadia, blessed is Navadvipa!"[^38]

[^36]: "What… I have seen" (kiye hāma pekhaluṃ) is a Vrajabuli turn of phrase.

## 10. Vasu Ghosha — "śacīra āṅgināya nāce biśvambhara rāy…" (PK 1151; BR, p. 774)
*Hide-and-seek: Vishvambhara dances in the courtyard and, laughing, hides from His mother, covering His face with the

He skips along with the gait of a wagtail. In BR it comes right after the previous pada. The same raga (BR: vibhasa).*[^39]

**10.1.** In Shachi's courtyard Vishvambhara Raya dances;
laughing, turning about, He hides from His mother.

**10.2.** Covering His face with His cloth, He says: "I have hidden!"
Shachi says: "Vishvambhara, I cannot see You!"[^40]

**10.3.** Holding the end of His mother's sari, on restless little feet
He goes skipping with the gait of a wagtail.[^41]

**10.4.** Says Vasudeva Ghosha: "A wondrous beauty!
Seeing the form of the Child, the world is filled with longing."

## 11. Vasu Ghosha — "gorā nāce śacīra dulāliyā…" (PK 1161; BR, pp. 774–775)
*Shachi's darling Gora dances, the boys around Him clap their hands: "Haribol!"; on His head a red cap, on His neck golden
beads, His mother has lovingly tied His little dhoti; the poet exclaims: "Take Him in your arms — Gora is the life of my
life!" In BR it is the third pada of the same series. In PK: "Now the rasa of parental love, another mode. Shri
Gaurachandra" (the beginning of the 20th pallava). Raga bhatiyari (BR: bhatyali).*[^42]

**11.1.** *(Refrain)* Gora dances, Shachi's darling;
the boys have gathered around, all clap their hands,
saying "Haribol, Haribol!"

**11.2.** On His head a red cap, on His neck golden beads;
with love His mother has tied His little dhoti tight.[^43]

**11.3.** Lovely curling hair, a well-formed body;
a dress that charms the world, eyebrows — Kamadeva's bow.

**11.4.** Silver and golden ornaments
adorn His body charmingly.
As He lifts His feet — red lotuses —
the anklet bells ring.

**11.5.** In Shachi's courtyard He dances and dances,
babbling half-words.
Says Vasudeva Ghosha: "Hold Him, hold Him, take Him in your arms —
Gora is the life of my life!"[^44]

## 20. Krishnadasa — "dekha dui bhāi gaura nitāi…" (PK 1576)
*The abhisheka of Gaura and Nitai in Gauridasa's house: the two brothers sit on the altar as if the moon and the sun had

**20.1.** Behold: the two brothers, Gaura and Nitai,

**20.4.** Having bathed and dressed Them,

**20.5.** Having fed Them with all kinds of offerings,
at the end — betel and sandal;
putting on flower garlands and performing arati,
Krishnadasa bowed down [before Them].

## 21. Krishnadasa — "śrībṛndābana nāma ratna-cintāmaṇi-dhāma…" (PK 2360)

*A prayer of repentance: Nitai and Chaitanya are the greatest avataras, none will be more generous than They; They

**22.4.** However many avataras have come into the world —

**22.5.** Not worshipping the two feet of such a Lord,

**22.6.** The world drowned in the prema of the gaura-kirtana —

**22.7.** Krishnadasa weeps, tearing his hair:

## 24. Krishnadasa — "jaya jaya nityānanda rāy…" (PK 3006)

**24.1.** *(Refrain)* Glory, glory to Nityananda Raya!
