# Nagari Padas — VII: the "Narahari" padas of GPT (sect. 3.2, nos. 169–180; sects. 2.4, 3.1, 5.1, 5.2): the quarrels of the heavenly women, the townswomen's words at the Lord's wedding; concluding note

*On what nagari-bhava is, the position of the tradition (Vrindavana Dasa, Krishnadasa Kaviraja, Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati) and the rules of translation, see the introductory note to the section of nagari padas in portion 27 (`en/27.md`); on the two poets named Narahari (Narahari Sarakara and Narahari Chakravarti) and on the attribution labels ("probably Sarakara", "probably Chakravarti", "not established"), see the introductory note to the "Narahari" appendix in portion 17 (`en/17.md`).*

## 1. Narahari — "vrajapure rasavilāsa viśeṣa…" (GPT1 p. 233, no. 169; GCC 10.25)
*Nagari-bhava (the heavenly women's talk). The heavenly woman who closed the dispute (portion 32, no. 23) tells her friend in confidence: who can
tell the special plays of rasa in Vraja? Yet He is the very same, and with all the same dear companions He ever sports in Nadia, absorbed in singing of
His own virtues; first giving His own the nectar of His beauty to drink, He made them as if mad, and the young noble wives, bidding farewell to family
and shame, made Gora their very essence. Raga "yatharaga". **Attribution: probably Chakravarti** — GCC, kirana 10, pada 25.*[^1]

**1.1.** "The special plays of rasa in the city of Vraja — who can tell them all? Keep it secret; attend to what I tell you, counting you as my own.

**1.2.** This is He, and He is this: taking with Him all the same dear companions, He ever sports in the city of Nadia, absorbed in singing of His own virtues.

**1.3.** First giving His own the nectar of the wondrous sweetness of His form to drink, He made their minds and their steps as if mad — and they count nothing else.

**1.4.** Ever new noble wives, giving the last libation to family, to the family's disgrace and to shame, have all made their essence Gora, full of joy, the Beloved of their life — Narahari is witness to it."[^2]

[^1]: Signature — "Narahari" (narahari sāthī — "Narahari the witness", cf. portion 31, 13.10). GPT1 — p. 233, no. 169 (scan PDF p. 492); GPT2 — sect. 3.2, no. 169 (catalogue: NG-128).
GCC — kirana 10, pada 25 (ed. 1947, p. 130; scan PDF p. 135), "yathārāga". **Who speaks.** The tenth kirana of GCC is the morning talk of the heavenly women (devaramaṇī)
looking down on Nadia from the sky (portion 32, note 24); padas 10.25–10.26 (nos. 1–2 of this portion) close their talk: one tells another "in confidence", and the other asks
to be taught how to come and live in Nadia. GCC readings: "gupate rākhiyā dio cita yāhā kahiye āpana jāniyā tore" ("keeping it secret, attend…"), "nija guṇagaṇe"
("absorbed in His own virtues"). B. Nagari-bhava — see the introductory note to the section (portion 27).
[^2]: "This is He, and He is this; with all the same companions" (1.2): that Gaura is Krishna Himself and that Krishna's companions came with Him to Navadvipa is the
teaching of the tradition (CC, Adi 1 and 3–4; Kavi Karnapura's "Gaura-ganoddesha-dipika" names who among Gaura's companions was whom in Vraja). But the gopis in
Gaura-lila are not townswomen of Nadia in love with Gaura: the Lord Himself came in Radha's bhava to taste Her love (CC, Adi 4), and therefore the Gaudiya tradition
does not accept the speaker's conclusion — that "the young wives of Nadia made Gora, as a nagara, their essence" (introductory note, portion 27). Rendered as in the
original. "Absorbed in singing of His own virtues" — cf. CB, Madhya (the Lord in bhavavesha sings of Himself). "To give the last libation" (tilāñjali) — to abandon for good.

## 2. Narahari — "gaurāṅgacām̐dera sucāru carita…" (GPT1 p. 233, no. 170; GCC 10.26)
*Nagari-bhava (the heavenly women's talk). The listener, hearing of Gaurangachand's lovely deeds, is filled with joy and cannot hold back tears of
love; joining her palms, she asks to be taught how she might once come to live in Nadia and be a companion of its young playful women, keep Narahari's
Beloved in her heart and be rid of the cruel barrier. Raga "yatharaga". **Attribution: probably Chakravarti** — GCC, kirana 10, pada 26.*[^3]

**2.1.** Hearing and hearing of Gaurangachand's lovely deeds, the fair one was filled with joy; she cannot hold her composure — again and again both eyes fill with tears of love.

**2.2.** Joining her palms and bowing, she again speaks soft, sweet words: "Counting me as your own, at last you have, as it were, taken all the pain from my heart.

**2.3.** I pray: how might I once dwell in this city of Nadia? And how might I live as a companion of these ever new playful beauties?

**2.4.** Keeping the Beloved of Narahari's life within my heart, I shall be rid of the cruel barrier. Tell me, tell me: by what means will all these wishes be fulfilled?"

[^3]: Signature — "Narahari" (narahari prāṇapiyā — "the Beloved of Narahari's life", glossary). GPT1 — p. 233, no. 170 (scan PDF p. 492); GPT2 — sect. 3.2, no. 170
(catalogue: NG-362). GCC — kirana 10, pada 26 (ed. 1947, p. 130; scan PDF p. 135). 2.4 "be fulfilled" follows GPT2 and GCC "saphala" (GPT1 "sakala", a misprint); "barrier" —
GPT1 and GCC "bādhā" (GPT2 "dhām̐dhā", "bewilderment"). The wish of heavenly beings to be born where the Lord reveals His lila is a commonplace of the hagiographies (CB,
Adi: the gods long to be born in Navadvipa to share in the Lord's sankirtana); the "cruel barrier" is that one living in the city of the gods cannot come to Nadia. The
closing pada of the kirana (GCC 10.27) is not in GPT: the fair one told her friend all she had asked, and the heavenly women, trembling and winking at one another,
gave themselves to merry play, gazing at Narahari's Lord; the colophon: "the description of the play of the heavenly women filled with love for Him". B. Nagari-bhava —
see the introductory note to the section (portion 27).

## 3. Narahari — "surapura mājhe vasati kariyā…" (GPT1 pp. 233–234, no. 171; GCC 11.4)
*An answer: defence of the chastity of the women of Nadia (the heavenly women's talk; by content not nagari-bhava). The opening of the eleventh
kirana of GCC — "the love-quarrels of the heavenly women". A heavenly woman who has just arrived angrily rebukes her friends: living in the city of the
gods, they have grown proud and slander the women of Nadia; what they describe happens only with the young wives of Vraja, while the wives of Nadia are
faithful to their husbands, do not see another man's face even in a dream, revere their elders; whoever sings of others' disgrace is disgraced. Raga
"yatharaga". **Attribution: probably Chakravarti** — GCC, kirana 11, pada 4.*[^4]

**3.1.** "Living in the city of the gods, why have you grown so proud? You feel no fear at heart in slandering the women of Nadia!

**3.2.** Alas, alas! Hearing such perverse words, how can I bear it? I do not know what wrong these women of Nadia have done to you all.

**3.3.** You judge others by your own ways and do not know the secret of others' hearts; you understand nothing — what comes of what; so I say: in vain do you bear a body.

**3.4.** What you describe is possible only with the young wives in the city of Vraja; but all the young wives of Nadia are the most faithful of the faithful — the whole world knows it.

**3.5.** They do not see another husband's face even in a dream, never walk an evil road; they know no tricks, no deceit, no guile — all of them always simple of heart.

**3.6.** Steadfast, skilful in their work, of lovely conduct — always on the highest path of dharma; mindful of the matchless shame of family and of fear, never, forgetting themselves, do they sit with a bad man.

**3.7.** They all love their elders as their life; their auspicious virtues cannot be counted. In my heart there is one thing: always to keep them with care in my eyes.

**3.8.** So I tell you: knowing that you are my neighbours, you will surely accept these words — I say nothing else. Whoever sings of another's disgrace is herself disgraced: this Narahari knows."[^5]

[^4]: Signature — "Narahari". GPT1 — pp. 233–234, no. 171 (scan PDF pp. 492–493); GPT2 — sect. 3.2, no. 171 (catalogue: NG-129). GCC — kirana 11, pada 4 (ed. 1947, pp. 132–133;
scan PDF pp. 137–138), "yathārāga". **Who speaks.** The eleventh kirana of GCC is headed "Again in the morning — the love-quarrels and so on of the heavenly women"; its
opening is not in GPT: from the sky the heavenly women drink the nectar of Gaura's form as He plays in Shachi's courtyard and argue — some say that the nagaris of
Nadia "have gone mad for the Beloved" and will bring disgrace on themselves, others answer that the wives of Vraja did the same (GCC 11.1); a short pada on the heavenly
women's rapture (11.2); praise of the love of Nadia's young wives — and then "a heavenly woman arrived and, waving her hand, said angrily to all" (11.3). GCC readings:
"nā jāniyā" (3.2), "tem̐i bali" (3.3). B. Nagari-bhava — see the introductory note to the section (portion 27); this pada answers it.
[^5]: The defender sets Vraja against Nadia: a love that takes no account of husbands and of talk "is possible only with the young wives of Vraja" (3.4), while the women
of Nadia are faithful wives. Gaudiya theology agrees: the parakiya-rasa of the gopis belongs to Vraja (CC, Adi 4), and in Navadvipa the Lord did not accept the worship
of women as a nagara (CB, Adi). The pada is therefore by content not nagari-bhava (like nos. 19–20 of portion 32); in the heavenly women's dispute, however, her words
will be mocked (nos. 4–10). "Neighbours" (sahavāsī, 3.8) — those who live with her in the city of the gods.

## 4. Narahari — "bhāla bhāla ihā śikhāte habe nā…" (GPT1 p. 234, no. 172; GCC 11.6)
*Nagari-bhava (the heavenly women's quarrel). A mocker answers the defender: they need no teaching — as if they did not know: on earth only the
wives of Nadia are faithful, and in the city of the gods only she; they themselves, forever singing of others' disgrace, will be disgraced, but will not
make her a sharer; how can so clever a woman live among the unchaste? Let her go to Nadia with her dharma and her shame and live among the chaste.
Raga "yatharaga". **Attribution: probably Chakravarti** — GCC, kirana 11, pada 6.*[^6]

**4.1.** "Well, well! No need to teach us this — as if I did not know it all! On earth only the wives of Nadia are faithful to their husbands, and in the city of the gods only you!

**4.2.** We, ever singing of others' disgrace, shall all be disgraced — do not you worry about that: we shall not make you a sharer in it.

**4.3.** Besides, how will you, the cleverest of women, live alone in the city of the gods? Living with the unchaste, you will easily become unchaste yourself.

**4.4.** So I say: go to this city of Nadia, taking your dharma, your shame and the rest. Narahari is glad of it: be always careful, living among the chaste."

[^6]: Signature — "Narahari". GPT1 — p. 234, no. 172 (scan PDF p. 493); GPT2 — sect. 3.2, no. 172 (catalogue: NG-363). GCC — kirana 11, pada 6 (ed. 1947, p. 133; scan PDF p. 138);
before it, the short pada 11.5 (not in GPT): hearing this, a heavenly woman versed in rasa laughed inwardly with her own group, exchanged signs with them and spoke.
4.1 — GPT1 "nā jāni e āmi", GCC "nā jāniye āmi" (GPT2 "jānie āmi", "I know"): the gibe — "as if I did not know"; 4.2 GCC "ihāra bhāgī kakhana nā karite yāba"; 4.4 GCC "tāhe bali".
The tone of the pada is mocking — rendered as in the original. B. Nagari-bhava — see the introductory note to the section (portion 27).

## 5. Narahari — "hera āisa ogo pativratā saha…" (GPT1 p. 234, no. 173; GCC 11.7)
*Nagari-bhava (the heavenly women's quarrel). Another calls her friend: why talk with a "faithful wife"? Joy is among equals; not all heavenly women
are adroit — as the owl does not see the sun; the secret stories of Nadia's young wives she will not reveal for shame; but our Gora — beautiful, good —
speaks to no one with raised face, is always careful on the path of dharma, knows nothing of such things — and such a Lord of Narahari they bewitched
with signs from the corners of their eyes. Raga "yatharaga". **Attribution: probably Chakravarti** — GCC, kirana 11, pada 7.*[^7]

**5.1.** "Come here, friend: why say all this to a 'faithful wife'? Among equals joy arises; with unequals pain grows.

**5.2.** Being born heavenly women — are all of us then adroit? Never think so: as the owl does not see the sun — so, know, are many.

**5.3.** The young wives of Nadia — the young and the experienced — who equals them in the world? Their secret stories I cannot reveal for shame.

**5.4.** Look, look here: our Gora, the life of our life, beautiful and good, speaks to no one with raised face; wondrous is His way, He is wholly absorbed within.

**5.5.** Always careful on the path of dharma — what shall I say! — He knows nothing of all this. And such a Lord of Narahari they bewitched, signalling from the corners of their eyes."[^8]

[^7]: Signature — "Narahari" (naraharinātha — "Narahari's Lord"). GPT1 — p. 234, no. 173 (scan PDF p. 493); GPT2 — sect. 3.2, no. 173 (catalogue: NG-130; the catalogue's "paratatratā" is an
OCR error for "pativratā"). GCC — kirana 11, pada 7 (ed. 1947, p. 134; scan PDF p. 139), "anyā ca — yathārāga" ("and another"). 5.1 in GCC "ki lāgi kahicha" — "why are you
saying all this to a faithful wife". "The owl does not see the sun" (5.2) — a proverb for blindness to the obvious. B. Nagari-bhava — see the introductory note to the
section (portion 27).
[^8]: The Lord's portrait in 5.4–5.5 is the same as in the hagiographies: He speaks to no one with raised face and is "always careful on the path of dharma" (CB, Adi: in this
incarnation the Lord did not look at women and allowed no jesting with them; cf. nos. 19–20 of portion 32). The words "they bewitched Him" are the speaker's slander of
the women of Nadia, rendered as in the original; the tradition's answer — introductory note (portion 27).

## 6. Narahari — "ki baliba ogo nadīyāra nava…" (GPT1 p. 235, no. 174; GCC 11.8)
*Nagari-bhava (the heavenly women's quarrel). Another: the young wives of Nadia do not reveal their secrets, are always gracious outwardly, well-behaved
before mother-in-law and sister-in-law, yet gain their ends so that no one notices, and secretly try to win the Moon of Nadia; she herself saw how they
bewitched Him — and there was no one to restrain the women: Narahari was not with Gauranga. Raga "yatharaga". **Attribution: probably Chakravarti** —
GCC, kirana 11, pada 8.*[^9]

**6.1.** "What shall I say, friend, of the ways of the young wives of Nadia! They do not reveal what is within, but outwardly they are always most gracious.

**6.2.** Before mother-in-law and sister-in-law they always behave well; whatever everyone praises, that they do with great care.

**6.3.** In many ways they gain their ends — no one can ever notice; to win the Moon of Nadia they go about most secretly.

**6.4.** With my own eyes I saw that day how, with so many graceful poses, they bewitched [Him]. Who would restrain the women? Narahari was not with Gauranga."

[^9]: Signature — "Narahari", merged with the speech (6.4). GPT1 — p. 235, no. 174 (scan PDF p. 494); GPT2 — sect. 3.2, no. 174 (catalogue: NG-131). GCC — kirana 11, pada 8 (ed. 1947,
p. 134; scan PDF p. 139), "anyā ca — yathārāga". "Narahari was not with Gauranga": in GCC the poet usually accompanies the Lord and leads Him away from what is improper
(GCC 11.11, 11.14 — see notes 18 and 19); here the speaker seems to explain why this time "there was no one to restrain the women". As in no. 5, the words about
"bewitching" are the speaker's surmise; in the following padas (nos. 7–8) the mockers themselves describe how the Lord turns away and leaves. B. Nagari-bhava — see the
introductory note to the section (portion 27).

## 7. Narahari — "nadīyāte kata kata e kautuka…" (GPT1 p. 235, no. 175; GCC 11.9)
*Nagari-bhava (the heavenly women's quarrel). The next one confirms it mockingly: she knows well how "faithful" these young chaste ones are: the other
day, standing under a young kadamba, she saw Gora walking alone in the late afternoon toward Murari Gupta's quarter; the "faithful wives" were coming
along with a fearsome mother-in-law and sister-in-law — and yet, finding Gorachand on a near path, they contrived to stand in His way, signalled to Him
and did not keep the end of the sari over the breast; and Narahari's Lord, lowering His face in shame, stepped aside. Raga "yatharaga". **Attribution:
probably Chakravarti** — GCC, kirana 11, pada 9.*[^10]

**7.1.** "How many such pranks there are in Nadia — how many will you tell! And how 'faithful to their husbands' all these young chaste ones are — that I know.

**7.2.** That day I saw with my own eyes, standing under a young kadamba: Gora was walking alone in the late afternoon toward Murari Gupta's quarter.

**7.3.** At that hour the 'faithful wives' were coming with a fearsome mother-in-law and sister-in-law — and still, finding Gorachand on a near path, they stood there on a pretext, swaying their bodies.

**7.4.** Signalling to Him again and again, luring Him, not keeping even half the end of the sari over the breast… And Narahari's Lord, lowering His face in shame, stepped aside and stood far off."[^11]

## 8. Narahari — "ki kahiba ogo e sakala kathā…" (GPT1 p. 235, no. 176; GCC 11.10)

**8.4.** One, holding a fine pomegranate in her hand, says: 'To whom shall I give this wondrous [fruit]?' Another says: 'He whose body is like new gold will take it Himself, unasked.'

## 10. Narahari — "surapure kevā nā jāne nadīyā…" (GPT1 p. 236, no. 178; GCC 11.17)
*Nagari-bhava (the heavenly women's quarrel). The defender, frightened, has fallen silent (GCC 11.16); then a playful one, glancing at her own group, says:
in the city of the gods everyone knows the ways of the nagaris of Nadia — why be vainly angry with her? What she said is secret; and taking her hand and
praising her, she goes on gently: the ways of Nadia's young wives are past counting; no one looks at the bracelet on her own hand in a mirror — here is the
jewel in Ai's house: if she came on the astrologer's word, why does she stare unblinking at Gora, playing with her brows, tremble, let her clothes and
ornaments slip, break into gooseflesh and sweat? Narahari's Lord is the husband of them all. Raga "yatharaga". **Attribution: probably Chakravarti** —
GCC, kirana 11, pada 17.*[^15]

**10.1.** "In the city of the gods who does not know what the ways of the nagaris of Nadia are? Why then are you so vainly angry with her?

**10.2.** What shall I say: what she said is most secret; no one knows it. Have patience, all of you, and listen with care to what I say."

**10.3.** So, glancing at her own group, she quickly took her hand, praised her in many words and speaks very softly, full of rasa:

**10.4.** "The ways of the young wives of Nadia — who can tell how many? Many days I have seen many pranks with my own eyes — and I tell no one.

**10.5.** Let that be; no one looks at the bracelet on her own hand in a mirror: look here — the jewel in Ai's house: why has she come running in the morning?

**10.6.** If you say: on the auspicious word of the astrologer she came to Ai on her own business — then why, playing with her brows, does she gaze unblinking at Gora?

**10.7.** And her body trembles again and again, her clothes and ornaments slip, the knot of her hair comes loose; she is all gooseflesh, sweat trickles, her champa-earrings sway again and again.

**10.8.** Who does such things — say, say? Why should anyone not believe it? Narahari's Lord is the husband of them all: have no doubt of this."[^16]

## Concluding note on the "Narahari" nagari padas (portions 29–33)

*1. **How many.** In the section of nagari padas, 102 padas signed "Narahari" from the "Gaura-pada-tarangini" have been translated: portion 29 — 15 (Narahari Chakravarti's
pada NG-079 from the cycle of the twelfth wave of the "Bhakti-ratnakara"; GPT1 3.2 nos. 39, 40, 42–45; nos. 86–93), portion 30 — 21 (nos. 94–110, 120–123), portion 31 — 23 (nos. 119,
124–145), portion 32 — 23 (nos. 146–168), portion 33 — 20 (nos. 169–180; 2.4 nos. 2, 3, 7, 21; 3.1 nos. 129–130; 5.1 no. 12; 5.2 no. 39). Of the 95 padas signed Narahari in section
3.2 of GPT, 94 are translated here — all except no. 41 (= PK 103 of Narahari Sarakara, translated in the main part, portion 03); the other 8 are nagari padas from sections 2.4,
3.1, 5.1, 5.2. No. 119 (Gaura in sleep speaks Krishna's words) is by content not nagari-bhava, as are the rebuttals within the heavenly women's dispute (portion 32, nos. 19–20;
portion 33, no. 3).*

*2. **Attribution.** "Probably Chakravarti" (B) — 81 padas: 75 are found in Narahari Chakravarti's "Gaura-carita-cintamani" (GCC, ed. 1947) — padas 2.13; 7.2–7.12, 7.14–7.16,
7.29–7.30; 8.2–8.20; 9.1–9.2, 9.6–9.16, 9.22; 10.8–10.10, 10.12–10.17, 10.19, 10.21–10.22, 10.24–10.26; 11.4, 11.6–11.10, 11.14 (two padas in GPT), 11.15, 11.17 — and 6 occur only in the
"Bhakti-ratnakara" (NG-079; 2.4 nos. 2, 3, 21; 5.1 no. 12; 5.2 no. 39). "Not established" or "probably Sarakara" (C) — 21 padas: six short padas GPT1 3.2 nos. 39, 40, 42–45 in plain Bengali
(the GPT2 index assigns them to Sarakara; there are no other witnesses; no. 43 — "not established") and fifteen padas nos. 100–110, 120–123, which stand in GPT in the place of the
lost pages of the only available scan of GCC (pp. 81–88 = GCC 7.17–7.28; apparently also from GCC). Not one "Narahari" nagari pada of GPT is confirmed by PK or by other early
collections as a song of Narahari Sarakara, the Lord's companion; almost all of them are Narahari Chakravarti's poetic elaboration of the nagari motif (first half of the
18th century). The collation confirms Majumdar's argument: the long padas of GPT with this signature do not belong to Sarakara.*

*3. **The composition of GCC 7–11 in GPT.** Kiranas 7–11 of the "Gaura-carita-cintamani" describe morning in Navadvipa: the townswomen of Nadia talk of Gaura and of how,
deceiving their strict elders, they saw Him (kirana 7), tell their dreams (8), go to Shachi's house and dream of meeting Him (9); then the heavenly women look down on Nadia
from the sky — praise its nagaris, argue about Gaura (10) and quarrel among themselves (11). These five kiranas have 121 padas (12 of them on the lost pages of the scan);
the compiler of GPT took at least 78 and distributed them among his sections:
— kirana 7: 7.1 — in sect. 2.2 (GPT1 p. 68, no. 19; portion 19); 7.2–7.12, 7.14–7.16 = 3.2 nos. 86–99 (no. 97 is only the second half of 7.14); 7.29–7.30 = nos. 124–125; not taken:
7.13 and the closing 7.31; 7.17–7.28 are lost in the scan (in their place — nos. 100–110, 120–123);
— kirana 8: 8.2–8.20 = nos. 126–144; not taken: the opening 8.1 and the closing 8.21;
— kirana 9: 9.1–9.2 = nos. 145–146; 9.6–9.16 = nos. 147–157 (9.12 = no. 150 stands earlier in GPT); 9.18–9.19, 9.21–9.22 — in sect. 2.4 ("the second marriage", nos. 4–7: where they
also stand in BR's wedding narrative); not taken: 9.3–9.5, 9.17, 9.20 and the closing prayer 9.23;
— kirana 10: 10.7 — GPT1 p. 494, no. 30 (portion 18, no. 28); 10.8–10.9 — in sect. 3.1, "form" (nos. 129–130); 10.10, 10.12–10.17, 10.19, 10.21–10.22, 10.24–10.26 = 3.2 nos. 158–170;
not taken: the opening 10.1–10.6, 10.11, 10.18, 10.20, 10.23 and the closing 10.27;
— kirana 11: 11.4, 11.6–11.10, 11.14, 11.15, 11.17 = nos. 171–180 (in a different order; 11.14 split into nos. 179–180); not taken: the opening 11.1–11.3, the links 11.5, 11.16, the
padas 11.11–11.13 and the close 11.18–11.19. Besides these, GCC 2.13 (Gaura's words in sleep) stands in GPT 3.2 (no. 119).*

*4. **Who speaks.** In GCC the padas of kiranas 7–9 are the words of the townswomen of Nadia (and of a mother-in-law who sends her daughters-in-law to Shachi, 9.2); the padas
of kiranas 10–11 are the words of heavenly women (devaramaṇī), who only watch Nadia from the sky and long to be born there. In GPT the openings, colophons and links of
the kiranas are left out, and all the padas of section 3.2 read as the words of townswomen; but traces remain in the GPT text itself — "you, connoisseurs of rasa in the city of
the gods", "in the city of the gods a rumour has arisen", "living in the city of the gods, why have you grown so proud", "how will you live alone in the city of the gods" (portion
32, nos. 17, 20; portion 33, nos. 3, 4, 10). Therefore 25 padas of GPT (3.2 nos. 158–180 and 3.1 nos. 129–130) are translated with the label "the heavenly women's talk".*

*5. **Where GPT softens GCC.** The compiler of GPT did not take the most outspoken padas (10.11 — one heavenly woman's guess at where Gaura spent the night; 11.11–11.13 —
the antics of women at the ghat and on the road), left out single lines (8.15; the couplet in 11.10 about a shadow touching Gora's body) and replaced single words (8.6 "lay
down" → "sat"; 8.11, 8.14, 8.16 — mentions of Madana, e.g. 8.14 "rapt with Madana" → "rapt with joy"; 8.18 — "breast"; 9.6 — "under Madana's weight"; 9.14 "the cloth over
the breast" → "the cloth over the face"; 9.15 "wants to embrace" → "wants to see your dear face"). Throughout, GPT's text is translated and the GCC reading is noted in VAR
and in the notes. The reverse case occurs once: the last couplet of no. 180 (portion 33, no. 12), where in place of GCC's words that Narahari, being with Gauranga, preserved
propriety stands the formula "in Nadia the meeting of the nagaris and the nagara came to pass"; the compiler's work is also the signature "Narahari" at the end of no. 179,
where GCC has no poet's name.*

*6. **Conclusion.** The "Narahari" nagari padas of GPT are not songs of a contemporary of the Lord but a late (18th-century) poetic elaboration of the nagari motif, carried over
from the "Gaura-carita-cintamani" and the "Bhakti-ratnakara" into an anthology of the early 20th century, abridged and without the author's frame. Within that frame
Narahari Chakravarti himself never shows Gaura responding to the women's attraction: the Lord passes by without raising His face, turns away, hurries home, performs
expiation, and His companion leads Him away (GCC 11.11–11.14; portion 32, nos. 19–20; portion 33, nos. 5, 7, 8, 12); the wishes and dreams are the heroines' imagining, and in
kiranas 10–11 it is even a dispute of heavenly women about what they have not seen. Everything is translated faithfully to the author — without worldly vocabulary beyond
the original and without softening — with notes on the tradition's position: Vrindavana Dasa, Krishnadasa Kaviraja and Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati do not accept the
worship of Gaura in the mood of "gaura-nagari"; Gaura is Krishna Himself in Radha's bhava and radiance, tasting Her love as a devotee, not as the enjoyer (introductory note,
portion 27). What remains of the section is the padas of Lochana Dasa from GPT 3.2 (nos. 61–85, 116; portion 34) and the concluding note to the whole section of nagari padas.*
