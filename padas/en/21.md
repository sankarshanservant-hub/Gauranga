# Appendix. Padas signed "Narahari" from the Gaura-pada-tarangini — V: the wedding with Lakshmipriya and the beginning of the second wedding

*Continuation of the appendix (the introductory note on the two Naraharis, on the arguments for attribution and on the marks in the
headings is in `17.md`). Portion 21: the end of GPT section 2.3 "Karnavedha and the wedding" — the wedding day with Lakshmipriya (the
morning after the adhivasa, the water rite, the bath and dressing of the bridegroom, the procession, the wedding in the house of Vallabha
Acharya, the bridal chamber, the return home) and life with Lakshmipriya; the beginning of section 2.4 "The second marriage" — the news of
the wedding with Vishnupriya, the women of Nadia with Shachi, the adhivasa of bridegroom and bride, the morning of the wedding day (the bath
and dressing of the bridegroom; the procession and the wedding itself follow in the next portion). Narahari Chakravarti quotes almost all
the padas of the portion one after another in the 12th wave of the Bhakti-ratnakara, where Ishana shows Srinivasa the house of Jagannatha
Mishra (two padas of section 2.4 are also in Chakravarti's Gaura-carita-cintamani); one (no. 13) has not been found in any witness. Before
translation the "Narahari" signatures in sections 2.3–2.4 (GPT1 pp. 84–100) were checked throughout, in the OCR and on the scan: the
catalogue had missed two padas of section 2.4 (nos. 18 and 23, new rows NG-285, 286). From section 2.4, up to the adhivasa and the morning
of the wedding, the following are not translated: nos. 4 and 11 — signed Ghanashyama (outside the appendix); nos. 2, 3 and 7 — padas in
nagari-bhava (a young wife's dream in which Gaura invites her to the wedding and embraces her; the "anuraga" of the women for Gaura; women
who give Him "life and youth") — assigned to the section of nagari padas. The base text is GPT1 (doubtful places from the scan), collated
with GPT2, the BR (eds. 1912 and 1960) and the Gaura-carita-cintamani (ed. 1947); PK, KGC, PS and the Gita-candrodaya have been checked. On
Lakshmipriya and Vishnupriya as the eternal energies of the Lord and on the wedding rites as lila see notes 2 and 30; on the motif of the
women of Nadia, note 5. The notes of the GPT editors are not reproduced.*

## 1. Narahari — "adhivāsa niśi pohāile…" (GPT1 p. 85, section 2.3, no. 16; BR)
*The wedding day: the night of the adhivasa is over; Nimai, surrounded by brahmanas, performs the Vedic rites; crowds, music, songs, the
praises of the bhatas, the women's cries of joy; everyone rejoices at the women's rites. Raga kamoda. **Attribution: probably
Chakravarti** — the pada is in the BR (12th wave).*[^1]

**1.1.** When the night of the adhivasa had passed, everyone set about the wedding tasks.

**1.2.** Surrounded by brahmanas, Nimai performs the rites enjoined by the Vedas.[^2]

**1.3.** The crowds cannot be described: the clamour of "take! give!" is immense.

**1.4.** Instruments of every kind play without pause; the singers sing song after song.

**1.5.** The bhatas recite verses of praise; the women raise sweet cries of joy.

**1.6.** All rejoice at the women's rites; Narahari floats in that ocean of happiness.[^3]

[^1]: GPT1 — p. 85, section 2.3 ("karnavedha and the wedding"), no. 16 (kamoda; scan, PDF p. 344); GPT2 — section 2.3, no. 16 (catalogue: NG-282).
BR — 12th wave (ed. 1912, pp. 790–791; ed. 1960, vv. 1277–1282), straight after the adhivasa of the bride (no. 22 of portion 20); then the BR gives
the padas nos. 2–12 of this portion in sequence. Variants: 1.4 GPT1 "পূর্ব্বাপর" ("one after another"), BR "মনোহর" ("enchantingly"); 1.6 GPT1
"সায়রে", BR "পাথারে" (both "in the ocean"). Not in PK, KGC, PS, the Gita-candrodaya or the Gaura-carita-cintamani. B. The wedding with
Lakshmipriya — CB Adi 10; CC Adi 15; Murari 1.9–1.10.
[^2]: Lakshmipriya is the eternal consort of the Lord, Lakshmi herself (CB Adi 10; CC Adi 14–15); the Lord's wedding is not a worldly event but His
lila with His own energy. The Lord, the goal of all the Vedas, is bound by nothing enjoined; He accepts the wedding rites in keeping with the
lila and for the joy of His mother and His associates — as Krishna did in Dvaraka. So are all the padas of this portion on the Lord's
"marriage" to be understood.
[^3]: The women's rites (স্ত্রী-আচার) are the wedding customs performed by married women in addition to the Vedic rites (the cries of "ulu-lu", the
water rite, the anointing of the bridegroom, etc.). Here too the poet places himself in the lila as a witness and participant (cf. note 24 of
portion 20).

## 2. Narahari — "kulavadhūgaṇa ulasita mana…" (GPT1 p. 85, no. 17; BR)
*The wives of noble families dress to go for the water of the wedding bath; looking at Gora's face, they cry "ulu-lu"; they go, talking among
themselves; anklets ring, music plays; having performed the water rite, they enter the house. Raga kamoda. **Attribution: probably
Chakravarti** — the pada is in the BR.*[^4]

**2.1.** The wives of noble families, their hearts rejoicing, gaily dress to go for the water;
gazing again and again at Gora's moonlike face, they smile and cry "ulu-lu", and a thrill runs through their bodies.

**2.2.** They come out of their houses — how many feelings rise in their hearts! — intoxicated by the fragrance of the body of the Gaura-moon;
their hearts know no rest, brimming with feeling; from the corners of their eyes — how many different glances!

**2.3.** How much they say to one another of what cannot be told! Who would not give himself away, seeing their playful ways?
With their dress, ornaments and attire they shatter all composure — who could find a likeness for that beauty?

**2.4.** Anklets, girdles of bells, the sounds of many instruments — how sweet, words cannot tell!
Having completed the water rite, they enter the house — and Narahari's heart overflows with joy.[^5]

[^4]: GPT1 — p. 85, no. 17 (kamoda; scan, PDF p. 344); GPT2 — section 2.3, no. 17 (catalogue: NG-035). BR — 12th wave (ed. 1912, p. 791; ed. 1960,
vv. 1283–1286). Variant: 2.1 GPT1 "পানি-সহিবারে", BR "পানি সাইবারে" — "to perform the water rite" (translated by the sense of both readings).
Not in PK, KGC, PS, the Gita-candrodaya or the Gaura-carita-cintamani. B. "Pani-sai" (জল সাধা) is a wedding custom: married women go with music
to the Ganga and draw water for the bath of the bridegroom (or bride).
[^5]: The poet describes the delight of the women of Nadia at the Lord's wedding in words close to the motif of the townswomen (nagaris) enamoured
of Gaura. We translate faithfully to the author; but the Gaudiya tradition (Vrindavana Dasa, Krishnadasa Kaviraja, Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati)
does not accept the worship of Gaura in the mood of "Gaura-nagari": Gaura is Krishna Himself in the mood and lustre of Radha; He came to taste Her
love, not to enjoy as a nagara. The Lord's attractiveness for the people of Navadvipa is the quality of Bhagavan, who attracts all; it is not
worldly passion. The same holds for the other padas of the portion about the women of Nadia (cf. note 39 of portion 20).

## 3. Narahari — "kibā śrīśacī-bhavana mājhe…" (GPT1 p. 85, no. 18; BR)
*The wedding bath of the bridegroom: amid auspicious cries the women loosen His locks and braid them again, oiling them, rub Him with
turmeric and perfumes; He is bathed with Ganga water, and the poet playfully dries Him with a towel. Raga kamoda. **Attribution: probably
Chakravarti** — the pada is in the BR.*[^6]

**3.1.** What [bustle] in the house of Sri Shachi! Amid manifold auspicious cries all are busy with the wedding tasks.

**3.2.** For Gora is the Moon of Gokula: at the bath enjoined before the wedding the ocean of joy surges beyond measure.

**3.3.** The wives of noble families, sweetly and gracefully, saying, "Let us oil His lovely locks," loosen and braid them again and again.

**3.4.** One rubs His body with turmeric; seeing the turmeric grow dim, all laugh and give their lives for Him.[^7]

**3.5.** One anoints His body with perfumes — but the fragrance of that body robs the perfumes of their pride: with what to compare it?

**3.6.** They bathed Him with Ganga water; Narahari, taking a towel, dries His body as if in play.

[^6]: GPT1 — p. 85, no. 18 (kamoda; scan, PDF p. 344); GPT2 — section 2.3, no. 18 (catalogue: NG-036). BR — 12th wave (ed. 1912, p. 791; ed. 1960,
vv. 1287–1292). Variants: 3.2 GPT1 "স্থানে" ("in the place"), BR "স্নানে" ("at the bath") — following the BR; 3.5 GPT1 "উপমা দিব কি সঙ্গে", BR-1912
"কে দিবে উপমা অঙ্গে". Not in PK, KGC, PS, the Gita-candrodaya or the Gaura-carita-cintamani. B. Line 3.1 is the refrain (ধ্রু).
[^7]: Grown dim beside the golden radiance of the Lord's body. "Give their lives" (পরাণ নিছয়ে) refers to the rite of nimchhani: out of love one
waves something (in poetry — one's very life) around the beloved to ward off misfortune. The playful care of the women who perform the wedding
rites (anointing the bridegroom with turmeric and oil) is festive merriment, not nagari-bhava.

## 4. Narahari — "āju kata nā ānanda mane…" (GPT1 p. 86, no. 19; BR)
*His friends dress the bridegroom: they anoint Him with sandal, draw the tilaka, adorn His curls with a garland of malati, put on earrings,
necklace, bracelets and rings and a golden chain on His waist; the poet, having clothed Him in a red-bordered cloth, beholds His beauty.
Raga kamoda. **Attribution: probably Chakravarti** — the pada is in the BR.*[^8]

**4.1.** How much joy in the heart today! Seating Vishvambhara on a seat, His friends dress Him.

**4.2.** They anoint His body with fragrant sandal paste and draw a lovely tilaka on His forehead — whom would it not enchant?

**4.3.** Gathering the curling locks above His brow, in gladness of heart, sweetly and gracefully they wind them with a garland of malati.

**4.4.** They put earrings in His ears — they flash beside His cheeks, and His cheeks rob mirrors of their pride.

**4.5.** They put a necklace of jewels on His neck; it sways charmingly on His broad chest — who can find a likeness for it?

**4.6.** On His arms armlets and bracelets, on His fingers they put rings — and, looking at His face, they cannot keep their composure.

**4.7.** His waist is slender, slenderer than a lion's; as they adorn it with a golden chain, their eyes cease to blink.

**4.8.** An array that tempts the world! Having clothed Him in a red-bordered cloth, Narahari beholds His beauty.[^9]

[^8]: GPT1 — p. 86, no. 19 (kamoda; scan, PDF p. 345); GPT2 — section 2.3, no. 19 (catalogue: NG-037). BR — 12th wave (ed. 1912, pp. 791–792; ed. 1960,
vv. 1293–1300). Variant: 4.6 GPT1 "চাহিলা ধৈরজ ধরে" ("having looked, they kept composure"?), BR "চাহি না ধৈরয ধরে" — translated following the BR. Not
in PK, KGC, PS, the Gita-candrodaya or the Gaura-carita-cintamani. B. Line 4.1 is the refrain (ধ্রু).
[^9]: Vishvambhara ("He who sustains the universe") is the name given to the Lord at His naming (CB Adi 4). The red-bordered cloth is the
bridegroom's wedding garment. Here too the poet takes part in the lila, clothing the Lord (cf. 3.6).

## 5. Narahari — "veśa banāiyā sahacare…" (GPT1 pp. 86–87, no. 20; BR)
*His friends, having dressed Nimai, give Him a golden mirror; even the gods cannot take their eyes off Him; Shachi and her friends perform
auspicious rites; the young wives look on from afar; bowing to His mother, Gaura sets out for the wedding amid Vedic chanting and music.
Raga kamoda. **Attribution: probably Chakravarti** — the pada is in the BR.*[^10]

**5.1.** Having dressed [Him], His friends put into His hand a golden mirror like the moon.

**5.2.** Seeing the attire of Nimai-chand — what to speak of others? — even the gods cannot turn their eyes away.

**5.3.** Mother Shachi with her friends performs many auspicious rites, gazing at her son's face.

**5.4.** The young wives, standing at a distance, cannot keep their composure as they look at Gorachand.

**5.5.** The women cry "ulu-lu"; the news of the joyful wedding has filled the world.

**5.6.** Bowing at His mother's feet, Gaura Raya sets out to be married.[^11]

**5.7.** The brahmanas chant the Vedas; instruments of many kinds resound — the sound pierces the sky.

**5.8.** Who can tell of this merriment? Narahari swims in that ocean of happiness.

[^10]: GPT1 — pp. 86–87, no. 20 (kamoda; scan, PDF pp. 345–346); GPT2 — section 2.3, no. 20. The catalogue did not know it (new row NG-283, portion 20).
BR — 12th wave (ed. 1912, p. 792; ed. 1960, vv. 1301–1308; the 1960 editor's heading before the next pada: "the wedding of Sri Nimai"). Variant: 5.6 BR
"গৌররায়" — the same. Not in PK, KGC, PS, the Gita-candrodaya or the Gaura-carita-cintamani. B. Line 5.1 is the refrain (ধ্রু).
[^11]: The Lord bows to His mother, setting an example of respect for one's parents. On the young wives (5.4) see note 5.

## 6. Narahari — "āju godhūli samaya śubhakṣaṇa…" (GPT1 p. 87, no. 21; BR)
*The wedding procession: at the auspicious hour of dusk Gaura in wedding attire mounts the palanquin and, surrounded by brahmanas, moves
slowly to Vallabha's house; praises, dances, songs, a sea of music; the gods gaze unblinking from the sky and descend unseen to earth;
crowds, hundreds of lamps, moonlight; the Earth, the Ganga, beasts and birds rejoice. Raga bhupali. **Attribution: probably Chakravarti** —
the pada is in the BR; ornate Vrajabuli.*[^12]

**6.1.** Today, at the auspicious hour of dusk, Gaura — the jewel of virtues, enchanter of the world — is in the attire enjoined for a wedding,
and the beauty of His tender body seems to brim over;
crushing the pride of millions of Kamas, lotus-eyed, delighting people's hearts, glancing in all four directions with a soft smile, He mounts
the palanquin, shining.[^13]

**6.2.** Surrounded by brahmanas He moves towards Vallabha's house — His gait very slow and sweet; the bards proclaim abundant blessings, the
world is filled with cries of "jaya, jaya";
dancers dance, beating out "thai-tata", how many singers are absorbed in song to "thonga-thongina"; composing lovely [songs on His] deeds,
tuning the mode, the artists rain down sweet rasa.

**6.3.** How many instruments play in every manner, the rhythmic syllables sound without a break; skilled musicians, as if churning the ocean of
music, swim in it;
in the sky the gods are utterly absorbed — they gaze steadily with unblinking eyes; thrilling all over they descend unseen to earth — what
delight in their hearts!

**6.4.** Countless men and women run; the broad road is incomparably beautiful; hundreds upon hundreds of lamps blaze, the moon, lord of the
night, sheds its light;
the Earth has blossomed in great joy, the waters of the Jahnavi surge; what can the servant Narahari say? — beasts and birds all float in
happiness.

[^12]: GPT1 — p. 87, no. 21 (bhupali; scan, PDF p. 346); GPT2 — section 2.3, no. 21 (catalogue: NG-038). BR — 12th wave (ed. 1912, pp. 792–793; ed. 1960,
vv. 1309–1312). Variants: 6.1 GPT1 "ছলকায়", "ঝলকায়", BR "ছলকয়ে", "ঝলকয়ে"; 6.2 GPT1 "বন্দীগণ, ভুরি মঙ্গলভণ", BR "বন্দিগণ ভণ ভুরি মঙ্গল"; 6.3 GPT1
"জনু", BR "যনু" (both "as if"). Not in PK, KGC, PS, the Gita-candrodaya or the Gaura-carita-cintamani. B. The syllables "thai-tata",
"thonga-thongina" imitate the dancers' counting and the strokes of the drums (cf. note 11 of portion 20).
[^13]: Dusk (গোধূলি, "the hour of cow-dust", when the herds return home) is an auspicious hour for a wedding. The palanquin (চৌদোল) is the litter
on which the bridegroom is carried to the bride's house. The wedding with Lakshmipriya takes place in the house of her father, Vallabha
Acharya (CB Adi 10).

## 7. Narahari — "gorācā̃dera bibāha dekhibāre…" (GPT1 pp. 87–88, no. 22; BR)
*The young wives of Nadia, in fine dress and ornaments, their bells ringing, hurry to see Gorachand's wedding; they talk among themselves,
smiling under their veils; their glances dart in all directions; seeing the poet's Lord enter Vallabha's house, they are beside themselves
with joy. Raga bhupali. **Attribution: probably Chakravarti** — the pada is in the BR.*[^14]

**7.1.** To see Gorachand's wedding, with what longing the young wives of Nadia run — not one can keep her composure!

**7.2.** Incomparable attire and garments, bodies adorned with ornaments — their charm glitters and shines;
as they walk, the bells of their girdles and the anklets on their feet ring; with their sweet gait they light up the road.

**7.3.** Absorbed in that feeling, how much they say to one another — how charmingly! — the rings at their nostrils sway;
on their lovely faces hidden by veils, a soft, soft smile; whom would the radiance of that smile not enchant?

**7.4.** Darkened with collyrium, delighting the heart, surpassing the khanjana bird, their lovely glances [dart] on every side;
and, seeing Narahari's Lord of life entering Vallabha's house, they are beside themselves with joy.[^15]

[^14]: GPT1 — pp. 87–88, no. 22 (bhupali; scan, PDF pp. 346–347); GPT2 — section 2.3, no. 22 (catalogue: NG-039). BR — 12th wave (ed. 1912, p. 793; ed.
1960, vv. 1313–1316). Variants: 7.4 GPT1 "উথলয়ে", BR-1960 "উলয়ে" (OCR?); 7.3 GPT1 "বেশর", BR "বেসর" (the same word, "nose-ring"). Not in PK, KGC, PS,
the Gita-candrodaya or the Gaura-carita-cintamani. B. Line 7.1 is the refrain (ধ্রু).
[^15]: The khanjana is the wagtail, in Indian poetry an image of lively, restless eyes. "Narahari's Lord of life" (নরহরি পরাণনাথ) is the poet's Lord, a
turn of the signature (like "Narahari's Lord", note 34 of portion 20); the young wives' joy is festive delight at the wedding (see note 5).

## 8. Narahari — "vallabha-bhavane gorā rāya…" (GPT1 pp. 88–89, no. 23; BR)
*The wedding in Vallabha's house: Vishvambhara stands on a little board under the wedding canopy; the bride's mother with the married women
welcomes the bridegroom, circling Him seven times with seven lamps; Vallabha Acharya brings his daughter, seats her on a throne and by the
rite gives Lakshmi to the son of Shachi; Vedic chanting, cries of joy, music; from their celestial chariots the gods invisibly shower the
wedding with flowers. Raga kamoda. **Attribution: probably Chakravarti** — the pada is in the BR.*[^16]

**8.1.** Gora Raya in Vallabha's house increases the great joy of Vallabha Mishra.

**8.2.** Vallabha, rejoicing, has the auspicious rites enjoined for a wedding performed.

**8.3.** Vishvambhara, His heart glad, stood on the little board under the wedding canopy.[^17]

**8.4.** The movements of His body steal one's life; the radiance of His form lights up the ten directions.

**8.5.** To what shall His moonlike face be compared? With a slight smile it destroys the pride of nectar.

**8.6.** The glance of His eyes is lovely: whomever He looks at cannot hold his composure.

**8.7.** At the roots of His ears, makara-shaped earrings; whom would the arrangement of His curling hair not enchant?

**8.8.** Armlets and bracelets sit beautifully; seeing that beauty, how many Madanas die of shame!

**8.9.** To welcome such a bridegroom, the bride's mother comes out with the married women.

**8.10.** Who can describe that beauty? With seven lamps in her hands she circles Him seven times.

**8.11.** Wondrous are the women's rites! Having welcomed the bridegroom, all go into the house.

**8.12.** Fortunate Vallabha Acharya had his daughter brought to perform the kanya-dana — the giving of the daughter.

**8.13.** They seated her on a divine throne — and it shone with the radiance of her body.

**8.14.** Her body is so tender, her smiling face surpasses the full moon.

**8.15.** She wears many-coloured garments; ornaments of many jewels glitter.

**8.16.** Such a daughter, with every prescribed rite, Mishra gave to the son of Shachi.[^18]

**8.17.** The brahmanas chant the Vedas; all the women of noble families cry "ulu-lu".

**8.18.** Instruments of every kind resound, dancers dance, the bhatas recite verses of praise.

**8.19.** The gods, mounting their celestial chariots, invisibly shower flowers, crying "jaya!".

**8.20.** Great happiness has spread through the world — how can Narahari tell of it with one mouth?

## 9. Narahari — "gorā guṇamaṇi prāṇapriyā saha…" (GPT1 p. 89, no. 24; BR)
*The bridal chamber: Gora is in the chamber with His beloved; the women keep coming in and going out, joke, give Him betel to put into
Lakshmi's mouth, themselves offer Him betel, braid and loosen His hair, seat Lakshmi on His lap. Raga bhupali. **Attribution: probably
Chakravarti** — the pada is in the BR.*[^19]

**9.1.** Gora, the jewel of virtues, with His beloved, dear as life, passes the time in the bridal chamber;
the wives of noble families keep coming in and going out, full of merriment.

**9.2.** Some, inventing various pretexts, tease and laugh, laugh for the joy of their hearts;
another, placing betel in Gora's lotus hand, says, "Put it into Lakshmi's mouth!"

**9.3.** One offers betel to Gora's moonlike mouth — and her affection grows and grows;
another, longing to touch Him, braids His hair — and, loosening it, cannot contain herself.

**9.4.** Yet another, seating Lakshmi on Vishvambhara's lap, looks at them with a charming smile;
says Narahari: the rasa that surges in the bridal chamber has no comparison.[^20]

[^19]: GPT1 — p. 89, no. 24 (bhupali; scan, PDF p. 348); GPT2 — section 2.3, no. 24 (catalogue: NG-041). BR — 12th wave (ed. 1912, p. 795; ed. 1960, vv.
1337–1340). Variant: 9.1 GPT1 "শোজ" (misprint), BR "সে যে" — following the BR. Not in PK, KGC, PS, the Gita-candrodaya or the Gaura-carita-cintamani. B.
[^20]: Vasara (বাসর) is the first night after the wedding, which the couple spend in the chamber while the women of the bride's house keep them awake

Lakshmipriya is the eternal energy of the Lord (note 2), and the whole scene is the Lord's lila with His energy, not worldly love.

## 10. Narahari — "gorācā̃dera bibāha paradine…" (GPT1 p. 89, no. 25; BR)
*At dawn after the wedding the women run together to admire bride and bridegroom; Vallabha's fortunate wife rejoices; generous Vallabha
Mishra does everything for his son-in-law; Gaurahari, having performed the wedding rites, is glad; the gods praise Vallabha. Raga todi.
**Attribution: probably Chakravarti** — the pada is in the BR.*[^21]

**10.1.** The day after Gorachand's wedding, at daybreak — how much joy surges up!

**10.2.** The wives of noble families run together from every side; seeing the beauty of bride and bridegroom, all delight their eyes.

**10.3.** How fortunate is Vallabha's wife! Having gained a jewel of a son-in-law, she does not know where she is.

**10.4.** Mishra Vallabha is generous beyond measure: what does he not do for the good of his son-in-law!

## 21. Narahari — "adhivāsa divasera pare…" (GPT1 p. 98, no. 13; BR)
*The wedding day: through Nadia rings "today is Nimai's wedding"; to see it there come invisibly Shiva with Parvati, Ananta with his
associates, the residents of Vaikuntha, Brahma with his consort, Indra with Shachi, the gods, the celestial women, gandharvas and kinnaras,
the heavenly dancers, the divine sages; the Yamuna and the Ganga surge; the brahmanas of Nadia gather in Nimai's house; Gaurahari, having
performed the wedding rites, sits in joy. Raga not specified. **Attribution: probably Chakravarti** — the pada is in the BR.*[^43]

**21.1.** On the day after the adhivasa, joy grows in the new town of Nadia.

**21.2.** Everywhere people run about, saying: "Today is Nimai's wedding!"

**21.3.** The world is full of cries of "jaya, jaya" — who would not long to see the wedding?

**21.4.** Shiva in joy, with Parvati, leaving Kailasa, comes to see the wedding.

**21.5.** Ananta with his own associates remains here unseen to behold the wedding.

**21.6.** All the residents of Vaikuntha, saying "we shall see the wedding", are restless at heart.

**21.7.** The four-faced [Brahma] with his consort longs every moment to see the wedding.

**21.8.** The lord of the gods, taking Shachi with him, dresses in great joy to see the wedding.[^44]

**21.9.** The gods say with zeal: "We shall see the wedding, staying in the Lord's house!"

**21.10.** The celestial women have resolved: "We shall make merry at the wedding together with the wives of Nadia!"

**21.11.** The gandharvas and kinnaras think: "We shall join the artists in song and music at the wedding!"

**21.12.** Indra's dancers say: "We shall dress for the wedding together with the dancers of Nadia!"

**21.13.** The divine sages, rejoicing at heart, long so much to see the wedding!

**21.14.** The Yamuna and the Jahnavi surge; the Earth rejoices in the delight of the wedding festivities.

**21.15.** The brahmanas and worthy people of Nadia — all go to the wedding at Nimai's house.

**21.16.** The son of Shachi, Gaurahari, having performed the rites enjoined for the wedding, sits [among them] in joy.

**21.17.** Gazing at the Lord's moonlike face, how much they say — no one can master his heart.

**21.18.** How much auspiciousness arises here! How can Narahari tell of it with one mouth?

## 24. Narahari — "nadīyāra śaśī rasika-śekhara…" (GPT1 p. 99, no. 16; BR)

**24.1.** The Moon of Nadia, crown of the connoisseurs of rasa, shines beautifully in auspicious wedding attire;

**24.4.** Paddy, durva and the rest tied with thread, and a beautiful golden mirror in His right hand;
says Narahari: seeing every limb adorned with ornaments, who can keep his composure?
