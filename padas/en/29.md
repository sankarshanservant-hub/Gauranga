# Nagari Padas — III: the cycle of the *Bhakti-ratnakara* (Yadunandana, Narahari Chakravarti) and the "Narahari" padas of GPT (sect. 3.2, nos. 39–45, 86–93)

*On what nagari-bhava is, the position of the tradition (Vrindavana Dasa, Krishnadasa Kaviraja, Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati) and the rules of translation, see the introductory note to the section of nagari padas in chapter 27; on the two poets named Narahari (Narahari Sarakara and Narahari Chakravarti) and on the attribution labels ("probably Sarakara", "probably Chakravarti", "not established"), see the introductory note to the "Narahari" appendix in chapter 17.*

## 1. Yadunandana — "gaurāṅga carita āju ki pekhalum̐ māi…" (BR 12, pp. 904–905; GPT1 p. 167, no. 34)
*Nagari-bhava. A woman of Nadia tells what she saw on the road: crying "Radha, Radha!", Gauranga weeps, embracing Gadai, rolls on the ground,
streams flow from His eyes; she herself loses all composure; Dasa Gadadhara smiles softly. Raga dhanashi.*[^1]

**1.1.** What have I seen today, mother — what Gauranga does!
Crying "Radha, Radha!" He weeps, embracing Gadai.[^2]

**1.2.** Unable to hold His heart, He rolls on the earth —
how much dust has clung to that golden body!

**1.3.** I look at that face — and do not know what my heart is doing;
how many streams, like the Suradhuni, pour from His eyes![^3]

**1.4.** I am dying, dying! Why did I go along that road!
There is no steadiness in my mind, my heart is bursting.

**1.5.** Seeing this, Dasa Gadadhara smiles softly, softly.
Yadunandana says: he floats in that rasa.[^4]

[^1]: Signature: "Yadunandana." BR, 12th wave: ed. 1913 (Murshidabad), pp. 904–905 (scan PDF pp. 912–913); ed. 1960, vv. 2803–2807. Narahari
Chakravarti, describing the places of Navadvipa, says that the Lord lingered by this tree and then went along the bank of the Ganga, "and here
women full of anuraga (anurāgavatī) joyfully and softly spoke with one another" — and gives their words in songs, "the first in a song of Sri
Yadunandana Chakravarti, disciple of Sri Dasa Gadadhara Thakura" (so in BR). Eleven more songs follow in BR (nos. 2–7 of this chapter; Narahari's
padas "nadīyāra mājhāre nācaye gorācām̐da" and "sai kivā aparūpa rūpa", translated in chapter 17; Yadunandana's pada "dekha dekha gorācānde" —
chapter 11, no. 12; Vasu's PK 2149 — chapter 27, no. 7; Devakinandana's pada "nadīyāra mājhāre onā rūpa" — the poet is outside the corpus).
GPT1 — p. 167, no. 34 (dhanashi; scan PDF p. 426); GPT2 — sect. 3.2, no. 34 (catalogue: YN-002). Yadunandana Chakravarti was a disciple of
Gadadhara Dasa, a poet of the late 16th century; his padas in BR are B. Nagari-bhava — see the introductory note to the section (chapter 27).
[^2]: "Mother" (māi) is an exclamation common in the speech of women in Vrajabuli ("ah, mother!"), here addressed to a friend. Gauranga, in the bhava
of Krishna, calls Radha and embraces Gadadhara Pandita: according to the *Gaura-ganoddesha-dipika* (147–153), Gadadhara is Radha Herself; cf.
chapter 11 (nos. 12–14) and Yadunandana's padas on Gaura and Gadadhara. The woman's words that she is "dying" express wonder and compassion
at the sight of the Lord's ecstasy; there is no desire in the text.
[^3]: "Streams like the Suradhuni" (suradhunī-dhārā) — the Lord's tears, abundant as the current of the Ganga.
[^4]: Dasa Gadadhara (Gadadhara Dasa), a companion of the Lord, was Yadunandana's teacher; he "smiles", understanding what is happening to the Lord.
"He floats in that rasa" may refer either to Gadadhara Dasa or to the poet himself; GPT: a "lahu lahu" (light, soft) smile.

## 2. Yadunandana — "dāsa gadādhara vadana heri…" (BR 12, p. 905)
*Nagari-bhava. The second of Yadunandana's two "gitas" at the beginning of the cycle: Gaura, looking at the face of Dasa Gadadhara, signals to him
with the corner of His eye, thrills, smiles; the woman saw Him on the bank of the Ganga — He steals the eyes of all young women. Raga kamoda.*[^5]

**2.1.** Looking into the face of Dasa Gadadhara,
He speaks with the corner of His eye, giving a sign.[^6]

**2.2.** Who knows why His body thrills;
in His smile nectar seems to rain.

**2.3.** On the bank of the Suranadi I saw Gora —
the thief of the eyes of all young women.

**2.4.** From the natural curve of His brows —
little friend, while I live, what shame is left?[^7]

**2.5.** The curve of His neck cannot be told:
the bird of the eye stays there, its wings outspread.

**2.6.** The beauty of His arms reaching to the knees —
seeing it, the hearts of young women crave.

**2.7.** At the soles of His reddish lotus feet
may Yadu's mind abide as a bee.[^8]

[^5]: Signature: "Yadu" ("yadu mana rahu…"). BR 12: ed. 1913, p. 905 ("punaḥ | kaścit kāmodaḥ", scan PDF p. 913); ed. 1960, vv. 2808–2814. The pada is
not in GPT1 or GPT2; in the catalogue — YN-005 (the second gita). Majumdar mentions both of Yadunandana's "gitas" at the beginning of the cycle as
songs of a disciple of Gadadhara Dasa. Nagari-bhava — see the introductory note to the section (chapter 27).
[^6]: The subject of 2.1 is not named in the original; in the sense of the cycle it is Gaura exchanging glances with Dasa Gadadhara (on Gaura and
Gadadhara Dasa in radha-bhava — chapter 11, no. 15). "Suranadi" (suranadī, "river of the gods") — the Ganga; so in the original.
[^7]: "Little friend" (ājuli) is a playful, affectionate address to a friend (cf. 7.5 below); the exact meaning of the word is not established. The
line can also be read: "in my heart, little friend, what shame is left?" — i.e., modesty is lost at the sight of Gaura. The nagari motif is rendered
as in the original.
[^8]: The image of the mind as a bee at the lotus feet is a common prayer formula of the signature (cf. "Narahari's mind is a bee" in the padas of
the appendix).

## 3. Narahari — "taruṇī-parāṇa-corā gorārūpa…" (BR 12, pp. 905–906; GPT1 p. 171, no. 46)
*Nagari-bhava. Gora's form steals the life of young women; blessed are those who drink it even once from the corner of the eye; Gaura, looking at
Pandita Gadai, calls him "Radhika!", holds Dasa Gadadhara by the hand, thrills and smiles — the depth of this rasa cannot be reached; He walks the
roads of Nadia, swaying like a nagara. Raga dhanashi. **Attribution: probably Chakravarti** — the pada stands in the cycle of BR 12, in Vrajabuli.*[^9]

**3.1.** Gora's form, which steals the life of young women — its sweetness is a stream of nectar;
blessed, blessed, blessed are they who drink it even once from the corner of the eye!

**3.2.** *(Refrain)* Friend, to whom shall I tell this?
Gazing intently at Pandita Gadai, He calls him: "Radhika!"[^10]

**3.3.** Placing His hand in the hand of Dasa Gadadhara, He thrills with joy, His body bristling;
He smiles softly, softly — in what rasa does He float? I cannot reach the bottom.

**3.4.** With a nagara's airs He goes along the roads of Nadia, swaying;
His pose enchants Narahari's mind — Madana swoons at it.[^11]

[^9]: Signature: "Narahari." BR 12: ed. 1913, pp. 905–906 ("punaḥ kācit dhānaśī", scan PDF pp. 913–914); ed. 1960, vv. 2815–2818. GPT1 — p. 171, no. 46
(dhanashi; scan PDF p. 430); GPT2 — sect. 3.2, no. 46 (catalogue: NG-079). The pada stands third in the cycle of the speeches of the "anuragavatis",
between two padas of Yadunandana, and, like all of Narahari Chakravarti's songs in BR, was written by him for this narrative (B). GPT1 readings:
"mādhurī amiñā dhārā", "kichui nā pāi thā", "helite dulite". Nagari-bhava — see the introductory note to the section (chapter 27).
[^10]: Gaura calls Gadadhara Pandita "Radhika": Gadadhara is Sri Radha in Gaura-lila (GGD 147–153); the Lord, in the bhava of Krishna, recognizes
His beloved in him. This is the Lord's own confidential mood, which the townswoman sees but does not understand ("to whom shall I tell?").
[^11]: "A nagara's airs" (nāgarāli-ṭhāṭa) — a word from the language of the padas about Krishna ("nagara" — lover, gallant). The translation is
faithful to the author; the tradition does not accept the image of Gaura as nagara (introductory note, chapter 27): here it is the townswoman's
view, while the Lord Himself is absorbed in Krishna's bhava for Radha (3.2–3.3). "Narahari's mind" — genitive of possession: the Lord's pose
enchants the poet's mind.

## 4. Yadunandana — "sajani sai śuna gorā-aparūpa gāthā…" (BR 12, p. 906; GPT1 p. 169, no. 37)
*Nagari-bhava. "Friend, listen to the wondrous song of Gora": the tale of the secret play with the young wives of Vraja has flooded the world; the
fragrance of His body maddens the gods of love, the garland on His chest makes noble wives swoon; everything in Him is like the Moon of Gokula,
only the colour is golden; now He calls "Radha!", now laughs, now weeps aloud. Raga karnatika.*[^12]

**4.1.** *(Refrain)* Friend, listen to the wondrous song of Gora:
of the secret play with the young wives of Vraja —
that tale has flooded the world.[^13]

**4.2.** From the fragrance of His body how many Manmathas, maddened,
fly to Him in the guise of bees;
the garland of rangana flowers plays upon His chest —
and the noble wives swoon.

**4.3.** I see the golden colour — and all else, friend, is the same:
the speech, the gait, the radiance of the body;
the form of the Moon of Gokula, the plain snare of the brows —
a thorn to noble wives in both their families.[^14]

**4.4.** What woman, seeing the aim of His eyes
and the sweetness of the smile on His moon-face,
will keep her composure — and only then go home —
not driven mad by Manmatha?

**4.5.** Now He calls "Radha!" and sits with His eyes closed,
now laughs, overwhelmed by bhava,
now weeps aloud, His whole body bristling —
this is what Yadunandana loves.[^15]

[^12]: Signature: "Yadunandana." BR 12: ed. 1913, p. 906 ("punaḥ kācit karṇāṭikā", scan PDF p. 914); ed. 1960, vv. 2819–2823. GPT1 — p. 169, no. 37
(karnatika; scan PDF p. 428); GPT2 — sect. 3.2, no. 37 (catalogue: YN-003). Readings: GPT1 "āra sava sei śākhī", BR 1913 "sei sakhi" (noting the
reading "sei sāthi"); BR 1960 "gorārūpa-gāthā". B. Nagari-bhava — see the introductory note to the section (chapter 27).
[^13]: A tripadi; the refrain has three lines. The song is about Gora, while "the secret play with the wives of Vraja" is Krishna's lila: the
townswoman (and the poet) recognizes in Gaura Krishna of Vraja Himself; cf. 4.3 "the form of the Moon of Gokula" and the endings of nos. 5–7
("is He not the Moon of Gokula?").
[^14]: "Plain" (paratek, from pratyakṣa) — "evident", "in sight"; BR 1913 "payatek" is a misprint. "A thorn in both families" (dui kule kām̐ṭā) — the
ruin of one's good name in the father's and the husband's family; so the padas about Krishna speak of the gopis. The image is as in the original;
Manmatha, the "god of love", is Kamadeva.
[^15]: Weeping, laughter, closed eyes, bristling hair (pulaka) are the Lord's sattvika-bhavas in the bhava of Krishna longing for Radha (cf. no. 1).
The stanza turns from the townswomen to Gaura Himself: it is this bhava of His that the poet loves.

## 5. Yadunandana — "sai go nadīyā-jāhnavī-kūle…" (BR 12, p. 908; GPT1 p. 168, no. 36)
*Nagari-bhava (praise of His form). "Friend, on the bank of the Jahnavi in Nadia — what creator fashioned that body from golden shirisha flowers?"
His face a lotus, His lips bandhuli, His nose a kimshuka bud, His arms golden creepers; Gauranga cries "Hari!" and weeps on the lap of Dasa
Gadadhara; is He not the Moon of Gokula? Raga mallarika.*[^16]

**5.1.** *(Refrain)* Friend, on the bank of the Jahnavi in Nadia —
what creator, how, fashioned that body from golden shirisha flowers?

**5.2.** Who, seeing, would not believe?
His face a lotus, His lips bandhuli, His teeth — are they kunda?[^17]

**5.3.** To whom shall I tell it?
His lovely nose a kimshuka bud, His eyes red utpala lotuses.

**5.4.** I cannot say it in words:
His arms golden creepers, upon them lotuses, His nails blossoming jasmines.

**5.5.** His eyes — an ocean of bliss;
His soles — red land-lotuses, His nails put pearls to shame.

**5.6.** He bears the fragrance of love —
the people of the three worlds, seeing it, are intoxicated and do not return home.

**5.7.** "Hari, Hari, Hari!" He cries;
I do not know why Gauranga weeps on the lap of Dasa Gadadhara.

**5.8.** Why such bewilderment?
Yadunandana says: don't you know? — is He not the Moon of Gokula?[^18]

[^16]: Signature: "Yadunandana." BR 12: ed. 1913, p. 908 ("kācicca mallārikā", scan PDF p. 916); ed. 1960, vv. 2843–2850. GPT1 — p. 168, no. 36 (mallarika;
scan PDF p. 427; first line "soi lo nadīyā-jāhnavīkūle"); GPT2 — sect. 3.2, no. 36 (catalogue: YN-006; Majumdar knew line 5.7). B. The nagari motif
is only in the address to a friend and in line 5.6; in substance this is praise of the Lord's beauty. Nagari-bhava — see the introductory note to
the section (chapter 27).
[^17]: Each stanza is a short opening line and a long line of comparisons (tripadi). Bandhuli — a red flower; kunda — white jasmine; kimshuka — the
red palasha flower; utpala — a lotus; shirisha — a delicate fragrant flower. GPT1 "daśana kunaki tāya" is probably a corruption of "kunda ki".
[^18]: "The Moon of Gokula" (gokulacanda) is Krishna: the friend's bewilderment as to why Gaura weeps is resolved by the poet: this is Krishna
Himself, and He weeps in the bhava of Radha (on the lap of Dasa Gadadhara; cf. chapter 11, no. 15). Cf. the endings of nos. 6 and 7.

## 6. Yadu — "dekha gorā-raṅga sai dekha gorā-raṅga…" (BR 12, pp. 908–909; GPT1 p. 138, no. 79)
*Nagari-bhava (praise of His form). "Look, friend, at Gora's play!" Through the town of Nadia walks a golden Ananga: His beauty surpasses a mirror
of gold and gems, His face is the full moon, His brows a bow, His eyes arrows; the young women lose their heads, and He does not even look back;
"He is that very Beauty of Gokula." Raga kamoda.*[^19]

**6.1.** *(Refrain)* Look at Gora's play, friend, look at Gora's play:
through the town of Nadia walks a golden Ananga.

**6.2.** His loveliness surpasses a mirror of gold and gems;
His reddish feet have lit up the earth.

**6.3.** His face has taken the splendour of the full moon;
the sky is bright with that radiance — for joy one cannot find the way.[^20]

**6.4.** His brows a bow, His eyes arrows, His aim sidelong:
His whole make is like the Madana of Vraja.

**6.5.** Arms to the knees, a broad chest —
who, seeing Him, does not feel the joy of a touch?[^21]

**6.6.** His gait is lovelier than that of an intoxicated king of elephants;
the young women are lost — and He does not even turn to look.

**6.7.** Yadu says: He is that very Beauty of Gokula;
you know and do not know — that is why you are afraid.[^22]

[^19]: Signature: "Yadu" ("yadu kahe"). BR 12: ed. 1913, pp. 908–909 ("kaścicca kāmodaḥ", scan PDF pp. 916–917); ed. 1960, vv. 2851–2857. GPT1 — p. 138,
no. 79 (sect. 3.1 "form", kamoda; scan PDF p. 397); GPT2 — sect. 3.1, no. 79 (catalogue: YN-011). In BR the pada stands in Yadunandana's cycle and is
his (B). Nagari-bhava — see the introductory note to the section (chapter 27).
[^20]: "Ananga" ("the bodiless one") is the god of love; "a golden Ananga" is Gaura, beautiful as Kamadeva. 6.3b ("diśā nārī sukha", so in BR 1913, 1960
and GPT1) is understood as "diśā nā ri (pāri) — sukhe", "for joy one cannot find the way"; "the joy of the women of all quarters" is also possible.
The reading is obscure.
[^21]: "The joy of a touch" (paraśana-sukha) — from a mere glance: a delight as if one had touched Him. Rendered as in the original; this is an
image of the fullness of darshana, not of worldly desire.
[^22]: "The Beauty of Gokula" (gokulasundara) is Krishna. The friend "knows and does not know": she sees something extraordinary but does not
understand that before her is Krishna Himself — and fears for herself; the poet reveals the secret to her.

## 7. Yadunandana — "gauravaraṇa sonā chaṭaka cām̐dera jonā…" (BR 12, p. 910; GPT1 p. 168, no. 35)
*Nagari-bhava (only in the address "little friend" and in the words "destroying the composure of young women"). Golden Gaura, stirred by bhava,
weeps in streams, His whole body bristling, cries "Hari!", embraces Gadai and weeps: "What more, what more?…" — "is He not the Beloved of
Gokula?" Raga ashavari.*[^23]

**7.1.** Gaura's colour is gold, His radiance moonlight;
red as the young dawn, He stands motionless — His mind stirred by bhava.[^24]

**7.2.** From His reddish eyes flow streams, like the streams of the Suradhuni;
dense bristling covers His body — the burden of His feeling is heavier than the earth.

**7.3.** On His face a slight smile that destroys the composure of young women;
again and again, choking, He cries "Hari!" — and floods the world with weeping.

**7.4.** Embracing Gadai, He speaks sweetly, sweetly;
"What more, what more?" He says and weeps; I do not know in what rasa He is lost.

**7.5.** Whoever knows, knows it in the heart; my mind is immersed in that rasa.
Yadunandana says: little friend, is He not the Beloved of Gokula?[^25]

[^23]: Signature: "Yadunandana." BR 12: ed. 1913, p. 910 ("kācicca āśāvarī", scan PDF p. 918); ed. 1960, vv. 2870–2874. GPT1 — p. 168, no. 35 (ashavari;
scan PDF p. 427; the number is garbled in the recognized text); GPT2 — sect. 3.2 (catalogue: YN-012). GPT1 readings: "taruṇī dhairaja nāśi",
"nā jāni ki rase bhule". B. Nagari-bhava — see the introductory note to the section (chapter 27).
[^24]: "Stands motionless" (caraṇe thira, lit. "firm on the feet") — the Lord stands still in bhava; it can also be read as "His feet are the young
dawn". "Heavier than the earth" (mahī jini bhāra bharā) — the fullness of feeling. The stanzas are tripadi.
[^25]: "The Beloved of Gokula" (gokula-piyā) is Krishna, the Beloved of Radha and the gopis; Gaura, embracing Gadadhara (Radha) and asking
"what more?", experiences the love of Radha and Krishna. "Little friend" (ājuli) — see note 7.

## 8. Narahari — "velā avasāne nanadinī sane…" (GPT1 p. 169, no. 39)
*Nagari-bhava. At dusk a young wife went with her sister-in-law to fetch water, saw Gaurangachand and came back having broken her pot; her body
trembles, her feet will not walk, in the ocean of His beauty there is no bottom; the poet: whoever has the sweetness of Gauranga awakened in the
heart, his family honour and good conduct sink in anuraga for Gorachand. Raga kamoda. **Attribution: probably Sarakara** — simple Bengali, brief;
not in BR, GCC, the *Gita-chandrodaya* or PK; the GPT2 index assigns the page to Sarakara; the preface of GPT2 quotes the pada as an example of
Narahari Sarakara's nagari padas.*[^26]

**8.1.** At the end of the day I went with my sister-in-law to fetch water;
I saw the form of Gaurangachand — and came back having broken my pot.[^27]

**8.2.** My body trembles, fever comes over me, my feet will not walk;
in the ocean of Gaurangachand's beauty I swim — and find no bottom.

**8.3.** His long, long eyes are fearful flower-arrows;
how can a woman keep her composure? — Madana himself trembles with fear.

**8.4.** Narahari says: whoever has the sweetness of Gauranga awakened in the heart —
his family honour and good conduct have all sunk in anuraga for Gorachand.[^28]

[^26]: Signature: "Narahari." GPT1 — p. 169, no. 39 (kamoda; scan PDF p. 428); GPT2 — sect. 3.2, no. 39 (catalogue: NG-075). GPT only: there are no
witnesses (BR, GCC, GCd, PK, KGC, PS checked). By the features that Majumdar takes as marks of Sarakara (simple Bengali without Vrajabuli, no
piling up of comparisons, correct metre, brevity) the pada is closer to Sarakara; but he considers padas known only from GPT unreliable — C. The
preface of GPT2 quotes the pada with an extra couplet (He stands by the river, lighting up both banks — cf. 12.4 below). Nagari-bhava — see the
introductory note to the section (chapter 27).
[^27]: The broken pot is a common motif of padas on purvaraga: so the gopis, seeing Krishna, drop their pots by the Yamuna. Cf. nos. 12 and 20 below.
[^28]: "Family honour and good conduct sink" (kulaśīla majila) is a formula of the supremacy of prema over worldly ties, known from the padas about
the gopis; so Vaishnavas understand it (cf. *Bhag.* 10.29: the gopis leave their homes on hearing the flute). The translation is faithful to the
author; the tradition does not accept worship of Gaura in the mood of the nagaris (introductory note, chapter 27).

## 9. Narahari — "śayane gaura svapane gaura…" (GPT1 pp. 169–170, no. 40)
*Nagari-bhava (only in the address to a friend). Gaura is in sleep and in dream, in life and in death; He is the pupil of the eyes and the necklace
on the neck; the poet wishes to keep Him in the heart and, in solitude, to gaze at Him; refrain: "Friend, speak to me of Gaura!" — His name is the
abode of nectar. Raga dhanashi. **Attribution: probably Sarakara** — simple Bengali; no witnesses; the GPT2 index — Sarakara; the preface of GPT2
quotes it as Sarakara's.*[^29]

**9.1.** In sleep — Gaura, in dream — Gaura, Gaura is the pupil of my eyes;
in life — Gaura, in death — Gaura, Gaura is the necklace on my neck.

**9.2.** Keeping Gauranga in my heart, I will sit in solitude
and to my heart's content hold that moon before me, eye to eye.

**9.3.** *(Refrain)* Friend, speak to me of Gaura!
Gora's name is the abode of nectar, the giver of love embodied.[^30]

**9.4.** He in whose heart the word "Gaura" ever sounds, the treasure "Gaura" ever lives —
says Narahari — at his feet he begs for shelter always.

[^29]: Signature: "Narahari." GPT1 — pp. 169–170, no. 40 (dhanashi; scan PDF pp. 428–429; first line "śayane gaura"); GPT2 — sect. 3.2, no. 40 ("emane gaura" —
probably a misreading of the recognized text; catalogue: NG-233, formerly NS-004 — the link with PK 103 is mistaken; PK 103 is the neighbouring
no. 41, translated in chapter 3, no. 2). GPT only — C. Nagari-bhava — see the introductory note to the section (chapter 27).
[^30]: "The giver of love embodied" (pīriti-mūrati-dātā) can also be read as "Gora's name is love itself and the giver (of prema)". The ending (9.4)
is not a nagari motif but an ordinary Vaishnava prayer for shelter at the feet of a devotee in whose heart the name of Gaura lives.

## 10. Narahari — "marama kahiva sajani kāya…" (GPT1 p. 170, no. 42)
*Nagari-bhava. "To whom shall I reveal the secret of my heart, friend?" Wherever one looks there is Gauranga: He has entered the lake of the heart,
everything has become Gauranga; awake and asleep, at meals and on the way — only He; in the sky — He; Gauranga's feet are bound to the poet's
heart. Raga dhanashi. **Attribution: probably Sarakara** — simple Bengali; no witnesses; the GPT2 index — Sarakara.*[^31]

**10.1.** *(Refrain)* To whom shall I reveal my heart's secret, friend, to whom reveal my heart's secret?
Rising, sitting, looking about — I see Gauranga Raya.

**10.2.** Into the lake of my heart Gauranga has entered — everything has become Gauranga;
how much can I see with these two eyes? — if only I had a hundred thousand eyes!

**10.3.** Awake — Gauranga, asleep — Gauranga, always I see Gauranga;
at meals — Gauranga, on the way — Gauranga: what has become of me, friend?

**10.4.** I look at the sky — and there is Gauranga, always I see Gauranga;
Narahari says: Gauranga's feet are bound to the heart.[^32]

[^31]: Signature: "Narahari." GPT1 — p. 170, no. 42 (dhanashi; scan PDF p. 429); GPT2 — sect. 3.2, no. 42 (catalogue: NG-076). GPT only — C. Cf. no. 9: the same
motif, "Gaura everywhere". Nagari-bhava — see the introductory note to the section (chapter 27).
[^32]: "Seeing the beloved everywhere" is a sign of the highest prema (so the gopis see Krishna everywhere; cf. CC Madhya 8, the conversation with
Ramananda Raya: the maha-bhagavata sees his Lord in everything moving and unmoving). The ending is the poet's prayer.

## 11. Narahari — "majilum̐ gaura-pīrite…" (GPT1 p. 170, no. 43)
*Nagari-bhava. "I have drowned in love for Gaura, friend": His incomparable form has dissolved into the whole world — the atasi and champaka

**21.6.** On the road by which we both go to the ghat of the Suradhuni,
on that very road Gora stands with His dear companions.

**21.7.** Seeing the sweetness of that form, my sister-in-law could not keep her composure:
she began to shake all over, trembling under Madana's onslaught.[^58]

**21.8.** The pot fell from her hip to the ground, her hair came loose,
her clothes slip from her body of themselves — and there is not a trace of memory.

**21.9.** With difficulty she regained her composure, greatly embarrassed,
took me by both hands and says softly, softly, looking into my face:

**21.10.** 'Know for certain: you, my virtuous brother's wife, are dearer to me than life;
all I have said to you — do not hold it against me; I am in your power.

**21.11.** Whatever you do — tell me without embarrassment;
I will help you, even at the cost of my life,' she swears.

**21.12.** 'Only tell no one about this, keep it secret;
I have fallen into this rasa — what can I say? — Gaurahari has driven me mad!'

**21.13.** Thus she said much — hearing it, my joy knew no bounds,
and remembering what had gone before, much bitterness rose in me.

**21.14.** I thought: what is the use of spreading all this?"
Narahari says: one whose conduct is noble guards another's shame.[^59]

## Summary of chapter 29

*The chapter opens the nagari padas signed "Narahari" from section 3.2 of GPT. Before translating, section 3.2 of GPT1 (pp. 157–237, 180 padas) was
read through on the scan: 95 padas bear Narahari's signature, of which 32 were not in the catalogue (new lines NG-334…365); the witness of almost
all the long padas of this series is Narahari Chakravarti's *Gaura-charita-chintamani*, kiranas 7–11 ("the nagaris of Navadvipa"), which confirms
Majumdar's argument: the long padas of GPT with this signature are not Sarakara's. Translated: the cycle of the *Bhakti-ratnakara* (12th wave) —
the speeches of the "anuragavatis", the women of Nadia, by the tree on the bank of the Ganga (nos. 1–7: five padas of Yadunandana, a pada of Yadu
and a pada of Narahari Chakravarti; in them the townswomen see Gaura in the bhava of Krishna calling Radha and recognize in Him "the Moon of
Gokula"); the short padas of GPT1 3.2 nos. 39–45 in simple Bengali, which the GPT2 index assigns to Sarakara (nos. 8–13; "probably Sarakara" — C,
no. 43 — "not established"); the beginning of the 7th kirana of GCC — the townswomen's morning conversations and their stories of how, deceiving
their elders, they saw Gaura (nos. 14–21; "probably Chakravarti" — B). The padas of other poets of the section (nos. 49–60, Govinda Dasa, Jnanadasa)
were not translated; Lochana Dasa's padas (nos. 61–85, 116) are translated in chapters 28 and 34.*
