# Padas of the Puri Period

*Padas by contemporaries on the Lord's life in Puri after the sannyasa, in the order of events: the arrival in Nilachala
and Sarvabhauma's house, the darshan of Jagannatha, sankirtana on the seashore, Radha's mood in the company of Svarupa
Damodara and Ramananda Raya, the states of separation (dasha), the Gambhira, and the divine madness of prema
(divya-unmada). Majumdar (*Sri-Chaitanya-chariter upadan*, ch. 2) regards the Puri padas of Narahari Sarakara, Vasu Ghosha
and Vasu Ramananda as the testimony of those who came to the Lord every year with the pilgrims from Gauda. The text
follows the *Padakalpataru* (PK) and the *Gaura-pada-tarangini* (GPT1 — 1st ed., 1903; GPT2 — 2nd ed., 1934); the Bengali
original, variants and word-for-word translation are in the full version of the book.*

## 1. Madhavi Dasa — "kalaha kariyā chhalā…" (PK 2239)
*Lila: the arrival in Puri (1510). Making a quarrel His pretext, the Lord goes on ahead to Jagannatha; Nityananda and the
devotees follow His footprints; at the Lion Gate Nitai questions the people of Nilachala; the Lord enters the house of the
Bhattacharya (Sarvabhauma). Raga varadi (PK), suhai (GPT).*[^1]

**1.1.** Making a quarrel His pretext, the Lord went on ahead
to see the Lord of Nilachala.
All the devotees, their hearts full of grief,
run after Him, following His footprints.[^2]

**1.2.** *(Refrain)* Nitai, in the fire of separation, is as if in a daze.
From Atharanala, weeping and weeping on the road,
goes Nitai, the moon of avadhutas.[^3]

**1.3.** Coming to the Lion Gate, with pain in His heart,
Nityananda Raya stopped.
Crying "Hare Krishna, Hari!" He asks the people of Nilachala:
"Have you seen a sannyasi?

**1.4.** His golden form surpasses the gold of the Jambu river,
a red cloth shines on His body;
brimming with prema, He sheds tears from both eyes
and runs, crying 'Hari, Hari!'"[^4]

**1.5.** Leaving the dress of a nagara, the Lord wanders from land to land —
now He wears the garb of a sannyasi.
Madhavi Dasa says: the wondrous Gora Raya
has entered the house of the Bhattacharya.[^5]

[^1]: The signature in PK is "mādhavī dāsa" (Madhavi Dasa). Who this poet was is not established: Majumdar does not name
him among the contemporaries; some identify the poet with Madhavi Devi of Puri, the sister of Shikhi Mahiti, whom the
Lord counted among Radha's closest maidservants (CC Antya 2), but there is no proof — the attribution is doubtful. GPT1
and GPT2 give the same pada with different readings in almost every line and with another ending — the signature of
Govinda Dasa ("Govinda Dasa says: I saw His entry into Sarvabhauma's house"). In PK the pada opens the 21st pallava
("The sannyasa of Sri Gauranga, etc."). The events: CC Madhya 5–6; CB Antya 2; Murari 3.10–3.11; Lochana, Shesha-khanda.
[^2]: The "quarrel": according to CC (Madhya 5) and CB (Antya 2), Nityananda broke the Lord's sannyasa staff, and the
Lord, as if angered, went on ahead alone. The poet calls the quarrel a "pretext": the Lord Himself wished to come first
to Jagannatha (and to Sarvabhauma).
[^3]: Atharanala is the bridge of eighteen arches at the entrance to Puri; here, according to CC (Madhya 5), the Lord
learned that His staff had been broken and went on ahead alone. An avadhuta is a wanderer above outward rules; so
Nityananda is called.
[^4]: Jambunada is the gold of the river Jambu, the finest gold (Puranas). The red cloth is the saffron garb of a
sannyasi. According to CC (Madhya 6), on seeing Jagannatha the Lord fell senseless in the temple, and Sarvabhauma had
Him carried to his house; when Nityananda and the companions came to the Lion Gate, people told them of the sannyasi
who had been taken to the Bhattacharya's.
[^5]: The first line of this couplet ("Leaving the dress of a nagara…") is absent from the "P-R-Sa" manuscript.
"Nagarali" is the manner and dress of a nagara (an elegant young townsman): a hint at the Lord's Navadvipa form as it
is sung by the poets of nagari-bhava. Vrindavana Dasa stresses that in this incarnation the Lord did not so much as
glance at another man's wife; the Gaudiya tradition (Vrindavana Dasa, Krishnadasa Kaviraja, Bhaktisiddhanta) does not
accept the worship of Gaura in the spirit of "Gaura-nagari".

## 2. Madhavi — "nityānanda saṅgati mukunda gadādhare…" (PK 2240)
*Lila: the sequel to the previous pada — the companions find the Lord in Sarvabhauma's house; the people of Nilachala
gaze at Him. Raga — the same (varadi).*[^6]

**2.1.** Together with Nityananda, Mukunda and Gadadhara
beheld Gaurachandra in Sarvabhauma's house.

**2.2.** His lustre is like molten gold, His cloth is red;
His two reddish eyes brim with tears of prema.

**2.3.** His arms reach to His knees, adorned with sandal;
His nose is high, His brow graced with the urdhva-tilaka.

**2.4.** Gopinatha, Vaninatha, Sarvabhauma, Kashi —
all the people of Nilachala gaze at Gora's form.[^7]

**2.5.** Nitai sits on the right, Gadadhara on the left:
all the followers of Gorachand have gathered.

**2.6.** Whoever sees Gora's face floats in prema;
Madhavi alone is left out — through the fault of Madhavi's own deeds.[^8]

[^6]: The signature is "mādhavi" (MS "ka": "mādhavī"; "P-R-Sa": "mādhava"); on the poet see note 1. In GPT (GPT1, p. 402) the
pada follows the pada on Sarvabhauma seeing the Lord's forms. Cf. CC Madhya 6: Nityananda, Mukunda, Gadadhara (and
Jagadananda) come to Sarvabhauma's house, where the Lord lies senseless.
[^7]: Gopinatha Acharya, Sarvabhauma's brother-in-law (his sister's husband), had known the Lord in Navadvipa; Vaninatha
Raya was the brother of Ramananda Raya; Kashi is evidently Kashi Mishra, in whose house the Lord later lived. According
to CC (Madhya 10), the Lord met Vaninatha and Kashi Mishra after His return from the South; the poet gathers them into a
single scene.
[^8]: A formula of humility usual in a bhanita: the poet laments being deprived of what was given to others.

## 3. Narahari (Sarakara) — "dekhi gorā nīlāchala-nātha…" (PK 799)
*Lila: the darshan of Jagannatha — the Lord, in a gopi's mood, reproaches Krishna. In PK it is the gaura-chandrika to
the section "reproaches to Krishna" (akshepanuraga). According to Majumdar, an eyewitness description unequalled in
literature. Raga suhai.*[^9]

**3.1.** Seeing the Lord of Nilachala,
Gora, together with His own associates,

**3.2.** was overwhelmed by a gopi's mood,
and, reproaching, the Lord says:

**3.3.** "Without You I cannot live,
yet You will not even turn round to look at Me!

**3.4.** You wove a snare of love —
You put the moon of the sky into My hands;

**3.5.** and now to see You is only tidings and hearsay," —
so says Gora, overwhelmed by the mood.[^10]

**3.6.** His reddish eyes are brimming with tears,
His face, so full of sweetness, has grown wan.

**3.7.** Wondrous is Gauranga's play —
Narahari Dasa has told a little of it.

[^9]: The signature is "Narahari Dasa"; Majumdar ascribes the pada to Narahari Sarakara of Shrikhanda and places it among
the Puri padas written in the Lord's lifetime. GPT (GPT1, p. 299, no. 23) has the same pada with minor variants. In
Radha's mood the Lord sees Krishna Himself in Jagannatha — cf. CC Madhya 13 (at the Ratha-yatra: "You are the same, I am
the same…") and Antya 14 (darshan at the Garuda-stambha). The gopi's reproaches are a manifestation of the highest prema,
not of worldly resentment.
[^10]: The reading "sandeśa" (PK, GPT) means "tidings", "news"; a similar line of Chandidasa reads "sandeha" ("doubtful").
The sense is the same: the meeting that was once so close has become unattainable.

## 4. Vasu Ramananda — "nāchaye chaitanya chintāmaṇi…" (PK 2082)
*Lila: the Lord dances in the sankirtana. According to Majumdar, Puri: the Lord is called here by His sannyasa name —
"Chaitanya". Raga pathamanjari.*[^11]

**4.1.** *(Refrain)* Chaitanya dances — the chintamani gem;
streams of tears flow down His chest like strings of pearls.

**4.2.** In prema, His voice choked, He rolls on the ground;
suddenly, with a thundering cry, He rises to His full height.

**4.3.** Again and again He whirls, arms raised,
and makes the fallen say "Hari, Hari!"[^12]

**4.4.** He sings the name of Hari, ceaselessly repeating it —
no one can fathom these rare signs.

**4.5.** His boundless glory and virtues the whole world sings;
Vasu Ramananda begs Him for the treasure of prema.

[^11]: Vasu Ramananda was from Kulinagrama, a descendant of Maladhara Vasu (Gunaraja Khan); according to CC (Madhya 15),
the Lord charged the people of Kulinagrama — Satyaraja Khan and Ramananda Vasu — to bring silk ropes every year for the
Ratha-yatra. Majumdar regards him as a contemporary and an eyewitness. In GPT2 the pada is given twice (the section
"dance and kirtana", nos. 17 and 74); in the second instance the first couplet is marked as the refrain. Cf. CC Madhya 11,
13 (the Lord's kirtanas in Puri).
[^12]: The chintamani is the wish-fulfilling gem. "Makes the fallen say the name": the Lord gives the holy name to all
without distinction.

## 5. Narahari (Sarakara) — "tribhuvana-manohara śachīra nandana mora…" (PK 2241)
*Lila: from the sannyasa to Nilachala — the Lord leaves His former appearance and gives away the holy name and prema;
sankirtana on the seashore with Svarupa, Rupa, Ramananda, Govinda and Paramananda Puri. According to Majumdar, a pada by
an eyewitness of the Puri period. Raga bhatiyari.*[^13]

**5.1.** The enchanter of the three worlds, Shachi's son, my Lord,
who lived in the town of Nadia,
gave up all His wealth and took sannyasa —
and He is in Nilachala, near Jagannatha.

**5.2.** Those curling locks at the sight of which Rati's husband himself loses his wits —
such locks He has had shaved;
golden armlets and bracelets, necklaces of gems and pearls —
all that enchanting dress He has abandoned.[^14]

**5.3.** Compassionate to all living beings, giving everyone the name of Hari,
He delivers the greatest sinners;
the prema rare even for the gods, which Lakshmi and others long for,
He gives away to the fallen.

**5.4.** With all the devotees, in the great joy of sankirtana,
He roams along the seashore;
Svarupa, Rupa, Ramananda, Govinda, Paramananda —
all the companions have gathered.[^15]

**5.5.** Narahari Dasa says: my Gaurahari
became so out of love for Radha.
By such a flood of prema the whole world is blessed —
why then am I alone left out?[^16]

[^13]: The signature is "Narahari Dasa". Majumdar names the pada twice: as PK 2241 (kirtana on the seashore) and, by a
misprint, as "PK 1241" (the line about armlets and necklaces — his argument that the Lord's "dress" in the Navadvipa padas
is no invention). GPT1 (p. 403, no. 4) has the same pada with minor variants. In PK it stands in the 21st pallava ("The
sannyasa of Sri Gauranga, etc."), immediately after Madhavi's two padas.
[^14]: Rati's husband is Kamadeva, the god of love. The shaven locks: the sannyasa at Katwa (CC Adi 17; CB Madhya 28;
Lochana, Madhya-khanda).
[^15]: Svarupa Damodara and Ramananda Raya were the Lord's inseparable companions in the last years (CC Madhya 2; Antya 14–20);
Rupa Gosvami came to Puri (CC Antya 1); Govinda was the Lord's servant; Paramananda Puri, a disciple of
Madhavendra Puri, lived with the Lord in Puri (CC Madhya 10). "Rupa" is absent from MSS "ka", "kha", "cha" ("Svarupa"
without "Rupa"); "P-R-Sa" reads "Svarupa and others".
[^16]: "Became so out of love for Radha": cf. CC Adi 1.6 and 4 — Krishna took on Radha's lustre and mood in order to
taste Her love. A bhanita with the words "why then am I alone left out" is Narahari's habitual formula of humility (cf.
PK 2259 in chapter 1).

## 6. Narahari (Sarakara) — "rāmānanda svarūpera sane…" (PK 820)
*Lila: the Lord with Svarupa and Ramananda, in the mood of Radha who has heard Krishna's flute. In PK it is the
gaura-chandrika to the section "reproaches to the flute" (akshepanuraga). According to Majumdar, Puri, an eyewitness.
Raga suhini (PK), suhai (GPT).*[^17]

**6.1.** Sitting with Ramananda and Svarupa,
Gora is lost in thought.

**6.2.** With a start He cries: "Friend, friend!" —
and at times, falling silent, He rails at the flute.

**6.3.** And again He says to Svarupa:
"The flute destroys My family and My honour.

**6.4.** Its sound has entered My ears and stayed there —
it has deafened Me."[^18]

**6.5.** Narahari smiles within himself,
seeing this play of Gauranga.

[^17]: The signature is "Narahari"; Majumdar ascribes the pada to Narahari Sarakara and to the Puri period. In GPT1 (p. 299,
no. 24) it follows directly on "Seeing the Lord of Nilachala" (PK 799). In the last years Svarupa Damodara and Ramananda
Raya sustained the Lord with songs and verses answering to His mood (CC Madhya 2; Antya 14–17); on Krishna's flute heard by the
Lord in Radha's mood, see CC Antya 15–17.
[^18]: Radha's words: the sound of Krishna's flute draws the gopis out of their homes, making them forget their duty to
family and their good name. This is the language of pure prema, not of worldly passion.

## 7. Narahari (Sarakara) — "āre mora gaurakiśora. puruva prema-rase bhora…" (PK 840)
*Lila: holding the hands of Svarupa Damodara and Ramananda Raya, the Lord laments: "No one understands My heart." In PK
it is the gaura-chandrika to the section "reproaches to a friend" (akshepanuraga). According to Majumdar, Puri, an
eyewitness. Raga suhai.*[^19]

**7.1.** Ah, my young Gaura —
lost in the rasa of a former love!

**7.2.** Holding the hands of Svarupa Damodara and Ramaraya,
He cries: "Alas, alas!"

**7.3.** He speaks softly, His voice breaking,
and again and again heaves long sighs.

**7.4.** "No one understands My heart," —
says the Lord, beside Himself.

**7.5.** "Why did I let this love grow?
I am still alive — yet My heart is already lost."[^20]

**7.6.** Tears pour unceasingly from both eyes;
Narahari's face is sorrowful.

[^19]: The signature is "Narahari"; Majumdar (whose text misprints "PK 804") cites the third line ("Svarupa Damodara,
Ramaraya…") as proof that the pada describes Puri. GPT1 (p. 299, no. 26) has it with minor variants. A pada by
Chaitanyadasa, PK 1985, opens with the same line — but there it is the joy of meeting: "I have found the young Lord of
Vraja." Cf. CC Antya 14–20 (at night the Lord reveals His mood to Svarupa and Ramananda) and Antya 20 (Shikshashtaka 8:
Radha's words).
[^20]: Words in Radha's mood: love for Krishna in separation burns so fiercely that the heart seems already lost. The
line conveys the force of vipralambha, not worldly despair.

## 8. Narahari (Sarakara?) — "prema kari kulavatī sane…" (GPT1, pp. 309–310)
*Lila: the Lord, in the mood of Radha as khandita (wounded by Her beloved's faithlessness), complains to Svarupa of Kanu,
who did not come to the tryst. Only in GPT (section "khandita, mana"). Raga suhai.*[^21]

**8.1.** Having loved a wife of a noble house —
how much deceit there is in Kanu's heart!

**8.2.** With the sound of His flute He gave the sign —
and I came out of My house.[^22]

**8.3.** He said: "We shall meet again," —
so I came to the bower-grove.

**8.4.** In so many ways I adorned Myself,
and in hope I spent the night in the bower.

**8.5.** But Kanu, deceiving Me,
spent the night — in whose house?

**8.6.** Having said this to Svarupa, Gora
weeps in wounded love, beside Himself.

**8.7.** Narahari, seeing this, weeps:
how is he to steady his heart?

[^21]: The signature is "Narahari". The pada is in neither PK nor BR — only in GPT1 and GPT2 (identically); so by
Majumdar's criterion (1959) its authenticity is not proven: padas known only from GPT he regards as unreliable.
B. B. Majumdar in a later work (1961) accepts it as a pada of Narahari Sarakara, known from the collection of Advaitadasa
Pandita Babaji. The language is simple, without Vrajabuli, and there is no sambhoga — this fits the marks of Narahari's
genuine padas named by Majumdar. The khandita is one of the eight heroines of rasa-shastra; in Radha's mood the Lord
speaks to Svarupa as Radha to Lalita (cf. CC Antya 14–19).
[^22]: "I came" — in the original "mui āila" (a 3rd-person form with "I", so in both editions of GPT). The speech is
Radha's; the poet conveys what the Lord says in Her mood. Radha's jealousy and wounded pride (mana) are a manifestation
of the highest prema, not of a worldly feeling.

## 9. Narahari (Sarakara) — "gaura sundara mora…" (PK 853)
*Lila: the Lord, sitting in solitude, in Radha's mood reproaches Kamadeva. In PK it is the gaura-chandrika to the section
"reproaches to Kandarpa" (akshepanuraga). Raga tudi (PK), pahadi (GPT1).*[^23]

**9.1.** *(Refrain)* My beautiful Gaura!
Why, sitting alone in solitude, does He shed tears?

**9.2.** His heart is shaken by love for Hari;
He speaks softly, His voice breaking:
"How much harm Manasija does —
can the soul bear so much?

**9.3.** The body of a weak woman he has tormented,
entering the very depth of the heart."
Saying such words from the former lila,
He lowers His moonlike face.[^24]

**9.4.** Incoherently, as if in a trance, Gora says something —
no one knows the secret of His heart;
He is ever absorbed in His former deeds, —
sings Narahari Dasa.

[^23]: The signature is "Narahari Dasa"; Majumdar mentions the pada among Narahari Sarakara's gaura-chandrikas in the
akshepanuraga sections of PK; B. B. Majumdar (1961) also gives it as Sarakara's. GPT1 (p. 292, no. 51) has it with
variants. No place is named; the mood of separation from Krishna that the pada describes is, according to CC, the mood of
the last years in Puri (Antya 14–20).
[^24]: Manasija ("born of the mind") is Kamadeva. The scriptures call the gopis' love "kama" in name only: "the prema of
the gopis became known as 'kama'" (*Bhakti-rasamrita-sindhu* 1.2.285; CC Madhya 8) — there is not a trace of
self-seeking desire in it. "Words from the former lila": Radha's speeches in separation from Krishna.

## 10. Narahari (Sarakara) — "ki lāgi dhulāya dhūsara soṇāra varaṇa gaura-deha…" (PK 1902)
*Lila: the mood of separation — the Lord's body has grown wan (malinangata, one of the ten states of separation). In PK
it is in the section "The ten states" (dasha-dasha). Raga malashi-vala (PK), suhai (GPT1).*[^25]

**10.1.** Why has Gaura's golden body
turned grey with dust?
His seat and adornments He has given up entirely:
I know not for whose love.

**10.2.** *(Refrain)* Hari, Hari! Gaurangachand has grown wan!
Moaning "uhu, uhu", crying aloud,
He beats His breast with His hand and weeps.

**10.3.** His whole body is drenched with sweat,
He heaves long sighs.
Such is the way of Rai's love, —
says Narahari Dasa.[^26]

[^25]: The signature is "Narahari Dasa"; Majumdar quotes the line "Moaning 'uhu, uhu'…" among Narahari Sarakara's padas on
the mood of separation (viraha). GPT1 (p. 311, no. 11) has it with variants. The ten states (dasha) of separation
according to rasa-shastra (*Ujjvala-nilamani*): anxious brooding, sleeplessness, agitation, emaciation, wanness of the
body (malinangata), incoherent speech (pralapa), sickness, the madness of prema (unmada), swoon (moha), and the last, extreme state. In PK the pada
is headed: "Now — wanness of the body. Sri Mahaprabhu, overcome by this mood".
[^26]: "The way of Rai's love": the Lord experiences Radha's separation from Krishna (CC Antya 14–20); this is no worldly
pining but the highest stage of prema — mahabhava.

## 11. Narahari (Sarakara) — "soṇāra varaṇa gaurāṅgasundara pāṇḍura bhai gela deha…" (PK 1908)
*Lila: the mood of separation — "sickness" (vyadhi-dasha): pallor, trembling, sweat, tears, incoherent speech. Raga
kamoda.*[^27]

**11.1.** Golden, beautiful Gauranga —
His body has turned pale;
as if from cold, He trembles again and again,
remembering a former love.

**11.2.** He says nothing, only heaves long sighs —
like a painted doll;
from both His eyes tears stream
like the currents of the Mandakini.[^28]

**11.3.** His whole body is drenched with sweat —
who knows from what fever;
now He sings, now He sobs,
He murmurs something incoherent.

**11.4.** Narahari says: my Gaurahari
gazes about like a beggar;
He cries "Hari, Hari", raising both His arms —
who will fathom His heart?

[^27]: The signature is "Narahari"; B. B. Majumdar (1961) gives the pada as Narahari Sarakara's. GPT1 (pp. 317–318, no. 17)
has it with minor variants. In PK it is in the section "The ten states", under the heading "The state of sickness
(vyadhi). Sri Gaurachandra, accordingly".
[^28]: The Mandakini is the heavenly Ganga. "A painted doll": stillness (stambha), one of the sattvika-bhavas.

## 12. Vasu Ramananda — "āre mora gaurakiśora. sahachara-kandhe pahum̐…" (PK 1924; GPT1, p. 318)
*Lila: the mood of separation — "the ninth state": leaning on His companions' shoulders, the Lord falls senseless and,
naming Mathura, calls: "O Natha!" According to Majumdar, Puri. Raga pahida (GPT1).*[^29]

**12.1.** *(Refrain)* Ah, my young Gaura!
Resting His arms on His companions' shoulders,
the Lord, in the ninth state, has lost Himself.[^30]

**12.2.** Fallen on the ground, He cannot utter a word;
no one dares to touch Him.
Golden Gaurahari says: "Alas, alas for Me!" —
His body has become thin as a thread.

**12.3.** With a fixed gaze, naming Mathura,
the Lord weeps: "O Natha!"
Vasu Ramananda says: why is Gauranga so —
I have not understood for what reason.[^31]

[^29]: The signature is "Vasu Ramananda". In PK the pada stands in the section "The ten states" (no. 1924), but the
available scan of PK vol. 3 lacks printed pp. 147–150, and only the last couplet survives (on p. 151); the full text is
given from GPT1 (no. 20, p. 318), where the pada follows the previous one (PK 1908). Majumdar (who misprints it "PK
1920") assigns the pada to Puri: the body "like a thread" and the weeping with the name of Mathura describe the mood of
the last years, not of Navadvipa.
[^30]: The ninth of the ten states of separation is moha, swoon (see note 25).
[^31]: Radha, in separation from Krishna who has gone to Mathura, calls Him "Natha" — "Lord" (cf. CC Antya 19–20;
Madhavendra Puri's verse "Ayi dina-dayardra natha…", which the Lord loved to repeat — CC Madhya 4, Antya 8).

## 13. Narahari (Sarakara) — "gambhīrā bhitare gorā rāya…" (PK 1643)
*Lila: the Gambhira — a sleepless night: the Lord laments, weeps, trembles, rubs His face against the walls and cries:
"Where is the Lord of My life?" In PK — "lamentation in the state of mere sleeplessness" (kevala-jagarana-dasha).
According to Majumdar, the quintessence of the Antya-lila of the *Chaitanya-charitamrita*. Raga — the same as that of
the preceding pada (PK).*[^32]

**13.1.** In the Gambhira Gora Raya
passes the night without sleep.

**13.2.** Again and again He laments,
now He weeps, now He trembles.

**13.3.** Now He rubs His face and head against the wall —
no one is beside the Lord.[^33]

**13.4.** Now He weeps, raising both His arms:
"Where is the Lord of My life?"

**13.5.** Narahari says: my Gora
is lost in the love of Rai.

[^32]: The signature is "Narahari"; Majumdar places the pada among Narahari Sarakara's Puri padas. Neither the archive.org
OCR nor the new recognition of PK contains the text — it has been read from the scan (vol. 3, printed p. 30); GPT1
(p. 314, no. 5) and GPT2 have it with variants. The Gambhira is the small inner room in Kashi Mishra's house where the
Lord spent His last years (CC Antya 6, 14–20).
[^33]: According to CC (Antya 19), one night the Lord, in the agony of separation, rubbed His face against the walls of
the Gambhira until it was covered with wounds; after that Svarupa asked Shankara Pandita to sleep at the Lord's feet.
Here is the same state: "no one is beside Him".

## 14. Narahari (Sarakara) — "gaurāṅga-chāndera bhāva kahane nā yāya…" (PK 832)
*Lila: in solitude the Lord tells His companions: "I will throw Myself into the sea." In PK it is the gaura-chandrika to
the section "reproaches to oneself" (akshepanuraga). According to Majumdar, Puri: the pada foreshadows the Lord's fall
into the sea. Raga tudi.*[^34]

**14.1.** Gaurangachand's mood cannot be put into words:
sitting in solitude, the Lord moans: "Alas, alas!"

**14.2.** His dear companions question Him —
He says: "I will throw Myself into the sea!"[^35]

**14.3.** "By My own will I began this cruel love:
disgrace has fallen on both families — and still life goes on."[^36]

**14.4.** Having said this, Gorachand sighs deeply;
understanding His heart, Narahari Dasa speaks.

[^34]: The signature is "Narahari Dasa"; Majumdar ascribes the pada to Narahari Sarakara and to Puri. GPT1 (p. 299, no. 25)
has it with variants. In PK the pada stands as the gaura-chandrika to the words of Radha reproaching Herself for Her love
of Krishna.
[^35]: Instead of "into the sea", MS "ka" of PK, GPT1 and GPT2 read "into the waters of the Yamuna", MSS "cha" and
"P-R-Sa" — "into the Yamuna". In Radha's mood the sea is the Yamuna to the Lord: according to CC (Antya 18), one night the
Lord, taking the sea for the Yamuna, threw Himself into it, and a fisherman drew Him out with his net. The PK reading
("into the sea") points to Puri; the reading "into the Yamuna" conveys Radha's words.
[^36]: "Both families" (du kule) — the father's family and the husband's; the words of Radha as parakiya. Her readiness to
give up everything for Krishna is the highest expression of prema, not worldly despair.

## 15. Vasu Ghosha — "siṃhadvāra teji gorā samudra āṛe dhāya…" (PK 1662)
*Lila: the divine madness of prema (divya-unmada): the Lord runs from the Lion Gate towards the sea, asking everyone:
"Where is Krishna?", and falls senseless. In PK — the section "Divya-unmada. Here — Sri Mahaprabhu". According to
Majumdar, an eyewitness's testimony whose vividness is matched only by Raghunatha Dasa. Raga suhai.*[^37]

**15.1.** Leaving the Lion Gate, Gora runs towards the sea,
asking everyone: "Where is Krishna? Where is Krishna?"

**15.2.** All around, the devotees sing the glories of Hari,
and in their midst a golden mountain rolls in the dust.

**15.3.** He falls headlong, He rolls on the ground;
tall Gora falls senseless.[^38]

**15.4.** He lies on His back, foam comes from His mouth;
Vasudeva Ghosha's heart is breaking.

[^37]: The signature is "Vasudeva Ghosha". Majumdar counts the pada among Vasu Ghosha's historical padas (the run to the
sea in Puri). GPT1 (p. 314, no. 6) and GPT2 have it with minor variants. The Lion Gate (Simha-dvara) is the main, eastern
gate of Jagannatha's temple. Cf. CC Antya 14 (seeing the sand hill Chataka-parvata, the Lord took it for Govardhana and
ran towards it) and Antya 17–18; Raghunatha Dasa's *Gauranga-stava-kalpataru*. In PK (no. 1663) and GPT (no. 7) there
follows a pada on how the Lord, coming to Himself, sees Svarupa and Ramaraya before Him: in PK it is unsigned, in the
"P-R-Sa" MS it bears the signature of Govinda Dasa, in GPT that of Vasu Ghosha; it is not included in this chapter.
[^38]: Falling, trembling, foam at the mouth, unconsciousness are sattvika-bhavas and manifestations of mahabhava,
described in CC as well (Antya 14, 17); this is not illness but the working of the highest prema.
