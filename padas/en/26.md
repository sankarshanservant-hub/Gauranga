# Appendix. Padas signed "Narahari" from the Gaura-pada-tarangini — X: companions and followers

*Continuation and conclusion of the appendix (the introductory note on the two Naraharis, on the arguments for attribution and on the marks in
the headings is in `17.md`). Portion 26: GPT section 6.3 "Companions" (GPT1 pp. 451–489) — all eighteen padas signed Narahari (nos. 2, 4, 7, 8,
42–44, 49, 55, 58, 61, 62, 68, 69, 71, 72, 77, 83), and also one pada on the sannyasa found in the continuous check of signatures in sections 3.x,
4.x and 5.3–7 (GPT1 5.3, no. 7). The first padas of the section are about Gaura's companions (Gadadhara Pandita, Srivasa, Gadadhara Dasa, the circle
of companions); then come padas about the followers of later generations: Shyamananda, the poet Jnanadasa, Srinivasa Acharya, Narottama,
Ramachandra and Govinda Kaviraja and their disciples. Such padas are marked in the heading "late layer: on the followers": they are not
Gaura-lila in the narrow sense but praises of the acharyas of the tradition through whom Gaura's mercy reached the following generations. The
signatures of the section were checked throughout, in the OCR and on the scan; the 14th and 15th waves of the Bhakti-ratnakara (BR) and its
conclusion, and the Gaura-charita-chintamani (GCC, kiranas 1 and 4), were read continuously at the relevant places. Not translated (outside the
appendix): nos. 15, 41, 48, 54, 63, 82 — signed Ghanashyama; no. 13 — signed Papiya Shekhara (about Narahari Sarakara; the catalogue had
counted it as Narahari's); no. 70 — signed Vallabha; no. 6 — no signature; the other padas of the section are by other poets. Pages 462–463 of
GPT1 (no. 27, end, to no. 30) are missing from the scan: these padas were checked in GPT2 — signed Madho and Radhavallabha. The base text is
GPT1 (by the scan), collated with GPT2, the BR (eds. 1912, 1960 and 2006) and the GCC (ed. 1947); the PK, KGC, PS, Gita-chandrodaya and
Narottama-vilasa were checked. Pronouns referring to Gaura, Nityananda and Advaita are capitalized, those referring to the companions and
followers are not. The notes of the GPT editors are not reproduced. At the end — a concluding note to the whole appendix.*

## 1. Narahari — "dhanya dhanya bali mena…" (GPT1 pp. 452–453, section 6.3, no. 2)
*The birth of Gadadhara Pandita: on the new-moon day of the month of Vaishakha, in the house of Madhava Mishra in Nadia, Gadadhara, Gauranga's
beloved, was born; Madhava and Ratnavati are beside themselves with joy, the people of Nadia praise their fortune, the brahmanas' wives bless
the infant, placing rice and durva grass on his head; the poet asks to sing Gadai's qualities birth after birth. Raga pahida. **Attribution: not
established** — GPT only; plain Bengali tripadi; the GPT2 index does not assign it to Sarakara.*[^1]

**1.1.** "Glory, glory!" all say: in all four yugas there was nothing like it — the fortune of Kali knows no limit:
in beautiful Nadia, in the house of Madhava Mishra — what wondrous rejoicing!

**1.2.** On the new-moon day of Vaishakha, at an auspicious moment, Gadadhara, Gauranga's beloved, was born.
Sri Madhava and Ratnavati, seeing their son's face, are beside themselves with joy.

**1.3.** What beauty Gadadhara has — a delight to the eyes of all, as if the abode of every joy!
The colour of his skin shines, surpassing pure gold; his whole body is beautiful, incomparable.

**1.4.** All the people of Nadia, forgetting grief and sorrow, say to one another with delight:
"How fortunate Madhava is — as if he had gained a jewel! We do not know for what merits!"

**1.5.** The wives of the brahmanas come, floating in an ocean of bliss, praise mother Ratnavati,
and, gazing on the golden son, place rice and durva grass on his head and joyfully bless him.

**1.6.** By Gadadhara's power all kinds of auspicious rites are performed; the bards hasten one after another [with praises].
Narahari says: "O that birth after birth I might sing Gadaichand's qualities just so!"[^2]

[^1]: GPT1 — pp. 452–453, section 6.3 "Companions" (parikara), no. 2 ("pahida"; scan, PDF pp. 711–712); GPT2 — section 6.3, no. 2 (NG-217). Not found in
the BR, GCC, Gita-chandrodaya, PK, KGC or PS. The reading in 1.1 "bali mena" (so in GPT1 and GPT2) is unclear; understood as "[all] say". C.
[^2]: Gadadhara Pandita — son of Madhava Mishra and Ratnavati of Navadvipa, inseparable companion of Mahaprabhu; according to the
Gaura-ganoddesha-dipika (147–153) he is Sri Radha Herself, "the Lakshmi of Gaura's love". "The new moon of Vaishakha" (kuhu — the new moon) is
the day of his appearance, as also in the Prema-vilasa (vilasa 22). The appearance of the Lord's eternal companion is not a birth under karma:
he descends together with the Lord. Rice and durva grass on the head — a Bengali rite of blessing a newborn. Gadai, Gadaichand — affectionate
names of Gadadhara.

## 2. Narahari — "gadādhara parama sughara rasadhāma…" (GPT1 p. 453, no. 4; GCC)
*Gadadhara among the companions in the morning: his fair body captivates Kama; the smile of his lotus face is nectar; his speech eclipses the
mantra that enchants the world; remembering his former pastimes he loses his composure; the poet smiles, seeing the hidden become manifest. Raga
not specified ("yatharaga"). **Attribution: probably Chakravarti** — the pada is in the GCC; Vrajabuli.*[^3]

**2.1.** Gadadhara is supreme elegance, an abode of rasa:
to his beautiful fair body, captivating in its lustre, how many Kamadevas offer themselves in expiation!

**2.2.** That lotus face, which has conquered a whole forest of lotuses; his gentle smile — like lovely flower-nectar;
again and again, filling the cups of his eyes, he drinks — and in his heart there is great delight.

**2.3.** His soft, sweet speech, ever new, eclipses the mantra that enchants the world;
hearing it, greedy ears — coveted even by the Vedas — forget the hearing of Vedas and tantras.

**2.4.** Remembering in his heart his former pastimes, he loses steadiness, is bereft of motion and floats in boundless happiness.
Away with gold! — so peerless is his prema; Narahari, seeing the hidden become manifest, smiles.[^4]

[^3]: GPT1 — p. 453, no. 4 ("yatharaga"; scan, PDF p. 712); GPT2 — section 6.3, no. 4 (NG-218). GCC — kirana 4 ("The Lord in the circle of devotees":
in the morning the companions gather around Gaura, and the poet describes each of them), pada 17 ("yatharaga"; before it, pada 16 — also on
Gadadhara, signed Narahari, not in the GPT; after it — Ghanashyama's pada on Narahari Sarakara = GPT no. 15). The variants are insignificant (in the
OCR of the GCC the lines of 2.3 are distorted). Not in the BR, PK, KGC, PS or Gita-chandrodaya. B.
[^4]: "How many Kamadevas offer themselves" (niramañchana — a rite of waving something around a beloved and giving it away in expiation) — an
image of beauty before which the god of love fades. "Former pastimes" — the lila of Vraja: according to the Gaura-ganoddesha-dipika (147–153)
Gadadhara Pandita is Sri Radha; in Gaura-lila he hides this bhava, but in ecstasy it shows through — this is "the hidden become manifest".
"Away with gold" — neither the gold of his skin nor anything else compares with his prema. The subject in 2.2 (who "drinks") is not named:
Gadadhara drinks Gaura's beauty with his eyes (so in the context of the GCC), or the onlookers drink the nectar of his smile.

## 3. Narahari — "ājuka sukha kachu baraṇe na jāta…" (GPT1 p. 454, no. 7; GCC)
*Srivasa among the companions: seeing the Lord's play, he smiles softly; from love his body sways, his hairs stand on end as if he wore a
jacket of kadamba flowers; he trembles like a creeper in the wind, repeats "Friend of the lowly!", sings, dances, weeps. Raga not specified
("yatharaga"). **Attribution: probably Chakravarti** — the pada is in the GCC; Vrajabuli.*[^5]

**3.1.** Today's happiness cannot be described in words:
Srivasa — knower of rasa, wise and elegant — seeing the Lord's play, smiles very softly.

**3.2.** His stately body sways with love; graceful is his bearing, incomparable his wondrous beauty;
his thickly risen hairs shine as if he had put on a most beautiful jacket of kadamba flowers.

**3.3.** He trembles like a creeper in the wind and cannot master himself, helpless in the flood of rasa;
ceaselessly he repeats: "Friend of the lowly!" — his heart melts, and steadiness has left him.

**3.4.** He sings the beautiful qualities [of the Lord], lightly dances, streams of tears flow from his eyes.
Narahari says: one cannot fathom how many stirrings of bhava arise in his heart.[^6]

[^5]: GPT1 — p. 454, no. 7 ("yatharaga"; scan, PDF p. 713); GPT2 — section 6.3, no. 7. **The catalogue did not know it** (new row NG-323). GCC — kirana 4,
pada 19 ("yatharaga"). Variants: 3.3 "dīnabandhu" by the GCC and the GPT1 scan (GPT1 OCR — "bīṇa bandhu"). Not in the BR, PK, KGC, PS or
Gita-chandrodaya. B.
[^6]: Srivasa Pandita — in his house the Lord held the nightly sankirtana; according to the Gaura-ganoddesha-dipika (90) he is Narada Muni.
"A jacket of kadamba flowers" — the thrill of the hairs (pulaka), like the knobbly kadamba flower. "Friend of the lowly" (dīnabandhu) — an
address to the Lord.

## 4. Narahari — "sundara sughara gadādhara dāsa…" (GPT1 p. 454, no. 8; GCC)
*Gadadhara Dasa beside Gaura — like a moon beside the moon; his tender body eclipses the champaka flower; his eyes are languid; former bhavas
arise ceaselessly in his heart, and however much he hides them, he cannot. Raga not specified ("yatharaga"). **Attribution: probably
Chakravarti** — the pada is in the GCC; Vrajabuli.*[^7]

**4.1.** Beautiful, elegant is Gadadhara Dasa:
beside Gaura, the jewel of qualities, he shines as if beside the moon another moon had risen.

**4.2.** His most tender body is full of love, his sweet charm robs the champaka flower of its pride;
his bearing shatters composure, the movement of his brows enchants, his lotus feet cannot take a step.

**4.3.** Both his eyes are languid and lovelier than lovely, tremulous, barely blinking;
his clear cheeks gleam, on his red lips — a charming smile.

**4.4.** Unfathomable: in his heart former bhavas ceaselessly arise — in every way;
however he tries to hide them — they cannot be hidden; Narahari, looking at him, laughs, intoxicated with happiness.[^8]

[^7]: GPT1 — p. 454, no. 8 ("yatharaga"; scan, PDF p. 713); GPT2 — section 6.3, no. 8 (NG-219; the signature in GPT1 is "narahari", distorted in the
OCR — "nabrahari"). GCC — kirana 4, pada 20 ("yatharaga"). Not in the BR, PK, KGC, PS or Gita-chandrodaya. B.
[^8]: Gadadhara Dasa — a companion of Gaura and Nityananda (later lived in Ariadaha, cf. portion 24, no. 22); according to the
Gaura-ganoddesha-dipika (154–155) — Chandrakanti, a manifestation of Radha's radiance, and in him also Purnananda, the beloved of Balarama.
"Former bhavas" — his eternal service in Vraja. "Like a moon beside the moon" — both are fair: Gauranga and His companion.

## 5. Narahari — "o mora parāṇa-bandhu śyāmānanda…" (GPT1 p. 469, no. 42; BR)
*Late layer: on the followers. The life of Shyamananda: he left home and came to Ambika to Hridaya Chaitanya, who accepted him as a disciple and
entrusted him to Nitai and Chaitanya; receiving at his departure the garland of permission, he came alone to Vrindavana, at Radha-kunda received
the mercy of Raghunatha Dasa, from Jiva Gosvami fatherly love; he studied the books and, at the Gosvamis' command, returned through Gauda and Ambika
to Utkala, and there intoxicated even the godless with Gaura's name. Raga kamoda. **Attribution: probably Chakravarti** — the pada is in the BR
(15th wave, "the song of Shyamananda's life").*[^9]

**5.1.** O my dear friend Shyamananda, ocean of happiness, ever intoxicated with the qualities of Gora!
Leaving his home far behind, he came joyfully to Ambikapur, to the house of his master.

**5.2.** Seeing Hridaya Chaitanya, he weeps unrestrainedly and falls, rolling on the ground;
taking his feet on his head, surrendering himself, he stands with his whole heart fixed on him.

**5.3.** Seeing Shyamananda's nature, the Thakura grew fond of him, kept him near and made him a disciple;
showing him great mercy and teaching him the way of bhakti, he entrusted him to Nitai and Chaitanya.

**5.4.** After some days Shyamananda grew restless with longing — to be sent to Vraja;
the Lords Nitai and Chaitanya blessed Shyamananda: at the time of departure he was given the garland of permission.

**5.5.** Shyamananda goes on his way, drowned in tears, remembering his master's qualities;
alone, after some days, having visited many holy places, he entered Vrindavana.

**5.6.** Seeing Sri Vrindaranya, he counted himself blessed and could not contain his bliss;
bathed in tears, he rolls on the ground, his whole body thrilling.

**5.7.** Going to Mount Govardhana, he did what was in his heart; coming to the bank of Sri Radha-kunda,
he was overwhelmed with prema — seeing this, Sri Das Gosai, a treasury of virtues, showed him mercy.

**5.8.** He went to Sri Jiva and told who he was; Jiva showed him mercy with fatherly love.
Whatever he had longed for was as if fulfilled — by the mercy of Hridaya Chaitanya.

**5.9.** He wandered through the twelve forests, studied the books, became most skilled in service;
by way of Sri Gauda and Ambika he went and settled in Utkala — at the command of the Gosvamis.

**5.10.** The godless and the asuras he intoxicated with Gora's qualities — to whom did he not give bhakti!
The fallen float in bliss — who did not gain deliverance by a particle of Shyamananda's mercy?

**5.11.** Who will understand his true nature? Ever intoxicated with sankirtana, he is famed on earth for his greatness;
with his companions he sports joyfully — in Utkala happiness knows no bounds.

**5.12.** Whoever sees him even once cannot keep his composure — how enchanting is his form!
Narahari says: "Will the master of Rasikananda ever appear before these eyes?"[^10]

[^9]: GPT1 — p. 469, no. 42 ("kamoda"; scan, PDF p. 726); GPT2 — section 6.3, no. 42 (NG-221). BR 15 (ed. 1912, pp. 1059–1060; ed. 1960, 15.93–104, "the
song of the life of Shrila Shyamananda Prabhu — in song, kamoda"): after the account of Rasikananda and Ghanashyama's pada about him (GPT no. 48) —
"who is not charmed by Shyamananda's qualities? All sing of his life in various ways" (15.92), then this pada and the next (GPT no. 43). Variants:
5.2 BR "eka bhite rahe dāṅḍāiyā" ("stands to one side"; GPT "ekacite" — "single-mindedly"); 5.6 BR "ānande dharite nāre thehā"; the rest
orthographic. Not in the GCC, PK, KGC, PS or Gita-chandrodaya. B.
[^10]: Shyamananda (in his youth Duhkhi Krishnadasa) — a disciple of Hridaya Chaitanya (himself a disciple of Gauridasa Pandita) of Ambika-Kalna;
together with Srinivasa and Narottama he studied under Jiva Gosvami and returned to Gauda with the Gosvamis' books; he preached in Utkala
(Orissa). In Ambika are the Gaura-Nitai Deities of Gauridasa Pandita: the "garland of permission" (ājñāmālā) is a garland from the Deity as a sign
of leave; hence in 5.4 it is "the Lords Nitai and Chaitanya", not only the teacher, who release Shyamananda (cf. the Prema-vilasa, vilasa 12).
"Sri Das Gosai" — Raghunatha Dasa Gosvami at Radha-kunda. "The twelve forests" — the forests of Vraja. "The master of Rasikananda" — Shyamananda,
guru of Rasikananda (Murari), his chief disciple in Utkala. The poet's wish to "see" Shyamananda is a prayer for darshan in the heart (the poet
lived later).

## 6. Narahari — "jaya śrīla duḥkhī kṛṣṇadāsa…" (GPT1 p. 470, no. 43; BR)
*Late layer: on the followers. Praise of Duhkhi Krishnadasa: his mind is a bee at the lotus feet of Hridaya Chaitanya; in Vrindavana, in a grove,
he found Radha's anklet and became known as Shyamananda; he flooded Utkala, where there was not a particle of bhakti, with Gaura's prema; the
poet praises his mercy. Raga suhai. **Attribution: probably Chakravarti** — the pada is in the BR (15th wave, right after the preceding one).*[^11]

**6.1.** Glory to Shrila Duhkhi Krishnadasa! Who has the power to tell his qualities?
His mind-bee is ever at the lotus feet of Hridaya Chaitanya.

**6.2.** In Vrindavana, in a new grove, he found the anklet of Rai;
there he became known as Shyamananda — who will understand his life?

**6.3.** In Utkala, where people utterly darkened in mind had not a trace of bhakti,
he flooded everyone with the rasa of Gaura's prema and made the land blessed.

**6.4.** My Shyamananda, "the Sorrowful" — sorrowing at the great sorrow [of others], the master of Rasikananda —
how can I tell his mercy? He never abandons lowly Narahari.[^12]

[^11]: GPT1 — p. 470, no. 43 ("suhai"; scan, PDF p. 727); GPT2 — section 6.3, no. 43. **The catalogue did not know it** (new row NG-324). BR 15 (ed. 1912,
p. 1060; ed. 1960, 15.105–108, "again — suhai"). Variants: 6.1 BR "śrīduḥkhinī kṛṣṇadāsa" (so in the OCR of the 1960 ed.; GPT "duḥkhī"). Not in the
GCC, PK, KGC, PS or Gita-chandrodaya. B.
[^12]: According to tradition, Duhkhi Krishnadasa, sweeping a grove in Vrindavana, found Sri Radha's anklet (nupura) and received Her mercy; from then
on he was called Shyamananda, and the tilaka of his line is in the form of a foot (Prema-vilasa, vilasa 12; event "Radha's nupura…"). "Duhkhi"
("sorrowful") is his former name; the poet plays on it: Shyamananda grieves at the suffering of fallen souls. Rai — Sri Radha.

## 7. Narahari — "śrī-vīrabhūmete dhāma…" (GPT1 p. 470, no. 44)
*Late layer: on the followers. Praise of the poet Jnanadasa: born in the village of Kandra in Birbhum, renounced from childhood, he received
initiation from Jahnava; in Kandra to this day a three-day festival is held in his honour at the full moon; his names are "Madana-mangala" and
"Manohara"; he went to the festival at Kheturi with Baba Aul; he is the sun among poets, equal to Chandidasa. Raga kamoda. **Attribution: not
established** — GPT only; plain Bengali tripadi; the pada speaks of events after the passing of Narahari Sarakara and of a festival held "to this
day", i.e. it was written much later (not Sarakara; possibly not Chakravarti either).*[^13]

**7.1.** His abode is in Sri Birbhum, the village of Kandra-Mandra: there Jnanadasa was born.
Renounced from his youth, devoted [to God] from childhood, he received initiation from Jahnava.

**7.2.** To this day in the village of Kandra, in the name of the poet Jnanadasa, a great fair is held at the full moon;
three days of festival — all the mahantas come, and there are their games and merriment.

**7.3.** His name is "Madana-mangala", peerless in beauty and virtue, and another title — "Manohara";
when Jnanadasa went to the great festival at Kheturi, Baba Aul was his companion.

**7.4.** He is like the sun in the race of poets, a poet equal to Chandidasa; Jnanadasa is known to the whole world.
His padas are the essence of nectar, like a stream of amrita — so sings Narahari Dasa.[^14]

[^13]: GPT1 — p. 470, no. 44 ("kamoda"; scan, PDF p. 727); GPT2 — section 6.3, no. 44. **The catalogue did not know it** (new row NG-325). Not found in
the BR, GCC, Gita-chandrodaya, Narottama-vilasa, PK, KGC or PS. The BR (14.180) calls Kandra the village "of Sri Mangala Jnanadasa"
(śrīmaṅgala jñānadāsera ālaya) — the same title "Mangala" as in the pada. C.
[^14]: Jnanadasa — one of the greatest Vaishnava poets of the 16th century, a disciple of Jahnava Devi (Nityananda's consort); a participant in the great
festival at Kheturi (the Prema-vilasa, vilasa 19, names him among those who came there from Nityananda's branch). Chandidasa — a poet of the age
before Chaitanya, whose songs Mahaprabhu loved to hear (CC Madhya 2). Who "Baba Aul" is cannot be determined from the pada itself. The pada is a
praise of a poet, not Gaura-lila; included as a pada on the followers (coordinator's decision).

## 8. Narahari — "jaya jaya harirāma ācāryavarya…" (GPT1 pp. 471–472, no. 49; BR, GCC)
*Late layer: on the followers. Praise of Harirama Acharya, disciple of Ramachandra Kaviraja: his life is wondrous and steals hearts; the fame of his
victory over the Lord's opponents has spread through the world; he is ever intoxicated with Gaura's prema, greedy for the rasa of sankirtana,
skilled in serving the Vaishnavas; his exposition of the Bhagavata pours nectar; his life is the Deity Sri Krishna Raya. Raga purabi.
**Attribution: probably Chakravarti** — the pada is in the BR (15th wave) and in the GCC (kirana 1).*[^15]

**8.1.** Glory, glory to Harirama, best of acharyas, whose wondrous life steals hearts!
His virtues are pure, he crushes the pride of calamities, his form is sweet and increases joy.

**8.2.** The fame of his victories over the invincible asuras, turned away from the Lord's feet, has spread through the world;
he is wise and firm, he steals the firmness [of others], his mind is full of compassion, he is boundlessly generous.

**8.3.** Ever intoxicated with the weight of Gaura's prema, he walks more mightily than an intoxicated king of elephants;
he is greedy for the rasa of sankirtana, skilled in serving the Vaishnavas — who can describe his happiness?

**8.4.** His expositions of the Shrimad-Bhagavata and other books pour an incomparable stream of nectar;
Sri Sri Krishna Raya is his very life. How can Narahari describe his boundless greatness?[^16]

[^15]: GPT1 — pp. 471–472, no. 49 ("purabi"; scan, PDF pp. 728–729); GPT2 — section 6.3, no. 49 (NG-222). BR 15 (ed. 1912, p. 1061; ed. 1960, 15.117–120;
"in song, pauravi"): "Srinivasa Acharya's dearest disciple is Ramachandra Kaviraja; Ramachandra's disciple is Harirama Acharya, whose deeds are
wondrous; distributing Sri Krishna Chaitanya's prema-bhakti he destroys the stain of souls… poets sing his life" (15.113–116), then the pada; after
it — the pada on Ramakrishna Acharya (GPT no. 72). GCC — kirana 1 (in a row of vandana-padas to the companions and followers), pada 23 (between the
padas on Ramachandra Kaviraja — GPT no. 69 — and on Ramakrishna — no. 72). Variants: 8.1 BR "śrīharirāma ācāryavarya" (with "śrī"), "madhura
murati"; 8.3 BR the same; in the OCR of the GCC the lines are distorted. Not in the PK, KGC, PS or Gita-chandrodaya. B.
[^16]: Harirama Acharya and his brother Ramakrishna — brahmana scholars converted by Ramachandra Kaviraja and Narottama at the river Padmavati
(Prema-vilasa, vilasa 14): Harirama became Ramachandra's disciple, Ramakrishna Narottama's. "The asuras turned away from the Lord's feet" — the
opponents of bhakti, defeated in debate. "Steals the firmness" (dhīra-dhṛti-hāraka) — with his prema he robs even the steady of their composure.
Sri Krishna Raya — the Deity he served.

## 9. Narahari — "jaya jaya śrīnivāsa ācārya…" (GPT1 pp. 473–474, no. 55; BR)
*Late layer: on the followers. Praise of Srinivasa Acharya: the life of the people of the world, refuge of the fallen, his body like a champaka
flower, beloved companion of Gaurachandra; Narottama is his life; he tastes the nectar of the pastimes of the Divine Couple; Ramachandra Kaviraja
is the servant of his feet; he planted on earth a new wish-fulfilling tree of bhakti. Raga belabali. **Attribution: probably Chakravarti** — the
pada is in the BR (14th wave, Srinivasa at Budhari).*[^17]

**9.1.** Glory, glory to Srinivasa Acharya, life of all the people of the world, supreme knower of rasa, abode of virtues!
Refuge of the wretched, the helpless and the fallen, friend of the lowly, ornament of noble deeds.

**9.2.** He is beautifully adorned with the wondrous ornaments of bhavas, his body like a champaka flower, golden-red;
the peerless beloved companion of Gaurachandra — seeing him, the heart cannot keep its composure.

**9.3.** Famed in the world, a cloud raining the rasa of prema — blissful Narottama, his master and life;
ceaselessly drinking the nectar of the Divine Couple's pastimes, he rejoices in intoxication — what more can a poet compose?

**9.4.** Ah, ah! — the servant of his feet is merciful Ramachandra Kaviraja.
What can I say? — this new wish-fulfilling tree of bhakti he planted on earth for Narahari's sake.[^18]

[^17]: GPT1 — pp. 473–474, no. 55 ("belabali"; scan, PDF pp. 730–731); GPT2 — section 6.3, no. 55. **The catalogue did not know it** (new row NG-326). BR 14
(ed. 1912, pp. ≈1037–1038; ed. 1960, 14.78–81, "again — belabali"): Srinivasa with his disciples comes to the village of Budhari, Narottama comes
there from Kheturi; "all, seeing the Acharya's beauty, lose their composure — who does not sing of his form and virtues?" (14.73), then
Ghanashyama's pada (GPT no. 54), this pada and the pada on Narottama (GPT no. 61); afterwards — the festival at Boraquli. Variants: 9.1 BR "jaya

pada, then — on Ganganarayana (GPT no. 71). GCC — kirana 1, pada 24 ("raga kamoda"). Variants: 16.2 BR "paṇḍitavara durmukha-madahārī" ("best of
panditas"; GPT "baṛa durmmukha-madahārī"); the rest orthographic. Not in the PK, KGC, PS or Gita-chandrodaya. B.
[^32]: Ramakrishna Acharya — Harirama's brother (no. 8), a brahmana pandita who became a disciple of Narottama (Prema-vilasa, vilasa 14). Shri
Manmohana Raya — the Deity he served. "The evil-tongued" (durmukha) — the revilers of bhakti, defeated by him in debate.

## 17. Narahari — "ki kahaba parikara parama udāra…" (GPT1 p. 483, no. 77; GCC)
*The companions around Gaura in the morning: they ceaselessly drink the nectar of His face, lose their composure, are adorned with bhavas — thrill,
trembling, tears, a choked voice; one whirls, one repeats the Name, one laughs and dances; the poet longs to see this. Raga not specified
("yatharaga"). **Attribution: probably Chakravarti** — the pada is in the GCC; Vrajabuli.*[^33]

**17.1.** How can I describe the companions, boundlessly generous!
Gaura's peerless face is a mine of nectar, and they ceaselessly drink that nectar.

**17.2.** However they try to keep their composure — they are ever unsteady, helplessly intoxicated with rasa;
adorned with abundant, wondrous ornaments of bhava, they are beautiful in every way.

**17.3.** One's body thrills, there are no words — from his lovely throat comes only a choked sound;
another is shaken through his whole being by trembling, from a third one's eyes pour streams of tears.

**17.4.** One whirls about, making gestures with his arms, another again and again utters the sweet Name;
one laughs softly and dances again and again — when will Narahari, seeing this, find his life fulfilled?[^34]

[^33]: GPT1 — p. 483, no. 77 ("yatharaga"; scan, PDF p. 740); GPT2 — section 6.3, no. 77 (NG-226). GCC — kirana 4, pada 21 ("yatharaga"), the last of the padas on
the companions around Gaura in the morning; after it — a couplet with the same signature: "thus all the companions gaze at the moon of Gaura's face,
their hearts in great joy, and they form various wishes" (pada 22) — and the colophon of the kirana ("The Lord in the circle of devotees"). Variants:
17.1 GCC "piyata"; 17.3 "kāṅpāota janama" (so in GPT1; understood as "the whole being"; the word is distorted in the OCR of the GCC). Not in the BR, PK,
KGC, PS or Gita-chandrodaya. B.
[^34]: The sattvika-bhavas are listed — ecstatic manifestations of prema: thrill (pulaka), choked voice (svara-bhanga), trembling (kampa), tears (ashru).
"Find his life fulfilled" (saphala hoyaba) — the poet considers life fulfilled if he beholds this lila (in contemplation).

## 18. Narahari — "ei abhilāṣa mane…" (GPT1 pp. 484–485, no. 83; BR)
*The poet's prayer about Gaura's companions: intoxicated with His virtues, to wander day and night, to sing of Nitai with a thousand mouths, to honour
Advaita, to dance crying the names of Gadadhara, Narahari, Svarupa, Srivasa and others, to serve the feet of the Lord's parents and of His companions,
to glorify the Gosvamis of Vrindavana, to become "a dog at the door" of the kirtaniyas — and to wear the names of all the companions on his neck as a
necklace. Raga kamoda. **Attribution: probably Chakravarti** — the pada is in the concluding chapter of the BR (eds. 1960 and 2006), right before the
author's word about himself; among the companions it also names Narahari (Sarakara).*[^35]

**18.1.** This is the desire of my heart: intoxicated with the virtues of Gaurangachand, to wander day and night,
[remembering] His company with Lakshmi and Vishnupriya and His pastimes in Nadia — as if floating in that ocean of happiness.

**18.2.** With a hundred thousand mouths again and again to sing the virtues of Nitaichand with Vasudha and Jahnava;
ceaselessly to bow my head to Sitanatha with Sita — and with His fame to flood the world.

**18.3.** Gadadhara, Narahari, Svarupa — with jubilant cries ever to dance, slapping my armpits;
crying "Srinivasa! Vanamali! Dasa Gadadhara!" — so that my heart would overflow with bliss.

**18.4.** Haridasa, Vakreshvara, Ramananda, Damodara, Gauridasa, Sri Raghunandana,
Murari, Mukunda, Rama — with their names ceaselessly to perform kirtana.

**18.5.** Shachi and Mishra Jagannatha — the Lord's mother and father, Padmavati, Hadai Pandita —
known to the world for their virtues: at their feet, birth after birth, may my mind abide.

**18.6.** Sri Madhava and Ratnavati, Malati, Madhavi, loving Damayanti Devi,
Sri Achyutananda — the root of bliss, merciful Virachandra — may I serve their lotus feet.

**18.7.** Sri Vallabha, Sanatana, Sadashiva, Sudarshana, Nandana, Vijaya, Kashishvara,
Vishvarupa — repeating their names, may I wander rejoicing, so that the godless, seeing me, take fright.

**18.8.** Dear Sanatana and Rupa, the two Bhattas — wells of rasa, Raghunatha and deep Sri Jiva —
repeating these names, may this sinful body of mine become grey with dust.

**18.9.** Subuddhi with Raghava, Bhugarbha, Sri Lokanatha, who wander in Vraja in the joy of prema —
may I have attachment to these names, may the evil mind go far away, may a thrill pervade my whole body.

**18.10.** Govinda, Madhava, Hari, Shuklambara Brahmachari, Vasu Ghosha, whose life is Gaura —
by their grace may I wander with a lion's roar, not counting the non-devotees even as a straw.

**18.11.** The kirtaniya Shashthivara, Haridasa — the best of brahmanas, Shridhara Thakura, the seller of banana leaves,
Kamsari, Vallabha and Dhananjaya — may I become their dancing dog.

**18.12.** Kavichandra, Vidyanidhi, Sri Madhu Pandita and all the others dear to Gaura —
Narahari Dasa sings: of these jewel-names I will make a necklace and put it on my neck.[^36]

PS or Gita-chandrodaya. B. This is the strongest argument for attribution in the whole section: the pada is part of the author's conclusion of the BR, and

## 19. Narahari — "gorācāṅda chāṛi yābe naidā…" (GPT1 pp. 367–368, section 5.3, no. 7; BR)
*Before the sannyasa: Gorachand will leave Nadia — and the Jahnavi grew still, the murtis of Shambhu, Bhagavati and Ganapati faded, the trees do not
blossom, the bees do not hum, the cuckoos have fallen silent, the peacocks do not dance, the birds weep, the cows and deer find no rest: the land of Nadia
cannot contain its grief. Raga ashabari or deshpal. **Attribution: probably Chakravarti** — the pada is in the BR (12th wave); found in the
continuous check of signatures (supplement).*[^37]

**19.1.** Gorachand will leave Nadia — therefore the stream of the Jahnavi is left without waves;
the murtis of Shambhu, Bhagavati, Ganapati — all there were — seem to have faded.

**19.2.** The trees and creepers do not put forth leaves, the flowers do not open — without fragrance;
the bee and his mate do not alight on them, do not drink the nectar, do not hum — downcast.

**19.3.** The flocks of cuckoos have fallen silent, the peacock does not dance with the peahen;
the shari, the shuka and other birds weep — they cannot fly, they flutter about the forest.

**19.4.** The cows do not run lowing, the deer and other beasts find no rest.
Narahari says: beauty has gone, and the land of Nadia cannot contain its grief.[^38]

[^37]: GPT1 — pp. 367–368, section 5.3 ("Sannyasa"), no. 7 ("ashabari or deshpal"; scan, PDF pp. 626–627); GPT2 — section 5.3, no. 7. **The catalogue did not know
it** (new row NG-333): the continuous check of signatures in sections 3.x, 4.x and 5.3–7 found it to be the only unrecorded Narahari pada outside the
nagari section. BR 12 (ed. 1912, p. 958; ed. 1960, vv. 3526–3529, "in song, deshpal"): the Lord consoles Shachi (a quotation from the Chaitanya-bhagavata,
Madhya-khanda: "twice more, at the beginning of sankirtana, I shall become your son", vv. 3519–3520) and Vishnupriya; all are absorbed in sankirtana; "the
Lord will take sannyasa — and all that was beautiful in Nadia faded" (v. 3525), then the pada. Variants: 19.1 BR "taraṅga rahita jāhnavī-dhārā"; 19.4 BR
"bhaṇe narahari, śobhā dūre, dukha sambarite nāre nadīyā-khiti" — the same. The Navadvipa-parikrama is the same as the BR. Not in the GCC, PK, KGC, PS or
Gita-chandrodaya. B.
[^38]: The nature of Nadia shares the separation from the Lord, as Vrindavana shares the separation from Krishna. The murtis of Shiva (Shambhu), Durga

departure. Shari and shuka — a pair of parrots (female and male), constant images of forest poetry.

## Concluding note to the appendix

*The appendix "Padas signed 'Narahari' from the Gaura-pada-tarangini" (portions 17–26) is complete: **241 padas** have been translated — all the padas
signed Narahari known only from the GPT (with their witnesses — the Bhakti-ratnakara, the Gaura-charita-chintamani, the Gita-chandrodaya), except the padas
in nagari-bhava (they will form a separate section with an introductory note). Attribution as marked in the headings: **"probably Sarakara" — 4**
(mangalacharana and Gaura's form: plain Bengali, not in the BR, assigned to Sarakara by the GPT2 index; authority C); **"probably Chakravarti" — 221**
(almost all found in the Bhakti-ratnakara or in the Gaura-charita-chintamani of Narahari Chakravarti, and one stands in the author's
conclusion of the BR; authority B);
**"not established" — 16** (GPT only; among them several late padas, written apparently after both Naraharis; authority C); no pada of the
appendix has
authority D (doubtful authenticity). The result of the continuous check: the great majority of the "Narahari" padas known only from the GPT belong to Narahari Chakravarti (Ghanashyama
Dasa), an 18th-century author who wove his songs into his accounts of the holy places and of the Lord's associates; the legacy of Narahari Sarakara,
Mahaprabhu's companion, is above all the padas preserved in the Padakalpataru (the main part, portions 01–06). Left outside the appendix: the padas in
nagari-bhava (a separate section), the padas signed Ghanashyama, prayers to the guru and praises of poets without Gaura-lila, padas in which Narahari is
only mentioned while the signature is another's, and one pada on Advaita known only from the BR (12th wave, vv. 3755–3760).*
