# Balarama Dasa, Yadunandana, Kanu Dasa and Others: Praise of Gaura, His Bhavas in Navadvipa, the Sannyasa, Nityananda in Gauda

*This portion completes the core — padas of the Lord's contemporaries known from the *Padakalpataru* (PK), the
*Bhakti-ratnakara* (BR) and the *Kshanada-gita-chintamani* (KGC), excluding padas in nagari-bhava. Here are the
remaining padas signed by Balarama Dasa; padas of Yadunandana Chakravarti (a disciple of Gadadhara Dasa) cited by
Narahari Chakravarti in the *Bhakti-ratnakara*; and padas of Kanu Dasa (Kanu Thakura), Raya Ananta, Murari and
Krishnadasa, in the order of the lilas: praise of the Gaura-avatara and of Nitai-Chaitanya; Gaura's form and His bhavas in
the kirtanas of Navadvipa, when, holding Gadadhara's hand, He speaks of Radha and Vrindavana; the lament over the
sannyasa; Shachi in Shantipura; the Lord in Nilachala; Nityananda, sent to Gauda, with Shachi; Gaura's bliss after long separation. Majumdar counts among the
Lord's contemporaries Balarama Dasa (on the strength of three BR padas, translated in portion 10) and Yadunandana (on the
strength of his BR padas); the signature "Balarama Dasa" in PK covers padas of several poets, so the attribution of the
rest is doubtful. The text follows PK, checked against the scans, with the manuscript variants from PK's apparatus, BR,
the *Gaura-pada-tarangini* (GPT1 — 1st ed., 1903; GPT2 — 2nd ed., 1934) and KGC; the Bengali original, variants and
word-for-word translation are in `bn/11.md`.*

## 1. Balarama Dasa — "baraṇa-āśrama kiñcana akiñcana…" (PK 2212)
*Praise of the Gaura-avatara: making no distinction of varnas and ashramas, of rich and destitute, Gaura gives away the
prema inaccessible to Shiva and Brahma; sinners, beasts and birds weep; kings, yogis and jnanis abandon their own — only
the poet's heart seems made of stone. Raga suhai.*[^1]

**1.1.** Making no distinction of varnas and ashramas, of those who have and those who have not,
He takes no account of anyone's faults;
the treasure of prema, inaccessible to Shiva and Brahma,
He distributes to the people of the world, offering it Himself.[^2]

**1.2.** An ocean of mercy is the incarnation of Gaura:
I would give my life to turn misfortune away from Him![^3]
Who knows what His qualities are, what His sweetness is?
My soul weeps — I cannot forget.

**1.3.** The low, the godless, the poor, the wretched, the weak and the low-born —
hearing of His qualities, the whole world weeps;
even unreasoning beasts and birds weep, tears pour from their eyes:
with what has He bound the hearts of all?[^4]

**1.4.** The king abandons royal pleasures, the yogi meditation and yoga,
the jnani weeps, abandoning the taste of knowledge.
Who, then, fashioned Balarama's heart out of stone,
that this rasa has not touched it?

[^1]: Signature: "Balarama" ("কেবা বলরাম-হিয়া…"). PK: vol. 3, PDF pp. 272–273 (printed pp. 261–262), 20th pallava, no. 29
(label "॥২৯॥২২১২॥"); raga suhai; P-R-Sa MS no. 2320. GPT1 — p. 38, no. 38; GPT2 — sec. 1.3, no. 38. The catalogue (BD-010)
assigns the pada to PK 2214 — a mistake: PK 2214 is a pada of Govinda Dasa, "কুন্দন-কনয়-কলেবর-কাতি", and PK 2213, which
follows ours (Govinda Dasa, "পতিত হেরি কান্দে…"), repeats its line "বরণ-আশ্রম কিঞ্চন অকিঞ্চন". Which of the Balarama
Dasas wrote the pada has not been established.
[^2]: "Those who have and those who have not" — কিঞ্চন অকিঞ্চন. On the prema which the Lord gives away without
distinguishing the worthy and the unworthy, cf. CC Adi 7.
[^3]: "নিছনি লইয়া মরি" is a set phrase of love: "I would die taking on myself (the beloved's misfortune)"; nichhani is
a rite by which misfortune is turned away from a beloved.
[^4]: "The weak" — খীণ; MS "ka", P-R-Sa: খল ("the wicked"). "The low-born" — জাতি ("by birth"); P-R-Sa: মতি ("the weak
in understanding").

## 2. Balarama Dasa — "dekha dekha aparūpa gaura-carita…" (PK 2286)
*Gaura's pastimes are like those of Vamana: the Lord of Gokula has appeared again, and Prataparudra, like Bali, gave Him
body, mind and all his possessions, asuras such as Jagai and Madhai bowed to His feet; Advaita is Bhagiratha, who brought
down the Ganga of bhakti, Nityananda is Shiva, who received it; in it all the devotees bathe, and the fallen attain the
supreme abode. Raga kamoda.*[^5]

**2.1.** *(Refrain)* Look, look — wondrous are Gaura's deeds!
That very Lord of Gokula has now appeared —
was it not so that Vamana once appeared?[^6]

**2.2.** Seeing His majesty, Prataparudra, like Bali,
gave Him body, mind and all he possessed;
Jagai, Madhai and the other asuras
He brought to bow at His feet.[^7]

**2.3.** From His feet, with Advaita as Bhagiratha,
flowed the stream of the Ganga of bhakti;
Nityananda is Girisha: He received it
and brought it down into the Himalaya…[^8]

**2.4.** Bathing in it, all the devotees
revel in the bliss of prema;
the low and the fallen attained the supreme abode —
only worthless Balarama is left out.[^9]

Shri Gauranga and so on", no. 69 (label "॥৬৯॥২২৮৬॥"); raga kamoda; P-R-Sa MS no. 2390. GPT1 — p. 46, no. 66; GPT2 — sec.
1.3, no. 66. The catalogue (BD-013) assigns the pada to PK 1245 — wrongly: PK 1245 is Krishna-lila (Govardhana, signed
"Chaitanyadasa"). The pada follows Ananta Acharya's PK 2285 (portion 03, no. 9).
[^6]: Vamana is the Lord's incarnation as a dwarf brahmana who begged three steps of land from Bali, king of the asuras,
and covered the universe with them (Bhag. 8.18–21). The comparison: Gaura, like Vamana, "begs" — and receives everything.
[^7]: Prataparudra is the king of Orissa (CC Madhya 11–14); Bali gave himself to Vamana. Jagai and Madhai are the wicked
brothers of Navadvipa delivered by the Lord (CB Madhya 13). "Brought to bow at His feet" — চরণ প্রবণ নিজ কেল; MS "ka":
প্রবলে, "kha", "gha", "cha": প্রবণে.

P-R-Sa: "devotee" (ভকত) instead of "bhakti".
[^9]: "The supreme abode" — পরম পদ; MSS "ka", "kha" and GPT1: "the supreme mercy" (পরম দয়া).

## 3. Raya Ananta — "nitāi caitanya duṭī bhāi dayāra avadhi…" (KGC 28.2)
*Nitai and Chaitanya, two brothers, are the limit of mercy: the prema inaccessible to Shiva and Brahma and sought in vain
by the four Vedas They give away unceasingly; the fallen of the age of Kali, crying "Nitai-Chaitanya", dance and sing.
Raga shri.*[^10]

**3.1.** Nitai and Chaitanya, two brothers, are the limit of mercy:
the prema inaccessible to Shiva and Brahma They give away without end.

**3.2.** The prema which the four Vedas search for, to attain it —
such prema the two brothers give away unceasingly.

**3.3.** The fallen, the wretched, as many as are struck down by Kali —
crying "Nitai-Chaitanya!" they dance and sing.[^11]

**3.4.** The good of the whole world has come about in the rasa of sankirtana —
Raya Ananta weeps: not a drop of it has come to him.[^12]

[^10]: Signature: "Raya Ananta" ("রায় অনন্ত"). KGC: 28th kshanada, no. 2 (1925 ed., p. 223; scan PDF p. 257), a
gaura-chandrika to Radha-Krishna lila, raga shri. GPT1 — p. 40, no. 43 (raga mangala); GPT2 — sec. 1.3, no. 43. The pada
is not in PK. According to B. B. Majumdar, the "Raya Ananta" of KGC is Ananta Acharya of Advaita's branch (cf. portion 04,
no. 9).
[^11]: GPT1, GPT2: "the fallen, the wretched, the sinful, struck down by Kali" (পতিত দুর্গত পাপী কলিহত যারা).
[^12]: "Not a drop of it has come to him" follows GPT1 (না পাইয়া লেশে, "not obtaining a particle"); KGC reads শেষে ("at
the end": "weeps, not obtaining [it] at the last").

## 4. Krishnadasa — "soṅaro nava gauracanda…" (PK 1085)
*Remembering the youthful Gaurachandra — the moon of Navadvipa, Krishna Himself: the moon-face, eyes full of prema, locks
dressed with flowers, earrings, the Kaustubha, red garments, sandal; the king of serpents holds an umbrella over Him,
Lakshmi serves His feet, the devotees sing His glory; at the end the poet places his hope in Nitai too, the giver of
prema. Raga bhairavi.*[^13]

**4.1.** *(Refrain)* I remember the youthful Gaurachandra —
the graceful Vanamali,
the moon of Navadvipa, the ocean of mercy,
tenderly loving His devotees.[^14]

**4.2.** His face is the moon, His lips are red,
waves of prema stream from His eyes;
the beauty of millions of moons, of millions of suns
I offer to Him as a gift to ward off harm.
His locks, dressed with flowers,
the tilaka on His brow, His shining nose,
His teeth are pearls, His smile nectar,
like lightning in a cloud.[^15]

**4.3.** Makara earrings flash on His cheeks,
His neck is lit by the Kaustubha gem,
red garments, merciful words —
beauty beyond measure.
His body anointed with sandal, in garlands —
millions of Kamadevas are put to shame;
bracelets on arms and wrists, jewelled anklets,
the sacred thread upon Him.[^16]

**4.4.** The king of the bearers of the earth holds an umbrella over Him,
the host of devotees sings His glory;
to the two feet served by Kamala
I offer myself in sacrifice.
Says humble Krishnadasa,
placing his hope in Gaura's feet:
the saviour of the fallen, moonlike Nitai,
is the giver of prema.[^17]

[^13]: Signature: "Krishnadasa" ("কহত দান কৃষ্ণদাস"; GPT1 reads "দীন", "humble"; "দান" in PK is apparently a misprint).
PK: vol. 2, PDF pp. 251–252 (printed pp. 238–239), 15th pallava, section "রসালসোপযুক্তরূপং" (the form befitting the
languor after the rasa), "শ্রীগৌরচন্দ্রঃ" — a gaura-chandrika to Radha-Krishna lila, no. 23 (label "॥২৩॥১০৮৫॥"); raga
bhairavi; P-R-Sa MS no. 1494. GPT1 — p. 136, no. 75 (bhairava, ektala). In the catalogue (KR-006) the pada stood without a
first line, marked as probably Krishna-lila: it is a gaura-pada. Which Krishnadasa wrote it has not been established (C).
[^14]: "The graceful Vanamali" — নাগর বনয়ারি: nagara is a graceful youth skilled in love; Banvari (Vanamali, "wearing the
forest garland") is a name of Krishna. Gaura is Krishna Himself (CC Adi 1–4). MSS "ka", "kha", "gha", "cha":
"Gaurasundara" instead of "Gaurachandra"; GPT1: "Remember the youthful Gaurasundara…" (সোঙর).
[^15]: "I offer to Him as a gift to ward off harm" — নিছয়ারি (from nichhani: everything beautiful is offered up to turn
misfortune away from the beloved). MSS "ka", "kha": "the beauty of His face" (মুখশোভা). "Like lightning in a cloud" —
দামিনি ঘনয়ারি; the sense of the second word is not wholly clear.
[^16]: The Kaustubha is the gem on the breast of Vishnu-Krishna. MSS "ka", "kha", "gha", "cha" and GPT1: "sandal" (চন্দন)
instead of "armlets" (অঙ্গদ); MS "kha": তরল instead of রতন.
[^17]: "The king of the bearers of the earth" is Ananta-Shesha, the serpent who bears the universes; he serves the Lord as
His umbrella. Kamala is Lakshmi. Instead of the first line GPT1 reads "the devotees run and sing…" (ধারত গাওত ভকতবৃন্দ)
and adds: "majestically He walks, slowly, slowly". MSS "ka", "kha", "gha", "cha": বলিয়া instead of বলিয়ে.

## 5. Balarama Dasa — "kothāya āchila gorā emana sundara…" (PK 2110)
*Gaura's form: where was such a beauty until now? He has charmed the whole town of Nadia: His hair dressed with flowers,
bees hovering above it, pearls on His breast, sandal and kunkuma, the forest garland, a gliding gait, the ringing of
anklets. Raga shri.*[^18]

**5.1.** Where was such a beauty — Gora — until now?
With this form He has charmed the town of Nadia.

**5.2.** His glossy hair is dressed with flowers of every kind —
rangana, malati, yuthi, bandhuli, bakula.[^19]

**5.3.** Thirsting for honey, how many bees hover over them!
Gazing at this form, life will not stay in the body.

**5.4.** A necklace of gems and pearls glitters on His breast,
on every limb ornaments flash like lightning.

**5.5.** His body is anointed with kunkuma mixed with sandal,
His arms reach to His knees, the forest garland is on His neck.

**5.6.** A gliding gait, swaying now to one side, now to the other —
as if nectar welled up when His neck sways.

**5.7.** As He walks, the anklets on His feet ring sweetly —
Balarama Dasa says: I give myself for Him.[^20]

[^18]: Signature: "Balarama Dasa." PK: vol. 3, PDF pp. 233–234 (printed pp. 222–223), 18th pallava, section "অথ
শ্রীগৌরচন্দ্রস্য রূপাদিবর্ণনং" (description of Shri Gaurachandra's form), no. 2 (label "॥২॥২১১০॥"); raga shri; P-R-Sa MS no.
2218. GPT1 — p. 110, no. 2; GPT2 — sec. 3.1, no. 2. The catalogue (BD-014) listed it as "GPT only": the PK number was found
on the scan. The preceding PK 2109 ("গৌর-বরণ মণি-আভরণ নাটুয়া-মোহন বেশ", with the same signature; BD-017) is in
nagari-bhava (deferred).
[^19]: Rangana (ixora), malati and yuthi (kinds of jasmine), bandhuli (pentapetes, a red flower), bakula (mimusops). GPT1:
পারুলী (the patali flower) instead of বান্ধুলী.
[^20]: "I give myself for Him" — নিছনি যাঙ তায় (see note 3).

## 6. Balarama Dasa — "kusume khacita ratane racita…" (PK 2164)
*The form of Gaura the dancer: His hair dressed with flowers and gems, bees above it; a sandal tilaka, locks, earrings;
at His glances the women of Nadia lose their peace; lips, smile, speech are nectar; jasmine garlands, gold and diamonds,
red garments; the gait of an elephant, feet that outshine the lotus, nails that outshine the moon. Raga tori.*[^21]

**6.1.** Dressed with flowers, studded with gems —
His glossy tresses;
drunk with honey, greedy for fragrance,
swarms of bees whirl above them.

**6.2.** On the tablet of His brow is a tilaka of sandal,
curling locks adorn it;
skilled in the tandava dance, adorned with earrings —
His rounded cheeks shine.

**6.3.** Seeing this form, the chaste wives of noble families
abandoned their shame before their kin:
dharma, duties, modesty, honour —
as though struck by thunder.[^22]

**6.4.** By the hints of sidelong glances, by the play of the brows,
all around is tinged with Ananga;
the longing of Kama has made its abode
in the hearts of the young women of the world.

**6.5.** His lips are bandhuka flowers, sweeter than honey-wine
is His half-smile;
His speech, unhurried, pitcher after pitcher
pours out streams of nectar.

**6.6.** Garlands of jasmine here and there,
rows of lovely flowers;
greedy bees, male and female,
fly down to them, intoxicated.

**6.7.** Gold, diamonds, motionless lightning
adorn His enchanting body;
garments that outshine the red rays of dawn
captivate the young women with their colour.

**6.8.** His bearing and poise astonish Kama —
Gora, like pure gold;
His gait is slow, like a maddened elephant's —
gazing at Him, the world forgets itself.

**6.9.** His lotus feet put the wagtails to shame,
softly His anklets ring;
His nails outshine the moon —
so Balarama Dasa describes Him.[^23]

(label "॥৫৬॥২১৬৪॥"; the preceding pada of Ramananda, PK 2163, is by a misprint also labelled "২১৬৪"); raga tori; P-R-Sa MS no.
2271. GPT1 — p. 125, no. 42, and p. 140, no. 86 (the same pada twice); GPT2 — sec. 3.1, nos. 42 and 86. The catalogue (BD-016)
gives PK 2165 (a mistake: PK 2165 is Narottama Dasa, "কাঞ্চন দরপণ বরণ সুগোরা রে").
[^22]: The words about the women of Nadia who, seeing Gaura, lose their shame and peace are an image of the poetics of

("abode"); MS "gha": সোহন instead of হোয়ল.

## 7. Balarama Dasa — "sahajai kāñcana-kānti kalevara…" (PK 2145)
*Gaura dancing, overwhelmed by Radha's prema: a body the colour of gold, before which millions of Madanas swoon; He
lingers, falls to the ground, trembles, roars, sheds tears and, embracing the fallen, bathes them in His tears. Raga
beloar.*[^24]

**7.1.** His body by nature is the colour of gold:
gazing at Him, the people of the world lose their hearts;
before Him how many millions of Madanas swoon!
His garment outshines the red rays of dawn.

**7.2.** Overwhelmed by Rai's prema, He walks slowly, slowly,
His heart brimming over — He falls to the ground;
sweat, trembling, again and again wave upon wave of thrills,
again and again He roars: "Hum! Hum!"[^25]

**7.3.** His body sways, He cannot stand firm,
from both cloud-like eyes the rain pours unceasingly;
drunk with that rasa, He finds no end to it,
clasping the fallen to His breast, He bathes them in tears.

**7.4.** Crying "Hari, Hari," how much He weeps and laments! —
only Balarama is left out, day and night.[^26]

[^24]: Signature: "Balarama" ("বঞ্চিত বলরাম"). PK: vol. 3, PDF p. 247 (printed p. 236), 18th pallava, no. 37 (label
"॥৩৭॥২১৪৫॥"); raga beloar; P-R-Sa MS no. 2252. The pada follows Vasu Ghosha's PK 2143 and 2144 (portion 08). GPT1 — p. 116,
no. 16 (beloar); GPT2 — sec. 3.1, no. 16. The catalogue (BD-015) gives PK 788: a mistake (PK 788 is a pada of Govinda Dasa,
"তপত-কাঞ্চন-কান্তি কলেবর").
[^25]: "Rai's prema" — রাই-প্রেম: the Lord appeared in Radha's bhava and complexion to taste Her love for Krishna (CC Adi
4). MSS "kha", "gha": "immersed" (মগন) instead of "walks" (গমন); "ka", "kha": কর instead of করত.
[^26]: Instead of the last couplet GPT1 reads: "Crying 'Hari, Hari,' He weeps and laments, mad with bliss day and night;

## 8. Balarama Dasa — "bihare āju rasika-rāja…" (PK 2111)
*Gaurachandra, the king of rasikas, sports in Nadia: beauty outshining millions of Kamadevas, a moon-face, smile and speech
of nectar; remembering the groves of Vraja He loses Himself, stumbles, falls into the arms of the fallen, repeats "Rai,
Rai!" — all drink the nectar of prema except the poet. Raga turi.*[^27]

**8.1.** Today the king of rasikas,
Gaurachandra, sports in Nadia:
shining like a cluster of nagakeshara flowers,
with the lovely lustre of gold.
The abode of the beauty of millions of Kamadevas,
loveliness that enchants the world —
seeing Him, the young women of the world in turmoil
abandon composure and dharma.[^28]

**8.2.** A face whose charm outshines the rays
of the boundless full autumn moon;
a row of lovely teeth
puts the kunda flowers to shame;
on His bimba lips a sweet smile
pouring out so many streams of nectar;
His words, likewise, flow
in torrents of sweet drink.[^29]

**8.3.** The sweet groves of the forests of Vraja,
the sweet heaps of love and longing —
remembering, remembering them, He loses Himself more and more,
rapt day and night.
Overcome by bhava, languid, bewildered,
He walks, walks — and slowly stumbles;
enraptured, He falls into the arms of the fallen,
intoxicated with deep bliss.[^30]

**8.4.** With reddened eyes He gazes, full of compassion,
unceasingly He repeats: "Rai, Rai!";
He dances, mad with prema, rolls on the ground, wanders —
His breast bursting with what is in His heart.
The high, the middling, the low beings —
all drink the nectar of prema;
only Balarama alone is left out,
having offended the sadhus.[^31]

"॥৩॥২১১১॥"); raga turi (todi); P-R-Sa MS nos. 1430 and 2219. GPT1 — p. 110, no. 3 (turi). The pada is not in the catalogue
(in portion 10 it was noted from Ray's footnote to PK 2066 as "outside the catalogue"); new row BD-043.
[^28]: Nagakeshara (কেশর) is a flower with golden stamens; "cluster" — পুঞ্জ (the word কুঞ্জ at the start of the line is
