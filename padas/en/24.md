# Appendix. Padas signed "Narahari" from the Gaura-pada-tarangini — VIII: Gaura's night sleep; Nityananda

*Continuation of the appendix (the introductory note on the two Naraharis, on the arguments for attribution and on the marks in the headings
is in `17.md`). Portion 24: the end of GPT section 5.2 "Ashtakaliya" — the songs on Gaura's night sleep (GPT1 pp. 362–365, nos. 55–63, 65: the
companions gaze at the sleeping Lord and do not dare to wake Him), then section 6.1 "Nityananda" (GPT1 pp. 414–439) — all padas signed Narahari
(nos. 10, 26, 64–69, 71–78, 80, 81: the advent at Ekachakra, the first meeting with the Lord, the coming to Gauda, Panihati, Khardaha, the wedding,
praises), with the addition of the pada on Balarama's rasa manifested by Nityananda (section 5.1, no. 44). The signatures in both sections were
checked throughout, in the OCR and on the scan; the Gaura-carita-cintamani (GCC, kiranas 2–4) and the 12th wave of the Bhakti-ratnakara (BR) were
read continuously at the relevant places. Not translated (outside the appendix): 5.2 nos. 54, 64 and 6.1 nos. 6, 11, 19, 70, 79 — signed Ghanashyama;
the other padas of section 6.1 are by other poets (Atmarama, Govinda Dasa, Vrindavana Dasa, Krishnadasa, Lochana, Jnanadasa, Vasu Ghosha, Balarama
Dasa, Kanu Dasa, Raya Ananta and others; some were translated earlier). The base text is GPT1 (doubtful places by the scan), collated with GPT2, the
GCC (ed. 1947), the BR (eds. 1912 and 1960) and the Gita-candrodaya; the PK, KGC and PS were checked. On the ashtakaliya as eternal lila and on the
Lord's sleep see note 36 of portion 22. The notes of the GPT editors are not reproduced.*

## 1. Narahari — "ko varaṇava vara gaura uttāna śayana…" (GPT1 p. 362, section 5.2, no. 55; GCC)
*The end of the night: Gaura sleeps on His back; the poet describes His form — the shining limbs, the face outshining autumn moons, the
earrings, lips, teeth, the necklaces on His chest, the folded hands: as if even in sleep He were pleading with an offended beloved. Raga not
specified ("yathārāga"). **Attribution: probably Chakravarti** — the pada is in the GCC; Vrajabuli.*[^1]

**1.1.** Who can describe the delightful beauty of fair Gaura sleeping on His back?
His shapely, graceful limbs shine, robbing hosts of steady lightning of their pride.

**1.2.** The sweet light of His face, which conquers young maidens, defeats hosts of autumn moons;
His ears and clear cheeks are adorned with new earrings, matchless, set with gems and pearls.

**1.3.** His lips, putting to shame the bimba fruit and the rays of dawn, are slightly closed, lovely, full of radiance;
the white, beautiful light of His teeth gleams — the stars and the jasmine stay far behind.

**1.4.** On His broad chest are many necklaces, and on it His hands, joined palm to palm, rest unmoving.
Narahari says: this cannot be grasped — surely He is pleading before an offended beloved.[^2]

[^1]: GPT1 — p. 362, section 5.2 "Ashtakaliya", no. 55 ("yathārāga"; scan, PDF p. 621); GPT2 — section 5.2, no. 55, p. ≈233–234 (catalogue: NG-193,
"5.3" — wrongly). GCC — kirana 2 (the "pastimes on the couch" in the last part of the night), pada 18: before it Gadadhara, waking first, serves the
Lord's feet, and He turns onto His back (padas 15–16); then comes a pada of Ghanashyama (GPT no. 54) and this one. Readings: 1.1 GCC "vara gaura
sarasa uttāna śayana"; "dāminī-puñja" ("of lightning") — GPT1 and GPT2 "yāminī puñja" ("of nights"), a misprint; the GCC reading is adopted; 1.4 GCC
"anubhava na hota" (GPT "anubhava nohata"). Not in the BR, PK, KGC, PS, Gita-candrodaya. B. On the ashtakaliya and on the Lord's sleep see note 36
of portion 22.
[^2]: The Lord's sleep is not the sleep of a conditioned soul: He is absorbed in the lilas of Vraja and, in the mood of Krishna, pleads in sleep
with Radha who is in mana (offended pride) — the folded hands betray His inner mood (cf. in the same kirana of the GCC, pada 13: in sleep Gaura calls
upon "the crown of beautiful women" to wake). "Which conquers young maidens" is a stock formula of the lyric on Krishna's all-attractive beauty (cf.
note 7 of portion 23); here it refers to Gaura as Krishna Himself.

## 2. Narahari — "ki kahava gaura śayana anupāma…" (GPT1 pp. 362–363, no. 56; GCC)
*Gaura's matchless sleep: His limbs shine like Kama embodied; His body is soft as fresh butter — it seems to melt on the couch; His
breathing gently lifts His belly, as the Malaya wind stirs the waves of a river on Sumeru. The poet prays that the Lord may so rest on the
couch of his heart. Raga lalita. **Attribution: probably Chakravarti** — the pada is in the GCC; Vrajabuli.*[^3]

**2.1.** What shall I say of Gaura's matchless sleep!
Each of His shapely limbs shines — as if Kama himself, in visible form, were displaying his play here.

**2.2.** His body is soft as a lump of fresh butter churned from a golden ocean of milk;
and, wonder of wonders, without the sun's heat it seems to melt upon the couch.

**2.3.** Languid, limp, He breathes softly, and His radiant belly rises and falls —
as if the Malaya wind, touching Sumeru, drove the quiet waves of its river.

**2.4.** Words cannot convey it — who could compose it? However long one looks, the eyes are never sated.
Narahari says: when will He so recline on the couch of my heart and give me joy?[^4]

[^3]: GPT1 — pp. 362–363, no. 56 ("lalita"; scan, PDF pp. 621–622); GPT2 — section 5.2, no. 56, p. ≈234 (catalogue: NG-194). GCC — kirana 2, pada 7
("lalita rāga"; preceded by a description of the chamber and the couch). Readings: 2.2 GCC "kṣīrododadhi" ("ocean of milk"; GPT "kṣīroda dadhi"); 2.4 GCC
"viracava" (GPT1 "viracana"). Under line 2.3 GPT1 has an editor's note on the reading "vilāse" (not reproduced). Not in the BR, PK, KGC, PS. B.
[^4]: The comparison with Kama (Manmatha) is a common image of the poetry on Krishna; but Krishna is "the Manmatha of Manmatha himself", who
enchants even the god of love (Bhag. 10.32.2), and Gaura's beauty is not mundane: the poet prays not for anything sensual but that the Lord may
reign in his heart. Sumeru is the golden mountain — an image of Gaura's golden body.

## 3. Narahari — "ki kava analpa talpa jhalakata ati…" (GPT1 p. 363, no. 57; GCC)
*Gaura's couch: spacious, spotless as autumn, fashioned by Kama himself; the white pillows are like blocks of Kailasa and the stilled
waves of the Ganga; heaps of flowers, a painted canopy of moonbeams, a silver frame set with gold and gems. Raga lalita. **Attribution:
probably Chakravarti** — the pada is in the GCC; Vrajabuli (the text is obscure in places).*[^5]

**3.1.** What shall I say! The spacious couch shines beyond measure, spotless as the autumn season;
wondrous, beyond even the dreams of the king of the gods, a charming work of mighty Manobhava.

**3.2.** Lovely pillows, full of languor and awakening desire, shine — there is nothing like them in the world:
like blocks of Hara's mountain, whole lumps of fresh curd, the stilled waves of the Ganga.

**3.3.** On it, in heaps — karavira, kunda, ketaki, golden lotuses, jasmine;
and when all their tender flowers are strewn, one can no longer tell that this is Gaurahari's couch.

**3.4.** It is as if the Creator painted pictures on a cloth of moonbeams and with great care brought it into the Lord's chamber;
Narahari will say: this couch rises on a silver frame studded with gold and gems.[^6]

[^5]: GPT1 — p. 363, no. 57 ("lalita"; scan, PDF p. 622); GPT2 — section 5.2, no. 57, p. ≈234 (catalogue: NG-195). GCC — kirana 2, pada 8 ("yathārāga";
right after no. 56). Readings: 3.2 GCC "ālasa-dhara jana lālasa-kara vara vālisa" (GPT "ālasa dhara jala lālasa karavara vālisa"; translated after the
GCC: "… awakening desire in people"); 3.3 GCC "vandhuṇa", "kṛta nayana", "sumanagaṇa" (GPT "vandhure", "kṛtanayanā", "samana gana"); 3.4 GCC "deyala",
"bhaṇava sumati" (the sense of "sumati urathita" is unclear; taken as "rises beautifully"). Not in the BR, PK, KGC, PS. B.
[^6]: Manobhava ("born of the mind") is Kama: the Lord's couch is the matchless furnishing of His eternal chamber; yet even in this sumptuous picture
"Gaura-Hari" is Krishna Himself, not a worldly prince. Hara's mountain is Kailasa, the white abode of Shiva; curd and the waves of the Ganga are
images of whiteness.

## 4. Narahari — "mari mari gaura-mūrati aparūpa…" (GPT1 p. 363, no. 58; GCC)
*One of the companions, looking at the sleeping Lord: wondrous is Gaura's form; alas that I have only two eyes; I keep this treasure in my
heart; every morning I see Him, but I have never seen such sleep — shall I wake Him today? Raga vibhasa. **Attribution: probably
Chakravarti** — the pada is in the GCC; Vrajabuli.*[^7]

**4.1.** Ah, ah! Wondrous is Gaura's form — the enchanter of the world, the king of Manmathas.

**4.2.** What can I do — I was not given countless eyes! Cruel fate, letting me see Him, gave me grief as well.

**4.3.** I keep this longing, filling my heart with it; like a priceless jewel I reveal it to no one.

**4.4.** Who sculpted His body, this graceful frame? It is all my wealth, matchless in the world.

**4.5.** Every day at the end of the night I see Him, but such sleep I have never seen.

**4.6.** So I understand: a new, unheard-of sleep reigns. Narahari: shall I wake Him now?[^8]

[^7]: GPT1 — p. 363, no. 58 ("vibhāsa"; scan, PDF p. 622); GPT2 — section 5.2, no. 58, p. ≈234 (catalogue: NG-196). GCC — kirana 3 ("the awakening"), pada 4
("ko 'pi ca — vibhāṣa rāgeṇa", "and another [said] in raga vibhasa"): after a pada describing the sleeper (3.3) the GCC puts padas 4–7 into the
mouths of various companions who have come to the Lord's chamber at dawn. The readings differ insignificantly (4.5 GCC "kavahi"). Not in the BR, PK,
KGC, PS. B.
[^8]: "King of Manmathas" — cf. note 4. Hiding one's love (4.3) is a mark of mature prema: the devotee does not display his feeling. "A new sleep":
the Lord is absorbed in lila more deeply than usual; the companion hesitates to wake Him, not daring to interrupt His bliss.

## 5. Narahari — "dhani dhani āju rajanī dhani lekhi…" (GPT1 p. 363, no. 59; GCC)
*Another companion: blessed is this night! The Lord, greedy for sankirtana, did not close His eyes at night, speaking of Vraja in His former
mood — and now He has sunk into a matchless sleep and speaks nectarean words in it; it is already dawn, and He does not wake. Raga bhairava.
**Attribution: probably Chakravarti** — the pada is in the GCC; Vrajabuli.*[^9]

**5.1.** Blessed, blessed — I count this night blessed!
Such sleep of the Lord, greedy for the rasa of sankirtana, I have never seen.

**5.2.** He who, maddened by His own former bhava, ceaselessly speaks of lovely Vraja —
whose eyelids do not close even for a moment, who stays awake all night and so meets the dawn —

**5.3.** He has now sunk into a matchless, deep sleep: will He wake — or is this a still greater play?
Wondrous is the way of His sleep: as if in a dream, He utters words like nectar.

**5.4.** The night has gone, the morning has come — and He who sports in Nadia still does not wake.
How can one grasp this deed of Narahari's Lord? I cannot even say whether this sleep will break.[^10]

[^9]: GPT1 — p. 363, no. 59 ("bhairava"; scan, PDF p. 622); GPT2 — section 5.2, no. 59, p. ≈234 (catalogue: NG-197). GCC — kirana 3, pada 5 ("ko 'pi ca —
bhairava rāga"). Readings: 5.3 GPT1 and GPT2 "adbhuta ghuma karīta" — a wrong word division; the GCC reading "ghumaka rīta" ("the way of sleep") is
adopted; 5.4 GCC "bujhava". Not in the BR, PK, KGC, PS. B.
[^10]: "Former bhava" is the mood of Vraja: the Lord, Krishna Himself, in the mood of Radha and the gopis, does not sleep at night, speaking of Vraja
and of Krishna (cf. CC Antya 14–19: the Lord's nights in the Gambhira). The word "maddened" (unamata) refers to the madness of prema, not to anything
mundane. His sleep too is lila, "a still greater play": in sleep He utters words full of the rasa of Vraja (cf. no. 1 of portion 23).

## 6. Narahari — "pekhaha aparūpa pahuṅka vilāsa…" (GPT1 pp. 363–364, no. 60; GCC)
*Another companion: behold the Lord's wondrous sleep! He whose radiance is not revealed even in the hearts of the gods shines in this
chamber brighter than suns; sages perform sacrifices for ages to imagine Him on the couch of their hearts — and here He is easily
reached; it is not the praises of Brahma and Shiva that please the Lord but the words of the parrot in its cage — the custom of Vraja
returns. Raga lalita. **Attribution: probably Chakravarti** — the pada is in the GCC; Vrajabuli.*[^11]

**6.1.** Behold the wondrous play of the Lord!
His sleep — lovely, boundless, sweet — gives rise to the joy of body, mind and eyes.

**6.2.** He, a glimpse of whose bodily radiance is not revealed in the hearts of the gods, however great their effort —
that mass of radiance, by good fortune, shines in this chamber, outdoing rows of suns.

**6.3.** To imagine Him on the fine couch of their hearts, sages perform sacrifices kalpa after kalpa —
and on this couch He, so hard for them to reach, is easily found: what poet can describe this couch's good fortune?

**6.4.** The words of Brahma and Shiva do not gladden Him now — yet He listens with love to the parrot in its new cage, chattering on.
How long will the poet Narahari's Lord stay hidden? His former custom is plainly showing itself.[^12]

[^11]: GPT1 — pp. 363–364, no. 60 ("lalita"; scan, PDF pp. 622–623); GPT2 — section 5.2, no. 60, p. 235. **The catalogue did not know it** (new row NG-302). GCC —
kirana 3, pada 6 ("ko 'pi ca — rāga lalita"). Readings: 6.2 GPT1 "suruci puñja sukṛti iha mandira mājha" — GCC and GPT2 "so rucipuñja" (adopted); 6.4 GCC
"suprakaṭa". Not in the BR, PK, KGC, PS. B.
[^12]: Brahma and Shiva glorify the Lord with hymns, but the confidential lila is open not to them but to the companions of Navadvipa. The parrot is the
one who, according to the GCC, was reared by a servant of the Lord and sings the awakening songs at dawn (portion 22, note 35): in Vraja Vrinda's
parrots wake Radha and Krishna — the "former custom" of Vraja reveals itself in Navadvipa. The sages "imagine" the Lord on the couch of the heart —
an image of meditation: what is not reached by yoga is revealed in love.

## 7. Narahari — "hera cāñā dekha rajanī pāne…" (GPT1 p. 364, no. 61; GCC)
*Another companion: look — who ever knew such sleep? The pose of the hands and feet, the eyes, the moon-face, the smile that seems to drip
nectar; one cannot tell whether He sleeps or wakes; it will be hard to wake Him. Raga vibhasa. **Attribution: probably Chakravarti** —
the pada is in the GCC.*[^13]

**7.1.** Here, look, see this night: who ever knew such sleep?

**7.2.** How beautifully His hands and feet lie! I never knew that sleep could be like this.

**7.3.** And the eyes — so charmingly closed: how can languor be like this?

**7.4.** The beauty of His moon-face is greater still: as if nectar dripped from His gentle smile.

**7.5.** I cannot tell whether He sleeps or not; what is in my heart I cannot utter.

**7.6.** How much can Narahari say of it? It seems it will be hard to wake Him.[^14]

[^13]: GPT1 — p. 364, no. 61 ("vibhāsa"; scan, PDF p. 623); GPT2 — section 5.2, no. 61, p. 235 (catalogue: NG-198). GCC — kirana 3, pada 7 ("ko 'pi ca — rāga
lalita"; the GCC edition notes that the Agartala manuscript lacks this pada). Readings: 7.1 GCC "hera cāya dekha gaurāṅga pāne" ("look at Gauranga";
GPT "rajanī pāne", "at the night"); 7.2 GCC "kara-pada-bhaṅgimā", "kabhu"; 7.3 GCC "subhagati"; 7.5 GCC "kahite nāri". Not in the BR, PK, KGC, PS. B.
[^14]: The Lord's sleep cannot be told from waking: it is not the unconsciousness of a conditioned soul but absorption in lila (cf. 5.3: "or is this a
still greater play?").

## 8. Narahari — "gorācāṅdera rajanī śayana…" (GPT1 p. 364, no. 62; GCC)
*The companions gaze at the sleeping Gorachand, and their eyes are refreshed; joyful, they banter with one another and again and again
admire His sleep. Raga vibhasa. **Attribution: probably Chakravarti** — the pada is in the GCC.*[^15]

**8.1.** Gorachand's night sleep: gazing and gazing at it, everyone's eyes find rest.

**8.2.** Each one's heart is full of joy, and playfully they tell one another many things.

**8.3.** Can a poet describe it — the matchless virtues of Gauranga?

**8.4.** Again and again, with full eyes, they gaze at the sweetness of the sleep of the poet Narahari's Lord.[^16]

[^15]: GPT1 — p. 364, no. 62 ("vibhāsa"; scan, PDF p. 623); GPT2 — section 5.2, no. 62, p. 235 (catalogue: NG-199). GCC — kirana 3, pada 9 ("yathārāga"; before it,
pada 8: Gadadhara sits at the Lord's feet and presses them, while Narahari "floats in an ocean of happiness"). Readings: 8.1 GCC "gaurāṅga cāndera rajani-śayana";
8.2 GCC "ati ānanda hṛdaya". The search of the witnesses had missed it (distorted OCR of the GCC); found by reading the GCC continuously. Not in the BR, PK,
KGC, PS. B.
[^16]: "Playfully they tell" — the companions' loving talk about the Lord; it too is part of His lila (cf. portion 23, no. 5: the companions honour one
another).

## 9. Narahari — "kivā se niśira śobhā…" (GPT1 pp. 364–365, no. 63; GCC)
*Night in Nadia: the moon has washed the town, the trees blossom, a cool wind blows, all the inhabitants sleep in bliss; around the Lord's
chamber are His dear companions, some asleep, some awake; others have gone home at His bidding and pine. The poet prays to be near at this
hour. Raga bhairava. **Attribution: probably Chakravarti** — the pada is in the GCC (it opens the nishanta-lila).*[^17]

**9.1.** How lovely is that night — a heap of blessings filling the town of Nadia!
The moon, a washerman, with his own hands has washed all darkness from it.

**9.2.** Wondrous young trees and creepers shine with a sweetness that enchants sages;
over the freshly opened flowers bees roam in hope of honey.

**9.3.** Softly, softly a cool breeze blows, breathing waves of fragrance;
in supreme bliss all the inhabitants of Nadia lie asleep.

**9.4.** The deep house [of the Lord], ever full of happiness — its beauty has no bounds;
in all three worlds I have seen nothing to compare with it.

**9.5.** Around the Lord's chamber all His dear companions have taken their places;
some sleep, some lie awake — who can fathom such love?

**9.6.** And others, at His bidding, lie in their own homes, pining;
Narahari: when will it be so for me — when shall I too be near at that hour?[^18]

[^17]: GPT1 — pp. 364–365, no. 63 ("bhairava"; scan, PDF pp. 623–624); GPT2 — section 5.2, no. 63, p. 235 (catalogue: NG-200). GCC — kirana 2, pada 1 ("rāga bhairava"):
the first pada of the description of the ashtakaliya ("first, nishanta", the end of the night), right after the "table of contents" in kirana 1 (cf. portion
23, no. 19). Readings: 9.1 GCC has, at the words "purā se", an editor's mark of a reading "vāsa" (sense: "abode"); 9.2 GCC "praphullita nava kusume bhramara
bhramaye madhura āśe" ("bees roam" — the subject missing in the GPT is restored after the GCC); 9.5 GCC "keha śuñā keha jāgiyā rayeche". Not in the BR, PK,
KGC, PS. B.
[^18]: The moon as a "washerman" washing away darkness is a familiar image of moonlight. The pining of the companions sent home is a lila of separation
(cf. portion 23, nos. 16–17). The poet's wish "to be near at that hour" is a prayer for eternal service to the Lord in His nitya-lila.

## 10. Narahari — "ratana mandira madhi śuti gora sundara…" (GPT1 p. 365, no. 65)
*The Lord sleeps in a chamber of jewels, surrounded by His companions, like the moon among the stars; at the end of the night the
companions sing auspicious songs, as court singers wake a prince; the Lord leaves sleep and sits up on the couch to listen. Raga turi.
**Attribution: not established** — only in the GPT; Vrajabuli.*[^19]

**10.1.** In the chamber of jewels fair Gora sleeps, enjoying the play of sleep;
the host of dear companions sleeps all around the beloved Lord.

**10.2.** As in the wide sky the moon, encircled by rows of stars —
what poet has the power to describe that wondrous beauty?

**10.3.** Seeing that the night is ending, the companions sing auspicious songs —
as court singers and bards strike up a tune to break a prince's sleep.

**10.4.** Leaving sleep, He who sports in Nadia sat up on the couch to listen;
enchanted Narahari is enchanted beyond measure, beholding that joy.[^20]

[^19]: GPT1 — p. 365, no. 65 ("tuḍī"; scan, PDF p. 624; the last pada of section 5.2); GPT2 — section 5.2, no. 65, p. ≈235–236. **The catalogue did not know it** (new row
NG-303). Not found in any witness (GCC, BR, PK, KGC, PS, Gita-candrodaya). The GPT1 OCR is badly distorted ("vatana mandira maṇi śuṇaṭha … bhugcai
śanavilāsa"); the text follows the scan. Before it, no. 64 is a pada of Ghanashyama on the Lord's chamber (= GCC 2.10). "Not established", C.
[^20]: Vaitalikas and magadhas are court singers and bards who woke kings with songs and praises. The Lord's awakening by His companions' songs is the
beginning of the morning lila (cf. the awakening songs, portion 22, nos. 17–26). The companions sleep "around" the Lord, as in 9.5.

## 11. Narahari — "āhā mari āju ki ānanda…" (GPT1 p. 417, section 6.1, no. 10; BR)
*Nityananda's advent at Ekachakra, in the house of Hadai Pandita: the parents' joy; Advaita in Shantipura roars and dances: "I have brought
Balavira!"; the gods shower flowers; the Ojha gives gifts; blessed are the land of Radha and the bright thirteenth night of the month of
Magha. Raga kamoda. **Attribution: probably Chakravarti** — the pada is in the BR.*[^21]

**11.1.** Ah, what joy today!
In Ekachakrapura, in the house of Hadai Pandita, Nityananda has descended!

**11.2.** His body is most tender, like fresh golden butter; by His beauty the world is enchanted.
Gazing at His moon-face, Padmavati and Hadai Pandita cannot contain their delight.

**11.3.** Sri Advaita in Shantipura roars in a flood of joy and cannot be still for a moment;
He dances with arms raised, slaps His sides and says: "I have brought, I have brought Balavira!"

**11.4.** Brahma and the other gods shower Him with flowers; "Jaya, jaya!" resounds without end;
all the gandharvas and kinnaras play hundreds of instruments and sing His glory — an ocean of happiness.

**11.5.** The Ojha, most fortunate, for his son's welfare gives away gifts beyond counting;
in what merriment the people come running — the house is too crowded to enter!

**11.6.** Blessed is the land of Radha, blessed that nakshatra and that day, blessed the bright thirteenth night of Magha!
Narahari says: well done! Blessed, blessed is the age of Kali — by His appearance He has destroyed the heaps of sorrow.[^22]

[^21]: GPT1 — p. 417, section 6.1 "Nityananda", no. 10 ("kāmoda"; scan, PDF p. 676); GPT2 — section 6.1, no. 10, p. ≈273 (catalogue: NG-202, "in the BR"). BR 12 (ed. 1912,
pp. 824–825; ed. 1960, vv. 1711–1716): Narahari Chakravarti, showing Srinivasa the places of Navadvipa, tells of Nityananda, His parents and the day of His
advent — the bright thirteenth night of Magha (a Sanskrit verse, v. 1709) — and introduces the pada with the words "this joy the wise have described in many
ways"; then follows a pada of Ghanashyama (GPT no. 11). Readings: 11.2 BR "putramukha nirakhiyā" ("gazing at their son's face"; GPT "candra mukha"), "hāḍāi"
(GPT "hārāi"); 11.5 BR "yautuka laiyā" ("bringing gifts"; GPT "kautuka lañā", "in merriment"). Not in the PK, KGC, PS, GCC. B.
[^22]: Nityananda is Balarama Himself, the first expansion of Krishna (CC Adi 5); therefore Advaita, foreseeing His advent, cries: "I have brought Balavira"
("the hero Bala", Balarama) — just as He called Gaura into the world. Ojha is Hadai Pandita's family title. On Nityananda's childhood at Ekachakra — CB Adi 9.
The land of Radha (Rāḍha) is the region of Bengal west of the Ganga where Ekachakra lies.

## 12. Narahari — "bhāve gara gara nitāi sundara…" (GPT1 p. 422, no. 26; BR)
*The first meeting in Navadvipa: seeing Gorachand's radiance, Nityananda enters ecstasy — roaring, rolling on the ground, sighs, laughter,
leaps, trembling; at last He grows still in Vishvambhara's embrace; the Lord, holding Him in His lap, weeps; Srivasa and the others weep.
Raga mayura. **Attribution: probably Chakravarti** — the pada is in the BR.*[^23]

**12.1.** Overflowing with bhava, beautiful Nitai, seeing Gorachand's radiance —
how much wells up in His heart! He cannot stand still; on every limb new waves of thrill.

**12.2.** What madness! Now a lion's roar, now He rolls on the ground;
