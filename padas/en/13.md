# Balarama Dasa, Murari, Yadunatha, Ananta, Ramachandra: Padas from the *Gaura-pada-tarangini* — from Krishna's Resolve to the Prayers

*This chapter continues the group of padas known from the *Gaura-pada-tarangini* (GPT1 — 1st ed. by Jagadbandhu Bhadra,
1903; GPT2 — 2nd ed., 1934), excluding padas in nagari-bhava. Here are padas signed by Balarama Dasa, Yadunatha Dasa, Murari,
Ananta (Raya Ananta) and Ramachandra, in the order of the GPT sections (that is, of the lilas): the nandi prologue (Krishna's
resolve to appear as Gaura), praise of the Gaura-avatara and wonder at the sannyasa, Gaura and Gadadhara, childhood plays,
the kirtanas of Navadvipa, the bhava of Radha, the Lord's morning, the people of Nadia in Shantipura, the waiting for His
return, praise of Nityananda and Advaita, the books of Jiva Gosvami, and prayers. Checking against the *Padakalpataru* (PK)
showed that nine padas listed as "GPT only" or missing from the catalogue altogether are in PK (nos. 4–7, 9, 15, 19–21) —
there the text follows PK with the GPT variants; no. 4 is signed by Vasu Ghosha in PK, no. 5 by "Natavara," no. 8 is
Balarama's pada PK 2201 (formerly misidentified with Lochana's pada PK 2203; corrected in the full PK check of chapter 16). Seven padas (nos. 4, 5, 8, 9, 19, 20, 25) were missing from
the stage-1 catalogue: they were found in a full check of the signatures of GPT1. Majumdar considers the GPT unreliable for
attribution; padas of several poets are known under the signature "Balarama Dasa"; the "Ramachandra" of nos. 24–25 is, by his
own words, a grandson of Vamshivadana who lived after the Lord's departure. The attribution of the padas of this chapter is
therefore provisional. The full PK check (chapter 16) found nos. 16 (PK 2512) and 18 (PK 1976) in PK as well;
their variants have been added. The Bengali original, the variants of GPT1/GPT2, PK and KGC, and the word-for-word translation are in the full version of the book.*

## 1. Balarama Dasa — "śunaite rāi vacana adharāmṛta…" (GPT1 p. 2, no. 2)
*Prologue (nandi) of the *Gaura-pada-tarangini*: having heard Radha tell of Her dream in which She saw a golden youth,
Krishna reveals to Her that He Himself will take that form: three desires — to know what Her prema is like, what its
sweetness is, and what happiness She tastes — were not fulfilled in Vraja; taking Her bhava and lustre, making Her prema
His guru, He will rise in Nadia.*[^1]

**1.1.** Hearing the nectar of Rai's words, the clever Kanu, full of rasa,
received, know it, the fair one's consent — to reveal Her bhava in His own self.[^2]

**1.2.** "Beautiful one, the form of Gaura you have spoken of
no one knows: only by your prema will you make Me such."

**1.3.** "What your prema is like, what its sweetness, in what happiness you are absorbed —
these three longed-for treasures were not fulfilled in Vraja; what can I say — I have found no end to them.[^3]

**1.4.** Having pondered, I have understood: except in your very form this joy can never be tasted.
Taking your bhava and lustre, making your prema My guru, I shall rise in Nadia.

**1.5.** I shall fulfil My heart's longing, remove My heart's obstacle, distribute to the world the treasure of prema."
Balarama Dasa says: my Lord is all-merciful — and I, lowest of men, have not served Him.

[^1]: GPT1 — printed p. 2, section "Nandi, or Prelude" (nāndī vā pūrvābhāsa), no. 2, no raga given; checked against the IA scan
in.ernet.dli.2015.477854 (PDF p. 261); GPT2 — sec. 1.1, no. 2. The pada is not in the *Padakalpataru* (PK), the
*Bhakti-ratnakara* (BR), the *Kshanada-gita-chintamani* (KGC) or the *Padamrita-samudra* (PS). Padas nos. 2–4 are a dialogue
of Krishna and Radha continuing no. 1 (Radha's dream of a golden youth, signed by Jagadananda) and concluded by no. 5
(Vaishnava Dasa). Majumdar considers the GPT unreliable for attribution; padas of several poets are known under the
signature "Balarama Dasa," and this pada on the purpose of the Gaura-avatara contains nothing pointing to an eyewitness —
the attribution is provisional (C).
[^2]: Kanu (Kan) is Krishna. "To reveal Her bhava in Himself" is what Krishnadasa Kaviraja names as the cause of the
Gaura-avatara: Krishna, taking Radha's bhava and lustre, tastes His own sweetness (CC Adi 1.5–6; 4).
[^3]: The three desires — CC Adi 1.6 and 4: to know the greatness of Radha's love, the sweetness of Himself which Radha
tastes, and the happiness She feels. The poet puts this theological motif into Krishna's mouth almost word for word.

## 2. Balarama Dasa — "vām̐dhu he śunaite kām̐pai dehā…" (GPT1 p. 2, no. 3)
*Radha's reply: at this news Her body trembles — He is the life of Vraja; like a fish without water or a serpent
without its jewel, Vraja cannot live a moment without Him; will He, leaving Her, reveal prema with His brother and friends
on the bank of another river?*[^4]

**2.1.** "Beloved, hearing this my whole body trembles.
You are the life of Vraja: without You how will Vraja keep its footing?

**2.2.** As a fish without water, as a serpent without its jewel gives up its life,
so Vraja without the sight of You for even half a moment — You know what its state will be.

**2.3.** Having finished everything, what perfection will You attain, what happiness will You find?
Will others know Your heart? For this my breast is torn.

**2.4.** You dwell in the groves and bowers of Vrindavana, You, Kanu, best of lovers;
day and night, without the sight of You, all will pine and give up their lives.

**2.5.** With Your elder brother, with Your friends You will sport on the river bank —
and forgetting me You will reveal prema?" Balarama Dasa does not understand.[^5]

[^4]: GPT1 — p. 2, no. 3, no raga (scan PDF p. 261); GPT2 — sec. 1.1, no. 3. In 2.5 GPT1 reads "pāsaravi mujhe" (you will forget
me), GPT2 "parihari mujhe" (abandoning me). Radha's anguish is not worldly sorrow but the highest prema (mahabhava) by which
Vraja lives; the fear of separation is inseparable from it (CC Madhya 2).
[^5]: "Elder brother" — Balarama, who appeared in the Gaura-lila as Nityananda; "friends" — the cowherd boys who became
the Lord's associates. The "river bank" (yamunātaṭa) is in the poem the bank of the Yamuna; in the Gaura-lila its place is
taken by the Ganga of Navadvipa. Radha's fear is answered by Krishna in the next pada: He will not leave Vraja (3.4) — in
Bhaktisiddhanta's teaching Navadvipa and Vraja are non-different, and Gaura is Radha and Krishna in one form (CC Adi
4.55–56).

## 3. Balarama Dasa — "śunaha sundari majhu abhilāṣa…" (GPT1 p. 2, no. 4)
*Krishna's reply: He will reveal the prema of Vraja; with all the cowherd men and boys He will sport in Nadia; the two
bodies will merge into one, and He will ceaselessly repeat Her name; He will never leave Vraja — without Vraja prema is
not attained.*[^6]

**3.1.** "Hear, beautiful one, My desire:
the prema of Vraja I shall reveal to the world.

**3.2.** Gathering all the cowherd men and boys,
in the town of Nadia I shall begin My play.

**3.3.** Our bodies, joined, will become one,
and My lips will ceaselessly repeat your name.

**3.4.** Leaving Vraja, I shall never go away:
without Vraja prema is not attained.

**3.5.** With the bhava of Vraja I shall fulfil My heart's desire" —
this Balarama Dasa has known by realization.[^7]

[^6]: GPT1 — p. 2, no. 4, no raga (scan PDF p. 261; the last line is smudged in the scan, the reading "anubhavi jānala" is
confirmed by GPT2); GPT2 — sec. 1.1, no. 4.
[^7]: "Two bodies will become one" — Gaura is Radha and Krishna united in one form (CC Adi 1.5; 4.55); "repeat Her name" —
the Lord in Navadvipa and Puri cries "Radha, Radha!" "The heart's desire" (mana-kāma) is the thirst to taste Radha's love,
not worldly lust.

## 4. Vasu Ghosha (in GPT1 — Balarama Dasa) — "purave vāndhila cūṛā eve keśa-hīna…" (PK 2255; GPT1 p. 18, no. 47)
*The Lord's sannyasa as Krishna's transformation: formerly He tied His hair in a top-knot — now His head is shaven;
instead of the milk-pail in His left hand — the sannyasi's water-pot; in the Treta-yuga He held the bow, in the
Dvapara the flute, in the Kali-yuga He took the staff; Balarama has become an avadhuta, Kanai a sannyasi. Raga
gandhara.*[^8]

**4.1.** Formerly He tied His hair in a top-knot — now He is without hair;
leaving the dress of the best of dancers, He has put on the kaupina.

**4.2.** A pail for milking the cows was in His left hand —
after that very pattern Gora has taken the sannyasi's water-pot.[^9]

**4.3.** In the Treta-yuga He held the bow, in the Dvapara the flute;
in the Kali-yuga, taking the staff, He has become a sannyasi.

**4.4.** Vasu Ghosha says: listen, people of Nadia:
Balarama is an avadhuta, Kanai a sannyasi![^10]

[^8]: PK: vol. 3, PDF p. 288 (printed p. 277), 21st pallava "The sannyasa of Sri Gauranga, etc.," label "॥৩৮॥২২৫৫॥", raga
gandhara, MS P-R-Sa no. 2310 — signed by **Vasu Ghosha** (4.4: "vāsughoṣa kahe…"). GPT1 — p. 18, section "Mangalacharana,"
no. 47 (gandhara), checked against the scan (PDF p. 277): the same pada, but in 4.4 "valarāma kahe śuna nadīyānivāsī," i.e. signed by
Balarama; the compiler of GPT1 himself noted that a manuscript and PK ascribe it to Vasu Ghosha. GPT2 — sec. 1.2, no. 47.
The pada was missing from the stage-1 catalogue (new row); the attribution to Vasu Ghosha (per PK) is preferable: the
signature "Balaram" in GPT1 probably arose from the second half of 4.4, where Balarama's name refers to Nityananda.
[^9]: Kaupina — the loincloth of the renunciant; karanga (karaṅga) — the sannyasi's water-pot (kamandalu). The poet sets the
marks of Krishna the cowherd beside those of Gaura the sannyasi: the milk-pail in His left hand has "become" the water-pot.
[^10]: Avadhuta — a renunciant above outward rules; so Nityananda was called, who is Balarama (CC Adi 5; CB Adi 9). Kanai
(Kanu) is Krishna, appearing as Gauranga. The Lord's sannyasa is not the renunciation of a conditioned soul but a lila
for the deliverance of the people of Kali-yuga (CC Adi 17; CB Madhya 26).

## 5. Balarama Dasa (in PK — "Natavara") — "gopīgaṇa-kuca-kuṅkume vañcita…" (PK 2250; GPT1 p. 18, no. 48)
*Wonder at the Lord's sannyasa: He whom the maidens of Vraja embraced, whose body was coloured with their kunkuma, is
now in saffron cloth, covered with thrills, with streams of tears of prema; the dark-bodied Dancer of Vrindavana has
become a pauper begging prema from house to house. Raga dhanashi (in GPT1 — kedara).*[^11]

**5.1.** Bereft of the kunkuma from the gopis' breasts, He has a ruddy cloth shining on His body;
His body, whose lustre puts gold to shame, is from the joy of Rai's touch.[^12]

**5.2.** Look, look — the wondrous play of Gaura!
He who was the teacher of love to hundreds of thousands of young maidens has now taken sannyasa.[^13]

**5.3.** He whom the maidens of Vraja ceaselessly held in their firm embrace
is now all thrilling with goosebumps, and tears of prema stream from His eyes.

**5.4.** That best of dancers, with a body dark as a rain-cloud, who roamed the forest of Vrinda —
says Natavara — now, a pauper, begs prema from house to house.[^14]

[^11]: PK: vol. 3, PDF p. 286 (printed p. 275), 21st pallava, label "॥৩৩॥২২৫০॥", raga dhanashi, MSS P-R-Sa no. 2355 and P-R no. 12 —
signature "kahaye naṭavara" (according to Ray's apparatus, P-R-Sa reads "valarāma"). GPT1 — pp. 18–19, no. 48 (kedara), checked
against the scan (PDF p. 277): "kahaye valarāma naṭavara so ava…" — signed by Balarama. GPT2 — sec. 1.2, no. 48. In PK the pada follows
PK 2249 of Balarama (chapter 11, no. 16), where it was noted that PK 2250 is signed "Natavara" and had not been
translated; it was missing from the catalogue (new row). Attribution to Balarama — per P-R-Sa and GPT1 (C).
[^12]: The first half-line in PK reads "kuṅkume vañcita" ("bereft of the kunkuma"): formerly Krishna's body was ruddy with the
gopis' kunkuma, now the sannyasi's cloth is ruddy; GPT1 reads "kuṅkume rañjita" ("coloured with kunkuma"). Gaura's golden
lustre comes from Radha's touch: He has taken Her complexion (CC Adi 4).
[^13]: "Guru-lampata" (guru lampaṭa) — literally "the foremost of lovers": the language of madhurya-rasa. The gopis' love for
Krishna is not worldly passion but the purest prema, only called "kama" (CC Madhya 8; Adi 4.165–171); the contrast in the
pada stresses that the same Krishna, the Lord of Vraja, has become a renunciant.
[^14]: "Natavara" — in PK apparently the poet's name (or an epithet of Krishna taken by a scribe for a signature); in P-R-Sa
and GPT1 — "Balarama." "A beggar of prema" — the Lord, who Himself distributes prema, in the mood of a devotee begs it of
everyone (CC Adi 7).

## 6. Yadunatha Dasa — "gaura-varaṇa tanu sundara sukhamaya…" (PK 2180; GPT1 p. 36, no. 28)
*Gaura and Gadadhara in Navadvipa: a golden body full of bliss, a heart full of compassion; at Gaura's left is the
beloved Gadadhara, and the hidden rasa is revealed, as if the circle of the rasa; in Nadia there are so many moons that the
darkness has gone far away; Gaura and Gadadhara are a lake of prema that has overflowed the earth. Raga the same as that of
the preceding PK pada (in GPT1 — kamoda).*[^15]

**6.1.** A golden-hued body — beautiful, full of bliss; a compassionate heart — an abode of rasa;
a garland of jasmine and oleander, woven row upon row, sways — a forest garland.

**6.2.** At Gaura's left is the best one, the beloved Gadadhara: the hidden rasa is revealed;
as if the circle of the rasa had shone forth — and their speech falters with prema.[^16]

**6.3.** In the town of Nadia so many moons — the darkness has gone far away;
however many spotless lamps are lit — beside them they cannot even be discerned.

**6.4.** Gaura and Gadadhara are a lake of prema: overflowing, it has filled the earth;
Dasa Yadunatha, cheated by fate, not getting even a touch of it, weeps.[^17]

[^15]: PK: vol. 3, PDF p. 261 (printed p. 250), label "॥১৩॥২১৮০॥", raga "tathā" (the same as PK 2179), MS P-R-Sa no. 2288. GPT1 — p. 36,
no. 28 (kamoda), checked against the scan (PDF p. 295); GPT2 — sec. 3.1 (OCR, line 25224). In the catalogue (YD-006) the pada was
listed as "GPT only"; it is found in PK — **core**. In PK it stands among Nayanananda's padas on Gaura and Gadadhara. On Yadunatha
Dasa (Kavichandra), a fellow villager of Jagannatha Mishra, see the catalogue (CB Madhya 1; CC Adi 11); other padas signed
"Yadunatha Dasa" in PK are PK 2125, 2126, 2191 (chapter 6).
[^16]: Gadadhara Pandita is the Lord's eternal associate, in whom Vaishnavas see a manifestation of Radha (GGD 147–153); the
"hidden rasa" is their prema, not worldly intimacy. The "circle of the rasa" is the image of Krishna's rasa-lila showing
through the kirtana of Gaura and Gadadhara.
[^17]: "Cheated by fate" is the usual formula of humility in a poet's signature.

## 7. Balarama Dasa — "yata yata avatāra-sāra…" (PK 2346; GPT1 p. 38, no. 37)
*Praise of the Gaura-avatara: among all incarnations the incarnation of Gora is renowned; prema for Krishna, inaccessible
even to Brahma, the Lord distributed down to the chandalas; the flood of prema has drowned foreigners and unbelievers, beasts
and birds sing and weep in kirtana — only Balarama has been left out. Raga bhatiyari.*[^18]

**7.1.** Of all and all the incarnations the essence —
renowned has remained my incarnation of Gora!

**7.2.** Prema for Krishna and the treasure of the Name, hard for even Brahma to obtain,
the Lord gave down to the chandalas and filled the world with them.

**7.3.** In the flood of prema foreigners, unbelievers and the rest —
all people, immersed, laugh, dance and sing.

**7.4.** Beasts and birds, tigers, deer, the creatures of the waters
laugh, weep, dance, sing and perform kirtana.

**7.5.** Heaven, earth and the netherworlds have drowned in prema for Gora —
left out has been only Dasa Balarama.

[^18]: PK: vol. 3, PDF p. 320 (printed p. 309), 23rd pallava, label "॥১৫॥২৩৪৬॥", raga bhatiyari, MS P-R-Sa no. 2450; it is followed in PK by
PK 2347 (chapter 12, no. 1). GPT1 — p. 38, no. 37 (bhatiyari), checked against the scan (PDF p. 297); GPT2 — sec. 1.3, no. 37. In the
catalogue (BD-009) the pada was listed as "GPT only"; it is found in PK — **core** (C). A chandala is an outcaste beyond the
varnas; the gift of prema to all without distinction — CC Adi 7; CB Madhya 13.

## 8. Balarama Dasa — "sava avatāra sāra gorā avatāra…" (PK 2201; GPT1 p. 38, no. 39)
*Praise of the Gaura-avatara: such mercy there has never been; the Lord, entreating, gave prema to the humble and fallen;
whoever does not serve such a treasure of mercy drinks poison with his own hands and will not be saved even in millions of
kalpas. Raga shri.*[^19]

**8.1.** The essence of all incarnations is the incarnation of Gora:
such mercy is seen nowhere else.

**8.2.** To the humble, the destitute, the low, the fallen — to each one
the Lord, entreating and entreating, gave the treasure of prema.

**8.3.** Whoever does not serve such a treasure of mercy
has lifted poison with his own hand and drunk it.

**8.4.** Whoever has been left out at such an incarnation
will find no deliverance even in millions of kalpas.

**8.5.** And I, low as I am, have not served such a Lord —
says Balarama: now I am burning and perishing.[^20]

[^19]: GPT1 — p. 38, no. 39 (shri), checked against the scan (PDF p. 297); GPT2 — sec. 1.3, no. 39. **The pada is in PK — PK 2201**
(vol. 3, 20th pallava "Śrī-Gauracandra — prakārāntara," PDF p. 269, printed p. 258, label "॥১৮॥২২০১॥", raga "tathā", MS P-R-Sa no. 2309) —
with the same signature of Balarama ("…kahe valarāma eve marinu puṛiyā" — "now I am burning"). The earlier statement that 8.1–8.4 are
the beginning of Lochana Dasa's pada PK 2203 was mistaken: PK 2203 begins "ke yāve ke yāve bhāi bhava-sindhu pāra" (MS P-R-Sa
no. 2311) and merely stands near this pada (corrected in the full PK check, chapter 16). Balarama's signature is attested both in PK and
in GPT — C. The pada was missing from the stage-1 catalogue (row BD-045).
[^20]: "Burning and perishing" (PK: marinu puṛiyā; GPT: paṛiyā — "fallen and perishing") is a formula of penitence in the signature (cf. 7.5); the poet speaks of himself, not of the Lord.

## 9. Balarama Dasa — "navadvīpa-gagane uyala dina rāti…" (PK 2196; GPT1 p. 39, no. 40)
*Gaura as a rain-cloud: in the sky of Navadvipa a cloud stands day and night, watering all that lives; a downpour of the
nectar of prema; foul weather has covered the world; the peacock-devotees dance, the frog-non-devotees croak in the water;
the creeper of bhakti has overspread the three worlds; the lightning of kirtana has burnt the forest of yoga, the thunder
has split knowledge; the serpent of karma is driven out, the chataka is satisfied; the earth has drowned — only Balarama
is left on the mountain of samsara. Raga kamoda.*[^21]

**9.1.** In the sky of Navadvipa a cloud has risen and stays day and night:
with a downpour of rasa it has watered all that lives — the unmoving and the moving.

**9.2.** Look, look: Gaura is the incarnation of a cloud,
ceaselessly raining the nectar of prema!

**9.3.** Since then the whole world is wrapped in foul weather;
with the rasa of Hari the people of the world, intoxicated, have forgotten themselves.

**9.4.** In frenzy the peacock-devotees dance;
the non-devotees — the frogs — croak, sunk in the water.[^22]

**9.5.** The creeper of bhakti has overspread the three worlds:
the fruit of prema the high and the low have received.

**9.6.** With the thunderbolt of kirtana the forest of yoga is burnt,
and knowledge too the cloud's thunder splits apart.

**9.7.** From the hole of the heart the serpent of karma is driven out;
the waves of Kali's burning intoxication have subsided.

**9.8.** The scorched chataka has drunk its fill;
in all ten directions rivers have flowed.

**9.9.** The earth has drowned, for no one is there any dry place —
on the mountain of samsara only Balarama is left.[^23]

[^21]: PK: vol. 3, PDF pp. 266–267 (printed pp. 255–256), 20th pallava, label "॥১৩॥২১৯৬॥", raga kamoda, MS P-R-Sa no. 2304. GPT1 — p. 39,
no. 40 (kamoda), checked against the scan (PDF p. 298); GPT2 — sec. 1.3, no. 40. The pada was missing from the catalogue (new row); **core** (PK, C).
**core** (PK, C).
[^22]: Images of the rainy season: peacocks dance at the sight of clouds, frogs croak in the water; so the Bhagavata describes the
rains in Vraja (Bhag. 10.20), and the poet transfers the images to Gaura's kirtana. "Croak" (royata) — literally "weep, wail."
[^23]: The chataka is a bird that drinks only rainwater — an image of the soul thirsting only for prema. "Rivers" (nadiyā) puns on
the name Nadia. "The mountain of samsara" — the poet is left "high and dry," not washed by the flood of prema (cf. 7.5).

## 10. Ananta (Raya Ananta?) — "gaura navaghana premadhārā variṣila…" (GPT1 p. 40, no. 44)
*Gaura's mercy as husbandry: the young cloud Gaura has rained down prema; the hard soil of evil thoughts is broken by the
ploughing of bhakti, and prema has sprouted in hearts; Nitai waters the shoot with the water of bhakti, and the tree of prema
grows day by day, bearing fruit for all. Raga suhai.*[^24]

**10.1.** Gaura, a young rain-cloud, has poured down streams of prema:
thirsty, scorched souls have drunk their fill.

**10.2.** The hard soil of evil thoughts is broken by the tillage of bhakti —
in the hearts of living beings a shoot of prema has sprung up.

**10.3.** That shoot Nitai watered with the water of bhakti,
and day by day the tree of prema grew higher.

**10.4.** It has borne the fruits of prema for all living beings —
Ananta has been left out by the turns of his own karma.

[^24]: GPT1 — p. 40, no. 44 (suhai), checked against the scan (PDF p. 299); GPT2 — sec. 1.3, no. 44 (last word "kammaphere"). The pada is not
in PK, BR, KGC or PS (catalogue: AN-010; the signature given in the catalogue as "…karmadoṣe" reads "karmaphere" in the scan). In GPT it
follows the pada of Raya Ananta from KGC (no. 43, chapter 11, no. 3), and the signature "Ananta" here is probably the same poet's —
Ananta of Advaita's branch (per M-1961); attribution only by GPT (C). The image of Nityananda watering the shoot of devotion — cf.
the "tree of prema," CC Adi 9.

## 11. Murari — "śacīra āṅginā mājhe bhuvanamohana sāje…" (GPT1 p. 77, no. 47)
*Childhood: in the middle of Shachi's courtyard, enchanting the world, little Gorachand crawls on all fours; holding His
mother's finger He toddles and falls; a tiger-claw swings at His neck, His chest is wet with dribble, on His moon-face is the
lightning of a smile; Shachi takes the dusty child to her breast, but He, crying, climbs down and rolls on the ground again;
Murari, laughing, says: this is no child for the lap — Gaurahari will become a sannyasi. Raga pahira.*[^25]

**11.1.** In the middle of Shachi's courtyard, in an attire that enchants the world, Gorachand crawls on all fours;
holding His mother's finger, now He toddles along — and, tripping, falls down.

**11.2.** A tiger-claw swings at His neck, His chest is wet with dribble, on His moon-face is the lightning of a smile;
covered all over with dust — can a mother bear it? — she lifts Him to her breast.

**11.3.** Crying, displeased at this, Gora climbs down from her lap and rolls on the ground again;
Murari, laughing, says: "This is no child for the lap — Gaurahari will become a sannyasi."[^26]

[^25]: GPT1 — p. 77, section "Childhood plays," no. 47 (pahira), checked against the scan (PDF p. 336); GPT2 — sec. 2.2, no. 47. The pada
is not in PK, BR, KGC or PS. Catalogue: MU-001; the row MU-006 ("…hāsiyā murāri vole e nahe kolera chele," after Majumdar's reference to

*Sutramalika*, *Dhatu-sangraha* are well-known works of Jiva; *Rasamrita-shesha* is his supplement to the *Bhakti-rasamrita-sindhu*;
*Krishnarchana-dipika* is a manual of worship; *Krishna-* and *Radha-pada-chihna* describe the marks on the feet; the *Sapta-sandarbha* is

## 26. Balarama Dasa — "bhāi re sādhusaṅga kara sādhu haiyā…" (GPT1 pp. 515–516, no. 59)
*A prayer-instruction: brothers, keep the company of saints, becoming saints yourselves — you will cross the ocean of existence,

**26.1.** Brothers, keep the company of saints, becoming saints yourselves:
you will cross the ocean of existence and find great bliss, singing the glories of Nitai and Chaitanya.

**26.2.** Weary of wandering through eight million four hundred thousand births, you have at last obtained the rare body;

**26.3.** Wearing beads and marks, but having not a trace of devotion, I go about showing myself to people —
like the makal fruit: red, handsome and fine to look at, but break it open — fit only to be thrown away.

**26.4.** The trees and creepers growing near the sandal tree it makes like itself by its breeze;
but this essence — the company of saints — wretched Balarama has not: he has remained in the well of existence.[^60]
