# Balarama Dasa, Murari, Yadunatha, Ananta, Ramachandra: Padas from the *Gaura-pada-tarangini* — from Krishna's Resolve to the Prayers

*This portion continues the group of padas known only (or chiefly) from the *Gaura-pada-tarangini* (GPT1 — 1st ed. by
Jagadbandhu Bhadra, 1903; GPT2 — 2nd ed., 1934), excluding padas in nagari-bhava. INTRODUCTORY NOTE — AT THE END OF THE WORK.*

## 1. Balarama Dasa — "śunaite rāi bacana adharāmṛta…" (GPT1 p. 2, no. 2)
*Prologue (nandi) of the *Gaura-pada-tarangini*: having heard Radha tell of her dream in which she saw a golden youth,
Krishna reveals to her that He Himself will take that form: three desires — to know what her prema is like, what its
sweetness is, and what happiness she tastes — were not fulfilled in Vraja; taking her bhava and lustre, making her prema
His guru, He will rise in Nadia.*[^1]

**1.1.** Hearing the nectar of Rai's words, the clever Kanu, full of rasa,
received, know it, the fair one's consent — to reveal her bhava in His own self.[^2]

**1.2.** "Beautiful one, the form of Gaura you have spoken of
no one knows: only by your prema will you make Me such."

**1.3.** "What your prema is like, what its sweetness, in what happiness you are absorbed —
these three longed-for treasures were not fulfilled in Vraja; what can I say — I have found no end to them.[^3]

**1.4.** Having pondered, I have understood: except in your very form this joy can never be tasted.
Taking your bhava and lustre, making your prema My guru, I shall rise in Nadia.

**1.5.** I shall fulfil My heart's longing, remove My heart's obstacle, distribute to the world the treasure of prema."
Balarama Dasa says: my Lord is all-merciful — and I, lowest of men, have not served Him.

[^1]: GPT1 — printed p. 2, section "Nandi, or Prelude" (নান্দী বা পূর্বাভাস), no. 2, no raga given; checked against the IA scan
`in.ernet.dli.2015.477854` (PDF p. 261); GPT2 — sec. 1.1, no. 2. The pada is not in the *Padakalpataru* (PK), the
*Bhakti-ratnakara* (BR), the *Kshanada-gita-chintamani* (KGC) or the *Padamrita-samudra* (PS). Padas nos. 2–4 are a dialogue
of Krishna and Radha continuing no. 1 (Radha's dream of a golden youth, signed by Jagadananda) and concluded by no. 5
(Vaishnava Dasa). Majumdar considers the GPT unreliable for attribution; padas of several poets are known under the
signature "Balarama Dasa," and this pada on the purpose of the Gaura-avatara contains nothing pointing to an eyewitness —
the attribution is provisional (C).
[^2]: Kanu (Kan) is Krishna. "To reveal her bhava in Himself" is what Krishnadasa Kaviraja names as the cause of the
Gaura-avatara: Krishna, taking Radha's bhava and lustre, tastes His own sweetness (CC Adi 1.5–6; 4).
[^3]: The three desires — CC Adi 1.6 and 4: to know the greatness of Radha's love, the sweetness of Himself which Radha
tastes, and the happiness she feels. The poet puts this theological motif into Krishna's mouth almost word for word.

## 2. Balarama Dasa — "bā̃dhu he śunaite kā̃pai dehā…" (GPT1 p. 2, no. 3)
*Radha's reply: at this news her body trembles — He is the life of Vraja; like a fish without water or a serpent
without its jewel, Vraja cannot live a moment without Him; will He, leaving her, reveal prema with His brother and friends
on the bank of another river?*[^4]

**2.1.** "Beloved, hearing this my whole body trembles.
You are the life of Vraja: without You how will Vraja keep its footing?

**2.2.** As a fish without water, as a serpent without its jewel gives up its life,
so Vraja without the sight of You for even half a moment — You know what its state will be.

**2.3.** Having finished everything, what perfection will You attain, what happiness will You find?
Will others know Your heart? For this my breast is torn.

**2.4.** You dwell in the groves and bowers of Vrindavana, You, Kanu, best of lovers;
day and night, without the sight of You, all will pine and give up their lives.

**2.5.** With Your elder brother, with Your friends You will sport on the river bank —
and forgetting me You will reveal prema?" Balarama Dasa does not understand.[^5]

[^4]: GPT1 — p. 2, no. 3, no raga (scan PDF p. 261); GPT2 — sec. 1.1, no. 3. In 2.5 GPT1 reads "পাসরবি মুঝে" (you will forget
me), GPT2 "পরিহরি মুঝে" (abandoning me). Radha's anguish is not worldly sorrow but the highest prema (mahabhava) by which
Vraja lives; the fear of separation is inseparable from it (CC Madhya 2).
[^5]: "Elder brother" — Balarama, who appeared in the Gaura-lila as Nityananda; "friends" — the cowherd boys who became
the Lord's associates. The "river bank" (যমুনাতট) is in the poem the bank of the Yamuna; in the Gaura-lila its place is
taken by the Ganga of Navadvipa. Radha's fear is answered by Krishna in the next pada: He will not leave Vraja (3.4) — in
Bhaktisiddhanta's teaching Navadvipa and Vraja are non-different, and Gaura is Radha and Krishna in one form (CC Adi
4.55–56).

## 3. Balarama Dasa — "śunaha sundari majhu abhilāṣa…" (GPT1 p. 2, no. 4)
*Krishna's reply: He will reveal the prema of Vraja; with all the cowherd men and boys He will sport in Nadia; the two
bodies will merge into one, and He will ceaselessly repeat her name; He will never leave Vraja — without Vraja prema is
not attained.*[^6]

**3.1.** "Hear, beautiful one, My desire:
the prema of Vraja I shall reveal to the world.

**3.2.** Gathering all the cowherd men and boys,
in the town of Nadia I shall begin My play.

**3.3.** Our bodies, joined, will become one,
and My lips will ceaselessly repeat your name.

**3.4.** Leaving Vraja, I shall never go away:
without Vraja prema is not attained.

**3.5.** With the bhava of Vraja I shall fulfil My heart's desire" —
this Balarama Dasa has known by realization.[^7]

[^6]: GPT1 — p. 2, no. 4, no raga (scan PDF p. 261; the last line is smudged in the scan, the reading "অনুভবি জানল" is
confirmed by GPT2); GPT2 — sec. 1.1, no. 4.
[^7]: "Two bodies will become one" — Gaura is Radha and Krishna united in one form (CC Adi 1.5; 4.55); "repeat her name" —
the Lord in Navadvipa and Puri cries "Radha, Radha!" "The heart's desire" (মন-কাম) is the thirst to taste Radha's love,
not worldly lust.

## 4. Vasu Ghosha (in GPT1 — Balarama Dasa) — "purabe bāndhila cūṛā ebe keśa-hīna…" (PK 2255; GPT1 p. 18, no. 47)
*The Lord's sannyasa as Krishna's transformation: formerly He tied His hair in a top-knot — now His head is shaven;
instead of the milk-pail in His left hand — the sannyasi's water-pot; in the Treta-yuga He held the bow, in the
Dvapara the flute, in the Kali-yuga He took the staff; Balarama has become an avadhuta, Kanai a sannyasi. Raga
gandhara.*[^8]

**4.1.** Formerly He tied His hair in a top-knot — now He is without hair;
leaving the dress of the best of dancers, He has put on the kaupina.

**4.2.** A pail for milking the cows was in His left hand —
after that very pattern Gora has taken the sannyasi's water-pot.[^9]

**4.3.** In the Treta-yuga He held the bow, in the Dvapara the flute;
in the Kali-yuga, taking the staff, He has become a sannyasi.

**4.4.** Vasu Ghosha says: listen, people of Nadia:
Balarama is an avadhuta, Kanai a sannyasi![^10]

[^8]: PK: vol. 3, PDF p. 288 (printed p. 277), 21st pallava "The sannyasa of Sri Gauranga, etc.," label "॥৩৮॥২২৫৫॥", raga
gandhara, MS P-R-Sa no. 2310 — signed by **Vasu Ghosha** (4.4: "বাসুঘোষ কহে…"). GPT1 — p. 18, section "Mangalacharana,"
no. 47 (gandhara), checked against the scan (PDF p. 277): the same pada, but in 4.4 "বলরাম কহে শুন নদীয়ানিবাসী," i.e. signed by
Balarama; the compiler of GPT1 himself noted that a manuscript and PK ascribe it to Vasu Ghosha. GPT2 — sec. 1.2, no. 47.
The pada was missing from the stage-1 catalogue (new row); the attribution to Vasu Ghosha (per PK) is preferable: the
signature "Balaram" in GPT1 probably arose from the second half of 4.4, where Balarama's name refers to Nityananda.
[^9]: Kaupina — the loincloth of the renunciant; karanga (করঙ্গ) — the sannyasi's water-pot (kamandalu). The poet sets the
marks of Krishna the cowherd beside those of Gaura the sannyasi: the milk-pail in His left hand has "become" the water-pot.
[^10]: Avadhuta — a renunciant above outward rules; so Nityananda was called, who is Balarama (CC Adi 5; CB Adi 9). Kanai
(Kanu) is Krishna, appearing as Gauranga. The Lord's sannyasa is not the renunciation of a conditioned soul but a lila
for the deliverance of the people of Kali-yuga (CC Adi 17.264–265; CB Madhya 26).

## 5. Balarama Dasa (in PK — "Natavara") — "gopīgaṇa-kuca-kuṅkume bañcita…" (PK 2250; GPT1 p. 18, no. 48)
*Wonder at the Lord's sannyasa: He whom the maidens of Vraja embraced, whose body was coloured with their kunkuma, is
now in saffron cloth, covered with thrills, with streams of tears of prema; the dark-bodied Dancer of Vrindavana has
become a pauper begging prema from house to house. Raga dhanashri (in GPT1 — kedara).*[^11]

**5.1.** Bereft of the kunkuma from the gopis' breasts, a ruddy cloth shines on His body;
His body, whose lustre puts gold to shame, is from the joy of Rai's touch.[^12]

**5.2.** Look, look — the wondrous play of Gaura!
He who was the teacher of love to hundreds of thousands of young maidens has now taken sannyasa.[^13]

**5.3.** He whom the maidens of Vraja ceaselessly held in their firm embrace
is now all thrilling with goosebumps, and tears of prema stream from His eyes.

**5.4.** That best of dancers, with a body dark as a rain-cloud, who roamed the forest of Vrinda —
says Natavara — now, a pauper, begs prema from house to house.[^14]

[^11]: PK: vol. 3, PDF p. 286 (printed p. 275), 21st pallava, label "॥৩৩॥২২৫০॥", raga dhanashri, MSS P-R-Sa no. 2355 and P-R no. 12 —
signature "কহয়ে নটবর" (according to Ray's apparatus, P-R-Sa reads "বলরাম"). GPT1 — pp. 18–19, no. 48 (kedara), checked
against the scan (PDF p. 277): "কহয়ে বলরাম নটবর সো অব…" — signed by Balarama. GPT2 — sec. 1.2, no. 48. In PK the pada follows
PK 2249 of Balarama (portion 11, no. 16), where it was noted that PK 2250 is signed "Natavara" and had not been
translated; it was missing from the catalogue (new row). Attribution to Balarama — per P-R-Sa and GPT1 (C).
[^12]: The first half-line in PK reads "কুঙ্কুমে বঞ্চিত" ("bereft of the kunkuma"): formerly Krishna's body was ruddy with the
gopis' kunkuma, now the sannyasi's cloth is ruddy; GPT1 reads "কুঙ্কুমে রঞ্জিত" ("coloured with kunkuma"). Gaura's golden
lustre comes from Radha's touch: He has taken her complexion (CC Adi 4).
[^13]: "Guru-lampata" (গুরু লম্পট) — literally "the foremost of lovers": the language of madhurya-rasa. The gopis' love for
Krishna is not worldly passion but the purest prema, only called "kama" (CC Madhya 8; Adi 4.165–171); the contrast in the
pada stresses that the same Krishna, the Lord of Vraja, has become a renunciant.
[^14]: "Natavara" — in PK apparently the poet's name (or an epithet of Krishna taken by a scribe for a signature); in P-R-Sa
and GPT1 — "Balarama." "A beggar of prema" — the Lord, who Himself distributes prema, in the mood of a devotee begs it of
everyone (CC Adi 7).
