# Vamshidasa, Nayanananda, Shivananda, Yadu, Govinda and Vasu Ghosha, Krishnadasa, Kanu Dasa and Others: Padas from the *Gaura-pada-tarangini* — Completing the "GPT Only" Group

*This portion completes the group of padas by poets other than Narahari that are known from the *Gaura-pada-tarangini*
(GPT1 — 1st ed. by Jagadbandhu Bhadra, 1903; GPT2 — 2nd ed., 1934), excluding padas in nagari-bhava. The padas follow the
order of the lilas: mangalacharana and praise of the Gaura-avatara, Gaura's form, the abhisheka in Srivasa's house and the
adhivasa of the kirtana of the "seven groups," Gaura with Gauridasa in the bhava of Krishna and of Radha, the goshtha-bhava and the
festival of colours, the decision to take sannyasa and the lament after it, the Ratha-yatra in Puri, Nityananda (His marriage — in
a pada that Majumdar considers a forgery — His dance and His mercy), Ramananda Raya. Seven padas are signed "Yadu" (nos. 4, 5,
20–23, 26); five of them are also in the *Padakalpataru* (PK 2459, 2460, 1546, 1547, 2300; and PK 1548 without signature, no. 23) —
there the text follows PK with the GPT variants; who this "Yadu" is — Yadunatha Kavichandra, a contemporary of the Lord, or the
later Yadunandana — is not established. Checking against the text of GPT1 showed that the stage-1 catalogue had ascribed to Vamshi,
Gauridasa, Paramananda and Narahari a good many padas of other poets: of the seven padas of "Vamshi," six belong to others
(Vaishnava Dasa, Premadasa, Rajavallabha, Govinda Dasa, Vasu Ghosha, or are unsigned); instead, a goshtha-pada of Vamshidasa
(no. 15) is translated, and the pada "āhā mari kothā gela gorā kā̃cā sonā" (no. 19 — "Vamshi" in the catalogue) is signed by Vasu
Ghosha; the padas of "Gauridasa" are signed by Jagannatha Dasa and Chaitanyadasa, those of "Paramananda" by Vrindavana Dasa and
Gopala Dasa (see the summary of portion 14 in `CATALOG.md`). Four padas (nos. 9, 17, 23, 26) were missing from the catalogue.
Majumdar considers the GPT unreliable for attribution; the attribution of all the padas of this portion is therefore provisional
(C; D only where authenticity itself is in doubt: an added signature, a forgery). The Bengali original, the variants of GPT1/GPT2 and PK,
and the word-for-word translation are in
`bn/14.md`.*

## 1. Vamshidasa — "jaya re jaya re mora gaurāṅga rāya…" (GPT1 p. 5, no. 8)
*Mangalacharana: glory to Gauranga, to Nityananda and to Gaura's devotees; the poet asks Sitanatha — Advaita Acharya — to
give him the shade of His feet and to keep his wicked mind there: he has no other hope. Raga kamoda.*[^1]

**1.1.** Glory, glory to my Gauranga Raya!
Glory to Nityananda the moon, glory to Gaura's devotees! Sitanatha, give me the shade of Your feet!

**1.2.** Glory, glory to my Acharya Thakura! I am helpless, utterly fallen:
be merciful — keep this sinful mind of mine at Your feet.[^2]

**1.3.** In Your feet alone I trust — I see no other way;
keep my wicked mind at Your holy feet — this I beg at Your feet.

**1.4.** Whatever I long for at all times — all of it You know.
Says Vamshidasa: fulfil all my hopes — what more shall I say?

[^1]: GPT1 — p. 5, section of mangalacharana, no. 8 (kamoda), checked against the scan IA `in.ernet.dli.2015.477854` (PDF p. 264);
GPT2 — sec. 1.2, no. 8. The pada is not in PK, the *Bhakti-ratnakara* (BR), the *Kshanada-gita-chintamani* (KGC) or the
*Padamrita-samudra* (PS) (catalogue: VV-001). Under the signature "Vamshi, Vamshidasa" there are padas of Vamshivadana Thakura, the
Lord's associate, and of a later Vamshidasa (by Majumdar, PK 2851 is his; see portion 06, no. 16); this pada is a prayer with no
sign of an eyewitness; attribution by GPT (C).
[^2]: "Acharya Thakura" — by the refrain (Sitanatha, the husband of Sita) this is Advaita Acharya. After the Lord's sannyasa
Vamshivadana lived with Shachi and Vishnupriya in Navadvipa (according to tradition); a prayer to Advaita is possible from him, but the pada
may also belong to a disciple of Advaita's line.

## 2. Nayanananda — "gorā mora guṇera sāgara…" (GPT1 p. 31, no. 15)
*Praise of Gaura in images: He is an ocean of virtues where waves of prema rise; a spotless moon pouring the nectar of the
holy name; the Himalayan peak from which the Ganga of prema flows; a wish-fulfilling tree; a new cloud that cools everyone; a
mine of bliss. Raga dhanashi.*[^3]

**2.1.** My Gora is an ocean of virtues:
in it waves of prema rise without cease.

**2.2.** My Gora is a spotless moon:
day and night the nectar of Hari's name drips from it.

**2.3.** My Gora is the peak of the Himalayas:
from it the Ganga of prema flows without cease.

**2.4.** My Gora is a wish-fulfilling tree that gives prema:
in the shade of His feet living beings dwell happily.

**2.5.** My Gora is a new rain-cloud:
pouring rain, He cools men and women.

**2.6.** My Gora is a mine of bliss:
Nayanananda's life is given for Him as a ransom.[^4]

[^3]: GPT1 — p. 31, section of praise of the Gaura-avatara, no. 15 (dhanashi), checked against the scan (PDF p. 290); GPT2 — sec. 1.3,
no. 15. The pada is not in PK, BR, KGC or PS (catalogue: NN-003). Nayanananda Mishra is the nephew and disciple of Gadadhara
Pandita; his padas on Gaura in PK were translated in portions 04 and 10. This pada is known only from the GPT (C).
[^4]: "Given as a ransom" (nichani) — a rite in which something dear is waved around the beloved and given away to avert harm from
him: the poet gives his life for Gaura. In 2.1 GPT1 has "guṇera nāgara" (a misprint or misreading), GPT2 "guṇera sāgara": the image of an
ocean with waves requires "sagara."

## 3. Shivananda — "akhila bhuvana bhari hari-rasa-bādara…" (GPT1 p. 35, no. 25)
*The cloud of Chaitanya: a downpour of Hari's rasa has filled the universe, and the devotees, like chataka birds, drink and ask
for more; the cloud was born on the full moon of Phalguna and flooded high and low with prema; the holy name became the
maha-mantra, prema was given out by handfuls, the fallen became bhagavatas, Jagai and Madhai were saved; the poet laments
that he has remained in forgetfulness. Raga mangala.*[^5]

**3.1.** Filling the universe, a downpour of Hari's rasa pours from the cloud of Chaitanya;
the devotees, like chataka birds, drink and drink without end, every moment begging for the water of prema.

**3.2.** The full moon of Phalguna is the cloud's birthday; that cloud poured the downpour:
high and low — it flooded all with the water of prema: Gora is a great ocean of mercy.

**3.3.** Making living beings His yantra and Hari's name the maha-mantra, He gave prema by handfuls from hand to hand;
all the low and wretched became bhagavatas — so Gauranga's glory grew.[^6]

**3.4.** There were Jagai and Madhai — them too He saved with prema: such mercy He showed to living beings.
Says Shivananda-dasa: why have I remained in the forgetfulness of maya? Lord, give me the shade of Your feet![^7]

[^5]: GPT1 — p. 35, no. 25 (mangala), checked against the scan (PDF p. 294); GPT2 — sec. 1.3, no. 25. The pada is not in PK, BR, KGC or
PS. In the catalogue (SS-001) the first line was wrong: "iha kaliyuga dhana nityānanda caitanya" is the preceding GPT pada (no. 24), signed by
Haridasa; the pada signed "dāsa śibānanda" is no. 25. Whether this is Shivananda Sena, the father of Kavi Karnapura, is unclear (C);
Majumdar considers Shivananda Sena the author of padas on the Lord composed in His lifetime.
[^6]: The full moon of Phalguna is the day of the Lord's appearance (1486). "Yantra" and "mantra" — an image from ritual: as a sacred
diagram and a mantra serve worship, so living beings became the Lord's vessel and Hari's name the great mantra. The sense of the
line is not quite clear. A footnote in GPT1 gives "durgata" (distressed) for "duḥkhita" (wretched). "Glory" (ṭhākurāli) — the Lord's
majesty, lordship.
[^7]: Jagai and Madhai — CB Madhya 13; CC Adi 17. "The forgetfulness of maya" (māyā-bhole) — the poet of himself: "maya" here in the
everyday sense — forgetting God and attachment to the world; it has nothing to do with the Lord.

## 4. Yadu — "eke se kanayā-kaṣila tanu…" (PK 2459; GPT1 p. 120, no. 27)
*Gauranga's form: His body like gold tested on the touchstone, outshining the moon; curls tied in a knot, garments of red silk;
raising His arms He stretches — and lightning seems to flash; the gaze slips over His body, over which waves run; all pride is
broken. Raga "the same" (in GPT1, "yatharaga").*[^8]

**4.1.** First of all, His body, like gold tested on the touchstone:
as if it subdues even the spotless moon.

**4.2.** And then — the curly locks tied in a knot:
even a trace of that beauty intoxicates the playful fair ones.[^9]

**4.3.** How wondrous is Gauranga's beauty —
coveted by the fair women of the three worlds!

**4.4.** In the guise of His red silk garment
the red passion of young hearts seems to overflow.

**4.5.** Raising His arms, He stretches His whole body —
and in its radiance lightning seems to flash.

**4.6.** The gaze cast upon His body slips off:
over all His body waves run in play.

**4.7.** A lovely garland of keshara flowers —
says Yadu: all pride is broken.[^10]

[^8]: PK — vol. 4 (pt. 2), 4th shakha, 29th pallava, mark "॥২॥২৪৫৯॥," raga "tatha," checked against the scan IA `354459` (PDF p. 35,
printed p. 26); in PK the pada stands among the gaura-chandrikas to the description of beauty (before it, Premadasa's PK 2458;
after it, PK 2460 and the description of Radha's beauty, "atha śrīrādhāyāṃ yathā"). GPT1 — p. 120, section on Gaura's form, no. 27
("yatharaga"), scan PDF p. 379; GPT2 — sec. 3.1, no. 27. The pada is not in BR, KGC or PS (catalogue: YD-008). The signature "yadu" (Yadu)
may belong to Yadunatha Kavichandra, a contemporary of the Lord (M-1961 ascribes to him padas on Gaura's bhavas), or to the later
poet Yadunandana Dasa (17th c.); PK 2459–2460 show no sign of an eyewitness; C.
[^9]: "Playful fair ones" (raṅgiṇī), "young hearts" (4.4), "pride" (4.7) — the motif of the townswomen captivated by Gaura's beauty:
the language of nagari-bhava (Gaura as nagara); we translate faithfully to the author. Vrindavana Dasa stresses that in this
incarnation the Lord never looked at another man's wife; worship of Gaura in the spirit of "gaura-nagari" is not accepted by the
Gaudiya tradition (Vrindavana Dasa, Krishnadasa Kaviraja, Bhaktisiddhanta). In PK the pada is a gaura-chandrika: it sets the tone
for the description of the beauty of Radha and Krishna, and the attraction here is an image of prema for Krishna, not of a worldly
feeling. "Knot" (loṭana) — hair gathered in a knot; "cā̃cara keśa" — curly locks.
[^10]: "Red silk… red passion" — a play on words: "raga" means both "red colour" and "attraction, love." Keshara — the nagakeshara flower
(or kadamba). "Pride is broken" (bhāṅgala māna) — "mana," the beloved's offended pride, melts before Gaura's beauty. PK manuscripts:
"ka" — "chāla," "uchāla," "araṅga" for "chale," "uchale," "taraṅga"; "kha, gha, cha" — "madana" for "damana" (4.1: "subdues Madana").

## 5. Yadu — "bikaca kanayā-kamala-kā̃ti…" (PK 2460; GPT1 p. 120, no. 28)
*Gauranga's form and bhava: the lustre of a full-blown golden lotus, a face like the full moon, teeth like pomegranate seeds,
lips redder than the bandhuli; He walks intoxicated with an unknown rasa, with the gait of a king of elephants; tears flow from
His reddish eyes; remembering His former love He weeps, thundering like a new cloud, and calls: "Where is Gadadhara?" Raga
"the same."*[^11]

**5.1.** A lustre like that of a full-blown golden lotus,
a face shining like the full moon.

**5.2.** Teeth in an even row, like pomegranate seeds,
lips red — redder than the bandhuli flower.[^12]

**5.3.** Sweet, sweet is Gauranga's beauty —
coveted by the eyes of the three worlds.

**5.4.** Who knows with what rasa He is ever intoxicated? —
His gait is slow, like a king of elephants'.

**5.5.** From His reddish eyes tears stream —
is it a pair of chakoras pouring out nectar?[^13]

**5.6.** Remembering His former love, He weeps —
as a new cloud thunders.

**5.7.** "Where is Gadadhara?" He calls.
Says Yadu: the Lord is caught in a whirlpool.[^14]

[^11]: PK — vol. 4 (pt. 2), 29th pallava, mark "॥৩॥২৪৬০॥," raga "tatha," checked against the scan (PDF pp. 35–36); GPT1 — p. 120, no. 28
("tatharaga"); GPT2 — sec. 3.1, no. 28. The pada is not in BR, KGC or PS (catalogue: YD-009); attribution as for the preceding (C).
[^12]: "Shikhara" (śikhara) — in poetics, a ruby the colour of a ripe pomegranate seed; PK manuscripts "ka, kha" and the GPT: "daśana sikara nikara"
(a row of drops), "cha": "śikara." Bandhuli — a red flower (Pentapetes phoenicea), a common image for lips. "Redder" (āti = ati; "ka, kha": "ati").
[^13]: Chakoras — birds that drink moonlight; Gaura's eyes are a pair of chakoras on the moon of His face. GPT: "āsiyā base ki cakora jorā" —
"has a pair of chakoras come and settled?"
[^14]: "Former love" — the love of Vrindavana: remembering His lila as Krishna, the Lord calls Gadadhara — the associate in whom the
tradition sees a manifestation of Radha (*Gaura-ganoddesha-dipika* 147–153). His tears and "thunder" are manifestations of prema,
not of worldly grief. "Caught in a whirlpool" (ṭhekilā pāke) — in the power of prema, unable to come out of it. PK MSS "ka, kha, cha": "lorā,"
"jorā"; "gha": "kānde ki."

## 6. Govinda Ghosha — "snāna kari śrīgaurāṅga basilena dibyāsane…" (GPT1 p. 240, no. 9)
*The abhisheka in Srivasa's house: after the bath Gauranga sits on a splendid seat with Nitai and Gadai to His right and left;
Advaita offers sweets and payasa, Srivasa runs to bring the dishes; Gora eats with Nitai and Gadai, Advaita gives Him tambula,
Narahari fans Him with a chamara, gazing at the three forms; Advaita places tulasi at His feet, saying "Krishnaya namah." Raga
mangala.*[^15]

**6.1.** Having bathed, Sri Gauranga sat on a splendid seat, with Nitai and Gadai to His right and left;
Advaita, seated before Him, holds sweets and payasa in His hands, Srivasa runs to and fro serving.

**6.2.** Ah, ah, what bliss of the abhisheka!
With Nitai and Gadai Gora sat down to eat — the devotees watch in bliss.

**6.3.** Having finished His meal, Gora rinsed His mouth, Advaita placed tambula in His mouth;
Narahari, standing beside, gazes at the three forms and joyfully fans Him with a chamara.[^16]

**6.4.** Placing tulasi leaves with sandal at Gora's feet, the Acharya says: "Krishnaya namah!"
Says Govinda Ghosha: and all, rejoicing, began to call out "Hari!" again and again.[^17]

[^15]: GPT1 — p. 240, section of the abhisheka, no. 9 (mangala), checked against the scan (PDF p. 499); GPT2 — sec. 4.1, no. 9. The pada is not
in PK, BR, KGC or PS (catalogue: GG-008). The preceding GPT pada (no. 8) is PK 1538 with the signature of the three Ghosha brothers
(portion 01, no. 4), on the same scene: Gauranga on the throne after the abhisheka, the meal with Nityananda, tulasi from Advaita; this
pada is its continuation with the signature of Govinda Ghosha alone. Govinda Ghosha is the Lord's associate, an eyewitness according to
Majumdar; but the pada is known only from the GPT (C).
[^16]: Gadai — Gadadhara Pandita. "The three forms" — Gaura, Nitai and Gadai; Narahari Sarakara, the Lord's associate from Shrikhanda,
served Him with the chamara (CB Madhya 9 names Narahari among those present; cf. portion 01, nos. 3–4). The abhisheka and the "great
revelation" (mahaprakasha) in Srivasa's house — CB Madhya 9; Murari 2.12; CC Adi 17.
[^17]: "Krishnaya namah" — "obeisance to Krishna": Advaita worships Gaura as Krishna Himself. The Acharya — Advaita Acharya.

## 7. Vasu Ghosha — "jaya jaya dhvani uṭhe nadīyā-nagare…" (GPT1 p. 240, no. 10)
*The abhisheka: cries of "Jaya!" throughout Nadia; Advaita roars: "I have brought Him!" — and dances, Haridasa after Him;
Srivasa and his brothers dance; devotees come running from all sides. Raga dhanashi.*[^18]

**7.1.** Cries of "Jaya, jaya!" have risen in the town of Nadia:
today is Gora's abhisheka in the Pandita's house.

**7.2.** "I have brought Him, brought Him!" cries Advaita Gosani;
He lets out a great roar, oblivious of everything around.[^19]

**7.3.** Arms raised, "Nara" dances — "ta-theiya, ta-theiya!";
behind Him Haridasa goes round, dancing.

**7.4.** Srivasa, Shripati, Shrinidhi and Shrirama
dance in delight — a joy to the eyes.

**7.5.** "Glory to Gauranga, glory to Advaita and Nitai!" —
crying so, the devotees come running.

**7.6.** Some dance and sing in prema, some laugh in prema;
the lila of Gora's abhisheka is sung by Vasu Ghosha.

[^18]: GPT1 — p. 240, no. 10 (dhanashi), checked against the scan (PDF p. 499); GPT2 — sec. 4.1, no. 10. The pada is not in PK, BR, KGC or PS
(catalogue: VG-165). The colloquial form "enechi, enechi" ("I have brought") is modern Bengali, unusual for the 16th century; by it the pada is
probably late or reworked; Majumdar warns that the GPT contains forgeries under Vasu's name; but this pada shows no sign of forgery. C.
[^19]: By His worship and His call Advaita "brought" the Lord to earth (CC Adi 3; CB Adi 2); for this the Lord called Him "Nara" (nāṛā, 7.3)
(CB, Madhya-khanda). "The Pandita" — Srivasa Pandita; Shripati, Shrinidhi and Shrirama are his brothers (CC Adi 10).

## 8. Vasu Ghosha — "gorā-abhiṣeka kathā adbhuta kathana…" (GPT1 p. 240, no. 11)
*The abhisheka: hearing the wonderful news, the devotees run to the Pandita's house and dance, crying "Glory to Gorachand!";
with them the whole universe dances — moon, sun, stars, Brahma, Indra, the gods, the nagas, the guardians of the quarters.
Raga dhanashi.*[^20]

**8.1.** Wonderful is the news — of Gora's abhisheka:
hearing it, the devotees run to the Pandita's house.

**8.2.** Running together, they dance joyfully;
raising both arms, they cry: "Glory to Gorachand!"

**8.3.** The moon dances, the sun dances, the stars dance;
Brahma dances, Vayu dances, the Thousand-eyed dances.

**8.4.** Aruna and Varuna dance, all the gods;
in the underworld Vasuki dances, the nagas dance.

**8.5.** Heaven dances, earth dances, the underworld dances;
in supreme bliss the guardians of the ten quarters dance.

**8.6.** In bliss the devotees roar out —
boundless is the joy in Vasu Ghosha's heart.[^21]

[^20]: GPT1 — p. 240, no. 11 (dhanashi), checked against the scan (PDF p. 499); GPT2 — sec. 4.1, no. 11. The pada is not in PK, BR, KGC or PS.
In the catalogue (VG-031) it was listed under section 3.2, "nagari-bhava" — wrongly: it is the abhisheka, section 4.1, with no nagari
motif; it had not been translated. GPT only (C).
[^21]: The Thousand-eyed — Indra; Aruna — the charioteer of the Sun; Varuna — lord of the waters; Vasuki — king of the naga serpents. The
rejoicing of the whole universe at the Lord's revelation — cf. CB Madhya 9 (the gods come to see the mahaprakasha).

## 9. Krishnadasa — "abhiṣeke gauracā̃dera ānanda apāra…" (GPT1 p. 241, no. 13)
*The abhisheka: Gaurachand in boundless bliss tells the devotees of His former plays; His body thrills, His speech falters;
listening to Him, the devotees' hearts are comforted. Raga suhai.*[^22]

**9.1.** At the abhisheka boundless is Gaurachand's bliss:
He tells the devotees of His former plays.[^23]

**9.2.** From head to foot His body thrilled:
the golden hairs stood like a kadamba bud.

**9.3.** The heart melts in bhava, the speech falters with emotion:
with great effort fate has fulfilled our hope.

**9.4.** Shachi's son Gora is the treasure of the life of all:
hearing the words of His moonlike mouth, the heart was comforted.

**9.5.** Whoever has come to believe in Gorachand's lila —
wretched Krishnadasa is the servant of his servants.

[^22]: GPT1 — p. 241, no. 13 (suhai), checked against the scan (PDF p. 500); GPT2 — sec. 4.1, no. 13. The pada is not in PK, BR, KGC or PS; it was
missing from the catalogue (new row KR-020). The preceding GPT pada (no. 12, "dekha dui bhāi gaura nitāi") is Krishnadasa's PK 1576 (portion 07).
The signature "Krishnadasa" in GPT and PK covers padas of several poets; attribution by GPT (C).
[^23]: "Former plays" — Krishna's lilas in Vraja and the deeds of earlier avataras: during the mahaprakasha the Lord revealed to each devotee
his past and His own former lilas (CB Madhya 9–10). In 9.4 "jāti-prāṇadhana" (lit. "the life-treasure of the kin/of all") — the sense is not
quite clear.

## 10. Vasu Ghosha — "aguru candana lepiyā gorā-gāya…" (GPT1 p. 243, no. 19)
*The abhisheka: Gora's body is anointed with aguru and sandal, the associates fan Him with chamaras and pour water over Him; they wipe
His face, put on Him a garland of malati, dress Him in red garments and ornaments; Vasudeva gazes at that form. Raga
dhanashi.*[^24]

**10.1.** Having anointed Gora's body with aguru and sandal,
the dear associates fan Him with chamaras.

**10.2.** Someone, bringing water and holding it in his own hands,
pours it over Gauranga as his heart desires.

**10.3.** The face that outshines the moon they wipe to make it shine still more;
on Gora's body is a garland of malati flowers.

**10.4.** Red garments and various ornaments shine on Him —
Vasudeva gazes at that form.

[^24]: GPT1 — p. 243, no. 19 (dhanashi), checked against the scan (PDF p. 502); GPT2 — sec. 4.1, no. 19. The pada is not in PK, BR, KGC or PS
(catalogue: VG-164). GPT only (C). The bathing, anointing and dressing of the Lord at the abhisheka — cf. Vasu Ghosha's PK 1536–1537
(portion 01, nos. 2–3) and CB Madhya 9.

## 11. Parameshvari Dasa — "prabhura ādeśa pāñā bhakata sakala…" (GPT1 p. 246, no. 27)
*The adhivasa of the kirtana: at the Lord's command the devotees form seven groups, led by Nityananda, Mukunda, Advaita, Gadadhara,
Vasu Ghosha, Murari and Narahari; fourteen madals and fourteen pairs of kartals resound; in the courtyard pots of curd mixed with
turmeric are broken, and with it they mark one another's foreheads. Raga dhanashi.*[^25]

**11.1.** Having received the Lord's command, all the devotees
divided into seven parts and formed seven groups.

**11.2.** At the head of the first group was Nityananda;
the lead singer of the second was Mukunda.

**11.3.** The head of the third was Sitapati Himself;
Gadadhara became the leader of the fourth.

**11.4.** Of the fifth — Vasu Ghosha, of the sixth — Murari;
the leader of the seventh group was Narahari.

**11.5.** All at once fourteen madals thundered;
from fourteen pairs of kartals rose a great clamour.

**11.6.** Setting curd with amrasara in pots,
in the courtyard they broke them, mixing it with turmeric.[^26]

**11.7.** The curd mixed with turmeric all took up,
and in the bliss of prema they mark one another's foreheads.

**11.8.** Thus was the adhivasa — the blessing of the kirtana — performed;
in the bliss of prema sings Parameshvari Dasa.

[^25]: GPT1 — p. 246, no. 27 (dhanashi), checked against the scan (PDF p. 505; the catalogue had "p. 247"); GPT2 — sec. 4.1, no. 27. The pada
is not in PK, BR, KGC or PS (catalogue: PD-003). The signature "parameśvarīdāsa" is the same as that of the preceding GPT pada (no. 26 = PK 23,
the kirtana festival at Advaita's house, portion 05, no. 4), which this pada continues. Parameshvari (Parameshvara) Dasa is an associate of
Nityananda, one of the twelve gopalas (CC Adi 11). By the user's decision it is translated though known only from the GPT (C).
[^26]: Madal (mridanga, khol) — a clay drum; kartals — hand cymbals. "Seven groups" (sāta dala, sampradayas) — as at the Ratha-yatra in Puri

**24.1.** "Nitai, I shall not leave Nilachala any more.
There was Haridasa, dear to Me as life — he has wound up his lila; with whom shall I now play?

**24.2.** Advaita, Sri Srinivasa, Puri, Damodara Dasa — they have gone, leaving this joy;
who will share rasa with Me, with whom shall I wander? They have gone, laying a stone upon My breast.[^55]

**24.3.** Vishvarupa, My brother — there is no news of him: he went away, taking renunciation;
Krishnadasa, a mine of rasa — I shall hear his songs no more: he too has gone, driving a spear into My heart.

**24.4.** Nitai, live as a householder — go to the Pandita: seeing You, he will be happy;
with care he will give You two daughters in marriage — show him Your own form.[^56]

**24.5.** The fallen, the low, the ignorant — do not grieve them, be merciful to all;
call them Your own, have pity on living beings — Your mercy will be proclaimed in the three worlds.

**24.6.** And that land is My own abode; keep My glory, Balarama," — saying this mercifully, the Lord weeps.
