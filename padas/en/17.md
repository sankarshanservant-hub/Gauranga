# Appendix. Padas signed "Narahari" from the Gaura-pada-tarangini — I: mangalacharana, Gaura's form, abhisheka, kirtana and dance, bhavavesha

## Introductory note to the appendix

*In the Gaura-pada-tarangini (GPT1 — 1st ed. by Jagadbandhu Bhadra, 1903; GPT2 — 2nd ed., 1934) more than three hundred padas
are signed with the name "Narahari" (narahari, "narahari dāsa", "dāsa narahari", "narahari-pahu"). Those also found in the
Padakalpataru (PK) were translated in the main part (portions 01–06): they are padas of Narahari Sarakara, which Majumdar
examines as songs of an eyewitness. Here, as a separate appendix, are gathered the padas with the same signature known only
from the GPT (and from the Bhakti-ratnakara) — by decision, all are to be translated, with a caveat about attribution.*

*Narahari is the name of two poets. **Narahari Sarakara Thakura** of Shrikhanda was an associate of Mahaprabhu, the teacher
of Lochana Dasa, a singer of the Lord's bhavas in Navadvipa and Puri; according to the Gaura-ganoddesha-dipika (177), he is Madhumati, an intimate friend of
Radha in Vrindavana. **Narahari Chakravarti** (also called Ghanashyama Dasa), who lived in Vrindavana in the first half of the 18th
century, is the author of the Bhakti-ratnakara (BR), Narottama-vilasa, Gita-chandrodaya and other books; in the BR he inserted
many of his own songs signed "Narahari" or "Ghanashyama", mostly in Vrajabuli, and often put them in the mouths of the people
of Navadvipa while describing the holy places.*

*The signature alone does not tell whose a pada is. The index of poets in GPT2 divides the "Narahari" padas into three groups:
≈30 of Sarakara (taken from the journal Gauranga-madhuri, published in Shrikhanda), ≈171 of Chakravarti (those found in the
BR) and 112 of "Narahari Dasa", undistinguished. Majumdar accepts as Sarakara's only the padas preserved in PK and regards
padas known only from the GPT as unreliable; of the "Narahari" padas in the BR, by his analysis, only one belongs to Sarakara
(BR p. 924 = PK 2122, portion 03, no. 1). Sarakara's marks, according to Majumdar, are a simple Bengali without Vrajabuli,
without heaped comparisons and alliterations, a correct metre, brevity; ornate Vrajabuli with chains of assonance is
Chakravarti's manner. But even the index groups are not beyond doubt: thus pada no. 128 of the section on Gaura's form stands
in the index on a page assigned to Sarakara, while in the BR it is put in the mouth of a woman of Nadia (below, no. 8).*

*Therefore each pada is marked in its heading: **"probably Sarakara"** — simple Bengali, absent from the BR, assigned by the
GPT2 index to Sarakara; **"probably Chakravarti"** — the pada is in the BR (usually the language too is Vrajabuli with
alliterations); **"not established"** — the arguments conflict or are lacking. In the lila database the padas "probably
Chakravarti" have authority B (the author is a recognized acharya, the author of the Bhakti-ratnakara; his authorship is established by his
works), the padas "probably Sarakara" and "not established" have authority C; no pada of the appendix has authority D (doubtful
authenticity). Chakravarti's padas are a late (18th-century) poetic elaboration of the lilas by a recognized acharya: not the testimony of an
eyewitness, but not a forgery either; they often follow the Chaitanya-bhagavata and the Chaitanya-charitamrita. Padas in nagari-bhava
(speech of townswomen in love with Gaura) are deferred to the
section of nagari padas; padas in which "Narahari" is merely mentioned among the associates while the signature is
another's are excluded (see the portion summary in `CATALOG.md`).*

*Portion 17 is the first portion of the appendix: three padas of mangalacharana (by the GPT2 index — Sarakara's), Gaura's form,
the abhisheka in Srivasa's house and arati, kirtana and dance in Navadvipa (a large cycle, almost all of it known also from
the BR), bhavavesha — the Lord in the mood of Shiva and in the mood of Krishna. The padas follow the order of the lilas. The
base text is GPT1 (tesseract recognition, doubtful places by the IA scan `in.ernet.dli.2015.477854`) collated with GPT2 and
the BR (eds. 1912 and 1960); variants are in `bn/17.md`. All the padas were also checked against Narahari Chakravarti's Gita-chandrodaya — none of them is there. The notes of the GPT editors are not reproduced.*

## 1. Narahari — "gaura-līlā daraśane icchā baḍa haya mane…" (GPT1 p. 11, no. 27)
*Mangalacharana: the poet, seeing Gaura-lila, wishes to write it down in his own tongue but does not know how; the one who will
write a book of it is not yet born; the lila of Gaura and Gadadhara, which melts stone, cannot be described even by Sarasvati
and Shiva — may at least the poet's few songs help someone to reveal the Lord's lila. Raga pahida. **Attribution: probably
Sarakara** — the GPT2 index assigns the pada to Sarakara; it is not in the BR; simple Bengali tripadi; the poet speaks as an
eyewitness, before books on Gaura had appeared.*[^1]

**1.1.** Seeing Gaura's lila, I greatly long in my heart to write it all down in our own tongue;
but I am most lowly, I do not know how to write in due order — how then shall I write it?[^2]

**1.2.** He who will write this book is not yet born — long yet is the delay before his birth;
when it is composed in our own tongue, all people will understand — when will the Lord fulfil my wish?[^3]

**1.3.** The lila of Gaura and Gadadhara softens stone — who has the power to describe it?
Even if Sharada were to write ceaselessly, without end, and five-faced Sadashiva with her…[^4]

**1.4.** If, seeing the few songs I write, someone reveals to the world the Lord's lila —
Narahari will find happiness, the grief of his heart will go: at the singing of that book stones will melt.

## 2. Narahari Dasa — "vraja-bhūma kari śūnya nadīyāya avatīrṇa…" (GPT1 pp. 11–12, no. 28)
*Mangalacharana: an address to Gaura, recognized as the Krishna of Vraja: having emptied Vraja, He has descended in Nadia; there
is no peacock feather, no yellow cloth, no flute, no curving pose — yet, since He has let Himself be seen, the mind will not be
deceived: this is that very Kanai of Vraja. Raga pahida. **Attribution: probably Sarakara** — GPT2 index (Sarakara); not in
the BR; simple Bengali tripadi.*[^5]

**2.1.** Having emptied the land of Vraja, You descended in Nadia — such is Your cunning!
Giving ceaseless pain, having changed colour and bhava, You again multiply the snares of separation.[^6]

**2.2.** There is no crown of peacock feather, none of that yellow cloth, in Your hand none of that enchanting flute —
that flute with whose song You stole the very life of the gopis: where is that flute, Gaurahari?

**2.3.** None of those slanting eyes — now I see lovely eyes; none of that curve, none of that bending.
But since You have let Yourself be seen, the mind is not deceived by this form: You are that very Kanai of Vraja.[^7]

**2.4.** Says Narahari Dasa: whoever has no faith, let him come and see with his own eyes.
To speak of what happened that day at the meeting of the two is a pain in the heart.[^8]

## 3. Narahari — "rase tanu ḍharaḍhara gaura-kiśora-bara…" (GPT1 p. 12, no. 29)
*Mangalacharana: the Shyama of Dvapara is called Sri Krishna Chaitanya in Kali; within He is Shyama, without golden: to play with
Rai in the groves, out of love He became golden; the poet fears to speak of the hidden and cannot keep silent. Raga pahida.
**Attribution: probably Sarakara** — GPT2 index (Sarakara); not in the BR; simple Bengali tripadi.*[^9]

**3.1.** His body brimming with rasa, the best of youths, Gaura-kishora — now His name is Sri Krishna Chaitanya.
To speak of these hidden things is a pain in the heart: without bhakti no one else will know them.

**3.2.** In the Dvapara age Shyama, in Kali the name Chaitanya — so are Garga's words written in the Bhagavata.
I ponder in my heart: Shyama has become Gauranga — the body of Radha and Krishna is its witness.[^10]

**3.3.** Within, the dark body; without, the golden body — wondrous is Gauranga's lila!
To play with Rai, to enjoy the groves, out of love He took the golden body.[^11]

**3.4.** This is not a thing to be told — who knows what may come of telling it; yet not to tell is great pain in the heart.
Pondering in his mind, holding Gauranga in his heart, Narahari laments.

## 4. Narahari — "nācata bhuvana-mano-mohana…" (GPT1 p. 135, no. 70; BR)
*Gaura's form in the dance: His colour brighter than champaka, gold and lotus, His body in sandal paste and Chinese silk, His face
gentler than moons, His brows a row of bees, necklace and earrings swaying, curls scattering, His tendril-like hands playing,
His feet keeping the rhythm. Raga karnata. **Attribution: probably Chakravarti** — the pada is in the BR (12th wave); Vrajabuli
with chains of comparisons.*[^12]

**4.1.** He dances, the Enchanter of the minds of all the worlds — His colour surpassing champaka, gold and lotus;
His shapely, tender body anointed with sandal, He wears garments of Chinese silk, densely shining.

**4.2.** His sweet face puts to shame hosts of moons; sweetly He smiles — as if nectar were flowing;
His brows a row of bees; His eyes gleam and sway, robbing the dawn's rays of their power.[^13]

**4.3.** The jewelled necklace sways, stealing all composure; the earrings tremble, flashing at His ears;
His curling locks, under their own weight, scatter and sway like strands of darkness in the wind.

**4.4.** In graceful play of His tendril-like hands, stepping on the earth, He keeps hundreds of rhythms;
Narahari, in great delight, sings His glory: who can describe this boundless beauty?

## 5. Narahari — "nadīyāra mājhāre nācaye gorācā̃da…" (GPT1 p. 135, no. 72; BR)
*Gaura's form, from face to feet: Gorachand dancing in the midst of Nadia is a snare for every heart; seeing Him, the young
wives lose their senses; His face is the autumn moon, His speech nectar, His eyes the abode of love, His brows shame Kama's bow,
His curls are twined with malati, on His brow a drop of sandal; garland, long arms, slender waist, feet before whose nails Kama
swoons — this is the Nagara of Gokula, the ocean of rasa. Raga kamoda. **Attribution: probably Chakravarti** — the pada is in the
BR (12th wave, among the songs of the people of Nadia).*[^14]

**5.1.** In the midst of Nadia dances Gorachand —
a snare to capture the hearts of all people.

**5.2.** His body like golden filaments, His radiance beyond compare;
seeing Him, crowds of young wives lose their senses.[^15]

**5.3.** How sweet His face — the autumn moon!
His speech a stream of nectar, cooling the afflicted.

**5.4.** Slightly mingled with a smile, His lips shine;
the pearl-row of His teeth flashes.

**5.5.** His eyes are the abode of love;
with a glance He steals the life of the whole world.

**5.6.** Who carved these brows — so full of play —
to break the pride of Kama's bow?

**5.7.** The knot of His curling hair, flashing, is bent aside;
around the malati twined in it, swarms of bees are circling.

**5.8.** Who can keep composure, seeing that lovely brow?
The drop of sandal on it is death to the moon's pride.

**5.9.** On His breast sways the garland that has conquered the world;
whoever has seen it once keeps his eyes on it and remembers it ever.

**5.10.** How graceful the curve of His long arms!
How many movements in them — crushing the steadfastness of the chaste.

**5.11.** His slender waist — it would hide within a fist;
without price He buys hearts and refreshes the eyes.

**5.12.** The soles of His lotus feet are beyond compare;
before the radiance of His nails many a Kama swoons.[^16]

**5.13.** Says Narahari: what play does He not know! —
the Nagara of Gokula, that ocean of rasa.[^17]

## 6. Narahari — "suradhunī-tīre gaura naṭa-nāgara…" (GPT1 p. 136, no. 73; BR)
*Gaura's dance on the bank of the Suradhuni: Gaura, the graceful dancer, sports with His associates, captivating the eyes of the world;
His body outshines a golden mountain, His arms put to shame an elephant's trunk, again and again He cries "Hari, Hari!"; seeing
Gadadhara, He sheds tears like a cloud of the month of Shravana, and the earth trembles beneath His feet. Raga somaraga.
**Attribution: probably Chakravarti** — the pada is in the BR (12th wave); Vrajabuli.*[^18]

**6.1.** On the bank of the Suradhuni, Gaura, the graceful dancer, sports together with His associates;
the peerless, many-sided dance, ever-new sweetness — steals the eyes of all the people of the world.

**6.2.** His body, robbing a golden mountain of its pride, shines with abundant thrills;
the curve of His arms, which surpass an elephant's trunk, puts to shame hundreds of Kamas.[^19]

**6.3.** His teeth flash, lovely — the lustre of kunda flowers — in a soft smile on His sweet lips;
His face sways, His lips cry ceaselessly "Hari, Hari!" — who, hearing it, can keep composure?

**6.4.** His heart overflows when He sees Gadadhara: His eyes pour tears like a cloud in the month of Shravana;
says Narahari: the earth trembles under the weight of His lovely, light feet.[^20]

## 7. Narahari — "viharata surasarita-tīra…" (GPT1 pp. 151–152, no. 119; BR)
*Gaura's form on the bank of the Ganga, from face to feet: youthful, He sports by the river; His colour outshines lightning, gold
and kunkuma, His face is a snare for the eyes of young wives, His curls dark clouds, His brows serpents; broad chest, slender
waist, lotus feet about which bee-devotees hover; a cloud of mercy, He ever showers the nectar of prema — only foolish Narahari it
does not touch. Raga nataraga. **Attribution: probably Chakravarti** — the pada is in the BR (12th wave); Vrajabuli with strings of
comparisons.*[^21]

**7.1.** By the bank of the heavenly river strolls Gaura — young, in unchanging youth: the lustre of His body crushes the pride of
lightning, gold and kunkuma; His moon-face torments Madana, a snare for the eyes of all young wives; in His smile His teeth gleam —
a row of kunda flowers.

**7.2.** The colour of dense collyrium, His curling locks steal composure; peerless shine His pure attire and scattered tresses;
the tilaka on His brow flashes brightly, His brows are gracefully gliding serpents; the corners of His restless eyes, tinged with
rasa, are an abode of beauty.[^22]

**7.3.** Earrings touch His cheeks, on His neck the forest garland; long arms, bracelets, tender palms — I give my life for them!
His broad, peerless chest destroys the steadfastness of many a noble wife; His graceful, slender waist humbles the pride of the
lion.

**7.4.** His arms sway, His knees are youthful; His feet are rays of the red dawn; to the sweet fragrance of these lotuses cling the
bee-devotees, intoxicated; a cloud of mercy, famed in the world, He ever showers the nectar of prema — only foolish Narahari is not
touched by a single drop.[^23]

## 8. Narahari — "ki jāni ki bhāve bhāvita antara…" (GPT1 p. 154, no. 128; BR)
*Gaura's form with Gadadhara: absorbed in an unknown bhava, His eyes reddened, He holds Gadadhara's hand and says something — as
if honeyed; His lips a bandhuli flower, His teeth lightning, His smile pours nectar; He dances in the guise of a nagara, the thief
of the hearts of the women of Nadia — this is Gora, the Enchanter of Gokula. Raga vallari. **Attribution: not established
(more likely Chakravarti)** — the GPT2 index assigns the page to Sarakara, but the pada is in the BR, where it is sung by a woman
of Nadia.*[^24]

**8.1.** Who knows by what bhava His heart is seized — both eyes are reddened;
holding Gadadhara's hand, what He says I do not know — as if steeped in honey.[^25]

**8.2.** His lips a tender bandhuli flower, His teeth a flash of lightning;
mingled with His smile pours a sea of nectar — the splendour of His moon-face.

**8.3.** In the guise of a nagara dances the Thief of the hearts of Nadia's women;
says Narahari: do you not know? Gora is the Enchanter of Gokula.[^26]

## 9. Narahari — "āju abhiṣeka sukhera avadhi…" (GPT1 pp. 241–242, no. 14; BR)
*The abhisheka in Srivasa's house: Gaura, the treasury of virtues, sits on the throne; His curls stream down His back like the
Yamuna down a golden mountain; Nityananda and Advaita recite the abhisheka mantras, Srivasa and others pour fragrant water from
pitchers on the Lord's head; Jagadisha, Vasudeva, Narayana, Mukunda, Madhava sing, Govinda and Govindananda play the mridanga,
Vakreshvara dances; the invisible gods shower flowers, the devoted wives cry "Jaya!". Raga suhai or mayura. **Attribution:
probably Chakravarti** — the pada is in the BR (12th wave, account of the abhisheka).*[^27]

**9.1.** Today is the abhisheka — the very limit of happiness: on the throne sits Gora, the treasury of virtues;
peerless is the beauty of His bearing — no one on earth can keep composure.

**9.2.** Smooth curling locks adorn His head and stream down His back — their lustre charms the mind,
as if on the peak of a golden mountain the stream of the Yamuna were flowing.[^28]
