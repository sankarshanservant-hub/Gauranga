# Appendix. Padas signed "Narahari" from the Gaura-pada-tarangini — II: kirtana and dance (conclusion), bhavavesha, the festivals of the year, prarthanas

*Continuation of the appendix (the introductory note on the two Naraharis, on the arguments for attribution and on the marks in the
headings is in `17.md`). Portion 18: the end of the cycle of kirtana and dance in Navadvipa (GPT, section 4.2), two padas on the
Lord's bhavas (the bhava of Balarama; the Lord in the bhava of Radha waiting for Krishna), the festivals of the year — the "twelve-month
lila" (GPT, section 5.1: the swing-jhulana, Janmashtami and Nanda-utsava, Radhashtami, the remembrance of the rasa-lila, spring and
Holi) and two prarthanas. Before translation the "Narahari" signatures in these sections were checked throughout in GPT1 (tesseract
recognition): the catalogue had missed several more padas. The base text is GPT1 (doubtful places from the scan), collated with GPT2 and
with the Bhakti-ratnakara (eds. 1912 and 1960; almost all padas of the portion are in its 12th wave); PK, KGC, PS and the
Gita-candrodaya have been checked. Padas signed "Ghanashyama" (the same Narahari Chakravarti) are not included in the appendix. The
notes of the GPT editors are not reproduced.*

## 1. Narahari — "kibā khola karatāla bāje…" (GPT1 p. 267, no. 61; BR)
*Kirtana: the khol and karatalas sound, the associates stand around; they sing the sweet lila at which wood and stone melt; Gaura
dances, and no one knows by what bhava He is seized; His body thrills, streams of tears flow from His eyes; He embraces the fallen and
gives the treasure of prema to everyone — only the poet is left out. Raga nata. **Attribution: probably Chakravarti** — the pada is
in the BR (12th wave); short payar with Vrajabuli forms.*[^1]

**1.1.** How the khol and karatalas resound!
All around stand the adorned associates.

**1.2.** Today they sing the sweet lila —
hearing it, wood and stone melt.

**1.3.** *(Refrain)* Joyfully the beautiful Gora dances —
who knows by what bhava He is seized?

**1.4.** His body is covered with fresh thrills —
as if a golden jackfruit were shining.[^2]

**1.5.** Like the current of the celestial river,
streams flow from both His eyes.[^3]

**1.6.** Again and again raising both arms,
He thunders: "Hari, Hari!"

**1.7.** Seeing the most fallen and sinful,
again and again He clasps them in His embrace.

**1.8.** To each and every one He gives the treasure of prema,
passing over only one wretched Narahari.[^4]

[^1]: GPT1 — p. 267, no. 61 (nata); GPT2 — sec. 4.2, no. 61 (catalogue: НГ-249). BR — 12th wave (ed. 1912, p. 903; ed. 1960,
vv. 2783–2790): Ishana, showing Srinivasa Navadvipa, quotes it at the house of Sanjaya, "dear to the Lord", where Vishvambhara
danced, right after the pada "nācata śacītanaya gaura" (portion 17, no. 27). The GPT2 index assigns the page to Chakravarti. Not in
PK, KGC, PS or the Gita-candrodaya. B. The Lord's dancing in kirtana in Navadvipa — CB Madhya 8, 23; CC Adi 17.
[^2]: Thrills (পুলক) are one of the sattvika-bhavas, signs of prema: the hairs stand up like the spikes on the rind of a jackfruit
(পনস).
[^3]: "The celestial river" (সুরসরিত্) is the Ganga.
[^4]: Self-reproach is the usual ending of the padas of this cycle (cf. portion 17, nos. 13–27): the poet regards himself as the only
one passed over by the mercy given to all.

## 2. Narahari — "bhuvanamohana gorācāṁda…" (GPT1 p. 268, no. 63; BR)
*Dancing in prema: Gora-chand, enchanter of the world, dances weeping, cries "Hari!", embraces the fallen, floods everyone with His
rasa, throws Himself on the ground and rolls in the dust; seeing this, all living beings weep. Raga varadi or dhanashi. **Attribution:
probably Chakravarti** — the pada is in the BR (12th wave), though its language is a simple Bengali payar.*[^5]

**2.1.** Gora-chand, the enchanter of the world —
a net that snares the hearts of all people.[^6]

**2.2.** The Lord dances, seized by prema;
His reddened eyes swim in tears.

**2.3.** Raising His arms, He cries: "Hari, Hari!" —
and, seizing the fallen, clasps them to His breast.

**2.4.** With His own rasa He floods everyone;
all around His associates sing.

**2.5.** Flinging His most tender body to the ground,
He falls and rolls in the dust.

**2.6.** Seeing this, all living beings weep;
Narahari's heart finds no rest.

[^5]: GPT1 — p. 268, no. 63 (varadi or dhanashi); GPT2 — sec. 4.2, no. 63 (catalogue: НГ-149). BR — 12th wave (ed. 1912, p. 904; ed.
1960, vv. 2795–2800), at the same place, the house of Sanjaya; after the pada — "under this tree the Lord stayed a while and went on
by the road to the Ganga". Not in PK, KGC, PS or the Gita-candrodaya. B.
[^6]: Marginal variants of GPT1: "purifier of the world" instead of "enchanter of the world", "of all living beings" instead of "of
all people". These are the readings of the second occurrence of the same pada in the BR: Narahari Chakravarti quotes it twice — at the
house of Sanjaya (vv. 2795–2800) and at the house of Srivasa (ed. 1960, v. 3397 ff., "ভুবনপাবন গোরাচাঁদ। অখিল জীবের মন-ফাঁদ").

## 3. Narahari — "nācata gaurakiśora. suradhunītīre ujora…" (GPT1 p. 268, no. 65; BR)
*Dancing on the bank of the Ganga: young Gaura with hundreds of associates, making no distinction between His own and others, gives
away the treasure of prema, weeps in a peerless bhava and, speaking in a faltering voice, takes Gadadhara by the hand. Raga dhanashi.
**Attribution: probably Chakravarti** — the pada is in the BR (12th wave); short payar with Vrajabuli forms.*[^7]

**3.1.** Young Gaura dances,
illumining the bank of the Ganga.

**3.2.** With Him are hundreds of associates;
in the kirtana His movements are peerless.[^8]

**3.3.** Making no distinction between His own and others,
He gives away the treasure of prema.

**3.4.** Immersed in a peerless bhava,
He sheds tears from His reddened eyes.

**3.5.** Uttering so many words in a faltering voice,
He takes Gadadhara by the hand.[^9]

**3.6.** Again and again His body trembles —
what can Narahari grasp of this play?

[^7]: GPT1 — p. 268, no. 65 (dhanashi); GPT2 — sec. 4.2, no. 65 (catalogue: НГ-250). BR — 12th wave (ed. 1912, p. 912; ed. 1960,
vv. 2889–2894): Ishana shows the bank of the Ganga where the Lord danced with His associates and quotes several padas in a row
(including the next one, no. 4); after them — "having spread the good fortune of the Ganga, the Lord went home this way with His
associates". Not in PK, KGC, PS. The Gita-candrodaya (ed. 1948, no. 30) has a pada with the same opening ("হেরনু গৌরকিশোর।
সুরধনীতীরে উজোর"), but it is a different song — signed by Kavishekhara Raya and with a different text after the first couplet; the
common opening is probably a current formula. B.
[^8]: The BR reads: "in the kirtana — peerless play" (রঙ্গ instead of অঙ্গ).
[^9]: Gadadhara Pandita is Radha in Krishna-lila (cf. portion 17, note 20): in the dance the Lord turns to him in the bhava of
Krishna-lila. This is the prema of Vraja, not worldly attachment.

## 4. Narahari Dasa — "gaura suradhunītīre nācata…" (GPT1 p. 268, no. 66; BR)
*Dancing on the bank of the Ganga: Gaura's body takes away the pride of a golden mountain, His locks are adorned with flowers, at the
sight of His arms and breast Kama loses his peace; He walks with the gait of a king of elephants, from His lotus eyes flow as it were
two Gangas, the earth rejoices at the rhythm of His feet. Raga gauri. **Attribution: probably Chakravarti** — the pada is in the BR
(12th wave); Vrajabuli with chains of assonance.*[^10]

**4.1.** Gaura dances on the bank of the Ganga with His skilful associates;
His body, full of supreme sweetness, takes away the pride of a golden mountain.

**4.2.** His peerless locks are adorned with ketaki, kunda jasmine and bright flowers;
seeing the curve of His arms and His broad chest, Ananga loses his peace.[^11]

**4.3.** Seized by bhava, He steps, putting to shame the king of elephants, and thunders unceasingly;
tears roll from His lotus eyes — as if two Gangas had appeared.

**4.4.** When His unsteady feet keep the rhythm, the earth rejoices beyond measure;
Narahari Dasa cries: "Glory, glory!" — what can be said of this play?

[^10]: GPT1 — p. 268, no. 66 (gauri, গৌরড়ী); GPT2 — sec. 4.2, no. 66 (catalogue: НГ-251). BR — 12th wave (ed. 1912, p. 912; ed. 1960,
vv. 2895–2898), right after the preceding pada, at the Ganga. The GPT1 recognition of this pada is faulty; the readings are restored
from GPT2 and the BR (see `bn/18.md`). Not in PK, KGC, PS or the Gita-candrodaya. B.
[^11]: Ananga ("the Bodiless") is Kama, the god of love: the Lord's beauty surpasses Kama's (a usual image of the gaura-padas). Ketaki
and kunda are flowers.

## 5. Narahari Dasa — "bali-kalidamana śamanabhayabhañjana…" (GPT1 p. 269, no. 67; BR)
*The son of Shachi dancing in the midst of the kirtana: subduer of Kali and of the fear of death, skilful giver of prema, more
beautiful than a golden mountain; at the rhythm of His feet the earth sways, He spreads His arms, smiles, and seeing Gadadhara's face
loses His composure; His eyes reveal the former play of Vraja. Raga belavali. **Attribution: probably Chakravarti** — the pada is in
the BR (12th wave); Vrajabuli.*[^12]

**5.1.** Subduer of mighty Kali, dispeller of the fear of Yama, giver of joy to the people of all the worlds,
skilled in giving away the rare treasure of prema, taking away the pride of all the wish-fulfilling trees —[^13]

**5.2.** *(Refrain)* the son of Shachi dances in the midst of the kirtana;
His body, lovelier than a golden mountain, shines like a new lord of Manmathas.

**5.3.** At the rhythm of His feet the earth sways; gracefully bending, He spreads His arms;
smiling gently, His lips tremble — and seeing Gadadhara's face, He loses His composure.

**5.4.** His lotus eyes sway, turning again and again, revealing the peerless former play;
the skilful associates rejoice — only Narahari Dasa is deprived of this rasa.[^14]

[^12]: GPT1 — p. 269, no. 67 (belavali); GPT2 — sec. 4.2, no. 67 (catalogue: НГ-150). BR — 12th wave (ed. 1912, p. 921; ed. 1960,
vv. 3018–3021): "here Gaurachandra, with joy in His heart, danced in sankirtana in a wondrous bhava". Not in PK, KGC, PS or the
Gita-candrodaya. B.
[^13]: "Subduer of mighty Kali" — the Lord who conquers the age of Kali by sankirtana; "the fear of Yama" — the fear of death and its
consequences. The wish-fulfilling tree (sura-taru, kalpa-vriksha) grants only what is asked for, but the Lord gives prema beyond asking.
[^14]: "The former play" (পূরব রঙ্গ) is Krishna's lila in Vraja, which the Lord remembers in the dance. Gadadhara is Radha in
Krishna-lila (note 9).

## 6. Narahari — "āju gorā nagarakīrtane…" (GPT1 p. 269, no. 68; BR)
*The night-time city kirtana: adorned Gora walks with His associates, dances, pours down a rain of prema; gods disguised as men shower
flowers, the townspeople run and sing, fools hearing the lion's roar expect disaster; hundreds of thousands of lamps are burning, the
whole world is intoxicated. Raga kamoda. **Attribution: probably Chakravarti** — the pada is in the BR (12th wave, the account of the
night kirtana); Bengali payar.*[^15]

**6.1.** Today Gora is in the city kirtana:
having dressed Himself, He goes with His dear associates.

**6.2.** Beautifully shines the attire on His body;
He dances in every manner, charming the hearts of the world.

**6.3.** Ceaselessly He pours down a rain of prema;
in the midst of Nadia flows a river of bliss.

**6.4.** The gods, mingling with men,
shower flowers with joy in their hearts.[^16]

**6.5.** All the townspeople run;
in the depths of their hearts they sing the virtues of Gora-chand.

**6.6.** Fools, hearing the lion's roar,
grow sullen and expect disaster.[^17]

**6.7.** Hundreds of thousands of lamps are burning —
what comparison is there? Earth and sky are lit up.

**6.8.** What does Narahari know how to tell?
The world is intoxicated — no one can keep his composure.

[^15]: GPT1 — p. 269, no. 68 (kamoda); GPT2 — sec. 4.2, no. 68 (catalogue: НГ-151). BR — 12th wave (ed. 1912, p. 927; ed. 1960,
vv. 3104–3111): Ishana tells of the night kirtana, when the Lord went through the streets of Navadvipa by the light of hundreds of
thousands of lamps, while "the foolish pashandis took it for a calamity" (v. 3103). Not in PK, KGC, PS or the Gita-candrodaya. B. The
night procession with kirtana through Navadvipa (to the Kazi's house) — CB Madhya 23; CC Adi 17.
[^16]: According to the CB (Madhya 23), gods who had taken human form were also in the procession.
[^17]: "The lion's roar" (সিংহনাদ) is the loud singing of Hari's names. "Fools" are the opponents of kirtana (pashandis), who expected
the noise to bring down the ruler's wrath.

## 7. Narahari — "śacīra dulāla gorā nāce…" (GPT1 pp. 269–270, no. 69; BR)
*The dance of the son of Shachi: Gora offers to anyone the treasure unattainable for the gods; seeing the fallen, He cannot restrain
Himself, waves of bhava rise in Him; His face and breast are bathed in tears; devotees intoxicated with prema circle around, no one can
keep his composure. Raga kamoda. **Attribution: probably Chakravarti** — the pada is in the BR (12th wave); Bengali payar.*[^18]

**7.1.** *(Refrain)* Gora, the darling son of Shachi, dances:
the treasure unattainable for the gods He offers to whoever comes.

**7.2.** Seeing the fallen, He cannot restrain Himself;
again and again waves of bhava rise in Him.

**7.3.** A radiance surpassing gold flashes;
how beautiful He is, covered with abundant thrills!

**7.4.** His holy face and breast are bathed in tears;
raising both arms, He cries again and again: "Hari!"

**7.5.** All around move devotees intoxicated with prema;
cries of "Glory, glory!" fill earth and sky.

**7.6.** Looking at the Lord, no one can keep his composure;
Narahari, fallen at His reddish feet, weeps.

[^18]: GPT1 — pp. 269–270, no. 69 (kamoda); GPT2 — sec. 4.2, no. 69 (catalogue: НГ-152). BR — 12th wave (ed. 1912, p. 948; ed. 1960,
vv. 3381–3386): at the house of Srivasa — "how Gaura plays in Navadvipa even Brahma cannot fathom; the fortunate will see the
dances, kirtana, bhavas and mercy of this place" (vv. 3379–3380). Not in PK, KGC, PS or the Gita-candrodaya. B.

## 8. Narahari — "nāce gorā guṇamaṇi…" (GPT1 p. 270, no. 70; BR)
*The dance of Gora, mine of prema, among His associates — like a golden moon among the stars; His body more tender than a shirisha
flower, a smile on His red lips; seeing the fallen, He weeps, and as it were the Ganga flows from His eyes; the khol and karatalas
sound, all are intoxicated — only the poet is left out. Raga kamoda. **Attribution: probably Chakravarti** — the pada is in the BR
(12th wave); Bengali tripadi.*[^19]

**8.1.** Gora dances, the jewel of virtues, a very mine of prema, His dear associates around Him;
a wondrous beauty — as if a golden moon had risen among the stars.

**8.2.** His body, more tender than a shirisha flower, is lovely, covered with thrills;
the full-blown lotus is left far behind, Madana weeps before His face; a smile is on His red lips.[^20]

**8.3.** Striking so many poses, He raises His arms and cries "Hari!" — ceaselessly He rains nectar;
His heart is full of compassion: seeing the fallen He weeps, and from His eyes flows a stream like the Ganga.

**8.4.** The khol and karatalas sound, lovely is His gait — who, seeing this, would not be enchanted?
No grief or sorrow remains, all the people are intoxicated — only Narahari is deprived of this joy.

[^19]: GPT1 — p. 270, no. 70 (kamoda); GPT2 — sec. 4.2, no. 70 (catalogue: НГ-252). BR — 12th wave (ed. 1912, p. 949; ed. 1960,
vv. 3387–3390), right after the preceding pada, at the house of Srivasa. Not in PK, KGC, PS or the Gita-candrodaya. B.
[^20]: The shirisha is a tree with very delicate flowers (an image of tenderness). Madana (Kama) "weeps" seeing that the Lord's face is
more beautiful than his own.

## 9. Narahari — "nācata naṭavara gaurakiśora…" (GPT1 p. 270, no. 72; BR)
*Young Gaura, the best of dancers, enchants the world with new movements: the radiance of His body is peerless, a garland of forest
flowers sways on His neck, His moon-face pours nectar which the chakora-devotees drink; He sings the names of Hari, and all weep — only
the poet's heart does not melt. Raga suhai. **Attribution: probably Chakravarti** — the pada is in the BR (12th wave); Vrajabuli.*[^21]

**9.1.** Young Gaura dances, the best of dancers;
with new movements He sends the world into rapture.

**9.2.** The peerless radiance of His body flashes;
seeing it, countless Kamas swoon.

**9.3.** His large eyes sway;
on His neck swings a garland of forest flowers.

**9.4.** His radiant moon-face pours down nectar —
the chakora-devotees drink it to the full with their eyes.[^22]

**9.5.** Again and again He utters the sweet name of Hari —
who, hearing it, does not weep without end?

**9.6.** The sinful and the fallen are intoxicated with the rasa of prema —
only Narahari's hard breast does not melt.

[^21]: GPT1 — p. 270, no. 72 (suhai); GPT2 — sec. 4.2, no. 72. The pada was not in the catalogue — **new row НГ-254** (the signature
sweep had matched it to another pada). BR — 12th wave (ed. 1912, p. 952; ed. 1960, vv. 3430–3435), right after a pada of Vasu
Ramananda (GPT1 no. 73), before the account of Advaita's dance in the house of Srivasa. The GPT2 index assigns the pages of this
place to Chakravarti. Not in PK (PK 2065 "nācata naṭavara gaura kiśora" is a different pada, by Balarama Dasa), KGC, PS or the
Gita-candrodaya. B.
[^22]: The chakora is a bird which, by poetic tradition, feeds on moonlight: the devotees "drink with their eyes" the beauty of the
Lord's face.

## 10. Narahari — "prabhu viśvambhara priya parikara…" (GPT1 p. 283, no. 21; BR)
*The Lord's dream of Balarama: Vishvambhara tells His associates that at night He saw a beautiful chariot with a palm-tree banner arrive
here; a tall man with an earring in one ear, a pitcher in his hand, dressed in blue and with a blue cloth on his head, swaying, looked
around and asked for Him by name again and again — Haladhara must have come; having said this, the Lord was absorbed in the bhava of
Balarama. Raga kamoda. **Attribution: probably Chakravarti** — the pada is in the BR (12th wave) and is set in the account of
Nityananda's arrival in Navadvipa; Bengali tripadi.*[^23]

**10.1.** Lord Vishvambhara says to His dear associates: "Listen to what I saw in a dream.
What a wondrously made, beautifully adorned chariot with a palm-tree banner has come here!

**10.2.** I saw a handsome man of tall stature — what can He be compared with?
In one ear an earring, and what a neck! His moon-face enchants the world.

**10.3.** A dark pitcher in His hand, a blue cloth on His head, and He was fittingly dressed in blue;
He looked about on all sides and walked swaying — who, seeing that gait, could keep his composure?

**10.4.** Calling My name, He asked for Me again and again — Haladhara must have come."
Having said this, Narahari's Lord was absorbed in the bhava of Balarama.[^24]

[^23]: GPT1 — p. 283, sec. 4.3 (bhavavesha), no. 21 (kamoda); GPT2 — sec. 4.3, no. 21 (catalogue: НГ-253). BR — 12th wave (ed. 1912,
p. 826; ed. 1960, vv. 1731–1734): Nityananda came to Navadvipa and stayed with Nandana Acharya; the Lord, knowing this, in the temple of
Vishnu where He worshipped Vishnu and where the Vaishnavas gathered, "told here the dream of that night"; after the pada Srivasa and
others, seeing the Lord in this bhava, offer prayers, and Haridasa and Srivasa are sent to search for Nityananda (vv. 1735–1738). The
GPT2 index assigns this page to Chakravarti. Not in PK, KGC, PS or the Gita-candrodaya. B. The dream and the bhava of Balarama — CB
Madhya 3; cf. Murari 2.14 (the bhava of Balarama).
[^24]: The palm-tree banner (tala-dhvaja), blue garments, an earring in one ear are marks of Balarama (Haladhara, "Bearer of the
plough"); the one who has come is Nityananda, the Balarama of Gaura-lila, whom the Lord recognises before meeting him. "Narahari's Lord"
(নরহরি-প্রভু) is the Lord whom the poet serves (cf. portion 17, note 44). The bhava of Balarama is one of the bhavas the Lord revealed
in Navadvipa: a manifestation of His own fullness, not a transformation into another.

## 11. Narahari — "pālaṅka upare gaurāṅga sundara…" (GPT1 p. 306, no. 23)
*The Lord in the bhava of Radha waiting for Krishna (vasaka-sajja): beautiful Gauranga sits sadly on the couch; He has decked the bed
with flowers because Shyama promised to come, but the night is almost over — where is the dark moon? Kala has not come — how much longer
to bear the burning of love? "I will break off love with cruel Shyama!" Raga dhanashi. **Attribution: not established** — the pada is
not in the BR; the GPT2 index assigns the page to "Narahari Dasa" (undistinguished); simple Bengali tripadi, like the neighbouring pada
of Sarakara PK 307 (GPT1 no. 22), which it follows in content.*[^25]

**11.1.** On the couch beautiful Gauranga sits in sadness:
His heart is seized by the bhava of Radha — He shows the state of vasaka-sajja.[^26]

**11.2.** He says: "My dark friend said He would come — and I decked the bed with flowers;
the night is almost gone — where is He, the dark moon? The night has passed in vain.

**11.3.** Kala has not come — how much longer will the heart bear the burning of love?"
Narahari conveys: "I will break off love with that cruel Shyama!"[^27]

[^25]: GPT1 — p. 306, sec. 4.5 (abhisara, rasodgara, utkanthita), no. 23 (dhanashi); GPT2 — sec. 4.5, no. 23 (the catalogue did not know
this pada — **new row НГ-255**; the signature sweep had matched it to another pada). Before it (no. 22) stands Narahari Sarakara's pada
"kī lāgiyā mora gaurasundara basiyā gṛhera mājhe" (PK 307, portion 03, no. 3) on the same theme. Not in BR, PK, KGC, PS or the
Gita-candrodaya. C.
[^26]: Vasaka-sajja is the heroine who has adorned herself and the bed and waits for her beloved (one of the eight states of the heroine
in alankara-shastra; Rupa Gosvami, Ujjvala-nilamani). The Lord, Krishna Himself, appeared in order to taste Radha's love for Him (CC Adi
4), and lives through Her waiting and separation. This is pure prema, not worldly passion: Radha's bhava is the highest manifestation of
the hladini-shakti.
[^27]: Anger at Krishna (mana) is one of the expressions of Radha's prema, in which love only grows stronger; the words "I will break off
love" are not a cooling but the voice of wounded love. PK 307 has a similar ending (portion 03, no. 3). The pronoun "I" is the Lord's
speech in Radha's bhava, which the poet conveys.

## 12. Narahari — "jhulata rasamaya gaurakiśora…" (GPT1 p. 324, no. 10; BR)
*Jhulana: on the bank of the Ganga, under a lofty tree, a peerless swing has been made; the skilful associates gently swing young
Gaura, sing and play; the people of Nadia leave their homes and run to see His smiling face, the gods shower flowers, and Narahari
cannot count the virtues of the Lord of his life. Raga mallara or imana. **Attribution: probably Chakravarti** — the pada is in the BR
(12th wave, the beginning of the account of jhulana); Vrajabuli.*[^28]

**12.1.** *(Refrain)* Young Gaura, full of rasa, is swinging;
on the bank of the Ganga, under a lofty tree, a peerless, charming swing has been made.

**12.2.** The skilful associates gently swing Him and sing, rapt in the rasa of the melodies;
they utter a sweet "dhik-dhik-dhini" and play on so many sweet-sounding instruments.[^29]

**12.3.** Crowds of the men and women of Nadia leave their homes and come, unable to restrain themselves;
their eyes, unblinking, gaze at His moon-face lit with a smile.

**12.4.** The gods in the sky, immersed in bliss, with their companions shower flowers and cry "Glory!";
Narahari, intoxicated by the virtues of the Lord of his life, speaks of them without end — and cannot count them.[^30]

## 28. Narahari — "mari mari ogo nadīyā mājhe…" (GPT1 p. 494, no. 30)

**28.4.** All the beasts and birds, all the insects and moths are like gold;
