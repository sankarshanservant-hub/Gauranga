# Appendix. Padas signed "Narahari" from the Gaura-pada-tarangini — III: the childhood pastimes of Nimai

*Continuation of the appendix (the introductory note on the two Naraharis, on the arguments for attribution and on the marks in the
headings is in ch. 17). Chapter 19: padas on the Lord's childhood from the "Balya-lila" section of the GPT (2.2). First come three
padas which Narahari Chakravarti quotes in the Bhakti-ratnakara while showing the house of Jagannatha Mishra (the first feeding with
rice, Nimai in His father's arms, the first steps); then a large cycle in which the old men and elderly women of Navadvipa, coming at
dawn to the house of Shachi, gaze at Gaura, bless Him and recall His childhood pranks. Almost the whole of this cycle is found in the
Gaura-charita-chintamani of Narahari Chakravarti (chapters 5 and 6 of the "morning pastimes": the love of the old men and the love of the
women in vatsalya) — a strong argument for Chakravarti; two padas (nos. 24 and 27) have not been found in any witness. The index of poets of GPT2
assigns the padas of the section to Chakravarti (those found in the BR) or to an undistinguished "Narahari Dasa" (the GCC padas); none to Sarakara. According to
Majumdar, the padas on the birth and childhood of Nimai are late: Narahari Sarakara, who lived in Shrikhanda, did not see the Lord's
childhood, and the "Narahari" padas known only from the GPT are unreliable for attribution. Before translation the "Narahari" signatures
in sections 2.1–2.2 were checked throughout in GPT1 (tesseract recognition): the catalogue had missed 14 padas of this section (13 of
them in this chapter). The base text is GPT1 (doubtful places from the scan), collated with GPT2, the BR (eds. 1912 and 1960) and the
Gaura-charita-chintamani (ed. 1947); PK, KGC, PS and the Gita-chandrodaya have been checked. The childhood games of the Lord are the lila
of Bhagavan, not an ordinary childhood (note 2). The notes of the GPT editors are not reproduced.*

## 1. Narahari — "nadīyāra nārī puruṣa…" (GPT1 p. 65, no. 10; BR)
*The first feeding with rice: the men and women of Nadia gather with gifts; the Child sits on Nilambara's lap in new ornaments;
tasting grains of sweet rice, He smiles; all praise Shachi and Jagannatha. Raga kamoda. **Attribution: probably Chakravarti** — the
pada is in the BR (12th wave); tripadi with chains of chiming sounds.*[^1]

**1.1.** The men and women of Nadia, counting it the reward of their pious deeds, in great joy
gather for Nimai's first feeding with rice — each bringing various gifts.[^2]

**1.2.** They gaze their fill at the beauty of Shachi's son on the lap of fortunate Nilambara:[^3]
all in ever-new ornaments, a silk cloth about His hips, the hem of His garment swaying.

**1.3.** His body surpasses a golden lotus, and His face — what can compare? — the splendour of the moon;
how wondrously, tasting grains of sweet rice, He flashes a gentle smile!

**1.4.** At such a festival who can keep his composure? The joy cannot be put into words.
All praise Shachi and Jagannatha — Narahari's heart overflows with happiness.

[^1]: GPT1 — p. 65, section 2.2 ("Balya-lila"), no. 10 (kamoda; checked against the scan, PDF p. 324); GPT2 — section 2.2, no. 10. The
catalogue did not know it (new row NG-262). BR — 12th wave (ed. 1912, p. 768; ed. 1960, vv. 956–959): Ishana, showing Srinivasa the
house of Jagannatha Mishra, says that here at six months the Lord was given His first rice and named, and quotes the pada. Not in PK,
KGC, PS, the Gita-chandrodaya or the Gaura-charita-chintamani. B. The first feeding with rice (annaprashana) — CB Adi 4; CC Adi 14;
Murari 1.6.
[^2]: The childhood games of the Lord are not an ordinary childhood but the lila of Bhagavan (balya-lila): by His own will He appears
as an infant and a child for the sake of His eternal associates who love Him in vatsalya (parental love). Shachi and Jagannatha Mishra
are His eternal parents (in Krishna-lila, Yashoda and Nanda, CC Adi 13); their love, like the love of the people of Navadvipa, is pure
prema, not worldly attachment. So the whole Gaudiya tradition (Vrindavana Dasa, Krishnadasa Kaviraja, Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati)
understands Gaura's childhood games, and so they are to be understood in all the padas of this chapter.
[^3]: Nilambara Chakravarti — the Lord's maternal grandfather (CC Adi 13).

## 2. Narahari — "jagannātha miśra mahāsukhe…" (GPT1 p. 65, no. 11; BR)
*Jagannatha Mishra fondles his son; Nimai climbs down from his lap and crawls about the courtyard, looking back; Shachi calls Him into
her arms — "dust will stick to You"; He climbs up to her, smiling. Raga tudi. **Attribution: probably Chakravarti** — the pada is in
the BR (12th wave).*[^4]

**2.1.** Jagannatha Mishra in great happiness
takes his son on his lap and kisses the moonlike face.

**2.2.** The father adorns the hair on His little head —
running his fingers through it, his heart overflows with love.

**2.3.** From His father's lap Nimai
gracefully gets down to roam about the courtyard.

**2.4.** On all fours He crawls about the courtyard;
golden nupuras ring on His lovely feet.[^5]

**2.5.** As He crawls, He looks back;
Mishra stands watching the sweetness of His movements.

**2.6.** His mother comes before Him and says:
"Come up into my arms, darling — dust is sticking to Your body."

**2.7.** Putting His little hand in His mother's hand,
He climbs onto her lap, smiling and smiling.

**2.8.** His little teeth shine like drops of milk;
when they show in His smile — who can keep his composure?

**2.9.** Whomever He looks at with both eyes,
him He immerses for ever in an ocean of happiness.

**2.10.** Beautifully He shines on His mother's lap;
Narahari offers himself to the Enchanter of the world's heart.[^6]

[^4]: GPT1 — p. 65, no. 11 (tudi; scan, PDF p. 324); GPT2 — section 2.2, no. 11 (catalogue: NG-006). BR — 12th wave (ed. 1912, p. 769;
ed. 1960, vv. 970–979): Ishana shows the courtyard where the Lord crawled on His knees (jānu-caṃkramaṇa), quotes the pada of Vasu Ghosha
"ek mukhe ki kahiba" (GPT no. 2, chapter 12) and after it this one. Not in PK, KGC, PS, the Gita-chandrodaya or the
Gaura-charita-chintamani. B. Cf. Murari 1.6; CC Adi 14.
[^5]: Nupuras — ankle bells.
[^6]: "Offers himself" (nichani) — Bengali nichhani: the rite of waving something around the beloved and offering oneself for his welfare
(cf. chapter 17, note 34). The signature of this cycle is usually "Narahari offers himself…" or "Narahari's life is Nimai".

## 3. Narahari — "śacī ṭhākurāṇī cāru chām̐de…" (GPT1 p. 65, no. 12; BR)
*The first steps: Shachi teaches Gora to walk, holding out her finger; He stands up, takes two or four steps, lets go of the finger
and falls; His mother takes Him in her arms and nurses Him. Raga tudi. **Attribution: probably Chakravarti** — the pada is in the BR
(12th wave).*[^7]

**3.1.** Shachi Thakurani tenderly
teaches Gorachand to walk.

**3.2.** Very gently she says, smiling:
"Come, take hold of my finger."

**3.3.** Hearing this, the Moon of Nadia joyfully
takes hold of His mother's finger with a smile.

**3.4.** Slowly, slowly He rises and stands up,
and takes two or four steps.

**3.5.** Letting go of the finger, He falls to the ground;
Shachi takes Him in her arms and kisses His little face.

**3.6.** Climbing onto her lap, He swings His little feet,
and the nupuras ring on His reddish soles.

**3.7.** Fingering with His little hand, He drinks at the breast —
there is no comparison for it.

**3.8.** He looks about on all sides with charming gestures —
Narahari offers himself for this.

[^7]: GPT1 — p. 65, no. 12 (tudi; scan, PDF p. 324); GPT2 — section 2.2, no. 12. The catalogue did not know it (new row NG-263). BR — 12th
wave (ed. 1912, pp. 770–771; ed. 1960, vv. 990–997, "todi"): Ishana says that here in the courtyard the Lord took His first steps,
quotes a verse from Murari's Shri-Krishna-Chaitanya-carita (part 1: "walking with His reddish feet, He took from the earth the fever
of separation") and the pada. Not in PK, KGC, PS, the Gita-chandrodaya or the Gaura-charita-chintamani. B. The first steps — Murari 1.6;
CB Adi 4; cf. the pada of Vasu Ghosha "māyera aṅguli dhari" (chapter 12).

## 4. Narahari — "gaura-vadana sukha-sadana sudhāmaya…" (GPT1 p. 66, no. 15; GCC)
*The old men of Navadvipa gaze at Gaura's face: each offers himself for Him; trembling, in tears, leaning on one another, they say:
who created Mishra's son, who has stolen our hearing, mind and eyes? A moment without Him is like a kalpa; people tease us as madmen, we
have forgotten all order in eating and sleeping, home has grown hateful, we have no love for our own sons. Yatharaga. **Attribution:
probably Chakravarti** — the pada is in his Gaura-charita-chintamani; Vrajabuli with chains of chiming sounds.*[^8]

**4.1.** Again and again the old men gaze at the face of Gaura — the abode of happiness, full of nectar;
counting many of their births crowned with fruit, each again and again offers his whole self for Him.

**4.2.** They totter, their eyes brimming with tears, their bodies covered with abundant thrills;
leaning on another's hand, someone speaks soft, sweet words:

**4.3.** "Tell me, who fashioned Mishra's son, who has stolen my hearing, my mind and my eyes?
A moment when I do not see Him is like a kalpa; I am thrown into turmoil, and not a drop of steadiness remains.[^9]

**4.4.** It is boundless happiness to follow Him everywhere; for this everyone laughs at us, calling us madmen;
but as soon as those words reach our ears, the heart rejoices all the more.

**4.5.** Eating, walking, sleeping, speaking — all order is forgotten, everything goes topsy-turvy;
the household routine is nothing but a burden, and we have no love for our own sons."[^10]

**4.6.** Having spoken thus, they fall silent, absorbed within, and again cherish desires in their hearts.
How can I, Narahari Dasa, the crest-jewel of fools, describe their overflowing supreme love?

[^8]: GPT1 — pp. 66–67, no. 15 (yatharaga; scan, PDF pp. 325–326); GPT2 — section 2.2, no. 15 (catalogue: NG-007). Gaura-charita-chintamani
(GCC) — chapter 5 ("morning pastimes": the love of the old men), pada 8, the last song of the chapter. In the GCC this whole cycle is
framed by Gaura's morning in Navadvipa: at dawn He sits in the courtyard of Shachi's house among His associates, and the people of the
town gather to gaze at Him; therefore many padas of the cycle speak of Gaura no longer as an infant, and the childhood pranks in them are
recollections. Not in the BR, PK, KGC, PS or the Gita-chandrodaya. B. Cf. the love of the people of Navadvipa for Nimai — CB Adi 6–7.
[^9]: A kalpa is a day of Brahma, an immeasurably long time; cf. the gopis' complaint about the blinking of the eyelids that hinders
them from seeing Krishna (Bhag. 10.31.15).
[^10]: Cf. Parikshit's question why the people of Vraja loved Krishna more than their own sons (Bhag. 10.14.49–57) — Krishna is the Soul
of all souls. Chakravarti transfers this to the people of Navadvipa.

## 5. Narahari — "rajanī prabhāta teji nija gṛha…" (GPT1 p. 67, no. 16; GCC)
*At dawn the old men, leaning on their sticks, come to Shachi's courtyard and cannot take their eyes off Gaura; each prays for Him: to
the Creator for long life, to Vishnu for grace and universal love, to Shiva for fame and freedom from misfortune, to Sarasvati for
invincible learning, to Bhagavati that they may always see Nimai. Raga vibhasa. **Attribution: probably Chakravarti** — the pada is in
the GCC; Vrajabuli.*[^11]

**5.1.** At the end of the night, leaving their homes, the venerable old men
all come to the lovely courtyard of Shachi, and in their hearts joy upon joy arises.

**5.2.** They stand leaning on their sticks and again and again gaze at Gaura's body,
as if after long days, with great toil, they had found a jewel for their hearts.

**5.3.** One, overcome by love, says to the Creator: "Fulfil all His wishes;
may He live longer than I, may He, the beautiful one, always live in this town of Nadia."[^12]

**5.4.** Another, with joined palms, says to Vishnu: "Cast a gracious glance on the son of Mishra;
may He be a stranger to no one, may all love Him and ceaselessly sing His virtues."

**5.5.** Another says to the Lord of Kailasa: "Increase the radiance of each of His limbs;
may His fame fill the world, may there be plenty; ward off the hosts of invincible misfortunes."

**5.6.** Another says to Sarasvati: "Make Him a pandit, invincible, so that He may never know defeat";
another says to Bhagavati: "May we always see Nimai — the life of Narahari."

[^11]: GPT1 — p. 67, no. 16 (vibhasa; scan, PDF p. 326); GPT2 — section 2.2, no. 16 (catalogue: NG-008). GCC — chapter 5, pada 6; in the
GCC it is followed by a short stanza (rasika-chandas) with the same signature — "thus all the old men ceaselessly bless Him…" — which is
not in the GPT. Not in the BR, PK, KGC, PS or the Gita-chandrodaya. B.
[^12]: The old men pray to Brahma (the Creator), Vishnu, Shiva (the Lord of Kailasa), Sarasvati and Bhagavati (the goddess Durga) for
Gaura's welfare — this is how vatsalya, parental love, shows itself: in it the devotee forgets the Lord's majesty and, seeing Him as in
need of protection, entreats all the gods on His behalf (cf. the cowherds of Vraja and Yashoda entrusting Krishna to the protection of
the gods, Bhag. 10.6, 10.7). The sweetness (madhurya) of this love, by the will of yogamaya, hides from them the majesty (aishvarya) of
the Lord; this is not independent worship of the demigods, and the Lord, of course, needs no one's protection. Bhagavati here is an
epithet of the goddess, not of Bhagavan.

## 6. Narahari — "gaura-sneha-bhare gara gara gāta…" (GPT1 p. 67, no. 17; GCC)
*At the end of the night the old men, overflowing with love for Gaura, tell their households about Him; unable to live without His
darshan, they gather all their strength, rise from their beds and, trembling, sticks in hand, go to Him. Raga dhanashi. **Attribution:
probably Chakravarti** — the pada is in the GCC; Vrajabuli.*[^13]

**6.1.** Their bodies overflowing with love for Gaura,
the old men rejoice at the end of the night.

**6.2.** Each told his household everything in detail;
hearing it all, they rejoice without end.

**6.3.** Unable to stay without the darshan of Gaura,
they left their beds, gathering all their strength.

**6.4.** Sticks in hand, bodies trembling —
Narahari watches this matchless spectacle.

[^13]: GPT1 — p. 67, no. 17 (dhanashi; scan, PDF p. 326); GPT2 — section 2.2, no. 17 (catalogue: NG-009). GCC — chapter 5, pada 5
(yatharaga). GPT1 has "tejala śeṣe" ("left… at last"), the GCC "tejala śeja" ("left their beds"); the GCC reading is adopted. Not in the BR,
PK, KGC, PS or the Gita-chandrodaya. B.

## 7. Narahari — "śuna mora vāṇī nā jāni ki have…" (GPT1 p. 67, no. 18; GCC)
*An old man says to his household: "I have grown utterly decrepit, and Nimai is our life and all our wealth; I always want to see Him,
but have no strength to walk. Worship the gods, give gifts to brahmanas, send Him presents that please Him; go with Narahari to Shachi,
teach her auspicious rites and make Nimai swear to give up His obstinacy." Raga suhai. **Attribution: probably Chakravarti** — the pada
is in the GCC.*[^14]

**7.1.** "Listen to what I say: I do not know what will be — I have grown utterly decrepit.
Our treasure of life, all our wealth, is Nimai, the solace of the soul.

**7.2.** *(Refrain)* Ah, I always long to see Him!
But I have no strength to walk — that is my grief: Providence has turned against me.

**7.3.** Worship the gods, give gifts to the brahmanas, always think of His welfare;
carefully send Him various presents — whatever pleases Him.

**7.4.** Go with Narahari and teach Shachi auspicious rites;
let Nimai swear to give up His dreadful obstinacy."[^15]

[^14]: GPT1 — p. 67, no. 18 (suhai; scan, PDF p. 326); GPT2 — section 2.2, no. 18. The catalogue did not know it (new row NG-264). GCC —
chapter 5, pada 4 (yatharaga). Not in the BR, PK, KGC, PS or the Gita-chandrodaya. B.
[^15]: The poet introduces himself into the lila as one of the people of Navadvipa ("go with Narahari") — a frequent device of
Chakravarti in the GCC. "Obstinacy" (ām̐khuṭi) — Nimai's childish whims, of which the following padas tell (cf. CB Adi 6: the neighbours'
complaints to Jagannatha Mishra).

## 8. Narahari — "niśi parabhāta samaye yerūpa ānanda śacīra ghare…" (GPT1 p. 68, no. 19; GCC)
*The joy in Shachi's house at dawn cannot be told even by a thousand mouths through hundreds of yugas: Gaurachand, who gives happiness
to His own, has spread in the courtyard a net that enchants the world; people leave their beds, come running, drown in the ocean of His
beauty and cannot go home. Raga vibhasa. **Attribution: probably Chakravarti** — the pada is in the GCC.*[^16]

**8.1.** What joy there is in Shachi's house at the end of the night —
not even in hundreds of yugas could a thousand mouths describe a fraction of it![^17]

**8.2.** How many games Gaurachand knows to give happiness to His own!
It seems that in the middle of the courtyard He has spread a net that enchants the world.

**8.3.** Leaving their beds, running and running, everyone gathers, rejoicing;
drowned in the ocean of that beauty, they can no longer return home.

**8.4.** A wondrous love ceaselessly arises in everyone's heart;
they give body and mind to those reddish feet, says Narahari Dasa.

[^16]: GPT1 — p. 68, no. 19 (vibhasa; scan, PDF p. 327); GPT2 — section 2.2, no. 19. The catalogue did not know it (new row NG-265). GCC —
the first pada of chapter 7, which is devoted to the townswomen of Navadvipa (nāgarī) seized by love for Gaura; the pada itself speaks
only of the joy of all who come at dawn to Shachi's house, without the nagari motif, and in the GPT it stands in the section on
childhood — therefore it is translated here. In Chakravarti's frame it belongs to the motif of the townswomen (cf. chapter 17, note 15):
worship of Gaura in the spirit of "gaura-nagari" is not accepted by the Gaudiya tradition (Vrindavana Dasa, Krishnadasa Kaviraja,
Bhaktisiddhanta). Not in the BR, PK, KGC, PS or the Gita-chandrodaya. B.
[^17]: "A thousand mouths" — an allusion to the thousand-headed Ananta-Shesha, who eternally sings of the Lord and finds no end to His
qualities.

## 9. Narahari — "āhā mari mari gaurāṅgacām̐dera carite…" (GPT1 p. 68, no. 20; GCC)
*The people of Nadia cannot live through the night without Gaura: in sleep they see only Him and at dawn they wake with His name;
matchless are their old men and women; Gora, overflowing with tenderness, speaks sweet words with these old kinsfolk. Raga vibhasa.
**Attribution: probably Chakravarti** — the pada is in the GCC.*[^18]

**9.1.** Ah, who does not pine for the deeds of Gaurangachand!
The people of Nadia cannot live through the night without seeing Him.

**9.2.** Lying down, in their dreams they know nothing else: Gora is all their wealth;
at dawn they wake crying "Gora, Gora!", absorbed in that rasa.

**9.3.** There is no one to equal all these old men and women;
who with such care created their beautiful nature and conduct?

**9.4.** Narahari offers himself to his Lord: among all these old kinsfolk
Gora, overflowing with tenderness, says something to them in sweet words.

[^18]: GPT1 — p. 68, no. 20 (vibhasa; scan, PDF p. 327); GPT2 — section 2.2, no. 20 (catalogue: NG-010, wrongly as no. 10). GCC — chapter
5, pada 1; the chapter opens with the heading "Again in the morning — the excellence of the old men's love". Not in the BR, PK, KGC, PS or
the Gita-chandrodaya. B.

## 10. Narahari — "śuna he sumati ati nirajane…" (GPT1 p. 68, no. 21; GCC)
*One old man tells another in secret: "It seems the Son of the Lord of Vraja has appeared here: there is no one in Nadia who does not
pine for His qualities; Srivasa, Murari and the others do not live without His darshan; the ascetic Advaita of Shantipur is constantly
here and tells everyone of His sweet qualities. Make Gorachand the necklace of your throat." Raga vibhasa. **Attribution: probably
Chakravarti** — the pada is in the GCC.*[^19]

**10.1.** "Listen, wise one, in great secrecy I tell you a hidden thing:
it seems that the Son of the Lord of Vraja, who was in Vraja, has appeared here.[^20]

**10.2.** There is no one in the town of Nadia who does not pine for His qualities;
Srivasa, Murari and all the others do not live without His darshan.

**10.3.** The resident of Shantipur, the ascetic Advaita, is constantly here,
and with what zeal He tells of His sweet qualities to whomever He meets!

**10.4.** Ah, will there ever be another such wondrous youth?
Make Gorachand, Narahari's all, the necklace of your throat."

[^19]: GPT1 — p. 68, no. 21 (vibhasa; scan, PDF p. 327); GPT2 — section 2.2, no. 21 (catalogue: NG-011). GCC — chapter 5, pada 2, marked
"someone says" (kopyāha); the address there is "listen, O worthy one, O wise one" (śuna ohe sati he sumati), that is, apparently to his
wife. Not in the BR, PK, KGC, PS or the Gita-chandrodaya. B. Srivasa, Murari and Advaita, "who do not live without His darshan", are a
sign of the GCC frame (Gaura's morning among His associates in Navadvipa after His return from Gaya); the "youth" here is the young
Gaura.
[^20]: The old man guesses the truth: Sri Gauranga is Krishna Himself, the Son of Nanda, the Lord of Vraja (CC Adi 1–4); "secret" — because in this incarnation
the Lord hides His divinity (the "concealed" avatara of the Kali-yuga, Bhag. 7.9.38).

## 11. Narahari — "śuna ohe sati nadīyā vasati…" (GPT1 p. 68, no. 22; GCC)
*Another old man says to his wife: "Not in vain have I lived in Nadia — in my old age Providence has been merciful: I have gazed my fill
at Nimai; a moment without Him, and my soul weeps. Worship the gods, make vows for His welfare, reward the astrologers; go with Narahari
to Shachi's house and perform auspicious rites." Raga vibhasa. **Attribution: probably Chakravarti** — the pada is in the GCC.*[^21]

**11.1.** "Listen, good wife: my life in Nadia has been crowned with fruit;
in my old age Providence has been merciful — there is no end to my happiness.

**11.2.** With these two eyes I have gazed my fill at Nimaichand, the son of Shachi;
not seeing Him for half a moment, my soul always weeps bitterly.

**11.3.** May I die taking His misfortunes upon myself: I have never seen or heard of anyone like Him;[^22]
worship the gods with various rites and make vows for His welfare.

**11.4.** Generously reward the astrologers so as to propitiate the planets;
go with Narahari to Shachi's house and perform auspicious rites there."

[^21]: GPT1 — pp. 68–69, no. 22 (vibhasa; scan, PDF pp. 327–328); GPT2 — section 2.2, no. 22. The catalogue did not know it (new row
NG-266). GCC — chapter 5, pada 3, marked "and another [says]" (kopi ca). Not in the BR, PK, KGC, PS or the Gita-chandrodaya. B. On the
prayers to the gods for the Lord see note 12.
[^22]: "To die taking His misfortunes upon oneself" (vālāi laiyā mari) — a Bengali expression of tender love: "may all His troubles fall
on me".

## 12. Narahari — "āju śubhakṣaṇe pohāila niśi…" (GPT1 p. 69, no. 23; GCC)
*An auspicious dawn: the people of Nadia run to Shachi's house to see Gaurangachand; Gora sits in the middle of the courtyard,
surrounded by His associates like the moon by the stars; all have forgotten themselves and cannot take their eyes off His face. Raga
vibhasa. **Attribution: probably Chakravarti** — the pada is in the GCC.*[^23]

**12.1.** Today at an auspicious hour the night has ended;
the people of Nadia are immersed in bliss.

**12.2.** To see Gaurangachand with love,
all come running to Shachi's house.

**12.3.** In the middle of the courtyard Gora is shining,
the stealer of the hearts and eyes of all the world.

**12.4.** On all sides His associates shine —
He among them like the moon among the stars; but what is that comparison!

**12.5.** No one remembers anything —
everyone's eyes are fixed on that face.

**12.6.** What can Narahari tell with only one mouth?
Each rejoices in his own rasa.

[^23]: GPT1 — p. 69, no. 23 (vibhasa; scan, PDF p. 328); GPT2 — section 2.2, no. 23 (catalogue: NG-012, wrongly as no. 13). GCC — the first
pada of chapter 6, devoted to the love of the women in vatsalya ("morning pastimes": the love of the elderly women). Not in the BR, PK,
KGC, PS or the Gita-chandrodaya. B.

## 13. Narahari — "advaita-gharaṇī sītā ṭhākurāṇī…" (GPT1 p. 69, no. 24; GCC)
*Sita Thakurani, Advaita's wife, gazes unblinking at Gaura's smile; remembering His new deeds, she becomes as she was before, weeps,
carefully instructs Shachi, blesses and praises. Yatharaga. **Attribution: probably Chakravarti** — the pada is in the GCC.*[^24]

**13.1.** Advaita's wife, Sita Thakurani — the very embodiment of rasa —
with unblinking eyes gazes at the beautiful smile on Gaura's face.

**13.2.** Thinking and thinking of His new deeds, she became just as she was before;[^25]
unable to restrain herself — streams of tears flow from both her eyes.

**13.3.** So many words arise in her heart, her mind is faint with tenderness;
and with care she gives Shachi instructions on what to do.

## 26. Narahari — "e mora nimāicām̐da khāite cāhile go…" (GPT1 p. 74, no. 37; GCC)
*A woman tells: let Nimai ask for food and you delay a moment — in anger He smashes the house and spoils everything; scold Him — it gets

gets into other people's houses and everywhere makes the children cry. Yatharaga. **Attribution: probably Chakravarti** — the pada is in
the GCC.*[^52]

**26.1.** "This Nimaichand of mine — if He asks for food and you delay even a moment,
in great anger He smashes the house and the doors, spoils and scatters everything.

**26.2.** Say anything to Him — He plays up twice as much; He fears neither father nor mother;
none of the neighbours can restrain Him — He fears only Vishvarupa.[^53]

**26.3.** I have never seen such a boy anywhere: He wanders alone through Nadia,
slips unnoticed into anybody's house and playfully gets up to all sorts of things.

**26.4.** Wherever He goes, He makes the children cry — what can their mothers say?
At the doings of Vishvambhara, the life of Narahari, who in Nadia does not tremble?"

## 27. Narahari — "ekadina nimāi praveśi gṛhamājhe go…" (GPT1 p. 74, no. 38)
*Nimai mixed together in the house rice, dal, salt, oil, yogurt, milk, butter and ghee; His mother ran after Him with a stick, and He sat
down on a heap of discarded pots; "Fie, get down, it is impure!" — "How can a pot in which bhoga was cooked for Vishnu be impure? The
impurity is in your mind"; the astonished mother bathed Him and took Him in her arms: "This child is not a child, but Hari of
Vaikuntha." Yatharaga. **Attribution: not established** — the pada is neither in the GCC (although it stands in the middle of its
cycle) nor in the BR or other witnesses; the metre is uneven, the refrain "go" drops out — perhaps a later imitation.*[^54]

**27.1.** One day Nimai got into the house and made a great deal of mischief:
He mixed together rice, dal, salt, oil, yogurt, milk, butter and ghee.

**27.2.** Unable to bear His wildness, His mother, a stick in one hand,
ran after Nimai, and the child raced ahead of her.

**27.3.** Where a heap of discarded pots lay, there Nimai sat down on top;
Shachi says: "Fie, fie, my son, leave that impurity, come here — I'll bathe You and take You home."[^55]

**27.4.** The child replies: "A pot in which bhoga was cooked for Vishnu — how can it be impure?
The impurity is in your mind; I see everything as pure; tell Me, Mother, what in the world is impure?"[^56]

**27.5.** Hearing words of truth from the child's mouth, the mother was amazed, bathed Him and took Him in her arms.
"This child is no child: your son is Hari who dwells in Vaikuntha," says Narahari.

## 28. Narahari — "nimāicām̐dera e carita kata kava go…" (GPT1 p. 75, no. 39; GCC)
*At bathing time on the bank of the Suradhuni Nimai gives neither men nor women any peace and quarrels with those carrying offerings to the
gods; but when they complain of Him to Shachi and Mishra, He is virtue itself; all marvel: as Kanu was in the house of Nanda, so is He in
the house of Shachi — it must be He. Yatharaga. **Attribution: probably Chakravarti** — the pada is in the GCC.*[^57]

**28.1.** "How much can I tell of such doings of Nimaichand! At bathing time on the bank of the Suradhuni
neither women nor men can find any peace — such havoc He wreaks there.

**28.2.** Whoever goes with offerings, carefully carrying them to worship a deity —
with all of them He quarrels without number: what does He not do in Nadia!

**28.3.** But if someone complains to Shachi and Mishra — then what good manners He has!
Everyone's merriment grows: seeing Him, one loses one's wits.[^58]

**28.4.** Such as were the pranks of Kanu in the house of Nanda, such I see in the house of Shachi;
Nimai, the life of Narahari — it must be He Himself: otherwise who would act like this?"[^59]
