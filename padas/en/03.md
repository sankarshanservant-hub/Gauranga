# Padas of Radha's Mood: Navadvipa, Sannyasa, Puri

*Narahari Sarakara's Navadvipa padas in which the Lord experiences Radha's mood (in the *Padakalpataru* they stand as
gaura-chandrikas to the sections on purvaraga, vasakasajja, vipralabdha and khandita) and which Majumdar
(*Sri-Chaitanya-chariter upadan*, ch. 2) regards as descriptions of real moods seen by an eyewitness; padas by Ananta and
Gauridasa on Gaura and Nitai, on the sannyasa and on Nityananda's "market"; then Puri and Navadvipa in the years of
separation: Sarvabhauma, the waiting for the Lord and a dream of His return, Radha's mood in the company of Svarupa, the
divine madness of prema. The text follows the *Padakalpataru* (PK), the *Bhakti-ratnakara* (BR), the
*Kshanada-gita-chintamani* (KGC) and the *Gaura-pada-tarangini* (GPT1 — 1st ed., 1903; GPT2 — 2nd ed., 1934); the
Bengali original, variants and word-for-word translation are in `bn/03.md`.*

## 1. Narahari (Sarakara) — "gaurāṅga ṭhekila pāke…" (PK 2122; BR, p. 924)
*Lila: Navadvipa — the Lord in Krishna's mood: the Ganga seems to Him the Yamuna, a flowering garden — Vrindavana; He asks
for yellow cloth and a flute and embraces Gadadhara. According to Majumdar — an eyewitness's pada (1509). Raga mallar
(PK, KGC, GPT), ashavari (BR).*[^1]

**1.1.** *(Refrain)* Gauranga is caught in a whirlpool:
absorbed in bhava, He calls, "Radha, Radha!"[^2]

**1.2.** Seeing the Suradhuni, the Lord takes it for the Yamuna;
seeing a flowering garden, He remembers Vrindavana.

**1.3.** In the mood of His former lila He stands bent in three places
and asks for yellow cloth and a flute.[^3]

**1.4.** Taking His beloved Gadadhara into His embrace,
the Lord says in a faltering voice, "Where were you? Where were you?"

**1.5.** Understanding His mood, the Pandita stays at His left.
Narahari Dasa does not fathom this play.[^4]

[^1]: The signature is "Narahari Dasa". Narahari Chakravarti quotes the pada in BR (wave 12, p. 924) with the note "a song
of Sri Narahari Sarakara Thakura" — the only pada signed "Narahari" in BR that he expressly ascribes to Sarakara, and Majumdar
puts it first among Narahari's Navadvipa padas. It is also found in KGC (27th kshanada, as a gaura-chandrika) and in GPT1
(p. 280, no. 9); in PK it stands in the section "The form of Sri Gauranga, etc." In BR it is preceded by the words: "By this
road Gora goes to the bank of the Suradhuni… He calls, 'Radha, Radha!'" Cf. CC Antya 18: in Puri the sea seems to the
Lord to be the Yamuna.
[^2]: Pak — a whirlpool, a noose: the Lord is as if drawn into the whirlpool of bhava. Suradhuni ("the river of the gods") is
the Ganga.
[^3]: The threefold-bending pose (tribhanga), yellow cloth (pitambara) and flute are Krishna's form; the "former lila" is His
Vrindavana lila. Here the Lord is in Krishna's mood, while Gadadhara Pandita, according to the *Gaura-ganoddesha-dipika*
(vv. 147–153), is a manifestation of Radha in Gaura's lila.
[^4]: Krishna's left side is Radha's place. Instead of "the Lord taking into His embrace", PK manuscripts ka, kha, gha, cha,
KGC and GPT read simply "taking into His embrace", BR — "clasping Gadadhara to His breast". The humble "does not fathom" in
the signature is Narahari's usual formula.

## 2. Narahari (Sarakara) — "mo mena manu gorācāndere dekhiyā…" (PK 103)
*Lila: Navadvipa — Gaura walks along the road to the Ganga; a young townswoman tells how she saw Him. In PK — a
gaura-chandrika to purvaraga (love dawning at first sight). According to Majumdar — Narahari Sarakara's pada about a real
mood, though it contains expressions of nagari-bhava. Raga dhanashi.*[^5]

**2.1.** Seeing Gorachand, I all but died:[^6]
wondrous is His form — it outshines pure gold.

**2.2.** Now He walks swiftly, slapping His shoulders like a wrestler,
now, grown calm, He steps slowly along the road to the Suradhuni.[^7]

**2.3.** With His reddish eyes He gazes again and again, unblinking —
and has driven the arrows of His glances into the very heart.

**2.4.** His arms, reaching to the knees, sway on either side;
young women, in love, long to give Him their youth.[^8]

**2.5.** Now He smiles softly, now He shouts for joy;
not fathoming it, Narahari is lost in rapture.

[^5]: The signature is "Narahari". In PK the pada is the second gaura-chandrika to the section "Purvaraga — the age of
transition" (after a pada by Radhamohana); in GPT1 — p. 170, no. 41 (raga dhanashi). Majumdar discusses it among the nine
Navadvipa padas of Narahari Sarakara in PK and cites the line "With His reddish eyes He gazes again and again".
[^6]: The opening is obscure: "mo mena manu" (PK; ms. ka and GPT1 — "mo mene manu"). We translate tentatively: "mo" — "I",
"manu" — an old form for "I died" (cf. "malum̐"); "I think I perished" is also possible. The sense is the shock of seeing Gaura.
[^7]: "Slapping one's shoulders" (mālasāṭa mārā) is the gesture of a wrestler challenging to a bout; in another pada of GPT the
Lord does the same in kirtana ("now He slaps His shoulders, now calls 'Krishna!', now 'Radha!'"). The Suradhuni is the Ganga.
[^8]: The words about young women belong to the language of nagari-bhava (Gaura as the nagara, the beloved of the
townswomen); we translate them faithfully to the author. Vrindavana Dasa stresses that in this incarnation the Lord never
looked at another man's wife; the Gaudiya tradition (Vrindavana Dasa, Krishnadasa Kaviraja, Bhaktisiddhanta) does not
accept the worship of Gaura in the spirit of "gaura-nagari". In PK the pada is a gaura-chandrika: it sets the tone for
Radha's purvaraga, and the attraction here is an image of prema for Krishna, not of a worldly feeling.

## 3. Narahari (Sarakara) — "ki lāgiyā mora gaura-sundara…" (PK 307)
*Lila: Navadvipa — the Lord in the mood of Radha as vasakasajja (the heroine who adorns herself and the bower while
awaiting her beloved) dresses Himself and waits for Krishna. In PK — a gaura-chandrika to "vasakasajja in spring".
According to Majumdar — a description of a real mood; the Lord's dress and ornaments in the Navadvipa padas are not
invented. Raga dhanashi.*[^9]

**3.1.** Why does my beautiful Gaura
sit in the house
and, preparing clothes, a seat and jewelled ornaments,
adorn His body with them?

**3.2.** Seeing the shadow of His own body,
He starts in His heart:
"Why has the Lord not come even now?
Why does He delay so long?"[^10]

**3.3.** Narahari says: my Gaurahari,
thinking of Rai's condition,
looks at the road with tear-filled eyes
and speaks in a faltering voice.

[^9]: The signature is "Narahari". In GPT1 (p. 306, no. 22) with minor variants (section "vasakasajja"). Majumdar adduces
the pada as an example that Narahari's gaura-chandrikas are not conventional preludes but descriptions of moods he saw;
that the Lord wore ornaments in Navadvipa is attested by the sannyasa pada PK 2241 (portion 02, no. 5): "golden
armlets… — all that enchanting attire He gave up."
[^10]: A shadow mistaken for the beloved's step is a usual trait of the vasakasajja in rasa-shastra: Radha starts at every
rustle, thinking Krishna has come. The Lord experiences Her waiting; this is a manifestation of prema, not of worldly
longing.

## 4. Narahari (Sarakara) — "hema darapaṇi gaurāṅga-lābaṇi…" (PK 316)
*Lila: Navadvipa — the Lord in the mood of Radha as vipralabdha (the deceived heroine whose beloved did not come to the
tryst) rolls in the dust, calls "Lord of My life!" and weeps before His companions. In PK — a gaura-chandrika to
"vipralabdha in spring". According to Majumdar — a pada of vipralambha (separation). Raga bhairavi.*[^11]

**4.1.** Gauranga's beauty is like a golden mirror,
yet its lustre is grey with dust;
leaving His seat and clothes, He weeps
like a beauty of Vraja.

**4.2.** Crying "Hari, Hari!", calling Him Lord of His life,
He rises, leaning on the ground:
"Where shall I go? Whom shall I tell?
My heart is breaking!"

**4.3.** Weeping, He speaks to His companions,
raising His face:
"What is happening to My heart —
to whom can I tell this pain?"

**4.4.** Narahari Dasa says in a faltering voice:
my Gauranga
wanders under another pretext, delivering all —
yet He Himself is ever drunk with Radha's love.[^12]

[^11]: The signature is "Narahari Dasa". S. Ray notes that the pada is found in only one PK manuscript (kha) and placed it in
the text because the other sections of this pallava open with gaura-chandrikas. In GPT1 (p. 307, no. 27) with minor
variants. B. B. Majumdar (1961) gives the pada from a Baranagar manuscript with the reading "aśana vasana" ("food and clothes")
instead of "āsana vasana" ("seat and clothes").
[^12]: Cf. CC Adi 4: the external reason for the Lord's appearance is to give the world the holy name and prema, the internal
one — to taste Radha's love. Narahari expresses the same thought: delivering the world is the "pretext", while the Lord's
heart is ever in Radha's prema.

## 5. Narahari (Sarakara) — "āre mora āre mora gaurāṅga rāya…" (PK 408)
*Lila: Navadvipa — the Lord in the mood of Radha as khandita (the heroine whose beloved comes at dawn bearing the marks of a
tryst with another) angrily reproaches Krishna. In PK — a gaura-chandrika to the section "Khandita — the impatient
(adhira) madhya". According to Majumdar — Narahari Sarakara's pada of the khandita. Raga suhai.*[^13]

**5.1.** Ah, my, ah, my Gauranga Raya!
Full of His former love, He walks with slow steps.

**5.2.** His eyes have reddened, His face has darkened;
in anger, His heart trembling, the Lord speaks haltingly:

**5.3.** "I know You, I know Your false love!
Go and bow to her with whom You spent the night."[^14]

**5.4.** Having said this, Gauranga is wholly overcome by bhava:
in the waves of bhava He is as if after a sleepless night.

**5.5.** Narahari says: in Radha's mood He has become
like one who was given a promise and deceived.

[^13]: The signature is "Narahari". In GPT1 (p. 309, no. 4) with minor variants (section "khandita, mana"). Majumdar cites
the line "I know You, I know Your false love". The khandita is one of the eight heroines of rasa-shastra
(*Ujjvala-nilamani*).
[^14]: The words of Radha as khandita to Krishna, who has come in the morning from Her rival. Radha's jealousy and pique
(mana) are a manifestation of the highest prema, not a worldly feeling; the Lord experiences them in Her mood.

## 6. Narahari (Sarakara) — "gorā pahuṅ birale basiyā…" (PK 421)
*Lila: Navadvipa — the Lord in the mood of Radha as khandita sits alone after a sleepless night and weeps: "Having given Me
hope, You deceived Me all night." In PK — a gaura-chandrika to "khandita — the composed-impatient (dhira-adhira) madhya".
According to Majumdar — Narahari Sarakara's pada of the khandita. Raga gandhara.*[^15]

**6.1.** Lord Gora, sitting alone,
has lowered His face.

**6.2.** In bhava His eyes are heavy with sleep —
the sleepless night is witness to it.

**6.3.** With a faded face He says:
"Having given Me hope, You deceived Me all night."

**6.4.** Weeping, Gora Raya says:
"I cannot bear this pain."

**6.5.** He laments, full of grief;
Narahari begs for grace.[^16]

[^15]: The signature is "Narahari". In GPT1 (p. 309, no. 5) with minor variants, immediately after the preceding pada
(PK 408). Majumdar cites the lines "Having given Me hope, You deceived Me all night, — weeping, says Gora Raya".
[^16]: "Begs for grace" — in the original "begs for prasada" (parasāda): the Lord's mercy.

## 7. Ananta Dasa — "āpāda-mastaka prema-dhārā barikhata…" (PK 2208)
*Praise of Gauranga: from head to foot He pours streams of prema; at His qualities wood and stone melt, beasts and birds
weep; the poet asks only one thing — to sing of Gauranga, wandering from land to land. In PK — the section "Sri Gaurachandra —
in another way". Majumdar names the pada among Ananta's padas on Gauranga's mood. Raga mangala.*[^17]

**7.1.** From head to foot He pours down streams of prema,
His rays flash in every direction;
His gait is lovelier than a lordly elephant's,
moons rise at His feet.[^18]

**7.2.** What kind of Creator, having fashioned Gaurangachand's body,
remained firm himself?
What kind of heart is his — of wood and stone —
that it did not melt at once?[^19]

**7.3.** At my Gauranga's qualities wood and stone —
what of wood and stone! — melt and flow down to the earth;
the beasts and birds of the forest weep and weep:
there is no living being that does not weep.

**7.4.** In whatever family I may be born,
whatever body I may receive, —
Ananta Dasa's one wish is to sing of Thakura Gauranga's qualities,
wandering from land to land.

[^17]: The signature is "Ananta Dasa". According to Majumdar, Ananta Dasa was a poet of Advaita's branch and a contemporary
of the Lord; PK has 32 padas with his signature. In GPT1 (p. 39, no. 41) and GPT2 with variants.
[^18]: The moons at His feet are the Lord's toenails, shining like moons.
[^19]: PK: "…fashioned Gaurangachand's body, remaining firm himself" (daṛhiyā); the P-R-Sa manuscript and GPT2 read "dhariyā",
and GPT "Gaurangachand" (cām̐dere ye): "…fashioned Gaurangachand, taking on his own body". The image of the Creator (vidhi) who "sculpted" beauty is a
poetic convention (poets speak so of Radha and Krishna too); the Lord's body is not created — it is eternal, full of being,
knowledge and bliss ("ishvarah paramah krishnah sac-cid-ananda-vigrahah", *Brahma-samhita* 5.1).

## 8. Ananta Dasa — "dekha dekha aparūpa gaurāṅga nitāi…" (PK 2336)
*Praise of Gaura and Nitai — the two brothers who purify the fallen: They give prema to all without distinction and weep on
seeing the fallen. In PK — the section "The form and qualities of Sri Chaitanya and Nityananda". According to Majumdar it
"sounds like the word of a contemporary". Raga kamoda.*[^20]

**8.1.** *(Refrain)* Look, look: the wondrous Gauranga and Nitai!
By the good fortune of all living beings there wander the earth
the two brothers who purify the fallen.

**8.2.** Whomever They see, They give prema to him, entreating him,
making no distinction between high and low;
the people of the three worlds, drinking the nectar of prema,
know neither old age, nor death, nor sorrow.

**8.3.** The wish-fulfilling tree and the ocean will not give a drop unasked —
shame even to compare them with Them!
Seeing the fallen, Both weep, unable to restrain Themselves,
and, entreating, give priceless bhakti and prema.[^21]

**8.4.** If someone does not serve such merciful Lords —
what hope is there in his wretched life?
Even if he be a sannyasi or a brahmana, he is counted among the asuras:
such is the word of Ananta Dasa.[^22]

[^20]: The signature is "Ananta Dasa". In GPT1 (p. 39, no. 42, raga shri or kamoda) and GPT2 with minor variants; there the
pada immediately follows the preceding one (PK 2208).
[^21]: "Entreating" (yāciyā): the Lord and Nityananda Themselves beg people to accept the name and prema (cf. CB Madhya
13: Nitai and Haridasa go from house to house begging, "Sing Krishna's name"; CC Adi 7). Instead of "Both" (dom̐he)
manuscript ka reads "body" (deha): "Their body knows no rest".
[^22]: Cf. *Bhagavad-gita* 7.15: the deluded do not surrender to the Lord, "having taken shelter of the nature of the asuras".

## 9. Ananta Acharya — "jaya śacīnandana jaga-jībana sāra…" (PK 2285)
*Lila: from the appearance to Nilachala — the Lord of Goloka descends in Navadvipa, teaches the dharma of the age —
sankirtana, accepts sannyasa from Keshava Bharati, shows the six-armed form in Utkala and settles in Nilachala. In PK —
the section "The sannyasa of Sri Gauranga, etc." According to Majumdar — probably a pada by Ananta Acharya, who lived in
Navadvipa; an important detail: the Lord Himself "gave the mantra" to Bharati. Raga shri (as for the preceding PK pada).*[^23]

**9.1.** *(Refrain)* Glory to the Son of Shachi, the essence of the world's life!
In life and in death Gora is my Lord.

**9.2.** Coming with His associates, the Lord of Goloka
descended in Navadvipa;
establishing the dharma of the age — His own sankirtana —
He explained it by dancing and singing.

**9.3.** He of golden form put on the kaupina and the cord,
an upper cloth the colour of the morning rays;
Gaurangachandra took the kamandalu and staff in His hands,
casting aside Vishnupriya's hopes.[^24]

**9.4.** Hari, the guru of the whole world, made Bharati His guru:
having given him the mantra, He received it from him.[^25]
The scoffers and unbelievers, who had once reviled Him much,
began to worship Him as Narayana.[^26]

**9.5.** Going to the land of Utkala, He taught the holy name,
manifesting the six-armed form.
Ananta Acharya says: with all the great souls
He made His dwelling in Nilachala.[^27]

[^23]: The signature is "Ananta Acharya"; in S. Ray's index of poets it is the only pada of Ananta Acharya in PK. Majumdar
considers it probably a pada of Ananta Acharya of Advaita's branch, who lived in Navadvipa (CC Adi 12). In GPT1
(pp. 19–20, no. 53) and GPT2 with minor variants; the refrain there (and in PK manuscripts ka, kha, cha) begins "The Son of
Shachi…" without "Glory".
[^24]: The kaupina is the sannyasi's loincloth; the kamandalu, a water-pot; the staff (danda), the sign of sannyasa.
"Vishnupriya's hopes" — in the original "vishnupriya-abhilasha": the Lord left His wife, who hoped to keep Him at home
(CC Adi 17; Lochana, Madhya-khanda).
[^25]: According to CB (Madhya 28), the Lord spoke into Keshava Bharati's ear a mantra He said He had heard in a dream, and
then received it from him as from a guru; thus He, the guru of the world, showed the example of a disciple's humility. Cf.
Murari 3.2.
[^26]: Cf. CC Adi 17: the Lord accepted sannyasa so that those who had reviled Him in Navadvipa, by bowing to a sannyasi,
would be freed from their offences; a sannyasi is honoured as Narayana.
[^27]: Utkala is Orissa. The six-armed form the Lord showed to Sarvabhauma (CB Antya 3; see the next pada). Instead of
"with" (laiyā) the P-R-Sa manuscript reads "in lila" (līlā).

## 10. Gauridasa Pandita — "pahu mora nityānanda rāya…" (PK 2313)
*Praise of Nityananda — the "market of the holy name" (hat-pattan): Nityananda is king of the market on the Ganga's bank,
Ramai carries out His orders, Haridasa is the kotwal, Krishnadasa the weighman, Srivasa the scribe; Vishvambhara and
Gadadhara trade in the Acharya's courtyard; Gauridasa, sitting near the king, hears of the market's glory. In PK — the
section "The qualities of Sri Nityananda". According to Majumdar — the first pada of the hat-pattan genre, written after
the sannyasa (the Lord is called "Chaitanya"). Raga shri.*[^28]

**10.1.** *(Refrain)* My Lord is Nityananda Raya!
Having churned all the tantras, the great mantra — the name of Hari —
He explains to living beings, holding them by the hand.

**10.2.** His name is "Chaitanya's elder brother", He has no equal in the three worlds;
on the bank of the Suradhuni He set up a post;
arranging a market in due form, Nityananda became its king,
with the hero's banner that crushes the unbelievers.[^29]

**10.3.** Ramai, a worthy servant, carries out the royal orders,
Haridasa became the kotwal;
Krishnadasa became the weighman — no one can slip past by cheating;
the writing and reading is Srinivasa's.[^30]

**10.4.** The traders are Vishvambhara and the beloved Gadadhara:
in the Acharya's courtyard there is buying and selling.
Gauridasa, smiling, sits near the king
and hears a little of the market's glory.[^31]

[^28]: The signature is "Gauridasa". Gauridasa Pandita of Ambika (Kalna) was a companion of the Lord and of Nityananda;
Jayananda praises his poetry; PK has only two padas with his signature (161 and 2313). In GPT1 (p. 420, no. 20) with
minor variants. "Hat-pattan" ("opening a market") is a genre of padas where the preaching of the holy name is pictured
as a market at which prema is given away; later poems also belong to it. Majumdar regards Gauridasa's pada as the first
example of the genre and dates it after the sannyasa: Nityananda is called "Chaitanya's elder brother". Cf. CC Madhya 15
and Murari 4.21–4.22: the Lord sends Nityananda from Puri to preach in Gauda.
[^29]: Tantras here means the scriptures in general. A post (thānā) — a guard station. "The hero's banner that crushes the
unbelievers": Nityananda is Balarama, and His preaching is a victory over the pashandas.
[^30]: Ramai is probably Ramai Pandita (Sri Rama Pandita), Srivasa's younger brother, or Abhirama (Ramadasa), Nityananda's
companion; a kotwal is the chief of the town watch; Haridasa is Haridasa Thakura. "Weighman" — in the original "dāṛyā"
(mss. "ḍāṛyā", "ḍeṛyā"): the meaning is unclear — the one who holds the scales (dām̐ṛi) or a gatekeeper; "by cheating"
(bhām̐ṛyā) — manuscript cha reads "bhem̐ṛyā", P-R-Sa "chāṛyā" ("let off"). Krishnadasa is probably Gauridasa's younger
brother, the author of PK 2358 (portion 01, no. 24). Srinivasa is Srivasa Pandita (Srinivasa is his full name).
[^31]: The Acharya is Advaita Acharya. Vishvambhara is the Lord Himself (His name before sannyasa). The humble "hears a
little" in the signature: the poet is merely present at this play.

## 11. Vasu Ghosha — "acaitanya śrīcaitanya sārbbabhauma-ghare…" (GPT1, p. 402)
*Lila: Puri, 1510 — the Lord lies unconscious in Sarvabhauma's house; Sarvabhauma sees Him in the forms of the avataras, and
when the Lord stands up in the six-armed form, he falls at His feet. Only in GPT (section "Antya-lila"). Raga suhai.*[^32]

**11.1.** Sri Chaitanya lies unconscious in Sarvabhauma's house;
Gopinatha, sitting beside Him, massages His feet.[^33]

**11.2.** Sarvabhauma gazes unblinking at the Lord's face
and cannot make out Who He is.

**11.3.** Now he sees the Lord in the form of Nrisimha,
now again in the form of the boy Vamana;

**11.4.** now he sees the forms of Matsya, Kurma, Varaha,
now Bhrigurama with a fearsome axe in His hand;

**11.5.** at times he sees a form dark as a blade of durva grass,
at times the Holder of the flute, the colour of a rain-cloud.[^34]

**11.6.** Seeing all this, he gave up his doubts;
the Lord rose and stood in the six-armed form.[^35]

**11.7.** Shachi's darling is that very Butter-thief:
within — the dark Kanu, without — Gaura.[^36]

**11.8.** Falling to the ground, Sarvabhauma prostrates himself.
Vasu Ghosha says: why any more vain delusion?

[^32]: The signature is "Vasu Ghosha". The pada is neither in PK nor in BR — only in GPT1 (p. 402, no. 2 of the section
"Antya-lila") and GPT2; by Majumdar's criterion Vasu Ghosha's padas known only from GPT are unreliable, and the attribution
remains doubtful. In GPT the pada stands between Madhavi's two padas on the Lord's arrival in Puri (PK 2239, 2240; portion
02, nos. 1–2). Cf. CC Madhya 6, CB Antya 3, Murari 3.11–3.12.
[^33]: Gopinatha Acharya, Sarvabhauma's brother-in-law, had known the Lord already in Navadvipa. According to CC (Madhya 6),
the Lord fell unconscious on seeing Jagannatha, and Sarvabhauma had Him carried to his house.
[^34]: The forms of the avataras: Nrisimha, Vamana (the boy brahmana), Matsya, Kurma, Varaha, Parashurama (Bhrigurama);
"dark as durva grass" — Ramachandra; the Holder of the flute — Krishna.
[^35]: The six-armed form (with Rama's bow, Krishna's flute, and the sannyasi's staff and water-pot) the Lord showed to
Sarvabhauma according to CB (Antya 3); according to CC (Madhya 6) — first a four-armed form, then Krishna's two-armed form
with the flute.
[^36]: The Butter-thief is Krishna. Cf. *Tattva-sandarbha* (2): "antah krishnam bahir gauram" — "Krishna within, Gaura
without"; CC Adi 1, 4.

## 12. Narahari (Sarakara) — "āoba gaura punahi nadiyāpura…" (PK 1970)
*Lila: Navadvipa in the years when the Lord lives in Puri: the devotees dream of His return — to see His face and His
kirtana again. In PK — a gaura-chandrika to the section "Bhavollasa" (Radha's joy at the news of Krishna's return), headed
"The words of the devotees, the residents of Navadvipa". Raga dhanashi.*[^37]

**12.1.** Gaura will come again to the town of Nadia —
and the heart will be filled with joy.
Shall we really see again that fountain of bliss,
His play in kirtana?

**12.2.** *(Refrain)* Hari, Hari! When shall I see that moon-like face?
When shall I swim across the ocean of separation,
and the heart's bewilderment be dispelled?

**12.3.** When shall I see a lustre like the purest gold
and the gleaming sacred thread?
Raising both arms, He will cry "Hari, Hari!",
dancing amid the devotees.[^38]

**12.4.** Having said this, all fell still with closed eyes,
drunk with love for Gaura.
Narahari Dasa: when will the hope be fulfilled —
to see the youthful Gaura?

[^37]: The signature is "Narahari Dasa". Majumdar does not discuss this pada; its attribution to Narahari Sarakara is likely
(simple language) but not proven. In GPT1 (p. 411, no. 27) with variants, in the section on the years in Puri; there
instead of "will come" (āova) it reads "is coming" (āota). In PK the pada follows a gaura-chandrika by Radhamohana to the
same section.
[^38]: The Lord wore the sacred thread (upavita) before sannyasa; on accepting sannyasa He gave it up. The Navadvipa
devotees remember His former form — as they saw Him in the kirtanas of 1509.

## 13. Narahari (Sarakara) — "locane jharajhara ānanda-lora…" (PK 1707)
*Lila: Navadvipa in the years of separation: the poet sees in a dream how Gaura, after a long separation, has come to
Navadvipa and strolls among the devotees; on waking he weeps. In PK — a gaura-chandrika to the section "Telling of a dream"
(svapna-rasodgara), headed "Here — Sri Mahaprabhu". Raga suhai.*[^39]

**13.1.** Tears of joy stream from my eyes:
in a dream I saw the youthful Gaura.

**13.2.** After so many days He came to Navadvipa
and joyfully strolls in the circle of devotees.

**13.3.** What shall I tell you, friend, of the bliss of that night!
After so many days I saw Gora's moon-like face.

**13.4.** All the people of Nadia, tormented by separation,
saw Gora's face — and all their sorrow went far away.

**13.5.** And now, not seeing Him, the heart is breaking:
Narahari Dasa weeps, rolling in the dust.[^40]

[^39]: The signature is "Narahari Dasa". Majumdar does not discuss the pada. In GPT1 (p. 410, no. 25) with minor variants,
in the section on the years in Puri, next to padas on the Lord's meeting with the Navadvipa devotees. In PK the section
"telling of a dream" consists of Radha's words relating to a friend Her dream of meeting Krishna; the gaura-chandrika
transfers them to Gaura. Cf. Shachi's dream of Nimai's coming (Vasu Ghosha, portion 01, no. 22) and the Lord's visit to
Navadvipa in 1514 (CC Madhya 16).
[^40]: Waking after a dream of meeting is a usual motif of separation: the joy of the dream makes separation still keener.

## 14. Vasu Ghosha — "svarūpera kare dhari gorārāya…" (GPT1, p. 310)
*Lila: Puri — the Lord in Radha's mood, holding Svarupa's hand, reviles "Shyama, the friend": He ruined Her honour and went
to another. Only in GPT (section "Khandita, mana"). Raga suhai.*[^41]

**14.1.** Holding Svarupa's hand, Gora Raya
heaps abuse on Shyama, the friend:

**14.2.** "That cheat, that rake, that thief of love —
how many woes He has brought on Me!

**14.3.** He ruined My family and My honour,
He set fire to My husband's house;

**14.4.** and in the end, the Dark One left Me
and sports with another.[^42]

**14.5.** Have I become a stranger to Him?" —
saying this, Gaurahari sobs in despair.

**14.6.** Vasu says: what can I understand?
The One You weep for, Lord, is You Yourself.[^43]

[^41]: The signature is "Vasu". The pada is neither in PK nor in BR — only in GPT1 (p. 310, no. 8) and GPT2 (sec. 4.6, no.
8); Majumdar considers Vasu Ghosha's padas known only from GPT unreliable, and two padas signed Vasu in which Svarupa
accompanies the Lord in Navadvipa or on the Yamuna outright spurious (Svarupa came to the Lord only in Puri). Here, to judge
by Svarupa's presence, the scene is Puri, but the attribution remains doubtful. In GPT the pada follows the pada of
Narahari (?) "prema kari kulabatī sane" (portion 02, no. 8). Svarupa Damodara in the Lord's mood is Radha's friend Lalita
(CC Antya 14–19; *Gaura-ganoddesha-dipika*).
[^42]: "Another" — Radha's rival (Chandravali). Radha's abuse in mana is the language of the highest prema, not of worldly
jealousy; the Lord experiences it in Radha's mood.
[^43]: Cf. CC Adi 4: Krishna accepted Radha's mood and lustre to taste His own sweetness and Radha's love for Him. The poet
says: the Krishna You seek is You Yourself.

## 15. Narahari (Sarakara) — "kanaka-campaka gorācānde…" (PK 849)
*Lila: the Lord in Radha's mood reproaches the Creator (vidhi): "Who has driven Me mad? Why did he make such a union?" In PK
— a gaura-chandrika to the section "reproaches to the Creator" (akshepanuraga). Raga suhai.*[^44]

**15.1.** Gorachand, the colour of the golden champaka —
why does He weep, fallen on the ground?

**15.2.** Now He rises and cries, "Hari, Hari!
Who has driven Me mad?"

**15.3.** Raising His arms that reach to the knees,
He keeps reviling the Creator:

**15.4.** "Shame on the Creator's decrees!
Why did he make such a union?"[^45]

**15.5.** With what feeling does Gora Raya say this? —
Narahari goes about asking everyone.

[^44]: The signature is "Narahari". Majumdar does not discuss this pada separately, but he calls Narahari Sarakara's
gaura-chandrikas in the PK sections on akshepanuraga (853, 799, 820, 832, 840) descriptions of the Lord's real moods. In GPT1
(p. 300, no. 27) and GPT2 the pada comes immediately after PK 840 ("Ah, my youthful Gaura!", portion 02, no. 7), among the
Puri padas. The pada itself does not name the place.
[^45]: "Union" (yoṭanā; ms. ka "yoṭana", kha "yoṭinā"; GPT "joṭana"): Radha reproaches the Creator because Her love for Krishna
has been joined with the bonds of family and duty and brings only suffering. The madness (vāuri) here is the madness of
prema. Instead of "Shame…" PK reads "vahe dhik" — apparently a misprint for "kahe dhik" ("says: shame…"), as in GPT.

## 16. Narahari (Sarakara) — "āre mora gaura kiśora. nāhi jāne dibāniśi…" (PK 1746)
*Lila: the mood of separation — the Lord does not distinguish day from night, laughs without cause, sings aloud asking,
"Where is the Lord of My life?", trembles, leaps, dances, speaks incoherently, weeps with closed eyes. In PK — a
gaura-chandrika to the section "Separation in the winter season" (hemanta and shishira). According to Majumdar — Narahari
Sarakara's pada of viraha ("laughter without cause"). Raga pahida.*[^46]

**16.1.** *(Refrain)* Ah, my youthful Gaura!
Not knowing day from night, He laughs without cause —
the Lord is lost in the bewilderment of His heart.

**16.2.** Now He sings aloud — of whom, of what does the Lord ask? —
"Where is the Lord of My life?"
Now His whole body trembles as with cold, now He leaps:
"Where shall I find Him? Where shall I go? With whom?"[^47]

**16.3.** Now, raising His arms, He dances, whirling and whirling,
now again and again He speaks incoherently;
now He closes His eyes and weeps, "O Natha!",
now again and again He is in torment.

**16.4.** Narahari Dasa says: ah, my Gaurahari
has become like this from Radha's love.
He resolved in His heart to deliver the age of Kali —
why then am I left out?[^48]

[^46]: The signature is "Narahari Dasa". Majumdar cites the line "Now He sings aloud — of whom, of what does the Lord ask?"
among Narahari Sarakara's viraha padas. In GPT1 (p. 315, no. 8) and GPT2 (sec. 4.7, no. 8) the pada stands immediately after
PK 1662 and 1663 (no. 17 of this portion), among the padas on the divine madness of prema; there instead of "speaks
incoherently" (pralāpa) it reads "laments" (vilāpa), with a note of the reading "pralāpa". In PK the pada is preceded by the
heading "Separation befitting winter. Here — Sri Gaurachandra".
[^47]: Radha's words in separation: She seeks Krishna and does not know where to go. Trembling is a sattvika-bhava; in PK
the pada opens the section on winter separation, and trembling "as with cold" matches the season. This is the madness of
prema (divya-unmada) described in CC Antya 14–20, not worldly longing.
[^48]: Cf. CC Adi 4. The formula "why then am I left out" is Narahari's usual signature (cf. PK 2241, portion 02, no. 5).

## 17. Unsigned (Vasu Ghosha? Govinda Dasa?) — "cetana pāiyā gorā rāya…" (PK 1663)
*Lila: a continuation of PK 1662 (Vasu Ghosha, portion 02, no. 15): the Lord, coming to Himself after running to the sea,
sees Svarupa and Ramaraya before Him and laments: "Where is My Krishna with the flute at His lips? I saw Him just now!" In PK
— the section "Divya-unmada". Raga shri.*[^49]

**17.1.** Coming to His senses, Gora Raya,
lying on the ground, looks this way and that.

**17.2.** Before Him are Svarupa and Ramaraya;
seeing them, the Lord cries, "Alas, alas!

**17.3.** Where is My Krishna with the flute at His lips?
I saw Him just now!

**17.4.** O Lord, supremely merciful,
be gracious, show Yourself!"[^50]

**17.5.** So laments Gorachand;
seeing this, the devotees weep.

[^49]: In PK the pada is unsigned; in the P-R-Sa manuscript "Govinda Dasa" stands instead of "the devotees" ("seeing this,
Govinda Dasa weeps"), so that the pada is signed by Govinda Dasa; in GPT1 (p. 315, no. 7) and GPT2 (sec. 4.7, no. 7) a sixth
couplet is added with Vasu Ghosha's signature: "Vasu Ghosha says: my Gora is drunk with love for Krishna". The attribution
is disputed: it may be a continuation of Vasu Ghosha's PK 1662, but the witnesses disagree. Ramaraya is Ramananda Raya. Cf.
CC Antya 14: coming to Himself after running to Chataka-parvata, the Lord says to Svarupa, "Why have you brought Me here? I
just saw Krishna…"; Antya 17–18.
[^50]: "Krishna with the flute at His lips" — in the original "murali-badana", "He whose face bears the flute". The Lord
speaks in the mood of Radha, who has just seen Krishna and lost Him again.

## 18. Narahari — "gaurāṅga ke jāne mahimā tomāra…" (PK 2293)
*Praise of Gauranga, the avatara who purifies the fallen in the age of Kali: from the ocean of Shyama, as if by churning,
the sweet Gauranga appeared, and a downpour of prema flooded the three worlds. In PK — the last pada of the section "The
sannyasa of Sri Gauranga, etc." Raga varadi ("the same" as for the preceding PK pada), dhanashri (GPT).*[^51]

**18.1.** *(Refrain)* Gauranga, who knows Your greatness?
To deliver the age of Kali You appeared — the avatara who purifies the fallen.

**18.2.** How long the Creator
churned the ocean of Shyama!
How much nectar he made from it —
and the sweet Gauranga appeared.[^52]

**18.3.** In the three worlds a downpour of prema came —
the rain of Gaura's love;
the lowly and wretched are drowned in that rasa, —
so Narahari sings of His qualities.

[^51]: The signature is "Narahari". Majumdar does not discuss the pada; in the stage-1 catalogue another pada ("nīlācale yave
majhu nātha" — in GPT1 signed by Vaishnava Dasa) was wrongly linked to PK 2293. The language and imagery (churning, a downpour
of prema) are closer to later stutis; the pada may also belong to Narahari Chakravarti — its attribution to Sarakara is not
established. In GPT1 (p. 28, no. 6) and GPT2 with the variant "mathiyā se karatāla" instead of "mathiyā se kata kāla".
[^52]: The image is the churning of the Milk Ocean, from which the amrita appeared. "The Creator" (vidhi) is a poetic figure:
Gauranga is not created — He is Shyama (Krishna) Himself, who accepted Radha's lustre and mood (CC Adi 4).
