# Appendix. Padas signed "Narahari" from the Gaura-pada-tarangini — VII: the ashtakaliya (the last part of the night, morning, day, evening)

*Continuation of the appendix (the introductory note on the two Naraharis, on the arguments for attribution and on the marks in the
headings is in `17.md`). Portion 23: the continuation of GPT section 5.2 "Ashtakaliya" (GPT1 pp. 348–362, nos. 16–51): the Lord's words
in sleep at the end of the night and His awakening; the morning in Shachi's house among the companions; the pastimes of the day — the Lord
playfully takes away the companions' clothes, the meal on the bank of the Ganga; the walk through Navadvipa — old men, old women, the blind,
the lame, the dandies and the merchants gaze at Him; the companions' longing at night as they wait for the dawn; two "table-of-contents"
padas of the ashtakaliya (the eight parts of Gaura's day and night); the evening kirtanas — the Lord's dance in the guise of Rukmini and the
dance in Srivasa's courtyard. The signatures in the section were checked throughout, in the OCR and on the scan; the Gaura-carita-cintamani
(GCC, kiranas 1–4) and the 12th wave of the Bhakti-ratnakara (BR) were read continuously at the relevant places. Not translated (outside
the appendix): nos. 17, 52, 54 — signed Ghanashyama; no. 18 — Yadunatha Dasa (translated in portion 13, no. 16); nos. 24, 25, 30, 32, 53 — Vasu
Ghosha (no. 24 — portion 12, no. 7; no. 25 = PK 1108 — portion 05; no. 32 — portion 05; no. 53 = PK 656 — portion 09; no. 30 — the festival in the
house of Narahari Thakura at Shrikhanda, signed Vasu); nos. 29, 41 — Narottama Dasa; no. 31 — Madhava (= PK 2638); nos. 33, 46 — Radhamohana; no. 44 —
without the Narahari signature (= PK vol. 4); no. 45 — Vaishnava Dasa; no. 39 — a pada in nagari-bhava (assigned to the section of nagari padas).
The padas on Gaura's night sleep (nos. 55–63, 65) go to the next portion. The base text is GPT1 (doubtful places by the scan), collated with
GPT2, the GCC (ed. 1947) and the BR (eds. 1912 and 1960); the PK, KGC, PS and the Gita-candrodaya were checked. On the ashtakaliya as eternal
lila and on the Lord's sleep see note 36 of portion 22; on the motif of the women of Nadia, note 7. The notes of the GPT editors are not
reproduced.*

## 1. Narahari — "alasa avaśa pahuṅ rasika-śiromaṇi…" (GPT1 pp. 348–349, section 5.2, no. 16; GCC)
*The last part of the night: the Lord, in the languor of half-sleep, speaks as in a dream words full of rasa: without the sight of
Radharamana His life is burning; calling by name Gauridasa, Haridasa, Dhananjaya and dozens of companions, He asks them to sing of the deeds
of the people of Vraja to quench the burning of His heart; in humility He says that enemies have subdued Him and He has taken a body in
vain, and prays to be, birth after birth, a servant of the feet of the Lord of the gopis; having said this, He grows still again in sleep.
Raga not stated. **Attribution: probably Chakravarti** — the pada is in the GCC; Vrajabuli.*[^1]

**1.1.** Languid and overcome, my Lord, the crown of the relishers of rasa, speaks as in a dream words full of rasa:
"Bereft of the rasa of seeing Radharamana, My life is wasting — My life is burning away.[^2]

**1.2.** Listen, Gauri[dasa], Haridasa, Dhananjaya, Sanjaya, Vijaya, Mukunda, Murari,
Madhava, Vasudeva, Purushottama, Shridhara, Krishnadasa, giver of joy,

**1.3.** Shrinidhi, Madhusudana, Vakreshvara, Satyaraja, wise Kavichandra,
Shankara, Garuda, Bhagavata, Nandana, Chandrashekhara, deep Saranga,

**1.4.** Shuklambara, Yadunatha, Nakula, Vanamali, Mahesha, Shrinidhi, abode of virtues!
The Creator, most kind, understanding My heart, gave Me all of you as inseparable companions.

**1.5.** So accept My humble request: those lovely deeds of the people of Vraja, full of rasa,
sing in all the beauty of sweet ragas — and quench this cruel burning of My heart.

**1.6.** How much shall I reveal the secret of My heart? These mighty wicked enemies have subdued Me;
in vain I have taken a body — I have understood nothing, I have lost the way in the bewilderment of prema.

**1.7.** Again, with joined palms, I say: all of you, with joy, fulfil the heart's desire of one who is your own —
that birth after birth, without hindrance, I may be a servant of the lotus feet of the Lord of the gopis."[^3]

**1.8.** Having spoken such words, the Lord of the twice-born, sinking for a little into sleep, again fell silent.
Narahari, bewildered, cannot describe this incomparable lovely deed, rare even for the gods.

[^1]: GPT1 — pp. 348–349, section 5.2 "Ashtakaliya", no. 16 ("যথারাগ"; scan, PDF pp. 607–608); GPT2 — section 5.2, no. 16 (p. ≈224). **The catalogue did
not know it** (new row NG-295). GCC — kirana 2 ("the pastimes on the bed" in the last part of the night), pada 25: after the awakening songs sung by
the parrot (padas 14–23; portion 22, nos. 17–26, note 35), the GCC says that Gaura, absorbed in rasa, heard a little of them (pada 24) — and the Lord
speaks these words in His sleep. Variants: 1.1 GCC "রসময় বাত" ("words full of rasa"; GPT "রস রস বাত"); 1.6 GCC "মরমক বাত", "হোঁয়লু"; 1.7 GCC "জগ" for
"জনি". The name "Shrinidhi" stands twice in the list in all witnesses. Not in the BR, PK, KGC, PS or the Gita-candrodaya. B. On the ashtakaliya and
the Lord's sleep see note 36 of portion 22.
[^2]: Radharamana, "the beloved of Radha", is Krishna: the Lord, Krishna Himself appearing in the mood of Radha, longs for Krishna as Radha longs
for Him. The list names the Lord's companions in Navadvipa, mostly known from the biographies: Gauridasa Pandita (in the GCC "গৌরি"), Haridasa
Thakura, Dhananjaya, Sanjaya, Vijaya Dasa, Mukunda Datta, Murari Gupta, Shridhara, Vakreshvara Pandita, Satyaraja Khan, Garuda Pandita, Nandana
Acharya, Chandrashekhara Acharya, Shuklambara Brahmachari, Nakula Brahmachari, Vanamali and others.
[^3]: The words of 1.6–1.7 are the Lord's humility (dainya) in the mood of a devotee: He, the Supreme Lord, is subject neither to maya nor to the
"enemies" (lust, anger and the rest), but, relishing the mood of a bhakta, speaks of Himself as a fallen soul — cf. His words "there is not a
trace of love for Krishna in Me" (CC Madhya 2.42), the prayer "birth after birth, causeless devotion to You" (Shikshashtaka, CC Antya 20.29) and
the verse "a servant of the servant of the servant of the lotus feet of the Lord of the gopis" (CC Madhya 13.80). The reading "প্রেম ভরাতি" (GCC
"প্রেমভরাতি") is unclear; understood as "প্রেম-ভ্রান্তি", "the bewilderment of prema".

## 2. Narahari — "śuti rahuṅ sundara gaurakiśora…" (GPT1 pp. 349–350, no. 19; GCC)
*Dawn: the beautiful young Gaura is still asleep; the companions read prose and verse, sing songs about Him to instruments, compose verses,
loudly recite the names, weep in humility; the cows low, calling their calves — and the Lord, hearing them, wakes, stretches, yawns, leaves
the chamber and asks for the hobbling rope, calling Gauridasa and the others by their former names. Raga not stated. **Attribution: probably
Chakravarti** — the pada is in the GCC; Vrajabuli.*[^4]

**2.1.** The beautiful young Gaura still lies [asleep].
The sun is already on its way to the east, people are waking — the night is over.

**2.2.** Someone reads the sweetest prose and verse, wholly absorbed in it — a wondrous thing!
Someone, to the sound of many instruments, beautifully sings songs of the Lord's loving deeds.

**2.3.** Someone composes lovely verses, someone by vow loudly recites the names,
someone, struck down by humility, is drunk with the rasa of bhakti — not even an autumn downpour compares with it.

**2.4.** The cows low, distressed by the weight of their udders — they must give milk to their calves;
hearing their call, the Lord at once woke and sat up on the bed.

**2.5.** Then He stretched, covered both eyes with His lovely palms, yawned a little,
left the chamber and goes with an eager heart, asking again and again for the hobbling rope.[^5]

**2.6.** Seeing Gauridasa and the others, He joyfully calls each by his former name.
Narahari says: wondrous is this lovely deed — is it deep sleep, or a play of prema?

[^4]: GPT1 — pp. 349–350, no. 19 ("যথারাগ"; scan, PDF pp. 608–609); GPT2 — section 5.2, no. 19, pp. 224–225 (catalogue: NG-177). GCC — kirana 3 ("the
awakening"), pada 10. Variants: 2.2 GCC "কোউ" for "কোই" (and so on); 2.3 GCC "শব্দ ঘটাপটতর নাহি হোয়" for "শরদ ঘটা পটতর…" — the end of the line is
unclear in both readings, the translation is tentative ("not even the roar of autumn clouds compares with it" by the GCC); 2.4 GPT1 "রত সপিয়া অন" —
a misprint, corrected by the GCC "বতস পিয়াঅন" ("to give milk to the calves"); 2.5 GCC "পুন তনু মোড়ি" ("stretched His body"). Not in the BR, PK, KGC, PS
or the Gita-candrodaya. B.
[^5]: The hobbling rope (ছাঁদন-ডোর) is used to tie a cow's hind legs at milking. Woken by the lowing of the cows, the Lord is in the mood of a
cowherd boy of Vraja: He hurries to the cows and calls the companions by their "former names", their names in Krishna's lila (thus Gauridasa
Pandita is Subala, Krishna's friend, according to Kavi Karnapura's Gaura-ganoddesha-dipika). Here too the Lord's sleep is absorption in the
lilas of Vraja (note 36 of portion 22).

## 3. Narahari — "pekhaha gauracandra aparūpa…" (GPT1 p. 350, no. 20; GCC)
*Morning: Gaurachandra, still drowsy, sits on a jewelled seat; the radiance of His body fills the three worlds and rises in the hearts of His
own; His smile is lovelier than a forest of lotuses, His sleepy eyes are reddish; jasmine flowers fall from His loosened hair like stars to
the earth. Raga not stated. **Attribution: probably Chakravarti** — the pada is in the GCC; Vrajabuli.*[^6]

**3.1.** Behold the wondrous Gaurachandra!
On a gleaming lovely seat of jewels sits the incomparable king of Kamadevas.

**3.2.** His beautiful body crushes the pride of the peaks of the mountain of the gods; His mighty radiance has filled the three worlds;
ceaselessly rising in the hearts of His own, He crushes the pride of millions of suns and moons.

**3.3.** His lovely face with its gentle smile — a forest of blossoming lotuses is no match for it;
His reddish eyes droop in deep drowsiness — and destroy the shame, fear and family honour of young women.[^7]

**3.4.** From His loosened hair fall jasmine flowers — as if stars, leaving the sky, were falling to the earth.
What poet will describe this wondrous pose? Narahari again and again offers himself for it.

[^6]: GPT1 — p. 350, no. 20 ("যথারাগ"; scan, PDF p. 609); GPT2 — section 5.2, no. 20 (catalogue: NG-178). GCC — kirana 3, pada 12 (before it the GCC has
pada 11 "গুণমণি গৌরচন্দ্র দ্বিজরাজ", which is not in the GPT: Gaura comes out of the chamber and sits on the seat). Variants: 3.1 GPT1 "বিলপিত" (a
misprint), GCC "বিলসত"; 3.3 GCC "নাশত যুবতি-লাজ-ভয়-কুল". Not in the BR, PK, KGC, PS or the Gita-candrodaya. B. "The mountain of the gods" is
Sumeru, the golden mountain; "the king of Kamadevas" (মনমথ-ভূপ) — an image of beauty surpassing that of the god of love.
[^7]: The line repeats a common formula of love poetry about the all-attractive beauty of Krishna. We translate faithfully to the author; but
the Gaudiya tradition (Vrindavana Dasa, Krishnadasa Kaviraja, Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati) does not accept the worship of Gaura in the mood of
"Gaura-nagari": Gaura is Krishna Himself in the mood and lustre of Radha; He came to taste Her love, not to enjoy as a nagara. The Lord's
attractiveness for the people of Navadvipa is the quality of Bhagavan, who attracts all; it is not worldly passion. The same holds for the other
places of the portion speaking of the young women of Nadia (no. 22, v. 22.2). GPT pada no. 39 ("নদীয়ার শশী রঙ্গে রাজপথে": a young woman "has drowned in
the rasa of Narahari's Lord and given up shame and family"; = BR 12, vv. 2380–2383) is in nagari-bhava and is set aside for the section of nagari
padas. "Offers himself for it" (নিরমঞ্ছন) — the rite of "warding off" (nirmanchhana): the lover waves something around the beloved and gives it
(or himself) away for him, averting misfortune.

## 4. Narahari — "śrīśacībhavane adhika sukha āja…" (GPT1 p. 350, no. 21; GCC)
*Morning in Shachi's house: Gaura sits on a seat, around Him circle upon circle of companions, like gods around Sumeru; someone, leaning on
another's hand, stands still as a painting; someone's cloth is slipping; someone, startled, has run from his bed with sleepy eyes — all
drink, like bees, the honey of His face. Raga lalita. **Attribution: probably Chakravarti** — the pada is in the GCC; Vrajabuli.*[^8]

**4.1.** Great joy today in the house of Sri Shachi:
on an incomparable seat sits the beautiful Gaurachandra, king of the twice-born.

**4.2.** Around the Lord — circle upon circle of dear companions, wondrously beautiful,
like the gods surrounding Mount Sumeru: even Shesha cannot describe this beauty.

**4.3.** Someone, leaning his hand on another's hand, stands still like a painted doll;
someone's cloth is slipping, and he does not set it right — what feeling is he experiencing?

**4.4.** Someone, startled, has left his bed and come — his eyes still drooping in deep drowsiness.
Narahari says: they are like bees drunk on the honey of this lotus-face.

[^8]: GPT1 — p. 350, no. 21 ("ললিত"; scan, PDF p. 609); GPT2 — section 5.2, no. 21. **The catalogue did not know it** (new row NG-296). GCC — kirana 4
("the Lord surrounded by devotees"), pada 2 (pada 1 of kirana 4 is a list of companions signed Ghanashyama). Variants: 4.3 GCC "কৈছন ভাব ন
অনুভব হোয়" ("what feeling — it cannot be grasped"); 4.4 GCC "মুখ পঙ্কজ-মধুপানে". Not in the BR, PK, KGC, PS or the Gita-candrodaya. B. Shesha
(Ananta) is the thousand-mouthed serpent who eternally sings the Lord's glories.

## 5. Narahari — "āju ānanda parabhāta śacī-aṅganahi…" (GPT1 p. 350, no. 22; GCC)
*A joyful morning in Shachi's courtyard: the companions come and go, sing, dance, whisper and laugh, embrace and bow to one another, ask
whether the night passed well, call one another blessed; someone recites verses, someone with a sigh takes a blade of grass between his
teeth; the companions are countless — like a surging ocean of joy. Raga not stated. **Attribution: probably Chakravarti** — the pada is in
the GCC; Vrajabuli.*[^9]

**5.1.** Today a joyful morning in Shachi's courtyard — unending love, new delights of every kind!
Someone comes and goes, someone sings a lovely raga, dances wondrously, wanders drunk with rasa.

**5.2.** Someone, putting his lips to another's ear, softly says many words — and, laughing, collapses, unable to stand;
someone calls another and embraces him; someone bows down at someone's feet.

**5.3.** Someone asks another whether the night passed well; someone says: "Now all is well — from seeing the Lord";
someone says to another: "Blessed are you, blessed! Sorrow will go at the mere touch of the breeze from your body."

**5.4.** Someone recites verses and prose; someone with a sigh takes a blade of grass between his teeth;
the companions are countless — as if an ocean of joy had surged: how can Narahari describe it with one mouth?[^10]

[^9]: GPT1 — p. 350, no. 22 ("যথারাগ"; scan, PDF p. 609); GPT2 — section 5.2, no. 22 (catalogue: NG-179). GCC — kirana 4, pada 3 (the search through
the witnesses missed it because of the garbled OCR; found by reading the GCC continuously). Variants: 5.1 GCC "ভঙ্গ হু নেহ" (OCR unclear); 5.2 GCC
"কহু বচন"; 5.4 GCC "কুতকারি" (OCR). Not in the BR, PK, KGC, PS or the Gita-candrodaya. B.
[^10]: A blade of grass between the teeth is a sign of humility: so one begs for mercy in Bengal. The Lord's companions honour one another
("blessed are you!") — for each of them is His eternal intimate.

## 6. Narahari — "ki kahaba ājuka aparūpa raṅga…" (GPT1 p. 351, no. 23; GCC)
*The courtyard of Shachi's house: Gaurahari and the companions are like a host of moons in a clear starless sky, which will dispel the
darkness of the threefold miseries; like an arena of mighty wrestlers, who will overcome the asuras averse to the Lord's feet; like a field
of blossoming wish-fulfilling trees giving priceless fruits — no one will be left without. Raga not stated. **Attribution: probably
Chakravarti** — the pada is in the GCC; Vrajabuli.*[^11]

**6.1.** What shall I say of today's wondrous delight!
In the midst of the spacious courtyard shine Gaurahari and His dear companions, inseparable from Him.

**6.2.** Is it a host of moons shining brightly in the clear starless [sky]?
Surely they will destroy the dreadful, densest darkness of the world's threefold miseries.

**6.3.** Is it an arena that removes the burden of the fear of worldly existence — a lovely assembly of mighty wrestlers?
Having overcome the invincible asuras averse to the Lord's feet, they will surely accomplish their work.

**6.4.** Is it a field free of every obstacle, where wish-fulfilling trees have appeared and blossomed?
They will give away incomparable, priceless fruits; Narahari says: surely no one will be left without.

[^11]: GPT1 — p. 351, no. 23 ("যথারাগ"; scan, PDF p. 610); GPT2 — section 5.2, no. 23 (catalogue: NG-180). GCC — kirana 4, pada 4. Variants: 6.2 GCC
"উডুগণ-বিহীন বিমল আকাশ কিয়ে…" (the GPT lacks the word "আকাশ", "sky"; supplied in brackets); 6.4 GCC "ফিরে" for "কিয়ে" (OCR). Not in the BR,
PK, KGC, PS or the Gita-candrodaya. B. The threefold miseries are those from one's own body and mind, from other beings and from the forces of
nature. "The asuras averse to the Lord's feet" — the godlessness of the age of Kali; the companions are warriors of sankirtana who conquer it with
the holy name; "the wish-fulfilling trees" — the companions who give everyone the fruit of prema (cf. CC Adi 9: the Lord is the gardener and the
tree of prema).

## 7. Narahari — "gorācāṅdera kibā e līlā…" (GPT1 p. 351, no. 26; BR)
*The Lord in the mood of His former lila: as in Vraja He stole the gopis' clothes, so now, glancing at His dear companions, He playfully takes
away their clothes and then, His purpose fulfilled, gives them all back. Raga shri. **Attribution: probably Chakravarti** — the pada is in the
BR; plain Bengali.*[^12]

**7.1.** What a lila of Gorachand!
Once He stole the gopis' clothes — now He is lost in that mood.

**7.2.** Glancing at His dear companions,
He playfully takes away their clothes — who knows the secret of this?[^13]

**7.3.** As if everything had become as it was then;
having reached the limit of joy and fulfilled His purpose, He gives everyone back their clothes.

**7.4.** Seeing this, Narahari-dasa says:
who in the world would not go mad singing this lovely deed?

[^12]: GPT1 — p. 351, no. 26 ("শ্রীরাগ"; scan, PDF p. 610); GPT2 — section 5.2, no. 26 (catalogue: NG-181). BR 12 (ed. 1912, p. 946; ed. 1960, vv. 3358–3361):
Ishana shows Srinivasa the place where "Gaurachandra manifested His former lila — He stole the clothes of the devotees" (v. 3355), and quotes Murari
Gupta's verses about it (in our translation, Murari 2.10.16–17: "at the beginning of the night" the Lord pulled the clothes off His servants, filled
with bhava, and gave them back a moment later). Variants: 7.1 GPT1 "গারাচাঁদের" (a misprint), GPT1 and GPT2 "বিভোল হৈলা", BR "বিহ্বল হৈলা"; 7.3 BR "যেন
হইল সকলি সেই". Not in the PK, KGC, PS, GCC or the Gita-candrodaya. B.
[^13]: The stealing of the clothes (চীর-হরণ) is Krishna's pastime in Vraja (Bhag. 10.22): by carrying off the clothes of the bathing gopis He
fulfilled their vow and desire and removed the last barrier between them and Himself; it is a play of pure prema, not a worldly prank. Here the
Lord repeats it with the devotees in whom the mood of the gopis has awakened (cf. Murari 2.10.15–17) — this is the "secret" of the lila the poet
has in mind.

## 8. Narahari — "suradhunītīre kata raṅge…" (GPT1 pp. 351–352, no. 27; BR)
*Midday on the bank of the Ganga: Srivasa and Murari have food brought; Advaita seats everyone in a circle on palasha leaves; Nitai, forgetting
himself, feeds Gora sweets, Advaita gives Nitai a drink, Nitai puts sweets into Advaita's mouth; the companions merrily feed one another and
snatch food from one another's leaves, and drink the water of the Ganga; Narahari carefully gathers the remnants. Raga saranga. **Attribution:
probably Chakravarti** — the pada is in the BR.*[^14]

**8.1.** On the bank of the Ganga, in many delights,
Gaura wanders with His dear companions.

**8.2.** Two praharas of the day have passed —
who knows what was then in the Lord's mind?

**8.3.** At that hour Srivasa and Murari
had all kinds of food brought on platters.

**8.4.** The Moon of Nadia rejoices,
gazing with a gentle smile at the Lord of Sita.

**8.5.** Advaita, with great joy in his heart,
seated everyone — how lovely! — in a circle.

**8.6.** Spreading palasha leaves there,
he serves everyone all kinds of dishes.

**8.7.** Given leave to eat,
all gaze steadily at Gora's face.

**8.8.** Nitai cannot contain himself:
love surges in his heart — who can fathom it?

**8.9.** Sweet rice, cream, fresh butter, curd cheese
he puts into Gora's mouth and forgets himself.

**8.10.** Advaita with his own hand
gives Nitaichand a curd drink.

**8.11.** Lovely Nitai, the mighty,
puts sweet balls into Advaita's mouth.

**8.12.** Their bodies thrilled with rapture —
what merriment arose among the companions!

**8.13.** Someone eats after first putting food into another's mouth,
someone snatches [food] from another's leaf.

**8.14.** There are sweets of many kinds —
eating them, everyone's joy grew boundless.

**8.15.** Handful after brimming handful
all drink the cool water of the Ganga.

**8.16.** Whatever was left on the leaves
Narahari-dasa carefully gathered.[^15]

[^14]: GPT1 — pp. 351–352, no. 27 ("সারঙ্গ"; scan, PDF pp. 610–611); GPT2 — section 5.2, no. 27 (catalogue: NG-182). BR 12 (ed. 1912, pp. 937–938; ed. 1960,
vv. 3241–3256): Ishana shows Srinivasa the bank of the Ganga where "the Lord with His companions holds a vana-bhojana" (a meal in the open, v. 3240).
Variants: 8.3 BR-1960 "প্রবাস মুরারি" (OCR; GPT1 on the scan "শ্রীবাস মুরারি"); 8.8 BR "কিবা মেহা" for "কিবা লেহা"; 8.9 BR "ক্ষীর সর নবনীত ছেনা"; 8.10 BR
"ছেনা পানা". Not in the PK, KGC, PS, GCC or the Gita-candrodaya. B. Two praharas are half the daylight (a prahara is a quarter of the day): in the
ashtakaliya this is midday, the time of Gaura's pastimes on the bank of the Ganga (below, no. 18, v. 18.4).
[^15]: The meal of the Lord and His companions is prasada; the remnants from the leaves of the Lord and His eternal companions are mahaprasada,
precious to a devotee: so the poet gathers them "carefully" (cf. CC Antya 16 on the glory of the remnants of the Vaishnavas' meals). Srivasa,
Murari, Advaita ("the Lord of Sita"), Nitai (Nityananda) are the Lord's closest companions in Navadvipa. The curd drink (ছানা-পানা) is curd cheese
mashed with water and sugar.

## 9. Narahari — "āju gorā parikara saṅge…" (GPT1 p. 352, no. 28; BR)
*After the meal Gora wanders with His companions along the bank of the Ganga; standing in the shade of a tall tree, He says something sweet
and gazes at the Ganga, takes Gadai by the hand and gently smiles. Raga saranga. **Attribution: probably Chakravarti** — the pada is in the
BR.*[^16]

**9.1.** Today Gora with His companions,
having finished the merry meal, joyfully wanders along the bank of the Ganga.

**9.2.** Standing in the shade of a very tall tree,
saying something sweet, He gazes again and again at the Ganga.
