# Preface

## About this book

A pada is a short song sung in kirtana; at its close the poet usually names himself (the signature, *bhanita*). Sri
Chaitanya Mahaprabhu (1486–1533) was surrounded by poets: Vasu, Madhava and Govinda Ghosha sang before Him in Navadvipa
and Puri, and Narahari Sarkar, Murari Gupta, Ramananda Vasu and others made songs about His lilas while He was still
present. These padas are the earliest witnesses to the Lord in Bengali, and in them one hears the living voice of His
companions: the rapture of the Navadvipa kirtanas, the grief of Nadia after the sannyasa, the longing for Nilachala.

The padas lie scattered through later anthologies. We have gathered those which research (above all Bimanbihari
Majumdar, *Sri-Chaitanya-chariter upadan*, ch. 2) assigns to Mahaprabhu's contemporaries, established their text from
several editions and translated them into Russian and English — 754 padas in all. The book has three parts.

- **Part I. Padas of the Contemporaries** (chapters 1–16) — the core of the collection: padas signed by the Lord's
  contemporaries, from the *Padakalpataru*, the *Bhakti-ratnakara*, the *Gaura-pada-tarangini* and other collections;
  first the padas Majumdar dates to the Lord's lifetime, then by poets and themes.
- **Part II. Appendix: Padas Signed "Narahari" from the *Gaura-pada-tarangini*** (chapters 17–26). Two poets sang
  under this name: Narahari Sarkar of Srikhanda, the Lord's companion, and Narahari Chakravarti (18th c.), author of the
  *Bhakti-ratnakara*. Whose each pada is, is stated in its heading; how we distinguish the two poets is explained in the
  introductory note to chapter 17.
- **Part III. Nagari Padas** (chapters 27–34) — songs in which the women of Nadia speak of Gaura in amorous feeling
  (*nagari-bhava*). They are translated in full and faithfully to their authors, but the Gaudiya Vaishnava tradition —
  Vrindavana Dasa, Krishnadasa Kaviraja, Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati — does not accept the worship of Gaura in this
  mood: Gaura is Krishna Himself in the mood and lustre of Radha, come to taste Her love as a devotee, not to enjoy. This
  is set out in the introductory note to chapter 27, and every sharp passage has a short note. Concluding notes to the
  section stand at the end of chapters 33 and 34.

## Sources

The text of the padas is established from editions in the public domain; where a pada is found in several
collections, the base text follows the oldest or most correct one, and the variants are given in the notes and in the
Russian full edition.

- **PK** — *Padakalpataru* of Gokulananda Sena (Vaishnavadasa), 18th c.; ed. Satishchandra Ray, Bangiya Sahitya
  Parishat, 1915–1931, vols. 1–5 (the pada number is the running number of this edition; a "pallava" is its section).
  Ray records manuscript readings; their sigla are in the list of abbreviations.
- **BR** — *Bhakti-ratnakara* of Narahari Chakravarti (18th c.); eds. 1888, 1912/1913, 1960 (Gaudiya Mission), 2006.
  Its chapters are "waves" (*taranga*).
- **GPT1, GPT2** — *Gaura-pada-tarangini*, compiled by Jagadbandhu Bhadra: 1st ed. 1903, 2nd ed. 1934.
- **PS** — *Padamrita-samudra* of Radhamohana Thakura (18th c.); ed. 1878.
- **KGC** — *Kshanada-gita-chintamani* of Vishvanatha Chakravarti (17th–18th c.); ed. 1925.
- **GCC** — *Gaura-charita-chintamani* of Narahari Chakravarti; ed. Haridasa Dasa, 1947. Its chapters are "kiranas"
  (rays).
- **GCd** — *Gita-chandrodaya* of Narahari Chakravarti; ed. 1948.

For dating and attribution we have consulted Majumdar, but his arguments are given only briefly, in our own words; the
editors' prefaces and notes are not translated.

## Principles of the translation

**Faithfulness to the author.** Each pada is translated as it stands — line for line, without rhyme, keeping the
song's images and tone; nothing is added and nothing softened. Where an author speaks otherwise than the Gaudiya
Vaishnava tradition teaches, the translation stays exact and a note explains how the passage is to be understood. The
measure of interpretation is Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati.

**Theology.** The Lord is not subject to maya (His "maya" is yogamaya, His internal energy); He is not born but
appears; the Deity is the Lord Himself, not an image; the prema of Radha and the gopis and the Lord's bhava are not
worldly passion. Pronouns referring to the Lord are capitalised (Me, My, Mine; "I" is unchanged), also in His own
speech and in speech in the mood of Radha.

**Attribution and authority.** The heading of each pada gives its source, its lila and, where needed, its attribution.
We grade the reliability of the attribution on a scale A–D:

- **A** — a contemporary of the Lord with a reliable signature (confirmed by several witnesses or by Majumdar);
- **B** — a recognised later author of established authorship (above all the padas of Narahari Chakravarti from his
  BR, GCC and GCd: a later poetic elaboration of the lila accepted by the tradition), or a contemporary with a probable
  but not certain attribution;
- **C** — author not established or attribution disputed (the pada is known only from GPT, the signatures differ
  between sources, it is unclear which of the two Naraharis is meant);
- **D** — authenticity doubtful (a forgery, an anachronism, another's couplet bearing the signature).

The heading marks "**probably Sarkar**", "**probably Chakravarti**", "**not established**" concern the padas signed
"Narahari" and say to which of the two poets we incline.

**Numbering.** The padas in each chapter are numbered; a couplet (in a tripadi, a stanza) is marked "pada.couplet":
**1.1**, **1.2**, … **2.1**. The refrain (*dhruva*) is marked "(Refrain)" and counts as a separate unit; the kirtana
singers' insertions in some padas of Lochana Dasa are given in brackets. Notes are numbered within each chapter; a
reference "ch. 22, no. 7" means pada 7 of chapter 22, "n. 36 of chapter 22" — note 36 of that chapter. Labels like
VG-…, NG-…, NS-…, LD-… are the numbers of our working catalogue of padas (from the initials of the poet or section);
they are kept in the notes so that a pada can be found in the project materials.

**Transliteration.** Bengali words are given in IAST, with the inherent vowel written in full; ব is rendered *v*
(but *b* in the clusters ম্ব, ব্দ, ব্ধ, ব্জ), ঁ is *m̐*, ং is *ṃ*, ড় is *ṛ*, য় is *y*.

## How to read the full version

The book appears in two forms. The **standard** version gives the translation with notes. In the **full** version
each couplet is preceded by:

1. the Bengali original (medieval spelling; Vrajabuli as in the source);
2. a transliteration in IAST;
3. a word-for-word translation;
4. the translation.

The readings of the witnesses are recorded in the notes; their full apparatus appears in the Russian full edition.
