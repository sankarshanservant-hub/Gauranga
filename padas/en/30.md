# Nagari Padas — IV: the "Narahari" padas of GPT (sect. 3.2, nos. 94–110, 120–123)

*On what nagari-bhava is, the position of the tradition (Vrindavana Dasa, Krishnadasa Kaviraja, Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati) and the rules of translation, see the introductory note to the section of nagari padas in chapter 27; on the two poets named Narahari (Narahari Sarakara and Narahari Chakravarti) and on the attribution labels ("probably Sarakara", "probably Chakravarti", "not established"), see the introductory note to the "Narahari" appendix in chapter 17.*

## 1. Narahari — "śuna śuna ago parāṇa soi…" (GPT1 p. 194, no. 94; GCC 7.10)
*Nagari-bhava. A townswoman complains to her friend about her household: whenever she sighs and murmurs "Gora", her mother-in-law burns with
anger, her sister-in-law is like poison, the neighbours taunt her day and night, she lives like a bird in a cage; but yesterday the Creator took
pity — at the close of day Gaura passed along their street, and she secretly saw Him from the window: His face, brows, eyes, garland, gait; and
the pain of her heart only doubled. Raga "yatharaga". **Attribution: probably Chakravarti** — GCC, kirana 7, pada 10.*[^1]

**1.1.** Listen, listen, friend of my heart: I know you feel my pain — so to you I will tell it.

**1.2.** The ways of our house are unheard of in the land: as I tell them, my heart weeps.

**1.3.** If only I sigh, murmuring "Gora", the old woman hears and burns with anger.

**1.4.** My sister-in-law is like deadly poison: through her "virtues" my life is burnt away.

**1.5.** The neighbours are nothing but thorns to a good name: day and night they prick me with taunts.

**1.6.** Whom, friend, shall I call to witness this? I stay at home like a bird in a cage.

**1.7.** What more shall I tell of all this? There is no end to my sorrow.

**1.8.** But yesterday the Creator was kind to me — he put the moon of the sky into my hand.

**1.9.** At the close of day Gaura Raya was walking along our street.

**1.10.** Hurrying to the window, unseen, I saw Him.

**1.11.** How sweet is that moon of a face — a snare for the hearts of young women!

**1.12.** The curve of His brows is so graceful — who has the composure to bear it?

**1.13.** His eyes, outdoing wagtails, dance — I understood: from them no one escapes.

**1.14.** A garland of flowers sways at His throat — seeing it, many a Kamadeva swoons.[^2]

**1.15.** What more shall I say of that wondrous beauty? His gait enchants the world.

**1.16.** For only a moment could I see Him there — and the pain of my heart grew twofold.

**1.17.** Narahari says: sorrow will not remain — all will come to pass as your heart desires.[^3]

p. 73; scan PDF p. 78). Nos. 94–99 of this chapter continue the 7th kirana of GCC — the townswomen of Navadvipa telling their friends in the morning
(beginning — chapter 29, nos. 14–21): GCC 7.10–7.12 = nos. 94–96, 7.14–7.16 = nos. 97–99; GCC 7.13 (how a townswoman remembered Gaura while cooking)
was not taken into GPT. GCC pp. 73–80 were checked against the scan (PDF pp. 78–85; re-OCRed with tesseract); pp. 81–88 (GCC 7.17–7.28) are missing
from the only available scan (see n. 18). GCC readings: "parāṇa sai" (so also GPT2), "deya e khom̐ṭā", "bhuruyuga vara bhaṅgima chānde". Metre — short payar (a couplet to a
line). B. Nagari-bhava — see the introductory note to the section (chapter 27).
[^2]: "Many a Kamadeva swoons" (mūrache kateka kāma) — the usual praise in the padas of the Lord's beauty, surpassing that of the god of love
(cf. Krishna's name Madana-mohana, "He who enchants Madana himself").
[^3]: The poet's consolation is a convention of the purvaraga padas; in the GCC padas the fulfilment of the wish is a darshan of Gaura seen from
afar (cf. nos. 2–3 and 5).

## 2. Narahari — "ki valiva ago gharera kathā…" (GPT1 pp. 195–196, no. 95; GCC 7.11)
*Nagari-bhava. A townswoman's story: yesterday morning her mother-in-law went out, leaving her with her sister-in-law; the latter picked a quarrel
and went off in a huff to the neighbours; alone at the window, the townswoman saw Gaura passing and threw Him a withered kumuda bud; Gora, taking
it, said to the flower: "Do not grieve — the moon will rise"; at His glance she lost all strength; to her mother-in-law, on her return, she
complained of her sister-in-law and explained her agitation by fatigue and fear — and the mother-in-law comforted her and scolded the
sister-in-law. Raga "yatharaga". **Attribution: probably Chakravarti** — GCC, kirana 7, pada 11.*[^4]

**2.1.** "What shall I say, friend, of what goes on in our house? If you hear it, it will pain you too.

**2.2.** Yesterday, as soon as night gave way to a bright morning, I secretly saw the Moon Gaura.

**2.3.** Perish shame now — what can it do to me? Something of it I will tell you.

**2.4.** Leaving me with my sister-in-law, my mother-in-law went off to the other end of the village.

**2.5.** And here my sister-in-law picked a quarrel and said many bad things to me,

**2.6.** and, to have her own way, she dropped everything and went off in a huff to someone else's house.

**2.7.** I stayed at home alone; I went and sat by the window.

**2.8.** Thinking of Gaura's beauty and virtues, I kept my eyes on the road.

**2.9.** And at that very moment Gauranga, the dear friend, appeared in our street.

**2.10.** Gazing unseen at that moonlike face, I forgot a little of the sorrow of my heart.

**2.11.** At once I threw a withered kumuda bud through the window.

**2.12.** Seeing it, Gora — and He is very quick-witted — took it in His hand and says, speaking to the kumuda:

**2.13.** 'Do not grieve: the moon will rise, and the heat of the sun will go far away.'[^5]

**2.14.** Having said this, smiling, He glanced just once from the corner of His eye towards me.

**2.15.** At once my body lost all strength, as if a deadly snake had bitten me.

**2.16.** Try as I might, I could not keep my composure: I longed to go and touch Him.[^6]

**2.17.** I tremble all over, my body sweats; I rise to walk — my feet will not move.

**2.18.** What shall I say? Trying to calm my heart, I remained in great agitation.

**2.19.** Just then my mother-in-law came home and asks me: 'Why are you like this?'

**2.20.** Very piteously I told her: 'My sister-in-law will not let me live in the house.

**2.21.** When you yourself are at home, she says nothing, but her heart burns like fire.

**2.22.** You left the house to go there — and here she picked a quarrel with me.

**2.23.** There is no end to the telling of it; and there was no fault of mine in it at all.

**2.24.** If you have any doubt in your mind, ask the neighbours here.

**2.25.** What shall I say! Leaving me alone, my sister-in-law went off to someone else's house —

**2.26.** judge from this how much sense she has. And why I am like this — listen:

**2.27.** besides being afraid all alone, I was also thinking of the housework;

**2.28.** whatever I did, I grew very tired, and that is why my head was spinning.'

**2.29.** Hearing my faltering words, my mother-in-law tenderly laid her hand on my head,

**2.30.** fanned me with the end of her own cloth, took my hands and comforted me at length.

**2.31.** Calling my sister-in-law, she scolded her — and hearing that, I was delighted."

**2.32.** Narahari says: you are truly fortunate — who else knows such tricks?[^7]

[^4]: Signature — "Narahari". GPT1 — pp. 195–196, no. 95 (scan PDF pp. 454–455); GPT2 — sect. 3.2, no. 95 (catalogue: NG-087). GCC — kirana 7, pada 11
(pp. 73–75; scan PDF pp. 78–80). GCC readings: "henai samaye gaurāṅga ekā" (GPT1 "gaurāṅgasakhā"; the GCC reading, "Gauranga alone", rhymes better with
dekhā), "nija kara dila āmāri māthe". Short payar. On 2.13 GPT1 has an editor's footnote with an interpretation (not reproduced). B. Nagari-bhava —
see the introductory note to the section (chapter 27).
[^5]: The kumuda is the white night-lily: it opens at moonrise and closes under the sun; in poetry it is an image of the loving woman pining for
her moon-beloved. The withered bud thrown from the window is the townswoman's mute sign ("I wither without my moon"); Gora answers the flower, not
her: "the moon will rise, and the heat of the sun will go." The moon (śaśī, cām̐da) is a constant name of Gaura in the GCC padas ("the Moon of
Nadia"), so the answer is both consolation and a promise of darshan; translated literally, without interpretation.
[^6]: The longing "to go and touch" and the signs of agitation (loss of strength, trembling, sweat) are stated plainly in the original; translated
exactly. These are formulas of purvaraga shared with the padas on the gopis; the Lord merely passes along the street and answers the flower. The
tradition does not accept worship of Gaura in the nagari mood (introductory note, chapter 27).
[^7]: The deception of the mother-in-law (the complaint against the sister-in-law, "fatigue" instead of the true cause) and the poet's praise of
the "tricks" are rendered as in the original — a poetic convention of the purvaraga padas (cf. n. 47 of chapter 29).

## 3. Narahari — "śuna go sajani valie tore…" (GPT1 p. 196, no. 96; GCC 7.12)
*Nagari-bhava. A townswoman's story: putting on a new sari, she went alone to her brother's house and on the way met Gora; at His glance she lost
her composure and barely made it home; her mother-in-law, seeing tears in her eyes, asked what had happened — "I went out of the gate and saw a
terrible snake"; the frightened mother-in-law splashed water on her face. Raga "yatharaga". **Attribution: probably Chakravarti** — GCC, kirana 7,
pada 12.*[^8]

**3.1.** "Listen, friend, I will tell you: I do not know what has come over me.

**3.2.** Hastily putting on a new sari, I set out alone for my brother's house.

**3.3.** On the way I met Gora — what shall I say? There is no measure to His beauty.

**3.4.** He glanced just once towards me — and I do not know what He did with the corner of His eye:

**3.5.** composure, dharma, modesty — all there was perished at that very moment.

**3.6.** My heart became I know not how — I reason with it and cannot calm it.

**3.7.** In agitation I walk — my feet will not move; my whole body trembles and trembles.

**3.8.** Again and again my clothes slip from my body — and He, seeing all this, smiles.[^9]

**3.9.** What could I do? For fear of the elders I somehow made my way home.

**3.10.** And there again my body languishes without the sight of that beautiful Gaura.

**3.11.** Just at that moment my mother-in-law came, sat down beside me and asks:

**3.12.** 'Why are you like this today? Your eyes are brimming with tears.

**3.13.** You speak so piteously — I do not know what pain has come to you.'

**3.14.** Hearing this, I told her: 'I had gone out of the gate,

**3.15.** and there I saw a terrible snake — my heart shook, all my courage was gone.

**3.16.** Then it slid away into the ditch — I was saved by the power of your feet.'

**3.17.** Hearing this, she grew very alarmed and herself splashed water on my eyes and face."[^10]

**3.18.** Narahari says: you fear nothing — you certainly know how to fool your mother-in-law!

[^8]: Signature — "Narahari". GPT1 — p. 196, no. 96 (scan PDF p. 455); GPT2 — sect. 3.2, no. 96 (catalogue: NG-088). GCC — kirana 7, pada 12 (p. 75; scan PDF
p. 80). GCC has two extra couplets: after 3.15 — "se punaḥ mo pāne āila dhāiyā | palāinu mui vyākula haiyā ||" ("It rushed at me — I fled in
alarm"), after 3.17 — "nija kara dhari āmāra śire | māne śive kata manera ḍare ||" ("Laying her hand on my head, she in fear vowed many offerings to
Shiva"); the translation follows GPT1. Short payar. B. Nagari-bhava — see the introductory note to the section (chapter 27).
[^9]: The slipping clothes are a sign of agitation, as in the padas on the gopis (cf. n. 58 of chapter 29); the original states it plainly, translated
exactly. The Lord's smile is the same as in no. 2 (2.14): He merely passes along the road. The tradition does not accept the image of Gaura as
nagara (introductory note, chapter 27).
[^10]: "A terrible snake" is an ambiguous answer: in no. 2 (2.15) Gaura's glance stung the townswoman "as if a deadly snake had bitten" her. The
deception of the mother-in-law and the poet's praise (3.18) are a poetic convention of the purvaraga padas (cf. n. 47 of chapter 29).

## 4. Narahari — "nanadī vicāra kariyā garave…" (GPT1 pp. 196–197, no. 97; GCC 7.14)
*Nagari-bhava. A townswoman's story: her sister-in-law, having planned mischief, dressed up and went for water, leaving her alone at home; on the
road she met Gaura with His companions and, forgetting all her schemes, followed Him with the pot on her hip; the mother-in-law caught her so and
snatched away the pot — while the daughter-in-law meanwhile saw Gaura on her own road; the mother-in-law praised her, and the sister-in-law was
put to shame. Raga "yatharaga". **Attribution: probably Chakravarti** — GCC, kirana 7, pada 14 (second half).*[^11]

**4.1.** "Having thought it all out, my sister-in-law proudly put on a new sari
and went to fetch water, leaving me alone at home.

**4.2.** Gleeful, she hurried along her road —
and along that very road, with His companions, comes Gaura Raya.

**4.3.** Seeing the sweetness of that form, my sister-in-law quite lost her head:
all the schemes she had in mind she clean forgot.

**4.4.** Along that road came my mother-in-law — and, looking, sees her close by:
with the pot on her hip she is wandering after Gaurangachand.[^12]

**4.5.** 'Very good, very good!' she said in great anger, and snatched away the pot;
what could my sister-in-law say, and to whom? — she wished the earth would swallow her.

**4.6.** And I meanwhile, meeting beautiful Gauranga, my life, on my own road,
eased the pain of my heart gazing at that moonlike face.

**4.7.** A little later my mother-in-law came and praised me at length;
and what shall I say of my sister-in-law's shame — she is at home as if she were not there."

**4.8.** Narahari says: one who is a fool sees nothing:
tying her own fault in the end of her sari, she seeks to blame another.

[^11]: Signature — "Narahari". GPT1 — pp. 196–197, no. 97 (scan PDF pp. 455–456); GPT2 — sect. 3.2, no. 97 (catalogue: NG-089). GCC — kirana 7, pada 14
(pp. 77–79; scan PDF pp. 82–84). In GCC the pada is twice as long: GPT begins it from the middle (GCC "erūpa vicāra kariyā garave" — "having thus
thought it out…"; GPT "nanadī vicāra kariyā"). In the omitted first half (13 couplets, pp. 77–78) the townswoman tells how her sister-in-law and
her friends plotted to disgrace her: she told the mother-in-law that the bride would bring shame on the house with "a brahmana's son" (a hint at
Gaura); the mother-in-law replied that she would believe it only when she saw it with her own eyes; then the sister-in-law resolved to leave the
bride alone, on her way to fetch water to send a young man she knew to the house, and then to call the neighbours and expose her. The translation
follows GPT1; the omitted part is not translated (the text is that of GPT), only retold here. GCC readings in the second half: "prāṇa-gaurāṅgasundare",
"praśaṃsā karaye more". B. Nagari-bhava — see the introductory note to the section (chapter 27).
[^12]: The strict sister-in-law who forgets her schemes at the sight of Gaura is a recurrent motif of GCC (cf. chapter 29, no. 21): even the
townswomen's adversaries lose their composure on seeing Gaura. The Lord here merely walks along the road with His companions.

## 5. Narahari — "ki valiva sakhi kakhana saphala…" (GPT1 p. 197, no. 98; GCC 7.15)
*Nagari-bhava. A townswoman's story: her wish has never come true — the Creator has always put obstacles in the way; but yesterday, hearing that
Gaura was coming, she went up to the upper gallery and saw Him; the fragrance of His body reached her; Gora turned towards her — and just then her
mother-in-law called her; she went away, but the mother-in-law let her go for water. Raga "yatharaga". **Attribution: probably Chakravarti** —
GCC, kirana 7, pada 15.*[^13]

**5.1.** "What shall I say, friend? Never has the wish of my heart come true:
to make me suffer, the pitiless Creator has put many obstacles in the way.

**5.2.** But yesterday, truly, along our road came the Beloved of my life;
hearing of it from people, I took courage, went up and stood on the upper gallery.[^14]

**5.3.** Seeing the sweetness of that form, both my eyes were drowned in it;
I thought: if only I could become a bird and fly to Him!

**5.4.** The fragrance of His graceful body came and entered my nostrils;
I lost all calm — what shall I say? There was no end to my joy.

**5.5.** Gora turned and looked towards me — and at that very moment the old woman
keeps calling and calling me; my heart shook — I came away, leaving that joy.

**5.6.** She gave me leave to go for water — I floated on waters of joy."
Narahari says: such a mother-in-law is found only by great good fortune.

[^13]: Signature — "Narahari". GPT1 — p. 197, no. 98 (scan PDF p. 456); GPT2 — sect. 3.2, no. 98 (catalogue: NG-337). GCC — kirana 7, pada 15 (pp. 79–80; scan
PDF pp. 84–85); GCC readings: "loka mukhe śuni sāhase upara-dālāne dām̐ṛānu giyā", "bhāgyete mile". In letting her daughter-in-law go for water (to the
Ganga), the mother-in-law unknowingly lets her see Gaura again — hence the poet's playful praise. B. Nagari-bhava — see the introductory note to the
section (chapter 27).
[^14]: "The Beloved of my life" (parāṇa-piyā) — the heroine's word for Gaura, as in the padas on the gopis for Krishna (cf. n. 46 of chapter 29);
the translation is faithful to the author.

## 6. Narahari — "sajani, kata nā kahiva āmāra dukhera kāhinī kathā…" (GPT1 p. 197, no. 99; GCC 7.16)
*Nagari-bhava. A townswoman's story: her mother-in-law, hearing that Gaura was coming, hurried home, bolted the door and sat in the courtyard on
guard; the daughter-in-law pressed her eyes to a tiny window and saw Gaura among His companions; with a look she let Him know how it was with her;
when the mother-in-law unbolted the door, the daughter-in-law was already sitting by the hearth. Raga "yatharaga". **Attribution: probably
Chakravarti** — GCC, kirana 7, pada 16.*[^15]

**6.1.** "Friend, however much I tell, there is no end to my tale of woe;
but yesterday the merciful Creator relieved my pain a little.

**6.2.** Leaving me at the cooking, my mother-in-law had gone out of the house;
hearing that Gaura was coming, she came back to me at once.

**6.3.** Glancing at me once more, she bolted the door of the house,
sat down in the middle of the courtyard and kept watching warily on every side.

**6.4.** And here my heart is in turmoil — I see no way out;
there was a tiny window — to it I pressed both my eyes.

**6.5.** Among His companions — the Crown of connoisseurs of rasa; who can fathom His ways?
Glancing about quite unobtrusively, He walks with the gait of an elephant.

**6.6.** Beholding but once the sweetness of that form, meeting His eyes with mine,
I, as if catching His sign, let Him know what state I was in.

**6.7.** Coming level with me, the Jewel of the clever smiled a little and spoke;
and I sat down again at the cooking — and then my one-eyed mother-in-law unbolted the door.[^16]

**6.8.** Squinting sideways, she looked at me with her left eye and was reassured."
Narahari says: if that calamity of an eye were gone too, all would be well![^17]

[^15]: Signature — "Narahari". GPT1 — p. 197, no. 99 (scan PDF p. 456); GPT2 — sect. 3.2, no. 99 (catalogue: NG-090). GCC — kirana 7, pada 16 (p. 80; scan PDF
p. 85) — the last pada of GCC before the lacuna in the scan (pp. 81–88 are missing; the next surviving one is GCC 7.29 = GPT1 no. 124). GCC readings:
"āmāke randhane rākhiyā", "alapa gavākṣa āchila tāhāte sam̐pinu yugala ām̐khi", "calaye kuñjara-gati". B. Nagari-bhava — see the introductory note to the
section (chapter 27).
[^16]: "Crown of connoisseurs of rasa" (rasikaśekhara), "Jewel of the clever" (caturamaṇi), "the gait of an elephant" are epithets of Krishna
transferred by the poet to Gaura; what He said the pada does not tell. As in the other GCC padas, the Lord merely passes along the street among His
companions; all the rest takes place in the townswoman's heart.
[^17]: The poet's joke at the one-eyed (kāṇī) mother-in-law — literally "let that calamity of an eye be gone too, and it will be well"; translated
without softening.

## 7. Narahari — "ekadina āmi śāśuṛī nanadī vasiyāchi āṅgināya…" (GPT1 p. 198, no. 100)
*Nagari-bhava. A townswoman's comic story: once she, her mother-in-law and her sister-in-law were sitting in the courtyard when Gauranga passed by;
the sister-in-law, gazing at Him, so forgot herself that her clothes fell from her; the daughter-in-law went to dress her — and forgot herself
the same way; the mother-in-law came to rebuke them — and the same befell her; coming to their senses, the mother-in-law told her daughter-in-law
to tell no one. Raga "yatharaga". **Attribution: not established** — GPT only; not in the surviving pages of GCC (it may have stood on the lost
pp. 81–88).*[^18]

**7.1.** "One day we — I, my mother-in-law and my sister-in-law — were sitting in the courtyard;
through the back gate I looked out and saw: Gauranga Raya is passing.

**7.2.** As befits a modest woman, I drew my veil and stayed seated;
but my sister-in-law, first of all, intoxicated by Madana, stood up, smiling and smiling.

**7.3.** Gazing and gazing through the window, she began to look at Gora;
seeing that her clothes had come loose, my mother-in-law shouted at her.

**7.4.** Overpowered, gazing at Gora's form, my sister-in-law did not hear that shout;
and as she gazed, her clothes fell from her — she was left quite naked.[^19]

**7.5.** Seeing this, I, laughing and laughing, went to dress her —
dress her, indeed! — looking at Gaura's form, I too was left naked.

**7.6.** To rebuke the two of us, my mother-in-law came over in anger —
what a deed of the Creator! — looking at Gauranga, the old woman too became naked.

**7.7.** Naked, the three of us gazed at Gora;
and as we gazed, He — the star of our eyes — went away, leaving us sightless.

**7.8.** Then all three of us came to our senses and bit our tongues;
my mother-in-law said: 'Daughter-in-law, tell no one of today's disgrace.'"

**7.9.** Narahari says: who will say what? — the plight of all three is the same:
keep quiet and carefully cover the cut-off ear with your hair.[^20]

and 124–180 stand in the same order as the padas of Narahari Chakravarti's *Gaura-charita-chintamani* (GCC 7.2–7.16 and 7.29 onwards), so nos. 100–110
and 120–123, absent from the OCR text of GCC, were checked against the scan of GCC (ed. 1947; IA gaurcharitra-chintamani, the only available copy):
the scan (a 200-page PDF) **lacks printed pp. 81–88** — after p. 80 (PDF 85; end of GCC 7.16 = no. 99) comes p. 89 at once (PDF 86; beginning of GCC
7.29 = no. 124), and PDF pp. 94–98 repeat pp. 89–93. The lost pages held GCC 7.17–7.28 — 12 padas; in GPT this place holds 15 padas signed Narahari
(nos. 100–110, 120–123; between them — padas of Vasu, Lochana, Jnanadasa and no. 119 = GCC, kirana 2). Eight pages of GCC hold about 95 couplets of
the long metre, and the 15 GPT padas about 113; so most of them probably come from GCC, but all 15 cannot have stood there, and which ones did

[^19]: 7.2–7.6: "intoxicated by Madana" (madane mātiyā) and the nakedness of the three women are as in the original; translated exactly, without

padas are a monument of late poetry, not an image of the Lord.

## 8. Narahari — "ki kava sajani āṅgināra mājhe…" (GPT1 p. 199, no. 101)
*Nagari-bhava. A townswoman's story: hearing that Gora was approaching their house, she hastily finished the housework so as to go and look — but
her mother-in-law, guessing, shouted at her, and she could not take a single step. Raga "yatharaga". **Attribution: not established** — GPT only
(see n. 18).*[^21]

**8.1.** "What shall I say, friend? We were sitting in the middle of the courtyard —
and we heard: Gora, Shachi's son, is coming near our house.

## Summary of chapter 30

*The chapter continues the padas signed "Narahari" from section 3.2 of GPT — the townswomen of Nadia telling their friends in the morning how,
despite the strictness of their mothers-in-law, sisters-in-law, fathers-in-law and husbands, they saw Gaura. Nos. 94–99 (nos. 1–6) were found in
Narahari Chakravarti's *Gaura-charita-chintamani* (kirana 7, padas 10–12, 14–16; "probably Chakravarti" — B; no. 97 in GPT is only the second half of
the GCC pada 7.14). Nos. 100–110 and 120–123 (nos. 7–21) stand in GPT in the place of GCC 7.17–7.28, but those pages (pp. 81–88) are lost in the
only available scan of GCC, and there are no other witnesses: they are labelled "not established" (C), with a note on their probable origin in GCC
(n. 18). In several padas the strict elders — a mother-in-law, a father-in-law, the husband's aunt — themselves lose their composure on hearing the
kirtana or seeing Gaura and become His devotees (nos. 9, 13, 18); the direct motifs of attraction and of deceiving the elders are rendered
exactly, with notes on the position of the tradition (introductory note, chapter 27).*
