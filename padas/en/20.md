# Appendix. Padas signed "Narahari" from the Gaura-pada-tarangini — IV: the appearance of Gaura, the end of childhood, the samskaras and the betrothal

*Continuation of the appendix (the introductory note on the two Naraharis, on the arguments for attribution and on the marks in the
headings is in `17.md`). Portion 20: a pada surveying Gaura's lila from the section "aishvarya and madhurya" (GPT 1.3); padas on the
appearance of the Lord (section 2.1); the last padas of the section "Balya-lila" (2.2); the beginning of the section "Karnavedha and the
wedding" (2.3) — the first tonsure and ear-piercing, the upanayana, the first meeting with Lakshmipriya by the Ganga, the news of the
wedding and the adhivasa (the wedding day follows in the next portion). Almost all the padas of the portion are quoted by Narahari
Chakravarti in the 12th wave of the Bhakti-ratnakara, where Ishana shows Srinivasa the house of Jagannatha Mishra and tells what happened
there (two are in Chakravarti's Gaura-carita-cintamani); one pada (no. 12) has not been found in any witness. According to Majumdar, the
padas on the birth and childhood of Nimai known under the name "Narahari" are late: Narahari Sarakara did not see the Lord's childhood.
Before translation the "Narahari" signatures in sections 1.3 and 2.1–2.3 were checked throughout in GPT1: the catalogue had missed five more
padas of section 2.3 (here nos. 13 and 15, new rows NG-280, 281; NG-282…284 follow in the next portion). The base text is GPT1 (doubtful places from the scan), collated with GPT2, the
BR (eds. 1912 and 1960) and the Gaura-carita-cintamani (ed. 1947); PK, KGC, PS and the Gita-candrodaya have been checked. On the "birth" of
the Lord see note 4; on the childhood games as lila, note 2 of portion 19; on the samskaras, note 26. The notes of the GPT editors are not
reproduced.*

## 1. Narahari — "śacīsuta gaurahari…" (GPT1 p. 26, section 1.3, no. 2; BR)
*A survey of the lila: the son of Shachi appeared in Navadvipa and revealed sankirtana; He took sannyasa, remained in Nilachala and,
intoxicated with Radhika's love, intoxicated the world with it; His associates are beside themselves with prema; the treasure unattainable
even for the gods He gave to the world. Raga kamoda. **Attribution: probably Chakravarti** — the pada is in the BR (12th wave).*[^1]

**1.1.** The son of Shachi, Gaurahari, having descended in Navadvipa, displayed manifold pastimes:
together with His dear associates He revealed sankirtana and increased the joy of all.

**1.2.** And then, in the dress of a sannyasi, the Lord wandered from land to land and, coming to Nilachala, remained there;
intoxicated with Radhika's love, knowing neither day nor night, He intoxicated the whole world with that love.[^2]

**1.3.** Nityananda who is Balarama, Advaita the abode of virtues, Gadadhara, Srivasa and all the rest,
seeing this wondrous way, cannot keep their composure — ceaselessly overwhelmed with prema.

**1.4.** The treasure unattainable even for the gods He has, with effort, bestowed [on us] — may I die warding off all ill from such mercy!
The Lord Krishna-Chaitanya has blessed the age of Kali — Narahari Dasa sings His glory.

[^1]: GPT1 — p. 26, section 1.3 ("aishvarya and madhurya of the Gaura-avatara"), no. 2 (kamoda; scan, PDF p. 285); GPT2 — section 1.3, no. 2
(catalogue: NG-003). BR — 12th wave (ed. 1912, p. 914; ed. 1960, vv. 3605–3608): Ishana, having told Srinivasa of the Lord's departure from
Navadvipa to Puri, quotes this pada as a brief survey of His whole lila. Variant: the BR has "বিলাইলা" — "distributed", the GPT "মিলাইলা" —
"procured, bestowed". Not in PK, KGC, PS, the Gita-candrodaya or the Gaura-carita-cintamani. B.
[^2]: In Puri the Lord is absorbed in the bhava of Radha longing for Krishna: it was to taste Her love that He appeared (CC Adi 4; Antya 14–20).
Nityananda is Balarama Himself (1.3; CC Adi 5).

## 2. Narahari — "phālguna-pūrṇimā śubhakṣaṇe…" (GPT1 p. 59, section 2.1, no. 19; BR)
*In the auspicious hour of the full moon of Phalguna Shachi, having given birth to a son, cannot gaze at Him enough; Jagannatha Mishra,
hearing the news, floats in an ocean of happiness and performs the rites; the Ganga and the Earth forget their sorrows; with the rising
of Gaurachandra the darkness of sin departs. Raga vasanta. **Attribution: probably Chakravarti** — the pada is in the BR (12th wave).*[^3]

**2.1.** In the auspicious hour of the full moon of Phalguna Shachi, having given birth to a son, cannot take her eyes from Him.[^4]

**2.2.** How many feelings rise in her heart at every moment! As if He were golden fresh butter, she dares not touch Him.

**2.3.** With what care she takes Him on her lap and sends word of the son's birth to the best of the Mishras.

**2.4.** Jagannatha, the crown of brahmanas, hearing of the birth of his son, floats in an ocean of happiness.

**2.5.** With what joy he hastens [to the son] — and, seeing the moonlike face, cannot contain himself.

**2.6.** Gathering his dear ones, he performs auspicious rites for the welfare of the son.

**2.7.** On all sides sounds "jaya, jaya"; all say: "Blessed, blessed are the father and the mother!"

**2.8.** Happiness grows in everyone's heart; the Suradhuni and the Earth forget all their sorrows.[^5]

**2.9.** All ten directions have become bright; beasts, birds, trees and creepers — all have blossomed.

**2.10.** What more can Narahari say? With the rising of Gaurachandra the darkness of sin has gone.

[^3]: GPT1 — p. 59, section 2.1 ("the appearance"), no. 19 (vasanta; scan, PDF p. 318); GPT2 — section 2.1, no. 19. The catalogue did not know it
(new row NG-276). BR — 12th wave (ed. 1912, p. 764; ed. 1960, vv. 907–916): Ishana, showing Srinivasa the place of the Lord's appearance in
the house of Jagannatha Mishra, quotes padas of Vasu Ghosha and Vrindavana Dasa on the birth and then — in a row — this one and the next four
(nos. 3–5; between them a pada signed by Ghanashyama, GPT 2.1 no. 23, outside the appendix) and no. 6. Variant in the last line: the BR has
"the darkness of suffering" (তাপ), the GPT "the darkness of sin" (পাপ). Not in PK, KGC, PS, the Gita-candrodaya or the Gaura-carita-cintamani. B.
The GPT1 footnotes to lines 2.8–2.9 (editor's notes) are not reproduced.
[^4]: The poet speaks in the language of the lila: "gave birth to a son", "birth". But the Lord is not born as a conditioned soul is, by karma and
in a material body: He appears (avirbhava) by His own will in His eternal form — as Krishna appeared before Devaki (Bhag. 10.3; Gita 4.6, 4.9;
CC Adi 13–14). So the appearance of the Lord is understood by Vrindavana Dasa, Krishnadasa Kaviraja and Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati; so too are all the
padas on the "birth" in this portion to be understood.
[^5]: The Suradhuni ("the river of the gods") is the Ganga. The poet says that with the appearance of the Lord both the Ganga and the Earth,
burdened with the sins of the age of Kali, forgot their sorrows: on the banks of the Ganga He will perform His pastimes, and the sins of the world
will depart.

## 3. Narahari — "phālguna-pūrṇimā maṅgalera sīmā…" (GPT1 pp. 59–60, no. 20; BR)
*The full moon of Phalguna: the Moon of Gokula has appeared, in every house of Nadia there is an ocean of joy; Shachi with her son on her lap
in the birth-chamber; in the darkness of the eclipse crowds run to see the Child and forget themselves. Raga not stated (in the BR —
dhanashi). **Attribution: probably Chakravarti** — the pada is in the BR.*[^6]

**3.1.** The full moon of Phalguna — the summit of all blessings: the Moon of Gokula has appeared!
In the town of Nadia, in every house, an ocean of joy surges.

**3.2.** What merriment among them all!
Shachi-devi, taking her son on her lap, shines in the birth-chamber.

**3.3.** To see the Child they run from all sides — no one can restrain himself;
in the darkness of the eclipse who can tell who is who? There is no counting the people coming.[^7]

**3.4.** Gazing their fill at the sweetness of the Child, they forget their own bodies.
Narahari says: the son of Shachi reveals some new love!

[^6]: GPT1 — pp. 59–60, no. 20 (no raga; scan, PDF pp. 318–319); GPT2 — section 2.1, no. 20. The catalogue did not know it (new row NG-277). BR —
12th wave (ed. 1912, pp. 764–765; ed. 1960, vv. 917–920, "পুনর্ধানশী"). Not in PK, KGC, PS, the Gita-candrodaya or the Gaura-carita-cintamani. B.
Line 3.2 is the refrain (ধ্রু).
[^7]: The Lord appeared in the evening of the full moon of Phalguna, during a lunar eclipse, when by custom everyone was bathing in the Ganga and
repeating the name of Hari (CC Adi 13; CB Adi 2–3; Murari 1.5). "The Moon of Gokula" is Krishna: Gaura is Krishna Himself, the Lord of Gokula.

## 4. Narahari — "parama śubha śacīgarbhe…" (GPT1 p. 60, no. 21; BR)
*Gaura, the Lord of Gokula, in the womb of Shachi; the gods praise Him; on the night of the full moon, when Rahu had swallowed the moon, the
Lord appeared, having first revealed His name; those entering Mishra's house, seeing the Child, lose their senses; the pride of Kali is
crushed. Raga kamoda. **Attribution: probably Chakravarti** — the pada is in the BR; Vrajabuli.*[^8]

**4.1.** In the womb of Shachi, in the supremely auspicious hour, abides Gaura — the Lord of Gokula;
again and again the gods offer praises and bow down; the house brims with jubilation.

**4.2.** On the auspicious night of the Phalguna full moon, when Rahu had swallowed the rising moon —
at such an hour the Lord appeared, having first revealed His name.[^9]

**4.3.** "Jaya, jaya" resounds through the whole world, no one can restrain himself;
those entering Mishra's house, beholding the Child, go mad with happiness.

**4.4.** They perform various auspicious rites, ever new; all desires are fulfilled.
Narahari says: the pride of mighty Kali is crushed.

[^8]: GPT1 — p. 60, no. 21 (kamoda; scan, PDF p. 319); GPT2 — section 2.1, no. 21. The catalogue did not know it (new row NG-278). BR — 12th wave
(ed. 1912, p. 765; ed. 1960, vv. 921–924). Not in PK, KGC, PS, the Gita-candrodaya or the Gaura-carita-cintamani. B. "In the womb of Shachi" —
on the appearance of the Lord see note 4.
[^9]: During the eclipse all the people of Nadia, bathing, were repeating "Hari, Hari" — thus the Lord, before appearing, made the whole world sing
His name (CC Adi 13). Rahu — according to the Puranas, the demon who swallows the moon at an eclipse.

## 5. Narahari — "jaya jaya jaya maṅgalarava…" (GPT1 pp. 60–61, no. 22; BR)
*The night of the Phalguna full moon: from the womb of Shachi Gaura, the delight of Vraja, has appeared; His body flashes like steady
lightning; the gods shower flowers, the kinnaras sing, the heavenly dancers dance; it is spring, the Malaya breeze blows, the waters of the
Ganga surge; the poet is beside himself with the festivity in Mishra's house. Raga vasanta. **Attribution: probably Chakravarti** — the pada
is in the BR; ornate Vrajabuli with rhythmic syllables.*[^10]

**5.1.** "Jaya, jaya, jaya!" — auspicious cries; the night of the Phalguna full moon shines anew:
from the womb of Shachi Gaura has appeared — the delight of Vraja.
The lovely body of the Child flashes — like steady lightning the colour of kunkuma;
His moonlike face gleams, sweetly shattering all composure.

**5.2.** Seeing the Lord's appearance, the gods with hosts of clouds shower from the sky
garlands of flowers; their bodies are filled with great thrills.
How many longings in their hearts! Excitedly they sing of His lovely deeds;
through the tears that flood their eyes, how many images they behold, in what rapture!

**5.3.** The kinnaras sing to a sweet sound, the mridanga softly plays:
"dhā-dhiki dhikitā dhik dhik dhikkaṭak dhinnānā";
the heavenly dancers dance, enacting performances of every kind:
"uṭṭata kathai thai thai ti ai ai atennānā".[^11]

**5.4.** Pure and bright are all ten directions, gently blows the breeze from the Malaya hills,
the flocks of cuckoos sound "kuhu" — spring, the king of seasons, is full of sweetness.
The waters of the river of the gods surge, joy spreads over the land of Nadia;
at the festivity in Mishra's house Narahari's heart is beside itself.

[^10]: GPT1 — pp. 60–61, no. 22 (vasanta; scan, PDF pp. 319–320); GPT2 — section 2.1, no. 22. The catalogue did not know it (new row NG-279). BR —
12th wave (ed. 1912, pp. 765–766; ed. 1960, vv. 925–928). Line 5.3: GPT1 has "নৃত্যসুর", the BR "নৃত্যত সুর" ("the heavenly ones dance") — translated
after the BR; 5.4: GPT1 and GPT2 have "মিশ্রভুবন", the BR "মিশ্রভবন" ("Mishra's house") — translated after the BR. Not in PK, KGC, PS, the
Gita-candrodaya or the Gaura-carita-cintamani. B.
[^11]: Kinnaras — heavenly singers. The syllables "dhā-dhiki…", "thai thai…" imitate the strokes of the mridanga and the dancers' counting (as in the
dance padas, portions 17–18).

## 6. Narahari — "bhuvanamanocorā gokulapati gorā…" (GPT1 pp. 61–62, no. 24; BR)
*The birth festival: the women of Nadia come with trays of gifts, and with them, mingling with the humans, the wives of the gods; Sita-devi and
Malini perform auspicious rites; the cowherd men and women, after their former nature, scatter curd in the courtyard and dance; the gods in
the guise of cowherds dance with them; in Mishra's house is the custom of Nanda's house. Raga vasanta or tori. **Attribution: probably
Chakravarti** — the pada is in the BR.*[^12]

**6.1.** The Stealer of the world's hearts, the Lord of Gokula, Gora-chand — in what an auspicious hour was He born!
How much happiness Shachi had when she saw her son's face — who else could tell it?

**6.2.** The women of the town of Nadia come in rows, bringing trays full of all kinds of gifts;
and, richly adorned, the wives of the gods, mingling with the humans, cannot gaze at the Child enough.

**6.3.** Sri Sita-devi came and, entering the birth-chamber, rejoices at heart seeing the Child;
together with Malini and others, floating in merriment, she performs many auspicious rites.[^13]

**6.4.** How many cowherd women, hundreds of cowherd men come in lovely attire, bringing curd;
all, their hearts overwhelmed, after their former nature scatter curd in the middle of the courtyard.

**6.5.** Clapping their hands, laughing, they dance splendidly; seeing this, the gods, taking the guise of cowherds,
dance in the courtyard — who does not dance there? — again and again crying "jaya, jaya".[^14]

**6.6.** So the instruments sound, such festivity is nowhere else: in Mishra's house is the custom of Nanda's house.
What can Narahari say? At the festival of the Lord's birth, for jubilation no one remembers anything.

[^12]: GPT1 — pp. 61–62, no. 24 (vasanta or tori; scan, PDF pp. 320–321); GPT2 — section 2.1, no. 24 (catalogue: NG-004). BR — 12th wave (ed. 1912,
pp. ≈765–766; ed. 1960, vv. 937–942, "পুনর্ব্বসন্ত—তোড়ী"), immediately after a pada of Ghanashyama (GPT 2.1 no. 23). Not in PK, KGC, PS, the
Gita-candrodaya or the Gaura-carita-cintamani. B. Signature "নরহরি কি কব" (6.6).
[^13]: Sita Thakurani, Advaita's wife, came to bless the newborn and brought gifts (CC Adi 13); Malini is Srivasa's wife (note 27 of portion 19).
[^14]: As at the festival of Krishna's birth in Nanda's house the cowherds scattered curd, milk and butter and danced (Bhag. 10.5), so here: the
cowherds of Navadvipa act "after their former nature" (পূর্ব্ব স্বভাবেতে), that is, as the cowherds of Vraja. Cf. CB Adi 3 (the gods in human
guise come to see the Child).

## 7. Narahari — "nimāicā̃dera kathā ati aparūpa go…" (GPT1 p. 75, section 2.2, no. 40; GCC)
*One of the elderly women of Navadvipa about Nimai: where have His pranks gone — now He is grave, the pandits respect Him, the Vaishnavas are
inseparable from Him; Nitai loves Him as Balai loved Krishna; Shachi is the very Queen of Vraja, Nimai and Nitai are the two stars of her eyes.
Yatharaga. **Attribution: probably Chakravarti** — the pada is in the GCC.*[^15]

**7.1.** "Wondrous are the ways of Nimai-chand: now He is like a gracious deity of our family!
Where have all those pranks gone? Who else is as grave as He?

**7.2.** All the pandits living in Nadia — which of them does not respect Him?
Srivasa, Murari and all the Vaishnavas — none can bear to part from Him.

**7.3.** And my Nitai loves Him like his own life — as Balai loved Krishna;
it seems that very thing has appeared here again: nowhere have I seen the like.[^16]

**7.4.** Blessed in this world is virtuous Shachi — it seems she is that very Queen of Vraja;
Nimai and Nitai are the two stars of her eyes." Narahari offers himself to this love.

[^15]: GPT1 — p. 75, section 2.2, no. 40 (yatharaga; scan, PDF p. 334); GPT2 — section 2.2, no. 40 (catalogue: NG-021; in the GPT2 recognition the
number was read as "70"). GCC — chapter 6, pada 15 (ed. 1947, printed pp. 65–66). Not in the BR, PK, KGC, PS or the Gita-candrodaya. B. The pada
continues the GCC cycle in which the people of Navadvipa come at dawn to Shachi's house and gaze at Gaura, returned from Gaya (portion 19,
nos. 4–28): "now" (এবে) is the time after Gaya, when Nityananda is already in Navadvipa and Srivasa and Murari are inseparable from the Lord. In
line 7.3 the GCC has "কৃষ্ণে যেন করিল বলাই" ("as Balai [loved] Krishna"); the GPT "কৃষ্ণ যেন…", with the same sense.
[^16]: Nityananda is Balarama, Krishna's elder brother in Vraja; Shachi is Yashoda (CC Adi 13; Adi 5). Shachi loved Nityananda as a son; cf. her
dream in which Nimai and Nitai appear as Krishna and Balarama (CB, Madhya-khanda). "My Nitai" — the affectionate words of an elderly woman.

**8.1.** Thus all the elderly women of Nadia speak to one another.

**8.2.** How wondrous is the way of them all! With what to compare it? They are full of tenderness.

**8.3.** Again and again gazing at the moonlike face of Gauranga-chand, they count themselves fortunate.

**8.4.** How many times they bless Him, again and again! Narahari, hearing this, swims in happiness.

## 9. Narahari — "nāco āre bāpa viśvambhara…" (GPT1 p. 76, no. 43; BR)

**9.1.** "Dance, dear Vishvambhara! We will give You handfuls of butter, thickened milk and cream!"

**9.2.** The virtuous wives around say this tenderly to Nimai-chand.

**9.3.** Saying "Hari, Hari-bol", all together clap their hands again and again.

**9.4.** Gora, looking at His mother, dances in various ways crying "Hari-bol".[^19]

**9.5.** What a gentle smile on the moonlike face! It enchants the world, pouring out nectar in streams.

**9.6.** Lovely is the glance of His eyes; seeing the curves of His arms, who can stay calm?

**9.7.** What a sweet, sweet radiance! The courtyard gleams with the rays of His golden body.

**9.8.** Sweetly the belt of bells and the anklets jingle; Narahari offers himself to the rhythm of His feet.

**10.3.** If anyone calls You — say You are afraid: Your father forbids it.

## 12. Narahari — "āji āṅginā para nadīyā bālaka saṅge…" (GPT1 p. 77, no. 46)
*Shachi's child plays in the courtyard with the boys of Nadia — like the moon among the stars; Shachi, beside herself with joy, kisses Him,
