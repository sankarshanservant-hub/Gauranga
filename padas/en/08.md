# Vasu Ghosha: the Gaura Avatara and Gaura's Form, the Lord's Kirtana and Bhavas in Navadvipa

*Padas of Vasu (Vasudeva) Ghosha known from the *Padakalpataru* (PK), in the order of the lilas: praise of the Gaura
avatara and His qualities; Gaura's form in Nadia; the Lord's dance and kirtana with the devotees on the bank of the
Ganga; the Lord in Radha's mood — gaura-chandrikas to the sections of PK on Radha's states of love (prema-vaichittya,
vasaka-sajja, khandita, mana). Majumdar regards Vasu Ghosha as an eyewitness of the Navadvipa kirtanas and counts these
padas among his padas on the Lord's form and bhava. The text follows PK, checked against the scans, with the
manuscript variants from PK's apparatus, the *Gaura-pada-tarangini* (GPT1 — 1st ed., 1903; GPT2 — 2nd ed., 1934), the
*Bhakti-ratnakara* (BR) and the *Kshanada-gita-chintamani* (KGC); the Bengali original, variants and word-for-word
translation are in `bn/08.md`.*

## 1. Vasu Ghosha — "ki kahiba śata śata tuyā avatāra…" (PK 2292)
*Praise of the Gaura avatara: of hundreds of incarnations, the poet holds dear Gauranga alone; as Rama built a bridge
across the ocean, Gaura made kirtana a bridge to carry across the lame, the dull and the blind; the poet knows neither
japa, nor austerity, nor Vedic reasoning, and begs Gauranga to carry him across too. In PK it stands at the end of the
21st pallava. Raga barari.*[^1]

**1.1.** What shall I say of Your hundreds and hundreds of incarnations?
Gaurangachand alone is my life.

**1.2.** In the incarnation of Vishnu You are a beggar asking for prema,
taking four with You: Shiva, Shuka, Narada…[^2]

**1.3.** In the incarnation of Rama You built the bridge across the ocean —
but now Your fame will resound through all the world.[^3]

**1.4.** In the age of Kali You made kirtana the bridge:
let all the lame, the dull and the blind cross over with ease.[^4]

**1.5.** By what qualities do men dance, by what qualities women?
By Gora's qualities the fourteen worlds are intoxicated.[^5]

**1.6.** I know neither japa, nor austerity, nor this Vedic reasoning.
Says Vasu: "Gauranga, carry me across!"

[^1]: Signature: "Vasu." PK: vol. 3, PDF p. 300 (printed p. 289), 21st pallava, no. 75 (mark "॥৭৫॥২২৯২॥"); raga
barari; MS P-R-Sa no. 2396. GPT1 — p. 46, no. 67 (raga barari); GPT2 — sect. 1.3, no. 67. The catalogue listed the pada
twice: under PK 2294 (wrongly: PK 2294 is a pada of Atmarama Dasa on Nityananda, "āre mora nitāi nāyara") and under
PK 2292. Majumdar counts PK 2292 among Vasu Ghosha's stavas and prarthanas.
[^2]: The sense of the line is not fully clear. Probably the poet speaks of Gaura as the Lord Himself (Vishnu) come as
a devotee — a "beggar" asking for prema — together with the greatest bhaktas: Shiva, Shuka, Narada (and presumably
Brahma; "janā cāri" — "four," or simply "a handful of people"). The Gaudiya tradition sees in Gaura's associates
incarnations of these bhaktas (Advaita — Shiva, Srivasa — Narada; GGD).
[^3]: Rama built the bridge (setu-bandha) to Lanka. MS P-R-Sa: "at last" (অবশেষ) instead of "but now."
[^4]: The "bridge" of kirtana carries one across the ocean of worldly existence. MSS "ka," "ca" and GPT1: without "all"
(যত); "kha": "let them cross over now."
[^5]: The fourteen worlds — the whole universe (the seven higher and seven lower planetary systems).

## 2. Vasu Ghosha — "nā jāni ki jāni mora bhela…" (PK 2210)
*Praise of Gaura's qualities: remembering them, trees and creepers weep, the forest deities, beasts and birds weep,
stones melt, no one can stay at home; the poet loses himself. In PK it stands in the 20th pallava ("Sri Gaurachandra —
another mode"). Raga suhai.*[^6]

**2.1.** *(Refrain)* I do not know what has happened to me:
thinking of Gauranga's qualities, I have forgotten my body.

**2.2.** Remembering Gora's qualities, the trees and creepers weep;
remembering His qualities, the deities of the forest weep.[^7]

**2.3.** Remembering Gora's qualities, the stones melt;
remembering His qualities, no one stays at home.

**2.4.** Vasudeva Ghosha weeps, remembering His qualities;
at His qualities beasts and birds weep, finding no rest.

[^6]: Signature: "Vasudeva Ghosha." PK: vol. 3, PDF p. 272 (printed pp. 260–261), 20th pallava, no. 27 (mark
"॥২৭॥২২১০॥"); raga suhai; MS P-R-Sa no. 2318. GPT1 — pp. 43–44, no. 57 (raga suhai); GPT2 — sect. 1.3, no. 57.
Majumdar does not discuss this pada separately.
[^7]: Cf. CC Madhya 17: in the forests of Jharikhanda the beasts and birds, trees and creepers came into ecstasy hearing
the Lord sing the holy name. GPT1: "no one remains in the house" (রয় ঘরে) — the same meaning.

## 3. Vasu Ghosha — "gorā-guṇa gāo gāo śuni…" (PK 2185)
*Praise of Gaura: the poet asks that His qualities be sung; the Lord is the touchstone of prema; a single glance of His
dries up the ocean of grief of all souls; His eyes are full of tears, His words are engraved on the heart, His body is
covered with the thrill of ecstasy. In PK it stands in the 20th pallava. Raga mallar.*[^8]

**3.1.** *(Refrain)* Sing, sing of Gora's qualities — let me hear!
By the fruit of many pious deeds that Lord has been revealed to us —
the touchstone of the rasa of prema.[^9]

**3.2.** This ocean of grief of all souls
He dries up in the blink of an eye.
Without the touch of even a drop of that prema,
with what will you cool your heart?

**3.3.** His reddish eyes are the abode of Varuna
when the Merciful One gazes;
in His sweet talk every word
is as if written into the heart.[^10]

**3.4.** He sways in prema; the thrill of ecstasy
fills His body from head to foot.
Says Vasudeva: a thousand streams are flowing —
as if Sumeru were being bathed.[^11]

[^8]: Signature: "Vasudeva." PK: vol. 3, PDF pp. 262–263 (printed pp. 251–252), 20th pallava, no. 2 (mark
"॥২॥২১৮৫॥"); raga mallar; MS P-R-Sa no. 2290. GPT1 — pp. 41–42, no. 50 (raga mallar); GPT2 — sect. 1.3, no. 50. The
catalogue listed the pada twice (by GPT and by PK). Majumdar counts PK 2185 among Vasu's padas on the Lord's bhava.
[^9]: The touchstone (পরশ-মণি) turns iron into gold; so the prema the Lord bestows transforms the soul.
[^10]: Varuna is the lord of the waters: the Lord's eyes are full of tears, like the ocean. "Written into the heart" —
lit. "written, laid out upon the ribs" (পাঁজরে পাতিয়া লিখনে). GPT1: "reddish eyes" (অরুণ-নয়নে), "gaze" (নিরিখণে) — the
same meaning.
[^11]: Sumeru is the golden mountain; the Lord's golden body, washed by streams of tears, is like Sumeru with rivers
running down it.

## 4. Vasu Ghosha — "cīta-cora gaura mora…" (PK 341)
*Gaura's form: the stealer of hearts, intoxicated with prema, embraces the destitute and the fallen; His name is the
cause of the deliverance of the worlds; for the sake of souls He left His abode and appeared in Nadia like the autumn
moon; the Creator fashioned Him an ocean of rasa; seeing Him, all sorrow departs. In PK it is the gaura-chandrika at
the beginning of the 6th pallava (abhisarikas of the rainy season, etc.). Raga tori.*[^12]

**4.1.** The stealer of hearts is my Gaura,
intoxicated with prema, immersed, enraptured;
the destitute He takes in His embrace —
the friend of the fallen and the low.

**4.2.** His name is the cause of the deliverance of the worlds;
for the sake of souls He left His abode
and appeared in the town of Nadia
like the autumn moon.[^13]

**4.3.** Who can find the limit of His boundless glory?
He steals the very life of young women.
From what did the Creator fashion Gaura —
a vast ocean of rasa?[^14]

**4.4.** Looking and looking, joy arises,
all the sorrow of the heart is taken away.
Says Vasu Ghosha: what beauty is this!
Beholding it, the heart is full of bliss.

[^12]: Signature: "Vasu Ghosha." PK: vol. 1, PDF p. 232 (printed p. 220), 2nd shakha, 6th pallava ("Now the
abhisarika, vasaka-sajja and the rest befitting the rainy season. First of all, Sri Gaurachandra"), no. 1 (mark
"॥১॥৩৪১॥"); raga tori; PK gives no manuscript number or variants. GPT1 — pp. 131–132, no. 60 (raga tori); GPT2 — sect.
3.1, no. 60. Majumdar counts PK 341 among Vasu's padas on the Lord's form.
[^13]: "Left His abode" — the spiritual world, Goloka (cf. CC Adi 3: the Lord descends to give souls prema). "Appeared"
(প্রকট হইলা) — the Lord is not born like a conditioned soul but reveals Himself. GPT1: "appeared in the town of Nadia
like the autumn moon."
[^14]: "Steals the very life of young women" is a stock image of the poetics of describing beauty; we translate
faithfully. Vrindavana Dasa stresses that in this incarnation the Lord never looked at another man's wife; worship of
Gaura in the spirit of "Gaura-nagari" is not accepted by the Gaudiya tradition (Vrindavana Dasa, Krishnadasa Kaviraja,
Bhaktisiddhanta).

## 5. Vasu Ghosha — "dekha dekha gorā naṭa-rāy…" (PK 2143)
*The form of Gaura, the king of dancers: His face is the autumn moon with a gentle smile; champaka buds in His curls;
golden earrings, lips redder than the bimba; His voice is a wave of nectar drowning the world in the rasa of Hari; His
arms are lovelier than an elephant's trunk; His chest is a golden mountain, His navel a lotus lake; He wears crimson
cloth, and ankle-bells ring on His feet. In PK it stands in the 18th pallava (the form of Gauranga). Raga
gandhara.*[^15]

**5.1.** Behold, behold Gora, the king of dancers!
His face is the autumn moon, and on it a gentle, gentle smile;
seeing it, chaste women swoon.[^16]

**5.2.** On His head, curling locks, and in them champaka buds —
and the bee-hearts of young women.
At the roots of His lotus ears golden earrings sway;
His lips surpass the ripe bimba fruit.[^17]

**5.3.** From His conch-like throat come soft words, a wave of nectar
that drowns the world in the rasa of Hari.
Surpassing the trunk of a mighty elephant are His two rounded arms,
and on them armlets and bracelets shine.

**5.4.** His chest a golden mountain, His navel a lotus lake;
seeing His waist, the lion runs away.
He is clad in crimson cloth, ankle-bells ring on His feet —
Vasu Ghosha sings of Gora's qualities.[^18]

[^15]: Signature: "Vasu Ghosha." PK: vol. 3, PDF p. 247 (printed p. 236), 18th pallava (the form of Sri Gauranga,
etc.), no. 35 (mark "॥৩৫॥২১৪৩॥"); raga gandhara; MS P-R-Sa no. 2250. GPT1 — p. 116, no. 15 (raga gandhara; the refrain is
marked after the first stanza); GPT2 — sect. 3.1, no. 15. The catalogue listed the pada under PK 2144 (wrongly: PK 2144
is the next pada, no. 11 here), while under PK 2143 it gave a first line from PK 2139 (Govinda Dasa). Majumdar counts
PK 2143 among Vasu's padas on the Lord's form.
[^16]: The words about women are a stock image of the poetics of describing beauty; we translate faithfully (see note
14).
[^17]: Champaka is a tree with fragrant golden flowers; the bimba is a fruit with bright red skin, a usual comparison for
lips. "The bee-hearts of young women" is an image of beauty that draws every heart (see note 14).
[^18]: "The lion runs away" — the Lord's slender waist surpasses a lion's (a usual comparison). Crimson (saffron) cloth
is a frequent detail of Gaura's form in Vasu's padas (cf. no. 15).

## 6. Vasu Ghosha — "jāmbunadacaya rucira gañjaya…" (PK 2087)
*Gaura's form: the radiance of His body outshines a heap of the finest gold; His arms, anointed with sandal, are like
the trunk of a king of elephants; the youthful dancer, absorbed in bhava, with faltering voice, dances throughout Nadia,
knowing neither day nor night; on the bank of the Ganga, beneath a young tree, He sits revealing Himself and sprinkles
all His servants with prema. In PK it stands in the 17th pallava (the dance and play of Gauranga). Raga
dhanashi.*[^19]

**6.1.** Outshining a heap of jambunada gold,
the radiance of His body glitters;
His arms, anointed with sandal and adorned,
shine like the trunk of a king of elephants.[^20]

**6.2.** I beheld Gaura, the youthful dancer, the hero;
in seeing Him, bliss knows no end.
His body is rapt in bhava, His heart overflowing,
in His throat a faltering voice.[^21]

**6.3.** Filling the whole town of Nadia with endless delights,
the Knower of rasa, the Wise One, dances;
manifold artistry, the grace of play —
He knows neither day nor night.

**6.4.** On the bank of the Suradhuni, at the roots of a young tree,
He sits, revealing Himself.
Sings Vasudeva Ghosha: the gift of prema has been received —
He has sprinkled all His servants with it.[^22]

[^19]: Signature: "Vasudeva Ghosha." PK: vol. 3, PDF p. 225 (printed p. 214), 17th pallava, no. 29 (mark
"॥২৯॥২০৮৭॥"); raga dhanashi; MS P-R-Sa no. 2196. GPT1 — p. 131, no. 58 (raga dhanashri; the second stanza "I beheld
Gaura…" serves as the refrain); GPT2 — sect. 3.1, no. 58. The catalogue's former first line ("ঐছন ভাব বুঝই না পারিয়ে")
is the reading of MS P-R-Sa in the closing lines (see note 22). Majumdar counts PK 2087 among Vasu's padas on the Lord's
form.
[^20]: Jambunada is the finest gold (according to the Puranas, from the river Jambu). GPT1: "surpassing" (গঞ্জন). MS
"kha": "beautifully adorned" (সুমণ্ডিত).
[^21]: "Hero" (নায়র, i.e. নাগর) — the graceful hero, a usual epithet of Gaura with Vasu Ghosha. MS P-R-Sa: "young" (নব)
instead of "dancer."
[^22]: The Suradhuni is the Ganga. "Revealing Himself" (নিজ পরকাশে) — in His true form (or "in His own radiance"). MS
P-R-Sa, instead of the last two lines: "Such a bhava cannot be understood, says Vasudeva Ghosha." GPT1: "Vasudeva Ghosha
sings."

## 7. Vasu Ghosha — "avatāra bhāla gaurāṅga avatāra kailā bhāla…" (PK 665)
*Kirtana in Navadvipa: wondrous is Gauranga's incarnation — even Jagai and Madhai dance; the moon, the sun and the stars
dance, Vasuki in the underworld dances repeating "Gora, Gora"; the devotees and all the destitute dance; the Lord
delivers the dull, the blind, the infirm and the fallen — only the poet is left deprived. In PK it is a gaura-chandrika
in the 6th pallava (rasodgara). The raga is that of the preceding pada — vibhasa.*[^23]

**7.1.** Wondrous is the incarnation — wondrously has Gauranga descended!
Jagai and Madhai are dancing — such is His great power![^24]

**7.2.** The moon dances, the sun dances, and the stars dance;
in the underworld Vasuki dances, repeating: "Gora, Gora!"[^25]

**7.3.** The devotees dance, enraptured;
all the destitute dance, intoxicated with prema.

**7.4.** He delivers the dull, the blind, the infirm, the fallen.
Says Vasu Ghosha: "I alone have been left deprived."[^26]

[^23]: Signature: "Vasu Ghosha." PK: vol. 2, PDF pp. 57–58 (printed pp. 44–45), 3rd shakha, 6th pallava (rasodgara),
no. 2 (mark "॥২॥৬৬৫॥"); "তথা রাগ" — the raga of the preceding pada PK 664 (Chaitanyadasa, part 06, no. 10) — vibhasa;
MS P-R-Sa no. 1140. GPT1 — pp. 248–249, no. 2 (raga vibhasa), right after PK 664; GPT2 — sect. 4.2, no. 2. Majumdar does
not discuss this pada separately.
[^24]: Jagai and Madhai are the robber brothers whom the Lord and Nityananda delivered in Navadvipa (CB Madhya 13; CC Adi
17); their dancing in the kirtana is a sign of the Lord's all-conquering mercy. "Great power" (বড় ঠাকুরাল) — the Lord's
majesty. MS "ka": "made" (কৈল).
[^25]: Vasuki is the king of the serpents in the lower worlds (Patala). MS "ka," GPT1: "saying" (বলি); "ca": বোলে; "kha":
"Gora" only once.
[^26]: MSS "gha," "ca": অতুর (the same, "infirm"); P-R-Sa: "the infirm and the rest" (অতুরাদি). The usual self-abasement
of Vaishnava poets.

## 8. Vasu Ghosha — "kā̃cā se soṇāra tanu…" (PK 2100)
*Gaura's dance: His body is pure gold, whole Gangas flow from His eyes; His companions surround Him and sing to
karatalas and mridangas; seeing this, the Suradhuni surges in waves; the poet marvels at His smile. In PK it stands in
the 17th pallava (the dance and play of Gauranga). Raga gandhara (as for PK 2098).*[^27]

**8.1.** His body is pure gold, every limb brimming over;
how many Suradhunis flow in waves from His eyes![^28]

**8.2.** *(Refrain)* Gora dances in supreme bliss;
surrounding Him on all sides, His companions sing.

**8.3.** In their hands are karatalas, the mridanga sounds;
seeing this, the Suradhuni surges in waves.

**8.4.** His limbs are powerless with bhava, His speech falters.
Says Vasu: how sweet is that smile of joy![^29]

[^29]: MSS "ka," "ca," P-R-Sa: "surrounding" (বেড়িয়া) in 8.2; "kha," "ca," P-R-Sa: "sounds" (বাজয়ে) in 8.3. In 8.4 MSS

## 9. Vasu Ghosha (?) — "gorā nāce prema-vinadiyā…" (PK 2079)
*Dance in the sankirtana: the Lord of all the worlds, rapt in the play of prema, dances in Nadia without regard to

**9.1.** *(Refrain)* Gora dances, rapt in the play of prema:
the Lord of all the worlds sports in Nadia.

**9.2.** Dancing and dancing, Gora knows no direction;
moon-faced, He cries "Hari!", weeping and weeping.

**9.3.** Distributing to souls the wealth of Goloka's prema,
Gora dances in the sankirtana, crying: "Haribol!"

**9.4.** Whomever He sees, He begs: "Take the sweet name!"
Vasu Ghosha, not having received it, weeps without end.[^31]

## 10. Vasu Ghosha — "śuni vṛndāvana-guṇa…" (PK 2078)
*Dance in the kirtana: hearing of the glories of Vrindavana, Gaura, maddened by rasa, raises His arms and cries "Hari!",
whirls in the dance, and streams flow from His eyes; He is beyond purusha and prakriti and enchants Manmatha Himself;
His body thrills with ecstasy and is anointed with perfume and sandal. In PK it stands in the 17th pallava (the dance
and play of Gauranga). Raga tori.*[^32]

**10.1.** Hearing of the glories of Vrindavana, His heart maddened by rasa,
raising both arms, He cries: "Hari!"
Whirling, Gora Raya dances — how many streams are flowing:
His two eyes are pitchers of prema.

**10.2.** A dancer skilled in rasa, greedy for kirtana —
how many merry companions are with Him!
In the sidelong glances of His eyes
hundreds of thousands of Lakshmis play.[^33]

**10.3.** Beyond purusha and prakriti, enchanting Manmatha Himself,
He is the very limit of the joy of beauty.
Gaura is an ocean of rasa, deep, an elusive thief:
the pride of the town's women could not stand.[^34]

**10.4.** His neck held high, beautiful as Manmatha,
His body rejoices, thrilling with ecstasy;
perfume and sandal anoint His body —
in such prema Vasu Ghosha is drowning.[^35]

## 11. Vasu Ghosha — "kā̃cā kāñcana maṇi gorā-rūpa…" (PK 2144)
*Play on the bank of the Ganga: Gaura, lovelier than a jewel of pure gold, wearing a garland of fresh flowers and
leaning on Narahari, strolls with Nityananda and His companions, crying "Hari, Hari!"; He roars like a lion, the hairs
of His body stand on end; Gadadhara holds His left hand, Govinda sings; remembering the rasa of the Vrindavana lilas, the
Lord smiles softly and speaks with longing. Narahari Chakravarti quotes the pada in the *Bhakti-ratnakara*: Srinivasa is
shown the bank of the Ganga where the Lord sported with His companions. In PK it stands in the 18th pallava. Raga
kamoda.*[^36]

**11.1.** Lovelier than a jewel of pure gold is Gora's form,
rippling with waves of prema;
on His neck a garland of fresh flowers sways beyond compare,
and He leans upon Narahari.[^37]

**11.2.** *(Refrain)* He sports in supreme bliss:
taking Nityananda with Him, on the bank of the Ganga, rejoicing,
He cries with His own people: "Hari, Hari!"[^38]

## 12. Vasu Ghosha — "hari hari gorā kene kā̃de…" (PK 764)
*The Lord's bhava: His companions do not understand why Gora weeps; tears fall like pearls from a broken string;
remembering Vrindavana and looking at His own body, He calls "Rai, Rai!" and falls senseless; Gadadhara embraces Him and
says something into His ear — and the Lord laughs aloud. In PK it is the gaura-chandrika to the section on Radha's
prema-vaichittya (the beginning of the 9th pallava). Raga gandhara.*[^41]

**12.1.** *(Refrain)* Hari, Hari! Why does Gora weep?
His companions ask the reason,
gazing at the moon of Gora's face.[^42]

**12.2.** His reddened eyes, from the fullness of prema, grow twice as red;
the tears of prema stream and stream —
as if from a broken string pearls
were falling one after another.[^43]

**12.3.** Remembering Vrindavana, He sighs again and again,
looking at His own body;
pressing both hands to His breast, crying "Rai, Rai!",
He fell to the ground senseless.[^44]

**12.4.** Then dear Gadadhara embraced Him
and said something into His ear —
and again He laughs aloud, gladdening the people,
while Vasu Ghosha pines away in tears.[^45]

## 13. Vasu Ghosha — "aruṇa nayane dhārā bahe…" (PK 356)

clasping His feet. In PK it is the gaura-chandrika to the section on vasaka-sajja (6th pallava). Raga suhai.*[^46]

**13.1.** Streams flow from His reddened eyes;
Gora sits with His head bowed.

**13.2.** Seeing a shadow, He starts in His heart
and again and again rolls on the ground.[^47]

**13.3.** Having spread out lotus petals,
the Lord sits absorbed in meditation.[^48]

**13.4.** Sitting in seclusion, all alone,
He lives the bhava of vasaka-sajja.[^49]

**13.5.** Vasudeva Ghosha, seeing this,
says something to Him, clasping His feet.

## 14. Vasu Ghosha — "āji kene gorācā̃dera virasa vayāna…" (PK 370)
*The Lord in Radha's mood — khandita: Gorachand's face is cheerless, His eyes full of tears, His lips pale, His body
weary, His steps unsteady; the poet wonders where Gora was and in hope of what rasa He spent the night without sleep. In
PK it is the gaura-chandrika to the section on the khandita (6th pallava). Raga vibhasa or tori.*[^50]

**14.1.** Why is Gorachand's face so cheerless today?
What bhava is in His heart — His eyes are full of tears?[^51]

**14.2.** Why has the moon of His face withered — for what reason?
Why have His red lips grown pale?

**14.3.** His limbs are powerless with weariness — He cannot hold Himself up;
swaying and swaying, He falls whenever He takes a step.[^52]

**14.4.** Says Vasu Ghosha: where was Gora?
In hope of what rasa did He spend this night?[^53]
