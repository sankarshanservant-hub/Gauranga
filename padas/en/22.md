# Appendix. Padas signed "Narahari" from the Gaura-pada-tarangini — VI: the wedding with Vishnupriya; the beginning of the ashtakaliya

*Continuation of the appendix (the introductory note on the two Naraharis, on the arguments for attribution and on the marks in the
headings is in `17.md`). Portion 22: the end of GPT section 2.4 "The second marriage" — the wedding procession to the house of Sanatana
Mishra, the reception of the bridegroom, the wedding, the gods and celestial women at the wedding, the bridal chamber (vasara), the morning
after the wedding and the Lord's return home with Vishnupriya (nos. 1–16; GPT 2.4 nos. 18–20, 22–34); the beginning of section 5.2
"Ashtakaliya" — the songs of Gaura's awakening at the end of the night (nos. 17–26; GPT 5.2 nos. 1–10). Narahari Chakravarti quotes all the
wedding padas of the portion one after another in the 12th wave of the Bhakti-ratnakara (Ishana shows Srinivasa the house of Jagannatha
Mishra), and all the awakening songs in his Gaura-charita-chintamani (kiranas 2–3: the last part of the night and the awakening). Before
translation the signatures in these sections were checked throughout, in the OCR and on the scan; the Bhakti-ratnakara (ed. 1960, vv.
1491–1626) was read continuously. Not translated: 2.4 no. 17 — signed Ghanashyama (outside the appendix); no. 21 — a pada in nagari-bhava
(assigned to the section of nagari padas); nos. 35–38 — without the Narahari signature: an extract from Lochana Dasa's Chaitanya-mangala
(no. 38 is signed "দাস ত্রিলোচন"; translated with Lochana, `../lochana/en/22.md`, vv. ≈146–178); nos. 39–40 — Vrindavana Dasa (= CB Adi 15).
The base text is GPT1 (doubtful places from the scan), collated with GPT2, the BR (eds. 1912 and 1960) and the Gaura-charita-chintamani (ed.
1947); PK, KGC, PS and the Gita-chandrodaya have been checked. On Vishnupriya and the wedding rites as lila see note 2; on the motif of the
women of Nadia, note 5; on the ashtakaliya as eternal lila, note 36. The notes of the GPT editors are not reproduced.*

## 1. Narahari — "gaura rasika-śekharavara…" (GPT1 p. 100, section 2.4, no. 18; BR)
*The wedding procession: Gaura, surrounded by brahmanas, sets out for the wedding in a palanquin; instruments of the four kinds sound;
invisible celestial dancers dance together with earthly ones, the gandharvas sing with the singers; Ananta, Brahma, Shiva, Indra, Ganesha,
Parvati float in an ocean of joy; horses, elephants, guards, jesters, hundreds of banners; having gone round Nadia and paused on the bank of
the Ganga, the procession approaches Mishra's house. Raga not stated. **Attribution: probably Chakravarti** — the pada is in the BR; ornate
Vrajabuli.*[^1]

**1.1.** Gaura, best of the crowns of the connoisseurs of rasa, surrounded by His dear brahmanas — "joyfully I shall celebrate an auspicious
wedding!" — sets out, mounting the palanquin;
strings, metal, drums and winds — splendid instruments of the four kinds — sound in many manners: the sound has filled the vault of the sky.[^2]

**1.2.** All the instruments are splendid and new; the boom of the mardala increases the joy: "dho-dho dhigi taga dhilanga, dha-dha ni-ni
nidhiya";
invisible, the celestial dancers together with the [earthly] dancers dance the lasya without pause: "bhana taka-taka thai-thai-thai, ai ati
ni-ni-ni tiya".

**1.3.** Together with the singers, rejoicing, the gandharvas sing sweetly; shrutis, sweet gramas and the rest — in every manner they
display their delight;
the Thousand-mouthed One, Brahma, Mahesha, the lord of the gods with his retinue, Ganesha, Girija and others — how could they keep their
composure? — float in an ocean of joy.[^3]

**1.4.** Horses, elephants, many armed men; jesters display their arts; hundreds of banners and the like shine — the crowd blocks the road;
going round the town of Nadia, pausing again and again on the bank of the heavenly river — near Mishra's house Narahari beholds this
splendour.

[^1]: GPT1 — p. 100, section 2.4 ("the second marriage"), no. 18 ("যথারাগ"; scan, PDF p. 359); GPT2 — section 2.4, no. 18 (catalogue: NG-057). BR — 12th wave
(ed. 1912, pp. 809–810; ed. 1960, vv. 1495–1498), immediately after pada no. 17 on the same procession signed by Ghanashyama ("ঘন ঘনশ্যাম হিয় উমতাঅঈ";
= BR vv. 1491–1494 — outside the appendix); after it the BR gives all the wedding padas of this portion one after another (vv. 1499–1614), then prose:
Vishnupriya's service to her mother-in-law and the Lord's journey to Gaya (vv. 1615–1625). Variants: 1.1 GPT1 "সুরত চির" (misprint?), BR "সুরুচির" —
following the BR; 1.3 BR "সুরপতি" — the same. Not in PK, KGC, PS, the Gita-chandrodaya or the Gaura-charita-chintamani. B. The wedding with Vishnupriya —
CB Adi 15; CC Adi 16; Murari 1.14.
[^2]: Vishnupriya is the eternal consort of the Lord, His own Bhu-shakti (the energy of the Earth); she is not a worldly wife but His own
energy, and the wedding with her is the Lord's lila (note 30 of portion 21). The Lord is bound by nothing that is enjoined; He accepts the
wedding rites following the lila, for the joy of His mother and His associates (note 2 of portion 21). So are all the padas of the portion
about the Lord's "marrying" to be understood. The instruments "of the four kinds" (তত ঘন আনদ্ধ শুষির) — strings, metal (cymbals, gongs),
skin-covered (drums) and winds — are the classical division of Indian music.
[^3]: As in the pada on the wedding day (no. 21 of portion 21), the demigods gather at the Lord's wedding: Ananta-Shesha ("the Thousand-mouthed
One"), Brahma, Shiva (Mahesha), Indra ("the lord of the gods"), Ganesha, Parvati (Girija); invisible to men, they come to serve their Lord
and to behold His lila. The gandharvas are the celestial singers; shrutis and gramas are the intervals and scales of Indian music; lasya is
the tender, "feminine" kind of dance.

## 2. Narahari — "gorācā̃dera vivāha dekhivāre…" (GPT1 p. 100, no. 19; BR)
*The wives of noble families, with great longing, adorn themselves to see Gorachand's wedding: collyrium, braids with golden clasps,
sindura and sandal, necklaces, flowers in their ears, silk saris; with stately gait they go to the wedding, and on seeing the poet's Lord
they whisper. Raga not stated. **Attribution: probably Chakravarti** — the pada is in the BR.*[^4]

**2.1.** To see Gorachand's wedding, with what longing the wives of noble families adorn themselves — not one can keep her composure!

**2.2.** Absorbed in rasa, how charmingly they line their eyes with collyrium — sidelong glances, arched brows;
smooth braids fall down their backs — how lovely the golden clasps!

**2.3.** On the forehead a dot of sindura, how beautifully the sandal shines! Their ornaments glitter;
on their necks sway necklaces of gems and pearls; in their ears they put gandharaja and champaka flowers.

**2.4.** Putting on silk saris, leaving their homes, they walk with a stately gait like that of a royal elephant;
seeing the Lord Narahari — the heart overflows — one whispers something into another's ear.[^5]

[^4]: GPT1 — p. 100, no. 19 (scan, PDF p. 359); GPT2 — section 2.4, no. 19 (catalogue: NG-058). BR — 12th wave (ed. 1912, p. 810; ed. 1960, vv. 1499–1502).
Variant: in 2.3 the BR has the second half-lines interchanged ("…গন্ধরাজ চাঁপা দেই কাণে" in the first line, "…ঝলমল করে আভরণে" in the second). Not in PK,
KGC, PS, the Gita-chandrodaya or the Gaura-charita-chintamani. B. Line 2.1 is the refrain (ধ্রু). The first line is the same as that of the pada on the
first wedding (2.3 no. 22, no. 7 of portion 21), but the text is different.
[^5]: The poet describes the delight of the women of Nadia at the Lord's wedding in words close to the motif of the townswomen (nagaris)
enamoured of Gaura. We translate faithfully to the author; but the Gaudiya tradition (Vrindavana Dasa, Krishnadasa Kaviraja,
Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati) does not accept the worship of Gaura in the mood of "Gaura-nagari": Gaura is Krishna Himself in the mood and
lustre of Radha; He came to taste Her love, not to enjoy as a nagara. The Lord's attractiveness for the people of Navadvipa is the quality
of Bhagavan, who attracts all; it is not worldly passion. The same holds for the other padas of the portion about the women of Nadia (nos.
3, 11, 12; cf. note 5 of portion 21). GPT pada no. 21 ("আহা মরি কি মধুর রীতি": the "nagaris of Nadia" dream of being "with Gora day and night" and of
touching His golden body in the bridal chamber; = BR vv. 1507–1510) is openly in nagari-bhava and is assigned to the section of nagari padas.

## 3. Narahari — "sai ai dekha nadīyāra cā̃de…" (GPT1 p. 101, no. 20; BR)
*A woman says to her friend: look at the Moon of Nadia — hundreds of Kamadevas, offering themselves for His beauty, fall weeping at His
feet; His glance, His face, His smile; who has arranged this wedding attire — the crown, the curls, the yellow thread and durva, the golden
mirror? Raga not stated. **Attribution: probably Chakravarti** — the pada is in the BR.*[^6]

**3.1.** Friend, look — there is the Moon of Nadia! Offering themselves for His world-enchanting beauty, hundreds upon hundreds of Madanas
fall weeping at His feet.

**3.2.** The glance of His two eyes, drowning in rasa — I think the Creator made it to slay young women;
the beauty of His moon-face takes away the moon's pride; mingled with His smile, nectar pours down in streams.[^7]

**3.3.** Ah, I die, I die! With what longing of heart has someone arranged this wedding attire!
On His head a wondrous, dazzlingly bright crown, covered by His smooth, curling, lovely locks.

**3.4.** In His hand the yellow thread and the blades of durva enjoined for the auspicious rite; the incomparable golden mirror shines
beautifully;
His garments and ornaments are sweet; by the grace of every limb [He] enchants Narahari's mind.

[^6]: GPT1 — p. 101, no. 20 (scan, PDF p. 360); GPT2 — section 2.4, no. 20 (catalogue: NG-059). BR — 12th wave (ed. 1912, p. 810; ed. 1960, vv. 1503–1506).
The variants are minor (3.1 GPT1 "লৈঞা", BR "লৈয়া"; 3.3 GPT1 "এনা", BR "এ না"). Not in PK, KGC, PS, the Gita-chandrodaya or the Gaura-charita-chintamani. B.
Line 3.1 is the refrain. The next pada in the GPT and the BR (no. 21) is in nagari-bhava (note 5).
[^7]: "Made it to slay young women" is a conventional formula of love poetry about Krishna's beauty; here it speaks of the all-attractive
beauty of the Lord (see note 5). Madana is Kamadeva, the god of love; hundreds of Madanas "offer themselves" (নিছনি — give themselves as a
ransom) for His beauty: it surpasses the beauty of Kamadeva himself.

## 4. Narahari — "sanātana miśrera bhavane…" (GPT1 pp. 101–102, no. 22; BR)
*The reception of the bridegroom in the house of Sanatana Mishra: music sounds; Sanatana with his kin comes out to meet Him; the
bridegroom shines in the palanquin; around Him brahmanas and worthy men, a countless crowd; the blind, holding others' hands, stand
where Gaurachandra is, the lame come out on the road, the infirm totter along with sticks; all sing Gora's glories; children and old men
dance; hundreds of lamps burn; Mishra, embracing his son-in-law, brings Him into the house, seats Him on a wondrous seat, showers Him
with flowers and performs the reception of the bridegroom. Raga not stated. **Attribution: probably Chakravarti** — the pada is in the BR.*[^8]

**4.1.** What auspicious rites are performed in the house of Sanatana Mishra — who could describe them?

**4.2.** All kinds of splendid instruments sound; an immense joyful clamour surges up.

**4.3.** Sanatana with his kin comes forward to receive his jewel of a son-in-law.

**4.4.** How captivating the son-in-law's attire! He shines in the midst of the wondrous palanquin.

**4.5.** On all sides brahmanas and worthy men; the crowd of countless people cannot be counted.

**4.6.** The blind, holding others' hands, stand on the side where Gaurachandra is.[^9]

**4.7.** The lame come out on the main road and gaze to their heart's content at Gora's beauty.

**4.8.** Whoever cannot walk comes slowly along the road, leaning on a stick.

**4.9.** Who does not sing Gora's glories! They do not know how happiness grows in their hearts.

**4.10.** Various instruments sound in various manners; children and old men dance — no one can stand still.

**4.11.** Hundreds of great lamps burn; the light has filled the earth and the vault of the sky.

**4.12.** Someone displays some entertainment — and all that happens there spreads over the whole earth.

**4.13.** Mishra, greatly rejoicing, embracing his son-in-law, enters the house.

**4.14.** Seating [Him] on a wondrous seat, he showers [Him] with flowers, gazing at His moon-face.

**4.15.** Cries of "jaya, jaya" sound without pause; the musicians of both sides play their instruments in rivalry.

**4.16.** Mishra performs the reception of his son-in-law; Narahari, seeing it, refreshes his eyes.

[^8]: GPT1 — pp. 101–102, no. 22 (scan, PDF pp. 360–361); GPT2 — section 2.4, no. 22 (catalogue: NG-061; the number in the catalogue is garbled — "no. 2").
BR — 12th wave (ed. 1912, pp. 811–812; ed. 1960, vv. 1511–1526). Variants: 4.12 GPT1 "কুল-রঙ্গ", BR "কুন রঙ্গ" ("some entertainment") — following the BR;
others are minor. Not in PK, KGC, PS, the Gita-chandrodaya or the Gaura-charita-chintamani. B. The reception of the bridegroom in Sanatana's house — CB
Adi 15; in Lochana — `../lochana/en/22.md`, vv. 146–147.
[^9]: The blind, the lame, the infirm — an image by which the poet shows that the Lord draws all to Himself without distinction. "The
reception" (বরণ) is the honouring of the bridegroom by the bride's father: water for washing the feet, arghya, clothes, ornaments, a garland
(in Lochana, vv. 146–147, 153). "Of both sides" (দোঁহার) — the musicians of the bridegroom and of the bride.

## 5. Narahari — "nadīyāra śaśī vilasaye cāru…" (GPT1 p. 102, no. 23; BR)
*The bridegroom under the wedding canopy: Vishnupriya's mother, under various pretexts, looks at Him again and again, her feet not
touching the ground; with the married women skilled in auspicious rites she places rice and durva on His head, blesses Him, circles Him
seven times with a lamp and, going into the house, looks back at the poet's Lord and cannot take even half a step. Raga not stated.
**Attribution: probably Chakravarti** — the pada is in the BR.*[^10]

**5.1.** The Moon of Nadia shines charmingly under the wedding canopy — how sweet His array!
His youthful body surpasses gold and fresh butter — seeing His bearing, who could keep composure?

**5.2.** Again and again Vishnupriya's mother, under various pretexts, gazes [at Him] with unblinking eyes;
with what joy her heart overflows — her feet do not touch the ground!

**5.3.** Finely dressed, she comes with the married women, very skilled in auspicious rites;
on His lovely head she places rice and durva and blesses Him in a matchless way.

**5.4.** Lamp in hand she circles [Him] seven times; having performed the reception of the bridegroom, she goes into the house,
but, looking back at the Lord of Narahari, under the weight of tenderness she cannot take even half a step.[^11]

[^10]: GPT1 — p. 102, no. 23 (scan, PDF p. 361); GPT2 — section 2.4, no. 23 (catalogue: NG-062). BR — 12th wave (ed. 1912, p. 812; ed. 1960, vv. 1527–1530).
Variant: 5.4 GPT1 "চাহে পালটি না চলে পদ আধ" ("looks back — cannot take even half a step"), BR "চাহে পালটিয়া, চলে পদ আধ" ("looking back, takes half a
step") — following GPT1. The signature is garbled in the GPT1 OCR ("নরহবি"), so the signature-checking script misses the pada (portion 21). Not in PK,
KGC, PS, the Gita-chandrodaya or the Gaura-charita-chintamani. B.
[^11]: The wedding canopy (ছোঁড়লা) is the place of the rites in the courtyard of the bride's house. The reception of the bridegroom (বরে উরথিয়া)
is performed by the bride's mother and the married women: rice and durva on the head, a lamp, the sevenfold circling (in Lochana, vv.
149–152). The tenderness of the mother-in-law for the Lord is parental love, which He accepts from His associates.

## 6. Narahari — "sanātana miśrera gharaṇī…" (GPT1 pp. 102–103, no. 24; BR)
*The wedding: Sanatana's wife adorns her daughter; at Mishra's bidding Vishnupriya is brought out and seated on a throne under the canopy;
Vishnupriya first, offering a garland, gives herself to the Lord's feet; Gaura with a smile puts a garland round her neck; They shower each
other with flowers; Sanatana performs the kanya-dana, placing his daughter's hand in Vishvambhara's, and gives the dowry; at the end the
homa is performed. Raga not stated. **Attribution: probably Chakravarti** — the pada is in the BR.*[^12]

**6.1.** The wife of Sanatana Mishra performs so many customary rites — I cannot even tell them.

**6.2.** She swims in an ocean of happiness, adorning her daughter with many ornaments.

**6.3.** Seeing Vishnupriya's beautiful attire, joy grows boundlessly in everyone's heart.

**6.4.** The venerable Mishra at the auspicious moment told his dear ones to bring his daughter.

**6.5.** Mishra's house is captivating; the spacious courtyard glitters.

**6.6.** There the wedding canopy is beautiful; they brought the daughter and seated her on a throne.

**6.7.** All the rites there are by custom are also performed — and in this is boundless merriment.

**6.8.** First the goddess Vishnupriya, offering a garland, gave herself to the Lord's feet.[^13]

**6.9.** Smiling slightly, Gaura Raya put a garland of flowers round Vishnupriya's neck.

**6.10.** The two shower each other with flowers; each knows well what is in the other's heart.

**6.11.** Moment by moment the joy grows: Gaurachandra rejoices together with Vishnupriya.

**6.12.** What new beauty without end! On all sides the women raise cries of "jaya".

**6.13.** Everyone makes a clamour; various instruments sound — the sound pierces the sky.

**6.14.** The blessed Sanatana Mishra joyfully sat down to perform the kanya-dana.

**6.15.** Having performed the rites enjoined by the Vedas and the rest, he gave his daughter, placing her hand in Vishvambhara's.

**6.16.** Floating in happiness, he gave the dowry: splendid cows, wealth, land, a bed, menservants and maidservants.

**6.17.** At the very end the homa is performed: the daughter is seated at Vishvambhara's left.

**6.18.** How wondrous the sweetness of the two of Them! What can one say? Narahari gives himself for Them both.[^14]

[^12]: GPT1 — pp. 102–103, no. 24 (scan, PDF pp. 361–362); GPT2 — section 2.4, no. 24 (catalogue: NG-063). BR — 12th wave (ed. 1912, pp. 812–813; ed. 1960,
vv. 1531–1548). The variants are minor (6.10 GPT1 "কেলাকেলি", BR "ফেলাফেলি" — the same "shower each other"). Not in PK, KGC, PS, the Gita-chandrodaya
or the Gaura-charita-chintamani. B. The wedding — CB Adi 15; in Lochana — vv. 155–160.
[^13]: Vishnupriya is the eternal energy of the Lord (note 2); offering the garland, she "gives herself" to the Lord (আত্মসমর্পণ) — not a worldly
bride but His shakti, eternally serving Him. The exchange of garlands and flowers is a wedding custom; "each knows what is in the other's
heart" — the inseparability of the Lord and His energy.
[^14]: Kanya-dana is the "gift of the daughter" by her father; the homa is the fire sacrifice; the bride is seated at the bridegroom's left.
The Lord accepts these rites following the lila (note 2). "Gives himself for Them" (নিছনি) is a formula of love and devotion (cf. note 7).

## 7. Narahari — "dekhi pahũka vivāha mādhurī…" (GPT1 p. 103, no. 25; BR)
*The gods at the wedding: seeing the sweetness of the Lord's wedding, Shesha, Shiva, Brahma, Indra, Ganapati and others thrill; the
chariots of the gods block the path of the sky; invisible, telling one another of His deeds, the gods shower the wedding with flowers and
praise the good fortune of the brahmana Sanatana; the poet longs to be intoxicated with that joy. Raga not stated. **Attribution: probably
Chakravarti** — the pada is in the BR; Vrajabuli.*[^15]

**7.1.** Seeing the sweetness of the Lord's wedding, no one can stay steady:
Shesha, Shiva, Brahma, Indra, Ganapati and the others — their whole bodies thrill.

**7.2.** The crowd is immense: many chariots of the gods have blocked the path of the sky;
sweet cries of "jaya, jaya" sound — how it was cannot be told.

**7.3.** In great delight, telling one another of [the Lord's] wondrous deeds, full of rasa,
invisible, they pour down a rain of flowers, spreading lovely merriment.

**7.4.** Praising the great good fortune of the brahmana Sanatana, they are utterly enraptured;
the hope of the servant Narahari: will my mind become intoxicated with this joy?[^16]

[^15]: GPT1 — p. 103, no. 25 (scan, PDF p. 362); GPT2 — section 2.4, no. 25 (catalogue: NG-064). BR — 12th wave (ed. 1912, p. 813; ed. 1960, vv. 1549–1552).
Variants: 7.3 BR "অলক্ষিত" ("invisible") — the same; 7.4 GPT1 "ভাগভর", BR "ভাগ ভর" — the same. Not in PK, KGC, PS, the Gita-chandrodaya or the
Gaura-charita-chintamani. B.
[^16]: The demigods are the Lord's servants: invisibly they come to behold His lila and to serve it (note 3). Sanatana Mishra's good fortune
is that the Lord Himself has accepted his daughter. Shesha is Ananta; Ganapati is Ganesha.

## 8. Narahari — "deva-ramaṇīvṛnda viraci veśa…" (GPT1 p. 103, no. 26; BR)
*The celestial women at the wedding: adorned, with flashing bangles, their countless hosts roam the path of the sky and deem their eyes
rewarded at seeing Gaura's wedding; praising Mishra's house, they thrill; with full eyes they drink in the form of the Lord and His beloved
and give their lives for Them. Raga not stated. **Attribution: probably Chakravarti** — the pada is in the BR; Vrajabuli.*[^17]

**8.1.** The hosts of celestial women have adorned themselves in various ways;
[their ornaments] ring, the lustre of their conch-shell bangles flashes matchlessly.

**8.2.** Countless hosts roam the path of the sky, eagerness in their hearts;
they deem their eyes rewarded, seeing the wedding of the beautiful Gaura.

**8.3.** Praising the lovely custom of Mishra's house, they thrill all over;
new and ever new desires arise in them — composure cannot be kept.

**8.4.** With full eyes they drink in the form of the incomparable Lord and His beloved;
how much can Narahari say? — all give their lives for Them.[^18]

[^17]: GPT1 — p. 103, no. 26 (scan, PDF p. 362); GPT2 — section 2.4, no. 26 (catalogue: NG-065). BR — 12th wave (ed. 1912, pp. 813–814; ed. 1960, vv. 1553–1556).
Variants: 8.2 GPT1 "মানত দিঠি সকল নিরখি গৌরবর নিবাহ", BR "সফল", "বিবাহ" — following the BR; 8.1 "বাজত থর মাহি" (BR 1912 "বাজত থর, নাহি অতুল") — the
sense is unclear, the translation conjectural. Not in PK, KGC, PS, the Gita-chandrodaya or the Gaura-charita-chintamani. B.
[^18]: The celestial women, like the demigods, come to behold the Lord's lila and to serve Him (cf. no. 21 of portion 21: "we shall make merry
at the wedding together with the wives of Nadia"). "His beloved" (প্রেয়সী) is Vishnupriya, the eternal energy of the Lord (note 2). "They give
their lives" (প্রাণ নিছনি দেত) is a formula of love and devotion (note 7).

## 9. Narahari — "āhā mari mari suranārīgaṇa…" (GPT1 p. 103, no. 27; BR)
*The celestial women, watching the wedding of the Moon of Nadia, talk among themselves: Sanatana Mishra is beside himself with joy at
having given his daughter to Krishna; seating his daughter at his son-in-law's left he performs the homa but cannot take his eyes from His
face; Mishra's wife is as if maddened in the wedding bustle and, gazing at the poet's Lord, does not feel the ground under her feet. Raga
not stated. **Attribution: probably Chakravarti** — the pada is in the BR.*[^19]

**9.1.** Ah, I die! The celestial women, seeing the wedding of the Moon of Nadia,
all swim in that ocean of beauty and sate their thirsting eyes.

**9.2.** One says to another: "Look — Mishra Sanatana's heart cannot contain his joy!
With what longing he has given his daughter to Krishna — how much he says, how many gifts of every kind he gives!"[^20]

**9.3.** Another says: "Seating his daughter at his son-in-law's left, he deems himself blessed;
he performs the homa, but his mind is not on it — he keeps gazing at the moon-face".
