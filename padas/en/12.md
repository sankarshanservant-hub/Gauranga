# Vasu Ghosha: Padas from the *Gaura-pada-tarangini* — Appearance and Childhood, Sannyasa, the Lament of Nadia, Nilachala

*This chapter opens the group of padas known only from the *Gaura-pada-tarangini* (GPT1 — 1st ed. by Jagadbandhu
Bhadra, 1903; GPT2 — 2nd ed., 1934), excluding padas in nagari-bhava. Here are padas signed by Vasu (Vasudeva) Ghosha, in
the order of the lilas: praise of the Gaura-avatara; Gauranga's appearance on the night of the lunar eclipse, the
name-giving, His first steps and "the moon from the sky"; the Lord's morning in Navadvipa and the jhulana; Vishnupriya's
forebodings, the sannyasa at Katwa, the lament of Shachi and the devotees, the Lord with Advaita in Shantipura and His
departure for Nilachala; the lament of Shachi and Vishnupriya; the Lord in Nilachala, His bhava of Radha, and the festival
at Shrikhanda. Two padas were found in the *Padakalpataru* (PK) without a signature (nos. 1 and 15) — there the text
follows PK; no. 27 is an abridged recension of a pada by Govinda Dasa (PK 1463). Ten padas (nos. 1, 3–5, 9–12, 14, 17) were
missing from the stage-1 catalogue: they were found in a full check of the signatures of GPT1 (the catalogue section on Vasu
Ghosha has been supplemented). Majumdar considers the GPT unreliable for attributing padas to Vasu Ghosha: Jagadbandhu included spurious
ones as well; the childhood padas he regards as the poet's invention, and the festival at Shrikhanda (no. 29) and the
pada about Svarupa (no. 28) as forgeries. The attribution of all the padas of this chapter is therefore provisional. The
Bengali original, the variants of GPT1/GPT2 and PK, and the word-for-word translation are in the full version of the book.*

## 1. Vasu Ghosha — "abatāra baṛa abatāra kailā baṛa…" (PK 2347; GPT1 no. 68)
*Praise of the Gaura-avatara: there was no such mercy in any yuga; in every house there is the weeping of prema, the
name of Hari is proclaimed; Gaura has brought a boat laden with prema to the landing and opened a market; Jagai and
Madhai are saved by the name.*[^1]

**1.1.** A great incarnation, a great incarnation You have made!
Such mercy You have shown in no other yuga.

**1.2.** In every house the weeping of prema is heard;
in the age of Kali the name of Hari is proclaimed.[^2]

**1.3.** To the landing of the ocean of happiness You have brought a boat full of prema;
a fine market You have opened, Gaura — with wares of prema![^3]

**1.4.** Jagai and Madhai — they were two brothers:
by the name of Hari Chaitanya Gosani saved them.[^4]

**1.5.** Vasudeva Ghosha says: there will be no other such;
in the age of Kali blessed is the name — the jewel Chaitanya.

[^1]: PK: vol. 3, PDF p. 320 (printed p. 309), 4th shakha, last pada of the 23rd pallava (mark "॥১৬॥২৩৪৭॥"), MS P-R-Sa
no. 2451 — **without a signature** (in PK the pada ends at 1.4; it is preceded by PK 2346, signed by Balarama Dasa). GPT1 —
pp. 46–47, no. 68 (raga yatharaga), with the closing couplet 1.5 — the signature of Vasudeva Ghosha; GPT2 — sec. 1.3,
no. 68. Text of 1.1–1.4 follows PK, 1.5 follows GPT. The attribution to Vasu Ghosha is known only from GPT (a new catalogue
entry, VG-154). The first line reads differently in the PK manuscripts and in GPT ("abatāra kailā baṛa baṛa" etc.), see the full version of the book.
[^2]: "The weeping of prema" (premera kāndanā) — tears of love for God in kirtana, not worldly grief. The name of Hari as the
dharma of the age of Kali — CC Adi 3, 17.
[^3]: Images of trade: the boat (bharā — a boat's cargo; in the PK manuscripts bhārā, "load") comes in to the landing, and Gaura
opens a market (hāṭa) whose "wares" are prema, given away freely. Cf. the padas about the "market" of Nityananda and Gaura
(hāṭa-pattana).
[^4]: Jagai and Madhai — the villainous brothers of Navadvipa, saved by the Lord and Nityananda (CB Madhya 13; CC Adi 17).

## 2. Vasu Ghosha — "nadīyā-ākāśe āsi udila gaurāṅga-śaśī…" (GPT1 p. 51, no. 3)
*The appearance of Gauranga: in the sky of Nadia the moon Gauranga has risen, and the moon of the sky, out of shame,
has smeared its face with soot — the eclipse; conches and drums sound in every house; Jagannatha Mishra and Shachi are
beside themselves with joy; in the darkness of the eclipse gods mingle with men, celestial women together with the women
of Nadia gaze at the Child; the father gives gifts to the poor. Raga kalyana.*[^5]

**2.1.** In the sky of Nadia a moon has risen — Gauranga,
and all are drowned in delight;
and the moon of the sky, out of shame, has smeared its face with soot,
seizing the moment — as if in an eclipse.[^6]

**2.2.** The women cry aloud: "Jaya, jaya!" —
in every house bells and conches sound;
kettledrums, drums, gongs, shehnais, horns and flutes,
turi and bheri trumpets and the great victory drum.[^7]

**2.3.** Jagannatha Mishra is immersed in great bliss,
Shachi's joy knows no bounds:
seeing Nimai's face, she has forgotten the pains of childbirth
and, unblinking, gazes at her son's face.[^8]

**2.4.** In the darkness of the eclipse no one recognizes anyone —
gods have mingled with men;
together with the women of Nadia, celestial women, coming joyfully,
behold the beauty of Gauranga.[^9]

**2.5.** Gazing at his son's face, Jagannatha, overjoyed,
gives gifts to all the poor.
The world is full of bliss — the moon Gaura has risen:
this, says Vasu, is the fruit of the good fortune of living beings.

[^5]: GPT1 — p. 51, no. 3 (raga kalyana), checked against the scan (PDF pp. 310–311); GPT2 — sec. 2.1, no. 3. The pada is not in
PK, BR, KGC or PS. The preceding pada of GPT (no. 2, "jaya jaya kalaraba nadiyā nagare") is PK 1121, translated in chapter 7 (no. 6).
Majumdar regards Vasu's padas on the Lord's birth and childhood as the poet's invention: Vasu was not in Navadvipa at that
time (and the attribution of this pada is moreover known only from GPT). The Lord's appearance on the phalguna purnima
during a lunar eclipse, when everyone was chanting the name of Hari — CC Adi 13; CB Adi 2.
[^6]: A play of images: the moon Gauranga has eclipsed the moon of the sky, and it has hidden its face "out of shame" — this is
how the poet explains the eclipse on the night of the Lord's appearance.
[^7]: The shehnai (sānāi) is a wind instrument like an oboe; the bheur (bhem̐uṛa) is a horn; the turi and bheri are trumpets.
[^8]: The Lord's appearance is not the birth of a conditioned soul: He comes by His own will, in His eternal form, and Mother
Shachi, like Devaki and Yashoda, only in the sweetness of love experiences it as the birth of a son (CC Adi 13; Bhag. 10.3).
The words about "the pains of childbirth" convey her motherly experience in the humanlike lila.
[^9]: "The women of Nadia" (nadīyā-nāgarī) here are simply townswomen who came to see the Child, not "nagaris" in the sense of
nagari-bhava. The gods who came in human guise to see the newborn Lord — CB Adi 2.

## 3. Vasu Ghosha — "miśra purandara kichu mane bicāriyā…" (GPT1 p. 62, no. 1)
*The name-giving: Purandara Mishra calls the family priest and gives him gifts, and the priest places the arghya and his
blessing on the Child's head; Shachi asks that her son be given the name "Nimai". Raga suhai.*[^10]

**3.1.** Purandara Mishra, having considered something,
called to him the family priest, the best of brahmanas.

**3.2.** Wealth, jewels and ornaments he gave to the brahmana;
the brahmana, pronouncing his blessing, accepted the gift.

**3.3.** The arghya and the blessing the brahmana took in his own hands
and gladly placed them on Gorachand's head.[^11]

**3.4.** Shachi Thakurani began to speak:
"Providence has given me this son — He is to me as seven sons.[^12]

**3.5.** Give Him the name 'Nimai', O best of brahmanas!" —
so, joining his palms, says Vasudeva Ghosha.

[^10]: GPT1 — p. 62, section "Childhood pastimes" (bālyalīlā), no. 1 (raga suhai), checked against the scan (PDF p. 321); GPT2 —
sec. 2.2, no. 1. The pada is not in PK, BR, KGC or PS; it was missing from the catalogue (new entry VG-155). On Majumdar's view
of Vasu's padas on the Lord's childhood — note 5. Purandara is another name of Jagannatha Mishra. According to CB (Adi 3–4)
and CC (Adi 13–14), the name "Nimai" was given to the Child by the women (born under a nim tree — as a protection), while
at the name-giving ceremony the brahmanas named Him Vishvambhara; in the poem it is Shachi herself who asks for the name
"Nimai".
[^11]: Arghya — a respectful offering (water, rice, durva grass); here it is placed on the child's head as a blessing.
[^12]: "sāta putrera ei putra" — lit. "this son is (as) seven sons": an expression of the priceless worth of an only child. It may
also allude to the children Shachi lost before the Lord's appearance (according to CC Adi 13 — eight daughters).

## 4. Vasu Ghosha — "māyera aṅguli dhari śiśu gaurahari…" (GPT1 p. 63, no. 6)
*The first steps: holding His mother's finger, little Gaurahari toddles step by step, pulls her by the hand, stumbles and
falls; Shachi wants to take Him in her arms, but He, being stubborn, rolls on the ground; the mother wipes Him and kisses
Him. Raga beloar (tala dashakoshi).*[^13]

**4.1.** Holding His mother's finger, the child Gaurahari
toddles forward step by step.

**4.2.** Pulling His mother by the hand, He suddenly walks faster —
and, before going half a step, stumbles and falls.

**4.3.** Mother Shachi, brushing off the dust, goes to take Him in her arms —
but Gora, throwing a tantrum, rolls on the ground.

**4.4.** Saying "ah, ah", the mother wipes Him with the end of her sari,
takes Him in her arms and kisses the lotus of His face.

**4.5.** Vasu says: this child will roll in the dust —
how long, mother, will you hold Him back out of tenderness?[^14]

[^13]: GPT1 — p. 63, no. 6 (raga beloar, tala dashakoshi), checked against the scan (PDF p. 322); GPT2 — sec. 2.2, no. 6. The pada
is not in PK, BR, KGC or PS; new catalogue entry VG-156. The neighbouring GPT padas (nos. 2–5) are PK 1141, 1161, 1150, 1151
(translated in chapter 7, nos. 8–11). On Majumdar's view — note 5.
[^14]: The Lord's childhood play in the dust — CB Adi 4; Murari 1.6. The sweetness of these pastimes lies in Shachi's motherly
love (vatsalya), which keeps her from seeing her Son's majesty.

## 5. Vasu Ghosha — "pūrṇimā-rajanī cām̐da gagane udaya…" (GPT1 pp. 63–64, no. 7)
*The moon from the sky: on the night of the full moon little Nimai, seeing the moon, cries: "Give me the moon, mother!";
the moon does not come, and in despair He falls in the dust; Shachi gives Him a picture of Radha and Krishna — and He is
happy: in it, says the poet, the Lord sees His own face. Raga beloar (tala dashakoshi).*[^15]

**5.1.** On the night of the full moon the moon rose in the sky;
seeing the moon, Gorachand rejoiced in His heart.

**5.2.** "Give me the moon, mother!" — the child cries aloud;
raising her hand, Shachi calls: "Come, moon, come!"

**5.3.** The cruel moon does not come — Nimai is distraught:
weeping, He falls in the dust and tears His hair.

**5.4.** In Mishra's house there was a picture of Radha and Krishna:
to soothe her son, Shachi gave it into His hands.

**5.5.** Getting the picture, Gorachand rejoiced with all His heart;
Vasu says: in the picture, Lord, You behold Your own face.[^16]

[^15]: GPT1 — pp. 63–64, no. 7 (raga beloar, tala dashakoshi), checked against the scan (PDF pp. 322–323); GPT2 — sec. 2.2, no. 7.
The pada is not in PK, BR, KGC or PS; new catalogue entry VG-157. On Majumdar's view — note 5. The child asking for the moon is
a well-known motif of Krishna's childhood pastimes as well (cf. the story of the moon in a bowl of water).
[^16]: The sense of the signature: Gaura is Krishna Himself, who has taken the bhava and complexion of Radha (CC Adi 1.5; 4);
therefore in the picture of Radha and Krishna He sees Himself: in the Child's play the poet reveals the secret of Gaura — Radha and Krishna in one form (cf. the
verse of Svarupa Damodara, CC Adi 1.5).

## 6. Vasu Ghosha — "uṭha uṭha gorācām̐da niśi pohāila…" (GPT1 p. 347, no. 11)
*The awakening of Gaura: the night is over, Nadia has woken, the cuckoo is calling, the dawn has risen, the lotuses have
opened — how long will You sleep, Gora, in the languor of prema? Raga dhanashi.*[^17]

**6.1.** Rise, rise, Gorachand — the night is over;
all the people of Nadia have woken and risen.

**6.2.** The cuckoo calls "kuhu" — how charming its voice;
how long will You sleep, O Gora, jewel of virtues?

**6.3.** The dawn has risen, the lotuses have opened;
the moon has left the abode of the night lilies.[^18]

**6.4.** Vasudeva Ghosha, with joy in his heart, says:
how long will You sleep, Gora, in the languor of prema?

[^17]: GPT1 — p. 347, section "the eight periods of the day" (ashtakaliya), no. 11 (raga dhanashi); GPT2 — sec. 5.2, no. 11. The
pada is not in PK, BR, KGC or PS. Ashtakaliya songs are a meditation on the Lord's daily pastimes hour by hour; Majumdar
does not discuss such padas.
[^18]: The night lilies (kumudini) open under the moon and close at dawn; the lotuses do the opposite.

## 7. Vasu Ghosha — "bāyasa kokilakula ghughu dahiyāla-raba…" (GPT1 p. 351, no. 24)
*Morning and bath: the birds call, the companions call Gora; He rises, rubs His eyes, performs the morning duties, slowly
anoints Himself with fragrant oil and goes with everyone to the Ganga. Raga dhanashi.*[^19]

**7.1.** Crows, cuckoos, doves and magpie-robins are calling,
and together with them all the companions call Him.

**7.2.** Casting off drowsiness, Gora rises from His bed,
rubs His eyes with His hands and looks all around.

**7.3.** Having finished the morning duties with His companions,
Gora slowly anoints His body with fragrant oil.

**7.4.** Anointed with oil, they all go toward the Ganga;
Vasu Ghosha joyfully sings the lila of bathing.

[^19]: GPT1 — p. 351, section ashtakaliya, no. 24 (raga dhanashi); GPT2 — sec. 5.2, no. 24. The pada is not in PK, BR, KGC or PS.
The next GPT pada (no. 25, "jalakeli gorācām̐dera manete paṛila", the water play) is PK 1108, translated in chapter 5 (no. 7). The
dahiyal (dahiyāla) is the magpie-robin (Copsychus saularis), a songbird of Bengal.

## 8. Vasu Ghosha — "jhulata gorācām̐da sundara raṅgiyā…" (GPT1 p. 326, no. 17)
*Jhulana: Gorachand swings on the swing, overflowing with prema; in the bhava of Radha tears stream down His face;
understanding His bhava, Gadadhara swings at His left; "The flute!" Gora exclaims, gazing into his face. Raga
dhanashi.*[^20]

**8.1.** Gorachand swings, beautiful and joyous,
overflowing with the fullness of prema.

**8.2.** In the bhava of Radha streams of tears flood His face;
understanding His bhava, Gadadhara swings at His left.[^21]

**8.3.** "The flute!" He exclaims, gazing into Gadadhara's face;
Vasu Ghosha sings, remembering Gora's qualities.

[^20]: GPT1 — p. 326, section "festivals of the year", no. 17 (raga dhanashi), checked against the scan (PDF p. 585); GPT2 —
sec. 5.1, no. 17. The pada is not in PK, BR, KGC or PS. Vasu's other pada on the jhulana (PK 1550, where Gaura sits on the
swing with Gadadhara; according to Majumdar, an eyewitness testimony) is translated in chapter 4 (no. 5). Jhulana is the
swing festival of Radha and Krishna in the month of shravana.
[^21]: The bhava of Radha is pure prema, Radha's love for Krishna; in it the Lord tastes the sweetness of His own love (CC Adi 4).
The left side is Radha's place beside Krishna; Gadadhara, the Lord's eternal companion, understands His mood. The cry "The
flute!" is a remembrance of Krishna's flute that has seized the Lord in this bhava.

## 9. Vasu Ghosha — "biṣṇupriyā saṅginīre pāiyā birale…" (GPT1 p. 367, no. 4)
*Vishnupriya's foreboding: alone with a friend, she says that Nadia seems desolate to her today, the right side of her
body twitches, her earring has fallen, on the bank of the Suradhuni the trees have wilted, the current has stopped, the cuckoo
is silent; the poet fears that Gora may leave Navadvipa. Raga dhanashi.*[^22]

**9.1.** Vishnupriya, finding her friend alone,
with an anxious heart, faltering, says:

**9.2.** "Why does Nadia seem desolate to me today?
There is no ease in my body, both my eyes weep.

**9.3.** The right side of my body twitches, my right eye;
my earring has slipped and fallen.[^23]

**9.4.** On the bank of the Suradhuni the trees and creepers have wilted,
the bees do not drink honey, the leaves have withered.

**9.5.** Why has the current of the Jahnavi stopped?
The cuckoo is not heard — as if it had grown mute."

**9.6.** A great fear has settled in Vasu's heart:
lest Gora, the king of brahmanas, leave Navadvipa!

[^22]: GPT1 — p. 367, section "sannyasa", no. 4 (raga dhanashi), checked against the scan (PDF p. 626). In the scan of GPT2 (IA
340178) p. 235 (end of sec. 5.2) is followed by p. 236, which begins with the end of no. 8 of sec. 5.3: padas nos. 1–8
(including this one and the next) are missing from it. The pada is not in PK, BR, KGC or PS; new catalogue entry VG-158. The
neighbouring GPT padas are on the same theme (no. 3 — Govinda Ghosha, no. 5 — Madhava).
[^23]: According to folk belief, the twitching of the right eye and the right side of the body is an ill omen for a woman.

## 10. Vasu Ghosha — "pāgalinī biṣṇupriyā bhijā bastra cule…" (GPT1 p. 367, no. 6)
*Vishnupriya, as if distraught, runs home in wet clothes and, weeping, tells Shachi of the ill omens: while she was
bathing her nose ring fell into the water, her right eye twitches, she keeps seeing a snake on her right; the poet: today
the lord of your life will leave Navadvipa. Raga dhanashi.*[^24]

**10.1.** Vishnupriya, as if distraught, with wet clothes and wet hair,
hurriedly comes home and speaks to her mother-in-law —

**10.2.** but she cannot say anything, breathless with weeping.
Shachi says: "My dear, why are you so distressed?"

**10.3.** Vishnupriya says: "What more can I say, mother?
On all sides there are ill omens — my soul trembles.

**10.4.** As I was bathing, my nose ring fell into the water:
my fortune will break, a thunderbolt will fall on my head.[^25]

**10.5.** Again and again my soul weeps, my right eye twitches,
on my right I keep seeing as if a snake."

**10.6.** Weeping, Vasu Ghosha says: "What shall I say, O faithful wife?
Today the lord of your life will leave Navadvipa."

[^24]: GPT1 — p. 367, no. 6 (raga dhanashi), checked against the scan (PDF p. 626); missing from the GPT2 scan (note 22). The pada is
not in PK, BR, KGC or PS; new catalogue entry VG-159. The night before the Lord's departure and Vishnupriya's grief — CC Adi 17;
CB Madhya 25–26; in Lochana — Madhya-khanda.
[^25]: The nose ring (beśara) is a sign of a married woman; its loss is an ill omen for the husband.

## 11. Vasu Ghosha — "prabhu kahe nijaguṇe deota sannyāsa…" (GPT1 p. 369, no. 11)
*Katwa: the Lord asks Keshava Bharati to give Him sannyasa; the people of Katwa try to dissuade Him, Bharati recalls
that the scriptures allow sannyasa after fifty; the Lord replies: if death comes earlier, when will there be the company
of saints? Bharati agrees, the barber is called; he does not dare touch the Lord's head — the Lord tells him to give up
his trade and promises him a happy life by Krishna's grace. Raga shri.*[^26]

**11.1.** The Lord says: "By your grace give Me sannyasa!"
— "Do not become a sannyasi, Nimai, do not shave Your hair!" —

**11.2.** so all the people of Kanchana-nagara try to dissuade Him:
"Do not take sannyasa, dear child, go back home!"[^27]

**11.3.** "When one is past fifty and the passions have ceased,
only then do the scriptures allow sannyasa to be given."[^28]

**11.4.** Hearing this, the Lord speaks thus:
"What do I know to say before you, O guru?

**11.5.** If death should come before fifty,
when then will there be the company of saints?"

**11.6.** Hearing this, Bharati Gosani says:
"I will give You sannyasa — listen, Nimai!"

**11.7.** Hearing this, the Lord was filled with joy and delight;
then they called the barber to shave His head.

**11.8.** The barber says: "Master, I humbly submit:
there is no such person in the three worlds!

**11.9.** Having touched Your head with my hand, whose feet shall I touch then?
Say what you will, Master — my whole body trembles.

**11.10.** Touching whose feet shall I now shave every day?
I am a barber of low caste — such is my custom."[^29]

**11.11.** Hearing this, Vishvambhara Raya speaks,
the Lord says: "Do not follow your trade any longer.

**11.12.** By Krishna's grace you will pass your life in happiness,
and at the end your way will lead to the abode of Vishnu."

**11.13.** The people of Kanchana-nagara are kind-hearted;
Vasu Ghosha, with joined palms, speaks to Bharati…

[^26]: GPT1 — p. 369, section "sannyasa", no. 11 (raga shri), checked against the scan (PDF p. 628); GPT2 — p. 236, sec. 5.3, no. 11
(scan PDF p. 525). The pada is not in PK, BR, KGC or PS; new catalogue entry VG-160. The preceding GPT pada (no. 10, "kāñcana nagare
eka bṛkṣa manohara", with the double signature of Krishnadasa and Vasu) is PK 2223 (VG-136), translated in chapter 4 (no. 12). The sannyasa at
Katwa — CB Madhya 28; CC Adi 17, Madhya 3; Lochana, Madhya-khanda; Murari 2.18. Keshava Bharati is the Lord's sannyasa-guru.
[^27]: Kanchana-nagara is Katwa (Kantaka-nagara) on the Ganga, where the Lord took sannyasa.
[^28]: Apparently the words of Keshava Bharati (GPT1 gives them in quotation marks without naming the speaker): sannyasa was usually
taken in old age, after the stages of grihastha and vanaprastha. The Lord's answer (11.4–11.5) is the word of a man seeking sannyasa: the Lord, who teaches by His own
example, speaks of the brevity of human life, not of Himself — time has no power over Him.
[^29]: The barber's trade is shaving and paring nails, touching people's feet; having touched the Lord's head, he can no longer
touch the feet of others (the same motif in Lochana, where the barber is called Haridasa (in the 1983 ed.), and in Govinda's *Karcha*, where he is
Deva).

## 12. Vasu Ghosha — "madhuśīla bale gosāñi nā bhām̐ṛāo more…" (GPT1 p. 369, no. 12)
*Continuation: the barber Madhushil says to the Lord: "Do not deceive me — I know You are God Himself; I shall do Your
will, but first place Your feet on my head"; the Lord places His foot on his head. Raga shri.*[^30]

**12.1.** Madhushil says: "Gosani, do not deceive me:
You are Brahma, You are Vishnu — I know it in my heart.[^31]

**12.2.** I shall fulfil Your wish — You are all-powerful;
I shall obey Your order without doubt.

**12.3.** You say: by Krishna's grace I shall live happily,
and after death my way will lead to the abode of Vishnu.

**12.4.** The Krishna who will keep me in happiness — that is You;
Your feet are my abode of Vishnu: what else do I know?

**12.5.** I shall shave Your curly hair, I shall touch Your head with my hand —
but first, Lord, place Your holy feet on my head."

**12.6.** At these sweet words the Lord placed His foot on his head —
the foot, says Vasu, before which the post of Brahma is trifling.

[^30]: GPT1 — p. 369, no. 12 (raga shri), checked against the scan (PDF p. 628); GPT2 — p. 236, sec. 5.3, no. 12. The pada is not in
PK, BR, KGC or PS; new catalogue entry VG-161. The barber's name is Madhushil (madhuśīla; Shil is a surname of the barber caste).
[^31]: "You are Brahma, You are Vishnu" — the simple words of a barber who has recognized God in the Lord; according to Gaudiya
teaching Gaura is Krishna Himself, the Supreme Lord, the source of both Vishnu and Brahma (CC Adi 2) — which the barber himself
says in 12.4: "that Krishna is You".

## 13. Vasu Ghosha — "prabhura muṇḍana dekhi kānde yata paśu pākhī…" (GPT1 p. 370, no. 15)
*After the shaving: beasts, birds and people weep, a cow runs to the Lord with grass in her teeth, all stand silent like
trees; the Lord, in kaupina and with staff, asks the Vaishnavas to bless Him so that His sannyasa may not become a mockery
and He may attain the Lord of Vraja in Vraja, and runs crying "Where is Vrindavana?" through the land of Rarh, with
Nityananda beside Him. Raga pahida.*[^32]

**13.1.** Seeing the Lord shaven, all the beasts and birds weep,
all the dwellers of the holy place weep;[^33]

**26.5.** The eyes were cooled, seeing the face of Sri Gauranga,
by the touch the heart was soothed:
"I shall not let Him go again — I shall keep Him in my heart!" —
and Vasu Ghosha's bliss increased.

[^65]: GPT1 — pp. 409–410, section "antya-lila" (the final pastimes), no. 23 (raga dhanashi), checked against the scan (PDF pp. 668–669);
GPT2 — sec. 5.5, no. 23. The pada is not in PK, BR, KGC or PS. In GPT the pada stands before Vasu's pada on the coming of the people of
Nadia to the Lord (no. 24 = PK 2041, chapter 9, no. 6), i.e. it concerns the Lord's coming to Navadvipa on His way to Vrindavana
(1514/15; CC Madhya 16; CB Antya 3). The poet depicts this meeting in his own way: with him Gadadhara is in Nadia (according to CC
Madhya 16 Gadadhara accompanied the Lord from Puri to Cuttack) and the Lord resolves to stay.
[^66]: "Crosses a yojana in a moment" — hearing the devotees' call, the Lord comes to them, however long the way; the power of His love
for His devotees.
[^67]: The reading "gadādhara-bhāṣā" (lit. "Gadadhara's speech") is found in both editions of GPT; possibly a corruption of "gadagada bhāṣā" ("in a
choked voice").

## 27. Vasu Ghosha — "āju re kanakācala nīlācale gorā…" (GPT1 p. 341, no. 64)
*Phagu in Nilachala: Gora, like a golden mountain, is lost in the play of colours together with Govinda; on His neck sways a garland of
bakula, red with colour; the devotees, red with colour, sing sweetly; bhavas spread over His body, His eyes are half-closed in waves of
prema; looking at Gadadhara, He smiles softly. Raga todi.*[^68]

**27.1.** Today Gora, a golden mountain, in Nilachala
together with Govinda is lost in the play of colours.[^69]

**27.2.** On His neck sways a garland of bakula, red with colour;
the devotees, red with colour, sing sweetly.

**27.3.** So many bhavas arise — they have spread over all His body;
His eyes are half-closed in waves of prema.

**27.4.** Looking at Gadadhara, He smiles softly, softly —
that smile Vasudeva Ghosha did not understand.

[^68]: GPT1 — p. 341, section "festivals of the year", no. 64 (raga todi), checked against the scan (PDF p. 600); GPT2 — sec. 5.1, no. 64. The
pada repeats almost word for word — shorter and with the signature of Vasudeva Ghosha — the pada of **Govinda Dasa** PK 1463
("nīlācale kanakācala gorā…", vol. 2; = GPT1 p. 341, no. 66; also in PS), which Jagadbandhu placed right here, one pada later. Probably this is
a recension of Govinda Dasa's pada which, by mistake or by singers' custom, received Vasu's signature; the attribution to Vasu Ghosha is
highly doubtful. Another pada on the phagu in Nilachala is by Madhavi Dasa (PK 2290; chapter 6).
[^69]: Govinda is Krishna; in Puri, at the festival of Dola-yatra, His festival Deity (Dola-Govinda) is sprinkled with coloured powder.
Perhaps the poet means the Lord's play with the Deity at this festival.

## 28. Vasu Ghosha — "svarūpera kare dhari bale kām̐di gaurahari…" (GPT1 pp. 306–307, no. 25)
*The Lord in the bhava of Radha disappointed in Her waiting (vipralabdha): holding Svarupa's hand, He weeps — the night has passed in
vain, the moon has set, Shyama has not come; the bed of flowers has wilted, the garland has withered; "Come, Svarupa, to the Suradhuni, let us
set all this afloat; let honour and family be lost — I shall throw Myself into the water"; the poet: where can the pain of this heart be
eased? Raga kamoda.*[^70]

**28.1.** Holding Svarupa's hand, weeping, Gaurahari says:
"My Shyama is not with Me!
In vain have I spent the night — the moon has set;
My heart is breaking.

**28.2.** *(Refrain)* Where is My beloved Shyama?
The bed of flowers has wilted, the garland has withered —
I have not tasted the honey of Shyama's love.

**28.3.** Come, Svarupa, let us go to the waters of the Suradhuni —
let us set all this afloat.
Let honour and family be lost — I shall live no more:
I shall leap into the water and give up My life.[^71]

**28.4.** That dark moon of Mine — in whose bower does He spend the night?
Why has He turned away from Me?"
Says Vasudeva Ghosha: in this sorrow the heart burns —
where can the pain of this heart be eased?[^72]

[^70]: GPT1 — pp. 306–307, section on the vipralabdha, no. 25 (raga kamoda), checked against the scan (PDF pp. 565–566); GPT2 — sec. 4.5,
no. 25. The pada is not in PK, BR, KGC or PS. Majumdar considers that the pada does not belong to Vasu: Svarupa Damodara was not the Lord's
companion in Navadvipa (at that time he was called Purushottama Acharya), while the Suradhuni (the Ganga) here points to Navadvipa. Cf. another GPT pada
with the same signature and the same opening, "svarūpera kare dhari gorārāya" (chapter 3, no. 14). Such bhavas of the Lord in Puri, in the
company of Svarupa — CC Antya 14–19.
[^71]: Radha's words in separation: "let honour and family be lost" is a sign of prema that gives up for Krishna everything the world
values; this is not worldly passion but the highest, pure love (CC Adi 4). The Lord in the bhava of Radha speaks Her words (Svarupa in
this lila is Radha's friend Lalita). "I shall leap into the water" is the utmost expression of separation
(vipralambha), in which prema reaches its peak.
[^72]: "The dark moon" (kālaśaśī) is Krishna; the kunja is a bower-grove in Vrindavana.

## 29. Vasu Ghosha — "ki ānanda khaṇḍapure ṭhākura naraharira ghare…" (GPT1 p. 353, no. 30)
*The festival at Shrikhanda: in the house of Narahari Thakura all the mahantas gather, the twelve gopalas, the sixty-four mahantas and the
six Gosvamis; Srinivasa seats everyone according to rank, in the middle — Gaurahari between the two Prabhus, Nityananda and Advaita;
Raghunandana serves the food, Mahaprabhu puts a mouthful into Nitai's mouth, Narahari and Gadadhara feed each other; after the meal
Srinivasa gives out garlands and sandal. Raga dhanashi.*[^73]

**29.1.** What bliss in Shrikhanda, in the house of Narahari Thakura!
Who can describe the joy of this festival?
All the mahantas have come and, floating in the rasa of the bliss of prema,
gaze at the moon of Gaura's face.

**29.2.** The twelve gopalas and with them the sixty-four mahantas,
and then the six Gosvamis;
all the branches and sub-branches — all the devotees have come,
joyfully singing Gaura's qualities.[^74]

**29.3.** Srinivasa seated each one in his place —
the mahantas sat in rows;
according to each one's standing he seated them all,
and between the two Prabhus — Gaurahari.

**29.4.** On the right — Nityananda, on the left — Advaitachandra,
and to His left — Gadadhara Acharya.
All sat down to the meal; then Raghunandana came
and began to serve the food.

**29.5.** Mahaprabhu in joyful delight, taking a mouthful in His hand,
puts it into Nitai's mouth;
so also Narahari and Gadadhara feed
each other in the happiness of prema.

**29.6.** After the meal — the cry: "Glory to Gaura, jewel of brahmanas!" —
and all together rinsed their mouths.
Srinivasa in joyful delight, taking mouth-fresheners,
gave everyone garlands and sandal.

**29.7.** Blessed is Narahari Thakura, in whose house Sri Chaitanya
is present Himself — together with Nityananda!
Seeing this, the Vaishnavas cry "Hari!" again and again —
Vasu begs for His two feet.[^75]

[^73]: GPT1 — p. 353, section ashtakaliya, no. 30 (raga dhanashi), checked against the scan (PDF p. 612); GPT2 — sec. 5.2, no. 30. The pada is
not in PK, BR, KGC or PS. Majumdar cites it as an example of forgery in GPT: the participation of the six Gosvamis is impossible — they
never left Vrindavana together; moreover, the festival is arranged by Srinivasa Acharya, who came to Shrikhanda only after the Lord's
departure (*Bhakti-ratnakara*, *Prema-vilasa*). The attribution to Vasu Ghosha is unreliable (D).
[^74]: The twelve gopalas are companions of Nityananda (in the bhava of the cowherd boys of Vraja); the sixty-four mahantas are
devotee-preachers in Bengal (a later tradition); the six Gosvamis are Rupa, Sanatana, Jiva, Gopala Bhatta, Raghunatha Bhatta and
Raghunatha Dasa. "Branches and sub-branches" — the image of the tree of Chaitanya (CC Adi 10–12).
[^75]: The presence of Chaitanya and Nityananda "Themselves" at a festival arranged by Srinivasa is understood in the tradition as Their
appearance (avirbhava) in the devotees' kirtana: thus, according to the *Bhakti-ratnakara* and the *Narottama-vilasa*, the Lord with His
companions appeared at the festival in Kheturi. The poet, however, describes the festival as if the Lord were present in person, together
with Gadadhara, Advaita and Narahari.
