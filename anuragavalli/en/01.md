# Manjari 1. Mangalacharana and the Life of Gopala Bhatta (beginning)

*Sanskrit verses of salutation; a song on the Lord's appearance in Navadvipa; obeisances to Chaitanya, Nityananda, Advaita, Gadadhara, Srivasa and the Vaishnavas; on the books of Vrindavana Dasa, Lochana Dasa and Krishnadasa Kaviraja; the author's plan — to describe his guru's branch*

**1.** *I bow to that Sri Guru by whose renowned mercy I have obtained the best of names, the mantra, the son of Shachi, Svarupa, Rupa and his elder brother, the great city of Mathura, the cowherd villages, Radha-kunda, the best of hills and — oh! — the hope of Radhika and Madhava.*[^1]

**2.** *I bow to the beautiful lotus feet of Sri Guru, to the gurus and the Vaishnavas, to Sri Rupa with his elder brother, with his companions and Raghunatha, to Jiva with them; to Krishna Chaitanya with Advaita, the Avadhuta and His associates, to the feet of Sri Radha and Krishna with Their companions, Lalita, Sri Vishakha and the others.*[^2]

**3.** *Song in raga Prema-sindhu:*
Sri Krishna Chaitanya-chandra is the son of the king of Vraja:
together with His associates of Vraja He sports eternally.
In Sri Navadvipa, by the River of the Gods,
there Prabhu appeared together with His own.

**4.** *"Gaura is born," says the Shruti; leaving His own abode in Vraja, for His own purpose He appeared with them in Gauda and made the whole world float on the waves of prema; He is the single body of the Two who are full of rasa, especially tasting…*[^3]

**5.** His endless pastimes Vrindavana Dasa
described in the *Sri Chaitanya-bhagavata*.

**6.** What remained in it in the form of sutras
Thakura Lochana told in particular:

**7.** the book *Sri Chaitanya-mangala*, full of rasa,
he revealed in the form of songs — that was his intent.

**8.** What those two did not expand in detail,
that, setting it out in detail, was spread

**9.** by Sri Krishnadasa Kaviraja Mahashaya:
his book is the *Sri Chaitanya-charitamrita*.

**10.** These books became renowned on earth;
even fools came to know the secret siddhanta of Chaitanya.

**11.** The embodiment of compassion, separation and peace, an ocean of mercy,
the friend of the low, the unfortunate and the fallen —

**12.** with a surging wave He flooded the three worlds;
there was no discrimination at all — that is the reason.

**13.** Such a Merciful One I have never seen or heard of,
by hearing of whom all hearts melt.

**14.** Together with His associates I take shelter at Mahaprabhu's feet,
bowing countless times, (asking) forgiveness for offences.

**15.** What can I say of my faults — however many I recall,
the one hope of all is the name "Purifier of the Fallen".

**16.** I bow to Nityananda Raya, Prabhu's elder brother,
whose title "Purifier of the Fallen" the three worlds sing;

**17.** by whose mercy one attains Sri Krishna Chaitanya,
who out of mercy made the land of Gauda blessed.

**18.** If anyone even once says "Nityananda" with love —
at once gooseflesh, tears, trembling, a faltering voice.

**19.** Whoever was hostile to him, to him he showed mercy —
Madhai in the town of Nadia is witness to this.

**20.** With devotion I bow to Sri Advaita Acharya-chandra,
by whose mercy one attains Chaitanya and Nityananda;

**21.** at whose call those two descended,
by whose mercy the world was delivered.

**22.** I bow to Sri Pandita Gosani, Prabhu's own shakti,
by whose mercy firm devotion to Chaitanya arises.

**23.** The devotees headed by Srivasa I praise, taking courage,
by whose breeze the three worlds became Vaishnavas.

**24.** Show mercy to me, fallen as I am, all of you:
may my heart's desire be fulfilled, may my heart melt.

**25.** Whoever takes your name even with duplicity
will not be deprived — so say the sadhus and scriptures.

**26.** In this hope I take shelter at your feet:
if you neglect me, I have no other way — this is settled.

**27.** My way, bad as it is, is you, the purifiers of the fallen;
on every side I am put to shame — this is my plea.

**28.** Whatever pleases you all, do that:
in no way can (I) be neglected.

**29.** Being low, I say this with joy in my heart,
daring to take shelter at Prabhu's lotus feet.

**30.** Firm faith in the "Purifier of the Fallen" is (my) faith;
I write without duplicity — listeners, do not laugh.

**31.** Whoever delights in hearing the *Anuraga-valli*,
his two feet are the ornament of my head.

**32.** And now hear — I shall tell further of my desire,
to tell of the path of succession of my guru.

**33.** Mahaprabhu descended in the land of Sri Gauda,
and the earth became blessed by the sight of Him;

**34.** for forty-eight years His manifest pastimes lasted,
and His own family (of associates) became endless.

**35.** In the Adi-khanda, in the tenth and eleventh chapters
and in the twelfth, this is told — listen especially:

**36.** how many branches there were on the whole circle of the earth —
this cannot be written even with a thousand mouths;

**37.** and among them the many branches in Gauda and Utkala:
these too are not counted, not written.

**38.** In those three chapters the principal persons
he wrote down, merely pointing the way.[^4]

**39.** The first twenty-four years — the pastimes in Navadvipa,
the last eighteen years — the pastimes in Nilachala,

**40.** and in the middle six years of travelling;
who can describe His wanderings everywhere?

**41.** How He toured the South
is told in part in the *Chaitanya-charitamrita*:

**42.** in the Madhya-khanda see the ninth chapter,
relish the pilgrimage to the South.

**43.** There too He gained countless associates:
in describing the branches, did he not show them?

**44.** I shall tell of one branch, so that the chain of gurus may be known.
Wandering, Prabhu came to Ranga-kshetra;

**45.** on the bank of the Kaveri He saw Sri Ranganatha,
danced and sang with the devotees.

**46.** In that holy place lived the best of the brahmanas of Telingana,
named Sri Trimalla Bhatta, (head of) the brahmana community;

**47.** his younger and elder — two brothers:
Venkata and Prabodhananda Bhatta, so they sing.[^5]

**48.** Venkata Bhatta came and invited Prabhu,
and He, seeing his Vaishnavism, accepted the invitation.

**49.** Having bathed at midday, Prabhu came to his house,
and seeing (Him), the whole family was filled with prema.

**50.** Prostrating himself like a rod, (Venkata) washed His feet
and drank the water from His feet with all his family.

**51.** Seating Him on a worthy seat, he fed Him —
how many different dishes there were, how can I describe them?

**52.** After the meal, offering mukhavasa, clasping His feet,
becoming humble and lowly, he made a request:

**53.** "I am very afraid to say one word,
yet if I do not say it, I cannot bear the grief.

**54.** Now the four months of the rains have come, Prabhu:
at this time no one travels to the holy places.

**55.** If out of mercy You stay here,
to serve You is the constant desire of my heart."

**56.** Prabhu was greatly pleased by his words:
consenting to his service, He stayed with him.

**57.** Bathing in the Kaveri, seeing Ranganatha,
kirtana and dancing with the devotees in joy;

**58.** meals in someone's house, the mahaprasada of Sri (Ranganatha);
wandering (in the mood of) Vrindavana, which raises madness —

**59.** there He stayed, having reached the limit of happiness;
thus the chaturmasya was completed.

**60.** Trimalla's boy, named Gopala Bhatta,
guilelessly served the abode of Gaura.

**61.** His father, knowing his good character,
gladly set him to attend on Him.

**62.** For four months he served in countless ways —
all of it cannot be told in detail.

**63.** A golden complexion, learning, sweet speech,
his whole body beautiful, full of streams of loveliness.

**64.** Knowing Mahaprabhu's wishes,
he gladly fulfilled them before He spoke.

**65.** Seeing the expertise of his service, He was pleased every moment
and showed mercy to his family, the servants and maids.

**66.** Formerly they were all Sri Vaishnavas,
worshippers of Narayana together with Lakshmi;

**67.** obtaining Prabhu's sight, touch and the nectar of His mercy,
all of them with their own became worshippers of Radha and Krishna.

**68.** By Mahaprabhu's mercy — great mahabhava;
something of this is revealed in the *Chaitanya-charitamrita*:

**69.** in the Madhya-khanda, in the first chapter,
listing in sutras the pastimes of the Madhya-lila,

**70.** within the description of the travels in the South —
Prabhu goes to Ranga-kshetra:

**71.** there He took His meals in the house of Trimalla Bhatta
and at the Bhatta's request spent the chaturmasya.

**72.** In the ninth chapter this sutra is expanded:
there (is named) his younger brother Venkata, as written.[^6]

**73.** Trimalla Bhatta's chain of gurus and the rest
was left out — therefore there is an omission in the writing.

**74.** Venkata's younger is named Prabodhananda:
he is the earlier guru of Gopala Bhatta — this is the evidence.[^7]

**75.** Study and upanayana, proper conduct —
all his training earlier (he received) from his paternal uncle.

**76.** Then came the sight of Mahaprabhu's feet:
all was accomplished, and the earlier is recorded.

**77.** Especially (feeling) great attachment to his teacher,
(Gopala Bhatta) honoured him above other ties.[^8]

**78.** Sanatana Gosani composed the *Hari-bhakti-vilasa* —
in its mangalacharana he revealed this.

**79.** Here (is the verse):
*"Gopala Bhatta, disciple of Prabodhananda, dear to Bhagavan, compiles this 'Bhakti-vilasa' for the pleasure of Raghunatha Dasa and of Rupa and Sanatana."*[^9]

**80.** Its meaning (according to the commentary):
*"In the commentary 'Dig-darshini' on the 'Hari-bhakti-vilasa', composed by Sanatana Gosvami: 'Bhakti-vilasa', because in it Bhagavan delights with great bliss; the book is called 'Bhakti-vilasa' because it collects the pastimes of bhakti — this is the one reason indicated here. 'Bhagavat-priya' — (said) as a compound by virtue of a special sense: he to whom Bhagavan is dear — thereby the greatness of Prabodhananda is indicated; and his disciple Sri Gopala Bhatta is (to be understood) as like him. Sri Raghunatha Dasa is a great bhagavata, the jewel of the Kayastha clan of Gauda; Srimat Rupa and his elder brother Sanatana are his intimates; may they be pleased.'"*[^10]

**81.** The meaning of this commentary I shall tell in brief,
firm knowledge heard from the lips of great souls:

**82.** Sri Sanatana Gosani composed the book,
but in every place gave the enjoyment (of credit) to Bhatta Gosani;

**83.** by this he showed the wave of prema between the two,
in which there is no difference — most intimate.

*[p. 9]*

**84.** Now listen attentively to the meaning of the shloka:
Sri Sanatana's words are most convincing.

**85.** Sri Rupa, Sanatana, Raghunatha Dasa —
to give joy to them all, the *Hari-bhakti-vilasa*

**86.** he compiled, chiefly from the *Sri Bhagavata*,
searching out the statements of all the Puranas.

**87.** Bhagavan, bhakti, the good conduct befitting devotees —
he showed the farthest shore of all these truths.

**88.** (The shloka) names Sri Gopala Bhatta as the compiler,
and in the same (breath calls him) the disciple of Prabodhananda.

**89.** And that Prabodhananda — whose disciple is he?
Know for certain: (it says) "dear to Bhagavan".

**90.** By the word "Bhagavan" Sri Krishna Chaitanya is meant:
(Prabodhananda) is the vessel of His mercy and therefore blessed.

**91.** In the whole body of books composed by Sri Rupa and Sanatana,
wherever Mahaprabhu is referred to,

**92.** everywhere the word "Bhagavat" is written:
they knew Sri Krishna Chaitanya as Svayam Bhagavan.

**93.** Gopala Bhatta served (Him) with body, word and mind —
for that reason he became the vessel of Mahaprabhu's mercy.

**94.** Thus (it is said):
*"And his disciple, Sri Gopala Bhatta, should also be understood as such."*[^11]

**95.** From this (it follows): Prabodhananda is an associate of the Lord,
and so is Gopala Bhatta — know this for certain.

*[p. 10]*

**96.** Such is the exact meaning of the word "also" —
the essence of siddhanta issued from Sanatana's lips.

**97.** Otherwise — all the great souls had earlier gurus;
some we know, some we do not — who can count them?[^12]

**98.** Sri Sanatana composed a tippani on the Tenth (Canto);
in its mangalacharana there are such words —

**99.** he writes of Vidya-vachaspati as his guru.
See for yourself the words from his own lips.

**100.** Thus (the verse):
*"I bow to my gurus Bhattacharya Sarvabhauma and Vidya-vachaspati, and to Vidya-bhushana, the ornament of the land of Gauda; I bow to Sri Paramananda Bhattacharya, the lover of rasa, to Ramabhadra and to the instructor Vani-vilasa."*[^13]

**101.** So also what is written of Gopala Bhatta's guru:
consider it, all of you, and see, applying your minds.

**102.** All of them are the dearmost associates of Chaitanya,
by whose grace the treasure of prema is obtained.

**103.** Sanatana, Rupa, Gopala — the difference is only of three bodies;
whoever knows this truth is a vessel of mercy.

**104.** Thus it is said by the ancients as well:
*"I bow to Gopala Bhatta, whose heart is flooded with Sanatana's love, who is marked in everything by Sri Rupa's friendship, whose sole life is Radha-ramana, who grants the desired to those who worship."*[^14]

*[p. 11]*

**105.** Whoever sees even a sesame seed's difference among these three —
for that offense there is no deliverance.

**106.** I shall tell a second proof — listen attentively,
having drunk the nectar of words from his moonlike lips.

**107.** Sri Bhatta Gosani composed a commentary on the *Karnamrita*;
he wrote countless special explanations in it.

**108.** Seeing it, devotees and pandits are amazed:
in it is the perfection of rasa, the essence of siddhanta.

**109.** The mangalacharana of that commentary is two shlokas
which he wrote; seeing and hearing them, all people

**110.** forget themselves and remain astonished;
thrills of joy, tears flow down face and breast.

**111.** Thus the two shlokas:
*"I delight Him who is dear to Radha — a splendour charming in the pastimes of the Vrindavana groves; shining with the wonder of the lovely peacock feather that kisses His crest; whose lotus face sports sweet nectar, whose brows dance like bees; whose bimba-red lips gleam in the glow of the crimson flute. — Gopala Bhatta, a god (among brahmanas) of the Dravida land, composes the commentary 'Sri Krishna-vallabha' on (the book) …"*[^15]

**112.** Here it is written: his dwelling is the Dravida land.
I shall explain it, having heard the account from beginning to end.

**113.** Brahmanas have many divisions;
among them ten houses are the best of all.

*[p. 12]*

**114.** They are called the five Gaudas and the five Dravidas.
First I shall briefly describe the Gaudas:

**115.** Kanyakubja, Maithila, Gauda, Kamarupa,
Utkala — know these as the five kings among the twice-born.[^16]

**116.** I shall name the five Dravidas — listen carefully:
each (division) by the name of its region.

**117.** Maharashtra, Dravida, Tailanga, Karnata,
Gurjara — where the seat of royal brahmanas is seen.

**118.** Among the five Dravidas are the Tailangas;
for that reason he is called "a god of the Dravida land".[^17]

**119.** Such is its exact meaning — know it.
Having heard the ancient tradition, I have written the essence.

**120.** Finding occasion, I wrote this beforehand;
the account of the coming to Vrindavana remained (to be told).

**121.** At the end of the chaturmasya, when the Lord took leave —
the grief that arose, who can describe?[^18]

**122.** The Bhatta with his family follows and will not turn back;
in turning them back the Lord and (His) servants were at a loss.

**123.** With great effort giving them some composure,
He went to travel through the south, heeding nothing.

**124.** As He left, He spoke sweet words,
embracing (them) again and again, absorbed in prema.

**125.** To the three Bhatta brothers He said: "Here
remain, serve day and night, and do bhajana.

*[p. 13]*

**126.** When you can no longer stay and the longing grows,
then without doubt you will obtain My darshana."

**127.** To Gopala Bhatta He says, overwhelmed by prema:
"Serve these three, being steady.

**128.** When they have all attained perfection, go to Vrindavana:
there are My dear Rupa and Sanatana.

**129.** Soon I shall send (them) — there is no doubt;
with both of them you will have loving friendship.

**130.** Together with those two do bhajana:
the joy of service, darshana, the tasting of books of rasa.

**131.** From time to time there will be meetings with Me.
Be careful and carry out the order."

**132.** Saying this, embracing him, He infused shakti
and revealed in his heart all His own truth.

**133.** What transformations of bhava both had then —
whoever saw them knows; no one else knows.

**134.** In that absorption Mahaprabhu went on, intoxicated (with prema);
the Bhatta and his family became as if dead.

**135.** Travelling for some days through all the tirthas,
He went again to see the Moon of Nilachala.[^19]

**136.** The Bhatta fell senseless with his family,
and all the people of the village likewise.

**137.** After a while, regaining consciousness, they grew calm
and, to carry out the order, went each to his home.

*[p. 14]*

**138.** In separation from Chaitanya their hearts always burn;
day and night they sing His qualities, tears without end.

**139.** Some days they passed in this way;
hearts overwhelmed, a burning rises again and again.

**140.** Gradually the three brothers attained perfection:
one after another they departed (from this world).[^20]

**141.** Settling everything and becoming detached,
(Gopala Bhatta) came to Vrindavana, intoxicated with prema.

**142.** Arriving, he obtained the company of Rupa and Sanatana;
with the two Raghunathas — waves of prema.

**143.** For Sri Jiva — tenderness greater than millions of lives;
they always tasted the honey of the pastimes of Radha and Krishna.

**144.** And when they taste the pastimes of Chaitanya,
then all are in the great madness of prema.

**145.** Madana-gopala with Sri Radhika,
Sri Govinda-lal with Vrindavaneshvari,

**146.** Gopinatha with the daughter of Vrishabhanu —
by their darshana and service he deemed his life fulfilled.

**147.** His longing grew to perform a service entrusted to himself alone;
understanding (this), Gosani had a substance (for the Deity) brought from Gauda.

**148.** Taking some craftsman merely as a pretext,
pondering in his mind the yearning of (his) heart,

**149.** knowing the desire of Gopala Bhatta Gosani,
Sri Rupa Gosani manifested (the Deity) with his own hand.

*[p. 15]*

**150.** Holding a festival with the associates, they performed the abhisheka
and revealed the name — Sri Radha-ramana.[^21]

**151.** Having a temple built, he gave (the Deity) to him as his own service;
how extraordinary it is cannot be told.

**152.** Even today see: the service (there) shines in full splendour;
realizing this, know all that was before.

**153.** Those whose all-in-all is Sri Rupa with his family —
for their joy this pastime is proclaimed.

**154.** Wishing to describe this connection, the gurus and the rest,
Manohara Dasa tells the *Anuraga-valli*.

*Thus ends the first manjari of the* Srimad Anuraga-valli, *called "Tasting the Life of Sri Gopala Bhatta".*

[^1]: A verse of Raghunatha Dasa Gosvami (the closing verse of the *Mukta-charita*).
[^2]: The well-known mangalacharana verse "vande 'haṁ śrī-guroḥ śrī-yuta-pada-kamalam…" (cf. the opening of CC Adi 1.1).
[^3]: The beginning of the verse is poorly legible in the edition; the translation is approximate.
[^4]: CC Adi 10–12, the chapters on the branches of Mahaprabhu, Nityananda and Advaita.
[^5]: According to the *Anuraga-valli*, the Lord's host is Trimalla Bhatta, while Venkata and Prabodhananda are his brothers (which is younger and which elder is unclear), and Gopala Bhatta is Trimalla's son (60). In the CC (Madhya 9) the host is Venkata Bhatta; according to the *Karnananda* (5.95, 109) the host is Trimalla and Gopala is "Venkata's boy"; the *Bhakti-ratnakara* (1) makes Gopala Venkata's son. The author himself notes the discrepancy (72–73).
[^6]: CC Madhya 1.109 names Trimalla; Madhya 9.82 names Venkata.
[^7]: Prabodhananda Sarasvati, according to Gaudiya Vaishnava tradition the uncle and first teacher of Gopala Bhatta.
[^8]: The sense of the couplet is unclear; translated by sense.
[^9]: *Hari-bhakti-vilasa* 1.2.
[^10]: A translation of the Sanskrit commentary (the *Dig-darshini*, the commentary on the *Hari-bhakti-vilasa* attributed to Sanatana Gosvami), which the author quotes verbatim; the text is corrupt in places.
[^11]: A continuation of the same *Dig-darshini* commentary (cf. 80).
[^12]: The author's argument: the "also" in the commentary places Gopala Bhatta on a par with his guru Prabodhananda, "dear to Bhagavan", i.e. an associate of Mahaprabhu; the other "earlier gurus" of great souls carry no such weight.
[^13]: The mangalacharana of the *Brihad-vaishnava-toshani*, Sanatana Gosvami's commentary on the Tenth Canto of the *Bhagavata*.
[^14]: The source of the shloka has not been identified.
[^15]: The mangalacharana of Gopala Bhatta's commentary *Krishna-vallabha* on Bilvamangala's *Sri Krishna-karnamrita*. The words "kṛṣṇa-varṇa-tanoḥ" in the second shloka are unclear (apparently a reference to the author or the book); the end of the translation is approximate.
[^16]: The five Gaudas are usually listed as Sarasvata, Kanyakubja, Gauda, Maithila and Utkala; in place of Sarasvata the author names Kamarupa.
[^17]: The author's interpretation: Gopala Bhatta was a Tailanga, and the Tailangas belong to the five Dravidas. According to the CC (Madhya 9) the Bhatta family lived at Shrirangam on the Kaveri.
[^18]: Cf. CC Madhya 9; *Prema-vilasa* 18.54–79 (where the order to go to Vrindavana is given to the father and to Prabodhananda); *Karnananda* 5.
[^19]: The Moon of Nilachala — Jagannatha in Puri.
[^20]: Literally "they took the path — one earlier, another later"; the reading of the word in the scan is uncertain.
[^21]: According to Gaudiya tradition (and the lila database), Sri Radha-ramana Himself appeared from Gopala Bhatta's shalagrama (1542). In the author's account the Deity is manifested by the hand of Sri Rupa Gosvami at Gopala Bhatta's wish, the craftsman being only a "pretext"; here too the Deity is not a product of art but a manifestation of the Lord Himself.
