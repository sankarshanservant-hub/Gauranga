# Manjari 3. Meetings in Gauda and the Road to Vrindavana

*The bhava of Gadadhara Dasa; the darshana of Sita Thakurani and Jahnava; Srinivasa with Abhirama Gosani at Krishnanagara: the test of five gandas of cowries and the feeding of the Vaishnavas, three strokes of the whip "Jaya-mangala"; the road to Vrindavana*

*[p. 30]*

*Raga.*

**1.** Bow down to Sri Krishna Chaitanya with His associates —
the limit of compassion, besides whom there is no other.

**2.** Begging them Himself, He distributes the highest goal to the fallen;
the name "Purifier of the fallen" has now come true.

**3.** Thus some days passed in Navadvipa;
seeing and hearing, (Srinivasa) was amazed at heart.

**4.** The bhava of a single devotee is deep as millions of oceans —
what wise man can fully measure it?

**5.** Of Sri Gadadhara Dasa nothing can be understood:
outwardly nothing is seen, yet his heart always burns.

**6.** Sometimes he sits and says nothing,
sometimes he moves here and there and laughs aloud.

**7.** Speaking of Chaitanya, he brings up all sorts of stories;
at times he says and does things in great agitation.

**8.** Now he speaks to himself in the subtlest voice,
as if in question and answer understanding everything fully.

*[p. 31]*

**9.** Thrilled, full of tears, he laughs softly;
his body cannot be held for excess of joy.

**10.** Biting his tongue with his teeth, he rolled his eyes,
sprang up as if in anger with a loud cry.

**11.** He bites his lips, his brows quiver;
in anguish he speaks in faltering words.

**12.** A moment later, again — as if mad:
rolling his reddened eyes, he looks in all directions.

**13.** He sighs heavily, says nothing to anyone;
from inner pain his breast seems about to burst.

**14.** And the next moment neither tears nor anything else is seen —
who can know the transformations of this bhava?

**15.** One day someone, seeing his behaviour,
grew uneasy in his mind:

**16.** "In separation from Chaitanya everyone's heart has melted,
yet this Thakura — why does he go about like this?"

**17.** By chance one day he came close to him,
and Gadai's breath touched his body.

**18.** That spot was scorched; he sprang up with a cry,
a moment later came to himself and fell down weeping.

**19.** What had been in his mind
he told him all in private:

**20.** "I offended you, not knowing you;
as was the offense, so the punishment I received."

*[p. 32]*

**21.** Gosani says: "Go, do not fear;
may Gosani (the Lord) always be kind to all."

**22.** When he is alone,
separation grows overwhelmingly strong in his heart.

**23.** He falls senseless on the ground;
his whole body motionless, his breath does not move.

**24.** Having lain thus for some time,
regaining consciousness, he suddenly sits up.

**25.** How he laments then cannot be told;
at that time the whole mahabhava arises.

**26.** Witnessing all these states of absorption in bhava,
(Srinivasa), amazed, reflected in his mind.

**27.** What I heard from the lips of the mahantas,
this wondrous account, I have told very briefly.

**28.** Meanwhile Sri Sita Thakurani,
mother of the world, the wife of Srila Advaita,

**29.** Sri Jahnava, endowed with every shakti,
purifier of the fallen, the wife of Nityananda —

**30.** the darshana of the feet of both he obtained in turn
and deemed his life fulfilled.

**31.** Words would not come; tears, trembling, thrills;
he rises and falls again, not regaining awareness.

**32.** The feet whose darshana grants fearlessness everywhere —
such darshana did Acharya Mahashaya obtain.

*[p. 33]*

**33.** Thus he stayed there some days
and abundantly received the mercy of the feet of both.[^1]

**34.** Next — the meeting with Abhirama Gosani:
listen, all, attentively — it is most extraordinary.

**35.** He heard from people about Krishnanagara:
Sri Abhirama Gosani lives there.[^2]

**36.** In Navadvipa, at the gate of the (Lord's) house, he bowed down
and placed on his head the dust of the feet of all the devotees.

**37.** What transformations of bhava he had then —
who could describe them?

**38.** In rapture he set out to see (Abhirama)
and gradually arrived there.

**39.** He saw (Abhirama) seated with his associates,
his heart immersed in waves of talk about Krishna.

**40.** At that moment (Srinivasa) came and offered dandavat obeisances;
he asks: "Who are you, what is your name?"

**41.** Humbly he says: "My name is Srinivasa;
born in a brahmana family, I long for the Lord's darshana."

**42.** Saying this, he took the dust of his feet;
(Abhirama), placing a hand on his head, was delighted.

**43.** He said: "Stay here for some days;
whatever you need is all at your disposal."

**44.** Telling the storekeeper to arrange everything,
he spoke of other things and then held talk about Krishna.

*[p. 34]*

**45.** That day Thakura accepted sidha (uncooked provisions);
from the next day he lived, as usual, on his own.

**46.** Bathing in the river, walks in the gardens on the bank,
beholding the service (of the Deity), hearing talk of Krishna.

**47.** East of the house, while Rama-kunda was being dug,
Krishna appeared in person in the form of a Deity —

**48.** named Sri Gopinatha, supremely enchanting;
(Abhirama) serves Him in countless special ways.[^3]

**49.** There (Srinivasa) found the limit of happiness and stayed;
all the money he had ran out.

**50.** After that, the vessels he had with him
were also sold off little by little.

**51.** When at the end five gandas of cowries remained,
that day Gosani gave a certain order:

**52.** "Hey, my boy, today in a rich man's house
there will be a wedding — go there quickly.

**53.** Today's meal you will get first,
and on the dakshina you will live five more days."

**54.** Hearing this, Thakura kept silent;
Gosani began to say the same again.

**55.** Then Thakura said: "I have enough to live on."
"What do you have? Tell the truth," Gosani asks.

**56.** When he heard, "I have five gandas of cowries,"
astonished, he thought to himself:

*[p. 35]*

**57.** "Today I shall test him: let me see what the brahmana does."
To people he says: "See where he cooks."

**58.** Thakura bought rice for sixteen cowries,
took a clay pot for one cowrie,

**59.** firewood for two cowries, salt for one,
and with these went to the river Darukeshvara.

**60.** In the garden there are many banana leaves:
they are had for nothing, no one buys them.

**61.** When, having bathed there, he set the rice to cook,
a spy came and told Gosani everything.

**62.** Gosani said: "Go, four Vaishnavas,
to where Srinivasa is cooking.

**63.** Stay hidden, do not show yourselves at first;
only when he has offered the bhoga, go near."

**64.** Receiving Gosani's order, they went
and appeared just as he finished the offering.

**65.** Distinctly chanting the names "Hare Krishna",
they all came up before Thakura.

**66.** In the garb of renunciants — waist-string, water pot, kaupina,
patched quilt — the marks of great detachment were seen.

**67.** Seeing them, he was very glad
and humbly began to speak:

**68.** "If out of mercy you have come — it is my good fortune;
please accept some of this prasada rice."

*[p. 36]*

**69.** They say: "We shall do as you have said."
Thakura says: "Then you have bought me."

**70.** On one side he seated the four Vaishnavas
and prepared five banana-leaf tips.

**71.** Serving the (rice) there in equal portions,
he put a pinch of salt for each.

**72.** Having seated them, he sat down himself;
having eaten, they were very pleased.

**73.** Satisfied, they took leave and went
and, coming to Gosani, told him everything.

**74.** As soon as he heard it, he was overcome with prema
and began to speak in a faltering voice:

**75.** "In Chaitanya's time I saw such renunciation —
and it exists even today! At this I marvel.

**76.** I used to say: 'That Thief has carried everything off' —
and for that my heart always burns.

**77.** Now I know: here and there, a little,
He has left it behind — this has become evident."

**78.** As he said this, the former happiness came back to him:
surging up, with a loud cry he fell to the ground.

**79.** His breath does not move, not a limb stirs;
seeing this, all the Vaishnavas cry out in alarm.

**80.** For some time he lies senseless in bliss;
then Acharya Thakura comes up.

*[p. 37]*

**81.** He heard all that had happened, saw his state,
and streams (of tears) began to flow down his face and breast.

**82.** Hear further of the transformations of his prema:
after Mahaprabhu's disappearance he showed the signs of (prema's) madness.

**83.** Nowhere seeing that gift of prema,
seeing joylessness everywhere, he constantly suffers.

**84.** There is a horsewhip named "Jaya-mangala":
striking people with it, he makes them overwhelmed with prema.[^4]

**85.** When in the third prahara he regained consciousness,
the eight sattvika bhavas appeared in his body.

**86.** Thus after some time, half returned to outer awareness,
he sees Srinivasa standing before him.

**87.** Having the whip brought from a servant's hand,
he strikes Thakura as if with an angry face.

**88.** When he had struck him three times with that whip,
Malini, in alarm, seized his hand:[^5]

**89.** "You have flooded him! What more do you want to do?
Look just once at the state that has come upon him!"

**90.** One sees thrills, tears, trembling — he shakes all over;
pallor, a breaking voice, he cannot utter a sound.

**91.** Now sweat breaks out, now he stands stiff as a post,
now he is restless like a madman.

**92.** Whatever sanchari bhava comes into his mind,
so he acts at that moment — it cannot be told.

*[p. 38]*

**93.** Again Malini says: "Gosani, what have you done!
You have spoiled the brahmana youth's studies.

**94.** Show mercy, so that the signs of madness
do not hinder his study of the scriptures of truth."

**95.** Thakura humbly fell down in obeisance;
Gosani placed his foot on his head,

**96.** took him on his lap and, holding his chin,
spoke sweet words, absorbed in prema:

**97.** "Do not worry about anything — what the Creator has ordained (will be);
go to Vrindavana — there all perfection will come."

**98.** Saying this, embracing, they began to weep;
both were in turmoil at the coming separation.

**99.** So the dust of the feet of all the devotees
he took again and again and placed on his head.

**100.** That night he passed absorbed in bhava;
rising, he sees a little of the night remains.

**101.** Then he went out beyond the gate of the house
and, unable to compose himself, offered dandavat obeisances.

**102.** For a long time he wept there, rolling on the ground;
regaining awareness, he turned his face toward Vrindavana.

**103.** All day he walks as far as he can;
wherever evening falls, there he halts.

**104.** If he receives something unasked, he cooks
and eats; if he receives nothing, he fasts.

*[p. 39]*

**105.** Body and mind always overflowing with the divine madness of bhava,
he walks without fear; hunger and thirst do not hinder him.

**106.** When stupor or swoon comes upon him,
he lies fallen, and people think he is tired from the road.

**107.** After some days he came to Sri Mathura;
as soon as he saw its beauty, he was absorbed in bhava.

**108.** Collecting himself, he came to the Vishranti tirtha;
bathing and drinking water, he was rid of his bodily fatigue.

**109.** There the people of Mathura were talking among themselves —
and there he heard of Sri Rupa's disappearance.

**110.** Hastily approaching one of them, he asked for news,
and he told of the departure of the three Gosanis:

**111.** "Sanatana withdrew (from the manifest world) many days ago;
after him, by his own will, Raghunatha Bhatta.

**112.** Recently, some days ago, Rupa disappeared.
So I have told you about these three."

**113.** As soon as he heard this, he turned pale;
he wants to lament, but no sound leaves his throat.

**114.** His body thrills, streams of tears flow from his eyes;
sweat gleams on his face like scattered pearls.

**115.** A trembling runs over his body, seizing it entirely;
a moment later, helpless, his throat rattles.

**116.** He fell senseless to the ground, unconscious;
his body became motionless and remained so for some time.

*[p. 40]*

**117.** Regaining consciousness, he rolls on the ground again,
like a golden image tossing in the dust.

**118.** With cries he laments in such a way
that, hearing it, no one could keep his composure.

**119.** All around, weeping, people ask what has happened;
who will answer? — there is no consolation at all.

**120.** As soon as he heard the news of the Gosanis,
he was distraught — the wise know this.

**121.** At that time no one travelled to and from
Vrindavana: the road was very dangerous.

**122.** For fear of robbers and beasts no one can pass along the road;
if anyone carries money tied on him, he is killed at once.

**123.** Only one with such longing can come;
the Lord's will is beyond the reach of reasoning.

**124.** So no news (from Vrindavana) can be had;
one learns everything only on reaching Mathura.

**125.** Such was formerly the road to Vrindavana;
some time later travelling to and fro became safe.

**126.** In a moment a bhava arose with the signs of madness;
in the midst of it he made this resolve:

**127.** "I came to Vrindavana with a firm intention:
as soon as I arrived, I would take shelter at Rupa's feet,

**128.** study the *Sri Bhagavata* with Raghunatha,
and serve Sanatana with body, mind and word.

*[p. 41]*

**129.** If that has not come about — why go on?
Death does not come, nor does a thunderbolt fall on my head!"

**130.** As he thought thus, a tempest of anxiety arose;
his judgement was lost, he grew utterly restless.

**131.** He turned back, heeding neither what lay ahead nor behind,
and walked as far as he could until evening.

**132.** Hungry, thirsty, stricken with grief, exhausted,
his body gave way; he fell and lay there.

**133.** Distraught with thoughts, at night not a wink of sleep;
only at the end of the night a light doze came.

**134.** In that place he received the darshana of Sri Rupa;
gazing at him, he knew that the name "Rupa" (beauty) was true.[^6]

**135.** Not very tall, full-bodied, a shapely form,
a golden complexion outshining the flash of lightning.

**136.** Shaven, in the garb of renunciation, only the shikha flutters in the wind;
a high nose, on his lips the glow of dawn.

**137.** Beautifully red palms and soles shine,
and on them the light of the moons of the nails.

**138.** A body fashioned as if from love, most tender;
his speech — what streams of nectar!

**139.** On his forehead a tilaka shaped like the temple of Hari;
the ornament of his neck a necklace of tulasi.

**140.** Seeing him thus, he fell down in dandavat obeisance;
unable to contain his bliss, tears flow down his breast.

*[p. 42]*

**141.** When he had bowed down once, twice, three, four times,
then (Rupa) placed his foot on his head,

**142.** raised him, took him on his lap, and with sweetest words
began to speak — hearing them, life is soothed:

**143.** "By my order, go back to Vrindavana;
study the books of bhakti with Jiva.

**144.** By my mercy their meaning will be fully revealed to you;
in a few days, having studied the scriptures, you will become a teacher.

**145.** You wished to be initiated by me for worship;
but that (I) — and Gopala Bhatta: there is no difference.

**146.** Go to him and be initiated by him;
every perfection will come — accept this word of mine."

**147.** Saying this, casting a merciful glance with tears,
he vanished; and here (Srinivasa) sprang up with a cry.

**148.** "Ha Rupa! Ha Rupa!" — he rolls on the ground;
hearing that lament, life leaves the body.

**149.** At the sound of weeping people ran up and surrounded him
and began with all care to ask:

**150.** "Who are you, and why are you so distraught?
Hearing of your grief, the heart breaks."

**151.** Thakura, ceaselessly intoxicated with the absorption of bhava,
hears nothing — whoever speaks and however much.

**152.** Seeing his anguish, the people were distressed;
tears flow down everyone's breast.

*[p. 43]*

**153.** As he lamented thus for some time,
his body grew limp — suddenly he swooned.

**154.** He lay there, not a limb stirring;
seeing this, the people cry out in grief.

**155.** For a moment he remained thus, stunned;
then he sat up again, regaining consciousness.

**156.** He reflected: "The order that Gosani has given —
that is my goal; in it I see my welfare."

**157.** Rising, he set off on the road to Vrindavana;
he sees nothing — his eyes are full of tears.

**158.** When Sri Acharya Thakura set out
for Sri Vrindavana, his heart overflowing with the rapture of prema —

**159.** here, in that same part of the night, to Jiva Gosani
Sri Rupa speaks lovingly in a dream:

**160.** "On the Vaishakha full moon, at the time of the evening arati,
a brahmana who comes from the land of Gauda

**161.** will behold Sri Govinda from behind everyone,
absorbed in prema — so it is ordained.

**162.** His body is golden, his name is Srinivasa;
by my order, place your trust in him."

**163.** In separation Gopala Bhatta Gosani day and night,
waking and sleeping, converses in visions with Sri Rupa.

**164.** That night (Rupa) told him: "The brahmana youth
who will come today — accept him."

*[p. 44]*

**165.** Thus before evening he arrived in Vrindavana;
seeing the temple enclosure, he asked what was happening there.[^7]

**166.** People say: "It is the time of Govinda's arati;
go quickly for darshana, if you wish."

**167.** As soon as he heard it, he ran in haste;
but in the great crowd he could not get in.

**168.** Standing behind, he gazed at the (Lord's) lotus face,
but his eyes filled with tears — he sees nothing.

**169.** When the arati was over, the crowd still being great,
Thakura went and sat to one side.

**170.** Tears, trembling, thrills are seen on his body;
the joy of beholding the face cannot be contained in it.

**171.** Meanwhile Sri Jiva Gosani looked everywhere,
but in the great crowd could not see him anywhere.

**172.** Greatly surprised, he reflects in his mind:
"Gosani told me this for certain.

**173.** Can his word ever turn out otherwise?
The crowd has gone — what can be done now?"

**174.** Reflecting thus, taking five or seven people,
he himself goes about searching everywhere.

**175.** He sees that near the door, inside, there is a place:
"It seems to me someone is sitting there."

*[p. 45]*

**176.** Going there himself,
seeing the absorption of bhava, he was delighted.

**177.** He saw that it all agreed with Sri Gosani-jiu's order
and, to remove all doubt, began to question him.

**178.** Thakura, looking, knew him to be Sri Jiva Gosani,
hastily wiped his tears and fell (at his feet) right there.

**179.** Whoever hears of his humility at that time —
let alone men, even stone would melt.

**180.** Raising him respectfully, Gosani embraced him
and, in tears, speaks in faltering words:

**181.** "Sri Gosani told me about you;
it is good that I have seen you so soon.

**182.** By my good fortune my Prabhu has been kind
and joined me with such a friend as you.

**183.** We shall live together, no one will go anywhere,
we shall ceaselessly relish talk of Krishna."

**184.** Thakura told everything about the dream;
hearing it, he embraced him again and again in bliss.

**185.** Taking him by the hand, he brought him to Govinda's cook
and through the cook had him partake of prasada,

**186.** brought him to his own lodging and gave him a place to stay,
where he was provided for in every way.

**187.** Thus passing that night there,
early in the morning, having bathed in the Yamuna and so on,

**188.** Gosani himself, taking Thakura with him,
came to Sri Radha-ramana, rejoicing.

*[p. 46]*

**189.** They saw Gopala Bhatta seated;
step by step they went up to that place.

**190.** Greeting him fittingly, they sat down on seats
and told all the news from beginning to end.

**191.** As soon as he heard it, Bhatta Gosani was overwhelmed;
he says: "Yesterday I received such a command (in a dream)."

**192.** In separation from Sri Rupa the Bhatta grieved boundlessly:
"What shall I do with a disciple? The body has become a burden to me."

**193.** And yet, hearing of the dreams of both
and thinking of his own dream, he showed him great honour:

**194.** "His order is my duty;
whatever he says at any time I shall do gladly.

**195.** I know: Sri Gosani, being gracious to me,
has joined me with a priceless jewel."

**196.** Hearing these words, Sri Acharya Thakura
fell down in dandavat obeisance and remained, bathed in tears.

**197.** Then Sri Jiva Gosani speaks:
"Tomorrow, on the second lunar day — I think it good."

**198.** "So be it: whatever has come from your lips
has no flaw in it — it is excellent in every way."

**199.** Saying this, Bhatta Gosani, with anguish on his face,
compassionately asks for news of the land of Gauda.

**200.** Hearing of the state of Mahaprabhu's family,
all three wept long, sobbing aloud.

*[p. 47]*

**201.** Who can describe the weeping at that time?
Not to speak of people — trees and stones were rent.

**202.** Thus for some time they talked of the Beloved (Lord);
then (Srinivasa) asked leave to go to his lodging.

**203.** Gosani had dry prasada brought and gave it to him,
and, giving him darshana of Thakura (the Deity), sent him off.

**204.** Both bowed down, Bhatta Gosani embraced (them);
thus that day he went to his lodging.

**205.** Early in the morning, having bathed and so on in the same way,
he came quickly together with Sri Jiva Gosani.

**206.** Bhatta Gosani was engaged in the service of Thakura (the Deity);
offering obeisances and praises, both sat down on seats.

**207.** The articles of worship that Sri Jiva Gosvami had prepared
he placed in Acharya Thakura's hands and led him (there).

**208.** Accepting them, Bhatta Gosani performed the service;
compassion filled his body beyond restraint.

**209.** First he bestowed the mercy of the name of Sri Hari,
then the two incomparable mantras of Radha and Krishna.

**210.** Having made him hear the five names, he gave him a siddha name:
"Sri Mani-manjari" — (Srinivasa) heard from the guru's lips.[^8]

**211.** His own name he gives as Sri Guna-manjari,
holding to what Sri Rupa wrote in the *Ganoddesha*.[^9]

*[p. 48]*

**212.** Thus (the shloka):
*"Lavanga-manjari, Rupa-manjari, Guna-manjari, Bhanumati — another name of the first — the very dear Rati-manjari, Ragalekha, Kalakeli, Manjula and others are maidservants."*

**213.** Friends devoted to service, foremost in attendance —
therefore the account calls them maidservants.

**214.** This parakiya-lila in Vraja, in Vrindavana —
Sri Rupa showed the way to it in the *Smarana-mangala*.[^10]

**215.** All follow within the group of Sri Rupa-manjari;
as is one's meditation, so is one's attainment.

**216.** Sri Radha-ramana is the son of the king of Vraja;
Radha has no contact with Vasudeva and the others.

**217.** Therefore Sri Rupa Gosani's desire
he expressed so that we may know the path of worship.

**218.** Thus (the verse) of Srimad Rupa:
*"Your parents are the lord and lady of the cowherds; O Holder of the mountain, Sri Radhika is Your beloved; Sridama, Subala and others are Your friends; Nilambara is Your elder brother; the flute is Your instrument, the peacock feather Your ornament, Nandishvara Your home, the forest of Vrindavana Your pleasure garden; beyond this, O Lord, I know no other form of Yours."*[^11]

**219.** That Radha-ramana is the son of Shachi;
always meditate on Them as non-different.

**220.** A verse of the *Bhagavata*, as a definition,
Sri Rupa expounded in the *Sri Krishna-Chaitanya-ashtaka*.

*[p. 49]*

**221.** Thus in the *Sri Bhagavata*:
*"Thus, O king, in Dvapara they praise the Lord of the universe; hear now how in Kali (He is worshipped) according to the injunctions of various tantras: in Kali intelligent people worship, with sacrifices consisting chiefly of sankirtana, Him who chants (the name of) Krishna, whose complexion is not dark, together with His parts, intimates, weapons and associates."*[^12]

**222.** Shlokas from Sri Rupa's hymn:
*"He whom the wise in Kali openly worship with sacrifices consisting of loud kirtana — Krishna, whose body is not dark owing to the abundance of His radiance; whom they call the object of worship of all who have entered the fourth ashrama — may that God in the form of Chaitanya be exceedingly merciful to us! — He who, eager to steal and taste the sweet, unfathomable fullness of rasa of one among His loving ones, covered His own colour, manifesting here her radiance — may that God in the form of Chaitanya be exceedingly merciful to us!"*[^13]

**223.** Spoken by Srimad Dasa Gosvami:
*"Perform neither the dharma nor the adharma described in the shrutis; here in Vraja render abundant service to Radha and Krishna; O mind, always remember the son of Shachi as the son of the lord of Nandishvara, and the best guru as dear to Mukunda."*[^14]

**224.** The settled sense and intent of these three shlokas:
Sri Shachinandana is the son of the king of Vraja.

**225.** Accepting the bhava and radiance of Sri Radhika,
Gaurahari descended in Sri Navadvipa.

*[p. 50]*

**226.** The son of the king of Vraja tried hard in Vraja,
(but) three desires of His heart were not fulfilled:

**227.** "What is the nature of Radha's prema for Me —
this I cannot grasp, however I try.

**228.** How is My sweetness tasted,
and what is Radhika's bliss?

**229.** The ocean of bliss Sri Radhika has from My touch —
not one drop of it can I taste.

**230.** Therefore, without accepting Radha's bhava,
these three tastes cannot be achieved."

**231.** The source of avataras descended — that is the root purpose;
the incidental one is the establishment of the yuga-dharma.

**232.** When the source of avataras becomes manifest,
the separate yuga-avatara does not remain apart:

**233.** the avatara enters into the source of avataras.
The condensed essence of the meaning I have now set forth.[^15]

**234.** Then Gosvami-jiu had an apprehension —
the interpretation of those averse (to the Lord) came to his mind:

**235.** if they say: Mahaprabhu took sannyasa,
and by accepting the danda He is a manifestation of Narayana,

**236.** and that it is with this in view that (Sri Rupa) speaks of such glory,
and think, "This is the intent, I have reached the limit" —

**237.** no, it is not so: the assembly of sannyasis of the fourth ashrama —
their object of worship is He, the son of the king of Vraja.[^16]

*[p. 51]*

**238.** He imparted to (Srinivasa) the most secret essence,
knowing him in every way a vessel of Sri Rupa's mercy.

**239.** "So much about worship have I told you;
gradually knowledge of its details will come.

**240.** In the *Hari-bhakti-vilasa* and the *Rasamrita-sindhu*
the order of service and sadhana is clearly set out.

**241.** But — according to the practitioner's qualification;
you will see the whole arrangement for yourself."

**242.** Thakura, sitting in solitude, recited the mantras in order within
and everywhere, as was fitting, offered dandavat obeisances.

**243.** Then, having performed the midday arati,
distributing chatuhsama, tulasi buds and so on,

**244.** they brought wonderful prasada cooked in ghee
and served it in various kinds.

**245.** Until Bhatta Gosani sits, neither of the two will sit;
knowing this, he sat down, and someone else served.

**246.** All who there bore the name of Vaishnava
were brought and seated first.

**247.** Relishing all kinds of talk about Krishna,
in bliss they partook of maha-prasada.

**248.** After rinsing hands and mouth, camphor betel was given,
sandal paste on the whole body and a prasada garland on the neck.

**249.** Satisfied, all went to their own lodgings.
Thus (Srinivasa) settled in Vrindavana.

*[p. 52]*

**250.** Those whose all-in-all is Sri Rupa with his family —
for their joy this pastime is proclaimed.

**251.** Wishing to describe this connection, the gurus and the rest,
Manohara Dasa tells the *Anuraga-valli*.

*Thus ends, in the* Srimad Anuraga-valli, *in the life of Srimad Acharya Thakura, the third manjari, called "The Mercy of Sri Gopala Bhatta".*
