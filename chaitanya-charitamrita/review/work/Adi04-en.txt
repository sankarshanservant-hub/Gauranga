tr: An Account of the Root Purpose of Caitanya’s Descent

## 1

w: having seen  of the Enjoyer in Vraja
tr: By the grace of Śrī Caitanya even a child, looking into the scriptures, can ascertain the true form of Him who sports in Vraja.
fn: In the edition the verse is headed “By the grace of Gaura, the nature of Śrī Kṛṣṇa ascertained”. *Anubhāṣya*: “a child” — one who knows nothing; “His form” — the form of Gaura, who is not different from Rādhā and Kṛṣṇa; “the Enjoyer in Vraja” — the son of the king of Vraja; “ascertainment” — pointing out the truth (*tattva*).

## 3
w: of the fourth  verse  of the meaning  I have made  the explanation
w: of the fifth  verse  the meaning  hear  giving  the mind
tr: I have explained the meaning of the fourth verse. Now hear with attention the meaning of the fifth.

## 4
w: of the root  verse  the meaning  in order to make  manifest
w: the meaning  in order to apply  first  I speak  an outline
tr: To bring out the meaning of the root verse, I shall first give an outline before applying it.

## 5
w: of the fourth  verse  of the meaning  this  I have made  the essence
w: love and the name  to spread  this  descent
tr: The essence of the fourth verse, as I have explained it, is this: the Lord descended to spread divine love and the holy name.

## 6
w: true  this  reason  but  this indeed  external
w: another  one  reason  hear  there is  internal
tr: This reason is true, but it is external. There is another reason, an inner one — hear it.
fn: As in the Gauḍīya Maṭha edition: «এতো» (another reading: «এহো», “this too”); the sense is the same. *Amṛta-pravāha-bhāṣya*: Gaurāṅga descended to preach bhakti and the name of Kṛṣṇa; that reason is true, but it is external, not the hidden one.

## 7
w: formerly  so  of the earth  the burden  to remove
w: Kṛṣṇa  descended  became  in the scriptures  they proclaim
tr: The scriptures proclaim that in the past Kṛṣṇa descended to remove the burden of the earth.
fn: As in the Gauḍīya Maṭha edition: «হেন» (“so”); another reading has «যেন» (“as”); the sense is the same.

## 8
w: of Bhagavān Himself  the work  is not  the removal of the burden
w: the maintainer  Viṣṇu  does  of the world  the protection
tr: But removing the burden is not the work of Bhagavān Himself. It is Viṣṇu, the maintainer, who protects the world.
fn: In the edition the verse is headed: “Viṣṇu’s work is the deliverance of the saints and the destruction of the wicked; it is not the work of Kṛṣṇa Himself.”

## 9
w: but  of Kṛṣṇa  which  is  of descent  the time
w: of the removal of the burden  the time  with it  became  mixed
tr: But the time of Kṛṣṇa’s descent coincided with the time for removing that burden.

## 10
w: the complete  Bhagavān  descends  at which  time
w: other  all  avatāras  in Him  coming  meet
tr: When the complete Bhagavān descends, all the other avatāras come and meet in Him.

## 11–12
w: Nārāyaṇa,  the Catur-vyūha,  Matsya and the other  avatāras
w: of the yugas  of the manvantaras  the avatāras  as many as  there are  others
w: all  coming  in Kṛṣṇa’s  body  become  descended
w: thus  descends  Kṛṣṇa  Bhagavān  complete
tr: Nārāyaṇa, the Catur-vyūha, Matsya and the other avatāras, the avatāras of the yugas and the manvantaras, and as many others as there are — all come and descend within the body of Kṛṣṇa. So does Kṛṣṇa, the complete Bhagavān, descend.
fn: *Amṛta-pravāha-bhāṣya*: the Catur-vyūha are Vāsudeva, Saṅkarṣaṇa, Pradyumna and Aniruddha; Matsya and the others are partial avatāras (*aṁśāvatāra*). All the avatāras are parts of the complete Bhagavān and take shelter in Him, and so Viṣṇu, the maintainer of the world, was then present within the form of Kṛṣṇa.

## 13
w: therefore  Viṣṇu  then  of Kṛṣṇa  in the body
w: through Viṣṇu  Kṛṣṇa  makes  of the demons  the slaying
tr: Therefore Viṣṇu was then within the body of Kṛṣṇa, and through Viṣṇu Kṛṣṇa slew the demons.

## 14
w: secondary  work  this  of the demons  the killing
w: for which  the descent  I tell  that  root  cause
tr: The killing of the demons is a secondary work. Now I shall tell the root cause for which He descends.

## 15–16
w: of the rasa of love  the essence  in order to make  tasting
w: of the path of rāga  devotion  in the world  in order to make  spreading
w: of the relishers of rasa  the crown  Kṛṣṇa  supremely merciful
w: these  two  reasons  from  of the desire  the rising
tr: To taste the very essence of the rasa of love, and to spread in the world devotion on the path of rāga (*rāga-mārga*) — from these two reasons the desire arose in Kṛṣṇa, the crown of all who relish rasa, who is supremely merciful.
fn: In the edition the verse is headed: “Viṣṇu descends to preach *vidhi-bhakti*; Kṛṣṇa comes as Gaura to preach *rāga-bhakti*.”

## 17
w: of majesty  with the awareness  the whole  world  is mixed
w: by majesty  slackened  in love  is not  My  pleasure
tr: The whole world is permeated by the awareness of My majesty, and love slackened by awe gives Me no joy.
fn: The same verse as Ādi 3.16 (the *Anubhāṣya* refers to it). *Amṛta-pravāha-bhāṣya*: in love born of the awareness of majesty prema slackens, and Kṛṣṇa takes no pleasure in it; one who thinks himself lowly and Him the Lord loves Him with that awareness, and to such love He is not subject.

## 18
w: Me  as the Lord  considers  himself  as low
w: his  by love  subdued  I  not  become  dependent
tr: One who regards Me as the Lord and himself as lowly — by his love I am not subdued; I do not become dependent on him.

## 19
w: to Me  indeed  whichever  whichever  devotee  worships  in which  mood
w: him  in that  in that  mood  I worship  this  My  nature
tr: In whatever mood a devotee worships Me, in that very mood I reciprocate with him. Such is My nature.

## 20
w: who  as  to Me  surrender
w: them  in that same way  I reward  I
w: My  path  follow
w: men  O Pārtha  in every way
tr: “As people surrender to Me, so do I reward them. O Pārtha, all men follow My path in every way.” (Bhagavad-gītā, 4.11)
fn: *Anubhāṣya*: the Lord speaks this verse about the cause of His manifest pastimes; “I reward” — “I show favour”; “My path” — the path of perfection. *Amṛta-pravāha-bhāṣya*: “O Pārtha, in whatever way one worships Me, in that way I become attainable to him; all men follow the path shown by Me.”

## 21–22
w: my  son  my  friend  my  of life  lord
w: in this mood  whoever  to Me  makes  pure devotion
w: himself  great  considers  Me  equal or lower
w: in that  mood  become  I  his  dependent
tr: Whoever renders Me pure devotion in the mood “He is my son, my friend, the lord of my life”, considering himself great and Me equal or lower — in that mood I become subject to him.
fn: *Amṛta-pravāha-bhāṣya*: pure devotion (*śuddha-bhakti*) is the cultivation of Kṛṣṇa with a favourable intent, free of other desires and uncovered by knowledge and fruitive work. *Anubhāṣya*: devotion mixed with other desires, knowledge, work or yoga is “mixed devotion” (*viddha-bhakti*); devotion by rule (*vaidhī*) assists pure devotion, but the word *śuddha-bhakti* points to the service of the *rāgānugā* path — the devotion of Goloka, full of rāga, while *vaidhī-bhakti*, full of reverence, belongs to Vaikuṇṭha.

## 23
w: to Me  devotion  indeed  of beings
w: for immortality  is fit
w: by good fortune  which  arose  for Me  the love
w: of you  bringing Me
tr: “Devotion to Me brings living beings to immortality. And how fortunate it is that you have love for Me — for it brings you to Me!” (Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam, 10.82.44)
fn: Kṛṣṇa’s words to the gopīs at Kurukṣetra (Samanta-pañcaka), where, at a solar eclipse, both the Yādavas from Dvārakā and Nanda Mahārāja with all Vraja had come. *Anubhāṣya*: “immortality” — eternal service as the Lord’s associate; their love “brings Me” — grants direct meeting with Him; “by good fortune” — “by My good fortune”.

## 24
w: mother  Me  in the mood of a son  makes  binding
w: utterly helpless  thinking  does  fondling  caring
tr: My mother binds Me in the mood of a son; thinking Me utterly helpless, she fondles and cares for Me.

## 25
w: a friend  in pure friendship  makes  on the shoulders  climbing
w: You  what  great  person  You  I  equal
tr: My friend, in pure friendship, climbs on My shoulders and says, “What great man are You? You and I are equal!”

## 26
w: the beloved  if  pique  showing  makes  rebuke
w: of the Vedas  the hymns  than  steals  that  My  mind
tr: When My beloved, in her pique (*māna*), rebukes Me, that steals My heart more than the hymns of the Vedas.
fn: *Anubhāṣya*: the rebuke with which the lover (*āśraya*), in pure attachment, chides the Beloved (*viṣaya*) is a sign of utmost, exclusive love; where the Beloved is honoured as worshipful, natural love slackens, and so such rebuke stands higher than the awe-filled praise of the Vedas.

## 27–28
w: this  pure devotion  taking  I shall make  descent
w: I shall make  of many kinds  wondrous  pastimes
w: in Vaikuṇṭha and elsewhere  there is not  which  which  of pastimes  the display
w: those  those  pastimes  I shall perform  in which  My  wonder
tr: Taking this pure devotion, I shall descend and perform many kinds of wondrous pastimes. I shall perform pastimes such as are not shown even in Vaikuṇṭha — pastimes that astonish even Me.
fn: *Amṛta-pravāha-bhāṣya*: the pastimes not found in Vaikuṇṭha and the other worlds Kṛṣṇa will show in this descent, and in them He Himself will be astonished. *Anubhāṣya*: in Vaikuṇṭha and the other realms beyond māyā the Lord’s pastimes do not give Him Himself the ever-new joy of wonder; the excellence of the pastimes of Goloka, where He lives for His own joy, He wishes to show in the world to devotees in whom the sense of majesty prevails.

## 29
w: toward Me  of the gopīs  in the mood of a paramour (*upapati*)
w: Yogamāyā  will make  by her own  power
tr: By her own power Yogamāyā will awaken in the gopīs a love for Me as a lover who is not their husband (*upapati*).
fn: *Amṛta-pravāha-bhāṣya*: Yogamāyā is Kṛṣṇa’s own potency (*svarūpa-śakti*); by His will and by her inconceivable power she awakens in the hearts of His eternal beloveds, the gopīs, the mood of *upapati* toward Him. *Anubhāṣya*: the tendency to *parakīyā* service, in which the lover, drawn by a sweetness (*mādhurya*) that surpasses majesty, passes beyond all ordained reverence, comes from Yogamāyā; this working of the spiritual (*cinmayī*) māyā is unknown even to the Beloved Himself, and is possible only through Yogamāyā, the embodiment of His grace.

## 30
w: I too  not  know  that  not  know  the gopīs
w: of the two  by beauty and qualities  of the two  always  steals  the mind
tr: Neither I nor the gopīs will know of it, for each of us will ever steal the other’s heart with beauty and virtues.
fn: *Amṛta-pravāha-bhāṣya*: for the nourishment of rasa My inconceivable potency will cover My omniscience and give rise to a wondrous rasa, and the gopīs, though they are the Lord’s own potency, will not know it either.

## 31
w: dharma  leaving  in rāga  the two  make  meeting
w: sometimes  meet  sometimes  not  meet  of destiny  the arrangement
tr: Leaving dharma behind, the two will meet in love (*rāga*) — sometimes meeting, sometimes not, as destiny arranges.

## 32
w: these  all  of rasa  essences  I shall make  tasting
w: by this  means  I shall make  to all  devotees  grace
tr: I shall taste the essence of all these rasas, and by this means I shall show grace to all My devotees.

## 33
w: of Vraja  the pure  love  hearing  the devotees
w: on the path of rāga  worship  so that  leaving  religious duties
tr: Hearing of the pure love (*rāga*) of Vraja, the devotees will worship Me on the path of rāga, leaving aside their religious duties (*dharma-karma*).
fn: *Amṛta-pravāha-bhāṣya*: the pure rāga that Kṛṣṇa will show in Vraja is the means by which He gives rasa to all devotees: hearing of it, they will leave *dharma-karma* and worship Him on the path of rāga.

## 34
w: for the favour  of the devotees
w: a human  body  having taken
w: performs  such  pastimes
w: which  having heard  devoted to Him  one must become
tr: “To show favour to His devotees, Kṛṣṇa takes a body like a human one and performs such pastimes that whoever hears of them must become wholly devoted to Him.” (Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam, 10.33.36)
fn: In the edition the verse is headed: “Only the liberated soul, perfected on the path of *rāgānugā*, is fit to hear the transcendental *rāsa-līlā*.” *Anubhāṣya*: this is Śukadeva’s answer to Parīkṣit’s question about the truth and the need of Kṛṣṇa’s *parakīyā* pastimes; “a human body” — the supreme, transcendental body that resembles a human one; “having heard” — even an outsider, gaining faith in the Lord, must become devoted to Kṛṣṇa’s service. One who, not understanding the transcendental nature of these pastimes, takes “such pastimes” for worldly enjoyment only distorts their meaning (as the *prākṛta-sahajiyās* do); the principal sense of the word “such” is transcendental love (*rati*).

## 35
w: ‘bhavet’  the verb  injunctive (*vidhi-liṅ*)  that  here  says
w: a duty  certainly  this  otherwise  a fault
tr: The verb *bhavet* is in the injunctive mood (*vidhi-liṅ*), and so it says here: this is certainly a duty, and to neglect it is a fault.
fn: *Anubhāṣya*: seeing an injunction in the word *bhavet*, let no one think that the path of *rāgānugā*, open only to those who have gained a taste (*ruci*), is a path of rules open to anyone, even to one full of *anarthas* and seeking enjoyment. Injunctions and prohibitions belong to this world; in Goloka-Vṛndāvana there are none — there all who have taken shelter, moved by longing, seek only one thing: Kṛṣṇa’s pleasure.

## 36–37
w: this  desire  as  of Kṛṣṇa’s  appearance  the cause
w: of the demons  the slaying  a secondary  purpose
w: in the same way  Caitanya-Kṛṣṇa  the complete  Bhagavān
w: of the yuga-dharma  the inauguration  is not  His  desire
tr: As this desire was the cause of Kṛṣṇa’s appearance and the slaying of the demons only a secondary purpose, so too Caitanya-Kṛṣṇa is the complete Bhagavān, and to inaugurate the religion of the age (*yuga-dharma*) is not His own desire.
fn: In the edition verse 37 is headed: “Establishing dharma and the like is not the main work of Kṛṣṇa in His own form, nor of Gaura.”

## 38
w: for some  reason  when  arose  to descend  the mind
w: of the yuga-dharma  the time  became  with that  time  coinciding
tr: When for some reason He resolved to descend, the time of the *yuga-dharma* coincided with it.
fn: In the edition the verse is headed: “The meeting of the Source of all avatāras with His own part, the avatāra of the age.” *Amṛta-pravāha-bhāṣya*: when the complete Bhagavān resolved to descend for a hidden reason, the time of the *yuga-dharma* happened to arrive; so Gaurāṅga descended for two reasons — the inner, hidden one and the outer one, the preaching of the *yuga-dharma* — and with His devotees tasted prema and *nāma-saṅkīrtana*.

## 39
w: for two  reasons  descending  taking  the devotees
w: Himself  tastes  love  of the name  the congregational chanting
tr: Descending for these two reasons, together with His devotees He Himself tastes prema and the congregational chanting of the name.

## 40
w: by that  means  even to the outcastes  kīrtana  He spreads
w: of the name  of love  a garland  weaving  He put on  the world
tr: By this means He spread kīrtana even among the outcastes; weaving a garland of the holy name and divine love, He placed it around the whole world.

## 41
w: in this way  of a devotee  the mood  making  acceptance
w: Himself  practising  devotion  made  preaching
tr: Thus, accepting the mood of a devotee, He preached devotion by practising it Himself.
fn: *Anubhāṣya*: wishing to show His pastimes of magnanimity, Kṛṣṇa manifested in the world His eternal pastimes as Gaura, and the wonder of these eternal pastimes is that Kṛṣṇa takes the mood of a devotee. The *prākṛta-sahajiyās*, not understanding transcendental separation (*vipralambha*), distort this mood of a devotee: they want to dress Śrī Gaurasundara as an enjoyer (*sambhoga-vigraha*) and call themselves “Gaura-nāgarīs”, thereby destroying the mood of the *āśraya*; Kṛṣṇa is not pleased with them, and Śrī Gaura rejects them.

## 42
w: servitude,  friendship,  parental love,  and  which  the love of the beloved
w: four  loves  of four kinds  devotees  the vessel
tr: Servitude, friendship, parental love and the love of the beloved (*śṛṅgāra*) — these are the four kinds of prema, and the four kinds of devotees are their vessels.
fn: In the edition the verse is headed: “Apart from *śānta*, those who hold these four rasas desire only Kṛṣṇa’s pleasure.” *Śṛṅgāra* is the love of the beloved ones of Vraja, which in the gopīs is *parakīyā* (4.46–47), not a conjugal relationship (see Ādi 1.4 and 3.11).

## 43
w: own  own  mood  all  best  making  consider
w: in their own mood  make  with Kṛṣṇa  of happiness  the tasting
tr: Each considers his own mood the best, and in that mood he tastes happiness with Kṛṣṇa.

## 44
w: impartial  becoming  in the heart  consideration  if  I make
w: all  rasas  than  in śṛṅgāra  greater  sweetness
tr: But if one considers it in one’s heart without partiality, the sweetness of *śṛṅgāra*, the love of the beloved, surpasses that of all the other rasas.
fn: *Amṛta-pravāha-bhāṣya*: to each of the four kinds of devotees his own rasa seems best, but seen impartially the sweetness of *madhura*, that is, of *śṛṅgāra-rasa*, surpasses the other three. The edition’s heading: on impartial consideration the transcendental *madhura-rasa* includes all the other rasas, and so the effort to please Kṛṣṇa is greatest in it.

## 45
w: in ascending order  this
w: of special taste  full of delight  although
w: love  by inclination  sweet
w: appears  some one  to someone
tr: “Although love (*rati*), rising from one kind to the next, grows ever sweeter and more joyful, by the inclination of the heart a particular one appears sweetest to a particular devotee.” (Bhakti-rasāmṛta-sindhu, 2.5.38)
fn: *Anubhāṣya*: “in ascending order” — rising step by step; “by inclination” — by the difference of inclinations. *Amṛta-pravāha-bhāṣya*: *rati*, full of delight, reveals itself in ever new tastes and, according to inclination, shows in the best hearts the highest taste — *madhura-rasa*.

## 46
w: therefore  madhura  rasa  I call  its  name
w: svakīyā  parakīyā  in the form  twofold  existence
tr: Therefore I call it *madhura-rasa*, the sweet rasa. It exists in two forms: *svakīyā*, conjugal love, and *parakīyā*, love that is not conjugal.
fn: *Amṛta-pravāha-bhāṣya*: when *madhura-rasa* awakens toward Kṛṣṇa as one’s husband, it is *svakīyā*; when toward Him as a lover who is not one’s husband (*upapati*), it is *parakīyā*. The *Anubhāṣya*, after the *Ujjvala-nīlamaṇi*: *svakīyā* are the beloveds who became His wives by the law of scripture and are faithful to Him as husband (the queens of Dvārakā); *parakīyā* are those who out of rāga alone have given themselves to Kṛṣṇa, regarding neither this world nor the next, though dharma does not recognise them as His wives.

## 47
w: in the parakīyā mood  greatest  of rasa  the delight
w: Vraja  except  of it  elsewhere  is not  the abode
tr: In the mood of *parakīyā* rasa rejoices most of all, and nowhere but in Vraja does it dwell.
fn: In the edition the verse is headed: “Of these, Kṛṣṇa’s pleasure is greatest in the *parakīyā* mood, and its only abode is Vraja.” *Amṛta-pravāha-bhāṣya*: the knowers of *madhura-rasa* have unanimously concluded that in the *parakīyā* mood the delight of rasa is greatest and that outside Vraja it does not exist. Some think that Kṛṣṇa, who sports eternally in Goloka, only for a short time appeared in Vraja in this mood; but that is not the view of the Gosvāmīs: the pastimes of Vraja too are eternal, and Vraja is the innermost chamber of eternal Goloka.

## 48
w: of Vraja  of the young women  this  mood  boundless
w: of them  among  of Śrī Rādhā  of the mood  the summit
tr: In the young women of Vraja this mood is boundless, and among them it reaches its summit in Śrī Rādhā.
fn: In the edition the verse is headed: “The *parakīyā* mood dwells eternally in the ladies of Vraja and reaches its climax in Śrī Rādhā.”

## 49
w: mature  pure  of mood  love  the highest of all
w: of Kṛṣṇa  of sweetness  of the rasa  of the tasting  the cause
tr: Her love, mature and pure in feeling, is the highest of all; it is through this love that the sweetness of Kṛṣṇa is tasted in its fullness.
fn: *Amṛta-pravāha-bhāṣya*: the mature, spotless love of Śrī Rādhā in Vraja is the highest of all; whatever tasting of Kṛṣṇa’s *mādhurya-rasa* is possible at all, it comes through her.

## 50
w: therefore  that  mood  acceptance  making
w: fulfilled  His own  desire  Gaurāṅga  Śrī Hari
tr: Therefore Śrī Hari, appearing as Gaurāṅga, accepted that mood and fulfilled His own desire.
fn: In the edition the verse is headed: “Accepting the mood of Śrī Rādhā, in the form of Gaura He fulfils His three desires.”

## 51
w: of the lords of the gods  the inaccessible refuge  the goal  supremely  of the Upaniṣads
w: of the sages  the all-in-all  of the bowing hosts  the sweetness
w: the essence  of love  of all  the cowherd girls  lotus-eyed
w: that  Caitanya  will  of my  again  even  of the eyes  come  to the path
tr: “He is the refuge hard to reach even for the lords of the gods, the supreme goal of the Upaniṣads, the all-in-all of the sages, the sweetness of the hosts who bow to Him, and the very essence of the love of the lotus-eyed cowherd girls of Vraja. Will that Caitanya ever again come before my eyes?” (Stava-mālā, the first Caitanyāṣṭaka, 2)
fn: *Anubhāṣya*: *durgam* — “a refuge hard to attain” for Mahendra and the other lords of the gods; “the all-in-all of the sages” — the only wealth of those who have turned away from matter; “sweetness” — the abode of beauty for the hosts of devotees.

## 52
w: boundless  of a certain  of loving  persons  of the host  eager for delight
w: of rasa  the mass  having stolen  sweet  to relish  indescribable  who
w: lustre  His own  covered  radiance  here  Hers  revealing
w: that  Lord  in the form of Caitanya  exceedingly  to us  may He be merciful
tr: “The Lord, eager for delight, stole from one among the host of His beloveds (Śrī Rādhā) Her boundless, indescribably sweet wealth of rasa, that He might relish it Himself. For this He covered His own lustre and revealed here Her radiance. May that Lord, in the form of Caitanya, be boundlessly merciful to us!” (Stava-mālā, the second Caitanyāṣṭaka, 3)
fn: *Anubhāṣya*: “eager for delight” (*kutukī*) — He who rejoices in tasting the moods; “to relish” — to taste it Himself by accepting Her mood; “His own lustre” — His form, dark as a rain-cloud; “Her radiance” — the beauty of her who embodies love for Him; the Lord is the Beloved of the gopīs. The same verse recurs as 4.275.

## 53
w: of the mood  of accepting  for the sake  made  of dharma  the establishment
w: of it  the chief  reason  I tell  hear  all people
tr: It was to accept that mood that He established dharma. Now I shall tell its chief reason — listen, everyone.

## 56

fn: In the edition the verse is headed: “The beginning of the explanation of the fifth verse: the hidden cause of Gaura’s descent; first, the truth of Rādhā and Kṛṣṇa.” *Amṛta-pravāha-bhāṣya*: the words of these verses are plain, but their inner sense is deep. Rādhā is the potency (*śakti*), Kṛṣṇa the possessor of potency; the Vedānta’s words “the potency and its possessor are not different” mean that a potency cannot be separated from its bearer; yet by inconceivable power Rādhā and Kṛṣṇa are eternally separate, to taste the rasa of pastimes with each other, and at the same time one.

## 61

His one spiritual potency in the aspect of being is Sandhinī, which extends existence; in the aspect of knowledge Samvit, complete knowledge, the very nature of Kṛṣṇa; in the aspect of bliss Hlādinī, who gives joy to Him Himself.

## 62
w: in the aspect of bliss  hlādinī  in the aspect of being  sandhinī

tr: In the aspect of bliss She is Hlādinī, in the aspect of being Sandhinī, and in the aspect of consciousness Samvit, which we know as knowledge.
fn: *Anubhāṣya* (after Śrī Jīva’s *Bhagavat-sandarbha*, 102): Sandhinī is the potency by which the Lord upholds His own being and that of all, and manifests time, place and all else; Samvit is that by which He knows and makes others know; Hlādinī is that by which He Himself tastes bliss and makes others taste it. The self-luminous working of this one supreme potency is called *viśuddha-sattva*: in it there is no touch of māyā, and from it the abode of Vaikuṇṭha is manifest.

## 63
w: hlādinī  sandhinī  samvit
w: in You  one  of all  the basis
w: of joy  of pain  causing  mixed
w: in You  not  of the guṇas  devoid
tr: “O Lord, You are the basis of all, and in You Hlādinī, Sandhinī and Samvit are one potency. But that which causes joy, pain and a mixture of the two is not in You, for You are beyond the guṇas.” (Viṣṇu Purāṇa, 1.12.69)
fn: In the edition, “the words of Dhruva”. *Amṛta-pravāha-bhāṣya*: in You, the shelter of all and beyond the guṇas, the three workings of the potency — Hlādinī, Sandhinī and Samvit — are wholly spiritual; only the *jīva*, a tiny spark subject to māyā, dwelling in the three guṇas, knows this potency as giving joy (*hlādakarī*), pain (*tāpakarī*) or both (*miśrā*); in You it is pure and one. *Anubhāṣya* (after Śrī Jīva’s *Prīti-sandarbha*, 65): the potency by which the Lord delights in His bliss is not the worldly bliss of *sattva*, as the Sāṅkhyas hold, for māyā has no power over the Lord; nor the bliss of His impersonal essence, as those who hold Him to be without attributes say; nor the bliss of the *jīva*, for the *jīva* is tiny. It is His own potency Hlādinī: through it He tastes a special bliss, and through it He gives that bliss to His devotees; and so the conclusion of the impersonalists must be rejected.

fn: In the edition the verse is headed: “The service of Sandhinī — to make Bhagavān manifest.” *Amṛta-pravāha-bhāṣya*: the essence of Sandhinī, the potency that extends being, is called *śuddha-sattva*. *Sattva* is of two kinds, mixed (*miśra*) and pure (*śuddha*); *sattva* is the name of being itself, and without the working of Sandhinī there is no being at all; the manifestation of Bhagavān’s being is also her work.

When the spiritual Samvit, joined with Hlādinī, shows mercy to the *jīva*, the knowledge that Kṛṣṇa is Bhagavān awakens in him — this is the essence of Samvit; the knowledge of Brahman and the knowledge of the world are its “retinue”, that is, only coverings at different stages.

## 68

fn: *Amṛta-pravāha-bhāṣya*: the working of Hlādinī is called prema. Prema is pure or mixed: when Hlādinī in Kṛṣṇa, giving Him bliss, together with pure Samvit shows mercy to the *jīva*, love for Kṛṣṇa is born in the *jīva*; but when the transformation of Hlādinī in the *jīva* draws him, through māyā, toward the world, the *jīva*, intoxicated with worldly love, loses love for Kṛṣṇa and falls under the sway of joy and sorrow. The ideal of love for the *jīvas* is the gopīs of Vraja, and highest among them is Śrī Rādhā.

## 97

## 103

## 104

tr: That most hidden reason is threefold, and it was made known by Svarūpa Dāmodara.
fn: *Amṛta-pravāha-bhāṣya*: the chief reason for Gaura’s descent is very hidden, and it is of three kinds; it is told later in the root verse “*Śrī-rādhāyāḥ praṇaya-mahimā…*” (4.230).

## 105
w: Svarūpa  Gosāñi  of the Lord  very  intimate
w: therefore  knows  of the Lord  these  matters
tr: Svarūpa Gosāñi is the Lord’s most intimate associate, and so he knows all these matters of the Lord.
fn: *Anubhāṣya*: Śrī Puruṣottama Bhaṭṭācārya lived in Navadvīpa. Even before Mahāprabhu’s *sannyāsa*, wishing to take *sannyāsa*, he went to Vārāṇasī and became a *brahmacārī* among the Daśanāmī *daṇḍīs*; then he was named Śrī Dāmodara-svarūpa. Without waiting for full *sannyāsa*, he lived for the rest of his life at Mahāprabhu’s lotus feet in Nīlācala, always beside Him, and by singing what He asked for gave Him the highest joy at every moment; by his grace the devotees came to know the hidden moods of the Lord’s heart. In the pastimes of Vraja he is Lalitā-devī, Rādhikā’s second self; according to Kavi Karṇapūra’s *Gaura-gaṇoddeśa-dīpikā*, Viśākhā-devī: “She who in Vraja taught Rādhā the arts, Viśākhā, is now Svarūpa Gosvāmī, who delights in those moods.”

## 106
w: of Rādhikā  of the mood  the embodiment  of the Lord  the heart
w: in that mood  joy  sorrow  arise  incessantly
tr: The Lord’s heart is the very embodiment of Rādhikā’s mood, and in that mood joy and sorrow arise in Him incessantly.
fn: In the edition the verse is headed: “Gaurasundara, immersed in Rādhā’s *mahābhāva*.” *Anubhāṣya*: the heart of Śrī Gaurasundara is filled with a form woven of the moods of Śrī Rādhikā; people of gross mind, busy gratifying the senses, cannot understand what “the embodiment of a mood” means. Rādhikā’s mood is the highest and complete stage of *mādhurya*; it is *rūḍha* or *adhirūḍha*, and here the *adhirūḍha-mahābhāva* of Śrī Gaurasundara is meant.

## 107
w: in the last pastimes  of the Lord  separation from Kṛṣṇa  madness
w: of bewilderment  full  actions  always  of delirium  full  speech
tr: In His last pastimes the Lord was mad with separation from Kṛṣṇa: His actions were full of bewilderment, and His speech was always full of delirium.
fn: As in the Gauḍīya Maṭha edition: «সদা» (“always”); another reading has «আর» (“and”). *Anubhāṣya*: in the conduct of a perfected soul Śrī Gaurahari displays the supreme height of *vipralambha-rasa*; those who trust only the knowledge of the senses take His experiences for delirium and wandering of mind. The doctrine of the “Nadīyā-nāgarīs”, which distorts the path of following, is only an attempt at sense gratification, not the path shown by Śrī Gaurasundara; its adherents are materialists and therefore enemies of Viṣṇu.

## 108
w: of Rādhikā  the mood  as  on seeing Uddhava
w: in that  mood  intoxicated  the Lord  remains  night and day
tr: As Rādhikā felt on seeing Uddhava, so the Lord remained, intoxicated with that mood, day and night.
fn: *Amṛta-pravāha-bhāṣya*: Kṛṣṇa sent Uddhava from Mathurā to the gopīs with news of Himself, and on seeing Uddhava Śrīmatī Rādhikā showed wondrous moods. *Anubhāṣya*: on seeing Uddhava, Kṛṣṇa’s friend, Rādhikā voiced in many kinds of speech a hidden anger full of feeling and of utmost longing — this is *citra-jalpa* (*Ujjvala-nīlamaṇi*: “speech full of many moods at the sight of the Beloved’s friend, in which hidden anger shows through, and the utmost longing”); in that “Mathurā” mood Śrī Gaurasundara, one with Śrī Rādhā, was intoxicated day and night.
