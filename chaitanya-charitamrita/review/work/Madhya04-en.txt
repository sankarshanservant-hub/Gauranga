tr: Relishing the Life of Śrī Mādhavendra Purī

## 1
w: to whom  to give  stealing  of sweet rice  the pot
w: Gopīnātha  Kṣīra-corā  named  became
w: Śrī Gopāla  appeared  conquered  being
w: by whose love  to that  Mādhavendra  bowed down  am I
tr: I bow down to Mādhavendra Purī, for whose sake Gopīnātha stole a pot of sweet rice and so became known as Kṣīra-corā, “the thief of sweet rice”, and conquered by whose love Śrī Gopāla revealed Himself.
fn: Heading in the edition: “Obeisance to Mādhavendra Purī, the servant of ‘Kṣīra-corā Gopīnātha’”. *Anubhāṣya*: Gopīnātha — the famous Deity of the village of Remuṇā; Śrī Gopāla, the Holder of Govardhana — the Deity installed by Vajra; Mādhavendra Purī — the disciple of Lakṣmīpati, a guru of the Madhva sampradāya.

## 2
w: glory  glory  to Gauracandra  glory  to Nityānanda
w: glory to Advaitacandra  glory  of Gaura  the devotees  to the multitude
tr: Glory, glory to Gauracandra! Glory to Nityānanda! Glory to Advaitacandra! Glory to all the devotees of Gaura!
fn: The same verse as Ādi 4.2, except that the first line here has *gauracandra* in place of *śrī-caitanya*; translation as there, with that name.

tr: The Lord’s coming to Nīlādri, His seeing Jagannātha, His meeting with Sārvabhauma Bhaṭṭācārya—all these pastimes Śrī Vṛndāvana dāsa has described in detail, and his description is excellent.
fn: *Amṛta-pravāha-bhāṣya*: see *Caitanya-bhāgavata*, Antya-khaṇḍa, chapters 2 and 3.

tr: The pastimes of Caitanya are by their very nature wonderful and sweet, and from the lips of Vṛndāvana dāsa they flow as a stream of nectar.

fn: *Amṛta-pravāha-bhāṣya*: Remuṇā is a village five miles west of Bāleśvara; there Kṣīra-corā Gopīnātha resides.

fn: Heading in the edition: the Lord stays there to honour the *kṣīra-prasāda*, which, as He had heard from His guru, awakens the memory of Kṛṣṇa’s love for His devotees.

fn: The Gauḍīya Maṭha edition reads *mādhava-purī lāgi’*, without the genitive ending.
fn: *Amṛta-pravāha-bhāṣya*: Mādhava Purī is Mādhavendra Purī.

## 27

## 197
w: O  melting with compassion for the wretched  Lord  O
w: of Mathurā  Lord  when shall You be seen
w: heart  at not seeing You  stricken
w: O Beloved  wanders  what  do  I
tr: “O Lord, melting with compassion for the wretched! O Lord of Mathurā, when shall I see You? My heart, stricken at not seeing You, has lost all rest. O Beloved, what am I to do?” (Padyāvalī, 400)
fn: The source, by the heading in the edition: “*tathāhi padyāvalīte catuḥśatāṅka-dhṛta mādhavendrapurī-vākya*” — “the words of Mādhavendra Purī, given in the *Padyāvalī* as number 400”. The verse is quoted again in Antya 8. It is Śrī Rādhā’s speech (194), hence the capitals, as in Madhya 1.58. *Anubhāṣya*: *ayi* is the tender address of the daughter of King Vṛṣabhānu to her Beloved; the wretched are the gopīs, tormented by separation; “Lord of Mathurā” is a reproach: forgetting the gopīs, You delight the women of Mathurā. *Amṛta-pravāha-bhāṣya*: with this verse Mādhavendra Purī sowed the seed of bhakti full of *śṛṅgāra-rasa* — worship in the mood of Śrīmatī Rādhikā’s separation from Kṛṣṇa, who has gone to Mathurā; hence the mahājanas call Mādhavendra Purī the root of the tree of *śṛṅgāra-rasa*, Īśvara Purī its sprout and Mahāprabhu its trunk.
