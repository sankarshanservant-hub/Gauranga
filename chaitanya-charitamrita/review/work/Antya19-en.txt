## 1

tr: I bow down to Śrī Kṛṣṇa Caitanya, the crown jewel of all who are devoted to their mothers — to Him who, raving in His madness, rubbed His face against the walls and sported in the spring garden.
fn: So in the edition: «মধুদ্যানে» (*madhudyāne*); by the rule of sandhi (*madhu* + *udyāna*) — «মধূদ্যানে», as in the *Anubhāṣya*. The sense is the same: “in the spring garden”.
fn: Heading in the edition: obeisance to the Lord, full of matchless tenderness towards His devotee-mother and overwhelmed by *mahābhāva* in the Jagannātha-vallabha garden. *Anubhāṣya*: the *madhūdyāna* is the grove of spring pastimes called Jagannātha-vallabha. *Amṛta-pravāha-bhāṣya*: He rubbed His face against the walls of the house, and He sported in the spring garden to show His longing for love of Kṛṣṇa.

## 20
w: to the madman  tell  people  have become  mad
w: to the madman  tell  in the market  not  sells  rice
tr: Tell the madman (*bāul*) that the people have gone mad. Tell the madman that rice no longer sells in the market.
fn: *Amṛta-pravāha-bhāṣya*: the people have gone mad with love (prema), and in the market of love there is no longer any place to sell rice — that is, love; the *āul*, the *bāul* mad with love, is no longer engaged in worldly affairs; and Advaita, himself mad with love, has said this. The meaning: the purpose for which the Lord appeared has been fulfilled; now let it be as the Lord wishes. Heading in the edition: a hint that the purpose of Mahāprabhu’s descent has been fulfilled and that it is time to conclude His pastimes. *Anubhāṣya*: there is a variant reading, “the people have become *āul*”; *āul* means “overcome [by feeling]”, that is, full of love, and also “possessing nothing”, “afflicted”.

fn: *Anubhāṣya*: some explain *kāye nāhika āula* thus: in the work of preaching love there is no longer any disorder.

fn: *Amṛta-pravāha-bhāṣya*: the nature of the *seyākul* thorn is that once it catches, it hardly lets go. *Anubhāṣya*: mango sap, once it sticks, is hard to remove.
