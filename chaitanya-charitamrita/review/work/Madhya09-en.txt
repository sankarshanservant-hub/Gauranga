## 0
tr: The Pilgrimage to the Holy Places of the South

## 1
w: of various  doctrines  by the crocodiles  seized
w: of the South  the people  elephants
w: with the disc of mercy  freeing  them
w: Gaura  made  He  Vaiṣṇavas
tr: The people of the South were like elephants seized by the crocodiles of various doctrines. Gaura freed them with the disc of His mercy and made them Vaiṣṇavas.
fn: *Amṛta-pravāha-bhāṣya*: the people of the South are like Gajendra, and the crocodiles are Buddhism, Jainism, Māyāvāda and other doctrines.

## 2
w: glory  glory  to Śrī Caitanya  glory  to Nityānanda
w: glory to Advaitacandra  glory  of Gaura  the devotees  to the multitude
tr: Glory, glory to Śrī Caitanya! Glory to Nityānanda! Glory to Advaitacandra! Glory to all the devotees of Gaura!
fn: The same verse as Ādi 4.2; translation as there.

## 3
w: to the south  the journey  of the Lord  most  extraordinary
w: thousands  upon thousands  of holy places  He made  visit
tr: The Lord’s journey to the south was most extraordinary: He visited thousands upon thousands of holy places.

## 4
w: those  all  holy places  touching  great holy places  He made
w: under that  pretext  of that  land  the people  He delivered
tr: By touching all those holy places He made them great holy places, and under that pretext He delivered the people of that land.

## 5
w: of those  holy places  the order  to arrange  not  I am able
w: right and left  to the holy places  the journey  was  back and forth
tr: I cannot set those holy places in order, for His journey to them went right and left, back and forth.
fn: Heading in the edition: the Lord went this way and that across the South, so the author’s account does not keep to the geographical order and gives only the general direction of the journey.

## 6
w: therefore  the names  only  I make  enumeration
w: to describe  not  I am able  their  proper  sequence
tr: Therefore I simply list their names; I cannot describe them in their proper sequence.

## 7–8

w: other  villages  deliver  them  Vaiṣṇavas  making
tr: As before, whoever saw Him on the road and all the people of every village He came to became Vaiṣṇavas and chanted “Kṛṣṇa” and “Hari.” They in turn delivered other villages, making their people Vaiṣṇavas.

## 9
w: of the south  of the land  the people  of many  kinds
w: some  jñānīs  some  karmīs  unbelievers  countless
tr: The people of the South were of many kinds: some were *jñānīs*, some were *karmīs*, and the unbelievers were countless.
fn: *Amṛta-pravāha-bhāṣya*: the *pāṣaṇḍīs* (unbelievers) are the *jñānīs* and the followers of *karma-vāda*, the opponents of pure devotion.

w: those  all  people  of the Lord  of the sight  by the power

tr: By the power of seeing the Lord, all those people gave up their own doctrines and became Vaiṣṇavas.

## 25

tr: The brāhmaṇa said, “This is by the power of seeing You. When I saw You, the nature I had from birth left me.

## 26

tr: From childhood I have chanted the name of Rāma. When I saw You, the name of Kṛṣṇa came to me once.

## 27
w: from that  time  the name of Kṛṣṇa  on my tongue  sat

tr: Since then the name of Kṛṣṇa has dwelt on my tongue: it is Kṛṣṇa’s name that now comes forth, and the name of Rāma has gone far away.

## 28
w: from childhood  time  my  nature  one  is
w: of the name  of the glory  of the scriptures  I make  collecting
tr: From childhood I have had one habit: I collect what the scriptures say about the glory of the holy name.

## 29
w: take delight  the yogīs  in the Infinite
w: in true bliss  in the spiritual self
w: thus  by the word “Rāma”  this
w: the Supreme  Brahman  is called
tr: “The yogīs delight (*ramante*) in the Infinite, in true bliss, in the spiritual Self; therefore the Supreme Brahman is called ‘Rāma.’ ” (Padma Purāṇa, Śrī Rāmacandra’s Śata-nāma-stotra, 8)
fn: The verse explains the name “Rāma” by its derivation: from the root *ram*, “to delight” (*ramante*, “they delight”).

## 30
w: “kṛṣ”  being  denoting  the sound
w: “ṇa”  and  bliss  denoting
w: of the two  the union  the Supreme  Brahman
w: Kṛṣṇa  thus  is called
tr: “The sound ‘kṛṣ’ denotes being, and ‘ṇa’ denotes bliss. Their union, the Supreme Brahman, is called ‘Kṛṣṇa.’ ” (Mahābhārata, Udyoga-parva, 71.4)
fn: Source as in the edition: a verse of the *Mahābhārata* (Udyoga-parva, 71.4) quoted by Śrīdhara Svāmī.

tr: Both names belong to the Supreme Brahman and so are equal. But in the scriptures I found a further distinction.

w: the name of Rāma  O fair-faced one

## 40
w: of the Lord  by the influence  people  came  for sight

tr: Drawn by the Lord’s power, people came to see Him — hundreds of thousands, millions, beyond counting.

## 41
w: of the Gosāñi  the beauty  seeing  in it  absorption in love
w: all  “Kṛṣṇa”  say  Vaiṣṇava  became  the whole  land
tr: Seeing the Lord’s beauty and His absorption in love, everyone said “Kṛṣṇa,” and the whole land became Vaiṣṇava.

## 42
w: logicians  Mīmāṁsakas  all  Māyāvādīs
w: Sāṅkhya  Pātañjala  smṛti  Purāṇa  Āgama
tr: There were logicians and Mīmāṁsakas, all the Māyāvādīs, and followers of Sāṅkhya and Pātañjala (yoga), of the smṛtis, the Purāṇas and the Āgamas.
fn: *Amṛta-pravāha-bhāṣya*: the logicians (*tārkikas*) are the Naiyāyikas, followers of Gautama, and the Vaiśeṣikas, followers of Kaṇāda; the Mīmāṁsakas follow Jaimini; the Māyāvādīs, Śaṅkara; Sāṅkhya is the teaching of Kapila; Pātañjala is the yoga-śāstra; the smṛtis are the twenty dharma-śāstra *saṁhitās* of Manu, Atri and others; the Purāṇas are the eighteen *mahāpurāṇas* and eighteen *upapurāṇas*; the Āgamas are the tantras.

## 43

w: all  doctrines  finding fault  the Lord  makes  to pieces  to pieces
tr: All of them were fierce in insisting on their own scriptures, but the Lord exposed the faults of every doctrine and broke them to pieces.

## 44

## 46

## 48

w: still  spoke  the Lord  the pride  to break
tr: Although the Buddhists are not to be spoken with, and it is improper even to look at them, the Lord spoke in order to break their pride.
fn: *Amṛta-pravāha-bhāṣya*: one who opposes the Vedas and has turned away from bhakti is not to be spoken with; and of one who has seen such an unbeliever the śāstra says, “let him enter the water with his clothes on.”

## 49
w: on logic  based  Buddhist  scriptures  “nine  principles”
w: by logic itself  refuted  the Lord  not  they can  establish
tr: The Buddhist scriptures, with their “nine principles,” rest on logic, so the Lord refuted them by logic alone, and they could not uphold them.
fn: *Anubhāṣya*: the Buddhist śāstras do not follow the path of *śruti*, Vedic revelation, and so the Lord refutes them by logic alone.
fn: *Amṛta-pravāha-bhāṣya*: the Buddhists have two paths, the *hīnayāna* and the *mahāyāna*, and both start from nine tenets: the universe is beginningless and so has no Īśvara; the world is unreal; the truth is the “I”; rebirth and the other world are real; Buddha alone is the way to know the truth; *nirvāṇa* is the highest truth; Buddhist philosophy alone is true; the Vedas were composed by men; the Buddhist life is the practice of true dharma — compassion and the like.
