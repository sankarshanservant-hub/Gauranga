fn: *Caitanya-caritāmṛtam*, “the nectar of Caitanya’s deeds,” is also the title of the book. *Anubhāṣya*: hear, sing and remember it again and again. *Amṛta-pravāha-bhāṣya*: always hear this *Caitanya-caritāmṛta*, sing it and joyfully meditate on it.

fn: As in Madhya 16.19. *Anubhāṣya*: when pilgrims passed through a landlord’s estate they had to pay a toll, which went to the owners of the roads and crossings; at every toll post (*ghāṭī*) Śivānanda paid the toll due from the pilgrims to Jagannātha.

fn: The Gauḍīya Maṭha edition reads *ghāṭite*, “at the toll post”; another reading is *ghāṭiyāle*, “by the toll collector.” *Anubhāṣya*: the toll collectors oppressed the pilgrims and held them at the toll post to extort more than was due; Śivānanda stood surety for them all and had them released.

fn: Heading in the edition: Nityānanda, in His transcendental form as a cowherd boy of Vraja, has not satisfied His hunger and shows Śivānanda a feigned semblance of anger (cf. verse 33).

## 23

fn: *Tāṅra bālāi laña*, “taking His misfortunes upon themselves”: an expression of loving devotion, wishing that every ill that threatens the beloved may fall upon oneself. *Anubhāṣya*: *bāuli* means “crazy woman”; another reading is *bāunī*, “brāhmaṇa lady,” a respectful way of addressing women in those days.

fn: *Viparīta* (in quotation marks in the text): “the reverse,” contrary to what one would expect. Heading in the edition: the semblance of anger in Nityānanda, the guru, is a hidden, supreme mercy and the sign of eternal good.

## 59

w: yet  his  out of affection  anything  not  He said
tr: Hearing the name of Mukunda’s mother, the Lord felt embarrassed, yet out of affection for him He said nothing.
fn: Heading in the edition: the Lord, the guru of the world and teacher of mankind, who enacted the pastime of sannyāsa, feels embarrassed on hearing a woman’s name, even though she is elderly and like a mother to Him.

## 60
w: the boldness of loving familiarity  pure courtesy  not  knows
w: within  pleased  became  the Lord  his  that  quality
tr: The boldness born of loving familiarity knows nothing of the niceties of courtesy. Within His heart the Lord was pleased with that quality of his.
fn: *Praśraya-prāgalbhya* — “the boldness of loving familiarity”; *śuddha-vaidagdhī* — “pure courtesy.” The Gauḍīya Maṭha edition reads *praśraya-prāgalbhya*; another reading is *praśraya-pāgala*, “simple in his affection.” *Amṛta-pravāha-bhāṣya*: to say to a sannyāsī, “Mukunda’s mother has come,” is the boldness born of long familiarity, and such boldness never knows the pure art of speech. *Anubhāṣya*: *praśraya* — affection, affectionate respect, humility, trust, loving liberty; *prāgalbhya* — boldness, forwardness; *vaidagdhī* — refinement, polish, skill, elegance of manners. Heading in the edition: the Lord, who accepts the feeling of the heart, is pleased with his guilelessness.
