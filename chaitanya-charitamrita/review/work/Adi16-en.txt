## 0
tr: A Brief Description of the Youthful Pastimes

## 1
w: of mercy  nectar  the river  whose
w: the universe  flooding  although
w: flowing downward  always  shines
w: that  Caitanya Prabhu  I worship
tr: I worship Caitanya Prabhu. The river of His nectarean mercy floods the whole universe, and yet, like every river, it always flows downward — to the lowly.
fn: A play on words: *nīca-gā* means both “flowing downward” and “going to the lowly”. *Anubhāṣya*: the Lord’s mercy is always turned towards the humble and destitute, who have neither wealth nor greatness.

## 2
w: glory  glory  to Śrī Caitanya  glory  to Nityānanda
w: glory to Advaitacandra  glory  of Gaura  the devotees  to the multitude
tr: Glory, glory to Śrī Caitanya! Glory to Nityānanda! Glory to Advaitacandra! Glory to all the devotees of Gaura!

## 3
w: may He be victorious  in His youth  Caitanya
w: embodied  on entering household life
w: by Lakṣmī  worshipped  then  by the goddess of speech
w: of the directions  of the conqueror  of the conquest  under the pretext
tr: Glory to Caitanya in His youth! When He entered household life, Lakṣmī herself served Him in visible form; and then the goddess of speech, Sarasvatī, worshipped Him too, making His victory over the conqueror of all directions the occasion of her worship.
fn: *Anubhāṣya*: Lakṣmī, having taken a body, served the Lord when He entered household life; Sarasvatī, the goddess of speech, honoured Him when He defeated the *digvijayī* — the scholar Keśava Kāśmīrī, “conqueror of the directions”. Heading in the edition: “Gaurahari, worshipped by Lakṣmī and Sarasvatī.”

## 4
w: thus  of youth  of the pastimes  of the outline  the sequence
w: the students  to teach  He made  beginning
tr: Here, in outline, are the pastimes of His youth. He began to teach students of His own.
fn: *Anubhāṣya*: *kaiśora*, youth, is the period from the eleventh year of life to the fifteenth.

## 5
w: hundreds  hundreds  students  with  always  teaching
w: the explanations  hearing  of all  people  astonished  minds
tr: He taught constantly, with hundreds of students around Him, and all who heard His explanations were amazed.

## 6
w: in all the śāstras  all  paṇḍitas  meet  defeat
w: by His courteous manner  anyone’s  pain  not  arises
tr: In every branch of learning every paṇḍita met defeat at His hands, yet His manner was so courteous that no one was hurt.

## 7
w: various  pranks  He plays  the students  with
w: in the Jāhnavī  water sports  He has  with many  delights
tr: With His students He played all kinds of pranks, and they sported merrily in the waters of the Gaṅgā.

## 8
w: after some  days  made  the Lord  to Baṅga  journey
w: wherever  He went,  there  He made them take up  of the holy name  the chanting
tr: After some time the Lord went to East Bengal, and wherever He went He had the people take up the chanting of the holy name.
fn: *Amṛta-pravāha-bhāṣya* (introduction to the chapter): the Lord went to East Bengal to gather funds; there He held learned discussions and spread the chanting of the holy name.

## 9
w: of learning  the power  seeing  wonder  in heart
w: hundreds  hundreds  students  coming  began  to study
tr: Seeing the power of His learning, hundreds of students came in wonder to study with Him.

## 10
w: in that  land  a brāhmaṇa,  named —  Miśra  Tapana
w: to settle  could not  the goal and the means
tr: In that land lived a brāhmaṇa named Tapana Miśra, who could not settle what the goal of life is and how it is reached.
fn: *Amṛta-pravāha-bhāṣya*: *sādhya* is what is attained; *sādhana* is the means by which it is attained.

## 11
w: in many scriptures  in many statements  in mind  confusion  arises
w: the goal and the means  best  not  is  certain
tr: Many scriptures and many opinions had confused his mind, and he could not decide which goal is highest and which means is best.
fn: *Anubhāṣya*: one who seeks the highest good should not study many books or live by expounding scripture (*Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam* 7.13.8); the flowery words of scriptures that nourish karma and knowledge lead the inexperienced away from pure devotion (11.21.30, 36). Through Tapana Miśra’s doubts the Lord teaches those who follow many scriptures and many teachers. Later, in Vārāṇasī, Tapana Miśra heard the Lord’s answers to Śrī Sanātana’s questions on the goal and the means, and his doubts were resolved.

## 12
w: in a dream  a  brāhmaṇa  says —  listen,  Tapana
w: of Nimāi Paṇḍita  to the place  make  going
tr: In a dream a brāhmaṇa said to him, “Listen, Tapana. Go to Nimāi Paṇḍita.

## 13
w: He  your  goal and means  will make  certain
w: directly  the Lord  He is —  there is no  doubt
tr: He will settle for you the goal and the means. He is the Lord Himself — have no doubt of it.”

## 14
w: the dream  seeing  Miśra  coming  of the Lord  at the feet
w: of the dream  the account  all  made  submission
tr: After this dream Miśra came to the Lord’s feet and told Him all that he had dreamt.

## 15
w: the Lord  pleased  becoming  the goal and the means  told
w: of the holy name  the chanting  do —  instruction  gave
tr: Pleased with him, the Lord explained the goal and the means, and instructed him: “Chant the holy name.”
fn: *Amṛta-pravāha-bhāṣya*: the Lord said that neither knowledge of the non-dual Brahman nor the pleasures of heaven is the goal of the soul; its only goal is love for Kṛṣṇa. Karma and knowledge are not the means to it; the only means is pure devotion that takes shelter of the name of Kṛṣṇa.

## 16
w: his  wish —  with the Lord  in Navadvīpa  to live
w: the Lord  order  gave —  you  go  to Vārāṇasī
tr: He wished to live with the Lord in Navadvīpa, but the Lord told him, “Go to Vārāṇasī.

## 17
w: there  with Me  your  will be  meeting
w: the order  receiving  Miśra  made  to Kāśī  going
tr: There we shall meet again.” Receiving this order, Miśra went to Kāśī.

## 18
w: of the Lord  endless  pastimes  to understand  not  I can
w: from His own company  sending away  why  He sends  to Kāśī
tr: The Lord’s pastimes are endless and beyond my understanding. Why did He send Miśra away from His company to Kāśī?

## 19
w: in this  way  of Baṅga  to the people  did  to all  good
w: the name  giving  devotees  He made,  teaching  paṇḍitas
tr: In this way the Lord did good to all the people of East Bengal: giving them the holy name He made them devotees, and teaching them He made them paṇḍitas.
fn: *Amṛta-pravāha-bhāṣya*: the Lord gave the people of Bengal the name of Kṛṣṇa — “Hare Kṛṣṇa Hare Kṛṣṇa Kṛṣṇa Kṛṣṇa Hare Hare / Hare Rāma Hare Rāma Rāma Rāma Hare Hare” — and so made them devotees, and by teaching them the śāstras He made many of them scholars.

## 20
w: in this  way  in Baṅga  the Lord  enjoys  various  pastimes
w: here  in Navadvīpa  Lakṣmī  in separation  sorrowful  became
tr: While the Lord enjoyed these many pastimes in East Bengal, here in Navadvīpa Lakṣmī grieved in separation from Him.

## 21
w: of the Lord  the snake of separation  Lakṣmī  bit
w: of the snake of separation  by the poison  her  passing to the other world  came about
tr: The snake of separation from the Lord bit Lakṣmī, and by its poison she passed from this world.
fn: *Amṛta-pravāha-bhāṣya*: the pain of separation from the Lord took the form of a snake and bit Lakṣmī, and she went to “the other world” — her own abode, Vaikuṇṭha, the highest of all.

## 22
w: within  knew  the Lord,  since  the Indweller
w: to His homeland  came  the Lord  of Śacī  the grief  knowing
tr: The Lord knew of it within, for He dwells in every heart; and knowing Śacī’s grief, He returned home.

## 23
w: home  came  the Lord  much  bringing  wealth and people
w: the truth  speaking  made  of Śacī  of grief  the removal
tr: He came home with much wealth and many followers, and by speaking of the truth He freed Śacī from her grief.
fn: *Amṛta-pravāha-bhāṣya*: there is a reading “with the net of truth” (*tattva-jāle*): “Who is whose husband, who is whose son, who is whose wife?” — casting such a net of true knowledge, the Lord relieved Śacī’s grief.

## 24
w: the students  gathering  again  of learning  the play
w: by the power of learning  all  conquering  pride  displaying
tr: Again He gathered His students and delighted in the play of learning; by the power of His knowledge He conquered everyone and showed a proud spirit.

## 25
w: then  of Viṣṇupriyā  Ṭhākurāṇī  the marriage
w: then  indeed  made  the Lord  over the conqueror of the directions  victory
tr: Then came His marriage to Viṣṇupriyā Ṭhākurāṇī, and after that the Lord’s victory over the *digvijayī*, the conqueror of all directions.
fn: Heading in the edition: “Marriage to Śrī Viṣṇupriyā, daughter of the royal paṇḍita Sanātana, and the defeat of Keśava Kāśmīrī.” *Anubhāṣya*: the *digvijayī* was the paṇḍita Keśava of Kashmir. He travelled through India conquering scholars by his genius and was defeated in Navadvīpa by Śrī Gaurasundara. Having learned the truth about Mahāprabhu, he entered the Nimbārka sampradāya for the sake of devotion and wrote the *Kaustubha-prabhā*, a gloss on Śrīnivāsācārya’s *Vedānta-kaustubha*, the commentary on Nimbārka’s *Pārijāta-bhāṣya*.

## 26
w: Vṛndāvana-dāsa  this  has made  elaboration
w: clearly  not  makes  of faults and merits  analysis
tr: Vṛndāvana dāsa has described all this at length, but he did not set out clearly the merits and faults of the *digvijayī*’s verse.

## 27
w: that  part  I tell,  to him  making  obeisance
w: which  hearing  the *digvijayī*  made  of himself  reproach
tr: Bowing to him, I shall tell that part, on hearing which the *digvijayī* cursed himself.

## 28
w: moonlit  night,  the Lord  the students  with
w: was sitting  on the bank of the Gaṅgā  in discussion of learning
tr: One moonlit night the Lord sat with His students on the bank of the Gaṅgā, discussing points of learning.

## 29
w: at that time  the *digvijayī*  just there  came
w: to the Gaṅgā  homage  having made  the Lord  he met
tr: Just then the *digvijayī* came there and, after offering his prayers to the Gaṅgā, met the Lord.

## 30
w: seated  him  the Lord  respect  showing
w: the *digvijayī*  speaks  in his mind  disdain  holding
tr: The Lord received him with respect and gave him a seat, but the *digvijayī* spoke with disdain in his heart:

## 31
w: grammar  You teach,  Nimāi  Paṇḍita —  Your  name
w: in the children’s science  people  Your  speak of  merits
tr: “You teach grammar, and Your name is Nimāi Paṇḍita. People praise Your skill in the science that children learn.
fn: *Anubhāṣya*: “the children’s science” is grammar, for before studying any śāstra one customarily learns grammar in order to know the language.

## 32
w: among grammars,  I know,  You teach  the Kalāpa
w: I have heard  on the tricky points  Your  students’  discussions
tr: Of the grammars, I know, You teach the *Kalāpa*, and I have heard of Your students’ debates on its tricky points.”
fn: *Amṛta-pravāha-bhāṣya*: the Lord teaches the *Kalāpa* grammar, and His students hold discussions on its *phāṅki* — its intricate and puzzling questions.

## 33
w: the Lord  says,  grammar  I teach —  so I fancy
w: the students  not  understand,  I  to explain  cannot
tr: The Lord said, “Yes, I teach grammar — or so I like to think. But My students do not understand, and I cannot explain it to them.

## 34
w: where  you,  in all the śāstras  in poetry  expert
w: where  I,  merely  a child —  a student  new
tr: “How far above Me you are! You are expert in every śāstra and in poetry, while I am only a child, a new student.

## 35
w: your  poetry  some  to hear  is  My mind
w: mercy  showing  make  if  of the Gaṅgā  a description
tr: “I should like to hear some of your poetry. Be kind enough, if you will, to describe the Gaṅgā.”

## 36
w: hearing this  the brāhmaṇa  with pride  to describe  began
w: in a ghaṭī  one  a hundred  verses  the Gaṅgā  he described
tr: Hearing this, the brāhmaṇa proudly began, and within one *ghaṭī* he described the Gaṅgā in a hundred verses.
fn: A *ghaṭī* (*ghaṭikā*) is a measure of time of twenty-four minutes.

## 37
w: hearing  made  the Lord  great  honour
w: you  like  in the world  poet  not is  another
tr: When He had heard them, the Lord honoured him highly: “There is no other poet in the world like you!

## 38
w: your  poetry  the verses  to understand  whose  power
w: you  well  know  the meaning,  or  Sarasvatī
tr: “Who has the power to understand your verses? You alone know their meaning well — you, or Sarasvatī.

## 39
w: of one  verse  the meaning  if  you make  with your own mouth
w: hearing  all  people  then  obtain  with great joy
tr: “If you would explain the meaning of one verse yourself, everyone here would be delighted to hear it.”

## 40
w: then  the *digvijayī*  for explanation  the verse  asked
w: of the hundred verses  one  verse  the Lord  indeed  recited
tr: The *digvijayī* asked which verse he should explain, and the Lord recited one of the hundred.

## 41
w: the greatness  of the Gaṅgā  ever  this  shines  beyond compare
w: since  she  of Śrī Viṣṇu  by birth from the lotus feet  blessed
w: a second  Śrī Lakṣmī  like  by gods and men  worshipped  whose feet
w: of Bhavānī’s  husband  who  on the head  reigns  wondrous in qualities
tr: “This greatness of the Gaṅgā ever shines beyond compare, for she is blessed by her birth from the lotus feet of Śrī Viṣṇu. She is like a second Śrī Lakṣmī, whose feet gods and men worship; and, endowed with wondrous qualities, she reigns upon the head of Bhavānī’s husband.”
fn: Heading in the edition: “Keśava Kāśmīrī’s verse on the greatness of the Gaṅgā.” *Anubhāṣya*: *subhagā* — possessed of a splendid fortune, since she was born from the Lord’s lotus feet; *dvitīya-śrī-lakṣmīr iva* — like a second Kamalā (Lakṣmī), full of beauty; *bhavānī-bhartuḥ* — of the husband of Bhavānī, that is, of Śiva; therefore the Gaṅgā is endowed with wondrous qualities.

## 42
w: of this  verse  the meaning  make —  the Lord  when  said
w: astonished  becoming  the *digvijayī*  the Lord  asked
tr: When the Lord said, “Explain this verse,” the *digvijayī* asked Him in astonishment:

## 43
w: a storm wind  like  I  the verses  recited
w: of them  among  a verse  You  how  by heart  learned
tr: “I recited those verses like a storm wind. How could You learn one of them by heart?”

## 44
w: the Lord  says,  of a god  by the boon  you —  ‘the best of poets’
w: so  of a god  by the boon  someone  becomes —  ‘one who holds all he hears’
tr: The Lord said, “By the boon of a deity you are the best of poets; and by the boon of a deity, too, someone may become a *śruti-dhara*, one who holds all that he hears.”

## 45
w: of the verse  the meaning  made  the brāhmaṇa  obtaining  satisfaction
w: the Lord  says —  tell  of the verse  what  merits and faults
tr: Well pleased, the brāhmaṇa explained the verse. Then the Lord said, “Tell Me, what are the merits and faults of this verse?”

## 46
w: the brāhmaṇa  says,  in the verse  not  of fault  appearance
w: of the ornament of simile  the merit,  some  alliteration
tr: The brāhmaṇa said, “No fault appears in it. Its merits are the ornament of simile and some alliteration.”
fn: *Amṛta-pravāha-bhāṣya*: simile (*upamā*) is a poetic ornament (*alaṅkāra*); alliteration (*anuprāsa*) is the verbal art shown by setting many *bha*s close together in the last line.

## 47
w: the Lord  says —  I shall speak,  if  not  you become  angry
w: tell  your  in this  verse  what  there is  fault
tr: The Lord said, “May I say something, if you will not be angry? Tell Me what faults there are in this verse of yours.

## 48
w: of genius  the words  your  the gods  please
w: well  if examined  are known  merits and faults
tr: “Your words, full of genius, please even the gods; yet if they are examined well, both merits and faults come to light.
fn: *Amṛta-pravāha-bhāṣya*: *pratibhā*, genius, is the power of mind that arranges words in ever new ways; in this verse you have shown it and pleased even the gods, but on careful examination both merits and faults appear.

## 49
w: therefore  well  doing  the verse  make  examination
w: the poet  says —  what  You recited  that  the essence of the Veda
tr: “So let us examine the verse carefully.” The poet replied, “The verse You have recited is the very essence of the Vedas.

## 50
w: a grammarian  You,  not  have studied  poetics
w: You  what  will know  of this  poetry  the essence
tr: “You are only a grammarian; You have not studied poetics. What can You know of the essence of this poetry?”
fn: *Amṛta-pravāha-bhāṣya*: a grammarian is versed only in the elementary science and cannot judge poetics (*alaṅkāra-śāstra*) and other śāstras.

## 51
w: the Lord  says —  therefore  I ask  you
w: examining  the merits and faults  explain  to Me
tr: The Lord said, “That is just why I ask you. Examine its merits and faults and explain them to Me.

## 52
w: not  I have studied  poetics,  I have done  hearing
w: thereby  in this  verse  I see  many  faults and merits
tr: “I have not studied poetics, but I have heard it taught, and so I see many faults and merits in this verse.”

## 53
w: the poet  says —  tell,  let us see,  which  merits and faults
w: the Lord  says —  I tell,  listen,  not  become  angry
tr: “Come, then,” said the poet, “tell me — what merits and faults?” The Lord replied, “I shall tell you; listen, and do not be angry.

## 54
w: five  faults  in this  verse,  five  ornaments
w: in order  I  tell,  listen,  make  judgement
tr: “In this verse there are five faults and five ornaments. I shall name them in order; listen, and judge for yourself.

## 55
w: ‘the predicate ill placed’ —  in two  places  the mark
w: ‘the contradictory sense’,  ‘the broken order’,  ‘the resumed sentence’,  faults  three
tr: “In two places the predicate is badly placed, and there are three more faults: a contradictory sense, a broken order, and a return to a finished sentence.
fn: The names of the faults (*doṣa*) in the original: *avimṛṣṭa-vidheyāṁśa*, “the predicate not duly considered”, that is, what is asserted about the subject is badly placed (vv. 56–61); *viruddha-mati-kṛt*, “that which suggests a contradictory sense” (vv. 62–65); *punar-ātta*, “taken up again”: a return to a sentence already completed by its verb (v. 66); *bhagna-krama*, “broken order” (v. 67).

## 56
w: ‘of the Gaṅgā  the greatness’ —  in the verse  the main  ‘predicate’
w: by the word ‘idam’  the ‘subject’ —  afterwards  is expressed
tr: “In this verse the main predicate is ‘the greatness of the Gaṅgā’, while the subject, expressed by the word *idam*, ‘this’, comes after it.
fn: *Vidheya*, the predicate, is what is asserted about a thing, the new matter for the hearer; *anuvāda*, the subject, is the thing itself, already known. *Amṛta-pravāha-bhāṣya*: in the verse the main predicate is the greatness of the Gaṅgā and the subject is the word *idam*; to write “the greatness of the Gaṅgā” first and *idam* after it is against the rule.

## 57
w: the ‘predicate’  first  having said,  afterwards  you said  the ‘subject’
w: for this  reason  of the verse  the meaning  has become  lost
tr: “You stated the predicate first and the subject afterwards, and so the meaning of the verse has suffered.
fn: *Amṛta-pravāha-bhāṣya*: if the subject is not stated first, the meaning is impaired.

## 58
w: the subject  not having stated  indeed
w: not  the predicate  one should utter
w: not  indeed  without a footing  anything
w: anywhere  stands
tr: ‘One should not state the predicate without first stating the subject; for nothing that has not found its footing can stand anywhere.’ (Ekādaśī-tattva, 13)
fn: Heading in the edition: “Thus a maxim cited in the *Ekādaśī-tattva*.” The same verse is quoted in Ādi 2.74 (there *anuktvā tu*, here *anuktvaiva*, with the same meaning).

## 59
w: ‘a second  Śrī Lakṣmī’ —  here  ‘being second’  the predicate
w: in the compound  subordinate  it became,  the word’s meaning  went  to ruin
tr: “In *dvitīya-śrī-lakṣmī*, ‘a second Śrī Lakṣmī’, the predicate is ‘being second’; but in the compound it has become subordinate, and the meaning of the word is lost.
fn: *Amṛta-pravāha-bhāṣya*: the purpose of these words is to show that the Gaṅgā is equal to Lakṣmī; but “second”, the predicate, was put into a compound (*samāsa*), became secondary, and that meaning perished.

## 60
w: ‘second’  the word —  the predicate,  it  fell  into the compound
w: ‘equality with Lakṣmī’  the meaning  it made  destroyed
tr: “The word *dvitīya* is the predicate, but it has fallen into a compound, and so the point — that the Gaṅgā is equal to Lakṣmī — is destroyed.
