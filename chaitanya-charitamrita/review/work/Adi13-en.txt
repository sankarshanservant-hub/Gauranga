## 19

fn: *Anubhāṣya*: on this full moon Śrī Kṛṣṇa Caitanya — the Lord of Goloka, the original source of all avatāras, in whom Rādhā and Kṛṣṇa are one — descended together with the names of Kṛṣṇa from His own abode, the Gaura-prakoṣṭha, into the earthly Navadvīpa; and the full moon itself is no worldly day but a transcendental goddess of the lunar day (*tithi*), who descended into time to serve Him. The *Amṛta-pravāha-bhāṣya* cites a verse: “In the twenty-eighth age of Vaivasvata Manu, in the year fourteen hundred and seven, on the lovely bank of the Bhāgīrathī, from the great ocean of Śacī’s womb, on a full moon seized by Rāhu, Gaurāṅga appeared.”

fn: *Anubhāṣya*: Vidyāpati, Jayadeva and Caṇḍīdāsa sang of the transcendental rasa of separation, and the Lord, absorbed in the heart of Śrī Rādhā, relished their songs with His two most intimate devotees, Svarūpa Dāmodara and Rāmānanda Rāya. But for those who identify with the body, who look in these songs for worldly poetry, or who, like the *prākṛta-sahajiyās*, claim to follow *rāgānugā* in order to please their senses, any discussion of the pastimes of Rādhā and Kṛṣṇa is strictly forbidden: they would take those transcendental pastimes for worldly love and ruin themselves.

## 75

fn: Heading in the edition: “Viśvarūpa is Mahā-saṅkarṣaṇa of Vaikuṇṭha.” *Amṛta-pravāha-bhāṣya*: Viśvarūpa is the avatāra of Mahā-saṅkarṣaṇa of the spiritual sky. *Anubhāṣya*: Viśvarūpa, the elder brother of Śrī Gaurahari, took sannyāsa before marriage, receiving the name Śaṅkarāraṇya, and in Śaka 1431 passed from view at Pandharpur; He is both the material and the efficient cause of the universe.
