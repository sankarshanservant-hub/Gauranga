## 19

fn: *Anubhāṣya*: on this full moon Śrī Kṛṣṇa Caitanya — the Lord of Goloka, the original source of all avatāras, in whom Rādhā and Kṛṣṇa are one — descended together with the names of Kṛṣṇa from His own abode, the Gaura-prakoṣṭha, into the earthly Navadvīpa; and the full moon itself is no worldly day but a transcendental goddess of the lunar day (*tithi*), who descended into time to serve Him. The *Amṛta-pravāha-bhāṣya* cites a verse: “In the twenty-eighth age of Vaivasvata Manu, in the year fourteen hundred and seven, on the lovely bank of the Bhāgīrathī, from the great ocean of Śacī’s womb, on a full moon seized by Rāhu, Gaurāṅga appeared.”

fn: *Anubhāṣya*: Vidyāpati, Jayadeva and Caṇḍīdāsa sang of the transcendental rasa of separation, and the Lord, absorbed in the heart of Śrī Rādhā, relished their songs with His two most intimate devotees, Svarūpa Dāmodara and Rāmānanda Rāya. But for those who identify with the body, who look in these songs for worldly poetry, or who, like the *prākṛta-sahajiyās*, claim to follow *rāgānugā* in order to please their senses, any discussion of the pastimes of Rādhā and Kṛṣṇa is strictly forbidden: they would take those transcendental pastimes for worldly love and ruin themselves.

## 75

fn: Heading in the edition: “Viśvarūpa is Mahā-saṅkarṣaṇa of Vaikuṇṭha.” *Amṛta-pravāha-bhāṣya*: Viśvarūpa is the avatāra of Mahā-saṅkarṣaṇa of the spiritual sky. *Anubhāṣya*: Viśvarūpa, the elder brother of Śrī Gaurahari, took sannyāsa before marriage, receiving the name Śaṅkarāraṇya, and in Śaka 1431 passed from view at Pandharpur; He is both the material and the efficient cause of the universe. According to the *Gaura-gaṇoddeśa-dīpikā* (58–59), Viśvarūpa is the *vyūha* Saṅkarṣaṇa, while Nityānanda Avadhūta is Baladeva Himself, the Balarāma of Vraja.

## 76

## 77

## 78
w: therefore  the Lord  Him  calls  ‘elder  brother’
w: Kṛṣṇa,  Balarāma  the two  —  Caitanya,  Nitāi
tr: That is why the Lord called Him His elder brother. Kṛṣṇa and Balarāma — these two are Caitanya and Nitāi.
fn: *Amṛta-pravāha-bhāṣya*: Mahā-saṅkarṣaṇa, the material and the efficient cause, pervades the universe through and through, and therefore Mahāprabhu calls Viśvarūpa His elder brother; but the Kṛṣṇa and Balarāma of Kṛṣṇa’s own abode are Caitanya and Nitāi, and so Nityānanda Prabhu is the original Saṅkarṣaṇa, Baladeva Himself.

## 80
w: fourteen hundred  six  in the Śaka year  at the end  in the month of Māgha
w: of Jagannātha  Śacī  into the bodies  of Kṛṣṇa  the entering
tr: At the end of the month of Māgha in Śaka 1406, Kṛṣṇa entered the bodies of Jagannātha and Śacī.
fn: *Anubhāṣya* to 80–86: Jagannātha and Śacī are the Lord’s eternal associates (*nitya-siddha*); their hearts and bodies are made of pure goodness (*śuddha-sattva*) and are not like those of ordinary living beings. Pure goodness is called *vasudeva*, and in it Vāsudeva, who eternally enjoys His spiritual pastimes, becomes manifest. The union of Jagannātha and Śacī and the conception in Śacī’s womb were not like the carnal union and pregnancy of worldly people; even to think so is an offence. According to the *Bhāgavatam* (10.2.16–18, with the comments of Śrīdhara Svāmī and Baladeva Vidyābhūṣaṇa) and the *Laghu-bhāgavatāmṛta*, Kṛṣṇa first became manifest in the heart of Vasudeva, and from his heart in the heart of Devakī; so too Śrī Gaurasundara appeared in the hearts of Miśra and Śacī, wholly absorbed as they were in serving the feet of Govinda (13.79).

## 90

tr: The moon was in Leo, Leo was rising and the planets were exalted; the six divisions and the eight divisions all showed auspicious signs.
fn: *Anubhāṣya*: the *ṣaḍ-varga* are the six divisions of a sign — *kṣetra*, *horā*, *drekkāṇa*, *navāṁśa*, *dvādaśāṁśa* and *triṁśāṁśa*; by them Cakravartī determined the auspicious signs. The *aṣṭa-varga* is reckoned from the positions of the planets, as taught in the *Bṛhaj-jātaka* and other books; by this reckoning too he found Mahāprabhu’s signs auspicious.
