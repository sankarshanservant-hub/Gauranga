tr: I take refuge in Śrī Kṛṣṇa Caitanya, who by the ropes of His mercy cleverly lifted Raghunātha dāsa out of the blind well of a wretched household life, entrusted him to Svarūpa, and made him His intimate associate.
fn: The last line is as in Madhya 19.54 (*śrī-kṛṣṇa-caitanyam amuṁ prapadye*, “I take refuge in this Śrī Kṛṣṇa Caitanya”). *Anubhāṣya*: *ku-gṛha* — a wretched home, full of talk of husbands, wives, sons and the like, a blind well with no way out; *bhaṅgyā* — skilfully; compare Raghunātha dāsa’s *Vilāpa-kusumāñjali*: “I worship Caitanyacandra… by the ropes of His mercy He at once pulled me out of the impassable, waterless great well of endless suffering… and gave me to Śrī Dāmodara.”

Edition heading: at home he practises *yukta-vairāgya* — outwardly like a worldly man, inwardly free of the world, owning nothing and doing everything for Kṛṣṇa.

fn: *Amṛta-pravāha-bhāṣya*: *makrari* — *ijārā*, a permanent lease. *Anubhāṣya*: the Muslim *caudhurī* lost all his income, and seeing this he was enraged to the core.

## 19

fn: A lakh is a hundred thousand. *Anubhāṣya*: of the twenty lakhs collected, Hiraṇya should have kept back a quarter (five lakhs) and paid the king fifteen; instead he paid twelve, so the “Turk”, that is, the Muslim, lost the share due to him and became their enemy.
