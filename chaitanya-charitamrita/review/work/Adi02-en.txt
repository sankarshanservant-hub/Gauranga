# English translation, Ādi-līlā 2 — from the Bengali/Sanskrit, following the «Anubhāṣya» (2026 draft).
# Format: tools/build_rev.py. «w:» — word-for-word for each transliteration line, in order.

## 0
tr: The Auspicious Invocation Indicating the Object: Determining the Truth of Caitanya

## 1
w: Śrī Caitanya  the Lord  I worship
w: a child  even  by whose  mercy
w: may cross  of various  doctrines  crocodiles
w: filled with  of conclusions  the ocean
tr: I worship Śrī Caitanya Mahāprabhu, by whose mercy even an ignorant child can cross the ocean of philosophical conclusions, which teems with the crocodiles of various doctrines.
fn: «Anubhāṣya»: *bālaḥ* — “an inexperienced child”; the crocodiles are the mutually contending doctrines of Aulūkya, Jina, Buddha, Jaimini, Patañjali, Gautama, Kaṇāda, Kapila, Śaṅkara, Dattātreya and others; by the Lord’s mercy even the inexperienced one sets them aside as straw and comes to know the pure feet of Kṛṣṇa.

## 2
w: of Kṛṣṇa  loud glorification  singing  dancing  arts  lotuses  adorned
w: of true  devotees  rows  swans  cakravāka birds  bees  swarms  of pleasure  abode
w: delighting the ears  sweet  sound  may it flow  my  of the tongue  desert  over the expanse
w: O Śrī Caitanya  of mercy  ocean  Your  shining  pastimes  nectar  celestial Gaṅgā
tr: O Śrī Caitanya, ocean of mercy! May the celestial Gaṅgā of the nectar of Your shining pastimes flow over the desert of my tongue. She is adorned with the lotuses of the loud glorification of Kṛṣṇa, of singing and of dancing; she is the place of delight for the rows of true devotees, who are like swans, cakravāka birds and bees; and her sweet murmur gladdens the ear.
fn: «Anubhāṣya»: the swans, cakravākas and bees are pure devotees in different moods (*bhāva*), for whom these pastimes are a place of supreme joy; the murmur of the river is the sweet, indistinct sound of the stream of Śrī Hari’s pastimes, sung by His devotees.

## 3
w: glory  glory  to Śrī Caitanya  glory  to Nityānanda
w: glory to Advaitacandra  glory  of Gaura  the devotees  to the multitude
tr: Glory, glory to Śrī Caitanya! Glory to Nityānanda! Glory to Advaitacandra! Glory to all the devotees of Gaura!

## 4
w: of the third  verse  the meaning  I make  explanation
w: the Object  indicating  in the form of  auspicious invocation
tr: Now I shall explain in detail the meaning of the third verse — the auspicious invocation that indicates the Object, the Supreme Truth (*vastu-nirdeśa*).
fn: Indicating the Object (*vastu-nirdeśa*) is one of the three kinds of auspicious invocation (Ādi 1.22). In the edition the verse is headed: “explanation of the third of the first fourteen verses — indicating the Object”.

## 5
w: which  non-dual  Brahman  in the Upaniṣads  that  also  His  bodily  effulgence
w: who  the Supersoul  the Indweller  the Puruṣa  thus  He  His  portion  manifestation
w: with six  opulences  full  who  here  Bhagavān  He  Himself  this one
w: not  than Caitanya  Kṛṣṇa  in the world  supreme  truth  higher  here
tr: “There is no Truth in the world higher than Śrī Kṛṣṇa Caitanya. That which the Upaniṣads call the non-dual Brahman is the effulgence of His body; the Puruṣa, the Supersoul who dwells within every heart, is His partial manifestation; and Bhagavān, full in all six opulences, is He Himself.”
fn: The same verse as Ādi 1.3. «Anubhāṣya»: Brahman is the radiance of His transcendental body, and the Paramātmā a particular manifestation of His majesty; both are only two aspects of His form, perceived according to the capacity of the seer — not the fullness of the Lord, as Bhagavān is. «Amṛta-pravāha-bhāṣya»: “He whom they call Bhagavān, the shelter and source of Brahman and Paramātmā, is my Lord, Bhagavān Himself.”

## 6
w: Brahman,  Paramātmā,  Bhagavān —  subjects  three
w: of the body  effulgence,  portion,  own form —  three  predicates  marks
tr: Brahman, Paramātmā and Bhagavān are the three *anuvādas* — that which is spoken of; the effulgence of the body, the partial manifestation and His own form are the three *vidheyas* — that which is stated about them.
fn: «Amṛta-pravāha-bhāṣya»: by the rules of rhetoric one first names the *anuvāda*, the known, and then the *vidheya*, the new. That Brahman, Paramātmā and Bhagavān exist is known from the Vedas; that Brahman is the effulgence of Śrī Kṛṣṇa Caitanya’s body, Paramātmā His portion and Bhagavān He Himself, is not yet known — and this is what is stated.

## 7
w: the subject  first,  afterwards  the predicate  placing
w: that  meaning  I tell,  hear  of scripture  the explanation
tr: First the *anuvāda* is stated, and afterwards the *vidheya* is set down. In this way I shall tell the meaning of the verse; hear now the explanation according to scripture.

## 8
w: Himself  Bhagavān  Kṛṣṇa,  of Viṣṇu  the supreme truth
w: full  knowledge  full  bliss  supreme  greatness
tr: Kṛṣṇa is Bhagavān Himself, the supreme truth among all forms of Viṣṇu (*viṣṇu-tattva*). He is fullness of knowledge, fullness of bliss and the highest greatness.
fn: «Amṛta-pravāha-bhāṣya»: “The conclusion of the scriptures is this: the supreme truth of *viṣṇu-tattva* is Bhagavān Himself, Kṛṣṇacandra.”

## 9
w: ‘Nanda’s son’  calling  whom  in the Bhāgavata  they sing
w: that  Kṛṣṇa  has descended  (as) Caitanya  the Lord
tr: He whom the «Bhāgavatam» glorifies as the son of Nanda — that same Kṛṣṇa has descended as Śrī Caitanya Gosāñi.

## 10
w: in particular manifestations  He  bears  three  names
w: Brahman,  Paramātmā  and  Himself  Bhagavān
tr: In His particular manifestations He bears three names: Brahman, Paramātmā and Bhagavān Himself.

## 11
w: they call  that  of truth  the knowers
w: the Truth  which  knowledge  non-dual
w: Brahman  thus  Paramātmā  thus
w: Bhagavān  thus  is named
tr: “The knowers of the Truth call the non-dual knowledge the Truth; it is named Brahman, Paramātmā and Bhagavān.” (Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam, 1.2.11)
fn: «Amṛta-pravāha-bhāṣya»: the first realization of that non-dual knowledge is Brahman, the second Paramātmā, the third Bhagavān.

## 12
w: His  of the body  pure  of rays  the orb
w: the Upaniṣads  call  that  Brahman  most pure
tr: That which the Upaniṣads call the most pure Brahman is the pure radiance of the rays of His body.

## 13
w: with fleshly  eyes  one sees  as  the sun  without features
w: on the path of knowledge  to perceive  cannot  His  particular features
tr: As the sun appears to fleshly eyes as mere brilliance without features, so on the path of knowledge one cannot perceive His particular features.

## 14
w: whose  effulgence  of the all-mighty  of universes  of millions
w: in millions  unlimited  earths and the like  opulences  distinct from
w: that  Brahman  undivided  infinite  all-complete
w: Govinda  the original  Person  Him  I  worship
tr: “I worship Govinda, the original Person. The effulgence of Him, the all-mighty, is Brahman — undivided, infinite and complete, distinct from the opulences of countless universes, their earths and all else.” (Brahma-saṁhitā, 5.40)
fn: «Amṛta-pravāha-bhāṣya»: “… the Brahman distinct from the unlimited opulences — earths and the rest — in millions upon millions of universes, undivided, infinite and complete, has arisen from the effulgence of that original Person, Govinda.”

## 15
w: millions  upon millions  in the universes  which  of Brahman  the glory
w: that  Brahman  of Govinda  is  of the body  the radiance
tr: That Brahman whose glory is spread through millions upon millions of universes is the radiance of Govinda’s body.

## 16
w: that  Govinda  I worship  I,  He  my  Lord
w: His  by the grace  to me  is  of creation  the power
tr: I worship that Govinda; He is my Lord. By His grace I (Brahmā) have the power to create.

## 17
w: the sages  the wind  as clothing
w: ascetics  upward  (who keep their seed)
w: called Brahman  the abode  they  go to
w: peaceful  renunciants  pure
tr: “Sages clothed by the wind, ascetics who keep their seed drawn upward, peaceful, pure renunciants — they go to the abode called Brahman.” (Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam, 11.6.47)
fn: «Anubhāṣya»: with this verse it is shown that attaining Kṛṣṇa’s feet is easy, whereas renunciants who labour hard attain, as the fruit of their practice, only the realm of Brahman; *vāta-vasanāḥ* — naked; *śramaṇāḥ* — mendicants who emaciate the body; *brahmākhyam* — featureless.

## 18
w: the Supersoul  the Indweller  whom  in the yoga  scriptures  they speak of
w: He  of Govinda  portion  manifestation  indeed  is
tr: The Supersoul, the Indweller, of whom the scriptures of yoga speak, is a partial manifestation of Govinda.
fn: «Anubhāṣya»: the Puruṣa who dwells within is the Paramātmā, “only a manifestation of a portion (*aṁśa-vibhūti*) of Govinda”; the three Puruṣa-avatāras govern the material world, while Bhagavān Himself, abiding in the fourth, *turīya*, is attached to none of its affairs.

## 19
w: in countless  crystals  as  one  sun  is reflected
w: so  in living beings  of Govinda  the portion  is manifest
tr: As one sun is reflected in countless crystals, so Govinda’s portion, the Supersoul, is manifest in every living being.
fn: «Anubhāṣya»: the one Śrī Govinda, ever manifest in Goloka-Vṛndāvana, appears in the hearts of countless souls as the Paramātmā, the Person whom the soul is to serve.

## 20
w: but  or  with much  this
w: what (use)  by knowing  for you  Arjuna
w: pervading  I  this  entire
w: by one portion  I stand  the universe
tr: “But what need have you, Arjuna, of all this detailed knowledge? By one portion of Myself I pervade and support this entire universe.” (Bhagavad-gītā, 10.42)
fn: «Amṛta-pravāha-bhāṣya»: “by one portion, as the Paramātmā, I have entered and abide in the whole world.”

## 21
w: Him  this  I  the unborn  of the embodied
w: in heart  in heart  abiding  created by Himself
w: to each  eye  as if  in many ways  the sun  one
w: have attained  I  having cast off  of difference  the illusion
tr: (Grandsire Bhīṣma said:) “O Śrī Kṛṣṇa! Having cast off the illusion of ‘difference’ between all Your forms, I have attained You, the Unborn, who abide in the heart of each of the embodied beings You have created — like the one sun, which each eye sees in its own way.” (Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam, 1.9.42)
fn: «Anubhāṣya»: *bheda-mohaḥ* — the illusion that the Lord’s forms (*prakāśa*, *vilāsa*) differ in name, form, qualities and pastimes; *ātma-kalpitānām* — “created by Himself”. Reference to the «Bhāgavatam» as in the edition.

## 22
w: that  indeed  Govinda  directly  Caitanya  the Lord
w: the souls  in delivering  so  merciful  another  there is not
tr: That very Govinda is Śrī Caitanya Gosāñi Himself. There is none so merciful in delivering the souls.
fn: «Amṛta-pravāha-bhāṣya»: by the word *sākṣāt* (“directly, Himself”) the author establishes that Śrī Kṛṣṇa Caitanya is Govinda Himself, not His *prakāśa* or *vilāsa*.

## 23
w: in the spiritual  sky  dwells  Nārāyaṇa  named
w: with six  opulences  full  of Lakṣmī  the beloved  Bhagavān
tr: In the spiritual sky dwells the Lord called Nārāyaṇa — Bhagavān, full in the six opulences, the beloved of Lakṣmī.

## 24
w: the Vedas,  the Bhāgavata,  the Upaniṣads,  the Āgamas
w: ‘the complete  Truth’  whom  they call,  there is not  to whom  an equal
tr: He whom the Vedas, the «Bhāgavatam», the Upaniṣads and the Āgamas call “the complete Truth”, and who has no equal —

## 25
w: through bhakti-yoga  the devotees  attain  whose  sight
w: the sun  as  with its form  see  the gods
tr: Him the devotees behold through bhakti-yoga, as the gods behold the sun in its own form.
fn: «Amṛta-pravāha-bhāṣya»: the eternal form of the Lord cannot be seen by material senses or by the efforts of knowledge; through bhakti, through love, only the devotees become fit to see it — as the gods, whose divine eyes pierce the net of the sun’s rays, see the sun’s own form.
