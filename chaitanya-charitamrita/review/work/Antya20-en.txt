## 12

It swells the ocean of bliss, gives at every step a taste of the full nectar, and bathes and cools the whole self. May the supreme chanting of Śrī Kṛṣṇa’s name be ever victorious!” (Śikṣāṣṭaka, 1)
fn: *Anubhāṣya*: the saṅkīrtana of Śrī Kṛṣṇa cleanses the mirror of the mind, puts out the great forest fire of worldly existence, spreads moonlight for the white lilies of the highest good, keeps alive the bride of knowledge, swells the ocean of bliss — prema — gives at every moment a taste of the full nectar and bathes the whole being; it is one without a second and stands above all. Heading in the edition: the *Śrī Śikṣāṣṭaka*, which came from the Lord’s holy mouth — the essence of the *Bhāgavatam*; the verse is cited in the *Padyāvalī* (10).

fn: *Amṛta-pravāha-bhāṣya*: Your name alone brings every good to the jīvas, and so You have revealed many names — “Kṛṣṇa”, “Govinda” and others; yet my misfortune — offences to the name (nāmāparādha) — does not let attachment arise in me even for this easily attained name. Heading in the edition: why the practice of the name is so easy — Kṛṣṇa’s great generosity; and why a jīva caught in offences cannot utter the pure name; the verse is cited in the *Padyāvalī* (19).

## 57
w: of the leprous  brāhmaṇa  the wife  of chaste wives  the crest jewel
w: her husband  for the sake of  she did  to a prostitute  service
w: she stopped  the sun’s  course  she revived  dead  husband
w: pleased  made  the chief  three gods
tr: The wife of a leprous *brāhmaṇa*, the crest jewel of chaste wives, served a prostitute for her husband’s sake. She stopped the course of the sun, brought her dead husband back to life, and pleased the three principal deities.
fn: *Anubhāṣya*: the story is told in the *Āditya*, *Mārkaṇḍeya* (15.29) and *Padma Purāṇas*. The leprous husband of the chaste wife unknowingly disturbed the samādhi of the sage Māṇḍavya and was cursed to die at sunrise; she vowed not to let the sun rise, and Brahmā, Viṣṇu and Śiva, pleased with her fidelity, healed her husband and gave him new life. The point: only such selfless fidelity — the desire for happiness in service alone — befits pure devotees. *Amṛta-pravāha-bhāṣya*: firm fidelity to Kṛṣṇa is the highest dharma of the jīva, born of śṛṅgāra-rasa.

tr: The fullness of joy in serving the beloved is sweeter even than union. The goddess Lakṣmī is witness to this: though she dwells on Nārāyaṇa’s heart, she longs to serve His feet, and she serves Him thinking of herself as His maidservant.”

w: mind and body  holding still  not  possible
tr: Śrī Gaura Rāya relished these words of Rādhā, which bear the marks of pure love. His mind grew unsteady with emotion, sāttvika symptoms pervaded His body, and He could no longer hold His mind and body still.
fn: Heading in the edition: the Lord, filled with the feeling of Śrī Rādhā, relishes pure love (kevala-prema).

## 62
w: for the Lord of Vraja  pure  love  like  of the Jambū  river  gold
w: of one’s own happiness  in which  there is no  trace
w: His own love  to make known  to the world  the Lord  made  this  verse
w: songs (pada)  He made  of the meaning  as the exposition
tr: In pure love for the Lord of Vraja there is no trace of desire for one’s own happiness: it is like gold from the river Jambū. To make this love known to the world, the Lord composed this verse and set out its meaning at length in songs.
fn: The Gauḍīya Maṭha edition reads *vrajeśvara-śuddha-prema*, “pure love for the Lord of Vraja,” which is translated here; it notes the reading of another manuscript, *vrajera viśuddha prema*, “the pure love of Vraja”; the *Anubhāṣya* also cites the reading *se prema*, “that love.” In the edition — *jāmbunada* (short *u*).
fn: Heading in the edition: in the desire to please Kṛṣṇa’s senses there is no desire to please one’s own; the supremely generous Gaura, through the *Śikṣāṣṭaka*, gives the jīvas the essence of the *Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam* — sambandha, abhidheya and prayojana.

fn: Heading in the edition: for all His matchless depth, the Lord loses His composure in mahābhāva — the divine madness born of separation — as the ocean heaves at the rising of the full moon.

## 72

tr: His actions and His words of divine madness have no limit; to describe them all would make the book enormous.
fn: Heading in the edition: fearing to make the book too long, the author stops describing the Lord’s manifestations of love.

## 73

fn: Heading in the edition: what is described in detail in the *Caitanya-bhāgavata* is described briefly in this book, and what is described briefly there is described here in detail.

## 74

fn: Heading in the edition: though the author is guided by the will of Śrī Caitanya Himself, he speaks in humility.

## 78

## 79

fn: *Anubhāṣya*: compare *Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam* 1.18.23 — as birds fly in the sky as far as their strength allows, so the wise describe the ways of Viṣṇu as far as they are able. Heading in the edition: the ocean of Gaura’s pastimes is beyond this world and beyond the senses (adhokṣaja); a conditioned soul cannot touch it, and the author, thinking himself a jīva, in deep humility only tries to touch a drop of it.

fn: *Anubhāṣya*: the later pure ācāryas who described Gaura’s pastimes after him are, as his non-different parts, also called “Vyāsa” (cf. 86–87). Heading in the edition: the greatness of Ṭhākura Vṛndāvana, and Gaura’s pastimes.

## 87

fn: *Anubhāṣya*: on the variant reading *āge vyāsa karibena varṇane*, “Vyāsa will describe them in the future”, these and similar words of the *Caitanya-bhāgavata* refer to the later pure Vaiṣṇava ācāryas, headed by Kavirāja Gosvāmī, who described Gaura’s pastimes; following the “original Vyāsa” Vṛndāvana dāsa Ṭhākura, such ācāryas are also called “Vyāsa” (on 82).
