Seeing the folly of his disciple and his disrespect for the guru, Mādhavendra Purī rejected him and drove him away.

## 24

tr: Śrī Mādhavendrapāda thus disregarded him, and through that offence worldly desires (*vāsanā*) were born in Rāmacandra Purī.
fn: Heading in the edition: from the offence of disrespect for the guru arises the desire for sense enjoyment, that is, worldly desires. The *Anubhāṣya* quotes the *Bhagavat-pariśiṣṭa*: even those liberated in this life fall back into worldly desires if they offend the Lord. The *Amṛta-pravāha-bhāṣya* explains *vāsanā* otherwise — as the desire for dry knowledge, from which blasphemy of the devotees is born.

## 25
w: dry  Brahman  knower  there is not  with Kṛṣṇa  ‘relationship’
w: of all  people  fault-finding  makes  in fault-finding  stubbornly fixed
tr: A dry knower of Brahman, he had no relationship with Kṛṣṇa. He found fault with everyone and stubbornly persisted in it.
fn: *Anubhāṣya*: *nirbandha* — a stubborn attachment to blaming others. Impersonalists, weak in the knowledge of one’s relationship with the Lord (*sambandha-jñāna*), cannot accept a relationship with Kṛṣṇa and blame the devotees and their service; a soul falls into such ignorance when he offends the feet of the guru or of a devotee.

fn: Śrī Īśvara Purī is the Guru of Mahāprabhu. *Anubhāṣya*: *śrīpāda-sevana* — service to Śrī Mādhavendra Purīpāda. Heading in the edition: the undivided devotion of Śrīla Īśvara Purī to his guru.

fn: Heading in the edition (to 69): the devotees headed by Paramānanda Purī ask the Lord to take the proper measure of food and censure before Him the nature and conduct of Rāmacandra Purī.

## 71
w: of the Purī  the nature  as much as one likes  food  having made eat
w: who  not  eats  him  he feeds  effort  making
tr: It is the Purī’s nature to make people eat as much as they like; and if someone will not eat, he takes great pains to feed him.

## 72
w: having fed  then  him  makes  fault-finding
w: so much  food  you eat  your  how much  is there  wealth
tr: Having fed him, he then finds fault with him: ‘You eat so much food — how much wealth do you have?

## 73
w: a sannyāsī  so much  feeding  you make  of dharma  ruin
w: therefore  I know  your  any  there is not  ‘semblance’
tr: By feeding a sannyāsī so much, you ruin his dharma! Now I see that you have not even a semblance of piety.’
fn: *Bhāsa* — “a semblance, a shadow” (*Amṛta-pravāha-bhāṣya* to 14), as in 14: there is not even a semblance of dharma in you.

## 74
w: who  how  in behaviour  who  how  eats
w: this  inquiry  he  makes  always
tr: He is always inquiring into how each person behaves and how each one eats.

## 75
w: in the scriptures  which  two  duties  have made  rejection
w: that  activity  constantly  his  doing
tr: The two things that the scriptures forbid are what he does constantly.

## 76
w: of others  the nature  and actions
w: not  one should praise  not  one should blame
w: the universe  of one nature  seeing
w: with prakṛti  with the puruṣa  and
tr: ‘One should neither praise nor blame the nature and actions of others, but see the universe as one, a union of prakṛti and the puruṣa.’ (Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam, 11.28.1)
fn: *Ekātmakam* — “one in nature” (*Amṛta-pravāha-bhāṣya*). *Anubhāṣya*: thus Śrī Bhagavān describes to Uddhava the conduct of a pure jñānī; the verse speaks of praise and blame of the nature and acts of others meant to harm them.

## 77
w: of them  among  the earlier injunction  ‘praise’  giving up
w: the later injunction  ‘blame’  he does  ‘stronger’  knowing it
tr: Of the two, he gives up the earlier injunction, praise, and practises the later one, blame, knowing that the later one is ‘stronger’.
fn: *Anubhāṣya*: in verse 76 the earlier injunction is “do not praise”, the later “do not blame”; the later is stronger, so praising people is no great sin, but blaming them must be avoided at all costs. Rāmacandra kept the earlier and not the later; the verse may be read as mockery.

## 78
w: of the earlier  and later  between  the later injunction  stronger
tr: ‘Between an earlier and a later injunction, the later injunction is stronger.’ (a maxim of Nyāya)
fn: The source follows the heading in the edition: “a maxim of Nyāya” (*nyāya-vacana*).

## 79
w: where  virtues  a hundred  are  those  not  makes  acceptance
w: virtues  among  by deceit  makes  of faults  imputation
tr: Where there are a hundred virtues he accepts none of them; instead he deceitfully imputes faults to those very virtues.

## 80
w: his  nature  this  to tell  not  is fitting
w: still  I tell  something  heart-felt  pain  receiving
tr: It is not fitting even to speak of his nature. Still I tell You something of it, for it pains us to the heart.
fn: The Gauḍīya Maṭha edition reads *iṅhā kahite*, “to speak of this” (another reading: *iṅhā karite*, “to act so”). *Anubhāṣya*: *pāya* — “having felt” (the heart’s pain).

## 81
w: his  at the word  why  food  renunciation  You make
w: as before  invitations  accept  of all  the request  heed
tr: Why do You give up food at his word? Accept invitations as before and heed the request of us all.”

## 82

tr: The Lord said, “Why are you all angry with the Purī? He speaks the natural dharma of a sannyāsī. What fault is there in him?

## 83
w: a renunciant  being  of the tongue  greed  very  improper
w: a renunciant’s  dharma  life  to maintain  food  only  he eats
tr: For a renunciant to be greedy of the tongue is very improper. A renunciant’s dharma is to eat only as much food as keeps him alive.”
fn: Heading in the edition (to 82): the Lord, guru of the world and teacher of the people, sets down the rules of the renunciant’s dharma; though He is Īśvara Himself, He regards Himself as a jīva bound by the rules of renunciation and teaches detachment.

## 84

## 89

## 90

w: where  how  fitting  there  He makes  conduct
tr: The Lord descended to give happiness to His devotees, and He acted as was fitting in each place and situation.
fn: Heading in the edition: the purpose of the Lord’s descent and the way He acts.

## 91

w: sometimes  independent  makes  ‘divine majesty’  manifestation
tr: Sometimes He followed worldly ways, like an ordinary person; sometimes, being independent, He displayed His divine majesty (*aiśvarya*).
fn: Heading in the edition: sometimes the Lord behaves like a worldly jīva and so deceives, and sometimes, as the Supreme Lord, He bestows full mercy.

## 92

w: sometimes  him  not  regards  sees  a straw  like
tr: Sometimes He was almost like a servant of Rāmacandra Purī; sometimes He paid him no regard and looked on him as a straw.
fn: Heading in the edition: sometimes He shows Rāmacandra Purī worldly honour, and sometimes He holds him of no account.

## 93

tr: The Lord’s divine conduct is beyond the reach of the intellect. Whatever He does, at whatever time, is wholly enchanting.
fn: Heading in the edition: all that the inconceivable Īśvara does is eternal, beneficial and beautiful.
