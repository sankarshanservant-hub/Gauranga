fn: The same verse as Ādi 1.36; translation as there. The edition (p. 850) prints *dāsa raghunātha* without a hyphen.

## 5
w: glory  to the merciful (Pair)  of the lame
w: of me  of dull mind  the refuge
w: whose lotus feet are my all
w: Rādhā  and Madana-mohana
tr: All glory to the merciful Śrī Śrī Rādhā and Madana-mohana! I am lame and dull of mind, and They are my only refuge; Their lotus feet are all I have.
fn: The same verse as Ādi 1.15 and Madhya 1.3; translation as there. *Anubhāṣya* (to Ādi 1.15): *manda-mati* — “a mind absorbed in the world, feeble, unable to follow other paths such as karma or jñāna”; *suratau* — “merciful”, or “deeply attached to each other”.

## 6
w: shining  in Vṛndāvana forest  wish-fulfilling  tree  beneath
w: in a beautiful  jewel temple  on a lion-throne  seated
w: the beautiful  Rādhā  Śrīla  Govinda  the Deities
w: by Their dearest friends  being served  I remember
tr: I remember Śrīmatī Rādhā and Śrī Govindadeva, seated on a throne in a beautiful temple of jewels beneath a wish-fulfilling tree in the radiant forest of Vṛndāvana, served by Their dearest companions.
fn: The same verse as Ādi 1.16 and Madhya 1.4, except the third line: here *śrīmad-rādhā* (“the beautiful Rādhā”, that is, Śrīmatī Rādhā), there *śrī śrī rādhā*; translation as there, with this difference. *Anubhāṣya* (to Ādi 1.16): *preṣṭhālī* — Lalitā and the other *priya-narma-sakhīs*, attended by Śrī Rūpa-mañjarī and others.

## 7

tr: May the beautiful Śrī Gopīnātha, who begins the sweet rāsa dance, be our good fortune. Standing on the bank by Vaṁśīvaṭa, He draws the gopīs to Himself with the sounds of His flute.
fn: The same verse as Ādi 1.17 and Madhya 1.5; translation as there. *Anubhāṣya* (to Ādi 1.17): the flute “loosens every desire in the gopīs other than for Kṛṣṇa and draws them from their homes with the rope of prema”; *śriye* — “for the wealth of prema”.

fn: The same verse as Ādi 1.18; translation as there.

## 78
w: who  stole my heart in my girlhood  He  the same  indeed  my beloved  those  same  nights of Caitra

tr: “He who stole My heart in My girlhood is now My lord. These are the same moonlit nights of Caitra, and the same full breezes blow from the kadamba groves, fragrant with blossoming *mālatī*. I too am the same. Yet My heart yearns for Our play of love there, beneath the *vetasī* trees on the bank of the Revā.” (Padyāvalī, 382)
fn: The same verse as Madhya 1.58; translation as there. Reference as in the edition heading (p. 853): *Kāvya-prakāśa* 1.4, *Sāhitya-darpaṇa* 1.10, *Padyāvalī* 382; in Madhya 1.58 — 386, after the numbering of other editions. *Amṛta-pravāha-bhāṣya* (to Madhya 1.59): it was composed about a worldly hero and heroine; why Mahāprabhu recited it with such love, no one but Svarūpa Dāmodara knew.
