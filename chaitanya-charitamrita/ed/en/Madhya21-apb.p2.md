## 100

That form of Śrī Kṛṣṇa was accepted in order to display the strength of His own *cit-śakti* (spiritual potency), and it is suited to pastimes in the world of mortals. It astonishes even Kṛṣṇa Himself. It is the highest abode (the culmination) of all good fortune and prosperity, and it can adorn every ornament.

## 103

Śrī Kṛṣṇa's form is the transformation (*pariṇāma*) of *viśuddha-sattva* (pure goodness), which abides in the *sandhinī* of His Yogamāyā, called the *cit-śakti*.

## 104

Beauty and the other qualities are the supreme good fortune of the spiritual truth (*cit-tattva*), and they abide eternally in this very form of Kṛṣṇa.

## 110

“Sweetness is the essence of *bhagavattā*.” Complete wealth, complete strength, complete fame, complete beauty, complete knowledge and complete renunciation — these six qualities are called *bhagavattā* (the nature of Bhagavān). Among them complete beauty (*śrī*) is called “sweetness” (*mādhurya*). It is the essence of the sixfold *bhagavattā*; another name for it is “sweetness.” In the form of Śrī Kṛṣṇa, *bhagavattā* is predominated by sweetness, and in the forms of Nārāyaṇa and others, by majesty.

## 113

Like the waves of the great ocean of eternal youth, the essence of loveliness (*lāvaṇya*) is seen in Kṛṣṇa's body. In that ocean the awakening of feelings (*bhāva*) is the whirlpool, and the sound of the flute is the whirlwind. In such a place, once a woman's heart falls like a blade of grass, it can no longer rise.

## 117

This sweetness of Kṛṣṇa is *ananya-siddha*, that is, perfect by itself (*svayaṁ-siddha*): no other qualities bring it about. In His other manifestations — in the forms of Nārāyaṇa and others — this form of Kṛṣṇa manifests, according to the work each manifestation is to perform, the qualities that suit it: majesty, power and the rest.

## 120

The glorious existence (*vaibhava-sattā*) of Nārāyaṇa and the other forms — know it as *bhagavattā* bestowed by Kṛṣṇa.

## 121

The qualities that shine in Nārāyaṇa — beauty (*śrī*), modesty, compassion, fame, steadiness and a skilful intelligence — are all established in Him by Kṛṣṇa. But good character, gentleness and generosity are seen in no other manifestation except Kṛṣṇa.

## 123

The moon of His (Kṛṣṇa's) face, the ears adorned with *makara*-shaped earrings, the shining cheeks, the beauty, the playful smile — all this is a perpetual festival. Men and women drank it with their eyes and were filled with supreme joy, and they grew angry at the blinking of their eyes, which hindered their seeing.

## 125

The Kāma-gāyatrī mantra is Kṛṣṇa's *svarūpa*. Counting the *kāma-bīja* as half a syllable, it has twenty-four and a half syllables.

## 126

*Dvija-rāja-rāja* means the king of the moons. The moon of Kṛṣṇa's face, having become king, sits on the throne of Kṛṣṇa's body and, together with the society of moons (His limbs and their parts), rules the kingdom of sweetness. Which moon is where is told next.

## 127

*Aṣṭamī-indu* means the half-moon.

## 131

*Vipula* means wide. The two wide eyes, reddish in hue, are the ministers of the king — Kṛṣṇa's face; they destroy the intoxication of Madana.

## 132

*Dui āṅkhi ki karibe pāne* (“how much can two eyes drink?”): how can the viewer's two eyes drink that ocean of nectar?

## 135

*E-tine lāgila mana* (“the mind clung to these three”): the sweetness of Kṛṣṇa's body is like an ocean, His most sweet face is like the moon risen from that ocean, and His exceedingly sweet smile is like the rays of that moon. To these three the mind clung.

## 136

The body of this Kṛṣṇa is sweet; His face is sweeter still; and His gentle smile, fragrant with honey, is sweeter yet. Oh! Everything about Him is sweet.

## 137

When all three disorders (*doṣas*) arise in the bodily constituents (*dhātus*), it is called *sannipāta*. My mind has been struck by three blows — the sweetness of Kṛṣṇa's body, the sweetness of Kṛṣṇa's face and the sweetness of Kṛṣṇa's smile — and there is no doubt that it suffers from the disease of *sannipāta*. Thirsting, it runs toward that ocean of the *rasa* of beauty. The physician for ordinary *sannipāta* does not let the patient drink even a drop of water. In the same way, my disease has no physician but Kṛṣṇa — yet He does not let me drink even a drop from the ocean of the nectar of His beauty. That is the sorrow (the ill fate)!

## 143

*Nīvi* is the cord with which the skirt (*ghāgrā*) is tied at the waist.

## 144

*Kānera bhitara vāsā kare* (“it makes its home within the ears”): “We gopīs — the sound of the flute has made its home within our ears,” that is, it seems always to be ringing in our ears.

## 145

In this ecstasy of love, while speaking of the truth of Kṛṣṇa, Mahāprabhu lost external awareness and brought in a composition on *rasa* (*rasa-sandarbha*), but this is not the place to describe it. Therefore He says: “I meant to speak of one thing, and I am speaking of another. Kṛṣṇa, in His mercy, bewildered My mind and let you hear of His own majesty and sweetness.”

*Thus ends the twenty-first chapter of the “Amṛta-pravāha-bhāṣya.”*
