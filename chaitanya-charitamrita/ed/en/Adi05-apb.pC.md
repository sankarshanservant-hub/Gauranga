## 124

*Śeṣatā* means ultimate servitude (*dāsya*).

## 128

One who does not know how an avatāra differs from the source of the avatāras once equated Kṛṣṇa with Vāmana and the other avatāras. In the same way, a person who sees no difference calls Nityānanda “Ananta” and so on. But since devotees have said so, it is not false: in the highest Truth everything is possible.[^128-1]

## 133

Therefore Śrī Kṛṣṇa Caitanya, the highest Truth, showed everyone the pastimes of Varāha, Nṛsiṁha, and the other avatāras.

## 138

Sometimes, like ordinary people, the two brothers become bulls and butt each other, bellowing. And sometimes they imitate swans, peacocks, and other creatures and make their sounds.

## 139

Sometimes, tired from play, Kṛṣṇa Himself lies down with His head on the lap of the cowherd boys, and He has Baladeva lie down and massages His feet.

## 140

“Who is this *māyā*? Where has she come from? Is she divine, human, or demoniac? No *māyā* can bewilder me except the *māyā* of my Lord Kṛṣṇa.”

## 141

The dust of His feet is the holy place of all holy places. The guardians of the worlds carry it on their heads. Brahmā, Śiva, I, Baladeva, and Lakṣmī — some of us are His parts, others parts of parts — forever carry the dust of His feet. What does an ordinary royal throne mean to Him?

## 155

Dividing Himself into parts (*kalās*), Bhagavān manifested various avatāras in the world in the forms of Rāma and others. But I worship Govinda, the original Person: this Supreme Person Himself appears in the form of Kṛṣṇa.

---

[^128-1]: The verse translation reads differently: the knowledge that the avatāra and the source of the avatāras are nondifferent is ascribed to those who know the truth; Bhaktivinoda calls “one who sees no difference” the person who confuses them.
