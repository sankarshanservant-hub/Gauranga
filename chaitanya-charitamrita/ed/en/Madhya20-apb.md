# Amṛta-pravāha-bhāṣya on Madhya-līlā 20

## 0

**Summary.** Sanātana Gosvāmī was held in prison in Gauḍa when Rūpa Gosvāmī wrote to him: “Mahāprabhu has gone to Mathurā.” With sweet words and a gift of seven thousand coins Sanātana won over the jailer, crossed the Gaṅgā and fled. His companion Īśāna carried eight gold coins, and the *bhaumika* (landholder) of the Pātaḍā hills received Sanātana as a guest, hoping to kill them and take the coins. By questioning Īśāna, Sanātana learned that he had gold coins with him. Knowing those coins to be a source of calamity (*anartha*), he gave them to the *bhūñā* and crossed the hill country. Once over the hills, he sent Īśāna back home. When Sanātana reached Hājipura, his sister's husband Śrīkānta, a royal official, saw him, heard the whole story and took him across the Gaṅgā. Walking on and on, Sanātana came to Kāśī and arrived at Candraśekhara's door. Mahāprabhu called him in and, showing him mercy, told him to change his dress and shave (*bhadra*). When Sanātana came back shaven, he put on an old cloth given by Tapana Miśra, making of it a *kaupīna* and an outer garment. He exchanged the woollen Bhoṭa blanket he carried for a torn quilt (*kāṅthā*) from the bank of the Gaṅgā, and so gave the Lord joy. Staying there, Sanātana asked Mahāprabhu about the truth. First the Lord explained to him the “true nature (*svarūpa*) of the *jīva*” and “Kṛṣṇa's potency.” Then He taught him knowledge of *sambandha* (relationship with the Lord) and explained devotion, which is the *abhidheya* (the means). In considering Kṛṣṇa's *svarūpa*, He explained Brahman, Paramātmā and Bhagavān; He spoke of the *svayaṁ-rūpa*, the *tad-ekātma-rūpa* and the *āveśa*, and within them, in the order of *vaibhava-* and *prābhava-vilāsa* and the rest, of the different forms of Bhagavān. After that He explained the manifestation of *māyā* (*māyā-vaibhava*) of the *puruṣa-avatāras*, the *manvantara-avatāras*, the *guṇa-avatāras*, the *śaktyāveśa-avatāras*, the pastimes that differ by age — childhood (*bālya*) and boyhood (*paugaṇḍa*) — and the eternity of the pastimes of youth (*kaiśora*).

## 1

I bow down to Śrī Caitanya Mahāprabhu, who possesses unlimited and wondrous opulence: by His mercy even a lowly person can become a propagator of the scriptures of devotion.

## 3

*Patrī* (letter). The commentator on the book *Udbhaṭa-candrikā* writes that Śrī Rūpa wrote the following *śloka* in Bākalā and sent it to Sanātana in the prison of Gauḍa. Since the *śloka* hints that Mahāprabhu had gone to Mathurā, it can be trusted as Rūpa Gosvāmī's letter: “Where now is Mathurā, the city of the Lord of the Yadus? Where is Uttara-Kośalā of the Lord of the Raghus? Reflecting on this, make your mind steady and know for certain that this world is not lasting.”

## 5

*Jindā-pīr* — a living *pīr* (saint).

## 12

*Dāḍukā* — fetters.

## 37

Hājipura lies opposite Patna, across the river, where the river Gaṅgā meets the river Gaṇḍaka.

## 59

I consider even a *śva-paca* (dog-eater) whose mind, words, endeavours, wealth and life are offered to Kṛṣṇa to be better than a *brāhmaṇa* who has twelve qualities but is averse to Kṛṣṇa's lotus feet. For he — the devotee born in a family of *śva-pacas* — purifies his own family, whereas the *brāhmaṇa* swollen with pride cannot do so.

## 61

O Vaiṣṇava, the fruit of the eyes is to see a person like you; the fruit of the body is to touch the body of a person like you; the fruit of the tongue is to glorify the qualities of a person like you. For in this world *bhāgavatas* are very rare.

## 70

*Bhadra karāñā* — having had him shaved: that is, having had his dervish beard and hair shaved off and making him dressed as a true Vaiṣṇava.

## 97

Bhagavān mercifully instructed Sanātana in the truth — in the sweetness (*mādhurya*) of Śrī Kṛṣṇa's own form, in the majesty (*aiśvarya*) of that form, and in the shelter of *bhakti-rasa*.

## 102–103

Sanātana asked: “Who am I? Why do the three kinds of misery — *ādhyātmika*, *ādhibhautika* and *ādhidaivika* — torment me? And how can I attain my good? I do not know how to ask about the truth of *sādhya* and *sādhana* (the goal and the means). Please be merciful and tell me what I need to know.”

## 106

Those who are firmly resolved to awaken the true *dharma* within themselves soon attain every goal they desire.

## 108–109

In answer to the question “Who am I?” Mahāprabhu says: “You are a *jīva*. Are you this body born of matter? No. Or are you, perhaps, the subtle body (*liṅga-śarīra*) made of mind, intelligence and false ego? No, not that either. By your intrinsic nature you are Kṛṣṇa's eternal servant. You are Kṛṣṇa's marginal potency (*taṭastha-śakti*): you stand on the boundary between Kṛṣṇa's spiritual world and the world of *māyā*, and you are related to both worlds; that is why you are the marginal potency. Your relationship with Kṛṣṇa is twofold: you are His manifestation both different and nondifferent from Him (*bhedābheda-prakāśa*). In your spiritual nature you are a nondifferent manifestation of Kṛṣṇa; and because you are an atomic consciousness (*aṇu-caitanya*), you are a different manifestation of Kṛṣṇa, who is the great consciousness (*bṛhat-caitanya*). Difference and nondifference (between you and Kṛṣṇa) hold at the same time.” This simultaneous manifestation of difference and nondifference follows from the *jīva's* marginal nature itself. The *jīva* is a ray, a particle, of Kṛṣṇa, who is like the sun; or again, the mass of sparks from a blazing fire also illustrates the *jīvas*.

## 110

As the light of a fire situated in one place spreads all around, so the potency of the Supreme Brahman pervades the whole universe.

## 113

The potencies of all that exists, which only inconceivable knowledge can grasp, reside in Brahman; that is why these potencies of Brahman act as the potencies of being — of creation and the rest. O best of ascetics, as heat is a property inherent in fire itself, so the potencies are properties inherent in Brahman itself.

## 117

The *jīva* falls into the bondage of *māyā* precisely by forgetting, “I am Kṛṣṇa's eternal servant.” The *jīva*, being the marginal potency, is situated where the spiritual world and the world of *māyā* meet; while there, he desired to enjoy *māyā*, and so he entered *māyā*. The reckoning of māyic time begins only from this entry into *māyā*. Since the *jīva* turned away from Kṛṣṇa before that reckoning of time began, his aversion is called “beginningless”: it arose before māyic time.

## 119

Because the *jīva* is absorbed in *māyā*, which is other than Kṛṣṇa, “fear” comes upon him; and because he has turned away from that Lord, a reversed memory born of *māyā* arises in him. Therefore a wise person, regarding the guru as his “deity” and his “self,” should worship that Supreme Lord with exclusive devotion.

## 120

That the *jīva* fell because he turned away from Kṛṣṇa is learned by the mercy of the *sādhus* and the scriptures. The *jīva* who learns this and turns toward Kṛṣṇa again is delivered, and *māyā* leaves him.

## 121

This *māyā* of Mine, made of the three *guṇas*, is extremely hard to cross. Only one who surrenders to Me can cross this *māyā*.

## 123–125

Seeing that the *jīva*, bewitched by *māyā*, had lost the knowledge that is remembrance of Kṛṣṇa, the boundlessly compassionate Kṛṣṇa revealed the scriptures — the Vedas and Purāṇas. In the form of those scriptures, in the form of the guru who reveals their meaning, and in the form of the indwelling Self, the Antaryāmī, He makes the *jīva* know the truth about himself. All the Vedic scriptures teach knowledge of *sambandha*, knowledge of *abhidheya* and knowledge of *prayojana*. The truth that Kṛṣṇa is the One whom the *jīva* is to attain is found in knowledge of *sambandha*. The means of attaining Kṛṣṇa is called “*bhakti*”; it is called the “*abhidheya*.” In attaining Kṛṣṇa there is a wonderful thing called “*prema*”; it is called the “*prayojana*.”

## 127

When the *jīva* turned away from Kṛṣṇa and gradually lost the memory of his own true nature, Kṛṣṇa revealed Himself by producing the Vedas, the Purāṇas and the other scriptures. The story of the poor man and the all-knowing one (*sarvajña*) illustrates just this.

## 135

The Vedas and Purāṇas speak in many places of many kinds of means. On one side is the *karma-kāṇḍa*, like hornets and wasps (*bhīmarula-barulī*); on another, the *jñāna-kāṇḍa*, like a *yakṣa*; on another, *kaivalya* attained by *yoga*, like a black python; and on yet another, a pot of hidden treasure that comes into one's hands with little effort. Therefore the Vedic scriptures say that Kṛṣṇa is attained only on the path of devotion, by giving up *karma*, *jñāna* and *yoga*.

## 138

I, who am dear to the *sādhus*, am attained only by devotion born of exclusive faith. Devotion fixed on Me frees even a *caṇḍāla* from the defect of birth.

## 142

The chief fruit of tasting service to Kṛṣṇa is the happiness of love. The *jīva's* poverty is his aversion to Kṛṣṇa. The end of this poverty and the end of *saṁsāra* come along with the taste of service to Kṛṣṇa as by-products; in truth they are not the chief fruit.

## 145

Let all those Purāṇas and Āgamas declare, until the end of the *kalpa*, that the deity each of them speaks of is “the chief,” and so bewilder the moving and the unmoving. Yet when all those Āgamas and other scriptures are examined well, in their conclusions they establish Viṣṇu alone as Bhagavān.

## 147–148

What all the statements of the Vedas enjoin, what they establish, what they have in view when they weigh alternatives (*vikalpa*) — no one but Me knows this purport of the Vedas. I say: the statements of the Vedas directly enjoin and name Me, and when they weigh alternatives, they speak of Me as well. I alone am the purport of all the Vedas. The Vedas examine *māyā* alone, at last reject it completely, and come to rest (cease from reasoning).

## 150

The potency of His own nature (*svarūpa-śakti*) and the world, which is the work of all His potencies, have one shelter only — Kṛṣṇa.

## 155

The *para* name is the highest, that is, the principal name: “Kṛṣṇa,” “Govinda” and the like are the principal names of Bhagavān.

## 157

To those who seek that nondual Truth through knowledge of the featureless, it appears as the featureless Brahman. To those who seek that Supreme Reality on the path of eightfold yoga, it appears as the Paramātmā, who abides in the heart and pervades the world. And those who pursue the supreme Truth by pure *bhakti* see Bhagavān.

## 162

Know this Śrī Kṛṣṇa to be the Self of all selves. Wishing the good of the world, He has appeared here like a human being, relying on His *svarūpa-śakti*.

## 164–165

When one worships Bhagavān in *bhakti*, one experiences His full form: in that one eternal form (*vigraha*) countless forms shine forth. First of all Bhagavān is seen in three forms: *svayaṁ-rūpa*, *tad-ekātma-rūpa* and *āveśa-rūpa*. In the *svayaṁ-rūpa* He appears in two ways, as *svayam* and *prakāśa*; of these, in the *svayaṁ-rūpa* Śrī Kṛṣṇa appears in Vraja in the form of a cowherd boy. According to the *Bhāgavatāmṛta*, the *svayaṁ-rūpa* is Kṛṣṇa's cowherd form, because it depends on no other form of His. That form of His which is nondifferent from the *svayaṁ-rūpa* but differs in shape, opulence and so on is called *tad-ekātma-rūpa*. And the *jīvas* whom Bhagavān's potency enters and who perform great works are Bhagavān's *āveśa* forms.

## 169

Saubhari and other sages made *kāya-vyūhas* (many bodies) by the power of yoga and so accomplished their aims. But the many forms that Kṛṣṇa manifests are not like that, for Nārada would not have been amazed at seeing the *kāya-vyūha* of the path of yoga.

## 173

Those who have been purified (*saṁskṛtātmā*) by the rules enjoined (in the Sātvata and Śaiva *tantras* and others) worship You Yourself, the one form in many forms.

## 174

To understand *svayaṁ-rūpa*, *tad-ekātma-rūpa*, *āveśa*, *vaibhava*, *prābhava* and the rest, their relation to one another is shown briefly below.

The three original forms of Śrī Kṛṣṇa.

(1) The *svayaṁ-rūpa*: Śrī Kṛṣṇa in the form of a cowherd boy in Vraja. (2) The *tad-ekātma-rūpa*: (a) *svāṁśa* — (1) Kāraṇābdhiśāyī, Garbhodaśāyī, Kṣīrodaśāyī; (2) Matsya, Kūrma, Varāha, Nṛsiṁha and others; (b) *vilāsa* — (1) *prābhava*: Vāsudeva, Saṅkarṣaṇa, Pradyumna, Aniruddha; (2) *vaibhava*: twenty-four forms — (a) the *āvaraṇa* (surrounding circle): the four forms of the *catur-vyūha*, Vāsudeva and the others; (b) each of them manifests three forms, twelve in all, the deities of the twelve months and the pattern of the (marks of) *tilaka*; (c) the eight *vilāsa* forms of those four — Puruṣottama, Acyuta and others. These twenty-four forms bear different names according to how they hold their weapons.

## 181

O friend, this *cāraṇa*, as if My second form, is portraying My pastimes — the pastimes of a cowherd boy, full of a wondrous fragrance of sweetness. My mind, stirred by eagerness for play, looks upon My own deeds and longs to take the form of the young women of Vraja.

## 202

*Ācamana* — the touching of the mouth with water that is prescribed after the daily worship (*āhnika*).

## 242

The four — Vāsudeva and the others — and Nārāyaṇa, Nṛsiṁha, Hayagrīva, Varāha and Brahmā: these are the nine.

## 249

O twice-born, just as thousands upon thousands of small pools flow out of a vast reservoir, so the *avatāras* of Hari, the ocean of goodness (*sattva*), are countless.

## 250

Up to the words “That Puruṣa is of three kinds,” Kṛṣṇa's many forms have been examined. Now Kṛṣṇa's potencies will be examined.

## 252–256

Kṛṣṇa, the nondual Truth, has unlimited potencies; among them, every action bears three distinguishing marks: *icchā-śakti*, *jñāna-śakti* and *kriyā-śakti*. He in whom *icchā-śakti* predominates is “Kṛṣṇa,” by whose will everything takes place. He in whom *jñāna-śakti* predominates is “Vāsudeva,” and He in whom *kriyā-śakti* predominates is “Saṅkarṣaṇa.” These three, with Their three potencies, have created, or manifested, the material and the transcendental worlds. “Saṅkarṣaṇa,” the presiding Lord of *ahaṅkāra*, by Kṛṣṇa's will and through the *cit-śakti* has manifested the abodes Goloka, Vaikuṇṭha and the others, the display (*vilāsa*) of the *cit-śakti*.

## 258

The great abode (*mahat-pada*) called Gokula is a lotus with a thousand petals; its whorl is His abode; and all of it is born of a part of Ananta.

## 262

These two, Rāma and Kṛṣṇa, are the source of the *jīvas* of this world (*jīva-yoni*). Both of Them have entered all beings and have produced in them the knowledge of difference from one another.

## 270

In that Vaikuṇṭha there is neither *rajas* nor *tamas*, nor *sattva* mixed with them, nor the might of time; even *māyā* is not there — what to speak of the rest. There live the associates, the devotees faithful to Śrī Kṛṣṇa, who are worshiped by gods and *asuras* alike.

## 274

That Supreme Person placed His own seed in His own *māyā*, whose properties (*dharmas*) had been agitated by destiny (*daiva*). Then *māyā* gave birth to the golden *mahat-tattva*.

## 275

Adhokṣaja (the Lord of Mahā-Vaikuṇṭha), who possesses the seed (that is, the *cit-śakti*), through the Puruṣa who is His own part — that is, the original Puruṣa, the master of *prakṛti* — by the power of time (*kāla-vṛtti*) placed the seed (the *jīva-śakti*, a mass of atomic particles of spirit) in *māyā*, made of the *guṇas* (and agitated).

## 276

The three kinds of *ahaṅkāra* (ego) are *vaikārika*, *taijasa* and *tāmasa*.

## 299

You become various avatāras — in the forms of Matsya, Aśvagrīva, Kacchapa, Nṛsiṁha, Varāha, Haṁsa, Dāśarathi, Paraśurāma, Vāmana and others — and protect us and the three worlds. O best of the Yadus, we bow down to You. O Lord, now remove the burden of this earth.

## 301–303

This Garbhodaśāyī, the *puruṣa-avatāra* Viṣṇu, taking up the *guṇas* of *sattva*, *rajas* and *tamas*, manifests the three *guṇa-avatāras* — Viṣṇu, Brahmā and Śiva. Among them, by virtue of piety mixed with devotion, He imbues some excellent *jīva* with *rajo-guṇa*, infuses His own *śakti* into him, and “in the form of Brahmā” carries out the individual creation.

## 304

The sun displays its brilliance in small measure in various separate stones. So too the original Person Govinda places His own *śakti* in some *jīva*, becomes “Brahmā,” and arranges the universe. Him I worship.

## 307–308

Taking up *tamo-guṇa* through His own *aṁśa-kalā*, for the sake of destruction He assumes the form of “Rudra” in association with *māyā*. Because of the transformation caused by association with *māyā*, Rudra is a *tattva* manifest as both different and non-different. Therefore He is counted within the *jīva-tattva* and is not Kṛṣṇa's “*svarūpa*.”[^307-308-1]

## 310

Through contact with a particular transforming agent, milk (*kṣīra*) becomes yogurt, and there is no other cause in it besides that transformation. So too the original Person Govinda, for the sake of a task, accepts the nature of Śambhu. Him I worship.

## 312

The *tattva* covered by the three kinds of *ahaṅkāra* — *vaikārika*, *taijasa* and *tāmasa* — and always joined with *māyā-śakti* is “Śiva.”

## 313

Śrī Hari is directly the Person beyond the *guṇas*, transcendent to *prakṛti*. He sees all and is the witness of all. By worshipping Him, the *jīva* becomes free from the *guṇas*.

## 314–315

Brahmā is a *śaktyāveśa*, yet he is a *guṇa-avatāra*. Rudra is both different and non-different, yet He too is a *guṇa-avatāra*. Viṣṇu, however, though a *guṇa-avatāra* as a *svāṁśa*, must be called beyond the *guṇas* of *māyā*, for in Him pure *sattva* (*śuddha-sattva*) is seen. Viṣṇu is the part (*aṁśa*); Kṛṣṇa is His whole (*aṁśī*). Therefore Viṣṇu, like Kṛṣṇa, is full of the majesty of His own nature.

## 316

The flame of a lamp, in another vessel, acts as a separate lamp; that is, it has the same properties as the first lamp. So too the original Person Govinda manifests as “Viṣṇu.” Him I worship.

## 318

Brahmā said: “Only by Hari's appointment do I create; by His command Śiva destroys. And this very Hari, the holder of the three *śaktis*, maintains the universe in the form of the Puruṣa.”

## 320

The *manvantara-avatāras*: in one day of Brahmā there are fourteen *manvantaras*, and in them fourteen avatāras. In one month of Brahmā there are 420, and in one year (of 360 days) 5,040 avatāras; in Brahmā's lifetime, 504,000 *manvantara-avatāras*.

## 325

“In Svāyambhuva”: in the *manvantara* of Svāyambhuva, the avatāra Yajña; in the *manvantara* of Svārociṣa, “Vibhu,” and so on — in fourteen *manvantaras*, fourteen avatāras.

## 332

Kardama is the *prajāpati* who married Devahūti, the daughter of Manu, and whose son is Kapiladeva. Pleased with his austerities, Bhagavān gave him *darśana* in a white form.

## 336

Obeisances to Bhagavān Vāsudeva, Saṅkarṣaṇa, Pradyumna and Aniruddha.

## 342–343

O King, Kali, the treasury of faults, has one great quality: in Kali-yuga, by *kīrtana* of Kṛṣṇa alone, the *jīva* is freed from the tightest bondage. The fruit that was gained by meditating on Viṣṇu in Satya-yuga, by worshipping with sacrifices in Tretā-yuga and by serving the Deity in Dvāpara-yuga — in the age of Kali all those fruits are gained through *hari-kīrtana*.

## 345

The Āryans who know merit and grasp the essence call Kali “blessed” precisely because in the age of Kali every goal is attained by *saṅkīrtana* alone.

## 353

The Supreme Lord has no material body; His body is transcendental, and so the truth of His avatāras is hard for the *jīva* to grasp. Your avatāras are known to some extent by the matchless, surpassing and unearthly power (*vīrya*) they display.

## 355

*Ākṛti*: shape; *prakṛti*: nature; *svarūpa*: form (*mūrti*); *svarūpa-lakṣaṇa*: the activity of that form (*vigraha*); *taṭastha-lakṣaṇa*: knowing by deeds.

## 366

*Śaktyāveśa* is of two kinds, secondary and principal. One in whom the *śakti* itself descends is a principal *śaktyāveśa-avatāra*. Where the *śakti* is seen only as a reflection, in the form of *vibhūti*, there is a secondary *śaktyāveśa-avatāra*.

## 370

“In Śeṣa, the *śakti* of serving Himself”: in the avatāra of Bhagavān in the form of Śeṣa is invested the *śakti* that consists in serving Him.

## 371

Where Bhagavān enters by a *kalā* of the *jñāna-śakti* and other *śaktis*, those greatest *jīvas* are counted as “*āveśa-avatāras*.”

## 373

Those *jīvas* who are endowed with *vibhūti* and splendor (*śrī*) — know them as born of a portion of My radiance.

## 378

Kṛṣṇa, the possessor of eternal pastimes (*vilāsa*), the shelter of all the *rasas* of devotion, has various ages, yet the best of them is *kiśora*.

## 397

Bhagavān Hari, who in the *nāṭya-śāstra* is glorified by the words “best,” “middling” and so on, is of three kinds: *pūrṇa*, *pūrṇatara* and *pūrṇatama*.

## 398

Hari who displays few qualities is *pūrṇa* (complete); Hari who displays all qualities, but in lesser measure, is *pūrṇatara* (more complete); and Hari in whom all qualities are displayed is *pūrṇatama* (most complete). So the learned glorify Him.

## 399

In Gokula Kṛṣṇa's most complete fullness (*pūrṇatamatā*) was manifest, in Dvārakā and Mathurā His greater fullness (*pūrṇataratā*), and in Paravyoma His fullness (*pūrṇatā*).

*Thus ends the twentieth chapter of the “Amṛta-pravāha-bhāṣya.”*
