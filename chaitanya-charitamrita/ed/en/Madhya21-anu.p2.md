## 96

> Subheading: The meaning of *svārājya-lakṣmī* —

Kṛṣṇa eternally abides together with His own *cit-śakti* (spiritual potency), the *svārājya-lakṣmī*. The wealth of the Lord's *cit-śakti* is what is called the “six opulences” (*ṣaḍ-aiśvarya*). The *cit-śakti* is the very own potency of Kṛṣṇa, the form who possesses the *cit-śakti*, and she is His maidservant.

## 100

> Subheading: Kṛṣṇa Himself is enchanted by the transcendent sweetness of pastimes befitting His pastimes in human form —

After the departure of Śrī Kṛṣṇa, Śrīla Uddhava, stricken with grief in separation from Him, glorifies before Śrī Vidura the matchless sweetness of His beauty:

**yat** (that form) **martya-līlaupayikaṁ** (suited to pastimes in the world of mortals, humanlike) **sva-yoga-māyā-balaṁ** (the might of His own *cit-śakti*) **darśayatā** (by Him who displays it — by Bhagavān Himself) **gṛhītam** (accepted), **svasya ca** (even to Himself) **vismāpanaṁ** (astonishing), **saubhagardheḥ** (of the abundance of good fortune) **paraṁ padaṁ** (the highest limit, the foundation), **bhūṣaṇa-bhūṣaṇāṅgam** (the form whose limbs are the ornaments of ornaments; “having shown this His own form, He left the world” — so it is construed with the preceding verse).

## 101

> Subheading: The two-armed, ever-youthful form holding the flute —

Bhagavān's pastimes are endless. There are Kṛṣṇa's pastimes in Gokula; the pastimes of Vāsudeva, Saṅkarṣaṇa and others in Paravyoma; the pastimes of the *puruṣa-avatāras* such as Kāraṇārṇavaśāyī; the pastimes of the avatāras who come for a particular occasion (*naimittika*), such as Matsya and Kūrma; the pastimes of the *guṇa-avatāras* such as Brahmā and Śiva; the pastimes of the *āveśa-avatāras* such as Pṛthu and Vyāsa; the pastimes of the Paramātmā and others endowed with attributes (*saviśeṣa*); and the impersonal (*nirviśeṣa*) Brahman. When all these plays of Bhagavān are compared, Kṛṣṇa's pastimes in human form (*nara-līlā*) are the best of all. Kṛṣṇa's *svarūpa* is a human body, a cowherd's dress, a flute in hand, fresh youth (*nava-kiśora*) and the finest of dancers (*naṭavara*). Kṛṣṇa's *svarūpa* corresponds to His pastimes in human form. Yet it bears none of the impurity of material attributes: it is not base, mortal, impermanent, unworthy, limited, divided or confined.

## 102

> Subheading: A description of the sweetness of Kṛṣṇa's form; Kṛṣṇa's form attracts all living beings —

A single particle of Kṛṣṇa's sweet form can flood the three worlds — Gokula, Mathurā and Dvārakā; or else these three worlds: the inner abode, Goloka-Vṛndāvana, the middle abode, Paravyoma, and the outer abode, Devī-dhāma. And the sweetness of His form attracts the beings of these three worlds.

## 103

> Subheading: Manifesting the eternal pastimes displays the power of Yogamāyā —

In Paravyoma and the other abodes, Yogamāyā — the *cit-śakti* that is the transformation of *viśuddha-sattva* — does not abide. To show the working of this Yogamāyā's unprecedented, extraordinary power, Kṛṣṇa manifested in the world (*prapañca*), from Goloka, His eternal pastimes — a jewel that the devotees keep deeply secret and hold most dear.

## 104

> Subheading: Kṛṣṇa's own intense longing to enjoy His form —

Kṛṣṇa's form is so extraordinarily wondrous that it astonishes Kṛṣṇa Himself, and Kṛṣṇa's own eagerness to taste it grows. His own supreme good fortune, full of the six opulences — complete beauty, knowledge, wealth, strength, fame and renunciation — abides eternally in Kṛṣṇa alone.

## 105

> Subheading: The *āśraya* of Goloka — those who hold love — are enchanted and attracted by the form of its object (*viṣaya*) —

*Alaṅkāra* means an ornament of the body. But the beauty of Kṛṣṇa's body is so wondrous that His body is like the ornament of ornaments! Despite such beauty of His body, the graceful pose bent in three places (*lalita-tribhaṅga*) seems to increase it still more. Moreover, above His eyes the bow-like brows are dancing. Fitting the arrows of His sidelong glances to the bow of His brows, Kṛṣṇa takes firm aim to pierce the hearts of Rādhā and the gopīs who follow Her.

## 106

> Subheading: Kṛṣṇa's form attracts even Nārāyaṇa and the Lakṣmīs of Paravyoma —

Kṛṣṇa's form is so captivating that — to say nothing of all the beings and gods of the material world — it forcibly steals the hearts even of Nārāyaṇa and the other forms (*svarūpas*) of Kṛṣṇa in Paravyoma, above the universes. Even the Lakṣmīs, whom the Vedas call the one “crest-jewel of chaste wives,” are attracted by Kṛṣṇa's beauty and long for His lotus feet.

## 107

> Subheading: “When He shines together with Rādhā, then He is Madana-mohana” —

Mounting the chariot of the gopīs' minds — their favourable disposition of heart — and accepting their service to Himself, Kṛṣṇa agitates the mind of Kandarpa himself, and so He is called “Madana-mohana.” Madana is the lord of five arrows: form, taste, smell, sound and touch. With His own beauty Kṛṣṇa tramples his pride — the pride of enchanting women. And Kṛṣṇa Himself, under the name *nava-kandarpa* (the new, transcendental Madana of Vraja), sports in the *rāsa* dance with the gopīs.

## 109

> Subheading: A description of Kṛṣṇa's form —

The pearl necklace on Kṛṣṇa's neck is like a row of white cranes; the peacock feather on Kṛṣṇa's head is like a rainbow; and Kṛṣṇa's yellow garment is like lightning. Kṛṣṇa is like a fresh cloud, and the gopīs are like the crops of the world. As a cloud rains upon the crops, Kṛṣṇa pours upon them the streams of the nectar of His pastimes and so gives them life. In the rainy season cranes fly, and the rainbow and the lightning are seen as well.

## 110

> Subheading: The highest *bhagavattā* — the sweetness of Kṛṣṇa — is described only in the *Bhāgavatam* —

The very essence of the *bhagavattā* of Bhagavān, who is full of the six opulences, is sweetness, and this sweetness was revealed in Vraja itself. Śukadeva, the son of Dvaipāyana, described for the devotees, in many places of the *Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam*, this particle of sweetness that maddens the hearts of the devotees.

## 111

> Subheading: While describing Kṛṣṇa's qualities, the Lord describes the good fortune of the gopīs —

The women of Mathurā, overflowing with feeling, described the extraordinary fortune of the gopīs and the transcendent qualities of Kṛṣṇa. In the same way Śrī Gaurahari, beginning to speak of “the taste of Kṛṣṇa” (*kṛṣṇa-rasa*), was filled with *prema* (love of God), took Sanātana's hand and, absorbed in love, recited that verse.

> Subheading: Without *rāgānugā-bhakti* Kṛṣṇa's sweetness is very hard to attain —

Karma, austerity, yoga, *jñāna*, *vidhi-bhakti*, *japa*, meditation and other practices (*sādhana*) do not bring one to sweetness. Kṛṣṇa's sweetness is easily attained only by one who, on the path of *rāga* (*rāga-mārga*), worships Kṛṣṇa's name with *anurāga*.
