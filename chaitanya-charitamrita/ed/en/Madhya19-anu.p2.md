## 151

> Subheading: Devotion is likened to a creeper; another name for devotion is *kṛṣṇānurāga* (attachment to Kṛṣṇa). A description of the path by which the conditioned *jīva*, step by step, attains loving service to Kṛṣṇa: at its root lies the *sukṛti* that bestows devotion, which is Kṛṣṇa's mercy; the fruit of that *sukṛti* is meeting a genuine guru; by his mercy, through hearing, comes the realization of *sambandha* and faith awakens —

“Brahmāṇḍa” means the fourteen worlds (see Ādi 5.98).

*Bhāgyavān* (fortunate) means a *jīva* endowed with *sukṛti* (pious merit). When a *jīva* unknowingly renders some service to Viṣṇu and the Vaiṣṇavas, *sukṛti* arises in him (see the story of Nārada's birth, *Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam* 1.5.23–30). Such *sukṛti*, which turns the *jīva* toward devotion, is not material activity: it is the first, still unblossomed stirring of the soul's own spiritual nature (*cid-vṛtti*). When through *sukṛti* faith appears, and through hearing comes knowledge of *sambandha*, real pure devotion begins.

*Guru-prasāda* (the guru's mercy): out of his mercy the guru grants his disciple the highest grace (*anugraha*) — devotion to Kṛṣṇa. For the highest good of one who has *sukṛti* and is fit for mercy, Śrī Bhagavān empowers the person dearest to Him and sends him into the world as the *mahānta-guru*, to distribute His power of mercy. Śrī Gurudeva grants the disciple his own grace — service to Kṛṣṇa.

*Kṛṣṇa-prasāda* (Kṛṣṇa's mercy): the gurudeva who gives the seed of the creeper of devotion is a manifestation of Bhagavān of the *āśraya* category (the vessel of love). Kṛṣṇa sends him to the disciple — that is Kṛṣṇa's mercy. By the guru's mercy one obtains Kṛṣṇa's mercy, and by Kṛṣṇa's mercy, the guru's mercy.

*Bhakti-latā-bīja* (the seed of the creeper of devotion) is the seed from which the creeper of service to Bhagavān grows. The cause of the creeper of devotion is the guru's mercy and Kṛṣṇa's mercy. From the seed of other desires (*anyābhilāṣa*), the seed of *karma* and the seed of *jñāna* grow their own kinds of trees. The seed of the creeper of devotion is different from all these seeds. One obtains it only when the guru and Kṛṣṇa are pleased. If they are displeased, one may get the seed of other desires, of *karma* or of *jñāna*, but the seed of pure devotion is lost. Those who lack true good fortune never receive the seed of the creeper of devotion. Only a *jīva* endowed with faith takes shelter at the lotus feet of the guru. The mantra of grace (*anugraha-mantra*) given by the genuine guru, and the path he shows, are the “path of devotion” (*bhakti-mārga*).

## 152

> Subheading: From that very moment the practice (*abhidheya*) begins: worship (*bhajana*) even while *anarthas* remain —

To hear from the guru's lotus feet and then to chant what one has heard — that is the watering, and by it the seed gradually becomes a creeper.

## 153

> Subheading: Worship also when the *anarthas* are gone; the shelter of devotion full of *rāga* is Kṛṣṇa's sweetness (*mādhurya*), not the majesty of Brahmā or of Nārāyaṇa —

Within the “brahmāṇḍa,” that is, in the fourteen worlds, there is no tree that can support (shelter) the creeper of devotion. Devotion cannot be directed toward anything within the brahmāṇḍa. Beyond the brahmāṇḍa flows the river “Virajā.” In it one sees the state of equilibrium of the three *guṇas*; this river washes away material impurity. Only after crossing it does one reach “Brahmaloka,” the ideal of the *jñānīs*. Just as at the Virajā there is no tree fit to support the creeper of devotion, so at Brahmaloka there is no tree the creeper could serve. Finding no tree to lean on, the creeper, watered by hearing and chanting, grows on, passes beyond Brahmaloka and reaches the abode of “Paravyoma.”

## 154

On one side of Brahmaloka and the Virajā lies the brahmāṇḍa made of *māyā* — this is “Devī-dhāma.” Devī-dhāma, or the “other sky” (*itara-vyoma*), exists in subjection to *prakṛti*. On the far side of *prakṛti* lies “Vaikuṇṭha,” or “Paravyoma.” There *māyā* is unable to “measure” anything. Above the wholly spiritual (*brahma-maya*) Vaikuṇṭha lies “Goloka Vṛndāvana.” There the creeper of devotion takes shelter of the desire tree of Kṛṣṇa's feet. In Paravyoma the prescribed worship is offered to Śrī Nārāyaṇa, the Lord of Paravyoma, and in that worship one sees the *rasas* of *śānta*, *dāsya* and “half of *sakhya*.” In Goloka Vṛndāvana, however, in the service of Śrī Kṛṣṇa, five *bhāvas* blossom in full measure: *śānta*, *dāsya*, “half of *sakhya* marked by reverence (*gaurava*)” together with “half of *sakhya* marked by complete confidence (*viśrambha*),” *vātsalya* and *madhura*. Here alone the creeper of devotion finds shelter in every respect.

## 155

> Subheading: Attaining the goal (*prayojana*), love for Kṛṣṇa; in the stage of practice, always hearing and chanting —

*Tāṅhā* (there) means in Goloka Vṛndāvana. *Prema-phala* (the fruit of *prema*) is a wondrous transcendental thing, supremely desirable; at its root is the desire to please Kṛṣṇa's senses. It is the very own treasure of Kṛṣṇacandra, whose form is eternity, knowledge and bliss (*sac-cid-ānanda*); the material intelligence of the conditioned *jīva*, absorbed in enjoyment, cannot grasp it.

*Ihāṅ* (here) means in the material world. Remaining here, one must water the planted seed of the creeper of devotion with the water of hearing, chanting and so on — about Kṛṣṇa's eternal, transcendental name, form, qualities and pastimes.

## 156

> Subheading: In the unripe stage the chief “obstacle” on the path of practice is offense to a Vaiṣṇava —

An offense to a Vaiṣṇava is like a mad elephant. *Aparādha* (offense) means the ten offenses to the holy name (see Ādi 8.24). *Hātī mātā* (a mad elephant) is a strong mood hostile to devotion, or an offense to a Vaiṣṇava in the form of disregard for the guru; it is this that destroys the creeper of devotion.

## 157

> Subheading: The key to one's welfare is to guard against offenses to the holy name —

The creeper of devotion must be fenced in on all sides. The enclosure, or fence, is the effort to avoid the company of those who are not devoted to Kṛṣṇa. Without it, association with nondevotees gives birth to the mad elephant of offense, which may uproot the creeper of devotion, break it or trample it. A *sādhaka* must therefore be extremely careful that this does not happen. Śrī Rūpa Prabhu says in the *Upadeśāmṛta*: “Devotion is ruined by six things: eating (or collecting) more than needed, overendeavor, idle talk, stubborn attachment to rules (or neglect of them), association with worldly people, and greed.”[^157-1]

## 158

> Subheading: Things that look like devotion but are not devotion — kinds of non-devotion —

*Upaśākhā* (side shoots)[^158-1] are not the real creeper's own branches but branches of another creeper that looks like it. They twine around the real creeper and seem to be “part” of it, but in fact they are not the real creeper. *Bhukti* is what those who preach enjoyment of the fruits of *karma* obtain; *mukti* is what the followers of *jñāna* obtain; *vāñchā* (desires) are the fruits of *yoga* that the followers of *siddhi* seek: mystic powers (*vibhūti*) and the like.

## 159

*Niṣiddhācāra* (forbidden conduct) means conduct unbecoming a *siddha*, or conduct that obstructs perfection — that is, conduct by which devotion is lost. For example, when a *jīva*, thinking himself the enjoyer and absorbed in enjoyment, keeps company with women and with those not devoted to Kṛṣṇa, or looks at worldly people and at women.

*Kuṭīnāṭī* means play-acting full of crookedness, duplicity; *ku-ṭī* and *nā-ṭī* mean what opposes inner contentment (*ātma-prasāda*) — that is, discontent.

*Jīva-hiṁsā* (violence toward living beings) means hesitancy or stinginess in chanting and preaching the message of eternal welfare whose root is devotion to Kṛṣṇa — that is, indulging the Māyāvādīs, the *karmīs* and those with other desires (*anyābhilāṣīs*); it also means killing living beings, or causing anxiety or pain to any living being at all.

*Lābha* (gain) means the desire to obtain or amass wealth and the like in the world for the satisfaction of the material senses.

*Pūjā* (worship) means honor gained by adding fuel to the mental notions (*mano-dharma*) of worldly people.

*Pratiṣṭhā* (position) means hankering for worldly greatness, or love of one's own perishable fame among people.

## 160

> Subheading: If indulged, non-devotion flourishes, and devotion may weaken and be smothered —

Under the influence of watering with hearing, chanting and so on, the side shoots grow strong and spread, while the main creeper of devotion, unable to grow, comes to a halt. When hearing and chanting are practised not free from offenses — that is, without giving up bad association, with offenses — the *jīvas* gradually come to be known by many different names. They are devoted to enjoyment, eager to escape bondage, greedy for *siddhis*, given to deceit, debauched with women not lawfully theirs, supporters of false devotion or of the doctrine of the *prākṛta-sahajiyās*. Through the deception of status by seminal lineage they lay claim to spiritual status; they dwell in the five places that Parīkṣit gave to Kali; they see caste in Vaiṣṇavas. They make their living from the holy name, the mantra, the Deity and the *Bhāgavatam*, and are busy amassing wealth and the like by impure means. They seek honor by passing themselves off as those who “delight in solitary worship,” seek fame by supporting the doctrine that reconciles spirit and matter, or else, as servants of a *guru-bruva*, submit to and support an ungodly *varṇāśrama* hostile to Viṣṇu and the Vaiṣṇavas. In other words, maddened by the urge to gratify their own senses, they cheat foolish people for the sake of perishable, extraneous things, not pure devotion, and long to be known in the world as “religious,” “sādhus” or “great souls.” But real pure servants of Hari they cannot become.

## 161

> Subheading: First of all the *sādhaka* must see to giving up bad association —

If, noticing that the sprouts of the “side shoots” mentioned above are appearing, one at once destroys them root and all, only then will the branch of the main creeper of devotion bear the transcendental fruit of *prema* in Vṛndāvana. Otherwise, when the side shoots prevail, it is inevitable that one gives up the service of Hari for good — that is, suffers within the brahmāṇḍa (in heaven and other higher worlds, in the world of mortals, or in hell).

## 162

> Subheading: Only then is it possible to reach the goal (*prayojana*), love for Kṛṣṇa —

Holding on to the creeper, the devotee-gardener reaches the tree of Kṛṣṇa's lotus feet. When the fruit of *prema* in Goloka Vṛndāvana ripens and falls, the devotee living in the material world can taste it.

## 163

*Tāṅhā* (there) means in the transcendental Goloka Vṛndāvana. *Sei kalpa-vṛkṣera* (that desire tree) means the desire tree of Kṛṣṇa's feet. *Āsvādana* (tasting): serving transcendentally, the devotee attains the transcendental happiness of service.

## 164

> Subheading: Love for Kṛṣṇa is the highest goal, which puts the four goals of life (*catur-varga*) to shame —

*Tṛṇa-tulya* (like a blade of grass) means insignificant, worthless by comparison. Before *prema*, the four goals of life — *dharma*, *artha*, *kāma*, *mokṣa* and the rest, which the *karmīs*, *jñānīs*, *yogīs* and others desire — are utterly unacceptable.

## 165

> Subheading: The bliss of serving in love for Kṛṣṇa puts the bliss of Brahman to shame — (*Lalita-mādhava*, Act 5, verse 2; words spoken offstage)

**yāvat** (so long as) **madhu-ripu-vaśī-kāra-siddhauṣadhīnām** (which are perfect herbs for subduing Madhuripu, Kṛṣṇa, and making Him obedient) **premṇām** (of *prema* — of *śānta* and the other *rasas*) **gandha-leśo 'pi** (even the slightest trace) **antaḥ-karaṇa-saraṇī-pānthatām** (the position of a traveler on the path of the heart) **na prayāti** (does not reach), **ṛddhā** (rich) **siddhi-vraja-vijayitā** (supremacy — that is, the position of victor — over the host of *siddhis*, mystic powers; this is the sense), **satya-dharmā** (founded on the *dharma* of truthfulness, cleanliness, charity and austerity) **samādhiḥ** (concentration of the mind), **brahmānandaḥ** (the highest happiness of Brahman) **ca** **guruḥ** (praiseworthy, great) **api** — **tāvat** (until then) **eva** **camatkārayati** (astonishes; the sense is: once the happiness of serving Kṛṣṇa is attained, the happiness of sense objects, *kaivalya* and the happiness of Brahman become insignificant).

## 166

> Subheading: The characteristics of pure devotion: (1) *sādhana-bhakti* —

*Śuddha-bhakti* (pure devotion) is the highest (*uttamā*) devotion: it is beyond the three *guṇas*, unmixed with *karma* and *jñāna*, causeless (*ahaitukī*) and free from the *guṇas* (*nirguṇā*).

## 167

> Subheading: The essence of the whole *Bhāgavatam* — (*Bhakti-rasāmṛta-sindhu*, *Pūrva-vibhāga*, first wave, verse 11)

This verse is not quoted in the *Amṛta-pravāha-bhāṣya*. Here is its translation.

The highest devotion is constant worship for Kṛṣṇa's sake — that is, connected with Kṛṣṇa or directed toward Kṛṣṇa. It is free from all desires rooted in immorality (such as unlawful association with women) that are hostile to the service of Kṛṣṇa; it is not covered by the desire for liberation or the desire for enjoyment; and it consists entirely of efforts favorable for pleasing Kṛṣṇa's senses.

(First its marginal characteristic, *taṭastha-lakṣaṇa*, is given:) **anyābhilāṣitā-śūnyam** (empty of, devoid of *anyābhilāṣitā* — desire rooted in immorality, such as association with women, which is hostile to performing worship of Kṛṣṇa), **jñāna-karmādy-anāvṛtam** (not covered, not screened off [from Kṛṣṇa], not obstructed by *jñāna*, *karma* and so on: *jñāna* here means the search for undifferentiated Brahman, but not the search for knowledge of the One who is worshiped, since that is indispensable; *karma* means the daily and occasional duties (*nitya-naimittika*) and the like enjoined in the *smṛti* and other scriptures, but not service to the One who is worshiped and so on, since that is itself the cultivation [of devotion]; the word “and so on” (*ādi*) means renunciation, *yoga*, the study of *sāṅkhya* and the like), **ānukūlyena** (favorably: favorableness here means an inclination pleasing to Śrī Kṛṣṇa, for whom the worship is performed; unfavorableness is to be understood as its opposite, since it opposes worship; the instrumental case here is defining (*viśeṣaṇa*), not incidental (*upalakṣaṇa*), so favorableness too must be understood as part of the definition of devotion) **kṛṣṇānuśīlanam** (cultivation directed to Kṛṣṇa: the word “Kṛṣṇa” here should be understood to denote Bhagavān Śrī Kṛṣṇa Himself, and also His forms and the others — all who are *viṣṇu-tattva*; *anuśīlana*, connected with Kṛṣṇa or done for Kṛṣṇa's sake, means activities of body, speech and mind directed to Him as the object of love (*prīti*) — that is, carrying out one service after another again and again, without slackness) **eva** **uttamā bhaktiḥ** (this alone is the highest devotion; this clearly states its excellence on both paths, *vaidhī* and *rāgānugā*, and in both stages, that of the *sādhaka* and that of the *siddha*).

## 168

> Subheading: The first two quarters give the “marginal” (*taṭastha*) characteristic, the last two the characteristic of the “essence” (*svarūpa*) of pure devotion —

*Anya-vāñchā* (other desires) means desires other than for the service of Kṛṣṇa. *Anya-pūjā* (worship of others) means worship of anyone other than Kṛṣṇa. *Karma* means the pious acts prescribed for those who, having forgotten their true nature, thirst to enjoy the fruits. *Jñāna* means the endeavor of *saṁvit* alone, without its two eternally inseparable potencies, *sandhinī* and *hlādinī*; it is undertaken for self-elevation by those who, having forgotten their true nature, seek freedom from enjoyment (*mukti*). *Ānukūlye kṛṣṇānuśīlana* (cultivating service to Kṛṣṇa favorably) means serving Kṛṣṇa for Kṛṣṇa's pleasure: giving up the cultivation of [service to] *māyā*, which is other than Kṛṣṇa, and serving Kṛṣṇa in a favorable spirit. *Sarvendriye* (with all the senses) means with all the organs of sense. With the material senses one cultivates only [service to] *māyā*. The “material senses” are speech, hands, feet, anus and genitals, and eyes, ears, nose, tongue, skin and the “mind.” If one serves *māyā*, which is other than Kṛṣṇa, with the material senses, it all ends in one's own enjoyment. For this reason, at the stage of *sādhana-bhakti*, sixty-four limbs (*aṅgas*) of devotion are prescribed. By the strength of this *sādhana-bhakti* the conditioned *jīva* escapes the clutches of material enjoyment, becomes free of *anarthas* and becomes qualified for transcendental service.

## 169

> Subheading: The goal (*prayojana*), love for Kṛṣṇa, comes only from the means (*abhidheya*), pure devotion; this is the view of the *Sātvata-pañcarātra* and the *Bhāgavatam* —

The characteristics of pure devotion: the *Pāñcarātrika* and the *Bhāgavata* *sampradāyas* both establish the same meaning.

## 170

> Subheading: The view of the whole *Pañcarātra* — (words of the Śrī *Nārada-pañcarātra* quoted in the *Bhakti-rasāmṛta-sindhu*, *Pūrva-vibhāga*, first wave)

**sarvopādhi-vinirmuktam** (free from all coverings of difference, devoid of other desires apart from Kṛṣṇa) **tat-paratvena** (with the sole aim of serving Kṛṣṇa, in a favorable spirit) **nirmalam** (free from the impurity of designations — the covering of *karma*, the bewilderment of *jñāna* and so on) **hṛṣīkeṇa** (with senses turned toward service) **hṛṣīkeśa-sevanam** (cultivation [of service] to Viṣṇu, the master of all the senses — this alone) **bhaktiḥ** **ucyate** (is called).

## 171

> Subheading: Kṛṣṇa is attained only through causeless, or exclusive (*aikāntikī*), pure devotion — (*Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam*, Canto 3, Chapter 29, verses 11–14)

## 171–173

See Ādi 4.205–207.

## 174

The words of Bhagavān Śrī Kapiladeva to his mother Devahūti, who wished to hear about the path of pure *bhakti-yoga*:

**saḥ** (with the characteristics stated) **bhakti-yogākhyaḥ** (called *bhakti-yoga*) **eva** **ātyantikaḥ** (existing at the very end, at the limit of all — having reached the highest point) **udāhṛtaḥ** (is described), **yena** (by which — by absolute *bhakti-yoga* — a person) **tri-guṇam** (the cycle of birth and death, made of *māyā*) **ativrajya** (transcending) **mad-bhāvāya** (for directly seeing Me, for the state of *brahma-bhūta*) **upapadyate** (becomes able).

## 175

> Subheading: If there is cheating (*kaitava*) or offense, practice even for millions of births is all in vain —

If the heart holds the desire to enjoy the fruits of *karma* or the desire for liberation from the bondage of worldly existence, then however much a person who seeks such fruits performs the sixty-four kinds of *sādhana-bhakti*, such so-called mixed (*viddha*) worship will turn into mere *karma* or fruitless endeavors of *jñāna*. Therefore the fruit of *sādhana-bhakti* — attaining love for Kṛṣṇa — will not fall to his lot.

## 176

> Subheading: The witches of the desire for enjoyment and the desire for liberation destroy devotion — (*Bhakti-rasāmṛta-sindhu*, *Pūrva-vibhāga*, second wave)

**yāvat** **hṛdi** (within the mind) **bhukti-mukti-spṛhā** (in the form of the desire for enjoyment and liberation) **piśācī** (a devouring witch) **vartate**, **tāvat** **atra** (in the heart) **bhakti-sukhasya** (of the bliss of service that pleases Kṛṣṇa) **katham** (how) **abhyudayaḥ** (manifestation) **bhavet**?

## 177

> Subheading: From *sādhana-bhakti* comes (2) *bhāva-bhakti*, or *rati*, and from *rati* comes (3) *prema-bhakti* —

*Sādhana-bhakti* (*Bhakti-rasāmṛta-sindhu*, *Pūrva-vibhāga*, second wave): “Devotion attained through the activity of the senses, whose goal is *bhāva*, is called *sādhana*. Its goal is to manifest in the heart the eternally perfect *bhāva*.” Devotion performed with the senses that assist hearing, chanting and so on is called “*sādhana-bhakti*.” To manifest the eternally perfect *bhāva* in the heart is “*sādhana*.” It extends through faith (*śraddhā*), the company of sādhus (*sādhu-saṅga*: initiation and hearing), worship (*bhajana*: offenseless service to Viṣṇu and the Vaiṣṇavas), steadiness (*niṣṭhā*), taste (*ruci*) and up to attachment (*āsakti*). See Madhya 23.11–15.

*Rati* (*Bhakti-rasāmṛta-sindhu*, *Pūrva-vibhāga*, third wave): “The sign of *rati* is a manifest softness seen within. If such softness appears in those who seek liberation and the like, it is not *rati*.” When the softness that lies within becomes manifest, it is called “the sign of *rati*.” If such softness appears in those who seek liberation or enjoyment, it cannot be called “*rati*.”

*Prema* (*Bhakti-rasāmṛta-sindhu*, *Pūrva-vibhāga*, fourth wave, 1): “When *bhāva* has fully softened the heart, is marked by an intense sense of ‘mine,’ and has become dense, the wise call it *prema*.” When the heart has been thoroughly softened and *bhāva* full of the feeling “He is mine” grows dense, the learned call it “*prema*.”

## 178

> Subheading: The varied degrees of the intensity of *prema-bhakti*; the highest is *mahābhāva* —

*Sneha* (*Bhakti-rasāmṛta-sindhu*, *Paścima-vibhāga*, third wave): “*Prema* that grows dense and melts the heart is called *sneha*. It cannot tolerate separation even for a moment.” When the melting of the heart becomes dense, *prema* is called “*sneha*.” Then even a moment's separation is unbearable.

*Māna* — see Madhya 2.66.

*Praṇaya* — see Madhya 2.66.

*Rāga* (*Bhakti-rasāmṛta-sindhu*, *Paścima-vibhāga*, second wave): “*Rāga* is *sneha* in which even suffering clearly becomes happiness; in it, at even the slightest connection [with Him], one loves at the cost of life itself.” *Sneha* in which suffering is clearly felt as “happiness” is “*rāga*.” At the mere connection [with Him] there arises the urge to give pleasure to Śrī Kṛṣṇa even at the cost of one's own life.

*Anurāga*, *bhāva* and *mahābhāva* — see the discussion of *adhirūḍha-mahābhāva* in the *Anubhāṣya* on Madhya 6.13.

## 180

> Subheading: *Rati* runs through all of them —

*Sthāyi-bhāva* (*Bhakti-rasāmṛta-sindhu*, *Dakṣiṇa-vibhāga*, first wave): “When this love for Kṛṣṇa (*kṛṣṇa-rati*), the *sthāyi-bhāva*, is made tasteable in the hearts of devotees through hearing and so on, by means of *vibhāvas*, *anubhāvas*, *sāttvikas* and *vyabhicārīs*, it becomes *bhakti-rasa*.” *Kṛṣṇa-rati* is the *sthāyi-bhāva*. When through hearing and so on, combined with *vibhāva*, *anubhāva*, and the *sāttvika* and *vyabhicārī bhāvas*, it is brought to a tasteable state in the hearts of devotees, it becomes “*bhakti-rasa*.”

*Vibhāva* (*Bhakti-rasāmṛta-sindhu*, *Dakṣiṇa-vibhāga*, first wave): “Here the causes of tasting *rati* are to be known as *vibhāvas*. They are of two kinds: some are *ālambanas*, the others *uddīpanas*.” The causes of tasting *rati* are called “*vibhāva*”; *vibhāva* is of two kinds, *ālambana* and *uddīpana*.

*Anubhāva* (*Bhakti-rasāmṛta-sindhu*, *Dakṣiṇa-vibhāga*, second wave[^180-1]): “*Anubhāvas* are what reveal the feelings that dwell in the mind; they are mostly outward transformations and are called *udbhāsvara* (radiant).” What reveals the radiant (*udbhāsvara*) feelings dwelling in the mind and displays outwardly actions resembling transformations — these are the “*anubhāvas*.”

## 181

> Subheading: When four kinds of *bhāva* combine with *rati*, *rasa* arises —

*Sāttvika* and *vyabhicārī* — see Madhya 3.162 and Madhya 14.167.

## 182

> Subheading: A comparison —

*Sitā* means *misri* (crystallized sugar).

## 183

> Subheading: Five kinds of *rati*, according to the difference of devotees —

*Śānta-rati* (*Bhakti-rasāmṛta-sindhu*, *Dakṣiṇa-vibhāga*, fifth wave): “Freedom of the mind from wavering is called *śama*” — that is, a state of mind free from doubt and the like is called “*śama*.” (The same:) “When, turning away from sense objects, one abides in one's own bliss, this is here called the nature of the self — *śama*. In those in whom *śama* predominates, there usually arises toward Kṛṣṇa as the Paramātmā *śānta-rati*, devoid of even a trace of the sense of ‘mine.’” Abiding in one's own bliss after giving up the desire for sense objects is called the nature of “*śama*.” In those in whom *śama* predominates, through realization of the Paramātmā, *śānta-rati* toward Kṛṣṇa arises, devoid of even a trace of the feeling “He is mine.”

*Dāsya-rati* (*Bhakti-rasāmṛta-sindhu*, *Dakṣiṇa-vibhāga*, fifth wave): “Those who are lower than He are considered recipients of Hari's mercy. Their *rati*, whose essence is [seeing Him as] the One to be worshiped, is called *prīti*. It creates attachment to Him and destroys love for everything else.” When the *jīva*'s *rati* is full of the consciousness of being lower than Śrī Bhagavān, he becomes a fit recipient of Hari's mercy. The *rati* called “*prīti*” — “Bhagavān is the One to be worshiped” — produces “attachment” (*āsakti*) to the worshipable Bhagavān Kṛṣṇacandra and destroys love for things of *māyā* other than Bhagavān.

*Sakhya-rati* (*Bhakti-rasāmṛta-sindhu*, *Dakṣiṇa-vibhāga*, fifth wave): “Those who are equal to Mukunda are, in the view of the saintly, His friends. Their *rati*, whose nature is confidence (*viśrambha*) born of equality, is here called *sakhya*. It gives rise to joking, laughter and the like, and is free from restraint.” In the view of the wise and the saintly, *sakhās* (friends) are those whose *rati* is full of the consciousness of equality with Mukunda. *Rati* full of trust, which, owing to mutual equality with Śrī Kṛṣṇa, shows itself as freedom from all constraint, is called “*sakhya-rati*.” This *sakhya-rati* gives rise to joking, laughter and the like; it is called *ayantraṇā* — *rati* free of constraint.

## 184

> Subheading: Five kinds of *bhakti-rasa*, according to the difference of *rati* —

*Vātsalya-rati* (*Bhakti-rasāmṛta-sindhu*, *Dakṣiṇa-vibhāga*, fifth wave): “Those who are senior to this Hari are known as worthy of respect. Their *rati*, full of benevolence (*anugraha*), is called *vātsalya*. It shows itself in caring, wishing well, blessing, touching the chin and so on.” *Jīvas* whose *rati* is full of the consciousness of their own seniority are “worthy of respect” to Bhagavān; their *rati*, full of benevolence, is called “*vātsalya-rati*.” In this *vātsalya-rati* there are caring, looking after one's welfare, blessings, touching the chin and so on.

*Madhura-rati* (*Bhakti-rasāmṛta-sindhu*, *Dakṣiṇa-vibhāga*, fifth wave): “The original cause of the mutual[^184-1] enjoyment (*sambhoga*) of Hari and the doe-eyed ones is the *rati* called *priyatā*; its other name is *madhurā*. In it are sidelong glances, movements of the brows, tender words, smiles and the like.” The original cause of the eight kinds of *sambhoga* — mutual remembrance, seeing one another and so on — between Śrī Bhagavān and the doe-eyed ones is *priyatā*, or *madhurā-rati*. In *madhurā-rati* there are sidelong glances, movements of the brows, tender words, sweet smiles and so on.

## 185

> Subheading: The five principal *bhakti-rasas* and the seven secondary ones —

*Śānta-bhakti-rasa* (*Bhakti-rasāmṛta-sindhu*, *Paścima-vibhāga*, first wave): “When the *sthāyī* — *śānti-rati* — through the *vibhāvas* and the rest that will be described, becomes tasteable to those endowed with *śama*, the wise call it *śānta-bhakti-rasa*. Here the yogīs' happiness is usually of the same kind as the happiness of their own self. But the happiness of the self is not dense, whereas the happiness full of the Lord (*Īśa*) is dense. Here too the chief cause is the experience of the Lord's form; charm of pastimes and the like are not considered [a cause] to the same degree as they are for servants and others.” When the *sthāyi-bhāva* in the form of *śānta-rati* combines with the *vibhāvas* and the rest that will be described and becomes tasteable to *śānta* devotees — that is, takes on that form — then it becomes “*śānta-bhakti-rasa*.” In *śānta-rasa* the yogīs attain happiness of the same kind as the bliss of impersonal Brahman, the foundation of all. But this bliss of the self is *aghana*, that is, meager; whereas the abundant happiness of service, when the *sac-cid-ānanda* form of Bhagavān manifests, is “dense.” The *śānta* devotees do have greater happiness through direct vision, but they do not have such a taste for Bhagavān's charming pastimes as the servants and others do.

*Dāsya-bhakti-rasa* (*Bhakti-rasāmṛta-sindhu*, *Paścima-vibhāga*, second wave): “When *prīti*, through *vibhāvas* and the rest suited to it, is made tasteable in the hearts of devotees, it is considered *prīta-bhakti-rasa*. According to whether the recipient of mercy is a servant or one who is cared for, it is divided in two: *sambhrama-prīta* and *gaurava-prīta*.” When, through suitable *vibhāvas* and the rest, *prīti-rati* becomes tasteable in the hearts of devotees, it becomes “*prīti*,” or “*dāsya-bhakti-rasa*.” Since the servants who are fit for mercy may be servants or those who are cared for, two kinds of *prīti* are seen in *dāsya-rasa*: *sambhrama-dāsya* (with awe) and *gaurava-dāsya* (with reverence for a superior).

*Sakhya-bhakti-rasa* (*Bhakti-rasāmṛta-sindhu*, *Paścima-vibhāga*, fourth wave): “When the *sthāyi-bhāva*, *sakhya*, through suitable *vibhāvas* and the rest, is brought to fullness in the hearts of the saintly, it is called the *rasa* of *preyas*.” When the *sthāyi-bhāva*, *sakhya-rati*, through suitable *vibhāvas* and the rest, reaches fullness in the hearts of devotees, it becomes “*preyo-rasa*,” or “*sakhya-bhakti-rasa*.”

*Vātsalya-bhakti-rasa* (*Bhakti-rasāmṛta-sindhu*, *Paścima-vibhāga*, fourth wave): “When the *sthāyī*, *vātsalya*, reaches fullness through *vibhāvas* and the rest, the wise call it the *bhakti-rasa* named *vatsala*.” When the *sthāyi-bhāva*, *vātsalya-rati*, reaches fullness in the devotee's heart through *vibhāvas* and the rest, learned devotees call it “*vātsalya-bhakti-rasa*.”

*Madhura-bhakti-rasa* (*Bhakti-rasāmṛta-sindhu*, *Paścima-vibhāga*, fifth wave): “When *madhurā rati*, through *vibhāvas* and the rest suited to it, reaches fullness in the hearts of the saintly, it becomes the *bhakti-rasa* named *madhura*.” When the *sthāyi-bhāva*, *madhura-rati*, reaches fullness in the heart of a true devotee through suitable *vibhāvas* and the rest, it is celebrated as “the *bhakti-rasa* called *madhura*.”

## 188

> Subheading: The five principal *rasas* are permanent (*sthāyī*); the seven secondary ones are occasional visitors (*āgantuka*) —

## 187–188

*Hāsya-bhakti-rasa* (*Bhakti-rasāmṛta-sindhu*, *Uttara-vibhāga*, first wave): “When *hāsa-rati*, through the *vibhāvas* and the rest that will be described, reaches fullness, the wise call it *hāsa-bhakti-rasa*.” When *hāsa-rati* (the *rati* of laughter), through the *vibhāvas* and the rest that will be described, reaches fullness, the learned call it “*hāsya-bhakti-rasa*.”

*Adbhuta-bhakti-rasa* (the same, second wave): “When *vismaya-rati*, through *vibhāvas* and the rest suited to it, is made tasteable in the devotee's heart, it becomes *adbhuta-bhakti-rasa*.” When, through suitable *vibhāvas* and the rest, “*vismaya-rati*” (the *rati* of wonder) becomes tasteable in the devotee's heart, there is “*adbhuta-bhakti-rasa*.”

*Vīra-bhakti-rasa* (the same, third wave): “When that same *utsāha-rati*, the *sthāyī*, is made tasteable through *vibhāvas* and the rest suited to it, it becomes *vīra-bhakti-rasa*. The hero (*vīra*) is said to be of four kinds — in battle, in giving, in compassion and in *dharma*.” When, through suitable *vibhāvas* and the rest, “*utsāha-rati*” (the *rati* of enthusiasm) becomes tasteable in the devotee's heart, there is “*vīra-bhakti-rasa*.” According to four activities — “battle,” “giving,” “compassion” and “*dharma*” — four kinds of “hero” (*vīra*) are spoken of.

*Karuṇa-bhakti-rasa* (the same, fourth wave): “When *śoka-rati*, through *vibhāvas* and the rest suited to it, reaches fullness in the hearts of the saintly, it becomes the *bhakti-rasa* called *karuṇa*.” When, through suitable *vibhāvas* and the rest, “*śoka-rati*” (the *rati* of sorrow) reaches fullness in the devotee's heart, it is called “*karuṇa-bhakti-rasa*.”

*Raudra-bhakti-rasa* (the same, fifth wave): “When *krodha-rati*, through *vibhāvas* and the rest suited to it, reaches fullness in the devotee's heart, it becomes *raudra-bhakti-rasa*.” When, through suitable *vibhāvas* and the rest, *krodha-rati* (the *rati* of anger) reaches fullness in the devotee's heart, there is “*raudra-bhakti-rasa*.”

*Bhayānaka-bhakti-rasa* (the same, sixth wave): “When *bhaya-rati*, through the *vibhāvas* and the rest that will be described, reaches fullness, the wise call it the *bhakti-rasa* named *bhayānaka*.” When *bhaya-rati* (the *rati* of fear), through the *vibhāvas* and the rest that will be described, reaches fullness, the learned call it “*bhayānaka-bhakti-rasa*.”

*Bībhatsa-bhakti-rasa* (the same, seventh wave): “When *jugupsā-rati*, through its own *vibhāvas* and the rest, reaches fullness, the wise call this *bhakti-rasa* *bībhatsa*.” When, through suitable *vibhāvas* and the rest, “*jugupsā*,” or “*ghṛṇā-rati*” (the *rati* of disgust), reaches fullness in the devotee's heart, the learned call it “*bībhatsa-bhakti-rasa*.”

*Pañca-vidha bhakte* (in the five kinds of devotees): in the devotees of the five permanent *rasas* — *śānta*, *dāsya*, *sakhya*, *vātsalya* and *madhura* — the seven secondary *rasas*, beginning with laughter, manifest when there is a “cause” for them.

## 201

## 202

> Subheading: The evidence — (*Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam*, Canto 10, Chapter 60, verse 24)

Once, in her own home, Rukmiṇī began to serve Śrī Kṛṣṇa with her own hands. Śrī Kṛṣṇa, wishing to test her attachment, jokingly described Himself as poor, possessionless (*niṣkiñcana*) and indifferent, and so entirely unworthy of Rukmiṇī's love, and told her to leave Śrī Kṛṣṇa's company and give her love to someone else. On hearing this, Rukmiṇī, whose very life is Kṛṣṇa alone, fell into this condition:

**suduḥkha-bhaya-śoka-vinaṣṭa-buddheḥ** (of her, Rukmiṇī, whose reason was lost through intense pain — from hearing something unpleasant — through fear of being abandoned, and through grief, that is, remorse) **ślathad-valayataḥ** (from which bracelets were slipping and falling) **hastāt** **vyajanam** (the fan) **papāta**; **viklava-dhiyaḥ** (of her whose mind was overwhelmed, out of control) **dehaḥ ca** **sahasā** **eva** **muhyan** (fainting) **keśān** **pravikīrya** (scattering them in all directions) **vāta-vihatā** (struck by the wind) **rambhā** (a banana tree) **iva** **papāta** (fell).

## 203

> Subheading: In Vraja, in *kevalā-rati*, devoid of majesty, Kṛṣṇa is regarded as one's own, subject to oneself —

A devotee in whom majesty predominates cannot understand the greatness of the pure *prema* of *kevalā*. Even when seeing Bhagavān's majesty, a devotee wholly given to *kevalā-rati* acknowledges only his own relationship [with Him].

## 204

> Subheading: (1) Yaśodā regards Svayaṁ Bhagavān Kṛṣṇa as her own son — (*Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam*, Canto 10, Chapter 8, verse 45)

When Yaśodā saw in Śrī Kṛṣṇa's mouth the universal form full of majesty, knowledge of the truth (*tattva-jñāna*) awakened awe (*sambhrama*) in her. But at once, by Kṛṣṇa's will, in her heart, in which natural *mamatā* (the feeling “he is mine”) is strong, her love for Kṛṣṇa grew still deeper and increased:

**trayyā** (by the three Vedas — the *Ṛg*, *Yajur* and *Sāma* — consisting of *karma* and *upāsanā*, as Indra and others), **upaniṣadbhiḥ** (by the *śrutis* forming the *jñāna-kāṇḍa*, the higher part of the Vedas, as “Brahman”), **sāṅkhyaiḥ** (as “the Puruṣa”), **yogaiḥ** (as “the Paramātmā”), **sātvataiḥ** (by the *Pañcarātras* and *āgamas*, as “Bhagavān”) **upagīyamāna-māhātmyam** (whose greatness is sung and praised) **harim** **sā** (Yaśodā, endowed with *kevalā-rati*) **ātmajam** (as her son) **amanyata** (regarded).
