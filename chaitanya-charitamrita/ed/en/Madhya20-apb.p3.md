## 274

That Supreme Person placed His own seed in His own *māyā*, whose properties (*dharmas*) had been agitated by destiny (*daiva*). Then *māyā* gave birth to the golden *mahat-tattva*.

## 275

Adhokṣaja (the Lord of Mahā-Vaikuṇṭha), who possesses the seed (that is, the *cit-śakti*), through the Puruṣa who is His own part — that is, the original Puruṣa, the master of *prakṛti* — by the power of time (*kāla-vṛtti*) placed the seed (the *jīva-śakti*, a mass of atomic particles of spirit) in *māyā*, made of the *guṇas* (and agitated).

## 276

The three kinds of *ahaṅkāra* (ego) are *vaikārika*, *taijasa* and *tāmasa*.

## 299

You become various avatāras — in the forms of Matsya, Aśvagrīva, Kacchapa, Nṛsiṁha, Varāha, Haṁsa, Dāśarathi, Paraśurāma, Vāmana and others — and protect us and the three worlds. O best of the Yadus, we bow down to You. O Lord, now remove the burden of this earth.

## 301–303

This Garbhodaśāyī, the *puruṣa-avatāra* Viṣṇu, taking up the *guṇas* of *sattva*, *rajas* and *tamas*, manifests the three *guṇa-avatāras* — Viṣṇu, Brahmā and Śiva. Among them, by virtue of piety mixed with devotion, He imbues some excellent *jīva* with *rajo-guṇa*, infuses His own *śakti* into him, and “in the form of Brahmā” carries out the individual creation.

## 304

The sun displays its brilliance in small measure in various separate stones. So too the original Person Govinda places His own *śakti* in some *jīva*, becomes “Brahmā,” and arranges the universe. Him I worship.

## 307–308

Taking up *tamo-guṇa* through His own *aṁśa-kalā*, for the sake of destruction He assumes the form of “Rudra” in association with *māyā*. Because of the transformation caused by association with *māyā*, Rudra is a *tattva* manifest as both different and non-different. Therefore He is counted within the *jīva-tattva* and is not Kṛṣṇa's “*svarūpa*.”[^307-308-1]

## 310

Through contact with a particular transforming agent, milk (*kṣīra*) becomes yogurt, and there is no other cause in it besides that transformation. So too the original Person Govinda, for the sake of a task, accepts the nature of Śambhu. Him I worship.

## 312

The *tattva* covered by the three kinds of *ahaṅkāra* — *vaikārika*, *taijasa* and *tāmasa* — and always joined with *māyā-śakti* is “Śiva.”

## 313

Śrī Hari is directly the Person beyond the *guṇas*, transcendent to *prakṛti*. He sees all and is the witness of all. By worshipping Him, the *jīva* becomes free from the *guṇas*.

## 314–315

Brahmā is a *śaktyāveśa*, yet he is a *guṇa-avatāra*. Rudra is both different and non-different, yet He too is a *guṇa-avatāra*. Viṣṇu, however, though a *guṇa-avatāra* as a *svāṁśa*, must be called beyond the *guṇas* of *māyā*, for in Him pure *sattva* (*śuddha-sattva*) is seen. Viṣṇu is the part (*aṁśa*); Kṛṣṇa is His whole (*aṁśī*). Therefore Viṣṇu, like Kṛṣṇa, is full of the majesty of His own nature.

## 316

The flame of a lamp, in another vessel, acts as a separate lamp; that is, it has the same properties as the first lamp. So too the original Person Govinda manifests as “Viṣṇu.” Him I worship.

## 318

Brahmā said: “Only by Hari's appointment do I create; by His command Śiva destroys. And this very Hari, the holder of the three *śaktis*, maintains the universe in the form of the Puruṣa.”

## 320

The *manvantara-avatāras*: in one day of Brahmā there are fourteen *manvantaras*, and in them fourteen avatāras. In one month of Brahmā there are 420, and in one year (of 360 days) 5,040 avatāras; in Brahmā's lifetime, 504,000 *manvantara-avatāras*.

## 325

“In Svāyambhuva”: in the *manvantara* of Svāyambhuva, the avatāra Yajña; in the *manvantara* of Svārociṣa, “Vibhu,” and so on — in fourteen *manvantaras*, fourteen avatāras.

## 332

Kardama is the *prajāpati* who married Devahūti, the daughter of Manu, and whose son is Kapiladeva. Pleased with his austerities, Bhagavān gave him *darśana* in a white form.

## 336

Obeisances to Bhagavān Vāsudeva, Saṅkarṣaṇa, Pradyumna and Aniruddha.

## 342–343

O King, Kali, the treasury of faults, has one great quality: in Kali-yuga, by *kīrtana* of Kṛṣṇa alone, the *jīva* is freed from the tightest bondage. The fruit that was gained by meditating on Viṣṇu in Satya-yuga, by worshipping with sacrifices in Tretā-yuga and by serving the Deity in Dvāpara-yuga — in the age of Kali all those fruits are gained through *hari-kīrtana*.
