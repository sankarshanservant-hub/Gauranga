## 42

> Subheading: Nitāi is ordered to preach —

*He ordered Nityānanda.* The *prākṛta-sahajiyās* look upon Śrī Nityānanda Prabhu, who is not different from the son of Rohiṇī, with mundane vision. They claim that “Śrīman Mahāprabhu sent Śrī Nityānanda Prabhu from Śrī Nīlācala to Śrī Gauḍa-deśa to continue His family line (?)”. Such a godless notion springs only from offense at the feet of Śrī Nityānanda. People of this kind regard Śrīman Nityānanda — the original source of all forms of the Lord, of all *viṣṇu-tattva* — as just like themselves: one of the mortal beings who take the corpse-like body for the self (*kuṇapātma-vādīs*), who pursue material sense gratification and are subject to Yama's punishment. So they become travelers on the road to hell. These selfish people with a merchant's nature, greedy for gold, women and fame, invent such an anti-scriptural notion with their fertile brains. Using Nityānanda's name, they look for a chance to justify through His divine acts — unfairly and against scripture — their trade, their cheating of fools and, out of evil intent, the universally condemned craving for the company of women and the *dharma* of the *gṛha-vratas*, or *gṛhamedhīs*.

In fact, no authoritative book records that Śrīman Mahāprabhu, the supremely magnanimous giver of love for Kṛṣṇa, gave any such order to Śrīman Nityānanda Prabhu, His own manifestation, not different from Him: that He should, like the Prajāpatis who rest in the mode of passion, maintain creation by increasing His line, or serve as some instrument to justify the material sense gratification of *jīvas* averse to Kṛṣṇa. Nor can any book record it, for such talk is wholly unfit to be heard. By spreading such talk the *prākṛta-sahajiyās*, who keep company with worldly women, deprive themselves of the highest goal, deceive a world unable to tell the true from the false, and bring nothing but harm upon it.

## 81–83

Śrī Rāghava Paṇḍita was not like a man sunk in *karma* and sick with the mundane “disease of cleanliness,” nor like a mundane *kaniṣṭha* devotee who thinks in terms of duality and “considers the earth worshipable”[^81-83-1] — that is, attributes spirit to matter and lives by the speculations of the mind. He was an eternally perfect servant of Kṛṣṇa. Absorbed in the mood of transcendental service, free from the slightest scent of mundane desire, he served the object of his worship at every moment. Selfish devotees, on the other hand, whose devotion is mixed with *karma* and tainted, have no understanding of transcendental service. If they imitate his outward conduct and deliberate over artificial mundane purity and impurity, that alone does not show that they have pure devotion to Kṛṣṇa or a desire to please Kṛṣṇa: “In the transcendental there is no notion of things as good or bad. In duality the notion of good and bad is all a speculation of the mind; ‘this is good, this is bad’ — all this is illusion.” (See Antya 4.174, 176 and *Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam* 11.28.4.)

## 99

“The first poet, Guṇarāja Khān Mahāśaya, set about this book in Śaka 1395 and completed it in Śaka 1402.

The style of the *Śrī Kṛṣṇa-vijaya* is very simple: even half-literate women of Bengal and men of the lower classes who barely know their letters read and understand it easily. Its language is unadorned, and in many places the verse is not very sweet. A *payāra* line should have fourteen syllables, yet often one finds sixteen or seventeen, or twelve or thirteen. Many words are taken from the usage of that time, and only natives of Rāḍha understand them. No Bengali library lacking this book can be called “complete.”

To those who seek the highest goal this book is extremely dear. The foremost Vaiṣṇava, the worshipable Śrī Guṇarāja Khān Mahāśaya, composed it by rendering the tenth and eleventh cantos of the *Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam*, the crown of all scriptures, in a way that ordinary people came to love. For this reason the Vaiṣṇava world honors this book everywhere. Mahāprabhu praised it so highly — needless to say how greatly the Gauḍīya Vaiṣṇava community will treasure it. This poem is therefore a precious treasure for the people of Bengal; moreover, some say it is the first poem in the Bengali language.

In Śaka 1405, two years before the appearance of Śrī Śrī Mahāprabhu, the book was copied by the hand of Śrī Devānanda Vasu (quoted from the “Introduction” by Śrīmad Bhaktivinoda Ṭhākura to the *Śrī Kṛṣṇa-vijaya*).

The Bengali emperor Ādiśūra brought from Kānyakubja five noble *brāhmaṇas* and with them five noble Kāyasthas; one of those Kāyasthas was Daśaratha Vasu. Śrī Guṇarāja Khān was born in the thirteenth generation of his line. His real name was Śrī Mālādhara Vasu; Guṇarāja Khān was a title given by the emperor of Gauḍa. The generations are as follows:

1. Daśaratha Vasu, 2. Kuśala, 3. Śubhaśaṅkara, 4. Haṁsa, 5. Śaktirāma (Bāgāṇḍā), 5. Muktirāma (Māinagara), 5. Alaṅkāra (Baṅgaja).

5. Muktirāma, 6. Dāmodara, 7. Anantarāma, 8. Guṇīnāyaka, 8. Vīṇānāyaka.

8. Guṇīnāyaka, 9. Mādhava, 10. Lakṣmīnātha, 10. Cakrapāṇi, 10. Udayacāṅda, 10. Lauha, 10. Tauha, 10. Śrīpati, 10. Acyutānanda.

10. Śrīpati, 11. Yajñeśvara, 11. Trilocana, 11. Vaṭeśvara, 11. Prajāpati, 11. Īśāna, 11. Sāgara, 11. Kṛpārāma.

11. Yajñeśvara, 12. Bhagīratha, 12. Kāmeśvara, 12. Sadānanda, 12. Vaśiṣṭha.

12. Bhagīratha, 13. Mālādhara Vasu — title Guṇarāja Khān.

He had fourteen sons. The second of them, Lakṣmīnātha Vasu, bore the title Satyarāja Khān, and his son was Śrī Rāmānanda Vasu; so Śrī Rāmānanda Vasu is the fifteenth generation.

Śrī Mālādhara Vasu Mahāśaya was a very famous wealthy man. Judging by his fort, temples and so on, his estate was very prosperous. Here is one proof of Guṇarāja Khān Mahāśaya's social courage. Considering the custom of Kulīnism introduced by Ballāla to be hollow, he declined the request of his relative Purandara Khān (also of the Vasu line) and married his eldest son to the daughter of Śrīpati Datta Mahāśaya, of the thirteenth generation of the line of Śrī Puruṣottama Datta, who had come from Kānyakubja” (collected by Śrīmad Bhaktivinoda Ṭhākura in the winter of 1292 at the Śrī Kulīnagrāma *pāṭa*).

## 106

> Subheading: The Lord gives the marks of a “*kaniṣṭha* Vaiṣṇava” —

Know as a Vaiṣṇava one who has faith that by the name of Śrī Kṛṣṇa alone every perfection is attained, for such faith gives the initial qualification for being a Vaiṣṇava. Still, because his faith in Kṛṣṇa's name is as yet soft, he does not chant the name constantly. Śrī Rūpa Gosvāmī Prabhu writes in his *Upadeśāmṛta*: “One on whose tongue is the name ‘Kṛṣṇa’ should be honored in the mind; if he has initiation…”[^106-1] There are those who know that the holy name is a transcendental touchstone, Kṛṣṇa Caitanya Himself, the embodiment of *rasa*, complete, pure, eternally free and not different from the Named, and they worship [the Lord] with great faith. Yet because of their conditioned state they do not deliberate on the conclusions of *bhakti* and so cannot understand that the components of *bhakti* and the pure devotee are wholly transcendental. They too, by serving the pure devotees and Śrī Guru and by hearing the conclusions of pure *bhakti* from their lips, are gradually freed from all sins and attain transcendental realization, or divine knowledge of *sambandha*. (*Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam* 11.2.47): “One who faithfully worships Hari only in the Deity, but not His devotees or others, is known as a materialistic devotee.” In the teachings to Śrī Sanātana (Madhya 22.64, 67): “One who has faith is qualified for *bhakti*; according to faith there are the topmost, the middle and the neophyte.” “One whose faith is soft is a neophyte; gradually he will become a topmost devotee. *Bhakti* rises by degrees according to the degree of *rati* and *prema*.”

“The best of all” means the best in all Devī-dhāma. A neophyte devotee who worships with a *mantra* consisting of Śrī Viṣṇu's name is superior even to a pious *karmī* or a *jñānī*. For the *karmī* or *jñānī*, however exalted, does not believe that Śrī Viṣṇu is the Reality and the eternal object of service. So, though they accept the Vedas in word, they are in fact atheists. But one who worships Śrī Viṣṇu — however little greatness he may have in the realm of transcendental *bhajana* — has at least heard from Śrī Guru that the Deity of Śrī Viṣṇu is His real, true form and has faith in Him.

## 107

> Subheading: The glory of the fruit of a single name of Kṛṣṇa —

The nine kinds of *bhakti* (*Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam* 7.5.23): “Hearing about Viṣṇu and chanting about Him, remembering Him, serving His feet, worshiping Him, offering Him prayers, serving Him, befriending Him and offering Him one's very self — such is devotion with nine characteristics, if a person offers it to Viṣṇu.”

One who avoids offenses against the name and takes shelter of the name of Kṛṣṇa alone has all his sins destroyed, and all his mundane desires for enjoyment, rooted in piety and sin, perish. One who takes the holy name is the best of all and worthy of honor. The nine kinds of *bhakti* reach fullness only through *bhajana* of the holy name (“Even if other forms of *bhakti* are performed in Kali, they must be joined with the *bhakti* called *kīrtana*,” *Bhakti-sandarbha* 173).

## 108

> Subheading: The holy name, Lord Kṛṣṇa Himself, does not depend on other acts —

*Dīkṣā.* A statement of the Āgamas cited by Śrī Jīva Prabhu in the *Bhakti-sandarbha* (283): “Because it gives divine knowledge and completely destroys sin, teachers who know the truth call it *dīkṣā*.” Paṇḍitas versed in the scriptures of truth have precisely defined *dīkṣā* as that from which transcendental, divine knowledge awakens and sin is completely destroyed.

The rule of *dīkṣā* (a statement of the Āgamas cited in the *Hari-bhakti-vilāsa*, second *vilāsa*, and in the *Bhakti-sandarbha* 283): “Twice-born men who have not received the sacred thread have no right to their duties, to Vedic study and so on; they gain it only after *upanayana*. So too here the uninitiated have no right to worship the *mantra*, the Deity and so on. Therefore one should sanctify oneself with auspiciousness.” As a *brāhmaṇa* without *upanayana* has no right to his duties, to study and so on, and gains it only after taking the sacred thread, so an uninitiated person has no right to worship the deity of the *mantra* and so on. Therefore one who seeks the highest good should accept *dīkṣā* to sanctify himself with auspiciousness. For it is said (a statement of the *Viṣṇu-yāmala* cited in the *Hari-bhakti-vilāsa*, second *vilāsa*): “O fair-thighed one, all that an uninitiated person does is fruitless. A person without *dīkṣā* takes birth in an animal womb.” (A statement of the Yāmala, or Āgama, cited in the same *Hari-bhakti-vilāsa* and in the *Bhakti-sandarbha* 283): “Therefore, having thus bowed to the *guru* and offered him everything, one should take a Vaiṣṇava *mantra* by rule, receiving *dīkṣā*.” (A statement of the *Tattva-sāgara* cited in the *Bhakti-sandarbha* 298): “As bell metal turns to gold by the alchemical process, so by the process of *dīkṣā* men attain the status of the twice-born.”

*Puraścaryā* (a statement of the *Agastya-saṁhitā* cited in the *Hari-bhakti-vilāsa*, seventeenth *vilāsa*): “Daily worship at the three junctures of the day, *japa*, *tarpaṇa*, *homa* and feeding *brāhmaṇas* — this is called *puraścaraṇa*. For the perfection of a *mantra* received by the *guru*'s grace, this fivefold worship is prescribed beforehand, according to rule.” Morning, noon and evening — at these three times — daily worship, daily *japa*, daily *tarpaṇa*, daily *homa* and daily feeding of *brāhmaṇas*: these five parts are called *puraścaraṇa*. For the perfection of a *mantra* obtained by the *guru*'s grace, the fivefold worship is prescribed first of all; that is why it is called *puraścaraṇa*.

The rule of *puraścaryā* (a statement of the Āgamas cited in the *Hari-bhakti-vilāsa*, seventeenth *vilāsa*): “Without it a *mantra* will not be perfected even in hundreds of years; by performing it the *sādhaka* obtains the desired fruit.[^108-1] A *mantra* endowed with *puraścaraṇa* bears fruit. Therefore one who knows the *mantra* and desires perfection should perform the preliminary rite. The preliminary rite is said to be the chief potency of *mantras*. As an embodied being without strength is unfit for any act, so is a *mantra* declared to be without *puraścaraṇa*.”

Śrī Jīva Prabhu (*Bhakti-sandarbha* 283): “According to the *Śrī Bhāgavata*, the path of Deity worship is not obligatory as it is in the *Pañcarātra* and similar works, for it is stated that the goal of human life is attained even without it, by just one [form of *bhakti*] such as surrender. Still, those who follow the path of Śrī Nārada and others and wish to establish, through the rite of *dīkṣā* given at the feet of Śrī Guru, a special relationship with the Lord, certainly perform Deity worship once they have received *dīkṣā*.” And (ibid. 284): “Although in essence there is no (dependence on *dīkṣā* and the like), people, through their bond with the body and so on, are usually by nature ill-behaved and distracted in mind. To restrain these [tendencies], the venerable sages and others have set some restrictions here and there on the path of Deity worship.” In the *Rāmārcana-candrikā*: “Even without *dīkṣā*, O best of *brāhmaṇas*, even without *puraścaryā*, even without the rite of *nyāsa* — by *japa* alone [the name] bestows perfection.”

The name does not depend on the rules of *dīkṣā*. The *mantras* of the *Pañcarātra* awaken transcendental knowledge and destroy absorption in the mundane. When [one's vision] becomes transcendental, one understands that the *mantra* and its deity are not different. If one regards the name and the *mantra* as “ordinary sound” (like other everyday sounds invented by the mind to please the senses), one ends up in hell. The deity of the *mantra* is to be worshiped only with transcendental understanding. The rule is to receive a *mantra* through *dīkṣā*, as the scriptures prescribe. But the name of Kṛṣṇa is precious to both the bound and the liberated: by taking Kṛṣṇa's name the bound soul is freed from mundane understanding, and once liberated he can take the pure name of Kṛṣṇa. “From the Kṛṣṇa-mantra will come release from saṁsāra, and by Kṛṣṇa’s name You will attain Kṛṣṇa’s feet” (see Ādi 7.73). The name of Kṛṣṇa, not different from Kṛṣṇa, is the great *mantra* itself, and so it is not subject to any injunction of the *Pañcarātra*.

The name does not depend on the rules of *puraścaryā*. *Puraścaraṇa* is prescribed for the perfection of a *mantra*; the holy name, the great *mantra*, needs no such rule of *puraścaraṇa*. Since a single utterance of the name brings every fruit attainable by *puraścaryā*, the holy name has no need of *puraścaraṇa*.

The name delivers by touching the tongue. Here “tongue” must be understood as a tongue inclined to service. Otherwise — since offenses dwell on a tongue inclined to mundane enjoyment — the name of Śrī Kṛṣṇa will never arise on it. (*Bhakti-rasāmṛta-sindhu*, Pūrva-vibhāga, *Sādhana-bhakti-laharī*): “Therefore the name of Śrī Kṛṣṇa and all that is His cannot be grasped by the senses. Only when the tongue and the other senses are inclined to service does He Himself appear on them.” Madhya 17.134: “Therefore Kṛṣṇa's name, form and pastimes cannot be grasped by mundane senses; they are self-manifest.” (See *Bhakti-sandarbha* 256–275[^108-2] and 283.) See Antya 3.59–69, 75, 80, 176–180, 182–187; 20.11, 13; *Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam* 1.1.14; 6.2.29, 39.

> Subheading: (A verse by Śrīdhara Svāmī cited in the *Padyāvalī*, 29) —

**śrī-kṛṣṇa-nāmātmakaḥ** **ayaṁ** **mantraḥ** **kṛta-cetasāṁ** (of the liberated) **sumahatāṁ** (great — beyond the three modes; with the reading *sumanasām*, of the wise) **ākṛṣṭiḥ** (the attractor; with the reading *ākṛṣṭī-kṛta-cetasām*, of those whose hearts are attracted) **aṁhasām** (of piety and sin — acts born of absorption in the mundane) **uccāṭanam** (uprooting) **ā-caṇḍālam** (down to the *caṇḍālas*) **amūka-loka-sulabhaḥ** (that is, easily attainable by people who are not mute — all who have the power of speech) **mukti-śriyaḥ** (of the wealth of liberation — the touchstone in which liberation takes shelter) **vaśyaḥ** (bringing under control) **ca**; (and this great *mantra*, the name) **dīkṣām** (the practice that destroys sin and gives divine knowledge) **sat-kriyām** (the *dakṣiṇā* for the perfection of the fruit) **puraścaryāṁ** **ca** (the fivefold worship) **manāk** (slightly) **api** **na** **īkṣate** (does not look for)
