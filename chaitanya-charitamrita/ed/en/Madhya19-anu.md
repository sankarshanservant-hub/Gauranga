## 17

*Bhāgavata-vicāra*: discussing the *Bhāgavatam*. Knowledge is of “two” kinds (Muṇḍaka Upaniṣad 1.1.4–5): “The knowers of Brahman, as is well known, say that two kinds of knowledge are to be known, the higher and the lower. Of these, the lower is the Ṛg Veda, the Yajur Veda, the Sāma Veda, the Atharva Veda, *śikṣā*, *kalpa*, *vyākaraṇa*, *nirukta*, *chandas* and *jyotiṣa*. And the higher is that by which the Imperishable is attained.” The higher knowledge is spoken of in the *Brahma-sūtra*, or Vedānta. Vedāntists who desire liberation are as full of deceit (*kaitava*) as those who desire *dharma*, *artha* and *kāma*. Therefore everything the scriptures of the lower and the higher knowledge say with an intent toward liberation, which opposes pure *bhakti*, is full of deceit. But the scripture *Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam* is not like that. *Karmīs* liable to Yama's punishment, or *ahaṅgrahopāsakas* (who worship themselves as God), are wholly unfit to discuss the *Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam*. Only the Vaiṣṇavas, discussing the *Bhāgavatam*, are freed from *saṁsāra* by the power of *bhakti*. “The *Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam* is the spotless Purāṇa, dear to the Vaiṣṇavas. In it is sung the one pure, supreme knowledge of the *paramahaṁsas*. In it freedom from *karma* (*naiṣkarmya*) is revealed, together with knowledge, renunciation and *bhakti*. A person who hears it with devotion, reads it properly and reflects on it is liberated.” (Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam 12.13.18)

## 61

> Subheading: Vallabha Bhaṭṭa meets the Lord —

Vallabha Bhaṭṭa was the son of Lakṣmaṇa Dīkṣita of the village of Kāṅkaḍavāḍa, or Kākũrapāḍa, 16 miles from Niḍāḍāvalu railway station in the land of Trailaṅga. The Āndhra *brāhmaṇas* have five divisions: Vellanāṭī, Vegī-nāṭī, Muraki-nāṭī, Telagu-nāṭī and Kāśala-nāṭī. Of these, Śrī Vallabhācārya was born in a family of Vellanāṭī Āndhra *brāhmaṇas* in Śaka 1400. Some say that even before Vallabha's birth his father took *sannyāsa* and left home, and later returned home again and obtained Vallabhācārya as his son.

According to another view, in Vikrama Saṁvat 1535, that is, Śaka 1400, on the dark *ekādaśī* of the month of Caitra, Vallabhācārya appeared in “Campakāraṇya” as the son of Lakṣmaṇa Bhaṭṭa Dīkṣita, who belonged to a Vellanāṭī *brāhmaṇa* family of the land of Trailaṅga and bore the title “Khambhampāṭīvāru.” According to yet another view, he appeared in the village of Cā̃pājhāra near Rājima station on the B. N. R. line in Madhya Pradesh. He lived in Kāśī until the age of eleven, and when, his studies completed, he was returning home, on the way, at Śeṣādri, he heard of his father's death. Leaving his brother and mother at home, he went to Vidyānagara on the bank of the Tuṅgabhadrā and delighted Kṛṣṇadeva, the grandson of King Bukka. After this he spent eighteen years in three *digvijayas* of six years each. At the age of thirty, in Kāśī, he married a *brāhmaṇa* girl of his own caste named “Mahālakṣmī.” After installing a Deity on the plateau of Govardhana Hill, he settled in the village of Āḍāila near Prayāga. He had two sons, Gopīnātha and Viṭhṭhaleśvara. In his last years he accepted *tridaṇḍi-sannyāsa*, and in Śaka 1452 he left this world in Vārāṇasī. Besides a few of Vallabha's books, such as the *Ṣoḍaśa-grantha*, the *Anubhāṣya* on the *Brahma-sūtra* and the *Subodhinī* commentary on the *Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam*, there are many more books of his.

Bhagavān possesses unlimited energies. A *jīva* who has pious merit (*sukṛti*) receives the energy of mercy from Bhagavān, the possessor of energies. Fallen into the jaws of *māyā*, the *jīva*, because of ignorance, that is, aversion to Kṛṣṇa, remains uninitiated into the truth of *sambandha*, *abhidheya* and *prayojana*. Bhagavān Gaurahari mercifully first bestowed on Śrī Rūpa Gosvāmī the power to understand the truth and then taught him the truth.

## 128

*Sthūla-bhikṣā*: alms after which one need not beg any other food from others to fill the stomach.

*Mādhukarī*: a bee gathers honey from many flowers and carries it to the hive. In the same way some fill their stomach by gathering a little food in many places; their way of life is called “*mādhukarī*.”

*Bhoga parihari* (“giving up enjoyment”): for pleasure people use stimulating substances that increase sensual urges. Giving all that up, they kept themselves alive on dry bread and roasted chickpeas, to preserve a body fit for *bhajana*.

## 131

In such a life of renunciation they served Kṛṣṇa in various ways: sometimes by writing scriptures on *bhakti-rasa*, sometimes by *nāma-saṅkīrtana*, and sometimes by remembering Gaura's pastimes, meditating on them and so on. Among the *prākṛta-sahajiyās* and the like there is a firm belief that the “*sādhana*” of *bhakti* is to give up writing, reading and other study of the scriptures on *bhakti* and, in order to cultivate one's own ignorance, to stop studying the *śāstras* and so on. The true followers of Śrī Rūpa put no faith in such talk. But if a *sādhaka* writes and reads the *śāstras* and so on with some petty side goal, out of a desire to earn money, for gratifying the material senses, for worldly fame or worship, for gain or some other petty purpose, which is called the *upaśākhās* (side branches), then such a person, given to fallen conduct, never attains any good. A genuine follower of Śrī Rūpa has no such karmic desires rooted in enjoying petty fruits.
