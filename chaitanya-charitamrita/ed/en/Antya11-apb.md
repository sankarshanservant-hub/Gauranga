# Amṛta-pravāha-bhāṣya on Antya-līlā 11

## 0

**Summary.** In this chapter, when Brahma Haridāsa Ṭhākura[^0-1] gave up his body with Mahāprabhu's permission, Mahāprabhu carried him with special devotion and great ceremony to the seashore and laid him in *samādhi* there. With His own hands He covered him with sand and raised a *cautārā* (platform) over him. Then He bathed in the ocean, Himself begged alms and held the festival of Haridāsa's departure (*vijaya-mahotsava*).

## 1

I bow to Haridāsa, and I bow to his Lord, that Caitanyadeva who danced holding in His arms the body Haridāsa had left.

## 20

*Rañca* — a particle.

## 32

*Sei līlā* (“that pastime”) — the pastime of Your departure (*antardhāna-līlā*).

## 56

*Utkrāmaṇa* — going out, exit.

## 77

*Pichāḍā* — people who walk behind (according to another opinion, “a basket” or “a load”; see verse 79).

## 78

*Puñjā* — a portion counted out in fours.

## 101

*Haridāsera vijaya* (Haridāsa's departure). If in Śrī Puruṣottama-kṣetra one goes from Ṭoṭā-Gopīnātha to the seashore, there, right by the ocean, the *samādhi* of Ṭhākura Haridāsa stands to this day. Every year, on the day of Ananta-caturdaśī, the festival of Ṭhākura Haridāsa's departure (*vijaya-utsava*) is held.

*Thus ends the eleventh chapter of the “Amṛta-pravāha-bhāṣya.”*

---

[^0-1]: Haridāsa Ṭhākura is regarded as the incarnation of Brahmā who appeared together with Prahlāda (Brahmā being the son of the sage Ṛcīka): *Gaura-gaṇoddeśa-dīpikā*, 93.
