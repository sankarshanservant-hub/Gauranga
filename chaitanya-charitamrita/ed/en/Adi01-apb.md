# Amṛta-pravāha-bhāṣya on Ādi-līlā 1

## 0

**Summary.** The chapter opens with fourteen verses that establish the truth (*tattva*). In verses 15–17 the author offers the *maṅgalācaraṇa*, the auspicious invocation, glorifying Śrī Madana-mohana, Śrī Govinda and Śrī Gopīnātha. The first of the fourteen verses is a general obeisance to six truths, and the whole chapter is nothing but its detailed explanation. The word “guru” means both the *dīkṣā-guru*, who gives initiation, and the *śikṣā-guru*, who instructs. The disciple must regard them as a manifestation (*prakāśa*) of Kṛṣṇa. The Lord's devotees (*īśa-bhaktas*) are of two kinds: *siddhas*, who have reached perfection, and *sādhakas*, who are still on the path of practice (*sādhana*). The Lord (*Īśa*) is Kṛṣṇa in His original form, His *svayaṁ-rūpa*, together with His *kāya-vyūhas*, the expansions of His body. The avatāras are of three kinds: *aṁśa-avatāras*, *guṇa-avatāras* and *śaktyāveśa-avatāras*. In this connection the chapter examines the truth of Kṛṣṇa's *prakāśa*, and with it the truth of *vilāsa*. Kṛṣṇa has three kinds of potency (*śakti*): the Lakṣmīs in Vaikuṇṭha and other abodes, the queens in Dvārakā, and, best of all, the gopīs of Vraja. Kṛṣṇa and His *kāya-vyūhas* are *īśa-tattva*, the truth of the Lord. All the devotees are *āvaraṇa-tattva*, His surrounding retinue, and are therefore a special potency of His. A potency and its possessor are eternally one when they are seen as non-different, and eternally distinct when the potency is seen apart from its possessor. Thus His inconceivable potency establishes one undivided Truth. This conclusion agrees with Vedānta, and its name is *acintya-bhedābheda-tattva*, the truth of inconceivable oneness and difference. The last part of the chapter tells of the true nature (*svarūpa*) and greatness of Śrī Caitanya and Nityānanda.

Śrī Caitanya, Nityānanda, Advaita — the root of *prema* —
Haridāsa, Svarūpa Gosvāmī,
Śrī Vaṁśīvadanānanda, Sārvabhauma, Rāmānanda,
the two brothers Rūpa and Sanātana;
Śrī Jīva, Gopāla Bhaṭṭa, Raghunātha — Dāsa and Bhaṭṭa,
Śivānanda, Kavi Karṇapūra,
Narottama, Śrīnivāsa, Rāmacandra, Kṛṣṇadāsa,
Baladeva, and Cakravartī, the foremost.
I have bowed with care to the Lord and His devotees,
and in the *Amṛta-pravāha-bhāṣya*, which gives the essence,
I have explained the *Caitanya-caritāmṛta* at length.
O devotees, weigh it well!
The stories of Gaura are an ocean; Kṛṣṇadāsa sailed upon it
and brought back a stream of nectar.
Vaiṣṇavas drink the nectar of this poem, their hearts grow peaceful,
and they long to drink it again and again.
Everyone told me, poor and destitute as I am,
to compose a commentary on it.
I took the sādhus' command upon my head, composed this commentary with care,
and placed it in the sādhus' hands.

## 15

I am lame and dull-witted. My only refuge is the supremely merciful Śrī Śrī Rādhā-Madana-mohana, and Their lotus feet are all my wealth. May They be glorified![^15-1]

## 16

Śrī Śrī Rādhā-Govinda sit on a throne in a jewelled temple, beneath the wish-fulfilling tree of Vṛndāvana, a land of radiant beauty. Their dear companions serve Them. I remember Them.

## 17

Śrīmān Gopīnātha, who begins the rapturous *rāsa* dance, stands on the bank by Vaṁśīvaṭa and draws the gopīs with the sound of His flute. May He grant us what is auspicious.

## 19

These three Deities — Śrī Madana-mohana, Śrī Govindadeva and Śrī Gopīnātha — are the presiding Lords of Vṛndāvana. They gave the Gauḍīya devotees the right to serve each of Them, and so made them Their own.

## 31

What the scriptures establish about Kṛṣṇa, who appeared as Caitanya.

## 34

I worship the two gurus, distinguished as the *dīkṣā-guru* and the *śikṣā-guru*; the Lord's devotees, Śrīvāsa and others; the Lord's avatāras, Advaita Prabhu and others; the Lord's manifestations, Prabhu Śrī Nityānanda and others; and the Lord's potencies, Śrī Gadādhara and others. And I worship the Supreme Truth, the Lord Himself, named Mahāprabhu Śrī Kṛṣṇa Caitanya.

## 35

*Tāṅhāra* (“his”): the singular is used because both kinds of guru are regarded as one truth. A variant reading has *tāṅ-sabāra* (“of them all”).

## 43

*Āvaraṇa*, the surrounding retinue: the devotees who surround the Lord on every side are His *āvaraṇa*. I have bowed to Him together with this retinue. The six truths — the guru, the Lord's devotee, the Lord's avatāra, the Lord's manifestation, the Lord's potency, and the Lord Himself, Kṛṣṇa Caitanya — are His own *svarūpa*. Now I shall consider how this is so.

## 44

All *jīvas* are servants of Kṛṣṇa, so my guru too is in truth Kṛṣṇa's servant. Even so, I shall know my guru as Kṛṣṇa's manifestation (*prakāśa*). For the disciple, the guru is the very form of Kṛṣṇa's manifestation. But Nityānanda, who is Baladeva, is in truth *prakāśa-tattva* whose nature is *vilāsa*.

## 46

Bhagavān said to Uddhava: “O Uddhava, know the guru to be My very self. Do not regard the guru with *asūyā*, that is, with disrespect, thinking him an ordinary man. The guru embodies all the gods.”

## 48

O Lord! The wise are filled with bliss when they remember You, yet even with a lifespan like Brahmā's they could not repay You. For in Your boundless mercy You abide outside as the ācārya and within as the indwelling Lord (*antaryāmī*). You do this to destroy all that is inauspicious in embodied souls and to reveal to them their own goal.

[^15-1]: Bhaktivinoda gives only the glorification here; the Russian verse translation (VCD) adds a prayer for attachment to the lotus feet.
