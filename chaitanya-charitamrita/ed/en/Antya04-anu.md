## 63

> Subheading: Rukmiṇī reveals her *anurāga* to Vāsudeva —

Rukmiṇī, the daughter of Bhīṣmaka, had heard from people of Śrī Kṛṣṇa's countless excellent qualities and chose Him as her husband. But she learned that her elder brother Rukmī, hostile to Kṛṣṇa, had chosen Śiśupāla, king of Cedi, as her bridegroom. Then in seclusion she wrote a letter full of love and at once sent it to Śrī Kṛṣṇa with a trustworthy *brāhmaṇa*. The *brāhmaṇa* arrived in Dvārakā, Śrī Kṛṣṇa received him with all due honor, and with Śrī Kṛṣṇa's permission the *brāhmaṇa* began to read Rukmiṇī's letter:

**he ambujākṣa** (O lotus-eyed One), **ātmanaḥ** (their own) **tamaḥ** (ignorance) **apahatyai** (to destroy) **umā-patiḥ** (Śiva) **iva** **mahāntaḥ** (Brahmā and others) **yasya** (Your) **aṅghri-paṅkaja-rajaḥ-snapanam** (bathing in the dust of the lotus feet) **vāñchanti**, (that) **bhavat-prasādam** (Your favor) **yarhi** **aham** **na labheya** (if I do not obtain, then) **vrata-kṛśān** (wasted by vows — fasting and the like) **asūn** (my life) **jahyām** (I shall give up; in this way) **śata-janmabhiḥ** (even after a hundred births, Your favor) **syāt**.

## 64

> Subheading: The *gopīs*, eager for the *rāsa* dance, reveal their *anurāga* to Śrī Kṛṣṇa —

On an autumn night bathed in moonlight, the wives of the cowherds, drawn by the sound of Śrī Kṛṣṇa's flute, came to Śrī Kṛṣṇa, forgetting themselves. Wishing to increase their *anurāga* still more, He told them to go home. Then the *gopīs*, whose lives were given to Kṛṣṇa, grew sorrowful and, their voices choked and faltering, said to Kṛṣṇa:

**he aṅga** (O Kṛṣṇa), **tvad-adharāmṛta-pūrakeṇa** (with the flood of nectar from Your lips) **naḥ** (our) **hāsāvaloka-kala-gīta-ja-hṛc-chayāgnim** (the glance with a smile and the sweet song — the sweet sound of the flute; what is born of these and lies, that is, dwells, in the heart is the *hṛc-chaya*, love's desire; that itself is the fire, the thing that burns — that) **siñca** (quench); **no cet**, **he sakhe**, **vayaṁ** **viraha-jāgny-upayukta-dehāḥ** (our bodies consumed, that is, burned, by the fire born of separation; being such, like *yogīs*) **dhyānena** **te** (Your) **padayoḥ** **padavīm** (nearness) **yāma** (we shall reach).

## 70

> Subheading: From *abhidheya* come *sambandha* and *prayojana* —

***Nava-vidhā bhakti*** (the nine kinds of devotion): “Hearing about Viṣṇu, chanting, remembering, serving His feet, worship, offering prayers, servitude, friendship and offering oneself.” (Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam 7.5.23) The nine kinds of devotion (*abhidheya*) hold the great power to bestow love for Kṛṣṇa (*prayojana*) and Kṛṣṇa Himself (*sambandha*). *Sādhana-bhakti* manifests as *abhidheya* and later attains its true nature, *prema-bhakti*. And love for Kṛṣṇa, the *prayojana*, bestows Kṛṣṇa in full.

## 71

> Subheading: Love for Kṛṣṇa is attained only as the fruit of unbroken, untiring *kīrtana* of Kṛṣṇa free of the ten offenses —

Śrī Jīva Gosvāmī Prabhu writes in the *Bhakti-sandarbha* (270): “The *śrutis*, the Purāṇas and other scriptures proclaim that this *bhakti* called *kīrtana* is full of Bhagavān's boundless compassion, directed exclusively to people poor in wealth, birth, qualities and deeds. <…> Therefore in Kali, when people are by their very nature utterly wretched, it appears among them and, effortlessly granting them the whole fruit of the great *sādhanas* of each age, brings them to fulfillment. That is why in Kali Bhagavān is especially pleased by it alone.” And in the same work (273): “Therefore, even if other forms of *bhakti* are performed in Kali, they should be performed only in conjunction with it (with *kīrtana*).”

Śrī Rūpa Prabhu (*Nāmāṣṭaka* 1): “O Hari-nāma! The tips of Your lotus feet are worshiped, as if with *ārati* lamps, by the radiance of the garland of jewels on the crests of all the *śrutis*. Hosts of liberated souls worship You. I take shelter of You completely.”

Śrī Sanātana Prabhu (*Bṛhad-bhāgavatāmṛta*, ch. 1, verse 9): “All glory, all glory to the name of Murāri, the embodiment of bliss! It puts an end to the labors of one's own *dharma*, of meditation, worship and the like. Taken up somehow even once, it grants liberation to living beings. It is the supreme nectar, my only life and my ornament.”

“O king, for those who are disgusted with the world, for those who seek what they desire and for *yogīs*, one thing has been ascertained: constant chanting of Hari's name — in it there is no fear at all.” (Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam 2.1.11)

“*Bhakti-yoga*, devotion to Bhagavān beginning with chanting His name, is declared the supreme *dharma* for human beings in this world.” (Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam 6.3.22)

Gaurahari (in His own *Śikṣāṣṭaka* 3): “Humbler than a blade of grass, more tolerant than a tree, seeking no honor and honoring others, one should always chant Hari's name.”

***Niraparādhe***: that is, free of the ten offenses against the Name, constantly, or untiringly, devoted to serving the Name. For the ten offenses against the Name, see the *Amṛta-pravāha-bhāṣya* and the *Anubhāṣya* on Ādi 8.24.

## 79–81

Through Śrī Sanātana Gosvāmī, Śrī Mahāprabhu, first, had him write the *Śrī Bṛhad-bhāgavatāmṛta* and so established the truth about the devotee, devotion and love for Kṛṣṇa. Second, He had him compile the *Śrī Hari-bhakti-vilāsa* and so established the duties and conduct of Vaiṣṇavas. Third, through Sanātana Gosvāmī's wonderful deeds He introduced in Śrī Vṛndāvana the service of Śrī Vigraha, and through his exemplary character, full of the bliss of *bhajana*, the worship of Vraja (*vraja-bhajana*) within the mind. Fourth, by restoring the *kuṇḍas* and other lost holy places, and by his exemplary devotional life full of the taste of *bhakti* and renunciation, He taught how to live a detached life far from worldly objects, as a pure devotee should be emulated. Śrī Mathurā and Vṛndāvana are a land supremely dear to Śrī Gaurasundara. By settling Śrī Sanātana in that land, the Lord wished to propagate through him all the *dharmas* mentioned above.

## 172

> Subheading: Out of humility a devotee free of pride (*amānī*) regards himself as a material *jīva*, the lowest of all. Yet in truth, seen with spiritual vision, he is a transcendental spiritual entity (*brahma-vastu*) whom Bhagavān embraces —

## 173

A devotee turned toward Kṛṣṇa allows himself no bodily license (*kāmacāra*) to satisfy the desire to enjoy, the desire for his own happiness. He longs for Kṛṣṇa's happiness and performs all his transcendental worship (*bhajana*) solely for loving service to Kṛṣṇa. The *karmīs* engage the material body, the vessel in which the fruits of action are enjoyed, in enjoying perishable fruits. Devotees have no such aims: wholly and always they accept that their bodies exist only for the service of Hari, and with every bodily act they serve Śrī Kṛṣṇa. The *karmī's* body is material, because, absorbed in material nature (*prakṛti*), he craves to enjoy material fruits. But a devotee is wholly devoted to Kṛṣṇa's service: the very existence of his body and all his bodily acts are devoted to transcendental service to Kṛṣṇa. Therefore the devotee's spiritual (*cinmaya*) body is certainly transcendental (*aprākṛta*).

The *karmīs*, averse to Kṛṣṇa, consider the body of a pure devotee “material,” like their own material bodies, which serve their enjoyment. But pure devotees and their servants never consider the body of a pure devotee “material.” In other words, they do not regard as “equal” or “one” spirit and non-spirit, the transcendental and the material, what is beyond rules and what is subject to them — or Kṛṣṇa and *māyā*, which is other than Kṛṣṇa. They do not invoke, under the pretense of artificial broad-mindedness or impartiality, the doctrine that reconciles spirit and matter (*cij-jaḍa-samanvaya-vāda*), and so they never become offenders against the holy Name. Rather, they know that the pure devotee's body, full of spiritual bliss, is transcendental in nature, and regard it as fit for Kṛṣṇa's service.

The highest devotee (*uttama-adhikārī*) finds no love for Kṛṣṇa in his own experience, and so he considers himself a pauper, a material *jīva*. But people averse to Kṛṣṇa in the *prākṛta-sahajiyā* and other offshoot sects (*upasampradāyas*) foolishly consider their own material bodies to be “transcendental Vaiṣṇava bodies” — and are cast far away from the pure Vaiṣṇava's transcendental conduct, that is, from devotion. Seeing this, Ṭhākura Bhaktivinoda, to teach people, wrote in his book *Kalyāṇa-kalpataru*: “If I think, ‘I am a Vaiṣṇava,’ I shall never be free of pride. The desire for prestige will come and defile my heart, and I shall go to hell. Thinking myself the best and giving out my remnants and the like, I shall be weighed down by pride. Therefore I shall always remain your disciple and accept worship from no one.” And Kavirāja Gosvāmī has written (Antya 20.28): “Such is the nature of love: where there is a relation of love, one thinks, ‘I have not even a trace of devotion to Kṛṣṇa.’”

## 174

> Subheading: In the transcendental realm beyond the *guṇas* there are no mundane deliberations about injunctions and prohibitions: these belong to the mind and arise from secondary, non-spiritual vision —

## 175

> Subheading: In mental notions based on the mundane perception of difference, which arises from secondary, non-spiritual vision, pure and impure, injunction and prohibition are all of equal worth and unreal — (*Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam* 11.28.4)

Bhagavān, while telling Uddhava about pure knowledge of Bhagavān, which He has described in detail before, condemns vision born of the senses (*akṣaja-darśana*):

(Since) **vācā** (by speech) **uditam** (what is expressed — and also what is seen by the eyes and the other senses, and what is) **manasā dhyātam** (conceived by the mind), **tat** (all of it) **eva ca** **anṛtam** (is perishable, not true for all time; therefore) **avastunaḥ** (of the unreal: whatever is other than nondual knowledge has no separate existence and cannot be accepted as reality) **dvaitasya** (within the world of duality) **kiṁ kiyat** (how much) **bhadram** (is good), **kim** (how much) **vā** **abhadram** (is bad)?

## 176

> Subheading: The plain essential meaning of the verse —

In the nondual Knowledge, in Vrajendra-nandana, imperishable Truth eternally abides. The *jīva* has become absorbed in a “second” and fallen into the hands of *māyā*, which is other than Kṛṣṇa. Everything such a *jīva* has — judging his own good or ill and the like — is only a property of the mind, whose nature is to accept and reject (*saṅkalpa-vikalpa*). Having forgotten his own true nature (*svarūpa*) and Kṛṣṇa, the *jīva* imagines himself an enjoyer. Therefore his attempts to distinguish good and bad by knowledge born of the senses produce all kinds of delusion.

## 177

> Subheading: (*Bhagavad-gītā* 5.18)

**vidyā-vinaya-sampanne** (endowed with learning and humility — joined with them, shining with every *brāhmaṇa* virtue, and not in one who is foolish and ill-mannered) **brāhmaṇe** (in a *brāhmaṇa*), **śvapāke** (in a *caṇḍāla*, the lowest of all), **gavi** (in the pure cow), **śuni** (in the impure dog), **hastini** (in the elephant, which knows no distinction of pure and impure) **paṇḍitāḥ** (those who know what bondage and liberation are) **sama-darśinaḥ** (those whose nature is to see the same thing, Brahman, in all; that is, those who regard all alike).

## 178

> Subheading: Only a *gosvāmī* — a pure devotee who follows *yukta-vairāgya* — sees all things equally, because everywhere he sees their relation to Kṛṣṇa — (*Bhagavad-gītā* 6.8)

**jñāna-vijñāna-tṛptātmā** (*jñāna* is knowledge from instruction, *vijñāna* is direct realization; one whose self, that is, whose mind, is satisfied by them and desires nothing more; therefore) **kūṭasthaḥ** (abiding at all times in one and the same nature, that is, unchanging; therefore) **vijitendriyaḥ** (one who has conquered the senses; therefore) **sama-loṣṭrāśma-kāñcanaḥ** (one to whom a lump of clay, a piece of stone and gold are equal, that is, who has no notion that clods of earth and the like are to be rejected or accepted) **yogī** **yuktaḥ** (established in yoga, *yogārūḍha*) **ucyate** (is called).

## 179

> Subheading: Only a *vidvat-sannyāsī*, who has attained *naiṣkarmya* and gone beyond mundane injunctions and prohibitions, or the topmost *mahā-bhāgavata*, sees Viṣṇu everywhere. Therefore his vision is beautiful (*sudarśana*): it has none of the partiality born of the mundane perception of difference —

To see all things alike is the *dharma* of a *sannyāsī*, a *paṇḍita* or a Vaiṣṇava, for he is not absorbed in matter. He has no wish to inhale the fragrance of sandalwood to gratify his senses, nor any wish to avoid the stench of mud for his senses' pleasure. There are two impulses: to accept material things and to reject them. A Vaiṣṇava who follows *yukta-vairāgya* does not hasten to serve them, that is, to fall under their control. He is indifferent both to mundane enjoyment and to mundane renunciation, and so he is endowed with beautiful vision (*sudarśana*), the vision of spiritual pastimes (*cid-vilāsa*).

## 191

> Subheading: The Lord describes the transcendental nature of a Vaiṣṇava —

Śrī Gaurasundara explained to those who had taken shelter at His feet that a Vaiṣṇava's body is never material, never meant for enjoyment. It is not like the material body of a *karmī*, a *jñānī* or one with other desires (*anyābhilāṣī*), a body full of enjoyment and mundane pleasure. A devotee's body is full of spiritual bliss (*cid-ānanda-maya*), that is, fit for Kṛṣṇa's service and imbued with a feeling that lies beyond material nature. In it eternity, knowledge and bliss (*sac-cid-ānanda*) shine forth.

## 192

> Subheading: Viṣṇu has accepted the Vaiṣṇava as His own “*āśraya*,” and so the Vaiṣṇava is a spiritual pastime (*cid-vilāsa*) not different from Him; one initiated by the guru becomes Acyuta's own (*acyutātmatā*) —

## 193

> Subheading: It is the *brāhmaṇa* — one who is initiated, that is, who has gained knowledge of his relation with Bhagavān — who, engaging in *viṣṇu-bhakti-yoga* (the *abhidheya*), receives the name “Vaiṣṇava.” Therefore brahminhood is contained in Vaiṣṇavism —

At initiation (*dīkṣā*) the devotee gives up all his mundane perceptions and gains knowledge of his transcendental relation (*sambandha*) with the Lord. Having gained transcendental divine knowledge, he receives, in his transcendental nature, the right to serve Kṛṣṇa. As soon as the surrendered devotee leaves the shelter of *māyā*, which is other than Kṛṣṇa, Kṛṣṇa makes him His own. Then his mundane conception of himself as an “enjoyer” in the realm of material enjoyment vanishes, and in his very sense of self (*asmitā*) eternal service to Kṛṣṇa manifests. Then the devotee realizes that in his own true nature, full of eternity, knowledge and bliss, he is an eternal servant, and in a transcendental body he gains the right to serve Kṛṣṇacandra. The devotee serves in *bhāva* with a transcendental body suited to that time. But the *karmīs*, through the fault of mundane thinking, take even this transcendental service for material activity aimed at enjoyment, like their own. By that offense they are deprived of the mercy of the transcendental guru. On this, see Śrī Sanātana Prabhu's discussion in *Bṛhad-bhāgavatāmṛta* 1.3.45 and 2.3.139.

> Subheading: The guru and the Vaiṣṇavas, the associates of Bhagavān, are entities of Vaikuṇṭha, beyond the secondary properties of the senses —

To a sense of smell driven by a mind bent on mundane enjoyment, the body of Śrī Sanātana Gosvāmī — a *mahā-bhāgavata*, the crest jewel of the line of *paramahaṁsas* — may seem foul-smelling.
