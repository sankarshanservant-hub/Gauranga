# Amṛta-pravāha-bhāṣya on Madhya-līlā 5

## 0

**Summary.** Mahāprabhu passed through Yājapura and reached the city of Kaṭaka. There He went for *darśana* of Śrī Sākṣi-gopāla and heard from the mouth of Nityānanda Prabhu the story of Gopāla. Two brāhmaṇas of Vidyānagara (one old, the other young) had visited many holy places and come to Śrī Vṛndāvana. The old brāhmaṇa, pleased with the young one's service, promised to give him his daughter in marriage. The young brāhmaṇa had the old one repeat this promise before the Gopāla who resides in Vṛndāvana, and made Gopāla the witness. When they returned home and the young brāhmaṇa proposed the marriage, the old one, giving in to the urging of his son, wife and other relatives, said, “I do not remember my promise.” The young brāhmaṇa then went back to Gopāla, told Him everything, won His consent by his devotion, and brought Him to the southern country. Gopāla walked behind the young brāhmaṇa, sounding His ankle bells, came as far as the outskirts of Vidyānagara, and stopped there. The young brāhmaṇa gathered there the respectable people of the region, together with the old brāhmaṇa and his son, and had Gopāla give His testimony. Astonished, they arranged the marriage of the young brāhmaṇa to the old one's daughter. The king of that country, filled with devotion for Gopāla, built Him a temple and so on. Long afterward the king of Vidyānagara scornfully called Puruṣottama-deva, the lord of Utkala, “Jagannātha's sweeper” and refused to give him his daughter. Puruṣottama-deva then won the help of Śrī Jagannātha, made war on that king, defeated him, and took both his daughter and his kingdom. From that time Gopāla, bound by the rope of devotion of the Vaiṣṇava king Puruṣottama-deva, was brought to the city of Kaṭaka. Having heard this story, Mahāprabhu had *darśana* of Gopāla with great love. From Kaṭaka He came to Bhuvaneśvara for *darśana* of Śiva, and at Kamalapura, on the bank of the Bhārgī River, on the pretext of seeing Śiva as Kapoteśvara He left His staff in Nityānanda's hands and went away. Nityānanda broke the staff into three pieces and set it floating on the Bhārgī. When Mahāprabhu reached “Āṭhāranālā” and did not get His staff back, He left His companions behind and went on to the Śrī Mandira.

## 1

That Brahmaṇya-deva who, while in the form of a Deity (*pratimā*), walked on His own feet for the good of a brāhmaṇa to a land a hundred days' journey away — to that Sākṣi-gopāla of wondrous deeds I bow down.

## 3

***Yājapura*** is a special holy place in the land of Utkala, on the bank of the Vaitaraṇī River, in Virajā-kṣetra; it is “Nābhi-gayā.”

## 8

***Sākṣi-gopāla.*** Kaṭaka is the chief city on the bank of the Mahānadī, and at that time Sākṣi-gopāla resided there. When Sākṣi-gopāla was brought from the southern country, He first stayed in Kaṭaka for some days, and then for some time in the temple of Śrī Jagannātha at Śrī Puruṣottama. There a kind of loving quarrel (*prema-kalaha*) arose, and so the Mahārāja, the lord of Utkala, founded a village called “Satyavādī” three *krośas* from Puruṣottama and kept Gopāla there. Now Śrī Sākṣi-gopāla resides in that village, in a solidly built temple.

## 12

***The twelve forests.*** Bhadra, Bilva, Lauha, Bhāṇḍīra and Mahāvana — these five forests lie east of the Yamunā; Madhu, Tāla, Kumuda, Bahulā, Kāmya, Khadira and Vṛndāvana — these last seven forests lie west of the Yamunā. After seeing these twelve forests the brāhmaṇas finally went to the place called “Pañcakrośī Vṛndāvana.” The meaning is this: the Vṛndāvana that is one of the twelve forests begins from this Vṛndāvana and extends sixteen *krośas* up to Nandagrāma and Varṣāṇa; within it lies the village called “Pañcakrośī Vṛndāvana.”

## 44

Do not tell such a lie as “I did not say I would give my daughter”; say only, “I do not remember it.”

## 96

For the good of a brāhmaṇa You do all that is otherwise not fitting for You to do.[^96-1]

## 140

See the *Caitanya-bhāgavata*, Antya-līlā, Chapter 2. Leaving Kaṭaka by the royal road, from Bālihastā, or Bālakāṭi-caṭi, to Bhuvaneśvara is two or three *krośas*.

## 141

***The Bhārgī River*** is now known as the “Daṇḍabhāṅgā” River; it lies three *krośas* north of Purī.

## 142

***Kapoteśvara*** is near the Daṇḍabhāṅgā River.

---

[^96-1]: The translation of the verse differs: Vṛndāvana Candra dāsa renders it as a request — that the Lord do for this brāhmaṇa what He has never done before.
