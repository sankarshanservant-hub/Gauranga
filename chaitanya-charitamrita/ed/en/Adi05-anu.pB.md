## 58

> Subheading: The two forms of *māyā*, “*pradhāna*” and “*prakṛti*” —

**“*Pradhāna*” and “*prakṛti*”**: see Madhya 20.271. Śrī Jīva Prabhu writes in the *Paramātma-sandarbha* (section 49):

“This *māyā* has two portions. One is the efficient portion (*nimitta*): it consists of the *guṇas* and is called *māyā*. The other is the material portion (*upādāna*): it is substance and is called *pradhāna*. The difference between them is described in four verses (*Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam* 11.24.1–4). … (section 53) Elsewhere (*Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam* 10.63.26) the differences between these two, the material and the efficient, are also stated according to the difference in their functions: ‘Time, destiny, *karma*, the *jīva*, his nature, the subtle elements, the field, the vital air, the ego, the transformations, their aggregate, and the flow like seed and sprout — this is Your *māyā*. I take shelter of You, the limit of its negation.’ Here time, destiny, *karma* and nature are efficient portions; the rest are material portions. The *jīva*, who possesses them, is of a dual nature: thus a particle of the efficient potency is present within the material class as well. … (section 55) The potency well known by the name *māyā*, as the efficient portion, is seen to be threefold: knowledge, desire and action. … Now the characteristic of *pradhāna*, the material portion (*Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam* 3.26.10): ‘That which is made of the three *guṇas*, unmanifest, eternal, consisting of cause and effect, without distinctions and yet containing distinctions, they call *pradhāna* and *prakṛti*.’ That which is made of the three *guṇas* — the combination of the three *guṇas*, *sattva* and the others — is what they call the unmanifest, *pradhāna* and *prakṛti*. Why is it called unmanifest? It is ‘without distinctions’: the three *guṇas* are in balance in it, so its distinctions are not manifest; hence one understands why it is also called *avyākṛta*, the undeveloped. Why is it called *pradhāna*? It ‘contains distinctions’: the *mahat* and the other distinctions are effects of its own portions, and it is their shelter and therefore superior (*śreṣṭha*) to them. … The efficient portion is *māyā*; the material portion is *pradhāna*.”

Bhagavān's external potency, *māyā*, has two portions: the efficient portion, “*māyā* made of the *guṇas*,” and the material portion, “*pradhāna* made of substance.” The mutual difference between these two names is described in four verses of the twenty-fourth chapter of the eleventh canto of the *Bhāgavatam*. Elsewhere, in the twenty-sixth verse of the sixty-third chapter of the tenth canto, the two portions — material and efficient — are divided according to the difference in their functions: “O Bhagavān! Agitating ‘time,’ efficient ‘*karma*,’ ‘destiny,’ which brings forth the fruits, and its impression, ‘nature’ (*svabhāva*) — this is the conditioned *jīva*, marked by these four efficient portions of *māyā*. The subtle elements are ‘substance’; *prakṛti* is the ‘field’; the *sūtra* is the ‘vital air’; *ahaṅkāra* is the ‘self’; and the eleven senses and earth, water, fire, air and ether are the sixteen transformations; their total aggregate is the ‘body.’ From the body comes *karma* as a seed, and from *karma* the body as a sprout: this endless flow is ‘*māyā*.’ O Prabhu, You are the Truth at which all this is negated; You I worship.” “Although the *jīva* is a particle of the efficient potency, he possesses portions of both natures and so follows the material class as well.” The potency known by the word “*māyā*,” the efficient portion, has three divisions: “knowledge,” “desire” and “action.” The characteristic of “*pradhāna*,” the material portion: “That in which the three *guṇas* — *sattva*, *rajas* and *tamas* — are combined is called ‘the unmanifest,’ ‘*pradhāna*’ and ‘*prakṛti*.’ The reason for the name ‘unmanifest’ is this: it is without distinctions — the three *guṇas* are in balance in it — so its distinctive qualities are not manifest; therefore *pradhāna* also received the name *avyākṛta*. The reason for the name ‘*pradhāna*’: the *mahat-tattva* and the other distinctions are *māyā*'s own effects, and she, like a distinct object, is their shelter and is therefore superior to them.” … In the efficient portion, “*māyā*”; in the material portion, “*pradhāna*.”

## 59

> Subheading: *Māyā*, made of the *guṇas*, is never the chief cause of the world —

## 60

> Subheading: By the power of Bhagavān's glance *prakṛti* becomes the secondary cause of the world —

## 61

> Subheading: Bhagavān is the original cause of the world —

## 59–61

See Madhya 20.259–261. The external potency, *māyā*, is known in the material portion of the world by the names “*pradhāna*” and “*prakṛti*,” and in the efficient portion of the world by the name “*māyā*.” Inert *prakṛti* is not the cause of the world. For Kṛṣṇa, as Kāraṇārṇavaśāyī Mahā-Viṣṇu, grants *prakṛti* the material, substantial potency and so infuses her with power. The example is red-hot iron. Iron has no “power” to burn or give heat, but touched by fire, red-hot iron can burn and heat other things. In the same way inert *prakṛti*, which is like iron, has no independent capacity to be the substance or the material cause. Only when the glance-potency of Kāraṇodakaśāyī, who is like fire, is infused into her does iron-like *prakṛti*, a mere likeness of the material cause, acquire the power to burn and give heat. To regard *prakṛti*, known as the material, as the material cause of the world is therefore sheer delusion. Śrī Kapiladeva also says: “As the firebrand, the sparks and the smoke are born of the fire itself and are thought of as one with it, yet the fire is distinct from the firebrand…” (*Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam* 3.28.40) Smoke, the burning log and the sparks contain the substance of fire, and so they are spoken of as one with fire; nevertheless fire is something distinct from the firebrand. “The elements,” like the smoke, “the *jīva*,” like the sparks, and “*pradhāna*,” like the firebrand — all of them receive their powers from Bhagavān, who is like the fire, the substance of everything, and so each displays its own separate identity. Even so, the substance of them all is Bhagavān. “*Pradhāna*” is held to be the material of the world, but it appears as such only through the substance Bhagavān has placed in it. “*Pradhāna*” cannot be an independent material cause separate from Bhagavān. To forget Kṛṣṇa, the root shelter of all material causes, and to ascribe that role to the *prakṛti* of Sāṅkhya is as fruitless as expecting milk from the teat-like lumps of flesh on a goat's neck.
