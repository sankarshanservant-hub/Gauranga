# Amṛta-pravāha-bhāṣya on Ādi-līlā 15

## 0

**Summary.** In the fifteenth chapter the Lord studies grammar with Gaṅgādāsa Paṇḍita, becomes expert in the *Pañjī-ṭīkā* and forbids His mother to eat grains on Ekādaśī. He tells a story: Viśvarūpa, having taken *sannyāsa*, called Him to take *sannyāsa* too, but He did not listen and said He wished to serve His parents — and so Viśvarūpa sent Him back home. The departure of Purandara Miśra[^0-1] from this world, the marriage to Lakṣmī-devī, daughter of Vallabhācārya, and other events are described in brief.

## 1

One need only offer *sumanas* (jasmine flowers) at His lotus feet, and even a person of evil mind (*ku-manāḥ*) gains a good mind (*sumanas*)[^1-1]. That Caitanya Prabhu I worship.

## 3

*Mukhya adhyayana*: His chief activity was the pastime of study.

## 4

The charming pastimes of the *paugaṇḍa* age (boyhood) of Śrī Kṛṣṇa Caitanya, from the beginning of His schooling to His marriage, are very extensive.

## 5

First He received His elementary education from Viṣṇu and Sudarśana, and then He studied grammar with Gaṅgādāsa Paṇḍita.

## 6

*Pañjī-ṭīkā*: there was a well-known commentary on grammar called the *Pañjī-ṭīkā*, and Mahāprabhu composed glosses (*ṭippanī*) on it.

## 27

It is not the house that is called “home”; the mistress of the house is called “home.” Together with her one will enjoy all the goals of human life (*puruṣārthas*).

*Thus ends the fifteenth chapter of the “Amṛta-pravāha-bhāṣya.”*

---

[^0-1]: Purandara Miśra is another name of Jagannātha Miśra, the Lord's father.

[^1-1]: A play on words: *sumanas* means both “flower” and “good mind.”
