## 59

> Subheading: The incomparable power of the Name —

**vyavahita** (interrupted): Here what is meant is not an interval in the sounds or letters, nor an “interval” in truth (*tattva*). For such a material interval arises in a faithless *jīva* from his tendency to gratify his own senses or to enjoy matter. It is therefore not the pure Name but merely a collection of material sounds or letters; it only obstructs the fruit of chanting the pure Name — attaining love for Kṛṣṇa. Here, on the other hand, the interval meant is the indistinct, broken or partial utterance of the Name by one turned toward service (*sevonmukha*). For in spite of it, Śrī Nāma-Prabhu manifests His own power in the faithful heart of one turned toward service — the power to bestow the fruit: the destruction of *anarthas* and the dawning of *prema*.

## 60

> Subheading: (*Hari-bhakti-vilāsa* 11.289; words of the *Padma Purāṇa*)

**he vipra**, **ekaṁ nāma** (the name of Kṛṣṇa) **yasya** (of one who has pious merit) **vāci** (uttered) **smaraṇa-patha-gataṁ** (that is, remembered) **śrotra-mūlaṁ gataṁ** (heard) **vā**, **śuddha-varṇaṁ** **aśuddha-varṇaṁ vā**, **vyavahita-rahitaṁ** (*vyavahitāni* are intervals, gaps in the form of the ten offenses against the Name; *rahitam*, free of them — that is, uninterrupted; or else *vyavahitaṁ tad-rahitaṁ ca*: then *vyavahitam* means separated by another word, another sound or another mood, and *tad-rahitam* means lacking some part) **vā** (being such, [it delivers] one who utters it in this way) **tārayati** (delivers) **eva** — this is **satyam** (the truth); **cet** (if) **tat** (that Name) **deha-draviṇa-janatā-lobha-pāṣaṇḍa-madhye** (*deha*, the perishable corpse; *draviṇa*, wealth; *janatā*, desire for relatives, for women, or for the prestige that rests on gathering people; *lobha*, greed for transient external objects, or lust of the tongue; *pāṣaṇḍa*, the offense of disregarding Hari, guru and the Vaiṣṇavas — among these) **nikṣiptaṁ** (is placed, that is, employed or cultivated with the desire to gratify one's own senses, then) **atra** (here, in this world — because it gives a trifling fruit) **śīghraṁ** (at once) **phala-janakaṁ** (giving the supreme fruit) **na syāt** (it will not be).

In his *Dig-darśinī* commentary on the *Hari-bhakti-vilāsa* (11.289) Śrī Sanātana Prabhu writes: “*Vāci gataṁ*: even if [the Name] has entered one's speech incidentally, in the course of conversation; *smaraṇa-patha-gataṁ*: even if it has somehow touched the mind; *śrotra-mūlaṁ gataṁ*: even if it has been heard only slightly; *śuddha-varṇaṁ vā aśuddha-varṇam api vā*: with correct sounds or even with incorrect ones. *Vyavahitam*: an interval made by another word — when, after part of the word ‘Nārāyaṇa’ (to be spoken of below) has been uttered, another word incidentally comes in; [the Name] free of such an interval. Or else: although in expressions like ‘*halaṁ riktam*’ the name ‘Hari’ is present by way of the sounds ‘ha’ and ‘ri,’ and likewise the name ‘Rāma’ in ‘*rāja-mahiṣī*’ (and similar cases are to be understood in the same way), still within those names there is another, intervening sound; the meaning is: free of such an interval. Or else: *vyavahitaṁ ca tad-rahitaṁ cāpi vā*. Here *vyavahitam* is when, after part of the Name has been uttered, another word somehow comes in, and after finishing it one then utters the remaining sounds of the Name — that is, the Name is interrupted in the middle by another word; *rahitam* means without uttering the remaining sounds afterwards, that is, lacking some part. Even so, it surely delivers — it surely frees one from all sins and offenses and even from *saṁsāra*: this is true. But the principal fruit of serving the Name does not come at once. Next, with the words *tac cet* and so on, he says that even when one serves the Name for the sake of maintaining the body and so on, the principal fruit is not achieved quickly. If the Name is placed ‘among the body and the rest,’ that is, employed only for maintaining the body and the like, does it then bear no fruit? No, it does bear fruit — but here, in this world, not quickly, only after some delay. That is the meaning.”

See the *Anubhāṣya* on Madhya 16.72, and also Śrī Jīva Prabhu's explanation (*kārikā*) of this verse in the *Bhakti-sandarbha* (section 265).

## 63

> Subheading: *Nāmābhāsa* grants liberation — (*Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam* 6.2.49)

At the time of death Ajāmila obtained the fruit of *nāmābhāsa* by way of an indication and was freed from sin. Having regained his life, he worshiped Śrī Hari in detachment (*nirveda*) and as a result went to Vaikuṇṭha. After describing this, Śukadeva at the end of the chapter incidentally glorifies to Parīkṣit how the greatness of *nāmābhāsa* differs from that of the pure Name:

**ajāmilaḥ** **mriyamāṇaḥ** (in the jaws of death, helplessly, even without faith) **putropacāritaṁ** (uttered as his son's name, “Nārāyaṇa”) **hareḥ nāma** **gṛṇan** (uttering) **dhāma** (the abode of Vaikuṇṭha) **agāt** (reached); **śraddhayā** (with transcendental firm faith, that Name) **gṛṇan** (a person who chants) — **kim uta** (what need be said of him)?

## 123

> Subheading: Ṭhākura Haridāsa describes his own initiation into the *mahā-mantra*; a *brāhmaṇa* by initiation —

If one chants the name of Hari every day, counting more than three hundred thirty-three thousand three hundred thirty-three names, in one month the count reaches a *koṭi* (ten million) names. In this sacrifice of chanting the Name, Bhagavān, the Bearer of the Name (*nāmī*), is worshiped. By ordinary worldly notions Haridāsa Ṭhākura was not regarded as entitled to perform sacrifice, either by birth (*śaukra*) or by Sāvitrī initiation. Yet, because he was initiated into the sacrifice of the Name, he performed it as a Vedic *brāhmaṇa* by initiation (*daikṣa-brāhmaṇa*) of the Ekāyana branch. The twice-born Haridāsa Ṭhākura is a transcendental *brāhmaṇa* priest (*yājñika*), and the sacrifice concerning the Name that he had begun was nearly complete. And yet he let it be known that, if it were not completed, his sacrifice would again be broken[^123-1].
