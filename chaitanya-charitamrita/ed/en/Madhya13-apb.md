# Amṛta-pravāha-bhāṣya on Madhya-līlā 13

## 0

**Summary.** After His morning bath the Lord watched the Pāṇḍu-vijaya of Jagannātha, Baladeva and Subhadrā, as They were brought out and raised onto Their chariots. At that time the king was sweeping the road with a golden broom. Taking Lakṣmī's permission, Jagannātha set out for the Guṇḍicā-bāḍī. The road was broad and sandy, with houses and gardens on both sides, and along the middle of it the Gauḍas began to pull the chariot. Mahāprabhu divided His own people into seven groups (*sampradāyas*) and began *kīrtana* with fourteen *mṛdaṅgas* (*mādalas*). During the *kīrtana* many kinds of *bhāva* arose in Mahāprabhu; it even seemed that Jagannātha and Mahāprabhu were exchanging their feelings with each other. When the chariot reached Balagaṇḍi, an offering of *bhoga* from the common people began to be made there. In a grove near a garden Mahāprabhu rested a little from the fatigue of dancing.

## 1

All glory to Kṛṣṇa Caitanya, who danced before Jagannātha's chariot! Seeing that dance of His, the whole world and Jagannātha Himself were struck with wonder.

## 5

The servants tie Jagannātha, Baladeva and Subhadrā — these three Śrī-mūrtis — with silken ropes, bring Them out of the temple and raise Them onto the chariots near the Siṁha-dvāra. This procedure is called the “Pāṇḍu-vijaya.”

## 8

*Dayitās*: the word *dayitā* comes from *dayita* (beloved). There is a class of servants called *dayitās*. By birth they do not belong to the respectable (*bhadra*) castes, but having obtained the service of Jagannātha, they have gained the respect given to the higher castes. From the day of the Snāna-yātrā until the return from the chariot, the *dayitās* hold special rights in the service of Śrī Jagannātha. In the *Kṣetra-māhātmya* the *dayitās* are called Śabaras. Those among them who are *brāhmaṇas* are called *dayitā-patis*. During the *anavasara* they offer Jagannātha sweet *bhoga*, and every morning they offer the sweets of the *bāla-bhoga*. During the *anavasara* too, saying, “Jagannātha has a fever,” they offer Him medicines and *pā̃cana* (a drink of sweet juice). The essence is this: before Śrī Jagannātha was installed, the Deity of Śrī Nīla-mādhava dwelt among the Śabaras, and that Nīla-mādhava later became “Jagannātha.” That is why the Śabara *dayitās* have gained the right to Jagannātha's intimate service.

## 11

*Tūlī*: cotton in a cover — small cotton cushions (like bolsters).

## 14

*Maṇimā*: the people of Utkala address those worthy of honor, and the king, as “*maṇimā*.”

## 23

The fortnight that Śrī Jagannātha spends in seclusion after His bath is called the *anavasara*, the time of seclusion. After that, taking Lakṣmī's permission, He goes out on the chariot.

## 27

*Gauḍa*: the cowherds (*goyālās*) of Utkala are called Gauḍas.

## 36

*Pāli-gāna*: those who sing the refrain in chorus (*dohāra*).

## 48

Seven groups: the group of Kulīnagrāma, the group of Śāntipura and the group of Śrīkhaṇḍa joined the four groups mentioned earlier, making seven groups; with two *mādalas* (*khols*) to each, the *kīrtana* was performed with fourteen *mādalas*.

## 52

In the *rāsa* dance and at the wedding with the queens, Śrī Kṛṣṇa simultaneously appeared as “many” forms (*prakāśa*). In the same way Śrī Kṛṣṇa Caitanya, displaying that same power, “manifested” Himself in each group. The people of every group thought, “The Lord is in our group, not in any other.”

## 77

I bow to that Supreme Truth who is Brahmaṇya-deva, the good of cows and *brāhmaṇas*, the welfare of the world, Kṛṣṇa and Govinda.

## 78

All glory to this God, the son of Devakī! All glory to Kṛṣṇa, the lamp of the Vṛṣṇi dynasty! All glory to Kṛṣṇa, dark as a fresh rain cloud, whose body is tender! All glory to Mukunda, who removes the burden of the earth!

## 79

All glory to Kṛṣṇacandra — the abode of people, renowned as born from Devakī's womb (*devakī-janma-vāda*), the head of the assembly of the Yadus. With His own arms He destroys *adharma*, He takes away the sins of moving and nonmoving beings, and with His sweetly smiling face He increases the loving desire (*kāma*) of the women of Vraja-pura.

## 80

I am not a *brāhmaṇa*, not a *kṣatriya* king, not a *vaiśya* or a *śūdra*; I am not a *brahmacārī*, a householder, a *vānaprastha* or a *sannyāsī*. I identify myself only as “a servant of the servants of the lotus feet of Śrī Kṛṣṇa,” the nectar-ocean full of all supreme bliss, which has blossomed forth (that is, is eternally self-manifest).

## 82

“He whirled like a firebrand wheel (*alāta*)”: like a wheel of burning (blazing) coal, Mahāprabhu began to spin round and round (to whirl) like a wheel.

## 113

Leaving the wild dance (*tāṇḍava*), Mahāprabhu felt in His heart the *bhāva* of Śrī Rādhā at the meeting in Kurukṣetra. After a long separation, this song came to Him of itself.

## 118

When Gauracandra, acting out the song, stepped backward, Jagannātha stood still. When Gaura went forward, Jagannātha slowly moved ahead.

## 137

For other people the mind is the heart; but My mind is not separate from Vṛndāvana. I know the mind and Vṛndāvana to be “one.”

## 139–146

O Kṛṣṇa, when You were in Mathurā, You sent instruction in “*jñāna-yoga*” through Uddhava and said that You can be attained by *jñāna-yoga*. And now, meeting face to face here at Kurukṣetra, You speak of the same “*jñāna-yoga*”! My heart is full of *prema*, and in it there is no room for *jñāna-yoga*. You know this — it does not become You to give such instruction! Even if I wanted to take My mind away from You and fix it on worldly things, I could not do it! Attachment to You is My very nature, so teaching Me meditation only makes people laugh. So You have not considered what is fitting and where. A *gopī* is not some master of *yoga*, to find bliss in meditating on Your lotus feet. Your words have elegance enough, but teaching a *gopī* meditation is mere duplicity, and hearing this (that she needs to learn meditation), the *gopī* feels still more hurt (*abhimāna*). The *gopīs* by nature have no remembrance of the body, so they have nothing called “the well of *saṁsāra*”; the method of meditation that grants liberation is therefore (simply) useless to them. The *gopīs* have fallen into the ocean of separation from You and are being swallowed by the *timiṅgila* (a gigantic fish) — the desire (*kāma*) for nothing but service to You;[^139-146-1] save them from that, that is, from that separation. What is astonishing is this: how could You forget those people of Vraja — Your mother, father and friends? You are a pure person, gentle, always well-mannered through Your good qualities, affectionate and compassionate, and so there is not even a semblance of fault in such conduct of Yours. If You no longer remember the people of Vraja, it is only the play of My misfortune (the sport of ill fate). I am not looking at My own sorrow; (but, to tell the truth) seeing the grief of Yaśodā, the queen of Vraja, the hearts of the people of Vraja truly break. Sometimes by separation You make the residents of Vraja as if dead, sometimes by union You bring them back to life — why You keep them alive only to suffer, I cannot say. You wear the royal dress of Mathurā and so on, You live in a place apart from Vraja and keep company with the queens — the people of Vraja do not like this at all. A strange thing befalls the people of Vraja: they cannot leave the land of Vraja and go elsewhere, and yet without seeing You they die. What will become of the people of Vraja, You alone know.

## 149

*Jhuro̐*: I keep weeping.

## 152

That a beloved woman deprived of her beloved's company, and a beloved man deprived of his beloved's company, cannot live — this is true proof. Yet (each of the two thinking this) they stay alive only because “if my beloved hears that I have died, my beloved will die too.”

## 154

You are My eternal beloved. Knowing that You will not survive separation from Me, I serve Nārāyaṇa, and by the power of His all-pervasiveness (*vibhutva*) I come to Vraja every day, sport with You and then return again to Yadupurī. So You, remaining in Vraja, think that You have had only a vision (*sphūrti*) of Me.[^154-1]

## 164

When Svarūpa Dāmodara sings of these *bhāvas*, the Lord's own senses — His eyes, ears and the rest — enter Svarūpa's senses and taste the song. In other words, a oneness of heart and a perfect attunement arise in both to the full.

## 170

*Jhañjhā-vāta*: a gusty wind that blows hard from time to time.

## 172

“*Bhāvodaya*,” “*bhāva-śānti*,” “*sandhi*,” “*śābalya*”: the rising of a *bhāva* (*bhāvodaya*), the subsiding of a *bhāva* (*bhāva-śānti*), the meeting of *bhāvas* (*bhāva-sandhi*), the mingling of *bhāvas* (*bhāva-śābalya*).

## 177

*Cauguṇa-maṅgala*: auspicious sounds multiplied fourfold.

## 178

*Manthara*: slowly.

## 193

“At Balagaṇḍi”: the place between Śraddhābālu and [the temple of] Ardhāsanī-devī is called Balagaṇḍi.

## 204

“*Ārāma*”: a garden (a grove, an orchard, a garden).

## 207

That Caitanyadeva who, before the Lord of Nīlācala seated on the chariot, overwhelmed by the joy of dancing in which waves of abundant *prema* shone forth, joyfully performed *saṅkīrtana*, surrounded by Vaiṣṇavas — will He come again within the path of my eyes?

*Thus ends the thirteenth chapter of the “Amṛta-pravāha-bhāṣya.”*

[^139-146-1]: The verse translation of 142 (VCD) reads differently, “the gigantic fish of passion”; for Bhaktivinoda *kāma* here is the desire for nothing but service to Kṛṣṇa.
