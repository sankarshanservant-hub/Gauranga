## 13–17

Acyutānanda. The *Caitanya-bhāgavata* (Antya-khaṇḍa, Chapter 9) records that he was Advaita Prabhu's eldest son: “Advaita's eldest son, Śrī Acyutānanda.” The book *Advaita-carita*, written in Sanskrit, says: “Acyuta, Kṛṣṇa Miśra and Gopāla dāsa — these three jewels are said to be born from the ocean of Sītā's womb. These three sons of the Ācārya are known as associates of Gaura. The fourth is called Balarāma, the fifth Svarūpa, and the sixth is named Jagadīśa: the Ācārya had six sons.”

So of Śrī Advaita Prabhu's three sons who were devotees of Gaura, Acyutānanda was the eldest. Advaita married at the very beginning of the fifteenth century of the Śaka era. In the year when Śrī Mahāprabhu resolved to go from Nīlācala to Vṛndāvana by way of Rāmakeli, that is, in Śaka 1433–34, Acyutānanda was only five years old. The *Caitanya-bhāgavata* (Antya-khaṇḍa, Chapter 4) calls him “five years old, charming, naked.” Acyutānanda was therefore born in Śaka 1428. Even before Acyuta's birth, when Mahāprabhu was born, Advaita's wife Sītā came to see the Lord's birth. So there is nothing impossible in her having three more sons within twenty-one years.

There is a Bengali book in verse called *Sītā-advaita-carita*. It was composed by one Lokanātha dāsa of the *sakhī-bhekī* party of the *prākṛta-sahajiyās*, and it was printed in Śaka 1792 by the journal *Nityānanda-dāyinī*. It describes Acyutānanda as Mahāprabhu's schoolmate; this contradicts the *Caitanya-bhāgavata*. When Mahāprabhu, after taking *sannyāsa*, came to Advaita's house in Śāntipura, the year was Śaka 1431, and Acyutānanda was then a child of three (*Caitanya-bhāgavata*, Antya, Chapter 1): “Advaita's son, a naked little child, came and fell at Gauracandra's feet. The Lord took him on His lap, dust and all. The Lord said: ‘Acyuta, the Ācārya is My father. By that relation you and I are two brothers.’”

Before the great manifestation (*mahā-prakāśa*) in Śrī Navadvīpa, Śrīman Mahāprabhu sent Śrīrāma Paṇḍita to Śāntipura to bring Advaita. Acyutānanda then joined his father and mother in weeping for joy: “Advaita's son, named ‘Acyutānanda,’ just a little boy — he too weeps without stopping.” And when Advaita Prabhu was expounding knowledge (*jñāna*) against devotion and Mahāprabhu was beating Him, Acyutānanda was present there too. All these events must be placed two or three years before the Lord's *sannyāsa*. (*Caitanya-bhāgavata*, Madhya, Chapter 19): “Acyuta, Advaita's son, bows down.”

From childhood Śrī Acyuta was a devotee of Śrīman Mahāprabhu. Nothing is known of his ever taking a wife and living as a householder. In the description of Śrī Advaita's branch his name stands first among the disciples. Śrī Yadunandana dāsa wrote the *Śākhā-nirṇayāmṛta* on the branches of Śrī Gadādhara Paṇḍita Gosvāmī, and from it we learn that Acyutānanda Ṭhākura was a disciple and branch of Gadādhara: “Acyutānanda, the bliss of the nectar of the great *rasa*, the son of Śrīmad Advaita, most dear to Gadādhara.” Acyutānanda performed his worship in Nīlācala, having taken shelter of Mahāprabhu's feet (Ādi, Chapter 10): “Acyutānanda, the son of Advaita Ācārya, who stayed in Nīlācala, taking shelter at the Lord's feet.” (CC, Ādi 10.150) At the end of his life Śrī Gadādhara Paṇḍita Gosvāmī lived in Nīlācala near Śrī Mahāprabhu. That is why, as we see, many of Advaita Prabhu's true servants, headed by Acyutānanda, took shelter of Śrī Gadādhara's feet.

From his earliest childhood Acyutānanda showed intense devotion to Śrī Mahāprabhu. In the dancing and *kīrtana* before the chariot, too, we see the Lord's dear Acyutānanda every time (see Ādi, Chapter 13, 452[^13-1]): “There was one party of the Ācārya of Śāntipura; Acyutānanda danced there, and all the rest sang.” (CC, Madhya 13.45) The boy was then only six years old. In the *Śrī Gaura-gaṇoddeśa-dīpikā* by Śrī Kavi Karṇapūra, Acyutānanda is glorified as “Gadādhara's disciple, dear to Kṛṣṇa Caitanya.” Some identify him with Kārttikeya, others with the *gopī* named “Acyutā.” The author has concluded that both views are correct: “His son Acyutānanda, dear to Kṛṣṇa Caitanya, is known to be the beloved disciple of Śrīmat Paṇḍita Gosvāmī. Some say that formerly he was Kārttikeya. Others, knowers of *rasa*, say he is the *gopī* named Acyutā. Both are correct, for the two have come together in one.”

Śrī Narahari dāsa's *Narottama-vilāsa* describes in detail how Śrī Acyutānanda came to the festival at Khetari and took part in it. Though he was by then quite infirm, he joined the festival at the request of his mother, Śrī Sītā, and of Śrī Jāhnavā. According to that same Narahari dāsa, at the end of his life he lived in his home in Śāntipura. Yet it is known that while Śrī Mahāprabhu was manifest in this world, Acyutānanda lived with Him in Purī, and afterwards near Śrī Gadādhara. Needless to say, since he never married, Acyutānanda had no children.

## 18

> Subheading: (2) Kṛṣṇa Miśra —

Kṛṣṇa Miśra. In the Sanskrit book *Advaita-carita*: “Acyuta, Kṛṣṇa Miśra and Gopāla dāsa — these three jewels are said to be born from the ocean of Sītā's womb.” Of Śrī Advaita Prabhu's six sons, three brothers — “Acyuta, Kṛṣṇa Miśra and Gopāla” — were engaged in the service of Śrī Gaurāṅga. *Gaura-gaṇoddeśa*, verse 88: “Some say that Kṛṣṇa Miśra is Kārttikeya, because he is like him.”

Kṛṣṇa Miśra had two sons: (1) Raghunātha Cakravartī and (2) Dolagovinda. Raghunātha's descendants live in Śāntipura, in the Madana-Gopāla quarter, and in Gaṇakara, Mṛjāpura and Kumārakhāli. Dolagovinda had three sons: (1) Cāṅda, (2) Kandarpa, (3) Gopīnātha. Kandarpa's descendants live in Māladaha and Jikābāḍī. Gopīnātha had three sons: (1) Śrīvallabha, (2) Prāṇavallabha and (3) Keśava. Śrīvallabha's descendants live in Maśiyāḍārā (Mahiṣaḍerā?), Dāmukadiyā, Caṇḍīpura and other places. The Maśiyāḍārā line descends from Śrīvallabha's eldest son, Gaṅgānārāyaṇa, and the lines in the villages of Dāmukadiyā, Caṇḍīpura, Śolamāri and others from his youngest son, Rāmagopāla. The descendants of Prāṇavallabha and Keśava live in Uthalī.

Prāṇavallabha's son was Ratneśvara; his son was Kṛṣṇarāma; Kṛṣṇarāma's youngest son was Lakṣmīnārāyaṇa, and his son was Navakiśora. Navakiśora's second son was Rāmamohana. Rāmamohana's eldest son, “Jagabandhu,” and his third son, “Vīracandra,” entered the order of mendicants (*bhikṣu-āśrama*) and installed a Deity of Śrī Mahāprabhu at Kāṭoyā. People called them “Baḍa Prabhu” and “Choṭa Prabhu,” the elder and the younger Prabhu. It was they who started the circumambulation (*parikramā*) of Śrī Dhāma Navadvīpa. For the complete genealogy of Kṛṣṇa Miśra see the article “Advaita-vaṁśa” in the *Vaiṣṇava-mañjuṣā* (issue 4).

## 27

Balarāma, Svarūpa and Jagadīśa. In the Sanskrit book *Advaita-carita*: “The fourth is called Balarāma, the fifth Svarūpa, and the sixth is named Jagadīśa: the Ācārya had six sons.” All three turned away from Gaura; they are *smārtas*, or Māyāvādīs, and therefore not Vaiṣṇavas.

Balarāma's three wives bore him nine sons. The youngest son of the first wife, Madhusūdana, became known as “Gosāñi Bhaṭṭācārya” and adopted the religion of the *smārtas*. His son Rādhāramaṇa took the name “Gosvāmī Bhaṭṭācārya” and so degraded the word “*gosvāmī*,” a title fit only for one who has left home. Following the *smārta* Raghunandana, he burned a *kuśa-puttalikā* (an effigy of *kuśa* grass) of Śrī Advaita Prabhu and performed over it the *śrāddha* rite done for a ghost (*preta*) or a demon (*rākṣasa*). By acting against the *smṛtis* devoted to Viṣṇu-bhakti, such as the *Śrī Hari-bhakti-vilāsa*, he displayed his foolishness and a great offense. Without being a pure devotee, he wrote several books and commentaries on primary works; pure devotees do not value them. For Balarāma's genealogy see the *Mañjuṣā* (issue 4).

And Viśvāsa Kamalākānta is the very man of that name mentioned in Ādi, Chapter 10, verse 119. The brāhmaṇa Kamalākānta is one of the Lord's own associates. Kamalākānta Viśvāsa, being Advaita's servant, is also the Lord's associate. When Śrī Paramānanda Purī came from Navadvīpa to Nīlācala, he brought with him the brāhmaṇa Kamalākānta, or Kamalānanda, a resident of Navadvīpa. Madhya, Chapter 10, 94: “There was a devotee of the Lord, a brāhmaṇa named ‘Kamalākānta’; taking him along, he set out for Nīlācala.” (CC, Madhya 10.94)

## 80

> Subheading: (5) Anantācārya, (6) Kavidatta, (7) Nayana Miśra, (8) Gaṅgāmantrī, (9) Māmu Ṭhākura, (10) Kaṇṭhābharaṇa —

Kavidatta. *Śākhā-nirṇayāmṛta*, 14: “I praise Kavidatta, in whose heart Rādhā and Kṛṣṇa dwell eternally and by nature, the very wonder of *mahābhāva*.” He is Kalakaṇṭhī of Vraja (*Gaura-gaṇoddeśa*, verses 197 and 207).

Nayana Miśra (*Gaura-gaṇoddeśa*, verses 196 and 207) is Nitya-mañjarī of Vraja. *Śākhā-nirṇayāmṛta*, verse 1: “I praise Śrī Nayanānanda Miśra, an ocean of the nectar of *prema*, the one vessel of the jewel of love of Gadādhara and Gaura.”

Gaṅgāmantrī (*Gaura-gaṇoddeśa*, 196 and 205) is Candrikā of Vraja. *Śākhā-nirṇayāmṛta*, 16: “I glorify Gaṅgāmantrī, who delights in the joy of service, with whom the Gaṅgā herself, the Svardhunī, took counsel in order to reveal love for the holy name.”

Māmu Ṭhākura. Śrī Mahāprabhu used to call him “*māmā*” (maternal uncle), and so people called him “Māmu Ṭhākura”: in East Bengal and in Utkala a maternal uncle is called “*māmu*.” His real name was “Jagannātha Cakravartī.” He was the nephew of Śrī Nīlāmbara Cakravartī, his brother's son, and came from the village of Magaḍobā in Pharidapura district. After Śrī Gadādhara's disappearance, Māmu Ṭhākura became the superintendent of the service of Śrī Ṭoṭā-Gopīnātha in Purī. According to the *Gaura-gaṇoddeśa* (verses 196 and 205), he is Kalabhāṣiṇī of Vraja. *Śākhā-nirṇayāmṛta*, 17: “I praise Māmu Ṭhākura, whom Gauracandra and His associates lovingly addressed in Utkala as ‘*māmu*.’”

The guru line of the servitors of Ṭoṭā-Gopīnātha: (1) Śrī Gadādhara Paṇḍita Gosvāmī (Śrīmatī Rādhikā; by another view, Saubhāgya-mañjarī); (2) his follower Śrī Jagannātha Cakravartī, “Māmu” Gosvāmī (Śrī Rūpa-mañjarī?); (3) his follower Raghunātha Gosvāmī; (4) Rāmacandra; (5) Rādhāvallabha; (6) Kṛṣṇajīvana; (7) Śyāmasundara; (8) Śāntāmaṇi; (9) Harinātha; (10) Navīnacandra; (11) Matilāla; (12) Dayāmayī; (13) Kuñjabihārī.

Kaṇṭhābharaṇa. His name was Śrī Ananta Caṭṭarāja. *Gaura-gaṇoddeśa*, 196 and 206: “Ananta, born in the Caṭṭa family, who bore the title Śrī Kaṇṭhābharaṇa.” He is Gopālī of Vraja. *Śākhā-nirṇayāmṛta*, 18: “I praise Śrī Kaṇṭhābharaṇa, full of pastimes, the very embodiment of the *rasa* of Rādhā and Kṛṣṇa, [the necklace] descended from Their necks.”

## 83

> Subheading: (15) Śrīnātha, (16) Uddhava, (17) Jitāmitra, (18) Jagannātha —

Śrīnātha Cakravartī. *Śākhā-nirṇayāmṛta*, 13: “I praise the *paṇḍita* named Śrīnātha, the shelter of good qualities, by whom the service of Kṛṣṇa was performed with great care in all its proper order.”

Uddhava dāsa. *Śākhā-nirṇayāmṛta*, 35: “I praise Śrīmad Uddhava dāsa, rich in virtues, who gives the full wealth of *prema* to the most lowly.”

Jitāmitra. *Gaura-gaṇoddeśa*, 202: “He who conquered and subdued the six enemies headed by lust was given by Gaura a name true to its meaning — Jitāmitra, ‘he who has conquered his foes.’” He is Śyāma-mañjarī of Vraja. *Śākhā-nirṇayāmṛta*, 36: “I praise Jitāmitra, the fulfiller of all desires, whose holy book nourishes love for Kṛṣṇa's sweetness.”

Jagannātha dāsa lived in the village of Kāṣṭhakāṭā (Kāṭhādiyā) in Vikramapura, Ḍhākā. His descendants now live in the village of Āḍiyala, in Kāmārapāḍā and in Pāikapāḍā. The Deity he installed, “Yaśomādhava,” is served by the “*gosvāmīs*” of Āḍiyala. In the *Kṛṣṇa-gaṇoddeśa* Śrī Rūpapāda describes sixty-four *sakhīs* of one assembly; Jagannātha dāsa is the twenty-sixth of them, “Tilakinī,” a junior friend of Citrā-devī. Verse 142: “Rasālikā, Tilakinī, Śaurasenī, Sugandhikā.” His line: (2) Rāmanṛsiṁha, (3) Rāmagopāla, (4) Rāmacandra, (5) Sanātana, (6) Muktārāma, (7) Gopīnātha, (8) Goloka, (9) Harimohana Śiromaṇi, (10) Rākhālarāja. Gopīnātha's younger sons were (8) Mādhava and (9) Lakṣmīkānta.

In the *Bhoga-nirṇaya-paddhati* of Sūryadāsa Sarakhela: “Then those who became *mahāntas* in the groups headed by Sucitrā: Jagannātha dāsa and Ṭhākura Jagadīśa.”[^83-1] *Śākhā-nirṇayāmṛta*, 48: “I praise Jagannātha dāsa, known as ‘Kāṣṭhakāṭā,’ who gave the blessing of Śrī Hari's holy name in Tripura as well.” That is, he preached the holy name in the region of Tripura.

## 84

> Subheading: (19) Hari Ācārya, (20) Puriyā-Gopāla, (21) Kṛṣṇadāsa Brahmacārī, (22) Puṣpagopāla —

Hari Ācārya (*Gaura-gaṇoddeśa*, 196 and 207) is Kālākṣī of Vraja. *Śākhā-nirṇayāmṛta*, 37: “With supreme devotion I praise Haridāsācārya, a resident of Bengal, made radiant by his own *ujjvala-rasa*.”

Puriyā-Gopāla dāsa. *Śākhā-nirṇayāmṛta*, 38: “I praise him who is called Gopāla dāsa, a resident of Sādipura, who flooded the town of Vikrama[pura] with the *rasa* of love for Rādhā and Kṛṣṇa.” That is, he preached the holy name in Vikramapura.

Kṛṣṇadāsa Brahmacārī is Indulekhā, one of the eight *sakhīs* of Vraja. *Gaura-gaṇoddeśa*, 164: “She who formerly in Vraja was Indulekhā, Śrī Rādhā's friend, is Kṛṣṇadāsa Brahmacārī, who made his home in Vṛndāvana.” *Śākhā-nirṇayāmṛta*, 41: “I praise the *brahmacārī* Kṛṣṇadāsa, who reveals love for Kṛṣṇa, bright of mind, a resident of Vṛndāvana.”

Puṣpagopāla. *Śākhā-nirṇayāmṛta*, 39: “I praise him who is called Puṣpagopāla, who delights in *prema*: by his *rasa* the village of Svarṇagrāma blossomed (*puṣpita*), and hence his name.”

## 85

> Subheading: (23) Śrīharṣa, (24) Raghu Miśra, (25) Lakṣmīnātha, (26) Caitanya dāsa, (27) Raghunātha —

Śrīharṣa (*Gaura-gaṇoddeśa*, 194 and 201) is Sukeśinī of Vraja. *Śākhā-nirṇayāmṛta*, 40: “I praise him who is called Śrīharṣa Miśra, who delights in love for Kṛṣṇa, whose heart is intoxicated with love for Gaura, a shoot of the *rasa* of great bliss.”

Raghu Miśra (*Gaura-gaṇoddeśa*, 195 and 201) is Karpūra-mañjarī of Vraja.

Lakṣmīnātha Paṇḍita (*Gaura-gaṇoddeśa*, 196, 205) is Rasonmādā of Vraja. *Śākhā-nirṇayāmṛta*, 42: “I praise Vraja-Lakṣmīnātha dāsa, compassion embodied, filled with *mahābhāva*, the bestower of Vraja's good fortune.”

Baṅgavāṭī-Caitanya dāsa (*Gaura-gaṇoddeśa*, 196 and 206) is Kālī of Vraja. *Śākhā-nirṇayāmṛta*, 43: “I praise Śrī Caitanya dāsa of Baṅgavāṭī, the great soul, whose body is always adorned with tears of love and with hair standing on end.” The succession of his branch: (2) Mathurāprasāda, (3) Rukmiṇīkānta, (4) Jīvanakṛṣṇa, (5) Yugalakiśora, (6) Ratanakṛṣṇa, (7) Rādhāmādhava, (8) Uṣāmaṇi, (9) Vaikuṇṭhanātha, (10) Lālamohana Śāhā Śaṅkhanidhi (a resident of Ḍhākā).

Raghunātha is Varāṅgadā of Vraja. *Gaura-gaṇoddeśa*, 194 and 200: “Raghunātha, a certain brāhmaṇa, an exclusive servant of Gaurāṅga.” *Śākhā-nirṇayāmṛta*, 44: “I praise him who is called Śrī Raghunātha, the great soul, the root of *prema*: simply by hearing his name one obtains the *rasa* of Vṛndāvana.”

## 86

> Subheading: (28) Amogha, (29) Hastigopāla, (30) Caitanya-vallabha, (31) Yadu, (32) Maṅgala Vaiṣṇava —

Amogha Paṇḍita. *Śākhā-nirṇayāmṛta*, 59: “I praise Amogha Paṇḍita, whom Śrī Gaura made His own, whose body is filled with *prema* and whose voice is choked with it, whose body trembles with hair standing on end.”

Hastigopāla is Hariṇī of Vraja (*Gaura-gaṇoddeśa*, 196 and 206). “With supreme devotion I bow to him who is called Hastigopāla dāsa, whose body is intoxicated with *prema*, full of the highest love for Gaura.” (*Śākhā-nirṇayāmṛta*, 61)

Caitanya-vallabha. *Śākhā-nirṇayāmṛta*, 60: “I praise him who is called Caitanya-vallabha, the abode of the *rasa* of *prema*, who longs to sing of the qualities of Gadādhara and Gaura.”

Yadu Gāṅguli. *Śākhā-nirṇayāmṛta*, 34: “With devotion I praise Yadunātha Cakravartī, called Līlā-bhāgavata, the root of *prema*, a great scholar, a great soul.” A descendant of this branch is Śrī Nalinākṣa Ṭhākura, a resident of Pāligrāma-Cāṇaka in Barddhamāna district.

Maṅgala Vaiṣṇava. *Śākhā-nirṇayāmṛta*, 47: “I praise Maṅgala Vaiṣṇava, pure in heart and body, whose body is moistened by the nectar of the pastimes of the Lords of Vṛndāvana.” He lived in the village of Ṭiṭakaṇā in Murśidābāda. His father's family were servitors of the goddess Kirīṭeśvarī in Murśidābāda. According to tradition, he first took the great vow (*bṛhad-vrata*) and left home, and later, in Mayanāḍāla, married the daughter of his disciple Prāṇanātha Adhikārī. His descendants are now known as “the Ṭhākuras of Kāṅdaḍā.” Kāṅdaḍā is a village near Kāṭoyā in Barddhamāna district. Not long ago thirty-six households of Maṅgala Ṭhākura's line lived there.

Among Ṭhākura Mahāśaya's well-known disciples, Prāṇanātha Adhikārī of Mayanāḍāla, Puruṣottama Cakravartī of Kāṅdaḍā and Nṛsiṁhaprasāda Mitra Ṭhākura of Mayanāḍāla deserve mention. The Adhikārī line of Mayanāḍāla has died out, but descendants through their daughters remain. Of Puruṣottama Cakravartī's line, Śrī Kuñjabihārī Cakravartī and Rādhāvallabha Cakravartī now live in the village of Āṅgaḍā under Sākuleśvara in Bīrabhūma. They sing the *Śrī Caitanya-maṅgala*. In the line of Nṛsiṁhaprasāda Mitra Ṭhākura, Sudhākṛṣṇa Mitra Ṭhākura and Nikuñjabihārī Mitra Ṭhākura are well known; they are masters of the art of the *mṛdaṅga*.

Maṅgala Ṭhākura Mahāśaya obtained the pair of Deities “Śrī Rādhāvallabha” when the king of Gauḍa was building a road from Gauḍa to Kṣetra and digging a pond. At that time he lived in the village of Rāṇīpura, west of Kāṅdaḍā. The Śrī Nṛsiṁha-śilā that Ṭhākura Mahāśaya worshipped is still at Kāṅdaḍā. When the property granted by the king of Gauḍa for the service of the Deities was lost, Maṅgala Ṭhākura maintained the service by begging.

Maṅgala Ṭhākura had three sons: (1) Rādhikāprasāda, (2) Gopīramaṇa, (3) Śyāmakiśora. The lines of these three brothers still exist. Later the service of Śrī Vṛndāvanacandra was established at Kāṅdaḍā.

## 87

> Subheading: (33) Śivānanda Cakravartī —

Śivānanda Cakravartī. *Gaura-gaṇoddeśa*, verse 183: “Śivānanda Cakravartī, who made his home in Vṛndāvana, is known as a manifestation of Śrīmatī Lavaṅga-mañjarī.” *Śākhā-nirṇayāmṛta*, 10: “I praise Śivānanda, who is called Kumudānanda, endowed with *ujjvala-rasa*, pure, a resident of Vṛndāvana.” See Ādi, Chapter 8, 70.

Besides these, Śrī Yadunandana dāsa in his *Śākhā-nirṇaya* mentions several more members of Gadādhara's branch: 1. Mādhavācārya; 2. Gopāla dāsa; 3. Hṛdayānanda; 4. Vallabha Bhaṭṭa (after whose name the “Vallabha,” or “Puṣṭi-mārgīya,” *sampradāya* is known); 5. Madhu Paṇḍita (his *śrīpāṭa* is in the village of Sāiṁbonā, two miles east of Khaḍadaha; it was he who installed and served the famous Deity of Gopīnāthadeva in Vṛndāvana); 6. Acyutānanda; 7. Candraśekhara; 8. Vakreśvara Paṇḍita (?); 9. Dāmodara; 10. Bhagavān Ācārya (another); 11. Anantācārya-varya (another); 12. Kṛṣṇadāsa; 13. Paramānanda Bhaṭṭācārya; 14. Bhavānanda Gosvāmī; 15. Caitanya dāsa; 16. Lokanātha Bhaṭṭa (the guru of Śrī Ṭhākura Narottama, a native of Tālakhaḍi in Yaśohara district, who installed Śrī Rādhā-vinoda in Vṛndāvana, a close friend of Bhūgarbha Ṭhākura) (?); 17. Govindācārya; 18. Akrūra Ṭhākura; 19. Saṅketācārya; 20. Pratāpāditya; 21. Kamalākānta Ācārya; 22. Yādavācārya; 23. Nārāyaṇa Paḍihārī (a resident of Kṣetra).
