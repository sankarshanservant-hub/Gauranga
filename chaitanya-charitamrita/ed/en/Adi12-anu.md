## 13–17

Acyutānanda. The *Caitanya-bhāgavata* (Antya-khaṇḍa, Chapter 9) records that he was Advaita Prabhu's eldest son: “Advaita's eldest son, Śrī Acyutānanda.” The book *Advaita-carita*, written in Sanskrit, says: “Acyuta, Kṛṣṇa Miśra and Gopāla dāsa — these three jewels are said to be born from the ocean of Sītā's womb. These three sons of the Ācārya are known as associates of Gaura. The fourth is called Balarāma, the fifth Svarūpa, and the sixth is named Jagadīśa: the Ācārya had six sons.”

So of Śrī Advaita Prabhu's three sons who were devotees of Gaura, Acyutānanda was the eldest. Advaita married at the very beginning of the fifteenth century of the Śaka era. In the year when Śrī Mahāprabhu resolved to go from Nīlācala to Vṛndāvana by way of Rāmakeli, that is, in Śaka 1433–34, Acyutānanda was only five years old. The *Caitanya-bhāgavata* (Antya-khaṇḍa, Chapter 4) calls him “five years old, charming, naked.” Acyutānanda was therefore born in Śaka 1428. Even before Acyuta's birth, when Mahāprabhu was born, Advaita's wife Sītā came to see the Lord's birth. So there is nothing impossible in her having three more sons within twenty-one years.

There is a Bengali book in verse called *Sītā-advaita-carita*. It was composed by one Lokanātha dāsa of the *sakhī-bhekī* party of the *prākṛta-sahajiyās*, and it was printed in Śaka 1792 by the journal *Nityānanda-dāyinī*. It describes Acyutānanda as Mahāprabhu's schoolmate; this contradicts the *Caitanya-bhāgavata*. When Mahāprabhu, after taking *sannyāsa*, came to Advaita's house in Śāntipura, the year was Śaka 1431, and Acyutānanda was then a child of three (*Caitanya-bhāgavata*, Antya, Chapter 1): “Advaita's son, a naked little child, came and fell at Gauracandra's feet. The Lord took him on His lap, dust and all. The Lord said: ‘Acyuta, the Ācārya is My father. By that relation you and I are two brothers.’”

Before the great manifestation (*mahā-prakāśa*) in Śrī Navadvīpa, Śrīman Mahāprabhu sent Śrīrāma Paṇḍita to Śāntipura to bring Advaita. Acyutānanda then joined his father and mother in weeping for joy: “Advaita's son, named ‘Acyutānanda,’ just a little boy — he too weeps without stopping.” And when Advaita Prabhu was expounding knowledge (*jñāna*) against devotion and Mahāprabhu was beating Him, Acyutānanda was present there too. All these events must be placed two or three years before the Lord's *sannyāsa*. (*Caitanya-bhāgavata*, Madhya, Chapter 19): “Acyuta, Advaita's son, bows down.”

From childhood Śrī Acyuta was a devotee of Śrīman Mahāprabhu. Nothing is known of his ever taking a wife and living as a householder. In the description of Śrī Advaita's branch his name stands first among the disciples. Śrī Yadunandana dāsa wrote the *Śākhā-nirṇayāmṛta* on the branches of Śrī Gadādhara Paṇḍita Gosvāmī, and from it we learn that Acyutānanda Ṭhākura was a disciple and branch of Gadādhara: “Acyutānanda, the bliss of the nectar of the great *rasa*, the son of Śrīmad Advaita, most dear to Gadādhara.” Acyutānanda performed his worship in Nīlācala, having taken shelter of Mahāprabhu's feet (Ādi, Chapter 10): “Acyutānanda, the son of Advaita Ācārya, lived in Nīlācala, sheltered at the Lord's feet.” (CC, Ādi 10.150) At the end of his life Śrī Gadādhara Paṇḍita Gosvāmī lived in Nīlācala near Śrī Mahāprabhu. That is why, as we see, many of Advaita Prabhu's true servants, headed by Acyutānanda, took shelter of Śrī Gadādhara's feet.

From his earliest childhood Acyutānanda showed intense devotion to Śrī Mahāprabhu. In the dancing and *kīrtana* before the chariot, too, we see the Lord's dear Acyutānanda every time (see Ādi, Chapter 13, 452[^13-1]): “There was one party of the Ācārya of Śāntipura; Acyutānanda danced there, and all the rest sang.” (CC, Madhya 13.45) The boy was then only six years old. In the *Śrī Gaura-gaṇoddeśa-dīpikā* by Śrī Kavi Karṇapūra, Acyutānanda is glorified as “Gadādhara's disciple, dear to Kṛṣṇa Caitanya.” Some identify him with Kārttikeya, others with the *gopī* named “Acyutā.” The author has concluded that both views are correct: “His son Acyutānanda, dear to Kṛṣṇa Caitanya, is known to be the beloved disciple of Śrīmat Paṇḍita Gosvāmī. Some say that formerly he was Kārttikeya. Others, knowers of *rasa*, say he is the *gopī* named Acyutā. Both are correct, for the two have come together in one.”

Śrī Narahari dāsa's *Narottama-vilāsa* describes in detail how Śrī Acyutānanda came to the festival at Khetari and took part in it. Though he was by then quite infirm, he joined the festival at the request of his mother, Śrī Sītā, and of Śrī Jāhnavā. According to that same Narahari dāsa, at the end of his life he lived in his home in Śāntipura. Yet it is known that while Śrī Mahāprabhu was manifest in this world, Acyutānanda lived with Him in Purī, and afterwards near Śrī Gadādhara. Needless to say, since he never married, Acyutānanda had no children.

## 18

> Subheading: (2) Kṛṣṇa Miśra —

Kṛṣṇa Miśra. In the Sanskrit book *Advaita-carita*: “Acyuta, Kṛṣṇa Miśra and Gopāla dāsa — these three jewels are said to be born from the ocean of Sītā's womb.” Of Śrī Advaita Prabhu's six sons, three brothers — “Acyuta, Kṛṣṇa Miśra and Gopāla” — were engaged in the service of Śrī Gaurāṅga. *Gaura-gaṇoddeśa*, verse 88: “Some say that Kṛṣṇa Miśra is Kārttikeya, because he is like him.”

Kṛṣṇa Miśra had two sons: (1) Raghunātha Cakravartī and (2) Dolagovinda. Raghunātha's descendants live in Śāntipura, in the Madana-Gopāla quarter, and in Gaṇakara, Mṛjāpura and Kumārakhāli. Dolagovinda had three sons: (1) Cāṅda, (2) Kandarpa, (3) Gopīnātha. Kandarpa's descendants live in Māladaha and Jikābāḍī. Gopīnātha had three sons: (1) Śrīvallabha, (2) Prāṇavallabha and (3) Keśava. Śrīvallabha's descendants live in Maśiyāḍārā (Mahiṣaḍerā?), Dāmukadiyā, Caṇḍīpura and other places. The Maśiyāḍārā line descends from Śrīvallabha's eldest son, Gaṅgānārāyaṇa, and the lines in the villages of Dāmukadiyā, Caṇḍīpura, Śolamāri and others from his youngest son, Rāmagopāla. The descendants of Prāṇavallabha and Keśava live in Uthalī.

Prāṇavallabha's son was Ratneśvara; his son was Kṛṣṇarāma; Kṛṣṇarāma's youngest son was Lakṣmīnārāyaṇa, and his son was Navakiśora. Navakiśora's second son was Rāmamohana. Rāmamohana's eldest son, “Jagabandhu,” and his third son, “Vīracandra,” entered the order of mendicants (*bhikṣu-āśrama*) and installed a Deity of Śrī Mahāprabhu at Kāṭoyā. People called them “Baḍa Prabhu” and “Choṭa Prabhu,” the elder and the younger Prabhu. It was they who started the circumambulation (*parikramā*) of Śrī Dhāma Navadvīpa. For the complete genealogy of Kṛṣṇa Miśra see the article “Advaita-vaṁśa” in the *Vaiṣṇava-mañjuṣā* (issue 4).

## 27

Balarāma, Svarūpa and Jagadīśa. In the Sanskrit book *Advaita-carita*: “The fourth is called Balarāma, the fifth Svarūpa, and the sixth is named Jagadīśa: the Ācārya had six sons.” All three turned away from Gaura; they are *smārtas*, or Māyāvādīs, and therefore not Vaiṣṇavas.

Balarāma's three wives bore him nine sons. The youngest son of the first wife, Madhusūdana, became known as “Gosāñi Bhaṭṭācārya” and adopted the religion of the *smārtas*. His son Rādhāramaṇa took the name “Gosvāmī Bhaṭṭācārya” and so degraded the word “*gosvāmī*,” a title fit only for one who has left home. Following the *smārta* Raghunandana, he burned a *kuśa-puttalikā* (an effigy of *kuśa* grass) of Śrī Advaita Prabhu and performed over it the *śrāddha* rite done for a ghost (*preta*) or a demon (*rākṣasa*). By acting against the *smṛtis* devoted to Viṣṇu-bhakti, such as the *Śrī Hari-bhakti-vilāsa*, he displayed his foolishness and a great offense. Without being a pure devotee, he wrote several books and commentaries on primary works; pure devotees do not value them. For Balarāma's genealogy see the *Mañjuṣā* (issue 4).

## 28

> Subheading: (4) Kamalākānta —

Kamalākānta Viśvāsa. The “Kamalānanda” mentioned in Ādi, Chapter 10, verse 149, and the “Kamalākānta” of Madhya, Chapter 10, verse 94, are probably the same person. And Viśvāsa Kamalākānta is the very man of that name mentioned in Ādi, Chapter 10, verse 119. The brāhmaṇa Kamalākānta is one of the Lord's own associates. Kamalākānta Viśvāsa, being Advaita's servant, is also the Lord's associate. When Śrī Paramānanda Purī came from Navadvīpa to Nīlācala, he brought with him the brāhmaṇa Kamalākānta, or Kamalānanda, a resident of Navadvīpa. Madhya, Chapter 10, 94: “There was a devotee of the Lord, a brāhmaṇa named ‘Kamalākānta’; taking him along, he set out for Nīlācala.” (CC, Madhya 10.94)
