> Subheading: To deliver the wretched is already the nature of a great soul like Him; moreover, He is the Lord who acts by His own will —

By their coming the pure devotees (*śrī-bhāgavatas*) purify the holy places, and on the pretext of these journeys they deliver the worldly people who live there. This is the eternal nature of pure devotees, who suffer at the suffering of others. But Śrī Mahāprabhu, although He performs His pastimes in the form of a dependent devotee, is Himself the independent Supreme Lord. ***Niścala*** — unmoving, everlasting, eternal.

## 23

> Subheading: The Bhaṭṭācārya conveys the king's order to Kāśī Miśra; Miśra's joy —

All *jīvas* who regard themselves as His servants call Bhagavān Himself, Śrīman Mahāprabhu, “Prabhupāda.” Śrīman Nityānanda Prabhu and Śrīmad Advaita Prabhu are likewise called “Prabhupāda,” for all of Them are the *viṣaya-vigraha*, *viṣṇu-tattva*, and Viṣṇu alone is the eternal master (*prabhu*) of the *jīva*. Further, Śrī Śrī Gurudeva, the *āśraya-vigraha* who knows the truth of Kṛṣṇa, is for the disciple, who stands below him, “Kṛṣṇa-Caitanya” Himself, the very form of “Hari”; therefore he is called “Oṁ Viṣṇupāda.” Besides him, every other pure devotee, every pure Vaiṣṇava, is called “Śrīpāda” by all *jīvas* who stand in the position of disciples. But the guru, the Vaiṣṇavas, and the disciples they have accepted are all, each to the other, “*prabhu*,” a word that denotes one worthy of worship. This true conclusion is widely applied in authoritative books such as the *Bhāgavatam*, the *Caritāmṛta*, and the *Caitanya-bhāgavata*, and in the *sampradāya* of pure devotees. Some cheats who are *gosvāmīs* in name only (*gosvāmi-bruvas*), *prākṛta-sahajiyās* and non-Vaiṣṇavas, along with their foolish, cheated disciples, call themselves “servant of the servants of the Vaiṣṇavas” or “a mere semblance of a Vaiṣṇava's servant” and so make a show of humility. Yet this is only deceit and hypocrisy. In truth, deep in their hearts, out of hostility to Viṣṇu, they consider the word “Prabhupāda” to be tied to lineage by birth (*śaukra*) and to be their own possession. Therefore they regard the true guru or Vaiṣṇava who knows the truth of Kṛṣṇa as a mortal, and caste pride is strong in them. All this is only a sign of their misfortune and an aid on their journey to hell.

## 102

> Subheading: The arrival of Śrī Dāmodara Svarūpa and his special qualities —

**Svarūpa-Dāmodara.** Among the Vedic *sannyāsīs* of the ten names (*daśanāmī*) we find a rule introduced by Śrī Śaṅkarācārya. When someone wishes to take *sannyāsa* from the *daṇḍīs* of the two orders “Tīrtha” and “Āśrama,” the *daṇḍī* guru gives the disciple a *brahmacārī* name according to the rules for *naiṣṭhika-brahmacārīs*. In this way Śrī Puruṣottama Ācārya of Navadvīpa received the *brahmacārī* name “Dāmodara-Svarūpa.” When the *yogapaṭṭa* of *sannyāsa* is received, the *naiṣṭhika-brahmacārī's* title “Svarūpa” is replaced by the *sannyāsa* title “Tīrtha.”

## 108

> Subheading: Why he was named “Svarūpa” —

He performed the rites of *sannyāsa* — the eight *śrāddhas*, the *virajā-homa*, the shaving of the *śikhā*, the giving up of the sacred thread, and so on — but did not wait for the guru's summons, the *yogapaṭṭa*, the *sannyāsa* name, the staff, and the rest. Therefore the name “Dāmodara Svarūpa,” which indicates *naiṣṭhika-brahmacarya*, remained with him.

## 119

> Subheading: In the *Śrī Caitanya-candrodaya-nāṭaka*, Dāmodara-Svarūpa enters during the conversation of the Lord and Sārvabhauma and offers a prayer (8.14) —

**he dayā-nidhe śrī-caitanya**, **helod-dhūlita-khedayā** (by which, playfully, without effort, distress, the anguish of the mind, has been scattered and removed) **viśadayā** (pure, illuminating all) **pronmīlad-āmodayā** (in which *āmoda*, the highest bliss, is fully unfolding and manifest) **śāmyac-chāstra-vivādayā** (in which the disputes of the *śāstras*, argument and counterargument, subside) **rasa-dayā** (*rasa-dā*, bestowing *mādhurya* and the other *rasas*) **cittārpitonmādayā** (by which *unmāda* is placed in the heart: indifference to the body and the like; or the bewilderment of the heart born of great bliss, calamity, separation and so on, that is, *divyonmāda*) **śaśvad-bhakti-vinodayā** (which by its very nature ceaselessly impels one to devotion) **sa-madayā** (with *mada*, the exultation born of an abundance of the love-transformations caused by Anaṅga, which takes away discrimination; with the reading *śama-dayā*, free from thirst for anything but Kṛṣṇa) **mādhurya-maryādayā** (in which is the limit, the summit of sweetness; the instrumental case of a qualifier) **tava** **amandodayā** (*manda* is constraint; *amanda*, free from it, is the highest good; in which it rises) **dayā** (upon me) **bhūyāt** (may it be).

Bhagavān Caitanyacandra, magnanimous, the embodiment of *prema*, distributes His compassion in three ways to *jīvas* who possess *sukṛti*. The *jīva*, dejected by material wants, tries by all sorts of means to drive away his suffering, but does not succeed. The Lord's mercy cannot be won by the *jīva's* own effort. By the Lord's mercy the fragrance of Kṛṣṇa's lotus feet unfolds in the *jīva's* heart. Then the dust of the mind's distress blows away without effort, and the heart becomes pure. Then the highest bliss, born of service to Kṛṣṇa, becomes manifest in the heart. Because of the different interpretations of the *śāstras*, disputes arise in the mind and give rise to many arguments and counterarguments. But as soon as the *jīva* obtains the Lord's mercy, the heart that has received that mercy becomes intoxicated with the Lord's *rasa*. And the intoxication that bestows *kṛṣṇa-rasa* also arises only by the power of the Lord's mercy; therefore the disputes over the *śāstras* subside. The limit of sweetness (*mādhurya-maryādā*) keeps the *jīva* always at Kṛṣṇa's feet, and the fortunate *jīva* then finds joy in *prema-bhakti* alone. Kṛṣṇa's mercy is pure, bestows *rasa*, and is full of *mada*.

When by Kṛṣṇa's mercy the heart is gradually purified, no dirt of distress born of want remains in it. When by Kṛṣṇa's mercy the *jīva* obtains *rasa*, the disputes over the *śāstras* subside and the conclusion of devotion becomes firm; therefore the heart goes mad with love for Kṛṣṇa. Gradually attaining peace by Kṛṣṇa's mercy, the *jīva* ceaselessly delights in devotion in the majesty of sweetness.

At first the *jīva* is turned away from the Lord and wearied by worldly objects; next he seeks the Lord; and finally he is devoted to the Lord's service. By the Lord's mercy, first his *anarthas* cease; from this the heart becomes pure, and the purity of the heart results in the unfolding of the bliss of Kṛṣṇa. By the Lord's mercy, in the middle stage the *jīva* grasps the conclusion of devotion, through it obtains *rasa*, and attains the madness of love. By the Lord's mercy, in the end attachment to devotion arises; from it the Lord's pastimes appear to him everywhere (*sphūrti*), and from *sphūrti* he reaches the summit of sweetness. Thus by Kṛṣṇa's mercy a *jīva* who is free from thirst, that is, liberated, loses all attraction to anything but Kṛṣṇa through the service of Kṛṣṇa's *kīrtana*. One who seeks liberation, on receiving the medicine for the disease of worldly existence, gives up the desire for liberation and realizes the Supreme Lord. And a *viṣayī*, by the power of Kṛṣṇa's mercy, through hearing the descriptions of Hari's qualities, delightful to the ear and the mind, gives up worldly enjoyment and can become established in pure devotion. Therefore one should always take shelter of the Lord's mercy alone.

## 137

> Subheading: The Lord's wise reply: such is the conduct of the Lord, that is, of one who is powerful; the distinction between love and mercy and formal respect (*maryādā*) —

**Śrī Īśvara Purī** was a *sannyāsī* of the Vaiṣṇavas of Śrī Madhva. How could he make Govinda, a brāhmaṇa by initiation (*daikṣa-brāhmaṇa*) born in a *śūdra* family, his disciple as a “servant”? This was the reason for Sārvabhauma's question. According to the *smṛti*, if a brāhmaṇa accepts someone of another *varṇa* as a disciple or servant, the brāhmaṇa guru becomes fallen. How then could Īśvara Purī, a man of impeccable conduct, transgress what the *smṛti* prescribes? In answer Mahāprabhu said: “My gurudeva is an ‘*īśvara*,’ that is, a master of the world; therefore he is not subject to the *smṛti*, which governs ordinary *jīvas*. The mercy of the gurudeva, who is an *īśvara*, that is, one who is powerful, is never subject to Vedic rule.”

## 138

The Supreme Lord Kṛṣṇa, the guru of the world, silenced worldly considerations of caste and family and ate in the house of Vidura. In the same way My master mercifully set aside considerations of Govinda's birth in a *śūdra* family and the like. Knowing that a Vaiṣṇava is worthy of brāhmaṇa status by initiation (*daikṣa-vipratva*), he gave him initiation and accepted him as his “servant.”

## 154

> Subheading: The Lord is pained to see Bhāratī dressed in deerskin —

Brahmānanda Bhāratī was one of the *sannyāsīs* of the ten names (*daśanāmī*) of Śaṅkara's school. Deerskin, or clothing of grass, bark and the like, is worn only by one who has left home. The *Manu-saṁhitā* (Chapter 6): “Having left the village and gone to the forest, let him live with his senses controlled… let him wear a skin or rags.” The commentary of Kullūka Bhaṭṭa: “let him cover himself with the skin of a deer or another animal, or with a piece of cloth.”

But to put on clothing of skin for the sake of *loka-saṅgraha* (attracting people), under the sway of pride, does not deliver one from worldly existence. The *Manu-saṁhitā* (Chapter 6): “Although the fruit of the *kataka* tree clears water, the water does not become clear merely by pronouncing its name.”[^154-1] Kullūka: “The fruit of the *kataka* tree makes muddy water clear. Yet the water is cleared not by uttering its name, but by throwing the fruit into it. Likewise the mere wearing of outward marks (*liṅga*) is not the cause of *dharma*.”

> Subheading: The comparison with Bilvamaṅgala —

## 175–177

Even when the disciple's words are true, the guru's words prevail over the disciple's. At all times the guru's words deserve more respect than the disciple's. Mahāprabhu said that by this rule Brahmānanda Bhāratī was the guru, and Mahāprabhu Śrī Kṛṣṇa Caitanya considered Himself his disciple; therefore Brahmānanda's words had won. But Brahmānanda did not accept that the cause of Mahāprabhu's defeat was the rule of “guru and disciple” He had cited. He said there was another cause. Bhagavān accepts defeat before His devotee; such is the nature of Godhood. So says Bhīṣma (*Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam* 1.9.34[^175-1]): “To keep my promise true, He broke His own. He leapt down from the chariot, seized a wheel and rushed at me — the earth trembling under Him, His upper cloth falling away — like a lion rushing to kill an elephant.”

“All my life I was devoted to meditation on the formless Brahman. But when I saw You, the transcendental form of Śrī Kṛṣṇa arose before me. Kṛṣṇa's name appears in my mouth and in my mind, and my eyes see Kṛṣṇa. And seeing Kṛṣṇa's form in You, my heart has been filled with longing. In his earlier life Ṭhākura Bilvamaṅgala was an *advaita-vādī* devoted to meditation on the formless Brahman; later he became a devotee of Kṛṣṇa and told his own story. Today the very same thing has happened to me.”

The *Śrī Caitanya-candrāmṛta*: “For those touched by the glory of His merciful glance, *kaivalya* becomes like hell… — that Gaura alone we praise”; “…and they scorn the knowers of Brahman and of *yoga* — to that Gauracandra we bow”; “Only until one sees a dear servant of Śrī Caitanya's lotus feet does talk of Brahman and the path of liberation fail to turn bitter, does the order of the world and of the Vedas remain unshaken, and do the scholars of the *śāstras* keep up their noisy quarrels over various external paths”; “A certain Gaura, a thief of irresistible power, has stolen everything I had.”

## 178

> Subheading: Kṛṣṇa's mere wish suffices to destroy one's devotion to *karma* and *jñāna* —

**advaita-vīthī-pathikaiḥ** (by the travelers on the path of *advaita*: *advaita*, that which is free of internal, homogeneous and heterogeneous distinctions, is itself the path, and the travelers on it are the *kevalādvaita-vādīs*, who teach the formless Brahman) **upāsyāḥ** (worshiped) **svānanda-siṁhāsana-labdha-dīkṣāḥ** (initiated on the throne of one's own bliss: the bliss of the self is the throne, the high seat; those like the followers of the path of *yoga* receive initiation on it) **vayam** (we, that is, I; the plural is one of respect) **kena api** **śaṭhena** (by some cheat) **gopa-vadhū-viṭena** (by a rogue enamored of the cowherd wives, the son of Nanda) **haṭhena** (by force) **dāsī-kṛtāḥ** (made a maidservant, engaged in his service; to be understood in the singular).

## 179–181

Śrī Mahāprabhu said: “You, Brahmānanda Bhāratī, are a *mahā-bhāgavata* full of *prema*; therefore you will see Kṛṣṇa everywhere. What doubt is there?” The Bhaṭṭācārya, acting as mediator between them, said: “Mahāprabhu says that the *mahā-bhāgavata* Brahmānanda Bhāratī has seen Kṛṣṇa, and this too is true. For Kṛṣṇa reveals Himself directly before a *mahā-bhāgavata*. But without an abundance of *prema* in the devotee such a vision is impossible.” The first *iṅhāra* means “by Śrī Mahāprabhu's mercy”; the second *iṅhāra* means “of Brahmānanda Bhāratī”: he had *darśana*, that is, he saw Kṛṣṇa.[^180-1] “With eyes of devotion anointed with the salve of *prema*, the saints always see Him, even within their hearts.” (*Brahma-saṁhitā*, Chapter 5[^181-1])
