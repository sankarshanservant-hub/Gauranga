# Amṛta-pravāha-bhāṣya on Madhya-līlā 19

## 0

**Summary.** After seeing Mahāprabhu in the village of Rāmakeli, Rūpa and Sanātana began to seek a way to give up their worldly affairs. To gain shelter at Caitanya’s feet, they had two *puraścaraṇas* of the “Kṛṣṇa mantra” performed. Rūpa Gosvāmī left ten thousand coins in Gauḍa, loaded all his accumulated wealth onto a boat and went to Bākalā-Candradvīpa. There he divided the money among the *brāhmaṇas*, the Vaiṣṇavas and his relatives, and set part of it aside to avert punishment and imprisonment. To learn on what day Mahāprabhu would set out for Vṛndāvana by the forest path, he sent two messengers to Puruṣottama-kṣetra. Meanwhile Śrī Sanātana Gosvāmī stayed at home on the pretext of illness and began to discuss the *Bhāgavatam* and other scriptures with *paṇḍitas*. The ruler of Gauḍa, the emperor Husain Shāh, first through a physician and then by seeing for himself, understood that Sanātana had given up the royal service on that pretext. He confined Sanātana in jail (prison) and himself marched to war against Orissa. When Mahāprabhu set out by the forest path, Śrī Rūpa Gosvāmī, leaving home, sent word to Sanātana Gosvāmī and went with his brother Anupama Mallika to find Mahāprabhu. Reaching Prayāga, he stayed near Mahāprabhu for ten days. Meanwhile Vallabha Bhaṭṭa invited Mahāprabhu and showed Him special honour, and Mahāprabhu introduced Śrī Rūpa to Vallabha Bhaṭṭa. After that Raghupati Upādhyāya arrived there and had many conversations with Mahāprabhu about *rasa*. At this point Kavirāja Gosvāmī partly describes the life of Śrī Rūpa and Śrī Sanātana in Vraja. During the ten days in Prayāga Mahāprabhu taught Śrī Rūpa the truth of *bhakti-rasa* in the form of *sūtras* and ordered him to compose the *Rasāmṛta-sindhu*. Sending Śrī Rūpa from there to Vṛndāvana, Mahāprabhu went to Kāśī and stayed at the house of Candraśekhara.

## 1

Before creation the Lord inspired in Brahmā’s heart the truth about Bhagavān, comprising *sambandha*, *abhidheya* and *prayojana*. In the same way, full of eagerness, He infused His own power into Rūpa Gosvāmī and spread anew the account of Vṛndāvana’s pastimes full of *rasa*, an account that had been lost over time.

## 8

*Daṇḍa-bandha*: to avert an impending calamity, such as royal punishment or imprisonment.

## 15

*Chadma*: a pretext.

## 16

When Sanātana Gosvāmī was the royal minister, several “Kāyastha” officials served under him. Seeing Sanātana’s mood of renunciation, some of them, hoping to get his post, began to show special diligence in the royal business. Tradition says that when Sanātana Gosvāmī resigned, his subordinate, the well-known official Purandara Khān, received that post.

## 24–27

It is said that the emperor Husain Shāh regarded Sanātana Gosvāmī as his “younger brother.” When Sanātana showed his firm resolve to give up his post, Husain Shāh said in loving anger: “I am your ‘elder brother.’ I do not govern the kingdom at all: I merely go about with my army, plundering one country after another in war, and, being a ‘Yavana’ by birth, I hunt in the districts of Gauḍa and destroy many living beings and beasts, and that is all. You are my only support. If I, your elder brother, am occupied only in plunder and in destroying living beings, and you, my younger brother, also give up your work and ruin everything, how will the kingdom go on?” Sanātana replied with a hidden meaning: “You are the ruler of Gauḍa, an independent king, the dispenser of punishment. Whoever has committed a fault, give him its fruit.” These words hold a deep secret. The king himself behaves like a bandit, so let him take the fruit of that. And when the minister (that is, I) is idle in his work, let him (me) receive the fruit of dismissal. Understanding from this what Sanātana wanted, the ruler of Gauḍa got up and left.

## 33

*Āmi dui bhāi* (“we two brothers”): I, Rūpa, and my (younger) brother Anupama, or Vallabha.

## 50

One who reads the four Vedas, that is, a Caube *brāhmaṇa*, does not become a “devotee” just by being so. My devotee is dear to Me even if he is a *caṇḍāla*. Only a devotee is truly a worthy recipient of gifts and truly a worthy giver from whom one may accept. Every devotee is as worthy of worship as I am.

## 53

O Lord, most magnanimous, giver of love for Kṛṣṇa, Kṛṣṇa Himself, bearing the name Kṛṣṇa Caitanya and the form of Gaurāṅga, I offer obeisance to You.

## 54

That merciful Lord cured the world, maddened by ignorance, of the disease of ignorance and intoxicated it with the nectar of the treasure of His love. I take shelter of that Śrī Kṛṣṇa Caitanya, whose deeds are wonderful.

## 61

Vallabha Bhaṭṭa was a Vaiṣṇava *paṇḍita*. At first he entered Śrī Mahāprabhu’s *sampradāya*, but, not receiving much honour there, he became an *ācārya* in the Viṣṇusvāmī *sampradāya*. It is he whom people call “Vallabhācārya.” He has great influence in Gokula and in the Bombay region. He wrote many books, such as the *Anubhāṣya* and the *Ṣoḍaśa-grantha*.

Āḍāila-grāma: the village of Aḍelī, or Āḍāila, on the other bank of the Yamunā near the confluence (about a mile away). An ancient Viṣṇu temple of the “Vallabhī” *sampradāya* still stands there.

## 74–75

One whose conduct is pure, whose taint of low birth has been burned up by the blazing fire of true *bhakti*, even if he is a *caṇḍāla*, is honoured by *paṇḍitas*. But an atheist, even one who knows the Vedas, is not worthy of honour. The noble birth, scriptural knowledge, *japa* and austerity of one devoid of devotion to Bhagavān are like ornaments on a dead body: they serve no purpose and merely please people.

## 83

That region was largely devoid of *prema*, and Vallabha Bhaṭṭa, who was present, was a man much given to argument. Seeing this, Mahāprabhu restrained Himself.

## 92

Several *ślokas* composed by Raghupati Upādhyāya are found in the *Padyāvalī*. He lived in Tirhut, that is, in the land of Mithilā.

## 96

Let those who fear worldly existence worship, some the *śruti*, some the *smṛti*, and others the *Mahābhārata*. But I (here) honour Śrī Nanda alone, on whose veranda (*alinda*) the Supreme Brahman, Kṛṣṇa, plays.

## 98

To whom can I say it, and who now will believe, that in the groves of the Sun’s daughter the Supreme Brahman, the lover (*lampaṭa*) of the cowherd wives, performs His pastimes?

## 106

Śyāma’s form is the best of forms, Madhupurī the best of cities, the age of *kaiśora* is the one to meditate upon, and the original *rasa*, that is, *śṛṅgāra*, is the best of *rasas*.

## 115

*Prānta*: the limit.

## 119

Over time the account of the pastimes of Vṛndāvana had been lost. To spread those pastimes in a special way, Śrī Gaurāṅgadeva there and then anointed Śrī Rūpa and Śrī Sanātana with the nectar of His mercy.

## 120

Śrī Rūpa had previously been tightly bound by many qualities dear to him, yet he became free from household life. Gaurāṅgadeva Himself — like *rasa*: intangible, as *rasa* is, yet the supreme embodied one — favoured him in Prayāga together with his younger brother Anupama with loving conversations and firm embraces.

## 121

Śrī Rūpa Gosvāmī is dear to the Lord by his very nature, beloved by nature, *prema* embodied, endowed with natural captivating beauty, the principal form, like the Lord Himself: he is the Lord’s own *vilāsa*. Through such a Śrī Rūpa the Lord spread (the scriptures of *bhakti-rasa*).

## 129

*Karoṅyā*: the water pot that *sannyāsīs* carry in the hand.

## 134

I bow to the lotus feet of Śrī Caitanyadeva Hari, by whose inspiration in my heart I, a wretched pauper, have taken up the writing of a book on *bhakti*.

## 140

If the tip of a hair is divided into a hundred parts, and one of them again into a hundred, the minute *svarūpa* of the *jīva* is like such a part. The *jīva* is a particle of spirit (*cit-kaṇa*), and the *jīvas* are countless.

## 141

If the hundredth part of the tip of a hair is divided many hundreds of times, the resulting part is minute, and the *jīva* is just as minute. So say the principal *śrutis*.

## 142

Some manuscripts read thus, with Śrī Bhagavān’s words to Uddhava in the *Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam*: “Among those possessing qualities I am the *sūtra*, among the great I am the *mahat*, among the subtle I am the *jīva*, among the hard to conquer I am the mind.” Among the subtle I (Bhagavān) am the “*jīva*” (a manifestation of oneness and difference).

## 143

O Dhruva (Eternal One)! If the embodied *jīvas* were unlimited, eternal (*dhruva*) in the sense of supreme eternity, and all-pervading, there would be no law that they are subject to Your rule. Only if the *jīva* is accepted as “atomic” and “eternal” in the ordinary sense is it subject to You. The *jīvas* were born consisting of that cause; only by not abandoning them can it be their controller. Therefore the opinion of those who consider the *jīva* and You to be “one” is corrupted by “*matavāda*” (the fault of mere opinion).

## 144–149

*Jīvas* are of two kinds: eternally liberated and eternally conditioned. The eternally conditioned are divided into the immobile and the mobile. Those who are motionless (such as trees) are “immobile” (*sthāvara*) *jīvas*, and those who move are “mobile” (*jaṅgama*). The mobile are of three kinds: beasts and birds, aquatics and land-dwellers. Among land-dwellers humans are very few. If from these few humans one excludes the *mlecchas*, *pulindas*, Buddhists and *śabaras*, those faithful to the Vedas remain. Those faithful to the Vedas are of two kinds: those who follow *dharma* and those who do not. Among those who follow *dharma*, many are devoted to *karma* and some are devoted to knowledge. Among millions devoted to knowledge only one is truly “liberated” (*mukta*); here those free from material understanding are called “liberated.” And among these liberated, one who, endowed with faith, has turned to the service of Kṛṣṇa is a “devotee of Kṛṣṇa.” A devotee of Kṛṣṇa has no desires. All the others, up to the “liberated” mentioned above, have desires. Those who follow *dharma* and those devoted to *karma* are “desirers of enjoyment” (*bhukti-kāmī*), the knowers up to the liberated are “desirers of liberation” (*mukti-kāmī*), and some of them also “desire *siddhis*,” the fruits of *yoga*. As long as these three kinds of desire remain in their hearts, those desires give them no peace; therefore they are all “restless” (*aśānta*). Hence only the desireless devotee of Kṛṣṇa is peaceful (*śānta*), that is, has attained peace.

## 150

O great sage, among many millions of the liberated and the perfected, a person devoted to Nārāyaṇa, peaceful in heart, is extremely rare.

## 151–164

The *jīvas*, each following the thread of his own *karma*, wander through the brahmāṇḍa, taking birth in various species. Among them, one in whom good fortune dawns — *sukṛti* capable of giving birth to devotion — receives by the mercy of the guru and Kṛṣṇa the seed of the creeper of devotion, which is “faith” (*śraddhā*). Having received this seed, he becomes a gardener and plants it in the field of his heart. The planted seed sprouts, and the gardener waters that field with the water of hearing and chanting — of narrations about Bhagavān and about His devotees. The creeper of devotion springs up and grows and grows; it pierces this brahmāṇḍa made of *māyā*, pierces the Virajā and the effulgent Brahmaloka, and finds its place in Paravyoma. In Paravyoma the creeper spreads and rises still higher, up to Goloka Vṛndāvana, and climbs onto the desire tree of Kṛṣṇa’s feet. Only on the creeper of devotion that has climbed onto Kṛṣṇa’s feet does the “fruit of *prema*” grow. Until then the gardener keeps watering it with the water of hearing and chanting.

At this time, besides watering, there is one more task. After some days of watering, when the creeper begins to grow, some wicked animal may come and uproot or tear it, and the creeper withers. Here the wicked animal is offence to a Vaiṣṇava. It is offence to a Vaiṣṇava that, like a mad elephant, does all this damage. At this time, therefore, the gardener takes special care: he puts up a fence and makes an enclosure, and then the elephant of offence does not appear. The offences to a Vaiṣṇava, or offences to the holy name, are ten (see Ādi 8.24).

At this time there is one more trouble. As the creeper of devotion rises, side shoots may grow too much, and this does it harm. The side shoots are the desire for *bhukti*, the desire for *mukti*, forbidden conduct, duplicity (*kuṭīnāṭī*), the tendency to harm living beings, the craving for gain, and the hope for material honour or position. Unless one is especially careful, then from the water of hearing, chanting and so on the side shoots grow most of all, to the detriment of the main creeper, and the main shoot is checked and cannot grow. The gardener must therefore cut away these *anarthas* — the side shoots — from the very beginning, while he is still watering with hearing and chanting. Only then will the main shoot grow and reach Vṛndāvana. This *prema* is the *jīva*’s highest goal (*parama-puruṣārtha*); beside it the four goals of life — *dharma*, *artha*, *kāma* and *mokṣa* — are worth no more than a blade of grass.

## 165

As long as not even a trace of *prema* — of *dāsya* and the other *rasas*, which act like a perfect herb for bringing Kṛṣṇa under control — has become a traveller on the path of the heart, so long the *jīva* is dazzled by the glitter of each of these: the supremacy of the rich *siddhis*, *samādhi* founded on *dharma* such as truthfulness, and the highest bliss of Brahman.

## 166

*Prema* does not arise from a semblance of devotion (*bhakty-ābhāsa*); *prema* arises only from pure devotion.

## 168

The characteristic of pure devotion is this. In pure devotion there can be no desire other than the desire for one’s own advancement (on the path to spiritual perfection) for the sake of serving Kṛṣṇa. There can be no worship of any object of worship other than Kṛṣṇa — of Brahman, the Paramātmā or any other form — nor can *jñāna* and *karma* be present in their own forms. To be free of all this, to accept in life only what is favourable to devotion, and with all the senses to cultivate service to Kṛṣṇa (*kṛṣṇānuśīlana*) — this is called “pure devotion.”

## 170

Serving Hṛṣīkeśa with all the senses is called “devotion.” This service (which constitutes the characteristic of essence, the *svarūpa-lakṣaṇa*) has two “marginal” (*taṭastha*) characteristics: such pure devotion remains free of all designations (*upādhis*), and, being directed to Kṛṣṇa alone, is itself pure.

## 174

Such devotion is called “absolute *bhakti-yoga*” (*ātyantika-bhakti-yoga*). By this *bhakti-yoga* the *jīva* transcends *māyā*, which is made of the *guṇas*, and attains pure love for Me.

## 176

The desire for enjoyment and the desire for liberation are two witches (*piśācīs*). As long as they dwell in someone’s heart, the happiness of devotion cannot dawn in that heart.

## 177–181

Devotion has three states: the state of practice (*sādhana*), the state of *bhāva*, and the state of *prema*. The nine limbs — hearing, chanting and the rest — are at first performed only in *sādhana-bhakti*. The more the *anarthas* mentioned above diminish as one hears and chants with faith, the higher faith itself (*śraddhā-vṛtti*) gradually rises, and it becomes known by the names *niṣṭhā*, *ruci*, *āsakti*, and *bhāva* or *rati*. *Rati* arises from *sādhana-bhakti*; the denser this *rati* grows through engagement (cultivation) in hearing, chanting and so on, the more it takes the names *prema* and the rest. (Gradually) *prema*, as it increases, rises to *sneha*, *māna*, *praṇaya*, *rāga*, *anurāga*, *bhāva* and *mahābhāva*. Here is an example. Sugarcane juice is like the seed corresponding to *rati*; the denser it grows, the further it passes through the stages of molasses (*guḍa*), then *khaṇḍasāra*, *śarkarā*, *sitā-misri* and the finest *misri*. Everything from *rati* to *mahābhāva* is known in *kṛṣṇa-bhakti-rasa* as *sthāyi-bhāva*; but everywhere it is *rati* above all that is called the *sthāyi-bhāva*.

When the four *bhāvas* — *vibhāva*, *anubhāva*, *sāttvika* and *vyabhicārī* — combine with the *sthāyi-bhāva*, *rasa* arises. When, in devotion to Kṛṣṇa, these ingredients join the *sthāyi-bhāva*, there is “*kṛṣṇa-bhakti-rasa*.” The *sthāyi-bhāva* is the chief foundation on which *rasa* is kindled; with it the four ingredients — *vibhāva* and the rest — are combined. Thus the *sthāyi-bhāva* is the “root” of *rasa*, *vibhāva* its “cause,” *anubhāva* its “effect,” *sāttvika-bhāva* a special “effect,” and the *sañcārī* or *vyabhicārī bhāvas* its “helpers.” *Vibhāva* is divided into two kinds, *ālambana* and *uddīpana*. *Ālambana* is again divided into two, *viṣaya* and *āśraya*. In *kṛṣṇa-bhakti-rasa* the devotee is the *āśraya*, Kṛṣṇa is the *viṣaya*, and Kṛṣṇa’s qualities[^177-181-1] are the *uddīpana*.

The *anubhāvas* are of thirteen kinds: (1) dancing, (2) rolling on the ground, (3) singing, (4) loud crying, (5) stretching the body, (6) roaring (*huṅkāra*), (7) yawning, (8) heavy breathing, (9) disregard for public opinion, (10) drooling, (11) loud laughter, (12) reeling, (13) hiccups. Not all the signs of the *anubhāvas* appear at once: as the *rasa* works, now one sign and now another appears. The *sāttvika-bhāvas* are of eight kinds, and the *sañcārī* or *vyabhicārī bhāvas* number thirty-three (see the *Amṛta-pravāha-bhāṣya* on Madhya 14.167).

## 186

The “principal *rasas*” (*mukhya-rasas*) are of five kinds: *śānta*, *dāsya*, *sakhya*, *vātsalya* and *madhura*. Laughter (*hāsya*), wonder (*adbhuta*), heroism (*vīra*), compassion (*karuṇa*), anger (*raudra*), fear (*bhayānaka*) and disgust (*bībhatsa*) are the seven kinds of “secondary *rasa*” (*gauṇa-rasa*).

## 188

The five principal *rasas* mentioned above abide in the devotee’s heart permanently, as *sthāyi-bhāvas*. The secondary *rasas* — laughter, wonder and the rest — arise in the devotee’s heart as visitors when a “cause” is present; they nourish the principal *rasa* and then subside.

## 190

“In Vraja,” Śrīdāmā and others; “in the city,” in the pastimes of Dvārakā, Bhīma and Arjuna.

## 192–194

*Kṛṣṇa-rati* is of two kinds: mixed with awareness of majesty (*aiśvarya*), and *kevalā* — that is, devoid of awareness of majesty. In the “two cities” — Dvārakā and Mathurā — and in Vaikuṇṭha and other abodes, devotion is mixed with awareness of majesty. Therefore *prema* there is contracted. But in Gokula the cowherd men and women, whose *rati* is *kevalā*, do not wish to acknowledge Kṛṣṇa’s majesty even when they see it.

## 195

*Kāṅhā* (somewhere) means in certain places.

## 197

Devakī and Vasudeva, knowing Baladeva and Śrī Kṛṣṇa to be the “Lords of the universe,” became afraid and could not embrace Them.

## 199–200

Thinking of You as a friend and not knowing Your greatness, out of carelessness or out of love I rashly said to You, “O Kṛṣṇa, O Yādava, O friend!” For all that I said in this way, and for all the disrespect I showed You in jest — while eating, at play, lying down or sitting, alone or before everyone — for all this, O You whose nature is immeasurable, I beg forgiveness.

## 202

When in Dvārakā Kṛṣṇa teased Rukmiṇī, her reason was lost through intense pain, fear and grief: the fan fell from her hand, from which the bracelets were slipping; her hair came loose; and her body, like a banana tree struck by the wind, suddenly gave way and she fainted.

## 204

Kṛṣṇa, whose greatness is sung by the three Vedas, the Upaniṣads, *sāṅkhya-yoga* and the scriptures on devotion — Yaśodā knows Him as her “son.”

## 205

Him who is unmanifest and beyond the senses — Adhokṣaja, who appeared as if in a human body — Yaśodā, thinking Him her son, bound with a rope to the mortar like an ordinary child.

## 206

Bhagavān Kṛṣṇa, having lost, carried Śrīdāmā on His shoulders; Bhadrasena carried Vṛṣabha, and Pralamba carried Rohiṇī’s son Baladeva.

## 207–209

“Leaving the gopīs who long for Him, this beloved Kṛṣṇa lovingly serves me” — with such pride (Rādhikā thought herself the best of all the gopīs, and at last), going into the forest, Rādhikā said to Śrī Kṛṣṇa, “O Kṛṣṇa, I can’t walk any further; carry me wherever You like.” When Rādhikā said this, Kṛṣṇa replied, “Climb onto My shoulders.” Having said this, Kṛṣṇa vanished, and Rādhikā, Kṛṣṇa’s beloved, began to lament.

## 210

O Kṛṣṇa, we have come to You, disregarding everyone — husbands, sons, kinsmen, brothers and friends. You know why we have come: we came enchanted by Your song. O rogue, who abandons women like this at night?

## 211

From awareness fixed on Me (*man-niṣṭhatā-buddhi*) the quality of *śama* arises; from *śama*, *śānta-rasa*. Therefore in *śānta-rasa* one sees this firm conviction (*niṣṭhā*): “Kṛṣṇa alone is the supreme truth; the whole universe (though it rests on Kṛṣṇa, it is distinct from Kṛṣṇa, other) is an ‘other’ thing.”

## 212

From the words of Bhagavān — “the quality of *śama* comes from awareness fixed on Me” — one should understand that without *śānta-rati* such fixity (*niṣṭhā*) is hard to achieve.

## 213

Awareness fixed on Me is the quality of *śama*; control of the senses is *dama*; bearing suffering is called *titikṣā*; and conquest of the tongue and genitals is called *dhṛti*.

## 219–228

Exclusive devotion to Kṛṣṇa and (therefore) giving up craving for other things — these are the two qualities of *śānta-rasa*[^219-228-1]. Just as sound, the sole quality of ether, pervades all the elements — air, fire, water and earth — so the qualities of *śānta-rasa* are present in *dāsya*, *sakhya*, *vātsalya* and *madhura-rasa*. *Śānta-rasa* has these two qualities, but it lacks *mamatā* (the property “He is mine”). Therefore the object of worship in this *rasa* is “Parabrahman,” “the Paramātmā” and the like, and in this worship itself knowledge (*jñāna*) predominates. When *mamatā* is added to it — “This Paramātmā is my Master, and I am His eternal servant” — *śānta-rasa* blossoms and turns into *dāsya*; yet in it there is still abundant “awareness of the Lord” (*īśvara-jñāna*) and “reverence” (*gaurava*) in the form of awe. In *śānta-rasa* there is no service; service begins only in *dāsya-rasa*. In *dāsya-rasa* two qualities are seen: the quality of *śānta*, and *mamatā*.

Further, in *sakhya-rasa* the quality of *śānta* and the quality of *dāsya* are certainly present, but to them is added a little love full of trust. Trust is called *viśrambha*; in *sakhya-rasa*, where *viśrambha* predominates, there is no reverence or awe. Therefore *sakhya-rasa* has “three” qualities. The *mamatā* that was in *dāsya* has increased in *sakhya*: Kṛṣṇa is regarded as “equal to oneself” (*ātma-sama*). In *vātsalya* the quality of *śānta* and the service of *dāsya* turn into “nurturing” (*pālana*). Besides this, there are the qualities of *sakhya* — freedom from reserve and absence of reverence — and, from an excess of *mamatā*, punishing and scolding, as well as regarding oneself as the “protector” and Kṛṣṇa as the one “to be protected.” Through such qualities of four *rasas*, *vātsalya* has become like nectar.

## 230

O Bhagavān, I bow to You lovingly hundreds of times, for by such pastimes of Yours You immerse the gopīs in a lake of bliss and show the devotees who are aware of Your majesty that You are conquered by devotees.

The exclusive devotion to Kṛṣṇa (*kṛṣṇa-niṣṭhā*)[^230-1] of *śānta*, the “abundant service” of *dāsya*, the “service without reserve” of *sakhya*, and the “nurturing from an excess of *mamatā*” of *vātsalya* — when to all these feelings is firmly added the “service of giving one’s own body,” which belongs to the mood of a beloved (*kāntā-bhāva*), there arises *madhura-rasa*, endowed with five qualities. In it all the *bhāvas* are combined. Therefore, as the fullness of taste increases, its supreme wonder is seen. Reflecting on the *sūtras* of this *bhakti-rasa*, told in brief, [Śrī Rūpa] will bring forth the scripture *Bhakti-rasāmṛta-sindhu*.

*Thus ends the nineteenth chapter of the “Amṛta-pravāha-bhāṣya.”*
