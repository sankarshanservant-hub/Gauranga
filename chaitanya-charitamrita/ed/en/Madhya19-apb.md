# Amṛta-pravāha-bhāṣya on Madhya-līlā 19

## 0

**Summary.** After seeing Mahāprabhu in the village of Rāmakeli, Rūpa and Sanātana began to seek a way to give up their worldly affairs. To gain shelter at Caitanya's feet, they had two *puraścaraṇas* of the “Kṛṣṇa mantra” performed. Rūpa Gosvāmī left ten thousand coins in Gauḍa, loaded all his accumulated wealth onto a boat and went to Bākalā-Candradvīpa. There he divided the money among the *brāhmaṇas*, the Vaiṣṇavas and his relatives, and set part of it aside to avert punishment and imprisonment. To learn on what day Mahāprabhu would set out for Vṛndāvana by the forest path, he sent two messengers to Puruṣottama-kṣetra. Meanwhile Śrī Sanātana Gosvāmī stayed at home on the pretext of illness and began to discuss the *Bhāgavatam* and other scriptures with *paṇḍitas*. The ruler of Gauḍa, the emperor Husain Shāh, first through a physician and then by seeing for himself, understood that Sanātana had given up the royal service on that pretext. He confined Sanātana in jail (prison) and himself marched to war against Orissa. When Mahāprabhu set out by the forest path, Śrī Rūpa Gosvāmī, leaving home, sent word to Sanātana Gosvāmī and went with his brother Anupama Mallika to find Mahāprabhu. Reaching Prayāga, he stayed near Mahāprabhu for ten days. Meanwhile Vallabha Bhaṭṭa invited Mahāprabhu and showed Him special honor, and Mahāprabhu introduced Śrī Rūpa to Vallabha Bhaṭṭa. After that Raghupati Upādhyāya arrived there and had many conversations with Mahāprabhu about *rasa*. At this point Kavirāja Gosvāmī partly describes the life of Śrī Rūpa and Śrī Sanātana in Vraja. During the ten days in Prayāga Mahāprabhu taught Śrī Rūpa the truth of *bhakti-rasa* in the form of *sūtras* and ordered him to compose the *Rasāmṛta-sindhu*. Sending Śrī Rūpa from there to Vṛndāvana, Mahāprabhu went to Kāśī and stayed at the house of Candraśekhara.

## 1

Before creation the Lord inspired in Brahmā's heart the truth about Bhagavān, comprising *sambandha*, *abhidheya* and *prayojana*. In the same way, full of eagerness, He infused His own power into Rūpa Gosvāmī and spread anew the account of Vṛndāvana's pastimes full of *rasa*, an account that had been lost over time.

## 8

*Daṇḍa-bandha*: to avert an impending calamity, such as royal punishment or imprisonment.

## 15

*Chadma*: a pretext.

## 16

When Sanātana Gosvāmī was the royal minister, several “Kāyastha” officials served under him. Seeing Sanātana's mood of renunciation, some of them, hoping to get his post, began to show special diligence in the royal business. Tradition says that when Sanātana Gosvāmī resigned, his subordinate, the well-known official Purandara Khān, received that post.

## 24–27

It is said that the emperor Husain Shāh regarded Sanātana Gosvāmī as his “younger brother.” When Sanātana showed his firm resolve to give up his post, Husain Shāh said in loving anger: “I am your ‘elder brother.’ I do not govern the kingdom at all: I merely go about with my army, plundering one country after another in war, and, being a ‘Yavana’ by birth, I hunt in the districts of Gauḍa and destroy many living beings and beasts, and that is all. You are my only support. If I, your elder brother, am occupied only in plunder and in destroying living beings, and you, my younger brother, also give up your work and ruin everything, how will the kingdom go on?”[^24-27-1] Sanātana replied with a hidden meaning: “You are the ruler of Gauḍa, an independent king, the dispenser of punishment. Whoever has committed a fault, give him its fruit.” These words hold a deep secret. The king himself behaves like a bandit, so let him take the fruit of that. And when the minister (that is, I) is idle in his work, let him (me) receive the fruit of dismissal. Understanding from this what Sanātana wanted, the ruler of Gauḍa got up and left.

## 33

*Āmi dui bhāi* (“we two brothers”): I, Rūpa, and my (younger) brother Anupama, or Vallabha.

## 50

One who reads the four Vedas, that is, a Caube *brāhmaṇa*, does not become a “devotee” just by being so. My devotee is dear to Me even if he is a *caṇḍāla*. Only a devotee is truly a worthy recipient of gifts and truly a worthy giver from whom one may accept. Every devotee is as worthy of worship as I am.

## 53

O Lord, most magnanimous, giver of love for Kṛṣṇa, Kṛṣṇa Himself, bearing the name Kṛṣṇa Caitanya and the form of Gaurāṅga, I offer obeisance to You.

## 54

That merciful Lord cured the world, maddened by ignorance, of the disease of ignorance and intoxicated it with the nectar of the treasure of His love. I take shelter of that Śrī Kṛṣṇa Caitanya, whose deeds are wonderful.

## 61

Vallabha Bhaṭṭa was a Vaiṣṇava *paṇḍita*. At first he entered Śrī Mahāprabhu's *sampradāya*, but, not receiving much honor there, he became an *ācārya* in the Viṣṇusvāmī *sampradāya*. It is he whom people call “Vallabhācārya.” He has great influence in Gokula and in the Bombay region. He wrote many books, such as the *Anubhāṣya* and the *Ṣoḍaśa-grantha*.

Āḍāila-grāma: the village of Aḍelī, or Āḍāila, on the other bank of the Yamunā near the confluence (about a mile away). An ancient Viṣṇu temple of the “Vallabhī” *sampradāya* still stands there.

## 74–75

One whose conduct is pure, whose taint of low birth has been burned up by the blazing fire of true *bhakti*, even if he is a *caṇḍāla*, is honored by *paṇḍitas*. But an atheist, even one who knows the Vedas, is not worthy of honor. The noble birth, scriptural knowledge, *japa* and austerity of one devoid of devotion to Bhagavān are like ornaments on a dead body: they serve no purpose and merely please people.

## 83

That region was largely devoid of *prema*, and Vallabha Bhaṭṭa, who was present, was a man much given to argument. Seeing this, Mahāprabhu restrained Himself.

## 92

Several *ślokas* composed by Raghupati Upādhyāya are found in the *Padyāvalī*. He lived in Tirhut, that is, in the land of Mithilā.

## 96

Let those who fear worldly existence worship, some the *śruti*, some the *smṛti*, and others the *Mahābhārata*. But I (here) honor Śrī Nanda alone, on whose veranda (*alinda*) the Supreme Brahman, Kṛṣṇa, plays.

## 98

To whom can I say it, and who now will believe, that in the groves of the Sun's daughter the Supreme Brahman, the lover (*lampaṭa*) of the cowherd wives, performs His pastimes?

## 106

Śyāma's form is the best of forms, Madhupurī the best of cities, the age of *kaiśora* is the one to meditate upon, and the original *rasa*, that is, *śṛṅgāra*, is the best of *rasas*.

## 115

*Prānta*: the limit.

## 119

Over time the account of the pastimes of Vṛndāvana had been lost. To spread those pastimes in a special way, Śrī Gaurāṅgadeva there and then anointed Śrī Rūpa and Śrī Sanātana with the nectar of His mercy.

## 120

Śrī Rūpa had previously been tightly bound by many qualities dear to him, yet he became free from household life. Gaurāṅgadeva Himself, who is like *rasa*, intangible yet the supreme embodied one, favored him in Prayāga together with his younger brother Anupama with loving conversations and firm embraces.

## 121

Śrī Rūpa Gosvāmī is dear to the Lord by his very nature, beloved by nature, *prema* embodied, endowed with natural captivating beauty, the principal form, like the Lord Himself: he is the Lord's own *vilāsa*. Through such a Śrī Rūpa the Lord spread (the scriptures of *bhakti-rasa*).

## 129

*Karoṅyā*: the water pot that *sannyāsīs* carry in the hand.

## 134

I bow to the lotus feet of Śrī Caitanyadeva Hari, by whose inspiration in my heart I, a wretched pauper, have taken up the writing of a book on *bhakti*.

## 140

If the tip of a hair is divided into a hundred parts, and one of them again into a hundred, the minute *svarūpa* of the *jīva* is like such a part. The *jīva* is a particle of spirit (*cit-kaṇa*), and the *jīvas* are countless.

## 141

If the hundredth part of the tip of a hair is divided many hundreds of times, the resulting part is minute, and the *jīva* is just as minute. So say the principal *śrutis*.

## 142

Some manuscripts give it in full, as Śrī Bhagavān's words to Uddhava in the *Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam*: “Among those possessing qualities I am the *sūtra*, among the great I am the *mahat*, among the subtle I am the *jīva*, among the hard to conquer I am the mind.” Among the subtle I (Bhagavān) am the “*jīva*” (a manifestation of oneness and difference).

## 143

O Dhruva (Eternal One)! If the embodied *jīvas* were unlimited, eternal (*dhruva*) in the sense of supreme eternity, and all-pervading, there would be no law that they are subject to Your rule. Only if the *jīva* is accepted as “atomic” and “eternal” in the ordinary sense is it subject to You. The *jīvas* were born consisting of that cause; only by not abandoning them can it be their controller. Therefore the opinion of those who consider the *jīva* and You to be “one” is corrupted by “*matavāda*” (the fault of mere opinion).[^143-1]

## 144–149

*Jīvas* are of two kinds: eternally liberated and eternally conditioned. The eternally conditioned are divided into the immobile and the mobile. Those who are motionless (such as trees) are “immobile” (*sthāvara*) *jīvas*, and those who move are “mobile” (*jaṅgama*). The mobile are of three kinds: beasts and birds, aquatics and land-dwellers. Among land-dwellers humans are very few. If from these few humans one excludes the *mlecchas*, *pulindas*, Buddhists and *śabaras*, those faithful to the Vedas remain. Those faithful to the Vedas are of two kinds: those who follow *dharma* and those who do not. Among those who follow *dharma*, many are devoted to *karma* and some are devoted to knowledge. Among millions devoted to knowledge only one is truly “liberated” (*mukta*); here those free from material understanding are called “liberated.” And among these liberated, one who, endowed with faith, has turned to the service of Kṛṣṇa is a “devotee of Kṛṣṇa.” A devotee of Kṛṣṇa has no desires. All the others, up to the “liberated” mentioned above, have desires. Those who follow *dharma* and those devoted to *karma* are “desirers of enjoyment” (*bhukti-kāmī*), the knowers up to the liberated are “desirers of liberation” (*mukti-kāmī*), and some of them also “desire *siddhis*,” the fruits of *yoga*. As long as these three kinds of desire remain in their hearts, those desires give them no peace; therefore they are all “restless” (*aśānta*). Hence only the desireless devotee of Kṛṣṇa is peaceful (*śānta*), that is, has attained peace.

## 150

O great sage, among many millions of the liberated and the perfected, a person devoted to Nārāyaṇa, peaceful in heart, is extremely rare.[^150-1]
