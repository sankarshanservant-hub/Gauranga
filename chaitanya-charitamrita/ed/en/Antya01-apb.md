# Amṛta-pravāha-bhāṣya on Antya-līlā 1

## 0

**Summary.** When the Gauḍīya devotees received word that Mahāprabhu had returned from Śrī Vṛndāvana to Śrī Kṣetra, they set out for Puruṣottama. Śivānanda Sena was taking a dog along with him and paying its fare at the river crossings. One night the dog was not given its rice, and it went away to the Lord. The next day Śivānanda and the others reached Mahāprabhu and saw that very dog eating prasāda — coconut pulp the Lord had given it. Afterwards the dog attained liberation and went (to Vaikuṇṭha). Meanwhile Śrī Rūpa Gosvāmī had set out from Vṛndāvana, but he could not travel together with the Gauḍīya devotees: he reached Nīlācala some days later and stayed with Haridāsa. Mahāprabhu read the verse “*priyaḥ so ’yaṁ*” composed by Śrī Rūpa and was overjoyed. One day Mahāprabhu came to Haridāsa’s dwelling with Rāmānanda Rāya, Sārvabhauma and other devotees and heard the introductory and other verses of Śrī Rūpa’s two plays, the *Lalita-mādhava* and the *Vidagdha-mādhava*. Rāmānanda Rāya examined many of their parts, major and minor, and concluded that both plays were of the highest excellence. After the *cāturmāsya* the Gauḍīya devotees, by the Lord’s order, set out for Gauḍa. Śrī Rūpa Gosvāmī remained in Śrī Kṣetra.

## 1

I worship the Lord, Kṛṣṇa Caitanya, whose mercy gives the lame the strength to cross a mountain and makes the mute recite the *śruti*.

## 2

May the saints give me the staff of their mercy and become my support, for I am blind, and my feet keep slipping on a difficult path.

## 20

***Pāsarilā*** — forgot.

## 22

***Cāhite*** — to search.

## 34

***Kṛṣṇa-līlā-nāṭaka*** — a play about Kṛṣṇa’s pastimes.

## 35

***Nāndī-śloka*** — the auspicious invocatory verse recited at the start of a play is called the *nāndī* verse.

## 36

***Kaḍacā*** — a draft, that is, a rough manuscript.

## 39

***Lāg nā pāila*** — Śrī Rūpa heard that Śivānanda and the other devotees were going to the Lord, and he came intending to go to Nīlācala with them. But he could not meet them: they had already left for Nīlācala.

## 53

***Tāṅhā*** — in Haridāsa’s dwelling, that is, in the Siddha-bakula Maṭha.

## 67

Kṛṣṇa born in the Yadu dynasty is the *tattva* of Vāsudeva; He is therefore distinct from the son of the king of the cowherds. It is He who performs pastimes in Mathurā and Dvārakā. But the one who is the son of the king of the cowherds never leaves Vṛndāvana to go anywhere.

## 75

***Kene*** — in what mood.

## 91

The cause of a fruit is inferred from the fruit itself.

## 92

We eat the tips of the stems of the golden lotuses of the heavenly Gaṅgā, and so we have gained a corresponding beauty of body: qualities appear in keeping with their original cause.

## 99

I do not know with how much nectar these two syllables, “Kṛṣ-ṇa,” were born. See: when they dance on the lips like an actress, they awaken a longing to obtain many mouths — that is, they increase one’s attachment. When they enter the hollows of the ears and sprout there, they create a craving for millions of ears. When they rise in the courtyard of the mind as a companion, they conquer the activity of all the senses.

## 108

This Bhagavān, Puruṣottama, is pure in mind. By His good nature He does not notice even the grave offences of His servant. He considers the slightest service great. Even towards a wicked person who reviles Him He shows no ill will.

## 128

May this sweet drink (*śikhariṇī*) — the pastimes of Hari — wholly take away your unholy thirst, born of wandering along the path of worldly existence, which breeds suffering. This *śikhariṇī* of Hari’s pastimes subdues the intoxication born of the sweetness of the moon’s nectar, and through the camphor of the love (*praṇaya*) of Śrī Rādhā and others it bears a special fragrance.

## 134

Those who play the roles — the hero and the other persons spoken of in the play — are called *pātra*. For example, the *Sāhitya-darpaṇa*, in its sixth chapter, verse 283, says: “In a play about the divine or the mortal he takes the same kind of form, and in a mixed one, either of the two. Let him hint at the subject (*vastu*), the seed (*bīja*), the opening (*mukha*), or else a character (*pātra*).” The meaning of the word *āmukha* is given in the *Nāṭaka-candrikā*: “When the *sūtradhāra*, answering the actress, speaks of his own business in striking words that hint at what is to come, this is called the *āmukha*.” The point of Rāmānanda Rāya’s question is this: in which *āmukha* (*prastāvanā*) do the actors who play the characters come onto the stage in this play? Śrī Rūpa’s answer: the characters appear in the *āmukha* called *pravartaka* (entrance onto the stage) — *kāla-sāmye*, that is, at the time that has arrived.

## 135

When an actor playing a character enters the stage prompted (called forth) by a fitting time, that is, by the time that has arrived, this is called *pravartaka*.

## 136

Spring has come. At this season, at night, Paurṇamāsī will unite the complete Lord, Śrī Kṛṣṇa, filled with fresh love (*anurāga*), with the supremely beautiful Śrī Rādhikā, to enhance the beauty of the pastimes. This verse has two meanings, one referring to the moon and one to Śrī Kṛṣṇa; of these, the meaning referring to Śrī Kṛṣṇa is the principal one.

## 137

***Prarocanā*** — the technique of awakening in the audience a desire to listen by praising the place, the time, the hero, the assembly and so on.

## 138

Devotees whose intelligence knows no obstruction and whose nature is radiant have gathered. This work about Śrī Kṛṣṇa, the Lord of the life of the young wives of the cowherds, has also blossomed with many virtues. And the stage itself is the arena for the dance in the *rāsa-maṇḍala* of Vṛndāvana. Therefore I think that the ripened fruit of the pious merit (*sukṛti*) of people like us has come to light.

## 139

O wise ones! Though this composition, full of descriptions of Hari’s qualities, has been brought forth by me, insignificant by nature, may it fulfil your cherished desires. Cannot fire, kindled by rubbing wood even by a Pulinda (a man of the lowest birth), remove the inner impurity (the dross) of bars of gold?

## 140

*Pūrvānurāga* means *pūrva-rāga*, the first attraction, before meeting; *vikāra* is the transformation of love (*praṇaya-vikāra*), that is, the lovesickness (*vyādhi*) born of divine madness; *ceṣṭā* means the bodily actions born of prema; *kāma-likhana* is a letter of the gopīs that reveals their love. The Lord[^140-1] asked about the causes from which prema arises, and Śrī Rūpa told everything.

## 142

Rādhikā, overcome by *pūrva-rāga*, says: “I heard the syllables of the name ‘Kṛṣṇa’ belonging to some other man — and I lost My senses. The sound of another man’s flute awakens a deep madness in My heart. And ever since I saw in a picture the soft, cloudlike lustre of yet another man, it has remained fixed in My heart. Alas, shame on Me! How could such *rati* for three different men arise in Me? It is better for Me to die.”

## 143

“Sakhī, Rādhā’s heartache can hardly be cured: even if She is treated, it all ends only in disgrace.”

## 144

“O beautiful one, taking the form of an image — a painted picture — You dwell in My house (*mandira*). Wherever I run in fright, there You block My way.” The verse is written in Prakrit; its Sanskrit rendering is this: “Taking the form of an image, O beautiful one, You dwell in My house. Wherever I run in fright, there You forcibly block My way.”

## 145

“Seeing a peacock feather before Her, this girl suddenly begins to tremble, and seeing *guñjā* berries She cries aloud, shedding tears. I do not know what new *graha*, a power that possesses the soul, has entered the field of Her heart and awakens in Her the wonder of an unprecedented dancing play.”

## 146

“Since Kṛṣṇa Himself has become merciless to Me, what fault is yours, sakhī? Do not weep in vain. You can do one thing for Me — My funeral rite: in Vṛndāvana, wind these creeper-arms of Mine around the trunk of a *tamāla* tree, and leave My body so forever.”

## 149

When Rāya asked about the “natural” (*sahaja*) characteristic of prema, Śrī Rūpa answered: “The nature of prema is natural (*sāhajika*) in itself.”

## 150

*Svārasika* means natural. Natural prema acts and plays in this way. When the loving one hears herself praised (by the beloved), she shows indifference but feels a special pain. When she hears herself blamed (by the beloved), she takes it as the charm of a jest, and it gives her (great) joy. When she sees some fault in the one she loves, her love does not diminish at all; and when she sees some virtue in him, (her love) does not grow either.

## 151

“Hearing of My cruelty, moon-faced Rādhā will break the sprout of love, somehow establish calm or steadiness in Her suffering heart, and probably turn away from Me. Or else, frightened by the bow of wicked Kandarpa, She will give up Her life. Alas, in My folly I have uprooted the tender creeper of desire that was just about to bear fruit.”

## 152

“Sakhī, I longed for happiness in His embrace — and for His sake I cast off even My great shyness before the elders. For His sake I caused you so much pain, though you are dearer to Me than life. For His sake I counted as nothing even that dharma (of fidelity to a husband) on which chaste women rely. And now Kṛṣṇa Himself has neglected Me, yet I, sinful as I am, still live! Shame on My steadiness!”

## 153

“By My natural childlike simplicity I was playing at home and knew nothing at all of what is called ‘good’ and what ‘bad’! Was it worthy of You to plunge us into such a helpless state? And is it just of You now to take the path of indifference?”

## 154

“With hearts stained by suffering we are going to the abode of Yama this very day, and still this Kṛṣṇa does not give up His affectionate laughter full of deceit (a cruel laughter so full of deceit)! O wise Rādhikā, how could so lofty a prema arise in You for this philanderer of the cowherd village, so full of deep guile?”

## 155

“O Kṛṣṇa, ocean! The river Rādhikā, whose nature is ever-new rasa, has left far behind the path that ran beside the tree — her lawful husband; she has swiftly broken the bridge of dharma, forcibly crossed the mountain of the elders, and reached You. How is it that You now show her estrangement with waves of words?”

## 158

“Sweet with the honey of mango buds, full of swarms of bees captured again and again by that fragrant flow, set swaying by the gentlest wafting of the breeze from the sandalwood mountain (Malaya), this Śrī Vṛndāvana increases My incomparable bliss.”

## 159

“Look: this Vṛndāvana is encircled by divine creepers; the tips of the creepers are adorned with flowers; the flowers are full of bees; and the bees are absorbed in songs that captivate the ear.”

## 160

“Friend, this Vṛndāvana delights our senses in every way: in one place the bees sing, in another it is cool with the gentle Malaya breeze, in another the creepers dance, in another flows the pure fragrance of *mallikā* flowers, and in another the pomegranates, overflowing with juice, let it run in streams.”

## 161

“Inlaid with sapphires (*indranīla*) for three fingers’ length at each end, adorned with rubies (*aruṇa-maṇi*) for the same length, and in between made of pure gold and glittering with diamonds — this auspicious flute of Kṛṣṇa’s play sports in Kṛṣṇa’s hand.”

## 162

“Sakhī Muralī, you were born of a good bamboo, you dwell in the hand of Puruṣottama, and you are straight by birth. Why, then, have you taken initiation into such an especially grave (*viṣama*, ‘crooked’) mantra, one that bewilders the gopīs?”

## 163

“Sakhī Muralī, you are full of large holes, light, very hard, dry, and knotty. By what pious merit, then, does Kṛṣṇa unceasingly embrace you with His hand — an embrace dense with the bliss of kissing His lips?”

## 164

The sound of Śrī Kṛṣṇa’s flute stopped the movement of the clouds, struck Tumburu and the other Gandharvas with wonder, broke the meditation of Sanandana and the other sages, astonished Brahmā, stirred the composed (that is, unshakable and unmoving) King Bali with impatient longing, set Ananta, the king of serpents who holds up the earth, spinning, pierced the walls of the shell of the universe — and spread in all directions.

## 165

By the beauty of His eyes this Kṛṣṇa has stolen the lustre of the finest white lotus. His yellow garments, which outshine the glow of fresh *kuṅkuma*, sparkle. By His forest dress, ornaments, and the like He has taken away all regard for divine attire. So the radiant Kṛṣṇacandra shines, with a lustre more enchanting than a sapphire (*indranīla*).

## 166

“Sakhī, O beautiful one! His right foot rests below His left shin, His waist is slightly bent in three places, His neck is turned to the side and held still, the corners of His eyes are crooked; to His slightly closed, budlike lips He holds the flute, touched by His restless fingers, and His lotus face is adorned with brows like bees. Accept Him, full of supreme bliss, who stands before you.”

## 167

“O fair-faced one, what Viśvakarmā is this standing before us? With the flashing, sharp, long chisel of His sidelong glances He splits the stones of the *svadharma* of noble wives, and at the same time builds chambers in the cowherd village out of His own beautiful dark form, which is like countless emeralds.”

## 168

“Sakhī, a youth in the freshness of youth has appeared — the moon of the dynasty of the king of Vraja; the lustre of His body crushes the pride of clusters of great sapphires. Glory to the sound of His flute, which playfully breaks the knots of the sashes of steadfast noble wives!”

## 169

The beauty of Her eyes forcibly swallows the beauty of a newly blossomed blue lotus; the joy shining on Her blooming face surpasses a forest of lotuses; the lustre of Her body plunges even beautiful gold into distress. So wondrously does Śrī Rādhikā’s beauty play, that is, manifest itself.

## 170

“The beauty of the moon, though lovely at night, fades by day; and the lotus, though lovely by day, grows dim (closes) at night. But, friend, the face of My beloved Rādhikā always, day and night, shines with beauty. With what, then, can it be compared?”

## 171

“Her cheeks, with a gentle, gentle smile, are stirred by waves of the rasa of joy; Her creeper-brows dance like the bow of Kāma, resembling a bee that darts about in intoxicated abandon. And the glance that flew from Her lashes has stung My heart.”

## 175

The *cakravāka* birds — the breasts of the wives of the enemies of the gods — and the lotuses — their faces — the moon of Mukunda’s fame casts into dejection, into sorrow, while it forever delights the *cakora* birds — all His friends. May that full moon of Mukunda’s fame give you happiness.

## 177

Rising over the earth, He pours out the nectar of the rasa of love for Himself; He has accepted the position of supreme king of the twice-born; He dispels the darkness; He wins the minds of the whole world. May that moon named Śacīnandana grant me auspiciousness.

## 184

Dancing on the stage, Kalānidhi (Kṛṣṇacandra) will destroy Kaṁsa, the king of the Kirātas, and at an auspicious time called “Pūrṇa-manoratha” is to take the hand of Tārā (Śrī Rādhā).

## 186

When people join words whose meaning is unclear with other words in order to grasp their meaning, this is called *udghātyaka*.

## 188

The dust raised by the cows’ hooves points to Hari; the darkness ahead unites Him with the gopīs. Therefore the path of the cowherd women is beyond the reach even of the all-knowing Śruti.

## 189

Glory to that messenger — the sweet, soft tone (*kākalī*) of the flute born of the finest bamboo! Skilful and entrusted with the purpose, she frees Śrī Rādhā from shyness and draws Her from home into the forest.

## 190

“Friend, who is this fearless youth, shining like a fresh cloud, sporting like an intoxicated elephant? From where has He come to the land of Vraja? Ah, with His restless gait and thieflike glances He is plundering from the treasury of My heart the wealth of My composure!”

## 191

“Rādhikā is, for the king-elephant of My mind, the Gaṅgā in which it sports; for the *cakora* birds of My eyes, the brilliant light of the autumn moon; for the sky of My chest, an ornament like a lovely string of stars. Today I have attained that Rādhikā — and with Her My lofty desires are fulfilled.”

## 195

If the poet’s poetry and the archer’s bow, having entered another’s heart, cannot make his head spin — what use are they?

*Thus ends the first chapter of the “Amṛta-pravāha-bhāṣya.”*
