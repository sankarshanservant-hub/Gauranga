## 9

> Subheading: Devotion to Gaura without devotion to Kṛṣṇa, and devotion to Kṛṣṇa without devotion to Gaura, are non-devotion (*abhakti*) —

Jarāsandha and others abandoned the worship of Śrī Kṛṣṇa — the Supreme Truth, Viṣṇu, the original form (*svayaṁ-rūpa*) — out of hatred or indifference toward Him. They worshipped Viṣṇu with Vedic mantras, but even that worship ended in nothing but demoniac *dharma*. In the same way, when the *jīva* forgets his nature as a minute particle of spirit (*aṇu-cid-dharma*), that is, his servitude to Caitanya, his attempt to worship Viṣṇu is also nothing but a calamitous, demoniac *dharma* — that is, non-Vaiṣṇavism.

## 10

> Subheading: The reason for the Lord's pastime of *sannyāsa* —

## 11

Śrī Caitanyacandra is a Reality nondifferent from the truth of Śrī Rādhā and Kṛṣṇa. Out of ignorance some people equated the pastimes of Śrī Gaurasundara with their own material attachments, full of the desire for enjoyment, and so they could not understand Him. Considering their predicament, Śrī Gaurahari Himself, before the eyes of the world, accepted the *dharma* of a renunciant (*yati-dharma*) and thus showed mercy to foolish people.

## 12

> Subheading: To lack devotion to the supremely magnanimous Gaura is a demoniac disposition —

“O *jīva*, worship Kṛṣṇa alone!” — such is the mercy of Śrī Caitanyacandra. Whoever cannot even perceive it is certainly an *asura*, a non-Vaiṣṇava devoid of devotion to Viṣṇu, however much worldly people may honor him as the best in the world. And if one abandons the worship of Kṛṣṇa and worships Caitanya, Śrī Caitanya's mercy is not upon him: such worship is imaginary, born of Kali. The same holds for one who follows the godless *smārtas* or the society of *pañcopāsakas* (worshippers of five deities) and strives to worship Viṣṇu for some petty, perishable self-interest. He may reject one of the six *tattvas* whose very self is Kṛṣṇa Caitanya and show faith or worship to another. Or he may consider Kṛṣṇa Caitanya Prabhu merely the best of mortal beings, separate from Kṛṣṇa, and so have no faith in Gaura's name, Gaura's mantra and Gaura's *śakti*. This too is demoniac *dharma* — imagination born of Kali and opposed to the truth.

> Subheading: Gaura's mercy is more wonderful than any other mercy —

## 14–15

By the power of their own narrow, pleasure-seeking intelligence, people imagine one ideal of mercy or another. But the mercy of Śrī Caitanyacandra is not of that kind.

After studying the science of logic (*tarka-śāstra*), a person comes to think that no other faculty equals reasoning (*tarka*) in the power to determine the nature of a thing and to uncover the truth. So, falling into the hands of reasoning, the *jīva* gives it the place of his sole patron and protector. But if an intelligent *jīva* carefully examines the foundation on which reasoning stands, he can understand how weak, how incapable and how deficient his worldly senses of knowledge are when it comes to grasping the subject of Bhagavān. Often reasoning merely settles on untruth as a truth established by argument; and so, as the fruit of false argument (*kutarka*), one obtains birth as a jackal.

Even so, there are people ready to determine the true nature of things relying chiefly on direct perception (*pratyakṣa*) and inference (*anumāna*). Kavirāja Gosvāmī addresses them too. He says: let those who have sight and the power of judgment, who have experienced every picture of every kind of mercy or have gained the ability and the opportunity to see them, make a list of all kinds of mercy and compare them with the mercy of Śrī Gaurahari. Then they will know that a mercy like that of Śrī Gaurahari is found in no created thing, nor in the creator, nor in the series of avatāras, nor even in the source of the avatāras — in Kṛṣṇa. The mercy of Gaurahari, the embodiment of magnanimity (*udāra-vigraha*), certainly brings more wonder and amazement than any other.

## 16

> Subheading: As long as offenses remain, hearing and chanting about Kṛṣṇa countless times is in vain —

If someone takes up the devotion called hearing and chanting without taking shelter of Śrī Caitanya's feet, he has no prospect of attaining love for Kṛṣṇa even in many births. According to the teaching of Śrī Caitanyacandra, devotees are lower than a blade of grass and more tolerant than a tree; free from pride themselves, they honor others and are not occupied with any worldly conceit. Such persons become free from the grip of the ten offenses, are able to take Kṛṣṇa's name constantly, and attain *prema*.

The meaning is this. The *jīva* who has turned away from the service of Kṛṣṇa seeks to gratify the material senses or to achieve some perishable self-interest. He imagines a difference between Kṛṣṇa's name and Kṛṣṇa, the possessor of the name — between the word that designates (*vācaka*) and the One designated (*vācya*) — as if it were the label of some object perceptible to the material senses, or ordinary material letters. He does not know himself to be the eternal servant of Śrī Nāma-Prabhu. Such a *jīva* may utter the name and so forth with offenses countless times, yet he will not be able to attain *kṛṣṇa-prema*, which is attained through pure *sādhana-bhakti*. The words of the *Padma Purāṇa* quoted in the *Hari-bhakti-vilāsa* (11.289): “If even one name [of the Lord] is on a person's tongue, or has entered the path of his memory, or has reached his ears — whether pronounced correctly or incorrectly, so long as it is not broken — it certainly delivers him. But if, O brāhmaṇa, it is cast amid [concern for] body, wealth, followers, greed and godlessness, it does not quickly bear fruit here.” And further: “Therefore the name of Śrī Kṛṣṇa and all that is His cannot be grasped by the senses. Only when the tongue and the other senses are turned toward service does it reveal itself upon them.” (*Bhakti-rasāmṛta-sindhu*, *Pūrva-vibhāga* 2.234)

## 24

> Subheading: While offenses remain, the holy name of Kṛṣṇa, worshipped by the liberated, does not arise —

The original verses on the ten offenses against the holy name: “(1) Blasphemy of the saints is the greatest offense against the name: how can it tolerate the reviling of those through whom it has gained fame? (2) Whoever here sees with his mind all the qualities, names and so on of the auspicious (*śiva*) Śrī Viṣṇu as separate, truly harms himself with Hari's name.[^24-1] (3) Disregard for the guru; (4) blasphemy of the *śruti* and the *śāstras*; (5) the view that [the praise of the name] is *arthavāda*; (6) imagined interpretations of Hari's name. (7) One who, relying on the strength of the name, is inclined to sin is not purified by any *yamas* (restraints). (8) To consider [the name] equal to all auspicious acts — *dharma*, vows, renunciation, fire sacrifices and the rest — is also negligence. (9) Instruction given to one who lacks faith, has turned away [from the Lord] and does not wish to hear is an offense against the auspicious name. (10) And a man who, even after hearing of the glory of the name, has no love for it, absorbed in ‘I’ and ‘mine,’ also commits an offense.”

(1) Blasphemy of the saints is the greatest offense before Śrī Nāma. Through the saints wholly devoted to the name, the glory of Kṛṣṇa's name becomes known in the world, that is, it is preached. How then will Śrī Nāma-Prabhu tolerate blasphemy of these saints? Therefore blasphemy of sādhus is an offense against the name. (2) Someone in this world sees with his intellect a difference among the name, form, qualities, pastimes and so on of the all-auspicious Śrī Viṣṇu. That is, he thinks that the name, form, qualities and pastimes of Śrī Viṣṇu are different from Śrī Viṣṇu, the possessor of the name, as is the case with material things. Or he regards Śiva and the other gods as rivals and sees them as independent of Śrī Viṣṇu or different from Him. Such an offense against the name, committed under the guise of the name, certainly does him harm. (3) Envy (*asūyā*) toward the guru, the knower of the truth of the name, from regarding him as material and mortal; (4) blasphemy of the Vedas, the Sātvata Purāṇas and the other scriptures; (5) considering the glory of Hari's name to be exaggerated praise, and (6) thinking the names of Bhagavān to be imagination — these are offenses against the name. (7) If someone, relying on the strength of the name, is inclined to sinful conduct, that offender is certainly not purified by many *yamas*, many *niyamas*, or by the artificial methods of yoga — *dhyāna*, *dhāraṇā* and the rest. (8) To consider the chanting of the transcendental name equal or similar to material auspicious acts — *dharma*, vows, renunciation, fire sacrifices and the like — is also inattention, or negligence (*pramāda*); this too is an offense against the name. (9) To instruct one who lacks faith or is unwilling to hear the name is an offense before the all-auspicious Śrī Nāma. (10) Whoever, even after hearing the wonderful glory of the name, thinks in bodily terms of “I” and “mine” and shows no love or regard for chanting and hearing Śrī Nāma is also an offender against the name.

## 25

> Subheading: Without the desire to please Kṛṣṇa, the emotion in an offender's stony heart is not pure — it is merely feigned (*Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam*, Canto 2, Chapter 3, verse 24) —

The sages were hearing from the lips of Śrī Sūta the conversation between Śrī Śuka and Parīkṣit and wanted to hear more and more. Condemning people unwilling to hear *hari-kathā*, Śaunaka said to Śrī Sūta:

**yat** **hṛdayaṁ** **gṛhyamāṇaiḥ** (even while they are being chanted) **hari-nāma-dheyaiḥ** **na** **vikriyeta**, **bata** (alas!) **tat** **idaṁ** **hṛdayam** **aśma-sāram** (that whose substance [*sāra*], because of offenses against the name, is like stone, like a piece of rock — that is, it is hard). **atha** **yadā** **vikāro** (occurs), ([then]) **netre** **jalaṁ** (tears) [and] **gātra-ruheṣu** **harṣaḥ** (the hair stands on end) (occurs). (In deeply grave *mahā-bhāgavatas* the heart melts at the names of Hari, yet outward tears, thrills and so on are not seen. But in those whose hearts are slippery (*picchila-citta*), who merely imitate by artificial practice and long for material prestige, feigned tears, thrills and the like are seen outwardly, though there is not even a semblance of *sattva* (*sattvābhāsa*). Therefore in *kaniṣṭha-adhikārīs*, even when they chant the name many times, the artificial melting of the heart, owing to their inclination toward sense enjoyment, is merely a sign of offense against the name. So says the *Sandarbha*.[^25-1])

See the *Gauḍīya-bhāṣya* on the *Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam*: the commentary of Śrīla Viśvanātha Cakravartī, the *Tathya* and the *Vivṛti*.

## 31

> Subheading: Gaura and Nitāi, and Their names, do not judge offenses —

Those who have taken shelter of Śrī Caitanya and Nityānanda chant the pure name without duplicity, as the verse “*tṛṇād api*” teaches, and tears of love are seen to flow from them.

The name of Kṛṣṇa judges offenses; the name of Gaura and Nityānanda does not. If an offender chants Kṛṣṇa's name, he never obtains the fruit of the name, *kṛṣṇa-prema*. But one who chants the name of Gaura and Nityānanda, even while he remains an offender, is freed from his offenses as he goes on chanting and obtains the fruit of the name. The reasoning and conclusion here are as follows. A *sādhaka* who has turned away from Kṛṣṇa goes to Gaura and Nityānanda in order to turn toward Kṛṣṇa. The name of Kṛṣṇa is uttered by one who has attained perfection through practice, is free from *anarthas* and is turned toward Kṛṣṇa; as long as *anarthas* remain, it never gives its fruit, *kṛṣṇa-prema*. Gaura and Nityānanda can be served even by *jīvas* who have *anarthas*. Therefore, for an unfortunate *jīva*, serving Them is more necessary than serving Kṛṣṇa. If a *sādhaka* who has not received instruction thinks himself perfected and sets out to serve Kṛṣṇa's name, only *anarthas* come to him. But if, in the worship of Nitāi-Gaura, he does not pretend to be perfected and approaches the two Teachers, the gurus of the world, even while burdened with *anarthas*, They free such persons from *anarthas* and let them realize the true nature of Their *svayaṁ-rūpa* and *svayaṁ-prakāśa*. Then knowledge of the *jīva*'s own true nature (*svarūpa*) dawns in him.

The name of Kṛṣṇa and the name of Gaura are both nondifferent from the possessor of the name. If someone considers Kṛṣṇa lesser or narrower than Gaura, that must be understood as the work of ignorance (*avidyā*). In truth, judged by what the *jīva* needs, chanting the names of Śrī Gaura and Nityānanda is more beneficial for him. Śrī Gaura and Nityānanda are magnanimous, and within that magnanimity They are sweet. Kṛṣṇa's magnanimity extends only to the liberated, the perfected and those surrendered to Him. But in the stream of Gaura-Nityānanda's magnanimity even an offending *jīva* with *anarthas* is freed from the grip of offenses born of the desire for enjoyment and attains the lotus feet of Gaura-Kṛṣṇa.

## 32

> Subheading: There is no other path than the worship of the supremely magnanimous Gaura —

“The worship of Śrī Caitanya” does not mean the worship of a Gaura other than Rādhā and Kṛṣṇa, giving up Kṛṣṇa. In such imagined worship, which is service to *māyā*, the sweetness of *kṛṣṇa-prema* is not present. There are people who override the ācāryas — Śrī Svarūpa, Rūpa, Raghunātha and the others, Śrī Caitanya's dearest intimate associates — and think that by their invented efforts they have worshipped Gaura. Such people are never delivered. When their outrages, conceived by *māyā*, are directed at the form of Śrī Gaurāṅga, nondifferent from Rādhā and Kṛṣṇa, great offense, and with it their descent toward hell, quickly increase. Then, trying to find fault with the devotees devoted to the service of Rādhā and Kṛṣṇa, they offend the feet of the ācāryas headed by Śrī Rūpa. Thus they reveal that, though with their lips they call Śrī Gaurāṅga “the source of the avatāras” (*avatārī*), in their minds they regard Him merely as a sādhu, like other preachers of passing, mind-made religions (*mano-dharma*).

## 36

> Subheading: The *Caitanya-bhāgavata* is a mine of the glory of Gaura and Nitāi and of the conclusions of devotion —

The *Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam* is the original source of the conclusions of devotion (*bhakti-siddhānta*). But it is very extensive, and many are unable to grasp its essence. Śrī Vṛndāvana dāsa Ṭhākura recorded that essence in his own *Śrī Caitanya-bhāgavata*. Only those who know the conclusions of devotion to Kṛṣṇa are able to understand properly the glory of Śrī Nitāi-Gaura. All books of devotion declare at the top of their voice that without *bhakti-siddhānta* Bhakti-devī cannot be served.
