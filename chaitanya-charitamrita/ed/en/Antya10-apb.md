# Amṛta-pravāha-bhāṣya on Antya-līlā 10

## 0

**Summary.** For the Ratha-yātrā the devotees of Gauḍa set out for Puruṣottama. Rāghava Paṇḍita went carrying many kinds of food in the *jhālis* (bags) his sister Damayantī had given him. Makaradhvaja Kara of Pāṇihāṭi went along too, as the “*munsib*” (steward) of Rāghava’s *jhālis*. On the day the devotees reached Puruṣottama, Govinda had boarded a boat to sport on the waters of Narendra. Mahāprabhu and the devotees also sported in the water. As before, the cleaning of the Guṇḍicā temple and the rest took place. Inside the temple they sang the kīrtana “Jagamohana-parimuṇḍā.” When the kīrtana ended, Mahāprabhu honoured prasāda and lay down at the door of the Gambhīrā. Govinda somehow got close to Him and massaged His feet, but he could not get out, and so that day he did not honour prasāda. Through this conduct of Govinda the conclusion of the pure Vaiṣṇavas was made known: “For the sake of service one should accept even an offence, but for one’s own enjoyment one should shun even the shadow of an offence.” Everything the devotees of Gauḍa had given for Mahāprabhu’s service Govinda offered to the Lord, and the Lord partook of it. The Vaiṣṇavas invited the Lord from house to house and fed Him. At the invitation of Caitanya dāsa, the son of Śivānanda, the Lord affectionately ate rice with yogurt.

## 1

I worship Śrī Kṛṣṇa Caitanya, who shows grace to His devotees and is pleased with whatever a devotee offers Him with faith.

## 14

*Upayoga* — use, acceptance.

## 16

*Purāṇa śuktā* — dried bitter jute greens (*pāṭa-śāka*).

## 21

A lover strung a garland and, in the presence of her rivals (*sapatnīs*), placed it on the breast of a full-bosomed beauty. She did not cast it off, though it was soiled, for good qualities do not reside in a thing — they reside in love alone.

## 23

*Kuthalī* — small bags.

## 24

*Kola-śuṇṭhi* — dried *kul* berries.

## 25

*Nāḍu-gaṅgājali* — white *nāḍus* (sweet balls).

## 27

*Śālikācaṭi dhānya* — (a kind of) dry unhusked rice.

## 31

*Ukhḍā* is *muḍki*: the small husked grains of the *khai* type that remain after the large *khai* (popped rice) have been picked out.

## 50

*Caitanya-maṅgala* — see the *Caitanya-bhāgavata*, Antya, Chapter 9.

## 68

Inside the Jagannātha temple there is a large hall called the “*jagamohana*.” On one side of it, set apart, stands the “Garuḍa pillar” (*garuḍa-stambha*). The place in this *jagamohana* where the devotees dance is called the “*parimaṇḍala*”; “*parimuṇḍā*” is the Utkala corruption (*apabhraṁśa*) of “*parimaṇḍala*.”[^68-1] Since the Oriyā line is not given here in full, no clear meaning emerges. Such a song is not known in Utkala today; surely it only points to some special feeling.

## 96

For the sake of serving the Lord I take no account even of millions upon millions of offences. But I fear even a shadow of offence committed for my own enjoyment.

## 102

*Parimuṇḍā-nṛtya*: dancing in a circle (*parimaṇḍala-nṛtya*).

## 116

*Ādivasyā*: one who has lived here from long before is called *ādivasyā*. The Lord said: “Those who are *ādivasyā* — that is, who have long lived together with Me — feel no sorrow over this. For it is those who have now come from Gauḍa who brought all these delicacies.”

## 118

*Paiḍa* (an Utkala word): coconut.

## 125

*Mukutā*: with the top cut open.

*Thus ends the tenth chapter of the “Amṛta-pravāha-bhāṣya.”*

---

[^68-1]: In the verse translation, otherwise: “May my head bow down in the hall for kīrtana, the Jagamohana, before the Deity of Lord Jagannātha” (the *Anubhāṣya* also takes the line this way: *muṇḍā* is “head”).
