# Amṛta-pravāha-bhāṣya on Madhya-līlā 15

## 0

**Summary.** When the Ratha-yātrā was over, Śrī Advaita Prabhu worshiped Mahāprabhu with flowers and *tulasī*, and Mahāprabhu in turn worshiped Advaitācārya with the flowers and *tulasī* left on the tray of worship, reciting the *mantra* “Whoever You are, that You are.” After that Advaitācārya invited Mahāprabhu and fed Him. On the day of the Nandotsava the Lord and His associates dressed as cowherds and held a festival of joy. On Vijayā-daśamī, at the festival of the conquest of Laṅkā, He dressed His devotees as an army of monkeys, and He Himself, absorbed in the mood of Hanumān, displayed great joy. Then, after the assembled devotees had seen the other festivals, He told them to return to Gauḍa-deśa. Mahāprabhu sent Nityānanda Prabhu to Gauḍa-deśa as well, together with Rāmadāsa, Gadādhara dāsa and a few other Vaiṣṇavas. Afterwards, with many humble words, He sent *prasāda*, cloth and other things for His mother (through Śrīvāsa). He gave leave to Rāghava Paṇḍita, Vāsudeva Datta, the devotees of Kulīnagrāma and all the other Vaiṣṇavas, describing their many virtues at length. Answering the question of Rāmānanda and Satyarāja, Mahāprabhu instructed householder Vaiṣṇavas to serve Vaiṣṇavas devoted to the pure name. He described the glory of the Vaiṣṇavas of Khaṇḍa (and assigned them their service), (ordered Sārvabhauma and Vidyā-vācaspati to serve the Dāru-brahma and the Jala-brahma), explained Murāri Gupta's devotion to the feet of Śrī Rāma, and, in response to Vāsudeva's prayer, wholly worthy of a Vaiṣṇava, considered Kṛṣṇa's power to deliver the whole world (without effort). Then, while the Lord was accepting a meal at Sārvabhauma's house, some evil thought arose in Amogha, and the next morning he was struck by cholera. The Lord mercifully cured him and gave him a taste for the name of Kṛṣṇa.

## 102–106

Rāmānanda Vasu and his father Satyarāja Khān were householder Vaiṣṇavas of the Kāyastha Vasu line, the glory of Bengal. They asked the Lord, “What is the duty, the *sādhana*, of a householder Vaiṣṇava?” The Lord answered, “To serve Kṛṣṇa, to serve the Vaiṣṇavas and to chant Kṛṣṇa's name without cease — this is the only duty of a householder Vaiṣṇava.” Then Satyarāja asked, “Serving Kṛṣṇa and chanting Kṛṣṇa's name are easy to understand. But if one cannot recognize a Vaiṣṇava, serving Vaiṣṇavas becomes very difficult. So, O Lord, who is a Vaiṣṇava, and what are his general marks?” The Lord answered, “One from whose lips Kṛṣṇa's name is heard even once is the best of all, a Vaiṣṇava worthy of honor.”

## 110

This great *mantra*, the very name of Śrī Kṛṣṇa, attracts the hearts of *sādhus* rich in pious merit, destroys sins, is easily available to all people from the *caṇḍālas* on, except the mute, and brings the wealth of liberation under its control. It gives its fruit as soon as it touches the tongue and does not depend in the least on initiation and the like or on *puraścaraṇa*.

## 111

So for a householder to serve Vaiṣṇavas, it is enough to find a Vaiṣṇava devoted to the single name of Kṛṣṇa, and his service succeeds. A “Vaiṣṇava initiated into a *mantra*” is not considered here. The reason is this: many who are initiated into a Viṣṇu *mantra* lack knowledge of the truth and so may be tainted by Māyāvāda and other faults. But a Vaiṣṇava who utters Kṛṣṇa's name without offense cannot have such faults. One initiated into a *mantra* is “almost a Vaiṣṇava,” but one who has uttered Kṛṣṇa's name even once without offense, even if he is the least of all, is a “pure Vaiṣṇava.” It is such a Vaiṣṇava that a householder Vaiṣṇava should serve.

## 170

I worship the original Person, Govinda. For all living beings, from the *indragopa* insects up to the king of the gods, He arranges (ordains) the enjoyment (*bhoga*) of fruits that match the bonds of their own karma; but in one who possesses devotion He — how wondrous! — burns all karma to ashes.

## 171–179

The literal sense of these verses is simple, but their inner meaning is hard. The inner meaning is this. When the *jīva* turns away from Kṛṣṇa and falls into the bondage of *māyā*, *māyā* creates endless millions of universes and makes those *jīvas* experience the fruits of karma, the consequence of their aversion to Kṛṣṇa. People turned away from Kṛṣṇa must certainly suffer the fruits of their karma. But for those who face Kṛṣṇa (*kṛṣṇonmukha*), that bondage of karma is destroyed completely by Kṛṣṇa's will.

Here one may object: “If becoming a devotee cuts off one's karma, and if all *jīvas* are delivered without punishment the moment some devotee wishes it, then whether a universe exists or not depends on a devotee's will. How then can Kṛṣṇa's world be properly governed?”

The Lord replied: “Kṛṣṇa's spiritual world is endless and immeasurable. There the hosts of His *svarūpa-śakti* serve Kṛṣṇa, their husband, in the form of wish-fulfilling cows (*kāma-dhenus*). That spiritual world, the opulence (*vaibhava*) of the *svarūpa-śakti*, is three quarters (*tripāda*). This material world, ruled by *māyā*, the shadow of the spiritual world, is one quarter (*ekapāda*). *Māyā* is only the shadow of the *svarūpa-śakti*. So for Kṛṣṇa, the master of millions of *kāma-dhenus*, she is no more than a single goat. If by the wish or at the request of a pure devotee one universe of *māyā* is delivered, Kṛṣṇa perceives no loss in it. Far from it: even if the very existence of goatlike *māyā* should vanish together with all the universes of *māyā*, Kṛṣṇa, the Lord of the six opulences, the master of millions of *kāma-dhenus*, suffers not the slightest loss. Can the real object suffer any harm when its shadow is destroyed?”

## 180

O Ajita! Destroy that unborn one (*ajā*), *avidyā* or *māyā*, [who rules] the moving and the nonmoving, who has taken up the modes of goodness, passion and ignorance as faults. (Show Your victory, show Your victory!) For in You, who stand beyond *māyā* by Your own potency, all opulences are contained (by Your very nature). You awaken all the potencies of the world (as the indwelling Lord who awakens them). By Your own potency You enjoy pastimes in the vast spiritual world; and for some reason You glance at *māyā*, Your shadow potency, and through her You perform pastimes (of creation and so on). The Vedas describe (and establish) both kinds of Your pastimes.

## 183

A variant reading is “in Jaleśvara.” This reading seems neither correct nor meaningful, for no pastime of Gadādhara Paṇḍita in the village of Jaleśvara is mentioned anywhere. On the sandy road by the sea, in Yameśvara-ṭoṭā, stands the temple of Śrī Ṭoṭā-Gopīnātha. There Gadādhara Paṇḍita (Gosvāmī) lived, absorbed in the service of Gopīnātha and the service of Mahāprabhu.

## 262–263

Amogha is a brāhmaṇa, so he cannot be killed; [Sārvabhauma] himself is also a brāhmaṇa, so suicide too is improper: both acts are unfit. Therefore the only thing left is not to look at that blasphemer's face.

## 265

One should abandon a fallen husband. (*Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam* 7.11.28): “Content, free from greed, capable, knowing *dharma*, speaking pleasantly and truthfully, attentive, pure and affectionate, [a wife] should serve a husband who has not fallen.”

## 269

What we would have had to accomplish with great effort, gathering many elephants, horses, chariots and foot soldiers, the Gandharvas have already done.

## 270

Life, fortune, fame, *dharma*, the worlds and blessings — a man loses all these highest goods by offending great souls.

*Thus ends the fifteenth chapter of the “Amṛta-pravāha-bhāṣya.”*
