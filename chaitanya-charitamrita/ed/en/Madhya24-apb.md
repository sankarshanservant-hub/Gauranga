# Amṛta-pravāha-bhāṣya on Madhya 24

## 0

**Summary.** At Sanātana’s request Mahāprabhu gave sixty-one meanings of the verse *ātmārāmāś ca munayaḥ*. He explained each word separately and drew out all these meanings by joining them with the senses of the words *ca* and *api*. At the end, explaining the verse, He established its conclusive meaning: *jñānīs*, *karmīs* and *yogīs* all give up their own faults and, along with that, worship Kṛṣṇa. In the course of the explanation He told the story of Nārada and a hunter and spoke of the glory of the company of sādhus: Nārada brought the sage Parvata and showed him the hunter’s devotion to Hari. Then the Lord heard the prayer (*stava*) in which Sanātana glorified Him and revealed the purport and glory of the *Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam*. Last of all, as Sanātana wished, Mahāprabhu told him the outlines (*sūtras*) of the *Hari-bhakti-vilāsa*.

## 1

Śrī Kṛṣṇa Caitanya revealed the rays of meaning of the sun that is the *ātmārāma* verse and dispelled the darkness of the world. May He, who is like the mountain of sunrise (Udayācala), protect the world.

## 11

The word *ātmā* means the body, the mind, Brahman, one’s nature (*svabhāva*), steadfastness (*dhṛti*), intelligence (*buddhi*) and effort (*yatna*).

## 18

The prefix *nir* is used in the senses of certainty, sequence, construction and prohibition. The word *grantha* is used in the senses of wealth, a literary work, and the stringing of letters into words.

## 20

In the word *urukrama*, *uru* means “very great,” and *krama* means a step, as well as trembling and the like (the original causes of power). So the word *urukrama* indicated Viṣṇu in the form of Vāmana, for with His great strides He made the world tremble.

## 21

Even if someone could count all the dust of the earth, who could count Viṣṇu’s mighty deeds? In the form of Vāmana, with the unchecked speed of His stride, He shook [everything] from the root of *prakṛti*, made of the three *guṇas*, up to Tripṛṣṭha (Satyaloka) — and held it fast.

## 24

The word *krama* means power, orderly arrangement (*paripāṭī*), movement and trembling.

## 26

A root that takes both voices (*ubhayapadī*) is marked with the *svarita* accent and the indicatory letter *ñ* (*it*). If the fruit of the action is meant for the agent himself, the *ātmanepada* is used. Here that is not so, and therefore the *parasmaipada* is used.

## 28

*Siddhi*: the eighteen *siddhis*, beginning with *aṇimā* (see *Bhāgavatam*, Canto 11, Chapter 15).

## 30

*Prema-bhakti* is of nine kinds: *rati*, *prema*, *sneha*, *māna*, *praṇaya*, *rāga*, *anurāga*, *bhāva* and *mahābhāva* — these nine.

## 46

“O saintly king, although I was firmly established in transcendence of the *guṇas* (*nairguṇya*), Śrī Kṛṣṇa’s pastimes attracted me, and I studied the *Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam*.”

## 48

“O Kṛṣṇa, we have seen Your face framed by locks of hair, adorned with earrings, cheeks and the nectar of Your lips, Your glance with a gentle smile, Your two arms that grant fearlessness, and Your chest adorned by Śrī alone — and we have become Your maidservants.”

## 49

“O most beautiful in all the worlds, Your qualities enter the ears of those who hear of them and destroy the sufferings of their bodies. Those who have eyes attain all the aims of life by seeing Your form. O Acyuta, hearing of these qualities, my heart has cast off shame and is entering into You.”

## 52

“O Kṛṣṇa, what woman in the three worlds, enchanted by the nectar of the sweet words of Your flute-song, would not swerve from the conduct of the Āryans (*dharma*)? Seeing this form of Yours, the beauty of the three worlds, even the cows, birds, trees and beasts bristle with rapture.”

## 56

The four kinds of suffering are the sufferings of the four kinds of sin: (1) *pātaka*, (2) *urupātaka*, (3) *mahāpātaka* and (4) *atipātaka*.

## 57

“O Uddhava, as fire turns wood to ashes, so devotion to Bhagavān at once destroys all the sins of the *jīva*.”

## 63

The word *ca* is used in the senses of *anvācaya* (that is, adding something secondary), *samāhāra* (combining into a whole), *anyonya* (mutuality), *samuccaya* (conjunction), *yatnāntara* (another effort), *pāda-pūraṇa* (filling out the metrical line) and *avadhāraṇa*, that is, certainty.

## 64

The word *api* is used in the senses of *sambhāvanā* (possibility), *praśna* (question), *śaṅkā* (doubt), *garhā* (censure), *samuccaya* (conjunction), *yukta-padārtha* (fitting application) and *kāmacāra-kriyā* (acting at will).

## 67

The Truth to which *bṛhattva* and *bṛṁhaṇatva* — that is, the power to make things grow — belong is called the “Supreme Brahman.” “O Self of all, You are the one on whom the yogīs meditate, the Unchanging. Obeisance to You.”

## 74

Hari, to whom extension (*vistṛtatva*) and the power to give birth (*prasavitṛtva*) belong, is Himself the Paramātmā.

## 83

Those whose bodies bristle with rapture, wet with drops of tears born of the agitation of *anurāga*, when they tell one another about Kṛṣṇa, follow Kṛṣṇa, the God of gods, and cast *yama*, *niyama* and the like far away. With a character that makes us long to be like them, they go to Vaikuṇṭha, above us.

## 86

This verse says that if one is *udāra-dhī*, that is, intelligent and able to reason, one will worship Kṛṣṇa even while having desires.

## 89

“O Arjuna, the distressed, the inquisitive, the seeker of wealth and the *jñānī* — these four kinds of people, when they gain pious merit and turn towards *bhakti*, give up their particular desires and worship Me.”

## 93

Through the company of saintly devotees a wise person gives up bad association. Having once heard the delightful glory of the One who is glorified, he can never give it up.

## 97

“Covering the desire” (*icchāra pidhāna*) means fulfilling it.

## 99

The desireless (*akāma*), the one full of desires (*sarvakāma*) and the one who desires liberation (*mokṣakāma*) — these three give up the fault of their particular desires and attain pure *bhakti* for Kṛṣṇa.

## 102–106

Those who worship on the path of knowledge are of two kinds: *kevala-brahmopāsakas* (who worship Brahman alone) and *mokṣākāṅkṣīs* (who aspire to liberation). One who worships Brahman with the desire for *kaivalya* is a *kevala-brahmopāsaka*. They have three stages: the *sādhaka* (beginner), the (eternally perfect) *brahmamaya* (absorbed in Brahman), and the *prāpta-brahma-laya* (merged in Brahman, that is, the *brahma-bhūta*). Without *bhakti*, knowledge cannot give liberation. Only one who has attained merging in Brahman (that is, has become *brahma-bhūta*) can practice *bhakti*. When the practice of *bhakti* appears, the nature of *bhakti* appears too. By this nature of hers, *bhakti* gradually draws him away from Brahman, gives him a divine body and engages him in the worship of Kṛṣṇa. Having received the body of his choice in which he serves the Lord, the devotee remembers all of Kṛṣṇa’s qualities and, attracted by them, worships Him with pure worship.

## 107

Even the liberated, assuming a form (*vigraha*) in *līlā*, worship Bhagavān.

## 112

The mind of Bhagavān Śukadeva, dear to the Vaiṣṇavas, was captivated by Hari’s qualities, and he studied this great narration.

## 115

The nine Yogendras entered Brahmā’s assembly, which is free from distress, and heard the Upaniṣads. Having become knowers of the Śruti and bristling with rapture, they (in order to go to Dvārakā, the city of the Yadus) reached the *raṅga-kṣetra*.

## 118

The *mumukṣus* give up the lords of the *bhūtas*, whose forms are terrible, yet without hostility towards them, and worship the portions (*kalās*) of Nārāyaṇa.

## 119

Having shown that the four Sanas and Śukadeva were absorbed in Brahman (*brahmamaya*) and that the nine Yogendras were *sādhakas*, the Lord considers the three kinds of *jñānīs* who aspire to liberation: the *mumukṣu* (seeker of liberation), the *jīvan-mukta* (liberated while living) and the *prāpta-svarūpa* (one who has attained his true nature). First He speaks of the *mumukṣus*: in the company of sādhus Bhagavān’s qualities manifest in them, and so these *mumukṣus* give up the desire for liberation and worship Kṛṣṇa.

## 120

“O great soul, though this worldly existence has many faults, it has one great virtue — the company of sādhus. This one virtue brings happiness, and by it our desire for liberation has today grown weak.”

## 123

“When here in Vṛṣṇi-pattana, Dvārakā, Kṛṣṇa — the embodiment of dense spiritual bliss — appeared to me, happiness dawned within me. Alas, I clung to being an *ātmārāma*, and many of my days have passed in vain!”

## 125

“Because of offence they sink down”: the *jīvan-muktas* produced by dry knowledge fall because of offences and sink, that is, they are ruined.

## 130

When the *ātmā*, together with the potencies (*śaktis*), lies down to rest (*anuśayana*), this is called the *jīva’s* *nirodha*. To give up the other form and to be established (situated in a special way) in one’s own *svarūpa* is what is called *mukti*.

## 139

The six kinds of *ātmārāmas* — the *sādhaka* (the beginner), the *brahmamaya* (one absorbed in Brahman) and the *prāpta-brahma-laya* (one merged in Brahman), and the *mumukṣu* (the seeker of liberation), the *jīvan-mukta* (one liberated while living) and the *prāpta-svarūpa* (one who has attained his true nature) — these six kinds[^139-1] are “*ātmārāmas*.”

## 140

“*Munayaḥ santaḥ*” means attachment to contemplating Kṛṣṇa: all the *ātmārāmas* become sages (*munis*) and grow attached to contemplating Kṛṣṇa.

## 145

When several words have the same form and one remaining word (*ekaśeṣa*) with one case ending expresses their meaning, one form is kept and all the others are dropped. Thus instead of “*rāmaś ca, rāmaś ca, rāmaś ca*” a single “*rāmāḥ*” is used.

## 150

Some yogīs, by fixed concentration (*dhāraṇā*), remember the Puruṣa who dwells in the heart within their own body: He is the size of a span (*prādeśa*), has four arms, and holds conch, disc, club and lotus. This is the mark of the *sagarbha* yogī.

## 151

Having thus attained *bhāva* for Bhagavān Hari, the yogī feels his heart melt with devotion, and the hairs of his body stand on end with bliss. Again and again streams of tears of bliss, born of longing, overwhelm him — he is drowning in bliss. Then, little by little, he draws his meditating mind away from holding the object of meditation, as one draws out a hook (*baḍiśa*) used to catch fish. This is an example of the *nirgarbha* yogī.

## 153–154

One who wishes to ascend to the stage of yoga is an “*ārurukṣu*.” For such an *ārurukṣu* sage, the “cause,” the means, is action: *yama*, *niyama*, *āsana* and *prāṇāyāma*. For one who has already ascended to yoga, the “cause” is tranquillity (*śama*): *dhyāna*, *dhāraṇā* and *pratyāhāra*. And when the yogī has no attachment to sense objects or to actions, he gives up all intentions (*saṅkalpas*) and becomes “absorbed in *samādhi*,” or “*yogārūḍha*” (one who has ascended to yoga).

## 158

“When all these *śāntas* serve” — that is, when all these yogīs have risen to *śānta-rasa* and serve the Lord.

## 160

On the path followed by the *sampradāyas* of the *ṛṣis*, some, in *karma-yoga*, worship the “*udara*,” that is, Brahman situated in the *maṇipūra*: these are the Śārkarākṣa *ṛṣis*, “*kūrpa-dṛk*,” that is, of gross vision. The Āruṇi *ṛṣis*, also followers of a *sampradāya* of *ṛṣis*, worship subtle Brahman in the “*dahara*,” that is, in the space of the heart, where the *nāḍīs* spread out. O Ananta! Higher than this is the path that leads to the head: beginning from the *mūlādhāra*, it passes through the heart and rises to the head, up to the *brahma-randhra*. This is the *suṣumnā* — the place where You are realised, the thousand-petalled lotus, the supreme, effulgent abode. Having risen there, yogīs no longer fall into *saṁsāra*, into the jaws of death.

## 163

There is a rare treasure that cannot be gained even by wandering through the higher worlds — Satyaloka, or Brahmaloka, and the rest — and the lower ones — Sutala, Atala and the rest. For that alone the wise should strive. For all the happiness found above and below, in all fourteen worlds, comes of itself, without effort, just as suffering does: time brings it, time whose course cannot be resisted.

## 166

*Bhakti* is very hard to attain in two ways. Without *āsaṅga* (longing for Kṛṣṇa’s love) it is not gained quickly even by thousands upon thousands of spiritual practices (*sādhanas*); and Kṛṣṇa, too, does not give *bhakti* readily.

## 170

O mother! The birds of this forest fly up onto the branches of the trees, adorned with lovely young shoots, close their eyes, fall silent, and listen to the sweet song of the flute that flows from Kṛṣṇa’s lips. They too are much like sages.

## 171

O sinless one, O Original Person! These bees sing Your glory, which purifies all the worlds, and serve You (following You along Your path). These sages in the guise of bees do not leave You — their Lord, the Deity of their hearts — even though Your form is hidden.

## 172

When Śrī Kṛṣṇa plays the flute, the birds — *sārasa* cranes, swans and others — are drawn by its sound, close their eyes and worship Him.

## 173

The Kirātas, Hūṇas, Andhras, Pulindas, Pukkaśas, Ābhīras, (Śuhmas,) Yavanas, Khaśas and others — all who are born in sinful castes — become purified by taking shelter of the Vaiṣṇavas who have taken shelter of Him. I bow down to Viṣṇu, who possesses such power.

## 175

*Dhṛti* is freedom from suffering through attaining the highest, and the awareness of fullness. When something desired is not obtained, or something once obtained is lost, grief comes — and it is *dhṛti* that removes it.

## 178

In this world where every living being is unsteady, that is, in transient *saṁsāra*, only one whose senses are fixed in Hṛṣīkeśa, Kṛṣṇa, has attained steadiness.

## 183

“I am the source (the place of origin) of all, and everything proceeds from Me.” Knowing this, the wise, endowed with *bhakti*, worship Me.

## 184

Women, *śūdras*, Hūṇas, Śabaras and other sinful beings, and even beings of animal species — birds and others — receive instruction from those wholly devoted to Adbhuta-krama (Bhagavān Śrī Urukrama), that is, they follow the conduct of pure devotees and themselves become devotees of Bhagavān. Then they are delivered from (insurmountable, divine) Māyā. What, then, to speak of those who follow the path of the Śrutis?

## 187

“*Bhāgavata*” and “*nāma*” are the *Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam* and the name of Śrī Kṛṣṇa.

## 200

This land (the land of Vraja) has today become blessed. Blessed are the grasses and creepers that Your feet touch; blessed are the trees and vines that Your fingers touch; blessed are the rivers, hills, birds and beasts on whom You cast Your merciful glance. And blessed are all the *gopīs*, for they have gained the place between Your arms — which even Lakṣmī longs for.

## 201

O friends! Kṛṣṇa and Baladeva go from forest to forest with the cows and the cowherd boys, bearing as Their marks the ropes and nooses for tying the cows, and so on. Their wondrous flute-playing and songs make the moving beings among the embodied grow stunned, while the motionless trees break out in “gooseflesh.” All this is truly wonderful.

## 209

(O Sūta!) On this path of *karma* there is nothing to rely on (that is, no hope of a sure result), and we have been blackened by smoke. Yet you are giving us to drink the honeyed nectar (*āsava*) of Govinda’s lotus feet.

## 211

The taste for serving His feet, like the Gaṅgā flowing from the great toe of Kṛṣṇa’s foot, grows day by day. At once it destroys the impurity of the intelligence that (worldly) ascetics have accumulated over countless births.

## 217

“*Baṭo, bhikṣām aṭa, gāṁ cānaya*” — “O *baṭu*, go and beg alms, and bring the cow as well.” Just as in this sentence the word *ca* has the meaning of *anvācaya*, so take it in the *ātmārāma* verse.

## 228

“*Õta*” — in hiding, concealed in among [the branches].

## 244

“*Kadarthanā diyā*”: by inflicting torment.

## 259

“*Nāradera upadeśe*”: according to Nārada’s instruction.

## 262

“*Śunaha, Parvate*”: O sage Parvata, listen.

## 272

O *devarṣi*, you alone are truly blessed: by your mercy even a low *lubdhaka*, that is, a hunter, thrilled with ecstasy and attained *rati* for Kṛṣṇa.

## 281–285

The *pārṣadas* — that is, the *nitya-siddhas* — the *sādhana-siddhas*, the *jāta-rati sādhakas* and the *ajāta-rati sādhakas* are each of four kinds, on the path of rules (*vaidha*) and on the path of *rāga*. The eternally perfect *pārṣadas* are again of four kinds: servants, friends, elders (*gurus*) and beloveds. And the *sādhana-siddhas*, the *jāta-rati sādhakas* and the *ajāta-rati sādhakas* each have these same four kinds as well.

## 294

The word *vṛkṣāḥ* (“trees”) expresses the *aśvattha*, the banyan, the *kapittha* and the mango. Therefore words whose meaning is already expressed are not used here.

## 300

By adding *eva* to the four words *urukrama*, *bhakti*, *ahaitukī* and *kurvanti*, I shall give one more meaning.

## 304

The word *kṣetrajña* means *ātmā*, *puruṣa*, *pradhāna* and *prakṛti*.

## 314

Mahādeva said: “I know, Śuka knows, and Vyāsa may know or may not.” The *Bhāgavatam* is grasped by *bhakti* alone; it is never grasped by intellect or by commentaries.

## 315

Tell us: now that Kṛṣṇa — the Lord of yoga, Brahmaṇyadeva, the very armour of *dharma* — has reached His own *kāṣṭhā* (His eternal abode), with whom has *dharma* taken shelter?

## 316

Now that Kṛṣṇa has gone to His own abode together with *dharma*, knowledge and the rest, this sun among the Purāṇas has risen for the good of people stricken by Kali, who have lost their sight.

## 320

“*Nīca-jāti*”: Sanātana said, “I am of the brāhmaṇa caste, but I have fallen through association with *mlecchas*.”

## 343

This Sanātana, Rūpa’s elder brother, was the jewel adorning the court of the lord of Gauḍa, the emperor Husain Shāh. He gave up prosperous royal wealth and embraced the youthful wealth of renunciation. Within, his heart was full of *bhakti-rasa*; outwardly he looked like an *avadhūta*. Like a great lake covered with moss, that Śrī Sanātana delighted those who know the truth of *bhakti*.

## 344

As soon as He saw that Sanātana had come, Gaurasundara, golden like a *campaka*, was filled with boundless compassion; He stretched out both arms and, showing His mercy, embraced him.

*Thus ends the twenty-fourth chapter of the “Amṛta-pravāha-bhāṣya.”*
