# Amṛta-pravāha-bhāṣya on Antya-līlā 12

## 0

**Summary.** At night Mahāprabhu was overwhelmed by the transformations of love (*prema-vikāra*), and by day these went on being discussed. Meanwhile Śivānanda Sena set out from Gauḍa (with the devotees), taking his wife and three sons. On the way there was a delay in finding lodging for Nityānanda Prabhu, and He, showing Śivānanda the anger of love (*prema-kopa*), kicked him. Śivānanda himself felt blessed by this, but his nephew Śrīkānta Sena was hurt and went on ahead to Mahāprabhu. That year Parameśvara dāsa, a confectioner (*modaka*), also came with his family to see Mahāprabhu. As in previous years, the devotees invited Mahāprabhu to meals. When they were leaving, Mahāprabhu spoke many humble words to them. The year before, Mahāprabhu had sent Jagadānanda Paṇḍita to Śrī Śacīmātā with cloth and *prasāda* [of Jagannātha]. At Śivānanda’s house Jagadānanda prepared a pot of fragrant oil called *candanādi*, brought it with him and asked Govinda to apply it to Mahāprabhu’s head. Mahāprabhu would not accept the oil, so Jagadānanda broke the pot, oil and all, and fasted for two days. To pacify him, Mahāprabhu asked him for alms (*bhikṣā*). Then Jagadānanda Paṇḍita cooked rice and vegetables, fed Mahāprabhu and himself took *prasāda*.

## 1

O devotees, always hear this *Caitanya-caritāmṛta*, sing it, and joyfully meditate on it.

## 19

*Bhoke* — from hunger.

## 37

*Peṭāṅgi* — a shirt (*aṅgarākhā*), a jacket.

## 53

*Śivānandera prakṛti* — Śivānanda’s wife.

## 60

To say to a *sannyāsī*, “Mukunda’s mother has come,” is nothing but the boldness born of long familiarity (*pūrva-praśraya-prāgalbhya*). The boldness of familiarity (*praśraya-prāgalbhya*) never knows pure courtesy (*śuddha-vaidagdhī*), that is, the pure art of speech.

## 102

*Ekamātrā* — sixteen *sers*.

## 103

*Gāgarī* — a pitcher (*kalasī*).

## 114

*Dārī sannyāsī* — a *sannyāsī* who lives with a wife.

## 154

*Prema-vivarta.* The first meaning: the *vivarta* of love, that is, behaviour in which the acts of love appear to be anger. The second meaning: what Jagadānanda wrote about Mahāprabhu’s life in his own book, the *Prema-vivarta*.

*Thus ends the twelfth chapter of the “Amṛta-pravāha-bhāṣya.”*
