## 126

> Subheading: The Vaiṣṇava who plays the part of a “deceiver” —

The guru-Vaiṣṇava is rich with the treasure of transcendental love for Kṛṣṇa, and to him the worth of external material wealth is utterly trifling. Therefore one who seeks the highest good (*niḥśreyas*) and tries to become a disciple of the guru-Vaiṣṇava should never parade before him the arrogance of worldly intoxication. Sometimes a person, relying on his pride of wealth, learning and beauty, comes before the guru-Vaiṣṇava only in appearance, but in reality does not approach him with obeisance, sincere inquiry and service. Then the Vaiṣṇava too gives him the outward honor he desires and sends him away. Regarding him as a non-brāhmaṇa or a *śūdra*, the Vaiṣṇava never gives him divine knowledge, that is, the transcendental realization of one's relationship with Kṛṣṇa. As a result, that person is cheated of the highest goal and goes straight down the road to hell. This is what Śrī Gaurasundara taught as the ācārya, the guru of the world. In the eyes of worldly people He Himself stood in the highest position of *varṇāśrama-dharma* (the brāhmaṇa *varṇa* and the *sannyāsa āśrama*), and He showed Śrī Rāmānanda Prabhu standing lower than Himself (in the *śūdra varṇa* and the householder *āśrama*). In this way He warned the foolish *jīvas*, struck down by Kali, for whom knowledge born of the senses is everything, against such folly.

## 127

> Subheading: Whatever his position, only a knower of the truth of Kṛṣṇa gives divine knowledge —

Whether by *varṇa* he is a brāhmaṇa or a *kṣatriya*, *vaiśya* or *śūdra*, whether by *āśrama* he is a *sannyāsī*, *brahmacārī*, *vānaprastha* or householder — in whatever *varṇa* or *āśrama* he may be, only a knower of the truth of Kṛṣṇa can be a guru, that is, one who shows the path, a *dīkṣā-guru* and a *śikṣā-guru*. A guru's qualification depends only on his knowledge of the truth of Kṛṣṇa, not on *varṇa* or *āśrama*. This order of Śrī Mahāprabhu does not contradict the orders of scripture. In keeping with this purport, Śrī Viśvambhara Mahāprabhu received initiation from the *sannyāsī* Śrī Īśvara Purī, Śrī Nityānanda Prabhu from the *sannyāsī* Mādhavendra Purī Gosvāmī (according to another opinion, from Śrīmad Lakṣmīpati Tīrtha), and Śrī Advaita Ācārya from that same *sannyāsī* Śrī Mādhavendra Purī. Śrī Rasikānanda received *pāñcarātrika* initiation from Śrī Śyāmānanda, who was not born in a family of brāhmaṇas by birth; Śrī Gaṅgānārāyaṇa Cakravartī and Śrī Rāmakṛṣṇa Bhaṭṭācārya from Śrīla Narottama Ṭhākura, who also was not born in a family of brāhmaṇas by birth; and Śrī Yadunandana Cakravartī of Kāṭoyā from Śrī Gadādhara dāsa. Nothing prevented many others, such as Dharma-vyādha, from being *śikṣā-gurus*.

The *Mahābhārata* orders this clearly, and the *Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam* says the same: “Whatever marks are declared to reveal a person's *varṇa*, if they are seen in someone else as well, he should be designated by that very [*varṇa*].” (*Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam* 7.11.32[^127-1]) The verb here stands in the injunctive mood (*vidhi-liṅ*). Therefore, for one who follows the conviction of the Vaiṣṇavas, it is natural to recognize a knower of the truth of Kṛṣṇa as a brāhmaṇa by conduct (*vṛtta-brāhmaṇa*). And where, by the custom current in Kali, there is no brāhmaṇa status without a connection by seed, even a *śūdra* by birth, if he knows the truth of Kṛṣṇa, attains brāhmaṇa status according to scripture and can become a guru. This is what Śrī Mahāprabhu subtly explained.

Those knowers of the truth of Kṛṣṇa who do not accept the *sāvitrya-saṁskāra* described in Kātyāyana's “*guhya-sūtra*”[^127-2] of the Vedic Vājasaneya branch are simply *daikṣya* brāhmaṇas of the Ekāyana branch. But foolish people often fail to understand that they are “brāhmaṇas of Acyuta,” and go to hell. For this reason, in the family of Rasikānanda Prabhu, in the family of Śrī Mukunda dāsa of Śrīkhaṇḍa and in the family of Navanī Hoḍa, the *sāvitrya* brāhmaṇa *saṁskāra* has been performed from time immemorial, together with the work of ācāryas for a *sampradāya* whose disciples are brāhmaṇas by birth. Vaiṣṇavas whose joy is *bhajana* (*bhajanānandīs*) did not accept the *sāvitrya-saṁskāra*, but it does not follow that their example is the only rule. Vaiṣṇavas determine *varṇa* by its marks. Fools, however, cannot determine *varṇa* by such marks, and therefore Śrī Mahāprabhu explained the purport of scripture clearly.

The conclusion collected in the *Hari-bhakti-vilāsa* agrees with Śrī Mahāprabhu's own example and teaching, yet to a fool they seem different. In his judgment the word “guru” in this verse means only the *śravaṇa-guru* or the *śikṣā-guru* in *bhajana*, not the guru who gives *dīkṣā* or mantra. For in his opinion the right to give divine knowledge is determined and certified by family descent, that is, by blood or seed. So in his view devotion to Kṛṣṇa, the natural function of the pure self, is not independent. What is more, by his foolishness the superiority and greatness of the *dīkṣā-guru*, the giver of mantra, exceed those of the “*śravaṇa-guru*” or the “*śikṣā-guru* in *bhajana*”! On this one should especially study the *Anubhāṣya* on Ādi 1.47. In fact such a notion is merely the fruit of an offense born of sense-derived knowledge.

## 137

> Subheading: Madana-mohana, the form eternally served in Vraja —

***Vṛndāvana*** — the *Brahma-saṁhitā* (Chapter 5, verse 56) says: “There the beloveds are Lakṣmīs, and the Lover is the Supreme Person; the trees are wish-fulfilling trees, the earth is made of multitudes of *cintāmaṇi* gems, the water is nectar; speech there is song, walking is dance, the flute is the dear companion; the light is spiritual bliss, and that supreme [reality] is what is relished. There a great ocean of milk flows from the *surabhi* [cows], and time does not pass away, not even half a blink of an eye. I worship that Śvetadvīpa, which only a few saints, rarely wandering on this earth, know as Goloka.”

[The meaning of the verse:] “In the transcendental Vṛndāvana everything is spiritual. The transcendental Lakṣmīs, that is, the gopīs, are the beloveds; the Supreme Person, Kṛṣṇa, is the Lover of all. The trees there are wish-fulfilling trees, the ground is full of *cintāmaṇi* gems, the water is nectar, speech is song, walking is dance, the flute is the dear companion; all that shines — the moon, the sun and the rest — is full of spiritual bliss. It is this transcendental, spiritual mood that one should relish and cultivate. There an ocean of milk flows from the spiritual cows. There even half a blink of an eye is eternity; in other words, time there does not pass in vain and turn into another time. This abode of Vṛndāvana, manifest in the world, that Śvetadvīpa which a few rare saints who know the truth of Kṛṣṇa know as Goloka — that is what I worship.”

Vṛndāvana cannot be seen through earthly knowledge, which one gains and enjoys with one's own material senses guided by a material intellect; for the transcendental Vṛndāvana is the transcendental field of Kṛṣṇa's transcendental pastimes. Śrīla Narottama Ṭhākura sings in his *Prārthanā*: “When will Nitāicānd bestow His mercy on me? When will the desires of worldly life become trifling to me? When will my mind give up the objects of the senses and become pure? When shall I behold Śrī Vṛndāvana? When will longing for the feet of Rūpa and Raghunātha arise in me? When shall I understand the love of the Divine Couple?” See Madhya 14.219–226.

***Aprākṛta navīna madana*** (the transcendental, ever-new Madana). *Kāma* exists in both states: in the material, worldly one, and in its opposite, the spiritual, transcendental one. But material *kāma* is disturbed by time: it is felt only at the moment it appears, and the next moment it fades and is gone. Transcendental *kāma*, however, is ever new: in time it has no end, and it shines always. The *kāma* perceptible to the material senses is a movement of the material body and mind. It is present in the nature of every *jīva* who has turned away from Kṛṣṇa and is bent on gratifying his senses, but it is only momentary. And the Madana whom the spiritual senses serve is Kṛṣṇacandra, Manmatha-manmatha: He is ever new, He is the *svayaṁ-rūpa*, the original form.

***Kāma-gāyatrī.*** “You are called *gāyatrī* because you save the one who sings [you]” — that is, that which saves the singer or gives salvation through singing. (*Caitanya-caritāmṛta*, Madhya 21.125): “The *kāma-gāyatrī*, in the form of a mantra, is Kṛṣṇa's own nature. It has twenty-four and a half syllables. Those syllables are moons that rise upon Kṛṣṇa and have filled the three worlds with *kāma*.” [The mantra itself:] “*Klīṁ.* Let us know Kāmadeva; let us meditate on Puṣpabāṇa; may that Anaṅga inspire us.” Kāmadeva (see verse 187), that is, Kṛṣṇa as Madana-mohana, is the Deity who is the Lord of *sambandha*; Puṣpabāṇa, that is, Govinda, is the Lord of *abhidheya*; and Anaṅga, that is, Gopījana-vallabha, is the Lord of *prayojana*. The *kāma-gāyatrī* is transcendental. The *sādhaka* worships Kṛṣṇa in transcendental realization, relying on the transcendental composition [of the mantra].

The *Brahma-saṁhitā* (Chapter 5, verses 27–28) says: “Then the movement of the sound of the flute, the embodiment of the three Vedas, appeared and at once entered the lotus mouths of Svayambhū. The lotus-born, receiving from it the *gāyatrī* as it was sung, and purified by the original guru, became twice-born. Then Vidhi, awakened by the three Vedas, having come to know the ocean of truth, praised Keśava with this hymn, the essence of the Vedas.” Then the movement of the sound of Śrī Kṛṣṇa's flute, the embodiment of the three Vedas (the mother of the Vedas, of three times eight syllables, threefold, that is, consisting of *sambandha*, *abhidheya* and *prayojana*), appeared and suddenly entered the lotus mouths of Svayambhū Brahmā. Brahmā, born of the lotus, received initiation into the *gāyatrī* that came forth from the song of the flute, and the original guru, Śrī Kṛṣṇa, performed for him the rite of the twice-born (see the commentary of Śrī Jīva Prabhu). Awakened by remembering the *gāyatrī* — the embodiment of the three Vedas, that is, endowed with *sambandha*, *abhidheya* and *prayojana* — Brahmā became immersed in the ocean of truth, that is, attained realization. With that hymn, the essence of the Vedas, he served Keśava and won His love.

***Kāma-bīja*** — the transcendental “*klīṁ*.” The *Brahma-saṁhitā* (Chapter 5, verse 3) says: “…[that] which abides in the *rasa* of the great bliss, the bliss of *prema*, and is joined with the mantra shining as light, the *kāma-bīja*.” With the transcendental *kāma-gāyatrī* joined to the transcendental *kāma-bīja*, one performs transcendental worship of the transcendental, ever-new form of Madana-mohana. Thus the *Gopāla-tāpanī Upaniṣad* says: “Its utterance is this: beginning with the *kāma* [*bīja*] formed by the union of water, earth, [fire] and the moon, ‘*kṛṣṇāya*’ is the first word, ‘*govindāya*’ the second, ‘*gopījana*’ the third, ‘*vallabhāya*’ the fourth, ‘*svāhā*’ the fifth. Chanting this five-word [mantra] with its five limbs — heaven and earth, sun and moon together with fire — one attains Brahman in that form.” The commentary of Śrī Viśvanātha Cakravartī: “‘Water’ is the syllable *ka*, because it denotes water; ‘earth’ is the syllable *la*, because *la* is its *bīja*; ‘*ī*,’ the long *ī*, is fire, because it arises in the joining of sounds (*sandhi*); the ‘moon’ is the *anusvāra*, because it resembles it in shape. Their union is their joining; the *kāma-bīja* born of that joining comes first, and ‘*kṛṣṇāya*’ is one word — this is the meaning.” In other words, the *bīja* “*klīṁ*” is manifested by the joining of water (the syllable *ka*), earth (the syllable *la*), *ī* (the long *ī*, or fire) and the moon (the *anusvāra*). Placing this *bīja* “*klīṁ*” at the beginning of the Kṛṣṇa mantra, one worships the Supreme Brahman named Kṛṣṇa — the *rasana*, that is, the worship that pleases Him. See Ādi 5.212–214, 219, 221–222.

## 166–170

The *svarūpa* of Śrīmatī Rādhikā is the *cintāmaṇi* of *mahābhāva* that fulfills Kṛṣṇa's desires. Lalitā and the other *sakhīs* are like expansions of Her body (*kāya-vyūha*), that is, Her manifestations. (1) Anointed with the paste of Kṛṣṇa's *sneha*, She takes the first, morning bath; its water is the nectar of compassion, that is, new youth full of compassion at the beginning of *kaiśora*, when *paugaṇḍa* has passed. (2) The water of the second, midday bath is the nectar of youth, that is, manifest youth. (3) The water of the next, afternoon bath is the nectar of loveliness, that is, full youth. In other words, the bodily qualities — age, form and loveliness — are the water of the three baths.

Her garments are of two kinds: (1) the lower garment and (2) the upper. (1) The lower garment is modesty: a blue *sāṛī* woven of dark silk threads. The second garment is red: it is *anurāga* for Kṛṣṇa. Śrī Rādhā's breast is covered by a bodice, the *māna* of *praṇaya* for Kṛṣṇa. The *kuṅkuma* is beauty, a bodily quality of Śrī Rādhā; the sandal is Her comeliness (*abhirūpatā*), that is, the love (*praṇaya*) of the *sakhīs*; the camphor is Her sweetness (*mādhurya*), that is, the luster of Her smile. These three anoint Her body, that is, Her body is adorned with beauty, comeliness and sweetness. The musk (*mṛgamada*) is Kṛṣṇa's *ujjvala-rasa*; this is Her bodily quality of softness (*mārdava*).

## 171

**Hidden *māna*** — though full of crookedness within, She shows compliance (*dākṣiṇya*) outwardly. ***Vāmya*** — contrariness, the absence of straightforwardness; see the *Anubhāṣya* on Madhya 14.161. ***Dhammilla*** — hair tied in a knot (*khõpā*).

**The quality of being *dhīrādhīrā*.** The *Ujjvala-nīlamaṇi* says: “The *dhīrādhīrā* speaks to her Beloved with crooked words and with tears; one endowed with the qualities of the *dhīrā* and the *adhīrā* is called *dhīrādhīrā*.” The heroine who speaks to her Beloved with crooked words, as is the way of the *dhīrā*, and speaks with tear-filled eyes, as is the way of the *adhīrā*, is the “*dhīrādhīrā*.” See Madhya 14.143–153. The qualities of the “*dhīrādhīrā madhyā*” are also found in the *dhīrādhīrā pragalbhā*. Of the three — the *pragalbhā*, the *madhyā* and the *mugdhā* — the *pragalbhā*, greatly angered, is ready to strike; the *madhyā*, possessed by incomplete anger, speaks harsh words; and the *mugdhā*, only slightly angered, weeps. This quality shows itself especially in the state of the *khaṇḍitā*.

***Paṭṭavāsa*** — a head covering (*pāgaḍī*), a silken upper cloth of a single piece. A variant reading is *paṭavāsa*: a tent, fragrant powder, rice paste (*piṭāli*), a *sāṛī*.

## 172

The color of the betel is *rāga* for Kṛṣṇa; it makes Her lips shine. The black on Her eyes is the crookedness of love.

## 173

**The *sūddīpta sāttvika-bhāvas*** — see Madhya 6.12. **The thirty-three *sañcārī-bhāvas* beginning with *harṣa*** — see Madhya 3.127 and the *Amṛta-pravāha-bhāṣya* on Madhya 14.167–168.

## 174

**The *bhāvas* beginning with *kila-kiñcita*** — see Madhya 14.168. **The flower garland of qualities** — see Madhya 23.82–86.

The twenty-five qualities described in the *Ujjvala-nīlamaṇi*: “Why say much? Her qualities, like Hari's, are beyond count. Here four kinds of qualities of the Queen of Vṛndāvana are named: those residing in the body, in speech, in the mind, and those connected with others.” What more can be said? Like Śrī Hari, Śrī Rādhikā eternally possesses countless qualities. They fall into four groups: (a) those residing in the body (*aṅgastha*), (b) those residing in speech (*uktistha*), (c) those residing in the mind (*manastha*), and (d) those connected with others (*para-sambandhaga*).

(a) The qualities residing in the body are six: 1) *madhurā* — charming; 2) *nava-vayāḥ* — youthful (*kiśorī*); 3) *calāpāṅgā* — with restless sidelong glances; 4) *ujjvala-smitā* — with a radiant smile; 5) *cāru-saubhāgya-rekhā-yuktā* — marked with beautiful lines of good fortune, the signs of the moon on Her feet and elsewhere; 6) *gandhonmādita-mādhavā* — who maddens Mādhava with Her fragrance.

(b) The qualities residing in speech are three: 1) *saṅgīta-prasarābhijñā* — expert in the art of song; 2) *ramya-vāk* — whose speech is charming; 3) *narma-paṇḍitā* — skilled in jesting.

(c) The qualities residing in the mind are ten: 1) *vinītā* — modest; 2) *karuṇā-pūrṇā* — full of compassion; 3) *vidagdhā* — clever and refined; 4) *pāṭavānvitā* — adroit; 5) *lajjā-śīlā* — shy, which comes from noble birth, good character and the like; 6) [endowed with] *maryādā* — never straying from the path of the virtuous; 7) *dhairya-śālinī* — steady, that is, patient in suffering; 8) *gāmbhīrya-śālinī* — deep; 9) *suvilāsā* — graceful in play; 10) *mahābhāva-paramotkarṣa-tarṣiṇī* — who longs for the highest summit of *mahābhāva*.

(d) The qualities connected with others are six: 1) *gokula-prema-vasati* — the abode of Gokula's love; 2) *jagac-chreṇī-lasad-yaśāḥ* — whose fame shines through all the worlds; 3) *gurv-arpita-guru-snehā* — on whom the elders bestow great affection; 4) *sakhī-praṇayitā-vaśā* — subject to the love of Her friends; 5) *kṛṣṇa-priyāvalī-mukhyā* — foremost among Kṛṣṇa's beloveds; 6) *santatāśrava-keśavā* — to whom Keśava is always obedient.

## 175

***Prema-vaicittya***: “The pain that, by the very nature of love at its height, arises from the thought of separation even in the Beloved's presence is called *prema-vaicittya*.” By the nature of love at its height, even when She is near the Beloved, anguish (*ārti*) arises from fear of separation from Him: this is “*prema-vaicittya*,” and it is a jewel. ***Tarala*** — the gem in the middle of a necklace, a pendant (*dhukdhuki*).

## 176

Laying Her hand on a friend's shoulder is the very mood of a *kiśorī* in the middle of youth; and the friends near Her are Her thoughts of Kṛṣṇa's pastimes.

## 177

In the chamber of fragrance, Her own body; on the couch, or bed, of pride.

## 178–179

***Avataṁsa*** — a particular ear ornament. Kṛṣṇa's name, qualities and fame are the ornament of Her ears. The stream of words about Kṛṣṇa's name, qualities and fame is the flow of the honey of *śyāma-rasa*; that is what Śrīmatī gives Kṛṣṇa to drink.

## 165–179

The author shows in detail that Śrī Rādhā is the transformation (*vikāra*) of love (*praṇaya*) for Kṛṣṇa, that is, love for Kṛṣṇa embodied. Her feelings within, all the limbs of Her body, Her dress and the rest — every one of them is an adornment, a beauty, of love for Kṛṣṇa.

## 180

> Subheading: Śrī Rādhikā is the embodied ocean of love for Kṛṣṇa —

Śrīmatī Rādhikā is the mine of the jewel of pure love for Kṛṣṇa, that is, the embodied ocean of love for Kṛṣṇa; and Śrī Rādhikā's body is full of incomparable qualities. See Madhya 23.81–86.
