## 106–108

*Sthāṇu* is a tree without leaves. Seeing a leafless tree from a distance, one mistakenly thinks, “Some man is coming.” In the same way the people of Vraja fell into error: they took the fisherman's boat for Kāliya, the lamp on it for a jewel, and the fisherman catching fish for Kṛṣṇa.

## 112–113

Māyāvādī *sannyāsīs* call themselves “Brahman” and keep saying “Nārāyaṇa, Nārāyaṇa.” By smārta custom, everyone — householder brāhmaṇas and others — bows to such a *sannyāsī* on seeing him, taking him for Nārāyaṇa. To put an end to this mistaken custom, Mahāprabhu said that a *sannyāsī* is nothing more than a *jīva*. He can never be like the sun of Kṛṣṇa, full in all six opulences. He is only a particle of consciousness (*cit-kaṇa*); the *jīva* is therefore like a particle of a ray of the Kṛṣṇa-sun, and one should never bow to him as “Nārāyaṇa.”

## 114

The Lord (*Īśvara*) is always full of eternal being, consciousness and bliss, and is embraced by His *hlādinī* and *saṁvit* potencies. The *jīva*, however, is always covered by his own (superimposed) ignorance (*avidyā*), and is therefore a mine of manifold suffering.

## 116

One who sees Śrī Nārāyaṇa as “equal” to Brahmā, Rudra and the other gods is certainly a *pāṣaṇḍī*.

## 126

The characteristic by which a thing is known of itself (*svataḥ-siddha-lakṣaṇa*), without comparison with other things, is its *svarūpa-lakṣaṇa* (essential characteristic). The characteristic by which a thing becomes known through comparison with other things is called *taṭastha* (marginal). All the glories described above, as marginal characteristics, have established that You are “Vrajendra-nandana.” But the realization that arises the moment one sees You — “This is Vrajendra-nandana” — is Your *svarūpa-lakṣaṇa*; it is by this essential characteristic that You are recognized as “Kṛṣṇa.”

## 135

Akrūra-ghāṭa: this *ghāṭa* lies halfway between Vṛndāvana and Mathurā. Here Akrūra stopped his chariot and, taking Rāma and Kṛṣṇa with him, bathed in the Yamunā. While bathing, Akrūra saw “Vaikuṇṭha” in the water, and the people of Vraja saw Goloka in the water of this *ghāṭa*.

## 144

*Soro-kṣetre*: “Soro-kṣetra” is on the bank of the Gaṅgā nearest to Mathurā.

## 165

*Bāṭoyāra*: those who rob on the roads; *māri' ḍāriyāche*: they have killed.

## 170

*Abahi*: now.

## 174

*Ghoḍā-piḍā*: the horses and what is on their backs — saddles and so on.

## 182–190

*Sva-śāstra*: the Koran. Among the Muslims there is a community called the “Sufis.” Their view is the “featureless (*nirviśeṣa*) Brahman,” or *advaita-brahma-vāda*; their great saying (*mahā-vākya*) is *anal-ḥaqq*[^182-190-1]. There is no doubt that this Sufi doctrine arose from the doctrine of Śaṅkara. In your Muhammadan scripture, where it describes how Muhammad saw God in the seventh heaven, the complete form (*pūrṇa-vigraha*) of God is acknowledged.

## 194

Unless one serves that Lord with *ibādat* — that is, with prayer (*namāz*) five times a day and so on — the *jīva* does not attain the goal of life. Your scripture calls love (*prīti*) itself the goal of life. Therefore, after first establishing *karma*, *yoga*, *jñāna* and the rest, at the very end it refutes them and establishes the supremacy of the Lord's *ibādat*, that is, of service to Him.

## 199–200

The Muslim teacher (*mleccha-ācārya*), who wore black clothes like a *pīr*, said: “Ordinary scholars cannot understand the hidden meaning of our scripture. That is why people explain Allah only in terms of His ‘formless’ aspect (*nirākāra-bhāva*). Few know that His form, full of eternal being, consciousness and bliss, is the ultimate object of service.”

*Thus ends the eighteenth chapter of the “Amṛta-pravāha-bhāṣya.”*
