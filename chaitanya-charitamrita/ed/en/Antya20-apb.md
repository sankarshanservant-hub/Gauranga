# Amṛta-pravāha-bhāṣya on Antya-līlā 20

## 0

**Summary.** This chapter describes how Mahāprabhu spent His nights with Svarūpa and Rāmānanda, relishing the *Śikṣāṣṭaka* with eagerness (*utkaṇṭhā*), humility, anxiety, and other feelings. From time to time the Lord would read verses from *Śrī Gīta-govinda* (composed by Jayadeva), *Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam*, *Śrī Jagannātha-vallabha-nāṭaka* (composed by Śrī Rāmānanda Rāya), and *Śrī Karṇāmṛta* (composed by Śrī Bilvamaṅgala), and would become absorbed in divine feelings — this and more is told in this chapter. The author indicates that, having relished *rasa* in this way for twelve years, Śrīman Mahāprabhu concluded His pastimes at the age of forty-eight. He then gave a brief recapitulation of all that is described in the Antya-līlā and with that concluded the book.

## 1

Only fortunate people relish the lamentations (*vilāpa*) of Gauracandra, filled with the joy, jealousy, anxiety, humility, and anguish that *prema* evokes.

## 12

Śrī Kṛṣṇa-saṅkīrtana cleanses the mirror of the mind, extinguishes the great forest fire of worldly existence (*bhava*), and spreads moonlight for the white lilies (*kairava*) that are the welfare of the *jīvas*. It is the life of the bride Knowledge (*vidyā*); it swells the ocean of bliss and at every step gives a taste of the full nectar; it bathes the true self (*svarūpa*) of everyone in coolness[^12-1]. May Śrī Kṛṣṇa-saṅkīrtana be especially victorious!

## 16

O Bhagavān, Your name alone bestows every blessing on the *jīvas*, and so You have revealed Your many names — “Kṛṣṇa,” “Govinda,” and others. Into these names You have placed all Your potencies, and You have set no rules (no injunctions or restrictions) of time or anything else for remembering the name. O Lord, You have shown the *jīvas* such mercy and made Your name so easy to obtain. Yet my misfortune — offenses against the name (*nāmāparādha*) — does not let attachment (*anurāga*) arise in me even for Your easily obtained name.

## 28

This is the nature of *prema*: one who has entered into a true relationship with *prema* humbly thinks, “I have not even a trace of devotion to Kṛṣṇa.”

## 29

O Jagadīśa, I do not desire wealth, followers, or beautiful poetry[^29-1]. (In my heart I desire only this:) birth after birth may I have causeless devotion to You.

## 32

O son of Nanda, though I am Your eternal servant, through the fruits of my own karma I have fallen into the fearsome ocean of worldly existence. Be merciful: consider me like the dust on Your lotus feet.

## 36

O Lord, when will my eyes be adorned with streams of tears as I utter Your name? When will my voice falter as soon as I begin to speak, and my whole body be covered with horripilation?

## 39

O Govinda, not seeing You, I feel every moment (*nimeṣa*) to be a *yuga*; tears pour from My eyes like the rains; the whole world seems to Me almost empty.

## 47

Let Kṛṣṇa embrace this maidservant devoted to His feet and crush her in His arms, or, by not appearing, break My heart[^47-1]. He is a libertine (*lampaṭa-puruṣa*), and however He may deal with Me, He is no one else: He is the Lord of My life.

## 50

“His body and mind are in My power” — that is, in body and mind He is wholly submissive to Me.

## 57

It is said that the faithful wife of a certain brahmin afflicted with leprosy served a prostitute dear to her husband, to give her husband pleasure. And when the hour of her husband's death came, by the power of her faithfulness she stopped the movement of the sun, pleased the three deities — Brahmā, Viṣṇu, and Maheśvara — and brought her dead husband back to life. The point is this: firm faithfulness to Kṛṣṇa (*pātivratya*) is the *jīva*'s highest *dharma*, born of *śṛṅgāra-rasa*.

---

[^12-1]: The verse translation reads differently: “As the night lotus blossoms in the cool rays of the moon, so the heart begins to bloom in the nectar of this name. In the end the soul awakens to its inner treasure — a life of love for Kṛṣṇa.”

[^29-1]: The verse translation reads differently: “I do not desire wealth, followers, or beautiful women.”

[^47-1]: The verse translation reads differently: “Kṛṣṇa may embrace this maidservant who has fallen at His feet and call her His own” (the *Anubhāṣya* understands it the same way); the word *lampaṭa* (libertine) is rendered there as “in His willfulness.”
