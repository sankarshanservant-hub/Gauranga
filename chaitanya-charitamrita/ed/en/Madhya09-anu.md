## 11

> Subheading: Mādhvas and Śrī Vaiṣṇavas who worshiped Rāma begin to worship Kṛṣṇa —

**Tattvavādīs.** To distinguish the Vaiṣṇavas of Śrī Madhva from the Māyāvādīs of Śrī Śaṅkara, the Mādhva Vaiṣṇavas are called “Tattvavādīs.” The ācāryas of Tattvavāda refuted *nirviśeṣa-brahma-vāda*—the false reasoning of *kevala-advaita-vāda*—and established *bhagavat-tattva*, the truth of Bhagavān. The Mādhva Vaiṣṇavas are Brahma Vaiṣṇavas (they belong to the Brahma-sampradāya). Therefore they do not accept that their original guru, Brahmā, was bewildered (Tenth Canto), for in his commentary *Bhāgavata-tātparya* Śrīman Madhvācārya left out this “pastime of Brahmā's bewilderment.” Śrī Mādhavendra Purī was one of the Śrī Mādhva Vaiṣṇavas, and he preached *prema-bhakti*, the final goal of Tattvavāda. Although the Gauḍīya Vaiṣṇavas are Mādhvas, they did not receive the name “Tattvavādī.”

**Śrī Vaiṣṇavas.** The original guru of Śrī Rāmānuja's *sampradāya* is “Lakṣmī,” and so they are called “Śrī Vaiṣṇavas.”

The Tattvavādīs worship Śrī Kṛṣṇa and the Śrī Vaiṣṇavas worship Lakṣmī-Nārāyaṇa, yet in both a strong worship of Śrī Rāmacandra is seen.

A present-day writer of the Tattvavādī *sampradāya*, Śrī Padmanābhācārya, says: “In our principal Mādhva monasteries the Deities of Śrī Rāma and Sītā are especially worshiped. In the book *Adhyātma-rāmāyaṇa*, in chapters 12, 13, 14, and 15, the story of the original Deities of Śrī Rāma and Sītā is told as follows. A brāhmaṇa vowed not to eat any food unless he had seen Śrī Rāmacandra. Once, because of affairs of state, Śrī Rāmacandra could not come out before His subjects for a whole week. So the brāhmaṇa, steadfast in his vow to see Rāma, did not take even a drop of water for that week. At last, after eight days, on the ninth day, the brāhmaṇa was brought before Śrī Rāma and received His *darśana*. Hearing of the brāhmaṇa's steadfastness, Śrī Rāmacandra told Lakṣmaṇa, ‘Give this true devotee brāhmaṇa the pair of Rāma and Sītā Deities kept in My own house.’ The brāhmaṇa received Them from Lakṣmaṇa and served these two Deities all his life, and before leaving this world he gave Them to Śrī Hanumān. Śrī Hanumān served these two Deities for a long time, holding Them to his heart. Long afterward Bhīmasena came to Gandhamādana mountain, and when Hanumān took leave of him, he gave him these two Deities. Bhīma kept Them in the royal palace. Up to the time of the last king of that dynasty, ‘Kṣemakānta,’ these two Deities were served in the royal palace. Then They came into the possession of the Gajapati kings of Utkala and were kept in their royal treasury. Śrī Madhvācārya permitted his disciple Śrī Narahari Tīrthapāda to take the original Rāma and Sītā Deities from the royal treasury and serve Them. From the time of King Ikṣvāku these Rāma and Sītā Deities had been kept in the palace of the kings of the solar dynasty, and even before Rāmacandra's birth Daśaratha served Them. Later, while Lakṣmaṇa was serving Them, by Rāmacandra's order They were given to the brāhmaṇa mentioned above.” Three months and sixteen days before his departure Śrī Madhva obtained these two Deities and installed Them in the Uttara-rāḍhī Maṭha, the principal monastery of the village of Uḍupī. Since then the ācāryas of Śrī Madhva have been Their custodians.

In Rāmānuja's *sampradāya* the path of “Śrī Rāmāyaṇa-guru-karaṇa” (accepting a guru according to the Śrī Rāmāyaṇa) is current.[^11-1] In Tirupati and elsewhere the Rāmānujīyas worship the Deity of Śrī Rāma. In the “Rāmānandī,” “Jamāyet,” or “Rāmāt” *sampradāya*, which came out of Rāmānuja's *sampradāya*, the worship of Śrī Rāma and Sītā is firmly established. The Rāmānujīyas are more devoted to Rāma than to Kṛṣṇa.

## 14

> Subheading: Gautamī-gaṅgā —

**Gautamī-gaṅgā** is one of the branches of the Godāvarī. On the far bank, opposite Rājamahendrī, was the *āśrama* of the sage Gautama, and so the Godāvarī is called Gautamī-gaṅgā.

## 15

> Subheading: At Mallikārjuna-tīrtha the Lord sees Śambhu, the servant of Rāma —

**Mallikārjuna** is Śrīśailam. It lies 70 miles downstream from Kurnool, on the southern bank of the river Kṛṣṇā. In the center of a walled courtyard stands the temple of the chief deity, Śiva “Mallikārjuna.” This Śiva-liṅga is renowned as one of the *jyotir-liṅgas* (*Kurnool Manual*).

> Subheading: *Darśana* of the Deities of Rāma and Sītā at Siddhavaṭa —

**Siddhavaṭa** lies 10 miles east of the town of Cuddapah. It is known by the name “Sidhout,” and at some time in the past it was also known as “Southern Kāśī.” The name comes from *āśrama-vaṭa-vṛkṣa*, the banyan tree of the *āśrama* (*Cuddapah Manual*).

## 29

> Subheading: The etymological meaning of the word “Rāma” —

**yoginaḥ** (those detached from sense objects) **anante** (in what is beyond matter) **satyānande** **cid-ātmani** (in *sac-cid-ānanda*) **ramante**. **iti** (therefore) **rāma-padena** **asau** (Rāmacandra) **paraṁ brahma** **abhidhīyate** (is called).

## 30

> Subheading: The etymological meaning of the word “Kṛṣṇa” —

**kṛṣiḥ** **śabdaḥ** **bhū-vācakaḥ** (which determines existence), **ṇaḥ ca** **nirvṛti-vācakaḥ** (which means bliss); **tayoḥ** (of the two) **aikyam** **kṛṣṇaḥ** **paraṁ brahma** **iti** **abhidhīyate** (is called).
