## 11

> Subheading: Mādhvas and Śrī Vaiṣṇavas who worshiped Rāma begin to worship Kṛṣṇa —

**Tattvavādīs.** To distinguish the Vaiṣṇavas of Śrī Madhva from the Māyāvādīs of Śrī Śaṅkara, the Mādhva Vaiṣṇavas are called “Tattvavādīs.” The ācāryas of Tattvavāda refuted *nirviśeṣa-brahma-vāda*—the false reasoning of *kevala-advaita-vāda*—and established *bhagavat-tattva*, the truth of Bhagavān. The Mādhva Vaiṣṇavas are Brahma Vaiṣṇavas (they belong to the Brahma-sampradāya). Therefore they do not accept that their original guru, Brahmā, was bewildered (Tenth Canto), for in his commentary *Bhāgavata-tātparya* Śrīman Madhvācārya left out this “pastime of Brahmā's bewilderment.” Śrī Mādhavendra Purī was one of the Śrī Mādhva Vaiṣṇavas, and he preached *prema-bhakti*, the final goal of Tattvavāda. Although the Gauḍīya Vaiṣṇavas are Mādhvas, they did not receive the name “Tattvavādī.”

**Śrī Vaiṣṇavas.** The original guru of Śrī Rāmānuja's *sampradāya* is “Lakṣmī,” and so they are called “Śrī Vaiṣṇavas.”

The Tattvavādīs worship Śrī Kṛṣṇa and the Śrī Vaiṣṇavas worship Lakṣmī-Nārāyaṇa, yet in both a strong worship of Śrī Rāmacandra is seen.

A present-day writer of the Tattvavādī *sampradāya*, Śrī Padmanābhācārya, says: “In our principal Mādhva monasteries the Deities of Śrī Rāma and Sītā are especially worshiped. In the book *Adhyātma-rāmāyaṇa*, in chapters 12, 13, 14, and 15, the story of the original Deities of Śrī Rāma and Sītā is told as follows. A brāhmaṇa vowed not to eat any food unless he had seen Śrī Rāmacandra. Once, because of affairs of state, Śrī Rāmacandra could not come out before His subjects for a whole week. So the brāhmaṇa, steadfast in his vow to see Rāma, did not take even a drop of water for that week. At last, after eight days, on the ninth day, the brāhmaṇa was brought before Śrī Rāma and received His *darśana*. Hearing of the brāhmaṇa's steadfastness, Śrī Rāmacandra told Lakṣmaṇa, ‘Give this true devotee brāhmaṇa the pair of Rāma and Sītā Deities kept in My own house.’ The brāhmaṇa received Them from Lakṣmaṇa and served these two Deities all his life, and before leaving this world he gave Them to Śrī Hanumān. Śrī Hanumān served these two Deities for a long time, holding Them to his heart. Long afterward Bhīmasena came to Gandhamādana mountain, and when Hanumān took leave of him, he gave him these two Deities. Bhīma kept Them in the royal palace. Up to the time of the last king of that dynasty, ‘Kṣemakānta,’ these two Deities were served in the royal palace. Then They came into the possession of the Gajapati kings of Utkala and were kept in their royal treasury. Śrī Madhvācārya permitted his disciple Śrī Narahari Tīrthapāda to take the original Rāma and Sītā Deities from the royal treasury and serve Them. From the time of King Ikṣvāku these Rāma and Sītā Deities had been kept in the palace of the kings of the solar dynasty, and even before Rāmacandra's birth Daśaratha served Them. Later, while Lakṣmaṇa was serving Them, by Rāmacandra's order They were given to the brāhmaṇa mentioned above.” Three months and sixteen days before his departure Śrī Madhva obtained these two Deities and installed Them in the Uttara-rāḍhī Maṭha, the principal monastery of the village of Uḍupī. Since then the ācāryas of Śrī Madhva have been Their custodians.

In Rāmānuja's *sampradāya* the path of “Śrī Rāmāyaṇa-guru-karaṇa” (accepting a guru according to the Śrī Rāmāyaṇa) is current.[^11-1] In Tirupati and elsewhere the Rāmānujīyas worship the Deity of Śrī Rāma. In the “Rāmānandī,” “Jamāyet,” or “Rāmāt” *sampradāya*, which came out of Rāmānuja's *sampradāya*, the worship of Śrī Rāma and Sītā is firmly established. The Rāmānujīyas are more devoted to Rāma than to Kṛṣṇa.

## 53

> Subheading: Under the name of “*mahā-prasāda*” they try to cheat the Lord with impure food —

A non-Vaiṣṇava may announce a thousand times, with a thousand voices, that the food he offers is “*mahā-prasāda*.” Outwardly there may be not the slightest flaw in the way he prepares and decorates the offering. But he has no service to Viṣṇu (*viṣṇu-dāsya*) and no spiritual vision; that is, he has turned away from Viṣṇu, and therefore Viṣṇu never accepts the food he offers. So a pure servant of the Vaiṣṇavas will regard it as “impure” (*amedhya*) and will never accept or eat it!

## 59–61

**All the Buddhists.** The Buddhists received from the Lord's holy lips initiation (*dīkṣā*) into Kṛṣṇa's name. After that they were no longer the Buddhists they had been, acting like *pāṣaṇḍīs*: they became “Vaiṣṇavas” and began to worship Viṣṇu, practicing the *dharma* inherent in the nature of the *jīva*. The guru delivers the disciple: he awakens consciousness in the unconscious disciple, inspires him to worship Viṣṇu, and engages him in that service. This is “*dīkṣā*,” or divine knowledge. Here, however, the former disciples of the unconscious Buddhist ācārya gained consciousness in Kṛṣṇa's name by the Lord's mercy, uttered Kṛṣṇa's name into the ear of their *guru-bruva*, and did the work of an ācārya. Outwardly, guru and disciples—the Buddhist ācārya and his disciples—exchanged places. In truth, however, the one who received Kṛṣṇa's mercy, gained consciousness, and utters Kṛṣṇa's name became the “guru” (the great one), and the unconscious person became the “*laghu*” (the small one), that is, his disciple. This is the teaching of the Lord, the guru of the whole world.

## 64

> Subheading: The Lord comes to Tirupati and Tirumalaya and sees Bālājīu —

Here the places the Lord visited are described almost exactly.

**Tirupati**, “Tirupatur,” is a famous holy place in the Chandragiri taluk of North Arcot. The hill is named after Veṅkaṭeśvara—Veṅkaṭagiri, or Veṅkaṭādri; on it, at 8 miles, stands the Viṣṇu Deity, the four-armed “Bālājī,” or Veṅkaṭeśvara, together with His two potencies, “Śrī” and “Bhū.” The place is also called “Veṅkaṭa-kṣetra.” In all South India it is one of the finest and richest temples. In the month of Āśvina a very large fair is held here. On the M. S. M. line there is a railway station, “Tirupati.” “Lower Tirupati” lies in the valley of Veṅkaṭācala; several temples stand there. Here are the Deities of Govindarāja and Rāmacandra. “Tirumalaya” is probably the ancient name of “Upper Tirupati.”

> Subheading: Mahāprabhu's *darśana* of Pānā-nṛsiṁha; absorbed in love, He bows down and offers prayers —

**Pānā-nṛsiṁha** (Panakal Narasiṁha) is a famous temple in the Kistna district, 7 miles from the town of Bezwada, on “Maṅgalagiri”; one reaches it by climbing 600 steps. Tradition says that when sherbet is offered to this Nṛsiṁhadeva, He accepts no more than half of it. A former Mahārāja of Tanjore gave this temple a conch said to have been used by Kṛṣṇa. In the month of March a very large fair is held here.

## 71

> Subheading: *Darśana* of Śambhu at Trikālahastī —

**Trimalaya** is in Tanjore, or Tauṇḍīra-maṇḍala.

**“Trikālahastī”** lies 21 miles northeast of Tirupati, on the southern bank of the river Suvarṇamukhī. It is also called “Śrī Kālahastī,” or in common speech “Kālahastī.” It is famed for the temple of “Śiva as the Vāyu-liṅga” (*North Arcot Manual*).

## 72

> Subheading: *Darśana* of Śiva at Pakṣī-tīrtha and of the Deity of Śveta-varāha at Vṛddhakola-tīrtha —

**Pakṣī-tīrtha**, “Tirukaḍikuṇḍam,” lies 9 miles southeast of Chingleput: a Śiva temple on a range of hills 500 feet above the plain. The hill is called Vedagiri, or Vedācalam, and the Deity Vedagirīśvara. Every day two hawks come and receive food from the attending *pūjārī*. Tradition says this has gone on from time immemorial (*Chingleput Manual*).

**Vṛddhakola** is a temple of the Deity Śrī Varāha, carved from a single stone. It stands about a mile south of “Balipīṭham,” in “Mahābalipuram,” or the “Seven Pagodas.” In this temple the serpent “Śeṣa” holds an umbrella over the Viṣṇu Deity in the form of Varāha.

## 73

> Subheading: *Darśana* of Śambhu Pītāmbara —

**Pītāmbara**, “Chidambaram,” lies 26 miles south of the town of “Cuddalore.” The Deity's name is Śiva “Ākāśa-liṅga.” This huge temple stands on a plot of 39 acres and is surrounded on all sides by a road 60 feet wide (*South Arcot Manual*).
