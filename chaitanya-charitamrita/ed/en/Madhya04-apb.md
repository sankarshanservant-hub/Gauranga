# Amṛta-pravāha-bhāṣya on Madhya-līlā 4

## 0

**Summary.** Śrīman Mahāprabhu went by the Chatrabhoga road, passed Vṛddha-mantreśvara, and entered the borders of the kingdom of Utkala. Along the way He joyfully sang *kīrtana* in many ways and gathered alms, and on reaching the village of Remuṇā He had *darśana* of Śrī Gopīnātha. In supreme bliss He told His devotees about Śrī Mādhavendra Purī, as He had heard it from Śrī Īśvara Purī. Long before, Śrī Mādhava Purī[^0-1] had gone to Vṛndāvana, and one night at Govardhana he dreamed: “Gopāla is in the forest.” The next morning, after this dream, he gathered the residents of Govardhana, brought the Deity of Śrī Gopāla out of the forest, and installed Him on top of the hill. Gopāla was worshipped with great pomp, and the great festival of Annakūṭa was held. As word of it gradually spread, many people came from the villages and held festivals for Gopāla. One night Gopāla gave Purī this command in a dream: “Go to Nīlācala at once, obtain *malayaja* sandalwood, anoint Me with it, and relieve My heat.” On receiving this command Purī Gosvāmī went through Gauḍa and reached the village of Remuṇā in Utkala. There he received the *kṣīra-prasāda* that Śrī Gopīnātha gave him, and he went on to Śrī Puruṣottama. Because Gopīnātha stole *kṣīra* and gave it to Mādhavendra Purī, He is called “Kṣīra-corā Gopīnātha.” Having reached Nīlācala, Purī obtained, through the servants of Śrī Jagannātha, one maund of sandalwood and twenty *tolās* of camphor (*śrī-karpūra*) from the king's officers, and with two porters he brought these as far as Remuṇā. Then Gopāla, the Holder of Govardhana, again commanded him in a dream: “Smear this sandalwood and camphor on the body of Gopīnātha, and My heat will go away.” Mādhavendra Purī obeyed this command and went back to Nīlācala. Mahāprabhu told this story to Śrī Nityānanda and the other devotees and praised Śrī Mādhavendra Purī's pure *prema-bhakti* at length. When He recited the verse Purī had composed, the madness of love (*premonmāda*) came over Him. Seeing the crowd, the Lord returned to outward consciousness. He honored the *prasāda* of *kṣīra* (sweet rice, *paramānna*), spent that night there, and the next day set out for Nīlācala.

## 1

To give him *kṣīra*, Śrī Gopīnātha stole a pot of *kṣīra* and received the name “Kṣīra-corā”; conquered by his devotion, Śrī Gopāladeva manifested Himself. To that Mādhavendra Purī I bow down.

## 4

***All these pastimes*** — see the *Śrī Caitanya-bhāgavata*, *Antya-khaṇḍa*, Chapters 2 and 3.

## 12

***Dānī*** — the ferrymen at the river crossings[^12-1].

## 13

***Remuṇā.*** Near Bāleśvara (five miles to the west) there is a village called Remuṇā. “Kṣīra-corā Gopīnātha” resides there.

## 20

***Mādhava Purī*** — Mādhavendra Purī.

## 26

***Bhoga-śoṣa*** — the desire for food.

## 33

***Bāṭa*** — road; a word of the Utkala language.

## 37

***Kāḍha*** — bring out; ***maṭha*** — a temple.

## 41

***Installed by Vajra.*** Vajra was the son of Aniruddha, Śrī Kṛṣṇa's grandson[^41-1]. The Pāṇḍavas brought him from Dvārakā and made him king in Mathurā. Śrī Gopāla, the Holder of Govardhana, is one of those Deities [that Vajra installed].

## 61

***Pañca-gavya*** — milk, yogurt, ghee, cow's urine and cow dung; ***pañcāmṛta*** — yogurt, milk, ghee, honey and sugar.
