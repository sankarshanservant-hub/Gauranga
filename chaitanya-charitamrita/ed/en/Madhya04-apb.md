# Amṛta-pravāha-bhāṣya on Madhya-līlā 4

## 0

**Summary.** Śrīman Mahāprabhu went by the Chatrabhoga road, passed Vṛddha-mantreśvara, and entered the borders of the kingdom of Utkala. Along the way He joyfully sang *kīrtana* in many ways and gathered alms, and on reaching the village of Remuṇā He had *darśana* of Śrī Gopīnātha. In supreme bliss He told His devotees about Śrī Mādhavendra Purī, as He had heard it from Śrī Īśvara Purī. Long before, Śrī Mādhava Purī[^0-1] had gone to Vṛndāvana, and one night at Govardhana he dreamed: “Gopāla is in the forest.” The next morning, after this dream, he gathered the residents of Govardhana, brought the Deity of Śrī Gopāla out of the forest, and installed Him on top of the hill. Gopāla was worshipped with great pomp, and the great festival of Annakūṭa was held. As word of it gradually spread, many people came from the villages and held festivals for Gopāla. One night Gopāla gave Purī this command in a dream: “Go to Nīlācala at once, obtain *malayaja* sandalwood, anoint Me with it, and relieve My heat.” On receiving this command Purī Gosvāmī went through Gauḍa and reached the village of Remuṇā in Utkala. There he received the *kṣīra-prasāda* that Śrī Gopīnātha gave him, and he went on to Śrī Puruṣottama. Because Gopīnātha stole *kṣīra* and gave it to Mādhavendra Purī, He is called “Kṣīra-corā Gopīnātha.” Having reached Nīlācala, Purī obtained, through the servants of Śrī Jagannātha, one maund of sandalwood and twenty *tolās* of camphor (*śrī-karpūra*) from the king's officers, and with two porters he brought these as far as Remuṇā. Then Gopāla, the Holder of Govardhana, again commanded him in a dream: “Smear this sandalwood and camphor on the body of Gopīnātha, and My heat will go away.” Mādhavendra Purī obeyed this command and went back to Nīlācala. Mahāprabhu told this story to Śrī Nityānanda and the other devotees and praised Śrī Mādhavendra Purī's pure *prema-bhakti* at length. When He recited the verse Purī had composed, the madness of love (*premonmāda*) came over Him. Seeing the crowd, the Lord returned to outward consciousness. He honored the *prasāda* of *kṣīra* (sweet rice, *paramānna*), spent that night there, and the next day set out for Nīlācala.

## 1

To give him *kṣīra*, Śrī Gopīnātha stole a pot of *kṣīra* and received the name “Kṣīra-corā”; conquered by his devotion, Śrī Gopāladeva manifested Himself. To that Mādhavendra Purī I bow down.

## 4

***All these pastimes*** — see the *Śrī Caitanya-bhāgavata*, *Antya-khaṇḍa*, Chapters 2 and 3.

## 12

***Dānī*** — the ferrymen at the river crossings[^12-1].

## 13

***Remuṇā.*** Near Bāleśvara (five miles to the west) there is a village called Remuṇā. “Kṣīra-corā Gopīnātha” resides there.

## 20

***Mādhava Purī*** — Mādhavendra Purī.

## 26

***Bhoga-śoṣa*** — the desire for food.

## 33

***Bāṭa*** — road; a word of the Utkala language.

## 37

***Kāḍha*** — bring out; ***maṭha*** — a temple.

## 41

***Installed by Vajra.*** Vajra was the son of Aniruddha, Śrī Kṛṣṇa's grandson[^41-1]. The Pāṇḍavas brought him from Dvārakā and made him king in Mathurā. Śrī Gopāla, the Holder of Govardhana, is one of those Deities [that Vajra installed].

## 61

***Pañca-gavya*** — milk, yogurt, ghee, cow's urine and cow dung; ***pañcāmṛta*** — yogurt, milk, ghee, honey and sugar.

## 62

***Śaṅkha-gandhodaka.*** *Śaṅkhodaka* is water kept in a conch; *gandhodaka* is water made fragrant with flowers and sandalwood.

## 74

***Māṭhā*** — buttermilk (*ghola*). ***Śikhariṇī*** is prepared by mixing five ingredients: yogurt, milk, sugar, camphor and black pepper. ***Mathani*** — fresh butter (*navanīta*) and *haiyaṅgava*.

## 80

***Viḍaka*** — rolls of betel (*pān*); ***sañcaya*** — a store.

## 86

In the Dvāpara-yuga the cowherds of Vraja used to worship Indra. Śrī Kṛṣṇa stopped that worship and arranged instead for Govardhana Hill to be worshipped and fed (Girirāja) with an *annakūṭa*. Enraged, Indra poured rain for seven days, trying to destroy Gokula. Śrī Kṛṣṇa held Govardhana Hill on His little finger like an umbrella against the rain and so protected Gokula. Mādhavendra Purī held an *annakūṭa* as great as the one held at that worship of Govardhana.

## 113

***Jagamohana*** is the name of the hall in front of the temple from which one has *darśana* of Bhagavān. ***Kyā kyā*** — in another reading, *kāhā kāhā*. The sense is *keyā keyā* (“what, what”) — what *bhoga* is offered.

## 117

***Kṣīra*** — *paramānna*.

## 146

The fame created by the Creator (*vidhātā*) comes precisely to one who does good without desiring fame. But one who does good deeds in the hope of fame does not attain fame — this is the secret of fame.

## 151–152

***Karpūra*** — *śrī-karpūra*, with which *ārātrika* is performed for Śrī Jagannāthadeva. The servants of Jagannātha obtained this *śrī-karpūra* and *malayaja* sandalwood from the king's officers. They sent one brāhmaṇa and one servant with Purī Gosāñi, and gave them money for the journey.

## 153

***Ghāṭī*** — the *ghāṭoyāls*, who collect the road tolls. ***Dānī*** — those who take the fare for crossing a river. So that all of these would let him pass — that is, so that Purī Gosāñi could go on without paying them — a royal letter (*rāja-lekhā*), that is, a *paravānā*, was placed in his hand through the king's officer.

## 166

***These two*** — those who had come with Purī.

## 176–177

***In the land of the mlecchas.*** A large part of the Medinīpura district belonged to the kingdom of the kings of Utkala: that was the land of a Hindu king. Beyond it almost the whole country was under a *mleccha* king. Here and there the *mleccha* king's agents would seize good things from travelers if they carried any. In Gauḍa such camphor and sandalwood are rare. Fearing such trouble, Purī Gosāñi would have found the journey all the way to Vṛndāvana very hard, and to spare him that hardship [Gopāla] allowed him to offer the sandalwood to Śrī Gopīnātha in Remuṇā.

## 181

***Bhoke rahe*** — remains hungry.

## 184

***Jagāti*** — the *jagāitas*, those who stay awake on the roads in the guise of watchmen.

## 185

***Baṭa*** — a cowrie, a small coin (*kapardaka*).

## 195

***Cauṭha-jana*** — a fourth person. That is, only three relished this verse — Rādhā Ṭhākurāṇī, Mādhavendra Purī and Mahāprabhu; no fourth person was fit to relish it.

## 197

O Lord whose heart melts with compassion for the wretched! O Lord of Mathurā! When shall I see You? Because I do not see You, my afflicted heart has lost all peace! O beloved, what shall I do now?

***Purport.*** The Vaiṣṇavas who proclaim pure devotion and base themselves on Vedānta are divided into four *sampradāyas*. Of these, Śrī Mādhavendra Purī accepted the *sampradāya* of Śrī Madhvācārya and took Vaiṣṇava *sannyāsa*. In that *sampradāya*, from Madhvācārya down to Lakṣmīpati, Mādhavendra's guru, there was no devotion imbued with *śṛṅgāra-rasa*. What kind of devotion they had can be seen from the debate Mahāprabhu held with the Tattvavādīs during His travels in the South. By composing this incomparable verse Śrī Mādhavendra Purī sowed the seed of devotion imbued with *śṛṅgāra-rasa*. Its mood is this: the highest worship of Kṛṣṇa follows the feeling, the surge of great love (*mahā-prema*), that arose in Śrīmatī Rādhikā in separation from Śrī Kṛṣṇa when He had gained the kingdom of Mathurā. A devotee of this *rasa*, thinking himself utterly wretched (*dīna*), will call in this mood to the Lord whose heart melts with compassion for the wretched. For the *jīva*, the natural worship is the mood of separation from Kṛṣṇa. Kṛṣṇa has gone to Mathurā. Not seeing Him, Śrīmatī's heart is in deep distress, and longing to see Him She says: “O beloved (*kānta*), without Your *darśana* My heart is in turmoil. Tell Me, what must I do to see You? Knowing that I am wretched, melt with compassion.” This mood of Śrī Mādhavendra Purī is easily recognized in the variety of feelings (*bhāva-vaicitrya*) of Śrīmatī on meeting Uddhava, which is described as manifest in Śrī Mahāprabhu. Therefore the *mahājanas* say that the root of the tree of *śṛṅgāra-rasa* is Mādhavendra Purī, Īśvara Purī is its sprout, Śrīman Mahāprabhu is its trunk, and the devotees who follow the Lord are its branches and twigs.

*Thus ends the fourth chapter of the “Amṛta-pravāha-bhāṣya.”*
