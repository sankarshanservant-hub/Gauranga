## 8

> Subheading: (1 — a, b, c, d) The branch of the four brothers, Śrīvāsa, Śrīrāma and the others —

Śrīvāsa. In the *Gaura-gaṇoddeśa* (verse 90): “Wise Śrīvāsa Paṇḍita is he who was formerly the sage Nārada. The best of sages named Parvata, who was dear to Nārada, is the glorious Śrīrāma Paṇḍita, his younger brother.” “She who was formerly in Vraja the nurse named Ambikā, who suckled [Kṛṣṇa], is considered to be Śrīvāsa's wife, named Mālinī.” The daughter of Śrīvāsa's own brother was Nārāyaṇī Devī, the mother of Ṭhākura Vṛndāvana.

After Śrīman Mahāprabhu accepted *sannyāsa*, Śrīvāsa left his home in Navadvīpa and settled in Kumārahaṭṭa. This is known from the *Caitanya-bhāgavata* (Antya, Chapter 5) and other books.

## 12

> Subheading: (2) The branch of Śrī Candraśekhara —

## 13

Śrī Candraśekhara is one of the glorious nine *nidhis* (treasures), or else Candra (?). It was in his house that Mahāprabhu danced, enacting the role of the Goddess (*Caitanya-bhāgavata*, Madhya, Chapter 18). This house of Candraśekhara is now well known by the name “Vrajapattana.” He learned of Śrīman Mahāprabhu's *sannyāsa* beforehand from Śrī Nityānanda's own lips (*Caitanya-bhāgavata*, Madhya, Chapter 26). At the time of the *sannyāsa* he was at Kāṭoyā with Śrī Nityānanda and Mukunda Datta and performed all the duties proper to the occasion. Returning to Navadvīpa, he told everyone that the Lord had accepted *sannyāsa*. On Mahāprabhu's *kīrtana* in his house, see the *Caitanya-bhāgavata*, Madhya, Chapter 8. On his presence in the town *kīrtana* when the Kazi was subdued, and when the Lord showed mercy to Śrīdhara, see Madhya, Chapter 23. He used to travel with the devotees from Gauḍa to Śrī Kṣetra.

## 14

> Subheading: (3) The branch of Śrī Puṇḍarīka —

Śrī Puṇḍarīka Vidyānidhi. In the *Gaura-gaṇoddeśa* (verse 54): “He who was formerly known in Vraja-maṇḍala as Vṛṣabhānu is now Puṇḍarīkākṣa Vidyānidhi Mahāśaya. Tasting His own mood and tormented by Rādhā's separation, Caitanya Himself called Puṇḍarīkākṣa, ‘O father!’ Wise Gaura gave him the fame of ‘Premanidhi’ (treasury of *prema*), and because he was a disciple of Mādhavendra, He always showed him reverence. His wife Ratnāvatī is declared by the wise to be Kīrtidā.”

His father's name was Bāṇeśvara (by another account, Śuklāmbara Brahmacārī), and his mother's name was Gaṅgādevī. By another account, Bāṇeśvara was born in the line of Śivarāma Gaṅgopādhyāya. Vidyānidhi's father was a brāhmaṇa of the Vārendra class who lived in the village of Bāghiyā in Ḍhākā district, and so the Rāḍhīya brāhmaṇa community there did not accept him. For this reason his descendants[^14-1] became *ekaghare* — outcasts from the community — and since then have served as priests only for the community's other outcasts. One of them, named Sarojānanda Gosvāmī, now lives in Vṛndāvana. Their family has one peculiarity: among brothers only one has a son, while the others have daughters or no children at all. That is why the family has not spread very far.

Six *krośas* north of Caṭṭagrāma there is a police station called Hāṭ-Hājārī. One *krośa* east of it, in the village of Mekhalā, was his family's former home. From the town of Caṭṭagrāma one can go there by land, in a horse-cart or bullock-cart, or by water, by boat or steamer. The steamer stops at Annapūrṇā's *ghāṭa*, and from there the *śrīpāṭa* (holy site) lies two miles to the southwest. Vidyānidhi's temple of worship (*bhajana-mandira*) is now utterly decayed and neglected; unless it is restored, it is likely to vanish soon. Two verses are carved on brick tablets in the temple wall, but the letters are worn away, and they can be neither read nor understood. Some 400–500 cubits southeast of this temple another temple can be seen; the inscriptions on the tablets of its walls cannot be read either. Directly in front of it, 15–20 cubits to the north, there once stood another temple: the many scattered pieces of brick show this. Tradition says it was Mukunda Datta's temple of worship.

Mahāprabhu called Vidyānidhi “Father” and gave him the name “Premanidhi.” He was the guru of Śrīla Gadādhara Paṇḍita Gosvāmī and the friend of Śrī Dāmodara Svarūpa. To warn ignorant *jīvas* and give them auspicious instruction, Paṇḍita Gosvāmī at first acted as if he had mistaken Vidyānidhi for a worldly man, and in the end displayed the pastime of taking initiation from him. On how Śrī Jagannāthadeva slapped him on the cheeks, see the *Caitanya-bhāgavata*, Antya, Chapter 11.

In Puṇḍarīka's family Śrī Harakumāra Smṛtitīrtha and Śrī Kṛṣṇakiṅkara Vidyālaṅkāra are now living (see the *Vaiṣṇava-mañjuṣā*, issue 1).

## 15

> Subheading: (4) The branch of Śrī Gadādhara —

Gadādhara Paṇḍita Gosāñi (*Gaura-gaṇoddeśa*, verses 147–153): “She who was formerly the Queen of Vṛndāvana, the embodiment of Śrī Rādhā's *prema*, is now Śrī Gadādhara, the beloved of Gaura, who bears the name ‘Paṇḍita.’ Śrī Svarūpa has determined that he is the Lakṣmī of Vraja: ‘She who formerly in Vṛndāvana was Lakṣmī, the beloved of Śyāmasundara, is now the Lakṣmī of Gaura's *prema*, Śrī Gadādhara Paṇḍita.’ Lalitā follows Rādhā and is therefore also called Anurādhikā; so she too entered him, as described in the *Gauracandrodaya*.”

For the branch of Gadādhara, see the end of Ādi, Chapter 12.

## 17

> Subheading: (5) The glory and the branch of Śrī Vakreśvara —

Śrī Vakreśvara Paṇḍita. In the *Gaura-gaṇoddeśa* (verse 71): “He who was Aniruddha, the fourth *vyūha*, is Vakreśvara Paṇḍita. With his dancing, born of absorption in Kṛṣṇa, he gave the Lord joy. ‘O merciful one, give me a thousand singers!’ — these sweet words he spoke at Caitanya's feet. Śaśirekhā entered him as a distinct manifestation of herself.”

Śrī Dhyānacandra Gosvāmī Prabhu writes: “I worship Śrī Vakreśvara Paṇḍita, the best of the twice-born, the devotee of Caitanya. He is devoted to revealing the *rasa* of Rādhā and Kṛṣṇa, adorned with garlands of songs, born to spread the happiness of Vṛndāvana, and endowed with *stambha* and the other *bhāvas*; he is the sprout of the avatāra of dance that merges with the *rasa* of Śrī Gaurāṅga Mahāprabhu. Within him Tuṅgavidyā always dwells, full of eager longing: she has become a *vipralabdhā* (a heroine disappointed in her waiting) and is ever filled with love for Śrī Kṛṣṇa. Her age is the measure (?).[^17-1] In Gaura's *rasa*, in the Kali age, she again became famous as Vakreśvara.”

He used to dance in Mahāprabhu's *kīrtana* in Śrīvāsa's courtyard and in Candraśekhara's house. On how the Lord told Devānanda of Vakreśvara's glory, see the *Caitanya-bhāgavata*, Antya, Chapter 3.

The Utkala poet Śrī Govinda writes of Śrī Vakreśvara in the *Śrī Gaura-kṛṣṇodaya*: “Considering that he was the Lord's first disciple, they settled Vakreśvara in that hermitage and each went to his own dwelling.”

His disciple was Śrī Gopāla-guru, and Gopāla-guru's disciple was Śrī Dhyānacandra. In Utkala most of those in the line of Śrī Vakreśvara's disciples call themselves Gauḍīya Vaiṣṇavas.

## 21

> Subheading: (6) The greatness of Śrī Jagadānanda —

Jagadānanda. In the *Gaura-gaṇoddeśa* (verse 51): “By a certain inner distinction the Sātvatas set him apart: Jagadānanda Paṇḍita is also a manifestation of Satyabhāmā.” He was the Lord's companion in *kīrtana* in Śrīvāsa's courtyard and in Candraśekhara's house. When the Lord went to Orissa after His *sannyāsa*, he carried His *daṇḍa* and begged alms.

## 24

> Subheading: (7) The branch of Śrī Rāghava Paṇḍita —

Rāghava Paṇḍita. In the *Gaura-gaṇoddeśa* (verse 44): “Dhaniṣṭhā, who in Vraja gave Kṛṣṇa countless foodstuffs, is now Rāghava Paṇḍita, dear to Gaurāṅga.”

Rāghava's house is in the village of Pāṇihāṭī on the bank of the Gaṅgā: on the E.B.R. line from Śiyāladaha station to Sodapura station, and from there one mile west. Over Rāghava Paṇḍita's *samādhi* a high platform has been built, surrounded by a bower of creepers. North of the *samādhi*, in a nearly ruined, dilapidated house, resides the Deity Śrī Madana-mohana, who is served without proper care. This service is managed under the supervision of the present zamindar of Pāṇihāṭī, Śrī Śivacandra Rāya Caudhurī.

Makaradhvaja. In the *Gaura-gaṇoddeśa* (verse 141): “He who was formerly the actor Candramukha is Makaradhvaja Kara.” He lived in the village of Pāṇihāṭī.

## 31–32

Dāmodara Paṇḍita. In the *Gaura-gaṇoddeśa* (verse 169): “The fierce Śaibyā who was in Vraja is Dāmodara Paṇḍita. For some purpose the goddess Sarasvatī entered him.” By the Lord's order Dāmodara would come to Gauḍa to see Āi (Śacīmātā), and then, before the Ratha-yātrā, go again with the devotees to Puruṣottama (*Caitanya-bhāgavata*, Antya, Chapter 9). For the Lord's question about Śacīdevī's devotion to Kṛṣṇa and Dāmodara Paṇḍita's answer, see the *Caitanya-bhāgavata*, Antya, Chapter 10. For Dāmodara's verbal chastisement of the Lord, see Antya-līlā, Chapter 3.

## 33

> Subheading: (11) The greatness and the branch of Śrī Śaṅkara Paṇḍita —

Śaṅkara Paṇḍita. In the *Gaura-gaṇoddeśa* (verse 157): “She on whose breast Kṛṣṇa formerly slept in Vṛndāvana, Śrī Bhadrā, is now Śaṅkara Paṇḍita, dear to Gaurāṅga.” See Antya 19.67–74.

Mahāprabhu's love for Paṇḍita Dāmodara was mixed with reverence, while for his younger brother, Paṇḍita Śaṅkara, He had pure (*kevala*) *prema*: see Madhya 11.146–148.

## 34

> Subheading: (12) Śrī Sadāśiva Paṇḍita —

Sadāśiva Paṇḍita. *Caitanya-bhāgavata*, Antya, Chapter 9 (at the time of the Ratha-yātrā): “Sadāśiva Paṇḍita, pure of mind, set out — he in whose house Nityānanda formerly lived.”

He lived in Navadvīpa and was the Lord's companion in *kīrtana*. On returning from Gayā, Mahāprabhu told him and other devotees, in Śuklāmbara's house, of His own worship of Kṛṣṇa. When Mahāprabhu was going to dance in Candraśekhara's house in the guise of Lakṣmī, He told Sadāśiva to prepare the costumes and everything else for the play (*Caitanya-bhāgavata*, Madhya, Chapter 18).

## 35

> Subheading: (13) Śrī Pradyumna Brahmacārī —

Pradyumna Brahmacārī. Antya, Chapter 2: “His own name was Pradyumna Brahmacārī; Gaura, the abode [of all], named him ‘Nṛsiṁhānanda.’” (CC Antya 2.53) Coming from Rāghava's house in Pāṇihāṭī to Śivānanda's house in Kumārahaṭṭa, Mahāprabhu appeared within his heart and accepted the offerings meant for three — Jagannātha, Nṛsiṁha and Himself (CC Antya 2.48–78). When he heard in Kuliyā that Mahāprabhu was going to Vṛndāvana, he became absorbed in meditation and in his mind built a road from Kuliyā to Vṛndāvana. Then his meditation broke, and he told the devotees that this time the Lord would go only as far as Kānāi Nāṭaśālā and would not go to Vṛndāvana (Madhya 1.155–162).[^35-1]

In the *Gaura-gaṇoddeśa* (verse 74): “*Āveśa* should likewise be recognized in the Miśra named Pradyumna.” *Caitanya-bhāgavata*, Antya, Chapter 3: “… in whose body Nṛsiṁha is manifest”; and Antya, Chapter 9: “… with whom Nṛsiṁha Himself converses face to face.”

## 36

> Subheading: (14) Śrī Nārāyaṇa Paṇḍita —

Nārāyaṇa Paṇḍita went to Nīlācala with Śrīrāma Paṇḍita, the brother of Śrīvāsa, to see Śrī Mahāprabhu. See the *Caitanya-bhāgavata*, Antya, Chapter 9, verse 93.

## 37

> Subheading: (15) The branch of Śrī Śrīmān Paṇḍita —

Śrīmān Paṇḍita lived in Śrī Navadvīpa and was the Lord's companion in His first *kīrtana*. On the day of the play, when the Lord danced in the mood of the Goddess, and whenever the Lord danced, he held a lighted torch. *Caitanya-bhāgavata*, Madhya, Chapter 18: “In the guise of Ādyā-śakti the Lord, Gaura the lion, dances, and all the bees at His feet joyfully watch … Paṇḍita Śrīmān holds the torch before Him.”

## 38

> Subheading: (16) Śrī Śuklāmbara Brahmacārī —

Śuklāmbara Brahmacārī lived in Śrī Navadvīpa and was the Lord's companion in His first *kīrtana*. On returning from Gayā, Mahāprabhu gathered the devotees in his very house and asked to hear the narrations of Kṛṣṇa from him (*Caitanya-bhāgavata*, Madhya, Chapter 1). In His Navadvīpa pastimes Mahāprabhu, in great joy, would snatch and eat the rice cooked from the grain Śuklāmbara had collected as alms (*Caitanya-bhāgavata*, Madhya, Chapters 16 and 25). In the *Gaura-gaṇoddeśa* (verse 191): “Śuklāmbara Brahmacārī was formerly one of the wives of the sacrificing brāhmaṇas; the Lord, Śrī Gaurāṅga, begged his food and ate it. Some say that this *brahmacārī* was formerly a brāhmaṇa who performed sacrifice.”

## 39

> Subheading: (17) The branch of Śrī Nandana Ācārya —

Nandana Ācārya lived in Navadvīpa and was the Lord's companion in *kīrtana*. After travelling to many holy places in the dress of an *avadhūta*, Śrī Nityānanda Prabhu came first to his house. There Mahāprabhu met Him together with the devotees. On the day of the Mahā-prakāśa, Mahāprabhu sent Rāmāi Paṇḍita to bring Advaita Prabhu. Advaitācārya was hiding in Nandanācārya's house, but Gaurasundara, who knows every heart, came to know of it. Śrī Mahāprabhu too once hid in his house. See the *Caitanya-bhāgavata*, Madhya, Chapters 6 and 17.

## 40

> Subheading: (18) The branch of Śrī Mukunda Datta —

Śrī Mukunda Datta was born in the village of Chanharā, under the Paṭiyā police station in Caṭṭagrāma district, ten *krośas* from Mekhalā, the village of Vidyānidhi's *śrīpāṭa*. In the *Gaura-gaṇoddeśa* (verse 140): “Madhukaṇṭha and Madhuvrata, the two singers in Vraja, are Mukunda and Vāsudeva, the two Dattas, the singers of Gaurāṅga.”

In His student days Nimāi used to argue with His classmate Mukunda, posing trick questions of logic (*nyāya*) (*Caitanya-bhāgavata*, Ādi, Chapters 5 and 8). When the Lord returned from Gayā, mad with love for Kṛṣṇa, Mukunda would delight Him by reciting verses of the *Bhāgavatam*. It was through his efforts that his companion Śrī Gadādhara Paṇḍita Gosvāmī took initiation from Śrī Vidyānidhi (Madhya, Chapter 7). When he sang *kīrtana* in Śrīvāsa's courtyard, Mahāprabhu would dance and sing. During the *bhāva* that lasted seven *praharas* (“*sāt-prahariyā*”), he sang the “Abhiṣeka.” For the Lord's punishment of Mukunda and His mercy to him, see the *Caitanya-bhāgavata*, Madhya, Chapter 10. When the Lord danced in Candraśekhara's house in the guise of Lakṣmī, it was he who first began to sing.

After telling Nityānanda Prabhu that He would take *sannyāsa*, the Lord went to Mukunda's house. Hearing everything, Mukunda begged the Lord to continue His *kīrtana* pastimes in Navadvīpa a few days longer (*Caitanya-bhāgavata*, Madhya, Chapter 25). When Mukunda learned of the Lord's *sannyāsa* from Nityānanda's lips, he went to Kāṭoyā with Gadādhara and Candraśekhara. There he sang *kīrtana* and performed everything proper to the Lord's *sannyāsa*. After the *sannyāsa* he followed the Lord together with Nitāi, Gadādhara and Govinda (Madhya, Chapter 26; Antya, Chapter 1), and in this way followed Him all the way to Puruṣottama (see Antya, Chapter 2). On the way to Jaleśvara he was present when Nityānanda broke the Lord's *daṇḍa*, and he reached Jaleśvara shortly after the Lord. Every year he came with the devotees from Gauḍa to Nīlācala to see the Lord.

## 41

> Subheading: (19) The virtues of Śrī Vāsudeva Datta Ṭhākura —

Śrī Vāsudeva Datta was born in Caṭṭagrāma; he was the brother of Mukunda Datta. *Caitanya-bhāgavata*, Antya, Chapter 9: “… to whom Kṛṣṇa Himself is sold.” When the Lord was staying at Śrīvāsa's house in Kumārahaṭṭa (*Caitanya-bhāgavata*, Antya, Chapter 5): “Such was the Lord's love for Datta! The Lord says: ‘I certainly belong to Vāsudeva … This body of Mine belongs to Vāsudeva Datta. Wherever Datta sells Me, there I am sold. It is true, true — it cannot be otherwise. I speak the truth; hear, O assembly of Vaiṣṇavas: this body of Mine belongs to Vāsudeva alone.’”

Yadunandana Ācārya, who received his mercy, was the *dīkṣā-guru* of Śrī Raghunātha dāsa Gosvāmī (Antya 6.161). Seeing his tendency to spend lavishly, the Lord ordered Śivānanda Sena to become his *sarakhela* (manager) and to manage his expenses (Madhya 15.93–96). For his prayer to Mahāprabhu on seeing the suffering of the *jīvas*, see Madhya 15.159–180.

On the E.I.R. line, one mile from Pūrvasthalī station, in the village of Māmagāchi, the birthplace of Ṭhākura Vṛndāvana, the Śrī Madana-Gopāla he established still resides. His service is very much neglected. It is desirable that His service be made splendid.

## 43

> Subheading: (20) The virtues of Ṭhākura Haridāsa, the Nāmācārya, and his branch —

## 43–47

Śrī Haridāsa Ṭhākura. *Caitanya-bhāgavata*, Ādi, Chapter 2: “Haridāsa descended in Būṛhana … After staying there some days, he came to the bank of the Gaṅgā and settled in Phuliyā, near Śāntipura.” The persecution by the Muslims is described in the *Caitanya-bhāgavata*, Chapter 11. On Haridāsa's humble words and the Lord's mercy, see the *Caitanya-bhāgavata*, Madhya, Chapter 10; on preaching the holy name from door to door, Madhya, Chapter 13; on Haridāsa playing the *koṭāla* (chief of the watch) during the play in Candraśekhara's house, the *Caitanya-bhāgavata*, Madhya, Chapter 18; on his worship of the holy name of Hari at Benāpola and his test, the *Caitanya-caritāmṛta*, Antya, Chapter 3; and on Haridāsa's departure, Antya, Chapter 11.

In the Sātakṣīrā subdivision of the Twenty-four Parganas (now Khulnā district) there is a *paragaṇā* called Būṛhana. Whether the Ṭhākura appeared there is not known.

## 48

> Subheading: (20a) Haridāsa's disciples, Satyarāja Khān (Vasu) and others —

Satyarāja Khān was the son of Guṇarāja Khān of Kulīnagrāma and the father of Rāmānanda Vasu. Ṭhākura Haridāsa lived in Kulīnagrāma during Cāturmāsya, performing his worship, and bestowed his mercy on the Vasu family. On Mahāprabhu's merciful order to bring silk ropes (*paṭṭa-ḍorī*) for Jagannāthadeva every year, see Madhya, Chapter 14. On how Satyarāja asked about a householder's duties and heard of the characteristics of a Vaiṣṇava, a better Vaiṣṇava and the best Vaiṣṇava and of the gradation of their qualification, see Madhya 15.102–109 and 16.69–75. At the place where he worshipped there is now a Deity of Śrī Mahāprabhu.

Kulīnagrāma lies within two miles of Jaugrāma station on the Howrah–Barddhamāna New Chord line. For Mahāprabhu's words on the glory of Kulīnagrāma, see Ādi 10.82–83 and Madhya 15.100–101.[^48-1]

## 49

> Subheading: (21) The greatness and the branch of Śrī Murāri Gupta —

Śrī Murāri Gupta was the author of the book *Śrī Caitanya-carita*. He was born in a Vaidya family of Śrīhaṭṭa and later settled in Navadvīpa. He was older than Mahāprabhu. In his house Mahāprabhu showed the form of Varāha (*Caitanya-bhāgavata*, Madhya, Chapter 3), and in the state of the Mahā-prakāśa He showed him His form as Śrī Rāma (*Caitanya-bhāgavata*, Madhya, Chapter 10).

One day in Śrīvāsa's house Gaurasundara was seated with Nityānanda. Murāri Gupta first bowed down to Gaura and then offered prostrated obeisances to Nityānanda. The Lord said to Murāri, “You have offered obeisances in violation of proper order,” and at night, in a dream, He revealed to him the truth about Nityānanda. The next morning Murāri first worshipped the feet of Nityānanda and then those of Mahāprabhu. The Lord gave Murāri chewed betel. One day Murāri offered rice with ghee to Śrī Mahāprabhu in thought. The next morning Mahāprabhu, suffering indigestion from eating so much rice, came to Murāri for treatment. Saying, “The only medicine for this is the water in Murāri's water pot,” the Lord drank that water. In Śrīvāsa's house Mahāprabhu assumed a four-armed form; Murāri felt himself to be Garuḍa, and the Lord mounted his shoulders. Thinking that he could not bear the separation when the Lord concealed Himself, Murāri resolved to give up his body while the Lord was still manifest, but the Lord, the indweller of the heart, held him back (*Caitanya-bhāgavata*, Madhya, Chapter 20). One day the Lord, absorbed in *bhāva*, manifested the form of Varāha in Murāri Gupta's house, and Murāri, seeing Him, offered prayers (*Caitanya-bhāgavata*, Antya, Chapter 4). For Murāri Gupta's humble words, see Madhya 11.152–158; for Murāri's steadfast devotion to Śrī Rāma, see Madhya 15.137–157.

## 52

> Subheading: (22) Śrīmān Sena —

Śrīmān Sena lived in Navadvīpa and was a companion of the Lord.

## 53

> Subheading: (23) The branch of Śrī Gadādhara dāsa —

Śrī Gadādhara dāsa. The village of Eṅḍiyādaha lies on the bank of the Bhāgīrathī, four *krośas* north of Calcutta. After Mahāprabhu withdrew from the world's sight, Dāsa Gadādhara went from Navadvīpa to Kāṭoyā (*Bhakti-ratnākara*, seventh wave), and later came from there to this village and lived there. He is the luster (*kānti*) of Śrī Rādhā: as Śrīla Paṇḍita Gosvāmī is Śrīmatī Vṛṣabhānu-nandinī Herself, so Śrīla Gadādhara dāsa is the beauty of Śrīmatī's body. Gaura is “adorned with the mood and luster of Rādhā,” and Gadādhara dāsa is that very luster of Gaura. In the *Gaura-gaṇoddeśa* he is named the *vibhūti*, the expansion, of Vṛṣabhānu-nandinī. He is counted among the associates of both Gaura and Nityānanda. Gaura's associates are connoisseurs (*rasikas*) of the *madhura-rasa* of Vraja; Nityānanda's associates are connoisseurs of *sakhya* and the other *rasas* in which pure devotion predominates. Although Śrī Dāsa Gadādhara is among Nityānanda's associates, he is not a *gopāla*, a cowherd boy full of *sakhya-bhāva*: he was a connoisseur of *madhura-rasa*. At Kāṭoyā he had the worship of Gaura.

In 1434 Śaka, when Śrī Mahāprabhu requested Śrī Nityānanda Prabhu to go from Nīlācala to Gauḍa to preach devotion, Gadādhara became one of Nityānanda's chief helpers in the work of preaching (Ādi 11.13–14). Śrī Gadādhara dāsa taught everyone to chant the holy name of Hari. The Kazi of that village opposed *kīrtana*. One night Śrī Dāsa Gadādhara, while performing *kīrtana*, went to the Kazi's house, wishing to deliver him, and asked him to utter the holy name of Hari. The Kazi replied, “I will say ‘Hari’ tomorrow,” and Gadādhara dāsa, filled with the joy of *prema*, said: “… Why tomorrow? You have just said ‘Hari’ with your own mouth.” In the *Gaura-gaṇoddeśa*: “She who was formerly in Vraja Candrakānti, the *vibhūti* of Rādhā, is Gadādhara of the Dāsa family, who is near Śrī Gaurāṅga. She who in Vraja was Pūrṇānandā, the foremost of Baladeva's beloveds, also entered that Gadādhara for some purpose.”

On the way from Nīlācala to Gauḍa, Śrī Dāsa Gadādhara, in the mood of Śrī Rādhā and laughing loudly, became a seller of yogurt and forgot his external identity. Nityānanda Prabhu saw this. Sometimes Gadādhara, absorbed in the mood of a gopī, would carry on his head a pot full of Gaṅgā water and sell it as milk. When Śrīman Mahāprabhu wished to go to Śrī Vṛndāvana by way of Gauḍa-maṇḍala, He came to Rāghava's house in the village of Pāṇihāṭī. Then “hearing that Śrī Gaurasundara was in Rāghava's house, Gadādhara dāsa came running at once. The Lord, seeing pious Gadādhara, raised His feet and placed them on his head” (*Caitanya-bhāgavata*, Antya, Chapter 5). During his manifest presence there was a Deity of Bāla-Gopāla in Gadādhara's house in Eṅḍiyādaha. With the help of Śrī Nityānanda Prabhu and Śrī Dāsa Gadādhara, Śrī Mādhava Ghoṣa sang and danced before the Deity of Śrī Gopāla, enacting the “Dāna-khaṇḍa” (*Caitanya-bhāgavata*, Antya, Chapter 5).

When Śrī Gadādhara disappeared, his *samādhi* was made in that village. The *samādhi* was in the possession of *saṁyogī* Vaiṣṇavas. On the instruction of the perfected Śrī Bhagavān dāsa Bābājī Mahārāja of Kālnā, the late Madhusūdana Mallika of Nārikeladāṅgā in Calcutta founded a *pāṭabāṭī* (holy residence) there and in the Bengali year 1256 arranged the service of the Deity Śrī Rādhākānta. His son Balāi Mallika in 1312 established a service of Śrī Gaura-Nitāi. On the throne of the temple reside the Deities of Śrī Gaura-Nityānanda and of Śrī Rādhā-Kṛṣṇa; beneath the throne a Sanskrit verse is engraved. A Gopeśvara Śiva-liṅga is also installed there. At the temple entrance all that is stated above is engraved on a stone tablet. See the *Vaiṣṇava-mañjuṣā-samāhṛti* (issue 1).

## 54

> Subheading: (24) The branch of Śrī Śivānanda Sena —

## 56

> Subheading: The Lord bestows mercy by descending in three forms —

“*Sākṣāt*”: Gaurasundara in His own form; “*āveśa*”: in Nakula or Pradyumna Brahmacārī; *āvirbhāva* (CC Antya 2.34–35): “In Śacī's house, where Nityānanda dances, at Śrīvāsa's *kīrtana* and in Rāghava's house — in these four places the Lord is always manifest (*āvirbhāva*). To be drawn by love is the Lord's natural disposition.”

According to the *Gaura-gaṇoddeśa*, the Lord's *āvirbhāva* and *āveśa* took place in Nakula Brahmacārī and Pradyumna Miśra, thus (verse 74): “The *āvirbhāva* of Gaurahari was in Nakula Brahmacārī, and *āveśa* likewise should be recognized in the Miśra named Pradyumna.”

## 57

In the direct manifestation (*sākṣāt*) all the devotees are seen without distinction among themselves — as servants of one kind, not different from one another.[^57-1] But because Śrīman Mahāprabhu entered Nṛsiṁhānanda, Śrī Śivānanda Sena and other devotees saw him exactly like Śrī Gaurasundara: full of love for Kṛṣṇa and performing more superhuman, divine acts than all the other devotees.

## 58

Pradyumna Brahmacārī formerly lived in the hamlet of Piyārīgañja near Kālnā. He is mentioned in Ādi 10.35 and Antya 2.3–83. See the *Caitanya-bhāgavata*, Antya, Chapters 3 and 9.

## 61

> Subheading: (24a) The branch of Śrī Śivānanda — his sons, servants and others —

Śivānanda Sena was a resident of Kumārahaṭṭa, or Hālisahara, and a devotee of the Lord. One and a half miles from there, in Kāñcaḍāpāḍā, there is a Deity of Gaura-Gopāla established by him (still present today in the temple of Śrī Kṛṣṇa-rāya). His son Paramānanda (Purīdāsa) writes in the *Gaura-gaṇoddeśa* (verse 176): “She who formerly in Vṛndāvana, as the messenger Vīrā, brought all the gopīs to Kṛṣṇa, is now my father.” Every year he guided the devotees from Gauḍa, bore their travel expenses, looked after them and brought them to Mahāprabhu in Nīlācala (Madhya 16.19–26). His three sons were Caitanya dāsa, Rāmadāsa and Paramānanda (Kavi-karṇapūra). On Karṇapūra's *dīkṣā-guru* (Śivānanda's guru and family priest) Śrīnātha Paṇḍita, see Ādi 10.107. Seeing Vāsudeva Datta's lavish spending, Mahāprabhu ordered Śivānanda to act as his *sarakhela* (manager) (Madhya 15.93–97).

Śrī Mahāprabhu bestows mercy on the devotees in three ways — “*sākṣāt*,” “*āveśa*” and “*āvirbhāva*.” Śivānanda Sena experienced and tasted these three *rasas* (Antya, Chapter 2), and the story of his dog, who longed to see Gaura's feet, is told in Antya, Chapter 1. When Raghunātha dāsa Gosvāmī Prabhu fled to Nīlācala to see the Lord, Govardhana sent Śivānanda a letter and learned news of his son. He then sent a cook, a servant and much money for Raghunātha's comfort, and the next year Śivānanda took them to Nīlācala (Antya 6.245–267). Once in Nīlācala Śivānanda invited the Lord and fed Him a very heavy meal. The Lord's mind was not fully pleased, so the next day Śivānanda's son Caitanya dāsa fed the Lord foods that aid digestion and satisfied Him (Antya 10.124–151).

Once, on the way to Nīlācala, while Śivānanda was settling matters at the toll station (*ghāṭi*), Nityānanda and all the others found no lodging and had to stay under a tree in a nearby village. Nitāi, feigning hunger and anger, cursed: “May Śivānanda's three sons die!” Śivānanda's wife, fearing misfortune, began to weep. Śivānanda came from the toll station, heard everything and spoke to his wife in praise of his own good fortune. As soon as he came to Nitāi, he received the good fortune of being kicked by Nitāi. Seeing this, Śivānanda's nephew Śrīkānta took offense and went alone to the Lord. The Lord, the indweller of the heart, forgave and consoled him. On that same visit the Lord put His big toe into Purīdāsa's mouth; at first the boy kept a vow of silence, and later, on another day, by the Lord's order he composed a verse (Antya 16.15–75). At that time the Lord ordered Govinda: “As long as Śivānanda's wife and sons are here, let them receive the plate of My remnants” (Antya 12.15–53).

## 62

> Subheading: His three sons —

Caitanya dāsa was Śivānanda's eldest son. His commentary on the *Kṛṣṇa-karṇāmṛta*, with a translation by Śrīmad Bhaktivinoda Ṭhākura, was published in the journal *Śrī Sajjana-toṣaṇī*. In the opinion of those who know, he, and not Kavi-karṇapūra, is the author of the Sanskrit *mahākāvya* (great poem) called *Caitanya-carita*.

Rāmadāsa was the middle son. In the *Gaura-gaṇoddeśa* (verse 145): “The two parrots famous in Vṛndāvana, Dakṣa and Vicakṣaṇa, have now been born as my elder brothers, Caitanya and Rāmadāsa.”

Karṇapūra is Paramānanda dāsa, also called Purīdāsa or Kavi-karṇapūra. He was a disciple of Śrīnātha Paṇḍita of the Advaita branch. He composed the *Ānanda-vṛndāvana-campū*, the *Alaṅkāra-kaustubha*, the *mahākāvya* *Śrī Caitanya-carita* (?), the play *Śrī Caitanya-candrodaya*, the *Gaura-gaṇoddeśa-dīpikā* and other books. He was born in 1448 Śaka and wrote books until 1498 Śaka.

## 63

> Subheading: (24b) Śrī Śivānanda's two nephews —

Śrī Vallabha Sena and Śrīkānta Sena were the sons of Śivānanda's sister. When they were coming to Purī, Nityānanda Prabhu abused, cursed and kicked their maternal uncle Śivānanda. Śrīkānta took offense, left the party and came to Mahāprabhu ahead of the others. The omniscient Lord knew this and told Govinda to give him His own *prasāda* for as long as he stayed in Nīlācala. At the Ratha-yātrā these two brothers were singers in Mukunda's party, one of the seven *kīrtana* parties (Madhya 13.41). In the *Gaura-gaṇoddeśa* (verse 174): “She who in Vraja was Kātyāyanī is now Śrīkānta Sena.”

## 64

> Subheading: (25) Śrī Govindānanda and (26) Śrī Govinda Datta —

Govindānanda was Mahāprabhu's companion in His first *kīrtana* in Navadvīpa. On the day the Lord drank water at Śrīdhara's house, he wept. He went to Puruṣottama for the Ratha-yātrā.

Govinda Datta was the Lord's companion in *kīrtana* in Navadvīpa and sang *kīrtana* with Mahāprabhu as the lead singer (*Caitanya-bhāgavata*, Antya, Chapter 9): “… he who, as lead singer, performs *kīrtana* with the Lord.” His *śrīpāṭa* is in the village of Sukhacara on the southern border of Khaḍadaha.

## 65

> Subheading: (27) Śrī Vijaya dāsa —

Vijaya dāsa was a scribe of Navadvīpa, one of the nine *nidhis*. He copied many books for the Lord, and for this Gaurahari gave him the name “Ratnabāhu.” On the mercy Mahāprabhu showed Vijaya in Śuklāmbara's house, see the *Caitanya-bhāgavata*, Madhya, Chapter 25.

## 66

> Subheading: (28) Akiñcana Kṛṣṇadāsa —

Akiñcana Kṛṣṇadāsa lived in Navadvīpa and was the Lord's companion. He came to Puruṣottama for the Ratha-yātrā. See the *Caitanya-bhāgavata*, Antya, Chapter 9.

## 67

> Subheading: (29) The virtues of Śrīdhara —

## 67–68

Śrīdhara was a poor brāhmaṇa of Navadvīpa who lived on what the banana groves gave him. On the Lord's snatching from Śrīdhara, see the *Caitanya-bhāgavata*, Ādi, Chapter 8; on the Lord's mercy to Śrīdhara during the *sāt-prahariyā bhāva*, Madhya, Chapter 9; on Śrīdhara's dancing when he heard the *kīrtana* on the day the Kazi was subdued, the beginning of Madhya, Chapter 23; on the Lord's drinking water in great joy from his old, holed iron pot, the end of Madhya, Chapter 23; and on how, the night before His *sannyāsa*, the Lord had Śacīdevī cook the gourd Śrīdhara had brought and ate it, Madhya, Chapter 16. He used to go to Puruṣottama for the Ratha-yātrā. According to Karṇapūra he is one of the twelve *gopālas*, “Kusumāsava-gopāla.” In the *Gaura-gaṇoddeśa* (verse 133): “Paṇḍita Śrīdhara, the brāhmaṇa famed as a seller of *kholā* (banana bark), was in Vraja the jester named Kusumāsava.”

## 69

> Subheading: (30) Śrī Bhagavān Paṇḍita —

Bhagavān Paṇḍita. *Caitanya-bhāgavata*, Antya, Chapter 9: “The scribe Paṇḍita Bhagavān set out, in whose body Kṛṣṇa had resided.”

## 70

> Subheading: (31) Śrī Jagadīśa Paṇḍita and (32) Hiraṇya —

## 70–71

Jagadīśa Paṇḍita. In the *Gaura-gaṇoddeśa* (verse 192): “Two other wives of the sacrificing brāhmaṇas are Śrī Jagadīśa and Hiraṇya, whose food the Lord begged and ate on Ekādaśī.” Verse 143: “He who in Vraja was Candrahāsa, a dancer expert in *rasa*, is this Śrī Jagadīśa Paṇḍita, who delights in dancing.” How on the day of Ekādaśī the Lord ate the food offered to Viṣṇu in the house of Hiraṇya and Jagadīśa is described in CC Ādi 11.30 and 14.39 and in the *Caitanya-bhāgavata*, Ādi, Chapter 4. *Caitanya-bhāgavata*, Antya, Chapter 6: “Jagadīśa Paṇḍita is the abode of supreme effulgence; Nityānanda with His associates is his wealth and life.”

Hiraṇya Paṇḍita. *Caitanya-bhāgavata*, Antya, Chapter 5: Nityānanda, alone and wearing very costly ornaments, was staying in Navadvīpa in the house of a good brāhmaṇa named Hiraṇya Paṇḍita. A chief of robbers tried again and again to take those ornaments from His body, but in the end, his plans frustrated, he took shelter at Nitāi's feet.

## 72

> Subheading: (33) Śrī Puruṣottama and (34) Śrī Sañjaya —

Puruṣottama and Sañjaya. These two *paṇḍitas* lived in Navadvīpa: first they were the Lord's students, and later, when the *kīrtana* began, His companions. *Caitanya-bhāgavata*, Ādi, Chapter 10: “… Mukunda Sañjaya, a servant for many births. Puruṣottama dāsa is his son. Every day Gauracandra goes to teach at that fortunate man's home. The Lord first comes and sits in the Caṇḍī-maṇḍapa, and then the students gather one by one.” *Caitanya-bhāgavata*, Antya, Chapter 9: “Puruṣottama Sañjaya set out with a joyful heart, he who was formerly the Lord's chief student.” Thus, according to the description in the *Caitanya-bhāgavata*, Puruṣottama Sañjaya is the son of Mukunda Sañjaya. But Kavirāja Gosvāmī, intending to speak of two persons named “Puruṣottama” and “Sañjaya,” used these two words three times and so corrected the idea of the *Caitanya-bhāgavata*.

## 73

> Subheading: (35) The branch of Śrī Vanamālī Paṇḍita —

Vanamālī Paṇḍita. “The auspicious Vanamālī Paṇḍita set out, he who saw the golden plough and club.” In the *Gaura-gaṇoddeśa* (verse 144): “He who in Vraja, named Mālādhara, held the *veṇu* and the *muralī* (flutes) is now called Vanamālī Paṇḍita and is dear to Gaura.” He saw the Lord in the mood of Baladeva. CC Ādi 17.119 and the *Caitanya-bhāgavata*, Antya, Chapter 9: in Śrīvāsa's house the Lord ascended the throne of Viṣṇu, took the plough and club from Nityānanda Prabhu and displayed a pastime; but it is not mentioned there that on that day Vanamālī Paṇḍita saw a golden plough and club in the Lord's hands.

## 74

> Subheading: (36) Śrī Buddhimanta Khān —

Buddhimanta Khān was a wealthy devotee of Navadvīpa. He bore all the expenses of the Lord's second marriage, to Viṣṇupriyā Devī, the daughter of the royal *paṇḍita* Sanātana Miśra. When the Lord suffered a disorder of *vāyu* (air in the body), he arranged His treatment. He was the Lord's companion in water sports and in *kīrtana*, and in Candraśekhara's house, when the Lord enacted the play in the guise of Mahā-lakṣmī, he collected the clothes, ornaments and other things. He went to Nīlācala for the Ratha-yātrā.

## 75

> Subheading: (37) Śrī Garuḍa Paṇḍita —

Garuḍa Paṇḍita lived in Navadvīpa and was the Lord's companion. *Caitanya-bhāgavata*, Antya, Chapter 9: “Śrī Garuḍa Paṇḍita set out joyfully, he whom snake poison could not overcome by the power of the holy name.” In the *Gaura-gaṇoddeśa* (verse 17): “Garuḍa Paṇḍita is now he who was formerly known as Garuḍa.”

## 76

> Subheading: (38) Śrī Gopīnātha Siṁha —

Gopīnātha Siṁha. *Caitanya-bhāgavata*, Antya, Chapter 9 (at the Ratha-yātrā): “Gopīnātha Siṁha Mahāśaya set out, he whom Gauracandra calls ‘Akrūra.’” In the *Gaura-gaṇoddeśa* (verse 117): “He who was formerly named Akrūra is this Gopīnātha Siṁha.”

## 77

> Subheading: (5a) The branch of Devānanda Paṇḍita —

Devānanda. *Caitanya-bhāgavata*, Madhya, Chapter 21: “The Lord Viśvambhara went to the embankment of Maheśvara Viśārada, the father of Sārvabhauma. There Devānanda Paṇḍita lived.” CC Madhya 1.153: “In the village of Kuliyā He bestowed His mercy on Devānanda.” He desired liberation (he was a *mumukṣu*) and used to recite the *Bhāgavatam*. One day, during his recitation, Śrīvāsa Paṇḍita began to weep, and Devānanda's godless students drove Śrīvāsa out (*Caitanya-bhāgavata*, Madhya, Chapters 9 and 21). Long afterwards, one day Mahāprabhu came that way and saw Devānanda explaining the *Bhāgavatam*. In anger the Lord sharply rebuked Devānanda, who had no faith in the Vaiṣṇavas. Devānanda had no faith in Mahāprabhu either. But by his great good fortune Vakreśvara Paṇḍita once stayed at his house and sang *kīrtana* of Kṛṣṇa. Then by Vakreśvara's grace Devānanda understood the Lord's greatness, and the Lord told him to explain the *Bhāgavatam* in terms of devotion. He is Bhāguri Muni, the court *paṇḍita* of Nanda in Vraja: see the *Gaura-gaṇoddeśa*, verses 106 and 77.

## 78

> Subheading: (39) Mukunda, (40) Narahari, (41) Cirañjīva and (42) Sulocana of Śrī Khaṇḍa —

Mukunda dāsa was the son of Nārāyaṇa dāsa and the elder brother of Narahari Sarakāra Ṭhākura; the middle brother's name was Mādhava dāsa. His son was Raghunandana. Raghunandana's descendants still live in the village of Śrīkhaṇḍa, four miles west of Kāṭoyā. Raghunandana's son was Kānāi, and Kānāi had two sons: Madana Rāya (a disciple of Narahari Ṭhākura) and Vaṁśīvadana. In this family somewhat more than four hundred persons have been born up to the present. Their unbroken genealogy is kept at Śrīkhaṇḍa. In the *Gaura-gaṇoddeśa-dīpikā* (verse 170): “She who was formerly the mistress of Vraja, named Vṛndā-devī, is now Śrī Mukunda dāsa of Khaṇḍa, dear to the Lord.” For a description of Mukunda's most wonderful love for Kṛṣṇa, see this book, Madhya 15.113–131.

Raghunandana served the Deity of Śrī Gaura (see the *Bhakti-ratnākara*, eighth wave). In the *Gaura-gaṇoddeśa* (verse 70): “The third *vyūha*, Pradyumna, became the dear confidential friend (*priya-narma-sakha*) who assisted Rādhā and Mādhava in Their pastimes in Vraja.” He is the third *vyūha*, Pradyumna Viṣṇu (see “Mukunda dāsa” above). For a description of the great festival at Śrīkhaṇḍa, see the *Bhakti-ratnākara*, ninth wave.

Narahari dāsa Sarakāra Ṭhākura. In the *Gaura-gaṇoddeśa* (verse 177[^78-1]): “She who was formerly Madhumatī in Vṛndāvana, a dearest friend, is now the one called Narahari Sarakāra, dear to the Lord.” His disciple was Śrī Locana dāsa Ṭhākura of Kogrāma near Jhāmaṭapura, the author of the *Śrī Caitanya-maṅgala*. In that book Śrī Gadādhara and Śrī Narahari are described as most dear to Śrī Mahāprabhu. In the *Caitanya-bhāgavata*, however, there is hardly any special mention of the residents of Khaṇḍa.

Cirañjīva and Sulocana were both residents of Khaṇḍa. Their homes can still be seen in Śrīkhaṇḍa, and their descendants are living. Cirañjīva Sena had two sons: the elder, Rāmacandra Kavirāja, was a disciple of Śrīnivāsa Ācārya and a companion of Ṭhākura Mahāśaya; the younger was Govinda dāsa Kavirāja, the composer of *padas*. Cirañjīva's wife was Sunandā, and his father-in-law was Dāmodara Sena Kavirāja of Khaṇḍa. Cirañjīva formerly lived in the village of Kumāranagara on the bank of the Bhāgīrathī (*Bhakti-ratnākara*).

Cirañjīva is Candrikā of Vraja. *Gaura-gaṇoddeśa*, verses 197 and 207: “Cirañjīva and Sulocana of Khaṇḍa, great through their association with Narahari, whose only shelter is Gaurāṅga.”

## 80

> Subheading: Residents of Kulīnagrāma — (20b) Rāmānanda, (20c) Yadunātha, (20d) Puruṣottama, (20e) Śaṅkara, (20f) Vidyānanda —

Rāmānanda Vasu. In the *Gaura-gaṇoddeśa*: “Kalakaṇṭhī and Sukaṇṭhī, the two singer-actresses in Vraja, are Rāmānanda Vasu and Satyarāja respectively.” Yadunātha, Puruṣottama, Śaṅkara, Vidyānanda and the others were all born in the Vasu family. Everyone in this Vasu family was a devotee of Kṛṣṇa and highly skilled in dramatic performances of Kṛṣṇa's pastimes; the memory of these performances is preserved even today. They were pure devotees, followers of Haridāsa Ṭhākura. See the comment on verse 48 above.

## 81

> Subheading: (20g) The glory of the residents of Kulīnagrāma headed by Vāṇīnātha Vasu —

## 84

> Subheading: (43) The branch of Śrī Sanātana, (44) Śrī Rūpa and (45) Śrī Anupama —

Anupama was the father of Śrī Jīva Gosvāmī and the younger brother of the two Gosvāmīs, Śrī Sanātana and Śrī Rūpa. His former name was Śrī Vallabha, and the name “Anupama” was given him by Śrī Mahāprabhu. Because the brothers served the Bādaśāha of Gauḍa, they bore the title “Mallika.” “Anupama Mallika — his name was Śrī Vallabha; Śrī Rūpa Gosāñi's younger brother, a great Vaiṣṇava.” (CC Madhya 19.36)

In the twelfth century of the Śaka era, in the land of Karṇāṭa, in a royal family of brāhmaṇas, there arose a great soul named Sarvajña, a *jagad-guru* of the Bharadvāja *gotra*. His son Aniruddha had two sons, Rūpeśvara and Harihara. Both were deprived of the kingdom, and the elder, Rūpeśvara, settled in Śikharabhūmi. Rūpeśvara's son Padmanābha settled in a village called Naihāṭī on the bank of the Gaṅgā and had five sons. The son of the youngest of them, Mukunda, was Kumāradeva, a man of exemplary conduct, the father of Sanātana, Rūpa and Anupama. Kumāradeva lived in Bākalā-Candradvīpa; his home was in a place called Phateyābāda, in what was then the province of Yaśohara. Of his several sons, three accepted the Vaiṣṇava *dharma*. Śrī Vallabha, together with his elder brothers Śrī Rūpa and Śrī Sanātana, moved from Candradvīpa for their work to the village of Rāmakeli in Gauḍa. There Śrī Jīva Gosvāmī was born. Because they served in the Nawab's government, all three received the title “Mallika.” When Śrī Mahāprabhu went to Rāmakeli, Anupama met Him for the first time. When Śrī Rūpa Gosvāmī gave up worldly work and set out for Śrī Vṛndāvana, seeking Śrī Mahāprabhu's feet, Vallabha became his companion. For the description of their meeting with Mahāprabhu at Prayāga, see Madhya, Chapter 19.

From Śrī Mahāprabhu, Śrī Rūpa and Anupama went to Vṛndāvana. This is known from Śrī Mahāprabhu's words to Sanātana: “Rūpa and Anupama, both went to Vṛndāvana” (CC Madhya 20.66). At that time Subuddhi Rāya was living in the city of Mathurā, selling dry wood; with this he maintained himself and served other Vaiṣṇavas. When Śrī Rūpa and Anupama reached him, he was especially pleased and took them with him through the twelve forests of Vṛndāvana. The two brothers stayed a month in Vṛndāvana and then came to Prayāga along the Gaṅgā in search of Śrī Sanātana Gosvāmī. But Śrī Sanātana came to Mathurā by the royal road, and the three brothers could not meet. Subuddhi Rāya told Sanātana about Anupama and Rūpa. Anupama and Śrī Rūpa both came to Kāśī, heard everything from Mahāprabhu, and after ten days set out for Gauḍa. Having settled their worldly affairs there, both set out for Nīlācala by Śrī Mahāprabhu's order. In 1436 Śaka, on the way, on the bank of the Gaṅgā, Anupama attained the abode of Śrī Rāmacandra. Śrī Rūpa Gosvāmī informed Śrīman Mahāprabhu in Nīlācala that his younger brother had attained that abode.

Because Vallabha was a worshipper of Śrī Rāma, he could not fully accept the path of worship in Vraja shown by Śrīman Mahāprabhu. He knew Śrīman Mahāprabhu to be Śrī Rāmacandra Himself. In the book *Bhakti-ratnākara* it is written of Anupama: “Śrī Gaurasundara gave him the name Anupama. He was always absorbed in serving the Deity of Raghunātha and knew no one but Raghunātha. Caitanya Gosāñi is Śrī Raghunātha Himself.”

Śrī Rūpa. In the *Gaura-gaṇoddeśa* (verse 180): “She who was formerly known in Vṛndāvana as Śrī Rūpa-mañjarī has now become manifest as the one called Rūpa Gosvāmī.” *Bhakti-ratnākara*, ninth wave: “Śrī Rūpa Gosvāmī wrote sixteen books: (1) the poem *Haṁsadūta* and (2) the *Uddhava-sandeśa*; (3) the *Kṛṣṇa-janma-tithi-vidhi*, with its countless injunctions; (4) the *Gaṇoddeśa-dīpikā*, both the large and the small; (5) the *Stava-mālā*; (6) the *Vidagdha-mādhava*, full of *rasa*; (7) the *Lalita-mādhava*, the very limit of *vipralambha*; (8) the *Dāna-līlā-kaumudī*, a great ocean of bliss — (9) known by this name, the *Dāna-keli-kaumudī*; (10) the incomparable book *Bhakti-rasāmṛta-sindhu*; (11) the book *Śrī Ujjvala-nīlamaṇi*, full of *rasa*; the most sweet book (12) *Prayuktākhyāta-candrikā*; (13) the *Mathurā-mahimā*, (14) the well-known *Padyāvalī*; (15) the *Nāṭaka-candrikā*, (16) the *Laghu-bhāgavatāmṛta*. At the Vaiṣṇavas' wish he composed eleven verses and gave them to Kṛṣṇadāsa Kavirāja to elaborate. The Gosvāmī also composed separate prayers (*stavas*); collected by Śrī Jīva, they were named the *Stava-mālā*. Briefly he also set out the characteristics of the *viruda*; the *Govinda-virudāvalī* is its example.” See CC Madhya 1.35–44 and Antya 4.219–231.

Śrī Rūpa's giving up worldly affairs — Madhya 19.4; his division of wealth — 7; his coming to Prayāga and meeting the Lord — 37 and 45; his honoring *prasāda* with Anupama in Vallabha Bhaṭṭa's house — 88; the Lord's instruction at Prayāga — 135–233; the Lord's order to go to Vṛndāvana — 237; Rūpa's return to Gauḍa and Anupama's attaining the Gaṅgā (leaving his body on the Gaṅgā) — Antya 1.37; his coming to Ṭhākura Haridāsa's hut in Śrī Kṣetra and meeting the Lord and His associates — 48 and 54; the Lord's praise of Śrī Rūpa's handwriting; the verse in accord with the feelings of the Lord's heart; the beginning of the *Lalita-mādhava* and *Vidagdha-mādhava* and the praise of Śrī Rāma Rāya — 68–192; the Lord's wish to reveal the scriptures of devotion and His order to Śrī Rūpa to go to Vṛndāvana — 202, 216; Rūpa's going to Vṛndāvana — 211; his meeting Sanātana Prabhu when Sanātana came to Vṛndāvana — Antya 4.213; and his carrying out Gaura's order — 217–222.

Sanātana. In the *Gaura-gaṇoddeśa* (verse 181): “She who was formerly Rati-mañjarī, dearest to Rūpa-mañjarī, and whom the wise call by another name, Lavaṅga-mañjarī, is now Sanātana, whose body is non-different from Gaura's and who is worshipped by all. For some purpose Sanātana, the jewel among sages, also entered him.” *Bhakti-ratnākara*, first wave: “Śrī Sanātana's guru was Vidyāvācaspati; from time to time he stayed in the village of Rāmakeli. Under him Sanātana studied all the scriptures. I cannot describe how great his devotion to his guru was. His father, on seeing a *yavana*, would perform atonement, yet he himself constantly had to keep company with such *yavanas*. Depending on them, he went to the *yavanas*' houses, and so he considered himself equal to a *mleccha*. Therefore he considered himself utterly low and wretched. Sinking into the ocean of humility, he considered himself lower than the *mlecchas* and others. With people of low birth he always behaved humbly; that is why he spoke of himself as ‘of low birth and so on.’ Though a king among brāhmaṇas, with deep sorrow in his heart he never thought of himself as a brāhmaṇa.”

*Bhakti-ratnākara*, first wave: “Sanātana Gosvāmī's four books: the two parts of the *Bhāgavatāmṛta* with commentary; the commentary on the *Hari-bhakti-vilāsa*, the *Dig-darśinī*; the *ṭippanī* on the Tenth Canto named *Vaiṣṇava-toṣaṇī*; and the *Līlā-stava*, which is called the *Daśama-carita*. These are the four books of Sanātana Gosvāmī. In fourteen hundred seventy-six (1476) the *Bṛhat* (that is, the *Vaiṣṇava-toṣaṇī*) was completed, and in fifteen hundred four Śaka (1504) the well-accepted *Laghu-toṣaṇī*. (Mahāprabhu) through Rāmānanda destroyed the pride of Kandarpa, through Dāmodara revealed indifference (*nairapekṣya*), through Haridāsa made tolerance known, and through Sanātana and Rūpa revealed humility.”

Sanātana's pondering how to give up worldly affairs — Madhya 19.13; his study of the *Bhāgavatam* on the pretext of illness — 15; the Bādaśāha's coming to see him — 18; his imprisonment by the Bādaśāha — 27; his refusal to go with the Bādaśāha to war in Orissa — 29; the letter Rūpa sent to Sanātana in prison when leaving home — 32 and 20.3; his release by bribing the jailer — 4; his escape with his only servant Īśāna, saving himself from the clutches of a robber chief by giving eight *mohars*, crossing the mountains with the robber's help, dismissing Īśāna and going on alone — 16–25; his meeting with his sister's husband Śrīkānta at Hājipura, his refusal to stay there, and the Bhoṭa blanket he accepted from him — 38–44; his arrival in Vārāṇasī and meeting the Lord in Candraśekhara's house — 51; how he had himself shaved, changed his dress and made an outer cloth and *kaupīna* from the old cloth given by Tapana Miśra — 68–77; alms in the house of a Mahārāṣṭrian brāhmaṇa — 79; his questions to the Lord about the truth and the Lord's teaching: (1) knowledge of *sambandha* — 20.98 through the whole of Chapter 21; (2) *abhidheya* — the whole of Chapter 22; (3) *prayojana* — 23.3–93; the order to Sanātana to write the conclusions of devotion, restore lost holy places, establish a Vaiṣṇava society by compiling the Vaiṣṇava *smṛti*, and spread the scriptures of devotion — 97; the blessing — 118; the explanation to him of the *ātmārāma* verse, first in eighteen and then in sixty-one ways — 24.4–308; Sanātana's going to Mathurā by the royal road and meeting Subuddhi Rāya — 203–204; Sanātana's coming again to Nīlācala by the Jhārikhaṇḍa route — Antya 4.3; his resolve to give up his body under the wheel of the chariot — 12; meeting Haridāsa and seeing the Lord with His associates — 14–22; hearing of Anupama's attaining the Gaṅgā and of his glory — 30–47; the Lord's disapproval of Sanātana's resolve to give up his body — 54–65; the Lord's wish to accomplish His own purposes through Sanātana Gosvāmī — 76–88; Sanātana's description of the glory of Haridāsa Ṭhākura — 100–103; his walking to the Lord over hot sand, keeping to propriety for fear of touching the servants of Jagannātha, and the Lord's satisfaction on seeing this — 115–131; his request, on Jagadānanda's words, for permission to go to Vṛndāvana — 141–155; the Lord's praise of Sanātana — 163–170; the Lord's love for Sanātana's transcendental body and His embrace, by which he obtained a divine body — 172–198; the Lord's order to stay a year in Nīlācala — 200; His sending him to Vṛndāvana — 207; his going to Vṛndāvana and meeting Śrī Rūpa after a long time — 213; and his carrying out Gaura's order — 217–222.

The *śrīpāṭa* of Śrī Rūpa and Śrī Sanātana Gosvāmīs is Śrī Rāmakeli (“Gupta Vṛndāvana,” the hidden Vṛndāvana), about eight miles south of the present town of Iṁrejabājāra. The following places can be seen there: (1) the Deity Śrī Śrī Madana-mohana, installed by Śrī Sanātana; (2) the *keli-kadamba* tree: tradition says that beneath this tree, at midnight, Śrī Rūpa and Śrī Sanātana Gosvāmīs met Śrīman Mahāprabhu, and Mahāprabhu with His associates gave the devotees the nectar of *prema*; (3) the Rūpa-sāgara, a large pond made by Śrī Rūpa Gosvāmī Prabhu. To dredge this pond and restore the lost glory of the Śrī Rāmakeli *pāṭa*, the “Rāmakeli Restoration Committee” (*Rāmakeli-saṁskāra-samiti*) was founded at Māladaha on 8.6.24 (English calendar).

## 85

> Subheading: (44a) Śrī Jīva —

Of Jīva the *Gaura-gaṇoddeśa* (verse 203) says: “Jīva, the son of Śrī Vallabha, is gentle, learned and handsome.” According to verse 195, in the pastimes of Vraja he is Vilāsa-mañjarī. As a boy Śrī Jīva loved the *Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam*. Later he came to Navadvīpa and, following Śrī Nityānanda, walked around Śrī Navadvīpa-dhāma and saw its holy places. From there he went to Kāśī and studied all the scriptures under Madhusūdana Vācaspati; then he went to Vṛndāvana and took shelter of Śrī Rūpa and Sanātana. The *Śrī Bhakti-ratnākara* (first wave) says: “Twenty-five books of Śrī Jīva are known: (1) the grammar *Harināmāmṛta*, wonderful in its design; (2) the *Sūtra-mālikā*; (3) the excellent *Dhātu-saṅgraha*; (4) the most wonderful book *Kṛṣṇārcā-dīpikā*; (5) the *Gopāla-virudāvalī*; (6) the *Rasāmṛta-śeṣa*; (7) the *Śrī Mādhava-mahotsava*, excellent in every part; (8) the book *Śrī Saṅkalpa-kalpavṛkṣa*; (9) the most wonderful *campū Bhāvārtha-sūcaka*; (10) a commentary on the *Gopāla-tāpanī*; (11) a commentary on the *Brahma-saṁhitā*; (12) a commentary on the *Rasāmṛta* (13) and on the *Śrī Ujjvala*; (14) a commentary on the *Yogasāra-stava*, in which everything is well reconciled; (15) a commentary on the Śrī Gāyatrī of the *Agni Purāṇa*; (16) the marks on Śrī Kṛṣṇa's feet according to the *Padma Purāṇa*; (17) separately, the marks on Śrī Rādhikā's hands and feet; (18) the *Gopāla-campū* in two parts, *Pūrva* and *Uttara* — what a wonder he described, the whole world knows; (19–25) the seven famous *Sandarbhas*, which follow the *Bhāgavatam*: the *Krama-*, *Tattva-*, *Bhagavat-*, *Paramātma-*, *Kṛṣṇa-*, *Bhakti-* and *Prīti-sandarbha*.”

After Śrī Rūpa, Sanātana and the others withdrew from view, he stood at the head of the Gauḍīya Vaiṣṇava *sampradāya* as its foremost ācārya in Gauḍa, Utkala and Māthura-maṇḍala. He proclaimed to everyone the truth that Śrī Gaurasundara had preached and brought them to the worship of Hari. From time to time he walked around Vraja-dhāma with the devotees and went to Mathurā to see Viṭṭhala-deva. Śrīla Kavirāja Gosvāmī wrote the *Caitanya-caritāmṛta* while Śrī Jīva was still present. Some time later Śrī Jīva gave Śrīnivāsa, Narottama and Duḥkhī Kṛṣṇadāsa, who had come from Gauḍa, the names “Ācārya,” “Ṭhākura” and “Śyāmānanda,” and sent them to Gauḍa with all the books of the Gosvāmīs to preach the holy name and *prema*. First he heard the news that the books had been stolen, and later the news that they had been recovered. He gave the name “Kavirāja” to Śrīnivāsa's disciple Rāmacandra Sena and to Rāmacandra's younger brother Govinda. While he was still present, Śrīla Jāhnavā Devī came to Vṛndāvana with a few devotees. When devotees arrived from Gauḍa, he arranged *prasāda* for them and showed them where to stay. His disciple Śrī Kṛṣṇadāsa Adhikārī gave a list of the books of Śrī Rūpa, Sanātana and Jīva in his own book.

In the ignorant *sampradāya* of the *prākṛta-sahajiyās* three slanders against Śrī Jīva Gosvāmī Prabhu are current. The *sahajiyās* have turned away from Kṛṣṇa, and these slanders grow from enmity toward Hari, guru and Vaiṣṇavas; by spreading them they only increase their offenses.

(1) A *digvijayī paṇḍita* who hungered for worldly fame obtained from the selfless Śrī Rūpa and Sanātana a written admission of defeat, and so declared Śrī Jīva's gurus (Śrī Rūpa and Sanātana) to be ignorant; then he demanded the same document from Śrī Jīva. Hearing this, Śrī Jīva Prabhu defeated the *digvijayī* and silenced the tongue of the guru's slanderer. In this way he showed that he honored even the luster of the nails on his Gurudeva's feet, and gave the example of a true disciple, for whom the guru is his deity and his very self[^85-1]. These *sahajiyās* say that such conduct contradicted the humility of one “lower than a blade of grass” and the duty to honor others; therefore Śrī Rūpa Gosvāmī Prabhu rebuked him sharply and sent him away, and later, at a sign from Śrī Sanātana Gosvāmī Prabhu, accepted Śrī Jīva Prabhu again.

On the day when, by Kṛṣṇa's mercy, these opponents of guru and Vaiṣṇavas understand that they are eternal servants of guru and Vaiṣṇavas, they will receive the mercy of Śrī Jīva Prabhu. Then they will truly become “lower than a blade of grass” and “giving honor to others,” and will become qualified for the *kīrtana* of the holy name.

(2) Some ignorant people say: Śrī Jīva saw how beautifully Kavirāja Gosvāmī Prabhu had written the *Caritāmṛta* and how it revealed the glory of the transcendental *rasa* of Vraja. He feared for his own fame, envy arose in him, and he threw the original *Caritāmṛta* into a well. Hearing of this, Kavirāja Gosvāmī gave up his body. And the *Caritāmṛta*, they say, came to light again only because his disciple named “Mukunda” had earlier copied the original manuscript; otherwise the book would have vanished from the world.

Such fabrications, born of hatred for Vaiṣṇavas, are utterly false and absurd.

(3) Certain other libertines, devoted to gratifying their senses, say: Śrī Jīva Prabhu did not accept the *parakīyā-rasa* of the *gopīs* of Vraja, which Śrī Rūpa Gosvāmī accepts, and approved the *svakīyā-rasa*; therefore he was not a *rasika* devotee, and his example is not to be followed.

During his lifetime Śrī Jīva saw that some devotees among his followers had a taste for *svakīyā-rasa*. For their good he took their qualification (*adhikāra*) into account. He also feared that unqualified people would fail to grasp the beauty and glory of the transcendental, supreme and most wonderful *parakīyā-rasa* of Vraja, would try to imitate it themselves and so fall into debauchery. For this reason Śrī Jīva Prabhu, the ācārya of the Vaiṣṇavas, accepted the *svakīyā* doctrine. But one must not conclude from this that he opposed the transcendental *parakīyā-rasa* of Vraja, for he himself is the foremost follower of Śrī Rūpa and one of the *śikṣā-gurus* of Śrīla Kavirāja Gosvāmī himself.

> Subheading: (45) Śrī Raghunātha dāsa —

Raghunātha dāsa was born in about 1416 Śaka in the village of “Śrī Kṛṣṇapura” in Hugalī district, in a family of Kāyasthas (by birth), in the house of Govardhana, the younger brother of Hiraṇya Majumadāra.

Śrī Kṛṣṇapura, where Śrīla Raghunātha dāsa Gosvāmī Prabhu appeared, lies a little more than a mile from Saptagrāma and about one and a half miles from Triśabighā station. There resides the Deity of Śrī Rādhā-Govinda, said to have been installed by Śrī Govardhana dāsa, the father of Śrīla Dāsa Gosvāmī Prabhu in his former *āśrama*. In front of the temple is a wide courtyard; there is no *nāṭya-mandira*, only a *jagamohana*. A year ago Śrīyukta Haricaraṇa Ghoṣa of Simlā, Calcutta, had the temple repaired. The temple courtyard is enclosed by a wall. In a small room next to the one where the Deity resides, a low stone seat (one and a half cubits long, one and a quarter cubits wide and three quarters of a cubit high) is shown as the “seat of worship” (*bhajanāsana*) of Śrīla Dāsa Gosvāmī Prabhu. Tradition says that Śrīla Dāsa Gosvāmī Prabhu sat on it and performed his worship. Beside the temple lies the Sarasvatī river, shallow and without current, thin and faded.

His forefathers were nearly Vaiṣṇavas and very wealthy. His *dīkṣā-guru* was Yadunandana Ācārya. Soon after entering family life he sought the shelter of Śrī Mahāprabhu. A little later, in 1439 Śaka, he seized an opportunity, left home for Puruṣottama, attained the cool lotus feet of Śrī Caitanya-deva and remained under the guidance of Dāmodara Svarūpa. He stayed there sixteen years; after the Lord withdrew from view, he came to Śrī Vṛndāvana and lived near Śrī Rūpa and Sanātana. *Bhakti-ratnākara* (first wave): “The three books of Raghunātha dāsa Gosvāmī: the *Stava-mālā*, which is called the *Stavāvalī*, the *Śrī Dāna-carita* and the sweet *Mukta-carita*.”

He lived a very long life. Before Śrīnivāsa Ācārya returned from Vṛndāvana with the books, he received the mercy of Śrī Dāsa Gosvāmī, as the same book says in the sixth wave: “His body was extremely emaciated, weaker at every moment; he ate a little once in two or four days. Day and night he knew nothing but chanting the holy name; there was no sleep in his eyes, and streams of tears flowed from both. On seeing Śrī Dāsa Gosvāmī, Śrīnivāsa thought himself fortunate and fell at his feet. Śrī Dāsa Gosvāmī embraced Śrīnivāsa. Śrīnivāsa told him that he was going to Śrī Gauḍa. Hearing this, Śrī Gosvāmī gave his permission with his own lips.” This took place after 1512 Śaka. The *Gaura-gaṇoddeśa* (verse 186) says: “The former name of Dāsa Śrī Raghunātha is Rasa-mañjarī. Some call him Śrīmatī Rati-mañjarī, and some call him Bhānumatī; the difference is only in the name.”

## 105

> Subheading: (46) The branch of Śrī Gopāla Bhaṭṭa —

Śrī Gopāla Bhaṭṭa was the son of Veṅkaṭa Bhaṭṭa, a resident of Raṅga-kṣetra, and the disciple of Prabodhānanda (formerly a follower of Rāmānuja, later a Gauḍīya). In 1433 Śaka, when Mahāprabhu stayed for the Cāturmāsya vow in Śrī Raṅga-kṣetra in the house of Veṅkaṭa Bhaṭṭa of the Śrī-sampradāya, Gopāla Bhaṭṭa served the Lord with all his heart. He received initiation from his paternal uncle, the *tridaṇḍī* Śrīmat Prabodhānanda Sarasvatī, as the *Hari-bhakti-vilāsa* (first *vilāsa*, verse 2) says: “Gopāla Bhaṭṭa, the disciple of Prabodhānanda, who is dear to the Lord, gathers the pastimes (*vilāsas*) of devotion to please Raghunātha dāsa, Rūpa and Sanātana.” *Bhakti-ratnākara* (first wave): “Gopāla's mother and father were most fortunate: they gave their minds and lives to the feet of Śrī Caitanya. They told their son to go to Vṛndāvana, and both departed remembering the Lord. After some time Gopāla went to Vṛndāvana and met Rūpa and Sanātana. *** (To the Lord in Nīlācala) Śrī Rūpa and Sanātana wrote in a letter that Gopāla Bhaṭṭa had come to Vṛndāvana. (The Lord) rejoiced at Gopāla's coming and writes in a letter to His dear Rūpa and Sanātana: ‘Know Gopāla Bhaṭṭa to be your own brother.’ *** In Gopāla's name Śrī Gosvāmī Sanātana composed the *Śrī Hari-bhakti-vilāsa*. Śrī Rūpa Gosvāmī loved him as his own life and entrusted him with the service of Śrī Rādhā-ramaṇa. *** (To Kavirāja Gosvāmī) Śrī Gopāla Bhaṭṭa gladly gave his order, but forbade him to write about him in the book. Kavirāja could not disobey his order: he writes only his name and makes nothing else known. He always considered himself most lowly.” — “All this I heard from the mouths of the elders” (the words of the author, Ghanaśyāma dāsa).

At the beginning of the *Tattva-sandarbha*, the first of the *Ṣaṭ-sandarbha* (after the obeisances to Śrī Rūpa and Sanātana), it is said: “A certain Bhaṭṭa, their friend, born in a family of southern brāhmaṇas, wrote a book after sifting what the elder Vaiṣṇavas had written. His original draft ran in order in some places, out of order in others, and in fragments in yet others. Having examined it and arranged it in order, the insignificant Jīva writes.” That is: Śrī Gopāla Bhaṭṭa, the dear friend of the two masters Śrī Rūpa and Sanātana, a southerner born in a twice-born family, gathered arguments and other material from the books of the ancient Vaiṣṇava ācāryas — Śrī Madhva, Śrī Rāmānuja, Śrīdhara Svāmī and others — and wrote a book. What he wrote there in order in some places, out of order — that is, breaking the sequence — in others, and in fragments in yet others, I, a tiny *jīva*, have examined and now set down properly and in order. The same words appear at the beginning of the *Bhagavat-sandarbha* and the other *Sandarbhas*. Gopāla Bhaṭṭa is the author of the *Sat-kriyā-sāra-dīpikā*, the compiler of the *Hari-bhakti-vilāsa* and the first writer of the *Ṣaṭ-sandarbha*. “He wrote a commentary on the *Kṛṣṇa-karṇāmṛta*; hearing it, the Vaiṣṇavas feel the highest joy.” He installed the Deity of Śrī Rādhā-ramaṇa. The *Gaura-gaṇoddeśa* (verse 184) says: “She who was Anaṅga-mañjarī is now Gopāla Bhaṭṭa. Some call Bhaṭṭa Gosvāmī Śrī Guṇa-mañjarī.” Śrīnivāsa Ācārya and Gopīnātha Pūjārī were his disciples.

Mukunda (Mukunda Sañjaya). Viśvambhara kept His school in Mukunda's house, and his son Puruṣottama was a student of the Lord.

Kāśīnātha was the brāhmaṇa *paṇḍita* who arranged Viśvambhara's wedding. He advised Rāja-paṇḍita Sanātana to give his daughter Viṣṇupriyā-devī in marriage to the Lord. The *Gaura-gaṇoddeśa* (verse 50) says: “The brāhmaṇa whom Satrājit sent to Mādhava to arrange the marriage of Satyā — that matchmaker is now Śrī Kāśīnātha.”

Rudra. The *Gaura-gaṇoddeśa* (verse 135) says: “Kṛṣṇacandra's friend in Vraja named Varūthapa is now Rudra Paṇḍita, dear to Gaurāṅga.”

Vallabhapura lies a mile north of Māheśa, the *śrīpāṭa* of Kamalākara Pippalāi. Here, in a large temple, resides Śrī Rādhā-vallabha Jiu, installed by Śrī Rudrarāma Paṇḍita, the son of Kāśīśvara Gosvāmī's sister. The present servitors of Śrī Rādhā-vallabha Jiu are the “Cakravartīs,” descendants of Yadunandana Bandyopādhyāya Mahāśaya, the younger brother of Rudrarāma Paṇḍita. Formerly, at the time of the Ratha-yātrā, Śrī Jagannātha-deva used to come from Māheśa to the temple of Śrī Rādhā-vallabha Jiu at Vallabhapura, but from the (Bengali) year 1262 the custom was discontinued because of quarrels among the servitors of that Deity.

## 107

> Subheading: (48) Śrīnātha Paṇḍita, who received the mercy of Śrīman Mahāprabhu, a wonderful example of service to Śrī Kṛṣṇa —

Śrīnātha Paṇḍita. The *Gaura-gaṇoddeśa* (verse 211) says: “He who skillfully expounded the *Bhāgavata-saṁhitā*, and whose glory is Kṛṣṇa-deva, who resides in Kumārahaṭṭa.”

About one and a half miles from Kumārahaṭṭa, at Kāṁcaḍāpāḍā, is the place of Śivānanda Sena. There, in a very large temple, reside the Deity “Śrī Gaura-Gopāla,” installed by Śivānanda, and the Deity of Śrī Rādhā-Kṛṣṇa named “Śrī Kṛṣṇa-rāya,” installed by the brāhmaṇa Śrīnātha. In front of the temple are a large courtyard, a kitchen for cooking the offerings, a guesthouse and so on. The courtyard is enclosed by a high wall. This temple is larger even than the temple at Māheśa. The present temple was built in 1708 Śaka. In front of the temple a verse in the *anuṣṭubh* meter is engraved, giving the names of the temple's founder, his father and his grandfather, and the date. The temple was built by a wealthy man named Nimāi Mallika of Pāthuriyāghāṭā, Calcutta, now deceased. Śrīnātha Paṇḍita was a disciple of Śrī Advaitācārya; Śivānanda's third son, Paramānanda Kavi Karṇapūra, the author of the *Gaura-gaṇoddeśa-dīpikā*, received the mercy of Śrīnātha Paṇḍita. The Deity Śrī Kṛṣṇa-rāya probably manifested in the time of Kavi Karṇapūra. Tradition says that Vīrabhadra Prabhu brought a large and beautiful stone from Murśidābāda, and from it three Deities manifested: Śrī Rādhā-vallabha of Vallabhapura, Śrī Śyāmasundara of Khaḍadaha and Śrī Kṛṣṇa-rāya of Kāṁcaḍāpāḍā.

The old place of Śivānanda Sena is on the bank of the Gaṅgā; a small, almost ruined temple stood there. It is said that Nimāi Mallika was traveling to Kāśī, went ashore at this place, saw with his own eyes the ruined state of Śrī Kṛṣṇa-rāya's temple and built the present large temple.

> Subheading: (72) Rāmadāsa, (73) Kavidatta, (74) Gopāla dāsa, (75) Raghunātha, (76) Śārṅga Ṭhākura —

Śrī Gopāla dāsa. The *Gaura-gaṇoddeśa* (verse 158) says: “Those who were formerly Śrī Tārakā and Pālī in Vraja-maṇḍala are now Jagannātha and Śrī Gopāla, dear to the Lord.”

Bhāgavatācārya. The *Gaura-gaṇoddeśa* (verse 203) says: “Śrīmad Bhāgavatācārya, who wrote the book *Kṛṣṇa-prema-taraṅgiṇī*, is extremely dear to Gaurāṅga.” *Caitanya-bhāgavata* (Antya, Chapter 5): “Then the Lord came to Varāhanagara, to the house of a most fortunate brāhmaṇa. That brāhmaṇa was thoroughly learned in the *Bhāgavata*. Seeing the Lord, he began to recite the *Bhāgavata*. Hearing him recite on *bhakti-yoga*, Gauracandra Nārāyaṇa was overwhelmed with ecstasy. The Lord said: ‘Never have I heard anyone else recite the *Bhāgavata* like this. Therefore your name is “Bhāgavatācārya.” Do no other work but this.’” His name is said to have been “Raghunātha.” His *pāṭa* is at Mālipāḍā in Varāhanagara (about three and a half miles north of Calcutta), on the bank of the Gaṅgā. The present servitor of this dilapidated *pāṭa* is Dhīrendra Gāṅguli, the son of the late Rājacandra Gāṅguli.

Ṭhākura Sāraṅga dāsa: his other name is Śārṅga Ṭhākura. Some also call him Śārṅgapāṇi and Śārṅgadhara. He lived in Modadrumadvīpa within Śrī Navadvīpa and worshipped in solitude on the bank of the Gaṅgā. The Lord urged him again and again to accept a disciple, and at last he resolved: “Whomever I meet tomorrow at dawn, him I will accept as my disciple.” The next day at dawn, as he bathed in the Bhāgīrathī, a dead body drifted against his feet; he brought it back to life and accepted it as his disciple. That disciple became famous by the name “Śrī Ṭhākura Murāri.” His followers, in the line of descent, now live in a village called “Śar.” The story of Murāri is bound up with the name of Śrī Śārṅga: even now the name “Śārṅga-Murāri” is heard everywhere.

Today in the village of Māmagāchi there is an old service of Śārṅga Ṭhākura. Recently a temple of Śrī Ṭhākura was built in front of an old *bakula* tree. One wishes that the service there were arranged even better.

The *Gaura-gaṇoddeśa* (verse 172) says: “She who in Vraja was Nāndīmukhī is now Sāraṅga Ṭhākura. Some consider him to be Prahlāda, but my father does not think so.”

Vāṇīnātha. The *Gaura-gaṇoddeśa* (verse 204) says: “The brāhmaṇa Vāṇīnātha, who lived in Campāhaṭṭa, is dear to the Lord.” In Vraja he is Kāmalekhā. Campāhaṭṭa, or Cāṁpāhāṭi, is a small, little-known village in Barddhamāna district, under Pūrvasthalī police station and Samudragaḍa post office. The servitors of the Śrī Caitanya Maṭha at Śrī Māyāpura in ancient Navadvīpa, headed by Śrī Paramānanda Brahmacārī, saw how disordered and neglected the service at this ancient *śrīpāṭa* had become. In the Bengali year 1328 they restored the *pāṭa* and built a new temple. In it the two life-sized Deities installed by Śrī Vāṇīnātha, Śrī Gaura and Gadādhara, delightful to the eyes and the mind, are worshipped according to scripture. The temple of Śrī Gaura-Gadādhara is at Cāṁpāhāṭi, two miles from Samudragaḍa, or Navadvīpa, station on the E.I.R. line.

## 130

> Subheading: (3) The branch of Sārvabhauma, (4) Gopīnāthācārya —

Sārvabhauma Bhaṭṭācārya's name was “Vāsudeva.” He was the son of Maheśvara Viśārada, a well-known resident of the village “Vidyānagara,” two and a half miles from present-day Navadvīpa, or Cāṁpāhāṭī. It is said that he learned the entire *nyāya-śāstra* by heart from Pakṣadhara Miśra, the chief teacher of the famous school of logic in Mithilā and the foremost logician of India in those days; then he founded a school of logic in Navadvīpa and began to teach. In the history of *nyāya-śāstra* this marked a turning of the ages: from then on Navadvīpa eclipsed Mithilā, and even today it is known throughout India as the foremost seat of the study of logic. According to some, the famous logician Raghunātha Śiromaṇi, author of the *Dīdhiti*, was his student. However that may be, Sārvabhauma gained profound learning in logic and Vedānta and, while remaining in the householder *āśrama*, accepted *kṣetra-sannyāsa* and taught Vedānta in Nīlācala. He made Mahāprabhu hear Vedānta as interpreted in Śaṅkara's commentary, and afterwards learned the true meaning of Vedānta from the Lord. He saw the Lord's six-armed form. His *Caitanya-śataka* reveals devotion to Gaura; in particular the two verses beginning *vairāgya-vidyā-nija-bhakti-yoga* are the summit of Sārvabhauma's learning. For the Lord's meeting with Sārvabhauma, their discussion and his deliverance, see Madhya, Chapter 6. The *Gaura-gaṇoddeśa* (verse 119) says: “Bhaṭṭācārya Sārvabhauma was formerly Gīṣpati (Bṛhaspati) in heaven.”

Gopīnātha Ācārya was a brāhmaṇa of Navadvīpa and a companion of the Lord. He was the husband of Sārvabhauma's sister. The *Gaura-gaṇoddeśa* (verse 178) says: “She who was formerly in Vraja the *prāṇa-sakhī* named Ratnāvalī is now the ācārya named Gopīnātha, renowned for his purity.” According to some, he is Brahmā. The *Gaura-gaṇoddeśa* (verse 75) says: “Brahmā, the lord of the universe, is to be known by the name Gopīnāthācārya; those who know the Tantras count him among the nine *vyūhas* in the Tantra.”

For this see Antya, Chapter 5.

Bhavānanda Rāya was the father of Śrī Rāmānanda Rāya. He lived near Brahmagiri, or Ālālanātha, six *krośas* west of Purī. By caste he was a Karaṇa (by birth). For his five sons see verse 133. He was formerly known as “King Pāṇḍu.”

## 133

> Subheading: (8) The five brothers headed by Śrī Rāya Rāmānanda —

## 134

Rāmānanda Rāya. The *Gaura-gaṇoddeśa* (verses 120–124) says: “A certain Arjuna, a *priya-narma-sakha* (an intimate friend of Kṛṣṇa), and Arjuna the Pāṇḍava together became Rāmānanda Rāya, dear to the Lord. Therefore the expert Rāmānanda described to Gauracandra day after day the truths of devotion and love for Rādhā and Kṛṣṇa and so on. Some call him Lalitā, but others do not accept this[^134-1]. Gaura said to Bhavānanda: ‘You are the husband of Pṛthā.’ Therefore the best authorities say that Arjuna the Pāṇḍava, having become one with the *gopī* Arjunīyā, is Rāya Rāmānanda. That both Arjunīyā and Arjuna the Pāṇḍava were fully present in him is clearly stated in the *Uttara-khaṇḍa* of the *Padma Purāṇa*. Therefore Rāmānanda Rāya Mahāśaya is all three.” According to some, he is Viśākhā-devī (see Madhya, Chapter 8, Antya, Chapter 5 and elsewhere). His place among the intimate devotees is very high. The Lord's words: “I am a *sannyāsī* and consider Myself detached. Yet, let alone seeing a woman, if I merely hear a woman's name, My body and mind are disturbed. Who can remain steady on seeing a woman? … His body and mind are unmoved, like wood or stone; how wonderful that his mind stays unmoved at the touch of young women! Only Rāmānanda has this qualification. From this I know that his body is transcendental. Only he knows the state of his mind; there is no one else able to know it. ***” (*Caitanya-caritāmṛta*, Antya 5.35–36, 41–43) “Though a householder, Rāya is not under the sway of the six enemies; though engaged in worldly affairs, he instructs *sannyāsīs*.” (Antya 5.80)

With Śrī Svarūpa and with him, Mahāprabhu in His final pastimes constantly tasted the many varieties of Rādhā's *mahābhāva* in separation from Śrī Kṛṣṇa (Madhya, Chapter 2, verse 77); the Lord was won over by his pure friendship (*sakhya*) (ibid., 78). Sārvabhauma's words (Madhya, Chapter 7): “Rāmānanda Rāya lives on the bank of the Godāvarī; he is the governor in Vidyānagara. … On earth there is no *rasika* devotee equal to him. He is the very limit of both learning and *bhakti-rasa*.” (Madhya 7.62, 64–65)

See further: the Lord's meeting with Śrī Rāmānanda Rāya; the Lord has Rāya speak on the truths of the goal and the means (*sādhya* and *sādhana*) and listens to him; the Lord shows Rāya His form as Rasarāja and Mahābhāva; the Lord's words: “I am one madman and you are a second madman; therefore you and I are equal” (Madhya 8.290); the Lord tells Rāma Rāya to give up his government service and go to Śrī Kṣetra (Madhya, Chapter 8). After the Lord's preaching in the South — His second meeting with Rāma Rāya; Rāya sends the Lord on to Nīlācala (Madhya, Chapter 9, verses 319–335) and later meets the Lord in Nīlācala (Madhya, Chapter 11, verse 15). Rāya's efforts to obtain the Lord's mercy for King Pratāparudra (Madhya, Chapter 12, verses 41–57); water sports with Sārvabhauma after the *kīrtana* on the day of the Ratha-yātrā (Madhya, Chapter 14, verse 82). Rāya is unwilling to let the Lord go to Vṛndāvana (Chapter 16, verses 10 and 85) but at last, yielding to the Lord's request, consents; the Lord's meeting with the king at Kaṭaka (ibid., 105); the Lord takes leave of Rāya at Remuṇā (ibid., 153); instead of going to Vṛndāvana the Lord returns to Nīlācala by way of Gauḍa and meets Rāya (ibid., 254). His discussion of *rasa* with Śrī Rūpa and his praise of Rūpa's poetry (Antya, Chapter 1, verses 115–196); Rāma Rāya's renunciation equals Sanātana's (ibid., 201). He is one of the “three and a half” vessels of the *rasa* of love for Śrī Rādhā and Kṛṣṇa (Antya, Chapter 2, verse 106); his meeting with Sanātana (Antya, Chapter 4, verse 110); Rāya speaks about Kṛṣṇa to Pradyumna Miśra, whom the Lord sent to him; the Lord praises Rāya (Antya, Chapter 5, verses 4–85). “As Subala formerly helped Kṛṣṇa's happiness, so Rāma Rāya is there to give happiness to Gaura.” (Antya 6.9) “The power of Rāmānanda cannot be described. By Rāya's grace I came to know the pure mood of Vraja.” (Antya 7.37) “Rāmānanda Rāya is the treasury of the *rasa* of Kṛṣṇa; he made Me understand that Kṛṣṇa is Bhagavān Himself.” (Antya 7.23) In the final pastimes the Lord, with Rāma Rāya and Svarūpa, tasted the *rasa* of songs and laments of separation from Kṛṣṇa (Antya, Chapters 14–20). Rāmānanda Rāya wrote the drama *Śrī Jagannātha-vallabha*.

## 135

> Subheading: (9) King Pratāparudra, (10) Kṛṣṇānanda, (11) Paramānanda Mahāpātra, (12) Śivānanda —

Pratāparudra was the emperor of Utkala, of the Gaṅgā dynasty (a Gajapati). His capital was Kaṭaka. Having heard of Mahāprabhu's qualities, he served Him long in humble dress and longed for Him, and then, with the help of Rāma Rāya and Sārvabhauma, received His mercy. The *Gaura-gaṇoddeśa* (verse 118) says: “The great king Indradyumna, who formerly worshipped Jagannātha, has now been born as Pratāparudra, equal to Indra.” At his wish Karṇapūra wrote the drama *Caitanya-candrodaya*.

Paramānanda Mahāpātra. *Caitanya-bhāgavata* (Antya, Chapter 5): “All the followers born in Utkala recognized the Lord of their lives. Śrī Paramānanda Mahāpātra Mahāśaya — Śrī Caitanya is his very body and life; he is full of *bhakti-rasa*.”

## 136

> Subheading: (13) Bhagavān Ācārya, (14) Brahmānanda Bhāratī, (15) Śikhi and (16) Murāri Māhiti —

Bhagavān Ācārya lived in Hālisahara; his son's name was Raghunātha (*Bhakti-ratnākara*). He was lame. Madhya 10.184: “*** and Bhagavān Ācārya: both stayed at the Lord's feet, giving up all their work.” Antya, Chapter 2: “In Puruṣottama, near the Lord, was Bhagavān Ācārya, a supreme Vaiṣṇava, very learned and noble. His heart was filled with the mood of friendship; he was an incarnation of a cowherd boy, and he dealt with Svarūpa Gosāñi as a friend. He had taken shelter of Caitanya's feet with exclusive devotion, and from time to time he invited the Lord for a meal.” (Antya 2.84–86) It was in his house, when the Lord came for a meal, that it came out that Choṭa Haridāsa had begged fine rice from Mādhavī-devī for the Lord and so had spoken with a woman; for this Mahāprabhu rejected him (see Antya, Chapter 2, verses 101–166). Bhagavān Ācārya was very generous and guileless. His father, Śatānanda Khān, was a thoroughly worldly man, and his younger brother, Gopāla Bhaṭṭācārya, was a Māyāvādī. Gopāla studied the Māyāvāda commentary in Kāśī and came to his elder brother in Purī; out of affection Bhagavān wanted to hear Māyāvāda from him, but Śrī Svarūpa stopped him, saying that it is hostile to devotion (Antya, Chapter 2, verses 89–100). One day a Bengali poet, a mere versifier whom Bhagavān had known before, composed a drama contrary to the teachings of devotion, brought it, stayed at his lodging and wanted to recite it to the Lord. Śrī Svarūpa had doubts, but Bhagavān begged him to hear the drama. As soon as the poet recited the *nāndī* verse, Śrī Svarūpa showed how much in the drama contradicted the teachings of devotion. In the end the poet took shelter at the feet of the devotees (Antya, Chapter 5, verses 91–166). See *Caitanya-bhāgavata*, Antya, Chapter 3. The *Gaura-gaṇoddeśa* (verse 74) says: “The lame Ācārya Bhagavān is said to be a *kalā* (portion) of Gaura.”

Śikhi Māhiti. The *Gaura-gaṇoddeśa* (verse 189) says: “Rāgalekhā and Kalākeli, maidservants of Rādhā, are now known as Śikhi Māhāti and his sister Mādhavī, respectively.” Both he and his sister were intimate and qualified devotees of the Lord from Utkala. The *Caitanya-carita-mahākāvya* (canto 13, 89–109) tells of them. In Puruṣottama-kṣetra lived a great soul named Śikhi Māhiti (Mahānti), pure-hearted and compassionate. He was a servant of Śrī Jagannātha, the ornament of Nīlācala. His younger brother was named Murāri Māhiti, and his younger sister was the pure-minded Mādhavī-devī. The younger brother and sister were both devoted to Gaurasundara: their inborn, steady and auspicious intelligence never allowed them to forget Śrī Gaurasundara. Now Vṛndāvanacandra Kṛṣṇa has risen on this earth in the form of Gauracandra and constantly showers this brother and sister with Gaura's affection. They tried their utmost to bring their elder brother Śikhi Māhiti, the loving servant of Jagannātha, the Lord of Nīlācala, to the worship of Śrī Gaurasundara, but Śikhi Māhiti would not take up the worship of Gaura at all.

One day, after hearing his siblings' advice and reflecting on it long and hard, he fell asleep, and toward dawn he started up: he had dreamed that his younger siblings, who see Gaura's lotus feet, were waking him. At this wonderful dream his hair stood on end; doubly amazed and overjoyed, he slowly opened his tear-filled eyes and saw his brother and sister. Awake now and seeing those great younger ones beside him, he embraced them with great joy. They were all astonished. Śikhi Māhiti began to tell them: “Brother, listen to the dream I have had — it is most wonderful. Only today have I become convinced that the glory of Śrī Śacī's son is immeasurable. I saw Gaurasundara looking at the Lord of Nīlācala, entering into Him moment after moment, coming out again and again and looking at Him once more — such is the pastime He was displaying. How wonderful! Even now I see the Supreme Lord Gaurasundara in that very state — are my eyes deceiving me? Ah, that ocean of boundless mercy, Gaurasundara, saw me near Jagannātha-deva, called me by name and, as it were, embraced me with His long, raised, graceful arms.” So, with his hair standing on end, tears in his eyes and his voice choked with love, Śikhi spoke these words and went out. Murāri and Mādhavī, hearing their elder brother's words, asked him to go to Jagannātha for the Lord's *darśana*. Then all three agreed and went for the *darśana* of the Lord of Nīlācala. Murāri and Mādhavī saw the Lord in the *jagamohana* and shed tears of joy, but the elder, Śikhi Māhiti, saw Gaurasundara on all sides exactly as he had seen Him in the dream, in the very same mood, and his heart blossomed with love. The supremely magnanimous Mahāprabhu said, “Are you Murāri's elder brother?” and embraced him with both arms; and Śikhi Māhiti, his mind absorbed in service to Gaura, felt immense happiness. From then on Śikhi Māhiti, intoxicated by the fragrance of Gaura's lotus feet, forgot everything else and served Śrī Gaura, the Lord of his heart.

Murāri Māhiti. Madhya 10.44: “This is Murāri Māhiti, the brother of Śikhi Māhiti. He has no refuge but Your feet.”

## 138

> Subheading: (18) Kāśīśvara, (19) Govinda —

Govinda was Mahāprabhu's personal servant. The *Gaura-gaṇoddeśa* (verse 137) says: “Those who were formerly the servants Bhṛṅgāra and Bhaṅgura in Vṛndāvana have now been born as Śrī Kāśīśvara and Govinda, the servants of the Lord.” For the Lord's meeting with Govinda, the disciple of Īśvara Purī, see Madhya, Chapter 10, verses 131–148. The Lord was won over “by the pure *dāsya-rasa* of Govinda and others” (Madhya, Chapter 2, verse 78). For the sake of the Lord's service Govinda did not hesitate to step over the Lord's body, but for his own sake he feared the offense in such an act: “Govinda said: ‘My service is my rule. Let there be offense, or even going to hell.’” (Antya 10.95) See Madhya, Chapter 10, verses 82–100[^138-1].

## 146

> Subheading: (23) Balabhadra Bhaṭṭa —

Balabhadra Bhaṭṭācārya is Madhurekṣaṇā of Vraja. Cooking and other practical work (*karma-kāṇḍa*) are forbidden to *sannyāsīs*; they accept such services from householders. *Sannyāsīs* are gurus, *brahmacārīs* are disciples. Balabhadra performed the duties of a *brahmacārī* for Mahāprabhu when the Lord went to Vṛndāvana.

## 154

> Subheading: The account of Śrī Raghunātha Bhaṭṭa —

## 153–158

Raghunātha Bhaṭṭācārya was one of the six Gosvāmīs and the son of Tapana Miśra. He was born in about 1425 Śaka. He was especially expert in the *Bhāgavata-śāstra*. Antya, Chapter 13, verses 107–108: “Raghunātha Bhaṭṭa was very skilled in cooking; whatever he cooked became like nectar. The Lord ate it with great satisfaction, and Bhaṭṭa ate the Lord's remnants. … After eight months the Lord sent Bhaṭṭa home, forbidding him, ‘Do not marry.’ ‘Go and serve your aged mother and father, and study the *Bhāgavata* with a Vaiṣṇava.’ … Saying this, He put His own neck-garland around Raghunātha's neck.” (Antya 13.107–108, 112–114) “For four years at home he served his father and mother and studied the *Bhāgavata* with a Vaiṣṇava scholar. When his parents passed away in Kāśī, he became indifferent to the world, left his home and other ties, and came to the Lord again.” (Antya 13.117–118) “‘By My order, Raghunātha, go to Vṛndāvana; there, stay with Rūpa and Sanātana. Read the *Bhāgavata* and always chant Kṛṣṇa's name; soon Kṛṣṇa, Bhagavān, will bestow His mercy.’ Saying this, the Lord embraced him, and by the Lord's mercy he became maddened with love for Kṛṣṇa. … a fourteen-cubit *tulasī* garland of Jagannātha — that garland and some betel the Lord gave him.” (Antya 13.120–124) “In Rūpa Gosāñi's assembly he recites the *Bhāgavata*; as he reads the *Bhāgavata* his mind is overwhelmed. … His voice is like the cuckoo's, and in it the *rāgas* are clearly distinguished; in reciting a single verse he changes three or four *rāgas*. … He had his disciple build a temple for Govinda, and he provided ornaments — a flute, *makara* earrings and others.” (Antya 13.126, 128, 131) “He does not hear worldly talk nor speak it with his tongue; his eight *praharas* pass in talk of Kṛṣṇa, worship and the like. He does not let blame of Vaiṣṇavas enter his ears; he knows only this: everyone is worshipping Kṛṣṇa.” (Antya 13.132–133) The *Gaura-gaṇoddeśa* (verse 185) says: “The Bhaṭṭa named Raghunātha, who was formerly Rāga-mañjarī, lived in a hut at Śrī Rādhikā-kuṇḍa.”
