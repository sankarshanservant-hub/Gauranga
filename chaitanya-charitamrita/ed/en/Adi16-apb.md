# Amṛta-pravāha-bhāṣya on Ādi-līlā 16

## 0

**Summary.** The sixteenth chapter describes Mahāprabhu's pastimes in His youth (*kaiśora*): He teaches, defeats the *paṇḍitas*, sports in the waters of the Jāhnavī, travels to Vaṅga (East Bengal) to gather funds, holds learned debates there and leads *nāma-saṅkīrtana*, meets Tapana Miśra, instructs him about *sādhya* and *sādhana*, and orders him to go to Vārāṇasī. While Mahāprabhu was conquering Vaṅga, Lakṣmī-devī, on the pretext of a snakebite, departed for Vaikuṇṭha. The Lord returned home and consoled Śacīdevī with knowledge of the truth (*tattva-jñāna*). Then He married Viṣṇupriyā. He spoke with the *digvijayī* (conqueror of all directions) Keśava Kāśmīrī, examined his verse on the glory of the Gaṅgā, showed in it five poetic ornaments and excellences and five faults, and crushed his pride. That night the *digvijayī* poet learned from Sarasvatī who the Lord truly is, and the next morning he surrendered to Him.

## 1

The river of the nectar of His mercy floods the whole world, yet it always appears to flow downward (*nīca-gā*). I worship that Lord Caitanya.

## 3

All glory to the youthful Caitanyadeva! Lakṣmī-devī in person, who had come to His home, worshipped Him, and the goddess of speech worshipped Him on the pretext of the *digvijayī*'s defeat.

## 6

The Lord defeated the *paṇḍitas* in every *śāstra*, but His humility was so skillful that the *paṇḍitas* were not hurt.

## 10

***Sādhya* and *sādhana*.** What one attains through *sādhana* is called *sādhya*, the goal. The means by which one attains the goal is called *sādhana*.

## 11–13

The *śāstras* are many, and what each of them defines as *sādhya* and as *sādhana* turns out to differ. Whoever sets out to read many *śāstras* cannot decide which *sādhya* is best and which *sādhana* is best, and his mind falls into confusion. Tapana Miśra's mind was confused in just this way, and so in a dream he was told to go to Nimāi Paṇḍita and become certain of *sādhya* and *sādhana* from Him. In the dream Miśra was also told: “Have no doubt: Nimāi Paṇḍita is the Lord (Īśvara) Himself.”

## 15

The Lord said: “Neither knowledge of Brahman as undifferentiated oneness nor enjoyment (*bhukti*) in heaven and other worlds is the goal (*sādhya*) of the *jīva*. The *jīva*'s only goal is love for Kṛṣṇa. *Karma* and *jñāna* are not the *sādhana*, the means of attaining that goal. The only means of attaining it is pure *bhakti* that takes shelter of Kṛṣṇa's name.”

## 19

“Giving the name”: that is, by giving Kṛṣṇa's name — “Hare Kṛṣṇa Hare Kṛṣṇa Kṛṣṇa Kṛṣṇa Hare Hare / Hare Rāma Hare Rāma Rāma Rāma Hare Hare” — the Lord made the people of Vaṅga devotees, and by teaching them the *śāstras* He made many of them *paṇḍitas*.

## 21

The pain of separation from the Lord took the form of a snake and bit Lakṣmī. Then she went to the “other world” (*para-loka*), that is, to her own abode Vaikuṇṭha, the best of all worlds.

## 23

“Telling the truth”: another reading has “with the net of truth.” The Lord cast a net of knowledge of the truth — “‘Who is whose husband, whose son…,’ that is, who is anyone's husband, who anyone's son, who anyone's wife” — and freed Śacī from her grief.

## 25

**The *digvijayī*** was a *paṇḍita* from Kashmir named Keśava Miśra. After being taught by Mahāprabhu he became an *ācārya* in the sampradāya of Śrī Nimbāditya and wrote a gloss (*ṭippanī*) on the commentaries Nimbāditya composed, the *Vedānta-pārijāta* and others.

## 32

“You teach the grammar called *Kalāpa*. And I have heard that your students hold discussions (*saṁlāpa*), that is, special conversations, about the *phāṅki* of grammar, that is, about intricate questions.”

## 36

“*Ghaṭī eke*”: within one *ghaṭikā*[^36-1].

## 37

“*Karila sat-kāra*”: He showed respect.

## 38

“*Kiṁbā*”: that is, *kibā*, “or.”

## 40

He asked which verse he should explain.

## 41

The glory of this Gaṅgā-devī always shines, because she is supremely blessed. She was born from the lotus feet of Śrī Viṣṇu, and, like a second form of Lakṣmī-devī, she is the one whose feet gods and men worship. Endowed with wonderful qualities, she has risen above Mahādeva, the husband of Bhavānī.

## 46

***Upamā-alaṅkāra***, the ornament of simile: to display a poetic excellence by giving a comparison (*upamā*). ***Anuprāsa***, alliteration: the art of sound shown in the last line by placing many *bh*'s close together.

## 48

The mental power to arrange speech in ever new ways is called *pratibhā*, poetic genius. By showing such a mind in this verse you have pleased even the gods[^48-1]; that is, your genius is richly displayed in these words. But if the verse is examined well, both its merits and its faults will come to light.

## 50

“*Vaiyākaraṇa*”: that is, a grammarian, expert (only) in the learning meant for children and unable to examine poetics (*alaṅkāra*) and other *śāstras*.

## 52

“I have not studied poetics, but I have heard about it from the lips of *paṇḍitas*. That is why I see many faults and merits in this verse.”

## 54–84

The verse “*Mahattvaṁ gaṅgāyāḥ*” has five ornaments (*alaṅkāra*), which are its merits, and five faults (*doṣa*). In two places the fault is *avimṛṣṭa-vidheyāṁśa* (the *vidheya*, the new statement, is not properly set apart), and in three more there are the faults *viruddha-mati* (creating a contradictory idea), *punarukti* (repetition) and *bhagna-krama* (broken order).

The first *avimṛṣṭa-vidheyāṁśa* fault is this. The main *vidheya* of the verse is the glory of the Gaṅgā, and the word *idam*, “this,” is the *anuvāda* (what is being spoken of, the already known). Here “the glory of the Gaṅgā” is written first and the word *idam* after it, and that is against the rules. If the *anuvāda*, the known subject, is not written first, the meaning suffers.

The second *avimṛṣṭa-vidheyāṁśa* fault is this. In the expression *dvitīya-śrī-lakṣmīr iva*, “like a second Śrī Lakṣmī,” the *vidheya*, the unknown, is “secondness” (*dvitīyatva*). It was placed first and made part of a compound (*samāsa*), and so the meaning became subordinate and was lost. For the point of the meaning was to show [the Gaṅgā's] equality with Lakṣmī, and the faulty compound destroyed it.

The third fault, *viruddha-mati-kṛt*, is seen in the word *bhavānī-bhartuḥ*, “of Bhavānī's husband.” In such usage the word *Bhavānī* means Mahādeva's wife, and the word *bhavānī-bhartā* gives rise to another idea, that of a second husband of Bhavānī. Such words taint poetry with the fault *viruddha-mati-kṛt*.

The fourth fault is this. The sentence ended with the verb *vibhavati*, “reigns,” and after it the epithet *adbhuta-guṇā*, “endowed with wonderful qualities,” is given. This is the fault *punar-ātta* (returning to a finished sentence).

The fifth fault is *bhagna-krama*. Three lines, the first, third and fourth, have alliteration on *t*, *r* and *bh*, but the second line has none. That is the fault *bhagna-krama*.

The verse has five ornaments and merits, yet these five faults have ruined it. If a verse has ten ornaments and even one fault, it is condemned (*vigīta*), like a beautiful body adorned with jewels but marked by a white patch of leprosy.

Now I will speak of the merits. This verse of yours has two ornaments of sound (*śabdālaṅkāra*) and three ornaments of sense (*arthālaṅkāra*). (1) The alliteration in three lines is an ornament of sound. (2) The expression “Śrī Lakṣmī” is not the fault of repetition (*punarukti*): it is the ornament of sound *punarukta-vad-ābhāsa*, apparent repetition. If “Śrī” and “Lakṣmī” are taken as one and the same, there is no fault at all. If the sense is taken as “Lakṣmī endowed with beauty (*śrī*),” the meanings do differ, and there is no repetition: it is a special ornament of sound, *punarukta-vad-ābhāsa*. (3) The expression *lakṣmīr iva*, “like Lakṣmī,” is an ornament of sense, the simile (*upamālaṅkāra*). (4) There is one more ornament of sense, *virodhābhāsa*, apparent contradiction, in what is said of the Gaṅgā's birth from Viṣṇu's lotus feet. A lotus is born from water, but here water is born from a lotus; such contradictory words give rise to the ornament of contradiction (*virodha*). But the Gaṅgā appeared by the inconceivable energy of the Lord, so there is no contradiction here at all, only an “apparent contradiction,” and that is the ornament. (5) The words that prove the *sādhya*, the glory of the Gaṅgā, that is, the words about her birth from Viṣṇu's feet, are the ornament *anumāna*, inference.

## 71

A beautiful, adorned body becomes unsightly when it has a patch of white leprosy (*śvitra*). In the same way poetry endowed with *rasa* and ornaments becomes unsightly if it has a fault.

## 82

A lotus is born only in water; water is never born from a lotus. But here is the wonder: in Śrī Kṛṣṇa the opposite is seen — from His lotus feet the great river Gaṅgā was born.

## 107

***Bandhana***, bondage: the bondage of *māyā*, that is, pride in learning[^107-1].

*Thus ends the sixteenth chapter of the “Amṛta-pravāha-bhāṣya.”*

[^36-1]: A *ghaṭikā* is twenty-four minutes. The verse translation reads otherwise: “in just one hour.”

[^48-1]: The verse translation reads otherwise: “is surely pleasing to God.”

[^107-1]: The verse translation reads otherwise: “freed him from the bondage of *karma*.”
