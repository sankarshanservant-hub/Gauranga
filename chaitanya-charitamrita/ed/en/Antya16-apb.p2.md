## 80

***Dalai*** — a doorkeeper.

## 87

“O friend, doorkeeper, where is Kṛṣṇa, the Lord of My life? Show Him to Me here, quickly!” Saying this to the doorkeeper like a madman, [the Lord] took him by the hand and hurried to see Kṛṣṇa.[^87-1] May such a Gaurāṅga rise in my heart and madden me.

## 88

*Vallabha-bhoga* is what in this region is called *bāla-bhoga*.

## 100

A pious act that gives rise to Kṛṣṇa's mercy is called *sukṛti* (*sukṛti* turned toward *bhakti*).

## 117

O hero! The nectar of Your lips increases love (*prema*)[^117-1] and destroys the grief of the world. The sounding flute kisses it beautifully, and it makes people forget their attachment to all that is not spiritual. Give us that nectar.

## 119

O friend! The nectar of His lips takes away from the incomparable noble ladies of Vraja their thirst for other tastes. A particle of His remnants (*phelā*) can be obtained by *sukṛti*. He chews rolls of betel that put nectar to shame. That Madana-mohana increases the longing of my tongue.

## 121–133

O *nāgara*, I am describing the nature of Your lips; listen.[^121-1] They agitate people's bodies and minds, increase the longing of love (*kandarpa-lobha*),[^121-2] and remove the burden of joy, grief and the like. They make one forget other tastes, bring the world under their sway, and destroy shame, duty (*dharma*) and composure. They intoxicate the minds of women and draw them by inflaming the craving of the tongue. Yet when I consider it, I see that with them everything is the other way around. O Kṛṣṇa, You are a man, and the nectar of Your lips ought to attract the minds of women: that is the rule. But it attracts the flute, which is male, makes it drink, and makes it forget all other tastes. To say nothing of conscious beings, it makes even the insentient conscious. Your lips, therefore, are a great magician.

See what else is the other way around. Your flute is mere dry wood, yet the nectar of Your lips makes it drink, gives it senses and a mind (endows it with the power of awareness), and gives it happiness. Like a bold man, this flute itself drinks (again and again) the lips of a man and proclaims its drinking, saying, “Hey, *gopīs*, if you take pride in being women, then drink your own wealth — the nectar of a man's lips.”[^121-3] Rādhikā says: “That flute, angry with me, says, ‘Give up shame and fear and drink it; then I will yield (these lips) to you. But if you do not give up shame and fear, I alone will drink without end. Seeing your special right to the nectar of Kṛṣṇa's lips, I am a little afraid of you; all the others I regard as straw.’”[^121-4] With its sound the flute conveys the nectar of the lips — that is, joined with it (in league with it, by force), it thus attracts the three worlds. If we *gopīs*, afraid of transgressing our duty, keep our composure, it humiliates us all the more. It even makes us give up shame and duty: in front of our elders it loosens our *nīvi*, that is, the sash; it drags us off as if by the hair and makes us Your maidservants, and people laugh when they hear of it (thus it leads the *gopīs* wherever it likes). The flute is no more than a stick of dry bamboo, yet (as if it were a master)[^121-5] it insults us and brings us to this state. What can we do but bear it? When a thief is punished, his mother cannot call out to people and cry aloud (for his rescue or for an impartial judgment): it is not for her to call out and cry, or crying is out of place. So I too keep silent.

Such is the way of the lips. Now hear about the evil custom of whatever meets with the lips. Food, dishes and drinks touched by those lips become like nectar and bear the name “Kṛṣṇa-phelā.” Even by worship the demigods do not obtain a particle of that *phelā*. And *phelā* is so proud that ordinary people cannot believe it: for only by the power of “*sukṛti*” — *sukṛti* turned toward *bhakti*, gained through the piety of many lives — does a servant obtain a particle, a grain, of Kṛṣṇa-phelā. The betel *prasāda* chewed by Kṛṣṇa is called “the essence of nectar,” and the mouths of the *gopīs* are like an *ālabāṭī*, that is, a spittoon, to keep it. Therefore, O Śyāma, give up this artful cleverness (Your tricks). Do not take the lives of the *gopīs* any longer with the flute. Do not, in jest, become a killer of women — give us the nectar of Your own lips.

## 133

**āpanāra hāsi lāgi'** — the first meaning: if You become a killer of women, You Yourself will be blamed; so do not do this — give us the nectar of Your lips. The second meaning: do not kill women for Your own amusement.

## 140

O *gopīs*, what *sukṛti* has this flute performed that it enjoys Kṛṣṇa's lip-nectar itself — nectar that is the *gopīs'* to obtain — and leaves only a remnant of its taste? Noble people (when they see that a great descendant, a devotee of the Lord, has been born in their family) shed tears of joy. In the same way the rivers (by whose water this flute was nourished) rejoice: the hair on their skin stands on end — that is, the blossoming lotuses on their surface. And all the trees of the same kind (as the tree from which it was born) shed tears (streams of honey, out of joy).

## 142–149

One *gopī* says to the others: “Look at this wondrous pastime of the son of the king of Vraja! He will surely marry the maidens of Vraja, and so the *gopīs* know that the nectar of Kṛṣṇa's lips is their own wealth and that others cannot have it. O *gopīs*, consider: in a previous birth this flute of Kṛṣṇa must have visited some holy place, performed some austerity, chanted some perfect *mantra*. By that it has made the nectar of Kṛṣṇa's lips, by which the *gopīs* live, its own ‘seal of nectar’ (*amṛta-mudrā*).[^142-1] This flute is utterly unworthy: it is immovable, of the bamboo kind, and on top of that ‘of the male kind’ — and yet it constantly drinks the nectar of Kṛṣṇa's lips. That wealth belongs to the *gopīs*, yet the flute drinks it by force without asking them, and loudly calls the *gopīs* to drink.[^142-2] See also the fruit of its austerity and the power of its fortune: even *mahājanas* eat its remnants. When Kṛṣṇa bathes in the world-purifying daughter of Kalinda and in Mānasa-gaṅgā, those *mahājanas* (the Yamunā and Mānasa-gaṅgā), overcome by longing, joyfully drink the taste of His lips that the flute has left. But what of the rivers! The trees on their banks are like ascetics, doing good to others. Why do they draw up through their roots the remnant of taste the river has enjoyed and drink it? I cannot understand. These trees thrill in their sprouts, blossom with the laughter of flowers, and in the guise of honey pour out streams of tears. It seems that, just as noble men are transformed with bliss when their sons and grandsons become ‘Vaiṣṇavas,’ the trees act in the same way, regarding the flute, which is of the tree kind, as their own kin. The point is this: the flute is utterly unworthy, but we are worthy women. If we could learn what austerity the flute performed, we would perform the same. What is in our minds is this: seeing the unworthy flute drink the nectar of Kṛṣṇa's lips, we are dying of grief. That is why we are pondering the flute's austerities.”

## 145

**mahā-jane** — Mānasa-gaṅgā and the Yamunā. They are “*mahājanas*” because they are holy rivers.

## 147

Though they are holy rivers, they are still rivers. Therefore such an act of theirs (that is, drinking the taste of Kṛṣṇa's lip-nectar left by the flute) is possible.

## 149

**e ayogya** — this flute is an immovable object, and so it is unworthy to obtain the nectar of Kṛṣṇa's lips.

*Thus ends the sixteenth chapter of the “Amṛta-pravāha-bhāṣya.”*
