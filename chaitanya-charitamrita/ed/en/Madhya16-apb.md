# Amṛta-pravāha-bhāṣya on Madhya-līlā 16

## 0

**Summary.** When Mahāprabhu wished to go to Vṛndāvana, Rāmānanda and Sārvabhauma raised all kinds of obstacles. Meanwhile, in the third year, the Gauḍīya devotees came to Nīlācala. This time the wives of the Vaiṣṇavas brought from Bengal many kinds of food that He loved, to invite Śrīman Mahāprabhu and feed Him. When they reached Śrī Kṣetra, Mahāprabhu honored them by sending them garlands. That year too the Guṇḍicā temple was washed and the other services were done as before. When the four months of Cāturmāsya had passed, the devotees began to leave for home. Mahāprabhu forbade Nityānanda Prabhu to come to Nīlācala every year. At the question of the residents of Kulīnagrāma He again described the marks of a Vaiṣṇava. That year Vidyānidhi stayed in Nīlācala and saw the Oḍana-ṣaṣṭhī festival. When the devotees had taken leave, Mahāprabhu showed His firm resolve to go to Vṛndāvana and set out on the day of Vijayā-daśamī. King Pratāparudra made many arrangements along Mahāprabhu's way. After they crossed the river Citrotpalā, Rāmānanda, Maṅgarāja (Mardarāja?) and Haricandana went along with Mahāprabhu. Mahāprabhu asked Gadādhara Paṇḍita to go back to Nīlācala, but he would not listen. From Kaṭaka Mahāprabhu bound the Paṇḍita Gosvāmī by an oath and sent him to Śrī Puruṣottama, and at Bhadraka He bade farewell to Rāmānanda. Then, reaching the border of the Oḍra country, He traveled by boat as far as Pāṇihāṭi with the help of a *yavana* governor. After that the Lord went from the house of Rāghava Paṇḍita by way of Kumārahaṭṭa to the village of Kuliyā and freed many people from their offenses. From there He went to Rāmakeli and accepted Śrī Rūpa and Śrī Sanātana. Turning back from Rāmakeli, He instructed Raghunātha dāsa and sent him home. Coming once more to Nīlācala, the Lord began to deliberate about going to Vṛndāvana alone.

## 1

In the garden of Gauḍa the cloud in the form of Gaura sprinkled the nectar of His glance on the creepers — the multitudes of people burned in the forest fire of worldly existence — and brought them back to life.

## 50

*Vāpī* means a well (an *indārā*?), that is, a reservoir.

## 55–56

*Caitanya-bhāgavata*, Antya-khaṇḍa, chapter 8. Once Śrī Advaita invited Mahāprabhu and thought, “If no other *sannyāsī* comes with the Lord, I shall feed the Lord well.” The other *sannyāsīs* had gone out for their midday rites, and just then a storm with rain arose. Since they could not come, the Lord came alone and ate Śrī Advaita's rice and vegetables.

## 60

*Tarjā* is speech in *payār* and similar meters that other people cannot easily understand.

## 62

What Śrī Advaitācārya prayed for in his *tarjā*, and what Śrī Śacīnandana's smile meant — this no one else could understand.

## 64–65

In Gauḍa, in Mahāprabhu's absence, no one but Śrī Prabhu Nityānanda can fulfill His purpose — to give the holy name and *prema* to everyone, down to the *caṇḍālas*.

## 66–67

Nityānanda said, “I am the body, You are the life; these two are never separate. But that we live apart — You in Nīlācala and I in Gauḍa — comes about only by Your inconceivable power.”[^66-67-1]



---

[^66-67-1]: The verse translation of 16.67 reads differently: by His inconceivable energies nothing is unattainable for the Lord, and nothing is impossible for Nityānanda, since the Lord Himself moves Him to act (Madhya 16.67).
