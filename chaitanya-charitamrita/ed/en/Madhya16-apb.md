# Amṛta-pravāha-bhāṣya on Madhya-līlā 16

## 0

**Summary.** When Mahāprabhu wished to go to Vṛndāvana, Rāmānanda and Sārvabhauma raised all kinds of obstacles. Meanwhile, in the third year, the Gauḍīya devotees came to Nīlācala. This time the wives of the Vaiṣṇavas brought from Bengal many kinds of food that He loved, to invite Śrīman Mahāprabhu and feed Him. When they reached Śrī Kṣetra, Mahāprabhu honored them by sending them garlands. That year too the Guṇḍicā temple was washed and the other services were done as before. When the four months of Cāturmāsya had passed, the devotees began to leave for home. Mahāprabhu forbade Nityānanda Prabhu to come to Nīlācala every year. At the question of the residents of Kulīnagrāma He again described the marks of a Vaiṣṇava. That year Vidyānidhi stayed in Nīlācala and saw the Oḍana-ṣaṣṭhī festival. When the devotees had taken leave, Mahāprabhu showed His firm resolve to go to Vṛndāvana and set out on the day of Vijayā-daśamī. King Pratāparudra made many arrangements along Mahāprabhu's way. After they crossed the river Citrotpalā, Rāmānanda, Maṅgarāja (Mardarāja?) and Haricandana went along with Mahāprabhu. Mahāprabhu asked Gadādhara Paṇḍita to go back to Nīlācala, but he would not listen. From Kaṭaka Mahāprabhu bound the Paṇḍita Gosvāmī by an oath and sent him to Śrī Puruṣottama, and at Bhadraka He bade farewell to Rāmānanda. Then, reaching the border of the Oḍra country, He traveled by boat as far as Pāṇihāṭi with the help of a *yavana* governor. After that the Lord went from the house of Rāghava Paṇḍita by way of Kumārahaṭṭa to the village of Kuliyā and freed many people from their offenses. From there He went to Rāmakeli and accepted Śrī Rūpa and Śrī Sanātana. Turning back from Rāmakeli, He instructed Raghunātha dāsa and sent him home. Coming once more to Nīlācala, the Lord began to deliberate about going to Vṛndāvana alone.

## 1

In the garden of Gauḍa the cloud in the form of Gaura sprinkled the nectar of His glance on the creepers — the multitudes of people burned in the forest fire of worldly existence — and brought them back to life.

## 50

*Vāpī* means a well (an *indārā*?), that is, a reservoir.

## 55–56

*Caitanya-bhāgavata*, Antya-khaṇḍa, chapter 8. Once Śrī Advaita invited Mahāprabhu and thought, “If no other *sannyāsī* comes with the Lord, I shall feed the Lord well.” The other *sannyāsīs* had gone out for their midday rites, and just then a storm with rain arose. Since they could not come, the Lord came alone and ate Śrī Advaita's rice and vegetables.

## 60

*Tarjā* is speech in *payār* and similar meters that other people cannot easily understand.

## 62

What Śrī Advaitācārya prayed for in his *tarjā*, and what Śrī Śacīnandana's smile meant — this no one else could understand.

## 64–65

In Gauḍa, in Mahāprabhu's absence, no one but Śrī Prabhu Nityānanda can fulfill His purpose — to give the holy name and *prema* to everyone, down to the *caṇḍālas*.

## 66–67

Nityānanda said, “I am the body, You are the life; these two are never separate. But that we live apart — You in Nīlācala and I in Gauḍa — comes about only by Your inconceivable power.”

## 69–75

The residents of Kulīnagrāma had heard the answer to their question the year before: “Anyone from whose mouth I hear the name of Kṛṣṇa even once,” and so on. Yet this time they asked the same question again, and the Lord said, “Know the one from whose mouth you hear the name of Kṛṣṇa constantly to be the best of Vaiṣṇavas, and serve his feet constantly.” The next year the residents of Kulīnagrāma asked the very same question, and this time the Lord answered, “Know the one at whose mere sight the name of Kṛṣṇa comes naturally to the viewer's mouth to be the foremost Vaiṣṇava.” If we consider these three answers given in three years, the Lord's words yield the marks of three kinds of Vaiṣṇavas: the *vaiṣṇava*, the *vaiṣṇavatara* (the better Vaiṣṇava) and the *vaiṣṇavatama* (the best Vaiṣṇava). To serve all three kinds of Vaiṣṇavas is the duty of a Vaiṣṇava householder. The purport of the Lord's words is this: one who has merely received Vaiṣṇava initiation but has never once chanted the name of Kṛṣṇa without offense is not to be served as a Vaiṣṇava; he need only be honored as a “well-wisher” and a “guest.”

## 76

Vidyānidhi is Puṇḍarīka Vidyānidhi.

## 78

Oḍana-ṣaṣṭhī. The first *ṣaṣṭhī* after the onset of the cold season is called Oḍana-ṣaṣṭhī. On that day warm cloth is put on the body of Jagannāthadeva. This warm cloth is *māṛuyā*, that is, a weaver's unwashed cloth with starch (*māṛ*) in it. Because the Deity was given a starched cloth, Puṇḍarīka Vidyānidhi “found fault” a little on this account and showed some disdain for the devotees of Utkala. Therefore he received what he deserved.

## 81

See the *Caitanya-bhāgavata*, Antya-khaṇḍa, chapters 10 and 11.

## 97

Bhavānīpura is Jānakādeipura; that is, “Bhavānīpura” lies before Jānakīdevīpura.

## 110

A *viṣayī* is a royal officer who collects the revenue of the villages.

## 116

Caturdvāra. From Kaṭaka, after crossing the Mahānadī, one comes to the village of Caturdvāra; it is commonly called “Caudār.”

## 119

The river Citrotpalā. The place where one comes out from Kaṭaka to the Mahānadī is called “the river Citrotpalā.” The *paṇḍitas* of Utkala, citing some *tantra*, say: “In the Kali age the Citrotpalā is the Gaṅgā.”

## 130

*Kṣetra-sannyāsa*. Some leave their former homes and live — alone or with their families — in some special (Viṣṇu-) *tīrtha*, that is, in Puruṣottama-kṣetra, in Navadvīpa-dhāma, in Mathurā-maṇḍala and similar places, regarding this as the highest goal. Their *āśrama* is called *kṣetra-sannyāsa*. This very *āśrama* is the *vānaprastha-dharma* suited to the age of Kali. Sārvabhauma Bhaṭṭācārya's *kṣetra-sannyāsa* of this kind has been spoken of before.

## 135

Having received the service of Śrī Gopīnātha, Śrī Gadādhara Paṇḍita Gosvāmī had vowed to spend his whole life in that service. Had he gone with the Lord to Gauḍa, he would have committed two faults: “the fault of breaking a vow” and “the fault of abandoning service.” On the path of *anurāga* great souls take all such faults upon themselves.

## 145

“I shall not take up weapons in the battle of Kurukṣetra” — this promise of His Kṛṣṇacandra gave up. Wishing to make my promise true, He got down from the chariot, seized a wheel and, His upper cloth falling away, rushed forward to kill me.

## 153

Many accounts describe that Mahāprabhu, travelling in this way together with Rāmānanda Rāya, let Rāmānanda Rāya go from Bhadraka — before He reached Remuṇā near Bāleśvara.

## 159

Pichaladā is a village named Pichaladā on the bank of the Rūpanārāyaṇa river, near Tamluk (?).

## 161

The Oṛiyā *kaṭaka* is the “army camp” (*sainya-kaṭaka*), that is, the encampment on the frontier of the Utkala king's realm; it is what the verse calls the “Oṛiyā *kaṭaka*.”

## 169

*Viśvāsa*. The Yavana ruler of the land of Gauḍa had a department (*daptar*) called the *viśvāsa-khānā*. Only the most trustworthy Kāyasthas were given posts in it. Whenever the king had important business somewhere, Kāyastha *viśvāsas* were sent there.

## 186

O Bhagavān, even one born in a family of *caṇḍālas* or Yavanas at once becomes fit for the *savana* sacrifice as soon as he hears Your name, repeats it after hearing it, bows to You and remembers You. Such a Lord You are — what does the sight of You not bestow?

## 199

Mantreśvara is the name of a large river near Diamond Harbour. Along this river the boat came to the village of Pichaladā on the bank of the Rūpanārāyaṇa; on one side the village of Pichaladā borders the Mantreśvara (?).

## 202

Pāṇihāṭī is the village of Pāṇihāṭī on the bank of the Gaṅgā, not far from the *śrīpāṭa* of Khaḍadaha.

## 205–210

The present name of Kumārahaṭṭa is “Hālisahara.” Soon after Mahāprabhu took *sannyāsa*, Śrīvāsa Paṇḍita left his home in Navadvīpa and built a house in Kumārahaṭṭa. From Kumārahaṭṭa the Lord went to Kāñcanapallī, that is, to Kāñcaḍāpāḍā, to the house of Śivānanda Sena, and then to the house of Vāsudeva Datta, near Śivānanda's house. From there the Lord went to Śrī Vidyānagara on the western bank of Śrī Navadvīpa. (Because of the crowds) He came from Vidyānagara to the village of Kuliyā and stayed in the house of Mādhava dāsa. Staying there seven days, He freed Devānanda and others from their offences. Because Kavirāja Gosvāmī mentions here the Lord's coming in this way to the house of the Ācārya of Śāntipura, many people suspect that there must be some “Kuliyā” near Kāñcaḍāpāḍā. One may surmise that this false notion gave rise to some “new Kuliyār Pāṭ.” In fact Mahāprabhu went to the house of the Ācārya of Śāntipura straight from Vāsudeva's house. That from there He went to the other (western) bank of Navadvīpa — to Vidyānagara, to the house of Vidyā-vācaspati, and to the village of Kuliyā — is clearly described in the *Śrī Caitanya-bhāgavata*, the *Śrī Caitanya-maṅgala*, the *Śrī Caitanya-candrodaya-nāṭaka*, Premadāsa's Bengali rendering, and the *Śrī Caitanya-carita-kāvya*. Śrī Kavirāja Gosvāmī did not describe this journey in order, and this is the root of all these disturbances and doubts.

## 212

See the *Śrī Caitanya-bhāgavata*, Antya-khaṇḍa, chapter 3.

## 238

*Markaṭa-vairāgya* (monkey renunciation). Thoughts of sense objects in the heart and secret relations with women, while outwardly wearing a *kaupīna*, an outer cloth (*bahirvāsa*) and the other marks of renunciation — all these are the signs of a “*markaṭa-vairāgī*.”

## 242–243

Raghunātha dāsa came back from Śāntipura to Saptagrāma and began to follow Mahāprabhu's teaching. He kept his renunciation within his heart; outwardly he no longer showed any renunciation or madness, and without attachment he took up the household duties that befitted him.

## 265

*Prahelī*: a *prahelikā*, a riddle (*tarjā*).

## 272

When the *bādiyās*, that is, the *bedes* (itinerant conjurers), set up a place for their show, a crowd gathers. That I am going to Vṛndāvana with just such a crowd is not good.

## 287

The love that Gadādhara Paṇḍita showed when he fed the Lord, and the way the Lord relished that loving *prasāda* — neither can be described by human power.

*Thus ends the sixteenth chapter of the “Amṛta-pravāha-bhāṣya.”*
