# Amṛta-pravāha-bhāṣya on Ādi-līlā 7

## 0

**Summary.** This seventh chapter describes the glory of the Pañca-tattva. Kṛṣṇa Caitanya, who comprises the five *tattvas*, descended and gave the world the holy name and *prema*, and a great flood of *prema* arose. Several kinds of sophists (*kutārkikas*) — the Māyāvādīs, the blasphemers and others — fled from that flood. Wishing to deliver them, Mahāprabhu accepted *sannyāsa* and, by preaching pure devotion, drew all those people to His feet. He also wished to deliver the Māyāvādī *sannyāsīs* living in Kāśī. At the entreaty of the devotees He met all those *sannyāsīs* together in the house of a certain brāhmaṇa in Vārāṇasī-dhāma. First He showed them the majesty of His own true nature (*svarūpa*) and so won their faith. Then, answering their questions, He showed that the doctrine of Māyāvāda has no foundation and pointed out every kind of fault in the views of Śrī Śaṅkarācārya. By the strength of the *sukṛti* they gained by seeing Bhagavān, He brought them onto the path of *bhakti* and bestowed His mercy on them.

## 1

I bow down to Śrī Caitanya, the refuge of those who have no other refuge — that is, of the *akiñcanas* — who grants the greatest goal to people bereft of the highest goal (*paramārtha*). And I describe the magnanimity with which He gives *prema-bhakti*.

## 3

In the first chapter I described *guru-tattva*, dividing it into the *dīkṣā-guru* and the *śikṣā-guru*. The six *tattvas* are those named in the verse “*vande gurūn īśa-bhaktān*….” Now, in this verse, setting *guru-tattva* aside, I examine the remaining five *tattvas*.

## 6

I bow down to Śrī Kṛṣṇa, who comprises the five *tattvas*: Kṛṣṇa's *bhakta-rūpa*, *bhakta-svarūpa*, *bhakta-avatāra*, *bhakta* and *bhakta-śakti*.

## 20–21

Śrī Kṛṣṇa's own pastimes are the storehouse of *prema*. That storehouse had indeed come into the world, but its doors were locked and sealed. In the avatāra of Śrī Caitanya the five *tattvas* together broke that seal, opened the doors and, plundering *prema*, tasted it.

## 25–27

When the storehouse of *prema* was thrown open, the flood of the *rasa* of *prema* drowned the whole world with tremendous force. In it perished the seed from which the bondage of ignorance (*avidyā*) grows in the conditioned *jīvas* — their forgetfulness that they are Kṛṣṇa's servants.

## 29

The Māyāvādīs are Prakāśānanda and the other *sannyāsīs*. In every matter that is real they raise disputes about “*māyā*”! They call Brahman “beyond *māyā*,” yet they make Īśvara “an associate of *māyā*” and call the bodies of all Īśvara's avatāras “made of *māyā*.” They say that *māyā* takes part in the very constitution of the *jīva*, that is, that every notion of “I” in the *jīva* is made by *māyā*. From this they conclude that once the *jīva* is liberated, no state that could be called “the pure *jīva*” remains. In other words, they teach that the liberated *jīva* becomes nondifferent from Brahman.

Those devoted to karma (*karma-niṣṭha*) are Devānanda and other *karmīs* devoid of *bhakti*, and the *smārtas* dulled by karma — that is, those who declare karma and its fruits to be the chief aim of the *jīva*.

The sophists are Sārvabhauma and other logicians who deny Īśvara.

The blasphemers are the man whom the Lord chased with a stick, and Gopāla Cāpāla and others who blasphemed the Lord and His devotees.

The *pāṣaṇḍīs* are those who explain other gods as equal to Bhagavān.

The lowly students are those who take learning to be a cause for argument and do not know that learning is the means to attain Īśvara.

## 39

As soon as the Lord accepted *sannyāsa*, the sophists, those devoted to karma, the blasphemers, the *pāṣaṇḍīs* and the lowly students one after another took shelter at His feet, and even many *mlecchas* accepted His guidance. Only the Māyāvādīs of Vārāṇasī-dhāma, having fled from the flood of *prema*, remained apart!

## 45–46

The Vaidya Candraśekhara was a *śūdra* by *varṇa*. It is not proper for *sannyāsīs* to spend the night in a *śūdra*'s house, but Mahāprabhu, out of mercy to him, stayed in his house: for He is the independent Īśvara, and before His mercy all are equal, brāhmaṇa and *śūdra* alike. He accepted *bhikṣā*, that is, His meals, in the house of Tapana Miśra. And nowhere did He accept invitations together with other *sannyāsīs*.

## 57

Even so, the Lord, wishing to bestow mercy on the *sannyāsīs*, inspired the brāhmaṇa from within his heart, and that is why he made such an insistent request.

## 67

“A *sannyāsī* of the *sampradāya*”: according to the teaching of Śrī Śaṅkarācārya, the brāhmaṇas who accept *sannyāsa* in the order of the ten names are the world-honored “Vedic *sannyāsīs*,” that is, genuine *sannyāsīs* sanctioned by the *śāstras*.

## 76

In Kali there is no way other than the name of Hari; the name of Hari is the only way.

## 84

“*Dharma*,” “*artha*,” “*kāma*” and “*mokṣa*” are the four goals of human life (*puruṣārthas*). Love for Kṛṣṇa is the fifth goal. The bliss of Brahman and the like — the first stage of *mokṣa* — cannot be compared even with a single drop of it. *Dharma*, *artha*, *kāma* and *mokṣa* are not the “fruit” of Kṛṣṇa's name. All the *śāstras* agree that love for Kṛṣṇa is the one and only fruit of Kṛṣṇa's name.

## 94

A person whose vow is service to Kṛṣṇa loses control of his heart: as he chants the name of his most beloved Śrī Kṛṣṇa, attachment (*anurāga*) awakens in him, and his heart melts. Like a madman he becomes *loka-bāhya*, that is, he no longer looks to anyone: now he laughs, now he weeps, now he cries out, now he sings, dances and does much else.

## 97

*Khātodaka*: a little water in a ditch.

## 98

O Teacher of the world! I have seen You Yourself (Your *svarūpa*), and now I abide in a pure ocean of joy. All other happiness seems to me like a cow's hoofprint; even the happiness the *jīva* finds in dissolving into Brahman is only a hoofprint. Where a cow steps, a hollow is left; the water in it is tiny compared with the ocean.

## 108

The Upaniṣads are the Īśa, Kena, Kaṭha, Praśna, Muṇḍaka, Māṇḍūkya, Taittirīya, Aitareya, Chāndogya, Bṛhad-āraṇyaka and Śvetāśvatara: eleven Upaniṣads, the crown of the Vedas.

The *sūtra* is the *Brahma-sūtra*: four chapters, sixteen *pādas*. These two are the chief among the scriptures.

## 108–110

The truth that these chief scriptures teach by their primary meaning (*mukhya-vṛtti*), that is, by *abhidhā-vṛtti*, is supremely great. Śrī Śaṅkarācārya abandoned the primary meaning of these scriptures, established the conclusion of *kevalādvaita* by their secondary meaning (*gauṇa-vṛtti*), that is, by *lakṣaṇā-vṛtti*, and wrote his commentary. Whoever hears it loses every spiritual endeavor. You may ask: why did Śaṅkara Svāmī, a direct *avatāra* of Śiva, do such an improper thing? Then listen: he undertook this work at the Lord's command, and so he bears no fault. Thus in the *Padma Purāṇa* Śrī Mahādeva says: “Māyāvāda is a false doctrine; it is called covert Buddhism. O Devī, in the age of Kali I Myself devised it in the form of a *brāhmaṇa*. To bewilder all the people of this world in the age of Kali, I shall describe Brahman in another form — as devoid of qualities (*nirguṇa*). O Devī, in the great scripture, the Vedānta, I Myself shall expound the non-Vedic Māyāvāda, to bring the world to ruin.” And in the *Śiva Purāṇa* Bhagavān says: “At the beginning of Dvāpara and the other ages, appear by your partial power among humans and others, and by *āgamas* of your own invention turn people away from Me.”

## 111–115

The meaning that appears clearly, that is, directly, as soon as one reads a text is called the primary meaning (*mukhyārtha*). The *Bṛhad-āraṇyaka* says: “That is full, this is full; from the full the full comes forth” (5.1). The *Śvetāśvatara* says: “The ancient Puruṣa, possessed of wondrous potencies”; “He is beyond the tree [of worldly existence], time and forms, and other than them; from Him this world revolves; He brings *dharma*, removes sin, and is the Lord of all opulence” (6.6); “I know this great Puruṣa, radiant like the sun, beyond darkness” (3.8); “the supreme Master of masters, higher than the highest” (6.7); “the Puruṣa is the great Lord” (3.12); “His supreme potency is heard to be manifold” (6.8), and so on. The *Ṛg-veda* says: “The sages always see that supreme abode of Viṣṇu.” The *Praśna* says: “He glanced” (6.3). The *Aitareya* says: “He glanced” (1.1.1) and “He created these worlds” (1.1.2). The *Talavakāra* says: “Brahman knew [their pride] and appeared before them” (3.2). As soon as one reads many such statements of the Vedas, it becomes clear that [they speak] only of Bhagavān, the one Supreme Truth, full of the six opulences, who has none equal to Him or above Him. There are, of course, statements that deny form, such as *apāṇi-pādaḥ* (“without hands and feet”) (*Śvetāśvatara* 3.19). They must be understood only to mean that this Bhagavān's form is spiritual, and that His body and His opulence are spiritual opulence. The Māyāvādīs, headed by the ācārya, cover His spiritual opulence and declare Him “formless,” a transformation of the quality of *sattva*. But He Himself, His abode and His associates are all, by nature, spiritual bliss beyond *prakṛti*. How, then, can He be called a transformation of material *sattva*? In truth His form too, full of transcendental spiritual opulence, is real. Is the ācārya at fault for describing Him as formless? No — he is a servant carrying out an order. As the *Nārada-pañcarātra* says: “And conceal Me, so that this creation may keep on increasing.” But anyone else who hears such an explanation loses everything. There can be no worse blasphemy of Viṣṇu than to consider Viṣṇu's body “material.”

## 116–117

If the truth of the Lord is compared to a blazing fire, the countless *jīvas* can be compared to tiny sparks of that fire. The point is this: the Lord is a special fire — spiritual, unlimited and blazing. The countless *jīvas* have come forth from Him like tiny sparks and become separate *tattvas*. Here *māyā* plays no part in forming the *jīva*'s *svarūpa*; that is, nothing material takes place. You may ask: why form such particles of spirit at all? Then listen: the Lord's wondrous *svarūpa-śakti* has two kinds of tendencies — toward unlimited action and toward minute action. From the tendency toward unlimited action come the Lord's own form and the Vaikuṇṭha-*tattva*, the spiritual world; this tendency is called *cit-śakti*. From the tendency toward minute action come the countless *jīvas*, atomic consciousness; this tendency is called *jīva-śakti*. If the *svarūpa-śakti* did not have both these functions, His fullness would be impaired. Bhagavān is full of all opulence, and so the *jīva* — the minute action of His potency — must exist inevitably and necessarily. Therefore it is through the *jīva-tattva* that Kṛṣṇa's possession of potency (*vilāsa*) [is manifest] in the Kṛṣṇa-*tattva*. Without the *jīva-tattva*, Kṛṣṇa's full possession of potency could not be admitted. Where there are none to be ruled, there is no Ruler.

## 118

Earth, water, fire, air and ether — this is the gross world of the five elements; mind, intelligence and *ahaṅkāra* — the subtle (*liṅga*) world. *Prakṛti*, divided in these eight ways, is “inferior” (*aparā*), or “inert” (*jaḍā*); it is called *māyā-prakṛti*. Apart from it I have another, “superior *prakṛti*” (*parā-prakṛti*). This *prakṛti*, whose nature is the *jīva*, fills this world. The point is this: Bhagavān is the one Reality; He has a potency called *svarūpa*, or *ātma*. There is a potency that appears almost separate from this *svarūpa-śakti*, yet shows itself as its shadow; it is called *māyā-śakti*. The material universe, gross and subtle, is born of that *māyā*. Beyond it is the *jīva-tattva*. The *jīva*'s pure being, pure *ahaṅkāra* and mental functions are all formed by a certain superior potency (*parā-śakti*) beyond *māyā*. So *māyā* had no authority at all in the making of the “*jīva*.” When the *jīva* has entered *māyā*, a minute intelligence and *ahaṅkāra* tinged with materiality appear in him — that alone is *māyā*'s work. When the *jīva* is cleansed of this connection with *māyā* and abides in his own *svarūpa*, this is called *mukti*. In *mukti* not even the *ahaṅkāra* made by *māyā* remains; but all the spiritual faculties natural to the *jīva* will be able to act in their pure form. Therefore the *jīva* is a particular potency of Bhagavān.

## 119

Viṣṇu's potency is of three kinds: the superior (*parā*), the *kṣetrajñā*, and the one called ignorance (*avidyā*). Viṣṇu's superior potency is *cit-śakti*; the *kṣetrajñā* potency is *jīva-śakti* (it is said to be “other” [distinct] from *māyā*, that is, *avidyā*); and the potency of *avidyā*, which is called *karma*, is named *māyā*.

## 120–127

The *jīva-tattva* is a particular potency. If one tries to establish the *jīva-tattva* not as atomic consciousness (*aṇu-caitanya*) but as “Brahman,” the conclusion will certainly be mistaken. Śrī Śaṅkarācārya, carrying out the Lord's order, intended to conceal the Lord's sovereignty; so he established the oneness of the *jīva-tattva* with the Supreme Truth (*para-tattva*) and preached a mistaken conclusion. In fact, Vyāsa's *sūtras* accept the doctrine of the transformation (of potency) — (*śakti-*)*pariṇāma-vāda*. The ācārya raised the argument: “If *pariṇāma-vāda* is accepted, the Lord must be said to undergo change.” He reasoned: “If *pariṇāma-vāda* is admitted, Vyāsa will have to be admitted to have ‘erred,’” and so he established *vivarta-vāda*. In his commentary on the fourteenth *sūtra* of the first *pāda* of the second chapter of the *Brahma-sūtra* — “It is not other [than the cause], according to the word *ārambhaṇa* and the rest” — he cited such Vedic statements as “Change is only a verbal designation, a name” (*Chāndogya* 6.1.4) and argued that *pariṇāma-vāda* is a faulty doctrine of change (*vikāra-vāda*). In reality, the *Brahma-sūtra* presents *pariṇāma-vāda* thus: by the mere will of the Lord, His inconceivable potency is transformed into effects. The definition of *pariṇāma* is: “*Vikāra* is said to be the perception [of a thing] as other while it is real.” When from one real *tattva* another real *tattva* arises, and it is perceived as a different thing, that is *vikāra*, that is, *pariṇāma*. “Brahman” is a real Entity; from Him there have arisen separately a real entity, the “*jīva*,” and a real entity, the “material universe of *māyā*.” This understanding is called the *vikāra*, or *pariṇāma*, of Brahman. An example of *pariṇāma*: “milk” is a real substance, and it changes into another real substance, “curd.” Vedic statements such as “All this has That as its soul” (*Chāndogya* 6.8.7) leave no doubt that the world is Brahman. Brahman has an inconceivable potency; this is established by the Vedic statement “His supreme potency is heard to be manifold” (*Śvetāśvatara* 6.8). By that potency Brahman's real nature is transformed into the world — and no fault can attach to such a conclusion. From the statements of the *Chāndogya* — “In the beginning, my dear, all this was Being alone, one without a second” (6.2.1), “It glanced: may I become many, may I be born” (6.2.3), “All these creatures, my dear, are rooted in Being, abide in Being, are established in Being” (6.8.4), “All this has That as its soul” (6.8.7) — it is well known that this Brahman, by His own superior potency, is transformed into this world of spirit and matter. The world and the *jīva* are *upādeya* (what is made from the material), and Brahman is the *upādāna* (the material cause). The Vedic statement “That from which these beings are born” (*Taittirīya*, *Bhṛgu-vallī* 1) accepts that Brahman is the material cause and that the *jīva* and matter are made from Him. One who has not grasped the true essence of *pariṇāma-vāda* does not understand that this “world” and the “*jīva*” are separate real *tattvas*. From such statements as “All these creatures, my dear, are rooted in Being, abide in Being” (*Chāndogya* 6.8.4), it is clear that the “*jīva*” and the “material world,” the *jīva*'s dwelling, are indeed real. To imagine the *jīva* and the world as false, like a snake in a rope or silver in a shell, out of a groundless fear that Brahman would then turn out to be subject to change — this is mere deception. True, the *Māṇḍūkya* and other Vedas give the examples of “a snake in a rope” and “silver in a shell,” but these have their own particular fields of application. The *jīva* is a pure particle of spirit. The *jīva* in a human body identifies himself with this material body — that is the place of *vivarta*. *Vivarta* is explained thus: “*Vivarta* is said to be the perception [of a thing] as other when it is not so in reality.” To take a thing for what it is not — that is *vivarta*. For the *jīva*, *vivarta* is a great defect: the conditioned *jīva* is stained by this fault of false perception (*vivarta-buddhi*). To impose such a fault of *vivarta* on the original *tattva* of the universe and on the *jīva-tattva* is utterly worthless. Such an error arises only when one forgets the inconceivable potency. How Bhagavān has transformed into the world can be illustrated by a simple example. Many say that in the material world there is a treasure, the stone *cintāmaṇi*: it produces heaps of various jewels, yet itself remains unchanged. If a material thing has such an inconceivable power, what is surprising in the Lord's having an inconceivable potency that surpasses it infinitely in its qualities?

## 128–132

The root statement of the Vedas is the *praṇava*; therefore it alone is the great statement (*mahā-vākya*) that denotes Brahman. The *praṇava* is the word that reveals the *svarūpa* of the Lord; therefore it is the Lord's eternal name and points to the Lord — *sarva-viśva-dhāma*, the shelter of all. To call “*mahā-vākyas*” such statements as “You are That” (*Chāndogya* 6.8.7), “All this is what this Self is,” “All this is Brahman” (*Bṛhad-āraṇyaka* 2.5.1), “All this is the Self” (*Chāndogya* 7.25.2) and “There is no diversity here” (*Kaṭha* 2.1.11; *Bṛhad-āraṇyaka* 4.4.19) is a grave error. For the chief among them, “You are That,” is only a partial statement: what the words “You are That” teach covers only one part of the Vedas. A *mahā-vākya* is what covers the Vedas as a whole; therefore nothing but the *praṇava* can be a “*mahā-vākya*.”[^128-1] Concealing this truth, Śrī Śaṅkarācārya called “You are That” a *mahā-vākya*. Relying on this invented *mahā-vākya*, he everywhere in the Vedas abandoned the primary meaning, that is, *abhidhā-vṛtti*, and explained them by *lakṣaṇā*, the secondary meaning. In this way he rejected without any reason the way all the Vedas and *sūtras* reveal the Kṛṣṇa-*tattva*. The Vedas are their own evidence (*svataḥ-pramāṇa*); so to impose a figurative sense (*lakṣaṇā*) on their words is merely to destroy the authority of evidence that is self-established.

## 135

O Śrī Kṛṣṇa Caitanya! You presented these arguments and refuted Śaṅkara's interpretation; this is no empty dispute (*vivāda*) — that is, a fortunate person accepts it as “the truth.”

## 138–140

In the *Bṛhad-āraṇyaka* (5.1), in the statement “That is full…” and so on, the Supreme Truth, full of the six opulences, is called *bṛhad-vastu*, the great Reality. In the Purāṇas these characteristics are described by the word “Bhagavān”; therefore wherever the Vedas say “Brahman,” the word fulfills its purpose only when “Śrī Bhagavān” is put in its place. So in all the Vedas the one *sambandha* is Bhagavān. Bhagavān embraces even the quality of featurelessness, yet He is eternally possessed of features. To call Him “featureless” is to deny His *cit-śakti*. Brahman possesses *cit-śakti* — that is, possesses features; so if one does not accept half of His *svarūpa*, His fullness is impaired.

## 141–142

To attain shelter at the feet of that Bhagavat-*tattva*, all the Vedas have determined *sādhana-bhakti* to be the *abhidheya*. It is from the ninefold *sādhana-bhakti*, beginning with hearing, that love for Kṛṣṇa awakens.

## 146

“Who am I? What is this material universe? What is Bhagavān? And what is our relationship with one another?” When one finds the true answer to these four questions, one has “knowledge of *sambandha*.” What is the duty of one who has gained knowledge of *sambandha*? One must understand this, and taking up that duty is to be known as the *abhidheya* of all the scriptures. The fruit one attains by carrying out that duty is called the *prayojana*. The *Brahma-sūtra* teaches precisely these three meanings.

## 162

“Later”: see Madhya, Chapter 25.

*Thus ends the seventh chapter of the “Amṛta-pravāha-bhāṣya.”*
