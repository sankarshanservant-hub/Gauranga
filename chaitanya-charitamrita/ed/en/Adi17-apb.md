# Amṛta-pravāha-bhāṣya on Ādi-līlā 17

## 0

**Summary.** In the seventeenth chapter all of Mahāprabhu's pastimes, from His sixteenth year until He accepted *sannyāsa*, are written briefly, in the form of *sūtras*. The reason is that Vṛndāvana dāsa Ṭhākura, the incarnation of Vyāsa, has described all these pastimes in detail in the *Śrī Caitanya-bhāgavata*. But wherever Vṛndāvana dāsa Ṭhākura left something out, this chapter describes it more fully. The mango festival and Mahāprabhu's conversation with the Kazi are told in particular detail. At the end the author shows that the son of Yaśodā, having become the son of Śacī, tasted the four kinds of devotional moods. To taste the sweetness of the *rasa* of Rādhā's *prema*, He accepted Rādhā's *bhāva* and took up the mood of the *gopīs* exclusively. Of all the kinds of devotional moods, the mood of the *gopīs* is the best, because in the mood of the *gopīs* no one but Vrajendranandana appears as worthy of worship. When Śrī Kṛṣṇa playfully became four-armed, the *gopīs* merely offered Him obeisance and withdrew: the ordinary mood of the *gopīs* rejects every form other than Kṛṣṇa's form, and no more. But the *bhāva* of Śrīmatī Rādhikā, the crown jewel of the *gopīs*, is the highest of all. On seeing Rādhā, Śrī Kṛṣṇa could not keep His four-armed form. Nanda, the lord of Vraja, is in this (Gaura) pastime the father, Jagannātha; Yaśodā, the queen of Vraja, is Śacīmātā. Caitanya Gosāñi is the son of Nanda Himself: He is not a *prakāśa* or a *vilāsa* of Nanda's son, but Nanda's son in person. Nityānanda Prabhu has three moods, *vātsalya*, *dāsya* and *sakhya*; Advaita Prabhu has two, *sakhya* and *dāsya*. All the others serve Mahāprabhu each according to his former position. The Truth is one: now Kṛṣṇa in a dark form, the flute at His lips, sporting among the cowherds; now a brāhmaṇa; now, in the dress of a *sannyāsī*, Kṛṣṇa Caitanya in a golden form. The apparent contradiction is this: He who is Kṛṣṇa becomes a *gopī*. This idea is certainly very hard to grasp, but by Kṛṣṇa's inconceivable potency even this is possible. Arguing about it is futile, for to apply logic to what is inconceivable is the height of foolishness. At the end of this chapter Kavirāja Gosvāmī, following what Vyāsa did in the *Bhāgavata*, has separately recapitulated the seventeen chapters of the Ādi-līlā one by one.

## 1

I worship Śrī Caitanyadeva, whose deeds are free and wonderful: by His grace even the *yavanas* become righteous and chant the name of Kṛṣṇa.

## 4

Through learning, beauty, fine dress, enjoyment, dancing, *kīrtana*, and the gift of *prema* and of the holy name, Gauracandra shone in His youth.

## 7

Having finished His studies and His teaching, Gauracandra, to preach pure devotion, pretended for some days to suffer from a disorder of *vāyu* (the air in the body). Everywhere He explained Kṛṣṇa's name to His students and showed the connection with Kṛṣṇa in every *sūtra* of grammar — and in this way He began to turn them away from their studies.

## 8–9

Thinking, “I shall offer *śrāddha* at Gayā for My father, who has departed to the other world,” Mahāprabhu set out for Gayā with many students. On the way He was seized by fever; He drank water that had washed a brāhmaṇa's feet and was freed from the illness. By this pastime He showed that householders must honor brāhmaṇas. On reaching Gayā He accepted initiation into the Kṛṣṇa *mantra* from Śrī Īśvara Purī. From that initiation Mahāprabhu's *prema* began to manifest. Having finished His duties at Gayā, He returned to Śrī Dhāma Navadvīpa and began to preach *prema*.

## 10

One day Mahāprabhu, sitting on Viṣṇu's couch (*viṣṇu-khaṭṭā*) in Śrīvāsa's house, said that His mother had committed an offense against a Vaiṣṇava, Śrī Advaitācārya. Unless she sought forgiveness for that offense and Advaita forgave her, she would not attain loving devotion (*prema-bhakti*). Hearing this, the devotees brought Advaita Prabhu, and Śrī Advaita (as he sang the glories of Āi) was overcome by *prema*. Śacīdevī seized that moment, took the dust of his feet, and was freed from the offense. Then “the Lord, pleased, says to His mother: ‘Now you have devotion to Viṣṇu. There is no more offense against Advaita.’” From then on Śacīdevī had *prema-bhakti*.

One day Advaita, overcome by *prema*, said to the Lord in Śrīvāsa's courtyard, “Show me the universal form You once showed Arjuna.” And the Lord mercifully showed him the universal form.

## 11

One day in Śrīvāsa's house all the devotees together performed Mahāprabhu's *abhiṣeka*. Mahāprabhu sat on Viṣṇu's couch and displayed His majesty as the King of kings. Many devotees sang *kīrtana* at that time. And Advaita and the other devotees began to worship Mahāprabhu with the sixteen articles of worship. To each the Lord granted whatever boon he desired.

## 12

Śrī Nityānanda Prabhu was born in the village of “Ekacakrā” in the Vīrabhūma district, as the son of Hāḍāi Paṇḍita, from the womb of Padmāvatī. When Nityānanda had grown a little, a *sannyāsī* came to Hāḍāi Paṇḍita and begged Nityānanda from him as alms. From then on Nityānanda traveled with that *sannyāsī* through many lands, came to the Mathurā-maṇḍala, and lived there a long time. Drawn by Mahāprabhu, Prabhu Nityānanda came to Śrī Navadvīpa and stayed in the house of Nandana Ācārya. Mahāprabhu and the devotees brought Nityānanda from there to His own place.
