# Amṛta-pravāha-bhāṣya on Ādi-līlā 17

## 0

**Summary.** In the seventeenth chapter all of Mahāprabhu's pastimes, from His sixteenth year until He accepted *sannyāsa*, are written briefly, in the form of *sūtras*. The reason is that Vṛndāvana dāsa Ṭhākura, the incarnation of Vyāsa, has described all these pastimes in detail in the *Śrī Caitanya-bhāgavata*. But wherever Vṛndāvana dāsa Ṭhākura left something out, this chapter describes it more fully. The mango festival and Mahāprabhu's conversation with the Kāzī are told in particular detail. At the end the author shows that the son of Yaśodā, having become the son of Śacī, tasted the four kinds of devotional moods. To taste the sweetness of the *rasa* of Rādhā's *prema*, He accepted Rādhā's *bhāva* and took up the mood of the *gopīs* exclusively. Of all the kinds of devotional moods, the mood of the *gopīs* is the best, because in the mood of the *gopīs* no one but Vrajendranandana appears as worthy of worship. When Śrī Kṛṣṇa playfully became four-armed, the *gopīs* merely offered Him obeisance and withdrew: the ordinary mood of the *gopīs* rejects every form other than Kṛṣṇa's form, and no more. But the *bhāva* of Śrīmatī Rādhikā, the crown jewel of the *gopīs*, is the highest of all. On seeing Rādhā, Śrī Kṛṣṇa could not keep His four-armed form. Nanda, the lord of Vraja, is in this (Gaura) pastime the father, Jagannātha; Yaśodā, the queen of Vraja, is Śacīmātā. Caitanya Gosāñi is the son of Nanda Himself: He is not a *prakāśa* or a *vilāsa* of Nanda's son, but Nanda's son in person. Nityānanda Prabhu has three moods, *vātsalya*, *dāsya* and *sakhya*; Advaita Prabhu has two, *sakhya* and *dāsya*. All the others serve Mahāprabhu each according to his former position. The Truth is one: now Kṛṣṇa in a dark form, the flute at His lips, sporting among the cowherds; now a brāhmaṇa; now, in the dress of a *sannyāsī*, Kṛṣṇa Caitanya in a golden form. The apparent contradiction is this: He who is Kṛṣṇa becomes a *gopī*. This idea is certainly very hard to grasp, but by Kṛṣṇa's inconceivable potency even this is possible. Arguing about it is futile, for to apply logic to what is inconceivable is the height of foolishness. At the end of this chapter Kavirāja Gosvāmī, following what Vyāsa did in the *Bhāgavata*, has separately recapitulated the seventeen chapters of the Ādi-līlā one by one.

## 1

I worship Śrī Caitanyadeva, whose deeds are free and wonderful: by His grace even the *yavanas* become righteous and chant the name of Kṛṣṇa.

## 4

Through learning, beauty, fine dress, enjoyment, dancing, *kīrtana*, and the gift of *prema* and of the holy name, Gauracandra shone in His youth.

## 7

Having finished His studies and His teaching, Gauracandra, to preach pure devotion, pretended for some days to suffer from a disorder of *vāyu* (the air in the body). Everywhere He explained Kṛṣṇa's name to His students and showed the connection with Kṛṣṇa in every *sūtra* of grammar — and in this way He began to turn them away from their studies.

## 8–9

Thinking, “I shall offer *śrāddha* at Gayā for My father, who has departed to the other world,” Mahāprabhu set out for Gayā with many students. On the way He was seized by fever; He drank water that had washed a brāhmaṇa's feet and was freed from the illness. By this pastime He showed that householders must honor brāhmaṇas. On reaching Gayā He accepted initiation into the Kṛṣṇa *mantra* from Śrī Īśvara Purī. From that initiation Mahāprabhu's *prema* began to manifest. Having finished His duties at Gayā, He returned to Śrī Dhāma Navadvīpa and began to preach *prema*.

## 10

One day Mahāprabhu, sitting on Viṣṇu's couch (*viṣṇu-khaṭṭā*) in Śrīvāsa's house, said that His mother had committed an offense against a Vaiṣṇava, Śrī Advaitācārya. Unless she sought forgiveness for that offense and Advaita forgave her, she would not attain loving devotion (*prema-bhakti*). Hearing this, the devotees brought Advaita Prabhu, and Śrī Advaita (as he sang the glories of Āi) was overcome by *prema*. Śacīdevī seized that moment, took the dust of his feet, and was freed from the offense. Then “the Lord, pleased, says to His mother: ‘Now you have devotion to Viṣṇu. There is no more offense against Advaita.’” From then on Śacīdevī had *prema-bhakti*.

One day Advaita, overcome by *prema*, said to the Lord in Śrīvāsa's courtyard, “Show me the universal form You once showed Arjuna.” And the Lord mercifully showed him the universal form.

## 11

One day in Śrīvāsa's house all the devotees together performed Mahāprabhu's *abhiṣeka*. Mahāprabhu sat on Viṣṇu's couch and displayed His majesty as the King of kings. Many devotees sang *kīrtana* at that time. And Advaita and the other devotees began to worship Mahāprabhu with the sixteen articles of worship. To each the Lord granted whatever boon he desired.

## 12

Śrī Nityānanda Prabhu was born in the village of “Ekacakrā” in the Birbhum district, as the son of Hāḍāi Paṇḍita, from the womb of Padmāvatī. When Nityānanda had grown a little, a *sannyāsī* came to Hāḍāi Paṇḍita and begged Nityānanda from him as alms. From then on Nityānanda traveled with that *sannyāsī* through many lands, came to the Mathurā-maṇḍala, and lived there a long time. Drawn by Mahāprabhu, Prabhu Nityānanda came to Śrī Navadvīpa and stayed in the house of Nandana Ācārya. Mahāprabhu and the devotees brought Nityānanda from there to His own place.

## 13–15

One day Mahāprabhu showed Nityānanda Prabhu a six-armed form holding conch, disc, club, lotus, the Śārṅga bow and flute. Then He showed a four-armed form, holding conch and disc in two hands and the flute in the other two. Finally He showed the form of Śrī Kṛṣṇa holding the flute alone. (*Śrī Caitanya-maṅgala*, Madhya.)

## 16

By Mahāprabhu's order Śrī Nityānanda Prabhu resolved to perform the Vyāsa-pūjā on the full-moon night and had Śrīvāsa arrange the necessary articles. During the *saṅkīrtana*, Nityānanda Prabhu placed a flower garland around Mahāprabhu's neck. At that moment Nityānanda Prabhu saw the six-armed form. Nothing more of the Vyāsa-pūjā took place.

On the night before the Vyāsa-pūjā, during *saṅkīrtana* in Śrīvāsa's house, Mahāprabhu, in the mood of Balarāma, sat on Viṣṇu's couch and asked Nityānanda for the plough and the pestle (*muṣala*). Nityānanda Prabhu placed his own hand in His hand, and at that moment the devotees saw the plough and the pestle before their eyes.

## 17

One night Śacīdevī dreamed that Kṛṣṇa and Balarāma, the two Deities of her house, were snatching the offered food (*naivedya*) from each other together with Gaurāṅga and Nityānanda. The next day, by Gaurāṅga's wish, Śacīdevī invited Nityānanda to eat at her house. While Viśvambhara and Nityānanda were eating, Śacīdevī saw Kṛṣṇa and Balarāma Themselves eating, and at that sight she fainted in *prema*.

Jagāi and Mādhāi were born in Navadvīpa and were given to all kinds of sin. When Nityānanda and Haridāsa, by Mahāprabhu's order, went from house to house preaching the holy name, they incurred the anger of these two drunkards. The two, in a frenzy, chased them, and they fled. Another day Mādhāi struck Nityānanda on the head with a shard of a broken pot. Jagāi was somewhat sorry for that deed. Hearing of it, Mahāprabhu came there with His followers, ready to punish Jagāi and Mādhāi. But the merciful Gaurāṅga, hearing of Jagāi's decent conduct, embraced him with love. By seeing and touching the Lord, the two sinners had a change of heart, and the Lord delivered them by giving them the name of Hari.

## 18

One day in Śrīvāsa's house Mahāprabhu sat on Viṣṇu's couch, and the devotees, reciting the *Puruṣa-sūkta* — “The Puruṣa with a thousand heads, a thousand eyes, a thousand feet…” — performed His *abhiṣeka* with Gaṅgā water. They worshiped Him with various articles and gave Him many kinds of food to eat. The Lord began to eat everything the devotees offered. That day He remained in that *bhāva* for a full seven *praharas* and displayed the moods of all the avatāras. He revealed secret events from the devotees' past, dispelled everyone's doubts, and gave boons to all. Some call this *bhāva* the “*sāt-prahariyā bhāva*” (the *bhāva* that lasted seven *praharas*), and some the “*mahā-prakāśa*” (the great manifestation).

## 19

One day Mahāprabhu, crying “Boar! Boar!”, Himself assumed the form of Varāha and entered Murāri Gupta's house. With His tusks He lifted a vessel full of water (a *gāḍu*), as He once lifted the earth, and drank the water. And another day the Lord climbed onto Murāri's shoulders and danced at length.

## 20

Śuklāmbara Brahmacārī lived on the bank of the Gaṅgā in Śrī Navadvīpa. While Mahāprabhu was dancing, he arrived there with his bag of rice collected as alms. Out of love for His devotee, the Lord took that begged rice from his bag and began to eat it with great *prema*.

## 31

One who considers himself lower than grass, who is tolerant like a tree, who seeks no honor himself and gives honor to others — he alone is qualified to chant about Hari always.

## 32–33

The author says: “O people! With upraised arms I tell you: listen! String this verse on the garland of Kṛṣṇa's name and wear it around your neck.” The meaning is this: if one chants the name without being qualified, the result is *nāmābhāsa* (a mere shadow of the name) or *nāmāparādha* (offense against the name). Then the *jīva* does not attain the fruit of the name, “love for Kṛṣṇa.” Chant the name of Hari while acting on the instruction Mahāprabhu gave in the verse *tṛṇād api*; then you will certainly attain the feet of Śrī Kṛṣṇa.

## 35–45

While Mahāprabhu, with the doors shut, was tasting the bliss of *kīrtana* in Śrīvāsa's courtyard, many brāhmaṇas of the town who were turned away from God tried in many ways to ridicule the Vaiṣṇavas. A certain garrulous Bhaṭṭācārya nicknamed “Gopāla Cāpāla” left outside the closed door the articles for worshiping the Devī — a banana leaf, a *javā* flower, red sandalwood and so on — together with a pot of wine. In the morning Śrīvāsa Paṇḍita saw it and said to everyone in jest, “Look, look! Every night I worship Bhavānī. Now you have learned how great I am as a ‘*śākta*’!” The respectable people were deeply distressed at the sight. They called a *hāḍi* (a sweeper), had the wine and the other vile things thrown far away, and purified the place with water and cow dung. For that offense against a Vaiṣṇava, Gopāla Cāpāla was stricken with a rotting leprosy.

## 55

Kuliyā-grāma: at that time Navadvīpa was on the eastern bank of the Gaṅgā, and on the other bank was the village of Kuliyā — the place now known as “Navadvīpa.”

## 65

On the day of the Lord's *mahā-prakāśa*, Mukunda Datta stayed lying outside the door. When the Lord had shown His grace to the other devotees one by one, they told the Lord that Mukunda Datta was outside. The Lord replied, “I will not soon be pleased with Mukunda Datta. Before devotees he speaks of ‘pure devotion,’ but sitting among the Māyāvādīs he accepts the ‘Māyāvāda’ set out in the *Yoga-vāsiṣṭha*. This always pains Me.” Mukunda Datta heard these words from outside and said, “I am fortunate! Though not soon, at some time Mahāprabhu, the deliverer of the world, will show me mercy.” Seeing that Mukunda Datta was firmly resolved to give up the company of Māyāvādīs, the Lord at once had him brought near and showed him His favor. In this way the Lord punished him for the offense of associating with Māyāvādīs and granted him grace as the fruit of associating with pure devotees.

## 66–68

Advaitācārya was a godbrother of Īśvara Purī, Mahāprabhu's guru. For this reason the Lord, although Advaita was His servant, honored him as a guru. Advaita was pained that Mahāprabhu showed him such honor. To receive Mahāprabhu's grace in the form of punishment, he went to Śāntipura and began to explain the path of *jñāna* to some unfortunate people. Hearing of this, the Lord went to Śāntipura in anger and gave Advaita Prabhu a sound beating. Having received the blows, Advaita Prabhu began to dance, saying, “Look, today my desire is fulfilled! Mahāprabhu was stingy and treated me as His guru; today He saw me as His own servant and disciple and tried to save me from the evil notion of Māyāvāda.” Seeing this ploy of Advaitācārya, the Lord was embarrassed and became pleased with him.

## 69

One day Mahāprabhu asked Murāri Gupta, who worshiped Śrī Rāma with the Rāma *mantra*, to recite a prayer to Śrī Rāma. Murāri recited the *Rāmāṣṭaka* with great *prema*: “Having thus heard the eight verses about Raghunandana, the lion among kings, Bhagavān placed His foot on the head of Murāri the physician and wrote on his forehead: ‘O, by My grace, become “Rāmadāsa”!’”

## 70

On the night of the first city *kīrtana*, after the Kāzī had been delivered, Chā̃d Kāzī came along with the *kīrtana* as far as Śrīdhara's courtyard. When the *kīrtana* stopped there to rest, Mahāprabhu mercifully drank the water that was in Śrīdhara's leaky iron pot, calling it “water offered by a devotee.” From that spot the Kāzī turned back. That place, in the northeastern part of Māyāpura, is still called the place where the *kīrtana* rested.

## 71

On the day of the *mahā-prakāśa*, Mahāprabhu embraced Haridāsa, declared him an avatāra of Prahlāda, and gave him a boon.

When Viśvarūpa took *sannyāsa*, Śacīmātā blamed Advaita Ācārya for it. She thus committed an offense against a Vaiṣṇava, and the Lord freed her from that offense by having His mother take the dust of the Ācārya's feet.

## 72–73

One day Mahāprabhu was describing to the devotees the boundless glory of the holy name. Hearing it, an unfortunate student said, “All this glory of the name is not real: the scriptures merely praise the name.” To reinterpret the glory of the name like this is the offense against the name called *arthavāda*. No offense is as terrible as an offense against the name. The Lord forbade everyone to look at that offending student's face and, with His associates, bathed in the Gaṅgā *sacele*, that is, with His clothes on. The meaning is: whoever has seen the face of an offender of the name should bathe with his clothes on — that is the lesson.

## 76

O Uddhava! As strong devotion to Me can win Me over, so I am not won over by *aṣṭāṅga-yoga*, by *sāṅkhya-jñāna* — the doctrine of non-difference from Brahman, by *svādhyāya* — a brāhmaṇa's study of his own branch of the Veda, by every kind of austerity, or by *sannyāsa* and the like, which is renunciation.

## 78

Where am I, a great sinner and a pauper, and where is Kṛṣṇa, the abode of Śrī! Knowing me to be an unworthy son of a brāhmaṇa, He embraced me. This is truly astonishing.

## 79–86

One day the Lord, tired from the city *kīrtana* with the devotees, came to a certain place and planted a mango seed in the courtyard of the devotee who lived there. At once it bore fruit, and a mango festival took place. That place is now known as “Āmraghaṭṭa” (“Āmaghāṭā”).

## 89

One day Mahāprabhu was performing *saṅkīrtana* far away, and at that time the sky was massed with clouds. The Lord willed the clouds to go and ordered them away, and they at once dispersed. For this reason that sandbank of the Gaṅgā came to be called “Megher Car” (“the Cloud Sandbank”). Now, because the Gaṅgā has changed its course, that “Megher Car” has shifted to the village of “Belpukhuriyā.” And the place where Belpukhuriyā used to be is now called “Tāraṇavāsa” and “Ṭoṭā.”

## 111

Serving cows brings piety. In My previous birth I was a cowherd and served the cows. The piety I earned in this way was the cause (bore the fruit) of My becoming a “brāhmaṇa” this time.

## 117

The pastime of dragging the Yamunā: one day Baladeva, angry with the Yamunā, dragged the Yamunā with His plough and pestle. When Mahāprabhu, in the mood of Baladeva, cried, “Bring honey, bring honey!”, all the others at that moment were seeing that very pastime of dragging the Yamunā.

## 121

When preaching the name in the town, the Lord first ordered the townspeople who lived near Śrīvāsa's courtyard to chant the name of Hari while clapping their hands; gradually *mṛdaṅgas*, *karatālas* and the like began to be played. From then on *saṅkīrtana* has spread from door to door, and it continues to this day.

## 126

From the coming of Bakhtiyar Khilji up to the time of Cāṅd Kāzī, Hinduism in Navadvīpa had sunk into utter decline. Those who truly had faith in the Hindu religion would quietly say “Hari, Hari” once and then fall silent. That is why the Kazi said, “For so long Hinduism has not shown itself at all — by whose strength are you now undertaking such an effort?”

## 140

Then, by the strength of Śrī Śrī Gauracandra, the people grew bold and went almost mad.

## 148

In one part of the village of Brāhmaṇa-puṣkariṇī the old house of the Kazis still stands. Joined to the other part of that village is Tāraṇavāsa, formerly Bilva-puṣkariṇī. That village and the Kazis' Brāhmaṇa-puṣkariṇī were one and the same village, and so Mahāprabhu stood to Cāṅd Kāzī in the relation of a sister's son to a maternal uncle.

## 156–163

(The Kazi said:) That scripture, the Koran, distinguishes two kinds of path: *pravṛtti* (the path of engagement) and *nivṛtti* (the path of renunciation). On the path of *nivṛtti* killing living beings is forbidden. But those who, like us, follow the path of *pravṛtti* do not become sinners if they kill cows by the order of scripture. And look at your Vedas: they contain injunctions to kill cows, and that is why great sages have killed cows from time immemorial.

Mahāprabhu said: The Vedas contain no injunction to kill cows. The statements about sacrifice performed by killing a cow that one does find in them concern the *jaradgava*, that is, a very old cow. The sages would kill a *jaradgava* and then, by Vedic mantras, bring it back to life in a youthful body. Such killing is no killing at all; it is only a kindness to the old cow. Since the brāhmaṇas of Kali have no such power, cows may not be killed now.

## 164

The *aśvamedha*, the *gomedha*, *sannyāsa*, a *śrāddha* to the forefathers with meat, and begetting a son by the husband's brother — these five are forbidden in the age of Kali.

## 169–171

The scriptures of the *yavanas* are of three kinds: the old book of the “Yu” (the Jews), the Koran and the Bible. All these books have a beginning; none of them is beginningless like the words of the Veda. Therefore, since the reasoning in those scriptures has no firm root, it is open to doubt.

## 195

Even though the Pādśāh is your relative, he can punish you. The Pādśāh — the Pādśāh of Gauḍa, “Husain” Shah.

## 196–202

The Kazi said: “O Gaurahari, when I questioned that *mleccha* peon, he answered: ‘I said to the Hindus, “Some of you, calling yourselves Kṛṣṇadāsa, Rāmadāsa or Haridāsa, keep saying ‘Hari, Hari.’ But the word *hari, hari* means ‘I steal, I steal,’ so it seems you say ‘Hari, Hari’ (‘I take away, I take away’) because you mean to steal other people's wealth.” Ever since the day I joked with them like this, my tongue keeps saying “Hari, Hari” against my will, and I can do nothing about it.’”

## 211

***Nīca bāḍa bāḍa***: he performs Kṛṣṇa's *kīrtana* with many low-born people, and by this the low are “growing,” that is, rising.

## 222

***Tālāka*** — a solemnly made vow.

## 229

One night Mahāprabhu was performing *kīrtana* in Śrīvāsa's courtyard, and at that time one of Śrīvāsa's sons departed from this world. Fearing that the *rasa* of the *kīrtana* would be broken, Śrīvāsa forbade everyone to show any grief, and Mahāprabhu danced and sang *kīrtana* until late in the night. When the *kīrtana* ended, Mahāprabhu could understand that some misfortune had befallen the house. Learning that Śrīvāsa's son had departed, the Lord first expressed sorrow that He had not been told sooner. Then He had the dead boy laid before Him and asked him, “Boy, why are you leaving Śrīvāsa?” The dead boy replied, “I was destined to stay in Śrīvāsa's house for a certain number of days. Those days are over, and now by Your will I am going elsewhere. I am a *jīva*, eternally subordinate to You and dependent on You, and I have no right to do anything beyond Your will.” Hearing the dead boy's words, Śrīvāsa's family gained divine knowledge, and no grief remained in them. Then the boy's funeral rites were performed. The Lord said to Śrīvāsa, “The son you had has gone. But Nityānanda and I are your eternal sons; We can never leave you.”

## 231–232

A *yavana* tailor who lived near Śrīvāsa used to sew his clothes. When he watched Mahāprabhu's dancing with faith and was charmed, the Lord showed him the spiritual (*cinmaya*) nature of His own form. The tailor began to dance, mad with *prema*, crying, “I have seen! I have seen!” ***Āgala*** — foremost.

## 241

One night in the house of Śrī Candraśekhara Ācāryaratna the Lord assumed the form of Rukmiṇī and others and enacted a pastime. Advaita, Haridāsa and many others also put on various costumes.

## 250

***Doṣodgāra*** — blaming with mockery.

## 265

According to the scriptures, when a brāhmaṇa accepts *sannyāsa*, everyone — householders, brāhmaṇas and all the rest — bows to him with the thought of a *sannyāsī*, that is, knowing that a *sannyāsī* is worthy of obeisance. If I accept *sannyāsa*, the blaspheming brāhmaṇas will surely bow down to Me and receive good sense from Me.

## 272

At the end of Mahāprabhu's twenty-fourth year, on the day of the *saṅkramaṇa* (the sun's passage into a new sign) that begins the *uttarāyaṇa* and that fell in the bright fortnight of Māgha, Mahāprabhu left Śrī Navadvīpa at the end of the night. He swam across the Gaṅgā at “Nidayā's Ghāṭa,” reached Kaṇṭakanagara, that is, the village of Kāṭoyā, and accepted (one) *daṇḍa* from Keśava Bhāratī. On Mahāprabhu's order Candraśekhara Ācāryaratna performed all the ritual parts of *sannyāsa*. The *kīrtana* went on all day, and only when the day was nearly over was the shaving of the head completed. The next morning Śrī Kṛṣṇa Caitanya, dressed as a *sannyāsī* and bearing the *daṇḍa*, set out to wander through Rāḍha-deśa. Keśava Bhāratī went with Him for some distance.

## 275

***Caturvidha bhakta-bhāva*** (the four kinds of devotees' moods) — the four kinds of devotional mood based on the *rasas* of *dāsya*, *sakhya*, *vātsalya* and *madhura*.

## 281

Once Śrī Kṛṣṇa, in jest, manifested the four-armed form of Nārāyaṇa, endowed with wondrous beauty — and the love (*rāga*) of the *gopīs*, instead of awakening, shrank. So which *paṇḍita* can understand the workings of the *gopīs'* *bhāva*? For they follow the inaccessible path of *parakīya*, worshipping the son of Nanda alone.

## 293

At the beginning of the *rāsa*, Kṛṣṇa, in play, hid in a *kuñja*. Seeing the doe-eyed *gopīs* coming toward Him, He grew anxious and displayed His charming four-armed form. The ordinary *gopīs* said only, “This is not Śrī Kṛṣṇa, the object of our love.” But how wondrous is the glory of Rādhā's love! As soon as Śrī Rādhā arrived, Kṛṣṇa, try as He might, could not keep that four-armed form.

## 308

The truth that lies beyond *prakṛti* — that is the mark of the inconceivable. Logic (*tarka*) is material, and so it cannot touch that truth. Therefore one should not apply logic to what is inconceivable.

*Thus ends the seventeenth chapter of the “Amṛta-pravāha-bhāṣya.”*
