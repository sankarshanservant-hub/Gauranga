## 29

> Subheading: The body and senses without service to Kṛṣṇa are fruitless: (1) the futility of eyes engaged in enjoyment —

Śrī Kṛṣṇa's moonlike face is the shelter of the nectar of His flute-song and a mine of the nectar of beauty. If a gopī's eyes are deprived of seeing such a supremely charming form of Kṛṣṇa, it is better that a thunderbolt strike the head of the gopī who bears those eyes. In truth, when a gopī sees anything other than Kṛṣṇa, she shows aversion or remains indifferent — she feels no pleasure. The only object her sense of sight worships is the moon of Kṛṣṇa's face: it delights her eyes, and she serves it. When it is absent, it is better that a thunderbolt strike the head — the bearer and support of the eyes. And she finds no reason to keep her eyes for looking at other things when she is deprived of seeing Kṛṣṇa.

(*Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam* 2.3.17–24) “Rising and setting, the sun takes away the life span of all men — except of one whose moments are spent in talks about Uttamaśloka. Do the trees not live? ## 35

***Dainya* (humility).** (*Bhakti-rasāmṛta-sindhu*, Southern Division, fourth wave) “A feeling of powerlessness born of sorrow, fear, offense and the like is called *dainya*. It produces flattering words, sluggishness, dejection, anxious thoughts, numbness of the limbs, and so on.” When through sorrow, fear, offenses and the like one considers oneself utterly low, that is *dainya*. When *dainya* comes, there arise humble entreaties, weakness of heart, unease, various anxious thoughts and numbness of the limbs.

***Nirveda* (bitter despondency).** (*Bhakti-rasāmṛta-sindhu*, Southern Division, fourth wave) “Contempt for oneself born of great distress, separation, jealousy, right discernment and the like is called *nirveda*. Here appear anxious thoughts, tears, pallor, humility, sighs and so on.” Sorrowful self-contempt — because of great distress, separation or jealousy, or because one has done what should not be done and failed to do what should be done — is called *nirveda*. When *nirveda* comes, there arise anxious thoughts, tears, pallor, humility, sighs and so on.

***Viṣāda* (dejection).** (*Bhakti-rasāmṛta-sindhu*, Southern Division, fourth wave) “Remorse arising because the desired object is not attained, a task begun does not succeed, or calamity strikes, and also from an offense and the like — that is *viṣāda*. Here appear the search for a means and a helper, anxious thoughts, weeping, lamentation, sighs, pallor, dryness of the mouth and so on.” The remorse that comes when the desired object is not attained, when a planned and undertaken task fails, when calamity strikes or an offense has been committed and so on — that is *viṣāda*. When *viṣāda* comes, one searches for a means and a helper, grows anxious, weeps, laments, sighs, turns pale, one's mouth goes dry, and so on.

*Dainya*, *nirveda*, *viṣāda* and the others — thirty-three *vyabhicārī-bhāvas* in all — gain special prominence within the *sthāyi-bhāva* and move about in it. A *vyabhicārī-bhāva* is recognized by its signs: by words, by the eyebrows, eyes and other limbs, and by the *sāttvika-anubhāvas*. And because the *vyabhicārī-bhāvas* spread the movement of the emotion, they are called *sañcārī*.

## 47

The meeting of two *tattvas* — the *viṣaya*, the one who is served, and the *āśraya*, the servant — is called *avalambana*. The *āśraya* has hearing; the *viṣaya* has the sound of the flute. ## 63

> Subheading: The Lord's divine madness in *mahābhāva* —

***Sandhi*** (a joining of emotions). (*Bhakti-rasāmṛta-sindhu*, Southern Division, fourth wave) “*Sandhi* is the joining of two *bhāvas*, alike or different.” *Sarūpa-sandhi*: “The *sandhi* of two similar *bhāvas* that arise from different causes.” *Bhinna-rūpa sandhi*: “[The *sandhi*] of two different *bhāvas* born of one cause or of different causes.” The joining or meeting of two *bhāvas*, alike or different, is called *sandhi*. When two similar *bhāvas* born of different causes meet, that is *sarūpa-sandhi*. The meeting of two different *bhāvas* born of one cause or of different causes is called *bhinna-rūpa sandhi*. Examples of the *sandhi* of two *bhāvas* from one cause or from different causes: the joining of joy and apprehension, the joining of joy and dejection.

***Śābalya*** (a mingling of emotions). (*Bhakti-rasāmṛta-sindhu*, Southern Division, fourth wave) “*Śabalatva* is the clash of *bhāvas* with one another.” The mutual clash of *bhāvas* is called *śābalya*. When pride, dejection, humility, resolve, remembrance, apprehension, indignation, fear, despondency, fortitude, eagerness and other *bhāvas* clash, *śābalya* arises.

***Autsukya*** (eagerness). (*Bhakti-rasāmṛta-sindhu*, Southern Division, fourth wave) “*Autsukya* is the inability to bear delay, born of the longing to see or attain the desired object and the like. It produces dryness of the mouth, haste, anxious thoughts, sighs, immobility and so on.” The inability to bear delay because one longs to see the desired object and to attain it is called *autsukya*. In *autsukya* one sees dryness of the mouth, agitation, anxious thoughts, deep sighs and immobility.

***Cāpala*** (restlessness). (*Bhakti-rasāmṛta-sindhu*, Southern Division, fourth wave) “Lightness of mind caused by attachment, aversion and the like is *cāpala*. In it there arise thoughtlessness, harshness, acting as one pleases, and so on.” Lightness of mind through attachment and aversion is called *cāpala*. In it there arise thoughtlessness, harsh words, acting as one pleases, and so on.

***Roṣa*** (anger). (*Bhakti-rasāmṛta-sindhu*, Southern Division, fourth wave) “Fury born of an offense, harsh words and the like is *ugratā*. It leads to killing, binding, shaking the head, scolding, beating and so on.” Anger born of an offense or of objectionable words is called *ugratā* or *roṣa*. In it there arise killing, binding, shaking the head, scolding, beating and so on.

***Amarṣa*** (indignation). (*Bhakti-rasāmṛta-sindhu*, Southern Division, fourth wave) “Intolerance caused by reproach, dishonor and the like is *amarṣa*. In it there arise perspiration, shaking of the head, pallor, brooding, searching for a means, crying out, turning away, beating and so on.” Intolerance caused by reproach or censure, dishonor and the like is called *amarṣa*. In it there arise perspiration, shaking of the head, pallor, brooding, searching for a means, crying out, turning away, beating and so on.

## 72

> Subheading: The symptoms of the Lord's *mahābhāva* —

***Stambha*** (stupor) — one of the eight *sāttvika* transformations. (*Bhakti-rasāmṛta-sindhu*, Southern Division, third wave) “When the mind is filled with *sattva*, it places itself, agitated, in the *prāṇa*. The *prāṇa*, being transformed, greatly agitates the body. Then these *bhāvas*, *stambha* and the others, appear in the devotee's body. The *prāṇa*, resting in the element earth, produces *stambha*. *Stambha* arises from joy, fear, wonder, dejection and indignation. In it there is absence of speech and the like,[^72-1] motionlessness, blankness and so on.” When the mind reaches a *sāttvika* state, it places the restless mind in the *prāṇa*. The *prāṇa*, transformed, agitates the body. Then these *bhāvas*, *stambha* and the others, appear in the body of one engaged in *bhajana*. When the *prāṇa* rests in earth, one of the five elements, *stambha* arises. *Stambha* is born of joy, fear, wonder, dejection and anger. In *stambha* the activity of speech, hands, feet and so on ceases, and there is motionlessness, blankness and the like. *Stambha* is a particular state of the mind. The absence of speech and so on — a transformation born of the body — pervades both the outside and the inside. First it is subtle, later gross. Speechlessness and the like — that is, the blankness even of the organs of action — shows that the organs of knowledge, too, have ceased to act.

***Kampa*** (trembling). (Ibid.) “*Vepathu*, which makes the limbs quiver, arises from fright, indignation, joy and the like.” When the body quivers from great fear, anger, joy and the like, it is called *vepathu* or *kampa*.

***Sveda*** (perspiration). (*Bhakti-rasāmṛta-sindhu*, Southern Division, third wave) “*Sveda* is the moistening of the body born of joy, fear, anger and the like.” That is, the moisture the body produces from joy, fear, anger and so on is called *sveda*.

***Vaivarṇya*** (pallor). (*Bhakti-rasāmṛta-sindhu*, Southern Division, third wave) “*Vaivarṇya* is a change of color from dejection, anger, fear and the like. Here the knowers of *bhāva* name paleness, thinness and so on.” That is, the change of the body's color from dejection, anger, fear and so on is called *vaivarṇya*. With *vaivarṇya* paleness, thinness and the like are mentioned.

***Aśru*** (tears). (*Bhakti-rasāmṛta-sindhu*, Southern Division, third wave) “*Aśru* is water welling up in the eyes from joy, anger, dejection and the like. Tears of joy are cool; tears of anger and the like are hot. With all tears there is agitation of the eyes, redness, wiping and so on.” That is, the water that wells up in the eyes without any effort from joy, anger, dejection and the like is called *aśru*. Tears of joy are cool and tears of anger and so on are hot, but with all tears one sees restlessness of the eyes, redness, wiping and the like.

***Gadgada*** (a choked voice). (Ibid.) “*Vaisvarya*, a change of voice, arises from dejection, wonder, indignation, joy, fear and the like; it causes stammering and so on.” From dejection, wonder, anger, joy, fear and the like the voice changes — this is *vaisvarya*, or *svara-bheda*. This change of voice makes the speech falter.

***Pulaka*** (hair standing on end). (*Bhakti-rasāmṛta-sindhu*, Southern Division, third wave) “*Romāñca* arises from wonder, joy, enthusiasm, fear and the like. In it the hairs stand up, one touches one's limbs, and so on.” That is, from wonder, joy, enthusiasm, fear and the like the hairs stand on end — this is *pulaka*, or *romāñca*; with it one touches one's body and so on.

## 78

> Subheading: The Lord's devotees, sheltered in different *rasas* —

The mood of Śrī Paramānanda Purī (in Vraja, Uddhava), in which *vātsalya-rasa* predominates; the pure friendship (*sakhya*) of Rāmānanda (Arjuna, or Viśākhā); the pure servitude (*dāsya*) of Govinda and others, devoted to service; and the principal *rasa*, *madhura*, of the intimate devotees Gadādhara, Jagadānanda and Dāmodara-Svarūpa. ## 85

> Subheading: The author's impartiality —

In describing Śrī Gaurasundara's conduct exactly as it was, I do not seek the praise of the proponents of every kind of doctrine. I have not, in the hope that they would accept me, passed over the true facts of the Lord's life here — leaving things out, adding to them, veiling them or “correcting” them. Because Sanskrit verses are included in this book, many may object that one who does not know Sanskrit will not understand the true meaning of the verses.

## 86

In this book I have written nothing out of regard for anything, in opposition to anyone or in compliance with anyone's request: I have only described the *sahaja-vastu*, the natural reality. If someone attains the orientation of *rāga*, then, absorbed in it, he will easily realize all these descriptions. The natural reality is experienced by those who follow *rāga* (*rāgānuga*).
 If it is written down, such writing will reveal itself only in the hearts of those absorbed in *rāga*; one without *rāga* will not be able to enter it in that way. There is no use in writing about it here in order to make known the natural reality, which can only be experienced. Another reading is *yadi haya rāga-dveṣa* (“if there is attachment and aversion”), and then the meaning is: “If a person has given up service to Kṛṣṇa and is overcome by attachment — that is, absorption — in things other than Kṛṣṇa, and by aversion or dislike, he can in no way describe the transcendental love for Kṛṣṇa that is innate in the pure soul.”

## 88

> Subheading: A comparison with the *Bhāgavatam*, written in Sanskrit —

About the objection of the critics mentioned in verse 85, I can say this. The book *Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam* is full of Sanskrit verses, and all its commentaries are written in Sanskrit. Yet people in the three worlds understand it and attain devotion to Kṛṣṇa. In this *Caitanya-caritāmṛta* only two or four Sanskrit verses are quoted, and they are explained in Bengali verse. Why, then, should all the devotees of Gaura not be able to understand them?

## 93

> Subheading: Homage to the devotees, and fidelity to the path of *śrauta* (knowledge descending through hearing) —

Those “expert in *bhajana*,” those “engaged in *bhajana*,” and those “initiated into Kṛṣṇa's name and chanting it” — may all these three kinds of devotees, small and great, be merciful to me. A neophyte devotee (*kaniṣṭha*) devoted to argument lacks *siddhānta*, yet thinks himself a *rasika* devotee. He may consider that I write about *siddhānta* along with the pastimes out of pedantry, lack of devotion and a fondness for empty argument, may judge me guilty and withhold his mercy. Fearing this, I humbly submit: I have no independence of my own. I have written only what I learned of the truth of Śrī Gaura's pastimes from Śrī Rūpa and Raghunātha and from Śrī Dāmodara-Svarūpa, at whose lotus feet I am sold.
