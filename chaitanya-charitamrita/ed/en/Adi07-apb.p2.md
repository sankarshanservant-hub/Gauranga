## 94

A person whose vow is service to Kṛṣṇa loses control of his heart: as he chants the name of his most beloved Śrī Kṛṣṇa, attachment (*anurāga*) awakens in him, and his heart melts. Like a madman he becomes *loka-bāhya*, that is, he no longer looks to anyone: now he laughs, now he weeps, now he cries out, now he sings, dances and does much else.

## 97

*Khātodaka*: a little water in a ditch.

## 98

O Teacher of the world! I have seen You Yourself (Your *svarūpa*), and now I abide in a pure ocean of joy. All other happiness seems to me like a cow's hoofprint; even the happiness the *jīva* finds in dissolving into Brahman is only a hoofprint. Where a cow steps, a hollow is left; the water in it is tiny compared with the ocean.

## 108

The Upaniṣads are the Īśa, Kena, Kaṭha, Praśna, Muṇḍaka, Māṇḍūkya, Taittirīya, Aitareya, Chāndogya, Bṛhad-āraṇyaka and Śvetāśvatara: eleven Upaniṣads, the crown of the Vedas.

The *sūtra* is the *Brahma-sūtra*: four chapters, sixteen *pādas*. These two are the chief among the scriptures.

## 108–110

The truth that these chief scriptures teach by their primary meaning (*mukhya-vṛtti*), that is, by *abhidhā-vṛtti*, is supremely great. Śrī Śaṅkarācārya abandoned the primary meaning of these scriptures, established the conclusion of *kevalādvaita* by their secondary meaning (*gauṇa-vṛtti*), that is, by *lakṣaṇā-vṛtti*, and wrote his commentary. Whoever hears it loses every spiritual endeavor. You may ask: why did Śaṅkara Svāmī, a direct *avatāra* of Śiva, do such an improper thing? Then listen: he undertook this work at the Lord's command, and so he bears no fault. Thus in the *Padma Purāṇa* Śrī Mahādeva says: “Māyāvāda is a false doctrine; it is called covert Buddhism. O Devī, in the age of Kali I Myself devised it in the form of a *brāhmaṇa*. To bewilder all the people of this world in the age of Kali, I shall describe Brahman in another form — as devoid of qualities (*nirguṇa*). O Devī, in the great scripture, the Vedānta, I Myself shall expound the non-Vedic Māyāvāda, to bring the world to ruin.” And in the *Śiva Purāṇa* Bhagavān says: “At the beginning of Dvāpara and the other ages, appear by your partial power among humans and others, and by *āgamas* of your own invention turn people away from Me.”

## 111–115

The meaning that appears clearly, that is, directly, as soon as one reads a text is called the primary meaning (*mukhyārtha*). The *Bṛhad-āraṇyaka* says: “That is full, this is full; from the full the full comes forth” (5.1). The *Śvetāśvatara* says: “The ancient Puruṣa, possessed of wondrous potencies”; “He is beyond the tree [of worldly existence], time and forms, and other than them; from Him this world revolves; He brings *dharma*, removes sin, and is the Lord of all opulence” (6.6); “I know this great Puruṣa, radiant like the sun, beyond darkness” (3.8); “the supreme Master of masters, higher than the highest” (6.7); “the Puruṣa is the great Lord” (3.12); “His supreme potency is heard to be manifold” (6.8), and so on. The *Ṛg-veda* says: “The sages always see that supreme abode of Viṣṇu.” The *Praśna* says: “He glanced” (6.3). The *Aitareya* says: “He glanced” (1.1.1) and “He created these worlds” (1.1.2). The *Talavakāra* says: “Brahman knew [their pride] and appeared before them” (3.2). As soon as one reads many such statements of the Vedas, it becomes clear that [they speak] only of Bhagavān, the one Supreme Truth, full of the six opulences, who has none equal to Him or above Him. There are, of course, statements that deny form, such as *apāṇi-pādaḥ* (“without hands and feet”) (*Śvetāśvatara* 3.19). They must be understood only to mean that this Bhagavān's form is spiritual, and that His body and His opulence are spiritual opulence. The Māyāvādīs, headed by the ācārya, cover His spiritual opulence and declare Him “formless,” a transformation of the quality of *sattva*. But He Himself, His abode and His associates are all, by nature, spiritual bliss beyond *prakṛti*. How, then, can He be called a transformation of material *sattva*? In truth His form too, full of transcendental spiritual opulence, is real. Is the ācārya at fault for describing Him as formless? No — he is a servant carrying out an order. As the *Nārada-pañcarātra* says: “And conceal Me, so that this creation may keep on increasing.” But anyone else who hears such an explanation loses everything. There can be no worse blasphemy of Viṣṇu than to consider Viṣṇu's body “material.”

## 116–117

If the truth of the Lord is compared to a blazing fire, the countless *jīvas* can be compared to tiny sparks of that fire. The point is this: the Lord is a special fire — spiritual, unlimited and blazing. The countless *jīvas* have come forth from Him like tiny sparks and become separate *tattvas*. Here *māyā* plays no part in forming the *jīva*'s *svarūpa*; that is, nothing material takes place. You may ask: why form such particles of spirit at all? Then listen: the Lord's wondrous *svarūpa-śakti* has two kinds of tendencies — toward unlimited action and toward minute action. From the tendency toward unlimited action come the Lord's own form and the Vaikuṇṭha-*tattva*, the spiritual world; this tendency is called *cit-śakti*. From the tendency toward minute action come the countless *jīvas*, atomic consciousness; this tendency is called *jīva-śakti*. If the *svarūpa-śakti* did not have both these functions, His fullness would be impaired. Bhagavān is full of all opulence, and so the *jīva* — the minute action of His potency — must exist inevitably and necessarily. Therefore it is through the *jīva-tattva* that Kṛṣṇa's possession of potency (*vilāsa*) [is manifest] in the Kṛṣṇa-*tattva*. Without the *jīva-tattva*, Kṛṣṇa's full possession of potency could not be admitted. Where there are none to be ruled, there is no Ruler.
