## 91

Give up these two kinds of association with non-sādhus, and attachment to *varṇāśrama-dharma*, and take shelter of Kṛṣṇa alone, as an *akiñcana*, one who has nothing but Kṛṣṇa.

## 93

What wise man would leave You — who love Your devotees, who are truthful in speech, a friend and grateful — and take shelter of another? To the friends who serve You, You give all they desire, even Your very Self, and yet You neither decrease nor increase.

## 95

Ah, this Pūtanā, the sister of Bakāsura, full of wicked intent, made Him whom she wished to kill drink the *kālakūṭa* poison from her breast, and even so she attained the destination worthy of a mother. Apart from Him, Kṛṣṇa, what other merciful one can I take shelter of?

## 96

The “*akiñcana* devotee” and the “*śaraṇāgata* devotee” — these two have the same symptoms. But the *śaraṇāgata* has one symptom more: “offering of the self” (*ātma-samarpaṇa*).

## 97

The six symptoms of *śaraṇāgati*, taking shelter of Kṛṣṇa:

(1) *ānukūlya-saṅkalpa*, that is, the resolve: “Whatever is favorable to *kṛṣṇa-bhakti*, I will certainly accept”;

(2) *prātikūlya-vivarjana*, that is, renunciation: “Whatever is unfavorable to *kṛṣṇa-bhakti*, I will certainly reject”;

(3) “He will protect,” that is, the faith: “I have no protector but Kṛṣṇa.” This is not the faith that “knowledge of the nondifferent Brahman will save me from death,” but the faith that “Kṛṣṇa, in His mercy, will protect me”;

(4) accepting Kṛṣṇa as the *goptā*, the maintainer. One gives up the belief “By performing all my *karmas* I too will be maintained by the gods who preside over them,” and firmly believes: “Kṛṣṇa alone is my maintainer; among gods and men I have no other”;

(5) *ātma-nikṣepa*: the understanding “My will is not independent; it depends on Kṛṣṇa's will” — this is the offering of the self (*ātma-samarpaṇa*);

(6) *kārpaṇya*, that is, considering oneself lowly.

## 98

With his body the *śaraṇāgata* takes shelter of the places of the Lord's pastimes; with his mouth he says, “O Bhagavān, I am Yours”; in his mind he knows it — and he is filled with joy.

## 100

When the mortal *jīva* gives up all *karma*, offers himself fully to Me (Bhagavān) and acts by My will, he attains immortality and becomes *kalpita*, that is, fit to taste together with Me the *rasa* whose nature is spirit.

## 102

When *bhāva-bhakti*, which is the goal (*sādhya*), becomes attainable by action (*kṛti*), that is, by the senses, it is called *sādhana-bhakti*. Bhakti is the eternally perfect *bhāva* of the *jīva*, and *sādhyatā* means bringing it to manifestation in the heart. The meaning is this. In the *jīva*, a particle of spirit, there is by nature a particle of the bliss of Kṛṣṇa, the spiritual sun. Bound by *māyā*, the *jīva* has almost lost it in this life. That eternally perfect *bhāva* is what is to be manifested in the heart. In this condition the eternally perfect becomes a goal (*sādhya*). And when that bhakti — the *bhāva* to be attained — begins to be practiced with the senses of the conditioned *jīva*, it is called *sādhana-bhakti*.

## 103–106

Hearing, chanting and remembering in a favorable mood are the *svarūpa-lakṣaṇa*, the essential characteristic, of this bhakti. When one gives up other desires (*anyābhilāṣa*) and cuts one's ties with *jñāna* and *karma*, this essential characteristic produces “the treasure of *prema*.” Love for Kṛṣṇa is an eternally perfect reality. It can never be attained (by any means, any *abhidheya*, other than pure bhakti);[^103–106-1] it can arise only in a heart purified by hearing and the other practices. Therefore *sādhana-bhakti* consists above all of pure hearing, chanting and the other activities. It is of two kinds, *vaidhī* and *rāgānugā*. When those in whose hearts *rāga* has not yet awakened take up worship at the command of scripture, that is *vaidhī-bhakti*.

## 107

O Bhārata, Bhagavān Hari, the Lord and the Soul of all, is always to be heard about, glorified and remembered by those who desire fearlessness.

## 109

“Viṣṇu is always to be remembered and never forgotten” — all injunctions and prohibitions follow these two statements. The meaning is this: all the injunctions (*vidhi*) that have arisen in scripture and all the prohibitions (*niṣedha*) it states rest on these two statements. Whatever brings the Lord to mind is a duty, and so a *vidhi*; any act that makes one forget the Lord is a *niṣedha*.

## 111–125

(1) Taking shelter at the feet of the guru; (2) *dīkṣā*, that is, initiation into the mantra; (3) service to the guru; (4) learning about true *dharma* and inquiring about it; (5) following the path of the sādhus; (6) giving up one's own enjoyment for Kṛṣṇa's pleasure; (7) living in a holy place of Kṛṣṇa; (8) accepting only what one needs to maintain life; (9) fasting on Ekādaśī; (10) honoring the *dhātrī* (*āmalakī*) tree, the *aśvattha* tree, cows, *brāhmaṇas* and Vaiṣṇavas. These ten limbs are the beginning of *bhajana*. Then: (11) keeping far away from offenses in service (*sevāparādha*) and offenses against the holy name (*nāmāparādha*); (12) giving up the company of non-Vaiṣṇavas; (13) not making many disciples; (14) not studying many books in part (*kalā*, that is, piecemeal) and not indulging in explanations for argument's sake; (15) being equal in loss and gain; (16) not being overcome by grief and the like; (17) not disrespecting other gods or scriptures; (18) not listening to blasphemy of Viṣṇu and the Vaiṣṇavas; (19) not listening to *grāmya-vārtā*, that is, household talk rooted in the sense gratification of men and women; (20) not disturbing the mind of any living being. These last ten limbs, marked by prohibition, are to be practiced negatively (*vyatireka*). Two limbs — “freedom from miserliness in dealings” (*akārpaṇya*) and “not undertaking great enterprises” — the *Bhakti-rasāmṛta-sindhu* counts among those ten limbs. And the limb “not listening to *grāmya-vārtā*,” mentioned in this book, the *Bhakti-rasāmṛta-sindhu* does not include among those ten.

These twenty limbs are like the entrance to the temple of *bhajana*. Three of them — “taking shelter at the feet of the guru,” “*dīkṣā*” and “service to the guru” — are counted as principal limbs. (1) Hearing; (2) chanting; (3) remembering; (4) worship; (5) offering prayers (*vandana*); (6) service (*paricaryā*); (7) *dāsya*, servitude; (8) *sakhya*, friendship; (9) *ātma-nivedana*, offering oneself; (10) dancing before the Śrī Vigraha; (11) singing; (12) *vijñapti*, opening one's heart to Him; (13) *daṇḍavat-praṇāma*, falling flat like a rod; (14) *abhyutthāna*, that is, rising on seeing Bhagavān or a devotee approach; (15) *anuvrajyā*, that is, following behind when a devotee or Bhagavān sets out; (16) going to holy places and to the temples of the Lord; (17) circumambulation (*parikramā*); (18) reciting prayers; (19) *japa*; (20) *saṅkīrtana*; (21) smelling the fragrance of incense and garlands that are the Lord's *prasāda*; (22) honoring *mahā-prasāda*; (23) seeing the festive *ārati*; (24) seeing the Śrī Mūrti; (25) offering to Bhagavān what is dear to oneself; (26) meditation; serving the *tadīya*, that is, (27) serving Tulasī and the others, (28) serving the Vaiṣṇavas, (29) living in Mathurā and (30) relishing the *Bhāgavatam*; (31) every endeavor for Kṛṣṇa; (32) waiting for His mercy; (33) celebrating His appearance days and other festivals with the devotees; (34) taking shelter of Him in every way; (35) Kārttika and other vows. To these thirty-five limbs four more must be added: (1) wearing the marks of a Vaiṣṇava on the body, (2) wearing the letters of Hari's names, (3) wearing *nirmālya*, the remnants of what has been offered, and (4) drinking *caraṇāmṛta*. Kavirāja Gosvāmī considered these four limbs part of *arcana* and the rest. With them there are thirty-nine (39) limbs. To these the five limbs must be added again: 1. *sādhu-saṅga*, 2. *nāma-kīrtana*, 3. hearing the *Bhāgavatam*, 4. living in Mathurā, 5. serving the Śrī Mūrti with faith. At the end of his description of the sixty-four limbs of *vaidhī-bhakti* (*Bhakti-rasāmṛta-sindhu*, Pūrva-vibhāga, second *laharī*), Śrī Rūpa Gosvāmī writes: “These five limbs, though already written of above, are praised here again to show that they are the best of all.” Adding these five gives forty-four limbs, and these forty-four together with the twenty mentioned before make sixty-four limbs of bhakti in all. These sixty-four limbs of bhakti are distinct kinds of worship (*yajana*, or *upāsanā*) with the body, the senses and the inner faculty (*antaḥ-karaṇa*). Some of them are entirely separate, and some are of a mixed nature.

## 126

One should associate with a sādhu who is full of affection (*snigdha*), whose desires are of the same kind as one's own, and who is superior to oneself. In the same way, one should relish the meaning of *Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam* together with *rasika* sādhus.

## 127

Out of special faith: love for serving the feet of the Śrī Mūrti, *nāma-saṅkīrtana*, and living in Mathurā-maṇḍala.

## 128

These last five limbs are hard to fathom at once, and their power is wondrous. Let alone faith in them — even the slightest connection with them becomes, for a person free from offenses, the cause of the birth of *bhāva*.

## 131

King Parīkṣit attained Kṛṣṇa in the best way by hearing about Śrī Viṣṇu; Śukadeva by glorifying Him; Prahlāda by remembering Him; Lakṣmī by serving His feet; King Pṛthu by worshiping Him; Akrūra by offering Him prayers; Hanumān, the lord of the monkeys, by serving Him as a servant; Arjuna by friendship with Him; and Bali by offering Him all he had, and himself.

## 132–134

Mahārāja Ambarīṣa engaged his mind in Kṛṣṇa's lotus feet, his speech in describing the qualities of Vaikuṇṭha, his two hands in cleaning Hari's temple and similar service, his ears in narrations of Kṛṣṇa, his two eyes in seeing Kṛṣṇa's Śrī Mūrti, his body in touching the bodies of Kṛṣṇa's servants, his sense of smell (his nose) in the fragrance of Kṛṣṇa's lotus feet, his tongue in tasting the *tulasī* offered to Kṛṣṇa, his two feet in walking to Kṛṣṇa's holy places, his head in bowing at the feet of Hṛṣīkeśa, and his “desire” (*kāma*) in selfless servitude. And he engaged them in such a way that there arose in him the *rati* whose shelter is Kṛṣṇa's devotees.

## 136

O King, one who has given up worldly duties and with his whole being taken shelter of Mukunda, the giver of shelter, is no longer a debtor to the gods, the sages, the other living beings, his kinsmen or the ancestors. The meaning is this. As soon as a person is born, he becomes a debtor of all these debts, and, as the scriptures prescribe, he repays them by performing many kinds of duties. But one who has given up all desires and taken shelter at Kṛṣṇa's feet finds all these debts repaid even without performing the prescribed rites.

## 137–138

One who has given up all the forms of *dharma* based on Vedic injunctions and worships the Lord as a *niṣkiñcana*, one who has nothing but Kṛṣṇa, by his very nature has no inclination toward any forbidden sin. And if for some reason a sin does occur, that is, happens to be committed, Kṛṣṇa purifies him without making him perform any atonement.

## 139

One who, giving up every other feeling, serves the feet of Hari Himself — if such a person, dear to Kṛṣṇa, ever somehow commits *vikarma* (sin), the Supreme Lord Hari, staying in his heart, destroys that sin.

## 141

For a yogī dear to Me, endowed with devotion to Me and with his mind fixed on Me alone, efforts for *jñāna* and *vairāgya* are generally not beneficial. The meaning is this: bhakti is by its very nature independent. *Jñāna*, *vairāgya*, *yoga* and the rest may be slightly useful to it at first, but they are not counted among its limbs.

## 142

O hunter, it is not surprising that nonviolence and the other virtues have appeared in you: those who have taken up *hari-bhakti* cause no suffering to others.

## 144

The bhakti of the devotees who live in Vraja, whose nature is *rāga*, is the principal one (*mukhyā*); that is, such bhakti exists nowhere else. And the bhakti that lives in following the residents of Vraja is called *rāgānugā-bhakti*.

## 145

The natural inclination to serve the beloved (*iṣṭa-vastu*), full of supreme absorption, is called *rāga*. When *kṛṣṇa-bhakti* is *tanmayī*, full of such *rāga*, it is called *rāgātmikā*.

## 148

*Anugati* means following.

## 149

Among the residents of Vraja and others, *rāgātmikā-bhakti* shines in manifest form. And the bhakti that follows (*anusṛtā*, that is, *anugatā*) that bhakti is *rāgānugā-bhakti*.

## 150

When one hears of the sweetness of the *bhāvas* and the rest of the residents of Vraja, the intelligence relies on greed (*lobha*) — and this is what gives one the qualification for *rāgānugā-bhakti*. Neither scripture nor reasoning is the sign that this greed has arisen.

## 153

Those in whom greed for *rāgātmikā-bhakti* has arisen should, following the activities of the people of Vraja, render external service in the form of a *sādhaka* and internal service in the form of a *siddha*.

## 154

Those dearest to Kṛṣṇa are the devotees who live in Vraja. Whoever among them longs for the sweetness of a particular devotee of Vraja and considers following him to be the service he desires keeps behind him and, with his mind turned within, constantly serves Kṛṣṇa.

## 155

Always remembering Kṛṣṇa and the one he himself has chosen from among those dearest to Him, and absorbed in narrations about them, he should always live in Vraja. If he cannot live in Vraja in body, he should live in Vraja at least in mind.

## 157

Those for whom I am the beloved, the Self, the son, the friend, the guru, the well-wisher, the deity and the cherished one are always devoted to Me. O mother of peaceful form, My wheel of time never destroys them.

## 158

Again and again I bow to those who always, with eagerness, meditate on Hari as husband, son, well-wisher, brother, father, friend and so on.
