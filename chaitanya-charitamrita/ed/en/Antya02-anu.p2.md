## 117

> Subheading: The Lord's reply: He cannot bear to see the face of a *vairāgī* who converses with a *prakṛti* (a woman) —

“Simplicity” is the chief mark of a Vaiṣṇava, and “duplicity” is a side shoot (*upaśākhā*) hostile to devotion. As attachment to Kṛṣṇa grows, the devotee loses his taste for all that is not Kṛṣṇa and gives up the sense objects born of a gaze that seeks worldly enjoyment. But if people see such an attachment in him and his duplicity comes to light, they can no longer trust his conduct.

## 118

> Subheading: The material senses by nature incline to enjoyment; looking at a woman bears poisonous fruit —

To take in the five objects — form, sound, smell, taste and touch — is the nature of the five senses: eyes, ears, nose, tongue and skin. Some conditioned souls think themselves able to control the senses, but because they have turned away [from the Lord] (*bahirmukhatā*), the senses remain untamable for them. When one looks with a desire for enjoyment, the sense object is present before him; so a man of worldly mind, even after taking up the *dharma* of a *muni*, is shaken and agitated at the sight of a wooden figure of a woman.

## 119

> Subheading: For one who thinks himself a man, the company of women is forbidden, as fire must be kept from ghee — (*Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam* 9.19.17 and *Manu-saṁhitā* 2.215)

Mahārāja Yayāti, ruled by lust and conquered by a woman, long enjoyed gross worldly objects. At last, realizing that he was ruining himself completely, he became disillusioned (*nirveda*); describing his own character and conduct to his wife Devayānī, he condemns the company of women:

**mātrā** (with one's mother) **svasrā** (with one's sister) **duhitrā** **vā** (or with one's daughter) **aviviktāsanaḥ** (one whose seat is not apart, that is, close beside them) **na** **vaset** (one should not stay; there is a variant reading *bhavet*[^119-1]; for) **balavān** (possessing great strength) **indriya-grāmaḥ** (the host of the senses) **vidvāṁsam** (a man who knows how to be freed from bondage) **api** (even) **karṣati** (draws, that is, binds him in bondage).
