# Amṛta-pravāha-bhāṣya on Antya-līlā 17

## 0

**Summary.** Mahāprabhu was overcome by the madness of love (*premonmāda*) in many different forms. This chapter describes how one night, without opening the doors, He passed over three walls and fell among the cows from Tailaṅga, having taken the shape of a tortoise.

## 1

I write of the most wonderful, unearthly acts of Śrī Gauracandra in His divine madness only after hearing of them from the mouths of those who saw them with their own eyes.

## 29

**karṇa-tṛṣṇāya** — from thirst to hear of Kṛṣṇa's qualities.

## 33–38

Absorbed in their feeling, the *gopīs* entered the *rāsa-līlā*. There they heard Kṛṣṇa's words of disregard (*upekṣā-vacana*), that is, His indifferent words, and took it as true that Kṛṣṇa had rejected them. So they speak to Kṛṣṇa in anger: “O lover (*nāgara*), tell us: is there any worthy woman in the three worlds whom Your flute does not attract? When You play Your flute in this world, its sound, like a *yoginī* who has mastered mantras and the like, becomes a go-between (*dūtī*) and enchants (allures) the minds of women. It greatly increases their longing, makes them abandon the path enjoined by the Vedas (serving their husbands, elders, and others), and delivers them to You (in the mood of *parakīyā* beloveds). With Your flute and with the arrows of Kāma — Your glances — You pierced us, made us give up the path of *dharma*, shame, and fear, and brought us to You. You Yourself made us leave our husbands and so on, and now You point out to us that this is a sin and, like a righteous man, teach us *dharma*! In Your mind is one thing, in Your words another, and in Your conduct a third. All this is the skilled trickery of a cheat (mere cunning). To You it is only a jest, but for a woman it is utter ruin. So give up all this deceit. First, the sound of the flute is nectarean buttermilk; on top of that, sweet words — the nectar of speech; and on top of that, a nectarean sound [of ornaments]. These three nectars together steal our ears, mind, and life.”

## 38

**śiñjita** — sound.

## 40

O friend! His voice is deep like a cloud; the sound of His ornaments draws the ears; His playful words are full of artful turns (*bhaṅgī*); the sound of His flute draws the hearts of Lakṣmī and other women. This Madana-mohana increases the longing of My ears.

## 41–48

The deep sound of His voice reigns, having conquered the rumbling of a new cloud; His sweet singing puts the cuckoo to shame. When even the smallest particle of it enters the ears, it so submerges (overpowers) the world's “ears,” that is, all other sounds, that those “ears” can never come back.[^41-1] O friend, the virtues of that sound of Kṛṣṇa have stolen My ears, and now, deprived of it, I must die of thirst. The tinkling of the bells on His ankles and waist defeats the voices of swans and *sārasa* cranes, and the jingling of His bracelets puts the *caṭaka* bird to shame. Whoever lets that sound into his ears even once allows no other sound to enter them.

The sweetness of Kṛṣṇa's words is a nectar surpassing even nectar, and the camphor of His smile is mixed into it as well. His words convey (*vyañjanā*) the power of the word (*śabda-śakti*), the power of meaning (*artha-śakti*) and the various *rasas*, beginning with *śṛṅgāra*, and every syllable of them is adorned with *narma*, that is, jest. A single drop (*bindu*) of that nectar is the very life of the *cakora* bird of the ears; on the hope of it alone the *cakora* of the ears lives. Sometimes by good fortune it gets that drop, sometimes by misfortune it does not, and when it does not, it lies at death's door from thirst.

Once the women of the world hear the sweet sound of His flute, their minds lose all hold (grow slack), the knots of their sashes come loose, and they become His maidservants without any price and run to Kṛṣṇa like madwomen. Lakṣmī-ṭhākurāṇī, hearing His soft song (*kākalī*), comes to Kṛṣṇa in hope. But she does not obtain Kṛṣṇa's company, and the waves of her thirst[^41-2] swell. In that hope she performs austerities, yet even so she cannot obtain Kṛṣṇa.

These four kinds of nectar of sound — His words, the tinkling of His anklets and bracelets, the sound of His voice and the sound of His flute — enter only the ears of the fortunate. For one whose ears this fourfold nectar of sound has not entered, those ears were born in vain: like a cracked cowrie shell, they are worthless.

## 43

**caṭaka** — a particular bird.

## 44

**śabda, artha — dui śakti**: *abhidhā* (the direct sense) and *lakṣaṇā* (the figurative sense) are the two powers of the word; of these, ornaments of meaning (*arthālaṅkāra*) and the like are the power of meaning.

## 50

**Līlāśuka** — Bilvamaṅgala Gosvāmī.

## 51

Alas, what shall I do! And whom shall I tell! What I did in the hope of Him — let it end there; now speak of something else, something auspicious (*dhanya*, good). For He Himself (as Kāma) has lain down in My heart — how then can I give up talk of Him? Toward Kṛṣṇa — the embodiment of the sweetest of sweet smiles, a festival for the mind and eyes — My thirst, full of humility (wretched), keeps growing all the time.

## 54

**piṅgalāra vacana-smṛti** — the courtesan Piṅgalā said: “Hope is the greatest misery; giving up hope is the greatest happiness.”[^54-1] Remembering those words, the Lord awakened through them a new feeling within Himself and began to determine the meaning of the verse.

## 57

**kṛṣṇe karāya ‘kāma’-jñāna** — makes one see Kṛṣṇa as Kandarpa.

## 59

**vāma-dīna** — wretched because of contrariness (*vāmya*). The thirst for Kṛṣṇa, whose sweetly smiling face is an elixir (*rasāyana*) for the mind and eyes, doubles.

## 72

Without the three bolted doors being opened, He came out of that room, crossed three walls and fell among the Tailaṅga cows. His whole body contracted, and in separation from Kṛṣṇa He took the shape of a tortoise. That Śrī Gaurāṅgadeva, rising in my heart, maddens me.

*Thus ends the seventeenth chapter of the “Amṛta-pravāha-bhāṣya.”*

[^41-1]: The verse translation reads differently: even one drop of that sound floods the ears of the whole world, and the ears can never return (for Bhaktivinoda, the “ears” here are the other sounds of the world).

[^41-2]: The edition reads *tṛṣṇā-raṅga* (“the play of thirst”); verse 47 has *tṛṣṇā-taraṅga* (“the waves of thirst”); apparently a misprint.

[^54-1]: Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam 11.8.44.
