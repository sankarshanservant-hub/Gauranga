## 93

The highest limit of Kṛṣṇa's sweetness lies in His beauty, which has no equal and nothing above it (*asamordhva*). Yet in the company of the goddesses of Vraja (*vraja-devīs*) that sweetness grows without end. Therefore love for Gopījana-vallabha, the beloved of the gopīs, is the essence of the goal for all devotees. In no other state of *rasa* does the devotee attain Kṛṣṇa as he does here, in the fullness of His sweetness.

## 94

Bhagavān, the son of Devakī, is the essence of all beauty. Yet among the goddesses of Vraja He shone with special splendor, like a great emerald (*marakata*) among golden jewels.

## 95

Having heard these conclusions, Mahāprabhu said, “Love for Śrī Gopījana-vallabha is indeed the limit of the truth of the goal (*sādhya-tattva*). Still, if there is anything more, tell Me.”

## 97

Of the love for Śrī Kṛṣṇa that all the gopīs share, Śrī Rādhā's love for Kṛṣṇa is the crest jewel of all goals (*sādhya-śiromaṇi*). Ordinary *jīvas* are given no instruction to take up the *bhāva* that belongs to Her position. In the perfected state, however, a *jīva* may become fit to take up a very exalted *bhāva* of love for Kṛṣṇa that follows that *bhāva*, that is, conforms to it. In the stage of practice one should follow the *bhāva* of Rādhikā's friends (*sakhīs*) and their maidservants. The *bhāva* of Rādhikā on seeing Uddhava, which was seen in Mahāprabhu, is beyond the *jīva's* reach, though it may be followed in some other form.

## 101–102

In the *rāsa-līlā* Śrī Kṛṣṇa saw that while Rādhikā was together with all the other gopīs, His love for Her could not be independent (*nirapekṣa*)[^101-1]. Because of regard for the others, the depth of love did not unfold. Therefore Śrī Kṛṣṇa, wary of the gopīs, stole Rādhikā away from the place of the *rāsa* dance and went off, apart from the other gopīs. The verse *kaṁsārir api* (verse 105) is the example here.

## 104

In the circle of the *rāsa* Śrī Rādhikā saw an attachment to the gopīs (*mamatā*) of the kind found in ordinary love, and out of the contrariness born of [Her love's] crookedness (*kauṭilya-vāmatā*) She left the circle. Kṛṣṇa wanted Śrīmatī to nourish the *rasa* of the *rāsa-līlā*. Without Her, Śrī Kṛṣṇa grew dejected and, lamenting, began to wander through the forest in search of Śrīmatī.

## 106

Mādhava's mind was wounded by the arrows of Anaṅga, and He was filled with remorse. In the forest on the bank of the Yamunā, the daughter of Kalinda, He searched everywhere for Rādhikā but did not find Her; then He entered a grove and gave way to grief.

## 109–110

In the circle of the *rāsa* there was one form of Kṛṣṇa between every two gopīs, and one form of Kṛṣṇa beside Śrī Rādhikā; thus He manifested Himself. At this Rādhikā displayed the “contrariness” (*vāmatā*) of Her crooked love. The *Ujjvala-nīlamaṇi* says that love by nature moves crookedly, like a snake; therefore between a young man and a young woman *māna* of two kinds arises, “without cause” and “with cause.”

## 127

The Lord said, “Do not think that since I was born in a brāhmaṇa family and have taken *sannyāsa*, it is improper for Me to learn *dharma* from *śūdras*.” For a brāhmaṇa guru is needed only for instruction in the *dharma* of *varṇāśrama* and for initiation (*dīkṣā*). But the knowledge of the truth of Kṛṣṇa is the highest goal (*paramārtha*) of all *jīvas*. When one asks who is qualified to be the guru of this knowledge of the truth, the conclusion is simply this: whether brāhmaṇa or *śūdra* by birth, householder or *sannyāsī*, only one who knows the truth of Kṛṣṇa can be a guru. The *Śrī Hari-bhakti-vilāsa* says that one should not take a Kṛṣṇa mantra from a person of low *varṇa* while a qualified person of high *varṇa* is available. That statement, however, is meant for Vaiṣṇavas who are mindful of public opinion, that is, for those who live in the world and wish to aim in some measure at the highest goal by the accepted rules. For those who know the purport of *vaidhī* and *rāgānugā bhakti* and wish to attain pure devotion to Kṛṣṇa, the rule is to choose as guru a qualified knower of the truth of Kṛṣṇa, in whatever *varṇa* or *āśrama* he may be found. The *Śrī Hari-bhakti-vilāsa* quotes the *Padma Purāṇa*: “The devotees of Bhagavān are not *śūdras*; they are the best of *bhāgavatas*. In every *varṇa*, the *śūdras* are those who are not devoted to Janārdana. A brāhmaṇa expert in the six duties and learned in mantras and *tantras* cannot be a guru if he is not a Vaiṣṇava; a Vaiṣṇava, even a dog-eater, is a guru. Even one born in a great family, initiated into all the sacrifices and studying a thousand branches [of the Vedas] cannot be a guru if he is not a Vaiṣṇava. Brāhmaṇas, *kṣatriyas* and *vaiśyas* are the gurus of those born *śūdras*; and *śūdras* who are dear to Bhagavān are the gurus of those three.”

## 137–138

In Vṛndāvana, the abode made of spirit, Śrī Kṛṣṇa is present in the form of the ever-new Madana, beyond *prakṛti*. By the word *madana* worldly poets usually mean *kāma* as it is in the material world — the mutual attraction of lumps of flesh, a principle utterly material and base. Bound by matter, the *jīvas* have identified themselves with the body and accepted the rule of that *kāma*. When a *jīva* comes to know the truth of his relationship with Kṛṣṇa (*kṛṣṇa-sambandha-tattva*), he attains a transcendental, spiritual position. That position is of two kinds: *svarūpa-gata* (in one's own true nature) and *vastu-gata* (in actual fact). Sometimes the *jīva* has already realized the truth, but “in actual fact” his connection with matter has not yet gone. If in such a state the spiritual truth has dawned in him to some degree, he dwells in Vṛndāvana “in his true nature,” but not “in actual fact.” When by Kṛṣṇa's will even the trace of his connection with gross and subtle matter is gone, he dwells in Vṛndāvana “in actual fact.” Dwelling in one's true nature includes *sādhana*: then the *jīva* worships Kṛṣṇa with the spiritual *kāma-gāyatrī* and the spiritual *kāma-bīja*. Man and woman, the moving and the unmoving — Kṛṣṇa, who captivates every heart, whose very nature is Manmatha-manmatha, the Cupid of Cupid, attracts them all.

The *kāma-gāyatrī* is a particular Vedic mantra of twenty-four and a half syllables. The *kāma-bīja* is the seed syllable (*bīja*) that is chanted in the worship of Kṛṣṇa.

## 140

In the five kinds of worship through the nectar of *rasa* described above, the devotee is the *āśraya* (the abode) of that *rasa*, and Śrī Kṛṣṇa, the object of worship, is its *viṣaya* (the object).

## 141

(*Bhakti-rasāmṛta-sindhu*) May Śrī Kṛṣṇacandra be victorious — the embodiment of the nectar of all *rasas*, who with His spreading radiance captivated the two *sakhīs* named Tārakā and Pāli, who won over Śyāmā and the *sakhī* Lalitā, and who is supremely dear to Rādhā. The purport is this: in whatever *rasa* one worships Him, Śrī Kṛṣṇa, though He is the embodiment of the nectar of that *rasa*, is the one supreme object (*viṣaya*) of Rādhikā's *rasa* alone.

## 142

*Śṛṅgāra* is the king of *rasas* (*rasa-rāja*), and Śrī Kṛṣṇa bears a form made of it. Therefore Kṛṣṇa's beautiful form steals the heart even of Kṛṣṇa Himself.

## 145

The Bhūmā-puruṣa said, “O Kṛṣṇa and Arjuna, I brought the brāhmaṇa's sons here because I wished to see you. You have descended with all Your parts to protect the *dharma* of the world; kill the *asuras* who burden the earth and quickly come back here.” The purport is this: the Bhūmā-puruṣa, the husband of Lakṣmī, was enchanted by Śrī Kṛṣṇa's sweetness and, wishing to see His form, took *darśana* of Kṛṣṇa on the pretext of stealing the brāhmaṇa's sons.

## 146

“O Lord! Desiring the dust of Your feet, Kamalā performed austerities for a long time, giving up all desires and keeping vows. By what pious merit this serpent Kāliya earned the right to that dust of Your feet, we do not know.”

## 147–161

If one studies the fourth chapter of the Ādi-līlā here, all these points will be well understood.

## 174

“The *bhāvas* beginning with *kila-kiñcita*” — there are twenty of them. The twenty *bhāvas* are: (1) those born of the body (*aṅgaja*) — *bhāva*, *hāva*, *helā*; (2) those born of the self (*ātmaja*) — *śobhā*, *kānti*, *dīpti*, *mādhurya*, *pragalbhatā*, *audārya* and *dhairya*; (3) those born of nature (*svabhāvaja*) — *kila-kiñcita*, *līlā*, *vilāsa*, *vicchitti*, *vibhrama*, *moṭṭāyita*, *kuṭṭamita*, *bibboka*, *lalita* and *vikṛta*.

“The flower garland of qualities.” Śrīmatī's qualities are of three kinds: mental, verbal and bodily. Gratitude, forbearance, compassion and the like are mental; speech that delights the ear and the like are verbal; and excellences, age, form, loveliness, beauty and the like are bodily qualities.

## 176

“The *sakhīs* are Her thoughts of Kṛṣṇa's pastimes.” The eight states of mind of Śrīmatī, who is the very bliss of Kṛṣṇa's pastimes, are the eight *sakhīs*, and the states that follow them are all the other *mañjarīs*.

## 179

*Śyāma-rasa* is the *madhura-rasa*.

## 164–180

In describing Śrī Rādhikā's qualities, Kavirāja Gosvāmī relies on the prayer of Śrī Raghunātha dāsa Gosvāmī called *Premāmbhoja-makaranda*:

“[You,] whose form is manifested by the radiant *cintāmaṇi* of *mahābhāva*, who shine anointed with the finest fragrant unguent — the love (*praṇaya*) of Your friends; who are bathed in the waves of the nectar of compassion, the stream of the nectar of youth and the floods of the nectar of loveliness, and who make Indirā fade; whose limbs are covered by the silken garment of modesty, who are adorned with the saffron of beauty, whose body is painted with radiant dark musk; trembling, tears, hair standing on end, stupor, perspiration, faltering voice, flushing, madness and numbness — You who are adorned with ornaments of these nine finest jewels and wear a garland of flowers, the series of Your qualities, who are perfumed with the fragrant powder of the fine quality of being *dhīrā-adhīrā*; whose braided hair is hidden *māna*, who shine with the *tilaka* of good fortune, whose ears glitter with the earrings of hearing Kṛṣṇa's name and fame; whose lips are red with the betel of *rāga*, whose eye-black is the crookedness of love, who are scented with the camphor of the smile that flows from playful words; who sit playfully on the couch of pride in the inner chamber of fragrance and are marked with the trembling pendant of *prema-vaicittya*; whose breasts are hidden by the fine bodice tied with the anger of *praṇaya*, whose *kacchapī vīṇā* sounds with the fame and beauty that dry up the faces and hearts of Your rivals; who have playfully laid Your lotus hand on the shoulder of Your friend, youth (*madhyatā*), who are Śyāmā and serve the honey drink of the joy of love for Śyāma — bowing to You, this person, holding a straw between his teeth, begs: revive this miserable one by sprinkling him with the nectar of service to You! The merciful one does not abandon one who has come for shelter, even if he is wicked; therefore, O Gāndharvikā, alas, alas, do not abandon this one either, such as he is! Whoever recites this king of prayers called *Premāmbhoja-makaranda*, the cause of Śrī Rādhikā's mercy, will attain service to Her.”

[The meaning of the prayer:] Her form is manifested by the radiant *cintāmaṇi* of *mahābhāva*; the love (*praṇaya*) of the *sakhīs* for Kṛṣṇa is the fragrant *kuṅkuma* and the rest, from which She has gained a beautiful luster (1). In the morning Her form is bathed in the nectar of compassion, at midday in the nectar of youth, and in the evening in the nectar of loveliness (2). She wears the silken garment of modesty, is adorned with the *kuṅkuma* of beauty, and Her body is painted with dark musk, the *rasa* of *śṛṅgāra* (3). She is adorned with nine finest jewels: trembling, tears, hair standing on end, stupor, perspiration, faltering voice, flushing, madness and numbness (4). Her qualities — beauty, sweetness and the rest — rest on Her body as a garland of flowers. She has perfumed Her moods of being *dhīrā* and *adhīrā* with fragrant powder, that is, with camphor and the like (5). Her braided hair, that is, Her hair tied in a knot (*khõpā*), is *māna* hidden within; Her forehead shines with the *tilaka* of good fortune; the ornament of Her ears is hearing Kṛṣṇa's name and fame (6). Her lips are colored red with the betel of *anurāga*; She wears the crookedness of love as eye-black; She is scented with the camphor of the gentle smile that jokes (*narma*) bring (7). When She reclines on the couch of pride in the inner chamber of fragrance, the necklace of *vipralambha* swings on Her as a pendant, *prema-vaicittya* (8). Her breasts are covered by a bodice, the anger of *praṇaya*; Her *kacchapī vīṇā* is the fame and beauty that dry up the faces and hearts of Her rivals (9). She has laid Her lotus hand, Her play, on the shoulder of Her friend, youth; though She has many qualities, She serves the honey that delights Kṛṣṇa, the Kandarpa (10). Holding a straw between my teeth I pray to such a Śrī Rādhā: revive this miserable one by giving him the nectar of service to You (11). O Gāndharvikā, as merciful Kṛṣṇa does not abandon those who surrender to Him, so You too do not abandon one who takes shelter of You (12).

## 181

Who is the birthplace of Śrī Kṛṣṇa's *praṇaya*? Śrīmatī Rādhikā alone. Who is Kṛṣṇa's Beloved of incomparable qualities? Rādhikā alone, and no other. Crookedness in the hair, restlessness in the eyes, firmness in the breasts and the like belong to Rādhikā alone. Rādhikā alone can fulfill Hari's desires, and no one else.

## 185

The greatness of the pastimes is the glory of the love-pastimes of the Two.

## 187

The hero who is clever, freshly youthful, expert in jesting, free from anxiety and submissive to his beloveds is “*dhīra-lalita*.”

## 190–192

“O Rāmānanda, all that you have said — the determination of the ‘goal’ (*sādhya*), the description of Rādhā and Kṛṣṇa, and the greatness of the pastimes of the Two — is true. But if there is anything beyond this, tell it.” Rāya replied, “Beyond this I see no further course for the intellect. But there is one *bhāva* called ‘*prema-vilāsa-vivarta*,’ and of that I shall speak; only I cannot say whether hearing it will please You.” The purport is this: “Thus far I have described the true nature of the pastimes of love (*prema-vilāsa*). In the truth of *prema-vilāsa* there are two kinds of *bhāva*: *sambhoga* and *vipralambha*. Without *vipralambha*, *sambhoga* is not nourished. Separation itself is called ‘*vipralambha*’; this is the *vivarta* of *prema-vilāsa*, that is, during separation, by the force of *adhirūḍha-bhāva*, *sambhoga* is manifest (*sphūrti*) even without *sambhoga*.” Hardly had Rāya Rāmānanda begun to sing a song of his own composition about this *rasa* when Mahāprabhu, overwhelmed by His own *bhāva*, covered his mouth. The song is Śrīmatī's words in separation, that is, the manifestation of *sambhoga* in the state of *vipralambha*.
