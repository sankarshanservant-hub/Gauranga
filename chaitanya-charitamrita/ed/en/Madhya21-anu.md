# Anubhāṣya on Madhya-līlā 21

## 1

> Subheading: The author's *maṅgalācaraṇa* as he describes the sweetness and majesty of Gaura-Kṛṣṇa —

**agaty-eka-gatiṁ** (the sole support of those who have no refuge) **hīnārthādhika-sādhakam** (who abundantly fulfils the aims of the destitute, that is, of those poor in love for Kṛṣṇa) **śrī-caitanyaṁ** **natvā** (having bowed down) **asya** (of the Lord, Caitanyadeva) **mādhuryaiśvarya-śīkaram** (a drop of the majesty that lies within sweetness — or a drop of sweetness and majesty) **likhāmi**.

## 3

> Subheading: In Paravyoma are the countless Vaikuṇṭha abodes of all the forms of Viṣṇu —

## 4

Vaikuṇṭha has no measure, as the *māyic* universes have. Vaikuṇṭha extends a hundred, a thousand, ten thousand, a hundred thousand, ten million — that is, countless — *yojanas*. Where there is no limitation (*kuṇṭha-dharma*) tied to any kind of measure, that is “Vaikuṇṭha.”

## 5

> Subheading: In Paravyoma the container and the contained, the abode and the Lord's form, are one; the form of Bhagavān is a spiritual play of pure goodness (*śuddha-sattva*) —

## 7

> Subheading: Goloka is the whorl (*karṇikā*) of Paravyoma, which is like a thousand-petalled lotus —

## 8

> Subheading: Viṣṇu and His abode are both *adhokṣaja*, and so beyond the reach even of Brahmā and others —

The places of Vaikuṇṭha, full of the six opulences, and the limits of the *avatāras* endowed with the six opulences are beyond the reach even of Brahmā, Śiva and the other lords of the *māyic* realm. What then to speak of the *jīva*, who is subject to *māyā*!

## 9

> Subheading: The transcendental Viṣṇu is *adhokṣaja*: the mind cannot grasp Him — (Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam 10.14.21)[^9-1] —

Brahmā stole the calves, and as a result Śrī Kṛṣṇa crushed his pride. Then Brahmā, understanding that Śrī Kṛṣṇa is the Supreme Lord, offers Him praise in the following two verses:

**bhūman** (Virāṭ) **bhagavan**, **parātman**, **yogeśvara**, **bhavataḥ** **ūtīḥ** (pastimes) **kva** **vā**, **kathaṁ** **vā**, **kadā** **vā**, **kati** **vā** **yoga-māyāṁ** **vistārayan** **krīḍasi** — **iti** **tri-lokyāṁ** **kaḥ** **vetti**? (No one knows; and so the glory of Your Yogamāyā is inconceivable — such is the sense.)

## 11

> Subheading: Even the most skilful counter in the universe is unable to measure Viṣṇu's qualities — (Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam 10.14.7) —

**yaiḥ** **su-kalpaiḥ** (by the most skilful people, over many births) **vā** (supposing) **kālena** **bhū-pāṁśavaḥ** (the atoms of the earth), **khe** (in the sky) **mihikāḥ** (the snowflakes), **dyu-bhāsaḥ** (the atoms of the rays of the luminaries in the heavens) **api** **vimitāḥ** (have been counted one by one) — of them **ke** (which people) **asya** (of the universe) **hitāvatīrṇasya** (who appears for its good, or who has descended to maintain it by revealing many qualities) **guṇātmanaḥ** (the master of the three *guṇas*) **te** (Your) **guṇān** **api** (in turn) **vimātum** (to count — “there are so many”) **īśire** (were able? — to say nothing of telling of each one in particular: such is the sense). See Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam 2.7.40 and 11.4.2.

## 12

> Subheading: Even Śeṣa Himself, singing Kṛṣṇa's qualities, finds no end to them —

What to speak of four-faced Brahmā or five-faced Śiva! Ananta-deva sings ceaselessly with a thousand mouths, yet even He does not reach the limit of Kṛṣṇa's qualities. There is another reading: “What to speak of Brahmā and the rest — the thousand-mouthed Ananta sings ceaselessly with His mouths, yet cannot count them.”

## 13

> Subheading: The evidence of scripture — (Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam 2.7.41) —

Brahmā tells his disciple Nārada of the deeds, purposes and opulences of Viṣṇu's *līlā-avatāras*, and then speaks of His unknowable, immeasurable glory of energies:

**puruṣasya** (of Bhagavān) **viṣṇoḥ** **māyā-balasya** (of the glory of His *māyā*) **antam** **aham** (I, Brahmā) **na** **vidāmi** (do not know; likewise) **te** (your) **agra-jāḥ** (elder brothers) **amī** **munayaḥ** (Sanaka and the others) do not know; **daśa-śatānanaḥ** (thousand-mouthed) **ādi-devaḥ** **śeṣaḥ** (Ananta, who holds up the earth) **api** **asya** (of Bhagavān) **guṇān** **gāyan** (glorifying) **adhunā** (now) **api** **pāram** (the limit) **na** **samavasyati** (does not ascertain, that is, does not reach; therefore) **ye** **apare** (other people — they) **kutaḥ**? (how could they know? — such is the sense).

## 14

> Subheading: Even for Kṛṣṇa Himself Kṛṣṇa's qualities are immeasurable —

*Teṅho rahu*: what to speak of Ananta-deva! Śrī Kṛṣṇa Himself does not reach the limit of His own qualities, and so He is full of longing.

## 15

> Subheading: Having rejected “not this” and described the featureless, the *śrutis* culminate in describing the form full of attributes — (Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam 10.87.41) —

In Janaloka, at the Brahma-satra sacrifice, before sages eager to hear, the *brahmarṣi* Sanandana, one of the four Kumāras, recited this praise of Bhagavān offered by the *śrutis*. Later the original sage Nārāyaṇa recounted the same praise to the *devarṣi* Nārada.

**bhagavan**, **dyu-patayaḥ** (the lords of heaven, the lords of the worlds — Brahmā and others) **eva** (even) **te** (Your) **anantatayā** (because You are without end) **antam** (the limit of Your qualities) **na** **yayuḥ** (did not reach; that is, whatever has an end is not You); (what to speak of the lords of heaven,) **yat** (since) **tvam** **api** (You Yourself do not reach Your own limit, being endless); **nanu** (oh!) **yat** (of You) **antarā** (within) **sāvaraṇāḥ** (in seven coverings, each ten times the one before) **aṇḍa-nicayāḥ** (the multitudes of universes) **vayasā** (by the wheel of time) **khe** (in the sky) **rajāṁsi** **iva** **saha** (all at once, not in turn) **vānti** (revolve); **yat** (because) **śrutayaḥ** **atan-nirasanena** (by ceaselessly rejecting matter) **bhavan-nidhanāḥ** (those whose end, whose conclusion, is in You) **tvayi** (in You, who are full of distinct spiritual pastimes) **hi** **phalanti** (culminate).

## 16

> Subheading: The description of Kṛṣṇa's wondrous pastime of herding the cows in Vraja —

## 17

> Subheading: Because of the theft of the calves, Kṛṣṇa manifests a spiritual pastime and destroys Brahmā's pride —

In a single moment Kṛṣṇa created countless transcendental Vaikuṇṭhas with the lords of Paravyoma, and countless material universes with many Brahmās and others.

## 18

*Avadhūta*: shaken, stirred, overflowing, overwhelmed, overcome.

There is another reading: “hearing which, the mind is washed of its impurity.”

## 25

## 27

> Subheading: (Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam 10.14.38) —

Brahmā stole the calves, and as a result Śrī Kṛṣṇa crushed his pride. Then Brahmā glorifies Kṛṣṇa's affection for His devotees and His being beyond the senses (*adhokṣajatva*):

**prabho**, **jānantaḥ** (the learned, who fancy that they know Your inconceivable, endless qualities) **eva** **jānantu**; **bahūktyā** (by much talk) **kim**? (a flood of words bears no fruit — such is the sense); **tava** **vaibhavam** **me** (to me, Brahmā) — **manasaḥ** **vapuṣaḥ** **vācaḥ** (to body, mind and speech) — **na** **gocaraḥ** (is not accessible: they have no right even to touch it).

## 28

> Subheading: Śrī Vṛndāvana-dhāma is non-different from Kṛṣṇa —

## 29

> Subheading: In one part of Vṛndāvana are the countless millions of Vaikuṇṭhas of Paravyoma —

Scripture says that Vṛndāvana extends for “sixteen *krośas*.” In just one part of it all the Vaikuṇṭhas and the vast universes are manifest.

## 30

> Subheading: Pointing to a single drop of the boundless ocean of Kṛṣṇa's glory —

## 31

> Subheading: Brahmā[^31-1] is overwhelmed as he describes Kṛṣṇa's majesty, which lies beyond speech and mind —

## 33

> Subheading: The form of the One without a second, the Lord of the three creations, worshipped by the imperishable lords of the worlds — (Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam 3.2.21) —

For the explanation and the *kārikās* on this verse, see the *Laghu-bhāgavatāmṛta*, Pūrva-khaṇḍa, 302–323: there, refuting the opponent's view (*pūrva-pakṣa*) that “Śrī Kṛṣṇa is a *vilāsa* of Nārāyaṇa,” the author sets out the conclusion (*uttara-pakṣa*).

After Śrī Kṛṣṇa's departure, Śrīla Uddhava, stricken with the grief of separation from Him, tells Śrī Vidura of Kṛṣṇa's childhood pastimes and His supreme majesty:

**svayam** (Bhagavān) **tu** **asāmyātiśayaḥ** (He who has no equal and none higher — *asamordhva*), **try-adhīśaḥ** (the Lord and Master: of the abodes Goloka, Paravyoma and Devī-dhāma; or of the abodes Gokula, Mathurā and Dvārakā; or of the three creations — the causal, the aggregate [Hiraṇyagarbha] and the individual [Virāṭ]; or of Viṣṇu, Brahmā and Śiva, the presiding lords of goodness, passion and ignorance; or of the energies — *cit*, *jīva* and *māyā*; or of the three *vyāhṛtis* — *bhūḥ*, *bhuvaḥ* and *svaḥ*; or of the three worlds — heaven, earth and the netherworld), **svārājya-lakṣmy-āpta-samasta-kāmaḥ** (who has gained every enjoyment simply by the wealth of His own supreme nature of spirit and bliss) **baliṁ** (tribute, or an offering) **haradbhiḥ** (bringing) **cira-loka-pālaiḥ** (by the age-old guardians of the worlds — Brahmā, Rudra and others) **kirīṭa-koṭīḍita-pāda-pīṭhaḥ** (He whose footstool, the throne of His feet, is praised, that is, saluted, by the tips of crowns; this connects with the next verse: “that He reported to Ugrasena pains us”).

> Subheading: Kṛṣṇa: (2) the Lord of the three (*tryadhīśa*); (a) the first (external) meaning, concerning the *guṇa-avatāras* —

> Subheading: (c) The third (confidential) meaning, concerning the abodes under Kṛṣṇa's control —

The three abodes are: (1) the inner abode, Goloka; (2) the middle abode, Paravyoma; (3) the outer abode, Devī-dhāma.

## 50

## 51

> Subheading: Where Paravyoma, or Vaikuṇṭha, lies —

**tasyāḥ** (of the river Virajā) **pāre** (on the bank) **tripād-bhūtam** (*turīya*, transcendent) **sanātanam** (eternally existing) **amṛtam** (imperishable) **śāśvatam** **nityam** **anantam** **paramam** **padam** — **paravyoma**.

## 52

> Subheading: (3) The outer abode, Devī-dhāma, is the realm of *māyā*, the field of the *jīvas'* enjoyment —

## 53

*Jīva*: the conditioned *jīva*, bent on enjoyment, lives in Devī-dhāma. Svārājya-lakṣmī, as Kṛṣṇa's servant, fulfils Kṛṣṇa's desires, while Jagal-lakṣmī protects the *jīvas* who live in Devī-dhāma. *Yāṅhā*, “where”: in this Devī-dhāma Māyā, the maidservant of Jagal-lakṣmī, is the presiding deity.

## 54

The three abodes are the topmost abode, Goloka; Hari's abode, Paravyoma; and Devī-dhāma. A *jīva* freed from Devī-dhāma who does not attain Hari's service in Paravyoma reaches the abode of Maheśa. Though that abode lies above Devī-dhāma, it is not Hari's abode, not Paravyoma.

## 55

> Subheading: The play of the *cit-śakti*, made of pure goodness, and the *tad-rūpa-vaibhava*[^55-1] — Kṛṣṇa's *tripād-vibhūti*; Devī-dhāma — the *ekapād-vibhūti* —

Hari's abode Paravyoma and Goloka form the transcendental abode endowed with the opulences of the *cit-śakti*; it is called “*tripād-aiśvarya*.” Devī-dhāma, endowed with *māyic* opulence, is known by the name “*ekapād*.”

## 56

> Subheading: The *tripād-vibhūti* lies beyond *māyā*, and the *ekapād-vibhūti* is *māyic* — (Laghu-bhāgavatāmṛta 1.563) —

**tat** **padam** **tri-pād-vibhūteḥ** **dhāmatvāt** **tri-pād-bhūtam** **hi** (is called precisely “consisting of three quarters”), **yataḥ** **sarvā** **māyikī** **vibhūtiḥ** **pādātmikā** (one quarter) **proktā** (is said to be).

## 58

> Subheading: The meaning of the word *cira-loka-pāla* —

The *cira-loka-pālas* are Brahmā, Rudra and the others who hold office in the universe and carry out long-lasting duties. The word *lokapāla* usually means the eight guardians of the directions: Indra, Agni, Yama, Varuṇa, Nairṛti, Vāyu, Kuvera and Śiva.

## 59

> Subheading: A Purāṇic account of how the pride of Brahmā, who came to see Kṛṣṇa's majesty, was destroyed —

## 79

Four-faced Brahmā experienced the unearthly majesty of Kṛṣṇa and of Dvārakā-dhāma. Brahmās and Rudras with ten, a hundred, a thousand, ten thousand, a hundred thousand and ten million faces gathered in one place, and four-faced Brahmā and Kṛṣṇa saw this assembly. Yet by Kṛṣṇa's will the great Brahmās and great Śivas who had come did not see one another. Or else the crowd of Brahmās and Śivas was so dense that they had no chance at all to see one another and converse, and no one had the leisure even to show another respect or welcome him.

## 84

If a universe is taken to be a hundred *koṭi yojanas*, half of it is fifty *koṭi yojanas*. Manu writes: “He Himself, by His meditation, divided that egg into two.”[^84-1] The *Sūrya-siddhānta* (12.90) says: “18,712,080,864,000,000[^84-2] — such is the circumference that encircles the hemispheres of the universe; within it the sun's rays spread on all sides.” The *Siddhānta-śiromaṇi*, in the *Graha-gaṇita*, in the chapter *Madhyamādhikāra*, in the section on the order of the orbits, and likewise in the *Golādhyāya*, in the section *Bhuvana-kośa*, verse 67, says: “Those versed in *jyotiṣa* say that this orbit of the sky is 1,871,206,920 *koṭi yojanas*. Some Purāṇic sages have said that it girds the rim where the halves of the bowl of the universe meet, and others have called it the mountain that is both visible and invisible.” The orbit of the sky (*kha-kakṣā*) is 18,712,069,200,000,000 *yojanas*; some say it is the measure of the circumference where the two halves of the bowl of the universe meet.

## 91

> Subheading: Kṛṣṇa's three abodes —

In Goloka there are three chambers (*prakoṣṭhas*): (1) Gokula, (2) Mathurā, (3) Dvārakā. Just as Kṛṣṇa's pastimes have three chambers, so Gaura's pastimes too have three intimate chambers full of complete majesty: (1) Navadvīpa-maṇḍala, (2) Śrī Kṣetra-maṇḍala (South India?) and (3) Vraja-maṇḍala.

## 96

> Subheading: The meaning of *svārājya-lakṣmī* —

Kṛṣṇa eternally abides together with His own *cit-śakti* (spiritual potency), the *svārājya-lakṣmī*. The wealth of the Lord's *cit-śakti* is what is called the “six opulences” (*ṣaḍ-aiśvarya*). The *cit-śakti* is the very own potency of Kṛṣṇa, the form who possesses the *cit-śakti*, and she is His maidservant.

## 99

> Subheading: Setting out to describe the charm of His majesty, the Lord felt the sweetness of Kṛṣṇa's form revealed (*sphūrti*) within His heart —

## 100

> Subheading: Kṛṣṇa Himself is enchanted by the transcendent sweetness of pastimes befitting His pastimes in human form —

After the departure of Śrī Kṛṣṇa, Śrīla Uddhava, stricken with grief in separation from Him, glorifies before Śrī Vidura the matchless sweetness of His beauty:

**yat** (that form) **martya-līlaupayikaṁ** (suited to pastimes in the world of mortals, humanlike) **sva-yoga-māyā-balaṁ** (the might of His own *cit-śakti*) **darśayatā** (by Him who displays it — by Bhagavān Himself) **gṛhītam** (accepted), **svasya ca** (even to Himself) **vismāpanaṁ** (astonishing), **saubhagardheḥ** (of the abundance of good fortune) **paraṁ padaṁ** (the highest limit, the foundation), **bhūṣaṇa-bhūṣaṇāṅgam** (the form whose limbs are the ornaments of ornaments; “having shown this His own form, He left the world” — so it is construed with the preceding verse).

## 101

> Subheading: The two-armed, ever-youthful form holding the flute —

Bhagavān's pastimes are endless. There are Kṛṣṇa's pastimes in Gokula; the pastimes of Vāsudeva, Saṅkarṣaṇa and others in Paravyoma; the pastimes of the *puruṣa-avatāras* such as Kāraṇārṇavaśāyī; the pastimes of the avatāras who come for a particular occasion (*naimittika*), such as Matsya and Kūrma; the pastimes of the *guṇa-avatāras* such as Brahmā and Śiva; the pastimes of the *āveśa-avatāras* such as Pṛthu and Vyāsa; the pastimes of the Paramātmā and others endowed with attributes (*saviśeṣa*); and the impersonal (*nirviśeṣa*) Brahman. When all these plays of Bhagavān are compared, Kṛṣṇa's pastimes in human form (*nara-līlā*) are the best of all. Kṛṣṇa's *svarūpa* is a human body, a cowherd's dress, a flute in hand, fresh youth (*nava-kiśora*) and the finest of dancers (*naṭavara*). Kṛṣṇa's *svarūpa* corresponds to His pastimes in human form. Yet it bears none of the impurity of material attributes: it is not base, mortal, impermanent, unworthy, limited, divided or confined.

## 102

> Subheading: A description of the sweetness of Kṛṣṇa's form; Kṛṣṇa's form attracts all living beings —

A single particle of Kṛṣṇa's sweet form can flood the three worlds — Gokula, Mathurā and Dvārakā; or else these three worlds: the inner abode, Goloka-Vṛndāvana, the middle abode, Paravyoma, and the outer abode, Devī-dhāma. And the sweetness of His form attracts the beings of these three worlds.

## 103

> Subheading: Manifesting the eternal pastimes displays the power of Yogamāyā —

In Paravyoma and the other abodes, Yogamāyā — the *cit-śakti* that is the transformation of *viśuddha-sattva* — does not abide. To show the working of this Yogamāyā's unprecedented, extraordinary power, Kṛṣṇa manifested in the world (*prapañca*), from Goloka, His eternal pastimes — a jewel that the devotees keep deeply secret and hold most dear.

## 104

> Subheading: Kṛṣṇa's own intense longing to enjoy His form —

Kṛṣṇa's form is so extraordinarily wondrous that it astonishes Kṛṣṇa Himself, and Kṛṣṇa's own eagerness to taste it grows. His own supreme good fortune, full of the six opulences — complete beauty, knowledge, wealth, strength, fame and renunciation — abides eternally in Kṛṣṇa alone.

## 105

> Subheading: The *āśraya* of Goloka — those who hold love — are enchanted and attracted by the form of its object (*viṣaya*) —

*Alaṅkāra* means an ornament of the body. But the beauty of Kṛṣṇa's body is so wondrous that His body is like the ornament of ornaments! Despite such beauty of His body, the graceful pose bent in three places (*lalita-tribhaṅga*) seems to increase it still more. Moreover, above His eyes the bow-like brows are dancing. Fitting the arrows of His sidelong glances to the bow of His brows, Kṛṣṇa takes firm aim to pierce the hearts of Rādhā and the gopīs who follow Her.

## 106

> Subheading: Kṛṣṇa's form attracts even Nārāyaṇa and the Lakṣmīs of Paravyoma —

Kṛṣṇa's form is so captivating that — to say nothing of all the beings and gods of the material world — it forcibly steals the hearts even of Nārāyaṇa and the other forms (*svarūpas*) of Kṛṣṇa in Paravyoma, above the universes. Even the Lakṣmīs, whom the Vedas call the one “crest-jewel of chaste wives,” are attracted by Kṛṣṇa's beauty and long for His lotus feet.

## 107

> Subheading: “When He shines together with Rādhā, then He is Madana-mohana” —

Mounting the chariot of the gopīs' minds — their favourable disposition of heart — and accepting their service to Himself, Kṛṣṇa agitates the mind of Kandarpa himself, and so He is called “Madana-mohana.” Madana is the lord of five arrows: form, taste, smell, sound and touch. With His own beauty Kṛṣṇa tramples his pride — the pride of enchanting women. And Kṛṣṇa Himself, under the name *nava-kandarpa* (the new, transcendental Madana of Vraja), sports in the *rāsa* dance with the gopīs.

## 108

> Subheading: A description of the sweetness of Kṛṣṇa's flute —

## 109

> Subheading: A description of Kṛṣṇa's form —

The pearl necklace on Kṛṣṇa's neck is like a row of white cranes; the peacock feather on Kṛṣṇa's head is like a rainbow; and Kṛṣṇa's yellow garment is like lightning. Kṛṣṇa is like a fresh cloud, and the gopīs are like the crops of the world. As a cloud rains upon the crops, Kṛṣṇa pours upon them the streams of the nectar of His pastimes and so gives them life. In the rainy season cranes fly, and the rainbow and the lightning are seen as well.

## 110

> Subheading: The highest *bhagavattā* — the sweetness of Kṛṣṇa — is described only in the *Bhāgavatam* —

The very essence of the *bhagavattā* of Bhagavān, who is full of the six opulences, is sweetness, and this sweetness was revealed in Vraja itself. Śukadeva, the son of Dvaipāyana, described for the devotees, in many places of the *Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam*, this particle of sweetness that maddens the hearts of the devotees.

## 111

> Subheading: While describing Kṛṣṇa's qualities, the Lord describes the good fortune of the gopīs —

The women of Mathurā, overflowing with feeling, described the extraordinary fortune of the gopīs and the transcendent qualities of Kṛṣṇa. In the same way Śrī Gaurahari, beginning to speak of “the taste of Kṛṣṇa” (*kṛṣṇa-rasa*), was filled with *prema* (love of God), took Sanātana's hand and, absorbed in love, recited that verse.

## 112

See Ādi 4.156.

## 113

> Subheading: The gopīs float eternally on the waves of the nectar of loveliness in the ocean of the nectar of Kṛṣṇa's youth —

*Cakravāta* is a whirlwind, round like a wheel.

## 114

> Subheading: Drinking the nectar of Kṛṣṇa's form, the gopīs attain the goal of life —

## 115

> Subheading: The sweetness of Kṛṣṇa's form has no equal and no superior; it is absent in Nārāyaṇa —

## 116

> Subheading: The proof: even Lakṣmī, the consort of Nārāyaṇa, longs for Kṛṣṇa's sweetness —

## 117

> Subheading: In His other manifestations Kṛṣṇa, by His own will and as needed, reveals a portion of His sweetness, which is perfect by itself —

## 118

> Subheading: Kṛṣṇa's sweetness and the gopīs' love are both ever new —

## 119

> Subheading: Without *rāgānugā-bhakti* Kṛṣṇa's sweetness is very hard to attain —

Karma, austerity, yoga, *jñāna*, *vidhi-bhakti*, *japa*, meditation and other practices (*sādhana*) do not bring one to sweetness. Kṛṣṇa's sweetness is easily attained only by one who, on the path of *rāga* (*rāga-mārga*), worships Kṛṣṇa's name with *anurāga*.

## 120

> Subheading: All other *bhagavattā* comes from Bhagavān Himself in Vraja, the son of the king of Vraja —

## 121

> Subheading: Kṛṣṇa is the shelter of all spiritual virtues —

## 122

> Subheading: The eyes that drink the sweetness of Kṛṣṇa's form long never to blink —

“Reproaching the blink”: the covering of the eye, the eyelid, is called *pakṣma*. Because it lies over the eye, it hinders sight; hence the reproach.

## 123

> Subheading: One who drinks the honey of Kṛṣṇa's lotus face never finds the satiety common in the world of matter; the gopīs' ocean of bliss swells at every moment —

Śrīla Śukadeva Mahārāja, describing the Yadu dynasty to Parīkṣit, describes the purpose of Śrī Kṛṣṇa's avatāra and the incomparably beautiful sweetness of His form, which enchants all the worlds:

**yasya** (of Kṛṣṇa) **makara-kuṇḍala-cāru-karṇa-bhrājat-kapola-subhagaṁ** (lovely with cheeks that shine, lit by the ears beautifully adorned with *makara*-shaped earrings), **sa-vilāsa-hāsam** (with a playful smile), **nityotsavam** (in which there is a perpetual festival — bliss) **ānanam** (the lotus face) **nāryaḥ narāḥ ca** (women and men) **dṛśibhiḥ** (with their eyes) **pibantyaḥ** (even while drinking) **na tu tatṛpuḥ**[^123-1] (were not satisfied: unable to bear even the interval in which the eyes close and open, they at its maker) **nimeḥ** (at the Creator) **kupitāḥ** (were angry).

## 124

See Ādi 4.153.

## 125

> Subheading: The Kāma-gāyatrī is Kṛṣṇa's very form; each of its syllables is one of His limbs or features —

For the Kāma-gāyatrī, see Madhya 8.137. The twenty-four and a half syllables of the Kāma-gāyatrī are like twenty-four and a half moons on Kṛṣṇa's body. And it is Kṛṣṇa's *svarūpa*, because it contains the three truths: *sambandha*, *abhidheya* and *prayojana*.

## 126

> Subheading: The moon of Kṛṣṇa's face reigns over the twenty-four and a half moons of His body —

## 128

*Ṭhāṭa* means abiding (*sthiti*); *nāṭa* means dance (*nāṭya*).

## 126–128

The moon of Kṛṣṇa's face is the king of the moons. The moons are these: (1) the moon of the face, (2) the moon of the left cheek, (3) the moon of the right cheek, (4) the moon of the sandal dot, (5–14) the moons of the fingernails, (15–24) the moons of the toenails, (24½) the half-moon on the forehead. With this society of twenty-four and a half moons, the king of the moons — Kṛṣṇa's face — sits on the royal throne of Kṛṣṇa's body and rules.

## 129

> Subheading: Like a kṣatriya intoxicated with play, Kṛṣṇa's lotus face pierces the gopīs' hearts —

The moon of Kṛṣṇa's face is a king given to play (*vilāsī*). This moon of His face makes the *makara* earrings and the lotuses of His eyes dance constantly. The brows are like a bow, the eyes are its arrows, and the two ears are the bowstring drawn to them. With eyes that stretch to His ears Kṛṣṇa pierces the target — the hearts of the cowherd women.

## 130

> Subheading: As the most munificent one, He distributes nectar to all from the moons of His body —

The dance of this moon of His face surpasses everyone. Setting out the other twenty-three and a half moons as wares in a market, it distributes its own nectar free of charge. Some buyers Kṛṣṇa satisfies with the moonlit nectar of His sweet smile, some with the nectar of His lips, and all others in other ways.

## 131

> Subheading: A description of the ministers of the king — the moon of the face, intoxicated with amorous play — and of his pleasure-palace and the rest —

## 132

> Subheading: When the gopīs see the moon of Kṛṣṇa's face, their unsatisfied longing is ever new, ever growing, supremely wondrous and spiritual; therefore they reproach the Creator —

Only from acts born of *bhakti* does *sukṛti* arise that turns one toward *bhakti*. How much of such a face of Kṛṣṇa can a viewer drink with two eyes? His thirst and greed grow twofold, yet he cannot drink as much as he wishes. His own incapacity and want trouble his mind deeply, and then the viewer, sorrowful at heart, begins to blame the one who created him.

## 133

> Subheading: The Creator does not understand the taste of Kṛṣṇa's sweetness —

The unsatisfied viewer then says with regret: “I have no hundreds of thousands, no millions of eyes — I have only two, and even these are covered with leaves. Now and then the eyelid falls for a brief moment, and then my seeing of Kṛṣṇa is interrupted again. So Vidhi, the maker of bodies, is utterly foolish. He gave up the service of seeing Kṛṣṇa and took to trivial austerity, and so he is not at all a knower of *rasa* (*rasajña*). He is a mere doer of dry tasks such as creation and knows nothing at all of how and where things should be arranged.”

## 134

> Subheading: Advice and instruction to the Creator —

“Had he accepted my advice and given millions of eyes to the one who beholds Kṛṣṇa's face, I would have known Vidhi to be skilled in the work of creation.”

## 135

> Subheading: The Lord, filled with the feeling of the gopīs, longs for the sweetness of Kṛṣṇa's body, the sweetness of His face and the sweetness of His smile —

Ordinarily, the first glance sees the ocean of sweetness that is Kṛṣṇa's body; the second, closer glance sees the most sweet moon of His face, which stands above the ocean of His body; and the third, closest glance sees the rays of the moon of His face — His gentle smile, sweeter than sweetness itself. The sweetness of these three rose in the Lord step by step as He recited the verse, and a transformation (*vikāra*) appeared in Him: He began to wave His hand.

## 136

> Subheading: The degrees of sweetness of Kṛṣṇa's body, face and smile in the eyes of the gopīs —

**asya vibhoḥ** (of this Kṛṣṇa) **vapuḥ** (the form, or the body) **madhuraṁ madhuraṁ** (compared with the beauty of all the other forms besides such a *svayaṁ-rūpa*, exceedingly sweet); (Kṛṣṇa's) **vadanaṁ** (and) **madhuraṁ madhuraṁ madhuram** (compared with Kṛṣṇa's body, sweeter still); **aho**, **etat** **madhu-gandhi** (fragrant with honey) **mṛdu-smitam** (and the gentle smile) **madhuraṁ madhuraṁ madhuraṁ madhuram** (compared with Kṛṣṇa's body and face, the sweetest of all).

## 137

> Subheading: The Lord, filled with the feeling of the gopīs, tastes Kṛṣṇa's sweetness, and His unsatisfied longing grows eternally —

The Lord, steeped in the feeling of the gopīs in the *rasa* of separation (*vipralambha*), thirsts so intensely to taste Kṛṣṇa's sweetness that even while tasting Kṛṣṇa's boundless sweetness He is not satisfied. This lack of satisfaction is transcendental, supremely wondrous and ever increasing. Therefore, out of strong emotion and longing, He laments and grieves that He cannot taste Kṛṣṇa's sweetness even in a small measure.

## 138

> Subheading: Kṛṣṇa's body is sweet, Kṛṣṇa's face sweeter, Kṛṣṇa's smile the sweetest —

*Tāra* means “of the moon of Kṛṣṇa's face.” The gentle smile on Kṛṣṇa's face is like the full light of the moon that delights the gopīs.

## 139

> Subheading: All three worlds are bathed in the moonlight of that smile —

Although that smile appears only on one side of His beautiful face, it pervades Goloka, Paravyoma and Devī-dhāma and fills the ten directions with light.

## 140

> Subheading: The sweetness of the flute of Kṛṣṇa, the embodiment of play, maddens all three worlds —

*Smita-kiraṇa-sukarpūre*: in the camphor of the rays of His slight smile. *Paiśe* means “enters.”

## 141

> Subheading: Kṛṣṇa's flute maddens all who are of pure goodness (*śuddha-sattva*) in the universes, in Paravyoma and in Goloka — especially those who hold the *śṛṅgāra-rasa* —

*Aṇḍa bhedi'* (“piercing the egg of the universe”): breaking through the material realm, the universe, the sound goes to transcendental Vaikuṇṭha and forcibly enters the ears of the world of the gopīs.

## 142

> Subheading: The power of the sweetness of the flute —

## 144

> Subheading: It stops every sound other than the sounds of Kṛṣṇa —

The sound of Kṛṣṇa's flute makes its home in the gopīs' ears and, revealing itself there (*sphūrti*) on its own, keeps the gopī almost mad. Then no other sound can enter her ears, and, her mind elsewhere, she cannot answer as she should. That sound of the flute leaves the gopī utterly absent to everything else.

## 145

> Subheading: The Lord returns to external awareness; He seeks no honour for Himself and gives honour to another (*amānī* and *mānada*) —

## 146

> Subheading: The Lord, filled with the feeling of the gopīs, proclaims that His heart has been overcome by Kṛṣṇa's sweetness, and speaks of its good fortune —

## 147

> Subheading: The Lord falls silent for a moment —

## 148

> Subheading: One who hears of Kṛṣṇa's sweetness is immersed in the happiness of love for Kṛṣṇa —

*Thus ends the twenty-first chapter of the “Anubhāṣya.”*
