# Amṛta-pravāha-bhāṣya on Antya-līlā 18

## 0

**Summary.** One night, in the light of the autumn moon, Mahāprabhu saw the ocean from Āiṭoṭā and, mistaking it for the Yamunā, leapt into the water. The essence of this pastime is the relishing of Rādhā and Kṛṣṇa's water pastimes (*jala-keli*). Floating on the waves in this way, the Lord drifted toward Koṇārka. A fisherman, thinking he had caught “a big fish,” drew the Lord in with his net and saw that the Lord was unconscious and His form greatly transformed. As soon as he touched Him, the fisherman was overcome by divine love (*premāveśa*). Yet he was frightened: “This ghost has seized me (has climbed onto my shoulders).” With this thought he was on his way to an exorcist when he met Svarūpa Gosvāmī and the other devotees, who had been searching everywhere for Mahāprabhu in every way and were coming along the shore. When they questioned him, the fisherman told them all that had happened, and Svarūpa Gosvāmī understood that this fisherman had drawn the Lord onto the shore. With a slap of Kṛṣṇa's name he drove the “ghost” — his fear — out of the fisherman. Then, by chanting the holy name (*kīrtana*), the devotees brought Mahāprabhu back to consciousness, raised Him, heard from Him of His pastime, and brought Him home.

## 1

On a night lit by the autumn moon, Śacīnandana saw the ocean and, taking it for the Yamunā, plunged into the burning ocean of separation from Hari, fell into the water, and lay unconscious the whole night, and in the morning He was found (by Svarūpa and others, His own intimate companions). May that Śacīnandana protect us by His pastimes.

## 25

As the king of elephants sports in the water with his she-elephants, so Bhagavān Śrī Kṛṣṇa, who is beyond worldly codes, tired from the *rāsa-līlā* with the gopīs, entered the water to relieve His fatigue. Bees followed Him (flew close behind Him) like the lords of the Gandharvas. At that time His garland, colored with the kuṅkuma from the gopīs' breasts, had been crushed (pressed) by contact with their bodies.

## 31

Koṇārka is Arka-tīrtha, which is nowadays called “Kaṇārak.”

## 40

A friend's heart always fears that harm may befall the friend.

## 84

**kara-puṣkara** — lotus hands (according to another opinion, the tip of the trunk).

## 86

The *gopīs*, like motionless flashes of lightning, began to shower water on Kṛṣṇa, dark like a fresh cloud. And the fresh cloud — dark Kṛṣṇa — in turn showered water on the lightning (the *gopīs*).

## 91

To cover the body, a lotus leaf, like a hand. Some loosened their hair and imagined it to be a lower garment; others made their hands a *kañculī* (bodice).

## 94

**hemābja** — the *gopīs*; **nīlābja** — Kṛṣṇa. The *gopīs* devoted to service stood on the bank and watched both sides at play.

## 95–98

The breasts of the *gopīs* are the flock of *cakravāka* birds. When they all rose from the water in pairs, Kṛṣṇa's hands, like blue lotuses, covered those *cakravākas* in pairs. The *gopīs*' hands are red water lilies (*raktotpalas*): they rose in pairs and began to hold off the blue lotuses. The blue lotuses want to plunder the *cakravākas*, and the red water lilies want to protect them; so a quarrel broke out between the two. The blue lotuses and the red water lilies are senseless with love; the *cakravākas* are conscious, yet the blue lotuses began to taste them. Here the natures are reversed: usually the *cakravāka* bird tastes the lotus, but in this pastime of Kṛṣṇa the senseless lotus tasted the conscious *cakravāka*. The lotus, the sun's friend, naturally lives beside the *cakravāka*; yet, though it is the bird's friend, it plunders the *cakravāka*. The *utpala*, that is, the *kumuda* (night water lily), opens at night, and so it is a stranger to the *cakravāka* and hostile to it. Yet this *kumuda* — a *gopī*'s hand — protects the *cakravāka*, her own breast. This is truly wonderful, and therefore here we have the ornament *virodha* (contradiction).

*Thus ends the eighteenth chapter of the “Amṛta-pravāha-bhāṣya.”*
