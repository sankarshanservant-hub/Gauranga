# Amṛta-pravāha-bhāṣya on Antya-līlā 18

## 0

**Summary.** One night, in the light of the autumn moon, Mahāprabhu saw the ocean from Āiṭoṭā and, mistaking it for the Yamunā, leapt into the water. The essence of this pastime is the relishing of Rādhā and Kṛṣṇa's water pastimes (*jala-keli*). Floating on the waves in this way, the Lord drifted toward Koṇārka. A fisherman, thinking he had caught “a big fish,” drew the Lord in with his net and saw that the Lord was unconscious and His form greatly transformed. As soon as he touched Him, the fisherman was overcome by divine love (*premāveśa*). Yet he was frightened: “This ghost has seized me (has climbed onto my shoulders).” With this thought he was on his way to an exorcist when he met Svarūpa Gosvāmī and the other devotees, who had been searching everywhere for Mahāprabhu in every way and were coming along the shore. When they questioned him, the fisherman told them all that had happened, and Svarūpa Gosvāmī understood that this fisherman had drawn the Lord onto the shore. With a slap of Kṛṣṇa's name he drove the “ghost” — his fear — out of the fisherman. Then, by chanting the holy name (*kīrtana*), the devotees brought Mahāprabhu back to consciousness, raised Him, heard from Him of His pastime, and brought Him home.

## 1

On a night lit by the autumn moon, Śacīnandana saw the ocean and, taking it for the Yamunā, plunged into the burning ocean of separation from Hari: He fell into the water and lay unconscious the whole night, and in the morning He was found (by Svarūpa and others, His own intimate companions). May that Śacīnandana protect us by His pastimes.

## 25

As the king of elephants sports in the water with his she-elephants, so Bhagavān Śrī Kṛṣṇa, who is beyond worldly codes, tired from the *rāsa-līlā* with the gopīs, entered the water to relieve His fatigue. Bees followed Him (flew close behind Him) like the lords of the Gandharvas. At that time His garland, colored with the kuṅkuma from the gopīs' breasts, had been crushed (pressed) in their embraces.

## 31

Koṇārka is Arka-tīrtha, which is nowadays called “Kaṇārak.”

## 40

A friend's heart always fears that harm may befall the friend.
