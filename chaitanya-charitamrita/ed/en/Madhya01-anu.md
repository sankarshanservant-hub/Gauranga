> Subheading: The promise to give in the form of *sūtras* the events described in detail in the *Caitanya-bhāgavata*, and to describe in detail the main events that it describes briefly —

It is known that until the time the *Caitanya-caritāmṛta* was composed, Śrī Vṛndāvana dāsa Ṭhākura's *Caitanya-bhāgavata* was called *Caitanya-maṅgala*. The book mentioned under the title *Caitanya-maṅgala* by Jayānanda — a thoroughly modern work, opposed to the conclusions of *bhakti* — is a forgery. In no ancient book do we find that book, or even the name of its author, “Jayānanda.” For this reason it is also easy to see that the name “Jayānanda” itself is fabricated.

## 13

> Subheading: Constant homage to Ṭhākura Vṛndāvana —

“The Vyāsa of Caitanya's pastimes” is Śrī Kṛṣṇa Dvaipāyana Vyāsa, the writer of the Lord's *avatāras* and of Śrī Kṛṣṇa's pastimes. Śrī Vṛndāvana dāsa Ṭhākura, the writer of Śrī Caitanya's pastimes, is Śrī Vyāsa's own form (*svarūpa*). Śrī Kṛṣṇadāsa writes of Caitanya's pastimes in order to continue the description of those remaining pastimes that Vṛndāvana dāsa left undescribed — as his servant, his ward (*pālya*), and his follower.

*Bhakti* is spread through service that is not of this world, and through it the *tīrthas* (holy places) are revealed in their true nature. ## 35

> Subheading: The four books of Śrī Sanātana —

See Madhya 24.331–347.

Śrīla Kavirāja Gosvāmī describes, in the twenty-fourth chapter of the Madhya-līlā, the portion of the *Hari-bhakti-vilāsa* that Śrīmad Gopāla Bhaṭṭa Gosvāmī Prabhu compiled. In the present book compiled by Gopāla Bhaṭṭa the full flowering of Vaiṣṇava *smṛti* is not seen. At the command of Śrī Gaurasundara, only selected portions of Śrī Sanātana Gosvāmī's vast collection of *smṛti*, suited to that time, were strung together in it. Only when the *Vaiṣṇava-smṛti-kalpadruma* — that is, Śrī Sanātana's *Śrī Hari-bhakti-vilāsa* — is published will every need of the Vaiṣṇava community for guidance in daily life be met. Śrī Gopāla Bhaṭṭa Gosvāmī Prabhu's *Bhakti-vilāsa* was written in brief on the basis of the *Śrī Hari-bhakti-vilāsa*; moreover, the *smārtas* are powerful, and so this book does not give a resolution of all practical matters. The commentary *Digdarśinī* on the *Hari-bhakti-vilāsa*, written by Śrī Sanātana Gosvāmī himself for his own work — a portion of it, now published as a commentary on the present *Bhakti-vilāsa* — is declared by some to have been compiled by Śrī Gopīnātha Pūjādhikārī. This Śrī Gopīnātha was one of the disciples of Śrīmad Gopāla Bhaṭṭa Gosvāmī Prabhu, who was engaged in the service of Śrī Rādhā-ramaṇa in Vṛndāvana.

***Bṛhad-bhāgavatāmṛta*** is a book in two parts (*khaṇḍas*), full of the conclusions about devotion to Bhagavān. The first part is called *Bhagavat-kṛpā-bhara-nirdhāra*. It has seven chapters: *Bhauma*, *Divya*, *Brahmaloka and Vaikuṇṭha*, *Bhakta*, *Priya*, *Priyatama* and *Pūrṇa*. The second part is called *Goloka-māhātmya-nirūpaṇa*. It has seven chapters: *Vairāgya*, *Jñāna*, *Bhakti*, *Vaikuṇṭha*, *Prema*, *Abhīṣṭa-lābha* and *Jagadānanda*. The book is complete in fourteen chapters in all.

***Daśama-ṭippanī*** is the commentary on the Tenth Canto of the *Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam*, also called the *Bṛhad-vaiṣṇava-toṣaṇī* (the name by which it is well known). The *Bhakti-ratnākara*, first wave, says: “In 1476 the *Bṛhat* (*Vaiṣṇava-toṣaṇī*) was completed, and in Śaka 1504 the *Laghu* (*Toṣaṇī*) was well approved.” See the Anubhāṣya on Ādi 10.84 and the *Bhakti-ratnākara*, first wave: “The four books of Sanātana Gosvāmī.”

## 37

> Subheading: Many books of Śrī Rūpa concern the sweet (*madhura*) service —

***Grantha*** — *anuṣṭubh*; here it does not mean “book.” In one *śloka* there are four “*granthas*,” that is, four *pādas*. A prose work is measured in the same way.

## 38

***Bhakti-rasāmṛta-sindhu*** is a work that gathers everything related to *kṛṣṇa-bhakti* and *bhakti-rasa*. It was composed in Śaka 1463. It has four divisions (*vibhāgas*): eastern, southern, western and northern. The eastern division is called *Sthāyi-bhāvotpādana*; it contains four *laharīs* (waves): general *bhakti*, *sādhana-bhakti*, *bhāva-bhakti* and *prema-bhakti*. The southern division is called *Bhakti-rasa-sāmānya-nirūpaṇa*; it contains five *laharīs*: *vibhāva*, *anubhāva*, *sāttvika*, *vyabhicārī* and *sthāyi-bhāva*. The western division is called *Mukhya-bhakti-rasa-nirūpaṇa*; it contains five *laharīs*: *śānta*, *prīti-bhakti-rasa* or *dāsya*, *preyo-bhakti-rasa* or *sakhya*, *vātsalya-bhakti-rasa* and *madhura-bhakti-rasa*. The northern division is called *Gauṇa-bhakti-rasādi-nirūpaṇa*; it contains nine *laharīs*: *hāsya-bhakti-rasa*, *adbhuta-bhakti-rasa*, *vīra-bhakti-rasa*, *karuṇa-bhakti-rasa*, *raudra-bhakti-rasa*, *bhayānaka-bhakti-rasa*, *bībhatsa-bhakti-rasa*, *maitrī-vairasthiti* (the friendly and hostile relations among the *rasas*) and *rasābhāsa*.

***Vidagdha-mādhava*** is a drama about Śrī Kṛṣṇa's pastimes in Vraja. This book was composed in Śaka 1454. The first act is called *Veṇu-nāda-vilāsa*, the second *Manmatha-lekha*, the third *Rādhā-saṅga*, the fourth *Veṇu-haraṇa*, the fifth *Rādhā-prasādana*, the sixth *Śarad-vihāra* and the seventh *Gaurī-vihāra*. It is a drama in seven acts.

***Ujjvala-nīlamaṇi*** is a book of poetics (*alaṅkāra-grantha*) on the transcendental sweet *rasa* of Vraja. The second verse says: “The *madhura* *rasa*, the king of *bhakti-rasas*, which was stated earlier only briefly among the principal *rasas* because of its great secrecy, is described here separately and in detail.”

That is: in the *Bhakti-rasāmṛta-sindhu* the *madhura-rasa* is described briefly, because it is the most confidential of the principal *rasas*, *śānta* and the rest; in this *Ujjvala-nīlamaṇi* only the *madhura-rasa*, the king of *bhakti-rasas*, is taken up separately. Here are described the kinds of heroes, the kinds of helpers, Kṛṣṇa's beloveds, Śrī Rādhikā, the kinds of heroines, the kinds of leaders of groups (*yūtheśvarī*), the kinds of messengers, the *sakhīs*, Hari's beloveds, *uddīpana*, *anubhāva*, *udbhāsvara*, the *sāttvika* and *vyabhicārī* moods, *sthāyi-bhāva*, *vipralambha* as one of the kinds of *śṛṅgāra*, *pūrva-rāga*, *māna*, *prema-vaicittya*, *pravāsa*, the states of union and separation, *sambhoga* (primary and secondary), and other subjects.

***Lalita-mādhava*** is a drama about Śrī Kṛṣṇa's pastimes in Dvārakā. This book was composed in Śaka 1459. The first act is called *Sāyaṁ-utsava*, the second *Śaṅkhacūḍa-vadha*, the third *Unmattā Rādhikā*, the fourth *Rādhikābhisāra*, the fifth *Candrāvalī-lābha*, the sixth *Lalitā-prāpti*, the seventh *Nava-vṛndāvana-saṅgama*, the eighth *Nava-vṛndāvana-vihāra*, the ninth *Citra-darśana* and the tenth *Pūrṇa-manoratha*. It is a drama in ten acts.

## 41

***Laghu-bhāgavatāmṛta*** is divided into two parts, the *Kṛṣṇāmṛta* and the *Bhaktāmṛta*. The first part describes: the supremacy of the spoken word (*śabda*) as a means of knowledge; then, first of all, Śrī Kṛṣṇa in His original form (*svayaṁ-rūpa*); His forms, not different from Him in essence but distinguished as *vilāsa*, *svāṁśa* and *āveśa*; the threefold *avatāra* (the three *puruṣa-avatāras*); the transcendence of Viṣṇu and of devotion to Viṣṇu among the three *guṇa-avatāras*; the 25 *līlā-avatāras* (the four Kumāras, Nārada, Varāha, Matsya, Yajña, the sage Nara-Nārāyaṇa, Kapila the theist, Dattātreya, Hayagrīva, Haṁsa, Pṛśnigarbha, Ṛṣabha, Pṛthu, Nṛsiṁha, Kūrma, Dhanvantari, Mohinī, Vāmana, Paraśurāma, Dāśarathi, Kṛṣṇa Dvaipāyana, Balarāma or Śeṣa-Saṅkarṣaṇa, Vāsudeva, Buddha and Kalki); the 14 *manvantara-avatāras* (Yajña, Vibhu, Satyasena, Hari, Vaikuṇṭha, Ajita, Vāmana, Sārvabhauma, Ṛṣabha, Viṣvaksena, Dharmasetu, Sudhāmā, Yogeśvara, Bṛhadbhānu); the four *yuga-avatāras* (of white, red, dark and black color); the various *kalpas* and their *avatāras*; and the examination of the *avatāras* as they stand in four states: *āveśa*, *prābhava*, *vaibhava* and *para*. Next are described the variety in the glory of the Lord's name in different pastimes; the examination of *śakti* and the possessor of *śakti*; the inconceivable harmony of the mutually opposed qualities of Bhagavān; that Śrī Kṛṣṇa is Bhagavān Himself, His supremacy (*paratamya*), that He is the source of the *avatāras* (*avatārin*), the possessor of the parts (*aṁśin*) and the Lord of lords; that impersonal Brahman is Śrī Kṛṣṇa's radiance; the sweetness and unsurpassed nature of Śrī Kṛṣṇa's two-armed human pastimes; the refutation of any difference between body and embodied in the transcendental things of Vaikuṇṭha; Śrī Kṛṣṇa's unborn nature, the beginninglessness of His appearance, and the absence of contradiction between the two; the eternity of the pastimes, the two kinds of pastimes, manifest and unmanifest, the variety of *rasa* in the manifest pastimes, the eternity of the pastimes of Vraja, Mathurā and Dvārakā, and the examination of the various abodes and their glory; the variety of sweetness in the stages of age — childhood (*bālya*), boyhood (*paugaṇḍa*), youth (*kaiśora*) and young manhood (*yuvā*) — and finally the four kinds of sweetness (*aiśvarya*, *krīḍā*, *veṇu* and *śrī-vigraha*). The second part describes: the need to worship devotees; the gradation of devotees according to the gradation of *bhajana*; Prahlāda, the Pāṇḍavas, the Yādavas, Uddhava and the maidens of Vraja, and above all Śrīmatī Rādhikā; and the glory of Śrī Rādhā-kuṇḍa.

## 43

***Bhāgavata-sandarbha***, also called the *Ṣaṭ-sandarbha*. In the first, the *Tattva-sandarbha*: the supremacy of the *Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam* over all other means of knowledge (*pramāṇas*) and the explanation of *tattva*. In the second, the *Bhagavat-sandarbha*: the examination of Brahman and Paramātmā; the description of Vaikuṇṭha and pure *sattva* (*viśuddha-sattva*); that the *svarūpa* possesses His own *śaktis* and is the shelter of opposed potencies; the establishment of the inconceivability and multiplicity of *śakti*; the division of internal (*antaraṅga*) and other *śaktis*; *māyā-śakti* and *svarūpa-śakti*; that the qualities are identical with the *svarūpa*; the eternity of Śrī Vigraha, His all-pervasiveness (*vibhūtā*), that He is the shelter of all, that He is beyond the gross and the subtle, self-luminous, made of form, qualities and pastimes, transcendental, and by nature perfectly full; that His paraphernalia (*paricchada*) are parts of His *svarūpa*; the transcendental nature of Vaikuṇṭha, His associates and the three-quarters manifestation (*tri-pād-vibhūti*); the difference between Brahman and Bhagavān; fullness in the state of Bhagavān (*bhagavattā*); that He is the subject of all the Vedas; an exposition of *svarūpa-śakti*; and that Bhagavān can be reached only through the Vedas and *bhakti*.

In the third, the *Paramātma-sandarbha*: Paramātmā and his kinds; the relative rank of the *guṇa-avatāras*; the *jīva*, *māyā*, the world; the establishment of *pariṇāma-vāda*; the resolution of the question of *vivarta*; the non-difference of the world from Paramātmā; the reality of the world and the opinion of Śrīdhara Svāmī; the attribution of agency to the *nirguṇa* Īśvara; the activity of the *līlā-avatāras* for the sake of devotees; and that, by the six kinds of signs, the purport (*tātparya*) is Bhagavān alone.

In the fourth, the *Kṛṣṇa-sandarbha*: that Kṛṣṇa is Bhagavān Himself; Kṛṣṇa's pastimes and qualities; the agency of the *puruṣa-avatāras*; Śrīdhara Svāmī's agreement; the harmony of all the scriptures in Kṛṣṇa; that Baladeva and others are Mahā-Saṅkarṣaṇa; the examination of the entry of all the portions [of the *avatāras*] into Kṛṣṇa and their eternal presence in Him; His two-armed form; the description of Goloka; the eternity of Vṛndāvana and the other abodes as Kṛṣṇa's abodes; the identity of Goloka and Vṛndāvana; the eternal status of the Yādavas and the cowherds as Kṛṣṇa's associates; the arrangement of the manifest and unmanifest pastimes; the harmony of the manifest and unmanifest pastimes; Śrī Kṛṣṇa's exceptional abundance of manifestation in Gokula; that the chief queens are *svarūpa-śakti*; the superiority of the *gopīs* over them; their names; and Rādhikā's superiority over all.

In the fifth, the *Bhakti-sandarbha*: that devotion to Bhagavān is directly the *abhidheya*; the establishment of the truth of *bhakti* by the method of *anvaya* and *vyatireka*; the hearing of all the scriptures; disregard of *karma* in the *anvaya* order — through *varṇāśrama* conduct and the knowledge included in it; the censure of *brāhmaṇas* who have turned away from Hari; disregard of *karma* offered to Bhagavān; disregard of *yoga*; showing the wearisomeness of knowledge and disregarding an independence that rests on some other shelter, whereby reverence for those who belong to Him (*tadīya*) is established; disregard of all non-devotees; the eternity of *bhakti*, and its status as *abhidheya*, even for devotees who are *jīvan-mukta* and *parama-mukta*, such as Śiva and others; that *bhakti* gives all fruits; its *nirguṇa* nature, self-luminosity and nature of supreme bliss; the special causes of *prīti* for Bhagavān; the fruit even of *bhajanābhāsa*; the praise of *niṣkāma-bhakti*; the renewed establishment of *niṣkāma-bhakti* according to differences in qualification; that association with *sādhus* is the root cause; the kinds and special features of *mahā-bhāgavatas*; the discernment of the “shelter of all” (*sarvāśraya-viveka*); the definition of *jñāna* in the enumeration of the kinds of *bhakti*; the definition of *ahaṁ-grahopāsanā*; the definition of *bhakti*; the definition of *āropa-siddhā* and the rest; surrender in *vaidhī-bhakti*; service to the guru; discussion of *mahā-bhāgavatas*; service to them; general service to Vaiṣṇavas; hearing, chanting, remembering, serving the feet, worship, offenses and their remedy, prayer, *dāsya*, *sakhya*, self-surrender; the examination of *rāgānugā-bhakti*, the special features of the worship of Kṛṣṇa, and the order of attaining perfection (*siddhi*).

In the sixth, the *Prīti-sandarbha*: the establishment of *prīti* as the highest goal (*parama-puruṣārtha*); the difference between *saviśeṣa* and *nirviśeṣa* in liberation; the difference between *jīvan-mukti* and *utkrānta-mukti*; the superiority of *prīti* for Bhagavān over all kinds of liberation; the attainment of the highest goal upon direct realization of the Supreme Truth; immediate and gradual liberation (*sadya-krama-mukti*); *jīvan-mukti* and *utkrānta-mukti* described as realization of Brahman and realization of Bhagavān; the twofold nature of realization of Bhagavān, internal and external; its superiority to the realization of Brahman; *jīvan-mukti* and *utkrānta-mukti* of external realization; the kinds [of liberation] beginning with *sālokya*; the superiority of *sāmīpya*; that *bhakti* is liberation and worthy of acceptance; the reasoning for this; the intrinsic definition (*svarūpa-lakṣaṇa*) of *prīti*; the marginal definition (*taṭastha-lakṣaṇa*) of *prīti*, which is beyond the *guṇas*, and the differences in its appearance; the distinctions among *prīti*, *rati* and the rest; the establishment that the *kāma* of the maidens of Vraja is pure *prema*; the mixed nature of *jñāna-bhakti* and the like; the excellence of the *prīti* of those who identify themselves with the associates (*parikara*); the difference in the experience of majesty and sweetness; the superiority of the residents of Gokula, and above them the successive superiority of the friends, the parents, the *gopīs* and Rādhikā; the *rasa*-nature of imitative acts; the superiority over mundane *rasas*; the division of *ālambana*; the division of *uddīpana*; qualities; the types of heroes, *dhīrodātta* and the rest; the supreme excellence of sweetness; *anubhāva*, *sañcārī*; the fivefold nature of *rasa*; the sevenfold nature of secondary *rasas*; *rasābhāsa*; in *śānta*, *dāsya*, *praśraya*, *vātsalya* and *ujjvala*, the kinds of *vallabhas* (beloveds); *sthāyī*, *sambhoga* and the kinds of *vipralambha*; *pūrva-rāga*, *māna*, *prema-vaicittya*, *pravāsa*, and the glory of Śrīmatī Rādhikā-devī.

## 44

***Śrī Gopāla-campū*** has two parts, Pūrva and Uttara. The Pūrva-campū has thirty-three *pūraṇas* (completions) and the Uttara thirty-seven. The Pūrva-campū was written in Śaka 1510. In the Pūrva-campū: *pūraṇa* 1 — Vṛndāvana and Goloka; 2 — the prologue, the description of the killing of Pūtanā, the going to the houses of the *gopīs* at Yaśodā's command, the bathing of Rāma and Kṛṣṇa, Siddhakaṇṭha and Madhukaṇṭha; 3 — Yaśodā's dream; 4 — the birth festival; 5 — the meeting of Nanda and Vasudeva, the killing of Pūtanā; 6 — the *autthānika-līlā*, the breaking of the cart, the naming; 7 — the killing of Tṛṇāvarta, the eating of earth, childish mischief, stealing; 8 — churning curd, nursing at the breast, breaking the curd pot, binding, the felling of the two *arjuna* trees (*yamalārjuna*), Yaśodā's lament; 9 — entering Vṛndāvana; 10 — the killing of Vatsāsura, the killing of Bakāsura, the killing of Vyomāsura; 11 — the killing of Aghāsura, the bewildering of Brahmā; 12 — going to the pasture; 13 — grazing the cows, the subduing of Kāliya; 14 — the killing of Gardabhāsura, Kṛṣṇa's caresses; 15 — the *pūrva-rāga* of the *gopīs*; 16 — the killing of Pralambāsura, the swallowing of the forest fire; 17 — the *gopīs'* efforts directed toward Kṛṣṇa; 18 — the lifting of Govardhana; 19 — Kṛṣṇa's *abhiṣeka*; 20 — Nanda's return from Varuṇa's abode, the cowherds' vision of Goloka; 21 — the observance of the Kātyāyanī vow; 22 — the begging of food from the wives of the sacrificers; 23 — the meeting of the *gopīs*; 24 — the pastimes with the *gopīs*, the disappearance of Rādhā and Kṛṣṇa, the search of the *gopīs*; 25 — Kṛṣṇa's reappearance; 26 — the *gopīs'* resolve; 27 — water sports; 28 — the release of Nanda, seized by a serpent; 29 — various secret sports; 30 — the killing of Śaṅkhacūḍa, Holī; 31 — the killing of Ariṣṭa; 32 — the killing of Keśī; 33 — Nārada's arrival, and the Śaka year and *saṁvat* in which the book was composed.

***Hari-bhakti-vilāsa*** is a book of Vaiṣṇava *smṛti* compiled by Śrīla Sanātana Gosvāmī Prabhu and gathered together (*samāhṛta*) by Śrīla Gopāla Bhaṭṭa Gosvāmī Prabhu; it is complete in twenty *vilāsas*. The first *vilāsa* treats of the guru and disciple and of the *mantra*; the second, of initiation; the third, of right conduct (*sadācāra*), remembrance, and purity (bathing and the *sandhyā* rites); the fourth, of purificatory rites, *tilaka*, *mudrās*, beads, and the worship of the guru; the fifth, of posture, *prāṇāyāma*, *nyāsa*, and the Deity (*śrī-mūrti*), such as the *śālagrāma*; the sixth, of invoking the Deity, bathing Him, and the necessary accompanying duties; the seventh, of the flowers suitable for the worship of Śrī Viṣṇu; the eighth, of what is offered before the Deity: incense, lamps, food offerings (*naivedya*), dance, song, instrumental music, *nīrājana* (*ārati*), prayers, obeisances, and the washing away of offenses; the ninth, of *tulasī*, Vaiṣṇava *śrāddha*, and *naivedya*; the tenth, of the devotee of Bhagavān, that is, the Vaiṣṇava or *sādhu*; the eleventh, of the worship (*arcana*) of the Deity, the holy name of Śrī Hari, *japa* and *kīrtana* of the name, offenses against the name and release from them, the glory of *bhakti*, and surrender (*śaraṇāgati*); the twelfth, of the rules of *ekādaśī*; the thirteenth, of fasting and the vow of Mahādvādaśī; the fourteenth, of various duties in various months; the fifteenth, of *nirjalā ekādaśī*, bearing heated marks (*tapta-mudrā*), *Cāturmāsya*, *Janmāṣṭamī*, *pārśva-ekādaśī*, *śravaṇā-dvādaśī*, *Rāma-navamī*, and *Vijayā-daśamī*; the sixteenth, of the Kārttika duties, or the Dāmodara (*ūrja*) vow, the offering of lamps and so on, the worship of Govardhana, and the Ratha-yātrā; the seventeenth, of *puraścaraṇa*, *japa*, and beads; the eighteenth, of the kinds of Deities of Viṣṇu; the nineteenth, of the installation of the Deity, His bathing, and so on; and the twentieth, of the building of temples and the like, and of the duties of those devoted solely to the Lord (*ekāntika-bhakta*).

## 82

> Subheading: The longing for union after a long separation —

The *gopīs* are wholly devoted to pure service to Kṛṣṇa. They do not serve because they are enchanted by Kṛṣṇa's majesty or by any similar grandeur; therefore they never have any liking for the elephants, horses and royal dress of Kurukṣetra. Just as Kṛṣṇa, the beloved of the *gopīs*, is bound only by the *gopīs'* spotless love, so the *gopīs* too are the eternal maidservants of that very beloved of the *gopīs*. Yogis who have withdrawn from sense objects and whose minds are fixed on Him alone meditate on the Lord of inconceivable power. But those who are absorbed in sense objects take shelter at the feet of Hari for the sake of the welfare of their own bodies, sons, wives and the rest in this world, for the growth of worldly prosperity, or to be freed from their own cycle of birth and death. The *gopīs* have no such meditative effort or skill in good works. With all their senses, with body, mind and speech, they are absorbed in the pure service of Kṛṣṇa. Having renounced both dry, tasteless reasoning and worldly *rasa* — whether absent or present — the *gopīs* do not want their own beloved to be busy with others' affairs or, having become a person of dignity, to live elsewhere. Having established the son of the king of Vraja in Vṛndāvana, the *gopīs* find happiness only in pleasing Him by service to Kṛṣṇa, with body, mind and speech.

***Unmāda*** is divine madness (*divyonmāda*), which includes *udghūrṇā*, *citra-jalpa* and the rest. The *Ujjvala-nīlamaṇi* says: “A certain wondrous variety [of states], resembling delusion, in one who has attained some state (*gati*) of this *bhāva* called *mohana*, is called *divyonmāda*. Its kinds, *udghūrṇā*, *citra-jalpa* and others, are many.”

In *adhirūḍha-mahābhāva* there are two kinds, *modana* and *mādana*. The *modana bhāva* in the state of separation (*viśleṣa*) is known as “*mohana*.” In *mohana*, in the helplessness caused by separation, the *sāttvika-bhāvas* blaze brightly. “A certain wondrous variety [of states] in one who has attained an indescribable state (*gati*) and disposition (*vṛtti*) is *divyonmāda*.” That is, a certain inexpressible state of *mohana*, reached in a special disposition and full of a variety resembling delusion, is called “*divyonmāda*”; it has many kinds, such as *udghūrṇā* and *citra-jalpa*.

***Udghūrṇā*** is a distinctive state accompanied by various helpless acts. “*Udghūrṇā* is a distinctive state shown in various helpless acts. For example: ‘Rādhā, tormented by the delusion of separation from You, at one moment makes a bed in a bower as *vāsaka-sajjā*; at another, having taken the state of *khaṇḍitā* and fallen into a rage (*caṇḍī*), she scolds the blue cloud; at another, in the agitation of hurrying to a tryst (*abhisāra*), she reels in dreadful darkness — what state does she not assume?’” Uddhava said to Kṛṣṇa: “Rādhā, tormented by the delusion of separation from You, at times arranges a bed in a bower as *vāsaka-sajjā*, at times, becoming *khaṇḍitā*, rebukes the blue cloud, at times, becoming an *abhisārikā*, wanders in thick darkness — what state does she not assume!”[^87-1]

The *sannyāsa* of Śrī Mahāprabhu is not like the *sannyāsa* of the *karmīs* or the *jñānīs*. ***Kṣīra-corā Gopīnātha*** resides at the *śrīpāṭa* of Remuṇā (five miles west of Balasore station on the B. N. R. line). The servitor (*sevāyet*) of the present temple, Śyāmasundara Adhikārī, is a disciple [in the line] of Śrī Rasikānanda Murāri, a successor of Śrī Śyāmānanda Prabhu, from the *śrīpāṭa* of Gopīvallabhapura, at the edge of the Midnapore district.

***Sākṣi-gopāla.*** The temple stands in the village called Satyavādī, a short distance from the station of that name on the B. N. R. line on the way to Purī. In the time of Śrī Mahāprabhu the temple of Sākṣi-gopāla was in the city of Kaṭaka (see the *Amṛta-pravāha-bhāṣya* on Madhya 5.8).
