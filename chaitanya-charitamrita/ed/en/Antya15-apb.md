# Amṛta-pravāha-bhāṣya on Antya-līlā 15

## 0

**Summary.** After the *upala-bhoga* [offering to Lord Jagannātha], Mahāprabhu began to lament: a feeling stirred by Kṛṣṇa's form awoke in Him. When Kṛṣṇa disappeared on the night of the *rāsa*, the *gopīs* searched for Him from forest to forest; the same feelings now began to awaken in the Lord. Svarūpa Gosvāmī sang a song from the *Gīta-govinda*, and emotions (*bhāvas*) arose in Mahāprabhu: their rising (*bhāva-udaya*), their meeting (*bhāva-sandhi*), their mingling (*bhāva-śābalya*), the eight spiritual transformations (*aṣṭa-sāttvika-vikāra*) and more, and all of it became for Him an object of the highest relish. When He saw a garden on the seashore, the memory of Vṛndāvana awoke in Him, and all these feelings rose up with great force.

## 1

Plunged into the unfathomable ocean of feeling for Kṛṣṇa, Gaurahari, whose heart [now sank and now] rose to the surface, revealed the glory of *prema* in its many forms.[^1-1]

## 8–9

The five qualities are form for the eyes, song for the ears, fragrance for the nose, taste for the tongue and touch for the skin. These five transcendental qualities of Kṛṣṇa revealed themselves all at once in [the Lord's] five transcendental senses. When the mind was pulled toward these five objects at the same time, it lost consciousness.

## 14

“He who, with the flood of the nectar-ocean of beauty, inundates the mountains of women's hearts; who speaks charming words that delight the ear and is cool as millions of moons; who has covered the whole world with a nectar-flood of fragrance; whose lips are full of nectar — that Kṛṣṇa, the son of the king of the cowherds, O friend, forcibly draws all five of My senses to Himself.”

## 15–23

Kṛṣṇa's form, His words, the sound of His flute and so on — form, sound, touch, fragrance and the taste of His lips — these five are full of great sweetness. My five senses of knowledge long to experience each its own object in Śrī Kṛṣṇa. So each of them wants to mount the one horse, My mind, and gallop off in five directions at once. Friend, what can I say of My sorrow? My five senses of knowledge are utterly greedy for their objects, like robbers. They know that Kṛṣṇa belongs to another (*para-puruṣa*), and still they set out to steal each its own object in Kṛṣṇa. My mind is only one horse. Each sense of knowledge — the eyes and the rest — takes that horse and pulls it toward its own object (form and the rest), in five directions. When it is pulled like this all at once, the only gain is that the horse loses its life. How can I bear that?

You may say, “Why don't you restrain your senses?” But, friend, how can I blame the senses? Kṛṣṇa's form and the other four objects are by their very nature irresistibly attractive. Form and the rest, five in all, each pull the five senses toward themselves. The horse of the mind, ridden by these five senses of knowledge, is drawn and rushes in just those directions. And when the horse dies, My life too will depart.

The hearts of all the women in the three worlds are indeed like high mountains, and a drop from a wave of the nectar-ocean of Kṛṣṇa's form drowns those high mountains. Kṛṣṇa's clever speech — His playful jests (*rasa-narma*, that is, *parihāsa*) — treats women so unfairly that words cannot describe it: entering women's ears, it binds them with the cords of its sweetness and pulls, and the ears lose their life. Kṛṣṇa's body is exceedingly cool; its cool rays defeat millions of moons and sandal paste. Kṛṣṇa's body is supremely skilled at drawing women's mountain-like breasts, and it attracts their minds. The fragrance of Kṛṣṇa's body destroys the pride of enchanting musk and of the blue lotus. It enters the nostrils of the women of the world, makes its home there and draws them. The nectar of Kṛṣṇa's lips, mixed with the camphor of His gentle smile, steals women's minds by its sweetness. It takes away their craving for everything but Kṛṣṇa; yet by its very nature it is hard to obtain, and when it cannot be had, the mind is thrown into turmoil. That nectar of His lips is the chief wealth of the women of Vraja.

## 32

O *cūta* (a kind of mango), *piyāla*, *kā̃ṭhāla* (jackfruit), *āsana*, *kovidāra*, *jambu*, *arka*, *bilva*, *bakula*, mango, *kadamba*, *nīpa* (a kind of *kadamba*) and other trees! O all you other trees who dwell on the banks of the Yamunā and care for the highest good [of others] (who have vowed to do good to others)! Tell us, who have lost our very selves (whose minds are empty), where Kṛṣṇa is.

## 33

O auspicious *tulasī*, dear to Govinda's feet! Acyuta loves you dearly. Did you see Kṛṣṇa pass by here, wearing you, with a swarm of bees around Him?

## 34

O *mālatī*, O *mallikā*, O *jāti*, O *yūthikā*! Did you see Kṛṣṇa pass by here, giving you joy with the touch of His hand?

## 44

From here comes the fragrance of Kṛṣṇa, who wears a garland of *kunda* flowers colored with the *kuṅkuma* from His beloved's breasts as He touched Her body. O doe, did Kṛṣṇa pass along this path with Rādhikā, increasing the joy of your eyes?

---

[^1-1]: The verse translation reads differently: the Lord plunged wholeheartedly into this ocean in order to display the various aspects of its boundless glory (it does not say that His heart now sank and now rose to the surface).
