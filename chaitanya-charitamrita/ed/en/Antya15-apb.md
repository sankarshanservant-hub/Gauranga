# Amṛta-pravāha-bhāṣya on Antya-līlā 15

## 0

**Summary.** After the *upala-bhoga* [offering to Lord Jagannātha], Mahāprabhu began to lament: a feeling stirred by Kṛṣṇa's form awoke in Him. When Kṛṣṇa disappeared on the night of the *rāsa*, the *gopīs* searched for Him from forest to forest; the same feelings now began to awaken in the Lord. Svarūpa Gosvāmī sang a song from the *Gīta-govinda*, and emotions (*bhāvas*) arose in Mahāprabhu: their rising (*bhāvodaya*), their meeting (*bhāva-sandhi*), their mingling (*bhāva-śābalya*), the eight spiritual transformations (*aṣṭa-sāttvika-vikāra*) and more, and all of it became for Him an object of the highest relish. When He saw a garden on the seashore, the memory of Vṛndāvana awoke in Him, and all these feelings rose up with great force.

## 1

Plunged into the unfathomable ocean of feeling for Kṛṣṇa, Gaurahari, whose heart [now sank and now] rose to the surface, revealed the glory of *prema* in its many forms.

## 8–9

The five qualities are form for the eyes, song for the ears, fragrance for the nose, taste for the tongue and touch for the skin. These five transcendental qualities of Kṛṣṇa revealed themselves all at once in [the Lord's] five transcendental senses. When the mind was pulled toward these five objects at the same time, it lost consciousness.

## 14

“He who, with the flood of the nectar-ocean of beauty, inundates the mountains of women's hearts; who speaks charming words that delight the ear and is cool as millions of moons; who has covered the whole world with a nectar-flood of fragrance; whose lips are full of nectar — that Kṛṣṇa, the son of the king of the cowherds, O friend, forcibly draws all five of My senses to Himself.”

## 15–23

Kṛṣṇa's form, His words, the sound of His flute and so on — form, sound, touch, fragrance and the taste of His lips — these five are full of great sweetness. My five senses of knowledge long to experience each its own object in Śrī Kṛṣṇa. So each of them wants to mount the one horse, My mind, and gallop off in five directions at once. Friend, what can I say of My sorrow? My five senses of knowledge are utterly greedy for their objects, like robbers. They know that Kṛṣṇa is a man who belongs to another (*para-puruṣa*), and still they set out to steal each its own object in Kṛṣṇa. My mind is only one horse. Each sense of knowledge — the eyes and the rest — takes that horse and pulls it toward its own object (form and the rest), in five directions. When it is pulled like this all at once, the only gain is that the horse loses its life. How can I bear that?

You may say, “Why don't you restrain your senses?” But, friend, how can I blame the senses? Kṛṣṇa's form and the other four objects are by their very nature irresistibly attractive. Form and the rest, five in all, each pull the five senses toward themselves. The horse of the mind, ridden by these five senses of knowledge, is drawn and rushes in just those directions. And when the horse dies, My life too will depart.

The hearts of all the women in the three worlds are indeed like high mountains, and a drop from a wave of the nectar-ocean of Kṛṣṇa's form drowns those high mountains. Kṛṣṇa's clever speech — His playful jests (*rasa-narma*, that is, *parihāsa*) — treats women so unfairly that words cannot describe it[^15-23-1]: entering women's ears, it binds them with the cords of its sweetness and pulls, and the ears lose their life. Kṛṣṇa's body is exceedingly cool; its cool rays defeat millions of moons and sandal paste. Kṛṣṇa's body is supremely skilled at drawing women's mountain-like breasts, and it attracts their minds. The fragrance of Kṛṣṇa's body destroys the pride of enchanting musk and of the blue lotus. It enters the nostrils of the women of the world, makes its home there and draws them. The nectar of Kṛṣṇa's lips, mixed with the camphor of His gentle smile, steals women's minds by its sweetness. It takes away their craving for everything but Kṛṣṇa; yet by its very nature it is hard to obtain, and when it cannot be had, the mind is thrown into turmoil. That nectar of His lips is the chief wealth of the women of Vraja.

## 32

O *cūta* (a kind of mango), *piyāla*, *kā̃ṭhāla* (jackfruit), *āsana*, *kovidāra*, *jambu*, *arka*, *bilva*, *bakula*, mango, *kadamba*, *nīpa* (a kind of *kadamba*) and other trees! O all you other trees who dwell on the banks of the Yamunā and care for the highest good [of others] (who have vowed to do good to others)! Tell us, who have lost our very selves (whose minds are empty), where Kṛṣṇa is.

## 33

O auspicious *tulasī*, dear to Govinda's feet! Acyuta loves you dearly. Did you see Kṛṣṇa pass by here, wearing you, with a swarm of bees around Him?

## 34

O *mālatī*, O *mallikā*, O *jāti*, O *yūthikā*! Did you see Kṛṣṇa pass by here, giving you joy with the touch of His hand?

## 44

From here comes the fragrance of Kṛṣṇa, who wears a garland of *kunda* flowers colored with the *kuṅkuma* from His beloved's breasts as He touched Her body. O doe, did Kṛṣṇa pass along this path with Rādhikā, increasing the joy of your eyes?

## 51

O trees, tell us: Kṛṣṇa, the younger brother of Balarāma (Rāmānuja), is walking along with His arm on Rādhikā's shoulder and a lotus in His hand, followed (pursued) by bees maddened by the *tulasī*. As He passes, does He accept your bows and answer them with a loving glance?

## 63

O friend! He wears a charming yellow garment, like fresh lightning flashing on a fresh cloud; a beautiful flute is at His lips, and His face is like the moon in the full splendor of autumn. He is adorned with peacock feathers and shines with the luster of a lovely necklace (*tāra-hāra*). That Madana-mohana is spreading the thirst of My eyes.

## 64

Śrī Kṛṣṇa's luster outshines the smooth gloss of ground (*dalita*, pounded or crushed) collyrium. Its hue is soft like a fresh cloud; it is softer than the *indīvara* (blue lotus) and beyond all comparison[^64-1].

## 65–68

O friend, Śrī Kṛṣṇa is a wondrous cloud. The *cātaka* bird of My eyes cannot see that cloud and is dying of thirst. Kṛṣṇa's yellow garment is the lightning in that cloud, but it is unsteady. The pearl necklace on His neck shines like a row of (white) cranes beneath the cloud. His peacock feather is like the rainbow in the cloud, and His (five-colored) Vaijayantī garland is also like a rainbow. The soft sound of the flute at Kṛṣṇa's lips is the sweet thunder of the cloud of His form; hearing it, the peacocks of Vṛndāvana dance. The moonlight of Kṛṣṇa's beauty has risen like a wondrous moon, spotless and full (in all sixteen digits). The cloud of Kṛṣṇa pours the nectar of His pastimes and waters all fourteen worlds. But as soon as that cloud, Kṛṣṇa's form, appeared, the whirlwind of My misfortune swept it away elsewhere. And now, not seeing the cloud, the *cātaka* of My eyes is all but dead for want of water.

## 65

*Valāhaka*: a cloud.

## 67

*Akalaṅka pūrṇa-kala*: a wondrous moon risen spotless and full in all its sixteen digits (*kalās*).

## 68

*Jhañjhā-vāta*: a whirlwind.

## 71–76

Defeating the moon and the lotus, Kṛṣṇa has set the snare of His face to catch the does (the *gopīs*). In that snare the bait of His sweet smile has been laid, that is, a deceptive food to lure the (*gopī*) does. Leaving home and hearth, husband and shame, the does — the women of Vraja — fall into that snare and become His maidservants. Ah, our friend Śrī Kṛṣṇa behaves just like a hunter! This hunter cares nothing for *dharma* or *adharma*; he devises many tricks to steal the hearts of the does, the beauties of Vraja. His *makara* earrings glitter and dance on His cheeks and steal the women's minds. He pierces their hearts with the arrows of His smiling sidelong glances and has no fear at all of killing women. Kṛṣṇa's broad chest (given to plunder, like a bandit) bears two marks as ornaments: Lakṣmī and Śrīvatsa (a curl of hair turning to the right). It forcibly draws hundreds of thousands of goddesses of Vraja, their minds and their breasts, and makes them maidservants of that very Hari. Kṛṣṇa's two arms, exquisitely beautiful, are like long door-bolts, almost like the bodies of black serpents. They enter the gap (the middle) between the two mountain-like breasts of the women and bite the heart. (From the poison of that bite, His touch, the *gopī* burns in the fever of loving desire.) The palms and soles of Kṛṣṇa surpass camphor, *veṇāmūla* root and sandalwood and are cooler than millions of moons. Whoever they touch even once is freed from the burning poison of Kandarpa.

## 74

*Ḍākātiyā vakṣa*: the chest (of Kṛṣṇa) that, like a robber, forcibly carries off all the (Vraja) women.

## 75

*Śaila-chidre*: into the gap (the middle) between the two breasts upon the heart.

## 78

O friend! His chest is broad and charming like a door made of sapphires (*indranīla* gems). His arms remove the turmoil of mind (the fever of loving desire) of young maidens tormented by Kāma. His body is cool like the moon, *hari-candana*, the lotus (*utpala*) and camphor. That Madana-mohana is spreading the longing of My breast.

## 81

Seeing their pride in their good fortune, Kṛṣṇa vanished right there, to quell that pride and to show them grace.

## 84

My mind remembers Hari, who here, in the *rāsa* dance, enjoyed many pastimes and jested.

## 97

Seeing the rows of beautiful gardens on the seashore, the Lord again and again remembered the forest of Vṛndāvana and became helpless with *prema*. Gaurāṅga, the connoisseur of the taste of *bhakti*, is saying “Kṛṣṇa! Kṛṣṇa!” with a restless (*pracala*, unsteady) tongue. Will such a Caitanyadeva come again within the path of my sight?

*Thus ends the fifteenth chapter of the “Amṛta-pravāha-bhāṣya.”*

[^15-23-1]: The translation of verse 20 reads differently: it speaks of the indescribable devastation that this nectar brings.

[^64-1]: The verse translation reads differently: Kṛṣṇa's body resembles collyrium in its luster.
