## 81

*Sijera bāḍī.* In Utkala a flower garden is called a *phul-bāḍī*. *Sij* shrubs grow there, that is, the *manasā-sij* and the thorny *sij*; these are called a *sijera bāḍī*. Here *bāḍī* means a hedge.

## 91

*Viśvāsa-khānāra kāyastha.* The accounts office of the ruler of Gauḍa was called the *Viśvāsa-khānā*. *Kāyasthas* worked there, because the king trusted them.

## 92

*Parama-vaiṣṇava.* One who at heart longs for liberation (a *mumukṣu*) is not counted among pure Vaiṣṇavas. In fact, since Rāmadāsa worshipped Rāma, he may be called “almost a Vaiṣṇava” (*vaiṣṇava-prāya*). But in those days many were unable[^92-1] to distinguish the grades of pure Vaiṣṇavas, and so Śrī Rāmadāsa, born in a *kāyastha* family, was known in the world as a *parama-vaiṣṇava* (a great Vaiṣṇava).

## 110

The desire for liberation and pride in learning — these two faults kept Rāmadāsa from becoming a “pure Vaiṣṇava.”

*Thus ends the thirteenth chapter of the “Amṛta-pravāha-bhāṣya.”*

[^92-1]: The edition reads *asakta* (“unattached”); by the sense we read *aśakta* (“unable”).
