# Anubhāṣya on Madhya-līlā 23

## 1

> Subheading: Obeisance to Gaura, great in generosity, the giver of the name and *prema* never given before —

**aty-udāraḥ** (great in generosity) **yaḥ** (Gaura — Kṛṣṇa) **cirāt** **adattam** (never before given) **nija-gupta-vittaṁ** (His own treasure, a deep secret) **sva-prema-nāmāmṛtam** **ā-pāmaraṁ** (without distinguishing the righteous from the sinful, the qualified from the unqualified) **janebhyaḥ** **vitatāra** (bestowed), **tam** (Gaura-Kṛṣṇa) **ahaṁ** **prapadye** (take as my shelter).

## 3

> Subheading: The instructions to Śrī Sanātana — (3) the description of *prayojana* (love for Kṛṣṇa); the fruit of *abhidheya*, *sādhana-bhakti*, is the *sādhya*, *prema-bhakti*, which is the *prayojana* —

## 4

> Subheading: *Bhāva*, or *rati*, is the liquid, sprouting state of *prema*; in its dense, ripe state it is *prema* itself —

## 5

> Subheading: The definition of *bhāva*; its essential and accompanying characteristics — (*Bhakti-rasāmṛta-sindhu*, Pūrva-vibhāga, third *laharī*, first verse) —

**śuddha-sattva-viśeṣātmā** (the *bhāva* whose *ātmā* — that is, whose eternally perfect nature, residing in the Lord's eternal beloved ones — is that very special *śuddha-sattva*: a function [*vṛtti*] of Bhagavān Kṛṣṇa's *svarūpa-śakti* called *saṁvit*, which illumines everything)[^5-1] **prema-sūryāṁśu-sāmya-bhāk** (resembling a ray of the sun of *prema* — the first glimmer of *prema*) **rucibhiḥ** (by desires: the desire to attain [the Lord], the desire oneself to serve Him favorably, and the desire for intimate affection with Him) **citta-māsṛṇya-kṛt** (which makes the heart soft and moist) **asau** **bhāvaḥ** (the sprout of *prema*) **ucyate**.

## 6

> Subheading: The description of the characteristics of *prema* —

These two verses describe: (1) *śuddha-sattva-viśeṣātmā* and so on — the essential characteristic of *bhāva*; (2) its melting the heart with taste — the accompanying characteristic of *bhāva*.

The two kinds of *bhāva*: (1) *bhāva* born of absorption in *sādhana*; (2) *bhāva* born of the grace of Kṛṣṇa and His devotee. “*Bhāva* is born in two ways: through absorption in *sādhana*, and — for the most fortunate — through the grace of Kṛṣṇa and His devotee.”[^6-1] Some, again, take these two *bhāvas* to mean *kevalā* and *miśrā*; but that meaning does not fit here; the meaning given before is the fitting one.

## 7

> Subheading: The definition of *prema* — (*Bhakti-rasāmṛta-sindhu*, Pūrva-vibhāga, fourth *laharī*, first verse) —

**samyaṅ-masṛṇita-svāntaḥ** (that by which the heart is completely softened, moistened) **mamatvātiśayāṅkitaḥ** (marked by an intense sense of “He is mine”)[^7-1] — having these two accompanying characteristics — **yaḥ** **sāndrātmā** (dense in its very nature) (having this essential characteristic) **bhāvaḥ**, **sa eva** **budhaiḥ** **‘premā’** **nigadyate** (is called).

## 8

> Subheading: The definition of *prema* according to the Pañcarātra — (*Bhakti-rasāmṛta-sindhu*, Pūrva-vibhāga, fourth *laharī*, second verse) (the words of the *Nārada-pañcarātra*) —

**viṣṇau** (toward Bhagavān) **prema-saṅgatā** (full of *prema*) **ananya-mamatā** (exclusive [*aikāntikī*], full of relationship with Him)[^8-1] **mamatā** (love [*prīti*]) — **iti** **bhīṣma-prahlādoddhava-nāradaiḥ** (by the devotees) (it) **bhaktiḥ** (*bhāva*) **ucyate**.

## 9

> Subheading: The path to *prema-bhakti* by stages: first, from *śraddhā* to *āsakti*, the *abhidheya*, *sādhana-bhakti*, and the dawning of *rati*, or *bhāva-bhakti*; when *rati* becomes dense, the *prayojana*, *prema-bhakti* —

## 9–13

*Sādhana-bhakti* is this: first the *sādhaka's* faith; its fruit, the company of sādhus, or taking shelter at the feet of the guru; in that company, *bhajana-kriyā* (the practice of worship); its fruit, freedom from *anarthas* (*anartha-nivṛtti*); its fruit, *niṣṭhā*, or undistracted constancy; its fruit, *ruci*, or desire preceded by understanding; its fruit, *āsakti*, or spontaneous (*svārasikī*) taste. The *sādhya*, *rati*, that arises from *sādhana-bhakti* as the fruit of *āsakti* is called *bhāva*.

*Bhāva-bhakti* is by nature a special *śuddha-sattva*, resembling a ray of the sun of *prema*; through taste it moistens the heart. The first, sprouting state of *prema* is what is called *bhāva-bhakti*. Before it becomes *prema* it is called *bhāva*; when it ripens and reaches its highest fullness, it is given the name *prema-bhakti*. Therefore, with the words *prema-sūryāṁśu-sāmya-bhāk* (resembling a ray of the sun of *prema*), the author pointed out the difference in degree between *bhāva-bhakti* and *prema-bhakti*. The devotee in whom *rati* has arisen gradually rises higher and attains *prema-bhakti*. When *rati* becomes dense it is called *prema*. This *prema* is the fruit of *bhakti*, the *prayojana*; it is full of supreme bliss.

## 14–15

> Subheading: Evidence from the scriptures — (*Bhakti-rasāmṛta-sindhu*, Pūrva-vibhāga, fourth *laharī*, the “Prema-bhakti-laharī,” verses 15–16) —

**ādau** **śraddhā** (loosening one's attachment to what is unreal and changing — firm faith in Viṣṇu, whose form is transcendental, full of eternity, knowledge and bliss), **tataḥ** (once faith is gained) **sādhu-saṅgaḥ** (taking shelter at the feet of the guru and the Vaiṣṇavas, seeing them as not of this world, and then learning how to worship, beginning with initiation into Kṛṣṇa's mantra), **atha** (then, following them on the path of *śruti*, near the guru's feet) **bhajana-kriyā** (the practice of worship of Kṛṣṇa), **tataḥ** (from the practice of worship) **anartha-nivṛttiḥ** (when one sets out toward the highest goal, ceasing to enjoy anything else) **syāt** (comes about); **tataḥ** (after giving up association with sense objects) **niṣṭhā** (undistracted constancy — by the Lord's words, “*Śama* is the fixing of the intelligence on Me”[^14-15-1]), **tataḥ** **ruciḥ** (*rāga*), **atha** (after that) **āsaktiḥ** (spontaneous taste), **tataḥ** **bhāvaḥ** (first, the sprout of *prema*), **tataḥ** **premā** (the ultimate goal) **abhyudañcati** (arises) — **sādhakānām** **premṇaḥ** **prādurbhāve** (in the arising) **ayam** **kramaḥ** **bhavet**.

When the nine sprouts of *prīti* — the signs of *bhāva* — described above have arisen in someone's heart, then even when difficulties come to him in this material realm, he takes no account of them.

## 41

> Subheading: The five kinds of *rati* —

*Rati* (*Bhakti-rasāmṛta-sindhu*, Pūrva-vibhāga, third *laharī*): “If the sign of *rati* — as if the heart had softened within — is clearly seen in those who desire liberation and others, it is not *rati*. But seeing its signs, the simple-minded (*bāla*) are amazed, while one who knows easily recognizes it. Therefore it is called *ratyābhāsa*, a semblance of *rati*.” The sign of *rati* is the softness, or moistness, of the heart when it becomes manifest. But when it is seen in *mumukṣus* or *bubhukṣus*, it never deserves the name *rati*. Seeing the signs of this *rati*, rooted in motives other than service to Kṛṣṇa, the ignorant and simple-minded are amazed. But those versed in the *siddhānta* of *bhakti* call it “a semblance of *rati*” (*ratyābhāsa*).

## 42

> Subheading: The five *rasas* bring Kṛṣṇa under control —

*Sthāyi-bhāva* (*Bhakti-rasāmṛta-sindhu*, Dakṣiṇa-vibhāga, fifth *laharī*, first verse): “The *bhāva* that brings under its control both the *bhāvas* in harmony with it and those opposed to it, and reigns like a good king, is called the *sthāyi-bhāva*. Here the *sthāyi-bhāva* is said to be *rati* whose object is Śrī Kṛṣṇa.” The *bhāva* that brings under its control the harmonious *bhāvas*, beginning with laughter (*hāsa*), and the opposing ones, beginning with anger (*krodha*), and reigns like the best of kings, is the *sthāyi-bhāva*. Here only *rati* whose object is Śrī Kṛṣṇa is called the *sthāyi-bhāva*.

## 46

> Subheading: The “cause” of *rasa*, *vibhāva*, is twofold: (1) *avalambana* and (2) *uddīpana* —

(Ibid.) “The *vibhāvas* are to be known as the causes by which *rati* is relished. They are twofold: some are *ālambanas*, others *uddīpanas*.” As the *Agni Purāṇa* says about this: “That in which and by which *rati* and the rest are made relishable (*vibhāvyate*) is called *vibhāva*; it is twofold — *ālambana* and *uddīpana*.” The cause by which *kṛṣṇa-rati* is relished is called *vibhāva*; it is of two kinds, *ālambana* and *uddīpana*. That in which and by which *rati* and the rest are made relishable is called *vibhāva* (consisting of *ālambana* and *uddīpana*) in the *Agni Purāṇa* and other scriptures.

*Ālambana* (ibid.): “The wise regard Kṛṣṇa and His devotees as the *ālambanas* — as the object (*viṣaya*) of *rati* and the rest, and as its vessel (*ādhāra*).” These two — Kṛṣṇa as the object of *rati* and the rest (that is, also of the secondary *rasas*, beginning with *hāsya*), and Kṛṣṇa's devotee as its vessel — the learned call *ālambana*.

*Uddīpana* (ibid.): “Those things are called *uddīpanas* that kindle *bhāva*. They are Śrī Kṛṣṇacandra's qualities, His activities, His adornment, His smile, the fragrance of His body, His flute, horn, ankle bells and conch, His footprints, the holy places, *tulasī*, the devotees, the day of Hari and so on.” Whatever manifests *bhāva* is *uddīpana*: Śrī Kṛṣṇa's qualities, activities and adornment (the means of decorating the body — arranging the hair with a comb and so on), and His smile (gentle laughter), the fragrance of His body, His flute, horn, ankle bells, conch, His footprints, the holy places, *tulasī*, the devotees, the day of Hari — the vow of Ekādaśī and so on.

## 47

> Subheading: The “effect” of *rasa*, *anubhāva*, of 13 kinds; the 8 kinds of *sāttvika*, also an “effect” of *rasa* —

*Anubhāva* (*Bhakti-rasāmṛta-sindhu*, Dakṣiṇa-vibhāga, second *laharī*, first verse): “The *anubhāvas* reveal the *bhāvas* residing in the heart. They are mostly external transformations and are called *udbhāsvaras*. They are dancing, rolling on the ground, singing, loud crying, stretching the body, roaring (*huṅkāra*), yawning, heavy breathing, disregard for people's opinion, salivation, loud laughter, dizziness, hiccups and so on. According to their meaning they are given two names, *śīta* (cool) and *kṣepaṇa* (impetuous). Singing, yawning and the like are *śīta*; dancing and the like are called *kṣepaṇa*.” The external transformations that reveal the *bhāvas* residing in the heart and are known by the name *udbhāsvara* are the *anubhāvas*: dancing, rolling on the ground, singing, loud crying, stretching the body, roaring, yawning, long sighs, disregard for people's opinion, salivation, loud laughter, dizziness, hiccups and so on. They are given two names, *śīta* and *kṣepaṇa*: singing, yawning and so on are *śīta*, and dancing and so on are *kṣepaṇa*.

*Udbhāsvara*: “The wise call *udbhāsvaras* those things that shine forth (*udbhāsante*) in their own abode [the body]: the slipping of the waist-knot (*nīvi*), the upper cloth and the braid, stretching the body, yawning, flaring of the nostrils, sighs and so on.” Whatever manifests in the body of a person filled with *bhāva* the learned call *udbhāsvara*: the loosening of the waist-knot (*nīvi*), the slipping of the upper cloth and the braid, stretching the body, yawning, flaring of the nostrils, sighs, and also the external transformations described above — rolling on the ground, hiccups and so on.

*Stambha* and the rest: see Madhya 14.167.

## 48

> Subheading: The “helpers” of *rasa*, the *vyabhicārī-bhāvas*, 33 in number —

*Nirveda*, *harṣa* and the rest: see Madhya 14.167.

*Vyabhicārī* (*Bhakti-rasāmṛta-sindhu*, Dakṣiṇa-vibhāga, fourth *laharī*, first verse): “… the thirty-three *bhāvas* that are called *vyabhicārīs*. They move toward the *sthāyi-bhāva* in a special way, facing it. They are recognized by words, by movements of the body and by *sattva*; these are the *vyabhicārīs*. They impart movement (*sañcārayanti*) to the *bhāva*, and so they are also called *sañcārīs*. They rise and sink in the nectar ocean of the *sthāyi-bhāva* like waves, swell it, and become one with it.” The *vyabhicārī-bhāvas* are thirty-three. They move in the *sthāyi-bhāva* in a special way, coming to the fore. With words, with the body (the brows, the eyes and so on) and with *anubhāvas* born of *sattva*, the *vyabhicārī-bhāvas* impart movement to the *bhāva*; therefore they are called *sañcārīs*. Sinking into the nectar ocean of the *sthāyi-bhāva*, they swell it like waves and become one with it.

## 51

> Subheading: The limit of *sakhya* and *vātsalya* is *anurāga*; among them, for the *priya-narma-sakhās* — Subala and the others — the limit is even *bhāva* —

## 50–51

In *śānta-rasa*, *rati* grows and reaches its limit in *prema*. In *dāsya-rasa*, *dāsya-rati* grows through *sneha*, *māna* and *praṇaya* up to *rāga*. In *sakhya-rasa*, *sakhya-rati* grows through *sneha*, *māna*, *praṇaya* and *rāga* up to *anurāga*. In *vātsalya-rasa*, *vātsalya-rati* grows through *sneha*, *māna*, *praṇaya* and *rāga* up to *anurāga*. The special point is that the *sakhya-rati* of Subala and the others, though they belong to *sakhya-rasa*, grows through *sneha*, *māna*, *praṇaya*, *rāga* and *anurāga* up to *bhāva*.

## 52

> Subheading: The variety of divisions of the five *rasas*, beginning with *śānta* —

*Bhakti-rasāmṛta-sindhu*, Paścima-vibhāga, second *laharī*, verse 41: “Two divisions of it are stated — *ayoga* and *yoga*.” That is, for this *prīta-bhakti-rasa* two divisions are stated, *ayoga* and *yoga*.

*Ayoga*: “The wise call the absence of association with Hari *ayoga*. In *ayoga* the actions of all [the devotees] are these: fixing the mind on Him, contemplating His qualities and so on, thinking of the means to attain Him, and the like.” The learned call the absence of association with Bhagavān *ayoga*. In *ayoga* one fixes the mind on Hari, that is, gives one's mind to Hari, and contemplates His qualities and so on. For all devotees, beginning with the servants, thinking of the means to attain Kṛṣṇa and the like are called “actions” (*kriyā*).

*Yoga*: “Meeting with Kṛṣṇa is called *yoga*.” That is, meeting with Śrī Kṛṣṇa is called *yoga*.

Of the *rasas* beginning with *śānta*: in *śānta* and *dāsya* there are two divisions, *yoga* and *viyoga*; in them *yoga* and *ayoga* have few subdivisions. The distinction of *yoga* and *ayoga* exists in all five *rasas*, but in *sakhya* and *vātsalya* there are many subdivisions.

The subdivisions of *yoga*: “*Yoga* too is said to be of three kinds: *siddhi*, *tuṣṭi* and *sthiti*.” That is, *yoga* has three kinds — *siddhi*, *tuṣṭi* and *sthiti*.

The subdivisions of *ayoga*: “*Ayoga* too is said to be of two kinds: *utkaṇṭhita* and *viyoga*.” That is, *ayoga* is of two kinds — *utkaṇṭhita* and *viyoga*.

## 53

> Subheading: In Dvārakā, in the *svakīya madhura-rati* full of majesty (*aiśvarya*), there is *rūḍha-mahābhāva*; in Vṛndāvana, in the pure (*kevalā*) *parakīya madhura-rati* full of sweetness (*mādhurya*), *adhirūḍha-mahābhāva* —

*Adhirūḍha* (*Ujjvala-nīlamaṇi*, the section on the *sthāyi-bhāva*, 170): “Where the *anubhāvas* attain a certain special excellence beyond the *anubhāvas* described for *rūḍha*, that *bhāva* is called *adhirūḍha*.” In *madhura-rasa*, *madhura-rati* grows through *sneha*, *māna*, *praṇaya*, *rāga*, *anurāga* and *bhāva* up to *mahābhāva*. *Rūḍha-* and *adhirūḍha-mahābhāva* exist only in *madhura-rasa*. *Rūḍha* is seen in Dvārakā, and *adhirūḍha-bhāva* only in Gokula.

## 58

> Subheading: *Śṛṅgāra-rasa* is twofold — (1) *sambhoga* and (2) *vipralambha*; *sambhoga* has countless kinds —

“It is held to be twofold, divided into *vipralambha* and *sambhoga*.” That is, *madhura-rasa* is of two kinds, *sambhoga* and *vipralambha*.

*Vipralambha* (*Ujjvala-nīlamaṇi*, the section on *vipralambha*, verses 3–4): “The *bhāva* of the youthful pair, whether not yet united or united, that intensifies when the embraces and so on that they desire are not attained, is to be known as *vipralambha*. It enhances *sambhoga*.” “Without *vipralambha*, *sambhoga* does not attain fullness.” The *bhāva* that arises in the *nāyaka* and *nāyikā* (the hero and the heroine) — before their first meeting, when they are not united, and after the meeting, when they are united — at these two times, when the embraces and so on desired by each other are not attained, is called *vipralambha*; it nourishes *sambhoga*.

*Sambhoga*: “The *bhāva* of the youthful pair that rises to jubilation as they partake, pleasing each other (*ānukūlya*), of glances, embraces and so on is called *sambhoga*.” The *bhāva* that arises as the *nāyaka* and *nāyikā* share glances, embraces and so on for each other's happiness and rise to jubilation is called *sambhoga*. In the waking state the principal (*mukhya*) *sambhoga* is of four kinds: (1) after *pūrva-rāga*, *saṅkṣipta*; (2) after *māna*, *saṅkīrṇa*; (3) after a separation (*pravāsa*) that was not far, *sampanna*; and (4) after a distant separation, *samṛddhimān*. In dreams, the secondary (*gauṇa*) *sambhoga* is likewise of four kinds, as before.

## 59

> Subheading: *Vipralambha* is of four kinds —

*Pūrva-rāga* (*Ujjvala-nīlamaṇi*, the section on *vipralambha*, fifth verse): “The *rati* that, before their meeting, is born of seeing, hearing and so on, and blossoms in both, the wise call *pūrva-rāga*.” The *rati* that, before the meeting, is born of seeing, hearing and so on and, blending with the *vibhāvas* and the rest in both, becomes full of relish — that is *pūrva-rāga*.

*Māna*: “The *bhāva* of the hero and heroine, devoted to each other, that even when they are together obstructs the embraces, glances and so on that they desire, is called *māna*.” The *bhāva* of the *nāyaka* and *nāyikā*, devoted to each other — whether together or in different places — that obstructs the glances, embraces and so on that they desire is called *māna*.

*Pravāsa*: “The separation of a youthful pair who have met before — by distance and so on — the wise call *pravāsa*.” The wise call *pravāsa* the separation, by distance and so on, of the *nāyaka* and *nāyikā* who have met before.

*Prema-vaicittya*: “The pain that, by the very nature of the height of *prema*, comes from a particular thought even in the beloved's presence is called *prema-vaicittya*.” The pain that, by the nature of the height of *prema*, comes even in the beloved's presence — from fear of separation from Him — is *prema-vaicittya*.

## 61

> Subheading: Even while near Kṛṣṇa, the queens, owing to *prema-vaicittya*, fear separation from Him — (*Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam*, Canto 10, Chapter 90, verse 15) —

In Dvārakā Bhagavān Śrī Kṛṣṇa kept stealing the hearts of the queens with His playful pastimes. Their hearts were absorbed in Him, and though they were very near Him, the feeling never left them: “We are losing Him, losing Him!” And, as if maddened, they would speak to one another thus —

**he kurari**, **īśvaraḥ** (Kṛṣṇa) **rātryām** **gupta-bodhaḥ** (as if with consciousness asleep) **svapiti** (lies down); **tvam** **tu** **jagati** (alone) **vīta-nidrā** (being) **na śeṣe** (do not sleep, do not even wish to lie down, but on the contrary, breaking sleep,) **vilapasi** (the sense: this is improper). **he sakhi**, (or else it is not your fault, because) **vayam iva** **tvam** **nalina-nayana-hāsodāra-līlekṣitena** (by the generous, playful glance of the lotus-eyed Bhagavān together with His smile — His free, smiling sidelong glance) **kaccit** (a question) **gāḍha-nirviddha-cetāḥ** (with a heart utterly captivated)?
