# Amṛta-pravāha-bhāṣya on Madhya-līlā 21

## 0

**Summary.** In this chapter the Lord described the truth of Kṛṣṇaloka, the truth of Paravyoma, the truth of the Causal Waters and the truth of the *māyic* universes (*brahmāṇḍas*), and then told of one of Kṛṣṇa's pastimes in Dvārakā — how He humbled Brahmā's pride. After that the author, presenting them as Mahāprabhu's words, wrote several sweet verses that reveal the beauty of Kṛṣṇa's form. With this the explanation of *sambandha-tattva* comes to an end.

## 1

Bowing to Śrī Caitanya — the refuge of those who have no refuge, the generous giver of wealth to the destitute, their benefactor — I describe a drop of His sweetness and majesty.

## 7

The spiritual world is like a lotus. The highest (central) part of that lotus is Kṛṣṇaloka, its whorl (*karṇikā*); and around it on all four sides, like rows of petals, the countless Vaikuṇṭhas shine in Paravyoma.

## 9

“O Bhūman! O Bhagavān! O Supreme Soul! O Lord of *yoga*! Who can know where in these three worlds Your pastimes take place, what they are like, and when You, spreading out Yogamāyā, perform them?”

## 11

In time learned men have counted the specks of dust on the earth, the snowflakes in the sky, the stars and so on. But which of them can count Your qualities — You who have descended for the good of the world, You whose very form is endless qualities?

## 13

I, Brahmā, and the sages, your elder brothers — even we cannot know the limit of the Puruṣa, the Lord of *māyā*. Who else, then, will know it? The thousand-faced Ananta-deva sings His qualities on and on, yet even today He has not reached their end.

## 15

You are endless, and so those gods did not find Your limit. You Yourself do not find the limit of Your qualities. The universes in their coverings revolve along with time, like specks of dust in the sky. Therefore the *śrutis*, setting out in search of You, say of everything they point to, “This is not You.” In this way everything comes to its conclusion in You. Having made sure of this, they conclude that You alone are the support of all.

## 17–20

When Kṛṣṇa descended, Brahmā, wishing to test His glory, stole the calves and the cowherd boys. Then Kṛṣṇa, by His inconceivable power, manifested everything, both material and transcendental. By manifesting spiritual cows, cowherd boys and countless Vaikuṇṭha realities, He performed a transcendental creation. By creating countless universes, each with its own Brahmā, He performed a material creation. Whoever hears this wondrous account has the impurity of the mind washed away. The words “countless calves of Kṛṣṇa” mean that Kṛṣṇa's calves and cowherd boys appeared in countless forms.

## 27

“Let those who say, ‘We know the truth about Kṛṣṇa,’ know it; I do not wish to say much. O Lord, I say only this: Your glories lie beyond the reach of my mind, my body and my speech.”

## 29

In Vraja-maṇḍala there are twelve forests, which together cover eighty-four *krośas*. Among them the forest called Vṛndāvana extends for 16 *krośas*, from the border of the present town of Vṛndāvana to Nandagrāma and Vṛṣabhānupura.

## 30

*Śākhā-candra-nyāya*, the maxim of the moon on the branch: just as one points out the moon by showing a branch of a tree, so by showing one part of some truth one can give a little knowledge of the whole. This maxim is called *śākhā-candra-nyāya*.

## 33

He Himself is the Lord of Brahmā, Viṣṇu and Śiva, and so no one is equal to Him or above Him. By the wealth of His own kingdom (*svārājya-lakṣmī*) He has every desire fulfilled. The age-old guardians of the worlds come to bring Him tribute; praising His footstool, they bow the crowns that shine on their heads, and the crowns ring out.
