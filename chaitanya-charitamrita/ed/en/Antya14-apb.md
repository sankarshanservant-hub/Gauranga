# Amṛta-pravāha-bhāṣya on Antya-līlā 14

## 0

**Summary.** This chapter describes Mahāprabhu's incoherent words (*pralāpa*) in *adhirūḍha divyonmāda*, the divine madness that seized Him in separation from Kṛṣṇa. Once, as the Lord stood by the Garuḍa pillar looking at Jagannātha, an old Oriyā woman put her foot on His shoulder and began to look [at Jagannātha] with great eagerness. Govinda set about stopping her, but Mahāprabhu praised her eagerness and displayed great love. Absorbed in *prema*, the Lord had been seeing Kṛṣṇa; but as soon as this incident with the woman took place, His external consciousness returned, and He saw not Kṛṣṇa but Jagannātha, Baladeva and Subhadrā. Having lost the sight of Kṛṣṇa that He had received in a dream, the Lord was filled with *rāga*.[^0-1] Then He compared Himself to a yogī and described how, as a yogī, [His mind] dwells in Vṛndāvana. From time to time all ten well-known states (*daśā*) began to appear in the Lord. One night the Lord lay down to sleep in the inner room with the three doors shut. A little later Govinda and Svarūpa saw that all the doors were shut, yet the Lord was nowhere to be seen! Seeing this, Svarūpa and the other devotees [went searching and] found Mahāprabhu north of the Lion Gate: His joints had loosened, His body had become extremely long, and He was unconscious. The devotees chanted Kṛṣṇa's name, and when the Lord regained consciousness they took Him home again. At another time the Lord mistook Caṭaka-parvata for Govardhana and ran swiftly toward it, but on the way He was stunned; Mahāprabhu's hair stood on end like [the filaments of] a *kadamba* flower, and another state full of *mahābhāva* was seen in Him. Then the devotees calmed Him by chanting Hari's name and brought Him home.

## 1

In His bewilderment from separation from Kṛṣṇa, Śrī Gaurāṅgacandra did various things with His mind, intelligence and body; some of these I shall now tell.

## 9

“Briefly and at length”: Svarūpa Gosvāmī composed his diary (*kaḍacā*) briefly, and Raghunātha Dāsa Gosvāmī at length.

## 10

Svarūpa Gosvāmī wrote the *sūtras*, and Raghunātha their explanation (*vṛtti*). I am writing both these descriptions somewhat expanded, in the manner of a *pā̃ji-ṭīkā* (an introductory commentary). *Pā̃ji-ṭīkā*, or *pañji-ṭīkā*, means this: the author of such a commentary “cards” like cotton the arguments of the original source written by the author of the *vṛtti*, and presents them somewhat expanded.

## 16

When the *bhāva* called *mohana*, in the course of its development, reaches a certain [state] and comes to resemble bewilderment, there arises the divine madness called *vaicitrī* (the wondrous).[^16-1] *Udghūrṇā*, *citra-jalpa* and the rest are the many varieties of divine madness.

## 41

My self (*ātmā*) once obtained a treasure, Kṛṣṇa, but lost it again. In grief it left its house, the body, took up the *dharma* of a *kāpālika* yogī, and set off for Vṛndāvana with its disciples, the senses. A simile (*upamā-alaṅkāra*) should be seen here.

## 43–51

Mahāprabhu said: “Craving Kṛṣṇa's sweetness, My mind gave up Vedic *dharma*, became a yogī and turned beggar. Yogīs wear conch-shell earrings; so My mind, having become a yogī, wears the circle of Kṛṣṇa's pastimes as pure conch-shell earrings.[^43-51-1] The earrings of ordinary yogīs are made by conch-shell craftsmen, but the earrings of the Bhāgavatam — the circle of Kṛṣṇa's pastimes — for the yogī of My mind were fashioned by the craftsman Śrī Śuka himself, the son of Bādarāyaṇa. Whatever a yogī needs, the yogī of My mind has taken up. An ordinary yogī has a gourd *kamaṇḍalu* and a begging bowl (*sthālī*); the yogī of My mind has made a bowl of the gourd of thirst for Kṛṣṇa, slung over his shoulder the bag of hope, ‘I shall attain Kṛṣṇa,’ and put on the patched wrap of anxious thought, ‘How shall I attain Kṛṣṇa?’ Yogīs smear themselves with dust and ashes; the yogī of My mind is soiled with the ash of dust and answers every word with incoherent speech: ‘Hā hā Kṛṣṇa!’ Ordinary yogīs wear twelve bangles on their arms; on the arm of the yogī of My mind shine twelve bangles: the eight *sāttvika-vikāras*, agitation of mind, trembling, sighing, restlessness (*cāpalya*) and anxious thought.[^43-51-2] He has tied the bag of craving for Kṛṣṇa's sweetness to his head; and, receiving no alms, he has grown thin. The yogīs Vyāsa, Śuka and others described in the Bhāgavatam and other *śāstras* all the Vraja pastimes of Kṛṣṇa, whose nature is the pure Self; the yogī of My mind constantly recites the *tarjās* they composed. As Bāul yogīs take ten disciples each, so the yogī of My mind took the name ‘Mahā-bāul,’ made the ten senses his disciples, left the great wealth of sense enjoyment in his own home, My body, and went to Vṛndāvana. In Vṛndāvana the disciples beg alms from all the subjects, moving and nonmoving, and at the homes of the householders — the trees, creepers and others — and live on fruits, roots and leaves. The *gopīs* of Vraja ever taste the nectar of Śrī Kṛṣṇa's qualities, form, taste, fragrance, sound and touch; the five disciples, the five knowledge-acquiring senses, bring the remnants of their meal and sustain their lives by eating that *prasāda*. As ordinary yogīs sit in a corner to meditate, so the yogī of My mind, with his disciples, practices yoga in meditation on Kṛṣṇa in the corner of a bower empty of Kṛṣṇa.[^43-51-3] Kṛṣṇa is the embodiment of the pure Self; the yogī of My mind longs to see Him face to face, and, not finding Him, stays awake through the nights in meditation. Separated from Kṛṣṇa, the mind in great sorrow became this yogī, and in separation from Kṛṣṇa it reached the ten states. In them it became utterly distraught, saw that being a yogī was futile, and fled; My body was left empty.” This last figurative expression describes everything up to the state of *pralaya* (fainting).

## 53

Anxious thought, wakefulness, agitation, emaciation of the body, uncleanliness of the body, incoherent speech, sickness, madness, fainting and death — these are the ten states.

## 68

*Uttāna-nayana* — the eyes have rolled upward.

## 73

Once, in Kāśī Miśra's house, in separation from Kṛṣṇa, all the Lord's joints loosened, and His arms and legs grew longer. That Gaurāṅga who wept as He rolled on the ground, in a plaintive, faltering voice, utterly distraught, rises in my heart and maddens me.

## 109

*Kandarāte* — into a cave.

## 114

*Nipaṭṭa-bāhya haile* — when He came into “uncovered” external consciousness, that is, into full external consciousness.

## 120

Near Nīlācala Mahāprabhu saw Caṭaka-giri, a hill of sea sand, and with the words “I shall see Govardhana, the king of hills, in Vraja!” He ran off at great speed.[^120-1] That Gaurāṅgadeva, surrounded by the Vaiṣṇavas, rises in my heart and maddens me.

*Thus ends the fourteenth chapter of the “Amṛta-pravāha-bhāṣya.”*
