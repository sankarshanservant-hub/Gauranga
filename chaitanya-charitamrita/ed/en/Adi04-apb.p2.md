## 108

Śrī Kṛṣṇa sent Uddhava from Mathurā to the *gopīs* to bring them news of His well-being. On seeing Uddhava, Śrīmatī Rādhikā displayed a wondrous emotion.

## 111

“Later I shall describe this” — that is, in the Antya-līlā.

## 115

Up to five years of age is *kaumāra*, up to ten is *paugaṇḍa*, from eleven to sixteen is *kaiśora*, and after that comes *yauvana* (full youth). In *kaumāra* parental love (*vātsalya*) is manifest, in *paugaṇḍa* friendship (*sakhya*), and in *kaiśora* the *rasa* of amorous love (*śṛṅgāra*). *Kāma* is Kṛṣṇa, Manmatha (the god of love) Himself in person, who acts by His own will. In the age of *kaiśora* He performed the *rāsa* dance and other pastimes, and so He made fruitful the whole world and all three ages — *bālya*, *paugaṇḍa* and *kaiśora*.[^115-1]

## 116

Śrī Kṛṣṇa, free of everything inauspicious, enjoyed pastimes at night in the age of *kaiśora*, staying in the midst of women, and so paid special honor to that age. Śrī Kṛṣṇa, the Supreme Consciousness, abides in the circle of Rādhā, full of *mahābhāva*, and of the *gopīs*, full of *bhāva* — He is the *kūṭastha-tattva*, the unchanging Truth at the center.

## 117

This Kṛṣṇa boldly spoke of the art of love of the previous night and so made Śrī Rādhikā all but close Her eyes in shame. Then He drew playful designs — bees and the like — on Her breasts and displayed special skill before Her friends. By such pastimes of *rasa* in the grove Hari makes the age of *kaiśora* fruitful.

## 118

“O friend, if Hari and sweet-eyed Rādhikā had not appeared in Mathurā, the whole creation, and especially the creation of Kandarpa (the god of love), would have been fruitless.”

## 121

*Rasera nidāna*: the root cause of *rasa*. Variant reading: *rasera nidhāna*, the treasury of *rasa*.

## 125

“Dear friend Vṛndā, where are you coming from?” — “O Rādhā, I am coming from Kṛṣṇa's feet.” — “Where is Kṛṣṇa?” — “In Kuṇḍāraṇya (the forest by Rādhā-kuṇḍa).” — “What is He doing?” — “He is learning to dance.” — “Who is His dancing teacher?” — “Your image. It has made every tree and creeper in every direction into Your image and, like a *śailūṣī*, a conjurer, it dances along, drawing Him after itself. Beside it Śrī Kṛṣṇa dances.” This verse is made of questions and answers.

## 127–130

“I, Kṛṣṇa, am the shelter of mutually contradictory qualities. I am changeless yet act by My own will, all-pervading yet possessed of a beautiful form, impartial yet partial to My devotees, self-satisfied (*ātmārāma*) yet longing for My devotees' love, and so on. In the same way Rādhā's love is full of contradictory qualities: it is the highest *mahābhāva* yet always growing, full of the gravity of love yet without any deference, pure yet full of contrariness (*vāmya*) and the like.”

## 131

Rādhikā's *anurāga* is *vibhu*, that is, it has reached the furthest limit, yet it is always growing; it is supremely exalted, yet it knows no deference; it is pure and spotless, yet again and again it takes crooked ways. May such *anurāga* of Rādhikā for Kṛṣṇa be glorious!

## 132–135

One who loves is the *āśraya*, the abode, of love; one who is loved is its *viṣaya*, the object. In the science of *rasa* there are four kinds of ingredients: *vibhāva*, *anubhāva*, *sāttvika* and *vyabhicārī*. *Vibhāva* is of two kinds: *ālambana*, the support of the emotion, and *uddīpana*, that which awakens it. *Ālambana* in turn is of two kinds: *viṣaya* and *āśraya*. The *āśraya* of Rādhā's love is Rādhikā, and the one and only *viṣaya* of that love is Kṛṣṇa. “The happiness that I, Kṛṣṇa, relish is the happiness of the *viṣaya*. But the delight, or happiness, of the *āśraya* is millions of times greater than My happiness as *viṣaya*. Only Rādhikā relishes the happiness of the *āśraya*; I, as Kṛṣṇa, cannot relish it. Only if one day I become the *āśraya* of this love shall I experience that supreme bliss, the happiness of the *āśraya*. The longing to taste love as the *āśraya* tastes it — that is My desire.”

## 137–145

The second desire is this. Kṛṣṇa's sweetness is wondrous, endless and unlimited. Rādhikā alone relishes this sweetness, through the love that fills Her as the *āśraya*. “The mirror of Rādhikā's pure love is spotless, yet its clarity increases at every moment. My sweetness is unlimited and so cannot increase — yet it does increase, and before the clear mirror of Rādhikā's love it shines in ever newer forms. Therefore My sweetness and Rādhā's love rival each other, each striving to surpass the other, and neither wants to yield. Seeing My own sweetness in the mirror of Rādhikā's love and in other such mirrors, I long to relish it. Out of that longing My mind runs to accept the *svarūpa* of Rādhikā.”
