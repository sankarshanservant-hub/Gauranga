# Amṛta-pravāha-bhāṣya on Ādi-līlā 12

## 0

**Summary.** In this twelfth chapter the author describes the branches of Advaita Prabhu. Among them he calls the Vaiṣṇavas who follow the teaching (*mata*) of Śrī Acyutānanda *sāra-grāhī*, those who accept the essence, and all the others *asāra*, worthless. At the end he describes the branch of Śrī Gadādhara Paṇḍita Gosvāmī and so concludes the chapter. In between, two stories are told: one about Śrī Advaita's son Gopāla Miśra, the other about Advaita's servant Kamalākānta Viśvāsa. Early in Śrī Gopāla's life, during the cleaning of the Guṇḍicā temple, he fell unconscious in *prema*, and by Śrī Mahāprabhu's mercy he came back to himself. Kamalākānta Viśvāsa, the Ācārya's servant, begged three hundred rupees from King Pratāparudra to pay off Advaita Prabhu's debt. When Mahāprabhu learned of this, He punished that *bāuliyā* Viśvāsa, and at Advaitācārya's request He set him right.

## 1

The followers of Śrī Advaita Prabhu are of two kinds: *sāra-grāhī*, those who accept the essence, and *asāra-vāhī*, those who carry what is worthless. Rejecting those who carry the worthless, I bow down to all who carry the essence, the servants of Caitanya.

## 3

I bow down to all the associates of Advaita Prabhu, the branches of the second trunk of the wish-fulfilling tree named Śrī Caitanya.

## 8–12

At first all of Advaita Prabhu's followers held one teaching. Later, through an unhappy turn of fate, some of them came to hold separate views. Those who walked by the Ācārya's own teaching are pure Vaiṣṇavas. But those who, subject to fate, invented some view of their own, different from what the Ācārya taught, are worthless. We have no need of the names of worthless people. Still, to keep the Vaiṣṇavas who accept the essence apart from those who carry the worthless, we mention them, counting them all together — as one separates rice by winnowing away the empty husks (*pātnā*). “*Pātnā*” is the name for a worthless grain of rice with no kernel.

## 34

“*Daivata īśvara*”: in fact Īśvara, the Lord[^34-1].

## 36

“*Bāuliyā* Viśvāsa”: Kamalākānta Viśvāsa's conclusions are like the talk of a *bāul*, a madman; that is why he was called “*bāuliyā* Viśvāsa.”

## 40

While explaining the *Yoga-vāsiṣṭha*, Advaita Prabhu on some pretext declared liberation (*mukti*) superior to devotion.

## 49–53

Kamalākānta declared Advaita Ācārya to be “Īśvara” and on that basis begged money from the king. Mahāprabhu was greatly displeased by such an act. Even though the Ācārya is Īśvara, His human pastime as the teacher of the world is well known. To fall into debt and beg money from a king is shameless conduct for an ācārya. Greed for money must be avoided in every way; and if one displays that greed before a foreign king in order to pay off a debt, *dharma* suffers. A king is by nature a worldly man (*viṣayī*). Eating a worldly man's food corrupts the heart; and when the heart is corrupted, one does not remember Kṛṣṇa, and life is wasted. This is forbidden to everyone, and to teachers of religion (*dharmācāryas*) above all. To instruct people in the holy name is the duty of an ācārya. But those who instruct in the name for money are unfit for the title of “instructor in the name”; rather, they are offenders against the name. For such people this is forbidden all the more strictly. If an ācārya who instructs in the name accepts gifts, he brings public shame upon himself and gravely damages his religious reputation.
