## 45

> Subheading: One who follows *rāgānugā-bhakti* with transcendental faith attains perfection (*siddhi*), that is, becomes a *gosvāmī*, by hearing and chanting Kṛṣṇa’s pastimes —

## 45–46

Whoever, with faith and with a transcendental heart, describes or hears Kṛṣṇa’s transcendental sweet pastimes — the *rāsa* and the rest — as the *Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam* describes them, his worldly lust born of the mind (*manasija*) is completely destroyed. The speaker or hearer of Kṛṣṇa’s transcendental pastimes feels his own existence in the transcendental realm, and so the three *guṇas* of nature cannot overcome him. Towards matter he is wholly beyond the *guṇas* (*nirguṇa*): his mind is unshakable, and he is able to understand his own qualification (*nija-adhikāra*) for serving Kṛṣṇa.

Let no one, like the *prākṛta-sahajiyās*, think in this connection: “A *jīva* greedy for worldly lust need not gain knowledge of *sambandha*. Keeping a worldly mind, he may live in his own realm of enjoyment, give up *sādhana-bhakti*, and regard Kṛṣṇa’s transcendental play, His pastimes — the *rāsa* and the rest — as a model of worldly enjoyment like his own. If he simply hears and chants about them, his material lust will be destroyed.” It is precisely to forbid this that Mahāprabhu, by the word *viśvāsa* (faith), dispelled the worldly mentality of the *prākṛta-sahajiyās*. Śrī Śuka also said: “One who is not a controller (*anīśvara*) must never do this, even in his mind. Whoever does it out of folly perishes, as one who is not Rudra would perish by drinking the poison born of the ocean.” (*Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam* 10.33.30)

## 49

> Subheading: By constantly cultivating Kṛṣṇa’s pastimes, the *rāgānuga* devotee attains perfection in his own true nature (*svarūpa-siddhi*) and a body of spirit and bliss —

## 49–50

Whoever hears of and chants Śrī Kṛṣṇa’s transcendental play — the *rāsa* and the rest — and, following Śrī Rūpa’s transcendental mood, constantly serves Kṛṣṇa in his mind, absorbed in pure, unfeigned *rāga*, attains a matchless fruit that worldly language cannot describe. He is an eternally perfect associate (*nitya-siddha pārṣada*). Or else his body is almost perfect (*siddha-prāya*)[^49-1]: though it is visible to people’s eyes, in it, as he attains *svarūpa-siddhi*, dwell the feelings devoted to Kṛṣṇa’s service, and so all his activities are transcendental. Until, by Kṛṣṇa’s will, he attains *vastu-siddhi*, his body is almost perfect and transcendental.

## 80

> Subheading: What Rāya’s great qualities teach: outward conduct according to *varṇāśrama* is no sign of devotion to Kṛṣṇa or of being a guru —

In the eyes of worldly people Śrī Rāmānanda Prabhu appears to be a householder on the path of *pravṛtti* (worldly engagement), not a *brahmacārī* with controlled senses, a *vānaprastha* or a *bhikṣu*. Worldly householders, ruled by their senses, take up the *dharma* of those attached to home (*gṛhavrata*). But a transcendental Vaiṣṇava living at home does not, like a non-Vaiṣṇava householder, become one whose senses are uncontrolled (*adānta-go*), and he never falls under the power of the six enemies (*ṣaḍ-varga*). In Śrī Rāmānanda Prabhu’s pastimes in the householder *āśrama*, worldly people, with their enjoying vision, see him as a *viṣayī*, one absorbed in worldly objects. But his transcendental mind of pure goodness worships at every moment a single object, the transcendental pastimes of Śrī Kṛṣṇa. He is therefore a *kṛṣṇa-viṣayī*, one whose object is Kṛṣṇa, and not an impersonalist logician hostile to Bhagavān’s spiritual pastimes. He is able to make *sannyāsīs* who have renounced worldly objects and stand beyond the *guṇas* (*nirguṇa*) give up petty material objects that hold no realisation of Kṛṣṇa, and to engage them in constantly cultivating Kṛṣṇa as their only object.

## 84

> Subheading: Worldly *varṇāśrama*, learning and the like are no sign of one who can speak the true *dharma* —

## 83–84

**Paṇḍita**: a *brāhmaṇa* entitled to study the Vedas; **sannyāsī**: the highest of the four *āśramas* of a *brāhmaṇa*. According to worldly ideas, only *brāhmaṇas* by seed have the right to Sāvitrī initiation. Birth through Sāvitrī initiation gives the right to study the Vedas, and having gained *brāhmaṇa* birth through Sāvitrī and passed through three *āśramas*, one rises to the high position of a *sannyāsī*. The *brāhmaṇa* is the guru of the three *varṇas*, and the *sannyāsī* is the guru of the *brāhmaṇas* in the other three *āśramas*. Wishing to humble their worldly pride, born of intoxication with their position, the Lord had Śrī Rāmānanda Rāya Prabhu instruct a *brāhmaṇa* by seed named Pradyumna Miśra, although worldly vision knew Rāmānanda as a *śūdra*, the lowest *varṇa*, and as a householder, the lowest *āśrama*. And Mahāprabhu Himself, who had accepted *sannyāsa*, accepted the *dharma* that Śrī Rāmānanda preached.

Śrī Gaurahari wished to reveal to people the essential conclusion of the scriptures about the *āśramas*. So He spread the force of His natural majesty (*aiśvarya*) against the mistaken notions of those who think themselves worldly scholars and renunciants. Ordinary ignorant people do not know the purport of the scriptures; but seeing the pure, righteous conduct and the qualities of the servants who had taken shelter of Gaurasundara, they could understand the true purport of the scriptures about *varṇa* and *āśrama*. A transcendental Vaiṣṇava wholly devoted to Hari, in whatever family he has appeared and in whatever *āśrama* he lives, can be the highest *ācārya* of the true *dharma* — a guru who eternally shows mercy to worldly people of the four *varṇas* and *āśramas*. The scriptures say this again and again. Bhagavān Gaurahari indisputably preached to people the hidden and true aim of the scriptures, and so revealed His natural majesty (*aiśvarya-svabhāva*).

> Subheading: The only cause of the magnanimous Lord’s anger is a contradiction of the message of *bhakti-siddhānta* —

***Rasābhāsa***: “Rasas that lack [any of] the characteristics of *rasa* set out earlier are called *rasābhāsas* by the knowers of *rasa*. They are of three kinds — *uparasas*, *anurasas* and *aparasas* — and are called, in that order, the highest, the middling and the lowest. The twelve *rasas* beginning with *śānta* become *uparasas* when the *sthāyi-bhāva*, the *vibhāvas*, the *anubhāvas* and the rest in them are distorted. The seven *rasas* beginning with *hāsya*, and *śānta* as well, are considered *anurasas* when their *vibhāvas* — devotees and the rest — have no relation to Kṛṣṇa. And if Kṛṣṇa and His opponents become the object (*viṣaya*) and the abode (*āśraya*) of *hāsya* and the other *rasas*, the wise call them *aparasas*. All *bhāvas* have their semblances, and some of them are *rasābhāsas*; yet the knowers of *rasa* call all of them *rasas*, because they are relished.” (*Bhakti-rasāmṛta-sindhu*, Uttara-vibhāga, 9th Laharī)

When something at first appears to be *rasa* but, judged by the characteristics of *rasa* described earlier, is incomplete, the *rasikas* call it *rasābhāsa*. *Rasābhāsa* is divided into *uparasa*, *anurasa* and *aparasa*, which are called, respectively, the highest, the middling and the lowest. The twelve *rasas* beginning with *śānta*, in which the *sthāyi-bhāva*, *vibhāva*, *anubhāva* and the rest are distorted, are called *uparasa*. The seven *rasas* beginning with *hāsya*, produced by *vibhāvas* — devotees and the rest — that have no relation to Kṛṣṇa, together with dry *śānta-rasa*, are called *anurasa*. If Kṛṣṇa and His opponents, the *asuras*, filled with mutually hostile feelings, become the object and the abode of *hāsya* and the other *rasas*, the scholars who know *rasa-tattva* call this *aparasa*. Some call the *bhāvas* *tad-ābhāsa* (their semblance) or *rasābhāsa*; but those expert in *rasa-tattva* call all of these *rasa*, because they are sweet to taste and give joy. Śrī Viśvanātha Cakravartī says: “If two mutually hostile [*rasas*] are joined, that is *rasābhāsa*.” That is, when two opposed *rasas* are joined, *rasābhāsa* arises.

***Siddhānta-virodha*** — contradiction of the *siddhānta* of the path of *bhakti*; contradiction of *tattva*.

> Subheading: The *nāndī* verse —

**kanaka-ruciḥ** (whose lustre [*ruci*] is like the lustre of gold [*kanaka*]) **yaḥ** (Gaura) **iha** (here, in Puruṣottama-kṣetra) **vikaca-kamala-netre** (in Him whose eyes are like blossoming lotuses) **śrī-jagannātha-saṁjñe** (in Him who is called — whose name is — “Śrī Jagannātha”) **ātmani** (in the body) **ātmatām** (the status of the owner of the body) **prapannaḥ** (having attained) **prakṛti-jaḍam** (inert by nature — the Deity of Śrī Jagannātha, because the worshipable Deity is seen as wood — so in the poet’s sense) **aśeṣaṁ** **cetayan** **āvirāsīt** (appeared), **saḥ** (that Śrī Kṛṣṇa Caitanyadeva) **tava** **bhavyam** (welfare) **diśatu** (may He bestow).

(In Sarasvatī’s sense: **yaḥ** (that) Śrī Kṛṣṇa **śrī-jagannātha-saṁjñe** (in Him who is called Śrī Jagannātha — in the Master of *māyā*, the Dāru-brahma, the Supreme Soul full of the six opulences) **kanaka-rucinā** (in the golden form of Gaura) **ātmatām** (complete nondifference from Him, that is, the form of Jagannātha) **prapannaḥ** (attained); “**saḥ**” and the rest are clear.)

> Subheading: Everyone rejoiced, yet Śrī Svarūpa — the Vaiṣṇava *ācārya*, the teacher of the world — grows angry and shows mercy: with the sword of scriptural *siddhānta* he cuts down the false doctrine —

## 117

> Subheading: The cause of the anger: (1) regarding Viṣṇu as a *jīva* leads to hell —

## 118

Someone may see in the Deity of Jagannātha only a wooden image (*pratimā*) and think Him perishable, made of material substances, a mere inert object. By the word of the *Padma Purāṇa* — “One who regards the worshipable Viṣṇu as stone … is a dweller in hell” — whoever thinks this way commits an offence. For the Deity (*arcā-vigraha*) is completely nondifferent from Śrī Kṛṣṇa, whose form is eternity, knowledge and bliss, and the devotee of Bhagavān, with eyes of *bhakti* lit by the bliss of *prema*, sees Him directly as the complete form of eternity, knowledge and bliss.

## 119

From the words of the *śruti*, “As sparks fly up from a fire …,”[^119-1] we learn that the *jīva* is like a spark of the fire that is the great Viṣṇu; that is, the *jīva* is a particle of spirit (*cit-kaṇa*). A *jīva* under the sway of *māyā* is capable of being bound by matter. But Śrī Caitanyadeva is Parameśvara, whose form is eternity, knowledge and bliss, the cause of all causes; and His having taken a human form does not mean that He is a tiny *jīva* subject to matter. He is the Master of *māyā*, full of the six opulences, Svayaṁ Bhagavān, the son of Yaśodā. (*Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam* 1.11.39): “This is the lordship of the Lord: though He abides within *prakṛti*, He is not bound by its *guṇas*, though they are ever about Him — just as the intelligence that has taken shelter of Him is not bound by them.”

## 120

> Subheading: The audacity of one who, relying on sense-born knowledge and logic and ignorant of *bhakti-siddhānta*, sets out to describe Kṛṣṇa —

***Dui-ṭhāñi*** (in two places) — Śrī Jagannāthadeva and Śrī Caitanyadeva. The poet took Them both to belong to the material world, as matter and as a *jīva*: he supposed that the particle of spirit residing in the material body of the One had entered the material body of the other. Hence the offence lies in two places. ***Atattva-jña*** — one who has not grasped *tattva*, that is, a Māyāvādī *ahaṅgrahopāsaka* (who worships himself as God), a *karmī* who enjoys the fruits of his work, or a self-willed person devoted to the senses, who cannot tell the real from the unreal. ***Tattva varṇe*** — describes *tattva*, that is, what concerns Bhagavān.

## 121

> Subheading: (2) The blunder (*pramāda*) is an offence: positing in Īśvara a difference between the body and the embodied one —

If one regards Īśvara as one does a bound *jīva* and holds His body and His embodied self (*dehī*) to be different things, that is an “offence.” In the material world a bound *jīva* has a bodily existence fashioned by *guṇa-māyā*, and a *jīva*’s self-awareness fashioned by *jīva-māyā*, the marginal potency (*taṭastha-śakti*). The difference between Īśvara and the bound *jīva* is this. Īśvara bestows the fruits of *karma* and rules over them; that is, He is the cause of all causes, the Master of *māyā*, the Lord (*prabhu*) and the all-pervading truth (*vibhu-tattva*). The *jīva*, in the bound state, enjoys the fruits of *karma* and is subject to them, and even in the liberated state, in his own true nature, he is engaged in serving Īśvara. In other words, Īśvara is never under the control of *māyā*, whereas the *jīva* is liable to fall under it. Īśvara is immeasurable, undivided consciousness; the *jīva* is measurable, fragmentary consciousness.

The perishable, transient body of a bound *jīva* is *māyic*, that is, material. The transcendental body of a liberated, pure *jīva* is eternal; and Īśvara, who is beyond *māyā*, is also an eternal form endowed with features (*saviśeṣa-vigraha*). Though His eternal form appears in the world by the power of His own inconceivable potency, it is never *māyic* or material and never bears the qualities of this world. (*Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam* 1.11.39): “This is the lordship of the Lord: though He abides within *prakṛti*, He is not bound by its *guṇas*, though they are ever about Him — just as the intelligence that has taken shelter of Him is not bound by them.” To try to separate the body from the embodied one on the pretext of making the eternal form “featureless” (*nirviśeṣa*) is a work of grave offence.

## 122

> Subheading: The name, form and essential nature of Viṣṇu, the nondual Knowledge, are one —

The nondual Knowledge (*advaya-jñāna*) is Kṛṣṇa, the son of the king of Vraja. In determining the nature of the Truth, the verse *vadanti tat tattva-vidaḥ*[^122-1] refutes the idea of duality in the one Parameśvara, who has no second. He is the nondual Knowledge; therefore His name, form, qualities and pastimes are not distinct from one another as the things of the material world are — they must be known as absolutely “nondifferent.” It is precisely by seeing in Īśvara a difference between body and embodied self that one mistakes Him for a “bound *jīva*,” for a bound *jīva* lacks the perception of the nondual Knowledge.

## 123

> Subheading: Evidence —

**īśvare** (in the Supreme Soul, in Bhagavān, the Truth endowed with features) **ayaṁ** **deha-dehi-vibhāgaḥ** (a *māyā*-made difference of this kind: the name is one thing and its bearer another; the form one thing and the possessor of the form another; the quality one thing and its possessor another; the pastime one thing and its performer another — in Viṣṇu, the nondual Knowledge, made of pure *sattva*, in Goloka, in Paravyoma, in Devī-dhāma or within the fourteen worlds) **kvacit** (anywhere) **na vidyate** (does not exist).
