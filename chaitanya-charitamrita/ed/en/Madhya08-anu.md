## 3

> Subheading: *Darśana* of Jiyaḍa-Nṛsiṁha; dancing, prayers and songs —

**Jiyaḍa-Nṛsiṁha-kṣetra** is a place called “Simhachalam” on the B. N. R. line, five miles due north of Vizagapatam, or Viśākhāpattana. There is also a railway station called “Siṁhācala.” The temple of Śrī Nṛsiṁhadeva stands high on the hill. Of all the temples in Vizagapatam it is the most famous and the richest, and it remains the finest example of architecture. A stone slab records that a pious queen of King Goṅkā III covered the Deity with gold (*Vizagapatam Gazetteer*). Near the temple live the servants of Śrī Nṛsiṁha and other residents. Now on the hilltop, next to the temple, there are many places where pilgrims can stay and many houses. The *vijaya-mūrti* (the festival Deity) resides in a lighted place, and the original Deity of Nṛsiṁha within. Several Śrī Vaiṣṇavas of Rāmānuja's line serve the Deity under the patronage of the king of Vizianagaram.

## 35–36

Out of natural humility Śrīla Rāya Rāmānanda called himself by low names such as “*viṣayī*” and “the lowest of *śūdras*.” Although he was not born in a family of brāhmaṇas by birth (*śaukra-vipras*), in the manifest pastimes he was an *akiñcana*, a pure *bhāgavata*, a *paramahaṁsa*. Therefore to call him a transcendental brāhmaṇa by initiation (*daikṣa-brāhmaṇa*) of the Vedic Ekāyana branch expresses only a small part of his greatness. Even a man born into a great family, initiated into every sacrifice and versed in a thousand branches of the Vedas, will surely go to hell if he sees caste (*śūdra*) in Rāmānanda and considers him equal to others born in *śūdra* families: “…he who sees in him only a common caste certainly goes to hell.” (*Padma Purāṇa*) The eternal good of a *jīva* who seeks the highest goal lies only in the self-conception of being his servant.

## 57

> Subheading: The conversation of the Lord and Rāmānanda. The Lord asks about the goal and the means (*sādhya* and *sādhana*). Rāya's answer: (a) the stages of *sādhana* (*abhidheya*): (1) first, keeping *svadharma* in the form of *daiva-varṇāśrama*, the beginning of a theistic moral life, or a life of *dharma* —

Śrī Rāmānujapāda writes in the *Vedārtha-saṅgraha*: “This special knowledge, *para-bhakti*, is produced only by *bhakti-yoga* assisted by action preceded by knowledge, which, as said above, grows day by day. So says the venerable Parāśara: ‘*Varṇāśrama*…’ And the Supreme Person, Parabrahman Himself, who descended to the earth to deliver the whole world, said: ‘Hear how one devoted to his own work attains perfection. By worshiping with his own work Him from whom all beings have come forth and by whom all this is pervaded, a man attains perfection.’ (*Bhagavad-gītā* 18.45–46) Thus [the Lord] is attained only by *bhakti* matured in the order described. This path is shown by the multitude of *śrutis* whose meaning is clearly set forth in the ancient explanations of the Vedas and Vedānta accepted by irreproachable sages such as the venerable Bodhāyana, Ṭaṅka, Dramiḍa, Guhadeva, Kapardī and Bhāruci.”

*Bhakti* is a special knowledge: it is dear beyond measure, it alone is needed, and it produces indifference to everything else. Only a soul endowed with such *bhakti* chooses Bhagavān, and only to devotees is He attainable. This special knowledge, the highest *bhakti*, is produced only by the *bhakti-yoga* described above, which grows without cease and is assisted by action preceded by knowledge. This is what the venerable Parāśara said in the verse *varṇāśramācāravatā*… And the Supreme Person, Parabrahman Himself, who descended to the earth to deliver the whole world, said: “Hear how a man engaged in his own work will attain perfection: by especially worshiping with his own work that Bhagavān from whom all living beings have come forth and by whom this world is spread out, a man will attain perfection.” This path to perfection is assisted by *karma*, and on it [the Lord] is attained only by *bhakti* matured in due order. The venerable Bodhāyana, Ṭaṅka, Dramiḍa, Guhadeva, Kapardī, Bhāruci and other sages approve this irreproachable path; it is the path shown by the ancient explanations of the Vedas and Vedānta and by the *śrutis* whose meaning is beautifully (clearly) set forth.

The *ācāryas* of Rāmānuja's *sampradāya* say: “The means of attaining Brahman is only *para-bhakti*, constant meditation (*anudhyāna*) that has become the clearest direct vision, dear without limit and beyond measure. It is attained by steady *bhakti* assisted by one's own *karma* preceded by knowledge of the truth gained from scripture. As the verse *varṇāśramācāravatā*… shows, [this *bhakti*] is not restricted to *sannyāsa*, nor to any one *varṇa*: it is bound to each person's own *varṇa* and *āśrama*. True knowledge is only the knowledge of which *karma* is a part, not *naiṣkarmya*, nor an equal combination of knowledge and *karma*.”

***Sādhya*** — that by which perfection (*siddhi*) is attained, that which is gained by *sādhana*: see *Bhāg.* 1.2.13.

**varṇāśramācāravatā** (by one devoted to the conduct of the *varṇas*, brāhmaṇa, kṣatriya, vaiśya and śūdra, and intent on keeping the conduct of the *āśramas*, *brahmacārī*, *gṛhastha*, *vānaprastha* and *bhikṣu*, that is, one who keeps the *dharma* of his own *varṇa* and *āśrama*) **puruṣeṇa** **paraḥ** **pumān** (the Supreme Person, Viṣṇu) **ārādhyate**; **tat** (His, Viṣṇu's) **anyat** (any other, one that destroys *varṇāśrama-dharma*) **panthāḥ** (path) **toṣa-kāraṇam** (for His pleasure) **na** **bhavati**.

## 59

> Subheading: (2) Devotion mixed with *karma*, offering one's actions to Bhagavān, is not pure devotion —

Setting out to determine the *sādhya*, that is, the devotion that is to be practiced, Śrī Rāmānanda first took the standpoint of a *sādhaka* living within the universe. Rejecting other desires (*anyābhilāṣitā*), he took the side of the moralists (*nīti-vādīs*) and cited the evidence for the *sādhya* that Viṣṇu is pleased when one simply follows *varṇa-dharma* and *āśrama-dharma*. But one who determines the goal in this way grasps his relationship [with the Lord] through his “I” (*asmitā*), and that “I” lies within the universe. Hence the function of such *asmitā* also remains within the universe, and so it is external. Śrī Bhagavān Gaurahari called the external experience of a person situated outside His abode, Vaikuṇṭha or Goloka, an “external *sādhya*,” rejected it, and told him to go further. The evidence about the *sādhya* cited above did not point to the independence of Viṣṇu's personal nature (*viśeṣatva*). Therefore *sādhakas* of this level on the path of *karma* may aim at the worship of Viṣṇu of both kinds, “*nirviśeṣa*” and “*saviśeṣa*.” Understanding this, [Rāmānanda] gave up the leaning toward featureless truth and cited the evidence showing that the personal nature (*saviśeṣatva*) [of the Lord] alone is the purport of the aim of *karma*.

## 61

> Subheading: (3) Merely giving up the enjoyment of results, or *naiṣkarmya*, is not pure devotion; the first step of pure devotion is to give up *svadharma*, surrender, and serve Śrī Hari —

The word *mad-arpaṇa* (“offered to Me”) rejects the material featureless and means offering to Kṛṣṇa alone, the independent, personal (*saviśeṣa*) Truth. Yet the *sādhaka* still grasps himself through his “I” (*asmitā*), which lies within the universe, and the activity he practices also remains within the universe. Therefore this too is external. That is, the *jīva* engaged in action, with external perception, receives only the instruction to give his external actions to the independent Reality that is beyond all action. Then Rāmānanda purified this understanding: he took up the mood to which the understanding of a *jīva* risen above *karma* must be raised, and cited the evidence that the goal is attained by giving up *svadharma*.

## 63

Śrī Bhagavān's confidential instruction to Arjuna:

**sarva-dharmān** (the *dharmas* of the *varṇas* within the universe, brāhmaṇa, kṣatriya, vaiśya, śūdra and so on, and the *dharmas* of the *āśramas* within the universe, *brahmacārī*, *gṛhastha*, *vānaprastha*, the fourth *āśrama* and so on) **parityajya** (casting far away) **ekam** (beyond them, nondual Knowledge, with an unwavering mind) **mām** (Bhagavān Kṛṣṇa, the personal Truth) **śaraṇaṁ** **vraja** (go); **ahaṁ** **tvāṁ** **sarva-pāpebhyaḥ** (from the *adharma* within the universe that arises from abandoning the material, regular Vedic rites) **mokṣayiṣyāmi** (I shall deliver); **mā** **śucaḥ** (do not grieve over transient *dharmas*).

Śrī Raghunātha dāsa Gosvāmī Prabhu says in his own *Manaḥ-śikṣā*: “Do not perform either the *dharma* or the *adharma* that the *śrutis* describe; here in Vraja render abundant service to Rādhā and Kṛṣṇa…”[^63-1] “When Bhagavān, on whom one meditates within, shows someone His mercy, that person gives up a mind fixed on worldly and Vedic ways.” (*Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam* 4.29.46) In this connection see *Bhāg.* 1.5.17.

## 64

> Subheading: (4) Devotion mixed with knowledge is not pure devotion —

In the realization of a *jīva* who has risen above *karma*, his “I” (*asmitā*) abides in the river Virajā beyond the universe. There the three *guṇas* do not predominate: there is only equilibrium, the unmanifest state. Between Vaikuṇṭha, manifested by the internal potency, and the universe, manifested by the external potency, lie Brahmaloka and the river Virajā. These two places are the shelter of the realization of *jīvas* who are detached from matter and know only material featurelessness. They are not Vaikuṇṭha and lie outside it, and so they are external. A *sādhaka* within the universe who has given up all *dharmas* has no experience of Vaikuṇṭha or Goloka. Therefore such an inclination toward the goal, though it has given up material enjoyment, establishes only a nonspiritual featurelessness (*acit-nirviśeṣatva*), and so it too is external. Rāmānanda then understood this mood to be an external conception of the goal and cited the evidence that the goal higher than it is devotion mixed with knowledge.

## 65

> Subheading: Only a brāhmaṇa who has gained knowledge of his relationship with the Lord (*sambandha-jñāna*) becomes a Vaiṣṇava by serving Kṛṣṇa —

The words of Śrī Bhagavān to Arjuna:

**brahma-bhūtaḥ** (freed from an awareness confined to the universe and intent on featureless experience) **prasannātmā** (free from the quality of want) **na** **śocati** (does not grieve over material lack), **na** **kāṅkṣati** (does not long for material enjoyment), **sarveṣu** **bhūteṣu** (knowing that all beings are connected with service to Me) **samaḥ** **san**, **parām** (supreme, pure) **mad-bhaktiṁ** **labhate**.

## 66

> Subheading: (5) Only devotion free from knowledge deserves the name of pure devotion —

Even in this state the *asmitā* and its function do not abide in pure Vaikuṇṭha and are not directed toward Vaikuṇṭha, and so this too is external. As long as there is only freedom from subjection to matter, or only an inclination toward pure experience beyond matter, there is no independent and personal realization of the real, true Object. Hence one's own experience and one's own mental disposition face outward. In truth, this is not the goal of the pure *jīva* either. When one imagines featurelessness, all the particulars of *sac-cid-ānanda* lie dormant. And before that, all the reasonings born of imagination have as their purport only meditation on the featureless. Therefore such an imagined state of liberation, intent on the featureless and devoid of the inclination to serve Bhagavān, is also external.

## 67

> Subheading: Only one who has completely surrendered to Kṛṣṇa is a pure devotee, who brings Kṛṣṇa under his control —

**jñāne** (for the sake of knowledge) **prayāsam** (the troubles and so on born of effort) **udapāsya** (casting far away), **san-mukharitām** (sung by saints, the *mahā-bhāgavatas*, naturally manifest) **śruti-gatām** (reaching the ears) **bhavadīya-vārtām** (narrations about You, about Your name, form, qualities and pastimes) **ye** **sthāne** **sthitāḥ** (remaining in their own place, on the path of the *sādhus*) **tanu-vāṅ-manobhiḥ** (with body, mind and words) **namantaḥ** (surrendering themselves in every way) **eva** **jīvanti**, **ajita** (O Unconquerable One: beyond the senses, not to be overcome by nondevotees, dependent on no one, immeasurable), **tri-lokyām** **taiḥ** (by Your devotees alone) **prāyaśaḥ** (You) **jitaḥ** (conquered) **api** **asi**.

## 68

> Subheading: (b) The perfection of *sādhana*: *prema-bhakti* (*bhāva*, the sprout of *prema*) —

When the verse *jñāne prayāsam*… was spoken in determining the goal, Mahāprabhu accepted that inclination as the inclination toward the goal (*sādhya-vṛtti*). This is called *sādhana-bhakti*. Then the Lord told him to go further. After *sādhana-bhakti* comes *bhāva-bhakti*, the sprout of *prema-bhakti*, and in *śānta-rasa* the quality of equanimity (*nairapekṣya*) predominates. Therefore Rāmānanda Rāya named as the goal *prema-bhakti* endowed with the four *rasas*. “*Sādhana-bhakti*” means *śraddhā*, *sādhu-saṅga*, *bhajana-anuṣṭhāna*, *anartha-nivṛtti*, *niṣṭhā*, *ruci* and *āsakti*.

## 71

> Subheading: (1) “*Dāsya-prema*” is excellent —

In the two verses quoted above *prema-bhakti* was determined as “the goal” in general. Therefore Śrī Mahāprabhu told Rāmānanda to go further and explain that “goal” in particular, step by step. Then [Rāmānanda] establishes “*dāsya-prema-bhakti*” as “the goal.”

## 72

> Subheading: Only Kṛṣṇa's servant is heir to all His opulence —

Durvāsā saw with a perverted vision born of the senses, was possessed by the evil desire to oppose Vaiṣṇavas, and was proud of being a brāhmaṇa. When Sudarśana, the Vaiṣṇava's weapon, began to torment him, it was stopped by the prayer of the *mahā-bhāgavata* Ambarīṣa. Seeing this, Durvāsā was freed from caste-consciousness and praised the pure devotee and Bhagavān thus:

**yan-nāma-śruti-mātreṇa** (simply by hearing the name of that Bhagavān) **pumān** (the *jīva*) **nirmalaḥ** (pure) **bhavati**, **tasya** **tīrtha-padaḥ** (of that Bhagavān at whose feet are the holy places) **dāsānām** (for the servants) **kiṁ** **vā** **avaśiṣyate**? (That is, nothing at all.)

## 75

> Subheading: The glory of the Vraja cowherd boys' friendship with Kṛṣṇa —

Kavirāja Gosvāmī introduced these words in connection with the five perfected *bhāvas* (*siddha-bhāvas*). The *rasikas* of these five *rasas* serve in five *bhāvas*: *śānta*, *dāsya*, *sakhya*, *vātsalya* and *madhura*. After the *anarthas* have ceased, whichever of these perfected *bhāvas* awakens in someone according to the nature of his eternally perfect *svarūpa* is indeed the highest for one qualified for that *rasa*. For the object of them all is Śrī Kṛṣṇa, not material demigods and others who are not Śrī Kṛṣṇa. Yet when one judges from outside, that is, impartially, a difference of degree is felt in the relishing of *rasa* in these five *bhāvas*. Thus in *dāsya-rasa* both *śānta-rasa* and *dāsya-rasa* are present at once, and so it is higher than *śānta-rasa*. In *sakhya-rasa* *śānta* and *dāsya* are present, and so it is higher still than *śānta* and *dāsya*. *Vātsalya-rasa* includes *śānta*, *dāsya* and *sakhya*, and so it holds more wonder than those three earlier *rasas*. And in *madhura-rasa* all four earlier *rasas* are present, and so its wonder and sweetness are the highest of all. The Vaiṣṇava *mahājanas*, expert in *bhakti-siddhānta*, have examined in this graded order the subtlest truths of realizing the *svarūpa*. Unfortunately, people bewildered by the divine *māyā* and expert in false *siddhānta* cannot grasp any of these *siddhāntas* and so find fault with pure Vaiṣṇava *siddhānta*. This only shows the misfortune of all these childish talkers.

## 90

> Subheading: The deeper a devotee's worship, the more devotion to Kṛṣṇa he attains —

Worldly people reason like this: “In whatever mood one worships, one attains Bhagavān. By whatever means one worships Bhagavān, by *karma*, knowledge, *yoga* or austerity, it makes no difference. Just as different roads lead to one place, so different paths lead to Bhagavān. By whatever name one calls Bhagavān, Kālī, Durgā, Śiva, Gaṇeśa, Rāma, Hari or Brahman, it is all the same. Or, just as one man has different names and answers to any of them, so it is with Bhagavān.”

All these words please people of a childish mental *dharma*, but those who take the essence (*sāragrāhīs*) consider them full of false conclusions. When someone desires heaven and the like and worships an administrative demigod, the *māyā-śakti*, which is turned away from Bhagavān, gives him as his fruit faith in those administrative demigods. So she deprives him of the fruit of the ultimate good and makes him wander on the wheel of *karma*, along the chain of births and deaths, now in heaven, now in the world of mortals. But to those who pray for eternal service to Bhagavān, Bhagavān gives His eternal service. It is therefore true that however one worships, one gets a fruit according to one's worship. But it must be specially noted that not all fruits are equal. The fruit of one who desires *dharma*, *artha*, *kāma* and *mokṣa* and the fruit of one who prays for eternal, causeless service to Kṛṣṇa are not the same. The fruit of *dharma*, *artha* and the rest is a perishable heaven and the like; the fruit of *mokṣa* in the form of *sāyujya* and the like is self-annihilation and the like; the highest fruit of causeless service to Hari is ever-fresh service to Hari, that is, love for Bhagavān. Therefore between the fruits of one who desires *dharma*, *artha* and *kāma*, one who desires featureless liberation, and one devoted to Hari's service, the difference is as between heaven and the netherworld. Of this there is no doubt.

Mahāmāyā, the mother of the world, who presides over the material world, and the other administrative demigods are the external potency of Śrī Bhagavān and a distorted display of His majesty (*virūpa-vaibhava*). By Bhagavān's order they manage the various parts of the work of creation. The creation of the world is no concern of Bhagavān's internal potency. All that takes place in the spiritual abode is the work of the internal potency, and it is accomplished by *yogamāyā*. *Yogamāyā* is Bhagavān's internal potency, or *cit-śakti*. Those who pray for service to Bhagavān in the spiritual abode receive the sincere mercy of *yogamāyā*, which turns them toward the service of Kṛṣṇa. But those who in the material universe desire other things, *dharma*, *artha*, *kāma* and the like, for the sake of worldly prosperity (*abhyudaya*), or who wish for a featureless destiny turned away from Bhagavān's service, worship Mahāmāyā or demigods such as Rudra. That is why we see the damsels of Vraja worshiping the *cit-śakti*, *yogamāyā*, to obtain the son of the cowherd Nanda as their husband, that is, to obtain eternal service to Him in the spiritual abode. And in the *Saptaśatī* we see that King Suratha and a vaiśya named Samādhi considered themselves needy *jīvas* within *varṇāśrama* and so devoted themselves to the worship of Mahāmāyā, who presides over matter. Therefore wherever a “doctrine of synthesis” (*samanvaya-vāda*) is preached that makes “*yogamāyā*” and “material *māyā*” one, like trying to sell “puffed rice and rock candy” at the same price, one must recognize ignorance, foolishness, and a failure to realize Bhagavān's true nature.

Secondly, in this world one sees “a blind boy named Padmalocana” (“lotus-eyed”), but with Bhagavān it is not so. There is no difference between Bhagavān's name and Bhagavān Himself: no name of Bhagavān is meaningless or different from His real being. Śrī Bhagavān has many names: Paramātmā, Brahman, the Creator, Nārāyaṇa, Rukmiṇī-ramaṇa, Gopīnātha, Kṛṣṇa and others. But one who calls Bhagavān “the Creator” will not be able to relish the *rasa* of Nārāyaṇa. For names such as “the Creator” were given by sense-born knowledge, the knowledge of the *jīvas* of this world who are turned away from Viṣṇu. The word “Creator” does not give a realization of Bhagavān's full being: creation is not the work of His *svarūpa-śakti*; it points only to His outward-turned potency. The word “Brahman” does not reveal Bhagavān's six kinds of majesty either: His featureless aspect is called “Brahman,” and so this name does not point to His complete form of eternity, knowledge and bliss (*sac-cid-ānanda-vigraha*). The word “Paramātmā” does not give full knowledge of Bhagavān either: “Paramātmā” is the name of only a partial manifestation of Him, as the indwelling Lord in the heart of each individual *jīva*. Again, even one who worships Nārāyaṇa cannot realize Kṛṣṇa's sweetness (*mādhurya*). And a devotee of Kṛṣṇa sees in Kṛṣṇa alone the whole of the supreme wonder, in which Nārāyaṇa's majesty is covered by sweetness, and so he has no desire to worship Nārāyaṇa. The *gopīs* never address Śrī Kṛṣṇa as “Rukmiṇī-ramaṇa.” In worldly dictionaries “Rukmiṇī-ramaṇa” and “Śrī Kṛṣṇa” are synonyms, words of one class, yet one cannot be used in place of the other. If someone does so out of foolishness, the fault of *rasābhāsa* arises. Those who have realized Bhagavān's true nature do not commit such *rasābhāsa* or go against *siddhānta*, as ignorant society does. And yet, through the strength of Kali, fools proclaim unruly false conclusions as broad-mindedness, a “great doctrine of synthesis,” and brand true *siddhānta* as bigotry and narrowness.

## 127

> Subheading: Whatever his position, only a knower of the truth of Kṛṣṇa gives divine knowledge —

Whether by *varṇa* he is a brāhmaṇa or a *kṣatriya*, *vaiśya* or *śūdra*, whether by *āśrama* he is a *sannyāsī*, *brahmacārī*, *vānaprastha* or householder — in whatever *varṇa* or *āśrama* he may be, only a knower of the truth of Kṛṣṇa can be a guru, that is, one who shows the path, a *dīkṣā-guru* and a *śikṣā-guru*. A guru's qualification depends only on his knowledge of the truth of Kṛṣṇa, not on *varṇa* or *āśrama*. This order of Śrī Mahāprabhu does not contradict the orders of scripture. In keeping with this purport, Śrī Viśvambhara Mahāprabhu received initiation from the *sannyāsī* Śrī Īśvara Purī, Śrī Nityānanda Prabhu from the *sannyāsī* Mādhavendra Purī Gosvāmī (according to another opinion, from Śrīmad Lakṣmīpati Tīrtha), and Śrī Advaita Ācārya from that same *sannyāsī* Śrī Mādhavendra Purī. Śrī Rasikānanda received *pāñcarātrika* initiation from Śrī Śyāmānanda, who was not born in a family of brāhmaṇas by birth; Śrī Gaṅgānārāyaṇa Cakravartī and Śrī Rāmakṛṣṇa Bhaṭṭācārya from Śrīla Narottama Ṭhākura, who also was not born in a family of brāhmaṇas by birth; and Śrī Yadunandana Cakravartī of Kāṭoyā from Śrī Gadādhara dāsa. Nothing prevented many others, such as Dharma-vyādha, from being *śikṣā-gurus*.

The *Mahābhārata* orders this clearly, and the *Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam* says the same: “Whatever marks are declared to reveal a person's *varṇa*, if they are seen in someone else as well, he should be designated by that very [*varṇa*].” (*Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam* 7.11.32[^127-1]) The verb here stands in the injunctive mood (*vidhi-liṅ*). Therefore, for one who follows the conviction of the Vaiṣṇavas, it is natural to recognize a knower of the truth of Kṛṣṇa as a brāhmaṇa by conduct (*vṛtta-brāhmaṇa*). And where, by the custom current in Kali, there is no brāhmaṇa status without a connection by seed, even a *śūdra* by birth, if he knows the truth of Kṛṣṇa, attains brāhmaṇa status according to scripture and can become a guru. This is what Śrī Mahāprabhu subtly explained.

Those knowers of the truth of Kṛṣṇa who do not accept the *sāvitrya-saṁskāra* described in Kātyāyana's “*guhya-sūtra*”[^127-2] of the Vedic Vājasaneya branch are simply *daikṣya* brāhmaṇas of the Ekāyana branch. But foolish people often fail to understand that they are “brāhmaṇas of Acyuta,” and go to hell. For this reason, in the family of Rasikānanda Prabhu, in the family of Śrī Mukunda dāsa of Śrīkhaṇḍa and in the family of Navanī Hoḍa, the *sāvitrya* brāhmaṇa *saṁskāra* has been performed from time immemorial, together with the work of ācāryas for a *sampradāya* whose disciples are brāhmaṇas by birth. Vaiṣṇavas whose joy is *bhajana* (*bhajanānandīs*) did not accept the *sāvitrya-saṁskāra*, but it does not follow that their example is the only rule. Vaiṣṇavas determine *varṇa* by its marks. Fools, however, cannot determine *varṇa* by such marks, and therefore Śrī Mahāprabhu explained the purport of scripture clearly.

The conclusion collected in the *Hari-bhakti-vilāsa* agrees with Śrī Mahāprabhu's own example and teaching, yet to a fool they seem different. In his judgment the word “guru” in this verse means only the *śravaṇa-guru* or the *śikṣā-guru* in *bhajana*, not the guru who gives *dīkṣā* or mantra. For in his opinion the right to give divine knowledge is determined and certified by family descent, that is, by blood or seed. So in his view devotion to Kṛṣṇa, the natural function of the pure self, is not independent. What is more, by his foolishness the superiority and greatness of the *dīkṣā-guru*, the giver of mantra, exceed those of the “*śravaṇa-guru*” or the “*śikṣā-guru* in *bhajana*”! On this one should especially study the *Anubhāṣya* on Ādi 1.47. In fact such a notion is merely the fruit of an offense born of sense-derived knowledge.

## 137

> Subheading: Madana-mohana, the form eternally served in Vraja —

***Vṛndāvana*** — the *Brahma-saṁhitā* (Chapter 5, verse 56) says: “There the beloveds are Lakṣmīs, and the Lover is the Supreme Person; the trees are wish-fulfilling trees, the earth is made of multitudes of *cintāmaṇi* gems, the water is nectar; speech there is song, walking is dance, the flute is the dear companion; the light is spiritual bliss, and that supreme [reality] is what is relished. There a great ocean of milk flows from the *surabhi* [cows], and time does not pass away, not even half a blink of an eye. I worship that Śvetadvīpa, which only a few saints, rarely wandering on this earth, know as Goloka.”

[The meaning of the verse:] “In the transcendental Vṛndāvana everything is spiritual. The transcendental Lakṣmīs, that is, the gopīs, are the beloveds; the Supreme Person, Kṛṣṇa, is the Lover of all. The trees there are wish-fulfilling trees, the ground is full of *cintāmaṇi* gems, the water is nectar, speech is song, walking is dance, the flute is the dear companion; all that shines — the moon, the sun and the rest — is full of spiritual bliss. It is this transcendental, spiritual mood that one should relish and cultivate. There an ocean of milk flows from the spiritual cows. There even half a blink of an eye is eternity; in other words, time there does not pass in vain and turn into another time. This abode of Vṛndāvana, manifest in the world, that Śvetadvīpa which a few rare saints who know the truth of Kṛṣṇa know as Goloka — that is what I worship.”

Vṛndāvana cannot be seen through earthly knowledge, which one gains and enjoys with one's own material senses guided by a material intellect; for the transcendental Vṛndāvana is the transcendental field of Kṛṣṇa's transcendental pastimes. Śrīla Narottama Ṭhākura sings in his *Prārthanā*: “When will Nitāicānd bestow His mercy on me? When will the desires of worldly life become trifling to me? When will my mind give up the objects of the senses and become pure? When shall I behold Śrī Vṛndāvana? When will longing for the feet of Rūpa and Raghunātha arise in me? When shall I understand the love of the Divine Couple?” See Madhya 14.219–226.

***Aprākṛta navīna madana*** (the transcendental, ever-new Madana). *Kāma* exists in both states: in the material, worldly one, and in its opposite, the spiritual, transcendental one. But material *kāma* is disturbed by time: it is felt only at the moment it appears, and the next moment it fades and is gone. Transcendental *kāma*, however, is ever new: in time it has no end, and it shines always. The *kāma* perceptible to the material senses is a movement of the material body and mind. It is present in the nature of every *jīva* who has turned away from Kṛṣṇa and is bent on gratifying his senses, but it is only momentary. And the Madana whom the spiritual senses serve is Kṛṣṇacandra, Manmatha-manmatha: He is ever new, He is the *svayaṁ-rūpa*, the original form.

***Kāma-gāyatrī.*** “You are called *gāyatrī* because you save the one who sings [you]” — that is, that which saves the singer or gives salvation through singing. (*Caitanya-caritāmṛta*, Madhya 21.125): “The *kāma-gāyatrī*, in the form of a mantra, is Kṛṣṇa's own nature. It has twenty-four and a half syllables. Those syllables are moons that rise upon Kṛṣṇa and have filled the three worlds with *kāma*.” [The mantra itself:] “*Klīṁ.* Let us know Kāmadeva; let us meditate on Puṣpabāṇa; may that Anaṅga inspire us.” Kāmadeva (see verse 187), that is, Kṛṣṇa as Madana-mohana, is the Deity who is the Lord of *sambandha*; Puṣpabāṇa, that is, Govinda, is the Lord of *abhidheya*; and Anaṅga, that is, Gopījana-vallabha, is the Lord of *prayojana*. The *kāma-gāyatrī* is transcendental. The *sādhaka* worships Kṛṣṇa in transcendental realization, relying on the transcendental composition [of the mantra].

The *Brahma-saṁhitā* (Chapter 5, verses 27–28) says: “Then the movement of the sound of the flute, the embodiment of the three Vedas, appeared and at once entered the lotus mouths of Svayambhū. The lotus-born, receiving from it the *gāyatrī* as it was sung, and purified by the original guru, became twice-born. Then Vidhi, awakened by the three Vedas, having come to know the ocean of truth, praised Keśava with this hymn, the essence of the Vedas.” Then the movement of the sound of Śrī Kṛṣṇa's flute, the embodiment of the three Vedas (the mother of the Vedas, of three times eight syllables, threefold, that is, consisting of *sambandha*, *abhidheya* and *prayojana*), appeared and suddenly entered the lotus mouths of Svayambhū Brahmā. Brahmā, born of the lotus, received initiation into the *gāyatrī* that came forth from the song of the flute, and the original guru, Śrī Kṛṣṇa, performed for him the rite of the twice-born (see the commentary of Śrī Jīva Prabhu). Awakened by remembering the *gāyatrī* — the embodiment of the three Vedas, that is, endowed with *sambandha*, *abhidheya* and *prayojana* — Brahmā became immersed in the ocean of truth, that is, attained realization. With that hymn, the essence of the Vedas, he served Keśava and won His love.

***Kāma-bīja*** — the transcendental “*klīṁ*.” The *Brahma-saṁhitā* (Chapter 5, verse 3) says: “…[that] which abides in the *rasa* of the great bliss, the bliss of *prema*, and is joined with the mantra shining as light, the *kāma-bīja*.” With the transcendental *kāma-gāyatrī* joined to the transcendental *kāma-bīja*, one performs transcendental worship of the transcendental, ever-new form of Madana-mohana. Thus the *Gopāla-tāpanī Upaniṣad* says: “And the worship (*rasana*) is this: beginning with the *kāma* [*bīja*] formed by the union of water, earth, [fire] and the moon, ‘*kṛṣṇāya*’ is the first word, ‘*govindāya*’ the second, ‘*gopījana*’ the third, ‘*vallabhāya*’ the fourth, ‘*svāhā*’ the fifth. Chanting this five-word [mantra] with its five limbs — heaven and earth, sun and moon together with fire — one attains Brahman in that form.” The commentary of Śrī Viśvanātha Cakravartī: “‘Water’ is the syllable *ka*, because it denotes water; ‘earth’ is the syllable *la*, because *la* is its *bīja*; ‘*ī*,’ the long *ī*, is fire, because it arises in the joining of sounds (*sandhi*); the ‘moon’ is the *anusvāra*, because it resembles it in shape. Their union is their joining; the *kāma-bīja* born of that joining comes first, and ‘*kṛṣṇāya*’ is one word — this is the meaning.” In other words, the *bīja* “*klīṁ*” is manifested by the joining of water (the syllable *ka*), earth (the syllable *la*), *ī* (the long *ī*, or fire) and the moon (the *anusvāra*). Placing this *bīja* “*klīṁ*” at the beginning of the Kṛṣṇa mantra, one worships the Supreme Brahman named Kṛṣṇa — the *rasana*, that is, the worship that pleases Him. See Ādi 5.212–214, 219, 221–222.

## 190

> Subheading: The highest state of the eternal, spiritual pastimes of service is “*prema-vilāsa-vivarta*”; it is not the attainment of a material, featureless *kevalādvaita* —

## 191

“That which goes beyond the path of imagination (*bhāvanā*), becomes the ground of an abundance of wonder, and is deeply relished in a heart made radiant by [pure] *sattva* — that is considered *rasa*.”[^191-1] *Rasa* is relished only in a heart full of the radiance of *viśuddha-sattva*. It is not something that the body and mind, bearing the gross and subtle *upādhis* of the outer or inner world, can relish. The semblance of selfhood (*asmitābhāsa*) seen in the secondary, gross and subtle world is what the words “intellect” (*buddhi*) and “mind” (*manas*) denote; they are not the self. An object full of *rasa* is perceived only by senses full of *rasa*. Senses full of *rasa*, by seeing, touching and the rest, all full of *rasa*, render loving service to Nanda-nandana — the crown of *rasikas*, the fountain of *rasa*, the *viṣaya-vigraha*. This is not merely the impersonalist's state of the absence of matter, in which “what is not That” has simply been rejected (*atan-nirasta*); that is why the definition of *rasa* specially records that it goes beyond the path of *bhāvanā*. One who teaches of the seer and the seen (*dṛg-dṛśya-vādī*) gains experiences in the material world rooted in sense gratification. Even if they serve merely as an antidote to material *vivarta*, they cannot approach transcendental *rasa*. The wonder that arises from the properties of the body and mind is incomplete, light and perishable; that is why spiritual *rasa* is said to reveal the weight of wonder. *Prema* is a pure, spiritual reality. Love (*prīti*) for what is not spiritual is perishable and rests only in *kāma*, whose nature is base. The suffering that comes in the material world when sense gratification is obstructed is the “*vivarta*” of material love. *Prema-vilāsa* and *vilāsa-vivarta* produce no lack, no inferiority (*avaratā*) and nothing unworthy. Śrī Rāmānanda, the relisher of transcendental *rasa*, enacting the pastime of asking whether Śrī Gaurasundara would approve the song he had composed and sung, described *prema-vilāsa-vivarta*.

The book *Vivarta-vilāsa* by Bhaktadāsa Bāul is the exact opposite of Śrī Jagadānanda's *Prema-vivarta* and Śrī Rāmānanda's *prema-vilāsa-vivarta*. The present community proud of its education, following talk of material *vivarta-vilāsa*, sets up a perverse understanding of *prema-vilāsa-vivarta*. By this it may obtain the title “Ph.D.” from the material *vidyāsāgaras* in the “temple of material learning.” But to obtain the title “Ph.D.” in the “temple of higher knowledge” (*parā-vidyā*), one must give up material ego and be established in one's eternal *svarūpa*. Between religious scriptures made by men and transcendental philosophical scriptures there is a difference as between heaven and the netherworld. Śrīmad Madhvācārya showed it clearly to the *sampradāya* that rejects *svagata* and *sajātīya* distinctions, relying on their own favorite method of reasoning. The community of material philosophers lives in a material *prema-vilāsa-vivarta*; therefore they will not be able to understand *prema-vilāsa-vivarta* through their own concocted experience. As an elephant cannot be fitted onto a plate, so transcendental experience is impossible for the advocate of the ascending path (*āroha-vādī*), with his wild dancing and yet utterly feeble strength.

Śrī Rāmānanda Rāya's words speak of the variety of pastimes and the *vilāsa-vivarta* of *adhirūḍha mahābhāva* — *mohana*, *mādana* and the rest — contained in “*prema-vaicittya*.” The *prākṛta-sahajiyā* cannot follow this. The *prākṛta-sahajiyā* will only be eager to drag the meaning of Śrī Rāmānanda's song into *kevala-nirviśeṣa-vāda*. But that was neither what Śrī Rāmānanda meant to say nor what Śrī Gaurasundara was to hear. Therefore Śrī Gaurasundara judged it improper to proclaim the hidden wonder and uniqueness of *bhajana* in a society of shallow material philosophers, and with His own hand He covered the lotus mouth of Śrī Rāmānanda, who was singing of Kṛṣṇa. This establishes that the song cannot be grasped by the experience of a material philosopher who looks at the external world.

If, however, the reasoning given above is adopted, the field of the *śuddhādvaita-vādī* can be established in place of *kevalādvaita-vāda*. The *śuddhādvaita-vādī* says: the nondual knowledge (*advaya-jñāna*) that is pointed to in an effort to remove the inferiority present in the dual world is imaginary; it has no *prema-vilāsa*. Moreover, in *prema-vilāsa-vivarta* the line *nā so ramaṇa, nā hāma ramaṇī* (“he is not the lover, nor am I the beloved”)[^191-2] can be misinterpreted. If this *vivarta* of interpretation seizes the material *vivarta-vādī*, it makes him call the attainment of *kevalādvaita* — the absence of *viṣaya* and *āśraya* — “*advaya-jñāna*,” and throws him back into material *vivarta*. Here the *śuddhādvaita-vādī* says: the words *nā so ramaṇa, nā hāma ramaṇī* do not destroy the real truth; their aim is to remove the apprehension of impure duality that arises when, in the Reality, the Reality and its potency are distinguished. To say this in the language of the *viśiṣṭādvaita-vādī*: the Reality is hard to know, but when potency (*śakti*) and its possessor (*śaktimān*) are known as nondifferent, the Reality becomes knowable through knowing the potency. Some separate the potency of the Reality from the Reality and imagine two separate entities, the “*ramaṇa*” (the lover) and the “*ramaṇī*” (the beloved). In their judgment this statement of Śrī Rāmānanda merely removes the distinction between a material possessor of potency and material potency.

The community of *prākṛta-sahajiyās* thinks that the material enjoyer, the “*ramaṇa*,” is different from the material object of enjoyment, the “*ramaṇī*,” and greatly honors impure dualistic reasoning. To be saved from it, the reasoning of *cintya-dvaitādvaita* was introduced. The *acintya-bhedābheda* reasoning of Śrī Rāmānanda or Śrī Gaurasundara is somewhat different from this. Here the reasoning of *śuddha-dvaita* and the reasoning of *acintya-bhedābheda* should be considered of one order. The *acintya-bhedābheda* reasoning differs from the reasonings of the *śuddhādvaita-vādī*, the *śuddha-dvaita-vādī*, the *cintya-dvaitādvaita-vādī* and the *viśiṣṭādvaita-vādī*. Therefore the Ācārya of *acintya-bhedābheda*, Himself not different from Vrajendranandana, proclaimed this through the holy mouth of Śrī Rāmānanda, the transcendental *sahajika*. It is of this very Rāmānanda that Śrī Gaurāṅgasundara said: “In the whole southern country I have seen no one like you, who follows the transcendental *sahaja-dharma*. ‘I am one *bāul*, you are a second *bāul*! Therefore you and I are equal.’”[^191-3] In saying this Śrī Gaurasundara displayed the reasoning of *acintya-bhedābheda*; therefore Śrī Jīvapāda in his *Sarva-saṁvādinī* accepted the Vedānta philosophy of the Gauḍīyas as “*acintya-bhedābheda*.”

In explaining *acintya-bhedābheda* the community of *prākṛta-sāhajikas* tells the story of bathing in the Gaṅgā with a body smeared with oil; but this is merely a *vivarta* of the material world. From such an example the truth of the manifestation of *bhedābheda* cannot be known. In establishing the nondifference of potency and its possessor, Śrī Rāmānanda's song shows the lover and the beloved as if exchanging the awareness of their own *svarūpa* (*vyatyaya*) — precisely in order to explain clearly how the *viṣaya* awakens (*uddīpana*) the *āśraya* and the *āśraya* awakens the *viṣaya*. Yet let no *jīva* on this account become an *ahaṅgrahopāsaka*. *Ahaṅgrahopāsanā* is the folly of the *cin-mātra-vādī* and simply the opposite of spiritual *vilāsa*. It is for the sake of those who think that the nondual Reality (*advaya-jñāna-vastu*) has no *bhāva* of the *āśraya* that Śrī Kṛṣṇa's eternal Gaura-līlā, abiding in Goloka in the chamber of magnanimity (*audārya*), descends into the world. This exemplary pastime of *prema-vilāsa-vivarta* shows that the Gaura-līlā descended into the world will never be an object of enjoyment for the “Gaura-nāgarīs,” who teach material *sambhoga*. Transcendental *prema-vilāsa* has no need of imaginary messengers such as “Kāñcanā.” If one proclaims the topics of the *rasa* of worshipping Kṛṣṇa (*kṛṣṇa-bhajana-rasa*) in a world starving for *kṛṣṇa-kathā*, the community of *prākṛta-sahajiyās*, wishing to show itself equal to the pure devotees, may fall into the *vivarta* of various doctrines. Knowing this, all these explanations have been kept for people of pure devotion alone.

***Sakhī.*** The *Ujjvala-nīlamaṇi* says: “A *sakhī* is she who fully expands the pastimes and sports of love; she is a casket holding the jewel of confidence.” She who fully expands the loving pastimes, sports and so on of Śrī Kṛṣṇa is called a *sakhī*. The *sakhīs* are a casket holding the jewel of Kṛṣṇa's confidence.

The activities of the *sakhīs*: “To praise to each other the love and qualities of the two, to awaken Their attachment, to arrange the meeting (*abhisāra*) of the two, to offer [another] *sakhī* to Kṛṣṇa, to jest, to reassure, to dress [Them], to skillfully open the heart, to conceal Her faults, to deceive her husband and others, to instruct, to bring Them together at the right time, to serve by fanning and so on, to reproach both of Them, to send messages, and to strive to protect the heroine's life — these are the activities of a *sakhī*.” That is: (1) praising the love and qualities of the hero and heroine to each other; (2) increasing the attachment of each for the other; (3) arranging the meeting (*abhisāra*) of the two; (4) offering a *sakhī* to Kṛṣṇa; (5) jesting; (6) giving reassurance; (7) dressing the hero and heroine (*nepathya*); (8) skill in expressing the feelings hidden in the heart; (9) concealing the heroine's faults; (10) deceiving her husband and others; (11) instruction; (12) bringing hero and heroine together at the proper time; (13) fanning with a *cāmara* and so on; (14) reproaching both; (15) sending messages; (16) striving to protect the heroine's life. See Ādi 4.211, 217–218.

The “Sakhī-bhekī,” the “Gaura-nāgarī” and other groups of *prākṛta-sahajiyās* identify with the body and adorn the material body, senses and skin — food for dogs and jackals. That never pleases Kṛṣṇa. All such artificial efforts, foreign to Kṛṣṇa, gratify only the material senses, and so Kṛṣṇa takes no enjoyment and no joy in them. The body, home, dress, ornaments and every act and effort of the spiritual Śrī Rādhā and Her *sakhīs* are all spiritual; they all please Kṛṣṇa's senses and bring Kṛṣṇa under their control; none of them is an event or object of the fourteen worlds within Devī-dhāma. Kṛṣṇa enchants the worlds, but they do not enchant the worlds: they enchant the heart of Him who enchants the worlds.

To yield to a mind bent on enjoyment and imagine oneself a *sakhī* in an imagined *siddha* body is also nothing but *ahaṅgrahopāsanā* (worshipping oneself as God); as a result the one who imagines this stays on in Devī-dhāma. Śrīla Jīva Gosvāmī Prabhu warned the conditioned *jīvas* about this too. Thus in the *Bhakti-rasāmṛta-sindhu* (Eastern Division, second wave) his commentary *Durgama-saṅgamanī* on the verse *pūrvair vatsala-sakhyādau…* says: “The meaning is: but not with the self-identification of being the king of Vraja and so on. The conception of being [His] father and so on can arise in two ways: as an independent [identity], and by imagining oneself identical with His father and others. Of these the latter is improper, just as worshipping oneself as identical with Bhagavān is improper: for their eternity will be established just like that of Bhagavān Himself, and so [such identification] is improper; and also because by such a particular conception about His associates one falls into offense.” That is why Śrīla Rūpa Gosvāmī Prabhu said (ibid.): “Remembering Kṛṣṇa and His dear associate whom one longs to serve, and delighting in talks about them, one should always live in Vraja. Longing for that *bhāva*, one should serve both in the *sādhaka* form and in the *siddha* form, following the people of Vraja.” (Commentary: “The people of Vraja here are those dear to Kṛṣṇa and those who follow them; following them.”) See Madhya 22.153, 155 and 157.

## 228

***Siddha-deha*** — a spiritual body fit for the service of Rādhā and Kṛṣṇa, other than the present material body and the subtle body of the mind. By the fruits of material *karma* the *jīva* obtains a material body; in time that body changes, and through the desire for gross enjoyment he again obtains a material body. > Subheading: The *iṣṭa-goṣṭhī* of the two —

***Goṣṭhī*** — conversation.



## 243

> Subheading: The conversation of the Lord and Rāmānanda: the Lord asks, Rāya answers —



## 244

> Subheading: (1) The highest knowledge is devotion to Kṛṣṇa —

To the question about the best knowledge (*vidyā*), Rāya answers that the best of all knowledge is the knowledge of devotion to Kṛṣṇa (*kṛṣṇa-bhakti-vidyā*). Above the knowledge that begets material enjoyment, and above *brahma-vidyā*, which lies beyond matter, stands the knowledge of devotion to Viṣṇu; and higher still is the knowledge of devotion to Kṛṣṇa. (*Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam* 4.29.49): “That is action which pleases Hari; that is knowledge by which the mind is turned to Him.” (*Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam* 7.5.23–24): “Hearing and chanting about Viṣṇu, remembering Him, serving His feet, worshiping Him, offering prayers, serving Him, being His friend and offering Him one's self — if a person offers Viṣṇu such devotion with nine features and performs it directly for Bhagavān, I consider that the highest learning.” (*Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam* 11.19.40): “Knowledge is the removal of the notion of separateness within the self.”



## 245

> Subheading: (2) The best fame, or honor, is to be Kṛṣṇa's servant —

Fame as a “devotee of Kṛṣṇa” surpasses every other fame. Greedy for material things, the *jīva* prizes only gross service to matter. But better than passing fame under some title in Devī-dhāma, or fame as a “knower of Brahman” in the realm beyond matter, is fame as a “devotee of Viṣṇu”; and on a still higher level is fame as a “devotee of Kṛṣṇa.” (*Garuḍa Purāṇa*, the words of Indra): “In Kali the name *bhāgavata* is rare and is never obtained; it is higher than the position of Brahmā and Rudra — so my guru told me.” (*Itihāsa-samuccaya*, the conversation of Śrī Nārada and Puṇḍarīka): “He in whom, after thousands of births, the thought arises, ‘I am the servant of Vāsudeva,’ delivers all the worlds.” (*Ādi Purāṇa*, the conversation of Kṛṣṇa and Arjuna): “The forms of liberation follow the devotees, together with the Śrutis.” (*Bṛhan-nāradīya Purāṇa*): “Even today the best of sages and the gods headed by Brahmā do not know the power of those whose hearts delight in devotion to Viṣṇu.” (*Garuḍa Purāṇa*): “One who performs sacrifices (*satra-yājī*) is better than thousands of *brāhmaṇas*; one who has mastered all Vedānta is better than thousands of *satra-yājīs*; a devotee of Viṣṇu is better than ten million knowers of all Vedānta; and one *ekāntin* is better than thousands of Vaiṣṇavas. The *ekāntins* reach the supreme abode.” (*Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam* 3.13.4): “The wise have declared that the highest fruit of long and laborious study of the scriptures is to hear constantly of the qualities of those in whose hearts are Mukunda's lotus feet.” (*Nārāyaṇa-vyūha-stava*): “O Hari, may I not become even Brahmā if I am without devotion to You; but if I am Your devotee, may I be even a worm, birth after birth.” See also *Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam* 3.25.38, 4.24.29, 4.31.22, 7.9.24, 10.14.30 and so on.

Among them is Prahlāda's fame, as Śrī Rudra says in the *Skanda Purāṇa*: “Only a devotee truly knows Kṛṣṇa — not I. Among all the devotees of Hari, Prahlāda is the very greatest” (*Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam* 7.9.26 and 7.10.21). Higher than he are the Pāṇḍavas (*Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam* 7.10.48–50, 7.15.75–77). Higher than they are the Yadus (*Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam* 10.82.28, 30). Among the Yadus Uddhava is the highest (*Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam* 3.4.31, 11.14.15, 11.16.29). Higher than he are the goddesses of Vraja (*Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam* 10.47.58). In the *Bṛhad-vāmana Purāṇa* Brahmā says to Bhṛgu and the other sages: “Long ago I performed austerities for sixty thousand years to obtain the dust of the feet of the women of the cowherd Nanda's Vraja. Even so I did not obtain the dust of their feet. Neither I, nor Śiva, nor Śeṣa, nor Śrī [Lakṣmī] is ever their equal.” In the *Ādi Purāṇa* Bhagavān says: “Neither Brahmā nor Rudra, O king, nor Lakṣmī, nor even My own Self is as dear to Me as the gopīs.” And among them Śrī Rādhikā is the highest of all.

Those who wholly follow Śrīla Rūpa Gosvāmī Prabhu — the best of Śrī Rādhā's dearest servants and the most intimate servant of Śrī Gaurāṅga — are known as *rūpānugas*. About their glory the *Śrī Caitanya-candrāmṛta* says: “Let there be ten million renunciations, ten million [virtues such as] *śama*, *dama*, forbearance, friendliness and the rest, ten million meditations on the truth — or even ten million kinds of Vaiṣṇava devotion; all of it is not equal to even a ten-millionth part of the host of qualities naturally present in those who relish the joy of the effulgence of the toenails on the feet of Śrīmat Caitanyacandra's beloved ones.”



## 246

> Subheading: (3) The highest wealth is loving devotion to Rādhā and Govinda —

Given over to material enjoyment, the *jīva* considers the best of all attainments to be wealth that better satisfies his craving for enjoyment. But if one weighs the different kinds of wealth with subtle, transcendental intelligence, there is no wealth equal to love for Rādhā and Kṛṣṇa. (*Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam* 10.39.2): “What is unattainable when Bhagavān, the abode of Śrī, is pleased? Yet, O king, those devoted to Him desire nothing.”



## 247

> Subheading: (4) The most bitter sorrow is separation from a devotee of Kṛṣṇa —

(*Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam* 3.30.6): “A mortal who has not worshiped Me, tormented by suffering, his mind attached to family, deprived of the company of saints, fallen from the service of his elders….” (*Bṛhad-bhāgavatāmṛta* 5.44): “More than our own life we long for the company of Śrī Viṣṇu's people; separated from them even for a moment, we find here not a particle of happiness.”



## 248

> Subheading: (5) Highest among the liberated is one who loves Kṛṣṇa —

(*Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam* 6.14.4): “O great sage, even among millions of the liberated and perfected, a peaceful devotee wholly devoted to Nārāyaṇa is very hard to find.”



## 249

(*Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam* 10.33.36): “To show mercy to His devotees He accepts a human body and performs such pastimes that one who hears of them becomes wholly devoted to Him.”



## 250

(*Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam* 11.2.28): “Therefore, O sinless ones, we ask you about the highest good. In this cycle of birth and death, association with saints even for half a moment is a treasure for human beings.”

(*Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam* 2.2.36): “Therefore, O king, with all one's being, everywhere and always, people should hear about Bhagavān Hari, glorify Him and remember Him.”



## 252

(*Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam* 1.2.14): “Therefore, with one-pointed mind, one should constantly hear about Bhagavān, the Lord of the Sātvatas, glorify Him, meditate on Him and worship Him.”

(*Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam* 10.47.61): “Oh, may I be born in Vṛndāvana as some bush, creeper or herb that receives the dust of these gopīs' feet! For they gave up their own people, so hard to give up, and the path of the respectable, and took the path of Mukunda, which the Śrutis seek.”

(*Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam* 10.30.39):[^254-1] “One who with faith hears of this play of Viṣṇu with the girls of Vraja and then describes it attains supreme devotion to Bhagavān, becomes steady, and quickly drives out lust, the disease of the heart.”



## 255

> Subheading: (12) Only the names “Hare Kṛṣṇa” are to be chanted —

(*Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam* 6.3.22): “Only this is considered the highest *dharma* for people in this world: *bhakti-yoga*, devotional service to Bhagavān, beginning with chanting His name.”



## 256

> Subheading: (13) The destination of those who seek liberation and of those who seek enjoyment —

The *mukti-vādīs*, who have rejected material enjoyment, in the end obtain a stationary (*sthāvara*) body devoid of spiritual activity, that is, with consciousness asleep. And the *bhukti-vādīs*, bound to material enjoyment, obtain in the next world the bodies of gods, fit for enjoyment.

“The great sage who, for the sake of liberation — that is, becoming stone — set forth a *śāstra*: know that he is Gautama, and he is just as you know him to be.”[^256-1] This is the fruit of the philosophy of the adherents of Buddhist doctrine.

(*Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam* 11.10.22): “Having worshiped the gods with sacrifices, the performer of sacrifice goes to Svarga, and there, like a god, he enjoys the celestial pleasures he has earned.” (*Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam* 4.29.29): “One becomes a god, a human or an animal according to one's *karma* and *guṇas*.” See also *Bhagavad-gītā* 9.20.



## 244–256

In the answers to the questions of verses 244–256, the material and the transcendental are compared in worth. It is shown that the material outlook is contemptible, and that what belongs to transcendental Goloka — free from material self-interest and aimed at Kṛṣṇa alone — surpasses everything.



## 257

> Subheading: The difference between the practice of a *jñānī* and of a devotee —

*Jñāna* is like the *nimba* fruit: it cannot be relished, and it is food for *jīvas* who, like crows, cling to harsh logic. But the taste of the mango buds of *prema* is pleasing and very sweet: it is relished only by Kṛṣṇa's devotees, who are like cuckoos that know how to relish *rasa*.



## 258

Dry knowledge is what falls to the lot of the unfortunate *jñānī* to taste; but the nectar of love for Kṛṣṇa, full of *rasa*, is the drink of the fortunate devotee.

## 265

> Subheading: The definition of the Supreme Lord, full of spiritual play, and meditation on Him —

**yataḥ** (from whom, the possessor of potencies) **asya** (of this universe) **janmādi** (birth, maintenance and destruction) — according to the Śruti *yato vā imāni bhūtāni jāyante* and so on, and the *sūtra* *janmādy asya yataḥ* (*Brahma-sūtra* 1.1.2); **anvayāt itarataḥ ca** (this comes about by *anvaya* and *vyatireka*); **yaḥ** **anvayāt** **artheṣu** (in spiritual affairs, which befit spiritual form, taste, smell, sound and touch) **abhijñaḥ** (attached), and **vyatirekāt** **artheṣu** (in material objects of form, taste, smell, sound and touch) **abhijñaḥ** (untouched by them), and being such — **sva-rāṭ** (He who shines by Himself, self-luminous); **yat** (in which supreme Truth) **sūrayaḥ** (Brahmā and others — according to the account in the Tenth Canto of Brahmā's bewilderment; the gods — according to the Talavakāra Śruti; the *brāhmaṇas* and others, and the sages — Dattātreya, Durvāsā, Vasiṣṭha, Śaṅkara, Vidyāraṇya and others — according to the words *daivāhatārtha-racanā*, *Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam* 3.9.10) **api** **muhyanti** (are bewildered, that is, cannot ascertain the supreme Truth); **tat brahma** (that truth — according to the words *vadanti tat tattva-vidaḥ* and so on) **ādi-kavaye** (to Brahmā) **hṛdā** (through the mind — according to the *Brahma-saṁhitā*'s words *trayyā prabuddhaḥ*) **yaḥ** **tene** (revealed); **yathā tejo-vāri-mṛdāṁ vinimayaḥ** (as in fire, water and earth there is an interchange, one appearing as another, so) **tri-sargaḥ** (the perishable creation of the three — *rajas*, *tamas* and *sattva*; in the other sense, the eternal manifestation of the three potencies — internal, external and marginal) **yatra** (in the supreme Truth, in Bhagavān's *svarūpa*, the form of eternity, knowledge and bliss, nondual Knowledge) **amṛṣā** (true); **svena dhāmnā** (by His power — His transcendental internal opulence, *sandhinī* and the rest, proper to His nature) **sadā nirasta-kuhakam** (in whom deception, *kuhaka*, the mark of *māyā*, is rejected and cast away) **satyam** (the true essence, the eternal) **param** (Him who is above all, the Supreme Lord) **dhīmahi** (we meditate). For more, see the *Gauḍīya-bhāṣya* on the *Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam*.

> Subheading: Seeing everywhere Kṛṣṇa and what belongs to Kṛṣṇa (*kārṣṇa*) —

Nimi, the king of Videha, wished to know the marks, the vision, the conduct and the words of the three kinds of devotees, or *bhāgavatas*. In answer to his question Havi, one of the nine Yogendras, said:

**yaḥ** **sarva-bhūteṣu** (in all beings, conscious and unconscious) **ātmanaḥ** (of the self — transcendental, beyond the matter that is enjoyed) **bhagavad-bhāvam** (in beings, the *siddha-svarūpa* and the rest, fit for the service of Bhagavān) **paśyet**, **ātmani bhagavati** **bhūtāni** (engaged in eternal transcendental service in their own *siddha* forms) **paśyet**, (he) **eṣaḥ** **bhāgavatottamaḥ**. (By the strength of their transcendental feeling the *mahā-bhāgavatas* everywhere see Kṛṣṇa and what belongs to Kṛṣṇa (*kārṣṇa*), standing in the relation of the One who is served and His servants — for they have no external vision.)

## 286

The *prākṛta-sahajiyās*, relying on the words *gaura aṅga nahe*, take Gaura and Kṛṣṇa to be different. In fact both are the form of the *svayaṁ-rūpa*. Gaura Himself, in His “*svarūpa* as Kṛṣṇa,” is in the *rasa* of *sambhoga* the *nāgara*, the *viṣaya-vigraha*; and Kṛṣṇa Himself, in His “*svarūpa* as Gaura,” is in the *rasa* of *vipralambha* Śrī Kṛṣṇa Caitanya, full of the *bhāva* and radiance of Śrī Rādhā, the *āśraya-vigraha*. No form of Viṣṇu other than Śrī Nandanandana, the *svayaṁ-rūpa* — the embodiment of transcendental *śṛṅgāra*, the king of *rasas*, the *dhīra-lalita* hero — can enjoy Śrī Rādhikā, Kṛṣṇa's complete *cit-śakti*, or *svarūpa-śakti*, *mahābhāva* itself. For in all forms of Viṣṇu other than Kṛṣṇa there is no *śṛṅgāra-rasa* and no nature of a *dhīra-lalita* hero, and the mood of majesty (*aiśvarya*) predominates. That is why Śrīmatī's names are: “Rādhā is the delight of Govinda and the enchantress of Govinda; She is Govinda's all-in-all and the crest-jewel of all His beloveds.” (*Caitanya-caritāmṛta*, Ādi 4.82)

## 308

> Subheading: Bhagavān's inconceivable nature is beyond logic —



## 309

> Subheading: Love for Kṛṣṇa is attained only by one wholly devoted to Nitāi, Gaura and Advaita —



## 307–309

“Kṛṣṇa is attained by faith; by logic one goes far away.” That is, only through faith (*viśvāsa*), that is, *śraddhā*, following the path step by step, does one come to experience this supramundane, supremely secret, true Reality — the pastimes of Śrī Kṛṣṇa. It is not the fiction or “whim” of a *jīva* who does not follow the path of *śruti*, doubts the real truth and has turned away from service — a product of the mind with its accepting and rejecting, which can be shaped however one likes. One who relies on material logic has so great a desire for material enjoyment that the spiritual pastimes stay far away. Thus (*Kaṭha Upaniṣad* 1.2.9): “This understanding cannot be reached by logic; O dearest, only when taught by another does it lead to true knowledge.” (*Muṇḍaka Upaniṣad* 3.2.3): “This Self is not attained by discourse, nor by intellect, nor by much hearing. Whom He chooses, by him He is attained; to him this Self reveals His own form.” (*Brahma-sūtra* 2.1.11): “For logic has no firm ground.” A person who tries to grasp the supramundane truth with knowledge full of material, mundane judgments goes far from the Reality, because the subject under consideration here (the *rasa* of love for Kṛṣṇa) is supramundane. When materialistic *sahajiyās* or men of letters reason about it with the mind, that is, the intellect, what they think they have reasoned about is mundane; and so all such efforts of theirs are futile. Only one who has given up such reasoning and has faith in Viṣṇu-*tattva* alone has knowledge of *sambandha* that is pure and attained without difficulty.

> Subheading: The author glorifies Rāya —



## 311

What he means to establish is this: the event “the meeting of the Lord and Rāmānanda” was written and described exactly according to the diary (*kaḍacā*) of Śrīla Dāmodara Svarūpa; it is not something invented out of one's own head, not the proud effort of a worldly man who has given up hearing from the mouth of the guru.
