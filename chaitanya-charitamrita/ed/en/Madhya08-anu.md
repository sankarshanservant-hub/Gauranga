## 3

> Subheading: *Darśana* of Jiyaḍa-Nṛsiṁha; dancing, prayers and songs —

**Jiyaḍa-Nṛsiṁha-kṣetra** is a place called “Simhachalam” on the B. N. R. line, five miles due north of Vizagapatam, or Viśākhāpattana. There is also a railway station called “Siṁhācala.” The temple of Śrī Nṛsiṁhadeva stands high on the hill. Of all the temples in Vizagapatam it is the most famous and the richest, and it remains the finest example of architecture. A stone slab records that a pious queen of King Goṅkā III covered the Deity with gold (*Vizagapatam Gazetteer*). Near the temple live the servants of Śrī Nṛsiṁha and other residents. Now on the hilltop, next to the temple, there are many places where pilgrims can stay and many houses. The *vijaya-mūrti* (the festival Deity) resides in a lighted place, and the original Deity of Nṛsiṁha within. Several Śrī Vaiṣṇavas of Rāmānuja's line serve the Deity under the patronage of the king of Vizianagaram.

## 35–36

Out of natural humility Śrīla Rāya Rāmānanda called himself by low names such as “*viṣayī*” and “the lowest of *śūdras*.” Nor was he born in a family of brāhmaṇas by birth (*śaukra-vipras*). Yet in the manifest pastimes he was an *akiñcana*, a pure *bhāgavata*, a *paramahaṁsa*. Therefore to call him a transcendental brāhmaṇa by initiation (*daikṣa-brāhmaṇa*) of the Vedic Ekāyana branch expresses only a small part of his greatness. Even a man born into a great family, initiated into every sacrifice and versed in a thousand branches of the Vedas, will surely go to hell if he sees caste (*śūdra*) in Rāmānanda and considers him equal to others born in *śūdra* families: “…he who sees in him only a common caste certainly goes to hell.” (*Padma Purāṇa*) The eternal good of a *jīva* who seeks the highest goal lies only in the self-conception of being his servant.

## 57

> Subheading: The conversation of the Lord and Rāmānanda. The Lord asks about the goal and the means (*sādhya* and *sādhana*). Rāya's answer: (a) the stages of *sādhana* (*abhidheya*): (1) first, keeping *svadharma* in the form of *daiva-varṇāśrama*, the beginning of a theistic moral life, or a life of *dharma* —

Śrī Rāmānujapāda writes in the *Vedārtha-saṅgraha*: “This special knowledge, *para-bhakti*, is produced only by *bhakti-yoga* assisted by action preceded by knowledge, which, as said above, grows day by day. So says the venerable Parāśara: ‘*Varṇāśrama*…’ And the Supreme Person, Parabrahman Himself, who descended to the earth to deliver the whole world, said: ‘Hear how one devoted to his own work attains perfection. By worshiping with his own work Him from whom all beings have come forth and by whom all this is pervaded, a man attains perfection.’ (*Bhagavad-gītā* 18.45–46) Thus [the Lord] is attained only by *bhakti* matured in the order described. This path is shown by the multitude of *śrutis* whose meaning is clearly set forth in the ancient explanations of the Vedas and Vedānta accepted by irreproachable sages such as the venerable Bodhāyana, Ṭaṅka, Dramiḍa, Guhadeva, Kapardī and Bhāruci.”

*Bhakti* is a special knowledge: it is dear beyond measure, it alone is needed, and it produces indifference to everything else. Only a soul endowed with such *bhakti* chooses Bhagavān, and only to devotees is He attainable. This special knowledge, the highest *bhakti*, is produced only by the *bhakti-yoga* described above, which grows without cease and is assisted by action preceded by knowledge. This is what the venerable Parāśara said in the verse *varṇāśramācāravatā*… And the Supreme Person, Parabrahman Himself, who descended to the earth to deliver the whole world, said: “Hear how a man engaged in his own work will attain perfection: by especially worshiping with his own work that Bhagavān from whom all living beings have come forth and by whom this world is spread out, a man will attain perfection.” This path to perfection is assisted by *karma*, and on it [the Lord] is attained only by *bhakti* matured in due order. The venerable Bodhāyana, Ṭaṅka, Dramiḍa, Guhadeva, Kapardī, Bhāruci and other sages approve this irreproachable path; it is the path shown by the ancient explanations of the Vedas and Vedānta and by the *śrutis* whose meaning is beautifully (clearly) set forth.

The *ācāryas* of Rāmānuja's *sampradāya* say: “The means of attaining Brahman is only *para-bhakti*, constant meditation (*anudhyāna*) that has become the clearest direct vision, dear without limit and beyond measure. It is attained by steady *bhakti* assisted by one's own *karma* preceded by knowledge of the truth gained from scripture. As the verse *varṇāśramācāravatā*… shows, [this *bhakti*] is not restricted to *sannyāsa*, nor to any one *varṇa*: it is bound to each person's own *varṇa* and *āśrama*. True knowledge is only the knowledge of which *karma* is a part, not *naiṣkarmya*, nor an equal combination of knowledge and *karma*.”

***Sādhya*** — that by which perfection (*siddhi*) is attained, that which is gained by *sādhana*: see *Bhāg.* 1.2.13.

## 58

> Subheading: Viṣṇu is pleased only when *svadharma* in the form of *daiva-varṇāśrama* is kept —

**varṇāśramācāravatā** (by one devoted to the conduct of the *varṇas*, brāhmaṇa, kṣatriya, vaiśya and śūdra, and intent on keeping the conduct of the *āśramas*, *brahmacārī*, *gṛhastha*, *vānaprastha* and *bhikṣu*, that is, one who keeps the *dharma* of his own *varṇa* and *āśrama*) **puruṣeṇa** **paraḥ** **pumān** (the Supreme Person, Viṣṇu) **ārādhyate**; **tat** (His, Viṣṇu's) **anyat** (any other, one that destroys *varṇāśrama-dharma*) **panthāḥ** (path) **toṣa-kāraṇam** (for His pleasure) **na** **bhavati**.

## 59

> Subheading: (2) Devotion mixed with *karma*, offering one's actions to Bhagavān, is not pure devotion —

Setting out to determine the *sādhya*, that is, the devotion that is to be practiced, Śrī Rāmānanda first took the standpoint of a *sādhaka* living within the universe. Rejecting other desires (*anyābhilāṣitā*), he took the side of the moralists (*nīti-vādīs*) and cited the evidence for the *sādhya* that Viṣṇu is pleased when one simply follows *varṇa-dharma* and *āśrama-dharma*. But one who determines the goal in this way grasps his relationship [with the Lord] through his “I” (*asmitā*), and that “I” lies within the universe. Hence the function of such *asmitā* also remains within the universe, and so it is external. Śrī Bhagavān Gaurahari called the external experience of a person situated outside His abode, Vaikuṇṭha or Goloka, an “external *sādhya*,” rejected it, and told him to go further. The evidence about the *sādhya* cited above did not point to the independence of Viṣṇu's personal nature (*viśeṣatva*). Therefore *sādhakas* of this level on the path of *karma* may aim at the worship of Viṣṇu of both kinds, “*nirviśeṣa*” and “*saviśeṣa*.” Understanding this, [Rāmānanda] gave up the leaning toward featureless truth and cited the evidence showing that the personal nature (*saviśeṣatva*) [of the Lord] alone is the purport of the aim of *karma*.

**kaunteya** (O Arjuna), **yat** **karma** **karoṣi**, **yat** **aśnāsi**, **yat** **dadāsi**, **yat** **juhoṣi**, **yat** **tapasyasi**, **tat** **sarvaṁ** **mad-arpaṇam** **kuruṣva**. See *Bhāg.* 11.2.36.

## 61

> Subheading: (3) Merely giving up the enjoyment of results, or *naiṣkarmya*, is not pure devotion; the first step of pure devotion is to give up *svadharma*, surrender, and serve Śrī Hari —

The word *mad-arpaṇa* (“offered to Me”) rejects the material featureless and means offering to Kṛṣṇa alone, the independent, personal (*saviśeṣa*) Truth. Yet the *sādhaka* still grasps himself through his “I” (*asmitā*), which lies within the universe, and the activity he practices also remains within the universe. Therefore this too is external. That is, the *jīva* engaged in action, with external perception, receives only the instruction to give his external actions to the independent Reality that is beyond all action. Then Rāmānanda purified this understanding: he took up the mood to which the understanding of a *jīva* risen above *karma* must be raised, and cited the evidence that the goal is attained by giving up *svadharma*.

## 62

> Subheading: Giving up *svadharma* in the form of *varṇāśrama* and serving Hari —

The words of Śrī Bhagavān answering Uddhava, who wished to know the marks of a *sādhu* dear to Bhagavān:

**yaḥ** (that *sādhaka* who) **guṇān** **doṣān** (material good and bad states and so on) **ājñāya** (knowing fully), **api** **mayā** (by Me, the teacher of Vedic *karma*, to those attached to *karma*) **ādiṣṭān** (enjoined) **sarvān** **svakān** **dharmān** (the worldly *dharmas* of the *varṇas*, brāhmaṇa, kṣatriya, vaiśya and śūdra, and the *dharmas* of the *āśramas*, *brahmacārī*, *gṛhastha*, *vānaprastha*, *sannyāsī* and so on) **santyajya** (casting them far away and completely) **mām** (Bhagavān Kṛṣṇa, the independent, the shelter of the personal Truth) **bhajet**, **sa** **tu** **sattamaḥ** (the best of *sādhus*).

## 63

Śrī Bhagavān's confidential instruction to Arjuna:

**sarva-dharmān** (the *dharmas* of the *varṇas* within the universe, brāhmaṇa, kṣatriya, vaiśya, śūdra and so on, and the *dharmas* of the *āśramas* within the universe, *brahmacārī*, *gṛhastha*, *vānaprastha*, the fourth *āśrama* and so on) **parityajya** (casting far away) **ekam** (beyond them, nondual Knowledge, with an unwavering mind) **mām** (Bhagavān Kṛṣṇa, the personal Truth) **śaraṇaṁ** **vraja** (go); **ahaṁ** **tvāṁ** **sarva-pāpebhyaḥ** (from the *adharma* within the universe that arises from abandoning the material, regular Vedic rites) **mokṣayiṣyāmi** (I shall deliver); **mā** **śucaḥ** (do not grieve over transient *dharmas*).

Śrī Raghunātha dāsa Gosvāmī Prabhu says in his own *Manaḥ-śikṣā*: “Do not perform either the *dharma* or the *adharma* that the *śrutis* describe; here in Vraja render abundant service to Rādhā and Kṛṣṇa…”[^63-1] “When Bhagavān, on whom one meditates within, shows someone His mercy, that person gives up a mind fixed on worldly and Vedic ways.” (*Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam* 4.29.46) In this connection see *Bhāg.* 1.5.17.

## 64

> Subheading: (4) Devotion mixed with knowledge is not pure devotion —

In the realization of a *jīva* who has risen above *karma*, his “I” (*asmitā*) abides in the river Virajā beyond the universe. There the three *guṇas* do not predominate: there is only equilibrium, the unmanifest state. Between Vaikuṇṭha, manifested by the internal potency, and the universe, manifested by the external potency, lie Brahmaloka and the river Virajā. These two places are the shelter of the realization of *jīvas* who are detached from matter and know only material featurelessness. They are not Vaikuṇṭha and lie outside it, and so they are external. A *sādhaka* within the universe who has given up all *dharmas* has no experience of Vaikuṇṭha or Goloka. Therefore such an inclination toward the goal, though it has given up material enjoyment, establishes only the featurelessness of the nonconscious (*acit-nirviśeṣatva*), and so it too is external. Rāmānanda then understood this mood to be an external conception of the goal and cited the evidence that the goal higher than it is devotion mixed with knowledge.

## 65

> Subheading: Only a brāhmaṇa who has gained knowledge of his relationship with the Lord (*sambandha-jñāna*) becomes a Vaiṣṇava by serving Kṛṣṇa —

The words of Śrī Bhagavān to Arjuna:

**brahma-bhūtaḥ** (freed from an awareness confined to the universe and intent on featureless experience) **prasannātmā** (free from the quality of want) **na** **śocati** (does not grieve over material lack), **na** **kāṅkṣati** (does not long for material enjoyment), **sarveṣu** **bhūteṣu** (knowing that all beings are connected with service to Me) **samaḥ** **san**, **parām** (supreme, pure) **mad-bhaktiṁ** **labhate**.

## 66

> Subheading: (5) Only devotion free from knowledge deserves the name of pure devotion —

Even in this state the *asmitā* and its function do not abide in pure Vaikuṇṭha and are not directed toward Vaikuṇṭha, and so this too is external. As long as there is only freedom from subjection to matter, or only an inclination toward pure experience beyond matter, there is no independent and personal realization of the real, true Object. Hence one's own experience and one's own mental disposition face outward. In truth, this is not the goal of the pure *jīva* either. When one imagines featurelessness, all the particulars of *sac-cid-ānanda* lie dormant. And before that, all the reasonings born of imagination have as their purport only meditation on the featureless. Therefore such an imagined state of liberation, intent on the featureless and devoid of the inclination to serve Bhagavān, is also external.

## 67

> Subheading: Only one who has completely surrendered to Kṛṣṇa is a pure devotee, who brings Kṛṣṇa under his control —

Brahmā stole the calves and so on, and for this Śrī Kṛṣṇa crushed his pride. Then Brahmā, taking exclusive shelter of Śrī Kṛṣṇa, offers Him prayers:

**jñāne** (for the sake of knowledge) **prayāsam** (the troubles and so on born of effort) **udapāsya** (casting far away), **san-mukharitām** (sung by saints, the *mahā-bhāgavatas*, naturally manifest) **śruti-gatām** (reaching the ears) **bhavadīya-vārtām** (narrations about You, about Your name, form, qualities and pastimes) **ye** **sthāne** **sthitāḥ** (remaining in their own place, on the path of the *sādhus*) **tanu-vāṅ-manobhiḥ** (with body, mind and words) **namantaḥ** (surrendering themselves in every way) **eva** **jīvanti**, **ajita** (O Unconquerable One: beyond the senses, not to be overcome by nondevotees, dependent on no one, immeasurable), **tri-lokyām** **taiḥ** (by Your devotees alone) **prāyaśaḥ** (You) **jitaḥ** (conquered) **api** **asi**.

## 68

> Subheading: (b) The perfection of *sādhana*: *prema-bhakti* (*bhāva*, the sprout of *prema*) —

When the verse *jñāne prayāsam*… was spoken in determining the goal, Mahāprabhu accepted that inclination as the inclination toward the goal (*sādhya-vṛtti*). This is called *sādhana-bhakti*. Then the Lord told him to go further. After *sādhana-bhakti* comes *bhāva-bhakti*, the sprout of *prema-bhakti*, and in *śānta-rasa* the quality of equanimity (*nairapekṣya*) predominates. Therefore Rāmānanda Rāya named as the goal *prema-bhakti* endowed with the four *rasas*. “*Sādhana-bhakti*” means *śraddhā*, *sādhu-saṅga*, *bhajana-anuṣṭhāna*, *anartha-nivṛtti*, *niṣṭhā*, *ruci* and *āsakti*.

## 69

> Subheading: Kṛṣṇa is conquered only by a devotee's *prema* —

**yāvat** **jaṭhare** (in the stomach) **jaraṭhā** (intense) **kṣut** **asti**, **pipāsā** (and there is), **tāvat** **bhakṣya-peye** (as) **sukhāya** (for joy) **bhavataḥ**, (so) **ārta-bandhoḥ** (of Śrī Kṛṣṇa, the Lord of the wretched) **nānopacāra-kṛta-pūjanam** (worship and so on with the various sixteen offerings) **bhakta-hṛdayam** **premṇā** (by devotion that pleases Kṛṣṇa's senses) **eva** **sukha-vidrutam** (melted with bliss) **syāt**.

## 70

> Subheading: Love for Kṛṣṇa is attained only on the path of *rāga*; it is hard to attain in *vaidhī-bhakti* born of pious merit —

**kṛṣṇa-bhakti-rasa-bhāvitā** (absorbed in the *rasa* of service to Kṛṣṇa) **matiḥ** (intelligence) **yadi** **kutaḥ** (from anywhere: from some place, time or person, or from practice) **labhyate**, (then by you such intelligence) **krīyatām** (must certainly be acquired by paying the price); **tatra** (in this trade, in buying that intelligence) **ekalam** **laulyam** (greed) **eva** (indeed) **mūlyam**, (for that intelligence) **janma-koṭi-sukṛtaiḥ** (by fortune stored up over many births) **na** **labhyate** (that is, it is supremely rare).

## 71

> Subheading: (1) “*Dāsya-prema*” is excellent —

In the two verses quoted above *prema-bhakti* was determined as “the goal” in general. Therefore Śrī Mahāprabhu told Rāmānanda to go further and explain that “goal” in particular, step by step. Then [Rāmānanda] establishes “*dāsya-prema-bhakti*” as “the goal.”

## 72

> Subheading: Only Kṛṣṇa's servant is heir to all His opulence —

Durvāsā saw with a perverted vision born of the senses, was possessed by the evil desire to oppose Vaiṣṇavas, and was proud of being a brāhmaṇa. When Sudarśana, the Vaiṣṇava's weapon, began to torment him, it was stopped by the prayer of the *mahā-bhāgavata* Ambarīṣa. Seeing this, Durvāsā was freed from caste-consciousness and praised the pure devotee and Bhagavān thus:

**yan-nāma-śruti-mātreṇa** (simply by hearing the name of that Bhagavān) **pumān** (the *jīva*) **nirmalaḥ** (pure) **bhavati**, **tasya** **tīrtha-padaḥ** (of that Bhagavān at whose feet are the holy places) **dāsānām** (for the servants) **kiṁ** **vā** **avaśiṣyate**? (That is, nothing at all.)
