# Amṛta-pravāha-bhāṣya on Antya-līlā 4

## 0

**Summary.** Śrī Sanātana Gosvāmī came alone from Mathurā-maṇḍala to Puruṣottama by the forest path through Jhārikhaṇḍa. On the way, from the bad water and from fasting, his body broke out in oozing sores (*kaṇḍu-rasā*). Tormented by them, he resolved: “Before the Lord I shall give up this body under the wheel of Jagannātha’s chariot.” Arriving in Puruṣottama, he stayed at Haridāsa’s dwelling. Mahāprabhu was overjoyed to see him. Sanātana Gosvāmī then told the Lord of Anupama’s departure from this world on the bank of the Gaṅgā and of his steadfast devotion to Śrī Rāmacandra. One day Mahāprabhu said to Sanātana: “Giving up the body and the like is a quality of darkness (*tamo-dharma*): by giving up the body one cannot attain love for Kṛṣṇa. Abandon this notion born of darkness. You have offered your body to Me, and you have no right to give it up. Through this body of yours I shall propagate many scriptures on *bhakti* and restore the lost holy places of Vṛndāvana.” When Mahāprabhu had left, Haridāsa and Sanātana talked at length. One day the Lord sent for Sanātana, calling him to Yameśvara-ṭoṭā, and he came there along the seashore. When Mahāprabhu questioned him, Sanātana replied: “The servants of Jagannātha come and go by the way past the Lion Gate, so I came over the sand. I did not even notice that blisters had risen on my feet.” These words of Sanātana, upholding due reverence (*maryādā*), pleased the Lord. Fearing that the discharge from his sores would touch the Lord’s body, Sanātana kept his distance from Him, yet the Lord would forcibly embrace him all the same. Distressed by this, Sanātana asked Jagadānanda Paṇḍita for advice, and Jagadānanda advised him to go to Vṛndāvana after the Ratha-yātrā. Hearing of this, Mahāprabhu chided Jagadānanda somewhat and showed that Sanātana was superior to him. He also said to Sanātana: “You are a pure devotee, and one should not judge whether your body is good or bad. Above all, I am a *sannyāsī*, and such judgement does not befit Me at all.” Finally He said: “You are the ones I cherish, and I am the one who cherishes you; therefore the discharge from your sores does not disgust Me.” After these exchanges Mahāprabhu embraced Sanātana, and the sores and everything else vanished from his body. The Lord kept Sanātana in Kṣetra that year and (the following year) ordered him to go to Śrī Vṛndāvana. Following that order, Sanātana went to Vṛndāvana by the forest path. Meanwhile Śrī Rūpa Gosvāmī took leave at Mahāprabhu’s feet, stayed in Gauḍa for a year, distributed all his wealth among his relatives, the *brāhmaṇas* and the temples, and then went to Vṛndāvana and joined Sanātana. After that Kavirāja Gosvāmī names the books written by Rūpa, Sanātana and Jīva.

## 1

When Sanātana came from Vṛndāvana, Śrī Gauracandra in His affection saved him from giving up his body, tested him and made him pure.

## 5

*Khājuyāite*: scratching an itching rash (*khos-pā̃carā*).

## 51

*Cakra*: the Nīla-cakra.

## 61

In separation from Kṛṣṇa a loving devotee wishes to give up his body. But by the very power of that love he attains Kṛṣṇa and cannot give up his body; that is, Kṛṣṇa does not let him die.

## 63

O Lotus-eyed One! Great souls like Śiva long to bathe in the dust of Your lotus feet to destroy the darkness within themselves. If I do not obtain this grace of Yours, I shall give up my life, emaciated by vows undertaken to attain You — and after a hundred births, if need be, I shall obtain Your grace.

## 64

O beloved! When we saw Your smiling glances and heard Your sweet song, the fire of love’s desire (*kāma*) blazed up in us. Quench it with the flood of nectar from Your lips and cool it. If You do not, O friend, then with bodies burned by the fire of separation from You we shall reach the path to Your feet by meditation.

## 150

**Nirviṇṇa**: filled with *nirveda*, that is, with detachment (*virāga*).

## 174

The Lord said to Sanātana: “You are a Vaiṣṇava, and your body is transcendental: you should not think of it as ‘good’ or ‘bad.’ Moreover, I am a *sannyāsī*: even if your body were material, I still could not disregard it. For a *sannyāsī* is transcendental by nature, and it never befits him to perceive things as good and bad.”[^174-1]

## 175

What is dual (marked not by the perception of Kṛṣṇa, the nondual Knowledge, but by a different, māyic perception) is unreal. Therefore all (whatever it may be) that is uttered (spoken) by speech and conceived by the mind is “untrue” (*anṛta*). What, then, in it is “good,” and what is “bad”? (That is, in it there are indeed such mundane) differences as “good” and “bad,” but in the perception of the nondual Truth there is nothing of the kind.

## 177

Only those who look alike on a *brāhmaṇa* endowed with learning and humility and on a *caṇḍāla*, on a cow, an elephant and a dog, are *paṇḍitas*.

## 178

One who is satisfied with knowledge and realisation (*jñāna-vijñāna*), who is *kūṭastha*, that is, established in his spiritual nature, who has conquered the senses and looks alike on a clod of earth, a stone and gold — he alone can be called a “yogī,” that is, “one established in yoga” (*yogārūḍha*).

*Thus ends the fourth chapter of the “Amṛta-pravāha-bhāṣya.”*

[^174-1]: In the verse translation, otherwise: “In the highest spiritual reality there are no mundane notions that divide everything into ‘good’ or ‘bad.’”
