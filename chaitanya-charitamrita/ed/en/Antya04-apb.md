# Amṛta-pravāha-bhāṣya on Antya-līlā 4

## 0

**Summary.** Śrī Sanātana Gosvāmī came alone from Mathurā-maṇḍala to Puruṣottama by the forest path through Jhārikhaṇḍa. On the way, from the bad water and from fasting, his body broke out in oozing sores (*kaṇḍu-rasā*). Tormented by them, he resolved: “Before the Lord I shall give up this body under the wheel of Jagannātha's chariot.” Arriving in Puruṣottama, he stayed at Haridāsa's dwelling. Mahāprabhu was overjoyed to see him. Sanātana Gosvāmī then told the Lord of Anupama's departure from this world on the bank of the Gaṅgā and of his steadfast devotion to Śrī Rāmacandra. One day Mahāprabhu said to Sanātana: “Giving up the body and the like is a quality of darkness (*tamo-dharma*): by giving up the body one cannot attain love for Kṛṣṇa. Abandon this notion born of darkness. You have offered your body to Me, and you have no right to give it up. Through this body of yours I shall propagate many scriptures on *bhakti* and restore the lost holy places of Vṛndāvana.” When Mahāprabhu had left, Haridāsa and Sanātana talked at length. One day the Lord sent for Sanātana, calling him to Yameśvara-ṭoṭā, and he came there along the seashore. When Mahāprabhu questioned him, Sanātana replied: “The servants of Jagannātha come and go by the way past the Lion Gate, so I came over the sand. I did not even notice that blisters had risen on my feet.” These words of Sanātana, upholding due reverence (*maryādā*), pleased the Lord. Fearing that the discharge from his sores would touch the Lord's body, Sanātana kept his distance from Him, yet the Lord would forcibly embrace him all the same. Distressed by this, Sanātana asked Jagadānanda Paṇḍita for advice, and Jagadānanda advised him to go to Vṛndāvana after the Ratha-yātrā. Hearing of this, Mahāprabhu chided Jagadānanda somewhat and showed that Sanātana was superior to him. He also said to Sanātana: “You are a pure devotee, and one should not judge whether your body is good or bad. Above all, I am a *sannyāsī*, and such judgment does not befit Me at all.” Finally He said: “You are the ones I cherish, and I am the one who cherishes you; therefore the discharge from your sores does not disgust Me.” After these exchanges Mahāprabhu embraced Sanātana, and the sores and everything else vanished from his body. The Lord kept Sanātana in Kṣetra that year and (the following year) ordered him to go to Śrī Vṛndāvana. Following that order, Sanātana went to Vṛndāvana by the forest path. Meanwhile Śrī Rūpa Gosvāmī took leave at Mahāprabhu's feet, stayed in Gauḍa for a year, distributed all his wealth among his relatives, the *brāhmaṇas* and the temples, and then went to Vṛndāvana and joined Sanātana. After that Kavirāja Gosvāmī names the books written by Rūpa, Sanātana and Jīva.

When Sanātana came from Vṛndāvana, Śrī Gauracandra in His affection saved him from giving up his body, tested him and made him pure.

## 51

## 61

In separation from Kṛṣṇa a loving devotee wishes to give up his body. But by the very power of that love he attains Kṛṣṇa and cannot give up his body; that is, Kṛṣṇa does not let him die.
