# Anubhāṣya on Ādi-līlā 1

## 0

Mahāprabhu Śrī Caitanya, none other than Rādhā-Kṛṣṇa,
is the life of Rūpa's followers.
Śrī Svarūpa Dāmodara is the delight of Viśvambhara,
and his friends are Rūpa and Sanātana.
Raghunātha, a great soul (*mahājana*) dear to Rūpa, is the treasure of the devotees;
dear to him is the poet Kṛṣṇadāsa.
Narottama, wholly given to service, is dearest of all to Kṛṣṇadāsa;
Viśvanātha longed for his feet.
Viśvanātha is the king of devotees; Jagannātha had faith in him,
and dear to Jagannātha is Bhaktivinoda.
The excellent Śrī Gaurakiśora is the best of the *mahā-bhāgavatas*;
his joy is in serving Hari.
All these are servants of Hari, Gaurāṅga's own.
One who longs for their remnants
is called the servant of the Beloved of Śrī Vārṣabhānavī —
the excellent one, ever devoted to serving Him who is worthy of service.
I hope to serve the servants of Hari and wish devotion to grow,
and I follow the *Pravāha-bhāṣya*.
I have studied the writings of Gaura's associates, and in keeping with them
I write the *Anubhāṣya* by the teaching of Rūpa's followers.

## 1–14

At the very beginning the author wrote fourteen verses as the *maṅgalācaraṇa*, the auspicious invocation. In them he pointed out the subject this book establishes, blessed the listeners and offered them his respects. The first seven chapters of the Ādi-līlā explain exactly this, step by step and in detail.

## 15

> Subheading: Obeisance to his own cherished Deity, the Lord of *sambandha* (relationship with the Lord) —

*Verses composed by the author himself —*

- **paṅgoḥ** — of one who cannot go elsewhere on his own feet, by his own strength.
- **manda-mateḥ** — of one whose small intelligence is absorbed in sense objects; of one who makes no effort in practices driven by other desires, karma, jñāna and the like; of one devoted exclusively (*ekāntin*).
- **mama gatī** — my refuge: such are They for me. *Gati* is “that toward which one goes” (*gamyate*), that is, a shelter.
- **mat-sarvasva-padāmbhojau** — They whose lotus feet are all my wealth.
- **suratau** — merciful; or, boundlessly attached to each other.
- **rādhā-madana-mohanau** — the Deities who bear these names.
- **jayatām** — may They abide in all Their supremacy.

## 16

> Subheading: Obeisance to his own cherished Deity, the Lord of *abhidheya* (the means) —

- **dīvyad-vṛndāraṇya-kalpa-drumādhaḥ** — at the foot of the wish-fulfilling tree in the radiant forest of Vṛndāvana. “Radiant” means supremely beautiful and enchanting to the mind.
- **śrīmad-ratnāgāra-siṁhāsana-sthau** — seated on a jewelled throne within a jewelled chamber of wondrous beauty.
- **preṣṭhālībhiḥ** — by Their dearest companions, devoted to service: the intimate confidantes (*priya-narma-sakhīs*) led by Śrī Lalitā, who are surrounded by Śrī Rūpa-mañjarī and others.
- **sevyamānau** — Those whom they serve.
- **śrī-śrī-rādhā-śrīla-govinda-devau smarāmi** — I remember Śrī Śrī Rādhā and Śrīla Govindadeva.

## 17

> Subheading: Obeisance to his own cherished Deity, the Lord of *prayojana* (the goal) —

- **śrīmān** — He whose form is full of supreme beauty.
- **rāsa-rasārambhī** — He who begins the rapturous *rāsa* dance.
- **veṇu-svanaiḥ** — with the sounds of His flute.
- **gopīḥ** — the gopīs, the young cowherd women of Vraja.
- **karṣan** — drawing. He loosens in them every desire unrelated to Kṛṣṇa and, by the rope of *prema* that is His flute's sound, draws them out of their homes.
- **vaṁśīvaṭa-taṭa-sthitaḥ** — standing at the foot of the Vaṁśīvaṭa tree.
- **gopī-nāthaḥ** — He who sports freely.
- **naḥ** — for us.
- **śriye** — for the wealth of *prema*.
- **astu** — may He be.

## 19

> Subheading: The three Deities whom the Gauḍīyas long to worship —

The Gauḍīya Vaiṣṇavas serve the eighteen-syllable mantra. The Kṛṣṇa named in it is Madana-mohana; its Govinda is Govinda; and its Gopījana-vallabha is Gopīnātha. To realize Kṛṣṇa as Madana-mohana is *sambandha*. To serve Govinda is *abhidheya*. To be drawn by Gopījana-vallabha is *prayojana*. Śrī Mahāprabhu taught these three truths: *sambandha*, *abhidheya* and *prayojana*. These three Deities are forms of Bhagavān that embody those three truths, and They are the presiding Lords of Śrī Vṛndāvana.

The word *gauḍīya* means “of the land of Gauḍa.” The part of Bhārata-varṣa south of the Himālayas and north of the Vindhyas is called Āryāvarta. It holds the five lands of Gauḍa: Sārasvata, Kānyakubja (Lakṣmaṇāvatī), Madhya-gauḍa, Maithila and Utkala. Many call Bengal Gauḍa, and the name “Gauḍa” belonged above all to the capital of Bengal. Devotees from Utkala are called “Oriya devotees,” and in the same way the people of Bengal are called “Gauḍīya.” The south, in turn, is known as Pañca-draviḍa, “the five Draviḍas.” All four ācāryas of the Vaiṣṇava sampradāyas were born in the land of Draviḍa. The Draviḍian Vaiṣṇavas took shelter of these ācāryas. So the word “Gauḍīya” may well have come into use from a wish to be distinguished from them. Śrī Rāmānujācārya was born at Mahābhūtapurī in southern Andhra; Śrī Madhvācārya in the village of Uḍupī in Paraśurāma-kṣetra, in the Mangalore district; Śrī Nimbāditya in the village of Muṅgerapattana in Dakṣiṇāpatha; and Śrī Viṣṇusvāmī in the land of Pāṇḍya. Śrī Mahāprabhu accepted the Śrī Mādhva-sampradāya. Yet the Vaiṣṇava ācāryas of the Tattvavāda branch, who hold to Madhva's teaching, are Draviḍians. Therefore the sampradāya that has taken shelter at Śrī Gaura's feet is called “Gauḍīya.” Moreover, Śrī Ānandatīrtha Pūrṇaprajña Madhvācārya has another name: Śrī Gauḍa-pūrṇānanda. So Śrī Gaura's devotees may be called “Mādhva” as well as “Gauḍīya.”
