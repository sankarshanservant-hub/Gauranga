## 193

“Ah, in the time of *pūrva-rāga*, before We had met, when We looked at each other a feeling arose that is called *rāga*. That *rāga* grew and grew and reached no ‘limit’ (*avadhi*), no measure. That *rāga* is born of the very nature of Us both. It is not that Kṛṣṇa, the enjoyer (*ramaṇa*), is its cause, nor that I, the enjoyed one (*ramaṇī*), am its cause.[^193-1] The *rāga* that arose when We saw each other became Manobhava, that is, Madana; it ground Our hearts together and made them one. Now it is the time of separation. O friend, if you see that Kṛṣṇa has forgotten all those stories of Our love, tell Him this: when We met, We sought no messenger (*dūtī*) and asked no one else for anything — the five arrows of Anaṅga were the go-between of Our meeting. And now, in separation, that *rāga* has become *virāga*, that is, a special *rāga* — the *rāga* of separation, *adhirūḍha-bhāva* — and you, friend, are acting as the messenger! In the love of a noble hero you will see this custom everywhere.”

The meaning is this. In the time of *sambhoga*, *rāga* is a go-between, as it were, in the form of Anaṅga. In the time of *vipralambha* it likewise becomes a messenger filled with *adhirūḍha-bhāva*, and serves as the messenger in *prema-vilāsa-vivarta*, that is, in the appearance of *sambhoga* in the midst of *vipralambha*; and so Śrīmatī addresses it as “friend” (*sakhī*) and speaks these words. The essential meaning is this: in the *sambhoga* of *prema-vilāsa* there is joy, and in *vipralambha* there is the same joy. More than that, in *vipralambha* (because at the summit of service She is absorbed in Kṛṣṇa) — just as one takes a snake for a rope — a special kind of *sambhoga* arises: *adhirūḍha-mahābhāva* overcome by *vivarta*, in which She takes a *tamāla* tree and other things for Kṛṣṇa.

## 194

O king of elephants[^194-1] who dwell in the groves of Govardhana Hill! The Creator, expert in the art of *śṛṅgāra*, melted the lac of Your heart and Rādhikā's heart by the power of the *sāttvika-vikāras* and dispelled the illusion of difference. Then, in the palace of the universe, to increase the wonder of the world, He Himself richly dyed those two hearts with the vermilion of new *rāga*.[^194-2]

## 202–204

Having heard all this, Mahāprabhu said, “The goal (*sādhya*) has been described in full. Now tell Me the *sādhana*, the means of attaining this highest goal.” In reply Rāmānanda Rāya said, “In the *rasas* of *dāsya*, *vātsalya* and the rest this confidential truth cannot be attained. Without the *sakhīs* of Vraja no one can enter these pastimes. If one accepts the mood of a *sakhī* of Vraja and performs *sādhana* following the *sakhīs*, one attains the goal — service to Rādhā and Kṛṣṇa in the groves (*kuñja-sevā*); there is no other means.”

## 205

The *bhāva* of Rādhā and Kṛṣṇa is self-manifest; by nature it is happiness, and it is *vibhu*, that is, unlimited. Even so, without the *sakhīs* it cannot carry the fullness of *rasa* for even a moment — just as the Lord does not attain the fullness of His lordship without His spiritual opulences (*cid-vibhūti*). Which knower of *rasa* who has entered it, then, would not take shelter at the feet of the *sakhīs*?

## 208–209

Śrī Rādhā is Herself Kṛṣṇa's wish-fulfilling creeper of love, and the *sakhīs* are the shoots, flowers and leaves of that creeper. If one takes shelter at the feet of Rādhikā, who is that creeper, and waters the creeper, the shoots and the rest blossom wonderfully. If one waters the shoots themselves, they do not blossom so. In the same way the gopīs are happier from the meeting of Rādhā and Kṛṣṇa than from their own meeting with Kṛṣṇa.

## 210

The *sakhīs* of Vraja are equal to Śrī Rādhā. Śrī Rādhikā is the potency called *hlādinī* of Kṛṣṇa, the moon for the night-lilies of Vraja, and the *sakhīs* are like the young leaves, shoots, flowers and so on of the creeper of Her *prema*, the essence of that potency. When Rādhikā, full of the highest joy, is watered with the many nectarean *rasas* of Kṛṣṇa's pastimes, the *sakhīs* become a hundred times more joyful than from being watered themselves — and there is nothing surprising in this.[^210-1]

## 219–220

*Vaidhī-bhakti* consists of the sixty-four limbs of *bhajana*; pure faith (*śraddhā*) in it alone gives one the right to it. Seeing the natural *rāga* of the people of Vraja for Kṛṣṇa, some are filled with greed (*lobha*) to walk that path, and this very greed for the nectar of the gopīs' feeling gives them the right to the path of *rāgānugā*. In *bhajana* on the path of *rāgānugā*, giving up attachment to Vedic *dharma* — *varṇāśrama* and the rest — is naturally necessary.

## 221–222

In Vraja, Kṛṣṇa's servants — Raktaka, Patraka and others; Kṛṣṇa's friends — Śrīdāmā, Subala and others; Kṛṣṇa's parents — Nanda, Yaśodā and others — serve Kṛṣṇa, each in his own *bhāva*, his own *rasa*. When a person turns to worship in the *rasas* of Vraja and greed awakens in him for one particular *rasa* among them, he attains a spiritual nature (*cit-svarūpa*) fit for that *bhāva*, and at the time of perfection (*siddhi*) he attains Kṛṣṇa. The Upaniṣads, that is, the Śrutis, are an example of this. The Śrutis saw that without following the gopīs one cannot obtain the right to serve Kṛṣṇa in Vraja. Then they accepted the gopīs' guidance and, on the path of *rāga*, in the bodies of gopīs, worshiped Vrajendranandana.

## 223

The sages, restraining the breath by *prāṇāyāma* and firmly joining mind and senses in *yoga*, worshiped Brahman in the heart, and the enemies of Bhagavān, by the power of constant thought of Him, also entered that Brahman. The women of Vraja, their minds overcome by the strong poison — the beauty of Śrī Kṛṣṇa's arms, like the body of a serpent — attained the nectar of His lotus feet. We too, having obtained those bodies of gopīs, drank the nectar of His lotus feet in the mood of the gopīs.[^223-1]

## 224–225

In the fourth line of the verse, the word *sama-dṛśaḥ* is explained as “following the gopīs' feeling,” and the word *samāḥ* as the Śrutis' “attaining the bodies of gopīs.” The word *aṅghri-saroja-sudhā* is explained as “the bliss of association with Kṛṣṇa.”

## 226

Yaśodā's son, Bhagavān Śrī Kṛṣṇa, is easily attained by embodied beings who possess *bhakti*, but not so by *jñānīs* who have become one with the self (*ātma-bhūta*).[^226-1]

## 244–256

From the verse beginning “*prabhu kahe, — ‘kon vidyā…*’” up to the verse “*sthāvara deha, deva-deha yaiche avasthiti*,” in each verse the first line is the Lord's question and the second line is Rāya's answer. This conversation is found in the *Caitanya-candrodaya-nāṭaka* (Act 7).

## 265

We meditate on Śrī Kṛṣṇa, the supreme Truth, who by His own potency is eternally free of all deception. It is known for certain that from this Truth come the birth, maintenance and dissolution of the universe. When one reasons by *anvaya-vyatireka*, He is found in all things, or affairs, to be the one supreme *jña-tattva*, the knowing Truth, that is, the *svarūpa-tattva*. In the visible world He alone is *svarāṭ*, that is, the independent King. As the indwelling Lord He taught the truth of Brahman to Brahmā, the first poet. In Him intelligent scholars are bewildered again and again. In Him is the interchange, that is, the separate existence, of the many elements — fire, water, earth and the rest. In Him the three kinds of creation exist as real: creation as the rising of spirit, creation as the manifestation of the *jīvas*, and creation as the universes of *māyā*.[^265-1]

## 267–273

“Lord, at first I saw You as a *sannyāsī*, and now I see You as a dark cowherd boy. And before You I also see a golden doll.[^267-1] That doll has covered Your whole body with its golden radiance, and yet Your own color seems to show plainly; and Your lovely eyes tremble with many feelings. Lord, tell me frankly the cause of this wonderful state of Yours.” The Lord said, “Those who have deep love for Kṛṣṇa are the best of *bhāgavatas* (*bhāgavatottama*). The nature of their love is this: whatever they see, moving or nonmoving, they do not see its form, but everywhere see only the presence of Śrī Kṛṣṇa — the manifestation of their worshipable Lord (*iṣṭadeva*).”

## 274

The *bhāgavatottama* sees in all beings Bhagavān Śrī Kṛṣṇacandra, the Self of the self, and sees all beings in Śrī Kṛṣṇa, the Self of the self.

## 275

The forest creepers rich with flowers and fruits, and the trees bowed down by *bhāva* — the great trees of the forest, their bodies thrilling with love — revealed Kṛṣṇa within themselves and poured down streams of honey.

## 281

Śrī Kṛṣṇa in the form of Rasarāja and Śrīmatī Rādhikā in the form of Mahābhāva, united, are one *tattva*. It was this *svarūpa* that He showed, that is, He showed “the form of Śrī Kṛṣṇa adorned with Rādhā's *bhāva* and radiance.” In this way He showed a wondrous *svarūpa* in which one *tattva* is two, and two *tattvas* are one. Only those who are able to understand the truth of Śrī Kṛṣṇa Caitanya and the truth of Rādhā and Kṛṣṇa are granted, by the mercy of Śrī Svarūpa Gosvāmī, to serve that eternal *svarūpa*.
