> Subheading: The Lord's teaching to the people: the *jīva* is not “Kṛṣṇa,” and therefore to think of a *jīva* as Kṛṣṇa is forbidden —

## 112

> Subheading: The difference between the *jīva* and Kṛṣṇa —

## 111–113

*Jaṅgama-nārāyaṇa*: Nārāyaṇa endowed with the power to move[^111-113-1]. “As soon as a man accepts the *daṇḍa*, he becomes Nārāyaṇa,” say the *kevalādvaita-vādī* Māyāvādīs, and they greet *daṇḍīs* with the words “*oṁ namo nārāyaṇāya*.” But the *jīva*, whether liberated or conditioned, is in every state eternally subordinate to Nārāyaṇa, the Supreme Lord, the master of *māyā*. He therefore can never be called by the word “Nārāyaṇa.” Whoever says or thinks that the *jīva* is “equal” to Viṣṇu or “one” with Him is a Māyāvādī and an offender.

See Ādi 2.96.

## 114

> Subheading: Kṛṣṇa is the Lord (*Īśvara*), the *jīva* is subordinate to Him — (words of the *Sarvajña-sūkta* cited in the *Bhagavat-sandarbha*, or words of Śrī Viṣṇusvāmī quoted by Śrīdhara Svāmī in his commentary on *Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam* 1.7.5–6)

**īśvaraḥ** (Śrī Kṛṣṇa) **sac-cid-ānandaḥ** (the possessor of the *sandhinī*, *saṁvit* and *hlādinī* potencies) **hlādinyā** (by the *hlādinī* potency — that by which Bhagavān enjoys the special bliss of His own nature, and by which He gives others a taste of that bliss) **saṁvidā** (by the potency of consciousness, which is the very nature of nondual knowledge) **āśliṣṭaḥ** (embraced); **jīvaḥ tu** **svāvidyā-saṁvṛtaḥ** (completely covered by ignorance — by *māyā*, the potency of Bhagavān Himself that bewilders the conditioned *jīvas*) **saṁkleśa-nikarākaraḥ** (a mine of manifold suffering; suffering is of three kinds, according to the rule “Suffering is threefold: sin, its seed, and ignorance”[^114-1], and the *jīva* is the quarry, the mine, of their whole mass).

## 115

> Subheading: To regard the *jīva* and Nārāyaṇa as equal is itself godlessness (*pāṣaṇḍatā*) —

*Pāṣaṇḍī*: see Ādi 3.78. A *pāṣaṇḍī* is one who considers Śrī Viṣṇu — the master of *māyā*, whose form is pure *sattva* and consciousness — to be “one” with the *jīva*, who is subject to *māyā*, or with some material object made of *māyā*, or who considers them equal. The *Bhakti-sandarbha* uses the word *pāṣaṇḍa* in many places. In section 255, where the offenses against the holy name are described, it says, in describing one of them — blasphemy of the *śruti* and *śāstra*: “…as, for example, among the *pāṣaṇḍīs*, the worshippers of Dattātreya and Ṛṣabhadeva, who follow the path of *pāṣaṇḍa*.” Again, in describing another offense — the idea of “I” and “mine,” that is, identifying oneself with the body: “By the word *pāṣaṇḍa* arising from the body, wealth and so on, the ten offenses themselves are meant, for they are made of godlessness.” Again, in section 223: “One who offers oblations and gives gifts for the sake of the gods, or who acts on his own authority in his deeds, should be known as a *pāṣaṇḍī*”; that is, to be a *pāṣaṇḍī* means simply to fall from the Vaiṣṇava path. Again, in section 179, following the *Viṣṇu-dharma*, it relates how Uparicara Vasu, a devotee of Viṣṇu, delivered the *pāṣaṇḍī* demons. It describes how, at the order of Śukra, the guru of the Daityas, the *pāṣaṇḍīs* tried to instruct Uparicara Vasu in the path of *pāṣaṇḍa*, and how he nevertheless remained unshaken. And again, in section 153, describing *arthavāda* about the holy name of Hari and offenses against Vaiṣṇavas: “And we hear that such an offense paralyzes devotion… The *pāṣaṇḍas* who, out of greed for the body and so on, are guilty of the ten offenses, beginning with disregard for the guru…” The godlessness of the *pāṣaṇḍī* is described in many verses: *Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam* 4.2.28, 30, 32; 5.6.9; 12.2.13, 43, and others.

## 116

> Subheading: Evidence from the *śāstra* — (words of the *Vaiṣṇava-tantra*)

**yaḥ** (an unfortunate man) **tu** (in the sense of censure) **nārāyaṇaṁ devam** (Bhagavān Viṣṇu, the Lord of Brahmā and Rudra, whom both of them worship) **brahma-rudrādi-daivataiḥ** (with the gods — the four-headed one, the five-headed one and others, who are servants of Nārāyaṇa and *jīvas* by nature) **samatvena** (as equal: the eternal Master with His eternal servants called gods) **vīkṣeta** (sees), **saḥ** **dhruvam** (certainly) **pāṣaṇḍī** **bhavet** — for the *Padma Purāṇa* says: “One who thinks of the Deity of Viṣṇu as stone, of the guru as an ordinary man, of a Vaiṣṇava as belonging to a caste, of the water that has washed the feet of Viṣṇu or of the Vaiṣṇavas, which destroys the filth of Kali, as mere water, of the name of Śrī Viṣṇu, the mantra that destroys all sins, as an ordinary sound, and of Viṣṇu, the Lord of all lords, as equal to others — he is a denizen of hell.”
