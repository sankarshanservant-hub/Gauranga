## 72

> Subheading: The Lord indicates the marks of the “intermediate Vaiṣṇava” —

Know the Vaiṣṇava on whose lips the name of Śrī Kṛṣṇa sounds “constantly” to be better than “the junior (*kaniṣṭha*) Vaiṣṇava of tender faith, who utters the name of Kṛṣṇa only once” — that is, know him to be an “intermediate *bhāgavata*” (*madhyama-bhāgavata*), and serve his feet. In the *Upadeśāmṛta*, with the words *praṇatibhiś ca bhajantam īśam*, Śrī Rūpa Gosvāmī instructs *bhāgavatas* of the *madhyama-adhikārī* level to deal with one another by offering “obeisances.”

“Constantly” (*nirantara*): *antara* means “interval,” and *nirantara* is that in which there is no interval. The interval, or obstruction, is the absence of activity of the conscious faculty (*cetana-vṛtti*) in the form of other desires (*anyābhilāṣa*), *karma*, *jñāna* and laxity (*śaithilya*) — that is, inertness (*jāḍya*). As Śrī Rūpa Prabhu says: “The highest devotion is the cultivation of service to Kṛṣṇa in a favourable spirit, free from other desires and not covered by *jñāna*, *karma* and the like.” (*Bhakti-rasāmṛta-sindhu*, Pūrva-vibhāga, first *laharī*) Or else, by the word *antara* are meant: “the body” (*deha*), that is, sense gratification; “wealth” (*draviṇa*), the endeavour to amass money impurely; “people” (*janatā*), the company of the unworthy, that is, bad company; “greed” (*lobha*), greed of the tongue, or covetousness; and godlessness (*pāṣaṇḍatā*). Godlessness is to regard the form of Viṣṇu as “matter” — stone, wood, gold, brass and so on; the guru as a “mortal”; the Vaiṣṇava as belonging to a “caste” or as “earthly”; the water that has washed the feet of Viṣṇu and the Vaiṣṇava as ordinary “water”; the name and mantra of Viṣṇu, or the name given by the genuine guru, as “sound like any worldly sound.” Godlessness is also to place Viṣṇu, the Lord of all lords, or Śrī Kṛṣṇa — the original form (*svayaṁ-rūpa*), the Truth higher than Viṣṇu — and Their own potencies on a level with the other gods, who depend on the three modes. In the end it is the endeavour to realise the self, the conscious, by relying on the non-self, the inert, or to derive it from the inert; it is the knowledge that places the transcendental Reality on a level with worldly, partial things measurable by the senses; or, in other words, to regard Śrī Hari, guru and Vaiṣṇava with a dualistic mind as “others.” All this gives rise to offences. Śrī Jīva Prabhu says in the *Bhakti-sandarbha* (*anuccheda* 265): “In the verse ‘He on whose tongue there is even one name, in whose memory it is…’ and so on, the ten offences are indicated by the word *pāṣaṇḍa* connected with body, wealth and the like, for they all are godlessness.” Further: “One who has love (*prema*) for the Lord, friendship for those dependent on Him, mercy for the ignorant and indifference towards the hostile is a *madhyama* (an intermediate devotee).” (*Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam* 11.2.46) And in the instructions to Sanātana (Madhya 22): “One who has faith is qualified for devotion. According to faith, devotees are ‘highest,’ ‘intermediate’ and ‘junior.’ <…> One who does not know the reasoning of the scriptures well but whose faith is firm is a ‘*madhyama-adhikārī*,’ most fortunate. <…> Devotees are ranked higher and higher according to the degree of their *rati* and *prema*.” (*Caitanya-caritāmṛta*, Madhya 22.64, 66, 68)

In the intermediate *bhāgavata*, love (*prīti*) for Śrī Nāma grows. Therefore, worshipping Śrī Nāma constantly with the highest love in the sacrifice of *kīrtana*, he directs his *prema* to Bhagavān. Constantly filled with love for the transcendental Śrī Nāma and engaged in its cultivation, he can understand himself as a “transcendental servant of Kṛṣṇa” (*kṛṣṇa-dāsa*). Moreover, at times he shows mercy to a devotee whose taste for Śrī Nāma is comparatively small, explaining to him his transcendental nature (*svarūpa*). But the hostile person, wholly devoid of love for the pure devotee and for Bhagavān, he regards as “devoid of realisation of Kṛṣṇa’s transcendental nature, with his conscious faculty covered, entirely worldly,” and gives up his company. The intermediate *adhikārī* can also understand the components, that is, the paraphernalia, of pure devotion as “transcendental.”

## 74

> Subheading: The Lord indicates the marks of the *uttama-adhikārī*, or *mahā-bhāgavata* —

Know the Vaiṣṇava at whose sight the name of Kṛṣṇa comes naturally to the viewer’s lips to be a *mahā-bhāgavata*, perfect in his very nature (*svarūpa-siddha*). The activity of his consciousness is fully awakened, kindled, uncovered; in other words, he is wholly engaged in serving Kṛṣṇa in pure, unmixed *prema*. Therefore he always remains in the waking state. Endowed with knowledge and realisation of Bhagavān (*jñāna* and *vijñāna*), he sees Kṛṣṇa, or what belongs to Kṛṣṇa, everywhere; from his holy mouth the pure name of Śrī Kṛṣṇa constantly and beautifully resounds. Himself possessed of divine vision, he is able to open the closed eyes of ignorance of other *jīvas* who sleep the sleep of delusion — forgetfulness of Kṛṣṇa, that is, aversion to Kṛṣṇa. In other words, he can free them from inertness, give them divine vision, awaken the activity of their consciousness and engage them forever in the service of Kṛṣṇa and of what belongs to Kṛṣṇa. Sayings such as “Each of them holds the power to deliver the universe” and “Until a touchstone turns iron into gold by its touch, no one can recognise it as a touchstone” (*Caitanya-caritāmṛta*, Madhya 6.279) are spoken of just such devotees. Śrī Rūpa Gosvāmī writes in the *Upadeśāmṛta*: “And one who is expert in *bhajana*, exclusively devoted, and whose heart is free from blaming others and the like should be honoured with service, for his company is the gain one longs for.” He follows completely the verse *tṛṇād api sunīcena*, spoken by the Lord’s holy mouth. According to Madhya 9.36–37, he is Kṛṣṇa in the form of the shelter (*āśraya*), that is, a *mahā-bhāgavata*; he alone performs pure Hari-*kīrtana*. Therefore the intermediate *bhāgavata* should always long to hear from just such a person — one who does not see high and low in the material world, whose heart is free from blaming others — and satisfy him by every kind of service. Then at last, by the power of his mercy, that *madhyama-adhikārī* will gain the good fortune to become an *uttama-adhikārī*. “He who sees in all beings the love for Bhagavān that lives in his own soul, and sees all beings in Bhagavān, the Soul of all, is the best of *bhāgavatas*.” (*Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam* 11.2.45) In the instructions to Sanātana (Madhya 22): “One who has faith is qualified for devotion. According to faith, devotees are ‘highest,’ ‘intermediate’ and ‘junior.’ One who is expert in the reasoning of the scriptures and whose faith is firm is an ‘*uttama-adhikārī*’; he delivers the world.” (*Caitanya-caritāmṛta*, Madhya 22.64–65) Towards the three realities — “Bhagavān,” “devotion” and “the devotee” — the *mahā-bhāgavata* has a transcendental, unrestricted vision full of *prema*; apart from this he has no other vision: “he knows only this: everyone worships Kṛṣṇa.” Therefore he is one whom Kṛṣṇa Himself has accepted as His own.

> Subheading: Bhagavān breaks His own promise to keep His devotee’s promise — (*Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam*, canto 1, chapter 9, verse 37)

Bhīṣmadeva, a *mahā-bhāgavata* who could choose the hour of his own passing, had described *bhāgavata-dharma* and the other, general *dharmas* to the Pāṇḍavas headed by Yudhiṣṭhira, who had come with Śrī Kṛṣṇa. Then, seeing that the time of *uttarāyaṇa* had come and understanding that his departure was near, he offers prayers to Śrī Kṛṣṇa, who stands before him:

**sva-nigamam** (His own promise: “I shall protect the Pāṇḍavas without taking up weapons”) **apahāya** (giving up) **mat-pratijñāṁ** (my promise, the resolve “I shall make Śrī Kṛṣṇa take up a weapon”) **ṛtam** (true) **adhi** (all the more) **kartum** (to make) **ratha-sthaḥ** (standing on the chariot) **avaplutaḥ** (suddenly getting down) **dhṛta-ratha-caraṇaḥ** (He who seized a chariot wheel) **calad-guḥ** (He by whose fury the earth trembled) **gatottarīyaḥ** (He whose upper cloth, by that same fury, fell on the road) **ibham** (an elephant) **hantum** (to kill) **hariḥ** (a lion) **iva** (like) **abhyayāt** (rushed forward; it connects with the next verse: “may He be my Lord”).

## 150

> Subheading: At Yājapura the Lord bids farewell to the two *mahāpātras* —

Yājapura is a subdivision of Kaṭaka district, situated on the southern bank of the river Vaitaraṇī. The place got the name “Yājapura” from the sacrifices (*yajña*) that the sages performed on the left bank; according to some, the name “Yājapura” comes from “Yayāti-nagara.” The *Mahābhārata*, Vana-parva, chapter 114: “Here are the Kaliṅgas, O son of Kuntī, where the river Vaitaraṇī flows. Here even Dharma, taking shelter of the gods, offered sacrifice to them. Here too in ancient times the sages and others worshipped with sacrifices.” There are countless forms of the gods here; among them the form of Śrī Varāhadeva is especially worshipped. Worshippers of Śakti worship the Mothers — Vārāhī, Vaiṣṇavī, Indrāṇī and others. There are also many forms of Śiva and the Daśāśvamedha-ghāṭa. This place is also called Nābhi-gayā, Virajā-kṣetra and so on.

> Subheading: At Remuṇā (?) the Lord lets Rāya go —

“From there”: another reading has “from Bhadraka.” If “from there” is taken to mean “from Remuṇā,” it disagrees with the reading of Madhya 1.149: “Rāmānanda Rāya accompanied Him as far as Bhadraka.” Some hold that Remuṇā then belonged to the Bhadraka district, but there is no evidence for this. Others hold that in the verse just mentioned “Remuṇā” is the right reading in place of “Bhadraka.” It seems more likely, however, that Rāya turned back from Bhadraka. Bhadraka lies four *yojanas* south of Bāleśvara, and Remuṇā about half a *yojana* (5 miles) to its west.

Saptagrāma is an ancient port and town on the bank of the Sarasvatī river, near Triśabighā railway station on the E.I.R. line, in Hugalī district. In 1592 the Pathans plundered it, and in 1632, when the Sarasvatī’s current was blocked, this very ancient port was all but ruined. It is said that even in the seventeenth and eighteenth centuries Portuguese sailors came here by ship to trade. In the southern Bengal of those days Saptagrāma was famous as a prominent and prosperous town. Here lived the two brothers Hiraṇya and Govardhana, masters of enormous wealth. At that time they collected twelve *lakhs* of coins in revenue each year. See the first part of the *Anubhāṣya* on Ādi 11.41, on Uddhāraṇa Datta.

## 238

*Markaṭa-vairāgya* (monkey renunciation). Monkeys, though bent on enjoyment, seem to live without houses or clothes, and so they may be taken as the “equals” of men endowed with detachment; yet they never stop gratifying their senses. Such renunciation “for show” is called *markaṭa-vairāgya*. There is a renunciation that does not arise from pure *bhakti*, born together with it, but springs from the frustration of desires or longings for things other than Kṛṣṇa. It does not remain for life as a support of pure *bhakti* but proves “momentary,” or *phalgu*; this is *śmaśāna-vairāgya* (cremation-ground renunciation), or *markaṭa-vairāgya*. But if a person, for the sake of service to Kṛṣṇa, merely accepts what is strictly unavoidable among worldly things and lives without absorption in them, he does not fall under the sway of the fruits of *karma*. The words of the *Nārada Purāṇa* cited in the *Bhakti-rasāmṛta-sindhu* (Pūrva-vibhāga, 2nd Laharī): “One who knows the true goal should accept [worldly things] only as much as is needed to maintain himself. By taking more or less, he falls away from the highest goal.” In his commentary *Durgama-saṅgamanī* Śrī Jīva Prabhu explains the word *sva-nirvāhaḥ* (“maintaining oneself”) in this verse as *sva-sva-bhakti-nirvāhaḥ*, “maintaining one’s own *bhakti*.” Further, in the *Bhakti-rasāmṛta-sindhu* (Pūrva-vibhāga, 2nd Laharī, 125 and 126),[^238-1] on *phalgu-vairāgya*: “When those who seek liberation reject things connected with Hari, regarding them as worldly, that renunciation is called *phalgu* (worthless).” That is: “To reject as ‘worldly’ what is favourable to service of Śrī Hari is a mistake.” On *yukta-vairāgya*: “When one uses worldly things as is fitting, without attachment, and holds firmly to their connection with Kṛṣṇa, that is called *yukta-vairāgya*.” That is: “Worldly things, free of attachment and in their connection [with Kṛṣṇa], are all Mādhava.”

## 239

Show people the conduct that society ordinarily considers proper, while in your heart you give up absorption in material things and, fixed in Me, serve Bhagavān with devotion. If you serve Kṛṣṇa in this way with a guileless heart, Kṛṣṇa Himself will free you from the bonds of *saṁsāra*. The words “Be steadfast within, and outwardly behave as people do” simply explain the earlier words “Enjoy worldly things as is fitting, without attachment.” *Niṣṭhā* means steadfastness in Kṛṣṇa, that is, abiding resolutely in unmotivated cultivation of Kṛṣṇa, having given up desire and thirst for anything other than Kṛṣṇa. “Behave as people do”: the *Pañcarātra* text cited in the *Bhakti-rasāmṛta-sindhu* (Pūrva-vibhāga, 2nd Laharī): “One who desires *bhakti*, O sage, should perform only those acts, worldly or Vedic, that are favourable to service of Hari.”

## 280–281

Śrīman Mahāprabhu had already revealed His own mood among His associates while dancing before the chariot. Śrī Mahāprabhu’s heart itself is Vṛndāvana, the land of Śrī Rādhākānta’s pastimes. Even so, to teach people, He goes to the earthly (*bhauma*) Vṛndāvana manifested in the material world. Ordinary people, whose vision is mundane and who are intoxicated with sense enjoyment, think that the earthly Vṛndāvana is not transcendental: that it is a particular place like any other material land, which even senses bent on enjoyment can reach with the help of money, the means of enjoyment. Just as association with other such material things gives rise to material moods, so, if one goes to see “Vṛndāvana” (?) with the idea that it is an object of sense enjoyment, or with material intelligence, one gains no spiritual, eternal good — that is, service to Śrī Kṛṣṇa, the nondual Truth. This is proved by Śrī Mahāprabhu’s words “My mind is Vṛndāvana” (Madhya 13.137) and by the *Bhāgavatam* verse *āhuś ca te* (“They say that Your [lotus feet]…”).[^280-281-1] (*Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam* 10.84.8)[^280-281-2]: “One who takes this corpse-like body made of three elements for the self, who takes his wife and others for his own, who sees the object of worship in what is earthly and the holy place in water, but never in those who know the truth — he is like an ox or an ass.” In reality Mahāprabhu enacted the pastimes of travelling to Vṛndāvana, the Lord’s transcendental place of pastimes, and of seeing it, only to teach materialistic people. If a conditioned soul forgets this and takes Vṛndāvana to be one of the “places of enjoyment” of the material world, he will be going against Mahāprabhu’s teaching. The *prākṛta-sahajiyās* form their own idea of the holy *dhāma*, pride themselves on being “Vrajavāsīs” or “*dhāma-vāsīs*,” and proclaim it. In fact, instead of living in the spiritual Vṛndāvana, they live in dreadful *saṁsāra*, the field of their own sense gratification, and only multiply the rubbish. Pure *bhāgavatas* have no such mood or idea. Śrī Dāmodara Svarūpa is an eternal resident of Vraja, yet nothing is heard in his life of his going to the earthly Vṛndāvana. Nor does any book record such a journey by Śrī Puṇḍarīka Vidyānidhi, Śrī Haridāsa Ṭhākura, Śrīvāsa Paṇḍita, Śrī Śivānanda Sena, Śrī Rāmānanda Rāya, Śrī Śikhi Māhiti, Śrī Mādhavī Devī, Śrī Gadādhara Paṇḍita Gosvāmī and others. On the other hand, people commonly tell of many *prākṛta-sahajiyās*, *karmīs*, *jñānīs* and those with other desires — all devoid of pure *bhakti* — living in the earthly Vṛndāvana, seeing it or going there. Residence in the holy *dhāma* may give those without *bhakti* heaven or liberation, or the fruits that are won by sin, piety or renunciation; but only the pure devotee, endowed with the divine, spotless eyes meant in the verse *premāñjana-cchurita-bhakti-vilocanena* (“with eyes of devotion tinged with the salve of love”),[^280-281-3] truly and really lives in the transcendental holy *dhāma* Vṛndāvana. In a later age Ṭhākura Narottama in the village of Kheturi and Śrīnivāsa Ācārya in the village of Yājigrāma, and in the age after them Śrī Jagannātha dāsa Bābājī Mahārāja in Gauḍa-deśa, Śrī Bhagavān dāsa in Kālnā, Śrīmad Gaurakiśora dāsa Bābājī Mahārāja in Navadvīpa-dhāma, Śrī Śrīmad Bhaktivinoda Ṭhākura in Calcutta and other devotees wholly devoted to the holy name certainly never lived in any *dhāma* other than Śrī Vṛndāvana.
