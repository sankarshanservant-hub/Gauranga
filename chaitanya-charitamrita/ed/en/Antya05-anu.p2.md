## 97

> Subheading: The only cause of the magnanimous Lord's anger is a contradiction of the message of *bhakti-siddhānta* —

***Rasābhāsa***: “Rasas that lack [any of] the characteristics of *rasa* set out earlier are called *rasābhāsas* by the knowers of *rasa*. They are of three kinds — *uparasas*, *anurasas* and *aparasas* — and are called, in that order, the highest, the middling and the lowest. The twelve *rasas* beginning with *śānta* become *uparasas* when the *sthāyi-bhāva*, the *vibhāvas*, the *anubhāvas* and the rest in them are distorted. The seven *rasas* beginning with *hāsya*, and *śānta* as well, are considered *anurasas* when their *vibhāvas* — devotees and the rest — have no relation to Kṛṣṇa. And if Kṛṣṇa and His opponents become the object (*viṣaya*) and the abode (*āśraya*) of *hāsya* and the other *rasas*, the wise call them *aparasas*. All *bhāvas* have their semblances, and some of them are *rasābhāsas*; yet the knowers of *rasa* call all of them *rasas*, because they are relished.” (*Bhakti-rasāmṛta-sindhu*, Uttara-vibhāga, 9th Laharī)

When something at first appears to be *rasa* but, judged by the characteristics of *rasa* described earlier, is incomplete, the *rasikas* call it *rasābhāsa*. *Rasābhāsa* is divided into *uparasa*, *anurasa* and *aparasa*, which are called, respectively, the highest, the middling and the lowest. The twelve *rasas* beginning with *śānta*, in which the *sthāyi-bhāva*, *vibhāva*, *anubhāva* and the rest are distorted, are called *uparasa*. The seven *rasas* beginning with *hāsya*, produced by *vibhāvas* — devotees and the rest — that have no relation to Kṛṣṇa, together with dry *śānta-rasa*, are called *anurasa*. If Kṛṣṇa and His opponents, the *asuras*, filled with mutually hostile feelings, become the object and the abode of *hāsya* and the other *rasas*, the scholars who know *rasa-tattva* call this *aparasa*. Some call the *bhāvas* *tad-ābhāsa* (their semblance) or *rasābhāsa*; but those expert in *rasa-tattva* call all of these *rasa*, because they are sweet to taste and give joy. Śrī Viśvanātha Cakravartī says: “If two mutually hostile [*rasas*] are joined, that is *rasābhāsa*.” That is, when two opposed *rasas* are joined, *rasābhāsa* arises.

***Siddhānta-virodha*** — contradiction of the *siddhānta* of the path of *bhakti*; contradiction of *tattva*.

## 117

> Subheading: The cause of the anger: (1) regarding Viṣṇu as a *jīva* leads to hell —

## 118

Someone may see in the Deity of Jagannātha only a wooden image (*pratimā*) and think Him perishable, made of material substances, a mere inert object. By the word of the *Padma Purāṇa* — “One who regards the worshipable Viṣṇu as stone … is a dweller in hell” — whoever thinks this way commits an offense. For the Deity (*arcā-vigraha*) is completely nondifferent from Śrī Kṛṣṇa, whose form is eternity, knowledge and bliss, and the devotee of Bhagavān, with eyes of *bhakti* lit by the bliss of *prema*, sees Him directly as the complete form of eternity, knowledge and bliss.

## 119

From the words of the *śruti*, “As sparks fly up from a fire …,”[^119-1] we learn that the *jīva* is like a spark of the fire that is the great Viṣṇu; that is, the *jīva* is a particle of spirit (*cit-kaṇa*). A *jīva* under the sway of *māyā* is capable of being bound by matter. But Śrī Caitanyadeva is Parameśvara, whose form is eternity, knowledge and bliss, the cause of all causes; and His having taken a human form does not mean that He is a tiny *jīva* subject to matter. He is the Master of *māyā*, full of the six opulences, Svayaṁ Bhagavān, the son of Yaśodā. (*Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam* 1.11.39): “This is the lordship of the Lord: though He abides within *prakṛti*, He is not bound by its *guṇas*, though they are ever about Him — just as the intelligence that has taken shelter of Him is not bound by them.”

## 120

> Subheading: The audacity of one who, relying on sense-born knowledge and logic and ignorant of *bhakti-siddhānta*, sets out to describe Kṛṣṇa —

***Dui-ṭhāñi*** (in two places) — Śrī Jagannāthadeva and Śrī Caitanyadeva. The poet took Them both to belong to the material world, as matter and as a *jīva*: he supposed that the particle of spirit residing in the material body of the One had entered the material body of the other. Hence the offense lies in two places. ***Atattva-jña*** — one who has not grasped *tattva*, that is, a Māyāvādī *ahaṅgrahopāsaka* (who worships himself as God), a *karmī* who enjoys the fruits of his work, or a self-willed person devoted to the senses, who cannot tell the real from the unreal. ***Tattva varṇe*** — describes *tattva*, that is, what concerns Bhagavān.

## 121

> Subheading: (2) The blunder (*pramāda*) is an offense: positing in Īśvara a difference between the body and the embodied one —

If one regards Īśvara as one does a bound *jīva* and holds His body and His embodied self (*dehī*) to be different things, that is an “offense.” In the material world a bound *jīva* has a bodily existence fashioned by *guṇa-māyā*, and a *jīva*'s self-awareness fashioned by *jīva-māyā*, the marginal potency (*taṭastha-śakti*). The difference between Īśvara and the bound *jīva* is this. Īśvara bestows the fruits of *karma* and rules over them; that is, He is the cause of all causes, the Master of *māyā*, the Lord (*prabhu*) and the all-pervading truth (*vibhu-tattva*). The *jīva*, in the bound state, enjoys the fruits of *karma* and is subject to them, and even in the liberated state, in his own true nature, he is engaged in serving Īśvara. In other words, Īśvara is never under the control of *māyā*, whereas the *jīva* is liable to fall under it. Īśvara is immeasurable, undivided consciousness; the *jīva* is measurable, fragmentary consciousness.

The perishable, transient body of a bound *jīva* is *māyic*, that is, material. The transcendental body of a liberated, pure *jīva* is eternal; and Īśvara, who is beyond *māyā*, is also an eternal form endowed with features (*saviśeṣa-vigraha*). Though His eternal form appears in the world by the power of His own inconceivable potency, it is never *māyic* or material and never bears the qualities of this world. (*Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam* 1.11.39): “This is the lordship of the Lord: though He abides within *prakṛti*, He is not bound by its *guṇas*, though they are ever about Him — just as the intelligence that has taken shelter of Him is not bound by them.” To try to separate the body from the embodied one on the pretext of making the eternal form “featureless” (*nirviśeṣa*) is a work of grave offense.

## 122

> Subheading: The name, form and essential nature of Viṣṇu, the nondual Knowledge, are one —

The nondual Knowledge (*advaya-jñāna*) is Kṛṣṇa, the son of the king of Vraja. In determining the nature of the Truth, the verse *vadanti tat tattva-vidaḥ*[^122-1] refutes the idea of duality in the one Parameśvara, who has no second. He is the nondual Knowledge; therefore His name, form, qualities and pastimes are not distinct from one another as the things of the material world are — they must be known as absolutely “nondifferent.” It is precisely by seeing in Īśvara a difference between body and embodied self that one mistakes Him for a “bound *jīva*,” for a bound *jīva* lacks the perception of the nondual Knowledge.
