## 129

> Subheading: The source of the avatāras is called by the names of various avatāras —

## 126–132

In the *Laghu-bhāgavatāmṛta*, Śrī Rūpa Prabhu first refutes the opponent's views (*pūrvapakṣa*): “Kṛṣṇa is an avatāra of Kṣīraśāyī,” “Kṛṣṇa is an avatāra of Vāsudeva, the first *vyūha* of Nārāyaṇa, the Lord of Paravyoma,” “Kṛṣṇa is a *vilāsa* of Nārāyaṇa,” and so on. Then (in sections 136, 137, and 139) he quotes *Bhāgavatam* 3.2.15 and says: “When His own peaceful forms — Vasudeva and the other devotees — were being oppressed by distorted forms, that is, by fearsome-looking demons led by Kaṁsa, then Śrī Kṛṣṇa, the Lord of spirit and matter, supremely merciful and though unborn, joined and merged with His *vilāsas* — the Lord of Vaikuṇṭha and others — and descended from Kṛṣṇaloka into the material world, just as fire appears from the wooden *araṇi* when it is churned.” The *vyūha* of Śrī Kṛṣṇa became one with His own *vilāsa*, the *vyūha* of the Lord of Paravyoma, came into the material world, and appeared there. He always remains united with His famous avatāras: the Puruṣas and others, Śrī Rāma, Nṛsiṁha, Varāha, Vāmana, Nara-Nārāyaṇa, Hayagrīva, Ajita, and the rest. That is why in Vṛndāvana too the pastimes of all these avatāras are seen in Śrī Kṛṣṇa. And so the *Brahmāṇḍa Purāṇa* says: “He who is four-armed in Vaikuṇṭha, who is the Lord of Śvetadvīpa, who is Nārāyaṇa, the friend of Nara — He is Puruṣottama, the son of Nanda. As hundreds and thousands of sparks fly out of a great fire and vanish back into it, so all the other countless charming avatāras of this Kṛṣṇa become one with Him again.” Therefore, in the Purāṇas and other scriptures, some have glorified Śrī Kṛṣṇa as Nārāyaṇa, the friend of Nara, some as Upendra, that is, Vāmana, some as Kṣīrodaśāyī, some as the thousand-headed Garbhodaśāyī, and some as the Lord of Vaikuṇṭha. For the original Saṅkarṣaṇa abides in Śrī Kṛṣṇa, and from Him Badarīnātha and other forms are manifested (that is, they appear by His mere wish). The sages saw only the pastimes of one or another of these forms. They followed the accounts of Viṣṇu with those different pastimes, and so they called Śrī Kṛṣṇa by the names of those forms of Viṣṇu. Therefore, even if the original source of the avatāras is called an “avatāra,” in truth there is no fault in it. See Ādi 2.110–115.

> Subheading: Though Advaita Prabhu is an avatāra of Mahā-Viṣṇu, He considers Himself a servant of Gaura —

Śrī Mahāprabhu honored Advaita Ācārya, regarding Him as one of His elders (*guru-varga*). Yet Śrī Advaita Prabhu considered Himself a servant of Śrī Caitanya. He was a contemporary and friend of Śrī Mahāprabhu's father. Śrī Īśvara Purī and Śrī Advaita Prabhu were disciples of Śrī Mādhavendra Purī. Mahāprabhu accepted Śrī Īśvara Purī as His *dīkṣā-guru*, and so Advaita Prabhu was a godbrother of Mahāprabhu's guru and worthy of respect.
