# Amṛta-pravāha-bhāṣya on Madhya 25

## 0

**Summary.** A brāhmaṇa from Mahārāṣṭra was a servant of Mahāprabhu. Whenever he heard the Lord’s glory, he was filled with joy. One day he invited the sannyāsīs and Mahāprabhu, brought them together, and made the sannyāsīs fit to receive Mahāprabhu’s mercy; this is described in the seventh chapter of the Ādi-līlā. From that day the Lord’s greatness became known throughout the city of Vārāṇasī, and many of its residents became His followers. One of Prakāśānanda Sarasvatī’s disciples also became a follower of the Lord. When this disciple censured Māyāvāda and described the greatness of the teaching of pure devotion that Mahāprabhu had given, Prakāśānanda Svāmī defended his own position with many arguments. One day, after bathing at Pañcanada, Mahāprabhu began kīrtana with His devotees in the temple of Bindu-mādhava, and Prakāśānanda came there with his disciples. Prakāśānanda fell down and clasped Mahāprabhu’s feet, condemned his own past conduct, and asked about the truth of bhakti in harmony with Vedānta. Mahāprabhu taught him the unprecedented doctrine of devotion established in the Brahma-sampradāya, showed that the *Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam* is the commentary on the *Brahma-sūtra*, and, explaining the *Catuḥ-ślokī*, set forth the whole truth. From that day the sannyāsīs became “devotees.” Mahāprabhu instructed Sanātana, told him to go to Vṛndāvana, and set out for Puruṣottama. After this Kavirāja Gosvāmī described something of the history of Śrī Rūpa, Śrī Sanātana and Subuddhi Rāya. Mahāprabhu set out with Balabhadra through Jhārikhaṇḍa and arrived in Śrī Puruṣottama. In the last part of this chapter Śrī Kavirāja Gosvāmī listed the subjects of every chapter of the Madhya-līlā and instructed all jīvas to read this *Śrī Caitanya-caritāmṛta*.

## 1

Having made the sannyāsīs and the other residents of Kāśī “Vaiṣṇavas,” and having performed the purificatory rite (*saṁskāra*) for Sanātana in the best way, the Lord came to Nīlādri.

## 6

“I have described this before in detail” — see Ādi 7.

## 26

*Ācārya* means Śaṅkarācārya.

## 36

“O Supreme! This form of Yours is bliss alone, free of all differentiation (*avikalpa*), radiant and beyond māyā. There is no form higher than the one I now behold. O Soul (*ātmā*)! You create the universe and yet are distinct from it. This form of Yours that I behold is the source of the elements and the senses; in it I take shelter (*prapatti*).”

## 37

“O auspiciousness of the universe! For our good You have revealed in meditation this form of Yours, worthy of our worship. To this form of Bhagavān we bow down and render service. People defiled by bad company and destined for hell have no regard for this spiritual form.[^37-1]”

## 38

Fools disregard Me, who have assumed a human form: they regard My eternal spiritual body as dependent on māyā and so disregard it. For they do not know the supreme spiritual nature of My form as Kṛṣṇa, the Great Lord of all beings.

## 39

The cruel, the lowest of men, who hate My divine form (*śrī-mūrti*), I cast again and again in this world into the wombs of *asuras* and other beings.

## 46–55

Having heard the other *sannyāsī’s* words, imbued with devotion, Prakāśānanda Sarasvatī says: “Śaṅkarācārya was excessively eager to establish *advaita-vāda*, and so he explained the sūtras in another way. If one accepts that there is Bhagavān (*bhagavattā*), *advaita-vāda* cannot remain. That is why the Ācārya tried to refute all the other scriptures that establish the truth of Bhagavān. To abandon the plain meaning of the scriptures in order to establish one’s own opinion — that is the rule of every ‘doctrine’ (*matavāda*). Look: (1) The Mīmāṁsakas headed by Jaimini abandoned bhakti, the root purport of the Vedas, and made Īśvara ‘a limb of karma.’ (2) The godless (*nirīśvara*) authors of Sāṅkhya headed by Kapila abandoned the true meaning of the Vedas and declared *prakṛti* the cause of the world. (3) Gautama, Kaṇāda and others, in the scriptures of Nyāya and Vaiśeṣika, called the atoms the cause of the universe. (4) In the same way the Māyāvādīs headed by Aṣṭāvakra presented featureless Brahman as the cause of the world. (5) Rāja-yogīs such as Patañjali established as the ‘essential truth’ (*svarūpa-tattva*) the imaginary Īśvara described in his *Yoga-śāstra*. These ācāryas, devoted to their doctrines, rejected Svayam Bhagavān, established by the Vedas, and each set up an ‘opinion’ based on a partial (*khaṇḍa*) perception of Him. Śrī Vyāsadeva thoroughly examined the six opinions of the six *darśanas*, refuted each of them, and, relying on the Vedic statements that establish Bhagavān, composed the *Vedānta-sūtra*. According to Vedānta, Brahman is *sac-cid-ānanda* and possessed of form (*sākāra*). The *nirviśeṣa-vādīs* declare Brahman *nirguṇa* (without qualities), and in particular cases call Bhagavān *saguṇa*, that is, made of the three *guṇas*. In truth, however, the Absolute is not merely *nirguṇa*, merely beyond the three *guṇas*: He is a form ‘possessed of qualities’ (*saguṇa*), the abode of countless spiritual qualities. The adherents of these doctrines do not accept the supreme cause, Īśvara (Viṣṇu): none of them accepts Viṣṇu, the Lord of all lords and the cause of all causes, though each, refuting the opinions of others, has tried to establish his own. Therefore what the *mahājana* says must be known as ‘the truth.’”

## 74

If even those liberated in this life (*jīvan-muktas*) offend Bhagavān, who possesses inconceivable great potency, they fall again into the desires of worldly existence (*saṁsāra-vāsanā*).

## 75

By the touch of Śrī Kṛṣṇa’s feet that serpent was freed from all inauspiciousness; he gave up the serpent’s body and regained his former form, honoured by the Vidyādharas.

## 88

“Tell it in brief”: I have already heard from You the primary meaning of each sūtra. Now I wish to hear in brief the principal purport (*mukhya-tātparya*) of Vedānta.

## 92–95

The *praṇava* is the *mahā-vākya* (great statement) of all the Vedas. The meaning contained in the *praṇava* is also in the Gāyatrī, and that same meaning is unfolded in the *Śrī Bhāgavatam* in four verses, beginning with the verse *aham evāsam evāgre*. From Bhagavān to Brahmā, from Brahmā to Nārada, from Nārada to Śrī Vyāsa — in this way, through the succession of a true sampradāya, all the Vedas and their purport came into the *Śrī Bhāgavatam*. The *Śrī Bhāgavatam* itself is “the commentary on the *Brahma-sūtra*.”

## 97

*Ṛk* means a Vedic mantra; *viṣaya-vacana* means the statement of the subject (*uddeśya*). In the *Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam* that *ṛk* is set down in the form of a verse.

## 99

Whatever you see in this world — all of it, that is, this whole universe — is pervaded by the Ātmā. O jīvas, this Ātmā is your controller and protector. Use all the things of the world as things granted by His grace, and do not seize the wealth of others. The purport is this. The mantra of the *Īśopaniṣad*, *īśāvāsyam idaṁ jagat*, is a *śruti-mantra*, that is, a *viṣaya-vacana* on which the *Brahma-sūtra* rests. In the *Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam* that *ṛk* (mantra) is set down in the form of a verse as *ātmāvāsyam idam*. In this way all the *ṛks* of all the sūtras are set down in the verses of the *Bhāgavatam*.

## 104

“You are a *jīva*”: O Brahmā, you are a “*jīva*”; without My mercy you will not be able to know the supreme secret.

## 119

In the *dharma-śāstras*, for the sake of learning from the guru, four truths are examined: dharma, *artha*, *kāma* and *mokṣa*. In the same way the scriptures on the Truth (*tattva-śāstras*), to examine it in like manner, give instruction on “*jñāna*,” “*vijñāna*,” “*tad-aṅga*” (its limb) and “*tad-rahasya*” (its secret). But here it should be noted that the four subjects beginning with dharma follow the ordinary laws of worldly life. With the four subjects of the Truth (*jñāna* and the rest) the matter is different: even *sādhana-bhakti*, the first of them, stands above the four truths beginning with dharma — that is, it is superior to them.

## 126

Hari destroys all sins even when His name is uttered unwittingly. He does not leave the heart of one in whose heart His lotus feet are bound with the rope of love (*praṇaya*); such a person is “the foremost of *bhāgavatas*.”

## 128

Gathered together, the gopīs sang loudly of Kṛṣṇa’s qualities and, as if mad, began to search for Him from forest to forest. They began to ask the trees (*vanaspatis*) about that Supreme Person, Kṛṣṇa, who like the sky is present outside and within all beings.

## 134

Remembering Hari, who destroys masses of sin, and reminding one another of Him, they bear bodies whose hair stands on end through prema-bhakti born of *sādhana-bhakti*.

## 137

This *Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam* is the meaning of the *Brahma-sūtra*, the settled purport of the *Mahābhārata* and a commentary on the Gāyatrī; the meaning of all the Vedas enriches it. The *Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam* contains 18,000 verses.

## 138

[It is] the essence drawn from all the Vedas and *itihāsas* ([Vyāsa] had his son Śrī Śukadeva study the *Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam*).

## 139

The *Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam* may be called the essence of Vedānta. One who is satisfied by the nectar of the *Bhāgavatam*’s rasa finds no taste (*rati*) in any other scripture.

## 144

This scripture, the *Bhāgavatam*, is the fruit fallen from the wish-fulfilling tree of the Vedas, joined with the nectarean juice from Śukadeva’s mouth. O *rasikas*, always drink this fruit, which is *rasa* itself! O *bhāvukas*, until you are fully absorbed (*parama-laya*) in the truth of rasa, that is, until you are immersed in it, relish the *Bhāgavatam* in this world (as transcendental *bhāvukas*); and even when immersed, you will go on drinking this supreme rasa again and again, eternally.

## 145

The more we hear of the exploits of Kṛṣṇa, Uttamaśloka, the more our thirst grows; the satisfaction that would quench it never comes. For to listeners who know rasa, the accounts of Kṛṣṇa yield a [new] taste at every step.

## 181

*Munsīph*: the word *munsīph* comes from the word *insāph* (justice). One who takes charge of some matter on behalf of the principal owner is called a *munsīph*. *Chidra pāñā* — finding a fault.

## 183

“His wife” — Husain Shāh’s begum. “The mark of the blow” — the mark of the whip with which Subuddhi Rāya had struck him.

## 186

*Karoyāra pāni*: a *karoyā* is the vessel in which Muslims keep water. From such a *karoyā*, water touched by Muslims was put into Subuddhi Rāya’s mouth.

## 187

*Chadma* — a pretext. Subuddhi Rāya had already wished to give up worldly affairs; on the pretext of the loss of caste he left his family.

## 190

Subuddhi Rāya met Mahāprabhu when the Lord came to Vārāṇasī before going to Mathurā.

## 202

“Hearing of this”: in Mathurā Rūpa Gosvāmī heard that earlier Mahāprabhu had gone to Mathurā by the road along the Gaṅgā; now, eager to see that road, he came the same way with Anupama.

## 206

*Vyavahāra-sneha* — affection rooted in worldly relationships.

## 271

People are nourished by food and drink. The devotees, though they too take food and drink as those averse to God do, grow weak if they do not drink the nectar of Caitanya-līlā mixed with Kṛṣṇa-līlā.

## 275

May this *Caitanya-caritāmṛta* be offered to Kṛṣṇa Caitanya for the pleasure of Śrī Madana-Gopāla and Śrī Govinda-deva.

## 276

This most confidential nectar of Gaura’s pastimes is the treasure of a devotee’s life, but the unqualified will certainly not value it. That is no loss to me. On the contrary, all good-hearted sādhus have fully relished the nectar of these pastimes; may this book increase the joy of those great souls.

*Thus ends the twenty-fifth chapter of the “Amṛta-pravāha-bhāṣya.”*
