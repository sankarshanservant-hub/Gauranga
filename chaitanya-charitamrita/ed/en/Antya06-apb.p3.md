## 225

*Rasa*: [the six tastes] — bitter, sweet, sour, salty, pungent and astringent.[^225-1]

## 236–237

A man and a woman marry, beget children and so build a household (*saṁsāra*). All the talk connected with such household life is *grāmya*, mundane talk. A *vairāgī*, like any Vaiṣṇava, should never listen to it or speak it. Eating well and dressing well are also improper for a *vairāgī*. He should give respect to others, seek none for himself, always chant the name of Kṛṣṇa and serve Rādhā and Kṛṣṇa in Vraja within his mind — this is the duty of a *vairāgī*.

## 241

“Intimate service” (*antaraṅga-sevā*) is the service in Vraja that a devotee performs within the mind, in his own true spiritual form (*svarūpa-deha*). Svarūpa Gosvāmī is Lalitā-devī. Entering her group, Śrī Dāsa Gosvāmī performed his own intimate service in Vraja.

## 263

Śrī Vāsudeva Datta (who lived in Kāñcanapallī) loved the very gentle Yadunandana Ācārya;[^263-1] his disciple is Raghunātha dāsa. By his virtues Raghunātha is dearer than life to all of us. By Śrī Caitanya's boundless mercy he is always full of tender love; he is dear to Svarūpa Gosvāmī and is the sole treasure in the kingdom of renunciation. Who among those living in Nīlācala does not know him?

## 264

By delighting the minds of all people (enchanting their hearts), he became a certain (indescribable) soil of good fortune — one that bears fruit without plowing (manifest by itself) — that is, its very abode. In him the incomparable (matchless) tree of love (Śrī Caitanya's) bore fruit at the very time the seed was sown.

## 279

A “*rājasic*” invitation. Invitations are of three kinds: *sāttvic*, *rājasic* and *tāmasic*. The invitation of a pure Vaiṣṇava is *sāttvic*, the food of a pious *viṣayī* is *rājasic*, and the food of a sinner is *tāmasic*.

## 285

“Here comes a man — he is the one who will give; this one gave before; here comes another — he will give; the one who just passed did not give; another will come and give.” Such are the hopes of those who wear the dress of *vairāgīs* yet live without begging (*ayācaka*): abandoning impartiality, they become like a prostitute.

## 299

*Vitasti* (a span): a measure of half a cubit.

## 309

“Raghunātha's rules were like a line on stone”: Śrīla Raghunātha's rules of renunciation were as firm as a line carved in stone.

## 314

A person whose heart has been washed clean by knowledge attains everything once he comes to know the truth of the self. Why then — with what intention, for what reason — do the dissolute, instead of doing so, take pains only to nourish the body?

## 315

*Saḍi*: having rotted.

## 316

*Tailaṅgī gāi*: cows from the land of Tailaṅga.

## 327

I am the greatest of scoundrels, yet He, in His mercy, saw me fallen, rescued me from wealth and wife (by another reading, from the forest fire of *viṣaya*), gave me over to Śrī Svarūpa and was pleased.[^327-1] He gave me the *guñjā* garland from His own chest and the Govardhana-śilā. And that Gaurāṅga, rising in my heart, is maddening me.

*Thus ends the sixth chapter of the “Amṛta-pravāha-bhāṣya.”*
