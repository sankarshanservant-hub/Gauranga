## 66–72

This Kṛṣṇa in the role of the hero (*nāyaka*) is: 1) beautifully formed, 2) endowed with all auspicious marks, 3) handsome, 4) full of great radiance, 5) strong, 6) ever in the age of *kiśora* (youth), 7) a knower of various wonderful languages, 8) truthful, 9) pleasant in speech, 10) eloquent, 11) highly learned, 12) intelligent, 13) gifted with inspired insight (*pratibhā*), 14) artful (*vidagdha*), 15) clever, 16) expert, 17) grateful, 18) firm in His vows, 19) a knower of what suits place, time and person, 20) one who sees through the eyes of the *śāstra*, 21) pure, 22) self-controlled, 23) steady, 24) restrained, 25) forgiving, 26) deep, 27) steadfast, 28) even and gentle in conduct, 29) generous, 30) righteous, 31) heroic, 32) compassionate, 33) one who shows honor to others, 34) courteous (*dakṣiṇa*), 35) humble, 36) modest, 37) the protector of the surrendered, 38) happy, 39) the friend of His devotees, 40) subject to love, 41) the benefactor of all, 42) mighty, 43) famous, 44) beloved of the people, 45) the shelter of the *sādhus*, 46) the captivator of women's hearts, 47) worshipable by all, 48) full of opulence, 49) supreme over all, and 50) full of majesty (*aiśvarya*). These are His fifty qualities.

## 73

These fifty qualities exist in all *jīvas* as drops, but in Kṛṣṇa, the Supreme Person (Puruṣottama), they are present in all their fullness, like an ocean.

## 74–75

Beyond these fifty there are five more great qualities. They are present in full in Kṛṣṇa (in Viṣṇu) and partially in Śiva and the other gods: 1) He always abides in His own nature (*svarūpa*), 2) He is all-knowing, 3) He is ever new, 4) His *svarūpa* is concentrated being, knowledge and bliss (*sac-cid-ānanda*), 5) He commands all perfections (*siddhis*), and therefore all the *siddhis* serve Him.

## 76–77

In Nārāyaṇa, the Lord of Paravyoma, and in His other forms there are five more qualities. They are fully present in Kṛṣṇa as well, but neither Śiva and the other gods nor the *jīvas* possess them: 1) inconceivable great energy, 2) a form that holds millions of universes, 3) being the seed of all *avatāras*, 4) granting a good destination to the enemies He slays, 5) attracting the *ātmārāmas* (those content in the self). Though these five qualities are found in Nārāyaṇa and His other forms as well, in Kṛṣṇa they are manifest in a wondrous way.

## 78–79

Beyond these sixty qualities four more are manifest in Kṛṣṇa. They are not manifest even in Nārāyaṇa: 1) He is an ocean surging with waves of pastimes that amaze all the worlds; 2) He is surrounded by His dearest ones, adorned with the incomparable *prema* of *śṛṅgāra-rasa*; 3) He sings on His flute a song that captivates the hearts of the three worlds; 4) He possesses a beauty that has no equal and no superior, which has struck everything moving and unmoving with wonder.

## 80

Such (love-filled) pastimes, the incomparable company of His beloveds (that is, His tenderness toward the dear ones who love Him), the sweetness of His form and the sweetness of His flute — these are the four unique qualities of Śrī Kṛṣṇa. By distinguishing four levels — ordinary *jīvas*, Śiva and the other gods, Nārāyaṇa and the other forms of the Supreme Lord, and Govinda Himself (the *svayaṁ-rūpa*) — sixty-four qualities in all have been enumerated.

## 82–86

Now the chief qualities of Śrī Rādhikā, the Queen of Vṛndāvana, are glorified. She is: 1) sweet, 2) youthful, 3) with restless eyes, 4) with a radiant smile, 5) with beautiful lines of good fortune, 6) maddening Kṛṣṇa with Her fragrance, 7) versed in the art of song, 8) charming in speech, 9) expert in joking (*narma*), 10) humble, 11) full of compassion, 12) clever, 13) skillful, 14) modest, 15) keeping proper bounds (*sumaryādā*), 16) steadfast, 17) full of depth, 18) rich in play (*suvilāsā*), 19) full of *mahābhāva* in its highest degree, 20) the abode of Gokula's love, 21) one whose fame shines among the *āśrayas* of the whole world, 22) one on whom the elders bestow great affection,[^82-1] 23) subject to the friendly love (*praṇaya*) of the *sakhīs*, 24) the chief among Kṛṣṇa's beloveds, 25) one who always keeps Keśava under Her control.

## 89–92

Devotion has washed away all their faults; their hearts are clear and bright; they are devoted to the *Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam* and delight in the company of *rasikas*; the happiness and beauty of devotion to Govinda's feet is their very life; they perform the acts that are intimate (*antaraṅga*) to *prema*. In the hearts of such devotees *rati*, whose nature is bliss, made radiant by past and present *saṁskāras*,[^89-1] becomes relishable and shines forth. Through the *vibhāvas* — Kṛṣṇa and the rest — on the path of experience it reaches its highest limit: the wonder (*camatkāra*) of mature bliss.

## 94

For nondevotees this *bhagavad-rasa* is in every way unattainable. *Bhakti-rasa* is attainable only by those for whom Kṛṣṇa's lotus feet are everything.

## 98

“The *bhakti-smṛti-śāstra*” is the book *Hari-bhakti-vilāsa*.[^98-1]

## 99

To use the world in relation to Kṛṣṇa is *yukta-vairāgya* (fitting renunciation). To take *sannyāsa* while regarding the world as “worthless” is “dry renunciation.”

## 100–101

The devotee who bears no enmity toward any living being, who is friendly and compassionate, free of the sense of “mine,” free of self-conceit, equal in happiness and distress, forgiving, always content, self-controlled, firm in resolve, practicing *bhakti-yoga*, and who has given Me his mind and intelligence — he is dear to Me.

## 102

He on whose account people are not disturbed, who does not disturb people,[^102-1] and who is free from the disturbances of elation, anger and fear — he too is dear to Me.

## 103

My devotee who expects nothing, who is pure, skillful, impartial, free from distress, and who has given up all undertakings — he is dear to Me.

## 104

He who is free of elation, aversion, grief and longing, who has given up results both auspicious and inauspicious, and who is full of devotion — he is dear to Me.

## 105–106

The person who is equal toward enemy and friend, in honor and dishonor, in cold and heat, in happiness and distress, who is free of attachment, equal in blame and praise, silent, content with whatever comes, homeless, steady in mind and full of devotion — he is dear to Me.

## 107

Those who, full of faith and devoted to Me, worship this nectar of *dharma* (described from the second to the nineteenth verse) are My devotees, and they are exceedingly dear to Me.

## 108

Oh, do worn-out rags not lie on the road? Do the trees that nourish others not give alms? Have all the rivers dried up? Are the caves closed? Does Īśvara not protect those who have come to Him? If that is so, why do the wise serve those blinded by the wicked pride of wealth?

## 111

The interpretation that Kṛṣṇa is an *avatāra* in the form of “a hair black as a crow” is contrary to the truth. Rejecting it, [the Lord] taught the pure interpretation: “*ka* + *īśa* = *keśa*,” that is, Kṛṣṇa is “the Lord of Brahmā.”

*Thus ends the twenty-third chapter of the “Amṛta-pravāha-bhāṣya.”*
