## 39

> Subheading: The last 12 years: in separation from Kṛṣṇa He Himself tastes love for Kṛṣṇa at every moment and displays the states of *prema* —

One in whom *prema* has awakened abides in the transcendental *vipralambha-rasa*, the *rasa* of separation, which nourishes *sambhoga*, union. Śrī Gaurasundara wished to taste this state of *prema* Himself, and in this way He taught it to the *jīvas* of the world. Unless *vipralambha* arises, *sambhoga* is not nourished.

## 41

> Subheading: Like Śrī Rādhā on seeing Uddhava, the Lord is in *mahābhāva* —

Śrī Gaurasundara is the living ideal of the long, long *vipralambha-rasa*: He is filled with the mood of *citra-jalpa*, “varied talk,” that seized Śrīmatī Vṛṣabhānujā (Rādhā) when She saw Uddhava. With envy (*asūyā*), jealousy (*īrṣyā*) and pride (*mada*), in a manner of open disdain, Rādhikā set out to declare Kṛṣṇa's want of courtesy (*akauśala*) and, addressing the bumblebee, spoke *prajalpa*, words full of reproach. Śrī Gaurasundara was immersed in all those moods (see Ādi 4.107–108).

## 42

> Subheading: With Svarūpa and Rāya He relishes Caṇḍīdāsa, Vidyāpati and the *Gīta-govinda* —

Vidyāpati was a Vaiṣṇava poet who lived in Mithilā. He flourished during the reign of King Śivasiṁha and Queen Lachimādevī; that is, he composed his songs in the first quarter of the fourteenth century of the Śaka era. He appeared about a hundred years before Śrī Mahāprabhu's manifest pastimes. He was a Maithila *brāhmaṇa*, and his descendant in the twelfth generation is alive today. His songs about Kṛṣṇa abound in examples of transcendental *vipralambha-rasa*. Those moods were what Śrī Mahāprabhu relished.

Jayadeva was born, during the reign of Lakṣmaṇasena, the king of Bengal, to Bhojadeva and Vāmādevī. That period, according to some, is the eleventh or the twelfth century of the Śaka era. He lived for a long time in the city of Navadvīpa, the capital of Bengal. Jayadeva's birthplace is the village of Kenduvilva in the district of Birbhum; according to others, Utkala (Orissa); according to still others, the South. He spent the last part of his life at Śrī Jagannātha-kṣetra. His book of poetry is called the *Gīta-govinda*, or *Aṣṭapadī*. It too abounds in transcendental *vipralambha-rasa*. It describes the *vipralambha-rasa* that nourishes the *rasa* of *sambhoga* — the separation that arose when the prince of Vraja left the place of the *rāsa* dance, as the *Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam* tells. For the commentaries on the *Aṣṭapadī* and the names of the commentators, see the *Vaiṣṇava-mañjuṣā* (issue 1).

Caṇḍīdāsa was born in a *brāhmaṇa* family in the village of Nānnura in the district of Birbhum, at the close of the first quarter of the fourteenth century of the Śaka era. He was probably a friend of Vidyāpati Ṭhākura. His writings abound in transcendental *vipralambha-rasa*.

The moods that blossomed in Caṇḍīdāsa and other devotees were Śrī Mahāprabhu's most beloved delicacy. Steeped in the mood of Śrī Rādhā, Kṛṣṇacandra tasted *vipralambha-rasa* with His two intimate devotees, Śrī Dāmodara Svarūpa and Śrī Rāmānanda Rāya, and so fulfilled His own desire.


Svayaṁ Bhagavān Śrī Gaurasundara, together with unequalled knowers of the truth of spiritual *kṛṣṇa-rasa* — Śrī Dāmodara Svarūpa, the crest jewel of the *paramahaṁsas*, and Śrī Rāya Rāmānanda — relished the transcendental songs composed by these three devotees. These songs unfold the countless varieties of the *mahābhāva* of Śrī Rādhikā, “full of Kṛṣṇa” (*kṛṣṇamayī*), toward Śrī Nandanandana, the unequalled Enjoyer, whose form is eternity, knowledge and bliss. Only the most fortunate person can always follow them: one who is indifferent both to enjoyment and to renunciation in this perishable gross and subtle world, who is supremely liberated and owns nothing (*niṣkiñcana*), and who eternally longs to serve Śrī Rādhā. People who delight in mundane poetic *rasa*, who love godless literature, who take pleasure in the body — for the sake of research — and the *prākṛta-sahajiyā* sect — to gratify their material senses, fancying themselves *rāgānuga* — show the impudence of discussing the songs of Caṇḍīdāsa, Vidyāpati and Jayadeva. As a result, licentiousness and atheism grow in the world, and this leads them to hell. Unqualified people, who take the body for the self, are full of thirst for what is unworthy and burdened with *anarthas*, may take the supramundane pastimes of Śrī Rādhā and Kṛṣṇa, worshipped by the supremely liberated, to be the tasteless, vile amorous sports of a mundane hero and heroine, or something like them — and end up gratifying their senses. Therefore any kind of discussion of the pastimes of Rādhā and Kṛṣṇa is strictly forbidden to them.

> Subheading: Śacī and Nīlāmbara Cakravartī —

Nīlāmbara Cakravartī. The *Gaura-gaṇoddeśa* (verse 104) says: “Nīlāmbara Cakravartī foretold Gaura's future birth in an assembly, and so he is called ‘Garga.’ As the father of Śrī Śacī he is considered the cowherd Sumukha.” His kinsmen live in the village of Magaḍobā in the district of Faridpur. His nephew Jagannātha Cakravartī, or “Māmu Ṭhākura,” a disciple of Paṇḍita Gosvāmī, was the servant of Ṭoṭā-Gopīnātha at Śrī Kṣetra. The *Prema-vilāsa* records that Nīlāmbara's home in Navadvīpa was in Belpukuriyā. On the other hand, he also had a home in Kāzīpāḍā, and so, by village kinship, the Kazi too is spoken of as the Lord's maternal uncle. The Kazi's house, together with his tomb, could not have moved; hence it is known that all those places of the Belpukuriyā quarter mentioned above have become part of the present-day quarter called Bāmanpukur.

## 61

> Subheading: Śrī Nityānanda, Gaṅgādāsa, Murāri, Mukunda —

In Rāḍha-deśa: in the village of Ekacakrā in the district of Birbhum. It lies about eight miles east of Mallārpur station on the loop line of the East Indian Railway. From north to south the village of Ekacakrā stretches four *krośas*. Vīracandrapura, or Vīrabhadrapura, lies within the bounds of Ekacakrā. The place got the name Vīracandrapura, or Vīrabhadrapura, from the name of Vīrabhadra Prabhu.

Last year, 1331, in the month of Āṣāḍha, lightning struck the temple and broke its spire, and the temple itself has become badly dilapidated. Never before had such a calamity befallen the temple.

In the temple stands the Deity of Kṛṣṇa installed by Śrīman Nityānanda Prabhu, named “Śrī Baṅkima Rāya,” or “Bāṅkā Rāya.” To the right of Śrī Baṅkima Rāya stands Jāhnavā, and to the left Śrīmatī Rādhikā. The temple servants say that Śrīman Nityānanda Prabhu entered into Śrī Baṅkima Rāya, and that is why Jāhnavā-mātā was later installed on His right. Later still, other Deities were installed in the temple. On another throne in the temple are the Deities “Muralīdhara” and “Rādhā-Mādhava.” And on a third, separate throne, Śrī Manomohana, Śrī Vṛndāvanacandra and Śrī Nitāi-Gaura, brought to Ekacakrā from the *gādi* (seat) of Vipraghāṭī in the district of Murshidabad, have been served for a year now. Only Śrī Baṅkima Rāya is said to be the ancient Deity installed by Nityānanda Prabhu. Legend has it that the Deity of Śrī Baṅkima Rāya was floating in the waters of the Yamunā at Kadambakhaṇḍī Ghāṭ, east of the temple. Śrīman Nityānanda Prabhu lifted the Deity out of the water and installed Him for service. And Śrīmatī appeared under a *nimba* tree at a place called Bhaḍḍāpura, about half a mile west of Vīracandrapura. That is why many people used to call Baṅkima Rāya's Śrīmatī “the Ṭhākurāṇī of Bhaḍḍāpura.” On yet another throne in the temple, to the right of Bāṅkā Rāya, stands “Yogamāyā.” The temple and the *jagamohana* stand on a high brick plinth. Directly in front is a modest *nāṭa-mandira*. It is said that in the northern part of Śrī Bāṅkā Rāya's temple there was a Śiva named “Bhāṇḍīśvara,” and Hāḍāi Paṇḍita worshipped that Śiva, the king of Vaiṣṇavas. That Śiva-*liṅga* has now disappeared, and in its place a Deity of Śrī Jagannātha is installed.

Śrīman Nityānanda Prabhu built no temples or other buildings. When the temple from Vīrabhadra Prabhu's time collapsed in 1298, a *brahmacārī* named Śivānanda Svāmī restored it. Seventeen seers and ten *chaṭāks* of rice are allotted every day for the Deity's offering (*bhoga*).

The servitors, the “Gosvāmīs,” are a branch of the family of Śrī Gopījana-vallabhānanda, a descendant of Nityānanda. An estate (*zamindari*) is settled in the Gosvāmīs' name for the service, and the service is maintained from its income. The “Gosvāmīs” are three shareholders, and they serve by turns. Śrīyukta Goṣṭhavihārī “Gosvāmī” holds a share of eight annas eight *gaṇḍās* in the estate; Śrīyukta Vijayacandra “Gosvāmī” and others, five annas fourteen *gaṇḍās*; and Śrīyukta Prasanna Kumāra Bandyopādhyāya (a daughter's son of the “Gosvāmīs”), one anna eighteen *gaṇḍās*[^61-1].

Not far from the temple is a place called Viśrāmatalā. Legend has it that here, in His childhood, Śrī Nityānanda Prabhu acted out various pastimes of Vraja and the *rāsa* dance with His friends.

At a place called Āmlītalā stands a spreading tamarind tree. The Neḍās and similar sects have invented many fanciful stories about this place. Legend has it that a pond called “Śvetagaṅgā” was dug by Vīrabhadra Prabhu's twelve hundred Neḍās. A little farther on are the memorial pillars over the Gosvāmīs' tombs. Crossing the Yamunā, which flows north from Mauḍeśvara to the river Dvārakeśvara, one comes within half a mile to the temple at Śrī Nityānanda Prabhu's birthplace (*sūtikā-mandira*). In front of it stood an old *nāṭa-mandira*; it is now in ruins and covered by a spreading banyan. Later a temple was built in that courtyard, and the Deities of Śrī Gaura and Nityānanda reside in it. A stone tablet in the *jagamohana* records that this temple was restored in the month of Vaiśākha, 1323, in memory of the late Prasannakumāra, the steward (*kārpharmā*).

The place where the *sūtikā-mandira* stands is called “Garbhavāsa.” About 43 *bighās* of land are set aside for the service; of these, 20 *bighās* are tax-free — this is land of the Mahārāja of Dinajpur. Near Garbhavāsa stood the school (*ṭol*) of Hāḍāi Paṇḍita.

The servitors of this place: (1) Śrī Rāghavacandra Gosvāmī (in Vraja, Campakalatā, *Gaura-gaṇoddeśa* 162; he lived at Govardhana; the day of his departure, the thirteenth lunar day of the bright fortnight of Jyaiṣṭha); (2) Jagadānanda dāsa (day of departure, Rādhāṣṭamī); (3) Kṛṣṇadāsa (of Ciḍiyā-kuñja; day of departure, Kṛṣṇa-janmāṣṭamī); (4) Nityānanda dāsa; (5) Rāmadāsa; (6) Vrajamohana dāsa; (7) Kānāi dāsa; (8) Gauradāsa; (9) Śivānanda dāsa; (10) Haridāsa (the present servitor).

Not far from Garbhavāsa, the *sūtikā-mandira*, is Bakulatalā. Here Śrī Nityānanda Prabhu played the game of *jhāl-jhapeṭā* with His friends. This *bakula* tree is a great wonder: its branches and twigs look exactly like snakes, with heads and hoods. Probably they appear this way by the will of Anantadeva Himself, Śrī Nityānanda. The tree itself is very old. It is said that two of its branches once grew apart. Nityānanda Prabhu saw that during play His friends found it hard to cross from one branch to the other, and He joined the two branches.

Hāṅṭugāḍā. Tradition says that Śrī Nityānanda Prabhu gathered all the holy places (*tīrthas*) at this spot. To this day the residents of this *dhāma* do not go to the Gaṅgā or other *tīrthas*, but bathe at this *tīrtha*. Śrīman Nityānanda Prabhu held a festival of *dadhi-ciḍā*, yogurt with flat rice, here. Legend has it that He sat here on His knees eating *dadhi-ciḍā*, and the spot sank into a hollow; that is why it is called “Hāṅṭugāḍā” (“knees sunk in”). This pond (*kuṇḍa*) holds water all year round.

In the month of Kārttika, at the time of the Goṣṭha festival, a large fair takes place here. A huge fair is also held at Vīracandrapura for the festival of Śrī Nityānanda Prabhu's birth, on the thirteenth lunar day of the bright fortnight of Māgha. The *Gaura-gaṇoddeśa* (verse 11) says: “Nityānanda, who in Vraja is Śrī Halāyudha, in the form of a devotee”; and (verses 58–59): “Baladeva is Viśvarūpa, considered the *vyūha* Saṅkarṣaṇa. And Nityānanda Avadhūta is also called He, by way of *prakāśa* (manifestation).” He is the Balarāma of Vraja.

> Subheading: Their ninth child, Viśvarūpa —

Viśvarūpa was the elder brother of Śrī Gaurahari. Before marrying He took *sannyāsa* and received the name “Śaṅkarāraṇya.” In Śaka 1431 He departed from this world at Pāṇḍerapura in the district of Sholapur, in the Bombay Presidency. He is both causes of the universe, the “material” (*upādāna*) and the “efficient” (*nimitta*). The *Gaura-gaṇoddeśa* (verses 58–59) says: “Because the part and the whole are not different, the son of Śacī is the original *vyūha*; Baladeva is Viśvarūpa, considered the *vyūha* Saṅkarṣaṇa; and Nityānanda Avadhūta is also called He, by way of *prakāśa*.” In the *Gauracandrodaya*, Kali says to Dharma: “His elder brother, without ever taking a wife — Saṅkarṣaṇa, that Bhagavān who on earth was Viśvarūpa — gave, it is said, His own effulgence to Purīśvara, took *sannyāsa* before Him and left this world.”

And there too: “…Nityānanda Avadhūta, glorified as the Effulgence — indeed, Saṅkarṣaṇa…”[^74-1] And further: “When this eternal Śrī Viśvarūpa left this world, He joined with Nityānanda Avadhūta and remained [in Him].”

## 80–86

The conclusion is this. Jagannātha and Śacī are eternally perfect (*nitya-siddha*), and so their hearts and bodies consist of pure goodness (*śuddha-sattva*) and are not at all like those of ordinary mundane *jīvas*. Pure goodness (*viśuddha-sattva*) is called “Vasudeva”; it is in Vasudeva that Vāsudeva, who enjoys spiritual pastimes, becomes manifest (see the *Vivṛti* in the *Gauḍīya-bhāṣya* on *Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam* 4.3.23). The union of Śrī Jagannātha and Śacīdevī and the conception in Śacīdevī were not like the amorous play and the pregnancy of men and women with mundane bodies of blood and flesh, absorbed in gratifying their material senses. Therefore even to think so in one's mind is an offense. One who reflects with a heart turned toward the service of Bhagavān will grasp the glory of the transcendental womb of Śrī Śacīdevī, who is made entirely of pure goodness. The *Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam* (10.2.16) says: “Bhagavān, the Soul of the universe, who frees His devotees from fear, entered the mind of Ānakadundubhi with His portion.” Śrīdhara Svāmī comments: “‘Entered the mind’ — He became manifest in the mind; the meaning is that He has no connection with bodily elements (*dhātu*), as the *jīvas* do.” In this connection see also *Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam* 10.2.18 and 19.

On the same subject, here is a rendering of the sense of verses 160–165 from the section on the manifestation of the manifest pastimes in Śrī Rūpa Gosvāmī Prabhu's *Laghu-bhāgavatāmṛta*. The words of the *Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam* (10.2.16), “He entered the mind of Ānakadundubhi with His portion,” mean that Kṛṣṇa first became manifest in the heart of Ānakadundubhi. Then from Ānakadundubhi's heart He became manifest in Devakī's heart. Fondled by the nectar of loving bliss, Devakī's parental love, Śrī Kṛṣṇa grew more and more in her heart, like the waxing moon. Then Śrī Kṛṣṇa vanished from Devakī's heart and appeared on her bed in the birth chamber of Kaṁsa's prison. Devakī and the others, under the sway of *yogamāyā*, then thought that the child had been born in the ordinary way, easily and happily. Such humanlike yet transcendental feelings of the devotee and Bhagavān, supremely delightful, serve the most wonderful spiritual pastimes. They bewilder even great sages enchanted by *māyā*, and they establish that Mathurā-dhāma is higher even than Paravyoma-Vaikuṇṭha. Thus Śrī Kṛṣṇa eternally remains the eternal son of the eternal Yaśodā, and in the same way He enjoys pastimes in the endless unmanifest *līlā* as well. Śrī Līlā-puruṣottama Śrī Kṛṣṇa eternally sports in Vraja, rejoicing in such pastimes: they give bliss to His dearest devotees and astonish Him as well. Under the sway of the transcendental parental love of the transcendental Nanda and Yaśodā — a love that has no equal and none higher — Bhagavān eternally knows Himself as their eternal son. In the Tenth Canto (10.5.1): “When a son was born to the great-hearted Nanda, he was overjoyed.” In the same Tenth Canto (10.6.43): “Generous-hearted Nanda, returning from abroad, took his son Kṛṣṇa on his lap, smelled His head and was filled with supreme bliss.” And again (10.9.21): “This Bhagavān Śrī Kṛṣṇa, the son of a cowherd woman, so easily reached by devotees, is never easily attained by those who take the body for the self, by ascetics, or by *jñānīs* who contemplate the self.”

Śrīpāda Baladeva Vidyābhūṣaṇa writes: “This [portion], having become one with Kṛṣṇa — Svayaṁ Bhagavān in His form, who dwelt in Ānakadundubhi's heart — becomes manifest in Devakī's heart. For Śrī Śuka says: ‘Then the goddess (“meaning: made of pure goodness,” says Svāmipāda) held in her mind the portion of Acyuta — the auspiciousness of the world, the Soul of all, the Self — placed in her (“properly placed, that is, imparted through initiation according to the rules,” says Svāmipāda) by the son of Śūra, as the east holds the delightful moon’ (*Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam* 10.2.18). Although it says ‘in Devakī's heart,’ it should be understood that He was in her womb, as the gods' prayer shows: ‘Fortunately, O mother, the Supreme Person is in your womb’ (*Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam* 10.2.41). *** And in the section on the birth: ‘In Devakī, whose form is divine, Viṣṇu, who dwells in everyone's heart, appeared, like the full moon in the east’ (*Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam* 10.3.8).”

“Then, as the east reveals the rising moon, so Devakī, made of pure goodness, having received from Śūrasena (Vasudeva) initiation into Kṛṣṇa's *mantra*, held in her heart Śrī Acyuta — the auspiciousness of the world, the Soul of all and the Supreme Soul.” From these words of the *Bhāgavatam* it is known that Svayaṁ Bhagavān became manifest from the heart of Ānakadundubhi (Vasudeva) in the heart of Devakī. Although “in Devakī's heart” is said here, it must be understood to mean that He was in Devakī's womb, for in the *Bhāgavatam* there is the gods' prayer: “O mother, the Supreme Person resides in your womb.” And in the section on the Lord's birth these words of the *Bhāgavatam* should be especially noted: “As the full moon rises in the east, so Viṣṇu, who dwells in everyone's heart, appeared in Devakī's heart.”

Here, by the words “served the feet of Govinda with still greater devotion” (Ādi 13.79), it should be understood that Śrī Gaurasundara appeared in the very hearts of Miśra and Śacī, which were always immersed in the service of Govinda's feet.

## 90

The *ṣaḍ-varga*: *kṣetra*, *horā*, *dreṣkāṇa*, *navāṁśa*, *dvādaśāṁśa* and *triṁśāṁśa* — these six are called the *ṣaḍ-varga*, the six divisions of a sign. Nīlāmbara examined the lords of this *ṣaḍ-varga* according to the exact degree (*spaṣṭāṁśa*) of the ascendant and established the auspicious signs.

The *aṣṭa-varga*: the *aṣṭa-varga* is calculated by drawing prescribed lines from the positions of the planets at the moment, as described in the *Bṛhaj-jātaka* and other books. Experts in *horā-śāstra*, astrology, apply the stated results to it and determine what is auspicious and what is not. By this calculation too Cakravartī saw auspicious signs in Mahāprabhu.

The *Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam* (10.3.11) says: “Then Ānakadundubhi, his eyes wide with wonder, saw that his son was Hari. Stirred by the festival of Kṛṣṇa's descent, he joyfully bathed [in his mind] and [in his mind] gave ten thousand cows to the *brāhmaṇas*.” Seeing Bhagavān Hari as his son, Vasudeva rejoiced at the festival of Kṛṣṇa's birth and in the prison gave ten thousand cows to the *brāhmaṇas* in his mind.

Sāvitrī is Brahmā's wife; Gaurī, Śiva's wife; Sarasvatī, Nṛsiṁha's consort, as in Śrīdhara Svāmī's commentary: “I worship Nṛsiṁha, on whose lips is the goddess of speech, on whose chest is Lakṣmī, and in whose heart is knowledge”; Śacī, Indra's wife; Rambhā, a heavenly dancer; Arundhatī, Vasiṣṭha's wife.

Siddhas: beings of divine birth who attained that birth through perfection in *mantras*. Gandharvas: heavenly singers, born from Brahmā's luster; their abode is Guhyaloka. Cāraṇas: “The singers of the gods, the reciters of hymns of praise, are the Cāraṇas”; a particular class of celestial beings.

## 107

## 111

> Subheading: What Sītā Ṭhākurāṇī did —

One day after the Lord's birthday, Advaita Prabhu's wife Sītādevī, with His permission, came with gifts from Śāntipura to Navadvīpa to see the child. Although Advaita Prabhu also had a house in Navadvīpa at that time, the words “in His own home” (Ādi 13.99) show that He was then staying in Śāntipura.

## 113

## 114

> Subheading: Auspicious rites —

*Gorocana*: a bright yellow substance obtained from a cow's head, or dried bile. *Kuṅkuma*: a fragrant substance native to Kashmir. “*Kuṅkuma* from the fields of Kashmir — with fine filaments, reddish, with the fragrance of a lotus — is the best. *Kuṅkuma* from the land of Bāhlīka — pale, with the fragrance of *ketakī*, with fine filaments — is middling. Persian *kuṅkuma* is said to smell of honey; it is slightly pale, with coarse filaments, and is the lowest.”

*Vastragupta-dolā*: a litter (*ḍuli* or *thāñjām*) covered with cloth; *ceḍī*: a maidservant.

## 115

*Ṭhāma*: build; *kāna*: Kānu, that is, Kṛṣṇa. Kṛṣṇa's color is dark, like a dense *indranīla* sapphire; Viśvambhara's color is the opposite, golden (*gaura*).

## 116

> Subheading: Seeing the child's golden body, the women are filled with parental love —

*Sunirmāṇa*: beautifully formed limbs; *bhāna*: appearance.

## 117

> Subheading: The child is blessed and a protective amulet is tied on Him —

The two brothers are Viśvarūpa and Viśvambhara. *Ḍākinīs* and *śākhinīs* are the companions of Pārvatī and Maheśa, a particular class of malevolent ghosts (*preta*) born in female form. These lower deities (*apadevatās*) cannot approach the sacred *nimba* tree or places connected with it.

## 118

> Subheading: Śacī and Miśra show honor —

“On the day of the mother's bath”: that is, on the day of the *niṣkrāmaṇa*, the child's first going out of the house. In Bengal in former times the *brāhmaṇas* and other *varṇas* observed the impurity of childbirth (*jananāśauca*) for four months, followed by the rite of seeing the sun (*sūrya-darśana*). Later, instead of four months, twenty-one days of birth impurity were prescribed for the twice-born *varṇas*, the *brāhmaṇas* and others, while among the *śūdras* and others a month is still observed. Among the Kartābhajās and in the Satīmā group the birth impurity ends at once, with a *harinuṭ* (a scattering of sweets in Hari's name).

This custom at parting is still seen in Bengali social life. When relatives or in-laws visit someone's home, the householder honors them at their departure with cloth and other gifts. Having received such honor from Jagannātha Miśra and Śacīdevī, Sītā Ṭhākurāṇī returned to Śāntipura.

## 119

> Subheading: The joy of Śacī and Miśra at having a son —

*Lokamānya-kalevara*, “a body honored by the world”: gods, humans and all others, drawn by the sight of Śrī Mahāprabhu's beauty, majesty and loveliness, showed honor to His body; seeing this, His father and mother rejoiced.

## 120

> Subheading: Miśra, a peaceful, self-controlled and generous Vaiṣṇava —

Mundane, worldly people, for the sake of wife, sons and the like, take pride in enjoying wealth and bustle after it. The pure devotee Jagannātha Miśra was not like that. He offered everything he had to Bhagavān and gave the remnants to worthy recipients, *brāhmaṇas* and others. He accepted nothing for the sake of his own enjoyment.

## 123

> Subheading: The misfortune of worldly people hostile to Gaura —

*Amṛta-dhunī*: a river of nectar. Whoever refuses to drink the water of the nectarean stream of devotion to Kṛṣṇa and drinks the water of the poisonous well of sense objects (harmful to the soul) is utterly dead, and should not go on living.

In Śrīmat Prabodhānanda Sarasvatīpāda's *Caitanya-candrāmṛta*: “This world is without consciousness (*acaitanya*) if it does not worship Caitanya, the Lord whom the best of the immortals worship: death surrounds it on every side.” “This world is without consciousness if it does not know Caitanya, the Lord: such people, though they know all the scriptures, wander astray.” “Now that the moon of Caitanya has risen and the ocean of the nectarean *rasa* of great *prema* has spread wide, whoever remains poor is poor indeed.” “Now that Gauracandra has descended, the ocean of *prema* has spread wide and its heaps of jewels shine brightly, whoever remains poor is poor indeed.”

The *Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam* (2.3.19, 20, 23) says: “A man into whose ears the name of Gadāgraja has never entered is an animal, praised by dogs, dung-eating hogs, camels and asses.” “O Sūta, the ears of a man who does not hear of the exploits of Urukrama are mere holes; and the wicked tongue that does not sing the songs of Urugāya is like the tongue of a frog.” “A mortal who has never taken on himself the dust of the feet of a *bhāgavata* is a corpse though alive; and a man who has never known the fragrance of the *tulasī* from Śrī Viṣṇu's feet is a breathing corpse.” (*Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam* 10.1.4): “The recounting of Uttamaśloka's qualities is sung by those free of thirst; it is the medicine for the disease of worldly existence and a delight to the ear and the mind. Who, except a killer of animals, would turn away from it?” (*Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam* 3.23.56): “… whoever does not live for the service of Him whose feet are holy places is dead though alive.”

## 124

The lotus feet of Śrī Mahāprabhu, Nityānanda, Advaita, Dāmodara Svarūpa, Rūpa and Raghunātha dāsa are the very own wealth of Śrī Kṛṣṇadāsa Kavirāja Gosvāmī and of the pure, or intimate, devotees who follow him. The wealth of worldly people is a price set by *māyā*; in truth it deserves the name “debt.” The *jīva* who has turned away from Kṛṣṇa does not know that the highest goal is wealth, and takes lust (*kāma*), a debt in the form of material enjoyment, to be “wealth.” Worldly *jīvas* bustle after the things they take for wealth, but in these the servants of Hari see only a growth of debt, not a growth of wealth. On the other hand, when Bhagavān makes someone rich by giving him the wealth of His mercy, He takes away his material riches. “Upon whom I bestow My mercy, I gradually take away his wealth”[^124-1]. Ṭhākura Narottama says: “My wealth is Nityānanda”; “The holy feet of Rādhā and Kṛṣṇa are the wealth of my life”; “Glory to You, O purifier of the fallen! Give me this wealth; nothing but You appeals to me”; “The feet of Śrī Rūpa-mañjarī are my treasure, my worship and adoration, the wealth of my life”; “Carelessly I lost the jewel-wealth of *prema*. Striving for what is no wealth, I gave up true wealth” — and so on.

The *smārtas*, guided by the notion of kinship by seed, impute *śūdra*-hood — the lack of *brāhmaṇa* status — to the lotus feet of Śrī Raghunātha dāsa; in their wealth of devotion this is only a debt. But to realize transcendental *brāhmaṇa*-hood in his lotus feet is the devotee's very own wealth.

*Thus ends the thirteenth chapter of the “Anubhāṣya.”*
