## 184

“One opinion”: only the true *dharma* established on the plane of nondual knowledge (*advaya-jñāna*) is “eternal,” “everlasting” (*sanātana*) and one. In it the goal (*upeya*), or *sādhya*, is one, and so too the means (*upāya*), or *sādhana*, the path (*panthā*), is one, not different from the goal. “Various *dharmas*”: the particular properties of the body and mind of each *jīva*, differing from one *jīva* to another; they are rooted in external vision rather than in the vision of one's self (*ātma-darśana*).

## 185

> Subheading: The saintly act for the good of the people; therefore only the path Mādhavendra showed carries certain meaning, that is, bestows real truth —

*Sādhu*, or *mahājana*: a great person is called a *mahājana*. Spiritual and worldly thinking hold different notions of what is “great.” The conditioned *jīva* judges by the religion of the mind (*manodharma*), that is, by knowledge born of the senses, and so he regards as *mahājanas* those who supply him with objects of enjoyment and fuel for gratifying his senses. For a businessman a *mahājana* may be his “creditor” (*uttamarṇa*); for a *karmī* bent on enjoyment, “the sage Jaimini and others,” or the authors of the *dharma-śāstras* who uphold various opinions; for those who wish to restrain the mind, “the sage Patañjali and others”; for those who walk the path of dry knowledge, the *kevalādvaita-vādīs*—the godless Kapila, Vasiṣṭha, Durvāsā, Dattātreya and the rest. For those under *rajas* and *tamas*, the *mahājanas* are kings proud of brute strength and matchless in their persistence in opposing Viṣṇu—Hiraṇyākṣa, Hiraṇyakaśipu, Rāvaṇa and his son Meghanāda, Jarāsandha and others—and devotees of their *gurus* such as Ekalavya and Karṇa. For those who think themselves men and love the company of women, the *mahājanas* are the *prajāpatis* headed by Dakṣa, who worshiped women. Worldly people may choose as *mahājanas* those who wish or strive to rid them of bodily and mental disease, grief, suffering, fear or petty wants. A *jīva* bewitched by *prakṛti* may decide that his *mahājanas* are “philosophers,” “scientists,” “historians,” “men of letters,” “poets,” “orators,” “leaders of society” or “leaders of the nation” who have no connection with Viṣṇu. For the self-deceived, the *mahājanas* are cheats and greedy hirelings. They believe that devotion to Bhagavān, the highest goal and the very nature of the soul, is bound by semen and blood; that is, appealing only to their family by birth, they lay claim to devotion to Bhagavān, the mother of self-knowledge, or to the position of *guru*. Others may count as *mahājanas* the opponents of a true *sādhu* like Śrī Haridāsa—such as the “hypocrite brāhmaṇa” (*ḍhaṅga-vipra*)[^185-1]—and those who artificially, outwardly imitate his transcendental service to Hari; experts in tricks and sorcery; *asuras* such as Pūtanā, Tṛṇāvarta, Vatsa, Baka, Agha, Dhenuka, Kālīya and Pralamba; or the enemies of Viṣṇu—Pauṇḍraka, Śṛgāla-vāsudeva, Śukra the *guru* of the Daityas, the atheist Cārvāka, Veṇa, Sugata, Arhat and the rest. So too the cheats who do not believe that Gaura-Kṛṣṇa is the real Truth and the Supreme Lord imitate Him: they proclaim themselves *avatāras*, or else, using charming words that suit the anti-Viṣṇu *manodharma* of the deceived, they want the deceived to proclaim them *avatāras*. Foolish, deceived, unfortunate people may imagine them to be *mahājanas*. In short, for those without devotion to Bhagavān, such people—“*anyābhilāṣīs*” (those with other desires), “*karmīs*,” “dry *jñānīs*,” “yogīs without *bhakti*,” or those greedy for gold, women and fame—may indeed be called “*mahājanas*.” But the *Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam*, free of envy and of all deceit, which establishes the Supreme Truth, the real substance, says (6.3.25): “As a rule, even these *mahājanas* do not know this [the glory of *bhakti*], for the divine *māyā* has thoroughly bewildered their minds. Their minds are dulled by the flowery, honeyed words of the three Vedas, and they are engaged in great sacrificial rites.” That is, the *karmīs* famed in the world as “*mahājanas*,” these preachers of *dharma*, do not know the glory of devotion to Bhagavān. Their intelligence is bewildered by Viṣṇu's *māyā*, made of the three modes; so they disregard devotion to Bhagavān, which belongs to Vaikuṇṭha, busy themselves with the sprawling *karma-kāṇḍa* founded on the worship of *prakṛti*, and are caught in the net of *māyā*. The minds of these *mahājanas* are dulled by the sweet, superficially charming eulogies (*arthavāda*) of the Ṛg, Sāma and Yajur Vedas. Although worldly people take them to be “*mahājanas*,” they have no intelligence directed to the eternal service of Puruṣottama, Śrī Bhagavān.

People of this world may be known as “heroes of action” (*karma-vīras*), honored as “heroes of *dharma*,” renowned as “heroes of knowledge,” revered as “models of detachment and renunciation.” But the *Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam* says (3.23.56): “One whose work here does not lead to *dharma*, whose *dharma* does not lead to detachment, and whose detachment does not lead to service to the feet of Him whose feet are a holy place, is dead though alive.” That is, in this world the “hero of action” who does not act for the sake of *dharma*, the “hero of *dharma*” who does not follow *dharma* for the sake of detachment, and the “hero of renunciation” who does not give up enjoyment “for Śrī Viṣṇu's pleasure” is “dead while living.” In truth, “service” is the name for pleasing Hari. And work, *dharma* or renunciation that has no love for Śrī Kṛṣṇa and no connection with Him is in reality only “sense gratification,” or “enjoyment.” Such is service to the nation, service to the public, service to society, service to the *adaiva-varṇāśrama* of birth-lineage (*śaukra*) or caste, service to the sick, the poor, the destitute, the rich, service to women, service to various gods and the like, even though the world proclaims them “the finest virtues” or “deeds worthy of remembrance at dawn.” The unfortunate *jīvas* of this world regard as famous “*mahājanas*” those who supply fuel for such sense gratification—the speakers, preachers or authors of scriptures who foster it.

The *jīva* whose intelligence rests on *prakṛti* sees only the external world; he is a slave of the senses, stricken with the disease of worldly existence. With his distorted intelligence he cannot understand or recognize a real *mahājana*, for his intelligence is always corrupted by the four defects, error (*bhrama*) and the rest.

Since beginningless time, people who see themselves in a corpse made of seven elements—blood, flesh, semen and the rest—and take pleasure in it have drifted, like a flock of sheep, in the stream of knowledge born of the senses. Some of them, of the *prākṛta-sahajiyā* community, disregard even the words of a *mahājana* like Śrī Mādhavendra Purīpāda, pile up offenses at the *mahājana's* feet, call him “narrow-minded” and so on, and so bring trouble on themselves. Others create imaginary *mahājanas* according to their own notions, founded on sense gratification, and plunge into countless offensive deeds: debauchery, lechery, the pursuit of gain, honor and fame, intrigue, violence to living beings and the like.

In truth, until the real “*mahājana*” is identified, none of the *jīva's* efforts can bear good fruit. On what a *mahājana* is, Madhya 25.54–57 says: “No one accepts the Lord as the supreme cause. Each establishes his own view by refuting the views of others. Therefore we do not learn the truth from the six philosophies; what the *mahājana* says, that we accept as truth. The words of Śrī Kṛṣṇa Caitanya are a stream of nectar; whatever He declares to be reality, that is the essence of truth” (Madhya 25.54, 55, 57)[^185-2]. That is, none of the authors of the *darśanas*—Sāṅkhya, Pātañjala and the rest—truly accepts Viṣṇu as “Īśvara.” In a word, they are all “covert” or “overt” atheists; that is, none of them is an *āstika*. They merely tried, by argument, to refute the views of others and to establish their own, so as to show off the prowess of their doctrines. Therefore, although the teachers of these scriptures are known in the world as “*mahājanas*,” in fact they are not “*mahājanas*” at all; they themselves are the most “narrow” and “narrow-minded.”

Hearing this, the followers of these so-called *mahājanas*, with their mundane knowledge born of the senses, will offend the feet of Śrī Mahāprabhu and of the pure devotee and say, “This is mere ‘bigotry’!” In their view, Śrī Kṛṣṇa Caitanya Prabhu and Śrī Mādhavendra Purīpāda too are just more *mahājanas* among those named above! Sunk in the stream of thought of the *prākṛta-sahaja-dharma* and turned into advocates of blending spirit and matter, they will of course arrive at just such a conclusion—what doubt or wonder is there in that? But those in whom the transcendental inherent nature (*svarūpa-dharma*) has awakened are illumined by its light and can see the *svarūpa* of every *jīva*. Only the *mahā-bhāgavata*, or *paramahaṁsa*, sees Adhokṣaja, and this is true vision (*sudarśana*); therefore these *niṣkiñcanas* alone are the only real “*mahājanas*.” Śrīla Mādhavendra Purī Gosvāmī too is a *niṣkiñcana mahājana*: there is no envy or deception of people in his conduct. By his conduct he preached the *daiva-varṇāśrama-dharma*; if one follows it, seeing it as the model, the *jīva* who seeks the highest good (*niḥśreyasa*) will surely attain it. Mahāprabhu taught this by showing the model of *daiva-varṇāśrama-dharma*.

The *Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam* (6.3.19–21) names the “twelve” *mahājanas*. In the age of Kali the “*mahājanas*” are the four *ācāryas* of the *sampradāyas* of pure Vaiṣṇavas, who preach devotion to Bhagavān. In our *sampradāya* the original “*mahājana*” is the lord of the Gauḍīyas, Śrī Dāmodara Svarūpa. Śrī Rūpa and Sanātana, the dearest associates of the Supreme Truth, Śrī Gaurasundara, whose form is nondifferent from him, and the *sādhus* who follow Śrī Rūpa—all are “*mahājanas*.” Śrīdhara Svāmī, a follower of Śrī Viṣṇusvāmī and a *śuddhādvaita-vādī*, is also a “*mahājana*.” Caṇḍīdāsa, Vidyāpati and Jayadeva are all “*mahājanas*.” But those who see these *mahājanas* as objects of enjoyment—who, instead of “serving” them, rush to measure them as tools for their own petty ends, or to “play *guru* over the *guru*”—those unfortunate people stand very far from these *mahājanas*. Sense gratification, or servitude to *māyā*, comes to them in the guise of an “imaginary *mahājana*”; it deceives them, hides the real path of truth from their senses and leads them astray. Therefore the endeavors of a pure devotee of Bhagavān never become the object of their mundane understanding.

> Subheading: Only the path of the pure devotee is to be followed — (*Mahābhārata*, Vana-parva, Āraṇeya-parva, ch. 313, verse 117)

(The variant reading *vedā vibhinnāḥ smṛtayo vibhinnāḥ*, “the Vedas differ, the *smṛtis* differ,” is also found.) **tarkaḥ** **apratiṣṭhaḥ** (unsteady, not firm), **śrutayaḥ** **api** (*vibhinnāḥ*: differ, for they show contradictions in addressing people of different qualification); **asau** **ṛṣiḥ** **na** (cannot be called a sage), **yasya** **mataṁ** (conclusion) **bhinnaṁ** **na** (was not); (in such an age, ruled by argument) **dharmasya** (of the eternal *dharma* of the *jīva*) **tattvaṁ** **guhāyāṁ** (in the cave of the heart of the *sampradāya* of pure *sādhus* alone, hidden from the eyes of ordinary people) **nihitam** (is concealed, hidden; therefore) **yena** (by which good path) **mahājanaḥ** (a *sādhu* of earlier times, a servant of Adhokṣaja Acyuta) **gataḥ** (has gone), **saḥ** (that alone) **panthāḥ** (is the pure path).

> Subheading: The male parrot sings Kṛṣṇa's qualities — (*Govinda-līlāmṛta*, canto 13, verse 29: the male parrot's words to the female)

**yasya** (Kṛṣṇa's) **saundaryaṁ** (enchanting form) **lalanāli-dhairya-dalanaṁ** (whose nature is to shatter the composure of all beautiful women, the girls of Vraja), **yasya** **līlā** (play full of spiritual sport) **ramā-stambhinī** (whose nature is to stun and stir Ramā), **yasya** **vīryaṁ** (prowess) **kandukitādri-varyam** (by which the best of mountains, Govardhana, the king of mountains, was made a ball), **yasya** **pāre-parārdhaṁ** (gone beyond the limit of a *parārdha*, that is, immeasurable) **amalāḥ** (faultless) **guṇāḥ**, **aho** **yasya** **śīlaṁ** (character) **sarva-janānurañjanam** (delighting all people, the devotees), **saḥ** **ayam** **asmat-prabhuḥ** (the sole refuge of servants like me) **viśva-janīna-kīrtiḥ** (whose fame is for the good of all people) **jagan-mohanaḥ** (the most beautiful in the world) **kṛṣṇaḥ** **viśvam** **avatāt** (may He protect).

> Subheading: The female parrot sings Rādhikā's qualities — (*Govinda-līlāmṛta*, canto 13, verse 30: the female parrot's words to the male)

**śrī-rādhikāyāḥ** **priyatā** (love), **svarūpatā**[^212-1] (extraordinary beauty; or the form of *mahābhāva*, that by which Her own self is revealed [*nirūpyate*]), **su-śīlatā** (fine character, good conduct), **nartana-gāna-cāturī** (skill, expertise or refinement in dancing and singing), **guṇāli-sampat** (Her wealth, a succession of qualities), **kavitā** (poetic gift)—**ca** all of these, **jagan-mano-mohana-citta-mohinī** (enchanting, that is, delighting, the mind of Kṛṣṇa, who enchants the mind of the world), **rājate** (shine).

**śārike** (O Śārikā), **vaṁśī-dhārī** (the holder of the flute) **jagan-nārī-citta-hārī** (the thief of the hearts of the women of the world, of the fourteen worlds) **gopa-nārībhiḥ** (with the girls of Vraja) **vihārī** (absorbed in sport), **saḥ** (that famous) **madana-mohanaḥ** **jīyāt** (may He reign supreme over all).

## 216

> Subheading: The female parrot's song: Kṛṣṇa is Madana-mohana because of Śrī Rādhā — (*Govinda-līlāmṛta*, canto 13, verse 32: the female parrot's words to the male)

(O Śuka,) **yadā** (when) (Kṛṣṇa) **rādhā-saṅge** **bhāti** (shines), **tadā** (only then is) (Kṛṣṇa) **madana-mohanaḥ**; **anyathā** (otherwise, when Kṛṣṇa is without Rādhā's company, He) **svayam** (Himself) **viśva-mohaḥ** (the enchanter of the world) **api** **svayaṁ** **madana-mohitaḥ** (is enchanted by Madana, Kandarpa—by the principle “Searching for Rādhikā here and there, His heart tormented by the wounds of Anaṅga's arrows…”[^216-1]).
