## 127

> Subheading: Since the Māyāvādī does not believe in spiritual play, he utters the holy name with contempt; because of this offense to the name, his utterance must not be heard —

## 126–127

*Doṣa*: reviling. Names such as “Brahman,” “Caitanya,” “Ātmā,” “Paramātmā,” “Jagadīśa,” “Īśvara,” “Virāṭ,” “Vibhu,” “Bhūmā,” “Viśvarūpa” and “Vyāpaka,” as the one who utters them understands them, do not point to Kṛṣṇa's magnanimity (*audārya*) or sweetness (*mādhurya*); they point only partly to His majesty (*aiśvarya*). Therefore these names do not reveal that Kṛṣṇa is the embodiment of spiritual *rasa*, as His principal names do. This is why the Māyāvādīs, the worshipers of *prakṛti*, are great offenders. In the end they hold the Truth to be featureless, devoid of spiritual play: they disbelieve and doubt that the Truth's spiritual name, form, qualities, pastimes and distinctive associates are nondual knowledge (*advaya-jñāna*). Nor do they revere Bhagavān Kṛṣṇa's principal names with faith as the sole goal (*sādhya*) and the sole means (*sādhana*). No one who seeks the eternal, highest good should hear any talk of the highest goal from their mouths.

## 134

> Subheading: Kṛṣṇa's name, Kṛṣṇa's body and Kṛṣṇa's pastimes are transcendental, spiritual and self-manifest —

Kṛṣṇa's body, Kṛṣṇa's name, Kṛṣṇa's form, Kṛṣṇa's qualities, Kṛṣṇa's pastimes—the distinctive features of His associates—and the rest are full of eternity, knowledge and bliss. Therefore a *jīva* who identifies with the three modes, *sattva* and the others, cannot perceive them as material form, taste, smell, sound or touch; that is, they are not objects of enjoyment for the *jīva's* senses, which crave to enjoy fruits. All of them are self-manifest reality (*svataḥ-prakāśa-vastu*), eternal, spiritual and blissful. Material things, which fall under the modes, have name, form, qualities and actions that differ from one another materially; there is no oneness among them. But in Kṛṣṇa, Adhokṣaja (He who is beyond the senses), there is no such “difference.”

## 142

> Subheading: The *tulasī* from Nārāyaṇa's feet produces pure *sāttvika* transformations in the body and mind even of the four Sanas, knowers of Brahman — (*Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam*, Canto 3, ch. 15, verse 43)

The dialogue of Maitreya and Vidura. The demigods were terrified by the power of the child in Diti's womb, and Brahmā tells them the background of the two *asuras* she carries. Once, long ago, the *brahmarṣis*—the four Sanas, or Kumāras—wished to see Śrī Nārāyaṇa and entered Vaikuṇṭha, but at the seventh gate the two doorkeepers named Jaya and Vijaya stopped them. Then the Kumāras, in anger driven by the urge to violence that is born of a sense of difference, cursed them to take birth in the wombs of *asuras*. The all-knowing Bhagavān Padmanābha learned of this and at once came there Himself with Lakṣmī. The *ṛṣis* saw before them the One they worshiped, Bhagavān Nārāyaṇa Himself, the fruit of their *brahma-samādhi*, and bowed down. The verse describes the transformations of feeling in these *ātmārāmas*, knowers of Brahman:

**aravinda-nayanasya** (of the lotus-eyed) **tasya** (of that Bhagavān) **padāravinda-kiñjalka-miśra-tulasī-makaranda-vāyuḥ** (the breeze carrying the nectar of the *tulasī* mixed with the filaments [*keśara*] of His lotus feet) **sva-vivareṇa** (through their nostrils) **antar-gataḥ** (having entered within) **akṣara-juṣām** (devoted to the featureless Brahman) **api** **teṣāṁ** (of those Kumāras, Sanaka and the others) **citta-tanvoḥ** (in mind and body) **saṅkṣobham** (in the mind, rapture; in the body, hair standing on end and the like) **cakāra** (produced).
