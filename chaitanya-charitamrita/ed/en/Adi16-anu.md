## 11

> Subheading: The many views of many sages in many *śāstras* bewilder the mind —

(*Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam* 7.13.8): “…He should not study many books… he should not live by expounding [the scriptures]…” That is, one should not master many books and arts, nor make a living by expounding the *śāstras*. Whoever seeks the highest good (whoever wishes to serve the Lord) must first give up this temptation. (*Bhakti-rasāmṛta-sindhu*, Pūrva, second wave.)

(*Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam* 11.21.30, 36): “People whose minds are bewildered by such flowery speech, who are proud and very greedy, find no taste even in talk about Me. The Veda in sound is very hard to understand; it is manifest in the vital air, the senses and the mind; it is boundless, deep and unfathomable, like the ocean.”

This means the following. The merchants of the *śāstras*, who support the *karma-kāṇḍa* and the *jñāna-kāṇḍa*, speak words flowery as honey (charming), yet hostile to selfless devotion to the Lord, full of envy and aimed at enjoying fruits. Hearing or reading such words, inexperienced and fickle people of weak faith cannot grasp the supreme greatness and beauty of devotion to Kṛṣṇa and love for Kṛṣṇa, the *jīva*'s eternal *sādhana* and eternal *sādhya*. Turned away [from the Lord] since time without beginning, they very easily submit to the *karmīs* and *jñānīs*, and lose the mercy of the pure devotee of Kṛṣṇa, the *jīva*'s well-wisher, who is free from envy. So, lacking the pious merit (*sukṛti*) that turns one toward *bhakti*, they are flung far from pure *bhakti*, the only path to eternal good, and at last reach endless misfortune and the utmost misery. When Śrī Gaurasundara in Vārāṇasī expounded the truth of *sādhya* and *sādhana* in answer to the questions of Śrī Sanātana Gosvāmī Prabhu, Tapana Miśra was present there. He heard the Lord and found the answer to his doubts. For the good of ignorant *jīvas* who follow many *śāstras* and many gurus, Mahāprabhu taught a lesson through the example of His devotee Tapana Miśra (by resolving his doubts).

## 25

> Subheading: Marriage to Śrī Viṣṇupriyā, daughter of the royal *paṇḍita* Sanātana, and the defeat of Keśava Kāśmīrī —

**The *digvijayī*** was a *paṇḍita* from Kashmir named Keśava. At that time he was traveling through various parts of Bhāratavarṣa, intent on conquering the *paṇḍitas* by his genius, and at last he came to Gauḍa-deśa, to Navadvīpa. After his defeat by Śrī Gaurasundara he came to know who Śrīman Mahāprabhu truly is, and in order to serve the Lord he entered the Nimbārka sampradāya. He wrote a gloss (*ṭippanī*) called *Kaustubha-prabhā* on the *Vedānta-kaustubha*, Śrīnivāsācārya's commentary on the *Pārijāta-bhāṣya* that Nimbārka composed on the *Vedānta-darśana*. The *Bhakti-ratnākara*, in its twelfth wave, says: “The disciplic succession of Nimbāditya: 1. Śrīnivāsācārya, 2. Viśvācārya, 3. Puruṣottama, 4. Vilāsa, 5. Svarūpa, 6. Mādhava, 7. Balabhadra, 8. Padma, 9. Śyāma, 10. Gopāla, 11. Kṛpā, 12. Devācārya, 13. Sundara Bhaṭṭa, 14. Padmanābha, 15. Upendra, 16. Rāmacandra, 17. Vāmana, 18. Kṛṣṇa, 19. Padmākara, 20. Śravaṇa, 21. Bhūri, 22. Mādhava, 23. Śyāma, 24. Gopāla, 25. Balabhadra, 26. Gopīnātha, 27. Keśava, 28. Gokula, 29. Keśava Kāśmīrī.” And again in the *Bhakti-ratnākara*: “He chanted the *mantra* of the goddess Sarasvatī; all learning was revealed to him, and his power grew. Having conquered all directions, he became famous as the ‘*digvijayī*.’ He was from Kashmir, a most cultured brāhmaṇa. Giving up everything, he departed by the Lord's order. He described the pastimes (?)… under the name ‘Laghu-Keśava.’”[^25-1] See the *Vaiṣṇava-mañjuṣā* (issue 1).

## 101

> Subheading: There are faults even in the poetry of Jayadeva, Kālidāsa and Bhavabhūti —

**Bhavabhūti**, or Śrīkaṇṭha, wrote the Sanskrit plays *Mālatī-mādhava*, *Uttara-carita*, *Vīra-carita* and others. He lived in the reign of King Bhoja. A resident of Padmanagara, he was the son of Nīlakaṇṭha and the grandson of a *śrotriya* brāhmaṇa of the Kāśyapa *gotra* named Bhaṭṭa Gopāla.

**Kālidāsa** was a great poet, one of the renowned “nine jewels” at the court of Emperor Vikramāditya. He wrote the *Raghu-vaṁśa*, *Kumāra-sambhava*, *Abhijñāna-śakuntala*, *Megha-dūta* and others, some thirty or forty Sanskrit epic poems, plays and works on other subjects.

**Jayadeva**: see Ādi 13.42.
