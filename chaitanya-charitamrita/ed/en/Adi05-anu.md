## 14–18

Śrī Jīva writes in the *Śrī Kṛṣṇa-sandarbha* (section 106): “What, then, is that abode where He sports? To this it is said. The *Skanda Purāṇa* says: ‘As the cities dear to Bhagavān exist on earth, so they exist in Vaikuṇṭha, honored for the sake of those pastimes.’ According to these words, the places that exist in Vaikuṇṭha should be understood to be these very places. And they lie above all the Vaikuṇṭhas. * * * The *Svāyambhuva-āgama* also says that this place stands in its own right, above all: in the dialogue of Īśvara and Devī, in the section on meditation on the fourteen-syllable [mantra], in the eighty-fifth chapter (*paṭala*): ‘One should remember the all-pervading Vaikuṇṭha, strewn with many wish-fulfilling creepers. Below it is *prakṛti*, the equilibrium of the *guṇas*, the cause of all.’ * * * Therefore, by the rule ‘as they exist on earth’ as well, it is established that Śrī Kṛṣṇaloka, consisting of Dvārakā, Mathurā and Gokula, exists in its own right and, as the place of the pastimes of Bhagavān Himself, lies above all. Hence Vṛndāvana is that very Gokula which shines above all and is renowned as Goloka. In the *Brahma-saṁhitā*: ‘The great abode called Gokula is a lotus of a thousand petals. * * * Around it is a wondrous quadrangle called Śvetadvīpa.’ * * * The abode of this Śrī Kṛṣṇa, fit for living together with Nanda, Yaśodā and others, is the great inner palace; of its nature it is said that its origin, that is, its eternal manifestation, is from a part of Ananta, Śrī Baladeva. Read in a double sense,[^14-1] the words also convey this: there is the origin, that is, the dwelling, of Śrī Baladeva Himself, whose part is Ananta. * * * Next the coverings of Gokula are described, beginning ‘Outside it is a quadrangle.’ Outside that Gokula, on every side, a quadrangular, that is, four-cornered, place is called Śvetadvīpa, since that part has no particular name ‘Gokula.’ But the inner circle of the quadrangle should be known as Vṛndāvana, and the outer circle only as Śvetadvīpa; its other name is ‘Goloka.’ * * * Vaikuṇṭha is called Brahmaloka. * * * In the *Nārada-pañcarātra*, in the *Vijaya-ākhyāna*: ‘There, above all, in Goloka, Śrī Govinda Himself always sports, full of supreme bliss, the Lord of the gopīs and of Gokula.’ Thus it is established that Śrī Kṛṣṇaloka lies above all. It is also determined that this world, divided into parts according to the differences of those pastimes and associates, consists of three places — Dvārakā, Mathurā and Gokula. Elsewhere [in scripture], however, the places of the same names renowned on earth are spoken of as these very places: for of them, as of the other Vaikuṇṭhas, it is said that they lie beyond the material world, are eternal, have an unearthly form and are the eternal abode of Bhagavān.”

In what kind of abode does this Bhagavān move about? On this it is said: “As cities dear to Bhagavān exist in this material world, so three cities abide in Vaikuṇṭha for the sake of His pastimes.” According to these words of the *Skanda Purāṇa*, it should be known that the places existing in Vaikuṇṭha are the very same places that are in the material world. Above the material creation lie all the Vaikuṇṭhas. The *Svāyambhuva-tantra*, too, says that the place of Vaikuṇṭha stands in its own right, above all. In that book, in the section on meditation on the fourteen-syllable [mantra], in the 85th *paṭala*, in the dialogue of Devī and Maheśvara, it is said: “One should remember the all-pervading, undivided Vaikuṇṭha, strewn with many wish-fulfilling creepers. Below it lies *prakṛti* — the equilibrium of the *guṇas*, the cause of all material causes.” Therefore from the rule “as the abodes of Hari exist on earth, so also there” we understand that Kṛṣṇaloka, consisting of Dvārakā, Mathurā and Gokula, exists in its own right. It is the field of the pastimes of Bhagavān Himself, and so it is established that these abodes lie above all. Hence Vṛndāvana-Gokula is renowned as “Goloka,” shining above all. In the *Brahma-saṁhitā*: “The great abode shaped like a lotus of a thousand petals is known as ‘Gokula.’ The wondrous place around it, a quadrangle — that is, bounded by four straight lines — is called ‘Śvetadvīpa.’” In that abode of Kṛṣṇa is His great inner palace, fit for living with Nanda, Yaśodā and others. Its nature is described thus: that abode eternally arises from Ananta, the part of Baladeva Prabhu. The *tantra* scriptures convey the same: the abode of Bhagavān is where Anantadeva, the part of Baladeva, appears and dwells. The coverings of Gokula are described thus. Outside Gokula, on every side, lies a quadrangular place (a four-cornered field) known as “Śvetadvīpa.” The part that is Śvetadvīpa does not bear the name “Gokula”; but the inner circle of the quadrangle is known by the name “Vṛndāvana,” and only the outer circle should be known as “Śvetadvīpa”; its other name is “Goloka.” The word “Brahmaloka” means “Vaikuṇṭha.” In the *Nārada-pañcarātra*, in the account of the *Vijaya-ākhyāna*, it is said that in that abode, above all, in Goloka, Govindadeva Himself — Gopīnātha, the Lord of Gokula — always sports in supreme bliss. From this it is established that Kṛṣṇaloka lies above all worlds. It is also determined that this very Kṛṣṇaloka, by the differences of pastimes and associates and by the differences of parts, is the three places with many chambers, named “Dvārakā,” “Mathurā” and “Gokula.” Elsewhere [in scripture] we hear that the places of the same names on earth, in the material world, are of the same nature: for of them, as of the other Vaikuṇṭhas, it is said that they lie beyond the material world, are eternal, have an unearthly form and are the eternal abode of Bhagavān. Therefore they too should be known as nondifferent [from the abodes of Kṛṣṇaloka].

## 28

Śrī, Bhū and Nīlā. Following a Bengali reading, some call Nīlā “Līlā-śakti.” These three potencies abide in Vaikuṇṭha beside Nārāyaṇa. Once Bhūta-yogī, Sara-yogī and Bhrānta-yogī (the Āḻvārs) took shelter at night in a brāhmaṇa's house in the village of Gehalī. Then Nārāyaṇa entered that house and revealed His form to them.

*Prapannāmṛta*, Chapter 77, verses 61–62: “They saw Viṣṇu, the original Bhagavān. He was mounted on Tārkṣya (Garuḍa) and looked like a cloud with lightning; He bore Lakṣmī on His chest, His eyes were like lotuses, and in two hands shone the conch and the disc. His arms reached to His knees, His body was lovely, and Bhūmi and Nīlā shone at His two sides. He wore yellow garments, His body was adorned with ornaments and anointed with sandal paste, and He had four arms.”

In the *Sītā Upaniṣad*: “Mahālakṣmī of the Lord of the gods is both different and nondifferent from Him; She consists of the conscious and the unconscious. As potency this goddess is of three kinds: *icchā-śakti* (the potency of will), *kriyā-śakti* (the potency of action) and *sākṣāt-śakti* (the direct potency). *Icchā-śakti* is of three kinds: Śrī, Bhūmi and Līlā.”

In his commentary on the *Gītā* (Chapter 4, verse 6) Śrī Madhvācārya cites scripture: “She who is the mother of the *mahat* and the rest, conceived of as Śrī, Bhū and Nīlā, and the bewildering one named Durgā — through them Viṣṇu, though unborn, is renowned as if born among those whose minds are bewildered by the power of His own *cit*[-potency].” * * * “Mahāmāyā, Vaiṣṇavī, divided as Śrī, Bhū and Durgā, is infinitely below His potency. Yet because she has taken shelter of the Lord, even Ananta, Brahmā, Rudra and others have not a fraction of her power. Without the grace of Viṣṇu even they cannot overcome her.” (*Vyāsa-yoga*, Chapter 7, section 14.)

Madhva's commentary on the *Gītā* (Chapter 14, verse 3): “‘The great Brahman’ is *prakṛti*. She is divided as Śrī, Bhū and Durgā. Umā, Sarasvatī and others are other *jīvas*, possessing a part of her.” So too the *Kārṣāyaṇa-śruti*: “Śrī, Bhū and Durgā are the great *māyā*; she brings forth the worlds and binds the universe. Umā, Vāk and others are other *jīvas*, her parts; in all the Vedas they are sung as one with her in nature.”

Śrī Jīva Prabhu in the *Bhagavat-sandarbha* (section 80): “As the *Padma Purāṇa* says: ‘The eternal, auspicious form of the Lord abides in the supreme abode; He is eternally delighted by the sovereign Śrī and by Bhūmi and is surrounded by them.’ In the *Mahā-saṁhitā* this is distinguished by name and nature as well: ‘His threefold potency — Śrī, Bhū and Durgā — which is divided, is the *jīva-māyā* of the Great Soul. *Ātma-māyā* is His will, and *guṇa-māyā* is material.’” (Ibid., section 22): “Śrī here is the potency that maintains the world, Bhū the potency of its creation, Durgā the potency of its dissolution. His potency that has become distinct in these forms and is directed toward the *jīvas* is called *jīva-māyā*. In the *Padma Purāṇa*, in the dialogue of Śrī Kṛṣṇa and Satyabhāmā, after the words ‘I Myself, dividing threefold, abide through the three kinds of *guṇas*,’ it is said: ‘Then all the gods, hearing this and urged by those words, full of devotion, bowed down to Gaurī, Lakṣmī and Dharā.’”
