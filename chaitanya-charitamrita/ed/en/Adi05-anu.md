## 14–18

Śrī Jīva writes in the *Śrī Kṛṣṇa-sandarbha* (section 106): “What, then, is that abode where He sports? To this it is said. The *Skanda Purāṇa* says: ‘As the cities dear to Bhagavān exist on earth, so they exist in Vaikuṇṭha, honored for the sake of those pastimes.’ According to these words, the places that exist in Vaikuṇṭha should be understood to be these very places. And they lie above all the Vaikuṇṭhas. * * * The *Svāyambhuva-āgama* also says that this place stands in its own right, above all: in the dialogue of Īśvara and Devī, in the section on meditation on the fourteen-syllable [mantra], in the eighty-fifth chapter (*paṭala*): ‘One should remember the all-pervading Vaikuṇṭha, strewn with many wish-fulfilling creepers. Below it is *prakṛti*, the equilibrium of the *guṇas*, the cause of all.’ * * * Therefore, by the rule ‘as they exist on earth’ as well, it is established that Śrī Kṛṣṇaloka, consisting of Dvārakā, Mathurā and Gokula, exists in its own right and, as the place of the pastimes of Bhagavān Himself, lies above all. Hence Vṛndāvana is that very Gokula which shines above all and is renowned as Goloka. In the *Brahma-saṁhitā*: ‘The great abode called Gokula is a lotus of a thousand petals. * * * Around it is a wondrous quadrangle called Śvetadvīpa.’ * * * The abode of this Śrī Kṛṣṇa, fit for living together with Nanda, Yaśodā and others, is the great inner palace; of its nature it is said that its origin, that is, its eternal manifestation, is from a part of Ananta, Śrī Baladeva. Read in a double sense,[^14-1] the words also convey this: there is the origin, that is, the dwelling, of Śrī Baladeva Himself, whose part is Ananta. * * * Next the coverings of Gokula are described, beginning ‘Outside it is a quadrangle.’ Outside that Gokula, on every side, a quadrangular, that is, four-cornered, place is called Śvetadvīpa, since that part has no particular name ‘Gokula.’ But the inner circle of the quadrangle should be known as Vṛndāvana, and the outer circle only as Śvetadvīpa; its other name is ‘Goloka.’ * * * Vaikuṇṭha is called Brahmaloka. * * * In the *Nārada-pañcarātra*, in the *Vijaya-ākhyāna*: ‘There, above all, in Goloka, Śrī Govinda Himself always sports, full of supreme bliss, the Lord of the gopīs and of Gokula.’ Thus it is established that Śrī Kṛṣṇaloka lies above all. It is also determined that this world, divided into parts according to the differences of those pastimes and associates, consists of three places — Dvārakā, Mathurā and Gokula. Elsewhere [in scripture], however, the places of the same names renowned on earth are spoken of as these very places: for of them, as of the other Vaikuṇṭhas, it is said that they lie beyond the material world, are eternal, have an unearthly form and are the eternal abode of Bhagavān.”

In what kind of abode does this Bhagavān move about? On this it is said: “As cities dear to Bhagavān exist in this material world, so three cities abide in Vaikuṇṭha for the sake of His pastimes.” According to these words of the *Skanda Purāṇa*, it should be known that the places existing in Vaikuṇṭha are the very same places that are in the material world. Above the material creation lie all the Vaikuṇṭhas. The *Svāyambhuva-tantra*, too, says that the place of Vaikuṇṭha stands in its own right, above all. In that book, in the section on meditation on the fourteen-syllable [mantra], in the 85th *paṭala*, in the dialogue of Devī and Maheśvara, it is said: “One should remember the all-pervading, undivided Vaikuṇṭha, strewn with many wish-fulfilling creepers. Below it lies *prakṛti* — the equilibrium of the *guṇas*, the cause of all material causes.” Therefore from the rule “as the abodes of Hari exist on earth, so also there” we understand that Kṛṣṇaloka, consisting of Dvārakā, Mathurā and Gokula, exists in its own right. It is the field of the pastimes of Bhagavān Himself, and so it is established that these abodes lie above all. Hence Vṛndāvana-Gokula is renowned as “Goloka,” shining above all. In the *Brahma-saṁhitā*: “The great abode shaped like a lotus of a thousand petals is known as ‘Gokula.’ The wondrous place around it, a quadrangle — that is, bounded by four straight lines — is called ‘Śvetadvīpa.’” In that abode of Kṛṣṇa is His great inner palace, fit for living with Nanda, Yaśodā and others. Its nature is described thus: that abode eternally arises from Ananta, the part of Baladeva Prabhu. The *tantra* scriptures convey the same: the abode of Bhagavān is where Anantadeva, the part of Baladeva, appears and dwells. The coverings of Gokula are described thus. Outside Gokula, on every side, lies a quadrangular place (a four-cornered field) known as “Śvetadvīpa.” The part that is Śvetadvīpa does not bear the name “Gokula”; but the inner circle of the quadrangle is known by the name “Vṛndāvana,” and only the outer circle should be known as “Śvetadvīpa”; its other name is “Goloka.” The word “Brahmaloka” means “Vaikuṇṭha.” In the *Nārada-pañcarātra*, in the account of the *Vijaya-ākhyāna*, it is said that in that abode, above all, in Goloka, Govindadeva Himself — Gopīnātha, the Lord of Gokula — always sports in supreme bliss. From this it is established that Kṛṣṇaloka lies above all worlds. It is also determined that this very Kṛṣṇaloka, by the differences of pastimes and associates and by the differences of parts, is the three places with many chambers, named “Dvārakā,” “Mathurā” and “Gokula.” Elsewhere [in scripture] we hear that the places of the same names on earth, in the material world, are of the same nature: for of them, as of the other Vaikuṇṭhas, it is said that they lie beyond the material world, are eternal, have an unearthly form and are the eternal abode of Bhagavān. Therefore they too should be known as nondifferent [from the abodes of Kṛṣṇaloka].

## 28

Śrī, Bhū and Nīlā. Following a Bengali reading, some call Nīlā “Līlā-śakti.” These three potencies abide in Vaikuṇṭha beside Nārāyaṇa. Once Bhūta-yogī, Sara-yogī and Bhrānta-yogī (the Āḻvārs) took shelter at night in a brāhmaṇa's house in the village of Gehalī. Then Nārāyaṇa entered that house and revealed His form to them.

*Prapannāmṛta*, Chapter 77, verses 61–62: “They saw Viṣṇu, the original Bhagavān. He was mounted on Tārkṣya (Garuḍa) and looked like a cloud with lightning; He bore Lakṣmī on His chest, His eyes were like lotuses, and in two hands shone the conch and the disc. His arms reached to His knees, His body was lovely, and Bhūmi and Nīlā shone at His two sides. He wore yellow garments, His body was adorned with ornaments and anointed with sandal paste, and He had four arms.”

In the *Sītā Upaniṣad*: “Mahālakṣmī of the Lord of the gods is both different and nondifferent from Him; She consists of the conscious and the unconscious. As potency this goddess is of three kinds: *icchā-śakti* (the potency of will), *kriyā-śakti* (the potency of action) and *sākṣāt-śakti* (the direct potency). *Icchā-śakti* is of three kinds: Śrī, Bhūmi and Līlā.”

In his commentary on the *Gītā* (Chapter 4, verse 6) Śrī Madhvācārya cites scripture: “She who is the mother of the *mahat* and the rest, conceived of as Śrī, Bhū and Nīlā, and the bewildering one named Durgā — through them Viṣṇu, though unborn, is renowned as if born among those whose minds are bewildered by the power of His own *cit*[-potency].” * * * “Mahāmāyā, Vaiṣṇavī, divided as Śrī, Bhū and Durgā, is infinitely below His potency. Yet because she has taken shelter of the Lord, even Ananta, Brahmā, Rudra and others have not a fraction of her power. Without the grace of Viṣṇu even they cannot overcome her.” (*Vyāsa-yoga*, Chapter 7, section 14.)

Madhva's commentary on the *Gītā* (Chapter 14, verse 3): “‘The great Brahman’ is *prakṛti*. She is divided as Śrī, Bhū and Durgā. Umā, Sarasvatī and others are other *jīvas*, possessing a part of her.” So too the *Kārṣāyaṇa-śruti*: “Śrī, Bhū and Durgā are the great *māyā*; she brings forth the worlds and binds the universe. Umā, Vāk and others are other *jīvas*, her parts; in all the Vedas they are sung as one with her in nature.”

Śrī Jīva Prabhu in the *Bhagavat-sandarbha* (section 80): “As the *Padma Purāṇa* says: ‘The eternal, auspicious form of the Lord abides in the supreme abode; He is eternally delighted by the sovereign Śrī and by Bhūmi and is surrounded by them.’ In the *Mahā-saṁhitā* this is distinguished by name and nature as well: ‘His threefold potency — Śrī, Bhū and Durgā — which is divided, is the *jīva-māyā* of the Great Soul. *Ātma-māyā* is His will, and *guṇa-māyā* is material.’” (Ibid., section 22): “Śrī here is the potency that maintains the world, Bhū the potency of its creation, Durgā the potency of its dissolution. His potency that has become distinct in these forms and is directed toward the *jīvas* is called *jīva-māyā*. In the *Padma Purāṇa*, in the dialogue of Śrī Kṛṣṇa and Satyabhāmā, after the words ‘I Myself, dividing threefold, abide through the three kinds of *guṇas*,’ it is said: ‘Then all the gods, hearing this and urged by those words, full of devotion, bowed down to Gaurī, Lakṣmī and Dharā.’”

## 36

> Subheading: (*Bhakti-rasāmṛta-sindhu*, *Pūrva-vibhāga*, *Sādhana-bhakti-laharī*, verse 278)

**yat** (if in some scripture) **arīṇāṁ** (of the enemies of Bhagavān) **priyāṇāṁ ca** (and of the devotees of Bhagavān) **ekam prāpyam** **uditam** (is declared), **tat** **kiraṇārkopamā-juṣoḥ brahma-kṛṣṇayoḥ aikyāt** (because of the oneness — that is, it should be understood that the featureless Brahman, which is like the rays, and Kṛṣṇa, who is like the sun, are in truth nondifferent).

*Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam* 10.2.32: “Others, O lotus-eyed Lord, think themselves liberated, but their intelligence is impure because they lack devotion to You. Having climbed with difficulty to the highest position, they fall down from there, for they have neglected Your feet.”

Śrī Rūpa Prabhu writes in the *Laghu-bhāgavatāmṛta*, describing the glory of Śrī Kṛṣṇa (25–37): “There, in the fourth part [of the *Viṣṇu Purāṇa*] (*Viṣṇu Purāṇa* 4.15.1–10), is Maitreya's question: ‘Slain by Viṣṇu as Hiraṇyakaśipu and as Rāvaṇa, he obtained enjoyments unattainable even by the immortals. Yet then he did not attain *sāyujya* with Him. How, then, did he, as Śiśupāla, attain *sāyujya* with the eternal Hari?’ Parāśara's answer: ‘To slay the lord of the Daityas, He who creates, maintains and destroys all the worlds took an unprecedented body and manifested the form of Nṛsiṁha. But Hiraṇyakaśipu did not think, “This is Viṣṇu.” He thought, “This is a being born of an unsurpassed store of merit.” His one-pointed mind, driven by an excess of *rajas*, was absorbed in that thought. Therefore, having died at His hand, he obtained, as the Ten-headed One (Rāvaṇa), an unsurpassed wealth of enjoyment surpassing everything in the three worlds. But his mind did not dissolve into that beginningless and endless Bhagavān, the Supreme Brahman, for he had not made Him his support. As the Ten-headed One, too, subject to Anaṅga (Kāma), he gave his heart to Jānakī and saw only the form of Him who had taken the form of Daśaratha's son. He did not think, “This is Acyuta,” and because of his attachment, at the hour of death his heart saw in Him only a man. Then the sole fruit of being felled by Acyuta was birth as Śiśupāla in the line of the kings of Cedi, renowned throughout the earth, and unimpeded wealth. At that time all the names of Bhagavān found their causes [in Kṛṣṇa]. Therefore his mind, bound by an enmity toward Him that had run through many births, endlessly uttered all these names of Acyuta, prompted by those causes — in abuse, threats and the like. And because of his deep-rooted enmity, His form never left his mind — not while he walked, ate, bathed, sat or lay down, nor in any other state. So, uttering His names in his abuse and holding Him alone in his heart, when the hour of his destruction came, freed of faults such as enmity, he saw Bhagavān — shining with the garland of rays of the disc He had hurled, the embodiment of imperishable effulgence, the Supreme Brahman. At that very moment he was slain by Bhagavān's disc; remembrance of Him burned up his entire store of sins; Bhagavān brought him to his end — and he dissolved into Him alone. All this I have told you. For this Bhagavān, even when glorified and remembered with persistent enmity, bestows a fruit that all the gods and *asuras* find hard to attain. What then of those who possess true devotion?’

“Parāśara did not say here that these two were [the Lord's] associates. It is said only that the two of them had three births. Therefore they are not held to be [the Lord's] associates in every *kalpa*; otherwise their fall in every *kalpa* would make no sense.

“When Hari manifested the wondrous form of Nṛsiṁha, Hiraṇyakaśipu had no firm idea that this was Viṣṇu. He concluded, ‘This is someone endowed with merit.’ His mind was driven by an excess of *rajas*, and, absorbed in that idea, he then obtained as Rāvaṇa the best of all and most rare wealth of enjoyment, whose only cause was his death [at His hand]. Since he was not certain that this was Viṣṇu, there was no unbroken absorption through intense enmity. And without it enmity leads only to hell, as with Vena. Yet he obtained wealth as the fruit of death at His hand. Remembering this good, the sage spoke with the word *eva* (‘only’). Since there was no absorption and his faults were not destroyed, he did not see [the Lord] as pure; and so, although the form of the Supreme Brahman was manifest, he did not dissolve into Him. As Rāvaṇa too, having given himself over to great *kāma*, he likewise saw a man in Śrī Rāma even at the hour of death. Therefore, as king of Cedi, he again obtained the highest wealth. Then in Kṛṣṇa there arose causes for all the names of the Lord of Ramā. From this he concluded that He was Viṣṇu — for He had twice been the cause of his death. Out of intense enmity and great absorption he endlessly uttered all those names, constantly abusing, threatening and so on. And seeing such a form and knowing for certain, ‘This is Viṣṇu,’ he remembered it everywhere and always, just like the names. His heap of sins born of enmity was burned; when the disc was hurled, its radiance removed his demonic nature, and at the end, his vision purified, he saw the radiant Supreme Brahman in a form like a man's. When the blow of the disc destroyed the body of the Daitya, he dissolved into that very Supreme Brahman. Having said this, [the sage] recalled that Bakī (Pūtanā) and others attained liberation through [Kṛṣṇa's] childhood pastimes, while Kālanemi and others, even by the Lord's will, did not attain liberation in other [avatāras] — and he said again: ‘For this Bhagavān …’ *Hi* (‘for’) means ‘as is well known’: this Kṛṣṇa is Bhagavān Himself. Therefore He quickly draws the hearts both of those who love Him and of those who hate Him. Hence the greatness spoken of in the words ‘when glorified …’ is no wonder here.”

Paraphrase of the meaning: “In the fourth part of the *Viṣṇu Purāṇa* Maitreya asks Parāśara: ‘That Daitya who, in the bodies of Hiraṇyakaśipu and Rāvaṇa, obtained enjoyments unattainable even by the immortals, yet did not obtain liberation — how did he, in the body of Śiśupāla, attain *sāyujya* with Śrī Kṛṣṇa?’ Parāśara's answer: ‘When Śrī Nṛsiṁhadeva appeared, Hiraṇyakaśipu did not think of Him, “This is Viṣṇu,” but took Him for some being born of a heap of merit. Because of the predominance of *rajo-guṇa*, at the hour of death he could not think of His form. But because he died at His hand, in the body of Rāvaṇa he obtained boundless wealth of enjoyment that gave him power over the three worlds. He did not regard Bhagavān as his support (*ālambana*), that is, as the *viṣaya-vigraha*, the one to be served — so his mind did not dissolve into Bhagavān. In the body of Rāvaṇa, subject to *kāma*, he set his heart on Jānakī and only saw the form of Bhagavān Śrī Rāmacandra. But at the hour of death he did not understand that Śrī Rāma was Viṣṇu: in his heart there was only the idea that He was a man. Then, because he fell at Śrī Rāma's hand, he was born in the body of Śiśupāla in the renowned line of the kings of Cedi and obtained great wealth. Śrī Kṛṣṇa was called Vāsudeva, and [Śiśupāla] saw Viṣṇu in Him. For many births he had been hostile to Him; that enmity had grown firmly into his heart, and so even in abuse and threats he uttered the names of Kṛṣṇa. Because of his deep-rooted enmity the beautiful form of Bhagavān never left the mind of Śiśupāla, absorbed in Kṛṣṇa, in any state — not while he walked, ate, bathed, sat, lay down and so on. He uttered that name in anger and the like and held that form in his heart; so at the last hour his offenses — enmity and the rest — went away, and in the radiance of the rays of the Sudarśana disc that had come to destroy him, he saw the true form of Bhagavān, the Supreme Brahman. Remembrance of Bhagavān (though hostile) burned up his heap of sins; slain by Bhagavān's disc, Śiśupāla came near to Bhagavān and dissolved into Him. O Maitreya, this is the answer to your question. If the haters of Kṛṣṇa, by hostile cultivation (*pratikūla-anuśīlana*), even while persisting in enmity, attain a good destination, what doubt can there be that pure devotees, by favorable cultivation, will attain the highest goal of all — the lotus feet of Kṛṣṇa, that is, love for Kṛṣṇa?’ Parāśara did not say that these two Daityas had formerly been Bhagavān's associates Jaya and Vijaya; he said only that they took birth three times. Therefore Parāśara did not mean that these two associates of Bhagavān are born as *asuras* in every *kalpa*. Otherwise the associates of Bhagavān would fall in every *kalpa*, and that is entirely incongruous. (That is to say: in Bhagavān Viṣṇu, just as the will to create, the will to fight is also eternally present. A *mahārāja* fond of sport always plays with rivals set against him. When there are no rivals, he makes his own courtiers or servants into opponents and sports with them; and these servants, playing in a hostile mood, give their master delight. So too Bhagavān Viṣṇu: He makes hostile the *jīvas* who have been turned away from Him since time without beginning, or some of His own associates; they take on a hostile mood, and so both sides satisfy their longing for the play of battle. Therefore the fall of Bhagavān's associates in every *kalpa* is incongruous.) When Bhagavān appeared in the unearthly form of Nṛsiṁha, Hiraṇyakaśipu did not see Viṣṇu in Him: He seemed to him merely a being born of a heap of merit. The predominance of *rajo-guṇa* disordered his intelligence: he thought of Nṛsiṁha, ‘This is some mighty creature,’ and so at the last hour he could not think of His form. Therefore, merely because he died at Nṛsiṁha's hand, he obtained in the body of Rāvaṇa a rare wealth of enjoyment. Since there was no firm conviction that this was Viṣṇu, and no intense enmity, his absorption in Bhagavān did not grow. Enmity without such growing absorption in Bhagavān, as with King Vena, leads only to hell. Without the most intense absorption the offenses born of abuse and the like are not destroyed. There was no absorption, the offenses did not vanish, and so Hiraṇyakaśipu did not see the pure form of Bhagavān and could not dissolve into Him, though the Supreme Brahman Nṛsiṁhadeva stood manifest before him. In the body of Rāvaṇa, too, his mind was wholly subject to *kāma*, and so, like Hiraṇyakaśipu, he saw a man in Śrī Rāma. Therefore that Daitya, in the form of Śiśupāla, again obtained, as before, a fine wealth of enjoyment. Śrī Kṛṣṇa was called Vāsudeva, and by that name [Śiśupāla] then concluded that He was the cause of his death in his two previous births. Out of intense enmity and great absorption he constantly uttered all those names, even in abuse and threats; seeing in Him the four-armed form and so on, and convinced that this was Viṣṇu, he thought of that form unceasingly, just as he uttered the names. Therefore his heap of sins born of enmity was burned; the radiance of the disc hurled by Śrī Kṛṣṇa destroyed his demonic nature; gaining purified, divine vision, he saw the form of the Supreme Brahman, like that of a man. Then the blow of the Sudarśana disc destroyed his demonic body, and he dissolved into the Supreme Brahman. Having said that Śiśupāla, through the most intense absorption in Śrī Kṛṣṇa by way of enmity, attained merging (*sāyujya-laya*) in Him, and having considered that Pūtanā and others slain in His childhood pastimes attained liberation, while Kālanemi and others slain in other avatāras, by the design of the Lord, did not, [the sage] uttered this prose passage. *Hi* is used in the sense ‘as is well known.’ If one glorifies and remembers the source of the avatāras rather than other avatāras — even with enmity, that is, in a hostile mood — even such *asuras* attain a good destination.” See also the commentary of Śrīpāda Baladeva Vidyābhūṣaṇa.

Śrī Sanātana Prabhu in the *Bṛhad-bhāgavatāmṛta* (second part, called *Goloka-māhātmya*, 30–31): “Ah, how can liberation be praised, when even the Daityas obtain it — those whom the scriptures themselves condemn as killers of cows, brāhmaṇas and others? *Sādhus* and *asuras* are opposites in every way; so it is fitting that their means and their goal be opposite too.”

In the tenth volume of the *Śrī Sajjana-toṣaṇī*, the translation by Śrīmad Bhaktivinoda Ṭhākura: “The scriptures condemn the Daityas — Kaṁsa and others — as killers of cows, brāhmaṇas and others. How then can the *sāyujya* liberation those Daityas obtained be praised? The *sādhus* are the devotees of Bhagavān, and the *asuras* are those who hate Him. Just as *sādhus* and *asuras* are always opposite in nature, so their means and goals must be opposite too. The means of the *asuras* is enmity toward the *sādhus* and the killing of cows and brāhmaṇas, and their goal is liberation. The means of the devotees is devotion, and their goal is *prema*. Therefore those who strive for such liberation, like wicked people, take up a wicked means — the mere endeavor of *jñāna*.”

## 41–48

Saṅkarṣaṇa is another name for “Mahā-Saṅkarṣaṇa” (He is described below, in 42–48).

In the second *pāda* of the second chapter of the *Brahma-sūtra*, in the *Utpatty-asambhava-adhikaraṇa*, Śrī Śaṅkarācārya in his commentary put forward a mistaken argument about the *catur-vyūha*. Settling the matter, the author refutes that doctrine in 41–47. Śrīpāda [Śaṅkara] erred in taking Viṣṇu, the nondual Knowledge (*advaya-jñāna*), for one of the objects of the visible world; in the *Pañcarātra* Śrī Nārāyaṇa Himself made this clear to him. Yet to bewilder the conditioned *jīvas* of demoniac nature he had to resort to deception (*vipralipsā*). Because of this the followers of Advaita — Appayya Dīkṣita and others — have reached the utmost limit of error. With the capacities of conditioned *jīvas*, knowledge of the *catur-vyūha* is not possible. The ācārya spoke such ill words to increase their folly. The *catur-vyūha* is made of *śuddha-sattva*, delights in the play of the *cit-śakti* and possesses the six kinds of majesty. To call Them poor and powerless, and to think of Them so, is the trait of deluded *jīvas*. Such *jīvas* are fit only to be bewildered by *māyā*. Whoever cannot understand [the difference between] Vaikuṇṭha and the realm of *māyā* falls into just this kind of error. In his commentary on *sūtras* 42–45 of the second *pāda* of the second chapter of the *Brahma-sūtra*, Śrīpāda Śaṅkara made a vain attempt to refute “the doctrine of the *catur-vyūha*.” Below are quoted from Śrīpāda Śaṅkarācārya's commentary his words on the “*catur-vyūha*,” rooted in his distorted understanding.

*Utpatty-asambhavāt* (42) (Śaṅkara's commentary): * * * “On this the Bhāgavatas hold: Bhagavān Vāsudeva alone, stainless, whose nature is knowledge, is the highest reality. Dividing Himself fourfold, He abides in the form of the *vyūha* of Vāsudeva, the *vyūha* of Saṅkarṣaṇa, the *vyūha* of Pradyumna and the *vyūha* of Aniruddha. Vāsudeva is the name for the Supreme Self, Saṅkarṣaṇa for the *jīva*, Pradyumna for the mind, Aniruddha for the ego (*ahaṅkāra*). Of these, Vāsudeva is the highest *prakṛti*, and the others — Saṅkarṣaṇa and the rest — are effects. To this we say: what is asserted — that the supreme Nārāyaṇa, the Supreme Self, the Self of all, by Himself dividing Himself in many ways, abides in the form of *vyūhas* — is not refuted. But what is said further — that from Vāsudeva Saṅkarṣaṇa is born, from Saṅkarṣaṇa Pradyumna, and from Pradyumna Aniruddha — to this we say: the birth of the *jīva* called Saṅkarṣaṇa from the Supreme Self called Vāsudeva is impossible, for then defects such as impermanence would follow. If the *jīva* were born, defects such as impermanence would attach to it, and then liberation — attaining Bhagavān — would be impossible for it, for an effect, on reaching its cause, would dissolve into it. And the Ācārya will reject the birth of the *jīva* [by the *sūtra*] ‘The self [is not born], because of *śruti*, and because of eternity [known] from them.’ Therefore their construction is incoherent.”

The meaning of the commentary is this: “The Bhāgavatas hold that Bhagavān Vāsudeva is one, stainless, His body is knowledge, and He is the highest reality. He Himself has divided Himself into four and so abides. These four *vyūhas* are: 1) the *vyūha* of Vāsudeva, 2) the *vyūha* of Saṅkarṣaṇa, 3) the *vyūha* of Pradyumna, 4) the *vyūha* of Aniruddha; these four *vyūhas* are His body. Another name of Vāsudeva is ‘Paramātmā’; of Saṅkarṣaṇa, ‘*jīva*’; of Pradyumna, ‘mind’; and of Aniruddha, ‘*ahaṅkāra*.’ Of the four *vyūhas*, the *vyūha* of Vāsudeva is the highest *prakṛti*, that is, the root cause. Saṅkarṣaṇa and the others have arisen from the *vyūha* of Vāsudeva, and so Saṅkarṣaṇa, Pradyumna and Aniruddha are the effects of that highest *prakṛti*. For a long time the *jīva* goes to the temple of Bhagavān, gathers [the articles of worship], performs worship (*ijyā*), studies [scripture] and practices *yoga*; thus it becomes free from sin, obtains a pure body and attains the highest *prakṛti*, Bhagavān. The great-souled Bhāgavatas say that Nārāyaṇa is beyond *prakṛti*, renowned as the Paramātmā, and is the Self of all — this does not contradict *śruti*. We also accept that He by Himself abides in the form of many *vyūhas*. Therefore this part of the Bhāgavata doctrine is not refuted by this *sūtra*. The Ācārya composed this *sūtra* only to reject the part where the Bhāgavatas say that from Vāsudeva Saṅkarṣaṇa was born, from Saṅkarṣaṇa Pradyumna, and from Pradyumna Aniruddha. The birth of the *jīva* called Saṅkarṣaṇa from the Paramātmā called Vāsudeva is utterly impossible: the *jīva* would then be subject to defects such as impermanence. If the *jīva* is born, defects such as impermanence are unavoidable for it. And if the *jīva* is perishable by nature, liberation — attaining Bhagavān — is impossible for it altogether. When the cause perishes, the effect must perish too. Ācārya Vedavyāsa rejected the birth of the *jīva* by the *sūtra* of the third *pāda* of the second chapter, ‘The self [is not born], because of *śruti*, and because of eternity [known] from them,’ and by rejecting its birth he will prove its eternity. Therefore this construction is incoherent.”
