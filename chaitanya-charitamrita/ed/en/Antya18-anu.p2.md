## 98

The lotus opens at sunrise, and so the sun is the lotus's friend; at sunrise the *cakravākas*, too, are reunited. But here the lotus, though the sun's friend, plunders the *cakravāka*, the friend of its own friend the sun. The *cakravāka* is a living being, and the lotus an insentient object. But here the lotus — Kṛṣṇa's hand — though insentient, attacks the living *cakravāka* — the *gopī*'s breast. This is the ornament *virodhābhāsa* (apparent contradiction). The *utpala* closes at sunrise, and so the sun is the *utpala*'s enemy. The *utpala* opens at night, and so it is a stranger to the *cakravāka*. Here the sun is the *utpala*'s enemy, and the *cakravāka* is that enemy's friend. Yet here the *cakravāka* — the *gopī*'s breast — is protected by the *utpala* — the *gopī*'s hand. This too is the wonderful ornament *virodha* (contradiction).

## 99

**atiśayokti** (hyperbole) — in place of the thing compared (*upameya*), the standard of comparison (*upamāna*) is used with the firm certainty (understanding) that the two are not different; therefore this is the ornament *atiśayokti*. As the *Sāhitya-darpaṇa* says (ch. 10, *kārikā* 693): “When the identification (*adhyavasāya*) is complete, it is called *atiśayokti*.”

**virodhābhāsa** (apparent contradiction) — as the *Kāvya-prakāśa* says (tenth *ullāsa*, *kārikā* 24): “*Virodha* is speaking of what is not contradictory as if it were contradictory. A genus (*jāti*) may conflict with four — with a genus and the rest; a quality (*guṇa*) with three; an action (*kriyā*) with two; and a substance (*dravya*) only with a substance. Thus there are ten kinds.”

## 104–105

**koli** — jujube (*kul*, *badarī*); **panasa** — jackfruit (*kā̃ṭāl*); **nāraṅga** — a citrus of the orange kind (*kamalā-lebu*); **drākṣā** — grapes (*āṅgur*); **sāntārā** — a large citrus of the pomelo kind (*bātābi-lebu*) (so called in East Bengal, in the Chittagong division); **meoyā** — pistachios, almonds and other choice, delicious fruits grown in cold countries; **kharamujā** — a fruit related to the watermelon (*taramujā*) but smaller (in some regions also called *phuṭi* or *bāṅgī*); **kṣīrikā** — the *khirāi* cucumber; **tāla** — the soft flesh of the young palm fruit (*tāl-śā̃s*), or *phopal*; **keśura** — a particular grass root of the nut-grass (*muthā*) kind, also known as *kaśeru*, *kaseru* (with short or long *u*); **pānī-phala** — a particular fruit growing in the water of very old ponds and rivers overgrown with weed (*śaivāla*): the *śṛṅgāṭaka*; **mṛṇāla** — the lotus stalk, or the lotus root (?); **dāḍimba** — a fruit of the *maskaṭ* and *bedānā* kind, the pomegranate (*ḍālim*).

## 106
