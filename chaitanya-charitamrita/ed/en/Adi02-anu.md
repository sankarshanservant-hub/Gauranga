# Anubhāṣya on Ādi-līlā 2

## 1

> Subheading: Obeisance to Gaura through examining the conclusions (*siddhānta*) of devotion —

- **bālaḥ api** — even one who is ignorant, a child.
- **yad-anugrahāt** — by whose mercy, that is, by whose compassion.
- **nānā-mata-grāha-vyāptam** — full of doctrines that are like crocodiles and sharks. These doctrines were proclaimed by Aulūkya, Jina, Buddha, Jaimini, Patañjali, Gotama, Kaṇāda, Kapila, Śaṅkara and Dattātreya; they quarrel with one another and are full of worldly self-interest.
- **siddhānta-sāgaram** — the ocean of reasoning.
- **taret** — crosses. That is, he counts the mixed doctrines of these worldly people as straw and comes to know Kṛṣṇa's pure feet.
- **śrī-caitanya-prabhum** — (that) Śrī Caitanya Prabhu.
- **vande** — (I) worship.

## 2

> Subheading: A prayer for Śrī Caitanya's mercy, for the sake of *kīrtana* about Kṛṣṇa —

- **śrī-caitanya dayā-nidhe** — O Śrī Caitanya, treasury of mercy.
- **kṛṣṇotkīrtana-gāna-nartana-kalā-pāthojani-bhrājitā** — shining, that is, adorned, with lotuses (*pāthojani*). These lotuses are the arts (*kalā*): *utkīrtana*, the loud glorification of Kṛṣṇa's name, form, qualities, pastimes and so on, together with singing and dancing.
- **sad-bhaktāvali-haṁsa-cakra-madhupa-śreṇī-vihārāspadam** — the place of play, the field of *vilāsa*, for the hosts of true, that is, pure devotees. They abide in various moods (*bhāvas*) and are like different flocks of swans, *cakravāka* birds and bees. The sense is that in these pastimes the hosts of pure devotees find the highest joy.
- **karṇānandi-kala-dhvaniḥ** — she whose gentle sound delights the ear and becomes an elixir for the devotees' ears. This gentle sound is the soft, sweet murmur of the streams of Hari's pastimes; the servants of Hari, who are like swans, *cakravākas* and bees, make it.
- **tava lasal-līlā-sudhā-svardhunī** — such is Your shining (*lasatī*, radiant) heavenly river (*svardhunī*), the Mandākinī, the heavenly Gaṅgā, full of the nectar of Gaura's pastimes.
- **me** — my.
- **jihvā-maru-prāṅgaṇe** — in the desert courtyard of the tongue. It has no taste, because it has been deprived of relishing the *rasa* of Gaura's pastimes.
- **vahatu** — (may she) flow.

## 4

> Subheading: Explanation of the third of the fourteen opening verses. Pointing out the Truth —

## 5

- **upaniṣadi** — in the Upaniṣad, that is, in a special branch of the Vedas, the highest of all, called *brahma-vidyā* (the knowledge of Brahman). [The word *upaniṣad* is formed from the root *ṣad* with the prefixes *upa-* and *ni-* and the suffix *kvip*; the root means “to destroy,” “to go” and “to wear down.” Here *upa* means “having approached,” that is, having received it through the guru's instruction, and *ni* means “with resolve.” Some have approached *brahma-vidyā*, have resolutely fixed themselves in it and have lost their thirst for objects, both those that are seen and those promised in the Vedas. For them it destroys the seed of worldly existence — loosens it, wears it down, annihilates it — and leads them to Brahman.]
- **yat advaitam** — that which (is called) nondual, without a second,
- **brahma** — Brahman,
- **tat api** — that too
- **asya** — His (Gaura-Kṛṣṇa's)
- **tanu-bhā** — is the radiance (of His transcendental body).
- **yaḥ ātmā** — He who is the Self (the Paramātmā, the Controller of all living beings and the rest),
- **antar-yāmī puruṣaḥ** — the Puruṣa who dwells within,
- **saḥ asya aṁśa-vibhavaḥ** — is His partial manifestation (one of His opulences, a particular expression of His all-pervasiveness).
- **iha** — (in this examination of the truth).
- **yaḥ ṣaḍ-aiśvaryaiḥ** — He who in the six opulences, that is, in the six kinds of lordship — complete wealth, strength, fame, beauty, knowledge and renunciation —
- **pūrṇaḥ** — is full (needs nothing, is perfect),
- **saḥ ayam** — He, this Śrī Kṛṣṇa Caitanya,
- **svayam bhagavān** — is Bhagavān Himself.
- **iha** — (in the world, in the examination of the truth, or in the Kali age).
- **caitanyāt kṛṣṇāt** — than Kṛṣṇa Caitanya
- **param** — other
- **para-tattvam** — supreme truth (better shelter)
- **na** — there is none.

(Brahman is the goal of the scriptures of knowledge (*jñāna-śāstras*), and Paramātmā the goal of the scriptures of yoga. In truth they are equal to Bhagavān. But the view differs with the qualification (*adhikāra*) of the one who sees, and so they are only two aspects of Bhagavān's form — His spiritual radiance and His part. They are not the whole, complete Reality, endowed with all qualities and potencies; only Bhagavān is that.)

With this verse one should compare the following verse from Śrī Jīva Prabhu's *Tattva-sandarbha* (8): “His being, purely spiritual, receives the name Brahman in some places of the Vedas. His part is the Puruṣa, who subdues *māyā* and through His own parts displays variety. His one form sports in the spiritual sky (*paravyoma*) under the name Nārāyaṇa. May that Śrī Kṛṣṇa, Bhagavān Himself, grant love of God (*prema*) here to those who take shelter of His feet.”

Some are deprived of beholding the being and bliss (*sat* and *ānanda*) of Bhagavān, who is full of *sat*, *cit* and *ānanda*. They rely only on the function of consciousness (*saṁvit*) and, pursuing the Truth endowed with spiritual pastimes, in the end see Brahman. Others are deprived of beholding Bhagavān's bliss (*ānanda*). They rely only on the functions of being and consciousness (*sat* and *cit*) and, pursuing the same Truth, in the end see Paramātmā. Thus Bhagavān is the form of being, consciousness and bliss and of divine pastimes. The spiritual radiance of His body is Brahman, devoid of spiritual *vilāsa* and free from *māyā*,[^5-1] and the being that is a portion of His majesty (*aiśvarya*) is Paramātmā.

## 10

> Subheading: Examining Brahman, Paramātmā and Bhagavān —

Prabhu Śrī Jīva Gosvāmī writes in the *Bhagavat-sandarbha* (3): “Thus, when [the Truth] displays all Its distinguishing features, It is Bhagavān: He is Its full appearance and therefore the form of the undivided Truth. Brahman, however, is an incomplete appearance of that same Truth, for in Brahman the form with distinguishing features is not manifest. ‘O sage! The sound *bha* carries two meanings, *sambhartā* and *bhartā*; the meanings of the sound *ga* are *netā*, *gamayitā* and *sraṣṭā*. Beings dwell in Him, the Self of beings, the Self of all, and He, the Imperishable, abides in all beings without exception — this is the meaning of the sound *va*. Knowledge, potency, strength, lordship, power and splendor in their fullness, without inferior qualities and the like — this is what the word Bhagavān denotes.’ *Sambhartā* is He who nourishes His devotees; *bhartā*, He who sustains and establishes; *netā*, He who leads to the fruit of devotion to Him, that is, to *prema*; *gamayitā*, He who brings to His own abode; *sraṣṭā*, He who gives rise to various qualities in His devotees. (4) He Himself is not a cause: He is wholly absorbed in the *vilāsa* of His own potency (*svarūpa-śakti*) alone and is therefore indifferent to the world. Yet through the Puruṣa, His part, also called the Paramātmā, who sets *prakṛti* and the *jīvas* in motion, He becomes the cause of the world's creation, maintenance and so on. Know this as the form of Bhagavān. <…> As the efficient cause, He makes the *jīvas*, particles of Himself, enter bodies and the rest and so brings them to life. Impelled by Him, all the elements — *pradhāna* and the others, indicated by bodies and so on — move, that is, set about their work. Know this as the form of the Paramātmā. The *jīva* is an *ātmā*, a self, and compared with him He is the supreme (*parama*); therefore the word Paramātmā denotes just Him, the *jīva*'s companion. That same Truth is present as the accompanying presence (*anvaya*) in dream and the other states; outside them It likewise abides in the pure potency called the *jīva*; and the particle *ca* (‘and’) shows that It also abides beyond them, separately (*vyatireka*), remaining by Itself. Know this as the form of Brahman.”

Bhagavān is endowed with all potencies and reveals Himself in all His fullness; therefore He is the undivided Truth. In Brahman such a form with distinguishing features is not manifest; therefore Brahman is only a partial, incomplete appearance of Bhagavān. O sage! The first sound of the word Bhagavān, *bha*, has two meanings, *sambhartā* and *bhartā*; the sound *ga* means *netā*, *gamayitā* and *sraṣṭā*. Beings dwell in the universal Self, the Self of beings, and He, the imperishable Puruṣa, dwells in all beings without exception — this is the meaning of the sound *va*. Boundless knowledge, potency, strength, lordship, power and splendor, without inferior qualities — this is what the word Bhagavān denotes. *Sambhartā* means “He who nourishes His devotees”; *bhartā*, “He who sustains and establishes”; *netā*, “He who leads to the fruit of devotion to Him, that is, to *prema*”; *gamayitā*, “He who brings to His own abode”; *sraṣṭā*, “He who gives rise to various qualities in His devotees.” There is a Truth that is not Itself a cause: through His own potency (*svarūpa-śakti*) He has a single *vilāsa* and is entirely indifferent to the world. And yet, through the Puruṣa, His part, who sets *prakṛti* and the *jīvas* in motion, He becomes the cause of the world's birth, maintenance and so on. Know this as the truth of Bhagavān (*bhagavat-tattva*). There is One who, as the efficient cause, makes the *jīvas*, particles of Himself, enter the world and so brings the world to life. Impelled by Him, the elements — *pradhāna* and the others, indicated by bodies and so on — remain in place and each sets about its own work. Know Him as the Paramātmā. By his intrinsic nature the *jīva* is an *ātmā*, and the One higher than the *jīva* is *parama*; therefore the word Paramātmā reveals Him as the *jīva*'s eternal companion. There is a Truth that is present as the accompanying presence (*anvaya*) in waking, dreaming and deep sleep. In *samādhi* It abides [as] the pure *jīva-śakti*, yet afterwards It also abides beyond it, separately (*vyatireka*), remaining by Itself. Know It as Brahman.

## 11

> Subheading: (*Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam* 1.2.11)

The sages headed by Śaunaka asked Sūta, the disciple of Śukadeva, six questions. This verse answers the second of them: “What is the essence of the scriptures?”

- **tattva-vidaḥ** — those who know the truth
- **tat** — (just) that
- **tattvam** — the Truth,
- **advayam jñānam** — nondual knowledge (one, purely spiritual),
- **vadanti** — they call.
- **yat** — (This nondual knowledge)
- **brahma iti** — (in some places) Brahman,
- **paramātmā iti** — (in some places) Paramātmā,
- **bhagavān iti ca** — (and in some places) Bhagavān
- **śabdyate** — is called.

The sense is this: nondual knowledge realized through the function of knowledge alone is Brahman; nondual knowledge realized through the functions of being and consciousness (*sat* and *cit*) is Paramātmā; and that same nondual knowledge realized through the functions of being, consciousness and bliss (*sat*, *cit* and *ānanda*) is Bhagavān.

Bhagavān's devotees know that the embodiment of nondual knowledge is Vrajendranandana, the son of the king of Vraja. In Kṛṣṇa's name, form, qualities and pastimes they see nothing second. If one separates Kṛṣṇa from His transcendental name, form, qualities and pastimes, a mundane conception of Viṣṇu's body arises; this is the very absence of nondual knowledge. Things other than Kṛṣṇa are not Viṣṇu, and nondual knowledge is not in them. Therefore everything other than Kṛṣṇa is separated from Kṛṣṇa, that is, from nondual knowledge, by *māyā*, or dual knowledge.[^11-1] So it becomes liable to fall under *māyā*'s control, and is therefore subject to *māyā*, or dual knowledge. In all of Kṛṣṇa's manifestations (*prakāśas*) and forms of *vilāsa*, however, there is no knowledge of anything second; therefore they are *viṣṇu-tattva* and the masters of *māyā*. The yogīs regard only one state as free from knowledge of a second: the unmixed union (*yoga*) of the pure soul with the Paramātmā, the embodiment of nondual knowledge. The *jñānīs*, for their part, regard as nondual knowledge, as Brahman, a featureless knowledge free from all distinctions — internal, homogeneous and heterogeneous (*svagata*, *sajātīya* and *vijātīya*). See the *Gauḍīya-bhāṣya* on the *Bhāgavatam* by the author of this commentary, pp. 88–89.

[^5-1]: The edition reads *atan-māyā-rahita* (literally “free from *māyā*, [which is] not-That”); taken here as “untouched by *māyā*.”
[^11-1]: The edition reads *māyā vā advaya-jñāna-dvārā* (“by *māyā*, or nondual knowledge”); the sense probably requires *dvaya-jñāna*, dual knowledge (compare *dvaita-jñāna* in the same sentence); translated by the sense.
