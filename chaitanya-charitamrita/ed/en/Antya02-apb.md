# Amṛta-pravāha-bhāṣya on Antya-līlā 2

## 0

**Summary.** In telling where Mahāprabhu gave direct *darśana* (*sākṣāt-darśana*), where He entered someone (*āveśa*) and where He appeared (*āvirbhāva*), the author writes of Nakula Brahmacārī, of Nṛsiṁhānanda's glory and of other devotees. Bhagavān Ācārya was devoted to the Lord, yet Svarūpa Dāmodara forbade him to hear the Māyāvāda commentary from the mouth of his brother, Gopāla Bhaṭṭācārya. After this, on Bhagavān Ācārya's instruction, Choṭa Haridāsa begged rice from Mādhavī-devī. A renunciant (*vairāgī*) had spoken with a woman, and for this fault the Lord rejected him, forbidding him to enter His door. The Vaiṣṇavas pleaded for him, but the Lord would not accept him again. A year later Choṭa Haridāsa submerged himself in the Triveṇī at Prayāga and gave up his body; then, in a transcendental (*aprākṛta*) body, he sang for Mahāprabhu. Gauḍīya Vaiṣṇavas came and told this news, and Svarūpa and all the others learned what had happened.

## 1

I worship the lotus feet of Śrī Guru, and all the gurus, all the Vaiṣṇavas, Rūpa Gosvāmī, Sanātana Gosvāmī, Raghunātha with his companions, and Jīva, Advaita Prabhu, Nityānanda Prabhu, Śrī Kṛṣṇa Caitanyadeva with His associates, and Śrī Rādhā-Kṛṣṇa together with Lalitā, Viśākhā and the other [*sakhīs*] and their companions.

## 3–4

The Lord delivers the *jīvas* in three ways: to some He gives direct *darśana*, into some worthy devotee He enters (*āviṣṭa*), and to some devotee He appears (*āvirbhūta*).

## 16

**Āmbuyā-muluk.** In those days the land was divided into districts (*muluks*), and in various places the Yavana rulers had revenue offices (*tahśil-kāchāri*). There was one such *muluk* at a place called Ambikā (a village adjoining the town of Kālnā in Barddhamāna district). Within its bounds, at the place now known as Piyārīgañja, lived Nakula Brahmacārī.

## 31

**Gaura-Gopāla-mantra.** Those devoted to Gaura (*gaura-vādīs*) take this word to mean the four-syllable mantra of Gaura, “Gaurāṅga.” Those who hold to Kṛṣṇa alone (*kevala-kṛṣṇa-vādīs*) take the word “Gaura-Gopāla-mantra” to mean the four-syllable mantra of Rādhā-Kṛṣṇa.

## 44

***Sandeśa*** — news.

## 95

***Śārīraka-bhāṣya*** — Śrīmat Śaṅkarācārya's commentary on the *Vedānta-sūtra*.

## 96

Even a *mahā-bhāgavata* whose treasure of life is Kṛṣṇa — if he hears the *Śārīraka-bhāṣya*, full of Māyāvāda, his heart too sinks, and he falls away from devotion.

## 98–99

Your hearts may be fixed on Kṛṣṇa, and so hearing Śaṅkara's commentary and the like may not corrupt them. Yet that Māyāvāda contains such arguments as these: “Brahman is by nature pure consciousness and formless”; “this world is mere *māyā*, that is, false”; “the *jīva* does not really exist — it is only an imagination of ignorance”; “in Īśvara there is only ignorance — He is bewildered by *māyā*.” Hearing such words, a devotee feels deep pain.

## 109

**Śālyanna** — fine white rice.

## 117

A Vaiṣṇava should either live as a householder with his wife and family, or give up all connection with women and become a *vairāgī* (renunciant). Once he has become a *vairāgī*, he no longer has the right to look at women or converse with them. Even when there is no sinful desire, and even when outwardly the aim is some service of devotion, a *vairāgī* must not have such a connection with a woman. Therefore [the Lord says]: “I cannot look at the face of one who has become a *vairāgī* and converses with a *prakṛti* (a woman): he destroys *dharma*.”

## 118

***Dāru-prakṛti hare munerapi mana*** — even a woman made of wood can steal the mind of a sage. Therefore a *vairāgī* must give up all connection with women.

## 119

Never stay in a secluded place with your mother, sister or daughter: the powerful senses can draw away the mind even of a learned man.

## 120

Only one in whom, while he practices *sādhana-bhakti*, *bhāva* has awakened and detachment has been born has the right to renunciation. When people take the *bhek* (the renunciant's dress) before reaching that state, their renunciation is called *markaṭa-vairāgya*, monkey renunciation. Unqualified souls, for one reason or another, take up renunciation before their time; then, driven by their senses, they go to converse with *prakṛti*, that is, with women. Know them to be *dharma-dhvajīs* — those who make a show of *dharma* — a disgrace to *dharma*, and keep them far away.

## 123

**A slight offense.** In begging rice from Mādhavī, Choṭa Haridāsa had no other aim; he desired only the joy of serving Mahāprabhu. Yet that act contained an offense. To take the *bhek* and then converse with a woman again is an offense, and for a *vairāgī* it is certainly a great offense; but since it was done to serve the Lord, it may also be called “slight.”

## 144

**Trāsa upajila saba-bhakta-gaṇe** — such fear arose among the devotees wearing the *bhek* (the *sādhakas*) that they no longer spoke with any woman.

## 163

***Sva-karma-phala-bhuk pumān*** — a person reaps the fruit of his own actions (those he himself has performed).

## 165

If a Vaiṣṇava *sādhaka* who wears the *bhek* deliberately looks at women, his atonement is to die by drowning in the Triveṇī, so that in a future birth he may become faultless.

*Thus ends the second chapter of the “Amṛta-pravāha-bhāṣya.”*
