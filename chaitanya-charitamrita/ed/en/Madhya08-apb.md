# Amṛta-pravāha-bhāṣya on Madhya-līlā 8

## 0

**Summary.** Mahāprabhu took *darśana* of Jiyaḍa-Nṛsiṁha, and on the bank of the Godāvarī, at Vidyānagara, He met Rāmānanda Rāya, who had come there to bathe. When they had become acquainted, Rāmānanda asked the Lord to stay in that village for a few days. At his request the Lord stayed in the house of a Vedic Vaiṣṇava brāhmaṇa. In the evening Rāmānanda Rāya, humbly dressed, came to Mahāprabhu and offered Him prostrate obeisances, and Mahāprabhu told him to recite verses that determine the goal (*sādhya*). First Rāmānanda Rāya spoke of *varṇāśrama-dharma*, the duty common to all good people. Then he recited a few verses on “offering one's actions to Kṛṣṇa” (*karmārpaṇa*), then on “action free from attachment,” then on “devotion mixed with knowledge,” and finally on “pure devotion free from knowledge.” Mahāprabhu accepted this last as “the object of the goal” (*sādhya-vastu*). Then the Lord asked Rāya to describe the higher stages of devotion, and as “the essence of the goal” (*sādhya-sāra*) Rāya described first “*prema-bhakti* in the form of pure *rati* for Kṛṣṇa,” then “the *prema* of a servant,” “the *prema* of a friend,” “the *prema* of parents,” and finally “*prema* in the mood of the beloved” (*kānta-bhāva*).[^0-1] Rāya explained in various ways and at length why the beloved's *prema* is the essence of the goal. When the Lord accepted it as the limit of the goal (*sādhya-avadhi*), Rāya described Rādhikā's *prema*. Then Rāya described the nature of Kṛṣṇa, the nature of Rādhā, the nature of *rasa*, and the truth of *prema* (*prema-tattva*). After that, at Mahāprabhu's request, Rāmānanda Rāya sang a song of his own composition about the *prema-vilāsa-vivarta*, full of *adhirūḍha-bhāva* in separation (*vipralambha*). At the end it was explained in detail how to attain the highest goal, loving service to Rādhā-Kṛṣṇa: the means is to follow the *sakhīs* of Vraja. For several days, every night, they held all kinds of talks about Kṛṣṇa. Then Rāmānanda saw Mahāprabhu's original truth (*mūla-tattva*) and His own true form, and he fainted. A few days later the Lord told Rāmānanda to give up government service and go to Puruṣottama, and He Himself continued south. Kavirāja Gosvāmī wrote all this according to the diary (*kaḍacā*) of Svarūpa Dāmodara.

## 1

Śrī Gaurāṅga is the ocean of the nectar of *siddhānta*. He infused the nectar of the *siddhānta* of His own devotion into the devotee-cloud named Rāmānanda, and then, through that *siddhānta* spread by Rāmānanda, He Himself again became the ocean, the ocean of knowing the truth of devotion.

## 6

Though fierce in its prowess, a lion is gentle with its own young. In the same way Nṛsiṁhadeva, fierce toward demons such as Hiraṇyakaśipu, is full of tenderness toward His own devotees such as Prahlāda.

## 23

The natural love that Rādhā-Kṛṣṇa feel for Their friend Viśākhā, and that the *sakhī* Viśākhā feels for Rādhā-Kṛṣṇa, arose in them.

## 32

Rāmānanda Rāya said, “Sārvabhauma regards me as his servant and looks after my welfare even behind my back, that is, even when I am not present.”

## 40

O Bhagavān! Great persons go to the homes of householders whose minds are wretched only to bring them the eternal good; they go for no other reason.

## 43

*Ākṛti*, form, that is, the body of a *nyagrodha-parimaṇḍala*, and *prakṛti*, nature, that is, Your supremely merciful disposition: in these You are seen to be Īśvara.

## 55

*Sannyāsīs* bathe three times a day (*tri-savana*). Following this rule, the Lord had bathed in the evening and was sitting [on the bank].

## 57

The Lord said, “O Rāmānanda Rāya, recite a verse of scripture that determines the truth of the goal (*sādhya-tattva*).” Rāya said, “When human beings follow their own *dharma*, devotion to Viṣṇu arises.”

## 58

The Supreme Lord Viṣṇu is worshiped by a person who keeps the conduct of *varṇa-dharma* and *āśrama-dharma*. Apart from the conduct of *varṇāśrama* there is no other means of pleasing Him.

The purport is this: to please Bhagavān is the truth of the goal (*sādhya-tattva*). When human beings follow the *varṇa-dharma* determined by their own nature and the *āśrama-dharma* determined by their situation, Lord Viṣṇu is pleased. There are four *varṇas*: brāhmaṇas, kṣatriyas, vaiśyas and śūdras. A person should conduct his life by following the *dharma* that scripture has fixed for his *varṇa*. There are four *āśramas*: *brahmacarya*, *gārhasthya*, *vānaprastha* and *sannyāsa*. Each should please Bhagavān by following the *dharma* prescribed for his own *āśrama*. If one deviates from it, one incurs sin (*pratyavāya*) and goes to hell. To take up the path of the highest goal, a life of *dharma* is needed first of all. The *dharma* that sustains life is naturally different for people of different natures.

A person's nature arises from birth, association and education. No one can conduct his life skillfully without accepting a *varṇa* according to his nature. Natures are of many kinds, but at root they fall into four divisions. (1) Those whose nature turns to God and learning are “brāhmaṇas.” (2) Those whose natural inclination is valor and government are “kṣatriyas.” (3) Those whose natural work is farming, cattle-keeping and trade are “vaiśyas.” (4) Those whose nature is only to serve the three *varṇas* are “śūdras.” When a person worships [the Lord] in the *dharma* of his own *varṇa* and according to his *āśrama*, he rises naturally; when he acts contrary to it, he naturally falls. Therefore a life of *dharma* is the root of every human excellence.

## 59–60

[Kṛṣṇa] says in the *Gītā*: “O Kaunteya, whatever you eat, whatever you offer in sacrifice, whatever you give, and whatever austerity you perform, offer all of it to Me, Kṛṣṇa.”

In his first answer Rāya named as “the goal” (*sādhya*) the worship of Kṛṣṇa within *varṇāśrama-dharma*. The Lord therefore called this “external,” and so that Rāya would give a true answer to His question, He told him to speak of what is higher than ordinary *varṇāśrama-dharma*. Rāya then answered that “the essence of all goals” is declared to be offering to Kṛṣṇa all one's actions within *varṇāśrama*.

## 61

Hearing even this, the Lord said, “This too is external. The answer to My question lies beyond it; tell Me that.” In reply Rāya said, “Giving up one's own *dharma* (*svadharma*) is the essence of the goal.” That is, among the four *varṇas* the brāhmaṇa gives up his own (household) *dharma* and accepts *sannyāsa*, and the other *varṇas*, following him, accept the marks of renunciation and leave home. This *sannyāsa* is called giving up *svadharma*, or giving up *karma*. The *dharma* of renunciation brings the pleasure of Hari.

## 62

In the scriptures on *dharma* I, Bhagavān, have ordained many things as “*dharma*.” He who weighs their merits and faults, gives up all these pursuits of *dharma* and worships Me is the best of all (a *sādhu*).

## 63

Give up all *dharmas* and take shelter of Me alone, Bhagavān. Then I shall free you from all sins. Do not grieve.

## 64

Hearing this answer, the Lord called it too external and told him to speak of something higher. Then Rāya said, “Devotion mixed with knowledge (*jñāna-miśrā bhakti*) may be called the essence of the goal.”

## 65

It is said in the *Gītā*: “He who, by cultivating knowledge in the form of the doctrine of the nondifferent Brahman, himself becomes serene, free from grief and desire, and equal toward all beings, and who attains the state of Brahman, then obtains supreme devotion to Me.” The purport is this: devotion mixed with *karma* (*karma-miśrā bhakti*) was spoken of earlier, and higher than it is devotion mixed with knowledge.

## 66

Hearing this, the Lord said, “This too is external. Tell Me what lies beyond it.” Rāya said that devotion free from knowledge (*jñāna-śūnyā bhakti*) is the essence of all goals.

## 67

In the *Bhāgavata* Brahmā said to Bhagavān: “O Bhagavān! There are devotees who have completely given up the endeavor of knowledge, the contemplation of undifferentiated Brahman. They hear the narrations about You that flow from the lips of *sādhus*, and they live abiding on the path of the *sādhus* with body, mind and words. In all the three worlds You are hard to attain, yet to them You become easily attainable.”

## 68

Hearing this, the Lord said, “Now the goal has indeed been determined. Tell Me what is higher than this.” The purport is this. Offering one's actions [to Kṛṣṇa] is higher than merely following *varṇāśrama-dharma*. Giving up *svadharma*, that is, accepting *sannyāsa* after giving up the *dharma* of one's *varṇa*, is higher than merely offering one's actions. Higher than that is devotion mixed with knowledge, the cultivation of Brahman. Yet all of these are external, because the object of the goal, pure devotion, is not found in any of these four conclusions. Devotion that is *āropa-siddhā* or *saṅga-siddhā* is never known as “pure devotion.” “*Svarūpa-siddhā bhakti*” is a separate truth: it is eternally distinct from *karma*, from offering one's actions, from *sannyāsa* in the form of giving up *karma*, and from devotion mixed with knowledge. The mark of this pure devotion is this: it is the cultivation of Kṛṣṇa (*kṛṣṇānuśīlana*) in a favorable mood, free from other desires and unobstructed by knowledge, *karma* and the like. It alone is the object of the goal, for it is seen even while it is still being attained, and at the stage of perfection it is seen in its full purity. In answer to the Lord's last question Rāya said, “*Prema-bhakti* is the essence of all goals.” At first pure devotion appears as *śānta-bhakti*: in it there is as yet no sense that Kṛṣṇa is one's own (*mamatā*).

## 69

Food and drink give joy only as long as hunger and thirst are strong in the stomach. In the same way, the Friend of the distressed may be worshiped with many kinds of offerings, but the devotees' hearts melt with bliss only when that worship is full of *prema*.

## 70

There is a mind imbued with the *rasa* of devotion to Kṛṣṇa. It cannot be obtained by pious merit (*sukṛti*) earned in ten million births, yet it can be had for one single price, greed (*lobha*). Wherever you find such a mind, buy it at once.

Of these two verses, the first points to “*vaidhī-bhakti*,” rooted in faith (*śraddhā*), and the second to *rāgānugā-bhakti*, rooted in greed (*lobha*). From here on Rāya Rāmānanda's words will rest on *rāgānugā-bhakti* alone; that is, from now on he takes up the *siddhānta* of *rāga-bhakti* and leaves aside the talk of *vaidhī-bhakti*.

## 71

Having heard this much, the Lord said, “That is so. But tell Me what lies beyond it.” In reply Rāya said, “*Dāsya-prema*, the *prema* of a servant, is the essence of all goals.” When *mamatā*, the sense “He is mine,” joins “devotion marked by *prema*,” it becomes *dāsya-prema*. In *prema* in general (*prema-sādhāraṇya*) no relationship is yet established between Bhagavān and the devotee. When the sense of *mamatā*, “Bhagavān is my master,” joins it, general *prema* becomes *dāsya-prema*, which is higher than general *prema*.

## 72

The *Śrī Bhāgavata* says: “Simply by hearing the name of Bhagavān a *jīva* becomes pure. What then is left to attain for those who are servants of that Bhagavān, whose feet are holy places?”

## 74

Hearing this, the Lord said, “If you can go a little further, you will find the essence of all.” Rāya answered, “*Sakhya-prema*, the *prema* of a friend, for Śrī Kṛṣṇa is the essence of all goals.” Rāya's purport is this. There is *mamatā* in *dāsya-prema*, but “fear” and “awe” (*sambhrama*) easily arise in it from the thought “Bhagavān is my master.” When one gives up that “fear” and “awe” and embraces *viśrambha*, that is, “complete confidence,” that *prema* becomes *sakhya-prema*. In this *prema* a “sense of equality” arises between Kṛṣṇa and His friends.

## 75

The *Śrī Bhāgavata* says: “On the path of knowledge He is manifest as the experience of the bliss of Brahman, to the devotees in *dāsya-rasa* as the Supreme Deity, and to those who take shelter of *māyā* as a human boy. With this Bhagavān, Śrī Kṛṣṇa, the cowherd boys of Vraja, by the fruit of great pious merit, sported in *sakhya-rasa*.”

## 76

The Lord said, “*Sakhya-rasa* is indeed higher than *dāsya-rasa*. Yet go a little further and you will find the essence of the goal.” In reply Rāya said, “The *prema* of *vātsalya-bhāva*, the parental mood, is the essence of all goals.” When the *prema* of *sakhya-rasa*, which is based on confidence (*viśrambha*), is joined by greater affection (*sneha*), *vātsalya-rasa* arises.

## 77

*Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam* says: “O brāhmaṇa, what pious deeds did Nanda perform that Kṛṣṇa appeared as his son? And what pious deeds did Yaśodā perform that Kṛṣṇa, Parabrahman Himself, called her ‘mother’ and drank from her breast?”

## 78

The mercy that the *gopī* Yaśodā received from Śrī Kṛṣṇa, the giver of liberation to ordinary people, was not received by Brahmā, by Śiva, or even by Lakṣmī, who rests on Viṣṇu's chest.

## 79

The Lord said, “Step by step this has become ever higher. Yet beyond it there is one more *rasa*, and that you may call ‘the essence of the goal.’” Rāya answered, “*Kānta-bhāva*, the mood of the beloved, toward Śrī Kṛṣṇa is the essence of all goals, the highest limit (*parākāṣṭhā*) of *prema*.” The purport is this. In general *prema* there is no *mamatā*; in *dāsya-rasa* there is no *viśrambha*, “confidence”; in *sakhya-rasa* there is no “abundance of affection” (*snehādhikya*); and in *vātsalya-rasa* there is no “freedom from all reserve” (*niḥsaṅkoca-bhāva*). Therefore in none of these *rasas* does *prema*, the goal, reach its fullness. When *kānta-bhāva* toward Kṛṣṇa arises, then one finds a single, undivided truth of *prema*, free from all these lacks, the essence of all goals.

## 80

In Vṛndāvana, at the *rāsa* festival, Śrī Kṛṣṇa held the beautiful girls of Vraja by the neck with His mighty arms. The mercy that dawned upon them was not attained even by the wholly devoted potencies of Paravyoma, such as Lakṣmī, who rests on His chest. Nor was it the lot of the celestial women, who have the fragrance and luster of lotuses. What then to speak of other women?

## 82–86

“O Lord, earlier I named many means of the goal, that is, of attaining Kṛṣṇa. The only difference among them is that the degree of attaining Kṛṣṇa must be judged by the particular means. Whatever means people are qualified to adopt, it is best for them to hold to that means and attain Kṛṣṇa as befits their stage, and that is for them the object of the goal. In particular, for those qualified to attain *rasa*, all four *rasas*, *dāsya*, *sakhya*, *vātsalya* and *madhura*, are excellent. For one qualified for a particular *rasa*, that very *rasa* is the best of all.”

When one is absorbed in the *rāga* that awakens for a *rasa*, one sees no difference of degree among the four *rasas*. But viewed from outside, that is, impartially, these *rasas* do differ in degree. The five *rasas*, *śānta*, *dāsya*, *sakhya*, *vātsalya* and *madhura*, rise by degrees one after another. In *śānta-rasa* there is one quality, fidelity to Kṛṣṇa alone; in *dāsya-rasa* *mamatā* joins it, and it becomes richer. In *sakhya-rasa* fidelity to Kṛṣṇa alone and *mamatā* are joined by *viśrambha*, confidence, and blossom still more. In *vātsalya-rasa* the three qualities of *śānta*, *dāsya* and *sakhya* appear joined with an abundance of affection. In *madhura-rasa*, that is, in *kānta-bhāva*, these four qualities become free of all reserve and attain the highest sweetness. Here, as the qualities increase, the sweetness of taste increases. Therefore, judged impartially, *madhura-rasa* is the best of all.

## 87–88

To explain the difference of degree among the *rasas*, a material example is given. There are five great elements: “ether,” “air,” “fire,” “water” and “earth.” Ether has one quality, “sound.” Air has two qualities, “sound” and “touch.” Fire has three, “sound,” “touch” and “form.” Water has four, “sound,” “touch,” “form” and “taste.” Earth has five, “sound,” “touch,” “form,” “taste” and “smell.” Now see: from ether onward, the number of qualities grows step by step in each successive element, and in earth all five are found. In the same way, in *śānta*, *dāsya*, *sakhya*, *vātsalya* and *madhura* the qualities increase step by step, and in *madhura-rasa* all five qualities are found in fullness. Therefore the full attainment of Kṛṣṇa is found only in *prema* in the form of *madhura*, or *śṛṅgāra-rasa*. The *Bhāgavata* says that Kṛṣṇa is wholly conquered by *prema* that has blossomed in *madhura-rasa*.

## 90–91

Kṛṣṇa's general promise is that as one worships Him, so Kṛṣṇa reciprocates. In the other *rasas* Kṛṣṇa is able to reciprocate in the measure of the devotee's worship. But in *prema* that has blossomed in *madhura-rasa*, Kṛṣṇa found no reciprocation equal to their worship, and He said, “O beautiful girls of Vraja, I have not been able to repay My debt to you.”

## 93

The highest limit of Kṛṣṇa's sweetness lies in His beauty, which has no equal and nothing above it (*asamordhva*). Yet in the company of the goddesses of Vraja (*vraja-devīs*) that sweetness grows without end. Therefore love for Gopījana-vallabha, the beloved of the gopīs, is the essence of the goal for all devotees. In no other state of *rasa* does the devotee attain Kṛṣṇa as he does here, in the fullness of His sweetness.

## 94

Bhagavān, the son of Devakī, is the essence of all beauty. Yet among the goddesses of Vraja He shone with special splendor, like a great emerald (*marakata*) among golden jewels.

## 95

Having heard these conclusions, Mahāprabhu said, “Love for Śrī Gopījana-vallabha is indeed the limit of the truth of the goal (*sādhya-tattva*). Still, if there is anything more, tell Me.”

## 97

Of the love for Śrī Kṛṣṇa that all the gopīs share, Śrī Rādhā's love for Kṛṣṇa is the crest jewel of all goals (*sādhya-śiromaṇi*). Ordinary *jīvas* are given no instruction to take up the *bhāva* that belongs to Her position. In the perfected state, however, a *jīva* may become fit to take up a very exalted *bhāva* of love for Kṛṣṇa that follows that *bhāva*, that is, conforms to it. In the stage of practice one should follow the *bhāva* of Rādhikā's friends (*sakhīs*) and their maidservants. The *bhāva* of Rādhikā on seeing Uddhava, which was seen in Mahāprabhu, is beyond the *jīva's* reach, though it may be followed in some other form.

## 101–102

In the *rāsa-līlā* Śrī Kṛṣṇa saw that while Rādhikā was together with all the other gopīs, His love for Her could not be independent (*nirapekṣa*). Because of regard for the others, the depth of love did not unfold. Therefore Śrī Kṛṣṇa, wary of the gopīs, stole Rādhikā away from the place of the *rāsa* dance and went off, apart from the other gopīs. The verse *kaṁsārir api* (verse 105) is the example here.

## 104

In the circle of the *rāsa* Śrī Rādhikā saw the gopīs’ *mamatā* — their sense of “He is mine,” such as is found in ordinary love — and out of the contrariness born of [Her love's] crookedness (*kauṭilya-vāmatā*) She left the circle. Kṛṣṇa wanted Śrīmatī to nourish the *rasa* of the *rāsa-līlā*. Without Her, Śrī Kṛṣṇa grew dejected and, lamenting, began to wander through the forest in search of Śrīmatī.

## 106

Mādhava's mind was wounded by the arrows of Anaṅga, and He was filled with remorse. In the forest on the bank of the Yamunā, the daughter of Kalinda, He searched everywhere for Rādhikā but did not find Her; then He entered a grove and gave way to grief.

## 109–110

In the circle of the *rāsa* there was one form of Kṛṣṇa between every two gopīs, and one form of Kṛṣṇa beside Śrī Rādhikā; thus He manifested Himself. At this Rādhikā displayed the “contrariness” (*vāmatā*) of Her crooked love. The *Ujjvala-nīlamaṇi* says that love by nature moves crookedly, like a snake; therefore between a young man and a young woman *māna* of two kinds arises, “without cause” and “with cause.”

## 127

The Lord said, “Do not think that since I was born in a brāhmaṇa family and have taken *sannyāsa*, it is improper for Me to learn *dharma* from *śūdras*.” For a brāhmaṇa guru is needed only for instruction in the *dharma* of *varṇāśrama* and for initiation (*dīkṣā*). But the knowledge of the truth of Kṛṣṇa is the highest goal (*paramārtha*) of all *jīvas*. When one asks who is qualified to be the guru of this knowledge of the truth, the conclusion is simply this: whether brāhmaṇa or *śūdra* by birth, householder or *sannyāsī*, only one who knows the truth of Kṛṣṇa can be a guru. The *Śrī Hari-bhakti-vilāsa* says that one should not take a Kṛṣṇa mantra from a person of low *varṇa* while a qualified person of high *varṇa* is available. That statement, however, is meant for Vaiṣṇavas who are mindful of public opinion, that is, for those who live in the world and wish to aim in some measure at the highest goal by the accepted rules. For those who know the purport of *vaidhī* and *rāgānugā bhakti* and wish to attain pure devotion to Kṛṣṇa, the rule is to choose as guru a qualified knower of the truth of Kṛṣṇa, in whatever *varṇa* or *āśrama* he may be found. The *Śrī Hari-bhakti-vilāsa* quotes the *Padma Purāṇa*: “The devotees of Bhagavān are not *śūdras*; they are the best of *bhāgavatas*. In every *varṇa*, the *śūdras* are those who are not devoted to Janārdana. A brāhmaṇa expert in the six duties and learned in mantras and *tantras* cannot be a guru if he is not a Vaiṣṇava; a Vaiṣṇava, even a dog-eater, is a guru. Even one born in a great family, initiated into all the sacrifices and studying a thousand branches [of the Vedas] cannot be a guru if he is not a Vaiṣṇava. Brāhmaṇas, *kṣatriyas* and *vaiśyas* are the gurus of those born *śūdras*; and *śūdras* who are dear to Bhagavān are the gurus of those three.”

## 137–138

In Vṛndāvana, the abode made of spirit, Śrī Kṛṣṇa is present in the form of the ever-new Madana, beyond *prakṛti*. By the word *madana* worldly poets usually mean *kāma* as it is in the material world — the mutual attraction of lumps of flesh, a principle utterly material and base. Bound by matter, the *jīvas* have identified themselves with the body and accepted the rule of that *kāma*. When a *jīva* comes to know the truth of his relationship with Kṛṣṇa (*kṛṣṇa-sambandha-tattva*), he attains a transcendental, spiritual position. That position is of two kinds: *svarūpa-gata* (in one's own true nature) and *vastu-gata* (in actual fact). Sometimes the *jīva* has already realized the truth, but “in actual fact” his connection with matter has not yet gone. If in such a state the spiritual truth has dawned in him to some degree, he dwells in Vṛndāvana “in his true nature,” but not “in actual fact.” When by Kṛṣṇa's will even the trace of his connection with gross and subtle matter is gone, he dwells in Vṛndāvana “in actual fact.” Dwelling in one's true nature includes *sādhana*: then the *jīva* worships Kṛṣṇa with the spiritual *kāma-gāyatrī* and the spiritual *kāma-bīja*. Man and woman, the moving and the unmoving — Kṛṣṇa, who captivates every heart, whose very nature is Manmatha-manmatha, the Cupid of Cupid, attracts them all.

The *kāma-gāyatrī* is a particular Vedic mantra of twenty-four and a half syllables. The *kāma-bīja* is the seed syllable (*bīja*) that is chanted in the worship of Kṛṣṇa.

## 140

In the five kinds of worship through the nectar of *rasa* described above, the devotee is the *āśraya* (the abode) of that *rasa*, and Śrī Kṛṣṇa, the object of worship, is its *viṣaya* (the object).

## 141

(*Bhakti-rasāmṛta-sindhu*) May Śrī Kṛṣṇacandra be victorious — the embodiment of the nectar of all *rasas*, who with His spreading radiance captivated the two *sakhīs* named Tārakā and Pāli, who won over Śyāmā and the *sakhī* Lalitā, and who is supremely dear to Rādhā. The purport is this: in whatever *rasa* one worships Him, Śrī Kṛṣṇa, though He is the embodiment of the nectar of that *rasa*, is the one supreme object (*viṣaya*) of Rādhikā's *rasa* alone.

## 142

*Śṛṅgāra* is the king of *rasas* (*rasa-rāja*), and Śrī Kṛṣṇa bears a form made of it. Therefore Kṛṣṇa's beautiful form steals the heart even of Kṛṣṇa Himself.

## 145

The Bhūmā-puruṣa said, “O Kṛṣṇa and Arjuna, I brought the brāhmaṇa's sons here because I wished to see you. You have descended with all Your parts to protect the *dharma* of the world; kill the *asuras* who burden the earth and quickly come back here.” The purport is this: the Bhūmā-puruṣa, the husband of Lakṣmī, was enchanted by Śrī Kṛṣṇa's sweetness and, wishing to see His form, took *darśana* of Kṛṣṇa on the pretext of stealing the brāhmaṇa's sons.

## 146

“O Lord! Desiring the dust of Your feet, Kamalā performed austerities for a long time, giving up all desires and keeping vows. By what pious merit this serpent Kāliya earned the right to that dust of Your feet, we do not know.”

## 147–161

If one studies the fourth chapter of the Ādi-līlā here, all these points will be well understood.

## 174

“The *bhāvas* beginning with *kila-kiñcita*” — there are twenty of them. The twenty *bhāvas* are: (1) those born of the body (*aṅgaja*) — *bhāva*, *hāva*, *helā*; (2) those born of the self (*ātmaja*) — *śobhā*, *kānti*, *dīpti*, *mādhurya*, *pragalbhatā*, *audārya* and *dhairya*; (3) those born of nature (*svabhāvaja*) — *kila-kiñcita*, *līlā*, *vilāsa*, *vicchitti*, *vibhrama*, *moṭṭāyita*, *kuṭṭamita*, *bibboka*, *lalita* and *vikṛta*.

“The flower garland of qualities.” Śrīmatī's qualities are of three kinds: mental, verbal and bodily. Gratitude, forbearance, compassion and the like are mental; speech that delights the ear and the like are verbal; and excellences, age, form, loveliness, beauty and the like are bodily qualities.

## 176

“The *sakhīs* are Her thoughts of Kṛṣṇa's pastimes.” The eight states of mind of Śrīmatī, who is the very bliss of Kṛṣṇa's pastimes, are the eight *sakhīs*, and the states that follow them are all the other *mañjarīs*.

## 179

*Śyāma-rasa* is the *madhura-rasa*.

## 164–180

In describing Śrī Rādhikā's qualities, Kavirāja Gosvāmī relies on the prayer of Śrī Raghunātha dāsa Gosvāmī called *Premāmbhoja-makaranda*:

“[You,] whose form is manifested by the radiant *cintāmaṇi* of *mahābhāva*, who shine anointed with the finest fragrant unguent — the love (*praṇaya*) of Your friends; who are bathed in the waves of the nectar of compassion, the stream of the nectar of youth and the floods of the nectar of loveliness, and who make Indirā fade; whose limbs are covered by the silken garment of modesty, who are adorned with the saffron of beauty, whose body is painted with radiant dark musk; trembling, tears, hair standing on end, stupor, perspiration, faltering voice, flushing, madness and numbness — You who are adorned with ornaments of these nine finest jewels and wear a garland of flowers, the series of Your qualities, who are perfumed with the fragrant powder of the fine quality of being *dhīrā-adhīrā*; whose braided hair is hidden *māna*, who shine with the *tilaka* of good fortune, whose ears glitter with the earrings of hearing Kṛṣṇa's name and fame; whose lips are red with the betel of *rāga*, whose eye-black is the crookedness of love, who are scented with the camphor of the smile that flows from playful words; who sit playfully on the couch of pride in the inner chamber of fragrance and are marked with the trembling pendant of *prema-vaicittya*; whose breasts are hidden by the fine bodice tied with the anger of *praṇaya*, whose *kacchapī vīṇā* sounds with the fame and beauty that dry up the faces and hearts of Your rivals; who have playfully laid Your lotus hand on the shoulder of Your friend, youth (*madhyatā*), who are Śyāmā and serve the honey drink of the joy of love for Śyāma — bowing to You, this person, holding a straw between his teeth, begs: revive this miserable one by sprinkling him with the nectar of service to You! The merciful one does not abandon one who has come for shelter, even if he is wicked; therefore, O Gāndharvikā, alas, alas, do not abandon this one either, such as he is! Whoever recites this king of prayers called *Premāmbhoja-makaranda*, the cause of Śrī Rādhikā's mercy, will attain service to Her.”

[The meaning of the prayer:] Her form is manifested by the radiant *cintāmaṇi* of *mahābhāva*; the love (*praṇaya*) of the *sakhīs* for Kṛṣṇa is the fragrant *kuṅkuma* and the rest, from which She has gained a beautiful luster (1). In the morning Her form is bathed in the nectar of compassion, at midday in the nectar of youth, and in the evening in the nectar of loveliness (2). She wears the silken garment of modesty, is adorned with the *kuṅkuma* of beauty, and Her body is painted with dark musk, the *rasa* of *śṛṅgāra* (3). She is adorned with nine finest jewels: trembling, tears, hair standing on end, stupor, perspiration, faltering voice, flushing, madness and numbness (4). Her qualities — beauty, sweetness and the rest — rest on Her body as a garland of flowers. She has perfumed Her moods of being *dhīrā* and *adhīrā* with fragrant powder, that is, with camphor and the like (5). Her braided hair, that is, Her hair tied in a knot (*khõpā*), is *māna* hidden within; Her forehead shines with the *tilaka* of good fortune; the ornament of Her ears is hearing Kṛṣṇa's name and fame (6). Her lips are colored red with the betel of *anurāga*; She wears the crookedness of love as eye-black; She is scented with the camphor of the gentle smile that jokes (*narma*) bring (7). When She reclines on the couch of pride in the inner chamber of fragrance, the necklace of *vipralambha* swings on Her as a pendant, *prema-vaicittya* (8). Her breasts are covered by a bodice, the anger of *praṇaya*; Her *kacchapī vīṇā* is the fame and beauty that dry up the faces and hearts of Her rivals (9). She has laid Her lotus hand, Her play, on the shoulder of Her friend, youth; though She has many qualities, She serves the honey that delights Kṛṣṇa, the Kandarpa (10). Holding a straw between my teeth I pray to such a Śrī Rādhā: revive this miserable one by giving him the nectar of service to You (11). O Gāndharvikā, as merciful Kṛṣṇa does not abandon those who surrender to Him, so You too do not abandon one who takes shelter of You (12).

## 181

Who is the birthplace of Śrī Kṛṣṇa's *praṇaya*? Śrīmatī Rādhikā alone. Who is Kṛṣṇa's Beloved of incomparable qualities? Rādhikā alone, and no other. Crookedness in the hair, restlessness in the eyes, firmness in the breasts and the like belong to Rādhikā alone. Rādhikā alone can fulfill Hari's desires, and no one else.

## 185

The greatness of the pastimes is the glory of the love-pastimes of the Two.

## 187

The hero who is clever, freshly youthful, expert in jesting, free from anxiety and submissive to his beloveds is “*dhīra-lalita*.”

## 190–192

“O Rāmānanda, all that you have said — the determination of the ‘goal’ (*sādhya*), the description of Rādhā and Kṛṣṇa, and the greatness of the pastimes of the Two — is true. But if there is anything beyond this, tell it.” Rāya replied, “Beyond this I see no further course for the intellect. But there is one *bhāva* called ‘*prema-vilāsa-vivarta*,’ and of that I shall speak; only I cannot say whether hearing it will please You.” The purport is this: “Thus far I have described the true nature of the pastimes of love (*prema-vilāsa*). In the truth of *prema-vilāsa* there are two kinds of *bhāva*: *sambhoga* and *vipralambha*. Without *vipralambha*, *sambhoga* is not nourished. Separation itself is called ‘*vipralambha*’; this is the *vivarta* of *prema-vilāsa*, that is, during separation, by the force of *adhirūḍha-bhāva*, *sambhoga* is manifest (*sphūrti*) even without *sambhoga*.” Hardly had Rāya Rāmānanda begun to sing a song of his own composition about this *rasa* when Mahāprabhu, overwhelmed by His own *bhāva*, covered his mouth. The song is Śrīmatī's words in separation, that is, the manifestation of *sambhoga* in the state of *vipralambha*.

## 193

“Ah, in the time of *pūrva-rāga*, before We had met, when We looked at each other a feeling arose that is called *rāga*. That *rāga* grew and grew and reached no ‘limit’ (*avadhi*), no measure. That *rāga* is born of the very nature of Us both. It is not that Kṛṣṇa, the enjoyer (*ramaṇa*), is its cause, nor that I, the enjoyed one (*ramaṇī*), am its cause. The *rāga* that arose when We saw each other became Manobhava, that is, Madana; it ground Our hearts together and made them one. Now it is the time of separation. O friend, if you see that Kṛṣṇa has forgotten all those stories of Our love, tell Him this: when We met, We sought no messenger (*dūtī*) and asked no one else for anything — the five arrows of Anaṅga were the go-between of Our meeting. And now, in separation, that *rāga* has become *virāga*, that is, a special *rāga* — the *rāga* of separation, *adhirūḍha-bhāva* — and you, friend, are acting as the messenger! In the love of a noble hero you will see this custom everywhere.”

The meaning is this. In the time of *sambhoga*, *rāga* is a go-between, as it were, in the form of Anaṅga. In the time of *vipralambha* it likewise becomes a messenger filled with *adhirūḍha-bhāva*, and serves as the messenger in *prema-vilāsa-vivarta*, that is, in the appearance of *sambhoga* in the midst of *vipralambha*; and so Śrīmatī addresses it as “friend” (*sakhī*) and speaks these words. The essential meaning is this: in the *sambhoga* of *prema-vilāsa* there is joy, and in *vipralambha* there is the same joy. More than that, in *vipralambha* (because at the summit of service She is absorbed in Kṛṣṇa) — just as one takes a snake for a rope[^193-2] — a special kind of *sambhoga* arises: *adhirūḍha-mahābhāva* overcome by *vivarta*, in which She takes a *tamāla* tree and other things for Kṛṣṇa.

## 194

O king of elephants[^194-1] who dwell in the groves of Govardhana Hill! The Creator, expert in the art of *śṛṅgāra*, melted the lac of Your heart and Rādhikā's heart by the power of the *sāttvika-vikāras* and dispelled the illusion of difference. Then, in the palace of the universe, to increase the wonder of the world, He Himself richly dyed those two hearts with the vermilion of new *rāga*.

## 202–204

Having heard all this, Mahāprabhu said, “The goal (*sādhya*) has been described in full. Now tell Me the *sādhana*, the means of attaining this highest goal.” In reply Rāmānanda Rāya said, “In the *rasas* of *dāsya*, *vātsalya* and the rest this confidential truth cannot be attained. Without the *sakhīs* of Vraja no one can enter these pastimes. If one accepts the mood of a *sakhī* of Vraja and performs *sādhana* following the *sakhīs*, one attains the goal — service to Rādhā and Kṛṣṇa in the groves (*kuñja-sevā*); there is no other means.”

## 205

The *bhāva* of Rādhā and Kṛṣṇa is self-manifest; by nature it is happiness, and it is *vibhu*, that is, unlimited. Even so, without the *sakhīs* it cannot carry the fullness of *rasa* for even a moment — just as the Lord does not attain the fullness of His lordship without His spiritual opulences (*cid-vibhūti*). Which knower of *rasa* who has entered it, then, would not take shelter at the feet of the *sakhīs*?

## 208–209

Śrī Rādhā is Herself Kṛṣṇa's wish-fulfilling creeper of love, and the *sakhīs* are the shoots, flowers and leaves of that creeper. If one takes shelter at the feet of Rādhikā, who is that creeper, and waters the creeper, the shoots and the rest blossom wonderfully. If one waters the shoots themselves, they do not blossom so. In the same way the gopīs are happier from the meeting of Rādhā and Kṛṣṇa than from their own meeting with Kṛṣṇa.

## 210

The *sakhīs* of Vraja are equal to Śrī Rādhā. Śrī Rādhikā is the potency called *hlādinī* of Kṛṣṇa, the moon for the night-lilies of Vraja, and the *sakhīs* are like the young leaves, shoots, flowers and so on of the creeper of Her *prema*, the essence of that potency. When Rādhikā, full of the highest joy, is watered with the many nectarean *rasas* of Kṛṣṇa's pastimes, the *sakhīs* become a hundred times more joyful than from being watered themselves — and there is nothing surprising in this.

## 219–220

*Vaidhī-bhakti* consists of the sixty-four limbs of *bhajana*; pure faith (*śraddhā*) in it alone gives one the right to it. Seeing the natural *rāga* of the people of Vraja for Kṛṣṇa, some are filled with greed (*lobha*) to walk that path, and this very greed for the nectar of the gopīs' feeling gives them the right to the path of *rāgānugā*. In *bhajana* on the path of *rāgānugā*, giving up attachment to Vedic *dharma* — *varṇāśrama* and the rest — is naturally necessary.

## 221–222

In Vraja, Kṛṣṇa's servants — Raktaka, Patraka and others; Kṛṣṇa's friends — Śrīdāmā, Subala and others; Kṛṣṇa's parents — Nanda, Yaśodā and others — serve Kṛṣṇa, each in his own *bhāva*, his own *rasa*. When a person turns to worship in the *rasas* of Vraja and greed awakens in him for one particular *rasa* among them, he attains a spiritual nature (*cit-svarūpa*) fit for that *bhāva*, and at the time of perfection (*siddhi*) he attains Kṛṣṇa. The Upaniṣads, that is, the Śrutis, are an example of this. The Śrutis saw that without following the gopīs one cannot obtain the right to serve Kṛṣṇa in Vraja. Then they accepted the gopīs' guidance and, on the path of *rāga*, in the bodies of gopīs, worshiped Vrajendranandana.

## 223

The sages, restraining the breath by *prāṇāyāma* and firmly joining mind and senses in *yoga*, worshiped Brahman in the heart, and the enemies of Bhagavān, by the power of constant thought of Him, also entered that Brahman. The women of Vraja, their minds overcome by the strong poison — the beauty of Śrī Kṛṣṇa's arms, like the body of a serpent — attained the nectar of His lotus feet. We too, having obtained those bodies of gopīs, drank the nectar of His lotus feet in the mood of the gopīs.

## 224–225

In the fourth line of the verse, the word *sama-dṛśaḥ* is explained as “following the gopīs' feeling,” and the word *samāḥ* as the Śrutis' “attaining the bodies of gopīs.” The word *aṅghri-saroja-sudhā* is explained as “the bliss of association with Kṛṣṇa.”

## 226

Yaśodā's son, Bhagavān Śrī Kṛṣṇa, is easily attained by embodied beings who possess *bhakti*, but not so by *jñānīs* who have become one with the self (*ātma-bhūta*).[^226-1]

## 244–256

From the verse beginning “*prabhu kahe, — ‘kon vidyā…*’” up to the verse “*sthāvara deha, deva-deha yaiche avasthiti*,” in each verse the first line is the Lord's question and the second line is Rāya's answer. This conversation is found in the *Caitanya-candrodaya-nāṭaka* (Act 7).

## 265

We meditate on Śrī Kṛṣṇa, the supreme Truth, who by His own potency is eternally free of all deception. It is known for certain that from this Truth come the birth, maintenance and dissolution of the universe. When one reasons by *anvaya-vyatireka*, He is found in all things, or affairs, to be the one supreme *jña-tattva*, the knowing Truth, that is, the *svarūpa-tattva*. In the visible world He alone is *svarāṭ*, that is, the independent King. As the indwelling Lord He taught the truth of Brahman to Brahmā, the first poet. In Him intelligent scholars are bewildered again and again. In Him is the interchange, that is, the separate existence, of the many elements — fire, water, earth and the rest. In Him the three kinds of creation exist as real: creation as the rising of spirit, creation as the manifestation of the *jīvas*, and creation as the universes of *māyā*.

## 267–273

“Lord, at first I saw You as a *sannyāsī*, and now I see You as a dark cowherd boy. And before You I also see a golden doll. That doll has covered Your whole body with its golden radiance, and yet Your own color seems to show plainly; and Your lovely eyes tremble with many feelings. Lord, tell me frankly the cause of this wonderful state of Yours.” The Lord said, “Those who have deep love for Kṛṣṇa are the best of *bhāgavatas* (*bhāgavatottama*). The nature of their love is this: whatever they see, moving or nonmoving, they do not see its form, but everywhere see only the presence of Śrī Kṛṣṇa — the manifestation of their worshipable Lord (*iṣṭadeva*).”

## 274

The *bhāgavatottama* sees in all beings Bhagavān Śrī Kṛṣṇacandra, the Self of the self, and sees all beings in Śrī Kṛṣṇa, the Self of the self.

## 275

The forest creepers rich with flowers and fruits, and the trees bowed down by *bhāva* — the great trees of the forest, their bodies thrilling with love — revealed Kṛṣṇa within themselves and poured down streams of honey.

## 281

Śrī Kṛṣṇa in the form of Rasarāja and Śrīmatī Rādhikā in the form of Mahābhāva, united, are one *tattva*. It was this *svarūpa* that He showed, that is, He showed “the form of Śrī Kṛṣṇa adorned with Rādhā's *bhāva* and radiance.” In this way He showed a wondrous *svarūpa* in which one *tattva* is two, and two *tattvas* are one. Only those who are able to understand the truth of Śrī Kṛṣṇa Caitanya and the truth of Rādhā and Kṛṣṇa are granted, by the mercy of Śrī Svarūpa Gosvāmī, to serve that eternal *svarūpa*.

## 286–287

“Rāmānanda, you see in Me a separate ‘golden person’ (*gaura-puruṣa*) — but I am not that. I am that very Śrī Kṛṣṇa, the son of the king of the cowherds, and this golden hue of Mine — the touch of Rādhā's body — is eternal. Rādhikā touches no one but Kṛṣṇa. Steeping My *svarūpa* and mind in Śrī Rādhikā's feeling, I relish the *rasa* of My own sweetness, the sweetness of Kṛṣṇa.”

## 293

To Śrī Mahāprabhu's questions Śrī Rāmānanda Rāya first gave five answers (in this chapter, from verse 57 to 67). The first of them is like copper, an ordinary metal; the second is like bronze, a metal better than that; the third is like silver, a metal better still; the fourth is gold, the best of metals. But the fifth is *bhakti* free from *jñāna*; it is the jewel *cintāmaṇi*, that is, the goal (*sādhya*) itself, and by its power the other four acquire the worth of metals. And if the sixth answer (verses 68–81) is taken as the “first,” then the five answers about *prema* that follow it must be understood by the same comparison.

## 299

***Hanumān.*** In Vidyānagara the form (*mūrti*) of Hanumān is worshiped. Bowing to that village deity, the Lord went south.

## 303–304

Śrī Caitanya's life is like thickly boiled-down milk; Rāmānanda's life is *khaṇḍa*, or *khāṛ*, that is, a special kind of sugar in it; and the pastimes of Śrī Rādhā and Kṛṣṇa among them are camphor in that sweetened milk.

*Thus ends the eighth chapter of the “Amṛta-pravāha-bhāṣya.”*
