# Amṛta-pravāha-bhāṣya on Madhya-līlā 8

## 0

**Summary.** Mahāprabhu took *darśana* of Jiyaḍa-Nṛsiṁha, and on the bank of the Godāvarī, at Vidyānagara, He met Rāmānanda Rāya, who had come there to bathe. When they had become acquainted, Rāmānanda asked the Lord to stay in that village for a few days. At his request the Lord stayed in the house of a Vedic Vaiṣṇava brāhmaṇa. In the evening Rāmānanda Rāya, humbly dressed, came to Mahāprabhu and offered Him prostrate obeisances, and Mahāprabhu told him to recite verses that determine the goal (*sādhya*). First Rāmānanda Rāya spoke of *varṇāśrama-dharma*, the duty common to all good people. Then he recited a few verses on “offering one's actions to Kṛṣṇa” (*karmārpaṇa*), then on “action free from attachment,” then on “devotion mixed with knowledge,” and finally on “pure devotion free from knowledge.” Mahāprabhu accepted this last as “the object of the goal” (*sādhya-vastu*). Then the Lord asked Rāya to describe the higher stages of devotion, and as “the essence of the goal” (*sādhya-sāra*) Rāya described first “*prema-bhakti* in the form of pure *rati* for Kṛṣṇa,” then “the *prema* of a servant,” “the *prema* of a friend,” “the *prema* of parents,” and finally “*prema* in the mood of the beloved” (*kānta-bhāva*).[^0-1] Rāya explained in various ways and at length why the beloved's *prema* is the essence of the goal. When the Lord accepted it as the limit of the goal (*sādhya-avadhi*), Rāya described Rādhikā's *prema*. Then Rāya described the nature of Kṛṣṇa, the nature of Rādhā, the nature of *rasa*, and the truth of *prema* (*prema-tattva*). After that, at Mahāprabhu's request, Rāmānanda Rāya sang a song of his own composition about the *prema-vilāsa-vivarta*, full of *adhirūḍha-bhāva* in separation (*vipralambha*). At the end it was explained in detail how to attain the highest goal, loving service to Rādhā-Kṛṣṇa: the means is to follow the *sakhīs* of Vraja. For several days, every night, they held all kinds of talks about Kṛṣṇa. Then Rāmānanda saw Mahāprabhu's original truth (*mūla-tattva*) and His own true form, and he fainted. A few days later the Lord told Rāmānanda to give up government service and go to Puruṣottama, and He Himself continued south. Kavirāja Gosvāmī wrote all this according to the diary (*kaḍacā*) of Svarūpa Dāmodara.

## 1

Śrī Gaurāṅga is the ocean of the nectar of *siddhānta*. He infused the nectar of the *siddhānta* of His own devotion into the devotee-cloud named Rāmānanda, and then, through that *siddhānta* spread by Rāmānanda, He Himself again became the ocean, the ocean of knowing the truth of devotion.

## 6

Though fierce in its prowess, a lion is gentle with its own young. In the same way Nṛsiṁhadeva, fierce toward demons such as Hiraṇyakaśipu, is full of tenderness toward His own devotees such as Prahlāda.

## 23

The natural love that Rādhā-Kṛṣṇa feel for Their friend Viśākhā, and that the *sakhī* Viśākhā feels for Rādhā-Kṛṣṇa, arose in them.

## 32

Rāmānanda Rāya said, “Sārvabhauma regards me as his servant and looks after my welfare even behind my back, that is, even when I am not present.”

## 40

O Bhagavān! Great persons go to the homes of householders whose minds are wretched only to bring them the eternal good; they go for no other reason.

## 43

*Ākṛti*, form, that is, the body of a *nyagrodha-parimaṇḍala*, and *prakṛti*, nature, that is, Your supremely merciful disposition: in these You are seen to be Īśvara.

## 55

*Sannyāsīs* bathe three times a day (*tri-savana*). Following this rule, the Lord had bathed in the evening and was sitting [on the bank].

## 57

The Lord said, “O Rāmānanda Rāya, recite a verse of scripture that determines the truth of the goal (*sādhya-tattva*).” Rāya said, “When human beings follow their own *dharma*, devotion to Viṣṇu arises.”

## 58

The Supreme Lord Viṣṇu is worshiped by a person who keeps the conduct of *varṇa-dharma* and *āśrama-dharma*. Apart from the conduct of *varṇāśrama* there is no other means of pleasing Him.

The purport is this: to please Bhagavān is the truth of the goal (*sādhya-tattva*). When human beings follow the *varṇa-dharma* determined by their own nature and the *āśrama-dharma* determined by their situation, Lord Viṣṇu is pleased. There are four *varṇas*: brāhmaṇas, kṣatriyas, vaiśyas and śūdras. A person should conduct his life by following the *dharma* that scripture has fixed for his *varṇa*. There are four *āśramas*: *brahmacarya*, *gārhasthya*, *vānaprastha* and *sannyāsa*. Each should please Bhagavān by following the *dharma* prescribed for his own *āśrama*. If one deviates from it, one incurs sin (*pratyavāya*) and goes to hell. To take up the path of the highest goal, a life of *dharma* is needed first of all. The *dharma* that sustains life is naturally different for people of different natures.

A person's nature arises from birth, association and education. No one can conduct his life skillfully without accepting a *varṇa* according to his nature. Natures are of many kinds, but at root they fall into four divisions. (1) Those whose nature turns to God and learning are “brāhmaṇas.” (2) Those whose natural inclination is valor and government are “kṣatriyas.” (3) Those whose natural work is farming, cattle-keeping and trade are “vaiśyas.” (4) Those whose nature is only to serve the three *varṇas* are “śūdras.” When a person worships [the Lord] in the *dharma* of his own *varṇa* and according to his *āśrama*, he rises naturally; when he acts contrary to it, he naturally falls. Therefore a life of *dharma* is the root of every human excellence.

## 59–60

[Kṛṣṇa] says in the *Gītā*: “O Kaunteya, whatever you eat, whatever you offer in sacrifice, whatever you give, and whatever austerity you perform, offer all of it to Me, Kṛṣṇa.”

In his first answer Rāya named as “the goal” (*sādhya*) the worship of Kṛṣṇa within *varṇāśrama-dharma*. The Lord therefore called this “external,” and so that Rāya would give a true answer to His question, He told him to speak of what is higher than ordinary *varṇāśrama-dharma*. Rāya then answered that “the essence of all goals” is declared to be offering to Kṛṣṇa all one's actions within *varṇāśrama*.

## 61

Hearing even this, the Lord said, “This too is external. The answer to My question lies beyond it; tell Me that.” In reply Rāya said, “Giving up one's own *dharma* (*svadharma*) is the essence of the goal.” That is, among the four *varṇas* the brāhmaṇa gives up his own (household) *dharma* and accepts *sannyāsa*, and the other *varṇas*, following him, accept the marks of renunciation and leave home. This *sannyāsa* is called giving up *svadharma*, or giving up *karma*. The *dharma* of renunciation brings the pleasure of Hari.

## 62

In the scriptures on *dharma* I, Bhagavān, have ordained many things as “*dharma*.” He who weighs their merits and faults, gives up all these pursuits of *dharma* and worships Me is the best of all (a *sādhu*).

## 63

Give up all *dharmas* and take shelter of Me alone, Bhagavān. Then I shall free you from all sins. Do not grieve.

## 64

Hearing this answer, the Lord called it too external and told him to speak of something higher. Then Rāya said, “Devotion mixed with knowledge (*jñāna-miśrā bhakti*) may be called the essence of the goal.”

## 65

It is said in the *Gītā*: “He who, by cultivating knowledge in the form of the doctrine of the nondifferent Brahman, himself becomes serene, free from grief and desire, and equal toward all beings, and who attains the state of Brahman, then obtains supreme devotion to Me.” The purport is this: devotion mixed with *karma* (*karma-miśrā bhakti*) was spoken of earlier, and higher than it is devotion mixed with knowledge.

## 66

Hearing this, the Lord said, “This too is external. Tell Me what lies beyond it.” Rāya said that devotion free from knowledge (*jñāna-śūnyā bhakti*) is the essence of all goals.

## 67

In the *Bhāgavata* Brahmā said to Bhagavān: “O Bhagavān! There are devotees who have completely given up the endeavor of knowledge, the contemplation of undifferentiated Brahman. They hear the narrations about You that flow from the lips of *sādhus*, and they live abiding on the path of the *sādhus* with body, mind and words.[^67-1] In all the three worlds You are hard to attain, yet to them You become easily attainable.”

## 68

Hearing this, the Lord said, “Now the goal has indeed been determined. Tell Me what is higher than this.” The purport is this. Offering one's actions [to Kṛṣṇa] is higher than merely following *varṇāśrama-dharma*. Giving up *svadharma*, that is, accepting *sannyāsa* after giving up the *dharma* of one's *varṇa*, is higher than merely offering one's actions. Higher than that is devotion mixed with knowledge, the cultivation of Brahman. Yet all of these are external, because the object of the goal, pure devotion, is not found in any of these four conclusions. Devotion that is *āropa-siddhā* or *saṅga-siddhā* is never known as “pure devotion.” “*Svarūpa-siddhā bhakti*” is a separate truth: it is eternally distinct from *karma*, from offering one's actions, from *sannyāsa* in the form of giving up *karma*, and from devotion mixed with knowledge. The mark of this pure devotion is this: it is the cultivation of Kṛṣṇa (*kṛṣṇānuśīlana*) in a favorable mood, free from other desires and unobstructed by knowledge, *karma* and the like. It alone is the object of the goal, for it is seen even while it is still being attained, and at the stage of perfection it is seen in its full purity. In answer to the Lord's last question Rāya said, “*Prema-bhakti* is the essence of all goals.” At first pure devotion appears as *śānta-bhakti*: in it there is as yet no sense that Kṛṣṇa is one's own (*mamatā*).

## 69

Food and drink give joy only as long as hunger and thirst are strong in the stomach. In the same way, the Friend of the distressed may be worshiped with many kinds of offerings, but the devotees' hearts melt with bliss only when that worship is full of *prema*.

## 70

There is a mind imbued with the *rasa* of devotion to Kṛṣṇa. It cannot be obtained by pious merit (*sukṛti*) earned in ten million births, yet it can be had for one single price, greed (*lobha*). Wherever you find such a mind, buy it at once.

Of these two verses, the first points to “*vaidhī-bhakti*,” rooted in faith (*śraddhā*), and the second to *rāgānugā-bhakti*, rooted in greed (*lobha*). From here on Rāya Rāmānanda's words will rest on *rāgānugā-bhakti* alone; that is, from now on he takes up the *siddhānta* of *rāga-bhakti* and leaves aside the talk of *vaidhī-bhakti*.

## 71

Having heard this much, the Lord said, “That is so. But tell Me what lies beyond it.” In reply Rāya said, “*Dāsya-prema*, the *prema* of a servant, is the essence of all goals.” When *mamatā*, the sense “He is mine,” joins “devotion marked by *prema*,” it becomes *dāsya-prema*. In *prema* in general (*prema-sādhāraṇya*) no relationship is yet established between Bhagavān and the devotee. When the sense of *mamatā*, “Bhagavān is my master,” joins it, general *prema* becomes *dāsya-prema*, which is higher than general *prema*.

## 72

The *Śrī Bhāgavata* says: “Simply by hearing the name of Bhagavān a *jīva* becomes pure. What then is left to attain for those who are servants of that Bhagavān, whose feet are holy places?”[^72-1]

## 74

Hearing this, the Lord said, “If you can go a little further, you will find the essence of all.” Rāya answered, “*Sakhya-prema*, the *prema* of a friend, for Śrī Kṛṣṇa is the essence of all goals.” Rāya's purport is this. There is *mamatā* in *dāsya-prema*, but “fear” and “awe” (*sambhrama*) easily arise in it from the thought “Bhagavān is my master.” When one gives up that “fear” and “awe” and embraces *viśrambha*, that is, “complete confidence,” that *prema* becomes *sakhya-prema*. In this *prema* a “sense of equality” arises between Kṛṣṇa and His friends.

## 75

The *Śrī Bhāgavata* says: “On the path of knowledge He is manifest as the experience of the bliss of Brahman, to the devotees in *dāsya-rasa* as the Supreme Deity, and to those who take shelter of *māyā* as a human boy.[^75-1] With this Bhagavān, Śrī Kṛṣṇa, the cowherd boys of Vraja, by the fruit of great pious merit, sported in *sakhya-rasa*.”

## 76

The Lord said, “*Sakhya-rasa* is indeed higher than *dāsya-rasa*. Yet go a little further and you will find the essence of the goal.” In reply Rāya said, “The *prema* of *vātsalya-bhāva*, the parental mood, is the essence of all goals.” When the *prema* of *sakhya-rasa*, which is based on confidence (*viśrambha*), is joined by greater affection (*sneha*), *vātsalya-rasa* arises.

## 77

*Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam* says: “O brāhmaṇa, what pious deeds did Nanda perform that Kṛṣṇa appeared as his son? And what pious deeds did Yaśodā perform that Kṛṣṇa, Parabrahman Himself, called her ‘mother’ and drank from her breast?”

## 78

The mercy that the *gopī* Yaśodā received from Śrī Kṛṣṇa, the giver of liberation to ordinary people, was not received by Brahmā, by Śiva, or even by Lakṣmī, who rests on Viṣṇu's chest.

## 79

The Lord said, “Step by step this has become ever higher. Yet beyond it there is one more *rasa*, and that you may call ‘the essence of the goal.’” Rāya answered, “*Kānta-bhāva*, the mood of the beloved, toward Śrī Kṛṣṇa is the essence of all goals, the highest limit (*parākāṣṭhā*) of *prema*.” The purport is this. In general *prema* there is no *mamatā*; in *dāsya-rasa* there is no *viśrambha*, “confidence”; in *sakhya-rasa* there is no “abundance of affection” (*snehādhikya*); and in *vātsalya-rasa* there is no “freedom from all reserve” (*niḥsaṅkoca-bhāva*). Therefore in none of these *rasas* does *prema*, the goal, reach its fullness. When *kānta-bhāva* toward Kṛṣṇa arises, then one finds a single, undivided truth of *prema*, free from all these lacks, the essence of all goals.

## 80

In Vṛndāvana, at the *rāsa* festival, Śrī Kṛṣṇa held the beautiful girls of Vraja by the neck with His mighty arms. The mercy that dawned upon them was not attained even by the wholly devoted potencies of Paravyoma, such as Lakṣmī, who rests on His chest. Nor was it the lot of the celestial women, who have the fragrance and luster of lotuses. What then to speak of other women?
