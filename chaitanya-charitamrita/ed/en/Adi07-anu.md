# Anubhāṣya on Ādi-līlā 7

## 1

> Subheading: A description of the great magnanimity of Śrī Kṛṣṇa Caitanya —

**agaty-eka-gatiṁ** (the one, incomparable refuge of the *agati*, those who have no other refuge), **hīnārthādhika-** (to those who are *hīna-artha* — bereft of *artha*, that is, of the highest goal, deprived of it — that which is beyond all [*adhika*], the greatest: love for Kṛṣṇa, the fifth goal of human life) **sādhakam** (the giver) **śrī-caitanyaṁ** **natvā** (having bowed down), **asya** (of this Bhagavān, Śrī Kṛṣṇa Caitanya) **prema-bhakti-vadānyatā** (the great compassion of granting *prema-bhakti* for Kṛṣṇa) **likhyate** (is described).

## 3

> Subheading: Of the six *tattvas* in the verse “*vande gurūn*…,” apart from *guru-tattva*, the examination of the Pañca-tattva begins; though without difference, it is fivefold for the tasting of *rasa* —

## 5

The Reality possessed of potency (*śaktimān vastu*) is manifest as the Pañca-tattva, recognized through five different kinds of pastimes. In substance it has no duality and so is one, yet it is full of fivefold variety. This variety is a distinctive feature of the pastimes: unlike a dry existence without taste, it serves the sweetness of *rasa*. The *śruti* says: “His supreme potency is heard to be manifold indeed.”[^5-1] From these words it follows that the distinctions of the many potencies of the Reality, the nondual Knowledge (*advaya-jñāna*), exist eternally.

In the Pañca-tattva — Śrī Gaurāṅga, Nityānanda, Advaita, Gadādhara, Śrīvāsa and the others — there is no difference whatsoever in substance. But for the tasting of *rasa* this *tattva*, full of varied pastimes, is distinguished in five ways: “*bhakta-rūpa*,” “*bhakta-svarūpa*,” “*bhaktāvatāra*,” “*bhakta-śakti*” and “*śuddha-bhakta*.” Of these five *tattvas*, the “*bhakta-rūpa*,” the “*bhakta-svarūpa*” and the “*bhaktāvatāra*” are the Prabhu, the Viṣṇu-*tattva*, in the forms of “*svayam*,” “*prakāśa*” and “*aṁśa*.” The “*bhakta-śakti*” and the “*śuddha-bhakta*” are the *tattva* of potency: it is included within the Viṣṇu-*tattva*, rests on Him as its shelter and is nondifferent from Him. Therefore everything that serves *rasa* (*rasopakaraṇa*) is nondifferent from the Reality and intimately united with the Form full of *rasa*; in substance they cannot differ from one another. If, of the two — the “worshipper” and the “One who is worshipped” — one is separated or absent, there can be no pastimes of tasting *rasa*.

## 6

> Subheading: Explanation of the last of the fourteen opening verses of Ādi — (a verse from the diary of Śrī Rūpa Gosvāmī)[^6-1]

**bhakta-rūpa-svarūpakam** (Him whose form [*rūpa*] is Śrī Gaura, full of a devotee's feelings, with a pure body, intent on tasting Himself, and whose *svarūpa* is Nityānanda, who has taken the form of a brother), **bhaktāvatāraṁ** (Advaita), **bhaktākhyaṁ** (Śrīvāsa and others, who abide in *śānta*, *dāsya* and the other *rasas*), **bhakta-śaktikam** (Śrī Gadādhara, Dāmodara, Rāmānanda and others), **pañca-tattvātmakaṁ** (Him whose very self, the *svarūpa*, is the five *tattvas*) **kṛṣṇaṁ** (Kṛṣṇa Caitanya-deva) **namāmi**.

## 7

> Subheading: Only Śrī Nanda-nandana, the *svayaṁ-rūpa*, is the Lord of all —

## 8

> Subheading: All the Viṣṇus, the Vaiṣṇavas and everything that serves in the *dhāma*, the *jīvas* and *pradhāna* — all are Kṛṣṇa's servants —

## 9

> Subheading: That same Kṛṣṇa is Gaura —

## 10

> Subheading: Śrī Kṛṣṇa Caitanya is the Lord of all, and yet He is full of the mood of being subordinate —

The *śruti* mantra “The eternal among the eternals, the conscious among the conscious”[^10-1] points to countless spiritual entities, and their one Supreme Lord is Śrī Caitanya-deva. The Māyāvādīs try to merge the atomic particles of spiritual potency with the all-pervading Spirit (*vibhu-cit*), and so they fall into error; this verse is presented to dispel it. Śrī Caitanya-deva is nondifferent from Vrajendranandana, yet, regarding Him (Kṛṣṇa) as the One to be worshipped, He assumes a form full of the mood of serving Him. Let no one, then, consider this form of Bhagavān an object of material enjoyment, or count it among the *jīvas* within the material world. That is why Śrī Caitanya's form has not been called merely the form of a *sādhaka* within the material world. It is manifest in *viśuddha-sattva*, and so this form of *rasa* is recognized only in a heart made bright by *sattva*. Śrī Caitanya-deva is the Supreme Lord Himself, and yet He displays pastimes befitting a servant, not the pastimes of an enjoyer. To see His holy form, with a vision darkened by *tamas*, as some instrument engaged in gratifying the senses is forbidden.

## 11

> Subheading: Kṛṣṇa descends as Gaura “in the form of a devotee” only to taste His own sweetness —

Kṛṣṇa, the shelter of all sweetness, has one unprecedented disposition of heart: though He Himself is the *viṣaya-vigraha*, He accepts the mood of the *āśraya*, the worshipper, and becomes absorbed in tasting service to the *viṣaya*. Yet Śrī Caitanya-deva is not merely a form full of the mood of the *āśraya*: He is the *svayaṁ-rūpa*, the Reality Himself.

## 12

> Subheading: Nitāi is the “*bhakta-svarūpa*,” Advaita the “*bhaktāvatāra*” —

## 14

> Subheading: Nitāi and Advaita — Gaura is the Lord even of these two Lords —

## 15

> Subheading: Three *tattvas* are the worshipped, and the fourth and fifth *tattvas* are the worshippers —

## 14–15

From the description of the true nature of the Pañca-tattva we can understand that Śrī Mahāprabhu alone is the highest Truth (*para-tattva*), and that Śrī Nityānanda and Advaita Prabhu are the “Īśvara-*tattva*” subordinate to Him. The Supreme Lord and the two manifestations of the Lord are all *para-tattva*, and all of Them are worshipped by every other *tattva*. The fourth, the *tattva* of the pure devotee (*śuddha-bhakta*), and the fifth, the *tattva* of the intimate devotee (*antaraṅga-bhakta*), are both *tattvas* of “worshippers.” The two *tattvas* who are “worshipped” appear in the form of servants: though the two *tattvas* of “worshippers” revere Them, They Themselves abide in the service of Śrī Gaurāṅga, the One who is served.

## 16

> Subheading: Śrīvāsa and others — the *tattva* of the devotee —

## 17

> Subheading: Gadādhara and others — the *tattva* of potency —

## 16–17

The *tattvas* of the intimate devotee and of the pure devotee differ in this way: the *tattva* of potency abides in *madhura-rasa*, and in *vātsalya*, *sakhya* and *dāsya-rasa*. If one compares them impartially, the potencies are higher than the devotees, and therefore Śrī Gaurasundara's intimate servants are the devotees eternally sheltered in *madhura-rasa*. The servants of Śrī Nityānanda and Śrī Advaita generally abide in *vātsalya*, *sakhya*, *dāsya* and *śānta-rasa*. But when these pure devotees come to feel intense love for Śrī Gaurasundara, they take shelter of *madhura-rasa* under the guidance of the intimate devotees. This is made clear at the beginning of Śrī Ṭhākura Mahāśaya's *Prārthanā*: “When will my body thrill as I say ‘Gaurāṅga’? When will tears flow from my eyes as I say ‘Hari, Hari’? When will Nitāicānd be merciful to me? When will my desire for worldly life become insignificant? When will my mind become pure, giving up the objects of the senses? When will I see Śrī Vṛndāvana? When will I yearn for the feet of Rūpa and Raghunātha? When will I understand the love of the Divine Couple?”

Describing how the “*śuddha-bhakta*” and the “*antaraṅga-bhakta*” differ, Śrī Rūpapāda in his book *Upadeśāmṛta* depicted the steps by which the practicing *jīva* rises higher and higher: “Of all who perform karma, the knowers (*jñānīs*) are dearer to Hari — so it is said everywhere. Dearer than they are those who have left knowledge behind and are wholly given to *bhakti*. Dearer than these are those fixed in *prema* alone. Dearer than they are the lotus-eyed cowherd girls, and dearer even than they is Rādhikā, the most beloved. And just as dear to Him is this pond of Hers. What wise person would not take shelter of it?” (*Upadeśāmṛta* 10)

Of the five *tattvas* of the Pañca-tattva, two are potency (*śakti*) and three are the possessor of potency (*śaktimān*). The pure devotees and the intimate devotees are the two kinds of potency. Those who are free of other desires and do not cover their pure inclination to serve Kṛṣṇa with the covering of karma or *jñāna* are *śuddha-bhaktas*. The devotees wholly devoted to Kṛṣṇa (*aikāntika*) who have taken shelter in *madhura-rasa* alone are the *antaraṅga-bhaktas*. *Madhura-rasa* includes *vātsalya*, *sakhya* and *dāsya*. The *antaraṅga-bhakta* is a special kind of *śuddha-bhakta*.

## 18

> Subheading: With the four *tattvas* the Lord plays, preaches, tastes and gives —

## 18–19

Śrī Mahāprabhu, together with all of them — His *prakāśa*, the avatāra of His *puruṣa-avatāra*, the intimate devotees and the pure devotees — Himself eternally plays by tasting *prema*, and gives *prema* to the world by preaching *kīrtana*.

## 20

> Subheading: The five *tattvas* together eternally taste and distribute the *rasa* of love for Kṛṣṇa —

## 23

> Subheading: In distributing love for Kṛṣṇa there is no considering who is fit and who is unfit —

## 24

> Subheading: Distributing *prema* makes it increase rather than diminish —

## 25

> Subheading: The world drowns in the flood of *prema* —

## 27

> Subheading: Immersed in the *rasa* of love for Kṛṣṇa, the *jīva* is rid of the seed of karma —

In the Lord's marginal potency, called the *jīva-śakti*, together with the endeavor turned toward Kṛṣṇa there also lies, in unmanifest form, the seed of the desire to enjoy — aversion to Kṛṣṇa. This seed of desire, born of the tree of *saṁsāra*, is watered by the flow of time, and day after day it binds the conditioned *jīva* with all kinds of bonds of enjoyment and torments him with the threefold miseries. A seed buried in the soil will not sprout if water submerges it. Just so, in the flood of *prema* — the bottomless waters of the ocean of service to Bhagavān — the seeds of the desire to enjoy anything other than service to Kṛṣṇa were drowned and destroyed, and no sprouts of desire could arise from them any longer. Śrī Caitanyacandra descended in the form of the Pañca-tattva, and the purpose of His descent was fulfilled; seeing this, all were filled with joy. Śrīmat Prabodhānanda Sarasvatī, the *tridaṇḍipāda*, described this in his book *Śrī Caitanya-candrāmṛta*: “When Caitanyacandra revealed the supreme path of *bhakti-yoga*, the worldly gave up talk of wives, sons and the like; the learned gave up disputes over the *śāstras*; the kings of *yogīs* gave up the hardship of restraining the breath; ascetics gave up their austerities; and *sannyāsīs* gave up the practice of knowledge. No other *rasa* remained.”

## 28

> Subheading: The rain of *prema* increases the *rasa* of *prema* —

## 29

> Subheading: Those deprived of the *rasa* of love for Kṛṣṇa —

## 31

> Subheading: The ocean of causeless mercy thinks how to deliver them —

## 33

> Subheading: To deliver the fallen *jīvas* deprived of *prema*, He accepts *sannyāsa* —

One who mistakenly believes that “*māyā*” is present in Bhagavān's being, in His abode, in devotion to Him and in His devotee — though all of these are beyond *māyā* — is a “Māyāvādī.” One who mistakenly believes that these four truths are subject to karma and to enjoying its fruits is “devoted to karma” (*karma-niṣṭha*). People who mistakenly think that in these four truths there is room for argument born of ignorance are “sophists.” One who mistakenly thinks that these four truths deserve censure is a “blasphemer.” One who mistakenly thinks that other things of *māyā* are equal to these four truths is a “*pāṣaṇḍī*.” And those fond of study who mistakenly think that these four truths are on a par with other objects of material enjoyment are “lowly students.” All of them fled, driven by one aim: that the waters of the flood of *prema* given by Gaurasundara, who is full of love, should in no way touch them. Seeing this, Śrī Mahāprabhu wished to accept the ornament of the fourth *āśrama*. It is highly revered by those people of material nature already mentioned, who turn away from love for Kṛṣṇa and long for the four goals of life (*catur-varga*). He reasoned that in the eyes of these worldly people, bewitched by *māyā*, the fourth *āśrama* is the desirable ideal.
