# Anubhāṣya on Ādi-līlā 7

## 1

> Subheading: A description of the great magnanimity of Śrī Kṛṣṇa Caitanya —

**agaty-eka-gatiṁ** (the one, incomparable refuge of the *agati*, those who have no other refuge), **hīnārthādhika-** (to those who are *hīna-artha* — bereft of *artha*, that is, of the highest goal, deprived of it — that which is beyond all [*adhika*], the greatest: love for Kṛṣṇa, the fifth goal of human life) **sādhakam** (the giver) **śrī-caitanyaṁ** **natvā** (having bowed down), **asya** (of this Bhagavān, Śrī Kṛṣṇa Caitanya) **prema-bhakti-vadānyatā** (the great compassion of granting *prema-bhakti* for Kṛṣṇa) **likhyate** (is described).

## 3

> Subheading: Of the six *tattvas* in the verse “*vande gurūn*…,” apart from *guru-tattva*, the examination of the Pañca-tattva begins; though without difference, it is fivefold for the tasting of *rasa* —

## 5

The Reality possessed of potency (*śaktimān vastu*) is manifest as the Pañca-tattva, recognized through five different kinds of pastimes. In substance it has no duality and so is one, yet it is full of fivefold variety. This variety is a distinctive feature of the pastimes: unlike a dry existence without taste, it serves the sweetness of *rasa*. The *śruti* says: “His supreme potency is heard to be manifold indeed.”[^5-1] From these words it follows that the distinctions of the many potencies of the Reality, the nondual Knowledge (*advaya-jñāna*), exist eternally.

In the Pañca-tattva — Śrī Gaurāṅga, Nityānanda, Advaita, Gadādhara, Śrīvāsa and the others — there is no difference whatsoever in substance. But for the tasting of *rasa* this *tattva*, full of varied pastimes, is distinguished in five ways: “*bhakta-rūpa*,” “*bhakta-svarūpa*,” “*bhaktāvatāra*,” “*bhakta-śakti*” and “*śuddha-bhakta*.” Of these five *tattvas*, the “*bhakta-rūpa*,” the “*bhakta-svarūpa*” and the “*bhaktāvatāra*” are the Prabhu, the Viṣṇu-*tattva*, in the forms of “*svayam*,” “*prakāśa*” and “*aṁśa*.” The “*bhakta-śakti*” and the “*śuddha-bhakta*” are the *tattva* of potency: it is included within the Viṣṇu-*tattva*, rests on Him as its shelter and is nondifferent from Him. Therefore everything that serves *rasa* (*rasopakaraṇa*) is nondifferent from the Reality and intimately united with the Form full of *rasa*; in substance they cannot differ from one another. If, of the two — the “worshipper” and the “One who is worshipped” — one is separated or absent, there can be no pastimes of tasting *rasa*.

## 6

> Subheading: Explanation of the last of the fourteen opening verses of Ādi — (a verse from the diary of Śrī Rūpa Gosvāmī)[^6-1]

**bhakta-rūpa-svarūpakam** (Him whose form [*rūpa*] is Śrī Gaura, full of a devotee's feelings, with a pure body, intent on tasting Himself, and whose *svarūpa* is Nityānanda, who has taken the form of a brother), **bhaktāvatāraṁ** (Advaita), **bhaktākhyaṁ** (Śrīvāsa and others, who abide in *śānta*, *dāsya* and the other *rasas*), **bhakta-śaktikam** (Śrī Gadādhara, Dāmodara, Rāmānanda and others), **pañca-tattvātmakaṁ** (Him whose very self, the *svarūpa*, is the five *tattvas*) **kṛṣṇaṁ** (Kṛṣṇa Caitanya-deva) **namāmi**.

## 7

> Subheading: Only Śrī Nanda-nandana, the *svayaṁ-rūpa*, is the Lord of all —

## 8

> Subheading: All the Viṣṇus, the Vaiṣṇavas and everything that serves in the *dhāma*, the *jīvas* and *pradhāna* — all are Kṛṣṇa's servants —

## 9

> Subheading: That same Kṛṣṇa is Gaura —

## 10

> Subheading: Śrī Kṛṣṇa Caitanya is the Lord of all, and yet He is full of the mood of being subordinate —

The *śruti* mantra “The eternal among the eternals, the conscious among the conscious”[^10-1] points to countless spiritual entities, and their one Supreme Lord is Śrī Caitanya-deva. The Māyāvādīs try to merge the atomic particles of spiritual potency with the all-pervading Spirit (*vibhu-cit*), and so they fall into error; this verse is presented to dispel it. Śrī Caitanya-deva is nondifferent from Vrajendranandana, yet, regarding Him (Kṛṣṇa) as the One to be worshipped, He assumes a form full of the mood of serving Him. Let no one, then, consider this form of Bhagavān an object of material enjoyment, or count it among the *jīvas* within the material world. That is why Śrī Caitanya's form has not been called merely the form of a *sādhaka* within the material world. It is manifest in *viśuddha-sattva*, and so this form of *rasa* is recognized only in a heart made bright by *sattva*. Śrī Caitanya-deva is the Supreme Lord Himself, and yet He displays pastimes befitting a servant, not the pastimes of an enjoyer. To see His holy form, with a vision darkened by *tamas*, as some instrument engaged in gratifying the senses is forbidden.

## 11

> Subheading: Kṛṣṇa descends as Gaura “in the form of a devotee” only to taste His own sweetness —

Kṛṣṇa, the shelter of all sweetness, has one unprecedented disposition of heart: though He Himself is the *viṣaya-vigraha*, He accepts the mood of the *āśraya*, the worshipper, and becomes absorbed in tasting service to the *viṣaya*. Yet Śrī Caitanya-deva is not merely a form full of the mood of the *āśraya*: He is the *svayaṁ-rūpa*, the Reality Himself.

## 12

> Subheading: Nitāi is the “*bhakta-svarūpa*,” Advaita the “*bhaktāvatāra*” —

## 14

> Subheading: Nitāi and Advaita — Gaura is the Lord even of these two Lords —

## 15

> Subheading: Three *tattvas* are the worshipped, and the fourth and fifth *tattvas* are the worshippers —

## 14–15

From the description of the true nature of the Pañca-tattva we can understand that Śrī Mahāprabhu alone is the highest Truth (*para-tattva*), and that Śrī Nityānanda and Advaita Prabhu are the “Īśvara-*tattva*” subordinate to Him. The Supreme Lord and the two manifestations of the Lord are all *para-tattva*, and all of Them are worshipped by every other *tattva*. The fourth, the *tattva* of the pure devotee (*śuddha-bhakta*), and the fifth, the *tattva* of the intimate devotee (*antaraṅga-bhakta*), are both *tattvas* of “worshippers.” The two *tattvas* who are “worshipped” appear in the form of servants: though the two *tattvas* of “worshippers” revere Them, They Themselves abide in the service of Śrī Gaurāṅga, the One who is served.

## 16

> Subheading: Śrīvāsa and others — the *tattva* of the devotee —

## 17

> Subheading: Gadādhara and others — the *tattva* of potency —

## 16–17

The *tattvas* of the intimate devotee and of the pure devotee differ in this way: the *tattva* of potency abides in *madhura-rasa*, and in *vātsalya*, *sakhya* and *dāsya-rasa*. If one compares them impartially, the potencies are higher than the devotees, and therefore Śrī Gaurasundara's intimate servants are the devotees eternally sheltered in *madhura-rasa*. The servants of Śrī Nityānanda and Śrī Advaita generally abide in *vātsalya*, *sakhya*, *dāsya* and *śānta-rasa*. But when these pure devotees come to feel intense love for Śrī Gaurasundara, they take shelter of *madhura-rasa* under the guidance of the intimate devotees. This is made clear at the beginning of Śrī Ṭhākura Mahāśaya's *Prārthanā*: “When will my body thrill as I say ‘Gaurāṅga’? When will tears flow from my eyes as I say ‘Hari, Hari’? When will Nitāicānd be merciful to me? When will my desire for worldly life become insignificant? When will my mind become pure, giving up the objects of the senses? When will I see Śrī Vṛndāvana? When will I yearn for the feet of Rūpa and Raghunātha? When will I understand the love of the Divine Couple?”

Describing how the “*śuddha-bhakta*” and the “*antaraṅga-bhakta*” differ, Śrī Rūpapāda in his book *Upadeśāmṛta* depicted the steps by which the practicing *jīva* rises higher and higher: “Of all who perform karma, the knowers (*jñānīs*) are dearer to Hari — so it is said everywhere. Dearer than they are those who have left knowledge behind and are wholly given to *bhakti*. Dearer than these are those fixed in *prema* alone. Dearer than they are the lotus-eyed cowherd girls, and dearer even than they is Rādhikā, the most beloved. And just as dear to Him is this pond of Hers. What wise person would not take shelter of it?” (*Upadeśāmṛta* 10)

Of the five *tattvas* of the Pañca-tattva, two are potency (*śakti*) and three are the possessor of potency (*śaktimān*). The pure devotees and the intimate devotees are the two kinds of potency. Those who are free of other desires and do not cover their pure inclination to serve Kṛṣṇa with the covering of karma or *jñāna* are *śuddha-bhaktas*. The devotees wholly devoted to Kṛṣṇa (*aikāntika*) who have taken shelter in *madhura-rasa* alone are the *antaraṅga-bhaktas*. *Madhura-rasa* includes *vātsalya*, *sakhya* and *dāsya*. The *antaraṅga-bhakta* is a special kind of *śuddha-bhakta*.

## 18

> Subheading: With the four *tattvas* the Lord plays, preaches, tastes and gives —

## 18–19

Śrī Mahāprabhu, together with all of them — His *prakāśa*, the avatāra of His *puruṣa-avatāra*, the intimate devotees and the pure devotees — Himself eternally plays by tasting *prema*, and gives *prema* to the world by preaching *kīrtana*.

## 23

> Subheading: In distributing love for Kṛṣṇa there is no considering who is fit and who is unfit —

## 24

> Subheading: Distributing *prema* makes it increase rather than diminish —

## 25

> Subheading: The world drowns in the flood of *prema* —

## 27

> Subheading: Immersed in the *rasa* of love for Kṛṣṇa, the *jīva* is rid of the seed of karma —

In the Lord's marginal potency, called the *jīva-śakti*, together with the endeavor turned toward Kṛṣṇa there also lies, in unmanifest form, the seed of the desire to enjoy — aversion to Kṛṣṇa. This seed of desire, born of the tree of *saṁsāra*, is watered by the flow of time, and day after day it binds the conditioned *jīva* with all kinds of bonds of enjoyment and torments him with the threefold miseries. A seed buried in the soil will not sprout if water submerges it. Just so, in the flood of *prema* — the bottomless waters of the ocean of service to Bhagavān — the seeds of the desire to enjoy anything other than service to Kṛṣṇa were drowned and destroyed, and no sprouts of desire could arise from them any longer. Śrī Caitanyacandra descended in the form of the Pañca-tattva, and the purpose of His descent was fulfilled; seeing this, all were filled with joy. Śrīmat Prabodhānanda Sarasvatī, the *tridaṇḍipāda*, described this in his book *Śrī Caitanya-candrāmṛta*: “When Caitanyacandra revealed the supreme path of *bhakti-yoga*, the worldly gave up talk of wives, sons and the like; the learned gave up disputes over the *śāstras*; the kings of *yogīs* gave up the hardship of restraining the breath; ascetics gave up their austerities; and *sannyāsīs* gave up the practice of knowledge. No other *rasa* remained.”

## 33

> Subheading: To deliver the fallen *jīvas* deprived of *prema*, He accepts *sannyāsa* —

One who mistakenly believes that “*māyā*” is present in Bhagavān's being, in His abode, in devotion to Him and in His devotee — though all of these are beyond *māyā* — is a “Māyāvādī.” One who mistakenly believes that these four truths are subject to karma and to enjoying its fruits is “devoted to karma” (*karma-niṣṭha*). People who mistakenly think that in these four truths there is room for argument born of ignorance are “sophists.” One who mistakenly thinks that these four truths deserve censure is a “blasphemer.” One who mistakenly thinks that other things of *māyā* are equal to these four truths is a “*pāṣaṇḍī*.” And those fond of study who mistakenly think that these four truths are on a par with other objects of material enjoyment are “lowly students.” All of them fled, driven by one aim: that the waters of the flood of *prema* given by Gaurasundara, who is full of love, should in no way touch them. Seeing this, Śrī Mahāprabhu wished to accept the ornament of the fourth *āśrama*. It is highly revered by those people of material nature already mentioned, who turn away from love for Kṛṣṇa and long for the four goals of life (*catur-varga*). He reasoned that in the eyes of these worldly people, bewitched by *māyā*, the fourth *āśrama* is the desirable ideal.

## 34

There are four kinds of *āśramas*: *brahmacārī*, *gṛhastha*, *vānaprastha* and *yati*. Each *āśrama* in turn has four divisions. The *Bhāgavatam* says: “*Sāvitrya*, *prājāpatya*, *brāhma* and *bṛhat* [for the *brahmacārīs*]; *vārtā*, *sañcaya*, *śālīna* and *śiloñcha* for the householder; the *vaikhānasas*, *vālakhilyas*, *audumbaras* and *phenapas* in the forest; and for the *sannyāsīs*, first the *kuṭīcaka*, then the *bahūdaka*, the *haṁsa* and the *niṣkriya*.” (*Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam* 3.12.42–43) That is, *brahmacarya* is of four kinds: (1) *sāvitrya*, a *brahmacarya* of three nights, from the *upanayana* until the study of the Gāyatrī; (2) *prājāpatya*, a *brahmacarya* in which one keeps vows for a year after the *upanayana*; (3) *brāhma*, a *brahmacarya* from the *upanayana* until the three Vedas have been learned; (4) *bṛhat*, a *brahmacarya* from the *upanayana* until death. The first three are known as “*upakurvāṇa*” and the last as “*naiṣṭhika*.” Householders are of four kinds: (1) *vārtā*, who lives by agriculture and other occupations that are not forbidden; (2) *sañcaya*, who lives by performing sacrifices for others and the like; (3) *śālīna*, who lives on what comes unasked; (4) *śiloñchana*, who lives on grains gleaned from the ground. *Vānaprasthas* are of four kinds: (1) the *vaikhānasa*, who lives on what grows without plowing; (2) the *vālikhilya*, who, on obtaining new grain, gives up what he had stored before; (3) the *auḍumbara*, who, on rising from bed, accepts food brought from whatever direction he first looks; (4) the *phenapa*, who sustains life on fruits that have fallen by themselves. *Sannyāsīs* are of four kinds: (1) the *kuṭīcaka*, chiefly devoted to the duties of his own *āśrama*; (2) the *bahūdaka*, who has given up karma and is chiefly devoted to cultivating knowledge; (3) the *haṁsa*, fixed in the cultivation of knowledge; (4) the *niṣkriya*, a *paramahaṁsa*, or one who has realized the truth.

*Sannyāsa* is of two kinds, “*dhīra*” and “*narottama*”: “One who, detached and freed from bonds, gives up this body, which no longer serves any purpose, without anyone knowing where he has gone, is called ‘*dhīra*.’ And one who, self-possessed, has become disgusted with the world by himself or through another's instruction and, placing Hari in his heart, leaves home, is ‘*narottama*.’” (*Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam* 1.13.26–27) Śrī Mahāprabhu accepted *sannyāsa* in the bright fortnight of the month of Māgha in the Śaka year 1432 from Śrī Keśava Bhāratī, a *daṇḍi-svāmī* of the *sampradāya* of Śrī Śaṅkara, who lived in Kāṭoyā. These *sannyāsīs* are under the Śṛṅgerī *maṭha* in the south.

## 39

> Subheading: All people are delivered except the Māyāvādīs of Kāśī —

“The Māyāvādīs of Kāśī.” People bewildered by knowledge born of the senses say that the world they see with their senses is “created by *māyā*,” since it can be measured by sensory knowledge. There are people skilled in this kind of reasoning: “The Truth (*tattva-vastu*) is beyond *māyā*, yet in it there is no eternal spiritual variety and no spiritual pastime; it is pure consciousness alone (*cin-mātra*).” They are the “Māyāvādīs of Kāśī.” The “Māyāvādīs of Sārnāth,” or the “Māyāvādīs of Bodh Gayā,” do not accept the *māyā* of Brahman: in their reasoning, the doctrine that there is nothing but non-spirit (*acin-mātra-vāda*) is established. The “Māyāvādīs of Kāśī” and the Māyāvādīs of other places are all *prakṛti-vādīs*; none of them is a “*brahma-vādī* or *tattva-vādī*.” The Māyāvādīs of Kāśī call themselves “*brahma-vādīs*” with their lips, but they do not accept the distinction between Brahman and *prakṛti*: following their doctrine of merging (*samanvaya-vāda*), they hold Brahman and *māyā* to be one and the same. The Māyāvādīs know nothing of *bhakti* and Yogamāyā, and that is why they are nondevotees, averse to devotion to Kṛṣṇa. In their hearts the Māyāvādīs are convinced that everything said about eternal *bhakti*, the One who is worshipped and the devotee — all of it is subject to their sense-born knowledge. But in fact, judged by the real truth, such words are worth nothing. However many sophistries and disputes the Māyāvādīs put forward among themselves, they never approach the real truth, and so the Truth and His spiritual variety are not subject to their imaginary reasonings.

## 41

> Subheading: The Māyāvādīs blaspheme the Lord —

The *smṛti* enjoins: “The *sannyāsī* should give up the *tauryatrika* — singing, dancing and playing instruments — and should always study Vedānta.” The *sannyāsīs* of Kāśī saw that Śrī Mahāprabhu did not listen to Śaṅkara's Māyāvāda but, on the contrary, was absorbed in singing of Kṛṣṇa and so on, and danced overwhelmed with love. Measuring Him by this injunction, they concluded that He did not know the *dharma* of *sannyāsa*. Śaṅkara says: “Fortunate indeed are those who wear only a loincloth and always delight in the words of Vedānta.”[^41-1] Not seeing this mark in Śrī Mahāprabhu, the Māyāvādī *sannyāsīs* and the home-bound householders (*gṛhavratas*) blasphemed Him. On the deliverance of the Māyāvādī *sannyāsīs* of Kāśī, see especially Madhya 25.5–169.

## 44

> Subheading: The Lord disregards them and goes to Mathurā —

## 45

> Subheading: The Lord stays in Candraśekhara's house —

In the *Śrī Caitanya-candrodaya-nāṭaka* Śrī Candraśekhara is called a “*śaukra-vaidya*” (a Vaidya by birth). In those days the Vaidyas by birth, and every *varṇa* other than the brāhmaṇas by birth, were called “*śūdras*.” Later, in the present century, the Kāyasthas, resorting to the purificatory rite for the *vrātyas* (those who have lost the right to initiation), took and are taking the *saṁskāras* of *kṣatriyas*, and the Vaidyas those of *vaiśyas*. In the families of the Śrī Gauḍīya Vaiṣṇava ācāryas who follow the Vaiṣṇava faith — in the line of Ṭhākura Raghunandana, in the line of Ṭhākura Kṛṣṇadāsa and Navanī Hoḍa, and in the line of Śrī Rasikānanda-deva, the disciple of Śyāmānanda Prabhu — the *upanayana-saṁskāra*, the ideal of brahminhood, has continued for three or four centuries now. To this day they act as *dīkṣā-gurus* for all *varṇas*, brāhmaṇas included, and worship *śālagrāmas* and other Deities.

> Subheading: The Lord's humble words —

Among the *daṇḍīs* of the ten names, whose order Śrī Śaṅkarācārya founded, three *sampradāyas* — “Tīrtha,” “Āśrama” and “Sarasvatī” — rank above the *sannyāsīs* of the other *sampradāyas* in good conduct and in honor. Śrī Mahāprabhu had accepted *sannyāsa* in the “Bhāratī” *sampradāya*, and so He regarded Prakāśānanda Sarasvatī as belonging to a higher *sampradāya*. Or else: the *brahma-sannyāsīs* themselves consider their social standing high, and so Śrī Mahāprabhu, wishing to show the *dharma* of a Vaiṣṇava *sannyāsī* — to seek no honor for oneself (*amānī*) and to give honor to others (*mānada*) — spoke of Himself as belonging to a lower *sampradāya*. Even today the *sannyāsīs* of Śaṅkara's *sampradāya* are unwilling to call other *sannyāsīs* “*sannyāsīs*”: they give them only the title “*brahmacārī*” and fancy themselves “gurus.”

## 71

> Subheading: The Lord describes the glory of the holy name —

Śrī Caitanya-deva is the Form of the real Reality, the one thing Vedānta establishes. Determining who is qualified to study Vedānta, He proclaimed that the right to the path of *śruti* belongs to those who are “humbler than a blade of grass,” tolerant like a tree, who seek no honor for themselves and give honor to others.[^71-1] The guru chants the words of *śruti* with his holy mouth, and the listener chants them after him; only this *kīrtana* — the *abhidheya*, that is, the *sādhana* — brings forth the fruit, the *prayojana*. That part of the words of *śruti* in which realized knowledge (*vijñāna*) of the real Reality, the object of worship, is glorified is the all-embracing whole (*aṁśī*), the root and source of the *śruti-śāstras*. Within that whole lie both the perception and the non-perception of all its parts. The devotee who serves the Reality that is worshipped is, by his own worship, connected with It. But where the inclination to serve grows slack, the Reality is grasped only in part instead of as the whole. As the inclination to serve slackens, the potency that rests on the worshipped Reality as its shelter becomes separated from It; this is forgetfulness of one's own true nature, or aversion to serving Hari.

Śrī Caitanya-deva, in teaching the devotee's pure conduct, independent of the world, showed the highest ideal of where Vedānta ultimately leads. And the best example of it is what the Lord of the fourteen worlds, free of all pride, says here: He speaks as a disciple. People intoxicated by the brilliance of their learning are not fit to serve the lotus feet of the guru — nor fit to study the *Brahma-sūtra*. The nondevotee absorbed in a “second” (in something other than the Lord), while trying to serve the Reality that is worshipped, abandons service to the guru who himself worships It. To prevent any error in understanding the servant's own true nature in such a case, Bhagavān in the form of the guru, describing the pure nature of the disciple, speaks of his “foolishness.” Śrī Gurudeva, for the disciple's good, says in simple words that the disciple lacks the qualification. In doing so he ascribes no relative fault to the disciple. The disciple's “foolishness” lies in not knowing the truth about Bhagavān. The modesty that befits a “fool” is eternally present in the disciple. We, however, not knowing our own true nature, often hypocritically pose as disciples and deceive those who are really our disciples by calling them “guru” with our lips; this brings us no good. All the Vedas constantly serve the feet of the Puruṣa who is known through Vedānta; one who judges Him by knowledge born of the senses is a “fool” indeed. Whatever a person bewildered by sensory knowledge takes to be the real foundation of the Vedic *śāstras* is guided by external intelligence and belongs to the lower knowledge (*aparā vidyā*). Until the *jīva* loses the conceit, born of the *guṇas*, that the world is subject to him, all his limited, undesirable, changing knowledge born of the senses is that same “foolishness.”

One who is qualified for Vedānta is a servant of the Viṣṇu-*vastu* alone, the Great One and the Maintainer. Unless one goes beyond serving limited things and the like, one cannot become a knower of Brahman. Being qualified to read and teach the *Brahma-sūtra* on the level of karma or on the level of knowledge does not make one qualified for the name of Kṛṣṇa — the eternal, pure, complete, free Form of the *rasa* of consciousness, the transcendental *cintāmaṇi*. And one who is qualified for the name need not again acquire a qualification for Vedānta through knowledge born of the senses.

People who are not qualified to serve the name do not understand that the name and the One who bears the name are nondifferent, and they try to become Māyāvādī Vedāntists. By transcendental judgment they are, in the words of Śrī Gurudeva, the greatest “fools.” Whoever studies Vedānta by relying on the ascending method (*adhiroha-vāda*) is overtaken by “foolishness,” or dullness. But the one who is truly qualified for the name abides eternally on the far shore of Vedānta. In this connection one should reflect on the *Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam* verse “Oh, how great is even a dog-eater on the tip of whose tongue Your name abides! Those who chant Your name have already performed austerities and fire sacrifices, bathed at the holy places; they are true Āryans and have studied the Vedas,”[^71-2] and others like it, and also on the verse “One who has uttered the two syllables ‘Ha-ri’ has studied the *Ṛg-veda*, the *Yajur-veda*, the *Sāma-veda* and the *Atharva-veda*,” and others.

The foolish sect of the *sāhajikas*, fancying themselves Vaiṣṇavas while being Vaiṣṇavas in name only, consider Vedānta the playground of the *ahaṅgrahopāsakas* — the *kevalādvaita-vādīs* who worship themselves as God. But “Vedānta” is the playground only of Hari's servants from Vaikuṇṭha. The Vaiṣṇava ācāryas of the four *sampradāyas*, following *Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam* — the commentary on the *Brahma-sūtra*, Vedānta — composed works on Vedānta, and these are not to be shunned as bad association. This simple point the *prākṛta-sahajiyās* cannot grasp. So they imagine the genuine pure Vaiṣṇavas and Vaiṣṇava ācāryas to be *viddha-bhaktas*, whose devotion is mixed with knowledge and karma; thus they head for hell and themselves become Māyāvādīs devoid of service to Viṣṇu. If one seeks the qualification for Vedānta through knowledge born of the senses, the meaning of chanting the Kṛṣṇa mantra cannot be realized. Those enchanted by sensory knowledge are bound in *saṁsāra* on every side. The two *tattvas* — the enjoyer and the enjoyed — have the power to hold them in *saṁsāra* and do not let them turn the mind away from external objects.

## 73

> Subheading: The mantra and the *mahā-mantra*, the holy name: a variety of pastimes —

When the *jīva* attains divine knowledge (*divya-jñāna*), he is freed from absorption in the “second” and takes up the service of Adhokṣaja. Service to Mukunda is the only means (*upāya*) of ending one's endeavors in the external world, and it is also the goal (*upeya*). Chanting the mantra, the devotee gains transcendental experience step by step and ceases to perceive the external world as an object of enjoyment. Under the shelter of one of the five kinds of *rati*, when all the ingredients of *rasa* (*sāmagrī*) come together, by the power of serving *rasa* he tastes the One he worships in a heart made bright by *viśuddha-sattva*. Such practice is not mere enjoyment for the two *upādhis*. When the chanter of the name becomes truly established, in his very practice, in the divine knowledge that the name and the One who bears the name are nondifferent, he attains direct service to Kṛṣṇa. Then words in the dative case — the grammarians' language that defines relations — lose their hold on him. The real Reality addressed by the vocative word is at once captured in the heart made bright by *sattva*. And then he becomes able to serve Him without hindrance, addressing Him with vocative words. All the *śāstras* and all mantras consisting of divine knowledge free the *jīva* completely and engage him directly in the service of Kṛṣṇa.

All this I, a fool, have heard from Śrī Gurudeva. Śrī Vyāsa says: “For the sake of people who do not know [this], the learned sage compiled the *Sātvata-saṁhitā*.”[^73-1] *Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam* and similar books are the rungs of the ladder that leads to serving the name. Gurudeva taught that studying them, teaching them and discussing them all culminate in the service of the name. The name and the One who bears the name are one nondifferent Reality, knowable only to those free of the endeavors of *māyā*: this is the divine knowledge received at the guru's lotus feet. Until I took shelter at Śrī Guru's lotus feet I was a fool in my understanding of *sambandha*; but now that I have turned toward service, I see in myself the endeavors of one who knows what bondage and liberation are. The word “Kṛṣṇa's name” here does not mean *nāmābhāsa* or *nāmāparādha*.

## 74

> Subheading: In Kali-yuga the name of Kṛṣṇa alone is to be worshipped —

In the three ages — Satya, Tretā and Dvāpara — the path of *śruti* was honored; with the coming of Kali-yuga there arose a path alien to *śruti*, the path of argument. It is born when people doubt the descent of the real truth and when knowledge born of the senses prevails; this path opposes *śruti*. Kṛṣṇa's name is a Reality of Vaikuṇṭha, and so it is completely nondifferent from Kṛṣṇa, the real Reality. Because the name and the One who bears the name are nondifferent, the name of Vaikuṇṭha is just what Kṛṣṇa, the real Reality, is: the eternal, pure, complete, free Form of the *rasa* of consciousness and the transcendental *cintāmaṇi*. The name is distinct from the material names of everything other than Kṛṣṇa, and is Itself a Reality of Vaikuṇṭha. Followers of the path of argument have no access to this name. Only the worship of the name removes both *dharmas* of the *upādhis*, the gross and the subtle. That is why, in days when the path of argument prevails, all other limited (*kuṇṭha*) *dharmas* are hampered by it. Only the name Itself is the One who bears the name, the Reality beyond the arguments of the disputers. Only the name of the Reality of Vaikuṇṭha can deliver (*trāṇa*) the *jīva* from a mind (*manana*) intent on thoughts of material enjoyment, and therefore it is the essence of all mantras. The name, form, qualities, nature and actions of material things are subject to the path of argument; the Reality of Vaikuṇṭha is not. The transcendental name, form, qualities, distinctive associates and pastimes of this name of Vaikuṇṭha abide in nondual knowledge (*advaya-jñāna*). The Māyāvādīs, relying on knowledge born of the senses, posit a difference between the Reality's name, form and qualities, and so fall into the baseness of dualistic reasoning. That is why their teacher frees them from material reasoning with great sayings (*mahā-vākyas*) such as “In the beginning, my dear, there was Being alone”[^74-1] and “All this is indeed Brahman.”[^74-2] Without taking shelter of the holy name, while committing offenses against the name, one never gets free from the path of argument, born of the senses and full of enjoyment.

The glory of the mantra is stated in the *Nārada-pañcarātra*: “The three Vedas, the six *vedāṅgas*, the meters, the many gods — all of these, and whatever else is expressed in words, are contained within the eight-syllable mantra. It is the essential meaning of all Vedānta and carries one across the ocean of *saṁsāra*.” In the *Kali-santaraṇa Upaniṣad*: “‘Hare Kṛṣṇa Hare Kṛṣṇa Kṛṣṇa Kṛṣṇa Hare Hare / Hare Rāma Hare Rāma Rāma Rāma Hare Hare’ — these sixteen names destroy the contamination of Kali. No means higher than this is to be found in all the Vedas.” The words that Śrī Madhva cites in his commentary on the *Muṇḍaka Upaniṣad*: “The people of Dvāpara worship Viṣṇu by the *Pañcarātra* alone, but in Kali the Lord Hari is worshipped by the name alone.”

On the “Kṛṣṇa mantra” and the “name of Kṛṣṇa,” Śrī Jīva Prabhu says in the *Bhakti-sandarbha* (section 284): “One may object: mantras consist of the names of Bhagavān; moreover, they are specially adorned with the word *namaḥ* and the like, Śrī Bhagavān and the venerable *ṛṣis* have invested them with special potencies, and they establish a special personal relationship [of the chanter] with Śrī Bhagavān. Yet the names of Śrī Bhagavān alone, depending on nothing else, are able to grant the fruit up to the highest goal of human life. That being so, since mantras do not gain greater power than the name, why is there need for *dīkṣā* and the like? The answer: intrinsically there is no such need. But people are mostly, by their very nature, through their connection with the body and so on, of base character and distracted mind. To curb this, the venerable *ṛṣis* and others have established certain rules here and there on the path of *arcana*.”

You may say: mantras consist of the name of Bhagavān; what distinguishes a mantra is that, together with Bhagavān's name, it is adorned with the word *namaḥ* and the like — that is, it carries the mood of subordination to the name; by Bhagavān's will the *ṛṣis*, Śrī Nārada and others, invested the mantras with special potency; the mantras establish a special relationship between the one who utters the mantra and Śrī Bhagavān; and the names of Bhagavān contained in the mantra, depending on nothing else, are themselves able to grant the fruit up to the highest goal of human life. If so, then, since the mantra does not gain greater power than the name,[^74-3] why does one who chants the name need *dīkṣā* into that mantra? To this Śrī Jīva replies: intrinsically, the one who chants the name has no need of *dīkṣā*. But people mostly have a connection with a body and so on that is by nature bent on enjoyment; their character is base and their minds distracted. To curb that base nature and restlessness of mind, the *ṛṣis*, Śrī Nārada and others, have established certain rules in the mantra here and there on the path of *arcana*.

For the conditioned *jīva* to cease from enjoyment, that is, to be rid of the material ego, perfection in the mantra (*mantra-siddhi*) is necessary. In the word *namaḥ* the syllable “*ma*” means the ego and the syllable “*na*” its cessation; that is, the fruit of *mantra-siddhi* is the *jīva*'s transcendental experience. And Śrī Rūpa Gosvāmī Prabhu in the *Nāmāṣṭaka* invokes the name of Hari with the words “O You, who are worshipped by hosts of the liberated!”[^74-4]

## 83

> Subheading: The nature of Kṛṣṇa's name —

When Śrī Gurudeva sings the name, that holy name enters the ears of the disciple. Following Śrī Gurudeva, the disciple places the holy name he has heard in his heart with faith and worships it by repeating it (*japa*). When the holy name is worshipped, He Himself unfolds His nature as the independent Agent and grants the chanter the right to *kīrtana*. It is then that, by singing the name, he becomes able to make the whole world his disciple. Under the instructing power of the name's *kīrtana*, the world begins to chant the name of Kṛṣṇa. As one goes on chanting, the way of serving the name becomes manifest in him — laughing, weeping, dancing, *kīrtana* and the rest. Some people, out of foolishness, do not know that the sixteen names “Hare Kṛṣṇa” — thirty-two syllables — are the *mahā-mantra*. Regarding it as merely a mantra for *japa*, they artificially obstruct the *kīrtana* of this *mahā-mantra*. Therefore one who has attained *prema* sings the name of Kṛṣṇa and, together with the devotees, performs the full *kīrtana* of Kṛṣṇa's name; through such *kīrtana* the people of the world receive instruction in Kṛṣṇa's name. Along with hearing and chanting the name comes remembrance of the name. Because the name and the One who bears the name are nondifferent, the power of chanting Kṛṣṇa's name awakens the inclination to serve Kṛṣṇa, the Reality — and this is called “*bhāva*.” Those in whom *bhāva* has been born are not bound by the fetters of *avidyā* and are free of *anarthas*. *Rati* has been born in them, and so they taste the *rasa* that arises when its four ingredients (*sāmagrī*) come together. *Bhāva* in its condensed state is “*prema*.”

Kṛṣṇa's name is the *mahā-mantra*. The mantras of the *Pañcarātra* are known by the name “mantra,” while the name of Bhagavān is renowned as the “*mahā-mantra*.”

## 84

> Subheading: The four goals of life and love for Kṛṣṇa —

Love for Kṛṣṇa is the highest of all the goals of the *jīva*. If one compares the fifth goal, *prema*, with the *jīva*'s goals — *dharma*, *artha*, *kāma* and *mokṣa* — the gradation makes it clear that what those who hunger for enjoyment (*bubhukṣus*) and those who hunger for liberation (*mumukṣus*) attain is perishable and worthless. The hunger for enjoyment and the hunger for liberation are rooted in identifying oneself (*asmitā*) with the perishable *upādhis*. Love for Bhagavān is the eternal, unchanging *dharma* of the soul, and therefore all reasoning about the goals of the *catur-varga* — *bhukti* and *mukti* — is worth nothing compared with *prema*.

## 87

> Subheading: The nature of love for Kṛṣṇa —

## 88

Nondevotees devoid of love for Kṛṣṇa make a show of restless senses and, as if mad, laugh, weep, dance, sing and so on; but this is only a sign that misfortune awaits them. One engaged in *bhajana* must avoid in every way any artificial agitation of the body and senses. When the natural function of the soul awakens, *bhāva* and *prema* appear, and in them arise laughing, weeping, singing, dancing and longing (*utkaṇṭhā*). All of these are the genuine expressions of one who is turned toward service. But when a person in whom *prema* has not arisen audaciously claims the high position of a devotee, he brings calamity (*anartha*), that is, turmoil, into the world.

Śrī Jīva Prabhu says in the *Prīti-sandarbha* (section 66): “The function that is love for Bhagavān is not made of *māyā* or the like; it is the bliss of the *svarūpa-śakti*, on which even Śrī Bhagavān Himself depends.” (Ibid., section 69):[^88-1] “Thus the sign of *prīti* is the melting of the heart, and the signs of that are horripilation and the like. It has also been shown that if the melting of the heart, horripilation and the rest somehow arise but there is no purity of intention (*āśaya-śuddhi*), then even so *bhakti* has not fully appeared. Purity of intention means giving up every other aim and aiming at *prīti* alone. Hence its qualifiers: ‘causeless’ and ‘natural.’”

The function that is love for Bhagavān is never made of *māyā*: it is the *svarūpa-śakti*, whose essence is bliss, for even Śrī Bhagavān is subject to that bliss. The sign of such *prīti* is the melting of the heart, and its result is horripilation and the like. But if, despite some melting of the heart or horripilation, there is no purity of intention, one must understand that *bhakti* has not fully appeared. “Purity of intention” means giving up other aims and aiming at *prīti* alone. Hence its qualifiers: “*ahaitukī*” (causeless) and “*svābhāvikī*” (natural).

Those who are not qualified should chant the name of Kṛṣṇa in solitude. In such worship there is no association with others. Those who cannot believe the words of Śrī Gurudeva and pervert their own qualification (*adhikāra*) do not obtain the right to the *saṅkīrtana* of Kṛṣṇa's name. Śrī Gaurasundara upheld the words of the *śruti*: “To those great souls who have supreme devotion to God, and the same devotion to the guru, the whole meaning of what has been said is revealed”[^95-1] — and He began the *saṅkīrtana* of Kṛṣṇa's name. Following His guru (*ānugatya*), He did not disobey his order and never stopped the unbroken *nāma-saṅkīrtana*. The *kīrtana* of such a Kṛṣṇa-nāma-Prabhu, by His own power of will (*icchā-śakti*), moved Śrī Gaurasundara to dance and sing. Śrī Gaurasundara did not chant the holy name condescendingly, as if it were a material object. But those driven by the restlessness of the senses take Kṛṣṇa's name for their plaything: instead of serving the holy name, they try to become its masters and command it. Instead of *bhajana* they reap the fruits of *karma* — they merely aggravate their bile (*pitta*) and ruin their bodily health.

“I am a fool who cannot tell good from harm. I feared that if I went searching for the pure meaning of Vedānta, the false arguments of Māyāvāda spoken by Śrī Śaṅkarācārya would come and destroy My natural inclination to *bhajana*. So, knowing that I have no right to Vedānta as Śaṅkara explains it, I am freed from the *anarthas* of worldly existence simply by chanting the mantra of Kṛṣṇa. I take up the name of Kṛṣṇa, whom the liberated worship, and thereby attain Kṛṣṇa's lotus feet. In the quarrelsome age of Kali there is no *dharma* other than taking the name. I received all these instructions from Śrī Gurudeva, and by taking the name I became almost mad. Later I asked him again and learned that when the *jīva* attains the right to *prema*, an incomparable good comes to him. *Prema* is the highest goal of life (*parama-puruṣārtha*), desirable above all, and the petty hopes of those who crave the fruits of the four goals of life cannot be compared with it. A person in whom *prema* has awakened naturally disregards public shame and chants Kṛṣṇa's glories loudly — laughing, weeping, singing, dancing and so on. I learned that this is what is called ‘the life of a *bhāgavata*.’ I have done nothing artificially, hiding behind deceit. I chant Kṛṣṇa's glories because I firmly believe My Gurudeva's words. The holy name Himself has made Me a singer and a dancer, in defiance of the grave dignity of the *kaupīna*-clad Vedāntists. My own part in this — My own will or initiative — is very small: it is all the mercy of Śrī Nāma-Prabhu.”

The Māyāvādīs call “Vedānta” only the scripture that Śrī Śaṅkarapāda's *Śārīraka-bhāṣya* has in view. In other words, when the followers of Śaṅkara's doctrine say “Vedānta,” they mean the Upaniṣads and the *Brahma-sūtra* as read through their ācārya's commentary, which is built on the doctrine of *kevalādvaita*. The Māyāvādīs are pleased with the impermanent service that is rendered to Kṛṣṇa with a material body and mind. That is, they regard devotion to Kṛṣṇa as merely a special kind of ritual work (*karma*); so for them it too is “non-devotion” (*abhakti*), and with this they are content.

> Subheading: The Lord's view and explanation of Vedānta —

*Sūtra*: “Knowers of *sūtras* know a *sūtra* to be brief, unambiguous, full of essence, facing every side, free of superfluous words, and faultless.” (*Skanda Purāṇa* and *Vāyu Purāṇa*) The *Vedānta-sūtra* is known by various names: (1) *Brahma-sūtra*, (2) *Śārīraka*, (3) *Vyāsa-sūtra*, (4) *Bādarāyaṇa-sūtra*, (5) *Uttara-mīmāṁsā* and (6) *Vedānta-darśana*. It is a book composed of *sūtras*, with four chapters (*adhyāyas*) and sixteen quarters (*pādas*). Each *pāda* contains several topics (*adhikaraṇas*). Each topic contains the five-membered argument (*pañcāvayava-nyāya*): the proposition (*pratijñā*), the reason (*hetu*), the example (*udāharaṇa*), the application (*upanaya*) and the conclusion (*nigamana*). In other words: “One verse [states] the subject and the doubt and sets out the *pūrvapakṣa*; the other verse establishes the *siddhānta*; the connections between them are clear.”

According to the different commentaries, it has from 162 to 223 topics (*adhikaraṇas*) and from 520 to 560 *sūtras*.

On the word “Vedānta” the lexicographer Hemacandra says: “Vedānta is the Upaniṣads together with the Brāhmaṇas — the remainder of the Vedas, their last portion, that is, the end of the Vedas.” A scripture that shows the ultimate purpose of the Vedas is also “Vedānta.” A scripture that uses the Upaniṣads as its evidence, and the *sūtras* and other works that assist it, are also “Vedānta.” The *Vedānta-sūtra* is called the *nyāya-prasthāna*, one of the three *prasthānas* (foundations of doctrine). The Upaniṣads are the *śruti-prasthāna*, and the *Gītā*, the *Mahābhārata*, the Purāṇas and so on are the *smṛti-prasthāna*.

The Vedas came into the material world from the breath of Śrī Nārāyaṇa. The scripture spoken by Śrī Nārāyaṇa that unfolds the Vedas is called the *Sātvata-pañcarātra*. The *Vedānta-sūtra* was compiled by Śrī Vyāsa, the *āveśa-avatāra* of Śrī Nārāyaṇa — or, in the opinion of some (Śaṅkara's commentary 3.3.32),[^106-1] by the sage Apāntaratamā. Śrī Gaurasundara says that the *Pañcarātra* and the Vedānta express one and the same teaching. Since Śrī Vyāsa composed the *sūtras*, they too must be known as the word of Śrī Nārāyaṇa Himself.

While composing the *sūtras*, Śrī Vyāsadeva examined the Vedānta doctrines set forth by seven other sages: Ātreya, Āśmarathya, Auḍulomi, Kārṣṇājini, Kāśakṛtsna, Jaimini and Bādari. Besides these, two more collections — the *sūtras* of Pārāśarī and of Karmandī-bhikṣu — also preceded the *sūtras* of Śrī Vyāsa.

The first two chapters of the *Vedānta-darśana* describe knowledge of *sambandha*; the third chapter, *abhidheya*, the practice of devotion (*sādhana-bhakti*); and the fourth, *prayojana*, the fruit: love for Bhagavān. The genuine commentary on Vedānta, written by Vyāsa, the author of the *sūtras* himself, is the *Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam*. Besides it, the commentaries of the four Vaiṣṇava ācāryas, who follow the *Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam* to a greater or lesser degree, and the many glosses written by their successors in their *sampradāyas*, declare that Vedānta aims at the worship of Bhagavān. This *Vedānta-sūtra* is honored even in the *sampradāya* that lacks devotion to Viṣṇu and holds to the doctrine of the featureless. The commentaries on this Vedānta written from the standpoint of *māyā*, and the glosses, treatises and other works that follow them, are devoid of service to Viṣṇu, and their reasoning departs from the real truth.

The word *mukhya-vṛtti* means *abhidhā-vṛtti*. *Abhidhā* is the power [of a word] by which one understands the meaning known from dictionaries, grammar and so on. The word *gauṇa-vṛtti* means *lakṣaṇā-vṛtti*. *Lakṣaṇā* is the power by which, for some purpose or through frequent usage, one understands another meaning connected with the actual sense.

*Bhāṣya* — as it is said: “Knowers of *bhāṣya* know that a *bhāṣya* takes up the words of the *sūtra*, explains them in sentences that follow the *sūtra*, and also explains its own words.”

Śrī Mahāprabhu relied on *abhidhā-vṛtti* and honored the meaning of Vedānta. But the meaning of Vedānta that Śrī Śaṅkarācārya set down in his commentary, relying on *lakṣaṇā-vṛtti*, ruins everything. Thus in the *Padma Purāṇa*: “Hear, O Devī, I shall tell you in order of the *tāmasa* [scriptures], by merely hearing which even the learned fall. [In Māyāvāda] the words of the *śruti* are given a false meaning, which people condemn; in it, it is taught that action must be given up by its very nature. There, freedom from action (*naiṣkarmya*) is said to be the abandonment of all action. And in it I establish the oneness of the Supreme Soul and the *jīva*.”

The Truth possessed of features is Viṣṇu. Viṣṇu's *prakṛti* is the root of the material, inert world. Between His body and the possessor of that body there is nonduality (*advaya-jñāna*). In material things, the body and its possessor are different. Material things are objects of enjoyment, but Viṣṇu is eternally the enjoyer. To regard the “enjoyer” as the “enjoyed” is an offense. And to present the One whom the *jīva* eternally serves as a servant on a level with the *jīva* is to blaspheme Him.

## 121

> Subheading: The *Brahma-sūtra* accepts only the doctrine of the transformation of potency (*śakti-pariṇāma-vāda*) —

Setting out to describe the nature of the Lord, the *jīva* and *prakṛti* as indescribable and to explain it in terms of ignorance, Śaṅkara concealed the transcendent supremacy of the Lord.

Śrī Rāmānujapāda writes in the *Vedānta-sāra*: “Objection: the statement ‘In the beginning all this was the Ātmā alone’ establishes oneness before creation. How, then, can Nārāyaṇa, qualified by subtle spirit and matter, be the cause? Answer: [it is said:] ‘That from which these beings are born, by which, once born, they live, and into which, departing, they enter.’ What is taught here is that beings, having given up their gross forms and taken on subtle ones, abide in Brahman — not that their own nature ceases. For [in the *śruti*] we hear: ‘The *akṣara* dissolves into darkness (*tamas*); darkness becomes one with the supreme Deity’ — that is, *prakṛti*, called by the word *tamas*, becomes one with the Paramātmā. To become one is to exist without being perceived separately; and that is the meaning of the word *laya* (dissolution): as birds dissolve into a tree, and deer into a forest.”

You may ask: “Before the creation of the world only the Ātmā existed (*Bṛhad-āraṇyaka* 1.4.1); how then can Nārāyaṇa, possessed of subtle spiritual and material potencies, be the root cause of the world?” The answer can be given thus. The statement of the *Taittirīya*, “He from whom these beings were born, by whom they are sustained, and into whom they enter” (*Taittirīya*, *Bhṛgu-vallī* 1), shows that beings give up their gross material forms, are released from them, take on subtle forms and [so] abide in Brahman, but their *svarūpa* is not destroyed. For when the imperishable soul dissolves into *prakṛti*, called by the word *tamas*, *prakṛti* becomes nondifferent (one) with Brahman. At that time *prakṛti* is not perceived separately from Brahman, and so the existence of *prakṛti* abides in Brahman itself. This is what the word *laya* means. An example: just as birds in a tree or animals in a forest dissolve into the tree or the forest, that is, are hidden within it.

Śrī Vedavyāsa, the author of the *Brahma-sūtra*, says: “The *ānandamaya* [is Brahman], because [this] is repeated” (*Brahma-sūtra* 1.1.12). In connection with this *sūtra*, while explaining the *sūtra* “And [scripture] teaches its union with that in it” (*Brahma-sūtra* 1.1.19), Śrī Śaṅkara wrote what may be paraphrased thus: “The statement about the *ānandamaya* lacks the word ‘Brahman,’ so the *ānandamaya* cannot be called the principal Brahman. If the *ānandamaya* were called ‘Brahman,’ then, since it has parts, one would have to speak of a Brahman possessed of features. Yet at the end of the statement about the *ānandamaya* the featureless Brahman is spoken of. If one says that in the word *ānandamaya* the suffix *mayaṭ* means abundance — ‘abounding in bliss’ (the sense in which the *bhāgavatas*, who teach spiritual variety, have used it) — then it follows that suffering also exists in it. For the word ‘abundant’ is used according to predominance, not according to scarcity. The *śruti* does not repeat (*abhyāsa*, repeated mention) the ‘*ānandamaya*’ but repeats ‘*ānanda*’ alone — precisely because the *ānandamaya* is not ‘pure Brahman.’ Had it been established that the *ānandamaya* is Brahman, then perhaps the repetition of ‘*ānanda*’ alone might have been taken as a repetition of the *ānandamaya*. But since the *ānandamaya* is connected with parts, it is certain that it is not Brahman. For all these reasons, and because in *śrutis* such as ‘Brahman is *ānanda*’ the word *ānanda* is used of the Supreme Brahman, it is clear that in other *śrutis* too what is repeated is ‘*ānanda* alone, Brahman,’ not ‘*ānandamaya*.’ True, in the *śruti* ‘*ānandamayam ātmānam*’ we see the *ānandamaya* itself repeated; but it stands in the series with the *annamaya* and the rest, and so the *ānandamaya* cannot denote pure Brahman. Near the statement about the *ānandamaya* there are the words ‘He desired: may I become many’; but the *ānandamaya* has no close connection with pure Brahman, and so it does not denote pure Brahman. The statement that follows, ‘He is *rasa*,’ and the rest depends on that [pure Brahman] and so does not point to the *ānandamaya*. Since there are no words indicating parts, such as ‘joy is His head,’ it is certain that the principal Brahman is ‘*ānanda*,’ not ‘*ānandamaya*.’ You may say: does not that *śruti* intend the Brahman possessed of features? The answer: you cannot say so — that is refuted by the *śrutis* which say He is ‘beyond speech and mind.’ Therefore the suffix *mayaṭ* in the word *ānandamaya* indicates transformation (*vikāra*), not abundance.”

Thus Śrīpāda Śaṅkara, in explaining these *sūtras*, wanted to remove the suffix *mayaṭ*, that is, to show that it is useless or superfluous — and what effort he made to repeat one and the same point again and again in *sūtras* 12–19! On this Śrīmad Jīva Prabhu says in his *Sarva-saṁvādinī*: “If one secretly intends [to show] that the author of the *sūtras* did not know the meaning of Vedānta, and, with an artfulness that betrays one's own cleverness in correcting his mistake, explains that *sūtra* on the *ānandamaya* thus — ‘In [the passage] “*ānandamaya*” and so on, Brahman, principal in its own right, is taught [by the words] “Brahman is the tail, the foundation”’ — and in the *sūtra* on *vikāra* (1.1.13) explains the word *vikāra* as ‘part’ and the word *prācurya* as ‘similarity,’ then it would follow that the author of the *sūtras* did not know the meaning of words, since by those words he would not be expressing those meanings. That the words — the suffix *mayaṭ*, *vikāra* and *prācurya*, stated one after another — have some other sense would not occur even to a child.”

Reading Śaṅkara's commentary, one gets the impression that his hidden aim is, as it were, [to show] that Śrī Vedavyāsa, the author of the *sūtras*, could not understand the meaning of Vedānta. So Śrī Śaṅkara, on the pretext of correcting the mistake of the ācārya Śrī Vedavyāsa, the author of the *sūtras*, used his own cleverness and artfully explained the *sūtra* on the *ānandamaya* thus: “Among the statements of the *śruti* beginning with ‘*ānandamaya*,’ the principal Brahman is ‘taught’ in the statement ‘Brahman is the tail, the foundation’; and in *sūtra* 1.1.13 I shall explain the word *vikāra* as ‘part’ and the word *prācurya* as ‘similarity.’” Explained this way, it follows that the author of the *sūtras* (Vyāsa) had no knowledge of words, since the words he used do not yield those meanings of Vedānta. What other meaning could the words *vikāra*, *prācurya* and the rest, derived from the suffix *mayaṭ* and stated one after another, possibly have? Even a child can see this! In other words, it is easy to understand that to give the suffix *mayaṭ* any meaning other than “transformation” and “abundance” is a plain error.

See Madhya 6.170–175 and Madhya 25.40–41.

## 122–126

Śrī Jīva Prabhu writes in the *Paramātma-sandarbha* (section 58): “For in that doctrine the view is that all duality, beginning with the *jīvas*, is imagined through ignorance alone in Brahman, which is one's own nature. But Brahman — pure consciousness, without ego, free of any other attribute, distinct from everything — can be neither the shelter of ignorance, nor its object, nor the cause of error. Yet inconceivable potency is possible in Him, since He is a supreme and transcendent Reality. This is seen even in the *cintāmaṇi* and the like. [Brahman's potency], like a medicine that cures disorders of all three humors, holds even mutually contradictory qualities in Him; by it, although He is without parts and so on, He is acknowledged to have parts and so on. For this, the word [of scripture] is the evidence. It is: ‘The ancient Puruṣa has wondrous potencies, and others have no such potencies,’ and so on, in the *Śvetāśvatara Upaniṣad* and elsewhere; ‘the Self, the Lord, possessed of thousands of potencies beyond reasoning’ (*Bhāgavatam* 3.33.3), and so on, in the *Śrī Bhāgavatam* and elsewhere. So too the *Brahma-sūtra* (2.1.28): ‘For in the Self too it is so, since [in Him] there is variety.’ Therefore, even on the plea that duality cannot otherwise be explained, ignorance and the like cannot be imagined in Brahman — simply because it is impossible. And since inconceivable potency in Brahman is grasped by reason and heard in the *śruti*, the plea ‘duality cannot otherwise be explained’ falls away. In the end, then, inconceivable potency alone remains as what explains duality. Therefore, although the Paramātmā is by nature changeless and so on, by His inconceivable potency transformation and the like take place — into the shape of the universe and so on — just as the *cintāmaṇi* yields every desired object and the magnet moves iron. Śrī Bādarāyaṇa accepted this: ‘But on the basis of *śruti*, for [this] rests on the word’ (*Brahma-sūtra* 2.1.27). Hence, since He has such potency, it is also wrong to take the word *māyā*, as in worldly usage, to mean the art of sorcery (*indra-jāla*). It means the potency that creates wondrous variety: ‘that by which the manifold is measured out, that is, fashioned.’ Therefore the conclusion of scripture is the transformation of the Paramātmā Himself. *** Here He, remaining untransformed, is transformed by inconceivable potency; and it is understood that He is transformed only in the form of the potency called *dravya* (substance) — an expansion (*vyūha*) of His *svarūpa*, which appears as pure being alone — and not in His *svarūpa*. Just like the *cintāmaṇi*. *** That is why we sometimes hear that the material cause [of the world] is Brahman, and sometimes that it is *pradhāna*. *** Earlier, when one saw water, a mental image in the form of water arose. While there is no occasion for it, it lies asleep, but it awakens on seeing something similar; and then, without examining the difference, [the mind] arbitrarily imposes [water] on it, taking them as one. So it is not the water that is false, nor the image in the form of water made of its memory, nor the similar thing such as a mirage: only the imposition that identifies them is false, since it does not accord with reality. And in a dream — by the rule ‘But [the dream] is mere *māyā*, since its nature is not fully manifest’ (*Brahma-sūtra* 3.2.3) — the Paramātmā's *māyā* imposes the separate things on a state of mind shaped like things seen in waking, just as before. So in reality nothing anywhere is false. Only such an imposition of that [world] on the pure self or on the Paramātmā is false — not that the universe is false. ** Moreover, *vivarta* is mentioned in the sections on knowledge and the like, and so is secondary; whereas *pariṇāma* is mentioned in its own section and so is primary; and since it is mentioned in both — in the sections on knowledge and the like as well — its strength is established by the maxim of the tongs (*sandaṁśa-nyāya*). Therefore it is understood that the purport of the *Śrī Bhāgavatam* is *pariṇāma* itself.”

In the *Paramātma-sandarbha* [it is said]: relying on *vivarta*, that is, on the doctrine of falsity, they claim that all *tattvas* that have a second existence — the *jīva* and the rest — are imagined through ignorance in Brahman's own *svarūpa*. But Brahman is devoid of any other attribute, distinct from everything, free of ego, pure consciousness; so He can never be the shelter of ignorance, nor that toward which ignorance is directed, nor the cause of error. Brahman is a supreme and transcendent Reality, and so He can have a potency inconceivable to puny human beings. If transcendent power is seen even in material things such as the *cintāmaṇi*, then such power certainly exists in Brahman. When the three humors — *vāta*, *kapha* and *pitta* — afflict a patient all at once, a medicine is prescribed to correct the mutually opposed elements of the body. In the same way the potency that holds three mutually opposed qualities [allows us to accept] that Brahman, though formless and so on, possesses limbs and so on. There is Vedic evidence for this: “The eternal Puruṣa possesses wondrous potencies; others have no such potencies” — so says the *Śvetāśvatara*. And the *Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam* and other works say: “The Self is the Lord, possessed of thousands of inconceivable potencies.” And the *Brahma-sūtra*: “In the Self there is such variety.” Even though duality may find no explanation in Brahman, ignorance and the like still cannot be imagined in Brahman, since that is impossible. “Inconceivable potency in Brahman agrees with reason and is confirmed by the *śruti*, so the plea ‘duality cannot otherwise be explained’ has fallen away; what remains to explain duality is inconceivable potency alone. Therefore, although the Paramātmā is changeless by nature, by the power of His inconceivable potency transformation and the like take place into the shape of the universe. The *cintāmaṇi* does not change, yet it can yield every desired object; the magnet does not change, yet it can attract and move iron and so on. In the same way Brahman does not change: only Brahman's potency, which is capable of change, changes and is transformed into the shape of the universe. Śrī Bādarāyaṇa accepted this in the *Brahma-sūtra*. And since Brahman has such potency, it is wrong to take the word *māyā*, as in worldly usage, to mean the art of sorcery. This *māyā* creates variety, and so it is established that the word means the potency that produces wondrous variety. Therefore the conclusion of scripture is that this universe is the transformation of the Paramātmā Himself. *** Here what is transformed is the untransformed real Entity — by the power of inconceivable potency. So what is transformed is only the potency called *dravya*, an unfolding of the *svarūpa* of that Entity, which appears as pure being alone; the *svarūpa* itself is not transformed. So the *cintāmaṇi* exerts its power, yet itself undergoes no change at all. *** That is why, as we hear, some say that the material cause of this world is ‘Brahman,’ while others say it is ‘*pradhāna*.’ *** Earlier, when one saw water, a notion of it arose. While there is no occasion for it, that notion sleeps, but on seeing something similar it awakens. If, without examining that thing, one arbitrarily imposes on it identity with the earlier thing, then it is not the water that becomes false, nor the image in the form of water made of its memory, nor the water-like thing such as a mirage — only the imposition of identity with water is untrue, that is, false. And in a dream too, by the rule ‘[the dream] is mere *māyā*; its nature is not fully manifest,’ the Paramātmā's *māyā*, just as before, imposes identity with things on a state of mind shaped like things perceived (seen) in waking. Therefore in reality nothing is false. Only such an imposition on the pure self or on the Paramātmā is false; the universe is not false. *** Further, the examples of *vivarta* are mentioned among the sections on knowledge and the like, and so are secondary; *pariṇāma-vāda* is set forth in its own section, and so is primary; and since it is set forth in both — in the sections on knowledge and the like as well — its strength is established by the maxim of the tongs. Therefore it is known that the purport of the *Śrī Bhāgavatam* is the transformation of potency (*śakti-pariṇāma*).”

## 127

> Subheading: (3) Even when His potency is transformed, He Himself remains unchanged —

## 128

> Subheading: The *praṇava*, seed of the tree of the Vedas, is the great statement (*mahā-vākya*) and the very form of the Lord —

In the *Gītā*: “Whoever departs, leaving the body, uttering the single syllable *Om* — Brahman — and remembering Me, attains the supreme goal” (8.13); “[I am] what is to be known, the purifier, the syllable *Om*” (9.17); “*Om tat sat* — this is remembered as the threefold designation of Brahman” (17.23). In the *Chāndogya Upaniṣad* (1.1.1, 1.4.1): “One should worship the syllable *Om* as the *udgītha*, for one sings the *udgītha* beginning with *Om*. Here is its explanation”; (*Chāndogya* 1.5.1): “What is the *udgītha* is the *praṇava*; what is the *praṇava* is the *udgītha*”; (*Atharva-śikhā* 2): “It makes all the life-breaths bow (*praṇāvayati*) to the Paramātmā; therefore it is the *praṇava*. The *praṇava* abides in four forms; it is the source of the Vedas and of the gods, the object of meditation; it holds all together and carries one across all sorrows and fears, because it delivers; [pervading] all, it is Viṣṇu; it conquers all”;[^128-1] (*Māṇḍūkya* 1): “The syllable *Om* is all this. Here is its explanation: past, present and future — all this is *Om* alone; and whatever else lies beyond the three times is also *Om* alone”; (*Taittirīya*, *Śikṣā*, ch. 8): “*Om* is Brahman. *Om* is all this. With *Om* they sing the Sāmans. With *Om* a *brāhmaṇa*, about to recite [the Veda], says, ‘May I attain Brahman’ — and he attains Brahman.”

In the *Śrī Bhagavat-sandarbha* (section 48): “The *śruti* says of the *praṇava*: ‘*Om* is the nearest name of Brahman: as soon as it is uttered, it carries one across the fear of worldly existence; therefore it is called *tāra*, the deliverer.’ *** Therefore the name is the very *svarūpa* of Bhagavān. It is also clearly said in the *Śrī Nārada-pañcarātra* about the eight-syllable [mantra]: ‘Clearly, Bhagavān Himself — Nārāyaṇa directly — abides in [people's] mouths in the form of the eight syllables.’ And in the *Māṇḍūkya Upaniṣad* (4.4–7) about the *praṇava*: ‘*Om* is all this. The syllable *Om* is all this.’ ‘The *praṇava* is indeed the Supreme Brahman, and the *praṇava* is held to be the Supreme. The *praṇava* has nothing before it, nothing within, nothing without, nothing after; it is imperishable. The *praṇava* is the beginning, the middle and the end of all. Knowing the *praṇava* thus, one attains it at once. Know the *praṇava* to be the Lord, abiding in the heart of everyone. One who understands *Oṁkāra* as all-pervading is wise and does not grieve. It is without measure and of infinite measure; in it duality comes to rest; it is auspicious. One who has known *Oṁkāra* is a sage, not an ordinary person.’ One should not think that, since such things are possible only for the Supreme Lord, the words about a mere letter are only praise. For the same *śruti* accepts the sense that, like the other *avatāras*, this is an *avatāra* of the Supreme Lord Himself in the form of sound; and this is possible because the sound is nondifferent from Him. Therefore the name and the Bearer of the name are nondifferent.”

The *Śrī Bhagavat-sandarbha* [says]: the *śruti* says of the *praṇava* that *Om* is the nearest (the sweetest) of all the names of the Supreme Brahman: from the moment one begins to utter it, it saves the *jīva* from the fear of worldly existence; therefore He is also called “Tāra.” [Śrīdhara Svāmipāda, at the beginning of his own commentary on the *Bhāgavatam*, called the *Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam* *tārāṅkura*, “the sprout of Tāra,” because it begins with *Om*.] So the holy name is directly the *svarūpa* of Bhagavān. About the eight-syllable mantra the *Śrī Nārada-pañcarātra* says clearly: “It is well known that Bhagavān Śrī Nārāyaṇa Himself directly appears in the mouth of the *jīva* in the form of the eight syllables.” And about the *praṇava* the *Māṇḍūkya Upaniṣad* says: “All that is seen by spiritual vision is *Oṁkāra*, the syllable *Om*.” “The *praṇava* is another appearance of Brahman; He is called the Supreme Reality. He has nothing before Him, is unbounded, has nothing outside Him, is supreme and imperishable; He is the beginning, the middle and the end of all. Knowing the *praṇava* thus, one tastes immortality. Know that the *praṇava*, abiding in the heart of everyone, is the *svarūpa* of the Lord. As soon as a wise person understands *Oṁkāra* as all-pervading — *vibhu*, that is, the *svarūpa* of Viṣṇu — he no longer needs to grieve; that is, he is no longer a *śūdra*.[^128-2] He is without material measure, yet possesses infinite measure; through Him the material knowledge of duality subsides and nondual knowledge is attained; therefore He is the supreme good itself.” One should not think that such auspicious works are possible only for the Supreme Lord as an *avatāra*, and that therefore such statements about a material sound or letter contain no real truth and are mere praise. In fact, like the other *avatāras* of the Supreme Lord, this *praṇava* too is His *avatāra* in the form of sound. This sense is accepted on the very strength of the *śruti* statements quoted above, and since the *praṇava* is nondifferent from Him, this is possible; so this sense alone is correct. Therefore there is no doubt that the name of Bhagavān and the Bearer of the name, Bhagavān, are nondifferent.

*Om*, or the *praṇava*, is the *mahā-vākya*, the root of the Vedas. The *praṇava* is placed at the beginning and at the end of every Vedic mantra. The *praṇava* is the very form of the Lord. “By the sound *a* Kṛṣṇa is denoted, the sole Master of all the worlds; by the sound *u* Rādhā is denoted; *m* denotes the *jīva*.”

> Subheading: The Lord's explanation: (1) Bhagavān Kṛṣṇa is the *sambandha* —

## 140

Śrī Rāmānujapāda writes in the *Vedārtha-saṅgraha*: “If it is asked, ‘How is it known that the very nature [of Brahman] is defined by knowledge as an attribute, and not that Brahman is mere knowledge?’ — [the answer is:] this is understood from the *śrutis* that speak of Him as the knower, such as ‘He who is all-knowing, all-wise’; from hundreds of *śrutis* such as ‘His supreme potency is heard to be manifold’ and ‘By what, my dear, should one know the knower?’; and also because knowledge is only an attribute, and a mere attribute without a substrate cannot be accepted as an independent entity. Therefore words such as ‘truth,’ ‘knowledge’ and so on establish Brahman as possessed of knowledge and so on — the very things that are their own meanings. And if in ‘*tat tvam*’ both words abandon their own meanings in order to point to the nature of an entity without features, that is an abandonment of the primary meaning. [It may be said:] ‘Since the purport [of the statement] is established to be oneness, there is no fault of *lakṣaṇā*, as in the words “This is that very Devadatta.”’[^140-1] *** Moreover, a word gains its force as evidence because, by its very nature as words and sentences, it conveys differences of meaning and their particular relations; therefore it is incapable of conveying an entity without features, and for such an entity the word is not evidence. Words such as ‘featureless’ convey [meaning] in another way: of an entity known as possessed of some feature, they deny a feature known from some other thing.”

## 149

> Subheading: They constantly take the name of Kṛṣṇa —

The leader at that time of the *ekadaṇḍī sannyāsīs* of Śaṅkara's *sampradāya* living in Kāśī was Śrī Prakāśānanda Sarasvatī. Some mistakenly try to identify him with Śrī Prabodhānanda Sarasvatī, who lived in Śrī Raṅga-kṣetra and later in Kāmyavana. Needless to say, Prabodhānanda was a *tridaṇḍī* of Rāmānuja's line, a *jīyar svāmī*, who had come from the land of Mysore and lived in Raṅga-kṣetra. He is the author of such books as *Śrī Caitanya-candrāmṛta*, *Rādhā-rasa-sudhā-nidhi*, *Saṅgīta-mādhava*, *Vṛndāvana-śataka* and *Navadvīpa-śataka*. Veṅkaṭa Bhaṭṭa, Tirumalaya Bhaṭṭa and Prabodhānanda were three brothers. Mahāprabhu saw him in Śaka 1433, during the *cāturmāsya*, as a member of Rāmānuja's *sampradāya*; so it is unreasonable to think that in Śaka 1435 He saw him in Kāśī as a member of Śaṅkara's *sampradāya*. See the book *Śrī Bhakti-ratnākara*.
