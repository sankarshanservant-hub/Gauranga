@235
Nārada  says  if  living beings  you strike  you  with an arrow
half-killed  you make  why  not  you take  life
= Nārada said, “If you shoot living beings with your arrows, why do you leave them half-dead instead of taking their lives?”

@236
the hunter  says  listen  O gosāñi  Mṛgāri  my  name
of the father  by the teaching  I  do  such  work
= The hunter replied, “Listen, gosāñi: my name is Mṛgāri, ‘enemy of the beasts.’ I do this work just as my father taught me.

@237
half  killed  creature  when  writhes  does
then  indeed  joy  my  swells  in the heart
= “When a half-killed creature writhes and thrashes about, the joy in my heart swells.”

@238
Nārada  says  one thing  I ask  from you
the hunter  says  deer and others  take  whichever  in your  mind
= Nārada said, “I ask one thing of you.” The hunter said, “Take a deer or any other animal you like.

@239
deerskin  you want  if  come  my  to the house
whatever  you want  that  I will give  among deer and tiger skins
= “If you want a deerskin, come to my house. I will give you whatever you want — a deerskin or a tiger skin.”

@240
Nārada  says  this  I  any  not  want
another  one  gift  I  ask  from you
= Nārada said, “I want none of these. I ask another gift of you:

@241
tomorrow  from  you  whichever  deer and others  you will kill
at once  kill  half-killed  not  make
= “From tomorrow on, whatever animals you kill, kill them outright; do not leave them half-dead.”

@242
the hunter  says  what  gift  you asked  of me
half  killing  what  happens  that  tell  me
= The hunter said, “What a gift to ask of me! What harm is there in half-killing them? Tell me that.”

@243
Nārada  says  half  killing  creature  gets  pain
to creatures  pain  you are giving  yours  will be  such  plight
= Nārada said, “A half-killed creature suffers pain. You are giving pain to living beings, and the same plight will be yours.

@244
hunter  you  creatures  you kill  small  offense  your
torment  giving  you kill  this  sin  boundless
= “You are a hunter and you kill animals — that is a small offense. But to kill them by torment — that sin is boundless.

@245
tormenting  you  as many  you killed  creatures
they  likewise  you  will kill  birth after birth
= “All the creatures you have tormented to death will kill you in the same way, birth after birth.”

@246
in the company of Nārada  of the hunter  mind  pure  became
his  words  hearing  in the mind  fear  arose
= In Nārada’s company the hunter’s mind became clear, and on hearing his words he grew afraid.

@247
the hunter  says  childhood  from  this  my  work
how  will cross over  I  sinful  fallen
= The hunter said, “This has been my work since childhood. How can I, a sinful and fallen wretch, be delivered?

@248
this  sin  goes  my  what  by means
deliverance  do  to me  I fall  your  at the feet
= “By what means will this sin of mine be removed? Deliver me — I fall at your feet.”

@249
Nārada  says  if  you hold  my  word
then  only  to do  I can  your  release
= Nārada said, “If you accept my word, then I can set you free.”

@250
the hunter  says  whatever  you say  that  indeed  I will do
Nārada  says  bow  break  then  only  I will tell
= The hunter said, “Whatever you say, I will do.” Nārada said, “Break your bow; then I will tell you.”

@251
the hunter  says  bow  breaking  I will live  how
Nārada  says  I  food  will give  every day
= The hunter said, “If I break my bow, how will I live?” Nārada said, “I will give you food every day.”

@252
bow  breaking  the hunter  his  at the feet  fell
him  raising  Nārada  instruction  gave
= The hunter broke his bow and fell at Nārada’s feet. Nārada raised him up and instructed him:

@253
home  going  to the brāhmaṇas  give  whatever  there is  wealth
one  one  cloth  wearing  outside  go  two  persons
= “Go home and give the brāhmaṇas whatever wealth you have. Then leave home, both of you, each wearing a single cloth.

@254
of a river  on the bank  one  hut  making
it  in front of  on a raised mound  tulasī  planting
= “Build a small hut on the bank of a river, and in front of it plant a tulasī on a raised mound.

@255
tulasī  circumambulation  do  tulasī  service
constantly  Kṛṣṇa’s  name  do  chanting
= “Walk around the tulasī and serve her, and chant the name of Kṛṣṇa without cease.

@256
I  to you  much  food  will send  daily
that  food  take  as much as  you eat  two persons
= “I will send you plenty of food every day. Take of it only as much as the two of you eat.”

@257
then  those  deer and others  three  Nārada  healthy  made
healthy  becoming  deer and others  three  running  fled
= Then Nārada healed the three wounded animals, and once healed they ran off and were gone.

@258
seeing  of the hunter  in the mind  arose  wonder
home  went  the hunter  to the guru  making  obeisance
= Seeing this, the hunter was astonished. He bowed to his guru and went home.

@259
to his destination  Nārada  went  the hunter  home  came
of Nārada  by the instruction  everything  did
= Nārada went on his way, and the hunter went home and did everything Nārada had told him.

@260
in the village  word  went  the hunter  a Vaiṣṇava  became
of the village  people  all  food  to bring  began
= Word spread through the village that the hunter had become a Vaiṣṇava, and all the villagers began to bring him food.

@261
in one day  food  bring  ten or twenty  persons
daily  that much  he takes  as much as  eat  two  persons
= In a single day ten or twenty people would bring food, but each day he took only as much as the two of them would eat.

@262
one day  Nārada  says  listen  to Parvata
my  one  disciple  there is  let us go  to see
= One day Nārada said to Parvata, “Listen, I have a disciple. Let us go and see him.”

@263
then  two  sages  came  that  to the hunter’s place
far  from  the hunter  obtained  of the guru  sight
= So the two sages came to the hunter’s place, and the hunter caught sight of his guru from afar.

@264
hurriedly  running  he comes  path  not  finds
of the path  ants  here and there  touch  feet
= He came running in haste, yet he could find no place to step, for here and there the ants on the path were at his feet.

@265
for prostration  at the place  the ants  seeing
with the cloth  the place  sweeping  he falls  a rod  becoming
= Seeing ants where he was to offer his prostration, he swept the place clear with his cloth and then fell flat like a rod.

@266
Nārada  says  hunter  this  not  is  a wonder
by devotion to Hari  of violence  free  becomes  the best of saints
= Nārada said, “Hunter, this is no wonder. Through devotion to Hari the best of saints becomes free of all violence.”

@267
these  not  indeed  wonderful  O hunter
your  non-violence and the other  virtues
in devotion to Hari  engaged  who
not  they  are  causing pain to others
= “O hunter, it is no wonder that you have non-violence and these other virtues: those who are engaged in devotion to Hari never cause pain to others.” (Skanda-purāṇa)

@268
then  that  hunter  the two  into the courtyard  brought
kuśa seats  bringing  the two  with devotion  seated
= Then the hunter brought the two sages into his courtyard, fetched seats of *kuśa* grass and reverently seated them.

@269
water  bringing  with devotion  of the two  the feet  washed
that  water  wife and husband  drinking  on the head  placed
= He brought water and reverently washed the feet of both, and he and his wife drank that water and placed it on their heads.

@270
trembling  horripilation  tears  arose  Kṛṣṇa’s  name  singing
arms raised  dance  he does  cloth  waving
= As he sang the name of Kṛṣṇa, he trembled, his hair stood on end and tears flowed; and he danced with arms raised, waving his cloth.

@271
seeing  of the hunter  the love  Parvata  the great sage
to Nārada  says  you  are  a touchstone
= Seeing the hunter’s love, the great sage Parvata said to Nārada, “You are a touchstone!

@272
ah  blessed  you are  O sage among the gods
by the mercy  of whom  instantly
lowly  even  with hair standing on end  obtained
a hunter  love  for Acyuta
= “Ah, O sage among the gods, you are blessed! By your mercy even a lowly hunter at once attained love (*rati*) for Acyuta, his hair standing on end.” (Skanda-purāṇa)

@273
Nārada  says  Vaiṣṇava  your  food  any  comes
the hunter  says  whom  you send  he  giving  goes
= Nārada said, “Vaiṣṇava, does any food come to you?” The hunter said, “Whoever you send brings something and leaves it.

@274
so much  food  not  send  any  need  there is not
only  for two persons  fitting  food  only  we want
= “Do not send so much food; there is no need. We want only as much food as is fit for two people.”

@275
Nārada  says  thus  remain  you  fortunate
this much  saying  the two  became  vanished
= Nārada said, “Live like this — you are fortunate.” With these words the two sages vanished.

@276
this  indeed  I told  to you  of the hunter  the story
which  hearing  arises  of the company of sādhus  of the power  knowledge
= Thus I have told you the story of the hunter. Whoever hears it comes to know the power of the company of *sādhus*.

@277
these  further  three  meanings  in the count  were obtained
these  two  meanings  combining  twenty-six  meanings  became
= These three further meanings are added to the count, and the two sets of meanings together come to twenty-six.

@278
another  meaning  hear  which  of meanings  a storehouse
broadly  two  meanings  in finer detail  thirty-two  kinds
= Hear another meaning — a storehouse of meanings: broadly it is twofold, and in finer detail it is of thirty-two kinds.

@279
ātmā  by the word  is meant  of all kinds  Bhagavān
one  Svayaṁ Bhagavān  others  Bhagavān  so named
= The word *ātmā* means Bhagavān in all His forms: one is Svayaṁ Bhagavān, the Lord Himself, and the others are called Bhagavān.

@280
in Him  delight  who  all they  ātmārāmas
vidhi-bhaktas  rāga-bhaktas  two kinds  names
= All who delight in Him are *ātmārāmas*. They are of two kinds by name: devotees who follow the rules (*vidhi-bhakta*) and devotees of spontaneous attachment (*rāga-bhakta*).

@281
two kinds  devotees  are  four  four  kinds
eternal associates  perfected by practice  practitioners  and
= Each of these two kinds of devotees is of four kinds: the eternal associates (*pārṣada*), those perfected by practice (*sādhana-siddha*) and the practitioners (*sādhaka*).

@282
awakened  unawakened  rati  by the difference  practitioners  two  kinds
vidhi  rāga  on the paths  four  four  eight  kinds
= The practitioners are of two kinds, according to whether *rati* has awakened in them or not. So on the paths of *vidhi* and *rāga* there are four and four — eight kinds.

@283
in vidhi-bhakti  eternally perfect  associates  servants
friends  elders  beloveds  four kinds  manifestation
= On the path of *vidhi-bhakti*, the eternally perfect associates (*nitya-siddha pārṣada*) appear in four kinds: servants, friends, elders and beloveds.

@284
perfected by practice  servants  friends  elders  beloveds
with awakened rati  practitioner-devotees  four kinds  persons
= Those perfected by practice are servants, friends, elders and beloveds; and the practitioners in whom *rati* has awakened are also of four kinds.

@285
with unawakened rati  practitioner-devotees  these  four  kinds
on the path of vidhi  among devotees  sixteen  kinds  known
= The practitioners in whom *rati* has not yet awakened are of these four kinds as well. Thus on the path of *vidhi* the devotees are known in sixteen kinds.

@286
on the path of rāga  likewise  among devotees  sixteen  kinds
on the two  paths  of ātmārāmas  thirty-two  kinds
= On the path of *rāga* the devotees are likewise of sixteen kinds, so that on the two paths there are thirty-two kinds of *ātmārāmas*.

@287
muni  nirgrantha  ca  api  four  of words  meanings
where  which  fits  that  I make  apt
= The meanings of the four words *muni*, *nirgrantha*, *ca* and *api* I apply wherever each of them fits.

@288
thirty-two  with twenty-six  combining  fifty-eight
another  one  kind  hear  of meaning  disclosure
= The thirty-two and the twenty-six together make fifty-eight. Now hear yet another way of bringing out the meaning.

@289
mutual (itaretara)  ca  adding  compound  I make
fifty-eight times  ātmārāma  word  I take
= Joining them with *ca* in the sense of mutual conjunction (*itaretara*), I take the word *ātmārāma* fifty-eight times:

@290
ātmārāmas  and  ātmārāmas  and  fifty-eight times
at the end  all  elision  making  I keep  once
= “*ātmārāmāś ca ātmārāmāś ca*” — fifty-eight times. Then, dropping all the rest, I keep the word only once.

@291
of words of the same form  one  remains  in the same case
of words whose meaning is already expressed  non-use  thus
= “Of words of the same form in the same case only one remains (*eka-śeṣa*); words whose meaning has already been expressed are not used.” (Viśva-prakāśa)

@292
fifty-eight  of the word ca  all  elision  takes place
one  ātmārāma  in the word  fifty-eight  meanings  expresses
= All fifty-eight occurrences of *ca* are elided, and the single word *ātmārāma* expresses fifty-eight meanings.

@293
of words of the same form  one  remains  in the same case
of words whose meaning is already expressed  non-use
= “Of words of the same form in the same case only one remains (*eka-śeṣa*); words whose meaning has already been expressed are not used.” (Pāṇini, 1.2.64)

@294
aśvattha trees  and  banyan trees  and
kapittha trees  and  mango trees  and  trees
= “*Aśvattha* trees and banyan trees and *kapittha* trees and mango trees — trees.”
