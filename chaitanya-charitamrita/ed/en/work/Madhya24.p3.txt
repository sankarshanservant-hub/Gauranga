@235
Nārada  says  if  living beings  you strike  you  with an arrow
half-killed  you make  why  not  you take  life
= Nārada said, “If you shoot living beings with your arrows, why do you leave them half-dead instead of taking their lives?”

@236
the hunter  says  listen  O gosāñi  Mṛgāri  my  name
of the father  by the teaching  I  do  such  work
= The hunter replied, “Listen, gosāñi: my name is Mṛgāri, ‘enemy of the beasts.’ I do this work just as my father taught me.

@237
half  killed  creature  when  writhes  does
then  indeed  joy  my  swells  in the heart
= “When a half-killed creature writhes and thrashes about, the joy in my heart swells.”

@238
Nārada  says  one thing  I ask  from you
the hunter  says  deer and others  take  whichever  in your  mind
= Nārada said, “I ask one thing of you.” The hunter said, “Take a deer or any other animal you like.

@239
deerskin  you want  if  come  my  to the house
whatever  you want  that  I will give  among deer and tiger skins
= “If you want a deerskin, come to my house. I will give you whatever you want — a deerskin or a tiger skin.”

@240
Nārada  says  this  I  any  not  want
another  one  gift  I  ask  from you
= Nārada said, “I want none of these. I ask another gift of you:

@241
tomorrow  from  you  whichever  deer and others  you will kill
at once  kill  half-killed  not  make
= “From tomorrow on, whatever animals you kill, kill them outright; do not leave them half-dead.”

@242
the hunter  says  what  gift  you asked  of me
half  killing  what  happens  that  tell  me
= The hunter said, “What a gift to ask of me! What harm is there in half-killing them? Tell me that.”

@243
Nārada  says  half  killing  creature  gets  pain
to creatures  pain  you are giving  yours  will be  such  plight
= Nārada said, “A half-killed creature suffers pain. You are giving pain to living beings, and the same plight will be yours.

@244
hunter  you  creatures  you kill  small  offense  your
torment  giving  you kill  this  sin  boundless
= “You are a hunter and you kill animals — that is a small offense. But to kill them by torment — that sin is boundless.

@245
tormenting  you  as many  you killed  creatures
they  likewise  you  will kill  birth after birth
= “All the creatures you have tormented to death will kill you in the same way, birth after birth.”

@246
in the company of Nārada  of the hunter  mind  pure  became
his  words  hearing  in the mind  fear  arose
= In Nārada’s company the hunter’s mind became clear, and on hearing his words he grew afraid.

@247
the hunter  says  childhood  from  this  my  work
how  will cross over  I  sinful  fallen
= The hunter said, “This has been my work since childhood. How can I, a sinful and fallen wretch, be delivered?

@248
this  sin  goes  my  what  by means
deliverance  do  to me  I fall  your  at the feet
= “By what means will this sin of mine be removed? Deliver me — I fall at your feet.”

@249
Nārada  says  if  you hold  my  word
then  only  to do  I can  your  release
= Nārada said, “If you accept my word, then I can set you free.”

@250
the hunter  says  whatever  you say  that  indeed  I will do
Nārada  says  bow  break  then  only  I will tell
= The hunter said, “Whatever you say, I will do.” Nārada said, “Break your bow; then I will tell you.”

@251
the hunter  says  bow  breaking  I will live  how
Nārada  says  I  food  will give  every day
= The hunter said, “If I break my bow, how will I live?” Nārada said, “I will give you food every day.”

@252
bow  breaking  the hunter  his  at the feet  fell
him  raising  Nārada  instruction  gave
= The hunter broke his bow and fell at Nārada’s feet. Nārada raised him up and instructed him:

@253
home  going  to the brāhmaṇas  give  whatever  there is  wealth
one  one  cloth  wearing  outside  go  two  persons
= “Go home and give the brāhmaṇas whatever wealth you have. Then leave home, both of you, each wearing a single cloth.

@254
of a river  on the bank  one  hut  making
it  in front of  on a raised mound  tulasī  planting
= “Build a small hut on the bank of a river, and in front of it plant a tulasī on a raised mound.

@255
tulasī  circumambulation  do  tulasī  service
constantly  Kṛṣṇa’s  name  do  chanting
= “Walk around the tulasī and serve her, and chant the name of Kṛṣṇa without cease.

@256
I  to you  much  food  will send  daily
that  food  take  as much as  you eat  two persons
= “I will send you plenty of food every day. Take of it only as much as the two of you eat.”

@257
then  those  deer and others  three  Nārada  healthy  made
healthy  becoming  deer and others  three  running  fled
= Then Nārada healed the three wounded animals, and once healed they ran off and were gone.

@258
seeing  of the hunter  in the mind  arose  wonder
home  went  the hunter  to the guru  making  obeisance
= Seeing this, the hunter was astonished. He bowed to his guru and went home.

@259
to his destination  Nārada  went  the hunter  home  came
of Nārada  by the instruction  everything  did
= Nārada went on his way, and the hunter went home and did everything Nārada had told him.

@260
in the village  word  went  the hunter  a Vaiṣṇava  became
of the village  people  all  food  to bring  began
= Word spread through the village that the hunter had become a Vaiṣṇava, and all the villagers began to bring him food.

@261
in one day  food  bring  ten or twenty  persons
daily  that much  he takes  as much as  eat  two  persons
= In a single day ten or twenty people would bring food, but each day he took only as much as the two of them would eat.

@262
one day  Nārada  says  listen  to Parvata
my  one  disciple  there is  let us go  to see
= One day Nārada said to Parvata, “Listen, I have a disciple. Let us go and see him.”

@263
then  two  sages  came  that  to the hunter’s place
far  from  the hunter  obtained  of the guru  sight
= So the two sages came to the hunter’s place, and the hunter caught sight of his guru from afar.

@264
hurriedly  running  he comes  path  not  finds
of the path  ants  here and there  touch  feet
= He came running in haste, yet he could find no place to step, for here and there the ants on the path were at his feet.
