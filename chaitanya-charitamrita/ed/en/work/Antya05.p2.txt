@83
another  one  ‘trait’  of Gaura  hear  O devotees
hidden  majestic nature  He makes  manifest
= Hear, O devotees, of yet another trait of Gaura: He makes His hidden, majestic nature manifest.

@84
of the sannyāsīs and paṇḍitas  to make  of the pride  destruction
through a lowly śūdra  He makes  of dharma  the manifestation
= To destroy the pride of the *sannyāsīs* and *paṇḍitas*, He makes *dharma* manifest through a lowly *śūdra*.

@85
‘bhakti’  ‘prema’  ‘tattva’  He speaks  Rāya  making  ‘the speaker’
Himself  with Pradyumna Miśra  becomes  ‘the listener’
= He speaks of *bhakti*, *prema* and *tattva*, making Rāya the speaker, while He Himself, together with Pradyumna Miśra, becomes the listener.

@86
through Haridāsa  the glory of the holy name  the manifestation
through Sanātana  the play of the conclusions of bhakti
= Through Haridāsa He revealed the glory of the holy name; through Sanātana, the play of the conclusions of *bhakti*.

@87
through Śrī Rūpa  of Vraja  the pastimes of rasa and prema
who  to tell  can  profound  Caitanya’s  play
= Through Śrī Rūpa He revealed Vraja’s pastimes of *rasa* and *prema*. Who can describe the profound play of Caitanya?

@88
Śrī Caitanya’s pastimes  these  of nectar  an ocean
the world  to flood  can  whose  one  drop
= These pastimes of Śrī Caitanya are an ocean of nectar, a single drop of which can flood the world.

@89
the Caitanya-caritāmṛta  always  do  drink
from which  ‘the bliss of prema’  ‘knowledge of the truth of bhakti’
= Drink the *Caitanya-caritāmṛta* constantly, for from it come the bliss of *prema* and knowledge of the truth of *bhakti*.

@90
in this way  Mahāprabhu  the devotees  taking with Him
in Nīlācala  sports  bhakti  preaching
= In this way Mahāprabhu, together with His devotees, enjoyed His pastimes in Nīlācala, preaching *bhakti*.

@91
from Bengal  a  brāhmaṇa  on the Lord’s  life
a drama  having made  taking  came  to recite
= A brāhmaṇa from Bengal composed a drama on the Lord’s life and came, bringing it to recite.

@92
Bhagavān Ācārya  with  his  acquaintance
him  having met  in his  house  made  his lodging
= He was acquainted with Bhagavān Ācārya; having met him, he took lodging in his house.

@93
first  the drama  he  to him  recited
with him  many  Vaiṣṇavas  the drama  heard
= First he recited the drama to him, and many Vaiṣṇavas heard it along with him.

@94
all  praise  the drama  ‘most excellent’
to Mahāprabhu  to recite  of all  arose  the wish
= Everyone praised the drama as “most excellent,” and all wished to have Mahāprabhu hear it.

@95
song  verse  book  poem  —  whatever  having made  one brings
first  has it heard  that  in Svarūpa’s  presence
= Whatever song, verse, book or poem anyone composed and brought, he would first recite it before Svarūpa.

@96
before Svarūpa  passes  if  accepts  his  mind
then  before Mahāprabhu  causes  the hearing
= If it passed before Svarūpa and his mind accepted it, only then would it be recited for Mahāprabhu to hear.

@97
‘rasābhāsa’  is  if  ‘opposition to siddhānta’
to bear  not  can  the Lord  in the mind  arises  anger
= If there was *rasābhāsa*, a mere semblance of *rasa*, or anything opposed to *siddhānta*, the Lord could not bear it, and anger arose in His mind.

@98
therefore  the Lord  anything  first  not  hears
this  rule of propriety  the Lord  has made  as a fixed rule
= Therefore the Lord would not hear anything first; He had made this rule of propriety a fixed principle.

@99
before Svarūpa  the Ācārya  made  a request
a  brāhmaṇa  on the Lord  a drama  has composed  excellent
= Bhagavān Ācārya made a request to Svarūpa: “A brāhmaṇa has composed an excellent drama about the Lord.

@100
first  you  hear  if  your  mind  accepts
afterwards  Mahāprabhu  then  I shall have  hear
= First you hear it, if your mind accepts it; afterwards I shall have Mahāprabhu hear it.”

@101
Svarūpa  says  —  you  a ‘cowherd’  most generous
any whatever  śāstra  to hear  desire  arises  your
= Svarūpa said, “You are a ‘cowherd’ (*gopa*), most generous: a desire arises in you to hear any scripture whatever.

@102
‘any whatever’  of a poet  in the words  is  ‘rasābhāsa’
opposed to siddhānta  to hear  not  arises  joy
= In the words of just any poet there is *rasābhāsa*, and hearing what is opposed to *siddhānta* brings no joy.

@103
‘rasa’  ‘rasābhāsa’  whose  there is no  discrimination
of the conclusions of bhakti  the ocean  not  reaches  the shore
= One who cannot discriminate between *rasa* and *rasābhāsa* never reaches the far shore of the ocean of *bhakti-siddhānta*.

@104-105
‘grammar’  not  knows  not  knows  ‘rhetoric’
‘of the ornaments of drama’  knowledge  there is no  whose
Kṛṣṇa’s pastimes  to describe  not  knows  that  worthless one
especially  inaccessible  this  play of Caitanya
= One who knows neither grammar nor rhetoric and has no knowledge of the ornaments of drama — such a worthless one does not know how to describe Kṛṣṇa’s pastimes, and this play of Caitanya is especially hard to approach.

@106
Kṛṣṇa’s pastimes  Gaura’s pastimes  he  does  describe
Gaura’s  lotus feet  whose  are  the wealth of life
= Only one for whom the lotus feet of Gaura are the very wealth of life can describe the pastimes of Kṛṣṇa and the pastimes of Gaura.

@107
of a mundane poet  the poetry  to hear  is  ‘pain’
of one expert and intimate  the words  to hear  is  ‘joy’
= To hear the poetry of a mundane poet (*grāmya-kavi*) is painful; to hear the words of one who is expert and intimate (*vidagdha ātmīya*) is a joy.

@108
Rūpa  as  two  dramas  has composed  at the outset
to hear  joy  increases  whose  in the prologue
= Take the two dramas Rūpa has composed: on hearing their very opening, the prologue, one’s joy increases.”

@109
Bhagavān Ācārya  says  —  hear  once
you  having heard  good or bad  will know  the judgment
= Bhagavān Ācārya said, “Just hear it once. When you have heard it, you will be able to judge whether it is good or bad.”

@110
two or three days  the Ācārya  insistence  made
by his  insistence  in Svarūpa  to hear  the desire  arose
= For two or three days the Ācārya pressed him, and at his insistence Svarūpa became willing to hear it.

@111
all  taking  Svarūpa Gosāñi  to hear  sat down
then  that  poet  the nāndī verse  recited
= Svarūpa Gosāñi sat down with everyone to listen, and the poet recited the *nāndī* verse, the opening benediction.

@112
with eyes like blossoming lotuses  named Śrī Jagannātha
golden-hued  here in the body  selfhood  who  has attained
inert matter  all  enlivening  appeared
He  may grant  your  welfare  Kṛṣṇa Caitanya-deva
= “He of golden luster who has here taken as His self the body (*ātman*) named Śrī Jagannātha, whose eyes are like blossoming lotuses, and who has appeared to enliven the whole of inert matter — may that Kṛṣṇa Caitanya-deva grant you good fortune.”

@113
the verse  hearing  all the people  him  praised
Svarūpa  says  —  this  verse  do  explain
= Hearing the verse, everyone praised the poet, but Svarūpa said, “Explain this verse.”

@114
the poet  says  —  Jagannātha  —  the beautiful body
Caitanya Gosāñi  —  the embodied one  most steady
= The poet said, “Jagannātha is the beautiful body, and Caitanya Gosāñi, most grave and steady, is the one embodied in it (*śarīrī*).

@115
naturally  the inert world  conscious  to make
in Nīlācala  Mahāprabhu  became  manifest
= To bestow consciousness on the inert world, as is His nature, Mahāprabhu has appeared in Nīlācala.”

@116
hearing  of all  became  joyful  the mind
pain  feeling  Svarūpa  speaks  angry  words
= Hearing this, everyone was delighted, but Svarūpa, deeply pained, spoke angry words:

@117
O  fool  your own  you have made  utter ruin
in the two  indeed  Lords  your  there is no  faith
= “O fool, you have brought about your own utter ruin! You have no faith in either of the two Lords!

@118
full of bliss  a form of pure spirit  Lord Jagannātha
Him  you have made  an inert, perishable, material  body
= Lord Jagannātha is full of bliss, a form of pure spirit, yet you have made Him out to be an inert, perishable, material body!

@119
full of the six opulences  Caitanya  —  Svayam Bhagavān
Him  you have made  a tiny jīva  a spark  like
= Caitanya, full in all six opulences, is Svayam Bhagavān, the Supreme Lord Himself, yet you have made Him out to be a tiny *jīva*, like a spark!

@120
in two places  by the offense  you will get  a dire fate
one ignorant of truth  ‘truth’  describes  his  this  fate
= For this offense against both you will meet a dire fate! When one ignorant of the truth describes the truth, this is his fate!

@121
and  one  you have made  great  ‘blunder’
the difference of body and embodied  in the Lord  you made  ‘an offense’
= You have committed yet another great blunder (*pramāda*): by making a distinction between body and embodied in the Lord, you have committed an offense!

@122
of the Lord  there is not  ever  body–embodied  difference
His own form  body  —  spirit and bliss  there is no  difference
= In the Lord there is never any difference between body and embodied. His own form (*svarūpa*) and His body are both spirit and bliss; there is no difference between them.

@123
of body and embodied  division  this
not in the Lord  exists  ever
= “This division of body and embodied never exists in the Lord.” (Kūrma Purāṇa)

@124
not  than this  higher  O Supreme  which  Your  form
pure bliss  free of difference  of unobscured radiance
I see  of the universe  the creator  one  distinct from the universe  O Self
the source of the elements and senses  that  Your  sheltered  I am
= “O Supreme Lord, I see nothing higher than this form of Yours, which is pure bliss, free of all difference, its radiance unobscured. You are the one creator of the universe, yet You are distinct from it, O Self, and You are the source of the elements and the senses. I have taken shelter of that form of Yours.” (Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam, 3.9.3)

@125
that  indeed  this (form)  O good of the worlds  for the good
in meditation  indeed  to us  shown  by You  to the worshippers
to Him  obeisance  to Bhagavān  we offer  to You
who  is not honoured  by those bound for hell  attached to false arguments
= “O good of all the worlds, for our good You have shown this very form to us, Your worshippers, in meditation. We bow down to You, Bhagavān, whom those bound for hell, attached to false arguments, do not honour.” (Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam, 3.9.4)

@126
where  ‘full of bliss and opulence’  Kṛṣṇa  ‘the Great Lord’
where  ‘tiny’  jīva  ‘miserable’  ‘servant of māyā’
= Where is Kṛṣṇa, full of bliss and opulence, the Great Lord (*Maheśvara*)? And where is the tiny, miserable *jīva*, a servant of *māyā*?

@127
by hlādinī  embraced by saṁvit
eternal being, consciousness and bliss  the Lord
by its own ignorance  covered  the jīva
a mine of a multitude of miseries
= “The Lord, embraced by the hlādinī and saṁvit potencies, is eternal being, consciousness and bliss; the jīva, covered by its own ignorance, is a mine of countless miseries.” (words of Viṣṇusvāmī quoted by Śrīdhara Svāmī in his commentary on Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam, 1.7.5–6)

@128
hearing  of the assembly  there was  great astonishment
truly  speaks  Gosāñi  both  he has insulted
= Hearing this, the whole assembly was struck with wonder: “The Gosāñi speaks the truth! The poet has insulted both.”

@129
hearing  of the poet  arose  shame  fear  amazement
swans  among  a heron  like  anything  not  says
= On hearing this, the poet was filled with shame, fear and amazement. Like a heron among swans, he could say nothing.

@130
his  distress  seeing  Svarūpa  most merciful
instruction  gave  to him  so that  ‘good’  may come
= Seeing his distress, the most merciful Svarūpa instructed him in a way that would bring him true good:

@131

exclusive  shelter  take  at Caitanya’s feet
= “Go and study the *Bhāgavata* from a Vaiṣṇava, and take exclusive shelter at the feet of Caitanya.

@132

then  indeed  you will know  of the ocean of siddhānta  the waves
= Always keep the company of Caitanya’s devotees; then you will come to know the waves of the ocean of *siddhānta*.

@133

Kṛṣṇa’s  true nature and pastimes  you will describe  purely
= Then your learning will bear fruit, and you will describe Kṛṣṇa’s true nature and pastimes purely, without fault.

@134

your  of the heart  by the meaning  to both  attaches  ‘fault’
= You composed this verse with great satisfaction, but by the meaning in your heart it attaches fault to both Lords.

@135
you  any which way  speak  not  knowing  the proper way
Sarasvatī  with those  words  has made  praise
= You speak any which way, not knowing the proper method; yet with those very words Sarasvatī has offered praise.

@136
as  Indra  the demons and others  do  of Kṛṣṇa  rebuke
with those words  Sarasvatī  does  praise
= Just as when Indra, the demons and others rebuke Kṛṣṇa, Sarasvatī offers praise with those very words.

@137
talkative  childish  stubborn

Kṛṣṇa  a mortal  having taken shelter of
the cowherds  to me  have done  an offense

@138

= Indra, maddened by the intoxication of his opulence, was like a drunkard: his intelligence was lost, and he had no self-control at all.

@139

from his very  mouth  Sarasvatī  does  praise
= Indra says, “I have reviled Kṛṣṇa,” yet from his very mouth Sarasvatī offers praise.

@140

= ‘*Vācāla*’ (talkative) means the glorious promulgator of the Vedas; ‘*bāliśa*’ (childish) — though great, He is like a child, free of pride.

@141

= Because there is no one for Him to honor, He bows to none: this is what the word ‘*stabdha*’ (stubborn) says. And He beyond whom there is no other knower is ‘*ajña*’ (ignorant) — there is nothing for Him to learn.

@142

still  out of affection for devotees  ‘human’  considers Himself
= The one honored by the learned is ‘*paṇḍita-mānī*’; yet out of affection for His devotees He regards Himself as a human being, a ‘mortal’.

@143

@144

He  is  ‘puruṣādhama’  —  Sarasvatī’s  meaning
= He in comparison with whom all other persons are lower is ‘*puruṣādhama*’ — that is Sarasvatī’s meaning.

@145

= Because it binds everyone, ignorance (*avidyā*) is the ‘*bandhu*’; so the word ‘*bandhu-han*’ means the destroyer of ignorance.

@146

= In the same way Śiśupāla reviled Kṛṣṇa, and with those very words Sarasvatī offered praise.

@147

= Jagannātha is Kṛṣṇa’s very self (*ātma-svarūpa*), but here He is the Dāru-brahma, in an immobile form.

@149

@154

= The poet gave up everything and remained in Nīlācala. Who can describe the mercy of Gaura’s devotees?

= Within it I have told the glory of Rāmānanda, the full extent of which the Lord Himself described with His own holy mouth.

@162
Śrī Kṛṣṇa Caitanya’s pastimes  —  of nectar  the essence

= The pastimes of Śrī Kṛṣṇa Caitanya are the essence of nectar: from the stream of a single pastime flow hundreds upon hundreds of currents.
