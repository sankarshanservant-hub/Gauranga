@title Instructions to Sanātana Gosvāmī

@1
I bow down  to Him whose opulence is unlimited and wonderful
to Śrī Caitanya Mahāprabhu
a lowly one  even  by whose mercy  may become
of the scriptures of devotion  a promulgator
= I bow down to Śrī Caitanya Mahāprabhu, whose opulence is unlimited and wonderful. By His mercy even a lowly person may become a promulgator of the scriptures of devotion.

@2
glory  glory  to Śrī Caitanya  glory  to Nityānanda
glory  to Advaitacandra  glory  to Gaura’s  devotees  host
= Glory, glory to Śrī Caitanya! Glory to Nityānanda! Glory to Advaitacandra, and glory to all the devotees of Gaura!

@3
here  in Gauḍa  Sanātana  was  in prison
of Śrī Rūpa Gosvāmī  a letter  came  at that time
= Meanwhile, in Gauḍa, Sanātana was still in prison when a letter from Śrī Rūpa Gosvāmī reached him.

@4
the letter  receiving  Sanātana  delighted  became
to the yavana jailer  to speak  he began
= Sanātana was delighted to receive the letter, and he began to speak to the yavana who guarded him:

@5
you  are a  living saint (jindā-pīr)  greatly fortunate
in the books and the Koran  the scriptures  is  your  knowledge
= “You are a *jindā-pīr*, a living saint, and greatly fortunate. You are learned in the Koran and the other scriptures.

@6
one  prisoner  releases  if  one’s own religion  regarding
from worldly existence  him  liberated  makes  the Lord
= If someone, true to his own religion, releases a prisoner, the Lord frees him from worldly existence.

@7
before  I  for you  have done  favors
you  me  releasing  do  a favor in return
= I have done you favors in the past. Now release me and return the favor.

@8
five  thousand  coins  you  make  acceptance
merit  wealth  two  gains  will be  yours
= Please accept five thousand coins. You will gain twice over — merit and wealth.”

@9
then  that  yavana  says  listen  sir (mahāśaya)
you  I would release  but  I have  fear of the king
= The yavana replied, “Listen, sir. I would release you, but I fear the king.”

@10-11
Sanātana  says  you  do not  have  fear of the king
to the south  he has gone  if  coming back  he returns
to him  say  he  to relieve himself  went
of the Ganges  near  the Ganges  seeing  a leap  he made
= Sanātana said, “Do not fear the king. He has gone south. If he comes back, tell him that I went out to relieve myself and, coming near the Ganges and seeing it, jumped in.

@12
much  you looked  his  trace  not  you found
with the fetters  drowning  somewhere  carried away  he went
= Tell him that you searched long but found no trace of me — that I drowned with my fetters on and was carried away somewhere.

@13
any  fear  there is not  I  in this land  will not  stay
a dervish  becoming  I  to Mecca  will go
= There is nothing to fear: I shall not stay in this land. I shall become a dervish and go to Mecca.”

@14
still  of the yavana  the mind  pleased  not  he saw
seven thousand  coins  before him  in a heap  he made
= Still Sanātana saw that the yavana was not satisfied, so he heaped seven thousand coins before him.

@15
greed  arose  of the yavana  the coins  seeing
at night  the crossing of the Ganges  he made  the fetters  cutting
= At the sight of the coins the yavana grew greedy. That night he cut off Sanātana’s fetters and took him across the Ganges.

@16
the road by the fort gate  he gave up  he could not  there  go
night and day  walking  he came  to the Pātaḍā hills
= Sanātana avoided the road through the fort gate, for he could not go that way. Walking night and day, he came to the Pātaḍā hills.

@17
there  a  landholder (bhaumika)  was  to him  he went
the hills  across  take  me  a request  he made
= There lived a landholder (*bhaumika*), a chief of robbers. Sanātana went to him and humbly asked, “Please take me across the hills.”

@18
of that landholder  with him  was  a palmist
in the landholder’s  ear  says  he  knowing  this  matter
= With that landholder was a palmist. Perceiving the matter, he whispered into the landholder’s ear,

@19
with this man  of gold  eight  mohurs  there are
hearing  delighted  the landholder  to Sanātana  says
= “This man has eight gold mohurs.” Hearing this, the landholder was delighted and said to Sanātana,

@20
tonight  the hills  crossing  I will make  my own men  giving
a meal  take  you  cooking  having done
= “Tonight I will have my own men take you across the hills. Meanwhile, cook and have your meal.”

@21
so  saying  grain  he gave  showing  respect
Sanātana  going  then  made  a bath in the river
= With these words he respectfully gave him food grains, and Sanātana went and bathed in the river.

@22
after a fast of two days  he did  cooking and eating
the royal minister  Sanātana  reflected  in his mind
= Having fasted for two days, he cooked and ate. But Sanātana, once a royal minister, reflected in his mind:

@23
this  landholder  why  to me  respect  has shown
thus  thinking  Sanātana  Īśāna  asked
= “Why has this landholder shown me such respect?” Thinking this, Sanātana asked Īśāna,

@24
with you  I know  some  valuables  there are
Īśāna  says  with me  seven  mohurs  there are
= “I know you have some valuables with you.” Īśāna replied, “I have seven mohurs.”

@25
hearing  Sanātana  him  made  rebuke
with you  why  have you brought  this  death (kāla-yama)
= Hearing this, Sanātana rebuked him: “Why have you brought this death along with you?”

@26
then  those  seven  mohurs  in his hand  taking
the landholder  near  going  says  the mohurs  holding out
= Then he took the seven mohurs in his hand, went to the landholder and, holding out the coins, said,

@27
these  seven  gold  mohurs  were  mine
this  taking  religion  regarding  the hills  make  crossing
= “I had these seven gold mohurs. Take them, and as a matter of religious duty take me across the hills.

@28
a royal prisoner  I am  by the fort gate  to go  not  I can
merit  there will be  the hills  me  give  crossing having made
= I am the king’s prisoner and cannot go by the fort gate. You will earn merit — take me across the hills.”

@29
the landholder  smiling  says  I  had known  before
eight  mohurs  there are  of your  servant  in the hem of the cloth
= The landholder smiled and said, “I knew it already: your servant has eight mohurs tied in the hem of his cloth.

@30
you  killing  the mohurs  I would have taken  of today  night
good  it was  told  you  I was freed  from sin
= Tonight I would have killed you and taken the mohurs. It is good that you told me — I have been spared that sin.

@31
satisfied  have become  I  the mohurs  not  I will take
of merit  for the sake  the hills  you  across taking  I will give
= I am pleased. I will not take the mohurs. For the sake of merit alone I will take you across the hills.”

@32
the Gosvāmī  says  someone  the valuables  will take  me  killing
my  life  protection  do  the valuables  accepting
= Sanātana Gosvāmī said, “Someone else will kill me for these valuables. Accept them and save my life.”

@33
then  the landholder  with the Gosvāmī  four  armed men  gave
night after night  by forest paths  the hills  across  took
= Then the landholder sent four armed men with him, and night after night they led him across the hills along forest paths.

@34
across  having come  the Gosvāmī  then  asked  Īśāna
I know  remaining  valuables  some  are  with you
= Having crossed, Sanātana Gosvāmī asked Īśāna, “I know you still have something left with you.”

@35
Īśāna  says  one  mohur  is  remaining
the Gosvāmī  says  the mohur  taking  go  you  home
= Īśāna replied, “One mohur is left.” Sanātana Gosvāmī said, “Take the mohur and go home.”
