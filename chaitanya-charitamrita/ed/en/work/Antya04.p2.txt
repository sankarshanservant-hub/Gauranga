@121
of the meal  of the remnants  the plate  Govinda  to him  gave
prasāda  having taken  Sanātana  to the Lord’s side  came
= Govinda gave him the plate with the remnants of the Lord’s meal. Having taken the *prasāda*, Sanātana came to the Lord.

@122
the Lord  says  by which  path  have you come  Sanātana
he  says  by the path along the sea  I have come
= The Lord asked, “By which path did you come, Sanātana?” He replied, “I came by the path along the sea.”

@123
the Lord  says  over the hot  sand  how  did you come
of the Siṁha-dvāra  the path  is cool  why  not  did you come
= The Lord said, “How could you come over the burning sand? The path by the Siṁha-dvāra is cool — why did you not come that way?

@124
from the hot  sand  your  on the feet  have come  blisters
to walk  not  you can  how  did you endure
= The hot sand has blistered your feet; you cannot walk. How did you bear it?”

@125
Sanātana  says  pain  much  not  I felt
on the feet  blisters  have formed  that  not  I knew
= Sanātana said, “I did not feel much pain. I did not even know that blisters had formed on my feet.

@126
by the Siṁha-dvāra  to go  my  there is no  right
especially  of the Ṭhākura  there  of the servants  the movement
= I have no right to go by the Siṁha-dvāra, especially since the servants of the Ṭhākura, Lord Jagannātha, move about there.

@127
the servants  coming and going  do  there is no  pause
their  touch  if it happens  ruin  will be  mine
= The servants come and go without pause, and if I should touch them, it would be my ruin.”

@128
hearing  Mahāprabhu  in mind  satisfaction  felt
pleased  becoming  to him  something  to say  began
= Hearing this, Mahāprabhu was satisfied at heart, and, pleased, He began to speak to him.

@129-130
although  you  are  the purifier of the world
by your touch  purified  become  the gods  and sages
nevertheless  of a devotee  the nature  propriety  guarding
of propriety  the keeping  is  of a sādhu  the ornament
= “Although you purify the whole world, and even the gods and sages are purified by your touch, it is still a devotee’s nature to guard propriety (*maryādā*). Keeping propriety is the ornament of a *sādhu*.

@131
of propriety  in the transgression  people  make  mockery
this world  the next world  both  are  destroyed
= When propriety is transgressed, people mock, and both this world and the next are ruined.

@132
propriety  when you keep  pleased  becomes  My  mind
you  so  if not  do  would do  which  person
= When you keep propriety, My mind is pleased. If you do not act this way, who will?”

@133
this  having said  the Lord  him  embrace  made
his  the fluid of the sores  of the Lord  to the holy body  clung
= Saying this, the Lord embraced him, and the fluid from his sores smeared the Lord’s holy body.

@134
again and again  he forbids  still  He makes  embrace
to the body  the fluid  clings  sorrow  feels  Sanātana
= Again and again Sanātana tried to stop Him, yet the Lord kept embracing him. The fluid clung to the Lord’s body, and Sanātana was grieved.

@135
in this way  servant and master  both  home  went
the next  day  Jagadānanda  with Sanātana  met
= In this way servant and master went to their dwellings. The next day Jagadānanda came to see Sanātana.

@136
the two  sitting  about Kṛṣṇa  a talk  made
to the Paṇḍita  Sanātana  his grief  confided
= The two sat together and talked of Kṛṣṇa, and Sanātana confided his grief to the Paṇḍita:

@137
here  I came  the Lord  seeing  my grief  to remove
whatever  in mind  that  the Lord  not  let  me do
= “I came here to see the Lord and be rid of my grief, but the Lord did not let me do what I had in mind.

@138
though I forbid  the Lord  embrace  makes  me
my  the fluid of the sores  clings  of the Lord  to the body
= Though I try to stop Him, the Lord embraces me, and the fluid of my sores smears His body.

@139
offense  is  mine  there is no  deliverance
even Jagannātha  not  I see  this  grief  boundless
= I am committing an offense, and for me there is no deliverance. I cannot even see Jagannātha — this grief is boundless.

@140
for my welfare  came  I  it turned out  the opposite
what  by doing  welfare  comes  I cannot  determine
= I came for my welfare, but the opposite has happened. I cannot decide what I should do for my good.”

@141
the Paṇḍita  says  for you  fit to live in  is Vṛndāvana
the Ratha-yātrā  having seen  there  make  your going
= The Paṇḍita said, “The place fit for you to live is Vṛndāvana. After seeing the Ratha-yātrā, go there.

@142
of the Lord  the order  has been given  to you  two  brothers
in Vṛndāvana  settle  there  all happiness  you will find
= The Lord has ordered you two brothers to settle in Vṛndāvana; there you will find every happiness.

@143
for which purpose  you came  of the Lord  you have seen  the feet
on the chariot  Jagannātha  having seen  make  your going
= You came for the Lord’s feet, and you have seen them. Now see Jagannātha on His chariot and go.”

@144
Sanātana  says  good  you have given  advice
there  I shall go  that  my  the land given by the Lord
= Sanātana said, “You have given good advice. I shall go there: that is my ‘land given by the Lord.’”

@145
this  having said  the two  to their own duties  rising  went
the next  day  Mahāprabhu  to meet  came
= Saying this, the two rose and went about their duties. The next day Mahāprabhu came to meet them.

@146
Haridāsa  made  of the Lord  to the feet  obeisance
Haridāsa  made  the Lord  a loving embrace
= Haridāsa bowed at the Lord’s feet, and the Lord embraced Haridāsa with love.

@147
from afar  prostrate  obeisance  makes  Sanātana
the Lord  calls  again and again  to make  embrace
= Sanātana offered prostrate obeisances from a distance. Again and again the Lord called him, wanting to embrace him.

@148
of offense  in fear  he  to meet  not  came
Mahāprabhu  to meet  to that  place  went
= Fearing to offend, he did not come forward to meet Him, so Mahāprabhu went to that place to meet him.

@149
Sanātana  running away  backward  goes
by force  catching him  the Lord  made  embrace
= Sanātana drew back and fled, but the Lord caught him by force and embraced him.

@150
the two  taking  the Lord  sat  on the raised platform
filled with nirveda  Sanātana  began  to speak
= Taking the two with Him, the Lord sat down on the raised platform (*piṇḍā*), and Sanātana, filled with *nirveda* — detachment — began to speak:

@151
welfare  for the sake  came  I  it turned out  the opposite
for service  fit  I am not  offense  I commit  daily  daily
= “I came for my welfare, but the opposite has happened. I am not fit for service; day after day I commit offenses.

@152
by nature  of low birth  I  wicked  a reservoir of sin
me  You  if You touch  my  offense  occurs
= By nature I am of low birth, wicked, a ‘reservoir of sin.’ When You touch me, I commit an offense.

@153
moreover  my  on the body  sore-fluid and blood  flow
Your  to the body  it clings  still  touch  You  by force
= Moreover, fluid and blood flow from the sores on my body and smear Yours, yet still You touch me by force.

@154
the loathsome  in touching  not  You feel  disgust  even a trace
by this  offense  my  will be  utter ruin
= You do not feel even a trace of disgust in touching what is loathsome. This offense will be my utter ruin.

@155
therefore  here  if I stay  my  not  comes  welfare
leave  give  the chariot  having seen  let me go  to Vṛndāvana
= So if I stay here, no good will come to me. Give me leave: after seeing the chariot festival, let me go to Vṛndāvana.

@156
Jagadānanda Paṇḍita  I  counsel  asked
to Vṛndāvana  to go  he  advice  gave
= I asked Jagadānanda Paṇḍita for counsel, and he advised me to go to Vṛndāvana.”

@157
this  hearing  Mahāprabhu  with anger  within
Jagadānanda  angry  becoming  He  rebukes
= Hearing this, Mahāprabhu grew angry at heart and in anger rebuked Jagadānanda:

@158
yesterday’s  boy  Jagā  so  proud  has become
even you all  advice  to give  he has begun
= “Jagā, a mere boy of yesterday, has become so proud that he has begun to advise even you?

@159
in worldly and in spiritual matters  you  his  like a guru
you  advice  he gives  not  he knows  his own  worth
= In worldly and in spiritual matters alike you are like a guru to him. He advises you — does he not know his own worth?

@160
My  adviser  you are  an authority  noble
even you  he advises  a boy  does  such  a deed
= You are My adviser, an authoritative and noble soul (*ārya*). And this boy advises even you — such is his conduct!”

@161
hearing  Sanātana  the feet  clasping  to the Lord  said
of Jagadānanda  the good fortune  today  that  I have understood
= Hearing this, Sanātana clasped the Lord’s feet and said, “Today I have understood Jagadānanda’s good fortune.

@162
my own  misfortune  today  became  known
in the world  there is not  like Jagadānanda  one so fortunate
= And today I have come to know my own misfortune. In all the world there is no one as fortunate as Jagadānanda.

@163
Jagadānanda  You make drink  of intimacy  the nectar
me  You make drink  of respectful praise  of nimba  of niśindā  the juice
= You give Jagadānanda the nectar of intimacy to drink, but me You make drink the bitter juice of *nimba* and *niśindā* — Your respectful praise.

@164
even today  there was not  toward me  of intimacy  the feeling
my  misfortune  You  are  the independent  Bhagavān
= Even today You have not come to regard me as Your own. It is my misfortune; You are the independent Lord, Bhagavān!”

@165
hearing  Mahāprabhu  somewhat  abashed  became  at heart
him  to satisfy  some  He speaks  words
= Hearing this, Mahāprabhu felt somewhat abashed at heart, and to console him He spoke:

@166
Jagadānanda  dear  to Me  is not  than you  more
of propriety  the transgression  I  not  can  bear
= “Jagadānanda is not dearer to Me than you. But I cannot bear a breach of propriety.

@167
where  you  an authority  in the scriptures  experienced
where  Jagā  yesterday’s  boy  a novice
= Think what you are — an authority, versed in the scriptures! And what is Jagā — a boy of yesterday, a novice!

@168
even Me  to convince  you  possess  the power
in how many  places  you have made Me understand  propriety in devotion
= You have the power to convince even Me. On so many occasions you have made Me understand the propriety of devotional conduct (*vyavahāra-bhakti*).

@169
you  advice  he gives  not  can be  borne
therefore  him  I  make  rebuke
= That he should advise you is more than I can bear; that is why I rebuke him.

@170
as an outsider  regarding  you  not  I  praise
by your  qualities  praise  you make Me give  such are  your  qualities
= I do not praise you because I regard you as an outsider. Your qualities make Me praise you — such are your qualities.

@171
although  of someone  the sense of “mine”  toward many people  is
by love’s nature  toward someone  some  feeling arises
= Although one may feel many people to be one’s own, by the nature of love a particular feeling arises toward each.

@172
your  body  you  make  as loathsome  the notion
your  body  to Me  seems  like nectar
= You regard your body as loathsome, but to Me your body is like nectar.

@173
transcendental  body  your  material  ever  is not
nevertheless  your  about it  material  notion  is
= Your body is transcendental (*aprākṛta*); it is never material. Still, you think of it as material.

@174
material  even if it were  your  body  I cannot  disregard
of good and bad  in things  the perception  there is not  in the transcendental
= Even if your body were material, I could not disregard it, for in what is transcendental there is no perception of things as good or bad.

@175
what  good  what  bad  or
of duality  of the unreal  how much
by speech  spoken  that  untrue
by the mind  conceived  indeed  and
= “What is good and what is bad, and how much of either, in duality, which is unreal? Whatever is spoken by speech or conceived by the mind is untrue.” (Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam, 11.28.4)

@176
in duality  of good and bad  the notion  all  mental speculation
this  good  this  bad  all this  illusion
= In duality the notion of good and bad is all mental speculation (*mano-dharma*). ‘This is good, this is bad’ — all this is illusion.

@177

in a dog  and indeed  in a dog-eater  and
the wise  see equally
= “The wise see with an equal eye a *brāhmaṇa* endowed with learning and humility, a cow, an elephant, a dog and a dog-eater.” (Bhagavad-gītā, 5.18)

@178

= “One whose self is satisfied with knowledge and realization, who is unchanging (*kūṭastha*) and has conquered the senses, and to whom a clod of earth, a stone and gold are the same, is called a yogī established in yoga.” (Bhagavad-gītā, 6.8)

@179
I  indeed  am a sannyāsī  My  equal vision  the duty

= I am a *sannyāsī*; equal vision is My duty. Sandal paste and mud are the same to Me.

@180

= For this reason it is not right for Me to reject you. If I were to feel disgust, I would fall from My own duty.”
