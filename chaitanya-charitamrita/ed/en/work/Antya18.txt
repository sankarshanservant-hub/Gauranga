# 25 bhinna-setuḥ: of the elephant (“having broken through the dikes”) and of Kṛṣṇa (“beyond the bounds of worldly convention”), as in the Anubhāṣya and the Amṛta-pravāha-bhāṣya.

@title Śrī Caitanya in the Waters of the Ocean

@1
of the autumn  moonlit  ocean  by the sight  arisen  the Yamunā
from the mistaking  running  who  into this  from Hari  separation’s  burning  ocean  as if
plunged  unconscious  in the water  remaining  the night  whole
at dawn  found  by His own  may protect  He  Śacī’s  son  here  us
= Seeing the ocean in the light of the autumn moon, He took it for the Yamunā and ran into it. He plunged into it as if into an ocean of the burning pain of separation from Hari, lay unconscious in its waters the whole night, and at dawn was found by His own devotees. May that son of Śacī protect us here.

@2
glory  glory  to Śrī Caitanya  glory  to Nityānanda
glory  to Advaitacandra  glory  to Gaura’s  devotees  host
= Glory, glory to Śrī Caitanya! Glory to Nityānanda! Glory to Advaitacandra, and glory to all the devotees of Gaura!

@3

night  and day  of Kṛṣṇa  separation  in the ocean  floats
= In this way Mahāprabhu lived in Nīlācala, floating night and day in the ocean of separation from Kṛṣṇa.

@4
autumn  season’s  night  all  with moonlight  bright

= It was an autumn night, bright everywhere with moonlight, and the Lord wandered the whole night with His companions.

@5

= He wandered from garden to garden, watching wonders and reciting and hearing songs and verses about the *rāsa* dance.

@6

= Absorbed in love, the Lord sang and danced, and at times, absorbed in love, He enacted the *rāsa-līlā*.

@7

on the ground  falling  sometimes  swoons  sometimes  rolling  goes
= Sometimes, in the madness of ecstatic feeling, the Lord ran here and there; sometimes He fell to the ground and swooned, and sometimes He rolled about.

@8

= Whenever He recited or heard a verse about the *rāsa-līlā*, He Himself would explain its meaning, as before.

@9

= In this way He explained the meaning of every verse about the *rāsa-līlā*, feeling now joy, now sorrow.

@10

= To describe the meaning of all those verses and all those ecstatic transformations would make the book far too long.

@11

= For fear of making the book too long, I have not written down all the pastimes that took place moment by moment during those twelve years.

@12

= As I have only pointed out the direction before, so take this description of His ecstatic transformations and ravings.

@13

= Even if Ananta were to describe them with His thousand mouths, He could not reach the end of the pastimes of a single day.

@14

= Even if Gaṇeśa were to write for ten million ages, he could not come to the end of the pastimes of a single day.

@15

Kṛṣṇa  whose  not  finds  end  who  insignificant  else
= Seeing the transformations of love in His devotee, even Kṛṣṇa is struck with wonder! When Kṛṣṇa Himself cannot find their end, what of anyone else, so insignificant?

@16-17

Kṛṣṇa  that  fully  not  can  know
a devotee’s  mood  accepts  that  to taste
= Kṛṣṇa cannot fully know all the states of His devotee’s love, its courses and its kinds, all its sorrow, all its happiness and all its transformations. So, to taste them, He accepts the mood of a devotee.

@18

= Love makes Kṛṣṇa dance and makes the devotee dance, and it dances itself: all three dance together in one place.

@19

@20

@21

= Moment by moment endless waves of love rise up. How could an insignificant living being ever find their end?

@22

= What Śrī Kṛṣṇa Caitanya tasted only Svarūpa and the others of His inner circle know.

@23

= If anyone, being an ordinary living being, describes it, he only touches a single drop of it, to purify himself.

@24
in this way  of the rāsa  verses  all  recited

= In this way the Lord recited all the verses about the *rāsa* dance, and at the end He began to recite a verse about the water pastimes:

@25
with them  accompanied  fatigue  to remove  of bodies  by the contact
crushed  garland’s  He  from the breasts  with kuṅkuma  reddened
by bees like the lords of the Gandharvas  followed  entered  the water
weary  with she-elephants  the king of elephants  like  having broken the dikes
= “Weary, Kṛṣṇa entered the water with the gopīs to relieve His fatigue, as a tired king of elephants, having broken through the dikes, enters the water with his she-elephants: for He too had passed beyond the bounds of worldly convention. Bees followed Him, singing like the lords of the Gandharvas, drawn by His garland, crushed by the contact of their bodies and reddened with the *kuṅkuma* from their breasts.” (Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam, 10.33.22)
