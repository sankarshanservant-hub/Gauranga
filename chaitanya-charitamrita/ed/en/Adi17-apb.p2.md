## 126

From the coming of Bakhtiyar Khilji up to the time of Cāṅd Kāzī, Hinduism in Navadvīpa had sunk into utter decline. Those who truly had faith in the Hindu religion would quietly say “Hari, Hari” once and then fall silent. That is why the Kazi said, “For so long Hinduism has not shown itself at all — by whose strength are you now undertaking such an effort?”

## 140

Then, by the strength of Śrī Śrī Gauracandra, the people grew bold and went almost mad.

## 148

In one part of the village of Brāhmaṇa-puṣkariṇī the old house of the Kazis still stands. Joined to the other part of that village is Tāraṇavāsa, formerly Bilva-puṣkariṇī. That village and the Kazis' Brāhmaṇa-puṣkariṇī were one and the same village, and so Mahāprabhu stood to Cāṅd Kāzī in the relation of a sister's son to a maternal uncle.

## 156–163

(The Kazi said:) That scripture, the Koran, distinguishes two kinds of path: *pravṛtti* (the path of engagement) and *nivṛtti* (the path of renunciation). On the path of *nivṛtti* killing living beings is forbidden. But those who, like us, follow the path of *pravṛtti* do not become sinners if they kill cows by the order of scripture. And look at your Vedas: they contain injunctions to kill cows, and that is why great sages have killed cows from time immemorial.

Mahāprabhu said: The Vedas contain no injunction to kill cows. The statements about sacrifice performed by killing a cow that one does find in them concern the *jaradgava*, that is, a very old cow. The sages would kill a *jaradgava* and then, by Vedic mantras, bring it back to life in a youthful body. Such killing is no killing at all; it is only a kindness to the old cow. Since the brāhmaṇas of Kali have no such power, cows may not be killed now.

## 164

The *aśvamedha*, the *gomedha*, *sannyāsa*, a *śrāddha* to the forefathers with meat, and begetting a son by the husband's brother — these five are forbidden in the age of Kali.

## 169–171

The scriptures of the *yavanas* are of three kinds: the old book of the “Yu” (the Jews), the Koran and the Bible. All these books have a beginning; none of them is beginningless like the words of the Veda. Therefore, since the reasoning in those scriptures has no firm root, it is open to doubt.

## 195

Even though the Pādśāh is your relative, he can punish you. The Pādśāh — the Pādśāh of Gauḍa, “Husain” Shah.

## 196–202

The Kazi said: “O Gaurahari, when I questioned that *mleccha* peon, he answered: ‘I said to the Hindus, “Some of you, calling yourselves Kṛṣṇadāsa, Rāmadāsa or Haridāsa, keep saying ‘Hari, Hari.’ But the word *hari, hari* means ‘I steal, I steal,’ so it seems you say ‘Hari, Hari’ (‘I take away, I take away’) because you mean to steal other people's wealth.” Ever since the day I joked with them like this, my tongue keeps saying “Hari, Hari” against my will, and I can do nothing about it.’”

## 211

***Nīca bāḍa bāḍa***: he performs Kṛṣṇa's *kīrtana* with many low-born people, and by this the low are “growing,” that is, rising[^211-1].

## 222

***Tālāka*** — a solemnly made vow.

## 229

One night Mahāprabhu was performing *kīrtana* in Śrīvāsa's courtyard, and at that time one of Śrīvāsa's sons departed from this world. Fearing that the *rasa* of the *kīrtana* would be broken, Śrīvāsa forbade everyone to show any grief, and Mahāprabhu danced and sang *kīrtana* until late in the night. When the *kīrtana* ended, Mahāprabhu could understand that some misfortune had befallen the house. Learning that Śrīvāsa's son had departed, the Lord first expressed sorrow that He had not been told sooner. Then He had the dead boy laid before Him and asked him, “Boy, why are you leaving Śrīvāsa?” The dead boy replied, “I was destined to stay in Śrīvāsa's house for a certain number of days. Those days are over, and now by Your will I am going elsewhere. I am a *jīva*, eternally subordinate to You and dependent on You, and I have no right to do anything beyond Your will.” Hearing the dead boy's words, Śrīvāsa's family gained divine knowledge, and no grief remained in them. Then the boy's funeral rites were performed. The Lord said to Śrīvāsa, “The son you had has gone. But Nityānanda and I are your eternal sons; We can never leave you.”

## 231–232

A *yavana* tailor who lived near Śrīvāsa used to sew his clothes. When he watched Mahāprabhu's dancing with faith and was charmed, the Lord showed him the spiritual (*cinmaya*) nature of His own form. The tailor began to dance, mad with *prema*, crying, “I have seen! I have seen!” ***Āgala*** — foremost.

## 241

One night in the house of Śrī Candraśekhara Ācāryaratna the Lord assumed the form of Rukmiṇī and others and enacted a pastime. Advaita, Haridāsa and many others also put on various costumes.

## 250

***Doṣodgāra*** — blaming with mockery.

## 265

According to the scriptures, when a brāhmaṇa accepts *sannyāsa*, everyone — householders, brāhmaṇas and all the rest — bows to him with the thought of a *sannyāsī*, that is, knowing that a *sannyāsī* is worthy of obeisance. If I accept *sannyāsa*, the blaspheming brāhmaṇas will surely bow down to Me and receive good sense from Me.

## 272

At the end of Mahāprabhu's twenty-fourth year, on the day of the *saṅkramaṇa* (the sun's passage into a new sign) that begins the *uttarāyaṇa* and that fell in the bright fortnight of Māgha, Mahāprabhu left Śrī Navadvīpa at the end of the night. He swam across the Gaṅgā at “Nidayā's Ghāṭa,” reached Kaṇṭakanagara, that is, the village of Kāṭoyā, and accepted (one) *daṇḍa* from Keśava Bhāratī. On Mahāprabhu's order Candraśekhara Ācāryaratna performed all the ritual parts of *sannyāsa*. The *kīrtana* went on all day, and only when the day was nearly over was the shaving of the head completed. The next morning Śrī Kṛṣṇa Caitanya, dressed as a *sannyāsī* and bearing the *daṇḍa*, set out to wander through Rāḍha-deśa. Keśava Bhāratī went with Him for some distance.

## 275

***Caturvidha bhakta-bhāva*** (the four kinds of devotees' moods) — the four kinds of devotional mood based on the *rasas* of *dāsya*, *sakhya*, *vātsalya* and *madhura*.

## 281

Once Śrī Kṛṣṇa, in jest, manifested the four-armed form of Nārāyaṇa, endowed with wondrous beauty — and the love (*rāga*) of the *gopīs*, instead of awakening, shrank. So which *paṇḍita* can understand the workings of the *gopīs'* *bhāva*? For they follow the inaccessible path of *parakīya*, worshipping the son of Nanda alone.

## 293

At the beginning of the *rāsa*, Kṛṣṇa, in play, hid in a *kuñja*. Seeing the doe-eyed *gopīs* coming toward Him, He grew anxious and displayed His charming four-armed form. The ordinary *gopīs* said only, “This is not Śrī Kṛṣṇa, the object of our love.” But how wondrous is the glory of Rādhā's love! As soon as Śrī Rādhā arrived, Kṛṣṇa, try as He might, could not keep that four-armed form.

## 308

The truth that lies beyond *prakṛti* — that is the mark of the inconceivable. Logic (*tarka*) is material, and so it cannot touch that truth. Therefore one should not apply logic to what is inconceivable.

*Thus ends the seventeenth chapter of the “Amṛta-pravāha-bhāṣya.”*
