## 195

***Pātsāha*** (the Pādśāh) — ʿAlāʾ-ud-dīn Sayyid Husain Shah (1498–1511 A.D.), at that time the independent ruler of Bengal. He killed his patron and master, the terribly oppressive Nawab Muzaffar Khan of the Abyssinian (Habshi) dynasty, and ascended the throne of Bengal. On taking the *masnad* of Bengal he assumed the name “Sayyid Husain ʿAlāʾ-ud-dīn Sharīf Makka.” Ghulam Husain, the author of the chronicle *Riyāẓ-us-Salāṭīn*, says that one of Nawab Husain Shah's ancestors had been the Sharif of Mecca, and so he probably took that name in memory of his family's glory. In the stone inscriptions of Gauḍa, however, he is known simply as “Husain Shah.” After his death his eldest son Nasrat Shah became Nawab of Bengal (1521–1533 A.D.). This cruel-natured nawab terribly oppressed the Vaiṣṇavas and, as a result of his own sins, was killed in a mosque by one of his officials.

## 198–202

***Parihāsa*** (mockery) is one of the four kinds of *nāmābhāsa*, as it is said: “Chanting the name of Vaikuṇṭha — to indicate something else, in jest, for the sake of a tune, or with disregard — destroys all sins: so the wise know.” (*Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam* 6.2.14) Yet *nāmābhāsa* based on indication, mockery, musical filler or disregard is not merely the material pronunciation of syllables. Directed toward the eternal, real Substance, *nāmābhāsa* awakens remembrance of Viṣṇu, and so it destroys the *jīva's* desire for sense objects. As a result, the liberated *jīva*, now turned toward service, gains the qualification to utter the pure name.

## 203

> Subheading: The *smārta pāṣaṇḍīs* complain to the Kazi —

***Pāṣaṇḍī*** — a dull follower of *karma* who believes in many gods, an enemy of Viṣṇu and the Vaiṣṇavas, an idolater.

## 211–212

These polytheists (*bahv-īśvara-vādīs*) are called by the word *pāṣaṇḍa* because they considered the Lord, or the Lord's name, “a part of *karma*” (*karmāṅga*). They did not know how supremely magnanimous and glorious Kṛṣṇa's name is. Blinded by worldly high birth and social standing, they thought: if a low person, that is, one born in a low family, takes Kṛṣṇa's name, that is a special kind of sin; therefore taking Kṛṣṇa's name depends on good family or high birth. These polytheists equated the *mahā-mantra*, Kṛṣṇa's name, with other mantras for *japa*, and thought: if the *mahā-mantra*, which is to be chanted always, is spoken or sung aloud — that is, if it suddenly comes onto the tongue and reaches the ear — then instead of revealing its own matchless supreme majesty and delivering everyone up to Brahmā, it becomes fruitless. So opposed are they to the path of the Śrutis, these arguers whose reasoning rests on sense knowledge (*akṣaja-hetuvādīs*)!
