## 70

The humble Vidura was the pillow for the feet of Kṛṣṇa, the Thousand-headed Puruṣa. When he spoke thus, the sage Maitreya, his hairs standing on end with the bliss of speaking about Bhagavān, began to speak.

## 72

**ughāḍa-aṅge** — with the body uncovered.

## 76

In separation from Vraja, which was as dear to Him as countless lives, the incoherent words of madness arose in Him, and this state kept growing stronger. Therefore Gauracandra, His mind agitated, rubbed His moonlike face against the wall day after day and bore the blood that flowed from the wounds. This Gaurāṅgadeva, rising in my heart, is driving me mad.

## 91

The fragrance of His body conquers musk, and by its waves He draws the hearts of women to Himself. From the eight parts of His body, the eight lotuses, He spreads the fragrance of a lotus mixed with camphor. He is anointed with the scents of musk, camphor, sandalwood and *aguru*. O friend, this Madana-mohana is increasing the longing of My nostrils.

**hema-kīlita** — set in gold; **curi** — concealment (hiding).

**bāurī** — maddened.

## 101

**kṛṣṇadāsa rūpa-gosāñi-bhṛtya** — Kṛṣṇadāsa, the servant of Rūpa Gosvāmī. Reading this line, many think that Śrī Kṛṣṇadāsa Kavirāja Gosvāmī was a disciple of Rūpa Gosvāmī who received *mantra* from him. But when one reads other passages, it is hard to reach that conclusion. Here Śrīla Kavirāja Prabhu unfolds *rasa* by following the teaching of Śrī Rūpa's *Bhakti-rasāmṛta-sindhu*, and so he may have taken only Śrī Rūpa's name. Or the meaning may also be this: the author, Kṛṣṇadāsa, the servant of the Gosvāmīs, composed this line.

*Thus ends the nineteenth chapter of the “Amṛta-pravāha-bhāṣya.”*
