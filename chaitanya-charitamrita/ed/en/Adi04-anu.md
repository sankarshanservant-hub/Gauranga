## 20

> Subheading: (*Śrīmad Bhagavad-gītā*, Chapter 4, verse 11)

The teaching on *yoga* that Bhagavān once gave to Sūrya came down through the succession and in time became distorted. Then He gave the same teaching again to Arjuna. Bhagavān speaks this verse while explaining the reason for His manifest pastimes:

**[he] pārtha** (O Arjuna), **ye** (the devotees) **yathā** (with whatever feeling) **mām** (to Me, Kṛṣṇa) **prapadyante** (surrender), **ahaṁ tathaiva tān bhajāmi** (in that very way I show them mercy). **manuṣyāḥ sarvaśaḥ** (people, in every way) **mama vartma** (My path — the path to perfection) **anuvartante** (follow).

## 21

In the *Śrī Caitanya-caritāmṛta*, alongside the words “*bhakti*” and “*śuddha-bhakti*,” we also find the word “*viddha-bhakti*.” From this we see that devotion is divided into three kinds. *Viddha-bhakti*, “pierced” or mixed devotion, is the name for service to Hari joined with other desires, covered by *jñāna*, *karma*, *yoga* and the like, and accompanied by the pursuit of enjoying things unrelated to Kṛṣṇa. Covered attempts at service — mixed with *karma*, with *jñāna*, with *yoga*, with vows aimed at enjoyment and so on — belong to *viddha-bhakti*: in them there are endeavors other than pure devotion. When one follows rules that consist of unmixed service, “*bhakti*” arises. This devotion is distinct from *viddha-bhakti* and consists of endeavors favorable to Viṣṇu. But loving service that springs from greed (*lobha*) to follow the causeless, eternal service of Hari rendered by the *rāgātmika* devotees — that is *śuddha-bhakti*. It is not driven by rules alone. “*Vaidhī-bhakti*,” or “*bhakti*,” or “*aviddha-bhakti*” helps *śuddha-bhakti*, yet the word “*śuddha-bhakti*” points specifically to *rāgānugā* service. Among the stages of devotion *śuddha-bhakti* can be called “the highest limit” (*parākāṣṭhā*). It is devotion full of *rāga*, and it abides in Goloka. *Vaidhī-bhakti*, devotion full of reverence, abides in Paravyoma, or Vaikuṇṭha.

## 23

> Subheading: (*Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam*, Canto 10, Chapter 82, verse 44)

At the time of a solar eclipse the Yādavas came to Samanta-pañcaka from Dvārakā, and Nanda Mahārāja came from Vraja with all his people. When Śrī Kṛṣṇa met the *gopīs* of Vraja, the residents of Gokula, He said to them:

**mayi** (to Me) **bhūtānām** (of living beings) **bhaktiḥ** (devotion — hearing, chanting and the rest) **amṛtatvāya** (for immortality — for the position of an eternal associate) **kalpate** (makes fit). **bhavatīnām** (in you, the *gopīs*) **mad-āpanaḥ** (which brings about meeting Me face to face) **mat-snehaḥ** (love for Me) **yad āsīt** (has arisen), **tad diṣṭyā** (that is truly My good fortune).

## 26

Sometimes the *āśraya*, under the sway of pure *anurāga* and regarding the *viṣaya* as her very own, speaks harsh words to Him, like a scolding. Such words reveal the utmost and exclusive love (*prīti*). But where the *viṣaya* is regarded as worshipable and as a superior (*guru*), natural love is slackened to a greater or lesser degree. The Vedic scriptures have laid down injunctions and prohibitions for ignorant people devoid of love. Compare the respectful words that befit people bound by rules with words born of love. The latter lack reverential worship, yet in excellence they surpass *vaidhī* hymns of praise. A pure devotee, free from every object other than Kṛṣṇa, is more intimately related to Bhagavān. The eternal function (*nitya-vṛtti*) that awakens with awareness of such a relationship is in every respect more precious than *vaidhī* reverence, in which majesty predominates.

## 29

In the realms beyond *māyā* — Vaikuṇṭha and the others — a variety of Bhagavān's pastimes is manifest. Yet there the wonder of Kṛṣṇa, the original form (*svayaṁ-rūpa*), does not become ever new. Above those realms is Goloka, where pastimes are manifest whose aim is the *svayaṁ-rūpa*'s own happiness. He wished to manifest such pastimes in the material world in order to show their excellence to devotees for whom majesty is foremost. The *āśraya* herself understands that by the rules she owes greater reverence to another than to the *viṣaya*. Yet she deceives and abandons the one to whom her reverence is due by the rules and strives toward Kṛṣṇa — under the sway of *anurāga* for Him, drawn by a sweetness free of any trace of majesty. This urge to serve as *parakīyā* is brought about by *yogamāyā*. Such a working of the spiritual (*cinmayī*) *māyā* is beyond the comprehension even of the *viṣaya*; it is possible only through *yogamāyā*, the embodiment of His mercy.

## 30

When one experiences the feeling that the Lord (*Īśvara*) has toward those subject to Him, one cannot perceive how the *āśraya* strives to astonish the *viṣaya*. That is why, in describing what is special about *yogamāyā*, the author calls her the helper of those who belong to the *āśraya*. The *viṣaya* and the *āśraya*, each experiencing his or her own feeling, are drawn to each other when they come to perceive the other's feeling. People who wander in the external world cannot enter this variety of pastimes. Until the *jīva* reaches that state, or at least acquires a taste for it, the good fortune of experiencing it does not dawn for him.

## 31

Leaving aside the dignified *dharma* of rules, Kṛṣṇa and the *gopīs*, drawn to each other, overcome every obstacle and meet. At that time their duties under the rules fall still, and, kindling each other, they cannot help but meet. To bring their meeting to its fullness it is sometimes nourished by the *rasa* of *vipralambha*, separation. In the material, inert world there are qualities such as undesirability, and therefore *vipralambha* there appears as mere absence. But in the transcendental realm, in the time of separation, the wonder born of complete absorption (*samāveśa*) increases. In meeting, the sense that the desired one is present slackens somewhat. In separation, however, the longing to be united with those feelings is stronger, and so separation brings forth even greater wonder.

## 33

Śrī Raghunātha Dāsa Gosvāmī Prabhu in his *Manaḥ-śikṣā* — “Do not perform the *dharma* or the *adharma* spoken of by the Śrutis”[^33-1] — and the emperor Śrī Kulaśekhara in his prayer *Mukunda-mālā* — “I have no attachment to *dharma*, to amassing wealth or to sensual pleasure. Let whatever must be, according to my past deeds, come to pass, O Bhagavān. This alone I pray for, this I prize above all: birth after birth may I have unwavering devotion to Your two lotus feet” — in these and similar verses have described *rāga-bhakti*, which lies beyond *svadharma*. “Whoever, understanding merits and faults, gives up all his own duties, even those I have taught, and worships Me — he too is the best of the virtuous.” (*Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam* 11.11.32)

> Subheading: (*Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam*, Canto 10, Chapter 33, verse 36)

**bhaktānām** (to the servants of Hari abiding in their various *rasas*) **anugrahāya** (to distribute mercy) **mānuṣaṁ deham** (a body befitting a human being — a supreme transcendental body[^34-1]) **āśritaḥ** (having assumed) **tādṛśīḥ krīḍāḥ bhajate** (performs such pastimes), **yāḥ** (pastimes) **śrutvā** (on hearing [of which] even another person gains faith in Bhagavān and) **tat-paraḥ** (devoted to the service of Kṛṣṇa) **bhavet** (becomes).

## 34–35

Bhagavān, full of endless pastimes, eternally abides in His many forms of manifestation (*prakāśa*). Devī-dhāma, the material world, is a distorted reflection of Goloka and Vaikuṇṭha. The variety within the material world resembles that of Goloka and Vaikuṇṭha, but it has limitation, inferiority, insignificance or undesirability, and subjection to time. The many manifestations of the *viṣaya-vigraha* abide eternally in relationships of service suited to the different classes of *jīvas* who depend on Him (*āśrita*). In Vaikuṇṭha there is pure *sattva*; in the material world one sees mixed *sattva*, made of the *guṇas*. Because the *viṣaya* and the *āśraya* eternally experience each other, the variety of pastimes is eternal. Judged by what suits the *āśraya*, the saying “The human body is the root of worship” is justified. In the material world the human race stands on a high rung of creation. *Jīvas* belonging to the *āśraya*, while they dwell in the material world, gain the right to serve the *viṣaya-vigraha* suited to them.

Besides His human form, Bhagavān also has many nonhuman forms. The variety of pastimes depends on how the object of worship appears as the *jīva*'s original nature (*svarūpa-vṛtti*) awakens. If one compares these pastimes by what suits [the *āśraya*], the greatest mercy is given to devotees who have taken shelter of the eternal pastimes in human form. When such pastimes descend into the material world, the best of human beings become eager to serve the object of service, each in the way that suits him. Hearing how the object of worship is experienced at the summit of worship gives the *jīva* enormous help in the unfolding of his original nature. Of the five kinds of *rati*, or *sthāyi-bhāva*, the highest is *madhura-rati*. Joined with all the ingredients of *rasa*, it manifests the best of all *rasas*, and only a devotee who has acquired taste (*ruci*) is entitled to it. To make it easier to acquire taste, Bhagavān manifests in the world the pastimes of Rāma and others instead of the pastimes of Matsya, Kūrma, Varāha and others. Yet in the human pastimes of Rāma and the rest the wonder of *rasa* does not reach its full strength. That wonder is almost beyond the reach of the *jīva*'s intellect and is extremely rare; still, *parakīyā madhura-rati*, when the degrees of majesty and sweetness are compared, manifests it as incomparable and ever new.

The *sahajikas*, whose intelligence is worldly, cannot understand the teaching of the transcendental *sahaja-dharma* and bring licentiousness into it. By this they do not point to the variety of Goloka, which consists of pure *sattva*; they only drag an impure heart down into sense gratification. In the transcendental pastimes there is service to Adhokṣaja. The *prākṛta-sahajikas* do not understand this, and in their error they take service to Kṛṣṇa for sense gratification aimed at enjoyment. Bhagavān's pastimes that have descended into the material world are by no means a playground for the *prākṛta-sahajikas*. Kṛṣṇa's *rāsa* and His other pastimes are created by *yogamāyā*, and worldly reasoning cannot see them properly. The Sahajiyā sect regards Kṛṣṇa's pastimes as one more perishable enjoyment. They distort the meaning of the words *tat-paratvena nirmalam*[^34-2] and *tat-paro bhavet* and merely throw the rubbish of the mundane onto the transcendental. Mistaking the meaning of the words *tādṛśīḥ krīḍāḥ* (“such pastimes”), they sink into sense gratification. In truth, however, the primary meaning of the word *tādṛśī* is transcendental *rati*. The *jīva* seized by ignorance (*avidyā*) and turned away from Hari rejects the transcendental pastimes and, intoxicated with material enjoyment through knowledge gained by the senses, misinterprets this verse. As soon as *vivarta* — taking one thing for another — appears at the stage of *sādhana* or of perfection, the *jīva* becomes a *prākṛta-sahajiyā*.

Seeing the word *bhavet* in the *vidhi-liṅ* form, let no one think that this *rāgānugā* path, attained through taste, is a prescribed (*vaidha*) path for all regardless of qualification, even for a pleasure-seeker full of *anarthas*. In the material world there is consideration of what should and should not be done. In Goloka-Vṛndāvana such rules[^34-3] cannot exist. There, on the path of *anurāga*, all the *āśrita-tattvas*, under the sway of greed (*lobha*), seek only one desirable thing — Kṛṣṇa's pleasure.

The *madhura-bhāva* is the highest [*rati*] in the material world; the *jīva*-soul must eternally and necessarily serve in it. If anyone is indifferent to it, he will certainly abandon service to Bhagavān and fall into perishable worldly debauchery. If the *jīva* does not devote himself to *madhura-rati*, the base doctrine of material enjoyment, the opposite of *madhura-rati*, will prevail in him. In the same way, if in *vātsalya-rati* the *jīva* turns away from serving Kṛṣṇa, the urge to enjoy will drag him down into perishable affection for a son. In the same way, if one does not regard Kṛṣṇa as one's only friend, perishable friends devoted to the senses will come and drag the *jīva* down. In the same way, having turned away from Bhagavān, the *jīva* will become indifferent to serving Kṛṣṇa. He will play the servant to a perishable body that serves the senses for enjoyment, and he will forget his true nature. In the same way, if the mind does not find equanimity (*nirapekṣā*) in Kṛṣṇa, the *jīva* will turn away from Kṛṣṇa and become indifferent to matter. That is, he will become a slave of *mokṣa*, or *nirvāṇa*, which is called “turning to stone,” and become an impersonalist. Whoever is indifferent to entering Kṛṣṇa's pastimes will be dominated by an intelligence devoted to the senses and bent on enjoyment. Because of this his false ego, founded on designations, will swell in good and bad deeds alike and deprive him of the ultimate good.

## 41

Wishing to manifest His pastimes of magnanimity (*audārya-līlā*), Kṛṣṇa revealed in the material world His eternal pastimes as Gaura. In the eternal pastimes of Gaura, Kṛṣṇa is in the mood of a devotee, and this is the wonder of His eternal pastimes. Kṛṣṇa, the original form, brought the eternal pastimes of Gaura down into the world and made the service of Kṛṣṇa, the object of service, easily accessible to the *jīvas*. The debauched sect of *prākṛta-sahajikas* does not know the true nature of transcendental *vipralambha*. They pervert the meaning of Śrī Gaurasundara's acceptance of the mood of a devotee and wish illegitimately to present Him as a form of *sambhoga* (enjoyment in union). They adorn themselves with invented titles such as “Nadīyā-nāgarī” or “Gaura-nāgarī” and wipe out the mood of the devotee, that is, of the *āśraya*, in the *rasa* of eternal separation. Such outrages do not please Kṛṣṇa, the original form. Śrī Gaura-vigraha, the original form, does not bestow mercy on these *prākṛta-sahajikas* devoted to enjoyment but keeps them far away from Himself. To apply the *sambhoga* considerations of Kṛṣṇa's pastimes to Him whose pastimes are full of the *rasa* of *vipralambha* is an attempt to destroy devotion to Kṛṣṇa. It is nothing but hatred of Śrī Gaura.

## 60

> Subheading: The characteristics of the *hlādinī-śakti* —

Śrī Jīva Prabhu writes in the *Prīti-sandarbha* (section 65): “And in the Śruti it is heard: ‘Devotion leads him [to the Lord], devotion shows Him to him, the Puruṣa is subject to devotion, devotion is the greatest of all.’ Let us therefore consider: what are the characteristics of that [power] which thus intoxicates Bhagavān with its bliss? It is not the bliss of *māyā*, made of material *sattva*, as with the Sāṅkhyas, for the Śruti says that *māyā* cannot overpower Bhagavān and that He is satisfied in Himself. Nor is it the bliss of Bhagavān's own nature, as the impersonalists hold, for then it could not surpass [His own bliss]. Still less is it the bliss of the *jīva*'s own nature, for the *jīva* is exceedingly small. One possibility remains. According to the *Viṣṇu Purāṇa* — ‘*Hlādinī*, *sandhinī* and *saṁvit* are one in You, the support of all; [the potency] that gives joy, pain and a mixture of the two is not in You, who are free from the *guṇas*’ — it is the bliss of His *svarūpa-śakti* called *hlādinī*. Through her Bhagavān possesses the special bliss of His own nature, and through her alone He lets others too taste this or that bliss. But *hlādinī* too abides always in Bhagavān, and so she too could not surpass [Him]. Therefore one must reason thus, on the basis of *arthāpatti*, the inference of that without which the meaning of the Śruti cannot be explained. A certain function (*vṛtti*) of that very *hlādinī*, surpassing all bliss, is eternally deposited in the hosts of devotees and abides there under the name ‘love for Bhagavān’ (*bhagavat-prīti*). Therefore, by experiencing it, Śrī Bhagavān too feels an exceptional love for His devotees.”

The Vedas too say that devotion leads the devotee to Bhagavān, that devotion lets the devotee see Bhagavān, that Bhagavān is subject to devotion — and there the supremacy of devotion is declared. Therefore the question is examined: what are the characteristics of that *vastu-śakti*, the potency of the Reality itself, which intoxicates Bhagavān with His own bliss? The Śruti's answer is this. *Māyā* cannot overpower Bhagavān, and Bhagavān is satisfied in Himself. Therefore this *vastu-śakti* cannot be called, as the Sāṅkhyas teach, the bliss of *māyā* endowed with material being. Nor can it be called, as the impersonalists say, the bliss of Bhagavān's own nature: considered from beginning to end, that conclusion is especially untenable. Still less is it the bliss of the *jīva*'s own nature: the *jīva*, though eternal, is exceedingly small. Therefore [it remains to say] that the bliss is His *svarūpa-śakti* named *hlādinī*. Such is the sense of the words of the *Viṣṇu Purāṇa*: “Only in the all-powerful Bhagavān do the three potencies — *hlādinī*, *sandhinī* and *saṁvit* — abide. O Bhagavān, in You, who are free from the *guṇas*, there is no feeling of joy mixed with pain.” For only through this potency is a special bliss seen in Bhagavān's own nature, and only through it does Bhagavān bestow this or that bliss on other devotees. This is the final conclusion. Because the *hlādinī-śakti* abides eternally in Bhagavān, the impersonalist's conclusion just mentioned must be firmly rejected — this must be understood. For if the meaning of the Śruti cannot be reconciled otherwise, a different conclusion must be admitted. The testimony of the *Viṣṇu Purāṇa* agrees with the meaning of the Vedas, whereas the impersonalists' claim distorts the meaning of the Vedas and does not belong to what the Vedas intend. Therefore, when the eternal function of that very *hlādinī*, surpassing all bliss, is bestowed on the hosts of devotees, it receives the name *bhagavat-prīti*, love for Bhagavān. And Śrī Bhagavān, feeling that love in the devotee, accepts the devotee's love.

The *Viṣṇu Purāṇa* speaks of three kinds of potency in Śrī Bhagavān. Therefore the potency that gives bliss to Bhagavān is neither the material bliss of the Sāṅkhya nor mere undifferentiated spiritual bliss (*cid-ekānanda*), as with the impersonalist, who does not know the difference between the potency and its possessor. It is the *hlādinī-śakti* that gives Bhagavān bliss. Through the *hlādinī-śakti* Bhagavān endows the *jīva* with the *dharma* of love for Himself, and then, won over by the devotee's love for Bhagavān, He Himself nourishes that love.
