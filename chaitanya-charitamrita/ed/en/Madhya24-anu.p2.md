## 149

> Subheading: (1) *Sagarbha* yogīs and (2) *nirgarbha* yogīs, each of three kinds —

*Sagarbha* yogīs are those who rely on meditation on the form of the One they worship, and the like. *Nirgarbha* yogīs are those devoted to meditation on the void and the like, who have no support. The six divisions are: (1) the *sagarbha-yogārurukṣu*, (2) the *nirgarbha-yogārurukṣu*, (3) the *sagarbha-yogārūḍha*, (4) the *nirgarbha-yogārūḍha*, (5) the *sagarbha-prāpta-siddhi*, and (6) the *nirgarbha-prāpti-siddhi*.

## 150

> Subheading: Meditation on the third Puruṣa, Kṣīrodaśāyī —

Speaking to Parīkṣit, Śrī Śukadeva condemned the enjoyment of sense objects in the gross world and described the yogī who has turned away from it and is fixed in meditation on the universal form (*vairāja-dhāraṇā*). Now he describes a yogī superior to that one — the one who meditates on the form, full of spirit, of the Antaryāmī dwelling in each individual being (the *vyaṣṭi-antaryāmī*).

**kecit** (a rare few) **sva-dehāntar-hṛdayāvakāśe** (*sva-dehasya*, of one's own body; *antaḥ*, within, that is, in the heart; in its space) **vasantam** (dwelling there as the Antaryāmī) **prādeśa-mātram** (a *prādeśa* is the span from thumb to forefinger; He whose measure is such — for He is meditated upon in a heart the size of a *prādeśa*; yet even in a space of that size He, by His inconceivable potency, has the stature of a youth of fifteen) **catur-bhujaṁ** **kañja-rathāṅga-śaṅkha-gadā-dharam** (holding lotus, disc, conch and club) **puruṣam** (Kṣīrodaśāyī, the third Puruṣa) **dhāraṇayā** **smaranti**.

## 151

> Subheading: Devotion mixed with the yoga of meditation brings perfection —

Lord Śrī Kapiladeva explained to the surrendered Devahūti that the *nirgarbha* yogī must meditate on each limb and feature of the transcendental form of Śrī Bhagavān. Now He speaks of the fruit of meditation: control of the mind.

**evam** (on the path of meditation) **bhagavati harau** **pratilabdha-bhāvaḥ** (one who has attained *bhāva*, love) **bhaktyā** (with faith) **dravad-dhṛdayaḥ** (one whose heart melts, as if growing moist) **pramodāt** (from the excess of joy) **utpulakaḥ** (one whose hairs have risen — with the hairs of his body standing on end) **autkaṇṭhya-bāṣpa-kalayā** (with streams of tears born of longing) **muhuḥ** (again and again) **ardyamānaḥ** (drowning in a flood of bliss) **ca** **tat api citta-baḍiśam** (even that mind which served as the means to catch the hard-to-catch Bhagavān, like a hook for catching fish) **śanakaiḥ** (little by little — from the object of meditation) **viyuṅkte** (withdraws — he slackens his effort to hold Him in the mind).

## 160

> Subheading: One who controls the mind attains the supreme abode —

On Janaloka, at the *brahma-satra* sacrifice, Sanandana, a *brahmarṣi* and one of the four Kumāras, recited to sages eager to hear this hymn to Bhagavān, which the personified Śrutis had sung. Later the original *ṛṣi* Śrī Nārāyaṇa recounts it to Nārada.

**ṛṣi-vartmasu** (on the paths of the *ṛṣis*, the paths of the *sampradāyas*) **ye** **kūrpa-dṛśaḥ** (*kūrpa* is gravel dust; those who have it in their eyes — that is, those whose sight is covered with dust: the Śārkarākṣas, men of gross vision) **udaram** (Brahman in the *maṇipūra*, whose support is the belly) **upāsate** (meditate upon); **āruṇayaḥ** (the *ṛṣis* called Āruṇi) **parisara-paddhatim** (the *parisaras* are the *nāḍīs*, because they spread out in all directions; their *paddhati* is their path, the place where they spread) **hṛdayam** **daharam** (worship subtle Brahman, whose support is the space [of the heart]); **he ananta** **tataḥ** (from the heart) **tava** **paramam** (best, effulgent) **dhāma** (the place of realization, the *suṣumnā*) **śiraḥ** (to the head) **udagāt** (rose — that is, it rises from the *mūlādhāra*, through the heart, up to the *brahma-randhra*); **yat** (Your abode) **sametya** (having reached) **iha** **kṛtānta-mukhe** (into the jaws of Kṛtānta — Time, death — into *saṁsāra*) **na** **patanti**.[^160-1]

## 163

> Subheading: One must strive to attain the eternal, true Reality —

Śrī Vyāsadeva had performed much austerity, compiled all the scriptures and done much else, yet he had not found satisfaction of the soul. On the bank of the river Sarasvatī he indulged in his mind in various idle arguments and grieved. Then his guru, who knew his heart, Śrī Nārada Gosvāmī, came to him. Vyāsadeva asked him why his soul found no satisfaction. Śrī Nārada spoke to him of the glory of devotion to Hari and of narrations about Hari, and he tells him to strive for devotion to Kṛṣṇa, the eternal Truth.

**upari adhaḥ** (above, up to Brahmaloka; below, down to the immobile beings) **bhramatām** (a genitive in the sense of the instrumental: by *jīvas* who wander) **yat** (happiness) **na** **labhyate** (is never attained), **kovidaḥ** (one of discernment) **tasya eva** (for that very happiness) **hetoḥ** (for the sake of) **prayateta** (should strive); **gabhīra-raṁhasā** (irresistibly swift) **kālena** **duḥkha-vat** (just as unwanted sufferings come to embodied beings, so too) **tat** **sukham** (the happiness of sense pleasure) **anyataḥ** (from something else — from past *karma*) **sarvatra** (in every species of life, even without effort) **labhyate** (is attained).

## 170

> Subheading: The birds of Vraja, too, are sages devoted to Kṛṣṇa —

When autumn came to Vraja, Kṛṣṇa began to wander from forest to forest playing His flute. Hearing the sound of His flute, the *gopīs*, longing to be with Kṛṣṇa, sing:

**he amba** (O mother), **asmin vane** **ye** **vihagāḥ** (birds), **te** **prāyaḥ** (for the most part) **munayaḥ** **eva**, for **te** (the birds) **kṛṣṇekṣitam** (so as to see Kṛṣṇa with no screen of flowers, fruits and the like between) **rucira-pravālān** (having *rucira*, beautiful, *pravālas*, shoots — that is, with many-coloured young twigs) **druma-bhujān** (the branches of the trees) **āruhya** (with an indescribable happiness) **mīlita-dṛśaḥ** (closing their eyes) **vigatānya-vācaḥ** (giving up all other sounds) **tad-uditam** (manifested by Kṛṣṇa Himself) **kala-veṇu-gītam** (the sweet melody of the *muralī*) **śṛṇvanti**.

## 171

> Subheading: The bees of Vraja, too, are sages devoted to Kṛṣṇa —

Having entered the *paugaṇḍa* age, Bhagavān Śrī Kṛṣṇa once went with Śrī Balarāma into the Kusumākara forest. Seeing the beauty all around, He wished to sport, and praising His elder brother He says:

**he ādi-puruṣa** (the form endowed with the *sandhinī* potency, the cause of the existence of all beings), **ete** **alinaḥ** (bees) **tava** **akhila-loka-tīrtham** (purifying all the worlds) **yaśaḥ** **gāyantaḥ** **anupatham** (on every path) **bhajante**; **he anagha** (the form of the Lord of pure *sattva*), **amī** **bhavadīya-mukhyāḥ** (the foremost of Your own) **muni-gaṇāḥ** **vane** **gūḍham** (unrecognized) **api** **ātma-daivam** (their own Lord, You) **prāyaḥ** **na** **jahati** (do not leave; that is, while You are hidden in a human form, the sages, also hidden in the form of bees, serve You).

Here, in another reading, the verse “*nṛtyanty amī śikhina…*” (*Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam* 10.15.7) is found. Its meaning: “O worshipable one! Seeing that You have come to their home, the peacocks dance in supreme bliss; the does, like the *gopīs*, cast their glances at You, and the cuckoos please You with sweet song. Blessed are the residents of Vṛndāvana, for such is the nature of *sādhus* — to offer what is their own.”

## 172

> Subheading: The swans, *sārasa* cranes and other birds of Vraja, too, are sages devoted to Kṛṣṇa —

When Kṛṣṇa goes to the forest by day, the *gopīs*, burning in separation, sing this song to one another:

(“**yarhi** **adhare** **kṛṣṇaḥ** **saṁhita-veṇuḥ** **bhavati** **tarhi**” — so it is construed with the preceding verse.) **sarasi** (in the lake) **ye** **sārasa-haṁsa-vihaṅgāḥ** **cāru-gīta-hṛta-cetasaḥ** (whose hearts were stolen, drawn by the lovely song of the flute) **etya** (having come) **hanta** (alas) **yata-cittāḥ** (with controlled minds) **dhṛta-maunāḥ** (silent) **mīlita-dṛśaḥ** (with eyes closed) **harim** **upāsata** (served Him, or sat down beside Him).

## 173

> Subheading: By the mercy of pure devotees even impure castes are purified —

Having heard narrations of Hari from the lips of Śrī Śuka, Parīkṣit fixed his mind exclusively on Śrī Kṛṣṇa and asked how Bhagavān, the Lord of Māyā, creates the world, and about His other pastimes. In reply Śrī Śuka first bows to Bhagavān and offers a *maṅgalācaraṇa*:

**kirāta-hūṇāndhra-pulinda-pukkaśāḥ** **ābhīra-śumbhāḥ** **yavanāḥ** **khaśādayaḥ** (born in sinful castes) **anye** **ye** **pāpāḥ** (born in sinful castes because of their own past *karma*) **yad-upāśrayāśrayāḥ** (the *upāśrayas* of Bhagavān are the *bhāgavata* Vaiṣṇavas who have taken shelter of Him; those who take shelter of them — that is, by taking shelter of the devotees) **śudhyanti** (are purified), **tasmai** **prabhaviṣṇave** (to the all-powerful Bhagavān Viṣṇu) **namaḥ**.
