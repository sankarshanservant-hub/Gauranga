## 149

> Subheading: (1) *Sagarbha* yogīs and (2) *nirgarbha* yogīs, each of three kinds —

*Sagarbha* yogīs are those who rely on meditation on the form of the One they worship, and the like. *Nirgarbha* yogīs are those devoted to meditation on the void and the like, who have no support. The six divisions are: (1) the *sagarbha-yogārurukṣu*, (2) the *nirgarbha-yogārurukṣu*, (3) the *sagarbha-yogārūḍha*, (4) the *nirgarbha-yogārūḍha*, (5) the *sagarbha-prāpta-siddhi*, and (6) the *nirgarbha-prāpti-siddhi*.

## 150

> Subheading: Meditation on the third Puruṣa, Kṣīrodaśāyī —

Speaking to Parīkṣit, Śrī Śukadeva condemned the enjoyment of sense objects in the gross world and described the yogī who has turned away from it and is fixed in meditation on the universal form (*vairāja-dhāraṇā*). Now he describes a yogī superior to that one — the one who meditates on the form, full of spirit, of the Antaryāmī dwelling in each individual being (the *vyaṣṭi-antaryāmī*).

**kecit** (a rare few) **sva-dehāntar-hṛdayāvakāśe** (*sva-dehasya*, of one's own body; *antaḥ*, within, that is, in the heart; in its space) **vasantam** (dwelling there as the Antaryāmī) **prādeśa-mātram** (a *prādeśa* is the span from thumb to forefinger; He whose measure is such — for He is meditated upon in a heart the size of a *prādeśa*; yet even in a space of that size He, by His inconceivable potency, has the stature of a youth of fifteen) **catur-bhujaṁ** **kañja-rathāṅga-śaṅkha-gadā-dharam** (holding lotus, disc, conch and club) **puruṣam** (Kṣīrodaśāyī, the third Puruṣa) **dhāraṇayā** **smaranti**.

## 151

Lord Śrī Kapiladeva explained to the surrendered Devahūti that the *nirgarbha* yogī must meditate on each limb and feature of the transcendental form of Śrī Bhagavān. Now He speaks of the fruit of meditation: control of the mind.

**evam** (on the path of meditation) **bhagavati harau** **pratilabdha-bhāvaḥ** (one who has attained *bhāva*, love) **bhaktyā** (with faith) **dravad-dhṛdayaḥ** (one whose heart melts, as if growing moist) **pramodāt** (from the excess of joy) **utpulakaḥ** (one whose hairs have risen — with the hairs of his body standing on end) **autkaṇṭhya-bāṣpa-kalayā** (with streams of tears born of longing) **muhuḥ** (again and again) **ardyamānaḥ** (drowning in a flood of bliss) **ca** **tat api citta-baḍiśam** (even that mind which served as the means to catch the hard-to-catch Bhagavān, like a hook for catching fish) **śanakaiḥ** (little by little — from the object of meditation) **viyuṅkte** (withdraws — he slackens his effort to hold Him in the mind).

## 163

> Subheading: One must strive to attain the eternal, true Reality —

Śrī Vyāsadeva had performed much austerity, compiled all the scriptures and done much else, yet he had not found satisfaction of the soul. > Subheading: Śaunaka and the others condemn *karma-kāṇḍa* and praise Śrī Sūta's eagerness to speak about Hari —

When the *mahā-bhāgavata* Śrī Sūta Gosvāmī began to recite the *Bhāgavatam*, full of narrations about Hari, before Śaunaka and the others who were eager to hear, the *ṛṣis* condemn their own petty rites of *karma-kāṇḍa*:

**asmin** **anāśvāse** (untrustworthy) **karmaṇi** (in the sacrifice) **dhūma-dhūmrātmanām** (of those whose *ātmās* — bodies and minds — have become *dhūmra*, darkened, by smoke — of us) **bhavān** **madhu** (sweet) **govinda-pāda-padmāsavam** (the nectar of Śrī Kṛṣṇa's lotus feet, that is, the nectar of narrations about Śrī Hari) **āpāyayati** (makes us hear).

## 210

> Subheading: In the company of *sādhus* the *dehārāmī* — the ascetic absorbed in the world — gives up the enjoyment that takes the form of austerity and attains pure *bhakti* —

*Dehārāmīs* — ascetics and others — give up austerity in the company of Kṛṣṇa's devotees and serve Kṛṣṇa.

## 211

> Subheading: The testimony of scripture —

In ancient times Pṛthu Mahārāja, lord of the earth, performed a great sacrifice. Standing before the gods, *ṛṣis* and kings assembled there, he speaks of service to Viṣṇu, followed by the *mahājanas* of the past, as a duty and a necessity:

**yat-pāda-sevābhiruciḥ** (the taste for serving Hari's feet), **yathā** (His, Hari's) **padāṅguṣṭha-viniḥsṛtā** (arising from His lotus feet) **sarit** (river, that is, the Gaṅgā), **anvaham** (from day to day, every day) **edhatī** (increasing) **satī** (being pure, *sāttvika*) **tapasvinām** (of the sacrificers) **aśeṣa-janmopacitam** (accumulated in previous births) **dhiyaḥ** (of the intelligence) **malam** (impurity — the impressions of desire and the like) **sadyaḥ** **kṣiṇoti** (destroys); (it is construed with the third [verse]: “Him you should worship with your duties”).

## 212

> Subheading: By Kṛṣṇa's mercy even the *dehārāmī* full of desires gives up desires, that is, becomes selfless, and serves Kṛṣṇa purely —

*Ātmārāmas* — *dehārāmīs* full of all desires — give up the *anartha* of desire and, by the power of Kṛṣṇa's mercy, serve Kṛṣṇa.

## 214

> Subheading: Here are twenty-three meanings —

If these four kinds of “*dehārāma*” are added to the earlier nineteen meanings of the word *ātmārāma*, there are “twenty-three” meanings. And the three further meanings are (1) the *anvācaya* meaning of the word *ca*; (2) the *eva* meaning of the word *ca* together with the *garhā* (censure) meaning of the word *api*; and (3) the *nirdhana* (pauper) meaning of the word *nirgrantha*.

## 215

> Subheading: The explanation by the *samuccaya* meaning of the word *ca* —

The *samuccaya* (conjunction) meaning of the word *ca* has already been explained above: by it, the “*ātmārāmas*” and the “*munis*” serve Kṛṣṇa. “Another meaning” — that is, a meaning other than *samuccaya*.

## 216

“*Api*” is used in the sense of *nirdhāraṇa* (specification); “*nirgranthāḥ*” is the “qualifier” of both, of the *ātmārāmas* and of the *munis*; “*ihã*” means “in this case.” Thus, if one says, “Rāma and Kṛṣṇa sport in the forest,” it means that both of Them sport in the forest.

## 217

> Subheading: The explanation by the *anvācaya* meaning of the word *ca* —

## 217–218

The word *ca* in the meaning of *anvācaya*, that is, the primacy of one thing and the secondary place of another. As an example one may say: “O *brāhmaṇa* boy, beg alms, and if you can, bring the cow as well.” Here begging alms is primary, and bringing the cow is indicated as secondary. In the same way, the sages who contemplate Kṛṣṇa always serve Kṛṣṇa as the “primary” ones, and the *ātmārāmas* serve Kṛṣṇa as the “secondary” ones. This is how the *anvācaya* meaning is applied.

## 218

> Subheading: In this meaning the service of the sage is primary, and the service of the *ātmārāma* secondary —

## 219

> Subheading: The explanation by the *eva* meaning of the word *ca* and the *garhā* (censure) meaning of the word *api* —

If the word *ca* is used in the sense of *eva* and the word *api* in the sense of censure, the meaning is this: “Even though they are *ātmārāmas*, the sages give up the prestige of such a position — and they alone serve Kṛṣṇa.”

## 220

> Subheading: In both cases the word *nirgrantha* is a qualifier —

“*Nirgrantha*” is the “qualifier” of both, of the *ātmārāma* and of the *muni*. And the other, third meaning — that is, the twenty-sixth — is what was seen in the hunter as the fruit of association with Śrī Nārada, the best of *sādhus*.

## 221

> Subheading: The meaning of the word *nirgrantha* —

If *api* with the word *nirgrantha* is used in the sense of *nirdhāraṇa*, then even an unworthy hunter, devoid of such wealth as *sādhana*, becomes a *kṛṣṇārāma* through the power of association with a *sādhu* like Nārada, and serves Kṛṣṇa.

## 222

> Subheading: Through the company of *sādhus* even a hunter is freed from sin, his heart turns to Kṛṣṇa — he becomes a *mahā-bhāgavata* —

The meaning of the word *ātmā* is “Kṛṣṇa.” One who delights in Kṛṣṇa is a *kṛṣṇārāma*; and this *kṛṣṇārāma* is the one who contemplates Kṛṣṇa.
