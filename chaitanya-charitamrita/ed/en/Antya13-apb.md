# Amṛta-pravāha-bhāṣya on Antya-līlā 13

## 0

**Summary.** Mahāprabhu slept on banana bark, and this was very hard on Him. So Jagadānanda made a quilt (*lepa*), a pillow and so on, but Mahāprabhu would not accept them. Then Svarūpa Gosvāmī split dry banana leaves (*peṭo*) into shreds and made something like a quilt and a pillow, and this Mahāprabhu accepted after many objections. With Mahāprabhu's permission Jagadānanda went to Vṛndāvana and, together with Sanātana, relished devotion in many ways. In the matter of Mukunda Sarasvatī's outer cloth (*bahirvāsa*), [Sanātana] chose an excellent means: he took on the role of a teacher (*ācārya-abhimāna*). When Jagadānanda gave Mahāprabhu Sanātana's gifts, an amusing episode arose over eating *pīlu* fruits. Hearing a *devadāsī* sing, Mahāprabhu ran toward her, crashing through thorny thickets, not knowing that the singer was a woman. Govinda stopped Him, and on hearing the word “woman” the Lord thanked Govinda. This story shows that it is improper for a *sannyāsī* or a Vaiṣṇava to hear songs about Kṛṣṇa directly from the lips of a woman not his own (*para-strī*). As Raghunātha Bhaṭṭa Gosvāmī was traveling from Kāśī to Śrī Puruṣottama, the *kāyastha* Rāmadāsa Viśvāsa Paṇḍita joined him on the road. Viśvāsa Paṇḍita was proud of his learning, and a desire for liberation dwelt in his heart; therefore Mahāprabhu did not show him any special mercy. Part of the life of Bhaṭṭa Gosvāmī is briefly told at the end of this chapter.

## 1

Though His mind and body wasted away from the pain born of separation from Kṛṣṇa, He blossomed whenever ecstatic feelings arose in Him. I take refuge in that Gauracandra.

## 5

*Kalāra śaralāte* — on banana bark.

## 15

*Mastaka-muṇḍana* (“a shaven head”) — an expression for disgrace.

## 36

*Mathurāra svāmī-sabera* — of all the Caubes, the residents of Mathurā.[^36-1]

## 37

Out of pure parental love (*śuddha-vātsalya*) for Kṛṣṇa, they behave in ways that go against the views of the *smārtas*. Seeing this, you (for you are devoted to the mood of majesty, *aiśvarya-bhāva*) may come to disrespect them. But there must be no such disrespect for the residents of Vraja-maṇḍala, because their devotion is *rāgātmikā*. Therefore worship them with devotion from a distance (a devotee like you, who loves the mood of majesty, should not stay in the company of these devotees of the path of *rāga*).

## 39

If one lives in Vraja for long, one begins to see faults in the Vrajavāsīs, and one's faith (*śraddhā*) grows weak. Therefore those who have not yet attained the path of *rāga* should not live in Vraja; it is better to see Vraja and soon come away. One must not climb Govardhana to see Śrī Gopāla, for Govardhana is Bhagavān Himself in visible form, and it is not good to climb on Govardhana. It is better to take *darśana* of Gopāla at the times when He goes to some other place.

## 46–47

At that time Sanātana had made it his practice to live on pieces of flatbread (*ruṭi*) received as *mādhukarī* alms. Jagadānanda Paṇḍita, however, could not get through the day without eating rice, so he went to the temple to cook: in the temples of Vraja rice and *dāl* were not offered as *prasāda*.

## 52

*Rātula* — red (*kāṣāya*, ochre).

## 69

*Dvādaśāditya-ṭilā* — the high mound on which the old, ruined temple of Śrī Madana-mohana stands is called “Dvādaśa-āditya-ṭilā.” During Kṛṣṇa's pastimes the twelve Ādityas rose at that place.
