## 31

> Subheading: Words from His own lips — (a verse of the *Śikṣāṣṭaka* spoken by Śrī Kṛṣṇa Caitanyacandra)

**tṛṇād api** (than even grass, which all feet trample and which has no importance at all) **su-nīcena** (lower in every way, that is, endowed with a mood free of worldly claims to prestige) **taror api**[^31-1] (than even a tree) **sahiṣṇunā** (by a person endowed with tolerance) **amāninā** (who, though himself worthy of honor, has given up such worldly claims to prestige) **māna-dena** (giving honor and respect to others — even to those without honor and to the unworthy; by such a person) **sadā** (always) **hariḥ** (Hari alone) **kīrtanīyaḥ** (is to be chanted — uttered with the lips, the tongue and so on).

## 32

> Subheading: Kavirāja Gosvāmī earnestly urges everyone to live by this verse —

## 32–33

“Strung on the thread of the name”: the thread is Śrī Harināma, and what is strung on it as a garland or protective amulet (*rakṣā-kavaca*) is the four moods free of worldly self-conception: (1) humility (*sunīcatva*), (2) tolerance (*sahiṣṇutva*), (3) freedom from claims to honor (*amānitva*), (4) readiness to give honor (*mānadatva*). With worldly self-conception one cannot chant the name of Hari always: material self-conceptions obstruct the name of Hari. Free of these four kinds of self-conception, the pure *jīva* can chant the name of Hari always. Whoever obeys this command — the instruction of *sādhana-bhakti* — will certainly attain the feet of Śrī Kṛṣṇa as the fruit of chanting the name of Hari.
