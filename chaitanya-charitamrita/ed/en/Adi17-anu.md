## 31

> Subheading: Words from His own lips — (a verse of the *Śikṣāṣṭaka* spoken by Śrī Kṛṣṇa Caitanyacandra)

**tṛṇād api** (than even grass, which all feet trample and which has no importance at all) **su-nīcena** (lower in every way, that is, endowed with a mood free of worldly claims to prestige) **taror api**[^31-1] (than even a tree) **sahiṣṇunā** (by a person endowed with tolerance) **amāninā** (who, though himself worthy of honor, has given up such worldly claims to prestige) **māna-dena** (giving honor and respect to others — even to those without honor and to the unworthy; by such a person) **sadā** (always) **hariḥ** (Hari alone) **kīrtanīyaḥ** (is to be chanted — uttered with the lips, the tongue and so on).

## 32

> Subheading: Kavirāja Gosvāmī earnestly urges everyone to live by this verse —

## 32–33

“Strung on the thread of the name”: the thread is Śrī Harināma, and what is strung on it as a garland or protective amulet (*rakṣā-kavaca*) is the four moods free of worldly self-conception: (1) humility (*sunīcatva*), (2) tolerance (*sahiṣṇutva*), (3) freedom from claims to honor (*amānitva*), (4) readiness to give honor (*mānadatva*). With worldly self-conception one cannot chant the name of Hari always: material self-conceptions obstruct the name of Hari. Free of these four kinds of self-conception, the pure *jīva* can chant the name of Hari always. Whoever obeys this command — the instruction of *sādhana-bhakti* — will certainly attain the feet of Śrī Kṛṣṇa as the fruit of chanting the name of Hari.

## 52

Worship of Bhavānī is the business of a non-Vaiṣṇava, that is, it is not the business of a Vaiṣṇava. By condemning it, the Lord taught people to regard as “bad association” the worldly, mixed Vaiṣṇava (*prākṛta viddha-vaiṣṇava*) who at heart nourishes the doctrine of many gods, that is, who leans toward the worship of many gods and goddesses.

## 55

> Subheading: When the Lord came, he surrendered to Him —

Kuliyā-grāma: the present town of Navadvīpa. “Between Nadīyā and Kuliyā there is only the Gaṅgā” (*Caitanya-bhāgavata*, Antya 3.380): Kuliyā is on the western bank of the Gaṅgā, and Navadvīpa on the eastern bank. For mentions of Kuliyā on the western bank of the Gaṅgā, see the *Bhakti-ratnākara* (twelfth wave), the *Caitanya-carita* *mahākāvya*, the play *Caitanya-candrodaya*, and the *Caitanya-bhāgavata*. In the village of Kuliyā, which belongs to Koladvīpa, there is to this day a quarter called “Kuliyār Gañj” and a stream called “Kuliyār Daha”; it lies within the present municipal town of Navadvīpa. In Śrī Mahāprabhu's time there were villages called “Kuliyā” and “Pāhāḍapura” on the western bank of the Gaṅgā. They lie among the fields of “Bāhir-dvīpa.” But then and ever since, Navadvīpa was in “Antardvīpa,” on the eastern bank of the Gaṅgā. In Śrī Māyāpura that place is still known as “Dvīper Māṭh” (“the field of the island”). The village called “Kuliyā” near Kāñcaḍāpāḍā is not the Kuliyā mentioned above, nor the “*pāṭa* of Aparādha-bhañjana” (the place where offenses were forgiven). The false notion about it arose only a few years ago — from fancies born of hostility toward the Dhāma, and from error.

## 72

> Subheading: A godless student calls the glory of the holy name exaggeration (*arthavāda*) —

The glory of Śrī Nāma Prabhu is non-different from Kṛṣṇa Himself. To consider it “exaggerated praise,” “not real,” and therefore “untrue” — this divisive mentality is what is called “*arthavāda*,” that is, (false) praise or else defamation. It is outright godlessness, or atheism — nothing but enmity toward God.
