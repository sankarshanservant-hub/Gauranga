## 11

> Subheading: Growth in Īśvara Purī —

Śrī Īśvara Purī was born in a brāhmaṇa family in Kumārahaṭṭa (Hālisahara station on the E.B.R. line) and was the dearest disciple of Śrī Mādhavendra Purī. Antya 8.26–29: “Īśvara Purī served his master's feet and with his own hands cleaned away stool and urine. He constantly made him remember Kṛṣṇa's name and made him hear Kṛṣṇa's name and pastimes at every moment. Pleased, Purī embraced him and gave the blessing: ‘May you have the treasure of love for Kṛṣṇa.’ From then on Īśvara Purī became an ocean of love…”

Before initiating Śrī Mahāprabhu into the ten-syllable mantra at Gayā, Īśvara Purī came to the town of Navadvīpa and stayed several months in the house of Gopīnātha Ācārya. At that time he conversed with Śrī Mahāprabhu and had Him hear his own book, the *Kṛṣṇa-līlāmṛta*. See the *Caitanya-bhāgavata*, Ādi, Chapter 7.

When Śrīman Mahāprabhu came to Kumārahaṭṭa to see the birthplace of Śrīpāda Īśvara Purī, He showed this pastime to teach the *jīvas* devotion to the guru: “The Lord Himself picked up earth from that place, tied it in a bundle in His outer cloth and took it with Him.” (*Caitanya-bhāgavata*, Ādi, Chapter 12) Since then everyone who comes to see the place of Īśvara Purī takes earth from there.

> Subheading: By His inconceivable power, though the gardener, He Himself became the trunk and the support of all the branches —

## 13

> Subheading: The nine *sannyāsīs* — the nine roots —

Paramānanda Purī was a brāhmaṇa from Trihuta (Tirhut), a disciple of Śrī Mādhavendra Purī and very dear to Śrī Mahāprabhu. *Caitanya-bhāgavata* (Antya, Chapter 11): “Among the *sannyāsīs* the Lord's dear one is he alone, Purī Gosāñi, and no other. Dāmodara-Svarūpa and Paramānanda Purī — these two alone have a place among His *sannyāsī* associates. Both are always near Him, and in the Lord’s *sannyāsa* they take up the *daṇḍa*. Purī is absorbed in meditation, Dāmodara in *kīrtana*. *** As much love as the Lord has for Purī Gosāñi, so much He has for Dāmodara-Svarūpa.”

The Lord's words on seeing Paramānanda Purī (*Caitanya-bhāgavata*, Antya, Chapter 3): “Today My eyes are blessed, today My birth has borne fruit, today all My *dharma* has borne fruit. The Lord said: ‘Today My *sannyāsa* has borne fruit; today Mādhavendra has appeared to Me.’ For a long while they bowed to each other. Paramānanda Purī is Caitanya's beloved abode.”

Paramānanda Purī lived in Puruṣottama, west of the temple, where he made a *maṭha* for himself and dug a well. The water of the well was bad, and Mahāprabhu said (*Caitanya-bhāgavata*, Antya, Chapter 3): “‘Mahāprabhu Jagannātha, give Me this boon: let the Gaṅgā enter this well.’ The Lord said: ‘Listen, all devotees! Whoever bathes in or drinks the water of this well will truly obtain the fruit of bathing in the Gaṅgā and will have the purest devotion to Kṛṣṇa.’ The Lord said: ‘Know for certain that I remain on earth out of love for Purī Gosāñi.’” In the *Gaura-gaṇoddeśa* (verse 118): “Śrī Paramānanda Purī was formerly Uddhava.”

Keśava Bhāratī belonged to the “Bhāratī” *sampradāya*, one of the *sampradāyas* of the *daṇḍīs* of the ten names founded by Śrī Śaṅkara. Three *sampradāyas* — Sarasvatī, Bhāratī and Purī — are under the Śṛṅgerī *maṭha* in the south. Śrī Keśava Bhāratī was living at that time in a branch *maṭha* at Kāṭoyā. Some say that, although he was a *brahma-sannyāsī*, he received his mantra from Śrī Mādhavendra Purīpāda of the Śrī Mādhva-sampradāya and was a Vaiṣṇava *sannyāsī*. In the village of Khāṭundi (Kāndarā post office, Barddhamāna district) he established the service of a Deity and a *maṭha*. According to the proprietors of the *maṭha*, they are of Keśava Bhāratī's line: Keśava had sons (by another account, disciples) named Niśāpati and Uṣāpati. Of Niśāpati's line, Śrī Nakaṛicandra Vidyāratna is now the custodian of the service, and Uṣāpati's line lives at Rākhāladāsapura near Baiṁci in Hooghly. They may be descendants from Keśava Bhāratī's life before *sannyāsa*. According to some, Keśava Bhāratī's brother — or, by another account, Balabhadra, a disciple of his disciple Mādhava Bhāratī — also became a Bhāratī. From his life before *sannyāsa* he had two sons, Madana and Gopāla. Madana lived in Āuriyā and Gopāla in Denduḍa. Madana's descendants bear the title “Bhāratī,” and Gopāla's the title “Brahmacārī.” Many members of both families are still living. In the *Gaura-gaṇoddeśa* (verse 52): “The sage Sāndīpani, who formerly gave Kṛṣṇa the sacred thread in Mathurā, has now become Keśava Bhāratī.” Verse 117: “Some say that Keśava Bhāratī is Akrūra.” In 1432 of the Śaka era he gave *sannyāsa* to Nimāi Paṇḍita at Kāṭoyā. See the *Vaiṣṇava-mañjuṣā*, issue 2.

Śrī Brahmānanda Purī was Śrī Mahāprabhu's companion in *kīrtana* during His pastimes in Navadvīpa. Before and at the time of His *sannyāsa*, Mahāprabhu especially trusted him. He also came with the Lord to Nīlācala.

Śrī Brahmānanda Bhāratī came to Nīlācala for the Lord's *darśana* dressed in a deerskin. Śrī Mahāprabhu Himself went up to him and, as if He did not recognize him, said that He wished to see Bhāratī. Hearing this, Brahmānanda took off the deerskin and put on a saffron outer cloth. He lived for some time in Nīlācala near Mahāprabhu.

## 14

Keśava Purī, Kṛṣṇānanda Purī, Nṛsiṁha Tīrtha and Sukhānanda Purī. In the *Gaura-gaṇoddeśa* (verses 97–100): “Kṛṣṇānanda, Keśava, Śrī Dāmodara and Rāghava, Ananta, Sukhānanda, Govinda and Raghunātha — with the title ‘Purī,’ in that order — are to be known as the eight *siddhis* beginning with *aṇimā*. The nine sons of Jayantī, steadfast in celibacy and equal-sighted — the nine *bhāgavatas* who formerly, as the *Śrī Bhāgavata-saṁhitā* relates, answered Janaka — have now become *sannyāsīs* and always sport with the Lord Gaurahari, as before: Śrī Nṛsiṁhānanda Tīrtha, Śrī Satyānanda Bhāratī, Śrī Nṛsiṁha, Cidānanda and Jagannātha — the Tīrthas.”

## 40

> Subheading: Seeing Gaura's mercy, the *jīva* attains eternal good in chanting Gaura's name —

Piety makes people in the world happy, and the spread of sin increases human misery. The pure life of the pious is glorified, while people do not even wish to speak of the outrages of the sinful. In the same way, when the people of the world become happy by obtaining *kṛṣṇa-prema*, the good fame of the One who gives *prema* will grow.

## 41

> Subheading: Everyone born as a human being in the land of Bhārata must show people eternal mercy — that is, turn them toward Kṛṣṇa —

To be born a human being in holy Bhāratavarṣa and to do the world real, eternal good — this is the fulfillment of having received a body in the holiest land and among the highest of beings.

## 42

> Subheading: To turn the *jīva* toward devotion to Kṛṣṇa by body, mind and words is the highest mercy, the highest auspicious act (*Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam*, Canto 10, Chapter 22, verse 35) —

After the pastime of stealing the garments, Śrī Kṛṣṇa went far away with His friends, the cowherd boys, and sat down to rest beneath some trees. Seeing how the trees help others and how compassionate and tolerant they are, He pointed to them and said to His friends:

**sadā** **prāṇaiḥ** **arthaiḥ** **dhiyā** **vācā** (by every means) **dehiṣu** (toward the *jīvas*) **śreya** **ācaraṇam** (doing eternal good — that is, removing their aversion to Bhagavān, turning them toward Him, and so fully showing eternal mercy) — **etāvat** **eva** **iha** (in this world) **dehinām** (for the *jīvas*) **janma-sāphalyam** ([is], to be supplied).

## 46
