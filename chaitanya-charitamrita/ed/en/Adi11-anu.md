## 8

> Subheading: (1) The branch of Śrī Vīracandra Gosāñi —

Śrī Vīrabhadra Gosāñi was the son of Śrī Nityānanda Prabhu and a disciple of Mother Jāhnavā; He was born of Vasudhā. In the *Gaura-gaṇoddeśa* (verse 67): “That *vyūha* of Saṅkarṣaṇa named Payobdhiśāyī became Vīracandra, who is not different from Caitanya.” His disciple was Yadunātha Ācārya of the village of Jhāmaṭapura in Hugalī district. Vīrabhadra married Yadunātha's daughter Śrīmatī, born of Vidyunmālā (Lakṣmī), and also their adopted daughter Nārāyaṇī; see the *Bhakti-ratnākara*, wave 13. Three of His disciples, Gopījana-vallabha, Rāmakṛṣṇa and Rāmacandra, became famous as His sons. The youngest, Rāmacandra, lived in Khaḍadaha; he was a Śuddha-śrotriya of the Śāṇḍilya *gotra* with the surname “Baṭabyāla.” The eldest, Gopījana-vallabha, lived in the village of “Latā” near Mānakara in Barddhamāna district, and the middle one, Rāmakṛṣṇa, in Gayeśapura near Māladaha. If all three share the same *gotra* and the same village, no one can doubt that they were Vīrabhadra's sons by birth. Rāmacandra had four sons. The third son of the eldest, Rādhāmādhava, was Yādavendra; his son was Nandakiśora; his son, Nidhikṛṣṇa; his son, Caitanya-cā̃da; his son, Kṛṣṇamohana; his son, Jaganmohana; his son, Vrajanātha; and Vrajanātha's son was the late Śyāmalāla Gosvāmī.

## 9

> Subheading: His glory: though Viṣṇu Himself, He behaves as a Vaiṣṇava —

## 13

> Subheading: (2) Ṭhākura Abhirāma (*gopāla* 1), (3) Dāsa Gadādhara —

Gadādhara dāsa: see Ādi 10.53.

Śrī Rāmadāsa (Abhirāma). Ṭhākura Abhirāma, whose very life was Nityānanda, is one of the twelve *gopālas*; in Vraja he is Śrīdāma, [Kṛṣṇa's] friend. In the *Gaura-gaṇoddeśa* (verse 126): “He who was formerly named Śrīdāma is now the great Abhirāma, who carried a log that only thirty-two men could carry.” See Ādi 10.116–118.

The *Bhakti-ratnākara* (wave 4) tells of Śrīla Abhirāma Ṭhākura. By the order of Nityānanda, the crusher of the godless, Abhirāma Ṭhākura was an *ācārya* and a preacher of *bhakti-dharma*. “The might of Abhirāma Gosvāmī is fierce: at the sight of him the unconquerable godless always tremble. He is ever maddened, absorbed in Nityānanda; his enchanting mercy is known throughout the world.” A saying is current to this day: if he bowed down to any stone or image other than a Viṣṇu-*śilā* or a Deity of Viṣṇu, it would split and crumble to dust.

Abhirāma Ṭhākura's *śrīpāṭa* is in Khānākula-Kṛṣṇanagara, about ten miles southwest of Cā̃pāḍāṅgā station on the Hāoḍā–Āmtā line, past the “Helānāra hāṭ.” In the rainy season the road is flooded, so one goes by the B.N.R. line: from Kolāghāṭa by steamer to Rāṇīcaka, and from there Khānākula is 7½ miles to the north. Abhirāma Ṭhākura's *śrīpāṭa* is in Kṛṣṇanagara; because that village lies on the bank of the river Khānā, or Dvārakeśvara, it is called “Khānākula-Kṛṣṇanagara.”

Outside the temple, near the gate, stands a *bakula* tree; the spot is called the “Siddha-bakula-kuñja.” It is said that Abhirāma Ṭhākura sat here first when he came.

Inside the temple are Śrī Baladeva and Śrī Madana-mohana (alone, without Rādhā). On a single slab of black touchstone, 1¼ cubits high and nearly a cubit wide, the Deity of Śrī Gopīnātha is carved together with the pastime of stealing the garments: the *kadamba* tree, the Yamunā, and the cows and calves. Such are the *arcā* Deities. Besides these, on the throne are a sacred image of Abhirāma Ṭhākura in the ecstasy of dance (his feet not spread apart) and Śrī Vraja-vallabha (the Divine Couple). There are also a Śrī Śālagrāma and a Deity of Śrī Gopāla. Tradition says that Śrī Abhirāma Ṭhākura found the Gopīnātha Deity while the pond in front of the temple was being dug. Since then the pond has been known as “Abhirāma-kuṇḍa.” Directly south of the temple where the Deities now reside stands an old *nava-ratna* temple. A stone plaque high on that temple bears the inscription that it was built in the year 1181. The builder's name is not given. It is said that the late Nachirāma Siṁha “Gailā” of a neighboring village had the temple built. The Deity resided here even earlier, and before the temple was built He was served on this very spot in a thatched hut.

Directly north of the Śrī Gopīnātha temple is the ancient temple of Śrī Rādhā-vallabha Jīu, founded by the local Kāyastha Caudhurīs.

The stone plaque of the present temple bears the inscription: “Śrī Śrī Gopīnātha Jīu. Temple built in the month of Māgha, year 1219. Repaired in the month of Vaiśākha, year 1308.” It is said that the late Puṇḍarīkākṣa Rāya of Mādhavapura, near Ārāmabāga police station in Hugalī district, built it. In front of the old temple stands a spacious brick *nāṭya-mandira*. The fishermen (*dhīvaras*) of Medinīpura district built it in 1263; when it fell into disrepair, they restored it in 1320.

The servitors say that ever since the time of Śrīla Abhirāma Ṭhākura it has been the custom here to offer *bhoga* of parboiled (*siddha-cāula*) rice; even puffed rice (*muḍi*) is offered. Another unusual custom is that when the Ṭhākura is laid to rest, the temple doors stay open and He is laid to rest in everyone's sight. Nowadays there is no custom of performing *maṅgala-ārati* for the Ṭhākura in the morning.

At present there are thirty-six or thirty-seven families of servitors. It is said that inside the temple, in an iron chest, is kept Śrīla Abhirāma Ṭhākura's famous whip “Śrī Jayamaṅgala”; the chest is locked with all the keys of these servitors. The whip is two cubits long and wrapped in gold thread; at a festival it is brought out only when all the servitors agree together. The *Bhakti-ratnākara* (wave 4) writes of the whip “Śrī Jayamaṅgala” that love for Kṛṣṇa arose in anyone Abhirāma Ṭhākura struck with it. Once, when Śrīnivāsa Ācārya came to Abhirāma's house, Ṭhākura Abhirāma touched Śrīnivāsa's body three times with that whip. Then Abhirāma's wife, Mālinī Devī, a *brāhmaṇa*'s daughter, laughed, took the Ṭhākura by the hand, and said, “Ṭhākura, restrain yourself! Śrīnivāsa is a boy; at the touch of your whip he will lose all composure.”

His descendants (by birth, or in the line of disciples?) live in Kṛṣṇanagara and Āmtā in Hugalī district, and in Viṣṇupura, Kotalapura and other villages of Bā̃kuḍā district.

A certain “Abhirāmadāsa,” a disciple of Ratneśvara, gives the names and places of “Ṭhākura Abhirāma's” disciples in his book *Śākhā-nirṇaya* as follows: (1) Kṛṣṇadāsa Ṭhākura lived in Khānākula (the line has died out); (2) a devotee named Vedagarbha lived in the village of Kaiyaḍ (about 10 miles southwest of Barddhamāna); his descendants now serve the Deity there; (3) Haridāsa lived in the village of Būṛhana (nothing particular is known of him); (4) Pākhiyā-Gopāladāsa lived in the village of Helāla (?) (about two miles north of Khānākula, on the bank of the river Khānā); a small ruined temple known as his *samāja* stands there now, but there is no Deity; (5) “Gumphanārāyaṇa” lived in the village of Pāikamājiṭā (?) near Rāmajīvanapura in Medinīpura district; his descendants are living; (6) Dāḍiyā Mohana lived in Sītānagara (neither the place nor his line can be traced); (7) Satyarāghava lived in Mayanāmuḍi (Bā̃kuḍā) (where his descendants live is unknown); (8) Rajanī Paṇḍita lived in Sālikhā (near Hāoḍā?) (where his descendants are is also unknown); (9) Sundarānanda lived in Bhāṅgāmoḍā (about 4 miles northwest of Tārakeśvara); he has descendants; (10) Kṛṣṇānanda Avadhūta lived in Dvīpagrāma (its location is unknown); it is doubtful whether he had any descendants; (11) Raṅgaṇa-Kṛṣṇadāsa lived in the village of Sonātalā (-lī) (in Hugalī or Hāoḍā district?) (the line has died out); (12) Murāridāsa lived in Māladaha (where his descendants live is unknown); (13) Mohana Ṭhākura lived in Pāṇihāṭī (nothing is known of his lineage); (14) Yadu Hāladāra lived in Rādhānagara (south of Khānākula-Kṛṣṇanagara); because his line died out, the Deity he established, Śrī Balarāma, is still served together with Śrī Gopīnātha; (15) Harimādhava lived in Anantanagara (near Khānākula) (the line has died out); (16) Gopāladāsa lived in Māheśa (near Śrīrāmapura?) (nothing is known of his descendants); (17) Acyuta Paṇḍita lived in Koṭarā (near Khānākula police station) (a descendant is living); (18) Lakṣmīnārāyaṇa lived in the village of Pāṭalā (the line has died out); (19) Gopīnāthadāsa lived in Purī (no information); (20) Nandakiśora lived in Cuṇākhāli *parganā* (near Māheśa) (nothing is known of his descendants); (21) Vidura Brahmacārī lived in the village of Pātāgrāma (Pāṭuli in Barddhamāna district?); his descendants are living; (22) Rāmakṛṣṇa lived in Vinupāḍā (nothing is known of either the place or the man); (23) Kamalākara lived in Gaurāṅgapura (about 4 miles north of the *śrīpāṭa*); his *samāja* is nearby, and his descendants serve the Deities of Śrī Nitāi-Gaura; (24) Balarāma Ṭhākura lived in Viśvagrāma (neither the place nor the man can be traced); (24½) Śrīnivāsa Ācārya Prabhu (he was most dear to Abhirāma and received his affection and mercy, but because he had not taken initiation from him he is evidently counted as a “half disciple”). On the lunar day of Kṛṣṇa-saptamī in the month of Caitra a great festival is held here, and many people gather.

## 15

> Subheading: (4) Mādhava and (5) Vāsu Ghoṣa Ṭhākura —

Govinda Ghoṣa, Vāsu Ghoṣa. Govinda Ghoṣa Ṭhākura's most beloved Deity, Śrī Gopīnātha, is still present at Agradvīpa, near Dā̃ihāṭ and Pāṭuli in Barddhamāna district. On the day of His devotee's disappearance He offers *piṇḍa*, as a son does at his father's *śrāddha*. His service has long been carried on under the supervision of the royal family of Nadīyā-Kṛṣṇanagara. Every year in the month of Vaiśākha, at the time of the “Bāradola” in Kṛṣṇanagara, He is brought to the capital together with eleven other Deities, and after the Dola festival He is taken back to Agradvīpa.

Many *padas* in the “Gaura-nāgarī” mood, which gratify the senses of the *prākṛta-sahajiyās*, have been interpolated into Vāsu Ghoṣa's collection of *padas*. In truth these are not, and can never be, the *padas* of Vāsu Ghoṣa, a devotee of Gaura who relished the *rasa* of separation (*vipralambha*). *Sādhakas* will reject them. See Ādi 10.115.

## 20

> Subheading: (6) Murāri-Caitanyadāsa —

Murāri-Caitanyadāsa. *Caitanya-bhāgavata*, Antya, Chapter 5: “Caitanyadāsa's body has no external consciousness. He drives tigers deep into the forest, and at times he even rides on a tiger. By Kṛṣṇa's grace the tiger cannot harm him. He takes a huge python on his lap, and Caitanyadāsa fearlessly delights in it. He played with tigers without fear — such is the mercy the great Avadhūta bestows. Caitanyadāsa has entirely forgotten himself; in bliss he constantly speaks what is in his heart. For two or three days he stays submerged in water, and his body suffers no harm. In dress and behavior he is almost like an insensible man, and no one notices him. He is wild beyond measure and boundlessly brave, like a lion. How much can I tell of the transformations of *bhakti* in Caitanyadāsa? They are all boundless. Such is the worthy Śrī Caitanyadāsa Murāri Paṇḍita: even by the breeze that has touched him, one surely attains Kṛṣṇa.”

## 21

> Subheading: Nitāi's associates are pure devotees, the friends of Vraja —

All the devotees who took shelter of Śrī Nityānanda Prabhu took shelter of the *sakhya-rasa* of Vraja. All of them wear the dress of cowherd boys. The Prabhu's wife, the queen Mother Jāhnavā, is Anaṅga-mañjarī of Vraja, the younger sister of Śrīmatī Rādhikā. In the *Gaura-gaṇoddeśa* (verse 66): “Some say that in the age of Kali Anaṅga-mañjarī is Śrī Vasudhā-devī, while others declare her to be Jāhnavī. By the earlier principle, the saintly consider both views correct.” The devotees who took shelter of Mother Jāhnavā are also counted among Nityānanda's associates.

## 23

> Subheading: (8) Sundarānanda (*gopāla* 2) —

Sundarānanda. *Caitanya-bhāgavata*, Antya, Chapter 6: “An ocean of the *rasa* of *prema*, named Sundarānanda, the chief among the associates of Nityānanda-svarūpa.” In the *Gaura-gaṇoddeśa* (verse 127): “He who was formerly named Sudāma is now Ṭhākura Sundara.” He is one of the twelve *gopālas*, “Sudāma.”

His *śrīpāṭa* is the village of Maheśapura, 14 miles east of “Mājadiyā” station (formerly called “Śivanivāsa”) on the E.B.R. line; it is now in Yaśohara district. Nothing old remains here as a memorial except the site of Sundarānanda's ancestral home. A Bāul lives at the *śrīpāṭa* on the edge of the village. The temple, the Deities and everything else are recent. At present Śrī Śrī Rādhā-vallabha and Śrī Śrī Rādhā-ramaṇa are served in Maheśapura. The river Vetravatī flows nearby. Sundarānanda Ṭhākura remained a celibate all his life, so he has no descendants. Descendants of his kinsmen and of his servitor-disciples are living. In the village of Maṅgaladihi in Vīrabhūma district there is a family of Sundarānanda's kinsmen; Śrī Śrī Balarāma Jīu is served there. The Deity of Śrī Śrī Rādhā-vallabha established by Sundarānanda Ṭhākura was taken away by the Gosvāmīs of Saidābāda in Baharampura; later the present Deity was installed. Now the zamindars of Maheśapura are His servitors. On the full-moon day of Māgha the festival of Sundarānanda Ṭhākura's disappearance is held.

## 24

> Subheading: (9) Kamalākara Pippalāi (*gopāla* 3) —

Kamalākara Pippalāi. In the *Gaura-gaṇoddeśa* (verse 128): “He who was Mahābala came to be named Kamalākara Pippalāi.” He is one of the twelve *gopālas*, “Mahābala.” It was he who established the Deity of Śrī Jagannātha at Māheśa. The temple of Śrī Jagannāthadeva at Māheśa is about 2½ miles from Śrīrāmapura station on the E.I.R. line.

Kamalākara's son was named Caturbhuja. Caturbhuja had two sons, Nārāyaṇa and Jagannātha; Nārāyaṇa's son was Jagadānanda, and his son was Rājīvalocana. In his time the service of Jagannāthadeva fell short of funds. The Nawab of Ḍhākā, Oyālisa Sā (Sujā?), granted Jagannāthadeva 1185 *bighās* of land in the year 1060. The land lies in the village of Jagannāthapura, a *krośa* and a half west of Māheśa. That *mauzā* took the name “Jagannāthapura” from Jagannāthadeva's name.

There is a tradition that Kamalākara's younger brother, Nidhipati Pippalāi, searched for his elder brother, came to Māheśa, and found Kamalākara there. He could in no way persuade him to return home, so in the end he himself came to Māheśa with his own family and his brothers' families and settled there. Even now about thirty households of twice-born descendants of Kamalākara Pippalāi live in the village of Māheśa, among them Śrīyukta Prasādadāsa Adhikārī.

Another story is told. A certain renunciant Vaiṣṇava named “Dhruvānanda” went to Puruṣottama-kṣetra. He strongly desired to cook with his own hands and offer *bhoga* to Śrī Jagannātha. At night he saw in a dream that Śrī Jagannāthadeva appeared to him and told him to go to the village of Māheśa on the bank of the Gaṅgā, install Śrī Jagannātha there, and fulfill his desire by offering Him *bhoga* every day with his own hands. When Dhruvānanda reached Māheśa, he saw Śrī Jagannātha, Balarāma and Subhadrā-devī floating in the waters of the Gaṅgā. He lifted the three Deities out of the water, built a hut on the bank of the Gaṅgā, and began to serve Them. As the time of his disappearance drew near, one thought filled his heart: who would be a fit servitor for Śrī Jagannātha? Then in a dream he received Śrī Jagannāthadeva's order: “Tomorrow morning a resident of the village of Khālijuli near the Sundarbans, named ‘Śrī Kamalākara Pippalāi,’ a supreme devotee of Śrī Jagannāthadeva and a crest jewel of Vaiṣṇavas, will come to Māheśa; entrust the service to him.” The next morning Dhruvānanda met Kamalākara and entrusted him with the service of Śrī Jagannāthadeva. Ever since Kamalākara obtained the right to serve Śrī Jagannāthadeva, [his family] has borne the title “Adhikārī.” Among the fifty-six *grāmīs* (clans named after villages) of *brāhmaṇas* by birth of the Rāḍhīya class, “Pippalāi” is one.

## 25

> Subheading: (10) Sūryadāsa and (11) Kṛṣṇadāsa Sarakhela —

Sūryadāsa Sarakhela. *Bhakti-ratnākara*, wave 12: “Not far from Navadvīpa is ‘Śāligrāma.’ There lives a *paṇḍita* named Sūryadāsa. He is very able in the service of the Yavana king in Gauḍa; he became famous as ‘Sarakhela’ and earned great wealth. Sūryadāsa is one of four brothers, all of very pure conduct. His two daughters are named Vasudhā and Jāhnavā.” In the *Gaura-gaṇoddeśa* (verse 65): “His two beloveds, Vasudhā and Jāhnavī, descend from Śrī Vāruṇī and from the line of Revata.[^25-1] They are the daughters of the great soul Śrī Sūryadāsa, who is Kakudmī, radiant as the sun.”

Baḍagāchi. On the E.B.R. line, 2 miles from “Muḍāgāchā” station, lies Baḍagāchi, or Bahiragāchi, on the bank of the “Guḍaguḍe” canal, across from the village of Dharmadaha. Śāligrāma and Rukuṇapura are close by.

Kṛṣṇadāsa Sarakhela was the younger brother of Gaurīdāsa Paṇḍita and Sūryadāsa Sarakhela. For the *adhivāsa* of Nityānanda's wedding he went from Baḍagāchi to Śāligrāma. *Bhakti-ratnākara*, wave 12: “Taking various articles, Kṛṣṇadāsa Paṇḍita came from his home together with the *brāhmaṇas*.”

## 26

> Subheading: (12) Gaurīdāsa Paṇḍita (*gopāla* 4) —

Śrī Gaurīdāsa Paṇḍita. He was patronized by Rājā Kṛṣṇadāsa, the son of Harihoḍa. He is one of the twelve *gopālas* of Vraja, “Subala the friend.” He lived first in Śāligrāma, not far from Muḍāgāchā station on the E.B.R. line, and later at Ambikā-Kālanā. In the *Gaura-gaṇoddeśa* (verse 128): “He who was Subala, the best of the dear friends, is Gaurīdāsa Paṇḍita.” *Caitanya-bhāgavata*, Antya, Chapter 6: “Gaurīdāsa Paṇḍita is supremely fortunate; in body, mind and words Nityānanda is his life.” “Sarakhela Sūryadāsa Paṇḍita is generous. His brother Gaurīdāsa Paṇḍita is renowned. Taking leave of his elder brother at Śāligrāma, he came to ‘Ambikā’ and made his home on the bank of the Gaṅgā.”

He had twenty-two and a half branches: (1) Śrī Nṛsiṁha-caitanya, (2) Kṛṣṇadāsa, (3) Viṣṇudāsa, (4) Baḍa Balarāmadāsa, (5) Govinda, (6) Raghunātha, (7) Baḍu Gaṅgādāsa, (8) Āuliyā Gaṅgārāma, (9) Yādavācārya, (10) Hṛdaya-caitanya, (11) Cā̃da Hāladāra, (12) Maheśa Paṇḍita, (13) Mukuṭa Rāya, (14) Bhātuyā Gaṅgārāma, (15) Āuliyā Caitanya, (16) Kāliyā Kṛṣṇadāsa, (17) Pātuyā Gopāla, (18) Baḍa Jagannātha, (19) Nityānanda, (20) Bhāvi, (21) Jagadīśa, (22) Rāiyā Kṛṣṇadāsa, (22½) Annapūrṇā.

Gaurīdāsa Paṇḍita's elder son was (Baḍa) Balarāma and the younger, Raghunātha. Raghunātha's sons were Maheśa Paṇḍita and Govinda, and his daughter was Annapūrṇā. Śrī Kaṁsāri Miśra of Śāligrāma (family title “Ghoṣāla,” “Vātsya” *gotra*) had six sons: (1) Dāmodara, (2) Jagannātha, (3) Sūryadāsa Sarakhela (the father of Vasudhā and Jāhnavā), (4) Gaurīdāsa, (5) Kṛṣṇadāsa Sarakhela, (6) Nṛsiṁha-caitanya. Some descendants of Kaṁsāri Miśra's kinsmen still live in Śāligrāma. Neither Gaurīdāsa Paṇḍita nor Hṛdaya-caitanya has descendants. Some say that those living now are descendants in the disciplic line of Gaurīdāsa Paṇḍita or Hṛdaya-caitanya. When Jāhnavā-devī went to Śrī Vṛndāvana and saw the *samāja* of her uncle Gaurīdāsa Paṇḍita, she wept: “Seeing the *samādhi* of Gaurīdāsa Paṇḍita, she cannot stop the streams of tears flowing from her eyes.” (*Bhakti-ratnākara*, wave 11.)

Gaurīdāsa's disciple was Hṛdaya-caitanya; Hṛdaya-caitanya's disciple was Gopīramaṇa, the son of Annapūrṇā Devī. His descendants are the present *adhikārīs* of Mahāprabhu at Kālnā.

The *śrīpāṭa* of Ambikā-Kālnā is on the bank of the Gaṅgā across from Śāntipura, in Barddhamāna district; it is a subdivision and a small town. The *śrīpāṭa* is about 1 *krośa* east of Kālnā Court station on the Hāoḍā–Byāṇḍel–Bārahāroyā line, right next to the new *samāja-bāṭī* (mausoleum) of the Rājā of Barddhamāna, or the bazaar. At the entrance to Gaurīdāsa's temple stands a wonderful tamarind tree. It is said that Mahāprabhu and Gaurīdāsa Paṇḍita met beneath this tamarind. The temple is paved with white stone, and in the three chambers of the building the sacred forms reside thus: (1) Śrī Gaurīdāsa, (2) Śrī Rādhā-Kṛṣṇa, (3) Śrī Gaura-Nityānanda, (4) Śrī Jagannātha, (5) Śrī Balarāma and (6) Śrī Rāma-Sītā. West of Paṇḍita Gaurīdāsa's house is the temple of Śrī Sūryadāsa Paṇḍita, and a little farther away the *āśrama* of the perfected Śrī Bhagavān dāsa Bābājī.

The place where the present temple stands is called “Ambikā,” and north of it is Kālnā; so together they are called “Ambikā-Kālnā.” It is said that the oar Śrī Mahāprabhu plied with His own hand and the *Gītā* written in His own hand (see the *Bhakti-ratnākara*, wave 7) are still kept in the temple.

> Subheading: (13) Purandara Paṇḍita —

Purandara Paṇḍita. *Caitanya-bhāgavata*, Antya, Chapter 6: “Purandara Paṇḍita is supremely peaceful and self-controlled; he is exclusively dear to Nityānanda-svarūpa.” Antya, Chapter 5: “Then the Prabhu came to the village of Khaḍadaha, to the place of Purandara Paṇḍita's temple. ## 29

> Subheading: (14) Parameśvarī dāsa (*gopāla* 5) —

Parameśvara, or Parameśvarī dāsa. *Caitanya-bhāgavata*, Antya, Chapter 6: “Parameśvara dāsa, whose life is Nityānanda; in his body Nityānanda sports.” Antya, Chapter 5: “Kṛṣṇadāsa and Parameśvara dāsa — these two, in the mood of cowherd boys, shout ‘He! He!’ all the time. Purandara Paṇḍita and Parameśvarī dāsa, in whose body Gauracandra is manifest, came running at once. Seeing the Prabhu, both wept, overcome with love.” For some time he lived in Khaḍadaha. In the *Gaura-gaṇoddeśa* (verse 132): “He who was formerly the friend named Arjuna is the servant Śrī Parameśvara.” He is one of the twelve *gopālas*, the friend “Arjuna.” When the queen Śrī Jāhnavā went to the festival at Kheturi, Parameśvara was with her (*Bhakti-ratnākara*, wave 10). By the order of Mother Jāhnavā he installed the Deity “Śrī Rādhā-Gopīnātha” at Āṭapura (see the same, wave 13).

In the *Śrī Vaiṣṇava-vandanā*: “With care I shall honor Parameśvara dāsa Ṭhākura, who made a jackal utter the holy name where *saṅkīrtana* was held.” The thirteenth wave of the *Bhakti-ratnākara* tells of Parameśvarī Ṭhākura.

The *śrīpāṭa* of Parameśvarī Ṭhākura is Āṭapura, on the Cā̃pāḍāṅgā branch of the Hāoḍā–Āmtā railway, near Āṭapura station and the ancient temple of Śrī Rādhā-Govinda, founded by the late Kṛṣṇakumāra Mitra, *dewan* of the Barddhamāna Rāja Teja Bāhādura. Formerly the place was called “Viśakhālā.”

Right in front of the temple grow two *bakula* trees side by side, giving dense shade, and separately a *kadamba* tree; between them is the *samādhi* of Parameśvarī Ṭhākura, beautifully crowned with a *tulasī* platform. It is said that the present two trees grew from branches of the two *bakula* trees that stood in Śrīla Parameśvarī Ṭhākura's time. Every year the *kadamba* tree bears a single flower, and with it the Deity's lotus feet are worshiped.

Parameśvarī Ṭhākura was born in a Vaidya family. His brother's descendants are the present servitors of the *śrīpāṭa*. Some of them also live in the village of Garalagāchā (Caṇḍītalā post office, Hugalī district). Until recently they had many disciples who were *brāhmaṇas* by birth. But in the course of time they took up the Vaidya profession, like ordinary worldly people, and so all their disciples of *brāhmaṇa* families gradually left them. Their title is “Adhikārī.” The present servitors of the *śrīpāṭa* are Śrī Rādhikāprasanna Adhikārī Kavirāja Mahāśaya and the widowed, sonless mother-in-law of Naṭavara Adhikārī Mahāśaya. The family name of their caste is “Gupta.”

They regard themselves as ordinary “Vaidyas” and have the Ṭhākura worshiped by *devala brāhmaṇas*. There are now eight households of servitors, and the eight households have merged into two. Formerly much land was set aside for the Deity's service, but they have lost all of it. Now the service of the Deity is kept up with great difficulty on a small *debottara* endowment.

In the temple, Śrī Baladeva and Śrī Rādhā-Gopīnātha are seen on a single throne. The Baladeva Deity was probably installed later. To place Śrī Baladeva and Śrī Kṛṣṇa with Śrīmatī on the same throne is contrary to *tattva*. On the full-moon day of Vaiśākha the festival of Parameśvarī Ṭhākura's disappearance is held.

## 30

> Subheading: (15) Jagadīśa Paṇḍita —

The *śrīpāṭa* of Śrī Jagadīśa Paṇḍita is the village of Yaśaḍā, within a mile of Cākadaha station (on the E.B.R. line) in Nadīyā district. See the *Caitanya-bhāgavata*, Ādi, Chapter 4, and the *Caitanya-caritāmṛta*, Ādi 14.39. From the account of the Yaśaḍā *śrīpāṭa* we learn that Jagadīśa Bhaṭṭa was born in East Bengal, in the region of Gauhāṭī. His father, Kamalākṣa, was a descendant of Bhaṭṭanārāyaṇa, of the Bandyaghaṭīya family of Gayaghara. Both Jagadīśa's father and his mother were householders devoted to Viṣṇu. After his parents' disappearance, Jagadīśa left his birthplace with his wife “Duḥkhinī” and his brother Maheśa. In order to live on the bank of the Gaṅgā and spend his time with Vaiṣṇavas, he came to Śrī Māyāpura and settled near the house of Śrī Jagannātha Miśra. Gaurasundara ordered Jagadīśa to go to Nīlācala to preach the holy name. By Śrīman Mahāprabhu's order Jagadīśa Paṇḍita preached the name in Nīlācala. There he prayed to Jagannāthadeva, and in answer to his prayer he was allowed to bring back a form of Jagannāthadeva. Jagadīśa installed the Deity in the village of Yaśaḍā on the bank of the Gaṅgā, now within Cākadaha police station. There is a tradition that Jagadīśa Paṇḍita carried this Jagannātha Deity from Puruṣottama to Yaśaḍā on a staff. Even today the servitors at Yaśaḍā display a staff as “Jagadīśa Paṇḍita's staff on which the Jagannātha Deity was brought.”

It is said that Śrī Gaura-Nityānanda Prabhu came to the village of Yaśaḍā twice with Their associates, and here there were *saṅkīrtana* processions, talks about Hari, *kīrtana* and a great festival.

Jagadīśa Paṇḍita enacted the pastime of a householder. His only son was named “Rāmabhadra Gosvāmī.”

Formerly the Jagannātha Deity was installed under a banyan tree on the bank of the Gaṅgā; later Rājā Kṛṣṇacandra of Goyāḍī-Kṛṣṇanagara had a temple built. When the temple built by the Rājā of Kṛṣṇanagara fell into ruin, Mokṣadā Dāsī, the wife of the local Umeśacandra Majumadāra, in 1324 restored the present temple; this is inscribed on a stone plaque. The temple has no spire and looks like an ordinary house. In front of it is a modest courtyard. In the temple reside Śrī Jagannāthadeva, Śrī Rādhā-vallabha Jīu and the Gaura-Gopāla installed by Jagadīśa's wife, Mother Duḥkhinī.

When Mahāprabhu had sanctified Jagadīśa's house in Yaśaḍā and was about to leave for Nīlācala, Duḥkhinī was deeply grieved at the separation from Gaurasundara. So Mahāprabhu agreed to accept Duḥkhinī's service in the village of Yaśaḍā in the form of the Gaura-Gopāla Deity. Ever since, the Śrī Gaura-Gopāla Deity (a golden-hued wooden form of Gopāla) has been served there.

The Gaṅgā has moved away from this place by nearly a *krośa*. For some time the perfected Bhagavān dāsa Bābājī Mahāśaya of Kālnā performed *bhajana* in the village of Yaśaḍā. Later the Bābājī Mahāśaya left this place and settled in Kālnā, but even from Kālnā he came here from time to time. At that time Vijayacandra Gosvāmī Mahāśaya was the servitor of Śrī Jagannāthadeva. The present servitor of the *śrīpāṭa* is Śrī Lalitamohana Gosvāmī. The family are Bā̃ḍuyyes (Bandyopādhyāyas); their maternal uncles are of the Gāṅgulī family. A *sūcaka-gāna* (introductory song) on Jagadīśa Paṇḍita Gosvāmī, composed by a Vaiṣṇava poet named Gadādhara, is still sung in the village of Yaśaḍā. The song sets out the life of Jagadīśa Paṇḍita in a few words. Raghunāthācārya, the son of Khañja Bhagavān, was a disciple of Jagadīśa Paṇḍita Gosvāmī.

Jagadīśa Paṇḍita's disappearance day is the third lunar day of the bright fortnight of Pauṣa. Every year on the twelfth lunar day of the bright fortnight of Pauṣa the festival of Jagadīśa Paṇḍita's birth is held, and many people gather for the Snāna-yātrā.

## 31

> Subheading: (16) Dhanañjaya Paṇḍita (*gopāla* 6) —

Śrī Dhanañjaya Paṇḍita lived in the village of Śītala near Kāṭoyā. He is one of the twelve *gopālas*, the friend “Vasudāma.” In the *Gaura-gaṇoddeśa* (verse 127): “The friend Vasudāma is Paṇḍita Śrī Dhanañjaya.” *Caitanya-bhāgavata*, Antya, Chapter 6: “Dhanañjaya Paṇḍita is an extraordinary *mahānta*; Nityānanda is in his heart every moment.”

Śītalagrāma lies in Barddhamāna district, within Maṅgalakoṭa police station and Kaicara post office. From Barddhamāna one takes the Kāṭoyā light railway, gets off at Kaicara station, 9 miles from Kāṭoyā, and goes 1 mile to the northeast. The temple is a thatched hut with a mud wall around it. Long ago the Mallika Bābus of the village of “Bājārabana Kābāśī” built a brick house for the Deity. That temple collapsed sixty-four or sixty-five years ago; the foundation of the old temple still remains. To the left of the entrance is a *tulasī* altar: this is the *samādhi* altar of Dhanañjaya Paṇḍita. In the west-facing house are Śrī Gopīnātha, whom Śrī Dhanañjaya served, Śrī Śrī Nitāi-Gaura, and the Deity of Śrī Dāmodara. Every year in the middle of the month of Māgha the Deities are carried to a garden a little way from the temple, and the disappearance festival is held. Some say that Dhanañjaya Paṇḍita's real birthplace is the village of “Jāḍ” in Caṭṭagrāma district. He came from there and established Deity service in Śītalagrāma and in the village of Sā̃caḍā-Pā̃caḍā.

It is said that he performed *saṅkīrtana* with Mahāprabhu in Navadvīpa for some time, returned to Śītalagrāma, and from there set out to see Śrī Vṛndāvana-dhāma. On the way to Vṛndāvana he stayed for a while in the village of Sā̃caḍā-Pā̃caḍā, three *krośas* south of present-day Memārī station. There he permitted the disciple traveling with him to establish the service of the Deity, and he himself went on to Vṛndāvana. That is why people also call the village of Sā̃caḍā-Pā̃caḍā “Dhanañjaya's *pāṭa*.” Now this village holds no trace of Dhanañjaya Paṇḍita; his principal *śrīpāṭa* is Śītalagrāma itself. On returning from Vṛndāvana he served the Deity in the village of Jalandi, and from there he came again to Śītalagrāma and established the service of Śrī Gaurāṅgadeva.

It is said that Dhanañjaya had no descendants. He had a brother named Sañjaya, whose son was named Rāmakānāi Ṭhākura. Sañjaya's *śrīpāṭa* is the village of Jalandi (Lokanagara post office) in Barddhamāna district, four or five *krośas* to the east. Among Sañjaya's descendants, Śrī Nīlamaṇi Ṭhākura, Śrī Rākhālacandra Ṭhākura and others, as well as descendants through a daughter, Śrī Kānāilāla Mukhopādhyāya and others, live in Jalandi itself. Śrī Rādhā-Govinda is served there. The *śrīpāṭa* of that Rāmakānāi is in Mulukagrāma, very close to present-day Bolapura; the servitors, Śrī Yugalakiśora Mukhopādhyāya and Śrī Gaurakiśora Mukhopādhyāya, live there. Some say that Sañjaya was Dhanañjaya's disciple. The present servitors at Śītalagrāma are descendants of a disciple of Dhanañjaya Paṇḍita. The ancient Deity Śrī Śyāmasundara Jīu, installed by Dhanañjaya's disciple Jīvanakṛṣṇa, is now in the house of Gopāla Rāya Caudhurī.

## 32

> Subheading: (17) Maheśa Paṇḍita (*gopāla* 7) —

Maheśa Paṇḍita. His *śrīpāṭa* was at Pālapāḍā until the first few months of the Bengali year 1334. It was then moved to the village of Kā̃ṭhāladhuli near Cākadaha. He is one of the twelve *gopālas*, the friend “Mahābāhu.” In the *Gaura-gaṇoddeśa* (verse 129): “Maheśa Paṇḍita is the glorious Mahābāhu, a friend in Vraja.” *Caitanya-bhāgavata*, Antya, Chapter 6: “Maheśa Paṇḍita is a most exalted *mahānta*.”

Pālapāḍā lies in Nadīyā district, in the jungle, a mile south of Cākadaha station on the E.B.R. line. The Gaṅgā is far from here. Formerly Maheśa Paṇḍita lived across from Jirāṭ, on the eastern bank, at a place called Masipura or Yaśīpura (?). But Masīpura was swallowed by the Gaṅgā, and the Deity installed by Maheśa Paṇḍita stayed for some time at Beleḍāṅgā near Sukhasāgara. When the Gaṅgā eroded and destroyed Beleḍāṅgā as well, the zamindar of Pālapāḍā, Navakumāra Caṭṭopādhyāya, prevailed on the servitor of that time, Bābājī Rāmakṛṣṇadāsa, to bring the Deity to Pālapāḍā, and he built a temple there. Navakumāra Bābu's son, Rajanī Bābu, registered the temple lands in the name of Harekṛṣṇadāsa Bābājī in 1883 A.D. Since then the name “Pālapāḍā-pāṭa” has continued. Pālapāḍā is in Pā̃canagara *parganā*. Fourteen *mauzās* — Beleḍāṅgā, Berigrāma, Sukhasāgara, Cānduḍe, Manasāpotā, Pālapāḍā and others — lie in Pā̃canagara, so some call the area “Nāgaradeśa.” Some hold that this Maheśa Paṇḍita was the younger brother of Jagadīśa Paṇḍita of the Yaśaḍā *śrīpāṭa*. They say that Jagadīśa, Hiraṇya and this Maheśa Paṇḍita were three brothers: Jagadīśa the eldest, Hiraṇya the middle, and Maheśa the youngest. But authoritative books do not say whether this Maheśa Paṇḍita was Śrī Jagadīśa's brother, so the claim is doubtful.

Śrī Maheśa Paṇḍita was with Śrī Nityānanda at the festival at Pāṇihāṭī, and after the festival he went with Śrī Nityānanda to Saptagrāma. The eighth wave of the *Bhakti-ratnākara* shows that when Śrī Narottama Ṭhākura came to Khaḍadaha, Śrī Maheśa Paṇḍita was present there.

The temple of the *śrīpāṭa* is a simple house. In the dilapidated temple reside the sacred forms of Śrī Gaura-Nityānanda, Śrī Gopīnātha, Śrī Madana-mohana, Śrī Rādhā-Govinda and a *śālagrāma*. In front of the temple is the altar of Maheśa Paṇḍita's *phula-samāja* (flower tomb). Now the service is maintained only by begging. A local resident, Śrī Kālīkṛṣṇa Cakravartī, contributes 25 rupees a year. Śrī Maheśa Paṇḍita has no descendants. The present servitor is Śrī Sanātana dāsa Bābājī.

## 37

> Subheading: (22) Kālā Kṛṣṇadāsa (*gopāla* 9) —

Kālā Kṛṣṇadāsa. *Caitanya-bhāgavata*, Antya, Chapter 6: “Kāliyā Kṛṣṇadāsa is famous throughout the three worlds; by remembering him one attains Gauracandra.” In the *Gaura-gaṇoddeśa* (verse 132): “He who is Lavaṅga, the friend in Vraja, is Kāla Śrī Kṛṣṇadāsa.” He is one of the twelve *gopālas*, the friend “Lavaṅga.” His *śrīpāṭa* is the village of “Ākāihāṭ” in Barddhamāna district, within Kāṭoyā police station and post office; it lies beside the “Navadvīpa–Kāṭoyā” highway that runs from Kāṭoyā. On the E.I.R. line one goes from Byāṇḍel junction to Kāṭoyā station and walks two miles from there; or one gets off at Dā̃ihāṭ, the station before Kāṭoyā, and the way is about a mile. The village of Ākāihāṭ is very small, and few people live there. The *śrīpāṭa* is now in decay. The temple over Kālā Kṛṣṇadāsa Ṭhākura's *samādhi* has collapsed: its beams broke and everything lies buried. Going from the road through a mango grove, one sees ahead a ruined little room. In it is the Deity's empty altar, and in its southeast corner a thatched shed, under which is the *samāja* of the servitors. The present servitor is Hareramadāsa Bābājī. To the south is a pond: this is “Nūpura-kuṇḍa.” Tradition says that the anklet (*nūpura*) of Raghunandana Ṭhākura, the son of Mukunda of Śrīkhaṇḍa — or, as some say, of Nityānanda Prabhu — fell into this pond. It is said that this anklet, along with Śrī Rādhā-vallabha and Śrī Gopāla Jīu of the Ākāihāṭ *śrīpāṭa*, is still kept at the *mahānta-bāṭī* in the village of Kaḍui, three *krośas* from Ākāihāṭ. There is a handwritten *Śrī Caitanya-bhāgavata* of the year 1200 and a *Śrī Caritāmṛta* copied in 1271. In the month of Caitra, on the twelfth lunar day of the dark fortnight — the day of Vāruṇī — the festival of Śrī Kālā Kṛṣṇadāsa Ṭhākura's disappearance is held here.

According to the “Gosvāmīs” of the village of “Sonātalā,” on the north bank of the river Ichāmatī about 3 miles west of the well-known Beḍābandara in Pābanā district, Kālā Kṛṣṇadāsa Ṭhākura was born in a *brāhmaṇa* family of the Varendra class, of the Bharadvāja *gotra*, and a “Bhāduḍa-grāmī.” From Ākāihāṭ, Kālā Kṛṣṇadāsa Ṭhākura, preaching the holy name, came to Pābanā. In the field where he set up his *āśrama*, ruins of buildings can still be seen. Later his kinsmen also came to this place. As there were no Vārendra *brāhmaṇas* at Ākāihāṭ, he married in this region, and after some time he went again to Ākāihāṭ and to Śrī Vṛndāvana. While he was living in the village of Sonātalā, a son named “Śrī Mohanadāsa” was born to him. He left the boy at the home of his maternal uncle in the village of Sonātalā, or Bhāduṭī-Mathurāpura, gave him all his property, and went with his wife to Śrī Vṛndāvana. In Śrī Vṛndāvana another son was born to him, named “Gaurāṅgadāsa.” Because Gaurāṅgadāsa was born in Śrī Vṛndāvana, he was also called Vṛndāvanadāsa. Later the father sent him too to his eldest son, Mohanadāsa, and told him to take a six-*ānā* share (six-sixteenths) of the property. Gaurāṅgadāsa manifested the Deity of Śrī Kālācā̃da, resembling Śrī Govinda Jīu of Śrī Vṛndāvana, and served Him together with his elder brother.

Kālā Kṛṣṇadāsa Ṭhākura's descendants, headed by Śrī Vijayagovinda Gosvāmī, still live today in Sonātalā and other places in Pābanā district.

At Sonātalā one can still see the site of the *āśrama* house, the bricks of the temple, and the *ghāṭa* of the pond. The Deity here, Śrī Kālācā̃da Jīu, is served in turn by Kālā Kṛṣṇadāsa's descendants themselves. Here, in the month of Agrahāyaṇa, on the twelfth lunar day of the dark fortnight, the festival of Kālā Kṛṣṇadāsa Ṭhākura's disappearance is held.

> Subheading: (23) Sadāśiva Kavirāja, (24) Puruṣottama (*gopāla* 10) —

## 39

> Subheading: “Nāgara” Puruṣottama —

## 38–39

Sadāśiva Kavirāja and Nāgara Puruṣottama. *Caitanya-bhāgavata*, Antya, Chapter 6: “Sadāśiva Kavirāja is greatly fortunate; his son is named Śrī Puruṣottama dāsa. Puruṣottama dāsa's body has no external consciousness: Nityānanda-candra sports in his heart.” In the *Gaura-gaṇoddeśa* (verse 156): “She who was formerly Candrāvalī in Vraja, most dear to Kṛṣṇa, is now in Gauḍa the Kavirāja Sadāśiva.” Verse 131: “Sadāśiva's son, named Nāgara Puruṣottama, born in a Vaidya family, is he who in Vraja was the cowherd named Dāma.” Sadāśiva Kavirāja's father, Kaṁsāri Sena, is in Kṛṣṇa's pastimes the *sakhī* “Ratnāvalī” of Vraja. Some say that Kaṁsāri Sena lived at Guptipāḍā on the E.I.R. line, but no trace of him can be found there now. Be that as it may, perfected devotees of Gaura like these, four generations in a row, are rare anywhere else.

Śrī Puruṣottama Ṭhākura's *śrīpāṭa* was formerly at Sukhasāgara, equidistant from Cākadaha and Śimurāli stations. First, when the village of Beleḍāṅgā was destroyed, Śrī Puruṣottama Ṭhākura's Deities were brought to Sukhasāgara. Then Sukhasāgara too was swallowed by the Gaṅgā. So Puruṣottama Ṭhākura's Deity was brought to Sāhebaḍāṅgā-Beḍigrāma together with the Deities of Mother Śrī Jāhnavā's *gādi* (seat), which had been at that place. When Beḍigrāma too was destroyed, Puruṣottama Ṭhākura's Deity, together with the Deities of Mother Jāhnavā's *gādi*, was moved to the village of Cā̃nduḍe, where He has now resided for about fifty years.

The village of Cā̃nduḍe on the Bhāgīrathī is in Nadīyā district, within Cākadaha police station, a mile from “Simurāli” station on the E.B.R. line and a mile's walk from “Pālapāḍā.”

Since the old Sukhasāgara was swallowed by the river, the new Sukhasāgara lies 3–4 miles from the village of Cā̃nduḍe, south of Kālīgañja.

Śrī Nityānanda Prabhu's son-in-law, the husband of Śrīmatī Gaṅgā-devī, was Śrī Mādhavācārya (Mādhava Caṭṭopādhyāya) of Jirāṭ; according to some he was a disciple of Śrī Nityānanda Prabhu, according to others of Śrī Jāhnavā-devī, and according to others of this Śrī Puruṣottama Ṭhākura. The *Vaiṣṇava-vandanā* states plainly that its author, Śrī Devakīnandana dāsa, was a disciple of this very Puruṣottama.

The present temple is a thatched hut with a mud wall. In the temple reside Śrī Jagannātha, Balarāma and Subhadrā; Nitāi-Gaura, two pairs of Deities; Gopīnātha; Mother Jāhnavā; Bāla-Gopāla; Rādhā-Govinda, five pairs; Revatī and Balarāma; and a *śālagrāma*. Some say that several of these sacred forms were installed by Puruṣottama Ṭhākura and the rest belong to Jāhnavā-devī's *gādi*. This *śrīpāṭa* is also known as “the *pāṭa* of Vasu-Jāhnavā.” The names of the following servitor-*mahāntas* of the Cā̃nduḍe *śrīpāṭa* are known: (1) Gopāladāsa Mahānta, (2) Rāmakṛṣṇa, (3) Vihāridāsa, (4) Rāmadāsa, (5) Gopāladāsa and (6) the present aged servitor, Sītānātha dāsa.

## 40

> Subheading: (25) Kānu Ṭhākura —

Kānu Ṭhākura. Some count him among the twelve *gopālas*. He lived at Bodhakhānā. On the Central Section of the E.B.R. line one gets off at “Jhikaragāchā-ghāṭ” station, and from there, by boat on the river Kapotākṣa or overland, it is 2 or 2½ miles to the *śrīpāṭa* of Bodhakhānā.

Ṭhākura Kānāi's ancestor four generations back, Śrī Kaṁsāri Sena, was also called “Sambarāri.” In the *Vaiṣṇava-vandanā* Devakīnandana names four generations of Ṭhākura Kānāi's ancestors: “I honor Śrī Kaṁsāri Sena and Sena Śrīvallabha. With all my heart I honor Sadāśiva Kavirāja, who is ever mad with love and knows nothing external. I honor my chosen lord, named Śrī Puruṣottama. Who can describe his incomparable qualities?”

Puruṣottama Ṭhākura was the son of Sadāśiva, and Kānu Ṭhākura was the son of Puruṣottama Ṭhākura. Kānu Ṭhākura's descendants hold that Puruṣottama Ṭhākura and “Nāgara Puruṣottama” were different persons. They say that Kānu Ṭhākura's father is the one mentioned in the *Gaura-gaṇoddeśa* as “Dāsa Puruṣottama,” who is “Stokakṛṣṇa” in the pastimes of Vraja. But in the *Gaura-gaṇoddeśa* it is Puruṣottama, the son of Sadāśiva, born in a Vaidya family, who is called “Nāgara Puruṣottama.” This Nāgara Puruṣottama is the friend named “Dāma” in the pastimes of Vraja. Among Kānu Ṭhākura's descendants a tradition is current that Puruṣottama Ṭhākura lived in the village of “Sukhasāgara” on the east bank of the Gaṅgā. Puruṣottama's wife was named “Jāhnavā.” Soon after Ṭhākura Kānāi's birth, Jāhnavā passed away. When Nityānanda Prabhu learned that Puruṣottama Ṭhākura had lost his wife, He came to Puruṣottama's house and took the twelve-year-old[^40-1] child to His own home at Khaḍadaha.

According to Kānu Ṭhākura's descendants, Ṭhākura Kānāi appeared in Śaka 1457 (Bengali year 942), on the second lunar day of the bright fortnight of Āṣāḍha, a Thursday, the day of the Ratha-yātrā. Seeing the Ṭhākura's devotion to Kṛṣṇa from infancy, Nityānanda Prabhu named him “Śiśu Kṛṣṇadāsa” (the child Kṛṣṇadāsa).

In his fifth year the child Kṛṣṇadāsa went with the queen Mother Jāhnavā to Śrī Vṛndāvana-dhāma. The Vrajavāsīs headed by Śrī Jīva Gosvāmī, seeing the child Kṛṣṇadāsa's *bhāva* and other signs, gave him the name “Ṭhākura Kānāi.” There is a story that once in Vṛndāvana, as Ṭhākura Kānāi danced, overwhelmed by the bliss of *kīrtana*, an anklet vanished from his right foot. Ṭhākura Kānāi then said, “Wherever this anklet has fallen, there I will live.” It is said that the anklet fell in a village called “Bodhakhānā” in Yaśohara district, and from then on Ṭhākura Kānāi came to live at Bodhakhānā.

Another tradition is handed down in this family: the Deity “Prāṇavallabha” has been served since some ancestor of Sadāśiva several hundred years before the appearance of Śrīman Mahāprabhu. This “Prāṇavallabha” is still served at Bodhakhānā.

At the time of the “Bargī hāṅgāmā” (the Bargī raids), all of Ṭhākura Kānāi's sons, headed by Vaṁśīvadana, except the descendants of his eldest son, fled Bodhakhānā and settled in a village called “Bhājana-ghāṭ” in Nadīyā district. One of the descendants of Ṭhākura Kānāi's younger sons, named “Harikṛṣṇa Gosvāmī,” came back to Bodhakhānā after the Bargī raids had ended. He installed another, new Deity, also named “Prāṇavallabha.” Even now, in the village of Bodhakhānā, the descendants of Ṭhākura Kānāi's eldest son serve the ancient “Śrī Prāṇavallabha,” and the descendants of the younger son serve the newly installed “Prāṇavallabha.” At Bhājana-ghāṭ the Deity “Śrī Rādhā-vallabha” is served. The book *Prema-vilāsa* shows that Kānu Ṭhākura was present at the Kheturi festival together with Jāhnavā-devī and Vīrabhadra Prabhu.

Puruṣottama Ṭhākura and Kānu Ṭhākura had many disciples who were *brāhmaṇas* by birth. The four chief disciples of Puruṣottama Ṭhākura who were *brāhmaṇas* by birth are named thus: “His four dearest disciples, the best of *brāhmaṇas*: first Mādhavācārya, then Yādavācārya Paṇḍita, and Daivakīnandana dāsa, famous in Gauḍa-maṇḍala, who composed the book *Śrīmad Vaiṣṇava-vandanā*.” This Mādhavācārya is the husband of Gaṅgā-devī, the daughter of Śrī Nityānanda Prabhu. After the village of Sukhasāgara was destroyed, the Deities installed by Puruṣottama were brought to Cānduḍiyā, and they are now served together with his other Deities under the supervision of the descendants of Gaṅgā at Jirāṭ. Puruṣottama Ṭhākura's *śrīpāṭa* is also called the *pāṭa* of “Vasu-Jāhnavā.”

Kānu Ṭhākura's disciples live in the village of Gaḍabetā on the bank of the river Śilāvatī in Medinīpura district. A *brāhmaṇa* named “Śrīrāma,” of the Rāḍhī class and of the Kauthumī branch of the *Sāma Veda*, was a famous disciple of Kānāi Ṭhākura.

## 41

> Subheading: (26) Uddhāraṇa Ṭhākura (*gopāla* 11) —

Uddhāraṇa Datta. In the *Gaura-gaṇoddeśa* (verse 129): “He who in Vraja was the cowherd Subāhu is Datta, named Uddhāraṇa.” He lived in Saptagrāma, on the bank of the river Sarasvatī near Triśabighā station in Hugalī district. Formerly “Saptagrāma” meant Vāsudevapura, Bā̃śaberiyā, Kṛṣṇapura, Nityānandapura, Śivapura, Śaṅkhanagara and Saptagrāma taken together. *Caitanya-bhāgavata*, Antya, Chapter 6: “Uddhāraṇa Datta is a great and generous Vaiṣṇava, who was given the right to serve Nityānanda.” Antya, Chapter 5: “After staying some days at Khaḍadaha, Nityānanda came to Saptagrāma with all His associates. The best of lords stayed on the bank of the Triveṇī, in the temple of the fortunate Uddhāraṇa Datta. In body, mind and words Datta Uddhāraṇa served Nityānanda's feet without pretense. Through Uddhāraṇa the whole merchant community was purified — of this there is no doubt.” By birth he belonged to a Suvarṇa-vaṇik family.

At Saptagrāma there is a six-armed form of Mahāprabhu, installed by Śrīla Uddhāraṇa Ṭhākura and served by him with his own hands. To His right is Śrī Nityānanda and to His left Śrī Gadādhara. There are also forms of Śrī Rādhā-Govinda and a Śrī Śālagrāma, and beneath the throne-altar a painting of Śrī Uddhāraṇa Ṭhākura Mahāśaya is worshiped. In front of the present temple is a large *nāṭya-mandira*. A memorial stone plaque on a pillar of the *nāṭya-mandira* bears the names of the builder of the temple and of those who restored the *śrīpāṭa*. Right in front of the *nāṭya-mandira* is a cool, shady *mādhavī* bower. On either side of the bower stand two pillars: on one is a painting of Uddhāraṇa Ṭhākura Mahāśaya, and on the other, on a stone plaque, the four *tāraka-brahma-nāmas* of the four *yugas* are inscribed.

In 1283 a mendicant named Nitāidāsa Bābājī collected 12 *bighās* of land for the *śrīpāṭa*. Then, through the special efforts of certain people, the *śrīpāṭa* kept up for a while, but gradually the service fell into disorder. Later, in 1306, through the combined efforts of Balarāma Mallika, former Sub-judge of Hugalī, and many wealthy Suvarṇa-vaṇiks of Calcutta, some order is now seen again in the service of the *śrīpāṭa*.

Half a century ago a wooden image of Śrīla Uddhāraṇa Ṭhākura, installed by the late Jagamohana Datta of Bāli in Hugalī, resided in a broken-down hut. That image is no longer at the Saptagrāma *śrīpāṭa*, and his painting is worshiped now. On inquiry we heard that Uddhāraṇa Ṭhākura's former image is now in the house of Śrī Madana Datta Mahāśaya of Bāli, Hugalī, and the *śālagrāma* the Ṭhākura served is in the same village, in the house of Śrīnātha Datta.

Ṭhākura Uddhāraṇa was the *dewan* of the Rājā of the village of “Naihāṭī,” 1½ miles north of Kāṭoyā. Near Dā̃ihāṭ station the ruins of that royal family's palace can still be seen. The place where the Ṭhākura lived while serving the Rājā is still called “Uddhāraṇapura.” It is said that the Śrī Nitāi-Gaura Deity the Ṭhākura installed here was taken to the capital of Banoyārībāda. West of the temple here (or, according to some, in Vṛndāvana) is the Ṭhākura's *samādhi*. According to some, the Ṭhākura's father was named Śrīkara, his mother Bhadrāvatī, and his son Śrīnivāsa.

## 42

> Subheading: (27) Vaiṣṇavānanda Ācārya —

Vaiṣṇavānanda Ācārya. *Caitanya-bhāgavata*: “Ācārya Vaiṣṇavānanda is supremely generous; formerly he was known as ‘Raghunātha Purī.’” The *Gaura-gaṇoddeśa* (verse 97), naming Raghunātha Purī among the eight Purīs, describes him as one of the eight *siddhis*, beginning with *aṇimā*.

## 43

> Subheading: (28) Viṣṇudāsa, Nandana, Gaṅgādāsa — three brothers —

Viṣṇudāsa, Nandana and Gaṅgādāsa were three brothers, Bhaṭṭācāryas of Navadvīpa. Viṣṇudāsa and Gaṅgādāsa stayed with Mahāprabhu at Nīlācala for some time (Ādi 10.151). Mahāprabhu and Advaita Prabhu hid in Nandana Ācārya's house. Śrī Nityānanda Prabhu, on coming to Navadvīpa, also stayed there for a few days. *Caitanya-bhāgavata*, Antya, Chapter 6: “Gaṅgādāsa, the son of Caturbhuja Paṇḍita — in his house Nityānanda once sported.”

## 44

> Subheading: (29) Paramānanda Upādhyāya, (30) Śrī Jīva Paṇḍita —

Paramānanda Upādhyāya. *Caitanya-bhāgavata*, Antya, Chapter 6: “Paramānanda Upādhyāya is an exclusively devoted Vaiṣṇava.”

Śrī Jīva Paṇḍita was the middle son of Ratnagarbha Ācārya, a childhood friend of Hāḍāi Ojhā, Nityānanda's father. *Caitanya-bhāgavata*, Antya, Chapter 6: “The generous Jīva Paṇḍita is a great devotee; in his house Nityānanda-candra sported.” In Vraja he is Indirā (*Gaura-gaṇoddeśa*, verse 169).

## 45

> Subheading: (31) Paramānanda Gupta —

Paramānanda Gupta. *Caitanya-bhāgavata*, Antya, Chapter 6: “Famous is Paramānanda Gupta Mahāśaya; formerly Nityānanda lived in his house.” In the *Gaura-gaṇoddeśa* (verses 194 and 199) he is Mañjumedhā of Vraja: “The *Kṛṣṇa-stavāvalī* composed by Paramānanda Gupta.”

## 46

> Subheading: (32) Nārāyaṇa, (33) Kṛṣṇadāsa, (34) Manohara, (35) Devānanda —

Nārāyaṇa, Kṛṣṇadāsa, Manohara and Devānanda. *Caitanya-bhāgavata*, Antya, Chapter 6: “Kṛṣṇadāsa and Devānanda are two pure-hearted ones. Dear to Nityānanda are these four: ‘Manohara,’ ‘Nārāyaṇa,’ ‘Kṛṣṇadāsa’ and ‘Devānanda.’”
