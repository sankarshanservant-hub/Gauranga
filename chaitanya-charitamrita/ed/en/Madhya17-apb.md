# Amṛta-pravāha-bhāṣya on Madhya-līlā 17

## 0

**Summary.** That year, after seeing the Ratha-yātrā in Śrī Kṣetra, Mahāprabhu firmly resolved to go to Vṛndāvana. Śrī Rāmānanda and Śrī Svarūpa gave Him Balabhadra Bhaṭṭācārya as a companion, together with a brāhmaṇa (a servant) who accompanied him. Before daybreak the Lord set out towards Kaṭaka; then, keeping Kaṭaka on His right, He went on by lonely forest paths. On that forest road, full of love, He made tigers, elephants and other animals sing the name of Kṛṣṇa. Wherever they found a village, they begged alms there and cooked rice, vegetables and so on. Where there were no villages (no people), they cooked the rice they had stored and gathered wild greens and the like. The Lord was very pleased with Balabhadra Bhaṭṭācārya’s care. Travelling thus by the forest paths of Jhārikhaṇḍa, the Lord arrived in Vārāṇasī-dhāma. As He was bathing at the Maṇikarṇikā *ghāṭa*, Tapana Miśra met Him. Tapana Miśra took the Lord to his home and lodged Him there with great care. In Vārāṇasī the Lord was also served by His devotee of long standing, Candraśekhara Vaidya (who was now living there). A brāhmaṇa from Mahārāṣṭra saw how the Lord conducted Himself and told Prakāśānanda Sarasvatī, the chief of the sannyāsīs, about it, and Prakāśānanda spoke much against the Lord. The brāhmaṇa was pained by this. He came to the Lord, told Him what had been said, and asked why the name of Kṛṣṇa did not come to the lips of Prakāśānanda and the other sannyāsīs. In reply the Lord ruled that Māyāvāda is an offence (*aparādha*), forbade association with Māyāvādīs, and so showed the brāhmaṇa His mercy. (Then the Lord) went from Kāśī by way of Prayāga to Mathurā. There He blessed a Sanoḍiyā brāhmaṇa, a disciple of Śrī Mādhavendra Purī, and accepted a meal in his home. Afterwards, in great love, the Lord began to wander through the twelve forests of Śrī Vṛndāvana, listening to the conversation of Śārī and Śuka.

## 1

On His way to Vṛndāvana, in the forest (along His road), Śrī Gauracandra made the tigers, elephants, deer and birds repeat the name of Kṛṣṇa, maddened them with love, and made them dance.

## 12

“A *bhojyānna* brāhmaṇa” means a brāhmaṇa whose food may be eaten, that is, one from whom it is not wrong to accept food.

## 14

“There is no need for Kālā Kṛṣṇadāsa and others to come with Me as before; but I may take some new companion whose heart is loving.”

## 19

*Vastrāmbu-bhājana* means His cloth and water pot.

## 36

Blessed are these simple-minded does: they found the son of Nanda in His wonderful attire and heard the sound of His flute, and together with the black bucks (*kṛṣṇa-sāras*) they worshipped Him with glances full of love.

## 39

There men, tigers and others, though by nature hostile to one another, live together as friends. Anger, thirst and the like have abandoned that abode and fled, because it is the place of Kṛṣṇa’s delight (His eternal pastimes). (It was this transcendental abode of Vṛndāvana that Brahmā beheld.)

## 46

Jhārikhaṇḍa is the name of a well-known forest region on the road to Vṛndāvana (today the hilly, forested states of Āṭagaṛa, Ḍheṅkānala, Āṅgula, Lāhārā, Kiyoñjhara, Bāmaṛā, Bonāi, Gāṅgapura, Choṭanāgapura, Yaśapura, Saragujā and others).

## 80

I bow down to Mādhava, the embodiment of supreme bliss (*paramānanda-svarūpa*), whose mercy can make the mute eloquent and enable the lame to cross a mountain.

## 90

Tapana Miśra’s son Raghunātha, who later became famous as “Bhaṭṭa Gosvāmī,” began to massage the Lord’s feet.

## 92

*Likhana-vṛtti* means earning one’s living by copying manuscripts.

## 98

*Tāra* means “deliver.” *Bhṛtya duijane*, “two servants,” means the two of them: Candraśekhara and Tapana Miśra.

## 120

*Bhāva-kāli* means the ways of a *bhāvuka*, a sentimentalist.

## 121

Keeping company with those who have broken the chains of scriptural injunction ruins both worlds, this one and the next.

## 129–132

The Lord said: The Māyāvādī does not accept the *jīva-tattva* as transcendental (*aprākṛta*). He decides that a jīva is a fragment of Brahman covered by māyā, and since he takes Brahman to be featureless (*nirviśeṣa*), he calls even the (eternal, conscious and blissful) form of Bhagavān “a form made of māyā.” For this very reason the Māyāvādī, who considers Kṛṣṇa’s name, form, qualities and pastimes “noneternal,” has become a great offender. Rejecting Kṛṣṇa’s “principal names” (*mukhya-nāma*), he utters “secondary names” (*gauṇa-nāma*) such as “Brahman,” “Ātmā” and “Caitanya.” And even if the principal names—“Govinda,” “Mādhava,” “Kṛṣṇa”—sometimes come out of his mouth, the “name” of Kṛṣṇa, the spiritual Form, never comes from his mouth, because his understanding is faulty: he does not believe in Kṛṣṇa’s name and takes it for one of the ordinary, worldly words. In truth Kṛṣṇa’s name and Kṛṣṇa’s *svarūpa* are both spiritual; that is, the name, the form (*vigraha*) and the *svarūpa* are all three full of spiritual bliss. The body of a conditioned jīva is separate from the “embodied one” (*dehī*), the jīva himself, and the name his father gave him is separate from his “self,” or *svarūpa*, and depends on matter. But with Kṛṣṇa it is not so: Kṛṣṇa’s body is Himself the *dehī*, and Kṛṣṇa’s name is Himself the “bearer of the name” (*nāmī*). Since Kṛṣṇa has no connection with māyā or with the matter māyā produces, a difference between “body and *dehī*” or “name and *nāmī*” is impossible in Him. Only for the conditioned jīva do the body and the *dehī*, the name and the *nāmī*, differ; that is, only in the jīva do the name, the body and the *svarūpa* have properties separate from one another.

## 133

The name of Kṛṣṇa is a special touchstone (*cintāmaṇi*), spiritual by nature. It is Kṛṣṇa, the embodiment of spiritual rasa. It is complete, that is, not limited or fragmented like the things of māyā. It is pure, that is, unmixed with māyā. It is eternally free, that is, always spiritual and never bound by matter—for in their nature the name and the bearer of the name do not differ at all.

## 136

Therefore the name, form, qualities and pastimes of Śrī Kṛṣṇa are never perceived by worldly eyes, ears and other senses. When the jīva turns towards service (*sevonmukha*), that is, turns towards Kṛṣṇa in his spiritual nature, then Kṛṣṇa’s name and the rest manifest of Themselves in his transcendental senses—the tongue and the others.

## 137

In those in whom the thought “I am Brahman” arises, thoughts of māyā fall away, and a little happiness does arise in them: abiding in spiritual Brahman. But those who can awaken in their hearts the spiritual play of rasa—Kṛṣṇa’s name, Kṛṣṇa’s form, Kṛṣṇa’s qualities and Kṛṣṇa’s pastimes—taste the rasa of pastimes full of bliss, infinitely superior to “the bliss of Brahman.” Therefore Kṛṣṇa’s pastimes, the very rasa of pastimes full of bliss, suddenly attract the knower of Brahman and bring him under their sway.

## 138

At first his heart was absorbed in the happiness of Brahman; later he gave that happiness up, was drawn by Kṛṣṇa’s pastimes full of sweetness, and set forth the *Śrīmad-Bhāgavata Purāṇa*, the lamp of truth about Kṛṣṇa. To that guru, the destroyer of all sins, Śrī Śuka, the son of Vyāsa, I bow.

## 142

The breeze, laden with the honeyed fragrance of the tulasī mixed with the filaments of the lotus feet of the lotus-eyed Bhagavān, entered through the nostrils of the four Sanas, who were devoted to the featureless Brahman, and stirred their minds and bodies.

## 145

(The vessel of the spiritual rasa of the name) is a very heavy load; I sell it to the jīvas for the price of full faith (*śraddhā*). It is very hard for a merchant to carry such a heavy load back home. So if I get even a small price—that is, the price of a semblance of faith (*śraddhābhāsa*)—I will sell it here.

## 149

Mādhava: Veṇī-mādhava.

## 156

Viśrāma-tīrtha: the famous Viśrāma-ghāṭa. “Keśava at the birthplace”: the Deity of Śrī Keśavajī at Śrī Kṛṣṇa’s birthplace.

## 179

In the western country the *vaiśyas* are divided into several groups: the “Agarwāla,” the “Kalwār,” the “Sānoyāṛ” and others. Of these the Agarwālas are the purest, while the Kalwārs, Sānoyāṛs and other classes have fallen through the faults of their own occupations. Those who serve as priests (*yājana*) for these Kalwārs and Sānoyāṛs are called “Sānoḍiyā brāhmaṇas” and so on. The word “Sānoyāṛ” means “gold merchant” (*suvarṇa-vaṇik*), and the brāhmaṇas who serve them as priests are the “Sānoḍiyā (*varṇa*-) brāhmaṇas.” Since these brāhmaṇas have fallen through the fault of such priestly service, sannyāsīs do not eat in their homes.

## 186

Argument by its very nature has no firm ground; the *śrutis* too differ; and one whose opinion does not differ [from others] cannot be a sage. Therefore the truth of dharma lies deeply hidden; that is, it is hard to grasp the truth of dharma by reading the scriptures. So all other people should walk the path that he whom the sādhus have recognised as a “*mahājana*” has called “the path of the scriptures.”

## 190

The twenty-four *ghāṭas* of the Yamunā: (1) Avimukta, (2) Adhirūḍha, (3) Guhya-tīrtha, (4) Prayāga-tīrtha, (5) Kanakhala-tīrtha, (6) Tinduka, (7) Sūrya-tīrtha, (8) Vaṭasvāmī, (9) Dhruva-ghāṭa, (10) Ṛṣi-tīrtha, (11) Mokṣa-tīrtha, (12) Bodha-tīrtha, (13) Gokarṇa, (14) Kṛṣṇa-gaṅgā, (15) Vaikuṇṭha, (16) Asi-kuṇḍa, (17) the Catuḥ-sāmudrika well, (18) Akrūra-tīrtha, (19) the place of the sacrificing brāhmaṇas, (20) Kubjā’s well, (21) Raṅga-sthala, (22) Mañca-sthala, (23) the place of the wrestling match and (24) Daśāśvamedha.

## 192

The forests: the twelve forests. East of Śrī Yamunā there are five: Bhadravana, Bilvavana, Lohavana, Bhāṇḍīravana and Mahāvana. West of the Yamunā there are seven: Madhuvana, Tālavana, Kumudavana, Bahulāvana, Kāmavana, Khadiravana and Vṛndāvana.

## 210

Śrī Śuka said: “His beauty steals the composure of women; His pastimes stun the goddess Lakṣmī; His prowess turns Govardhana Hill into a plaything like a ball. His pure qualities lie beyond a *parārdha*; His character delights all people. May my Lord, Kṛṣṇa, the enchanter of the world, whose fame is for the good of the whole world, protect the universe.”

## 212

Śārī said: “The love, beauty, good conduct, skill in dancing and singing, poetic gift and other qualities of Śrīmatī Rādhikā shine, enchanting the heart of Kṛṣṇa, who enchants the mind of the world.”

## 214

Śuka said: “O Śārikā, may Madana-mohana be victorious—He who holds the flute, steals the hearts of the women of the world and sports with the gopīs!”

## 216

Śārī replied in jest: “Only when Kṛṣṇa shines beside Rādhā is He ‘Madana-mohana.’ When Śrī Rādhā is not with Him, He, though the enchanter of the universe, is Himself enchanted by Madana.”

## 233

*Pāthāra*: a flood, a rising of the waters.

*Thus ends the seventeenth chapter of the “Amṛta-pravāha-bhāṣya.”*
