# Amṛta-pravāha-bhāṣya on Madhya-līlā 17

## 0

**Summary.** That year, after seeing the Ratha-yātrā in Śrī Kṣetra, Mahāprabhu firmly resolved to go to Vṛndāvana. Śrī Rāmānanda and Śrī Svarūpa gave Him Balabhadra Bhaṭṭācārya as a companion, together with a brāhmaṇa (a servant) who accompanied him. Before daybreak the Lord set out toward Kaṭaka; then, keeping Kaṭaka on His right, He went on by lonely forest paths. On that forest road, full of love, He made tigers, elephants and other animals sing the name of Kṛṣṇa. Wherever they found a village, they begged alms there and cooked rice, vegetables and so on. Where there were no villages (no people), they cooked the rice they had stored and gathered wild greens and the like. The Lord was very pleased with Balabhadra Bhaṭṭācārya's care. Travelling thus by the forest paths of Jhārikhaṇḍa, the Lord arrived in Vārāṇasī-dhāma. As He was bathing at the Maṇikarṇikā *ghāṭa*, Tapana Miśra met Him. Tapana Miśra took the Lord to his home and lodged Him there with great care. In Vārāṇasī the Lord was also served by His devotee of long standing, Candraśekhara Vaidya (who was now living there). A brāhmaṇa from Mahārāṣṭra saw how the Lord conducted Himself and told Prakāśānanda Sarasvatī, the chief of the *sannyāsīs*, about it, and Prakāśānanda spoke much against the Lord. The brāhmaṇa was pained by this. He came to the Lord, told Him what had been said, and asked why the name of Kṛṣṇa did not come to the lips of Prakāśānanda and the other *sannyāsīs*. In reply the Lord ruled that Māyāvāda is an offence (*aparādha*), forbade association with Māyāvādīs, and so showed the brāhmaṇa His mercy. (Then the Lord) went from Kāśī by way of Prayāga to Mathurā. There He blessed a Sanoḍiyā brāhmaṇa, a disciple of Śrī Mādhavendra Purī, and accepted a meal in his home. Afterwards, in great love, the Lord began to wander through the twelve forests of Śrī Vṛndāvana, listening to the conversation of Śārī and Śuka.

## 1

On His way to Vṛndāvana, in the forest (along His road), Śrī Gauracandra made the tigers, elephants, deer and birds repeat the name of Kṛṣṇa, maddened them with love, and made them dance.

## 12

“A *bhojyānna* brāhmaṇa” means a brāhmaṇa whose food may be eaten, that is, one from whom it is not wrong to accept food.

## 14

“There is no need for Kālā Kṛṣṇadāsa and others to come with Me as before; but I may take some new companion whose heart is loving.”

## 19

*Vastrāmbu-bhājana* means His cloth and water pot.

## 36

Blessed are these simple-minded does: they found the son of Nanda in His wonderful attire and heard the sound of His flute, and together with the black bucks (*kṛṣṇa-sāras*) they worshipped Him with glances full of love.

## 39

There men, tigers and others, though by nature hostile to one another, live together as friends. Anger, thirst and the like have abandoned that abode and fled, because it is the place of Kṛṣṇa's delight (His eternal pastimes). (It was this transcendental abode of Vṛndāvana that Brahmā beheld.)

## 46

Jhārikhaṇḍa is the name of a well-known forest region on the road to Vṛndāvana (today the hilly, forested states of Āṭagaṛa, Ḍheṅkānala, Āṅgula, Lāhārā, Kiyoñjhara, Bāmaṛā, Bonāi, Gāṅgapura, Choṭanāgapura, Yaśapura, Saragujā and others).
