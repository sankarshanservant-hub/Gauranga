# Anubhāṣya on Ādi-līlā 3

## 1

> Subheading: Obeisance to Mahāprabhu in order to gather the conclusions (*siddhānta*) of devotion —

A verse composed by the author himself.

**ajñaḥ** (even an ignorant person) **yat-pādāśraya-vīryataḥ** (by the power of shelter at whose feet, that is, at the feet of Śrī Caitanya) **ākara-vrātāt** (from the many mines, the places where metals are born) **siddhānta-san-maṇīn** (the true jewels of conclusions) **saṅgṛhṇāti** (is able to gather fully) ([that]) **śrī-caitanya-prabhum** (Śrī Caitanya Prabhu) ([I]) **vande** (worship).

## 4

> Subheading: Explanation of the fourth of the fourteen opening verses (*Vidagdha-mādhava*, Act 1, verse 2) —

Śrī Rūpa Gosvāmī used this verse as the *maṅgalācaraṇa* (blessing the world) at the opening of his play *Vidagdha-mādhava*. The author, his follower, therefore follows in the footsteps of his own beloved guru.

**cirāt** (for a long time) **anarpita-carīm** (never given before) **unnatojjvala-rasām** (in which the *ujjvala-rasa*, the *rasa* of amorous love [*śṛṅgāra*], is exalted, that is, fully developed) **sva-bhakti-śriyam** (the beauty of love for Himself) **samarpayitum** (in order to bestow fully) **kalau** (in the age of Kali) **karuṇayā avatīrṇaḥ** (who has come into the world out of mercy) **puraṭa-sundara-dyuti-kadamba-sandīpitaḥ** (He who is brightly illumined by a mass of beautiful golden radiance) **śacī-nandanaḥ** (the son of Śacī) **hariḥ** (Hari) **vaḥ** (your) **hṛdaya-kandare** (in the cave of the heart, that is, of the mind) **sadā** (always, day and night) **sphuratu** (may He manifest).

## 5

> Subheading: The time of His descent —

## 7–8

The age of Kali lasts 432,000 solar years. Dvāpara has twice as many years as Kali, Tretā three times as many and Satya four times as many. Satya, Tretā, Dvāpara and Kali together therefore last 4,320,000 solar years. This *mahā-yuga* has been given the name *divya-yuga*. Seventy-one such *mahā-yugas* make one *manvantara*. Fourteen *manvantaras*, together with the junctions (*sandhi*) between them, which altogether equal fifteen Satya-yugas, make a thousand *yugas* — one day of Brahmā, or a *kalpa*.

“…A *catur-yuga* is declared to be, in solar years, [the numbers] two, three and [the four] oceans, multiplied by ten thousand.[^7-1] Seventy-one such *yugas* are here called a *manvantara*. Know that in a *kalpa* there are fourteen Manus together with their junctions [*sandhi*]; at the beginning of the *kalpa* there is one more junction, as long as a Kṛta-yuga, and so fifteen are counted. Thus a *kalpa* of a thousand *yugas*, which brings about the destruction of living beings, is called a day of Brahmā; his night is just as long.” (*Sūrya-siddhānta*, the chapter on mean motions [*Madhyamādhikāra*])

## 9

Mahāprabhu appears in the *manvantara* of the seventh Manu, named Vaivasvata. “The first Manu was Svāyambhuva. [After him] were born Svārociṣa, Uttama, Tāmasa, then Raivata and Cākṣuṣa; the present one, the seventh, is Vaivasvata. After him come Sāvarṇi, Dakṣa-sāvarṇi, Brahma-sāvarṇi, Dharma-sāvarṇi, Rudraputra, Raucya and Bhautyaka.” These are the fourteen Manus: (1) Svāyambhuva, (2) Svārociṣa, (3) Uttama, (4) Tāmasa, (5) Raivata, (6) Cākṣuṣa, (7) Vaivasvata, (8) Sāvarṇi, (9) Dakṣa-sāvarṇi, (10) Brahma-sāvarṇi, (11) Dharma-sāvarṇi, (12) Rudraputra (Sāvarṇi), (13) Raucya (Deva-sāvarṇi), (14) Bhautyaka (Indra-sāvarṇi). The reign of each lasts 71 *mahā-yugas*.

## 10

Of the 71 *mahā-yugas* of the Vaivasvata *manvantara*, 27 have passed. In the twenty-eighth *catur-yuga*, after Satya and Tretā have passed, the time of Kṛṣṇa's manifest presence comes at the end of Dvāpara. From the beginning of Brahmā's day to the end of that Dvāpara, six Manus pass, together with their junctions. Adding [these] together with the 27 *yugas* of Vaivasvata Manu and the periods of Satya, Tretā and Dvāpara (and subtracting the time of creation), one finds that 975,320,000[^10-1] solar years have passed.

## 11

> Subheading: The four principal *rasas*, apart from *śānta* —

*Śānta-rasa* is not named here for this reason. In the material world *śānta-rasa* stands highest of all, but in the spiritual world it stands very low. *Śānta-rasa* is transcendental, yet in it the *ālambana* of *rasa* — the *viṣaya* and the *āśrayas*, the object of love and those who love — do not exchange the roles of knower and known. Therefore in *dāsya*, *sakhya*, *vātsalya* and *madhura-rasa* love for Kṛṣṇa rises step by step in excellence.

## 22

> Subheading: The time of descent (*Bhagavad-gītā*, Chapter 4, verses 7–8) —

Śrī Kṛṣṇa recalled ancient times. The path of yoga He had once told the sun-god had been lost over time, so He told it again to Arjuna. To convince Arjuna, Bhagavān speaks of His own appearance:

**he bhārata yadā yadā hi dharmasya glāniḥ** (decline) **adharmasya abhyutthānam** (increase) **bhavati**, **tadā aham** (unable to bear these two [calamities], and in order to reverse them) **ātmānaṁ sṛjāmi**.

## 23

> Subheading: The work of the avatāra —

**sādhūnām** (of those wholly devoted to serving Me) **paritrāṇāya** (to remove the obstacles to their service) **duṣkṛtām** (of the enemies of the devotees — Rāvaṇa, Kaṁsa, Keśī and others, whom no one but I can slay) **vināśāya**, **dharma-saṁsthāpanārthāya ca** (and in order to practise and preach properly the *dharma* free of envy — devotion to the service of Bhagavān, which consists of *paricaryā* [service] and *saṅkīrtana*) **yuge yuge** (at the respective times) **sambhavāmi**.
