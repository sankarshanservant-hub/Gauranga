***Pahilehi*** — first of all. ***Rāga*** — *pūrva-rāga*: “The *rati* that is born before meeting, from seeing, hearing and so on, and that blossoms in the two, the wise call *pūrva-rāga*.”[^193-1] ***Nayana-bhaṅge*** — in exchanging glances with each other; by the play of the eyes, that is, by sidelong glances — signs that join the feelings of their hearts. ***Anudina bāḍhala*** — grew day by day. ***Avadhi nā gela*** — no limit remained.

In mature (*prauḍhā*) *samarthā-rati* there are ten states: intense longing (*lālasā*), anxiety (*udvega*), sleeplessness (*jāgaryā*), emaciation (*tānava*), numbness (*jaḍatā*), impatience (*vyagratā*), sickness (*vyādhi*), madness (*unmāda*), fainting (*moha*) and death (*mṛtyu*); that is, anxious thought (*cintā*), wakefulness (*jāgara*), anxiety, emaciation, uncleanliness of the body (*malināṅgatā*), incoherent speech (*pralāpa*), sickness, madness, fainting and death. In *samañjasā-rati* there are six states: desire (*abhilāṣa*), anxious thought, remembrance (*smṛti*), praise of qualities (*guṇa-kīrtana*), anxiety, and madness with lamentation (*savilāpa unmāda*). In *sādhāraṇī-rati* there are sixteen kinds, that is, [the states] of *prauḍhā* and *samañjasā* up to madness with lamentation.

Neither of Us is its cause — in other words, We have no sense of being separate. ***Manobhava*** — Kandarpa: knowing this, he ground (*peṣala*) the hearts of both, the *ramaṇa* and the *ramaṇī*. ***Prema-kāhinī*** — the pastimes of love (*prema-vilāsa*). ***Kānu-ṭhāme*** — to Kṛṣṇa, in His presence. ***Kahabi*** — you will say. ***Bichurala*** — has forgotten. ***Jāni*** — knowing. ***Khoṅjaluṅ*** — We sought. ***Dūtī*** — she who acts as a go-between and brings hero and heroine together, that is, arranges their meeting. Messengers are of two kinds: the self-messenger (*svayaṁ-dūtī*) and the trusted messenger (*āpta-dūtī*). The self-messengers are the sidelong glance and the sound of the flute; the trusted messengers are Vīrā, Vṛndā and others. The ordinary messengers (*sādhāraṇa-dūtī*) are the craftswoman (*śilpa-kāriṇī*), the fortune-teller (*daivajñā*), the female ascetic (*liṅginī*) and others. ***Nā khoṅjaluṅ ān*** — We asked or sought no one else. ***Duṅhuke*** — of these two, Śrī Rādhā and Kṛṣṇa. ***Milane*** — in Their union; ***madhye pā̃ca-bāṇa*** — the five arrows born of form, taste, smell, sound and touch. ***Ab*** — now. ***Sohi*** — that *rāga*. ***Virāga*** — *adhirūḍha-mahābhāva* in separation (*vipralambha*). ***Tuṅhu*** — you. ***Bheli*** — have become. ***Su-purukha*** — of the excellent hero (*nāyaka*). ***Premaka*** — of love. ***Aichana*** — such.

> Subheading: The pastimes of Rādhā and Kṛṣṇa are nourished only by the *sakhīs* who follow Them —

## 202–204

***Sakhī.*** The *Ujjvala-nīlamaṇi* says: “A *sakhī* is she who fully expands the pastimes and sports of love; she is a casket holding the jewel of confidence.” She who fully expands the loving pastimes, sports and so on of Śrī Kṛṣṇa is called a *sakhī*. The *sakhīs* are a casket holding the jewel of Kṛṣṇa's confidence.

The activities of the *sakhīs*: “To praise to each other the love and qualities of the two, to awaken Their attachment, to arrange the meeting (*abhisāra*) of the two, to offer [another] *sakhī* to Kṛṣṇa, to jest, to reassure, to dress [Them], to skillfully open the heart, to conceal Her faults, to deceive her husband and others, to instruct, to bring Them together at the right time, to serve by fanning and so on, to reproach both of Them, to send messages, and to strive to protect the heroine's life — these are the activities of a *sakhī*.” That is: (1) praising the love and qualities of the hero and heroine to each other; (2) increasing the attachment of each for the other; (3) arranging the meeting (*abhisāra*) of the two; (4) offering a *sakhī* to Kṛṣṇa; (5) jesting; (6) giving reassurance; (7) dressing the hero and heroine (*nepathya*); (8) skill in expressing the feelings hidden in the heart; (9) concealing the heroine's faults; (10) deceiving her husband and others; (11) instruction; (12) bringing hero and heroine together at the proper time; (13) fanning with a *cāmara* and so on; (14) reproaching both; (15) sending messages; (16) striving to protect the heroine's life. See Ādi 4.211, 217–218.

The “Sakhī-bhekī,” the “Gaura-nāgarī” and other groups of *prākṛta-sahajiyās* identify with the body and adorn the material body, senses and skin — food for dogs and jackals. That never pleases Kṛṣṇa. All such artificial efforts, foreign to Kṛṣṇa, gratify only the material senses, and so Kṛṣṇa takes no enjoyment and no joy in them. The body, home, dress, ornaments and every act and effort of the spiritual Śrī Rādhā and Her *sakhīs* are all spiritual; they all please Kṛṣṇa's senses and bring Kṛṣṇa under their control; none of them is an event or object of the fourteen worlds within Devī-dhāma. Kṛṣṇa enchants the worlds, but they do not enchant the worlds: they enchant the heart of Him who enchants the worlds.

To yield to a mind bent on enjoyment and imagine oneself a *sakhī* in an imagined *siddha* body is also nothing but *ahaṅgrahopāsanā* (worshipping oneself as God); as a result the one who imagines this stays on in Devī-dhāma. Śrīla Jīva Gosvāmī Prabhu warned the conditioned *jīvas* about this too. Thus in the *Bhakti-rasāmṛta-sindhu* (Eastern Division, second wave) his commentary *Durgama-saṅgamanī* on the verse *pūrvair vatsala-sakhyādau…* says: “The meaning is: but not with the self-identification of being the king of Vraja and so on. The conception of being [His] father and so on can arise in two ways: as an independent [identity], and by imagining oneself identical with His father and others. Of these the latter is improper, just as worshipping oneself as identical with Bhagavān is improper: for their eternity will be established just like that of Bhagavān Himself, and so [such identification] is improper; and also because by such a particular conception about His associates one falls into offense.” That is why Śrīla Rūpa Gosvāmī Prabhu said (ibid.): “Remembering Kṛṣṇa and His dear associate whom one longs to serve, and delighting in talks about them, one should always live in Vraja. Longing for that *bhāva*, one should serve both in the *sādhaka* form and in the *siddha* form, following the people of Vraja.” (Commentary: “The people of Vraja here are those dear to Kṛṣṇa and those who follow them; following them.”) See Madhya 22.153, 155 and 157.

## 214–217

*Kāma* is not a disposition devoted to the service of Śrī Kṛṣṇa, the embodiment of *saṁvit*: its aim is the happiness of something other than Śrī Kṛṣṇa. *Prema* aims solely at Śrī Kṛṣṇa's happiness and consists entirely of service to Kṛṣṇa. The *kāma* of the gopīs is really *prema*, for the gopīs do not seek the pleasure of their own senses. Only for Kṛṣṇa's happiness do they move *sakhīs* of their own circle to serve Him, and they themselves, moved by such *sakhīs*, serve Kṛṣṇa — they merely accept *kāma* directed to Kṛṣṇa (*kṛṣṇa-kāma*). See Ādi 4.162–166.

## 227

> Subheading: How a devotee freed from *anarthas* performs *rāgānugā-bhajana* —

## 228

***Siddha-deha*** — a spiritual body fit for the service of Rādhā and Kṛṣṇa, other than the present material body and the subtle body of the mind. By the fruits of material *karma* the *jīva* obtains a material body; in time that body changes, and through the desire for gross enjoyment he again obtains a material body. Through the desire for subtle material enjoyment he takes on a subtle mental body (*liṅga-deha*), enjoys material objects with the mind, and again obtains a similarly changed subtle body. In the same way the pure *jīva*-soul — instead of being born, by the force of the desire for enjoyment, in Devī-dhāma, where matter is enjoyed, and accepting two bodies, gross and subtle, agitated by time — eternally obtains two spiritual bodies in the spiritual Goloka or in Vaikuṇṭha. In them, intent on Kṛṣṇa's happiness, he renders transcendental service to Rādhā and Kṛṣṇa. The material and subtle bodies cannot think of what lies beyond matter, beyond one's own enjoyment. Therefore the devotee who has passed beyond the three *guṇas*, drawn by Kṛṣṇa's transcendental qualities, thinks of the transcendental with the help of the transcendental senses of his own *siddha* body, which is fit for this; rendering transcendental service, he follows the transcendental mood of the *sakhīs* and attains the transcendental feet of Rādhā and Kṛṣṇa. See Madhya 22.152–156, 160.

## 243

> Subheading: The conversation of the Lord and Rāmānanda: the Lord asks, Rāya answers —

## 244

> Subheading: (1) The highest knowledge is devotion to Kṛṣṇa —

To the question about the best knowledge (*vidyā*), Rāya answers that the best of all knowledge is the knowledge of devotion to Kṛṣṇa (*kṛṣṇa-bhakti-vidyā*). Above the knowledge that begets material enjoyment, and above *brahma-vidyā*, which lies beyond matter, stands the knowledge of devotion to Viṣṇu; and higher still is the knowledge of devotion to Kṛṣṇa. (*Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam* 4.29.49): “That is action which pleases Hari; that is knowledge by which the mind is turned to Him.” (*Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam* 7.5.23–24): “Hearing and chanting about Viṣṇu, remembering Him, serving His feet, worshiping Him, offering prayers, serving Him, being His friend and offering Him one's self — if a person offers Viṣṇu such devotion with nine features and performs it directly for Bhagavān, I consider that the highest learning.” (*Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam* 11.19.40): “Knowledge is the removal of the notion of separateness within the self.”

## 245

> Subheading: (2) The best fame, or honor, is to be Kṛṣṇa's servant —

Fame as a “devotee of Kṛṣṇa” surpasses every other fame. Greedy for material things, the *jīva* prizes only gross service to matter. But better than passing fame under some title in Devī-dhāma, or fame as a “knower of Brahman” in the realm beyond matter, is fame as a “devotee of Viṣṇu”; and on a still higher level is fame as a “devotee of Kṛṣṇa.” (*Garuḍa Purāṇa*, the words of Indra): “In Kali the name *bhāgavata* is rare and is never obtained; it is higher than the position of Brahmā and Rudra — so my guru told me.” (*Itihāsa-samuccaya*, the conversation of Śrī Nārada and Puṇḍarīka): “He in whom, after thousands of births, the thought arises, ‘I am the servant of Vāsudeva,’ delivers all the worlds.” (*Ādi Purāṇa*, the conversation of Kṛṣṇa and Arjuna): “The forms of liberation follow the devotees, together with the Śrutis.” (*Bṛhan-nāradīya Purāṇa*): “Even today the best of sages and the gods headed by Brahmā do not know the power of those whose hearts delight in devotion to Viṣṇu.” (*Garuḍa Purāṇa*): “One who performs sacrifices (*satra-yājī*) is better than thousands of *brāhmaṇas*; one who has mastered all Vedānta is better than thousands of *satra-yājīs*; a devotee of Viṣṇu is better than ten million knowers of all Vedānta; and one *ekāntin* is better than thousands of Vaiṣṇavas. The *ekāntins* reach the supreme abode.” (*Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam* 3.13.4): “The wise have declared that the highest fruit of long and laborious study of the scriptures is to hear constantly of the qualities of those in whose hearts are Mukunda's lotus feet.” (*Nārāyaṇa-vyūha-stava*): “O Hari, may I not become even Brahmā if I am without devotion to You; but if I am Your devotee, may I be even a worm, birth after birth.” See also *Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam* 3.25.38, 4.24.29, 4.31.22, 7.9.24, 10.14.30 and so on.

Among them is Prahlāda's fame, as Śrī Rudra says in the *Skanda Purāṇa*: “Only a devotee truly knows Kṛṣṇa — not I. Among all the devotees of Hari, Prahlāda is the very greatest” (*Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam* 7.9.26 and 7.10.21). Higher than he are the Pāṇḍavas (*Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam* 7.10.48–50, 7.15.75–77). Higher than they are the Yadus (*Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam* 10.82.28, 30). Among the Yadus Uddhava is the highest (*Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam* 3.4.31, 11.14.15, 11.16.29). Higher than he are the goddesses of Vraja (*Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam* 10.47.58). In the *Bṛhad-vāmana Purāṇa* Brahmā says to Bhṛgu and the other sages: “Long ago I performed austerities for sixty thousand years to obtain the dust of the feet of the women of the cowherd Nanda's Vraja. Even so I did not obtain the dust of their feet. Neither I, nor Śiva, nor Śeṣa, nor Śrī [Lakṣmī] is ever their equal.” In the *Ādi Purāṇa* Bhagavān says: “Neither Brahmā nor Rudra, O king, nor Lakṣmī, nor even My own Self is as dear to Me as the gopīs.” And among them Śrī Rādhikā is the highest of all.

Those who wholly follow Śrīla Rūpa Gosvāmī Prabhu — the best of Śrī Rādhā's dearest servants and the most intimate servant of Śrī Gaurāṅga — are known as *rūpānugas*. About their glory the *Śrī Caitanya-candrāmṛta* says: “Let there be ten million renunciations, ten million [virtues such as] *śama*, *dama*, forbearance, friendliness and the rest, ten million meditations on the truth — or even ten million kinds of Vaiṣṇava devotion; all of it is not equal to even a ten-millionth part of the host of qualities naturally present in those who relish the joy of the effulgence of the toenails on the feet of Śrīmat Caitanyacandra's beloved ones.”

(*Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam* 10.39.2): “What is unattainable when Bhagavān, the abode of Śrī, is pleased? Yet, O king, those devoted to Him desire nothing.”

(*Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam* 1.2.14): “Therefore, with one-pointed mind, one should constantly hear about Bhagavān, the Lord of the Sātvatas, glorify Him, meditate on Him and worship Him.”

## 253

> Subheading: (10) Vraja is the only place to live —

(*Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam* 10.47.61): “Oh, may I be born in Vṛndāvana as some bush, creeper or herb that receives the dust of these gopīs' feet! For they gave up their own people, so hard to give up, and the path of the respectable, and took the path of Mukunda, which the Śrutis seek.”

## 254

(*Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam* 10.30.39):[^254-1] “One who with faith hears of this play of Viṣṇu with the girls of Vraja and then describes it attains supreme devotion to Bhagavān, becomes steady, and quickly drives out lust, the disease of the heart.”

## 255

> Subheading: (12) Only the names “Hare Kṛṣṇa” are to be chanted —

## 256

> Subheading: (13) The destination of those who seek liberation and of those who seek enjoyment —

The *mukti-vādīs*, who have rejected material enjoyment, in the end obtain a stationary (*sthāvara*) body devoid of spiritual activity, that is, with consciousness asleep. And the *bhukti-vādīs*, bound to material enjoyment, obtain in the next world the bodies of gods, fit for enjoyment.

“The great sage who, for the sake of liberation — that is, becoming stone — set forth a *śāstra*: know that he is Gautama, and he is just as you know him to be.”[^256-1] This is the fruit of the philosophy of the adherents of Buddhist doctrine.

(*Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam* 11.10.22): “Having worshiped the gods with sacrifices, the performer of sacrifice goes to Svarga, and there, like a god, he enjoys the celestial pleasures he has earned.” (*Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam* 4.29.29): “One becomes a god, a human or an animal according to one's *karma* and *guṇas*.” See also *Bhagavad-gītā* 9.20.

## 244–256

In the answers to the questions of verses 244–256, the material and the transcendental are compared in worth. It is shown that the material outlook is contemptible, and that what belongs to transcendental Goloka — free from material self-interest and aimed at Kṛṣṇa alone — surpasses everything.

## 257

> Subheading: The difference between the practice of a *jñānī* and of a devotee —

*Jñāna* is like the *nimba* fruit: it cannot be relished, and it is food for *jīvas* who, like crows, cling to harsh logic. But the taste of the mango buds of *prema* is pleasing and very sweet: it is relished only by Kṛṣṇa's devotees, who are like cuckoos that know how to relish *rasa*.

## 265

> Subheading: The definition of the Supreme Lord, full of spiritual play, and meditation on Him —

**yataḥ** (from whom, the possessor of potencies) **asya** (of this universe) **janmādi** (birth, maintenance and destruction) — according to the Śruti *yato vā imāni bhūtāni jāyante* and so on, and the *sūtra* *janmādy asya yataḥ* (*Brahma-sūtra* 1.1.2); **anvayāt itarataḥ ca** (this comes about by *anvaya* and *vyatireka*); **yaḥ** **anvayāt** **artheṣu** (in spiritual affairs, which befit spiritual form, taste, smell, sound and touch) **abhijñaḥ** (attached), and **vyatirekāt** **artheṣu** (in material objects of form, taste, smell, sound and touch) **abhijñaḥ** (untouched by them), and being such — **sva-rāṭ** (He who shines by Himself, self-luminous); **yat** (in which supreme Truth) **sūrayaḥ** (Brahmā and others — according to the account in the Tenth Canto of Brahmā's bewilderment; the gods — according to the Talavakāra Śruti; the *brāhmaṇas* and others, and the sages — Dattātreya, Durvāsā, Vasiṣṭha, Śaṅkara, Vidyāraṇya and others — according to the words *daivāhatārtha-racanā*, *Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam* 3.9.10) **api** **muhyanti** (are bewildered, that is, cannot ascertain the supreme Truth); **tat brahma** (that truth — according to the words *vadanti tat tattva-vidaḥ* and so on) **ādi-kavaye** (to Brahmā) **hṛdā** (through the mind — according to the *Brahma-saṁhitā*'s words *trayyā prabuddhaḥ*) **yaḥ** **tene** (revealed); **yathā tejo-vāri-mṛdāṁ vinimayaḥ** (as in fire, water and earth there is an interchange, one appearing as another, so) **tri-sargaḥ** (the perishable creation of the three — *rajas*, *tamas* and *sattva*; in the other sense, the eternal manifestation of the three potencies — internal, external and marginal) **yatra** (in the supreme Truth, in Bhagavān's *svarūpa*, the form of eternity, knowledge and bliss, nondual Knowledge) **amṛṣā** (true); **svena dhāmnā** (by His power — His transcendental internal opulence, *sandhinī* and the rest, proper to His nature) **sadā nirasta-kuhakam** (in whom deception, *kuhaka*, the mark of *māyā*, is rejected and cast away) **satyam** (the true essence, the eternal) **param** (Him who is above all, the Supreme Lord) **dhīmahi** (we meditate). For more, see the *Gauḍīya-bhāṣya* on the *Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam*.

## 274

> Subheading: Seeing everywhere Kṛṣṇa and what belongs to Kṛṣṇa (*kārṣṇa*) —

Nimi, the king of Videha, wished to know the marks, the vision, the conduct and the words of the three kinds of devotees, or *bhāgavatas*. In answer to his question Havi, one of the nine Yogendras, said:

**yaḥ** **sarva-bhūteṣu** (in all beings, conscious and unconscious) **ātmanaḥ** (of the self — transcendental, beyond the matter that is enjoyed) **bhagavad-bhāvam** (in beings, the *siddha-svarūpa* and the rest, fit for the service of Bhagavān) **paśyet**, **ātmani bhagavati** **bhūtāni** (engaged in eternal transcendental service in their own *siddha* forms) **paśyet**, (he) **eṣaḥ** **bhāgavatottamaḥ**. (By the strength of their transcendental feeling the *mahā-bhāgavatas* everywhere see Kṛṣṇa and what belongs to Kṛṣṇa (*kārṣṇa*), standing in the relation of the One who is served and His servants — for they have no external vision.)
