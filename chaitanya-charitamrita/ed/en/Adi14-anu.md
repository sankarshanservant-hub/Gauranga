## 19

> Subheading: The name “Viśvambhara” —

The *Caitanya-bhāgavata* (Ādi, Chapter 3): “With His birth the world came to well-being — just as, long ago, Nārāyaṇa upheld the earth. Therefore His name is ‘Śrī Viśvambhara.’”

“Viśvambhara”: the *Atharva-veda-saṁhitā*, *kāṇḍa* 2, *anuvāka* 3, *prapāṭhaka* 3, mantra 16, number 5: “O Viśvambhara, protect me with all Your sustaining support. *Svāhā*!”

## 21

> Subheading: He displays superhuman deeds —

*Jānu-caṅkramaṇa*: crawling on the knees. The *Caitanya-bhāgavata* (Ādi, Chapter 3): “The Lord crawls on His knees — nothing could be more beautiful; little bells jingle at His waist — so charming! One day a snake was moving about the house, and in His childhood play the Lord caught the snake. The snake coiled itself around Him, and the Ṭhākura lay down on top of the snake. Then the snake left the Lord and slithered away.”

## 22

> Subheading: The name of Hari stops His crying —

The *Caitanya-bhāgavata* (Ādi, Chapter 3): “The lotus-eyed Lord cries only until He hears the name of Hari; then He at once falls quiet. Everyone understood this great sign: whenever He cries, they all chant the name of Hari. As soon as the Lord cries, the women clap their hands and sing *hari-saṅkīrtana*. The name of Hari is constantly on everyone's lips: on this pretext the Lord makes them chant it, for such is His will.”

## 27–33

To take objects of food for oneself is merely an activity of the material kind; there is no service to Hari in it. The *nirviśeṣa-vādīs* mistakenly regard things favorable to Kṛṣṇa's service as being of the same nature as unfavorable things. Such a notion is an utterly mistaken, immature expression of mundane conclusions. Through His mother's mouth, with the simple example of clay and a pot, Mahāprabhu showed how worthless such foolish impersonal thinking is.
