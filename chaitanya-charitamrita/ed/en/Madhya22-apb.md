# Amṛta-pravāha-bhāṣya on Madhya-līlā 22

## 0

**Summary.** In this twenty-second chapter Śrīman Mahāprabhu has described the truth of *abhidheya*. First He explained the truth of the *jīva*; then the supremacy of *bhakti* and the impotence of the paths of mere knowledge (*jñāna*), *yoga* and the rest, and that *bhakti* is the duty of all *jīvas*. He also showed that the *jñānīs*' conceit of being liberated is vain. One who gives up the desires for worldly enjoyment (*bhukti*), liberation (*mukti*) and mystic perfections (*siddhi*) attains everything desired — *prayojana* and all else — by the path of pure *bhakti*. And if, through ignorance, some of those desires still remain interwoven in someone at the time of his *bhajana*, Kṛṣṇa nonetheless removes them and grants him pure *bhakti*. Without the mercy of the great souls (*mahat*) *bhakti* does not arise; therefore the company of *sādhus* (*sādhu-saṅga*) is an absolute duty. Faith (*śraddhā*) alone gives one the right to exclusive (*ananya*) *bhakti*. Next the Lord described the kinds of *śraddhā* and of exclusive devotees, and the nature of Vaiṣṇavas. Bad company (*asat-saṅga*) is the company of women and of non-devotees. One must give up both, abandon attachment to *varṇāśrama*, and surrender to Kṛṣṇa. The six marks of surrender (*śaraṇāgati*) are also explained. *Sādhana-bhakti* is of two kinds, *vaidhī* and *rāgānugā*. In *vaidhī-bhakti* the sixty-four limbs (*aṅgas*) are principal, and of them the last five are exceedingly powerful. Practicing one limb of *bhakti* or many brings the fruit. Knowledge, renunciation, *yoga* and the like are never limbs of *bhakti*. No separate effort is needed for nonviolence, *yama*, *niyama* and the rest: they accompany *bhakti* of themselves. *Rāgānugā-bhakti* follows only *rāgātmikā-bhakti*. The foremost *rāgātmikā-bhakti* is that of the residents of Vraja. After describing the characteristics of *rāgātmikā-bhakti*, the Lord then told how *rāgānugā-bhakti* is practiced. (A. P. Bh.)

## 1

I worship Śrī Kṛṣṇa Caitanyadeva, the ocean of mercy, by whom even in the age of Kali the most hidden *bhakti* was revealed.

## 6

Mother *śruti*, when asked, teaches how You are to be worshiped. *Smṛti*, like a sister, teaches the same. The Purāṇas and other scriptures, like brothers, follow mother *śruti* and say the very same. Therefore, O Murahara, I have truly understood that You alone are the only refuge.

## 8–15

“In the form of His own portions (*svāṁśa*)” — that is, in the form of the *catur-vyūha* and its *avatāras*. In the state of *svāṁśa*, Kṛṣṇa's own nature (*svarūpa*) is seen everywhere. The *jīva* is His separated part (*vibhinnāṁśa*); the *jīva* too is counted among Kṛṣṇa's potencies. *Jīvas* are of two kinds: eternally liberated (*nitya-mukta*) and eternally in the cycle of worldly existence (*nitya-saṁsāra*, conditioned). The eternally liberated *jīvas* have never tasted any connection with *māyā*. They dwell in Kṛṣṇa's spiritual abode, turned toward His feet; they are known as Kṛṣṇa's associates (*kṛṣṇa-pāriṣadas*), and their enjoyment is the joy of serving Kṛṣṇa. The eternally conditioned *jīvas*, eternally turned away from Kṛṣṇa, experience happiness and distress in worldly existence — in heaven, in hell and elsewhere. For this fault of turning away from Kṛṣṇa, the witch Māyā binds them in the gross and subtle coverings (*sthūla* and *liṅga*) and punishes them; that is, the threefold miseries — *ādhyātmika* and the others — torment them terribly. Overpowered by the six waves — lust, anger and the rest — they go on taking the kicks of the witch Māyā. This is the *jīva*'s disease. If, while wandering up and down through worldly existence, the *jīva* ever finds a *sādhu*-physician, then at the *mantra* of his instruction the witch Māyā flees, and the *jīva* attains devotion to Kṛṣṇa and goes to Kṛṣṇa.

## 16

O Bhagavān, how many kinds of wicked orders of lust and the others I have obeyed! Yet neither did they take pity on me, nor did I feel shame or find peace. O Yadupati, now I have given them up, gained sound intelligence and taken refuge at Your feet, which grant fearlessness. Engage me now in Your service.

## 17–18

In many places the scriptures call *karma* the *abhidheya*, in many places *yoga*, and in many places knowledge (*jñāna*). Yet everywhere they call *bhakti* the foremost of all, the “eternal *abhidheya*.” The purport is this. Devotion to Kṛṣṇa is the one principal, that is, “direct,” means (*abhidheya*) for attaining the highest goal of life, *prema*. The status of *karma*, *yoga* and *jñāna* as a means is only “secondary.” For whatever fruits they bestow, they bestow only by looking to the face of *bhakti*: without the shelter of *bhakti*, *karma*, *yoga* and *jñāna* cannot give any fruit. Only when they obtain the shelter of *bhakti* can *karma* and *haṭha-yoga* give the fruit of worldly enjoyment (*bhukti*), and *jñāna* and *rāja-yoga* the fruits of liberation (*mukti*) and mystic perfection (*siddhi*).

## 19

If even pure knowledge — *naiṣkarmya* — does not shine when it lacks devotion to Acyuta, then how can *karma*, which is always inauspicious by nature, shine when it is not offered to Īśvara, even if it is free from selfish desire?

## 20

Ascetics, generous givers, the renowned, the wise, the knowers of Vedic *mantras* — though all their deeds are auspicious, without offering them to Him they can in no way attain auspiciousness. Again and again I bow to Bhagavān, whose fame is auspicious (Subhadraśravā).

## 21

From the scriptural statement “Liberation is easily attained by knowledge” one may conclude that knowledge gives liberation. But there is a subtle point here: knowledge gives liberation only under the shelter of *bhakti*. On the other hand, when *bhakti* turned toward Kṛṣṇa awakens, that liberation comes of itself, even if one makes no effort for knowledge.

## 22

O Vibhu, devotion to You is the path to the highest good. Some reject it and accept all kinds of hardship for mere knowledge — that is, to realize firmly, “I am Brahman.” As those who pound empty husks get no grain, so for them only hardship remains.

## 23

This *māyā* is My potency, and therefore by its very nature the weak *jīva* cannot overcome it. Only those who surrender (*prapatti*) to Me in My form as Bhagavān can cross this ocean of *māyā*.

## 27

From Brahmā's mouth were born the “*brāhmaṇas*,” from his arms the “*kṣatriyas*,” from his thighs the “*vaiśyas*,” and from his feet the “*śūdras*.” These four *varṇas* were born together with the separate *āśramas* and with the qualities proper to each *varṇa*.

## 28

Among the people of these four *varṇas* and *āśramas*, those who do not directly serve their Lord, Bhagavān Viṣṇu, but out of pride in their own *varṇa* and *āśrama* disregard His service, fall from their position and go down.

## 29

Followers of *māyāvāda* and other doctrines call themselves “*jñānīs*.” But in truth, without devotion to Kṛṣṇa, the intelligence does not become pure.

## 30

O Aravindākṣa, those who boast, “I have become liberated,” are impure in intelligence because they lack devotion to You. With great hardship they ascend to the supreme abode beyond *māyā* — to Brahman — but, disregarding devotion to Bhagavān, they fall down.

## 32

*Māyā* is ashamed to stand in the path of Kṛṣṇa's sight. Bewildered by that *māyā*, people of bad intelligence spread many nets of words such as “I” and “mine.”

## 33

Some, every day, only with their lips and out of habit, say again and again, “Kṛṣṇa, I am Yours.” Their words do not come from the heart (are not sincere). But whoever even once, from the heart — with body, mind and words — says, “O Kṛṣṇa, I am Your servant,” Kṛṣṇa carries across the bondage of *māyā*.

## 34

My vow is this: if anyone truly surrenders and, saying even once, “I am Yours,” begs Me for fearlessness, I always grant it to him.

## 35

Through bad desires and bad company, desires for liberation (*mukti*), worldly enjoyment (*bhukti*) and mystic perfection (*siddhi*) arise in the *jīva*. But if good intelligence awakens in him through the company of saints, he gives up the thirst for *mukti*, *bhukti* and *siddhi* and worships Kṛṣṇa by the path of deep, pure *bhakti*.

## 36

Whether a man was formerly without desires, full of all desires, or desirous of liberation (*mokṣa*) — as soon as his intelligence becomes broad, he will worship the Supreme Person, Kṛṣṇa, by the path of intense (*tīvra*), pure *bhakti*.

## 37–39

Those who desire *mukti*, *bhukti* and *siddhi* do not seek pure *bhakti*. But if by some good fortune they take up pure service to Kṛṣṇa, Kṛṣṇa mercifully grants them *prema*, the fruit of *sādhana-bhakti*, even though at the time they do not aim at it. Kṛṣṇa says: “In the heart of this man, who has just begun *bhajana*, there was a craving for worldly happiness, and a little of it still remains as part of his nature. He has given up the nectar of *prema* and wished for the poison of worldly objects; so he is a great fool. True, through ignorance he could not ask for what is truly good. But I am wise and experienced and know what is good and bad for him. Therefore I will give him the nectar of My feet and make him forget his thirst for the poison of worldly objects.”

## 40

It is true that Kṛṣṇa, when asked, fulfills people's requests. But He does not give the thing from which requests arise again and again. Some, having other desires, worship His feet, tender as new shoots, without even wishing to obtain them. To them He Himself gives those very feet, which quell all other desires.

## 41

If someone, for the sake of an ordinary desire, seeks out the worship of Kṛṣṇa and in the company of *sādhus* takes up pure service to Kṛṣṇa, the desire for which he came goes away, and he obtains the taste of Kṛṣṇa (*kṛṣṇa-rasa*). Service to Kṛṣṇa is so pure that one who takes it up gives up his former desire and longs to become Kṛṣṇa's servant.

## 42

When Kṛṣṇa wished to give Dhruva a boon, Dhruva said: “Svāmī, I engaged in austerities for You, desiring a high position. But now I have attained You, who are hidden even from the gods and the foremost sages, and my life has found its fulfillment: searching for ordinary glass, I have found a divine jewel! I ask no other boon.”

## 44

“I am so low that I will never see Bhagavān” — this fear of mine is false. For even among those carried off by the current of the river of time, some now and then cross the river.

## 45

What does the word “fortune” mean here — a mere chance event, or something more? The scriptures of *bhakti* call *sukṛti* “fortune.” *Sukṛti* is of three kinds: *sukṛti* turned toward *bhakti*, *sukṛti* turned toward enjoyment, and *sukṛti* turned toward liberation. Acts that are established as producing pure *bhakti* in this world produce *sukṛti* turned toward *bhakti*. Acts whose fruit is worldly enjoyment give *sukṛti* turned toward enjoyment. Acts whose fruit is liberation (*mokṣa*) produce *sukṛti* turned toward liberation. The *sukṛti* that ends worldly existence and awakens devotion to Kṛṣṇa — the *dharma* of one's true nature (*svarūpa*) — grows strong and becomes ready to bear fruit. Then the devotee is delivered from worldly existence in the company of *sādhus*, and *rati* for Kṛṣṇa arises in him.

## 46

O Acyuta, when, wandering through the world, the *jīva* reaches the end of that wandering (*apavarga*) and meets saints, then *rati* arises in him for You — the true goal (*sad-gati*) and the Lord of the higher and the lower.

## 47

Even if no great soul comes to the person mentioned above, who has *sukṛti* turned toward *bhakti*, Kṛṣṇa nonetheless teaches him pure *bhakti* in the form of the *guru* within the heart (*antaryāmī-guru*).

## 50

To the person who by good fortune has gained faith in narrations about Me, who is neither too weary (of the world) nor too attached to it — to him alone *bhakti-yoga* grants perfection, *prema-bhakti*.

## 52

O Rahūgaṇa, without being bathed in the dust of the feet of the great souls (*mahājanas*), devotion to Bhagavān can never be attained — not by austerity, not by Vedic worship and the like, not by observing *sannyāsa*, not by following the *dharma* of a householder, not by reciting the Vedas, nor by (worshiping) water, fire and the sun.

## 53

As long as people's minds are not bathed in the dust of the feet of Bhagavān's devotees who possess nothing (*niṣkiñcana*), they cannot touch Kṛṣṇa's lotus feet, which destroy the *anarthas*.

## 55

The boundless good that comes to the *jīva* through the company of those who associate with Bhagavān cannot be compared in the slightest with heaven or liberation (*mokṣa*), to say nothing of gaining a kingdom and the like.
