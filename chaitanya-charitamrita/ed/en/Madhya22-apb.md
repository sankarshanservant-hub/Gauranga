# Amṛta-pravāha-bhāṣya on Madhya-līlā 22

## 0

**Summary.** In this twenty-second chapter Śrīman Mahāprabhu has described the truth of *abhidheya*. First He explained the truth of the *jīva*; then the supremacy of *bhakti* and the impotence of the paths of mere knowledge (*jñāna*), *yoga* and the rest, and that *bhakti* is the duty of all *jīvas*. He also showed that the *jñānīs*’ conceit of being liberated is vain. One who gives up the desires for worldly enjoyment (*bhukti*), liberation (*mukti*) and mystic perfections (*siddhi*) attains everything desired — *prayojana* and all else — by the path of pure *bhakti*. And if, through ignorance, some of those desires still remain interwoven in someone at the time of his *bhajana*, Kṛṣṇa nonetheless removes them and grants him pure *bhakti*. Without the mercy of the great souls (*mahat*) *bhakti* does not arise; therefore the company of *sādhus* (*sādhu-saṅga*) is an absolute duty. Faith (*śraddhā*) alone gives one the right to exclusive (*ananya*) *bhakti*. Next the Lord described the kinds of *śraddhā* and of exclusive devotees, and the nature of Vaiṣṇavas. Bad company (*asat-saṅga*) is the company of women and of non-devotees. One must give up both, abandon attachment to *varṇāśrama*, and surrender to Kṛṣṇa. The six marks of surrender (*śaraṇāgati*) are also explained. *Sādhana-bhakti* is of two kinds, *vaidhī* and *rāgānugā*. In *vaidhī-bhakti* the sixty-four limbs (*aṅgas*) are principal, and of them the last five are exceedingly powerful. Practicing one limb of *bhakti* or many brings the fruit. Knowledge, renunciation, *yoga* and the like are never limbs of *bhakti*. No separate effort is needed for nonviolence, *yama*, *niyama* and the rest: they accompany *bhakti* of themselves. *Rāgānugā-bhakti* follows only *rāgātmikā-bhakti*. The foremost *rāgātmikā-bhakti* is that of the residents of Vraja. After describing the characteristics of *rāgātmikā-bhakti*, the Lord then told how *rāgānugā-bhakti* is practiced.

## 1

I worship Śrī Kṛṣṇa Caitanyadeva, the ocean of mercy, by whom even in the age of Kali the most hidden *bhakti* was revealed.

## 6

Mother *śruti*, when asked, teaches how You are to be worshipped. *Smṛti*, like a sister, teaches the same. The Purāṇas and other scriptures, like brothers, follow mother *śruti* and say the very same. Therefore, O Murahara, I have truly understood that You alone are the only refuge.

## 8–15

“In the form of His own portions (*svāṁśa*)” — that is, in the form of the *catur-vyūha* and its avatāras. In the state of *svāṁśa*, Kṛṣṇa’s own nature (*svarūpa*) is seen everywhere. The *jīva* is His separated part (*vibhinnāṁśa*); the *jīva* too is counted among Kṛṣṇa’s potencies. *Jīvas* are of two kinds: eternally liberated (*nitya-mukta*) and eternally in the cycle of worldly existence (*nitya-saṁsāra*, conditioned). The eternally liberated *jīvas* have never tasted any connection with *māyā*. They dwell in Kṛṣṇa’s spiritual abode, turned towards His feet; they are known as Kṛṣṇa’s associates (*kṛṣṇa-pāriṣadas*), and their enjoyment is the joy of serving Kṛṣṇa. The eternally conditioned *jīvas*, eternally turned away from Kṛṣṇa, experience happiness and distress in worldly existence — in heaven, in hell and elsewhere. For this fault of turning away from Kṛṣṇa, the witch Māyā binds them in the gross and subtle coverings (*sthūla* and *liṅga*) and punishes them; that is, the threefold miseries — *ādhyātmika* and the others — torment them terribly. Overpowered by the six waves — lust, anger and the rest — they go on taking the kicks of the witch Māyā. This is the *jīva*’s disease. If, while wandering up and down through worldly existence, the *jīva* ever finds a *sādhu*-physician, then at the mantra of his instruction the witch Māyā flees, and the *jīva* attains devotion to Kṛṣṇa and goes to Kṛṣṇa.

## 16

O Bhagavān, how many kinds of wicked orders of lust and the others I have obeyed! Yet neither did they take pity on me, nor did I feel shame or find peace. O Yadupati, now I have given them up, gained sound intelligence and taken refuge at Your feet, which grant fearlessness. Engage me now in Your service.

## 17–18

In many places the scriptures call *karma* the *abhidheya*, in many places *yoga*, and in many places knowledge (*jñāna*). Yet everywhere they call *bhakti* the foremost of all, the “eternal *abhidheya*.” The purport is this. Devotion to Kṛṣṇa is the one principal, that is, “direct,” means (*abhidheya*) for attaining the highest goal of life, *prema*. The status of *karma*, *yoga* and *jñāna* as a means is only “secondary.” For whatever fruits they bestow, they bestow only by looking to the face of *bhakti*: without the shelter of *bhakti*, *karma*, *yoga* and *jñāna* cannot give any fruit. Only when they obtain the shelter of *bhakti* can *karma* and *haṭha-yoga* give the fruit of worldly enjoyment (*bhukti*), and *jñāna* and *rāja-yoga* the fruits of liberation (*mukti*) and mystic perfection (*siddhi*).

## 19

If even pure knowledge — *naiṣkarmya* — does not shine when it lacks devotion to Acyuta, then how can *karma*, which is always inauspicious by nature, shine when it is not offered to Īśvara, even if it is free from selfish desire?

## 20

Ascetics, generous givers, the renowned, the wise, the knowers of Vedic mantras — though all their deeds are auspicious, without offering them to Him they can in no way attain auspiciousness. Again and again I bow to Bhagavān Subhadraśravā, whose fame is auspicious.

## 21

From the scriptural statement “Liberation is easily attained by knowledge” one may conclude that knowledge gives liberation. But there is a subtle point here: knowledge gives liberation only under the shelter of *bhakti*. On the other hand, when *bhakti* turned towards Kṛṣṇa awakens, that liberation comes of itself, even if one makes no effort for knowledge.

## 22

O Vibhu, devotion to You is the path to the highest good. Some reject it and accept all kinds of hardship for mere knowledge — that is, to realise firmly, “I am Brahman.” As those who pound empty husks get no grain, so for them only hardship remains.

## 23

This *māyā* is My potency, and therefore by its very nature the weak *jīva* cannot overcome it. Only those who surrender (*prapatti*) to Me in My form as Bhagavān can cross this ocean of *māyā*.

## 27

From Brahmā’s mouth were born the “*brāhmaṇas*,” from his arms the “*kṣatriyas*,” from his thighs the “*vaiśyas*,” and from his feet the “*śūdras*.” These four *varṇas* were born together with the separate *āśramas* and with the qualities proper to each *varṇa*.

## 28

Among the people of these four *varṇas* and *āśramas*, those who do not directly serve their Lord, Bhagavān Viṣṇu, but out of pride in their own *varṇa* and *āśrama* disregard His service, fall from their position and go down.

## 29

Followers of *māyāvāda* and other doctrines call themselves “*jñānīs*.” But in truth, without devotion to Kṛṣṇa, the intelligence does not become pure.

## 30

O Aravindākṣa, those who boast, “I have become liberated,” are impure in intelligence because they lack devotion to You. With great hardship they ascend to the supreme abode beyond *māyā* — to Brahman — but, disregarding devotion to Bhagavān, they fall down.

## 32

*Māyā* is ashamed to stand in the path of Kṛṣṇa’s sight. Bewildered by that *māyā*, people of bad intelligence spread many nets of words such as “I” and “mine.”

## 33

Some, every day, only with their lips and out of habit, say again and again, “Kṛṣṇa, I am Yours.” Their words do not come from the heart (are not sincere). But whoever even once, from the heart — with body, mind and words — says, “O Kṛṣṇa, I am Your servant,” Kṛṣṇa carries across the bondage of *māyā*.

## 34

My vow is this: if anyone truly surrenders and, saying even once, “I am Yours,” begs Me for fearlessness, I always grant it to him.

## 35

Through bad desires and bad company, desires for liberation (*mukti*), worldly enjoyment (*bhukti*) and mystic perfection (*siddhi*) arise in the *jīva*. But if good intelligence awakens in him through the company of saints, he gives up the thirst for *mukti*, *bhukti* and *siddhi* and worships Kṛṣṇa by the path of deep, pure *bhakti*.

## 36

Whether a man was formerly without desires, full of all desires, or desirous of liberation (*mokṣa*) — as soon as his intelligence becomes broad, he will worship the Supreme Person, Kṛṣṇa, by the path of intense (*tīvra*), pure *bhakti*.

## 37–39

Those who desire *mukti*, *bhukti* and *siddhi* do not seek pure *bhakti*. But if by some good fortune they take up pure service to Kṛṣṇa, Kṛṣṇa mercifully grants them *prema*, the fruit of *sādhana-bhakti*, even though at the time they do not aim at it. Kṛṣṇa says: “In the heart of this man, who has just begun *bhajana*, there was a craving for worldly happiness, and a little of it still remains as part of his nature. He has given up the nectar of *prema* and wished for the poison of worldly objects; so he is a great fool. True, through ignorance he could not ask for what is truly good. But I am wise and experienced and know what is good and bad for him. Therefore I will give him the nectar of My feet and make him forget his thirst for the poison of worldly objects.”

## 40

It is true that Kṛṣṇa, when asked, fulfils people’s requests. But He does not give the thing from which requests arise again and again. Some, having other desires, worship His feet, tender as new shoots, without even wishing to obtain them. To them He Himself gives those very feet, which quell all other desires.

## 41

If someone, for the sake of an ordinary desire, seeks out the worship of Kṛṣṇa and in the company of *sādhus* takes up pure service to Kṛṣṇa, the desire for which he came goes away, and he obtains the taste of Kṛṣṇa (*kṛṣṇa-rasa*). Service to Kṛṣṇa is so pure that one who takes it up gives up his former desire and longs to become Kṛṣṇa’s servant.

## 42

When Kṛṣṇa wished to give Dhruva a boon, Dhruva said: “Svāmī, I engaged in austerities for You, desiring a high position. But now I have attained You, who are hidden even from the gods and the foremost sages, and my life has found its fulfilment: searching for ordinary glass, I have found a divine jewel! I ask no other boon.”

## 44

“I am so low that I will never see Bhagavān” — this fear of mine is false. For even among those carried off by the current of the river of time, some now and then cross the river.

## 45

What does the word “fortune” mean here — a mere chance event, or something more? The scriptures of *bhakti* call *sukṛti* “fortune.” *Sukṛti* is of three kinds: *sukṛti* turned towards *bhakti*, *sukṛti* turned towards enjoyment, and *sukṛti* turned towards liberation. Acts that are established as producing pure *bhakti* in this world produce *sukṛti* turned towards *bhakti*. Acts whose fruit is worldly enjoyment give *sukṛti* turned towards enjoyment. Acts whose fruit is liberation (*mokṣa*) produce *sukṛti* turned towards liberation. The *sukṛti* that ends worldly existence and awakens devotion to Kṛṣṇa — the *dharma* of one’s true nature (*svarūpa*) — grows strong and becomes ready to bear fruit. Then the devotee is delivered from worldly existence in the company of *sādhus*, and *rati* for Kṛṣṇa arises in him.

## 46

O Acyuta, when, wandering through the world, the *jīva* reaches the end of that wandering (*apavarga*) and meets saints, then *rati* arises in him for You — the true goal (*sad-gati*) and the Lord of the higher and the lower.

## 47

Even if no great soul comes to the person mentioned above, who has *sukṛti* turned towards *bhakti*, Kṛṣṇa nonetheless teaches him pure *bhakti* in the form of the guru within the heart (*antaryāmī-guru*).

## 50

To the person who by good fortune has gained faith in narrations about Me, who is neither too weary (of the world) nor too attached to it — to him alone *bhakti-yoga* grants perfection, *prema-bhakti*.

## 52

O Rahūgaṇa, without being bathed in the dust of the feet of the great souls (*mahājanas*), devotion to Bhagavān can never be attained — not by austerity, not by Vedic worship and the like, not by observing *sannyāsa*, not by following the *dharma* of a householder, not by reciting the Vedas, nor by (worshipping) water, fire and the sun.

## 53

As long as people’s minds are not bathed in the dust of the feet of Bhagavān’s devotees who possess nothing (*niṣkiñcana*), they cannot touch Kṛṣṇa’s lotus feet, which destroy the *anarthas*.

## 55

The boundless good that comes to the *jīva* through the company of those who associate with Bhagavān cannot be compared in the slightest with heaven or liberation (*mokṣa*), to say nothing of gaining a kingdom and the like.

## 57–58

(O Arjuna,) you are most intimately My own; therefore, for your welfare, I give you the most confidential, the best of all instructions: think of Me, become My devotee, worship Me and surrender to Me — then you will surely attain Me. You are very dear to Me; that is why I have spoken to you these words of My vow.

## 62

Firm, unwavering faith that “by serving Kṛṣṇa with *bhakti* one performs all *karma*” is called *śraddhā*, which grants the right to *bhakti*.

## 63

When the root of a tree is watered, its trunk, branches, twigs and all the rest are satisfied. When the *prāṇa* is satisfied, all the senses are satisfied. In the same way, by worshipping Śrī Kṛṣṇa, the worship of all the gods is accomplished.

## 64–68

As stated above, one in whose heart *śraddhā* has arisen has the right to *bhakti*. Those who have *śraddhā* are of three kinds: “highest” (*uttama*), “middle” (*madhyama*) and “lowest” (*kaniṣṭha*). One who is expert in scripture and reasoning and firm in faith is an *uttama-adhikārī*. One who does not know the firm arguments of scripture yet has faith is a *madhyama-adhikārī*. One whose faith has not become firm is a *kaniṣṭha-adhikārī*. This threefold division is not merely a division of devotees; it divides the persons qualified for pure *bhakti*. One whose faith is weak (*kaniṣṭha-śraddha*) believes only this much: “Devotion to Kṛṣṇa is good.” But he does not know what pure devotion to Kṛṣṇa is, or what the method established by the marginal characteristics (*taṭastha-lakṣaṇa*) of *bhakti* is. Therefore in the hearts of those whose faith is tender (*komala*) an admixture of *jñāna* and *karma* is found. As soon as that admixture disappears, the practitioner becomes a *madhyama-adhikārī*. And when the faith of the *madhyama-adhikārī* is made firm by scripture and reasoning, he will become an *uttama-adhikārī*. Thus far the right to *bhakti* has been determined. Now (the Lord) divides the devotees themselves: by degree of *rati* and *prema* — “devotee,” “greater devotee” and “greatest devotee.” Such is the threefold division.

## 70

The devotee who has *prema* for Īśvara, friendship for the devotees, mercy for the ignorant and indifference towards the hostile is a *madhyama-bhakta*.

## 71

Someone worships Hari in the form of the *arcā-mūrti* with faith handed down to him by tradition — by worldly and family custom. But since he has not understood the truth of pure *bhakti* through the study of scripture, he does not honour Hari’s devotees. He is a *prākṛta-bhakta*, that is, one who has only begun the path of *bhakti*. He may be called “almost a devotee” (*bhakta-prāya*) or “a semblance of a Vaiṣṇava” (*vaiṣṇava-ābhāsa*).

## 69–71

The purport is this. When the devotee is able to have “*prema*” for Īśvara, “friendship” for the devotees, “mercy” for the ignorant and “indifference” towards those hostile to Bhagavān and hostile to Bhagavān’s devotees,[^69-71-1] he is counted as a pure devotee among the *madhyama-bhaktas*. Then, as he goes on with *bhajana*, he comes to see in all beings Bhagavān related to himself, and in Bhagavān, the true nature of his soul, the presence of all beings. Then he no longer makes distinctions in his attitude towards Īśvara, those dependent on Him, the ignorant and the hostile. In that state he is called a *bhāgavatottama*.

## 75–77

The qualities from “merciful” to “silent” are the special characteristics of a Vaiṣṇava.

## 78

*Sādhus* are tolerant, compassionate, well-wishers of all *jīvas*, without enemies, peaceful, and goodness is their ornament.

## 79

The wise have called service to the great souls (*mahat*) the door to liberation, and the company of those attached to women the door to darkness. *Sādhus* are people of great resolve, equal-minded, deeply peaceful, free from anger and well-wishers of all.

## 80

The company of *sādhus* is the original root from which devotion to Kṛṣṇa is born. Yet even when *prema* for Kṛṣṇa is born, that very company of *sādhus* is counted among the principal limbs of *prema*.

## 82

O sinless ones, I ask you about the *jīva*’s highest good. In this world even half a moment of the company of *sādhus* is a priceless treasure for the *jīvas*.

## 84

The company of *sādhus* is Vaiṣṇava conduct in its “direct” (*anvaya*) form, and giving up bad company is Vaiṣṇava conduct in its “indirect” (*vyatireka*) form. The *asat* are of two kinds. One attached to women, that is, infatuated with them, is the first kind of *asādhu*, and one who is “not a devotee of Kṛṣṇa” is the second kind of *asādhu*. The pure devotee will take special care to avoid these two kinds of bad company.

## 85–87

Truthfulness, cleanliness, compassion, silence, intelligence, modesty, beauty (*śrī*), fame, forgiveness, tranquility, self-control, power — all of these perish through association with an *asādhu*: a pitiable, self-destroying, restless and foolish man, a pet deer of women. Never associate with such a person. By no other connection is the *jīva* so bewildered and bound as by the company of women and of those who keep company with women.

## 88

It is better to suffer the torment of a cage amid flames than to keep the painful company of people turned away from the thought of Kṛṣṇa. The purport is this: if one must burn to death in fire or be imprisoned, one should accept even that, but not associate with people turned away from Kṛṣṇa.

## 89

Never even look at people devoid of devotion to Bhagavān, whose pious merit is exhausted.

## 91

Give up these two kinds of association with non-sādhus, and attachment to *varṇāśrama-dharma*, and take shelter of Kṛṣṇa alone, as an *akiñcana*, one who has nothing but Kṛṣṇa.

## 93

What wise man would leave You — who love Your devotees, who are truthful in speech, a friend and grateful — and take shelter of another? To the friends who serve You, You give all they desire, even Your very Self, and yet You neither decrease nor increase.

## 95

Ah, this Pūtanā, the sister of Bakāsura, full of wicked intent, made Him whom she wished to kill drink the *kālakūṭa* poison from her breast, and even so she attained the destination worthy of a mother. Apart from Him, Kṛṣṇa, what other merciful one can I take shelter of?

## 96

The “*akiñcana* devotee” and the “*śaraṇāgata* devotee” — these two have the same symptoms. But the *śaraṇāgata* has one symptom more: “offering of the self” (*ātma-samarpaṇa*).

## 97

The six symptoms of *śaraṇāgati*, taking shelter of Kṛṣṇa:

(1) *ānukūlya-saṅkalpa*, that is, the resolve: “Whatever is favourable to *kṛṣṇa-bhakti*, I will certainly accept”;

(2) *prātikūlya-vivarjana*, that is, renunciation: “Whatever is unfavorable to *kṛṣṇa-bhakti*, I will certainly reject”;

(3) “He will protect,” that is, the faith: “I have no protector but Kṛṣṇa.” This is not the faith that “knowledge of the nondifferent Brahman will save me from death,” but the faith that “Kṛṣṇa, in His mercy, will protect me”;

(4) accepting Kṛṣṇa as the *goptā*, the maintainer. One gives up the belief “By performing all my *karmas* I too will be maintained by the gods who preside over them,” and firmly believes: “Kṛṣṇa alone is my maintainer; among gods and men I have no other”;

(5) *ātma-nikṣepa*: the understanding “My will is not independent; it depends on Kṛṣṇa’s will” — this is the offering of the self (*ātma-samarpaṇa*);

(6) *kārpaṇya*, that is, considering oneself lowly.

## 98

With his body the *śaraṇāgata* takes shelter of the places of the Lord’s pastimes; with his mouth he says, “O Bhagavān, I am Yours”; in his mind he knows it — and he is filled with joy.

## 100

When the mortal *jīva* gives up all *karma*, offers himself fully to Me (Bhagavān) and acts by My will, he attains immortality and becomes *kalpita*, that is, fit to taste together with Me the *rasa* whose nature is spirit.

## 102

When *bhāva-bhakti*, which is the goal (*sādhya*), becomes attainable by action (*kṛti*), that is, by the senses, it is called *sādhana-bhakti*. Bhakti is the eternally perfect *bhāva* of the *jīva*, and *sādhyatā* means bringing it to manifestation in the heart. The meaning is this. In the *jīva*, a particle of spirit, there is by nature a particle of the bliss of Kṛṣṇa, the spiritual sun. Bound by *māyā*, the *jīva* has almost lost it in this life. That eternally perfect *bhāva* is what is to be manifested in the heart. In this condition the eternally perfect becomes a goal (*sādhya*). And when that bhakti — the *bhāva* to be attained — begins to be practiced with the senses of the conditioned *jīva*, it is called *sādhana-bhakti*.

## 103–106

Hearing, chanting and remembering in a favourable mood are the *svarūpa-lakṣaṇa*, the essential characteristic, of this bhakti. When one gives up other desires (*anyābhilāṣa*) and cuts one’s ties with *jñāna* and *karma*, this essential characteristic produces “the treasure of *prema*.” Love for Kṛṣṇa is an eternally perfect reality. It can never be attained (by any means, any *abhidheya*, other than pure bhakti); it can arise only in a heart purified by hearing and the other practices. Therefore *sādhana-bhakti* consists above all of pure hearing, chanting and the other activities. It is of two kinds, *vaidhī* and *rāgānugā*. When those in whose hearts *rāga* has not yet awakened take up worship at the command of scripture, that is *vaidhī-bhakti*.

## 107

O Bhārata, Bhagavān Hari, the Lord and the Soul of all, is always to be heard about, glorified and remembered by those who desire fearlessness.

## 109

“Viṣṇu is always to be remembered and never forgotten” — all injunctions and prohibitions follow these two statements. The meaning is this: all the injunctions (*vidhi*) that have arisen in scripture and all the prohibitions (*niṣedha*) it states rest on these two statements. Whatever brings the Lord to mind is a duty, and so a *vidhi*; any act that makes one forget the Lord is a *niṣedha*.

## 111–125

(1) Taking shelter at the feet of the guru; (2) *dīkṣā*, that is, initiation into the mantra; (3) service to the guru; (4) learning about true *dharma* and inquiring about it; (5) following the path of the sādhus; (6) giving up one’s own enjoyment for Kṛṣṇa’s pleasure; (7) living in a holy place of Kṛṣṇa; (8) accepting only what one needs to maintain life; (9) fasting on Ekādaśī; (10) honouring the *dhātrī* (*āmalakī*) tree, the *aśvattha* tree, cows, *brāhmaṇas* and Vaiṣṇavas. These ten limbs are the beginning of *bhajana*. Then: (11) keeping far away from offences in service (*sevāparādha*) and offences against the holy name (*nāmāparādha*); (12) giving up the company of non-Vaiṣṇavas; (13) not making many disciples; (14) not studying many books in part (*kalā*, that is, piecemeal) and not indulging in explanations for argument’s sake; (15) being equal in loss and gain; (16) not being overcome by grief and the like; (17) not disrespecting other gods or scriptures; (18) not listening to blasphemy of Viṣṇu and the Vaiṣṇavas; (19) not listening to *grāmya-vārtā*, that is, household talk rooted in the sense gratification of men and women; (20) not disturbing the mind of any living being. These last ten limbs, marked by prohibition, are to be practiced negatively (*vyatireka*). Two limbs — “freedom from miserliness in dealings” (*akārpaṇya*) and “not undertaking great enterprises” — the *Bhakti-rasāmṛta-sindhu* counts among those ten limbs. And the limb “not listening to *grāmya-vārtā*,” mentioned in this book, the *Bhakti-rasāmṛta-sindhu* does not include among those ten.

These twenty limbs are like the entrance to the temple of *bhajana*. Three of them — “taking shelter at the feet of the guru,” “*dīkṣā*” and “service to the guru” — are counted as principal limbs. (1) Hearing; (2) chanting; (3) remembering; (4) worship; (5) offering prayers (*vandana*); (6) service (*paricaryā*); (7) *dāsya*, servitude; (8) *sakhya*, friendship; (9) *ātma-nivedana*, offering oneself[^111-125-1]; (10) dancing before the Śrī Vigraha; (11) singing; (12) *vijñapti*, opening one’s heart to Him; (13) *daṇḍavat-praṇāma*, falling flat like a rod; (14) *abhyutthāna*, that is, rising on seeing Bhagavān or a devotee approach; (15) *anuvrajyā*, that is, following behind when a devotee or Bhagavān sets out; (16) going to holy places and to the temples of the Lord; (17) circumambulation (*parikramā*); (18) reciting prayers; (19) *japa*; (20) *saṅkīrtana*; (21) smelling the fragrance of incense and garlands that are the Lord’s *prasāda*; (22) honouring *mahā-prasāda*; (23) seeing the festive *ārati*; (24) seeing the Śrī Mūrti; (25) offering to Bhagavān what is dear to oneself; (26) meditation; serving the *tadīya*, that is, (27) serving Tulasī and the others, (28) serving the Vaiṣṇavas, (29) living in Mathurā and (30) relishing the *Bhāgavatam*; (31) every endeavour for Kṛṣṇa; (32) waiting for His mercy; (33) celebrating His appearance days and other festivals with the devotees; (34) taking shelter of Him in every way; (35) Kārttika and other vows. To these thirty-five limbs four more must be added: (1) wearing the marks of a Vaiṣṇava on the body, (2) wearing the letters of Hari’s names, (3) wearing *nirmālya*, the remnants of what has been offered, and (4) drinking *caraṇāmṛta*. Kavirāja Gosvāmī considered these four limbs part of *arcana* and the rest. With them there are thirty-nine (39) limbs. To these the five limbs must be added again: 1. *sādhu-saṅga*, 2. *nāma-kīrtana*, 3. hearing the *Bhāgavatam*, 4. living in Mathurā, 5. serving the Śrī Mūrti with faith. At the end of his description of the sixty-four limbs of *vaidhī-bhakti* (*Bhakti-rasāmṛta-sindhu*, Pūrva-vibhāga, second *laharī*), Śrī Rūpa Gosvāmī writes: “These five limbs, though already written of above, are praised here again to show that they are the best of all.” Adding these five gives forty-four limbs, and these forty-four together with the twenty mentioned before make sixty-four limbs of bhakti in all. These sixty-four limbs of bhakti are distinct kinds of worship (*yajana*, or *upāsanā*) with the body, the senses and the inner faculty (*antaḥ-karaṇa*). Some of them are entirely separate, and some are of a mixed nature.

## 126

One should associate with a sādhu who is full of affection (*snigdha*), whose desires are of the same kind as one’s own, and who is superior to oneself. In the same way, one should relish the meaning of *Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam* together with *rasika* sādhus.

## 127

Out of special faith: love for serving the feet of the Śrī Mūrti, *nāma-saṅkīrtana*, and living in Mathurā-maṇḍala.

## 128

These last five limbs are hard to fathom at once, and their power is wondrous. Let alone faith in them — even the slightest connection with them becomes, for a person free from offences, the cause of the birth of *bhāva*.

## 131

King Parīkṣit attained Kṛṣṇa in the best way by hearing about Śrī Viṣṇu; Śukadeva by glorifying Him; Prahlāda by remembering Him; Lakṣmī by serving His feet; King Pṛthu by worshipping Him; Akrūra by offering Him prayers; Hanumān, the lord of the monkeys, by serving Him as a servant; Arjuna by friendship with Him; and Bali by offering Him all he had, and himself.

## 132–134

Mahārāja Ambarīṣa engaged his mind in Kṛṣṇa’s lotus feet, his speech in describing the qualities of Vaikuṇṭha, his two hands in cleaning Hari’s temple and similar service, his ears in narrations of Kṛṣṇa, his two eyes in seeing Kṛṣṇa’s Śrī Mūrti, his body in touching the bodies of Kṛṣṇa’s servants, his sense of smell (his nose) in the fragrance of Kṛṣṇa’s lotus feet, his tongue in tasting the *tulasī* offered to Kṛṣṇa, his two feet in walking to Kṛṣṇa’s holy places, his head in bowing at the feet of Hṛṣīkeśa, and his “desire” (*kāma*) in selfless servitude. And he engaged them in such a way that there arose in him the *rati* whose shelter is Kṛṣṇa’s devotees.

## 136

O King, one who has given up worldly duties and with his whole being taken shelter of Mukunda, the giver of shelter, is no longer a debtor to the gods, the sages, the other living beings, his kinsmen or the ancestors. The meaning is this. As soon as a person is born, he becomes a debtor of all these debts, and, as the scriptures prescribe, he repays them by performing many kinds of duties. But one who has given up all desires and taken shelter at Kṛṣṇa’s feet finds all these debts repaid even without performing the prescribed rites.

## 137–138

One who has given up all the forms of *dharma* based on Vedic injunctions and worships the Lord as a *niṣkiñcana*, one who has nothing but Kṛṣṇa, by his very nature has no inclination towards any forbidden sin. And if for some reason a sin does occur, that is, happens to be committed, Kṛṣṇa purifies him without making him perform any atonement.

## 139

One who, giving up every other feeling, serves the feet of Hari Himself — if such a person, dear to Kṛṣṇa, ever somehow commits *vikarma* (sin), the Supreme Lord Hari, staying in his heart, destroys that sin.

## 141

For a yogī dear to Me, endowed with devotion to Me and with his mind fixed on Me alone, efforts for *jñāna* and *vairāgya* are generally not beneficial. The meaning is this: bhakti is by its very nature independent. *Jñāna*, *vairāgya*, *yoga* and the rest may be slightly useful to it at first, but they are not counted among its limbs.

## 142

O hunter, it is not surprising that nonviolence and the other virtues have appeared in you: those who have taken up *hari-bhakti* cause no suffering to others.

## 144

The bhakti of the devotees who live in Vraja, whose nature is *rāga*, is the principal one (*mukhyā*); that is, such bhakti exists nowhere else. And the bhakti that lives in following the residents of Vraja is called *rāgānugā-bhakti*.

## 145

The natural inclination to serve the beloved (*iṣṭa-vastu*), full of supreme absorption, is called *rāga*. When *kṛṣṇa-bhakti* is *tanmayī*, full of such *rāga*, it is called *rāgātmikā*.

## 148

*Anugati* means following.

## 149

Among the residents of Vraja and others, *rāgātmikā-bhakti* shines in manifest form. And the bhakti that follows (*anusṛtā*, that is, *anugatā*) that bhakti is *rāgānugā-bhakti*.

## 150

When one hears of the sweetness of the *bhāvas* and the rest of the residents of Vraja, the intelligence relies on greed (*lobha*) — and this is what gives one the qualification for *rāgānugā-bhakti*. Neither scripture nor reasoning is the sign that this greed has arisen.

## 153

Those in whom greed for *rāgātmikā-bhakti* has arisen should, following the activities of the people of Vraja, render external service in the form of a *sādhaka* and internal service in the form of a *siddha*.

## 154

Those dearest to Kṛṣṇa are the devotees who live in Vraja. Whoever longs for the sweetness of one of these devotees of Vraja and considers following him to be the service he desires keeps behind him and, with his mind turned within, constantly serves Kṛṣṇa.

## 155

Always remembering Kṛṣṇa and the one he himself has chosen from among those dearest to Him, and absorbed in narrations about them, he should always live in Vraja. If he cannot live in Vraja in body, he should live in Vraja at least in mind.

## 157

Those for whom I am the beloved, the Self, the son, the friend, the guru, the well-wisher, the deity and the cherished one are always devoted to Me. O mother of peaceful form, My wheel of time never destroys them.

## 158

Again and again I bow to those who always, with eagerness, meditate on Hari as husband, son, well-wisher, brother, father, friend and so on.

## 160

“The sprout of *prīti* has two names, *rati* and *bhāva*”: the sprout of *prema*, or *prīti*, has two names — *rati* and *bhāva*.

*Thus ends the twenty-second chapter of the “Amṛta-pravāha-bhāṣya.”*
