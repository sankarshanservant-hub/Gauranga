# Amṛta-pravāha-bhāṣya on Madhya-līlā 12

## 0

**Summary.** The king made every effort to see Śrīman Mahāprabhu. Nityānanda Prabhu, together with all the devotees, told the Lord how the king felt. Mahāprabhu still refused, so Nityānanda Prabhu took one of Mahāprabhu's outer cloths (*bahirvāsa*) and sent it to the king. On another day Rāmānanda Rāya asked Mahāprabhu to show mercy to the king. Mahāprabhu did not agree to this, but He told him to bring the king's son. The prince's appearance awakened remembrance of Kṛṣṇa, and seeing him, Mahāprabhu showed him mercy. Before the Ratha-yātrā, Mahāprabhu and His devotees washed and cleaned the Guṇḍicā temple (Guṇḍicā-bāḍī). After that He bathed in the Indradyumna lake and then, in a garden, honored *prasāda* with all the Vaiṣṇavas. While the temple was being cleaned, a devotee from Gauḍa poured water over Mahāprabhu's feet and drank that water, and so a secret of *prema* came to light. Further, when Advaita's son Śrī Gopāla fainted and did not come back to himself for a long time, Mahāprabhu brought him back to consciousness. During the meal Advaita Prabhu and Nityānanda Prabhu had a little loving quarrel. Advaita Prabhu said, “A householder *brāhmaṇa* should not eat in the same row as Nityānanda, whose family and character no one knows.” Nityānanda Prabhu replied, “Advaitācārya is expert in the ‘*advaita-siddhānta*.’ Who knows what will become of a decent man's heart if he eats with him?” The words of both Prabhus hide a very deep secret, and true devotees understand it easily. After the Vaiṣṇavas had eaten, Svarūpa and the other worthy associates honored *prasāda* inside the house. On the day of the Nava-yauvana *darśana*, Mahāprabhu and the devotees saw Jagadbandhu (Jagannātha) and felt a special joy.

## 1

Together with His intimate devotees, Gauracandra cleaned and washed the temple of Śrī Guṇḍicā. He made it as pure as His own cool and radiant heart, fit for Kṛṣṇa to sit there.

## 15

Sārvabhauma said, “Let us all go together and tell Mahāprabhu how the king conducts himself, like a true Vaiṣṇava. But we will not ask Him to give the king *darśana*.”

## 20

“*Mudrā* in the ears”: in the western regions yogīs are called “*kānphaṭā* yogīs” (“yogīs with split ears”), since yogīs wear in their ears a token made of conch shell.[^20-1]

The king said that without the *darśana* of Gaurahari enjoying his kingdom did not touch his heart, that is, it gave him no pleasure.

## 24–25

From the standpoint of the highest goal (*paramārtha*), a *sannyāsī* incurs fault by meeting a king. Quite apart from that fault, people censure a *sannyāsī* if they see in him even the slightest flaw. There is a reason to avoid people's censure. A *sannyāsī*'s work is to preach *dharma* in the world. If the world censures him, the preaching of *dharma* cannot go well. Therefore he must also keep the good opinion of the people. “Never mind people's censure: even from this Dāmodara Paṇḍita who sits beside Me it is hard to escape — he will surely scold Me. I cannot meet the king just because you tell Me to. If Dāmodara tells Me to meet him, then I can.” The Lord's words hold many hidden meanings. The Lord is won over by Dāmodara's devotion, yet Dāmodara's verbal chastisement is often improper toward the Lord. With these words the Lord shows that Dāmodara will have to give up that habit.

## 31–32

One day Śrī Kṛṣṇa, with the cowherd boys and the herds of cows, came near Mathurā, and the cowherd boys grew hungry. Kṛṣṇa said, “In the nearby forest some *brāhmaṇas* are performing a sacrifice. Go to them and beg for food in My name.” The cowherd boys went and asked for food, but the sacrificing *brāhmaṇas*, dulled by ritual work, gave none. The *brāhmaṇas'* wives, however, out of their natural attachment to Kṛṣṇa, heard the boys' request, left their husbands' sacrifice, and went through much trouble to bring food to Śrī Kṛṣṇa. The point is this: when a devotee has attachment (*anurāga*) to Bhagavān, he is ready even to give up his life in serving Him.

## 44

As the king's minister, Rāmānanda Rāya was highly skilled in every kind of business, including affairs of state. So he described the king's love for Mahāprabhu and melted the Lord's heart.

## 103

*Praṇālikāya*: into the drain.

## 136

The temple of Nṛsiṁha: near the Guṇḍicā-bāḍī stands a beautiful old temple of Nṛsiṁha. On the day of Nṛsiṁha-caturdaśī a great festival is held there. Śrī Murāri Gupta's *Śrī Caitanya-carita* describes the pastime of renovating the Nṛsiṁha temple in Śrī Navadvīpa-dhāma.

## 151–152

The Indradyumna pond is near the Guṇḍicā-bāḍī. The Lord bathed in that pond, bowed to Nṛsiṁhadeva and went to the garden.

## 167

*Lāphrā-vyañjana*: a vegetable dish, something like a plain *caccaḍi*. It is mixed with rice and served to the poor. *Amṛta-guṭikā*: thick *purīs* dipped in thickened milk (*kṣīra*), commonly called “*amṛta-rasāvalī*.”

## 191

“A *sannyāsī* (*maskarī*) is not defiled by faults in food” — that is, the defilement of food does not cling to a *sannyāsī*.

## 193–195

Nityānanda said, “You are Advaita Ācārya. All your *siddhāntas* are like *advaita-vāda*, which obstructs the activity of pure *bhakti*. Whoever is attached to your *siddhānta* sees nothing but the one substance, Brahman (*cid-vilāsa*). A *dvaita-vādī* ought to avoid such association with you, and yet I find myself eating with you — my mind cannot accept this.”

## 196

*Vyāja-stuti*: masked praise (*chala-stuti*), that is, words outwardly censuring but inwardly pointing to greatness.

## 197

Mahāprabhu had *mahā-prasāda* given to the Vaiṣṇavas. It was sprinkled with the nectar of the Lord's mercy and so became even more delicious.

## 203

*Dhoyā-pākhalā*: in the language of Utkala this pastime of cleaning the Guṇḍicā is called “*dhoyā-pākhalā*.”

## 204

“Netrotsava”: during the bathing (the Snāna-yātrā) Jagannātha's paint is washed off, and during the “*anavasara*” period the paint on the bodies of the three Deities (the “*aṅga-rāga*”) is renewed. On the very morning of the “Nava-yauvana” the *netrotsava* takes place, that is, the painting of the eyes.

## 205

*Pakṣa-dina*: fifteen days.

## 210

“Transgressing the *maryādā*”: the scriptural rule by which one is to take *darśana* of the Deity is called the “*maryādā*.” Eager for *darśana*, many transgressed this *maryādā* and went for the *darśana* of the Nava-yauvana.

## 212

*Nīlamaṇi*: that is, Jagannāthadeva's cheeks glittered like a mirror made of sapphire (*indranīla-maṇi*).[^212-1]

*Thus ends the twelfth chapter of the “Amṛta-pravāha-bhāṣya.”*

---

[^20-1]: The verse translation reads differently: it has “earrings of renunciation made of ivory,” whereas for Bhaktivinoda the token in the ears is made of conch shell.

[^212-1]: The verse translation reads differently: “His neck shone like a sapphire mirror”; for Bhaktivinoda it is the cheeks (*gaṇḍa*) that glitter.
