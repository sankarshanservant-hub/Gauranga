# Amṛta-pravāha-bhāṣya on Madhya-līlā 12

## 0

**Summary.** The king made every effort to see Śrīman Mahāprabhu. Nityānanda Prabhu, together with all the devotees, told the Lord how the king felt. Mahāprabhu still refused, so Nityānanda Prabhu took one of Mahāprabhu's outer cloths (*bahirvāsa*) and sent it to the king. On another day Rāmānanda Rāya asked Mahāprabhu to show mercy to the king. Mahāprabhu did not agree to this, but He told him to bring the king's son. The prince's appearance awakened remembrance of Kṛṣṇa, and seeing him, Mahāprabhu showed him mercy. Before the Ratha-yātrā, Mahāprabhu and His devotees washed and cleaned the Guṇḍicā temple (Guṇḍicā-bāḍī). After that He bathed in the Indradyumna lake and then, in a garden, honored *prasāda* with all the Vaiṣṇavas. While the temple was being cleaned, a devotee from Gauḍa poured water over Mahāprabhu's feet and drank that water, and so a secret of *prema* came to light. Further, when Advaita's son Śrī Gopāla fainted and did not come back to himself for a long time, Mahāprabhu brought him back to consciousness. During the meal Advaita Prabhu and Nityānanda Prabhu had a little loving quarrel. Advaita Prabhu said, “A householder *brāhmaṇa* should not eat in the same row as Nityānanda, whose family and character no one knows.” Nityānanda Prabhu replied, “Advaitācārya is expert in the ‘*advaita-siddhānta*.’ Who knows what will become of a decent man's heart if he eats with him?” The words of both Prabhus hide a very deep secret, and true devotees understand it easily. After the Vaiṣṇavas had eaten, Svarūpa and the other intimate associates honored *prasāda* inside the house. On the day of the Nava-yauvana *darśana*, Mahāprabhu and the devotees saw Jagadbandhu (Jagannātha) and felt a special joy.

## 1

Together with His intimate devotees, Gauracandra cleaned and washed the temple of Śrī Guṇḍicā. He made it as pure as His own cool and radiant heart, fit for Kṛṣṇa to sit there.

## 15

Sārvabhauma said, “Let us all go together and tell Mahāprabhu how the king conducts himself, like a true Vaiṣṇava. But we will not ask Him to give the king *darśana*.”

## 20

“*Mudrā* in the ears”: in the western regions yogīs are called “*kānphaṭā* yogīs” (“yogīs with split ears”), since yogīs wear in their ears a token made of conch shell.[^20-1]

The king said that without the *darśana* of Gaurahari enjoying his kingdom did not touch his heart, that is, it gave him no pleasure.
