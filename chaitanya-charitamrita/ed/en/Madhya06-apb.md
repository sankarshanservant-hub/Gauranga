# Amṛta-pravāha-bhāṣya on Madhya-līlā 6

## 0

**Summary.** When Śrīman Mahāprabhu saw Śrī Jagannātha, His great love brought on the *sāttvika* transformations that are the signs of *mahābhāva*, and Sārvabhauma took Him to his own house. Gopīnātha Ācārya, Sārvabhauma's brother-in-law (his sister's husband), met Mukunda, whom he had known before, and heard from him that Śrīman Mahāprabhu had accepted *sannyāsa* and come to Nīlācala. Word of Mahāprabhu's *mahābhāva* passed from mouth to mouth, and everyone went to Sārvabhauma's house. Nityānanda and the others went for *darśana* of Jagannātha with Candaneśvara, Sārvabhauma's son, and when they returned, Mahāprabhu regained consciousness (outward awareness) in the third *prahara* of the day. Sārvabhauma lovingly served everyone *mahā-prasāda*. Once he had become acquainted with Mahāprabhu, Sārvabhauma gave Him lodging in the house of his maternal aunt. Gopīnātha Ācārya declared that Mahāprabhu was Īśvara, God, and he had a long dispute with Sārvabhauma and his disciples. Gopīnātha explained well that without the Supreme Lord's mercy no one can know the truth of the Supreme Lord, and that God is not known through learning. From the *Bhāgavatam* and the *Mahābhārata* he proved that Mahāprabhu is Bhagavān Himself. Sārvabhauma Bhaṭṭācārya only laughed at this, and all of it reached Mahāprabhu's ears. Mahāprabhu said, “The Bhaṭṭācārya is Our guru; whatever he says out of affection is for Our good.” When Mahāprabhu met the Bhaṭṭācārya, the Bhaṭṭācārya told Him to hear Vedānta. Mahāprabhu agreed and listened to Vedānta in silence for seven days. The Bhaṭṭācārya said, “Kṛṣṇa Caitanya, can't You understand Vedānta?” The Lord replied, “You told Me to listen, and I am listening. I understand the *sūtras* of Vyāsa well; what I cannot understand is the Māyāvādī commentary you are reading.” Answering the Bhaṭṭācārya's questions, Mahāprabhu explained the Upaniṣads and Vedānta and established *saviśeṣa-vāda*, the doctrine of Brahman with attributes. He said, “The Māyāvādīs hold that Brahman is formless and without potency. Both of these ideas are a great error. Everywhere the Vedas accept the potency of Brahman, and they accept His eternal, transcendental form (*vigraha*), full of being, knowledge, and bliss. According to the Vedas, Īśvara and the *jīva* are at the same time eternally different in their intrinsic nature (*svarūpa*) and in their character, and eternally nondifferent. In short, the conclusion of the Vedas and Vedānta is the *siddhānta* of *acintya-bhedābheda*. In truth the Māyāvādīs are atheists.” The Bhaṭṭācārya argued at length, but he was defeated. Then, at the Bhaṭṭācārya's request, the Lord gave eighteen explanations of the *ātmārāma* verse. When knowledge dawned in the Bhaṭṭācārya, the Lord showed him His own form. The Bhaṭṭācārya recited a hundred verses and offered Him prostrated obeisances. Seeing the Lord's extraordinary mercy, Gopīnātha and all the others rejoiced. Later, one day at dawn, Mahāprabhu watched Śrī Jagannātha rise from sleep, took the *prasāda* called *pākāla*, and gave it to the Bhaṭṭācārya. By then the Bhaṭṭācārya was free of the dullness that doctrinal opinions breed, and in supreme bliss he accepted the *mahā-prasāda*. On another day the Bhaṭṭācārya wished to know which practice of devotion is best, and Mahāprabhu instructed him to perform *nāma-saṅkīrtana*. On yet another day Sārvabhauma replaced the word *mukti-pade* at the end of the verse *tat te ’nukampām* with *bhakti-pade* and recited it so to Mahāprabhu. The Lord said, “There is no need to change the text of the *Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam*. The word *mukti-pada* means Kṛṣṇa.” The Bhaṭṭācārya, who by then had become a vessel of pure devotion, replied, “Although the word *mukti-pada* does mean ‘Kṛṣṇa,’ its ambiguity is a defect, and I have no taste for using it; when one says *bhakti-pada*, the devotees are very happy.” Hearing how the Bhaṭṭācārya had been delivered from Māyāvāda, the learned men of Nīlācala took shelter of the Lord.

## 1

I bow down to Gauracandra, the Supreme Person greater than all (*sarva-bhūmā*), who filled with devotion Sārvabhauma Bhaṭṭācārya, whose heart had been hardened by false arguments.

## 5

**Paḍichā** — an officer like a superintendent (*dārogā*) of the Śrī Mandira. This *paḍichā* was Sārvabhauma's disciple in learning (*śikṣā-śiṣya*).

## 33

**Daraśana karite** — to have *darśana* of Jagannāthadeva.

## 47

After the Lord had eaten, Sārvabhauma, with His permission, ate together with Gopīnātha Ācārya and came back to the Lord.

## 67

**Śayyotthāna** — Jagannāthadeva's rising from His bed.

## 75

I will lead Him onto the path of *kevala-advaita*, which rests on the knowledge that this world of *māyā* is as worthless as a crow's droppings.

## 76

**Yogapaṭṭa** — a particular garment of *sannyāsīs*. I will give Him the *yogapaṭṭa* befitting the higher *sampradāya*, that is, the cloth that *sannyāsīs* wear, and perform His initiation rite (*saṁskāra*) again.

## 78–83

What the wise perceive as truth means nothing to the ignorant. That is why you take Him for “an ordinary man”; in fact, the signs of Godhood reach their limit in Him. Sārvabhauma's disciples said to Gopīnātha, “On what evidence do you call Him ‘Īśvara’?” Gopīnātha replied, “I call Him Īśvara by the very signs by which the wise establish who Īśvara is.” The disciples said, “The truth of Īśvara is known through inference (*anumāna*). Inference is defined by knowledge of invariable concomitance (*vyāpti*). For example: ‘The hill has fire, because there is smoke’ — that is, one must know that wherever smoke is seen, there is fire; what is proved here is: ‘Smoke is seen, so there is fire on the hill.’ About Īśvara, inference reasons like this: everything we see has a cause; this visible world is a thing; therefore it cannot exist without a cause. Thus the truth ‘Īśvara is the cause of the universe’ is proved. This is how we establish the truth of Īśvara. If you can show that this *sannyāsī* can be Īśvara by this line of reasoning, then we can accept it.” Gopīnātha replied, “To know the truth of Īśvara, inference cannot serve as evidence, for without Īśvara's mercy no one can know Him.”

## 84

O Lord, only one who has received a particle of the grace of Your lotus feet can know the truth of Your glory. But of those who search forever, reasoning about the scriptures by inference, not one can know that truth.

## 87–100

Gopīnātha said, “The scriptures have established that the truth of Īśvara cannot be known through learning and such qualities; so what fault is there in you?” Hearing this conclusion, Sārvabhauma said, “Ācārya, speak more carefully. What proof is there that Īśvara has shown mercy to you?” Gopīnātha replied, “Knowledge of the supreme Reality is called ‘knowledge of the Reality’ (*vastu-jñāna*), and this knowledge of the Reality is itself the proof of Īśvara's mercy. You saw in Him the signs of Īśvara — His absorption in great love — and yet, covered by Īśvara's *māyā*, you could not recognize Him as Īśvara! One who is turned outward sees Him and does not see. The sole reason for this is the nature[^87-1] of Īśvara's mercy.” Sārvabhauma laughed and said, “Setting mere wrangling aside, I have reasoned with the eye of scripture, following those who seek the desired truth, and I say — listen. This Caitanya Gosāñi is indeed a great *bhāgavata*, for in the age of Kali there is no avatāra of Viṣṇu; that is why one of Viṣṇu's names is ‘Triyuga.’” Gopīnātha replied, “You pride yourself on knowing the scriptures, but you pay no attention to the words of the two chief scriptures, the *Bhāgavatam* and the *Mahābhārata*. These two books conclude that in Kali there is a direct avatāra. It is true that in Kali Bhagavān has no *līlā-avatāra*; that is why He is called ‘Triyuga.’ But in every age Kṛṣṇa has a *yuga-avatāra* — and this your heart, devoted to argument, cannot grasp.”

## 108

Prajāpati Dakṣa said, “His potencies give rise to both dispute and agreement among disputants and again and again bewilder them about the self. To that Supreme Person (*bhūmā*), whose nature is unlimited qualities, I bow down.”

## 109

What the brāhmaṇas have said is sound in every case, for to those who speak by resorting to My *māyā* nothing is impossible. The point is that Bhagavān's *māyā* is a potency skilled at making the impossible possible. So in many cases she can hide the truth and make falsehood appear proven. Relying on that *māyā*, Kapila, Gautama, Jaimini, Kaṇāda, and other brāhmaṇas have presented many empty statements as though they were sound arguments.

## 131

A true commentary on a *sūtra* reveals the *sūtra's* meaning, but the commentary you are reciting keeps its meaning covered.

## 133–141

The primary meaning of the Upaniṣadic statements is what Vedavyāsa pointed to in his *sūtras*; that is, this primary meaning is what must be known. To set it aside and invent a secondary meaning, resorting to *lakṣaṇā* instead of the direct sense of words (*abhidhā-vṛtti*), leads to misfortune. Of the four kinds of evidence — direct perception (*pratyakṣa*), inference (*anumāna*), tradition (*aitihya*), and the word (*śabda*) — “the evidence of *śruti*,” that is, the evidence of the word, is the chief of all. The evidence is the primary meaning of the statements of *śruti*. Consider: the bones and dung of animals are utterly impure; yet the “conch” and “cow dung,” though counted among them, have become supremely pure by the power of the statements of *śruti*. To interpret Vedic statements by *lakṣaṇā* is to subordinate them to “inference” and so destroy their self-evidence. The meaning of Vyāsa's *sūtras* shines like the rays of the sun. The Māyāvādīs have covered it with the clouds of their self-invented commentary. The Vedas and the Purāṇas that follow them establish Brahman alone. By virtue of its quality of greatness (*bṛhattva*) that Brahman is marked by the signs of Īśvara. And when that Īśvara is seen in the fullness of all His majesty, that great Reality, Brahman, turns out to be Bhagavān Himself. So “Brahman” and “Īśvara” are particular aspects within the truth of Bhagavān. Bhagavān, full in six opulences, is always endowed with perfect beauty (*śrī*); therefore He is eternally possessed of attributes (*saviśeṣa*). To explain Him as “formless” distorts the meaning of the Vedas. The *śrutis* that call Him *nirviśeṣa* (without attributes) only deny “material attributes” and establish “transcendental attributes.” “Without hands and feet, He moves swiftly and grasps; without eyes He sees, without ears He hears. He knows all that is to be known, but no one knows Him. They call Him the foremost, the great Person” (*Śvetāśvatara Upaniṣad* 3.19). In many such *śrutis* the transcendental truth, possessed of form and full of being, knowledge, and bliss (*sac-cid-ānanda*), is described.

## 142

The *śrutis* that first present the Truth as “without attributes” in the end establish the Truth with attributes. Being “without attributes” and being “with attributes” — both these aspects of Bhagavān are eternal. But on reflection the Truth with attributes proves the stronger, for it is the Truth with attributes that is experienced in the world, while the Truth without attributes is not experienced.

## 143–148

From such *śruti* statements as “that from which these beings are born” (*Taittirīya Upaniṣad*, Bhṛgu-vallī 1) we learn that “this world of moving and nonmoving beings is born from Brahman, lives by Brahman, and again merges into that Brahman.” According to all these Vedic statements, Parabrahman has three characteristics: He is the ablative (*apādāna*), the instrumental (*karaṇa*), and the locative (*adhikaraṇa*) case. By these three eternal characteristics Bhagavān is seen to be eternally possessed of attributes. According to such *śrutis* as “May I become many” (*Taittirīya Upaniṣad*, Brahma-vallī 6), when Bhagavān wished to become many, then, as it is said, “He glanced” (*Aitareya Upaniṣad* 1.1) — He cast His glance on the material potency. At that time no material mind or eyes had yet been created; so the mind with which Bhagavān thought and the eyes with which He glanced at *prakṛti* existed before the material creation. Therefore Parabrahman had transcendental eyes and mind belonging to His very nature — and all the Vedas accept this. In the statements of the Upaniṣads the word “Brahman” is found almost everywhere. That Brahman in its full state is Bhagavān Himself: this is what the Vedas teach, and the evidence of scripture establishes that Kṛṣṇa is Bhagavān. If you say there is no such clear statement in the Vedas (none is to be seen), then reflect: the meanings of the Vedic statements are deeply hidden. To explain the purport of the Vedic statements to the world, the great sages determined the purport of the Vedas in the statements of the Purāṇas.

## 149

There is no limit to the good fortune of Nanda the cowherd and the residents of Vraja, for the eternal, complete Brahman, whose very nature is supreme bliss, has appeared as their friend.

## 150–153

“*Apāṇi-pādo javano grahītā*” (“without hands and feet, He moves swiftly and takes”; *Śvetāśvatara Upaniṣad* 3.19) — this is the *śruti*.[^150-1] First it says that Brahman has no material hands and feet; then, with the words “He moves swiftly and takes all things,” it states that He has transcendental hands and feet, and so shows Brahman to be possessed of features (*saviśeṣa*). The Māyāvādīs, however, abandon the primary meaning of the *śruti* and, by means of the secondary sense (*lakṣaṇā-vṛtti*), wrongly establish featurelessness (*nirviśeṣatva*), which denies Brahman's features. The Māyāvādīs declare Brahman “eternally formless,” but according to the scriptures that Brahman eternally abides as Bhagavān, whose form is full of bliss and of the six opulences. The Māyāvādīs have concluded that Brahman is “without potencies,” yet many scriptural statements rooted in the Vedic word “His supreme potency is heard to be manifold” (*Śvetāśvatara Upaniṣad* 6.8) acknowledge three natural potencies of that Brahman.

## 155–156

The *kṣetrajña-śakti* is the *jīva-śakti*. Although this *jīva-śakti* is all-knowing, it is covered by *avidyā*, a function of *māyā*, and constantly suffers all the miseries of worldly existence. Moreover, this potency named “*kṣetrajña*,” O king, covered and cramped by *avidyā*, exists in all beings in varying degrees. The meaning is this: Bhagavān's *cit-śakti* is the highest, the *jīva-śakti* is intermediate, and the *māyā-śakti*, called *avidyā* and karma, is the lowest. Covered by *māyā* — that is, removed from the function of the *cit-śakti* — the *jīva-śakti* suffers the miseries of worldly existence. In that state of remoteness it enters the wheel of karma that opens before it and attains higher and lower conditions.

## 158–163

According to the Vedas and Vedānta, one must know the nature (*svarūpa*) and the mutual relation of three truths: Īśvara, the *jīva* and *māyā*. First of all it is (absolutely) necessary to know the nature of Īśvara. His nature is eternity, knowledge and bliss (*sac-cid-ānanda*). Bhagavān's one *cit-śakti* manifests in three forms, according to three aspects — *sat*, *cit* and *ānanda*. In the aspect of bliss it is *hlādinī*, in the aspect of existence *sandhinī*, and in the aspect of consciousness *saṁvit*. That *saṁvit* is knowledge related to Kṛṣṇa. Īśvara's *svarūpa-śakti* manifests in three forms: the *antaraṅgā* (internal), which is the *cit-śakti* itself; the *taṭasthā* (marginal), which is the *jīva-śakti*; and the *bahiraṅgā* (external), which is the *māyā-śakti*. In each of these three manifestations one should understand three states, according to the functions of *hlādinī*, *sandhinī* and *saṁvit*. The *cit-śakti* gives the *jīva* the essence of her *hlādinī* and *saṁvit* combined. When the *jīva-śakti* accepts it, the *māyā-śakti* acts guilelessly as the *cit-śakti*: her (non-spiritual power of covering and projecting) withdraws, and she makes the *jīva* fit for loving devotion to Kṛṣṇa (*kṛṣṇa-prema-bhakti*). The sixfold opulence of the Supreme Lord is the play of His majesty; to call Him “formless” and “without potencies” is to speak in an utterly un-Vedic way. Īśvara by nature is the master of *māyā*, whereas the *jīva*, being by nature an atomic particle of consciousness, is subject to *māyā*. The *Muṇḍaka Upaniṣad* (3.1.1–2) says: “Two birds, inseparable friends, cling to the same tree. One of them eats the sweet *pippala* fruit; the other, not eating, only watches. On that same tree the person (*puruṣa*), sunk and bewildered, grieves over his helplessness. But when he sees the other, the Lord whom all serve, and His glory, he is freed from grief.” That is: “When the *jīva* forgets Īśvara, he becomes liable to punishment; for that offense *māyā*, Īśvara's jailer, imprisons the *jīva* and punishes him.” What is established here is that Īśvara's nature includes mastery over *māyā*, not subjection to her.

Although the *jīva*'s nature contains an existence free from *māyā*, it also has one property — the capacity to come under *māyā*'s control; this is called marginality (*taṭasthatva*). Since there is such an eternal difference both in nature and in essential identity, you cannot say that the *jīva* and Īśvara are one in any condition whatever. On the other hand, since the *Gītā* calls the *jīva* a “potency,” you are obliged to accept also that the *jīva* is one with Īśvara, according to the Vedānta saying “The potency and the possessor of potency are not different.” This inconceivable oneness and difference (*acintya-bhedābheda*) is the secret of the truth of Īśvara and the *jīva*.

## 164–165

Earth, water, fire, air, ether, mind, intelligence and *ahaṅkāra* — these eight are particular functions of My own lower (*aparā*) potency; the *jīva-tattva* is distinct from them.

## 166–167

According to the Vedic scriptures, Īśvara's form of eternity, knowledge and bliss is eternal. Formlessness is a particular transformation of material *sattva-guṇa*, its opposite — that is, a notion that denies the form found in material *sattva*. The spiritual form that lies beyond *prakṛti* has a spiritual shape as well. The formlessness of *māyic sattva* cannot touch it. Whoever does not accept such a Śrī Vigraha is counted among the godless (*pāṣaṇḍīs*).

## 168

Because the Buddhist Śākyasiṁha did not accept the injunctions of the Vedas, the Vedic Āryans condemn him as a *nāstika*, a denier of the Vedas. But the atheism that the Māyāvādī has preached while sheltering behind the Vedas is even more blameworthy than Buddhism, for a hidden enemy who comes disguised as a friend is far more dangerous than an open one.

## 169

The *sūtras* of Vyāsa contain the doctrine of pure devotion. In the commentary that the Māyāvādī wrote on those *sūtras*, the spiritual form of the Supreme Brahman is denied, and so is the *jīva*'s existence separate from Brahman. That commentary is therefore utterly opposed to the truth of pure devotion, and by hearing it the *jīva* is ruined. For the pride born of a vain hope — the desire to become one with Brahman — destroys pure devotion, and in reality Īśvara is not acknowledged at all.

## 172

“If *pariṇāma-vāda* is accepted, Īśvara will be subject to change (*vikārī*), and then Vyāsa will have to be called ‘mistaken’” — saying this, he found fault with the primary meaning of the *sūtra*, gave it a secondary meaning and established *vivarta-vāda*.

## 175

To explain the spiritual existence of the *jīva*, the statement *tat tvam asi* (“you are that”) is found in one part of the Vedas; it is not a *mahā-vākya*.

## 181

Bhagavān said to Śrī Mahādeva: “With your own imagined scriptures (*āgamas*) turn people away from Me. Conceal Me in such a way that the *jīvas* who have turned away from Me do not lose interest in the work of increasing the number of *jīvas*.”

## 182

Mahādeva said: “In the age of Kali I shall assume the form of a brāhmaṇa and by means of false scriptures establish hidden Buddhism in the form of Māyāvāda.”

## 186

Even sages whose delight is in the self (*ātmā*) and who are free from the knots of desire render causeless devotion to Śrī Kṛṣṇa, the doer of mighty deeds, for Hari, who steals the hearts of the whole world, has such a quality.

## 194–195

Eleven meanings of the eleven words of the verse, and seven more meanings obtained by joining the word *ātmārāma* to each of the seven principal words of the verse — *munayaḥ*, *nirgranthāḥ*, *urukrama*, *ahaitukī*, *bhakti*, *guṇa* and *hari* — eighteen meanings in all.

## 197

***Tine*** (these three) — Bhagavān, Bhagavān's potency and Bhagavān's qualities.

## 225–226

Whether the *mahā-prasāda* is dry, stale or brought from a distant land, the rule is to eat it as soon as it is given; there is no need to consider the time. The cultured (*śiṣṭa*) should eat Śrī Kṛṣṇa's *prasāda* as soon as they receive it, and here there are no rules of place or time — so Bhagavān has commanded.

## 235

Those who have taken shelter of His lotus feet in every way and to whom Bhagavān, whose nature is unlimited (Ananta), shows guileless mercy cross over this insurmountable divine *māyā*. But to those who think “I” and “mine” of this material body, food for jackals and dogs, Bhagavān shows no mercy.

## 241

Sārvabhauma Bhaṭṭācārya asked which of the sixty-four limbs of *sādhana-bhakti* is the best of all. Mahāprabhu answered: “*Nāma-saṅkīrtana* alone is the best of all.”

## 242

“What is attained in the Satya-yuga by meditating on Viṣṇu, in the Tretā-yuga by worshipping Him with sacrifices and in the Dvāpara-yuga by serving Him is attained in the age of Kali by chanting [the name of] Hari.”[^242-1]

## 254

I surrender to the one, primeval Person, ever an ocean of mercy, who has assumed the form of Śrī Kṛṣṇa Caitanya to teach renunciation, knowledge and devotion to Himself.

## 255

Seeing that in the course of time devotion to Himself had almost perished, the Person named “Kṛṣṇa Caitanya” appeared to preach it again. May the bee of my mind be deeply absorbed in His lotus feet.

## 261

One who, hoping for Your mercy, tastes the bad fruits of his own deeds and lives serving You with mind, words and body is an heir to the position of devotion — that is, he attains the position of devotion. When reciting this verse Sārvabhauma said *bhakti-pade sa dāya-bhāk* (“he is an heir to the position of devotion”).

## 263

The Bhaṭṭācārya said: “The highest fruit of devotion is (*prema-*)*bhakti* alone; *mukti* is not the fruit of devotion. For a person averse to devotion to Bhagavān, *sāyujya-mukti* is merely a kind of punishment.”

## 267–268

Of the five kinds of *mukti* — *sālokya*, *sāmīpya*, *sārūpya*, *sārṣṭi* and *sāyujya* — the first four, beginning with *sālokya*, are not so blameworthy, for they are like doors to the service of Bhagavān. Even so, a devotee of Kṛṣṇa does not accept even these four kinds of *mukti*, for birth after birth he desires only devotion to Kṛṣṇa.[^267-1] As soon as he hears the word *sāyujya*, the devotee feels disgust for it as something worthless and fear of it as an “offense” hostile to devotion.

## 269

*Sāyujya* is of two kinds: *brahma-sāyujya* and *īśvara-sāyujya*. According to the Māyāvādī Vedāntists, the *jīva*'s ultimate attainment is *brahma-sāyujya*; according to Patañjali's teaching, it is *īśvara-sāyujya* in the state of *kaivalya*. Of these two kinds of *sāyujya*, *īśvara-sāyujya* is the more contemptible. In *brahma-sāyujya* one reaches a featureless destination through featureless knowledge. But *īśvara-sāyujya* — *kaivalya*, attained by meditating on the very Īśvara who has features — turns, through the fault of desire, into a still deeper fall. “Īśvara is a special *puruṣa*, untouched by afflictions, actions, their fruits and their latent impressions.”[^269-1] “He is the guru even of the ancients, for time does not limit Him.”[^269-2] From these the eternality of Īśvara with features is evident. But in the same *Yoga-sūtra* of Patañjali, in the *Kaivalya-pāda*, the *sūtra* “*Kaivalya* is the reabsorption of [the *guṇas*], devoid of any purpose for the *puruṣa*, or the power of consciousness established in its own nature”[^269-3] shows that in the practitioner's state of perfection there is no other *puruṣa*, no Īśvara. The path of *yoga*, under the pretext of taking shelter of the Truth with features, is utterly fruitless. The meaning is this: (on the path of *yoga*) worship of the Truth with features yields not a fruit with features, but a fruit extremely remote from it and deserving of contempt.

## 272

He at whose feet *mukti* resides is *mukti-pada* — that is, Śrī Kṛṣṇa, the “tenth” topic. Or: the ninth topic is *mukti*, and He who is its shelter is Śrī Kṛṣṇa.

## 274

***Āśliṣya-doṣa*** — the defect in which a word can be understood in two ways, so that its primary meaning suffers somewhat.

## 275

***Rūḍhi-vṛtti*** — the primary meaning (*mukhya-vṛtti*).

*Thus ends the sixth chapter of the “Amṛta-pravāha-bhāṣya.”*
