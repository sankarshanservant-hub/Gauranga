# Amṛta-pravāha-bhāṣya on Madhya-līlā 6

## 0

**Summary.** When Śrīman Mahāprabhu saw Śrī Jagannātha, His great love brought on the *sāttvika* transformations that are the signs of *mahābhāva*, and Sārvabhauma took Him to his own house. Gopīnātha Ācārya, Sārvabhauma's brother-in-law (his sister's husband), met Mukunda, whom he had known before, and heard from him that Śrīman Mahāprabhu had accepted *sannyāsa* and come to Nīlācala. Word of Mahāprabhu's *mahābhāva* passed from mouth to mouth, and everyone went to Sārvabhauma's house. Nityānanda and the others went for *darśana* of Jagannātha with Candaneśvara, Sārvabhauma's son, and when they returned, Mahāprabhu regained consciousness (outward awareness) in the third *prahara* of the day. Sārvabhauma lovingly served everyone *mahā-prasāda*. Once he had become acquainted with Mahāprabhu, Sārvabhauma gave Him lodging in the house of his maternal aunt. Gopīnātha Ācārya declared that Mahāprabhu was Īśvara, God, and he had a long dispute with Sārvabhauma and his disciples. Gopīnātha explained well that without the Supreme Lord's mercy no one can know the truth of the Supreme Lord, and that God is not known through learning. From the *Bhāgavatam* and the *Mahābhārata* he proved that Mahāprabhu is Bhagavān Himself. Sārvabhauma Bhaṭṭācārya only laughed at this, and all of it reached Mahāprabhu's ears. Mahāprabhu said, “The Bhaṭṭācārya is Our guru; whatever he says out of affection is for Our good.” When Mahāprabhu met the Bhaṭṭācārya, the Bhaṭṭācārya told Him to hear Vedānta. Mahāprabhu agreed and listened to Vedānta in silence for seven days. The Bhaṭṭācārya said, “Kṛṣṇa Caitanya, can't You understand Vedānta?” The Lord replied, “You told Me to listen, and I am listening. I understand the *sūtras* of Vyāsa well; what I cannot understand is the Māyāvādī commentary you are reading.” Answering the Bhaṭṭācārya's questions, Mahāprabhu explained the Upaniṣads and Vedānta and established *saviśeṣa-vāda*, the doctrine of Brahman with attributes. He said, “The Māyāvādīs hold that Brahman is formless and without potency. Both of these ideas are a great error. Everywhere the Vedas accept the potency of Brahman, and they accept His eternal, transcendental form (*vigraha*), full of being, knowledge, and bliss. According to the Vedas, Īśvara and the *jīva* are at the same time eternally different in their intrinsic nature (*svarūpa*) and in their character, and eternally nondifferent. In short, the conclusion of the Vedas and Vedānta is the *siddhānta* of *acintya-bhedābheda*. In truth the Māyāvādīs are atheists.” The Bhaṭṭācārya argued at length, but he was defeated. Then, at the Bhaṭṭācārya's request, the Lord gave eighteen explanations of the *ātmārāma* verse. When knowledge dawned in the Bhaṭṭācārya, the Lord showed him His own form. The Bhaṭṭācārya recited a hundred verses and offered Him prostrated obeisances. Seeing the Lord's extraordinary mercy, Gopīnātha and all the others rejoiced. Later, one day at dawn, Mahāprabhu watched Śrī Jagannātha rise from sleep, took the *prasāda* called *pākāla*, and gave it to the Bhaṭṭācārya. By then the Bhaṭṭācārya was free of the dullness that doctrinal opinions breed, and in supreme bliss he accepted the *mahā-prasāda*. On another day the Bhaṭṭācārya wished to know which practice of devotion is best, and Mahāprabhu instructed him to perform *nāma-saṅkīrtana*. On yet another day Sārvabhauma replaced the word *mukti-pade* at the end of the verse *tat te ’nukampām* with *bhakti-pade* and recited it so to Mahāprabhu. The Lord said, “There is no need to change the text of the *Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam*. The word *mukti-pada* means Kṛṣṇa.” The Bhaṭṭācārya, who by then had become a vessel of pure devotion, replied, “Although the word *mukti-pada* does mean ‘Kṛṣṇa,’ its ambiguity is a defect, and I have no taste for using it; when one says *bhakti-pada*, the devotees are very happy.” Hearing how the Bhaṭṭācārya had been delivered from Māyāvāda, the learned men of Nīlācala took shelter of the Lord.

## 1

I bow down to Gauracandra, the Supreme Person greater than all (*sarva-bhūmā*), who filled with devotion Sārvabhauma Bhaṭṭācārya, whose heart had been hardened by false arguments.

## 5

**Paḍichā** — an officer like a superintendent (*dārogā*) of the Śrī Mandira. This *paḍichā* was Sārvabhauma's disciple in learning (*śikṣā-śiṣya*).

## 33

**Daraśana karite** — to have *darśana* of Jagannāthadeva.

## 47

After the Lord had eaten, Sārvabhauma, with His permission, ate together with Gopīnātha Ācārya and came back to the Lord.

## 67

**Śayyotthāna** — Jagannāthadeva's rising from His bed.

## 75

I will lead Him onto the path of *kevala-advaita*, which rests on the knowledge that this world of *māyā* is as worthless as a crow's droppings.

## 76

**Yogapaṭṭa** — a particular garment of *sannyāsīs*. I will give Him the *yogapaṭṭa* befitting the higher *sampradāya*, that is, the cloth that *sannyāsīs* wear, and perform His initiation rite (*saṁskāra*) again.

## 78–83

What the wise perceive as truth means nothing to the ignorant. That is why you take Him for “an ordinary man”; in fact, the signs of Godhood reach their limit in Him. Sārvabhauma's disciples said to Gopīnātha, “On what evidence do you call Him ‘Īśvara’?” Gopīnātha replied, “I call Him Īśvara by the very signs by which the wise establish who Īśvara is.” The disciples said, “The truth of Īśvara is known through inference (*anumāna*). Inference is defined by knowledge of invariable concomitance (*vyāpti*). For example: ‘The hill has fire, because there is smoke’ — that is, one must know that wherever smoke is seen, there is fire; what is proved here is: ‘Smoke is seen, so there is fire on the hill.’ About Īśvara, inference reasons like this: everything we see has a cause; this visible world is a thing; therefore it cannot exist without a cause. Thus the truth ‘Īśvara is the cause of the universe’ is proved. This is how we establish the truth of Īśvara. If you can show that this *sannyāsī* can be Īśvara by this line of reasoning, then we can accept it.” Gopīnātha replied, “To know the truth of Īśvara, inference cannot serve as evidence, for without Īśvara's mercy no one can know Him.”

## 84

O Lord, only one who has received a particle of the grace of Your lotus feet can know the truth of Your glory. But of those who search forever, reasoning about the scriptures by inference, not one can know that truth.

## 87–100

Gopīnātha said, “The scriptures have established that the truth of Īśvara cannot be known through learning and such qualities; so what fault is there in you?” Hearing this conclusion, Sārvabhauma said, “Ācārya, speak more carefully. What proof is there that Īśvara has shown mercy to you?” Gopīnātha replied, “Knowledge of the supreme Reality is called ‘knowledge of the Reality’ (*vastu-jñāna*), and this knowledge of the Reality is itself the proof of Īśvara's mercy. You saw in Him the signs of Īśvara — His absorption in great love — and yet, covered by Īśvara's *māyā*, you could not recognize Him as Īśvara! One who is turned outward sees Him and does not see. The sole reason for this is the nature[^87-1] of Īśvara's mercy.” Sārvabhauma laughed and said, “Setting mere wrangling aside, I have reasoned with the eye of scripture, following those who seek the desired truth, and I say — listen. This Caitanya Gosāñi is indeed a great *bhāgavata*, for in the age of Kali there is no avatāra of Viṣṇu; that is why one of Viṣṇu's names is ‘Triyuga.’” Gopīnātha replied, “You pride yourself on knowing the scriptures, but you pay no attention to the words of the two chief scriptures, the *Bhāgavatam* and the *Mahābhārata*. These two books conclude that in Kali there is a direct avatāra. It is true that in Kali Bhagavān has no *līlā-avatāra*; that is why He is called ‘Triyuga.’ But in every age Kṛṣṇa has a *yuga-avatāra* — and this your heart, devoted to argument, cannot grasp.”

## 108

Prajāpati Dakṣa said, “His potencies give rise to both dispute and agreement among disputants and again and again bewilder them about the self. To that Supreme Person (*bhūmā*), whose nature is unlimited qualities, I bow down.”

## 109

What the brāhmaṇas have said is sound in every case, for to those who speak by resorting to My *māyā* nothing is impossible. The point is that Bhagavān's *māyā* is a potency skilled at making the impossible possible. So in many cases she can hide the truth and make falsehood appear proven. Relying on that *māyā*, Kapila, Gautama, Jaimini, Kaṇāda, and other brāhmaṇas have presented many empty statements as though they were sound arguments.

## 131

A true commentary on a *sūtra* reveals the *sūtra's* meaning, but the commentary you are reciting keeps its meaning covered.

[^87-1]: So in the edition (*svabhāva*, “nature”); the sense would also allow *abhāva*, “the absence [of mercy].”
