# Amṛta-pravāha-bhāṣya on Ādi-līlā 14

## 0

**Summary.** The fourteenth chapter describes the Lord's childhood pastimes: He crawls on all fours; on the pretext of crying He spreads the holy name; on the pretext of eating earth He gives knowledge to His mother; He gives *prasāda* to a guest brāhmaṇa and delivers him; He climbs onto a thief's shoulders, bewilders him and brings him back to His own home; on the pretext of illness He eats, on the day of Ekādaśī, the offering (*naivedya*) of Hiraṇya and Jagadīśa; His childhood mischief; seeing His mother unconscious, He brings her coconuts; He jokes with the girls on the bank of the Gaṅgā; He accepts the worship of Lakṣmī-devī; sitting in a pit full of discarded pots, He gives His mother knowledge of Brahman and obeys her order; Miśra's pure parental love (*śuddha-vātsalya*). These are the topics of the childhood pastimes.

## 1

He whom one need only remember a little for the difficult to become easy, and whom one need only forget for even the easy to become difficult — that Caitanya I worship.

## 5

I bow down to the charming childhood pastimes of Caitanya-Kṛṣṇa. Although these childhood pastimes resemble ordinary, worldly play, the deeds of the Lord are mixed into them.

## 15

The nose, arms, jaws, eyes and knees — these five are long; the skin, hair, finger joints, teeth and body hair — these five are fine; the eyes, soles, palms, palate, lower lip, upper lip and nails — these seven are red; the chest, shoulders, nails, nose, waist and face — these six are raised; the neck, shanks and generative organ — these three are short; the waist, forehead and chest — these three are broad; the navel, voice and strength of character (*sattva*) — these three are deep. One who has these thirty-two marks is a *mahāpuruṣa*, a great personality.

## 17

“Both families”: the father's family and the mother's family.

## 37

A pilgrim (*tairthika*) came as a guest to the house of Śrī Jagannātha Miśra, and Miśra gave him all he needed for cooking and had him cook. When the pilgrim brāhmaṇa was offering the food to Gopāla in meditation, Nimāi came and began to eat his rice. Rejecting the food Nimāi had touched, the guest brāhmaṇa cooked once more; but this time too, as he offered it in meditation, the same thing happened. The food was cooked a third time. By then everyone in the house was asleep. The brāhmaṇa was offering the cooked rice to Gopāla in meditation when Nimāi came and began to eat that rice. Struck by fate, the brāhmaṇa began to cry out in distress. Then Nimāi said: “O brāhmaṇa, when I was Yaśodā's darling in Vraja, the same thing happened to you then. Now too, drawn by your devotion, I have mercifully shown Myself to you.” Seeing his own worshipful Deity, the brāhmaṇa was enchanted with great *prema*; he counted himself blessed and honored the remaining *prasāda*. The Lord forbade him to reveal this hidden pastime.
