> Subheading: Kālidāsa's qualities —

## 7

Śrī Kālidāsa had dedicated himself to Viṣṇu and the Vaiṣṇavas, was devoted to them alone, and possessed transcendental faith. Suppose some *jīva* full of *anarthas* does not follow his steadfast devotion to Kṛṣṇa's name, but instead, for the gratification of his own senses and seeing with outward, sense-born vision, imitates his misleading pastime and becomes attached to the futile vice of playing dice (*pāśā*, *dyūta*) and the like. Then (according to *Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam* 1.17.38–41[^7-1]) his servitude to Kali will increase his sin, that is, his inclination toward irreligion (*adharma*). And if he outwardly imitates chanting the name and makes a show of chanting it, that very imitation will lead him to sin on the strength of the name, and so it will end only in offense against the name (*nāmāparādha*). All the educated, self-controlled, and virtuous people of the world will condemn such hypocrisy, an immoral deceit practiced in the name of religion.

## 14

> Subheading: The account of Kālidāsa and Jhaḍu Ṭhākura —

## 5–14

Kālidāsa and Jhaḍu Ṭhākura. The *śrīpāṭas* of both were in the village of “Bhedo,” or “Bhaduyā.” It lies about three miles south of the village of “Kṛṣṇapura,” where Śrīla Dāsa Gosvāmī appeared, and about a mile west of Bandel Junction on the E.I.R. line; the post office is Devānandapura. The Deity Śrī Madana-gopāla, whom Jhaḍu Ṭhākura served, is worshiped here by a certain Rāmāyet named Śrī Rāmaprasāda dāsa. It is said that the Deity whom Kālidāsa served had until recently been served after a fashion in Śaṅkha-nagara on the bank of the Sarasvatī River. A little more than twenty years ago a resident of Triveṇī named Motilal Caṭṭopādhyāya took Him away, and he serves Him in his own home.
