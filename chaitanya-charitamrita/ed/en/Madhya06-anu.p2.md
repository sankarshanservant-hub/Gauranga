## 149

After Śrī Kṛṣṇa had crushed Brahmā's pride, Brahmā took exclusive shelter of Him and offered Him prayers. In them he praises the surpassing good fortune in love of the residents of Vraja, so dear to Kṛṣṇa:

**nanda-gopa-vrajaukasām** (of the residents of Vraja, who abide in the five *rasas*, headed by King Nanda) **aho bhāgyam**; **yat** (whose — of those residents of Vraja) **mitram** **sanātanam** (eternally manifest) **pūrṇam** (undivided) **paramānandam** (concentrated eternity, knowledge and bliss), **brahma**.

## 150

> Subheading: The *śruti-mantra* rejects material features and thereby points to the transcendental form of eternity, knowledge and bliss —

“Without hands and feet, He moves swiftly and takes; without eyes He sees, without ears He hears. He knows all that is to be known, yet no one knows Him. They call Him the foremost, the great Person.” (*Śvetāśvatara Upaniṣad* 3.19)

## 151

> Subheading: In the primary sense, possession of features; in the secondary sense, featurelessness —

The *śruti* statements quoted above establish only that Brahman is possessed of features. But the Māyāvādī abandons the primary, direct meaning (*abhidhā-vṛtti*) and by the secondary sense (*lakṣaṇā*) establishes the doctrine of featurelessness. Yet even the featurelessness derived through *lakṣaṇā* is merely one mark of the doctrine of features: its only purpose is to show the distinction from material features.

## 152

> Subheading: To present spiritual play as the absence of play — this is Māyāvāda —

## 153

The *kevalādvaita-vādīs* regard potency as a temporary condition born of ignorance, and so they take being without potency as the defining mark of Brahman. But since three potencies eternally abide in Brahman, there is no need at all to conclude, with the help of reasoning such as the doctrine of superimposition (*adhyāropa-vāda*), that Brahman is without potency.
