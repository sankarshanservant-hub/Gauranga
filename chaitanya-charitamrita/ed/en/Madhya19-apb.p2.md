## 151–164

The *jīvas*, each following the thread of his own *karma*, wander through the brahmāṇḍa, taking birth in various species. Among them, one in whom good fortune dawns — *sukṛti* capable of giving birth to devotion — receives by the mercy of the guru and Kṛṣṇa the seed of the creeper of devotion, which is “faith” (*śraddhā*). Having received this seed, he becomes a gardener and plants it in the field of his heart. The planted seed sprouts, and the gardener waters that field with the water of hearing and chanting — of narrations about Bhagavān and about His devotees. The creeper of devotion springs up and grows and grows; it pierces this brahmāṇḍa made of *māyā*, pierces the Virajā and the effulgent Brahmaloka, and finds its place in Paravyoma. In Paravyoma the creeper spreads and rises still higher, up to Goloka Vṛndāvana, and climbs onto the desire tree of Kṛṣṇa's feet. Only on the creeper of devotion that has climbed onto Kṛṣṇa's feet does the “fruit of *prema*” grow. Until then the gardener keeps watering it with the water of hearing and chanting.

At this time, besides watering, there is one more task. After some days of watering, when the creeper begins to grow, some wicked animal may come and uproot or tear it, and the creeper withers. Here the wicked animal is offense to a Vaiṣṇava. It is offense to a Vaiṣṇava that, like a mad elephant, does all this damage. At this time, therefore, the gardener takes special care: he puts up a fence and makes an enclosure, and then the elephant of offense does not appear. The offenses to a Vaiṣṇava, or offenses to the holy name, are ten (see Ādi 8.24).

At this time there is one more trouble. As the creeper of devotion rises, side shoots may grow too much, and this does it harm. The side shoots are the desire for *bhukti*, the desire for *mukti*, forbidden conduct, duplicity (*kuṭīnāṭī*), the tendency to harm living beings, the craving for gain, and the hope for material honor or position. Unless one is especially careful, then from the water of hearing, chanting and so on the side shoots grow most of all, to the detriment of the main creeper, and the main shoot is checked and cannot grow. The gardener must therefore cut away these *anarthas* — the side shoots — from the very beginning, while he is still watering with hearing and chanting. Only then will the main shoot grow and reach Vṛndāvana. This *prema* is the *jīva*'s highest goal (*parama-puruṣārtha*); beside it the four goals of life — *dharma*, *artha*, *kāma* and *mokṣa* — are worth no more than a blade of grass.

## 165

As long as not even a trace of *prema* — of *dāsya* and the other *rasas*, which act like a perfect herb for bringing Kṛṣṇa under control — has become a traveler on the path of the heart, so long the *jīva* is dazzled by the glitter of each of these: the supremacy of the rich *siddhis*, *samādhi* founded on *dharma* such as truthfulness, and the highest bliss of Brahman.

## 166

*Prema* does not arise from a semblance of devotion (*bhakty-ābhāsa*); *prema* arises only from pure devotion.

## 168

The characteristic of pure devotion is this. In pure devotion there can be no desire other than the desire for one's own advancement (on the path to spiritual perfection) for the sake of serving Kṛṣṇa. There can be no worship of any object of worship other than Kṛṣṇa — of Brahman, the Paramātmā or any other form — nor can *jñāna* and *karma* be present in their own forms. To be free of all this, to accept in life only what is favorable to devotion, and with all the senses to cultivate service to Kṛṣṇa (*kṛṣṇānuśīlana*) — this is called “pure devotion.”

## 170

Serving Hṛṣīkeśa with all the senses is called “devotion.” This service (which constitutes the characteristic of essence, the *svarūpa-lakṣaṇa*) has two “marginal” (*taṭastha*) characteristics: such pure devotion remains free of all designations (*upādhis*), and, being directed to Kṛṣṇa alone, is itself pure.

## 174

Such devotion is called “absolute *bhakti-yoga*” (*ātyantika-bhakti-yoga*). By this *bhakti-yoga* the *jīva* transcends *māyā*, which is made of the *guṇas*, and attains pure love for Me.

## 176

The desire for enjoyment and the desire for liberation are two witches (*piśācīs*). As long as they dwell in someone's heart, the happiness of devotion cannot dawn in that heart.
