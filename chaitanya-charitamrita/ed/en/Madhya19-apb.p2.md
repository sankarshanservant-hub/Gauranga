## 151–164

The *jīvas*, each following the thread of his own *karma*, wander through the brahmāṇḍa, taking birth in various species. Among them, one in whom good fortune dawns — *sukṛti* capable of giving birth to devotion — receives by the mercy of the guru and Kṛṣṇa the seed of the creeper of devotion, which is “faith” (*śraddhā*). Having received this seed, he becomes a gardener and plants it in the field of his heart. The planted seed sprouts, and the gardener waters that field with the water of hearing and chanting — of narrations about Bhagavān and about His devotees. The creeper of devotion springs up and grows and grows; it pierces this brahmāṇḍa made of *māyā*, pierces the Virajā and the effulgent Brahmaloka, and finds its place in Paravyoma. In Paravyoma the creeper spreads and rises still higher, up to Goloka Vṛndāvana, and climbs onto the desire tree of Kṛṣṇa's feet. Only on the creeper of devotion that has climbed onto Kṛṣṇa's feet does the “fruit of *prema*” grow. Until then the gardener keeps watering it with the water of hearing and chanting.

At this time, besides watering, there is one more task. After some days of watering, when the creeper begins to grow, some wicked animal may come and uproot or tear it, and the creeper withers. Here the wicked animal is offense to a Vaiṣṇava. It is offense to a Vaiṣṇava that, like a mad elephant, does all this damage. At this time, therefore, the gardener takes special care: he puts up a fence and makes an enclosure, and then the elephant of offense does not appear. The offenses to a Vaiṣṇava, or offenses to the holy name, are ten (see Ādi 8.24).

At this time there is one more trouble. As the creeper of devotion rises, side shoots may grow too much, and this does it harm. The side shoots are the desire for *bhukti*, the desire for *mukti*, forbidden conduct, duplicity (*kuṭīnāṭī*), the tendency to harm living beings, the craving for gain, and the hope for material honor or position. Unless one is especially careful, then from the water of hearing, chanting and so on the side shoots grow most of all, to the detriment of the main creeper, and the main shoot is checked and cannot grow. The gardener must therefore cut away these *anarthas* — the side shoots — from the very beginning, while he is still watering with hearing and chanting. Only then will the main shoot grow and reach Vṛndāvana. This *prema* is the *jīva*'s highest goal (*parama-puruṣārtha*); beside it the four goals of life — *dharma*, *artha*, *kāma* and *mokṣa* — are worth no more than a blade of grass.

## 165

As long as not even a trace of *prema* — of *dāsya* and the other *rasas*, which act like a perfect herb for bringing Kṛṣṇa under control — has become a traveler on the path of the heart, so long the *jīva* is dazzled by the glitter of each of these: the supremacy of the rich *siddhis*, *samādhi* founded on *dharma* such as truthfulness, and the highest bliss of Brahman.

## 166

*Prema* does not arise from a semblance of devotion (*bhakty-ābhāsa*); *prema* arises only from pure devotion.

## 168

The characteristic of pure devotion is this. In pure devotion there can be no desire other than the desire for one's own advancement (on the path to spiritual perfection) for the sake of serving Kṛṣṇa. There can be no worship of any object of worship other than Kṛṣṇa — of Brahman, the Paramātmā or any other form — nor can *jñāna* and *karma* be present in their own forms. To be free of all this, to accept in life only what is favorable to devotion, and with all the senses to cultivate service to Kṛṣṇa (*kṛṣṇānuśīlana*) — this is called “pure devotion.”

## 170

Serving Hṛṣīkeśa with all the senses is called “devotion.” This service (which constitutes the characteristic of essence, the *svarūpa-lakṣaṇa*) has two “marginal” (*taṭastha*) characteristics: such pure devotion remains free of all designations (*upādhis*), and, being directed to Kṛṣṇa alone, is itself pure.

## 174

Such devotion is called “absolute *bhakti-yoga*” (*ātyantika-bhakti-yoga*). By this *bhakti-yoga* the *jīva* transcends *māyā*, which is made of the *guṇas*, and attains pure love for Me.

## 176

The desire for enjoyment and the desire for liberation are two witches (*piśācīs*). As long as they dwell in someone's heart, the happiness of devotion cannot dawn in that heart.

## 177–181

Devotion has three states: the state of practice (*sādhana*), the state of *bhāva*, and the state of *prema*. The nine limbs — hearing, chanting and the rest — are at first performed only in *sādhana-bhakti*. The more the *anarthas* mentioned above diminish as one hears and chants with faith, the higher faith itself (*śraddhā-vṛtti*) gradually rises, and it becomes known by the names *niṣṭhā*, *ruci*, *āsakti*, and *bhāva* or *rati*. *Rati* arises from *sādhana-bhakti*; the denser this *rati* grows through engagement (cultivation) in hearing, chanting and so on, the more it takes the names *prema* and the rest. (Gradually) *prema*, as it increases, rises to *sneha*, *māna*, *praṇaya*, *rāga*, *anurāga*, *bhāva* and *mahābhāva*. Here is an example. Sugarcane juice is like the seed corresponding to *rati*; the denser it grows, the further it passes through the stages of molasses (*guḍa*), then *khaṇḍasāra*, *śarkarā*, *sitā-misri* and the finest *misri*. Everything from *rati* to *mahābhāva* is known in *kṛṣṇa-bhakti-rasa* as *sthāyi-bhāva*; but everywhere it is *rati* above all that is called the *sthāyi-bhāva*.

When the four *bhāvas* — *vibhāva*, *anubhāva*, *sāttvika* and *vyabhicārī* — combine with the *sthāyi-bhāva*, *rasa* arises. When, in devotion to Kṛṣṇa, these ingredients join the *sthāyi-bhāva*, there is “*kṛṣṇa-bhakti-rasa*.” The *sthāyi-bhāva* is the chief foundation on which *rasa* is kindled; with it the four ingredients — *vibhāva* and the rest — are combined. Thus the *sthāyi-bhāva* is the “root” of *rasa*, *vibhāva* its “cause,” *anubhāva* its “effect,” *sāttvika-bhāva* a special “effect,” and the *sañcārī* or *vyabhicārī bhāvas* its “helpers.” *Vibhāva* is divided into two kinds, *ālambana* and *uddīpana*. *Ālambana* is again divided into two, *viṣaya* and *āśraya*. In *kṛṣṇa-bhakti-rasa* the devotee is the *āśraya*, Kṛṣṇa is the *viṣaya*, and Kṛṣṇa's qualities[^177-181-1] are the *uddīpana*.

The *anubhāvas* are of thirteen kinds: (1) dancing, (2) rolling on the ground, (3) singing, (4) loud crying, (5) stretching the body, (6) roaring (*huṅkāra*), (7) yawning, (8) heavy breathing, (9) disregard for public opinion, (10) drooling, (11) loud laughter, (12) reeling, (13) hiccups. Not all the signs of the *anubhāvas* appear at once: as the *rasa* works, now one sign and now another appears. The *sāttvika-bhāvas* are of eight kinds, and the *sañcārī* or *vyabhicārī bhāvas* number thirty-three (see the *Amṛta-pravāha-bhāṣya* on Madhya 14.167).

## 186

The “principal *rasas*” (*mukhya-rasas*) are of five kinds: *śānta*, *dāsya*, *sakhya*, *vātsalya* and *madhura*. Laughter (*hāsya*), wonder (*adbhuta*), heroism (*vīra*), compassion (*karuṇa*), anger (*raudra*), fear (*bhayānaka*) and disgust (*bībhatsa*) are the seven kinds of “secondary *rasa*” (*gauṇa-rasa*).

## 188

The five principal *rasas* mentioned above abide in the devotee's heart permanently, as *sthāyi-bhāvas*. The secondary *rasas* — laughter, wonder and the rest — arise in the devotee's heart as visitors when a “cause” is present; they nourish the principal *rasa* and then subside.

## 190

“In Vraja,” Śrīdāmā and others; “in the city,” in the pastimes of Dvārakā, Bhīma and Arjuna.

## 192–194

*Kṛṣṇa-rati* is of two kinds: mixed with awareness of majesty (*aiśvarya*), and *kevalā* — that is, devoid of awareness of majesty. In the “two cities” — Dvārakā and Mathurā — and in Vaikuṇṭha and other abodes, devotion is mixed with awareness of majesty. Therefore *prema* there is contracted. But in Gokula the cowherd men and women, whose *rati* is *kevalā*, do not wish to acknowledge Kṛṣṇa's majesty even when they see it.

## 195

*Kāṅhā* (somewhere) means in certain places.

## 197

Devakī and Vasudeva, knowing Baladeva and Śrī Kṛṣṇa to be the “Lords of the universe,” became afraid and could not embrace Them.

## 199–200

Thinking of You as a friend and not knowing Your greatness, out of carelessness or out of love I rashly said to You, “O Kṛṣṇa, O Yādava, O friend!” For all that I said in this way, and for all the disrespect I showed You in jest — while eating, at play, lying down or sitting, alone or before everyone — for all this, O You whose nature is immeasurable, I beg forgiveness.

## 202

When in Dvārakā Kṛṣṇa teased Rukmiṇī, her reason was lost through intense pain, fear and grief: the fan fell from her hand, from which the bracelets were slipping; her hair came loose; and her body, like a banana tree struck by the wind, suddenly gave way and she fainted.

## 204

Kṛṣṇa, whose greatness is sung by the three Vedas, the Upaniṣads, *sāṅkhya-yoga* and the scriptures on devotion — Yaśodā knows Him as her “son.”

## 205

Him who is unmanifest and beyond the senses — Adhokṣaja, who appeared as if in a mortal body — Yaśodā, thinking Him her son, bound with a rope to the mortar like an ordinary child.

## 206

Bhagavān Kṛṣṇa, having lost, carried Śrīdāmā on His shoulders; Bhadrasena carried Vṛṣabha, and Pralamba carried Rohiṇī's son Baladeva.

## 207–209

“Leaving the gopīs who long for Him, this beloved Kṛṣṇa lovingly serves me” — with such pride (Rādhikā thought herself the best of all the gopīs, and at last), going into the forest, Rādhikā said to Śrī Kṛṣṇa, “O Kṛṣṇa, I can't walk any further; carry me wherever You like.” When Rādhikā said this, Kṛṣṇa replied, “Climb onto My shoulders.” Having said this, Kṛṣṇa vanished, and Rādhikā, Kṛṣṇa's beloved, began to lament.
