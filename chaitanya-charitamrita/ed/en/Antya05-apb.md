# Amṛta-pravāha-bhāṣya on Antya-līlā 5

## 0

**Summary.** Pradyumna Miśra, a resident of Śrīhaṭṭa, wished to hear talks about Kṛṣṇa from Mahāprabhu, and the Lord sent him to Rāmānanda. Hearing how Rāmānanda dealt with the *devadāsīs*, Miśra came back. Mahāprabhu then explained to him well the truth about Rāmānanda, and Miśra went again and received instruction in the truth from Rāmānanda. A *brāhmaṇa* from Bengal composed a play about Mahāprabhu’s pastimes and brought it. Svarūpa Gosvāmī heard it and pointed out the fault of Māyāvāda in it, yet he gave the poet’s verses a second meaning and so pleased him. His life’s purpose fulfilled, the poet gave up everything and stayed in Nīlācala under the shelter of the Vaiṣṇavas.

## 1

Bitten by the worm of *vaiguṇya*, tormented by envy and sunk in an ocean of humility, I have taken shelter of the physician, Caitanya.

## 7

**Prabhu kahena**: Mahāprabhu said.

## 10

A person may perform the *dharma* of the *varṇas* and *āśramas* in the best way. But if it does not awaken in him attraction (*rati*) for talks about Kṛṣṇa, even that *dharma* is only wasted labour.

## 20

Rāya Rāmānanda had composed a play called *Jagannātha-vallabha*. To stage it before Śrī Jagannāthadeva, he brought two *deva-kanyās*, that is, young *devadāsīs* (today they are called *māhārīs*), and was teaching them the mood of the gopīs that the play requires. These two girls were to act the pastimes of the chief gopīs. So he regarded them as the chief gopīs, the ones to be served (*sevya-buddhi*), took for himself the mood of a maidservant following them, and taught them the songs and other service for the coming performance. Śrī Rāmānanda knew himself to be Śrīmatī’s maidservant. Seeing in the actresses of Śrīmatī’s role the one he serves, he cared for their bodies, adorned them and so on.

## 46

**Tina guṇa** (the three *guṇas*): *sattva*, *rajas* and *tamas*. The agitation of these three *guṇas* gives rise to the desire for union between man and woman, but in him no such desire arises.

## 48

Whoever, filled with transcendental faith, hears or describes Kṛṣṇa’s transcendental play with the young women of Vraja in these “Five Chapters on the Rāsa” (*rāsa-pañcādhyāyī*) — that steady person (*dhīra*) gains abundant supreme devotion (*parā-bhakti*) for Bhagavān and quickly drives away material lust, the disease of the heart. The point is this: all of Kṛṣṇa’s pastimes are “spiritual” (*cinmaya*). The fully spiritual Kṛṣṇa, beyond the senses (*adhokṣaja*), plays with the spiritual gopīs. When one discusses these pastimes with faith, that is, striving to realise the spiritual truth, then, as spiritual love awakens, attachment to matter, material lust and the rest depart. And when the wholly spiritual pastimes have fully appeared, not a trace of material lust remains.

## 84–85

The *sannyāsīs* (of Śaṅkara’s school) think that, having carried out in worldly life all the duties proper to a *brāhmaṇa* and having cultivated the truth of Vedānta, they have become the “gurus” of the world. The *brāhmaṇa* scholars (by seed) think that, according to the *smṛti* (of the section on ritual work, the *karma-kāṇḍa*), only a *brāhmaṇa* by seed like themselves is the guru of all *varṇas*; therefore no one but such a *brāhmaṇa* scholar by seed has the right to teach the highest truth. Proud with these two kinds of pride, the *sannyāsīs* and scholars are unwilling to learn *dharma* from a pure devotee born in a family of the highest *śūdras*, though he is infinitely greater than they, and so they often remain undeveloped in understanding. Vaiṣṇava *dharma* accepts this: whoever, knowing the difference between the worldly and the transcendental truth, has learned transcendental devotion to Kṛṣṇa is the teacher of all *jīvas*; here neither *varṇa* by birth nor an *āśrama* received through rites (*saṁskāras*) matters. It was to teach this truth that Mahāprabhu, the deliverer of the world, sent Pradyumna Miśra, the son of a relative from His former *āśrama*, to learn the truth from Śrī Rāmānanda.

## 102

***Yadvā tadvā kavi*** — any poet whatever, that is, those who compose poetry without properly knowing either *rasa-tattva* or the *siddhānta* of the Vaiṣṇavas.

## 107

***Grāmya-kavi*** — poets who compose verses about mundane men and women. ***Vidagdha ātmīya-vākya*** — the composition of one who is our own (*ātmīya*): a person skilled in the knowledge of *tattva* who belongs to the community of pure devotees (that is, warm-hearted and of like aspiration).

## 112

He placed within Himself — or spread forth — a golden lustre and attained His selfhood in Śrī Jagannātha, whose eyes are blossoming lotuses. Giving boundless consciousness to inert *prakṛti*, He appeared. May that Kṛṣṇa Caitanyadeva bestow welfare upon you.

## 123

In Īśvara there is no difference between the body and its possessor (*dehī*).

## 137

Indra said: “Relying on this talkative, foolish, impudent, ignorant, mortal Kṛṣṇa, who thinks Himself learned, the cowherds have done what displeases me.”

## 141

***Vandyābhāve anamra — stabdha-śabde kaya***: there is no one whom He must worship, and so He bows to no one — this is what the word *stabdha* expresses.

## 143

***Nā yujhimu, ‘yāhi bandhu-han’*** — “O slayer of Your kin, go away; I will not fight with You.”

*Thus ends the fifth chapter of the “Amṛta-pravāha-bhāṣya.”*
