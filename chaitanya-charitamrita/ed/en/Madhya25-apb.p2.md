## 134

Remembering Hari, who destroys masses of sin, and reminding one another of Him, they bear bodies whose hair stands on end through *prema-bhakti* born of *sādhana-bhakti*.

## 137

This *Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam* is the meaning of the *Brahma-sūtra*, the settled purport of the *Mahābhārata* and a commentary on the Gāyatrī; the meaning of all the Vedas enriches it. The *Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam* contains 18,000 verses.

## 138

[It is] the essence drawn from all the Vedas and *itihāsas* ([Vyāsa] had his son Śrī Śukadeva study the *Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam*).

## 139

The *Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam* may be called the essence of Vedānta. One who is satisfied by the nectar of the *Bhāgavatam*'s *rasa* finds no taste (*rati*) in any other scripture.

## 144

This scripture, the *Bhāgavatam*, is the fruit fallen from the wish-fulfilling tree of the Vedas, joined with the nectarean juice from Śukadeva's mouth. O *rasikas*, always drink this fruit, which is *rasa* itself! O *bhāvukas*, until you are fully absorbed (*parama-laya*) in the truth of *rasa*, that is, until you are immersed in it, relish the *Bhāgavatam* in this world (as transcendental *bhāvukas*); and even when immersed, you will go on drinking this supreme *rasa* again and again, eternally.

## 145

The more we hear of the exploits of Kṛṣṇa, Uttamaśloka, the more our thirst grows; the satisfaction that would quench it never comes. For to listeners who know *rasa*, the accounts of Kṛṣṇa yield a [new] taste at every step.

## 181

*Munsīph*: the word *munsīph* comes from the word *insāph* (justice). One who takes charge of some matter on behalf of the principal owner is called a *munsīph*. *Chidra pāñā* — finding a fault.

## 183

“His wife” — Husain Shāh's begum. “The mark of the blow” — the mark of the whip with which Subuddhi Rāya had struck him.

## 186

*Karoyāra pāni*: a *karoyā* is the vessel in which Muslims keep water. From such a *karoyā*, water touched by Muslims was put into Subuddhi Rāya's mouth[^186-1].

## 187

*Chadma* — a pretext. Subuddhi Rāya had already wished to give up worldly affairs; on the pretext of the loss of caste he left his family.

## 190

Subuddhi Rāya met Mahāprabhu when the Lord came to Vārāṇasī before going to Mathurā.

## 202

“Hearing of this”: in Mathurā Rūpa Gosvāmī heard that earlier Mahāprabhu had gone to Mathurā by the road along the Gaṅgā; now, eager to see that road, he came the same way with Anupama[^202-1].

## 206

*Vyavahāra-sneha* — affection rooted in worldly relationships.

## 271

People are nourished by food and drink. The devotees, though they too take food and drink as those averse to God do, grow weak if they do not drink the nectar of Caitanya-līlā mixed with Kṛṣṇa-līlā.

## 275

May this *Caitanya-caritāmṛta* be offered to Kṛṣṇa Caitanya for the pleasure of Śrī Madana-Gopāla and Śrī Govinda-deva[^275-1].

## 276

This most confidential nectar of Gaura's pastimes is the treasure of a devotee's life, but the unqualified will certainly not value it. That is no loss to me. On the contrary, all good-hearted sādhus have fully relished the nectar of these pastimes; may this book increase the joy of those great souls.

*Thus ends the twenty-fifth chapter of the “Amṛta-pravāha-bhāṣya.”*
