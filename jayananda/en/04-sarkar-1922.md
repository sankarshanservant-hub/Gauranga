# Jadunath Sarkar on Jayananda (1922)

*Jadunath Sarkar, Chaitanya's Life and Teachings, 2nd ed., Calcutta, 1922 (Project Gutenberg #38016): from the introductory part and the appendix "The last scene". Original English text.*[^1]

Jayananda Mishra (b. about 1511) wrote his *Chaitanya-Mangal* about 1568, and his poem gives us much new information about the saint and his family. He is our only authority for the narrative of Chaitanya's death, which I have translated at the end of this work.

**The last scene** (translated from the *Chaitanya-mangal* of Jayananda, p. 150):[^2]

When dancing at the Bijaya of the Car festival in the month of Asharh, His left toe was suddenly pierced by a brick [lying on the road]. When Adwaita left for Bengal, the Master secretly told him [of His coming disappearance]. With all His followers He sported in the water of the Narendra tank [for the last time]. On the sixth day of the moon, the pain in His toe grew severer, and He was forced to take to His bed in the garden. Here He told the Pandit Goswami that He would leave the earth next night at 10 o clock. Celestial garlands of many-coloured flowers were thrown on Him from the unseen. Celestial singers (*vidyadhar*) began to dance on the highway. The gods began to cry out, "Bring the heavenly chariot!" The Master mounted into Vishnu's car with the figure of Garuda on its spire. His material body lay behind on the earth, while He went to Vaikuntha (Vishnu's heaven). Many of His servants killed themselves by serpent-bite. Meteors and thunderbolts fell on the earth. At the news Nityananda and Adwaita Acharya, Vishnupriya and Shachi swooned away. Purushottam and other servitors of the Master grew speechless at His departure.

Nityananda consoled the disciples and vowed before them, "We will keep the Name alive. We will make all men down to the Chandals, Vaishnavs. We will not differentiate [low] castes like the Chandals or Muslims, but will give them all love and *bhakti* and make them all dance [with us] at *kirtan*. We will make the realms of Bengal and Orissa blessed." The Vaishnavs shouted applause at his words.

[^1]: The first paragraph comes from the introductory part of the book, where Sarkar characterizes his sources; the rest is his translation at the end of the book. Sarkar's text is reproduced verbatim (spelling, punctuation and his square-bracketed additions kept; only line breaks and italics markup adapted). Translator's notes are added for the project.
[^2]: Sarkar translates from the 1905 edition of the Chaitanya-mangala (Bangiya Sahitya Parishat, ed. N. Vasu and K. Nath). The same episode from Vasu's manuscript (folios 75–76) is translated in `02-vasu-1897.md` (the section on Mahaprabhu's disappearance, notes 67–68). Agreements: the wound from a brick to the left foot while dancing at the car festival; the secret word to Advaita before his departure for Gauda; the water-play in the Narendra lake; the pain growing worse on the sixth day; the Lord taking to his bed at Tota (Sarkar's "garden" is a literal rendering of *tota*); the words to the Pandita Gosani (probably Gadadhara) that he would depart the next night; the garlands, the dancing vidyadharas, the gods' cry "Bring the chariot!", the chariot with the Garuda banner (Sarkar: "figure … on its spire"); the body left on earth; the servants' death by snake-bite; the falling meteors and thunderbolts. Differences: Vasu's extract opens with two couplets absent in Sarkar (Chaitanya at Tota announces his departure and asks for the chariot to be sent on the bright seventh lunar day of Ashadha), and it ends with the portents, whereas Sarkar continues (the swoons of Nityananda, Advaita, Vishnupriya and Shachi, Purushottama's speechlessness, Nityananda's vow) beyond Vasu's quotation; Vasu's portents also include an earthquake, which Sarkar lacks; "the tenth danda of the night" (about four hours after sunset) is rendered by Sarkar as "10 o clock"; where Vasu has the "illusory body", Sarkar has the "material body"; the obscure word before "car procession" (Vasu, note 67) is passed over by Sarkar, who keeps only "Bijaya" (going forth, procession). Sarkar translates in free prose and abridges in places. Sarkar's dating (the poem c. 1568) is later than Vasu's (shortly after 1480 Shaka, i.e. c. 1558); the birth year c. 1511 agrees with Vasu's conclusions (`02-vasu-1897.md`, note 24).
