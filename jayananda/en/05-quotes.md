# Quotations from the Poem in Later Authors

*Collected here are passages of Jayananda's poem quoted in the works of D. C. Sen (1914), Girijashankar Raychaudhuri (1946), B. B. Majumdar (1939/1959), Sukumar Sen (1940) and other authors. Only Jayananda's verse is translated; the prose of the quoting authors is not given. The passages are arranged by the khandas of the poem; page numbers refer to the 1905 edition where the quoting author gives them. Lines already translated in "The Surviving Fragments of the Poem" or in N. Vasu's article of 1897 are not repeated here: a cross-reference stands in their place.*

## Adi-khanda

### 1. The Calamities of the Kali Age

*Girijashankar Raychaudhuri, 1946*

**1.** And here, in the Kali age, great lawlessness arose;
the Earth went weeping to Brahma's door.

**2.** The wife does not heed her husband's word;
trees, creepers and fruit are seized by a king of the mlechchha race.[^1]

**3.** Young widows have grown fond of fish and meat;
the king does not care for his subjects — such is the mlechchhas' way.[^2]

**4.** Two, three, four varnas have become one;
the mlechchha race does violence to gods and brahmanas.

**5.** The kshatriyas are powerless in battle; there are no ascetics, no faithful wives.[^3]

**6.** All that Gauranga did as the avatara of the age[^4] —

**7.** keeping the dharma of the age, kirtana in every land —

**8.** all, down to the chandalas, will be saved.

### 2. Homage to Chaitanya's Mother

*B. B. Majumdar, Sri Chaitanya-chariter Upadan, 1959; 1905 ed., p. 2*

**1.** I honour Ai Thakurani, Chaitanya's mother,
to whom Pandita Gosani gave the mantra of initiation.[^5]

### 3. Homage to Advaita's Companions

*B. B. Majumdar, 1959, appendix; 1905 ed., p. 2*

**1.** Purushottama and the other companions of Advaita,
by whose names the wealth of prema and bhakti grows.[^6]

### 4. Homage to Nityananda's Wives

*B. B. Majumdar, 1959; 1905 ed., p. 3*

**1.** The daughters of Suryadasa, the moon-faced ladies,
full of love for Nityananda — Sri Vasu and Jahnavi.[^7]

## Nadia-khanda

### 5. The Birth of Gaurachandra

*Girijashankar Raychaudhuri, 1946*

**1.** The full moon of Phalguna arrived;
knowing the time of birth had come, Narayani came.[^8]

**2.** Shachi Thakurani gave birth to Gaurachandra;
cutting the cord, the nurse-mother took him in her arms.

### 6. The Infant's First Months

*Girijashankar Raychaudhuri, 1946*

**1.** On the sixth day, gathering all their kin and friends,
they performed the worship [of the goddess] of the birth-chamber by many rites.[^9]

**2.** On the twentieth day they gave him the name Vishvambhara;
the name "Nimai Pandita" became famed throughout the world.

**3.** At six months they performed his first feeding with rice;
everyone began to call him "Nimai."

**4.** A tiger's claw at his neck, a silk tassel on his back,[^10]
the crest-jewel of the twice-born crawls about on all fours.

**5.** Two teeth came through — buds of kunda jasmine;
his lips opened like a ripe telakucha fruit.[^11]

**6.** On his head a top-knot with a peacock feather, at his neck a string of gunja berries;
suddenly golden anklets rang on his feet.[^12]

### 7. Nimai's Infancy

*Sukumar Sen, Bangala Sahityer Itihas, vol. 1, 1940, p. 342; 1905 ed., pp. 14–15*

**1–2.** *[= passage 6, couplets 4–5.]*[^13]

**3.** Makara bracelets on his arms, a necklace, makara anklets on his feet;
a red stick, a golden stick — a whole basket of beauty.[^14]

**4.** Seeing that enchanting form, the moon stops still and gazes;
at such beauty a hundred thousand million Madanas swoon.

**5.** Seeing him, Mishra Purandara thinks of nothing else:
eating and sleeping he calls, "My little son, Nimai!"

**6.** Now he claps his hands, laughs and dances,
now, coaxing sweetly, he begs a kiss from mother and father.

**7.** Now he rolls on the ground and cries, grey with dust;
seeing it, Shachi and Mishra Purandara rejoice.

**8.** "Treasure of his mother's life, lord of his father,
god of my house — my little son Nimai!"

**9.** To all the people of Nadia you are witness;
these words are true, and Jayananda is witness to it.[^15]

### 8. Childish Pranks

*Girijashankar Raychaudhuri, 1946*

**1.** He struck his mother in the face with a brick — blood runs down Shachi's face;
Shachi fainted, her hair fell loose; the Lord ran off at full speed.[^16]

**2–3.** *[= "The Surviving Fragments," fragment 6, couplets 12–13.]*[^17]

**4.** "Regard all beings as equal, be compassionate to them as to yourself;
the difference between pure and impure — all of it is maya."[^18]

**5.** He snatched the sacred thread from his father
and ran off at full speed to his maternal uncle's house.

**6.** "When I do not see you, nothing pleases me."

### 9. The Tonsure; the First Letters

*Girijashankar Raychaudhuri, 1946*

**1.** With auspicious songs they performed the rite of tonsure and ear-piercing.[^19]

**2.** Writing "ka," "kha" — the thirty-four letters — on a board,[^20]
he crawls on all fours and learns them, looking at his guru and his mother.

**3.** "Why is this called 'ka,' 'kha'?" he asks his guru;
"Teacher, why do you call this 'ka,' 'kha'?"[^21]

**4.** Hearing this, Sudarshana in anger struck [him] with a stick.

### 10. Study with Gangadasa

*B. B. Majumdar, 1959, appendix; 1905 ed., p. 18*

**1.** In Navadvipa [lived] Gangadasa Pandita;
in his house he revealed his learning.[^22]

**2.** The grammars of Chandra and Sarasvata, new poems and dramas,
smriti, logic, poetics — he studied them one after another.[^23]

### 11. Vishvarupa

*Girijashankar Raychaudhuri, 1946*

**1.** All the best things Vishvarupa brings to Gauranga;
if he does not see Gaurachandra, he cannot live.

**2.** Vishvarupa says, "Nimai is my life."

**3.** They gave him the name Sri Shankararanya.[^24]

### 12. Study, Upanayana, the Father's Illness

*Girijashankar Raychaudhuri, 1946*

**1.** *[= "The Surviving Fragments," fragment 9, couplet 14, first half-line.]*[^25]

**2.** He studies the Kalapa with commentary, surpassing everyone.[^26]

**3.** "Is there any fellow who can refute my explanation?
I'll climb on his shoulders and rap him on the crown!"

**4.** Hearing this, the guru strikes him with a book;
weeping, he tears up the book and falls to the ground.

**5.** Apart from his own, he heeds no one's words.

**6.** In the eighth year from conception Vishvambhara was given the sacred thread.

**7.** They spoke the Gayatri into his ear.

**8.** A thread with [a strip of] black-antelope skin, by the Mithila rite;[^27]
a staff of bilva and bamboo they placed on his shoulder at an auspicious moment.

**9–11.** *[= "The Surviving Fragments," fragment 9, couplet 3, second half-line; couplet 14; couplet 10, second half-line, and couplet 11, first half-line.]*[^28]

**12.** In the guru's house he breaks a great many pitchers;
all the students' books floated off in the water.

**13.** From grief over Vishvarupa a fever came upon him.[^29]

**14.** The brahmanas, gathering together, took [him] to the Ganga, to the antarjala.[^30]

### 13. The Death of Jagannatha Mishra

*Girijashankar Raychaudhuri, 1946*

**1.** To where Gauranga was copying a book in the guru's house
Haridasa Thakura came running.

**2.** Haridasa Thakura says, "What are you writing books for?
Your father is at the antarjala; go quickly and see!"

**3.** Throwing down the book, he went to the Ganga, to the antarjala;
he weeps piteously, taking his father on his lap:

**4.** "Where are you going, father, leaving me?
How shall I live, father, where shall I stay?"

**5.** "…so that your mother suffers no dishonour."

**6.** He diligently performed the cremation of his father's body.[^31]

**7.** In the hot season of Jyaishtha, on the eighth lunar day of the dark fortnight.[^32]

**8.** "I have entrusted you to beloved Jagannatha."

### 14. Playmates of Childhood

*Gaurapada-tarangini, 2nd ed., 1934, introduction, p. 148; 1905 ed., p. 24*

**1.** Another day the Lord Gaurachandra
set off with the children to the guru's house.

**2.** Srinivasa Pandita Thakura with his brothers, four in all,
Vasudeva Datta and Mukunda Datta, Jagai the scribe,[^33]

**3.** Srigarbha Pandita, Murari, Govinda, Sridhara,
Gangadasa, Damodara, Sri Chandrashekhara,

**4.** Mukunda-Sanjaya, Purushottama, Vijaya,
Vakreshvara, Kata, Gangadasa, Udaya,[^34]

**5.** Sanatana, Hridaya, Madana, Ramananda —
with all of these Gaurachandra plays every day.

### 15. Gadadhara and Jagadananda in Gauranga's House

*Gaurapada-tarangini, 2nd ed., 1934, introduction, p. 148; 1905 ed., p. 27*

**1.** Gadadhara and Jagadananda in Gauranga's house
serve Gauranga every day, tending his person.

**2.** Srinivasa Pandita Thakura with his brothers, four in all,
Vasudeva, Mukunda Datta and Govindai,

**3.** Murari Gupta, Vakreshvara, Gangadasa Gosani,
Nandana, Chandrashekhara and Jagai the scribe —

**4.** hundreds of companions, playmates in his games;
on every side auspicious cries, the wealth of kirtana.[^35]

### 16. Shachi Brings Gadadhara and Jagadananda

*Sukumar Sen, Bangala Sahityer Itihas, vol. 1, 1940, p. 339; 1905 ed., p. 27*

**1.** One day in Navadvipa Shachi Thakurani
brought Gadadhara and Jagadananda, carrying them in her arms.

**2.** *[= passage 15, couplet 1.]*[^36]

### 17. A Charge Concerning Gadadhara

*Sukhamay, Tathya o Kalakram, [section on Gadadhara]; 1971 ed., Nadia-khanda, section 29*

**1.** Protect him, feed him and bring him up;
after some days give him the sacred thread.[^37]

### 18. The Journey to Gaya

*B. B. Majumdar, 1959; 1905 ed., p. 32*

**1.** Haridasa Thakura, Gadadhara Pandita,
Gopinatha, Murari, Mukunda, Vakreshvara,

**2.** with Jagadananda, Govinda and Acharyaratna —
[with them] he set out from Navadvipa for Gaya.[^38]

### 19. The Meeting with Ishvara Puri

*B. B. Majumdar, 1959; 1905 ed., p. 33*

**1.** Leaving the hard road behind and entering Magadha — [there,] at Rajagriha, lives Ishvara Puri;
the ten-syllable mantra of Gopala, endowed with the power of prema and bhakti, Ishvara Puri imparted to him as instruction.[^39]

### 20. The Intention to Go to Mathura

*Girijashankar Raychaudhuri, 1946*

**1.** "I will go to Mathura; I will not go home."

**2.** Gadadhara Pandita weeps, sighing heavily.

**3.** Hearing everyone's weeping, he did not go to Mathura.[^40]

### 21. An Ascetic on the Way

*Sukumar Sen, Bangala Sahityer Itihas, vol. 1, 1940, p. 337; 1905 ed., p. 34*

**1.** The lord of sages, practising austerity for a whole kalpa, had turned white;
at the sight of Gauranga the lord of sages' samadhi was broken.[^41]

### 22. After the Return from Gaya

*Girijashankar Raychaudhuri, 1946*

**1.** Seeing his great dance, everyone takes fright;
bones and flesh seem to be crushed by the blows as he falls to the ground;
hearing the gnashing of his teeth, people are terrified.[^42]

**2.** Shachi says, "You went to Gaya to deliver [the soul of] your father —
there is no such weeping when people dance in kirtana!

**3.** 'Day by day it will get better' — so your mother rejoiced in her heart;
everyone forgot [their grief], seeing your moon-like face.

**4.** You lost your father early, child;
you studied and did well — your mother's wish came true.

**5.** You will support everyone, you are the lord of all;
live at home in every comfort, my son Vishvambhara.

**6.** Eat, give away, save — as you please;
but when you rage like this, my son, I swoon away."[^43]

**7.** Hearing his mother's lament, he restrained his feelings.

**8.** "For some time I will study and teach without ceasing" —
such was the counsel Acharyaratna, a treasury of learning, gave.

**9.** Daily bali offerings and vaishvadeva, shraddha, bathing three times [a day],
the three sandhyas — he began to keep the customs of the brahmana line.

**10.** Repetition of mantras, recitation of hymns, meditation, concentration;
now he is busy with study, now absorbed in meditation.

### 23. Gaurachandra Worshipped in Chhakari's House

*B. B. Majumdar, 1959, appendix; 1905 ed., p. 38*

**1.** Chhakari and Chandrakala, bringing Gaurachandra to their house,
worshipped his lotus feet, knowing him to be the form of Vraja.[^44]

### 24. Lakshmi Prays for Marriage

*Girijashankar Raychaudhuri, 1946*

**1.** One day Gaurachandra went to the bank of the Ganga;
Lakshmi, with joined palms, was worshipping Shankara.

**2.** Prostrating again and again, with praise and devotion,
circling [the shrine], she meditates and asks a boon:

**3.** "Fulfil my wish, Three-eyed One:
may the Moon of Navadvipa take my hand."

**4.** At that moment her left eye began to quiver;[^45]
Lakshmi saw the Moon of Navadvipa before her.

**5.** Smiling, Gaurachandra, the ocean of mercy, says:
"At last fate has smiled on you!"[^46]

**6.** […] over the loveliness of her hair the bees hum.[^47]

**7.** "Go home, Lakshmi, with joy in your heart:
fate is favourable — your wedding is this month."

### 25. Lakshmi's Match

*Girijashankar Raychaudhuri, 1946*

**1.** "Therefore be the go-between and arrange the match."

**2.** "Oh father, give me in marriage to that bridegroom
who ties a garland of bakula flowers on his curly hair,

**3.** with a fine thread on his left shoulder, rubbed with kunkuma";
but now, when she is asked, she hangs her head for shame.[^48]

### 26. The Earlier Agreement on the Marriage

*B. B. Majumdar, 1959; 1905 ed., p. 46*

**1.** Already before, Mishra Purandara and Acharya Purandara
had agreed to make [this] match.[^49]

### 27. The Wedding: the Women of Nadia

*Girijashankar Raychaudhuri, 1946*

**1.** As Sita is to Sri Rama, as Rukmini to Krishna,
so to Gauranga is Gadadhara — know this, mother.[^50]

**2.** Hundreds of wives of good family run out, leaving their husbands.

**3.** One woman says, "I will go into the inner rooms";
another says, "I will die in the Ganga by the ocean."

**4.** Yet another says, "My whole body is trembling";
yet another says, "No sound comes from my lips."

**5.** One says, "Death take my sister-in-law!";
another says, "Let my husband do what he likes."

**6.** "What shall I say to the Creator? He made me a daughter of a good family, and on top of that I am not my own mistress;
to speak is shame and fear; I barely kept my life; I burn, pining with Madana.

**7.** To whom shall I tell it? If I tell, love is broken; neither caste nor family nor good name remains."[^51]

**8.** Hundreds of thousands of kisses she pressed on her daughter-in-law's face.

### 28. Talk of His Madness

*Girijashankar Raychaudhuri, 1946*

**1.** Some say, "From the plain food the wind has risen";
some say, "From studying and studying the wind has risen."[^52]

### 29. Departure for Bengal; Lakshmi in Separation

*Girijashankar Raychaudhuri, 1946*

**1.** She did not go to her father's house, leaving her mother-in-law.[^53]

**2.** "Serve my mother without ceasing" —
[saying this,] the ocean of mercy gave her the thread from his shoulder.

**3.** "Without earning, household life cannot go on;
I will go to the land of Bengal — on the pretext of earning."

**4.** In Bengal he acquired boundless, priceless wealth.

**5.** She worships Gauranga's thread with garlands and sandal;
the dust of the Lord's feet is the tilaka on her brow.

**6.** Not seeing his pair of sandals, her heart breaks;
drawing an image of Gauranga on a board,
clothing it in yellow cloth, she gazes on his form every day.[^54]

### 30. News of Lakshmi's Death

*B. B. Majumdar, 1959; 1905 ed., p. 50*

**1.** Hearing from people the news of Lakshmi's death,
the jewel of the twice-born dances in kirtana in the bliss of prema.[^55]

### 31. The Death of Lakshmi

*Girijashankar Raychaudhuri, 1946*

**1.** A black snake bit her on the little toe of her right foot.

**2.** "I am dying of the burning poison, mother; I can see nothing with my eyes."

**3.** The sky fell in on everyone's heads.

**4.** Kissing Lakshmi's face, mother Shachi says:
"My poor orphan, Lakshmi, my daughter, where are you going, leaving me?"

**5.** [Lakshmi:] "When my lord went away to Bengal,
he gave me the thread from his shoulder as a keepsake.

**6.** Bring that thread and put it round my neck;
comfort mother Thakurani and take her home.

**7.** Carry me to the antarjala — why delay?
To die at home, away from the Ganga, is a great shame."

**8.** Once she served food to Haridasa Thakura.

**9.** Haridasa lived in the hollow of a banyan tree.

**10.** "The world is transient, mother; Krishna alone is true."

**11.** "Where is Lakshmi, where am I, where is this wealth?
All you see — wealth and the rest — is all calamity.[^56]

**12.** Do not weep, do not weep, mother, do not torment yourself:
I have entrusted you to Gadadhara and Jagadananda."

**13.** Seeing the girl Vishnupriya in her first youth…

**14.** *[= "The Surviving Fragments," fragment 10, couplet 7, second half-line, and couplet 8, second half-line.]*[^57]

### 32. Nityananda in Navadvipa

*Girijashankar Raychaudhuri, 1946*

**1.** With rolling eyes, drunk with the intoxication of varuni.[^58]

**2.** "Everyone says that you are Vishvarupa;
mother suffers greatly in her grief for Vishvarupa.
Quickly, make yourself known."[^59]

**3.** "Ah, my little sons, Nimai and Nitai!
Put on the sacred thread and marry."[^60]

**4.** Then the Lord Gaurachandra became six-armed.

**5.** Near Gaurachandra, Nityananda and Advaita —
in Navadvipa there is not room for half a sesame seed for the mahantas.

**6.** Gauda, Banga, Telenga, Magadha, Utkala —
companions from many lands filled everything.

### 33. Jagai and Madhai

*Sukumar Sen, Bangala Sahityer Itihas, vol. 1, 1940, pp. 197, 341; 1905 ed., p. 56*

**1.** Reciting the Masnavi by heart, there live in the reed-beds
two great sinners — Jagai and Madhai.[^61]

### 34. The Crimes of Jagai and Madhai

*R. D. Banerji, Bangalar Itihas, vol. 2, pp. 237–255; Parishad ed., p. 57*

**1.** The two brothers make no distinction as to whom they lie with,[^62]
without bathing or sandhya — Jagai and Madhai.

**2.** Killing of cows, killing of brahmanas, killing of women — so many!
By force and by fraud they carry off their gurus' wives by the hundred.

**3.** They eat beef and pork and drink liquor;
they do not listen to talk of dharma, do not bathe in the Ganga.

**4.** They kill children, dashing them on a stone slab;
from how many pregnant women they cut out the unborn!

**5.** A sacred thread tied at the neck, a roar like a lion's;
deaf, as it were, to all that is good — a great calamity.[^63]

**6.** From drinking they know neither sunrise nor sunset;
their eyes roll — they are full of bhang.[^64]

**7.** Keeping company with robbers, they set houses on fire;
laying a bamboo on a man's chest, they take all he has.[^65]

### 35. Madhai's Blow; the Brothers' Deliverance

*Girijashankar Raychaudhuri, 1946*

**1.** With robbers they live in forests and wastelands;
people cannot sleep for fear of Jagai and Madhai.

**2.** Madhai struck Nityananda on the head with a potsherd;[^66]
as from a thunderbolt, blood spurted on every side.

**3.** From Nityananda's head the blood runs down his chest;
messengers went and told Gaurachandra everything.

**4.** Nityananda says, "Madhai struck me;
in today's danger Jagai saved me."

**5.** Jagai says, "Forgive the offence, Gaurachandra:
not knowing [who he was], Madhai struck Nityananda."

**6.** To Jagai Gaurachandra gave prema-bhakti;
to Madhai Nityananda gave the name of Hari.

### 36. The Plea of Jagai and Madhai

*Sukumar Sen, Bangala Sahityer Itihas, vol. 1, 1940, p. 342; 1905 ed., p. 57*

**1.** Your name — "Purifier of the Fallen" — is awake [in the world];
the fallen Jagai and Madhai beg for prema-bhakti.

### 37. The Taking On of Sins

*B. B. Majumdar, 1959; 1905 ed., p. 58*

**1.** Jagai and Madhai gave their sins into [his] hand;
and the Lord poured a handful of Ganga water on their heads.

**2.** His face turned black — people look on in fear;
in an instant his face shone forth like a golden moon.[^67]

### 38. The Wedding Rites

*Charuchandra, notes to Kavikankana's poem*

**1.** Then, after gazing at [the stars] Dhruva and Arundhati,
Gaura spent the night in his father-in-law's house.

**2.** [Performing] all that family custom and the Veda prescribe,
performing the kushandika, he took [the bride's] hand.[^68]

## Vairagya-khanda

### 39. A Teaching on Karma

*Sukumar Sen, Bangala Sahityer Itihas, vol. 1, 1940, pp. 342–343; 1905 ed., p. 60*

**1.** Good fortune and misfortune are all the fruit of karma:
the bark of one tree does not cling to another.

**2.** One tree does not bear different fruits;
one tree cannot bear the fruit of another.

**3.** The living being is bound by the thread of time: time makes it act;
the fish of the fathomless waters is caught in the net.

**4.** Children play all the time in the dust;
breaking up their games, they go off home.

**5.** And again those same children play in the dust;
breaking the house of dust, they go off home.

**6.** So it is — how many births and deaths!
Fruitless comings and goings — [such is] the striving of the living being.

**7.** Whom, after long striving, Krishna favours with his grace,
that person obtains a body of action in Krishna's heart.[^69]

### 40. Why He Takes Sannyasa

*Girijashankar Raychaudhuri, 1946*

**1.** The Lord's sannyasa is the triumph of the dharma of his line;[^70]
had he not done it, people would not sing his greatness.

**2.** If the dharma of one's line is not kept, the Veda is reviled;
for that reason Gauranga resolved in his heart to take sannyasa.

**3.** The Lord's birth and deeds are for the teaching of the world.

**4.** "You yourself with your holy lips told everyone:
'Apart from sankirtana there is no dharma in the world.'
And you abandon such sankirtana for renunciation?"

**5.** "Why should the Lord renounce?"

**6.** Gauranga says, "Renunciation is my own dharma;
renunciation is a forest fire, the refuge of great men."[^71]

**7.** "I will not go back into worldly life."

### 41. Gauranga's Renunciation

*D. C. Sen, Vanga-sahitya-parichaya, vol. 2, 1914, pp. 1163–1170*

**1.** He takes neither sandal nor garlands, puts on no clothes;
he sits over the nigamas and weeps all the time.[^72]

**2.** He does not tie up his curly hair, listens to no one;
at dawn, at noon Gaura wanders wherever he will.

**3.** "Hold him, hold him, people of Nadia — where will my Gauranga go?
I adjure you — let someone hold him back!" (Refrain.)[^73]

**4.** Agamas, nigamas, the Gita — books in his left hand;
Gora tied the water-pot at his waist.

**5.** He goes with the gait of a lordly elephant, not looking back;
with her hair loose Shachi runs after him.

**6.** Leaving betel with camphor, the beloved krishna-keli cloth,
golden earrings, necklaces, golden amulets,[^74]

**7.** leaving his bed on the bedstead, he sleeps on the ground;
again and again he cries aloud, "What then, what then?"[^75]

**8.** Gaura does not bathe, does not eat;
he does not adorn his holy body, does not anoint it with oil and unguents.

**9.** Gone are sandhya, tarpana, the worship of the gods;
gone are the repetition of mantras, the honouring of tulasi.

**10.** He whose body always bore fragrant pollen-powder —
how much dear Gadadhara, his delight, jested [with him]![^76]

**11.** Without telling Srinivasa or Murari Gupta,
the Lord, having renounced, went off alone.

**12.** Throwing away the water-pot, loincloth and books,
his mother brought him back and led him home.

**13.** Vishnupriya Thakurani, falling at his feet:
"Where will you go, my lord, leaving me?"

**14.** Seeing Shachi's grief, Malini the Vaishnavi
and the nurse-mother Narayani began to weep.

**15.** With Gauranga's renunciation there is no joy in Navadvipa;
Jayananda says, "You will hang your head for ever."[^77]

### 42. Shachi's Lament

*Girijashankar Raychaudhuri, 1946*

**1.** "Do not go, child, leaving your mother!
How shall I live without seeing you?

**2.** Mishra Purandara is no more in this world — I do not hear his voice;
in my grief for Vishvarupa, child, my soul is burning.

**3.** My bones have wasted away with grief for my daughter-in-law Lakshmi;
what will the people of Navadvipa say to me when they hear?

**4.** My daughter-in-law Vishnupriya will be left unprotected —
her first youth is like a blazing fire.

**5.** Eight daughters and two sons this womb has borne;
by some good fortune Narayana has preserved you, my son.

**6.** Eight daughters died, Vishvarupa became an ascetic;
the mother of such a son as you — such a wretched lot!

**7.** Heed my word — what need of sannyasa?
Dance without ceasing in kirtana, living at home."

**8.** With tales from the itihasas he comforted his mother.[^78]

### 43. Vishnupriya

*Girijashankar Raychaudhuri, 1946*

**1–3.** *[= "Vishnupriya's baromasya" in N. Vasu's article of 1897, stanzas 10 and 11.]*[^79]

**1.** Blessed, blessed is the town of Katwa, where Keshava Bharati [lives],

**2.** Carpenters' work, brick walls, beautiful monasteries;

**3.** Mangoes, jackfruit trees, areca and coconut palms, champaka, tala palms, kadamba;

**4.** Mynas, parrots, chakravakas, parijata, peacocks, geese, kokilas;

**5.** At every house, archways with pitchers, bright flags and banners;

**6.** Perfumes, flowers, incense, lamps — a flower-market goes on without ceasing;
flower-gardens, delightful places, temples of the gods are built.

**7.** All the brahmana pandits are godlike in form, versed in all the shastras;
the town of Katwa is like the city of the gods, in every joy and delight.

**8.** Abode of gods, rishis and munis, [by] the heavenly river — [here,] in the deceptive guise of a sannyasi,

**9.** Brahmana maidens like Indra's vidyadharis live in the town of Katwa;
their beauty and grace enchant the three worlds; when they speak, rubies fall from their lips.

**11.** Curds, honey, ghee, collyrium, gorochana, mirrors, paddy, silver;

**12.** To the east the lovely Indreshvara ghat, to the north the Ganga;

**15.** Meditating on the honey of the lotus feet of Chaitanya and Gadadhara, the lord of his life,
