# Passages from Manuscript Catalogues and Other Finds

*Collected here is Jayananda's verse as quoted in descriptions of manuscripts — in the manuscript reports of the Sahitya-parishat-patrika, in the catalogue of the Bangiya Sahitya Parishad of 1923, in the Calcutta University catalogue of 1928, in the Asiatic Society catalogue of 1941 and in the Visva-Bharati catalogue of manuscripts — and also in Sarada Charan Mitra's articles (Prabasi, 1904), in Sukumar Sen's history of literature and in the anthology Padakalpataru. Only Jayananda's verse is translated; the cataloguers' descriptions and discussions are not given. The passages are arranged by the khandas of the poem. The "Dhruva-charitra," which the catalogues describe as a separate work, is the Dhruva episode of the Vairagya-khanda copied out separately; it is gathered in a section of its own. Manuscript dates are given as the cataloguers give them. Many catalogues were read from poor scans: (?) marks uncertain readings, […] gaps and losses. Lines already translated in "The Surviving Fragments of the Poem," in N. Vasu's article of 1897 or in "Quotations from the Poem in Later Authors" are not repeated here: a cross-reference stands in their place; significant variant readings are given in the notes.*

### 12. Gauranga's Ecstasy and a Sermon on the Vanity of the World

*Panchanan Mandal, Punthi-parichay, vol. 4 (Visva-Bharati, 1980), no. 30, pp. 262–264; Visva-Bharati MS no. 1903, incomplete, "about 180 years old"; fols. 1 verso – 2 verso*[^32]

*[At the beginning: "Sri Sri Durga. Vairagya-khanda."]*

**1.** One day Gaurangachandra was dancing in sankirtana;
what is hard for [even] Brahma to attain he offered to all.[^33]

**2.** With the cry "What is it, what is it?", with a lion's roar,
slapping his arms like a wrestler, he sat down on the throne.[^34]

**3.** "Brother, brother!" he calls again and again:
"Vrindavana, Varanasi (?), the kadamba, Govardhana,

**4.** Keshi-ghata, Vamshivata, the place of the sacrificers' wives,
Bhandira, the Bahula forest, the place of the rasa dance!"[^35]

**5.** "Radha, Radha!" he calls aloud;
raising his arms that reach to his knees, he cries, "What is it, what is it?

**6.** Where is that lord Krishna, where are Nanda and Yashoda?
Where are Shridama, Sudama, Chandravali, Radha?[^36]

**7.** Where is my Mathura, Uddhava, Akrura,
[the elephant] Kuvalaya, Chanura, Mushtika, the asura Kamsa?"

**8.** Saying this, Gauranga, the ocean of mercy, weeps;
and again, crying "What is it, what is it?", he laughs without end.

**9.** "Ho, you people of the world, look at my dance!
Buy and sell at this market of prema of mine!

**10.** Whose father, whose mother, whose husband, whose friend?
The world is like a snake (?): who will meet whom?[^37]

**11.** As a juggler makes a wooden puppet dance,
so the world dances — it is Krishna at play.

**12.** False is wealth, false are people, false is family;
where there is fortune, there is misfortune without end.

**13.** How many births and deaths — I know no count;
the mother becomes the wife, the wife the mother.

**14.** As water on a lotus leaf does not stay still,
so fickle is the living being: it does not remain in one place.

**15–17.** *[= "Quotations from the Poem in Later Authors," passage 39, couplets 1–3.]*[^38]

**18.** Wife is death, son is death, wealth and people are death;
friends, kin, house and door — all is the net of maya.[^39]

**19.** The living being, bewitched by maya, is ever in torment:
for one part of merit, a thousand parts of sin.

**20–21.** *[= "Quotations from the Poem in Later Authors," passage 39, couplets 4–5.]*[^40]

**22.** So it is — how many births and deaths!
Because of maya all this action does not reach its goal.[^41]

**23.** If there is a holy man, what can anything else do?
When there is medicine, what can disease do?

**24.** When there is a wishing-tree, the beggar begs from another:
the sorrow written by the Creator never departs.

**25.** When there is a lion, they put their hope in a dog;
when there is the full moon, what need of the lightning's flash?

**26.** When there is service to Krishna, what is served by serving another?
It is like perishing in well-water when there is the water of the Ganga.

**27.** When there is the touchstone, what need of anything else?
Leaving nectar, [people] eat poison.

**28.** When there is Garuda, they rely on a crow;
leaving the hermitage of a muni, they dwell at the burning-ground.

**29.** Krishna is truth, Krishna is truth, Krishna is above all;
Brahma, Indra, Varuna — all are Krishna's servants.

**30.** He who eats with wife and sons without offering to Krishna
will not attain the divine when he goes to another birth.

**31.** He who eats and gives out food without offering to Krishna
is like […] (?) who will go to take alms.[^42]

**32.** Know it for certain: wealth is a calamity;
[from it comes] the fear of oppression by the king, of fire and of thieves.

**33.** The poor man has no fear in the world;
having become a Vaishnava, [a man] does not hoard wealth.

**34.** Whatever the body of a Vaishnava may require —
all those things then serve the work of Krishna.

**35.** A Vaishnava is born for the deliverance of the world;
apart from the work of Krishna he has no other work."

**36.** Meditating on the two feet of Chaitanya and Gadadhara,
Jayananda composes the Vairagya-khanda in bliss.

### 16. Gauranga Tells Srivasa of His Renunciation

*Panchanan Mandal, Punthi-parichay, vol. 4 (Visva-Bharati, 1980), no. 30, pp. 262–264; Visva-Bharati MS no. 1903; fols. 3 verso – 4 recto*[^49]

**1.** One day, at the end of the night, Gauranga
spoke to Srinivasa Pandita particularly:

**2.** "From this day I have given up the desire for worldly life;
I have given up now Navadvipa and life in a house.

**3.** I have studied and I have taught;
I no longer have any wish to live as a householder.

**4.** Sandal and all the rest that is called pleasure is torment;
the maya of the transient world is like a dream.

**5.** The poison of the serpent of sense-objects burns every moment;
without serving Krishna there is no relief from it."

**6.** Srinivasa says: "Lord, living beings are dying of grief;
in your mercy awaken consciousness in all people.[^50]

**7.** Seeing your renunciation, [people] will gain divine knowledge;
to protect your servants you abide on the earth.

**8.** *[= "Quotations from the Poem in Later Authors," passage 40, couplet 4, lines 1–2.]*[^51]

**9.** And you, leaving sankirtana, take up renunciation!
You have asked me — great is my good fortune.

**10.** Dancing in kirtana at Chandrashekhara's house,
you suddenly revealed the mood of Lakshmi.

**11.** At the festival [we] saw you in the mood of the gopis, in the mood of Rukmini;
you gave the breast to all the Vaishnavas.[^52]

**12.** You gave the breast to all the Vaishnavas —
and they will all die on seeing your renunciation.

**13.** You, Thakura, showed [them] a mother's love for her children too —
why then, Thakura, have you become so cruel?"

**14.** The Lord says: "Thakura Pandita Mahashaya,
I shall not live a worldly life — it is not fitting.[^53]

**15.** Renunciation… — seeing you, I forget everything;
for your pleasure I live in the world.

**16.** What is worldly dharma to me, what need have I of renunciation?
To see Jagannatha in Nilachala is great good fortune.

**17.** Gangadasa, the Vaishnavas of Nadia, Haridasa,
the Acharya, Murari Gupta and Srinivasa[^54] —

**18.** what joy is there in renunciation if I leave them all?
I hold the faces of them all [equal to] Vaikuntha."

**19.** At this time Shachi Thakurani came there
and, taking Gaurachandra on her lap, began to reason with him.

**20.** Gaurachandra says: "Mother, I was born of your womb —
what work should I do, if not to serve Krishna?

**21.** Do not forbid me, mother, give me leave!
Did not Dhruva's mother make [her son] a Vaishnava (?)?[^55]

**22.** Listen, I will tell you the story of Dhruva;
having heard it, command me as you will."

**23.** Hearing these words, Shachi Thakurani weeps;
in every way the jewel Gaura comforted his mother.

**24.** Meditating on the two feet of Chaitanya and Gadadhara,
Jayananda composes the Vairagya-khanda in bliss.

*Sarada Charan Mitra, "Utkale Sri Krishna-Chaitanya," Prabasi, vol. 4 (1311 BS), no. 3 (Asharh), p. 160*[^73]

**1.** The seat of Brahma, the city of Jajpur on the bank of the river Papahara,

**2.** [There are] the ghat of Brahma's shasana, the Dashashvamedha ghat: in Brahma's land [Brahma] performed the ashvamedha;

**3.** Jajpur is a lovely place, where Hari-Varaha abides, near the river Papahara;

**4.** The primal shakti is Viraja, whom Brahma worshipped; to the north-east is the temple of Nabhi-Gaya;
one gains the fruit of all tirthas; remembering [her], one goes to Vaikuntha — on seeing Viraja's face.[^75]

## The Dhruva-charitra (episode of the Vairagya-khanda)

*The catalogues describe the "Dhruva-charitra" as a separate work of Jayananda. But the colophons of its copies name the Vairagya-khanda, and in the Vairagya-khanda itself Gauranga, comforting Shachi, begins to tell the story of Dhruva (passage 16). It is an episode of the Vairagya-khanda, copied out separately as a pala, a narrative for performance. The passages are arranged in the order of the story.*

### The Prahlada-charitra

*The Sahitya-parishat-patrika report (vol. 4, 1304 BS, no. 100, p. 322) describes a manuscript of Jayananda's "Prahlada-charitra": two leaves without beginning or end, by appearance more than a hundred years old. The catalogue quotes no verse from it, and nothing else is known of it. Perhaps, like the "Dhruva-charitra," it is an episode of the poem copied out separately.*
