# Nagendranath Vasu. A Little More about the Poet Jayananda (1898)

*Sahitya-Parishat-Patrika, vol. 5, 1305 B.S. (1898), no. 4, pp. 294–296.*[^1]

*[p. 294]*

After the essay entitled "The Poet Jayananda and the Chaitanya-mangala" was published in last year's Parishat-Patrika, some of our friends became doubtful about the authenticity of the poet Jayananda and his book. And some writers in monthly magazines, raising the subject of Jayananda, cast many a sharp glance at us, and at the same time arranged for sentence to be passed on us at a court of judgment.[^2] We believe that they said all this with good intentions; had it not been so, we might perhaps have felt no inclination to write anything more about the poet Jayananda.

Efforts have begun to search out old Bengali manuscripts. Every day we learn of so many old Bengali poets and of hundreds of old Bengali books. Short descriptions of all these manuscripts are being published in due order in the Parishat-Patrika. It is from them that we have learned of several more books composed by the poet Jayananda. Besides the Sri Chaitanya-mangala, he composed the Dhruva-charitra, the Prahlada-charitra and several other books in Bengali. It has also come to light that the Chaitanya-mangala is not in our hands alone: it exists elsewhere too. We have collected from the Vishnupura region several more incomplete manuscripts of Jayananda's Sri Chaitanya-mangala, and have also found some evidence that our poet was particularly honoured and well known in the Gaudiya Vaishnava community. Today we must say a word or two, very briefly, on this subject.

In many places of his Sri Chaitanya-mangala Jayananda speaks of himself:

> "Bearing on my head the command of Gadadhara Pandita Gosani,
> I spread a song — a little of the Sri Chaitanya-mangala."

Now we learn from other authoritative books as well that he belonged to the branch of Gadadhara Pandita himself. Thus, in the Shakha-nirnayamrita composed by Sri Yadunatha Dasa:

> "I honour the great Jayananda, called Chaitanya-dasa,
> by whom the Sri Chaitanya-vilasa was made known with care. (57)
>
> *[p. 295]*
>
> I honour Sri Hridayananda, ever immersed in the rasa of love,
> whose body is the very form of Gaura, the wonder of mahabhava."[^3] (58)[^v1]

The great Vaishnava Yadunatha mentions Jayananda and the book composed by him called the Sri Chaitanya-vilasa. There is no particular reason to doubt that this Jayananda, author of the Sri Chaitanya-vilasa, and Jayananda, composer of the Sri Chaitanya-mangala, are one and the same person.

Many have heard of the Chaitanya-mangala of the famous Lochana Dasa. In two manuscripts of Lochana Dasa's Chaitanya-mangala collected by us, the title written instead of Chaitanya-mangala is Chaitanya-prema-vilasa or Chaitanya-vilasa. In the same way the Govinda-lilamrita of the famous Yadunandana Dasa is known under the title Govinda-vilasa in an old manuscript, two hundred and fifty years of age, collected by us.[^4] This being so, just as the Sri Chaitanya-vilasa and the Sri Chaitanya-mangala are one and the same book, there is no longer any particular objection to accepting as one person the Jayananda belonging to the branch of Gadadhara Pandita described by Yadunatha Dasa and the Jayananda who received Gadadhara's command described in the Chaitanya-mangala published by us.

We earlier mentioned the name of a relative of Jayananda's, Indriyananda Kavindra.[^v2] Now, in another old manuscript of the Sri Chaitanya-mangala collected from Vishnupura, we see the reading "Hridayananda" in place of "Indriyananda". This reading, Hridayananda, appears to be the true one: for in the kula-panjika[^5] of the Radhi brahmans collected from the Rarh region too, the genealogy of a man of the Bandyaghati line named Hridayananda follows the genealogy of Vaninatha, a close relative of Jayananda's. From the Sri Chaitanya-mangala we have learned that this Vaninatha and Jayananda's father Subuddhi Mishra were born of one line.[^v3][^v4] The foremost Vaishnava Sri Krishnadasa Kaviraja too, in describing the principal branches in his Chaitanya-charitamrita, mentions Subuddhi Mishra and Hridayananda together.[^6] We believe that the Hridayananda whom Sri Yadunatha presents immediately after Jayananda in the Sanskrit shlokas quoted above is the very one who is mentioned in Jayananda's Chaitanya-mangala and *[p. 296]* in the Chaitanya-charitamrita.[^7] In Krishnadasa's Svarupa-varnana[^8] too Jayananda's father Subuddhi Mishra is mentioned:

> "Graceful, of strong body, Subalita by name —
> her svarupa is the renowned Subuddhi Mishra."
> (Svarupa-varnana)

*Sri Nagendranath Vasu*
