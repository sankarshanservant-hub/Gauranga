# Madhya-khanda, part 22 (portion 45)

*The section "Meeting with Sarvabhauma" (end): Kapoteshvara and the Bhargavi; the boy with the flute above the temple; Jagannatha stretches out His hands to the Lord; the Lord at Sarvabhauma's house and the first darshana of Jagannatha; mahaprasada and its glory; Sarvabhauma censures the Lord and offers to teach Him Vedanta; the Lord reveals to him the essence of Vedanta and shows the six-armed form; the author's word; the end of the Madhya-khanda*

*Disha: "Sing the sweet name of Sri Krishna Chaitanya — Gorachand! (murchha) Brothers, there is no other way to be saved: this story is rare in the world. As long as you live in the world, drink it, filling your ears; never give up the songs of His virtues!"*[^1]

**112.** Now hear again of the deeds of Gorachand:
the Lord pours down a rain of prema — new nectar.

**113.** The Lord walks along the road with His own people
and with great joy sees Kapoteshvara.

**114.** Bowing to him, the Lord walks on,
looking at holy places and great lingas.

**115.** Then comes a river named Bhargavi, the blessed;
in it He bathed together with His own people.

**116.** Having finished His bath, the Lord walks on
and suddenly saw the temple of Jagannatha.[^2]

**117.** The temple shines, outshining the rays of the moon,
and on it a red flag flutters in the wind.

**118.** In the midst of the Blue Hill is the beautiful temple of Hari:
wondrously white, it outshines Kailasa with its brilliance.

**119.** A boy dark as collyrium
the Lord plainly sees above the temple.

**120.** With His hand holding a cloth He beckons Him again and again;
seeing this, the Lord, rapt, bows to Him.

**121.** The Lord fell to the ground unconscious,
lying silent, as if life had left Him.

**122.** Seeing this, all the people were stricken at heart;
they call, "Lord! Lord!" — He does not answer.

**123.** "What has happened? What has happened?" they wonder in alarm,
and no sound comes from them: as if alive, yet dead.

**124.** At that very moment the Lord quickly rose,
His whole body thrilling, intoxicated with prema.

**125.** Seeing this, all the people came back to life —
as if life had returned to a dead body.

**126.** Mahaprabhu asks them,
"Do you see anything with your eyes above the temple?

**127.** Radiant as the rays of a blue gem,
a beautiful boy who enchants the three worlds?"

**128.** Seeing nothing, they say, "We see,"
fearing that He might faint again.

**129.** Again the Lord says to them,
"Look: on the temple's banner — a beautiful boy!

**130.** His face is gracious, His form like full nectar;
wondrously the fingers of His palms are moving.

**131.** He beckons Me with the beauty of His lotus hand;
in His left hand is a flute, the blessing of the three worlds."[^3]

**132.** Saying this, the Lord hurried forward,
and all the Vaishnavas follow joyfully.

**133.** The radiance of Gaura's body outshines millions of moons;
His long tilaka of sandal paste gleams.

**134.** On Gora's body the reddish cloth glows bright;
its colour outshines the morning sun.

**135.** Seeing the temple of Jagannatha, Gora Raya
bows again and again and walks on.

**136.** Tears flow from His eyes without cease,
and the hair on His whole body stands on end.

**137.** Overwhelmed with prema, with an eager heart,
the Lord came to the great tirtha, the lake of Markandeya.

**138.** Having bathed and given gifts as custom prescribes,
He bowed down and hurried on.

**139.** Bowing to Yajneshvara, full of joy,
with eagerness in His heart He walks on quickly.

**140.** Seeing again the temple of Jagannatha,
He bows again, falling to the ground.

**141.** From both His eyes tears flow without cease;
rapt, He weeps in deep longing.

**142.** Seeing such longing in Gorachand,
Jagannatha appeared to Him, stretching out His hands.[^4]

**143.** "Come, come!" calls the King of the three worlds;
seeing this, the Lord, rapt, rolls on the ground.

**144.** Laughing with joy, He said,
"Be merciful, Jagannatha — I have seen Your feet!"

**145.** When He no longer sees [Him], He weeps again,
and seeing [Him] again, He is filled with exultation.

**146.** Nothing but wild surges of prema, His body thrilling;
with a thunderous roar the waves of the nectar of prema rise.

**147.** Then the Lord went on in the same haste
and came to the house of Vasudeva Sarvabhauma.

**148.** Sarvabhauma, joyfully seeing the Lord,
offered Him a seat as householders' custom requires.

**148+1.** He bowed, saying "Namo narayanaya";
[the Lord replied,] "May your mind soon turn to Radha and Krishna."[^5]

**148+2.** Hearing the Lord's words of blessing, the Bhattacharya
understood: this is a Vaishnava sannyasi, a great acharya.

**149.** The Lord said to Sarvabhauma,
"I long to see Jagannatha.

**150.** How shall I see the Lord, the God of gods?
To stand before Him — My heart is in awe."

**151.** Hearing this, the venerable Sarvabhauma
gazes in wonder at the Lord's form:

**152.** "He is golden like molten gold, beautiful as Sumeru;
His face, a moon with lovely eyes, is shining.

**153.** A lion's neck, a conch-like throat, long eyes,
arms reaching to the knees — all auspicious marks."

**153+1.** In bright prema for Krishna He is overcome with longing,
thrilling all over, His body sways.[^6]

**154.** Seeing this, Sarvabhauma Bhattacharya, overwhelmed,
began to ponder, seeing all these wonders:

**155.** "There is no such man in all the world;
not even among the gods can I place Him.

**156.** The Lord of Vaikuntha has come Himself:
this is that Bhagavan — so I conclude."

**157.** Thinking thus, the great Sarvabhauma,
seeing his son, says to him:[^7]

**158.** "Go quickly with Chaitanya;
listen carefully to what this great soul says.

**159.** Where Sri Jagannatha Mahaprabhu dwells —
take Him with His companions there, to Him."

**160.** Hearing this, Gora Raya was pleased
and went, guided by Sarvabhauma's son.

**161.** At the Lion Gate the Lord's body swayed:
rapt in prema, He cannot keep on His feet.

**162.** He cannot walk steadily, His limbs have gone limp;
all the companions walk carefully close beside Him.

**163.** With great effort He entered the Lion Gate
and at once hurried up into the Nata-mandira.

**164.** Standing behind Garuda, He gazes intently —
and saw the moon-face of the King of the three worlds.[^8]

**165.** His heart leapt up, filled with bliss;
His body was covered with thrills, like a kadamba blossom.

**166.** Five, seven streams of tears flow from His eyes;
He forgot Himself, overpowered by the bliss of prema.

**167.** The Lord fell to the ground, His body helpless —
as if the wind had toppled a peak of Sumeru.

**168.** In the rapture of prema Bhagavan fainted:
both hands clenched tight in fists, eyes closed.

**168+1.** His cloth slipped on His senseless body;
seeing this, the brahmanas went out of the temple.

**168+2.** Leaving His throne, Lord Jagannatha lifted [Him] up;
at each other's touch both were filled with delight.

**168+3.** Putting His arms around [Him], He at once embraced Him,
and [the Lord] dances before Jagannatha, crying "Haribol!"

**168+4.** At Gauranga's touch Jagannatha, intoxicated with prema,
seated Gora upon His throne.[^9]

**169.** The son of Shachi dances, crying "Hari!" —
and then all entered the temple.

**170.** Gadadhara dances, and Narahari, Nityananda,
Srinivasa, Damodara, Murari, Mukunda;

**171.** all the other devotees too dance in joy,
making kirtana, singing the virtues of Radha and Kanu.

**172.** Then all the companions, after deliberating,
brought the Lord to Sarvabhauma's house.[^10]

**173.** In Sarvabhauma's house the Lord came to Himself
and began to dance again in the kirtana of [Krishna's] virtues.

**174.** Seeing this, Sarvabhauma Vasudeva Bhattacharya
felt great delight in his heart, seeing this wonder.

**175.** When Mahaprabhu's dancing was over,
Sarvabhauma invited Him to accept alms.

**176.** He sent brahmanas to bring prasada,
and he, Sarvabhauma, converses with the Lord.

**177.** He holds a friendly conversation to learn His learning
and began to ask the Lord something about Himself:

**178.** "Where were You born? Tell me."
The Lord says, "What you have said is true."

**179.** The Bhattacharya says, "What are You saying?
I say one thing and You another — why?"

**180.** The Lord stays silent, deep as the ocean;
again the grave brahmana asks the Lord:

**181.** "Who are Your mother and father? Tell me."
The Lord says, "What you have said is true."

**182.** The Bhattacharya asks yet again,
"Tell me, where did You take sannyasa?"

**183.** The Lord says, "Know for certain: this is true."
Hearing this, Sarvabhauma was greatly amazed.

**184.** He could not at all understand the Lord's resolve —
the Lord of myriad Sarasvatis, the victory of all.[^11]

**185.** "Is He God — or of a madman's nature?"
Only vexation and anger fell to his lot.

**186.** The Bhattacharya had much prasada brought;
at the sight of prasada the madness of prema arose.

**187.** Receiving the mahaprasada — Jagannatha's rice —
the Lord, laughing, reverently raised it to His head.

**188.** He gave a roar in a deep voice —
that lion's roar of the Lord filled the universe.

**189.** Gods, gandharvas, men, jackals, dogs
and all the clans of the nagas came to Gauranga.

**190.** [He] joyfully put that prasada into all their mouths —
Gadadhara and the others and Prabhu Nityananda saw it.

**191.** No one said anything: all knew the truth.
All received prasada together with the devotees.

**192.** He ate a little with His own people;
then Srinivasa spoke:

**193.** "I have a request, Lord, but I am afraid to say it;
I shall ask without fear, if I have Your leave.

**194.** When You received the prasada and laughed,
I was startled to see it. Explain this to me."

**195.** Hearing this, the Lord, in great joy,
speaks, revealing what is hidden:

**196.** "By Katyayani's vow, such a treasure as prasada
is eaten by jackals and dogs — listen, brahmana.[^12]

**197.** Indra, Chandra, the gandharvas or the gods —
for all it is a rarity: not to be had even by effort.

**198.** Narada, Prahlada, Shuka and the other devotees —
even for them it is rare: I have told you the secret.

**199.** Such mahaprasada all are eating —
that is the secret I have told you, what was in My heart.

**200.** Whoever, having received such mahaprasada,
takes it for ordinary food or does not eat it —

**201.** even the dharma he gathered in former births perishes,
and he is born from the womb of a sow.

**202.** Even if [prasada] falls from a dog's mouth,
eat it as soon as you get it — there is no fault in this at all."[^13]

**203.** Then Mahaprabhu reverently accepted the alms
and in the evening goes to see Jagannatha.

**204.** Entering the temple, He sees the beautiful face;
the universe cannot contain His inner delight.

**204+1.** Incense, lamps, lovely flowers, enchanting fragrance
the brahmana offered, seeing [His] bliss.

**204+2.** Seeing the dazzling splendour of [Jagannatha's] body —
as if moons by the hundred thousand had gathered in one place —[^14]

**205.** the radiance of His body, outshining a fresh cloud,
and in it two wondrous lotus eyes,

**206.** the Thakura plunged into an ocean of bliss;
He rolls on the ground, His prema grew immensely.

**207.** His long body, like Mount Sumeru,
rolls on the ground, unable to contain the bliss.

**208.** By Gauranga's radiance Jagannatha became golden,
His body filled with bhava, utterly rapt.

**209.** Balarama too became golden, and the pandas;
the bodies of all were filled with bhava.

**210.** They raised Gauranga up, and a panda performed arati:
beside the unmoving Brahman — the moving form.[^15]

**211.** Jagannatha appeared in the form of a sannyasi —
such a wonder no one had ever seen.

**212.** After a while He came to Himself
and returned to His lodging with His own people.

**213.** Thus, seeing Jagannatha three times [a day],
He knows neither day nor night — an ocean of bliss.

**214.** Thus the Lord, expert in prema, spends some days
joyfully with His own people.

**215.** Now hear carefully the story
of how He first revealed Himself at Purushottama.

**216.** The Lord teaches the people, having become destitute;
not understanding this, fools take Him for a man.

**217.** By the sea Gaura Raya made Himself a garden
and there with His own people sings His own virtues.[^16]

**218.** Sarvabhauma, his mind deluded by learning,
said something mistaken about the Lord behind His back.

**219.** In an assembly full of brahmanas and worthy men,
that brahmana spoke what was in his heart:

**220.** "This sannyasi is of a noble family, a great scholar;
but it is not proper to take sannyasa at a young age.

**221.** At this time of life the dharma of sannyasa is unfitting;
not understanding this, the brahmana has done such a thing.

**222.** Let Him undergo His purificatory rites again,
learn Vedanta and keep the rule of an ashrama.

**223.** Kirtana and dancing are not a sannyasi's dharma;
let Him hear Vedanta from me."[^17]

**223+1.** "As many times as Jagannatha eats,
so many times this sannyasi eats too.

**223+2.** One who eats so much in youth —
how can his lust ever subside?

**223+3.** He remembers home — and weeps, 'Radha!'
To his misfortune the sannyasi has fallen into the noose of sannyasa."

**223+4.** Meanwhile Gorachand was with His own people
in talks about Krishna, in conversation on prema.[^18]

**224.** Suddenly Gora Panhu smiled faintly —
as if honey had poured down in unbroken streams.

**225.** Knowing everything, the Lord went there
where Sarvabhauma sits teaching Vedanta.

**226.** He came there with His own people;
seeing Him, the Bhattacharya rose in alarm.

**227.** He offered Him a seat with respectful words,
and the Thakura asks him for guidance: "What should I do?

**228.** You, Sarvabhauma Bhattacharya, know everything;
I ask you from My heart — tell Me the rule.

**229.** I do not understand the dharma of the sannyasa ashrama;
I have taken sannyasa — you judge what the law requires.

**230.** You know all truths, you expound Vedanta —
what is the rule? Teach Me now.

**231.** At a young age the dharma of sannyasa is not proper:
what is enjoined — to undergo the sacred-thread rite again?

**231+1.** Jagannatha's prasada has made Me drunk,
and in youth I cannot quell My lust.

**231+2.** I remember home — and weep, saying 'Radha!' —
that is why in the midst of kirtana I am beside Myself."[^19]

**232.** Hearing this, Sarvabhauma Bhattacharya
was abashed at heart and wonders:

**233.** "Only just now I said this with my disciples —
how did this sannyasi come to know all this?"

**234.** Guessing [the truth], tormented by shame,
he said nothing and remained in wonder.

**235.** The next day the Lord went with His own people
to Sarvabhauma's house to see him.

**236.** Sarvabhauma, sitting at home, is teaching Vedanta;
the Lord, smiling, asks about the conclusions of Vedanta.

**237.** The Thakura asked about the hidden meaning of Vedanta —
about refuge at the feet of Krishna, the sprout of nectar.[^20]

**237+1.** "The Veda declares that Brahman has a form like a man's —
yet you do not accept this, relying on your own mind.

**237+2.** Brahma's word in the Brahma-samhita says:
He is eternal being, consciousness and bliss, full of supreme majesty;

**237+3.** His body is made of rasa, His form is dark;
the other avataras are portions — Krishna is the complete, the highest.

**237+4.** Vyasa declared this in the Bhagavata —
yet you reject it and teach another doctrine.

**237+5.** The Varaha-samhita says: Radha is the complete truth;
all other prakritis are the radiance of Her toenails.

**237+6.** In the Gautamiya-tantra and the Sanatkumara-samhita
the truth of Radha is set forth.

**237+7.** The sage Vyasa wrote down the meaning of the Veda in the shastras:
what do you gain by reviling Vyasa?

**237+8.** The abode of Vrindavana is Krishna's land of chintamani;
there Krishna sports with His beloveds.

**237+9.** The crown of the beloveds is Radha, the great goddess;
the great truth is Lord Krishna, realized in the Vedas.

**237+10.** The kirtana of the two is sung by all the gopis —
and you revile that kirtana: you are base.

**237+11.** The Bhagavata speaks of the glory of kirtana:
the killing of a brahmana and other sins — all are destroyed.

**237+12.** So the name destroys mountains of sin,
and then [one] attains Krishna: the name is called chintamani.

**237+13.** Taste prasada, and millions of sins are destroyed —
yet you call it the display of greed, delusion and lust!

**237+14.** The glory of the Vaishnavas is proved by all the shastras;
you do not accept the shastras — what knowledge of the shastras do you have?"[^21]

**238.** Hearing this, Sarvabhauma was amazed at heart:
he understood that the son of Shachi is not a man.

**239.** Shame tormented him, fear was in his heart:
"In all these years I have never heard such an essence!

**240.** All that I have read and heard through so many years
I have taught my disciples with pride.

**241.** And now I have heard this conclusion of Vedanta:
this Mahaprabhu is the Lord of Sarasvati Himself."

**242.** Concluding thus, Sarvabhauma, king of brahmanas,
with joined palms offers praises, seeing this.

**243.** At that very moment the Lord [showed] a six-armed form;
seeing it, Sarvabhauma lost all composure in bliss.

**244.** In the upper two hands He holds bow and arrow,
in the middle two the flute at His lips,

**245.** in the lower two the staff and the kamandalu.
Seeing this, Sarvabhauma was beside himself with bliss.[^22]

**246.** Fallen at His feet, he weeps in great humility;
Sarvabhauma offers praises in a choked voice.

**247.** In a choked voice he recites a thousand praises —
people know them as the "Thousand Names of Chaitanya."[^23]

**247+1.** "Glory to Raghuvira, Yaduvira, the Great One!
Glory to the hero among brahmanas, the Lion Gaura, the refuge of all!

**247+2.** Drunk with the pride of learning I reviled You —
now I have sold myself to Your feet, which grant fearlessness.

**247+3.** Forgive my offence! Glory to Gaurahari!
You are supremely merciful to all."

**247+4.** Gaura, the great lion, showed mercy to Sarvabhauma;
the bliss of all the devotees grew — great bees.[^24]

**248.** Rapt, he falls at the lotus feet.
Lochana tells how [the Lord] revealed Himself to Sarvabhauma.

**249.** Thus the Lord stays in bliss and delight,
and the people of Nilachala behold Him with joy.

**250.** The glory of Jagannatha shone forth more than before;
in everyone's heart happiness touches the sky.

**251.** Who knows how to tell the story of Chaitanya?
I cannot hold back — I tell a little with my lips.

**252.** Sri Murari Gupta the vaidya, blessed in the three worlds —
Pandita Sri Damodara asked him;

**253.** Murari Gupta told it in verses —
what I have heard is by the grace of them both.[^25]

**254.** Hearing it, my heart was stirred with longing for its sweetness,
and, not seeing my own faults, my mind was enraptured.

**255.** What I have told according to my own understanding
I, a wretched fool, tell in a sung panchali.

**256.** The Sutra-khanda, Adi-khanda and Madhya-khanda are ended;
the Shesha-khanda remains — I shall tell it in the story.

**257.** The story of Chaitanya's deeds is the manifestation of Chaitanya.
"The Madhya-khanda is ended," says this Lochana Dasa.

*Thus ends the Madhya-khanda of the Sri Chaitanya-mangala, composed by Sri Lochana Dasa Thakura.*[^26]

[^1]: The name of the raga is illegible in all the editions. The 1983 edition begins new numbering here (१).
[^2]: Vv. 113–146 — cf. Murari 3.10. Kapoteshvara (the Kapota-linga) is a shrine of Shiva on the way from Bhubaneshvara to Puri; the Bhargavi is a river near Puri.
[^3]: As in Murari (3.10): above the temple Krishna appears to the Lord as a boy with a flute; His companions do not see Him.
[^4]: Cf. Murari 3.10.17–18: Jagannatha mercifully shows the Lord His hand.
[^5]: Vv. 148+1 — 148+2 are found only in the 1983 edition; the 1903 edition gives them only in a footnote, as an additional reading of one manuscript. Cf. the Chaitanya-charitamrita (Madhya 6): Sarvabhauma greets the Lord with "namo narayanaya," and the Lord blesses him: "May your mind be fixed on Krishna." The 1983 edition reads v. 147: "Overwhelmed with prema, with an eager heart, the Lord came to the house of Vasudeva Sarvabhauma."
[^6]: V. 153+1 is found only in the 1983 edition; the 1903 edition gives it only in a footnote, as an additional reading of two manuscripts.
[^7]: In Murari (3.11) it is Sarvabhauma's younger brother who goes with the Lord to the temple; one of the manuscripts of the 1903 edition also reads "younger brother." According to the Chaitanya-charitamrita (Madhya 6), Sarvabhauma himself found the Lord unconscious in the temple and brought Him to his house.
[^8]: The Garuda pillar stands in the Nata-mandira (the dancing hall) facing Jagannatha; according to tradition, the Lord always beheld Jagannatha standing behind it.
[^9]: Vv. 168+1 — 168+4 are found only in the 1983 edition and in the 1903 edition (in the main text, in brackets); Bhaktisiddhanta's edition does not have them. The other biographies do not tell this: according to the Chaitanya-charitamrita (Madhya 6), the Lord rushed to embrace Jagannatha and fell unconscious.
[^10]: So too in Murari (3.11) and the Chaitanya-charitamrita (Madhya 6): the unconscious Lord was carried to Sarvabhauma's house.
[^11]: Sarasvati is the goddess of learning and speech; the Lord is the Master of all knowledge (en/17, note 1). The Lord evades questions about Himself, concealing who He is.
[^12]: On the vow of Katyayani (Parvati), by which Vishnu's mahaprasada goes to all — see the Sutra-khanda (en/04, vv. 232–272).
[^13]: A hyperbole glorifying mahaprasada: nothing can defile its purity. On the glory of mahaprasada cf. Murari 3.11.
[^14]: Vv. 204+1 — 204+2 are found only in the 1983 edition and in the 1903 edition; Bhaktisiddhanta's edition does not have them.
[^15]: "The unmoving Brahman" is Jagannatha, Daru-brahma (the Supreme in a form of wood), not the impersonal Brahman; Gauranga is the same Jagannatha, but moving. Balarama is Baladeva, Jagannatha's elder brother on the altar; the pandas are the temple priests.
[^16]: That is, Krishna's virtues: He Himself is Krishna. The garden (tota) by the sea is apparently the later Tota-Gopinatha.
[^17]: Sarvabhauma Bhattacharya, the greatest master of logic and Vedanta of his time, then held the doctrine of advaita (mayavada): for him sannyasa was renunciation for the study of Vedanta, and kirtana and dancing were unworthy of a sannyasi. Lochana Dasa plainly calls this the error of a mind "deluded by learning" (v. 218). Cf. Murari 3.12 (Sarvabhauma wants to perform the sacred-thread rite on the Lord anew and teach Him Vedanta) and the Chaitanya-charitamrita (Madhya 6). Sarvabhauma's conversion from dry knowledge to bhakti is the heart of this lila.
[^18]: Vv. 223+1 — 223+4 are found only in the 1983 edition and in the 1903 edition; Bhaktisiddhanta's edition does not have them. This is the slander of the deluded Sarvabhauma, a worldly reading of what is beyond the world: the Lord, Krishna Himself in the mood of Radha, weeps in separation from Krishna; the taking of mahaprasada is not eating for the body. Later Sarvabhauma repents (v. 247+2).
[^19]: Vv. 231+1 — 231+2 are found only in the 1983 edition; the 1903 edition gives them only in a footnote, as an additional reading of one manuscript. The Lord ironically repeats Sarvabhauma's words, showing that He knows them.
[^20]: As in Murari (3.12): the true, "hidden" meaning of Vedanta is refuge at the feet of Krishna. According to the Chaitanya-charitamrita (Madhya 6), the Lord, after listening silently for seven days to Sarvabhauma's exposition, explained the Vedanta-sutra in its direct sense, rejecting the impersonal interpretation, and revealed the meaning of the atmarama verse.
[^21]: Vv. 237+1 — 237+14 are found only in the 1983 edition; the 1903 edition gives them only in a footnote, as an additional reading of one manuscript. Their content agrees with Gaudiya Vaishnava teaching, but they are apparently late: according to the Chaitanya-charitamrita (Madhya 9), the Lord found the Brahma-samhita later, on His pilgrimage in the South. "The name is chintamani" — cf. the Padma Purana: "The name of Krishna is chintamani, Krishna Himself." Chintamani is the wish-fulfilling gem.
[^22]: The bow and arrow are Rama's, the flute Krishna's, the staff and kamandalu those of Gaura the sannyasi. Lochana Dasa has described the six-armed form before (en/28, v. 101). According to the Chaitanya-charitamrita (Madhya 6), the Lord showed Sarvabhauma first a four-armed form and then the two-armed form of Krishna with the flute; the six-armed form shown to Sarvabhauma is honoured by tradition.
[^23]: A hundred verses in praise of the Lord (the Chaitanya-shataka) are ascribed to Sarvabhauma; two of them are quoted in the Chaitanya-charitamrita (Madhya 6.254–255; cf. Murari 3.12.17–18).
[^24]: Vv. 247+1 — 247+4 are found only in the 1983 edition; the 1903 edition gives them only in a footnote, as an additional reading of one manuscript.
[^25]: Lochana Dasa names his source: the Sanskrit poem of Murari Gupta, framed as Murari's narration to Damodara Pandita.
[^26]: In the 1983 edition chapter 16 and the Madhya-khanda end here.
