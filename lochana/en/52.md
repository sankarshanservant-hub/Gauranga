# Shesha-khanda, part 7 (portion 52)

*The section "The Final Pastimes": the poor brahmana from Dravida and Vibhishana — the end of the text in Bhaktisiddhanta's edition. Chapter 4 (only in the 1983 edition): the concealment of the Lord — He enters into Jagannatha; the devotees' grief; Lochana Dasa's closing word about himself*

## The Final Pastimes (Shesha-lila)

> *Summary by Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati.* Shortly before Sriman Mahaprabhu withdrew [from mundane sight], a poor brahmana from Dravida, tormented by want, came to Purushottama and fasted seven days to obtain the mercy of Sri Jagannatha-deva; not obtaining it, he resolved to give up his life in the ocean. Then, on the ocean shore, by providence he met Vibhishana, and Vibhishana, instructing him: "A living being experiences joy and sorrow according to the fruits of his karma; therefore one should worship Jagannatha-deva, remaining indifferent to joy and sorrow," went on his way; but the brahmana did not leave him and together with Vibhishana came to Sriman Mahaprabhu. [Mahaprabhu, having granted him wealth through Vibhishana,] instructed him in [the service of] Krishna and showed him mercy.

*Raga varadi*

**1.** I shall now tell another wondrous account:
the tale of Gaurachandra's glories is ever new[^1].

**2.** I shall tell something hidden — listen with one mind:
in the minds of low people there is no belief [in it],

**3.** but in the hearts of Vaishnavas — supreme joy:
it is a most hidden manifestation of Gaurachandra.

**4.** In Dravida there was a brahmana named Rama,
utterly wretched: on his body only bones and skin.

**5.** Wasted by hunger, he is burnt by the fire of hunger;
there is no blood or flesh in his withered body.

**6.** How much can the cruel misery of poverty be borne?
The brahmana ponders within on a means of deliverance:

**7.** "In a former birth I did much wrong —
through all those deeds I have become poor.

**8.** Without undergoing [the fruits], the writing of fate is not erased —
how then can this cruel torment and misery end?"

**9.** Pondering and pondering, the brahmana found a remedy:
"Except the Lord, no one can remove karma.

**10.** Jagannatha is present in person in Nilachala —
I will go to Him to beg.

**11.** I, in a brahmana's body, am dying of hunger —
and all the wise call Him fond of brahmanas.

**12.** If for my fault He pays me no heed,
the [sin of] killing [a brahmana] will be on Him — I shall give up my life"[^2].

**13.** Reasoning thus, the brahmana set out
and step by step came to where the Lotus-eyed One is.

**14.** Seeing Jagannatha, he makes his plea:
"I, a poor brahmana, am dying of hunger.

**15.** There is no one but You — save my life:
remove the burning of poverty, give me wealth."

**16.** Saying this, he remained all that day in that mind
and ate what he received as alms.

**17.** On the next day he pleads again:
"Remove my poverty, Lord — a brahmana is dying!

**18.** Give me wealth in abundance,
that I may not know this misery for the rest of my life.

**19.** I beg the boon of wealth, Lord — do not turn away,
or else I will give up my life before You."

**20.** Saying this, he began an unbroken fast.
And here, among His own, is Lord Gaurachandra.

**21.** With His own He sings the glories of Vrindavana —
and suddenly sorrow rises in the Lord's heart.

**22.** He remains in astonishment — His heart has become otherwise;
the rasa He was in He left off.

**23.** In the hearts of all — sorrow and wonder:
why has the Lord suddenly become absent-minded?

**24.** Meanwhile the brahmana fasted three days —
and receives no word from Jagannatha.

**25.** Then the brahmana fasted seven days —
Jagannatha-deva gives him no comfort.

**26.** The brahmana grew weak, wasted by fasting,
and at last resolved: "I shall die in the ocean."

**27.** Slowly the brahmana went to the ocean shore
and, bowing to the ocean, says: "Give me a place."

**28.** At that moment he sees a huge person,
the size of a mountain, coming through the midst of the ocean.

**29.** Seeing this, the brahmana began to ponder:
"Who is this coming through the midst of the ocean?"

**29+1.** In the midst of the ocean the water is knee-deep for him —
seeing all this, the brahmana reflects within[^3].

**30.** Even as he looked, he sees that person on the shore:
he became like an ordinary man.

**31.** The brahmana says: "This is Jagannatha Himself in person!
Who else could pass through the midst of the ocean?"

**32.** Saying so, he runs after him;
after going some way, the great one looks back

**33.** and sees that brahmana following him.
"Where are you going?" he asks the brahmana.

**34.** The brahmana says: "Listen, listen, great one!
Who are you, where are you going? Tell me truly.

**35.** I am a weakened brahmana; I have fasted seven days;
today I have seen you — my life is fulfilled.

**36.** Tell me for certain, do not deceive me,
or else the [sin of] killing a brahmana will be on you."

**37.** Hearing this, the great one says:
"What need have you to strive to know me?

**38.** Whoever I may be — what concern is it of yours?
Why do you die fasting with a desperate heart?"

**39.** The brahmana says: "The fever of misery and poverty
has wasted my whole body.

**40.** A brahmana's duties wretched me cannot keep:
day and night pass in wailing for food.

**41.** In my own family I find no regard anywhere;
I know no place where I am not insulted.

**42.** I love death more than life —
I tell you: that is why I am dying by fasting."

**43.** Hearing this, the great one's heart melted:
"My name is Vibhishana; listen, brahmana.

**44.** I am going to see Jagannatha's feet.
You suffer through the fault of your karma; listen, brahmana.

**45.** People bound in the fetters of karma receive joy and sorrow;
only by undergoing them is that karma, pious and sinful, ended.

**46.** Look with love on Jagannatha's face,
that in another birth too misery may not befall you."

**47.** Saying this, King Vibhishana went on,
yet the poor brahmana still follows behind him.

**48.** Gorachand is sitting among His own;
He says to Govinda: "See who is at the door."

**49.** At the door stands King Vibhishana;
seeing the brahmana, he put a finger to his nose[^4].

**50.** At that moment Govinda went to the garden gate
and saw at the gate two young brahmanas.

**51.** Seeing them, Govinda went to the Lord,
but before he could say anything, [the Lord] calls the two brahmanas.

**52.** "Come in, come in!" the Thakura greets them with a smile;
He seated one beside Him, while the other stays at a distance.

**53.** Leaving everything, the Lord converses with him with honour;
all who were near were amazed.

**54.** The Thakura says: "We meet after a long time!" —
and tears of love flow from the eyes of both.

**55.** He touches his body with His hand
and by signs asks: "Are you well?"

**56.** No one else understands the talk of those two.
Gaurachandra says: "This brahmana is very wretched.

**57.** The burning of poverty has robbed him of reason:
he is attacking Jagannatha.

**58.** A living being does not see its own fault at all:
having done wrong itself, it afterwards blames the Lord.

**59.** It does its own good and evil itself —
and when the time comes to reap, it blames the Lord.

**60.** When enjoying happiness, it speaks of its own merit,
but when reaping evil and sorrow, it blames the Lord.

**61.** By a seven-day fast the brahmana chose death —
what else could Jagannatha, fond of brahmanas, do?

**62.** By seeing you his poverty is ended:
give him wealth — let it become an ocean of riches."

**63.** "Good, good," he said and quickly rose;
all who were there were bewildered.

**64.** Bowing down, the two go off;
on the way the brahmana asks Vibhishana:

**65.** "You say: 'I am that King Vibhishana,' —
and now you are leaving, having bowed to a sannyasi.

**66.** Why did you not see Jagannatha-deva?
Tell the truth to a wretched brahmana.

**67.** You placed the sannyasi's order on your head —
tell me, who is this sannyasi? Do not be clever."

**68.** The king says: "Listen, foolish brahmana:
you have seen Jagannatha with your own eyes.

**69.** Your desire is fulfilled — you have received wealth:
I shall bring you the wealth in Dravida."

**70.** Hearing this, the brahmana strikes his head
and in anguish clasps Vibhishana's feet:

**71.** "Let us go again before that Lord!
I am an ignorant brahmana — I tell you."

**72.** [Vibhishana] tried hard but could not get away from him,
and they go back again to the Lord.

**73.** With fear at heart they came before the Lord;
seeing the two again, the Lord laughed.

**74.** The Lord says: "Why have you come back?"
The king says: "Ask the brahmana the reason."

**75.** The brahmana says: "Gosani, I am ignorant.
How many living beings there are — countless millions —

**76.** You are the life of all, You are the Lord of all,
there is no one but You: You are Jagannatha.

**77.** I am the lowest of the low, wretched, a great offender:
through the fault of my karma I have the disease of poverty.

**78.** Tormented by the disease, I long for harmful food:
the medicine is not to my taste; I crave what is harmful.

**79.** Give me the medicine with understanding — You are Dhanvantari:
through the fault of karma, wretched I am dying of the disease of worldly existence."

**80.** Hearing this, the Lord began to laugh:
"Jagannatha-deva has made all well for you.

**81.** First you will now enjoy what you desired,
and at the end you will attain Jagannatha's feet"[^5].

**82.** As this was said, the brahmana prostrates;
all the people around cry: "Hari, Hari!"

**83.** Hear, everyone, this wonderful account:
having received the boon, the poor brahmana went away.

**84.** Joyfully the two went out of the house;
the devotees softly question the Lord.

**85.** Puri Gosani says: "Lord, if You are kind,
tell us the meaning of this; purify us all."

**86.** No one dares to ask, though the desire is great —
and I, daring, asked afterwards[^6].

**87.** The Thakura says: "Listen, listen, Gosani:
none of you has understood this at all.

**88.** This poor brahmana lived in Dravida
and suffered much torment and misery there.

**89.** Burnt by the fire of poverty he came to this land
and at last attacked Jagannatha.

**90.** Seeing him wretched, Jagannatha was gracious —
and unexpectedly he met with Vibhishana.

**91.** It was Vibhishana who sat beside Me:
he gave the wealth, to the brahmana's satisfaction."

**92.** Hearing this, all rejoiced;
all this earth and sky were flooded with prema.

**93.** All dance, all cry: "Hari-bol!" —
and in joy all embrace one another.

**94.** Hear, everyone, of Gorachand's manifestation;
thus ends the Shesha-khanda, says this Lochana Dasa[^7].

## The Concealment of the Lord (chapter 4)

*Raga dhanashi; disha: "O Lord, glory, glory to Gaurangachand! He has caught the minds of living beings in the net of prema"*

**94+1.** Gora, the utmost of beauty on the circle of the earth,
gave away the treasure of prema to all, down to the chandalas[^8].

**94+2.** Gora's glories make the mute eloquent:
the lame leaps over mountains, the blind sees the stars.

**94+3.** As I tell it, I do not know mine from another's:
whatever comes [to mind], I say without fear.

**94+4.** Chaitanya Gosani is the essence of all avataras:
such a treasury of mercy there will never be again.

**94+5.** There is no Ishvara but Krishna —
not in Satya, nor Treta, nor Kali, nor Dvapara.

**94+6.** The Lord is one; only the names differ:
to make people understand, He shows various forms.

**94+7.** All the avataras in all those ages
people call lesser and greater according to their mercy.

**94+8.** Chaitanya Gosani is great precisely in this mercy —
therefore I firmly say: He is the crown of avataras.

**94+9.** Such an avatara people do not recognise:
as if small insects had covered the nectar [and hidden it].

**94+10.** I have told of so wondrous a descent:
worship such a Lord, Gorachand, leaving sorrow.

**94+11.** The Lord, an ocean of mercy, mad with prema,
is ceaselessly [absorbed] with His devotees in the lilas of Vrindavana.

**94+12.** Such were Mahaprabhu's pastimes in Utkala;
the account of the Utkala pastimes is very extensive.

**94+13.** If told at length, the book would be enormous;
I have told it briefly — hear, everyone.

**94+14.** At that time Mahaprabhu, in Kashi Mishra's house,
speaks of Vrindavana with an aching heart.

**94+15.** Sighing, Mahaprabhu said:
"Never have I seen such a company of devotees."

**94+16.** He rose in haste to see Jagannatha
and step by step came to the Lion Gate.

**94+17.** All His companions went with Him,
and He quickly went inside the temple.

**94+18.** The Lord gazes at [Jagannatha's] face — and cannot see;
then the Lord thought of a means in His mind[^9].

**94+19.** Then the doors closed of themselves,
and He quickly went [within] with longing in His heart.

**94+20.** In the month of Ashadha, on the seventh lunar day,
the Lord, sighing, makes His plea:

**94+21.** "Satya, Treta, Dvapara and the Kali-yuga —
and especially in the Kali-yuga the essence is sankirtana.

**94+22.** Have mercy, Jagannatha, purifier of the fallen:
the Kali-yuga has come — give shelter now!"

**94+23.** Saying this, that Lord of the three worlds
clasped [Him] in His arms, pressing Him to His heart.

**94+24.** In the third prahara of the day, on a Sunday,
the Lord Himself entered into Jagannatha[^10].

**94+25.** A brahmana panda who was in the Gunjabadi,
crying "What, what?", came hurrying at once[^11].

**94+26.** Seeing the brahmana, the devotees say: "Listen, padichha,
open the doors — we long to see the Lord!"

**94+27.** Seeing the devotees' distress, the padichha says:
"In the Gunjabadi the Lord disappeared from sight.

**94+28.** With my own eyes I saw Gaura unite with the Lord [Jagannatha] —
I say it for certain; hear, everyone."

**94+29.** Hearing this, the devotees cried out in grief:
"We shall see the Lord's moonlike face no more!"

**94+30.** Srivasa Pandita and Mukunda Datta,
Gauridasa, Vasu Datta and Sri Govinda,

**94+31.** Kashi Mishra, Sanatana and Haridasa —
all the people of Utkala weep, sighing deeply[^12].

**94+32.** King Sri Prataparudra heard [the news]
and together with his family lost consciousness.

**94+33.** Sarvabhauma Bhattacharya, together with his son,
calls: "Lord, Lord! Hear us, Gauraraya!"

**94+34.** All the devotees wept bitterly —
how much of this can I, a low person, write?

**94+35.** The Lord's qualities are endless — they cannot be counted;
now that I do not see Him, darkness has come over me.

**94+36.** I say imploringly: hear, all,
day and night worship, brothers, the feet of Gauranga.

**94+37.** Becoming pure, all hear Gora's glories:
this is the means to destroy the disease of worldly existence.

**94+38.** In such grief Lochana laments:
the Shesha-khanda, the glorification of the Lord, is concluded.

## Lochana Dasa's Word about Himself

**94+39.** Hear, everyone, about my home and family,
and at the same time remember Sri Hari[^13].

**94+40.** To all I make one request:
know as true the feet of the Vaishnavas.

**94+41.** I bow to Gaura's lotus feet:
for a moment regard [me] with a merciful glance.

**94+42.** The little I know and set forth is by the Vaishnavas' grace;
the Thakura of my life is Narahari Dasa.

**94+43.** By the grace of his feet I gained hope in this path
and conceived the desire to tell Gora's glories.

**94+44.** Sri Murari Gupta the vaidya, the Lord's intimate,
knows everything — that seasoned devotee.

**94+45.** To deliver the people he composed the life of Chaitanya;
by his grace the world was purified.

**94+46.** He composed in verses a poem on Gaura's glories —
it has now become the basis for all[^14].

**94+47.** Hearing it, in greed for its sweetness my heart was stirred:
I did not look at my own faults — my mind forgot itself.

**94+48.** Now I have composed [this account] in panchali form —
let no one judge harshly: I am most low.

**94+49.** I am not qualified, and yet I dared:
seeing the Vaishnavas' mercy, I took heart.

**94+50.** By the Vaishnavas' mercy the book in four parts came to be;
in finishing it, my heart aches.

**94+51.** In the Sutra-khanda is the opening account, a portion of nectar;
the secrets of the birth and the rest the Adi-khanda told.

**94+52.** The account of the Madhya-khanda is an abode of mercy;
the account of the Shesha-khanda comes after the three parts.

**94+53.** By the Vaishnavas' mercy the account in four parts came to be;
in finishing it, my heart aches.

**94+54.** This account of Gaura's glories is an ocean of nectar:
even the lords Prajapati and Rudra cannot tell it.

**94+55.** What can I tell of His glories, how much do I know?
All that I have said is by the strength of the Vaishnavas' mercy.

**94+56.** With joined palms I speak imploringly:
I have offered myself at the feet of the Vaishnavas.

**94+57.** There is no one on earth lower than I —
by the strength of the Vaishnavas' mercy the work is accomplished.

**94+58.** Who knows how to tell the story of Chaitanya's life?
Unable to restrain myself, I have spoken a little.

**94+59.** Having completed the account in four parts, I have made it known.
I was born in the vaidya caste and live in Kogram.

**94+60.** My pious mother is named Sadanandi:
born from her womb, I long for Krishna.

**94+61.** My father who gave me birth is Kamalakara Dasa:
by his grace I tell the tale of Gora's glories.

**94+62.** My mother's family and my father's family live in one village;
blessed is my grandmother, named Abhaya Dasi.

**94+63.** The name of my maternal grandfather is Sri Purushottama Gupta:
he was purified by many tirthas and content in austerity.

**94+64.** In my mother's family and my father's I am the only son:
I have no brothers, and my grandfather has no [other] line.

**94+65.** Wherever I went, they petted me:
because I was a spoiled darling, no one could teach me.

**94+66.** [But he,] by beating and strictness, taught me letters —
blessed is Purushottama Gupta and his character!

**94+67.** I bow at his feet:
by his grace I write the life of Chaitanya.

**94+68.** I have told of my mother's and my father's families.
Narahari Dasa is the giver of prema-bhakti to me.

**94+69.** By his grace is all that I have set forth:
[thus] this Lochana Dasa has completed the book.

*Thus ends the Shesha-khanda of the "Sri Chaitanya-mangala," composed by Sri Lochana Dasa Thakura. The "Sri Chaitanya-mangala" is complete*[^15].

[^1]: Vv. 1–94 — cf. Murari 4.21 (the poor brahmana from Dravida and Vibhishana at Gaura's feet). Vibhishana is Ravana's brother, a devotee of Rama, by tradition immortal (chiranjivi); in Puri it is said that he comes to see Jagannatha.
[^2]: The brahmana threatens to die at the Deity's door, so that the sin of killing a brahmana would fall on the Lord Himself. Lochana Dasa himself below (vv. 57–61), through the Lord's words, censures this disposition — blaming the Lord for the fruits of one's own karma.
[^3]: V. 29+1 is found only in the 1983 edition; it is absent in the 1903 edition.
[^4]: A finger to the nose is a gesture of astonishment: Vibhishana is surprised that the brahmana is still following him. At the gate Govinda sees "two young brahmanas" (v. 50): Vibhishana appears in the guise of a brahmana.
[^5]: Cf. the Chaitanya-charitamrita (Madhya 22): even when He is worshipped for other desires, Krishna grants the petitioner His feet, and in time that person gives up other desires.
[^6]: V. 86 is spoken by Puri Gosani (Paramananda Puri), who tells how he ventured to ask.
[^7]: Here the text in Bhaktisiddhanta's edition ends; what follows is a chapter found only in the 1983 edition (in the 1903 edition, in a footnote).
[^8]: This chapter is absent in Bhaktisiddhanta's edition (1922): his text ends with v. 94 of the section "The Final Pastimes" and the colophon (see the end of this portion). The chapter is translated from Haridasa Shastri's edition (1983), where it stands as chapter 4 of the Shesha-khanda (we have transcribed its text from Devanagari into Bengali script, see bn/52). The 1903 edition gives it not in the main text but in a footnote after v. 94, with the editor's remarks on the sources, with variant readings and several additional lines (these, being absent in the 1983 edition, we do not translate). The numbering continues that of the section: 94+1, 94+2…
[^9]: The sense of the couplet is unclear: apparently the Lord, in separation, cannot be satisfied with seeing Jagannatha and resolves to unite with Him for ever.
[^10]: Lochana Dasa writes that the Lord "entered into Jagannatha" (literally, "became lina," "merged"). In the Gaudiya Vaishnava understanding (Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati), the Lord's departure is antardhana-lila: He only withdrew from mundane sight, just as He appeared by His own will; His body is sach-chid-ananda, not material, and there is no death for Him. The "merging" here is not the impersonal dissolution of a jiva in Brahman: the Lord re-enters His own form — the Deity of Jagannatha, non-different from Him (cf. en/45, note 15, on the Daru-brahman and en/51, v. 85). Other traditions tell of the departure differently: Jayananda (Chaitanya-mangala) speaks of a wound to the foot from a brick during the Ratha-yatra; according to another tradition the Lord disappeared in the temple of Tota-Gopinatha; the Chaitanya-charitamrita gives no account of the Lord's departure. The traditional date is 1533 (Shaka 1455).
[^11]: The Gunjabadi is apparently a room or courtyard in the Jagannatha temple; the exact meaning is unclear. The padichha is the temple superintendent (cf. en/51, v. 75).
[^12]: The list is poetic: according to the Chaitanya-charitamrita, Haridasa Thakura departed before the Lord (Antya 11), and Sanatana Gosvami lived in Vrindavana. Gauridasa is apparently Gauridasa Pandita of Ambika (Kalna); Govinda is the Lord's servant (or Govinda Ghosha).
[^13]: Vv. 94+39 — 94+69 are Lochana Dasa's closing word; they are found only in the 1983 edition (in the 1903 edition, in the same footnote, with variant readings and with additional lines on Narahari Sarakara, Raghunandana and others that are absent in the 1983 edition). Lochana Dasa was a vaidya and lived in Kogram (now in Purba Bardhaman district, West Bengal); his mother was Sadanandi, his father Kamalakara Dasa; his maternal grandfather Purushottama Gupta, his grandmother Abhaya Dasi; his guru Narahari Sarakara Thakura of Shrikhanda.
[^14]: Murari Gupta is the author of the Sanskrit poem Sri Krishna-chaitanya-charitamrita (Murari's "Kadcha"), on which Lochana Dasa relies (see our translation in murari-gupta/).
[^15]: The colophon of Bhaktisiddhanta's edition — there it stands directly after v. 94 of "The Final Pastimes." In the 1983 edition, after chapter 4: "Thus ends the Shesha-khanda of the book 'Sri Chaitanya-mangala.'"
