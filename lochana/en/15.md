# Adi-khanda, part 7 (portion 15)

*Pauganda-lila (continued): Vishvambhara's upanayana; during the rite He is overcome by the ecstasy of prema, and the astonished pandits reason about the avataras for a special purpose and the yuga-avataras, about Krishna in the Dvapara and Gaura in the Kali, about Radha and the dharma of service, and conclude that Vishvambhara is the Lord Himself*

*Raga varadi; disha: "Gorachand is my life!"*

**517.** Thus in joy and gladness the days pass;
the town of Nadiya floats in an ocean of happiness.

**518.** Who can tell the happiness of a single moment!
The good fortune of Shachi and Jagannatha cannot be contained in the universe.

**519.** One day Jagannatha suddenly saw
his well-mannered son among His companions.

**520.** The son is in His ninth year — a fitting and auspicious time;
"I shall give Him the sacred thread," he thought in his heart.

**521.** Coming home, he took counsel with Shachi,
called an astrologer and fixed an auspicious day.

**522.** Gathering friends and kinsfolk, he announced to them:
"Give me leave to invest Vishvambhara with the sacred thread."

**523.** Mishra engaged as acharya a famous pandit
who knows the rites of sacrifice and knows the Vedic ordinances.

**524.** To the brahmanas were given betel nuts, sandal and garlands;
hundreds of respectable women put on sindura.

**525.** Puffed rice, bananas, oil and turmeric
the virtuous Shachi gave to each and every one.

**526.** Conches sound, the women's cries of "Jaya, jaya!" ring out;
at the hour of dusk they perform the rite of anointing with fragrances (adhivasa).[^1]

**527.** The brahmanas recite the Vedas, the bhat bards their eulogies;
giving blessings, they did all according to rule and custom.

**528.** Rising at daybreak, Mishra Purandara
performed the nandimukha-shraddha beautifully.[^2]

**529.** Having honoured the brahmanas with water for the feet and for sipping,
judging the time had come, he began the sacrificial rite.

**530.** Here, meanwhile, Shachi-devi with all the married women
goes about making merry at her son's festival.

**530+1.** All the nagaris, as many as there were, surrounded Gauranga[^3]
and wished to anoint His holy body.

**531.** They anointed Vishvambhara's body with oil and turmeric,
washed His head with fragrant amalaki

**532.** and bathed Him with water of the heavenly river;
Shachi forgets herself in waves of joy.

**533.** Conches, dundubhis, bheur and kahal horns resound;
mridangas, patahas and bronze cymbals resound.

**534.** The rumble of the dhak drums is heard a yojana away;
at the sound of the sahini the heart is gladdened.

**535.** Vinas, flutes, kapilasa horns, pipes, nisan drums,
rababs, upangas and pakhoyajes play in unison.

**536.** Dancers dance, singers sing songs;
at an auspicious moment His head was shaved.

**537.** Every limb of His was adorned with ornaments,
and He was dressed in perfumes, garlands and sandal.

**538.** Shachi's son was brought to the place of sacrifice,
where the host of brahmanas chant the Vedas.

**539.** They clothed Him in red garments and the sacred thread;
seeing His beauty, Ananga (Kamadeva) himself was entranced.

**540.** His father speaks the mantra into Vishvambhara's ear;
seeing the staff in His hand, sin itself trembled with fear.

**541.** The Lord begs alms — such is the custom of this ashrama;
the sannyasa ashrama is the essence of all ashramas.

**542.** It was in His mind: "For the dharma of the age I shall take sannyasa,"
and at the time His head was shaved, He remembered it.[^4]

**543.** "So I shall do," and ecstasy seized Him:
"Wandering, I shall dispel the sufferings of all the people of Kali!"

**544.** His whole body thrills from head to foot;
each single bristling hair surpasses the filaments of the kadamba.

**545.** His two long eyes are compassionate and reddish;
the radiance of His body is like the rising sun.

**546.** At the onset of prema — a great, proud, thundering roar;
seeing this, all the brahmanas were astounded.

**547.** Sudarshana[^5] and the other leading pandits,
gathering together, reason among themselves:

**548.** all the pandits, meeting together, deliberate:
"This son of Shachi is not a human being.

**549.** This is the effulgence of some god," they understood with certainty;
"such effulgence can belong to none but Govinda.

**550.** What do we know of the Lord's deeds and conduct?
We only conjecture by the reasoning of our intellect."

**551.** One said: "Listen to my words:
the Lord's deeds we do not understand — that is certain.

**552.** Listen to what I say, as I myself understand it:
to deliver the people, the Lord takes birth age after age.

**553.** There are many avataras, according to the purpose for which they come;
but according to the nature of the yugas there are only four avataras.[^6]

**554.** To establish dharma, to destroy adharma
and to deliver the righteous, He appears.

**555.** Those who come to slay the asuras and the like
are called avataras for a special purpose (karya-avataras).

**556.** All the avataras that are written of, beginning with Sri Rama,
are karya-avataras: their deeds bear witness to it.

**557.** In the Treta-yuga [the yuga-avatara] is red, its dharma is sacrifice;
the Lord, dark as a blade of durva grass, [came] to destroy the rakshasas.

**558.** Not in every Treta does Raghunatha appear
to play with the monkeys in order to slay Ravana.

**559.** Ravana's lifespan is fourteen chatur-yugas;
count, brothers, how many Tretas went by!

**560.** That is why I say: not all Tretas are the same —
[the avataras come] according to purpose, depending on what happens when.

**561.** In the Satya-yuga [the avatara] is white, the dharma is austerity, the name Hamsa;
Nrisimha and the others I consider karya-avataras.

**562.** He who, with a colour befitting the yuga, establishes [its] dharma —
him I know as the avatara of the yuga (yuga-avatara).

**563.** Listen attentively about Krishna in the Dvapara:
He alone is the Lord, and no one else.

**564.** Whether karya-avatara or yuga-avatara —
the son of Nanda is full of all the parts (kalas).

**565.** Him all call the full, complete Brahman;
know that in Vrindavana He is the One who longs for the gopis' love.[^7]

**566.** The avatara of Krishna is the crown of all avataras;
among the Dvaparas this Dvapara is the best.

**567.** In other Dvaparas there are two [kinds of] avataras:
either a karya-avatara or a yuga-avatara.

**568.** In the Kali-yuga that follows the Dvapara in which Krishna descends,
Gaurachand appears.[^8]

**569.** As is the Krishna-avatara, so is Gaurachandra;
these two yugas are distinct from all the yugas.

**570.** Not in every Dvapara are there Krishna's pastimes,
not in every Kali-yuga does Gora descend.

**571.** Many Dvaparas, Kalis, Satyas and Tretas pass —
in all of them the Lord appears as partial avataras.

**572.** In this Dvapara and in this Kali-yuga,
by great fortune, one meets Krishna and Krishna Chaitanya.

**573.** In one day of Brahma He descends once
and sports in the Dvapara and in the Kali-yuga.

**574.** In the manvantara of Vaivasvata, becoming dark and golden,
He performed the worship (service), [the dharma] of the Dvapara, and [in the Kali] kirtana.

**575.** Blessed, blessed is the Kali-yuga — above all the yugas:
in the sacrifice of sankirtana all became qualified to take part.

**576.** O, O merciful Lord Gaurachandra!
By sankirtana He carried across [the ocean of the world] the lame, the dull and the blind.

**577.** If you do not trust my words,
then answer properly what I shall ask.

**578.** Whatever colour and dharma belong to a yuga,
that work the Lord performs as the avatara of the yuga.

**579.** In the Dvapara the Lord Krishna was the avatara of the yuga:
how then did He carry out the dharma of the age?

**580.** The shastras say that in the Dvapara the dharma is service (paricharya);
where did the Lord establish this dharma?

**581.** If you will not disdain it, I shall say one thing;
I speak with reason — do not reject my words.

**582.** He Himself is the Lord, the independent Ishvara;
both the special purpose and the dharma of the age are all in His hands.

**583.** The establishment of the dharma of the age and every special purpose —
all this the Lord accomplished; it is wonderful to comprehend!

**584.** To sport by descending as Radha-Krishna,
He Himself is the independent [Lord], and Radha is in the form of [His] prakriti.[^9]

**585.** Like prakriti and purusha, they are soul and body;
both are one body, but, understanding the purpose [of the pastimes], they became two.

**586.** She bears the name Radha because her task is the worship (aradhana) of Krishna;
she serves Him together with the society of the gopis.

**587.** The gopis, hundreds upon hundreds of branches, serve with prema and devotion;
but the very essence of [His] prakriti is Radhika alone.

**588.** She offers [Him] her senses, her body, the very nature of her body;
and in this her ever-new love (anuraga) grows.

**589.** No one understood this dharma of service;
so say all the bhagavatas.[^10]

**590.** In other Dvapara-yugas He acts through a portion
and establishes dharma, [but people] do not grasp its essence.

**591.** Charity, vows and austerity are called dharma,
and all, having performed dharma, offer [its fruit] to Him.

**592.** That is why the Lord revealed Himself in person;
and still no one understood the root of dharma and adharma.

**593.** In the Kali-yuga He reveals Himself in a golden body
and, doing the work of the yuga-avatara, reveals prema.

**594.** His body has Radha's complexion — He became golden,
holding within His heart Radhika's feelings and rasa.

**595.** In that mood He weeps, the crown of the relishers of rasa;
His body is like a blossoming kadamba, all thrilling.

**596.** Intoxicated with that prema, brimming over with it,
He roars and thunders, weeping and weeping.

**597.** Hearing that roar, the age of Kali that lay senseless —
all regained consciousness, and the joy was immense.

**598.** Therefore, crying "Radha-Krishna", they dance, weep and laugh;
the darkness went away and they found light.

**599.** Krishna, born in the Dvapara, [now] in a golden body
awakened the senseless people of the Kali.

**600.** The Lord reveals prema, assuming a humble guise;
He gives Himself away and counts it His own gain.

**601.** What lord ever showed such lordship?
He gives prema even to those who do not worship Him — without discrimination.

**602.** That is why I say: this is the avatara of the yuga;
and He also manifested this full avatara.

**603.** In other Kali-yugas, however, there is an avatara of Narayana;
his name is Krishna, as in the Dvapara-yuga.[^11]

**604.** His colour surpasses a parrot's wing —
"the lustre of sapphire", says the commentator.

**605.** In this Kali-yuga Gaurachandra is the full Brahman,
and the portion [— the yuga-avatara —] entered into Him: this is the essence I have told.[^12]

**606.** Chaitanya Gosani is the fullest of full avataras;
there is no one else who is such an ocean of compassion.

**607.** Karya-avatara and yuga-avatara are one in Him;
therefore, in accordance with the yuga, Gaura is manifest before our eyes.

**608.** The shastras say: in the Kali [the avatara] is golden, the dharma is sankirtana;[^13]
this is the Lord Vishvambhara, and no one else."

**609.** Having reflected, all the pandits became firm in heart;
and the Lord, understanding the time, restrained Himself.

**610.** Then in a moment the Lord withdrew it all,
and Vishvambhara Gaurahari spoke [as usual].

**611.** All the people whisper to one another about the wondrous event;
guessing this way and that, they go off here and there.

**612.** Some remained in amazement, with doubt in their hearts:
"What have we seen? What lies behind Vishvambhara's deeds?"

**613.** What they had heard from people about Vishvambhara
they saw with their own eyes: He is the Creator of the world.

**614.** The town overflowed with joy, all cry "Jaya, jaya!";
the blessed song of Gora's glories is sung by this Lochana.

[^1]: Adhivasa is a preparatory rite on the eve of a samskara, with anointing with fragrances and the distribution of auspicious gifts.
[^2]: The nandimukha-shraddha is an offering to the ancestors performed before a samskara (upanayana, wedding and the like).
[^3]: V. 530+1 is found only in the 1983 edition (absent from Bhaktisiddhanta's edition); the 1903 edition gives its first line in a footnote as a variant of v. 531. On Lochana Dasa's nagari-bhava see ru/09, note 9.
[^4]: At the upanayana the brahmachari student receives a staff and begs alms like a sannyasi; Lochana Dasa sees in this a foreshadowing of the sannyasa the Lord will take in 1510.
[^5]: Sudarshana Pandita was one of the Lord's teachers in Navadvipa (in Murari, 1.09.1).
[^6]: Lochana Dasa's division: "karya-avataras" (avataras for a special purpose — slaying asuras and the like; in the Chaitanya-charitamrita the lila-avataras correspond to them) and "yuga-avataras", who establish the dharma of their age in the colour proper to the yuga (cf. Bhagavata 11.5.20–32).
[^7]: Literally "gopika-lampata", "avid for the gopis". In the Gaudiya Vaishnava understanding this is Krishna's transcendental love for the gopis, His internal energy, which has nothing in common with worldly lust.
[^8]: Cf. Chaitanya-charitamrita (Adi 3.6–10): Krishna descends once in a day of Brahma, at the end of the Dvapara of the twenty-eighth chatur-yuga of the Vaivasvata manvantara, and in the Kali following it appears as Sri Chaitanya. Verses 573–574 say the same.
[^9]: "Prakriti" here is not material nature but the Lord's internal energy (hladini-shakti), personified by Radha; Radha and Krishna are one soul in two bodies (cf. Chaitanya-charitamrita, Adi 4.55–56, 4.96–98).
[^10]: "Bhagavatas" are the Lord's devotees; the reading "so says the Bhagavata" is also possible.
[^11]: That is, in ordinary Kali-yugas the yuga-avatara is "krishna-varna", dark in colour (cf. Bhagavata 11.5.32 and Sridhara Svami's comment: "shining like sapphire"). The line is corrupt in A; the reading of the 1983 edition is adopted; its exact sense is unclear.
[^12]: Cf. Chaitanya-charitamrita (Adi 4.8–12): the dharma of the age is established by a portion of the Lord, the yuga-avatara; but when Krishna Himself descends, all the avataras, the yuga-avatara included, enter into Him.
[^13]: "Pita" means yellow, golden; cf. Bhagavata (10.8.13): Garga Muni speaks of the three former colours of the avataras — white, red and yellow (pita) — and the Gaudiya Vaishnavas see in "yellow" an indication of Gauranga.
