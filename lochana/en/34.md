# Madhya-khanda, part 11 (portion 34)

*The section "Mahaprabhu Distributes Prema in His Bhavas" (end): the Lord tells the devotees of the greatness of the sankirtana-yajna; the play in Chandrashekhara's house — the Lord in a gopi's dress, Srivasa in the bhava of Narada glorifies Gadadhara as Radha; the arrival of Haridasa and Advaita; the Lord in the bhava of Lakshmi and of the primal Shakti, Haridasa on Her lap; the bhava of Ishvara; Lochana Dasa's reflection on the hidden avatara*

**72.** The next day the Lord, seated on a divine seat,
began to speak to all the devotees:

**73.** "Such is the greatness of this sankirtana-yajna of Mine:[^1]
all the scriptures speak of its greatness and glory.

**74.** This dharma of sankirtana is the essence of all dharmas;
know that in the Kali age this above all is the work to do.

**75.** It is revealed from the fifth Veda[^2] —
therefore Shiva sings [it] ceaselessly with his five mouths.

**76.** Narada wanders dancing, singing to his vina;
the devotees — Shuka, Sanaka and others — wander singing.

**77.** In Vrindavana Radha and Krishna, taking up this Veda,
dance with the gopis, absorbed in prema.

**78.** Know that the fifth [Veda] abides eternally in Vrindavana —
therefore Shiva sings in great prema.

**79.** And yet, for all his singing, Shiva did not reach its end —
such a Veda has appeared in the Kali age.

**80.** Whoever performs it in song is awakened:
the utterance of the Veda in the form of song is a great mercy.

**81.** The hollows of everyone's ears are the expanse of the sacrificial pit,
the tongue is the ladle, the sweet rasa of the sound is the ghee.[^3]

**82.** Entering within, it kindles the fire of bhava;
its flames are thrill and tears, the trembling of the body.

**83.** Freed from all sins, all the people dance;
the liberations — salokya and the rest — follow them about,

**84.** but they never glance at them even from the corner of their eyes:
they wander dancing, tasting the rasa of Krishna.[^4]

**85.** The Vaishnava acharyas stand around this yajna;
know that the yajna of kirtana is the noblest of all yajnas.

**86.** From it this great treasure, prema, was born;
the master of this [yajna] is Nityananda with His circle,

**87.** and the mistress of this prema is Gadadhara Pandita;[^5]
know this truth, all you jewels among devotees.

**88.** Advaita Acharya Gosani, having brought Me down,
firmly establishes the sankirtana-yajna.[^6]

**89.** Srinivasa, Narahari and the other devotees —
with all of you I establish My yajna.

**90.** Carry this yajna in the Kali age from house to house —
let all the people, the fallen and the sinful, be delivered."

**91.** Hearing this, the devotees, weeping and weeping,
fall at the Lord's feet, swaying.

**92.** Gaura Bhagavan embraced them all;
hearing this joyful story, this Lochana sings.

*Raga varadi; the tune "dhulakhela"*

**93.** Another wondrous story — hear the song of Gora's qualities,
words unknown to the world and to the Vedas.
In the sway of His bhava, to proclaim bhakti-yoga,
He acts — mercy embodied, the jewel of all qualities.

**94.** Hear the story attentively, leaving other talk:
it is time to tell the rest as well.
Together with His own, Srila Vishvambhara Hari
went to the house of Sri Chandrashekhara.[^7]

**95.** In the course of talk the conversation turned to the qualities of the gopis —
and, speaking of them, His voice faltered;
His face grew red, tears streamed from His eyes —
immersed in rasa, He revealed rasa.

**96.** The Lord whose feet Kamala serves in rapture —
such a Lord, for the gopis' sake,
is overwhelmed at the mere mention [of them]: such was the devotion they showed!
At mere words He takes on their bhava.

**97.** Then Vishvambhara Hari, putting on a gopi's dress,
in the house of Sri Chandrashekhara Acharya
dances, lost in bliss; and at that very hour
the bhava of Narada came upon Srivasa.[^8]

**98.** He bows to the Lord and humbly says:
"Know me as Your servant."
Having spoken these words, that great sage
then addresses Gadadhara Pandita:

**99.** "Listen, gopi, to what I shall say:
do you know anything of your past?
I shall tell a wonder: you are rare in the world —
hear attentively about yourself.

**100.** Hear about all of you — I shall sing your qualities:
you were born, one and all, in Gokula;
leaving your fidelity to your husbands, you served [Krishna] unceasingly
and attained your heart's desire in Vrindavana.[^9]

**101.** You are the first of prakritis, you are Radha, Krishna's shakti:[^10]
what do I know, that I could tell of it?

**102.** Crown of all women, the favoured one in Krishna's love —
how could I tell your truth?

**103.** You showed such devotion that none can equal [you];
it is the supreme secret of the three worlds.
Brahma, the god Maheshvara, Lakshmi and even Ananta
have less grace than you.

**104.** Prahlada, Narada and others, Sanatana, Shuka and the rest
know not a drop of your devotion.
The Husband of the Lakshmi of the three worlds longs for your love —
and puts this lovely dress on His own body.[^11]

**105.** He whose maidservant is Lakshmi, hoping for your prema,
holds in His heart a longing [for you];
the Lord of all the worlds you have enchanted with that love —
blessed, blessed is your tenderness!

**106.** You have known the truth — the greatness of the Lord's inmost heart —
and have bound [Him] fast with love.
Uddhava, Akrura and the rest — all serve your feet:
do not forsake in your heart those who follow you."

**107.** Such words spoke Srinivasa, the jewel of brahmanas;
hearing them, all rejoiced.
All the Vaishnavas together embrace one another,
gazing at Vishvambhara's feet.

**108.** They dance, lost in bliss, overflowing with prema —
and at that hour Haridasa came.
Staff in hand, he stood in front and proclaims,
singing [the Lord's] qualities in supreme delight:[^12]

**109.** "Brothers, perform the sankirtana of Hari's qualities without cease!" —
so saying, he laughs aloud.
Lost in singing Hari's qualities, he sheds streams from his eyes
and in joy turns round to every side.

**110.** Hearing Haridasa's words, all the jewel-like Vaishnavas —
as if their whole bodies were sprinkled with nectar:
in joy they dance and sing, in their midst dances Gora-raya,
and, weeping, they clasp His reddish feet.

**111.** Then came the abode of all virtues, named Advaita Acharya,
the king of all the Vaishnavas;
His beauty lit up the earth; He stands in front and gazes —
born of the Lord's portion, of great might.[^13]

**112.** "Hari, Hari!" He calls, and the people start;
in joy He dances, filled with prema;
His whole body thrills from feet to head,
tears of prema stream from His eyes.

**113.** Again and again He gazes at Vishvambhara's holy feet,
roars and slaps His arms like a wrestler;
all the Vaishnavas together, opening baskets of the wares of prema,
spread out a wondrous market.

**114.** All the Vaishnavas, rejoicing at heart,
plunged into the ocean of prema;
with all the Vaishnavas Sri Gaurahari Himself
reveals happiness to the world.

**115.** And now I shall tell — listen, all, attentively:
the Lord, in the sway of a gopi's bhava,
puts a bodice on His breast, conch-shell bangles on His wrists;
both His eyes are drowning in rasa.

**116.** He wears silk, anklets on His feet,
His waist so slender a fist could clasp it;
His beauty charms the three worlds — to what can it be compared? —
the Thakura Himself in a gopi's dress.[^14]

**117.** From the unearthly radiance of His body a Malaya breeze blows,
on Him is a garland of fresh malati;
like the waters of the heavenly river on the peak of Sumeru,
two streams [of tears] flow over Gora's body.

**118.** Amid all the Vaishnavas dances the great king of dancers,
taking on bhavas in the rapture of rasa.
And as He danced thus, He thought of Lakshmi —
and in that bhava went into the Deity's chamber.

**119.** Entering the chamber and seeing the divine four-armed Deity,
He stood beside It;
He looks with half-closed eyes, walks with half-steps,
and has covered His eyes with the edge of His cloth.[^15]

**120.** Then all His own, falling at His holy feet,
offer praise with humble words;
someone recites the Sri-stava and, lost in bliss,
asks a boon: "Give prema-bhakti!"

**121.** All offer praise; hearing it, at that very moment
He thought of the primal Shakti
and took on that very bhava; all are amazed
and recite many hymns composed by the gods.[^16]

**122.** When all had offered praise with the great hymns of the gods,
the primal Shakti, pleased, speaks;
seated on the Deity's seat, She says with a soft smile:
"I have come to see [your] prema-bhakti.

**123.** I have come wishing to see your dancing and singing —
I have told you My desire."
Hearing this, all those people say again:
"Reveal [in us] devotion to You."

**124.** When they asked this boon, the primal Shakti said:
"Listen, listen, listen, all!
I am Chandi, the fierce: you too shall have the rod!" —
such was the boon She gave to all.[^17]

**125.** Hearing this, all bow down,
falling to the ground like a stick.
Then that Mistress, taking Haridasa by the hand,
smiling, seated him on Her lap.

**126.** Sitting on Her lap, Haridasa rocks to and fro
like a child of five.
Seeing this marvel, all rejoiced at heart;
even the birds and beasts were glad.

**127.** At that moment someone spoke these words:
"Cast a merciful glance on Murari:
he is Your own servant." Hearing this, [She] smiled
softly, sweeter than nectar.

**128.** Her eyes are full of tears of compassion, brimming with prema,
the tender moon of Her face grew red.
At that hour Shachi-devi, herself serving [His] holy feet,
was overcome by the bliss of prema.

**129.** Then that Katyayani, drawing all near,
regards them as Her own sons;
She shows the people a mother's tenderness, and all, looking at Her,
fill their eyes with tears of prema.[^18]

**130.** At that hour, at that very moment, a brahmana came
and loudly called: "Prabhu!"
Seeing the suffering of the sufferer, His eyes filled with tears,
and He entered the rapture [of the bhava] of Ishvara.

**131.** Becoming Ishvara Himself, revealing His prema,
He shows His lordship by His own qualities.
All, surrounding Him, prostrated themselves,
seeing again [in Him] the bhava of Ishvara.

**132.** Thus, spending the whole night in the rapture of rasa,
at dawn He went home.
All who went with Him saw Gora-raya
as if holding a fierce rod.[^19]

**133.** Thus Gaurahari, the one Maker of all the worlds,
revealed His mercy;
having come for mercy's sake and showing humility,
He Himself cares for the earth.

**134.** If anyone, hearing so wondrous a story, is not freed
in his heart from the pain of samsara —
he will never be freed from it; and whoever doubts this —
shame on him: there is no worse wretch.

**135.** Reason, experience and scripture — all three say just this,
though one does not see it openly proclaimed;
whoever does not ponder this — that what never was has come to be —
how will such a one be delivered?[^20]

**136.** Such an avatara as Gora, such a revelation of mercy,
has never been and never will be.
Let people say what they will — I tell them what I have realised:
let them reflect on it within themselves.

**137.** One care I have, a pain deep in my heart:
lest such an avatara pass [people] by.
For this my heart weeps — to whom shall I tell it?
This Lochana Dasa sings [Gora's] qualities.

[^1]: Cf. Bhagavata 11.5.32: in the age of Kali the intelligent worship the Lord by a yajna consisting chiefly of sankirtana. Bhaktisiddhanta's summary of these pastimes is at the head of the section (en/33).
[^2]: The "fifth Veda" usually means the itihasas and puranas (Bhagavata 1.4.20); Lochana Dasa gives this name to the sung word, the Veda "in the form of song" (v. 80) revealed in the Kali age. "With five mouths" is a play on words: Shiva has five faces.
[^3]: Kirtana is described as a yajna: the sacrificial pit (kunda), the ladle (sruva) with which ghee is poured into the fire, and the ghee itself.
[^4]: The pure devotees do not accept the five kinds of liberation (salokya, sarupya and so on) if they contain no service to the Lord (Bhagavata 3.29.13).
[^5]: The image is that of a Vedic yajna, which the master (yajamana) performs together with his wife. Nityananda is the giver of prema; Gadadhara Pandita is, in the Gaudiya Vaishnava understanding, Sri Radha (en/25, note 7), whose prema this is.
[^6]: Advaita Acharya (Maha-Vishnu, en/27, note 8) brought the Lord down into this world by His calls.
[^7]: Chandrashekhara Acharya (Acharyaratna) is the husband of Shachi's sister, the Lord's aunt. The play in his house: Murari 2.15.7–19, 2.16; Chaitanya-bhagavata, Madhya 18 (where the Lord appears in the bhava of Rukmini and of the primal Shakti, Gadadhara as Her companion, Haridasa as a watchman with a staff, Srivasa as Narada). Bhaktisiddhanta's summary is at the head of the section (en/33).
[^8]: According to the Gaura-ganoddesha-dipika, Srivasa is Narada. The gopi's dress and the bhavas of a gopi, of Lakshmi and of the primal Shakti which the Lord shows in this play are His own lila: the shakti is non-different from the Possessor of shakti, and He reveals the bhavas of His shaktis. This is no worldly dressing-up or play-acting.
[^9]: The gopis' love for Krishna is pure prema, above all worldly duties; their "husbands" are a mere appearance created by yogamaya (Bhagavata 10.33.37). There is nothing worldly here.
[^10]: Prakriti here means Krishna's shakti, His internal energy, not material nature (the pradhana of Sankhya). Gadadhara Pandita is Sri Radha (en/25, note 7). In A this stanza is printed under two numbers, 101 and 102 (one per half-stanza); in the 1983 edition it is a single stanza (9). We follow A's numbering; next, A misprints "100" for 103.
[^11]: That is, the Lord, longing for Radha's love, has Himself put on a gopi's dress — a hint at the secret of Gaura's appearance, in which He took Radha's bhava and complexion (Chaitanya-charitamrita, Adi 1.5–6).
[^12]: Cf. Murari 2.16.1. In the Chaitanya-bhagavata (Madhya 18) Haridasa in this play is a watchman-herald with a staff.
[^13]: Advaita Acharya is Maha-Vishnu (en/27, note 8); the word "portion" does not diminish Him. Cf. Murari 2.16.3–4.
[^14]: Cf. Murari 2.16.7–8.
[^15]: Cf. Murari 2.16.11–13. The half-closed eyes, half-steps and veil are the feminine modesty of Lakshmi, whose bhava the Lord reveals.
[^16]: The primal Shakti (Adya-shakti) — in Lochana Dasa she is also Chandi and Katyayani (vv. 124, 129). In the Gaudiya Vaishnava understanding the Lord's primal shakti is His internal energy (yogamaya); Durga, who rules the material world, is her shadow and the Lord's servant (Brahma-samhita 5.44). The Lord reveals the bhava of His own shakti, non-different from Him (cf. Murari 2.16.15 and note 8 above); this is neither possession nor the worship of another deity.
[^17]: The rod (danda) is a sign of power and fierce might; the 1983 edition has "you will all become fierce". In Murari (2.16.17–18) the Goddess jokes that people will take Her devotees for worshippers of Chandi. Cf. also v. 132.
[^18]: According to Murari (2.16.20–21) and the Chaitanya-bhagavata (Madhya 18), the Lord in this bhava gave the devotees milk from His breast, like a mother.
[^19]: Cf. Murari 2.16.23: the Lord, as if holding a staff in His hand, seemed to the people like the flame of the blazing sun.
[^20]: Gaura is the hidden (channa) avatara: the scriptures speak of Him covertly (Bhagavata 7.9.38; 11.5.32). The line "না ছিল সে হৈল সিয়া" is unclear in all witnesses; we translate by sense.
