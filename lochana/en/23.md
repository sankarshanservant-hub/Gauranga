# Adi-khanda, part 15 (portion 23)

*The journey to Gaya: the Lord teaches and resolves to perform the rite for His father; farewell to Shachi; on the way all hasten to see Him; the lesson of the deer; on Mount Mandara — a fever cured by the water of a brahmana's feet, and the teaching about brahmanas; the meeting with Ishvara Puri and initiation; the moods of Vraja; the rites in Gaya, prema at the Vishnupada; the wish to go to Vrindavana and the heavenly voice; the return to Nadia; the conclusion of the Adi-khanda*

## Kaishora-lila (Youth): the Lord's journey to Gaya

> *Summary by Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati.* After some time Gaurasundara, having concluded the lila of studying, took up teaching. Then, on the pretext of offering pinda for His father, He graciously set out for Gaya. On the way He gave His darshan to all the beasts and birds and drew them to His feet; and to teach reverence for brahmanas He enacted the lila of being cured of His illness by drinking the water that had washed a brahmana's feet, and He also taught that one who does not worship Krishna can never be a brahmana. Then, coming to Gaya and worshipping the gods and the ancestors, He went to see the Vishnupada. There He met Ishvara Puri, the best of devotees, and, to show him mercy, He asked him for a mantra. As soon as the Lord received the Krishna mantra from Ishvara Puri, bhava arose in Him, and He became eager to see the Vishnupada. Seeing the Vishnupada, Sriman Mahaprabhu, overwhelmed with prema, displayed laughter, dancing, singing and other signs of prema, and, staying there only a few days, returned home[^1].

*Raga varadi; disha: "My life — Dvijachand!"*

**1.** Thus Mahaprabhu, in joy and delight,
lived happily together with His relatives and friends.

**2.** All the brahmanas living in Navadvipa
say to one another: "Blessed, blessed!"

**3.** The students study the rules of the auspicious worldly rites —
they are taught by the Lord Himself, the jewel among men.

**4.** He surpasses Brihaspati, He is a poet who knows the taste of poetry;
He is Ishvara Himself: with what words can He be praised?

**5.** Who can describe the greatness of the students
whom the Teacher of the world Himself teaches?

**6.** The Lord Vishvambhara, beloved of millions of Sarasvatis,
grants all the pandits His mercy — the taste of learning.

**7.** Thus Vishvambhara instructed the people;
He resolved: "I shall go to perform the rite in Gaya[^2].

**8.** I shall offer pinda for My father on the head of Gaya
and bow to Gadadhara and to the Vishnupada."

**9.** Saying this, the Thakura set out on the auspicious journey;
with Him went brahmanas of noble families.

**10.** Shachi's heart burns, her voice falters;
coming to her son, she sighs heavily:

**11.** "You are going to foreign lands — listen, Vishvambhara:
without You there is darkness in my house.

**12.** You are the blind woman's staff, the pupil of my eyes,
the soul of this body — I have no one but You.

**13.** You are going to deliver the ancestors —
what shall I say to You for my own sake?"

**14.** When mother Shachi spoke such words,
He consoled her with sweet speech:

**15.** "Know, mother, that I am always
near you" — so He replied.

**16.** "All people need a son for the sake of the pinda;
kindly give Me leave — do not grieve"[^3].

**17.** Thus Vishvambhara set out to perform the rite in Gaya;
with Him went brahmanas with joy in their hearts.

**18.** Whatever road the Lord, the son of Shachi, travelled,
the people on that road, seeing Him, delight their eyes.

**19.** Children, the old, the lame, the infirm run to see;
all the beasts and birds run — tears stream from their eyes.

**20.** All the wives of noble families run, forgetting their families;
all say: "Look — here is Sri Hari of Vraja!"

**21.** Saying this, people run without tying their hair —
the Lord, passing through all lands, drove everyone mad.

**22.** Thus on every road all the people run —
He plunges all into the ocean of the rasa of prema.

**23.** Going along the road, Gaurahari sees in one place
a buck and a doe frolicking together.

**24.** Seeing the play of the deer, He was amused
and, like an ordinary man, laughed heartily.

**25.** Beasts are intoxicated with greed, delusion, lust and anger;
so are all people if they do not worship Krishna.

**26.** Smiling, Bhagavan explains to His companions:
"The same intelligence that beasts have is present in men;

**27.** only in the body of a beast there is no knowledge of Krishna —
and a man who does not worship Krishna is called a beast."

**28.** Having explained all this, the Lord, the Teacher of the world,
the Lord who is the Wish-fulfilling Tree, went on His way.

**29.** Then — a river named Chira;
there the Lord bathed and gave gifts as prescribed[^4].

**30.** Joyfully performing the worship of the gods and the ancestors,
the Lord climbed Mandara to see the Deity.

**31.** Having seen the Deity, the Lord quickly came down;
at the foot of the mountain He stayed in a brahmana's house.

**32.** Then the brahmanas accompanying Vishvambhara,
seeing the brahmanas of that land, found fault with them in their minds.

**33.** These followed the customs of their land as was their way;
seeing this, [the Lord's companions] did not regard them as brahmanas[^5].

**34.** Seeing this disregard for brahmanas, the Lord Vishvambhara
resolved to show reverence for brahmanas.

**35.** Suddenly a high fever seized the Lord's body;
seeing the fever, all were frightened at heart[^6].

**36.** The Thakura said: "Listen, listen, all of you:
for what reason has an obstacle arisen to the rite for the gods and the ancestors?

**37.** I do not know — through My fault or through the fault of My companions —
an obstacle has arisen to the auspicious work, and this grieves Me greatly.

**38.** There is a remedy that removes all obstacles:
give Me the water that has washed a brahmana's feet — it will bring relief.

**39.** The water of a brahmana's feet, when drunk, destroys all sins —
the fever will go at once: what can it do?"

**40.** A local brahmana was there;
[the Lord] rose Himself and washed his feet.

**41.** Vishvambhara drank the water of the brahmana's feet,
showed reverence for brahmanas — and the fever fled.

**42.** Then the abashed best of brahmanas speaks humble words:
"Because of the fault in my heart You have suffered pain.

**43.** Seeing [their] customs as bad, my mind found fault [with them];
because of the fault in my mind You were displeased.

**44.** Now You have shown reverence for brahmanas;
I have committed an offence — You, Lord, forgive me.

**45.** You are Brahmanya, You are the guardian of reverence for brahmanas,
bearing on Your chest the mark of the sage Bhrigu's foot[^7].

**46.** With Your own lips You reveal the glory of devotion to You
and thus deliver the world.

**47.** Glory to You, beloved Vishvambhara! Glory to You, king of brahmanas!
For one who serves You every undertaking succeeds.

**48.** Obeisance to You, merciful Gaurahari, lover of brahmanas!
Obeisance to You, establisher of dharma, Lord of all!"

**49.** Hearing such words from His companion, Vishvambhara
forgave all of them their many faults.

**50.** "They worship Thakura Madhusudana;
they are not to be rejected — do not regard them as outsiders.

**51.** One who does not worship Krishna is never twice-born —
this teaching is confirmed by the Puranas."

*Thus [it is said][^8]:*

**52.** "Even a chandala devoted to Vishnu is better than a sage;
but a twice-born devoid of devotion to Vishnu is lower than a dog-eater."

**53.** Saying this, He was pleased with His brahmana companions
and graciously forgave them their fault.

**54.** Having thus shown reverence for brahmanas,
[the Lord] came to the holy place on the river Punahpuna.

**55.** Performing there His bath and the worship of the Deity,
and completing the rites for the ancestors, He went on.

**56.** Then — the excellent holy place called Rajagiri:
coming to the Brahma-kunda, the Lord bathed and gave gifts[^9].

**57.** Having performed there the worship of the gods and the ancestors,
He hastened to see the Vishnupada.

**58.** On the way He saw the best of sannyasis,
a maha-bhagavata named Ishvara Puri.

**59.** Bowing to him, Vishvambhara said:
"By great good fortune I have seen these two feet!"

**60.** Falling at his feet, He speaks plaintive words;
His eyes, reddened and full of entreaty, fill with tears:

**61.** "How shall I cross this ocean of samsara?
Grant Me devotion to Krishna's lotus feet.

**62.** Without initiation into the Krishna mantra the body, I see, is useless:
the words of the Puranas and the lips of the saints all bear witness to this"[^10].

**63.** Hearing such words, Ishvara Puri
in private imparted to Him the great, the best of mantras.

**64.** Vishvambhara received the great mantra of Gopinatha[^11],
His whole body thrilled, His heart rejoiced.

**65.** Tears flow from His eyes, His body trembles;
repeating "Radha, Radha!", He stirs up waves of happiness.

**66.** All the moods of Vraja awoke in His mind,
and above all His mind plunged into madhurya-rasa.

**67.** His whole body overcome by the mood of Radha,
He calls in a loud voice: "Krishna, Krishna!"

**68.** Calling "Vrindavana, Govardhana!", He laughs;
crying "Kalindi, Yamuna!", He shouts in delight.

**69.** Now He calls Balarama, Shridama, Sudama,
now He calls out the names of Nanda and Yashoda.

**70.** "Dhavali, Shangali!" — He calls in a deep voice;
now, crying "Friend!", the Lord falls down in agitation.

**71.** Now in the mood of a servant He takes a straw between His teeth,
now proudly says: "It is I!

**72.** I held up the mountain, I killed Aghasura,
I killed Putana and all the other asuras!"

**73.** Now, standing in the threefold-bending pose, He holds a flute to His lips,
now, startled, He looks around in all directions[^12].

**74.** Tears flow from His eyes, His voice falters;
with sweet words He addresses the guru:

**75.** "By your mercy I have attained the goal:
from this day My embodiment has become meaningful."

**76.** Having [thus] shown devotion to the guru, the Lord went on;
seeing the river named Phalgu, He gently smiled.

**77.** He remembered the past — in joy and sorrow;
remembering Sita, He fell into great agitation[^13].

**78.** He worshipped the gods and the ancestors, bathed and gave gifts,
and duly offered pinda on the Pretashila.

**79.** For His father He gave wealth to the brahmanas;
having performed [the rite at] Udichi, He performed it at Dakshina-manasa.

**80.** Performing [the rite] at Uttara-manasa and at the holy place Jihvalola,
worshipping the gods and the ancestors, He distributed wealth[^14].

**81.** Then with great joy in His heart He arrived in Gaya;
His longing to see the foot of Vishnu grew.

**82.** The Lord offers pindas at the Sixteen Altars;
His eagerness to see the Vishnupada grew.

**83.** Having finished all the rites, He went quickly,
with a joyful heart, to see the Vishnupada.

**84.** "With My own eyes I shall see the marks of Vishnu's foot!" —
joyfully He says to Himself what is in His heart.

**85.** Thinking so, He came to the Vishnupada
and in supreme bliss, having bowed down, sat.

**86.** Gauranga says: "Listen, listen, all of you:
what happens in the heart when one sees the Vishnupada?

**87.** I have seen with My own eyes the marks of Vishnu's foot —
why then, having seen them, has prema not arisen?"[^15]

**88.** Saying this, Mahaprabhu washed the Vishnupada,
performed abhisheka — and His heart was filled with joy.

**89.** Showing bhakti, the Lord Vishvambhara Hari —
Gora, the possessor of prema — manifests [it]:

**90.** prema began — trembling, hair standing on end,
streams flow from His eyes, at times He becomes stunned.

**91.** Seeing the lotus foot, the Lord lost Himself
and in a great festival of prema moves about, dancing.

**92.** He offered pinda on the head of Gaya, on the lotus foot;
in joy all the brahmanas dance together [with Him].

**93.** On the next day the secret desire took firm hold in His heart:
the Lord suddenly set out for Madhupuri.

**94.** He said to His brahmana companions:
"Let us go to see Vrindavana."

**95.** Hearing this, the companions were dismayed:
"We cannot go — our means are small."

**96.** The Lord says: "From birth man is bound by the care for food;
not understanding [life's goal], he anxiously performs many works.

**97.** Human birth becomes meaningful if one worships Krishna;
but not worshipping Krishna, one sinks in an ocean of suffering."

**98.** Having explained all this, the Lord Gaurahari
set out from Gaya for Vrindavana.

**99.** He Himself set out together with His companions;
and then a voice rang out from the sky,

**100.** like the deep thunder of a fresh cloud;
addressing Vishvambhara, it said:

**101.** "Listen, listen, Mahaprabhu, O Vishvambhara:
do not go to Vrindavana — go home.

**102.** Having taken sannyasa, You will travel to the holy places
and in due time You will go to Vrindavana"[^16].

**103.** Hearing such a heavenly voice with their own ears,
the brahmana companions held Him back from going.

**104.** Turning back, Mahaprabhu went home
and little by little, on foot, came to Nadia.

**105.** Bowing at the feet of His mother Shachi,
He sent all His companions to their homes.

**106.** Shachi, with joy in her heart, embraced her son;
tears of love and joy stream from both her eyes.

**107.** Her whole body thrills, her body trembles;
the whole town of Nadia received joy.

**108.** In Vishnupriya's heart there are waves of joy:
she cannot contain herself, her happiness has no limit.

**109.** With joy the Lord came to His own abode —
happily this Lochana Dasa sings the glories of Gora.

*Raga varadi; disha: "Dvijachand!..." (murchha)*

**110.** The deeds [of the Lord] in Navadvipa are a wondrous tale:
the account of Gorachand's glories is steeped in nectar.

**111.** The deeds in Nadia are beyond the reach of the world and of the Vedas;
hearing them is auspicious — all love them.

**112.** Shiva, Shuka, Narada, Lakshmi, Ananta,
tasting this joy, consider themselves fortunate.

**113.** What can I say, wretched and utterly without understanding?
I know neither good nor bad, neither day nor night.

**114.** My conduct is like that of a beast,
therefore I write of myself that I am the lowest.

**115.** The essence of all avataras is the avatara of Gora,
and in it — the preaching of prema in Nadia.

**116.** Bowing down, I beseech the feet of the Vaishnavas:
be merciful — let my lips sing the glories of Gora.

**117.** Do not despise me because I am low:
people say that you are the deliverance of the fallen.

**118.** Out of your own goodness show me mercy:
I long so much to sing with my lips the glories of Gora.

**119.** I bow down to the lotus feet of Gora:
cast on me, even for a moment, a merciful glance.

**120.** Sri Narahari Dasa Thakura is my [master]:
on this alone I rely in speaking of Your glories[^17].

**121.** Otherwise — I, lowest of the low, sinful, wretched —
what right have I to speak of Your glories?

**122.** I am not worthy — show me mercy:
I long so much to speak of Your glories.

**123.** Come what may, I shall certainly tell it:
listen attentively to the secret of Nadia.

**124.** Knowing or not knowing, I speak with great hope:
"The Adi-khanda is ended," says this Lochana Dasa.

*Thus ends the Adi-khanda of the Sri Chaitanya-mangala, composed by Sri Lochana Dasa Thakura.*

[^1]: Cf. Murari 1.15–1.16. In Lochana Dasa's text the Lord meets Ishvara Puri already on the way to the Vishnupada (vv. 57–58), before the rites at the Phalgu and in Gaya itself.
[^2]: Gaya — a holy city in Bihar where pindas are offered to the ancestors. In performing the shraddha the Lord, as on other occasions, "instructs the people" by His own example (cf. Murari 1.15.6).
[^3]: After v. 13 the 1983 edition has one more couplet: Shachi asks Nimai to offer a pinda in Gaya on her behalf as well; A does not have it.
[^4]: In Murari (1.15.8) the river is called Chorandhayaka. Mandara — a holy mountain in Bihar where Madhusudana is worshipped (cf. v. 50).
[^5]: The Lord's companions from Bengal found fault with the local brahmanas, whose customs differed from those of Bengal; cf. Murari 1.15.12: these brahmanas had taken refuge in Madhusudana.
[^6]: The Lord is not subject to illness: Bhaktisiddhanta in his summary says that He "enacted the lila of being cured" in order to teach reverence for brahmanas; cf. Murari 1.15.9–13. The 1903 edition gives in its notes several more couplets from individual manuscripts (among them the Lord recalls how once in His childhood His mother gave Him the water of a brahmana's feet); A and B do not have them.
[^7]: Brahmanya — "kind to brahmanas", a name of the Lord. The sage Bhrigu, testing the gods, struck Vishnu on the chest with his foot, and the Lord bears the mark of his foot as a sign of respect for brahmanas (cf. Bhagavata 10.89). The 1903 edition gives vv. 45–47 only in a note as an additional reading of the printed edition.
[^8]: The 1983 edition: "Thus in the Padma Purana". Bhaktisiddhanta's prose rendering of the shloka (anvaya and translation) is not translated.
[^9]: Punahpuna (modern Punpun) — a holy river on the way to Gaya; Rajagiri (Rajagriha, modern Rajgir) with the pond Brahma-kunda — an ancient holy place in Bihar. Cf. Murari 1.15.13–14.
[^10]: The Lord, Himself the Teacher of the world, speaks as a seeking disciple, teaching people that everyone needs initiation from a genuine guru; according to Bhaktisiddhanta's summary He asked for the mantra in order to show mercy to Ishvara Puri. Cf. Murari 1.15.16–19.
[^11]: That is, the mantra of Krishna, the Lord of the gopis. In Murari (1.15.18) and in Vrindavana Dasa (Chaitanya-bhagavata, Adi 17) it is the ten-syllable mantra.
[^12]: After the initiation the moods of Vraja manifest in the Lord: the mood of Radha (vv. 65–67, 70), His own mood as Krishna (vv. 71–73), the mood of a servant (v. 71). Gaudiya Vaishnavas see in Gaura Krishna Himself, who has taken the mood and the radiance of Radha. After v. 75 the 1983 edition has two more couplets: Ishvara Puri sees the Lord as Krishna with the flute and remembers the words of Madhavendra Puri; the 1903 edition gives them in a note as the reading of the printed edition; A does not have them.
[^13]: The Lord remembered His lila as Rama. According to the tradition of Gaya, Sita, having offered pinda to Dasharatha in Rama's absence, called the Phalgu to witness, but the river lied, and Sita cursed it to flow beneath the sand.
[^14]: The Pretashila, Udichi, Dakshina-manasa, Uttara-manasa, Jihvalola and the Sixteen Altars (v. 82) are holy places of Gaya where the shraddha is performed; cf. Murari 1.16.1–4.
[^15]: Cf. Murari 1.16.6–7. The Lord, as a devotee, laments a seeming lack of love — thus He teaches humility; at once prema awakens in Him (vv. 88–91).
[^16]: Cf. Murari 1.16.8–10. The heavenly voice foretells the Lord's sannyasa and His journey to Vrindavana.
[^17]: Narahari Sarakara Thakura of Shrikhanda is Lochana Dasa's guru. "Your" (vv. 120–122) apparently refers to Gaura (cf. v. 119).
