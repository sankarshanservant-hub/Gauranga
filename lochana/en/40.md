# Madhya-khanda, part 17 (portion 40)

*The section "The Lord's Sannyasa" (conclusion): the Lord has His head shaved and receives the sannyasa mantra from Keshava Bharati; a voice from the sky names Him Sri Krishna Chaitanya; night kirtana with the guru; farewell to Bharati; three days absorbed in prema in Radha-desha; the Lord sends Chandrashekhara back to Navadvipa*

**113.** Again He bows to the best of sannyasis,
opens His heart to him and asks to be instructed in the rite.

**114.** The next day the Lord, taking the guru's leave,
smilingly performs the rites prescribed for sannyasa.

**115.** He performed all the prescribed rites,
His heart rejoicing: "I shall take sannyasa!"

**116.** Acharyaratna himself performs the worship of Krishna,[^1]
and all around the Vaishnavas cry, "Hari, Hari!"

**117.** With joined palms [the Lord] stands before the guru
and, bowing, asks for the sannyasa mantra.

**118.** The Lord had His head shaved — hear of it:
the heart of everyone who hears it aches.

**119.** The hearts of all the Vaishnavas trembled:
while He was being shaved, they covered their faces with cloth.

**120.** The hair cherished by Kamala, the most beautiful in the three worlds,
which with the garland flowed down to His shoulders, mighty as an elephant's;

**121.** which once, dressed in a chuda, enchanted the world,[^2] —
by meditating on it all the devotees live;

**122.** for its sake the cowherd wives gave up their modesty
and shattered, as with a thunderbolt, their fear for caste, family and good name;

**123.** singing its glory, Shiva, Brahma and Narada
count themselves blessed — it is all their wealth.

**124.** Such hair the Lord wishes to shave off, —
all the people weep and do not raise their faces.

**124+1.** Calling a barber, [the Lord] spoke to him humbly:[^3]
"Help Me in the service of Krishna.

**124+2.** Becoming a sannyasi, I shall belong to Krishna:
shave My head — it will be your good fortune."

**125.** The barber does not put his hand on His head:
his whole body trembles with fear.

**125+1.** "I have no such fortune, Lord, — I am utterly lost:[^4]
how can I put my hand on Your head?

**125+2.** Let me get leprosy and all my body rot away,
let me go to hell like Kamsa, — hear me, Gauranga, —

**125+3.** still I cannot put my hand on Your head:
humbly I tell You, hear me, Gaurahari."

**126.** The people of Kantakanagara, women and men,
weep aloud in piteous voices.

**127.** The barber says: "Lord, I pray at Your feet:
how could I dare to put my hand on Your head?

**128.** I have no strength to shave off
this beautiful curling hair that enchants the three worlds.

**129.** One look at it brings coolness to heart and eyes.
Do whatever You will, Lord, only do not shave it.

**130.** There is no one in the world like You:
You are the Lord of all the worlds — my heart has understood."

**131.** Hearing these words, the Lord is displeased;
seeing this, the barber grows afraid at heart

**132.** and pleads again, distressed within:
"How shall I put my hand on this head?

**133.** I fear to offend — my body shakes with fear:
having touched Your head, whose feet shall I touch afterwards?[^5]

**134.** Touching whose feet shall I ply my trade?
I am a lowly barber, of a wretched caste."

**135.** Hearing this, the compassionate Lord
says: "Ply your trade no more."

**135+1.** The Lord says:[^6] "Listen, Haridasa the barber:
shave Me — I shall take sannyasa.

**136.** By Krishna's grace you will pass your life happily,
and at the end your dwelling will be in My abode.

**136+1.** Having shaved Me, take all the instruments[^7]
and give them over to the waters of the Ganga."

**136+2.** Hearing this, Haridasa began to think:
"No good lot ever falls to me:

**136+3.** if I shave Him — ruin all the same,
and if I do not shave Him — I am undone.

**136+4.** I will do it out of love for Him — come what may:
here He is before my eyes — the Supreme Self, who rules dharma and adharma."

**137.** At the time of the shaving that barber received a blessing;
with a stricken heart Lochana Dasa sings of it.

*Raga purabi-sindhura*

**138.** Choosing an auspicious moment, the Lord had His head shaved
and takes sannyasa on the auspicious day of the sankranti.

**139.** At the hour when [the sun] leaves Makara and enters Kumbha,[^8]
the guru speaks the sannyasa mantra.

**140.** All around, the Vaishnavas perform sankirtana,
and the sannyasi speaks the mantra into Vishvambhara's ear.

**141.** Receiving the mantra, Vishvambhara thrilled all over:
the waves of love for Krishna swelled a hundredfold.

**142.** From His reddened eyes tears flow ceaselessly;
now He slaps His arms, now He lets out a roar.

**143.** "I have taken sannyasa!" He says, exulting,
and again and again laughs aloud in the bliss of prema.[^9]

**143+1.** [The people] of Kanchananagara, seeing that beauty,[^10]
knew for certain: this is He who delights in the rasa dance.

**143+2.** Gazing at His face, the devotees dance in bliss;
the Lord Himself dances, Nityananda dances.

**143+3.** Cymbals ring, mridangas sound, the kirtana roars,
and all the people around cry, "Haribol!"

**143+4.** The crest-jewel of dancers, with His skilful companions,
sings the glories of Radha and Krishna, overwhelmed with prema.

**144.** At that very time Bharati Gosani says:
"What will Your name be? Listen, Nimai."

**145.** All the Vaishnavas who were there,
together with the best of sannyasis, began to deliberate;

**146.** each speaks according to his understanding, whatever is in his mind, —
and then an auspicious voice arose in the sky.

**147.** Hearing it, everyone was amazed:
"Give Him the name 'Sri Krishna Chaitanya.'"

**148.** Mahamaya, the goddess Bhagavati, in the form of sleep,
has enveloped all people — their minds are clouded;

**149.** all that they do is [as] in a dream.
The Lord Himself awakens everyone;

**150.** being Himself Krishna, He reveals Krishna to all —
therefore He is called "Sri Krishna Chaitanya."[^11]

**151.** When all heard these words from the mouth of heaven,
the people joyfully raised the cry of Hari's name.

**151+1.** With a joyful heart the Lord cries, "Haribol!":[^12]
"From today My name is Sri Krishna Chaitanya."

**151+2.** Bowing many times at the guru's feet,
Vishvambhara circumambulated him and set out.

**151+3.** Seeing Him leaving, the king of sannyasis
calls: "Here, take the staff, do not delay!"

**151+4.** Hearing the guru's word, [the Lord] came back
and, smiling gently, received the staff from the guru.

**151+5.** The Lord reverently accepted the guru's staff
and bowed to him many times with fervent devotion.

**151+6.** Then Mahaprabhu cries, "Haribol!" —
and the waves of great prema surge up.

**152.** By the guru's order the Lord stayed there that day
and spent it happily in service to the guru.

**153.** At night the Vaishnavas together perform sankirtana;
together with the guru [the Lord] dances enchantingly.

**154.** Keshava Bharati dances in the joy of prema's bliss,
the Lord dances, and all the people cry, "Hari!"

**155.** Both, filled with the bliss of prema, forgot themselves;
both regard the happiness of Brahman as paltry.[^13]

**155+1.** Thus after a while, when the dancing ended,[^14]
the sannyasi sat down and spoke, and Vishvambhara listened:

**155+2.** "Who took the staff from my hand?
Touching the tip of the staff, I want to dance again!"

**155+3.** Saying this, he dances, beside himself —
dances wondrously, brimming with the bliss of prema.

**155+4.** In bliss all the Vaishnavas dance joyfully,
and all the people around cry, "Hari, Hari!"

**156.** Thus in joy and delight the night passes;
rising at dawn, the Lord asks leave to go.

**157.** Circumambulating the guru, He bows:
"I shall go to Nilachala, if I have your sanction."

**158.** The Lord asks permission at the guru's feet, —
and Keshava Bharati's heart throbs.

**159.** His eyes brim with tears of compassion;
at parting he embraces Gorachand:

**160.** "You are the independent Lord, [abiding] in Your own bliss;
out of compassion You walk on foot among people.

**161.** To teach [people] devotion to the guru You perform the prescribed rites,
to establish the dharma of sankirtana.

**162.** To deliver all people You have shown mercy,
and to put me in an awkward place You have taken this sannyasa.[^15]

**163.** That I too may be delivered, Vishvambhara —
this is my plea; keep it in Your heart."

**163+1.** [Bharati] gave leave: "Go to Nilachala, the king of mountains,"[^16]
and Gaurachandra, out of modesty, said nothing more.

**164.** Touching his feet, the Lord set out;
on the road the bliss of prema grew beyond measure.

**165.** In the exultation of prema He calls, "Krishna, Krishna!" —
now He weeps, now He laughs aloud.

**166.** Streams of tears run down His chest —
like the stream of the celestial river down the peak of Sumeru.

**167.** His abundant thrill surpasses the filaments of the kadamba:
His whole body bristles from head to foot.

**168.** He moves along playfully like an intoxicated elephant;
in boundless prema now He sings "Krishna!",

**169.** now He falls to the ground and lies stunned,
now He springs up crying, "Haribol!"

**170.** Now the bhava of a gopi is in Him, now the bhava of a servant;[^17]
now He walks slowly, now He runs swiftly.

**171.** Thus in bliss He knows neither day nor night.
In Radha-desha He did not hear even a trace of Krishna's name,[^18]

**172.** and, not hearing Krishna's name, He was filled with grief
and resolved to enter the water.[^19]

**173.** Seeing this, all the devotees lament:
"Gauranga is going to Goloka! Alas, what will become of us!"

**174.** Then Nityananda Prabhu says with a hero's boldness:
"By my own power I shall keep Chaitanya here."

**175.** There some boys were grazing cows —
Nityananda Prabhu entered their hearts.

**176.** When [the Lord], resolved, went near the water,
one boy suddenly called out, "Hari!"

**177.** Hearing this, Gaurahari turned back
and, holding the boy's hand, urges him: "Say it, say it [again]!

**178.** May the Lord Bhagavan be merciful to you:
you have blessed Me by letting Me hear the name of Hari."

**179.** The Lord floats in the bliss of prema, His heart rejoicing;
going on a little further, the Lord took alms.

**180.** Thus in bliss He knew neither day nor night —
only after three days did He take food and water.

**181.** Thus days and nights passed in the bliss of prema.
[Then the Lord] dismissed Chandrashekhara Acharya:

**181+1.** "All the people of Navadvipa[^20]
weep for Me in distress and keep calling Me.

**181+2.** They do not know for certain that I have taken sannyasa:
tell them all this account of Me."

**182.** The Lord said: "We shall see each other again;
soon we shall meet — do not be downcast."

**183.** Saying this, the Lord went on quickly,
and Sri Chandrashekhara goes away weeping:

**183+1.** "I shall die, I shall die, Lord, not seeing You,[^21]
I shall die, fallen into Navadvipa's ocean of grief!"

**184.** Meanwhile the people of Navadvipa wait, their eyes fixed on the road:
what news will Sri Chandrashekhara bring when he comes?

**185.** Says Lochana: this cannot be told in words.
Sri Chandrashekhara Acharya reaches Navadvipa.

[^1]: Acharyaratna is Chandrashekhara Acharya, who came to Kantakanagara with Nityananda (en/39, v. 43). Cf. Murari 3.2.6.
[^2]: "Once" — in Krishna's lila in Vraja; a chuda is a topknot adorned with a peacock feather. Lochana Dasa speaks of Gaura's hair as the hair of Krishna Himself.
[^3]: Vv. 124+1 — 124+2 are found only in the 1983 edition; in the 1903 edition they are in a footnote, as an additional reading of one manuscript.
[^4]: Vv. 125+1 — 125+3 are found only in the 1983 edition; the 1903 edition does not have them. The second line of v. 125+2 is unclear in the 1983 edition; we understand it as "let me go to hell like Kamsa".
[^5]: A barber's trade is to shave and pare nails, touching people's feet; having touched the Lord's head, he can no longer touch the feet of others.
[^6]: V. 135+1 is found only in the 1983 edition; the 1903 edition does not have it. The barber is called Haridasa here — not to be confused with Haridasa Thakura.
[^7]: Vv. 136+1 — 136+4 are found only in the 1983 edition; in the 1903 edition they are in a footnote, as an additional reading of one manuscript (with the lines of v. 136+3 in reverse order).
[^8]: That is, at the sun's passage from Capricorn into Aquarius — at the end of the solar month of Magha (January–February). Cf. Murari 3.2.10.
[^9]: Cf. Murari 3.2.11. In the 1983 edition a new chapter, "Acceptance of Sannyasa", begins at v. 138; in Bhaktisiddhanta's edition the section continues.
[^10]: Vv. 143+1 — 143+4 are found only in the 1983 edition; in the 1903 edition they are in a footnote, as an additional reading of one manuscript (with one more couplet: Gadadhara and Narahari dance beside the Lord, and all the Vaishnavas dance around Gora).
[^11]: Chaitanya means "consciousness", "awakening". Mahamaya keeps in oblivion the living beings who have turned away from Krishna; the Lord awakens them to Krishna consciousness. In the Chaitanya-bhagavata (Madhya 28) Keshava Bharati himself gives the Lord this name, with the same explanation; in Lochana Dasa it is proclaimed by a voice from the sky.
[^12]: Vv. 151+1 — 151+6 are found only in the 1983 edition; in the 1903 edition vv. 151+1 — 151+5 stand in the main text and v. 151+6 is in a footnote, as part of an additional reading of one manuscript. Cf. Murari 3.2.12: the guru himself puts the staff, together with the cloth, into the Lord's hand.
[^13]: Cf. Murari 3.2.13–14.
[^14]: Vv. 155+1 — 155+4 are found only in the 1983 edition; in the 1903 edition they stand in the main text. Cf. Murari 3.2.15–18: Bharati relates that someone snatched the staff from his hands and told him to dance.
[^15]: In Bhaktisiddhanta's edition v. 162 is misnumbered 161. Bharati speaks in humility: the Lord, the guru of the world, has placed him in the position of His guru (cf. en/39, v. 85).
[^16]: V. 163+1 is found only in the 1983 edition; in the 1903 edition it stands in the main text. Nilachala ("the Blue Mountain") is Puri, the abode of Jagannatha.
[^17]: Cf. Murari 3.3.17.
[^18]: Radha-desha (Rarh) is the region west of the Ganga. Vv. 164–180 — cf. Murari 3.3.1–10, 16–18; Chaitanya-bhagavata, Antya 1.
[^19]: As in Murari (3.3.6), the Lord speaks and acts in the mood of a devotee for whom life without the name of Hari is unbearable; this is a manifestation of prema, not the despair of a conditioned soul. The Lord's body is spiritual, and He, the independent one, is free to manifest and withdraw it.
[^20]: Vv. 181+1 — 181+2 are found only in the 1983 edition; in the 1903 edition they are in a footnote, as an additional reading of one manuscript. Vv. 181–185 — cf. Murari 3.3.11–14, 19.
[^21]: V. 183+1 is found only in the 1983 edition; in the 1903 edition it is in a footnote, as an additional reading of one manuscript (there: "burnt in the fire of Navadvipa's grief").
