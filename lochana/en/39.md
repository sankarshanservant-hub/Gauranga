# Madhya-khanda, part 16 (portion 39)

*The section "The Lord's Sannyasa" (beginning): at dawn the Lord leaves Navadvipa and swims across the Ganga; the lament of Shachi, Vishnupriya and the devotees; Nityananda with Chandrashekhara and others follows the Lord; the Lord in Kantakanagara with Keshava Bharati; Bharati hesitates to give Him sannyasa, and the Lord whispers the sannyasa mantra into his ear; the grief of the people of Kantakanagara; the Lord asks for their blessing*

## The Lord's Sannyasa

> *Summary by Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati.* [Sri Gaurasundara, having consoled the devotees] with instruction in the truth, [left home] and, in order to take sannyasa, crossed the Ganga and came to Katwa, to Keshava Bharati. [Mother Shachi and] Vishnupriya [were overwhelmed] in separation from the Lord; Nityananda Prabhu, consoling them, came with Chandrashekhara Acharya, Damodara Pandita and other devotees to the Lord in Kantakanagara. The Lord asked Keshava Bharati for sannyasa, but he would not agree to give Him sannyasa. Then Bhagavan Himself, [wishing to receive] the sannyasa mantra and knowing what was in Bharati's heart, on some pretext first spoke the sannyasa mantra into Keshava Bharati's ear, and then Bharati agreed to give Him sannyasa. All the inhabitants of Kantakanagara — children, old people, youths, women and men — lamented bitterly on seeing the Lord's sannyasa; Sri Gaurachandra consoled them and, [bearing Himself] as a devotee, asked them for [the blessing of] devotion to Krishna. [A voice from the sky proclaimed the name "Sri Krishna Chaitanya": the Lord] awakened [all to Krishna] — therefore His name in sannyasa became "Sri Krishna Chaitanya". Then, having taken sannyasa, Sriman Mahaprabhu, absorbed in prema, wandered for three days in Radha-desha.[^1]

*Raga karunashri; disha: "O Lord, O Gora, alas! O Gorachand, alas!"*

**1.** Rising at dawn and performing His morning duties,
Gaurahari settled His resolve: "I shall take sannyasa."

**2.** "In Kantakanagara lives Bharati Gosani —
there Pandita Nimai will take sannyasa."[^2]

**3.** Vishvambhara fixed this with all His heart
and, setting out, noted that His breath was flowing through the right nostril.[^3]

**4.** Mahaprabhu went to the Ganga;
swimming across the Ganga, He went away, leaving Navadvipa.[^4]

**5.** Bowing to the Ganga, He leaves Navadvipa —
as if a thunderbolt struck everyone on the head.

**6.** As if the sun had hidden in the middle of the day,
as if the swans had left the lake — where have they gone?

**7.** As if life had suddenly left the body,
as if the bee had forsaken its love for the lotus.

**8.** Navadvipa was filled with the grief of separation:
a mountain of sorrow seemed to crush everyone.

**9.** Household and townsfolk, Shachi and Vishnupriya
fall senseless, throwing themselves on the ground.

**10.** Shachi-devi weeps, holding Vishnupriya on her lap —
and Vishnupriya lies as if dead:

**11.** the body is there, but life has left it.
Shachi and Vishnupriya weep, rolling on the ground.

**12.** Shachi-devi weeps and calls: "Nimai!" —
her heart blazes as if on fire:

**13.** "All around is empty, all is shrouded in darkness —
how shall I live in this dreadful house?

**14.** This house comes to swallow me;
the words of friends and kin are like poison.

**15.** No one will call me 'mother' any more;
even Yama will not take me — he too has forgotten me.

**16.** What sorrow has befallen me — my son has left me!
Making me a mother bereft of children, where has he gone?

**17.** Alas, alas, Nimai, you have become pitiless!
To what lands have you gone, my child? Who will bring you back?

**18.** My heart breaks, my son, remembering your sweetness:
never again will you call me 'mother', Gaurahari!

**19.** Leaving me helpless, where have you gone, my son?
It must have been in your mind to give your mother pain.

**20.** You studied, you heard [the scriptures], my son — and this is what you learned:
to make your mother helpless and wretched!

**21.** Where have you fled, abandoning Vishnupriya?
The love of all the devotees you counted as nothing!"

**22.** Vishnupriya weeps, her heart senseless;
now she rises, now she falls — her mind distracted.

**23.** She puts on no clothes, does not bind her hair,
she sobs aloud like a madwoman.

**24.** Pressing to her bosom the garland from the Lord's body, [she says]:
"Light a fire — I shall burn in it and die."

**25.** She cannot recount His qualities — she dies in the depths of her soul
and repeats only one thing: "What was in my fate [has come to pass]."

**26.** "Lord, all Your qualities are sweeter than nectar —
and now all of it has turned to fire."

**27.** Of their intimate joys she cannot even speak —
her heart burns, and she weeps in a piercing voice.

**28.** Around her the devotees are dying of anguish within —
what can be said? they cannot restrain themselves.

**29.** Gathering all their strength, they say to her softly, softly:
"How can we console you? Steady your life.

**30.** What you have seen, what you have heard all this time —
remember all that and steady your life.

**31.** Do you know whose He is — Bhagavan?
You have heard of all the former avataras.

**32.** His deeds are beyond the reach of the world and the Vedas;
by great fortune you bear the name of His kin.

**33.** Whatever He commanded each of us — so let us live:
carry out that command with a firm heart."

**34.** When the devotees spoke such words,
she listened and, aching at heart, held back her weeping.

**35.** Then Nityananda, gathering all the devotees,
takes counsel: "Where shall we go to see Him?"

**36.** Someone says: "Let us go round all the holy places —
somewhere we shall see Gorachand."

**37.** Someone says: "Let us go to Vrindavana, to Varanasi,
let us go to Nilachala, where the sannyasis live."

**38.** [Nityananda says:] "In Kanchananagara lives Bharati Gosani —
there Pandita Nimai will take sannyasa.

**39.** These words I once heard from the Lord's lips,
but I am not sure they are true.

**40.** If on a false report everyone rushes there, [it will be bad];
first I shall learn the truth and tell everyone.

**41.** Give me a few steadfast devotees to go with me —
I shall seize my Lord Gauranga and bring Him back."

**42.** Then all the devotees, considering,
gave Him several of the foremost as companions.

**43.** Sri Chandrashekhara Acharya, Pandita Damodara,
Vakreshvara and others set out in haste.

**44.** With them Nityananda departs,
having consoled the hearts of Shachi and Vishnupriya.

**45.** Meanwhile Gaurahari pressed swiftly on
with a beautiful gait, like a myriad maddened elephants.

**46.** Streams of prema pour from His eyes,
the golden youth's body thrills, its hairs on end.

**47.** Binding His hair with His upper cloth, the Lord
[goes] as if [Krishna] to fight the wrestlers of Mathura.[^5]

**48.** Overwhelmed by the bhava of separation from Radha, [He cries]:
"Where is Radha? Where has my Gokula gone?"[^6]

**49.** Now His pace slows,
now He slaps His arms, looking around on all sides.

**50.** Thus, absorbed in prema, goes along the road
my Lord Jagannatha, the guru of all.

**51.** The Lord Vishvambhara came to Kanchananagara,
where lived Keshava Bharati, the best of sannyasis.

**52.** With the highest devotion He bows [to him],
and the sannyasi, rising respectfully, utters: "Narayana!"[^7]

**53.** Both deem [the meeting] great good fortune and converse warmly;
Vishvambhara says: "Give Me sannyasa."

**54.** While the two were thus together,
Nityananda arrived with Chandrashekhara and the others.

**55.** Bowing to the sannyasi, they bow to the Lord;
the Lord, smiling, says: "It is good that you have come.

**56.** With your coming all is auspicious for Me:
I shall take sannyasa — and My life will be fulfilled."

**57.** Saying this, He again addresses Bharati,
bowing and pleading for sannyasa.

**58.** Bharati says: "Listen, listen, Vishvambhara:
my heart trembles at giving You sannyasa.

**59.** Such a beautiful body, such young years —
since birth You have known no sorrow or hardship.

**60.** You do not even have offspring —
it is not for me to give You sannyasa.

**61.** When You are past fifty and the passions subside,
then it will be reasonable to give You sannyasa."[^8]

**62.** Hearing this, the Lord says softly:
"What can I say in your presence?

**63.** Do not dissemble with Me, jewel among sannyasis:[^9]
who but you knows the truth of dharma and adharma?

**64.** In samsara this birth as a human being is rare,
and in it rare is devotion to Krishna, the highest dharma.

**65.** And rarer still the company of devotees;
and the human body perishes in a moment.

**66.** If, while I delay, this body passes away —
when then shall I have the company of Vaishnavas?

**67.** Do not dissemble with Me — give Me sannyasa:
by your grace may I become a servant of Krishna."

**68.** Saying this, [He stands]: His merciful eyes reddened,
brimming with tears, His face full of anguish.

**69.** Roaring, He surpasses a lion in might;
His whole body is bhava itself, marked with every auspicious sign.

**70.** "Hari, Hari!" He calls with the thunder of a cloud,
tears of prema flow ceaselessly from His eyes.

**71.** Standing thrice-bent, He calls: "The flute, the flute!";
now, crying "The circle of the rasa!", He bends His body.

**72.** He calls "Govardhana! Radha-kunda!" and laughs;
the sannyasi was astonished, trembling at heart.

**73.** Reflecting in his heart, the king of sannyasis speaks —
he understood within: "This is not a good deed for me.

**74.** He is the guru of the world, the Lord of the world —
and He will bow to me with joined palms, calling me guru."

**75.** Reasoning thus, the sannyasi answered:
"If You want to take sannyasa, go home.

**76.** Take leave of Your mother in person;
and Your virtuous wife — go to her.

**77.** Having taken leave of everyone in person
and made everyone understand, come to me."

**78.** But in his mind: "Having sent Gorachand away,
I shall leave my seat and go elsewhere."

**79.** Bhagavan, the Knower of hearts, understood this intention
and said with a smile: "I shall carry out your command."

**80.** Mahaprabhu set out for Navadvipa;
watching Him, the sannyasi thought to himself:

**81.** "He in whose pores hosts of universes dwell —
where, to what land, shall I flee from Him?

**82.** Deluded in mind, I look and do not see:
He is the life of all, the witness of all."

**83.** Thinking thus, the sannyasi called Gaurahari
and began to speak, entreating Him:

**84.** "I shall say one more word, listen, Vishvambhara:
I am very much afraid to give You sannyasa.

**85.** You are the guru of the world: who is Your guru?
Why do You needlessly put me in a false position?"

**86.** Hearing this, Vishvambhara Raya wept
and in longing clasped the sannyasi's feet:

**87.** "Why do you speak harsh words to one who bows before you?
Even dying I shall not let go of your feet.

**88.** All that you say to Me is to test My mind.
I have one request — hear My words.

**89.** One day at the end of the night I had a dream:
a brahmana told Me the sannyasa mantra.

**90.** See now — is it this or not?" —
and saying this, He speaks the mantra into Bharati's ear.

**91.** Thus He spoke the mantra into the sannyasi's ear:
on a plausible pretext the independent [Lord] Himself became the guru.[^10]

**91+1.** Hearing the mantra, the best of sannyasis was filled with prema:[^11]
trembling, thrilling, in tears, he says, "Radha-Krishna!"

**91+2.** Again and again he cries out: "Vrindavana! Yamuna!" —
he understood: "This is Krishna Himself, the son of Shachi.

**91+3.** Love for Him is the highest fortune;
dharma without love for Krishna is base."

**92.** Bharati Gosani understood everything:
"I shall give You sannyasa, listen, Nimai."

**93.** Hearing this, the Lord dances in joy
and cries "Hari, Hari!" in a deep voice like thunder.

**94.** On Gaura's golden body the hairs stood on end in rows —
the sweetness of His body seemed to spread out a market of nectar.

**95.** From His reddened eyes tears flow ceaselessly;
seeing this, all the people cry out in grief.

**95+1.** From Navadvipa came Gadadhara and Narahari[^12]
and joined [them], crying "Hari, Hari!"

**95+2.** Many times they prostrated themselves [before the Lord],
and Shachi's son, smiling, embraced them.

**95+3.** The Lord says: "It is good that you have come:
by Krishna's grace you have met [with Me].

**95+4.** You two are My companions from beginning to end;
seeing you, My heart rejoices."

**95+5.** Looking at Gaura's face, the two great souls weep;
both stand motionless to His right and left.

**96.** The people of Kanchananagara run to see;
whoever sees [Him] — his heart and eyes are refreshed.

**97.** Old people, the blind, women, men,
the learned and utter fools;

**98.** children run, and young women of noble families —
so secluded they had never seen even their own shadow.[^13]

**99.** One, with a pitcher on her hip, has stopped and stares;
one who cannot walk hurries along leaning on a stick.

**100.** The lame, the infirm, pregnant women —
seeing His holy body, they revile the sannyasi.

**100+1.** "Who gives sannyasa to such a boy?[^14]
It does not befit a sannyasi — people will laugh.

**100+2.** Hard is his heart, a pitiless man:
let us not keep him in the town!" — so they said.

**100+3.** All revile the sannyasi again and again,
yet looking at Gora's face, all are in boundless joy.

**101.** "Blessed, blessed!" people say, extolling His beauty:
"At last we have seen something so wondrous!

**102.** Blessed, blessed is the mother who bore [such] a son in her womb:
she is like Devaki, of whom we have heard of old.

**103.** What fortunate woman obtained such a husband?
In the three worlds there is none her equal in fortune.

**104.** Seeing His beauty, we cannot turn our eyes away —
how shall we bear His sannyasa?

**105.** How will His mother live?
Hearing of this, she will die at once.

**105+1.** We think He has neither mother nor father:[^15]
He is Himself unfailing bliss (Achyuta-ananda), the essence of all the Vedas.

**105+2.** Has He perhaps lost Radha in Vrindavana?
Searching for Her, He wanders weeping and weeping.

**105+3.** Because of that separation He is taking sannyasa:
we know for certain — this is the son of Nanda."

**106.** Thinking thus, all the people weep.
The Lord calls them and says: "Do not grieve.

**107.** Bless Me, mothers and fathers:
I long to lay My head at Krishna's feet.

**108.** She who has her own husband longs for him
and seeks means to bind his heart.

**109.** Beauty, youth, all this rasa and loveliness —
all is blessed when it serves one's own husband.

**110.** Consider this, all of you, in your hearts:
without a husband, all a young woman has is in vain.

**111.** Apart from Krishna's feet I have no other refuge:
giving My whole Self, I shall serve the Lord of My life."[^16]

**112.** Saying this, Mahaprabhu wept,
but in a moment He held everything back [within Himself].

[^1]: The OCR of the summary is badly garbled; gaps are filled by sense and from the table of contents in the edition's preface (ch. 13: "consolation of the devotees by instruction in the truth; leaving home, the Lord goes to Kantakanagara, to Keshava Bharati; Nityananda Prabhu consoles Shachi and Vishnupriya; Nityananda's coming to Kantakanagara with Chandrashekhara Acharya, Damodara Pandita and other devotees; the Lord's request for the sannyasa mantra, Bharati's refusal and consent; the Lord speaks the sannyasa mantra into Bharati's ear; the grief of the villagers; the Lord consoles them and, as a devotee, asks for devotion to Krishna; the Lord's sannyasa name — Sri Krishna Chaitanya; after sannyasa, wandering in Radha-desha absorbed in prema"). The summary runs ahead: the end of the section is in the next portion. In the 1983 edition the disha is longer: "Why have You become like this, Gauranga, why? Why have You given up the dress of the best of dancers? Stand just a moment on the bank of the Ganga, Nimai, let me gaze at Your moon-face — and then go. If You will keep one word of mine, Nimai — no need of sannyasa, stay at home. Do not become a sannyasi, Nimai, do not become a vairagi, do not leave Your unfortunate mother. Your mother calls: stay, O Gauranga; do not go leaving Your mother, O Gauranga!"
[^2]: Kantakanagara is Katwa, on the west bank of the Ganga. Below (vv. 38, 51, 96) Lochana Dasa calls it Kanchananagara; the 1983 edition has that name here too.
[^3]: According to svara-shastra, setting out when the breath flows through the right nostril is a good omen.
[^4]: Cf. Murari 3.1.7: "Bhagavan crossed the divine river and went away."
[^5]: That is, like Krishna entering the arena against Kamsa's wrestlers in Mathura.
[^6]: Lochana Dasa here depicts the Lord in the mood of Krishna pining for Radha; according to the teaching of the Gaudiya Vaishnavas (Krishnadasa Kaviraja, Bhaktisiddhanta), Gaura is Krishna in the bhava of Radha, tasting Her love for Krishna; so Bhaktisiddhanta also interprets a similar passage above (en/35, note 5).
[^7]: "Narayana!" is the customary greeting of sannyasis.
[^8]: Bharati speaks by the general rule of sannyasa, that with age the senses grow calm. Worldly passions have no bearing on the Lord, whose body and senses are spiritual; in the lila Bharati speaks to Him as to a young brahmana.
[^9]: Maya here means "dissembling, pretence": the Lord asks Bharati not to evade Him with plausible excuses. So also in v. 67.
[^10]: Cf. Murari 3.2.7–9: "Thus the one Lord of the worlds… on a plausible pretext gave initiation to His own guru." On the meaning of the sannyasa mantra see en/35, note 10. In the lila Keshava Bharati is the Lord's sannyasa-guru; the Lord, the guru of the world, Himself accepts him as guru in order to establish reverence for the guru.
[^11]: Vv. 91+1 — 91+3 are found only in the 1983 edition; in the 1903 edition they are in a footnote, as an additional reading of one manuscript (with one more couplet).
[^12]: Vv. 95+1 — 95+5 are found only in the 1983 edition; in the 1903 edition they are in a footnote, as an additional reading of one manuscript. Narahari Sarakara is Lochana Dasa's guru. The Chaitanya-bhagavata (Madhya 28) also speaks of Gadadhara at Katwa.
[^13]: Lit. "so beautiful that they do not recognise their own shadow" — that is, they live in seclusion and never go out in public.
[^14]: Vv. 100+1 — 100+3 are found only in the 1983 edition; in the 1903 edition they are in a footnote, as an additional reading of one manuscript.
[^15]: Vv. 105+1 — 105+3 are found only in the 1983 edition; in the 1903 edition they are in a footnote, as an additional reading of one manuscript. Vv. 96–107 — cf. Murari 3.1.10–18 and 3.2.2.
[^16]: The Lord speaks in the mood of a gopi: Krishna is the one husband of all souls (cf. en/38, note 7).
