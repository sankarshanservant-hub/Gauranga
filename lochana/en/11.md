# Adi-khanda, part 3 (portion 11)

*Balya-lila (continued): Nimai pretends not to hear His mother; He sits again on the discarded pots, throws a brick at His mother and brings two coconuts; Shachi for a moment realizes that her son is Narayana, but forgets it again in love for her son and recites protective names over Him; Nimai cries for the moon from the sky; His play with a puppy*

*Raga shri; disha: "O-ki ho re, Gauranga, jaya, jaya!" (murchha). "O-ki na, my prema for Gauranga — nectar and bliss! Ki na, my Gauranga, ki are, jaya, jaya!"*

**208.** So day by day, changing every moment,
His body grows, shapely as Mount Sumeru.

**209.** The sweetness of His speech is like a stream of nectar;
hearing it, Shachi-devi rejoices at heart.

**210.** On the pretext of conversation the queen wants to hear Him speak;
the Lord says: "I can't hear what you say."

**211.** Shachi calls Him loudly, in great merriment;
"I can't hear!" says Gora, who wears the forest garland.

**212.** Mother Shachi, enchanted by parental love (vatsalya),
angrily seizes a switch and runs like a madwoman:

**213.** "Today You won't listen to [my] words, like a naughty boy —
in my old age You won't even give me rice to eat!"

**214.** And some days later the son of Shachi,
being stubborn, does not listen to His mother's words.

**215.** Shachi-devi grew angry, stared at Him fixedly,
and ran to catch Him with a switch in her hand.

**216.** Vishvambhara ran off to the impure place
where useless earthen pots are thrown away.

**217.** Seeing it, His mother strikes her head with her hand;
Shachi laments and scolds Him with harsh words.

**218.** Vishvambhara grew angrier still at heart:
He climbed onto the pots heaped one upon another and stood on them.

**219.** Hearing angry words, He does the opposite;
understanding this, His mother spoke gently:

**220.** "Come, come here, child, leave this disgusting business!
It is not fitting for You — it is not the dharma of a brahmana.

**221.** You are a brahmana's son, and of a noble (kulina) family besides;
what will people say when they hear of such shameful conduct?

**222.** Come, come, child, bathe in the water of the Ganga;
Your mother's heart is breaking — come, climb into my arms.

**223.** Otherwise I will throw myself into the Ganga and die —
and You will go weeping from house to house.

**224.** Your body is gold of the finest purity, tested on the touchstone:
why do You smear soot on such a beautiful body?

**225.** Leave this impure, foul place, my child:
the soot on Your body is like the stain on the moon."

**226.** Hearing this, Vishvambhara, the treasury of virtues, grew angry:
"Again and again I tell you — and still you do not understand!

**227.** Saying 'impure, impure', you speak ill words;
what is pure and what impure — judge that first concerning Me!"

**228.** Saying this, He took in His hand a brick lying before Him
and struck His mother on the head with the brick.

**229.** At the blow Shachi-rani fell into a seeming swoon;[^1]
"Mother, mother!" He Himself weeps.

**230.** Hearing the loud weeping, the women of the town
who were nearby came running at once.

**231.** Sprinkling Ganga water on her face, they brought her round;
as soon as she came to, she called: "Vishvambhara!"

**232.** Opening her arms, Shachi took her son on her lap;
coming out of her swoon, she forgot what had happened before.

**233.** Vishvambhara weeps, looking at His mother;
then a lovely woman said with a laugh,

**234.** taking Vishvambhara by the chin:
"Go and bring your mother two coconuts —

**235.** then your mother Shachi will live;
otherwise she will die — listen to me."

**236.** Hearing this, Vishvambhara was glad
and at once brought a pair of coconuts —

**237.** their stalks just broken off, fresh and full of juice;
He placed the two coconuts before His mother.

**238.** Seeing this, the women were amazed:
"Where did a child get coconuts in a moment?"

**239.** There was a lovely, playful woman there;
very softly she asks Vishvambhara:

**240.** "You are a child — where did You get the coconuts?
I have understood something about Your doings!"

**241.** Hearing such words, Vishvambhara Raya
gave a fierce "hum!" and clasped His mother's neck.

**242.** Coming to herself, Shachi took her son on her lap
and covered His lotus face with countless kisses.

**243.** With the end of her cloth she wiped His face and body
and washed His holy body with the water of the heavenly river.

**244.** Having bathed Him as if with an abhisheka of Ganga water,
she gazes in wonder at her son's face:

**245.** deep as the ocean, with the splendour of millions of suns;
His twenty nails shine like millions of moons;

**246.** beauty surpassing millions of Kamas, a graceful body,
playful, curving eyes, brows like Kama's bow.

**247.** The Lord of all the worlds has appeared on earth —
seeing it, the mother felt awe in her heart.

**248.** The secret of the past came back: when she was carrying Him in her womb,
she had seen the gods in celestial vehicles.

**249.** And all that He had done before in His childhood play —
all of it now came back to her mind.

**250.** She knew for certain: this is the radiant, eternal,
untainted, pure Narayana, without [mundane] form,[^2]

**251.** all-pervading, bearer of all energies, satisfied in Himself,
the incomparable object of meditation of the great yogis.

**252.** "No one can measure my good fortune —
not Brahma, nor Maheshvara, nor the other gods:

**253.** this son of mine is the One whom all worship!"
Saying this, she took Gauraraya into her arms.

**254.** But as soon as Shachi took Gaurahari into her arms,
in her love for her son Shachi-devi forgot His majesty.[^3]

**255.** Shachi came home in wonder:
"What god has manifested through my son?"

**256.** Thinking this, she ties a protective thread and, touching His body,
says: "Janardana, Hrishikesha, Govinda!

**257.** May the Sudarshana disc guard Your head,
may Narayana protect Your eyes, nose and mouth;

**258.** may Lord Gadadhara guard Your chest,
may Lord Giridhara guard Your arms;

**259.** may Damodara guard Your belly and groin,
may Lord Nrisimha guard Your navel;

**260.** may Lord Trivikrama guard both Your knees,
may Dharadhara guard Your feet."[^4]

**261.** Mother Shachi blows upon all His limbs
and, in love for her son, is utterly beside herself.

**262.** So in joy and merriment the day passed;
the blessed hour came — evening fell.

**263.** Happily Shachi left Gaurahari in the courtyard
and sent the servants and maids to do the evening tasks.

**264.** So the day came to an end, evening fell,
and the full moon of the full-moon day rose in the sky.

**265.** Then Vishvambhara, clever and all-knowing,
began to call "Mama, mama!" as if He knew nothing.

**266.** Shachi says: "Don't cry at evening time;
whatever You want I will give — do as I say."

**267.** The Lord says: "Pluck the moon and give it to Me!"
Laughing, Shachi replies: "Oh, You silly child!

**268.** What a son I've been given in my house!
Who can catch the moon in the sky?"

**269.** The Lord says: "You said, 'Whatever You want I will give' —
why then did you say such words?

**270.** That is why I wanted to take the moon."
Saying this, He wept loudly.

**271.** Clutching the end of her sari, He cries and throws tantrums of every kind,
stamps His feet, rubs His eyes.

**272.** Clinging to His mother's neck, Gora Raya weeps:
He wants the moon from the sky to play with.

**273.** Now He sulks, now rolls on the ground, now tears His mother's hair;
all grey with dust, He beats His own head with His hand.

**274.** Seeing it, His mother says: "Silly child,
Your doings are a marvel to me!

**275.** Where can I catch the moon in the sky?
How many such moons there are on Your body!

**276.** Look: the moon has grown pale with shame —
without thinking, it rose in front of You.

**277.** Not knowing of the rising of the moon of Navadvipa,
it was ashamed and has gone to hide in the clouds.

**278.** Listen: the bogey[^5] has come to Navadvipa!
Don't cry, my child, my life!"

**279.** Saying this, she takes Him in her arms and kisses His face,
and, happy in the bliss of love, the goddess forgets herself.

**280.** In joy and merriment Shachi, overwhelmed by her good fortune,
watching her son's play, cannot tell one direction from another.

**281.** Shachi's heart rejoices, her speech falters;
happily this Lochana Dasa sings of Gora's virtues.

*Raga dhanashi.[^6] "Glory, glory, glory to Shachi's son, the youthful source of bliss! With the boys He plays in many ways, performing childish pastimes."*

**282.** Taking along all the lively children,
He plays various games;
with the boys, in childish sport,
He performs playful pastimes.

**283.** As they played, suddenly [they saw] there
two or four puppies;
the fun grew: choosing one,
Gaurahari took it.

**284.** The boys who were with Him said to Him:
"Listen, listen, Vishvambhara:
You've left us the ugly ones and taken the good one for Yourself —
we won't play, we're going home!"

**285.** Then Vishvambhara replied:
"This puppy belongs to everyone;
all of you play with it together,
but it will live at My house."

**286.** Saying this, He took the puppy
and went to His own house;
at home He put a rope round its neck
and tied it on the raised step of the house.

**287.** Meanwhile Vishvambhara's mother,
having finished her housework,
went to the bank of the Ganga to bathe
together with the women of the town.

**288.** Then Vishvambhara, finding the house empty,
took the puppy
and plays with the boys in every way,
grey with dust.

**289.** As they played with the boys,
two of them quarrelled;
then Gaurahari, taking one's side,
began to scold the other:

**290.** "Every day you come and start a quarrel —
what sort of nature is that?
I see what you are and how you behave:
you are a puppy thief!"

**291.** Then that boy, angry at heart,
ran outside
and shouted at the top of his voice before Shachi,
choking with rage:

**292.** "Listen, your Vishvambhara
has taken a puppy:
now He holds it in His arms, now hugs it round the neck —
come and see for yourself!"

**293.** Hearing the boy's words,
Shachi-rani hurried home
and sees with her own eyes:
Vishvambhara is holding the puppy in His arms.

**294.** Striking her head with her hand, the mother says:
"I can't make out Your games!
There are all kinds of games, and You — how perverse! —
are playing with a puppy.

**295.** Your father is a most pious man,
and You are his son:
what will people say? With a puppy
You play — what pleasure do You find in it?

**296.** You are a brahmana's son —
yet there is nothing of such conduct in You;
whoever hears of this — who will speak well of it?
It is a spear in my heart.

**297.** You are so beautiful —
what joy is there in smearing Yourself with dust?
I speak to You, and You lower Your eyes —
may my mouth be burned!

**298.** Your face surpasses many moons,
Your body is steady lightning;
yet You care nothing for fine clothes, You smear dust on Your body,
You keep company with bad boys."

**299.** In anger Shachi-devi, biting her lip,
scolds the boy:
"Go home, take the puppy,
make a present of it to your father and mother!"

**300.** Saying this, she looks at her son's face
and calls Him, beside herself with joy:
"Come, come, child, climb into my arms,
let me kiss Your face!

**301.** Let the puppy go, child,
and bathe in the Ganga's water;
it is already midday — aren't You hungry?
How much grief You cause me!

**302.** Or else tie up the puppy, child,
and go to bathe;
in the afternoon You can play and take the puppy,
but now eat something.

**303.** Your face, a golden lotus, has grown pale,
as if withered in the sun;
drops of sweat stand on the tip of Your nose —
it breaks my heart to see!"

**304.** Hearing His mother's answer, Vishvambhara
laughed, stood up and says:
"If My puppy goes anywhere,
then you yourself will find out!"

**305.** Saying this, Hari hugged His mother's neck
and goes to bathe;
she brushed off His dust, wiped His face
and rubbed His body with fragrant oil.

**306.** To bathe He goes to the bank of the Ganga,
taking His companions along,
and in the water of the heavenly river, in great merriment,
He frolics and splashes.

**307.** They all splash one another with water
like frolicking elephants;
and Gora's body is like Mount Sumeru —
unshakeable, wondrous.

**308.** Meanwhile Shachi-devi, after some thought,
let the puppy go;
seeing its own mother, it ran after her —
who knows where it went.

**309.** There was a boy there —
he ran to the bank of the Ganga:
"Listen, Vishvambhara: your mother
has let the puppy go!"

**310.** Hearing the boy's words,
He came running at once
and went to look in the place
where the puppy had been.

**311.** He looks in all four directions — no puppy;
His heart blazed with anger.
He weeps loudly and scolds His mother,
grieving for the puppy:

**312.** "Listen, foolish one, what have you done, mother?
What grief you have caused Me!
The most beautiful, the best of puppies —
how could you give it to someone?"

**313.** Shachi-rani says: "But I don't know
[where] your puppy is;
it was here — someone took it somewhere;
what a thieving boy!

**314.** Why do You cry
over a puppy?
Whoever took it, I'll take the trouble
to ask for it and bring it tomorrow.

**315.** Enough now! I swear to You
by my own oath:
I will bring the puppy to You —
don't cry, don't cry now!"

**316.** Saying this, she wiped His face
and took her son in her arms;
gazing at His beautiful face, joyfully
she covered Him with countless kisses.

**317.** Rubbing His body and making Him clean,
she bathed Him in the Ganga's water
and fed Him well with sweets — sandesh and modakas,
thickened milk and bananas.

**318.** On His head are three tufts, with five knots on them,
tied together;
in His eyes is kajal, a clear, fine line;
His glance delights the world.

**319.** A red-bordered dhoti is wrapped round His waist,
its end swinging down to His feet;
on His breast a pearl necklace,
on His forehead a sandal tilaka.

**320.** Armlets and bracelets, priceless jewels,
magara anklets on His feet;
He goes to the boys to play,
holding a milk laddu in His hand.

**321.** His gait is beautiful, surpassing an elephant's,
His speech deep and sweet;
Gora, king of the twice-born, among the boys —
the moon surrounded by stars.[^7]

**322.** In such pastimes the Lord plays —
the gods watch and are swept away in bliss;
cats and dogs the Lord touches —
Lochana Dasa marvels.

[^1]: So in Bhaktisiddhanta's edition (*kapata murchchha* — "a feigned, seeming swoon"); the 1983 and 1903 editions have simply "she fainted". In Murari Gupta (1.06.22) Shachi loses consciousness from the blow. The word "seeming" indicates that this is no worldly misfortune but play: the Lord's mother is His eternal associate, and her swoon is part of the lila.
[^2]: The words "untainted, pure, without form" (*nirlepa, niranjana, nirakara*) are taken from the language of the Upanishads. The Gaudiya Vaishnavas, following the *Chaitanya-charitamrita* (Madhya 6, the conversation with Sarvabhauma), understand such epithets as denying only material qualities and a material form: the Lord has an eternal form full of being, knowledge and bliss — the very form Shachi sees before her (vv. 245–246).
[^3]: Cf. the *Bhagavata* (10.8.43): having seen the universe in Krishna's mouth, Yashoda by the Lord's will again forgot His majesty and saw in Him only her son. This is the working of yogamaya, which nourishes vatsalya-rasa, not worldly ignorance.
[^4]: A protective prayer assigning names of Vishnu to each limb — as the gopis protected the infant Krishna after the Putana episode (*Bhagavata* 10.6.21–29). Giridhara — "the lifter of the hill" (Krishna); Trivikrama — Vamana; Dharadhara — "the upholder of the earth" (Varaha or Shesha).
[^5]: *Hau* — a bogey used to frighten children (cf. portion 10, v. 121).
[^6]: In A an unclear word follows (perhaps an indication of metre).
[^7]: "King of the twice-born" (*dvijaraja*) — both the best of brahmanas and the moon.
