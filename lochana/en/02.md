# Sutra-khanda, part 2 (portion 02)

*The end of the "sutra": a list of the pastimes of the Adi- and Madhya-khanda; praise of Chaitanya, Nityananda and Advaita; the narrative begins — Damodara's question about the reasons for Gauranga's appearance, Murari's answer on the decline of dharma in Kali, and Narada's grief*

**82.** how He bade His mother keep Ekadashi —
of His many revelations I shall tell in due time;

**83.** how at that time Jagannatha [Mishra] passed to the other world,
and how the Lord wept, grieving for His father.

**84.** Then I shall tell another wondrous tale:
the Lord married — joy without bounds;

**85.** and the secret that came about when He saw the Ganga[^1] —
listen attentively, I shall surely tell it;

**86.** I shall tell well of the journey to East Bengal
and of how Lakshmi ascended to heaven;

**87.** returning to His own land He married again,
imparted knowledge to His disciples and set out for Gaya.

**88.** Of each I shall tell — listen, all of you:
you will find great joy; do not give up your effort.

**89.** Especially I shall tell of the return home:
He constantly manifests prema, absorbed in rasa.

**90.** The tale of the Madhya-khanda, brothers, is a great joy:
hearing it the hair stands on end — nectar without end.

**91.** The tale of the meeting with the devotees is the manifestation of prema:
even before I begin to tell it, my heart rejoices.

**92.** The tale of the Madhya-khanda, brothers, is the play in Nadia:
like a stream of nectar — so prema was spread.

**93.** The Lord revealed most unprecedented pastimes,
such as devotees never heard of in any of the four yugas.

**94.** This wondrous tale, the spreading of bhakti,
I shall tell in the Madhya-khanda — the play in Nadia.

**95.** How all the devotees came together —
of each I shall tell, as far as I know how to tell.

**96.** First I shall tell how Shachi received the gift of prema,
how on the road He heard the sound of the flute,

**97.** how, seized by bhava, He was overwhelmed with prema,
and suddenly a voice from heaven sounded in the sky;

**98.** how in the form of Varaha He showed mercy to Murari;
Brahma and the other gods behold [it], themselves enraptured;

**99.** how Shuklambara Brahmachari then obtained prema —
I shall tell it all, listen with all your heart;

**100.** Pandita Sri Gadadhara, [dear to] the Lord as life,[^2]
overwhelmed with prema, weeps day and night;

**101.** one by one He gave prema to all —
I shall tell it all as is fitting;

**102.** His mercy to a devotee — how He planted a mango stone:
hearing of it, all will be freed from doubt;

**103.** the Lord, eclipsing the doctrine of the Self (*adhyatma*), reveals prema:
He is not reached by knowledge — so He explains to all.

**104.** Then I shall tell an unprecedented account —
how the meeting with Nityananda came about,

**105.** how Haridasa meets the Lord,
of the meeting of Advaita Acharya with Nityananda,

**106.** how He delivered Jagai and Madhai,
how He showed mercy to a father and son, brahmanas,

**107.** how He showed mercy to a singer [of songs] of Shiva;
how suddenly grief arose from a brahmana's deed,[^3]

**108.** how the Lord threw Himself into the Jahnavi —
hearing of it, hearts will tremble in the three worlds;

**109.** then you will hear in due order another wonder —
how the Lord cleansed the temple;

**110.** you will hear many quite unprecedented things:
He delivered a leper — a great marvel;

**111.** especially I shall tell how the bhava of Balarama took hold of Him —
hearing of it, all will find joy without end;

**112.** the revelation in the house of Sri Chandrashekhara Acharya:
the radiance of prema floods earth and sky;

**113.** many secrets I shall tell there —
how wondrous renunciation awoke in the Lord,

**114.** how, seeing Keshava Bharati in the town of Nadia,
He rejoices at heart: "I will take sannyasa!";

**115.** how all the devotees lamented,
how Shachi and Vishnupriya plunged into an ocean of grief;

**116.** in hope of sannyasa He leaves Navadvipa
and takes sannyasa with Bharati's help.

**117.** I shall tell properly all the details:
how He went to the house of Acharya Prabhu,[^4]

**118.** what was said at the meeting with everyone,
how, consoling all, the Lord set out on His way,

**119.** how He went to see Purushottama;
I shall tell the secret of the village of Remuna;

**120.** little by little I shall tell of the events on the road —
hearing of them, all people will be filled with love;

**121.** what wonder the Lord worked on coming to Jajpur —
I shall surely tell of the town of Ekamra too;

**122.** how the meeting with Jagannatha came about,
the revelation to Sarvabhauma — you will hear; listen with one mind.

**123.** The tale of the Madhya-khanda, brothers, is the essence of nectar;
the tale of the Shesha-khanda remains — I shall tell it; hear further.

**124.** The Madhya-khanda in this book[^5] is the manifestation of prema —
so says Lochana Dasa with a jubilant heart.

*Raga dhanashi; taraja metre*

**125.** Glory, glory, glory to Sri Krishna Chaitanya —
He Himself has descended to earth!
Ah, what fortune for the people! The earth is full of pride:
her ornament is His holy feet.

**126.** The lamp of the three worlds has risen in Navadvipa,
shining with rays of compassion;
the devotees who had thirsted for many days
have obtained [what they hoped for] — the hope of prema.

**127.** Like six-footed bees circling a honeyed lotus,
like the meeting of moon and chakora,
like the chataka bird that, seeing a rain-cloud,
cries out in rapture: "Piu, piu!"

**128.** The enraptured devotees dance, Gora rains down prema,
hunkaras thunder, roars, lion-like cries;
like a poor man who lost his wealth and has found it again —
so His followers weep in their longing.

**129.** Like a forest elephant caught in a forest fire
that plunges into an ocean of nectar,
so in the play of prema they were immersed entirely
and forgot the former burning.

**130.** "Bravo, Thakura!" they cry; someone slaps his arms;
in the bliss of prema they forget themselves.
The prema that Lakshmi begs for with joined palms and ardent love,
He gives away to all without discrimination.

**131.** What more can I say? Even Ananta forgot where He was —
what rasa, what sweetness of prema!
He who is called Shesha, who holds the world upon His head,
now bears the name Nitai.

**132.** Brimming with the rasa of prema, knowing no difference between own and other,
He explains this to all;
under the measured tread of His feet the earth sways,
as when a maddened elephant in rut goes by.

**133.** Hear of another wonder: Mahesha, named Advaita,[^6]
whose virtues, when sung, surpass understanding,
in His talks with Thakura Chaitanya on the rasa of prema
forgot His knowledge and His yoga.

**134.** With their rasika companions, in the sport of love's pastimes,
They explain everything to all without dispute;
apart from these two Thakuras there is no Thakura of mercy —
it is for Their sake that Gorachand rose.

**135.** "Glory, glory!" — auspicious cries ring out, the people of the world say "Hari!",
all are hoping for prema;
prema, rare even for Brahma — all long for it —
so says Lochana Dasa, smiling.

## Grantharambha (The Narrative Begins)

> *Summary by Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati.* At the beginning of the narrative the author, relying on the conversation of Narada and Uddhava from the *Jaimini-bharata*, which is related in the dialogue of Damodara and Murari, has set down the reason why Krishna descended in the form of Gaura.
>
> Once the devarshi Narada, grieved by the sufferings of the living beings stricken by Kali and pondering how to save them, resolved to move the protector of dharma, Bhagavan Sri Krishna, to descend into the mortal world, and set out for Dvaraka. Sri Krishna was then staying in the palace of Rukmini-devi. Rukmini-devi, having learned that Krishna, accepting the bhava of Radha, would descend in the form of Gaura, was in the greatest distress at the thought of the coming separation; she was glorifying His lotus feet and confiding her grief to Him when the devarshi Narada arrived. Received by Krishna with honour, he told the reason for his coming, and Krishna revealed to him that He would descend in the form of Gaura in the house of Shachi and Jagannatha at Navadvipa, and at once showed him His form as Gaura.
>
> Seeing the supremely lovely form of Gaura, the devarshi Narada, beside himself with rapture, went on from there meditating on the form of Gaura and singing the glory of Gaura, the essence of all avataras, and came to Naimisharanya. There the best of devotees, Uddhava, asked the great sage how the living beings stricken by Kali could be saved; the devarshi Narada told him all that had happened, sang the glory of the Kali-yuga, the essence of all yugas, and of the dharma of that age, the sankirtana of Hari-nama, and came to Kailasa, to the best of Vaishnavas, Shambhu. There Narada, wishing to remind Parvati of her former vow, [related]: how, learning of the greatness of mahaprasada, he served Lakshmi for twelve years to obtain it, and by her grace obtained mahaprasada and gave a portion to Shiva; how Shiva, having tasted the mahaprasada, broke into a wild dance; how the Earth, unable to bear Shiva's dance, came to Parvati; and then the greatness of mahaprasada and Parvati's vow to distribute mahaprasada to all living beings; and he sang much of the descent of Gaura.
>
> Then Narada came to Brahma and told of Gaurasundara's descent in the Kali-yuga. Brahma quoted and explained to Narada the verses of the *Srimad-Bhagavata* concerning this descent, supported the sense of these verses of the *Bhagavata* with the words of other scriptures, and said that at the time of Gaura's descent he himself would appear on earth with all the gods. Next comes the proposal of the gods to take birth in the mortal world; [the account of how] Krishna, accepting the bhava and lustre of Srimati Radhika, together with the special qualities of His eternal consorts Rukmini, Satyabhama and others, taking up the weapon of the sankirtana of the Name, descends as Gaura, Balarama as Nityananda, Shiva as Advaita Prabhu, and the other associates as Murari, Mukunda, Srinivasa, Raya Ramananda, Ishvara Puri, Madhava Puri; having described this, the author sang the glory of his guru Thakura Narahari and of his nephew Raghunandana and concluded the Sutra-khanda.[^7]

*Raga varadi; disha: "O, o! — Murchha: — I would die taking on myself every ill that threatens Gora,[^8] taking on myself the ills that threaten His beauty and His virtues! In rapture He gave away prema, filling the whole world"*

**1.** Glory, glory to Sri Krishna Chaitanya and Nityananda,
glory, glory to Advaita Acharya, full of bliss!

**2.** Glory, glory to Narahari, [dear as] life to Gadadhara,[^9]
glory, glory to Srinivasa, worthy of bhakti!

**3.** All the beloved devotees of Chaitanya Gosani —
the feet of them all, [holding them] in my heart, I praise.

**4.** I shall tell of Chaitanya — listen attentively:
Damodara Pandita asked Gupta:[^10]

**5.** "Tell me, and I shall listen: for what reason did Gauranga appear?
To hear of it is a joy to my heart.

**6.** Why did He leave His dark colour and become Gaura?
Why in kirtana does He roll on the ground and cover His body with dust?

**7.** Why did He exchange the dress of a *nagara*[^11] for sannyasa?
Why does He wander from land to land as though in despair?

**8.** Why does He weep, calling out, 'Radha! Radha! Govinda!'?
Why does He go from house to house offering prema?

**9.** Tell me all this — the deepest secret,
hearing which even the most foolish of fools are saved."

**10.** Having heard, Murari says: "Listen, Pandita;
all this truth I will reveal to you.

**11.** In the Satya-yuga dharma has four quarters — so say the shastras;
in the Treta-yuga, I tell you, dharma has three quarters;

**12.** in the Dvapara-yuga, I tell you, dharma is half;
in the Kali-yuga, by the reckoning of dharma, one quarter.

**13.** Adharma grew, dharma dwindled;
[people] abandoned their own duty; there are neither varnas nor ashramas.

**14.** Kali became a sinful, impenetrable darkness;
all people sank, crippled by adharma.

**15.** Seeing that there was no dharma, the great sage Narada
was himself moved to compassion, [wishing] to save [the people of] Kali.

**16.** He thought: 'The serpent Kali has swallowed everyone,'
and he wished to establish dharma:

**17.** 'Without Krishna no one can establish dharma;
I shall surely bring Krishna into Kali, and quickly.

**18.** The devotee's wish is ever Govinda's wish:
such is the conclusion of the Vedas, agamas and shastras.

**19.** If I am in every way a servant of Krishna,
I shall bring into Kali the Lord, the king of the Yadus.

**20.** Let me first see what the Kali-yuga does,
and then I shall bring Krishna, the all-embracing dharma.

**21.** I shall bring all the gods with Him —
His weapons, His associates, all His intimates and retinue.'

**22.** Brahma and the other gods, Narada and the other sages,
the goddess Katyayani took birth on earth;[^12]

**23.** all who were in Dvaraka, in the line of Yadu,
took birth on earth, each by his own portion.

**24.** I shall tell it all — listen attentively —
how the descent upon earth came about.

**25.** The descent of Gora is the essence of all descents:
such compassion never was before.

**26.** The great sage Narada, grieved by the sorrow of others,
sings day and night the rasa of the tales of Krishna.

**27.** Thirsting for tales of Krishna, he roams the whole world,
but, looking over the world, he did not hear the name of Krishna.

**28.** Choking with the rasa of Krishna, he babbles in half-words,
now weeping, now laughing aloud;

**29.** he sings [Krishna's] virtues to the vina, tears stream from his eyes:
within and without, the sage is absorbed in the rasa of Krishna.

**30.** In such play of prema, rolling on the ground,
he did not hear the name of Krishna, though he went round the world.

**31.** Pained at heart, the sage was amazed:
he sees no way to save the people.

**32.** The black serpent Kali has bitten all people:
they burn unceasingly, blinded by the pride of maya.

[^1]: The Adi-khanda tells of the goddess Ganga's love for the young Nimai (so in Bhaktisiddhanta's survey of the parts and in the 1983 edition).
[^2]: So in Bhaktisiddhanta's text (A: "prabhura prana"); the 1983 edition has "by the Lord's grace".
[^3]: In the Madhya-khanda — the curse a brahmana pronounced upon the Lord (see Bhaktisiddhanta's summary of that chapter).
[^4]: Advaita Acharya's, at Shantipur.
[^5]: Literally "The Madhya-khanda ends: in the book, the manifestation of prema." Contrary to expectation, nothing is said here of the contents of the Shesha-khanda: Lochana Dasa only promises to tell of it; the meeting with Sarvabhauma he still assigns to the Madhya-khanda.
[^6]: Mahesha is Shiva. According to the *Gaura-ganoddesha-dipika* and the *Chaitanya-charitamrita* (Adi 6), Advaita Acharya is Maha-Vishnu, non-different from Hari, and at the same time Sadashiva; Lochana Dasa names here only the second aspect.
[^7]: The summary covers the whole section "Grantharambha" to the end of the Sutra-khanda (portions 02–08 of our translation). A few words are corrupt in the OCR and have been restored from the same summary in the *Khanda-vivarana* at the beginning of the edition.
[^8]: *Nichhani* is the rite in which one waves something around a beloved person to "take off" all harm and take it upon oneself; *balai laya*, "I take your ill upon myself", is an expression of tender love.
[^9]: So in Bhaktisiddhanta's text (A: "gadadhara-prana"); the 1983 edition has "Glory to Gadadhara Pandita, glory to Narahari".
[^10]: The 1983 edition has two couplets here instead of this one (a different redaction): "I shall tell of Chaitanya — listen attentively — how He descended in the age of Kali. Murari Gupta, the vaidya, knows the truth about the Lord; Pandita Damodara asked him." A (and the 1903 edition) has a single couplet coinciding with the first and last lines of B; we follow A.
[^11]: *Nagara*, an elegant townsman; here the fine dress of a fashionable householder that the Lord wore in His youth, as opposed to the garb of a sannyasi.
[^12]: In Bhaktisiddhanta's edition (A) couplets 22–23 are already narrative ("took birth"); in the 1983 edition they continue Narada's thought ("will take birth on earth"), and instead of "Narada and the other sages" it has "Sanaka and the other sages".
