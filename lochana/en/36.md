# Madhya-khanda, part 13 (portion 36)

*The section "Before Sannyasa" (end): the Lord speaks of the cruelty of samsara and of how only service to Krishna gives meaning to human birth, and consoles the devotees. The section "Mother Shachi's Grief": Shachi's lament and her pleading; the Lord begins the story of Dhruva — Dhruva's hurt, his mother's counsel, the meeting with Narada, the journey to Madhuvana*

*Raga vibhasa, the taraja tune; disha: "na hare are haya"*

**135.** "Listen, all: cruel samsara
has put Me in peril;[^1]
the dreadful objects of the senses, as if full of poison,
secretly burn the heart.

**136.** All the senses one calls one's own —
not one gives up its cravings;
however much one feeds them with ever new things,
they will not turn back.

**137.** Greed, delusion, lust — none is weaker,
and with them pride, conceit and anger;
having stolen the mind, they lie hidden in it
and are never calmed for a moment.

**138.** Outwardly maya binds and whirls [the soul] about,
[giving] it a footing in caste and lineage;
forgetting Krishna, it wanders,
rooted in sinful, evil desires.

**139.** Everything wondrous you see in the world —
all of it hides Krishna;
human birth has meaning only then
when one serves Sri Krishna.

**140.** Human birth, as is known, is rare —
[it is given] for serving Krishna;
yet, having obtained such a body, people abandon Sri Krishna
and die in this false samsara.

**141.** Listen, all — I have told you what is in My heart:
bless Me,
that I may have love for Krishna and this sorrow may flee —
this boon I ask of everyone.

**142.** To sing Krishna's deeds unceasingly —
this is what My lips long for;
My eyes — on His lotus face,
My heart I shall bind to His holy feet.

**143.** What shall I say of My heart? Not seeing Krishna,
the fire of separation burns in its depths;
fallen into the ocean of samsara, into the deep,
My mind is distraught.

**144.** He alone is father and mother, he alone is a deity,
he alone a guru and friend,
who lets one hear and tells of Krishna,
who brings one to serve Krishna's feet.

**145.** You are My friends, supreme Vaishnavas:
do not withdraw your mercy from Me in your hearts.
I shall take sannyasa and spread prema —
all for your own good."

**146.** Having said this, Vishvambhara
rolls on the ground;
Gaura's golden body is grey with dust,
His loosened hair sweeps the ground.

**147.** "Haribol!" He calls aloud,
breathing deep and fast;
His body thrills from feet to head,
His speech falters, words left half-said.

**148.** Now He weeps, now He is in anguish,
now He starts and looks about,
now He gasps and tosses, His whole body trembling,
now He springs up in separation from Krishna.

**149.** Now He cries aloud: "Vrindavana!"
now He calls: "Radha!"
He slaps His arms like a wrestler, says "Hari, Hari",
now He strikes His breast with His hand.

**150.** Seeing this, all ponder within themselves,
anguished at heart:
what more is there to say? — it was as if they had fallen
into an ocean of grief.

**151.** Murari says: "Listen, Gaurahari:
You are independent in everything;
to teach people, to reveal mercy,
You take on the bhava of the pain of separation.[^2]

**152.** Whatever You do by the will of Your own heart —
what can others say about it?
You know everything; whatever You ordain —
what can poor souls do about it?

**153.** We are all jivas, we do not know what will become of us,
we are like worms and ants;
You are the ocean of mercy, the friend of all people:
consider and do what is fitting."

**154.** Hearing this, the Lord smiled
and embraced them all;
revealing prema, addressing them all,
He speaks words of consolation:

**155.** "Listen, all, to what I say,
let no one doubt:
wherever I may go, I remain with you —
know this."[^3]

**156.** Then Vishvambhara, taking leave of all,
went home;
with sannyasa in His heart He makes every preparation,
but His mother does not know of it.

**157.** Shachi's heart beats anxiously,
she finds no peace.
Lochana says: how can He wish
to leave such an ocean of love?

## Mother Shachi's Grief

> *Summary by Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati.* Hearing from people that Sriman Mahaprabhu would soon take sannyasa, mother Shachi, beside herself with grief, weeping, earnestly begged the Lord not to take sannyasa in His youth but to keep the dharma of a householder. Then Sriman Mahaprabhu, telling [her the story] of Dhruva and of the instruction his mother gave him — to serve Krishna — [revealed to her] that apart from Krishna the living being has no other refuge; that therefore whoever teaches one to serve Krishna is one's true father, mother, brother and friend; that those intoxicated by the conceit of "I" and "mine" are the greatest fools; and that service to Krishna is the meaning of human life. Having declared this, He instructed her to give up attachment to her son such as souls in the grip of maya have, and vowed that, not like other sons [who bring their mothers] silver, gold and other perishable things, He would bring her the eternal, highest good — prema. Then Gaurahari showed Himself to her in the form of Sri Krishna and dispelled mother Shachi's grief.[^4]

*Raga ahiri; disha: "Ah, my son, do not leave me — but for you I have no one in the three worlds!"*

**1.** Thus, by guesses and rumours,
mother Shachi heard that her son would take sannyasa.

**2.** It was as if the sky had fallen on her head:
Shachi lost consciousness, her heart swooned.

**3.** Like a madwoman Shachi rushes about on every side;
whomever she sees she questions — all over Navadvipa.

**4.** Knowing for certain that her son would take sannyasa,
she came to Vishvambhara and heaves deep sighs:

**5.** "You are my only son, the one eye in my body:
when I do not see you, I see everything in darkness.

**6.** I hear from people, my child, that you will take sannyasa —
it is as if the sky had broken over my head.

**7.** I am alone, unprotected — I have no one else;
forgetting all else, I look only at your face.

**8.** You are the pupil of my eyes, the lamp of our family;
for having such a son as you Navadvipa calls me fortunate.

**9.** Do not destroy my pride, my son:
if you are gone, everything will go to ruin.

**10.** People counted it good fortune to see my face —
now, seeing me, they will turn away.

**11.** Having such a son as you, I am blessed in this world —
but not seeing you, I see nothing but wilderness around me.

**12.** Giving grief to this unfortunate woman, you will go away —
and I shall enter the Ganga and die.

**13.** How will you walk on such tender feet?
Hungry and thirsty — from whom will you beg food?

**14.** Your body is like a doll of butter: it will melt in the sun;
how will this wretched mother bear it?

**15.** Son of a mother bereft of her children, my golden Nimai!
Leaving me, where will you go?[^5]

**16.** I shall take poison and die before your eyes,
so as not to hear with my ears of your sannyasa.

**17.** Having killed me, my child, you will go to foreign lands —
and I shall light a fire and enter it.

**18.** To all beings you are merciful — but to me merciless;
I do not know why the Creator is so cruel to me.

**19.** In beauty, virtues and nature, my son, you are blessed in the three worlds;
your dress enchants women, your hair is lovely.

**20.** When you weave malati into your hair that falls to your shoulders,
my heart is eased at the sight of that attire.

**21.** You walk along the road surrounded by friends,
a book in your left hand — and seeing you, my heart is glad.

**22.** How will you leave your companions, my child?
Will you no longer perform sankirtana with them?

**23.** Will you no longer dance in such lovely attire,
at the sight of which the whole world is enchanted?

**24.** How will your dear ones live?
Your sannyasa will kill them all.

**25.** First I shall die, then Vishnupriya;
the devotees will die, their hearts bursting:

**26.** Murari, Mukunda Datta and Shrinivasa,
Advaita Acharya and others, and Haridasa,

**27.** Gadadhara, Narahari, Sri Raghunandana,
Vasudeva Ghosha, Vakreshvara, Sri Rama —

**28.** all will die, not seeing you;
seeing all this, my child, soften your heart.

**29.** You are a son without a father; I married you twice,
but I have seen no offspring from it.

**30.** Sannyasa in youth is not dharma;
live in the householder's ashrama and fulfil all your duties.

**31.** In youth lust, anger, greed and delusion are strong —
how will your sannyasa succeed?

**32.** In the Kali age the mind cannot be curbed;
through the mind's unsteadiness the dharma of sannyasa perishes.

**33.** A householder is not bound by sins of the mind,
but a sannyasi's dharma is lost if his mind is defiled by desire."[^6]

**34.** When Shachi-devi had spoken these words,
[the Lord], having heard them, spoke words of consolation to His mother:

*Raga — any; refrain: "The nectar words of the moon-like lips…"[^7]*

**35.** "Dhruva's mother made Dhruva a Vaishnava —
I shall tell that rasa: hear a wondrous tale.[^8]

*Thus [in the Padyavali]:*

**36.** What was the conduct of the hunter, what the age of Dhruva, what the learning of Gajendra, what the name and beauty of Kubja, what the wealth of Sudama,
what the lineage of Vidura, what the prowess of Ugrasena, lord of the Yadavas? Madhava, who loves devotion, is pleased by devotion alone, not by qualities.[^9]

**37.** Listen, mother, attentively to the story of Dhruva —
how Dhruva attained the very highest position.

**38.** The mind-born son of Brahma was Svayambhuva Manu,
of great might and valour, like a second Brahma.

**39.** He had two sons, Priyavrata and Uttanapada;
both became great kings by Brahma's grace.

**40.** Maharaja Uttanapada married twice —
two beauties named Suruchi and Suniti.

**41.** Suruchi had seven sons, beginning with Uttama;
from Suniti's womb only Dhruva was born.[^10]

**42.** Uttama's mother won her husband's favour,
while Dhruva's mother fell out of favour with her husband.

**43.** The beautiful Suruchi became the chief queen,
and Dhruva's mother went to wait on her.

**44.** The grief of Dhruva's mother cannot be told:
at such grief stones float and the ocean dries up.

**45.** Rice of coarse grain and unsalted vegetables —
that is what they gave Dhruva's mother to eat.

**46.** When Dhruva was five years old,
Dhruva's unhappy mother suffered all kinds of hardship.

**47.** One day the Maharaja with Suruchi
sat in pleasure on a high throne.

**48.** The seven brothers, beginning with Uttama, with their father and mother,
were on the jewelled throne, enjoying all kinds of amusements.

**49.** Five-year-old Dhruva with other children,
grey with dust, was playing all kinds of games.

**50.** Seeing his seven brothers on his father's lap,
Dhruva climbs up onto the jewelled throne.

**51.** As he climbed onto the throne to get onto his father's lap,
Dhruva's stepmother pushed him down to the ground.

**52.** Fallen on the ground, Dhruva began to cry;
the king, in his wife's power, said nothing.

**53.** Fallen on the ground, Dhruva weeps in wounded pride:
he does not know that his mother is out of favour with his father.

**54.** Dhruva's stepmother says: "You cry for nothing:
the son of a maidservant — and you climb onto the jewelled throne!

**55.** Birth after birth your mother has not served Krishna —
have you no shame, climbing onto the jewelled throne?

**56.** Son of an unfortunate woman, your mother is no Vaishnavi:
where will you ever sit on a jewelled throne?"

**57.** When Dhruva's stepmother had said this,
Dhruva went weeping to his mother.

**58.** He told his mother: "Stepmother hit me,
she pushed me off the throne.

**59.** Stepmother says: 'Your mother does not serve Krishna —
are you not ashamed to sit on the jewelled throne?'

**60.** And I have heard one more strange thing:
all this time I did not know that you are her maidservant."

**61.** Hearing this, Dhruva's mother weeps:
"I did not serve Krishna, my child — I am unfortunate.

**62.** Birth after birth I never once thought of Krishna;
I am Krishna's servant, yet I did not serve Him.

**63.** Do not cry, do not cry, child, son of an unfortunate woman:
your stepmother taunts you, calling you a maidservant's son."

**64.** Weeping, the mother speaks words of comfort to Dhruva;
Lochana Dasa happily sings of Gora's qualities.

*Raga sindhura*

**65.** "From the womb of an unfortunate woman, my son Dhruva,
you were born: I did not serve Krishna.
You are not your father's darling, yet you want to climb onto the throne —
poor child, better you had not been born [to me]!

**66.** Do not cry, do not cry, Dhruva, I shall tell you what I know —
listen, listen to my words:
your stepmother once worshipped Krishna —
that is why she became fortunate.

**67.** If you serve Krishna's feet, what need have you of the throne?
Whatever you desire, you will obtain.
Give up empty pride, serve Krishna's feet —
and you will easily obtain everything.

**68.** Such is my son — and the whole world taunts us —
how will you climb onto your father's lap?
I was born unfortunate" — saying this, the queen
was drowned in tears.

**69.** "O Dhruva, listen, listen to my words:
no one else can free you from your grief
but the one Lotus-eyed.

**70.** Brahma and all the gods, by serving Krishna much,
obtained high places in heaven;
if you serve Krishna, what is a throne? —
you will be honoured in the three worlds."

**71.** Hearing his mother's words, Dhruva ponders within:
"Where shall I find Krishna?"
"You will find Krishna in Madhuvana; how to go there
I shall teach you.

**72.** If you are [truly] the son of Uttanapada
and wish to obtain that throne —
then [Krishna] will make you fortunate,
and you will gain that throne."[^11]

**73.** Placing the dust of his mother's feet on his head as an ornament,
at an auspicious hour he sets out,
fixing his mind on Sri Krishna's feet —
and in heaven cries of "Victory!" ring out.

*Raga suhai; refrain: "Teach me — where shall I go to find news of my dark Friend?"*

**74.** Hear, all of you, another wondrous story:
the Lord tells it, mother Shachi listens.

**75.** Honouring with his head the dust of his mother's feet,
weeping and weeping, he consoles his mother.

**76.** The great soul Dhruva set out for Madhuvana,
having set his heart on the high position — devotion to Krishna.

**77.** When Dhruva, weary from the road, suffered from hunger,
he would suddenly find sweet ripe fruits.

**78.** When Dhruva went on, tormented by thirst,
he would find fragrant water on the way.

**79.** Seeing this, all the gods are amazed:
"We do not know whose place this Dhruva will take."[^12]

**80.** On the way Narada met Dhruva
and began to speak sweet words to him:

**81.** "At the age for play you, a king's son,
needlessly harbour hurt and pride in your heart.

**82.** It is not the time for you now to go to the forest;
in old age serve Govinda, the treasury of virtues."

**83.** Dhruva answers: "If one is to serve Krishna in old age —
then if one dies in youth, what of that rule?"

**84.** Hearing this, the great sage rejoiced
and imparted to Dhruva the twelve-syllable mantra.

**85.** [Dhruva said:] "Before, I did not serve Krishna — hence so much grief;
my heart is pierced by the arrows of my stepmother's words.

**86.** You are most merciful, and I am unfortunate:
remove my grief by instructing me [in the service] of Krishna.

**87.** By the power of serving Krishna I shall gain such a place
as neither my father nor my grandfather attained."

**88.** [Narada said:] "Go, Dhruva, to Madhuvana, on the bank of the Kalindi;
make a firm seat, sit and remain unmoving.

**89.** Make the great seed-mantra your support:
'Om namo bhagavate vasudevaya'.

**90.** Always repeat this mantra —
and within seven days you will have realisation."

**91.** Having received initiation and instruction, Dhruva rejoiced
and, bowing, went to Vrindavana.

**92.** In some days he reached Madhuvana;
seeing the wish-fulfilling trees, he cast off ignorance.

**93.** The son of Uttanapada reaches Madhuvana —
in joy Lochana Dasa sings of Gora's qualities.

[^1]: The Lord speaks in the person of a man, to instruct people: He Himself is not subject to maya; "samsara" and "maya" in vv. 135–140 are the lot of conditioned souls. So Murari too understands it in v. 151.
[^2]: That is, the Lord, the independent Master, takes on the bhava of separation from Krishna in order to teach people and bestow His mercy on them.
[^3]: Cf. Murari 2.18.23: "I Myself always dwell in your shrine."
[^4]: The OCR of the summary is badly garbled (vv. 15–16 of the poem have also strayed into it); gaps are filled by sense and from the table of contents in the edition's preface ("the grief of mother Shachi on hearing that the Lord will take sannyasa, … the request [to keep] the [householder's] dharma; the Lord, telling the story of Dhruva, instructs in the service of Krishna; consoling His mother with various words and showing her the form of Sri Krishna, He dispels her grief"). The summary also covers pastimes told in the next portion. The disha (refrain) is given from the 1903 edition (it is illegible in the OCR of A); the 1983 edition has: "Ah, Nimai, my Nimai, do not leave me — but for you I have no one in all the world."
[^5]: Before Vishvarupa's birth Shachi had lost eight daughters, and Vishvarupa had taken sannyasa. In A this couplet and the beginning of the next have strayed into the OCR of the summary; restored from B and C.
[^6]: Shachi's arguments are the usual objections to sannyasa in youth and in the Kali age; the Lord answers them below. Shachi is the Lord's eternal mother (Yashoda in Vraja); her grief is the expression of a mother's love (vatsalya-prema).
[^7]: The raga and refrain lines in A are badly corrupted ("যথা—রাগ । চান্দ-মুখের বচন অমিয়া । রূপ গল কেমন বিধি দৈরজ ধরিয়া ॥ ধ্রু ॥"); only the beginning is translated. In the 1903 edition these lines and the whole story of Dhruva (vv. 35–93 and beyond) are given in a footnote as an additional reading from one manuscript; the 1983 edition has no story of Dhruva: after v. 34 it goes straight on to the consolation of the mother. In Bhaktisiddhanta's edition the story is in the main text; we translate it but note that it is apparently a later insertion.
[^8]: The story of Dhruva is Bhagavata 4.8–9; Lochana Dasa retells it freely, with details not found in the Bhagavata.
[^9]: A shloka from Rupa Gosvami's Padyavali; the same as in en/24 (v. 15 of the section). Bhaktisiddhanta's prose rendering of the shloka is not translated.
[^10]: In the Bhagavata (4.8.8–9) Suruchi has one son, Uttama.
[^11]: The stanza is corrupt in A and in the 1903 edition ("তবে «'ননাম পরেশ তোমাকে মৌভাগ্য করে"; C: "তবে একবমাম ধরে? তোমাকে সৌভাগ্য করে"); translated by sense.
[^12]: The gods fear that the ascetic will take the place of one of them (cf. Bhagavata 4.8.80–81).
