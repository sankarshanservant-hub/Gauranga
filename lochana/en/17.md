# Adi-khanda, part 9 (portion 17)

*Pauganda-lila (conclusion): Shachi entrusts her son to the pandits; studies under Vishnu Pandita, Sudarshana and Gangadasa; the first meeting with Vallabhacharya's daughter on the way to the Ganga; Vanamali Acharya's matchmaking — Shachi's refusal, the matchmaker's lament, his meeting with the Lord and Shachi's consent; Vallabhacharya gladly agrees to give his daughter*

*Raga dhanashi; disha: "Ah, ah!"*

**697.** One day Shachi, taking Gaurahari by the hand,
put Gauranga to His studies.

**698.** Entrusting her son to all the pandits,
the goddess speaks humbly and plaintively:

**699.** "Teach my son, good sirs;
keep Him close to you, do not keep Him at a distance.

**700.** My son has lost His father — be kind to Him;
regard Him as your own son."

**701.** Hearing this, all the pandits, abashed at heart,
began to answer humbly:

**702.** "Today we have come to know our good fortune:
we have obtained the Lord of millions of Sarasvatis.[^1]

**703.** He will teach the whole world His own prema and His name;
He is the teacher of all people, the foremost of all.

**704.** It is we who shall learn in His presence —
know this for certain, mother; we have told you the truth."

**705.** Hearing this, Shachi-devi answered with humble words
and, having entrusted her son, returned to her home.

**706.** Then, after a few more days, the Lord Vishvambhara
went to study at the house of Vishnu Pandita.

**707.** Under Sudarshana and the pandit Gangadasa
the Teacher of the world studied — for their own good.

**708.** He who by His yogamaya has manifested a human form[^2] behaves like people;
out of kindness to people He studies and teaches knowledge.

**709.** One day, at the school of Pandita Sri Sudarshana,
the Lord jokes with His fellow students.

**710.** He talks in the speech of the East Bengalis — most amusingly;[^3]
His laughter, full of nectar, captivates the heart.

**711.** Thus some days passed in merry pranks;
He decided to visit Vanamali Acharya.

**712.** To see him He went to his house;
seeing [Him], he rose in reverence and bowed.

**713.** Taking him by the hand, He walks with him along the road,
talking of amusing and confidential things.

**714.** At that time the daughter of Vallabha Acharya,
famed in the three worlds for beauty, virtue and character,

**715.** is going with her friends to bathe in the Ganga;
Vishvambhara Hari suddenly sees her.

**716.** The Lord gazes at her steadily with a smiling face
and, seeing her, knew why she had been born.[^4]

**717.** Lakshmi Thakurani understood this from the sign,
and the goddess took the lotus feet of the Lord upon her head.[^5]

**718.** Acharya Vanamali was very shrewd:
he understood what was in their hearts — the sprouting feeling.

**719.** The next day Vanamali Acharya himself,
with joy in his heart, went to Shachi's house.

**720.** Smiling, he bowed at Shachi's feet
and, bowing, spoke with sweet words:

**721.** "There is a girl worthy of your son:
for beauty, virtue and character she is famed in the three worlds.

**722.** She is the daughter of Vallabha Acharya, most well-mannered;
if you have the wish, tell me what is in your heart."

**723.** Hearing the Acharya's words, Shachi-devi [said]:
"He is still just a boy; let Him study now.

**724.** My son has lost His father — let Him study for some more days;
see to that instead, so that He may become accomplished."

**725.** Hearing this, the Acharya was not pleased;
with a downcast face he set out for home.

**726.** He goes weeping and weeping, distressed at heart,
calling aloud: "O, O Gorachand!

**727.** You did not do it for my lot, O Purifier of the fallen —
why then do You bear the name 'Wish-fulfilling Tree'?

**728.** If You Yourself have not fulfilled my desire,
how will You bear the name 'Wish-fulfilling Tree'?

**729.** Glory, glory to You who took away Draupadi's shame and fear!
Glory to You who saved the king of elephants from the crocodile's jaws!

**730.** Glory to You who gave deliverance to Ajamila and the courtesan![^6]
Deliver me too, Father of all the world!"

**731.** Meanwhile, at His teacher's house, the Lord knew in His heart
how stricken with grief the Acharya was.

**732.** Hastily gathering His books, Bhagavan,
taking leave of His teacher, the Lord set out.

**733.** His gait was beautiful, like that of an intoxicated elephant;
His golden body sparkled with ornaments.

**734.** His curling locks enchant the whole world;
His lips are bandhuli flowers, His teeth pearls.

**735.** His limbs, anointed with sandal, are beautiful;
on His body fine garments that captivate the heart.

**736.** Gaurahari is the king of many millions of Kamadevas,
and His body throws chaste wives into turmoil.[^7]

**737.** The Lord hastened for the Acharya's sake —
that is why He is called the "Wish-fulfilling Tree".

**738.** And the Acharya goes weeping from road to road
and runs with arms raised, crying: "O, O Gorachand!"

**739.** Just then Mahaprabhu, coming back from His teacher's house,
met the Acharya.

**740.** The Acharya fell at His feet, stretched out like a stick,
and Mahaprabhu, smiling and smiling, raised him up.

**741.** Greeting him, He embraced him tightly
and asked sweetly: "Where have you been?"

**742.** The Acharya says: "Listen, listen, Vishvambhara:
I went to Your house.

**743.** To Your mother, the goddess, so well-mannered,
I made known what was in my heart:

**744.** there is a girl worthy to be Your bride —
the daughter of Vallabha Acharya, renowned for every virtue.

**745.** Your mother paid no heed to these words,
and I am going home with a heavy heart."

**746.** Hearing this, the Lord said nothing,
but with a playful smile went home.

**747.** Seeing that playfulness, that beauty, that soft, sweet smile,
the Acharya was filled with hope:

**748.** "My business will surely succeed," he understood;
he understood in his heart that the Lord would marry.

**749.** The Acharya came home rejoicing,
pondering in his heart all that the Lord had done.

**750.** Coming home, Vishvambhara said to His mother:
"What answer did you give Vanamali Acharya?

**751.** I saw him going along the road downcast;
talking with the Acharya gave Me no joy.

**752.** Why have you displeased him?
Seeing him downcast, I was saddened."

**753.** Hearing her son's words, the very shrewd Shachi
understood the hint and made haste.

**754.** At once a man went to fetch the Acharya;
hearing the news, he came quickly.

**755.** His whole body filled with joy, his voice choked with emotion,
he came before Shachi and bowed.

**756.** Prostrating himself, he took the dust of her feet:
"Why have you called me, mistress?"

**756+1.** Hearing the Acharya's words, Shachi-devi
then spoke to him with respect:[^8]

**757.** "What you spoke of before — arrange it;
I shall marry Vishvambhara, to everyone's joy.

**758.** You love my Vishvambhara more than I do;
you will do everything yourself — what should I tell you?

**759.** What you spoke of for Vishvambhara's marriage —
undertake it yourself, I tell you."

**760.** Hearing this, Vanamali, the excellent Acharya,
said: "I shall carry out your command."

**761.** Saying this, he went to the house of Vallabha Acharya;
Vallabha Acharya rose to meet him with great respect,

**762.** humbly offered him a seat
and, counting it his good fortune, said with a smile:

**763.** "By my good fortune you have come to me;
and if there is some other business — tell me now."

**764.** Hearing the words of Vallabha Mishra, the Acharya
set out at length the business of his heart:

**765.** "You are always kind to me;
out of affection for you I have come to your house.

**766.** The son of Mishra Purandara, Sri Vishvambhara, —
in lineage, character and virtues He is beautiful in every way.

**767.** Can I describe His virtues?
The Creator has gathered all virtues together in one.

**768.** What shall I say of His virtues — all people sing them;
you will hear of them from the mouths of all.

**769.** Vishvambhara is a bridegroom worthy of your daughter;
I have said everything — if it pleases your heart."

**770.** Hearing this, Mishra reflected and said:
"By my good fortune you have spoken these words to me.

**771.** I am poor, I cannot give anything [as dowry];
I have only one daughter — a beauty.

**772.** Knowing this, if you yourself so direct,
I shall give my daughter to Vishvambhara, a jewel of a son-in-law.

**773.** I shall gladden the gods, the rishis and the ancestors
when I give my daughter in marriage to Gaurachandra.

**774.** Such a thing comes only as the fruit of much austerity;
there is no greater friend than you — I have told you my heart.

**775.** This has been my heart's wish day and night,
but I did not dare to say it aloud."

**776.** Thus the two came to an agreement,
and the Acharya reported everything to Shachi.

**777.** Hearing it, Shachi-devi was very pleased
and blessed Vanamali Acharya.

**778.** Calling her friends and kinsfolk, she made it known to them;
her whole body filled with joy, she rejoiced.

**779.** All the kinsfolk and brothers gave their consent;
having considered it, all said: "Good, good."

*Here ends the Pauganda-lila.*

[^1]: Sarasvati is the goddess of learning and speech; the Lord is the Master of all knowledge (cf. Murari 1.08.12: "husband of Sarasvati").
[^2]: Literally "maya-manusha-vigraha", "a human form by maya": said of the Lord, maya is His yogamaya; so also in Murari (1.09.3).
[^3]: Literally "the speech of the bangajas" — natives of East Bengal (cf. Murari 1.09.4). The Lord's forefathers came from Shrihatta, and He loved to mimic the East Bengali accent in jest.
[^4]: Vallabhacharya's daughter is Lakshmipriya, Lakshmi herself, who appeared in order to become the Lord's consort; Bhaktisiddhanta in his summary (en/18) calls her Lakshmi-devi. Cf. Murari 1.09.7.
[^5]: That is, she bowed to His feet in her mind. The 1983 edition reads: "took upon her head the dust of the Lord's lotus feet".
[^6]: Draupadi, wife of the Pandavas, whom Krishna saved from disgrace in the assembly of the Kauravas; the king of elephants is Gajendra (Bhagavata, Canto 8); Ajamila (Bhagavata, Canto 6) was saved by uttering the name of Narayana; the courtesan is probably Pingala (Bhagavata 11.8).
[^7]: Literally "a body that spreads disgrace among chaste wives", that is, so beautiful that they lose their composure: a motif of Lochana Dasa's nagari-bhava (see en/09, note 9).
[^8]: V. 756+1 is found only in the 1983 edition (absent from Bhaktisiddhanta's edition); the 1903 edition does not have it.
