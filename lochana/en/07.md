# Sutra-khanda, part 7 (portion 07)

*Narada answers Brahma: on service to the Vaishnavas, on the hidden prema of the gopis and how the Lord will spread it in Kali; the rejoicing of the three worlds; Narada sees the coming of Kali, hears the voice of Jagannatha and comes to Nilachala; Jagannatha describes Goloka and sends him to Gaura; Narada with the Lord of Vaikuntha, who calls Himself a portion of Gaura; in Goloka Narada beholds the bathing of Gaurasundara*

*Raga sindhura*

**450.** Narada says: "Brahma, what more shall I say?
All that you have said is my very heart.

**451.** Wandering in the bonds of karma, [a soul] roams for so many kalpas!
If by fortune a little service to the Vaishnavas falls to its lot,

**452.** and it hears from them the exalted secret word,
then it keeps it with the utmost care, attentively.

**453.** Then, freed from bondage, it becomes devoted to Krishna
and will not touch with a finger the four liberations, salokya and the rest.

**454.** And beyond that is prema-bhakti, the bhava of the gopis:
who is qualified even to speak of all that?

**455.** By their rasa the Lord, the Master of the three worlds, is conquered —
yet to worldly people [their love] seems like consorting with an unchaste woman.[^1]

**456.** Who can tell of their prema-bhakti?
For the sake of their virtues Uddhava begs to be born a bush or a creeper.

*So in the* Srimad-Bhagavata *(10.47.61):*

**457.** 'Oh, that I might be anything at all — a bush, a creeper or a herb — in Vrindavana,
[to serve] the dust of the feet of those who, abandoning kinsmen hard to abandon and the path of the noble,
attained the way of Mukunda, which the shrutis seek!'[^2]

**458.** That Lord on whose feet Brahma and Mahesha meditate,
whom the best of yogis and sages seek and find not even a trace of;

**459.** whose feet are served by countless Lakshmis,
the honeyed radiance of whose feet is beyond the reach of words;

**460.** whose greatness the four Vedas ever sing,
finding no limit to His endless glory and virtues;

**461.** whose bed is the great Shesha —
such a Lord served the gopis.

**462.** There are hundreds of other devotees too,
but only the gopis so conquered Him that He became obedient to them.

**463.** Where is Krishna, the Supreme Self — and so hidden a prema —
and where the gopis, forest-dwellers, 'unchaste', 'lustful'?[^3]

**464.** Such is the truth of bhakti I long to understand:
there is no bhakti more hidden than this.

**465.** This bhakti the Lord will spread in the Kali-yuga —
that which even Lakshmi and Ananta have never heard of.

**466.** Tell everyone, Brahma, throughout Brahmaloka:
'Take birth by your portions in the Kali-yuga!'"

**467.** Having said this, with jubilation in his heart,
the great sage Narada went on his way, says this Lochana Dasa.

*Raga mallara, tripadi metre; disha: "O my life, Gorachand!"*[^4]

**468.** Narada Muni set out; the roar of his vina is heard,
softly flows a song that delights the ear;
as if it sprinkled nectar on the minds of the world's people —
the worlds thrilled with joy.

**469.** "Jaya, jaya! Haribol!" — all were immersed in bliss;
a proclamation went through the three worlds:
"With [His] weapons and associates, joyfully He will take birth —
Gora, the avatara for the Kali-yuga!

**470.** Such an ocean of mercy my eyes will see;
He will sprinkle nectar on my body.
Jaya, jaya, Jagannatha! Together with the devotees
He will spread devotion to Himself.

**471.** Blessed, blessed is the Kali-yuga, blessed are all its people,
[blessed] the earth, and Nadiya upon it;
blessed is Mishra Purandara, in whose house
Gora-raja has taken birth.[^5]

**472.** With these devotees, joyfully singing the virtues of Hari —
conches, mridangas and kartals sounding —
through the fourteen worlds, raining down the rasa of prema,
He will spread the kirtana of [His] virtues.

**473.** The rasa of the virtues of Vrindavana — love, which is all in all —
He will taste Himself and give to all;
to gods, nagas and men, to everyone down to the chandala,
He will give to drink that for which He awakens their thirst."

**474.** Bliss upon bliss are [His] virtues; hear: good upon good —
the treasure of Vrindavana is revealed;
the Lord of all the worlds has taken birth on earth —
in bliss this Lochana Dasa forgot himself.

*Raga varadi; disha: "My Lord, O my life, oh, Gorachand!"*

**475.** Yogis, great sages, Indra, Chandra and the rest in their worlds,
hearing [the news], filled with bliss, dance in delight.

**476.** Narada, full of bliss, roams in delight —
it seems to people as if a dead tree had put forth shoots.

**477.** Roaming thus, he suddenly
sees that dharma has been overturned in people's conduct:

**478.** abandoning charity, vows and austerity, all people
with body, speech and mind honour [only] their wives.[^6]

**479.** Reflecting on this, the sage knew for certain:
this is the Kali-yuga, there is no doubt of it.

**480.** "That for which a proclamation went through the three worlds —
to whom shall I tell it? — here it is, the Kali-yuga has come!"

**481.** Troubled, the sage sat down in meditation,
and suddenly an auspicious voice arose in the sky:

**482.** "I am Jagannatha, the Daru-brahman,[^7] in Nilachala,
on the shore of the ocean, for the deliverance of people.

**483.** Do you not remember the former account?
Through Katyayani's vow you received My command.[^8]

**484.** Go, go, king of sages, to the city of Nilachala
and act according to the command of Jagannatha."

**485.** Narada Muni set out with joy in his heart;
the sound of his vina arose, filling the world.

**486.** Crying "Ha, ha, Jagannatha!" he runs in love
and beheld the moon-like face of the King of the three worlds —

**487.** the abode and shelter of all avataras,
a gracious face full of the rasa of all arts.

**488.** Falling at His feet, the sage says with joined palms:
"Be merciful, Jagannatha — the Kali-yuga has come!

**489.** All people have fallen into the most terrible sin;
devoted to lust and belly, they have gone astray, in great sorrow."

**490.** Hearing this, the Lord, smiling a little, spoke;
touching him with His hand, He told him in private:

**491.** "I tell you this supreme secret:
at My word go to Goloka.

*Raga pahida, tripadi metre*

**492.** Above Vaikuntha there is an abode whose name is Goloka;
Sri Gaurasundara is king there;[^9]
the women are Lakshmi and the others, the one Purusha is Hari,
and all the subjects are full of happiness.

**493.** Radha and Rukmini — these two queens;
all the nagaris are their portions;
hundreds upon hundreds of branches of bhakti, holding the power of these two,
serve [Him] as their attendants.[^10]

**494.** And the goddess Satyabhama, incomparable in beauty and virtue,
the limit of the rasa of all clever wit;
blessed with lila, play and loveliness, with the rasa of all arts —
the supreme woman of the three worlds.

**495.** She who is called Music — with rhythm and the movement of tones —
the world praises as Shabda-brahman;
she is called the fifth Veda — [for one] who understands the distinctions of notes —
she is understanding itself, present equally in all.[^11]

**496.** The men are portions of the Lord, all of Vaishnava lineage;
the city, full of rasa, is named Ranga.[^12]
Such is the greatness of that [abode] — who has the power to tell it?
I cannot tell it with one mouth.

**497.** All the gopis who danced the rasa in Vrindavana
serve [Him] with Radha at their head;
all who were in Dvaraka follow Rukmini —
and all the others who taste rasa.

**498.** There is nothing there but bhakti; unceasingly they sing [His] glory;
though independent, they are dependent [on Him];
all are liberated, and yet, like ordinary people,
they practise bhakti as if humble.

**499.** The four liberations, salokya and the rest, are the power of the Lord of Vaikuntha;
devoid of bhakti, they are independent in themselves;
full of the riches of Lakshmi, they keep no humility —
bhakti alone depends on Another.

**500.** Sugar itself does not know its own sweetness —
another enjoys it;
such is the station of liberation: it bars the path of bhakti —
above all is the yoga of prema-bhakti.

**501.** That abode, beyond the reach of the Creator, is My home;
out of mercy I have come here.
Behold Sri Chaitanya, the Lord of all, golden and tall of stature,
and the pain of your heart will go.

**502.** In whatever form you see Me there, in that form I shall come here;
I shall spread the kirtana of [My] virtues,
remove all suffering, spread the joy of prema
and deliver the people of Kali."

**503.** Narada Muni set out, having heard these wondrous words:
"This speech is beyond the reach of the Vedas:
above Vaikuntha, further still, I shall see Goloka,
whose glory is sung in all the worlds.

**504.** Of liberation and supreme liberation, of the reasoning of the *Bhagavata*
I have heard all the secret words;
what is unknown to the world and the Vedas, unknown, unmanifest —
today I shall see it manifest there."

**505.** In love the sage runs; the sound of his vina is heard —
the people of Vaikuntha rejoiced;
reaching the gate of Vaikuntha, overwhelmed with bliss,
he sings an auspicious song of [His] virtues.

**506.** He saw the Lord of Vaikuntha with all His associates,
seated on a jewelled throne;
falling at His feet, the sage bows down,
and the Lord, raising him, embraced him.

**507.** Smiling, the Lord says: "What is in your heart?
Tell Me quickly, sage, what is in your mind.
My heart is eager: I shall fulfil your word,
I shall make the unseen seen."

**508.** With joined palms the sage says: "You are the Indweller of all,
what more shall I tell You?
That form of Yours of which You told me within, in the form of the Daru-brahman —
that is what I [wish] to see."

**509.** Again the Jewel of virtues says: "I tell you in private:
that form is [My] original, innate form;
His maya,[^13] His reflections — hundreds of avataras —
worship Him with the greatest zeal.

**510.** I am His bodily expansion, pervading all lands,
the all-pervading Vishnu, all in all;
Lakshmi is My attendant, and the four liberations too —
this connection also I tell you.

**511.** I, Vishnu, am His portion; Lakshmi is [His] wealth;
this Vaikuntha is a portion of [His] Vaikuntha;
the four liberations are a shadow of [true] freedom; all, clothed in bhakti,
serve that Master — that Lord of Vaikuntha.[^14]

**512.** Radha alone is the prakriti, her form is prema itself;
to her the supreme Purusha is subject;
in Vaikuntha there is one abode named Maha-Vaikuntha —
there is the union of the three qualities of shakti.[^15]

**513.** Firm is My word, without guile: Gaurahari
is the manifest wish-fulfilling tree of mercy.
Go, sage, go to that Mahaprabhu,
the teacher of all the worlds."

**514.** The king of sages set out, his vina singing Hari's virtues,
his body, helpless with bliss, trembling;
his whole frame thrilled, from foot to head,
tears of prema flooding both his eyes.

**515.** Drunk with the wine of prema, now he is amazed,
now he calls out "Gauranga!",
now he goes half a step, now looks back again and again,
now he weeps, now he runs on.

**516.** Suddenly a breeze blew, cooling the whole body;
a radiance as if surpassing millions of moons;
with the fragrance of the lotus feet the body's bonds grow loose;
whatever one sees there is the glory of Kama.[^16]

**517.** Many Madanas run obediently to serve;
one sees no one there without prema;
there is no knowing of day or night, no seeing of "mine" and "another's";
all are joyful, without sorrow.

**518.** Walking is the play of dance, speech the art of music,
a glance of the eyes is attraction;
there is no movement without play, no companionship without bhava;
the bodies there are formed of rasa.

**519.** Bodies are of consciousness and bliss, the ground is chintamani,
all the trees there are wish-fulfilling trees,
all the cows are like new kamadhenus,
the bushes and creepers are what Uddhava and others long for.

**520.** All the trees are wish-fulfilling trees; among them is one beyond compare,
with jewelled altars on both sides;
on it a golden throne, where Lord Gauranga sits
and sweetly, tenderly, softly smiles.

**521.** An auspicious pot with [mango] twigs right by the throne
touches the great toe of His left foot;
jewelled lamps burn, as if with the rays of the sun,
illuminating and filling the world.

**522.** Radhika on the right, keeping her maidservants near,
a jewelled pitcher in her hand;
on the left Rukmini, keeping her companions near,
fills a golden pot with water bright as jewels.[^17]

**523.** Nagnajiti draws water and gives it into Mitravinda's hands,
Mitravinda into Sulakshana's hands,
she gives it into Rukmini's hands, and the goddess pours it on the Lord's head —
a bathing with the water of the heavenly river.

**524.** Tilottama draws water and gives it into Madhupriya's hands,
Madhupriya into Chandramukhi's hands,
she gives it into Radhika's hands, and Rai pours it on the Lord's head —
a bathing with the water of the Ganga.

**525.** Satyabhama is near, holding divine scents in her hands,
divine garlands, garments and ornaments;
Lakshmana, Subhadra and Bhadra, obedient to Satyabhama,
in turn hand [everything] to her.

**526.** And all the other divine women, by hundreds on every side,
with divine ornaments and divine gifts,
holding jewelled bouquets, stand before the Lord,
crying the auspicious "Jaya, jaya!"

**527.** Such is the bathing of the Lord of Goloka — there is no other;
the Agamas have told the great meditation [on Him]:
a body golden-fair, a mantra of four syllables;[^18]
the Lord of Vaikuntha is by nature dark.

**528.** A dark body with four arms has the Lord of Vaikuntha,
in His four hands four weapons;
but the Lord radiant with gold, golden-limbed, softly smiles —
hear the essence: His body is two-armed.

**529.** At that moment the sage, seeing Gora, the Jewel of virtues,
fell enraptured at His feet;
he cannot open his eyes — and again he longs to look;
he bathed [Him] with the tears of his eyes.

**530.** Having finished His bath, the Lord, smiling, speaking very softly,
lifted Narada up and took him on His lap;
doubt and anxiety went, the pain of his heart was broken;
the Lord speaks loving words very softly.

[^1]: That is, worldly people, judging by appearances, see in the gopis' love for Krishna only a breach of marital fidelity. The Gaudiya Vaishnavas, following the *Bhagavata* (10.33.26–39) and the *Chaitanya-charitamrita* (Adi 4), teach that the gopis' *parakiya-rasa* is the purest prema, wholly unlike worldly passion; Lochana Dasa himself says that by this prema the Lord is conquered.
[^2]: The words of Uddhava. In the edition the verse is followed by Bhaktisiddhanta's anvaya and translation: "Oh! The gopis in Vrindavana, abandoning kinsmen hard to abandon — husbands, fathers-in-law and others — and the path of dharma, served the lotus feet of Mukunda, which the Vedas seek. Shall I obtain any birth at all in Vrindavana among the bushes, creepers and herbs that serve the dust of the gopis' feet?"
[^3]: An echo of the *Bhagavata* (10.47.59), where Uddhava says: "Where are these forest women, tainted by infidelity, and where is this love for Krishna, the Supreme Self!" — to show that such love is given irrespective of merit. The words "unchaste", "lustful" express the worldly view; in the Gaudiya understanding the *kama* of the gopis is pure prema, whose only desire is Krishna's joy (*Chaitanya-charitamrita*, Adi 4.164–171).
[^4]: The raga and disha are given after the 1983 edition; in A the line is illegible.
[^5]: A has the past tense ("has taken birth"); the 1983 edition the future ("will take birth"), as also in v. 474.
[^6]: The 1983 edition has four couplets here: people have abandoned charity, vows and austerity and toil only for the belly; brahmanas have abandoned the worship of Krishna, and kshatriyas, vaishyas and shudras the service of brahmanas; abandoning respect for father and mother, all honour their wives with body, speech and mind. A has a single couplet (from the first and last lines).
[^7]: *Daru-brahman*, "Brahman in wood": the Lord Himself, manifest in the wooden Deity of Jagannatha at Puri (Nilachala).
[^8]: See vv. 257–271: the Lord promised Katyayani (Parvati) to fulfil her vow concerning mahaprasada and to appear in Kali as Gaura; Narada then proclaimed this at His command.
[^9]: So Lochana Dasa: in Goloka, the highest abode above Vaikuntha, Gaurasundara Himself is king. In the Gaudiya Vaishnava understanding (Bhaktivinoda Thakura, Bhaktisiddhanta) Gaura's abode, Navadvipa-dhama, is non-different from Goloka-Vrindavana and is part of it; Gaura is Krishna Himself with the bhava and lustre of Radha.
[^10]: Lochana Dasa sets side by side Radha (source of the gopis) and Rukmini (source of the queens of Dvaraka). The Gaudiya Vaishnavas, following the *Chaitanya-charitamrita* (Adi 4.75–81), teach that Radha is the original *hladini-shakti* and the source of all the Lord's beloveds: the Lakshmis of Vaikuntha, the queens of Dvaraka and the gopis.
[^11]: The subject of the stanza is not named; probably the description of Satyabhama as the embodiment of music continues — or the deity of music is introduced as another dweller of Goloka.
[^12]: Perhaps "Ranga" ("Play") is the name of the city; it may also be understood as "the city full of rasa and play".
[^13]: *Maya* here is His energy, not an illusion that rules Him; the "reflections" (*chhaya*) are His expansions. The expression is Lochana Dasa's own; in Gaudiya theology all avataras are portions and parts (*amsha, kala*) of Krishna (*Bhag.* 1.3.28). In the 1983 edition the second half of the stanza reads: "…hundreds of avataras — He alone is the merciful King".
[^14]: This stanza (like the previous one) differs in the 1983 edition: "I, Vishnu, am His reflection (*chhaya*), Lakshmi the reflection of [His] wealth, this Vaikuntha the reflection of [His] Vaikuntha"; we follow A ("portion"). The thought agrees with Gaudiya teaching: Narayana of Vaikuntha is a *vilasa* expansion of Krishna, and Vaikuntha an expansion of Goloka (*Chaitanya-charitamrita*, Adi 5). Line 3 is obscure; the 1983 edition has "all, established in bhakti, serve…".
[^15]: Lines 3–4 are obscure; perhaps the "three qualities of shakti" are the Lord's three energies: *hladini*, *sandhini* and *samvit* (*Chaitanya-charitamrita*, Adi 4.62). In the 1983 edition line 3 differs: "prakriti on the right and left, named Lalita and Vishakha".
[^16]: Kama (Madana) is the god of love. Here not the worldly Cupid is meant: the spiritual beauty of Goloka is described, where, in the words of the Gaudiya scriptures, Krishna (and Gaura) is *aprakrita-Madana*, the transcendental Cupid who enchants Cupid himself.
[^17]: The bathing (*abhisheka*) of Gaura in Goloka. Among those taking part are queens of Dvaraka (Rukmini, Satyabhama, Nagnajiti, Mitravinda, Lakshmana-Sulakshana, Bhadra), Radhika and companions from Vraja (Chandramukhi, Madhupriya), and Tilottama; Lochana Dasa unites the consorts of Dvaraka and Vraja in one vision.
[^18]: Which four-syllable mantra is meant the author does not say.
