# Madhya-khanda, part 19 (portion 42)

*The section "The Lord's Journey to Nilachala; the Broken Staff" (beginning): at Shantipura the Lord takes leave of the devotees and tells them to chant the name of Hari; Haridasa's plea; the devotees' sorrowful speeches; the Lord promises to live in Nilachala and consoles Shachi; Advaita laments that he feels no prema, and the Lord unties the knot of His cloth*

## The Lord's Journey to Nilachala; the Broken Staff

> *Summary by Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati.* [Then] Bhagavan, having instructed the devotees to do good to all living beings by ceaseless chanting of the name of Hari, prepared to go to Nilachala; Thakura Haridasa, falling at His feet, poured out a humble and sorrowful plea. When the other devotees, telling of the grief of Shachi and Vishnupriya, began to follow the Lord, [the Lord] consoled them and Shachidevi with sweet words and, absorbed in prema, reciting the verse "Rama Raghava, Rama Raghava, Rama Raghava, protect me" and others, set out for Nilachala. Then, [when, having given His staff to Nityananda,] He was walking absorbed in prema in an excess of bliss, Nityananda Prabhu broke His staff, and Gaurahari showed anger towards him.[^1]

*Raga bhatiyari; disha: "Brothers, O brothers, sing the glory and the virtues of Gora Gosani! (murchha) O brothers, my dear brothers, give up worldly desires: as long as you live in the world, do not leave the feet of Mahaprabhu!"*

**1.** Thus the auspicious night turned to a bright morning;
having done His morning duties, the Lord sat on His seat.

**2.** Staff in hand — like the sovereign of all kingdoms;
the reddish cloth glitters on His body.

**3.** To all His own people sitting near Him
the Lord, smiling, speaks thus:

**4.** "Srinivasa and all you devotees,
go, all of you, to your homes.

**5.** I shall go to Nilachala to see Jagannatha,
if the Lord, with a gracious face, shows Me mercy.[^2]

**6.** And you stay here and carry out My order:
ceaselessly, day and night, perform kirtana.

**7.** Establish the name of Hari and service to devotees,
so that, following this dharma, all people may be delivered.

**8.** Let everyone's heart be free from envy;
all of you, honour one another's hearts."

**9.** Saying this, the Lord rose quickly
and, opening His arms, embraces everyone.

**10.** Both His eyes brim with tears of prema,
His throat is tight with tenderness, His voice falters.

**11.** At that very time the venerable Haridasa,
holding a straw between his teeth,[^3] falls at His lotus feet.

**12.** He weeps with a loud wail, in a piteous voice;
the heart of everyone who hears it breaks.

**13.** The Lord was pained, His eyes filled with tears;
with an aching heart He speaks:

**14.** "When will such good fortune be Mine —
to fall and weep at Jagannatha's feet?

**15.** Reaching His lotus feet, I shall tell Him of My distress
and, seeing His face, make My eyes fruitful."[^4]

**16.** As He said this, all the devotees around
fell on the ground and wept.

**17.** Shachi lost her senses and cannot even weep —
she only wants to clasp her son by the neck.

**18.** Some weep with dishevelled hair, clasping His feet;
then, with great effort composing themselves,

**19.** Srinivasa, Haridasa, Murari and Mukunda
venture to say to the Lord:

**20.** "You are the independent Lord, and we are all dependent,
wretched, wicked, sinful, and devoid of devotion besides.

**21.** What can we say, Lord? You have taken sannyasa
and now You are leaving, forsaking all Your servants.

**22.** How will You walk the road all alone?
From whom will You ask food in hunger and thirst?

**23.** You are Shachi's darling, of matchless character;
Vishnupriya served Your feet.

**24.** By Your glance, nectar to the devotees' eyes,
the tree of prema grew in this body before our eyes.

**25.** Great was our hope for the fruit of prema —
and You, taking sannyasa, have emptied that hope.

**26.** Life will not leave our sinful bodies —
and so, having seen You off, we shall go home!

**27.** Are we lowly ones now to go away?
That is not Your way: You are the purifier of the fallen.

**28.** The Creator fashioned Your body from the clay of compassion,
adding the many treasures of delightful pastimes;

**29.** into pure supreme prema he breathed life —
and You manifested a form wondrous in the three worlds.[^5]

**30.** There is none to compare with You in the three worlds, —
yet at Your cruel words the world is in anguish.

**31.** It does not befit You to act thus, Lord:
having planted the tree Yourself, why do You cut it at the root?

**32.** Whoever would go — take them with You,
or else we shall all die, burning in fire.

**33.** Look at Your mother Shachi, forlorn:
we cannot bear her plaintive weeping.

**34.** At Vishnupriya's weeping the earth splits;
Navadvipa has become empty — town and market alike.

**35.** The houses of all the Vaishnavas seem empty,
and each other's houses seem yojanas apart.

**36.** Where You sat, Lord, and spoke of Yourself —
if I see that place I shall die; I shall go there no more.

**37.** I shall hear no more intimate, delightful talks,
I shall not see the rapture of dancing, the spreading of prema.

**38.** At the time of dancing You will embrace us no more;
I shall not see tears of prema in Your reddish eyes.

**39.** I shall hear no more the nectar of Your roaring:
who has shut the doors of my ears and eyes?

**40.** How shall I live without seeing Your moon-like face?
Who has blinded me while I still have eyes?

**41.** Do not send us away, Lord — we shall go with You:
Your cruel words burn our whole body.

**42.** As a hunter, with the sound of a bell,
lures a doe near and then seizes and kills her, —

**43.** such, it seems, is Your love too — now we understand.
Why, having enticed us, do You then kill us?

**44.** In separation from You all the devotees will die —
how then will You bear the name 'Affectionate to devotees'?

**45.** How will You take leave of Shachi?
Who will tell her this?

**46.** Vishnupriya will die on merely hearing of it:
settle this matter Yourself."

**47.** When the devotees had spoken such words,
the Lord, compassionate at heart, began to speak:

**48.** "Hear My word in full, all you devotees:
never shall I be cruel to you.

**49.** I shall surely dwell in Nilachala;
you will always come there and see Me.[^6]

**50.** Prema will grow boundlessly, more than before;
the world will float in the sankirtana of Hari's name.

**51.** In no one's heart shall I leave sorrow and grief:
I shall immerse all people in the ocean of sankirtana.

**52.** Be it Vishnupriya or My mother Shachi —
whoever worships Krishna, in that one's embrace I abide."

**53.** Hearing this, all fell at His feet:
"Swear to it, Lord — to what You have said!"

**54.** "Truly, truly, truly," the Lord repeats again and again,
"truly I shall dwell in Nilachala."

**55.** Shachidevi cannot stand steadily on her feet;
she rose, holding on to the hands of two people:

**56.** "Where will you go, having become pitiless?
Not seeing you, my son, I shall die.

**57.** Everyone else will see your face many times —
but I, unfortunate, shall see it no more.

**58.** You yourself, child, have consoled everyone —
but who, my son, will console me?

**59.** I have no one else in this world,
and Vishnupriya is like a spear lodged in my breast."

**60.** Smiling, with a compassionate heart, the Lord says:
"You are dying of vain grief, forgetting what you knew before.[^7]

**61.** Go home, keep no grief in your heart,
live with everyone, free from envy."

**62.** Prostrating at His mother's feet,
the Lord consoled her with well-ordered words.

**63.** Having consoled His mother, the Lord cries "Haribol!"
and goes off quickly — a loud wailing arises.

**64.** Advaita Acharya goes along with the Lord;
after going two dandas,[^8] the Lord looks back.

**65.** Mahaprabhu stopped, for the Acharya had fallen behind;
the Acharya came up, holding his waist,

**66.** his face drawn, beads of sweat upon it,
and with an aching heart he asks the Lord:

**67.** "You are going to a foreign land — that is my sorrow;
but besides it another thing burns my breast.

**68.** I make known to You what is in my heart —
surely, Lord, You will answer me.

**69.** All Your own people, in separation from You,
weep in anguish at Your lotus feet, —

**70.** why then does my sinful heart not melt?
It is hard as wood; there are no tears in my eyes.

**71.** There is no one more wicked than I:
in separation from You no prema rises in my heart."

**72.** Hearing this, the Lord smiled and embraced him:
"I shall tell you the truth of it — hear My words.

**73.** Because of your prema I could not have left —
therefore I keep your prema tied up in a knot."[^9]

**74.** Saying this, He untied the knot of His cloth, —
and the Acharya, thinking of Him, became intoxicated with prema:

**75.** from the ocean of his eyes flowed five, seven streams;
in boundless prema he lost all awareness.

**75+1.** Advaita Prabhu fell, crying "Sri Chaitanya!"[^10]
and rolls in the dust in separation from Chaitanya.

**75+2.** Seeing that Advaita could not go on,
the Lord ties the knot again, [binding] his prema.

**76.** Hastily the Lord restrained it,
and then the wise Acharya composed himself.

**77.** "That is why your prema does not rise:
because of your prema I could not go on.

**78.** I am under the sway of your prema — hear, Acharya:
remember the past and consider your task."[^11]

**79.** Saying this, the Lord went off quickly,
and all the Vaishnavas went to their homes.

**80.** Lochana Dasa tells of Gora's lordly ways:
this is not sannyasa — it is a spear left lodged in the breast.[^12]

[^1]: The OCR of the summary is badly garbled; gaps are filled by sense and from the table of contents in the edition's preface (ch. 15: "the Lord instructs the devotees to do good to all living beings by chanting the name of Hari; when He prepares to go to Nilachala — Thakura Haridasa's humble words to the Lord; when the devotees begin to follow — He consoles them with the sweetest words and leaves for Nilachala reciting the verse 'Rama Raghava, Rama Raghava, protect me' and others; on the way Nityananda breaks the staff; the Lord shows anger"). The summary runs ahead: the journey to Nilachala and the broken staff are in the next portion.
[^2]: "The Lord" here is Jagannatha.
[^3]: A sign of utmost humility. Vv. 11–15 — cf. Murari 3.4.28–30.
[^4]: The Lord speaks in the mood of a devotee longing for the darshana of Jagannatha.
[^5]: A poetic image: the Lord's form is eternal and spiritual, and no one created it; the devotees, in their love, speak of Him as the most beautiful of creations.
[^6]: Vv. 47–54 — cf. Murari 3.4.24–26. The Lord kept this promise: the devotees from Bengal came to Him at Puri every year.
[^7]: "What you knew before" — that her son is Bhagavan Himself (this is hidden from her by yogamaya, which nourishes her motherly love: en/37, note 16). "Vain" (michha) means "futile", not "unreal" (en/37, note 14).
[^8]: A danda is a measure of time, about 24 minutes.
[^9]: Cf. Murari 3.4.32–33: Advaita asks why he feels no love on hearing of the Lord's departure, and the Lord replies: "If your love had shown itself, how could I have left?"
[^10]: Vv. 75+1 — 75+2 are found only in the 1983 edition; the 1903 edition does not have them.
[^11]: Apparently the Lord reminds Advaita — Maha-Vishnu in the form of a devotee (en/27, note 8) — that it was he who by his prayers called Him into the world, and of the work for which He came, the spreading of sankirtana.
[^12]: In the 1983 edition chapter 15 ends here; the next chapter, "The Journey to Nilachala", begins with v. 81.
