# Madhya-khanda, part 12 (portion 35)

*The section "Before Sannyasa": seven days of radiance in Chandrashekhara's house; Srivasa asks about the dharmas of the yugas, and the Lord proclaims sankirtana; the Lord's longing for Vrindavana and Murari's persuasion; the dream of the sannyasa mantra; the arrival of Keshava Bharati; Mukunda reveals the Lord's resolve to the devotees; the devotees' lament and the Lord's answer: He is burning in separation from Krishna*

## Before Sannyasa

> *Summary by Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati.* In answer to Srivasa Pandita's question Sriman Mahaprabhu told of the dharmas of the four yugas [and revealed that] in the Kali age [the best dharma] is the sankirtana of the name; [then,] in the bhava of Radha, He became agitated, [crying,] "Where is Vrindavana? Where is Lalita? Where is Govardhana?" Then, at Murari's words, Sriman Mahaprabhu grew calm and [again] gave Himself fully to [kirtana] with the Vaishnavas, and one day He told [His mother] that He had received the sannyasa mantra in a dream. Some days later Keshava Bharati came to Sriman Mahaprabhu's house, and the Lord received him fittingly. [At the sight of the sannyasi] Sriman Mahaprabhu's separation from Krishna became unbearable. Learning that Mahaprabhu would take sannyasa, the devotees, in fear of the coming separation from Him, began to lament bitterly, and the Lord, telling them [of the pain of separation from Krishna and of the nature of samsara], consoled them.[^1]

*Raga varadi; refrain: "My life is Gorachand!"*

**1.** I shall tell a wondrous story, unknown to the world —
never seen or heard of anywhere on earth.

**2.** Let no one doubt it for a moment in his heart:
the Lord revealed this for the good of all people.

**3.** Having danced and sung His fill in Chandrashekhara's house,
the Lord returned home in joy.

**4.** Sri Chandrashekhara Bhattacharya rejoices —
I shall tell of the marvel in his house.

**5.** The Lord had come there to dance, and from His radiance
it was as if moons had risen — hundreds of thousands of moons.[^2]

**6.** A wondrous, cool light, more delightful than nectar;
one cannot look at it — as if lightning flashed on all four sides.

**7.** It gladdens the heart — one longs to look,
but one cannot open one's eyes: the brilliance prevents it.

**8.** The people of the town of Nadia were astonished:
what a marvel they had seen in these days!

**9.** Coming there, everyone asks the Vaishnavas:
"Do you know what this means? Tell us, then!"

**10.** All the Vaishnavas answer: "What do we know?
Vishvambhara, the jewel of qualities, came here to dance —

**11.** this alone we know, and nothing more:
His deeds are unknown to the world and to the Vedas."

**12.** For seven days this sea of radiance did not break;
for its brilliance no one could tell day from night.

**13.** Ever new, ever more joyful deeds
the son of Shachi reveals — the dharma of mercy.

**14.** The next day Shrinivasa, the best of brahmanas,
asks the Thakura about what was in his heart:

**15.** "In the Kali age the sankirtana of Hari's name and qualities —
why is it said to give the full fruit, and [the ways of] the other yugas less?"

**16.** Hearing this, the Thakura says: "Listen, Shrinivasa:
you have asked a good question — I shall explain in detail.

**17.** In the Satya age, when dharma was complete, they attained by meditation alone;
in the Treta age the generous-minded practise the dharma of yajna;

**18.** in the Dvapara, it is said, the essence [of dharma] is the worship of Krishna;
but in the Kali age no one is capable of these works.

**19.** The Thakura Himself is Bhagavan in the form of the name:
in the Kali age the name of Hari is endowed with all powers.

**20.** In the three ages beginning with Satya, all the great souls
served Narayana by meditation, yajna and worship according to rule.

**21.** In the sinful Kali age people's conduct is wicked —
and for this very reason mercy has turned the other way.[^3]

**22.** The Thakura Himself is in His own form of sankirtana —
and in the Kali age all perfections are attained without effort.

**23.** What in Satya and the other ages is attained with great hardship,
by the Lord's mercy is attained with ease in the Kali age."[^4]

**24.** Placing the lotus feet of Narahari on my head,
Lochana Dasa tells of the sweetness of Gauranga.

**25.** Thus in joy and gladness the days go by —
and suddenly sorrow rises in the Lord's heart:

**26.** "I cannot, I cannot stay here —
I shall go to see the land of Vrindavana!

**27.** Where is My Kalindi, the Yamuna, Vrindavana?
Where are My Bahula, Bhandira, Govardhana?

**28.** Where have My Radha, Lalita and the others gone?
Where have My Nanda and Yashoda gone?

**29.** Where have My Shridama and Sudama stayed?" —
and calling "Dhavali! Shangali!" He runs in longing.[^5]

**30.** Now, holding a straw between His teeth, piteously
He sobs aloud, looking round on every side:

**31.** "How shall I leave this round of worldly existence?
Where shall I go to attain the feet of Nanda's son?"

**32.** Saying this, He tore the sacred thread from His neck —
so unbearable had the grief of separation from Krishna become.[^6]

**33.** Calling "Hari, Hari!" He heaves deep sighs;
tears pour down in streams, but He says nothing distinct.

**34.** His body thrills, His face has grown red;
seeing this, Murari speaks these words to Him:

**35.** "Listen, listen, Mahaprabhu, Gaura Bhagavan:
nothing is impossible for You — [but] I shall speak of the consequences.

**36.** You are free both to stay and to go —
and yet do not disregard my words.

**37.** If You now leave for distant lands,
all the Vaishnavas will grow self-willed at heart;

**38.** self-willed, they will do whatever they please,
and all will enter again the house of samsara.

**39.** All that You have done, Lord, will come to nothing —
this I tell You for certain."[^7]

**40.** Hearing this, the Lord fell silent:
He could not refute what Murari had said.

**41.** Thus several more days passed in merriment;
the people of Nadia could not look their fill of Him.

**42.** Gladdening His mother's heart and eyes,
Gaurahari passes His time in play with Vishnupriya.[^8]

**43.** With kinsmen and friends He lives in great happiness
and delights everyone in Navadvipa.

**44.** With the Vaishnavas — the pastimes of kirtana;
the ladies of the town, looking at Him, sigh with longing.[^9]

**45.** His form is the wonder of the three worlds, and in it is the charm of a nagara,
the very limit of playful grace, rasa and loveliness.

**46.** And on Him beautiful ornaments sparkle;
in the hair falling to His shoulders malati flowers gleam.

**47.** The sandal tilaka is drawn charmingly;
His cloth has a red border — the loveliest attire in the three worlds.

**48.** His own people and all the townsfolk —
each sees Him according to his own feeling.

**49.** And so, being with His own, the Lord
with a soft smile tells them all a dream:

**50.** "Listen, all: I saw a dream last night:
suddenly a jewel of a brahmana came to Me

**51.** and spoke into My ear a certain mantra of sannyasa;
even now it is vivid in My mind.

**52.** Since the mantra entered My ear,
My heart has not been My own.

**53.** How shall I leave My beloved Lord of life?
Leaving Him, what work would I do?

**54.** More beautiful than a sapphire, supremely beautiful,
He sits on My breast and smiles without cease."

**55.** Hearing this, Murari Gupta replied:
"Take that mantra as a compound with the genitive case."[^10]

**56.** Hearing this, the Lord said:
"Your words do not steady My mind.

**57.** The more I restrain Myself, the more the tears rise;
say nothing to Me — listen to Me:

**58.** such is the power of the words — what can I do?
I cannot transgress it, whatever you may say."

**59.** Hearing this, all were troubled at heart —
says Lochana Dasa, with pain in his heart.

**60.** Some days later Sri Keshava Bharati came,
the best of sannyasis, of utterly pure mind —

**61.** full of great splendour, the best of renunciants, a maha-bhagavata,
a mountain of merit gathered in former births.

**62.** Arriving unexpectedly, he saw Vishvambhara;
seeing Vishvambhara, the best of sannyasis rejoiced.

**63.** The Thakura rose and bowed at his feet;
at the sight of the sannyasi tears of prema streamed from His eyes.

**64.** That king of sannyasis gazes intently at the Lord:
the best of sannyasis, of great understanding, grasped how matters stood.

**65.** Keshava Bharati Gosani said:
"Are You not Shuka, or Prahlada? — so it seems to me."

**66.** Hearing this, Lord Vishvambhara
wept twice as hard — tears pour from His eyes.

**67.** Then the sannyasi, amazed, speaks again,
having reflected and become certain in his mind:

**68.** "You are the Lord, Bhagavan — I know it for certain;
You are the life of all people, there is no doubt."

**69.** Hearing this, the Lord wept:
"When shall I attain Krishna's feet?

**70.** Your love for Krishna is so great
that you see Krishna everywhere.[^11]

**71.** When shall I be able to see Krishna?
When shall I have such garb as yours?

**72.** In search of Krishna I shall go from land to land:
where shall I go to find Krishna, the Lord of My life?"

**73.** Having told the sannyasi [what lay in His heart], Vishvambhara,
the Lord, bowed to him to the ground and goes home.

**74.** Seeing Srivasa, the Lord said:
"Take the sannyasi to your house."

**75.** Hearing the Lord's words, Srivasa Thakura
took the sannyasi home and fed him abundantly.

**76.** Having taken his meal and spent that day there,
the best of renunciants went his way at dawn.[^12]

**77.** In the morning Shrinivasa, coming to the Lord,
with folded hands told Him that the sannyasi had left.

**78.** Hearing this, the Lord, with longing in His heart,
thinking of the sannyasi, went home.

**79.** Coming home and reflecting within,
Gaurahari resolved: "I shall take sannyasa."

**80.** Mukunda understood it from hints and from [the Lord's] look;
to keep the Lord, he sought every possible means.

**81.** He came where all the devotees were
and, weeping, spoke, [falling] at the feet of all the devotees:

**82.** "Listen, listen, all, to what I say:
this Lord Vishvambhara will take sannyasa.

**83.** While He is here, look at Him to your eyes' content;
hear the words of His holy mouth to your ears' content.

**84.** The Lord will leave His home,
will leave His mother and all His servants."

**85.** Hearing this, all, with aching hearts,
took counsel together and pondered what to do.

**86.** "The independent Lord will stay in no one's power" —
saying this, the devotees fell into dread.

**87.** Falling on the ground, covered with dust, they weep:
"O Lord of our life, our Lord Vishvambhara!

**88.** Alas, alas, Mahaprabhu! Where are You going, forsaking [us]?
The serpent of Kali will seize and devour us.

**89.** In fear of Kali we took refuge in You, Lord;
in fear of You the serpent of Kali does not come near us now."

**90.** At that time Lord Vishvambhara came there;
seeing Srivasa Pandita, He said:

**91.** "Listen, listen, O dear brahmana Shrinivasa:
I shall tell you one thing, if you will not be frightened.

**92.** To earn prema I shall go to distant lands,
and then I shall bring it to you — listen, best of brahmanas.

**93.** As a merchant boards a boat and sails to a far country,
enduring all manner of hardships to earn wealth,

**94.** and, bringing it home, maintains his kinsmen —
so I too shall bring and give you the wealth of prema."[^13]

**95.** Hearing this, Srivasa Pandita says:
"Lord, what is life if we cannot see You?

**96.** While a man lives, his kinsmen maintain him;
when he has left his body, they perform shraddha and tarpana for him.

**97.** It is to those who live that You will give the wealth of prema —
but without seeing You, all will die."

**98.** Mukunda says: "Lord, the body burns,
within, the life burns — and yet does not depart.

**99.** We are all low, wicked and ill-behaved;
and You are a deceiver, crafty-hearted: now we understand Your ways.[^14]

**100.** We simple folk, not understanding You,
took refuge in You, giving up the world;

**101.** giving up dharma and karma, we made Your feet our all —
and You, having made us outcasts, why do You forsake us?

**102.** Knowing from the scriptures that You are the Purifier of the fallen,
we took refuge in You, giving up all dharmas.

**103.** And now You are going away, forsaking us —
this is not right, Lord — I humbly say.

**104.** Not perceiving Your crafty mind, we took refuge in You —
but Your heart is harder than a thunderbolt, cruel.

**105.** Catching outside the fragrance of the lotus's nectar,
I thought it was the same within — so it was in my heart.

**106.** Now I know how hard Your heart is:
like a pot of poison with milk on top.

**107.** Like a wooden sweetball dusted with camphor:
one cannot swallow it, not seeing what it is.[^15]

**108.** If You go to distant lands, what use is life?
Why have You become cruel to all?"

**109.** "If I do not see Your face for even a moment, I die!" —
weeping and weeping, Murari says:

**110.** "Listen, listen, Vishvambhara, Gaura Bhagavan:
unworthy Murari speaks — pay heed!

**111.** You planted a wondrous tree, holding it with Your fingers,
You made it grow day and night, watering it and loosening the soil;

**112.** moment by moment You tended it, covered it with great care,
and laid all kinds of jewels about the tree's root —

**113.** and now, when the time of flowers and fruit has come, You cut the tree down:
we shall all die, our hearts will burst.

**114.** Ceaselessly, day and night, we know nothing else;
even in dreams we see Your moon-like face.

**115.** Worldly desires do not come near me:
[mine is] the breeze from Your feet, rare in the world.

**116.** You will go to distant lands, leaving everyone —
and the tiger of samsara will seize and devour us all.

**117.** You were merciful — why have You become pitiless?" —
saying this, they all together fell at [His] feet:

**118.** "O friend of the poor, Lord, master of the masterless!
O Deliverer of the fallen, Lord of the world!"

**119.** Some, with straw between their teeth, plead piteously,
some, raising their arms, call again and again.

**120.** The Lord says: "You are My very own servants;
I shall tell you My inmost conviction — listen."

**121.** As soon as He began to speak, His voice broke,
His reddish lotus eyes filled with tears.

**122.** In a piteous voice He speaks half-words;
unable to restrain Himself, for a moment He falls silent.

**123.** "You are distressed by fear of separation from Me —
but My body is in torment from separation from Krishna.

**124.** For your own happiness you cause Me pain:
what kind of love is this you have for Me, you people?

**125.** In separation from Krishna My heart burns;
My senses are scorched — a great fever has seized My body.

**126.** Even such a [loving] mother seems to Me like fire,
and your words seem mixed with poison.[^16]

**127.** Life without Krishna I do not count as life:
what use is such a life — like that of beasts and birds?

**128.** Like a corpse that has all its limbs;
so creepers, leaves and trees live by their breath.

**129.** Dharma and karma without Krishna are like a brahmana without the Vedas,
like a faithful wife without her husband, a fish without water.

**130.** There is no point in setting up a household without means;
it is like an unlearned man sitting in an assembly of the learned.

**131.** In separation from Krishna My life throbs —
whatever else you may say does not enter My ears.

**132.** Putting on a yogi's garb, I shall go from land to land —
to wherever I may find news of the Lord of My life."

**133.** Saying this, the Lord weeps, fallen on the ground,
and tore the sacred thread from His body.

**134.** "Krishna, Krishna!" He calls with an anguished cry,
and in a piteous voice weeps: "Lord of My life!"

[^1]: The OCR of the summary is badly garbled; gaps are filled by sense and from the table of contents in the edition's preface ("the Lord, telling Srivasa of the dharmas of the four yugas, reveals the superiority of sankirtana; in the bhava of Radha, agitation [crying], 'Where is Vrindavana, where is Lalita?'; calmed by Murari's words — kirtana and pastimes; the account to mother Shachi of receiving the sannyasa mantra in a dream; the arrival of Keshava Bharati; the Lord's separation from Krishna; the devotees' grieving words in anxiety about the Lord's sannyasa; the Lord consoles the devotees"). In the poem the Lord tells the dream to all His own (vv. 49–50). In the 1983 edition vv. 1–24 belong to the previous chapter, and the new chapter ("सन्न्यास प्रसङ्ग") begins with v. 25.
[^2]: Vv. 1–13 — cf. Murari 2.17.1–4: for seven days the Lord's radiance did not fade in Chandrashekhara's house.
[^3]: The more wicked the age, the greater the Lord's mercy: He Himself comes as the holy name and sankirtana.
[^4]: Vv. 14–23 — cf. Murari 2.17.5–10; Bhagavata 12.3.52: what was attained by meditation in the Satya age, by yajnas in the Treta and by worship in the Dvapara is attained in the Kali age by the kirtana of Hari.
[^5]: Shridama and Sudama are Krishna's cowherd friends; Dhavali and Shangali are His cows. The Lord is in the bhava of separation from Vraja; Bhaktisiddhanta's summary says "in the bhava of Radha".
[^6]: Cf. Murari 2.17.12–13. The Lord tears off His thread once more in v. 133.
[^7]: Vv. 34–40 — cf. Murari 2.17.14–19: Murari reminds Him that people follow the Lord's example.
[^8]: Vishnupriya is the Lord's eternal consort, His shakti; her pastimes with the Lord are not worldly.
[^9]: On nagari-bhava see en/09, note 9.
[^10]: Cf. Murari 2.18.1–5. The sannyasa mantra in its wording speaks of identity with Brahman ("I am He"); Murari proposes to understand it through the genitive case (shashthi-samasa): not "I am He" but "I am His", that is, the eternal servant of the Lord. The Lord answers that the very power of the words will not let Him rest (v. 58).
[^11]: The Lord, playing the part of a devotee, deflects Keshava Bharati's praise and ascribes his vision to his own love for Krishna. Vv. 60–72 — cf. Murari 2.18.7–15.
[^12]: Keshava Bharati is the future sannyasa-guru of the Lord at Kantakanagara (Katwa). Vv. 73–79 — cf. Murari 2.18.15–16.
[^13]: Cf. Murari 2.18.19–22.
[^14]: The words "deceiver", "crafty", "cruel" (vv. 99–108) are reproaches of love (pranaya), like the gopis' reproaches to Krishna, not abuse: Mukunda speaks in the grief of separation.
[^15]: Both proverbs are about one who is sweet outside but different within. The 1983 edition has three more couplets after this (a comparison with a woman whom a seducer abandons to disgrace); they are absent in Bhaktisiddhanta's edition. In A v. 107 is misprinted as "১০৮".
[^16]: The extreme of separation from Krishna (vipralambha), when everything, even the dearest, burns like fire. Cf. Murari 2.17.12–13.
