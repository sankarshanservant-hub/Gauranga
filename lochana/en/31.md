# Madhya-khanda, part 8 (portion 31)

*The section "Pastimes with the Devotees and the Deliverance of Jagai and Madhai" (end): the repentance of Jagai and Madhai; the Lord takes their sins upon Himself and embraces them. The section "Wondrous Pastimes of Mahaprabhu in the Bhava of Bhagavan": the poor brahmana Vanamali and his son see Krishna in Vrindavana in the Lord — the "new Creator"; the bhava of Nrisimha in Srivasa's house; the singer of Shiva and the Lord in Shiva's bhava on his shoulders*

**127.** At the sight of Mahaprabhu and the sound of the sankirtana
they stand astonished, staring motionless [after Him].[^1]

**128.** Within themselves they reflect in their hearts,
pondering Mahaprabhu's words:

**129.** "We have committed a sin such as not even a beast would commit:
what not even it would do — we struck a sannyasi!"[^2]

**130.** Reflecting thus, they became pure in heart —
see, see the power of Mahaprabhu's mercy!

**131.** In the anguish of repentance both run with faces raised [to the sky],
and the people of Nadia, seeing it, were amazed.

**132.** Coming to Mahaprabhu's door,
they cry out wildly: "Thakura! Thakura!"

**133.** The Lord, sitting at home among His own, [says]:
"Who is calling Me? Look at the outer door.

**134.** Murari, bring them to Me at once."
Receiving the command, [Murari] embraced them both and brought them.

**135.** Seeing the Lord, with a cry of anguish
they fell at [His] feet on the ground — both brothers weep:

**136.** "You are the Purifier of the fallen, the ocean of mercy,
the Lord of all the worlds and above all the Friend of the wretched!"

**137.** The Lord is an ocean of mercy, His heart full of compassion:
seeing the suffering of the afflicted, He melts at once.

**138.** Raising them, He asked: "Listen, Jagai and Madhai,
why do you weep? Why have you come to Me?

**139.** You two are the foremost masters of Navadvipa,
clever men — why then do you weep now?"

**140.** Hearing this, Jagai and Madhai say:
"By Your mercy we have come to You.

**141.** How many sins we have committed — the killing of cows and women!
Beyond all count and measure — how many men we have killed!

**142.** Shame on our lordship over Nadia!
This body of ours [is stained] with the killing of gurus and the killing of brahmanas.

**143.** Brahmana women, Muslim women, the wives of gurus we did not leave alone;
chandala women and others — we spared no one.

**144.** To the people of the world we have done nothing but harm;
rites for the gods and the ancestors are not to our liking.

**145.** What more can we, worthless as we are, say to You?
There are not as many hairs on our heads as the sins we have committed.

**146.** All people speak of a sinner named Ajamila,
but he is no greater sinner than we," they said. —

**146+1.** "Out of love for his son he uttered the name 'Narayana',
and the brahmana, obtaining a divine body, went to Vaikuntha[^3].

**147.** The name 'Narayana' delivered him,
but [Narayana Himself], coming in person, cannot deliver us.[^4]

**148.** For us there is no deliverance — You Yourself know it:
for what merit will You show us mercy?

**148+1.** Even if a thousand scribes were to count for a hundred births,
still the sins of us two could not be counted"[^5].

**149.** Hearing such anguished words
and seeing that they were without pretence, the Thakura was filled with boundless compassion.

**150.** Seeing the suffering of the afflicted, the Thakura Himself suffers —
compassion embodied, oh, the very form of mercy!

**151.** The ocean of mercy reveals His mercy:
taking them by the hand, He led them to the Jahnavi.

**152.** The people of Nadia ran to see the wonder:
the Lord reveals prema — a sight unheard of!

**153.** The brahmanas and good people all stand and watch;
before them all the Lord speaks words of mercy:

**154.** "I shall take your sins upon Myself —
and you yourselves give Me your sins."

**155.** Saying this, He holds out His hand for the tulasi,[^6]
but the two brothers in fear do not give the tulasi.

**156.** In His mercy Gaura Bhagavan speaks again:
"Jagai and Madhai, give Me your sins as a gift!"

**157.** Jagai and Madhai say: "Listen, Lord:
our sins are so many that they cannot even be written down.

**158.** We are the lowest of the low, a storehouse of sins, sin itself;
to give You our sins — our hearts tremble with fear."

**159.** Hearing this, His eyes filled with tears,
and in a voice deep as thunder from a cloud He cried: "Hari bol!"

**160.** Again, asking for the gift of their sins, He holds out His hand —
and Jagai and Madhai placed the tulasi in His hand.

**161.** On all sides rose the cry: "Hari, Hari bol!"
The Lord embraced Jagai and Madhai.

**162.** He delivered the two brothers, Jagai and Madhai —
such sinners were granted the touch of the Lord!

**163.** Their voices choke with prema, their speech is broken,
their clothes are soaked with tears.

**164.** Their bodies thrill, their whole frames tremble;
falling at [the Lord's] feet on the ground, they speak in anguish:

**165.** "Is there anywhere another such Thakura —
an ocean of mercy, the great Purifier of the fallen!"

**166.** He delivers such sinners as Jagai and Madhai:
by the touch of His divine body they dance, overflowing with prema.

**167.** Having taken upon Himself the sins of Jagai and Madhai,
the Lord Vishvambhara Hari Himself dances.[^7]

**168.** Where else is such a Thakura, a treasury of mercy,
who sees no fault and loves so boundlessly?

**169.** He delivers souls and dances with joy —
on this Lochana Dasa firmly sets his hope.

## Wondrous Pastimes of Mahaprabhu in the Bhava of Bhagavan

> *Summary by Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati.* One day, while Sriman Mahaprabhu was joyfully sporting with the devotees, a poor brahmana named Vanamali, a native of East Bengal, came there with his son, and Sriman Mahaprabhu cast a merciful glance upon them. At this they suddenly became mad with the bliss of prema, and when the sankirtana began, they saw Sriman Mahaprabhu right there in the form of Shyamasundara and fainted with supreme bliss; on regaining consciousness they praised Sriman Mahaprabhu, the Giver of prema, and called Him the "new Creator", since, freeing [people] from Vedic rites, He bestows prema on all living beings. Then one day in Srivasa's house, while listening to the hymn "The Thousand Names of Vishnu", [the Lord] suddenly roared in the bhava of Nrisimha; and when everyone in fear began to flee in all directions, He restrained His bhava of Nrisimha. On another day, when a worshipper of Shiva began to sing of Shiva's qualities, Gaurasundara, greatly pleased at hearing the qualities of the [great] devotee Shiva sung, climbed onto his shoulders and danced in the bhava of Shiva. On yet another day, when a brahmana woman touched Sriman Mahaprabhu's feet, Sriman Mahaprabhu, deeply grieved, leapt into the Ganga; the devotees drew Him out onto the bank and satisfied Sriman Mahaprabhu with all kinds of prayers and praises. Then Sriman Mahaprabhu taught that everyone who has obtained the rare human body must serve Hari, and that without serving Hari there is no meaning in human birth; [then are described] the embrace [of Murari], [his] humble words, the manifestation [by the Lord] of His divine form, the bathing [of the Lord] with Ganga water by Srivasa Pandita, the cleaning of the temple together with the devotees headed by Advaita Acharya, and so on; [at the end] the author, singing Gaura's qualities, instructs everyone to serve Gaura.[^8]

*Raga dhanashi; refrain: "O Lord, Dvijachand! To deliver the world He lays many snares"*

**1.** Glory, glory to Gadadhara, Gauranga and Narahari!
Whoever hears of Gauranga's qualities obtains prema.

**2.** Hear another wondrous story of another day:
in Navadvipa the supreme, great treasure was revealed.

**3.** The Lord stays at home with His own
and softly lets fall words from His lotus mouth.

**4.** A stream of a river of nectar flows unceasingly;
bathed in it, the devotees are manifestly intoxicated.

**5.** Thus the Lord abides in joyful play —
and suddenly a beggar came there,

**6.** named Vanamali, and with him his son;
born in a brahmana family, he lives in the east, in Banga.[^9]

**6+1.** Scorched by the fire of poverty, he had come to these parts,
and, seeing Gaurachandra, the brahmana was filled with gladness[^10].

**7.** He saw Vishvambhara surrounded by devotees —
and the brahmana and his son were filled with joy.

**8.** The brahmana and his son reflect [within];
he cannot speak — his voice chokes in his throat.

**9.** "It is well that I became poor:
because of poverty I came [here] — and was purified.

**10.** I know for certain: Vishvambhara is Bhagavan;
I have realized in my heart that He is no one else.

**11.** Today, I feel, my birth has been fulfilled:
I have seen Vishvambhara, the golden one, a mine of virtues.

**12.** As soon as I saw Him, my eyes and heart were soothed,
the wretched, unbearable fire of poverty was quenched.

**13.** As the heart is content on tasting nectar,
so at the sight of Gaurachandra my whole body is [as if] bathed."

**14.** Then Gaura Bhagavan, seeing him,
looked with merciful eyes upon the two brahmanas.

**15.** Joyfully He sings of Hari's qualities with the two,
and by the Lord's grace they obtained the treasure of prema.

**16.** In joy the brahmana dances, his son dances;
in a moment the thread of samsara was cut for him.

**17.** Such is Mahaprabhu Gora, the ocean of mercy:
there is no greater Friend of the wretched than He.

**18.** The next day, in the midst of the sankirtana,
the Thakura Vishvambhara, king of dancers, dances.

**19.** Then those two brahmanas suddenly
saw a certain boy — and their hearts were startled:

**20.** the Lord in His golden body had become dark,
a yellow cloth shines on His hips, in His hand a splendid flute,

**21.** a crest of peacock feathers flutters in the wind.
[Seeing] that form, all [His] companions sing.

**22.** In Vrindavana, in the depths of the forest, beside Radha,
they saw the Dark One, the king of dancers.

**23.** There too they see the Yamuna, Mount Govardhana,
Bahula, Bhandira, Madhuvana and the other [forests];

**24.** they see the cows, the gopis, the cowherd boys and Talavana —
in Navadvipa they saw Madana-Gopala.[^11]

**25.** Seeing this, the brahmana fell unconscious:
his body thrilling, his eyes in tears.

**26.** Again and again he cries "hum!", slaps himself like a wrestler,
shouts "Here is Krishna, Krishna!" — and a crowd gathered round.[^12]

**27.** Seeing this, the Thakura again stopped dancing
and, crying "Hold him, hold him!", again caught hold of the brahmana.

**28.** Hear, all of you, this song of Gora's qualities:
this new Creator reveals mercy.

**29.** Removing the bonds of karma, He gives the treasure of prema —
where else is there such a Thakura?

**30.** Beyond samsara He creates His own world —
prema-bhakti directed to Himself, beyond the objects of the senses.[^13]

**31.** Every day He wears divine garlands, sandal paste, prasada,
[yet] there is no attachment in Him — only love for all.

**32.** Unattached, He cannot live without the company [of devotees];
beyond karma, He performs acts according to injunction.

**33.** All that is proper according to the Vedic injunctions
He performs in full — yet in effect the result is the opposite.

**34.** Thus He reveals His treasure of prema-bhakti;
therefore I call Him: "The new Creator, a jewel."

**35.** Such is my Gora-raya, the ocean of mercy:
all without effort obtain the supreme treasure.

**36.** There is no other such Thakura, the Giver of prema —
says Lochana: worship the new Creator!

*Refrain: "Whoever has once seen Gora's beauty cannot forget it — he pines away all his life"*

**37.** And then one more day — hear of a wonder —
a joyful festival in the house of Srivasa Pandita.

**38.** That Srivasa Pandita performs the rite for the ancestors
and with a most pure heart listens to the "Thousand Names".

**39.** At that time Gaurahari came there
and, fulfilling His heart's desire, listens to the "Thousand Names".

**40.** As He listened, the bhava of Nrisimha arose in Him:[^14]
His two eyes reddened with anger, His hair stood on end.

**41.** His whole body thrilling, His colour crimson,
again and again a fierce "hum!", a lion's roar.

**42.** Suddenly He seized a club and rushed [forward] —
seeing this, all the people trembled at heart.

**43.** Everyone flees without even tying up their hair:
they cannot bear the Lord's wrathful bhava.

**44.** Seeing the people take to flight, Nrihari[^15]
in a moment threw down the club, restraining His bhava.

**45.** The son of Shachi is the seed of all avataras:
whoever comes to His mind, that He becomes.[^16]

**46.** Restraining His bhava, the Lord sat down on His seat
and, astonished, spoke:

**47.** "I do not know — have I committed some offence?
Or is something on your minds?"

**48.** Hearing this, all said:
"What offence of Yours? What are You saying?"

**49.** Srivasa said: "Whoever has seen You [thus]
has been released from all bonds."

**50.** The next day — hear this story, all of you —
there suddenly came a singer who sang of Shiva.

**51.** Bowing at the feet of Gaurahari,
he sings of Mahesha's qualities with a joyful heart.

**52.** In great ecstasy he calls out: "Shiva! Shiva!" —
devotion to Shiva shows itself in his body.

**53.** Hearing him, the Thakura rejoiced at heart:
at the song of Shiva's qualities His happiness grew abundantly.

**54.** Then, in the bhava of Shiva, the dancing began;
the singer of Shiva forgets himself in happiness.

**55.** There is no one more fortunate than he:
the Thakura Himself climbed onto his shoulders.

**56.** Carrying [the Lord] on his shoulders, the singer dances in joy,
and the Lord's eyes turned red in His bhava.

**57.** In the bhava of Shiva He speaks Shiva's words:
the damaru rattles, with His mouth He sounds a horn.

**58.** "Rama! Krishna!" He calls, weeping and laughing;
now Gora weeps in the bhava of Shiva.[^17]

**59.** Srivasa Pandita knows all this truth:
with attentive mind he recites a hymn to Shiva.

**60.** Sri Mukunda Datta recites the "Mahimna-stava";
they dance in joy — they know the whole truth.

**61.** The Thakura came down from the singer's shoulders
and, devoted to Hari, sings abundantly of Hari.

**62.** He dances in joy, as if drunk with wine,
and happily sings Hari's qualities — an ocean of bliss.

**63.** The ocean of mercy reveals mercy —
hearing this, Lochana Dasa is lost in joy.

[^1]: Vv. 127–169 are the end of the section (Bhaktisiddhanta's summary is at its beginning, portion 30). Cf. Chaitanya-bhagavata, Madhya 13; Chaitanya-charitamrita, Adi 17. Murari (2.13) has only the Lord's promise to deliver Jagai and Madhai. In the 1983 edition the second line reads: "purified [in heart], they stand motionless".
[^2]: The sannyasi is Nityananda, the avadhuta. The first line in Bhaktisiddhanta's edition is not entirely clear; we read "মৃগে", "a beast". The 1983 edition has: "There is no sin we have not committed; and what we had not committed — [we have committed now]: we struck a sannyasi."
[^3]: V. 146+1 is found only in the 1983 edition (the 1903 edition lacks it); cf. en/30, v. 67.
[^4]: On Ajamila see en/30, note 11.
[^5]: V. 148+1 is found only in the 1983 edition (the 1903 edition lacks it); cf. en/30, v. 62+1.
[^6]: A ritual gift (dana) is handed over by placing a tulasi leaf in the recipient's hand; the Lord asks the brothers to give Him their sins as a ritual gift.
[^7]: Sin cannot touch the Lord: "the powerful are not stained [by what stains others], like the all-consuming fire" (Bhagavata 10.33.29–30). In accepting the sins of Jagai and Madhai the Lord shows His causeless mercy; cf. Chaitanya-bhagavata, Madhya 13, where the Lord likewise takes all their sins upon Himself, on condition that they sin no more.
[^8]: The summary covers the whole section (vv. 1–135); this portion contains vv. 1–63, the rest is in the next. The OCR of the summary is garbled in places; gaps are filled by sense and from the table of contents in the edition's preface (there: "the Lord's teaching on serving Hari, mercy to Murari, Murari's praise, the Lord's manifestation of His divine form, the bathing [of the Lord] by Srivasa, the author's singing of Gaura's qualities and his instruction to serve Gaura"). "The bhava of Bhagavan" — the Lord reveals His own divinity and His own forms (Krishna, Nrisimha and others); this is not "possession" by some other being (cf. v. 45). Cf. Murari 2.11–2.13.
[^9]: This is not Vanamali Acharya, the matchmaker of the Lord's first marriage (en/17). Banga is East Bengal.
[^10]: V. 6+1 is found only in the 1983 edition (the 1903 edition lacks it).
[^11]: Bahula, Bhandira, Madhuvana and Talavana are forests of Vraja; Madana-Gopala is Krishna who enchants even the god of love. In the Gaudiya Vaishnava understanding Gaura is Krishna Himself, who has covered His dark complexion with Radha's golden radiance (Chaitanya-charitamrita, Adi 4); here for a moment He reveals His original form to the brahmanas. Cf. Murari 2.11.3–6. The 1983 edition has "and their retinue (avarana)" instead of "and Talavana".
[^12]: Literally "he set up a market" — a Bengali idiom: he raised such a commotion that people came running.
[^13]: Vv. 28–36 explain why the Lord is called the "new Creator" (navina vidhata): the old Creator, Brahma, made the world of karma and rites, while Gaura, removing the bonds of karma, "creates His own world" — prema directed to Himself. Bhaktisiddhanta explains in his summary that, freeing [people] from Vedic rites, He bestows prema on all. This is no rejection of the Vedas: the highest aim of the Vedas is bhakti; the Lord Himself follows the injunctions to set an example, but gives what rites cannot give (vv. 32–33).
[^14]: Nrisimha (Narasimha) is the Man-Lion, the avatara who appeared for Prahlada's sake. The bhava of Nrisimha is the Lord's own, not an alien "possession": the Lord is the source of all avataras (v. 45). The "Thousand Names" is the Vishnu-sahasranama. Cf. Murari 2.11.6–12.
[^15]: The text has "Narahari" ("Man-Hari", i.e. Nrisimha) — here the Lord Himself in that bhava, not Narahari Sarakara. Cf. Murari 2.11.9 ("Nrihari").
[^16]: Krishna is the source of all avataras (Bhagavata 1.3.28; Chaitanya-charitamrita, Adi 2), and Gaura is Krishna Himself; therefore the forms of Nrisimha, Varaha and others that Gaura reveals are His own.
[^17]: Shiva is the greatest of Vaishnavas (Bhagavata 12.13.16). Rejoicing in the song about His devotee, the Lord takes on his bhava and, like Shiva, who eternally worships Rama, calls: "Rama! Krishna!" The damaru and the horn are Shiva's emblems; the "Mahimna-stava" is Pushpadanta's Shiva-mahimna-stotra. Cf. Murari 2.11.13–19.
