# Adi-khanda, part 6 (portion 14)

*The beginning of the Pauganda-lila: Vishvambhara begins to learn His letters; the chudakarana rite; a game on the sandy bank of the Ganga following the tracks of birds; Jagannatha Mishra's anger and Shachi's intercession; Jagannatha's dream: a brahmana reveals to him that his son is the Lord Himself*

## Pauganda-lila (The Boyhood Pastimes)

> *Summary by Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati.* When Gaurasundara, having heard Murari expound the scripture of yoga, began to mock him by imitating his gestures and manners, Murari in anger spoke harsh words to Him. In return, Sriman Mahaprabhu, to show the worthlessness of yoga and what becomes of the yogi, urinated into Murari's plate during his midday meal and instructed him in the supremacy of devotion to Krishna. Then the author, having described how Sriman Mahaprabhu enacted the pastime of sankirtana with the boys of His age, the sannyasa of Vishvarupa as told in a conversation between Murari and Damodara, the grief of Shachi and Jagannatha and so on, proceeds to describe Gaurasundara's Pauganda-lila. In due time the chudakarana and other rites of Gaurahari were duly performed. As prescribed, at an auspicious moment chalk was placed in His hand;[^1] He […][^2] Mishra Purandara, learning [in a dream] that [his son] is Bhagavan, was enlightened; but when this mood of his […], he became absorbed once more in his parental love (vatsalya). At the appointed time Gaurahari's upanayana rite was performed according to rule. Then the teaching on the avataras of the four yugas is set out. At the end of the Dvapara-yuga, Bhagavan Himself, Sri Krishna, the son of the king of Vraja, appears. In the Kali-yuga, assuming the feelings and the lustre of Srimati Radha, He descends in a hidden way in the form of Sri Gaurasundara to preach the dharma of the age — the sankirtana of Hari's name. Intoxicated with prema, He gave prema away, offering it at the door of every living being. During His life as a householder, by forbidding His mother to eat grains on the day of Ekadashi, He taught the living beings of the world. When Jagannatha Mishra fell ill, He instructed about the impermanence of human life and comforted His mother in many ways. When Jagannatha Mishra passed from this world, Shachi-devi mourned her husband; Gaurahari too mourned His father. After that He began His studies […].[^3]

*Raga dhanashi*

**457.** Thus one day Mishra Purandara,
looking at Vishvambhara, began to reflect.

**458.** On an auspicious day, at an auspicious moment, under a good tithi and nakshatra,
he placed chalk in His hand[^1] — a wonderful occasion it was.

**459.** Day by day He studies, the Guru of the world;
seeing this, Shachi and Jagannatha forget themselves.[^4]

**460.** Thus some days passed in play,
and Jagannatha and Shachi both took counsel together.

**461.** Having resolved in their minds on Vishvambhara's chudakarana,[^5]
they then invited all their friends and kinsfolk.

**462.** Having considered the auspicious moment, tithi and day,
they made their decision firm: "We shall perform the chudakarana."

**463.** In the town of Nadiya every house rejoiced;
they invited brahmanas and worthy men honoured by all.

**464.** The brahmanas recite the Vedas, the singers sing songs;
the sacrificial rite was performed as was proper.

**465.** All the respectable women raise the cry "Jaya, jaya!";
to everyone were given perfumes, betel nut and sandal.

**466.** All kinds of instruments sound — the joy is boundless:
conches, dundubhis, bheur and kahal horns resound;

**467.** mridangas, pataha drums and bronze cymbals resound;
the sound of the sanai is sweetness itself to hear.

**468.** On all sides cries of "Hari!" fill the sky;
then the chudakarana and the ear-piercing were performed.

**469.** The whole town of Nadiya rejoiced:
seeing Vishvambhara's face, people forget themselves.

**470.** In the market, on the road, at the ghat — wherever anyone goes,
when two meet, they sing of Gorachand's glories.

**471.** Seeing another's son, this is what they feel in their hearts:
"It is Shachi and Jagannatha's good fortune — such a son!

**472.** It is the good fortune of Navadvipa and the good fortune of the world;
whoever sees that form — his eyes are worthy of praise."

**473.** On another day, on the sandy bank of the Ganga,
He is playing with the boys at the ghat.

**474.** Following the tracks of birds on the sand,
He walked, stepping exactly in the birds' footprints.

**475.** Saying this,[^6] Mahaprabhu Sri Gaurachandra
set a rule for the game with the boys:

**476.** "Whichever boy steps beyond these footprints
loses the game at once.

**477.** Whoever can run ahead and catch [another]
wins the game and rides on his shoulders.

**478.** Riding on his shoulders, he whips his back with a switch,
and the other carries him on his shoulders to the agreed ghat."

**479.** Saying this, He runs over the sand with the children;
from great exertion sweat streams down His body.

**480.** At that time Jagannatha Mishra Purandara
went to the bank of the Jahnavi to bathe.

**481.** Seeing his son's game, he grew angry;
seeing Him so worn out, the father's heart began to burn:

**482.** it was as if a golden lotus had wilted in the heat,
and the sweat on His face was like honey oozing out.

**483.** Calling out again and again, Mishra follows Him;
seeing His father, Vishvambhara was greatly abashed.

**484.** Out of shame He does not raise His face, fear in His heart;
the pandit himself went up to Gorachand,

**485.** took him by the hand and led his son away;
all the boys went off to their homes.

**486.** Having bathed in the Ganga, Jagannatha came home
and, on coming home, scolded Gorachand at length:

**487.** "All your studies are ruined, as with some good-for-nothing!
Why, with your foolish mind, do you roam about all the time?

**488.** You are a brahmana's son, yet you have no proper conduct —
I am giving you now the fruit you deserve."

**489.** Saying this, Jagannatha took a switch in his hand;
as he threatened, Shachi caught hold of his hand:

**490.** "Do not beat my son! He will not play any more;
He will always study, sitting beside you."

**491.** Vishvambhara hid in His mother's lap
and says very softly, "I won't play, I won't play."

**492.** Pushing Jagannatha back and shielding her son,
[Shachi says:] "Do not beat my son — He is dying of fright!"

**493.** Saying this, Shachi-devi took her son on her lap
and wiped His face with the end of her sari.

**494.** "Let my son not study, let Him be a dunce —
as long as, a dunce, He lives a hundred years!"

**495.** Hearing Shachi's words, Mishra Purandara
began to answer her in anger:

**496.** "If our son is a dunce, how will he make a living?
What brahmana will give him a daughter in marriage?"

**497.** Then Jagannatha looked at his son's face:
the son looks at his father with frightened eyes.

**498.** Mishra's heart burns, though outwardly he is stern —
and, overcome by love, he threw away the switch.

**499.** With tears in his eyes he took his son on his lap
and gently persuades Him with sweet words:

**500.** "Of one who studies and listens to his lessons, my boy, people speak well;
and I will give You a silk dhoti and bananas."

**501.** Thus in joy and gladness the day went by;
having finished his evening rites, Mishra went to bed.

**502.** He fell asleep, and in the third watch of the night
Mishra had a dream and was thrown into turmoil.

**503.** Rising at daybreak, he called everyone:
"I have seen a dream — I will tell you all.

**504.** I saw a brahmana of majestic stature,
radiant like the rays of the sun.

**505.** His divine body was adorned with jewels;
I could not look at him — the whole house was ablaze.

**506.** In a voice deep as thunder he said to me:
'Why do you regard Vishvambhara as your son?

**507.** I am God, Narayana — and you do not know it;
why do you regard [Me] merely as your son?[^7]

**508.** A beast does not know the touch of the philosopher's stone;
you take Me for your son — but for you that is only natural.[^8]

**509.** I know all the shastras, I am the Guru of all the gods;
why then do you take a switch in hand to teach Me?'

**510.** Such a dream I have seen today,
and ever since, my heart does I know not what."

**511.** Shachi was overjoyed, and so were all the people;
all gaze at Vishvambhara's face.

**512.** Shachi and Jagannatha embrace Him, pressing Him to their hearts:
"Our son is Vishvambhara Gaurahari!

**513.** He whose boundless glory the Vedas do not know,
whom Shiva, Sanaka and the others do not attain in meditation —

**514.** who can know the greatness of this Greatest One?
The god Gaura has been born as my son!"

**515.** But even as they spoke, tender parental love (vatsalya) awoke in them,
and every feeling of His majesty withdrew.[^9]

**516.** Hearing of the dream, all the people rejoice —
the glories of Gora are joyfully sung by this Lochana Dasa.

[^1]: "Hate khadi", "chalk in the hand": the rite of beginning to learn letters (vidyarambha), when the child first traces letters with chalk.
[^2]: Here in the OCR of the summary the lines are overlaid by text from a neighbouring page (vv. 416–418), and part of the sentence is lost; in substance it concerns the games with the boys, the father's anger (vv. 473–500) and Jagannatha's dream (vv. 501–516).
[^3]: The name or word before "began His studies" is unreadable in the OCR.
[^4]: The 1983 edition has three more couplets here: in two or three days He learned all the letters and constantly writes out the names of Krishna — "Rama, Krishna, Govinda, Gopala, Vanamali". A does not have them.
[^5]: Chudakarana is the samskara of the first cutting of the hair, leaving a lock (shikha) on the crown.
[^6]: That is, announcing this game; the 1983 edition has "thus".
[^7]: In the dream the brahmana speaks in the person of the Lord Himself. In Murari (1.07.14–16) the brahmana speaks to Jagannatha about his son, and Mishra replies that even if his son is Narayana, it is a father's duty to correct Him.
[^8]: So A ("e bada sahaja"). The 1983 edition reads "e bada sahasa": "this is great audacity".
[^9]: Cf. the Bhagavata (10.8.43–45): Yashoda, having seen the universe in Krishna's mouth, is at once covered again by His yogamaya and sees in Him only her son. The parental love (vatsalya) of Shachi and Jagannatha does not admit the thought of the Lord's majesty, and He Himself by His own will hides it for the sake of their love.
