# Adi-khanda, part 12 (portion 20)

*Banga-vijaya: the Ganga, loving the Lord, rises to touch His feet; a brahmana devoted to the Ganga recognizes Him as Bhagavan and tells how the Ganga was born from the Lord, who melted at Shiva's singing; the Lord journeys to East Bengal, sanctifies the Padmavati and gives the name of Hari to all; Lakshmi serves Shachi in Navadvipa*

## Kaishora-lila (The Pastimes of Youth): Banga-vijaya — the Lord in East Bengal

> *Summary by Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati.* One day, when Sri Sri Mahaprabhu went at sunset to see the Ganga, Ganga-devi, seeing her beloved Lord, surged up in love and tenderly began to touch His feet again and again. The acharyas, mishras, bhattas and other brahmana pandits who at that time, having finished their evening prayers on the bank of the Ganga, were praising the Ganga, were astonished to see the water suddenly rise and fell to pondering; and then a certain brahmana devoted to the Ganga, by her grace, came to know that the Lord is Bhagavan Himself. In explaining the cause of this rising of the water the author relates a Puranic story: once the devarshi Narada, together with Mahadeva and Ganesha, was singing the glories of Sri Hari, and at that time Bhagavan Sri Hari Himself appeared there. The perspiration that then came out on His divine body as He heard the kirtana about Himself is the Ganga, Brahman in liquid form. […] Then Bhagavan Gaurahari wished to go to East Bengal […]; having shown boundless mercy to the people of East Bengal, He returned to Navadvipa, but before that Lakshmi-devi, bitten by the serpent of separation from the Lord, disappeared from this world. Therefore Shachi-devi […]; and Gaurahari, consoling His mother, […].[^1]

*Raga shri;[^2] disha: "How wondrous — behold: the Darling of Navadvipa, dear as life — are haya!"*

**1.** Listen, everyone, to another story:
the song of Vishvambhara's virtues is ever new.

**2.** [He] went with His friends to see the Ganga;
the day was ending, the lovely evening hour had come.

**3.** On both banks of the Ganga all the brahmanas and good people,
bowing to the Ganga, were offering her praises as every day.

**4.** With pitchers on their hips the townswomen go
and, faces unveiled, gaze at the goddess Ganga.

**5.** Countless mishras, acharyas, bhattas and pandits,
so many pious people of excellent conduct —

**6.** all stand on the bank of the Ganga and gaze at her:
the pure waters of the Ganga are adorned with many flowers.

**7.** With scents, sandal, garlands and fine bananas
young men and women, the old and children worship her.

**8.** The Ganga, purifier of the three worlds, flows swiftly:
in her love for the Lord the Ganga cannot contain herself.

**9.** The goddess Ganga surged up, her waters rose,
and with a murmur she touched the Lord's body.

**10.** Longing to touch Him again, the goddess Ganga rises;
the people grew doubtful, thinking to themselves:

**11.** "Every day we see the Ganga — she is always the same,
but today — a wondrous force, a roaring is heard.

**12.** There has been no rain, yet the water rises;
the current runs ever swifter, the waters surge."

**13.** So all the people conjectured.
[But there] was a brahmana devoted to the Ganga.

**14.** By the Ganga's grace his heart was pure,
and that brahmana knew everything — past and future.

**15.** Seeing the Ganga's festival, he grows ever more joyful;
he pondered and pondered — and it was revealed to him.

**16.** Suddenly he sees: Mahaprabhu Vishvambhara,
surrounded by His friends, standing by the Ganga.

**17.** The Lord gazes at the Ganga with great love;
His body grew twice its size, so did the hairs on His body stand on end.

**18.** His eyes, reddened with tenderness, brim with tears;
seeing this, the brahmana found a witness [to what had been revealed] to his heart.

**19.** "This is He — Bhagavan, and none other!"
So pondering, he went up to the Lord.

**20.** Coming close to the Lord, he stands and sees:
the Lord is beside Himself with the Ganga's love.

**21.** The Lord in His heart knows the Ganga's heart;
the Ganga comes forward and touches His hands.

**22.** But touching His hands did not fulfil the Ganga's desire:
under the guise of waves the Ganga clings to His feet.

**23.** In ecstasy the Lord cries "Haribol!"
and, losing control of Himself, embraces His companions.

**24.** He turned crimson — thus prema begins;
the hairs on His body stood up like the filaments of kadamba flowers.

**25.** In her love for the Lord the Ganga abides in His heart:
hundreds of streams flow from the ocean of His eyes.

**26.** Moisture flows from every pore — people say, "It is sweat";
[but this] is the ocean of prema surging — Brahman in liquid form.

**27.** On all sides all the people cry "Hari, Hari!";
the ocean of prema surged in waves of bliss.

**28.** All the people of Nadiya were astonished;
the brahmana devoted to the Ganga understood the matter.

**29.** "This Lord, Vishvambharadeva, is Bhagavan Himself:
seeing Him, the Ganga rises" — so he realized.

**30.** Falling at His feet, the brahmana cries aloud:
"At last the Ganga has shown me her grace!

**31.** He whom the best of yogis and sages cannot attain in meditation —
such a Mahaprabhu I have seen today with my own eyes!"

**32.** He rolls on the ground, weeping and crying out;
the brahmana forgot himself in the bliss of prema.

**33.** All the people stand around;
and the brahmana openly tells the ancient story.

**34.** Seeing the brahmana beside himself, Thakura[^3] went away
to His home, His heart full of bliss.

**35.** The brahmana tells the original story, and all listen —
how the goddess Ganga was born

**36.** and for what reason the goddess Ganga rises now:
"I shall tell you all — listen attentively.

**37.** Once the great Mahesha Thakura[^4]
was singing of Krishna's virtues in great, abundant bliss.

**38.** Narada Thakura sang, and Ganesha played the drum;
the hairs of their bodies stood on end from head to foot.

**39.** All three sang a beautiful melody together;
the waves of sound-Brahman[^5] pierced the universe.

**40.** First, it was Mahesha himself, and in the ecstasy of love for Krishna besides;
Narada had his vina, and Ganesha was on the drum as well!

**41.** The Lord, unable to restrain Himself, came to the place
where Mahesha and Narada were singing His virtues together.

**42.** He said: 'Do not sing My virtues, listen, Mahesha:
I do not well understand the essence of your singing.

**43.** At your singing I cannot remain in My body:
the bonds of the body loosen, and it becomes liquid.'

**44.** Hearing Thakura's words, Mahesha smiles:
'I shall sing and see what the essence of this is.'

**45.** So saying, he sings His virtues with still greater delight;
the sound filled the universe — this earth and the sky.

**46.** The Lord's body melted, His form grew thin;[^6]
in alarm Mahesha stopped his singing.

**47.** When he stopped singing, [the Lord's] mind grew calm;
this is that water of mercy famed in the world.

**48.** This water of mercy is called Brahman in liquid form;
all proclaim it to be Janardana in the form of a holy place.[^7]

**49.** It is very, very rare in this world:
Brahma filled his kamandalu with it and kept it.

**50.** King Bali was a devotee of the Lord;
to show him grace, the Lord appeared [as Vamana].

**51.** The Lord asked for land on which to set three steps:
with two steps He covered the three worlds,

**52.** and the third foot He placed on Bali's head.
There is no other Lord so merciful!

**53.** Hear also another wonder — the glory of the third step,
whose mercy blessed the three worlds:

**54.** with the tip of that foot's toenail the shell of the universe was pierced,
and to that foot Brahma lovingly offered water for washing.[^8]

**55.** The water from the Lord's lotus feet [people] reverently place on their heads;
that is why the Ganga is called 'born of the third step'.

**56.** Such is Thakura — Mahaprabhu Vishvambhara:
behold, all of you, here He is before your eyes!

**57.** Seeing Him, the goddess Ganga remembered the past
and in love and tenderness began to rise.

**58.** The Lord looks at the Ganga with a loving gaze,
and Gora's body is sweeter to her than nectar.

**59.** Under the guise of waves she touches His feet again;
I have realized this by inner experience and told you all."

**60.** Hearing this, all the people rejoiced;
joyfully Lochana Dasa sings of Gora's virtues.

*Raga dhanashi; disha: "Are, the sweetness of my Gora's lotus feet! The bees, His devotees, fly up, circle and fall [upon them]. Are are haya!" (murchha). "Listen to this wondrous tale, the song of Gora's virtues — a name auspicious to the ear!"*

**61.** Thus [the Lord] spent some days in happiness,
in joy and play together with His friends.

**62.** One day He suddenly resolved within Himself:
"I shall go to the eastern country — for the good of all people.

**63.** Everyone says it is the land the Pandavas avoided;[^9]
[the Padma there] is the Ganga and yet not the Ganga: this is the proof of it.

**64.** By My touch the Padmavati will become blessed;
all the people will know no one but Me."

**65.** Having thus reasoned within Himself,
the Lord told His mother: "I shall go to earn money."

**66.** The Lord sets out on the journey together with His own;
Mother Shachi's heart is tossed with anxiety.

**67.** With an aching heart Shachi says to her son:
"Listen, my child, to what I tell You.

**68.** You are going to a distant land to earn money —
how shall I live without seeing You?

**69.** As a fish without water cannot live,
so it is with me without You.

**70.** Thinking of Your moonlike face,
I shall die, my child, not seeing You."

**71.** Hearing His mother's words, Lord Damodara[^10]
humbly answered her, consoling her:

**72.** "Do not fear separation from Me:
I shall soon come back to you."

**73.** To Lakshmi the Lord said with a smile:
"Be diligent in serving My mother."

**74.** Whatever His mother said, the Lord paid no heed:
setting out on the auspicious journey, He goes, softly smiling.

**75.** Mahaprabhu set out with His companions
and travels about, merry, with a joyful heart.

**76.** Wherever Lord Vishvambhara went,
the people there, seeing Him, lost their heads.

**77.** Seeing His beauty, no one can take their eyes away;
some say: "If only we could see this beauty day and night!"

**78.** The women, seeing His face, say:
"Today our birth is fruitful, our eyes are fruitful!

**79.** What fortunate mother bore Him in her womb?
Never have we seen so beautiful a body!

**80.** What fortunate woman, having worshipped Hara and Gauri,
has gained a husband of such beauty, such virtues?

**81.** The radiance of His body surpasses new gold,
the build of His body Mount Sumeru.

**82.** His natural beauty has no equal in the world,
and the sacred thread makes it lovelier still.

**83.** One could die seeing the smile on His beautiful face!"
He entered and remained in the hearts of the loving women.

**84.** One fortunate woman who knew the truth of Krishna's rasa,
guessing, spoke the very essence:[^11]

**85.** "Long, beautiful eyes surpassing lotuses,
and in them a wondrous, lovely, tremulous glance.

**86.** It seems to me this is Radha's Beloved in such a form —
I see before me a body of Radha's complexion."[^12]

**87.** [The Lord] bathed in the Padmavati as prescribed:
by the touch of His feet the river became like the Ganga.

**88.** The swift Padmavati with her sandbanks
is beautiful with crocodiles, tortoises and fish.

**89.** On her banks live brahmanas and good people;
noble men and women bathe at her ghats.

**90.** By Vishvambhara's bath the Padmavati was purified:
she takes away the sins of all who bathe in her.

**91.** Prema-bhakti for Krishna's lotus feet arises
in whoever bathes in her, if only he does not blaspheme the Vaishnavas.

**92.** All who lived on the banks of the Padmavati,
seeing Gaurachandra, made their eyes blessed.

**93.** Gaurahari wanders along the banks of the Padmavati;
that land, clasping His feet, became devoted.

**94.** Receiving His cool feet, the earth became cool;
the goddess thrilled with joy — all that was inauspicious departed.

**95.** Hari first of all delivered that land with great care:
He removed [the stigma] of the land the Pandavas avoided.

**96.** Chandalas and the fallen, the good and the wicked —
the Lord, Himself entreating them, gave all the name of Hari.

**97.** Clean or unclean, what customs, what rules —
He did not regard them and carried all across the ocean of existence.

**98.** Fitting out the boat of the sankirtana of the name,
the Lord, Himself entreating, carried all souls across.

**99.** And whoever runs away — Gaurahari catches and embraces him
and, as the helmsman, carries him to the other shore.

**100.** Never, in any age, has such mercy been heard of:
in what avatara, where, did anyone beg for sins?[^13]

**101.** He purified all, equally disposed towards all,
and made them fit for prema for Radha and Krishna.

**102.** The Lord gave knowledge generously, without end:
all became pandits in days, fortnights, months.

**103.** The Lord, the ocean of mercy, master of all the worlds,
revealing His mercy, purified people's minds.

**104.** Thus the Lord stays in the company of good people,
while here in Navadvipa are Lakshmi and Shachi-devi.

**105.** Faithful Lakshmi-devi, for whom her husband is her very life,
serves Shachi joyfully, as is proper.

**106.** She tidies the Deities' room, sweeps and cleans the house,
[prepares] incense, lamps, offerings, scents, garlands and sandal.

**107.** Having prepared everything, she takes it to the Deities' room;
her daughter-in-law's good nature makes Shachi forget herself.

**108.** Shachi-devi is won over by her daughter-in-law's conduct,
and the daughter-in-law thrills with joy at Mother Shachi's love.

[^1]: The end of the summary (on the journey to East Bengal and Lakshmi's departure) is almost illegible in the scan of the edition; it is translated from the surviving words, and gaps are marked […].
[^2]: The name of the raga is illegible in the scan of A; raga shri follows the 1903 edition (the 1983 edition has pathamanjari). In the 1983 edition this section is the "Fifth Chapter".
[^3]: Thakura ("master") — here the Lord Himself.
[^4]: Mahesha — Shiva; Lochana Dasa respectfully calls him, like Narada, "Thakura".
[^5]: Shabda-brahman — Brahman as sound, transcendental sound.
[^6]: This is the Puranic story of how Vishnu melted on hearing Shiva's song, and so the Ganga was born. The Lord's body is spiritual, and His "melting" is a manifestation of His love for His devotees' singing, not any loss to Him: Bhaktisiddhanta in his summary understands it as the perspiration that appeared on the Lord's body in ecstasy at the kirtana about Him — that moisture is the Ganga.
[^7]: I.e. the Ganga is the Lord Himself (Janardana) in the form of sacred water.
[^8]: When the Lord as Vamana-Trivikrama strode upward, the nail of His foot pierced the shell of the universe, and the waters of the Causal Ocean — the Ganga — poured in; Brahma washed that foot (Bhagavata 5.17.1, 8.21.1–4). Cf. portion 19, vv. 119–120.
[^9]: East Bengal was traditionally "the land the Pandavas avoided" (pandava-varjita): in their wanderings the Pandavas did not visit it, and it was held to be impure. The river Padma (Padmavati) is a branch of the Ganga, but the Ganga's sanctity was not acknowledged for it: "the Ganga and yet not the Ganga".
[^10]: Damodara — a name of Krishna; cf. v. 86.
[^11]: Here (vv. 76–86) again Lochana Dasa's motif of nagari-bhava (see portion 09, note 9); cf. Murari 1.11.6–10. In the 1983 and 1903 editions there are two more couplets after v. 86: the Lord, hearing the women, turns and looks at them, and they forget themselves in love; A does not have them.
[^12]: I.e. Gaura is Krishna Himself, Radha's Beloved, who has taken on Radha's complexion and mood; so too the Gaudiya Vaishnava tradition understands Him (cf. Chaitanya-charitamrita, Adi 1.5).
[^13]: I.e. the Lord Himself begged people to give Him their sins and take the name of Hari.
