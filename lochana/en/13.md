# Adi-khanda, part 5 (portion 13)

*Balya-lila (conclusion): Murari Gupta prostrates himself before the child Nimai, reveals to His parents that their son is Bhagavan, and tells Advaita Acharya about Him; Nimai and the boys hold a kirtana, and passing pandits join them; Murari tells Damodara Pandita of Vishvarupa's sannyasa; the grief of Shachi and Jagannatha; Vishvambhara promises to take care of His parents*

**400.** Here, meanwhile, Shachi and Jagannatha together
are fondling their son: "You are my whole wealth!
Wherever I go, whatever sorrow I meet,
I look at Your moonlike face, and all is forgotten."

**401.** Saying this, both kiss Him on both cheeks
and vie with each other to take Him on their lap.
At that very moment Murari came straight there —
from joy he cannot utter a word.

**401a.** Seeing him, Shachi and Jagannatha hastily
rose and went to greet the vaidya;
but he said nothing to anyone and forgot everything else,
gazing at Gorachand's face.[^1]

**393a.** His whole body thrills with ecstasy
from head to foot; streams of tears flow from his eyes;
his reddened eyes — these are the witnesses of prema;
in a faltering voice he babbles half-words.

**394a.** Unable to stand steady, he falls at His feet
and bows down again and again;
seeing this, Vishvambhara crept
into His mother's lap, as if He understood nothing.

**395a.** Shachi and Jagannatha say: "Ah, what have you done, what have you done!
We regard you as equal to a god.
This very little boy is fit for your blessing —
what a great breach of custom you have committed![^2]

**396a.** All people extol you, calling you a sage among the shudras;[^3]
what offence has my boy committed against you?
Whatever may come, let it fall on me — may the child's years increase!
Bless Him, saying, 'Live long!'"

**397a.** Saying this, holding his hands, bowing and entreating,
[stand] Shachi and Mishra Purandara;
and Murari, smiling, says: "This son of yours,
Vishvambhara, is the God of the god of gods.

**398a.** Cherish the boy, keep Him close to you — later you will understand this;
no one is as fortunate as you.
Keep these words of mine treasured in your heart, guarding the secret:
Vishvambhara is that very Bhagavan."

**399a.** Saying this, Gupta the vaidya spoke of nothing else
and went away with a joyful heart;
his heart overflowing with bliss, having seen Gora's feet,
he went to the house of the Acharya.

**400a.** His name is Advaita Acharya: he is the abode of all virtues,
he is the teacher of all people.
Falling at his feet, Murari humbly says:
"You are the wish-fulfilling tree for all devotees.

**401b.** I have seen a wonder: the son of Mishra Purandara,
Nimai Pandita Vishvambhara,
merrily plays His childhood games with all the boys —
I have seen deeds beyond the human."

**402.** Hearing this, the jewel of the twice-born thundered "hum-hum";
his whole body filled with thrills of ecstasy:
"This is a secret, a secret — I tell you in private:
He is that very Brahman, the Relisher of rasa, in a beautiful form."[^4]

**403.** Saying this, they embraced, both forgetting themselves in joy,
but they do not disclose their conviction [to others].
The Lord of all the worlds has come to earth out of mercy —
His glories are sung by this Lochana Dasa.

*Raga bhatiyari; disha (numbered as a couplet in the edition):*

**404.** The cry "Hari! Hari!" is heard, filling all the directions;
clapping His hands, [crying] "Jaya, jaya!", the jewel of the twice-born dances.

**405.** Gathering together all the boys of His age,
He has started a fine game — a kirtana of Hari's glories.

**406.** Surrounding Him on all sides, the boys cry "Hari, Hari!"
and the Lord, overwhelmed with joy, rolls on the ground.

**407.** "Sing, sing!" He calls in a voice deep as thunder,
and saying, "Come here, come!", He embraces the boys.

**408.** At the touch of His divine body a boy forgets himself —
see how the boy, overwhelmed, is weeping!

**409.** From head to foot — thrills of ecstasy; streams of tears flow;
clapping his hands, the boy cries "Hari, Hari!"

**410.** The boys surround Him on all sides, and in the middle is the lion Gora,
as if intoxicated bees had encircled a honey-filled lotus.

**411.** At that time a few pandits were passing along that road
and suddenly saw Vishvambhara's game.

**412.** They see the wonderful game of the boy Gora:
on everyone's neck are garlands strung of wildflowers;

**413.** with their mouths they cry "Hari, Hari!" and clap their hands,
and in the middle Gorahari dances about in joy.

**414.** Forgetting themselves, all the pandits joined them,
and, clapping their hands, they too cried "Hari, Hari!"

**415.** Whoever comes or goes along the road is entranced at the sight;
women with water-pots on their hips stand and gaze.

**416.** Hearing the cries of "Hari, Hari!" and the shouts of "Jaya, jaya!",
some came running, eager to see.

**417.** Hearing these cries, Shachi suddenly came
and saw her own son Nimai and the pandits.

**418.** Crying "My son, my son!", Shachi took Nimai in her arms,
and, looking at them all, she speaks harsh words:

**419.** "Fine conduct for a whole assembly of pandits!
You have driven someone else's son mad and make him dance like a lunatic!"

**420.** At her harsh words they all came to their senses:
"What have we done, what have we done?" each thinks to himself.

**421.** Vishvambhara's mother took Vishvambhara away;
in joy Lochana tells the glories of Gora.

*Raga sindhura; disha: "A spotless moon has risen in Nadia!"*

**422.** Now here I shall tell a story —
the conversation that took place between Murari and Damodara.[^5]

**423.** Damodara Pandita asked Murari:
"I make one request — it is a long-standing pain in my heart.

**424.** Tell me, tell me, Gupta the vaidya, I ask you:
where did Vishvarupa, the Thakura's brother, go?

**425.** With love I ask you about his life."
And Murari, with great joy in his heart, replies:

**426.** "Listen, listen, Damodara, foremost of pandits:
what I know I shall tell you.

**427.** Vishvambhara's elder brother, Vishvarupa, was an abode of virtues;[^6]
how can I describe his virtues and deeds!

**428.** In a short time he mastered all the shastras;
his mind was devoted to dharma and averse to worldly life.

**429.** Pure of heart, devoted to brahmanas, gods and guru,
he served his father and mother with great love.

**430.** He knew the conclusions of Vedanta, the essence of every dharma;
without devotion to Vishnu he did no act at all.

**431.** Beloved by all, he had attained the highest perfection;
detached at heart, his mind was fixed in the knowledge of truth.

**432.** One day his father Jagannatha saw him on the main road,
talking with his fellow students, a book in his left hand.

**433.** The son had reached the age of sixteen;
he was fit for marriage, endowed with beauty and youth.

**434.** The father harboured this wish in his heart
and began to consider brides worthy of Vishvarupa.

**435.** Deep in thought, the brahmana came home,
his heart full of one thought: 'I will get Vishvarupa married.'

**436.** Soon the brahmana Vishvarupa also came home,
and, seeing his father lost in thought, he understood what was in his heart.

**437.** So the best of brahmanas, Vishvarupa,
seeing his father lost in thought, understood the matter.

**438.** He understood in his heart: 'It is for my marriage —
they are both anxious how to arrange it.

**439.** For me to marry is not proper;
and if I do not, mother will grieve: it will go against her wish.'

**440.** Having reasoned thus, at daybreak
he went out of the house with a book in his left hand,

**441.** swam across the waters of the Ganga to the other bank,
and, as soon as he had gone, the great soul took sannyasa.

*Raga pathamanjari*

**442.** The third watch of the day has come — why has our son not returned?
The hearts of father and mother are full of anxiety.
Jagannatha searches, looks into house after house,
and did not find his son.

**443.** People whisper to one another, and the matter became known:
Vishvarupa had taken sannyasa.
Hearing the news passed from ear to ear,
the father Jagannatha suddenly lost consciousness.

**444.** Then Shachi-devi too, hearing it, fell senseless to the ground —
the three worlds became dark.
She calls, "Vishvarupa! Come, my son, let me see you!
Why have you renounced the world?

**445.** Such a beautiful body, such beautiful feet —
how will you walk along the roads?
You could not bear the hunger of a single watch —
with whom will you now be fussy?

**446.** When you went to study, son, my heart found no rest —
I kept looking at the time again and again;
when you went to bathe, even then I could not keep still:
'Vishvarupa will come any moment now.'

**447.** When you called me 'Mother', it was wealth of hundreds of thousands to me —
looking at your face I forgot myself.
I do not know what grief befell you that, putting fire into my mouth,[^7]
you took sannyasa, a life of poverty.

**448.** Where are you, his father? Go to where Vishvarupa is,
seize our son and bring him home!
Let people say what they will — bring my son and give him to me:
I will invest him with the sacred thread again."

**449.** Jagannatha replies: "Listen, goddess Shachi-rani,
calm your heart.
Grieve no more: all worldly life is vanity;[^8]
and Vishvarupa is the best of worthy men.

**450.** It is the good fortune of our line: Vishvarupa, a worthy son,
has taken sannyasa without ever marrying.
Give him this blessing: may he be steady on that path
and keep his sannyasa without hardship.

**451.** Listen: do not regard this as misfortune in the midst of good fortune;
do not grieve without cause.
If one takes sannyasa, millions of generations of the family are delivered —
Vishvarupa is a jewel among men."

**452.** Hearing Jagannatha's words, Shachi-rani speaks again:
"What did you say? Tell me again, my lord.
'If one takes sannyasa, millions of generations are delivered'?
Then my son has done well."

**453.** Thus the two of them, with joy and sorrow in their hearts,
passed some time.
What can I say of that glory? There is no limit to their fortune:
they obtained Vishvambhara as their son.[^9]

**454.** So Murari Gupta told, and Damodara Pandita heard,
of Vishvarupa's sannyasa.
Again he asks for the story — the song of Vishvambhara's glories —
says this Lochana Dasa.

**455.** At that time Vishvambhara, sitting on His mother's lap,
gazes into His father's face:
"Wherever My brother has gone — listen, listen, father and mother:
I will take care of you."

**456.** Hearing such words, Jagannatha and Shachi-rani
both together embraced their son;
looking at Vishvambhara's face, they forgot all their sorrows —
so says Lochana Dasa.

*Thus ends the description of the Balya-lila.*

[^1]: The numbering of Bhaktisiddhanta's edition goes astray here: after 399 it prints 400, 401, 401 again, then the count returns to 393 and runs 393–403, after which it continues 404, 405… (and so on through the edition). Apparently the compositor was correcting the jump 330 → 340 of portion 12. To keep the edition's numbers, on which all further numbering depends, we retain the printed numbers and distinguish the repeats by a letter: the second "401" is 401a, the repeated series 393–401 is 393a–400a and 401b. The sequence of the couplets (confirmed by the 1983 and 1903 editions) is unchanged.
[^2]: Murari, an elder and respected man, prostrated himself before a child; by popular belief, an elder's bow to a younger one brings misfortune on the younger and shortens his life. Hence the parents' alarm and their plea that he bless Nimai with long life.
[^3]: Murari Gupta was a vaidya (physician) by caste; the parents respectfully call him a "shudra-muni", a sage who is not a brahmana.
[^4]: "Brahman, the Relisher of rasa" is Brahman as the Person who enjoys rasa (cf. Taittiriya Upanishad 2.7: "He, verily, is rasa"), that is, Krishna Himself. Advaita at once discloses to Murari that Nimai is the Lord Himself, but bids him keep it secret.
[^5]: Murari Gupta and Damodara Pandita are the narrator and his interlocutor in Murari Gupta's Sanskrit biography (Sri Krishna-chaitanya-charitamrita); Lochana Dasa, who relies on that work, adopts its frame. The story of Vishvarupa is in Murari 1.07.1–10.
[^6]: In the Gaudiya Vaishnava understanding Vishvarupa is Sankarshana (in Murari, "a portion of Baladeva", 1.07.3). On taking sannyasa he received the name Shankararanya, and he left this world at Pandharpur (Chaitanya-charitamrita, Madhya 9.299–300).
[^7]: At a cremation it is the son who places fire in the mouth of the dead (mukhagni); Shachi says bitterly that by leaving, her son has as good as performed her funeral rite while she lives.
[^8]: That is, attachment to home and family is worthless; this is not a claim that the world is illusory, but a statement of the vanity of worldly life (samsara) beside renunciation for God's sake.
[^9]: In A this couplet was lost in the OCR (its number is restored from the neighbouring ones); the text follows the 1903 edition; the 1983 edition ends the stanza: "whose son is Gorachand".
