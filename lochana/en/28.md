# Madhya-khanda, part 5 (portion 28)

*The section "Meeting Nityananda": the name of Srivasa; Murari's Raghuvira-ashtaka and the name "Ramadasa"; service to Srivasa is worship of the Lord; the search for Nityananda and the meeting in the house of Nandana Acharya; the glory of Nityananda; Shachi accepts Nityananda as her son; in Srivasa's house the Lord reveals to Nityananda His six-armed, four-armed and two-armed forms*

## Meeting Nityananda

> *Summary by Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati.* One day Sriman Mahaprabhu asked Srivasa Pandita the meaning of his name and Himself revealed its sense: "Srivasa is the abode (avasa) of devotion (shri)." Then, when at the Lord's command Murari recited the Raghuvira-ashtaka, Sriman Mahaprabhu wrote the name "Ramadasa" on his forehead and showed him the form of Rama whom he worshipped. Having instructed Sri Rama Pandita to serve his brother Srivasa, a supreme devotee (parama-bhagavata), Sriman Mahaprabhu sent the devotees to look for Sriman Nityananda Prabhu. [Learning] that Sriman Nityananda Prabhu was staying in the house of Nandana Acharya, Mahaprabhu came there with His associates and met Him; before everyone He glorified the greatness of Nityananda Prabhu and [explained] the way to prema-bhakti for Krishna. When Sriman Nityananda Prabhu was taking His meal in the house of Srivasa Pandita, Sriman Mahaprabhu revealed to Him His six-armed, four-armed and two-armed forms[^1].

*Raga — any (yatha-raga); disha: "Ah, my life — Gorachand!"*

**1.** Then the Lord was sitting at home on a divine seat,
and around Him were His devotees.

**2.** Seeing Srivasa, the Lord said:
"Do you know how your name is formed?

**3.** You are the very abode (avasa) of Shri — of devotion;
that is why I say: your name is Srivasa."[^2]

**4.** Then, seeing Gopinatha, the Lord said:
"You are My devotee — go about with Me."[^3]

**5.** Seeing Murari, the Lord says again:
"Recite your verses — I shall listen to you."

**6.** Hearing these words, the skilful Murari
recites his own poetry, and the Thakura listens.

*So in the Sri Chaitanya-charita composed by Sri Murari Gupta, second prakrama, seventh sarga:*

**7.** "The rays of the gems of His gleaming crown light up the quarters; He wears earrings like the rising Brihaspati and Kavi; His face is like a spotless moon. Rama, the teacher of the three worlds, I worship ever.

**8.** His eyes are like lotuses awakened by the rays of the rising sun; His lips are like ripe bimba fruit, His nose is lovely; His charming smile outshines the moonbeams. Rama, the teacher of the three worlds, I worship ever."[^4]

**9.** Having thus heard the verses of the Raghuvira-ashtaka,
[the Lord] Himself placed His foot on Murari's head.

**10.** He wrote the name "Ramadasa" on his forehead:
"By My grace you have become Ramadasa.

**11.** Without Raghunatha you do not live even a moment;
know for certain: I am your Raghunatha."[^5]

**12.** Saying this, He showed him the form of Rama —
with Janaki, with all His associates and attendants.

**13.** Falling at His feet, Murari offers praise:
"Glory, glory to Raghuvira, the son of Shachi!"

**14.** Again and again he rises and falls, rolling on the ground,
offering many kinds of praise and humble entreaties.

**15.** The Lord mercifully said to Murari:
"Know nothing but devotion to Me.

**16.** Though your chosen Deity is I, Raghunatha,
still, taste the rasa of the Lord of Radha.

**17.** Go and sing of Radha and Krishna, following the dharma of sankirtana —
thus you will render devotion to Me; listen attentively."[^6]

**18.** Saying this, He Himself recited a verse:
"Hear one verse of Mine, brahmana Shrinivasa."

*So [in the Srimad-Bhagavata (11.14.20)]:*

**19.** "Neither yoga nor Sankhya nor dharma, O Uddhava, neither Vedic study nor austerity nor renunciation win Me over as does mighty devotion to Me."

**20.** Having recited it, He said: "Hear, all My own:
act in this way.

**21.** Following the words of Shrinivasa Pandita,
render devotion to Me — you will find great happiness.

**22.** Sri Rama Pandita, hear My words:
service to your elder brother is worship of Me.

**23.** Knowing this, serve Srivasa:
through this you will attain the radiance of My feet."[^7]

**24.** So spoke the Lord, affectionate to His devotees;
His eyes, reddened with compassion, brimmed with tears.

**25.** Then the skilful Shrinivasa Pandita
offered milk, and the Thakura partakes of it.

**26.** Scents, sandal, garlands, fragrant betel,
incense and lamps he offered before Him.

**27.** The Lord joyfully accepted [the offerings]
and gave the remnants to all His devotees.[^8]

**28.** Thus in joyous play the whole night passed;
at dawn the Lord rose and went home.

**29.** All performed their bathing and worship of the Deity in their own homes
and came again to see His lotus feet.

**30.** Laughing, the Lord said: "Hear a wonderful thing:
Shripada Nityananda Avadhuta has come!

**31.** Who knows how to tell the truth of His glory?
By great merit and fortune I shall see His feet today.

**32.** Ho, Rama, Narayana, Murari, Mukunda,
quickly find out where Nityananda is."[^9]

**33.** When Mahaprabhu gave this order,
they set off at once and searched south of the village.

**34.** They searched but found no trace of Him
and came back again to His lotus feet.

**35.** With joined palms they say before the Thakura:
"Lord, we searched but found no trace of Him."

**36.** Again the Lord says: "Hear, all of you:
now go, each of you, to your own dwelling."[^10]

**37.** At the Lord's command all set off at once
and one by one went to their own homes.

**38.** In the evening, having performed the evening prayers, they gathered together
and all came together to the Lord.

**39.** Going along the road, the Lord calls: "Murari!" —
"You did not see the Avadhuta," He says, and laughs gently.

**40.** "The great soul is staying in the house of Nandana Acharya;
I too shall go there," He said firmly.

**41.** Hearing these words, all rejoiced
and went with the Thakura, crying: "Jaya, jaya!"

**42.** Going along the road, again and again He cries: "Hari, Hari!";
His cheeks thrill, His voice falters.

**43.** Tears stream from His eyes in many streams;
from prema the golden Youth cannot walk.

**44.** Now with a lion's prowess He goes four steps —
like a maddened elephant that does not look back;

**45.** now, like the deep rumble of a new raincloud,
again and again He roars "hum!", intoxicated with bliss.

**46.** So in joy and delight He goes on,
and He saw the Avadhuta Nityananda-raya.

**47.** His body is golden with a reddish glow, supremely beautiful;
an enchanting body in glittering ornaments.

**48.** Yellow cloth shines on His hips,
on His head a loosely tied turban of champaka hue.

**49.** As He walks, the bells on His feet are heard jingling,
setting the hearts of doe-eyed women aquiver.

**50.** When He laughs, it is as if lightning were falling from the sky:
women give up their modesty to it then and there.

**51.** His roar is heard, deeper than thunder —
a lion's roar that tames the maddened elephant of Kali.

**52.** His gait is beautiful, like a maddened elephant's;
on His serene face are ceaseless streams of prema.

**53.** His body is overcome with thrills, brimming with prema;
in the bhavas of trembling, perspiration and others He is attached to rasa.

**54.** In His hand is a golden staff that subdues the pride of Kali;
His charming palms are like red lotuses.

**55.** Armlets, bracelets, necklace, upper-arm bands, a belt of bells;
by His cheeks earrings shine like the sun.[^11]

**56.** Falling again and again, He rises crying: "Steady!"
and asks everyone: "Where is Kanai the cowherd?"

**57.** In an unearthly childlike mood He now weeps, now laughs;
now He cries: "Give Me honey!" — now praises Revati.[^12]

**58.** Now, with feet together, He goes by leaps;
He says one thing and does another — there is no understanding it.

**59.** By the fragrance of His body all the young women
then gave up the pride of noble wives.[^13]

**60.** The Lord, falling to the ground, bowed down to Him
and offered auspicious praise in sweet words.[^14]

**61.** Nityananda-raya fell at the Lord's feet:
each seeks to clasp the other's feet.

**62.** Both embrace, weeping and weeping,
and, gazing into each other's faces, laugh: "Where have You been?"

**63.** "I have wandered through the whole earth,
but nowhere did I find any trace of You.

**64.** I heard that in the land of Gauda, in the town of Navadvipa,
the son of Nanda is hiding.

**65.** To catch the thief I have come here —
I have caught the thief! Where will You flee now?"[^15]

**66.** Saying this, Nityananda laughs, weeps and dances,
and Gauranga dances in joy beside Nityananda.

**67.** "I have found Nityananda, the tamer of Kali's pride —
now the fallen, the lame, the dull and the blind are as good as delivered by Me!

**68.** By the might of Nityananda the three worlds are purified,
yet the godless, the wicked and the foolish do not know it.

**69.** But they too will all fall later into Nityananda's net" —
so spoke the Lord Gorachand.

**70.** Joyfully He sings the sankirtana of Hari's qualities:
He Himself dances, and with Him dances Nityananda.

**71.** Ending the dance, the two sat down,
and everyone gazed at Them in joy.

**72.** Then [the Lord] Himself took the dust of Nityananda's lotus feet
and placed it on the devotees' heads.

**73.** Receiving the dust of Nityananda's feet, the devotees —
their hearts overflowing with prema, tears streaming from their eyes.

**74.** So they stayed there a while in joy,
and the Lord, Shachi's son, went home.

**75.** Going along the road, He tells of Nityananda's glory:
"In all the three worlds there is none to compare with Him.

**76.** Hear, hear, all of you, My words:
this prema-bhakti of His for Krishna is not ordinary.

**77.** First there is knowledge, then devotion arises,
and then comes detachment from all enjoyments.

**78.** So step by step it grows day by day;
attachment to Krishna grows, and one becomes mature."[^16]

**79.** On another day Mahaprabhu
invited Nityananda, the best of sannyasis, to His own house.

**80.** After the meal He anointed His body with sandal
and, according to the rules of worship, offered Him a divine garland.[^17]

**81.** Seeing Nityananda, Shachi's eyes were soothed;
beside herself with love, she gazes at His face.

**82.** The Lord says: "Know Him as your own son
and care for Him more than for Me."

**83.** As on a son, Shachi gazes at Nityananda's face.
"You have become my son," says Shachi-devi.

**84.** "Be kind Yourself to my Vishvambhara:
from today you two are my sons."

**85.** As she speaks, tears stream from Shachi's eyes,
and Shachi takes Nityananda in her arms as a son.

**86.** Nityananda, seeing her as His mother, prostrated Himself
at Shachi's feet and speaks in sweet words:

**87.** "All that you have said, mother, is true:
I am your son — know it for certain.

**88.** Do not hold your son's offences against Him, mother:
know it in every way — I am truly your son."

**89.** Receiving Nityananda's love as a son, queen Shachi —
tears flow from her eyes, her voice falters.

**90.** Thus all overflow with the rasa of affection;
seeing her two sons, Shachi was comforted at heart.[^18]

**91.** On another day Srivasa Pandita offered a meal,
and the Avadhuta took the meal in his house.

**92.** He found great satisfaction with the Pandita
and, having taken the meal, stayed there that day.

**93.** At that very moment Mahaprabhu Gaura Bhagavan
came with a serene face to Srivasa's house.

**94.** Entering the shrine room, He sits on a divine seat
and said: "Behold Me now with Your eyes."

**95.** Hearing these words, Nityananda, the best of sannyasis,
lovingly gazes at the form of Vishvambhara.

**96.** But He did not grasp any special meaning in this —
what purpose the Lord had hinted at.

**97.** Then Mahaprabhu Vishvambhara again,
looking at His own, said to them in reply:

**98.** "All of you, go out of this room" —
and they went out of the room, obeying the command.

**99.** What He says of Himself in the remaining words —
the inner meaning spoken in private — who will know?

**100.** He said: "Behold Me now Yourself:
for My sake You have toiled so much."

**101.** First the Lord showed His six-armed form,
then the four-armed form, and then the two-armed.

**102.** Seeing such a form, the greatest of wonders,
Nityananda Avadhuta remembered the past.[^19]

**103.** He saw: my Lord revealed Himself
and in one body showed three avataras.

**104.** He saw the divine forms of Rama, Krishna and Gauranga,
and afterwards saw the youthful pair — Radha and Kanu.[^20]

**105.** In delight Prabhu dances, His bliss boundless;
He knows no directions — an ocean of prema.

**106.** Hear, all of you, this wondrous story:
this servant Lochana tells the song of Gora's qualities.[^21]

[^1]: The summary covers the whole section (vv. 1–106). Some places in the OCR of the edition are illegible and have been restored by sense (in square brackets). The section as a whole follows Murari Gupta (2.7–2.8).
[^2]: So also in Murari (2.7.7): "Shri is devotion, and you are its abode."
[^3]: Gopinatha is a brahmana of Navadvipa, an associate of the Lord; in Murari (2.7.8): "You are known as My servant."
[^4]: Vv. 7–8 are the first two stanzas of Murari Gupta's Ramashtaka (eight verses on Rama) (Murari 2.7.10–11); Brihaspati and Kavi (Shukra) are the planets Jupiter and Venus. Murari is a devotee of Rama, in Gaudiya Vaishnava tradition Hanuman (en/24, note 12). Bhaktisiddhanta's anvaya and translation of the verses are not translated here or below.
[^5]: Cf. Murari 2.7.18. Ramadasa means "servant of Rama".
[^6]: This instruction is not in Murari. In the Chaitanya-charitamrita (Madhya 15.137–157) the Lord, testing Murari, advises him to worship Krishna, and then glorifies his unshakeable fidelity to Rama. Cf. also en/24, where the Lord instructs Murari to worship Radha and Krishna.
[^7]: The verse is Bhagavata 11.14.20; vv. 18–23 — cf. Murari 2.7.19–23. Sri Rama Pandita is the younger brother of Srivasa (Shrinivasa).
[^8]: Cf. Murari 2.7.25–27; in Murari the remnants of the meal are received also by Narayani, Srivasa's niece, the future mother of Vrindavana Dasa.
[^9]: Cf. Murari 2.8.1–9. Shripada is a respectful address to a sannyasi; an Avadhuta is a wandering renunciant who stands above external rules. Rama is Sri Rama Pandita; Mukunda is Mukunda Datta. According to the Chaitanya-bhagavata (Madhya-khanda), Nityananda came to Navadvipa after many years of pilgrimage and stayed in the house of Nandana Acharya.
[^10]: Literally "consider, all, your own dwelling"; so in A and in the 1983 edition. We understand it from v. 37 and from Murari (2.8.6–7), where the Lord says the Avadhuta can be found in the evening and sends everyone home.
[^11]: Vv. 47–55 describe Nityananda. According to Gaudiya Vaishnava theology Nityananda is Balarama Himself, Krishna's elder brother (cf. Murari 2.9.12); His staff, roar and gait "subdue Kali" — that is, His mercy breaks the power of the age of Kali. The yellow cloth is Lochana Dasa's; in Murari (2.9.12) Balarama wears blue.
[^12]: Nityananda is in the mood of Balarama: Kanai is Krishna, His younger brother; Revati is Balarama's consort; "honey" (madhu) is the intoxicating honey-wine (varuni) that Balarama drinks (cf. Murari 2.9.12, 2.14). This intoxication is the ecstasy of prema, not worldly drunkenness; the Avadhuta's strange behaviour (vv. 56–58) is likewise a manifestation of prema.
[^13]: An image from the songs of the gopis, as in Lochana Dasa's descriptions of Gaura (cf. en/09, note 9, on "nagari-bhava"): the beauty of the Lord and His associates draws souls away from worldly attachments; it is not worldly passion.
[^14]: Cf. Murari 2.8.10–13: the Lord bows to Nityananda with devotion. Thus the Lord honours Nityananda as His elder brother (Balarama) and shows how to honour a Vaishnava.
[^15]: Nityananda had wandered for many years through the holy places in search of Krishna, and found Him "hiding" in Navadvipa in the form of Gaura. The "thief" is Krishna, the Stealer of butter and of hearts.
[^16]: Vv. 75–78 — cf. Murari 2.8.15–16. "Knowledge" here is the knowledge of Krishna as Bhagavan Himself (cf. en/27, note 2); according to Gaudiya Vaishnava teaching, devotion itself brings both knowledge and detachment (Bhagavata 1.2.7; 11.2.42).
[^17]: Cf. Murari 2.8.18–19. In the 1983 edition, after v. 80 there are 17 more couplets not found in Bhaktisiddhanta's edition (the 1903 edition gives them in a footnote as an additional reading): the Lord distributes to the devotees pieces of Nityananda's kaupina, and Shachi recognizes in Nityananda her elder son Vishvarupa. Cf. the Chaitanya-bhagavata (Madhya-khanda), where Shachi sees Nityananda and Nimai in a dream as Balarama and Krishna.
[^18]: Vv. 81–90 are not in Murari. Nityananda, according to Gaudiya Vaishnava theology, is Balarama, and Vishvarupa too is an expansion of Sankarshana; hence Shachi naturally loves Nityananda as her son.
[^19]: Vv. 91–102 — cf. Murari 2.8.20–29. "The past" is His former pastimes with Krishna as Balarama (in Murari, 2.8.29, the Lord says: "You are My brother who sported in Vrindavana!"). In Murari Nityananda tells no one of what He saw.
[^20]: The six-armed form unites Rama (bow and arrows), Krishna (flute) and Gaura the sannyasi (staff and waterpot); cf. the Chaitanya-charitamrita (Madhya 6), where Sarvabhauma sees the same form. The vision of Radha and Krishna in Gaura: according to Gaudiya Vaishnava theology, Gaura is Radha and Krishna in one body (cf. Chaitanya-charitamrita, Adi 4; Madhya 8, Ramananda Raya's vision).
[^21]: In the 1983 edition this is followed by one more song with a detailed description of the six-armed form, which is not in Bhaktisiddhanta's edition.
