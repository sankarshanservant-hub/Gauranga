# Shesha-khanda, part 6 (portion 51)

*The section "The Lord's Return to Nilachala" (ch. 3): mercy to the cowherd; the Lord at Kulia and Navadvipa — Shachi's lament and the Lord's instruction; kirtana at Advaita's house in Shantipura; by way of Tamoluk to Puri; mercy to King Prataparudra: the sannyasi in Jagannatha's place, the six-armed form, the instruction to the king*

## The Lord's Return to Nilachala

> *Summary by Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati.* Having seen the places of Sri Krishna's lilas in the Mathura-mandala, Sriman Mahaprabhu wished to return to Nilachala. On the way He walked so fast that His companions could not keep up with Him and fell behind. The Lord was going alone along a forest path; meeting a cowherd boy on the way, He asked him for some buttermilk and, telling him to take the price of the buttermilk from the people coming behind, set off again. A little later the companions arrived there; the cowherd boy told them how the Lord had drunk the buttermilk and asked for payment — but the very next moment he saw that his pot was full of priceless treasure. Thus Sriman Mahaprabhu, as if for the sake of the buttermilk, showed mercy to this cowherd boy and, continuing on His way, came to Navadvipa. The people of Navadvipa ran like madmen to see the Lord. Mother Shachi came to the Lord weeping and began to tell Him the grief of separation. Then Mahaprabhu said to Mother Shachi: "I abide near whoever serves Krishna," and, instructing her in the service of Krishna, spent a day in the bliss of kirtana in the house of Acharya Gosani at Shantipura, and then by way of Tamoluk reached Nilachala and showed mercy to King Prataparudra.

*Raga suhai*

**1.** With a joyful heart the Lord goes to Nilachala;
crying "O Jagannatha!" He hastens [there] in love[^1].

**2.** The Lord goes in the bliss of prema with a lion's gait;
all who are with Him cannot keep pace.

**3.** Unable to keep up, the companions fell far behind,
and the Lord went on alone through the forest.

**4.** In the forest there is a village;
a cowherd youth is going to sell buttermilk.

**5.** The Thakura saw him — in His ecstasy He was thirsty:
"Give Me buttermilk, cowherd, I am thirsty."

**6.** Hearing this, the cowherd fell at His feet:
"Take the buttermilk — drink, Gosani, as much as You like."

**7.** He drank the buttermilk — the jar was empty;
having drunk the buttermilk, the sannyasi — a sannyasi only in guise — walks on.

**8.** He told the cowherd: "Wait here:
take the money from those who come behind."

**9.** Saying this, the Lord went off quickly,
and the cowherd, staying there, ponders within.

**9+1.** The cowherd thinks: "The sannyasi has told a lie," —
thinking so, the cowherd ponders long[^2]:

**9+2.** "When I go home, what shall I tell my family?
The sannyasi told a lie and went away."

**10.** After a while all the sannyasi's companions
come along that road, their minds absorbed in the Lord.

**11.** They asked: "Cowherd, did you see a sannyasi on the road?"
The cowherd says: "He drank a whole jar of buttermilk

**12.** and told me to take the money from you.
Give what is proper, and I will go home."

**13.** Hearing this, all look at one another;
all say: "Where would we have money?

**13+1.** We have with us neither a water-pot nor an outer cloth:
when we are thirsty, we drink water from cupped hands"[^3].

**14.** The cowherd said: "Go on, then there is no debt;
convey my obeisance to the sannyasi's feet."

**15.** Saying this, he takes the jar in his hands —
but the jar is very heavy; he cannot lift it onto his head.

**16.** Removing the lid, [he sees:] the jar is full of jewels —
and he ran off, crying "O sannyasi!"

**17.** A little way on, waiting for His lagging companions, stands the Lord;
seeing the cowherd, He smiles softly.

**18.** Then all the companions came up
and saw: the cowherd had attained the Lord's feet.

**19.** The Lord says: "Cowherd, go home:
Krishna has shown you grace; you have received a boon."

**20.** On his way back the cowherd received grace:
the cowherd goes about dancing in the madness of prema.

**21.** Seeing the cowherd, the joy of all increased;
happily this Lochana Dasa sings Gora's glories.

*Raga shyamagada; disha: "Ah, my, ah, my Gauranga-sundara! Under the weight of youthful prema He cannot walk"*

**22.** Thus step by step He comes along the road
and with His companions arrived in Gauda[^4].

**23.** Bathing in the Ganga, the Lord, by way of Radha-desha,
gradually arrived at the town of Kulia.

**24.** To see [the place of] one's former ashrama is a sannyasi's duty:
this is why He came near Navadvipa.

**25.** Hearing of the Lord's coming, the people of Navadvipa
came back to life, as it were, and forgot their grief.

**26.** Crying "O Gorachand!" they run [to Him] in love;
the ladies of good families run too, without looking back.

**27.** Shachi, beside herself, runs with upturned face,
her hair dishevelled, her cloth not drawn over her breast.

**28.** "Where is my Vishvambhara? Show him to my eyes!
I will kiss that beautiful face again.

**29.** My Nimai has come to Nadia!
People, seize him and keep him — there is no wrong in it.

**30.** He is the life of all; he alone is the soul:
without life, how can dharma be kept?"

**31.** Saying such things she came there
and saw where Gaurachandra was sitting.

**32.** Seeing the Lord, she says: "Listen, Nimai!
Come home — I have no use for [your] sannyasa.

**33.** By sannyasa you will keep dharma later,
but first the [sin of] my death will cling to you — everything else after."

**34.** Shachi, beside herself, weeps aloud
and gazes fixedly at His whole body.

**35.** Saying "My son, my son!" she wants to touch His body:
"Let all else be, my son," — and she lays her hand on Him.

**36.** "Dust has clung to the holy body — let me brush it off" —
saying this, she falls, flinging herself down.

**37.** And again, rising, she says: "My son, listen to me:
fulfil my heart's longing — let me hold you in my arms."

**38.** At the sight of Shachi's weeping the earth splits;
let alone people — even stones weep.

**39.** All the people around are distraught with weeping;
no one leaves His side; all have forgotten their homes.

**40.** Seeing the people weeping and His mother's distress,
the Lord considers: "What shall I say?"

**41.** Pondering within how to console His mother,
the Lord says sweetly: "Do not weep, do not weep!

**42.** You yourself gave permission to take sannyasa —
why then, overwhelmed, do you weep now?

**43.** The vain maya — [attachment to Me] as to a son — has not left you:
so hard to cast off is the maya of this dreadful samsara![^5]

**44.** Driven away, it does not go: so cruel is maya."
Shachi says: "Hear me, pitiless one!

**45.** You took birth on earth as my son
and made the people of the world honour me.

**46.** You are the friend of all, worshipped in the three worlds;
this 'maya' of Your affection I understand well from the scriptures.

**47.** Whatever becomes of me — be my son:
birth after birth may this thread of destiny remain mine."

**48.** At His mother's words the Lord was perturbed:
He cannot overcome her 'maya'; compassion overflows in Him[^6].

**49.** "Whatever you wish, do as pleases you;
I have told you only one last thing."

**50.** Shachi says: "You are going away, leaving Navadvipa —
and in Navadvipa Vishnupriya and I are grieving."

**51.** At His mother's word He went again to Navadvipa,
to the Barakona-ghata, near His own house.

**52.** He took alms in the house of Shuklambara Brahmachari
and in the morning, bowing to His mother, departed.

**53.** He told His mother: "I am captive to your virtues;
why have you forgotten the secret of the past?

**53+1.** I am Rama, Krishna, Vamana, Kapila and the rest:
look at all the births, considering everything[^7].

**53+2.** At all times such is My way:
I am near you — know this truth.

**53+3.** Now My descent is for the rasa of devotion:
except Krishnachandra, I shall worship nothing.

**54.** Be it a devotee, be it Vishnupriya, be it you —
whoever worships Krishna, I am in that one's embrace"[^8].

**55.** Bowing to His mother, the Lord says again and again:
"Do not abandon Krishna; do not serve this samsara."

**56.** Shachi's heart throbs;
the Thakura went; all the devotees run behind.

**57.** He went to Shantipura, to the Acharya's house;
in the joy of kirtana a day and a night passed.

**58.** Next morning the Lord again set out quickly:
His longing to see Jagannatha grew.

**59.** The Lord told everyone: "All of you go home;
I am in Nilachala" — so He answered.

**60.** "Whoever goes there to see Jagannatha —
there all will meet Me."

**61.** Saying this, the Lord cries "Hari-bol!"
The Thakura went — a sound of weeping rises.

**62.** Step by step He came to Tamoluk,
by the same way as He had come before.

**63.** The Lord goes along the road in the joy of prema's bliss;
the people along that road float in the rain of prema.

**64.** He goes laughing and playing, without the fatigue of the road,
and step by step reached Purushottama.

**65.** "I shall see Jagannatha, the King of Nilachala!" —
crying "O Jagannatha!" He hastens in love.

**66.** Coming to the Lion Gate, the Lord lets out a roar;
all the people found boundless joy.

**67.** Seeing Jagannatha, Goraraya was delighted;
seeing Him, the people find great happiness.

**68.** The people cry with loud voices: "Hari, Hari!"
and joyfully sing Hari's glories day and night.

**69.** Night and day the Lord delights in kirtana;
happily this Lochana Dasa sings Gora's glories.

*Raga lalita; disha: "Sing, sing of Gora's glories — the blessing of the whole world!"*

**70.** Joyful Mahaprabhu stays in Nilachala
and among the devotees performs the sankirtana of Hari's glories.

**71.** Many devotees gathered there;
every day Goraraya reveals Himself anew.

**72.** Now I shall tell how at that time
He, as He willed, showed mercy to Prataparudra[^9].

**73.** Hearing from people of Mahaprabhu's glories,
the king marvels but says nothing.

**74.** One day he went to see Jagannatha —
and does not see Jagannatha, but sees the best of sannyasis.

**75.** "What is this, what?" he ponders with an astonished heart
and asks the padichha: "What do you see, sir?"

**76.** The padichha says: "I see Lord Jagannatha."
The king says: "I keep you all in vain!

**77.** Look, in Jagannatha's place sits a sannyasi —
and you, for fear of my punishment, say you see nothing [else].

**78.** As if my eyes deceived me — that is never so!
Or else — what do you see? Tell the truth."

**79.** Hearing this, the padichha says again:
"Except Jagannatha we see nothing"[^10].

**80.** Then Prataparudra ponders within:
"Why then do I see the sannyasi with my own eyes?

**81.** I have heard that this sannyasi's greatness is boundless;
I must look into the cause of this."

**82.** Having thought thus, the king went quickly —
went himself to where the best of sannyasis was.

**83.** He saw: in the garden the sannyasi, among His own,
tells of Vrindavana and cries: "Hari, Hari!"

**84.** Again, looking at Jagannatha once more,
he saw that sannyasi, as vast as Mount Sumeru.

**85.** Seeing this, the king was amazed at heart:
"This Jagannatha is that very sannyasi-avatara!"

**86.** Love grows in Prataparudra's heart,
and he went quickly to where the Glorious One was.

**87.** There is no one in the garden — the gathering has broken up;
the king pleadingly says to Govinda[^11]:

**88.** "How may I see the Gosani's feet?
Tell me the means for it, great one."

**89.** Govinda says: "King, do not be distressed:
you will not see Him here — now is not the time."

**90.** "When shall I come, tell me, fortunate one?" —
the king speaks pleadingly, and his love grows.

**91.** That day the king stayed in that town
and, seeing [the Lord's] companions, implores them all.

**92.** All the devotees, headed by Puri Gosani,
resolved to bring this to the Gosani's notice.

**93.** When two or four days had passed thus,
they all gathered together in the house of Kashi Mishra.

**94.** All the devotees took counsel together
and resolved: "We shall tell Him all together."

**95.** The next day Mahaprabhu, as it happened,
was in Kashi Mishra's house among His devotees.

**96.** The king's anxiety weighed on all their hearts,
and Puri Gosani addressed the Lord:

**97.** "I have one request, Gosani, but fear to say it;
I will speak without fear if I have permission."

**98.** The Thakura says: "Listen, Puri Gosani:
before Me you need never be afraid.

**99.** Say what you would say — I will hear your heart."
Puri Gosani says: "Will You grant my request?

**100.** On behalf of all the devotees headed by Kashi Mishra
I say these words:

**101.** Sri Jagannatha-deva dwells in Nilachala,
and King Prataparudra is His own servant.

**102.** He entreats us all in order to see Your feet:
if he has permission, he will come before those feet."

**103.** The Lord says: "Everyone, hear My word:
it is not a sannyasi's dharma to see a king.

**104.** I am a sannyasi, and he is a great king:
neither of us has any purpose in meeting."

**105.** Puri Gosani says: "Lord, hear me:
if the king hears this, he will lose his senses.

**106.** We have seen what his love is like:
if he hears such words, he will give up his life, O Glorious One.

**107.** Today is already the king's tenth day of fasting:
leaving everything, he lies [at the door] in hope of [Your] feet."

**108.** And again all say pleadingly:
"Seeing the king's anxiety, we are asking."

**109.** Hearing this, the Lord says:
"Bring the king — I am pleased with him."

**110.** Hearing this, all rejoiced;
they brought the king — and the Lord reveals Himself.

**111.** Seeing the Lord, the king bows;
overwhelmed with prema, the king forgets himself.

**112.** His body filled with thrills, his eyes brimming;
seeing Gora, he was steeped in prema.

**113.** Seeing the king, the Lord smiles gently,
and the king sees the six-armed form [He] reveals[^12].

**114.** Seeing the Six-armed One, he prostrates;
under the weight of love his body sways.

**115.** His body is powerless, tears stream from both eyes;
all around, the name of Hari resounds, touching the sky.

**116.** Seeing the six-armed form, Sri Prataparudra,
overwhelmed with bliss, floats in the ocean of prema.

**117.** His whole body bristles from head to foot;
in a faltering voice he calls: "Lord, Lord!"

**118.** Raising his arms he dances and cries: "Hari-bol!
My life is fulfilled — the Lord is pleased with me!"

**119.** The devotees all around float in bliss.
The Lord says: "King, hear My word:

**120.** to protect your subjects is your great dharma:
the subjects are sons, the king is father — such is the essence.

**121.** Only Krishna's mercy is equal to all living beings;
know by experience the nature of your own body:

**122.** be it king or subject, joy and sorrow are the same for all;
only by karma does a living being become lesser or greater.

**123.** Whoever, judging by himself, understands all others —
he indeed is Krishna's servant, I tell you"[^13].

**124.** Such an answer the Lord gave as instruction;
the king bows in exceptional bliss.

**125.** Hear, everyone, of Gorachand's manifestation;
joyfully this Lochana Dasa tells His glories.

[^1]: Vv. 1–21 — cf. Murari 4.13 ("Mercy to the Cowherd"). In Lochana Dasa, as in Murari (4.14–4.15), the Lord goes from Vrindavana through Bengal (Kulia, Navadvipa, Shantipura) to Puri; according to the Chaitanya-charitamrita (Madhya 16–18, 25), the Lord visited Kulia and Shantipura on the way to Vrindavana and returned from Vrindavana by way of Prayaga and Kashi.
[^2]: Vv. 9+1 — 9+2 are found only in the 1983 edition; they are absent in the 1903 edition. The end of v. 9+1 is unclear; translated by the sense.
[^3]: V. 13+1 is found only in the 1983 edition; the 1903 edition gives it in a footnote as an additional reading of one printed edition.
[^4]: Vv. 22–69 — cf. Murari 4.14–4.15 (Kulia, the meeting with Shachi, Advaita's house, the return to Puri).
[^5]: "Vain" (michha) — cf. en/37, note 14. The Lord speaks as a sannyasi-teacher, addressing the whole world through His mother. Shachi's love itself is no worldly attachment: in the Gaudiya Vaishnava teaching Shachi is the Lord's eternal associate (Yashoda), and her motherly love (vatsalya-prema) is spiritual; Shachi herself answers that she knows this "maya of affection" of the Lord from the scriptures (v. 46), and the Lord admits that He is "captive to her virtues" (v. 53).
[^6]: "Maya" here is Shachi's motherly love, to which the Lord Himself submits (cf. v. 46).
[^7]: Vv. 53+1 — 53+3 are found only in the 1983 edition; the 1903 edition gives them in a footnote as an additional reading of a printed edition. The Lord reminds Shachi that she is His mother in many descents (as Kaushalya, Devaki and Yashoda, Aditi, Devahuti); in the Gaudiya Vaishnava teaching all avataras come from Krishna, who has appeared as Gaura.
[^8]: Cf. the Lord's farewell to Shachi in Murari (4.14).
[^9]: Vv. 70–125 — the mercy to Prataparudra; cf. Murari 4.16 (the king sees Gaura and Nityananda in kirtana and then, in dreams, as Krishna and Balarama). According to the Chaitanya-charitamrita (Madhya 11–14), the Lord long refused to meet the king, saying that a sannyasi should not see a king (cf. vv. 103–104), and received him during the Ratha-yatra, when the king, dressed as a simple Vaishnava, recited the Gopi-gita. Lochana Dasa's account is different.
[^10]: The padichha is the superintendent of the Jagannatha temple. The king sees Gaura in Jagannatha's place: Gaura is non-different from Jagannatha (cf. en/45, note 15, on the Daru-brahman; below, v. 85: "This Jagannatha is that very sannyasi-avatara").
[^11]: The garden (tota) is the Lord's residence in Puri; Govinda is His servant; Puri Gosani (v. 92) is Paramananda Puri. In the 1983 edition vv. 87–88 are in a different wording, one couplet longer: "There is no one in the garden… The king, beside himself, lost his senses. The king pleadingly says to Govinda: 'How may I see the Gosani's feet? Tell me the means for it, great one,' — so he says again and again."
[^12]: The six-armed form (shadbhuja) — by tradition, with the arms of Rama (bow and arrow), Krishna (flute) and the sannyasi (staff and water-pot). By tradition, the Lord also revealed the six-armed form to Sarvabhauma (en/45, vv. 243–245 and note 22).
[^13]: That is, whoever sees in others what he sees in himself — their joy and sorrow — is a true servant of Krishna.
