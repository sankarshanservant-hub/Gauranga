# Shesha-khanda, part 4 (portion 49)

*The section "The Lord Sees Vrindavana" (continued): Krishnadasa shows the Lord the places of Krishna's pastimes — the stealing of the clothes, Govardhana, the places of the nauka-lila and the dana-lila, the ponds; the ashoka grove blossoms at the Lord's glance; at the place of the rasa the Lord is in ecstasy, and Krishnadasa tells the rasa-lila (with passages found only in the 1983 edition)*

*Raga shri; disha: "Ah, my wondrous Gora! People say: a youth of pure gold"*

**206.** Here the cowherd maidens kept a vow;
they desired: "Let us become maidservants at Krishna's feet"[^1].

**207.** Leaving their clothes and ornaments on this ghata,
they entered the water and bathe naked.

**208.** Suddenly Hari, taking the clothes and ornaments,
climbed a kadamba tree and laughs softly.

**209.** The maidens praise Him with great effort;
pleased, He gave them back their clothes and ornaments.

**210.** [Here] He praised Vrindavana, addressing the boys;
[here] He asked food of the wives of the sacrificing brahmanas and ate.

**211.** Through Kamsa's outrages all the cowherds, in fear,
went and took shelter on the hill Nandishvara.

**212.** They settled on both banks of the Manasa-Ganga,
and He sported on the summit of Govardhana.

**213.** Contending with Indra, He holds up this mountain:
the great hill He lifted in His seventh year.

**214.** The stream of the Manasa-Ganga lies north-east of the hill;
there is no dry land, and the gopis cannot cross.

**215.** Ferrying them in a boat, He increases the fun,
and in the midst of the water the gopis gave themselves to Him as a gift[^2].

**216.** Through the hill runs a highroad:
the people of Gokula and Mathura come and go on it.

**217.** Here, see, on the hill is a lovely place:
here He exacts the great toll from the gopis.

**218.** Sitting on this very rock He exacted the toll;
this is the toll-collector's platform, Lord — see it before You"[^3].

**219.** Seeing the rock, the Lord spoke in a faltering voice,
and His whole body turned crimson.

**220.** With His own hand the Lord strokes the rock
and gazes fixedly at the place where [Krishna] sat.

**221.** Now He presses His chest to it, now He bows,
now He says: "Radha, why do you not pay Me My toll?"[^4]

**222.** His body powerless, the Lord falls to the ground,
then suddenly rises and embraces that stone.

**223.** Krishnadasa says: "Gosani, listen to me:
You will surely see all the places — do not be agitated.

**224.** See, east of the hill is this Kusuma-vana [the flower grove];
to its south is the place of the rasa circle."

**225.** As soon as he said this, Gora says: "Stop, stop!
Tell properly about the holy rasa circle."

**226.** "Radha and Krishna danced the rasa — this is that very place."
As soon as he said this, tears streamed from Gora's eyes.

**227.** "O Krishna! O Radha!" He says again and again;
from His reddened eyes flow countless streams.

**228.** Crying "The holy rasa circle!" He rolls on the ground,
now, raising both arms, He lets out loud roars.

**229.** Placing knee over knee He stands thrice-bent[^5],
and, saying "listen, listen," He tells of Radha and Krishna.

**230.** And again: "What more shall I say?" — and He laughs aloud:
"Right here Radha and Krishna danced the rasa!"

**231.** Seeing Gaura overwhelmed, Krishnadasa says:
"On the hill is the place of pastimes by the Radha-kadamba[^6].

**232.** See — the place of the Annakuta, where [the cowherds] worshipped Indra:
at this place Krishna stopped the worship of Indra.

**233.** In his pride King Indra forgot himself
and sent storm and rain upon the cowherd community.

**234.** See — on the hilltop is a Deity in that very form:
the Deity named Hariraya on the hill.

**235.** On Govardhana, on the southern side, dwells
[the Deity] named Gopalaraya: here are Krishna's pastimes.

**236.** Having crushed Indra's pride, He climbs to the hilltop:
here Indra performed the anointing of the King of kings[^7].

**237.** South of the hill is the pond that removes all sins;
above it, see, is the stone of anointing[^8].

**238.** See — on the hill are five more ponds:
the Brahma-kunda and the Rudra-kunda — the essence of all tirthas,

**239.** [and] the ponds named Indra-kunda, Surya-kunda and Moksha-kunda:
all the tirthas on earth rest in them.

**240.** Here, when [Nanda] bathed before breaking the fast on Dvadashi,
Varuna carried Nanda off in order to see Krishna.

**241.** Here is the Brahma-kunda where [the cowherds] were immersed — see, here is Vrindavana:
[here] the children [of Vraja] saw Krishna's glory with their own eyes[^9].

**242.** See — north of this pond is an ashoka grove;
hear a wonderful account about it.

**243.** On the full-moon day of Kartika, in the middle of the day,
the trees [here] burst into flower, and the whole land sees it."

**244.** Hearing this, the Lord gazes at the grove —
and at once the trees blossomed out of season.

**245.** Trees and creepers came into blossom, laden with fruit and flowers;
seeing this wonder, Krishnadasa speaks.

**246.** From Gora's body blows a breeze of wondrous fragrance.
Krishnadasa says: "Your sannyasa is only a disguise"[^10].

**247.** He prostrates on the ground and remains motionless.
"Speak, speak, speak!" Gaura says to Krishnadasa.

**248.** Krishnadasa says: "Gosani, listen:
in this Vrindavana Krishna performed the rasa play.

**249.** At the foot of this wish-fulfilling tree He played the flute —
and for sixteen kroshas around the gopis went mad.

**250.** Drawn by Krishna, the gopis lost their senses
and disregarded family, good conduct, shame, fear and honour.

**251.** All of them had their clothes and ornaments in disarray,
all their thoughts gone to Krishna, the call of Madana ringing in their hearts.

**252.** Enchanted by transcendental love[^11], the maidens of Vraja
came to Krishna and all gathered together.

**253.** Here, see, on the left — this Govindaraya"[^12].
As soon as He heard this, Gorachand was rapt in heart.

**254.** Ecstasy came again; His body was not His own;
waves of prema fill earth and sky.

**255.** With thunderous roars He rains the nectar of rasa;
beasts and birds go mad with the joy of love.

**256.** All the trees blossomed out of season,
the cuckoos sing sweetly, the bees grew intoxicated.

**257.** The Lord calls, crying "The flute!", and, praising the rasa,
says with a gentle smile: "Splendid, splendid!"

**258.** Now, [like] some gopi, He says: "You stay here!" —
now He speaks [like] someone in a dream[^13].

**259.** Now, suddenly startled, He embraces His own body;
His body seemed to melt, all His limbs streaming.

**260.** Now in a childlike rapture He dances with loud laughter;
Krishnadasa, overwhelmed, weeps, fallen at His feet:

**261.** "There is no one in the three worlds with fortune like mine:
by great fortune I have found my lost treasure!"

**262.** When at these words the Lord's outer awareness returned,
He asks Krishnadasa: "Tell Me, what happened then?"

**263.** "Here [Krishna] instructs the gopis about duty to family —
to discern the gopis' hidden devotion,

**264.** or to increase their love:
He heightens the bhava in their hearts in the proper course of rasa[^14].

**265.** 'O slender-waisted ones, why are you in the grove at night?
You were not afraid — why have you come here?

**266.** Your hearts long for another's husband — that is why [you came];
but I do not look at other men's wives or touch them.

**267.** Go home and serve your husbands:
a wife serves her own husband — this is the essence of dharma.

**268.** Be he sick or old, poor or ugly —
service to one's own husband is the very form of the highest dharma.

**269.** Go, go home, go, maidens of Vraja:
a chaste wife does not neglect her duty.

**270.** I keep the great dharma and never act unrighteously;
not understanding My heart, what have you done?'

**271.** Hearing this, the maidens swooned;
they stood motionless, like pictures on a wall.

**272.** Their breathing grew faint, none had words;
the fever of love consumed their bodies.

**273.** Now their breath comes fast from the heat of separation,
now their eyes stream, now their whole body trembles.

**274.** Sometimes they gaze fixedly at Krishna,
sometimes, from love's emotion, they cannot be still.

**275.** Overwhelmed by feeling, meaning to say one thing they say another,
and themselves speak what is in all their hearts:

**276.** 'He whose beauty and qualities enchant the world —
how then can we weak women keep our composure?

**277.** We are women of good families; we know only the vow of fidelity to family;
but the sound of the flute has broken that vow.

**278.** You seem to know nothing — and we [ourselves] do not know [how we came]:
by Your world-enchanting qualities You have drawn the women here.

**279.** You are the supreme Husband of [all] husbands, You are the Atmarama;
if one leaves You, a husband is no refuge — this is proven.

**280.** You are the delight of our soul; take Your delight in us;
where did You see us serving another's husband?[^15]

**281.** O refuge of husbands, Husband, shelter of all,
bliss, supreme bliss, full of every joy!'

**282.** Overflowing with feeling, the loving maidens speak the truth;
hearing these words of love, Krishna was filled with feeling.

**283.** He looked with a tender smile at all the gopis —
how much joy the gopis found no one knows.

**284.** All the gopis surrounded the Lord, the jewel of the Yadus,
like steady lightning flashing in a cloud.

**285.** Here took place this wondrous rasa play:
one gopi, one Krishna — such was its circle.

**286.** Like a garland strung of golden champakas and emeralds —
so was the circle.

**287.** As many gopis, so many Krishnas in this rasa circle:
on the ground of Vrindavana the market of the rasa opened.

**288.** At the wish-fulfilling tree are the two, Radha and Krishna:
Radha, the source of [all] the gopis, the cause of rasa[^16].

**289.** From Krishna there appeared Krishnas without end:
as many Radhas, so many Krishnas — such was the arrangement.

**290.** Over the market of the rasa the moon is the banner;
the cuckoo, as watchman, awakens Kama.

**291.** The bees are the market's music, the wares are youth,
the buyer is the best of connoisseurs of rasa, Madana-mohana.

**292.** Knowing the gopis' pure prema, Sri Hari
reveals His quality of being subject to His devotees.

**293.** In groups are the gopis, skilled dancers,
and the dancer in their midst is the Lord, the jewel of the Yadus.

**294.** Bangles, anklets, jewels and belts of bells jingle;
among them the sweet voice of the flute rings out bright.

**295.** Rabab, upanga, the song of the svaramandala,
mridanga, mandira cymbals, dampha, pakhavaj — sweet melodies.

**296.** And another wonder — here, see, at that place:
in this Vrindavana Krishna made Rai sovereign.

**297.** He placed divine sandal and a garland on Radha
and Himself praises her together with the gopis.

**298.** Having anointed her, He says: 'Listen, gopis:
from today Radha is sovereign in Vrindavana'[^17].

**299.** Thus the King of the Yadus sports in the rasa —
and suddenly none of the gopis can see Him.

**300.** Leaving them all, He went away taking one gopi with Him[^18];
here the gopis weep, flinging themselves on the ground.

**300+1.** 'Tulasi, malati, yuthi, I ask you:
have you seen Haladhara's brother going by this path?[^19]

**300+2.** Auspicious tulasi, sheltered at Krishna's feet,
have you seen Krishna, our life, the jewel of the Yadus?

**300+3.** Who has stolen my dark sapphire?' —
the cowherd girls wander in the deep forest.

**300+4.** 'Rama's younger brother, knowing our pride —
the stealer of hearts — where has He gone, leaving us all?

**300+5.** Listen, O yuthika and mallika!
Kadamba, have you seen Krishna?' the gopis ask.

**300+6.** Not finding Him, all the gopis, for His sake,
enact all of Krishna's lilas:

**300+7.** one became Putana, another became Kana
and, sucking at the breast, took the other's life;

**300+8.** one friend came in the form of the cart,
and another, taking Krishna's form, destroyed her;

**300+9.** then one friend came as Agha and Baka,
and another, as Krishna, slew her.

**301.** And the gopi who was with Him, leaning on His fondness,
says with a smile: 'I am too weary to walk;

**302.** carry me however You can.' Kanu says:
'Come, I will carry you on My shoulders.'

**302+1.** In the cool forest a sea [of joy] surged in her breast;
pulling her garment tight, she girds her waist with a silk cloth[^20].

**303.** Taking her in His arms, He carried her some distance further —
and suddenly became cruel to her too[^21].

**303+1.** At the moment when, placing her hand on Krishna's crest, she was about to mount [His shoulders],
at that very moment Gopinatha vanished.

**304.** Here He vanished from her,
and that gopi, distraught, weeps alone.

**305.** Having lost Krishna, all the other gopis
wander here, bewildered and frantic.

**306.** Distraught in separation, the gopis weep aloud;
hearing of it, grief swells in the heart.

**306+1.** Wandering, the other gopis
see their dear friend Radha weeping[^22].

**306+2.** At the sight of Radha the grief of all welled up,
and all together, flinging themselves down, began to weep;

**306+3.** weeping and weeping, all went mad
and, swooning, fell to the ground.

**307.** Here the gopis are absorbed in Krishna's deeds:
whatever Krishna did wherever, so they do,

**308.** acting out the same, all in just the same way —
all the gopis maddened, their hearts full of Krishna.

**309.** When the gopis thus swooned,
here Krishna then appeared to them[^23].

**310.** And again He performed this rasa play,
and again, in the festival of the rasa, the gopis' joy and delight.

**311.** Thus in joyful play the night passed;
their bodies limp with languor, their attire loosened.

**312.** Taking all the gopis, He went to the bank of the Yamuna
and, weary, slept in the gopis' laps.

**313.** Here is the Yamuna's water, the cool breeze —
and all the gopis sleep happily in Krishna's embrace.

**314.** Thus the auspicious night turned into a bright morning,
and the gopis bowed and went home."

**315.** Thus place after place Goraraya sees;
joyfully Lochana Dasa sings Gora's glories.

[^1]: Vv. 206–210 follow the Bhagavata (10.22–10.23): the maidens' vow to the goddess Katyayani, the stealing of the clothes, the praise of Vrindavana's trees, the wives of the brahmanas performing a yajna; cf. Murari 4.07. The stealing of the clothes is a transcendental (aprakrita) lila: Krishna fulfils the maidens' desire to belong to Him (cf. the note on Bilvavana, en/48).
[^2]: The nauka-lila — Krishna's boat pastime with the gopis on the Manasa-Ganga (cf. Murari 4.08). "Gave themselves as a gift" refers to the gopis' self-surrender; their love is transcendental (cf. en/48, note 10).
[^3]: The dana-lila: Krishna, like a toll-collector, stops the gopis on their way across Govardhana; the "Dana-ghati" and the "toll platform" are holy places of Govardhana.
[^4]: The Lord, in Krishna's mood, addresses Radha with the words of the dana-lila.
[^5]: Tribhanga — Krishna's pose with the flute, bent in three places.
[^6]: "Radha-kadamba" — so in A and B; apparently a kadamba tree on Govardhana associated with Radha's pastimes (C's note gives the variant "Kanu's kadamba").
[^7]: Vv. 232–236 follow the Bhagavata (10.24–10.27): Krishna stops the sacrifice to Indra and arranges the worship of Govardhana (Annakuta); Indra sends the deluge; Krishna lifts the hill; the humbled Indra anoints Krishna (Govinda-abhisheka). Hariraya (Hari-deva) and Gopalaraya are Deities on Govardhana; Gopala on Govardhana was revealed by Madhavendra Puri (CC Madhya 4).
[^8]: "The stone of anointing" (shila ubatane) — the reading is unclear (B: "to its south"); apparently the stone where, by tradition, Krishna was rubbed with fragrant paste.
[^9]: Vv. 240–241 follow the Bhagavata (10.28): Varuna's servant carried off Nanda, who had entered the Yamuna before dawn on Dvadashi; Krishna brought him back and then, immersing the cowherds in the Brahma-hrada, showed them His abode.
[^10]: Krishnadasa recognises Krishna Himself in the Lord: the sannyasi is only His outward guise (cf. en/48, note 13).
[^11]: Vv. 248–252 follow the Bhagavata (10.29.1–8); cf. Murari 4.09. Lochana Dasa himself calls the gopis' "kama" transcendental (aprakrita). In the Gaudiya Vaishnava teaching (CC Madhya 8; Bhaktisiddhanta), the gopis' "kama" is pure prema, the desire only to please Krishna, without a trace of worldly lust; Madana here is Krishna Himself, Madana-mohana.
[^12]: Govindaraya — the Deity of Govinda in Vrindavana, revealed by Rupa Gosvami.
[^13]: The Lord, absorbed in bhava, speaks the words of the participants in the rasa-lila. The 1983 edition reads: "now He says: 'Gopis, stay here!'"
[^14]: Vv. 263–270 follow the Bhagavata (10.29.18–27): Krishna tests the gopis. Lochana Dasa himself explains that He speaks of duty in order to reveal their hidden devotion and increase their love.
[^15]: Vv. 276–281 — cf. the gopis' reply in the Bhagavata (10.29.31–41): Krishna is the Atmarama, the Soul of all souls and the true Husband of all, so the gopis' love for Him is no breach of dharma in the worldly sense. Gaudiya Vaishnavas (Bhaktisiddhanta) stress that the parakiya-rasa of Vraja is wholly transcendental and is no model for imitation in the world.
[^16]: Vv. 285–298 — the rasa-lila (cf. the Bhagavata 10.33; Murari 4.09). Radha is the source from whom all the gopis expand (cf. CC Adi 4); the 1983 edition reads: "the gopis are portions of Radha, for the sake of rasa." In v. 289 A and C read "as many Radhas, so many Krishnas," B "as many gopis, so many Krishnas." In the 1983 edition vv. 287–295 stand after v. 310.
[^17]: Radha's anointing as sovereign of Vrindavana (Vrindavaneshvari) is a Vraja tradition, told briefly by Lochana Dasa; it is not in the Bhagavata.
[^18]: Vv. 299–310 follow the Bhagavata (10.30–10.32): Krishna vanishes from the rasa circle, taking one gopi away; the gopis search for Him, question the trees and enact His lilas; He leaves the chosen gopi too and then appears to all again. In the Gaudiya Vaishnava tradition this chosen gopi is Radha.
[^19]: Vv. 300+1 — 300+9 are found only in the 1983 edition (not in Bhaktisiddhanta's edition of 1922); the 1903 edition gives only the first of them, in a different wording, in a footnote as the reading of one manuscript. Cf. the Bhagavata 10.30.4–23. Haladhara is Balarama.
[^20]: V. 302+1 is found only in the 1983 edition; it is absent in the 1903 edition. The first line is unclear (B: "मातिल पाथर बुको"); translated by the probable sense.
[^21]: In the 1983 edition the first line is worded differently: "The gopi wished to climb onto [His] shoulders"; v. 303+1 is found only there (absent in the 1903 edition). Cf. the Bhagavata 10.30.37–38.
[^22]: Vv. 306+1 — 306+3 are found only in the 1983 edition; they are absent in the 1903 edition. Here the chosen gopi is named Radha outright (cf. the Bhagavata 10.30.39–41).
[^23]: Cf. the Bhagavata 10.32.1–2. In the 1983 edition vv. 307–308 stand before v. 301.
