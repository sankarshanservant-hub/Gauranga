# Adi-khanda, part 1 (portion 09)

*The beginning of the Adi-khanda: the Lord enters Shachi's womb; Advaita Acharya worships Shachi's womb; the praises of the gods; Gaura's appearance on the full moon of Phalguna during an eclipse; the infant's form and marks; the rapture of the people of Nadia; the name Nimai, the festivals of the eighth and ninth days; the beginning of the Balya-lila*

## Janma-lila (The Birth)

> *Summary by Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati.* In this khanda first of all the appearance on earth of the transcendental Sri Gaurahari is described. He who is the original cause of all causes, Parabrahman Narayana, appeared in the womb of Shachi. As her womb grew day by day, so day by day the lustre of her body increased. Seeing the wondrous lustre of her body, all were amazed and concluded: "Surely some great personage has appeared in Shachi's womb." When six months were complete, one day Advaita Acharya Prabhu came to the house of Shachi and Jagannatha, bowed to Shachi's womb and circumambulated it; the reason for this neither Shachi nor Jagannatha could then understand. On some days Shachi-devi saw Brahma, Shiva and the other gods come before her womb, offer praise and pray to the Lord, who gives prema even to chandalas, for the love never given before; and in bliss her heart was filled with compassion for all beings. Gradually ten months were completed. Then, on the full moon of Phalguna, on the pretext of an eclipse, accompanied by the sankirtana of Hari's name, Bhagavan Gaurachandra descended to earth from the ocean of Shachi's womb; the ten directions were filled with bliss, and gods and goddesses, men and women, all longing to see the moon-like face of Shachi's son, came to Shachi's house; at that time her house became Vaikuntha. Jagannatha Mishra and the men and women of Nadia (who had come to see) were all amazed to see on the lotus feet of the child — with a lion's neck, an elephant's shoulders and a broad chest — the marks of the flag, the thunderbolt and the goad, and various superhuman marks on His other limbs; all concluded that this child was surely not human. Then are described the distribution of the eight kinds of pulses on the eighth day, the festival on the ninth, and the one-pointed love of the neighbours, men and women, for [Shachi's] son.

*Raga dhanashi; disha: "Glory to Narahari and Gadadhara, the Lords of my life! Cast, O Lord, an auspicious glance upon living beings! Lord Gorachand — glory, glory!"*[^1]

**1.** Glory, glory to Gadadhara, Gauranga and Narahari!
Glory, glory to Nityananda, the bearer of all energies!

**2.** Glory, glory to Advaita Acharya, Maheshvara!
Glory, glory to the great devotees of Gauranga!

**3.** Placing on my head the dust of the feet of all,
I tell the story of the Adi-khanda — listen attentively.

**4.** When all His own had taken birth,
the cry rang out: "Make ready, make ready!"

**5.** "I go to earth, there must be no more delay" —
and the Lord Himself took shelter in Shachi's womb.

**6.** Cries of "Jaya, jaya!" filled the universe;
gods, nagas and men look on, overcome with prema.

**7.** He whom some call the eternal Effulgence,
whom others call Narayana, subtle and gross,

**8.** whom others call Parabrahman, gross and subtle —
He took shelter in Shachi's womb.

**9.** Radiant, airlike,[^2] the burden of the womb grows daily;
seeing it, the love of all people grows.

**10.** One, two, three, four, five, six months —
in Shachi's womb great bliss reveals itself.

**11.** Day by day the radiance of Shachi's body grows;
seeing it, all the people rejoice at heart.

**12.** "We do not know who has come into Shachi's house" —
so in every house all are pondering.

**13.** When six months of Shachi's womb were complete,
the house shone with the splendour of her body.

**14.** At that time a wondrous thing took place:
suddenly Advaita Acharya came there.

**15.** In the house sat Jagannatha, best of the twice-born;
seeing Advaita Acharya, he rose respectfully.

**16.** Advaita Acharya Gosani is the abode of all virtues,
blessed in the three worlds, without equal.

**17.** Seeing him, Mishra Purandara with great respect
himself brought a seat for him to sit on,

**18.** and placed the dust of his feet on his head;
the Acharya, abashed, showed much humility.

**19.** Shachi-devi gave water to wash his feet;
seeing Shachi, he, full of love, rose respectfully.

**20.** With love his two lotus eyes grew red,
his eyes brimmed with tears, his face was flushed,

**21.** his lips trembled, his voice faltered;
he could not hold his body steady — he staggered.

**22.** Circumambulating Shachi, he bows to her;
Shachi-devi started, seeing this breach of custom.[^3]

**23.** Jagannatha is perplexed, Shachi amazed:
"What are you doing, what are you doing?" she says, pained at heart.

**24.** Jagannatha says: "Listen, Acharya Gosani:
no one can understand your conduct.

**25.** Be kind and tell us, dispel our doubt,
or else the fire of this anxiety will burn my body."

**26.** The Acharya said: "Listen, Mishra Purandara:
later you will know everything" — such was his answer.

**27.** His whole body thrilled: knowing the matter,
he anointed Shachi's holy womb with fragrant sandal,

**28.** circumambulated [her] seven times, bowed down,
and without saying anything went to his own place.

**29.** Here Shachi and Jagannatha ponder:
"Why did he worship my womb?"

**30.** Acharya Gosani worshipped the womb —
and Shachi's radiance grew a hundredfold; she forgets herself.

**31.** She sees all as joy, sees no sorrow;
she sees all the gods before her.

**32.** Brahma, Shiva, Sanaka and the others — all the gods,
facing her womb, offer praise:

**33.** "Glory, glory to the Infinite, the Non-dual, the Eternal!
Glory to the Bliss of Achyuta, the Eternal Bliss, Janardana![^4]

**34.** Glory to Him who is beyond prakriti — sattva, rajas and tamas!
Glory to Maha-Vishnu [abiding] in the Causal Ocean!

**35.** Glory to the Lord of Paravyoma, whose greatness is boundless!
Glory to [Him who is] sattva, the highest sattva, whose form is pure sattva!

**36.** Glory to the Lord of Goloka, the beloved of Radha!
Glory, glory to the supreme Lord of the countless Vaikunthas!

**37.** Glory, glory to the carefree, playful hero (*dhira-lalita*)!
Glory, glory to the all-enchanting son of Nanda!

**38.** Now, in the Kali-yuga, appearing in Shachi's womb,
You have come to taste Your own play Yourself.

**39.** Glory, glory to this Lord, the giver of supreme bliss!
Such mercy has never been.

**40.** In the age of Kali You have become the giver of Yourself:
with Gadadhara there will be no judging of worthy and unworthy.[^5]

**41.** That prema for which we, all the gods, beg
we have not obtained — not a particle, not even its fragrance.

**42.** That prema, that sweet rasa, You will taste Yourself
and give to all to taste, down to the chandala, not looking at faults.

**43.** May we too obtain a particle of Your prema,
may we sing with You the virtues of Radha and Krishna.

**44.** Glory, glory to Gaurahari, the giver of sankirtana!"
So saying, the gods circumambulate [her].

**45.** With four mouths Brahma offers many kinds of praise;
Shachi-devi grew afraid, her mind startled.

**46.** Shachi's heart was filled with compassion for all beings:
she is kind [to all] as to herself, not distinguishing her own from others.

**47.** Ten months of the pregnancy were complete; [joy] on every side —
the goddess forgets herself with the joy of her heart.

**48.** An auspicious day, an auspicious hour, the lunar day of the full moon,
the auspicious night of Phalguna, the radiance of the moon.

**49.** At a wondrous hour Rahu swallows the moon —
and on every side arose the cry: "Hari, Hari!"[^6]

**50.** All around was filled with a divine, lovely fragrance;
the ten directions grew bright, the breeze blew soft and gentle.

**51.** At that time all six seasons appeared at once —
at such an hour the Lord took His auspicious birth on earth.

**52.** In the sky the gods look on from their celestial cars,
running in love to see the body of Gaura.

**53.** Only one sound is heard: "Haribol, Haribol!" —
this my Lord revealed the moment He was born.

**54.** In Shachi's house the riches of Vaikuntha appeared;[^7]
beside herself with bliss, Shachi speaks in a faltering voice;

**55.** with a gesture she calls Jagannatha Pandita:
"Our life has borne fruit — look at our son's face!"

**56.** The women of the town joyfully cry "Jaya, jaya!",
all beside themselves with bliss at the sight of the child.

**57.** The wives of sages, goddesses and naga maidens all came —
seeing Gauranga, they raised the cry "Jaya, jaya!"

**58.** The fragrance of Gaura's majesty filled the universe;
each of His limbs is a treasury of rasa, unbroken nectar.

**59.** Gazing and gazing, all their eyes were soothed;
to all it seemed: this is the life of the nagaris of Vraja.

**60.** "Such a child we have never seen or heard of;
looking at Him, the heart does — we know not what!

**61.** Such marks we have never seen in a human being,"
say the celestial beauties; "later we shall know [who He is]."

**62.** Jagannatha is beside himself, looking at his son's face:
the universe cannot contain the delight of his heart.

**63.** How many moons are rising — so looks His little face!
His face is likened to a blossoming lotus.

**64.** His high nose surpasses the sesame flower,
the radiance of Gora's body sparkles — nectar.

**65.** His lips are red, His cheeks lovely and shining;
seeing His beautiful chin, love wells up.

**66.** A lion's neck, an elephant's shoulders, a broad chest,
arms reaching to the knees, a body full of rasa.

**67.** Broad hips, thighs like plantain stems,
two feet like petals of a red lotus.

**68.** On the lotus soles — the flag, the thunderbolt, the goad,
the chariot, the umbrella, the chamara, the svastika, the jambu fruit,

**69.** the upward line, the triangle, the elephant's kumbha[^8] —
this whole wondrous form pours out nectar.

**70.** In such a wondrous form upon the earth
shine marks surpassing [those of] great kings.

**71.** Indra, Chandra, gandharvas, kinnaras and gods
came to earth — what a joyous wonder!

**72.** The eyes of all seemed anointed with a salve of nectar —
as if after long love they saw their beloved.

**73.** The child was just born — yet to those who saw Him
it seemed He had long been their friend of old.

**74.** From each limb He pours nectar in heaps;
why, when the eyes behold [Him], does the heart [so stir]?

**75.** Seeing the child, [the women's] bosoms filled with joy;
why are their eyes languid, why have the knots of their garments loosened?[^9]

**76.** The moment the newborn child was seen,
His beautiful face surpassed millions of Kamas.

**77.** So reflecting, all cry "Jaya, jaya!
Shachi's son by His very nature is not human.

**78.** Shachi's son is a new Kamadeva:
even His crying is nectar to the ears."

**79.** "The Lord of Goloka Himself has descended" —
such was the conclusion the women reached.

**80.** The Lord of all the worlds has appeared on earth —
beside himself with bliss says this Lochana Dasa.

*Raga mangala-gurjari; disha: "Rejoice, celebrate! In bliss, in Shachi's house, sing of Gora's virtues!"*

*Mishra Purandara, overflowing with bliss —
his voice faltering —
summoning his kin and friends without delay,
celebrates the festival of his son's birth.*[^10]

**81.** "Jaya, jaya, jaya!" — all around is full of joy,
the town is filled with bliss;
all the wives of good families came by hundreds
and distribute sindura and pithali.[^11]

**82.** Taking her son on her lap, in bliss, full of love,
Shachi-devi speaks in a faltering voice:
"Give your blessing, give the dust of your feet as a boon —
may the boy live long!

**83.** The boy is not mine — call Him your own and bless Him,
all you women!"
His body is sweeter than nectar; to ward off an evil outcome
they gave Him the name Nimai.[^12]

**84.** On this eighth day, for the children's delight,
the "eight kinds of pulses" are given out;
the festival of the ninth night — all is bliss,
joyful music plays.[^13]

**85.** Day by day Shachi's son grows —
a full moon upon the earth;
His two eyes bright with kajal,
a gorochana tilaka beautifully drawn.

**86.** Again and again He moves His little hands and feet,
smiling ever so slightly;
Shachi and Jagannatha, seeing this wonder,
gaze without blinking.

**87.** Every day she rubs His holy body
with fragrant oil and turmeric,
kisses His face and holds Him to her heart —
blessed is virtuous Shachi!

**88.** So day by day, moment by moment, bliss
grows in the town of Nadia;
they know not day or night, nor weekday nor lunar day;
in prema they forget themselves.

**89.** In the town of Nadia there is bliss in every house —
what to speak of men and women:
children, old men, the blind, bound by prema,
are drunk with matchless joy.

**90.** A face surpassing the autumn moon — they fancy
Madana himself dwells in it;
all the young women, as many as there were, went mad
and left their household duties to their elders.

**91.** Three times a day the women of the town run
to see the child;
"Let me see, let me see!" — all take Him on their laps,
and their bodies thrill with rapture.

**92.** So day by day, moment by moment —
can the bliss be told?
Hoping for the feet of Narahari Dasa,
Lochana Dasa sings [His] virtues.

*Thus ends the description of the Janma-lila.*

## Balya-lila (The Childhood Pastimes)

> *Summary by Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati.* When six months had passed, Gaurasundara's first feeding with rice and naming were performed according to rule; since with His appearance the whole world was filled with bliss, the wise named Him Vishvambhara. Gradually Vishvambhara, holding His father's finger, began to walk about the courtyard. Before the lustre of Gaurasundara's body, adorned with armlets, bracelets, a pearl necklace and other ornaments, the radiance of millions of moons grows dim. [The moon] can dispel only outer darkness, not inner darkness, but the moon of Gaura dispels darkness both outer and inner. Shachi-devi used to lull her son to sleep singing "Come, come, O moon, come" and other songs; at times she saw various gods and goddesses come and praise her son, and was greatly amazed. At times, seeing Gaurahari dance wildly with the gods, crying "Radha-Govinda", she fell senseless. At times she heard the sound of ankle-bells on His bare feet and was astonished; at times, fearing some coming misfortune, she became very anxious; and the next moment, seeing her son's face, she forgot everything and kissed His face again and again. When some time had passed in this way, Gaurasundara began to play outside the house with His playmates. When Shachi-devi tried to catch Gaurasundara, He would run away; and at times, growing angry, He would come home and spoil everything in the house. At times He explained to His mother the worthlessness of worldly notions of pure and impure and instructed her in the transcendental knowledge that Krishna is the Lord of all. Then are described: how He sat in a pit full of pots of leftover food and instructed His mother; how He struck His mother and, seeing her fall senseless, brought a coconut; various childish pranks; His play with a puppy; how Gaurahari, angry with His mother for letting the puppy go, wept; how the puppy, in a divine body, singing of Hari, went to Vaikuntha; how Brahma and others, seeing the puppy's good fortune, praised Gaura; how Gaurahari wept when Shachi-devi prepared an offering for the worship of Shashthi, and under the guise of words revealed to Shachi that He is the Lord of all.

*Raga sindhura*

**93.** So day by day the body of Shachi's son
grows — like a stream of nectar.

**94.** What comparison shall I give Him? I can find none;
my heart trembles — I cannot tell it.

**95.** His moon-like face is a moon full in all sixteen digits every day;
even those blind from birth run, longing to see Him.

**96.** In rapture a half-smile plays on His lips,
like an ocean of nectar with rolling waves.

**97.** His two red eyes are drowning in rasa —
who has built a dam of the nectar-mud of kajal?

**98.** Virtuous Shachi and fortunate Jagannatha
both gaze lovingly at their son's face.

**99.** Now He laughs, now He cries, now He frolics,
now He is on her lap, now He swings on her breast.

**100.** Planting both His feet on Shachi's breast,
He sways like a golden creeper in the breeze.

**101.** Very long eyes, beautiful ringing laughter —
nectar seems to spill from His lips.

**102.** His nose more charming than a parrot's beak,
both cheeks radiant, His form beautiful.

[^1]: A has "upon living beings"; the 1983 edition "upon me".
[^2]: That is, not gross-bodied. The Gaudiya Vaishnavas stress that the Lord's birth is not a worldly conception: the eternal Lord, full of knowledge and bliss, first enters the heart of the father, then of the mother, and reveals Himself, as Krishna does in the *Bhagavata* (10.2.16–18); cf. *Chaitanya-charitamrita* (Adi 13.83–88).
[^3]: An elder brahmana bowing to a young woman and circumambulating her goes against custom; but Advaita is worshipping the Lord in her womb.
[^4]: A play on words in the praise: "Advaita" ("non-dual") and "Nityananda" ("eternal bliss") are both epithets of the Lord and names of His closest associates.
[^5]: "Gadadhara" ("holder of the mace") is a name of Vishnu; perhaps there is also an allusion to Gadadhara Pandita, Gaura's inseparable companion.
[^6]: Cf. *Chaitanya-charitamrita* (Adi 13.89–98): the Lord appeared on the full moon of Phalguna during a lunar eclipse, when everyone, according to custom, was chanting the names of Hari.
[^7]: So after Bhaktisiddhanta's summary ("her house became Vaikuntha"); in A the line is corrupt; the 1983 edition has "in Shachi's womb the riches of the great Vaikuntha".
[^8]: Marks on the feet and body of a *mahapurusha*. The rendering of the last mark ("the elephant's kumbha", i.e. the frontal globe on an elephant's head) is tentative.
[^9]: Here and further on (vv. 59, 75, 90) Lochana Dasa describes the attraction of the women of Nadia to Gaura in the spirit of his *nagari-bhava*, in which Gaura is the *nagara*, the beloved of the townswomen. The Gaudiya tradition, following Vrindavana Dasa and Krishnadasa Kaviraja, does not accept worship of Gaura in such a bhava; Bhaktisiddhanta counts the "Gaura-nagari" among the deviant schools. We translate the text as it stands.
[^10]: In A this stanza is given in brackets together with the disha and is unnumbered; in the 1983 edition it is the first stanza of the song.
[^11]: Sindura is red powder, the mark of a married woman; pithali is rice paste for auspicious designs; they are distributed to women at a birth festival.
[^12]: The name comes from *nim*, the bitter neem tree: by custom a child is given a "bitter" name to avert misfortune and the evil eye. According to the *Chaitanya-charitamrita* (Adi 13.118), the name Nimai was given by Sita Thakurani to protect Him from witches.
[^13]: *Ashtakalai* — the distribution to children of eight kinds of pulses and grains on the eighth day after birth; on the ninth night, a festival with music.
