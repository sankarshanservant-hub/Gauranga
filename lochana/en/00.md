# Translator's Preface

*Sri Chaitanya-mangala* is a Bengali poem on the life and pastimes of Sri Chaitanya Mahaprabhu. Its author, Lochana Dasa Thakura, was a disciple of Narahari Sarakara Thakura of Shrikhanda, an associate of the Lord; in the poem he repeatedly calls himself a follower of Narahari. According to Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati (see his preface below), Lochana Dasa came of a vaidya family of the Rarh country and was born in the village of Kogram near Katwa (Burdwan district). The poem was probably written about the middle of the sixteenth century (it is usually placed in the 1550s–1560s); no exact date is known.

The basis of the poem, as the author himself says (Sutra-khanda, vv. 53–56), is Murari Gupta's Sanskrit poem on the Lord, composed as a dialogue between Murari and Damodara Pandita (the so-called *Kadacha* of Murari Gupta; Bhaktisiddhanta calls it *Sri Chaitanya-charita*). Lochana Dasa retells it "as a sung narrative", a *panchali*: the poem is divided into songs, each with its raga and many with a *disha* (refrain); the main metre is the *payar* (couplet), and *tripadi* also occurs in the songs. Many places are enlarged with traditions not found in Murari. The poem has four parts (khandas): the Sutra-khanda (introduction and the "prehistory" of the Lord's descent), the Adi-khanda (from His birth to the journey to Gaya), the Madhya-khanda (from the return from Gaya to His coming to Puri and the meeting with Sarvabhauma) and the Shesha-khanda (the southern tour, Vrindavana, the return to Nilachala, the last pastimes).

**The base text.** The translation follows the edition of Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati (Sri Chaitanya Math, Mayapur, 1922)[^1]. His text and his numbering of the couplets are the basis of our translation. The prose summaries which Bhaktisiddhanta placed at the head of chapters and sections are translated and given at the head of the corresponding sections ("Summary by Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati"). The scan of this edition available to us is badly damaged in places; gaps and obscure places have been restored from two other editions: that of Haridasa Shastri (Vrindavana, 1983; the Bengali text is printed there in Devanagari script) and that of Atulakrishna Gosvami (Calcutta, 1903). Where the editions differ in sense, we follow Bhaktisiddhanta and note significant differences in the footnotes.

**Text not found in Bhaktisiddhanta's edition.** The 1983 edition contains a good deal that is absent from Bhaktisiddhanta's edition: whole chapters, passages and single couplets. All of this has been translated and put in its place in the text (following the 1983 edition), numbered "80+1", "80+2" and so on (that is, after v. 80 of Bhaktisiddhanta's edition); at the start of each such insertion a footnote says that it is absent from Bhaktisiddhanta's edition and whether the 1903 edition has it. Translated in this way are, for example, the section on Shachi's grief over the brahmana's curse (Madhya-khanda, portion 33), the section "Consoling the devotees" (portion 38), many stanzas about the women of Nadia (portions 18–22) and others. Where the 1983 edition does not add text but gives a different redaction of the same passage, the main text follows Bhaktisiddhanta and the other redaction is given in translation in a footnote. Bhaktisiddhanta's edition ends with the story of the poor brahmana from Dravida and Vibhishana; the last chapter, on the Lord's disappearance (ch. 4 of the Shesha-khanda in the 1983 edition), and Lochana Dasa's closing words about himself exist only in the editions of 1983 and 1903; they are translated as an insertion of the same kind, with their own footnote (portion 52)[^2].

**Faithfulness to the author.** We translate Lochana Dasa's words as they stand, without correcting them to later theology and without softening them. Our authority in interpretation is Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati. Where Lochana Dasa departs from the Gaudiya siddhanta as he taught it — for example, in the lines about the women of Nadia drawn to Gaura as to a *nagara* (nagari-bhava), in some identifications of the Lord's associates, in details of the narrative that differ from the *Chaitanya-charitamrita*, in the account of the Lord's disappearance by entering Jagannatha — we give an exact translation and explain the difference in a footnote. Bhaktisiddhanta himself, in his preface, declines to ascribe the "Gaura-nagari" doctrine to the poem and warns against songs circulating under Lochana Dasa's name into which that doctrine was later introduced.

**Arrangement of the translation.** The couplets are numbered as in Bhaktisiddhanta's edition; at the start of some chapters and sections the edition's numbering begins anew, and so does ours. Where the edition repeats numbers by mistake, we keep the printed numbers and distinguish the repeats by a letter (371a; 393a–401b in portion 13, see footnote 1 there). Lines naming the raga and the refrain (disha) are given in italics without a number; words added for clarity are in square brackets. A tripadi stanza is given under one number. The translation is divided into 52 portions: Sutra-khanda 01–08, Adi-khanda 09–23, Madhya-khanda 24–45, Shesha-khanda 46–52. The full edition contains, besides the translation, the Bengali original (in Bengali script, in the medieval spelling of the edition), its Latin transliteration and a word-for-word translation. Modern English translations were consulted only for doubtful places.

There follow Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati's preface to his edition and his synopsis of the contents by parts and chapters (*Khanda-vivarana*).

# Preface by Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati

*Preface (bhumika) to Sri Chaitanya-mangala*[^3]

**The reader's qualification.** In taking up any book one must first of all make the mind [pure and calm], not allow former impressions to prevail, and set about reading without prejudice, in a mood favourable to grasping its meaning. Every author wishes his labour to serve to free the reader from ignorance. On the other hand, the reader too, having considered his own qualification (*adhikara*), should think: "[I wish to read this book] in order to receive light on what I do not know." The pride nursed by lovers of argument, who, regarding themselves as examiners, set out to test the author, is merely the attitude of a trader. If one seeks in reading a calculation of profit, the place [of the truth] is taken by hopes of gain, honour and fame. Worldly people, thus driven by their desires, take the subject the book speaks of for merchandise — and are deprived of [the benefit borne by] the words of the devotee author.

**The meaning of the book's title.** By the pastimes of Sri Chaitanyadeva the bonds will fall from the living beings bewildered by maya and blessing (*mangala*) will come — therefore the narrative of Sri Chaitanya is called *Sri Chaitanya-mangala*. Sri Chaitanyadeva Himself is full of the highest blessing; therefore the narrative of Sri Chaitanya may well be called *Chaitanya-mangala*. Srila Vrindavana Dasa Thakura too wrote a book under this name; Srila Krishnadasa Kaviraja Gosvami Prabhu mentions it in his *Sri Chaitanya-charitamrita*[^4]. That very book became famous under the name *Sri Chaitanya-bhagavata*.

Certain literati of Buddhist leanings and scholars attached to them have recently composed, in the guise of a literary monument, a fictitious book, and are trying to prove that it is old. [Certain] Shaiva literati and literati of [other] communities [hostile to bhakti], showing aversion to pure bhakti, have conceived the idea of uprooting pure bhakti by spreading [this book]. [The book] which mundane literati hostile to bhakti pass off as approved by devotees — displaying only contemptible efforts — we do not honour. Because of their Buddhist convictions and godlessness they have no access to the highest goal (*paramartha*); and the fruit of their illegitimate efforts, rooted in the desire to pass off the worthless (*anartha*) as the highest goal, is that the fictitious *Chaitanya-mangala* composed by Jayananda has been called into being[^5]. Pure devotees never read such a book and do not, [by accepting it,] bring defilement on themselves. In the *Bhakti-ratnakara* and other books of Sri Narahari Chakravarti there is not a single mention of the *Chaitanya-mangala* of Jayananda and similar books; therefore, [regarding] this book contrary to the truth as the work of a deviant community (*apasampradaya*), we have given it the name *A-chaitanya-mangala*[^6]. The admirers of such books may, together with the haters of bhakti, agree to support it, but we pay them our respects from afar.

In studying the life of Sri Chaitanya, let the indisputable books for us be the works of the reliable biographers of Sri Chaitanya — Srila Kavi-karnapura Gosvami, Srila Murari Gupta Vaidya, Srila Krishnadasa Kaviraja Gosvami and Srila Vrindavana Dasa Thakura. In the *Chaitanya-charita-mahakavya*, in the *Gaura-krishnodaya* of the Utkala poet Sri Govinda-deva, in *Sri Chaitanya-mangala* and [similar] books there is no glaring trace of doctrines opposed to bhakti, and therefore those who walk the path of bhakti accept them as evidence without hesitation[^7]. But the *Baul-chandrika*, the *[Bhakta]mala* of Laladasa[^8], the *Chaitanya-mangala* of Jayananda and some other books, although they are now the chief object of study among literati, are untouchable for us […]: walking the path of bhakti, we dare not accept them. Those who have realized even a little of the nature of bhakti do not leave the path of bhakti, and so in their writings there is no place for the doctrine of the Charvakas, Buddhist beliefs or the dominance of materialism.

Seeing the grace of language of Srila Lochana Dasa Thakura's *Sri Chaitanya-mangala* and his heartfelt love for Sri Gaura, some indeed say that he is inclined to stray somewhat from the truth of pure bhakti. We are not prepared to say so, for "the actions and gestures of a Vaishnava are not understood even by the wise"[^9]. May the abominable desire to impute the stench of the Gaura-nagari doctrine to *Sri Chaitanya-mangala* never take hold of our hearts[^10].

Our one hope with regard to the readers is that, praying to Sri Chaitanya for mercy, we shall be freed from various considerations full of anarthas and attain blessing. We also hope that the readers will kindly not make common cause with the ten or thirteen deviant communities (*apasampradayas*) who come forward claiming to be called Gaudiyas and offend the feet of the true Gaudiyas[^11].

**The source of Sri Chaitanya-mangala** is the *Sri Chaitanya-charita* of Sri Murari Gupta. The author writes in the Sutra-khanda:

> "All that the Lord did from His birth in childhood,
> how from beginning to end He spread prema —
> about all this Damodara Pandita questioned him,
> and he told him everything from beginning to end in order.
> Thus came about the book of Gauranga's deeds, composed in verse —
> the dialogue with Damodara that came from Murari's lips.
> Hearing it, I was filled with love,
> and I tell of Gaura's deeds in a sung narrative, a panchali."[^12]

The author of this book is a disciple of Srila Narahari Dasa, an associate of Sri Gaura; he was born in a vaidya family of the Rarh country, at Kogram in the Katwa subdivision of Burdwan district. In many places of the book Thakura Lochana Dasa calls himself a follower of Srila Narahari Sarakara Thakura Mahashaya.

*Sri Chaitanya-mangala* is a book of the panchali kind, that is, a work composed in song-metres of five kinds[^13]. Its language shows an abundance of feeling and extraordinary grace. The collections of popular songs that have lately come into circulation under the name "Lochana's panchali" must be approached with special caution: into many of them the stench of the newfangled Gaura-nagari doctrine has been introduced.

Many hesitate to accept the historical statements of *Sri Chaitanya-mangala*: part [of them] has been gathered on the basis of dreams[^14], and full regard for the truth of pure bhakti is not seen everywhere. Tradition says that Srila Lochana Dasa Thakura's book was written somewhat before the *Sri Chaitanya-bhagavata* of Thakura Srila Vrindavana Dasa; when Srila Vrindavana's mother said that [that] book had been written earlier, it became necessary to rename the book of Sri Vrindavana Dasa Thakura[^15]. No one can doubt the reliability of the geographical indications of *Sri Chaitanya-mangala*. The composition of *Sri Chaitanya-mangala* was begun on the basis of the childhood pastimes of Sri Gaurasundara recorded by Sri Murari Gupta Vaidya in the *Sri Chaitanya-charita*: Lochana Dasa took them as his source-evidence. The clear description in this book of Sri Chaitanyadeva's ideal of renunciation will give joy to the reader, and above all Srila Lochana Thakura's love for Sri Gaura will draw the love of Gaura's devotees — of this there is no doubt. The old panchali poetry of Gunaraja Khan was composed long before this book[^16]. The songs of *Sri Chaitanya-mangala* are sung to this day in various places of Rarh, as *jhumur* or the songs of the *Ramayana* are sung. The poetry of *Sri Chaitanya-charitamrita*, founded on Srila Kaviraja Gosvami's description of prema-bhakti, the charming verse of Sri Lochana Dasa Thakura, and the supremely magnanimous grace of language of Sri Vrindavana Dasa Thakura, rooted in pure bhakti, have always increased the bliss of the devotees of Sri Gaura.

# Synopsis of the Khandas (Khanda-vivarana)

*A brief account of the contents by parts and chapters, placed by Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati at the beginning of his edition*[^17]. *The portions of our translation are given in brackets.*

## Sutra-khanda

**Mangalacharana** (portion 01). Having sung the glory of Sri Krishna Chaitanya, [the author] prays for the mercy first of the Vaishnavas and then of his own gurudeva, Sriman Narahari Thakura; bows at the feet of the countless associates of Gaura-lila, known and unknown; praying for the blessing of guru and Vaishnavas, declares that the model of his book, a *panchali-prabandha*, is the Sanskrit mahakavya of Sri Murari Gupta called *Sri Chaitanya-charita*; and briefly describes what will be written in the Adi-, Madhya- and Shesha-khanda.

**Grantharambha (the beginning of the narrative)** (portions 02–08). At the beginning of the narrative the author, drawing on the dialogue of Narada and Uddhava from the *Jaimini-bharata* as related in the conversation of Damodara and Murari, describes why Sri Krishna descended in the form of Sri Gaura. To relieve the distress of living beings stricken by Kali, Devarshi Narada goes to Dvaraka; there, during a conversation of Rukmini and Krishna, the talk turns to Krishna's accepting the bhava of Radha; hearing this, Rukmini suffers at the prospect of separation; as Radha's greatness is being glorified, Sri Krishna, at the Devarshi's prayer, tells him that He will appear as Gaura in Navadvipa, in the house of Shachi and Jagannatha, and shows him His Gaura form. Having seen the Gaura form, the best of sages, singing of Gaura-lila, goes to Naimisharanya and there, in answer to Uddhava's question, glorifies the Kali-yuga, the essence of all yugas, and the dharma of the age, the sankirtana of Hari's name. Then [Narada] goes to Shambhu, best of Vaishnavas, on Kailasa and [reminds] Parvati of her former [vow] to distribute mahaprasada to all living beings without distinction; for this purpose he tells his own story: how he obtained the [Lord's] mahaprasada through Lakshmi-devi, how he gave that prasada to Shiva, how Parvati [was deprived of it] and made her vow, and how as a result the Lord's coming [was promised] — and he sings of Gaura's future appearance in the Kali-yuga. Then [Narada] goes to Brahma and tells of Gaura's appearance, and Brahma brings forward verses of the *Srimad-Bhagavata* and other [scriptures] that bear witness to this lila. Wandering about, Narada sees the misery of the living beings of Kali and becomes thoughtful; to the pensive sage a voice from heaven announces [the appearance] of the avatara, Jagannatha in Nilachala; the Devarshi goes to Purushottama and from there, at the command of the Lord of the gods, sets out for Goloka: first to Vaikuntha, then higher, to Goloka; there he beholds various lilas, faints at the sight of the Gaura form, and hears the news that [the Lord] will come to earth with all the gods. Coming to Shvetadvipa, he sees the wonderful lila of Sri Balarama, [the Lord in the form of] service; then comes the proposal to the gods to take birth in the world of mortals. [Having described how] Krishna, accepting the bhava and lustre of Srimati Radhika, together with His eternal associates [who will come] with their distinctive natures — Rukmini, Satyabhama and others — and taking up the weapon of the sankirtana of the name, descends as Gaura; Balarama as Nityananda; Shiva as Advaita Prabhu[^18]; and the other associates as Murari, Mukunda, Shrinivasa, Raya Ramananda, Ishvara Puri and Madhava Puri, [the author] glorifies his own guru, Thakura Narahari, and his nephew Raghunandana.

## Adi-khanda

**Chapter 1. Janma-lila (The Birth)** (portion 09). The appearance of Sri Gaurahari on earth with His associates; the coming of the Parabrahman Narayana, [beyond] the gross and the subtle, into the ocean of Shachi's womb; as the burden grows, the lustre of Shachi-devi's body grows; seeing the wonderful lustre, [people] conclude that some great personality has appeared in Shachi's womb; in the sixth month Advaita Prabhu bows to Shachi's womb and circumambulates it; Brahma, Shiva and other gods come to Shachi's womb and glorify the lila of the Lord, the Giver of prema, who bestows prema never given before; Shachi-devi, seeing this, forgets herself; on the full moon of Phalguna, during the eclipse, amid the sankirtana of Hari's name, Bhagavan Gaurahari descends to earth; the ten directions are filled with bliss; goddesses together with the women [of Nadia] come to Shachi's house to see the moon-like face of Shachi's son; Goloka appears in the house; Jagannatha Mishra and the men and women of Nadia are amazed to see, on the lotus feet of the infant with a lion's neck, an elephant's shoulders and a broad chest, the marks of the flag, thunderbolt and goad and various superhuman marks, and understand that the infant is beyond mortals; the distribution of eight kinds of pulses on the eighth day, the festival on the ninth day; the wholehearted love of the neighbours, men and women, for Shachi's son.

**Chapter 2. Balya-lila (Childhood Pastimes)** (portions 09–13). After six months, Gaurahari's first feeding with rice and name-giving; since at His appearance the whole world was filled with bliss, the wise give Him the name Vishvambhara; He walks about the courtyard holding His father's finger; Gaurasundara in armlets, bracelets and various ornaments: the moon in the sky can dispel only outer darkness, but the moon Gaura dispels the inner darkness of living beings as well; lulling her son to sleep, Shachi-devi sees various gods and goddesses glorifying her son; Gaurahari dances wildly with the gods and goddesses, crying "Radha-Govinda"; the sound of ankle-bells is heard on His bare feet; Gaurasundara takes to childish games with His playmates outside the house; when Shachi-devi tries to catch Him, He runs away, and sometimes in anger spoils everything in the house; having explained to His mother the worthlessness of mundane notions of pure and impure and the like, Gaurasundara instructs her in the transcendental knowledge that Krishna is the Lord of all; sitting in a pit full of pots with leftovers, He instructs His mother; He strikes His mother — at the blow she faints, and He brings a coconut; various childish pranks; play with a puppy; anger at His mother for letting the puppy go, and weeping; the puppy, in a divine body, singing of Hari, goes to Vaikuntha; seeing this, Brahma and others glorify Gaura; Gaurahari weeps when Shachi-devi is preparing the offering for the worship of Shashthi, and under cover of words reveals that He is the Lord of all.

**Chapter 3. Pauganda-lila (Boyhood)** (portions 14–17). Gaurasundara, having heard Murari Gupta expound the scripture of yoga, mocks him — Murari's anger; in return, to show the worthlessness of yoga and what the yogi comes to, [the Lord] urinates in Murari's plate during his midday meal and instructs him in the superiority of devotion to Krishna; the enactment of sankirtana with the boys of His age; in the conversation of Murari and Damodara — the sannyasa of Vishvarupa, the grief of Shachi and Jagannatha, and the pauganda-lila of Gaurasundara; the chudakarana and other rites for Gaurahari; at an auspicious hour, the beginning of writing; seeing that He is always absorbed in childish play and indifferent to study, Mishra Purandara punishes Him with reproaches and the like; at night in a dream Vishvambhara, revealing His divinity, admonishes the Mishra; the Mishra recognizes his son as Bhagavan, but on waking is again absorbed in parental love; Gaurahari's upanayana; the account of the avataras of the four yugas; in the Kali-yuga Krishna, accepting the bhava and lustre of Radha, descends in secret as Gaurasundara to preach the dharma of the age, the sankirtana of Hari's name, and, intoxicated with prema, gives prema away, offering it at the door of every living being; the instruction to His mother not to eat grain on the day of Ekadashi; when Mishra Jagannatha falls ill — instruction on the impermanence of human life and consolation of His mother; Shachi's grief after the Mishra's passing; Gaurahari's grief for His father and His zealous beginning of study.

**Chapter 4. Kaishora-lila (Youth) and the Wedding** (portions 18–19). Vanamali Acharya comes to Shachi-devi to propose a match for Gaurasundara; receiving no satisfactory answer from Shachi-devi, he returns with a heavy heart and on the way meets Gaura going home from His studies; by a hint [Gaura] gives His mother His consent to marry; at Shachi's call Vanamali Acharya comes and, going to the house of Vallabhacharya, arranges the marriage of Gaurahari with his daughter Lakshmi-devi; the announcement of the wedding and the invitation [of relatives]; on the day of adhivasa, according to family custom, anointing with turmeric and other [folk and Vedic] rites; Vallabha gives his daughter to Gaurahari, who arrives in great pomp with many companions, and after the feeding of brahmanas and the rest sends his daughter to his son-in-law's house.

**Chapter 5. Kaishora-lila and Banga-vijaya** (portions 20–21). Mahaprabhu goes with His friends to the bank of the Ganga; at the coming of her beloved Lord the Ganga rises [to] touch His feet; the story of a brahmana devoted to the Ganga; a Puranic legend: the Ganga arises from the feet of Sri Hari as He listened to the Devarshi singing of His qualities; Gaurahari, on the pretext of earning money, goes to East Bengal, shows mercy to the Padmavati and to the people of East Bengal and returns to Navadvipa; meanwhile Lakshmi-devi, bitten by the serpent of separation from the Lord, disappears from this world; when Shachi-devi, seeing the Lord, voices her grief, Gaurahari consoles His mother and tells the story of Lakshmi-devi.

**Chapter 6. The Second Wedding** (portions 21–22). Shachi-devi undertakes Vishvambhara's second marriage; through Kashinatha Mishra a match is arranged with the daughter of Sanatana Pandita; the customs and rites of the wedding; a splendid wedding, and the Mishra sends his daughter to his son-in-law's house.

**Chapter 7. The Journey to Gaya** (portion 23). Having completed the lila of study — the lila of teaching; on the pretext of offering pinda for His father — departure for Gaya; various pastimes on the way; under the pretext of fever — drinking water that has washed a brahmana's feet; [the teaching that] a man who does not worship Krishna is unfit to be a brahmana; the arrival in Gaya and darshana of the Vishnupada; the meeting with Ishvara Puri, the best of devotees, and the lila of receiving the mantra; having received the mantra, [the Lord] is intoxicated with prema for Krishna; at the sight of the Vishnupada — the rapture of prema and so on; then the return home.

## Madhya-khanda

**Chapter 1** (portions 24–25). The good fortune of [Mahaprabhu's] students [in His lila of teaching] is praised; [the Lord's] mercy to mother Shachi; the lila of the great manifestation of prema in the house of Shuklambara Brahmachari; the Lord's eight sattvika-bhavas during kirtana of Krishna; the Gaura-avatara of Sri Krishna, who accepted the bhava of a devotee, is the crown of all avataras; when the Lord performs the lila of preaching prema, the devotees headed by Gadadhara and devotees come from many regions and foreign lands gather together; by the Lord's mercy all go mad with prema; the Lord, maddened at the sound of Krishna's flute, hears a voice from heaven; in Murari's house [the Lord] shows the form of Varaha; Murari's hymn; Murari is told to worship the son of Nanda; at Murari's prayer the Lord shows the form of Sri Rama and glorifies the greatness of Krishna's name; the gods obtain prema; the Lord's mercy to Shuklambara, who sings the kirtana "O Radha, O Govinda"; the Lord gives Gadadhara the garland from His body; description of the beauty of Gaura and Gadadhara, [the inseparable] pair.

**Chapter 2** (portions 25–26). The author describes the beauty and charm of Mahaprabhu; the Lord plants a mango stone and distributes ripe fruit to the devotees; when [the tree] vanishes, [the Lord] shows the illusoriness of samsara and speaks of the way to conquer maya; to Mukunda Datta — instruction on the superiority of worshipping Sri Krishna and on serving Bhagavan, giving up speculation on the Self (*adhyatma*); blessing of Murari; the Lord delights in kirtana in Shrivasa's house; when a foolish brahmana calls the form of Sri Krishna a product of maya, the Lord bathes in the Ganga in His clothes.

**Chapter 3** (portions 26–27). With kirtana the Lord goes to Advaita's house; the pre-eminence of bhakti alone in the age of Kali is glorified; a certain brahmana is bewildered; the Lord delights in kirtana in Advaita's house; returning home, the Lord expounds [the doctrine of] the Self by an example and glorifies the greatness of supreme bhakti; in Shrivasa's house the Lord honours the club so as to destroy obstacles[^19]; Advaita Prabhu comes, [learning] of [the Lord's manifestation]; the greatness of Advaita is glorified; the Lord sits on the couch, and Advaita dances[^20]; the truth about Advaita is expounded, with instruction to serve Bhagavan.

**Chapter 4** (portion 28). Mahaprabhu explains [the meaning of the name] Shrivasa; Murari [recites the *Raghuvira-ashtaka*], the Lord writes "Ramadasa" on his forehead and shows him the form of Rama; Sri Rama Pandita is told to serve his brother; devotees are sent to look for Nityananda Prabhu; the meeting with Nityananda in the house of Nandana Acharya; before everyone, the greatness of Nityananda is glorified and the way to attain prema for Krishna is described; Nityananda Prabhu is shown the six-armed, four-armed and two-armed forms.

**Chapter 5** (portion 29). The Lord weeps at night in the third watch and tells Shachi-devi His dream; Nityananda Prabhu stays two days with Advaita[^21]; Murari describes Nityananda Prabhu's manifestations of prema; Advaita worships Mahaprabhu in Shrivasa's house; the meeting with Haridasa; Nityananda Prabhu takes leave of Mahaprabhu; the Lord distributes Nityananda Prabhu's kaupina, and the devotees tie it on their heads and dance; when Mahaprabhu disappears, the devotees suffer separation, and when He returns, their bliss increases.

**Chapter 6** (portions 30–31). Gaurahari plays with the devotees in the bliss of prema; [the devotees] accept the water from Nityananda Prabhu's feet; the meeting with Haridasa; Advaita Prabhu arrives; Advaita Prabhu is told to preach prema without distinguishing the worthy and the unworthy; the devotees are told [to go] from door to door distributing the name and prema; [when] the devotees mention the great sinners Jagai and Madhai, Mahaprabhu glorifies the greatness of the name, [which saves even] by namabhasa, and goes round the town with the devotees in kirtana; the deliverance of Jagai and Madhai; the author glorifies the mercy of Gaura and Nityananda.

**Chapter 7** (portions 31–32). The Lord's merciful glance on the brahmana Vanamali from East Bengal and his son; seeing [the Lord] in the form of Shyamasundara, they praise Him and call Him a "new Creator"; the Lord's bhava of Nrisimha in Shrivasa's house; mercy to a worshipper of Shiva; when a brahmana woman touches the Lord's feet, He throws Himself into the Ganga; the Lord's instruction to serve Hari; mercy to Mukunda; Mukunda's hymn; the Lord manifests His form as Bhagavan; Shrivasa performs [His] abhisheka; the author sings Gaura's qualities and instructs all to serve Gaura.

**Chapter 8** (portions 32–33)[^22]. [The Lord] makes a brahmana stricken with leprosy beg forgiveness of Shrivasa and frees him from offence to a Vaishnava; when [the Lord] dances, the devotees do not admit a certain brahmana [to Him], and he curses Mahaprabhu: "May Your worldly happiness perish!"; the brahmana's hymn; the Lord consoles the brahmana; the Lord in the bhava of Balarama cries, "Give Me honey!"; He goes with the devotees to Advaita's house; in the bhava of Baladeva He faints; at Gadadhara's coming He restrains the bhava; the devotees headed by Acharyaratna arrive; all see [the Lord] in the form of Baladeva; bathing in the Ganga with the devotees.

**Chapter 9** (portions 33–34). The Lord in the bhava of Varaha; the devotees headed by Advaita Acharya are told [to perform] sankirtana and to preach it; in the bhava of a gopi He sings of the qualities of the gopis; [the Lord] goes to Chandrashekhara's house; Shrivasa in the bhava of Narada; the greatness of Gadadhara is glorified: [the Lord] reveals that Gadadhara is Radhika; Thakura Haridasa arrives; the bliss of sankirtana; the Lord in the bhava of His majesty; in the bhava of Lakshmi He gives away the prema of service and at last is filled with the bhava of Ishvara.

**Chapter 10** (portions 35–36). The Lord, having told Shrivasa of the dharmas of the four yugas, reveals the pre-eminence of sankirtana; in the bhava of Radha — agitation: "Where is Vrindavana? Where is Lalita?"; at Murari's words [the Lord] calms down and delights in kirtana; He tells mother Shachi that in a dream He received the mantra of sannyasa; the arrival of Keshava Bharati; the Lord's separation from Krishna [becomes] unbearable; at the thought that the Lord will take sannyasa the devotees lament bitterly, and the Lord consoles them.

**Chapter 11** (portions 36–37). Hearing that the Lord will take sannyasa, mother Shachi grieves and asks [Him] to keep the dharma [of a householder]; the Lord, telling the story of Dhruva, instructs her to serve Krishna; having consoled His mother in various ways, He shows her the form of Sri Krishna and dispels her grief.

**Chapter 12** (portion 38)[^23]. The grief of Vishnupriya-devi; the Lord consoles her with many sweet words and, having instructed her in the truth, shows the four-armed form of Narayana; Shrivasa, Murari and other devotees arrive; the Lord consoles them.

**Chapter 13** (portions 39–40). Having consoled the devotees with instruction in the truth, [the Lord,] in order to take sannyasa, crosses the Ganga and comes to Kantakanagara, to Keshava Bharati; Nityananda Prabhu consoles mother Shachi and Vishnupriya; with Chandrashekhara Acharya, Damodara Pandita and other devotees Nityananda Prabhu comes to Kantakanagara; [the Lord] asks Bharati for the mantra of sannyasa; Bharati refuses, and then, [knowing the Lord's heart], agrees to give the mantra; the Lord speaks the mantra of sannyasa into Bharati's ear, and Bharati gives [Him] the mantra; the grief of the villagers at the Lord's sannyasa; the Lord consoles them and, [behaving] as a devotee, asks [for the blessing of] devotion to Krishna; the Lord's sannyasa name is Sri Krishna Chaitanya; having taken sannyasa, He wanders through Rarh intoxicated with prema.

**Chapter 14** (portion 41). When Chandrashekhara Acharya returns from Kantakanagara to Nadia — the grief and lament of mother Shachi and Vishnupriya; Nityananda Prabhu announces Mahaprabhu's coming to Shantipura; conversation of mother Shachi with Nityananda Prabhu; the people of Nadia come to Advaita's house to see the Lord; Mahaprabhu speaks fittingly with everyone.

**Chapter 15** (portions 42–43). The Lord instructs the devotees to benefit all living beings by the sankirtana of Hari's name; as He prepares to go to Nilachala, Thakura Haridasa speaks humbly with the Lord; when the devotees follow, [the Lord] consoles them with sweet words and sets out for Nilachala reciting the verse "Rama Raghava, Rama Raghava, protect me" and others; on the way Nityananda Prabhu breaks the staff, and the Lord shows a lila of anger.

**Chapter 16** (portions 44–45). On the way to Nilachala — through Tamoluk; bathing in the Brahma-kunda; having seen Madhusudana, [the Lord] comes to Remuna and dances and sings before Gopaladeva; having bathed in the Vaitarani, He sees Lord Varaha; He comes to Yajapura and, having seen the lingas of Shiva, sees Viraja; from there — bathing in the Brahma-kunda, Nabhi-gaya and the city of Shiva; a merciful glance on the toll-collector, and the form of the One who lies on the Milk Ocean shown [to him] in a dream; arrival at Ekamra-kanana, hymns to Shiva and a discussion of whether to accept Shiva's prasada; via Kapoteshvara to the river Bhargavi and bathing in it; [at the sight of] the temple of Sri Jagannatha — swoon; [the Lord is carried] to the house of Vasudeva Sarvabhauma and [brought to His senses]; standing behind the Garuda pillar, the Lord sees Jagannatha; discussion with Sarvabhauma Pandita on various subjects and the showing [to him] of the six-armed form.

## Shesha-khanda

**Chapter 1** (portions 46–47). Kirtana with Sarvabhauma in Puri; [the Lord] sets out on the road to Setubandha; He comes to Kurma-kshetra and delivers Vasudeva; He comes to Jiyada-Nrisimha — an ancient legend is told; meeting with Ramananda in the city of Kanchi[^25] and the showing of the form of Rasaraja-Mahabhava; through Panchavati to Shrirangam; mercy to Trimalla Bhatta; there [the Lord] spends the chaturmasya; meeting with Paramananda Puri; Puri praises Bhagavan Gaura.

**Chapter 2** (portions 47–50). On the way to Setubandha — the deliverance of the seven palms and the ancient story of the seven palms; at Setubandha, intoxicated with prema, [the Lord] utters the names of Rama, Lakshmana, Sita, Hanuman and others; from the Godavari — return to Alalanatha; [the Lord] accepts Vishnudasa as His own; He comes again to Purushottama; sets out to see Mathura-mandala; meeting with Rupa and Sanatana; with Krishnadasa He sees both banks of the Yamuna, the twelve forests and other places of Krishna's lila.

**Chapter 3** (portions 51–52). Krishnadasa humbly entreats at the Lord's feet; the Lord takes the road to Nilachala; on the way He drinks buttermilk from a cowherd, and the cowherd obtains the Lord's mercy; the Lord comes to Gauda, through Rarh to Kuliya; people come from Navadvipa to see the Lord; Shachi sees her son and weeps piteously; at His mother's wish the Lord comes to Navadvipa and enlightens His mother to serve Krishna; the Lord comes to Shantipura, to Advaita's house, and there sings the name; the Lord leaves Shantipura and goes via Tamoluk to Nilachala; darshana of Jagannatha and kirtana day and night; King Prataparudra obtains the Lord's mercy and sees the six-armed form; a poor brahmana from Dravida, tormented by poverty, comes to Nilachala, fasts for seven days, meets Vibhishana and at last obtains the Lord's mercy[^24].
