# Sutra-khanda, part 4 (portion 04)

*Narada parts from Uddhava and comes to Kailasa; Narada's account of the mahaprasada: Uddhava's words on the remnants of the Lord's meal, Narada's service to Lakshmi, the dance of Shiva after tasting mahaprasada, the Earth's complaint; Parvati's vow to give mahaprasada to all living beings; the Lord of Vaikuntha approves her vow and promises to appear in the Kali-yuga as Gaura*

**154.** You have refreshed my very body with your words"; —
and Narada went on, rejoicing, playing his vina.

**155.** The dialogue of Narada and Uddhava from the *Jaimini-bharata* —
hearing it, Lochana Dasa is mad with joy.

**156.** Whoever does not believe my words,
let him examine the book — the thirty-second chapter.[^1]

*Raga bhatiyari; disha: "Ah, my life — Gorachand!"*

**157.** The sage Narada goes on, and his vina sings [His] virtues;
hearing it, his heart overwhelmed, he falls again and again.

**158.** Now he weeps, now laughs aloud,
now trembles, now stammers in half-words;

**159.** now he roars and slaps his arms like a wrestler;[^2]
crying "Gora, Gora!" he weeps, his heart restless.

**160.** He cannot forget Gora's sweetest prema;
his body blazes with radiance like the sun.

**161.** For prema he cannot walk, his heart exults —
and in the twinkling of an eye he was at Shiva's Kailasa.

**162.** "I shall see Mahesha!" — and his joy grew:
"I shall tell him of Krishna, all in due order.

**163.** There is no such joyful news in the three worlds:
the treasure of Vrindavana [the Lord] will reveal in the Kali-yuga;

**164.** the prema for which Shiva, Brahma and Ananta pray
the fallen and unruly [people] of Kali will enjoy.

**165.** I shall tell Mahesha this wondrous news —
hearing it, the lord will be greatly pleased.

**166.** By Katyayani's grace I shall take the dust of her feet —
by the grace of [those] feet I utter Hari's name."[^3]

**167.** Musing thus, he came to Mahesha's gate;
seeing him, Nandi and Mahakala rose respectfully.[^4]

**168.** Bowing, Nandi went inside,
where Parvati and Mahesha were in their inner apartments,

**169.** and announced that Narada was at the gate;
both came out at once with joyful hearts.

**170.** Seeing Narada, the lord greets him with a smile;
the sage, a wise devotee, fell at his feet.

**171.** Mahesha, like no other, knows the greatness of Vaishnavas:
showing his love, he honours Narada.

**172.** Embracing him tightly, he seated him beside himself;
the sage, falling at the goddess's feet, greets her.

**173.** With a mother's tenderness Katyayani asks Narada:
"Tell me of your welfare, dear great sage.

**174.** You know the truth of the fourteen worlds;
from where have you come today — to our good fortune?"

**175.** Narada says: "Hear wondrous news;
you are mother and father, [come] for the deliverance of the world.

**176.** You have forgotten a former secret;
clasping your feet, I shall now remind you of it.

**177.** I shall tell you everything from beginning to end;
having heard it, you yourself will show me grace.

**178.** Once Uddhava asked the Lord:
'When You withdraw from sight, will the earth remain?

**179.** Will devotees remain on this earth?'
Hearing this, the Thakura explained to him the yoga of what He Himself is:[^5]

**180.** 'I am water, I am land, I am earth and trees,
I am gods and gandharvas, I am yakshas and rakshasas;

**181.** I am origin and dissolution, the life of all beings;
I pervade all — how can I vanish?'[^6]

**182.** Hearing such words of the Thakura, Uddhava,
striking his breast with his hand, speaks his own realization:

**183.** 'You are the all-pervading Lord, this I know;
but greater than You [Yourself] are Your two feet.

**184.** Whoever is caught in the net of the moonlight of Your toenails —
what more can I say? — his glory cannot be put into words.'

*So in the* Srimad-Bhagavata *(11.6.46):*

**185.** "Adorned with the garlands, scents, garments and ornaments that You have enjoyed,
we, Your servants who eat Your remnants, shall surely conquer Your maya."[^7]

**186.** 'My strength is this: I, Hari's servant, eat the remnants;
I conquer Your maya — all my hope is in Your remnants.'

**187.** Hearing such a conversation of the Thakura and Uddhava,
I felt pain in my heart:

**188.** for so long I have known the path [of bhakti],
yet until today I did not know for certain [the power of] such remnants.

**189.** By the strength of the remnants Hari's servant gains strength —
that is why in the Lord's presence he praised them so.[^8]

**190.** Such mahaprasada I have never tasted —
I realized in my heart that the Lord had deprived me.

**191.** How might I taste this mahaprasada?
By what means could I win fate's favour?

**192.** Absorbed in this thought, I went to Vaikuntha
and served Lakshmi-devi in every way.

**193.** The goddess, pleased, said graciously:
'Ask a boon — I shall give it,' and she made a vow.

**194.** Hearing the vow, my heart took hope,
and I had her confirm those kind words once more.

**195.** With a pleading voice and folded palms I said:
'For long there has been a great pain in my heart.

**196.** Everyone knows that Narada is your servant,
yet he has not tasted the remnants of the Great Lord's meal.[^9]

**197.** Give me a handful of the Lord's prasada —
this is the boon I ask; look on me with kindness.'

**198.** Hearing this, Lakshmi-devi, astonished,
began to speak humbly:

**199.** 'There is no order of the Lord to give His remnants to anyone;
how can I, O sage, transgress His order and give you the remnants?

**200.** Wait a little for my sake:
in time I may be able to give, having saved [some].'

**201.** So sweetly the queen spoke;
I answered, 'Good, good,' understanding the matter myself.

**202.** Some days passed, and one day the Lord, in a tender mood,
took the goddess by the hand and seated her beside Him.

**203.** Smiling, He talks lovingly with her;
the goddess, asking leave to speak, with fear at heart,

**204.** bowed and said: 'I have a petition;
my heart is afraid — I am in distress and abashed.

**205.** Remove my trouble, Lord, protect Your maidservant;
clasping Your feet I beg: hear me, treasury of virtues.'

**206.** Lakshmi speaks piteously to the Lord, in fear;
He looks at Sudarshana with a surprised smile.[^10]

**207.** The disc Sudarshana trembles and says pleadingly:
'Lord, I know nothing of Lakshmi's trouble.'

**208.** Lakshmi says: 'Sudarshana is not to blame:
because of Narada's words my heart has withered.

**209.** For twelve years he served me without revealing [his aim];
pleased, I made a vow —

**210.** "Ask a boon, I shall give it," I said: "Truly, truly";
and the sage made that word fast for ever.

**211.** The boon he asked is Your remnants;
but what power have I to transgress Your order?

**212.** So I have told You — calamity is upon me;
protect Your maidservant, Lord, remove my trouble.'

**213.** Understanding all, He said: 'Listen, Lakshmi:
a very grave matter is what you have told.

**214.** Give it to him in secret — so that I do not know.'
Hearing the Lord's words of leave, she was glad.

**215.** After some days that Mother of the world
called me and gave me mahaprasada.

**216.** By Lakshmi's grace I obtained mahaprasada
and, my desire fulfilled, I ate the mahaprasada.

**217.** A light surpassing millions of moons, the beauty of millions of Kamadevas,
the splendour of millions of suns — so wondrous did I become.

**218.** At the touch of mahaprasada my splendour grew a hundredfold;
playing my vina, happy, I came to Kailasa.

**219.** Seeing me, lord Mahesha
said with a smile: 'What a wondrous look you have today!

**220.** Your splendour is so wondrous that it amazes the eye;
why do you have such a form today? Tell me truly.'

**221.** I told him everything from first to last;
having heard it, Mahesha reproached me:

**222.** 'Having got such rare mahaprasada,
you, O sage, ate it alone without giving me any!

**223.** You have come again with love to see me —
why did you not bring so rare a treasure?'

**224.** Hearing Mahesha's words, ashamed,
[I] look down with lowered eyes, rubbing nail against nail.

**225.** 'There is a particle of mahaprasada,' I said, and gladly gave it;
without a second thought the lord put it into his mouth.

**226.** In bliss the great lord Mahesha dances —
beneath his feet the earth shudders.

**227.** Under the weight of prema Mount Sumeru sways,
the earth trembles, alarm is everywhere.

**228.** The lord of yoga trembles in prema, forgetting himself;
under Mahesha's weight the earth sinks to Rasatala.

**229.** Ananta's hoods press against the Tortoise's back;
bending his neck, Kurma stares fixedly [upward].[^11]

**230.** Under the weight all the bearers of the quarters bend their necks;[^12]
at [Shiva's] roar the shell of the universe cracks.

**231.** Unable to bear Mahesha's weight, the goddess [Earth]
hurried to Mahesha's abode.

**232.** With joined palms the Earth says to Katyayani:
'Under the weight of Mahesha's dance I am giving up my life.

**233.** Help me, if creation is to be saved:
I see calamity has fallen upon the whole world.'

**234.** Hearing the Earth's piteous words, Parvati
went quickly to where Pashupati was.

**235.** The king of gods dances fully absorbed in rasa;
with harsh words she breaks Mahesha's rapture.

**236.** The lord came to his senses, grieved,
and, seeing Parvati, speaks to her with a hardened heart:

**237.** 'What have you done, what have you done, goddess — what an outrage!
To break my rapture is the same as killing me.

**238.** I have no enemy in the three worlds but you:
why did you end such bliss of mine?'

**239.** Hearing this, the goddess says piteously again:
'Look, lord, at the Earth before you.

**240.** Under the stamping of your feet she is sinking to Rasatala,
creation is perishing — that is why I spoke harshly.

**241.** I have offended — forgive my fault, great one.'
Smiling, Mahesha dismissed the Earth.

**242.** And again the goddess asks entreatingly:
'Lord, I ask one thing — a doubt troubles me:

**243.** every day you dance absorbed in the rasa of Krishna —
why then does the earth sink to Rasatala today?

**244.** The splendour of millions of suns, fierce rays —
the universe cannot hold so wondrous a radiance.

**245.** Why this wondrous, endless bliss today?
Tell me in detail, lord, rich in virtues.'

**246.** Mahesha says: 'Hear a joyful tale:
the great sage gave me the Lord's prasada.

**247.** Rare in the three worlds is [a taste of] what is offered to Vishnu,
still more the nectar of His lips, unknown to the Vedas.[^13]

**248.** Such mahaprasada I have eaten —
my birth is fruitful, this is an auspicious hour.'

**249.** By Narada's grace — the touch of mahaprasada:
he told her this auspicious story, a delightful treasure.

**250.** Hearing the lord's words, Mahamaya says:[^14]
'Now at last I know how great your kindness is!

**251.** You hold me as half your body — it is all deceit;
now your false love is exposed!

**252.** Having got such rare mahaprasada,
you, god, ate it alone without giving me any!'

**253.** Helpless with shame, the Trident-bearer says:
'This treasure is not for you, Bhavani.'

**254.** Hearing this, angered, the primal Shakti says:
'My name is Vaishnavi; I serve Vishnu.

**255.** I make a vow before all —
and you will know whether there is kindness for me in the Lord's heart:

**256.** this mahaprasada I shall give to the whole world —
by my vow even jackals and dogs will receive it.'[^15]

**257.** When Katyayani made such a vow,
the Lord of Vaikuntha, hearing it, came quickly.

**258.** Rising respectfully, the goddess bowed
and with tearful eyes made her plea to Him;

**259.** with an aching heart, sighing, she speaks —
and with a joyful heart this Lochana Dasa tells it.

*Raga vibhasha*

**260.** Softly the Lord says: 'Do not be agitated, goddess;
what is this conduct of yours?
The whole world is blind in the bonds of your maya —
that is why My creation goes on.

**261.** You are My primal shakti, you know bhakti,
you are by nature My prakriti;
I am not apart from you, nor, I say, are you apart from Me;[^16]
whatever you do is your grace.

**262.** By worshipping Hara and Gauri all people come to know Me:
Hara and Gauri are My own body;
when your heart is pleased, all maya vanishes,
the division into 'mine' and 'another's' vanishes.[^17]

**263.** Such is your vow — these remnants of Mine
you will give to all without hindrance;
by the mere fragrance of mahaprasada all will be freed from bonds;
it will do away with restrictive rules.

**264.** Hearing the Lord's words, Katyayani says again:
'If there is kindness for me in Your heart,
surely give [Your] remnants: let all beings eat them,
let the three worlds receive them without hindrance.'

**265.** Again the Jewel of virtues says: 'Hear, goddess Katyayani:
I shall keep your vow — there is a story about it;
it is an ancient secret; I tell it to you privately —
and the anxiety of the fever of worldly life will go.

**266.** No one knows the truth of this ancient secret:
when the gods churned the ocean —
Mandara the churning rod, the serpent Ananta the rope —
from the friction a shoot arose.[^18]

**267.** That is My wish-fulfilling tree: let suppliants ask
whatever each holds in mind;
whoever wants whatever treasure receives that treasure —
it disappoints no one in his hope.

**268.** There, in a divine radiance, within that beautiful tree,
Sri Chaitanya is present in bodily form;[^19]
that is My natural form, a king of pure compassion —
all others are not equal to Him.

**269.** All the avataras — He is their abode and shelter,[^20]
[from Him they come] for the sake of pastimes, arts and delights.
I shall remain on earth, Lord and master of the three worlds,
and shall spread mercy.

**270.** Especially in the Kali-yuga, revealing sankirtana,
I shall take a human form;
My body will be golden; I shall keep your vow
and spread the highest love.

**271.** This secret of My heart I have told you —
keep it guarded in your mind:
the essence of all avataras, Gora, the avatara of Kali —
by My virtues I shall deliver the people.'"

**272.** The dialogue of Vishnu with Katyayani is in the *Brahma-purana*,
set forth in the *Utkala-khanda*;
King Prataparudra, an ocean of all virtues,
made it widely known.[^21]

**273.** "Why do you not remember this?" —
laughing, says the king of sages. —
"The Lord ordered me to proclaim
the work of the avatara of the Kali-yuga:

**274.** when the Kali-yuga comes, all of you will be born on earth
as your own portions, under other names;[^22]
all those lords of the worlds, with all the associates,
will be born in brahmana families."

**275.** Hearing Narada's words, the Trident-bearer rejoiced,
the goddess Katyayani rejoiced;
the abode filled with joy, all say "Hari, Hari!" —
a joyful clamour arose.

[^1]: A reference to the source of the dialogue, the *Jaimini-bharata* (v. 155). Which text by this name Lochana Dasa has in mind is unclear.
[^2]: *Malasat*: slapping the shoulders and thighs with the palms, as wrestlers do to challenge an opponent; in the Gaudiya biographies one of the signs of ecstatic rapture.
[^3]: Narada humbly names the grace of Katyayani (Parvati) and Shiva as the source of his devotion. For Gaudiya Vaishnavas Shiva is the best of Vaishnavas (*Bhag.* 12.13.16); so Bhaktisiddhanta also calls him in his summary of this section.
[^4]: Nandi and Mahakala are Shiva's servants and doorkeepers.
[^5]: The conversation of Uddhava with the Lord before His departure is in the *Bhagavata*, canto 11 (11.6.43–50 and the following *Uddhava-gita*, where the Lord explains yoga). Literally "explained yoga concerning His own doings".
[^6]: Cf. the *Bhagavad-gita*, chs. 7 and 10. The Lord pervades everything by His energies and dwells in all as the Paramatma, while remaining a person distinct from creation and inconceivably one with it (*achintya-bhedabheda*); there is no identification of God with the world here.
[^7]: In Bhaktisiddhanta's edition every quoted shloka is followed by his prose order of the words (*anvaya*) and a Bengali translation; on this page they are almost illegible. "Your maya" is the external energy of the Lord, which holds living beings captive; the Lord Himself is not subject to it.
[^8]: "Hari's servant" (*hari-dasa*) here is Uddhava.
[^9]: "The Great Lord" (*mahaprabhu*) here is Narayana, the Lord of Vaikuntha, not Sri Chaitanya.
[^10]: Sudarshana is the Lord's disc, the guardian of His apartments; the Lord wonders whether he has offended Lakshmi.
[^11]: The earth is upheld beneath by the serpent Ananta (Shesha) on his hoods, and beneath him by the Tortoise (Kurma).
[^12]: "The bearers of the quarters" (*digvaha*): the *diggajas*, the elephants said to support the world in the eight directions.
[^13]: "The nectar of His lips" (*adharamrita*): the remnants of food touched by the Lord's lips; this is mahaprasada in the proper sense.
[^14]: Mahamaya is a name of Parvati (Durga). For Gaudiya Vaishnavas Durga is the external energy of the Lord, carrying out His will as a shadow follows the movements of the body (*Brahma-samhita* 5.44); below the Lord Himself calls her His shakti (vv. 260–261).
[^15]: Cf. the tradition of Puri: everything offered to Jagannatha is then offered to Vimala-devi (Parvati) in Jagannatha's temple, and only then becomes the mahaprasada that is given to all without distinction.
[^16]: An energy is inseparable from its possessor (*shakti* and *shaktiman*). In the Gaudiya understanding Durga is the external energy of the Lord (*bahiranga-shakti*, maya), the shadow of His internal energy (*chit-shakti*) to which Radha and the eternal consorts belong; the Lord Himself is not subject to maya — she serves His will, keeping captive those who have turned from Him.
[^17]: So Lochana Dasa: "Hara and Gauri are My own body." According to Gaudiya Vaishnava teaching Shiva is not different from the Lord in source, but, in contact with tamo-guna, becomes distinct, as milk turned into curd (*Brahma-samhita* 5.45); Durga is the Lord's external energy (5.44). "The division into mine and another's" is the false "I" and "mine" (cf. v. 35) created by maya; there is no question of the difference between the soul and God disappearing.
[^18]: Literally "a hair" (*loma*); from the following verses, the shoot from which the wish-fulfilling tree (*kalpa-taru*) grew (cf. the Parijata, which came out of the churned ocean).
[^19]: Lochana Dasa probably connects this tree with the Kalpa-vata, the sacred banyan by Jagannatha's temple in Puri, honoured as a wish-fulfilling tree; the whole dialogue (v. 272) comes from the traditions of Utkala (Orissa) and Purushottama-kshetra.
[^20]: Cf. *Chaitanya-charitamrita*, Adi 2: Krishna (and Gaura as Krishna) is the *avatari*, the source of all avataras.
[^21]: The *Utkala-khanda* is the account of Purushottama-kshetra (Puri) known as part of the *Skanda-purana*; a Purushottama account is also found in the *Brahma-purana*. Prataparudra was the king of Orissa (reigned c. 1497–1540), a devotee of Sri Chaitanya. What exactly Prataparudra did to spread this account is not clear from the text.
[^22]: Cf. Kavi Karnapura's *Gaura-ganoddesha-dipika*, which states which eternal associates and gods appeared as whom in Gaura-lila. The words "in brahmana families" are the author's generalization: not all the Lord's associates were brahmanas by birth (Murari Gupta, for example, was a vaidya, and Haridasa Thakura was born in a Muslim family).
