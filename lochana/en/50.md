# Shesha-khanda, part 5 (portion 50)

*The section "The Lord Sees Vrindavana" (conclusion): Krishnadasa shows the Lord the Khadira-vana, Kumuda-vana, Ambika-vana and Nandishvara and tells of Akrura, the killing of Kamsa and Krishna's coming to Mathura; the Lord blesses Krishnadasa; the residents of the Mathura-mandala see Krishna in Him; the Lord sets out for Nilachala*

*Raga vibhasa; disha: "O Hari! Just once, only once, have mercy, Goraraya!"*

**316.** "See — here within is the Khadira-vana:
[by this way] Radha went to sell curd and milk.

**317.** Here Krishna plotted with the boys:
'Give her a fright — let Radha have a scare.'

**318.** Hiding in the forest, the boys raise a fearful noise;
terrified, Radha clings tightly to Krishna.

**319.** Embracing Radha, Krishna says, 'Ah, ah!' —
kisses her and soothes her with loving words.

**320.** Receiving Krishna's love, Radhika is rapt:
in love's languor Radha forgot her home.

**321.** Here in the bower are delightful pastimes:
enchanted by prema, both [danced] the great rasa.

**322.** Here [Krishna] received the name Madana-Gopala"[^1].
Hearing this, Gora joyfully says: "Good, good!"

**323.** "See — in Kumuda-vana the deeds of Krishna:
here He played games with the boys.

**324.** Shridama and Subala, the two chief ones in the pasture —
in the boys' game against each other a quarrel arose then;

**325.** that is why this place is called Kondaliya [— 'the place of the quarrel'].
I have told of the pastimes in the forest named Kumuda.

**326.** See — the forest of Ambika on the bank of the Sarasvati:
here the cowherd men and women worship Hara and Gauri.

**327.** For mocking the sons of Angira
the vidyadhara Sudarshana was in a serpent's body;

**328.** to end the curse he swallowed Nanda —
[and] disgorged Nanda, touching Krishna's feet.

**329.** [Here is] the death of Shankhachuda, Kuvera's servant:
with a blow of the fist on his head [Krishna] took [his] jewel.

**330.** Seizing the bull Arishta by the horns and legs,
[Krishna] dashed him down in the pasture and killed him — blood gushed from his mouth.

**331.** At Narada's words Kamsa grew troubled
[and] bound Vasudeva and Devaki in fetters.

**332.** Keshi, Kamsa's follower, takes the form of a horse —
mighty, black: the sight of him strikes fear.

**333.** Thrusting His arm into his mouth [and] stopping his breath,
here Gopinatha slew Keshi.

**334.** An asura in the form of a ram steals the boys
and, closing it with a stone, keeps them in a mountain cave.

**335.** Killing Vyoma by dashing him down, [Krishna] brought back the boys
and, rid of the villain, plays joyfully[^2].

**336.** Then Nanda's house was at Nandishvara;
see — west of here is also the Kamyaka-vana.

**337.** See — the sliding stone: these cowherd boys
slide down it here in play morning and evening[^3].

**338.** North of Nandishvara is the Pavana-sarovara;
see — all around are posts for tying the calves.

**339.** In Mathura Kamsa gave his order to Akrura —
and he entered [this] village at dusk[^4].

**340.** All the thoughts he had on the way here
were fulfilled when he saw the marks of the lotus feet.

**341.** In this pasture, seeing both Rama and Krishna,
he prostrated on the ground, falling at Their feet.

**342.** They took him home with honour,
and at night he told all of Kamsa's design.

**343.** In the morning Nanda proclaimed to all —
the word went round: 'Let us go to Kamsa with tribute.'

**344.** Here Rama and Krishna mounted the chariot
and go to the king together with Akrura.

**345.** Here the gopis are dying of weeping:
in separation from Krishna they weep, flinging themselves down.

**346.** Fallen on the ground they weep, their hair dishevelled,
clothes and ornaments all in disarray — such became their attire.

**347.** Their weeping cannot be told in words:
like a lifeless body, their hands and feet lie still.

**348.** Through a messenger Krishna Himself consoles them:
'I shall come within a few days[^5].

**349.** All of you are as dear to Me as life —
that a body should live without its life cannot be.

**350.** When I have destroyed the wicked, I shall come soon;
do not grieve; know all this to be true.'

**351.** Here all the cowherds mounted the carts
and all rested at the ghata of the Manasa-Ganga.

**352.** They reached the Yamuna ghata at the middle of the third prahara;
all the cowherds bathed and ate fruit.

**353.** When Akrura bathed, [Krishna] showed [him His] glory;
in the afternoon Nanda and the others go ahead, and Krishna behind.

**354.** Here all the people cried out in dismay:
'He is no match for a wrestler — he is a mere boy!

**355.** Kamsa acts unfittingly, acts wrongly!'
As each one's heart was, so he saw Krishna[^6].

**356.** Kamsa was startled, in deep confusion:
in Krishna and Balarama he sees Death embodied.

**357.** The wrestlers see Him as if made of thunderbolt,
the yogis see Him as the complete Bhagavan,

**358.** the Yadus see Him as the deity of their clan,
the ignorant as the universal form of the Creator,

**359.** the cowherds see Him as their own kin,
the women see Kandarpa embodied.

**360.** When the two brothers stood in the arena,
each one saw there according to his own perception.

**361.** The two brothers fight with Chanura and Mushtika;
seeing it, the king starts again and again.

**362.** Krishna slew Chanura — the menace was ended;
Rama slew Mushtika — a crash like thunder.

**363.** And again with His fists He slays countless wrestlers;
the wrestler named Shala Krishna slew by dashing him down.

**364.** With a kick He broke one platform;
before Krishna's might the wrestlers flee in all directions.

**365.** Seeing all this, Kamsa quickly orders:
'Seize Rama and Krishna and throw them out!

**366.** Put all the cowherds headed by Nanda in fetters,
kill Ugrasena, Vasudeva and Devaki!'

**367.** At that moment Krishnachandra, seeing the time had come,
with great daring leapt up onto the platform in one bound.

**368.** As Kamsa was hastily seizing his sword,
Krishna with a roar seized him by the hair.

**369.** Seizing him by the hair He threw him from the platform to the ground,
and the Vishvarupa — [the Lord whose form is the universe] — mounted his chest west of the platform.

**370.** Under the weight of the Vishvarupa Kamsa gave up his life.
Blessed is King Kamsa — Krishna is on his chest![^7]

**371.** He slew Kamsa — the people cry: 'Victory, victory!' —
and all the gods joyfully rain down flowers [on Him].

**372.** Holding him by the hair, Krishna dragged him along,
lifted him and, dashing him down, threw him some distance away.

**373.** Kamsa's eight brothers, headed by Kanka,
maddened by grief for their brother, gathered all their strength:

**374.** seven come on to kill Rama and Krishna —
Balarama alone slew them with a mere frown.

**375.** Through this village [Krishna] dragged Kamsa;
that is why [the place] is called Kamsa-khali — listen attentively.

**376.** [Here] He rested from His fatigue — hence the Vishranti-ghata;
Balarama consoles the lamenting wives of Kamsa[^8].

**377.** Then He set free His own mother and father;
overwhelmed with joy, they kiss [Him].

**378.** He made Ugrasena king and sent Nanda away [home] —
this is beyond my power to tell.

**379.** Krishna's hardness of heart is dreadful even to hear of[^9]:
in telling it he dies [of grief], says this Lochana Dasa.

*Raga bhatiyali; disha: "Come, come — I will take Kanai and Balai in my arms!"*

**379+1.** Then the father Vasudeva and the mother Devaki —
with the joy of the love of these two the earth was filled[^10].

**379+2.** He gave his sons the sacred thread and teaches [them] the gayatri;
for some time [They] pass Their days in pastimes in Mathura.

**379+3.** The tales of Krishna are almost without end;
there is no holding back — and the book grows too long.

**380.** Akrura tried to take [Them] to his house;
[Krishna] told him: "I shall come on the way back"[^11].

**381.** While Krishna lingered, the cowherds, near Mathura,
halted their carts there on the bank of the Sarasvati.

**382.** Leaving the cowherds headed by Nanda here,
Akrura himself goes ahead to inform Kamsa.

**383.** Understanding that they would stay here for a while,
the two brothers went to see Mathura.

**384.** They saw a washerman named Durmukha;
seeing him, Krishna and Balarama ask for clothes.

**385.** The sinful Durmukha speaks harsh words —
and [Krishna] struck off his head with His fingertips.

**386.** Putting on those fine clothes, happy and joyful,
They arrived at the house of Sudama the garland-maker.

**387.** Sudama rose, bowed to Their feet,
placed fine garlands on Their necks and offers praise.

**388.** Accepting his worship, the two brothers went on
and saw there a hunchbacked woman bent in three places.

**389.** Seeing the bent woman, [Krishna] laughed within
and in jest said to her: "Come, come."

**390.** Kubja with honour took both to her house
and anointed Their bodies with fragrant aloe and sandal.

**391.** Greatly pleased, Kubja paid Them honour —
and by the touch of the holy hand Kubja gained a beautiful form.

**392.** Senseless with desire, Kubja looks at Kanu
and, casting off shame, speaks openly[^12].

**393.** With words of assurance Hari pleased her,
and the two brothers went on in the attire of dancers.

**394.** Then at the place of the bow sacrifice [Krishna] broke the bow;
all Kamsa's men rushed to kill [Them],

**395.** but, taking the broken bow in hand, [They] slew Kamsa's men
and at dusk went to where Nanda and the others [were].

**396.** That very night Kamsa had evil dreams;
he built this very high platform.

**397.** Here, south of it, are two more platforms
for Vasudeva and Devaki to sit on:

**398.** "Tomorrow Rama and Krishna will come here and die —
let them sit here and see their sons' death!"

**399.** All around, all the ministers and friends set up platforms,
well arranged, to watch the wrestling in every detail.

**400.** To the west that villain dug a well,
to kill the two brothers and throw them in it[^13].

**401.** Rising in the morning, King Kamsa sits on the platform:
"Bring all the cowherds — let them pay the royal dues!

**402.** Bring his two sons, Krishna and Balarama:
I have heard much of them; I will watch them fight."

**403.** At the king's order all the runners ran;
hearing the din of the contest, Rama and Krishna hasten [there].

**404.** They quickly went to the gate of the fort —
and at the gate of the fort stands an elephant the size of a mountain.

**405.** Seeing Rama and Krishna, it charges in fury to kill [Them];
angered, Krishna stood before it.

**406.** Seizing its trunk, in the struggle [He] climbs onto its neck,
kills the driver and wrenches the elephant's tusks.

**407.** Tearing out the tusks, He seizes it by the tail, whirls it round,
lifts it into the air and hurls it four yojanas away.

**408.** "The great elephant has fallen!" — King Kamsa hears;
his body began to tremble, fear in his heart.

**409.** Then Rama and Krishna went before the king;
all the cowherds tremble in fear, their hearts pounding.

**410.** Kamsa says to Chanura and Mushtika:
"I have a mind to watch a wrestling match."

**411.** Here took place the great combat of the wrestlers:
Krishna with Chanura, Balarama with Mushtika.

**412.** That Lord of Vrindavana [appeared] in the Kali age;
what He did then I shall tell — hear now[^14].

**413.** Gora circumambulated the Mathura-mandala;
the great Krishnadasa made everything known to Him.

**414.** He humbly entreats the Lord, falling at His feet:
"I am in great distress — do not go away, deceiving me!

**415.** You are that very Krishna — this I have known for certain;
show me grace, hear me, Goraraya!"

**416.** Hearing this, the Lord speaks:
"By your grace My mind has been purified.

**417.** I had a great desire to see Mathura —
by your grace I have seen the secret places.

**418.** As My heart has rejoiced,
[so] may Krishna be gracious to you, Krishnadasa!"

**419.** All the residents of the Mathura-mandala
all turned one way — to see Gaurachandra.

**420.** Whoever sees Him once cannot forget Him;
overwhelmed with prema, he cannot contain himself.

**421.** Children, old and young, women and men —
even the unlettered — say: "This is Krishna, this is Krishna!

**422.** At last Krishna has come to Mathura again
to see the secret places of His former [pastimes]."

**423.** Someone says: "Why does He stand thrice-bent?
If He is not Kanai, why does He call 'Radha!'?"[^15]

**424.** People stay with Him day and night and do not leave His side;
one by one the Lord looks at the trees of Vrindavana.

**425.** One by one the Thakura beholds all the places:
here, in the forests, all is filled with prema.

**426.** In the Mathura-mandala He is manifest in every house:
some see a Child, some the pastimes of a Youth.

**427.** Someone at home suddenly hears the sound of the flute;
for one, in her husband's embrace — the madness of Krishna-rasa[^16].

**428.** No one thinks Him a stranger — all say: "[He is] our own";
in the hearts of all the seed of prema sprouted.

**429.** When the Lord goes roaming in the forests,
the trees and creepers of that forest float in the flood of prema.

**430.** Cuckoos, bees, peacocks that roam the fields and pastures
come racing and stay near the Lord.

**431.** All, with upturned faces, look at the Lord's face;
equal tenderness for all, eyes full of prema.

**432.** All knew: He is a sannyasi only in disguise.
And Mahaprabhu, the dweller of Nilachala, set out on His way.

**433.** Here the account of the Mathura-mandala is concluded;
joyfully Lochana Dasa sings Gora's glories.

[^1]: Vv. 316–322 follow a Vraja tradition about the Khadira-vana. Madana-Gopala — "the Gopala [who conquers] Madana," the Deity of Krishna in Vrindavana (Madana-mohana). The love of Radha and Krishna is transcendental (aprakrita); cf. en/48, note 10.
[^2]: Vv. 323–335 follow the Bhagavata (10.34–10.37): the deliverance of the vidyadhara Sudarshana, who swallowed Nanda in Ambika-vana; the killing of Shankhachuda, Arishta, Keshi and Vyomasura; Narada reveals to Kamsa the secret of Krishna's birth. Cf. Murari 4.10.
[^3]: The "sliding stone" (phisalani-shila) is a holy place of Kamyavana; the Pavana-sarovara is a pond at Nandagrama (Nandishvara).
[^4]: Vv. 339–353 follow the Bhagavata (10.38–10.39): Akrura comes to Vraja on Kamsa's order, sees the marks of Krishna's feet, at night tells of Kamsa's design; Krishna and Balarama leave for Mathura; the gopis lament; in the waters of the Yamuna Akrura sees Vishnu on Ananta. Cf. Murari 4.11.
[^5]: Cf. the Bhagavata 10.39.35 and 10.46–10.47 (the message sent with Uddhava).
[^6]: Vv. 354–360 follow the Bhagavata (10.43.17): each one sees Krishna in the arena according to his own disposition. Vv. 356–360 are absent in the 1983 edition; the 1903 edition gives them in a footnote as an additional reading of one manuscript. In the 1983 edition vv. 380–411 (the way to Mathura, the washerman, Kubja, the bow, the elephant) stand before v. 354, which is more consistent; we keep Bhaktisiddhanta's order. Cf. Murari 4.12.
[^7]: According to the Bhagavata (10.44.39), Kamsa, who thought of Krishna incessantly, though in fear and enmity, attained liberation. Gaudiya Vaishnavas explain that enemies slain by the Lord receive liberation, but not prema.
[^8]: In the Bhagavata (10.44.48–49) it is Krishna who consoles Kamsa's wives.
[^9]: That is, that Krishna sent Nanda and the cowherds back to Vraja and Himself remained in Mathura (Bhagavata 10.45): for the Vaishnava poet Vraja's separation from Krishna is the bitterest thing of all.
[^10]: Vv. 379+1 — 379+3 are absent in Bhaktisiddhanta's edition (1922); they are found in the 1903 edition (here, after v. 379, with a new raga) and in the 1983 edition (before v. 412, since vv. 380–411 are moved earlier there). Cf. the Bhagavata 10.45 (Vasudeva performs the sacred-thread ceremony for his sons).
[^11]: Vv. 380–411 go back to the arrival of Krishna and Balarama in Mathura (Bhagavata 10.41–10.43): the washerman, Sudama the garland-maker, Kubja, the bow, the elephant Kuvalayapida, the challenge to the wrestling; cf. Murari 4.12. The washerman's name (Durmukha) is not given in the Bhagavata.
[^12]: Lochana Dasa speaks plainly of Kubja's desire. Gaudiya Vaishnavas (following Rupa Gosvami) distinguish Kubja's love, which contains a desire for her own pleasure (sadharani-rati), from the selfless love of the gopis (samartha-rati); cf. en/49, note 11.
[^13]: Vv. 396–400 (Kamsa's high platform, the platforms for Vasudeva and Devaki, the well) are details not found in the Bhagavata; cf. the Bhagavata 10.42.26–38 (evil omens, the preparation of the arena).
[^14]: Lochana Dasa returns to the story of Gaura: that same Krishna, the Lord of Vrindavana, appeared in the Kali age as Gaura.
[^15]: V. 423 is absent in the 1983 edition; the 1903 edition puts it in brackets as the reading of some manuscripts.
[^16]: A spiritual experience is meant (the Lord revealing Himself in the heart), not a worldly feeling: the residents of the Mathura-mandala, seeing Gaura, experience Krishna's lilas — one sees the Child, another the Youth, another hears the flute.
