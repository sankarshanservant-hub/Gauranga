# Sutra-khanda, part 1 (portion 01)

*The blessing: the mangala-shloka; praise of Ganesha, of Shiva and Parvati, of Sarasvati and of the Vaishnavas; obeisance to the Lord, His associates and the author's guru Narahari; Murari Gupta and his Sanskrit book, the model of this poem; the beginning of the "sutra", a brief list of the pastimes of the Adi-khanda*

## Mangalacharana (The Blessing)

> *Summary by Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati.*[^1] Having sung the glory of Sri Krishna Chaitanya, the author prays for the merciful grace first of the Vaishnavas and then of his own guru, Sri Narahari Thakura; he bows at the feet of the countless associates of Gaura-lila, known to him and unknown; praying for the blessing of guru and Vaishnavas, he declares that the model of his book, a *panchali-prabandha*, is the Sanskrit poem of Sri Murari Gupta called *Sri Chaitanya-charita*; and he briefly states what will be written in the Adi-, Madhya- and Shesha-khanda.

*Mangala-shloka (Sanskrit)*[^2]

Glory to the Lord in the form of Chaitanya, crest-jewel of sannyasis! —
He who gladdens the devotees by giving away heaps of priceless jewels of bhakti and prema,
who descended in full in the age of Kali to free the devotees from their distress,
and who crushes the unbelievers of the three worlds with the thunderbolt-goad of His roar.

*Raga patha-manjari*

**1.** I bow, I bow, I praise you, god Ganeshvara,
great destroyer of obstacles;
one-tusked, huge of body, helper in every work —
glory, glory to the son of Parvati![^3]

**2.** I bow my head to Hara and Gauri, joining my palms;
falling at their feet, I serve them:
the sole doers in the three worlds, granting the boon of bhakti to Vishnu —
these two are to me all the gods and goddesses.

**3.** I bow my head to Sarasvati: play upon my tongue,
tell the tale of the virtues of Gaurahari —
unknown to the three worlds, unknown even to the Lord of speech in all his greatness[^4] —
a wondrous, unprecedented tale.

**4.** Humbly I beseech the gods and all my venerable elders:
let no one put obstacles here.
I ask neither wealth nor boons — I am the lowest of the low:
let the book be completed without hindrance.

**5.** First of all I praise the devotees of Vishnu, all those great fortunate ones
by whose virtues the earth is made pure:
they are merciful to all living beings, and above all to those in whom they find longing [for God];
their lives are a blessing to the three worlds.

**6.** I am quite unworthy, I do not know right from left,
and yet I want to seize the sky with my arms;
a blind man choosing heavenly gems, who does not see even the mountain beside him —
I do not know what the end will be.

**7.** I have but one hope: the Lord rejects no one —
high and low alike sing His glories;
to all living beings He is equally merciful, to all He gives the shade of His feet,
He keeps no rule of who is worthy.

**8.** And the Vaishnavas — hear of them:
they are causelessly merciful to all people,
they live for others, adorn themselves for others,
and find their happiness in the good of others.

**9.** Thakura Sri Narahari, master of his servant's life —
my hope is in his feet;
low though I am, I long to sing the virtues of Gaura —
this is what Lochana Dasa trusts in.

**10.** By the grace of his feet I shall sing without weariness —
this is the hope of my heart;
those two feet, the cause of attaining what I desire,
I keep in my heart unceasingly.

*Raga kedara*

**11.** Glory, glory to Sri Krishna Chaitanya and Nityananda,
glory to Advaitachandra, glory to the devotees of Gaura!

**12.** Glory to the Lord of the life of Narahari and Gadadhara!
Be merciful, Lord, cast Your auspicious glance on me.

**13.** His golden, fair body is all adorned with compassion;
bowing down, I shall sing of those cool, rosy feet.

**14.** With all the devotees, be pleased to sit [here] in love:
let the breeze of those cool feet touch my body.

**15.** Darling of Shachi, Lord, I bow to You:
for even a moment turn Your merciful glance on me.

**16.** Advaita Acharya Gosani, crest-jewel of the gods —
by the grace of His feet this earth is blessed.

**17.** Bowing down, I shall sing of Him, the Lord of Sita's life:
be merciful, Lord — I pray with joined palms.

**18.** He is not different from Chaitanya — that Thakura, the Avadhuta,
Nityananda-Rama; I praise the son of Rohini.

**19.** Garga, enraptured with pride in the virtues of Gora[^5] —
bowing down, I first sing of his feet.

**20.** I praise Mishra Purandara, father of Vishvambhara;
I praise Shachi Thakurani, mother of the Thakura.

**21.** I praise Lakshmi Thakurani, renowned in the world —
the serpent of separation from the Lord stung her.[^6]

**22.** I praise Vishnupriya-ma, [the lady] of Navadvipa:
her ornament is the rosy feet of the Lord.

**23.** Joyfully I praise Pundarika Vidyanidhi,
for whom Mahaprabhu wept aloud.[^7]

**24.** With one mind I praise Pandita Gosani[^8];
I bow at the feet of Ishvara Puri and Madhava Puri.

**25.** I praise Govinda Gosani and Vakreshvara —
intoxicated bees at the lotus feet of Gaura.

**26.** Puri Paramananda and Vishnupuri,
and Gadadhara Dasa I praise — [their feet] upon my head.

**27.** Gupta the vaidya[^9] I praise with joyful longing:
be kind at heart, that I may sing the virtues of Gora.

**28.** I praise Srivasa Thakura and Haridasa;
in the feet of Vasu Datta and Mukunda is my hope.

**29.** I praise Ramananda Raya, the abode of love;
unceasingly I praise Pandita Jagadananda.

**30.** I praise Rupa and Sanatana, Pandita Damodara;
I praise Raghava Pandita, bowing without end.

**31.** Sri Rama, Sundara, Gauridasa and all the rest,
the companions of Nityananda, all the devotees — I praise.

**32.** I praise the master of our family, my chosen object of worship:[^10]
in this world and the next he is my protector.

**33.** Without him I have no friend in the three worlds;
I praise Narahari Dasa, ocean of Gaura's virtues.

**34.** To Govinda and Madhava Ghosha and to Vasu Ghosha,
falling to the ground, with joined palms, I bow.

**35.** With one mind I praise Sri Vrindavana Dasa,
by whose song, the *Bhagavata*,[^11] the world is enchanted.

**36.** Brothers, one could sing praises without end;
I praise the Deity of our house and Raghunandana.[^12]

**37.** As a child he fed laddus to the Sri Murti[^13] —
no one took him for an ordinary man.

**38.** I praise his father, Sri Mukunda Dasa,
whose faith is pure on the path approved by Chaitanya.

**39.** Some names I know, some I do not;
I praise them all: all of them are the crown on my head.

**40.** I praise the mahantas and the people of the mahantas;
in one place I praise and sing the feet of all.

**41.** Let no one judge in his mind who comes first and who after:
for the sake of the metre the praises are not in order.

**42.** Those whom by mistake I have not named in my praise —
to them a hundred obeisances: forgive me the offence.

**43.** I praise the devotees who live on earth and who roam the sky;
I bow at the feet of each, one by one.

**44.** Sing the virtues of Gora with joy, in hope of love —
so sings Lochana Dasa with a jubilant heart.[^14]

*Raga varadi; disha: "Brothers dear as life, I will tell, I will tell you my own tale! — Murchha: — Ah, what is this, ah, O my life! — First I beg the blessing of all the great fortunate ones; then shall I sing the tale of [Gaura's] virtues"*

**45.** I am worthless, lowest of the low — what do I know of the truth?
What can I say of the greatness of Gora's virtues and deeds?

**46.** Not knowing, to babble on — what good is that?
To stumble before worthy people is a shame.

**47.** I am not qualified, and yet I dare:
so strongly am I drawn by the sweetness of Gora's virtues.

**48.** Murari Gupta, the vaidya, lives in Navadvipa;
he is always near Gorachanda.

**49.** Who could describe his greatness?
On earth he is renowned as Hanuman[^15] —

**50.** he who leapt across the ocean and burned Lanka,
found out the news of Sita and told it to Sri Rama,

**51.** who brought the vishalya-karani herb and revived Lakshmana —
that same Murari Gupta lives in Nadia.

**52.** He knows all the truth; he is the Lord's intimate,
a seasoned devotee of Gaura's lotus feet.

**53.** All that the Lord did from His birth in childhood,
how from beginning to end He spread prema —

**54.** about all this Damodara Pandita questioned him,
and he told him everything from beginning to end in due order.

**55.** So came about a book of Gauranga's deeds composed in verse[^16] —
the dialogue with Damodara, issued from Murari's mouth.

**56.** Hearing it, I was filled with love,
and I tell of Gaura's deeds in a sung narrative, a *panchali*.

**57.** I am not qualified, and yet I tell it — that is my fault;
do not scorn me, do not be angry.

**58.** Who, seeing nectar, is not drawn to it?
A foolish child wants [to reach] the moon in the sky.

**59.** Such is my wish — to sing the virtues of Gora;
at this hour I beg the mercy of the Vaishnavas.

**60.** I bow at the feet of the Vaishnavas:
to sing the virtues of Gora — that is my heart's desire.

**61.** My Thakura, Prabhu Narahari Dasa —
I bow and humbly pray: fulfil my desire.

*Raga mara-hati; disha: "Hari, Rama, Rama, Moon among brahmanas — ah, my life!"*

**62.** First of all I will tell an unprecedented tale:
how Acharya Gosani[^17] worshipped [the Lord in] the womb.

**63.** The Lord of the three worlds came to earth,
with all His companions, intimates and retinue;

**64.** how father and mother raised the child,
how at the annaprashana they joyfully gave Him a name.

**65.** I shall tell in due order the pastimes of childhood:
[how] the jingle of ankle-bells was heard from bare feet;

**66.** how He suddenly went about touching unclean places,
and how He instructed His mother in knowledge;

**67.** the women of the town spoke, to understand His ways,
and at their word He at once brought a coconut;

**68.** the Thakura plays with a puppy —
seeing it, everyone is full of joy;

**69.** He plays with the children on the royal road —
and how Gupta the vaidya beheld His revelation;

**70.** with the boys He dances in the sankirtana of Hari —
seeing it, everyone rejoices at heart;

**71.** how His father put the chalk [for writing] in His hand —
hearing of it, the fever of misfortune goes far away;

**72.** then I shall tell — listen attentively —
how Vishvambhara plays with the elder, Vishvarupa:

**73.** like two brothers born of one womb, Indra and Upendra;
I shall tell of them — you will hear later;

**74.** how Vishvarupa took sannyasa,
and how Vishvambhara consoled father and mother with words;

**75.** then I shall tell of Vishvambhara's deeds:
how He played unseemly games with the children —

**76.** all the boys together on the bank of the Jahnavi
run over the sand, following the tracks of birds;

**77.** seeing this, His father was grieved at heart,
brought Him home and scolded Him;

**78.** how [the Lord] showed him mercy in a dream —
I shall tell it all, listen with one mind;

**79.** the ear-piercing, the chudakarana and the sacred thread —
I shall tell it all with a joyful heart;

**80.** childhood ended, He enters youth,
day by day manifesting prema without end;

**81.** He studied with His guru together with His fellow students,
how He mimicked the speech of the people of East Bengal;[^18]

[^1]: The summary printed in Bhaktisiddhanta's edition before this section is almost wholly illegible in the OCR (only scattered words can be made out — a list of the coming pastimes: Vishvarupa's sannyasa, the passing of Jagannatha Mishra, the journey to East Bengal, etc.). We therefore give his own brief summary of this section from the *Khanda-vivarana* (survey of the parts) at the beginning of the same edition.
[^2]: In Bhaktisiddhanta's edition the shloka is followed by his Sanskrit commentary. He explains: "in the age of Kali" — "in the age of quarrel"; "to free the devotees from their distress" — to give the Lord's servants a joy free of thorns when they are troubled by disputants opposed to shruti; "descended in full" — appeared in His original form as Bhagavan (svayam-rupa); "crest-jewel of sannyasis" — the jewel in the crown of renunciants, full of beauty; "bhakti and prema" — service of the Lord, and prema, which is nothing but the desire to please Krishna's senses — these are the priceless jewels, and "giving away" means bestowing them freely; "the devotees" — those devoted to pure service; "the unbelievers" — those hostile to the devotees and turned away from Hari; "crushes" — subdues completely; "glory to" — may He abide above all.
[^3]: Ganesha, Shiva with Parvati, and Sarasvati are praised here after the custom of the Bengali *mangala* poems. For Gaudiya Vaishnavas the gods are honoured as devotees of the Lord: Ganesha worships Govinda (*Brahma-samhita* 5.50), and Shiva is the greatest of Vaishnavas (*Bhag.* 12.13.16); so too Bhaktisiddhanta, in his summary of the next section, calls Shambhu "the best of Vaishnavas". The words "the sole doers in the three worlds" are poetic praise; for the tradition the source of all the worlds is Krishna Himself, and Shiva and Parvati are His servants, "granting bhakti to Vishnu", as the same line says.
[^4]: Literally "unknown to the three worlds, in greatness — to the Lord of speech"; the Lord of speech (Vaninatha) is Brahma, husband of Sarasvati. The 1903 edition reads "to the golden [Lord], the Lord of speech"; the sense of the line is not quite clear in either reading.
[^5]: So in Bhaktisiddhanta's text (and in his index of names — "Garga"): Garga Muni, the family priest of the Yadavas, who in Gaura-lila is Nilambara Chakravarti, the Lord's maternal grandfather (*Gaura-ganoddesha-dipika*). The 1983 edition reads here "brimming over, enraptured with pride in Gora's virtues" and refers the line to Nityananda.
[^6]: The Lord's first wife, Lakshmipriya, left the world while the Lord was in East Bengal (see v. 86).
[^7]: In the 1983 edition this couplet stands right after the praise of Shachi (v. 20); we follow the order of Bhaktisiddhanta's edition.
[^8]: Pandita Gosani is Gadadhara Pandita.
[^9]: That is, Murari Gupta the physician (*vaidya*, physician, also the physician caste).
[^10]: "Master of the family" and "chosen Deity" (*ishta-devata*) here mean Narahari himself, the author's guru, named in the next couplet.
[^11]: Vrindavana Dasa's *Sri Chaitanya-bhagavata*.
[^12]: Literally "the Thakura of our house"; this usually means the household Deity (at Shrikhanda, Gopinatha). The 1983 edition reads: "I praise the Thakura of our house, Sri Raghunandana", i.e. Raghunandana himself is so called; it also adds a couplet: "Sri Raghunandana, beloved of all the mahantas, to whom the Lord gave a garland and sandal before all others."
[^13]: Sri Murti — the Deity. According to the tradition of Shrikhanda, the boy Raghunandana, son of Mukunda Dasa (Narahari's brother), gave a laddu to the Deity of Gopinatha, and the Deity ate it.
[^14]: The line is damaged in the OCR of Bhaktisiddhanta's edition; translated from the legible reading. The 1903 and 1983 editions have: "Sing the virtues of Gora — this is my hope; Lochana Dasa says: fulfil my desire."
[^15]: The identification of Murari Gupta with Hanuman is also accepted by Kavi Karnapura's *Gaura-ganoddesha-dipika*.
[^16]: Murari Gupta's *Sri Krishna-chaitanya-charitamrita*, a Sanskrit poem framed as Murari's dialogue with Damodara Pandita (translated in `murari-gupta/`). Bhaktisiddhanta in his survey of the parts calls it *Sri Chaitanya-charita*.
[^17]: Advaita Acharya.
[^18]: The people of East Bengal (*bangaja*) spoke a dialect that was thought comic in Navadvipa; in His youth the Lord loved to mimic it.
