# Madhya-khanda, part 2 (portion 25)

*The gods come to the Lord and receive prema; mercy to Shuklambara and Gadadhara; the dance in Srivasa's house — Vrindavana in Navadvipa; the Lord disperses the clouds over the kirtana; the section "Mercy to Mukunda": a song of Gaura's dance*

*Raga dhanashi*

**139.** In Navadvipa Gora is the ever-full moon:
He pours out streams of the nectar of His own prema.

**140.** The devotee-chakoras drink the nectar of His feet;
boundless compassion — prema — Gora reveals.

**141.** Hear a wonderful story of yet another day.
He sits in His own house, radiant as millions of Kamadevas:

**142.** a neck like a lion's, a throat like a conch, lotus eyes;
and aloud He speaks words deep as thunder:

**143.** "What do I see in this house — four-, five-, six-faced ones?
At the sight of them wonder grows in My heart."

**144.** Shrinivasa Pandita was beside the Lord;
hearing this, he answered as the matter stood:

**145.** "All the gods have come to see You —
Brahma and the others, the four-, five- and six-faced.

**146.** You are an ocean of prema: give [them] the treasure of prema!
All the gods are begging You for the treasure of prema"[^1].

**147.** Then Mahaprabhu sat on a divine seat,
leaning His body on one devotee and [placing] His feet on another[^2].

**148.** Shrinivasa and all the other devotees
fell at His feet and weep:

**149.** "I beg a boon — the honey-prema of Your lotus feet:
give it to me, Lord, O utmost limit of compassion!"

**150.** Then Lord Vishvambhara speaks in a voice like thunder:
"Take it!" — and gave to all the grace of prema.

**151.** At once prema awoke in all the gods:
their bodies, filled with bhava, became wondrous.

**152.** Crying "O Radha-Govinda!", the gods dance;
seeing it, all the Vaishnavas rejoice at heart.

**153.** The gods dance, and the goddesses with them:
tears, thrill, sweat — in waves of prema.

**154.** Now they roll on the ground, falling at His feet,
now they dance, loudly crying "Haribol!",

**155.** now they offer praise, calling "Gaura-Govinda!",
now they prostrate themselves, falling at His feet.

**156.** Now, placing His feet on their heads, the gods
beg a boon: "May my mind abide at Your feet!"

**157.** "So be it," the Lord replies, and says again and again:
"May you all be filled with the treasure of prema!"

**158.** Having received prema, the gods went to their own abodes;
seeing it, all the devotees rejoiced at heart.

**159.** Such mercy did the Lover of His devotees show.
Seeing this display of mercy, Shuklambara speaks.

**160.** Shuklambara Brahmachari is most pure:
his body is hallowed by holy places, his nature is gentle.

**161.** Before the Lord he speaks without fear;
longing for prema, he says all that is in his heart:

**162.** "Hear, hear, O Lord, Gaura Bhagavan:
at last today my eyes have found their joy.

**163.** I have travelled to many holy places,
[suffered] many torments and hardships — and have understood nothing.

**164.** I have travelled to Madhupuri and Dvaravati,
and still I am unhappy: give me the treasure of prema!"

**165.** Hearing these words, the Lord answered:
"Hear one word of Mine, Shuklambara:

**166.** in those forests live ever so many jackals and dogs —
what is that to Me?" said the Thakura[^3].

**167.** "As long as Krishna does not rise in the heart,
so long one has no grace from the holy places.

**168.** Without love for Krishna any dharma is nothing;
read and see: the shastras all say this."

*Thus [it is said]:*

**169.** "The fish is always bathing, the snake lives on air, the ram eats leaves, the oil-presser's ox is ever going round, the heron always stands in meditation,
the mouse lives in a hole, the lion always dwells in the thicket — but alas! what fruit do they have from austerity without perfection in true feeling?"[^4]

*Thus in the Narada-pancharatra (1.2.6):*

**170.** "If Hari is worshipped, what need of austerity? If Hari is not worshipped, what use is austerity?
If Hari is within and without, what need of austerity? If Hari is not within and without, what use is austerity?"

**171.** Hearing these words, the brahmana fell to the ground;
distraught, he weeps, and his longing grows.

**172.** The Lord cannot bear the distress of one devoted to Him:
Gaura's body flushed red with compassion.

**173.** "I have given prema, given prema!" He cries aloud,
and the brahmana Shuklambara obtained the grace of prema.

**174.** At once he obtained prema: his body trembles,
his whole body thrills, there are tears in his eyes.

**175.** Rejoicing, he performs sankirtana of [the Lord's] qualities and names;
seeing it, all the people were very glad[^5].

**176.** Pandita Sri Gadadhara — the abode of all virtues —
stays constantly with the Lord and repeats the Name.

**177.** One night he slept beside the Lord;
seeing his longing, the Lord, pleased, said:

**178.** "You will obtain rare prema at the dawn of this night:
by the grace of the Vaishnavas your desire will be fulfilled"[^6].

**179.** Saying this, He put the garland from His own body round his neck.
In the morning all came to see the Lord.

**180.** The Lord told everyone what had happened in the night —
and in the course of that talk Gadadhara Pandita obtained prema.

**181.** With great joy at heart he bathed in the waters of the Ganga;
his body, helpless with prema, sways.

**182.** He performed the worship of Jagannatha-deva according to the rules,
and worships again — before his own Lord:

**183.** he anointed His body with fragrant sandal paste,
put a divine garland round His neck and offers praise.

**184.** So every day he serves [the Lord]:
in the bedchamber he prepares His bed

**185.** and every night sleeps at His feet;
his heart is always full of faith and devotion[^7].

**186.** Before the Lord he speaks nectarean words;
hearing them, Lord Vishvambhara rejoices at heart.

**187.** His nectarean speech sprinkled [the Lord's] heart:
the Lord, taking him by the hand, goes to dance.

**188.** Placing His other arm on Narahari's arm,
the Charmer of the rasa dances in Srivasa's house.

**189.** In Gaura's body the devotees see a dark form,
and Gadadhara then took the form of Radha.

**190.** Narahari at that moment became Madhumati;
seeing this, all the Vaishnavas cry "Hari, Hari!"

**191.** In that place Vrindavana appeared:
the son of Shachi — with the cows, the gopis and the cowherd boys.

**192.** As the friends and girl-friends had been before,
so the devotees became beside the Lord, tasting rasa.

**193.** Sri Raghunandana is a new Kamadeva:
he is called the transcendental Madana.

**194.** All of them, taking their former bodies, beside the Lord
dance round the Lord, circle within circle.

**195.** Seeing this, all the associates of other avataras weep:
in Navadvipa the Moon of Vraja has risen!

**196.** Now it is Gaura's lila with Gadadhara,
now Shyama's lila, the joy of the rasa of the rasa dance with Radha[^8].

**197.** Seeing the wondrous lila, all the devotees
cry again and again: "Hari, Hari! Jaya, jaya!"

**198.** The day ended; it was evening, the quarters were lovely —
and suddenly clouds covered the vault of the sky.

**199.** Again and again the thunder rolls;
seeing this, the Vaishnavas took it for a calamity.

**200.** Seeing that a hindrance had come, all were dejected;
anxiously they think: "How can the hindrance be removed?"

**201.** [But] the clouds had come to receive the grace of prema:
seeing Gaura's lila, they thundered with love.

**202.** Then Mahaprabhu took the mandira cymbals in His hands
and loudly sings the sankirtana of names and qualities.

**203.** Wishing to fulfil [the desires of] the heavenly world,
the Lord raised His face and gazes at the sky.

**204.** The clouds went far away, the sky cleared,
and the jubilation of the Vaishnavas increased.

**205.** The night became clear, tinged by the moon;
the devotees sing His qualities, and He Himself dances.

**206.** The clouds, taking their own form, beside the Lord
dance, following the devotees.

**207.** In giving this prema Gaurahari makes no distinction:
what to speak of clouds — He filled the three worlds with it!

**208.** The Thakura Himself dances with the devotees:
amid the ecstasy of all dances the son of Shachi.

**209.** In the ecstasy of prema the great King of dancers dances;
on His lotus feet the anklets ring loudly.

**210.** The virtuous wives of the brahmanas joyfully cry "Jaya, jaya!";
from the sky the gods watch the festival[^9].

**211.** All the devotees dance, intoxicated with prema:
who knows what austerities they performed — for how many births —

**212.** that for them they dance with the Thakura
and make merry with such a treasure as prema?

**213.** With His compassion the Lord covered both earth and sky.
Hearing of it, Lochana Dasa speaks in joy.

## Mercy to Mukunda

> *Summary by Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati.* The author, having described the wondrous beauty and loveliness of Sriman Mahaprabhu, glorifies the lila of planting the mango tree. One day Sriman Mahaprabhu, before the devotees, planted a mango stone; before their eyes it at once sprouted, gradually became a tree and blossomed; mangoes appeared on the tree and at once ripened; the devotees offered them to Bhagavan and all together honoured the prasada. Afterwards they saw that none of it was there any more — everything had vanished. Then Sriman Mahaprabhu, by the example of that tree, showed the illusoriness of samsara and taught how to conquer maya; He instructed Mukunda Datta in the worship of Sri Krishna, whose form is full of sweetness, and bade him give up speculation about the Self (adhyatma) and serve Bhagavan. When Murari Gupta asked Sriman Mahaprabhu for the mercy of bhakti, the Lord blessed him. Srivasa and Sri Rama Pandita are very dear to Sriman Mahaprabhu; in their house Sriman Mahaprabhu enjoyed the bliss of kirtana with the devotees. One day, when a certain foolish brahmana called Sri Krishna a product of maya, Sriman Mahaprabhu, hearing it, bathed in the Ganga with His clothes on[^10].

*Raga shyamagada. [Opening:]*
*Like the peak of Sumeru is His beautiful tall body;
under the weight of prema He sways.
His whole body thrills from feet to crown,
His red eyes brim with tears.*

*Refrain: "The town of Nadia rejoices: splendidly dances Shachi's son!"*

**1.** The four brothers — Shrinivasa and his brothers — joyfully sing auspicious songs,
Haridasa cries "Hari, Hari!"
[Gora is] like Kishori and Kishora; [all] loudly proclaim Gora's glories,
roaring "hum!" in the surge of prema.

**2.** Murari and Mukunda Datta sing His qualities without pause,
exultant, thrilling.
In hope of the nectar of prema, near His lotus feet
they hover like intoxicated bees.

**3.** All around they cry "Jaya!", and in the middle dances golden Gaura;
everyone is beside himself with bliss.
Wherever one looks, there is joy everywhere,
in all ten directions — the weeping of prema.

**4.** Some, meeting in twos, embrace in the bliss of prema,
some sing His fame like bards.
Falling at His feet, Pandita Gosani says:
"You have spread out a wondrous market!"[^11]

**5.** As if golden pearls were strung — so the thrill [covers] His body,
His face is red with love.
In the languor of rasa He laughs, languid in rapture,
and reveals the treasure of His heart.

**6.** Now He speaks unearthly things, as if intoxicated,
now He says: "I am Bhagavan!"
Now He bows down, now He pronounces blessings,
now He grants boons to His own.

**7.** The Lord reveals a prema never seen before:
a trembling seized the seven continents.
All — women and men — seeing what Gora experienced, [forgot themselves],
and in prema this Lochana Dasa forgot himself.

*Raga dhanashi, tune taraja; refrain: "What creator is this — he made beautiful Gauranga and kept his own body? How hard he is, with a heart of wood and stone: seeing this beauty, he did not melt away!"*[^12]

**8.** Who, churning nectar, took off its butter
and from it fashioned Gora's body?
Who sifted the whole world, squeezed out its rasa
and blended it into one — pure tenderness?

**9.** Who set the curd of anuraga with a culture of prema
and made [of it] His two eyes?
Sweeter still are His soft words:
smiling, He lets them fall one by one.

**10.** Who boiled down an unbroken stream of nectar
till it became sugar the colour of gold?
Who, beating that sugar, raised the froth? —
so Gora's body seems to me.

**11.** Who, grinding lightning, rubbed His body with it,
rubbed His face with the moon?
Who, grinding loveliness, painted this picture —
the wondrous fashioning of [His] form?

**12.** All the full moons weep, distraught,
at the fragrance of the lotuses of His hands and feet.
The radiance of His twenty nails lit up the world:
those blind from birth received sight.

**13.** Such a charming Gora is seen nowhere —
in the wondrous play of prema.
Men, in the mood of women, weep, distraught —
how can a woman keep life [in her body]?

**14.** Who made His heart — an abode of play — from the rasa of all rasas,
shaping it with delight?
Who, grinding Madana, fashioned His face?
And I, without Him, must die in tears.

**15.** Who brought Indra's bow
and drew with it the line of sandal on Gora's forehead?
At the sight of this form all the women of noble families
long to turn their arms into wings.

**16.** [His body,] a temple of delight, was carved
with great care from many jewels.
The art of playful lilas, the play of bhavas...
thinking of Madana's pain, [a woman] weeps.

**17.** He does not glance from the corners of His eyes, yet always in everyone's heart
the eye-birds fly to see Him.
Seeing the eyes' thirst, the lips too are filled with longing;
the body is languid and exhausted.

**18.** Chaste wives leave their families, the lame run headlong,
asuras and atheists sing His qualities,
roll in the dust and weep; no one can stay composed:
Gora's qualities are unending nectar.

**19.** "Run, run!" they cry, and embrace in the bliss of prema;
some dance, some laugh aloud.
Even the modest wife of a noble house says: "Let everything go!" —
in the breeze of Gora's qualities and beauty.

**20.** The wives of the town of Nadia, beholding the moon of Gora's face,
ever shed streams of tears.
Their breasts fill with love, their bodies thrill,
in their hearts [He] is ever awake.

**21.** Even the best of yogis and sages, pondering day and night,
are bewildered by Gora's qualities.
The Lord of all worlds rolls in the dust and weeps,
ever remembering: "Radha, Radha!"

**22.** Leaving His play with Lakshmi, He longs for prema;
both His eyes are red with love.
[His] heart, absorbed in meditation on Radha, is not revealed;
the witness to it is that golden body[^13].

**23.** Look, look, people — what wondrous prema!
Though He is the Lord of the lords of the three worlds,
in the company of the destitute — who knows what wealth He begs? —
with what joy He goes about dancing!

**24.** Glory, glory, glory! Such a storehouse of the rasa of prema
Gora-raya broke open and gave away.
The lifeless received life, the lame leapt over mountains —
joyfully sings Lochana Dasa.

[^1]: The four-faced one is Brahma, the five-faced Shiva, the six-faced Skanda (Karttikeya). Cf. Murari 2.3.1–2.
[^2]: Cf. Murari 2.3.3–4: the Lord touches the devotees' bodies with His feet, and they beg devotion to His feet.
[^3]: Cf. Murari 2.3.5–8. The Lord does not belittle Mathura and Dvaraka: He says that mere bodily residence in holy places, without love for Krishna, does not yet bear fruit (cf. Bhagavata 10.84.13).
[^4]: "True feeling" (sad-bhava) is explained by Bhaktisiddhanta in his gloss on the verse as purity of mind. The source of the verse is not given in the edition.
[^5]: The 1983 edition has one more couplet here: "Rejoicing, he repeats the name of Krishna; the whole night passed in the rasa of Krishna."
[^6]: Cf. Murari 2.3.10–17; there the Lord tells Gadadhara that in the morning he will distribute His prasada to the Vaishnavas.
[^7]: According to Gaudiya Vaishnava tradition, Gadadhara Pandita is Sri Radha Herself (Gaura-ganoddesha-dipika 147–153); Murari (2.3.17) compares this service to Radha preparing Krishna's bed.
[^8]: Vv. 188–196: Vrindavana and the rasa-lila appear in Srivasa's house; Gadadhara is Radha, Narahari Madhumati (see the Sutra-khanda, en/08, v. 645), Raghunandana "the transcendental (aprakrita) Madana", i.e. Kandarpa, not the mundane god of lust (in the Gaura-ganoddesha-dipika — Pradyumna; cf. en/08, note 19). The rasa here is a spiritual lila, not mundane love. "Circle within circle" — lit. "in the order of the enclosures (avarana)": the associates surround the Lord in circles, as in meditation on His abode. Lochana Dasa, a disciple of Narahari, brings the Shrikhanda devotees into this scene; other biographies do not describe it.
[^9]: Vv. 198–213 — cf. Murari 2.3.18–27. The brahmanas' wives raise the auspicious cry "ulu-ulu".
[^10]: The summary covers the whole section; this portion contains only the song of Gaura's dance (vv. 1–24); the rest (the mango tree, the instructions to Mukunda and Murari, Srivasa and Sri Rama, the blaspheming brahmana) is in the next portion.
[^11]: Pandita Gosani is Gadadhara Pandita. The "market" is an image of the lavish distribution of prema.
[^12]: The voices of this song are the women of Nadia (vv. 13–20): here again is Lochana Dasa's "nagari-bhava" (see en/09, note 9). The "creator" who supposedly made Gaura is a poetic figure: the Lord's form is eternal and created by no one.
[^13]: That is, the Lord hides that His heart is absorbed in Radha, but His golden complexion — Radha's complexion — betrays it. So too in Gaudiya Vaishnava theology: Gaura is Krishna who has taken Radha's bhava and complexion (cf. Chaitanya-charitamrita, Adi 4).
