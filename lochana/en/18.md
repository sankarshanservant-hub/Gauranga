# Adi-khanda, part 10 (portion 18)

*The beginning of the Kaishora-lila: preparations for Vishvambhara's wedding; the adhivasa of bridegroom and bride; the women of Nadiya go to fetch water for the rite; the wedding morning — the nandimukha-shraddha, the tears of Shachi and of the Lord for His departed father; the bridegroom's attire and the wedding procession, the rejoicing of Nadiya*

## Kaishora-lila (The Pastimes of Youth): the Wedding

> *Summary by Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati.* One day, as Gaurasundara, having finished His lessons, was returning home from His teacher's house, He met Vanamali Acharya on the way. Talking with the Acharya, Gaurahari understood that he had gone to His mother Shachi-devi to arrange His marriage, but, not having received the hoped-for answer from Shachi-devi, was returning home with a saddened heart. Without saying a word to the Acharya on the way, Gaurahari went home and by a hint let Mother Shachi know His wish; then Mother Shachi again summoned the matchmaker Vanamali Acharya and told him to arrange her son's marriage. Receiving Mother Shachi's command, Vanamali Acharya came to the house of Vallabhacharya and settled the marriage of Gaurahari with his daughter Lakshmi-devi. When the marriage had been agreed upon, Shachi-devi called her relatives and neighbours and announced her son's wedding, and all were immersed in an ocean of supreme bliss. Meanwhile Shachi-devi set about making all kinds of preparations in the house for her son's wedding. Before long the day of the adhivasa arrived. The respectable ladies, following ancient popular custom, duly performed the anointing of the body with turmeric, the fetching of water[^1] and other rites; the Vedic rites too were performed as prescribed. In Vallabhacharya's house all these rites were likewise duly performed. Then on the wedding day Gaurahari, with great pomp, in a festive procession and with many companions, arrived at the house of Vallabhacharya, and the Acharya, according to ancient custom, received his son-in-law by offering Him water for His feet and arghya. Then the Acharya brought his daughter Lakshmi-devi to the wedding pavilion and gave her to Gaurahari. And after completing the rites of kushandika,[^2] the feeding of brahmanas and so on, he sent his daughter to his son-in-law's house.

*Raga varadi; disha: "Dvijachand is my life!"*

**1.** Then Shachi, looking at her son's face,
says to Him sweet words with a smile:

**2.** "Listen, listen, Vishvambhara, my golden son:
the daughter of Vallabha Acharya is simply wonderful.

**3.** I feel she is worthy to be Your wife;
what good fortune to have such a daughter-in-law!

**4.** Consider it and fix a splendid time;
gather all that is proper."

**5.** Hearing His mother's words, Vishvambhara Raya
got ready all that was needed.

**6.** He called an astrologer and an excellent pandit,
and they fixed the auspicious moment and time.

**7.** That auspicious day, that auspicious time arrived;
brahmanas and all good people came joyfully.

**8.** The whole town of Nadiya was filled with joy;
the ocean of prema surged, and all forgot themselves.

**9.** Gathering the married women,[^3] Shachi performs the auspicious rites;
all the acharyas perform the Lord's adhivasa.

**10.** On all sides the brahmanas recite the Vedas;
conchs and mridangas sound — auspicious signs.

**11.** All the quarters are adorned with rows of lamps and flags;
fragrant sandal, garlands — everything is beautiful.

**12.** All the brahmanas performed the Lord's adhivasa,
and He revealed a body as beautiful as millions of Kamadevas.

**13.** His body sparkles, all is lit by its radiance;
seeing it, all the brahmanas were amazed.

**14.** The brahmanas were given fragrant sandal and garlands
and, with gift after gift of betel, were greatly pleased.

**15.** Vallabha Acharya performs the bride's adhivasa;
with the best of brahmanas he performed the auspicious rites.

**16.** Exchanging fragrant garlands, sandal and scents,
at the adhivasa they adorned the jewel of a son-in-law.

**17.** When the adhivasa was finished towards the end of the night,
[the women] came rejoicing, saying: "Let us go for the water!"[^4]

**18.** All at once, in waves, all kinds of instruments sounded,
and all the respectable wives forgot their vows.

**19.** The young women in the town of Nadiya lost their senses
in the waves of the ocean of rasa of Gauranga's wedding.[^5]

**20.** In throngs go the nagaris — wives of brahmanas —
like moons adorning the circle of the earth.

**21.** Eyes like a doe's, gait like a she-elephant's,
the radiance of their bodies sparkles, humbling Madana's pride.

**22.** Their hair, dress, garments and ornaments are matchless:
one glance at them would steal even a sage's heart.

**23.** In their smiles lightning trembles, in their speech is nectar;
laughing and jesting, they go swaying along.

**24.** They sing of Gauranga's virtues in sweet melodies;
at the sound of the fifth note Ananga's body trembles.

**25.** In their noses pearl-rings sway —
as if stars were falling onto the red disc [of dawn].

**26.** The respectable wives came to Shachi's house;
all were given scents, betel nuts and sandal.

**27.** All the nagaris went for the water;
in every house there is auspicious joy.

*Sung in raga tudi*

**28.** A moonlit night, moon-faced maidens;
with sweet voices, friends, we shall sing of Gora's lilas.

*Refrain: "Who, who will go in front, friends, who will sing of Gora's virtues, friends? Let us go for the water! The heart surges — who can hold it back?"*

**29.** One in silken dress, another in yellow garments;
swaying, they go [fanned by] the breeze from Gora's body.

**30.** "Let Shachi go in front, and we shall go behind her;
coming and going, friends, we shall stand near Gora.

**31.** Take fragrant sandal and garlands in your hands, covered:
on that pretext I shall touch Gora's body.

**32.** Take camphor and betel carefully in your hands;
hand in hand, I shall place them right into Gora's hand."

**33.** The married women, gathered together in play and merriment,
brought the water — so sings this Lochana Dasa of [the Lord's] virtues.

*Raga bhatiyari*

**34.** In joy and gladness the night passed; at the bright dawn
with joyful hearts they performed the rites according to rule.

**35.** They performed the bathing and the giving of gifts, as was proper;
as prescribed, they worshipped the gods and the ancestors.

**36.** They performed the nandimukha-shraddha as the Vedas ordain;
abundant food was given to the brahmanas.

**37.** To dancers and to the bards gifts were given;
with gifts of every kind all were pleased.

**38.** But they valued [the Lord's] sweet words more than the gifts:
seeing His moonlike face, the heart is refreshed.

**39.** He satisfied each one as he had hoped;
then the Lord bathes again, as befits the wedding.

**40.** Then the barber did his work,
and the respectable wives together anoint His divine body.[^6]

**41.** All kinds of instruments sound with sweet tones;
on all sides the cries of "ulu-ulu" and "Jaya, jaya!" are heard.

**42.** Then Shachi-devi, taking with her all the married women,
lovingly honours each as befits her.

**43.** She honoured all the kinsfolk who had come to the house
and spoke to all, opening her heart:

**44.** "I, wretched one, have lost my husband, my son is fatherless;
how can I, poor as I am, honour you?"[^7]

**45.** As she said this, Shachi's voice faltered;
tears from her eyes soaked the garment over her breast.

**46.** When Shachi spoke these grieving words,
the Lord Vishvambhara, hearing them, bowed His head.

**47.** He began to think: "Where has My father gone?"
His heart began to burn; He felt great pain.

**48.** Tears fall from His eyes like a string of pearls;
seeing this, the goddess Shachi-rani was alarmed.

**49.** And all the respectable wives who were beside her,
seeing the Lord's tears, began to weep.

**50.** "Why, why, child, do I see Your face so sad?
At such an auspicious time — and You weep?

**51.** In all the world You are my only wealth;
if You are dejected, I shall give up my life this very moment."

**52.** Hearing His mother's words, the Lord Vishvambhara,
grieving for His father, speaks in a faltering voice.

**53.** His face was pale, like the moon at dawn;
[His voice] like the deep thunder of a fresh cloud.

**54.** The Lord said to His mother: "Listen to what I say:
why is there such pain in your heart?

**55.** What wealth do you lack? What sorrow has befallen you?
You speak so bitterly, as if you were poor and alone.

**56.** You have reminded Me that I no longer see My father —
and how My heart feels, how can I tell?

**57.** Give each person betel and sandal twice over,
give all kinds of gifts — as much as your heart desires.

**58.** Anoint everyone's whole body with fragrant sandal;
give abundantly, have no worry in your mind.

**59.** What no one on earth does for people,
I shall accomplish by a mere sign — I tell you."[^8]

**60.** Hearing these words, Shachi spoke very softly
and with sweet words calmed Vishvambhara.

**61.** Just as Vishvambhara had directed,
so she satisfied all the brahmanas.

**62.** Meanwhile Vallabha Acharya in his own house
worships the gods and ancestors together with brahmanas.

**63.** To his daughter he gave all kinds of ornaments
and adorned her with scents, sandal and garlands.

**64.** Seeing that the auspicious moment was near, the best of brahmanas
sent a brahmana to fetch the bridegroom.

**65.** Here, meanwhile, the Lord Vishvambhara, with His friends,
arrays His divine body in wonderful attire.

**66.** His body was anointed with scents and sandal;
on His forehead a tilaka like a moonbeam.

**67.** On His cheeks sparkle makara-shaped earrings;
on His chest shines a pearl necklace.

**68.** His red lotus eyes are brightly lined with kajal;
His two brows are like the two bows of Kama.

**69.** Armlets, bracelets, divine rings set with jewels;
a divine radiance sparkles — one cannot look at it.

**70.** He wears a divine garland and a cloth with a red border;
the breeze from His body is laden with fragrance.

**71.** In His hand a golden mirror, like the full moon;
seeing Him, people are no longer masters of their hearts.

**72.** The young wives lost their composure seeing His beauty;
seeing His beauty, the women cannot take their eyes away.[^9]

**73.** Bowing to His mother, the Lord sets out at the auspicious moment;
auspicious cries arose — "Jaya!" and the name of Hari.

**74.** The Lord mounts a divine palanquin, surrounded by His friends;
in front dancers dance and singers sing songs.

**75.** Brahmanas recite the Vedas, bards their panegyrics;
shinga and bargo horns sound, bheur and kahal horns.

**76.** Damama and dagar kettledrums sound, pataha drums and mridangas;
dosari and mohari pipes play — a joy to hear.[^10]

**77.** Hearing "Hari, Haribol!" and the cries of "Jaya, jaya!",
the people of Nadiya went mad with joy.

**78.** People run, jostling one another, unable to find a way;
a thrill ran through the assembly of townsfolk.

**79.** Some do not tie up their hair, do not adjust their clothes —
they run headlong to see, breathing hard.

**80.** Whispering, exchanging glances, with no more shame,
calling to one another, all the people of Nadiya run.

**81.** The proud ones, casting all their pride far away,
run joyfully to see Gauranga.

**82.** In the sky the gods on divine chariots look down
and with love press forward to see Gora's body.

**83.** The wives of the gods gaze at Vishvambhara's face;
on all sides celestial women sing auspicious songs.

*Raga bihagara; refrain: "On all sides cries of 'Jaya, jaya!' are heard — the wedding of Gauranga-chand! The respectable wives together raise the 'ulu-ulu': sing auspicious songs with joy!"*

**84.** "Do up your hair, put on silk saris,
put kajal on your eyes!"
To the wedding of Sri Vishvambhara, all gathering together,
dressed up, they set out.

**85.** "Necklaces, armlets, bracelets, belts with little bells,
anklets — put them on quickly!"
Sindura on the forehead close to the locks,
and below it a dot of sandal.

**86.** Betel on their lips, betel in the left hand,
they go playfully swaying;
seeing Vishvambhara, like the Five-arrowed One,[^11]
they cannot keep their composure.

**87.** All kinds of instruments sound, hundreds of conchs resound,
mridangas, pataha drums and kahal horns;
joyfully the dundubhis sound and the dindima drums,
sweetly the muhari pipes play.

**88.** The vinas play, the flutes softly speak,
rababs, upangas, pakhoyajs;
in the town of Nadiya there is joy in every house,
auspicious wedding music plays.

**89.** All the people gaze at Gaurachandra's face,
all the people of Nadiya rejoice;
praising that beauty which surpasses millions of Kamadevas,
they gaze at Him, forgetting all shame.

**90.** Braids come loose, garments go unadjusted —
they run in a frenzy;
forgetting husbands and sons, faces uncovered,
their hair falling over their breasts.

**91.** "Blessed, blessed, blessed!" say the women,
no other word is heard;
on all sides, in markets and roads, crowds of nagaris
rose up to see.

**92.** Some play the vina, some sing songs,
some run in delight;
on all sides "Jaya, jaya!", the auspicious triumph —
so says Lochana Dasa.[^12]

[^1]: The fetching of water (jal-saha; in Lochana Dasa "pani sahi"): on the morning of the wedding the married women go singing to the Ganga to fetch water for the bridegroom's bath (below, vv. 17–33).
[^2]: Kushandika is the rite at the sacred fire that concludes the wedding (in Lochana Dasa, on the day after the wedding).
[^3]: "Aiho-suho" are married women whose husbands are living: only they take part in the auspicious wedding rites.
[^4]: "Pani sahi" — see note 1.
[^5]: Here and below (vv. 18–33, 72, 78–81, 84–92) is a favourite motif of Lochana Dasa: the women of Nadiya, forgetting shame, are drawn to Gaura (nagari-bhava; see portion 09, note 9). The 1983 edition has even more such lines (after vv. 40, 72, 81, 86); they are absent in A.
[^6]: The 1983 edition adds here eight couplets on how the nagaris, anointing His body, lose their composure; they are absent in A.
[^7]: By custom a widow takes no part in the auspicious wedding rites and cannot honour the guests as before; hence Shachi's grief. Cf. Murari 1.10.5–12.
[^8]: That is, the Lord, encouraging His mother, promises to do for the guests by His mere sign more than people can; cf. Murari (1.10.11): "I have the power to do what is beyond mortals, and yet I follow the customs of the world."
[^9]: After this the 1983 edition has six more couplets on the women's agitation; they are absent in A.
[^10]: Shinga is a horn; bargo, a wind instrument; damama and dagar, large kettledrums; dosari and mohari (muhari), pipes like the shahnai. For bheur, kahal and the others see the glossary.
[^11]: The Five-arrowed One is Kamadeva, the god of love, armed with five flower-arrows.
[^12]: The 1983 edition has two more stanzas in this song (after v. 86) on the procession of the nagaris; they are absent in A.
