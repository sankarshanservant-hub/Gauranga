# Adi-khanda, part 8 (portion 16)

*Pauganda-lila (continued): Vishvambhara tells His mother not to eat grains on Ekadashi and for a moment becomes motionless after saying "I am going"; the conversation of Damodara and Murari Gupta on how the Lord abides in the body of His devotee; the illness and departure of Jagannatha Mishra on the bank of the Ganga, the grief of Shachi and of the Lord, the funeral rites*

*Raga shri; disha: "O, what do we see! Glory, glory to Gauranga!" Refrain: "What nectar-bliss is the prema of my Gauranga! What a Gauranga is mine! Glory, glory!"*

**615.** Another day the Lord, sitting in His house,
revealed what was in His heart.

**616.** His whole body was filled with the nectar of His own effulgence;
one could not look at it — the house was all ablaze with light.

**617.** Seeing His mother, He said: "Listen to what I say:
I have noticed one great fault in you.

**618.** Do not eat grains any more on the day of Ekadashi;[^1]
carefully keep this word of Mine."

**619.** In a voice deep as the rumbling of clouds He said this to His mother;
hearing it, the mother was amazed, and reverent awe filled her heart.

**620.** Her body filled with shyness, awe and love;
"I shall keep Your command," she said very softly.

**621.** Hearing His mother's words, He, kind at heart,
with a gladdened heart taught her dharma.

**622.** At that time a brahmana suddenly came
and with a pure heart brought Him betel leaf and nut.

**623.** Smiling, the Lord then ate the betel nut,
and a moment later called His mother again.

**624.** The Lord said to His mother: "I am going; [this] body —
guard it carefully: it is your own son."[^2]

**625.** Saying this, He remained motionless for half a moment
and lay stretched on the ground like a stick.

**626.** Again He lay silent; Shachi, terrified,
hastily pours Ganga water into His mouth.

**627.** In a moment the Lord came to Himself,
and the house was lit up by the effulgence of His natural form.

**628.** The Lord said to His mother: "I am going; the body…" —
is there anyone who can judge this matter?

**629.** Sri Murari Gupta the vaidya, intimate with the Lord,
a knower of every truth, a seasoned devotee —

**630.** Damodara Pandita asked him:
"Great soul, tell me the truth of this.

**631.** Did the Lord display [His] yogamaya,[^3] or some power?
Consider it and give me a reasoned answer."

**632.** Murari says: "Listen, listen, good sir:
do I know all the intentions of Krishna?

**633.** I shall say something as my own reason suggests;
if it proves well founded, keep it in your heart.

**634.** When they hear [of Him], see, meditate and sing sankirtana,
the Lord enters the hearts of His devotees.

**635.** His own body is not a [material] body: it is a form beyond the gunas;[^4]
the enjoyment of the gunas, conduct and deliberation [belong to those] in the gunas.

**636.** Therefore He accepts the devotee's body as [His] body
and there freely plays in all His doings.

**637.** He regards the worship of His devotee as greater than worship of Himself;
within Himself He knows that in this lies the fullness of all worship.

**638.** The Lord Himself is the very one who depends on Him;
only in worldly usage, by maya, are they spoken of as "two".[^5]

**639.** Why He regards the devotee as greater than Himself —
this the whole world cannot understand.

**640.** A form full of rasa, a body full of beauty,
a body endowed with every splendour — so He has manifested Himself.

**641.** He has nothing but play, sport and lila;
what wretch reviles Him by calling Him "without qualities"?

**642.** By His maya He does not openly manifest Himself[^6]
and plays unceasingly in the body of His devotee.

**643.** The devotee's eating, sleeping, resting and enjoyment —
in these very things Krishna's happiness becomes manifest.

**644.** The conduct of devotees and of other people is the same;
by the nature of the body they look the same to the eye.

**645.** Those who, seeing with their eyes, take [them] for men
[say]: 'Where is Krishna? With my eyes I see a man.

**646.** Krishna, the Lord of all lords, the Brahman beyond the gunas —
can He be doing mundane deeds in a human body?'

**647.** Saying this, the low man does not accept [it];
but the elevated one knows the Lord's body in the devotee's body.[^7]

**648.** Such is my reasoning, this is how I think;
understand for yourself and decide in your heart what is right.

**649.** Know that a Vaishnava's body is always full of Krishna;
so we hear in the Vedas, the Puranas and the Bhagavata.

**650.** By the dust of his feet all people are purified;
he purifies even the Ganga and all the other holy places.

**651.** The low man who disputes about the body of such a person
commits an offence out of incomprehension."

**652.** Thus Damodara and Murari Gupta
concluded their talk, both with gladdened hearts.

**653.** The Lord does not count His own body as a body,
but regards the body of His devotee as His own.

**654.** Having reasoned thus, the two departed;
hearing of it, this Lochana Dasa speaks with joy.

*Raga vibhasa; disha: "Ah, ah!" Refrain: "Ah, ah, my life!"*

**655.** Listen, everyone, to another wondrous account;
when you hear it, the pain in your heart will be dispelled.

**656.** Having learned the whole essence of the Vedas in his teacher's ashrama,
Jagannatha, the jewel of brahmanas, came home.[^8]

**657.** By the will of providence a fever seized his body;
seeing the grievous fever, all were struck with fear.

**658.** Seeing Shachi weeping in utter distress,
the Lord consoles her, explaining the truth:

**659.** "Mother, death is certain for all:
for Brahma and Rudra, the oceans, the mountains and the Himalaya,[^9]

**660.** Indra, Varuna, Agni — time destroys them all;
why are you so afraid of death?

**661.** Call all your kinsfolk and friends now:
let them all together help [him] remember the name of Krishna."

**662.** Knowing that at the time of death this is truly the duty of kin,
the Lord, the divine Jewel of the Yadus, has [His father] helped to remember [Krishna].

**663.** Hearing this, all the kinsfolk and friends came;
coming to the Lord's house, they surrounded Mishra.

**664.** Those friends who were mature in judgement,
seeing that the hour was near, took counsel.

**665.** Vishvambhara says: "Mother, why do you delay?
All our near ones and kin are needed at once."

**666.** Saying this, mother and son lifted him up
and with their kinsfolk went to the bank of the Jahnavi.

**667.** Clasping His father's feet, Vishvambhara weeps;
He cannot hold back His tears, His voice falters:

**668.** "Leaving Me, father, where are you going?
Never again shall I call anyone 'Father!'

**669.** From today this house of Mine is empty;
never again shall I see your feet, father.

**670.** Today all ten directions are empty, all is pitch dark:
no one will teach Me so lovingly, holding Me by the hand."

**671.** Hearing such nectarean words,
Jagannatha in his anguish cannot utter a word.

**672.** In a faltering voice he says: "Listen, Vishvambhara:
what was in my heart cannot be told.

**673.** I have entrusted You to the feet of Raghunatha;[^10]
only may You never at any time forget me."

**674.** Saying this, he gives himself to remembrance: "Hari, Hari!"
All the brahmanas lowered him into the water of the Ganga.[^11]

**675.** They placed a garland of tulasi around his neck;
on all sides kinsfolk and friends chant the name of Hari.

**676.** On all sides resounds the sankirtana of Hari's qualities;
and at that moment the best of brahmanas went to Vaikuntha.[^12]

**677.** Mounting a chariot, the brahmana departed for Vaikuntha;
at Shachi's wailing the earth is rent asunder.

**678.** Clasping her husband's feet, she weeps, rolling on the ground:
"I shall go! Take me with you!

**679.** For so long I served you;
you have gone to Vaikuntha, and I am left on the earth.

**680.** I served you at your rest and at your meals;
today all ten directions are empty, all is pitch dark.

**681.** I am left forsaken with your younger son;
where will Nimai live, to whose face will He look?

**682.** Your son Nimai, rare in all the world —
forgetting it all, you are going, my lord!"

**683.** Seeing His mother's weeping and His father's death,
Shachi's son weeps, tears streaming from His eyes.

**684.** Like a pearl necklace strung on a thread,
the tears flow from His eyes onto His broad chest.

**685.** Devotees and friends cry out, "Alas, alas!";
at the Lord's weeping the whole world weeps.

**686.** All soothed [Him] with sweet words:
"Lord, at Your weeping creation perishes!"

**687.** The women consoled Shachi-devi:
"Looking at Vishvambhara, Shachi, you will forget everything."

**688.** The Lord, steadfast Himself, restraining everything within,
performed the rites befitting the time.

**689.** Then, surrounded by His kinsfolk, He performed His father's rites
as the Vedic injunctions prescribe.

**690.** The Lord, loving His father, performed the offering to the ancestors
and, in due order and according to rule, honoured the brahmanas.

**691.** Water vessels, rice, food and all other gifts
the Lord, devoted to His father, gave to the brahmanas.

**692.** This is the account of Jagannatha's going to Vaikuntha —
of that best of brahmanas who was the father of Vishvambhara Himself.

**693.** If a person hears this account with faith,
he goes to Vaikuntha, as one who dies at the Ganga.

**694.** Looking at Gorachand, Shachi sighs:
"May the fatherless son not fall into despair!

**695.** If His heart becomes immersed in the taste of learning,
then my son will pass His days happily."

**696.** Listen, everyone, to this wondrous account:
Lochana tells something of the deeds of Chaitanya.

[^1]: Ekadashi is the eleventh day of each lunar fortnight, a day of fasting; "anna" means grain (rice and the like). Bhaktisiddhanta's summary (portion 14) stresses that by forbidding His mother to eat grain on Ekadashi the Lord taught the whole world. In Murari — 1.07.18–20.
[^2]: In Murari (1.07.21) the Lord says: "I am going. Guard the body of your son, which will for half a moment become motionless." The conversation of Damodara and Murari (vv. 629–653) interprets exactly these words.
[^3]: Literally "maya". In Murari (1.07.24, 27) the same question concerns the Lord's "yogamaya": when said of the Lord, maya is His internal power, yogamaya.
[^4]: "Nirguna" here means beyond the material gunas, not "devoid of form and qualities": in vv. 640–641 Lochana Dasa expressly condemns those who call the Lord "nirguna" in an impersonal sense. In v. 640 "niramila" literally means "made": the Lord Himself manifests His eternal form.
[^5]: The line also admits the reading: "He Himself is the Lord, and that one (the devotee) depends on Him." From the sense of the whole argument (vv. 624, 653), Lochana Dasa is explaining the words "I am going; the body is your son": in Gaura the Lord Himself plays the part of His own devotee, and that people see "two" here is the working of His maya (yogamaya). The eternal distinction between the Lord and the devotee (vv. 637, 639) is not denied by this.
[^6]: Here maya is the power by which the Lord conceals Himself, that is, yogamaya.
[^7]: The reasoning of vv. 634–653 follows Murari (1.08.1–7): the Lord enters the heart of the devotee and regards the devotee's body as His own. In the Gaudiya Vaishnava understanding (Bhaktisiddhanta), the Lord's body is not different from Himself and is not material, and Mahaprabhu is Krishna Himself who has assumed the form and feeling of His own devotee (bhakta-rupa); what is said of the "devotee's body" therefore does not mean that in Gaura the Lord uses a separate, human body.
[^8]: The subject of the line is Jagannatha Mishra; cf. Murari (1.08.13): the Lord's father too studied Vedanta and other scriptures.
[^9]: According to the Puranas, Brahma and Rudra, who govern the universe, are also not eternal in these offices: at the end of Brahma's life the universe is dissolved.
[^10]: Raghunatha ("Lord of the Raghu dynasty") is Sri Ramachandra; Jagannatha says the same words in Murari (1.08.17).
[^11]: The custom of antarjali: the dying person is carried to the bank of the Ganga and immersed waist-deep in its water so that he may leave the body remembering Hari.
[^12]: Jagannatha Mishra is an eternal associate of the Lord (in the Gaura-ganoddesha-dipika — Nanda Maharaja and Vasudeva); his departure is not an ordinary death, and Lochana Dasa speaks of it as leaving for Vaikuntha on a chariot.
