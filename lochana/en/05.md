# Sutra-khanda, part 5 (portion 05)

*Narada comes to Brahma and gives him the Lord's words about His appearance in the Kali-yuga; Brahma tells of the doubt of Sanaka and the other Kumaras, of the Lord's answer in four verses, and of how the* Bhagavata *was given through Narada; Brahma recalls Garga Muni's words about the colours of the avataras in the various yugas and, following the* Bhagavata, *describes the avataras and the worship of the Satya-, Treta- and Dvapara-yugas*

**276.** The sage Narada went on, his vina sounding —
its sound sprinkles sweet rasa;
as if a stream of sweet nectar filled the ears,
delighting the hearts of the people of the three worlds.

**277.** Going, he forgets himself, he cannot keep to the road,
his face reddened with love;
he feels no weariness of the way, drops of sweat on his brow —
and so he reached Brahma's abode.

**278.** Seeing him, Brahma hastily, with great joy at heart,
rose to greet the sage;
the sage bows, falling at his feet;
raising him, Brahma embraced him.

**279.** He asked of his welfare, deeming himself blessed by his coming,
with the love of long separation:
"It seems to me, looking at your bright face,
that you, fortunate one, will tell a secret.

**280.** Words from your mouth are nectar to the ear;
speak — I shall listen; let my heart be soothed:
what news of the worlds? Tell the song of the Lord's virtues —
what have you seen, what have you heard?"

**281.** In due order Narada tells his story, full of ardour:
his lips quiver, his body sways,
his eyes are full of tears, his lips are red —
as he begins to speak, his joy is doubled:

**282.** "Hear wondrous news. You are the maker of all creation;
I roam the universe that bears your name.[^1]
According to each yuga people practise the dharma of that yuga,
but in the Kali-yuga sin is rampant.

**283.** Seeing the people at the end of Dvapara, all in sorrowful grief,
I dreaded Kali;
with an anguished heart I went straight to the Lord
and asked with great boldness:

**284.** 'In the sinful age of Kali by what means
will You deliver the people? Tell me, Lord:
the brahmanas are without the Vedas, dharma has waned in all —
in my heart is a great doubt.'

**285.** Hearing the piteous words, the Lord, jewel of virtues, says:
'Put away the anxiety of your heart:
I shall deliver the people of Kali, I shall spread devotion to Me,
I shall descend there Myself.

**286.** Charity, vows, austerity, dharma and all other works —
all this I shall place in Hari's name;
see, Kali is full of faults, but note one great virtue:
release from bondage — by My sankirtana.[^2]

**287.** Proclaim: let Shiva, Brahma and the rest
all be born on earth when Kali comes;
I, the embodiment of compassion, shall be born on earth,
becoming golden according to the yuga.'"[^3]

*Shubhachhanda, raga pahida; disha: "Glory, glory to the moon Gauranga, risen in Nadia in the age of Kali! — Murchha: — Ah, hear the story of my Lord, whose virtues lit up these three worlds! Ah, hear the story of Gaurangachand! Ah, what is this, ah!"*

**288.** Hearing such words, lord Brahma
planted in his heart a sprout of the nectar of prema.

**289.** His cheeks thrill, tears stream from his eyes;
beside himself with joy, Brahma embraced the sage.

**290.** Brahma says: "Hear, best of great sages:
by your grace my heart is made clear today.

**291.** In the snare of worldly things all are blind in the bonds of maya;
by your grace they are freed from their bonds again.

**292.** You care only for the deliverance of people;
I shall tell you something of the past — about myself.

**293.** The sages Sanaka and the others, my sons,
opening their hearts, said to me:

**294.** 'You are the Lord's beloved son;
whatever we ask, explain to us its essence.

**295.** The Lord is inconceivable, imperishable, eternally blissful, He is Brahman,
subtle, lord of all lords, all-pervading, [the ground of] dharma;

**296.** infinite, beyond the gunas, spotless, formless,
without beginning, middle or end — so reason judges.[^4]

**297.** How then, being such a Lord, is He born on earth —
unborn, yet being born, does the deeds of ordinary men?

**298.** In Vrindavana He held the rasa with the cowherd wives,
like people given to the play of amorous pleasure.[^5]

**299.** Be they women or men, He is the Self in all:
why then such dalliance, as though He were not content?

**300.** Great is this doubt in my heart;[^6]
tell the truth, Four-faced one, clear away the confusion.'

**301.** When Sanaka and the others expressed such a doubt,
hearing it, I was amazed at heart.

**302.** My face darkened with anxiety:
this conduct of the Lord is beyond my grasp.

**303.** This is beyond Vedanta; who knows the truth?
Brahmas like me there are hundreds upon hundreds.

**304.** Just as I was about to voice this thought,
at that very moment the Lord came in the form of Hamsa and spoke:

**305.** in four verses He gave me the answer,
and that answer I passed on to them all.[^7]

**306.** All those great souls were pleased
and, satisfied, went wherever each wished.

**307.** Those four verses are to me the vessel of all rasa;
there is none in the universe who knows their truth.

**308.** Some time later Vyasa, in the Naimisha forest,
set forth all that is in the *Bharata* and the Puranas;

**309.** nothing was left to say, yet the heaviness of heart did not lift,
and he fell into distress.

**310.** Vyasadeva swooned in the forest;
knowing this, the Lord was filled with compassion.

**311.** Calling me, He gave these four verses:
"Take this supreme treasure and go to Vyasa.

**312.** Vyasa does not know the truth of My deeds;
let him compose the *Bhagavata* following these verses.

**313.** This *Bhagavata* you shall tell to Narada:
Sarasvati, Sharada, will speak with his tongue."[^8]

**314.** Therefore I say: hear, best of sages —
age after age you alone show mercy to living beings.

**315.** For the deliverance of living beings you are the great soul;
the *Bhagavata* is the divine scripture — there is no other treasure.

**316.** The *Bhagavata* is untouched by worldliness — it is an independent Person;[^9]
not understanding this, the fool takes it for mere book-learning.

**317.** Such a statement of the *Bhagavata* about Krishna's descent
Garga Muni made at the name-giving ceremony;[^10]

**318.** now I have remembered Garga Muni's words,
the account of [the Lord's] colours according to the four yugas.

*So in the* Srimad-Bhagavata *(10.8.13):*

**319.** "Taking bodies yuga after yuga, he had three colours —
white, red and likewise yellow; now he has become dark (Krishna)."[^11]

**320.** In the Satya-yuga people knew Him as white,
in the Treta as of rosy lustre, and His name was Yajna;

**321.** now this son of Nanda is dark,
and at the last there will be an avatara of yellow colour.[^12]

**322.** Whoever doubts the verse, saying that its order is broken,
understands it thus: three colours for four yugas.

**323.** White, red, yellow, dark — these are four colours,
and the yugas are four, there are no others.

**324.** Otherwise consider: in which yuga is [the Lord] golden?
Speaking hastily, one will not dispel the doubt.

**325.** I shall say a little about this — listen:
I shall now explain it to the devotees.

**326.** The *Bhagavata* speaks of this in the eleventh canto:
the king asked the sage Karabhajana.[^13]

*So in the* Srimad-Bhagavata *(11.5.19); the king said:*

**327.** "At what time, of what colour and form is that Bhagavan,
by what name and by what rite is He worshipped by men? Tell us this."

**328.** At what time does Bhagavan take what colour,
what is His name, at what time [did He appear];

**329.** at what time what dharma, what kind of people,
by what rite do they worship, what pleases [Him]?

*Sri Karabhajana said (11.5.20–22):*

**330.** "In the Krita, Treta, Dvapara and Kali yugas Keshava
is of various colours, names and forms, and is worshipped by various rites.

**331.** In the Krita He is white, four-armed, with matted locks, clad in bark,
bearing a black deerskin, sacred thread, rosary, staff and waterpot.

**332.** People then are peaceful, without enmity, friendly, even-minded;
they worship God with austerity and control of mind and senses."

**333.** The sage says to the king: "Listen attentively
to how people worship in the Satya and the other yugas.

**334.** In the Satya-yuga He is white and bears the name Hamsa;
four-armed, His dharma is austerity, He wears matted locks and bark,

**335.** staff, waterpot, antelope skin and sacred thread;
and people are by nature peaceful, without enmity, even-minded."

*So (11.5.24–25):*

**336.** "In the Treta-yuga He is red, four-armed, with a triple girdle,
golden-haired, the soul of the three Vedas, marked by sacrificial ladles and the like.

**337.** Then people, righteous and learned in the Veda, worship Him —
God Hari, who comprises all the gods — by the knowledge of the three Vedas."

**338.** That Lord in the Treta-yuga takes a red colour:
four arms, a triple girdle, sacrificial ladles in His hands,

**339.** on His head hair like molten gold;
the Lord who comprises all the gods Himself performs sacrifice.

**340.** The three Vedas are His soul; He bears the name Yajna;
those versed in dharma worship Him by the rules of the Vedas.

*So of the Dvapara (11.5.27–28, 31):*

**341.** "In the Dvapara Bhagavan is dark, in yellow garments, with His own weapons,
distinguished by the Shrivatsa and other marks.

**342.** Then mortals seeking knowledge worship Him, the Supreme Person,
bearing the marks of a great king, by the Vedas and tantras, O king.

**343.** Thus in the Dvapara, O lord of the earth, they praise the Lord of the world
by the rules of various tantras; and how [they praise Him] in Kali — hear."[^14]

**344.** In the Dvapara Bhagavan takes a dark colour:
the Shrivatsa and Kaustubha on His body, yellow garments;

**345.** the marks of a great king, sovereign of kings, shine on Him;
fortunate people worship Him by the Vedas and tantras.

**346.** Thus in every yuga [comes] the yuga-avatara,
and people practise the dharma of their yuga.

**347.** Satya, Treta and Dvapara — three yugas have passed,
and the colours were white, red and dark.

[^1]: *Brahmanda*, literally "Brahma's egg", the universe.
[^2]: Cf. *Bhag.* 12.3.51: "O king, Kali is an ocean of faults, but it has one great virtue: by chanting of Krishna one is freed from bondage and attains the Supreme."
[^3]: Cf. below (vv. 319–321 and 349): according to the *Bhagavata*, the Lord in each yuga takes a form of its own colour; in Kali, golden.
[^4]: This is the reasoning of the Kumaras, who formerly adhered to the impersonal Brahman (cf. *Bhag.* 3.15.43: the fragrance of the tulasi at the Lord's feet turned them to bhakti). In the Vaishnava understanding "beyond the gunas" and "formless" mean "free of material qualities and material form": the Lord has an eternal spiritual form.
[^5]: This is the doubt of the Kumaras, not the author's assertion. Parikshit voices the same doubt (*Bhag.* 10.33.27–28), and Shukadeva answers it (10.33.29–37): the rasa is the purest prema, not mundane passion; the Lord is self-satisfied and dwells as the Witness in the hearts of the gopis and their husbands. Bhaktisiddhanta stresses that the love of the gopis is the highest form of prema, free from all lust.
[^6]: A has "my heart", although many Kumaras are asking; so in both editions.
[^7]: In the *Bhagavata* the Lord in the form of Hamsa answers the Kumaras' question about detaching the mind from sense objects (11.13), while the four verses that are the essence of the *Bhagavata* (2.9.32–35) the Lord reveals to Brahma before creation. Lochana Dasa joins these accounts.
[^8]: In the *Bhagavata* (2.9; 1.5–1.7) Brahma passes on what he heard to Narada, and Narada instructs the dejected Vyasa.
[^9]: Cf. *Bhag.* 1.3.43: when Krishna departed to His abode, the *Bhagavata* rose like the sun for the people of Kali who have lost their sight.
[^10]: *Bhag.* 10.8: Garga Muni, the priest of the Yadus, gives names to Krishna and Balarama in Nanda's house.
[^11]: Garga Muni's words at Krishna's name-giving. The Gaudiya acharyas (CC Adi 3.36–40) take "yellow" (*pita*) as the golden form in which Krishna appears in the Kali-yuga as Sri Chaitanya, and "now He has become Krishna" as the appearance of Krishna Himself, the original Lord. Further on (portion 06) Lochana Dasa discusses this verse at length.
[^12]: "At the last" (*parisheshe*), i.e. in the Kali-yuga, the last of the four; so Lochana Dasa takes the "yellow" colour of the verse.
[^13]: In the *Bhagavata* (11.2–11.5) King Nimi (Videha) questions the nine sage-yogendras; Karabhajana answers about the yuga-avataras.
[^14]: Verses 11.5.29–30 (the hymn of the Dvapara) the author does not cite. The verse on Kali (11.5.32) is quoted at the beginning of portion 06.
