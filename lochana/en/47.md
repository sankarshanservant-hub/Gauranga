# Shesha-khanda, part 2 (portion 47)

*The end of the section "The Lord's Journey to the South": Panchavati; Ranganatha and Trimalla Bhatta; the meeting with Paramananda Puri and the prophecy of Madhavendra Puri. The section "The Lord Sees Vrindavana": the deliverance of the seven palms, Setubandha, the return to Puri; Nrisimhananda paves a road in his mind; the journey to Mathura through Kashi and Prayaga, the meeting with Rupa and Sanatana; the brahmana Krishnadasa and his account of the twelve forests*

*Raga shri; disha: "He takes away sins, torments and the fear of Yama. Glory to the son of Shachi, glory, glory!"*

**115.** Then Mahaprabhu, in that joyful play,
walks on — and His body in bliss was filled with thrill.

**116.** Thus, step by step, He walks along the road
and by way of the Godavari enters Panchavati[^1].

**117.** This great holy place named Panchavati —
there Sita, Lakshmana and Sri Rama lived.

**118.** Seeing Panchavati, the Lord lost consciousness in prema
and again and again calls: "Sri Rama! Lakshmana!"

**119.** "Here Lakshmana built a hut;
from here Rama went to kill the deer.

**120.** After Sri Rama went Lakshmana —
and here Ravana carried off Sita."

**121.** Saying this, the Lord weeps, overcome with prema;
now He cries: "Strike! Strike!", now He cries: "Seize him! Seize him!"

**122.** Loudly He calls: "Lakshmana! Lakshmana!" —
and remembering Sita, weeps with a fainting heart[^2].

**123.** The companions who were with Him cannot console Him;
Mahaprabhu Himself regains His composure.

**124.** Then, on another day, the Thakura went along the road
and little by little reached the bank of the Kaveri.

**125.** On the bank of the Kaveri He sees Sri Ranganatha;
seeing Him, He dances in prema with His own people.

**126.** There Trimalla Bhatta, seeing the Thakura,
gazes in amazement at the golden body.

**127.** The radiance of His body and the surges of prema!
His hairs standing on end in thrill outdo the filaments of the kadamba.

**128.** His body towers over all people like Sumeru —
a wish-fulfilling tree laden with the fruits and flowers of prema.

**129.** "Hari! Hari!" He calls in a loud voice;
seeing this, all the people who crowd round on every side weep.

**130.** Seeing such things, that Trimalla Bhattacharya
in wonder grasped the whole marvellous matter:

**131.** "This is Bhagavan Himself — no one else!"
He knew for certain: this is the life of all beings.

**132.** Knowing this, Trimalla Bhatta Raya
takes the Lord to his own dwelling[^3].

**132+1.** The Lord came to his house at the beginning of [the month of] Ashadha;
in all beings devotion to Krishna grows from day to day.

**132+2.** There He saw the Ratha-yatra,
and Sri Shachinandana danced before the chariot.

**132+3.** Staying [there] in Shravana, the Lord celebrated the swing festival;
all dance in the sankirtana of [the Lord's] names and qualities.

**132+4.** Staying in Bhadra, He celebrated the festival of Krishna's birth:
much did Gorachand dance in the dress of a cowherd.

**132+5.** Staying in Ashvina, the Lord, the son of Shachi,
performs sankirtana of the Name with the devotees[^4].

**133.** Conquered by [Trimalla's] love, Mahaprabhu came under his sway
and, filled with the highest joy, spent the chaturmasya [with him].

**134.** Having spent the chaturmasya, the Lord quickly set out [again]
and on the way met Paramananda Puri[^5].

**135.** Seeing each other, both rejoiced;
they gaze at each other, and tears flow from the eyes of both.

**136.** Looking [at the Lord], Paramananda Puri remembered
what his guru Madhavendra Puri had said:

**137.** "In the first twilight of Kali the Lord of Lakshmi will appear —
a sannyasi with a golden body, dwelling near the Daru-brahman"[^6].

**138.** "To establish in the Kali-yuga the dharma of sankirtana
Krishna will be born in the first twilight [of this yuga].

**139.** Golden, tall of body, arms reaching to the knees,
a neck like a lion's, shoulders like an elephant's, lotus eyes.

**140.** The Lord, an ocean of compassion, the abode of prema,
out of His own mercy will show grace.

**141.** I have not the fortune to see Him with my own eyes;
when you see Him, remember me."

**142.** These words of his guru came to his mind,
and he knew for certain: this is Bhagavan Himself.

**142+1.** "Madhavendra!" he repeated, remembering [his guru],
and wept with joy in his heart.

**142+2.** Hearing him glorify Madhavendra, the Lord dances,
and the devotees, crying "Hari! Hari!", dance close by.

**142+3.** Now and again He gives loud shouts in supreme bliss;
"Madhavendra!" cries the Lord and weeps in the bliss of prema:

**142+4.** "Today My sannyasa has borne fruit:
the name of Madhavendra has entered My ears"[^7].

**143.** Seeing [this], Paramananda Puri bows;
"What are you doing?" says the Lord and lifts him up by the hands.

**144.** In great joy He embraced him tightly,
and the Thakura went on, says this Lochana Dasa.

## The Lord Sees Vrindavana

> *Summary by Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati.* On the way to Setubandha Sriman Mahaprabhu showed the lila of delivering the seven palms. The ancient story of the seven palms [is this]: seven gandharvas, cursed by a sage, became trees; now, by the touch of the Lord, they attained liberation. Arriving at Setubandha, He, absorbed in prema, chanted the names of Rama, Lakshmana, Sita, Hanuman and others; then, having spent the chaturmasya on the bank of the Godavari, He returned again to Utkala, to Alalanatha. There He accepted as His own a devotee named Vishnudasa and, coming [to Purushottama], He stayed there and [spent] several months with the devotees in the bliss of kirtana. After this, setting out to see the Mathura-mandala, He met Rupa and Sanatana. Coming to Mathura, He, together with a devotee named Krishnadasa, absorbed in prema, saw on the eastern and western banks of the Yamuna the twelve forests, the prison of Devaki and Vasudeva, the houses of Kamsa, Ugrasena and others — countless places of Krishna's lilas[^8].

*Raga dhanashi; disha: "Gorachand is my life, Gora is my breath!"*

**1.** Hear attentively another wondrous story —
of the deliverance of the seven palms, as [the Lord] went along the road.

**2.** Those seven palms stand on that road;
seeing them, the Lord suddenly began to laugh.

**3.** Running up, He touched the seven palms —
and in the sky rose cries: "Glory! Glory! Glory!"

**4.** By a sage's curse seven gandharvas had dwelt [in them];
by the Lord's touch they attained deliverance.

**4+1.** Joining their palms, they prostrated themselves [before Him]
and, gaining divine bodies, all departed for Vaikuntha.

**4+2.** Seeing this, all the people bow;
all say: "This sannyasi is the avatara Rama"[^9].

**5.** Then Mahaprabhu goes along the road;
intoxicated with bliss, the Lord sings the glories of Hari.

**6.** In the bliss of prema He does not feel the weariness of the road
and little by little reached Setubandha.

**7.** Coming to Setubandha, He sees the linga of Rameshvara;
the Lord dances in bliss like a maddened lion.

**8.** Walking round the linga, He bows;
looking at Setubandha, He cries "Hari!" again and again.

**9.** In loving longing He weeps and calls: "Sri Rama! Lakshmana!";
at times, in His bhava, He calls: "Angada! Hanuman!"

**10.** Now in His bhava He calls: "Sugriva, My friend!" —
now in a frenzy He calls: "Vibhishana!"

**11.** Overcome with prema, He does not know one direction from another,
and looking at Setubandha He dances with all the devotees[^10].

**12.** Thus day and night He forgets Himself;
[at last] Mahaprabhu's mercy grew — to turn back.

**13.** So Mahaprabhu, going along the road [back],
again spent a chaturmasya, staying at the holy Godavari[^11].

**14.** Again the Thakura came to the land of the Udras;
in the bhava [of meeting] Jagannatha His prema grew beyond measure.

**15.** Then the Lord saw Sri Alalanatha
and made Vishnudasa the Oriya His own[^12].

**16.** Seeing Jagannatha, the Lord was filled with joyful excitement:
again and again raising His arms, He cries "Hari! Hari!"

**17.** Coming to Purushottama, the Lord stays in great happiness;
Lochana tells it — a great joy it is to people.

*Raga varadi; the tune "dhulakhela"*

**18.** Now I shall tell a story — hear the song of the virtues of Gora,
peerless in the three worlds.
In his mind he built an embankment, strewing it with pearls and coral —
a sannyasi named Nrisimhananda.

**19.** With gold, gems and rubies, with divine jewels on every side,
in his mind he built a road.
"Taking it as far as Mathura, I will offer it to Krishna" —
but just then his hour drew near.

**20.** The road was not finished, and grief remained in his heart;
he lamented within himself.
As far as Kanai's Natashala the road reached, and there it ended —
and the sannyasi attained Vaikuntha[^13].

**21.** This had been in his heart. The Lord suddenly set out —
no one knows where He is going.
Going on step by step, from Kanai's Natashala
Gora Raya turned back again.

**22.** The matter was not understood. Paramananda Puri says:
"Tell us, Lord, the reason for this."
Everything from beginning to end He then told him:
the reason was the fulfilment of a wish of the mind.

**23.** "From Purushottama all the way to Mathura
I will pave an embankment with gold, gems and rubies" —
such was the sannyasi's heart: "Along this road of mine
Gora, who wears the forest garland, will go."

**24.** Hear, all people, attentively, setting your minds on it,
of the manifestation of Sri Gorachand:
the wish of Nrisimhananda's mind Gaurachandra fulfilled —
of His glories this Lochana Dasa sings[^14].

*Raga shri; disha: "Gorachand — oh! — plays in Nilachala"*

**25.** And so in Nilachala the Lord with the devotees
delights in kirtana and stays in various sports.

**26.** Many devotees gathered there;
the Lord enjoys prema — He dances and makes others dance.

**27.** All the devotees who lived in various lands
little by little came to the feet of Chaitanya.

**28.** In bliss the Lord dwells in Nilachala;
of all this I shall tell later in detail.

**29.** Suddenly [the Lord] has a wish in His mind: "I will go to Mathura";
longing grew in His heart, His mind as though maddened.

**30.** Chaitanya Thakura set out on the road to Mathura;
on the way the bliss of prema grew beyond measure.

**31.** In loving longing the Lord runs, both eyes red;
He runs with a lion's gait — no sooner seen than gone.

**32.** The companions who were with Him cannot keep up;
the Lord goes far ahead, calling [for Krishna].

**33.** By the road through Jharikhanda the Lord went in haste;
He made beasts, birds, trees, even stones weep.

**34.** Surrounding Gauranga, deer and tigers dance;
there is no violence [in them] — all dance joyfully near the Lord.

**35.** Having blessed all the forest animals,
Gauranga went on His way, delighting in prema[^15].

**36.** Little by little He came to holy Varanasi;
many great sannyasis live there.

**37.** Bowing to Vishveshvara, He goes on along the road;
at Prayaga, seeing Madhava, He rejoiced at heart.

**38.** Rupa and Sanatana Gosani met the Lord;
showing them grace, He imparted His power to them[^16].

**39.** There, bathing at the Veni and seeing the Akshaya-vata,
He crossed the Yamuna near Agra.

**40.** He saw a wondrous village named Renuka —
the place where Parashurama appeared.

**41.** There the Yamuna, turning [back], faces Vrindavana;
seeing this, the Lord, beside Himself, is happy with the happiness of prema[^17].

**42.** Coming to Rajagrama, He sees Gokula on the far bank;
unable to restrain His heart, He became distraught.

**43.** With great effort the Lord restrained His heart;
overwhelmed with bliss, He sees Mahavana on the far bank.

**44.** He went on and on some distance more —
and, drawing near, He sees Madhupura.

**45.** Seeing Madhupura, the Lord became mad at heart;
overcome with prema, He seemed to lose consciousness.

**46.** Crying "Akrura! Akrura!", He fell to the ground;
in the bhava of separation, [when Krishna left for] Mathura, He fainted[^18].

**47.** Not knowing day or night, He remains in that place;
for three days the Lord was without consciousness.

**48.** People come and go and look at this marvel.
There lived [there] a foremost brahmana named Krishnadasa.

**49.** Seeing the Lord, he thinks within himself:
"From where has this jewel among men come?

**50.** By great fortune I have seen His feet;
is this Shuka or Prahlada? — so my mind takes it."

**51.** The Lord, overcome with prema, asked him:
"What is your name? Tell Me — listen, best of brahmanas."

**52.** The brahmana says: "Listen, listen, best of sannyasis:
my name is Krishnadasa" — so he answered.

**53.** Hearing this, the Lord laughed aloud:
"You, Krishnadasa, know everything of Krishna.

**54.** Your words have refreshed My body;
you will show Me all that is anywhere, in every detail.

**55.** This Mathura-mandala is Krishna's inner abode;
you, an experienced devotee, know everything.

**56.** You know what Krishna did and where —
show Me the Mathura-mandala, place by place."

**57.** The brahmana says: "I do not know all the places;
I know only the places of the twelve forests."

**58.** Hearing this, the Lord laughs in the bliss of prema
and manifested [His] power in his body.

**59.** In great bliss [the brahmana] says: "I will show everything;
from Krishna's birth to the slaying of Kamsa — I will tell it all."

**60.** The brahmana says: "Listen, listen, listen, sir:
I know for certain — You are the son of Nanda.

**61.** Seeing You, I have seen Vraja:
suddenly everything came back to my memory.

**62.** I [will reveal] the inner meaning of every place I know —
where Bhagavan was born and what He did."

**63.** Hearing this, the Lord, with joy in His heart,
embraced Krishnadasa and sings the glories of Krishna[^19].

**64.** That day He spent in Krishnadasa's house;
all night [Krishnadasa] tells of the Mathura-mandala:

**65.** "In the midst of the Mathura-mandala is the blessed Yamuna,
on both banks of which Krishna sports in love.

**66.** On the eastern bank of the Yamuna there are five forests,
on the western seven forests; now I shall tell of them.

**67.** In these twelve forests are Krishna's pastimes;
apart from devotees no one knows their secret.

**68.** Here is Kamsa's palace — west of the Yamuna;
to the north of it is the forest named Vrindavana.

**69.** From Mathura to it is one yojana of road;
of it I shall tell many secret things.

**70.** To the south-west of it is a forest named Kumuda,
one and a quarter yojanas' journey from Mathura.

**71.** South of it, Lord, is the Khadira-vana,
a yojana and a half's journey from Mathura.

**72.** The Tala-vana, Lord, is south of Mathura:
from Mathura it is half a yojana away.

**73.** There is a stream — a river named Manasa-Ganga:
west of Vrindavana, north-east of Mathura.

**74.** From the Kamyaka-vana [it flows] towards Madhuvana
and west of the Kaliya-daha enters the Yamuna.

**75.** There is also a stream named Sarasvati there:
north of Mathura it enters the Yamuna.

**76.** West of Mathura is the hill of Govardhana,
three and a half yojanas, counting from Mathura.

**77.** I shall tell of the Kamyaka-vana — west of Govardhana:
from Mathura, as people reckon, three and a half yojanas.

**78.** The forest named Bahula is north-east of Mathura,
beyond the Manasa-Ganga, at two yojanas.

**79.** These are the seven forests west of the Yamuna;
now I shall tell of the five forests on the eastern bank.

**80.** The forest named Mahavana is near the Yamuna,
one yojana's journey from Mathura.

**81.** To the west of it is the forest named Bilva,
half a yojana from Mathura, on the far bank.

**82.** To the north of it is the forest named Loha;
to the north-east of it is the forest named Bhandira.

**83.** These two forests lie together on the bank of the Yamuna;
from Mahavana, people say, three and a half yojanas.

**84.** These are the twelve forests — the Mathura-mandala;
all the places of Krishna's pastimes I will show [You]"[^20].

[^1]: Panchavati is the forest on the Godavari (near present-day Nasik) where Rama, Sita and Lakshmana lived in exile; cf. Murari 3.15.6.
[^2]: The Lord, in the mood of Rama, lives through the abduction of Sita as though it were happening now. Cf. Murari 3.16.3–4 (on the way to Setubandha).
[^3]: Shrirangam on the Kaveri; cf. Murari 3.15.7–17. In the Chaitanya-charitamrita (Madhya 9) the Lord spends the chaturmasya in the house of Venkata Bhatta, Trimalla's brother.
[^4]: Vv. 132+1 — 132+5 are found only in the 1983 edition; the 1903 edition gives them only in a footnote, as an additional reading of a printed edition. The chaturmasya is the four months of the rains (Ashadha to Ashvina), when sannyasis do not travel.
[^5]: Vv. 134–144 — cf. Murari 3.15.19–25. In the Chaitanya-charitamrita (Madhya 9) the Lord meets Paramananda Puri at the Rishabha hill after Shrirangam.
[^6]: In the 1983 edition the verse is cited as from the Vayu Purana (in A the heading of the quotation is corrupted by the OCR); the 1903 edition gives it only in a footnote, as an additional reading of a printed edition. Bhaktisiddhanta's prose rendering of the verse is not translated. "The first twilight of Kali" is the opening period of the Kali-yuga; on the Daru-brahman (Jagannatha) see en/45. Vv. 138–141 are Madhavendra Puri's words, a Bengali paraphrase of this verse.
[^7]: Vv. 142+1 — 142+4 are found only in the 1983 edition; the 1903 edition gives them only in a footnote, as an additional reading of one manuscript. Madhavendra Puri is the guru of Ishvara Puri, and so the Lord's "grandfather" in the line of gurus.
[^8]: The summary in A is badly corrupted by the OCR in places; gaps are filled from the sense and from Bhaktisiddhanta's table of contents (chapter 2 of the Shesha-khanda). In the 1983 edition chapter 2 ("The Lord Sees Vrindavana") begins later — at v. 18 (the song of Nrisimhananda); vv. 1–17 there close chapter 1.
[^9]: Vv. 4+1 — 4+2 are found only in the 1983 edition; the 1903 edition gives them only in a footnote, as an additional reading of a printed edition. Cf. Murari 3.16.1–2 (in Murari, tamala trees). According to the Chaitanya-charitamrita (Madhya 9), the Lord embraced the seven palms and sent them to Vaikuntha; these are the seven palms that Rama pierced with one arrow to convince Sugriva of his strength.
[^10]: Cf. Murari 3.16.3–6. The Lord, in the mood of Rama, calls His associates — Lakshmana, Angada, Hanuman, Sugriva, Vibhishana. Rameshvara is the linga of Shiva installed, by tradition, by Rama Himself; for Vaishnavas Shiva is the greatest of devotees.
[^11]: A second chaturmasya (on the Godavari) is peculiar to Lochana Dasa's account; according to the Chaitanya-charitamrita (Madhya 9), in the South the Lord spent one chaturmasya — at Shrirangam — and on the way back only met Ramananda again on the Godavari (cf. Murari 3.16.9–11).
[^12]: Alalanatha is Vishnu at Brahmagiri, south of Puri. Vishnudasa is in Murari (3.16.12) the brahmana with whom the Lord sees Alalanatha.
[^13]: "Attained Vaikuntha" — left this world. Nrisimhananda (Pradyumna Brahmachari) in Murari (3.17.5–8) and in the Chaitanya-charitamrita (Madhya 1.155–162) paves in his mind a road for the Lord from Bengal (from Kuliya) to Vrindavana, but cannot take it beyond Kanai's Natashala and therefore foretells that the Lord will turn back from there; in those accounts he does not die and himself tells the devotees about it. Kanai's Natashala is a village on the Ganga near Rajmahal, where the Lord, on His first attempt to go to Vrindavana, turned back.
[^14]: In Lochana Dasa the Lord turns back from Kanai's Natashala even before the journey to Mathura told next; of His route through Bengal (Ramakeli, Kuliya) Lochana Dasa says nothing here.
[^15]: Cf. the Chaitanya-charitamrita (Madhya 17): going through the forests of Jharikhanda, the Lord makes tigers and deer dance together and chant the name of Krishna. In Murari (4.01) the Lord goes from Puri through Kashi.
[^16]: Vv. 36–38 — cf. Murari 4.01–4.02.1. According to the Chaitanya-charitamrita (Madhya 19–20), the Lord meets Rupa at Prayaga and Sanatana at Kashi, on the way back from Vrindavana; Lochana Dasa places the meeting with both on the way to Mathura. "Imparted His power" (shakti-sanchara) — empowered them to reveal to the world the teaching and the lilas of Vraja.
[^17]: Vv. 39–45 — cf. Murari 4.02.2–6. The Akshaya-vata is the "imperishable banyan" at Prayaga; the Veni (Triveni) is the confluence of the Ganga, Yamuna and Sarasvati. V. 41 we understand as in Murari (4.02.5): the Yamuna "ever turned towards the forest of Vrinda."
[^18]: Akrura took Krishna away from Vraja to Mathura, and the gopis in separation blamed him. Seeing Mathura, the Lord lives through the separation of the gopis (of Radha) from Krishna.
[^19]: Vv. 48–63 — cf. Murari 4.02.9–13. The brahmana Krishnadasa, by the Lord's word and power, becomes His guide through the Mathura-mandala.
[^20]: Vv. 65–84 — cf. Murari 4.03.3–20 (the account of the twelve forests). Distances: a yojana is about 12–13 km. "Three and a half" (vv. 76, 77, 83) is the 1903 edition's reading ("আউট"); A and B read "আট" ("eight"), apparently a corruption of the same word. Only eleven forests are named; the twelfth in Murari is Bhadra.
