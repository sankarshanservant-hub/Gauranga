# Madhya-khanda, part 14 (portion 37)

*The section "Mother Shachi's Grief" (end): Dhruva's austerities in Madhuvana and his testing by the gods; Narada with Krishna in Vaikuntha; Krishna grants Dhruva the highest place; Dhruva's return and reign; Shachi wishes to go with her son as a yogini; the Lord instructs His mother, shows her the form of Krishna and promises to come whenever she calls*

*Raga sindhura; disha: "Hari, O great one! I shall take refuge at Govinda's feet: the great sweetness of those crimson feet I have only now come to know."[^1]*

**94.** Seeing Madhuvana, Dhruva was filled with joy;
keeping the pilgrim's fast, he spent the night [there].

**95.** Bathing at dawn, Dhruva repeats the mantra;
he feels neither hunger nor thirst — and is awash in tears.

**96.** Once in five or seven days he ate a single badari berry,
and after a fortnight — a drop of water touched by tulasi.

**97.** Fasting thirty days at a stretch,
Dhruva broke his fast only once a month.[^2]

**98.** Arms raised, palms joined, standing on one foot,
Dhruva repeats the twelve-syllable mantra.

**99.** [Standing] in the waters of the Kalindi, feet upward,[^3] [he practised austerity],
and in the hot season he performed tapas amid fires blazing on four sides.

**100.** In winter he lies in the waters of the Kalindi,
in the rains on an open platform: so many hardships he endures.

**101.** Meditating and meditating, Dhruva entered samadhi:
the form of Tribhanga did not leave him until he saw Krishna.[^4]

**102.** Indra and the other gods were seized with wonder:
"We do not know whose place he will take!"

**103.** Brahma says: "What if he takes my place?
Dhruva will take Brahma's seat — a remedy must be found."

**104.** Kubera and Varuna say: "He will take my place:
Krishna will give it to him — we sense it."

**105.** Indra says: "Dhruva will take my place —
Krishnachandra in His mercy will give it to him at once."

**106.** Indra says: "My place is what everyone desires —
Dhruva will take it and leave me with nothing."

**107.** All the gods say: "I sit on a high throne;
Dhruva, the great ascetic, will take my place."

**108.** To break Dhruva's fierce austerity,
Brahma and the other gods devise various schemes.

**109.** Dhruva abides [in meditation on] Tribhanga with all his mind and heart;
Brahma, with Indra and the others, went to test him.

**110.** Right at Dhruva's ear someone shouts loudly:
"You have come here to your death, Dhruva!"

**111.** Another shouts: "Dhruva, your father is dead!"
Someone shouts: "Hey, Dhruva, a black cobra is coming!"

**112.** Another shouts: "Dhruva, your mother is dead!"
Someone shouts: "Dhruva, run away quickly!"

**113.** Another shouts: "Dhruva, a forest fire is coming!"
Someone shouts: "Ah, Dhruva is dead, dead!"

**114.** Indra brought an elephant, and it drove its tusks into Dhruva's chest;
coiling its trunk, it pulls at Dhruva's tongue.[^5]

**115.** Vayu, becoming a python, swallowed Dhruva;
Surya, taking the form of a tiger, drank Dhruva's blood.

**116.** Binding Dhruva with a serpent-noose, they threw him into fire;
Chandra drowned Dhruva in the waters of the Kalindi.

**117.** He on whose tongue the name of Krishna resounds —
what can the bites of countless serpents do to him?

**118.** Unable to break his meditation on Tribhanga,
Brahma and the other gods fled.

**119.** With all his mind Dhruva meditates on the Lord's feet;
joyfully this Lochana Dasa sang of [His] qualities.

*Raga — any; disha: "I have taken refuge at the crimson feet, O Gopala, merciful to the lowly."*

**120.** Your name is Purifier of the fallen;
glory, glory, glory to the Deliverer of the lowly!

**121.** Listen, all, to another wondrous tale:
I shall tell what Narada said to Krishna.

**122.** In Vaikuntha, on a jewelled throne, [Krishna] with Kamala —
the three listen to Narada's song to the vina.[^6]

**123.** With a gentle smile Krishna says to Narada:
"Why today is your mind not given to your song to the vina?"

**124.** Narada says: "Listen, Lotus-eyed One,
why my mind does not stay with my song to the vina:

**125.** Your devotee has stolen my mind.
You, Lord, at all times are [the refuge] of those who have nothing [but You] in their hearts."[^7]

**126.** Hearing Narada's words, the Lotus-eyed One [says]:
"Tell Me, which devotee is remembering [Me]?"

**127.** "The son of Uttanapada, [a boy] of great mind;
his mother Suniti is out of favour with her husband.

**128.** Dhruva's stepmother is named Suruchi;
she sits merrily with her husband on the throne.

**129.** The seven brothers, beginning with Uttama, with father and mother,
laugh and play merrily on the jewelled throne.

**130.** [Dhruva] saw the seven brothers on their father's lap —
and seeing this, Dhruva climbs onto the jewelled throne.

**131.** When he climbed the throne to get onto his father's lap,
Dhruva's stepmother pushed him down to the ground.

**132.** Falling to the ground, Dhruva began to weep;
and the king, in his wife's power, said nothing.

**133.** By his stepmother's words Dhruva fell into distress —
and [now] he performs austerities in Madhuvana, near the Kalindi."

**134.** Hearing Narada's words, the Lotus-eyed One
with a gentle smile speaks sweet words:

**135.** "To the uninitiated I do not show mercy:
the sins of the uninitiated I do not remove.[^8]

**136.** [But he] who has vowed to meditate on Me
is performing austerities in Madhuvana, having left mother and father.

**137.** From a Vaishnavi's womb a non-Vaishnava is never born;
when [the son] is a Vaishnava and [the mother] a Vaishnavi, all sorrow burns away.

**138.** The vow of a Vaishnava I shall surely fulfil:
whatever boon Dhruva desires, that boon I shall give.

**139.** My devotees have bound Me with the rope of prema and bhakti:
when a devotee remembers [Me], I cannot stay [away]."

**140.** Narada says: "Dhruva is not without initiation;
go and show him mercy — he is burning as in a forest fire."

**141.** Hearing [this] from Narada's lips, the Lotus-eyed One
came, riding on Garuda, to that Madhuvana.

**142.** With a gentle smile Krishna said to Dhruva:
"I have left Vaikuntha and come to give you a boon."

**143.** Hearing Sri Krishna's word, [Dhruva] was filled with joy;
coming out of meditation, he stood before Him with joined palms.

**144.** Dhruva says: "Great Lord, what boon shall I ask?
Be merciful to me — by that Your glory will grow."

**145.** The Lord says: "Your wish I shall surely fulfil:
whatever place you desire, that place I shall give.

**146.** Tell Me now, why have you come to Madhuvana?
[Is it because] your stepmother would not let you sit on the throne?

**147.** If I do not give you the very highest place,
how shall I bear the name 'Tree of Desires'?"

**148.** Dhruva says: "A high place I regard as a straw:
without devotion to You all is a heap of ashes."[^9]

**149.** Krishna says: "I shall give you [what is above] all thrones:
go and abide in the highest place in the three worlds.

**150.** Son of Uttanapada, you will be king;
all your subjects will come to know My glory.

**151.** Above all is the circle of the sages' abodes; [above it]
Dhruvaloka will stand" — so He told of everything.[^10]

**152.** Having given this boon, Krishna disappeared,
and Vishvakarma built Dhruvaloka.

**153.** Having received this boon, Dhruva set out;
joyfully this Lochana Dasa sings of Gora's qualities.

*Raga — any; disha: "Come, O Gaura-Gopala, my life!"*

**154.** Hearing Krishna's command, Dhruva set out for his homeland;
and here Uttanapada's detachment from the world grew.

**155.** Dhruva's stepmother weeps: "Where has Dhruva gone?
I, wretched one, pushed the son [off the throne]!"

**156.** The king says: "It was my fate to destroy my son;
when shall I see Dhruva again?"

**157.** The king says: "Mother of Dhruva, you are the chief queen;
from today all your co-wives are your maidservants."

**158.** Not seeing his son, the king, distraught,
lies senseless on the ground.

**159.** Suddenly he saw Narada —
the maharaja rose, anxious at heart.

**160.** Offering water for the feet and arghya, he gave [Narada] a seat
and began to tell him what was in his heart:

**161.** "I had a five-year-old boy —
I do not know where that boy has gone."

**162.** Narada says: "Many troubles befell Dhruva;
having gained devotion to Krishna, he has come near [your] land.

*Thus [in the scriptures]:*

**163.** Holy is the family, fulfilled is the mother, blessed is the earth and the place where she dwells, blessed too are her forefathers abiding in heaven — [that mother] whose son is known in the world as a Vaishnava.

**164.** She whose son is a Vaishnava is truly called the mother of a son; but the mother of a hundred non-Vaishnava sons is like a sow.[^11]

**165.** In whose line even one Vaishnava is born,
[he] delivers the families of father, mother and father-in-law.

**166.** Your boy, having served Sri Krishna, is returning:
know that Dhruva is the ornament of your line."

**167.** At Narada's words the king, filled with joy,
sprinkled the royal road with chuya and sandal.

**168.** Curd, auspicious durva grass, kunkuma, musk,
delicate bright flowers; lamps burn in rows.

**169.** In search of his lost [son] the king hastens to meet him;
having gone some distance, he finds Dhruva.

**170.** Seeing Dhruva, the king seemed to regain his life;
covering his son with countless kisses, he took him in his arms.

**171.** Bringing Dhruva back, he made him king before all
and from hand to hand gave over to him the ministers and subjects.

**172.** Giving the kingdom to Dhruva, the king went to the forest;
[and Dhruva] ruled for a long time with a joyful heart.

**173.** By might and power he conquered many lands one after another
and ruled forty years free of enemies.[^12]

**174.** Having shown much valour among the gods and gandharvas,
Dhruva went with his mother to Dhruvapuri."

**175.** Shachi says: "I too shall go with you,
I shall stay with you — in the joy of talks about Krishna.

**176.** If such a golden son as you goes away with a shaven head,
I too shall shave my head and become a shaven-headed woman.

**177.** I shall put on red clothes, put kundala earrings in my ears,
become a yogini and go with you."[^13]

**178.** At His mother's words the Lord was at a loss
and began to think: "How shall I console her?"

**179.** The crest-jewel of the all-knowing, Shachi's son,
consoles His mother, [sings] this Lochana Dasa.

*Raga varadi; disha: "This wondrous tale is called 'a blessing to the ear': listen to the song of Gora's qualities, Shachi's darling moon!"*

**180.** "Do not be troubled, listen to My words:
why do you needlessly torment your heart with grief?

**181.** Again and again I tell you — and you do not heed:
in vain you [give yourself to] greed, delusion, anger and pride.

**182.** Who are you, who is your son, who is whose father?
In vain you lament, saying 'yours' and 'mine'.

**183.** What is woman, what is man, who is whose husband? —
apart from Sri Krishna's feet there is no other refuge.

**184.** He is father, He is mother, He is friend and kin;
He is the destroyer, He the creator, He the only wealth.

**185.** Without Him all is empty — this truth I have told you:
without Him empty is all there is in the world.[^14]

**186.** All people are firmly bound by the bonds of Vishnu's maya
and suffer only from their own intoxication and conceit.

**187.** Whatever deeds one does, thinking, 'This is good for me' —
those very dharmas hold one captive in the hereafter.

**188.** Captive on the thread of karma, one wanders and roams;
the fool, forgetting Krishna, does not know himself.

**189.** Among the fourteen worlds birth as a human being
is known to be rare — this secret I have revealed to you.

**190.** The fruits of sense enjoyment in this [world] are countless,
and this transient samsara itself crumbles in a moment.

**191.** And yet the human body is known to be rare
[for one] who serves Krishna, standing firm against maya.

**192.** All these bodies are only for the service of Sri Krishna;
he is freed from bondage who loves Krishna.

**193.** With what great tenderness you love Me as a son! —
if [that love] were given to Sri Krishna's feet, how great would be the gain!

**194.** Attachment to samsara [leads only] to death,
but attachment to Sri Krishna — to crossing [the ocean of] birth.

**195.** He alone is the highest friend, he alone mother and father,
who gives prema-bhakti to Sri Krishna's feet.

**196.** My heart is in anguish in separation from Krishna;
falling at your feet, I humbly answer you.

**197.** You have given Me boundless love;
by your command I purify My heart.

**198.** My deliverance is your salvation too:
serve Sri Krishna's feet, give up the thought 'he is my son'.

**199.** I shall take sannyasa for the sake of prema for Krishna —
and from distant lands I shall bring and give you the wealth of prema.

**200.** Other people's sons bring silver and gold:
spend it, and it is gone; this is not the highest dharma.

**201.** They earn wealth and bring it with great hardship —
and then either the wealth goes, or [the earner] himself dies.

**202.** But I shall bring you such wealth as prema for Krishna:
Krishna's feet are the sum of all treasures.

**203.** Prema is imperishable in this world and the next.
Give Me leave, mother, and calm [the grief] in your heart.[^15]

**204.** Father and mother everyone obtains in every birth,
but Krishna and the guru are not obtained — understand this in your heart.

**205.** Only in human birth can all come to know Krishna and the guru;
one who does not accept a guru I regard as a beast or a bird."

**206.** Hearing this, Shachi-devi, wonder-struck at heart,
gazes unblinking at Vishvambhara's lotus face.

**207.** The Lord of the fourteen worlds removed maya [from her]:
Shachi sees all beings as alike, equal.

**208.** At that very moment she saw Krishna in Vishvambhara:
maya — [the thought] 'this is my son' — went away.[^16]

**209.** His body dark, outshining a fresh cloud,
thrice-bent, holding the flute, in splendid yellow garments —

**210.** with cowherds, gopis and cowherd boys in Vrindavana
she then saw her own son — and stood amazed.

**211.** Seeing this, Shachi was astonished at heart;
her body thrilled, its hairs on end, her whole frame trembling.

**212.** [Yet] in her tenderness Shachi does not give up her own relation [to Him]:
"Krishna has become my son — so my good fortune has decreed.

**213.** Krishna, unattainable for the world, is my son;
He is subject to no one's control — what can my strength do?"

**214.** Reasoning thus, Shachi spoke:
"You are the independent Lord, the jewel among men.

**215.** By my good fortune You were so long in my power —
now, as it pleases You, take sannyasa.

**216.** I have one request of You:
why is such a treasure of mine going away?"

**217.** Saying this, she spoke in a piteous voice;
the tears streamed from her eyes.

**218.** Sobbing and sobbing weeps virtuous Shachi;
at His mother's weeping the Lord bowed His head.

**219.** Then, raising His face again, Vishvambhara says:
"Listen, mother, to My answer:

**220.** on any day when you long with love to see Me —
at that very moment you will be able to see Me."[^17]

**221.** Hearing these words, Shachi holds back her tears;
with an aching heart [this] was told by this Lochana Dasa.

[^1]: The disha is corrupt in A (OCR: "হরিএ মহাশয় … ও-রাজ।চ্ণের অনেক মাতরী এলে নে জানিলুং") and is restored from the 1903 edition (C). Vv. 94–179 are absent from the 1983 edition (B), and in C they stand in the same footnote as the beginning of the story of Dhruva (see en/36, note 8): apparently a later insertion.
[^2]: Lit. "performs parana (the breaking of the fast) once a month". The line is corrupt in A, restored from C.
[^3]: So in A and C. Lochana Dasa's account of Dhruva's austerities does not follow the Bhagavata (4.8.71–76), where Dhruva gradually gives up fruits, leaves, water and finally breath, standing on one foot.
[^4]: Tribhanga ("thrice-bent") is Krishna of Vrindavana with the flute. In Lochana Dasa Dhruva meditates on this form, while the Lord comes to him from Vaikuntha on Garuda (vv. 141–142); in the Bhagavata Narada teaches Dhruva to meditate on the four-armed form (4.8.44–52). Lochana Dasa calls the Lord of Vaikuntha Krishna: Krishna is the source of all His manifestations.
[^5]: The tongue that repeats the mantra (cf. v. 117). The gods' trials are Lochana Dasa's detail; in the Bhagavata the gods only suffocate from Dhruva's austerity and beg the Lord for protection (4.8.80–82).
[^6]: Kamala is Lakshmi. The third listener is not named — apparently Bhu-devi, Narayana's other consort.
[^7]: The line is obscure ("মনের দরিদ্র নাথ তুমি সর্বকাল", lit. "of the poor in mind Lord You are at all times"); translated by the probable sense: the Lord is the treasure of the humble, who have nothing but Him.
[^8]: The second line is corrupt in A and C (A: "অপরাত নহি ঘন", C: "অপরাধ নাহি ধার"); translated by sense. The thought concerns the importance of diksha: Narada answers (v. 140) that Dhruva is initiated (he himself gave him the mantra, en/36, vv. 84–90).
[^9]: Cf. Bhagavata 4.9.35: there Dhruva only after seeing the Lord laments that he desired what is transient; in Lochana Dasa he at once places devotion above every station.
[^10]: Dhruvaloka is the Pole Star, above the circle of the seven rishis (the Great Bear); cf. Bhagavata 4.9.20–25.
[^11]: Two Sanskrit shlokas; Bhaktisiddhanta adds a prose rendering of them (not translated). The second shloka is garbled by the OCR of A; restored from C and from Bhaktisiddhanta's rendering ("she whose son is a Vaishnava is truly the mother of a son").
[^12]: In the Bhagavata Dhruva reigns thirty-six thousand years (4.12.13).
[^13]: Vv. 175–179 are absent in B; in C they stand in the same footnote as the story of Dhruva. In place of en/36, v. 35 — en/37, v. 179 B has only a signature couplet (en/36, v. 34+1). Red (saffron) clothes and earrings are the garb of wandering yogis. Shachi's words are an outburst of a mother's prema: she cannot conceive of life apart from her son.
[^14]: "Michha" (lit. "false") here means "empty, vain": whatever is unconnected with Krishna brings no good. This is not the mayavada doctrine of the world's unreality: the world is real but transient (v. 190), and what is true in it is service to Krishna.
[^15]: The word before "মা" ("mother") is unclear (A: "নেদলী", C: "বেধনী", B: "के दो"); translated by sense.
[^16]: Maya here is that which hides from Shachi her son's divinity: it is yogamaya, which nourishes her motherly love (vatsalya), not a veil of ignorance — Shachi is the Lord's eternal mother (cf. en/36, note 6). The Lord lifts this covering for a moment, but, as v. 212 says, even after the vision Shachi does not give up her motherly feeling. Cf. Yashoda seeing the universe in Krishna's mouth (Bhagavata 10.8.43–45).
[^17]: Cf. the Lord's promise to the devotees (en/36, v. 155) and to Srivasa (Murari 2.18.23).
