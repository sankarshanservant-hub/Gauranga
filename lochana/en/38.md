# Madhya-khanda, part 15 (portion 38)

*The section "Vishnupriya's Lament": Vishnupriya learns of the coming sannyasa and begs the Lord not to leave; the Lord consoles her, instructs her and shows her His four-armed form; Srivasa asks to be taken along; in Murari's house the Lord tells him to worship Advaita. The section "The Consolation of the Devotees" (only in the 1983 edition): the last days at home and the last night with Vishnupriya*

## Vishnupriya's Lament

> *Summary by Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati.* [When mother Shachi could not tell] her of this, Vishnupriya-devi, beside herself with grief, wept unceasingly, and [the Lord] asked her the reason for her tears. Vishnupriya-devi laid her grief at the Lord's feet; [He], [learning] the cause of her grief, consoled her with many sweet words and at last, giving her full instruction in the truth — [that it is vain] to see one's husband in a body of blood and flesh [and that the true Lord of the soul's life is Sri Hari], — showed Vishnupriya-devi His four-armed form of Narayana. Then Srivasa, Murari and other devotees, hearing in anxiety the news that the Lord would take sannyasa, came to Him, poured out their grief and asked to go with the Lord; Gaurasundara, giving them instruction in the truth, consoled them.[^1]

*Raga varadi, the "dhulakhela" tune*

**1.** Then queen Shachi-devi [wishes] to tell what is in her heart —
her face is wan with grief;
but no word comes from her lips, tears stream from her eyes —
seeing this, Vishnupriya fell senseless.

**2.** She cannot ask; deep in her heart is pain;
she had heard people's whispers —
and by hints understood the matter: a thunderbolt fell out of season,
and the poor one lost her senses.

**3.** Vishnupriya ponders in her heart. At the close of day the Lord
came home in haste;
having eaten and drunk, He lay down contentedly on His bed —
and Vishnupriya hurried [to Him].

**4.** Sighing, she sits by His lotus feet
and gazes [at Him] with a sorrowful face;
laying on her bosom the feet of her beloved Lord of life,
she binds them with her creeper-arms.

**5.** Tears flow from both her eyes, the cloth over her bosom is soaked,
streams run down [the Lord's] feet.
Feeling it, the Lord suddenly rises
and asks His beloved what is wrong.[^2]

**6.** "You are dear to Me as life — why do you weep? I want to know:
answer Me, beloved."
Setting her on His lap, taking her chin in His right hand,
He asks in sweet words.

**7.** Vishnupriya-devi weeps, her heart is bursting;
when asked, she says not a word;
life smoulders within her, her body is senseless —
only tears stream from her eyes.

**8.** Again and again the Lord asks — and still she does not answer,
but only weeps, clasping His feet.
The Lord, who knows every art, asks in many ways,
wiping her face with the edge of His cloth.

**9.** He begins one playful talk after another, awakening feeling in her —
at such words even a stone would blossom.
Seeing the Lord's eagerness, moon-faced Vishnupriya
spoke in a faltering voice:

**10.** "Listen, listen, Lord of my life, put Your hand on my head [and tell the truth]:
are You really going to take sannyasa?
Hearing it from people, I feel my heart will burst —
I shall enter fire.

**11.** For You are my life and wealth, my beauty and youth,
my dress, my graces, feelings and arts;
when You go away, what use is this wretched life? —
my heart burns as with poison.[^3]

**12.** Shame on my body! One thing I ask You:
how will You walk the roads on foot?
Your feet are tender as shirisha flowers —
my hand is afraid to touch them.

**13.** When You stand on the ground, my heart quakes with fear
and my whole body shudders;
in the forests, among thorns, where will You go, to what lands?
How will You walk on Your crimson feet?

**14.** On Your nectar moon-face drops of sweat appear
at the slightest effort;
in the rainy season — now downpour, now scorching heat:
sannyasa will bring [You] great suffering.

**15.** Apart from Your feet I know nothing —
to whom will You leave me?
Have You no fear of [breaking] dharma? Shachi is old and half-dead —
how will You leave such a mother?

**16.** Murari, Mukunda Datta — such devotees,
Srinivasa and Haridasa,
Advaita Acharya and the others — leaving them, what will You achieve?
How can You take sannyasa?

**17.** You, Lord, are a treasury of prema — so the whole world thinks —
yet Your deeds and intentions are the opposite.
You will go to far lands — hearing it, everyone will die,
and all will be covered with disgrace.

**18.** What shall I, worthless one, say? If I am the root of Your household life
and You take sannyasa because of me —
let me take Your misfortunes upon myself and die by taking poison,[^4]
and You live happily in Your own home.

**19.** Lord, do not go to far lands: I have no one in this world;
when I look at Your face my heart burns."
She can say no more — deep in her heart is pain;
she only weeps, clasping His feet.

**20.** Hearing Vishnupriya's words, then that jewel Gauranga
smiling, took her onto His lap;
wiping her face with His cloth, He jests in many ways
and says: "Do not dwell on needless grief.

**21.** That I will leave you and go to take sannyasa —
who ever told you that?
Whatever I do, when I do it, I shall tell you then —
but now do not die of needless sorrow."

**22.** Saying this, Gaurahari embraces and kisses her
in many sweet and playful pastimes;
endless are the delights of His prema, He is the limit of grace in lila —
so in every way He comforted Vishnupriya.[^5]

**23.** In joyful play the night came to an end,
and Vishnupriya asks again:
there is fire in her heart — that is why she asks again,
gazing at the face of her beloved Lord of life.

**24.** Pressing the Lord's hand to her breast, Vishnupriya-devi asks:
"Do not lie to me for fear of me.
This is what I think: all You say is a trick —
You will run away without my knowing.

**25.** You, Lord, are Your own master, never in another's power,
and You do what pleases You.
If You will take sannyasa — what can I say?
[Only] tell me the truth."

**26.** Hearing these words, the Lord smiled softly
and says: "Listen, My beloved,
do not grieve at all in your heart: what I say for your good
listen to attentively.

**27.** All that you see in the world count as empty:
Bhagavan alone is true,
and true are the Vaishnavas; all else besides them
count as empty.[^6]

**28.** Empty are son, husband, wife, father, mother — all that goes by such names:
in the end, who belongs to whom?
Apart from Sri Krishna's feet there is no other kin;
all that you see is His maya.

**29.** Whether woman or man — see: the soul of all is one [in nature];
only in the bonds of maya does one imagine 'two'.
Sri Krishna is the husband of all, and all others are prakriti:
this no one understands.[^7]

**30.** From the union of blood and seed a person is born in a place of filth
and falls to the ground knowing nothing;
as a child, a youth, an old man, he suffers many miseries —
and takes pride in his body and home.

**31.** Those he raised as his own all revile him;
in pride he lives out his old age;
hearing and sight grow dim, he weeps thinking of his woes —
and still he does not serve Govinda.

**32.** He took a body in this world in order to serve Krishna —
yet in the bonds of maya he forgets himself;
drunk with conceit, forgetting his own Lord,
in the end he dies in the torments of hell.

**33.** Your name is Vishnupriya ('dear to Vishnu'): make it meaningful,
do not keep needless grief in your heart.
I have told you this — put away other thoughts
and give your mind to the deeds of Krishna."

**34.** Being Himself the Lord, He removed His own maya,[^8] —
and Vishnupriya's heart grew clear:
grief and sorrow went away, her breast filled with joy —
and suddenly she sees [Him] four-armed.

**35.** Yet even seeing the four-armed form,
Vishnupriya-devi does not give up thinking of Him as her husband;
falling at His feet, she bows and pleads:
"Hear, Lord, one request of mine.

**36.** I, the lowest and most worthless, was born in this world,
and You are my beloved husband, the Lord of my life;
such was my good fortune — I had become Your maidservant —
why then [now] such a fall?"

**37.** Saying this, Vishnupriya wept aloud,
and her distress grew greater still.
Seeing His beloved's anguish, the Lord, His eyes brimming with tears,
took her in His arms and bestowed His grace:

**38.** "Listen, Vishnupriya-devi, this is what I tell you:
whenever you think of Me —
wherever I may go, I am with you;
truly, truly I tell you firmly."[^9]

**39.** Hearing the word of Krishna's command, Vishnupriya thought:
"This Lord is the independent Master,
He does what pleases Him — who can stand in His way?" —
and she made no reply.

**40.** Vishnupriya lowered her face, her eyes brimming with tears;
seeing this, the Lord speaks tender words to her.
It is painful to hear of the Lord's deeds —
this Lochana Dasa sings of [His] qualities.

*Raga varadi; disha: "My life, O moon of the twice-born! The sweetness of the form of Gora, who enchants Madana, ever wakes [in my heart] — I would take that form's misfortunes on myself and die"*

**41.** Thus, in surmises, days and nights go by —
as if a fire had flared up in everyone's heart.

**42.** All the devotees, gathering together,
speak of Gora's qualities, weeping in the depths of their souls.

**43.** Shachi and Vishnupriya both weep day and night:
darkness on every side, all seems empty.

**44.** Townsfolk and household find no peace:
all wander restlessly about the town.

**45.** At this time Srinivasa, king of brahmanas,
with an aching heart asks the Lord:

**46.** "I have one request — I am afraid to say it:
if You permit, Lord, I shall go with You.

**47.** And whoever else can — let him go with You too:
not seeing You, no one will stay alive.

**48.** I shall die first, listen, Vishvambhara" —
and he laid open to [the Lord] what was in his heart.

**49.** Hearing this, the Lord laughed softly:
"Listen, Srinivasa, to what I say.

**50.** Do not fear separation from Me:
I shall never leave you all.

**51.** And especially in your house, in Krishna's shrine,
I abide always: set your mind at rest."[^10]

**52.** Having cheered him with words of comfort,
in the evening He went to the house of Murari Gupta.

**53.** Taking Haridasa with Him, [He came] to Murari's house
and speaks to him in private in the Deity's room:

**54.** "Listen, Murari, to My words:
you are dear to Me as life — that is why I speak.

**55.** I shall tell you something excellent — listen attentively:
I give you this instruction for your own good.

**56.** Advaita Acharya Gosani is blessed in the three worlds;
I have no other friend closer than He.

**57.** He Himself is a part of the Lord, the guru of all:[^11]
whoever wishes his own good, let him serve Him.

**58.** He is the benefactor of the world, the king of Vaishnavas:
worship Him with the highest devotion.

**59.** When He receives worship, Krishna receives it.
I have told you this in private — keep it in your heart.

**60.** I and Gadadhara Pandita Gosani,
Nityananda, Advaita, Srivasa, Ramai —

**61.** know that My body is one with all of them.[^12]
I have opened My heart to you — keep it in your heart."

**62.** Hearing this, Murari, king of physicians,
understood in his heart what the Lord intended in His.

**63.** Weeping, he fell at the Lord's feet:
he understood that the Lord would surely take sannyasa.

**64.** Bowing at Haridasa's feet,
he surrenders himself [to the Lord] with boundless humility.

**65.** The Lord cannot bear to hear Murari weep:
He hastily rose and went home.

**66.** He consoled Murari with these words:
"I am always near you.

**67.** My sannyasa is still some time off;
and what I have told you will be your support in the end."[^13]

**68.** Saying this, the Lord goes home;
with an aching heart Lochana sings of it.

## The Consolation of the Devotees

*Raga karunashri; disha: "Ah, alas, alas! The Lord at whose remembrance sorrow ends — ah, alas! If He goes, I shall die, O Gauranga; whose face shall I look upon then, O Gauranga?"*

**68+1.** The Lord spends the night with joy in His heart[^14]
and increases [His] love [for all] even more than before.

**68+2.** Knowing His mother's heart, He gladdens her
with such words as set her heart at ease.

**68+3.** With townsfolk and household — with each as is fitting —
thus He shows love to all.

**68+4.** Laying aside [His] absorption in renunciation,
the Lord reveals His love in every house.

**68+5.** In one house He jests and laughs —
He finds the way to each heart and charms everyone.

**68+6.** Those who had been [ready] in secret, in disguise, to go with Him —
by the power of [His] maya returned home.[^15]

**68+7.** He wears various ornaments, sandal paste on His holy body,
and every moment He is full of laughter, play and rasa.

**68+8.** Everyone concluded there would be no sannyasa,
and all the people [went] home at ease.[^16]

**68+9.** The Lord lay down in His chamber,
and Vishnupriya came with a bunch of betel in her hand.

**68+10.** Smiling with delight, the Lord says, "Come, come!" —
and with great tenderness seated her on His lap.

**68+11.** Vishnupriya anointed the Lord's body with sandal
and drew a tilaka with fragrant aguru and musk,

**68+12.** put a divine garland of malati on Gora's body
and with tender jests placed betel in His holy mouth.

**68+13.** Then Mahaprabhu, the crown of connoisseurs of rasa,
Himself began to adorn Vishnupriya.

**68+14.** Her long hair, whose lustre surpasses Kama's whisk,
He braided and decked with malati flowers —

**68+15.** as if the crescent moon had captured a cloud —
or is [the moon] swallowing it and giving it back? — one cannot tell.

**68+16.** On her lovely forehead He placed a dot of sindura —
as if the moon had taken the sun onto its lap.

**68+17.** And around the sindura, dots of sandal,
as if a host [of stars] had rushed to see the sun in the moon's lap.[^17]

**68+18.** On her eyes, restless as wagtails, He drew lines of collyrium
and made her brows the string of Kama's bow.

**68+19.** He anointed her bosom with fragrant aguru and musk
and fitted her a bodice of divine cloth.

**68+20.** He adorned her with various ornaments —
and plays [with her] endlessly, laughing and offering betel.

**68+21.** He gazes at her face, whose beauty is a wonder of the three worlds,
and lovingly kisses her lips, red as the bandhuli flower.

**68+22.** Now He enfolds her in His creeper-arms and embraces her —
like a young lotus in the trunk of a lordly elephant.

**68+23.** Many rasas the playful Nagara unfolds —
what of others: this is beyond the reach of Kama.[^18]

**68+24.** Like lightning flashing in the lap of Sumeru:
Madana himself is bewildered seeing this play of love.

**68+25.** He holds her on His breast and does not lay her on the bed;
they cannot turn from each other — the two are as one being.

**68+26.** Breast to breast, face to face they pass the night;
in the languor of rasa both fall blissfully asleep.

**68+27.** At the end of the night the Lord rose quickly;
Vishnupriya sleeps a deep sleep.

**68+28.** At the time of renunciation His prema surges higher still;
with the thought "I shall take sannyasa" His heart is as if maddened.

**68+29.** Why at such a time He unfolds play, rasa and bhava —
hear the reason, and what is gained by it.

**68+30.** As one serves, so the Lord responds to him:
He never repays service with more or with less.[^19]

**68+31.** But here there is a special distinction according to [the devotees'] qualification:
devotion is without guile and with guile, with knowledge and with detachment.[^20]

**68+32.** Without bhakti no one can serve Krishna;
[the highest] bhakti is that which is without guile and unwavering.

**68+33.** And when prema-bhakti is without anuraga,
no one can capture Krishna.

**68+34.** Such is Thakura Gaura, the ocean of mercy:
the friend of the lowly, He Himself begs for prema with anuraga.

**68+35.** In mercy He reveals His own anuraga,
so that in separation her bhava may grow in her heart.

**68+36.** "Whoever sees My body with bhava —
with him My bhava is never broken."

**68+37.** Who else is such a treasury of mercy,
who gives prema and anuraga so as to bind His very Self?

**68+38.** This is why [He showed] grace to Vishnupriya —
knowing this, let no one go astray in mind.[^21]

**68+39.** This prema-bhakti the Lord will reveal —
with joy in his heart says this Lochana Dasa.

[^1]: The OCR of the summary is badly garbled; gaps are filled by sense and from the table of contents in the edition's preface ("Vishnupriya-devi's grief; the Lord consoles her with many sweet words and, after instructing her in the truth, shows the four-armed form of Narayana; the coming of Srinivasa, Murari and other devotees; the Lord consoles them"). Before v. 1 B (the 1983 edition) has the disha "Gauranga, oh, why did You come to Nadia? O Gauranga, Gauranga!"; A lacks it.
[^2]: The end of the line is unclear (A and B: "অভিপারা"); translated by sense.
[^3]: After this stanza B has one more ("There is no other young woman as fortunate as I: You are my beloved Lord of life…"); Bhaktisiddhanta's edition lacks it. From here B's numbering is one higher than A's.
[^4]: Lit. "taking Your nichhani" — the rite in which a lover takes the beloved's misfortunes upon herself. The beginning of the stanza is corrupt in A and B; restored by sense.
[^5]: Vishnupriya is the Lord's eternal consort, His Bhu-shakti (en/21, note 9); her pastimes with the Lord are not mundane (en/35, note 8).
[^6]: "Empty" (মিছা, "false") in the sense of vain, transient; not the mayavada doctrine of the world's unreality (cf. en/37, note 14).
[^7]: Not monism: souls are one in nature (all are the Lord's energy, His prakriti) and in themselves neither male nor female; the imagined "two" is identification with the body. The one Purusha, the enjoyer, is Krishna.
[^8]: That is, the yogamaya that hid His divinity from Vishnupriya (cf. en/37, note 16).
[^9]: Cf. the promise to Shachi (en/37, v. 220).
[^10]: Cf. en/36, v. 155; Murari 2.18.19–23.
[^11]: Advaita is Maha-Vishnu (en/27); cf. Murari 2.18.29: "a part of the Lord". Vv. 52–61 — cf. Murari 2.18.25–30.
[^12]: Ramai is Sri Rama Pandita, Srivasa's brother. In the 1903 edition vv. 60–61 are bracketed as the reading of some manuscripts.
[^13]: In A v. 63 is misprinted "৬২", and the numbers of vv. 47 and 50 are not printed; restored from the neighbours. V. 66 is corrupt in A and restored from B and C.
[^14]: This section is not in Bhaktisiddhanta's edition (1922); it is translated from Haridasa Shastri's edition (1983), where it comes under the heading "The Consolation of the Devotees" (भक्तगणे प्रबोध) right after v. 68. The 1903 edition has it in the main text (some couplets bracketed as readings of some manuscripts). Bhaktisiddhanta apparently deliberately left out this description of the Lord's night with Vishnupriya, written in the spirit of nagari-bhava (cf. en/09, note 9): Gaudiya Vaishnavas, following Vrindavana Dasa, see in the Gaura of Navadvipa a model of the strict propriety of a brahmana householder. We translate it under the rule on the additions of the 1983 edition; Lochana Dasa himself explains the meaning of this pastime in vv. 68+29–68+38.
[^15]: Maya here is the Lord's lila-shakti (yogamaya), which conceals His intention for the sake of the lila.
[^16]: So in C ("না হবে সন্ন্যাস"); B has "লহিব সন্ন্যাস" ("will take sannyasa"), which contradicts the sense.
[^17]: The forehead is the moon, the dot of sindura the sun, the dots of sandal the stars. The end of the line is corrupt in B ("রাশি কোলে"); one manuscript of the 1903 edition has "like stars in the lap of the sun".
[^18]: The reading is unclear (B: "আহক অনের কাজ", C: "অস্ুক আনের কাখ"); the sense is that what in others would be a matter of lust is here "beyond the reach of Kama": the love of the Lord and His shakti transcends mundane desire (cf. v. 68+38).
[^19]: Cf. Bhagavad-gita 4.11.
[^20]: The couplet is obscure ("অমায়া সমায়া ভক্তি সবেদ নির্বেদ"; C: "এ বেদ নির্বেদ"); translated by the probable sense.
[^21]: That is, let no one think that the Lord's night with Vishnupriya before sannyasa is mundane love: it is a grace by which the Lord deepens her prema in separation (v. 68+35).
