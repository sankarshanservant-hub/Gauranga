# Madhya-khanda, part 20 (portion 43)

*The section "The Lord's Journey to Nilachala; the Broken Staff" (end): the Lord walks with His companions to Nilachala absorbed in prema, singing the verse "Rama Raghava"; toll-collectors release the pilgrims; Nityananda breaks the Lord's staff and answers His anger*

**81.** Having taken leave of all, the Thakura set out —
and the whole town of Navadvipa was left empty.[^1]

**82.** Pandita Sri Gadadhara, the royal Avadhuta,
Narahari and a few others go with Him;

**83.** Srinivasa, Murari, Mukunda, Damodara —
with these His own people the Lord set out.[^2]

**84.** "I shall see Jagannatha at the Dola festival," He thought,
and the Lord set off in haste, crying "Hari! Hari!"[^3]

**85.** Overwhelmed with prema, the Lord walks along the road;
His body sways, He cannot walk.

**86.** Now He rushes swiftly with the might of a lion,
now He roars aloud and calls out the name of Hari;

**87.** now He dances, now sings, now weeps piteously,
now in the madness of prema He slaps His arms.

**88.** From His reddish eyes streams of tears flow without cease,
and the hair on His whole body stands on end.

**89.** At times He walks slowly and says unearthly things,
at times He stands still and laughs aloud.

**90.** If ever food is brought to Him,
He says, "This is not offered [to Krishna]," and takes nothing.

**91.** Only with great effort [is He persuaded] to accept alms — once in two or three [days]:
out of mercy for people He shows them a lesson.[^4]

**92.** All night He stays awake and chants the name of Hari;
aloud He recites this verse, the abode of virtues.

*Thus:*

**93.** "Rama, Raghava, Rama, Raghava, Rama, Raghava, protect me!
Krishna, Keshava, Krishna, Keshava, Krishna, Keshava, save me!"[^5]

**94.** This verse the Lord sings in the sweetest voice —
softly, in faltering speech, in the bliss of prema.

**95.** Pilgrims going to see Jagannatha at the Dola festival
walk along with the Lord with joy in their hearts.

**96.** Once, at one place, a crowd of pilgrims
was held up on the road by a toll-collector, sinful and cruel.[^6]

**97.** He was giving them much torment and grief.
The Lord, who had gone ahead, quickly turns back.

**98.** The Avadhuta and Gadadhara Pandita are amazed:
"Why has the Lord turned back again?"

**99.** Wondering, they follow behind Him
and a little way off they see: the toll-collector has bound the pilgrims.

**100.** Seeing the reason, they started;
their hair stood on end and they were filled with joy.

**101.** Seeing the pilgrims, the Lord's face grew sad,
and He hurried forward with the gait of an enraged lion.

**102.** Seeing the Lord, the pilgrims weep aloud —
like a frightened child that has reached its mother's lap,

**103.** like wretched forest creatures scorched by a forest fire
that, tormented, plunge into the waters of the Jahnavi.

**104.** The pilgrims weep, fallen at the Lord's feet;
seeing this, the sinful toll-collector thinks to himself:

**105.** "There is no such man in the world!"
He understood in his heart: this is the Moon of Nilachala.[^7]

**106.** "I have given them all so much grief —
who knows what He will do? My heart trembles with fear."

**107.** Thinking thus, that great toll-collector
fell at the Lord's feet and speaks piteous words:

**108.** "I have released the pilgrims, I shall not take the toll:
I have understood in my heart, Lord — You are Bhagavan."

**109.** Saying this, he weeps, fallen at His feet,
and [the Lord] placed His lotus feet upon his head.

**110.** Trembling, in a choked voice he offers many praises:
"Do not despise me because I am a worldly man."

**111.** Hearing this, the Lord smiled faintly
and walks on happily, having set the pilgrims free.

**112.** Just then, a little way off, another toll-collector
comes calling out to them with his hand raised.

**113.** Seeing him, the Thakura raised His arm in reply —
and at that sign the toll-collector stopped where he was.

**114.** Tears stream from his eyes, his hair stands on end;
ceaselessly he chants the name "Hare Krishna."

**115.** Seeing the jubilation of Nityananda and Gadadhara,
Lochana Dasa tells the deeds of Gauranga.

*Raga sindhura; disha: "Brothers, sing, sing, hear of the virtues of Gora Gosani! (murchha) Aho, aho, aho! Long for the lotus feet of Gauranga. All that you see in the world you count as your own — ho-ho-ho, ho-ho-ho, brothers — yet all of it is vain forever. Brothers, sing, sing, hear of Gora's virtues!"*[^8]

**116.** Thus Gorachand walks along the road,
looking at every holy place He comes upon.

**117.** The Lord goes on, stopping in every village,
and dances at the shrines of the gods.

**118.** Hear of one wondrous thing on the way —
what Nityananda, the king of avadhutas, did.

**119.** Gaurahari gave His staff into Nityananda's hand
and went somewhat ahead, leaving Nityananda behind.

**120.** Overwhelmed with prema, the Lord walks very fast,
forgetting Himself in passionate love for Krishna.

**121.** Gadadhara and all the companions go with Him;
seeing this, Nityananda falls still farther behind.

**122.** Pondering, Prabhu [Nityananda] walks slowly:
"Before my eyes the Lord holds a staff in His hand!

**123.** Leaving that beautiful flute that enchants the three worlds,
He has taken up a staff — how can I bear it?

**124.** The Lord has taken sannyasa and shaved His head —
this cruel pain will stay [with me] all my life."

**125.** The more He pondered, the more His grief grew,
and, setting the staff across His thigh, He broke it.[^9]

**126.** Lifting the broken staff, He threw it into the water,
and, afraid of the Lord, walks slowly behind.

**127.** After a while the two came together,
and the Lord asked, "Why do I not see the staff?"

**128.** Shrinking before the Lord, He gives no answer;
the Lord was surprised and grew thoughtful.

**129.** Again the Lord asks, "Where have You put the staff?
Not seeing the staff, I feel great pain in My heart."

**130.** Hearing this, Nityananda Raya says:
"Seeing the staff in Your hand, I burn all over.

**131.** You took sannyasa — that alone, Your shaving Your head, [was bitter];
and more bitter still, You carry a staff on Your shoulder.

**132.** Unable to bear it, I broke it and threw it into the water.
Do as You will," He says in a faltering voice.

**133.** Hearing this, the Lord was grieved
and said angrily, "You do everything backwards!

**134.** In My staff abide all My gods:[^10]
what have You gained by breaking such a staff?

**135.** You are always mad, Your mind is unsteady;
Your ways are those of a lunatic, Your intentions a child's.

**136.** Never have You kept the duty that learning requires:
You make [Me] abandon My ashrama — You do everything backwards.

**137.** You see no sin in offending the gods and [violating] the ashrama,
and if I say anything to You, You grow terribly angry."

**138.** Hearing this, Nityananda Prabhu laughs
and answers the Lord in a faltering voice:

**139.** "I do not offend the gods or the ashrama;
whether I did well or ill — You know it all.

**140.** In Your staff abide all Your gods —
and You carry them on Your shoulder: how can I bear it?

**141.** You do them good and I do them harm;
why should I argue with You any further?

**142.** I have offended — forgive my fault this once:
by Your name the whole world is delivered.

**143.** Your name purifies the fallen even more than You Yourself —
so let this offence of mine be forgiven.[^11]

**144.** By the name alone the people of the world are delivered —
yet You have taken sannyasa, and the devotees are in great sorrow.

**145.** You shaved Your head with that lovely hair —
a cruel pain has lodged in the devotees' hearts.

**146.** My heart burns ceaselessly seeing this;
whether it is so or not, ask — all the devotees are witnesses.

**147.** For the devotees' grief I broke the staff and threw it away:
it was no staff — it was like a spear in my breast."

**148.** Hearing this, the Lord gave no reply:
His face was sad, but in His heart there was secret joy.[^12]

**149.** Nityananda, the great Lord, knows all rasas:
He broke the staff and threw it away, sings Lochana.

[^1]: In the 1983 edition chapter 16, "Sri Nilachala-yatra" ("The Journey to Nilachala"), begins here with new numbering; we follow Bhaktisiddhanta's numbering. Bhaktisiddhanta's summary of this section is at the beginning of en/42. The town is left empty because the Lord has left Bengal for good.
[^2]: In Murari (3.5.1) the Lord goes "with Mukunda, Gadadhara and others," putting the Avadhuta in front. According to the Chaitanya-charitamrita (Madhya 3), Advaita sent four with the Lord: Nityananda, Jagadananda, Damodara Pandita and Mukunda Datta; Lochana Dasa names the companions differently.
[^3]: The Dola-yatra is the spring swing festival of Krishna on the full moon of the month of Phalguna. According to the Chaitanya-charitamrita (Madhya 7), the Lord came to Nilachala in Phalguna and saw the Dola-yatra.
[^4]: Vv. 85–92 — cf. Murari 3.5.2–3. As a sannyasi, the Lord teaches people by His own example (loka-shiksha): to accept only food offered to Krishna and to live on alms.
[^5]: The same verse is in Murari (3.5.4–5) and in the Chaitanya-charitamrita (Madhya 7.96). Bhaktisiddhanta also mentions it in his summary (en/42).
[^6]: The toll-collector (dani) took payment from travellers on roads and at ferries. Vv. 96–114 — cf. Murari 3.5.7–9; the Chaitanya-bhagavata (Antya 2) also tells of toll-collectors on the way to Puri.
[^7]: The Moon of Nilachala is Jagannatha: the toll-collector recognizes in the Lord the Lord of Nilachala Himself.
[^8]: "Vain" (michha) means "futile", not "unreal" (en/37, note 14). The 1983 edition reads "the feet of Radha" instead of "the feet of Gauranga."
[^9]: The story of the broken staff is told by Murari (3.5.10–21), in the Chaitanya-bhagavata (Antya 2) and in the Chaitanya-charitamrita (Madhya 5): there Nityananda breaks the staff into three pieces and throws it into the river Bhargi. In Murari, Nityananda first excuses Himself by saying he stumbled; in Lochana Dasa He says plainly that he could not bear the sight of the Lord with a sannyasi's staff.
[^10]: A sannyasi's staff is held to be the abode of the gods (cf. Murari 3.5.16–17).
[^11]: Cf. the teaching that the Lord's name is more merciful than His form (Rupa Gosvami, Namashtaka 6).
[^12]: The Lord's anger is lila: in Murari (3.5.18–21) the Lord, setting aside His anger, admits that Nityananda has fulfilled His own intention, and embraces Him; Bhaktisiddhanta, in the edition's table of contents, calls this episode "the manifestation of the pastime of anger" (krodha-lila). Nityananda, the Lord's eternal servant, only carried out His hidden will.
