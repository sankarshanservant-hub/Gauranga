# Shesha-khanda, part 3 (portion 48)

*The section "The Lord Sees Vrindavana" (continued): Krishnadasa shows the Lord Mathura — the walls, Kamsa's palace, the prison, the "place where He passed water" as an infant, Uddhava's house, the twelve ghats, Kamsa's well; the story of the Setubandha lake; the places of Krishna's infant and childhood lilas in Mahavana and Gokula; Nanda entrusts the Child to Radha in Bilvavana; the cowherds move to Vrindavana; the places of Krishna's sports with the cowherd boys — the Lord in the mood of a friend*

**85.** Thus in conversation the morning came;
the Lord performed His morning rites as prescribed.

**86.** With a longing heart He called Krishnadasa;
His love was more than His body could hold.

**87.** The Lord set out to see the Mathura-mandala;
Ishvara Himself plays a trick on Krishnadasa[^1].

**88.** Krishnadasa says: "Lord, turn Your mind here:
see, on three sides of the city — the fortifications.

**89.** On the east the river Yamuna flows southward;
on two sides of the fortifications are the north and south gates.

**90.** See here Kamsa's palace — south-west of the city;
it has two gates, to the east and to the north.

**91.** See — north of the palace a platform for sitting;
and here, see, in the north-west of the city — the prison.

**92.** And here, see, south of it — the 'place where He passed water';
I will tell about it in detail — listen attentively.

**93.** In fear of Kamsa, Vasudeva carries away his son;
suddenly Krishna passed water in his arms.

**94.** Here Vasudeva quickly sat down;
Krishna passed water — and the stone melted.

**95.** The mark of the water remained on this stone;
therefore people call this place the 'place where He passed water'[^2].

**96.** North of here, see, is Uddhava's house."
Hearing this, the Lord's tears ran in two streams down His throat.

**97.** His whole body from head to foot thrilled:
each hair stood up like a kadamba filament.

**98.** "Here is Uddhava's house — I have come here now;
what Krishna did here I tell from My own experience.

**99.** Here took place the talk of Krishna and Uddhava;
I feel as if I saw it — and pain pierces My heart"[^3].

**100.** Saying this, the Lord looks all around;
then, with love, Krishnadasa speaks:

**101.** "East of Uddhava's [house], see, is the washerman's house;
and east of it, see, the dwelling of the garland-maker.

**102.** South of here, see, is Kubja's house;
south of it — the lovely arena.

**103.** See — south-east of it is Vasudeva's house."
Hearing this, the Lord smiles within Himself,

**104.** His voice falters, His face flushes a little.
"See — north-east of it is Ugrasena's house.

**105.** See — south of it is the Vishranti-ghata;
here the Deity named Gatashrama is renowned[^4].

**106.** When Krishna killed Kamsa and dragged him away, a channel was formed —
therefore south of here is the Kamsa-khali ghata.

**107.** See — south of it is the Prayaga-ghata;
south of it — the ghata named Tinduka.

**108.** South of here is the ghata called Saptatirtha;
south of it, see, [the ghata] named Rishitirtha.

**109.** South of here, see, is also Mokshatirtha;
south of it — the renowned Kotitirtha.

**110.** South of it, see, [the ghata] named Bodhitirtha;
to the south — Ganeshatirtha, there it is, see.

**111.** These are the twelve ghatas — the essence of all holy places.
And south of the city, see, there is also the arena.

**112.** South of it, see, is one more marvel:
the evil-minded King Kamsa dug a well.

**113.** 'Having killed Krishna, I will throw Him in here' — such was his plan;
Kamsa dug the well — [hence] the name Kamsa-kupa.

**114.** See — south-west of it is the Agastya-kunda;
north of here — the Setubandha lake."

**115.** Hearing this, the Lord cries: "What? What?" —
and His body was covered all over with thrill.

**116.** "Hear the account of the Setubandha lake;
listen attentively, Lord, with a collected mind.

**117.** Once Krishna, in the company of the gopis,
was playing the rasa dance on the bank of this lake.

**118.** He said to Radha: 'I am that very Raghunatha:
I killed Ravana together with the monkeys.'

**119.** Hearing this, Radha smiled faintly:
'Krishna is telling an untruth — that is what is on [His] mind.'

**120.** Seeing this, He, perturbed, asks Radha:
'Why do you laugh, Rai? Tell Me!'

**121.** Radha says: 'Tell no more untruths!
How could You have been the avatara Rama?

**122.** He is a great master of the senses, the Supreme Ishvara;
His conduct is not possible for You.

**123.** He dammed the ocean with trees and stones —
let us see You dam this lake!'

**124.** Hearing this, [Krishna] smiles gently:
'If I place bricks and stones in the water, they will float.'

**125.** Hearing this, the gopis say:
'We will bring stones — let us see You dam it now!

**126.** Do not boast in vain, listen, Kanai:
that stones float in water — we have never heard.'

**127.** The Thakura says: 'Bring these trees and stones —
I will dam this lake with stones.'

**128.** Hearing this, they carry bricks,
bring pieces of wood and whole stones.

**128+1.** Standing on one bank, Krishna dams the lake:
from this bank to that, stones were laid everywhere[^5].

**129.** With these trees and stones the lake was dammed;
'Well done, well done!' say the gopis, and Radha smiles faintly.

**130.** For Radha's sake a dam arose on the lake —
that is why it is called the Setubandha lake"[^6].

**131.** Hearing this, the Lord rejoiced at heart;
joyfully this Lochana Dasa sings the glories of Gora.

*Raga pathamanjari*

**132.** "North of here is the Saptasamudraka-kunda:
[here] Devaki's seven sons [were dashed] against a stone.

**133.** North of here, see, is the linga of Bhuteshvara;
see the Sarasvati-kunda to the north of the city.

**134.** Here, see, is the Dashashvamedha-ghata;
south of here — the way to the Soma-tirtha.

**135.** South of here is the Kanthabharana-majjana;
the stream of the Naga-tirtha flows, going down to Patala"[^7].

**136.** Then He went to Samyamana and the other kundas and ghatas
and walked round the city, absorbed in His own feelings.

**137.** Thus, wandering, the day passed;
having taken alms, the Lord spent the night.

**138.** Tormented by longing, the night seemed long to Him;
"Has it dawned? Has it dawned?" He asks, His heart aching.

**139.** Night turned to morning — His heart rejoices;
having performed the morning rites, He says: "Come, Krishnadasa!"

**140.** Krishnadasa says: "Lord, hear my word:
the land of the Mathura-mandala is twenty-one yojanas;

**141.** the twelve forests lie within six yojanas.
What Krishna did and where — I will show [You] everything.

**142.** Here Kamsa heard the words of Narada,
here he kept Vasudeva and Devaki.

**143.** Here Krishna appeared; seeing Him four-armed,
Vasudeva and Devaki here beg [Him to hide that form].

**144.** From here Vasudeva [went], taking Krishna in his arms,
while the guards, overcome with sleep, lay senseless.

**145.** Vasuki, spreading his hoods as an umbrella, hurries behind;
at the crossing of the Yamuna a she-jackal runs ahead.

**146.** Here is Mahavana, the dwelling of Nanda Ghosha;
here in her sleep the virtuous Yashoda bore a daughter.

**147.** Leaving the son in Nanda's house, [Vasudeva] took the daughter,
and calling [her] Devaki's daughter they deceived Kamsa.

**148.** When the wicked King Kamsa tried to kill the girl,
she went up into the sky as lightning.

**149.** Kamsa, having done wrong, praises both [Vasudeva and Devaki];
at that time he hears a voice from the sky.

**150.** Hearing that voice, he began to persecute the righteous:
he knew for certain that death [awaited him].

**151.** Having celebrated the festival for his son, Nanda came to Mathura;
Vasudeva said: 'Guard the child, keep him hidden.'

**152.** Seven days old, Krishna killed Putana;
at a month old He overturned and smashed the cart.

**153.** Krishna kills Trinavarta, becoming heavy as the Bearer of the universe;
yawning, He showed His mother the universe in His belly.

**154.** After six months came the name-giving;
when He ate earth, He showed the universal form.

**155.** Here He danced, holding on to the churning rod;
here Yashoda went off when the milk boiled over.

**156.** Climbing on a mortar and piercing the pot in its sling,
Hari, His face turned upward, ate butter.

**157.** Here Krishna stole and ate butter;
here mother Yashoda bound Him to the mortar.

**158.** Here He broke the twin arjuna trees;
here, giving grain, the God Narayana ate fruit[^8].

**159.** South of Mahavana, see, is the town of Gokula:
here Damodara with the boys herded the calves.

**160.** Here, see, is the lovely Deity of Gopeshvara;
see how beautiful the Saptasamudraka-kunda is.

**161.** See — west of the village is Ayana's house;
south of it — the house of the cowherd Sundara.

**162.** In the middle of the village — here is Upananda's house;
to the west, see, is Ravana's forest of austerity.

**163.** See — north of here is the ashrama of Durvasa;
near by, see, is the lovely Lohavana[^9].

**164.** I will tell a marvel: here is Bilvavana —
here Nanda was, with Krishna in his arms.

**165.** Seeing Radha, Nanda said to her:
'Take Krishna in your arms, carry Him home.'

**166.** At Nanda's word Radha took Krishna in her arms;
[and He] kisses her, as if in childish play.

**167.** Not understanding the matter, Radha carries Him along the road;
in a tight embrace He leaves the marks of His nails on her breast.

**168.** Seeing such conduct, Radha was amazed;
prema arose in her heart, but she did not show it[^10].

**169.** Here, see, is another deed of Krishna:
all the boys, tormented by thirst, are dying —

**170.** and He dug a pond with a cowherd's stick — there it is, see."
As soon as He heard this, Gaurachandra lost outer consciousness.

**171.** After some time Gaurachandra came back to Himself;
the Lord says: "Krishnadasa, what happened next?"

**172.** "Here, see, Upananda and all the others
held counsel, and all the cowherds agreed:

**173.** 'Unbearable is this oppression of the king, every day is danger';
at daybreak they all made ready the carts.

**174.** Seating the gopis in the carts, the cowherds
[set out] to settle near by, in Vrindavana.

**175.** With cries of 'hey, hey!' they go, driving the cows,
wraps on their feet, sticks in their hands, turbans on their heads.

**175+1.** Krishna and Balarama ride on a cart;
looking at Their faces, the cowherds go happily[^11].

**176.** Two months they lived in the Bhadra and Bhandira forests"[^12].
Joyfully this Lochana Dasa sings [His] glories.

**177.** "Then they crossed over nearer, to Vrindavana;
here they set the carts in a half-moon.

**178.** At the root of a kapittha tree He killed Vatsasura:
seizing him by the tail and legs, He lifted him and dashed him to the ground.

**179.** Here Bakasura swallowed Krishna and spat Him out;
seizing him by both beaks, He tore him apart and took his life.

**180.** In this pasture He plays with all the boys —
horns, flutes, sticks in hand, in various sports.

**181.** Some imitate the cry of some animal,
some want to catch the shadow of a flying bird."

**182.** Hearing this, Gaura, distraught at heart,
runs here and there like a boy.

**183.** He cries like a peacock, spreads [an imagined] tail;
His body is full of thrill, His eyes red.

**184.** "Brother, brother!" He calls, shouts: "Hey! Hey!";
"Shridama! Sudama!" He cries and embraces a tree.

**185.** Overwhelmed by the bhava of friendship, Gaura Raya,
maddened with prema, runs in all directions.

**186.** "Kali! Dhavali!" He calls again and again. —
"Where has Dhenukasura gone? I will kill him now!"

**187.** Saying this, He weeps; His body knows nothing outward.
Krishnadasa says: "This is He — the hero of the Yadus!"

**188.** And the companions with Him are in the same state:
they gaze at Gora's face, aware of nothing[^13].

**189.** After some time Gaurachandra came back to Himself
and again says to Krishnadasa: "Tell Me what happened."

**190.** "The serpent named Aghasura is Baka's younger brother;
here Krishna took his life.

**191.** Here the Yamuna used to be — now it is not;
here Brahma stole the calves and the boys.

**192.** For a year he kept them in [a cave of] Govardhana;
seeing those same calves and boys, Brahma offered praise.

**193.** [Here] Balarama killed Dhenuka and ate the palm fruits.
See — here on the Yamuna is the place Kaliya-daha.

**194.** Here He climbed the kadamba tree
and, leaping [into the water], subdued the serpent Kaliya.

**195.** Chilled through, Krishna came up on this ghata,
and then twelve suns rose in the sky.

**196.** Therefore people call it the ghata of the Twelve Adityas;
see before you the Deity of Kaliya-damana [the Subduer of Kaliya].

**197.** Here the boys and calves were burning in the forest fire;
drinking up the fire, He saved them all.

**198.** Here Krishna rode on Shridama's shoulders,
and Pralamba, having lost, carried Balarama on his shoulders.

**199.** The asura's illusion was revealed to Balarama:
He struck him on the head with His fist, and he gave up his life.

**200.** In the Bhandira forest — the death of Aghasura[^14].
Near by, see, Gosani, — here is Vrindavana.

**201.** See — the thickets of ishika and munja reeds, wondrously lovely:
here suddenly [the boys] could not see the cows.

**202.** Not seeing the cows, He blew on the flute —
and the cows, pricking up their ears, come running, heads raised.

**203.** The cows run with grass in their mouths, the calves to the udders;
beasts and birds are enchanted by the song of the flute.

**204.** Again the boys were alarmed by a forest fire;
[Krishna] drank up the fire, [while] the boys closed their eyes[^15].

**205.** Thus Krishna's pastimes, place by place,
Gaura sees with joy," says Lochana.

[^1]: That is, though He is Krishna Himself, the Lord seems not to know His places and questions Krishnadasa.
[^2]: Vv. 88–95 — cf. Murari 4.04. Vasudeva carries the newborn Krishna from the prison to Gokula; the "place where He passed water" (mutra-sthana) is a shrine of Mathura.
[^3]: Uddhava is Krishna's friend and counsellor in Mathura; Krishna sent him to Vraja with a message to the gopis (Bhagavata 10.46–47). The Lord speaks as Krishna Himself, from memory.
[^4]: The Vishranti-ghata is the "ghata of rest", where Krishna rested after killing Kamsa; Gatashrama ("He whose weariness is gone") is the Deity of Vishnu at this ghata. Vv. 101–114 — cf. Murari 4.04: the houses of the washerman, the garland-maker, Kubja, Vasudeva and Ugrasena, the arena, the twelve ghatas, Kamsa's well.
[^5]: V. 128+1 is found only in the 1983 edition; it is absent from the 1903 edition.
[^6]: Vv. 116–130 — cf. Murari 4.05. Radha playfully teases Krishna: in the play of love the gopis deliberately refuse to acknowledge His greatness. According to Gaudiya Vaishnava teaching, Krishna is the original Bhagavan, and Rama is His manifestation; in answer to her mockery He builds the dam. The Setubandha-sarovara is a lake near Kamana (Kamyavana).
[^7]: Vv. 132–136 — shrines of Mathura; cf. Murari 4.04. The Saptasamudraka-kunda is the "pond of the seven seas"; by tradition, at this place Kamsa killed Devaki's children. Patala is the underworld.
[^8]: Vv. 142–158 are a brief retelling of the Bhagavata (10.1–10.11): Narada's prophecy, Krishna's appearance in four-armed form, Vasudeva carries Him to Gokula, Yogamaya in the form of Yashoda's daughter goes up into the sky, the slaying of Putana, Shakata, Trinavarta, the universe in the Child's mouth, the stealing of butter, the Damodara-lila, the Yamala-arjuna trees, the fruit-seller. The she-jackal showing the ford is a Bengali tradition.
[^9]: Gopeshvara is Shiva, who by tradition took the form of a gopi to enter the rasa dance. Ayana (Abhimanyu) is by tradition Radha's husband; Gaudiya Vaishnavas teach that this marriage is only an appearance created by yogamaya for the sake of parakiya-rasa. "The cowherd Sundara" — so in A and C; the 1983 edition reads "Nanda-gopa". On "Ravana's forest of austerity" the 1903 edition gives in a footnote the reading: "where Ravana of Lanka performed austerities."
[^10]: Vv. 164–168 — a tradition (known from the Garga-samhita and the Brahma-vaivarta Purana): Nanda entrusts the infant Krishna to Radha in the Bhandira forest (in Lochana Dasa, in Bilvavana). Lochana Dasa tells it briefly and frankly. According to Gaudiya Vaishnava teaching (Bhaktisiddhanta), the love of Radha and Krishna is wholly transcendental (aprakrita): Krishna is ever Radha's Beloved, and His pastimes in the form of a child are not to be measured by worldly standards; these lilas must not be understood after the pattern of worldly relations.
[^11]: V. 175+1 is found only in the 1983 edition; it is absent from the 1903 edition.
[^12]: Vv. 159–176 — cf. Bhagavata 10.11 (the move to Vrindavana because of the troubles in Gokula). Bhadra and Bhandira are two of the twelve forests.
[^13]: The Lord, in the mood of a cowherd friend (sakhya-bhava), calls Shridama and Sudama, and also the cows. In the 1983 edition the cows are Dhavali and Shangali (cf. en/23). "This is He — the hero of the Yadus": Krishnadasa recognizes in the Lord Krishna Himself.
[^14]: Vv. 190–200 follow the Bhagavata (10.11–10.20): Aghasura, the stealing of the calves by Brahma, Dhenuka, Kaliya, the forest fire, Pralamba. Pralamba was killed by Balarama in the Bhandira forest; "the death of Aghasura" in v. 200 (so in A and B) is apparently a slip for Pralamba.
[^15]: Vv. 201–204 follow the Bhagavata (10.19): the cows strayed into the reed thickets, and a forest fire broke out; Krishna told the boys to close their eyes and swallowed the fire.
