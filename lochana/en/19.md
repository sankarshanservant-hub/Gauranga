# Adi-khanda, part 11 (portion 19)

*Kaishora-lila, the wedding (conclusion): the Lord at Vallabhacharya's house; the welcoming of the bridegroom; the bride is brought to the Lord, their first sight of each other; Vallabha honours his son-in-law and gives Him his daughter; the night in the bridal chamber; the kushandika, Vallabha's parting words; the return home with Lakshmi, Shachi receives the couple; a song of the Lord's strolls with His friends (1983 edition only)*

*Raga bhatiyari; disha: "Behold, my heart, the wonder: the Darling of Navadvipa, dear as life!" (murchha)*

**93.** Thus [the Lord] arrived at Vallabha Acharya's house;
cries of "Jaya, jaya!" filled the sky.

**94.** Hundreds upon hundreds of lamps are burning, the whole earth is lit,
and in their light the beauty of Gora's body shines.

**95.** Then Vallabha Mishra, offering water for the feet and arghya,
brought the bridegroom into the house with auspicious rites.

**96.** Then Mahaprabhu, going up onto the wedding platform,[^1]
joyfully stood upon the seat.

**97.** His face surpasses the full moon on the night of the full moon,
and on it is a sweet smile, as if blended with nectar.

**98.** The radiance of His body is like molten gold,
the build of His body like Mount Meru.

**99.** On His arms are armlets and bracelets, rings set with jewels;
His palms sparkle like red lotuses.

**100.** A splendid garland of malati jasmine swings on Gora's body —
like the waves of the Ganga upon the summit of Sumeru.

**101.** Beneath His crown His forehead is beautifully adorned;
millions of Kamas, seeing it, stand abashed and ashamed.

**102.** Earrings swing at His ears — to what can I compare them?
They have taken from proud beauties all desire for pride.

**103.** While Mahaprabhu stood thus on the platform,
the married women came there for the welcoming of the bridegroom.[^2]

**104.** They had put on splendid attire and garments;
in their hands were bright lamps, in their hearts jubilation.

**105.** Before and behind went the married women, and with them the bride's mother:
the noble lady herself went to welcome the bridegroom.

**106.** Circling [Him] seven times with seven lamps in her hands,
with a joyful heart she poured curd upon His feet.

**107.** Having welcomed the bridegroom, the lady went into the house;
the auspicious moment came — the hour of dusk.[^3]

**108.** Then Vallabha Acharya, the best of brahmanas,
quickly ordered his daughter to be brought.

**109.** The beautiful maiden [sat] on a finely made seat;
the radiance of her body lit up the earth.

**110.** Jewelled lamps are burning all around her;
her face surpassed the splendour of the full moon.

**111.** On all her body were ornaments of jewels and gold;
their radiance drove the darkness away.

**112.** Circling the Lord seven times,
[the bride], joining her palms at her head, bows to Him.

**113.** The cloth that screened them was removed, and they saw each other;
they gaze at each other — and the eyes of both are dancing.

**114.** As if the Moon and Rohini had met together:[^4]
the two shower each other with flowers, as if in a duel.

**115.** Like Hara and Parvati — so the two met;
both, beside themselves with bliss, waved the chhamuni.[^5]

**116.** On all sides are cries of "Jaya!" and shouts of "Hari, Hari!";
all the people dance, mad with joy.

**117.** Then the Lord of Kamala, Lord Vishvambhara,
sat down, seating the bride at His left.

**118.** Her face bowed in shyness, she sat beside the Lord;
and Mishra honours his son-in-law according to all the rules.

**119.** He to whose lotus feet Brahma offered water
and by whose grace became the creator of the world;[^6]

**120.** He from whose foot the Ganga came down to the earth,[^7]
and at that time all the worlds attained liberation;

**121.** He to whom Bali gave three steps of land
and on whose head He placed the dust of His lotus feet;

**122.** That foot by repeating [the name of] which the yogi became Maheshvara,
that foot which the goddess Kamala serves with joy;

**123.** He from whom comes Vishnu, His partial avatara;
whose portion is Adi-Varaha, who raised up the earth;

**124.** whose portions are Matsya, Kurma, Varaha, Nrisimha and the others,
[the Slayer of] Hiranyakashipu, Vamana, [the Chastiser of] the mlecchas and the rest;

**125.** Parashurama, Bhrigurama, Buddha, the sage Vyasa,
from whose lips the eighteen Puranas are heard[^8] —

**126.** hear now this song of the virtues of the ten avataras,
who descend age after age to deliver the souls —

**127.** that Lord became Vallabhacharya's son-in-law;
in all the three worlds there are no words for his good fortune.

**128.** The song of Gauranga's virtues is a morsel of nectar:
whoever hears it, from his heart godlessness departs.

**129.** To such lotus feet Mishra offers water —
feet whose worship dispels the darkness of samsara.

**130.** To Him to whom great Indra yielded his royal throne,[^9]
Mishra gives a seat — a mat of grass.

**131.** To the Lord who is clad in divine yellow garments
he gives a garment — it is fearful even to hear!

**132.** Thus, in due order, all that was prescribed —
the sacrifice and all the other rites — was completed.

**133.** No one is as fortunate as Vallabha Acharya:
the Lord of Vaikuntha Himself accepted his daughter as a gift.

**134.** What can I say of the boundless good fortune of Vallabha Mishra,
in whose house the Lord performed the rite of the five mouthfuls![^10]

**135.** Bride and bridegroom took their meal in one room;
hundreds upon hundreds of wives of noble families gathered in the bridal chamber.[^11]

**136.** In throngs the young women came to the Lord
and surrounded Vishvambhara in front and behind.

**137.** Seeing the rising of the smile on His moonlike face,
the darkness of everyone's shyness fled away.

**137+1.** All the beauties sat down near the Lord;[^12]
at the breeze from His body the bodies of the playful women tremble.

**137+2.** The garments and the speech of all began to slip,
and the eyes of some grew heavy with languor.

**137+3.** One, touching His body, in the waves of Ananga's play
sank down powerless upon Vishvambhara.

**137+4.** Another, unblinking, gazes at Him with a fixed look —
as the chakora is blissful gazing at the moon.

**137+5.** With the lotuses of their eyes all worship Gora's face;
one begs for His touch upon herself.

**137+6.** Like a dependent pauper who has found great wealth —
there is nowhere to hide it, yet out of attachment he cannot let it go.

**138.** In the bridal chamber some start the game of changing names;
enraptured by Vishvambhara's virtues, they jest.

**139.** One says: "Vishvambhara, listen to me:
give a betel nut — Lakshmi is overcome with sleep.

**140.** Put it into Lakshmi's mouth Yourself —
let all her friends watch with delight!"

**140+1.** One loosens Vishvambhara's hair and binds it again —
her eagerness to bind it comes from her longing to touch [Him].[^13]

**140+2.** Another puts a betel nut into Gorachand's mouth —
her heart trembles, she feels great bliss.

**140+3.** Another, swaying faint, her heart in a tumult,
lifts Lakshmi and places her on Gorachand's lap.

**141.** Another says: "Is anyone more fortunate than she?
She has gained a husband like Vishvambhara!

**142.** What austerity did she perform, what vows, what gifts?
What meditation did she perfect in the worship of the gods?

**143.** What faithful, chaste wife on earth
could keep her heart steady on seeing Vishvambhara's beauty?

**144.** His beautiful face surpasses Madana's abode;
He is the thief of the finest jewel — a proud beauty's heart.

**145.** His mighty arms surpass the staff of Kama:
women long to clasp them to their breasts.

**146.** Lakshmi will delight in all this body —
but when shall we be blessed with His touch?

**147.** This is our hope: to become His maidservants;
when shall we serve Sri Gauranga, who is like the moon?"[^14]

*Raga varadi; disha: "My life, O Gorachand, are haya!"*

**148.** Thus, in amusement and merriment, morning came;
the Lord performed the morning rites as prescribed.

**149.** On the day after the wedding came the rite of kushandika;
brahmanas are fed, as befits brahmanas.

**150.** The Lord performed all this there that day
and said: "Tomorrow I shall go home."

**151.** The Lord set out for home with a joyful heart;
the family honour Him with silver and gold.

**152.** The Lord sits on one seat with Lakshmi at His left;
all around, the women surrounded them.

**153.** In Vallabha Mishra's heart are joy and sorrow;
at the departure he blesses bride and bridegroom.

**154.** Offering durva grass, rice, scents, garlands, betel nuts and sandal,
he speaks to his son-in-law these words:

**155.** "I am poor and wretched, I have no good fortune;
what gift can I give You — what is worthy of You?

**156.** Only by Your own goodness have You shown me grace:
by accepting my daughter You have made me blessed.

**157.** What can I say to You, Lord — what worth have I?
Only by Your own goodness have You become my son-in-law.

**158.** Having found refuge at Your lotus feet, which grant fearlessness,
I shall no longer suffer at the hands of Yama.

**159.** The gods and the ancestors are pleased with me
since I gave You my daughter.

**160.** The feet which Brahma, Shiva and others worship in meditation —
those feet I have worshipped in person, as prescribed.

**161.** I shall say something more — listen, Vishvambhara."
As he said this, his voice faltered.

**162.** His eyes brimmed with tears of tenderness;
taking Lakshmi's hand, he placed it in Vishvambhara's hand.

**163.** "From this day I entrust Lakshmi to You;
knowing [all this], care for her and protect her.

**164.** In my house Lakshmi was the mistress of the house;
from this day she is Your maidservant, the daughter-in-law of Your family.

**165.** In my house she lived as she pleased:
she would be stubborn until her mother fed her [herself].

**166.** In my house she grew up in her mother's and father's arms;
wherever we came back from, she would throw her arms round our necks.

**167.** Lakshmi is everyone's darling; I have no son —
in all my house I have only this one little girl.

**168.** But what am I saying — she is Your own.
All that I say I say blinded by attachment.

**169.** All this I have said out of my foolishness:
what does my attachment matter, when You are her husband?[^15]

**170.** In the three worlds no one is as fortunate as Lakshmi;
all that I say comes only from love and attachment."

**171.** Having said this, Mishra fell silent;
his reddened eyes were brimming with tears of sorrow.

**172.** And Vishvambhara set out, with His beloved at His left:
together with Lakshmi He mounted the palanquin.[^16]

**173.** Conchs and dundubhis sound, cries of "Jaya, jaya!" resound;
all kinds of instruments play, joy spreads in waves.

**174.** Brahmanas recite the Vedas, bhat bards sing songs of praise;
in front the dancers dance — the joy is boundless.

**175.** Surrounded by His friends, the Lord travels along the road;
in the sky the gods follow in divine chariots.

**176.** Meanwhile Shachi, joyful, gathering the married women,
bustles about merrily at her son's festival.

**177.** At the door she set an auspicious pitcher with sprigs of leaves
and placed a coconut on top of it.

**178.** All is ready for the nirmanchhana, lamps of ghee are burning;
at that auspicious hour the Lord arrived home.

**179.** The women perform Vishvambhara's nirmanchhana;[^17]
cries of "Jaya, jaya!", "ulu-ulu", fine songs and dancing.

**180.** All kinds of instruments play, the joy is boundless;
Shachi's house was filled with every happiness.

**181.** Auspicious cries arose, the joy was great;
taking Lakshmi by the hand, [the Lord] entered His home.

**182.** Shachi-devi embraces her son and daughter-in-law;
showering them with durva grass and rice, she says: "Live long!"

**183.** She kisses her son's face, looking at her daughter-in-law,
and kisses her daughter-in-law's face, gazing at her son.

**184.** Shachi's dwelling was filled with every happiness;
joyfully Lochana Dasa sings of Gora's virtues.[^18]

*Raga sindhura*

**184+1.** Thus the Lord lives happily
with His friends and kinsmen;
in Shachi's heart is an ocean of bliss
as she sees Gorachand with His bride.[^19]

**184+2.** Gora, the delight of Nadiya,
absorbed in the joy of play;
His brows are Kama's bow,
the arrows are the glances of His eyes.

**184+3.** Among His friends, in jests and sport,
His body full of the rasa of lila;
on earth, without a cloud, a steady lightning:
[Kama] has thrown down his flower-bow.

**184+4.** Resting His hand on a friend's shoulder,
a book in His left hand,
at the close of day [He goes]
to the bank of the celestial river.

**184+5.** His body anointed with fragrant sandal,
on His brow a most charming tilaka;
at its fragrance the bees are bewildered,
and the young women came running in crowds.

**184+6.** His curling hair, the sweetness of His attire —
who, seeing it, can hold back her heart?
Allured by the beauty of His dress, the young women
do not heed the fear of their elders.

**184+7.** In the town of Nadiya, where He is the foremost of nagaras,
all are sunk in an ocean of rasa;
seeing Gaurachandra's lila, all lost their senses,
and their pride was ground to dust.

**184+8.** The nagaris praise His qualities
with sidelong glances of curving eyes;
setting fire to the temple of shame,
they fall [to Him] by hundreds of thousands in longing.

**184+9.** The beauties of Nadiya, forgetting themselves,
remain absorbed in meditation [on Him] in their hearts.
Lochana Dasa says: in those waves of happiness —
just imagine this.

[^1]: "Chhorla" — the raised wedding platform (under a canopy) where the bridegroom stands and the rite is performed.
[^2]: "Bar-urati" (in Lochana Dasa, "bara urathite") — the rite of welcoming the bridegroom: the married women, led by the bride's mother, circle him with lamps and bless him.
[^3]: Godhuli — "the hour of cow-dust", dusk, when the herds come home; in Bengal an auspicious time for a wedding.
[^4]: Rohini — the beloved consort of the Moon god; cf. Murari 1.10.22.
[^5]: The meaning of "chhamuni" is unclear (perhaps the covering that hides the faces of bride and groom until the rite of seeing each other); the 1983 edition reads "they left the chhamuni".
[^6]: When the Lord as Vamana-Trivikrama strode up to Brahma's abode, Brahma washed His foot; cf. Murari 1.10.24.
[^7]: Vv. 120–128 are in A but absent from the 1983 edition; the 1903 edition gives them in a note as an additional reading of one manuscript.
[^8]: Lochana Dasa's list of avataras is free: "Hiranyakashipu" apparently stands for the avatara who slew him (Nrisimha), "the mlecchas" for Kalki, who chastises them; Bhrigurama is another name of Parashurama (perhaps Rama of the Raghus was meant); Vyasa is a shaktyavesha-avatara. The sense is clear: all the avataras are portions of Him who appeared as Gauranga; so too the Gaudiya Vaishnava tradition teaches (Gaura is Krishna Himself, the source of all avataras).
[^9]: Indra, king of the gods, honours the Lord as his master; cf. Murari 1.10.25.
[^10]: "Pancha-grasi" — the rite of the five mouthfuls: in the bride's house the bridegroom eats five handfuls of food.
[^11]: Basara — the room where the newly-weds spend the wedding night while the women keep them awake with jests and songs.
[^12]: Vv. 137+1 – 137+6 are found only in the 1983 edition (absent from Bhaktisiddhanta's edition); the 1903 edition has them, partly in brackets. On the nagari-bhava see ru/09, note 9; cf. ru/18, note 6: Lochana Dasa depicts the attraction of the women of Nadiya to Gaura in the language of love poetry, but for a Gaudiya Vaishnava this is not worldly passion but attraction to the Lord, the transcendental Madana. The chakora is a bird which, by poetic tradition, feeds on moonbeams.
[^13]: Vv. 140+1 – 140+3 are found only in the 1983 edition (absent from Bhaktisiddhanta's edition); the 1903 edition has them.
[^14]: Vv. 136–147 — again Lochana Dasa's motif of nagari-bhava (see portion 09, note 9). Still more such lines are in the 1983 edition (vv. 137+1 – 137+6, 140+1 – 140+3).
[^15]: Here "maya" is Vallabha's own fatherly attachment: before such a husband as the Lord it counts for nothing.
[^16]: Lit. "a vehicle carried by men".
[^17]: Nirmanchhana — a rite in which women wave lamps and auspicious articles around the bridegroom to ward off all that is inauspicious.
[^18]: Cf. Murari 1.10.27, 1.11.1–2.
[^19]: The song in raga sindhura (vv. 184+1 – 184+9) is found only in the 1983 edition (absent from Bhaktisiddhanta's edition, where the new section begins at once); the 1903 edition has it. The 1983 edition marks stanza 184+2 as the refrain (dhruva). Vv. 184+4 and 184+6 are partly corrupt in both editions and are translated by sense. "The foremost of nagaras" (184+7) and the stanzas on the nagaris are Lochana Dasa's motif of nagari-bhava (see ru/09, note 9).
