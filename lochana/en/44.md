# Madhya-khanda, part 21 (portion 44)

*The section "Meeting with Sarvabhauma" (beginning): Tamoluk, Gopala at Remuna, the Vaitarani, Varaha, Yajapura and Viraja; a toll-collector binds Mukunda, and the Lord appears in a dream to his chief; Ekamra (Bhubaneshvara) and the hymn to Shiva; the conversation of Murari and Damodara on the remnants of offerings to Shiva*

## Meeting with Sarvabhauma

> *Summary by Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati.* Sriman Mahaprabhu, passing on the way through Tamoluk, bathing in the Brahma-kunda and seeing Sri Madhusudana, came within a few days to Remuna. There, having seen Sri Gopaladeva, installed by Uddhava, He spent that night absorbed in prema in dancing and singing. The next day, having bathed in the Vaitarani and seen Lord Varaha, He came to the village of Yajapura, saw many lingas of Shiva and joyfully beheld Viraja. There the Lord bathed in the Brahma-kunda, [made offerings to the forefathers at] Nabhi-gaya and from there came to a great holy place, the town of Shiva. [There] a toll-collector mistreated Mukunda, and Gaurahari appeared at night in a dream to the chief of the toll-collectors in the form of the One who lies on the Ocean of Milk and rebuked him for mistreating His devotee; he, frightened, surrendered at the Lord's feet. Then the Lord came from there to Ekamra-kanana, saw ten million lingas of Shiva, Mahesha and Parvati, recited many hymns of praise to Shiva and spent that night there. Next, in the conversation of Murari and Damodara, the question whether Shiva's prasada is worthy of a Vaishnava's respect is settled: it is established that the remnants of offerings to Shiva worshipped by non-devotees are not to be accepted. Then, [passing] Kapoteshvara, He came to the river Bhargavi, bathed there, and, going a little farther and seeing the pinnacle of the temple of Sri Jagannathadeva, fainted in prema; on regaining outer consciousness He bathed again and, offering praises and prayers, came to the house of Sarvabhauma. Sarvabhauma, seeing His form and mahabhava, surmised that He was Bhagavan and told his son to take Him to see Jagannatha. The Lord, standing behind the Garuda pillar, beheld Jagannatha and, absorbed in prema, danced and sang with the devotees; then He was brought from there back to Sarvabhauma's house. After this are described: Sarvabhauma's questions about the Lord, the taking of mahaprasada, the glorification of prasada, another darshana of Jagannatha, Sarvabhauma's [concern] for the Lord's [keeping of] sannyasa, the Lord's questions to Sarvabhauma, the showing of the six-armed form to Sarvabhauma, and so on.[^1]

*Raga bhatiyari; disha: "Brothers, sing, O sing the glory and the virtues of Gora Gosani! (murchha) O brothers, my dear brothers, harbour no worldly desires: as long as you live in the world, do not leave the feet of Mahaprabhu!"*

**1.** Thus the Lord walked along the road
and came to Tamoluk, a great holy place.

**2.** Having bathed in the Brahma-kunda and seen Sri Madhusudana,
the Lord is overcome with prema, and His heart rejoices.[^2]

**3.** Thus He walked for some days
and came, Mahaprabhu, to the village of Remuna.

**4.** In the great town of Remuna dwells Gopala;
the Lord goes to see Him with boundless joy.

**5.** Formerly He was installed by Uddhava in holy Varanasi,
and He came here to show mercy to a brahmana.[^3]

**6.** Saying this, [the Lord] bows again and again
and, crying "Lord of Uddhava!", roars aloud.

**7.** "Today My eyes have borne fruit: I have seen the Lord!"
Through the connection with Uddhava His prema grew immensely.

**8.** With a cry of anguish He calls, "Uddhava! Uddhava!"
and, overwhelmed with prema, falls again and again to the ground and weeps.

**9.** From His reddish eyes tears flow without cease,
His body thrills, and trembling seizes Him again and again.

**10.** Crying "Lord of Uddhava!", He walks around [the Deity]
and dances with His own people, chanting "Hari! Hari!"

**11.** The bliss of prema surged, the jubilation grew;
prema flooded the people, this earth and the sky.

**12.** In joy all the gods look on from the heavens,
with unblinking eyes they gaze at the Lord.

**13.** Indra with his thousand eyes gazes intently:
Gauranga seems to him sweeter than nectar.

**14.** At that very time, on the head
of the Deity Gopala there was a crown of flowers.

**15.** Suddenly the crown slipped from His head,
and, the moment it fell to the ground, [the Lord] picked it up in His hand.[^4]

**16.** All around, the Vaishnavas cry "Hari! Hari!";
the waves of prema seem to touch the sky.

**17.** The king of the gods beheld Lord Vishvambhara
and, seeing this wonder, weeps with his head bowed.

**18.** Till the end of the day the Lord dances without rest;
only at evening did the dancing cease.

**19.** Many dishes offered to Krishna
a brahmana brought and set before the Lord.

**20.** Joyful, Mahaprabhu with His own people
happily partook of the mahaprasada.

**21.** He spent the night in the bliss of talks about Krishna
and in the morning set out with His own people.

**22.** Thus the Lord went along the road
and unexpectedly came to the bank of the river Vaitarani.

**23.** Bathing in and drinking [the water of] this river purify the fallen —
and in it the Thakura Himself bathed.

**24.** Then He, the wisest, goes on:
His wish grows to see Lord Varaha,

**25.** seeing whom all people deliver their families on both sides.
Then the Lord goes to the village of Yajapura,

**26.** where Brahma with all the gods performed a sacrifice
and gave the village to the brahmanas by charter.

**27.** Even a great sinner, if he dies in this village,
is freed from all sins and attains the form of Shiva.[^5]

**28.** There are hundreds of lingas of Mahesha there;
Gaura-Govinda goes on, bowing to them.

**29.** With joy in His heart He goes to see Viraja;
who can describe the glory of Viraja?

**30.** The sight of her destroys millions of sins;
with a joyful heart the Lord beheld Viraja.

**31.** Bowing to Viraja, He said:
"Give Me prema-bhakti for the feet of Krishna."[^6]

**32.** Thus Mahaprabhu went along the road
and here, at Nabhi-gaya, offered pinda to the forefathers.

**33.** He bathed joyfully in the waters of the Brahma-kunda
and, having finished the rites, went on quickly.

**34.** That great holy place is the town of Shiva;
gazing at it, the Lord was rapt.

**35.** I cannot describe the splendour of that town:
there are millions of lingas, headed by Trilochana.[^7]

**36.** At that very time Sri Mukunda Datta,
who knew [the local] ways, says to the Lord:

**37.** "From here on there is nothing more to fear from toll-collectors:
I know them all, I know where each rogue lives."

**38.** Hearing this, the Lord smiled faintly:
"What shall I say to you? You are a man of consequence!

**39.** I have taken up the dharma of sannyasa —
what can a toll-collector do to Me? Tell Me truly."

**40.** Hearing this, Mukunda was not in the least afraid:
"Still, they cause trouble, Lord — I have told You."

**41.** Hearing this, the Thakura says, "Listen, Mukunda:
My body will be guarded by all My kinsfolk."

*Thus in the Shanti-shataka:*

**42.** "He whose father is fortitude, whose mother is forbearance, whose wife is lasting peace,
whose son is truth, whose sister is compassion, whose brother is control of the mind,
whose bed is the ground, whose garment the quarters, whose food the nectar of knowledge —
with such kinsfolk, tell me, friend, what has a yogi to fear?"[^8]

**43.** Hearing this, Mukunda felt no fear in his heart.
The Lord, laughing, said to him:

**44.** "You have looked after Me and brought Me this far!"
and with these words He went to beg alms.

**45.** Gadadhara and all the companions
went off to various places to beg alms.

**46.** Then a certain toll-collector held them all up
and in great anger bound Mukunda.

**47.** All day he kept him — his anger did not cool;
only in the evening, with great effort, was he talked round.

**48.** They had just one blanket among them —
and that sinful villain snatched it away.

**49.** In the evening, having begged alms in various places,
they all came one by one to the agreed pavilion.

**50.** The Thakura was already in that pavilion;
seeing Him, all rejoiced greatly.

**51.** Sri Mukunda Datta, fallen at His feet, weeps:
"Till today I did not know Your greatness, Lord!

**52.** Before You I said there was nothing to fear from toll-collectors —
and for that I have suffered so.

**53.** I knew, yet did not know, fool that I am, that You are Bhagavan:
who would take toll from You?

**53+1.** You have no fear in the three worlds:
You are the Lord of the lords of all — who knows You?[^9]

**54.** I said it so that You would be without fear —
and it served me right that the toll-collector did this to me."

**55.** Hearing this, the Lord asks Gadadhara,
and he told plainly all that the toll-collector had done.

**56.** Hearing it, the Thakura said, "Do not be upset,"
and spoke just one word: "It will be well."

**57.** That very night, to the chief of the toll-collectors of that region
the son of Shachi appeared in a dream.

**58.** He sees [Him] on the Ocean of Milk, lying on Ananta;
Lakshmi and Sarasvati serve His feet;

**59.** within he sees Sanaka and the others,
and Brahma and the other gods offer praises from afar.

**60.** Seeing this, the king of the toll-collectors trembled in his heart:
seeing [such] majesty, he was dismayed.

**61.** "I am near Viraja in the guise of a sannyasi;
all your servants have wronged My devotee."

**62.** He trembled in his heart, was seized with boundless fear,
and hurried to where Sri Gaura-Gopala was.

**63.** Arriving there shortly, that chief of the toll-collectors
bowed to the Lord and speaks with great humility:

**64.** "You are Bhagavan, who sports in the Ocean of Milk;
to deliver the jivas You have taken sannyasa, Lord.

**65.** You are the moon for the dreadful darkness of worldly existence;
You are the Veda and the utmost bound of the Vedas' highest truth."

**66.** Hearing this, Gorachand said to him with a smile,
"May Krishna soon show you mercy."

**67.** Saying this, He placed His feet upon his head
and, intoxicated with prema, dances with arms raised.

**68.** Having shown him mercy, [the Lord] left him in that land,
taught him devotion to Krishna and gave him the right to it.[^10]

**69.** At that time all the Vaishnavas say:
"Your servant caused us much trouble —

**70.** he took away our blanket."
Hearing this, he was embarrassed at heart

**71.** and gave them a new blanket, the chief of the toll-collectors;
then the Vaishnavas' hearts were at peace.

**72.** Then that chief of the toll-collectors, having bowed down,
took his leave and went home.

**73.** Returning home, he took refuge in the service of Krishna
and stays day and night in the sankirtana of Hari's name.

**74.** Thus the whole night passed happily,
and in the morning [the Lord] joyfully performed the morning rites.

**75.** The Lord goes once more to see Viraja,
seeing whom all people cross the ocean of worldly existence.

**76.** Bowing to Viraja, He goes on joyfully;
prema for Krishna rose, His body thrilling.

**77.** The Thakura went on with a lion's might
and little by little came to the village of Ekamra.

**78.** In that village is Shiva with Parvati;
the Lord, beside Himself, hastens to see them.

**79.** From afar the Lord saw the temple —
His longing grew, He was mad with prema.

**80.** Above the temple a beautiful flag is flying;
the whole town of Ekamra is full of Shiva-lingas.

**81.** Seeing the flag, the Lord bowed down
and little by little entered the town of Shiva.

**82.** Ten million lingas are in the town of Ekamra:
as one walks, the heart trembles with fear.[^11]

**83.** Vishveshvara and the rest — millions of lingas;
the very soil of the town looks like sandesh.

**84.** In the Maha-Bindu-sarovara is the water of all holy places,
and many other holy tirthas are in the town.

**85.** Entering the town, [the Lord] sees Parvati and Shankara
and, bowing down, is rapt in prema.

**86.** All beheld Parvati and Mahesha:
the sight of the linga ended everyone's afflictions.

**87.** Seeing Mahesha, the Lord lost control of His body:
His body sways, He cannot stand steady.

**88.** From His reddish eyes tears flow without cease,
His cheeks thrill — again and again He recites a hymn.

*Thus [the hymn]:*

**88+1.** "Obeisance, obeisance to you forever — to the lord of the gods, the master of the bhutas, the Gracious One (Mrida), whose crest is the young moon washed by the waves of the Ganga, the festival of Gauri's eyes;
to him whose faces shine like molten gold, the moon, the blue lotus, coral and the rain-cloud, who in the arena of the beautiful dance grants the desired boons — to the lord of kaivalya, whose banner is the bull!"[^12]

**89.** Thus Mahaprabhu recites a hymn to Shiva,
and all around Him all the Vaishnavas recite hymns.

**90.** At that time Shiva's servants
offered the Lord fragrance, sandal paste and a garland.

**91.** Bowing to Shiva, the Lord came outside
and, entering a house, rested.

**92.** He ate food offered by a devotee
and, tired from the road, lay down for the night.

**92+1.** At the hour of sleep, meditating on the lotus feet of Krishna,
He then thinks in His heart:

**92+2.** "If by good fortune I were to receive Shiva's mahaprasada,
I would partake of it — such is My hope."[^13]

**92+3.** Just as Mahaprabhu thought this,
someone says, "Take this prasada — pana."

**92+4.** The Thakura rose and took the prasada, the pana;
drinking the pana, He was filled with happiness.

**92+5.** What was left He gave to His own people,
and all the devotees partook of it with special reverence.

**93.** Thus in bliss He spent that night;
at dawn the Lord rose, the Master of the three worlds.

**94.** Having done the morning rites and bathed in the Bindu-sarovara,
the Thakura set out, bowing to Maheshvara.

**95.** His own people went with the Lord.
In this connection I shall tell a story.

**96.** What was said between Murari and Damodara —
listen, all of you, carefully: I shall tell it now.

**97.** Pandita Damodara asked Murari:
"Why did the Lord accept the remnants of offerings to Shiva?

**98.** Because of Bhrigu's curse, the remnants of offerings to Shiva are not to be accepted —
why then did the Lord Himself accept them?

**99.** This Mahaprabhu is Himself Brahmanya-deva:
how could He knowingly transgress [the rule]?"[^14]

**100.** Murari replies, "Listen, listen, Damodara:
what do I know of the Lord's inner answer?

**101.** I shall answer what I infer by my own understanding —
if your mind accepts it, keep it in your heart.

**102.** A servant of Shiva who serves Shiva
but does not accept [Hari's] remnants, making a difference between Hari and Hara —

**103.** to him the brahmana's curse applies; this is the point:
his mind is impure, he does not know [Shiva's] greatness.

**104.** But one who serves without dividing them
partakes of the remnants of offerings to Shiva.

**105.** The remnants of offerings to Shiva are eaten by one whose conduct makes no division:
in such a one there is greater love for both Hari and Hara.

**106.** Maheshvara is the king of all Vaishnavas;
whoever worships him seeing him thus —

**107.** from that person's hand Shiva partakes [of the offering];
eat such prasada, and bondage falls away.

**108.** In truth, that Maheshvara, as the Lord passed by,
offered Him hospitality with great joy in his heart.

**109.** Curses and the like, listen, are for those turned away [from the Lord];
but when [Shiva] is worshipped as [Hari's] friend, love for Sri Krishna arises.[^15]

**110.** The Lord descended to teach the people."
Damodara says, "Now the knot is untied!"

**111.** Hearing this, all the people rejoice at heart.
Lochana Dasa tells of the deeds of Chaitanya.

[^1]: The OCR of the summary is garbled in places; gaps are filled by sense and from the table of contents in the edition's preface (ch. 16). "Concern for the keeping of sannyasa" is a conjectural reading (OCR «অন্গটাস-সংরগণ-চিস্ত।»). The summary covers the whole section, whose end is in en/45. In the 1983 edition this section continues chapter 16 ("Sri Nilachala-yatra"), with new numbering.
[^2]: Vv. 1–31 — cf. Murari 3.6. Tamoluk (Tamolipta, Tamralipta) is present-day Tamluk in Bengal.
[^3]: So too in Murari (3.6.4): the Gopala of Remuna was once installed in Varanasi by Uddhava and came here to show mercy to a brahmana. In the Chaitanya-charitamrita (Madhya 4) the Deity of Remuna is Gopinatha Kshirachora, while the story of Gopala who followed a brahmana is told of Sakshi-gopala (Madhya 5).
[^4]: Cf. Murari 3.6.8–9: the crown fell from the Deity's head, and the Lord caught it.
[^5]: So says the mahatmya (glorification) of the holy place; likewise in Murari (3.6.16). For a Vaishnava the highest fruit of pilgrimage is not such rewards but prema — which is what the Lord asks of Viraja (v. 31).
[^6]: The Lord, playing the part of a devotee, asks of the goddess only love for Krishna (cf. Murari 3.6.18) — as the gopis prayed to Katyayani for Krishna.
[^7]: Trilochana ("the Three-eyed") is a linga of Shiva; the town is compared to Varanasi (cf. Murari 3.6.20). According to Murari this is Yajapura; Ekamra (Bhubaneshvara) comes below, v. 77.
[^8]: A verse from the Shanti-shataka. In A it is followed by Bhaktisiddhanta's anvaya and Bengali translation (OCR garbled); the text of the verse is restored from B. Vv. 36–73 — cf. Murari 3.7.
[^9]: V. 53+1 is found only in the 1983 edition; the 1903 edition does not have it.
[^10]: The dream vision is Lochana Dasa's; in Murari (3.7.12) the chief of the toll-collectors simply comes to the Lord.
[^11]: For fear of stepping on a linga.
[^12]: V. 88+1 (the hymn to Shiva) is found only in the 1983 edition and in the 1903 edition; Bhaktisiddhanta's edition does not have it. These are verses 3.8.13–14 of Murari Gupta's poem. The Lord, playing the part of a devotee, honours Shiva as the greatest of Vaishnavas and prays to him for love of Hari (Murari 3.8).
[^13]: Vv. 92+1 — 92+5 are found only in the 1983 edition and (with variants) in the 1903 edition. Cf. Murari 3.9: the Lord desires Shiva's prasada and, receiving it, partakes of it with reverence. Pana is a sweet cooling drink.
[^14]: Brahmanya-deva — "the God who favours brahmanas": He does not break a brahmana's word. Bhrigu's curse on the worshippers of Shiva — Bhagavata Purana 4.2. The conversation follows Murari 3.9 (Murari telling the story to Damodara Pandita).
[^15]: "Without division" (abheda) here is not a mayavada identification: Shiva is not worshipped as a separate god independent of Krishna and His rival, but honoured as the best of Vaishnavas (Bhagavata Purana 12.13.16), inseparable from the Lord (cf. Brahma-samhita 5.45: Shambhu is to the Lord as curd is to milk). Bhaktisiddhanta in his summary states the conclusion thus: the remnants of offerings to Shiva worshipped by non-devotees are not to be accepted (cf. vv. 102–103, 109).
