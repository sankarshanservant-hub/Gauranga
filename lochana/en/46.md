# Shesha-khanda, part 1 (portion 46)

*The beginning of the Shesha-khanda: the Lord sets out for the South; the brahmanas Kurma and Vasudeva at Kurma-kshetra; at Jiyada-Nrisimha the Lord tells the tale of the cowherd Punda who wounded the Boar, of the Deity rising from the earth as milk was poured, and of the merchant Jiyada, after whose name Nrisimha is called; the meeting with Ramananda Raya at Kanchi: the Lord appears to him in meditation and reveals His form of Krishna*

## The Lord's Journey to the South

> *Summary by Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati.* Sriman Mahaprabhu, after staying some time in Puri in the bliss of kirtana together with Sarvabhauma, went to the southern lands to see Setubandha. From there, having shown mercy at Kurma-kshetra to a certain brahmana named Vasudeva and having instructed him that the dharma of the Kali age is the holy name of Sri Hari alone, He arrived at Jiyada-Nrisimha. Here, in the course of the narrative, the author relates the ancient history of Jiyada-Nrisimha.
>
> From Jiyada-Nrisimha Sriman Mahaprabhu came to Kanchi-nagara, to Ramananda, and, having shown him the form of Rasaraja and Mahabhava[^1], went by way of the Godavari to Panchavati; there, absorbed in prema, He saw all the places where Ramachandra had performed His various lilas during His exile in the forest, and came to Sri Ranganatha on the bank of the Kaveri. There He showed mercy to Trimalla Bhatta and spent the period of chaturmasya in his house. After that He met Paramananda Puri, the disciple of Madhavendra Puri-pada. Remembering the prophecy about the descent of Sriman Mahaprabhu [that he had heard] from the lips of Madhavendra Puri-pada, Paramananda Puri understood that the Lord was Bhagavan Himself and offered Him many hymns and prayers.

**1.** Glory to You, Lord of the life of Narahari and Gadadhara!
Be merciful, O Lord — cast on us Your auspicious glance[^2].

**2.** I shall tell the story of the Shesha-khanda — the essence of nectar:
in one who hears it a shoreless ocean of happiness grows.

**3.** Sarvabhauma Bhattacharya offered praises [to the Lord],
and for some days [the Lord] spent day and night in kirtana.

**4.** To see Setubandha the Thakura set out [on His way];
in a town called Kurma He sees a brahmana named Kurma[^3].

**5.** In the same village lives a brahmana named Vasudeva —
He met them both in one place.

**6.** By the sight of the Lord they became pure;
they gaze at Gora's body, overwhelmed with prema.

**7.** His body is beautiful as Sumeru, His arms reach to the knees,
His neck is a lion's, His throat like a conch, His eyes long.

**8.** As they gazed, the joy in their hearts kept growing,
and they knew for certain: this Gaurachandra is Krishna.

**9.** Crying "O Mahaprabhu!" they fell at His feet;
all the people weep [hearing] their weeping of prema.

**10.** Raising them both, the Lord embraced them
and revealed to them sweet words:

**11.** "Listen, listen, O brahmanas, to My word:
for what have you come into the world, what do you follow?

**12.** In the Kali age the dharma is the sankirtana of Hari's name:
Krishna has revealed the great treasure of the name.

**13.** Rejoice in singing the names and qualities [of the Lord];
dance, dance — let people be freed from bondage!"[^4]

**14.** Having said this, the Lord quickly went away,
and they lost all awareness of themselves.

**15.** He cannot walk along the road, so does the rapture of prema grow;
going on a little, He sees Jiyada-Nrisimha[^5].

**16.** "I shall tell an ancient story, a wondrous tale," —
so, overwhelmed with prema, He Himself began the account.

**17.** "Listen, all people, to a joyful secret —
how Jiyada-Nrisimha appeared.

**18.** An ancient tale has come to My mind;
listen, all, to My words with one-pointed attention.

**19.** There lived here a cowherd [named] Punda;
Punda tilled the land from morning to evening[^6].

**20.** On the land he raised a crop called "shasa";
the mayambu crop turned out very abundant.

**21.** Day and night he guards the crop — he has no rest;
and no one knows when he [ever] goes home.

**22.** One day he reflected within himself:
'I will no longer come to guard the crop.'

**22+1.** Thinking it over, he decided thus: 'I will entrust [it] to Krishna;
if I entrust it to Him, I can turn to other work.'

**22+2.** Calling the name of Krishna, he entrusted the crop to Him:
'In Your name I will give something to the Vaishnavas'[^7].

**23.** So Punda lives, rejoicing in his heart —
and suddenly he sees: something is eating the crop.

**23+1.** Seeing this, the cowherd grieved and thought:
'Krishna, You have ruined my whole crop!'

**23+2.** Weeping, the cowherd said: 'Listen, Narayana!
Who has eaten my crop — I will see with my own eyes.'

**23+3.** Saying this, he took shelter in a hut
and stayed awake, guarding the crop, in great distress[^8].

**24.** The next night he keeps watch — and in the third watch
suddenly there came a huge boar.

**25.** Seeing it, the cowherd was on his guard:
the boar eats the crop, twitching its ears.

**26.** It eats the crop, tears the vines to its heart's content —
seeing this, the cowherd strung his bow:

**27.** 'Eat the crop, tear the vines, twitch your ears —
today at my hand you shall lose your life!'

**28.** Saying this, he took aim and loosed the arrow;
it struck deep — the boar utters the name of Rama[^9].

**29.** Running off, it hid inside a mountain cave;
seeing this, Punda the cowherd was bewildered:

**30.** 'It is a boar — why then does it repeat "Rama, Rama"?
This is no boar — this is Bhagavan Himself!'

**31.** Thinking so, Punda, pained at heart,
went up to the cave and calls out [for] an answer.

**32.** 'Who are You? Who are You?' he says, but gets no answer;
with an anguished heart he fasted three days:

**33.** 'What have I done, low wretch that I am!
There is no sinner like me — a vile unbeliever.'

**34.** Mercy arose in the Lord, the treasury of compassion;
with a voice from the sky He says: 'I am Bhagavan.

**35.** You wounded Me, and I ruined you —
do not worry, go to your home'[^10].

**36.** Hearing this, Punda was tormented still more:
'Fasting day after day, I will give up this body.'

**37.** So he fasted many days;
suddenly a voice arose in the sky:

**38.** 'Why, foolish Punda, do you die for no reason?
There is no fault [in you] — go to your home.'

**39.** Again Punda speaks in a voice full of pain:
'I shot an arrow at You — what use is my life?

**40.** Even if I die, this sin of mine will not be washed away;
for such a sin the punishment of Yama is my due.

**41.** By what atonement shall I be purified?
For it was I who shot an arrow at You!

**41+1.** On Your tender body I inflicted such pain —
shame, shame on my life: I wounded You!

**41+2.** My forefathers, Lord, have gone to hell,
and those who see me will go to hell'[^11].

**42.** Hearing this, the voice came again:
'There is no fault — I am boundlessly pleased.

**42+1.** All the offences you committed in former births —
all your sins I have taken on Myself at once.

**42+2.** Know: your body is My body, in every way;
truly, you have caused Me no pain'[^12].

**43.** Hearing this, Punda speaks with joined palms:
'By Your command I speak, setting fear aside:

**44.** how shall I know that this sin of mine is washed away?
If I receive grace and a witness, I shall be content.

**45.** I will tell this to the king —
give him too the same command.

**46.** Then I shall believe, having a witness for my heart,
and all people will know that You have made me happy.'

**47.** Then the Lord commanded again:
'As you have said, so it shall be: you have received the boon.'

**48.** Hearing this, Punda rejoiced
and in great haste came to the king's gate.

**49.** He said to the gatekeeper: 'Listen, best of gatekeepers!
Bring to the king's notice all that I shall say.

**50.** I shall tell of a wondrous thing, unknown to people;
hearing it, the king will love me.'

**51.** Hearing this, the gatekeeper told the king,
and at the king's command Punda came before [him].

**52.** Falling down like a staff, he tells everything in detail —
he related everything from beginning to end.

**53.** Hearing it, the maharaja was amazed
and said to Punda: 'Tell it for certain.'

**54.** Again Punda speaks with certainty:
'Come there, O king — dispel your amazement.

**55.** As the Thakura commanded me,
the same command you too will soon receive.'

**56.** The king says: 'If Ishvara commands,
for life I shall be your servant.'

**57.** Saying this, the king set out in haste,
on foot he went to where the mountain cave was.

**58.** At the mouth of the mountain cave, with all his heart,
he prays at length, rolling on the ground.

**59.** The Thakura relented — a command arose in the sky:
'Not false, O king, listen, are Punda's words.

**59+1.** You are the witness: Punda has become Mine;
over him Yama has power no more.'

**59+2.** Hearing this, the king dances with joy,
clasping the cowherd's feet, he weeps:

**59+3.** 'You have become my guru and united me with Krishna,
you have let me hear words from Krishna's own lips!'

**59+4.** Together with the queens [the king] falls at the cowherd's feet;
seeing this, Krishna was filled with mercy:

**59+5.** 'You did not judge My devotee by caste —
therefore, O king, I said that I would appear to you[^13].

**60.** Pour milk on this place:
by the pouring of milk you will see Me before your eyes.'

**61.** Hearing this, the king, with a joyful heart,
had it proclaimed through the kingdom that milk be brought.

**62.** By the Lord's command they pour milk there —
and suddenly they see before their eyes the crown of a head.

**63.** All kinds of instruments play, the joy is boundless;
[all] float in joy — in a shoreless ocean of happiness.

**64.** On every side resounds "Hari! Hari!" —
all the people dance with both arms raised.

**65.** As much milk as they pour — so far the body rises;
the body rises — the deep [hollow of the] navel is seen.

**66.** With joyful hearts they pour still more milk,
hoping to see all the limbs of the Lord.

**67.** The body rose — the knees are seen before their eyes.
'Pour no milk' — was the command: the measure is reached.

**68.** Yet with joyful hearts they pour much more milk —
but the soles of the feet did not rise in the end.

**69.** Then a command arose in the sky:
'The feet will not rise — strive no more'[^14].

**70.** Hearing this, the king [felt] both joy and sorrow;
having received grace, all hold a great festival.

**71.** He raised a temple and established various offerings;
[all] gaze and cannot gaze enough, their hearts full of love.

**71+1.** The king humbly said to Punda:
'Be king of this kingdom — show me mercy.'

**71+2.** The cowherd answers: 'You speak like an ignorant man:
I will not take the kingdom — why do you pain me?

**71+3.** You and I together will joyfully serve Krishna;
what happiness is there in a kingdom, O king, if one leaves Govinda?'

**71+4.** Hearing this, the king humbly said with joined palms:
'You and I — we shall both be entitled to the service'[^15].

**72.** So the king lives with a joyful heart;
suddenly a merchant came in a boat.

**72+1.** With him were two wives, most beautiful;
they want to go with him to see Sri Hari.

**72+2.** The merchant will not take them along — he is ashamed;
both wives weep, clasping the merchant's feet:

**72+3.** 'You are [our] guru — take us with you, show us Krishna,
do not, lord, destroy our good fortune.'

**72+4.** The merchant says: 'I will not take you with me;
I will bring prasada — stay at home.'

**72+5.** They say: 'What you have said — so be it,
[but] truly the desire to see Krishna has arisen in us.'

**72+6.** Then the merchant angrily says to them both:
'Go and see Krishna, and I will stay at home.'

**72+7.** Hearing this, both wives were sad at heart
and resolved: 'We will leave our husband and serve Krishna.'

**72+8.** The beautiful women went, leaving their husband;
Govinda was filled with mercy, seeing their devotion.

**72+9.** The Lord awakened mercy in the merchant's heart,
and the merchant, coming, sees his wives:

**72+10.** 'Shame, shame on me, worthless and sinful at heart!
It is not right to dishonour such wives.'

**72+11.** The merchant says: 'Come, I will take you with me:
you are utterly pure, your bodies are holy.

**72+12.** If a wife is devoted to Krishna, it is her husband's good fortune:
she is honoured by all, great is her worth'[^16].

**73.** To see the Thakura that merchant came,
with his two wives he went into the temple.

**74.** Bowing to the Lord, the merchant went outside;
the merchant went out — and the temple doors closed.

**75.** Turning back, he sees — the two wives are not beside him:
they are inside the temple, conversing with the Lord.

**76.** Understanding this, the merchant offers praises with laments;
the Thakura relented and showed him grace.

**77.** The temple doors opened — he sees: both
have turned to stone and attained the Lord's feet.

**77+1.** They went, leaving their husband, to attain Krishna as Husband —
therefore they attained Krishna as their steadfast Husband[^17].

**78.** Deeming this his good fortune, the merchant fell at [the Lord's] feet;
showing grace, the Lord says: 'Ask a boon.'

**79.** Falling at His feet, the merchant bows
and asks a boon: 'Let Your name be after my name.

**80.** My father and mother gave me the name "Jiyada"' —
so after his own name he asks as a boon a name for the Lord.

**81.** Therefore the name "Jiyada-Nrisimha" became renowned"[^18].
In joy this Lochana Dasa sings [His] glories.

*Raga sindhura*

**82.** Then Mahaprabhu, having seen Jiyada-Nrisimha
and spent that day there, set out on the next day.

**83.** He walks along the road, His heart in the power of prema;
the Lord arrived at the town of Kanchi[^19].

**84.** Kanchi-nagara is a town full of jewels;
seeing the town, the best of sannyasis was pleased.

**85.** Never does the Lord look on the faces of worldly men —
[and yet] the Lord suddenly came to the king's gate.

**85+1.** The king, having bathed in the Godavari with the brahmanas,
came to his inner apartments and joyfully serves Krishna.

**85+2.** At that time the Lord came to the gate —
His radiance supremely beautiful, the enchanter of Madana[^20].

**86.** Coming to the king's gate, He spoke to the doorkeeper
and quietly asked: "Where is the prince?"[^21]

**87.** Seeing the Lord, the doorkeeper bows
and says within himself: "This is Bhagavan."

**88.** The Lord says: "Give the prince a message:
it is for his sake that I have come here."

**89.** The doorkeeper went to where the prince was —
to his inner apartments, where he was worshipping the Deity.

**90.** Bowing, the doorkeeper gives the message:
"A gosani of great spirit has come to the gate."

**91.** Hearing this, the king said nothing,
and the doorkeeper in fear drew back.

**92.** Returning to the gate, the doorkeeper reports:
"I could not deliver Your message.

**93.** The king is worshipping the Deity in his inner apartments —
who has the power to go in there?"

**94.** Hearing this, the Lord smiles within Himself
and went Himself to where [the king] is worshipping.

**95.** With one portion of Himself He stays at the gate, with another He goes
to where Ramananda Raya is worshipping[^22].

**96.** [Ramananda] meditates on Krishna — and sees Gaurachandra;
again he meditates, chanting the great mantra —

**97.** and again he sees that same Gaura before his eyes.
"What is this? What is this?" he wonders within himself.

**98.** Again he meditates with a steadfast heart —
and again Gaurachandra enters his heart.

**99.** "What? What?" he says, opens his eyes and looks all around —
and suddenly sees Gaurachandra, the best of sannyasis[^23].

**100.** Seeing the sannyasi, the king rose in awe,
bowed at His feet and gazes at [Him] unhurriedly.

**101.** From head to foot he gazes at the Lord's body;
seeing the golden body, joy arose in his heart.

**102.** He was amazed: how did the sannyasi come in?
Smiling, he says to the Lord:

**103.** "How did You enter my apartments?
By great fortune I have seen Your feet."

**104.** The Lord says: "Why do you not know your own self?
You do not recognize Me — and I have come to take you."

**104+1.** Thus the Lord speaks in sweet words:
"You do not recognize Me — I am the son of Nanda."

**104+2.** Hearing this, the king, his eyes brimming with tears, [says]:
"Then show me that form — so my heart will have a witness"[^24].

**105.** Hearing this, the Lord laughed aloud;
the Lord makes Himself known and reveals Himself.

**106.** All that was there — dark, white, red —
everything appears as one golden form.

**107.** That colour of tested, purest gold
the Lord gave up — and became dark and glossy.

**108.** His body has the colour of the kanada flower,
a peacock feather on His head, a flute at His lips.

**109.** Various ornaments on His body, He is lustrous black,
dressed in yellow garments, a forest garland on His neck.

**110.** Seeing this, the maharaja rejoiced at heart,
and the Lord became golden once more[^25].

**111.** Beasts, birds, trees, and all the creepers and leaves
glitter there in the radiance of the golden body.

**112.** Seeing this, Ramananda Raya understood what it was:
overcome with prema, he clasped the feet of his Lord.

**112+1.** Again the Lord took a dark body —
thrice-bent, a flute at His lips, wearing yellow garments.

**112+2.** On His left is Radha, a supreme beauty, a great radiance;
all around are the gopis, young maidens of lovely form.

**112+3.** In Vrindavana, in a temple of jewels, on a throne,
the king sees [Him] — supreme bliss — with Radha.

**112+4.** Again the Lord took on His golden form —
red garments on His body, as on a great sage[^26].

**113.** Fallen at [His] feet, [Ramananda] weeps, his body powerless;
taking him by the hand, the Lord went outside [with him].

**113+1.** Such a manifestation the king beheld unexpectedly;
ten days the Lord stayed with the king.

**113+2.** Many were their talks about Krishna;
even Ananta does not know how to retell them in full.

**113+3.** Endless are the lilas of Chaitanya, beyond the reach of the Vedas;
some lila one devotee or another describes at length.

**113+4.** Who has the power to tell everything from beginning to end?
As one writes and writes, the book only grows[^27].

**114.** The meeting of Ramananda Raya and the Lord —
a song of the virtues of Gora; this Lochana Dasa sings it.

[^1]: Rasaraja ("king of rasa", Krishna) and Mahabhava (Radha) in one form. According to the Chaitanya-charitamrita (Madhya 8), at the end of the talks on the Godavari the Lord showed Ramananda just this form; in Lochana Dasa (vv. 105–110) the Lord shows the form of Krishna, and in the 1983 edition Krishna with Radha.
[^2]: On Narahari and Gadadhara see en/24, v. 1. In the 1983 edition the Shesha-khanda is divided into chapters; chapter 1 bears the same title as Bhaktisiddhanta's section: "The Lord's Journey to the South."
[^3]: Kurma-kshetra (Shrikurmam) is a shrine of Vishnu in the form of the Tortoise on the coast south of Orissa. Vv. 3–14 — cf. Murari 3.13–3.14.13–20 and the Chaitanya-charitamrita (Madhya 7): the brahmana Kurma receives the Lord at his home, and the leper brahmana Vasudeva is healed by His embrace. Lochana Dasa does not mention leprosy; "became pure" (v. 6) may hint at the healing.
[^4]: Cf. Murari 3.14.19 and the Chaitanya-charitamrita (Madhya 7.128): "Whomever you meet, instruct him about Krishna."
[^5]: Jiyada-Nrisimha is the Deity of Varaha-Nrisimha at Simhachalam, near Vishakhapatnam. The Lord tells the tale of this Deity (vv. 16–81) also in Murari (3.14.21–32), more briefly; in the Chaitanya-charitamrita (Madhya 8) the Lord only visits Him.
[^6]: "Punda" — in Murari (3.14.23) "Pundaya"; it may be not a name but the name of a caste. The words "shasa" (in the 1983 edition "shasha", apparently cucumbers) and "mayambu" (so too in Murari) are unclear; they are given as they read.
[^7]: Vv. 22+1 — 22+2 are found only in the 1983 edition; the 1903 edition gives them only in a footnote, as an additional reading of one manuscript. The 1983 edition reads the second line of v. 22: "To whom shall I entrust the guarding of the crop?"
[^8]: Vv. 23+1 — 23+3 are found only in the 1983 edition; the 1903 edition gives them only in a footnote, as an additional reading of one manuscript.
[^9]: The boar is the Lord Himself in the form of Varaha; by the name of Rama which He utters the cowherd recognizes Bhagavan (v. 30). Cf. Murari 3.14.24–25.
[^10]: The sense of the first line is not quite clear; we understand: "you wounded Me, and I ate your crop — we are quits."
[^11]: Vv. 41+1 — 41+2 are found only in the 1983 edition; the 1903 edition gives them only in a footnote, as an additional reading of one manuscript.
[^12]: Vv. 42+1 — 42+2 are found only in the 1983 edition; the 1903 edition gives them partly in a footnote, as an additional reading of one manuscript. "Your body is My body" are words of the Lord's love for His devotee (cf. Bhagavata 9.4.68: "The sadhus are My heart, and I am their heart"), not a doctrine of the identity of the soul and God.
[^13]: Vv. 59+1 — 59+5 are found only in the 1983 edition; the 1903 edition gives them only in a footnote, as an additional reading of one manuscript. The thought that a devotee is not judged by birth is constant in Lochana Dasa and in all Gaudiya Vaishnavas.
[^14]: Cf. Murari 3.14.26–28: at the Lord's word the place is sprinkled with milk, and He reveals His form. The body of the Deity rises out of the earth up to the knees; the feet remain hidden.
[^15]: Vv. 71+1 — 71+4 are found only in the 1983 edition; the 1903 edition gives them only in a footnote, as an additional reading of one manuscript.
[^16]: Vv. 72+1 — 72+12 are found only in the 1983 edition; the 1903 edition gives them only in a footnote, as an additional reading of one manuscript. Cf. the wives of the brahmanas (yajna-patnis) who went to Krishna in spite of their husbands' prohibition (Bhagavata 10.23): devotion to Krishna stands above worldly ties, and a righteous husband accepts it as his good fortune.
[^17]: V. 77+1 is found only in the 1983 edition; the 1903 edition gives it only in a footnote, as an additional reading of one manuscript. The tale of the wives who turned to stone at the feet of the Deity is a local legend; in Murari (3.14.29–30) the merchant comes with one wife, and nothing is said of her fate. Attaining Krishna as Husband (pati) is no worldly bond: Krishna is the true Lord and Beloved of every soul.
[^18]: So too in Murari (3.14.31–32): the merchant asks that the Lord bear his name — "for Hari is ever subject to His devotees."
[^19]: So in A and C and in Murari (3.15.1). The 1983 edition reads Vidyanagara (in v. 84 too), as in the Chaitanya-charitamrita (Madhya 8), where Ramananda is governor of a province on the Godavari under King Prataparudra, and the Lord meets him on the bank of the Godavari. Kanchi (Kanchipuram) lies much farther south; in Lochana Dasa the Lord only afterwards goes "by way of the Godavari" to Panchavati (v. 116).
[^20]: Vv. 85+1 — 85+2 are found only in the 1983 edition; the 1903 edition gives them only in a footnote (after v. 86), as an additional reading of one manuscript.
[^21]: Ramananda Raya, son of Bhavananda Raya, was a high nobleman of Orissa; Lochana Dasa calls him now "prince" (rajaputra), now "king".
[^22]: While remaining at the gate, the Lord at the same time appears to Ramananda in the inner apartments: He can be present in many places at once (cf. Krishna's presence in every palace of Dvaraka, Bhagavata 10.69). "Portion" (amsha) here is not a part cut off from the whole: the Lord is fully present in each of His manifestations.
[^23]: Vv. 96–99 — cf. Murari 3.15.2–3: having finished his worship of Krishna, Ramananda contemplates the son of the king of Vraja and three times sees the form of Gauranga, and on opening his eyes he sees Him before him.
[^24]: Vv. 104+1 — 104+2 are found only in the 1983 edition; the 1903 edition gives them only in a footnote, as an additional reading of one manuscript and of a printed edition. In Murari (3.15.4) the Lord says: "How is it that you do not remember, O bee at the lotus feet of Sri Radhika?"
[^25]: The 1903 edition gives vv. 107–110 only in a footnote, as an additional reading of one manuscript and of a printed edition. The kanada flower is apparently a dark-blue flower (commelina) to which Krishna's colour is compared. According to the Chaitanya-charitamrita (Madhya 8), Ramananda first sees the Lord as a dark cowherd boy, and then the Lord reveals to him the form of Rasaraja and Mahabhava — Krishna and Radha in one body.
[^26]: Vv. 112+1 — 112+4 are found only in the 1983 edition; the 1903 edition gives them in a footnote, as an additional reading of one manuscript (with one more couplet about the weeping queens). The vision of Radha and Krishna on a throne in the jewelled temple of Vrindavana is a vision of the eternal, transcendental lila of the Divine Couple. Red garments are the garments of a sannyasi.
[^27]: Vv. 113+1 — 113+4 are found only in the 1983 edition; they are absent from the 1903 edition. In the Chaitanya-charitamrita (Madhya 8) the Lord likewise spends several days and nights with Ramananda in talks about Krishna, and Krishnadasa Kaviraja relates these talks in detail.
