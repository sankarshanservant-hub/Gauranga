# Adi-khanda, part 14 (portion 22)

*The second wedding (conclusion): the adhivasa of the Lord and of Vishnupriya; the wedding morning; the bridegroom's attire and the bride's beauty; the wedding procession and the excitement of the women of Nadia; the welcoming of the bridegroom, the exchange of glances and the giving away of the bride in Sanatana's house; the wedding night, the kushandika, Sanatana's farewell; Shachi welcomes the couple home*

*Raga ramakeli; disha: "Hari, Rama, Narayana — Shachi's darling, golden Gora!"*

**79.** Then the pandit, with great joy in his heart,
happily fixes an auspicious day and an auspicious moment.

**80.** And here the Lord Vishvambhara, learning of it,
called an astrologer to His house and fixes the auspicious day.

**81.** After deliberation they fixed the day, a wonderful time:
an auspicious hour, an auspicious lagna, tithi and a favourable nakshatra[^1].

**82.** At the time of the adhivasa, holy men, brahmanas and good people
gathered and arranged for the Lord all that the auspicious rite required.

**83.** Joyful Shachi-devi, gathering the married women,
celebrates her son's festival, giving all kinds of gifts:

**84.** oil, turmeric and sindura for the forehead,
puffed rice, bananas, sweets and betel.

**85.** Joyfully all the married women sing auspicious songs;
all the brahmanas perform the Lord's adhivasa.

**86.** All the quarters are adorned with incense, lamps and banners;
after the svastivachana blessings they worship the gods.

**87.** The brahmanas recite the Vedas, auspicious conches sound,
all kinds of instruments play — pataha drums and mridangas.

**88.** On all sides the wives of noble families cry "Jaya, jaya!";
the Lord's adhivasa fell at an excellent hour.

**89.** The brahmanas were honoured with scents, sandal and garlands,
with camphor, betel and many ornaments.

**90.** At this time the venerable pandit Sanatana,
full of reverence, his heart rejoicing,

**91.** sent brahmanas and virtuous brahmana wives,
wishing to perform the adhivasa of his son-in-law,

**92.** while he himself performs the adhivasa of his daughter;
her body glitters with jewelled ornaments.

**93.** He duly performs the worship of the gods and the ancestors;
throughout the adhivasa "Jaya, jaya!" resounds without cease.

**94.** The brahmanas recite the Vedas, auspicious conches sound,
dundubhis joyfully resound, mridangas play.

**95.** Thus the adhivasa of the two was performed;
on the next day the Lord rose at dawn[^2].

**95+1.** All kinds of instruments sound, cries of "Jaya!" and "ulu-ulu";
full of rasa, the women went along, swaying[^3].

**95+2.** Rapt in rasa, how many feelings rise in their hearts!
The sweetness of Gauranga is what their hearts have gained.

**95+3.** On the moonlit night [rang] auspicious songs;
all that is proper for Vishnupriya's wedding they performed.

**95+4.** Thus, having fetched the water, the wives of noble families
came at dawn to Shachi's house.

**96.** After His morning rites the Lord bathed in the Ganga
and performed the nandimukha-shraddha as prescribed.

**97.** Having completed the worship of the gods and the ancestors,
the Lord bathes again, as is proper before a wedding.

**98.** Then the barber did his work,
and the wives of noble families anoint His body.

**98+1.** They apply fragrant amalaki, oil and turmeric;
one, touching His divine body, sank into a swoon of bliss[^4].

**98+2.** Another joyfully massages His feet;
another's face is uncovered — where has shame gone?

**98+3.** From the eyes of some flow tears of joy,
the bodies of others tremble at the breeze from His body.

**98+4.** The frenzied women bathe [the Lord],
openly showing the long-cherished wish of their hearts.

**98+5.** One, leaning, sinks [against Him], another pours Ganga water;
cries of "Jaya!" and "ulu-ulu" are heard with auspicious shouts.

**99.** In the town of Nadia there is joy and jubilation:
Vishvambhara's wedding is an all-auspicious festival.

**100.** Then Mahaprabhu Vishvambhara Raya
adorns His body with all that is fitting:

**101.** divine jewelled ornaments, a cloth with a red border;
the breeze from Gora's body spreads fragrance.

**102.** The natural fragrance of His divine body — and divine perfumes besides;
a sandal tilaka on the forehead, and the beautiful moon-like face.

**103.** The nails shine like moons, rings are on the fingers;
the radiance of His body dazzles — one cannot look at it.

**104.** The soft red lips are like bimba fruits;
by the ears the cheeks shine like bandhuka flowers.

**105.** Armlets and bracelets on the arms, anklets on the feet;
seeing Him, the nagaris tremble at heart[^5].

**105+1.** All the nagaris surrounded Gauranga —
as the stars in their beauty encircle the moon[^6].

**105+2.** All the women grew drunk with the wine of Madana;
casting off shame and fear, they stood gazing at His face.

**106.** And here, in his own house, the pandit Sri Sanatana
adorns his daughter with jewelled ornaments.

**107.** She was arrayed with scents, sandal and garlands;
yet even without adornment the radiance of her body lit up the land.

**108.** Vishnupriya's body surpasses the purest gold;
she glitters like lightning that has taken form.

**109.** Her braid surpasses a serpent and captivates the minds of sages;
the sindura on her forehead — to what can it be compared?

**110.** The curve of her brows is lovely as Ananga's bow;
her nose surpasses a parrot's beak — it is supremely beautiful.

**111.** Her eyes surpass the eyes of a doe;
her charming ears surpass the ears of a vulture[^7].

**112.** Her lips surpass bandhuli flowers — a peerless beauty;
her teeth surpass pearls — a glittering lustre.

**113.** Her throat surpasses the conch, enchanting the world;
surpassing the lion's neck, beautiful is her neck.

**114.** Her arms surpass in beauty golden lotus stalks;
her palms, one would think, surpass red lotuses.

**115.** Her fingers are lovelier than champaka buds;
her nails surpass the moon, their beauty glitters.

**116.** The breadth of her bosom surpasses Sumeru;
her waist surpasses the lion's — so very slender.

**117.** Her hips surpass the wheel of Kamadeva's chariot,
her thighs the trunks of the ramakadali plantain.

**118.** The Creator fashioned a beauty surpassing the three worlds;
the soles of her feet, red as the lotus, glow.

**119.** The row of her moon-like nails surpasses the spotless moon;
by their rays even one blind from birth received sight.

**120.** She was arrayed with scents, sandal and garlands;
yet even without adornment the radiance of her body lights up the land.

**121.** The maiden who enchants the three worlds surpasses Parvati;
by her ornaments the earth glitters.

**122.** Then, seeing that the auspicious hour had come,
[Sanatana] sent a brahmana to bring the bridegroom.

**123.** The brahmana stood before the Lord
and humbly says: "The brahmana [Sanatana] has sent me."

**124.** Seeing the dazzling radiance of His body, the brahmana
considers himself blessed: "Blessed is Sanatana too!"

**125.** He said before the Lord: "Listen, Vishvambhara:
the auspicious hour is near — come quickly.

**126.** What can I say in Your presence?
You are God, Bhagavan: I see it with my own eyes."

**127.** Then at that auspicious moment the Lord Vishvambhara
set out in a palanquin, gently smiling.

**128.** Shachi with the married women gives her blessing;
the Lord took the dust of His mother's feet upon His head.

**129.** Conches, dundubhis, bheurs and kahals sound,
dandimas and muharis sound, dindimas sound sweetly[^8].

**130.** Vinas, flutes, kapilasas, rababs, upangas
and pakhoyajs all play together.

**131.** Patahas and mridangas, bronze cymbals and karatalas sound,
shingas and bargos sound together with the sahinis.

**132.** All kinds of instruments sound — I do not know their names;
in front dancers dance, the chanting of the Vedas is heard.

**133.** Singers sing songs, the bhat bards sing praises;
surrounded by His friends, the Lord moved forward.

**134.** In the town of Nadia every house was astir;
people run to look, waving their arms.

*Raga bihagara*

**135.** They put on silk saris and bodices of fine cloth,
tie their hair in a knot in the kanada style;
stringing pearls and setting them in gold,
they let red tassels fall down their backs.

**136.** The beauties, the beauties — the ladies of Nadia —
are carried away into an ocean of joy:
"Let us go and see Vishvambhara's wedding,
we will sing auspicious songs!"

**137.** Some put on garments, silk saris,
gandharaja and champaka flowers in their ears;
they move with an elephant's stately gait — they cannot walk fast —
and glance sidelong with the eyes of a doe.

**138.** Their wagtail eyes, tinged with kajal, —
a restless pair of pupils —
have sunk in the mire of Gora's beauty
and, languid and entranced, will move no more.

**139.** In every quarter all the ladies of the town,
hearing the sound, ran out;
young girls ran with combs in their hair,
forgetting to arrange their clothes.

**140.** Young women, abandoning thoughts of their husbands,
leaving their families and kin,
do not arrange their clothes or ornaments,
as though forever maddened[^9].

**141.** Like motionless lightning — such is their gait,
the gait of she-swans;
row upon row, holding hands,
they are like autumn moons.

**142.** Women or men — all run in one direction,
no one heeds anyone;
jostling on the road, they run in a frenzy
to see the face of Gauranga.

**142+1.** The town of Nadiya is an ocean of joy,
Gauranga is the treasure-nagara;
on all sides people run, festive music sounds —
[the women] are like playful does[^10].

**143.** Children, the old, the blind, the lame, the crippled,
the sick look on with longing;
some, joining hands with their friends,
run without tying their clothes.

**144.** Seeing His face, the women, in the pangs of Madana,
lose their composure;
all the beasts and birds, seeing Gauranga,
stand still, row upon row.

**145.** Surrounded by friends, divinely adorned,
a crown just above the forehead…
Lochana says: seeing [Him], the nagaris lost their heads,
the doors of their hearts flew open.

*Raga varadi; tune "dhulakhela"[^11]*

**146.** Thus Vishvambhara came to the pandit's house;
the best of brahmanas was in an ocean of joy.
Taking in his hands water for the feet and arghya,
he went out to meet the Lord: "Blessed, blessed is Shachi's son!"

**147.** Offering water for the feet and arghya, he led Gaurachandra in,
and He stood on the wedding platform.
All cry "Hari!", hundreds upon hundreds of lamps burn,
but Gaura's body outshines them.

**148.** The married women rejoice, again and again the "ulu-ulu" cries ring out,
conches, dundubhis and instruments sound;
and there the married women, gathering — some in silk saris —
adorn themselves to circle round the Lord.

**149.** Having prepared all for the nirmanchhana, ahead of the married women
comes the mother of the bride;
her feet do not touch the ground, her whole body rejoices
at the sight of Vishvambhara, the jewel of virtues.

**150.** Thinking of Gaurahari, filling her heart with Him —
how many longings rise in her heart! —
"Vishnupriya, my daughter, will be a match for Him":
thinking so, she restrained her thoughts[^12].

**151.** Not only do the married women shine with beauty, with jewelled lamps in their hands, —
on top of that come the rays of Gora's body;
by the fragrance of that body the women all but die in frenzy
and with great effort hold their hearts in check.

**152.** Having circled Gorachand seven times and welcomed the bridegroom,
they pour curd on His lotus feet;
when the time comes to go back into the house, they gaze at Gora's face —
and cannot turn away: [they are held by] the fragrance of His body.

**153.** The pandit Sri Sanatana honours the bridegroom:
divine garments, divine ornaments;
with divine scents and sandal he anoints His body,
he placed a garland of malati round His neck.

**154.** His body is beautiful as Sumeru — and as if the heavenly Ganga,
dividing in two, were flowing in two streams:
a garland of malati on the body of Gora!
Seeing this, the pandit thrilled all over with joy[^13].

**155.** Then Sanatana Mishra, jewel among brahmanas,
ordered his daughter to be brought;
the beauty of the three worlds sits on a jewelled throne —
from the radiance of her body it was as if lightning flashed.

**156.** They brought her to the Lord — she who enchants the mind of the world,
Vishnupriya, whose name is Mahalakshmi[^14];
with tremulous, playful eyes she looks at Gauranga's face
with a gentle, peerless smile.

**157.** Circling the Lord, going round Him seven times,
she bows [to Him] with joined palms;
the screen between them was removed — four eyes met;
both shower each other with flowers.

**158.** Cries of joy arose, all repeat: "Hari, Hari!";
the bridegroom and bride waved the chhamuni[^15];
all say: "Wonderful, wonderful! — like the Moon and Rohini";
some say: "Parvati and Shankara."

**159.** Then the Lord Vishvambhara, gently smiling,
sat on an excellent throne;
Sanatana, the best of brahmanas, gives away his daughter,
offering her at His lotus feet.

**160.** All that was fitting — all kinds of gifts — he presented;
the two sat together.
After the wedding the two, in the house of the brahmana Sanatana,
ate together.

**161.** The joyful married women plot among themselves,
taking betel and camphor in their hands;
they gazed their fill at Sri Gaurangachand Hari, —
the Thakura sat in the bridal chamber.

**162.** Vishvambhara and Vishnupriya went and sat in the bridal chamber,
and the married women think to themselves:
"This Vishnupriya is Lakshmi, and Vishnu has become Vishvambhara —
thus They have come to the earth."

**163.** Those skilled in all the arts, taking divine garlands in their hands,
put them round Vishvambhara's neck;
some voice the desire that was in their hearts:
"This is my secret: I have sold myself to You"[^16].

**164.** Some anoint His body with scents and sandal —
at the touch their frenzy grows;
starting all sorts of talk, they lean against His body —
thus the desire of their whole life was fulfilled.

**164+1.** All the beauties who were there grew frenzied
and openly speak their hearts' secrets;
rapt in rasa they laugh and, swaying, lean against Gora,
maddened, overwhelmed by desire (kama)[^17].

**165.** One, filling a tray with betel, sets it at the Lord's feet
and offers a handful of flowers;
this is what is in her heart: "You are my Lord birth after birth" —
saying so, she gives herself [to Him].

**166.** Thus the jewel of virtues spent the night —
the good fortune of the married women was revealed;
in the morning, rising, the Lord, the treasury of virtues,
duly performed that same day the rite of kushandika[^18].

**167.** On the next day the Lord, seating the bride at His left,
said: "I shall go home."
The household honour [Him] — each as he wished;
cries of "Jaya, jaya!" and the sound of conches arose.

**168.** The brahmana Sanatana and his wife, placing in [Their] hands
betel nuts, sandal and garlands,
lay durva grass and rice on Their heads, wish Them good and well-being
and bless Them: "Live long!"

**169.** Then the heart of Vishnupriya-devi trembled,
she looks into the faces of her father and mother;
and [they], in a voice full of tears, give themselves over [to the Lord]
with humble entreaty.

**170.** Sanatana, the best of brahmanas, says with an aching heart:
"What can I say to You?
By Your own goodness You have accepted my daughter —
what worthy of You can I give?

**171.** And I say this too: You are my son-in-law —
blessed am I, blessed is my house;
blessed is my Vishnupriya, having attained Your lotus feet" —
saying this, he is choked with emotion.

**172.** His eyes brim with tears, his face is flushed,
he speaks haltingly, faltering;
taking Vishnupriya's hand, he placed it in Vishvambhara's hand,
and tears stream from his eyes.

**173.** Then at the auspicious moment the Lord mounted the palanquin —
the hearts of all rejoice;
all kinds of instruments sound, conches and dundubhis roar,
cries of "Hari!" touch the sky.

**174.** In front dancers dance; whatever skill anyone has,
all display it at once;
the Lord rides in the palanquin, people cry "Jaya, jaya!" —
and He arrived at His own home.

**175.** Shachi, rejoicing, taking all that was needed for the nirmanchhana,
together with the married women —
amid auspicious cries of "Jaya, jaya!", while all repeat "Hari!" —
circles [the couple] with all kinds of gifts and casts them away[^19].

**176.** In front is the auspicious pot, the bhat bards sing praises,
the brahmanas chant the Vedas;
holding Vishnupriya by the hand, Vishvambhara Gaurahari
enters the house at the auspicious moment.

**177.** Overwhelmed with love and joy, embracing Vishvambhara,
[Shachi] kisses His moon-like face;
beside herself with joy, she goes among the married women
and dances, holding her daughter-in-law in her arms.

**178.** Forgetting herself in happiness, she gives people all kinds of gifts,
and everyone was satisfied.
Seeing Vishvambhara and Vishnupriya together,
Lochana sings the glories of Gora[^20].

*Thus ends the description of the second wedding.*

[^2]: In the 1983 edition the second line is different: "towards the end of the night the wives went to fetch water" (cf. portion 18, note 1); we follow A.
[^3]: Vv. 95+1 – 95+4 (the fetching of water) are found only in the 1983 edition (absent from Bhaktisiddhanta's edition); the 1903 edition gives them in a note as the additional reading of a manuscript and a printed edition.
[^4]: Vv. 98+1 – 98+5 are found only in the 1983 edition (absent from Bhaktisiddhanta's edition); the 1903 edition has them, without brackets. Lochana Dasa's motif of nagari-bhava (see portion 09, note 9; cf. portion 18, vv. 40+1 – 40+9).
[^5]: Lochana Dasa's motif of nagari-bhava (see portion 09, note 9).
[^6]: Vv. 105+1 – 105+2 are found only in the 1983 edition (absent from Bhaktisiddhanta's edition); the 1903 edition has them.
[^7]: A traditional comparison of Bengali poetry: the graceful curve of the ear is likened to the ear of a vulture (gridhini).
[^8]: On the bheur, kahal, shinga, bargo, muhari and the others see portion 18, note 11, and the glossary. The meaning of the word "dandima" (so in A and B) is unclear.
[^9]: Vv. 135–145 (and 142+1) — again Lochana Dasa's motif of nagari-bhava (see portion 09, note 9; portion 18, note 5).
[^10]: Stanza 142+1 is found only in the 1983 edition (absent from Bhaktisiddhanta's edition); the 1903 edition has it, without brackets.
[^11]: A has "বরাড়ি রাগ — ধূলাখেলা-জাত"; probably an indication of the tune to which the song is sung (so also in the 1983 and 1903 editions).
[^12]: This stanza is absent in the 1983 edition; the 1903 edition gives it in a note as an additional reading of the printed edition. The bride's mother, seeing the bridegroom, rejoices that her daughter is a worthy match for Him and restrains her excitement.
[^13]: The white garland on the Lord's golden body is like the Ganga running down the golden mountain Sumeru in two streams; cf. Murari 1.14.22–23 and portion 19, v. 100.
[^14]: So Lochana Dasa names Vishnupriya; cf. v. 162, where the married women recognize in Vishnupriya Lakshmi and in Vishvambhara Vishnu. In the Gaudiya Vaishnava understanding Vishnupriya is the Lord's Bhu-shakti, Lakshmipriya His Shri-shakti (see portion 21, note 9).
[^15]: On the word "chhamuni" see portion 19, note 5.
[^16]: Vv. 161–165 are the scene in the bridal chamber (basara, see portion 19, note 11), where by custom the women joke with the bridegroom; in Lochana Dasa it is again the motif of nagari-bhava (see portion 09, note 9).
[^17]: Stanza 164+1 is found only in the 1983 edition (absent from Bhaktisiddhanta's edition); the 1903 edition has it. "Kama" (desire) is the word by which the scriptures speak also of the gopis' love; the Chaitanya-charitamrita (Adi 4.164–171) explains that such "kama" is pure prema, the desire to please Krishna, not oneself. Lochana Dasa transfers these images to the women of Nadiya (nagari-bhava; see portion 09, note 9).
[^18]: Kushandika — the rite at the sacred fire that concludes the wedding (see portion 18, note 2).
[^19]: Nichhani — auspicious objects are circled around the couple and cast away, warding off all that is inauspicious; cf. the nirmanchhana (portion 19, note 17).
[^20]: Cf. Murari 1.14.26–33. In A the last two numbers are printed as "১৭০" and "১৭৬"; 177 and 178 are restored.
