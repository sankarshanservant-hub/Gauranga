# Adi-khanda, part 2 (portion 10)

*Balya-lila (continued): the first rice-feeding and the name Vishvambhara; the first steps; Shachi's lullaby; at night the gods enthrone the child, and He dances with them singing of Radha and Krishna; the sound of ankle-bells on bare feet; pranks and broken pots; a rite for the boy's welfare; the Lord sits among discarded pots and teaches His mother about pure and impure*

**103.** One, two, three, four, five, six months [passed] —
and on the day of the first rice-feeding the naming was performed.

**104.** Mishra Purandara celebrates a festival for his son;
the golden body [of the child] is decked with ornaments:

**105.** armlets and bracelets on His arms, a pearl necklace on His neck,
a golden chain around His waist, and anklets on His feet.

**106.** His palms and soles look as if rubbed with vermilion,
His lips are a bandhuli flower, His eyes red lotuses.

**107.** It is as if lightning had been rubbed onto Gora's limbs here and there:
His body glitters so that one cannot look at it.

**108.** Because He sustains the world, He was given the name Vishvambhara[^1]:
such was the message of Sarasvati — this is the Supreme Person.

**109.** Now, holding the fingers of father and mother,
He takes two steps and falls, unsteady on His feet.

**110.** Indistinctly, by halves, softly, softly He babbles —
as if nectar were welling up in an ocean of moonlight.

**111.** So day by day He toddles about the courtyard;
sufferings of every kind are gone, the world is cooled.

**112.** The feet that Lakshmi caresses are on the lap of the earth:
from love the goddess Prithivi forgets herself.

**113.** In the sky there is one moon, but on the ground ten moons[^2]:
the brilliance of their rays blinds the eyes of those who look.

**114.** And ten more moons are at the tips of His fingers:
if a sinner but looks, the darkness flees from his heart.

**115.** The moon of His beautiful face is king of many millions of moons;
His brows are Kama's bow: Kama worshipped [Him] by offering his bow.

**116.** What more can I say of the moonlight of His mercy?
It cuts through the darkness in the heart and does not spare it.[^3]

**117.** Who can tell of His childhood pastimes?
Behaving like an ordinary child, He sanctified the earth.

**118.** His elder brother is the worthy Vishvarupa:
endowed with every virtue, in a short time he mastered all the scriptures.

**119.** Who can tell the truth of his greatness —
of him whose younger brother is Mahaprabhu Vishvambhara?

**120.** Day by day the Lord reveals His mercy;
hearing of it, the heart of this Lochana Dasa rejoices.

*Raga varadi*

*Moon, moon, moon up in the sky —
who will pluck it and bring it to me?
Wiping off its stain,
I will paint a design on my Gora's forehead.
Come, come, come! My golden little son Nimai
is crying — He wants to sleep;
when He is fretful, every little word of His
is sweeter than nectar.*

**121.** "Nimai's father will come right now,
bringing thickened milk and bananas.
Look, little one, here comes the dreadful bogey[^4] —
close Your eyes and go to sleep!"

**122.** His face a golden lotus, His eyes red lotuses,
the pupils half closed —
it seems as if in a sea of honey
bees had half drowned.

**123.** Making a bed — a silk coverlet
with a fine-cloth quilt upon it —
Shachi Thakurani, taking her son in her arms,
lay down on her side.

**124.** One breast He holds in His mouth and sucks now and then,
and fingers the other with His little hand.
Lochana says: all this
is the play of the Crest-jewel of the gods in the body of a child.

*Raga dhanashi; disha: "Are are haya!" (murchha). "Listen to this wondrous tale, the song of Gora's virtues — a name auspicious to the ear! O-ki are, o-ki are haya!"*

**125.** Listen carefully to what happened on another day —
how the Lord of His own will revealed Himself.

**126.** In one room is Jagannatha, in another Shachi:
taking her son with her, Shachi sleeps happily.

**127.** The empty room filled with many soldiers and nobles;
seeing such a thing, Shachi was frightened.

**128.** All the gods came and, taking [Him] from Shachi's arms,
quickly seated [Him] on a jewelled throne.

**129.** Performing the bathing rite (abhisheka) and worship of many kinds,
they circle [Him] and fall down, clasping [His] feet.

**130.** All of them sound conches and bells again and again,
and cries of "Jaya, jaya!" and "Hari!" spread all around:

**131.** "Glory, glory to Jagannatha, the protector of all![^5]
In the Kali-yuga You will nourish us all.

**132.** You will give us all the rasa that is the treasure of Vrindavana.
This is our prayer at Your feet, O Vishvambhara!"

**133.** Seeing this, mother Shachi starts again and again;
"My son, my son!" Shachi cries in great fear.

**134.** She has no fear for herself — her life is in her son:
she sent the boy away to Jagannatha:

**135.** "Your father is sleeping there, in the Deity's room;
go there and sleep happily in his arms."

**136.** Vishvambhara went as His mother told Him —
and from His bare feet came the sound of ankle-bells.

**137.** When the Crest-jewel of the gods came out,
all the gods followed Him with joined palms.

**138.** The Lord said: "O gods, make Me dance:[^6]
sing of the pastimes of Radha and Krishna," — so He told them all.

**139.** The gods blended the prema of Radha and Krishna into their song
and offered it in bliss — and Gaurachandra took it up.

**140.** He Himself weeps and makes the gods weep;
"Radha, Radha, Govinda!" the Lord cries in the courtyard.

**141.** He calls out: "Kalindi, Yamuna, Vrindavana!" —
He calls "Radha, Radha!" in great bliss.

**142.** Seeing her son's play, Shachi fainted;
hearing the noise, Jagannatha came running in alarm.

**143.** Jagannatha calls: "Shachi, what sounds are these I hear?" —
and frightened Shachi-rani calls out loudly too.

**144.** Coming outside, both took their son in their arms;
seeing His bare feet, they forget themselves.

**145.** At once the deeds of Krishna came to their minds;
Shachi-devi told what she had seen in her room:

**146.** "The Four-faced one, the Five-faced one[^7] and all the other gods
came in celestial vehicles and served the boy.

**147.** In the courtyard our son danced, crying 'Radha-Krishna' —
I heard it too, but it seemed to me like a dream.

**148.** Seeing this, I sent Him to you in fear —
and I heard the sound of ankle-bells from His bare feet.

**149.** Such a boy, such a divine, beautiful form —
who knows when someone may cast an evil eye on Him?

**150.** Seven daughters of mine have died — this child is what is left;[^8]
if anything happens to Him, I shall not live any longer.

**151.** I have not many — [He is] the apple of my eye;
like a blind man's staff — such is my treasure.

**152.** Home, all I possess, wealth, the soul in the body, the body itself — [all this is He];
without Gorachand I cannot live.

**153.** Think of a remedy to ward off misfortune:
may all the gods, from first to last, grant the boy their blessing."

**154.** In such musings [the] night [passed], and in the morning
Shachi's son is playing with the boys.

**155.** Now He rolls about the courtyard, grey with dust;
seeing it, His mother speaks plaintively:

**156.** "Your body a golden doll, Your little face so lovely —
[even] the moon in the sky I cannot compare with it.

**157.** Why do You, with such a beautiful body, fall in the dust
and roll about in it? I implore You by Your mother's life!"

**158.** Saying this, she brushes off the dust and kisses His face;
her body thrills with rapture, her eyes grow red.

**159.** Then, some days later, Shachi's son
began to roam outside the house with His companions.

**160.** Under the trees on the bank of the Ganga He wanders, playing;
He plays the "monkey game" — standing on one leg.

**161.** Shachi heard that Gaurahari was on the bank of the Ganga
and went to catch Him with a switch in her hand.

**162.** Knee upon knee — He stands on one leg;
seeing it, His mother calls in a piercing voice.

**163.** Seeing His mother, the Lord runs away,
looking back like a frolicking elephant.

**164.** "Catch Him, catch Him!" shouts Shachi-rani;
ahead runs my Lord, the jewel of brahmanas.

**165.** Shachi wants to catch Him and cannot;
the Lord ran into the house.

**166.** All the pots and vessels that were in the house,
while [His mother cried] "catch Him, catch Him!", He smashed, dashing them to the ground.

**167.** Shachi stands with a finger to her nose and looks;
the Lord Vishvambhara stands with His face lowered.

**168.** He trembles all over with shame;
He stands with downcast face, tears streaming from His eyes —

**169.** as if wagtails sitting on the moon
were letting fall a pearl necklace they had swallowed.[^9]

**170.** Seeing Gora's face, Shachi was filled with love
and says: "Come into my arms, my darling!"

**171.** Taking His hand, lifting Him into her arms, Shachi-rani says:
"May my home and all I own be given up as Your ransom (nichhani)!"

**172.** Thus Gaurahari performs various pastimes;
Shachi cannot fathom her son's clever ways.

**173.** His boundless deeds are beyond the reach of worldly sense and of the Vedas;
not understanding His conduct, Shachi took it for naughtiness.

**174.** She decided that Nimai was an incorrigibly restless boy,
and in her sorrow Shachi-devi calls upon God.

**175.** One day, gathering all the elderly women,
she humbly asked them all:

**176.** "How I prayed — and God gave me a son;
but He behaves like a madman, He has no sense at all.

**177.** He does one thing and says another — I cannot understand;
He pays no heed to custom or to purity."

**178.** Hearing this, they all began to weep with sorrow;
taking Gorachand on their laps, they all say together:

**179.** "Why, why, child, do You do so much that is wrong?"
Hearing this, Vishvambhara became more restless than ever.

**180.** Seeing it, the women were pained at heart:
what Shachi had told, they saw [for themselves] at once.

**181.** "Since when has your son been like this?"
Shachi says: "I cannot even tell all of it.

**182.** One night I was sleeping with my son in my arms;
all the gods came and filled the room.

**183.** Seating my Nimai on a divine throne,
they fell at His feet and prostrated themselves.

**184.** Waking, I saw such a wonder;
since then — I do not know what spell is upon Him."

**185.** Hearing this, they all gave this opinion:
"It seems some god is dwelling in Him.

**186.** Begin a sacrifice in the name of all the gods:
tell Mishra to bring all the brahmanas.

**187.** Perform a svastyayana[^10] for the boy's welfare,
so that the god, having received worship, may go back to his abode.

**188.** Do not worry, Shachi," they said with certainty,
"having received worship, the god will free you from fear."

**189.** Taking the dust of their feet, [Shachi] bade them all farewell
and, going to Mishra, told him everything.

**190.** Hearing this, the anxious Mishra prepared all that was needed
and, summoning the brahmanas, performed the sacrifice.

**191.** Meanwhile Shachi took Gaurachandra to bathe in the Ganga,
thinking: "Now my son's restlessness is over."

**192.** Ahead of Shachi goes Vishvambhara Raya;
playing, He goes into an impure place.

**193.** Touching discarded pots, He walks on;
seeing it, His mother cries: "Alas, alas!

**194.** My son has become more restless still:
so this is what the svastyayana has done!"

**195.** "Shame, shame!" she cries and scolds Him harshly;
having heard her, Vishvambhara replies with kindly words:

**196.** "What is pure and impure, what is the truth of dharma and adharma?
Not discerning this, the world wanders astray.

**197.** Earth, water, air, fire, ether — these are [all] the forms;
in the whole world there is nothing but these.

**198.** There is no other dharma than the feet of Sri Krishna:
Krishna is the Lord of lords" — such was the essence He told.[^11]

**199.** Hearing this, Shachi-devi was amazed
and, taking Gauranga, bathed in the heavenly river.

**200.** Returning home, Shachi-devi says to Jagannatha:
"Listen, my lord, to what the boy has been doing.

**201.** This son of yours is the embodiment of all sacrifices:[^12]
I know for certain — this is no evil affliction.

**202.** Going into an impure place, He spoke such words —
never was it seen or heard that a child spoke so."

**203.** Hearing this, Jagannatha took his son in his arms:
though He had touched what is impure — all became auspicious.

**204.** "Lamp of our family, apple of my eye!
This body has no other soul than You."

**205.** Saying this, both gaze at their son's face;
overflowing with love, they forget themselves.

**206.** From their reddened eyes tears flow in a hundred streams,
their whole bodies thrill, their words come in halves.

**207.** Looking into each other's faces, both burst out laughing;[^13]
happily this Lochana Dasa sings of Gora's virtues.

[^1]: Vishvambhara — "He who sustains (supports) the universe". In Bhaktisiddhanta's summary the name is given by the wise, because with the Lord's appearance the whole world was filled with bliss; in Murari Gupta (1.06.3) the father gives the name.
[^2]: The ten moons on the ground are His toenails; in the next couplet, His fingernails.
[^3]: Cf. Bhaktisiddhanta's summary: the moon dispels only outer darkness, but the moon of Gaura dispels both outer and inner darkness.
[^4]: *Hau* — a bogey with which children are frightened in Bengal.
[^5]: "Jagannatha" — "Lord of the universe"; it is both an epithet of the Lord and the name of His father.
[^6]: So in the 1983 and 1903 editions (*nachaha amare*); the OCR of Bhaktisiddhanta's edition can also be read *na chaha amare* — "do not look at Me (do not worship Me)". The following lines (the gods sing, the Lord dances) support the first reading.
[^7]: The Four-faced one is Brahma, the Five-faced one Shiva.
[^8]: According to the *Chaitanya-charitamrita* (Adi 13.73), before Vishvarupa eight daughters were born to Shachi one after another and died; Lochana Dasa speaks of seven.
[^9]: The eyes are wagtails (*khanjana*), the face the moon, the tears pearls.
[^10]: *Svastyayana* — a rite to avert misfortune and invoke well-being.
[^11]: Cf. Bhaktisiddhanta's summary: the Lord explained to His mother the worthlessness of worldly notions of pure and impure and instructed her in the transcendental knowledge that Krishna is the Lord of all. The point of v. 197 is not that only matter exists: distinctions of pure and impure concern combinations of the same material elements, while true dharma is service to Krishna's feet. Cf. Murari Gupta (1.06.13–17).
[^12]: Shachi guesses that her son is the Yajna-purusha Himself, the Lord of all sacrifices, so that a rite to appease a "possessing god" is out of place here.
[^13]: The 1983 edition and (in brackets) the 1903 edition have two more couplets before this one: "Then Vishvambhara plays various games with Vishvarupa, with songs and dances; like Indra and Upendra, two brothers born of one mother — seeing them, Shachi and Jagannatha rejoice at heart." They are absent from Bhaktisiddhanta's edition.
