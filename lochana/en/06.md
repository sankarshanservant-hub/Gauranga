# Sutra-khanda, part 6 (portion 06)

*Brahma continues: the Lord in the Kali-yuga is the golden Gaura of whom the* Bhagavata *speaks (11.5.32); explanation of the words "krishna-varna" and "sangopanga", of Garga Muni's verse on the four colours and of the word "tatha"; Krishna is not a yuga-avatara but Bhagavan Himself, and in the Dvapara and the Kali it is He Himself who descends; testimonies of the* Gita, *the "Thousand Names" and the* Bhavishya-purana; *the greatness of the Kali-yuga and of sankirtana*

**348.** Of the three colours in three yugas the sage has told;
now hear attentively the account of the Kali-yuga.

*So in the* Srimad-Bhagavata *(11.5.32):*

**349.** "Him who utters [the name] 'Krishna' but is not dark in lustre,[^1] with His limbs, sub-limbs, weapons and associates,
the intelligent worship with sacrifices consisting chiefly of sankirtana."

**350.** He in whom there are these two syllables, "Krish-na",
the *Bhagavata* calls "krishna-varna".[^2]

**351.** And in lustre He is "not dark" — hear, everyone:
this is why we sing, "Gora, Gora!"

**352.** With His limbs, sub-limbs, weapons and all His associates —
with all of them the Lord descended.

**353.** Balarama is called the limb — so I say "with limbs";
the lesser limbs are ornaments — so "with sub-limbs".[^3]

**354.** The weapons — Sudarshana and the rest — and all the associates,
Prahlada, Narada — all came together [with Him].

**355.** The servants and maidservants of former descents —
He descended with all His own: how many shall I name?

**356.** Of all this the Vaishnavas speak from their realization:
what name each had there and what name now.[^4]

**357.** How can an ordinary man know this?
The mind of the fallen cannot believe it.

**358.** For this reason the sage said:
only he who is intelligent will know it.

**359.** The sacrifice consisting of sankirtana is revealed as dharma —
he who is intelligent finds supreme joy in it.

**360.** Therefore I say: whoever does not accept this
has the explanation "three colours for four yugas".

**361.** [For him] "dark in lustre" and "dark in colour" merge into one,
while the colours of the other two yugas are not so:

**362.** in the Kali and in the Dvapara — in two yugas — one colour;
in two yugas the colour is one — that is his meaning;

**363.** in Satya and Treta there are two colours, white and red,
and then, in Kali and Dvapara, one colour.[^5]

**364.** Why do you say that the order is broken in Garga Muni's words?
The order is not broken — listen, there is great charm in it.

**365.** To express past, future and present,
he speaks of three times within the four yugas:

**366.** Satya and Treta are past, Dvapara is present;
in the Dvapara is the descent of Krishna, and His name is Krishna.

**367.** That is why Garga Muni says "now",
and within the past tense he means the future.

**368.** Knowing what is certain to be,
one speaks of its future in the past tense:

**369.** the learned accept the past tense in the sense of the future —
it points to certainty; that is the hint.[^6]

**370.** And yet the sage put the word "tatha" ("likewise") there:
after saying "white, red", what use is "tatha" in the account?

**371.** By the word "tatha" it is said: just as the white and red named before,
so in the Kali-yuga Hari will be yellow.

**372.** Now, in the Dvapara, this one has become Krishna;
so Garga Muni expressed three times within the four yugas.

**373.** Whoever contemptuously rejects my words,
let him say why the *Bhagavata* uses the word "tatha".

**374.** This is what I say — hear my words;
says Lochana: do not push my words aside.

**375.** Hear yet another wondrous explanation of the verse —
it alone is right, it is the highest proof.

**376.** To this explanation there is a remarkable objection:
Krishna a yuga-avatara? That is quite impossible.

**377.** The other yuga-avataras are described as portions and parts,
[but Krishna] is Bhagavan Himself; the *Bhagavata* is witness.

*So in the* Srimad-Bhagavata *(1.3.28):*

**378.** "All these [avataras] are portions and parts of the Purusha, but Krishna is Bhagavan Himself;
age after age they gladden the world troubled by the enemies of Indra."

**379.** How then can one call Krishna a yuga-avatara?
Why then does the *Bhagavata* say these words?

**380.** The moon of Vrindavana is not a yuga-avatara:
Krishna is the most complete Brahman, says the *Bhagavata*.[^7]

**381.** For this reason I shall say something — listen;
let no one disregard it; pay attention.

*So in the* Srimad-Bhagavata *(10.8.13):*

**382.** "Taking bodies yuga after yuga, he had three colours —
white, red and likewise yellow; now he has become Krishna."

**383.** Garga Muni spoke this with the deepest understanding —
how can we who lack understanding grasp it?

**384.** He who is intelligent learns [its meaning] from devotees;
intelligent people accept it as proof.

**385.** The sage named four colours in four yugas —
an account of the three times: past, future and present.

**386.** He wishes to express three times within four yugas —
all this Vyasa says in one verse.

**387.** Satya, Treta, Dvapara and the Kali yuga;
white, red, yellow and dark — are said of the four yugas.

**388.** If the four yugas had been [named as] four times,
then the three [other] avataras too would have come in order,

**389.** and the statement would be sequential:
as the avataras [come], so the words would follow.

**390.** That is why people see a break of order in such a verse;
[but] by the word "tatha" Garga Muni indicates the future time.[^8]

**391.** Who is the avatara, and whose are the four colours?
Who is the source of avataras — how is this to be judged?

**392.** Bhagavan Himself is born in the Yadu dynasty,
[while at other times] He descends to earth by His portions.

**393.** Why do you explain [the verse] making one word the qualified and the other the qualifier?
This is the doubt here, and hence the ambiguity.[^9]

**394.** In all four yugas [come] partial avataras,
and all of them have a colour according to the yuga.

**395.** To establish dharma and destroy irreligion
there is a partial avatara in every yuga.

**396.** But in the Dvapara [comes] Bhagavan Hari Himself,
the crest-jewel of avataras, above all.

**397.** "Now He has become Krishna," says Garga Muni:
[this is] Shyamasundara Krishna, and "krishna" here is not a colour.

**398.** In every Dvapara [comes] a portion with the name and colour "krishna";
[this time] the Lord Himself assumed that form — hear, this is the meaning.[^10]

**399.** As Krishna in the Dvapara, so [in Kali] Gaurachandra:
in Kali and Dvapara these two are independent.

**400.** In these two yugas [comes] one full avatara;
Vyasa gave an example of this.

*So in the* Brihat-sahasranama-stotra:

**401.** "Worshipping you, O Shambhu, I always accept this boon:
in the Dvapara and the following yugas, appearing as a portion among men and others,
you, by your own invented scriptures, turn people away from Me
and conceal Me, so that this creation may increase ever more."[^11]

**402.** I shall say more — hear the *Bhagavad-gita*,
the Lord's own words from His holy mouth.

*So in the* Srimad-Bhagavad-gita *(4.8):*

**403.** "To protect the righteous, to destroy the wicked
and to establish dharma, I appear age after age."[^12]

**404.** Protection of the righteous, establishment of dharma,
destruction of irreligion — this is the meaning of what was said.

**405.** "Yuge yuge" — I Myself take birth;
in these two yugas I am born Myself.

**406.** With one word "yuga" I mean [the age], and the other "yuga" means [a pair]:
people explain [them] as qualifier and qualified.

**407.** "Yuga" qualifies "yuga" — therefore I say "pair":
one is the Dvapara yuga, the other the Kali yuga.[^13]

**408.** Why do you take "yuge yuge" as the four yugas?
Krishna's descent is full; why do you call it partial?

**409.** Of those four yugas it is spoken elsewhere;
that too I shall tell — attend to it.

*So in the same (4.7):*

**410.** "Whenever dharma declines, O Bharata,
and irreligion rises, then I manifest Myself."

**411.** At whatever time, in whatever yuga, dharma declines
and irreligion rises — know that at that very time

**412.** I manifest Myself; [this] descent in every yuga
[takes place] by means of a portion.[^14]

**413.** This is what I say — hear my words;
says Lochana: do not push my words aside.

**414.** "That in the Kali-yuga Gora is Krishna I have come to know;
you have dispelled my great doubt.

**415.** Hear another wonder — the essence of the Kali-yuga:
in any ashrama the dharma of sankirtana delivers people.[^15]

**416.** Charity, vows, austerity, fire offerings, Vedic study, self-restraint —
these rules and observances [serve only] desires and worldly aims;

**417.** hearing the shruti's promises of fruits, all fall into the bonds of maya;
the glory of [the Lord's] name and virtues the wretched blind do not know.

**418.** The soul bound by the thread of karma wanders on,
and there is no end [for it]: karma cannot be restrained.

**419.** [Only] at the dissolution of the world are all the bonds of karma loosed —
but these bonds are loosed when one asks about Krishna.[^16]

**420.** Such sankirtana of [the Lord's] virtues is the dharma of the Kali-yuga;
not knowing its essence, people call [Kali] terrible and sinful.

**421.** How could [anyone] abolish the dharma of the yuga, sankirtana?
Who but the Lord establishes dharma?

**422.** [Such is] the Lord's former vow in the *Gita*, in His words:
for what reasons the Lord descends.

*So in the* Srimad-Bhagavad-gita *(4.8):*

**423.** "To protect the righteous, to destroy the wicked
and to establish dharma, I appear age after age."

**424.** Protection of the righteous, destruction of irreligion,
establishment of dharma are manifested in every yuga.

**425.** Accept this, then: in the Kali-yuga the dharma is sankirtana,
and the avatara of Kali is Gora; it is never otherwise."

**426.** Saying this, [Brahma] embraces the sage;
beside himself with joy, Brahma forgets himself.

**427.** He says one thing — and another rises [in his heart] by the splendour of Gora's virtues:
he longs to delight all his senses [with them].

**428.** "Hear more: in the Thousand Names of the Lord
names from two [periods] of one [appearance] stand in one place.[^17]

*So in the* Mahabharata, *Shanti-parva:*

**429.** 'Golden in colour, with limbs like molten gold, of beautiful form, adorned with sandal;
taking sannyasa, equipoised, peaceful, devoted to steadfastness and peace.'

**430.** His body is molten gold, His lustre golden;
having taken sannyasa, He is the supremely wise.

**431.** Hear Krishna's vow in the *Bhavishya-purana*:
'In Kali I shall be born' — three times this word is said.

*So in the* Bhavishya-purana:

**432.** 'I shall be born, I shall be born, I shall be born — there is no doubt:
in Kali, at the beginning of sankirtana, I shall be the son of Shachi.'[^18]

**433.** Hear attentively yet another wonder
and consider in your mind the essence of the dharma of the Kali-yuga.

**434.** Everyone says the Kali-yuga is sinful:
irreligion is manifest, dharma is weak in conduct.

**435.** [But] the sankirtana of Hari's name is its dharma,
and this name of Hari is the essence of all dharmas.

**436.** The fruits of charity, vows, austerity, fire offerings, knowledge and japa,
and liberation without effort, the power of the name alone bestows.

**437.** Even a worldly man who, amid his pleasures, thinks of the name —
[the name] first gives him enjoyment and then bestows devotion to Hari.[^19]

**438.** If a person of faith sings Hari's virtues,
the Lord, leaving all pleasures, runs after him.

**439.** Such sankirtana of Krishna's name and virtues
He made the dharma for the sinful Kali-yuga.

**440.** The nature of the yuga is one thing, and the dharma of the yuga that I name is another:
in the sinful Kali-yuga this is the highest dharma.

**441.** If you ask why — because sin is hard to cut:
[for this] He revealed the great sword, the sankirtana of the name.

**442.** Why do the people of Satya and the other yugas ask to be born in Kali?
[Because] in the Kali-yuga they will become devoted to Hari's name.

*So in the* Srimad-Bhagavata *(11.5.38):*

**443.** 'The people of Krita and the other yugas, O king, desire to be born in Kali,
for in Kali there will be [people] wholly devoted to Narayana.'

**444.** Why, in the descent of Krishna, appearing with all His potencies,
did He not give prema-bhakti to people of sinful hearts?[^20]

**445.** Tell me: in what other yuga was there such mercy?
Which avatara gives prema to those who do not worship?

**446.** For destroying sin there are dharma, rites, holy places —
but do you know whether the righteous obtain such a treasure?

**447.** Know, then: Kali is the essence of all yugas,
and there is no dharma other than the dharma of sankirtana."

**448.** Such a discourse Brahma set forth;
having heard it, Narada skilfully plays his vina.

**449.** Hearing such a wondrous conversation of Brahma and Narada,
the heart of this Lochana Dasa rejoices.

[^1]: *Krishna-varna*: literally "dark in colour" or "whose utterance (*varna*) is 'Krishna'"; *tvisha akrishna*, "not dark in lustre". In the edition the verse is followed by Bhaktisiddhanta's anvaya and translation (OCR damaged): *krishna-varna* — He who describes Krishna; "not dark in lustre" — golden; the "limbs" (*anga*) — Nityananda and Advaita; the "sub-limbs" (*upanga*) — Srivasa and others; the "weapons" (*astra*) — Hari-nama and the like; the "associates" (*parshada*) — Gadadhara and others. In translation: "The intelligent worship with the sacrifice of sankirtana the Mahapurusha who always reveals the virtues of Krishna and is golden in lustre, together with His limbs, sub-limbs, weapons and associates."
[^2]: A play on words: *varna* means both "colour" and "letter, sound". Cf. *Chaitanya-charitamrita*, Adi 3.53.
[^3]: Lochana Dasa's interpretation: the "limb" (*anga*) is Balarama, i.e. Nityananda; the "sub-limbs" (*upanga*) are ornaments. Krishnadasa Kaviraja (*Chaitanya-charitamrita*, Adi 3.66–76) and Bhaktisiddhanta in his anvaya take the "limbs and sub-limbs" as Nityananda, Advaita, Srivasa and the other associates, and the "weapon" as the holy name.
[^4]: Cf. Kavi Karnapura's *Gaura-ganoddesha-dipika*, which states which associates of Krishna appeared as whom in Gaura-lila.
[^5]: Vv. 360–363 set out the opponent's view: for him verse 11.5.32 speaks of a dark avatara of Kali, so that Kali and Dvapara have one colour, and the four yugas only three colours.
[^6]: Lochana Dasa has in mind the form *asan* ("were") in Garga's verse: the "yellow" one, not yet appeared, is spoken of in the past tense because His appearance is certain.
[^7]: Cf. *Chaitanya-charitamrita*, Adi 2 and 3: Krishna is the original Lord (*svayam bhagavan*), the source of all avataras; when He descends Himself, the yuga-avatara too is present in Him.
[^8]: The reasoning of vv. 385–390 is condensed: had the verse listed four colours one after another as four past yugas, the order would be strict; since Garga lists white, red and yellow in the past tense and says "now" of the dark one, the order seems broken; but "tatha" shows that yellow is the colour of the future, of the Kali-yuga.
[^9]: The sense of the verse is unclear. Perhaps the words "but Krishna is Bhagavan Himself" (*Bhag.* 1.3.28) are meant: which of them is the qualified and which the qualifier (cf. *Chaitanya-charitamrita*, Adi 2, where it is discussed which word in this verse is subject and which predicate).
[^10]: Lochana Dasa takes *krishnatam gatah* not as "has become dark" but as "has become Krishna": the yuga-avatara of every Dvapara is dark, but in this Dvapara Krishna Himself took his place. Cf. *Chaitanya-charitamrita*, Adi 3.5–10: Krishna appears once in a day of Brahma, at the end of the Dvapara (of the twenty-eighth *chatur-yuga*), and in the Kali that follows appears as Sri Chaitanya.
[^11]: This verse (from the *Padma-purana*) is also quoted in the *Chaitanya-charitamrita* (Madhya 6.182), as the Lord's order to Shiva to spread Mayavada scriptures in Kali. In Bhaktisiddhanta's translation in this edition: "I, ever worshipping Shambhu, shall accept this boon: you, appearing as a portion among men in the Dvapara and the following yugas, turn people away from Me by invented scriptures and conceal Me, so that creation may increase." Lochana Dasa cites the verse for the words "in the Dvapara and the following yugas" as an example of the Lord Himself descending in these yugas; this is his own interpretation.
[^12]: Bhaktisiddhanta's anvaya: "the righteous" are those devoted to the Lord's service; "the wicked" are those hostile to devotees; "to establish dharma" is Himself to practise the dharma of each yuga and teach it to living beings.
[^13]: A play on words: *yuga* means both "age" and "pair". According to Lochana Dasa, *yuge yuge* in *Gita* 4.8 means "in the pair of yugas" — Dvapara and Kali, when the Lord Himself descends — while the partial avataras of every yuga are the subject of verse 4.7 (vv. 409–412).
[^14]: So Lochana Dasa. The Gaudiya commentators usually refer both 4.7 and 4.8 to the Lord's descents in general; here the author distinguishes: in every yuga a partial avatara, in Dvapara and Kali the Lord Himself.
[^15]: A has "in an ashrama" (any of the ashramas); the 1983 edition, "under the supreme shelter".
[^16]: Line 1 is obscure; probably "the bonds of karma fall away only at the dissolution of the world [, and even then not for ever]".
[^17]: That is, one verse of the *Vishnu-sahasranama* gives names from two periods of Gaura's life. In Bhaktisiddhanta's translation (the text is partly damaged): the first four names ("golden", "with limbs like gold", "of beautiful form", "adorned with sandal") are marks of the pastimes of household life; the rest, of the pastimes in sannyasa: "taking sannyasa", "equipoised" (absorbed in contemplating Hari's mysteries), "peaceful" (firm in the great sacrifice of Hari-kirtana), "devoted to steadfastness and peace" (to the mahabhava that restrains the non-devotees).
[^18]: So Bhaktisiddhanta understands this verse: "In Kali, at the beginning of sankirtana, I shall be born as the son of Shachi — I shall be born, I shall be born, I shall be born, there is no doubt." In the 1983 edition the first line differs: "Be born on earth, be born, be born in the form of devotees" — the Lord's command to the gods.
[^19]: Cf. *Bhag.* 2.3.10: even one who has all desires, or desires liberation, should worship the Supreme Lord with intense devotion.
[^20]: Cf. *Chaitanya-charitamrita*, Adi 3.14–20 and ch. 4: Krishna reflects that for long He has not given prema-bhakti to the world, and appears as Gaura, giving prema to all without discrimination; cf. Rupa Gosvami's verse *anarpita-charim chirat…*
