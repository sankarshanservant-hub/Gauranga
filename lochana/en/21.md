# Adi-khanda, part 13 (portion 21)

*The end of the Banga-vijaya: the serpent of separation bites Lakshmi, and she departs from this world; Shachi's lament; the Lord returns from East Bengal and consoles His mother with the story of the cursed apsara. The beginning of the second wedding: Shachi seeks the daughter of Sanatana Pandita for her son; the Lord's jest with the astrologer and Sanatana's grief; the Lord dispels his grief*

*Raga vibhasa*

**109.** Thus Shachi lived together with Lakshmi;
but what destiny has ordained cannot be undone.

**110.** Not seeing the Lord, Lakshmi pined at heart:
separation from the Lord was ever present within her.

**111.** Separation took the form of a serpent —
Lakshmi Thakurani knew it in her heart.[^1]

**112.** A huge serpent bit Lakshmi on the foot;
in dismay Shachi ponders within herself.

**113.** From the burning pain of the bite Lakshmi writhes;
seeing this, Shachi-devi fell into great distress.

**114.** She called a snake-charmer who knew many mantras
and inquired about many remedies.

**115.** They tried hard, but the poison does not recede;
Shachi was greatly afraid and fell into despair.

**116.** Seeing that her time had come, all gave up their efforts
and, remembering Sri Hari, carried her into the waters of the Ganga.

**117.** They placed a garland of tulasi round her neck;
on all sides all the people chant the name of Hari.

**118.** Lakshmi went to the Lord — the people did not understand it,
but all beheld with their own eyes a supreme wonder:

**119.** the gandharvas brought a chariot along the path of the sky,
and Lakshmi, saying "Hari", left her body and went to heaven.

**120.** A certain shakti, a portion of Lakshmi, went to Vaikuntha;
seeing this, all the people were overwhelmed.

**121.** Lakshmi went to Indra's city, her own abode,[^2]
shining with the supreme radiance of Lakshmi, wholly Lakshmi.

**122.** Meanwhile here Shachi-devi weeps in grief;
surrounded by the women, she weeps, recounting [her daughter-in-law's] virtues.

**123.** Tears stream from her eyes, the cloth on her breast is soaked;
striking her head, she heaves hot, heavy sighs:

**124.** "My daughter-in-law Lakshmi was like Lakshmi in every virtue and in nature;
in all Nadia there is none to compare with her.

**125.** How shall I go home alone?
Why have you forgotten your love for me?

**126.** All that was prepared for the worship of the Deities lies there —
why have you gone, leaving your service to me?

**127.** From this day my home is empty;
Vishvambhara married you, but you never stayed at His side.[^3]

**128.** O wicked serpent, where were you hiding?
Why did you not devour me, but devoured my daughter-in-law, so full of life?

**129.** Having appointed my daughter-in-law to serve me,
my son went to foreign lands without a care.

**130.** How shall I, unfortunate one, look at my son's face?
What shall I do? My heart burns, not seeing my daughter-in-law."

**131.** Seeing such lamentation, all the relatives and friends
say: "Shachi-devi, compose yourself!

**132.** Who can change what is ordained for anyone?
This whole world is deceptive, and deceptive are all these bodies.[^4]

**133.** Who is to instruct you — you know everything;
knowing and hearing all this, why do you not accept consolation?

**134.** Having taken a body, no one escapes death:
even Brahma and all the other gods die.

**135.** Some sooner, some later — death awaits all;
birth and death are the lot of all.

**136.** The one true Reality is Krishna — this we know from the Vedas;
whoever does not worship such a Krishna is a mine of folly."

**137.** Saying this, all the relatives consoled her,
and all together, saying "Hari", restrain their weeping.

**138.** Then all together, having performed the funeral rites
as prescribed, went to their homes.

**139.** Shachi, weeping and weeping, went to her own home;
gathering together, relatives and friends consoled her.

**140.** And Vishvambhara, having stayed there a few more days,
set out for home with a joyful heart.

**141.** With silver, gold, cloth, pearls and coral
all the Vaishnavas honoured Him without measure.

**142.** The Lord came home with many riches
and joyfully gave them to His mother.

**143.** Bowing, the Lord looks at her face:
Shachi, her face downcast, does not say a word.

**144.** Vishvambhara again takes the dust of her feet;
seeing her sad face, He spoke.

**145.** Having given His mother all the wealth He had brought,
the Lord, astonished, says very softly:

**146.** "Why do I see your face so sad?
Seeing you sad, My heart burns."

**147.** Hearing this, Shachi, her voice choking,
began to weep, and her tears soaked the cloth on her breast.

**148.** She can say nothing — her voice is full of grief;
at last she said: "My daughter-in-law has gone to Vaikuntha."

**149.** Hearing this, the Lord grew sad at heart;
His eyes filled with tears of tenderness.

**150.** The Lord said to His mother: "Listen to My words:
I shall tell you the earlier story — why she was born.

**151.** Once an apsara of Indra was dancing,
and by the will of destiny she missed a step.

**152.** The rhythm was broken, and the lord of the gods cursed her:
'Be born on earth in a human home.'

**153.** Having cursed her, the king of the gods again took pity:
'You will not suffer — a great task awaits you.

**154.** On earth the Ishvara will appear as an avatara,
and you will become His wife' — such was the boon he gave.

**155.** 'And then you will return here, to Indra's city.'
That is all about that beauty of Indra.[^5]

**156.** Grieve no more, listen, My mother:
what the Creator has written cannot be changed."

**157.** Shachi listened attentively to her son's words,
ceased grieving and thought of it no more.

**158.** Consoled, Shachi turned to other thoughts;
sitting with the devotees, she speaks of her affairs.

**159.** Having said this, Vishvambhara became thoughtful;
concealing Himself, He speaks of various things.[^6]

**160.** Says Lochana Dasa: listen to this wonderful
deed of Gauranga — Lakshmi's ascent to heaven.

## Kaishora-lila (The Pastimes of Youth): the Lord's Second Wedding

> *Summary by Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati.* When some days had passed after Lakshmi-devi's ascent to heaven, Mother Shachi grew anxious to have Nimai married again and sent the brahmana Kashinatha to the house of Sanatana Pandita to settle a match with his daughter. When Kashinatha Mishra, coming to Sanatana Pandita's house, conveyed Shachi-devi's words, Sanatana Pandita with the greatest joy agreed to give his daughter to Vishvambhara. An astrologer was called and the day and hour of the wedding were fixed; on the eve of the wedding the respectable ladies and the brahmanas duly performed the popular and the Vedic rites. As before, the anointing with turmeric and the other rites were duly performed. On the wedding day everything was done with great pomp, just as the previous time. After the wedding Sanatana Mishra sent his daughter with his son-in-law to His house.[^7]

*Raga gandhara;[^8] disha: "Moon of the twice-born line, jewel-like Gora! Nadia rejoices, 'Hari' resounds, cries rise up. O-ki ho re, Gauranga, jaya, jaya!"*

**1.** Thus Lord Vishvambhara, the son of Shachi,
joyfully spent His days in Navadvipa.

**2.** He lived happily with His relatives and friends,
but in Shachi's heart sorrow suddenly arose.

**3.** Seeing the house without a daughter-in-law, she grew very anxious:
"I shall get Vishvambhara married" — so she resolved within.

**4.** Having considered, she came to a decision:
"There is a certain maiden — if only fortune favours us."

**5.** Seeing before her a brahmana named Kashinatha,
Shachi in private opened her heart to him:

**6.** "Go to the house of Sanatana Pandita
and tell him fully what I say:

**7.** my son is endowed with every virtue and good nature;
if it pleases him, He is a worthy bridegroom for his daughter."

**8.** So Shachi spoke to the brahmana,
and, having heard, the brahmana Kashinatha set out quickly.

**9.** Pandita Sanatana was in his house;
there came Kashinatha, the best of brahmanas.

**10.** "Come in, come in!" said [Sanatana], and offered him a seat;
"On what business have you come?" he asks with a smile.

**11.** Kashinatha says: "Listen, listen, pandit:
I shall tell you everything, as is proper.

**12.** You know all the shastras, you are renowned on earth;
what virtue is there unknown to you?

**13.** You are supremely righteous, devoted to Vishnu;
you are a brahmana faithful to his own dharma.

**14.** Knowing this, Shachi, Vishvambhara's mother,
called me and told me what is in her heart.

**15.** She has sent me straight to you;
listen attentively to what I say.

**16.** Regarding you as her own, I tell you her heart;
judge for yourself and do what is fitting:

**17.** Vishvambhara is a worthy bridegroom for your daughter.
I have said everything — give your answer."

**18.** Hearing this, Sanatana Mishra reflected
and with his relatives settled on his word.

**19.** Sanatana says to Kashinatha Pandita:
"I shall open my heart to you, listen, honoured sir.

**20.** This is what I have been thinking day and night,
but I did not dare to say it openly.

**21.** Today is an auspicious day — destiny favours me:
Vishvambhara, the treasure-house of virtues, will become my son-in-law.

**22.** Then indeed I understood how great my good fortune is,
when Shachi-devi herself gave the command.

**23.** Whose fortune can equal mine?
I shall give my daughter to the Parabrahman, Sri Govinda!

**24.** The feet that Brahma and Shiva ever worship —
giving my daughter to them, I too shall worship them."

**25.** As Kashinatha, the best of brahmanas, set out,
[Sanatana] said: "Convey this at the feet of Shachi-devi:

**26.** 'Having fixed the time, I shall send a brahmana;
see to the preparations for the auspicious event.'"

**27.** So Pandita Sri Sanatana replied,
and Kashinatha, the best of brahmanas, went off quickly.

**28.** Coming, he bowed at Shachi's feet
and in her presence related everything.

**29.** Receiving the answer, Shachi was overjoyed
and, smiling, began the preparations for her son's wedding.

**30.** Blessed Shachi gathers all kinds of things,
and on one pretext or another goes to see the maiden.

**31.** And Sanatana, the excellent pandit,
after waiting some days, sent a brahmana:

**32.** "Convey my words at Shachi's feet;
remind her of what the brahmana said before.

**33.** If, to my good fortune, she gives her word,
come back quickly, so that we may set to work here.

**34.** Sri Shachinandana is the Parabrahman, Sri Govinda:
by giving my daughter to Him I shall be freed from samsara."

**35.** Hearing this, the brahmana went to Shachi's house
and, arriving, related everything at Shachi's feet:

**36.** "Pandita Sri Sanatana has sent me
to open his heart to you:

**37.** if, to his good fortune, you, blessed lady, permit it,
he will give his daughter to your son Vishvambhara."

**38.** "Good, good!" said Shachi in great joy.
"I agree; do everything quickly."

**39.** Hearing this, the brahmana in great joy
began to speak sweet words:

**40.** "Vishnupriya will gain a husband like Vishvambhara —
and the name Vishnupriya ('beloved of Vishnu') will be true for her.

**41.** As Rukmini gained Sri Krishna as her husband,
so it will be with her — so my heart tells me."[^9]

**42.** Hearing this, Shachi was overjoyed,
and the brahmana went and told everything to the pandit.

**43.** Pandita Sri Sanatana was very pleased
and began to prepare all that is needed for a wedding.

**44.** The wise [pandit] has all kinds of things and ornaments made;
he planned to perform the adhivasa.

**45.** Calling an astrologer, he says humbly:
"I am giving Vishnupriya in marriage — fix the time."

**46.** The astrologer said: "Listen, listen, pandit:
today I unexpectedly met Vishvambhara.

**47.** Seeing Him, my heart rejoiced,
and I said to Him in jest:

**48.** 'Tomorrow is Your auspicious adhivasa,
there will be a wedding — hear what I say.'

**49.** Hearing this, He answered:
'Tell Me, where, whose wedding, who is the bride, who the groom?

**50.** When was this ever spoken of with Me?'
Understand how matters stand and act accordingly."[^10]

**51.** Hearing such words from the astrologer,
[Sanatana] took courage and said nothing at the time.

**52.** Sanatana Pandita, a man of noble character,
considered the main point with his relatives:

**53.** "I have prepared all kinds of things, had all kinds of ornaments made;
whom shall I blame? It is my karma.

**54.** I have committed no offence,
yet Gaurahari has without reason rejected [my] love."[^11]

**54+1.** Having lost the treasure — the joy of kinship with Gauranga —[^12]
he cried "Alas, alas, Gaurachandra!" and fell to the ground.

**54+2.** He weeps aloud, crying: "Hari, Hari!
Not seeing You, Vishvambhara, I am dying!

**54+3.** Glory to Vishvambhara, the deliverer of the fallen!
You fulfilled Bhishmaka's desire in the city of Vidarbha.

**54+4.** Glory to Murari, the guardian of Rukmini's desire!
You brought to Yourself all the beautiful princesses [held captive by] Naraka,

**54+5.** and, knowing their hearts, You married them all.
Marry my daughter too — You are the true dharma!

**54+6.** Do not spurn me in contempt as one fallen:
how many, many fallen ones You have taken up and saved!

**54+7.** Glory to Vishvambhara, the saviour of the people of the world!
Glory to the Lord of all lords, the Creator of the creator!

**54+8.** But I am the lowest of the low, my mind is very dull:
never have I known even the scent of worship of You."

**55.** Sorrow arose in his heart, and he gave voice to it;
and his brahmana wife, her heart aflame, speaks —

**56.** she was of noble birth, modest, well-bred, faithful to her husband,
endowed with every virtue and good nature, devoted to Vishnu —

**57.** seeing her husband's grief, she herself was much grieved
and, setting aside her shyness, speaks before her husband:

**58.** "If Vishvambhara Himself did not wish this match,
what blame can the people of Nadia lay on you?

**59.** If Vishvambhara Hari Himself did not wish it,
what can you do by your own power?

**60.** He is the independent Person, the Lord of all,
whose servants are Brahma, Rudra, Indra and the rest.

**61.** How can He become your son-in-law?
Calm your mind, remember Krishna.

**62.** It is not in our power — there is no point in grieving;
I am afraid to say it, but cast off your grief now."

**63.** When his beloved spoke these words to him,
Pandita Sri Sanatana restrained his grief.

**64.** Together with his relatives he came to this conclusion:
"What is my fault? Vishvambhara did not wish it."

**65.** Having said this, he spoke no more to anyone;
the brahmana and his wife grieved at heart.

**66.** He brooded within, and again sorrow arose:
"Alas, Vishvambhara-deva has put me to shame!

**67.** Glory, glory to You who took away Draupadi's shame and fear!
Glory, glory to You who saved the elephant from the crocodile's jaws![^13]

**68.** Saviour of the Pandavas, life of Rukmini!
Glory, glory to You who freed Ahalya from her sin!"

**69.** Thus the best of brahmanas offered many prayers,
and Lord Gauranga, the Lord of the world, came to know of it.

**70.** Hearing all about it, Vishvambhara
thought in His heart: "Why are they grieving so?

**71.** Two of My devotees have become sad at heart,
and yet I said it in jest, laughing."

**72.** Among His friends there was one dear friend;
in private He told him all that was in His mind:

**73.** "On some pretext go to the pandit's house
and speak as though I knew nothing of it:

**74.** 'It was in jest that I said this to the astrologer —
why then, without understanding, did [you] give up the matter?

**75.** There is no reason at all to give up the matter;
it is not right that the two of them should grieve.

**76.** What His mother has said — what more is there to discuss?
Who would go against her word?

**77.** The matter suffers for nothing, you grieve for nothing;
do all that is proper for the wedding.'"

**78.** Having so instructed him, the Lord sent the brahmana,
and he related everything to Sanatana Pandita.

[^1]: Cf. en/17, note 4: Lakshmipriya is Lakshmi herself. Lochana Dasa presents the serpent's bite as separation from the Lord that has taken the form of a serpent — and so does Bhaktisiddhanta in his summary: "bitten by the serpent of separation". Her departure is not a mundane death: she goes to the Lord (v. 118). Cf. Murari 1.11.21–28.
[^2]: The 1983 edition has it the other way round: a portion of Lakshmi went to heaven (svarga), and Lakshmi herself to Vaikuntha, "her own abode". In A Indra's city is called her abode in agreement with the Lord's story of the apsara (vv. 151–155). Gaudiya Vaishnavas, following Murari (1.12.17) and Vrindavana Dasa, see in Lakshmipriya Lakshmi herself, who went to the feet of her Lord; the story of the apsara is the words with which the Lord consoled His mother while concealing Himself (cf. v. 159).
[^3]: The 1983 edition: "having married, Vishvambhara went to foreign lands".
[^4]: "Mithya" ("deceptive, false") here refers to the transience of worldly life and of bodies, not to the unreality of the world in the Mayavada sense; the one eternal Reality is Krishna (v. 136).
[^5]: Cf. Murari 1.12.13–17, where the Lord tells the same story "concealing everything with words strong to hide His own self" and adds: "Or else it was the Mistress of the world herself, in the form of Lakshmi, who went to the lotus feet of her Lord". See note 2.
[^6]: I.e. without revealing His divinity. V. 158 is absent in the 1983 edition.
[^7]: Bhaktisiddhanta's summary does not include the episode with the astrologer (vv. 45–78); in the scan this summary is damaged in places and is translated from the surviving text.
[^8]: The name of the raga is illegible in the scan of A; raga gandhara follows the 1983 edition (where this is the "Sixth Chapter").
[^9]: Gaudiya Vaishnavas honour Vishnupriya as the embodiment of Bhu-shakti, the Lord's energy. Cf. Murari 1.13.13–15.
[^10]: The Lord's jest: He pretends to know nothing of the wedding. Cf. Murari 1.13.18–21. The second line is the astrologer's advice to Sanatana.
[^11]: Cf. Murari 1.13.22–30.
[^12]: Vv. 54+1 – 54+8 are found only in the 1983 edition (absent from Bhaktisiddhanta's edition); the 1903 edition gives them in a footnote as the additional reading of one manuscript. Bhishmaka is the king of Vidarbha, Rukmini's father; Rukmini longed to become Krishna's wife, and He carried her away (Bhagavata 10.52–54). Naraka (Bhaumasura) held sixteen thousand princesses captive; Krishna, having slain him, freed them and married them (10.59).
[^13]: On Draupadi, Gajendra and the others see en/17, note 6. Ahalya — the wife of the sage Gautama, freed from a curse by the touch of Rama's foot.
