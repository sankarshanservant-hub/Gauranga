# Adi-khanda, part 4 (portion 12)

*Balya-lila (continued): by Gaura's touch the puppy gains divine knowledge and departs for Goloka in a celestial chariot, while the gods praise Gaura's mercy; Nimai eats the offering Shachi has prepared for the goddess Shashthi and teaches her that worship of Achyuta is the worship of all the gods; Nimai mimics Murari Gupta expounding yoga and instructs him in bhakti*

**324.** By the touch of Gauranga the puppy became fortunate:
giving up its nature, it gained divine knowledge.[^1]

**325.** It cries "Radha-Krishna, Govinda!" and dances;
seeing it, all the people of Nadiya run after it.

**326.** Seeing such rapture in the puppy,
all thrill with ecstasy, their eyes full of tears.

**327.** Suddenly the fortunate one left its dog's body
and, becoming a dweller of Krishna's realm, set out for Goloka.

**328.** All at once a divine chariot appeared
and carries him away along the path of the sky.

**329.** A golden chariot, beautiful, with a thousand pinnacles,
glitters with pendants of jewels and pearls.

**330.** Hundreds of thousands of bells ring on it,
bronze cymbals sound in crowds.

**340.** The sound of conches is heard, cries of "Jaya!" and "Hari!";
gandharvas and kinnaras sing of Radha and Krishna.

**341.** Banners and pennants all fly above the chariot;
their shining radiance eclipses the disc of the sun.

**342.** In the middle of the chariot, on a jewelled throne,
sits he, lovely, of enchanting lustre.

**343.** Divine ornaments adorn every limb;
millions of Madanas swoon with shame.

**344.** Cool, surpassing millions of moons,
he cries out: "Radha-Krishna! Gauranga!"

**345.** The siddhas came, fanning [him] with chamaras,
and bore him along the path to Goloka.

**346.** Brahma, Shiva, Sanaka and the others, all with joined palms,
surrounding the chariot, sing the glory of Gauranga:

**347.** "Glory, glory to the ocean of mercy, the son of Shachi!
Never has the Lord shown such mercy!

**348.** He delivered a dog and sent it to Goloka —
no one has ever attained such a divine body!

**349.** Glory, glory to Gaurahari, the refuge of the helpless!
Glory, glory to Him who is above all avataras!

**350.** By Your mercy the souls of Kali will be delivered;
what superhuman pastimes will You not show!

**351.** When shall we gods become fortunate
and gain the supreme gift — the grace of Your feet?

**352.** By Your touch even a dog is delivered —
such mercy was never found even in Hrishikesha![^2]

**353.** When shall we too have such good fortune?
You blessed a dog — that same we beg for ourselves.

**354.** Obeisance, obeisance to Gauraraya, who sees no faults!
Obeisance, obeisance to Your two beautiful feet!"

**355.** So, following [the chariot], all the gods [pray]:
"When shall we attain the feet of Gaurachandra?"

**356.** And that greatly fortunate one arrived in Goloka
and there ceaselessly sings of Gauranga's pastimes.

**357.** Such is the wondrous manifestation of Gorachand —
in bliss this Lochana Dasa sings [His] virtues.

**358.** Now Shachi-devi resolved
to keep the vow of Shashthi:[^3]
all the women of the town, keeping the vow,
[gathered] under the banyan tree.

**359.** Having properly prepared the offering (naivedya)
and covering it with the end of her sari,
she goes to the banyan to keep the vow,
in great joy.

**360.** At that very moment Vishvambhara Raya,
playing on the road,
saw His mother and came running:
"What are you carrying in your hands?"

**361.** Spreading His arms, blocking the way,
He tries to stop His mother;
"What is it, what?" He keeps saying and tries to grab it,
fretting before His mother.

**362.** "I have carefully prepared the offering
for the worship of the goddess,
and I am going to the banyan to worship Shashthi —
You play here.

**363.** On my way back, listen, child,
I will bring You prasada;
I will worship the goddess and ask her for this boon —
that the fever of misfortune may go."

**364.** Hearing such a reply and knowing His mother's heart,
Sri Vishvambhara
speaks gentle words —
a nectarean loveliness spreads over His lips:

**365.** "How many times have I told you —
you don't understand, foolish one!
With hunger
I am burning inside: I will eat the offering."

**366.** Saying this, Gaurahari seized
the offering and filled His mouth with it;
seeing it, His mother cried out in dismay —
her heart blazed with grief.

**367.** Seeing that Vishvambhara had eaten what was meant for the goddess —
ghee, honey, milk-foods —
Shachi, her heart pounding,
her eyes brimming with angry tears, [says]:

**368.** "Silly child, how much must I teach You!
You do not honour the gods.
A brahmana's son — and so ill-behaved!
I shall die of this grief."

**369.** Hearing His mother's words,
the jewel Gaura blazed with anger:
"I told you all this — and still you do not understand
and speak ill words to Me.

**370.** Listen, foolish one: I know everything,
I am the essence of the three worlds;
whatever there is in the world is I alone,
in the three worlds there is no other.[^4]

**371.** As watering the root of a tree
nourishes the branches above,
as nourishing the life-breath (prana) [satisfies] the senses —
such is the law concerning Me."

*So in the Srimad-Bhagavata (4.31.14):*

**371a.** "As by watering the root of a tree its trunk, branches and twigs are satisfied,
and as by food offered to the prana the senses [are satisfied] —
so worship of Achyuta is the worship of all."[^5]

**372.** Saying this, Hari cunningly
put His arms round His mother's neck;
and Shachi, astonished at heart,
went to worship Shashthi.

**373.** Having served the goddess Shashthi in many ways,
she speaks plaintively:
"My child is a great rascal;
please forgive His fault.

**374.** You yourself gave me this wild boy —
how can you take offence at Him?
Grant your blessing to this son of mine,
do not be angry at all.

**375.** I have not many — this treasure Nimai
you gave me out of mercy;
let no misfortune befall my son —
this boy is yours, O goddess!"

**376.** Saying this, she falls at the feet
of all the elderly women,
humbly entreats them and bows:
"Bless Him from your hearts!

**377.** Calling [Him] your own, place the dust of your feet
on the head of my Vishvambhara;
this child of mine is very restless —
may His mind become steady."

**378.** Holding a blade of grass between her teeth,[^6] Shachi-rani says,
serving the feet of all:
"Bless Him, all of you — may my son Vishvambhara
live long!"

**379.** Having performed the worship of Shashthi and taking her son by the hand,
the goddess went home;
with Jagannatha she reflects,
pondering what she had experienced.

**380.** What more shall I say? The essence of all the gods
has appeared on earth
and plays with the boys in many ways —
says Lochana Dasa.

*Raga varadi*[^7]

**381.** And some days later that son of Shachi
plays in the dust on the highway;
His body a golden mountain, grey with dust,
with Him His devoted companions.

**382.** The children play with dust, now and then they squabble,
in the dust-battle their bodies are bare;
all of one age, all of one mind,
drops of sweat from the effort of play.

**383.** While they were all playing together, Gupta the vaidya[^8]
suddenly came along that road;
all his own people went with him,
and he, a scholar, was discoursing on the path of knowledge.

**384.** Reasoning with them, he expounds the yoga-shastra,
shaking his head and waving his hands;
seeing this, Vishvambhara Raya runs right behind him,
imitating his gait and speech.

**385.** Murari the vaidya, glancing [at Him] for a moment,
again expounds yoga;
just so Vishvambhara expounds yoga,
just so He moves His hands and head.

**386.** So again and again Gaurahari
mimics him together with the children;
Murari the vaidya, seeing his own manners mimicked,
grew angry and spoke rude words:

**387.** "Who would call this one good? I've seen the boy —
this is Mishra Purandara's son;
everywhere I hear talk and songs of his virtues —
a fine name he has: Nimai!"[^9]

**388.** Hearing such words, the jewel Gaura grew angry —
for the sake of mercy to His devotee;
frowning, He says slyly:
"You'll find out at mealtime!"[^10]

**389.** Hearing Vishvambhara's words, Murari pondered
and went home with a wondering heart;
busy with household affairs, his mind turned elsewhere and he forgot,
and then it was time to eat.

**390.** Meanwhile Vishvambhara Hari dressed Himself finely:
He wrapped His dhoti tightly round His waist,
three tufts adorn His head, rasakanti beads hang on His neck,
a double string of pearls at His throat.

**391.** A line of kajal in His eyes, His topknot tied with five knots,
golden ornaments glittering,
magara anklets on His feet; holding a milk laddu in His hand,
the Lord Vishvambhara set out.

**392.** He entered Murari Gupta's house, into the inner rooms,
where the best of vaidyas was eating his meal,
and in a voice deep as thunder, by His own grace,
He called: "Murari!"

**393.** Hearing the voice, Gupta the vaidya remembered
what Vishvambhara had said, and was startled;
and Gaurahari, saying "What are you doing, what are you doing?",
came right up to the spot.

**394.** "Don't be alarmed: I am here —
go on eating," He said;
and in the middle of the meal, approaching very slowly,
He filled his plate with urine.

**395.** "What's this, what?! Shame, shame!" — up jumped Murari;
and Gora, clapping His hands, says:
"Shaking his head and waving his hands, having left the path of bhakti,
this trickster expounds yoga!

**396.** Disregarding knowledge and ritual, worship Krishna wholeheartedly —
the relisher of rasa, the expert, full of consciousness and bliss.
One whose sight is fixed on the material finds no nourishment for bhajana;
you do not understand — your intelligence is very dull.

**397.** Hari is supremely merciful, He holds all energies;
is such a thing possible for a jiva?[^11]
He is the eternal Brahman, the treasure of the gopis' life;
why, not worshipping Him, do you cause [yourself] pain?"

**398.** Saying this, the jewel Gaura vanished — none knows where;
Murari cannot see Him
and reflects within himself: "This is surely no one else —
truly, the son of Shachi is the Lord."

**399.** Having reflected thus, Murari
set out at once, in haste and agitation;
from great joy he can hardly walk on the road,
[yet] he came to where Mishra Purandara was.

[^1]: In the 1903 edition the whole account of the puppy's deliverance (vv. 324–357) is enclosed in brackets — this editor's way of marking lines found not in all manuscripts. In Bhaktisiddhanta's summary the puppy "in a divine body, singing of Hari, went to Vaikuntha"; the text says Goloka ("becoming a dweller of Krishna's realm"; the 1983 edition: "of Vishnu's realm"). Cf. the *Chaitanya-charitamrita* (Antya 1.16–33): Shivananda Sena's dog, having received the remnants of Mahaprabhu's meal and chanting "Krishna", attained Vaikuntha.
[^2]: That is, even Krishna in His earlier pastimes did not bestow mercy so freely. Cf. the *Chaitanya-charitamrita* (Madhya 19.53): Gaura is "the most magnanimous", the giver of prema for Krishna.
[^3]: Shashthi — the goddess who protects children; women honour her with fasting and offerings under a banyan tree for the welfare of their children.
[^4]: This is not an assertion of impersonal oneness: all that exists is the energy of the Lord and does not exist apart from Him, while He remains the personal Lord. Cf. the *Bhagavata* (2.9.33): "Before creation I alone existed…", and Mahaprabhu's teaching of inconceivable oneness and difference (*achintya-bhedabheda*). This is the sense the following shloka brings out.
[^5]: In the edition the shloka is followed by Bhaktisiddhanta's anvaya and translation (the OCR is almost illegible); the translation runs: "As watering the root of a tree satisfies its trunk, branches, twigs and so on, and as food offered to the prana satisfies all the senses, so by worshipping Achyuta alone all the gods are worshipped." In the 1903 edition the shloka is given in square brackets as an editorial insertion.
[^6]: A blade of grass between the teeth is a sign of utmost humility before those whose favour is asked.
[^7]: In A the raga line is corrupt; "varadi" follows the 1983 edition.
[^8]: Murari Gupta, a vaidya (physician) by caste, later one of Mahaprabhu's closest associates and author of the Sanskrit biography *Sri Krishna-chaitanya-charitamrita*. Here he is still devoted to yoga and the path of knowledge.
[^9]: A gibe: "Nimai" comes from the bitter neem tree (see portion 09, note 12).
[^10]: Lochana Dasa stresses that the Lord's "anger" is mercy: He breaks Murari's attachment to dry yoga and knowledge so as to turn him to bhakti.
[^11]: That is, the perfections of which yoga speaks are attributes of the Lord; the jiva cannot acquire them by its own efforts — its path is service to Him.
