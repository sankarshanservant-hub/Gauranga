# Madhava Pattanayak. Vaishnava-lilamrita, chapter 4 (vv. 28–81)
## Sri Chaitanya's arrival in Puri and meeting with Ramananda Raya

*Pilot translation from Oriya (without a native speaker; doubtful places are marked (?) and (??)). Original, transliteration and word-for-word glosses are in `full.md`.*[^intro]

**4.28.** At that time there came to the Kshetra a new, young yati, a sannyasi, together with a company of devotees.  
**4.29.** His name was Krishna Chaitanya: He was as the Lord Himself, manifest before the eyes.[^samana]  
**4.30.** Beholding Jagannatha, He falls senseless in prema.[^faint]  
**4.31.** Sarvabhauma, who saw [this], had Him carefully tended (??).[^sastama]  
**4.32.** He took Him to his own house, and the devotees too came there.  
**4.33.** They went to the temple for darshan, and He Himself partook of prasad.  
**4.34.** For a whole month in the Kshetra their discussion of the shastras grew ever richer.[^month]  
**4.35.** For about a month He stayed in the Kshetra, frequenting the temple.  
**4.36.** At the foot of Bata-Ganesha, in plain view, Jagannatha Dasa would sit.[^jdas]  
**4.37.** He had built a pavilion there for rahasa and installed the murti of Radha-Krishna.[^rahasa]  
**4.38.** He was composing the Bhagavata, and the devotees listened attentively.  
**4.39.** All the bhava of the Bhagavata he sang day after day in a sweet voice.  
**4.40.** One day, in the leisure of evening, before a host of devotees, he was in particular  
**4.41.** expounding the meaning of the Bhagavata — and Chaitanya came and joined them.  
**4.42.** Hearing the exposition of the Bhagavata, He understood the swami's bhava.  
**4.43.** Tears trickle from the eyes; boundless thrills run through the body.[^whose]  
**4.44.** Such great bhava in Jagannatha [Dasa] Chaitanya discerned.[^thaba]  
**4.45.** He said: 'You are the best of devotees; your understanding is nourished by the bhava of devotion.  
**4.46.** Where could I obtain your bhava? I will not let go of your company!'[^humility]  
**4.47.** For two and a half praharas the two remained in each other's embrace.[^prahara]  
**4.48.** Then, sitting together, they praised one another.  
**4.49.** Chaitanya called him 'Swami', and he himself called Him 'Prabhu'.[^swami]  
**4.50.** As a river merging with the ocean — the waves of bhava rose high.[^uchhul]  
**4.51.** He met with Sarvabhauma and studied the shastras with him.  
**4.52.** Sarvabhauma, a great logician, versed in nyaya, deeply learned,[^sarva]  
**4.53.** held many learned disputations with the Lord in the Kshetra.  
**4.54.** He saw the Lord's devotion: the Lord's heart was by its very nature full of prema.  
**4.55.** Whenever the talk turned to devotion, sattvika bhavas appeared on the Lord's body:[^sattvika]  
**4.56.** His lips trembled, His body sweated; there was shaking, bhava, and rapturous dance.  
**4.57.** Seeing this, Sarvabhauma said: 'Are You not the Lord Himself?[^svayam]  
**4.58.** I do not know the bhava of devotion: with a dry heart I [deal only] in logical proofs.'[^pramana]  
**4.59.** [Sarvabhauma] spent his time with the Lord. [Then] the Lord said: 'I shall go on pilgrimage.'  
**4.60.** [Sarvabhauma:] 'When You go to the southern land, seek the company of Ramaraya.[^speaker]  
**4.61.** He knows the truth of devotion; his mind is set on the path of raga.[^raga]  
**4.62.** I do not know the talk of Krishna; Rama Raya knows it all.  
**4.63.** When You go to the southern land, be sure to meet Rama Raya.'  
**4.64.** He set out on His southern journey and came to the bank of the Godavari.[^south]  
**4.65.** Having bathed and purified Himself, He had spread His seat at the foot of a tree.[^godavari]  
**4.66.** His lustre was by nature golden — one might mistake Him for the rising sun.  
**4.67.** As if fashioned of gold, His body shone fair.  
**4.68.** His arms reached to His knees; His wide eyes were lifted up (?).[^ajanu]  
**4.69.** A brahminical radiance flashed from Him; a sweet smile flowed from His lips.  
**4.70.** At this time Ramaraya came in state [to the river] and was bathing at the ghat,[^ramananda]  
**4.71.** with his retinue; herald-drums and kettledrums resounded.[^tamaka]  
**4.72.** Ramaraya rose from the ghat, and the Raya set off in his palanquin.[^dola]  
**4.73.** Rising from the foot of the tree, Chaitanya ran up to meet him:  
**4.74.** 'Are you Ramaraya? You look like a king.'[^tumi]  
**4.75.** At once they embraced — as if lightning had merged with a cloud.[^cloud]  
**4.76.** [Chaitanya:] 'I have come and met you — behold my good fortune!'  
**4.77.** Then Ramaraya said: 'Come, pray, to my home;  
**4.78.** stay here as my guest, and we shall converse on the shastras.  
**4.79.** By my good fortune You have come and granted me the company of a sadhu.'  
**4.80.** The two became absorbed in conversation and spent ten days so.[^tendays]  
**4.81.** In verses (?), joining many meanings, Ramaraya told his account.[^samvada]  

[^intro]: Madhava Pattanayak, Vaishnava-lilamrita (c. 1535), ch. 4, vv. 28–81; text after B. C. Acharya's edition (Sambalpur 1986), pp. 22–25 (scan leaves 66–69). The chapter opens (vv. 1–27) with King Prataparudra, his southern war and the raids on Puri; v. 28 begins the arrival of Sri Chaitanya. The author, a disciple of Gadadhara Pandita, himself lived in Puri in Mahaprabhu's time (ch. 1, vv. 55–62).
[^samana]: Sākṣāt īśvara samāna — lit. 'equal to Ishvara, manifest'. The author conveys how the Lord appeared to the people of Puri; below (v. 57) Sarvabhauma asks outright whether He is not the Lord Himself. According to the Gaudiya Vaishnava teaching expounded by Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati after the Chaitanya-charitamrita (Adi 1–4), Sri Krishna Chaitanya is Svayam Bhagavan Krishna Himself, who has assumed the mood and lustre of Sri Radha; samāna does not mean 'merely like' here.
[^faint]: Cf. CC Madhya 6.1–14 and CB Antya 2: on seeing Jagannatha, Mahaprabhu falls senseless in ecstasy; Sarvabhauma Bhattacharya has Him carried to his house. Madhava keeps the same order ('first Jagannatha, then Sarvabhauma's house') in his Chaitanya-vilasa (according to B. B. Majumdar).
[^sastama]: Uncertain reading: the printed ସାଷ୍ଟାମ ଯତକ (sāṣṭāma yataka) is not found in the dictionaries. From the sense and from CC (Madhya 6), Sarvabhauma took care of the unconscious sannyasi (had Him carried, revived). Possible corruptions of sāṣṭāṅga (full prostration) and yatana (care). Question for a specialist — QUESTIONS.md.
[^month]: According to CC (Madhya 7.3–5), Mahaprabhu came to Puri in Phalguna and left for the south in Vaishakha, i.e. about two months later; Madhava says 'a month'. The 'discussion of the shastras' corresponds in CC (Madhya 6) to Sarvabhauma's seven-day exposition of the Vedanta-sutra and the debate that followed.
[^jdas]: Jagannatha Dasa — Oriya Vaishnava poet, author of the Oriya Bhagavata, first of the 'five friends' (Panchasakha). Bata-Ganesha — the shrine of Ganesha by the Kalpa-vata banyan in the Jagannatha temple compound. The meeting of Sri Chaitanya with Jagannatha Dasa belongs to the Oriya tradition (Divakara Dasa and others); the Gaudiya biographies (CC, CB) do not have it. We translate the author's text as it stands.
[^rahasa]: Rahasa (Oriya rahāsa, from rāsa) — the enactment of the pastimes of Radha and Krishna in song and dance; Madhava describes the rahasa custom of Puri in ch. 2. The murti of Radha-Krishna is an installed Deity, not an image.
[^whose]: The subject is not named: the tears and thrills may belong to Chaitanya hearing the Bhagavata or to Jagannatha Dasa himself; the next couplet ('such bhava in Jagannatha Chaitanya discerned') points rather to the latter.
[^thaba]: Printed ଠାନ (ṭhāna), but the rhyme with ଭାବ (bhāba) requires ଠାବ (ṭhāba); ṭhāba karibā — 'to locate, discern' (Brooks 1874, p. 103: ṭhāba — 'a place').
[^humility]: Mahaprabhu's words that He cannot obtain Jagannatha Dasa's bhava show the humility with which the Lord, playing the part of a devotee, extols His devotees (cf. His praise of Ramananda Raya in CC Madhya 8). According to the Gaudiya teaching followed by Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati, Sri Chaitanya is Krishna Himself in the form and mood of a devotee; in exalting a devotee He teaches humility, not that anyone is above Him.
[^prahara]: A prahara is a 'watch', an eighth of a day (≈3 hours); two and a half praharas ≈ seven and a half hours. The reading କୋଲାଗ୍ରତେ is uncertain (one would expect କୋଳାଗତେ, 'in embrace').
[^swami]: That is, Chaitanya respectfully called Jagannatha Dasa 'swami', and he called Him 'Prabhu', 'Lord'. So the Oriya tradition expresses their mutual regard.
[^uchhul]: The reading ଉଭରିଲ (ubharila) rests on the agreement of passes A and B and on the same verb in v. 68 (ଉଭରି). Cf. Purnachandra (vol. 1, PDF leaf 1045): ଉଭା 'standing, erect', ଉଭାରିବା 'to raise, lift; to stand up' (with a quotation from Jagannatha Dasa's Bhagavata); hence 'the waves of bhava rose up'. Not in Brooks.
[^sarva]: Vasudeva Sarvabhauma Bhattacharya — the famous Naiyayika (logician of the Nyaya school), court pandit of Prataparudra (CC Madhya 6).
[^sattvika]: Sattvika bhavas — the eight bodily signs of ecstatic love: stupor, perspiration, horripilation, faltering voice, trembling, change of colour, tears and fainting (Bhakti-rasamrita-sindhu 2.3).
[^svayam]: In CC (Madhya 6) Sarvabhauma recognises Mahaprabhu as the Lord when He shows him His six-armed form, and composes verses in His praise; in Madhava, Sarvabhauma's awakening comes from the sight of the Lord's sattvika bhavas.
[^pramana]: Paramāṇe = pramāṇe — 'in proofs': Sarvabhauma confesses that his learning is dry logic without bhakti; cf. CC Madhya 6, where before his conversion he expounds Vedanta in the spirit of advaita.
[^speaker]: The change of speaker is not marked; by sense vv. 60–63 are Sarvabhauma's reply (cf. CC Madhya 7.62–67: Sarvabhauma begs Mahaprabhu to meet Ramananda Raya on the Godavari and not to slight him as a shudra and a worldly man). The future forms yiba, labhiba, bheṭiba are rendered here as addressed to the second person.
[^raga]: Raga-marga — the path of spontaneous love (raganuga bhakti) in the mood of the residents of Vraja, as distinct from the path of injunctions (vidhi). In CC (Madhya 8) Ramananda Raya is the master of rasa through whom Mahaprabhu reveals the highest truths of bhakti.
[^south]: The southern pilgrimage — CC Madhya 7–9. Madhava passes straight to the meeting on the Godavari, omitting the way through Kurmakshetra and Jiyada-Nrisimha.
[^godavari]: The meeting with Ramananda takes place on the bank of the Godavari near Rajamahendri (Kovvur/Rajahmundry); cf. CC Madhya 8.11–23. The forms hoiachu, pārachu (ending -chu) are unusual (in literary Oriya this is the 2nd person singular) — question for a specialist.
[^ajanu]: 'Arms reaching to the knees' (ājānu-lambita-bhuja) is one of the marks of a mahapurusha; Madhava describes the Lord as Ramananda sees Him, cf. the description in CC Madhya 8 (effulgence like many suns, lotus eyes). The meaning of ଉଭରି (ubhari) is from context.
[^ramananda]: Ramananda Raya — son of Bhavananda Raya, governor of Rajamahendri under Prataparudra; Madhava served under him (ch. 3). ବଜେ (baje) is taken as ବିଜେ (bije) — the honorific 'deigns to go, proceeds' (of kings and Deities). The literal sense of v. 70 is not wholly clear.
[^tamaka]: Ṭamaka — the herald's drum, niśāṇa — great kettledrums: as governor of the region Ramananda goes out with music and retinue; cf. CC Madhya 8 (he comes to bathe in a palanquin, with music and brahmanas).
[^dola]: ହାଣ୍ଡୋଳା (hāṇḍoḷā?) — the conjunct is uncertain. In Purnachandra (vol. 7, PDF leaf 1171) ହାଣ୍ଡୋଳା is 'a small burnt earthen temple for village Deities', which does not fit here; by context a palanquin or litter is expected (cf. CC Madhya 8: dolāya caḍi', 'seated in a palanquin'; Oriya ଦୋଳା, ହିନ୍ଦୋଳା). The rendering 'palanquin' is provisional. ବଜେକ = ବିଜେ with the particle -କ (cf. କଲେକ, ହେଲେକ in this same text).
[^tumi]: Cf. CC Madhya 8: Mahaprabhu asks, 'Are you Raya Ramananda?' and, hearing 'yes', embraces him.
[^cloud]: Golden Gauranga and dark Ramananda — 'lightning and a cloud', a traditional image (in CC both weep with love at this meeting and fall to the ground).
[^tendays]: According to CC (Madhya 8), Mahaprabhu spent about ten days with Ramananda in talks about Krishna.
[^samvada]: There follows in Madhava the dialogue of Ramananda and Chaitanya (cf. the 'Ramananda-samvada', CC Madhya 8.57 ff., where Ramananda answers with verses of the shastras). The first word is printed ପଦ୍ମରେ (padmare, 'in the lotus'), which makes no sense; we read ପଦ୍ୟରେ (padyare, 'in verses') — question for a specialist. The pilot ends here.
