# Taranga 6. Srinivasa's Journey from Vrindavana to Gauda

*Shyamananda with Jiva Gosvami; Shyamananda meets Srinivasa and Narottama; the Deities from Purushottama Jana; the Holi play; Narottama's service in the mind; the cowherd boy Krishna at Govardhana and the dream; the Gosvamis decide to send Srinivasa and Narottama to Gauda with the books; Govinda's garland; Haridasa's dream; the story of Kanai; farewell to Lokanatha and Vrindavana*

**1.** Glory, glory to Sri Gaura-Govinda, the jewel of virtues!
Glory to Nityananda-Rama, the mine of the jewels of prema!

**2.** Glory to Advaitachandra, the ocean of compassion!
Glory to Gadadhara, the life's friend of the Pandita![^1]

**3.** Glory, glory to the merciful Srivasa Pandita!
Glory to Vakreshvara, Sri Murari, Haridasa!

**4.** Glory, glory to Sri Svarupa, Rupa, Sanatana!
Glory, glory to the devotees of Sri Gaurachandra!

**5.** Glory, glory to the listeners, abode of virtues!
Listen kindly to what I shall tell now.

**6.** Srinivasa Acharya and Narottama, the two of them,
live joyfully in supreme bliss in Vrindavana.

**7.** One day Srinivasa Acharya Thakura
speaks sweet words to Narottama:

**8.** "Today I see various good omens again and again:
my arm and my right eye are throbbing.[^2]

**9.** Suddenly great joy arises in my heart;
surely I shall meet some Vaishnava."

**10.** Narottama says, "The one I have heard about —
that very Duhkhi Krishnadasa will meet us here."

**11.** Thus, reflecting, they speak of many things with joy in their hearts
and set out to see Jiva Gosvami.

**12.** Meanwhile Shyamananda came to the Gosani's lodging;
the Gosani was pleased with his conduct.[^3]

**13.** Earlier I made known this course of Shyamananda's life;[^4]
now I shall tell something from which great good comes.

**14.** Shyamananda was born on the full moon of Chaitra;
day by day he grew as the moon grows.

**15.** Childhood, boyhood and the other [ages] he spent in play at home;
in early youth he became indifferent to home.

**16.** In the month of Phalguna Shyamananda, most steadfast,
firmly resolved in his mind to leave home.

**17.** In the village of Dandeshvara, in the presence of his mother and father,
taking leave [of them], he came to the village of Ambika.[^5]

**18.** He became the disciple of Hridaya Chaitanya Thakura
and surrendered himself at his lotus feet.

**19.** On the full moon of Phalguna, at an auspicious moment, having become a disciple,
he set out for Vrindavana, having received the command of his worshipful [master].

**20.** For some days he wandered through various tirthas,
and with great happiness he went round Vraja-mandala.

**21.** From Govardhana, with great joy in his heart,
Shyamananda came to the bank of Radha-kunda.

**22.** Gazing at the beauty of Radha-kunda and Shyama-kunda,
he is utterly overwhelmed and floats in tears.

**23.** Seeing Shyamananda's behaviour, Dasa the Vrajavasi,[^6]
floating in supreme bliss, asked [him] about everything.

**24.** He took [him] to Sri Dasa Gosvami
and told the story of Shyamananda's coming.

**25.** Shyamananda, falling to the ground again and again,
bows down; streams of tears flow from his eyes.

**26.** Sri Dasa Gosvami showed [him] great mercy;
seating him nearby, he asked about his welfare.

**27.** Shyamananda related everything in order;
hearing it, the Gosvami was very glad at heart.

**28.** That day he kept [him] with himself
and sent [him] to Vrindavana, giving him a man as companion.

**29.** That man took [him] to Jiva Gosvami
and told the whole story of Shyamananda.

**30.** Shyamananda, falling at the Gosvami's feet,
considers himself lowly and floats in tears.

**31.** Sri Jiva Gosvami, with great fatherly affection,
embraced [him] and told him to sit.

**32.** Having asked for news of the devotees of Sri Gauda,[^7]
he asks how the service of the two Prabhus [is performed].[^8]

**33.** He asked about the doings of Sri Hridaya Chaitanya;
Shyamananda related everything in order.

**34.** He tells of himself, humbly entreating:
"How shall I [be able to] relish the books of bhakti?"

**35.** The Gosvami says, "Do not worry at all:
you will relish [them] together with Srinivasa and Narottama."

**36.** As soon as he heard the names of Srinivasa and Narottama,
his whole body thrilled with horripilation, his heart rejoiced.

**37.** He again makes a request to the Gosvami:
"If I have permission, I shall go and see them both."

**38.** Just as he said this, Narottama and Srinivasa
joyfully came to the Gosvami.

**39.** The Gosvami joyfully says to Srinivasa:
"This is Duhkhi Krishnadasa; he has come from Gauda.

**40.** He is the disciple of Hridaya Chaitanya Thakura;
what can be said of his extraordinary virtues!

**41.** He has given the auspicious news of them all;
just a while ago he came from Radha-kunda.

**42.** He is very eager to see the two of you."
Saying this, he introduced Shyamananda.

**43.** When Shyamananda fell to the ground to bow down,
Srinivasa, clasping him in his arms, cannot let him go.

**44.** When he bowed to Narottama, he too, bowing in return,
embraced [him], wholly overcome with affection.

**45.** Their natural expressions of prema cannot be told;
at the meeting with Shyamananda there was boundless joy.

**46.** Srinivasa, Narottama, Shyamananda, the three of them:
who can tell how wonderful their ways together were?

**47.** Sri Jiva Gosvami was very pleased;
he had Shyamananda begin [the study of] the books of bhakti.

**48.** He entrusted Shyamananda to Srinivasa Acharya;
within some days Shyamananda became a teacher.

**49.** Wonderful is Sri Shyamananda's way of bhakti;
from time to time he sends news to Ambika.

**50.** The desire in his heart was [to attain] the mood of Radhika's maidservant;
by the command of Sri Guru it was obtained from Jiva.

**51.** Sri Jiva Gosvami, showing mercy to Shyamananda,
made him qualified for manasa-seva.[^9]

**52.** Knowing that he had given joy to Radha and Shyamasundara,
Sri Jiva gave him the name Shyamananda.[^10]

**53.** Day by day Shyamananda's bhakti grows;
all the residents of Vrindavana rejoiced.

**54.** Pure bhakti at the feet of Sri Jiva Gosvami;
constant dwelling in the company of Srinivasa and Narottama.

**55.** Singing the glories of Nitai and Chaitanya with Their associates,
he is ceaselessly in great intoxication and is unaware of himself.

**56.** Crying, "Sri Guru Sri Hridaya Chaitanya Prabhu!"
he constantly dances on the bank of the Yamuna with arms raised.

**57.** Not understanding the actions of a perfected devotee, foolish people
engage in idle argument — and by this they suffer great sorrow.

**58.** Shyamananda is always intoxicated with bhakti-rasa;
wherever he looks, his happiness grows without limit.

**59.** Seeing Sri Radha-Govinda, Radha-Madanamohana
and Radha-Gopinatha, he offers up his life [for Them].[^11]

**60.** What a wonder it is to see the beauty of these three!
Is there anyone who could keep composure in his heart?

**61.** The darshan of these three together with Their consorts (yugala-darshana) is not always:
the rule is on Ekadashi, the full moon and the new moon.

**62.** When They sit together on the throne,
at that time there is no comparison for that beauty.

**63.** When Sri Govinda appeared,
Srimati Radhika was not [with Him] at that time.

**64.** So too it was with Sri Madanamohana Prabhu.
Briefly I shall tell how [They] became the Divine Couple (Sri Yugala).

**65.** The son of Maharaja Sri Prataparudra,
named Purushottama Jana, was beautiful in every respect.[^12]

**66.** He, hearing this news about the two Prabhus,
with care sent two Thakuranis.[^13]

**67.** Within some days They arrived near Vrindavana;
hearing of it, everyone in Vrindavana was in supreme bliss.

**68.** Madanamohana, to the servitor in charge of the service,
in the guise of a dream, by a hint, speaks with joy in His heart:

**69.** "He has sent two Deities, taking [both] to be Sri Radhika;
that They are Radhika and Lalita — this no one knows.

**70.** Go forward quickly and bring the two of Them:
the small one, Sri Radhika, place at My left;

**71.** the large one, Lalita, place at My right."
Hearing this, the servitor goes that very moment.

**72.** Bringing the two of Them, with great joy in his heart,
he quickly did everything as commanded.

**73.** *Thus [it is said] in the Stavamrita-lahari [composed] by Sri Vishvanatha Chakravarti:*[^14]
*"O You whose glorious abode is in the jewelled house within the grove of priyaka trees that keep off the rays of the sun, on the land by the bank of the Daughter of the Sun!*
*O Madana-gopala, together with Lalita, who at every step gives delight to Radha, protect Your own abode [and] me!"*[^15]

**74.** The pastimes of Sri Madana-gopala became manifest;
in the society of Vaishnavas great jubilation arose.

**75.** Hearing this wonderful story in the Kshetra, Bara Jana[^16]
was beside himself with joy and unaware of himself.

**76.** He wishes to send a Thakurani to Sri Govinda;
he makes many efforts but sees no way.

**77.** One day, full of anxiety, he fell asleep;
in a dream Sri Radhika appeared before him.

**78.** Very softly she says to Purushottama Jana:
"Quickly send me to Sri Govinda.

**79.** When you were walking around the chakra-beda of Sri Jagannatha,[^17]
seeing me, you thought in your heart of Radhika.

**80.** For a long time I have been staying in the chakra-beda;
everyone calls me Lakshmi Thakurani.

**81.** But that I am Radhika — no one knows this."
Having said this, she vanished that very moment.

**82.** Waking from sleep, Bara Jana was greatly alarmed;
going into the chakra-beda, he saw [her] before his eyes.

**83.** How Radhika came to be in the chakra-beda —
since the subject has arisen, I shall now tell briefly.

**84.** Just as Sri Gopala, from the abode of Govinda,
came on foot to the south to bear witness —

**85.** *Thus [it is said] in the Sadhana-dipika:*
*"Since Sri Gopala, the ocean of mercy, who dwells in the abode of Sri Govinda,*
*went on His own feet to bear witness for a brahmana,*[^18]

**86.** *this One, affectionate to His devotees, shines even today in the land of Odra.*
*Hari, the Lord, is able to do, not to do, and to do [otherwise]."*[^19]

**87.** The journey of Sri Gopala [is told] in detail elsewhere;
in the same way I shall tell a little of the journey of Sri Radhika.

**88.** Once Radha came from Vrindavana
to the land of Utkala, for she is subject to her devotees.

**89.** In the land of Utkala there is a village — Sri Radhanagara;
there lived an excellent brahmana from the south.

**90.** A great Vaishnava, his name was Brihadbhanu;
he was learned in all the shastras — [this] was known everywhere.

**91.** Sri Radhika was like a daughter to Brihadbhanu;
in his house she plays with a joyful heart.

**92.** *Thus [it is said] in the Sadhana-dipika:*
*"About this too a certain auspicious ancient account is heard:*
*a brahmana named Brihadbhanu, from the south, a good Vaishnava,*

**93.** *lived in the land of Odra, in the village of Radhanagara;*[^20]
*by him she was served for some years with the feeling for a daughter.*

**94.** *Since this is her mercy, nothing in this is impossible."*

**95.** What the parental love of the brahmana Brihadbhanu was like —
how can wretched I describe it with one mouth?

**96.** Not seeing her for even half a moment, he thinks it an age;
Radha is his all — apart from Radha he knows nothing.

**97.** After some days the brahmana passed from sight;
the king heard of it from people's mouths.

**98.** That king, who lived in the Kshetra and was very dear to Jagannatha,
came to Sri Radhanagara and sees the divine Deity.

**99.** The most wise king ponders constantly in his mind;
Sri Radhika commands him in a dream:

**100.** "Quickly take me and place me in the temple of Jagannatha."
Receiving such a command, the king was overjoyed.

**101.** In the beautiful place — the chakra-beda of Sri Jagannatha —
he placed Sri Radhika with the greatest care.

**102.** Many days passed in the chakra-beda;
"This is Lakshmi" — this report spread everywhere.

**103.** Calling her Lakshmi, everyone worships her;
and that is true — Sri Radhika is Lakshmi in full.

**104.** Thus she stayed in the chakra-beda;
who can understand [this] lila — who has such power?

**105.** The time came to go to Vrindavana;
therefore she made [it] known to Purushottama Jana.

**106.** By the command in the dream the prince, with great care,
sent [her] to Vrindavana with many people.[^21]

**107.** Sri Radhika went from the Kshetra to Vrindavana;
in Gauda, Utkala and other lands everyone came to know of it.

**108.** On the day she entered Vrindavana,
on that day the ocean of happiness surged.

**109.** They seated her on the throne to the left of Govinda;
a wondrous festivity arose at the meeting of the two.

**110.** How beautiful Govinda is together with Sri Radhika —
who, and how, could describe it with one mouth?

**111.** Thus came about the arrival of the Thakurani;
all this the poets of old have described.

**112.** In the Sadhana-dipika and other books this is set out in detail;
whoever hears all this attains prema-bhakti.

**113.** The manifestation of Gopinatha together with Sri Radhika
I told of earlier — near Vamshivata.

**114.** Sri Govinda, Gopinatha, Madanamohana —
these three Deities are the treasure of life of the Gaudiyas.

**115.** These three are the all-in-all of the Gaudiyas — everyone knows this;
these Three have made the Gaudiyas Their own.

**116.** *Thus [it is said] in the Sri Chaitanya-charitamrita:*
"These three Deities have made the Gaudiyas Their own;
I worship these three Deities — these Three are my Lords."[^22]

**117.** Shyamananda, at the wondrous sight of these Three,
cannot keep patience in his mind for even half a moment.

**118.** Sri Radha-vinoda, and Sri Radha-ramana,
Radha-damodara — at the sight [of Them] his eyes blossomed.[^23]

**119.** Lokanatha, Bhugarbha, Gopala Bhatta and others —
all show Shyamananda the utmost limit of mercy.

**120.** The places where the samadhis of the Sri Gosvamis are —
what he was like on seeing them is impossible to tell.

**121.** From time to time, going to Sri Radhika-[kunda] and Shyama-kunda,
he returns, having had the darshan of Dasa Gosvami.

**122.** How Sri Shyamananda acted in Vrindavana
[has been] described; someone will describe it in detail.

**123.** Sri Acharya Thakura and Thakura Mahashaya —
with these two he always spends his time happily.[^24]

**124.** Seeing the extraordinary doings of Sri Shyamananda,
Srinivasa Acharya becomes very happy.

**125.** How wondrous is the way of Srinivasa Acharya!
Who has the power to tell it with one mouth?

**126.** The pastimes of the Lord in Navadvipa and Vrindavana
he contemplates in his mind — where and how they took place.

**127.** How he meditates on the Navadvipa-lila —
who could describe it in detail?

**128.** One day, in complete solitude, Srinivasa
contemplates the pastimes of Sri Navadvipa-chandra.

**129.** Navadvipa is a beautiful place, worshipped by Brahma and others;
the six seasons, beginning with spring, are always [present there] in person.

**130.** Various trees and creepers, full of flowers, shine there;
with the calls of cuckoos and other [birds] it draws every heart.

**131.** In the midst of Navadvipa — how wondrous is Mayapura!
At the sight of that place every distress goes far away.[^25]

**132.** There Gaurasundara, on a wondrous throne,
joyfully plays, surrounded by His beloved ones.

**133.** Srinivasa beholds that unprecedented beauty;
by the Lord's command all remain beside the Lord.[^26]

**134.** Taking fragrant sandal paste with great care,
he applied it to the Lord's holy body in a wondrous design.

**135.** Placing garlands of various flowers around the Lord's neck,
he fans [Him] with a chamara with a delighted heart.

**136.** Drinking the nectar of the moon-face of Sri Gaurachandra,
Srinivasa is overwhelmed — he does not know himself.

**137.** He cannot hold his body steady, it sways;
from his long eyes flow tears of the bliss of prema.

**138.** Many transformations of feeling — he has no awareness of his body;
seeing Srinivasa's state, the Lord was exceedingly pleased.

**139.** Through a devotee He gave the garland from His own neck;
receiving the touch of that garland, he swims in bliss.

**140.** At that moment the Acharya's outer consciousness returned;
he sees on his own neck the garland given by the Lord.

**141.** The beauty and fragrance of the holy garland have no limit;
on every side bees rush about, vying with one another.

**142.** The Acharya quickly hid the garland;
unnoticed, someone saw it.

**143.** Day after day the Acharya acts in secret;
day and night he is immersed in the pastimes of Navadvipa.

**144.** In the same way, in the waves of the ocean of the Vrindavana-lila
he floats unceasingly in the supreme delight of prema.

**145.** One day in springtime Srinivasa
contemplates in his mind Sri Krishna's Holi play.

**146.** There is a place named "Phalgunastha-lila";
now everyone calls it "Phagutala."[^27]

**147.** A place utterly secluded, its beauty enchanting;
a soft, soft, cool breeze blows without ceasing.

**148.** All around — what a fresh kadamba forest!
The calls of the shari, the shuka, the cuckoo and other [birds] are an elixir.

**149.** On various blossoming flowers the bees hum;
hundreds of thousands of peacocks and peahens dance.

**150.** Herds of stags and does roam about, intoxicated;
Rai and Kanu with the sakhis stand and watch.

**151.** There Vrinda, with hundreds of thousands of maidservants,
prepares the things for the Holi play in many delightful ways.

**152.** She made ready phagu and the rest, of various kinds,
and tuned the vina and many [other] instruments.[^28]

**153.** Rai and Kanu with the sakhis, rejoicing at heart,
began the Holi play in the bower.

**154.** Radhika, surrounded by the sakhis, in great merriment
throws wonderful phagu on Sri Krishna's body.

**155.** At a sakhi's sign Srinivasa, in the form of a maidservant,
staying close to Radhika, supplies the phagu.

**156.** With what wondrous art Rai and Shyama play!
Seeing the beauty, millions of Kamas swoon.

**157.** The phagu flies, and all is covered in red;[^29]
the ears are stopped by the clamor of many instruments.

**158.** Krishna, the crown of rasikas, boundlessly playful,
showers phagu on everyone without cease.

**159.** Drenching [them] with water of musk, kunkuma and the like,
He embraces, kisses and so on, under various pretexts.

**160.** The Two play an incomparable Holi;
Lalita and the other sakhis are filled with ecstatic thrill.

**161.** After a while all became calm
and seated Rai and Kanu, the Two of Them, on a throne.

**162.** To remove [Their] fatigue, they fanned [Them] with a chamara whisk;
the desire of Srinivasa the maidservant was fulfilled.

**163.** The service complete, on coming back to outer awareness,
he sees his body covered with phagu and cannot hide it.

**164.** The phagu glitters, its fragrance is boundless;
whoever's nose it touches cannot stay calm.

**165.** Thus, day after day, he is overwhelmed in the mind in various ways;
who can describe how unrestrained [his] prema is?

**166.** Seeing Srinivasa Acharya's acts of prema,
Narottama cannot contain his heart for joy.

**167.** How Sri Narottama serves in the mind —
who could describe it with a single mouth?

**168.** One day Radha and Krishna, with the sakhis,
were sporting in a nikunja in the supreme delight of prema.

**169.** Sri Radhika playfully says to a sakhi:
"Quickly make a fine arrangement of food here."

**170.** Lalita and the other sakhis, greatly delighted,
all prepare the food with care.

**171.** Narottama, in the form of a maidservant, with great care
boils down milk at the sakhi's sign.

**172.** Seeing the milk boil over, he was alarmed
and took the milk pot off the stove with his hands.

**173.** His hand was burnt — he had no awareness of it at all;
having boiled down the milk, he gave it to the sakhi.

**174.** With joy in [their] hearts [they] fed Radha and Krishna;
as soon as [he] received the remnants, he came back to outer awareness.

**175.** As soon as he saw his burnt hand, he concealed it;
some intimate person knew the secret.

**176.** How Sri Narottama meditates in the mind —
who will tell it in detail?

**177.** His mind always wanders in Navadvipa and Vrindavana;
he is overwhelmed with bliss together with Srinivasa Acharya.

**178.** Srinivasa Acharya, taking Sri Narottama with him,
from time to time goes to Sri Govardhana and stays there.

**179.** One day, in a cave of Sri Govardhana,
they hear the sound of the flute, by which the three worlds are enchanted.

**180.** Hearing the sound of the flute, they were overwhelmed;
they cannot hold their bodies steady — they sway.

**181.** As they entered the cave of Sri Govardhana,
the fragrance of Krishna's body came and entered their nostrils.

**182.** Receiving that fragrance, their happiness knew no bounds;
swooning, both fell right there.

**183.** After a while both came back to outer awareness;
before them they see a cowherd boy.

**184.** A wondrous turban on his head, a beautiful body,
in his hand only a stick; he is utterly composed.

**185.** Seeing such a son of a cowherd, Srinivasa, with affection,
asks him, joy in his heart:

**186.** "Tell me, tell me, son of a cowherd, why are you here?"
He says, "To protect you two.

**187.** There are many dangers here, which you do not know;
we know everything here from grazing the cows.

**188.** From afar I saw you two
lying on the ground; neither of you was conscious.

**189.** Leaving my companions, I came in great haste;
it has been a long time that I have been standing here.

**190.** Now, with an untroubled mind, I am going to graze the cows."
Saying this, he vanished right there.

**191.** Srinivasa Acharya thinks within his mind:
"Where has the cowherd boy gone from here?

**192.** Having sprinkled [us] with the nectar of his words, he disappeared;
through the fault of my own ill fortune I could not recognize him."

**193.** Thus the two say much, sitting under a tree;
they heave long sighs and float in the tears of their eyes.

**194.** In sorrow of heart the two passed the day;
when part of the night had gone, by Krishna's will sleep overcame them.

**195.** Under the guise of a dream, the son of the king of Vraja appeared,
in a beautiful dark form that enchants the worlds.

**196.** In the dress of the best of dancers, a flute gracing His hand;
at the radiance of the moon of His face, Madana swoons.

**197.** Smiling sweetly, sweetly, He speaks gently, gently:
"You were entranced by the sound of My flute;

**198.** you swooned, receiving the fragrance of [My] body —
and I came running to you two.

**199.** In the guise of a cowherd boy I gave you darshan;
when you regained consciousness, on a pretext I went away.

**200.** You two became distraught for My sake;
I have shown Myself [to you] — look at Me and be glad."

**201.** Having said this, after a while He disappeared;
when the dream broke, the streams from their eyes could not be stopped.

**202.** After a while the two became fully calm;
morning came, and in the morning they performed their morning duties.

**203.** At Govardhana the pastimes of Krishna are abundant;
in talk of them their hearts were always joyful.

**204.** Thus from time to time they stay at Radha-kunda;
the two rejoice in the darshan of Dasa Gosvami.

**205.** What mercy Dasa Gosvami shows the two of them —
I have no power to describe it.

**206.** Krishnadasa Kaviraja and others, full of prema —
the affection all of them have cannot be told.

**207.** Overwhelmed with the bliss of the affection of all these,
they think themselves fulfilled, beholding the beauty of the Kunda.

**208.** One day at midday Srinivasa
wanders with Narottama through various nikunjas.

**209.** He says to Narottama: "By this path
Sri Radhika goes to the temple of the Sun and worships the Sun."[^30]

**210.** Just as he said this, suddenly, in that very place,
the sound of ankle bells came and entered their ears.

**211.** The bliss that maddened the two of them —
all that cannot be described here at length.

**212.** At Nandagrama, Javata, Varshana and other places —
who knows how to tell the delight that overwhelmed them?

**213.** In Vrindavana they were immersed in an ocean of happiness;
I do not know how to tell the mysteries they saw.

**214.** What favor all the Gosvamis showed [them] —
for fear of enlarging the book, it could not be described.

**215.** All the Gosvamis together resolved firmly in their hearts
to send Srinivasa quickly to the land of Gauda.

**216.** This news became known everywhere:
"Srinivasa will go to Gauda with the books;

**217.** he will bestow the jewel-like books from place to place;
the departure will be in the bright fortnight of Agrahayana."

**218.** "If Srinivasa goes away from here,
how will the Lord's beloved ones keep their composure?

**219.** How will our hearts be steady?" —
even as they say this, tears of prema flow from their eyes.

**220.** The wise people of Vraja cannot keep their composure:
Srinivasa Acharya is like the very life of all.

**221.** Who does not find joy in Srinivasa's conduct,
who considers himself utterly lowly and destitute?

**222.** Seeing his way of devotion, Sri Jiva Gosani
constantly feels boundless happiness in his heart.

**223.** One day Sri Jiva and the others, in the temple of Govinda,
all gathered together with joyful hearts.

**224.** They say to Sri Govinda-deva in sweet words:
"Give Srinivasa the power to distribute the books."

**225.** Even as they said this, from Govinda's neck
the garland broke and fell, to be given to Srinivasa.

**226.** Hurriedly the pujari carefully took the holy garland
and gave it to Srinivasa, with tears of prema.

**227.** Srinivasa, carefully taking the holy garland,
lost his composure, gazing at Sri Govinda's face.

**228.** Again and again he bows, falling to the ground;
streams flow from his eyes; he cannot stop them.

**229.** Seeing Govinda's mercy toward Srinivasa,
all praise [him] with great joy of heart.

**230.** Sri Jiva Gosvami and all the others then and there
fixed the day for the departure to Sri Gauda.

**231.** The fifth day of the bright fortnight of Agrahayana is auspicious;
with everyone's consent they hastened to send [him] on his journey.

**232.** Sri Jiva Gosvami sent Srinivasa
to Dasa Gosvami to take leave.

**233.** Sri Dasa Gosani's state cannot be told:
his heart burns constantly with the pain of separation.

**234.** Saying, "Where are Sri Svarupa, Rupa and Sanatana?"
he is bathed in tears and rolls in the dust.[^31]

**235.** His body is extremely emaciated, weaker from moment to moment;
he eats a little only once every two or four days.

**236.** Although his dried-up body sways in the wind,
still he completes all his fixed practices.

**237.** Falling to the ground to bow down, he cannot rise;
to those who would stop him in this he says nothing.

**238.** But if someone supports him, he praises him again and again;
seeing such zeal for sadhana, even the gods are amazed.

**239.** The Govardhana-shila and the gunja necklace given by the Lord
he serves — with what wondrous happiness! — forgetting himself.[^32]

**240.** Absorbed in taking the holy name, he knows neither day nor night;
there is no sleep in his eyes, streams of tears flow from both eyes.

**241.** Who can understand what Dasa Gosvami does?
He is always immersed in the pastimes of Radha-Krishna and Chaitanya.

**242.** Sitting in solitude, he studies the books.
At that time Srinivasa Acharya arrived.

**243.** Srinivasa, on seeing Dasa Gosvami,
considers himself blessed, falling at his feet.

**244.** Sri Dasa Gosvami embraced Srinivasa,
asked after his welfare and seated him nearby.

**245.** At that very moment Narottama and Shyamananda came
and bowed at the holy feet of Dasa Gosvami.

**246.** With great kindness Dasa Gosvami, having asked
both of them about their welfare, looks at Srinivasa.

**247.** Srinivasa told of his going to Sri Gauda;
hearing it, Sri Gosvami gladly gave his consent.

**248.** Having warned Srinivasa in every way to be careful,
he embraces him and is bathed in the tears of both eyes.

**249.** He embraced Narottama and Shyamananda;
all of them with care worshipped the Gosvami's feet.

**250.** They took their leave. What the Gosvami's affection was like —
I wish to describe it, but I have no such power.

**251.** How they all were at the time of farewell —
who, seeing it, would not be bathed in tears?

**252.** Krishnadasa Kaviraja and other wise ones,
taking these three, quickly came to Vrindavana.

**253.** And the Vaishnavas, wherever each of them was,
hearing the news, all came to Vrindavana.

**254.** Sri Jiva Gosvami, receiving the Vaishnavas of Vraja with honour,
gave lodgings to them all.

**255.** To a certain fortunate mahajana of Mathura
he kindly gives an order:[^33]

**256.** "Srinivasa Acharya, taking the books,
will set out for Gauda in two or four days.

**257.** Find the means for him to go quickly."
Hearing this, the mahajana considers himself blessed.

**258.** "I have quickly arranged a royal officer, foot guards and carts,
and appointed experienced men to send along with him.[^34]

**259.** Giving them money for the expenses of the journey, [I have] made [all] ready,"
he informed the Gosvami.

**260.** The Gosvami too, seeing the four loads of books,
places them in a wooden chest, warding off the danger of rain.

**261.** The chest was filled with the jewels of books;
at the sight of those books sorrow goes far away.

**262.** The books that he arranged in the chest —
the names of those books were made known before.[^35]

**263.** Giving some books of his own composition — on siddhanta and others —
he speaks very softly, looking into Srinivasa's face:

**264.** "The books that remain I shall revise;
what I shall write I shall send in due course."

**265.** Having said this, he at once took Srinivasa
and went for the darshan of Sri Madana-gopala.

**266.** Srinivasa, seeing Sri Madana-gopala,
cannot keep his composure: his heart overflows with prema.

**267.** At the leave-taking his tears could not be checked;
with a gesture Madanamohana gave him leave.[^36]

**268.** The pujari Gosani gave him the garland-prasada;
how everyone consoled him — there is no end to telling it.

**269.** What happened at the darshan of Sanatana Gosvami's samadhi —
who can describe it?[^37]

**270.** Saying, "Prabhu Sanatana, who grieves at the grief of others!"
he cannot control his body and rolls in the dust.

**271.** He is utterly immersed in the deeds of Sanatana;
Sanatana's heart is beyond the reach of others.

**272.** Sri Krishna Chaitanya Prabhu, in supreme bliss,
spoke of him to Ramananda in Nilachala.[^38]

**273.** *Thus [it is said] in the Sri Chaitanya-charitamrita:*
"His elder brother is named Sanatana;
on earth there is no learned man his equal.[^39]

**274.** As is your renunciation of worldly things, such is his way;
in him indeed abide humility, renunciation and learning."

**275.** Thus the Lord [and] the devotees say in place after place:
Sri Gosvami Sanatana is the Lord's beloved.

**276.** There is no one else who so grieves at the grief of others;
he is an ocean of mercy, his deeds are boundless in the world.[^40]

**277.** *Thus [it is said] in the Vilapa:*
*He who with great effort made me — blind and unwilling — drink the rasa of bhakti joined with renunciation,*
*the ocean of mercy who grieves at the grief of others — in that master Sanatana I take shelter.*[^41]

**278.** His branches: Sri Rupa Gosvami above all,
Sri Rajendra Gosvami, the brahmachari named Krishna,[^42]

**279.** Krishna Mishra Gosvami, whose deeds are wondrous,
Gosvami Sri Bhagavanta Dasa and others — they are renowned.

**280.** Srinivasa Acharya is immersed in Sanatana's virtues;
he cannot check the streams of tears — how wonderful!

**281.** Sri Jiva Gosvami, calming him in every way,
took Srinivasa to his own dwelling.

**282.** There Srinivasa, gathering his composure,
had the darshan of Rupa Gosvami's samadhi.[^43]

**283.** Falling to the ground, he bows down, taking his leave;
streams flow from his eyes, he cannot grow calm.

**284.** Meditating on the charming character of Sri Rupa Gosvami,
Srinivasa Acharya feels his heart overflow.

**285.** Ah! The glory of Sri Rupa is boundless;
however anyone describes it, that is known everywhere.

**286.** *As [it is said] in the play composed by Sri Kavi Karnapura, Act Nine, verse forty-three:*
*In Rupa — dear [to Him] by nature, beloved by nature, the embodiment of prema, naturally wise,*
*like Himself, devoted to the one — in Rupa, the form of His own pastimes, the Lord expanded [Himself].*[^44]

**287.** *Thus [it is said] in the Sadhana-dipika:*
*Those who stand outside the teaching of Sri Rupa, the ocean of mercy —*
*travellers on the path of raga must certainly not keep company with them.*[^45]

**288.** *Again:*
*I bow again and again to the pair of lotus feet of Srimad Rupa;*
*by his grace even an ignorant man becomes a partaker in the knowledge of his teaching.*

**289.** *Again, in the Prema-bhakti-chandrika:*
*When will that Rupa, who established on earth the desire of Sri Chaitanya's heart,*
*grant me [a place] near his feet?*[^46]

**290.** *Again, in the Sadhana-dipika:*
*O tongue, always say the name "Rupa";*
*O mind, remember Rupa, the embodiment of compassion;*
*O head, bow down to Rupa, whose glance is merciful,*
*and to Raghunatha Dasa — his beautiful body, not different [from him].*

**291.** What wondrous qualities Sri Rupa Gosani has!
Thus the wise have described them in many ways.

**292.** *Thus [it is said] in a song (raga Vibhasha):*
If in [the age of] Kali Rupa had not taken a body,
who on earth would have opened the door of the great treasury of the prema of Vraja? (Refrain.)[^47]

**293.** Who, giving up everything, would worship Vrindavana; who would examine all the scriptures?
Without the swan, who could separate milk mixed with water?[^48]

**294.** Who would know Mathura and Vrindavana, who would know the ways of Vraja?
Who would know the love of Radha and Madhava, who would know all the precepts?

**295.** By the grace of whose feet all people, singing again and again, find happiness;
what pure love! People hear, and in Madho's heart bliss increases.[^49]

**296.** What to speak of others — Krishna Chaitanya Himself
loses His composure over the qualities of Sri Rupa.

**297.** This is known everywhere, there is no end to telling it:
Sri Rupa Gosani is the life of those dear to the Lord.

**298.** O brother! The glory of Sanatana and Rupa —
in however many ways they sing it, no one finds its limit.

**299.** *Thus [it is said] in a song (raga Vibhasha):*
Glory to my very life, Sanatana and Rupa!
The two brothers are the refuge of the helpless, the sacrificial post of yoga and sacrifice. (Refrain.)[^50]

**300.** The natural sweetness of Vrindavana, the well of the nectar of prema,
oceans of compassion, friends of the forlorn, kings of the assembly of devotees.

**301.** In following the teaching of bhakti and the Bhagavata — skilful and wise commanders;
their pure fame is known in the fourteen worlds; [they are] the relish that delights the tongue.[^51]

**302.** The shade of the soft dust of their lotus feet removes the scorching heat of the sun of Kali;
Vyasa the worshipper ever worships the incomparable feet of Radha.[^52]

**303.** *Again (raga Vibhasha):*
Glory to my Rupa and Sanatana, the crest-jewels of sadhus,
whose bhakti held steadfast to a single rasa, whose love was for Krishna and Radha. (Refrain.)

**304.** From the natural sweetness of Vrindavana there is joy in every hair of their bodies;
giving up everything, day and night they worshipped the pastimes in the kunjas, with deep love for Radha.

**305.** The two brothers are the fruits of the mercy of Krishna Chaitanya, the ocean of compassion;
without them Vyasa is forlorn, like the leaves of a withered tree.

**306.** Who can describe the deeds of Rupa and Sanatana?
Briefly I have told a little, in the course of the narrative.

**307.** The prayer Srinivasa offered before Sri Rupa's samadhi —
who could tell it with only one mouth?

**308.** Srinivasa, by the grace of Sri Rupa,
taking his leave, goes from the samadhi.

**309.** To the treasure of Sri Jiva's life, Radha-Damodara,
he goes for darshan, and his heart finds no rest.

**310.** Radha-Damodara Prabhu is the abode of rasa;
His favour toward Srinivasa is boundless.

**311.** How He gave him leave is beyond telling;
Sri Jiva Gosani gave the sacred garland-prasada.

**312.** Seeing Sri Damodara's mercy, Srinivasa
lost all composure in the joy of his heart.

**313.** Keeping Srinivasa near him for a while,
he speaks affectionate words to Srinivasa:

**314.** "Taking both Narottama and Shyamananda with you,
go to the Gosvami, holding on to your composure.[^53]

**315.** From here I am going to the temple of Govinda;
whatever task there is there I will quickly accomplish.

**316.** After a while I too shall go there;
today you will take leave everywhere."

**317.** Saying this, he set off for the temple of Sri Govinda
and had the cart for loading the books brought there.

**318.** Quickly settling whatever other tasks there were,
he makes his way to Sri Bhatta Gosvami.

**319.** Meanwhile Srinivasa, taking the two with him,
goes to the Gosvami to take leave.

**320.** On that path, in a secluded grove beneath a tree,
the brahmana Haridasa Acharya floats in tears.[^54]

**321.** Crying "Sri Krishna Chaitanya!" he heaves long sighs;
his body is extremely emaciated — there is no hope for his life.

**322.** Srinivasa went and saw him;
he bowed, and the other embraced him firmly.

**323.** The brahmana Haridasa Acharya, absorbed in deep affection,
speaks to Srinivasa in the sweetest words:

**324.** "Tomorrow at daybreak the departure for Gauda will take place.
What I tell you, you must surely do.

**325.** Shridasa and Gokulananda are my sons;
birth after birth those two are your disciples.

**326.** When you reach Gauda, give those two the mantra of initiation
and teach them the scriptures of bhakti, which are supremely rare."

**327.** Hearing this, Srinivasa became almost stunned;
the brahmana Haridasa Acharya consoles him:

**328.** "You yourself do not know how great your power is;
do not keep even a trace of worry about this in your mind.

**329.** You will keep my word — there is nothing wrong in this."
Saying so, he made Srinivasa glad.

**330.** Wonderful are the qualities of Haridasa Acharya;
I shall tell how he came to Vraja.

**331.** While the Lord was present here, by the Lord's order everyone
went back and forth between Gauda, Vraja and Nilachala.

**332.** Pandita Jagadananda, coming to Vrindavana,
went once more by way of Gauda into the Lord's presence.[^55]

**333.** Thus the circles of devotees in Gauda, in Kshetra and in Vraja
floated ceaselessly in the boundless ocean of happiness.[^56]

**334.** By Advaita's wish the Lord withdrew His pastimes;[^57]
the whole world sank into an ocean of grief.

**335.** The brahmana Haridasa Acharya, when the Lord was no longer seen,
resolved in his mind to give up his body.

**336.** Not for half a moment can he keep his composure;
he swims ceaselessly in the tears of his eyes.

**337.** Nothing pleases him — his heart burns as if in fire;
crying "Where has the Lord gone?" he rolls on the ground.

**338.** "At the end of the night I shall enter a pit of fire;
without Lord Gaurachandra I will not keep my life."

**339.** As he pondered thus, sleep drew him in a little;
in the guise of a dream Sri Gaurasundara appeared to him.

**340.** What wondrous beauty, enchanting the worlds!
The radiance of His limbs lights up the universe.

**341.** Gold, lightning — what comparison can stand before Him?
The pride of millions upon millions of Kandarpas flees in fear.

**342.** The moon of His face surpasses the full moon;
what sweet, gentle smile rains down nectar!

**343.** What arms, what broad chest, what enchanting eyes!
With what fresh grace His gait puts the elephant to shame!

**344.** The brahmana Haridasa, seeing Him, overwhelmed at heart,
places those feet on his head and rolls in the dust.

**345.** The Lord, the life of His devotees, with His two arms
lifted the brahmana Haridasa and took him to His bosom.

**346.** The Lord, who is subject to His devotees, could not keep His composure;
bathing him in tears, He speaks gently:

**347.** "Hearing of your grief, My heart breaks;
what you have resolved in your mind is not right.

**348.** My dear Srinivasa is the very form of prema;
he will reveal the jewels of books in Gauda.

**349.** What is there to say — you already know all this.
When you meet him, you will show him favour.

**350.** And these two sons of yours —
have them receive the sacred mantra from him.

**351.** Every perfection will come through Srinivasa's mercy;
the strength of these two's bhakti will spread through the world.

**352.** In Vrindavana the meeting with you will take place;
do not delay, go there quickly.

**353.** I am always near you;
from time to time you will be able to see Me."

**354.** Saying many such things and embracing him firmly,
the Lord, affectionate to His devotees, vanished from sight.

**355.** When his sleep broke, he became deeply agitated;
seeing that morning had come, at dawn he performed his morning rites.

**356.** Calling his sons, he says in sweet words:
"Today I shall set out for Vrindavana.

**357.** The good fortune of you two cannot be told;
the favour of Sri Chaitanya Prabhu is immense.

**358.** O my dear boys! From Srinivasa, dear to the Lord,
you will receive the mantra of initiation after some days.

**359.** Having gone to Vraja, he will come again to Gauda
to spread the supreme, priceless books of bhakti.

**360.** As soon as you see him, you will know his power;
the jewel of bhakti, hard for the gods to attain, will be gained."

**361.** Saying many such things and taking leave of his sons,
he leaves home by the will of Krishna Chaitanya.

**362.** After some days he entered Vrindavana
and spent some days in supreme bliss.

**363.** After that he was plunged into an ocean of grief;
to tell of all that makes the heart break.

**364.** Remembering the qualities of Rupa and Sanatana, he weeps;
seeing that state of his, no one can remain steady.

**365.** What shall I say of the ways of Haridasa Acharya!
By remembering him one attains pure bhakti.

**366.** In this way he went to Vrindavana;
fearing the length of the book, I have not told it in detail.

**367.** Showing his favour to Srinivasa Acharya,
he embraces him again and again, saying many things.

**368.** Losing all composure out of affection for Srinivasa,
as he bids him farewell he floats in tears.

**369.** Embracing Sri Narottama firmly,
he said so much that it cannot be described.

**370.** The merciful one, embracing Shyamananda,
in agitation contemplated his highest good.

**371.** Srinivasa Acharya and the others, having taken leave,
float in tears, their hearts utterly unsteady.

**372.** On the bank of the Yamuna there is a lovely tree;
a supremely secluded place, hidden from others.

**373.** A brahmana, a resident of Vraja, named Kanaiya
worships Krishna, sitting beneath that tree.[^58]

**374.** When Srinivasa went there and bowed to him,
he embraced him and could not let him go.

**375.** Sprinkling him with tears, he says again and again:
"This meeting that has happened — there will be no other.

**376.** You are full of prema — you will spread the books in Gauda
and easily destroy the sins of living beings.

**377.** You are the vessel of the mercy of Rupa and Sanatana;
how much can I tell of your good fortune?"

**378.** Having said this, [at the thought of] the deeds of Rupa and Sanatana
he became utterly overwhelmed and could not regain his composure.

**379.** What love he had for Rupa and Sanatana
I tell a little; I cannot describe it in full.

**380.** Kanai's mother was an abode of great affection;
her parental love for Rupa and Sanatana was boundless.

**381.** Who can understand Kanai's ways?
He always stayed close to Rupa and Sanatana.

**382.** Sri Rupa and Sri Sanatana, with great respect,
were from time to time brought home and offered a meal.

**383.** Fruits, roots, greens — whatever could be had
was brought with great care to the dwelling of the two.

**384.** One day Sri Krishna, taking the form of Kanai,
gave madhukari to Sanatana Gosvami.[^59]

**385.** Such was Krishna's pastime in the guise of Kanai;
and Kanaiya's qualities became known everywhere.

**386.** No one would part with Kanai even for a moment;
he was the supremely dear one of Sanatana and Rupa.

**387.** At the disappearance of Sanatana and Rupa Gosvami
he firmly resolved in his mind: "I shall give up my life."

**388.** By the will of those two his life was preserved;
leaving home, he wandered through Vraja.

**389.** He made his dwelling on the bank of the Yamuna beneath a tree;
he always rolls in the dust and floats in tears.

**390.** Saying "Rupa, Sanatana," he heaves long sighs;
without those two he has no hope of life.

**391.** Seeing that state, Srinivasa could not be calm;
he took his leave, tears of prema flowing from his eyes.

**392.** Going to Sri Bhugarbha Gosvami,
they all bowed to him, rolling on the ground.

**393.** In a rapture of affection he embraced them;
Srinivasa told him everything in due order.

**394.** Consoling them all, the Gosvami said:
"Tomorrow, at the time you set out, I will come to the temple of Govinda.

**395.** My heart breaks at having to send you off."
As he said this, streams of tears flowed from his eyes.

**396.** Who can describe what the Gosvami's affection was like!
He entrusted them all to Sri Krishna Chaitanya.

**397.** Bowing again at the Gosvami's feet,
they all went on, greatly distressed.

**398.** As they were going to Sri Bhatta Gosvami,
on the way they met other Vaishnavas as well.

**399.** Entreating them in many ways
and receiving their permission, they set off along the path through the kunjas.

**400.** Along that same path Sri Jiva Gosani was coming;
he led them to Bhatta Gosani.

**401.** Sri Gopala Bhatta is sitting in seclusion,
having given his eyes and mind to Sri Radha-ramana.

**402.** From time to time he recites in a sweet voice verses of his own composing;
hearing that string of names, who could keep his composure?

**403.** *Thus [it is said]:*
*O Lord of Bhandira, best of those adorned with the peacock feather, whose limbs are anointed with sandal paste!*
*O sovereign of Vrinda's forest, dark like a full-blown blue lotus!*
*O beloved of the Kalindi, son of Nanda, supreme bliss, lotus-eyed one!*
*O Sri Govinda, Mukunda, beautiful of form, gladden me, the wretched one!*[^60]

**404.** What Sri Bhatta Gosvami did cannot be described:
hearing that Sri Jiva was coming, he keeps looking at the path.

**405.** Sri Jiva Gosvami, together with Srinivasa and the others,
arrived before Sri Bhatta Gosvami.

**406.** Bowing to the Gosvami, he says again and again:
"Bestow on Srinivasa the full transmission of power.

**407.** Place your two feet on Srinivasa's head,
so that he may reach Sri Gauda-mandala without obstacles,

**408.** so that, crushing the pride of the unbelievers,
he may freely distribute the books."[^61]

**409.** Hearing this and much more, Sri Bhatta Gosani
offered a prayer to Radha-ramana.

**410.** Sri Radha-ramana, showing mercy to Srinivasa,
gave him leave in a way I cannot describe.

**411.** Sri Bhatta Gosani, seeing the mercy shown to Srinivasa,
brought a prasada garland and gave it to him in a rapture of affection.

**412.** Srinivasa, falling to the ground again and again,
offers obeisances, streams of tears flowing from his eyes.

**413.** Sri Gopala Bhatta, calming him with gentle words,
entrusted Srinivasa to Sri Radha-ramana.

**414.** Showing Srinivasa the utmost grace,
he commanded: "May all perfection come soon!"

**415.** To Narottama he speaks sweet words:
"May Sri Radha-ramana fulfill your desires!"

**416.** To Shyamananda he says with affection again and again:
"May Sri Radha-ramana be merciful to you!"

**417.** Saying this, he embraces them all;
they all reverently worshipped his feet.

**418.** Sri Bhatta Gosvami says to Jiva Gosvami:
"Tomorrow morning I will come to the temple of Sri Govinda."

**419.** Sri Jiva Gosvami, bowing together with everyone,
went to the place of Lokanatha Gosvami.

**420.** The Gosvami is sitting alone in seclusion,
his eyes fixed on the moonlike face of Sri Radha-vinoda.

**421.** Seeing Sri Jiva arrive, Lokanatha
was filled with an affection beyond description.

**422.** Bowing, Sri Jiva says in a gentle voice:
"Tomorrow morning they set out for the land of Gauda."

**423.** Lokanatha made this known to Sri Radha-vinoda
and gave His garland of grace to Srinivasa.

**424.** All that he said in a rapture of affection
to Srinivasa and all the others cannot be told.

**425.** Srinivasa, Narottama and Shyamananda — all three,
falling to the ground, bow at the Gosani's feet.

**426.** Lokanatha Gosvami cannot hold back his heart;
embracing them all, he bathed them in his tears.

**427.** Regaining his composure, he says to Sri Jiva:
"The charge of all of them rests entirely on you."

**428.** Sri Jiva Gosvami, displaying humility in many ways,
goes off with everyone, bowing to the Gosvami.

**429.** Going there, they had the darshan of Gopinatha —
how wondrous is His pose, enchanting the worlds!

**430.** What arose in their hearts on seeing that beauty —
who has the power to describe it with a single mouth?

**431.** Sri Jiva says to Sri Madhu Pandita and the others:
"Let Srinivasa's journey be free from obstacles."[^62]

**432.** Sri Madhu Pandita made this known to Gopinatha;
he brought the garland of the Lord's permission and gave it to Srinivasa.

**433.** Srinivasa bows again and again, prostrate on the ground;
as he takes his leave, streams of tears flow from his eyes.

**434.** Everyone, having calmed Srinivasa,
enjoined him: "Come to Vrindavana again."

**435.** What they said, showing grace to Narottama and Shyamananda —
all that I cannot tell.

**436.** In a rapture of prema they all embraced these [three];
all [three], falling to the ground, bowed to them all.

**437.** They all say to Sri Jiva Gosvami:
"Tomorrow morning, at the hour of departure, we will gather together."

**438.** Hearing this, Sri Jiva instructs Srinivasa:
"Now all of you go to the abode of Gopishvara."

**439.** Srinivasa Acharya and the others went to Gopishvara;
Sri Jiva Gosvami went to the temple of Sri Govinda.

**440.** Srinivasa, having the darshan of Gopishvara,
offered prayers beyond description.

**441.** Gopishvara was supremely pleased with Srinivasa;
unseen, in the guise of a brahmana, he gave him leave to go.

**442.** Narottama and Shyamananda, distraught,
say such things to Gopishvara that the heart melts to hear them.

**443.** Reverently bowing to Sri Shankara-Gopishvara,
Srinivasa Acharya and the others walk slowly on.

**444.** Seeing the samadhi of Kashishvara Gosvami,
they bowed down, rolling in the dust.[^63]

**445.** Who knows how to tell the glory of Kashishvara,
who brought Sri Gaura-govinda to Vrindavana?

**446.** He placed Him at Govinda's right;
seeing the beauty of Them both, the heart swells with joy.

**447.** Sri Chaitanya, out of love for Sri Kashishvara,
came to the western land in the form of a Deity.

**448.** *Thus [it is said] in the Sadhana-dipika:*
*I worship the glorious Kashishvara, out of love for whom*
*Chaitanya-deva Himself, by His mercy, came to the western land.*

**449.** Kashishvara, dear to the Lord, is renowned in the world;
Sri Rupa and Sri Sanatana are immersed in his virtues.

**450.** Srinivasa Acharya, remembering all this,
lost his composure and could not hold back his heart.

**451.** Again and again he bows, falling to the ground;
he does not know what will become of his heart at the parting.

**452.** Beholding the samadhi of Raghunatha Bhatta,
he floats in tears, and his heart is torn.[^64]

**453.** The virtues of Raghunatha Bhatta Gosvami —
whose heart is not soothed the moment he hears of them?

**454.** He was a teacher of all the shastras; hearing his expositions,
Brihaspati, with a joyful heart, applauds.

**455.** His reading of the Bhagavata is beyond compare;
Vyasa and others long to hear it and find joy.

**456.** Seeing his way of bhakti, the gods are amazed —
thus Srinivasa speaks of Bhatta's glory.

**457.** Srinivasa and the others, falling to the ground and bowing,
took their leave and went to the temple of Govinda.

**458.** At the darshan of Govinda he was utterly overwhelmed
and went with Sri Jiva Gosvami to his lodging.

**459.** His anuraga grows mightily moment by moment;
he sings a song of his own composing, forgetting himself.

**460.** Sri Radhika says to her friend again and again:
"I have seen the form of Govinda — an ocean of nectar."[^65]

**461.** *Raga Suhai:*
What carver turned the moon of [His] face,
who carved those two eyes?
As I look and look, what my heart goes through —
that heart alone is its witness.

**462.** Who, cutting jewels with care,
had [earrings] fashioned and set them in [His] ears?
Together with my mind, these five life-airs of mine
have become yogis, rapt in meditation on Him.

**463.** Above the nose shines a gaja-mukta pearl,
set in gold at its sides:
is it a sliver of the moon entwined with lightning,
laughing from behind a cloud?[^66]

**464.** On the lovely forehead shines a lovely tilaka,
and above it a row of curls;
in the middle of my heart it glitters
like a row of bees upon the moon.

**465.** That tilt of His chuda — Madana's snare —
where did He learn it?
I could not gaze on that face to my heart's fill —
this is the great pain in my heart.[^67]

**466.** How sweet are His words, one by one —
if only I could hold them in my hand!
If the Creator had made them so,
I would break them into pieces and eat them.

**467.** The curve of His arms, surpassing the trunk of a lordly elephant,
is tinged with vermilion at the tips;
the bird of the forest of youth is dying of thirst
and begs for the nectar of their touch.[^68]

**468.** He moves with a graceful, swaying gait, glancing with sidelong eyes,
like a maddened king of elephants.
Srinivasa Dasa says: that beauty cannot be grasped —
the Creator has fashioned an ocean of beauty.

**469.** In the surge of his love Srinivasa cannot keep his composure;
saying, "What sweet charm I have seen!" he weeps.

**470.** Sri Jiva Gosvami, calming him with great care,
overcome with affection, went to his own hut.

**471.** Srinivasa remained at his own lodging;
Narottama and Shyamananda went to their own lodging.

**472.** He spent the day absorbed in darshan everywhere,
but at night there is no end to his lament.

**473.** Raising both arms, he says again and again:
"Providence has deprived me of this happiness.

**474.** Sri Govinda, Gopinatha, Madanamohana —
will They again grant darshan to me, a fallen one?

**475.** Sri Radha-vinoda, Prabhu Radha-ramana —
shall I again see Prabhu Radha-Damodara?

**476.** Will Prabhu Sri Gopala Bhatta, bringing me to Vraja,
again give me the service of his lotus feet?

**477.** Gosvami Sri Lokanatha, the embodiment of compassion —
will he again show favour to me, a fallen one?

**478.** Will merciful Bhugarbha Gosvami, out of his mercy,
again bring me, a sinner, dragging me by the hair?

**479.** Gosvami Sri Raghunatha Dasa, the treasury of mercy —
will he again fulfil the desire of my heart?

**480.** Sri Jiva Gosvami is the life of the poor and the distressed;
shall I again see his holy feet?

**481.** Alas, alas, O dear ones of Prabhu! One as wicked as I —
will you bring him again to Vraja and keep him near you?"

**482.** Saying so much in this way, he can [speak] no more:
his throat is choked, he swims in the tears of his eyes.

**483.** The lament of Sri Narottama cannot be told:
on hearing it, wood and stone melt.

**484.** Shyamananda, utterly distraught at heart —
all the lament he makes, who can tell it?

**485.** No one can keep his composure;
in anxiety over the separation they stay awake through the night.

**486.** What agitation arose in Srinivasa's heart —
that only merciful Sri Govinda knows.

**487.** By the will of Sri Govinda-deva, at the end of the night,
Srinivasa fell into a light sleep.

**488.** In the guise of a dream Sri Govinda, from the temple,
with the gait of a king of elephants came before the Acharya.

**489.** He surpasses a mass of collyrium, a rain cloud, a sapphire;
to the radiance of His beauty millions of Madanas offer themselves in sacrifice.

**490.** His body is adorned with many jewelled ornaments,
on His head is a crown of peacock feathers, supremely beautiful.

**491.** Wondrous is the beauty of each of His limbs — what comparison is there for it?
The play of His long eyes maddens the world.

**492.** With a moon-face surpassing hundreds of thousands of moons,
smiling, He speaks to Srinivasa in great joy:

**493.** "O Srinivasa! Restrain your lament.
When I hear it, I do not know what becomes of My heart.

**494.** You are the embodiment of My prema — you do not know it.
I am always near you.

**495.** The desire of My heart I have in many ways
made manifest through Rupa and Sanatana.

**496.** Distributing the jewel-books through you,
I shall take away the sorrow of the living beings, giving them the wealth of prema.

**497.** Whoever comes and takes shelter of you,
him I shall certainly accept.

**498.** Fortunate people will become your disciples;
together with them all you will relish sankirtana.

**499.** Do not worry in your heart in any way:
from time to time you will be able to see Me like this."

**500.** Having said this and shown favour to Srinivasa,
He — how wondrous! — took the form of Sri Gaura.

**501.** Seeing this, Srinivasa cannot keep his composure:
he begs the Creator for hundreds of thousands of eyes.

**502.** Falling to the ground, he worships His holy feet;
Prabhu places His feet on Srinivasa's head.

**503.** Embracing him and giving him leave to go to Gauda,
He enters the temple, withdrawing the form of Gaura.

**504.** His heart distraught at no longer seeing Sri Govinda,
he wakes and sees that the night has come to dawn.

**505.** Srinivasa, profoundly grave, keeping his composure,
performed his morning duties and sat down in seclusion.

**506.** Sri Narottama came there,
and with him Shyamananda, discerning in every respect.

**507.** Srinivasa Acharya, taking these two with him,
went and met Sri Jiva Gosvami.

**508.** He, taking Srinivasa and all the others with him,
quickly came to the temple of Sri Govinda.

**509.** There all the mahantas arrived;
I shall tell their names for the sake of auspiciousness.

**510.** Gosvami Gopala Bhatta, exceedingly merciful,
Bhugarbha and Sri Lokanatha, abodes of virtues,

**511.** Sri Madhava, Sri Paramananda Bhattacharya,
Sri Madhu Pandita, whose character is wonderful,

**512.** Premi Krishnadasa, Krishnadasa Brahmachari,
Raghava Pandita, who is entitled to prema-bhakti,[^69]

**513.** Yadava Acharya, merciful Narayana,
Sri Pundarikaksha Gosani, Govinda, Ishana,

**514.** Sri Govinda, Vani Krishnadasa, most generous,[^70]
Sri Uddhava, who from time to time goes to Gauda,

**515.** Dvija Haridasa, Krishnadasa Kaviraja,
Sri Gopala Dasa, whose deeds are extraordinary.

**516.** All the Vaishnavas came — how many names shall I name?
The residents of Vraja came, abodes of bliss.

**517.** Sri Jiva Gosvami, Krishna Pandita and others joyfully
had the jewel-books brought before everyone.

**518.** Having obtained everyone's permission, at that very moment
he had the books loaded onto the cart.

**519.** He had the casket of books carefully placed;
all gave the word to set the cart going.

**520.** At an auspicious moment the carter set the cart going;
fortunate guards walk in front and behind.

**521.** One more man, competent in every way,
walks very carefully beside the cart.

**522.** In this way the cart goes along the road to Mathura;
for some distance all the Gosvamis go along with it.

**523.** Saying many things, with hearts utterly distraught,
they bade farewell to Srinivasa Acharya.

**524.** Srinivasa and the others, greatly distraught,
set out, bowing at the feet of all.

**525.** Sri Jiva Gosvami and a few other learned ones
went [with them] as far as Sri Mathura.

**526.** All the others went back to their own lodgings;
who will describe what became of them all in the separation?

**527.** Here, meanwhile, all arrived in Mathura;
the residents of Mathura were greatly delighted.

**528.** The festivity that took place that day in the city of Mathura
I cannot describe, for fear of lengthening the book.

**529.** Having spent day and night in the rasa of talks about Krishna,
rising at dawn, they set out from Mathura.

**530.** Sri Jiva Gosvami went along with them for some distance;
at the time of parting he floats on waves of sorrow.

**531.** Embracing Srinivasa Acharya Thakura,
he bathed him with the tears of both his eyes.

**532.** Entrusting both Narottama and Shyamananda [to him],
he bade farewell, greatly distraught.

**533.** Firmly embracing Sri Narottama,
he spoke in a way that cannot be described.

**534.** Lovingly entrusting Shyamananda
and embracing him, he could not remain steady.

**535.** Krishnadasa Kaviraja, Raghava Pandita,
Sri Gopala, Madhava and all the other Vaishnavas —

**536.** all lost their composure at the time of parting;
Srinivasa and the others were bathed in tears.

**537.** The mutual embraces, obeisances and so on, as they took place —
that is most wonderful; who could describe it, and how?

**538.** The householder Vaishnavas of Mathura, the virtuous people —
all of them wept bitterly.

**539.** Srinivasa Acharya and the others took leave of them all
as was fitting, weeping and weeping.

**540.** Sri Acharya, the best of the wise, took his leave;
all returned to their own homes.

**541.** Sri Jiva Gosvami and the others went to Vrindavana;
all of them constantly wish [the travellers] well.

**542.** Here, meanwhile, Srinivasa Acharya and the others, with great care,
set out for Gauda, taking the jewel-books.

**543.** Sri Srinivasa Acharya's journey to Sri Gauda —
whoever hears of it obtains the jewel of bhakti.

**544.** Meditating on the feet of Srinivasa Acharya,
Narahari Dasa tells the *Bhakti-ratnakara*.[^71]

*Thus in the Sri Bhakti-ratnakara ends the sixth wave, called "The Description of Srinivasa Acharya's Journey from Vrindavana to Gauda".*
