# Taranga 9. Srinivasa's Second Journey to Vrindavana and the Festivals at Katwa, Yajigrama and Shrikhanda

*King Vira Hambira and the Gosvamis' letters; Srinivasa teaches at Yajigrama; news of the departure of the Lord's associates; the journey to Vrindavana and back; Ramachandra; Vira Hambira and Kalachand; the list of the mahantas; the festival at Katwa at the place of the Lord's sannyasa; Virabhadra; Yadunandana; the festival at Shrikhanda with Raghunandana; sankirtana and Virabhadra's mercy*

**1.** Glory, glory to Gaurachandra, the son of Sri Shachi!
Glory to Nityananda, the son of Padmavati!

**2.** Glory to Sri Advaita, the son of Nabha Devi!
Glory to Gadadhara, the son of Ratnavati![^1]

**3.** Glory to Srivasa and the other devotees dear to the Lord!
Fulfill the desire of a fool such as I.

**4.** Glory, glory to the listeners, the abode of virtues!
Now listen with kind hearts to what I shall tell.

**5.** King Sri Vira Hambira, in Vana-Vishnupura,
longs with an anxious heart to see the Acharya.

**6.** The king constantly thinks thus within himself:
"In how many days will Prabhu come to Vishnupura?

**7.** I am utterly helpless; I have no one else;
without Prabhu, at every moment I see [only] darkness.

**8.** Who has not suffered sorrow from my deeds?
I caused pain to all the Gosvamis in Vrindavana.[^2]

**9.** I committed an offense such as no one commits —
will they all show mercy to me?"

**10.** Turning over many such thoughts in his mind, he keeps silent,
and with great effort he holds back the streams of tears from his eyes.

**11.** Seeing the king anxious, his ministers and friends
console [him] with very sweet words:

**12.** "Only a few days have passed since he went from here;
we think he will be somewhat delayed in coming.

**13.** Do not be anxious: [dear to him is] the sweetness of your devotion;
this good fame of yours has spread everywhere.[^3]

**14.** By his mercy [comes] the mercy of all;
have no doubt at all of this, Maharaja.

**15.** If you say that the Lord's dear ones who dwell in Vraja
will punish [you] — do not think so."

**16.** Just as they said this, two men from Vraja
arrived, bringing the Gosvamis' letter.

**17.** Seeing the two of them, the king was thrown into great agitation;
falling to the ground, he bowed down to them both.

**18.** Seeing such conduct, the two, as though stunned,
speak a few [words] to the king in sweet language:

**19.** "As everyone in Vrindavana praises you,
so [we] have seen it with our own eyes, and joy has grown in our hearts.

**20.** We have come bringing letters from all the Gosvamis:
this letter is for the Acharya, and this letter is for you."

**21.** Saying this, they gave the king the two letters.
Taking the letter, the king holds it to his eyes and his head.

**22.** In delight, praising his good fortune again and again,
he reads his own letter; streams of tears flow from his eyes.

**23.** The writing of Sri Jiva Gosani is exceedingly sweet:
the heart of whoever hears it loses all composure.

**24.** Having read the letter, the king says in great rapture:
"They have all become kind to a lowly one such as I!

**25.** Those devotees of the Lord do not see faults!"
He says many such things, and his tears cannot be checked.

**26.** Fortunate is he who sees the king's wondrous conduct!
The king showed the two of them the highest honor.

**27.** To send the Gosvamis' letter to Yajigrama,
he quickly wrote a letter with his own news.

**28.** He entrusted the two letters to two of his own men;
the two, coming to Yajigrama, gave [them] to the Acharya.

**29.** He reverently honored the Gosvamis' letter, [placing it] on his head;
as he read, streams of bliss flowed from both his eyes.

**30.** After some time Acharya Thakura grew calm;
then that man gave [him] the king's letter.

**31.** Having read the letter, the Acharya was glad at heart:
the letter expressed an intense longing to see him.

**32.** The Acharya quickly wrote a letter to the king;
in the letter he let him know that he would be somewhat delayed in coming.

**33.** He wrote in it all the other news [he wished],
and gave the letter into the hand of that man.

**34.** Taking the letter, the man went to Vana-Vishnupura;
reading the letter, the king felt great joy.

**35.** Here, meanwhile, Sri Acharya teaches his disciples;
proclaiming, "Bhakti is supreme above all," he roars like a lion.

**36.** The Acharya has one constant thought:
"Almost all the Lord's associates have passed from sight;

**37.** and of those who still remain, it is uncertain [how long they will stay]."
Reflecting thus, he is deeply troubled at heart.

**38.** His mind finds peace only in discussing the scriptures of bhakti.
The power of the Acharya's learning spreads through the world.

**39.** The learned men who come from many lands —
all of them he teaches, bestowing the jewel of bhakti.

**40.** To study the books of the Gosvamis,
one day two brahmanas residing in Kshetra came.[^4]

**41.** Meeting with those who had come earlier, [and] together with them,
they went into the presence of Acharya Thakura.

**42.** The two bowed down to the Acharya with devotion;
the Acharya, bowing [to them], embraced them both.

**43.** He asks the two for news of Shrikshetra.
The two say: "To tell it, there is no end to the sorrow.

**44.** The Lord's associates who were in Nilachala
are almost all passing from sight.

**45.** Shyamananda, full of prema, had gone there;
whoever saw his state cannot describe it.

**46.** He still obtained the darshana of some mahantas;
when they disappeared, he became as though dead.

**47.** Hearing that weeping, stones and trees split apart;
only by the Lord's will did life remain in him.

**48.** Some bhagavatas consoled him;
distraught with separation, he went to Vrindavana."

**49.** Hearing [this], the Acharya [sheds] streams of tears from both eyes;
whose heart would not melt to see that state?

**50.** The Acharya, consoling himself at once,
teaches the two of them the books of the Gosvamis.

**51.** A Vaishnava came from Navadvipa
and met the Acharya, greatly distressed.

**52.** Sri Acharya impatiently asks:
"Tell me, what is the news of Navadvipa?"

**53.** He says: "Shuklambara and other devotees
have passed from sight in these few days."

**54.** Just as he said this, someone came and said:
"Today Dasa Gadadhara has disappeared."

**55.** Hearing [this], Srinivasa Acharya cannot remain steady:
he fainted and fell to the ground.

**56.** Seeing that state of his, everyone was anxious;
by the Lord's will he regained a little consciousness.

**57.** Lamenting much, he weeps aloud;
a clamor of weeping rose in the Acharya's house.

**58.** Hearing that weeping, beasts and birds weep;
whoever saw [it] at that time is the witness to it.

**59.** Growing calm, the Acharya says to everyone:
"I must go soon to Vrindavana.

**60.** All of you will study the books;
Prabhu Rupa and Sanatana will reveal [to you] their meaning."

**61.** Saying this, he teaches the books to his disciples;
in this veiled way the Acharya gave all of them a blessing.[^5]

**62.** One day Sri Acharya reflects in his heart:
"Almost all have gone, leaving behind a wretched one such as I."

**63.** Just as he thought this, someone says aloud:
"Sri Thakura Narahari has passed from sight."

**64.** At the thunderbolt-blow of these words he cannot hold himself steady;
rolling on the ground, he weeps, saying, "What has happened!"

**65.** As he wept, the night came to its end;
"I will give up my life" — so he resolved firmly in his mind.

**66.** By the Lord's will he suddenly fell asleep;
in the guise of a dream the two at once appeared [before him].

**67.** Prabhu Dasa Gadadhara and Prabhu Narahari,
taking the Acharya by the hand, console [him]:

**68.** "What you have resolved in your mind is not right.
We are always present beside you."

**69.** Saying this and embracing Srinivasa,
in an outpouring of affection the two passed from sight.

**70.** When the two had passed from sight, his sorrow was boundless;
waking, Srinivasa sees that the night is at its end.

**71.** Having told Ramachandra something in private — I do not know what —
he set out for Vrindavana at daybreak.

**72.** Very quickly he entered the city of Mathura;
at the Sri Vishrama ghat he bathed in the Yamuna.

**73.** There a Mathura brahmana, from afar
seeing Srinivasa, was overwhelmed with affection.

**74.** "Having gone to Gauda, why has he come back so soon?" —
reflecting thus, he grew anxious in his mind.

**75.** Coming near, he asked for news;
Srinivasa, offering obeisances, told him.

**76.** When Srinivasa asked whether all was well in Vraja,
the Mathura brahmana, heaving a long sigh, says:

**77.** "In the month of Magha you have come here;
had you come some ten days earlier, you would have had [his] darshana.[^6]

**78.** On the Ekadashi of the dark fortnight of Magha — how astonishing! —
the brahmana Haridasa Acharya disappeared."

**79.** Hearing [this], Srinivasa floats in streams of tears;
saying, "I did not get to see [him]!" he rolls on the ground.

**80.** Seeing Srinivasa's state, the brahmana, greatly steadfast,
calmed Srinivasa in many ways.

**81.** From there Srinivasa, going to Vrindavana,
had darshan of the feet of all the Gosvamis.

**82.** That day was the tithi of Vasanta-panchami;[^7]
everyone was celebrating in the temple of Sri Govinda.

**83.** Sri Gopala Bhatta, Sri Bhugarbha, Lokanatha,
Sri Jiva Gosvami and others, together with their circle of dear ones.

**84.** Suddenly seeing Srinivasa, all of them,
overcome with affection, caught hold of him and embraced him.

**85.** Falling to the ground, Srinivasa bows down;
seeing that wonderful behaviour, everyone was filled with joy.

**86.** Everyone asked Srinivasa about his welfare;
Srinivasa told everything from beginning to end.

**87.** Hearing of the disappearance of Gaurachandra's dear devotees,
everyone weeps in distress.

**88.** Someone says, "When I saw Srinivasa, I thought:
why has he come back so soon?

**89.** He has met with terrible grief — that is the reason for his coming."
Saying many such things, everyone consoles [him].

**90.** When the disappearance of Haridasa Acharya was made known,
what became of everyone — who can tell?

**91.** Having calmed Srinivasa, everyone grew calm [themselves]
and saw the rajabhoga arati of Govinda.[^8]

**92.** Srinivasa, having darshan of Radha-Govinda,
was overwhelmed with prema beyond description.

**93.** All the Gosvamis, taking dear Srinivasa with them,
partook of Sri maha-prasada with care.

**94.** Everyone went to their own lodgings;
Sri Jiva Gosvami took Srinivasa with him.

**95.** At that time Shyamananda came from Kshetra;
falling to the ground, he bows to the Gosvami.

**96.** Overcome with affection, the Gosvami embraced him
and spoke sweet words full of nectar.

**97.** Who can tell what affection he had for Shyamananda?
He acted in such a way that the other's heart grew calm.

**98.** Having told him of Srinivasa Acharya's arrival,
he stayed for some time, sitting in seclusion.

**99.** Shyamananda, seeing Acharya Srinivasa,
fell to the ground and bowed with joy in his heart.

**100.** Srinivasa, behaving as was fitting,
took Shyamananda by the hand and seated him beside himself.

**101.** Having told each other all their news,
they cannot hold back their tears — streams flow from their eyes.

**102.** Remembering the Gosvami's words of consolation,
after a while the two of them grew calm.

**103.** Leaving Shyamananda right there, the Acharya
went in haste to bathe in the Sri Yamuna.

**104.** Having bathed and reported to Jiva Gosvami,
he went and bowed at the feet of Sri Bhatta Gosvami.

**105.** In this way, having been everywhere,
he studies the books with Sri Jiva.

**106.** Sri Jiva Gosvami, his heart greatly pleased,
seeing the Acharya's surpassing power of learning,

**107.** recited to him the beginning of the *Sri Gopala-champu*
and showed him the other books he had written.

**108.** The Acharya's heart was filled with great joy;
he studies the Gosvamis' books unceasingly.

**109.** Thus Srinivasa Acharya lives in Vrindavana;
in Gauda everyone is in anguish without the Acharya.

**110.** One day in Shrikhanda Sri Raghunandana
speaks very sweet words to Ramachandra:

**111.** "Everything has become empty — what more is there to say?
Go quickly to Vrindavana — this is your task."

**112.** Saying this, he explained the way to him;
that very moment Ramachandra came to Yajigrama.

**113.** There everyone says to Ramachandra again and again,
"Without Sri Acharya everything has become darkness.

**114.** Do not delay, go quickly to Vrindavana;
bring the Acharya back and preserve everyone's life."

**115.** Ramachandra, receiving everyone's permission,
returned to his own home in great joy.

**116.** Ramachandra thinks thus within himself:
"In how many days will I have the company of Sri Narottama?

**117.** When I have his company, all sorrow will go;
without seeing him no happiness will arise in my heart.

**118.** Without the Prabhu I shall not be able to stay at home;
there he will come and go with his associates.[^9]

**119.** I shall live in such a place where there is happiness in every way."
Thinking thus, he settled on a place in his mind.

**120.** Who has the power to understand the heart of a great soul?
He revealed his intention to no one.

**121.** His own younger brother Sri Govinda is learned,
charmingly skilful in affairs, foremost in every respect.

**122.** With great affection he tells him in private,
"When the night gives way to dawn, I shall go to Sri Vrindavana.

**123.** Now the circumstances of living here are not good;
great anxiety constantly arises in my heart.

**124.** There is a little land, [held] for many years;[^10]
what troubles it brings now — you see with your own eyes.

**125.** Quickly leaving this dwelling and all the rest
and living elsewhere without hindrance is best of all.

**126.** For this there is a place between the Ganga and the Padmavati —
a holy land, a village named Teliya Budhari.[^11]

**127.** A very large village, the home of cultured people;
if it pleases you, it is a fitting place to live.

**128.** Our revered maternal grandfather used to go there in former times;[^12]
everyone knows [him], he is famous everywhere.

**129.** If we live there, many will be happy."
Govinda says, "This is surely what must be done."

**130.** Ramachandra rejoiced at Govinda's words
and gave him much instruction in the way of the highest goal.

**131.** Ramachandra, at dawn, taking leave of his brother,
set out for Vrindavana.

**132.** The Acharya had gone at the end of the month of Margashirsha;[^13]
Ramachandra set out at the end of Pausha.

**133.** Sri Govinda, staying two or four days,
moved from Kumara-nagara to Teliya.

**134.** All the villagers of Teliya Budhari and other places —
who can tell how great their joy was?

**135.** The fortunate gentlefolk came and met him;
all together they made him a wonderful dwelling.

**136.** All are very happy with Govinda's good qualities;
Govinda found happiness in everyone's affection.

**137.** Thus he lives, and he has only one thought:
"When shall I become a servant of Sri Acharya's feet?

**138.** When will Sri Acharya Prabhu give me the mantra of initiation,
deliver this fallen one and make me his own?"

**139.** Thus Govinda laments constantly.
Here I shall tell of Govinda's former life.

**140.** He lived in Kumara-nagara, very pure in conduct;
apart from Bhagavati he knew nothing else.[^14]

**141.** In songs and verses he describes Bhagavati;
hearing this, his companions, worshippers of Shakti, rejoice.

**142.** How he came to be so towards Bhagavati —
the reason for this I shall now tell briefly.

**143.** His maternal grandfather Damodara was a worshipper of Shakti;
Bhagavati was always submissive to him.

**144.** Damodara Kaviraja was renowned everywhere;
his daughter was Sunanda, whose son was Govinda.

**145.** In his mother's womb Govinda would not be born;
because of this the mother's suffering became extreme.

**146.** The maidservant quickly told the Kaviraja;
at that time the Kaviraja was worshipping Bhagavati.

**147.** Without a word, by gestures of his eyes and hands,
he shows the maidservant the yantra of Sri Durga-devi:[^15]

**148.** "Take this and quickly let her see it:
the birth will take place and the pain will be removed."

**149.** What he said by gestures she did not understand:
quickly washing the yantra, she gave [the mother] the water to drink.

**150.** A son was born, supremely beautiful;
day by day he grew like the moon.

**151.** His birth came about through [his mother's] drinking the water from Bhagavati's yantra —
this is one reason; everyone knows it.

**152.** Soon his father disappeared [from the world], and he was left without his company;[^16]
what deeds the elders speak of, he did not understand.

**153.** From birth he lived in his maternal grandfather's house;
being under his influence — this is the other one.

**154.** The scriptures speak of company that is high, middling and low:
as is the company one keeps, so one becomes.

**155.** His devotion to Bhagavati [arose] in these two ways;
he instructs everyone to worship Bhagavati.

**156.** "Without Bhagavati no undertaking succeeds" —
such is the instruction Govinda gives.

**157.** When Ramachandra became a disciple of Sri Acharya,
Govinda, sitting alone, reflected in his heart:

**158.** "If one worships the lotus feet of Bhagavati,
will there not be release from the bonds of worldly existence and the rest?"

**159.** At that moment Bhagavati, remaining unseen, says,
"Unless one worships Krishna, no one's misfortune is dispelled."

**160.** Hearing these words, he felt great sorrow in his heart;
"I will worship the lotus feet of Sri Krishna," he firmly resolved.

**161.** "By all means I shall become a disciple of Acharya Prabhu;
only then will the pain of my heart be dispelled."

**162.** Having resolved thus, as soon as he set out for Yajigrama,
he heard that Sri Acharya had gone to Vrindavana.

**163.** Govinda's heart was filled with exceeding sorrow;
distraught, he reflects within himself:

**164.** "The Vaishnavas, too, cared for my welfare:
they told me about my father — I did not listen.

**165.** My father, Chiranjiva Sena, was a learned man,
a devotee of Chaitanyachandra, a treasury of virtues.

**166.** Being the son of such a man, I have gone to dust and ashes;
this is only the fault of my karma — what can I say, and to whom?

**167.** There is no one else in the world as fallen as I;
what I set my mind on did not come to pass for me.

**168.** If I had ever once seen the Acharya,
would my wicked mind not have turned?

**169.** My elder brother, at the sight of Acharya Prabhu,
turned his mind, and became steadfast in devotion to those feet.

**170.** To him Sri Acharya Prabhu showed favor;
through the fault of my karma I did not get to see him.

**171.** What shall I do? Where shall I go? What will become of me?"
Saying this, he weeps; streams of tears flow from his eyes.

**172.** At that moment a divine voice sounded from the sky:
"What is desired will be fulfilled within a few days."

**173.** From that day his love and his mind turned to Krishna;
seeing such conduct, Ramachandra rejoiced greatly.

**174.** Thus I have told of Govinda's former life;
by hearing all this, Krishnachandra is pleased.

**175.** Govinda lived in the village of Teliya Budhari;
in Teliya he was very fond of a secluded spot.

**176.** West of Budhari is a place named Sri Pashchimpara;
there he lived, carrying on all his affairs — that too is a lovely place.[^17]

**177.** His residence at Budhari is famous, known everywhere.
Without his elder brother Govinda has no peace.

**178.** What more is there to say? Here, very eager,
Ramachandra went in haste to Vrindavana.

**179.** Seeing Ramachandra, people come running;
all say, "We have never seen anyone like this!

**180.** He has come from the land of Gauda;
we do not know which king's son he is."

**181.** Someone says, "Oh, this is no human being at all;
he is some god — so it seems to me."

**182.** Someone went and said before Jiva Gosani,
"A wondrous person has come from Gauda.

**183.** His whole body is beautiful, its luster surpasses gold;
seeing him, I know not what happens to my heart.

**184.** He walks very slowly, gazing charmingly in all directions;
abundant thrills of ecstasy shine all over his body.

**185.** Seeing the beauty of Vrindavana — what feeling is in his heart! —
wondrous tears stream from his two long eyes."

**186.** Hearing this, Sri Jiva asked the Acharya;
the Acharya says, "Ramachandra must have come."

**187.** Earlier Sri Acharya had related
to the Gosvami an account of Ramachandra.

**188.** Sri Jiva Gosvami says, "Where is Ramachandra?"
He instructs the people, "Bring him here quickly."

**189.** Even as he said this, Ramachandra arrived there
and bowed at the feet of Sri Acharya and the Gosvami.

**190.** Both of them embraced Ramachandra again and again,
seated him near them and ask for news.

**191.** Ramachandra first of all related
what Sri Raghunandana, the resident of Khanda, had said.

**192.** And what each Vaishnava had asked him to say —
telling that, he made known the doings of them all.

**193.** As he told how the study of the books and all else went on,
he lost his composure, and with effort regained it.

**194.** How he came to Vraja by way of the holy places Gaya, Kashi, Ayodhya and Prayaga —
this he told in detail.

**195.** From Ramachandra's words Sri Jiva Gosvami
learned that great sorrow had spread there.

**196.** "I must send Srinivasa to Gauda quickly" —
thinking thus, he was overwhelmed with affection.

**197.** Speaking many sweet words to Ramachandra,
he took him for the darshan of Radha-Damodara.[^18]

**198.** Ramachandra, beholding Radha-Damodara,
fell to the ground in obeisance and is bathed in tears.

**199.** Seeing there the samadhi of Sri Rupa Gosani,
not a trace of composure remained in him; he rolls on the ground.

**200.** Crying, "Ha, ha, Prabhu Rupa!" he weeps;
Sri Jiva, taking him in his arms, consoles him in many ways.

**201.** After some time Ramachandra grew calm;
such absorption in prema comes over him at the darshan in every place.

**202.** Sri Govinda, Gopinatha, Madanamohana,
Radha-Damodara and Sri Radha-ramana —

**203.** from the darshan of all of Them his happiness knew no end.
He saw the samadhi of Sanatana Gosvami.

**204.** Seeing the samadhi, he was greatly distraught.
He saw the samadhi of Kashishvara Pandita.

**205.** Beholding the samadhi of Raghunatha Bhatta —
what can I say of how his heart was torn apart!

**206.** Sri Gopala Bhatta, the merciful Lokanatha,
Sri Bhugarbha and others showed him exceeding mercy.

**207.** "Ramachandra has come" — this spread everywhere;
in whose heart did the longing to see him not arise?

**208.** Ramachandra quickly went to Arit-grama;
seeing Radha-kunda and Shyama-kunda, he bathed in them.

**209.** He bowed to Raghunatha Dasa Gosvami;
he embraced him with affection and bathes him in tears.

**210.** Hearing the sweet poetry of Sri Ramachandra,
he showed him a mercy that is beyond words.

**211.** With Krishnadasa Kaviraja and all the others
he had a wondrous meeting.

**212.** He had darshan of Mount Govardhana;
wandering through the twelve forests, he was overjoyed.

**213.** In Vrindavana, Sri Bhatta Gosvami and all the others —
all praise Ramachandra without cease.

**214.** Hearing Ramachandra's wondrous poetry,
all agreed upon the title "Kaviraja" for him.[^19]

**215.** What can be said of the virtues of Sri Ramachandra,
whose fame for steadfast devotion to his chosen one is sung by all people!

**216.** Ramachandra, together with the Acharya, his own chosen one,
serves Bhatta Gosvami in many ways.

**217.** How the two of them delighted in Vrindavana
is not described, for fear of prolixity.

**218.** Sri Jiva Gosani's happiness grows continually
as he sees the charming conduct of guru and disciple.

**219.** He made known to the Acharya the journey to Sri Gauda;
the Acharya quickly took his leave everywhere.

**220.** On the full-moon day of Vaishakha, an auspicious lunar day,
there was the simhasana-yatra of Radha-ramana.[^20]

**221.** The great festival at Bhatta Gosvami's residence
Srinivasa beheld with a jubilant heart.

**222.** That very day Sri Jiva Gosvami, overcome with affection,
made his dear Srinivasa ready for the journey to Gauda.

**223.** On the day after the full moon Sri Jiva Gosani
entrusted Shyamananda to the Acharya.

**224.** The books he had earlier corrected
he sent along with him, arranging men to accompany them.

**225.** All the Gosvamis, in the temple of Govinda,
were distraught as they bade them farewell.

**226.** Srinivasa, bowing at the feet of all,
sets out, gazing at the moonlike face of Govinda.

**227.** Ramachandra and Shyamananda, with aching hearts,
bow again and again to all the Gosvamis.

**228.** Sri Jiva, distraught, walks some distance with them;
again and again Acharya Thakura holds him back.

**229.** Having bidden farewell, all went back to their dwellings;
the Acharya hastened on by way of Mathura.

**230.** After some days, as he was entering Vana-Vishnupura,
the king came forward to meet him, with great joy in his heart.

**231.** Beholding the lotus feet of Acharya Prabhu,
he offers obeisance, prostrating himself on the ground.

**232.** The Acharya placed his feet on the king's head
and, lifting him by the arms, embraced him.

**233.** Ramachandra and Shyamananda are abodes of virtue;
the Acharya introduced the two of them.

**234.** King Vira Hambira, falling to the ground,
bowed at the feet of both and is bathed in tears.

**235.** The king says joyfully, "What fortune is mine!
By the Prabhu's mercy I have attained the feet of these two."

**236.** The two embraced Vira Hambira;
the joy they felt cannot be described.

**237.** The royal ministers and others who had come with the king —
all of them were plunged in an ocean of bliss.

**238.** The king took the Prabhu to his residence;
the fortunate residents gaze at him to their eyes' content.

**239.** "Acharya Thakura has come to Vana-Vishnupura!" —
this spread everywhere from person to person.

**240.** In Vana-Vishnupura Sri Acharya with his companions
delights in sankirtana day and night.

**241.** Extraordinary are the ways of Sri Acharya Thakura;
who can understand the love that is in his heart?

**242.** Keeping Shyamananda in Vishnupura for some ten days,
with an anxious heart he sends him off to Utkala.

**243.** Shyamananda is to go to the land of Utkala;
at this the king, losing his composure, thinks in his heart:

**244.** "Who has the power to understand the doings of a great soul?
Wandering everywhere, he delivers living beings.

**245.** He has not stayed here even a few days;
I have not done such pious deeds that I should see him again."

**246.** Thinking thus, he carefully gathered many goods
and brought them before Sri Acharya Prabhu.

**247.** Seeing this, the Acharya was pleased at heart;
the porters, taking the provisions, set out ahead.

**248.** Shyamananda did what pleased the king's heart;
what can others understand of Shyamananda's ways?

**249.** Acharya Thakura, unable to restrain himself,
embraces Shyamananda and says many things to him.

**250.** Shyamananda, wet with the Acharya's tears,
falls to the ground and bows to the Acharya.

**251.** Holding Shyamananda's hand, Acharya Thakura,
overwhelmed with affection, accompanies him for some distance.

**252.** Shyamananda, saying much to Acharya Thakura,
turned him back; the Acharya went to his lodging.

**253.** How he took leave of Ramachandra Kaviraja
and all the others — who knows how to describe it?

**254.** What the king humbly said at the time of parting
I have not described, for fear of making the book too long.

**255.** Shyamananda leaves in great distress;
all the people weep, gazing down that road.

**256.** From Vana-Vishnupura, with many people,
Shyamananda reached Utkala in a few days.

**257.** His arrival became known everywhere;
people run from all sides to see him.

**258.** Sri Rasikananda and others were overjoyed;[^21]
Shyamananda stayed in Nrisimhapura.

**259.** He sent a letter with news to Vishnupura;
reading the letter, Acharya Thakura was glad.

**260.** The Acharya stayed in Vishnupura two months
and fulfilled the desires of many people.

**261.** Seeing the king's mastery of the books of bhakti,
the Acharya was amazed in his mind.

**262.** Recalling what he had said before,[^22]
he joyfully gave [the king] initiation into the mantra of Radha and Krishna.

**263.** He carefully explained to him the meaning of the Sri Kama-gayatri[^23]
and had him take up a fixed rule of Harinama japa.

**264.** He entrusted him to dear Ramachandra Kaviraja
and said, "You will learn the particulars from him."

**265.** Seeing the king's zeal, he says again and again:
"Sri Jiva Gosvami is pleased with you:

**266.** he has given you the name 'Sri Chaitanya Dasa.'"
Hearing this, streams of tears flow from the king's eyes.

**267.** His whole body thrills; he cannot hold his composure;
falling to the ground, he bows at the master's feet.

**268.** With folded hands he says again and again:
"By your grace everything of mine has borne fruit."

**269.** Standing beside the master, he says many such things;
my mouth cannot bring itself to tell them all.

**270.** King Vira Hambira's queen, Sulakshana,[^24]
made many entreaties to Acharya Prabhu.

**271.** The Acharya, pleased, gave [her] the initiation mantra;
receiving the mantra of the Divine Couple, the queen rejoiced.

**272.** Sri Dhari Hambira, the king's worthy son —
the merciful Sri Acharya made him a disciple.

**273.** Vira Hambira felt supreme joy;
he inaugurated the service of Sri Kalachand.[^25]

**274.** Sri Acharya Prabhu performs His abhisheka;
the fortunate people behold much wonder.

**275.** Someone says, "How enchanting Kalachand is!
Is this the prince of Vraja appeared in person?"

**276.** Someone says, "There is no limit to the king's fortune:
such a Deity I have seen nowhere."

**277.** Just as the king's heart had wished, so [He] became;
seeing Kalachand's beauty, who was not enchanted?

**278.** So they say many things, gazing at Kalachand;
who else could describe the festival of the abhisheka?

**279.** Sri Acharya Prabhu, showing mercy to the king,
entrusted him to the two feet of Sri Kalachand.

**280.** Besides the Acharya, the king knows nothing else;
the Acharya's lotus feet are the king's all.

**281.** His heart always overflows at the Acharya's virtues;
even in dreams the king sings the Acharya's virtues.

**282.** One day in a dream he composed songs;
the queen, in great joy, heard some of them.

**283.** When the king awoke and sat up, the queen humbly says:
"What two wondrous songs you composed in your dream!

**284.** I am afraid even to say it, yet if I do not, my heart pines;
be gracious and let this maidservant hear [them]."

**285.** The king, showing great humility, in a soft voice,
recites the sweetest songs, absorbed in prema.

**286.** *Raga Kamoda:*
My master Srinivasa, you have fulfilled my heart's hope;
besides you I have no other refuge.
I was a worm in worldly pleasures — how very sweet they tasted to me!
You dispelled my kingly pride.

**287.** I was drinking poison — and that was turned about;[^26]
you showed me a stream of nectar.
My mind keeps crying, "Let me drink, let me drink!" — everything in me is stirred;
all this is your doing.

**288.** Radha's feet are a treasury of nectar; to those feet you made [me] a maidservant;
you bound my heart to the feet of Gora.
Together with Sri Radhika's companions you showed me the kunja house;
you made known to me the ways of love of the Two.

**289.** I go to the bank of the Yamuna — on the shore the sakhis rush to and fro;
Radha and Kanu sport in happiness.
The heart of this Vira Hambira ever meditates on Vrajapura,
where bees fly by the hundreds of thousands.

**290.** *Raga Kamoda:*
Listen, dear friend of my heart: that Dark One with lotus eyes —[^27]
what He has done, I do not know at all.
How my heart feels! Everything seems unsettling;
by loving, I have lost my life.

**291.** Having heard [of Him], I saw the Dark One; seeing Him, I felt a burning,
and I find no water to quench it.
I brought aguru and sandal, mixed them and smeared my body —
it does not quench the fire in my heart.

**292.** Whenever I sit down, [something] comes and raises me
and leads me to the bank of the Yamuna.
Meaning to do one thing, I do another; I am forever pining away;
not for a moment do I stay still.

**293.** My mother-in-law and sister-in-law always take me for a thief;
the master of the house does not even turn to look.
The heart of this Vira Hambira, devoted to Srinivasa,
has sunk at the feet of Kalachand.

**294.** Hearing the songs, how much wells up in the queen's heart!
She cannot keep her composure; streams flow from both eyes.

**295.** At the king's feet she makes many entreaties;
overwhelmed, the queen is not aware of herself.

**296.** The king was drenched with his own tears;
becoming calm himself, he calmed the queen.

**297.** From time to time so many waves rise in the two of them!
Have I such power as to describe that prema?

**298.** The songs he composed under the name Sri Chaitanya Dasa
I have not presented, for fear of length.[^28]

**299.** Seeing the wonderful conduct of the king with his family,
Acharya Thakura with his companions is overjoyed.

**300.** Thus in Vana-Vishnupura Acharya Thakura,
making many disciples, distributes bhakti in abundance.

**301.** The deeds of all those disciples are most wonderful;
something of them will become known in the enumeration of the branches.

**302.** Some people made great efforts to become [his] disciples;
not doing it himself, he had [their initiation] done by another.

**303.** Harinarayana, the king of Shikhara-bhumi,[^29]
wished to become a disciple at the Acharya's hands.

**304.** He was to become a disciple in the Sri Rama mantra:
his natural love was for Sri Ramachandra.

**305.** Seeing Harinarayana's wonderful zeal,
Srinivasa Acharya was overjoyed.

**306.** To fulfil his desire, he himself
took pains — who can know [the measure of] his grace?

**307.** In Ranga-kshetra there was a son of Trimalla Bhatta;[^30]
by letter [the Acharya] very quickly had him come.

**308.** He came to Panchakuta and, his heart full of affection,
made Harinarayana a disciple with the Rama mantra.

**309.** Showing grace to Harinarayana,
he entrusted him to Srinivasa Acharya.

**310.** Acharya Thakura made known [to him] all truths;
what can be said of the king's most sweet character!

**311.** One day Acharya Thakura is sitting with everyone
in conversation about Krishna.

**312.** At that time a man came from Yajigrama;
handing over a letter of news, he bows to the ground.

**313.** After asking that man about his welfare,
[the Acharya] read the letter — and the Acharya's heart lost its composure.

**314.** The letter plainly said: "Come quickly:
the residents of Khanda and others are deeply anxious."

**315.** Everyone heard such a letter;
Vira Hambira's heart became distressed.

**316.** Seeing the king distressed, the Acharya says:
"I shall go to Kheturi by way of Khanda and Yajigrama,

**317.** and very soon I shall come back to Vishnupura."
The king says, "Be merciful and take me with you."

**318.** Sri Acharya, knowing the king's state of mind,
speaks to the king in the sweetest words:

**319.** "Do not be anxious; now steady your mind:
soon you will have the darshan of Sri Narottama.

**320.** When he comes to Yajigrama, I shall send [you] a letter;
if you go there, many things will be accomplished at once."

**321.** Hearing this, the king rejoiced together with his family;
all learned that the departure would be at daybreak.

**322.** With his companions, Sri Acharya, at daybreak,
sets out from Vishnupura for Yajigrama.

**323.** Countless people came and had [his] darshan;
the king carefully sent many provisions along with him.

**324.** Having gone some distance together with Sri Acharya Prabhu,
[the king] took his leave and returned to Vishnupura.

**325.** The king, with his family, thinks within himself:
"After how many days will I again have the darshan of Prabhu?"

**326.** Acharya Thakura, having bid farewell to the king,
quickly came with his companions to Yajigrama.

**327.** The villagers, seeing Acharya Thakura,
attained the highest bliss; their sorrow went far away.

**328.** The Acharya has come to Yajigrama —
this news spread everywhere from people's mouths.

**329.** From Yajigrama the Acharya, the best of the wise,
quickly went to Shrikhanda — who can understand [his] heart?

**330.** Bowing to Gaurachandra in the courtyard of Gauranga,[^31]
he cannot hold back the tears in his two long eyes.

**331.** Sri Raghunandana, seeing Srinivasa,[^32]
cannot keep his composure: his heart overflows with affection.

**332.** Spreading out both arms, he embraced [him]
and cannot let go — for a long time he holds him to his chest.

**333.** Srinivasa wants to fall to the ground and bow down,
but that did not happen: he was held fast in the embrace.

**334.** What could others understand of its depth? Nothing like it can be:
Sri Raghunandana seemed to have regained his life.

**335.** He asks about the welfare of the devotees living in Vraja;
Srinivasa, deeply moved, tells him:

**336.** "In separation from the Lord, those dear to the Lord
are, day by day, almost [all] disappearing from sight.

**337.** The state of those who still remain cannot be told:
that they are still here is only by the Lord's will.

**338.** I came back from Vraja after [staying there only] a few days;
about this a doubt arose — knowing that, I have told [you everything]."[^33]

**339.** Hearing of the disappearance of all the mahantas,
[he] fainted; from his eyes flowed streams like rivers.

**340.** Hearing this, Raghunandana says again and again:
"Day by day the earth is growing dark.

**341.** Prabhu Narahari, together with his dear companions,[^34]
has left me, to make me suffer sorrow.

**342.** For what joy does life still remain in [my] body?" —
saying much like this, Sri Raghunandana weeps.

**343.** Remembering the mercy of Prabhu Narahari,
Srinivasa weeps, rolling on the ground.

**344.** Who could keep composure at the weeping of these two?
A loud wailing arose in the village of Shrikhanda.

**345.** At that weeping, the beasts and birds of the forest weep;
whoever saw [it] at that time is witness to it.

**346.** Sri Raghunandana, becoming calm, calmed Srinivasa
and spoke much [to him] in soft words.

**347.** Keeping [him] for some time, he sent [him] to Yajigrama
and told him to go to Sri Kantaka-nagara.

**348.** Sri Acharya, coming quickly to Yajigrama,
went to Kantaka-nagara with an agitated heart.

**349.** There, where Gaurachandra accepted sannyasa,
what became of him — that cannot be described.

**350.** Seeing Sri Gauranga, he floats in tears,
and bows again and again, falling to the ground.

**351.** The devotees who were there, overcome with affection,
beside themselves, met Srinivasa.

**352.** Sri Yadunandana Chakravarti, the best of the wise,
whose worshipful lord is Prabhu Dasa Gadadhara,[^35]

**353.** his heart burned by grief at the concealment of his worshipful [lord],
lost his composure on seeing the Acharya.

**354.** Srinivasa Acharya, seeing his state,
cannot calm himself; streams of tears flow from his eyes.

**355.** Singing the qualities of Prabhu Gadadhara,
both weep aloud; everyone weeps.

**356.** Hearing that weeping, even stone melts;
how many waves of sorrow surge in the heart!

**357.** By the will of Sri Gaurachandra, after some time,
all, becoming calm, sit in the courtyard of Gauranga.

**358.** They ask the Acharya about the journey to Vrindavana and the rest;
he told them all of it in sweet words.

**359.** Sri Yadunandana says to the Acharya:
"It has been a year since [your] going to Vraja and coming back.

**360.** In the cruel grief of separation you went to Vrindavana
and came back soon — this has comforted [my] heart.

**361.** Look, here is the seat of Prabhu Gadadhara;
in this secluded [place] you had his darshan.

**362.** What shall I describe? On the day of krishna-ashtami in the month of Kartika[^36]
my Prabhu disappeared from sight here, in this very place.

**363.** To observe that lunar day,
I have prepared the provisions — look, here in the storeroom.

**364.** Letters of invitation have been sent everywhere,
and this place has been made ready as the lodging of the mahantas.

**365.** Go to Yajigrama and come back here quickly;
staying ten days, you will accomplish everything [with us]."

**366.** As he said much like this to the Acharya,
tears streamed from his eyes — he could not hold them back.

**367.** Acharya Thakura, seeing such a state [in him],
goes to Yajigrama, wet with tears.

**368.** Reaching the village, through people who lived in Vishnupura
he sent the king a letter with news.

**369.** Going quickly to Shrikhanda, to Sri Raghunandana
he told in private of the great festival.

**370.** Hearing this, Thakura, with a deeply agitated heart,
says something very softly to his dear Srinivasa:

**371.** "When Sri Dasa Gadadhara concealed himself in Kartika,
Prabhu Narahari wasted away moment by moment.

**372.** Who can understand the pain of his heart?
From that day he spoke with no one.

**373.** He was constantly bathed in the streams from his two eyes;
what shall I say of that? You saw it yourself.

**374.** In the month of Margashirsha, on the ekadashi of the dark fortnight,[^37]
he suddenly disappeared from sight here.

**375.** To observe that lunar day,
all the provisions have been prepared — look in the storeroom.

**376.** To the associates of Prabhu Nityananda, Advaita and Chaitanya
I have sent letters of invitation to many places.

**377.** The son of Prabhu Nityananda will come;
the son of Prabhu Advaita will also go [there].[^38]

**378.** Tomorrow at daybreak, [passing] through Yajigrama,
we shall go together to Kantaka-nagara.

**379.** The dear associates of Sri Prabhu will come there;
seeing them, the eyes and heart will be comforted.

**380.** When the great festival is completed, taking everyone with me,
I shall come to Shrikhanda, having stayed at Yajigrama."

**381.** Hearing this, Srinivasa was greatly delighted;
taking his leave, he quickly came back to Yajigrama.

**382.** To Ramachandra Kaviraja and his other dear companions
he told everything, sitting [with them] in private.

**383.** Hearing it, all at once settled on lodgings
and set about preparing the provisions.

**384.** The associates of Sri Chaitanya will come here —
all the residents of Yajigrama heard this news.

**385.** The hearts of all were filled with great joy;
each, together with the others, does the work proper to him.

**386.** Acharya Thakura, joyful, on the next day,
intending to go to Kantaka-nagara,

**387.** coming out of the house with his companions,
watches the road from Shrikhanda.

**388.** Sri Raghunandana, with his companions, from Khanda
came to Yajigrama at daybreak.

**389.** Staying for some time in Sri Acharya's house,
he went with the Acharya and the others to Kantaka-nagara.

**390.** All the mahantas are making their way to Kantaka-nagara;
people run to see, in great joy.

**391.** Which mahantas came from where —
for fear of making the book too long, I cannot tell in detail.

**392.** I shall tell only the names, with a heart full of jubilation —
names by hearing which the jewel of bhakti is attained.[^39]

**393.** Shripati, dear to the Lord, Shrinidhi, Vidyananda,
Vaninatha Vasu, Ramadasa, Kavichandra,[^40]

**394.** Purushottama, Sanjaya, Sri Chandrashekhara,
Sri Madhava Acharya, Shashthidhara the kirtan singer,

**395.** Sri Kamalakanta, Vaninatha, the best of brahmanas,
Vishnudasa, Nandana Pandita, Purandara,

**396.** Sri Chaitanyadasa, Karnapura, full of prema,
Sri Janakinatha, the brahmana, the abode of virtues,

**397.** Sri Gopala Acharya, and Gopala Dasa,
Murari Chaitanyadasa, supremely generous,

**398.** Raghunatha Vaidya, Upadhyaya Narayana,
Balarama Dasa, and Dasa Sanatana,

**399.** the brahmana Krishnadasa, Sri Nakadi, Manohara,
Hariharananda, Sri Madhava, Mahidhara,

**400.** Ramachandra Kaviraja, Vasanta, Lavani,[^41]
Sri Kanu Thakura, Sri Gokula, the jewel of virtues,

**401.** Sri Madhava Acharya, Rama Sena, Damodara,[^42]
Jnanadasa, Gopala the dancer, Pitambara,

**402.** Kumuda, Gauranga Dasa — the life of the suffering,
Nrisimha, Chaitanyadasa, Dasa Vrindavana,

**403.** Vanamali Dasa, Bholanatha, Sri Vijaya,
Sri Hridayananda Sena — an abode of virtues,

**404.** Lokanatha Pandita, Sri Pandita Murari,
Sri Kanu Pandita, Haridasa Brahmachari,

**405.** Sri Ananta Dasa, Krishnadasa, Janardana,
Dasa Narayana — the giver of the jewel of bhakti,

**406.** Bhagavata Acharya, Vaninatha Brahmachari,
Chaitanya-vallabha Dasa, endowed with bhakti,

**407.** Sri Pushpa-gopala, and also Sri Gopala Dasa,[^43]
Sri Harsha, the magnanimous Sri Lakshminatha Pandita.

**408.** What is there to tell — there is no end to the mahantas;
all the fortunate ones look, filling their eyes.

**409.** What a wondrous gait! Their splendor is like the sun's.
Whose eyes are not soothed on seeing that beauty?

**410.** And what of the two sons of Prabhu Advaitachandra —
Krishna Mishra and Gopala, full of supreme bliss!

**411.** Beautiful in every limb, expert in all the shastras;
Sri Krishna Chaitanya is the treasure of the life of both.

**412.** They distribute prema-bhakti to the fallen and the wretched;
who has the power to describe their deeds with one mouth?

**413.** Virabhadra, the son of Prabhu Nityananda —
he who purifies the worlds, an ocean of virtues.

**414.** Who will be able to describe him? There is no limit to his fame —
he who is renowned among the branches of Prabhu Nityananda.

**415.** *Thus [it is said] in the Sri Chaitanya-charitamrita:*
"Sri Virabhadra Gosani is a branch like a trunk;
his sub-branches, as many as there are, are beyond counting.[^44]

**416.** Being Ishvara, he calls himself a maha-bhagavata;
being beyond Vedic dharma, he is devoted to Vedic dharma.

**417.** Within, the activities of Ishvara; without, free of pride;
he is the main pillar in the pavilion of devotion to Chaitanya.

**418.** By the power of his mercy, even today
the whole world sings of Chaitanya and Nityananda."

**419.** Such qualities and deeds the devotees describe;
in every way the Prabhu is the life of all.

**420.** Prabhu Virabhadra is the source of great bliss;
some call him Virabhadra, some Virachandra.

**421.** Whoever sees such a Virachandra even once,
abandoning everything, makes those very feet the essence [of his life].

**422.** Seeing Virachandra's charming procession,
the residents of Kantaka-nagara say to one another:

**423.** "Look, look at Virachand, the son of Nitai!
Seeing this beauty, could even Madana keep his composure?

**424.** Ah, I die! What a tender body!
Gold and lightning are but an offering for this beauty.[^45]

**425.** What lovely, glossy, curling hair on his head!
What a tilaka on his forehead — by it the world is enchanted!

**426.** His brows a row of bees, his long eyes lotuses;
how sweet the cheeks, the ears, how beautiful the nose!

**427.** The moon of his face puts the moon's disk to shame;
the spotless luster of his teeth far surpasses clusters of kunda flowers.

**428.** A broad chest, and what a curve of the neck!
And what arms — they surpass serpents and the trunks of elephants!

**429.** What a wondrous belly, what a slender waist!
What endless sweetness of knees and feet!

**430.** His garments and the rest are glittering;
whoever sees him even once — his life is fulfilled.

**431.** Such matchless beauty I have seen with my eyes —
for my whole life I have sold myself to these feet."

**432.** Thus, saying much to one another in place after place,
they were overwhelmed at the sight of all these [mahantas].

**433.** Here, in Gauranga's courtyard, Raghunandana
thinks in his heart of the mahantas' arrival.

**434.** At that very moment Yadu softly says,[^46]
"All have come and entered Kantaka-nagara."

**435.** Hearing these words from Yadunandana's mouth,
he joyfully went some distance [to meet them] together with everyone.

**436.** The Lord's devotees go to the bank of the Ganges;
how impatient they are to see — who can tell?

**437.** What a wondrous meeting took place among them!
The ocean of the rasa of prema-bhakti surged up.

**438.** To the place where the Lord accepted sannyasa
everyone came.

**439.** On seeing that place the heart breaks;
they heave very long sighs, like tongues of flame.

**440.** Remembering the sannyasa of Sri Chaitanyachandra,
all weep, rolling on the ground.

**441.** A clamor of weeping arose that could not be checked;
no one was aware of the body — what to speak of composure.

**442.** Whoever saw that state is its witness.
What to speak of others? Seeing it, the beasts and birds weep.

**443.** All, clasping one another by the neck,
lament in a way I cannot describe.

**444.** They cannot restrain the ceaseless streams from their eyes;
everyone's body became grey with dust.

**445.** All the mahantas, going to Gauranga's courtyard
and seeing Gaurachandra, became calm after a while.

**446.** Sri Gaurachandra's will cannot be understood:
suddenly joy grows in everyone's heart.

**447.** Staying for some time in the Lord's courtyard,
all joyfully went and settled in wonderful lodgings.

**448.** Srinivasa Acharya, full of bhakti, with his associates,
being engaged everywhere, accomplishes all the tasks.

**449.** The festival held every day — there is no end to it;
all the fortunate villagers watch it.

**450.** And what a great festival there was on that day, the krishna-ashtami of Kartika —
who can find its limit?

**451.** The way the sankirtana began in Gauranga's courtyard —
there is nothing to compare it with in the three worlds.

**452.** How beautiful the mahantas were in the sankirtana,
what prema there was in the dancing of Krishna Mishra and Gopala,

**453.** what a wondrous dance Prabhu Virabhadra danced —
all that the fortunate ones will describe with joy.

**454.** At the place of the sankirtana there is no counting the people;
the demigods take their pleasure, mingling with humans.

**455.** Tears, trembling, hair standing on end and the rest are on everyone's body;
what a flood of prema it was — who can describe it?

**456.** Saptami, ashtami, navami — these three days;
how day and night passed, no one knows.

**457.** When the great festival was over, after that everyone
stayed for some days in Kantaka-nagara.

**458.** From Kantaka-nagara Sri Raghunandana,
taking everyone with him, sets out for Shrikhanda.

**459.** How distraught everyone was at the time of departure —
that can never be described with one mouth.

**460.** What Sri Yadunandana and the others said, weeping and weeping —
hearing it, the heart breaks.

**461.** How Sri Yadunandana honored [them] —
who will describe it? The fortunate ones saw it.

**462.** Sri Raghunandana says to Yadunandana,
"Come to Khanda quickly, so that there is no delay."

**463.** Thus, happily speaking many affectionate words,
first of all [he] set out in grand fashion for Yajigrama.

**464.** Here Yadunandana and the others completed all the tasks;
Yadunandana's conduct is the greatest wonder.

**465.** How merciful he is to the lowly cannot be told;
in the circle of Vaishnavas his praise is surpassing.

**466.** He is the one who composed the wondrous account of Gauranga;
hearing his songs, wood, stones and the like melt;[^47]

**467.** he who is foremost among the branches of Dasa Gadadhara;
he who is always absorbed in the service of the Deity of Gaura;

**468.** he for whom Dasa Gadadhara and Sri Pandita Gadadhara
are not different — this is known in the world.

**469.** Finding the occasion, I shall briefly make known here:
in the avatara of Chaitanya, Radha is Pandita Gosani,

**470.** and Dasa Gadadhara is the form of Radhika's vibhuti:[^48]
so Kavi Karnapura, the best of the wise, made known.

**471.** *Thus [it is said] in the Sri Gaura-ganoddesha-dipika, verses 147 and following:*[^49]
*"Sri Radha, the embodiment of prema, who formerly was the Queen of Vrindavana,*
*is [now] Sri Gadadhara, the beloved of Gaura, called Pandita,*

**472.** *he whom Sri Svarupa determined to be the Lakshmi of Vraja.*
*As formerly in Vrindavana the Lakshmi, beloved of Shyamasundara,*

**473.** *so now the Lakshmi of Gaura's prema is Sri Gadadhara Pandita.*
*And since Lalita, following Radha, is also 'like Radhika,'*
*she entered him — as [it is said] in the Gaura-chandrodaya:*[^50]

**474.** *'This one too is Lalita herself, the friend of Radhika;*
*truly, this is not Gadadhara, the best of brahmanas.*
*Or else this Hari, by His own energy,*
*became three — Himself, the friend and Radhika.'*

**475.** *Others have said that Lalita is Dhruvananda Brahmachari;*[^51]
*and that opinion is right — by the distinction of her own manifestations.*

**476.** *Or else Bhagavan Gaura by His own will took a threefold form;*
*therefore Sri Gadadhara Pandita is the form of Sri Radhika.*

**477.** *She who formerly was Chandrakanti, the form of Radha's vibhuti,*
*is now, near Gauranga, Gadadhara of the Dasa family.*

**478.** *Purnananda, who in Vraja was the foremost of Baladeva's beloved [friends] —*
*she too, for the sake of [the work of] the lila, entered that Gadadhara."*[^52]

**479.** In every respect the best is Gadai Pandita,
the branch of Sri Gaurachandra renowned in the world.

**480.** *Thus [it is said] in the Chaitanya-charitamrita:*
"A great branch is Gadadhara Pandita Gosani;
he is an embodiment of Lakshmi; there is none equal to him."[^53]

**481.** The power of Dasa Gadadhara is immense:
he is both a branch of Chaitanya and a branch of Nitai.

**482.** *In the same place:*
"The branch of Sri Dasa Gadadhara is above all:
he made the Kazis say, 'Hari, Hari.'"[^54]

**483.** That Dasa Gadadhara is a branch of Sri Nityananda
was made known by Krishnadasa, the poet, the best of the wise.

**484.** *In the same place:*
"Sri Ramadasa and Gadadhara Dasa,
devotees of Chaitanya Gosani, remain with Him."[^55]

**485.** When [the Lord] ordered Nityananda to go to the land of Gauda,
Mahaprabhu gave Him these two as companions.

**486.** Therefore both are counted in both groups —
so the wise say, explaining it clearly.

**487.** Gadadhara Dasa is always intoxicated, absorbed in bhava,
and Prabhu Nityananda likewise sports with him.

**488.** *Thus [it is said] in the same place:*
"Gadadhara Dasa is full of complete bliss in the mood of a gopi;
in his house Nityananda performs the dana-lila."[^56]

**489.** Thus Gadadhara Prabhu, together with Nityananda,
constantly rejoices, giving away the jewel of prema-bhakti.

**490.** I have told briefly of the deeds of Dasa Gadadhara;
I will tell of them elsewhere too, when the occasion arises.

**491.** How Sri Yadunandana passes [his days] without Dasa Gadadhara —
who could describe it?[^57]

**492.** Constantly he sings his glories —
Sri Yadunandana, ever absorbed in the rasa of bhakti.

**493.** How he accomplished the great festival [in honor] of his own master —
seeing it, all the people were amazed.

**494.** What to say — the greatly fortunate people,
filling their eyes, had the darshan of all the mahantas.

**495.** All the mahantas set out on the road to Yajigrama;
the news of [their] coming spread through Sri Yajigrama.

**496.** The people of Yajigrama, with great joy in their hearts,
came forward and led everyone to their lodgings.

**497.** Srinivasa Acharya felt great bliss;
it could not be described in the least with one mouth.

**498.** What could others know of Srinivasa's heart?
He gazes at the road in the utmost eagerness.

**499.** At that time Yadunandana and the others, with their companions,
came from Kantaka-nagara with joyful hearts.

**500.** And the bhagavatas who were in various villages —
all gathered at the Acharya's house.

**501.** A great, great festival took place at the Acharya's house;
everyone became greatly intoxicated in sankirtana.

**502.** Thus for four or five days at Srinivasa's house
everyone stayed with jubilant hearts.

**503.** The wise Srinivasa shows everyone full respect;
the fortunate praise Srinivasa.

**504.** Sri Raghunandana, in great joy, overcome by affection —
I do not know what he told Srinivasa in private.

**505.** With great care taking the Lord's associates [with him],
he set off for Khanda with a heart full of the highest bliss.

**506.** The people of Khanda, with greatly jubilant hearts,
came forward and led [them] into Khanda.

**507.** How everyone was engaged in service —
all that is not described here in detail.

**508.** People from other villages run from all sides;
[longing] to see the Lord's devotees, they cannot keep still.

**509.** In the joy of his heart someone says to another,
"Look, what beauty full of prema the masters have!

**510.** To see [them] together is supremely rare;
by our good fortune they have all come to Shrikhanda.

**511.** By seeing them for a short while the heart is not satisfied;
I fear they will suddenly leave, giving [us] sorrow."

**512.** Someone says, "O brother, they will not leave soon:
in Shrikhanda the ocean of prema will surge.

**513.** In Agrahayana comes the Ekadashi of the dark fortnight, supreme above all,
[the day] on which Sri Thakura Narahari disappeared from view.

**514.** Four days remain until that Ekadashi;
the festival that will take place you will see, filling your eyes.

**515.** What to say — in the incomparable, rare sankirtana,
what of humans — [even] the gods will be intoxicated."

**516.** Thus they say so much to one another, here and there;
in the town of Shrikhanda the people are beyond counting.

**517.** The festival that took place every day in the town of Shrikhanda
I do not describe, for fear of [swelling] the book.

**518.** Of the festival on the Ekadashi day, which had no end,
what I have heard I will make known a little, in brief.

**519.** On the morning of the Ekadashi Sri Raghunandana
offered himself to the Lord's associates.

**520.** Coming to Gauranga's courtyard with joy in his heart,
he had [it] beautifully decorated in countless ways.

**521.** What the beauty of the courtyard was cannot be put into words;
whoever looks even once, his eyes are soothed.

**522.** All the mahantas came there;
with their beauty they captivate everyone's heart.

**523.** On their foreheads, sandalwood tilaka, exceedingly lovely;
their arms brightly shining, their chests marked with [holy] names.

**524.** As soon as they see Gauranga, the life of Sri Sarakara Thakura,
their bodies fill with abundant thrills of ecstasy.

**525.** Seeing Him whom Sri Raghunandana fed laddus,
[all] felt great wonder and delight grow in their hearts.[^58]

**526.** For some time [they] beheld the two Deities;
the expressions of prema that arose [in them] cannot be described.

**527.** The brahmana Vaninatha, with exceedingly sweet words,
tells Sri Raghunandana all that is in his heart:

**528.** "Today, in the daytime, the hearing of the *Srimad-Bhagavatam*;
at night, the relishing of the bliss of sankirtana.

**529.** Srinivasa will read the *Srimad-Bhagavatam*."
Hearing this, Raghunandana rejoiced all the more.

**530.** At that very moment he had wonderful seats prepared
and requested all the mahantas to sit.

**531.** Shripati, Shrinidhi and all the other mahantas
sat on the seats — there is no end to their beauty.

**532.** Krishna Mishra and Gopala, with hearts full of the highest bliss —
and Prabhu Virabhadra sat on a divine seat.

**533.** Sri Raghunandana, overcome by great affection,
brought Srinivasa forward before all the mahantas.

**534.** All the mahantas say to Srinivasa,
"We very much wish to hear from your mouth.

**535.** Sit on this seat and read the *Srimad-Bhagavatam*;
do not hesitate — this is the word of all of us."

**536.** Hearing this, Srinivasa fell to the ground, bowing;
the humility he shows — who, hearing of it, could keep his composure?

**537.** Having received everyone's permission again and again,
he sat on the seat — the beauty [of it] was wondrous.

**538.** Offering flowers, tulasi and sandalwood to the book,
he begins the lovely mangalacharana.

**539.** In a voice surpassing the cuckoo, exceedingly sweet,
he utters the verses, as if raining down nectar.

**540.** The story of the sacred rasa pastimes is an ocean of rasa;
as he tells it, he loses his composure, and streams of tears flow from his eyes.

**541.** In various ways he explains every verse;
through verses he reveals the many varieties of raga.[^59]

**542.** What wondrous sweetness of speech — it destroys all composure!
There is nothing to compare with that sweet speech.

**543.** Like a great downpour, prema rains in that discourse;
all are overwhelmed, joy surges in their hearts.

**544.** With unblinking eyes [all] gaze at Srinivasa;
they cannot hold back their tears — they flow from their eyes.

**545.** The transformations of bhava that come over the mahantas —
how can I, wretched, describe them with one mouth?

**546.** Someone, forgetting himself, says within his mind,
"Sri Shuka has bestowed [his] power [on him] — that is why it is so."[^60]

**547.** Someone says, "Veda-vyasa has infused [him with] power —
that is why he reveals this wondrous meaning."

**548.** Someone says, "The power of the mercy of Gadadhara Pandita Gosani
must be fully manifest here."[^61]

**549.** Someone says, "By the mercy of Pandita Srivasa and the others —
such grace of reading; what can compare with it?"

**550.** Someone says, "He is the embodiment of Gaura's prema:
in this mouth He is the speaker — that is why there is such attraction."[^62]

**551.** Such feelings full of affection as arise in everyone's heart —
someone will describe them at length.

**552.** How wondrous is the conduct of the Lord's associates!
The way they listen is beyond compare.

**553.** Relishing the nectar of the discourse on the *Srimad-Bhagavatam*,
they do not notice at all how the day passes.

**554.** Srinivasa sees that the day has come to an end;
with a prayer he concluded the nectarean discourse.

**555.** Bowing to the book, with great humility in his heart,
he fell to the ground and bowed to the Lord's associates.

**556.** The Lord's associates, filled with jubilation,
show their affection for Srinivasa in this way:

**557.** someone places his hand on Srinivasa's head
and, saying, "I am soothed," is drenched with tears.

**558.** "May all the desires of your heart be fulfilled!
Whoever is deprived of you — let Fate deprive him.

**559.** Whoever takes shelter of you is blessed:
he will surely attain Sri Krishna Chaitanya."

**560.** Someone, touching his face with his hand, says again and again:
"May this face always remain in my mind."

**561.** Losing his composure, he again says very softly:
"Through you the sufferings of living beings will come to an end."

**562.** Someone says: "Let me take your misfortunes upon myself and die!
Come here, let me hold you in my arms just once."

**563.** Holding him in his arms, he cannot let him go for even a moment;
he feels he would keep him forever within his heart.

**564.** Some, giving him so many blessings,
hold him to their hearts and say: "My longing is fulfilled.

**565.** Everything had become empty, and with that the heart was burnt;[^63]
you have cooled it, making it drink the nectar of the narrative."

**566.** Someone, having embraced him, cannot calm down
and entrusts him to the feet of the two Sri Deities.[^64]

**567.** He is under the sway of the love of Narahari and Raghunandana,
intoxicated day and night by the qualities of these two.[^65]

**568.** "Plunge the lowly into the ocean of bhakti-rasa" —
so much he says, and tears stream from his eyes.

**569.** Someone, bowing, says: "You have fulfilled our aspirations,
you have plunged us into the taste of the narrative of the *Srimad-Bhagavatam*."

**570.** Someone, in great joy, keeps silent.
Such wonderful behavior I am unable to describe.

**571.** This kind of conduct toward Srinivasa
the fortunate people behold with great bliss.

**572.** Great joy arose in all the mahantas;
they were overwhelmed by the qualities of Sri Raghunandana.

**573.** Again and again they praise Raghunandana;
who has the power to describe all that glory?

**574.** In Raghunandana's heart there is great embarrassment;
he considers himself lowly and shows humility.

**575.** Who else could understand all these ways?
For some time they pass the time in talks about Sri Chaitanya.

**576.** To see the waking arati of the two Lords,[^66]
all quickly rose and bowed in the courtyard.

**577.** Having darshan of the two Sri Deities, they rejoiced
and had the preparations made for beginning the sankirtana.

**578.** Sri Raghunandana gave instructions to his own people;
all quickly came to Gauranga's courtyard.

**579.** Whatever still remained they arranged beautifully,
and with great care they set out the khols, karatalas and the rest.

**580.** "Everything is ready," they told Raghunandana;
Sri Raghunandana informed the masters.

**581.** Having seen the Lord's evening arati,
they see the preparations for beginning the sankirtana.

**582.** Looking over the many khols, karatalas and so on,
all praise it in the highest joy.

**583.** They see fragrant sandal paste in many vessels,
and in separate vessels flower garlands.

**584.** Various flower garlands — their fragrance is extraordinary;
the wonderful workmanship attracts every heart.

**585.** Seeing all this in abundance, the Lord's dear ones
say to one another: "What wonderful preparations!"

**586.** Sri Raghunandana says, humbly entreating:
"Please accept the sandal paste and garlands — they are prasada."

**587.** Hearing this, the delight of all the mahantas grew:
they will put them on one another — in this is great happiness.

**588.** Sri Raghunandana, in separate vessels,
offered sandal paste and garlands to everyone.

**589.** The khols and karatalas are the Lord's treasure;
someone offers them sandal paste and flower garlands.

**590.** Sandal paste and garlands shine on all the mardalas,[^67]
in which Brahma and the other gods always dwell.

**591.** Sri Yadunandana and Sri Lochana — these two[^68]
took flower garlands and fragrant sandal paste.

**592.** The two, having put them on Krishna Mishra and Gopala,[^69]
gaze their fill at the wondrous beauty.

**593.** With supreme joy in his heart, Sri Raghunandana
anoints Sri Virabhadra's body with sandal paste.[^70]

**594.** With various flower garlands he adorned him wonderfully;
seeing that beauty, [all] sank into an ocean of happiness.

**595.** At a sign from Prabhu Virachandra, Srinivasa
took a garland and sandal paste and went to the Prabhu.

**596.** Prabhu Virachandra himself, in great joy,
put the garland and sandal paste on Sri Raghunandana.

**597.** Sri Raghunandana, overwhelmed with affection,
put a garland and sandal paste on Srinivasa.

**598.** Thus they received garlands and sandal paste from one another;
the fortunate will describe this in detail.

**599.** All stood beneath the beautiful canopy;
an ocean of supreme, wondrous beauty surges.

**600.** The Lord's associates are abodes of virtues,
supremely expert in song, dance and instrumental music.

**601.** Auspicious sounds arose at the place of sankirtana;
all around, encircling it, many hundreds of lamps are burning.

**602.** At the mere sound of the mardala, crusher of the unbelievers,[^71]
the hair stood on end on the bodies of all the Vaishnavas.

**603.** How sweet is the skill in playing the jhanjh!
Freely they play the beautiful khamaks and khanjaris.[^72]

**604.** All the players recite the pathaksharas;[^73]
in this wealth of sound it is as if a rain of nectar were falling.

**605.** The singers — following the rules of alapa and varna —[^74]
perform alapa in various ways: what comparison could be given?

**606.** Having performed the alapa, they manifest the raga;
what is there to say — great is the good fortune of that raga!

**607.** Shrutis, svaras, gramas, murchhanas, talas and the rest,
and the kinds of gamaka they display wonderfully.[^75]

**608.** In various prabandhas they display the kinds of tala;
what to speak of others — they take away the pride of the Gandharvas.[^76]

**609.** As they sing kirtana, everyone's strength increases:
the old became like sixteen-year-olds.

**610.** The ocean of the joy of sankirtana surged up;
animals, birds, humans, gods and others were enchanted.

**611.** At the place of sankirtana there is no end of people;
from everyone's eyes streams of tears flow unceasingly.

**612.** The gods, mingling in the crowd of humans,
float on the waves of the ocean of the joy of sankirtana.

**613.** All the mahantas, forgetting themselves,
dance a dance by which the world is enchanted.

**614.** The bliss at the dancing of the two — Krishna Mishra and Sri Gopala —
how could poets describe it?

**615.** Sri Virabhadra dances, his movements most sweet;
whoever sees him even once — his sufferings go far away.

**616.** Seeing the wondrous dance, the people say:
"We were not given many eyes — we were given only two!"

**617.** However much they say this, their longing is not satisfied;
all keep gazing with unblinking eyes.

**618.** A blind man wanders all about, his heart agitated:
he heard that the son of Nityananda is dancing.

**619.** Someone asks another: "What is his name?"
He replies: "He is renowned in the world as Virabhadra."

**620.** Hearing this, the blind man, delighted, reflects within himself:
"The very name he bears takes away all that is inauspicious."

**621.** Having reflected thus, he offers praise in his mind:
"The word 'vira' is there for the destruction of the wicked,

**622.** and to destroy the great inauspiciousness of living beings,
therefore there is the word 'bhadra,' O merciful one.[^77]

**623.** The Creator made me blind — I cannot see.
Prabhu, consider in your heart what is fitting."

**624.** Thus the blind man says many things, weeping and weeping;
the Prabhu, the son of Nityananda, came to know of it.

**625.** Filled with compassion, he looked upon the blind;
the blind man gained sight — what pious merit the blind man had!

**626.** Freely he watches Virabhadra's dancing;
the cry "Jaya, jaya, jaya!" filled the world.

**627.** In sankirtana the night came to an end;
remembering the qualities of Gora, the heart breaks.

**628.** The Lord's associates cannot keep their composure;
raising their arms, they call out loudly:

**629.** "Where is Lord Gaurachandra, the son of Shachi?
Where is Nityananda Rama, the life of the wretched?

**630.** Where is Sri Advaita Acharya, the abode of virtues?
Where is Sri Pandita Gadadhara, full of prema?

**631.** Haridasa, Srivasa, Svarupa, Ramananda —
where are Sri Madhava, Vasu, Murari, Mukunda?[^78]

**632.** Where are my Gadadhara Dasa and Narahari?"[^79]
Uttering all these names, they weep, crying aloud.

**633.** "Show Yourself together with Your associates, O delightful Gora!" —
saying this, they fall and roll on the ground.

**634.** Their sighs, like tongues of flame, are unceasing;
everyone's body became grey with dust.

**635.** The cruel pain of separation grew greatly;
a clamor of weeping arose, and composure went far away.

**636.** At the devotees' distress the Lord cannot remain still;[^80]
I do not know how He consoled everyone.

**637.** This is the extraordinary lila of Sri Mahaprabhu:
out of sorrow He plunged [all] into an ocean of bliss.

**638.** What absorption in bliss came over everyone!
One clasps another's feet again and again.

**639.** One embraces another, faces beaming;
everyone's eyes are full of tears of bliss.

**640.** They address one another in many ways,
and see that the night has reached the hour of dawn.

**641.** Seeing the mangala-arati, with jubilant hearts
all bow down in the Lord's courtyard.[^81]

**642.** At that time, beholding the masters,[^82]
the people on all sides cry, "Hari bol!"

**643.** The throng of people cannot be described;
the people say all sorts of things to one another.

**644.** Someone says, "Tonight passed quickly;
merciless fate did not lengthen the night.

**645.** Such a Sri Ekadashi comes by great good fortune:
on it all the mahantas poured down a rain of prema."[^83]

**646.** Someone says, "What conduct the mahantas have!
Look: as the fast, so the vigil."

**647.** Someone says, "Apart from Chaitanya's associates,
who knows what ought to be done on Sri Ekadashi?"

**648.** Someone says, "On Sri Ekadashi this is the rule:
never take grain and the like.

**649.** But now some sinners on Sri Ekadashi
feed others grain and eat it themselves with glad hearts.

**650.** They do not accept the scriptures and invent opinions of their own;
seeing such sinners, I feel pain."

**651.** Someone says, "Whoever has gained the mercy of the Lord's associates —
to him the rules of the Ekadashi vow are given."

**652.** Someone says, "What will become of a sinner like me?
Will firm attachment to Sri Ekadashi arise [in me]?"

**653.** Someone says, "I was ceaselessly sunk in sin,
I understood nothing — I am the most wretched of men."

**654.** Someone says, "The Vaishnavas are supremely merciful;
they will deliver [us] in every way."

**655.** Someone says, "A great sorrow has remained in my heart:
I did not fall at the Vaishnavas' feet, rolling in the dust."

**656.** Someone says, "Do not worry about anything any more;
now the desire of all of us will be fulfilled."

**657.** Saying many such things, they went to the place of the sankirtana
and fell rolling on the ground, wet with their tears.

**658.** Seeing the people's fervor, the Lord's beloved ones
showed them a mercy that cannot be described.

**659.** What can be said of the mahantas' rapture of prema!
They praise Sri Raghunandana without end.

**660.** Someone says, "Whoever loves Sri Raghunandana —
Sri Krishna Chaitanya is under his control birth after birth."

**661.** Someone says, "How merciful is Sri Raghunandana!
He is the life of the utterly poor, the lowly and the suffering."

**662.** Someone says, "What humility! There is no modesty like it [anywhere]."
Someone says, "His beauty is just like Kandarpa's."

**663.** Someone says, "In song, music and dance he is a great master."[^84]

**664.** Someone says, "Through Raghunandana's great love
such bliss of kirtana arose — what comparison could be given?"

**665.** Thus they say much about Raghunandana;
at that moment Sri Raghunandana came there.

**666.** Hearing his own praise, he grew so abashed at heart
that it cannot be told.

**667.** He considers himself lowly and cannot bear praise;
hearing the humility he shows, who could remain composed?

**668.** Hearing Raghunandana's humility, everyone
was overwhelmed; tears stream from their eyes.

**669.** Firmly embracing Sri Raghunandana,
the Lord's beloved ones grew calm after a while.

**670.** Sri Raghunandana humbly submits to all:
"What is your command for the parana on Sri Dvadashi?"[^85]

**671.** All say, "Sitting together, all of us
will honor Sri Gauranga's prasada."

**672.** Hearing this, Raghunandana's heart rejoiced;
at once, taking great pains, he has various foods prepared.

**673.** All the mahantas went to their own lodgings
and with their companions performed their morning rites and the rest.

**674.** Here various cooked dishes were made ready;
the pujari quickly offered the bhoga to the Lord.

**675.** After a while, knowing that the Lord's time had come,
the pujari joyfully removed the bhoga.

**676.** Bringing all the mahantas, Sri Raghunandana
gave them darshan of the Lord's sacred bhoga.

**677.** The beauty of the Lord's bhoga cannot be put into words;
seeing it, all the mahantas rejoiced at heart.

**678.** Having seen the Lord's sacred arati,
they went and sat where they were to eat.

**679.** All sat down — in what a wonderful arrangement!
A wondrous beauty filled the place of the meal.

**680.** Banana leaves, fragrant water in the vessels;
many people serve with great care.

**681.** Here the pujari, full of loving devotion,
carefully and joyfully laid the Lord to rest.

**682.** He bowed again and again at the Lord's feet
and made ready to serve.

**683.** There were many cakes and the like of wheat flour,
various milk preparations, fruits, roots and more.

**684.** Many carry [them] in vessels with care
and serve in due order with joyful hearts.

**685.** Everywhere, setting out every dish in rows,
they serve the mahantas the sacred charanamrita.[^86]

**686.** All the mahantas say to Sri Raghunandana,
"If you do not sit down, there is no joy in this."

**687.** Hearing this, Sri Raghunandana humbly says,
"Please eat; let my eyes be soothed by the sight."

**688.** Raising the cry of "Hari!", all eat with delight;
Sri Raghunandana stands and watches happily.

**689.** From there, going quickly to the sacred bhoga-mandira,
he took one bhoga, setting it apart.[^87]

**690.** To where Sri Thakura Narahari used to stay in solitude
he himself went, carrying the sacred prasada.[^88]

**691.** Taking the seat on which he used to sit,
immersed in humility, he seated him on it in meditation.

**692.** Before the seat he arranged various items;
in the water vessel he put fragrant prasada water.

**693.** In one vessel he also placed prasada tambula,
in another, Gauranga's flower garland.

**694.** In meditation he offered the foods and the rest,
and having prayed, he closed the door of the room.

**695.** Coming outside, he remained [there] for a while;
knowing the time had come, he goes to give achamana.[^89]

**696.** Opening the door, he sees Prabhu Narahari
sitting on the seat, having assumed a divine form.

**697.** The very moment he saw [him], he forgot himself;
when [he] vanished, he sank into an ocean of sorrow.

**698.** After a while, regaining composure, he gave the achamana;
falling to the ground, he bowed down with tearful eyes.

**699.** Taking the seat on his head, he set it in its former place
and quickly went to the mahantas.

**700.** He sees what delight everyone takes in the meal;
all eat the food, praising it again and again.

**701.** Sri Raghunandana, with great humility,
entreats them again and again to eat more.

**702.** Having eaten in the highest bliss,
all, talking much with one another, performed achamana.

**703.** Overcome with affection, all say to Sri Raghunandana,
"Take everyone and quickly sit down to eat."

**704.** Sri Raghunandana, having carefully fed
Srinivasa and all the others, himself ate.

**705.** Many people everywhere eat joyfully;
all say, "We have never seen such a festival."

**706.** The great festival took place on the day of Dvadashi;
the news of all this spread through every land.

**707.** Sri Raghunandana, having finished all his tasks,
came joyfully to Gauranga's courtyard.

**708.** To see Gauranga's utthapana-arati,[^90]
the Lord's beloved ones came to Gauranga's courtyard.

**709.** Having seen the Lord's beautiful arati,
everyone sat in Gauranga's courtyard.

**710.** For a while they discussed Krishna's lilas;
seeing the evening arati, they rejoiced.

**711.** All, bowing to the Lord in Gauranga's courtyard,
became greatly intoxicated in the kirtana of the Holy Name.

**712.** The second watch of the night passed;
they went to their lodgings and lay down for a while.

**713.** At night's end, quickly leaving their beds,
all performed the cleaning of the teeth and other rites.

**714.** At daybreak Raghunandana himself
came to the lodgings of all the mahantas.

**715.** What loving exchanges took place between them!
Whose heart is not soothed to see all that?

**716.** Shripati and Shrinidhi say to Raghunandana,
"Everyone wishes to set out today."

**717.** Sri Raghunandana says, "There is no such luck!
For some days I shall see everyone together in one place.

**718.** Since by my good fortune you have come here,
my heart does not wish to let [you] go for [another] two or four days."

**719.** The brahmana Vaninatha says to Sri Raghunandana,
"Tomorrow morning you will give [us] leave."

**720.** Hearing [this], Raghunandana, smiling gently,
said, "What will happen tomorrow — why insist on that?

**721.** Yesterday, at the breaking of the fast, you ate [only] cakes and such,
and after that not even a drop of water was taken.

**722.** Today cooking will be done quickly at every lodging;
if you bathe and so on without delay, then I shall be happy."

**723.** Hearing Raghunandana's sweet words,
the Lord's dear ones bathed and so on.

**724.** Placing prasada sweets in various vessels,
the pujari of Gaurachandra brought them.

**725.** Together with Sri charanamrita he gave them out everywhere;
with great delight all partook of it all.

**726.** Everywhere cooking was done in various ways;
offering it to Krishna, all ate.

**727.** No one could remain without talk of Krishna;[^91]
day and night they float in the boundless ocean of prema.

**728.** Sri Raghunandana's joy was boundless;
how day and night passed he did not know at all.

**729.** Thus he kept everyone for some two to four days more;
at [the thought], "It is time to take leave," [all] became distraught.

**730.** In bidding farewell, he showed [them] much honor
and sent abundant provisions along with each.

**731.** Taking both hands of Sri Virabhadra,
he spoke much, bathed in tears.

**732.** Gazing at the faces of Krishna Mishra and Gopala,
he did not know what to say — his heart welled up.

**733.** What he said to each of the mahantas —
that some other worthy souls will describe.

**734.** The words they spoke to one another were hard to hear;
whoever heard them, his heart was torn apart.

**735.** In the morning, having taken leave, all
set out, unable to compose themselves; tears streamed from their eyes.

**736.** Coming to Gauranga's courtyard, all bowed down;
the pujari carefully brought and gave [them] prasada garlands.

**737.** From Shrikhanda all set out;
the people of Khanda could not keep their composure.

**738.** In the bitter pain of separation, how much rose in [their] hearts!
They go along with the Prabhus, unable to stay still.

**739.** Having gone some distance, Shripati and all the others
calmed Sri Raghunandana, saying many things to him.

**740.** He showed great favor to Srinivasa
and consoled everyone — Sri Yadunandana and the others.

**741.** Seeing how they showed love to one another,
whose heart would not melt?

**742.** Distraught, all went on;
Sri Raghunandana stands gazing after them down the road.

**743.** Sri Raghunandana, with Srinivasa and the others,
returned to his own home, speaking of [their] virtues.

**744.** That day, gathering everyone in Shrikhanda,
he became deeply absorbed in sacred talks and kirtana.

**745.** The next day, with a deeply troubled heart,
he bade farewell to each, wherever he was to go.

**746.** Srinivasa went to Yajigrama;
Sri Yadunandana went to Kantaka-nagara.

**747.** And all the other Vaishnavas, wherever they had come from —
they all went to their own homes.

**748.** The people from distant lands joyfully set out,
remembering Raghunandana's virtues.

**749.** The story of the great festival in the town of Shrikhanda
anyone at all tells to anyone, wherever they may be.

**750.** Whoever hears the story of the sacred festival
is effortlessly freed from suffering.

**751.** Whoever has firm attachment to these accounts
obtains bhakti that is rare even for the gods.

**752.** O brothers, give your mind to this constantly;
do not be lazy — you will obtain great happiness.

**753.** Meditating on the feet of Srinivasa Acharya,
Narahari Dasa tells the *Bhakti-ratnakara*.

*Thus in the Sri Bhakti-ratnakara ends the ninth wave, called "The Description of Srinivasa Acharya's Going Again to Sri Vrindavana and Return and Other Events, [and] of the Great Festivals at Sri Katwa, Yajigrama and Shrikhanda".*[^92]
