# Appendix. The Contents of the Book (Granthanuvada)

*A verse list of the contents of the fifteen tarangas; a song of prayer to the associates of Mahaprabhu; the author's account of himself.*

**1.** In the *Sri Bhakti-ratnakara* there are fifteen tarangas;
which pastimes are in which taranga I shall tell in few words.[^1]

**2.** In the first taranga I performed the mangalacharana
and told of the ancestors of Sri Jiva Gosani,[^2]

**3.** of the books of the Gosvamis — how many there are, and their names —
and also [gave] a brief outline of the birth of Srinivasa Acharya and other matters.[^3]

**4.** In the second taranga: the brahmana Sri Chaitanya Dasa
went to Nilachala, and his desire was fulfilled;[^4]

**5.** the birth of Srinivasa, many talks of father and son;
how Govinda became manifest in Vrindavana.[^5]

**6.** In the third taranga the Acharya set out for Kshetra
and, hearing of the disappearance of Sri Chaitanya, was scorched [with grief].

**7.** By the Lord's command in a dream he went to Nilachala;
the Lord's associates bestowed mercy [on him], and he returned to Gauda.[^6]

**8.** In the fourth taranga the Acharya wanders through Gauda;
the mercy of Sri Vishnupriya [towards him] was boundless.

**9.** The Lord's associates showed [him] great favour.
The journey to Vrindavana and other matters are described here.[^7]

**10.** In the fifth taranga Srinivasa and Narottama
went [on the circuit] of Vraja together with Sri Raghava.

**11.** The pastimes of the three — Gaura, Nityananda and Advaita —
and many [other] accounts were told along the way.[^8]

**12.** In the sixth taranga Sri Shyamananda came to Vraja;
the Beloved of Madana-gopala and Govinda arrived.[^9]

**13.** Srinivasa, taking the books of the Gosvamis,
took his leave and set out for Gauda.

**14.** In the seventh taranga: the theft of the books at Vishnupura,
and Sri Acharya's mercy to King Vira Hambira.

**15.** Sri Shyamananda went to Utkala.
The many accounts here are an elixir for the ears.[^10]

**16.** In the eighth taranga Sri Thakura Mahashaya,
having travelled through Sri Gauda, went to Kshetra.

**17.** Returning from Kshetra, he met Sri Acharya.
Sri Acharya made Ramachandra and others his disciples.[^11]

**18.** In the ninth taranga, having spread the books of bhakti,
Sri Acharya went again to Vrindavana and returned.

**19.** And the other accounts told here —
who has the strength to keep his composure on hearing them?[^12]

**20.** In the tenth taranga: the great festival in the village of Kanchanagariya,
[such] as cannot be put into words.

**21.** In the village of Sri Kheturi there was the greatest of festivals:
[Narottama] with his companions danced in the sankirtana of Gaura.[^13]

**22.** In the eleventh taranga: to the village of Sri Kheturi
Sri Jahnava Ishvari returned from Vraja.

**23.** Ishvari travelled by way of Ekachakra;
on coming to Khardaha she had a Deity made.[^14]

**24.** In the twelfth taranga the three — the Acharya and [his companions] —
went round Nadiya with Sri Ishana.

**25.** There were many accounts that give the highest bliss,
among them the marriage of Prabhu Nityananda and other matters.[^15]

**26.** In the thirteenth taranga Sri Acharya Thakura
entered a second marriage — [there was] much joy.

**27.** Prabhu Virachandra, having married with jubilation,
went with his companions to Vraja and returned to Gauda.[^16]

**28.** In the fourteenth taranga Sri Acharya with his companions
held a great festival in the village of Borakuli.

**29.** [All] were immersed in sankirtana without cease.
Here too are various other charming accounts.[^17]

**30.** In the fifteenth taranga great bliss is revealed:
with his companions Shyamananda performs his pastimes in Utkala.

**31.** To the greatest of pashandis he gave bhakti.
These accounts are relished by the fortunate.[^18]

**32.** The book *Bhakti-ratnakara* is supremely full of rasa.
Relish it without cease; do not be idle.

**33.** I am a fool — do not hold any fault against me;
correct [it], and relish the book with joy.

**34.** What do I know how to say? Considering me your own servant,
mercifully fulfil my desire.

**35.** *Thus [it is said] in a song (raga Kamoda):*
This is the desire in my mind: intoxicated with the qualities of Gauranga-chand,
to wander day and night;
[and in His pastimes] with Lakshmi and Vishnupriya, in the delights of His play in Nadiya,
as it were to float in that ocean of happiness.[^19]

**36.** With a hundred thousand mouths, moment after moment,
to sing the qualities of Nitai-chand together with Vasudha and Jahnava;
to bow my head always to Sitanatha together with Sita,
and to flood the world with His glory.[^20]

**37.** Calling out "Gadadhara, Narahari, Svarupa!",
may I always dance, clapping [my palms] under my arms;
crying "Srinivasa, Vanamali, Gadadhara Dasa!" —
may my heart overflow with bliss.[^21]

**38.** Haridasa, Vakreshvara, Ramananda, Damodara,
Gauridasa, Sri Raghunandana,
Murari, Mukunda-Rama — repeating the names of them all,
I weep without cease.[^22]

**39.** Shachi and Mishra Jagannatha — the Lord's mother and father,
Padmavati, Hadai Pandita —
at their holy feet, renowned throughout the world for [their] qualities,
may my mind abide birth after birth.[^23]

**40.** Sri Madhava, Ratnavati, Malini, Madhavi, Damayanti-devi,
full of great tenderness,
Sri Achyuta, the root of bliss, the merciful Virachandra —
oh, that I might serve their lotus feet![^24]

**41.** Sri Vallabha, Sanatana, Sadashiva, Sudarshana,
Nandana, Vijaya, Kashishvara,
Vishvarupa — repeating [these names], may I go about exulting,
so that the pashandis, seeing [it], may be afraid.[^25]

**42.** Dear [to the Lord] Sanatana, Rupa, the two Bhattas — wells of rasa,
Raghunatha, the profound Sri Jiva —
repeating these names, may this sinful body of mine
become grey with dust.[^26]

**43.** Subuddhi together with Raghava, Bhugarbha, Sri Lokanatha,
who roam through Vraja in the joy of prema —
may I have love for these names, may my wicked mind go far away,
may the thrill of rapture pervade all my limbs.[^27]

**44.** Govinda, Madhava, Hari, Shuklambara Brahmachari,
Vasu Ghosha — those for whom Gaura is life:
by their grace may I go about with a lion's roar,
counting non-devotees [as worthless as] a straw.[^28]

**45.** Shashthidhara the kirtan singer, Haridasa the best of brahmanas,
Shridhara Thakura the seller of kholas,
Kamsari, Vallabha and Dhananjaya — for all of them
may I become a dancing dog.[^29]

**46.** Kavichandra, Vidyanidhi, Sri Madhu Pandita and the rest —
all the associates dear to Gaura.
Dasa Narahari says: these jewels of names
I wear upon my neck, making them a necklace.[^30]

**47.** I am ashamed to tell about myself.
Formerly I lived on the bank of the Ganga — everyone knows this.[^31]

**48.** Vishvanatha Chakravarti is renowned everywhere;
his disciple is my father, the brahmana Jagannatha.[^32]

**49.** I do not know why I have two names —
Narahari Dasa and Dasa Ghanashyama.[^33]

**50.** I left household life and became detached,
[yet] day and night I sank in the most sinful worldly objects.[^34]

**51.** O ocean of mercy, O Vaishnava gosais!
The Vedas sing: without your mercy there is no way.

**52.** Narahari says: show me such mercy
that I may be immersed without cease in the *Bhakti-ratnakara*.[^35]

*Thus the book Sri Srimad Bhakti-ratnakara is complete.*

[^1]: *Granthānuvāda* (গ্রন্থানুবাদ), lit. "a retelling of the book." This section is found only in the Gaudiya Mission edition of 1960 (pp. 648–650); it is absent from the editions of 1888 and 1912/13. What the author says of himself agrees with the biography of Narahari Chakravarti accepted in the tradition. In the 1960 edition the section follows the colophon of taranga 15 and has its own numbering (1–52), retained here; the editor's prose summary before it is not translated.
[^2]: BR 1 (see the title of taranga 1); the ancestors of Jiva Gosvami are the family of Rupa and Sanatana.
[^3]: "Brief outline" renders *sūtra*; cf. the title of taranga 1: "…a Brief Statement of the Birth of Srinivasa Acharya and Other Events."
[^4]: BR 2: the brahmana Gangadhara, having seen the Lord's sannyasa at Katwa, became Chaitanya Dasa; later he is the father of Srinivasa.
[^5]: BR 2: the appearance of the Deities Govinda, Madanamohana and Gopinatha in Vrindavana.
[^6]: BR 3. Kshetra is Puri (Nilachala). The disappearance is Sri Chaitanya's departure from His manifest pastimes. "The Lord's associates" renders *prabhu-gaṇa*: Gadadhara Pandita and the other associates in Puri.
[^7]: BR 4: Navadvipa and Vishnupriya, Shantipura, Khardaha, Khanakul; the journey to Vrindavana.
[^8]: BR 5, the circuit of Vraja-mandala with Raghava Gosvami, with digressions on Sanatana, Rupa and Jiva, the birth and childhood of Advaita, the wanderings of Nityananda, and so on.
[^9]: BR 6: the Deities of Sri Radhika sent from Orissa by Purushottama Jana for Govinda and Madanamohana (Madana-gopala).
[^10]: BR 7. Utkala is Orissa.
[^11]: BR 8 (Narottama is "Thakura Mahashaya"); the initiation of Ramachandra Kaviraja comes at the end of taranga 8.
[^12]: BR 9: the letters of the Gosvamis, the departure of the Lord's associates, Ramachandra, the festivals at Katwa and Shrikhanda.
[^13]: BR 10. Kanchanagariya is a village in Bengal where the brahmana Haridasa Acharya and his sons lived; the ekadashi before the festival at Kheturi was kept there. In 21 the subject is not named; according to taranga 10 it is Narottama with his companions.
[^14]: BR 11: Jahnava returns from Vrindavana by way of Kheturi and Ekachakra; at Khardaha the sculptor Nayana Bhaskara makes the Deity of Sri Radha for Gopinatha in Vrindavana (cf. BR 13). "Returned" (*āilā*, lit. "came") follows the content of the taranga.
[^15]: BR 12. The three are Srinivasa, Narottama and Ramachandra; Ishana, an old servant in the house of Shachi and Jagannatha Mishra, is their guide through Navadvipa. The marriage of Nityananda with Vasudha and Jahnava comes at the end of taranga 12.
[^16]: BR 13: Srinivasa's second marriage; Virachandra's journey to Vraja and return.
[^17]: BR 14. Borakuli is the village of Govinda Chakravarti, where Srinivasa installed the Deity of Sri Radha-vinoda.
[^18]: BR 15. Pashandis are enemies of bhakti; cf. the conversion of the pashandis at Dharenda in the same taranga.
[^19]: Stanzas 35–46 are one song, with Narahari's signature in 46. Gauranga-chand is Sri Chaitanya; Lakshmi (Lakshmipriya) and Vishnupriya are His consorts.
[^20]: Nitai-chand is Nityananda; Vasudha and Jahnava are His consorts. Sitanatha ("the Lord of Sita") is Advaita Acharya; Sita is His consort.
[^21]: Gadadhara is Gadadhara Pandita; Narahari is Narahari Sarakara of Shrikhanda; Svarupa is Svarupa Damodara. Srinivasa here is apparently Srivasa Pandita (whose full name is Srinivasa); Vanamali is apparently Vanamali Pandita, an associate of the Lord in Navadvipa; Gadadhara Dasa is an associate of Nityananda. "Clapping under my arms" renders *kā̃katāli diyā*, a gesture in dancing.
[^22]: Haridasa is Haridasa Thakura; Vakreshvara Pandita; Ramananda is Ramananda Raya; Damodara is apparently Damodara Pandita; Gauridasa Pandita; Raghunandana is the son of Mukunda of Shrikhanda; Murari is Murari Gupta. "Mukunda-Rama" (*mukunda-rāma*): it is unclear whether one person or two is meant (e.g. Mukunda Datta and Rama[i] Pandita).
[^23]: Shachi and Jagannatha Mishra are the parents of Sri Chaitanya; Padmavati and Hadai Pandita are the parents of Nityananda.
[^24]: Madhava Mishra and Ratnavati are the parents of Gadadhara Pandita; Malini is the wife of Srivasa; Madhavi is Madhavi-devi, the sister of Shikhi Mahiti, among the Lord's associates in Puri; Damayanti is the sister of Raghava Pandita, who prepared foods for the Lord. "Sri Achyuta, the root of bliss" renders *śrī-acyutānanda-kanda*; it may also be read "Sri Achyutananda, the root [of joy]": Advaita's son Achyutananda is meant. Virachandra is the son of Nityananda.
[^25]: Vallabha (Vallabhacharya) is the father of Lakshmipriya; Sanatana (Sanatana Mishra) is the father of Vishnupriya; Sadashiva is apparently Sadashiva Pandita (or Kaviraja); Sudarshana is apparently Sudarshana Pandita, the Lord's teacher in childhood; Nandana is Nandana Acharya; Vijaya is Vijaya Dasa, the copyist of books; Kashishvara is Kashishvara Pandita (Gosvami); Vishvarupa is the Lord's elder brother. "Exulting" renders *phuli’ phuli’*, lit. "swelling [with joy]."
[^26]: The two Bhattas are Gopala Bhatta and Raghunatha Bhatta; Raghunatha is Raghunatha Dasa. To become grey with dust is to roll in the dust in ecstasy while repeating the names.
[^27]: Subuddhi Raya, a former ruler of Gauda, later lived in Vraja; Raghava is Raghava Pandita (Gosvami) of Govardhana, the guide of Srinivasa and Narottama in taranga 5; Bhugarbha and Lokanatha are associates of the Lord, the first whom He sent to Vrindavana.
[^28]: Govinda, Madhava and Vasu (Vasudeva) Ghosha are brothers, singers and associates of the Lord. Hari has not been identified. Shuklambara Brahmachari is an associate of the Lord in Navadvipa.
[^29]: Shashthidhara (in the scan *yaṣṭhīdhara*; cf. the note on Shashthidhara in BR 1) is a kirtan singer, not identified. Haridasa "the best of brahmanas" (*dvija-vara*): which Haridasa is meant is unclear. Shridhara, who sold kholas (cups of banana leaf), is a poor brahmana, an associate of the Lord in Navadvipa. Kamsari is apparently Kamsari Sena; Vallabha is not identified; Dhananjaya is Dhananjaya Pandita, an associate of Nityananda. A "dancing dog" is a tame one, obedient to its masters.
[^30]: Kavichandra is an associate of the Lord; Vidyanidhi is Pundarika Vidyanidhi; Madhu Pandita is the servant of the Deity Gopinatha in Vrindavana.
[^31]: The author does not say where exactly. According to the account accepted in the tradition, Narahari's family lived in Bengal, and he himself went to Vrindavana, where, by tradition, he served in the kitchen of the Deity Sri Govinda.
[^32]: Vishvanatha Chakravarti Thakura (17th–18th c.) is the acharya, commentator on the *Srimad-Bhagavatam* and the books of the Gosvamis, who lived in Vrindavana. Jagannatha Chakravarti, Narahari's father, is known only as his disciple (so also in the accepted biography of Narahari).
[^33]: The author signs his padas and books with both names; cf. "Ghanashyama" in the signatures of the songs at BR 15.70 and 15.90.
[^34]: "Detached" renders *udāsīna*, one who has renounced home. The second line is spoken in humility: the author regards himself as still attached to the world.
[^35]: A play on words: *Bhakti-ratnakara* means "the Ocean of the Jewels of Bhakti"; the author asks to be immersed in this ocean, that is, in bhakti, and not only in his book.
