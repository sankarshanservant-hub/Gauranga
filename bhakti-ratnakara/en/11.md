# Taranga 11. Ishvari Jahnava's Journey to Vrindavana and Her Return

*Narottama's kirtan at Kheturi; Jahnava's journey to Vrindavana; the pashandas, Chandi and the Yavana robbers; the place of Akrura; Jahnava in Vrindavana: the Gosvamis' books, the circuit of the forests, Radha-kunda; the gift of the Gopala-virudavali; the return; Kheturi and Budhari, the marriage of Bara Gangadasa and Hemalata; the old brahmana's account of Nityananda; Ekachakra, Maudeshvara; Jahnava at Shrikhanda; Srinivasa returns home*

**1.** Glory to Lord Gaurachandra, the lord of the lives of the devotees!
Glory, glory to Nityananda, the refuge of those who have no refuge!

**2.** Glory to Sri Advaita Acharya, worshiped in the world!
Glory to Gadadhara, glory to Srivasa Pandita!

**3.** Glory to Sanatana and Rupa, the abode of rasa!
Glory to Lokanatha and Sri Gopala, full of prema!

**4.** Glory to Srinivasa, Narottama, Ramachandra!
Glory, glory to the devotees of Sri Gaurachandra!

**5.** Glory, glory to the listeners, the abode of virtues!
Now listen with kind hearts to what I shall tell.

**6.** In the village of Sri Kheturi there was a great, great festival;
the news of all this spread through all lands.

**7.** When the festival was over, the people from other lands
all went back to their own homes.

**8.** In the village of Sri Kheturi there is no end of people;
all those fortunate ones are immersed in bhakti-rasa.

**9.** Seeing the Lords — Gauranga, Vallavikanta and the others —
the people in their joy forget themselves.

**10.** All bring various things, counting it their good fortune;
the pujaris joyfully offer [them] to the Lords.

**11.** The order and rules of the service of the Sri Lords —
what do I know, to tell of them? By it the heart is soothed.

**12.** All the people who come for darshana
cannot bring themselves to leave the Lord's courtyard.

**13.** Full of prema, the Lord's courtyard is enchanting;
Narottama revealed the glory of that courtyard.

**14.** Who can understand Narottama's heart?
He is always grey with the dust of the Lord's courtyard.

**15.** By the varieties of song, dance and music he himself created
the Gandharvas are astonished — what comparison could be given for it?

**16.** *Thus [it is said] in the Stavamrita-lahari:*
*"To him whose body is adorned with abundant dust, shining from his falls to the ground in swoons of bliss,*
*whom one may see only by great good fortune — to that Srila Narottama, obeisances, obeisances;*[^1]

**17.** *to him who by his own dance, which crushes the pride of the Gandharvas, astonished the whole host of the skilled,*
*who is renowned for songs composed by himself — to that Srila Narottama, obeisances, obeisances."*

**18.** With dear Ramachandra, with Gokula and the others,
he always tastes various rasas in sankirtana.

**19.** A full-moon night, the full moon had risen —
I shall tell how on that day he tasted rasa.

**20.** First, manifesting the wondrous nectar of music,
he sings, immersed in the rasa of the rasa-lila.

**21.** The demigods and others are enchanted by the varieties of song and music;
the crest-jewel of the connoisseurs of song cannot remain composed.

**22.** Suddenly all four quarters became bright;
like lightning in a cloud, a pure radiance shone forth.

**23.** Moment by moment a wondrous fragrance spreads;
the sound of anklets and kinkini bells does not cease.

**24.** Thus [it] took place unseen at the place of the sankirtana;
when [it] vanished, all fell unconscious.

**25.** Ramachandra and Narottama are awash in tears;
Devidasa, Gokula and the others roll on the ground.

**26.** This is the supramundane lila of Krishna with His Beloved;
knowing [this], by Krishna's will all became composed.

**27.** Narottama and Ramachandra, abodes of virtue,
sitting in seclusion, relish the deeds of Krishna.[^2]

**28.** Sri Jahnava Ishvari thinks about setting out;
outwardly she shows composure, but within she is restless.

**29.** All that Ishvari did on the way to Vrindavana —
all of that I cannot describe in detail.

**30.** Nevertheless I shall tell a little, heard from the mouths of saints;
by Ishvari's gift of bhakti this earth is blessed.

**31.** One day, going into a certain large village,
Ishvari wished to stay right there.

**32.** In that village she stayed that day;
seeing the ways of the people, she thinks of the people's welfare.

**33.** The people of that village were great pashandas, invincible;
they mock the conduct of the Vaishnavas beyond measure.[^3]

**34.** At the time of evening the great bhagavatas
reverently bow at the feet of Sri Ishvari.

**35.** Seeing that, the pashandas say with a laugh:
"This is a brahmana's wife — so it seems to me."

**36.** Someone says, "These fellows have no sense at all:
they bow to a human being, but to a deity they do not bow."[^4]

**37.** Someone says, "If Chandi shows mercy, then it will come about."
Someone says, "How can the ignorant understand Chandi's mercy?"

**38.** "A brahmana's wife, a brahmana — do they not bow to Chandi?
These fellows have committed an offense at Chandi's door."[^5]

**39.** Having said this, laughing and laughing, the crowd of pashandas
went to Chandi's temple and began to boast.

**40.** Bowing to Chandi, they say again and again:
"Tonight you will destroy these fellows.

**41.** If they worship [your] feet with body, mind and speech,
then, protecting [them], you will give [them] shelter at [your] feet."

**42.** Having said this, all the pashandas went home;
lying down to rest, all fell asleep.

**43.** At the words of the pashandas Chandi was filled with wrath;
her lips tremble, both her eyes turned blood-red.

**44.** In the guise of a dream, taking a very sharp sword in her hand,
she speaks to the pashandas, angrily upbraiding [them]:

**45.** "You there, you pashandas! [Your] grief cannot be averted:[^6]
today I shall cut off the heads of all of you.

**46.** Drunk with pride, you have destroyed yourselves:
you have reviled the bhagavatas, who are worshipable by all.

**47.** She whom you called a brahmana's wife and held in contempt —
O wicked pashandas, you do not know her truth.

**48.** She is to be borne on my head, she is worshiped by all:
she is the wife of Nityananda-Balaramachandra.

**49.** 'Jahnava Ishvari' — the name is exceedingly sweet;
by taking this name the fear of worldly existence goes away.

**50.** The beloved of Lord Nityananda, the embodiment of compassion,
by her own goodness she distributes prema-bhakti to living beings.

**51.** Who does not always worship her two lotus feet?
All sing her glory; she removes the threefold misery.

**52.** *Thus [it is said]:*
*'I worship Sri Jahnava Ishvari, the beloved of Nityananda,*
*the bestower of the jewel of prema-bhakti, the remover of the threefold misery.'*[^7]

**53.** If she shows grace to all of you,
then there will be welfare; otherwise ruin awaits you.

**54.** If you take shelter of them all, you will be saved;
otherwise no one will escape my hand."

**55.** Having said this, she vanished from them all;
they woke from sleep and, in fear, tremble without end.

**56.** Reproaching themselves, in the morning, distressed,
they fall at the feet of the mahantas, rolling [on the ground].

**57.** Wet with tears, they say again and again:
"We have committed an offense; protect us!

**58.** Your journey by this road is for the deliverance of pashandas;
remove [our] ill fate — we have taken shelter.

**59.** Ishvari is pleased when you are pleased;
you have the power to give and to take away bhakti for her feet.

**60.** What power have we to know her truth?
That we say even this much is only her mercy.

**61.** Otherwise, would we have such understanding?
We have surrendered ourselves at those feet — this is certain.

**62.** Everyone knows that we are pashandas, asuras;
if you deliver the wicked, your fair fame will be proclaimed."

**63.** Having said this, they bow to the ground again and again;
seeing [this], the prabhus showed them mercy.[^8]

**64.** Sri Ishvari showed great favor;
the hearts of the pashandas were filled with joy.

**65.** Staying in that village some two or four days,
she set out, having blessed the pashandas.

**66.** All the pashandas became immersed in bhakti-rasa;
whoever kept company with them also became full of bhakti.

**67.** Thus one day, near a certain village,
they stayed at a beautiful place on the bank of a river.

**68.** In that village there were two robbers, invincible Yavanas;[^9]
sitting in seclusion, they speak to their own men:

**69.** "These Gaudiyas have many jewels;[^10]
I shall take all of that — get ready, and be careful."

**70.** Taking various weapons, all quickly got ready;
first, to learn the situation, they sent a spy.

**71.** The spy came and says, "Having performed nama-sankirtana,
all the Gaudiyas have now gone to sleep.

**72.** The night has come almost to the second prahara;[^11]
if we go now, the deed will succeed — so I think."

**73.** Hearing [this], the robber chief, in a most fearsome guise,
taking his men, goes with joy in his heart.

**74.** They go very swiftly to get there,
[but] a very short road becomes endless.

**75.** Because of their evil minds they could understand nothing;
as they went on and on, the night turned to dawn.

**76.** Seeing that the night had turned to dawn, fear seized their minds;
the robber chief says to his followers:

**77.** "Look, all of you — what an impossible thing has happened!
We are right where we set out from.

**78.** It seemed as if we were going to the Gaudiyas —
that was only an illusion: we have walked all night till we dropped.

**79.** Moment by moment this dread grows in my heart:
through the wrath of the Gaudiya Gosani all will be ruined.

**80.** Therefore all of you, heed my words:
from today, all of you, give up the robbers' trade.

**81.** We have committed many sins — there is no end to them;
there is no escape for us from the torments of Yama.

**82.** Come, come, let us go before the Gaudiya Gosani:
she will surely show us mercy."

**83.** Saying this, they cast off the robbers' guise
and in distress went to where Ishvari was.

**84.** As soon as they beheld the mahantas,
the robbers' hearts became supremely pure.

**85.** Falling on the ground and weeping, all of them
in utter distress humbly entreat:

**86.** "In this land we are known as wicked robbers;
show us mercy — let the world proclaim [your] fame."

**87.** Saying this, they can say nothing more;
streams of tears flow from their eyes, their hearts are distraught.

**88.** Seeing [this], Sri Ishvari felt compassion in her heart,
and together with [her] companions she showed mercy to the robbers.

**89.** The news of the robbers' deliverance spread everywhere.
How they went on from there I cannot describe.

**90.** After some days she entered Mathura;
seeing the city of Mathura, she was filled with boundless joy.

**91.** Honoring the Mathura brahmanas,
she bathed in the Yamuna at the Vishrama ghat.[^12]

**92.** Suddenly hearing of Ishvari's arrival,
the bhagavatas of Mathura quickly came.

**93.** At the sight of Ishvari they are drenched in streams of tears;
seeing the mahantas, they are overwhelmed at heart.

**94.** How they showed prema to one another —
the fortunate ones saw it to their eyes' fill.

**95.** The Mathura brahmanas, in great joy, at that very moment
sent news of [her] arrival to Vrindavana.

**96.** From there they took [her] to a wonderful lodging;
that day everyone stayed in Mathura.

**97.** Having had the darshan of Varaha and Keshava-deva,[^13]
in the morning she set out for Vrindavana.

**98.** All the Vaishnavas of Mathura go along [with her];
whoever sees that splendor overflows with joy.

**99.** All the Gosvamis, quickly, from Vrindavana
come in great joy to go out and receive [her].

**100.** Coming to Akrura's place, everyone sees[^14]
Ishvari approaching, very close by.

**101.** Seeing from afar the Gosvamis approaching,
Ishvari says to Sri Parameshvari Dasa:

**102.** "Here come all these bhagavatas;
let me hear which of them is called by what name."

**103.** Sri Parameshvari Dasa, at Ishvari's command,
makes them known, pointing with his finger, in a soft voice:

**104.** "This is Sri Gopala Bhatta, full of prema for Gaura;
this is Sri Bhugarbha, [this is] Lokanatha, the abode of virtues;

**105.** Krishnadasa Brahmachari; this is Krishna Pandita;
Sri Madhu Pandita; and this is the renowned Sri Jiva."[^15]

**106.** Thus he made known the names and deeds of all;
hearing this, Ishvari's great joy increased.

**107.** Coming up to Ishvari, all the Gosvamis
in supreme joy bowed down to the ground.

**108.** Jahnava Ishvari is prema-bhakti embodied,
[yet] she considers herself lowly — who can understand her ways?

**109.** Beholding the Gosvamis' expressions of prema,
she, losing all composure at heart, did that

**110.** at which all the Gosvamis were filled with apprehension:
such are the extraordinary ways of Sri Bhakti-devi.[^16]

**111.** Krishnadasa Sarakhel, Madhava Acharya,
Sri Parameshvari Dasa and other great noble souls —

**112.** what the meeting of all the Gosvamis
with all of them was like — how can I, so worthless, describe it?

**113.** What they said to one another, absorbed in prema —
who, hearing all of that, could keep his composure?

**114.** Sri Parameshvari, with a joyful heart,
introduces the Acharya's disciples to all the Gosvamis.[^17]

**115.** With great affection he says: "His name is Govinda;
he is a vessel of bhakti-rasa, the life of all Vaishnavas.

**116.** He is the son of Chiranjiva Sena of Khanda,
the younger brother of dear Ramachandra."

**117.** Hearing this, Sri Gopala Bhatta and the others rejoiced
and embraced [him], showing great affection.

**118.** The affection and mercy they showed on being introduced
to Bhagavan Kaviraja and the others cannot be told.

**119.** Everyone, going to Akrura's place,
had the darshan of the Sri Deity Gopinatha.

**120.** Before Sri Ishvari, Sri Jiva humbly says:
"This place of Akrura is very secluded.

**121.** Because of the crowds of people the Lord, not staying in Vrindavana,
would come here and, at this very place, accept alms."[^18]

**122.** Hearing this, Sri Ishvari, drenched in tears,
heaved a deep sigh and bows down to that place.

**123.** The bhagavatas, losing their composure, bow down,[^19]
remembering the extraordinary pastimes of the Lord.

**124.** All go on, leading Sri Ishvari in front;
on entering Vrindavana, [all] felt great bliss.

**125.** What Jahnava Ishvari became on seeing the beauty of Vrindavana —
that I cannot describe.

**126.** To the place where Sri Jiva had arranged a lodging beforehand
Jahnava Ishvari went together with everyone.

**127.** How everyone settled in the lodging,
and what happiness spread — I cannot tell it in detail.

**128.** Who knows the efforts of the Vaishnavas engaged in the service
of Sri Govinda, Gopinatha and Madanamohana?

**129.** All of them, having completed the service of the Sri Lord,
came to the lodging and had the darshan of Ishvari.

**130.** There was a meeting with Krishnadasa Sarakhel and all the others —
what bliss of prema [there was] in their hearts!

**131.** Hundreds upon hundreds of the residents of Vraja, women and men,
come for darshan — who can tell how ardent their longing is?

**132.** Sri Gopala Bhatta and all the others, taking leave,
went to their lodgings, leaving Sri Jiva behind.

**133.** Sri Jiva remained near Ishvari —
he who is supremely skilled in arranging all things.

**134.** Sri Gopala Bhatta, Lokanatha and the others
after some time came to where Sri Ishvari [was].

**135.** Seeing the Gosvamis, Ishvari joyfully
said in a soft voice, "I shall go for darshan."

**136.** Hearing Ishvari's words, all, with great joy in their hearts,
went with Ishvari for darshan.

**137.** Sri Govinda, Gopinatha, Madanamohana,
Sri Radha-vinoda and Sri Radha-ramana,[^20]

**138.** Radha-damodara — on beholding them all,
what absorption in prema [there was] — who can describe it?

**139.** The various garments and ornaments that had been brought along
she offered, all of them, everywhere.

**140.** Considering herself lowly, the bhakti she displayed —
I have no power to describe all that in detail.

**141.** Coming back to the lodging with everyone, Sri Ishvari
sat down in seclusion, having everyone sit down.

**142.** What the great festival in the village of Sri Kheturi was like —
all of that she made known through Madhava Acharya and others.

**143.** Hearing this, Lokanatha and all the Gosvamis,
attaining supreme bliss, float in the tears of prema.

**144.** And all the other things they spoke of with one another
I shall not describe, for fear of making the book too long.

**145.** So that [all] might hear the nectar of Govinda's poetry,
Sri Parameshvari Dasa made a humble request.

**146.** Hearing Govinda's most charming poetry,
everyone was filled with great joy at heart.

**147.** All say, "[He] deserves the fame of a kaviraja,"
and praise him, calling him "Sri Govinda Kaviraja."[^21]

**148.** At this Sri Ishvari was greatly delighted at heart;
what can I say — what bliss there is in Vrindavana every day!

**149.** The news of Ishvari's arrival spread everywhere;
the wise were immersed in supreme bliss.

**150.** Sri Dasa Gosani, who lives at Radhika-kunda,
rejoiced on hearing it, but had no strength to go.[^22]

**151.** In separation from Sri Rupa his heart is ever restless;
without food and the like his body has grown extremely thin.

**152.** How he keeps his vows, what stirrings are in his heart —
whose heart does not break on seeing all this?

**153.** Krishnadasa Kaviraja and many others,
bowing down [to him], humbly asked [leave] to go.

**154.** Gopala, Raghava Pandita and others together
set out from Nandishvara, Govardhana and other places.[^23]

**155.** All of them, having had the darshan of Ishvari in Vrindavana,
conveyed Dasa Gosvami's humble message.

**156.** What took place in the heart of Sri Jahnava Ishvari —
who has the power to tell it in detail?

**157.** To Sri Gopala Bhatta and all the Gosvamis
she announced, "In the morning I shall go to Sri Kunda."

**158.** All say, "Having had the darshan of Sri Kunda and the other places,
please come back here soon.

**159.** When your fatigue has been well relieved,
then you may go to wander through the forests."

**160.** Hearing this, Sri Ishvari, with a joyful heart,
set out for Sri Kunda, surrounded by the wise.

**161.** She went to Sri Kunda by way of Bahulavana;
seeing the beauty of the kunda, her heart overflows with prema.

**162.** Where Raghunatha Dasa Gosvami dwells —
this was in her mind: to go and see him there.

**163.** Sri Dasa Gosvami, on the secluded bank of the kunda,
slowly, slowly performs the chanting of the holy name and the rest.

**164.** Krishnadasa Kaviraja, having come on ahead,
stood before Dasa Gosvami.

**165.** Finding a moment, he submits:
"Sri Jahnava Ishvari has arrived."

**166.** He heard — and what wondrous prema pervaded [his] heart!
He goes forward to meet her, both eyes filled with tears.

**167.** Sri Ishvari sees Dasa Gosvami coming:
his body extremely thin, his radiance like the sun.

**168.** Who can understand Ishvari's heart?
Tears stream from both eyes — she cannot hold them back.

**169.** When Sri Dasa Gosvami, mustering composure, bowed down,
Sri Ishvari, full of prema, did what was fitting.

**170.** What Dasa Gosani says before Sri Ishvari —
hearing it, whose heart would not break?

**171.** At the meeting with everyone — Madhava Acharya and the others —
wondrous prema surges up in the heart.

**172.** What wondrous streams of tears in everyone's eyes!
Only after some time did all grow calm.

**173.** The people of Arit-grama, the Vrajavasis —
all rejoiced at seeing Ishvari.

**174.** Staying three or four days at Sri Radha-kunda,
she cooked with the greatest care.

**175.** Offering bhoga to Krishna with a jubilant heart,
she fed all the Vrajavasi Vaishnavas.

**176.** The bliss and the dawning of prema in honoring the prasada —
who would not long to see that time?

**177.** The extraordinary ways of Sri Jahnava Ishvari —
what can I understand? A wretch like me has no reach of mind.

**178.** One day at midday on the bank of the kunda,
hearing the sound of the flute, she could not stay composed.

**179.** She saw a wonder hidden from others;
to the wise I shall set out this charming episode in full.

**180.** Still, I tell a little: Ishvari, in delight,
hearing the sound of the flute, looks around on all four sides.

**181.** Beneath a kadamba she sees glossy-dark Shyama
in His threefold-bent pose, surpassing millions of Kandarpas.

**182.** Smiling gently, He plays the sweet flute —
who can keep composure when by it He maddens the world?

**183.** Sri Radhika, with the sakhis headed by Lalita,
had surrounded Shyamala-sundara in great merriment.

**184.** Seeing that wondrous beauty, Jahnava Ishvari
fell into a swoon — in such a way as I cannot describe.

**185.** After some time, regaining consciousness, she grew calm;
this play [seen] in solitude she did not reveal to others.

**186.** She was to go to see Sri Govardhana and other places;
this she made known to Dasa Gosvami beforehand.

**187.** Sri Dasa Gosvami, falling to the ground and bowing down,
gave his leave, immersed in humility.

**188.** Hearing of that humility, whose heart would not break?
What shall I say of what took place in Ishvari's heart?

**189.** Amid her maidservants and others, Jahnava Ishvari
went slowly, slowly from the kunda to Govardhana.

**190.** On seeing Govardhana, Manasi-ganga and other places —
that absorption in prema, what comparison can be given for it?

**191.** Madhava Acharya and the others lost their composure;
Sri Jiva Gosvami and the others calmed everyone.

**192.** So too the absorption in prema on seeing Nandagrama and other places —
with one mouth I cannot describe even a trace of it.

**193.** Sri Ishvari, surrounded by the Sri Bhagavatas,
came to Vrindavana in a very few days.

**194.** Sri Govinda, Gopinatha, Madanamohana —
these three she beheld in great bliss.

**195.** Having beheld Sri Radha-vinoda and Sri Radha-ramana,
she came back to her lodging with a joyful heart.

**196.** Sometimes, carefully cooking rice, vegetables and the like,
Sri Ishvari feeds Sri Govinda-deva.

**197.** Sometimes, cooking rice and various vegetables,
in great bliss she has Gopinatha eat.

**198.** Sometimes, quickly cooking [dishes] in various ways,
with such longing she feeds Madanamohana.

**199.** Radha-damodara, and Sri Radha-ramana,
and Radha-vinoda she had eat.

**200.** How she fed the Vaishnavas with Sri prasada —
the bliss that arose, who can describe it?

**201.** Her heart was eager to hear the Gosani's books,
and Sri Jiva Gosvami had her hear them.

**202.** Hearing the *Brihad-bhagavatamrita* and other [books],[^24]
she was overwhelmed — from prema she could not stay composed.

**203.** [She] is attentive to the supremely rare angas of bhakti;
seeing those deeds, whose soul would not be soothed?

**204.** Some days later, from Vrindavana,
she set out with everyone to wander through the forests.

**205.** Madhu, Tala, Kumuda, Bahula, Kamyavana,
Khadira, Bhadra, Bhandira, Shri, the forest of Loha,[^25]

**206.** Mahavana, Vrindavana — in these twelve forests
the fortunate saw the manifestation of prema.

**207.** Still, I tell a little, in the delight of my heart:
Ishvari went near Govardhana.

**208.** Close to Govardhana hill, in deep solitude,
Sri Jahnava Ishvari reflects within herself:

**209.** "Here the two brothers, each with His own beloveds,
sport in springtime in great merriment."

**210.** Thinking thus, Sri Ishvari could not stay composed;
again and again she gazes at the place of the spring pastimes.

**211.** Suddenly the Sri spring rasa came into her view —
the pastimes of both, each with His own beloveds.

**212.** The son of Rohini, with His own beloveds,
plays phagu games and the like in many playful ways.

**213.** Krishna, the son of Yashoda, the abode of rasa,
sports merrily with His own beloveds.

**214.** The phagu play and the rest — who can tell what it was like?
No comparison can be given for that wondrous beauty.

**215.** Seeing such a pastime that enchants the worlds,
she falls to the ground in a swoon.

**216.** After some time she grew calm — she told no one;
with joy in her heart she went on from there.

**217.** The bliss at Rama-ghat I cannot describe:
thus did Ishvari behold the Lord of her own life.[^26]

**218.** Self-forgetful in absorption in prema, in that solitude,
she contemplates within her heart the rasa dance of Sri Rama.[^27]

**219.** Her limbs grew helpless, she heaves long sighs;
suddenly the Sri rasa pastime came into her view.

**220.** With His most expert beloveds
Balai sports in the merriment of dance, song and the rest.

**221.** Seeing that beauty, she swooned in bliss;
after some time, coming to herself, she looks around on all four sides.

**222.** The feeling in her heart others did not know;
with everyone she went on from Rama-ghat.

**223.** On the bank of the Yamuna she enters a village;
seeing the living beings in sorrow, she shows compassion there.

**224.** In that village lives a guileless brahmana;
in his old age a wondrous son was born to him.

**225.** At the age of pauganda that son died;[^28]
rolling on the ground, the brahmana began to weep.

**226.** Holding the dead son in her lap, his mother weeps;
at the weeping of the two, wood and stone melt.

**227.** Sri Jahnava Ishvari, at the weeping of the two,
her heart softened with compassion, could not stay composed.

**228.** She wishes to touch the brahmana's dead son;
"Do not touch the dead son," says his mother.

**229.** Ishvari says, "You are a Vrajavasi;
I shall become pure by touching your son."

**230.** Saying this, as she laid her hand on the dead son's head,
the child, regaining consciousness, looks around on all four sides.

**231.** Bowing to Sri Jahnava's lotus feet,
the boy rose — everyone rejoiced.

**232.** The brahmana and his wife, falling at her feet, say,
"By your merciful glance you have revived our dead son."

**233.** Ishvari says, "Seeing the grief of you both,
Krishna revived the son — what part is mine in this?"

**234.** Thus showing compassion in many places,
she came with everyone and entered Vrindavana.

**235.** Remembering the Lord's command at Khardaha,[^29]
she resolved in her mind to go quickly to Gauda.

**236.** One day, going before Sri Gopinatha,
she stands gazing at Radha and Gopinatha.

**237.** With great amusement she reflects within herself:
"If Sri Radhika were a little taller, it would be good."

**238.** Thinking this, she said nothing to anyone;
having seen the shayana-arati, she came back to her lodging.

**239.** In the guise of a dream, Gopinatha, revealing Himself,
speaks sweet words to Sri Jahnava:

**240.** "My beloved is not as tall as I am;
you were amused, seeing this disproportion.

**241.** Going to Gauda, quickly manifest the beloved and send [her here];
she will take her place on [My] left — you will see this too."[^30]

**242.** Sri Radhika, smiling, says to Jahnava:
"Do not be shy: this is my desire too."

**243.** Having said so much, They both vanished;
when her sleep broke, she looks joyfully in all four directions.

**244.** Seeing that night had turned to dawn, with delight in her heart
she graciously speaks to Nayana Bhaskara:

**245.** "Contemplate Gopinatha without cease:
you are to fashion [His] Beloved."[^31]

**246.** Nayana understood what was in Ishvari's mind;
how he would fashion [her] — this he firmly settled in his heart.

**247.** Ishvari kept all these matters secret;
Gopinatha revealed this through another.

**248.** The ways of Sri Gopinatha cannot be understood:
in the guise of a dream He gave Jahnava a flower garland.

**249.** What wonders [took place] with Sri Govinda and Madanamohana —
some fortunate person will relate that in detail.

**250.** Sri Ishvari is to go to Sri Gauda-mandala —
all the Gosvamis fixed [the time of] the journey.

**251.** The word spread everywhere: "Jahnava Ishvari
will soon go to Sri Gauda-mandala."

**252.** Wherever the Vaishnavas were living in seclusion,
they all quickly came to Vrindavana.

**253.** Sri Ishvari was taking leave everywhere,
and, thinking of this, she was in deep distress of heart.

**254.** At the sight of Sri Govinda, Gopinatha and Madanamohana
wondrous tears stream from her eyes.

**255.** Seeing Sri Radha-vinoda, and Radha-damodara as well,
and Radha-ramana, she lost her composure without restraint.

**256.** What did she say within herself, seeing Gopishvara?[^32]
Vrinda-devi and all [the others] she sees, place by place.

**257.** Raghunatha Bhatta, Pandita Kashishvara,
Gosvami Sri Sanatana, Rupa the most learned —

**258.** seeing the samadhis of these four,
she weeps, and her heart breaks with grief.

**259.** When she saw the samadhi of Gauridasa Pandita,
streams [of tears] flowed from her eyes — she cannot hold them back.

**260.** I do not know what wonder she saw there
and what she said again and again to Bara Gangadasa.

**261.** She grew calm at the words of Bara Gangadasa;
of who he is I shall relate a little here.

**262.** Bhadravati was the name of Sri Jahnava's mother,
the wholly devoted wife of Suryadasa.[^33]

**263.** Seeing what her bhakti was like, all were amazed;
Gangadasa is the son of her elder sister.

**264.** A loving disciple of Gauridasa Pandita,
after the Pandita's departure he nearly lost his life.

**265.** As the Pandita commanded him in the guise of a dream,
so he soon arrived in Vrindavana.

**266.** At Sri Dhira-samira, near his own Lord,
he serves the Lord and lives in seclusion.

**267.** While wandering through Govardhana and other places,
he suddenly heard of Sri Jahnava's arrival.

**268.** Coming to Vrindavana, he received the darshan of Ishvari.
[Thus] the account of Gangadasa has been told in brief.

**269.** When Sri Ishvari was taking leave everywhere,
someone gave [her] a Sri Deity together with [His] Beloved.

**270.** Having received the incomparable Deity, with joy in her heart
she entrusted the service to Bara Gangadasa.

**271.** She showed Bara Gangadasa great favor
and made known that she would take [him] with her.

**272.** "At dawn I will set out for Gauda" —
and everyone was thrown into deep distress.

**273.** Sri Govinda Kaviraja with his fellow disciples
went with all attention before the Gosvamis.

**274.** Bowing again and again at the feet of all,
at the leave-taking they shed streams of tears.

**275.** Sri Gopala Bhatta embraced Govinda;
who, hearing what he said, could keep his composure?

**276.** Lokanatha Gosvami, with affection for Govinda,
softly, softly tells him what to say to Narottama:

**277.** "Be attentive in the service of the Sri Deity,
honoring the Vaishnavas with body, mind and speech.

**278.** Observe with care the holy days of Vishnu and the Vaishnavas;
together with Ramachandra relish bhakti-rasa.

**279.** Give this message to Srinivasa."
Having said this, he could say nothing more.

**280.** What Bhugarbha Gosvami told him to say
to Narottama and Srinivasa cannot be retold.

**281.** Sri Jiva says with affection: "What more is there to say?
Give everyone my loving embraces.

**282.** So that I may, as it were, see Srinivasa Acharya,
from time to time I will send him letters.

**283.** The nectar of songs that you have composed — send [it to me];
and send again whatever more you compose."

**284.** Having said this, he gave [him] the *Gopala-virudavali* —
the book he composed at Sri Rupa's command in a dream.[^34]

**285.** Krishnadasa Kaviraja and the other learned ones,
saying many things, embraced Govinda.

**286.** To Bhagavan Kaviraja and all the others
they showed their love with the closest embraces.[^35]

**287.** Having taken leave, all went to their lodgings;
the night passed with hearts in deep distress.

**288.** All the Gosvamis, keeping their composure with great effort,
came quickly to where Jahnava Ishvari was.

**289.** All the people of Vraja, whether women or men,
came; no one's mind was at rest.

**290.** How distressed Sri Ishvari became, with Krishnadasa, Madhava and others —
this I cannot tell.

**291.** At an auspicious moment she sets out from Vrindavana for Gauda,
surrounded by maha-bhagavatas.

**292.** Coming to Akrura's place, Jahnava Ishvari
was overwhelmed, beholding the beauty of Vrindavana.

**293.** There Sri Ishvari takes leave
of all the Gosvamis, bathed in tears.

**294.** Sri Bhatta Gosvami and the others could not remain composed;[^36]
they took leave in a way I cannot describe.

**295.** At the time of parting, all the people of Vraja,
telling of Sri Jahnava's virtues, weep.

**296.** All the noble souls [who were] with Sri Ishvari
were in deep distress at parting from one another.

**297.** Sri Jiva Gosvami and the others, unable to control their hearts,
came with Sri Ishvari to Mathura.

**298.** That day, resting in Mathura,
she paid great honor to the brahmanas of Mathura.

**299.** Having taken leave with care of Sri Jiva and all,
she set out from there, and her heart was torn.

**300.** Within some days, entering Sri Gauda-mandala,
she came near the village of Sri Kheturi.

**301.** The news of Ishvari's coming spread everywhere;
from all four directions people ran to see.

**302.** Ramachandra and Narottama with their people —
all came forward joyfully to receive [her].

**303.** Receiving the darshan of Sri Jahnava Ishvari,
they bow again and again, rolling on the ground.

**304.** Seeing Narottama and Ramachandra with their people,
Sri Ishvari showed [them] great favor.

**305.** Narottama and Ramachandra, full of bhakti-rasa,
bow in great bliss to all the mahantas.

**306.** All, seeing Ramachandra and Narottama,
did what was fitting, overwhelmed with love.

**307.** Sri Santosha Datta and the others, floating in tears of love,
offered obeisance, rolling on the ground.

**308.** Sri Govinda Kaviraja and all the others
bow at the feet of Ramachandra and Narottama.

**309.** The mutual joy there was at that time —
can it be told with one mouth?

**310.** Surrounded by the Vaishnavas, Jahnava Ishvari
quickly entered the village of Sri Kheturi.

**311.** Going with almost weightless steps into the Lord's courtyard,
she bowed, and the darshan of the Lord soothed her heart.

**312.** Staying a while in the courtyard with all,
she went to her former lodging and rested.

**313.** All the mahantas had separate lodgings;
everything was ready there that each one needed.

**314.** Beforehand, in supreme bliss, Sri Santosha Raya
had placed various provisions in the lodgings.

**315.** And again he carefully brought various things
and appointed many people to serve.

**316.** Supreme bliss pervaded the village of Kheturi;
with people coming and going, even the pathless ground became a road.

**317.** Seeing Ishvari, seeing the mahantas,
who is doing what — no one remembers.

**318.** Together with Ramachandra, Sri Thakura Mahashaya
earnestly entreats the mahantas:

**319.** "Whatever desire has arisen in Santosha's mind —
please bathe quickly and fulfill it."

**320.** At once [they] went before Sri Ishvari and told [her];
everyone quickly bathed and was refreshed.

**321.** Very white, dry, washed new garments
Santosha with satisfaction offered to all.[^37]

**322.** Showing favor to Santosha, everyone
put on the garments with minds full of supreme bliss.

**323.** How everyone applied tilaka and performed the other rites —
whose heart would not be soothed seeing all that?

**324.** Sri Jahnava Ishvari, her heart full of supreme joy,
completed her bath and other rites in private.

**325.** To the brahmana women — Ishvari's attendants and others —
to all of them she herself gave garments to wear.

**326.** What more can be said of the good fortune of Sri Santosha Datta,
whose garments Ishvari, together with all, put on?

**327.** Ishvari was about to go for the darshana of Shyama-raya;
at that very moment Narottama and Ramachandra came.

**328.** The Deity that [she] had brought from Vrindavana —
His name is Shyama-raya; what comparison can be given for His beauty?

**329.** Bara Gangadasa, having completed His service,[^38]
came before Jahnava Ishvari and informed [her].

**330.** Taking Ramachandra and Narottama with her, Ishvari
bowed down and gazed at that beauty, filling her eyes.

**331.** Narottama and Ramachandra, at a single glance,
were overwhelmed with prema and could not regain their composure.

**332.** After beholding Shyama-raya for some time,
Ishvari came back to her own place, taking the two with her.

**333.** Again, with everyone, [she] went to Gauranga's courtyard
and, bowing to the Lords, came back to her lodging.

**334.** The Lord's pujaris, with joy in their hearts,
quickly brought a great deal of prasada.

**335.** Fruits, roots, sweets and other prasada
Sri Ishvari carefully served to the bhagavatas.

**336.** Having fed everyone, Ishvari ate a little [herself].
In the same way she carefully served [all] rice and other dishes.

**337.** After resting for some time with everyone,
Ishvari sat down, her mind full of supreme bliss.

**338.** She looked toward Narottama and Ramachandra;[^39]
as [she] told of Vraja, [her] heart welled up.

**339.** Holding herself steady, [she] told everything from beginning to end;
[then] Ishvari asks for news of Gauda.

**340.** Hearing [this], Narottama cannot say anything;
streams [of tears] flow from both his eyes — he cannot hold [them] back.

**341.** Ramachandra says: "The Lord's dear ones,
in this short time, have almost all gone into concealment.

**342.** Those who still remain are also all but hidden from sight."
Saying this, Ramachandra was distraught at heart.

**343.** Ishvari says: "Just as it has happened here,
I do not know what may be happening there in the meantime.

**344.** Everywhere the Lord will bring darkness."
As she said this, streams of tears flowed from her eyes.

**345.** What can be said? No one had a trace of composure left;
hearts were breaking, and [it] could not be held back at all.

**346.** After a while, becoming steady by the Lord's will,
all became absorbed in the Lord's pastimes.

**347.** At the time of dusk, going to the Lord's courtyard,
[she] beholds the evening arati with great joy of heart.

**348.** The enchanting Sri nama-kirtana begins;
hearing [it], Ishvari loses all composure in her heart.

**349.** The manifestation of prema [that took place] — I cannot describe it.
Six dandas of the night passed in nama-kirtana.[^40]

**350.** Coming back to the lodging, all sat on their seats;
Ramachandra came bringing prasada.

**351.** Though there was no hunger, still everyone
partook of the prasada with delight.

**352.** Sri Ishvari drank a little milk;
the attendants and others eat whatever each one wishes.

**353.** Weary from the journey, all lay down [to rest];
Ramachandra and Narottama went back to their own place.

**354.** Sri Govinda Kaviraja, finding a private moment,
conveyed the words of all the Gosvamis.

**355.** He carefully handed over the book *Gopala-virudavali*;
Narottama took it and entrusted it to Ramachandra.

**356.** Narottama was greatly distraught at heart;
Sri Gosvami consoled him in a dream.[^41]

**357.** With great joy Mahashaya, at the break of day,
sent letters to Khardaha and Yajigrama.

**358.** In the village of Sri Kheturi Sri Jahnava Ishvari
stays in supreme bliss for three or four days.

**359.** Sri Govinda Kaviraja and a few others
went ahead to the village of Budhari.

**360.** Sri Ishvari will set out in the morning —
this news spread, and at it everyone was distraught.

**361.** That Ramachandra and Narottama would go with Ishvari —
this, too, everyone heard.

**362.** At the end of the night Sri Ishvari, with everyone,
having performed the morning duties, went to the Lord's courtyard.

**363.** Seeing the Lords — Gauranga, Vallavikanta and the others —
[she] is overwhelmed; tears stream from her eyes.

**364.** Saying something softly before the Lords,
[she] took her leave — prema surged in her heart.

**365.** All the mahantas were deeply distraught at heart;
how they took their leave — what do I know to tell it?

**366.** Narottama and Ramachandra took their leave;
[they] made everyone attentive in the Lord's service.

**367.** What Sri Santosha was to send with Ishvari
[he] entrusted to Sri Parameshvari Dasa.

**368.** From Kheturi everyone set out;
on all sides people run to see [them].

**369.** On the bank of the Padmavati Sri Ishvari, with everyone,
seeing the people's yearning, showed the people mercy.

**370.** She quickly crossed the Padmavati;
surrounded by everyone, she went to Budhari.

**371.** The news of [her] coming spread, and the people run
with a most wondrous yearning to see [her].

**372.** Sri Ishvari, with everyone, by an auspicious glance
immersed the people in the rasa of Sri bhakti.

**373.** As before, Ishvari entered [her] lodging;
Vamshidasa and the others took up all the tasks.

**374.** To Sri Vamshi's brother, Shyamadasa Chakravarti,[^42]
Ishvari, smiling, says something:

**375.** "What I shall ask of you, you will have to give.
It is very easy to obtain — do not worry in your heart."

**376.** Hearing [this], Shyamadasa gave no answer;
it had grown late in the night, [and he] went to his own house.

**377.** In his mind he reflects: "Of one as unworthy as I am,
what could she ask like this — what is there in my house?"

**378.** While he was reflecting thus, sleep drew him in;
the brahmana sees a dream as if it were real:

**379.** at Ishvari's command, with great joy of heart,
[he] gives his daughter to Sri Bara Gangadasa;

**380.** all the Vaishnavas praise [it] with great joy —
[then] the sleep broke; the brahmana cannot calm himself.

**381.** The brahmana Shyamadasa, becoming steady after a while,
went before Sri Ishvari at daybreak.

**382.** Understanding Ishvari's intent, in sweet words
[he] told his dream to Ishvari.

**383.** Preparations for the wedding were quickly begun;
Jahnava Ishvari was delighted.

**384.** Sri Ishvari says softly to Gangadasa:
"Shyamadasa will give you [his] daughter.

**385.** Preparations have been made — the wedding will take place today.
You will marry; do not worry about it."

**386.** Hearing the words "The wedding will be today,"
[he] kept silent, saying nothing.

**387.** [He was] supremely detached — no desire in his heart;
yet he could not transgress Ishvari's command.

**388.** At the time of the wedding all was most auspicious;
Shyamadasa Chakravarti was overwhelmed with joy.

**389.** Sri Shyamadasa's daughter, named Hemalata,[^43] —
young in age, golden-complexioned, virtuous.

**390.** Whoever sees her even once, his eyes are soothed.
Such a daughter [he] gave to Bara Gangadasa.

**391.** The beauty of Bara Gangadasa is boundless:
a radiance like the sun; [he is] full of the rasa of prema-bhakti.

**392.** Having had such a Gangadasa married,
Sri Ishvari gave [him] Shyama-raya, entrusting [Him to him].

**393.** Gangadasa reflects in his mind:
"How can the bhoga be arranged now?"

**394.** To Gangadasa, in a dream, Shyama-raya says:
"Whatever is obtained at any time — feed Me with that."

**395.** [He] told the dream before Ishvari;
hearing it, [she] with great care fixed the arrangement of the bhoga.

**396.** Bara Gangadasa became immersed in service;
at this everyone was supremely delighted.

**397.** Ramachandra and Narottama, together with Govinda and the others,
are constantly overwhelmed by the deeds of Sri Ishvari.

**398.** Ishvari, with everyone, from the village of Budhari
set out for Ekachakra, in the holy land of Rarh.[^44]

**399.** Seeing the village of Ekachakra from afar,
Sri Jahnava Ishvari cannot hold her heart steady.

**400.** Krishnadasa Sarakhel, the handsome Gauranga,
Madhava Acharya, Balarama, Mahidhara,[^45]

**401.** Murari Chaitanya, Krishnadasa, the best of brahmanas,
Nrisimha Chaitanya, Sri Kanai, Damodara,

**402.** Raghupati Vaidya, Upadhyaya Manohara,
Sri Parameshvari Dasa, an ocean of virtues,

**403.** Sri Nakadi Dasa, Sri Mukunda and all the others,
seeing Ekachakra, float in tears.

**404.** What became of Narottama, Ramachandra, Sri Govinda and the others —
that cannot be told.

**405.** They walk along the road toward Ekachakra;
by the roadside ashvattha and other trees are beautiful.

**406.** The shade is very dense, the place exceedingly clean;
a gentle breeze always blows, fragrant and cool.

**407.** As Sri Ishvari with everyone came to that place,
suddenly a great bliss arose in her heart.

**408.** Someone says something; no one's mind is steady.
On the road to Ekachakra they see a brahmana.

**409.** Remembering the past, he, with an agitated heart,
wanders about, looking at Nitai's places of pastimes.

**410.** Very old, a staff in his hand, with a slow gait,
coming beneath a tree, he looks at them all.

**411.** Seeing the Vaishnavas, he reflects in his mind:
"From where have they suddenly arrived?

**412.** My eyes are soothed at the sight of them all."
Thinking thus, he looks on, saying nothing.

**413.** Seeing the old brahmana, the wise ones bowed to him
and with care quickly gave him a seat.

**414.** Seeing the brahmana's most extraordinary manner,
they ask the brahmana in the sweetest words:

**415.** "We have heard that the village of Ekachakra is very extensive;
here we see it in ruins — what is the reason for this?"

**416.** Hearing this, the king among brahmanas says in the sweetest words:
What you have heard is not at all false.[^46]

**417.** The name of the village Ekachakra dates from long ago;
during their exile in the forest, the Pandavas and others lived here.[^47]

**418.** In this region there were wicked Rakshasas and Asuras;
the Pandavas sent them to the city of Yama.

**419.** The ancients say: this is a supremely holy place;
in this village many gods reside.

**420.** Ekachakreshvara Shiva together with Parvati —
on the bank of the river, exceedingly powerful, worshiped by the gods and others.

**421.** The Deities of Shesha, Ganesha and others were on the bank of the river;
by the power of time they all became hidden.[^48]

**422.** The stream of this river was formerly broad;
on both banks it was adorned with many creepers and trees.

**423.** On many flowers the bees hum incessantly;
many birds wander there — their sound is wonderful.

**424.** Many harmless animals roam in the forest;
seeing the beauty of the forest, who would not rejoice?

**425.** Who founded the village — a wondrous settlement!
Separately, each apart, the four varnas dwell.

**426.** In the village of Ekachakra the people are beyond counting;
every day there are supreme festivals here and there.

**427.** All are wealthy, very fond of pious deeds;
what to speak of the brahmanas — even those of other varnas are learned.

**428.** Here and there the various shastras are constantly discussed;
whose mind would not be soothed on hearing all that?

**429.** In which places and in which forms the Lord appears —
in such talks they delight one another.

**430.** Among them all, a crest-jewel of astrologers
speaks to everyone in the sweetest words:

**431.** "In Ayodhya, Mathura and the other dhamas the Lord
enjoys His pastimes; now He is not visible to this world.

**432.** This Ekachakra is the abode of the Lord;
here Lord Balarama will soon appear.[^49]

**433.** All will see it — it will become known to the world;
my life is short, I shall not get to see it.

**434.** Who is able to tell the glory of Ekachakra?"
Having said this, he said nothing more.

**435.** O my dear! All his words are the very truth;
the greatest of the wise put them to the test.

**436.** At the words "The Lord will soon be born," in everyone
an incomparable bliss increases incessantly.

**437.** More than what I heard I cannot tell;
how the village fell into ruin — what I saw, that I shall tell.

**438.** In this village there lived a pious brahmana;
I know him by the title "Ojha" — I do not remember his name.[^50]

**439.** The Ojha was very wealthy, experienced in every respect;
his affection for his yajamanas was boundlessly special.

**440.** All his rites were like those of the ancient rishis;
all the marks of a brahmana were manifest in him.

**441.** Although he was of the Sundaramala Bandyaghati gai,
still he was held the best and honored everywhere.[^51]

**442.** At a very young age I saw him;
of his life I heard from knowledgeable people.

**443.** That Ojha's wife, supremely virtuous,
though she bore sons, was unhappy:

**444.** of the sons that were born, none survived.
At last, at an auspicious moment, one son was born.

**445.** Looking at the son, the Ojha felt both joy and sorrow in his heart;
he entrusted the son to Parvati and Shankara.

**446.** The Ojha, after deliberating with his wife,
in sorrow gave the son the name "Hado."[^52]

**447.** Others with joyful hearts gave him other names;
who did not come to see such a boy?

**448.** Day by day the son grows, very handsome;
looking at him, the Ojha and his wife refresh their eyes.

**449.** The anna-prashana and the other rites he performed in due order, as was fitting;
the Ojha always delighted in his son's doings.

**450.** Seeing that the son had come of age for marriage,
he married him in this same village, not far away.

**451.** As was the son, so was the daughter-in-law Padmavati;
at the time of the wedding there was good repute everywhere.

**452.** The Ojha and his wife, joyfully earning merit,
after some days both passed to the other world.

**453.** Without father and mother, Hado was distressed at heart;
he spent much wealth on the rites for them both.

**454.** In all the shastras Hado Ojha became a pandita;
the name "Hadai Pandita" became known.

**455.** An unalloyed Vaishnava, a knower of the truth of devotion to Vishnu;
his wife, a supreme Vaishnavi, devoted to her husband.

**456.** Their life cannot be described;
one feels that they are the mother and father of the world.

**457.** Everyone praises them, seeing their most pure conduct;
both are very fond of the worship of Vishnu.

**458.** By the grace of Vishnu a wondrous child was born —
the eldest of all; I shall tell of His birth and the rest.

**459.** From the time Sri Padmavati conceived,
Hadai Pandita attained great bliss.

**460.** Glory, glory to Hadai Pandita, the best of brahmanas!
Glory to Padmavati, glory to her womb!

**461.** At a most auspicious moment, from Padmavati's womb
a boy was born — what comparison can be given for Him?

**462.** The pious women, looking at that boy,
bless Him, utterly overwhelmed.

**463.** Someone says: "He is not at all like a boy —
surely this is a doll of golden fresh butter."

**464.** Someone says: "I have never seen such a boy:
at the sight of Him the pain is gone, the eyes are soothed."

**465.** In this way they say many things to one another;
many people come and go at the pandita's house.

**466.** For the welfare of his son, the wise Hadai Pandita
joyfully gave away much wealth.

**467.** The life of Padmavati and Hadai is in their son;
day by day the son grows like the moon.

**468.** The mother's affection is boundless; everyone praises it;
she does not put her son down from her lap onto the ground.

**469.** At the name-giving and the other rites there was great bliss;
some say "Rama," some say "Nityananda."

**470.** Some, with joy in their hearts, speak some other name;
who can describe the happiness of the anna-prashana?

**471.** When He crawls about the courtyard on all fours,
everyone says, "Come here, Nitai!" and takes Him in their arms.

**472.** Climbing into their arms, He laughs — the beauty of His face is enchanting;
His two little teeth are lovely, like drops of milk.

**473.** No one can let Him go from their arms;
Nityananda became as if the life of all.

**474.** When His mother carefully sets Him on a seat,
He does not sit on the seat — nothing but dust pleases Him.

**475.** One day, when I had great sorrow at home,
I went to the pandita's house to see Nitai.

**476.** His body, grey with dust, was sweetly beautiful;
at a single glance all my sorrow went away.

**477.** As soon as I said, "Come here, little one!" He climbed into my arms;
I do not know into what ocean of bliss He plunged me.

**478.** Laughing, Nitai went into His father's arms;
the father's affection is impossible to describe.

**479.** If he set out on some business, he could not go:
turning back, he looked again and again at his son's face.

**480.** Sometimes, having gone to a yajamana's house and returned home,[^53]
he would look around in all directions, saying, "Where is Nityananda?"

**481.** Nitai would run up and climb into His father's arms —
and Hadai seemed to regain a life as if already lost.

**482.** Not even for half a moment could he let Him out of his sight;
and the mother's love was greater still — who can describe [it]?

**483.** For the sake of her son's beauty [the mother] rubs [Him] with turmeric —
the turmeric grows dull beside the radiance of His body.

**484.** To make [His skin] soft, she anoints [Him] with fragrant oil,
[yet] His charming body is fragrant and soft by nature.

**485.** Bathing [Him], she is overwhelmed with tenderness;
gently, gently she dries His body with a towel.

**486.** Dressing Him in a blue silk dhoti with a red border,
she says to her son, "Play sitting at home."

**487.** Smiling, Nitai says to His mother,
"How can I play without playmates?"

**488.** From that day children of His age
came [to Him] — how many, who could count?

**489.** Seeing them all, [He] was supremely delighted;
it was as if [they shared] a love of long ages.

**490.** Nityananda began His games:
He was supremely sensible, without even a trace of mischief.

**491.** From the kaumara age He entered pauganda;[^54]
day by day [His] games increased — endless and extraordinary.

**492.** I am now more than a hundred years old,
[yet] such wonderful games I have never seen or heard of.

**493.** Whatever pastimes Sri Krishna showed in whatever avataras —
apart from those, Nitaichand has no [other] games.

**494.** Before beginning any game,
He teaches the children, each one separately, to imitate [that pastime].

**495.** Look: here on this riverbank is a lovely place;
here Padmavati's son used to play.

**496.** How, through the prayers of the gods, soon
Vasudeva [Krishna] was born in Vasudeva's house;

**497.** how Vasudeva, in fear of Kamsa, taking [the infant] Vasudeva,
went to Nanda's home — this game He plays.

**498.** What the festival of Krishna's birth was like in Nanda's house,
how Yashoda forgets herself in tenderness;

**499.** how Krishna, while sucking milk, slew Putana;
how, lying [in His cradle], He broke the cart;

**500.** how the Lord slew Trinavarta —
these games He plays, and seeing [them] the heart is soothed.

**501.** [How] Krishna merrily buys fruit for grain;
how Yashoda binds [Him] to the mortar;

**502.** how He breaks the twin arjuna trees —
who would not be amazed at the sight of this game?

**503.** Putting on various costumes, in a great gathering of children
He enacts all of Krishna's pranks in Gokula.

**504.** Children become Baka and Agha; taking the form of Krishna,
He playfully fights them and slays them all.

**505.** Fashioning a fearsome serpent, He carries [it] into the water —
and plays the wondrous game of subduing Kaliya.

**506.** Sometimes He plays how Krishna slew Dhenuka;
sometimes in the pasture He enacts the pastime of slaying Pralamba.[^55]

**507.** How Krishna slays the bull-asura,
how, summoning the tirthas, He bathes;[^56]

**508.** how Krishna with His friends tends the cows,
how He returns home with the cows;

**509.** how, lifting Govardhana, He protects Vraja;
how He steals the gopis' garments;

**510.** how the wives of the [brahmanas] performing the sacrifice, and others, acted —
all this Padmavati's son plays.[^57]

**511.** How at Kamsa's command Akrura, coming to Vraja,
takes Rama and Krishna away to Mathura;

**512.** how the cowherds follow, riding in carts —
who is so steady as to keep composure at the sight of this game?

**513.** As the gopis weep in separation from Krishna —
what to say: just so does Nityananda weep.

**514.** With the children He plays at strolling through Mathura;
at the garland-maker's He puts on a garland with great delight.

**515.** Someone, dressed as Kubja, anoints [Him] with fragrances;
roaring, He plays at breaking the bow.

**516.** Slaying [the elephant] Kuvalaya, Chanura and Mushtika,
He seizes Kamsa by the hair and drags [him] from the dais to the ground.

**517.** How Krishna slew [His] uncle Kamsa —
this game He plays; people are amazed to see [it].

**518.** Wherever a pastime [took place], those places He constructs;
in play He makes the places of the pastimes visible.[^58]

**519.** All the pastimes of Sri Ramachandra, beginning from His birth —
dressing up the children, He enacts them in play.

**520.** All [that is told in] the book *Ramayana*, which Valmiki composed —
all of it Padmavati's son makes visible.

**521.** Taking the guise of Vamana, He deceives Bali;
in the guise of Nrisimha He slays Hiranyakashipu.

**522.** Someone [among the children], like Prahlada, offers praises,
and He charmingly plays Nrisimha's fatherly tenderness.

**523.** All the Lord's pastimes, [shown] to give joy to His devotees —
all these Padmavati's son plays.

**524.** Whenever Nityananda goes off in some direction,
in that same direction all the children run along with Him.

**525.** The people of Ekachakra, with joy in their hearts,
do not hold back their own children.

**526.** Adorning the children with various ornaments,
everyone says, "Go and play with Nityananda."

**527.** Playing with the children, Nitai is overwhelmed by the joy of play;
what wondrous games they were — there is no end to telling.

**528.** What joy there was at the time of [His] sacred thread [ceremony]!
The beauty I saw cannot be put into words.

**529.** What [a wonder] — His entering kaishora at the pauganda age!
Seeing that beauty, no one keeps even a trace of composure.

**530.** In only a few days He acquired learning:
in grammar and the other shastras He became expert.

**531.** Nitai reached the age of twelve years,
yet to look at He was beautiful as if sixteen.

**532.** Hadai Pandita, informing his friends,
became eager to arrange his son's marriage.

**533.** All the brahmanas and worthy people living in Ekachakra —
everyone rejoiced at the talk of the wedding.

**534.** In some brahmana houses they had already settled on a bride;
someone secretly cherished a hope, telling no one openly.[^59]

**535.** So there was joyful talk everywhere —
who knew that fate would bring sorrow?

**536.** From somewhere there came a certain sannyasi Gosani,
beautiful in every respect — [but] without a drop of pity.[^60]

**537.** Hadai Pandita arranged a meal for him;
absorbed in the joy of talks about Krishna, he passed the night.

**538.** On leaving, he begged for Nityananda,
and Hadai, having weighed the former [matter], gave his son.[^61]

**539.** Taking Nityananda, the sannyasi departed in haste;
Hadai fell to the ground unconscious.

**540.** Almost lifeless, Padmavati falls to the ground;
the state the two were in — what power have I to tell?

**541.** All in this Ekachakra, women and men alike,
on merely hearing this news became as if dead.

**542.** The children, His companions, say, "Leaving us all,
where have You gone?" — and weep, throwing themselves on the ground.

**543.** This village of Ekachakra became almost empty;
everywhere people cry, "Alas, alas!"

**544.** A crowd gathered at Hado Pandita's house;
in many ways they bring the two back to consciousness.

**545.** Hadai Pandita and Padmavati — both of them
weep, crying, "Where is Nityananda?"

**546.** What the lament of the two was like, only one who heard it knows:
stones melt, and beasts and birds weep.

**547.** Nitai's younger brother says, weeping,
"Why did the sannyasi not take me?"

**548.** Saying this, he threw himself on the ground;
by the Lord's will life remained in that body.

**549.** A certain brahmana, weeping, says, "O brother!
Tell me, by which road did the sannyasi Gosani go?

**550.** What does Nityananda know of cooking and other tasks?
My son is skilled in carrying out every kind of work.

**551.** Clasping his feet, I will beg for Nityananda [back];
pleasing him, I will give him my son."

**552.** Saying this, he searches for the sannyasi;
nowhere does he find any trace — and he grieves within himself.

**553.** All the knowers of the shastras living in the village of Ekachakra,
sitting in seclusion, spoke much among themselves.

**554.** Someone says, "All that the astrologer said before —
all his words have come true before our eyes.[^62]

**555.** By ill fortune we could not recognize
[that] Balarama was born in Hadai's house."

**556.** Someone says, "This is true, never false:
at the time of [His] birth great auspiciousness arose —

**557.** famine ended, people's sufferings went far away,
the clouds gave rain, and there was abundant joy."

**558.** Someone says, "At the time of His birth I saw with my own eyes:
the gods offered praises and showered flowers on the house;

**559.** crowds of goddesses thronged without cease.
Only now have I understood — before, I did not consider it."

**560.** Someone says: "Could such a thing be, unless He is Balarama?
From His very birth He has captivated everyone's heart.

**561.** Is such beauty possible in a human being?
And in infancy — what wondrous gentleness!"

**562.** Someone says: "In childhood — such wondrous games!
Only the Lord knows all the pastimes of the Lord.

**563.** One day I saw His game with my own eyes:
Nitai Himself put on the dress of a sannyasi.

**564.** How beautifully the danda and kamandalu shone in His hands!
He wore a radiant garment the color of dawn.

**565.** The children put on a wonderful Vaishnava dress;
their bodies gleam with tilaka and malas.

**566.** Placing the sannyasi in the middle, they perform kirtana;
the sannyasi dances, and His movements enchant the world.

**567.** It seems the Lord will accept sannyasa in this [age of] Kali —
that is what He revealed through this playful game.

**568.** The Lord, Bhagavan, the eternal, complete Brahman,
will become a sannyasi — there is proof of it in the shastras.[^63]

**569.** Seeing that game, I thought, 'This is no ordinary [child],'
but I did not say it aloud, for fear of people's ridicule."

**570.** Someone says: "The son of Rohini, non-different from Krishna —
this Nityananda is he; what doubt is there?

**571.** If He, out of mercy, makes it known, then we shall know;
otherwise these living beings are under the sway of His yogamaya."[^64]

**572.** Someone says: "Having obtained [Him], we did not obtain [Him]:
falling under the sway of maya, we lost the jewel.

**573.** Depriving all of us of His beauty and virtues,
the sannyasi suddenly took Him away."

**574.** Someone says: "The sannyasi is only His pretext;
who has the power to understand the Lord's will?

**575.** Balarama once wandered to the holy places;[^65]
that is what He will do now — so my mind tells me."

**576.** Someone says: "To leave father and mother like this —
He did what is improper: how could He go away [from home]?"

**577.** Someone says: "Who can fathom the heart of the Lord?
It seems this has been the rule from the beginning."

**578.** Thus, speaking and speaking of so many things,
they weep, remembering Nityananda.

**579.** Everyone goes to console Hadai Pandita;
as they enter the house, a loud wailing rises.

**580.** Padmavati and Hadai Pandita, the two of them,
do not eat; they can barely sustain their bodies.

**581.** If ever someone wants to make them eat something —
how could they eat? Waves rise in the ocean of sorrow.

**582.** Thus for three months they take no food;
they reproach the Creator: why does life still go on?

**583.** Crying, "Where is Nityananda?" they roll in the dust;
meaning to say one thing, they say another, like madmen.

**584.** Not for half a moment is Hadai Pandita at peace;
whatever arises in his mind he speaks aloud.

**585.** Now he says: "Nityananda, it has been a long time!
Come, let me take You in my arms — let my life be comforted."

**586.** Now he says: "Come, my boy, climb up into my arms:
let us go to the ghat and bathe in the water of the pond."

**587.** Now he says: "Walk ahead of me on Your own feet;
the rice in the fields has ripened — let us go and look in the field."

**588.** Now he says: "Come, my boy, let us go quickly to the market:
whatever You wish, I will buy there."

**589.** Now he says: "Your mother is calling — go home:
it must be to eat the rice prasada of Vishnu."

**590.** Now he says: "Hold a discussion of the shastras
with my students — let me see who loses and who wins."

**591.** Now, calling to his wife, he says:
"Nityananda has come — come and see!

**592.** The sannyasi Gosani is a great ocean of mercy:
out of kindness he has sent Nityananda home."

**593.** Now he says: "What is this — have I gone mad?[^66]
I do not see Nityananda — I see only darkness."

**594.** Thus he says many things, unable to keep his composure;
and what Padmavati's state was — who could tell?

**595.** O my dear children, what shall I tell you of it all?
A great misfortune befell this Ekachakra.

**596.** No one can find peace without Nityananda;
the father, the mother and others disappeared [from this world] day by day.

**597.** Nityananda's companions, becoming distraught,
gave up everything and went wandering to the holy places.

**598.** No one can find peace in any way;
who goes where — no one tells anyone.

**599.** On the far bank of this river there lived a yavana;[^67]
he founded a village there and named it after himself.

**600.** Some people from here went and settled there;
what more is there to say — thus Ekachakra fell into ruin.

**601.** I, the lowest of brahmanas, together with these few people,
live in the village of Ekachakra, remembering the past.

**602.** From the anxiety of my heart I cannot stay at home;
I have become very decrepit and cannot walk.

**603.** And yet my mind runs to see the places
where Nityananda-Rama played.

**604.** These ashvattha and banyan trees here, with their deep shade —
here Nityananda played with the children.

**605.** Taking food, they would sit in a circle
and eat — I saw it with my own eyes.

**606.** When I think of all that, my heart bursts;
the Creator has kept me alive to make me taste sorrow.

**607.** I thought: since the Creator has kept me alive,
surely after some days he will also grant happiness:

**608.** remembering His birthplace, my Nitai
will come to Ekachakra, and I will see [Him] again.[^68]

**609.** By my ill fortune He proved merciless:
to such a village as Ekachakra He never came again.

**610.** Now I have lost hope — there is no hope left;
this is what I pray to the Creator again and again:

**611.** "Deprived in this birth — if I obtain another birth,
then may I see Nityananda right here.

**612.** May I die taking the name of Nitaichand."
As soon as he said this, the brahmana's heart broke.

**613.** Again he says: "Where are You, my life, my Nitai?
Seeing my state, show Yourself to me just once!"

**614.** Having said this, the brahmana weeps aloud;
at that weeping, wood and stone split apart.

**615.** What a wondrous state came over everyone!
Jahnava Ishvari floats away in tears.

**616.** Krishnadasa Pandita and all the others are beside themselves;[^69]
the earth turned to mud with their tears.

**617.** No one can be calm in any way;
again and again they take the dust of the brahmana's feet.

**618.** By the Lord's will all became calm
and, putting the brahmana in front, entered Ekachakra.

**619.** The brahmana says: "That is the Pandita's house."
Having said this, he can say nothing more.

**620.** Having shown the homestead, with a heart full of anguish,
the brahmana, weeping and weeping, went to his own home.

**621.** Seeing the brahmana's state, all became distraught
and went to the house of Hadai Pandita.

**622.** Although the house was empty and utterly ruined,
whose heart would it still not attract?

**623.** Seeing the place of Nityananda's pastimes,
they were filled with prema beyond description.

**624.** That day they stayed in the ruined house;
part of the night they spent in kirtana of the holy name.

**625.** Sleep does not touch the eyes of Jahnava Ishvari;
sitting in solitude, she reflects within herself:

**626.** "I never got to see my father-in-law and mother-in-law;
not even a particle of the joy of my in-laws' home has touched me."

**627.** Having reflected on one thing and about to reflect on another,
she suddenly fell asleep by the Lord's will.

**628.** In a dream she sees the settlement of Ekachakra:
there is nothing to compare it with — great beauty in every respect.

**629.** What a golden city, the work of Vishvakarma![^70]
The abode of Indra is not equal to the Pandita's abode.

**630.** Servants and maidservants without number, opulence beyond measure;
everything ever full of supreme auspiciousness and splendor.

**631.** Hadai Pandita and Padmavati, worshiped by the gods,
love their son Nityananda most tenderly, more than life.

**632.** Sri Vasu[dha] and Jahnava, the two daughters-in-law,[^71]
they always keep in their minds within the casket of their eyes.

**633.** With what delight they care for the daughters-in-law!
Seeing the daughters-in-law's ways, they refresh their eyes.

**634.** The daughters of Suryadasa, honored by the world,
always rejoice in the affection of their father-in-law and mother-in-law.

**635.** As Sri Jahnava was pondering this delightful scene,
her sleep broke — but sleep drew her in again.

**636.** Again she sees a dream: on the riverbank at Ekachakra
gardens of many kinds of flowers display a wondrous beauty.

**637.** Swarms upon swarms of bees hum incessantly;[^72]
many kinds of birds sing most wonderfully.

**638.** The gentle Malaya breeze is always blowing;
the beauty of the forest steals the minds of the best of sages.

**639.** There stands a tall tree in full bloom;
beneath it, a divine throne of jewels.

**640.** Surrounding the throne, the maidservants shine;
their many garments and ornaments glitter.

**641.** Palm-leaf fans, chamaras, sandal and chuya,
scented water in vessels, many flower garlands,[^73]

**642.** boxes of tambula and the rest — taking all this, they all
behold Nityananda's beauty on the jewelled throne.

**643.** Nityananda's beauty enchants millions of Kandarpas;
champaka, keshara and gold are but a token offering waved before His form.[^74]

**644.** On His moon-like face is always a sweet, gentle smile —
does it not pour forth fresh nectar in heaps upon heaps?

**645.** By the play of His eyes He steals the composure of young women;
for His whole form there is no comparison in all the world.

**646.** To the left and right of Sri Nityananda
shine Sri Vasu[dha] and Jahnava — what comparison could one give?[^75]

**647.** With the radiance of their beauty they light up that forest;
by the grace of their forms they steal the pride of millions of Ratis.

**648.** What a soft, soft smile on their lotus faces!
Ceaselessly a wondrous honey flows.

**649.** How sweet their ways — their long chakora-eyes
drink the nectar of Nityananda's moon-like face.

**650.** Seeing the ways of love, a maidservant, taking tambula,
joyfully places it in the hands of Sri Vasu[dha] and Jahnava.

**651.** Both offer tambula into Nityananda's mouth,
and the Lord has both of them taste the chewed tambula.

**652.** The maidservants supply both with chuya, sandal and the rest,
so that the two may joyfully offer them to the Lord of their life.

**653.** Some maidservant supplies many flower garlands;
as they place them on the Beloved's neck, the joy of both grows.[^76]

**654.** Putting the sandal and chuya from His own body on the bodies of His beloveds,
Nityananda joyfully embraces both.

**655.** Putting the garland from His own neck on the necks of both,
He remains in a lovely pose, body against body.

**656.** As she was beholding this truly wondrous play,
Sri Jahnava Ishvari's sleep was broken.

**657.** Saddened that the dream was broken, she reflects within herself:
"Such a wonder I have never seen in a dream."

**658.** The night gave way to dawn; joyful, Ishvari
says something to someone, but does not say it clearly.

**659.** Her heart weeps at the thought of leaving Ekachakra;
she tries, but her mind finds no rest.

**660.** Suddenly someone says: "You are always present here.
Go quickly to Khardaha and fulfil the wish of your heart."

**661.** Hearing this, she sets out from Ekachakra with everyone;
people run from all sides to have her darshan.

**662.** On that road a brahmana, a great drunkard,
intoxicated with wine, is dancing.

**663.** Now he laughs, now he weeps, floating in tears,
now he trembles, now he leaps, now he falls to the ground.

**664.** Seeing his behaviour, Jahnava Ishvari
very softly asks her companions:

**665.** "Tell me, tell me, why has he become like this?"
All reply: "This is a brahmana, a great drunkard."

**666.** Hearing this, Ishvari, in her mercy, says:
"May Lord Gaurahari make him intoxicated with prema in this way."

**667.** Hearing this, everyone cries "Hari bol!" —
"Blessed, blessed, blessed is this drunken brahmana!"

**668.** The good fortune of that brahmana I cannot describe:
by Ishvari's mercy he became qualified for bhakti.

**669.** Thus showing boundless mercy to souls,
she went with everyone on the road to Maudeshvara.[^77]

**670.** Coming to Maudeshvara, she had darshan of Shiva,
whom the son of Padmavati had worshipped.

**671.** Seeing the place where Nityananda performed the subduing of Kundali,
everyone was filled with joy.[^78]

**672.** The road by which Nityananda had gone to Vakreshvara
the local people showed to everyone.[^79]

**673.** Sri Ishvari, swiftly passing through the land of Rarh,
came with everyone to Kantaka-nagara.

**674.** Sri Yadunandana, greatly delighted,
sent the news to Yajigrama.

**675.** Hearing it, Srinivasa with his companions at that very moment
came to Kantaka-nagara with great joy in his heart.

**676.** The jubilation at the darshan of Sri Ishvari's feet,
the happiness that shone forth at the sight of the Bhagavatas,

**677.** all the talks that passed between them —
all this I cannot tell, for fear of prolixity.

**678.** Srinivasa, coming to Narottama
and sitting with him in private, told everything and heard everything.

**679.** Govinda told about the Gosvamis;
hearing all this, he became greatly distressed.[^80]

**680.** Ramachandra gave [him] the *Gopala-virudavali*;
Srinivasa Acharya took it and placed it on his head.[^81]

**681.** When much of the night had passed, he lay down,
and the Gosvami, in the guise of a dream, consoled the Acharya.[^82]

**682.** Before Sri Ishvari, when the night gave way to dawn,
he bows and asks her to come to his home.

**683.** Showing great mercy to Srinivasa Acharya,
Ishvari went with everyone to Yajigrama.

**684.** The people of Sri Yajigrama, with jubilant hearts,
run from all four directions to have her darshan.

**685.** Srinivasa Acharya, his heart full of great joy,
quickly sent the news to Shrikhanda.

**686.** Narottama, Ramachandra and other dear ones
he appointed to arrange all the affairs.

**687.** Seeing their care, all the mahantas, in great joy,
did not feel like strangers — as if they had come to their own home.

**688.** All the mahantas were lodged in a lovely place,
and Ishvari's lodging was in Srinivasa's house.

**689.** As Sri Jahnava Ishvari was entering the house,
the Acharya's wife came forward to receive her.

**690.** She is very modest, her gait most graceful;
her body, like golden fresh butter, is covered with cloth.

**691.** Her lotus face, mingled with a gentle smile, is spotlessly pure,
and very beautiful is her pair of long eyes.

**692.** At the sight of Ishvari tears of joy stream from her;
as she bows at the holy feet, thrills spread over her body.

**693.** Sri Ishvari, saying something in such sweet words,
lifted her up and embraced her in a wondrous surge of affection.

**694.** The Acharya's wife, showing much humility,
led her into the room and seated her on a fine seat.

**695.** She washed her feet with scented water;
I do not know how to describe the joy that welled up.

**696.** Seeing the good character of Srinivasa Acharya's wife,
Ishvari's great affection grows moment by moment.

**697.** The joy there was in Yajigrama at the cooking,
the joy there was at the honouring of mahaprasada,

**698.** the joy there was in the heart of each mahanta —
how could I, a lowly one, describe it in detail?

**699.** Their joyful exchanges with one another I cannot tell;
the people of Yajigrama watch, filling their eyes.

**700.** All the mahantas, conversing about Krishna,
are sitting in a very lovely place.

**701.** At that time Sri Raghunandana came from Khanda,
and with him great Bhagavatas.

**702.** What a wonderful meeting took place between them!
Who, seeing that absorption in prema, could keep his composure?

**703.** Telling one another the news of Gauda and Vraja,
they became distraught — no one can stay steady.

**704.** Gathering his composure, Sri Raghunandana
asked about Ishvari's journey there and back.

**705.** Sri Parameshvari Dasa, gathering his composure,
told Sri Raghunandana everything from beginning to end.

**706.** Sri Raghunandana joyfully requested the mahantas
to come to Shrikhanda in the morning.

**707.** Having made this request to Sri Jahnava Ishvari,
he said much to Srinivasa and hurried to Khanda.

**708.** Here, in the evening, the Bhagavatas
performed nama-sankirtana for some time.

**709.** At Ishvari's order Srinivasa, delighted,
poured down a rain of nectar by reading the Srimad-Bhagavatam.

**710.** All became immersed in the bliss of prema;
everyone's body was soaked with the tears from their eyes.

**711.** When the reading of the Srimad-Bhagavatam was finished,
after some time everyone grew calm.

**712.** Jahnava Ishvari, with great joy in her heart,
says to Srinivasa in sweet words:

**713.** "When the night gives way to dawn, I shall go to Khanda,
and from Khanda I shall hurry to Khardaha.

**714.** Only a very short time have I stayed here;
what is happening in my heart my mind cannot grasp."

**715.** Srinivasa says: "Now there must be no delay.
Go quickly to Khardaha and manifest the Deity.

**716.** When the Deity of Srimati Radhika has been made,
you will find peace by sending [Her] to Vrindavana.[^83]

**717.** Sri Gopinatha greatly desires this;
I feel that [She] will be made very soon."

**718.** Delighted by Srinivasa's words, Ishvari
again speaks softly to Srinivasa:

**719.** "When I reach Khardaha, I shall send news.
And where will you be staying now?"

**720.** Srinivasa says: "After staying here four days,
I shall go quickly to Navadvipa.

**721.** In Navadvipa almost everyone has become hidden;[^84]
of the Lord's [people] only Ishana remains, alone.

**722.** The few who are with him —
for all of them life hangs by a thread.

**723.** Ishana has told me to come;
after going there and returning, I shall go to the village of Kheturi.

**724.** After staying there some days and going to Vishnupura,
I shall come back from there and stay here."

**725.** While they spoke of many such things, the night grew late;
after honoring prasada, all lay down to rest.

**726.** At daybreak, setting out for Khanda, Ishvari
carefully consoles the Acharya's wife.

**727.** Seeing her condition, she became distressed;
showing great favor, she set off for Shrikhanda.

**728.** The people of Shrikhanda run from every side;
Sri Raghunandana comes forward to receive [her].

**729.** The procession with the company was wonderful beyond measure.
Seeing it from afar, a certain brahmana says again and again:

**730.** "Fortunate are the sons of Narayana Dasa —
the three: Mukunda, Madhava, Narahari.[^85]

**731.** Mukunda's son is Raghunandana Thakura;
at the sight of him all suffering goes far away.

**732.** How constantly he is immersed in the rasa of bhakti!"
Saying many such things, the excellent brahmana walks along.

**733.** Raghunandana's son is named Sri Kanai;[^86]
young in years, his beauty knows no limit.

**734.** He is always overwhelmed by the qualities of Sri Gaurachandra;
he cannot hold himself up — his whole body sways.

**735.** Seeing the mahantas, with joy in his heart
he asks his father who is called by what name.

**736.** Sri Raghunandana, having told his son everything,
was the first to meet them all, filled with great joy.

**737.** Thakura Kanai's eyes are full of tears;
as he bowed, all lifted him up and took him in their arms.

**738.** The hearts of all the mahantas were filled with great joy;
Sri Ishvari showed boundless motherly affection.

**739.** With everyone, Ishvari, supreme bliss in her heart,
arrived in Gauranga's courtyard.

**740.** How can a poet with a single mouth describe
even a trace of the prema that arose at the sight of Gauranga?

**741.** They had darshana of Sri Madana-gopala,
whom Sri Raghunandana fed with laddus.[^87]

**742.** After staying a while in the Lord's courtyard,
all went to their lodging near the Lord's temple.

**743.** How everyone bathed, ate and so on —
for fear of length I cannot describe it.

**744.** How the holy sankirtana and the rest went on at night
I shall tell in somewhat more detail in the *Narottama-vilasa*.

**745.** When Sri Ishvari was about to go to Khardaha,
Sri Raghunandana became utterly distraught.

**746.** What they said to each other at the time of parting —
hearing it, wood and stone would split.

**747.** Sri Raghunandana handed over
many provisions to Sri Parameshvari Dasa.

**748.** Sri Ishvari spoke much to Sri Raghunandana and all the others,
bathed in tears.

**749.** Together with Krishnadasa Sarakhel and all the others,
Sri Ishvari set off for Khardaha.

**750.** Srinivasa Acharya and the others, after staying in Shrikhanda,
came home, remembering Sri Ishvari's qualities.

**751.** From Khanda Sri Ishvari, coming to Nadia,
sees that the Lord's associates are almost all gone.

**752.** The few who were there — Sri Ishana and the others —
hearing of Ishvari's coming, came forward to meet [her].

**753.** Seeing Ishvari together with everyone,
they seemed to regain their life, and their hearts were soothed.

**754.** As Ishvari, with Krishnadasa and the others, sees them all,
what wondrous prema surges in the heart!

**755.** What became of them all as they entered
the house of Srivasa Pandita — who can tell?

**756.** Staying that day in Srivasa's house,
she passes day and night with anxiety in her heart.

**757.** At the very end of the night a little sleep came;
the Lord with His associates appeared [to her] in a dream.

**758.** How sweetly beautiful is Sri Gaurachandra's appearance!
On His head shines glossy, curling, lovely hair.

**759.** On His left is Gadadhara, on His right Nityananda,
before Him Advaita, together with Srivasa and the others.

**760.** As the sankirtana begins, Sri Gaurasundara dances;
Nityananda, Sri Advaita and Gadadhara dance.

**761.** Srivasa, Murari, Vakreshvara, Haridasa —
what wondrous movements they display in the dance!

**762.** Govinda, Madhava, Vasu, Mukunda and all the others[^88]
went mad with singing and playing on instruments.

**763.** The city of Navadvipa overflows with great bliss;
Brahma, Shiva and Shesha dance amid the crowd of people.

**764.** Raising cries of "Jaya!" people run from every side;
upon the sankirtana the demigods shower many kinds of flowers.

**765.** As soon as she saw such auspicious festivity in Navadvipa,
Jahnava Ishvari forgot all her sorrows.

**766.** As soon as she awoke, she became distraught;
by the Lord's will she regained her composure.

**767.** She bowed again and again to Navadvipa-dhama;
what she had seen in the dream she did not reveal to anyone.

**768.** She carefully consoled Sri Ishana and all the others;
she told them, "Srinivasa will come soon."

**769.** After staying thus two days in Nadia,
Ishvari went with everyone to Ambika.

**770.** She had darshana of Nityananda and Chaitanya;[^89]
she was overwhelmed, and her tears could not be held back.

**771.** After staying one day in Ambika, absorbed in prema,
she set out on her way by the command of Nityananda and Chaitanya.

**772.** She quickly sent a man to the village of Khardaha;
the news of Ishvari's coming spread everywhere.

**773.** All the communities of Vaishnavas living along the bank of the Ganga —
all came forward to receive [her].

**774.** The fortunate merchants, all of them, young and old:[^90]
who can tell how great their eagerness was?

**775.** At the sight of Ishvari everyone forgets himself;
Ishvari quickly went to Uddharana's house.

**776.** She stayed at the home of Uddharana Datta;
a great crowd of people gathered to see Ishvari.

**777.** Remembering the life and deeds of Uddharana Datta,
Sri Jahnava Ishvari cannot hold her heart steady.

**778.** In the talk of Uddharana, dear to Nityananda —
what the deeds of the Lord's associates were like cannot be told.[^91]

**779.** After staying in Uddharana's house, she boarded a boat;
showing favor to all, she went to Khardaha.

**780.** The people of Khardaha and the other villages,
seeing [her], attained supreme bliss.

**781.** As she entered the house at a most auspicious moment,
Sri Jahnava Ishvari's heart rejoiced.

**782.** Ganga and Virachandra, their hearts rejoicing greatly,[^92]
bowed at the feet of Sri Jahnava Ishvari.

**783.** Gazing at the faces of Ganga and Virachandra,
Ishvari, overcome with tenderness, looks on with tearful eyes.

**784.** What Ishvari's motherly love was like, I do not know how to tell;
nowhere do I see anything like it to offer as a comparison.

**785.** When Sri Jahnava bowed to Sri Vasu[dha] Devi,
what prema was revealed — who can tell?

**786.** Overcome with tenderness, Sri Vasu[dha] asked after her welfare;
Sri Jahnava told her everything in brief.

**787.** The mahantas who journeyed together with Ishvari —
what power have I to tell of their joy?

**788.** Sri Jahnava gave the command to Nayana Bhaskara;
he began to make the Deity of Sri Radhika.[^93]

**789.** All these events I have made known in brief;
some fortunate one will describe them in full and well.

**790.** Whoever, hearing all this, grows in firm attachment,
attains pure bhakti without effort.

**791.** Meditating on the feet of Srinivasa Acharya,
Narahari Dasa tells the *Bhakti-ratnakara*.

*Thus in the Sri Bhakti-ratnakara ends the eleventh wave, called "The Description of Sri Ishvari Jahnava's Journey to Sri Vrindavana and Back, and Other Events".*
