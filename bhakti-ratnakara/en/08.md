# Taranga 8. Narottama in Navadvipa and Nilachala

*Narottama's journey to Navadvipa; the old brahmana at the ashvattha tree; the house of Jagannatha Mishra; Shantipura, Ambika, Saptagrama; the story of Nityananda at Panihati; Khardaha and Khanakul; Nilachala: the places of the Lord's pastimes, the account of Mamu Gosvami, the disappearance of the associates; Shyamananda; Shrikhanda, Yajigrama, Katwa, Ekachakra; the return to Kheturi*

**1.** Glory, glory to Gaurachandra, the son of Shachi!
Glory, glory to Nityananda and Advaita, full of prema!

**2.** Glory, glory to Gadadhara Pandita, to Srivasa!
Glory to Vakreshvara, Sri Murari, Haridasa!

**3.** Glory to Gauridasa, to Sri Svarupa Damodara!
Glory to all the associates of Gaurachandra!

**4.** Glory, glory to the listeners, the abode of virtues!
Now listen with kind hearts to what I shall tell.

**5.** Acharya Thakura, the teacher of the scriptures of bhakti,
shatters the pride of the Mayavadis.

**6.** He lives happily in Yajigrama with his disciples
and every moment watches the road for Narottama.

**7.** "How many days until I am together with Sri Narottama?" —
this is the Acharya's constant thought within his heart.

**8.** Meanwhile Sri Thakura Narottama, joyful,
sets out for Navadvipa, meditating on the deeds of Gaura.

**9.** Coming near Navadvipa, the Mahashaya,
in agitation, speaks within himself:

**10.** "In Navadvipa, Gauranga's wondrous pastimes,
the ocean of joy of ceaseless sankirtana.

**11.** In every house, day after day, a great festival;
no one notices how the days and nights go by.

**12.** In Navadvipa there is no one without joy:
all ceaselessly behold Gaurachandra.

**13.** I was not born at such a time —
such a wealth of joy I did not get to see!"

**14.** Saying much in this way, he is awash with tears;
having gone some distance, he looks toward Navadvipa.

**15.** He sees a wondrous splendour in the town of Nadia:
in every house a river of bliss flows.

**16.** On every side people go about calling out "Hari!";
they tell one another of Gorachand's sweetness.

**17.** Amid His associates Gora, the enchanter of the worlds,
dances most wondrously in sankirtana.

**18.** Ceaselessly rises the clamour of "Glory, glory!";
supremely auspicious is the splendour of Nadia.

**19.** Gazing and gazing, he was overwhelmed with bliss;
he is unaware of himself, tears of prema stream from his eyes.

**20.** After a while, becoming steady, he looks again —
Nadia seems to be drowning in an ocean of sorrow.

**21.** In agitation Sri Thakura Mahashaya
says within himself: "What did I see — as if in a dream?"

**22.** Drenched in tears, he cannot walk on,
and sits beneath an extraordinary ashvattha tree.[^1]

**23.** What can I say of the exceeding power of that tree!
At the mere touch of its shade, composure and the like arose in him.

**24.** Narottama, reflecting again within himself,
looks about on all sides, consoling himself.

**25.** On that road he sees an aged brahmana;
he wants to ask something but cannot ask.

**26.** That brahmana had a rule: every day
he would come to the tree and stay there for a while.

**27.** It was Nimai's place of play — he loved it dearly;
looking toward the foot of the tree, he walks with slow steps.

**28.** Seeing Narottama, the brahmana thinks within himself:
"Surely this is one to whom Nimaichand has granted His mercy.

**29.** Otherwise — my heart is burning in this cruel fever,
yet seeing him, even so my joy grows.

**30.** What an extraordinary form! What sweetness of face![^2]
What tears of prema flow from his long eyes!

**31.** Where has he suddenly come here from?" —
thinking thus, he wishes to ask.

**32.** Coming near, he asks Narottama:
"What is your name, my son? Where have you come from?"

**33.** Narottama, telling the brahmana who he was,
bowed down to him with great humility.

**34.** The brahmana, learning who Narottama was,
no sooner embraced him than he was drenched with tears.

**35.** Embracing him tightly with the deepest fatherly affection
and sitting beneath the tree, he slowly says a few words to him:

**36.** "O my son! The joy that there was in Nadia —
could the Four-faced and the Five-faced describe it?[^3]

**37.** From the day Nimai went away, leaving it,
from that very day Nadia became empty.

**38.** Suddenly — I do not know what came into His mind —
He accepted sannyasa from Bharati.[^4]

**39.** The words about the sannyasa will not come to my lips;
remembering those locks of hair, the pain in my heart grows.

**40.** With my own eyes I saw His dress that enchanted the worlds —
and He put on a kaupina: can the heart bear this?[^5]

**41.** What can I say! He simply deceived us all —
otherwise would Nimai have left Nadia and gone away?

**42.** Having toured all the holy places, He made His home in Nilachala;
there, wondrous pastimes with His own associates.

**43.** Receiving good news through people coming and going,
the residents of Navadvipa would rejoice at heart.

**44.** When He suddenly disappeared in Nilachala —
hearing of it, Nadia was as if struck by a thunderbolt.

**45.** Nimai's countless associates in Nadia —
most of them have already passed from sight.

**46.** The state of Nadia now — I cannot tell it;
day by day Nadia is sinking into darkness.

**47.** Since Srivasa Pandita and the others disappeared,
what has befallen Nadia — who can tell?

**48.** Nimai's wife, Vishnupriya, devoted to her husband —
in speaking of her, the heart is torn apart.

**49.** Lakshmi herself, with qualities beyond this world —
only a few days ago she withdrew from sight.

**50.** The fire of separation from Nimai burns everyone;
whoever still remain alive are as if at their last breath.

**51.** The residents of Navadvipa have not a moment's composure;
lying down and in dreams they say, 'Where are You, O Nimai?'

**52.** People tell one another of Nimai's deeds
and weep ceaselessly, beyond all measure.

**53.** In whatever direction of Nadia, on whatever road one goes,
hearing the weeping, one weeps aloud oneself.

**54.** Those in Nadia who were once of wicked conduct —
what can I say of how they lament now!

**55.** What to speak of others? I was devoted to logic,
and, mistaking Him for a human boy, I could not recognize Him.

**56.** Nimai is Narayana Himself, according to the scriptures;
His superhuman deeds pervaded the world.

**57.** From His very childhood I saw His doings;
remembering them, I feel my heart break.

**58.** What can I say! Beneath this very ashvattha tree
He used to study the scriptures with great delight.

**59.** As the moon surrounded by the stars,
so the beautiful Nimai amid the host of His students.

**60.** From afar I gazed my fill at that splendour;
even now I cannot forget it for a moment.

**61.** O Narottama, my son, I tell you:
one day Nimai appeared here.

**62.** On all sides, thousands upon thousands of students,
and amid them shone the son of Shachi.

**63.** Seeing that wondrous splendour, I fainted;
when I came to, I look — and see it no more.

**64.** After a while, regaining steadiness, I reflected:
in Nadia He ever sports with His students.

**65.** Since then I come here every day;
by His will today I have seen you.

**66.** You are one to whom Nimaichand has granted His mercy;
that is why I have revealed this secret."

**67.** Hearing the brahmana's most affectionate words,
Narottama placed the dust of the brahmana's feet on his head.

**68.** With tears he says to the brahmana:
"Show your favour to me, an ignorant one, O venerable sir!"

**69.** The brahmana, embracing Narottama, says:
"Always gain bhakti, my son."

**70.** Saying this, he held him in his embrace for a while
and drenched Narottama's body with his tears.

**71.** Again he says softly, softly to Narottama:
"Navadvipa is a very extensive settlement.

**72.** Everywhere you will see His associates;
by this road go first to Mayapura.

**73.** There is the house of Shachi and Jagannatha Mishra,
where Narayana descended."

**74.** No sooner had he said this than the brahmana lost his composure;
Narottama went along that road.

**75.** Within Navadvipa there are many villages with their own names;
asking people, he enters Mayapura.

**76.** There, in great anxiety, he asks someone:
"Where is the house of Jagannatha Mishra?"

**77.** He says: "Go along this road.
Look — there is the house of Jagannatha Mishra."

**78.** Having said this, drenched in streams of tears,
he heaves a long sigh and looks at Narottama.

**79.** Narottama cannot hold back his streaming tears;
slowly, slowly he enters Mishra's house.

**80.** There the merciful Shuklambara Brahmachari,[^6]
seeing Narottama, reflects within himself:

**81.** "Although my heart burns with cruel grief,
still, seeing him, I find happiness.

**82.** Srinivasa has come from Vraja with the books;
this, it seems, is his dear Narottama Dasa —

**83.** the Narottama whom the Lord drew [to Himself] in the village of Ramakeli."[^7]
So he reflected within himself.

**84.** He asks Narottama, "What is your name?"
Narottama told him who he was.

**85.** Shuklambara, making himself known,
embraced Narottama and floats in tears.

**86.** Narottama, rolling on the ground, fell at his feet;
he cannot hold back his tears — they stream from his eyes.

**87.** Fallen in the Lord's courtyard, he laments greatly;
the brahmachari calmed him, consoling him at length.

**88.** There Narottama, [wishing] to bow
to Ishana, dear to the Lord, cannot keep his composure.[^8]

**89.** Sri Ishana, embracing Narottama,
in a surge of great affection gazes at his face.

**90.** With tears he says to Narottama:
"You have done well, my son, to show yourself at this time.

**91.** Through the Vaishnavas coming and going
I have heard all the news of you all, from beginning to end."

**92.** Having said this, he can say nothing more.
The brahmachari took Narottama to his own house.

**93.** There, at the sight of Damodara Pandita,[^9]
he lost his composure and bowed at his feet.

**94.** The brahmachari introduced [him] to Sri Pandita;
the Pandita firmly embraces Sri Narottama.

**95.** With great affection he says to Narottama again and again:
"All of us longed to see you.

**96.** By the Lord's will life still remains in the body;
it is good that you came soon — I have seen you.

**97.** Such cruel grief I cannot bear;
it seems I shall not see Srinivasa again."

**98.** Saying many such things, he calmed Narottama
and brought him together with Shripati, Shrinidhi and all the others.[^10]

**99.** Those dear to the Lord who had withdrawn from sight —
all of them gave him their darshan in the guise of dreams.

**100.** How much mercy the Lord's associates showed him —
how much of it can I describe with one mouth?

**101.** Keeping Narottama a few days in Nadia,
all soon sent him off to Nilachala.

**102.** Narottama, having taken leave of everyone,
floats in streams of tears and cannot steady his heart.

**103.** Coming to the Lord's house, he asked Ishana Thakura
for permission to go to Nilachala.

**104.** Ishana Thakura, dear to the Lord, with great affection,
agitated, says to Narottama:

**105.** "O Narottama! Go quickly to Shrikshetra.[^11]
Day by day it has grown darker everywhere.

**106.** In these few days, through people,
I have received all the news from there.

**107.** In what state Gopinatha Acharya[^12] and the others are,
by the Lord's will, cannot be told.

**108.** Going there, you will see them all;
[then] go quickly to Shrikhanda and Kantaka-nagara.

**109.** Together with Srinivasa you will come here again.
'I shall see [you] again' — that is in my heart; so I have told you.

**110.** I do not know what will happen in the meantime.
Go by way of Shantipura and Khardaha."[^13]

**111.** Having said this, he fell silent.
Who can understand his heart? His tears cannot be stopped.

**112.** Narottama, deeply agitated at heart,
set out from Navadvipa for Shantipura.

**113.** Immersed in the pastimes of Sitanatha,[^14]
he laments so much that it cannot be told.

**114.** Gazing toward the village of Shantipura,
he weeps, saying, "I have been deprived!"

**115.** Prabhu Sri Advaita, the lord of Shantipura,[^15]
sports in Shantipura, unseen by the world.

**116.** He showed Narottama the utmost limit of mercy:
the day before, He informed Sri Achyutananda.[^16]

**117.** Sri Achyutananda watches the road,
while here Narottama enters Shantipura.

**118.** In what state the people of Shantipura are since the Lord's withdrawal —
who could describe it?

**119.** When Narottama asks where the Acharya's house is,[^17]
someone, weeping, says, "Go by this road."

**120.** Abundant streams flow from Narottama's eyes;
he goes along that road with a deeply agitated heart.

**121.** Lord Sitanatha, showing great mercy,
unseen by others, revealed Himself [to him] together with His associates.

**122.** Narottama fainted in the ecstasy of prema;
by the Lord's will he soon regained consciousness.

**123.** Becoming steady, he enters the Lord's abode
and sees Achyutananda sitting there.

**124.** Without any introduction they recognized each other;
Narottama bowed to Sri Achyutananda.

**125.** Sri Achyutananda, afflicted by separation from the Lord,
had grown pale, his golden body thin.

**126.** Looking at Narottama, his heart losing its composure,
he stretches out his arms and, in the ecstasy of prema, embraces [him].

**127.** He bathes [his] body with the tears of his holy eyes;
who can understand how agitated [his] heart is?

**128.** He speaks sweet words to Narottama:
"I cannot keep you here for long.

**129.** At this time there is no need for delay;
quickly have the darshan of Nilachala-chandra.[^18]

**130.** There the Lord's associates will soon send [you] on;
they will accomplish much through you."

**131.** Saying many such things, Sri Achyuta
brought Narottama together with the dear associates of Lord Advaita.

**132.** All of them, with great affection for Narottama,
kept him in Shantipura for three or four days.

**133.** Soon they sent him off to go to Nilachala.
How Narottama set out cannot be told.

**134.** Quickly crossing the Ganga at the village of Harinadi,
he goes to Ambika and sees Nitai and Chaitanya.[^19]

**135.** Nitai and Chaitanya are the life of Gauridasa.
How wonderful is the beauty of the service, enchanting the world!

**136.** Narottama, rolling in the Lord's courtyard,
offered obeisances, drenched in tears.

**137.** With Hridaya Chaitanya and the others dear to the Lord
he had a most wonderful meeting.

**138.** All the talks he had with them
I cannot relate in detail, for the book would grow too long.

**139.** All, with great affection for Narottama,
sent him off to go to Nilachala.

**140.** Streams of tears flow from everyone's eyes;
tears flow ceaselessly from Narottama's eyes.

**141.** Surrendering himself at the feet of Nitai and Chaitanya,
he leaves Ambika, deeply agitated.

**142.** Through those villages where the Lord's dear ones live
he made his way.

**143.** What an extraordinary journey! He looks in all four directions;
seeing Saptagrama, he bows from afar.[^20]

**144.** It is the beautiful place of the austerities of the seven rishis;
[here are] the three streams — Sri Ganga, Yamuna and Sarasvati.[^21]

**145.** The sight of Saptagrama removes all sorrow;
here Lord Nityananda sports in bliss.

**146.** How Nityananda came to Saptagrama
I shall tell briefly here — give your mind to it.

**147.** At the command of Sri Chaitanyachandra in Nilachala,
Nityananda-deva set out for the land of Gauda.[^22]

**148.** Entering the land of Gauda from Utkala,
intoxicated with prema, He praises the land of Gauda.

**149.** What the land of Gauda is like cannot be described:
it is the crown ornament of many holy places.

**150.** *Thus [it is said] in the Chaitanya-chandrodaya-nataka, in the second act, in the fourteenth verse:*
*"Glory to the land of Gauda — how wondrous it is! Like the crown of holy places,*
*it bears the city named Sri Navadvipa,*
*where [took place] the descent of the Lord whose splendour is like the finest gold,*
*where Bhakti-devi in person stirs in every town."*

**151.** The land of Gauda is full of holy places — who knows its glory?
The Lord wished to travel for some days.

**152.** *Thus [it is said] in the Chaitanya-bhagavata:*
"In the Shesha-khanda, for some days Nityananda
enjoyed the rasa of wandering over the earth."[^23]

**153.** Nitai is very fond of wandering —
from it comes great good to all living beings.

**154.** Along both banks of the Ganga, the abode of all holy places,
Nityananda wanders in great joy.

**155.** To go soon to Nadia for the darshan
of Mother Sri Shachi — so He resolved.

**156.** Together with Ramadasa, Gadadhara Dasa and others,[^24]
the Lord arrived at the village of Panihati.

**157.** First, in the house of Raghava Pandita,[^25]
sankirtana began — happiness spread through the world.

**158.** The great devotee Raghava was born right there;
there is no end to the glory of a devotee's birthplace.

**159.** *Thus [it is said] in the same [work]:*
"In whatever family, in whatever land a bhagavata descends,
by his power [all] are delivered for a hundred thousand yojanas [around]."[^26]

**160.** Hearing that the Lord had come to the village of Panihati,
devotees come from all four directions.

**161.** All the places through which devotees pass
become holy, full of tirthas.

**162.** *Thus [it is said] in the same [work]:*
"The place that Vaishnavas grace with their coming
becomes supremely holy, full of tirthas."

**163.** What wonderful pastimes of the Lord with the devotees!
In the village of Panihati, the manifestation of many kinds of feeling.

**164.** The pastimes that took place in the house of Gadadhara Dasa —
who has the power to tell them with a single mouth?

**165.** In Khardaha the Lord, the son of Padmavati,[^27]
is ceaselessly and boundlessly intoxicated with sankirtana.

**166.** There, where the temple of Purandara Pandita stands,[^28]
He revealed a prema rare even for Brahma.

**167.** Removing the sufferings of the people of many villages,
The Lord made His auspicious journey to Saptagrama.

**168.** The Lord made Uddharana Datta His own;[^29]
the pastimes that took place there are famous in the world.

**169.** *Thus [it is said] in the same [work]:*
"In the house of the fortunate Uddharana Datta
Mahaprabhu stayed on the bank of the Triveni."[^30]

**170.** With body, mind and words, the feet of Nityananda
Datta Uddharana worshipped without guile.

**171.** The right to serve Nityananda-svarupa
Uddharana received — what greater fortune could there be?

**172.** Birth after birth Nityananda-svarupa is [his] Lord;
birth after birth Uddharana is His servant.

**173.** All the families of merchants, through Uddharana,
were purified — there is no doubt of this.

**174.** Nityananda descended to deliver the merchants;
to the merchants He gave the right to prema-bhakti.

**175.** In Saptagrama, in every house of the merchants,
Sri Nityananda Himself delights in kirtana.

**176.** All the merchants worshipped the feet of Nityananda
with their whole being, taking shelter of Him.

**177.** Seeing all the merchants worship Krishna,
the whole world is astonished at heart.

**178.** Boundless is the glory of Nityananda-Mahaprabhu,
who delivered the fallen, ignorant merchants.

**179.** In Saptagrama Nityananda-raya, the great wrestler,
performs sankirtana with His companions in play.

**180.** All the pastimes of kirtana that took place in Saptagrama
I could not describe even in a hundred years.

**181.** As there was happiness before in Gokula,
so there was happiness in the town of Saptagrama.[^31]

**182.** Who can know the good fortune of the merchants?
Thakura Vrindavana [Dasa] described much of this.[^32]

**183.** Uddharana Datta, ceaselessly intoxicated with prema,
serves the Lord with bliss in his heart.

**184.** In Saptagrama, at the great tirtha, the Triveni-ghat,
staying near the Lord, he sees all kinds of pastimes.

**185.** All the places to which Lord Nityananda comes
become the abode of all tirthas.

**186.** Who can count all the tirthas in the land of Gauda?
Together with the Lord, Uddharana wanders to all the tirthas.

**187.** In Shantipura Lord Nityananda, in great delight,
met with the Lord, Sri Advaita.

**188.** From there He went to Navadvipa;
on Nityananda's body many kinds of ornaments shine.

**189.** Captivating is the sound of the nupuras on His holy feet:
in the whole universe there is nothing to compare with it.

**190.** In the sutra of the Shesha-khanda the son of Narayani
described the journey of Nityananda-chandra.[^33]

**191.** *Thus [it is said] in the same [work]:*
"Who is able to understand [His] endless deeds?
With nupuras on His feet He wanders through all of Mathura."

**192.** Mathura and Sri Navadvipa are never different:
what is Mathura is Navadvipa — this is certain.

**193.** The son of Padmavati wanders about Nadia,
His body adorned with many kinds of jeweled ornaments.

**194.** *In the Chaitanya-bhagavata:*
"Silver nupuras and anklets shine on His holy feet;
their sound is supremely sweet, His gait like that of the king of elephants."

**195.** In house after house, with all His associates,
He ceaselessly delights in the joy of sankirtana.

**196.** Navadvipa is like Mathura, the capital:
he says much of this kind — I cannot retell it.

**197.** The joy that Nityananda in Navadvipa
gives to Mother Shachi cannot be put into words.

**198.** Wandering with His companions through the region of Nadia,
the wonderful pastimes He showed — who will describe them?

**199.** Absorbed in the qualities of Nityananda, Datta Uddharana
ceaselessly serves the feet of Nityananda.

**200.** Into Saptagrama, [the village] of that very Uddharana Thakura,
Narottama enters, overwhelmed with prema.

**201.** He asks people where Uddharana's house is;
someone, weeping, says: "Here it is.

**202.** Burning every moment in the grief of separation from the Lord,
it is now some days since he disappeared.

**203.** With his departure Saptagrama has sunk into darkness."
Hearing this, Narottama sheds streams of tears.

**204.** He became so distraught that it cannot be told;
those who were dear to the Lord met with him.

**205.** From Saptagrama he goes along the bank of the Ganges
and, wherever a devotee lives, he meets with them all.

**206.** Entering the village of Khardaha, the Mahashaya
sees a mystery that cannot be told.

**207.** Lord Nityananda fulfilled [his] desire;
by the Lord's will Narottama became calm.

**208.** As he made his way toward the Lord's house,
he met with the Lord's associates.

**209.** All quickly took him to the Lord's house
and made this news known to Sri Ishvari.

**210.** Sri Vasu[dha] and Jahnava, both of them, together with Virabhadra,[^34]
were sitting, speaking of the Lord's deeds.

**211.** Hearing of Narottama's unexpected arrival,
though they were distraught, still they rejoiced at heart.

**212.** At once they called Narottama into the inner apartments;
Narottama went and bowed to [their] lotus feet.

**213.** The Ishvaris Vasu[dha] and Jahnava, knowers of all truths —
how much mercy they showed, I cannot tell.

**214.** They kept Narottama for a few days;
days and nights passed in the delight of talks about Krishna.

**215.** In the rapture of prema they praise Narottama:
"The name 'Mahashaya' befits him."[^35]

**216.** Thus, having said much to one another in private,
they send Narottama off to Nilachala.

**217.** At the time of parting Sri Jahnava, very softly —
I do not know what she said, with tears in her eyes.

**218.** What Prabhu Virabhadra, in very sweet words,
said to Narottama cannot be told.

**219.** Sri Parameshvari Dasa, distraught,
told him all about the road.

**220.** Mahesha Pandita and others, with the greatest affection,
sent Narottama off and cannot find peace.[^36]

**221.** Narottama, falling to the ground, bowed to them all
and went from Khardaha with an anxious heart.

**222.** Narottama, a traveler on the road to Nilachala,
goes wherever there is an abode of devotees.

**223.** He quickly went to Khanakula-Krishnanagara
and bowed at the feet of Sri Thakura Abhirama.[^37]

**224.** In separation from Nityananda he has no outer awareness;
and Sri Malini is the same — there is nothing to compare her with.

**225.** Together with Malini he showed him much mercy
and bade him go quickly to Nilachala.

**226.** Seeing Sri Abhirama's condition, Narottama
is utterly distraught — from his eyes flow streams like a river.

**227.** At the sight of the service of Gopinatha [his] heart overflows;
how he took his leave cannot be told.

**228.** With all the devotees dear to the Lord
who lived in those parts, he met.

**229.** Remembering the qualities of the devotees, floating in tears,
in a very few days he went to Nilachala.

**230.** There Gopinatha Acharya and the Lord's other associates
keep gazing at the road, [awaiting] Narottama.

**231.** They all had an earlier order from the Lord
to console Narottama in Nilachala.

**232.** What was in the hearts of those dear to the Lord —
is there anyone who could describe it?

**233.** Gopinatha says to Kanai Khutiya:[^38]
"It seems to me that I shall soon see Narottama.

**234.** Until now the body has remained by the Lord's will;
how many more days will it remain like this?"

**235.** He says: "I have heard everything from people:
he will come to Nilachala by way of Navadvipa.

**236.** It seems there is not long now before he comes here."
Thus they say much — who has the power to understand their state?

**237.** Sri Shikhi Mahiti and others say to Gopinatha:[^39]
"The time has come for the darshan of Sri Jagannatha."

**238.** Hearing this, Gopinatha Acharya, with the devotees dear [to the Lord],
went for the darshan of Jagannatha-deva.

**239.** They speak with one another about Sri Narottama —
of how the Lord drew [him] in the village of Ramakeli.[^40]

**240.** Talking all the while of how the Lord's grace had been shown,
they go to Jagannatha's temple by way of the Simha-dvara.[^41]

**241.** From separation from the Lord their bodies are extremely thin;
still they are like the sun, though dimmed.

**242.** What can one say? All of them are embodiments of compassion;
whoever sees them even once floats in tears.

**243.** Standing far off, Narottama sees them all;
streams flow from his eyes, his heart is unsteady.

**244.** "These are the Lord's dear ones," he reasons thus in his mind;
"I learned of them from a certain brahmana."[^42]

**245.** Here at the Simha-dvara someone says to another,
"Today, I think, Narottama will come."

**246.** Just as this was said, they received the happy news,
and all stood gazing at Narottama.

**247.** Sri Narottama's body is full of bhakti;
from his two long eyes streams of tears flow without cease.

**248.** Wondrous is the way of prema! His heart unsteady,
falling to the ground he bows to the Lord's associates.

**249.** All, in the rapture of prema, embraced Narottama
and drenched Narottama's body with tears.

**250.** Though cruel sorrow burns them unceasingly,
still joy arose in them all.

**251.** All, showing him great favor and saying many things,
went before Jagannatha, taking Narottama with them.

**252.** Narottama, his heart unsteady in the rapture of prema,
beholds the beauty of Jagannatha and Baladeva.

**253.** Surpassing a mass of clouds and collyrium, silver and the kunda flower,[^43]
to the radiance of Their beauty millions of Kandarpas are offered up.

**254.** The moons of Their faces light up the three worlds;
how Their lotus eyes enchant the world!

**255.** How mighty Their arms, how charming Their pose!
Many beautiful ornaments glitter.

**256.** On the two sides the two Lords, Subhadra in the middle;
They shine together with the Sudarshana disc.[^44]

**257.** Gazing with unblinking eyes, Narottama,
overcome by bhava, cannot hold his heart steady.

**258.** Seeing that wondrous behavior, the Lord's dear ones
were overwhelmed; their tears could not be checked.

**259.** Gopinatha Acharya calmed Narottama;
the Lord's servant brought garland prasada and gave it to him.

**260.** Taking Narottama, the Acharya, slowly, slowly,
came back from Jagannatha's temple to his own home.

**261.** All the Lord's dear ones who were in Nilachala
heard of Narottama's arrival.

**262.** Though cruel sorrow burns them every moment,
still the hearts of all rejoiced.

**263.** To bring him to meet them all, Gopinatha Acharya
sent the brahmana Jagannatha with Narottama.

**264.** Narottama goes with him everywhere;
how he meets the Lord's people I have no power to tell.

**265.** At the sight of Haridasa Thakura's samadhi,[^45]
the lament he made — who knows how to describe it?

**266.** To where Gadadhara Pandita Gosvami had dwelt
he went, extremely agitated.

**267.** He bowed to Gopinatha, falling to the ground;[^46]
seeing that beauty, he weeps, [remembering] Gadadhara's qualities.

**268.** Seeing the behavior of the Pandita's dear ones who live there,
he sheds tears from both his eyes.

**269.** Sri Mamu Gosvami, beholding Narottama,[^47]
embraced him, becoming extremely unsteady.

**270.** Bathing him with tears, he says again and again,
"By the Lord's will it was granted me to meet you.

**271.** From the Vaishnavas coming and going I heard everything;
I longed to see you, and I have seen you."

**272.** Saying many such things, he took Narottama by the hand,
led him to a secluded place and again speaks softly, softly:

**273.** O Narottama! As I behold this garden, the Tota,[^48]
my heart weeps ceaselessly; I cannot restrain it.

**274.** Within the garden one sees a most lovely place;[^49]
the joyous sports that took place here the fortunate ones saw.

**275.** My lord Gadadhara, sitting here,
would read the *Srimad-Bhagavatam*, his heart overwhelmed.

**276.** Lifting his beautiful face, he would speak all the meanings,
and in them flowed so many, many rivers of the bliss of prema.

**277.** Who would not long to hear those words?
Whoever hears them even once never forgets them.

**278.** Prabhu Gaurahari, the Lord of Gadadhara's life,
sitting here, would listen to the sweetness of that exposition.

**279.** Right here sits Prabhu Nityananda Raya;
Sri Advaita Acharya Prabhu used to sit here.

**280.** Here Sri Svarupa Damodara and Vakreshvara,
Sri Murari Gupta, here Dasa Gadadhara.

**281.** Sri Mukunda and Narahari, sitting right here,
gaze fixedly at the Gosvami's face.

**282.** Ramananda Raya and the Lord's other dear ones
sit in all these places, their splendor like the sun's.

**283.** Who can describe the beauty of the Lord's associates?
The assembly of the gods is put to shame beholding it.

**284.** At the time of the Ratha-yatra they sported here in this way;
thinking of all that, the heart breaks.

**285.** O Narottama! With Dasa Gadadhara
he used to hold many conversations here in seclusion.

**286.** Showing affection to Narahari of Khanda,
what he said here I cannot recount.

**287.** All that Sri Gosvami, taking Damodara with him,[^50]
said here has remained in the heart.

**288.** Knowing it was the time of service, Prabhu Gaurachandra
stands here before Gopinatha.

**289.** Seeing that adornment, He praises it again and again;[^51]
remembering all that, I do not know what my heart does.

**290.** Service to Gopinatha and residence in Kshetra —
these were the Gosvami's two vows; there was no going anywhere else.[^52]

**291.** Sri Gaurasundara would be staying in Nilachala;
that was the reason for the vow: His company is hard to leave.

**292.** When Gauranga went from Kshetra elsewhere,
the Gosvami would abandon his vow and go with Him.

**293.** In so many ways Sri Gaurasundara forbids him;
still Ratnavati's son is restless.[^53]

**294.** O Narottama, how much can I tell of his conduct?
How he went with the Lord is known everywhere.

**295.** *Thus [it is said] in the Chaitanya-charitamrita:*[^54]
"When Gadadhara Pandita set out to go along [with Him],
the Lord forbade him: 'Do not give up your kshetra-sannyasa.'

**296.** The Pandita says, 'Wherever You are, that is Nilachala;
let my kshetra-sannyasa go down to Rasatala.'

**297.** The Lord says, 'Serve Gopinatha here.'
The Pandita says, 'To see Your feet is millions of services.'

**298.** The Lord says, 'If you abandon service, the fault will fall on Me;
stay here and serve — that is My satisfaction.'

**299.** The Pandita says, 'All the fault is on me.
I will not go with You; I will go alone.

**300.** I will go to see Ai, not for Your sake;[^55]
the fault of abandoning my vow and service — I bear it.'

**301.** Saying this, Pandita Gosani went separately;
on reaching Cuttack, the Lord had him brought.[^56]

**302.** The Pandita's love for Chaitanya cannot be fathomed:
his vow and his service to Krishna he cast away like a straw.

**303.** With his conduct the Lord is inwardly pleased;
taking his hand, He speaks with loving anger:

**304.** 'To abandon your vow and your service — that was your aim;
it has been achieved: you have left them and come to a distant land.

**305.** You wish to stay with Me — you desire your own happiness;
both your duties are being lost, and this is grief to Me.

**306.** If you want My happiness, go to Nilachala;
you are under My oath if you say anything more.'

**307.** Saying this, Mahaprabhu boarded the boat,
and the Pandita fell unconscious right there.

**308.** He sent Sarvabhauma off to take the Pandita [back];[^57]
the Bhattacharya says, 'Get up — such is the Lord's lila.'"

**309.** Seeing this wondrous behavior, the Lord's dear ones
were amazed; all understood the reason.

**310.** By the Lord's order, Sarvabhauma and all the others
brought the Gosvami back, consoling him at length.

**311.** Until Sri Gaurachandra came back to Kshetra,
he passed his days here in great distress.

**312.** It is manifest everywhere why he has this privilege:
the Pandita, king of brahmanas, lord of ascetics, is supremely magnanimous.

**313.** *Thus [it is said] in the Kadacha composed by Sri Svarupa Gosvami:*
*"The best of brahmanas, the lord of ascetics named Sri Pandita — he indeed is Radha in the descent of Srila Gaura. Such too is the essential view of Narahari Sarakara and of me, Damodara — the Lord's own beloved ones."*[^58]

**314.** O Narottama! What shall I say of his ways?
His Lord of life is Gaura — this is known everywhere.

**315.** He could never bear separation from Gauranga;
he always swims in the ocean of the bliss of seeing [Him].

**316.** When Sri Gaurasundara came here from Vrindavana
together with His dear associates,

**317.** Pandita Gosvami, gazing at the Lord,
fainted here in the bliss of prema.

**318.** Here the fortunate ones saw a great festivity;
O Narottama, there is no end to telling of it.

**319.** Prabhu Nityananda, coming from Gauda,
standing here, beheld Sri Gopinatha.

**320.** His joyful meeting with Pandita Gosani,
known everywhere — the fortunate ones saw it.

**321.** *Thus [it is said] in the Chaitanya-bhagavata:*[^59]
"Seeing the beautiful face with the flute at its lips and the graceful bend of [His] body,
Nityananda shed tears of bliss without limit.

**322.** Learning of Nityananda's arrival, Gadadhara,
leaving his reading of the *Bhagavata*, came quickly.

**323.** As soon as the two saw each other's faces,
clasping each other's necks, they began to weep.

**324.** The two bow to each other;
the two proclaim each other's greatness.

**325.** The two say, 'Today the eyes have become pure';
the two say, 'Today my birth has borne fruit.'

**326.** There is no outward awareness in the bodies of the two Prabhus;
the two Prabhus float in the ocean of prema-bhakti.[^60]

**327.** Such was this manifestation of prema-bhakti
that, seeing it, all the servants on every side fall down and weep.

**328.** What wondrous love between Nityananda and Gadadhara!
With one whom the one dislikes, the other does not speak.

**329.** Such is the resolve of Gadadhara-deva:
he does not look at the face of one who reviles Nityananda.

**330.** One who has no love for Nityananda Svarupa —
Pandita Gosani does not even let him see him."

**331.** O Narottama, my heart weeps when I remember it:
here the meeting of the two Prabhus took place.

**332.** Here the two, becoming calm, sat for some time
and sang of the deeds of Sri Chaitanya.

**333.** Pandita Gosani invited the son of Padmavati[^61]
to take his meal here that day.

**334.** Prabhu Nityananda, for Gadadhara's sake,
had brought a maund of rice from Gauda.[^62]

**335.** In His heart was this wish — others do not understand such ways:
"The Pandita will offer [the rice] to Gopinatha and eat it."

**336.** Here He gave that rice to Sri Pandita;
Pandita Gosani, seeing it, praises [the rice] in many ways.

**337.** Here Sri Pandita cooked that rice
and made a dish of greens picked in the Tota.

**338.** Quickly he made a sour dish of tender tamarind leaves;
the fragrance of the rice spread through the whole Tota.

**339.** Offering the bhoga to Gopinatha, he kept it here;
suddenly Gaura-raya, the Knower of hearts, arrived.

**340.** Laughing, He says, "Such a secret affair of you two!
Don't you know that I have a share in it?

**341.** I am never separate from you two;
you have done something improper — I wish to say a word."

**342.** Hearing this, in great joy Sri Pandita Gadadhara
set the prasada rice before the Lord.

**343.** The Lord says, "Making three equal portions,
we three will eat this rice, sitting together."

**344.** Saying this, quickly dividing the rice into three portions,
Gaurahari sat down here to eat.

**345.** On the right Sri Nityananda, on the left the Pandita —
thinking of that beauty, the heart loses awareness.

**346.** Sri Gaurachandra eats, smiling slightly,
praising the fine greens and the sour dish of tamarind leaves.

**347.** Sri Nityananda eats with a joyful heart,
smiling softly and glancing at the Gosvami.

**348.** Pandita Gosani eats in supreme bliss;
such delight arises that there is no end to telling it.

**349.** Having rinsed their mouths, the three sat here;
thinking of all that, the heart breaks.

**350.** O Narottama, look, see here in this solitude:
on this worn seat Sri Gosvami used to sit.

**351.** Here Gaurahari, the life of the Gosvami,
used to come alone and sit on this seat.

**352.** In relishing the verses of the *Bhagavata* tears would fall;
the book is soaked with them — see it here with your own eyes.[^63]

**353.** All the pastimes of the two here in the Tota —
I have no power to tell them.

**354.** O Narottama, here Gaurahari
softly told the Pandita something — I do not know what.

**355.** From the eyes of both, streams flowed profusely;
seeing that, even a heart of stone melts.

**356.** Who can understand the acts of the crest-jewel of sannyasis?
Suddenly He plunged the earth into darkness.

**357.** He entered this temple of Gopinatha,[^64]
vanished from sight, and did not come out again.[^65]

**358.** What happened at the time of the Lord's disappearance —
even with a hundred thousand mouths I could not tell it.

**359.** Here the Gosvami fell unconscious;
here the weeping of all the mahantas arose.

**360.** The Lord, affectionate to His devotees, Gaura, the jewel of virtues —
how He consoled everyone, I do not know how to tell.

**361.** The Lord gave the Gosvami this instruction:
"Srinivasa, a brahmana's son, has suffered great pain.

**362.** He is coming along the road; hearing of My disappearance,
he resolved to give up his life.[^66]

**363.** I consoled him; he will come here,
and his life will be saved by your mercy.

**364.** You know all truths — what more need I say?
Remain for some days, as I wish."

**365.** Saying this and much more, the Lord steadied him somewhat;
after some days Srinivasa came right here.

**366.** How full of prema he is! Tears stream from his eyes incessantly;
he is in his early youth — what a wondrous form!

**367.** O Narottama, here Srinivasa,
falling to the ground, bowed at the Gosvami's feet.

**368.** Spreading both arms, the Gosvami took him to his breast
and bathed Srinivasa's body with his tears.

**369.** As a father and mother cherish a son,
so was the Gosvami's feeling for Srinivasa.

**370.** What mercy the Gosvami showed him —
remembering all that, I know not what becomes of my heart.

**371.** Having sent Srinivasa off to Vrindavana,[^67]
he sat here, distraught.

**372.** Day by day that tender body grew thin;
with his tears he watered the earth night and day.

**373.** Again and again long sighs, like tongues of flame;
suddenly he disappeared here.

**374.** What happened at that time cannot be told;
only life remained [in me] — by his will.

**375.** He had told me about you earlier;[^68]
at such a time of sorrow I have seen you.

**376.** Although my heart has been burned,
still I have found joy — such is his command.

**377.** O Narottama, always hold to patience;
tell all this to Srinivasa, dear to the Lord.

**378.** From Nilachala go quickly to the land of Gauda
and, giving bhakti, bring the living beings to fulfilment.

**379.** Lord Chaitanya's mercy is upon you;
you will destroy the misfortune of many people.

**380.** You will be immersed in the ocean of the joy of sankirtana;
in great bliss you will reveal the Lord's innermost desires.

**381.** Saying this and much more, embracing him in the rapture of prema,
he bade him farewell, entrusting him to Gopinatha.

**382.** Narottama went to the house of Kashi Mishra;
[there] he met Sri Gopala-guru.[^69]

**383.** He, with great affection for Narottama,
speaks softly in very sweet words:

**384.** "Only life remains in me, by the Lord's will;
seeing this place, my heart breaks.

**385.** O Narottama, see: in utter solitude
the Lord used to sit alone on this seat of grass.

**386.** Here Mahaprabhu used to lie down,
and Sri Govinda used to massage His feet.[^70]

**387.** Here He revealed prema rare even for Brahma and others —
who can understand the lila of Krishna Chaitanya?"

**388.** Narottama, seeing the Lord's bed,
rolled on the ground and wept much.

**389.** Sri Gopala-guru, his heart utterly overcome,
taking Narottama to his breast, weeps aloud.

**390.** Sri Gopala-guru, after a while becoming steady,
steadied Narottama, consoling him at length.

**391.** All the places where the Lord was absorbed in bhava
he showed to Narottama.

**392.** He told of the charming deeds of Sri Vakreshvara
and entrusted [him] to the lotus feet of Sri Radha-kanta.[^71]

**393.** Narottama, having bowed, together with Jagannatha
set out for the darshan of the Gundicha temple.[^72]

**394.** The brahmana Jagannatha says to Narottama:
"Along this road Nilachala-chandra goes in procession.

**395.** Here the Lord danced before the chariot;
His sankirtana filled [all] the worlds.

**396.** Here He pushed the chariot with His head;
Brahma and others, seeing the Lord's lila, offered praises.

**397.** Here He showed mercy to Sri Prataparudra;
the Lord's associates rejoiced at heart.

**398.** Here Mahaprabhu, with His own associates,
spoke much, watching the procession of Sri Lakshmi.[^73]

**399.** In this garden the Lord with His associates
joyfully partook of Sri maha-prasada.

**400.** Look, here is the lovely Gundicha temple;
here the son of Shachi performed many lilas.

**401.** The joy there was in the cleaning of the Gundicha temple
I cannot describe, even if I had a hundred thousand mouths.[^74]

**402.** Together with the devotees, Sri Chaitanya Bhagavan
bathed in this Indradyumna-sarovara."[^75]

**403.** Thus the most learned brahmana Jagannatha Dasa
showed [him] all the places where the Lord displayed His pastimes.

**404.** He brought Narottama to the Acharya's house
and told the Acharya how Narottama had behaved.[^76]

**405.** The mercy that the Acharya and the others showed Narottama
I could not describe here in detail.

**406.** Everyone says, "We shall not see Srinivasa again;
you will give him all this news.

**407.** Shyamananda, the disciple of Sri Hridaya Chaitanya —
hearing about him, we felt joy.

**408.** Had he come soon, perhaps we would have met him."
Thus they say much, and tears pour from their eyes.

**409.** When they had hastily sent Narottama off,
what became of them I cannot tell.

**410.** Narottama set out from Nilachala;
he was seized by an eager longing to see Shyamananda.

**411.** In Utkala Shyamananda dwells joyfully;
together with his disciples he relishes sankirtana.

**412.** Delivering utterly ignorant unbelievers,
he bestows prema-bhakti, rare even for the gods.

**413.** Hearing from people's mouths of Mahashaya's coming,
he went out to meet him with his followers in great joy.

**414.** What a wondrous meeting the fortunate saw!
Shyamananda-deva seemed to regain his life.

**415.** He brought Sri Mahashaya to his own abode;
the people of Nrisimhapura were overjoyed.[^77]

**416.** I cannot describe here in detail how the two behaved;
the wonderful affection of both became known [to all].

**417.** Narottama and Shyamananda, sitting in seclusion,
spent days and nights in various discussions.

**418.** Having told Shyamananda about Sri Kshetra,
he set out for the land of Gauda, deeply stirred.

**419.** Shyamananda quickly set out for Nilachala;
Sri Thakura Mahashaya came to the land of Gauda.

**420.** Seeing Shrikhanda, he sheds tears from his eyes;
he enters the house of Thakura Narahari.

**421.** Hearing that Narottama had come, Sarakara Thakura
came into a state beyond words.

**422.** He says to his followers, "When I travelled to Gauda and back,
I met his father there.

**423.** He is a ruler, Krishnananda Raya by name;
in his house this one was born by the Lord's will.[^78]

**424.** The Lord will accomplish many works through him.
Where is Narottama? Let me see — bring him here."

**425.** At that moment Narottama, before the Thakura,
bows down; streams flow from his eyes like a river.

**426.** Sri Thakura, gazing at Narottama,
embraces him in an outburst of affection and bathes him with tears.

**427.** What [the Thakura] mercifully asked Narottama,
Narottama relates slowly, slowly.

**428.** Hearing all about how the residents of the Kshetra live,
he became so distressed that he cannot hold his heart.

**429.** After a while, becoming composed, he says to Narottama,
"You came quickly; therefore I have seen you with my own eyes.

**430.** The Lord will fulfil your desire:
you will live long and will spread bhakti."

**431.** Saying much in this way, he entrusted [him] to Raghunandana;
he took him to the courtyard of Mahaprabhu.[^79]

**432.** At the darshan of Gaurachandra, the enchanter of the worlds,
Narottama, overwhelmed by prema, bows down in the courtyard.

**433.** There he met the masters;
Sri Raghunandana sent him to Yajigrama.

**434.** To the abode of Srinivasa Acharya in Yajigrama
Narottama went, his heart restless.

**435.** Sri Acharya, together with everyone, is outside the house;
how he looks at Narottama — who can tell?

**436.** Before he could bow, he embraced Narottama
and, introducing him, made him known to everyone.

**437.** What he asks Narottama, sitting in private,
Narottama relates in agitation.

**438.** About Navadvipa and other places, about Nilachala —
he wants to tell it all, and there is no end to it.

**439.** How the Acharya became as he heard all this —
seeing that, even others cannot keep their composure.

**440.** What was in the hearts of the two — how can others understand?
Restraining their weeping, after a while they grew calm.

**441.** Srinivasa Acharya says to Narottama,
"You must go to the village of Kheturi without delay.

**442.** What is there to say — soon it will be revealed what this is for:
you will arrange the service of the Deity.

**443.** With everyone I shall soon come there;
do not worry if there is some delay in this."

**444.** Saying much in this way, with a heart full of anxiety,
he gave him people to accompany him and quickly sent him off.

**445.** As Narottama entered Kantaka-nagara,
streams flowed from both his eyes — he could not stop them.

**446.** Hearing that Narottama had come, Dasa Gadadhara —
even in his terrible grief, joy filled his heart.

**447.** Narottama, going up to Dasa Gadadhara,
bows down, rolling on the ground.

**448.** Seeing Narottama, Sri Dasa Gadadhara
takes him in his arms and bathes his body with tears.

**449.** Seating him close by, what he asked,
Narottama, agitated, related.

**450.** When the Thakura heard it, his heart is torn apart;
he heaves long sighs like tongues of flame.

**451.** What he said, showing mercy to Narottama,
I could not describe, for fear of making the book too long.

**452.** Entrusting him to the reddish feet of Sri Gaurachandra,
he quickly sent him off to the village of Kheturi.[^80]

**453.** Gazing at Gorachand, the life of Dasa Gadadhara,
Narottama cannot keep his composure.

**454.** Where Mahaprabhu accepted sannyasa —
seeing that place, he cannot restrain himself.[^81]

**455.** Losing consciousness, he fell to the ground
and wet the earth with the tears of his eyes.

**456.** He weeps in a way that cannot be told;
he accepts no consolation — his heart overflows constantly.

**457.** The Lord's associates who lived in various places —
there he had a meeting with all of them.

**458.** Taking leave of them all and consoling them much,
Narottama went on through the land of Rarh.

**459.** In the land of Rarh there is a village named Ekachakra,
where Prabhu Nityananda-Rama took birth.

**460.** As Narottama entered the village of Ekachakra,
Prabhu appeared to him in the form of an old brahmana.[^82]

**461.** The places where Prabhu sported with His associates —
all those places He showed to Narottama.

**462.** Prabhu could not deceive Narottama;
how He revealed Himself before his eyes — who can describe?

**463.** Narottama, seeing Nityananda-Balarama,
fell unconscious; streams flow from his eyes without cease.

**464.** By the Lord's will, after a while he came to himself;
the Lord forbade him to reveal this to others.

**465.** Narottama, offering himself at [His] holy feet,
circumambulated Ekachakra with a joyful heart.

**466.** Bowing to all the residents of Ekachakra,
he went on, absorbed in the qualities of Nityananda.

**467.** Asking people the way to the village of Kheturi,
he very quickly came to the bank of the Padmavati.[^83]

**468.** When, having crossed the Padmavati, he was going to Kheturi,
the villagers came forward to receive him.

**469.** What is there to say — they are all supremely fortunate:
seeing Narottama, their minds and hearts were soothed.

**470.** In the joy of their hearts one says to another,
"This wondrous renunciation has no comparison."

**471.** One says, "In my mind there is this worry:
since this is his own kingdom, will he stay here or not?"[^84]

**472.** One says, "For a Vaishnava all places are the same;
descending [into the world], he delivers the unbelievers."

**473.** One says, "Here there is no end of unbelievers;
though this is his own kingdom, will he stay in this place?"

**474.** One says, "To deliver all these lands
he has come — I have weighed it in my heart: it is true."

**475.** Though they said this, everyone had this doubt:
he will go to other holy places, leaving darkness here.

**476.** Saying this, all shed streams of tears
and gaze fixedly at Narottama.

**477.** At that very moment a voice came from the sky:
"Here Narottama will dwell joyfully without cease.

**478.** Having appeared [in the world] by the Lord's will,
he delivers the unbelievers, giving them the gift of bhakti."

**479.** Many such voices resounded; hearing them, [all] were amazed
and bow again and again at Narottama's feet.

**480.** Surrounding Mahashaya, all, with joy in their hearts,
enter the village — what wondrous splendor!

**481.** They led [him] to a most charming, utterly secluded [place];
Mahashaya stayed in that place.

**482.** Sri Kheturi is a very large village, a holy land;
here and there within it are wondrous settlements with other names.[^85]

**483.** The site of the capital is Gopalpura.
Such are the names of the villages; many wealthy people live [there].

**484.** All are sunk in false happiness and have no knowledge of dharma;
they do not know how good will come to them hereafter.

**485.** Seeing them all, Sri Thakura Mahashaya
shows such mercy as cannot be told.

**486.** Taking Sri Santosha Raya and all the others with him,
he speaks of the Acharya, deeply stirred.[^86]

**487.** Here, meanwhile, Sri Acharya with his companions in Yajigrama
finds no peace, having sent Narottama away.

**488.** In Khanda they heard that Narottama had left that day;
all go over his lovely deeds in their minds.

**489.** Sri Raghunandana came to Yajigrama
and quickly set about arranging the Acharya's marriage.

**490.** In Yajigrama lives Sri Gopala Chakravarti;
he has a great longing to give his daughter to the Acharya.

**491.** To the brahmana Sri Gopala Dasa, Sri Raghunandana
speaks very sweet words in private:

**492.** "A worthy groom for your daughter is Srinivasa."
Hearing this, Gopala was overjoyed.

**493.** He told his friends and kin of the proposal of marriage;
all say: "Give your daughter in marriage at once."

**494.** On the auspicious third day of the dark fortnight of Vaishakha
he gives his daughter in marriage to Srinivasa Acharya.

**495.** Formerly everyone called the daughter Draupadi;
at the time of the wedding her name became Ishvari.[^87]

**496.** What loveliness is hers — as if cast in gold!
She is bhakti embodied; there is no limit to her virtues.

**497.** How Ishvari shone when, at the time of the wedding,
the Acharya gave [her] the initiation mantra — I cannot describe.

**498.** Incidentally I will say: Sri Gopala, the best of brahmanas,
soon became a disciple of the Acharya.

**499.** Shyamadasa and Ramachandra are the sons of Gopala;
some call [them] Shyamananda and Ramacharana.[^88]

**500.** Both are disciples of the Acharya, of wondrous character;
here I have told it briefly — this is known everywhere.

**501.** Sri Gopala Chakravarti, having given away his daughter,
showed the highest honor to all.

**502.** The villagers — all, women and men alike —
all say: "Blessed, blessed is Gopala the brahmana!"

**503.** Thus Srinivasa Acharya married;
at this great joy arose in everyone.

**504.** Sri Sarakara Thakura, hearing the news of the wedding,
was so filled with fatherly love that I do not know how to tell it.[^89]

**505.** Dasa Gadadhara and others, hearing it, overcome with affection,
praise Srinivasa greatly among themselves.

**506.** Here Srinivasa constantly
teaches the books of the Gosvamis to his disciples.

**507.** Boundless is the power of Srinivasa Acharya's learning;
hearing of it, all are amazed at heart.

**508.** The sons of Dvija Haridasa, dear to Gaura —
Shridasa and Gokulananda — both reflect:[^90]

**509.** "In separation from the Lord, our father went to Vrindavana;
he ordered [us] to become disciples of this Acharya.

**510.** He came here from Vraja only a few days ago.
Why delay? Let us go quickly to see him."

**511.** Saying this, the two went to Yajigrama,
and at the sight of the Acharya their hearts rejoiced.

**512.** Their father's command was fulfilled before their eyes;
they sank into the ocean of the nectar of prema for Radha and Krishna.

**513.** When the Acharya asked, they told him who they were;
both bow to the Acharya again and again.

**514.** Learning who they were, Sri Acharya, overwhelmed by prema,
showed [them] great honor and is bathed in tears.

**515.** Shridasa and Gokulananda both entreat:
"Give [us] the initiation mantra; have mercy, O merciful one!"

**516.** The Acharya says: "There must be a little more delay."
Saying this, he had them make an auspicious beginning of the books.

**517.** The two study the books of the Gosvamis;
their wondrous zeal cannot be described.

**518.** Srinivasa Acharya has great affection for both.
In this way his own people come and gather [around him] day after day.

**519.** One day, in Yajigrama, Acharya Thakura
went to the bank of the pond to the west of his house.

**520.** He sits there with his companions, radiant like the sun;
with compassionate eyes he looks toward the road.[^91]

**521.** He sees a man on a splendid palanquin
who, having married, is going home in fine array.

**522.** His beauty is like Kandarpa's; he is adorned with jewels;
his body is exceedingly tender, surpassing fresh butter.

**523.** His beauty robs the golden ketaki and the champaka of their pride;
on his head his glossy hair gleams.

**524.** His bright forehead, brows and eyes are enchanting;
his ears, nose and the luster of his cheeks are peerless.

**525.** His moonlike face is lovely, his lips are rosy;
his neck is like a lion's, his throat like a conch, his chest broad.

**526.** Lovely are his belly and navel, [the belly] marked with three folds;
his arms reach his knees, his fingers are graceful.

**527.** His waist is slender, his knees and feet are beautiful;
he wears fine, new, wondrous cloth.

**528.** Seeing him, the Acharya reflects thus:
"Is this a Gandharva's son, or one of the Ashvini-kumaras?[^92]

**529.** What wondrous youth! One would take him for a celestial.
This body will be fruitful if he worships Krishna."

**530.** Having thus reflected, he asks the people with him:
"What is his name? What is his caste? Where does this bridegroom live?"

**531.** Someone, bowing, says: "This is a great pandita
named Ramachandra, known as the king of poets,[^93]

**532.** a conqueror of all quarters, a physician, foremost among the famed,
born of the Vaidya caste; he lives in Kumara-nagara."

**533.** Hearing all this, the merciful Sri Acharya,
smiling gently, went to his home.

**534.** Ramachandra, listening intently to all this,
beheld the Acharya from the palanquin.

**535.** Surrendering himself [to him], he thinks thus within his mind:
"How soon will I see him again?"

**536.** Utterly composed, he remained silent;
reaching home, he passed the day with great difficulty.

**537.** At night, coming to the house of a brahmana,
he meditates on the Acharya's feet with a restless heart.

**538.** At daybreak, coming before the Acharya,
he weeps as he gazes at the Acharya.

**539.** Like a tree cut at the root he falls to the ground;
again and again he bows down and cannot compose himself.

**540.** What he says to the Acharya in a choked voice —
who could hear it and keep his composure?

**541.** Laying his head at the Acharya's feet,
he lies on the ground, covered with dust.

**542.** The Acharya, taking both his arms in his two hands,
raised [him] up and joyfully embraces him tightly.

**543.** Placing his hand on [his] head and blessing him,
with tears he speaks slowly, slowly:

**544.** "Birth after birth you are my dearest friend;
today Providence, being kind, has brought [us] together.

**545.** In the same way it united [me] with Narottama in Vrindavana —
who does not pine without cease for his virtues?

**546.** He is one eye, you are the second eye;
you both are my eyes, you two are my two arms."

**547.** Ramachandra, on hearing the name of Narottama,
felt a natural prema awaken in his heart.

**548.** The Acharya knew the state of Ramachandra's mind
and told of Sri Narottama at length.

**549.** What arose in Ramachandra's mind on hearing this,
Ramachandra did not disclose to the Acharya.

**550.** Smiling, Sri Acharya says softly, softly:
"What you said in your mind will soon come to pass."

**551.** Saying this, he showed great favor
and had him begin reading the books of the Gosvamis.

**552.** Seeing [his] wondrous ability, with a joyful heart
he gave [him] initiation in the Radha-Krishna mantra at an auspicious moment.

**553.** Having become a disciple, Ramachandra floats in the rasa of bhakti;
day by day [his] wondrous prema grew.

**554.** All these events Kaviraja Karnapura
described most sweetly in his own book.[^94]

**555.** Ramachandra, full of prema, is like the Acharya himself;
whoever hears all this attains the jewel of bhakti.

**556.** Meditating on the feet of Srinivasa Acharya,
Narahari Dasa tells the *Bhakti-ratnakara*.

*Thus in the Sri Bhakti-ratnakara ends the eighth wave, called "The Description of Sri Narottama's Darshana of Sri Navadvipa, Nilachala and Other Places".*
