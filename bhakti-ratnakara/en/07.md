# Taranga 7. The Spreading of the Books of Bhakti

*Srinivasa, Narottama and Shyamananda journey to Gauda with the books; the books are stolen near Vishnupura; Srinivasa with King Vira Hambira: the reading of the Bhagavatam, the recovery of the books, mercy to the king; news sent to Vrindavana; accounts of the Lord's associates; Shrikhanda and Kantaka-nagara*

**1.** Glory, glory to Sri Krishna Chaitanya, the friend of the lowly!
Glory, glory to Nityananda, the ocean of compassion!

**2.** Glory to Sri Advaita-deva, the abode of virtues!
Glory to Sri Gadadhara Pandita, full of prema!

**3.** Glory to Srivasa Pandita, the giver of prema-bhakti!
Glory to Vakreshvara, Sri Murari and Haridasa!

**4.** Glory to Sarvabhauma, Kashi Mishra and Ramananda!
Glory to Vasudeva Ghosha, Madhava and Mukunda!

**5.** Glory to Dhananjaya and Sri Svarupa Damodara!
Glory to Narahari, Gauridasa and Kashishvara!

**6.** Glory to Gadadhara Dasa, Shridhara and Vijaya!
Glory to Shuklambara Brahmachari and Sri Sanjaya![^1]

**7.** Glory to Gopala Bhatta, Sri Rupa and Sanatana!
Glory to Raghunatha Dasa, the life of the sorrowful!

**8.** Glory to Sri Bhugarbha, Lokanatha and Sri Raghava!
Glory to Raghunatha Bhatta and Yadava Acharya!

**9.** Glory, glory to Sri Jiva, the treasury of virtues!
Glory to the merciful Krishnadasa Kaviraja!

**10.** Glory, glory to Srinivasa Acharya Thakura!
Glory to Narottama, whose greatness is boundless!

**11.** Glory, glory to Shyamananda, whose deeds are limitless,
whose former name was Sri Duhkhini Krishnadasa![^2]

**12.** Glory to the Vaishnavas, the utmost limit of mercy,
by whose grace all perfection is attained![^3]

**13.** Glory, glory to the listeners, the abode of virtues!
Now listen with kind hearts to what I shall tell.

**14.** Srinivasa Acharya, taking the jewel-like books,
goes along the road to Gauda, remembering Gauranga.

**15.** With him is Narottama — such that only their bodies are different;
and Shyamananda — the object of the Acharya's deepest affection.

**16.** Srinivasa, with Narottama and Shyamananda,
goes along the road without obstacle, rejoicing.

**17.** Finding a company of people going to Nilachala,
they travel with them along the forest road.

**18.** Especially: the path by which Sri Chaitanya went —
by that path Sanatana went to Nilachala.[^4]

**19.** Asking in place after place where the Lord and [His] servant had stayed,
they look at all those places, losing their composure.

**20.** Travelling the forest road is the greatest joy;
on no day and nowhere does any danger arise.

**21.** In which lands and in which villages they stayed —
for fear of the book growing too long, I have not written it.

**22.** Everywhere the word spread: "A certain merchant
is going to Nilachala, carrying great wealth with him."

**23.** The robbers of King Vira Hambira[^5] carefully
made their calculations and saw: the cart was full of all kinds of jewels.[^6]

**24.** They went and tell the king: "A certain merchant
is carrying a cartful of priceless jewels."

**25.** Hearing it from the robbers' mouths, the king was delighted.
What the king's conduct was like, I shall tell a little.

**26.** He is always robbing, taking robbers with him;
seeing him, people tremble with fear all the time.

**27.** And of his other misdeeds there is no end in the telling;
there is only one [good thing]: he listens to the Puranas from a brahmana.

**28.** So Vira Hambira commanded his formidable robbers:
"Get ready and go right now.

**29.** Bring the cart with the wealth here secretly;
frighten them, but take no one's life."

**30.** Receiving the king's order, the robbers set off;
seeing them, decent folk tremble.

**31.** As the king is, so are all these followers of his:
robbing is their constant delight.

**32.** Going from Vana-Vishnupura to distant lands,
they joined all these travellers unnoticed.

**33.** Srinivasa Acharya and the others, together with the cart,
pass through Panchakuta[^7] and go along the road to Vishnupura.

**34.** "We have reached our own country without obstacle" — so they reason;
that the king in Vishnupura is wicked, this they do not know.

**35.** Near the capital, Vana-Vishnupura,
in the middle of the forest is a large village — there they came.

**36.** Eating and their other tasks they finished while it was still day,
and half the night they spent in the joy of talk about Krishna.

**37.** That night all lay down to sleep
and fell asleep — quite unaware.

**38.** The decent villagers think to themselves:
"Will Krishna protect this merchant?

**39.** They are all at ease, they know no danger;
and we cannot even warn them — we fear the king.

**40.** Here the wicked king, for the sake of a little wealth,
sends his robbers very far afield.

**41.** How did this merchant, with a cartful of wealth,
come along the road and get through?"

**42.** Someone says: "This is a righteous merchant;
that is why the robbers cannot steal his wealth."

**43.** Someone says: "The robbers are following on his heels;
who knows when they will come and attack."

**44.** So people say many things, staying in their homes.
Here, meanwhile, the robbers ponder various schemes.

**45.** Someone says: "Hey, brothers, do this deed,
so that we are not shamed among the brotherhood of robbers.

**46.** Near the village of Tamar[^8] you made ready,
but there you could not carry out your task.

**47.** Near Raghunathpur,[^9] in the night,
you were all defeated before them.

**48.** Now you have come near Vana-Vishnupura:
whatever strength and wits each of you has — show it here.

**49.** If today we give that king the cart with the wealth,
he will be pleased; otherwise he will kill us all."

**50.** Saying this, all gathering together
worship Chandi,[^10] offering her goats, sheep, buffaloes and other victims.

**51.** Bowing at Chandi's feet they say again and again:
"Grant us success and protect us all."

**52.** Saying much in this way, they sent ahead, to where the Acharya and the others were,
one of their best thieves.

**53.** He came and sees: all have fallen asleep;
knowing the moment was right, he went and informed the robbers.

**54.** The robbers came quickly in fearsome guise,
and, freely taking the cart, entered the forest.

**55.** At the end of the night, entering Vana-Vishnupura,
they told the king the whole story.

**56.** All the decent folk of Vana-Vishnupura
heard that the king had stolen great wealth.

**57.** Sitting in seclusion, someone says to another:
"The evil-minded king has done a very wicked deed.

**58.** From Vrindavana a merchant with his wealth
was going to the Kshetra for the darshan of Jagannatha.

**59.** This sinful villain has brought him grief;
I understand: there will never be deliverance for him."

**60.** Someone says into another's ear, weeping:
"Vana-Vishnupura will go to ruin.

**61.** There is no king so wicked in the land of Bharata;
no one is able to punish this sinner."

**62.** Someone says: "This is the way of this wicked king:
he will suffer in hell, there will never be any way out for him."

**63.** Someone says: "All this wicked man does is wrong;
tell me, how can any good come to him?"

**64.** Someone says: "The giver of good is Lord Narayana;
the mercy He showed in Kali cannot be described.

**65.** In Navadvipa, in a brahmana family, Jagai and Madhai,
two brothers, the crown jewels of great sinners —[^11]

**66.** those two wretches, for fear of whom people trembled,
the One who sports in Nadia mercifully delivered.

**67.** At their deliverance the gods, mingling with men,
offered so many prayers that there is no end to them.

**68.** Jagai and Madhai became kings among devotees;
who knows how to tell of His extraordinary deeds?"

**69.** Someone says: "That Krishna Chaitanya, Bhagavan,
gave the living beings a jewel rare even for Brahma and the others.

**70.** That Lord has hidden Himself in Nilachala;
who now will deliver such a wicked man?"

**71.** Someone says: "Hey, brother, I tell you:
such a wicked man is saved by the mercy of His devotee."

**72.** Someone says: "The sight of such a devotee is rare;
why would he come to this sinful land?"

**73.** Someone says: "The scriptures say this is the devotee's way:
to deliver the living beings he wanders through every land.

**74.** Through the devotee that Lord accomplishes all things;
without the devotee's mercy there is never success."

**75.** Someone says: "Ah, this is what I feel:
surely some great soul will come here.

**76.** By a particle of his mercy not a trace of sorrow will remain;
evil thinking will vanish, and the king will become a Vaishnava."

**77.** Saying this, they pray to the Lord again and again:
"Put an end to the king's lawless conduct."

**78.** So the decent folk think of [the king's] welfare.
Here, meanwhile, the king rejoices at home at gaining the wealth.

**79.** Greatly pleased with the robbers,
he gave them clothes and ornaments, praising them.

**80.** King Sri Vira Hambira reflects in his mind:
"This cart is surely from the western land.

**81.** For many days I have gained much wealth,
but such joy has never arisen in my heart.

**82.** I understand: there are priceless jewels in it."
Saying this, he looks toward the chest of books.

**83.** Quickly opening the chest of books himself,
he sees the jewel-books inside the chest.

**84.** As soon as he saw the books, his mind became pure;
again and again he gazes at the jewel-books.

**85.** Astonished, the king says to the astrologer:[^12]
"How did you make your reckoning? Tell me."

**86.** He says, "Maharaja, whenever I reckon,
at once I see that there are priceless jewels in this."

**87.** Hearing this, the king says, "Do not fear at all:
whatever you reckon at any time all proves true.

**88.** What you reckoned now as well is no false word:
in every way these are priceless jewels.

**89.** These priceless jewels are obtained only by great fortune."
Saying much like this, he looks at the robbers.

**90.** Anxious, he says to the robbers again and again,
"Did you kill anyone? Tell me the truth."

**91.** The robbers say, "They were all asleep;
we brought the cart away — none of them knew.

**92.** You yourself forbade us earlier:
why should we take lives? So the task was done in this way."

**93.** Hearing this, the king grew steady and says to the brahmanas:
"The evil deed I have done has borne its fruit at last.

**94.** I have caused pain in the heart of some great soul;
in the fire of his anger I shall surely be burnt to ashes.

**95.** If I obtain the darshan of the acharya of these books,
then I shall take shelter at his feet.

**96.** O brothers, this is what comes to my sinful mind:
he will surely show me mercy."

**97.** Saying this, he sent messengers to search,
and carefully kept the jewel-books together with the cart.

**98.** Hearing about the books, the king's wife
herself became eager to see them.

**99.** What shall I say? With the arrival of the jewel-books
the king's palace shone exceedingly.

**100.** Suddenly auspiciousness spread through Vishnupura;
all the evil endeavours of the people vanished.

**101.** King Vira Hambira always had this in mind:
"When shall I see him whose books these are?"

**102.** Thinking thus, the king became agitated;
at that very moment the goddess of sleep drew him away.

**103.** As if in a dream he sees a beautiful person
whose wondrous body surpasses a mountain of gold.[^13]

**104.** With his moon-like face he says, smiling and smiling,
"Do not worry: he will come and meet you.

**105.** His heart will be pleased with you;
birth after birth be his servant."

**106.** Saying this, he vanished, and at that moment
the king's sleep broke; the king floats in tears.

**107.** "What did I see, what did I see!" he says again and again;
he looks in all four directions, but does not reveal the secret.

**108.** Meanwhile, when the robbers had taken away the cart of books,
suddenly sleep broke, and everyone awoke.

**109.** Srinivasa Acharya and the others at dawn
search here and there in distress.

**110.** Finding no trace, they weep;
"What thunderbolt has struck!" says everyone.

**111.** Narottama says, "I shall give up my life."
Shyamananda says, "I shall enter this fire."

**112.** What came into the mind of Srinivasa Acharya —
the heart breaks to tell of it; what shall I say of it?

**113.** All the people who were with them, with aching hearts,
resolved: "We will not go home any more."

**114.** The news of the theft of the books became known everywhere;
the Acharya and the others sank in an ocean of great sorrow.

**115.** After some time, all of them, taking courage,
said to one another what cannot be described.

**116.** Suddenly someone says to Srinivasa,
"You will find the books in Vishnupura; go to the king."

**117.** Hearing these words, joy arose in his mind;
and besides, he sees various auspicious signs.

**118.** Understanding the Lord's design, consoling everyone,
Srinivasa says to Sri Narottama:

**119.** "Go quickly to the village of Kheturi
and carry out the order of Prabhu Lokanatha.

**120.** Send Shyamananda in good company:
by way of Ambika he will go to Utkala.

**121.** When the books are recovered, I will send word;
do not be anxious: you will come, and we shall all meet."

**122.** Saying much like this, he sent the two away;
how distressed the two were cannot be described.

**123.** Not transgressing the Acharya's word, the two
went to the village of Kheturi; their minds are not at peace.

**124.** Who can understand this lila of the Mahashaya?
On arriving, the very first thing he did was to infuse spiritual power (shakti) into Sri Santosha.[^14]

**125.** At the sight of Sri Narottama all the people
became greatly joyful and forgot their grief and sorrow.

**126.** With great care keeping the two in complete seclusion,
the wise, hearing the story of the theft of the books, were grieved.

**127.** Here Srinivasa, having sent the two away,
became distressed, unable to hold his heart.

**128.** Leaving his companions elsewhere,
he quickly entered Vana-Vishnupura alone.

**129.** Who will understand the heart of the mahanta?
In search of the books he wanders alone.

**130.** Here and there people say to one another,
"A wondrous person has come to Vishnupura.

**131.** Is he a god, or a portion of the Lord?
Seeing his beauty, whose composure is not shattered?"

**132.** Saying this, to have the darshan of the Acharya
people run from all four directions in delight.

**133.** A brahmana's son named Sri Krishnavallabha —
at the darshan of the Acharya prema arose in him.[^15]

**134.** He took him to his own house in Deuli
and surrendered himself at the Acharya's lotus feet.

**135.** Whatever Acharya Thakura asked him,
he told it all in order and in detail.

**136.** "The king is listening to the *Bhagavata*" — hearing this,
he goes to the royal assembly, taking Krishnavallabha with him.

**137.** Seeing the Acharya's radiance, the king respectfully
fell to the ground, bowed, and considered himself blessed.

**138.** He had a wonderful seat brought and seated him;
the Acharya forbids him to ask anything:

**139.** "O king, when the discourse on the *Bhagavata* is finished,
whatever you ask, I will tell you."

**140.** Saying, "As you command," the king reflects in his mind:
"He is surely the keeper of these jewel-books.[^16]

**141.** By my good fortune he has suddenly appeared before me;
I shall surrender myself at his feet."

**142.** Thinking thus, the king gazes with fixed eyes;
at the end the Acharya said something to the king.

**143.** The king's mind had already become pure;
he asks to hear the true meaning:

**144.** "O mahashaya, this is what is in my mind:
explain a verse of the *Bhagavata* with your own blessed mouth."

**145.** Hearing the king's words, Acharya Thakura
knew that the king's evil mind had gone far away.

**146.** The Acharya says, "What does your mind wish to hear?"
The king says, "Please speak something of the *Bhramara-gita*."[^17]

**147.** At the king's words he was immersed in happiness;
the king's reader placed the book before him.

**148.** Acharya Thakura carefully began the reading;
he poured a nectar-rain of unheard-of, wondrous meanings.

**149.** In the assembly tears flow from everyone's eyes;
King Sri Vira Hambira was overwhelmed.

**150.** The king's reader, named Vyasa Chakravarti —
who can tell what longing seized him?

**151.** All who were there at the time of the holy discourse —
from their manifestations prema arose in others as well.

**152.** Acharya Thakura forgot himself;
he cannot become steady, so great is his absorption.

**153.** Sri Vira Hambira, falling at the Acharya's feet,
is not steady, even though the discourse has concluded.

**154.** After some time, becoming composed, he thinks in his mind:
"I have committed a terrible offence at these feet."

**155.** Thus immersed in the rasa of humility, Sri Vira Hambira
floats in tears and cannot become steady.

**156.** Giving the Acharya lodging in a very secluded place,
at evening he quickly went and met him.

**157.** Bowing with joined palms, he makes his request:
"Prabhu, tell me in detail how you came."

**158.** Hearing these words, the Acharya with a joyful heart
says to the king, "Now I shall tell you briefly.

**159.** Svayam Bhagavan Krishna, the prince of Vraja,
concealed His manifest pastimes in Vraja.

**160.** When the time came, taking with Him His angas and upangas,[^18]
He descended in Navadvipa in great joy.

**161.** In Navadvipa the Lord performed wondrous pastimes;
Shesha, Shiva and others cannot describe them.

**162.** What is attested in the scriptures He made directly visible;
with the sacrifice of sankirtana He made the world mad with joy.

**163.** After living for some time at home with His associates,
He took sannyasa from Keshava Bharati.[^19]

**164.** He became renowned by the name Sri Krishna Chaitanya;
to show mercy to living beings He travelled to all the holy places.

**165.** To give happiness to His devotees He resided in Nilachala;
there is the wondrous play of the moving and the unmoving Brahman.[^20]

**166.** His dear devotees, viziers of the king of Gauda,[^21]
possessed of great wealth, great and profound panditas —

**167.** Rupa and Sanatana, whose names are known throughout the world —
renouncing everything, went to Vrindavana.

**168.** They lived there at Mahaprabhu's command
and, following the scriptures, recovered the lost holy places of Vraja.

**169.** They composed many books, an ocean of nectar;
they opened the treasury of the jewels of the pastimes of Vraja.

**170.** How many meanings of the *Srimad-Bhagavatam* and other [works] they revealed —
how much of it could I tell with a single mouth?

**171.** I, utterly unworthy, born in Gauda,
went to Vrindavana at the command of the Lords.

**172.** I became a disciple of Sri Gopala Bhatta Gosvami
and studied the books of the Gosvamis.

**173.** Sri Jiva Gosvami and other greatly wise ones
entrusted [the books to me] to make them known in Gauda.

**174.** I carefully brought them to this land;
some distance [from here], at the end of the night, the books were stolen.

**175.** All together we searched for them everywhere;
in many ways we held on to patience.

**176.** There is a king's son named Narottama —
of supreme renunciation, versed in all the scriptures.

**177.** And one named Shyamananda, mature in every respect;
those two I sent each to his own land.

**178.** The armed Vrajavasis who are with me
I have left all in one place, giving them lodging.

**179.** For the sake of the books I wandered everywhere;
hearing of the Purana recitation, I came here.

**180.** I have told something of what happened — what more is there to say?
Without the books my heart is breaking."

**181.** Hearing these words of Srinivasa Acharya,
the king, distraught, falls at his holy feet.

**182.** Weeping, he says: "I am the chief of robbers;
the evil deeds I have done I cannot tell.

**183.** When you, Prabhu, came by the forest road,
I received the news then and there from the mouths of messengers.

**184.** For the sake of [the expected] gain I rejoiced;
I had an astrologer calculate — he determined with certainty:

**185.** 'A very great mahajana is bringing great jewels;[^22]
with little searching they will surely be obtained.'

**186.** Hearing these words, I sent the robbers;
'Take no one's life' — this much I said.

**187.** The robbers brought the cart without trouble;
seeing the chest, I was overjoyed.

**188.** Opening the chest, I see the jewels of books;
at the mere sight of them my mind was turned.

**189.** I grew eager to see the keeper of the books
and quickly sent messengers to search for him.

**190.** You, Prabhu, know the hearts, you are the purifier of the fallen;
to me, a lowly one, you suddenly granted your darshan.

**191.** At the mere sight of you I surrendered myself at your feet;
forgive my offence and be merciful to me.

**192.** Seeing what a great sinner I am, do not scorn me;
do what will free me from sin."

**193.** Saying this, he fell at the Acharya's feet
and bathes the Acharya's feet with his tears.

**194.** Seeing how deeply distressed the king's heart was,
the Acharya showed him the utmost favour.

**195.** In endless conversation the night turned to dawn;
what can be said — the ocean of prema surged.

**196.** The king quickly brought those people of the Acharya
and gave them lodging in a lovely place.

**197.** The king carefully had the Acharya bathed
and took him to where the jewels of books were.

**198.** The Acharya's mind blossomed with great joy;
the bliss [he felt] on seeing the books cannot be described.

**199.** The king, having the books worshipped in various ways,
took the Acharya into the inner apartments.

**200.** The king's wife, seeing the Acharya —
how overwhelmed with joy she was, I do not know how to tell.

**201.** Bowing at the Acharya's two feet,
she considers herself blessed and floats in tears.

**202.** Sri Acharya, having shown mercy to the king's wife,
came with the king to the secluded lodging.

**203.** Again and again the king says, falling at his feet:
"Because of the evil deed I did, my heart knows no peace."

**204.** Knowing the king's heart, Acharya Thakura
says again and again: "Put away all worry.

**205.** I have entrusted you to the feet of Sri Krishna Chaitanya;
meditate on those lotus feet within your heart.

**206.** Always considering yourself an offender,
perform this nama-sankirtana without cease."

**207.** Saying this, to remove all the king's distress,
he instructed him in the Harinama maha-mantra.[^23]

**208.** Again he says to the king with sweet words:
"Always be attentive in hearing and chanting.

**209.** Sri Krishna Chaitanya Prabhu, the purifier of the world,
distributed this mantra of the Name to living beings.

**210.** O king, after the relishing of the Gosani's books[^24]
I will give you initiation into the mantra of Radha and Krishna."

**211.** Saying this and telling him a little about the angas of bhakti,
he set at rest the heart of King Vira Hambira.

**212.** The king together with his family, with joy in his heart,
sold himself at the feet of Srinivasa Acharya.

**213.** The stolen books found, the robber king delivered —
this news spread everywhere.

**214.** Sri Krishnavallabha, Vyasa and all the others
took shelter at the Acharya's lotus feet.

**215.** An ocean of joy surged in Vishnupura;
Bhakti-devi showed her favour in every house.

**216.** By the qualities of Sri Krishna Chaitanya, Nityananda and Advaita
all are overwhelmed and know nothing else.[^25]

**217.** Gadadhara, Srivasa and all the other Prabhus —
with the names and qualities of them all they are ceaselessly intoxicated.

**218.** A wondrous longing to see Vaishnavas grew;[^26]
deep attachment arose for Navadvipa and Vrindavana.

**219.** Singing the glory of Srinivasa Acharya,
they are immersed in such joy — who can describe it?

**220.** Everyone praises his own good fortune,
and all ceaselessly perform kirtana of the holy Name.

**221.** King Sri Vira Hambira, with joy in his heart,
joining his palms, says to the Acharya:

**222.** "O Prabhu, you have removed our sorrow;
you have bestowed a jewel hard for the gods to attain.

**223.** O Prabhu, now I submit at your holy feet:
that the books were stolen, everyone came to know.

**224.** That the books are found and I, a lowly robber, am subdued —
kindly write a letter about this and send it to Vrindavana.

**225.** And let the Gosvamis know this too:
that they all may show mercy to me, a sinner.

**226.** Where Sri Thakura Narottama and Shyamananda are —
let there be [your] order to send such a letter there too."

**227.** Hearing the king's words, the Acharya, who had already
written a letter himself, gave it into the king's hands.

**228.** Seeing the letter, the king was exceedingly glad;
Acharya Thakura again says to the king:

**229.** "The people who came with the cart from Vraja —
they will leave quickly, taking the cart."

**230.** Saying this, the Acharya himself, with care,
gave the letter to his companions, instructing them at length.

**231.** The king bowed to the ground before all those people;
the honour he showed them — who can describe it?

**232.** On the very cart on which the great jewels of books had come,
he carefully placed various goods.

**233.** For Sri Govinda, Gopinatha and Madanamohana
and for all the other places he gave them, dividing them up.

**234.** Taking those goods, the armed men
took their leave and quickly set off.

**235.** All of them with the cart, in great delight,
reaching Vrindavana, gave the letter to the Gosvami.

**236.** They told all the news from beginning to end;
hearing it, everyone's anxiety was dispelled.

**237.** Learning the detailed news by reading the letter,
in great joy, saying much, they wish [them] well.

**238.** The goods that Sri Vira Hambira had sent
Sri Jiva Gosvami distributed everywhere.

**239.** With the thought "Srinivasa will send a letter,"[^27]
Sri Jiva Gosvami is in great joy at every moment.

**240.** Meanwhile King Sri Vira Hambira, making haste,
sent his master's letter to Kheturi.

**241.** Sri Thakura Mahashaya, together with Shyamananda,
distressed with worry, is staying in seclusion.[^28]

**242.** Coming to the village of Kheturi, the messenger asks,
"Where is Sri Thakura Mahashaya?

**243.** Sri Acharya Prabhu has sent a letter
from Vana-Vishnupura — let him know of this quickly."

**244.** Hearing this, someone quickly informed Mahashaya:
"A man has come from Vana-Vishnupura;

**245.** he has with him a letter from Acharya Prabhu."
On hearing these words — what joy! There is no end to it.

**246.** Calling the messenger near, [Mahashaya] asks after their welfare.
The messenger says, "All is supremely well, Mahashaya."

**247.** Hearing this, Shyamananda floats in tears of joy;
stretching out both arms, he embraces the messenger.

**248.** The messenger, in great haste, having given the letter to Mahashaya,
fell at the feet of both, rolling on the ground.

**249.** Reading the letter and learning all the news,
[Mahashaya] cannot contain in his heart his boundless joy.

**250.** To King Santosha Datta, the son of his paternal uncle,
he told it briefly in sweet words, like this:

**251.** "The books were soon recovered in Vana-Vishnupura;
Sri Acharya has shown mercy to Sri Vira Hambira."

**252.** Hearing of the recovery of the books and the deliverance of King Vira Hambira,
Santosha was comforted in mind and heart.

**253.** In supreme bliss Sri Santosha, the best of the wise,
showed great honor to the king's messenger.

**254.** He heard everything from him, from beginning to end,
and spent much wealth on the performance of auspicious rites.

**255.** Seeing Santosha's conduct, everyone was amazed;
Sri Thakura Mahashaya was delighted.

**256.** Seating Sri Shyamananda beside him,
he wrote a letter to Sri Acharya Srinivasa.

**257.** In it he revealed the state of his heart
and made known that Shyamananda would go to Utkala.

**258.** To Sri Vira Hambira he wrote a separate letter;
in it he proclaimed his supreme good fortune.

**259.** Taking the two letters, the messenger went to Vishnupura;
giving the letters to the king, he reported everything.

**260.** The king, praising his messenger's good fortune,
goes before Sri Acharya, full of delight.

**261.** Meanwhile Srinivasa Acharya, gathering his disciples,
is having them study the books of the Gosvamis.

**262.** He sits in the midst of the assembly like the sun;
whose eyes are not gladdened at the sight of that beauty?

**263.** Sri Vira Hambira, coming before Sri Acharya,
bowed with care, prostrating on the ground.

**264.** Standing with joined palms, he says to the Acharya,
"A letter came from Kheturi this morning.

**265.** Showing great mercy to me, a sinner,
Thakura Mahashaya has written this letter.

**266.** He wrote this letter to Prabhu" — saying this,
he handed over the letter, full of great delight.

**267.** The Acharya reads the letter, and all the people listen;
no one can hold back the tears in their eyes.

**268.** When the letter had been read, the king further reported
what, beyond the letter, he had heard from the messenger's mouth:

**269.** how Raja Sri Santosha himself, with enthusiasm,
performed auspicious rites in various ways,

**270.** and in what manner he gave gifts to the brahmanas —
hearing all this, everyone felt great joy.

**271.** A letter from Mahashaya had come to the king —
at this, everyone praises the monarch's good fortune.

**272.** After staying some time in the Acharya's assembly,
the king, taking leave, quickly went home.

**273.** Reading Sri Mahashaya's letter in private,
the king, overwhelmed, cannot be calm.

**274.** At that time the queen came and makes a request:
"Be kind and let me hear the letter."

**275.** Hearing the queen's words, the king at that very moment
read the letter to her with a most delighted heart.

**276.** As soon as she heard it, the queen forgets herself;
she prays to Providence again and again:

**277.** "O lord! Thakura Mahashaya Narottama —
mercifully show him even once to me, a lowly one!"[^29]

**278.** Saying this, the queen, bathed in tears,
fell at the king's feet, rolling on the ground.

**279.** She says to the king, "Now your life has found its purpose:
without effort you have gained the treasure of prema for Krishna's feet."

**280.** The king says, "That treasure is exceedingly rare.
Will it touch me? I am a great abode of sin.

**281.** I, a wicked man, have spent this birth in vain;
there is no counting the offenses I have committed."

**282.** Saying this, the king, his heart losing all composure,
crying "Sri Krishna Chaitanya!", rolls on the ground.

**283.** Crying "Prabhu Nityananda, Sri Advaita Prabhu!",
he laments bitterly again, raising both arms.

**284.** "Gadadhara, Srivasa, Svarupa, Vakreshvara,
Haridasa, Murari, Mukunda, Damodara,[^30]

**285.** Gauridasa, Kashishvara, Rupa-Sanatana" —
taking all these names, he weeps.

**286.** Heaving a long sigh, he again says to the queen,
"There is no one in the world so foolish as I.

**287.** In Navadvipa the Lord, the eternal complete Brahman,
performed wondrous pastimes with His dear ones.

**288.** Hearing of that Lord's pastimes, my heart did not melt;
how many false arguments I made — such were my deeds.

**289.** I do not know in what auspicious moment I stole the books —
because of that I obtained Srinivasa Acharya Prabhu.

**290.** He melted a lump of iron such as I;
in his mercy he plunged me into that ocean of pastimes.

**291.** My lord Srinivasa is the very limit of compassion;
he will fulfill whatever desire may arise [in the heart].

**292.** Do not worry: you will meet his dear ones.
Make those feet the essence [of all] in life and in death."

**293.** Thus the king says much and praises the queen;
I cannot tell it at length for fear of making the book too long.

**294.** Meanwhile Mahashaya, having joyfully sent off the letter,
is eager to see the Acharya.

**295.** In the absorption of affection he reflects within himself:
"How shall I remain calm without Shyamananda?

**296.** Tomorrow morning Shyamananda will leave for Utkala."
Reflecting thus, he became wet with tears.

**297.** Sri Thakura Narottama is the embodiment of affection;
what his affection for Shyamananda is like — where is my power to tell it?

**298.** After a while, becoming calm, he says to Shyamananda,
"At daybreak you will certainly set out.

**299.** When you reach your country, quickly send a letter here;
I shall meet you there, going to Nilachala."

**300.** Shyamananda had been extremely distressed;
hearing these words, he rejoiced in his mind.

**301.** Sri Thakura Narottama, together with Shyamananda,
spent the day and night absorbed in prema.

**302.** Taking courage, at daybreak
he sends Shyamananda off to Utkala.

**303.** He gave him a man as companion, with money and other things;
at the moment of departure he became greatly distressed.

**304.** Shyamananda, wet with the tears of his eyes,
bows to Narottama, falling to the ground.

**305.** Likewise Sri Thakura Narottama, bowing,
floats in tears as he embraces Shyamananda.

**306.** What Mahashaya became after sending off Shyamananda —
that cannot be put into words.

**307.** The people of the village of Kheturi run in all directions;
all are distressed at Shyamananda's departure.

**308.** Sri Raja Santosha Datta, together with his people,
bowing to Shyamananda, showed great humility.

**309.** Shyamananda embraced Santosha;
at the farewell, tears could not be held back.

**310.** Sri Raja Santosha, going to the bank of the Padmavati,
floats in tears as he seats him in the boat.[^31]

**311.** The most steadfast Shyamananda, boarding the boat,
crossed the Padmavati, his heart losing all composure.

**312.** There, after bathing and so on and staying a while,
he bowed to the Padmavati and set off.

**313.** Having darshan of Gauranga at Kantaka-nagara,
he went by way of Navadvipa to Shantipura.[^32]

**314.** In what places which devotees showed him favor —
for fear of the book's length I have not described.

**315.** Quickly going to Ambika-nagara,
he went to the abode of his master, absorbed in prema.

**316.** What joy there was at the sight of Sri Hridaya Chaitanya's feet —
who can describe it?

**317.** He, showing great favor to Shyamananda,
showed him Sri Krishna Chaitanya and Nityananda.[^33]

**318.** Shyamananda, having darshan of the two Lords,
was overwhelmed — his tears could not be held back.

**319.** The two Lords abide in silent form;
They showed Shyamananda great favor.

**320.** What do I know, to tell of the pastimes of these Lords,
to whose service Sri Pandita Gauridasa is devoted?

**321.** In this connection I shall tell a little of the Pandita's ways —
he to whose prema the Lord is subject, as is known in the world.

**322.** Gauridasa Pandita is supremely full of prema;
he is Sri Subalachandra, the abode of virtues.[^34]

**323.** Sri Subala, dear to Krishna, is supremely beautiful;
his character and the rest the best of the wise describe with care.

**324.** *Thus [it is said] in the Sri [Bhakti-]rasamrita-sindhu, in the Pashchima-vibhaga, in the third lahari, the seventeenth verse:*
*"Him who has conquered gold by the lustre of his body, dear to Hari, wearing a necklace, clad in green —*
*Subala, whose eyes are like blue lotuses, who delights his friends by the wisdom of his conduct, I praise."*

**325.** *Thus [it is said] in the Stavavali, in the Vraja-vilasa, the twenty-second verse:*
*"He who, from the fullness of deep attachment, out of fear of separation,*
*does not let go of the hand of the Moon of Gokula even in dreams,*
*whose heart is sprinkled by the waterfall of Radhika's love —*
*to that Subala, whose body is overwhelmed by prema, I bow."*

**326.** *Thus [it is said] in the Sri Ujjvala-nilamani, in the [section] on the kinds of helpers, the seventh verse:*[^35]
*"Having appeased her, he brings back the lovely girl who had gone off after a playful quarrel;*
*in the grove-bower he makes for the Destroyer of Agha a bed fit for the pastimes of Kandarpa;*
*Him, bathed in perspiration, His limbs exhausted, upon His beloved's breast, he fans —*
*in what service does glorious Subala not find himself qualified?"*

**327.** That Sri Subala is Gauridasa is known everywhere.
Non-different [from Subala], he is the beloved of Chaitanya and Nityananda.

**328.** *Thus [it is said] in the Sri Gaura-ganoddesha-dipika (128):*
*"Subala, the best of the dear [friends], is Gauridasa Pandita."*

**329.** *And elsewhere:*[^36]
*"Him who was formerly Subalachandra, Sri Gauridasa, endowed with virtues,*
*the beloved of Sri Krishna Chaitanya and Nityananda, I worship."*

**330.** Suryadasa Sarakhel is a magnanimous pandita;
his brother is the renowned Gauridasa Pandita.[^37]

**331.** Having told his elder brother, he came from Shaligrama
to Ambika and settled on the bank of the Ganga.

**332.** Supremely detached, he always stays in solitude;
the Lord knows well the Pandita's state of mind.

**333.** One day Gaura-raya, from Shantipura,
crossing the Ganga, came to Ambika.

**334.** He says to the Pandita: "I had gone to Shantipura;
coming to the village of Harinadi, I boarded a boat.[^38]

**335.** I crossed the Ganga, rowing the boat with the oar.
Here, take the oar: now I have given it to you.

**336.** Ferry the living beings across the river of worldly existence."
Saying this, He embraced the Pandita.

**337.** Taking the Pandita with Him, the Lord went to Nadia
and immersed [him] in most wondrous pastimes.

**338.** Who can understand the ways of Gaurachandra?
He gave the Pandita His own nectarean *Gita*.[^39]

**339.** After some days the Pandita, coming to Ambika,
constantly reads the *Gita* given by the Lord.

**340.** The *Gita* in the letters of the Lord's own sacred hand —
the happiness of seeing it I cannot express.

**341.** The *Gita* given by the Lord and the oar, in the presence of the Lords,
the fortunate see in Ambika even today.

**342.** There is no end to telling the Pandita's fair fame;
his all in all is Krishna Chaitanya and Nitai.

**343.** He is always intoxicated with singing the qualities of Nitai and Chaitanya;
apart from Nitai and Chaitanya he knows no other.

**344.** Nitai and Chaitanya are the two pupils of his eyes;
what can others know of this wondrous stream of prema?

**345.** I do not know what bliss grows when he beholds Them;
without Them for even a moment, sorrow knows no bounds.

**346.** Knowing the Pandita's heart, the Lord Gaurahari
one day says to the Pandita earnestly:

**347.** "Have a neem tree brought from Navadvipa
and make Me together with My brother.[^40]

**348.** Without difficulty the two Deities will be made;
I will surely fulfil your desire."

**349.** Hearing this, the Pandita was overjoyed
and with care had the wooden Deities made.

**350.** The one who made Them is a recipient of the Lord's mercy;
[the Lord] manifests Himself — the other's [part] is a mere pretext.

**351.** Seeing the wondrous Deities, the magnanimous Pandita
lost all composure; streams flow from his eyes unceasingly.

**352.** Considering himself blessed, together with his dear ones
he prepares the rite of abhisheka.

**353.** According to custom and scripture, at an auspicious moment,
having performed the abhisheka of the Deities, he seated [Them] on the throne.

**354.** Beholding Nitai and Chaitanyachand,
the Lord's dear ones were immersed in great bliss.

**355.** The forms of the two Lords enchant the worlds;
apart from the circle of devotees, this is beyond the reach of others.

**356.** Nitai and Chaitanya are subject to Gauridasa's prema —
this story spread through the world day and night.

**357.** The lila that Nitai and Chaitanya manifest in Gauridasa's house
is known throughout the world.

**358.** What do I know, to tell of the Pandita's intent?
He is ceaselessly immersed in the service of the two Lords.

**359.** One day Nitai and Chaitanya, absorbed in prema,
smiling gently, say to Gauridasa:

**360.** "Who will understand your ways?
Overwhelmed by prema, you do not know yourself.

**361.** O friend Subala! Do you not remember all that —
the fun of grazing the cows on the sandbanks of the Yamuna?"

**362.** Saying many such things, the two Lords, abodes of prema,
took dark and white forms — those of Krishna and Balarama.

**363.** Horn, staff, flute, ornaments of peacock feathers —
what beauty of the cowherd dress, enchanting the worlds!

**364.** Seeing this, Gauridasa forgot himself;
he is intoxicated with that mood — who will understand such ways?

**365.** By the Lord's will, becoming steady after a while,
he sees Nitai and Chaitanyachand on the throne.

**366.** Thus the two Lords perform various sports;
Gauridasa in his delight cannot keep his body still.

**367.** One day Gauridasa, having cooked,
says to the two Lords: "Please eat."

**368.** Hearing such gentle words from the Pandita,
They do not eat, but remain silent.

**369.** Seeing the Lords' manner, Pandita Thakura,
somewhat in anger, speaks sweet words:

**370.** "If You find happiness in Your hearts without eating,
then why do You make me cook?"

**371.** Saying this, Gauridasa remains silent;
smiling, the Lords say to the Pandita softly, softly:

**372.** "Your cooking is never settled with a little:
you make rice and the rest and many kinds of vegetable dishes.

**373.** You do not heed Our forbidding, and We cannot bear to see your toil.
Whatever comes without effort — that is best of all."

**374.** Gauridasa says: "I shall never do so;
I shall boil just one dish of greens (shak) and feed You."

**375.** Hearing the Pandita's words, the two Lords laugh;
They eat and, delighted, say a little:

**376.** "How did you cook these wondrous greens?
We are satisfied by eating just one dish of greens."

**377.** Praising thus, both of Them eat;
the Pandita, seeing that sport, refreshes his eyes.

**378.** One day Gauridasa, with joy in his heart,
longed to put some ornaments on [Them].

**379.** Knowing the Pandita's mind, the Lords, delighted,
became adorned with various jewelled ornaments.

**380.** Both stand on the jewelled throne;
the Pandita, entering the temple, saw the splendour —

**381.** he lost all composure, with not a trace of outer awareness;
after a while, becoming steady, he sees the former attire.

**382.** Gauridasa reflects within himself:
"Never have I seen such wondrous ornaments.

**383.** I had the desire to put ornaments on [Them] —
what could I put on? Now that delusion is dispelled."

**384.** As he reflected thus, the Lords say to the Pandita:
"With ornaments of flowers the happiness grows exceedingly."

**385.** Hearing the very sweet words, the Pandita himself
with great care put the flower ornaments on [Them].

**386.** Garlands hang down, one below another, to the very feet;
it is most charming — there is no end to that beauty.

**387.** The Pandita brought a mirror and set it before the Lords;
how much the delight increased I cannot tell.

**388.** Such deeds of the Pandita spread through the world;
I have told a little of this to purify myself.

**389.** The disciple of such a Pandita is Hridaya Chaitanya,
who knows no one but Pandita Thakura.

**390.** Formerly all knew him by the name of Sri Hridayananda;
ceaselessly he serves the Lord with care.

**391.** How the name Hridaya Chaitanya came about,
and what the Pandita's mercy was, I tell briefly.

**392.** One day, as the night gave way to dawn, Gauridasa
came to Gadadhara Pandita.

**393.** Gadadhara Pandita, seeing Gauridasa,
with no end of affection seated him by his side.

**394.** Smiling gently, he says again and again:
"Today at dawn I have seen my good fortune."

**395.** Gauridasa replies in very sweet words:
"Good fortune will be mine — that is why I have come."

**396.** Pandita Gadai says: "With what shall I please you?"
Gauridasa says: "I shall ask for it and take it."

**397.** Gadadhara says: "All this is yours;
whatever you wish, take it — what is there to consider?"

**398.** Pandita Thakura says: "I want Hridaya."
Hearing this, Pandita Gosani calls Hridaya.[^41]

**399.** Hridayananda came with a joyful heart
and, falling to the ground, bowed at the feet of both.

**400.** Pandita Gosani, having said many things to Hridaya,
entrusted [him] to Gauridasa Pandita.[^42]

**401.** How great Pandita Gosani's mercy to Sri Hridaya was
is known everywhere; who can tell how great [it was]?

**402.** He had raised him from childhood
and in a short time had him study the scriptures.

**403.** He was overwhelmed with parental love, yet did not act from the feeling "he is mine":
having given [him] to Pandita Thakura, he rejoiced.[^62]

**404.** The way of Pandita Gadai and Gauridasa —
who has the power to know it without the Lord's mercy?

**405.** For some time Gauridasa remained beside Gadadhara,
[absorbed] in the rasa of talks about the Lord's pastimes.

**406.** Taking leave of Pandita Gosani,
he came back to his residence, taking Hridayananda with him.

**407.** After some days he gave Hridaya the mantra of initiation
and entrusted him to the feet of Nityananda and Chaitanya.

**408.** Hridaya became absorbed in the service of the Lord;
seeing this, Gauridasa rejoices at heart.

**409.** Who will understand the play of Gauridasa Pandita?
Moment after moment so many waves of prema rise [in him]!

**410.** One day he says to Hridayananda:
"The time of the Lord's birth festival has come.[^43]

**411.** I will arrange all that is needed at a disciple's house
and come back to the residence soon — such is my intention.

**412.** Always be attentive in the service of the Lord."
Saying this, he set out from the residence.

**413.** Intoxicated with the rasa of the Lord's wondrous pastimes,
he wanders in seclusion with his dear companions.

**414.** At the residence Hridayananda thinks to himself:
"Why has Prabhu delayed so many days?[^44]

**415.** Here many provisions have already been made ready,
and only about two days remain before the festival."

**416.** Thinking thus and remembering Prabhu's feet,
he sent invitations to the festival everywhere.

**417.** On the day before the festival the Pandita came;
hearing about the invitations, he was glad at heart.

**418.** [But,] outwardly angry, he rebukes Hridaya:
"While I am here you have acted on your own —

**419.** you have sent letters of invitation here and there.
What you have done is done, but now you shall not stay here."

**420.** Hearing this, he bowed at [his] two feet,
went to the bank of the Ganga and stayed under a tree.

**421.** Here Gauridasa began the holy festival;
from everywhere all the mahantas came.

**422.** At that time a certain mahajana with care
sent various provisions, filling a boat.

**423.** [This] was made known to Hridayananda on the bank of the Ganga;
he sent word [of it] to Thakura.

**424.** Hearing this, [Thakura,] outwardly angry, says: "Go and tell [him]:
let him hold the festival, taking all those provisions."

**425.** Receiving the guru's order, Hridaya in joy
holds a great festival such as cannot be told.

**426.** The holy Vaishnavas arrived;
all together they perform a wondrous sankirtana.

**427.** The sound of khol and kartals touched the sky,
as if a great ocean of bliss had surged up.

**428.** All the Vaishnavas dance, forming circles;
tears of prema flow ceaselessly from everyone's eyes.

**429.** Nitai and Chaitanya, the two Prabhus full of prema,
dance in the midst of the sankirtana; Hridaya sees [Them].

**430.** What movements in Their dance — they madden the world!
The beauty of the Two lights up the universe.

**431.** The moon-faces of the Two destroy the pride of the moon;
from Hridayananda's eyes bliss rains down.

**432.** The tumult of cries of "Jaya!" in the bliss of the sankirtana —
hearing it, Gauridasa here is overwhelmed with joy.

**433.** The Pandita says softly to Gangadasa:
"It is time for the service; go into the holy temple."

**434.** Bara Gangadasa, entering the holy temple,[^45]
saw the throne empty and came back and told [him].

**435.** Hearing this, what a wondrous emotion arose in the Pandita!
He knew: the two Prabhus are subdued by Hridaya's prema.

**436.** Smiling gently, he took a stick in his hand;
outwardly he shows anger, within is bliss.

**437.** He went to the bank of the Ganga, where the sankirtana was,
and sees the two Prabhus dancing there.

**438.** The two Brothers, seeing the Pandita's anger,
went unnoticed and entered the temple.

**439.** Such is the wondrous pastime of Chaitanyachandra:
He enters the lake of Hridaya's heart — Gauridasa sees [this].[^46]

**440.** Seeing Chaitanyachand in Hridaya's heart,
he cannot hold back his tears; [his] eyes do not blink.

**441.** The outward anger that had been [in him] — he forgot it;
the stick fell from his hand — he did not notice it.

**442.** In the rapture of prema he runs with his arms stretched out
and, his heart rejoicing, embraces Hridaya.

**443.** He says to Hridaya: "You are blessed, blessed!
From today your name is Hridaya Chaitanya."

**444.** Saying this, he bathed [him] in tears;
Hridaya fell and rolled at [his] feet.

**445.** Taking Hridaya Chaitanya with him, Thakura Pandita
came to the courtyard of the Lord's temple.

**446.** What can I say — in what bliss, seeing the sweetness of the two,
he made Hridaya the one in charge of the holy service!

**447.** The bliss of all the Vaishnavas was boundless;
what that great festival was like I cannot describe.

**448.** The mercy [shown] to Hridaya spread through the world;
in this way the name "Hridaya Chaitanya" came about.

**449.** Hridaya Chaitanya is the life of Shyamananda;
by a particle of his mercy what one longs for is fulfilled.

**450.** Showing great mercy to dear Shyamananda,
Hridaya, agitated, sends him off to Utkala.

**451.** Shyamananda, bowing at Prabhu's lotus feet,
in taking leave has his heart awash in tears.

**452.** He made known to Nitai and Chaitanya [what was] in his heart
and, bowing down, became grey with the dust of the courtyard.

**453.** Making many entreaties to Prabhu's associates,
he sets out from Ambika — and cannot walk.

**454.** Seeing his distress, those dear ones of Prabhu
speak many consoling words to Shyamananda:

**455.** "Having distributed Prabhu's jewel of bhakti in Utkala,
you will come to Ambika again when the time comes."

**456.** They say many such things; hearing [them], the son of Durika[^47]
goes to Utkala, meditating on the feet of Sri Guru.

**457.** Ceaselessly he sings the virtues of Nitai and Chaitanya;
himself intoxicated, he intoxicates all.

**458.** Seeing Shyamananda, hosts of great unbelievers
consider themselves blessed and beg for shelter.

**459.** In the land of Gauda there is a village named Dandeshvara,
where formerly Krishna Mandala lived.[^48]

**460.** After that he settled in Utkala.
What can I say of the wondrous pastime at Dandeshvara!

**461.** Shyamananda went by that road;
seeing Shyamananda, everyone's eyes are soothed.

**462.** From there he went quickly to the village of Dharenda[^49]
and became anxious to send auspicious letters.

**463.** To Sri Acharya Thakura and Thakura Mahashaya
he wrote all the news in two letters.

**464.** Sri Mahashaya's man, who had been with him —
giving him the letters, he sent him off with great care.

**465.** Having sent the letters, he manifests prema-bhakti
and, compassionate, makes Utkala blessed.

**466.** Here Sri Thakura Mahashaya, receiving the letter,
read it and joyfully let everyone hear it.

**467.** Mahashaya again, by the hand of that same man,
sent the letter to Sri Acharya in Vishnupura.[^50]

**468.** Having sent the letter, Sri Thakura Mahashaya
sets out for the darshan of Sri Navadvipa and other places.

**469.** Receiving Sri Narottama's letter, the Acharya —
in what a wondrous rapture of affection! — lost his composure.

**470.** Learning the news in Mahashaya's letter,
he reads Sri Shyamananda's letter again and again.

**471.** Some extraordinary deeds of Sri Shyamananda
he made known to everyone in great joy.

**472.** King Sri Vira Hambira, with jubilation in his heart,
taking the letter from Prabhu's side, held it on his head.

**473.** Hearing of Sri Shyamananda's virtues and character,
he longs moment after moment to see him.

**474.** Acharya Thakura, seeing the king's eagerness —
little by little abundant joy grows in his heart.

**475.** Sri Acharya says unhurriedly to the king:
"I shall soon go to Shrikhanda and Yajigrama."

**476.** The king says: "You have made Vana-Vishnupura blessed!
Without Prabhu, Vishnupura will become a wilderness."[^51]

**477.** The Acharya says: "Do not worry at all:
you will soon see [me] in Vana-Vishnupura."

**478.** The king says: "Take me, a wretch, with you."
The Acharya says: "That will be after some days."

**479.** The king says: "I cannot keep [you] by insisting,
and what arises in my mind I cannot even carry out."[^52]

**480.** Having said this, the king could not keep his composure;
Sri Acharya consoled him in many ways.

**481.** Taking heart from the Acharya's words,
he quickly went to his own inner apartments.

**482.** He told the queen all this news;
she says, "Vishnupura will be plunged into darkness."

**483.** The king says, "Now I cannot keep him."
The queen says, "That is true; I have weighed it in my heart.

**484.** The Prabhu will go — how shall I keep my composure?"
As soon as she said this, tears fall from her eyes.

**485.** Sri Vira Hambira, outwardly showing composure,
consoled the queen and went before the Prabhu.

**486.** Everything needed for Acharya Prabhu's journey
the king arranged at that very moment.

**487.** Having prepared everything, with many entreaties
he brought Acharya Prabhu into the inner apartments.

**488.** What the king's wife became at the sight of her own Prabhu —
who could describe it?

**489.** Bowing to the ground, she made many entreaties;
at the time of the Prabhu's departure she sank into an ocean of sorrow.

**490.** Sri Acharya Prabhu, won over by that devotion,
consoled her with great kindness and came back to his lodging.

**491.** "The Acharya is leaving for Yajigrama" —
hearing this, the villagers weep.

**492.** One says to another in great sorrow,
"I see no means to keep him from going."

**493.** Saying many such things, people run to see [him];
all offer their lives at the Acharya's feet.

**494.** Beholding the Acharya to their eyes' fill,
they make entreaties beyond description.

**495.** Sri Acharya Prabhu set out from Vana-Vishnupura
with great wealth.

**496.** The king with his people goes along with him some distance;
the Prabhu commands, "Now go to Vishnupura."

**497.** What the king became in separation from the Prabhu —
who is there who could see it and keep his composure?

**498.** The king with his people went to Vana-Vishnupura,
and Acharya Thakura went on to Yajigrama.

**499.** The news that the Acharya was going to Yajigrama
spread everywhere; people speak of it here and there.

**500.** Hearing that the Acharya is coming home,
the people of Yajigrama came back to life.

**501.** They all went to Lakshmipriya Thakurani
and, overjoyed, told her the news.

**502.** The Acharya's mother, hearing of her son's coming,
was overwhelmed with motherly love beyond description.

**503.** Srinivasa Acharya, entering Yajigrama,
went to where his mother was, watching the road.

**504.** He bowed at the feet of his mother Lakshmipriya;
she looks at her son's face with gladdened eyes.

**505.** Moment by moment her body and mind overflow with joy,
as if a pauper had found a pot full of wealth.

**506.** The people of Yajigrama came running;
seeing Srinivasa, their eyes and hearts were soothed.

**507.** Sri Acharya gladdens everyone with gentle words;
there is a great throng of people at the Acharya's residence.

**508.** When people had come and gone in this way, after that
it became quiet — the beautiful hour of evening.

**509.** With his disciples and others, Sri Acharya in his own home
sat down — what wondrous splendour there was at that time!

**510.** Talk of the books of bhakti is ever on the Acharya's lips;
on every side the pious watch [him] with happiness.

**511.** The wise who lived near Yajigrama and elsewhere,
moved by affection, came to the Acharya's house.

**512.** The Acharya heard that the wise were coming;
he went out to meet them, and there was joy at the meeting.

**513.** Acharya Thakura, bringing them all into the house,
seated them on seats with the greatest respect.[^53]

**514.** The wise Vaishnavas were moved by the Acharya's ways;
they ask the Acharya, in order, all that had happened.

**515.** The Acharya tells how he went to Vrindavana,
how in a dream Rupa and Sanatana showed him mercy,

**516.** how he received the favour of Gopala Bhatta,
how he studied the books of the Gosvamis,

**517.** how he wandered through the land of Vrindavana,
how he came to Gauda with the books,

**518.** how the books were stolen in Vana-Vishnupura,
how the books were recovered, how he came home.

**519.** As they heard all these accounts from beginning to end,
various emotions arose in the hearts of the Vaishnavas.

**520.** All the Vaishnavas, growing calm after a while,
gaze fixedly at Srinivasa's face.

**521.** Srinivasa Acharya, in sweet and gentle words,
asks how the masters here are faring.

**522.** Hearing this, they heave deep sighs and say very slowly,
"Thakura Narahari is all but lifeless.[^54]

**523.** Day and night, in a swoon, he rolls on the ground,
constantly speaks incoherently, and floats in tears.

**524.** Sri Raghunandana and all his dear ones
unceasingly sing the glories of Gora.

**525.** Seeing Thakura's state, who could keep his composure?
What to speak of others — wood and stone split apart.

**526.** It is some days now since Dasa Gadadhara
came from Navadvipa to Kantaka-nagara.[^55]

**527.** Singing the glories of Gora, he moans day and night;
at the sight of that state the chest bursts.

**528.** He speaks incoherently, at one moment falls silent,
at another tells the glories of Gadadhara Pandita,

**529.** at another, crying 'Nityananda!', heaves a deep sigh,
at another says, 'Where has Pandita Srivasa gone?'

**530.** at another says, 'O Prabhu, to make [me] endure this sorrow,
how many more days will You keep [me] on earth?'

**531.** Saying many such things and sighing deeply,
he lies all but lifeless, fallen in the Lord's courtyard.

**532.** He stays in solitude, takes neither food nor water;
in the burning fire of separation his body sways.

**533.** O Srinivasa! The masters in Navadvipa
have, day by day, nearly all withdrawn from sight.

**534.** Words do not come to the lips, the heart breaks:
Devi Vishnupriya has passed from sight."

**535.** Hearing this, Srinivasa Acharya fainted:
his body motionless, his nostrils without breath.

**536.** Seeing Srinivasa's state, all the Vaishnavas
were distraught; their chests were flooded with tears.

**537.** Only late in the night did the Acharya regain outer consciousness;
he weeps so that stone would split.

**538.** A certain worthy man named Sri Gopala Dasa,
taking Srinivasa in his arms, consoles him at length.

**539.** After some time Srinivasa Acharya grew calm;
the night was nearly over in the kirtana of the Lord.

**540.** Everyone lay down for a little while;
the goddess of sleep drew Srinivasa to herself.

**541.** In the guise of a dream, Prabhu Sri Advaita, full of prema,
appeared in person — His form conquers Kandarpa.[^56]

**542.** His two charming eyes reach to His ears;
His beautiful face puts millions of moons to shame.

**543.** With His arms, which surpass golden lotus stalks,
He lovingly took hold of Srinivasa and held him to Himself.

**544.** Whatever brings relief from the burning fire of separation —
that the merciful Sri Advaita did.

**545.** Showing much fatherly affection, in sweet and gentle words,
smiling softly, He speaks to Srinivasa:

**546.** "Through you many souls will be delivered;
you will preach the Prabhu's teaching everywhere.

**547.** The wise will tell you to marry;
you shall marry — do not grieve over it in your heart."

**548.** Having said many such things, the Prabhu vanished;
Srinivasa, waking, sees that the night has given way to dawn.

**549.** Thinking of the lovely ways of Prabhu Advaita,
he cannot hold back his tears; his heart overflows.

**550.** Consoling himself, in the morning, having finished his morning duties,
Srinivasa went quickly to Shrikhanda.

**551.** Entering Shrikhanda with joy in his heart,
he went to the courtyard of Gauranga and beholds Gaurachand.[^57]

**552.** Rolling on the ground, he offered many obeisances;
his golden body turned grey with dust.

**553.** Hearing that Srinivasa had come, Sri Raghunandana
went before Thakura and told him.

**554.** Although Sri Thakura's heart was burnt with sorrow,
still he became joyful on hearing this.

**555.** He says to Sri Raghunandana in the sweetest words,
"Let my eyes be soothed — bring [him]; let me see Srinivasa."

**556.** Hearing Thakura's words, with a joyful heart
he goes and meets Srinivasa in the Lord's courtyard.

**557.** Sri Raghunandana, a great treasury of virtues,
on finding Srinivasa felt as if he had found life.

**558.** When Srinivasa bowed to Sri Raghunandana,
he embraced him and would not let him go from his arms.

**559.** With what wondrous affection his heart overflows!
Embracing [him], he cannot stop the streams from his eyes.

**560.** Srinivasa floats in the tears of both his eyes;
like one helpless, he stays in Raghunandana's embrace.

**561.** Sri Raghunandana, having bathed him in the tears of his eyes,
took him to where Sri Thakura Narahari was.

**562.** He is sitting in complete seclusion;
at that sight Srinivasa lost his composure.

**563.** Ah! That very form which gladdens the heart —
can a golden champaka be any comparison for it?

**564.** Such an incomparable form has grown wan;
the most tender body wastes away moment by moment.

**565.** The sweetness of his face was like the beauty of the moon;
now it is like a lotus without water.

**566.** Those eyes that showered bliss —
in those eyes there are now constant, abundant streams of tears.

**567.** Gazing and gazing at such Narahari Prabhu,
he bows to the ground, intoxicated with the rasa of bhakti.

**568.** Sri Thakura Narahari, seeing him, in a surge of affection
said, "Come, my son!" and embraced Srinivasa.

**569.** Embracing Srinivasa, he was overwhelmed;
he cannot hold back the tears of both his eyes.

**570.** He bathed Srinivasa in tears of prema
and, taking him by the hand, seated him by his side.

**571.** With the deepest fatherly affection he strokes his body with his hand;
seeing that wondrous way, who would not be happy?

**572.** Whatever he asks in the sweetest words,
Srinivasa relates it all in order.

**573.** He related everything that had happened from beginning to end
and told him that Narottama had gone to Kshetra.

**574.** What arose in his heart on hearing all this —
does anyone else have the power to tell it?

**575.** Again he says to Srinivasa with affectionate words:
"I greatly long to see Narottama soon.

**576.** I believe Narottama will come here quickly;
many works will be accomplished through him.

**577.** With him you will be intoxicated in sankirtana
and find relief from the burning of cruel separation.

**578.** O my son, it is good that you came quickly:
at this time I have looked at you to my eyes' content.

**579.** Live long and earn bhakti;
distribute the books of bhakti everywhere.

**580.** People will become self-willed, abandoning their own dharma;
they will not understand the inner truth of guru, Krishna and the Vaishnavas.

**581.** These unbelievers you will deliver by the power of bhakti;
all the Vaishnavas will sing your glory.

**582.** You are an eternal servant of Krishna-Chaitanyachandra;
the Lord will fulfil your desire.

**583.** Your mother is a supreme Vaishnavi;
stay for some days in Yajigrama, serving her.

**584.** Whatever her heart desires must certainly be done;
in this there will be no loss for you.

**585.** Marry, my son — this is my wish."
Having said this, he speaks again to Sri Raghunandana:

**586.** "I tell him to marry — how does it seem to you?"
Hearing this, he says, "This is in the minds of all of us."

**587.** Thakura says, "Do not delay in this."
Hearing it, Srinivasa felt great embarrassment.

**588.** Sri Thakura Narahari knows all truths;
saying many things to him, he dispelled his shyness and the rest.

**589.** The Acharya understood that such was Thakura's wish
and reflected on the command Prabhu Advaita had given him in a dream.[^58]

**590.** Breaking his silence, he says, "I cannot transgress the command."
At the Acharya's words joy arose in everyone.

**591.** Sri Thakura Narahari, with gentle words,
gave beloved Srinivasa leave to go to Yajigrama.

**592.** Sri Raghunandana, holding Srinivasa's hand,
slowly came to the Lord's courtyard.

**593.** With all the Vaishnavas living in Shrikhanda
Srinivasa met in the Lord's courtyard.

**594.** Staying there a while and taking leave,
he went quickly from Khanda to Yajigrama.

**595.** Staying there a while, he could not be at peace
and very soon came to Kantaka-nagara.

**596.** In a surge of prema he had darshan of Gauranga;
in Gauranga's courtyard he became grey with dust.

**597.** He went to the secluded place where Gadadhara Dasa was.
What shall I say of how distraught his heart was!

**598.** He has no food, no drink — nothing pleases him;
grey with dust, he constantly rolls on the ground.

**599.** That lovely body, which surpassed a golden lotus,
has grown wan beyond words.

**600.** He has not even half a sesame seed's worth of hope for life;
he sings Gora's virtues, and now and then heaves long sighs.

**601.** Now, remembering Nityananda's virtues and deeds,
taking the name of Advaita, he falls silent.

**602.** Now, taking the name of Gadadhara Pandita,
he speaks piteously, bathed in tears:

**603.** "O Gadadhara! What was in my heart before
did not fall to my lot: you went first, leaving me behind."

**604.** So he says many things that others find hard to understand;
the glory of Gadadhara is known to Gadadhara.[^59]

**605.** What wondrous affection Pandita Sri Gadadhara had for Gadadhara Dasa —
who can describe it?

**606.** Srinivasa, going before such a Gadadhara,
bows to the ground, bathed in tears.

**607.** Prabhu Gadadhara, seeing beloved Srinivasa,
opened his arms and in a surge of affection drew him to his breast.

**608.** With great kindness he says again, softly:
"By the Lord's will I see you again.

**609.** How you went from Gauda to Vrindavana,
how you lived there and studied,

**610.** how Sri Gopala Bhatta gave you the mantra of initiation,
what mercy all the Lord's dear associates showed you,

**611.** how there you met with great love Narottama,
whom the Lord drew to Himself in the village of Ramakeli,

**612.** how you wandered in Vraja with Narottama,
how you set out for Gauda, taking the books,

**613.** how the king of robbers stole the books,
how the books were recovered in Vana-Vishnupur —[^60]

**614.** all this I have heard, my son; what more is there to say?
In my heart is the wish to see Narottama just once.

**615.** O Srinivasa, this is what arises in my heart:
Narottama Dasa will soon come here."

**616.** Having said this and heaving a very long sigh,
he remained silent for some time.

**617.** Who can understand his ways? Again to Srinivasa
he speaks, distressed, in a faltering voice:

**618.** "However you saw things in Navadvipa when you went there —
day by day that ocean of sorrow has grown.

**619.** Srivasa Pandita and the other dear associates of the Lord,
before our very eyes, have almost all withdrawn from sight.

**620.** How Devi Vishnupriya disappeared from view —
it cannot be put into words; the heart breaks.

**621.** Navadvipa has fallen almost into darkness;
all who still remain there are barely alive.

**622.** What shall I say? I came here from there,
and shameless life still stays in this sinful body."

**623.** Hearing this, Srinivasa cannot keep his composure;
both his eyes were flooded with streams of tears.

**624.** After a while Gadadhara Dasa, composing himself,
speaks in a surge of affection, looking at Srinivasa's face:

**625.** "Live long, my son, remaining on earth;
you will make known the inner essence of bhakti together with your own people.[^61]

**626.** The supremely rare sankirtana of Sri Prabhu
you will relish ceaselessly together with your own people.

**627.** You will soon marry, with everyone's consent;
many will become your followers."

**628.** Saying many such things, he kindly
gave Srinivasa leave to go to his mother.

**629.** Srinivasa took his leave and went home,
and increased his mother's supreme joy.

**630.** Writing a letter with the news, he quickly
sent it by a messenger to Vana-Vishnupur.

**631.** In Yajigrama he lives happily with his disciples
and has them study the books of the Gosvamis.

**632.** Just as the Gosvamis revealed the doctrine that is best of all,
so Acharya Srinivasa expounds it.

**633.** Adherents of false doctrines, hearing his exposition of bhakti,
flee far away, like dogs in fear of a lion.

**634.** Hosts of learned men, knowing bhakti to be supreme,
come to Srinivasa's feet and beg for shelter.

**635.** Whoever feels joy on hearing all this —
to him Gaurachandra, together with His associates, shows mercy.

**636.** Whoever always recites this to people endowed with faith
sinks into the ocean of the rasa of bhakti for Krishna.

**637.** Meditating on the feet of Srinivasa Acharya,
Narahari Dasa tells the *Bhakti-ratnakara*.

*Thus in the Sri Bhakti-ratnakara ends the seventh wave, called "The Description of the Spreading of the Books of Bhakti and Other Matters".*
