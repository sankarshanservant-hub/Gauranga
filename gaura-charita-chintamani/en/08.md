# Kirana 8. The Deeds of the Morning: The Description of the Conversations about Dreams[^1]

*Morning in Navadvipa: the nagaris tell one another the dreams of Gaura they saw during the past night: one in a dream anointed Him with sandal paste and adorned Him with garlands in the house of Shrivasa; to another, in a forest bower, the youthful Shyama appeared and before her eyes became Gaura*

*Again, in the morning period — the conversations about dreams and so on of the nagaris of Shri Navadvipa.*

*Raga vibhasha*

**8.1.** Today, at the close of night, at dawn — how much merriment in the circle of friends!
All float in the rasa of Gauranga, laughing and frolicking in many ways.
Some playful one says: "Come now, tell how you spent the night."
Hearing this, they cast off shyness and, drunk with happiness, tell one another in many ways.[^2]

*A certain [woman] says — yatha[raga]*

**8.2.** Listen, listen, friend: I shall carefully tell you about the past night.
In the evening, having finished my work, I was sitting at home.
Thinking and thinking of Gora's beauty and virtues — I do not know what came over my heart —
I went with my mother-in-law to the house of Shrivasa Pandita Thakura.
There, among His incomparable associates, Gora sits —
the thief of the treasure of composure of young wives from worthy families, faithful to their husbands.
A radiant golden body, and on it lovely sandal paste, richly applied:
as if Mount Sumeru had been anointed with the ground-up autumn moon.
A garland of malati sways on His neck — like a snare that enchants the worlds;
how many, many hundreds of Madanas swoon at the sight of the contours of His face!
Smiling, smiling, He holds sweet conversation with Gadadhara —
He showers nectar in streams and dispels the longing of the ears.
Ah, ah! When I see that beauty, my heart is not my own.
What can I say? Not for a moment could I gaze my fill at Him with both eyes.
Unfortunate as I am — what can I do? The Creator made me a dependent woman:
for fear of my mother-in-law I could not stay — I came back, barely alive.
With sorrow in my heart I lay down; when my sister-in-law asked, I said to her:
"I am not hungry, do not wake me — I feel somehow unwell."
She, utterly guileless, went away, and I lay there with a troubled heart;
my body languished, oh — sleep came [only] late in the night.
In a dream I went to the house of Shrivasa Thakura and saw Him;
with what heartfelt joy I anointed Gaura's body with fragrant sandal paste!
Fresh, fresh garlands of many flowers I carefully placed around His neck.
The life of Narahari, the Crown of rasikas, on some pretext embraced [me].[^3]

*And another — yatharaga*

**8.3.** Listen, listen, O friend of my heart — I shall tell you:
such a dream as today's I have never seen.
I was sitting in a forest bower, adorned in many ways;
thinking and thinking [of Him], I had wasted away in body — there was not a trace of joy.
With restless eyes I look all around — I do not know what has happened to me.
And suddenly I see: someone has come to the outer door.
How wondrous — youthful, of kishora age, like rasa embodied;
what can I say of His grandeur as a nagara? His body is the colour of a cloud.
His palms and soles surpass the dawn [in redness], His nails are a host of moons;
the locks and the tilaka on His forehead shine — like a snare that enchants the worlds.
His crown is beautifully tilted, the peacock feather sways incomparably;
around it, drawn by the fragrance of lovely flowers, bees circle — there is no counting them.
Holding the flute to His lower lip, again and again He plays on it —
who in the world, hearing it, could keep composure?
On His neck is a double string of pearls, like the stream of the Suradhuni;
as He walks, the little bells at His waist and the lovely anklets on His feet jingle.
With a graceful play of His brows He came close to me
and jested on so many pretexts — who could understand it?
Smiling, smiling, He looks at me and signals with the corner of His eyes;
by a trick He draws aside my veil — what is He doing! — He draws my very life after Him.
And another wonder: as I watched, that Shyama became Gora —
what can I compare Him to? — the Thief of the hearts of so many faithful young wives.
Slowly, slowly coming near, He sat down beside me
and with sweet, sweet words tries to please [me], hoping for a touch.
In feigned anger I turned my face away — yet my happiness had no bounds.
"Forgive the offence!" He said, and again took hold of the edge of my sari.
At the touch I lost all strength and was immersed together with Him [in bliss].
Narahari's Lord of life, the most expert rasika, fulfilled what was in my heart.[^4]

*And another — yatharaga*

**8.4.** Tonight I saw a dream, all full of happiness, friend.
You are the most blessed in the world: listen, I shall tell you about it.
Each of you adorned herself, and, restless, you sat in seclusion;
singing and singing Gora's virtues, you passed [a whole] watch of the night.
Knowing the time had come, you sent a messenger to where Govinda abides;
she went very quickly and told Gauranga everything.
Then she hurried back to you, agitated;
hearing from her lips the news of the Beloved of your life, you all set out running.
From afar you saw the sweetness of Gora's form and were enchanted;
entering the forest bower, you came close to the Lord of your life.
With great affection He took each of you by the hand and seated you;
smiling, smiling, how many jests you exchanged with one another!
The most expert Gora loosed a fierce arrow from the corner of His eyes;
struck by it, you could not hold on to the pride of youth.
The serpents of the brows of all of you bit [Him] again and again —
and all the composure the moon of Nadia had seemed to leave at once.
Opening both arms, He embraces — incomparable is His tenderness.
In great joy I gave [Him] a sign; understanding it, He was enraptured all the more.
What was in your hearts the King of rasikas, as it were, fulfilled.
Narahari says: why are you shy to tell your own story?[^5]

*And another — yatharaga*

**8.5.** Listen, listen, friend: in a dream I saw Gora in the grove of bowers.
Gazing at the path by which you [were to come], in His distress He sheds tears from His eyes.
Hearing from my lips that you were coming, how He entreated me!
Seeing His state, in great haste I came and told you.
Hearing this, you joyfully adorned yourself and met [Him] in the bower;
welcoming [you] from afar, the King of rasikas took [you] by the hand and clasped [you] in His arms.
How much merriment arose! — and on some pretext you became a manini.
Narahari's Lord entreats [you] — and then I woke, distraught.[^6]

*And another — yatharaga*

**8.6.** Listen, listen, O [friend], I shall tell you of the night's dream.
I went to the house of Shrivasa Pandita Thakura, where Gaura [was].
How lovely is the courtyard of Shrivasa! — seeing it, my eyes were refreshed.
With joy in my heart I stood in a secluded spot, keeping my dharma with composure.
There the associates [of the Lord], happy at heart, taking khol and karatalas,
sing with sweet, nectarean voices, utterly enraptured.
In the midst of that circle the son of Shachi shines — what wondrous attire!
His glossy curling hair is beautifully arranged with flowers of many kinds.
At His ears graceful earrings sway — incomparable is the radiance of His cheeks.
On His forehead are dots of fragrant sandal, like a host of autumn moons.
On His very broad, ample chest necklaces of many kinds swing;
the new ornaments He has put on shine — what can I compare Him to?
With graceful movements of His arms the Most Expert One dances — lovely is the motion of His feet.
He calls out "Hari, Hari!" — hearing it, no one keeps composure.
I do not know what feeling arose in Him: His body trembles again and again,
from both eyes streams flow ceaselessly, like the current of a river.
Heaving deep sighs, distraught, He fell to the ground
and rolls like a golden lotus — no one can hold Him.
Seeing that, my heart trembled and I gave tilanjali to shame;
crying "What has happened? What has happened?", I wept aloud, beside myself.
At that moment my sleep broke; waking, I sat up where I lay.
"What is it, what is it?" — all came running and ask why I am weeping.
Whom could I tell, and what? With grief in my heart I fell asleep again, like a chataki bird.
And again I dreamt: Gora came into my courtyard.
"Come in, come in, friend!" I said, and quickly seated [Him] on a couch.
Knowing He was weary, I carefully fanned [Him] with the edge of my sari.
Preparing fresh tambula, smiling, I offered it to that moon-face —
and with Narahari's Lord of life I lay down [to rest] with joy in my heart.[^7]

*And another — yatharaga*

**8.7.** Listen, listen, O [friend]: I shall tell the night's dream that is in my mind.
The people of the world went mad over the virtues of Gaurangachandra.
The evil-minded, the crooked, the deceitful, the slanderers — as many as there were —
all gave up their perverse nature and became followers of Gaura.
Seeing many such things, I woke once, friend;
falling asleep again, I saw another wonder — I shall tell you all of it.
As Shyama on the bank of the Yamuna performed the rasa-vilasa and so on,
so Gora on the bank of the Suradhuni created an abode of rasa.
Casting off shame, [the honour of] family and fear, all the nagaris of Nadia,
mounted on the chariot of their longings, go in throngs, evading hundreds of thorns.
I, leaving the housework, was very restless to go there,
but suddenly my husband came and quickly shut the door of the house.
I fell into a plight — whom could I tell, and what? — and my distress grew.
In my heart it was thus: life will not remain… shall I see the Beloved again?
At that moment my clever [breeze], as if by chance, threw open the door,
and, by good fortune, softly, softly I stepped outside the house.
Overjoyed, I ran without turning to look at anyone —
as a young bird flies away from an iron cage.
Arriving quickly, I filled my eyes gazing at Gaura-raya:
He shines splendidly amid a circle of young women — what can I compare Him to?
Instruments of many kinds play on all sides — there is no limit to the happiness;
the women sing with sweet voices, showering streams of nectar.
Drunk with the honey of that lotus-face, I too joyfully began to dance.
Narahari's Lord, seeing that, smiled and held me to His breast.[^8]

*And another — yatharaga*

**8.8.** Listen, friend, to the night's dream — I tell it, having cast off shyness.
Softly, softly Gora enters the chamber, warily glancing on all sides.
Smiling, smiling, He came to the head of the bed and sat down;
with His own hand He touched my lips — and floats in an ocean of happiness.
Enraptured, He speaks the sweetest words in many ways, under the guise of jest;
twining His arms [about me] and holding [me] to His heart, He is drenched with tears from His eyes.
In His heart He fears to part from His treasure even for a moment.
The Beloved of Narahari's life is love embodied: what more can I say?[^9]

*And another — yatharaga*

**8.9.** Listen, listen to the night's dream, friend: casting off shyness, I tell you.
At daybreak, in lovely attire, Gora came to me.
Looking at that moon-face, I said: "Why have You come running?
Where You happily spent the night — go there quickly and meet [her] there.
The plain signs cannot be hidden: with her You passed the night awake."
Hearing this, He swears hundreds of oaths; hoping for a touch, He entreats me.
At that moment my sleep broke — Narahari knows what state I was in.[^10]

*And another — yatharaga*

**8.10.** Listen, listen, O friend! I shall tell you the night's dream.
With great effort the moon of Nadia came and slipped into the house.
At that moment my cruel sister-in-law, standing at the door, says:
"You dally with another man — and are you not afraid?
Very well, very well: when brother comes in the morning, I shall tell him all this.
I shall leave, taking my honour with me — I will not stay in this sinful house!"
Hearing this, I thought in fear: "When the night is over,
I do not know what harsh thing my husband will do when he comes home.
Let everyone revile me as much as they like — that will not pain me;
[but] lest wicked people speak ill of the Beloved of my life!
If the Creator makes this known, it will be terrible:
for the rest of my life I shall never again see the moon of Nadia.
He will never come towards our quarter, will not remember me.
I am unfortunate — now I know it for certain; otherwise why would it be so?"
Saying this, I wept, distraught, repeating that name again and again.
Narahari knows: I escaped with my life only by quickly coming to my senses.[^11]

*And another — yatharaga*

**8.11.** Friend, listen to my dream of the night — it is a very funny story!
To guard me, my sister-in-law lay down where my bed is.
He, the moon of Nadiya, came and entered, wholly in the sway of Madana,
and, mistaking her for me, sat down on my sister-in-law's couch.
Gently, rejoicing, He touched her chin with His hand, tender as a sprout.
My sister-in-law woke, started up with a shudder, and looks all around.
She says to me: "Wake up, wake up quickly — a thief has got into the house!"
Hearing this, Gora ran off in fright and stood at a distance.
Hard on His heels the fierce sister-in-law ran after Him, scolding;
running some way, she caught Him — the Beloved of my heart could not escape.
Drunk with the pride of youth, she seized Him firmly by both hands
and, again and again speaking bitter words, leads [Him] into the house.
Gora, young, of kishora age, full of rasa, looked at my sister-in-law,
clasped her in His arms, overcame her — and did what was in His mind.
Not finding me, the Jewel of virtues grew sad.
And my sister-in-law, not herself, came back to me.
Looking at her, I asked: "Why is there such joy in your heart?"
She, with downcast face, says: "I have fallen into the hands of a terrible thief.
Keep it secret — or in the morning the clamour of scandal will spread through the world."
Narahari is witness: while I was consoling her, sleep left my eyes.[^12]

*And another — yatharaga*

**8.12.** Listen, friend, to my dream: the Lord of my heart, the king of rasikas,
yesterday entered [my] house unnoticed, a blanket wrapped about His body.
Seeing this, I asked with a soft smile: "Why have You dressed like this?"
The Beloved says: "Lest your sister-in-law recognize Me tomorrow."
Much He said in this way; hearing it, I covered my mouth with the end of my cloth
and, taking the Lord of my life by His lovely hand, joyfully seated Him on the couch.
What can I say of the sweetness of His face at that moment? It seemed to me
as if a mass of golden lotuses had blossomed in the waters of the Kalindi.
Seeing this, I took heart and pulled that blanket off Him —
as if the autumn moon had come out from behind dense clouds.
At that very moment my mother-in-law asks: "What is that light in the room?"
Hearing this, my whole body trembled — my heart all but flew away.
Hurrying out, I stood in the doorway, looking about with fear in my heart,
and, plucking up courage, began to speak clever words to her:
"You know I keep the vow of Chandra-vrata — I observe it with care.
Today he, pleased with my worship, has graciously appeared to me.
He wishes to grant a boon — and what boon to ask I do not know at all.
Whatever you yourself say, that I will take; only do not come in here."
Hearing this, she, delighted, says softly and with great care:
"Ask for wealth, long life and well-being for everyone — take that much."
Hearing this, [I] quickly went back into the room in great joy,
sat beside the Beloved and wiped His body with the end of my cloth.
Narahari's Lord of life — with what tenderness He embraced me!
And at that moment sleep broke — in separation I was flooded with tears.[^13]

*And another — yatharaga*

**8.13.** Listen, listen, I shall tell you: in a dream He, the moon of Nadiya,
came smiling and sat beside me — like a mass of golden lotuses.
He says to me: "Today, with your own hands, adorn Me."
What happiness, hearing those clever words! What can I say?
The merriment grew. That hair by which the young women of Nadiya are enchanted
I anointed with many fragrant oils, tied in a graceful slanting knot and adorned with flowers.
On His forehead I drew a lovely dot of sandal paste, like a beautiful moon;
in His ears I placed earrings — they flash [like] the sun; with what can they be compared?
Wiping His lotus face, steeped in smiles, and seeing His brows — a row of bees,
gazing eye into eye, rapt in joy, I put a pearl in His nose.
His graceful arms, surpassing an elephant's trunk, steal away composure and dharma;
putting many ornaments on them, I lovingly placed bangles on His wrists as well.
On His broad chest I arranged a necklace — matchless is the beauty of His waist!
Around His hips I put a belt of little bells — the radiance of His knees shines.
On the feet of Narahari's Beloved I carefully put anklets —
and at that moment sleep broke: I could not gaze my fill [at Him].[^14]

*And another — yatharaga*

**8.14.** Listen, listen, friend of my heart!
Casting off shyness, I shall tell you all my dream of the night.
Smiling, smiling, floating in happiness, that playful One — with what tenderness —
opened both arms and with many graceful gestures quickly embraces me!
I cannot keep still: trembling, my body quivers like lightning;
like a greedy bee, rapt with Madana, He kisses my face.
That moon-face became beautifully adorned with kohl, kunkuma and sindura —
seeing this, I teased Him; hearing it, the Beloved grew shy.
To my heart's content I again wipe that moon-face, smiling a little…
[…]
My heart flew away — what to do? — the Beloved hid in fear.
At that moment sleep broke; awake — my heart is pounding.
When I fell asleep again, that young Nagara adorns me:
He decked my parting with sindura and with such care bound up my hair.
When He went to put a bodice on my breast, I said: "Why are You putting a bodice [on me]?"
The Beloved, laughing, says: "Have you no man's attire in mind?"
What more can I say? Putting a ring in my nose, He, quite unable to restrain Himself,
at once laid me upon His broad chest.
In many ways He tells stories of rasa — as if pouring out nectar.
Narahari's Lord — love embodied, like the Enchanter of young women.[^15]

*And another — yatharaga*

**8.15.** What can I say? How He jests with me in my dream — Gora, my crown of rasikas!
What eyes! His sidelong glances are irresistible — the Thief of the treasure of life and youth.
Smiling a sweet, sweet smile, floating in happiness, He puts His face to my face and embraces [me];
His body thrills in the waves of Madana — what talk of rasa He does not start!
Again and again swinging the ring in my nose — I do not know in what rasa He is lost —
covering [my] breast with His palm, He says again and again: "These two golden lotuses are Mine."
He wants to remove all the coverings from [my] body; when I restrain Him, His play only grows.
The Beloved of Narahari's heart is so shameless — He breaks the vows of young women.[^16]

*And another — yatharaga*

**8.16.** In my dream the Beloved sat on my couch — once I glanced [at Him] from the corner of my eye.
Gora, love embodied — with what tenderness He regards Himself as subject [to me]!
With that moon-face He tells me again and again: "You are dearer to Me than life."
Saying this, He embraces [me] and floats in happiness — and I am overcome with shyness.
Preparing tambula, He places it in my mouth and, lost in Madana, gazes [at me];
again with His tender hand He touches my lips — and gives His very life for them.
Smiling a sweet, sweet smile, He showers nectar — how He prizes the fine art of rasa!
The Beloved of Narahari's heart — the darling of the heart, the one who enchants young women.[^17]

*And a certain [other] — yatharaga*

**8.17.** Hear my dream: gazing and gazing at me, Gora, the thief of young women's hearts,
plays with His eyes that surpass a pair of wagtails — I do not know in what rasa He is lost.
Smiling, smiling, He comes, sits down near me and with His own hand draws back [my] veil;
saying "Ah, how lovely!" He touches [my] chin and looks at [my] face again and again.
What love is His! In His mind it is this — to make [me] a necklace and wear [me] round His neck.
At the touch of body to body, how the merriment grows! Young Madana is His companion.
And how much art of rasa in His kiss — what can I say? Never have I heard of such.
Where did the Beloved of Narahari's heart learn it? How many words of rasa He knows![^18]

*And another — yatharaga*

**8.18.** O friend! My Gora is a bee of rasa,
the thief of the honey of the lotus faces of ever new young women — who knows His secret?
Coming in a dream, He sat down near me and, smiling, smiling, speaks.
I do not know how He pours out that nectarean rasa — it dispels the longing of ears and heart.
With what tenderness He touches my chin — and on some pretext, what graceful gestures He makes!
Lips pressed to lips, never moving His eyes away, He covers [my] breast with His palm.
He holds heart to heart — what more can I say? — His golden body trembles again and again.
The Beloved of Narahari's heart — what does He not know! His love is an ocean of happiness.[^19]

*Yatharaga*

**8.19.** As they were telling their dreams, a wave of prema rose:
such is that matchless love — no one could keep her composure.
One says: "Ah, the Creator made [me] a woman only to make me suffer:
never could I gaze my fill at such a moon — Gora."
Another says: "Ah, being a woman, the wish of my heart is not fulfilled.
I shall use all kinds of cunning and be rid of this fear of the elders."
Another says: "Ah, a plague on these elders — what is there to fear?
For Gaurasundara, the treasure of my life, I shall surely leave my home."
Another says: "Ah, the people of Nadiya are so spiteful:
I never even see the Lord of my life, and still they say so many evil things."
Another says: "Ah, in the town of Nadiya there will be talk of scandal.
I shall pay no heed to it: I shall keep the Beloved in my heart and be rid of my pain."
Another says: "Ah, day and night there is one wish in my heart:
let me surely be slandered — [together] with Shri Shachinandana."
Another says: "Ah, say what you will — I can stay no longer:
without Him my heart is restless; tell me, what am I to do?"
Another says: "Ah, consigning this shame before the family to the fire,
come, come — let us go now and quickly meet the Lord of our life!"
Another says: "Look, what is this? Ah, my left eye is twitching!"
Narahari says: why worry? All these are companions of good fortune.[^20]

*Yatharaga*

**8.20.** At dawn, having seen many auspicious signs, the young women
partly forgot the pain of their hearts, and joy grew in their minds.
One says: "Ah, I have understood: today the Creator has become gracious;
whatever desire each of us has — all of it will be fulfilled."
Another says: "Ah, day after day I worship this Jahnavi;
by her blessing I shall surely attain the Lord of my life — know this."
Another says: "Ah, with great care I worship Gauri every day;
she will free [me] from sorrow — I shall meet Gauranga, the Lord of my life."
Another says: "Ah, I worship Bhanu in many ways;
by the power of his mercy I shall soothe my heart — do not be anxious in your mind."
Another says: "If today, without hindrance, I see the Beloved of my life,
then I shall carefully worship Budo-Shiva, offering various gifts."
Another says: "I feel as if I shall meet Him right now."
Thus, in the rapture of prema, they say so much to one another;
for the darshan of Shri Gaurasundara the hearts of all are restless.
Narahari says: ah, ah! I would take upon myself the misfortunes of such prema![^21]

*And a certain [other] — yatharaga*

**8.21.** The ways of the nagaris of Nadia — who has the power to describe them?
Without Gora their minds know nothing else; Gora is their all, their life and soul.
The tales of the moon Gaura are a well of nectarean rasa; His incomparable qualities are the water in it.
Narahari always hopes for this one thing: may the deeds of the nagaris shine forth in his heart.[^22]

*Thus [ends] the eighth kirana of the Shrimad Gaura-charita-chintamani, called "The Description of the Conversations about Dreams", in the description of the deeds of the morning period.*[^23]

[^1]: The name of the kirana is taken from its colophon at the end of the kirana (after 8.21, pdf 110, printed p. 105; checked against the scan): "ইতি শ্রীমদ্গৌরচরিত্র-চিন্তামণৌ প্রাতঃকালীয়-চরিত্র-বর্ণনে স্বপ্নপ্রসঙ্গ-বর্ণনং নামাষ্টমঃ কিরণঃ ॥৮॥" — "the eighth kirana, called 'The Description of the Conversations about Dreams' (*svapna-prasaṅga*), in the description of the deeds of the morning period" (the OCR gives *svapana prasaṅga*; the scan has *svapna-prasaṅga*). The subheading "Again, in the morning period — the conversations about dreams and so on of the nagaris of Shri Navadvipa" (*punaḥ prātaḥkāle śrī-navadvīpa-nāgarīṇāṁ svapna-prasaṅgādikaṁ*) stands immediately after the colophon of kirana 7 (pdf 88, printed p. 91 by the running head). Kirana 8 comprises verses 8.1–8.21 (pdf 88–93 and 102–110; pdf 94–101 repeat pdf 86–93 in the scan). The nagaris are the young married women of Navadvipa (see notes 1 and 3 of kirana 7). The summary line is based on 8.1–8.3 and a quick look at the OCR of the rest of the kirana.
[^2]: Vibhasha is a morning raga (cf. 7.1). Each line of the verse consists of two halves, separated in print by "।". "Merriment" — *kautuka*. "In the circle of friends" — *sakhīra mele*. "Float in the rasa of Gauranga" — *gaurāṅgera rase bhāse*. "Playful one" — *raṅginī*. "Come now" — the print has *bhāga*; by sense and rhyme (*bala*) probably *bhāla* ("well then, come now"; cf. note 7 of kirana 7, the same case); the reading is unclear. "Spent the night" — *bañcile* (cf. note 5 of kirana 7). "Drunk with happiness" — *sukhe māti*. No signature.
[^3]: The heading in print is "কাচিদাহ—যথা" (*kācid āha — yathā*), without the word "raga"; understood as an abbreviation of *yathārāga*. Here a new kind of the nagaris' mood begins — dreams in which they serve Gaura and He responds to them with intimacy. Gaudiya Vaishnavas, following Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati and the *Chaitanya-bhagavata* (Adi 15: in His lila the Lord did not jest with women or even look at them), reject *gaura-nagari-vada*, the worship of Gaura as an enjoying lover (*nāgara*) and of oneself as His nagari: Gaura is Krishna in the mood of Radha, tasting Her love, not an enjoyer. The nagaris' dreams and longings in the poem are understood as a glorification of Gaura's irresistible beauty and of the prema it awakens in hearts; in waking life Gaura here sits among His associates and converses with Gadadhara (cf. the main note of kirana 7, note 3). Further descriptions of the nagaris' dreams refer back to this note. Gauri is Parvati, the consort of Shiva; cf. the gopis who, for Krishna's sake, kept the vow of Katyayani (Durga) (*Bhagavata Purana* 10.22). "She will free [me] from sorrow" — *te̐ha dukha dūra kariba* (lit. "she… [I] shall be freed from sorrow").
