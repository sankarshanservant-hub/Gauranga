# Kirana 10. The Deeds of the Morning: The Description of the Delights and So On of the Heavenly Women Filled with Prema for Him[^1]

*Morning: the heavenly women, circling in the sky above Navadvipa, gaze at Gaura and marvel at the prema of the people and the nagaris of Nadiya; talking among themselves, they dispute about Gaura's nature — some speak of His secret plays as a nagara, others defend Him as the One who has conquered the senses — and one of them longs to become a companion of the nagaris of Nadiya*

*Again, in the morning period — the various delights and so on of the heavenly women, eager for the rasa of beholding Him.*

*Matanga-chhandah*

**10.1.** At the close of night the house of Shachi, the ornament of the world, is lovelier than ever.
From house to house through the town of Nadiya, charming men and women hasten in all directions.
What poet can describe it? Jubilation surges like the waves of an ocean of joy.
The women of the city [of the gods], with their companions, moving through the sky, glide gracefully and unceasingly.
With hearts most pure yet tremulous, they ponder whether to descend to earth;
with great, great effort they keep their composure, while their bodies tremble violently and irrepressibly.
With restless eyes they taste the lovely, delightful radiance of the excellent Gaura.
Balihari! — [says] Narahari: they are all overwhelmed, and the touch [of that radiance] again holds them in its power.[^2]

*Rangamala-chhandah*

**10.2.** The host of heavenly women, gazing at the Beloved, their hearts rejoicing, are beautiful in their wondrous manner;
their abundant new ornaments and garments sparkle, their limbs all thrill with rapture.
In shapely bands, unapproachable, lovely, beautifully attired, they shine in many ways;
risen into the sky, they illumine its vault like rows of heavenly lightning.
Filled with auspicious bhava, with incomparable grace, they move very slowly;
again and again the veil slips and falls, again and again the new knot of the waist-cloth loosens.
They smile softly, they bite their sweet lips — their teeth gleam;
holding one another by the hand, they say something to one another — Narahari Dasa is delighted.[^3]

*A certain one [of them] says — yatharaga*

**10.3.** Friend! Thinking day and night, we have understood for certain:
Gora has appeared in order to drive the people of Nadiya mad.
No one can stay apart from the moon Gauranga even for a moment;
on their beds and in their dreams — always in meditation [on Him]: their minds do not stay at home.
What more is there to say? As witness to it — look toward this quarter and that:
even lying down, they repeat "Gora, Gora" and bring no other [word] to their lips.
Listen, filling your ears — a wondrous sweet sound at dawn:
in singing the deeds of Narahari's Lord they are all supremely skilled.[^4]

*And a certain [other] — yatharaga*

**10.4.** Look, look, ah, what bliss there is today in the beautiful city of Nadiya!
All the auspicious splendour of the world is in the homes of the people of Nadiya.
Ah, ah! How to understand their ways — one is left bewildered:
leaving their beds, they wage a fierce struggle with the night.
One says: "Come, come quickly, no delay can be borne now:
come what may — let all the work just lie where it is."
How many such conversations pass among them!
To see Narahari's Lord of life, no one stays at home.[^5]

*And a certain [other] — yatharaga*

**10.5.** Blessed, blessed, O, in all the world are the residents of Nadia:
surely the treasure of life for all of them is Gauranga, the abode of virtues.
Be that as it may, the nagaris of Nadia are the most blessed among them:
I do not know how many, many hundreds of yugas they performed pious deeds!
Look, look, what wondrous love they have for the moon Gauranga:
at the end of the night they run from all sides, unable to restrain themselves.
Ah, ah, would that I could take upon myself all the misfortunes of these young women!
Though they are wives of [respectable] families, they count shame and propriety as nothing.
Shame, shame on dwelling in the city of the gods, shame on the burden of this body!
Shame, shame on these eyes! What use now is such beauty and youth?
If only I had such good fortune — to live in Nadia even once,
then, joining the company of these [women], I would fulfil my heart's wish.
Happy in Gora's happiness, they do not know any happiness of their own;
not seeing that face for a moment, they count it a grief greater than dying.
Singing their virtues, my heart rejoices day and night;
Narahari is witness to it: I always love them more than life.[^6]

*And a certain [other] — yatharaga*

**10.6.** Who has the power to understand the deeds of the nagaris of Nadia?
Such beauties, such connoisseurs of rasa, there are no others in the world.
Look, look, O — ah, would that I could take upon myself all the misfortunes of this wondrous beauty! —
the roads of Nadia are [like] rivers, and on them, it seems, rows of lotuses have bloomed.
How wondrously skilful their gait, how wondrously they glance at one another!
Laughing and jesting, overcome by rasa, how many graceful gestures they make!
Their new garments and ornaments glitter beyond compare;
I do not know why — again and again the necklaces sway on their breasts.
On their heads the lovely locks have come loose, strings of flowers are falling;
not even for a moment can they slow their hurried gait.
Rejoicing, [eager] to meet Narahari's Lord, they enter the house [of Shachi];
as if knowing that they will offer [Him] the treasure of their youth as a gift, the anklets ring auspiciously.[^7]

*And a certain [other] — yatharaga*

**10.7.** Ah, ah, O, what wondrous beauty in the midst of Nadia!
I do not know who built Shachi's house, which enchants the whole world.
On all sides glitter rows of new golden mansions;
in the golden courtyards so many golden men and women sport.
And another wonder — look: the town of Nadia has become golden;
the golden trees, the kadambas, are beautifully adorned with golden creepers.
The beasts and birds are golden, and all the insects and moths are like gold.
Who has stolen the whiteness [of the Ganga]? Jahnavi has become a golden stream.
The sky has become golden — what is this? Look: the whole world is like gold!
Surely this is how great is the power of the beauty of Narahari's Lord.[^8]

*And a certain [other] — yatharaga*

**10.8.** Look, look, O — the radiance of the beauty of Gauranga, who enchants the world!
Is this a mass of lightning that has left the cloud and [come down] upon the earth?
Is this a heap of pure, beautiful buds of the golden lotus?
Is this a heap of pure, lovely, finely ground gorochana?
Is this the fresh layer of kunkuma on the breasts of the young maidens of Vraja?
Is this the champaka garland on the necks of the nagaris of Navadvipa?
My heart feels: if only I could keep Him always within my heart!
The eyes are not sated looking [at Him] — Narahari is witness to it.[^9]

*And a certain [other] — yatharaga*

**10.9.** Look, look, O — the world-enchanting attire of the moon Gauranga!
His lovely curling hair, wreathed with kunda buds, has come loose.
On His graceful forehead is a tilaka of kunkuma, beautifully adorned with dots of sandal paste —
as if the lord of the stars had risen in the midst of a golden sky.
In His ears glitter earrings — who can find a comparison for them?
I understand: He will steal everything — composure, propriety and modesty.
On His neck is a garland that enchants young women, falling in incomparably graceful tiers.
I look at Narahari's Lord — what a form! — I cannot understand what play this is.[^10]

*And a certain [other] — yatharaga*

**10.10.** Listen, listen, O you who are dear as life: I will whisper in your ear —
what you have reasoned has come to my mind as well.
He seems somehow strange today — as you yourself see, clever one:
so overpowered, overcome by rasa, I have never seen Him.
And if I ever did see it, still I could make out nothing.
Tell me, tell me then: who has stolen the heart of the moon Gauranga?
Narahari's Moon is truly a nagara: one can understand it from [His] deeds;
and yet I cannot say this firmly to anyone, out of shyness.[^11]

*And a certain [other] — yatharaga*

**10.11.** Look, come here, O: I have understood all the thoughts of your heart.
Until now you did not know these deeds of Gorachanda.
So I tell you for certain: do not puzzle over it any longer —
Gauranga is enchanted by the love of these young women.
Today He spent the night with some fortunate woman
and, granting her the touch of this body, fulfilled all her heart's wishes.
She is a nagari most expert in rasa: I understood this, floating in supreme bliss —
she did not let the young crown of the nagaras of Nadia sleep at night.
If you say, "How am I to know this?" — look at the witness to it:
His two lovely reddened eyes — languid, overpowered, drooping [with sleep].
And besides, such disorder in His attire — what doubt can there be?
The secret pastimes of Narahari's Lord are manifest to rasikas.[^12]

*And a certain [other] — yatharaga*

**10.12.** What shall I say, O: you have felt it well and judged it rightly.
All the nagara-ways of the moon Gauranga I know,
[but] I am shy to tell you those stories;
and so, watching Gauranga's deeds, I laugh within.
He always thinks of Himself thus: "I am the king of the clever:
secret is My descent, and secret are all My deeds;
secret My walk and speech, secret the curves of My dance,
secret with the nagaris of Nadia the play of love;
I show My nagara-ways secretly — no one can notice it."
Thinking much in this way, He covers the rays of the sun with His hand.
Before one who is clever above the clever, can any cunning hold?
Not understanding this, whom does Narahari's Lord fear?[^13]

*And another — yatharaga*

**10.13.** That it is all so with the moon Gauranga — do not grieve over it:
what is open brings sorrow; in what is secret there is greater happiness.
Even on gaining a little wealth, [people] hide it more carefully than life;
if you say this is impossible, look at the people of the world.
Love is a supreme jewel: it is fitting to keep it hidden;
if it becomes open, shame arises in the hearts of rasikas.
Narahari's Lord is the crown of the adroit: does anyone know another such?
He sports in secret without cease — [and by this] reveals His adroitness.[^14]

*And another — yatharaga*

**10.14.** Say what you will, [but] to hide love is too heavy a burden:
love never stays hidden — its nature is to become manifest.
He acts like the sun — can I consider this secret?
You know [His pastimes] as "secret, secret," but I know them as manifest.
In the town of Nadia is the crown of rasikas, Shachi's darling, Gora,
the thief of the jewel of composure of all the young wives of good families.
I have seen: nowhere in all the world is there such a nagara;
know for certain: no one can escape Him.
If some young woman, holding to her composure, wishes to keep her propriety,
He, glancing [at her] with the irresistible corner of His eye, enchants her.
Day and night He plays endlessly with the young nagaris;
Narahari's Lord, the beloved of the nagaris, pines for the nagaris.[^15]

*And a certain [other] — yatharaga*

**10.15.** Listen, listen, O: I say it for certain — we are utterly unknowing:
we could not understand that Gora displays such nagara-ways in Nadia.
As we see Him outwardly — supremely noble —
so we know Him; what is within Him cannot be known.
Blessed, blessed, it seems, are you — the supreme connoisseurs of rasa in the city of the gods!
Who but all of you could understand these pastimes?
Be that as it may, now at last the darkness of my mind seems to have gone:
Narahari's Lord has appeared in the world subject to young women.[^16]

*Yatharaga*

**10.16.** Of all the nagara-ways of the moon Gauranga everyone speaks — so many, many opinions!
It is as if streams of nectar were raining down; I do not know what happiness is in everyone's heart.
Then yet another woman — from another, new group — came there, hearing these words.
Narahari does not know her nature: she boldly addresses them all.[^17]

*Yatharaga*

**10.17.** What shall I say to you, O? I have fallen into bewilderment:
why do you so revile such a worthy [person] — the moon of Nadia?
The most learned Jagannatha Mishra — who does not know him?
[He is] the lamp of his spotless family, and the world sings of Him;
He conquered the conqueror of the quarters, and the pandits of Nadia are subject to Him;
always devoted to the path of dharma, He knows nothing but the Vedas and the other [scriptures].
He never listens to talk about women: to hear it pains Him;
never, even inadvertently, does He look at the faces of others' wives.
If ever, while bathing in the Suradhuni, a woman's garment touches His body,
He at once performs the proper atonement — and still He cannot regain His composure.
Therefore, to cast false blame on Him deliberately will be an offence.
Narahari is witness to it: I teach you all — never speak these words.[^18]

*Yatharaga*

**10.18.** Yet another woman, her heart full to overflowing,
coming to that place, looks in all directions.
Who can understand her heart? There is no fear in her at all.
Taking her friend by the hand, she says to her:[^19]

*Yatharaga*

**10.19.** Look, come here, O: why are you quarrelling with all of them?
An empty commotion has arisen in the city of the gods — do not be troubled by it.
All good conduct has gone far away: no one respects anyone;
this is a great misfortune: they do not understand at all what comes from what.
"It is forbidden to censure even one who has faults" — everyone says this;
and whoever blames the faultless surely becomes faulty in the world himself.
The son of Shachi is a most worthy [person]: this is known throughout the world.
Let alone looking at anyone — He does not even raise His face, out of modesty.
He never touches even the shadow of other women —
I do not understand how [one can speak of Him] touching bodies and the like.
Knowing that young women crowd the ghats of the Suradhuni, He does not go there:
He goes to a pond and bathes where He sees it is secluded,
or else He performs His rites in His own house: His heart trembles for fear of disgrace.
The supreme Conqueror of the senses, dear to all — who does not praise Him?
Alas, alas! How can one speak such words about such a person,
whose incomparable fame, [like] an elixir, fills the world?
Who would believe such words about Him? I am ashamed of this.
Does a worthy person revile a worthy person? That is the work of the unworthy.
Still I speak, knowing [you as my] companions: you will accept the essence of my words.
Never, even inadvertently, let anyone revile Narahari's Lord any more.[^20]

*Yatharaga*

**10.20.** All these mocking women, [hearing] the words of instruction —
hearing them — were greatly amused at heart.
Looking at her, they laugh to themselves
and, slyly, speak with mocking gestures:[^21]

*Yatharaga*

**10.21.** Good, good, O! We are not afraid of such words:
as worthy a person as you are, so worthy is your Gora.
Aha, ah, ah! He knows nothing [of such things] — I have never seen such a person!
Supremely the Conqueror of the senses, like the king of sages — His righteousness is known to all.
He does not listen to talk about women — this fame is well known throughout the world;
leaving His own house, He never goes to anyone's home on any business.
How many more such incomparable virtues will you list?
And whatever else is within Him, He does not reveal outwardly.
So I say, listen: others have no power to know those virtues:
only in the flashes of the sidelong glances of these young women do they become manifest.
I see your eyes are like a bird that does not see the moon of the night:
how would others understand that Narahari's Lord is a snare enchanting women?[^22]

*Her ally says — yatharaga*

**10.22.** Look, come here, friend dear as life! There is no joy in my mind from this:
in vain you speak all these secret words of rasa with her.
Can anyone but a woman who knows rasa understand the heart of a connoisseur of rasa?
And besides, she is very simple-hearted — she never walks this path.
However much you explain to her, it is no concern of hers:
can she whose attachment is to the formless ever come to love form?
If ever she sincerely shares your rare company,
then she will be able to understand what the sports of the moon of Nadia are like.
Let all these words rest for now; just ask her once:
how does He, being supremely the Conqueror of the senses, play like this?
He who knows nothing [of such things], who has not a trace of [such] joy —
why, in the midst of the city of Nadia, does He take on the guise of a nagarini?
Nowhere have I heard of this: is this the act of a noble person?
By the fragrance of His body He breaks the dignity and modesty of the bee-like women.
He who is so composed — are these His deeds? How does it seem to you?
Seeing a man's face, He mistakes it for a woman's face and swoons.
This is very strange: one who has so much modesty — are such deeds His?
In the assembly of the righteous He dances incessantly, repeating a woman's name.
He who never listens with His own ears to talk about women —
how does He constantly repeat [the name of] a woman in His mind?
He who in the world is above all, endlessly righteous —
why does He gather so many young women in His own house?
Tell her: let her answer about this conduct of Narahari's Lord.
How has such faith in such a person become fixed in her heart?[^23]

*Yatharaga*

**10.23.** The mocking women said much in this way.
Who could answer? That young woman was abashed at heart.
Then yet another woman, who knows great skill [in speech],
looking at her own [companions], speaks sweet words:[^24]

*Yatharaga*

**10.24.** Listen, listen, O: I have understood everything — she is not at fault at all;
having considered it, I am angry with all of you.
If someone, not understanding, says something — why laugh at it?
If you explain it as she is able to understand, I will consider you wise.
She is of good character — ah! — and you do not know how to explain to such a person.
Be silent now and listen to what I say.
Look, come here, O worthy beauty, do not grieve at heart:
hearing your words, I have found supreme happiness.
You say that Gora does not look at other women even from the corner of His eye —
how am I to believe all these words?
If I could see even a little of what you praise Him for,
then, though you assert it and swear oaths, I would still come to believe.
The nagara-ways in the city of Nadia are beyond counting;
whatever others may say — this I have seen myself.
If you say, "How is this possible in this descent?" —
there is a reason for it, a well-known one: all people speak of it.
Whatever nature one has, no one can ever give it up:
one acts according to one's nature, heeding no one's prohibition.
If you think, "Nowhere have I seen such a nature in Him" —
then I tell you about this, listen: the whole world is witness to it.
This son of Shachi is the son of Yashoda: do you not know this?
What shall I tell you of all the secret pastimes in Vrindavan?
For the gopis' sake — herding the cows, lifting the hill and all the rest;
and how much can I tell of the pastimes with the gopis — where and what [they were]?
He is utterly subject to them all: not seeing them for a moment, He feels it as an aeon;
under how many pretexts He wanders constantly, hoping for the touch of [their] bodies!
If some proud one, in mana, does not turn to look at Kanu,
then, to end her mana, He clasps the feet of [her] sakhi.
He carries [her] on His shoulder, deeming it His own supreme good fortune;
hearing words of reproach, He rejoices more than at the hymns of the Vedas.
For the sake of young women He takes no account of grave disgrace in the world.
Tell me, tell me: how will He give up such a nature?
Therefore know this for certain, having considered it in your hearts:
[His] nature makes [Him] do all these deeds — you will understand it yourselves.
Narahari's Lord is the Crown of connoisseurs of rasa, Who has no equal:
who does not know these deeds? What further doubt is there?[^25]

*Yatharaga*

**10.25.** The special plays of rasa in the city of Vraja — who can tell of them all?
Keep hidden in your heart what I shall tell you, knowing you as my own:
this One is that very One, that very One is this; together with all those same dear companions
He ever sports in the city of Nadiya, absorbed in His own virtues.
First giving His own people to drink the nectar of the wondrous sweetness of [His] beauty,
He makes their minds and gait like those of the mad — and so they reckon with nothing.
Ever new hosts of wives of respectable families, having given tilanjali to the disgrace of their families and to shame,
have made their very essence Gora, full of joy, the Beloved, dear as life — Narahari is witness to it.[^26]

*Yatharaga*

**10.26.** Hearing again and again of the lovely deeds of the moon Gauranga, that young woman was filled with happiness;
she cannot keep her composure — again and again both her eyes fill with tears of prema.
Joining her palms, she bows and again speaks soft, sweet words:
"Accepting me as your own, you have, as it were, at last dispelled all the pain of my heart.
I pray: how may I find a dwelling, even once, in this city of Nadiya,
and how may I live as a companion of these ever new playful ones?
Keeping in my heart Narahari's Beloved, dear as life, I shall remove the grievous obstacle.
Tell me, tell me, O — by what means will all these desires be fulfilled?"[^27]

*Yatharaga*

**10.27.** Hearing the lovely words and accepting [them], the beautiful one rejoiced and, reflecting again in her heart,
she told generously of all that the other had asked and all that was in her mind.
That account is like streams of nectar: with one mouth I cannot describe it.
The hearts of all thrill with rapture — what more can I say? — they float in bliss.
Their bodies tremble again and again — a matchless manner! — and not a trace of composure can they hold.
One, leaning her body against another's, makes so many graceful movements from the fullness of rasa;
exchanging winks from the corners of their eyes, they laugh, absorbed in the sports of Ananga;
so many jests with one another intoxicate them — [as they] gaze at the Lord of Narahari.[^28]

*Thus [ends] the tenth kirana of the Shrimad Gaura-charita-chintamani, called "The Description of the Delights and So On of the Heavenly Women Filled with Prema for Him", in the description of the deeds of the morning period.*[^29]

The subheading "Again, in the morning period — the various delights and so on of the heavenly women, eager for the rasa of beholding Him" (*punaḥ prātaḥkāle deva-ramaṇīnāṁ tad-darśana-rasotsuka bibidha kautukādikaṁ*) stands immediately after the colophon of kirana 9 (pdf 122, printed p. 117). Kirana 10 comprises verses 10.1–10.27 (pdf 122–136). The heavenly women (*deva-ramaṇī*, *sura-nārī*) are the wives and maidens of the gods, who watch Gaura's pastimes from the sky; according to the pada glossary, they are the speakers in 10.10–10.24. The summary line is based on 10.1–10.4 and a quick look at the OCR of the following verses.
[^2]: The metre is "মাতঙ্গ ছন্দঃ" (Matanga-chhandah); the language is Brajabuli. The verse begins on pdf 122 (printed p. 117) and ends on pdf 123 (printed p. 118) with "॥১॥". Here begins a new form of the mood described in kiranas 7–9: the heavenly women, seeing Gaura from the sky, are filled with prema for Him and long to descend to earth. Gaudiya Vaishnavas, following Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati and the *Chaitanya-bhagavata* (Adi 15: in His lila the Lord did not jest with women and did not even look at them), reject gaura-nagari-vada — the worship of Gaura as an enjoying lover (*nāgara*) and of oneself as His nagari: Gaura is Krishna in the mood of Radha, tasting Her love, not an enjoyer. The rapture of the celestial women is understood in the poem as a glorification of Gaura's irresistible beauty and of the prema it awakens even in the world of the gods; from a quick look at the OCR, later in this same kirana some of the heavenly women defend Gaura before others as the One who has conquered the senses (*jitendriya*) and does not look at others' wives. Further notes in this kirana refer to this note (cf. the main note of kirana 9 — note 2). "The ornament of the world" — *bhubanaka bhūṣaṇa*. "Lovelier than ever" — *adhika suhāi*. "Charming" — *sughara*. "Hasten in all directions" — *cahũdiśa dhāi*: the people of Nadiya hasten to Shachi's house. "Jubilation surges like the waves of an ocean of joy" — *raṅga-jaladhi taraṅga sadṛśa umaṅgaye*. "The women of the city [of the gods]" — *pura-ramaṇī-gaṇa*; by the subheading and the words "moving through the sky" (*gagana-gata gati*) these are the heavenly women, *pura* being the city of the gods (cf. *surapura*). "With their companions" — *gaṇa-saha*. "Unceasingly" — *abhaṅgaye*; the understanding is uncertain. "Tremulous" — *tarala*. "To earth" — *khiti* (*kṣiti*). "Composure" — *dhṛti*. "Irrepressibly" — *anibāra*. "Taste" — *cākhaye*. "Balihari!" — *balihāri*, an exclamation of admiration ("I offer myself for this!"). The last half-line is *bibaśa saba punaḥ paraśa parabaśa rākhaye*: understood as "they are all overwhelmed, and the touch [of Gaura's radiance] again holds them in its power"; possibly also "Narahari… again restrains the overwhelmed ones"; the sense is uncertain. Signature: Narahari.
