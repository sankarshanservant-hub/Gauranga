# Kirana 2. The Deeds of Nishanta: Description of the Shayana-vilasa[^1]

*Nishanta — the close of night in Navadvipa: Nadia asleep; the associates, spending the night around the Lord's house, long for Him to wake; the beauty of Gaura asleep on His bed*

*Now — in the description of the deeds of the eight periods — first of all, nishanta.*

*Raga bhairava*

**2.1.** How lovely is that night! The town of Nadia is full of blessings.[^2]
The moon, the delighter [of all], has driven away the gloom with his rays.
Wondrous young trees and creepers shine with a sweetness that enchants the sages.
On fresh, blossoming flowers the bees roam in hope of honey.
A cool breeze blows softly, softly, spreading waves of fragrance.
In supreme bliss all the residents of Nadia are asleep.
The houses [are steeped in] deep [peace], ever full of happiness — there is no limit to their beauty;
nowhere in the three worlds have I seen its like.
Around the Lord's house all His dear associates have settled:
some lying down, some awake — who can understand such love?
Others, by [His] command, lie in their own homes, pining.[^3]
Narahari: when will such a lot be mine — to be near at that hour?

*Raga yatharaga*[^4]

**2.2.** Ah, ah! Who has the power to understand the deeds of Gaura's associates?
Lying down or dreaming, they know nothing but Gauranga.
The soft, soft smile of that moon-face destroys the pride of nectar;
not seeing it for even half a moment, they feel even a brief time as a kalpa.[^5]
What shall I say? In separation [from Him while He] sleeps, their hearts are all the more troubled;
"How soon will the night be over?" — they say, and gaze at the sky.
They strain their ears to hear the cries of peacocks, doves, cuckoos and other birds.
Narahari says: [their] hearts are restless — [they] think how [to hasten] the dawn.

*Raga yatharaga*

**2.3.** Who can describe the love of the associates!
Seeing the end of the night (nishanta), with all their lovely hearts [turned] towards the house (nishanta) [of the Lord],
never apart [from Him], their bodies thrilling all over! *(Refrain)*[^6]
Taking courage, [they] indulge in many hopes:
"The night is passing; now the dawn is coming.
Seeing the ever-new charm of Gaura rising from His lovely bed,
we shall satisfy these eyes.
From His softly smiling lips we shall drink our fill
of the nectar of His words, filling the cups of our ears.
Touching His feet with both our hands,
we shall then drive away the great sorrow of our hearts."
How many such hopes arise in [their] hearts!
Deeply immersed, [they] sing [His] host of virtues.
Narahari says: [their] hearts, like chatakas [awaiting] the cloud,
are full of longing, causeless — [they] do (not) understand [it].[^7]

*Raga yatharaga*

**2.4.** What shall I say? Today's happiness has no limit!
At the close of night, in the bedchamber,
lovely youthful Gaura (Gaura-kishora) lies asleep.
A charming, finely made bed shines,
most soft, white like the foam of milk.
On it Gauranga glitters and gleams,
dispelling the pride of many, many Madanas.[^8]
Heaps of lovely kunda flowers together with champakas —
as if lotuses opening in the waters of the Jahnavi.
On the wide [bed], white as camphor, [He is]
like a bright yellow creeper at play.
As if the chaste young Kirti[^9] with great care
had joyfully put a golden necklace upon her breast.
Says Ghanashyama: gazing at this lovely, fresh beauty,
the eyes are never satisfied.

*Kalindi-chhandah*

**2.5.** Who can describe Gaura's excellent body?
It glitters like a golden mirror.
A matchless reflection beautifully shines [in it];
hands, feet and the other [limbs] lie still in languor.

*Rangini-chhandah*

**2.6.** At night sleeps the root of happiness, lovely Gaurachandra, shining in full radiance,
in the middle of the chamber, on the lap of an excellent bed, free of care, He whom Shankara worships.[^10]
Through the clear openings of the lovely windows, which have enchanted hundreds of thousands of eyes, even [those] of rivals,
comes the slow movement of the breeze from Malaya, most gentle in its touch.[^11]
[His beauty] stops the blinking of people's eyes; [His] limbs are flawless, [His] posture graceful;
a delightful, shining mass of the rays of [His] beauty spreads around; wondrously [His] form shines.
Filling [all] with abundant fragrance, delighting — the glossy, charming, well-arranged mass of His hair;
from its fine knot kunda buds have slipped — as if stars shine upon a cloud.
Surpassing the morning dawn, the beautifully red lower lip — like a clinging bimba fruit;[^12]
the graceful, shining chin, lovely, enchants this world.
Swaying clusters of curls, clear and charming, touch (lit. "kiss") the loveliest face —
as if bees drunk with honey, [weary] at the end of the path [of their flight], adorn [it].[^13]
The excellent forehead, which has levelled the mountain of the moon's pride — on the forehead shines the sandal
tilaka, slightly smudged — it robs young women of their composure and [constancy in] dharma.[^14]
Ears [as receptive] as the minds of very noble people; earrings adorn the round cheeks;
this delightful pair surpasses Kama's bow: without a string (*guṇa*), yet richer in virtues (*guṇa*) in action.[^15]
The eyes, which outshine fresh lotus petals, are sealed by sleep — shining, captivating; I
imagine [them] as a pair of quivers of arrows, resting after conquering foes by their own strength.[^16]
The broad, remarkable chest is marked with shining, countless auspicious signs; upon it
many matchless garlands(?) in disarray — they bestow every delight.[^17]
On the soft, slender waist — a garment of Chinese silk, blue, spotless, pure,
slightly loosened; the belly, of lovely form, full of great beauty, oh![^18]
On the auspicious bed — [one] arm laid on the left side, slightly bent;
the right hand on the thigh; foot upon foot: [such is] Narahari's meditation, oh![^19]

*Raga lalita*

**2.7.** What shall I say? Matchless is Gaura's sleep!
His well-formed limbs glitter —
as if Kama Himself in embodied form were shining. *(Refrain)*[^20]
[His] body is soft as a lump of fresh butter
churned from a golden Ocean of Milk.
It is so wondrous that it seems, even without the heat of the sun,
it will melt upon the bed.
In languor, overcome, He breathes softly;
the shining belly rises and falls —
as if the breeze from Malaya, touching Sumeru, drove
waves along a lovely river [upon it] — now stronger, now gentler.[^21]
Let alone who could describe [this] —
the eyes gaze and are never satisfied.
Narahari says: when will He rest like this on the bed of my heart
and give me happiness?[^22]

*Raga yatharaga*

**2.8.** What shall I say? The great bed glitters exceedingly,
like the autumn season, free of [all] dullness.
Beyond the dreams even of the lord of the gods, wondrous,
it shines, charming, [surpassing] the might of Manobhava.[^23]
The excellent pillow, which stirs in the weary a longing [to lie down],
gleams — there is none like it in the world:
like a piece of Hara's mountain, like an unbroken(?) lump
of curd (dadhi), like the waves of the Ganga's waters.[^24]
On it — bandhuka, karavira, kunda, ketaki,
kanaka(?), jati, laid out [to delight] the eyes.[^25]
But though all these flowers are now [strewn upon it],
the bed of Gaurahari cannot be comprehended.
It seems the Creator, having painted a picture on a canvas of moonbeams,
offered [it] with great care to the Lord of the chamber.
Narahari says: O wise ones, this [bed] is raised
on a silver four-legged [frame] studded with gold and jewels.[^26]

*Bharati-chhandah*

**2.9.** The bed for sleep — graceful, spacious,
made of ivory(?), most lovely, softest of all.[^27]
Seeing [it], the heart falls into great bewilderment.
Matchless, [it] shines in the middle of the chamber.

*Raga lalita*

**2.10.** The chamber, enchanting people's minds, full of [Madana's charm] — who built it is beyond comprehension.
At the close of night its beauty is boundless — no poet is able to describe its splendour.
Four doors and eight windows are fashioned [in it] as is fitting; everything gives delight.[^28]
On wall after wall are painted graceful dances and many new pastimes in the kunjas, dispelling [all] bewilderment.
The spacious interior is lovely, like the Suradhuni; on all four sides are matchless jewel lamps.[^29]
Above is a matchless canopy; beneath it, in the middle of the bed, youthful Gaura shines.
The rays of each of His limbs wondrously glitter, matchless, within and without.
"This is not a chamber — it is a lovely casket made of a heap of gold, studded with jewels," says Ghanashyama.

*Tarangini-chhandah*

**2.11.** Who can describe [it], most lovely?
The chamber glitters boundlessly,
like a special ornament of the earth;
there is the endless bliss of sleep.

*Rangamala-chhandah*

**2.12.** At the close of night the breeze from Malaya is lovely, blowing softly, softly;
Gaurachandra, absorbed in the relish of sleep — the moon of the rasikas, oh!
His lovely limbs shatter the pride of Manmatha and steal the composure of women;
with abundant fragrance they fill the directions, gladdening the host of [His] dear ones, oh![^30]
Leaving the Nandana garden, the bees, greatly intoxicated, hurry [here],
enter the lovely chamber and, restless, circle and circle [there].
Their soft, lovely humming [He] hears as auspicious music —
and His sleep is slightly broken; with eyes closed, [His] mind takes delight.

*Raga lalita*

**2.13.** The son of Shachi is deep in sleep — who can understand His pastimes of prema?
[Recalling] the former happiness of rest in the nikunja, He speaks soft, half-formed (lit. "half-and-half") words:[^31]
"Wake up, lovely one, crest-jewel of women! How long will you sleep — the night is ending!
Without drinking the nectar of your words, [their] soft whisper, My eager ears are bereft of even a trace of happiness.[^32]
Leave off closing [your eyes]: with playful corners of the eyes, with lovely grace, sing [with Me].[^33]
My sly heart fearlessly tells you: give Me the jewel of [your] smile!
Understanding My wish, rise and sit up: with My own hands I will arrange your attire."
Thus Narahari's Lord speaks at length — and, fully awakened, forgets [these] words.[^34]

*Raga yatharaga*

**2.14.** Today the deep, dream-filled sleep of Gaura, [who sees Himself] in a kunja, cannot be described.
In the hope of seeing the lovely [scene] of Rai's awakening, [He] speaks anxious words: *(Refrain)*[^35]
"O dhani! The night and the moon have [already] shrunk; the chakravaki rejoices together with her mate.
Seeing their beloved, the lotuses laugh (open); the bee, greedy for honey, is bewildered in the kumuda.[^36]
Travellers, each on his own business, are [already] on the road; at this hour it is not fitting to lie in the lovely kunja.
Unnoticed, go quickly to your home: the elders are cunning, their nature hostile.[^37]
Cruel fate brings sorrow — what can I do now? [It] will burn My heart without respite."
Hearing these soft words, Gadadhara awoke and calmed his troubled [heart], — [says] Ghanashyama.[^38]

*Chatushpadi-chhandah*

**2.15.** Excellent Gadadhara, filled with composure,
reflected within himself: "The night is at its close."
He rose and sat up — joy in his heart, no timidity —
and gazes at the bed on which Gaura sleeps.[^39]

*Charumala*

**2.16.** Like a lovely peak of Sumeru in the midst of the Milk Ocean is the youthful
body of Gaura, adorned with unguents(?), shattering the pride of the sun.[^40]
Sri Gadadhara, steady, in supreme joy, his heart melting, thrilling all over,
gazes unblinking, with delight in his eyes, at Him who has put to shame hundreds of thousands of Smaras.
[He] serves [His] lovely lotus feet — gently, gently; waking a little,
the Lord stretched His limbs, paused — and lay down again on His back.
Narahari says: like a slender, full, inconceivably curving golden creeper
that, stirred by the touch of the breeze, softly quivers — [so He] has become the life of the good.[^41]

*Raga yatharaga*

**2.17.** Wondrous is the Lord's play upon the bed (shayana-vilasa)!
His two eyes are full of languor: the rays of the loveliest pupils [only] faintly peep out. *(Refrain)*
[They] shine like a jambu fruit on a silver dish, like darkness amid the rays of the autumn moon,
like an atasi flower amid kunda flowers, like the finest musk in a heap of camphor,
like a dark island in the midst of the Milk Ocean, like a sapphire pavilion on white ground,
like a wisp of fresh cloud on Hara's mountain, like a bee sitting in a white kumuda,
like [a dark emblem] in the middle of the lovely banner of spotless fame, like the collyrium in the eyes of young maidens that conquers Kama,
like madhura-rasa sporting on a ruby throne, — says Ghanashyama.[^42]

*Raga yatharaga*

**2.18.** Who can describe the beauty of excellent Gaura, full of rasa, — [as He] lies on His back, bestowing happiness!
[His] slender(?), graceful limbs flash — motionless, stealing the pride of masses of lightning.
The sweet radiance of [His] face, which has surpassed a host of autumn moons, conquers [the beauty of] young maidens.
On the spotlessly pure ears — new earrings adorning the cheeks, incomparable, studded with gems and pearls.
The lips, which shame the redness of the bimba fruit, are slightly parted — lovely, full of lustre;
the teeth appear, white with [their own] rays, beautiful, — the host of stars and the kunda flowers are left far behind.[^43]
On the broad chest are many necklaces; there [His] hands, joined palm to palm, shine without cease.
Narahari says: this cannot be fathomed, — it seems [He] is pleading for forgiveness beside a manini.[^44]

*Kumari-chhandah*

**2.19.** Today, at the close of night, there is no limit to the charm of [His] sleep:
as if awake — and yet not awake; bliss holds [Him] fast.
On the lovely bed is Gaura, abode of [golden] radiance;
[His] body, which shatters the pride of Manmatha, is smooth and incomparable.
Wondrous is this beauty, a delight to the eyes;
to see [it], the hearts of His own people are filled with great longing.
Knowing [the] hour, the beautiful flocks of birds are full of joy;
their hearts startled awake, giving up sloth, they sport, full of rasa.
Swans, lovely sarasa cranes — on the bank, by the celestial river;
birds (?) without fear [sit] on the pomegranate trees — flocks of parrots.[^45]
Hosts of cuckoos on the mango trees, many cocks — [all] are rapt;
on the fresh nipa trees, hundreds of thousands of peacocks.
All around, hundreds of rows of doves: there is no comparison for them;
flock upon flock, many khanjana wagtails in the lovely kunjas.
Thus, in many ways — who can describe [it]? There is no end [to it].
[All] display their gifts; Narahari proclaims [only] a small part.

*Mala-chhandah*

**2.20.** The residents of the town of Sri Navadvipa — people who are [embodied] heaps of happiness,
bees, beasts and birds, grass, trees, shrubs and the rest —
[their] hearts [are turned] to the beautiful deeds of Gaurachandra; [their] course is the highest prema.
[They] are wholly aprakrita (not of this world), though they look just like the prakrita (the mundane).[^46]

*Raga bhairava*

**2.14a.** Today, at the close of night, [this] hour is adorned with an assembly of joys.
Hosts of kinnaras [pour out] trills(?), flocks of parrots sing,
hosts of cuckoos sweetly and charmingly — [their] fifth note (panchama) reigns. *(Refrain)*[^47]
The fresh flowers have opened in the kunjas; there swarm upon swarm of bees
hum so charmingly, as if sweet instruments were playing.
Shadja, pairs [of voices], a lovely manner — [the peacocks] call out "dhi-dhi-kiti dhilanga";
the peacocks dance — seeing [them], the celestial dancing girls are ashamed.[^48]
The swans cry "Sadhu!" — hearing [it], the krauncha cranes lose their composure;
in the guise of sprouts there is a thrill of rapture: the earth, laden with creepers, does not let [it] go (?).[^49]
Wonderful! Intoxicated with that prema, many (hundreds of) rows of doves shine;
in the guise of the sound "ghu-ghu" [their] hunkara rumbles deep.
The wind, the robber of night's adornment, shakes the leaves with boundless delight;
in the guise of flowers it joyfully gives away corals and pearls.
On the grass fall drops of dew — as if tears of bliss were flowing.
Narahari says: [such is] the incomparable [dawn] in the town of Nadia on earth.[^50]

*Modaka-chhandah*

**2.15a.** At the close of night the lovely birds and the rest, in this way,
for the happiness of [their] Master, intoxicated with rasa, display their gifts.
Having woken, [He] is not awake: [still] He stays a little in sleep
on the lovely bed — Gaura, the thief of hearts.
There a skilful parrot, raised by the Lord's servant,
shines in a new cage — charming in nature, sedate.
With supreme joy in its heart, taking Narahari along,
it carefully wakes [the Lord], watching [His] play.[^51]

*Raga bhairava*

**2.16a.** Wake up, life of the world, new moon of the town of Nadia, O!
Auspicious King Madana, lovely in form [the colour] of gorochana,
full of rasa, overcome by rasa, ornament of the rasikas, root of rasa, O! *(Refrain)*[^52]
Most excellent beauty with teeth [like] kunda, with a lovely, soft, delight-giving face,
with lovely restless eyes — a snare for the eyes and hearts of people, O!
On the lovely chest a sweet garland, swaying; delight of women,
stealing [their] composure, [Yourself] the abode of composure, crushing Kama(?), O![^53]
Beauty-giving curling hair, incomparable charming dress;
in the lake of the devotees' hearts [You] rival a golden lotus, O!
Lion-necked, with pure ears, golden, marked with a tilaka of sandal,
wearing cloud-coloured cloth, king of dancers, delighting the host of loved ones, O!
Charming temple of jewel-like virtues, whose gait defeats the elephant, grateful one,
[You] whose feet, which break [the bonds of] worldly existence, are worshipped by the gods, O!
Narahari [says]: O Beloved, [here is] the word of [my] heart — what shall I say? nothing can be expressed, —
today, seeing You asleep, anxiety seizes me, O![^54]

*Again raga yatharaga*

**2.17a.** Leave Your bed, O Gaura, abode of virtues!
The moon has grown pale, the watches of the night have passed.
The eastern quarter, O friend, has forgotten everything
and in anuraga has robed herself in red.[^55]
The kumuda, the bee's dwelling, has closed;
[the bee] goes to the opened lotus.
The chakravaki rejoices together with her mate;
Narahari, seeing [this], laughs — so much fun!

*Again raga yatharaga*

**2.18a.** The night has gone, the moon's pride is far away;
in great sorrow the chakora pines.[^56]
Ashamed at heart over their husband's humiliation,
the stars have hidden in the forest of the sky.
The people of Nadia have already woken —
that is why I say: leave Your bed, Gora!
If You do not believe me,
then ask Narahari.

*Again raga yatharaga*

**2.19a.** Wake up, wake up, O Gora, [my] life,
thief of the hearts and eyes of the people of the world!
I do not know what has so absorbed You
that You sleep [even] at the hour of dawn.[^57]
Open Your eyes and see: the night is over,
all these neighbours have woken;
leaving sorrow behind, floating in an ocean of happiness,
they laugh [at You] in so many ways.
And I say more: in this town of Nadia
everyone praises [You] in all sorts of ways;
they come running to Your house —
does this not make You feel at all ashamed?
What strange laziness You have taken on:
morning comes, and You cannot get up!
Tell me, then, what work do You do at night?
You are so accomplished — why are You like this?
The peacock and peahen stay apart,
neither comes near the other;
dispirited, they sit in the trees:
if You do not look [at them], they do not dance.
The bee and his mate in the lovely kunja
have forgotten to settle on the clusters of flowers;
"To whom shall we sing?" they say, and do not hum,
but wander through the forest as if in despair.
The chakora, [seeing] in the moon of the sky a likeness of the moon of Your face,
had become attached to the moon of the heavens;
[now] it has grown pale — and, fallen into this confusion,
[the chakora] weeps in great sorrow: "What will become of me?"[^58]
Make it happy, O heap of happiness:
get up, come out into the courtyard and stand there,
or else my heart is heavy:
the blame of Narahari will not wash away.[^59]

*Again raga yatharaga*

**2.20a.** Wake up, wake up, O moon-Gaura! How long will You sleep — the night is over.
Leaving their homes, Your associates have quickly come and surrounded the courtyard.
I see no one like all of them: [they are] like chakora birds without the moon.
So quickly leave Your bed, show Yourself [to them] and satisfy [their] thirsting eyes.
What shall I say? [O You whose] deeds are lovely — in vain(?) [they] sit here in silence.
Let [them] hear even once the words of [Your] moon-like lips, and erase the line [of grief] from [their] hearts.
With restless minds [they] look all around; I have understood: [they] are utterly exhausted.
Embrace [them] and make [their] bodies stronger(?), O Lord of Narahari's life![^60]

*Again raga yatharaga*

**2.21a.** The night is over — the men and women, one and all, have come back to life (lit. "have found life").
Thinking of the host of Your lovely deeds, no one has a mind for household work.
In great haste they have all come to You to be satisfied [with the sight of You] —
and You do not wake: this is very hard. I do not know what happiness there is in [Your] sleep!
All the brahmanas of Nadia, about their own business, have hurried to the bank of the Suradhuni;
they laugh among themselves: "Look, Nimai Pandita is [still] lying there!"
So I say: leave Your bed, perform the morning rites, O Gora, jewel of virtues!
Otherwise everyone will sing of Your disrepute — and Narahari, hearing it, will be ashamed.[^61]

*Again raga yatharaga*

**2.22a.** Get up, get up! What a wondrous sleep You are sleeping today, O Clever One!
Never have I seen such a habit in You — how much longer must I reason with You?
At such an hour, so much laziness — what happiness [is in it]? People laugh at Your doings.
Were [things] as before, now there would be no need to wake [You] — [You would already] be running away out of shame.
In the same way Your Gadadhara, Narahari and all the others lie [asleep].
They have nothing to fear — so that is well; otherwise they would have run off, leaving You behind.[^62]
What shall I say? You lie with Your dear ones — what is lost by that?
[But] if it grows very late, afterwards You will not be able to blame Narahari for anything.[^63]

*Again raga yatharaga*

**2.23a.** Wake up, thief of people's hearts, best of the clever, beautiful One who sports in the town of Nadia!
Radha is the crest-jewel of women, full of rasa: You are the delight of the treasure of Her heart.
What shall I say? Again and again [I tell You]: the night has turned to dawn.
What laziness is this? I understand nothing — many doubts [remain] in my heart. *(Refrain)*[^64]
When You hear of the lovely deeds and virtues [of the people] of Vraja, neither food nor sleep pleases You;
while telling [of them], You do not distinguish(?) day from night — so how can sleep please You now?
The matchless sankirtana, the root of happiness, You always hold dearer than life;
without it a moment is for You like a kalpa. At this, Narahari's mind is bewildered.[^65]

*Manjumukhi-chhandah*

**2.24a.** The very skilful parrot spoke much in this way.
Gaura in His sleep heard a little, intoxicated with rasa.
Seeing that the bed was grieved, He who gives joy to all
stretched a little — and remained [lying]: He cannot leave [it].[^66]

*Raga yatharaga*

**2.25a.** Languid and limp, the Lord, crest-jewel of rasikas, speaks, as if in a dream, words full of rasa:
"Bereft of the rasa of seeing Radha-ramana, [My] life is burning, burning away in fever.[^67]
Listen, Gauri, Haridasa, Dhananjaya, Sanjaya, Vijaya, Mukunda, Murari,
Madhava, Vasudeva, Purushottama, Shridhara, Krishnadasa who gives joy,
Shrinidhi, Madhusudana, Vakreshvara, Satyaraja, wise Kavichandra,
Shankara, Garuda, Bhagavata, Nandana, Chandrashekhara, profound Saranga,
Shuklambara, Yadunatha, Nakula, Vanamali, Mahesha, Shrinidhi — abode of virtues![^68]
Providence, most kind, understanding My heart, has given Me unceasing company with all of you.
Therefore, accepting My humble request — that [narrative] full of the rasa of the lovely deeds of the people of Vraja —
sing it with brilliance in sweet ragas, and remove this cruel burning of [My] heart.[^69]
How much more shall I reveal what is hidden in My heart? These mighty wicked enemies have made [Me] their subject.
I took this body in vain, I have understood nothing; I have become [one wandering] without a path, [poor] in the fullness of prema(?).[^70]
Again, joining My palms, I say: all of you together, gladly fulfil the heart's desire of your own [dear one]:
birth after birth, without obstruction, may I be in this world a servant of the lotus feet of the Lord of the gopis."[^71]
Having spoken such words, the king of the twice-born, sinking again a little into sleep, fell silent.
Narahari is bewildered: he cannot in the least describe [these] matchless lovely deeds, rare even for the gods.[^72]

*Chatushpadi-chhandah*

**2.26a.** Who can tell [this] lovely lila to its end?
The youthful Gaura remained asleep in [His] house.
Narahari constantly longs:
"When shall I behold the play of the close of night?"

*Thus [ends] the second kirana of the Sri Sri Gaura-charita-chintamani, called "The Description of the Shayana-vilasa", in the description of the deeds of the nishanta period.*[^73]

[^1]: The title of the kirana is taken from its colophon (at the end of the kirana, after 2.26): "…niśānta-kālīya-caritra-varṇane śayana-vilāsa-varṇanaṁ nāma dvitīyaḥ kiraṇaḥ" — "the second kirana, called 'The Description of the Shayana-vilasa', in the description of the deeds of the nishanta period". Shayana-vilasa — the pastime in which the Lord reposes on His bed. Nishanta (lit. "the close of night") — the first of the eight periods of the ashtakaliya-lila, the last hours of the night before dawn (cf. 1.59, 1.61). The subheading "Now — in the description of the deeds of the eight periods — first of all, nishanta" stands in the edition directly after the colophon of kirana 1.
[^2]: "Full" — the edition has *pūrā se*, in quotation marks (a doubtful place); the edition's variant at the foot of the page is *suvāsa* ("fragrant"). "Delighter" — *rañjaka*; "rays" — *kara* (also "hands"): the moon wiped away the gloom with his ray-hands.
[^3]: Some associates spend the night by the Lord's house; others, by His command, stay in their own homes — and it is they whom separation torments.
[^4]: Yatharaga ("to a suitable raga") — a marking of the edition: no raga is specified.
