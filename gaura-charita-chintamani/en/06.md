# Kirana 6. The Deeds of the Morning: Description of the Excellence of the Prema of [the Women] Endowed with Parental Love[^1]

*Morning in Shachi's courtyard: the elderly women of Nadia, who love Gaura with a mother's love — Sita Thakurani, the wife of Srivasa and others — gaze at Him, bless Him and pray to the gods for His welfare; Shachi asks Sita to bless Nimai and Nitai*

*Again, in the morning period — the conduct and so on of [the women] endowed with supreme affection.*

*Raga vibhasha*

**6.1.** Today at an auspicious moment the night has passed; the people of Nadia are immersed in bliss.
To see Gaurangachandra with affection, all come running to Shachi's house.
In the midst of the courtyard shines Gora — the thief of the minds and eyes of the people of the world.
The associates adorn every side — the moon amid the stars: what [else] can compare?
No one has any memory [of anything] in mind; everyone's eyes are on that face.
What can Narahari tell with a single mouth? All rejoice, each in his own rasa.[^2]

*Raga yatharaga*

**6.2.** Advaita's wife, Sita Thakurani — a very heap of rasa —
with unblinking eyes beholds the beautiful smile on Gaura's face.
Thinking and thinking of those new deeds, she has become like a doll;
she cannot keep her composure — streams [of tears] flow from both eyes.
How many, many thoughts arise in her heart! Her mind is distraught with affection.
With care she gives Shachi instructions [on how to protect Him];
giving endless blessings, she praises [Him] — there is no limit to [her] happiness.
Says Narahari: who has the power to understand these deeds?[^3]

*Raga vibhasha*

**6.3.** The wife of Srivasa, most virtuous, is like the very embodiment of affection,
ever modest, chaste, devoted to her husband — there is none like her in the world.
Her body blossoming [with joy], matchless, covering half her face with the edge of her cloth,
standing beside Sita she gazes intently [at Gaura] in the joy of her heart.
In the midst of the courtyard, surrounded by dear associates, sits Gora;
the beautiful moon of [His] face sparkles, [His] body is like gold.
Ever new and new — what can one say? Malini(?) floats in that ocean of beauty.
Wondrous prema! Narahari Dasa would take all [her] misfortunes upon himself.[^4]

*Raga yatharaga*

**6.4.** At the dawn of night there is no end to the joy in Shachidevi's heart;
beholding that face she cannot restrain herself — tears flow from her eyes.
Taking hold of Sita's feet, with care she speaks sweet words:
"You alone are [my] support, O [lady]; I know neither good nor bad.
Regarding Nimaichanda as your own, be ever gracious [to Him];
give the blessing that He may live long and happily.
I have no one [else] — how much more shall I entreat? This child is the star of my eyes.
Make it so that He always stays at home, that [His] restless ways may cease.
And I say more: this Nitai is my Vishvarupa, [my] life and soul;
grant this boon — that [they] may not be parted even for half a moment."
Having said many such things, she quickly performs the customary auspicious rites.
What can Narahari tell with a single mouth of the incomparable love of the mother?[^5]

*Raga yatharaga*

**6.5.** Shachi's house has become radiant — what can one say of [that] joy?
The elderly women, glad at heart, stand there and watch.
Someone says: "O, the lamp of the family, Nimai, a heap of virtues,
has appeared [here], coming to make our eyes fruitful!"
Someone says: "O son of Shachi, ever remain in well-being!
All my merit I have given to Him: may He escape many thorns."
Someone says: "For His sake I will worship the King of Kailasa,
that He, long-lived, may thus remain in Nadia."
Someone says: "Worshipping Ganga daily, I will ask for a boon:
may Shachi's darling, with His own people, joyfully keep house."
Someone says: "Worshipping Chandi, I will ask for what is in my heart:
that He may never go to foreign lands to earn wealth."
Someone says: "I worship Lakshmi — and there is a reason for it:
that He may effortlessly become very rich and never know want."
Someone says: "Listen further — I do not understand the ways of my own mind:
a love a hundred times greater than for my own son arises for Him."
Someone says: "Leaving my house, I come for His sake;
I do not know how [my] heart [aches] when I leave Him even for a moment."
Someone says: "Shachi is fortunate — she has done many pious deeds;
that is why she obtained this moon of Nadia, the treasure of everyone's life."
Someone says: "Let them say what they will(?) — of the Creator I ask just this:
birth after birth may I see this boy in Nadia(?)."
Thus, in the rapture of prema, they speak without end.
Says Narahari: what power have I to tell of the affection of them all?[^6]

*Raga yatharaga*

**6.6.** What joy today in the house of Shri Shachi at dawn, when the night has ended!
Amid His dear associates Vishvambhara shines, beautiful in His bearing.
To each, in whatever mood he has, in that mood [He] appears, making everyone happy.
Enchanter of the world, jewel of virtues, so accomplished — never [have we] seen [such a One]!
All the old men and women are deeply stirred by overflowing tenderness:
gazing and gazing at that moon of a face, no one can keep composure.
Tears flow from the eyes unceasingly, in the heart is the highest joy;
and again [they] tell one another of the deeds of Gauranga, the life of Narahari.[^7]

*A certain [woman] says — yatharaga*

**6.7.** Listen, listen, dear friend, I tell you: blessed is this town of Nadiya!
In the Treta-yuga — Kaushalya-devi, in the Dvapara-yuga — Yashoda, in the Kali-yuga — fortunate Shachi.
Blessed is Jagannatha Mishra, renowned in the world — there is no limit to his piety;
and this wife of his, devoted to her husband, full of tenderness — she has such a son, Nimai.
She is like the mother of the universe, I tell you: who else has such a nature?
Who [else] could bear so much childish mischief? There is no one in the world to compare with her.
I do not know which god showed mercy — that is why He has now become good;
otherwise Nimai, the life of Narahari, was a terrible madcap.[^8]

*And another — yatharaga*

**6.8.** I will tell you about Nimaichand: Nimai is the crown of all madcaps!
Such a stubborn child I have never seen anywhere; blessed indeed are [His] father and mother!
On the full-moon day of the month of Phalguna, at the time of the eclipse, having been born, He cried bitterly;
however hard they tried, the infant would not take the breast — seeing this, everyone was afraid.
The great ascetic Gosai who lives in Shantipura knows that boy's ways:
I do not know by what device He made [Him] take the breast — and everyone's heart grew calm.
Let anyone say what they like — there is no fear in my heart; I have reasoned it out in my mind:
the treasure of Narahari's life will be a great madcap — it began with His very birth![^9]

*And another — yatharaga*

**6.9.** My Nimai, [my] life, is a great madcap indeed! One day I saw with my own eyes:
His body grey with dust — how wondrous! — He crawls on all fours about the courtyard;
on His lovely moon-face a smile; He calls "Mother!" — at once Shachi came running.
Climbing onto [her] lap, He weeps until He is exhausted — seeing this, the heart seems to break.
However much [she] tries, [He] will not be consoled; again and again He flings His body [to the ground].
"What has happened, what has happened?" weeps the pious [mother]; no one can stay calm.
At that very moment a woman, in great distress, clapping her hands, says "Hari!"
Hearing this, the restless infant stops crying — and laughs, clasping her round the neck.
Everyone in joy says "Hari, Hari!"; Nimai, getting down from the lap,
cannot yet stand — and still He dances merrily, holding His mother's hand.
Why He cried — no one could understand; everyone ponders it inwardly.
Nimai, the life of Narahari, knows well how to play the madcap like this![^10]

*And another — yatharaga*

**6.10.** The restless madcap Nimai heeds nothing! Listen to [what happened] one day.
Holding the edge of His mother's sari, He walks about the courtyard and sees His shadow there;
letting go of the sari's edge, He plays with the shadow; and there was a snake —
on its fearsome hood He lay down — and in what bliss, I cannot say!
Crying "Alas, alas!", everyone comes running; the serpent tries to slip away — [He] catches it again.
Everyone trembles; Shachi, distraught, carefully takes [Him] up in her arms.
At that very moment a bird flew past; seeing how it flew,
"Catch it and give it to Me!" He says again and again, and weeps, clasping His mother's neck.
From both eyes — streams [of tears] like a sapphire necklace; the line of kajal was washed away;
that moon-face grew dim — can one bear to see it with one's eyes?
Whatever anyone says — no one's words can distract [Him]; at His crying the heart breaks.
The infant, the life of Narahari, forgot by Himself — only then did [our] hearts grow calm.[^11]

*And another — yatharaga*

**6.11.** My golden Nimai is the doll of my heart: are there such games anywhere in the world?
Whatever He asks for — if it is not given, there is trouble: no one can console [Him].
One day Nimai, crying "Give Me butter!", weeps, clutching the edge of His mother's sari;
the more they console Him, the more He rolls in the dust — not for a moment does He restrain Himself.
I do not know where from, but the mother brought fresh butter and put it in Nimai's hand;
having eaten the butter, He says: "I am a cowherd's son! Nothing but this is to My taste."
Looking into [His] face, we laughed and asked: "Whose cowherd boy are You, then?"
Hearing this, the infant, the life of Narahari, ran away; embarrassed, Shachi says: "Well, well!"[^12]

*And another — yatharaga*

**6.12.** Listen to [what happened] with Nimai. One day Mother Shachi, gladly taking various offerings,
was worshipping the gods with care — so that restless Nimai would become good.
At that very moment, coming from who knows where, He ate the prepared food (naivedya) unseen
and, laughing, says: "I am the God of gods! Why do you not worship Me?"
Crying "Alas, alas!", Shachi chases [Him], great fear in [her] heart;
distraught, she thinks: "May nothing happen to Nimai!"
Meanwhile the child, seated on Mishra's lap, says: "Mother does not give Me anything to eat!"
Hearing the words of Nimai, the life of Narahari, the father's heart is filled with great joy.[^13]

*And another — yatharaga*

**6.13.** This Nimaichand of mine, if He wants to eat and [the food comes] even a moment late,
in great anger breaks the house and the doors, causes great damage.
If anyone says anything — He [makes mischief] twice as much; He fears neither father nor mother;
none of the neighbours can restrain Him — He fears Vishvarupa alone.
Such a boy I have never seen anywhere: He roams Nadiya on His own,
slips unnoticed into anyone's house and, in play, does all sorts of things;
everywhere He makes the children cry — what is there to say of their mothers!
Who in Nadiya is not afraid of the pranks of Vishvambhara, the life of Narahari?[^14]

*And another — yatharaga*

**6.14.** How much shall I tell of these deeds of Nimaichand! At bathing time on the bank of the Suradhuni
no one — neither woman nor man — can stay at peace: such great mischief He makes there!
Whoever comes carefully carrying various offerings to worship the gods —
there is no counting His quarrels with them; what does He not do in Nadiya!
And if anyone ever complains to Shachi and Mishra — how well-behaved He is then!
Great wonder grows in everyone's heart: seeing [this], the mind is lost.
As were the pranks of Kanu in the house of Nanda — such we see in the house of Shachi;
Nimai, the life of Narahari, surely is that very One; otherwise who would act like this?[^15]

*And another — yatharaga*

**6.15.** The story of Nimaichand is wondrous indeed: now the family deity has become gracious —
where have all those restless ways [of His] gone? Who [now] is as steady [as He]?
The people of Nadiya and all the panditas — who does not regard [Him] with respect?
Srivasa, Murari and the others — all the Vaishnavas — none can bear to leave His company.
And my Nitai loves [Him] like His own life — as Balai [loved] Krishna;
surely that very [love] has appeared here: such I have never seen anywhere.
Blessed in the world is pious Shachi: surely she is that same Queen of Vraja;
Nimai and Nitai are the two pupils of [her] eyes. Narahari offers himself in nirmanchhana to this prema.[^16]

*Raga yatharaga*

**6.16.** All the elderly women of Nadiya speak thus with one another.
How wondrous is the way of them all! What can [they] be compared to? — they are so full of tenderness.
Gazing and gazing at the moon of Gaurangachandra's face, they consider themselves blessed.
How many blessings they give again and again! Narahari, hearing [this], swims in that happiness.[^17]

*Thus [ends] the sixth kirana of the Shrimad Gaura-charita-chintamani, called "The Description of the Excellence of the Prema of [the Women] Endowed with Parental Love", in the description of the deeds of the morning period.*[^18]

[^1]: The name of the kirana is taken from its colophon at the end of the kirana (after 6.16, pdf 71, printed p. 66; read from the scan): "…pratah-kaliya-charitra-varnane vatsalyavatinam premotkarsha-varnanam nama shashthah kiranah" — "the sixth kirana, called 'The Description of the Excellence of the Prema of [the Women] Endowed with Vatsalya (Parental Love)', in the description of the deeds of the morning period". The subheading "Again, in the morning period — the conduct and so on of [the women] endowed with supreme affection" (*punaḥ prātaḥkāle paramasnehavatīnāṁ ceṣṭādikaṁ*) stands in the edition immediately after the colophon of kirana 5 (pdf 64, printed p. 59). Kirana 6 comprises verses 6.1–6.16 (pdf 64–71). It speaks of elderly women who love Gaura as a mother loves her child; this is not the mood of the "nagaris" (which begins from kirana 7 on).
[^2]: Vibhasha is a morning raga (cf. kirana 5). "Thief of the minds and eyes" — *mana-nayana-corā*. "The moon amid the stars" — *uḍu-madhi bidhu*. "With a single mouth" — cf. 4.3. Signature: Narahari.
[^3]: Sita Thakurani is the wife of Advaita Acharya; according to the *Chaitanya-charitamrita* (Adi 13), she came with gifts to the newborn Nimai and blessed Him. "A very heap of rasa" — *kebala rasera rāśi*. "New deeds" — *nava carita*: Gaura's youthful pastimes. "Like a doll" — *putula pārā* (the letters run together in print; the reading *putula* is probable): she stood motionless. "Gives Shachi instructions" — *upadeśa se-rūpa śacīra prati*: probably on how to protect the child. Signature: Narahari.
[^4]: The wife of Srivasa Pandita is Malini (cf. 1.13, note 30 of kirana 1). "Covering half her face with the edge of her cloth" — *ādha basana jhā̃piyā mukhe*: a sign of a married woman's modesty. In the seventh line the edition prints *myālānī* with the editor's own mark "(?)"; probably a corruption of *mālinī* — the name Malini; the translation is conjectural. "Ever new and new — what can one say?" — *naba naba saba ki kaba*. "Would take all [her] misfortunes upon himself" — *bālāi laiyā maru*, a Bengali expression of tender love (cf. note 5 of kirana 5). Signature: Narahari Dasa.
[^5]: Shachidevi is Gaura's mother. "You alone are [my] support" — Shachi addresses Sita with the respectful plural (*tomādera*, lit. "your [pl.]"). "The star of my eyes" — *ā̃khira tārā*. "Restless ways" — *cañcala dhārā*. Vishvarupa is Nimai's elder brother, who left home early and took sannyasa; Shachi fears that Nimai too will leave, and in Nityananda (Nitai) she sees her Vishvarupa. "Performs the customary auspicious rites" — *karaye maṅgala nīta* (*nīta* < *nīti*, "custom"). Signature: Narahari.
[^6]: "The lamp of the family" — *kulera pradīpa*. "All my merit I have given to Him" — *mora puṇya yata dilāma ihāre* (in print *dilāna*). "Thorns" — *kaṇṭaka*: dangers and misfortunes. The King of Kailasa — Shiva; Chandi — the goddess Durga; Lakshmi — the goddess of wealth; Ganga — the sacred river, worshipped as a goddess. On the elders' prayers to the gods for Gaura's welfare as a manifestation of vatsalya-prema, see note 8 of kirana 5. "With His own people joyfully keep house" — *nija-jane laiyā … ānande karuka ghara*. "What is in my heart" — *manete ye āche mena*: the last word is printed *mena*, probably for *hena* ("such"). "Never go to foreign lands to earn wealth" — later Nimai did in fact travel to East Bengal (*Chaitanya-bhagavata*, Adi 14). "Never know want" — *kabhu nā ṭhekibe bhāra*, lit. "never run up against a burden". Reading and sense unclear: *se bala se bala* — taken as "let [people] say what they will"; *nadyāya(?)* (print unclear; possibly another word) — taken as "in Nadia". Verse 6.5 begins on pdf 65 and ends on pdf 66 (printed p. 61); it is translated in full. Signature: Narahari.
[^7]: Yatharaga — "a raga of choice". Vishvambhara is a name of Gaura. "Each one, in whatever mood…" — *yāra yei bhāva se bhāve bhābita*: understood as "to each, in whatever mood he is, [the Lord] appears in that mood"; possibly also "each contemplates [Him] in his own mood". "Accomplished" — *sughaṛa*. "Old men and women" — *bṛddha naranārī*: the elderly people of Nadiya; further on (6.7–6.15) the elderly women speak, full of parental love (vatsalya) for Nimai. Signature: Narahari ("Gauranga, the life of Narahari").
[^8]: The headings of 6.7–6.15 are Sanskrit notes: *kācid āha* ("a certain [woman] says", 6.7) and *anyā ca* ("and another", 6.8–6.15; in 6.12 the print looks like *antāca*; cf. note 3 of kirana 5); the ragas — *yathārāga*. The particle *go* in the middle of each line is an address to a female friend; as a rule it is not rendered. Kaushalya is the mother of Rama (Treta-yuga); Yashoda is the mother of Krishna (Dvapara-yuga). The end of the second line is cut off by the edge of the scan: it reads *bhāgyaba…*, understood as *bhāgyabatī* ("fortunate"); the end of the seventh line — *bhāla* ("good") — is also at the edge. "Has become good" — i.e., Nimai, having grown, has calmed down. "Madcap" — *khepā* (lit. "wild, crazed", said fondly of a mischievous child). Signature: Narahari.
[^9]: "Stubborn child" — *ā̃khuṭi* (a capricious, wilful child). "Blessed indeed" — *dhanya yena*: the reading *yena* is uncertain (faint print). Gaura appeared on the full moon of the month of Phalguna during a lunar eclipse (*Chaitanya-charitamrita*, Adi 13; *Chaitanya-bhagavata*, Adi-khanda). The ascetic Gosai of Shantipura is Advaita Acharya (cf. 5.2). "Let anyone say what they like" — the speaker is not afraid to say that she sees something extraordinary in Nimai. *mu* — "I". Signature: Narahari.
[^10]: "Crawls on all fours" — *hāmāguṛi*. "Flings His body [to the ground]" — *aṅga āchāṛaye*. The pious one (*puṇyabatī*) is Shachi. The crying infant Nimai would quiet down only on hearing the name of Hari — a well-known episode of His infancy (*Chaitanya-bhagavata*, Adi-khanda; *Chaitanya-charitamrita*, Adi-lila). "Play the madcap" — *khepāmi*. Signature: Narahari.
[^11]: The episode with the snake — cf. *Chaitanya-bhagavata*, Adi-khanda (the infant Nimai plays with a snake in the courtyard; according to the CB, it was Ananta-Shesha, who had come to serve the Lord). "Tries to slip away — [He] catches it again" — *palāite nāga punaḥ dhare*. "How it flew" — *kibā se bhaṅgite*. "Like a sapphire necklace" — *nīlamaṇi hāra-pārā*: tears darkened by kajal (eye collyrium). "Can one bear to see it with one's eyes?" — *tāhā kiye ā̃khe yāya dekhā*, i.e., it is unbearable to see. "Forgot by Himself" — *āpani bhulila*: He was distracted, forgetting the bird. Signature: Narahari.
[^12]: "The doll of my heart" — *parāṇa putuli*. "Edge of the sari" — *ācara* (*ā̃cala*). "I am a cowherd's son" (*mu' gopa-tanaya*) — Nimai unwittingly reveals that He is Krishna, the son of the cowherd Nanda, who loved butter. "Nothing … is to My taste" — *kichu nāi bhāya*. "Cowherd boy" — *chāoyāla* (child, son). The last line is ambiguous: *lāje* ("from embarrassment") may refer either to Nimai ("ran away in embarrassment") or to Shachi; "Well, well!" — *bhāla bhāla* (lit. "good, good"). Signature: Narahari.
[^13]: "Listen" — the print has *śunaye* (probably for *śunaha*). Naivedya — food prepared for offering to the Deity. "Chases" — *dābāṛiyā yāya*. "May nothing happen" — Shachi fears the gods will be angry with the child for eating the offering. Mishra is Jagannatha Mishra, Nimai's father. Similar childhood episodes are in the *Chaitanya-bhagavata* (Adi-khanda). Signature: Narahari.
[^14]: The third line has no end mark in the edition; *go* occurs in it twice. Vishvarupa is Nimai's elder brother. "Anyone's" — the print has *yāba tāra*, understood as *yāra tāra*. "In play" — *helāte*. "What is there to say of their mothers" — *ki baliba tā sabāra māya*: understood thus (possibly "what can one say to [their] mothers?"). Signature: Narahari.
[^15]: The Suradhuni is the Ganga. Nimai's mischief at the bathing ghats of the Ganga — with the bathers and with the girls who came to worship the gods — and His innocent look when His parents came to punish Him are described in the *Chaitanya-bhagavata* (Adi-khanda). "The mind is lost" — *nā rahe buddhi gati*. Kanu is Krishna; the pranks (*dhāmāli*) in the house of Nanda are Krishna's childhood play in Vraja. Signature: Narahari.
[^16]: "The family deity has become gracious" — *ebe e prasanna kuladebā*: now, by the grace of the family deity, Nimai has grown up and become steady (cf. 6.7). "With respect" — *samiha* (*samīha*). Srivasa Pandita and Murari Gupta are associates of the Lord in Navadvipa. Nitai is Nityananda; Balai is Balarama, Krishna's elder brother (cf. note 11 of kirana 4). The Queen of Vraja (*brajeśvarī*) is Yashoda. Nirmanchhana — see note 3 of kirana 3. Signature: Narahari. The stanza begins on pdf 70 and ends on pdf 71; next on pdf 71 come stanza 6.16 and the colophon of kirana 6.
[^17]: The verse is in couplets with a caesura in the middle of each line (in the edition the halves of the lines are divided by "।"); the heading is *yathārāga*. "Speak thus with one another" sums up the speeches of the elderly women in 6.7–6.15. "So full of tenderness" — *ati snehavatī*. "Swims in that happiness" — *se sukhe sā̃tāre*. Signature: Narahari.
[^18]: The colophon was read from the scan (pdf 71, printed p. 66): "ইতি শ্রীমদ্গৌর-চরিত্র-চিন্তামণৌ প্রাতঃকালীয়-চরিত্র-বর্ণনে বাৎসল্যবতীনাং প্রেমোৎকর্ষ-বর্ণনং নাম ষষ্ঠঃ কিরণঃ ॥৬॥" (the OCR gives *baṣṭhaḥ*; the scan clearly has *ṣaṣṭhaḥ*). Kirana 7 begins on the same page immediately after the colophon.
