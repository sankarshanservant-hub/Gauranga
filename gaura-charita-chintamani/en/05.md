# Kirana 5. The Deeds of the Morning: Description of the Love of the Elders and Other Matters[^1]

*Morning in Navadvipa: the elderly men and women of Nadia, who love Gaura as their own child, speak of Him to one another in secret, come leaning on their sticks to Shachi's courtyard, pray to the gods for His welfare, and, having drunk in the beauty of His face, become young again, as it were*

*Again, in the morning period — the excellence of the prema of the elders.*

*Raga vibhasha*

**5.1.** Ah, how wondrous! Who does not pine for the deeds of Gaurangachandra?
The people of Nadia, not seeing [Him] at night, cannot keep life [in their bodies].
Asleep, in their dreams they know nothing else — they regard Gora as their all;
at the dawn of night, crying "Gora, Gora!", they wake, immersed in that rasa.
All the elders, men and women — none can be compared with them:
with what care someone must have fashioned [their] nature and charming conduct!
Narahari offers himself in nirmanchhana to those elder kinsfolk of the Lord:
overflowing with love for Gora, they speak [to one another] in very sweet words.[^2]

*Someone says — vibhasha*

**5.2.** "Listen, O virtuous one! O wise one, quite in private I shall tell [you] a secret:
it seems the son of the lord of Vraja, [who was] in Vraja, has appeared here.
In the town of Nadia there is no one who does not pine for His qualities;
Srivasa, Murari and the rest — none of them can live without seeing [Him].
The ascetic Advaita, a resident of Shantipura, stays here constantly;
how sweet [His] qualities are — with what eagerness He tells [of them] to everyone!
Ah, how wondrous! Will there ever be another such marvellous boy?
[Says] Narahari: make Gorachanda, [your] all, the necklace about your neck!"[^3]

*And someone else — vibhasha*

**5.3.** "Listen, O virtuous one! Not in vain have I lived in Nadia:[^4]
in this old age the Creator is merciful [to me] — there is no end to [my] happiness.
To my fill, with these two eyes, I have beheld Nimai the moon, [son] of Shachi;
not seeing Him for half a moment, [my] heart weeps bitterly all the time.
Let me take all His misfortunes upon myself! I have never seen or heard of another like [Him].
Worshipping the gods with various rites, let us make vows for His welfare;
with great care let us give away wealth, and through astrologers perform worship of the planets.
Go with Narahari, perform auspicious rites in Shachi's house!"[^5]

*Raga yatharaga*

**5.4.** "Listen to my words: I do not know what will happen — I have grown utterly old.
Nimai is the treasure of our life, our all, the comfort of the heart.
O, I always long to see [Him]!
I have no strength to walk — that is [my] grief: the Creator has become [my] enemy. *(Refrain)*
Worship the gods, give gifts to the twice-born, always think of [His] welfare(?);
with care send various presents — whatever pleases Him.
Go with Narahari and teach Shachi the auspicious rites:
let her, adjuring [Him], break Nimai of His terrible obstinacy."[^6]

*Raga yatharaga*

**5.5.** [Their] bodies overflow with love for Gaura; the elders are joyful at the dawn of night.
Each told his own kinsfolk [about Him] in detail; hearing all this, [they] rejoice without end.
Unable to remain without seeing Gaura, [they] left their beds, summoned all their strength,
took sticks in hand — [their] limbs trembling. Narahari beholds [this] matchless play.[^7]

*Vibhasha*

**5.6.** At the dawn of night, leaving each his own house, the venerable old men
all came to the lovely courtyard of Shachi; how many, many delights arise in [their] minds!
They stand leaning on the sticks in their hands and again and again gaze at Gaura's body —
as if after many long days, with great effort, they had found a jewel [and pressed it] to their breast.
Someone, wholly overcome by affection, says to the Creator: "Fulfil all [my] wishes:
may His life be longer than mine; may the Beautiful One always remain in this town of Nadia!"
Someone, with folded hands, says to Vishnu: "Cast Your [gracious] glance on the son of Mishra:
may He be a stranger to no one, may all love [Him], may [people] ceaselessly praise [His] virtues!"
Someone says to the Lord of Kailasa: "Increase the radiance of each of [His] limbs;
may [His] fame fill the world, may there be prosperity; drive away the clouds of invincible misfortune!"
Someone says to Sarasvati: "Make [Him] a pandita, so that He may never know defeat!"
Someone says to Bhagavati: "May [all] ever behold Nimai — the life of Narahari!"[^8]

*Rasika-chhandah*

**5.7.** Thus all the elders, absorbed [in prema], give [Him] blessings again and again.
Who could count [these] many delights? What can Narahari say of [that] beauty?[^9]

*Raga yatharaga*

**5.8.** The old men gaze again and again at Gaura's face — abode of happiness, full of nectar;
deeming their many, many births to have borne fruit, they offer their bodies in nirmanchhana times without number.
Tears tremble and brim in their eyes; their bodies are adorned with abundant horripilation;
leaning his hand on another's hand, someone softly speaks sweet words:
"Tell me, who fashioned the son of Mishra? He has stolen my ears, mind and eyes!
Not seeing [Him] for a moment seems a kalpa; [it] makes me restless — not a drop of composure remains.
To be with Him always, to wander [with Him], is great happiness; for this everyone mocks [me], calling me mad.
When all those words reach [my] ears, my mind finds still greater delight.
Eating, walking, sleeping, speaking, the order [of things] — I remember nothing; everything has turned upside down.
The household routine seems sheer deceit(?); I have no love [even] for my own son."
Having spoken such words he falls silent, absorbed, and again in his heart cherishes various wishes.
[He] overflows with supreme affection — what can Narahari Dasa, crest-jewel of fools, say [of it]?[^10]

*Rupamala-chhandah*

**5.9.** Having drunk the nectar of the face of the moon Gaura, their bodies thrilling,
the elders became as if sixteen years old:
the sticks stay far away, strength abounds, their gait is drunk with joy.
Dasa Narahari, beholding [this] play, is filled with great bliss.[^11]

*Thus [ends] the fifth kirana of the Shrimad Gaura-charita-chintamani, called "The Description of the Love of the Elders and Other Matters", in the description of the deeds of the morning period.*[^12]

[^1]: The name of the kirana is taken from its colophon at the end of the kirana (after 5.9, pdf 64, printed p. 59; read from the scan): "…pratah-kaliya-charitra-varnane vriddhanam snehadi-varnanam nama panchamah kiranah" — "the fifth kirana, called 'The Description of the Love (Sneha) of the Elders and Other Matters', in the description of the deeds of the morning period". The subheading "Again, in the morning period — the excellence of the prema of the elders" (*punaḥ prātaḥkāle vṛddhagaṇānāṁ premotkarṣaḥ*) stands in the edition at the top of pdf 61 (printed p. 56), immediately after the colophon of kirana 4 (pdf 60). Kirana 5 comprises verses 5.1–5.9 (pdf 61–64).
[^2]: Vibhasha is a morning raga (cf. note 9 of kirana 3). Nadia (*nadīyā*) — Navadvipa. *āhā mari mari* is an exclamation of wonder ("ah, how wondrous!"), not a literal "I am dying". "Cannot keep life" — *parāṇa dharite nāre*. "All the elders, men and women" — *vṛddha vṛddha yata puruṣa prakṛti*. Lines 7–8 are taken thus: *narahari pãhu nichani se saba vṛddha parijana pāśe* — "Narahari [offers himself in] nirmanchhana to those elder kinsfolk of the Lord" (*pãhu … parijana*); *pāśe* ("beside, near") may also belong to the next line: "beside those elders [someone] says…". Nirmanchhana — see note 3 of kirana 3. Signature: Narahari.
[^3]: "Someone says" — *kopyāha* (Skt. *ko 'pi āha*). The address "O virtuous one" (*sati*), "O wise one" (*sumati*) is to a woman; probably an old man is speaking in secret to his wife (cf. 5.5: "each told his own kinsfolk in detail"; in 5.4 he cannot walk and asks her to go to Shachi). The sex of the speakers in 5.2–5.4 is not entirely clear from the text. "The son of the lord of Vraja" — Krishna, son of Nanda: the elder guesses that Gaura is Krishna Himself. Srivasa (Pandita) and Murari (Gupta) are the Lord's associates in Navadvipa. Advaita lived in Shantipura but often stayed in Navadvipa. "Make [Him] the necklace about your neck" — i.e., keep Him always with you, in your heart. Signature: Narahari.
[^4]: "And someone else [says]" — *kopi ca*. The first line is printed thus: *śuna ohe sati-nadīyā! basati saphala haila mora*; taken as *sati! nadīyā-basati…* — "O virtuous one! My dwelling in Nadia has borne fruit" (the exclamation mark and the hyphen are transposed).
[^5]: "Let me take His misfortunes upon myself!" — *bālāi laiyā mari*, a common Bengali expression of tender love (lit. "taking [on myself] His misfortunes, may I die"). "Let us make vows" — *mānābe*, from *mānā* ("to vow, to promise an offering to a deity for another's welfare"). "Through astrologers perform worship of the planets" — *graha pūjiba daivajña-gaṇe*: rites to propitiate the planets (*graha-pūjā*) performed by astrologers (*daivajña*). In the edition the seventh line ends with "॥" instead of "।". "Go with Narahari" — the author's signature woven into the speech: Narahari as a participant in the scene (cf. 5.4). On the rites and prayers to the gods for Gaura's sake — see note 8.
[^6]: The third line stands centred, the fourth ends with the mark *dhru* (refrain). "The comfort of the heart" — *parāṇa-juṛā*, lit. "cooling the life". "The Creator has become [my] enemy" — *bidhātā karile bāda*. "Think of [His] welfare" — the last word of the line is unclear in print: probably *hita* ("welfare"); possibly *cita* ("in the heart"). "Obstinacy" — *ā̃khuṭi* (*ākhuṭi*): a child's wilful stubbornness, insistent demands (the same word occurs in kirana 6). "Adjuring [Him]" — *śapatha diyā*, lit. "giving an oath": a mother coaxes a stubborn child by adjuring him. Signature: Narahari (as a participant in the scene, cf. 5.3).
[^7]: The verse is in couplets with a caesura; the metre is not named in the edition (only "yatharaga"). "Joyful" — *mudita*. "Beds" — *śeja*. "Summoned all their strength" — *bāndhala bala bhāri*, lit. "bound up great strength". "Sticks" — *laguṛa*. Signature: Narahari.
[^8]: "The venerable old men" — *bṛddha bṛddha bara puruṣa-gaṇe*. "After many long days" — *cira dibasāntara*. "All [my] wishes" — *manaratha sagare* (*sagare* — "all"). "May His life be longer than mine" — *madadhika hau paramāyu*, lit. "may [His] span of life be greater than mine". "May [people] ceaselessly praise" — *nirata janu guṇa bhaṇaye* (*janu* taken as "people"; possibly "as if"). The son of Mishra — Gaura, son of Jagannatha Mishra. The Lord of Kailasa — Shiva; Bhagavati — the goddess Durga. "So that He may never know defeat" — *ajaya janu na hai kadā* (*ajaya* — "defeat"). The last line also admits another reading: "may Narahari ever behold Nimai — [his] life". The elders, who love Gaura with parental love (vatsalya), see Him as a child and therefore pray to the Creator, Vishnu, Shiva, Sarasvati and Durga for His long life and welfare — just as in Vraja Yashoda and the elder cowherds prayed to the gods for Krishna's welfare (*Srimad-Bhagavatam*, Canto 10). This is a manifestation of vatsalya-prema, in which yogamaya hides the Lord's majesty from those who love Him; such prayers seek only the beloved's good, not any fruit for oneself. Signature: Narahari.
[^9]: Rasika (in print *rasikā*) is the name of a metre. "Absorbed" — *magana*. Signature: Narahari.
[^10]: "Borne fruit" — *suphala*. A kalpa is a day of Brahma, an immense span: a moment without Gaura seems an eternity. "Not a drop remains" — *rahai na thora*. "Upside down" — *biparīta*. Reading and sense unclear: *gṛha-paripāṭī nipaṭa kuṭamaya* — taken as "the household routine is sheer deceit" (*kuṭa* < *kūṭa*, "deceit, falsehood"); possibly "sheer bitterness". "Crest-jewel of fools" — *mūrkha-śiromaṇi*: the author's humble self-designation. Signature: Narahari Dasa.
[^11]: Rupamala is the name of a metre. "Sixteen years old" — *ṣoṛaśa baraṣa sadṛśa*: sixteen is the bloom of youth. "The sticks stay far away" — *dūra rahu laguṛa*. Signature: Dasa Narahari.
[^12]: The colophon was read from the scan (pdf 64, printed p. 59): "ইতি শ্রীমদ্গৌরচরিত্র-চিন্তামণৌ প্রাতঃকালীয়-চরিত্র-বর্ণনে বৃদ্ধানাং স্নেহাদি-বর্ণনং নাম পঞ্চমঃ কিরণঃ ॥৫॥" (the OCR is correct here). Kirana 6 begins on the same page immediately after the colophon.
