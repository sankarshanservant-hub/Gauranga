# Kirana 9. The Deeds of the Morning: The Description of the Longings and So On of the Nagaris of Shri Navadvipa[^1]

*Morning in Navadvipa: a mother-in-law, delighted by an astrologer's words about the greatness of Shachi, herself sends her daughters-in-law to Shachi's house; the nagaris joyfully set out to see Gaura and one by one tell their longings about how they will meet Him*

*Again, in the morning period — the going [to Shachi's house], the longings, the darshan and so on of the nagaris of Shri Navadvipa.*

**9.1.** Today at dawn all the young, young nagaris,
hoping for the darshan of Gaura, dear to them as life, devise so many schemes.
All of them are most clever rasikas, floating in that ocean of rasa:
one contrives various pretexts, another now weeps, now laughs.
Another, driving away various fears from her mind, reflects on her mother-in-law's disposition:
and she, at the auspicious words of an astrologer, has now grown more kindly —
with a light heart the old woman has gone to sleep with the doors of the house open;
starting up again and again, she jumps up: "The night is over!"
Waking, she sees that in the east the dawn has risen;
leaving her bed, she hurried to her daughters-in-law
and sweetly asks: "Children, what are you doing sitting here?"
One says: "Ah, for the Lakshmi-puja we are learning the tale of Lakshmi."
Hearing this, she praises them many times, saying, "Good, good."
Narahari says: who in the world does not desire wealth?[^2]

*The elderly [mother-in-law] says — yatharaga*

**9.2.** Listen, listen, daughters-in-law: at last the Creator has become gracious to me.
Yesterday, during a prahara of the day, an astrologer came to our house.
What can I say of his qualities? I have never seen anyone like him here:
whatever anyone asks, he answers everything; he knows what is in one's heart.
"How shall good fortune come?" I said, and clasped his feet.
Seeing my distress, he said: "Do not worry about anything, mother.
In your village lives Shachi-devi — do you not know her greatness?
She is highly revered in the world; her deeds are known to all.
The dust of her feet is the rarest of things; whoever places it on his head
will gain wealth and kin — and is that so great a thing? — at once the threefold miseries are removed.
Whoever, rising at dawn, sees her face
floats in happiness birth after birth and never knows sorrow.
But whoever reviles mother Shachi burns and perishes in the fire of misery:
know for certain — the goddess Ugrachanda destroys him.
Therefore I advise: abandoning the guile of your mind,
at the close of night, at dawn, carefully send your daughters-in-law to the house of Shri Shachi-devi.
She will mercifully bless [them] and fulfil the desire of your heart:
prosperity will increase, there will always be happiness, many troubles will vanish.
Know that Nimaichanda's mother truly suffers at the suffering of others."
Having said much in this vein, the venerable astrologer went to another house.
Hearing all this, I was filled with great joy:
I felt in my heart as if all misfortune had gone.
Therefore go quickly: she is like my own family,
she affectionately calls me "didi" and never regards me as a stranger.
Going there, bow to her and speak humble words —
by her mercy every happiness will come; of this I am certain.
She will say to you: "Stay here this morning" —
so on what pretext would you come back early? I myself will go there too.
Hearing their mother-in-law's very pressing words, in great joy,
[but] for propriety's sake, they say softly, softly, covering their faces with their cloth:
"How can we leave the housework in the morning and go?"
Narahari says: you will come back and do it; now there must be no delay.[^3]

*Yatharaga*

**9.3.** Again she affectionately said many things to the daughters-in-law in various ways —
what can I say of it! All rose, intoxicated with prema for Gaura.
Keeping their faces in their veils, smiling softly, softly, full of rasa,
with sidelong glances of their eyes they say so much to one another.
In all of them the ocean of happiness surged up — how much can I tell of it?
Their bodies thrill with rapture, their hearts are restless, their feet do not touch the ground.
Quickly they put on fine new clothes and ornaments
and, bowing at their mothers-in-law's feet, went out of the house.
Thus, each having received the permission of her own mother-in-law,
at dawn they set out, jubilant, to see Gaurangachandra.
One says to another: "Friend, it will be hard [to keep composure] there:
having bowed at Shachi-devi's feet, stay calm."
Another says: "Ah, Gaurahari is the treasure of our life;
casting off shyness, I shall gaze at Him to my heart's content, with eyes full."
Another says: "Ah, when we get there, let no one say anything to me:
I will not leave Narahari's Lord of life and come home again."[^4]

*Raga lalita*

**9.4.** All the young women, enchanting everyone, have set out on the way, stepping with great sweetness;
on the road they do not tell path from pathlessness; their hearts are stirred, their creeper-like bodies thrill with rapture.
Their hair has come all loose, their attire glitters endlessly — how can poets describe it?
The edges of their new garments trail on the ground; not a trace of memory [of themselves] remains in their minds.
Thinking of the wondrous radiance of the beautiful Gaura, they are in boundless happiness;
holding hands, they laugh with one another and endlessly speak of His deeds.
Rasa blossoms; they jest and frolic in boundless enthusiasm.
How many, many longings they have! — Narahari will joyfully tell a little of them.[^5]

*Their longings [the author] relates in order.*

*A certain [woman] says — yatharaga*

**9.5.** Going to Shachi's house and bowing at Shachi's feet,
all bashful, I shall stand there, carefully keeping my composure.
Drawing my veil aside just a little, I shall glance but once at Gauranga —
and effortlessly steal His heart with the curved corner of my eye.
He will not be able to keep His composure — the King of nagaras will lose His calm.
Seeing that, I shall smile slyly and lean against my friend.
Looking at me again, the Beloved of my life will smile and be abashed.
Narahari, understanding this, will swim in the ocean of happiness.[^6]

*And a certain [other] — yatharaga*

**9.6.** With my friend, joyful, I shall go and stand in the courtyard of Shri Shachi-devi;
unnoticed, I shall look at Him just once — and soothe my eyes and heart.
And then He will look at me, giving an irresistible sign with His eyes —
neither composure nor restraint will remain: I shall tremble under the onrush of Madana.
My body will be drenched with sweat, again and again the hair on my head will come loose,
my garment will slip again and again — and not a trace of shame will remain.
With all my strength I shall rush to embrace the moon Gauranga —
my friend will hold me back by the end of my sari, and I shall heed her words.
Seeing me like this, the other [women] will laugh among themselves so much!
Understanding all this, I shall grow calm at heart, a little abashed,
and stand again, turned away, covering my face with my garment.
The Lord of Narahari's life, seeing this, will laugh with joy of heart.[^7]

*And a certain [other] — yatharaga*

**9.7.** Again I shall stand beside my friend, at a sign from my friend,
and shall not look at Gaura-nagara — I shall stay turned away.
Unable to see my face, He will grow still more restless
and, glancing unnoticed at my friend, will say to her with the corner of His eye:
"I understand nothing — why has she become so terribly angry?
Persuade her to forgive: no one holds a grievance against one's own.
Entreat her in My name to draw back her veil just once:
gazing on the sweetness of that face, let My eyes and heart be soothed."
Understanding this, my friend, pleading, will say to me again:
"Take the covering from your face and stand [openly] — let Gaurahari look."
Hearing this, I shall pretend not to hear — then she herself will draw [the veil] back with her own hand.
Stopping her, in still greater anger I shall go and stand far off.
Seeing this, Gauranga-raya will be awash in tears;
seeing that, my friend in distress will clasp my feet.
Then, smiling, I shall draw back my veil and look sidelong —
I shall give the highest joy to the Friend, the Lord of Narahari's life.[^8]

*And a certain [other] — yatharaga*

**9.8.** Gazing at Gaura-nagara, the ocean of rasa,
with a faint smile I shall begin talk of rasa with my friend.
Seeing my wondrous manner, He again will float in happiness
and, smiling ever so slightly, smiling, will give a sign with curving eyes.
Understanding this, I shall bite my lip and draw down my veil
and, unnoticed, taking aim with my brows, I shall steal the Friend's composure.
The King of rasikas will grow eager to embrace me —
Narahari will carefully hold [Him] back, reminding [Him] of shame before people.[^9]

*And a certain [other] — yatharaga*

**9.9.** Ai will show me much kindness — [but] I shall not stay near Ai:
quite unnoticed I shall stand behind my friend.
In the highest joy I shall satisfy the hunger of many days —
I shall let the chakoras of my eyes drink the nectar of that moon, His face.
I shall look my fill, but He will not be able to see me;
growing restless, He will say to my friend with a sign of His eyes:
"You have come alone — where has your companion stayed?
Not seeing the two of you together, I suffer at heart."
Understanding this, my friend will take me by the hand and bring me before her;
I, feigning anger and smiling slightly, will not go forward.
Yet my friend, holding [me], will keep [me] in front of her.
[…]
With a sign of her eyes she will say to the Friend: "Look — here is Your [own dear one]!"
Looking at me, the Crest of rasikas will say with the corner of His eye:
"Well, well! Where did you learn all these tricks?
Tell Me, tell Me: how shall I live without seeing you?"
Much like this He will convey; understanding, I shall admit my fault —
yet Gora, the Crest of rasikas, will still be angry with me.
Then Narahari will win [Him] over; relenting, [He] will show the necklace on the neck
and, smiling slightly, says: "Never do so again."[^10]

*And a certain [other] — yatharaga*

**9.10.** Gazing at the moon Gauranga, I shall fall into a heavy daze,
I shall grow faint — my earrings will slip and roll on the ground.
Quickly I shall cover myself with the end of my sari — at that movement of my hands
the bangles on my wrists will jingle; hearing that sound, He will be delighted.
Gauranga, who enchants the worlds, will convey with the slanting corner of His eye:
"Take off the covering just once — let Me see how beautiful [your] hair is."
Understanding this, I shall smile slightly and cover my face with the veil.
Seeing me abashed, He, pleased, will again give a sign to my friend.
My clever friend will say to me: "Stand turned away,
or else, looking at Gauranga, you will lose all your composure."
At these words, forgetting myself, I shall stand with my back to Gora —
and my friend with her own hand will quickly throw the covering off my head.
At that moment Gora, quite overcome in the rapture of rasa,
will forget everything and gaze with unblinking eyes.
I, greatly abashed, will hurriedly pull the covering back over my head
and, seemingly angry, will scold my friend: "Shameless one!"
I shall go and stand a little way off from my friend —
but she, admitting her fault, will pull [me] back and hold [me], swearing by her head.
Seeing this play of mine, playful Gauranga will again float in merriment,
and Narahari, the Friend's companion, will laugh within himself.[^11]

*And another — yatharaga*

**9.11.** Gazing at the moon Gauranga, I shall wink at my friend with a sidelong glance
and, smiling sweetly, sweetly, joyfully begin a sweet tale.
When, carried away by rasa, I toss my head, the knot of my hair will come loose;
sweetly, sweetly will swing my nose-ring and the champa in my ears.
But how the jhapa sways on my back — that He will not be able to see,
and the Nagara, giving a sign with the corner of His eye, will speak [of it] with a slight smile.
On some pretext I shall lift the covering with my left hand and show it to Him —
and at once Rasika-raya, the life of Narahari, will be overcome.[^12]

*And another — yatharaga*

**9.12.** I shall stand beside my friend, half my face covered by the veil,
and, glancing little by little, unnoticed, I shall fulfil my heart's longing.
When the Friend, half-smiling, looks at me,
I, understanding, will at once turn my eyes away and keep looking elsewhere.
The Beloved of my life, from shyness, will sweetly lower His eyes —
seeing this, I shall again glance sidelong at the moon of His face.
Unable to meet [my] eyes, He will grow restless; seeing this, I shall keep gazing [at Him] —
Narahari's Lord will float in happiness when eyes meet eyes.[^13]

**9.13.** I shall carefully stand in Ai's courtyard, holding my friend by the hand.
Gora, the jewel of virtues, looking at me, will say with a sign of His eyes:
"Take the covering from your breast just once — dispel the sorrow of [My] heart."
Understanding these words, I shall be abashed and at once turn my face away.
My friend is very clever: understanding [this], she will stretch out her hand
and, crying "What is this, what is this?" as if alarmed, will draw the covering from [my] breast.
Seeing this, Rasika-raya will cover His face with the end of His cloth and laugh;
Madana will torment [Him] — Narahari will calm Him.[^14]

*And another — yatharaga*

**9.14.** I shall stand beside my friend — the Nagara will not look at me,
but, smiling, smiling, will merrily exchange signs with my friend.
Unable to understand anything, I shall take my friend by the hand and ask:
"What fault have you seen in me that you laugh together?"
Hearing this, my friend will say: "Yes, there is a fault in you:
the Nagara wants to embrace [you] — and you are angry at that."
Hearing this, I shall say [something], giving a secret sign, and laugh as if pouring nectar —
Narahari will not be able to hold [Him] back: Gora will lose His composure.[^15]

*And another — yatharaga*

**9.15.** Seeing the smiling face of the moon Gauranga, in the fullness of rasa,
with the end of my cloth about my neck and palms joined, I shall say by a sign of the eyes:
"Very well, O Crown of rasikas, why do You practise deceit?
I do not know where You learned this — how skilfully You can deceive!
What more is there to say? Not once do You come and show Yourself [to me] on the road.
The Creator made me a woman — that is the sorrow; were it not so, I would stay beside You."
Hearing this, Narahari's Friend of life will be abashed
and will swear with the corner of His eye: "I shall surely come."[^16]

*And another — yatharaga*

**9.16.** Staying among my friends, glancing just once at that face,
with a sorrowful look I shall say to the Nagara with the corner of my eye:
"Very well, very well, O You — the inmost heart of love You have never known.
You can wander through this quarter and that — I alone am deprived.
You are the Crown of rasikas: You always stay absorbed in bliss.
But I, unfortunate one — what has not befallen me because of You!
My elders loved me more than life — [now] they regard me as poison;
those at whom I used to laugh when I saw them now laugh [at me] when they see me.
If even so You remained gracious to me, regarding me as Your own,
then all this disgrace I would count as no more than a straw.
It is not enough that day and night my body cannot bear the cruel burning —
on top of it You are pitiless: how then can life remain?
So dispel the doubt of my heart: why have You become angry?
Is such Your nature, or have You found some fault [in me]?"
Understanding this, He, overcome by rasa, will look at me
and, unseen, joining His palms, will say with the corner of His eye:
"A curse on My nature: I am stained with every fault!
[But] at every moment in My heart I know only this: you are dearer to Me than life."
Understanding this, I shall smile slyly and give a sign to my friend —
the heart of Narahari's Beloved, seeing this, will be at peace in great joy.[^17]

*Vilapa-chhandah*

**9.17.** Thus the host of young women, rejoicing, indulge more and more in longings;
the ever-new deeds of the charming, beautiful, excellent Gaura sport in their hearts.
Their bodies tremble intensely, like lightning, and tears stream from their eyes.
The new ways of prema, ever new and new, cannot be described even by hundreds upon hundreds of mouths.[^18]

*Raga lalita*

**9.18.** As night turned to dawn, all the beautiful women went with graceful, most charming gait;
wondrous was their attire; the sweet sound of their girdles and jewelled anklets captivates the minds of sages.
None can conceive who fashioned them: with the rays of every limb they illumine the world.
Seeing them, hundreds upon hundreds of Manmathas swoon; drawn by the fragrance of their bodies, bees come flying from all sides.
Rejoicing together, with supreme delight in their hearts, they quickly entered the lovely house.
The courtyard is an excellent lake of happiness: in it, as it were, a host of new lotuses has blossomed.
Approaching Ai, with great care, all of them, group after group, bow down —
as though the Creator were worshipping [her] feet with handfuls of champaka buds, says Ghanashyama.[^19]

*Yatharaga*

**9.19.** The bands of young women, bending most wondrously, offer obeisance with charming grace:
the slender ones bow like new golden creepers weighed down by a burden of flowers.
[…]
They lay their heads at the edge of [Shachi's] beautiful feet; their hair is loosened — black, of lovely sheen.
Lightly, lightly they stretch out their bud-like hands: their hearts long to take the rare dust [of her feet].
Their nails sparkle — as if jewels, with incomparable eagerness, were offering [themselves] as gifts out of respect.
Covering their lotus faces with the edge of their garments, they smile softly, softly — a radiance spreads,
as if, carefully straining fresh nectar, they were sprinkling [it] abundantly, says Narahari Dasa.[^20]

*Mukta-chhandah*

**9.20.** Resplendent is the wondrous gathering of young women; marvellous the radiance of their bodies, lovely their splendour.
What poet can describe their charming conduct and qualities? With humility they bow at Shachi's feet.[^21]

*Raga tudi*

**9.21.** Shachi — the Mother of the world, knower of the truth of motherhood, renowned for the loveliness of her deeds and ways —
regards them all in her heart as daughters-in-law dearer than her own life and shows them all the greatest affection.
She asks each and every one about her welfare and, placing her hand on her head, blesses her abundantly:
"May your wealth ever increase, and may your husbands and all [your kin] live long and remain well."
Hearing this, the young wives, laughing inwardly, floating in happiness, speak sweet words:
"Ah, tell us, how could any misfortune remain here once one has seen these auspicious feet?"
Having said a little with great shyness in their hearts, they stand nearby with joined palms.
Seeing this, the mother of Narahari's Lord of life — her eyes brimming with tears — is overwhelmed with affection.[^22]

*Yatharaga*

**9.22.** O, the young nagaris of Nadiya! They remain enraptured — who else could understand even a little of their deeds?
Glancing at the Beloved quite unseen, they tremble at heart from the arrows of Madana.
One, reflecting, says to herself: "Today, it seems, I have become shameless in front of everyone."
One, winking at another, cannot restrain herself and at once smiles a little, a little.
One, taking another by the hand, gently, gently entreats her; the heart overflows with great joy.
One tells another a tale of love, bringing veil a little close to veil.
One makes signs to another, hand touching hand — so many, many words arise in the mind!
One steadies another, warning her much with the lovely corner of her eye.
One, to show her own composure, carefully wipes another's face with the end of her cloth.
One, after whispering, realizes that things may turn out badly, and stays hidden to one side.
Thus all the chaste women of noble families were immersed in the ocean of the rasa of prema for Gaura.
What will Narahari say? To the Lord of their life they gave over their souls, their lives and their youth.[^23]

*Yatharaga*

**9.23.** May my mind remain at the feet of the nagaris of Nadiya — in life and at my passing.
Never have I seen or heard the like: for the sake of the Beloved they give up everything so easily.
The bodies of them all are fashioned of prema for Gora. Who can understand this? Whose power is it?
This is the hope in Narahari's heart: ever to sing of their virtues.[^24]

*Thus [ends] the ninth kirana of the Shrimad Gaura-charita-chintamani, called "The Description of the Longings and So On of the Nagaris of Shri Navadvipa", in the description of the deeds of the morning period.*[^25]

The subtitle "Again, in the morning period — the going [to Shachi's house], the longings, the darshan and so on of the nagaris of Shri Navadvipa" (*punaḥ prātaḥkāle śrī-navadvīpa-nāgarīṇāṁ gamana-manoratha-darśanādikaṁ*; in print *manoratha* looks like *ganoratha*) stands immediately after the colophon of Kirana 8 (pdf 110, printed p. 105). Kirana 9 comprises verses 9.1–9.23 (pdf 110–122). The nagaris are the young married women of Navadvipa (see notes 1 and 3 of Kirana 7). The summary line is based on 9.1–9.5 and a quick look at the OCR of the following verses.
[^2]: The verse has no metre or raga heading; it begins on pdf 110 (printed p. 105) and ends on pdf 111 (printed p. 106) with "॥১॥". Here a new kind of the nagaris' mood begins — longings (*manoratha*) about how they will come to Shachi's house and see Gaura. Gaudiya Vaishnavas, following Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati and the *Chaitanya-bhagavata* (Adi 15: in His lila the Lord did not jest with women or even look at them), reject *gaura-nagari-vada*, the worship of Gaura as an enjoying lover (*nāgara*) and of oneself as His nagari: Gaura is Krishna in the mood of Radha, tasting Her love, not an enjoyer. The longings of the nagaris in the poem are understood as a glorification of Gaura's irresistible beauty and of the prema it awakens in hearts; in reality they only bow to Shachi and look at Gaura from afar (see 9.3, 9.5). Further in this kirana, references are made to this note (cf. the main notes of Kirana 7 — note 3, and of Kirana 8 — note 3). "Young, young" — *naba naba*. "Dear as life" — *prāṇapriya*. "Hoping" — *āse* (*āśe*). "Schemes" — *yukati*. "Most clever rasikas" — *parama caturā rasikinī*. "Now weeps, now laughs" — the word before *hāsi* is smudged in print: probably *jhure* ("weeps"; the OCR has *bhare*); the reading is unclear. Lines 5 and 6 are badly smudged in print; "her mother-in-law's disposition" — *śāśuṛī-rīti* (possibly *-bhīti*, "fear of the mother-in-law"); the reading is unclear. "Astrologer" — *daibajña*. "The old woman" — *buṛī*: the mother-in-law. "Children" — *bāchā saba*. Lakshmi-puja is the domestic worship of Lakshmi, the goddess of prosperity, performed in Bengal by women; "the tale of Lakshmi" (*lakṣmīra kathā*) is read at this rite. The daughters-in-law use it as a pretext, and the mother-in-law, hoping for prosperity, is pleased: hence Narahari's jest about the desire for wealth. Signature: Narahari.
[^3]: The heading is "বৃদ্ধাহ—যথারাগ" (*vṛddhā āha*, "the elderly [woman] says"): the mother-in-law speaks. "Daughters-in-law" — *badhū*. "During a prahara of the day" — *dina-prahara-samaye*: a prahara is a three-hour "watch" of the day; probably "one prahara after sunrise", the exact time is unclear. "He knows what is in one's heart" — *jānaye manera kathā*. "Clasped his feet" — *dharinu tāhāra pā*: a gesture of supplication. Shachi-devi is the mother of Gaura (Nimai, Nimaichanda). "The threefold miseries" — *tritāpa*: those arising from one's own body and mind, from other beings, and from the forces of nature. "Burns and perishes in the fire of misery" — *dukha-anale puṛiyā mare*. Ugrachanda is a fierce form of the goddess Chandi (Durga). "Abandoning the guile of your mind" — *manera kapaṭa chāṛi*. "The venerable astrologer" — *daibajña-rāya*. "She is like my own family" — *se haya āmāra ghara*, lit. "she is my home". "Didi" — "elder sister", an affectionate address. "This morning" — *e belā*, "this part of the day". "So on what pretext would you come back early? I myself will go there too" — *tāhe kona chale āsibe sakāle āmi ye yāiba sethā*; another understanding is possible: "then come back early on some pretext, for I shall be going there"; the sense is uncertain. "Very pressing" — *ati ātura*. "For propriety's sake" — *ādara lāgiyā*: out of deference the daughters-in-law show that they are in no hurry. "Covering their faces with their cloth" — *basana jhā̃piyā mukhe*. "There must be no delay" — *nā sahe byāja*. Narahari playfully takes up the mother-in-law's words. Signature: Narahari.

"Intoxicated with prema for Gaura" — *gaura-premete māti*. "Veils" — *ghũghaṭa*. "With sidelong glances" — *terachha nayana bhaṅgī kari*.
