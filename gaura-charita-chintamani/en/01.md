# Kirana 1. The Mangalacharana: Description of the Sutras and Other Matters[^1]

*Benediction; glorification of Sri Guru and the author's humility; glory to Gaura, Nityananda, Advaita and the Lord's associates; the glory of Navadvipa; a brief statement (the sutras) of Gaura's pastimes in the eight periods of the day*

*Obeisance to Sri Krishna Chaitanya-chandra.*

*O Sri Guru, worshipable by all, benefactor of those who bow down, ocean of prema! O giver of full bliss, ocean of rasa, Gaura-Govinda — I bow [to You]! O Nityananda, spotless moon in the sky of the hearts of Your own people! O Sri Advaita! And you, host of devotees who take away the sufferings of the world — glory [to you]!*[^2]

*Sutra-prabandha, raga Karnata*[^3]

**1.1.** Glory, glory to Sri Gurudeva, worshipped in the three worlds, whose preaching delivers from fear,
like the lord of the stars, giver of every happiness, whose auspicious fame is beyond counting — the great enchanter of minds!
His body shines with clusters of necklaces, lovely are his ornaments; with his pure radiance he adorns the throngs of people;
he is a festival for the eyes of men; wondrous are his deeds — who can describe them?
*sa ri ri ri-ga ga ri ma ni-dha sa-ni dha-pa-dha sa sa ri-ga ri-ga pa-ma-ma*
*ga-ri ga-ri sa sa-ri-ga-ma ri-ga-ma ri-ga ri-ga-ma sa-ri-ga-ma.*[^4]
Beautiful, composed, magnanimous, ever wondrous, ocean of mercy, whose virtues delight the mind,
skilled in giving the rasa of Gaura-Krishna, destroyer of sorrow, who puts the wish-fulfilling tree to shame!
Wretched Narahari — without bhajana, utterly impure, evil-minded,
of low intent, king of deceit, crest-jewel of the crooked,
best of the foremost rogues — has fallen into the whirlpool of the terrible river of sin
and is in anguish: rescue me, O!
O friend of the fallen, grant me a dwelling near your lotus feet!
O protector of the surrendered, my desire is with a hundred thousand mouths
to repeat your name unceasingly, in supreme happiness.
The best of poets, rejoicing, sing your charming great glory
in ever-new movements, in dance, instrumental music and song —
[you are] renowned[^5] and worshipped in all the worlds.

*Lalita-chhandah (matra-vritta)*

**1.2.** The feet of Sri Guru crush calamities; they are the lovely abode of all good;
serve them always with care — all your desires will be fulfilled.
All know it: he gives the highest happiness, good qualities and priceless bhakti;
skilled in giving, never resting — no one is equal to him.
Meditate on the sweet form of Sri Guru — all torments will go far away;
always sing of his lovely virtues — you will obtain great wealth.
Terrible troubles will quickly pass — repeat [his] nectarean name;
constantly to see [him] and serve [him] is auspicious: this is true, says Ghanashyama.
Sri Guru is the root of all, full of happiness; giving up everything else in your mind, worship him;
there is no limit to his charming glory — so declare the Vedas and Puranas.
The dense darkness of ignorance makes the eyes utterly blind —
with the salve of knowledge he opens them, supremely merciful to the humble.[^6]
With one mouth there is no power to tell of his wondrous, lovely deeds;
seeing people in distress, he mercifully makes them utterly pure.
Seeing people devoted to [his] feet, Shamana[^7] is greatly afraid at heart.
Knowing this for certain, worship fearlessly, says Narahari Dasa.

*Shyama-chhandah*

**1.3.** I say it again and again: the feet of the guru are the essence of all.
Casting deceit far away, gather happiness in abundance.

*Kamini-chhandah*

**1.4.** Breaker of fear, delighter of people,
he is a jewel of virtues: make your effort!

*Yamini-chhandah*

**1.5.** He is a delight for the eyes of people, radiant in form, an abode of steadfastness;
there is no end to his good fame — O heart, meditate [on him] through all eight watches.[^8]

*Chatushpadi*

**1.6.** One whose mind thrills on hearing of the guru's virtues —
wondrous is his greatness in the world.
One who has firm love for the feet of the guru —
he will destroy the sorrow in Narahari's heart.

*Raga Veloara*

**1.7.** Listen, listen, O Creator — this is the boon I ask:
the touch of that evil-minded one who has turned away from the feet of Sri Guru I do not want even in a dream.
If somehow I should see his face,
hear his words or touch his body —
at that very moment let my two eyes go blind,
my ears go deaf, and my body fall.
One whose heart does not rejoice on hearing
of the guru's nectarean, charming virtues and deeds —
even if he has hundreds of thousands of virtues, twice over,[^9] —
let me never have even a word with him.
But one devoted to the guru's feet, even if he lacks good manners —
still, let me have his company.
Poor Narahari will count himself fortunate then,
when he has even a little pure bhakti.

*Tara-chhandah*

**1.8.** Love for the guru's feet — know that it is very rare.
In Narahari's heart is unceasing hope [for it].

*Veloara*

**1.9.** Hari, Hari! Will such a day ever come for me,
when I shall meditate unceasingly in my heart on the wondrous deeds and virtues of Sri Gurudeva?
Shall I fill the cup of my ears with the nectar of words from his softly smiling mouth and drink it?
When shall I satisfy my eyes, gazing on his incomparable lovely form that delights people?
The fragrance of his graceful body, ever new — I shall fill my nostrils with it and keep it there;
with this mouth I shall unceasingly repeat his sweet, auspicious name with joy in my heart.
What more can I say? All this is so hard to attain — will my hands bear fruit in serving him?
Will fallen Narahari, prostrate on the ground, place your lotus feet upon his head?

*Kumari-chhandah*

**1.10.** When shall I drink the nectar of Sri Guru's feet, taking it into my heart?
When shall I relish the wondrous nectar of his lips, counting it as happiness?[^10]
When shall I smear my body with the dust of his beautiful lotus feet?
Sorrowful, foolish Narahari longs [for this], though he has no means.

*Suvilapa-chhandah*

**1.11.** O Srila Sri Gurudeva, reveal your mercy!
I am utterly helpless in the midst of the ocean of vishaya — destroy my fear!
Grant me a dwelling always near your lovely lotus feet;
do not disappoint my hope, says your servant Narahari Dasa.

*Mangala-chhandah*

**1.12.** Taking into my heart Srimad Gurudeva, worshipped by the worlds, sweet in glory,
delighter of the mind, breaker of fear, [whose fame] has spread over the earth —
devoid of elegant diction, like a child, according to my understanding,
I have made this slight description, knowing it to be a treasure that gives happiness.
Now, following the earlier [poets],[^11] I shall speak the names of the Lord's associates —
this is my eager wish, though harm will come from breaking the order.
Even so, my heart is fearless: no one will hold it as my fault,
seeing that I am ignorant — pure people will accept [it] with joy.

*Rangini-chhandah*

**1.13.** Glory to Gaura-Govinda, treasure of bliss, crest-jewel of the beautiful, who sports in the town of Navadvipa,
the accomplished one, intoxicated with sankirtana, affectionate to the devotees, supremely composed, abode of virtues, whose incomparable greatness is renowned in this world;
whose father, mother and brother are Sri Jagannatha Mishra, Sri Shachi and Vishvarupa, rich in beauty;[^12]
whose lovely, good consorts are Lakshmi and the blessed Vishnupriya, equal in glory!
Glory, glory to Nityananda-Rama, the beloved of Revati, whose lovely might has lit up the ten directions, full of rasa, worshipped by hosts of sages,
the refuge of those utterly without refuge, the Lord, whose sun of mercy has dried up the river of sorrow;
full of happiness, accomplished, the renowned son of Hadai Pandita and the good Padmavati, the lord of the life of Vasudha and Jahnavi, [delighting in dance (?)], whose son is the blissful Sri Virachandra, sweet in his deeds![^13]
Glory, glory to Advaita the lion, mighty in valour, breaker of the frenzy of the maddened elephant of Kali, charming, supremely sweet, whose body is rich in abundant bhavas, the incomparable abode of all that is auspicious, whose charming glory has no equal;
the son of the blessed Shrimat Kuvera Acharya and Nabha — father and mother adorned with a wealth of blessings;[^14]
the father of Achyuta and other sons; [He] whose abundant virtues cannot be counted; the consort of Sri Sita — His shakti, — who by His beauty overpowers Kamadeva!
Glory to Srinivasa Pandita, the venerable, adorned with lovely virtues,
and to the glorious son of Madhava, renowned throughout the world, dear to Gaura — Gadadhara Pandita, ever rejoicing in abundant prema![^15]
Glory, glory to the Puri named Sri Madhavendra, entitled [to lead] on the path of bhakti, to Ishvara Puri, Vishnu, Bharati Keshava[^16] —
merciful, dear to Gaura, givers of prema, whose fame and the rest are known [to all]!
Glory, glory to Gadadhara Dasa, to Dasa Narahari, avid for kirtana of Gaura,
to the flawless scholars dwelling in Khanda — Raghunandana, Mukunda; to Govinda, Jagadananda, Nandana, the good Vamshivadana!
Glory, glory to Ramachandra, Bhudeva, Kavichandra, Paramananda Gupta,
to Ramananda Raya, whose host of virtues is known, whose wondrous works give great joy, to Yadunatha, Madhusudana — abodes of bliss![^17]
Glory, glory to Sanatana and Rupa, kings of the rasikas, supremely skilled in giving away the jewel of prema;
to Bhatta Gopala, Raghunatha, Raghunatha Dasa, Prabodhananda, Jiva — the life of living beings, …acharya, Tapana![^18]
Glory, glory to Lokanatha, renowned in the land of Vraja, to Bhugarbha, to Vakreshvara, ever new in the joy of dance,
to Pradyumna Mishra, Haribhatta, Madhava Pattanayaka, Sadashiva — skilled in good deeds!
Glory, glory to Vasudeva called Sarvabhauma, dear to the Lord; to the most venerable Svarupa Damodara;
to Shrimat Uddharana, Keshava, Kaliya-Krishna — rejoicing in the rasa of Krishna, clad in yellow garments!
Glory to Sri Murari Gupta, Acharyachandra, Gauridasa Pandita, whose greatness is manifest and pure — removers of all sorrows;
to Haridasa, Haridasa, ever devoted to the Hari-nama, whose tongues are connoisseurs of rasa![^19]
Glory, glory to Jagadisha, Kashishvara, Shivananda Sena, the giver of joy;
to the good companion Saranga, who sports beautifully, of many moods; to Gangadasa Pandita, Mahesha, Parameshvara — their fame has spread through the worlds!
Glory to Vallabhacharya, the best, of wondrous deeds, renowned in Nadia;
to Sanjaya, Jagannatha, Bhaskara; to Chiranjiva, Simheshvara, Mahidhara, Sudarshana — to hear their names is bliss!
Glory, glory to Chidananda, whose form delights the eyes of the people of the world,[^20] to Shrimat Bhavananda,
to Vidyananda, the giver of happiness, to Chandaneshvara, Purandara, Sundarananda, Chandrashekhara — bringers of good fortune!
Glory, glory to Hridaya Chaitanya, blessed on earth; to Madhava, the best of the wise;
to the beautiful Shrimat Nrisimha, dear to Gaura; to Kamsari, Vallabha Sena, Shankara, Halayudha, the wise Shridhara!
Glory, glory to Pitambara, Vijaya, Vishnudasa, whose greatness is manifest in the world;
to the most charming Nilambara, the good Nakula, Shuklambara, Shrila Balarama, Vanamali, Prataparudra![^21]
Glory, glory to Sanatana Mishra and Mishra Tulasi, dear to Gaurachandra, supremely merciful, full of joy;[^22]
to the good Lakshmikanta, Lakshmanacharya, Chaitanya, Vidyanidhi, the good bhagavata Ramabhadra!
Glory, glory to Pandita Sanatana Dasa, Buddhimanta Khan, Ishana, Garudadhvaja — their lovely greatness shines on earth —
to Shrinatha, Narayana, Abhirama!
Glory, glory to Sukhananda, Narayana-Vachaspati, Shrila Vrindavana, the king of poets,
to the young Krishnadasa, Krishnacharya, Surya, Haricharana, Makaradhvaja — abodes of bliss![^23]
Glory, glory to Sri Jagannatha Dasa, Gopala, Gopala Acharya, Bhagavan, Shrikanta — there is no end [to their glory],
their hearts ever immersed in prema; to Gopikanta, whose wondrous beauty shines!
Glory to Vaidya Raghunatha Bhattacharya, whose body is filled with the eight [bhavas] — tears, trembling and the rest;[^24]
to Janakinatha, Vaninatha Pattanayaka, Bhattasimha, Raghava, Vasanta!
Glory, glory to Sulochana, Satyaraja, Garuda Pandita, Garuda Avadhuta, Devananda Acharya —
delighters of people's eyes, invincible, removers of misfortune; to Hariharananda, Raghu Mishra, Nayana!
Glory, glory to Janardana, Janardana — treasuries of prema who gladden people; to Jagannatha Tirtha, Jnanadasa,
Balabhadra, Mangala, Hora Gopala, Gopala the dancer — with smiling faces!
Glory, glory to Haridasa, best of the brahmanas, excelling in steadfast singing of Gaura's virtues;
to the manifestly auspicious Shashthidharacharya, Vaishnavananda, Ramananda Vasu, Shrirama, the wise Vaidyanatha!
Glory, glory to Ramai, Nandai, Shriranga, Gaurangadasa, Sri Murari Chaitanya, who blessed the earth;
to Haridasa, revered in Vraja — the world sings their glory in ever new songs!
Glory, glory to Chaitanya-vallabha, Shivananda, Datta,[^25] Chaitanyadasa of Bangavati, Shridhara Brahmachari,
Shivananda, the Avadhuta of Orissa, Paramananda Sena, Rama![^26]
Glory, glory to Paramananda Puri, Brahmananda Bharati, Krishnananda Sena of Orissa, Hridayananda,
Purushottama Brahmachari, Murari Mahiti, Shikhi, Bhatta Kama!
Glory, glory to Kalanidhi, Sudhanidhi, the sweet Mukundai, Kanu, Kamalakara Pippalai,
the good Jagai, Madhai, Yadava, Dasashekhara, Hiranya, Kavidatta, Subaddha, Shrimanta![^27]
Glory, glory to Jita Mishra,[^28] Gokula, Pushpa-Gopala, Pandita Murari, Vaninatha, Acharya Puri,
Raghunatha, Bholanatha Dasa, Vishvasa, Vallabha — destroyers of the world's torments!
Glory, glory to Shikhari,[^29] Damayanti, Ratnavati, Narayani, Malini, Madhavi,
Sukhi, Nandini, Sri Jangali, Shathi — [they are] like bees at Gaura's lotus feet![^30]
Glory, glory to Ganga-mantri, Mamu Thakura, Kinkaracharya, Vaishnavananda, Vishnai, Shrimat Nakadi,
to Jagannatha the woodcutter, whose heart never [turns] to anything but Gaura!
Glory, glory to Lakshminatha Pandita, Amogha — treasury of virtues, Sri Nandadasa,[^31] Pandita Ananta Acharya,
Kamalakanta Vishvasa, Sri Lokanatha Pandita, Lokanatha, Navani!
Glory, glory to Nirloma Gangadasa, Dasa Damodara, Shrimat Shivai, Satyananda Bharati,
Vishveshvaracharya, best of acharyas, Hari, Hasti-Gopala, who blessed the earth!
Glory, glory to the destroyers of the world's torments, whose minds are immersed in Gaura's deeds and virtues —
Minaketana, Manohara, Kamadeva, Chaitanyadasa Acharya, Chakrapani, Kamalanayana, removers of the defilement of Kali!
Glory, glory to Shrigarbha, Yadava, Jagannatha Kara, overwhelmed by Gaura's rasa, rejoicing day and night;
to Ajaya, Vijaya, Rajendra, Anupama, Kanthabharana, Rudra, Dhruvananda — givers of joy!
Glory, glory to Vasu Ghosha, Madhava, Dhananjaya Pandita, of manifest fame, avid for kirtana;
to Karnapura, Kavi Lochana, Subuddhi, Vallabha, Bhatta Bhavaka, Purandaracharya, Kamala!
Glory, glory to Purushottama and the other foremost associates of Gaurahari — boundlessly merciful, removers of the torments of worldly existence, friends of the humble!
Hearing of [their] virtues the heart rejoices; Narahari, ever seeking [their] shelter, [prays]: give pure bhakti!

*Ujjvala-chhandah*

**1.14.** Glory, glory to Vitthalanatha, who made the line of Vallabha Bhatta shine, abode of mercy,
friend of the helpless and of all people, ever giving happiness, warding off terrible sorrows!
Adorned, as with ornaments, by a host of incomparable good virtues, dwelling at the foot of the king of mountains,
whose lovely deeds have spread everywhere, whose fame is pure and unfathomable — he is a great enchanter of minds.
Supremely beautiful, best among the kings of the rasikas — Shrimat Raghunatha, a kind friend, endowed with extraordinary marks,
magnanimous, best of the wise, ceaselessly spreading pure prema,
avid for the rasa of the charming singing of Gaura's virtues, highly skilled in dance —
with lovely poses, unfailingly beautiful — by the radiance of his body he delights the eyes of Narahari.[^32]

*Suchitra-chhandah*

**1.15.** Glory in the world to Shrimat Krishnadasa Kaviraja, king of poets, treasury of fame, lovely in virtues,
full of radiance, whose beautiful eyes put the petals of the lotus to shame!
His gait is most graceful; trembling, tears, the thrill of the hair and the other highest bhavas — the ornament of the world —
ceaselessly adorn him; in softness of body even the shirisha flower cannot compare with him.
Full of happiness, with great joy he always served the lake of Radhika, full of rasa,
having taken his dwelling there by a strict vow — he gives delight to Gaura-Govinda.
Day and night he is immersed in rasa; there is no limit to his mercy; he is known as the friend of the humble,
supremely skilled in giving away [this] wealth — he removes fear from the heart of the servant Narahari.[^33]

*Kadambini-chhandah*

**1.16.** Glory to Gadadhara Bhatta, crusher of the torments of worldly existence — great is his lovely might!
Richly adorned with the ornaments of bhava, he ceaselessly increases joy.
Like a wondrous cloud he pours down the rain of the nectar of the *Srimad-Bhagavata* — great, enchanting;
immersed in the nectar of Gaurachandra's deeds, the abode of renowned virtues — says Ghanashyama.[^34]

*Vichitra-chhandah*

**1.17.** Glory to Srinivasa Acharya, full of bliss, immersed in the bhajana of the Son of Nanda, who steals the hearts of people!
Seeing his wondrous form — the embodiment of prema for Gaura, gentle and lovely — one cannot keep one's composure.
A pandita, magnanimous, adorned with virtues, best of the wise, renowned in the universe, removing [its] sufferings,
giver of bhakti, ocean of mercy, incomparable in greatness — his feet, which surpass the lotus, the humble Narahari worships.

*Rangavardhini-chhandah*

**1.18.**[^35] Glory to Narottama, adorned with good fortune, the accomplished pandita, Narottama Mahashaya,
charming in every way, most venerable, profound, most composed, abode of virtues!
His form is full of prema, he is a well of rasa, incomparable; enraptured, day and night he is absorbed in singing,
his heart given to dance in new talas and rhythms, gentle and lovely in body.
Ceaselessly and in great joy he serves the beautiful Deity; he is a dear vessel [of mercy] for the devotees of Gaurahari;
known for his mercy, the friend of the humble — how many have had all their desires fulfilled [through him]!
His most lovely fame is the ornament of the world, without flaw; boundless are his virtues — no end of them can be found;
the best of poets sing [of him] unceasingly — [says] the servant Ghanashyama.

*Rangamala-chhandah*

**1.19.** Shyamananda, merciful, composed, profound, ever magnanimous in character,
always enraptured by the bliss of prema, absorbed in singing, unequalled in enchanting dance;
his mind is immersed in the virtues of Sri Gauranga, he is skilled in countless great auspicious deeds.
His beauty [dwells] freely in the heart of Ghanashyama; his body is lovely, endowed with auspicious marks.[^36]

*Ramani-chhandah*

**1.20.** Glory to the delighter of people, the lotus-eyed, beautiful with a wealth of fame,
the breaker of fear, Shrimat Gatigovinda, ocean of virtues, the composed one![^37]
Avid for auspicious sankirtana, skilled in new dance, in playing instruments
and in singing, most wondrous, richly adorned with bhavas — beautiful is his form.
Ceaselessly he drinks the sweetest nectar of Gaura's deeds —
from his blossoming eyes tears stream down like a river.
He removes the sorrow of the poor and lowly Narahari; merciful, giving honour to others,
increasing joy, the best friend of the fallen, beautiful, profound.

*Hemavati-chhandah*

**1.21.** Glory, glory to Shrimati Hemalata, who wards off the torments of worldly existence, whose tears put to shame the abundant downpours of the clouds; who ever gives happiness, who is ceaselessly immersed in the ocean of the rasa of the pastimes in the kunjas, whose heart is devoted to the wondrous nectar of Gaura's deeds!
She crushes the filth of Kali and shatters the fear of Shamana; she is like a most lovely tree [laden] with blessings[^38] — she whose father is Srinivasa Acharya, and whose mothers, givers of happiness, are Draupadi-Ishvari and Gauranga-priya, like two creepers fashioned of dear prema.[^39]
Ramakrishna, son of Kumuda Chattaraja, is a bee at the Lord's lotus feet, intoxicated with joy, avid for the rasa of sankirtana; his son, the excellent Gopijana-vallabha, is her husband: seeing his boundless, perfect bhakti, the gods break into joyful dance.[^40]
Her brothers are Shri Chaitanya Dasa, a jewel of virtues, and the one named Gatigovinda; their sons — Krishnaprasada, Subalachandra, Shri Radha-madhava, Sundara and Hari — these five are widely renowned in the world.[^41] What can foolish Narahari say [of them]?

*Raga pathamanjari*

**1.22.** Glory, glory to Ramachandra Kaviraja,
charming in his ways, ceaselessly devoted to the Name, immersed in the great ocean of bliss! *(Refrain)*
He is absorbed in worshipping the pollen of the lotus feet of the excellent Shri Srinivasa,
always drinks the nectar of his virtues and deeds; his pure prema is incomparable — it has not even a slight likeness.
His reading and realization of the rasa-filled *Srimad-Bhagavatam* and other books — their depth cannot be fathomed.[^42]
He is always in the company of Srila Narottama, with great affection; all his deeds are wondrous and renowned.
Shri Govinda, king of poets, a treasury of mercy, whose great mind [is absorbed] in Gaura's deeds —
his pure preaching of prema, his charming virtues and deeds purify the world.
Karnapura, full [of prema], a relisher of the rasa of prema — endless joy [is his] day and night.
Skilled Nrisimha, lion-like in valour — strong is his bhava, he is ever intoxicated.
Shri Bhagavan, adorned with bhavas, is the crest-jewel of the skilled, profound in character.
Gokula, a jewel of virtues, every moment loses his composure singing the virtues of Gaurachandra.
Shri Vallavikanta is an ocean of mercy, a preacher of bhakti, exceedingly magnanimous.
Gopiramana loves dance and song; he is worshipable, his might is fierce and boundless.
Merciful Shri Shyamadasa Chakravarti, who makes the line of the twice-born shine —
who can understand his nectarean deeds? Vast is his good fame, known in the three worlds.
…Ramacharana, stealer of hearts, skilful, best of panditas, abode of supreme mercy, composed;
…hearing the names of Gaura and Nitai, [he sheds] from both eyes abundant streams of tears.[^43]
Shrimat Vyasa, renowned, most learned, ceaselessly chants the sweetest Name of Hari;[^44]
he weeps, every moment his body trembles and thrills, he rolls on the ground — without respite.
Shri Govinda, avid for Gaura's virtues, floats in the ocean of prema.
Shri Shridasa is the life of the rasikas, a friend of the lowly; his pure fame has spread far.
Gokula Chakravarti is an ocean of virtues; what can I say? His greatness is manifest to the whole world.
Shrimat Rupa Ghataka, the arranger [of meetings], always contemplates in his mind the pastimes of the Divine Couple.[^45]
Shri Radha-vallabha Mandala, adorned with greatness, is the embodiment of virtue and bliss;
his all-in-all is Gaura with His associates; he is supremely magnanimous, a king of the rasa of bhakti.
King Vira Hambira, best of heroes, removes sorrow and fulfils desires.[^46]
Narahari, pained at heart, loudly cries out: "Keep me near [your] feet, making me your servant!"

*Raga dhanashi*

**1.23.** Glory, glory to Shri Hariram Acharya, whose deeds are wondrous, the stealer of hearts![^47]
Bright with virtues, crushing the pride of calamities, sweet in form, increasing joy,
he conquers the invincible asuras who are averse to the Lord's feet — [those who are] to be shunned; his fame has spread through the world.
Supremely composed, [he] steals the composure [even] of the composed; he is merciful and exceedingly magnanimous in mind.
Every moment, maddened by the fullness of prema for Gaura, [he moves] slowly, his gait putting the king of elephants to shame.[^48]
Avid for the rasa of sankirtana, skilful — who can tell the full extent of his joy in serving the Vaishnavas?
His exposition of the *Srimad-Bhagavatam* and other books is incomparable — he pours forth streams of nectar.
Shri Shri Krishna-raya is [his] life-giving life;[^49] what can Narahari tell of his boundless greatness?

*Raga kamoda*

**1.24.** Glory, glory to Ramakrishna Acharya, composed, noble, giver of happiness, magnanimous![^50]
Ceaselessly in bhavavesha, avid for kirtana, he is most skilled [in spreading it].
A rasika full of happiness, delighting people's minds, shattering the darkness of a multitude of sufferings,
ornament of the line of the twice-born, adorned with a host of virtues, best of panditas, humbling the pride of the arrogant,
he is constantly and above all engaged in the service of the beautiful Deity of Shri Manmohana-raya;
rejoicing day and night with wondrous love, he drinks the nectar of Gaurachandra's deeds.
The feet of supremely merciful Narottama are his all-in-all; he knows nothing else.
Who can understand his charming ways? Narahari, singing his fame, counts himself fortunate.

*Raga dhanashi*

**1.25.** Glory, glory to Shri Ganganarayana Chakravarti, exceedingly composed and profound![^51]
The hue [of his body] steals one's composure; incomparable is the lovely sweetness of his most tender, charming body.
Ceaselessly avid for the rasa of sankirtana, absorbed in graceful dance, intoxicated with prema,
devoted to the worship of the lotus feet of Srila Narottama, he is the light of the world.
His mind is always immersed in drinking the nectar of the deeds of Shri Chaitanya-chandra; he is magnanimous.
The enchanting Deity of Shri Govinda is the treasure of his life, the support of his breath.
Supremely merciful, a friend of the lowly, mighty, removing the darkness of suffering —
there is no power to describe all his wondrous, renowned fame, which gives happiness to the servant Narahari.

*Raga suhai*

**1.26.** Glory, glory to Rasika Murari, the fine relisher of rasa,
merciful, shattering the impurity of Kali, whose spotless virtues steal people's minds![^52] *(Refrain)*
Mighty, worshipable, most wondrous, revealer of bhakti, giver of happiness, composed,
brimming with prema — his charming body flashes brilliantly like gold.
In his mind he contemplates the feet of Shyamananda; every moment he drinks the rasa of sankirtana;
his all-in-all is Gaurachandra; what can I say? He knows nothing else even in dreams.
His wondrous kirtana the best of poets in the three worlds acclaim as charming and incomparable.
His character is most magnanimous and lovely — fallen Ghanashyama cannot grasp it even a little.

*Vilapa-chhandah*

**1.27.** Who can describe the so incomparable deeds of Gaura's associates, oh!
Known to the world, especially boundless is their glory — a spotless ornament, oh!
Destroyers of hosts of calamities, excellent, they are an ever-new abode of virtues, oh!
The root of all auspiciousness, full of happiness, of supreme sweetness — are their names, oh!

*Shobha-chhandah*

**1.28.** Now forgive my offence: in naming [some] before and [others] after, I am in great fear.[^53]
Seeing me utterly devoid of bhakti, wretched, afflicted with sorrow, show mercy, O benefactors!
Fallen here on the ground, night and day, with joined palms I bow down [to you].
Reveal here your soft, cool feet [to quench my] burning, says Ghanashyama.

*Kanta-chhandah*

**1.29.** Companions of Gaura, supreme bestowers of good,
removers of the world's sorrow, possessors of incomparable virtues!
How full of happiness is [their] love, so renowned!
How can a poet describe [it]? All [of it] is full of nectar.

*Drutagati-chhandah*

**1.30.** The Vedas, the shrutis, the itihasas and the rest
sing [their] spotless deeds in many ways.
The Lord is subject to His devotees — their virtues are proclaimed [everywhere].
Listen, listen, O mind, my friend, again and again.

*Vilasa-chhandah*

**1.31.** Giving up other desires, ceaselessly sing the host of Gaura's virtues, oh!
Every moment run to the lovely, most cooling lotus feet, oh!
Give up, O mind, this pride [of a knower] of the Vedas: pride destroys all happiness.
To the Name, the virtues, the glory and the deeds there is no end at all.[^54]

*Parvati-chhandah*

**1.32.** The devotees of Shri Gaura are endless in number, eternal, infinite; seeing no end to them, my heart is full of grief and humility — yet their wondrous, charming, manifold deeds, which have spread [through the world] to destroy the ailments of mind and body of the world's people, are inaccessible to others — and still, to describe them even a little, there is great eagerness in my heart.
On Mount Kailasa, amid his attendants, Shambhu with Parvati, overwhelmed [with feeling], abode of auspiciousness, most charming, beautifully sings their glory; [there sound] the horn and other [instruments], his beloved damaru, strains of music; [Shiva's] huge body in lovely poses, with incomparable joy, with mighty waves [of feeling] in his breast — by his dance he steals the minds of people.
How wondrous! In Amaravati the gods, and with them the devarshi Narada and others — unfailingly, with great elation in their hearts; on his throne Indra, maddened by telling their virtues, wishes his whole body to become mouths, and his thousand eyes, shedding tears, are like a cluster of reddish lotuses, oh![^55]
Brimming with bliss, surrounded by his attendants, beautifully resplendent, with wondrous, irrepressible eagerness, Brahma through all eight watches without pause drinks the nectar of their names; the recitation of the Vedas [is to him] like a dream, the service of his own chosen [Deity] is forgotten. What can insignificant Narahari describe, oh![^56]

*Revati-chhandah*

**1.33.** These associates of Gaura are eternal; together with the ever-new Lord — their eternal pastimes, oh!
And eternal is the land of the city of Sri Nadia; there is their eternal dwelling, oh!
Listen, O wise one:[^57] here there is nothing non-eternal — all is eternal, eternal, oh!
Whoever does not believe this is the greatest fool, [says] Ghanashyama, oh!

*Suvadani-chhandah*

**1.34.** Glory, glory to the best in the world — the household of the Lord of my life!
Well known is their boundless mercy; at their feet I make [my] humble plea.

*Revati-chhandah*

**1.35.** O excellent associates of Gaura, merciful, removing the fever of worldly existence, oceans of virtue, oh!
Hearing of your boundless greatness in the world, [my] heart rejoices, O friends of the humble!
Now, forgiving all [my] offences, grant [me] to dwell at your feet forever, oh!
Says the servant Narahari, utterly shameless: banish [my other] hopes, oh![^58]

*Dvipadi-chhandah*

**1.36.** My hope is the service of [your] lotus feet: having made [me your] servant, acknowledge [me];[^59] the world sings your host of lovely virtues —
hearing [it] unceasingly, I find joy, oh!
Narahari's heart is most crooked, filled with a load of sins; knowing for certain that he is fallen, grant [him] —
may the greatness of the highest bhakti be fully manifest [in him], oh!

*Savitri-chhandah*

**1.37.** O wise ones, I bow down at [your] feet.
I am utterly ignorant of how to make [my] petition [to you].
Not even a beast is equal to me; [and yet this] grievous life does not depart.[^60]
There is no sinner in the world like me.

*Dvipadi-chhandah*

**1.38.** Let me tell a little about myself: in the blessed land of Gauda, on the bank of the river of the gods
I lived; I was born in a brahmana family; [my] good father is Jagannatha, a Vaishnava dear [to all]; the two names given [to me] —
Narahari and Ghanashyama — so [I] am known. But though, on the advice of my friends,
I took shelter in the eternal Vraja-bhumi, utter deceit and crookedness never leave [me].[^61]
And what more shall I say? [My] heart is crooked, hard as wood, nourished by tormenting malice; there is no nobility in me;
[I am] made of vices, a plain rogue, insolent, persistent in offences, most sinful, ruined, a cheat, thoroughly, utterly
fallen, absorbed in [evil] undertakings, base, self-satisfied, ruled by the [six] enemies, greedy for tastes, giving pain to the good, cruel,
wicked, always absorbed in the objects of the senses.[^62]

*Komala-chhandah*

**1.39.** Listen, what more shall I say, my father![^63] The threefold fever burns [my] heart.
In the world no one is mine; now I take refuge in you.

*Karunavati-chhandah*

**1.40.** The people of this world call you the abode of happiness;
your fame is well known, [your] host of virtues incomparable.
Now let this lovely name become true and fruitful:
show abundant mercy to me — a wretched sinner!

*Raga pathamanjari*

**1.41.** Listen, listen, you who wish to hear: my human birth, which gives happiness, has passed in vain.
Utterly wicked-minded, base, devoid of virtues, a traveller on the path of sin, absorbed in the objects of the senses,
never did I wish for the company of the good; amusing myself with bad people, I took sorrow for happiness.
Kama and anger have made me their servant; I do not [even] know that terrible Kala is near.[^64]
What shall I say? Quite unexpectedly the incomparable virtues of the son of Shachi fell upon my ears —
He who, descending to deliver the fallen, cast the lovely net of Harinama.[^65]
Moreover He made a vow: "I will deprive no one" — the crest-jewel of givers, the remover of people's sorrow.
And I came to believe: the Lord will surely show mercy, mindful of His own honour.
A great longing grew in me: to make His lovely pastimes a necklace and wear [it] on my neck.
Is that possible? [Yet] like a dwarf thinking to catch the moon, eagerness arises [in me].
Seeing me, shameless, the rasikas joyfully gave [me] permission — and I rejoiced.
I compose the *Gaura-charita-chintamani* — contemplate [it] ever, says Narahari Dasa.[^66]

*Taruni-chhandah*

**1.42.** I compose this book; if it remains unfinished,
I will not worry: [people] will laugh — let everyone laugh at me.
[It is] a supreme, most rare treasure: from a little, great gain.
If [you] show mercy, then my lot will be fulfilled.
Listen, I say for certain: what is too wondrous for mercy?
The lame leaps over a mountain, the blind paints a picture;
a dwarf catches the moon, a pauper [becomes] a king — know this;
a small bird dries up the ocean, thinking it little;
the mute [becomes] eloquent, an ant carries [Mount] Meru;
a feeble beast many times takes away the pride of the lion;
a firefly shines brighter than the moon.
Seeing such a path of mercy, [I hold to] my hope.[^67]

*Shobha-chhandah*

**1.43.** Listen, skilled people: the poets of old, one after another, composed the lovely, excellent deeds of Gaura —
wondrous poems, ever new; hearing them all, the three worlds become pure.
Though my intelligence is very small, still my mind feels great joy.
For your laughter I, like an utter jester, put on display all kinds of antics.[^68]

*Bhadravati-chhandah*

**1.44.** But, [thinking] of this poem, I lose resolve and grieve.[^69]
[Yet] I know: in good fame even a flaw is a fine ornament.
However much care I take, [it] does not come out flawless:
without flaws [verse] does not flow — so say the poets.

*Kalavati-chhandah*

**1.45.** If you say: "Why, having no ability, do you indulge in a heavy display of words?
It is in vain: who, leaving the Ganga, will drink water from a well?" —
then hear my word on this and accept [it], though to some [it] may seem strange:
that [well] water becomes pure when the shila of the Lord is bathed in it.[^70]

*Anandavardhini-chhandah*

**1.46.** Though my speech is very loose, full of many faults,
still it is rich with the glory of the Lord — like the water of the Ganga.
The good, pure in mind, having purified [it], find happiness;
the wicked revile [it] — and receive the fruit befitting their nature.

**1.47.** Hearing the evidence "tad-vag-visargo…" and the rest,[^71]
I rejoiced in heart, was freed from fear and was again firm [in resolve]:
Gaura, the pastimes of Gauranga and the abode of Gaura — these three —
are truly one; with ardent longing I sing [of them] unceasingly — listen!

*Padmavati-chhandah*

**1.48.** That same Moon of Vraja is the Moon of Nadia: worship [Him], all!
The same pastimes are eternal; the eternal pastimes [go on] even now.
That same Vrindavana is the city of Sri Navadvipa.
The servant Narahari, ever devoted [to this], holds [it] in meditation in his heart.[^72]

*Raga suhai*

**1.49.** Glory, glory to Sri Nadia, the abode of happiness,
where wondrously, in joy, dwell [people of] the four ashramas,
where day after day there are incomparable festivals! *(Refrain)*
The eight siddhis, the nine treasures and the rest constantly go about to every house like servants.
Dharma, artha, kama and moksha no one [there] counts for anything — [they] are laughed at.
Mighty, destroying the threefold miseries, the ninefold bhakti shines unceasingly.
There, day and night, the moving and non-moving [beings], full of pure prema, ever remain in rapture.
Houses of many kinds gleam; the pure city is encircled by the white, lovely waters of the river of the gods —
as if a garland of fresh kunda flowers or of pearls, as if, one would think, a crescent moon were rising.
[Its] beauty is ever new, like that of Vrindavana; the six seasons serve [it], lush are [its] lands to the horizon.
Lovely is its great glory, spread over the earth: by the power of [merely] its name sins come to an end.
With the gods, the best of the gods, Hara and the Four-faced One hold [it] in meditation in their hearts with boundless joy.
Says Ghanashyama: that village of the Lord and His associates — when shall I dwell in that land?[^73]

*Hemadandaka-chhandah*

**1.50.** In the city of Sri Nadia — the eternal root of happiness, the son of Shachi, the beautiful youthful Gaura;
with special pastimes He greatly delights minds and shatters sorrow — *ã!*
To Shesha, the lord of the gods, Mahesha, the Four-faced One and the others — His own neighbours —
He ceaselessly gives happiness; they describe [Him], lovely, surpassing heaps of the nectar of rasa — *ã!*[^74]
On mountains, in caves, on the banks of holy rivers, at tirthas, in forests — there the best of munis meditate [on Him];
in their hearts is an incomparable festival, and all other meditation is forgotten.
The singers of the assembly of the gods, skilled, with vinas, sing [of Him] beautifully, ever anew,
charmingly — and still there is no end [to it]: [His glory] fills the quarters, shining and vast.

*Brihaddvipadi-chhandah*

**1.51.** Taste, taste, O people, the excellent, supremely intoxicating [nectar] that shatters heaps of torments — the charming, wondrous pastimes of Shrila Gaura in the eight periods [of the day]: by merely hearing them once the heart is filled with delight —
drink this nectar always in the company of rasikas.[^75]
The book is very accessible, concise, charming in its order, following the ancients; its speech is manifold,
interwoven of the local language and Sanskrit, most pleasing, not at variance with the books on music and others;
in accordance with [them] I have, with great delight, composed the sweetest metres, full of bliss.

*Dvipatha-chhandah*

**1.52.** I have composed metres of many kinds — here [they] are distinguished by matras and by syllables.
To hear them is a great delight to the ear; they dispel the sorrow of poets.
Having looked at the exposition of their characteristics in [the work of] Pingala and in many [other] books,
or in the excellent Sanskrit book *Chhandah-samudra* — examine [them].[^76]

*Lalitagati-chhandah*

**1.53.** With joy in my heart I have composed metres dear to the mind and heart,
those that the Lord of serpents propagated — [in them] the virtues of Gaura are to be described in words.[^77]

*Tvaritagati-chhandah*

**1.54.** Compose, O poets, with great joy in your minds —
not the least trace of sorrow will remain; all will be crowned with success.

*Kundavalli-chhandah*

**1.55.** As Gaura is the beautiful crest-jewel of all avataras,
so His deeds steal the minds of the virtuous.
Here there is only an indication of the direction: [more] is beyond my reach.
Let the sadhakas, according to their own realization, set it out more fully.

*Madhumati-chhandah*

**1.56.** This charming *Gaura-charita-chintamani* is a most precious jewel:
with great care keep it hidden in the casket of the heart.
Do not display it where there are those who have turned away [from the Lord] — thieves who cause sorrow.
All desires will be fulfilled, says Narahari Dasa.[^78]

*Vallavi-chhandah*

**1.57.** Listen, listen, O virtuous ones, [to the deeds] up to the end of the night —
to the incomparable, ever-new deeds; contemplate [them] in the heart without cease.
Great joy will arise, and all sloth will be broken.
Says Dasa Ghanashyama: but I have remained aside.[^79]

*Malati-chhandah*

**1.58.** Listen, listen, dear associates of Gaura!
Again I entreat you, joining my palms:
the pastimes of the eight periods, full of rasa —
fulfil the great hope of my heart!

*[The author] states the order of the eight periods.*

*Dvipadi-chhandah*

**1.59.** The end of night, morning, forenoon, midday, afternoon,
evening, pradosha and night — [so they are] well known.
Having looked at the view of the crest-jewel of the circle of poets, a devotee of Shri Gaura,
the servant Narahari has here strung [this book] together.[^80]

*Raga pathamanjari*

**1.60.** Listen, listen, O you who wish to hear! Again and again, joining my palms, I entreat you:
the wondrous, charming descent of Gaura in the age of Kali, which even the gods can hardly behold —
every day in Shri Nadia [His] many, sweetest, utterly incomparable pastimes.
For the happiness of the sadhakas the saints happily composed many books — abodes of rasa.
My mind longs to take up their remnants, but when I reflect, my heart is full of great fear.
Understanding this, the best of rasikas cast a glance [at me]: strength increased and my heart rejoiced.
By [his] leave I shall compose a little — a beautiful description of the order of the pastimes of the eight periods;
first I shall set [them] out in the form of sutras — give your mind to this, says Ghanashyama.[^81]

*Now [the author] sets out the pastimes of the eight periods.*

*Raga todi*

**1.61.** At the end of the night Gora, overcome by sleep, lies on His bed.
There is no one who, having seen that beauty even once, could keep his composure.
At dawn, having woken, [He] sits in the courtyard surrounded by His associates;
playfully stealing the minds of the people of the world, He enters [their] hearts and abides there.
Having finished cleaning His teeth and so on, [He] bathes in the Suradhuni in blissful rapture;
at home with His own [associates] — the meal, merriment, and at the end, rest.
In the forenoon, in the homes of Shuklambara and other devotees,
overwhelmed by the rapture of prema, [He] enacts manifold pastimes.
At midday, in the midst of an enchanting flower garden,
[He is] absorbed in countless waves of merriment — and with Him the associates look splendid.
In the afternoon, putting on attire that enchants the world,
[He] wanders playfully through the town of Nadia — there is no end to [that] beauty.
In the evening [He] goes to His home — how wondrous is His conduct!
With care [He] performs the worship of the Deity and so on, pleasing His mother.
At pradosha [He] enters the house of Srivasa, His heart full of great delight;
there [He] performs wondrous deeds after the hearts of His beloved ones.
At night, having joyfully performed sankirtana with all His associates,
[He] again lies down in bliss in His own home, says the servant Narahari.[^82]

*Subhangi-chhandah*

**1.62.** What more shall I say? The world is filled
with the most spotless deeds of Gaura.
Carefully and beautifully describing them all,
O poets, make your poetry fruitful.

*Thus [ends] the first kirana of the Shrimad Gaura-charita-chintamani, called "The Description of the Sutras and Other Matters", in the mangalacharana.*[^83]

[^1]: *Sri Gaura-charita-chintamani* ("The Chintamani Jewel of Gaura's Deeds") is a poem by Narahari Chakravarti (also known as Ghanashyama Dasa; 18th century), the author of *Bhakti-ratnakara* and *Narottama-vilasa*. It describes the ashtakaliya-lila of Sri Gaura — His pastimes in Navadvipa through the eight periods of the day — in "kiranas" ("rays") composed in verses of many metres and in padas set to ragas. Translated from the edition of Haridasa Dasa (Navadvipa, Gauranga year 461 / 1947), "first part"; the manuscript breaks off. The title of the kirana is taken from its colophon: "…mangalacharane sutradi-varnanam nama prathamah kiranah".
[^2]: A Sanskrit verse of benediction, unnumbered in the edition. "Gaura-Govinda" — Gaura, who is Govinda (Krishna) Himself.
[^3]: Prabandha — a form of old classical song; "sutra-prabandha" is the edition's term (its exact sense is not established). Karnata is a raga. The verse is built as a chain: the end of each line is taken up by the beginning of the next ("…bhaya-taraka / taraka-pati…", "…maha-mana-haraka / haraka-damba…", "…manava-vrinda / manava-vrinda…"). "Lord of the stars" (taraka-pati) — the moon.
[^4]: Two lines of solmization (sargam: sa, ri, ga, ma, pa, dha, ni) — the sound pattern of the melody; given in transliteration. Some syllables are unclear (the sign for "ri" is printed in two shapes; the end of the first line — *pamma*?).
[^5]: Reading unclear: *prathita* ("renowned"); it could also be read *praṇita*, *praṇamita* ("honoured with obeisances").
[^6]: Cf. the well-known verse on the guru: "ajñāna-timirāndhasya jñānāñjana-śalākayā…" ("…who opened the eyes blinded by the darkness of ignorance with the salve-stick of knowledge"). This verse carries two signatures of the author: "Ghanashyama" and "Narahari Dasa".
[^7]: Shamana — Yama, the lord of punishment.
[^8]: Reading unclear: *vapu rocana* ("radiant in form"; possibly *vapu mocana*). "Eight watches" (*vasu-yāmā*, lit. "eight yamas", i.e. the whole day and night) — a hint at the eight periods of the day that the poem will describe.

[^14]: Kuvera Acharya (Kuvera Pandita) and Nabha (Nabha-devi) are the father and mother of Advaita Acharya; Achyuta is Achyutananda, His son; Sita is Sita Thakurani, His consort.
[^15]: Srinivasa Pandita is Srivasa Pandita. Madhava is Madhava Mishra, the father of Gadadhara Pandita.
[^16]: Probably Vishnu Puri and Keshava Bharati (the Lord's sannyasa-guru); the line also allows the division "Vishnu-Bharati, Keshava".
[^17]: The edition prints "…madhusūdanānanda-sadanā॥" and then repeats "sadanā॥" at the start of the next typeset line — a repetition at the line break, an obvious misprint; translated once.

[^33]: Krishnadasa Kaviraja is the author of the *Chaitanya-charitamrita*; "the lake of Radhika" is Radha-kunda, where he lived. "Gaura-Govinda" — Gaura, who is Govinda Himself (cf. note 2 to the benediction at the beginning of the kirana).
[^34]: Gadadhara Bhatta is a Vrindavana reciter of the *Bhagavata* and poet (16th century). The signature "Ghanashyama" is the author's name.

[^37]: Gatigovinda is the son of Srinivasa Acharya.

[^40]: "The Lord" — *pahum̐* ("Prabhu"); possibly Srinivasa Acharya, Ramakrishna's preceptor, is meant here. *kumuda caṭṭarājātmaja* is taken as "son of Kumuda Chattaraja" (kumuda is also the "night-lily"; a pun is possible). Gopijana-vallabha is Hemalata's husband. The end of the line is unclear: *nṛtyanta(?) rukhe* — translated "break into joyful dance".
[^41]: Chaitanya Dasa and Gatigovinda — sons of Srinivasa Acharya (on Gatigovinda, see 1.20). Reading unclear: *bhrāta yattu(?) tat-putra* — taken as "brothers…, and their sons"; *su?alacandra* — probably Subalachandra. Which of the five is whose son is not clear from the text.
[^42]: Sense unclear: *anubhava nahu marma* — lit. "realization — no essence (depth)"; perhaps: "[others] cannot fathom the depth of his realization".
[^43]: Both lines begin with ellipsis dots in the edition — a lacuna (probably damage in the manuscript). The surviving *…rāma caraṇacita cora* is taken as "…Ramacharana, stealer of hearts" (*rāmacaraṇa citacora*); Ramacharana Chakravarti was a disciple of Srinivasa Acharya. It can also be understood otherwise: "…Rama, whose feet steal hearts".
[^44]: Shrimat Vyasa — probably Vyasacharya (Vyasa Chakravarti), a disciple of Srinivasa Acharya. The scan reads *śrīmadvyāsa* (the OCR gives *śrīmaddāsa*).
[^45]: All those named in 1.22 are disciples of Srinivasa Acharya: Ramachandra Kaviraja and his brother Govinda Kaviraja (the poet Govindadasa), Karnapura, Nrisimha, Bhagavan (Kavirajas), Gokula, Vallavikanta, Gopiramana, Shyamadasa Chakravarti and others. Ghataka is a family title and also means "matchmaker, arranger of meetings"; *ghaṭaka ghaṭanākṛta* is a pun: Rupa "arranges the meeting" of the Divine Couple in contemplation.
[^46]: Vira Hambira — the king of Vishnupura, a disciple of Srinivasa Acharya.
[^47]: Hariram Acharya and Ramakrishna Acharya (1.24) were brothers, disciples respectively of Ramachandra Kaviraja and of Ganganarayana Chakravarti (1.25), of Narottama's circle.
[^48]: Reading unclear: *gati thora* ("slow gait"?).
[^49]: *śrī-śrī-kṛṣṇarāya sañjīvana* — probably the Deity Krishna-raya whom Hariram served; the sense of "life-giving" (*sañjīvana*) is unclear: perhaps "he is the life of Krishna-raya".
[^50]: Shri Manmohana-raya (*śrīmanmohanarāya*) — the Deity whom Ramakrishna served; it can also be read "Shrimat Mohana-raya". "Humbling the pride of the arrogant" — reading unclear: *durmada(?)-mada-hārī*.
[^51]: Ganganarayana Chakravarti — a disciple of Narottama Thakura; Shri Govinda — the Deity he served.
[^52]: Rasika Murari — Rasikananda (Murari), the chief disciple of Shyamananda.
[^53]: Cf. 1.12: the author names the Lord's associates without keeping the order of seniority and asks forgiveness for this fault.
[^54]: Reading unclear: *vedā* (it may also be *khedā* — "give up, O mind, sorrow"); the last word of the stanza is *goai* (possibly *poai*, "one does not find").
[^55]: Reading unclear: *indranibha(?)* — taken as "Indra" (possibly *indra nita*, "Indra constantly"). Indra is thousand-eyed.
[^56]: "Eight watches" (*aṣṭa-yāma*) — the whole day and night. Reading unclear: *uddhata* ("irrepressible"; possibly *unnata*).
[^57]: Reading unclear: *su?āna* (the middle letter is damaged) — probably *sujāna* ("O wise one").
[^58]: *nirvāsana karaha āśa* — understood as "banish [other] hopes" (other than dwelling at the feet of Gaura's associates); it may also be understood as: "[Narahari], free of [other] desires (*nirvāsana*) — fulfil [his] hope."
[^59]: Reading unclear: *bhāntu(?)jānaye*; translated by sense (*jānaye* — "know, acknowledge").
[^60]: Reading and sense unclear: *asu viṣama na gate* — lit. "life (*asu*), grievous, has not gone"?
[^61]: Narahari's account of himself: from Gauda (Bengal), from the bank of the Ganga ("the river of the gods"), of a brahmana family; his father is Jagannatha (by tradition a disciple of Vishvanatha Chakravarti); his two names are Narahari and Ghanashyama (the stanzas of the poem are signed with them). Later he lived in Vraja.
[^62]: A long list of self-deprecating epithets. Reading unclear: *nati(?) sauṣṭhava* (understood as *nahi sauṣṭhava* — "there is no nobility"); *aguṇa sṛṣṭa(?)* ("made of vices"); *ceṣṭāti?viṣṭa* (probably *ceṣṭābhiniviṣṭa* — "absorbed in [evil] undertakings"); *hṛṣṭa* ("self-satisfied"?); *ripuva?ṣṭa* (understood as "ruled by the [six] enemies" — kama, anger, greed, illusion, pride and envy); *rasādhika* ("greedy for tastes").
[^63]: *bāpa* ("father") is an affectionate form of address. The addressee of 1.39–1.40 (singular: *tora*, *tohe*) is not named; from the context, the host of Gaura's associates (cf. 1.33–1.37) or the Lord Himself.
[^64]: Kala — Time, which carries life away.
[^65]: Reading: *bhārala*; understood as *ḍārala* ("cast, threw").
[^66]: Here the author names his poem; in the edition the title is set in bold. A play on words: *cintāmaṇi* (the wish-fulfilling chintamani gem) — *cintaha* ("contemplate").
[^67]: Cf. the well-known shloka *mūkaṁ karoti vācālaṁ paṅguṁ laṅghayate girim…* ("Whose mercy makes the mute eloquent and enables the lame to leap over a mountain…") — Shridhara Svami's mangalacharana to the *Bhagavata*, quoted in CC Madhya 17. "A small bird dries up the ocean" is probably an allusion to the well-known story of the little bird (the tittibha) that bailed out the ocean to recover its eggs.
[^68]: *sughara* (cf. Hindi *sughaṛ*) — "skilled, accomplished". *bhaṇḍa* — a jester, buffoon.
[^69]: The sense of the line is unclear: *kintu iha kāvya mati teji duḥkha māni* — lit. "but [about] this poem — giving up resolve (*mati*), I grieve."
[^70]: The shila is the Shalagrama-shila, the Deity of the Lord; water in which the Deity has been bathed becomes holy.
[^71]: *Bhag.* 1.5.11: *tad-vāg-visargo janatāgha-viplavo…* — "That creation of words which destroys the sins of people, in which — even though each of its verses is imperfect — the names of the Unlimited, marked with His glory, [resound], the sadhus hear, sing and accept." In the edition this stanza is preceded by a dividing rule; no metre is named.
[^72]: "The Moon of Vraja" is Krishna, "the Moon of Nadia" is Gaura. Cf. the Gaudiya Vaishnava teaching of the identity of Gaura and Krishna, of Navadvipa and Vrindavana.
[^73]: The eight siddhis (mystic perfections) and the nine treasures (nava-nidhi, the treasures of Kuvera) serve the residents of Nadia, while the four goals of life (dharma, artha, kama, moksha) are insignificant to them. "The river of the gods" is the Ganga (Suradhuni). "The best of the gods" is Indra; Hara is Shiva; the Four-faced One is Brahma. Reading unclear: *rasata* (line 2; "in joy"). *gā̃o* is understood as "village" (it may also be understood as "singing").
[^74]: *ã* at the end of lines 2 and 4 is a sung syllable (filling out the metre), given in transliteration. Shesha, Indra ("the lord of the gods"), Mahesha (Shiva) and Brahma ("the Four-faced One") are called the Lord's "neighbours" (*prativāsī*). *sugañjanu* is understood as "surpassing" (lit. "putting to shame"). Reading unclear: *antha* (line 6) — understood as *anya* ("other").
[^75]: Reading unclear: *pravara* ("excellent"; it may also be *gabara*). "Eight periods" (*aṣṭa-kālīya*) — the eight parts of the day of the ashtakaliya-lila (listed in 1.59 and 1.61). Stanzas 1.51–1.58 concern the book itself and its metres. "Interwoven of the local language and Sanskrit" — the poem is written in Bengali and Brajabuli with Sanskrit shlokas.
[^76]: "Here" — the edition has *ina (iha)*: the editor's correction in brackets. A matra is a mora (metres counted by quantities — *mātrā-vṛtta*); "by syllables" — *varṇa-vṛtta*. Pingala — the ancient author of the *Chhandah-sutra*, founder of Indian metrics. *Chhandah-samudra* ("The Ocean of Metres") — Narahari Chakravarti's own Sanskrit treatise on metrics.
[^77]: The Lord of serpents (*phaṇipati*) — Pingala, whom tradition honours as an incarnation of Shesha-naga (cf. 1.52). "To be described" — the edition has *liya*, with the editor's gloss *(varaṇanīya)*, "to be described", in brackets.
[^78]: The chintamani is the wish-fulfilling jewel (cf. note 66); hence "all desires will be fulfilled". Those who have turned away (*vimukha*) — those averse to the Lord.
[^79]: "Up to the end of the night" (*niśi-śeṣa pariyanta*) — probably the whole cycle of pastimes, up to the nishanta. The last line is unclear: *ima rahalu hama bāma* — lit. "thus I remained aside (contrary)"; taken as the author's word of humility.
[^80]: The eight periods (in Sanskrit): *rātryanta* (= nishanta, the end of night), *prātaḥ*, *pūrvāhṇa*, *madhyāhna*, *aparāhṇa*, *sāyam*, *pradoṣa* (the first part of the night, after dusk), *niśā*. "Well known" (*suprathita*) and "strung together" (*grathita*) are a play on words. Who is called "the crest-jewel of the circle of poets" (*kavi-cakra-cūḍāmaṇi*) is not clear from the text; possibly a pun on "chakra" / Chakravarti (Vishvanatha Chakravarti? cf. note 61) — only a conjecture.
[^81]: Reading unclear: *-darśana* (letters damaged). "Remnants" (*avaśeṣa*) — what the saintly authors left undescribed (and also the "remnants" of their meal, prasada). The best of rasikas — probably the author's preceptor. "Beautiful" — *lalāma*: in print it looks like *lalāna*; read by the rhyme (*-dhāma, -anupāma, Ghanaśyāma*). "In the form of sutras" — the brief statement follows in 1.61; the detailed one from kirana 2 onward.
[^82]: The names of the periods at the beginning of the couplets of 1.61 are set in bold in the edition. Shuklambara — Shuklambara Brahmachari, the Lord's associate in Navadvipa; Srivasa (Srivasa Pandita) — the night kirtana was held in his house. The Suradhuni is the Ganga.
