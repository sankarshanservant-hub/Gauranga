# Kirana 17. Again — the Morning Period[^1]

*Morning: the people of Nadiya hurry to Shachi's house, where Gaura shines among His associates; together with them the Ganga and the Yamuna come in secret, and the Yamuna asks the Ganga to tell of the pastimes of Gaura and Vishnupriya by day and by night; the Ganga glorifies Gaura and tells in order of the eight periods of the day, beginning with nishanta — of how Gaura and Vishnupriya wake at the end of the night and at dawn*

*Again — the morning period.*

*Raga vibhasha*

**17.1.** At dawn, in His own incomparable courtyard, Gora, full of merriment, is at play.
On all sides are the hosts of His dear associates; seeing the Lord's face, they are absorbed in bliss.
The night has passed: looking [that way], smiling, floating in happiness, all the people of Nadiya,
leaving their own homes, go to the house of Sri Shachi with graceful gait — with what delight!
Memory of the body is far away, they are greatly stirred — who knows the ways of this anuraga?
Entering the house with many graceful movements, seeing that beauty, they lose their composure.
Together with them the Ganga and the Yamuna secretly float in the ocean of the nectar of Gora's beauty.
Narahari gives himself up for the love of the two; how much the two say to each other![^2]

*The Yamuna says — raga bhairava*

**17.2.** The Yamuna, in great joy of heart, tenderly taking the Suradhuni by the hand,
softly, softly speaks sweet words, showing [her] honour in every way:
"You are most experienced, you know rasa, you are blessed — who could find a comparison for you?
I long to hear [it] from your lips — oh, do not be the least bit hesitant in your heart:
regarding me as your own, tell me in private of the deeds of Gaurangachanda;
relating in order [His] pastimes with Vishnupriya by day and by night, dispel my longing."
Hearing [this], the Suradhuni, after expressing humility, modesty and so on, with great joy in her heart
lovingly tells the Yamuna the most charming pastimes of Narahari's Lord.[^3]

*The Ganga says — raga vibhasha*

**17.3.** "Listen, listen, O playful Yamuna, to the lovely deeds of the Moon of Nadiya!
With His beauty and qualities He enchants the minds of all: in the three worlds no one keeps composure.
All the delights that are in Nadiya — not even Shesha, Shiva or Vidhi can describe them.
All the wishes He fulfils in secret He reveals to no one except those who know [His] heart.
Moment by moment He goes about in the fresh manner of a nagara — how can His attire and ornaments be described?
All the women of good families in Nadiya worship [Him] — and yet they cannot see [Him] near.
If one of them, on some pretext, sees [Him] even once, she gives Him her life and youth.
Narahari's Lord is the embodiment of love; for love's sake, what will one not do?"[^4]

*Now — the eight periods [of the day].*

*Raga bhairava*

**17.4.** "O Yamuna, this Moon of Nadiya is the rasa of love, a treasury of rasa.
Who equals Sri Vishnupriya in qualities? Seeing her beauty, Rama is ashamed.
The secret heart of each is known only to the two of Them; neither lives without the Other.
All the pastimes of the Two by day and by night — who could tell them with one mouth?
Narahari is overjoyed at this. In order I shall tell something of the ways of [Their] pastimes."[^5]

*Of these, first — nishanta.*

*Raga bhairava*

**17.5.** At the end of the night Gora, avid for rasa, the crown jewel of rasikas, full of rasa,
plays with Devi Vishnupriya on a charming new bed.
How [wondrous] in that rest — body blended with body — the beauty lights up the three worlds,
like a golden tree encircled by a golden creeper, like a garland of champaka entwined with lightning.
Knowing the time has come, a dear maidservant arrives and, smiling and smiling, puts her face to the little window.
Gazing and gazing at the sweetness of [Their] rest, her heart overflows with great joy.
To wake [Them] she utters lovely words, hearing which no one can keep composure.
Narahari's Lord, waking, opens His eyes a little — how steeped in drowsiness they are![^6]

*Raga bhairava*

**17.6.** The Moon of Nadiya, the rasika-nagara Gora, the jewel of virtues, at the end of the night,
giving up sleep, His heart thrilled with ecstasy, gazes at Vishnupriya's face.
With great care waking His moon-faced beloved, He takes her in His arms.
She, with drowsy eyes, seeing the face of her beloved, dear as life, smiles and floats in tears of bliss.
They speak words full of rasa to each other, arranging their attire in lovely fashion.
They place tambula in each other's mouths — what a sweet custom! — composure is not held.
Lip pressed to lip, slowly, playfully, They sink back again onto that bed.
Narahari again tries to wake [Them], yet even so They do not give up Their drowsiness.[^7]

*Now — the morning.*

*Raga lalita*

**17.7.** Gora, the jewel of virtues, waking at dawn, cannot hold His composure;
filling His eyes, He gazes at the face of the moon-faced one and wakes her in a sweet manner.
In the absorption of that rasa, speaking softly, softly, how tenderly He treats her!
Arranging His garment, smiling, the king of nagaras leaves the bed.
Knowing that His beloved's heart is troubled, embracing [her] again and again,
He goes out of the chamber slowly, slowly, carefully holding His composure.
Turning, He looks again from the corner of His eye — what sweet beauty!
The deeds of Narahari's Lord, the crown of rasikas, entice the world.[^8]

*Raga vibhasha*

**17.8.** At the hour of dawn Gora, full of rasa, floating in rasa, comes and sits down [in His place];
there His dear servant, rejoicing, brings fragrant water, filling a pitcher, and offers [it].
Having bowed to His elders, having finished the morning rites, surrounded by His associates,
looking at Gadadhara's face, He laughs — with what delight! — telling of former pastimes.
Mukunda and Madhava, in sweet ragas, pour out in song the nectar of those deeds;
hearing [it], He tries to keep His composure — and yet there is no steadiness in His heart.
Seeing these movements, someone says with what merriment: "This Gora is like the nagara of Gokula!"
Someone says: "This Lord of Narahari is the thief of the lives of the nagaris of Nadiya!"[^9]

*Raga lalita*

**17.9.** Gora at dawn, at play,
intoxicating the minds of the people of the three worlds, sports with His dear devotees.
Ah, what a way of prema!
Devi Vishnupriya, when closeness with the body of Him who is dear as life was broken off, became greatly distressed.
Quickly leaving the bedchamber,
having finished the morning rites, she speaks with her friends of Gora's qualities — with what fresh love!
At that time someone says softly:
"Sri Sita and Malini, together with Shachi, are coming to you, floating in tears of bliss."
Hearing [this], the lovely one quickly goes forward to meet them;
out of shyness covering the moon of her face with her garment, she bows — with what grace!
Sita, Malini, Shachi — with what happiness,
giving countless blessings, holding [her] to their hearts, with what tenderness they kiss [her] face!
All [of them], having allowed [her] to bathe,
go to see Gorachanda, [dear to them] as life; seeing [Him] — with what bliss the heart overflows!
Performing many auspicious rites,
all direct the servant Ghanashyama to fetch various things.[^10]

*Raga vibhasha*

**17.10.** Devi Vishnupriya swims in an ocean of bliss, sitting in seclusion.
Fragrant oil and turmeric [she wished] to apply — with what care the maidservant rubbed [them on her]!
Bringing scented water and bathing [her], [the maid] dries [her] body with a fine towel;
dressing [her] in garments and ornaments, she beholds [her] beauty — as if streams of nectar were pouring down.
The lovely one with wagtail eyes, having finished her own tasks, carefully taking various things,
places them all in the room for the worship of the Deity; Shachi Devi, seeing this — what bliss she feels!
Together with Shrivasa's wife, in great merriment, quickly entering the kitchen,
with what skill she cooks! Narahari gives up his life for it.[^11]

*Raga todi*

**17.11.** Advaita's wife, Sita Devi, merrily entering the kitchen,
says to Malini: "Tell me, I will listen: what is Vishnupriya's cooking like?"
Malini says: "Listen, O lady! How much of all this shall I tell?
I have never heard with my ears, never seen with my eyes such an art of cooking!
She has cooked dishes — hundreds of kinds; how many shall I name? — no one knows [them].
Look here: it delights the eyes to see! I have understood: she is [Lakshmi herself].
The friends of this moon-faced one have prepared many kinds of fine offerings:
those of whom I have heard [that they were] in Nanda's house in the town of Vraja — they have appeared here."
Hearing this, Sita laughs softly, softly. At that very moment Ai came;
seeing the excellence of the cooking, she praises [it], looking tenderly at her daughter-in-law.
At that time someone very carefully says into the ears of Shachi and Sita:
"Give the Moon of Nadiya, Narahari's life, permission to bathe quickly."[^12]

*Raga amara-todi (?)*

**17.12.** Shachi Devi, filled with joy, speaks, looking at Gadadhara's face:
"This morning I saw that that body has grown thin; I understood from the signs that hunger torments [Him].
Child! Do not disregard [my] words: look, it is the time for bathing.
You know how to persuade Him; He will heed [your] words — I know.
Go quickly and tell [Him] to bathe; otherwise [my] heart is anxious."
Before Gora Gadadhara says: "Bathe together with everyone."
Nectar drips in that sweet speech; all are glad to bathe.
Softly, softly Narahari says: "It is proper to bathe at home."[^13]

*Raga todi*

**17.13.** Gora, the jewel of virtues, smiling sweetly, gave His permission, sitting in seclusion.
His dear servant, rejoicing, having anointed [Him] with fragrant oil, gazes at that moon of His face.
Knowing the time [had come], he bathed the Moon of Nadiya in a lovely manner.
How he dressed [Him] in fine garments! Seeing that beauty, who could keep composure?
Tying the ends of the thick locks, he throws [them] onto His back — like a stream of the Yamuna.
Drawing a tilaka of sandal on His forehead, he puts a necklace of jewels and pearls on His neck.
Taking Narahari by the hand, the Lord goes slowly, slowly to the room of the Deity.
Having worshipped, offering various things, from the fullness of bliss He cannot contain His heart.[^14]

*Raga turushka-todi*

**17.14.** As Gora bathed,
so all bathed and came — to see [them] gladdens the soul.
Lord Nitai, in a lovely manner,
put on blue garments and ornaments — [seeing] this, who could keep composure?
That Advaita who attracts the world;
Gadadhara, Gauri[dasa], Gadadhara [Dasa] — and what is the beauty of Shrivasa!
Yadu, Mukunda and the others likewise:
they glitter in the middle of the courtyard, as if moons were rising.
Shachi, seeing this, in the fullness of bliss
tells Gadadhara, [dear to her] as life, to bring everyone to the dining room.
Gadadhara, in sweet speech,
conveys to all the loving words of Mother Sri Shachi.
Hearing this, all of them, laughing,
say so much to one another, with what gestures, floating in an ocean of happiness!
All go together with Gadai.
At that time the servant Narahari merrily washes everyone's feet.[^15]

*Raga amara-panchama*

**17.15.** At Sri Shachi's bidding, floating in happiness, all went into the dining room
and sit down in rows — seeing [this] incomparable beauty, whose heart would not rejoice?
Sita Devi carefully serves various sweets; all eat merrily.
What a sweet playful quarrel Advaita carries on with Nitaichanda!
Gadadhara, Shrinivasa, Gadadhara Dasa, Dhananjaya, Shridhara-vatu,
Murari, Mukunda, Sadashiva, Gauridasa, Vasu and others — masters of the meal.
Shachi Devi's heart overflows with bliss at the sight of the lovely sport of the meal.
With the corner of her eye she says to Vishnupriya: "Fill a plate and give it to Malini."
Vishnupriya, arranging various dishes, rice and so on, gives [them] into Malini's hands;
hiding behind Malini, unseen, she beholds the Lord of her life.
Malini serves rice and dishes; they eat all this, which surpasses nectar.
Having finished the meal, He performs achamana; Narahari brings and offers water.[^16]

*Raga todi*

**17.16.** Gora, the jewel of virtues, with all the assembly, talks merrily of the meal.
All sit on many-coloured seats; the servants offer tambula.
Having eaten their fill, no one can remain sitting [any longer].
All, going each to his own lodging, lie down for a while.
The Lord lay down on His bed — the weariness of the meal went away.
The servants fan [Him]; Narahari serves those feet.[^17]

*Raga bhupali*

**17.17.** Shachi Devi says to Sita and Malini, [sitting] beside her, in speech sweet as nectar:
"Eat, taking all these with you; let my daughter-in-law serve — this is the wish of my heart."
Hearing [this], they reply with great joy and sat down to eat, taking Shachi with them.
Vishnupriya serves [them]; [her] heart overflows with the fullness of bliss.
Rice and many different dishes they eat, praising [them], with what merriment!
Seeing that the meal is finished, the maidservants bring and offer water.
Having rinsed her mouth, Malini quickly comes and, smiling, says to Vishnupriya:
"You eat with your own friends; now I shall serve."
Having said this, unable to restrain herself for affection, she seats [them] beautifully in a circle.
Seeing that beauty — what thrills of ecstasy in her body! — Devi Malini merrily serves [them].
The lovely one, taking the nectar of her Beloved's lips, eats, giving [it] to many of her friends.
Having finished the meal — what happiness in her heart! — she washes her face with cool water.
Going into seclusion, she takes tambula; Shachi and Sita, seeing [this], are gladdened at heart.
What will Narahari understand of this way? The morning pastimes are most charming.[^18]

*Now — the forenoon period.*

*Raga deshapala*

**17.18.** The wondrous Gora in the forenoon, leaving His bed, sits alone in seclusion;
with cool water He washes the drowsy lotuses of His eyes and the moon of His face.
With a lovely movement He looks all around; Vishnupriya, unseen, seeing this,
covering her face with her garment, laughs softly, softly; out of shyness she cannot meet [His] eyes.
When the Lord full of rasa grew restless, His glance fell on Vishnupriya;
He cannot hold [them] back: the birds of His eyes, flying up and alighting, drink the sweet nectar of [her] face.
The bodies of the Two thrill ceaselessly with ecstasy; eyes upon eyes — what play, what a manner!
May Narahari be offered up to this sport! Seeing it, some are intoxicated with merriment.[^19]

*Raga mayura*

**17.19.** Ah, what a sweet manner!
The Two, looking at each other eye to eye — the heart keeps no composure.
Vishnupriya, filled with that rasa,
quickly preparing tambula, joyfully placed [it] in a maidservant's hand.
The maidservant, coming, smiling and smiling, says:
"Your Beloved, dear as life, has sent tambula; do as is fitting."
The Moon of Nadiya, opening that little box,
at the touch of the tambula rejoices in body, covered with thrills of ecstasy.
Having eaten the tambula with what delight,
He handed the maidservant the box with the remainder — saying so many things [to her]!
The maidservant, coming to Vishnupriya,
opens that box and, telling of many things, smiles sweetly, sweetly.
When Vishnupriya with delight
puts that tambula in her mouth — how much bliss wells up! — a thrill spreads through [her] body.
What little remained of it at the end —
that, the rarest of things, smiling softly, softly, she gave to the servant Narahari.[^20]

*Raga dhanashi*

**17.20.** Vishnupriya with a friend merrily tells of the deeds of Gora.
[Her] body is pervaded with the fullness of thrills; without Gora she keeps no composure.
What the Lord will eat at midday — that she prepares with how much care!
Going to a most secluded place, she sits, putting her eyes to a lovely little window:
the Lord will come to the flower garden — how great the eagerness to see it!
What will Narahari understand of this way? Ah, a love unequalled in the world![^21]

*Raga kamoda*

**17.21.** Thinking of the deeds of Vishnupriya, charming Gora floats in an ocean of rasa.
Moment by moment, how much rises in [His] heart! Gora offers up His life to Vishnupriya's qualities.
Having become steady with how much effort, the Lord went and sat in the outer house.
There His dear associates gather. What will Narahari say of that happiness?[^22]

*Raga deshapala*

**17.22.** Gaura is graceful, loving, full of rasa; [His] deeds are the great depth of the heart.
In meeting with all His associates, dear [to Him] as life, there is supreme joy.
Likewise the host of associates rejoices, seeing the face of the Lord of their life.
Speaking what sweet words, they sit in a radiant throng, surrounding [Him] on all sides!
In the midst of the lovely assembly shines the youth, the Subduer of Manmatha.
He sways with bhava, [on His body —] abundant thrills, His lotus eyes are filled with tears.
Taking Gadadhara by the hand, He reveals the excellent rasa of Vraja.
What will the servant Narahari tell of the pastimes of the forenoon?[^23]

*Now — midday.*

*Raga shri*

**17.23.** The Moon Gaura, floating in rasa, comes and enters the flower garden at the midday hour.
The place is very cool; gently, gently a fragrant breeze blows pleasantly.
Sharis, shukas and flocks of cuckoos sing most charmingly of Gora's qualities, intoxicated with bliss.
Bees roam in swarms upon swarms and hum, as if instruments were sweetly playing.
Peacocks, spreading their tails, dance; seeing [them], no one can turn his eyes away.
Each displays its own qualities; who can describe the beauty of their varied plumage?
Blossoming trees encircled by rows of creepers — incomparable is the shade there, with what delight!
The Lord of Narahari, seated, smiles softly; abundant thrills gleam on [His] body.[^24]

*Raga suhai*

**17.24.** To Lord Gora it seemed as if He were sitting in a kunja of Vrindavana.
What a wondrous way of rasa! He looks in all directions, very restless.
Devi Vishnupriya, at the little window, with an unseen glance saw Him.
Who else will understand that new love? Without blinking she looks.
The Two, seeing each Other, keep no composure; intoxicated with that rasa, They smile softly, softly.
How much merriment grows moment by moment — how much of it can Narahari tell?[^25]

*Raga dhanashi*

**17.25.** Gaura, the youth of Vraja, the Lord who breaks the pride of Rati's husband,
plays in the garden of lovely flowers near the house, most sweet.
Some come, [others] go, beholding the Lord — an incomparable way!
At such a time Nitai and Advaita arrived there.
Sri Gadadhara and the other associates sit, surrounding [Him] on all sides.
No one's eyes blink — seeing the moon of Gaura's face.
As befits the hour, He begins to speak of former [pastimes] — a stream of nectar;
it overflowed and flowed in all directions. What will Narahari say of that joy?[^26]

*Raga saranga*

**17.26.** Lord Gora with His associates plays in the flower garden with joy in His heart.
And here Shachi, the mother of the world, speaks most sweet words to her daughter-in-law:
"Prepare and give all the food: Nimai and Nitai will eat together with everyone.
Their bodies are tormented by hunger: They ate in the morning, and now it is already midday."
Hearing [this], the lovely one floated in joy and prepared everything before her mother-in-law.
Shachi Devi, calling Ishana, quickly sent off the various foods.
Advaita, with supreme bliss in his heart, seated everyone in rows to feed them.
In what a mood Nitai, the jewel of qualities, first put kshira and butter into Gorachanda's mouth!
Advaita cannot contain his heart: he puts kshira and cream into the mouth of Nitaichanda.
At the meal great merriment arises; moment by moment the waves of the ocean of happiness rise.
To behold the sport of the meal — how Brahma and the other gods long for it in their hearts!
What will the servant Narahari say? At the midday hour — most charming pastimes.[^27]

*Now — the afternoon period.*

*Raga bhatiyali*

**17.27.** Who else will understand the secret heart of Gora? For half a moment He wanders alone in that garden.
The afternoon has come; floating in that rasa, He looks all around.
He sees Vishnupriya at the little window; with an unseen glance she saw Him.
Smiling softly, softly with the corner of her eyes — I do not know what she told Him.
That jewel among women, smiling softly, speaks with a friend — who knows of what?
The Moon of Nadiya with the rasikas again wanders through the garden together with everyone.
Seeing the beauty of the flowers — great delight! — He walks with slow gait to the bank of the Ganga.
What they say to one another, smiling — of that Narahari understands nothing.[^28]

*Raga paurabi*

**17.28.** Gora, the jewel of qualities, went slowly, slowly to the bank of the Suradhuni with happiness in His heart.
On all sides His dear associates shine — that beauty does not come to the lips to be told.
Hearing [of it] from one another, the people of Nadiya, keeping no composure, come running.
With what bliss their hearts surge as they look at the face of Gorachanda!
In the sky the celestial women with their companions, smiling at one another as if in play,
seeing that form which enchants the minds of the three worlds, float in the tears of their eyes.
The daughter of Jahnu surged up, joyful at heart in the hope of the touch of the Lord's feet.
What new, charming pastimes of the Lord of Narahari's life at the close of the day![^29]

*Now — evening.*

*Raga gauri*

**17.29.** Gora, the crown of rasikas, merrily
plays at the evening hour with His lovely dear associates.
On the most beautiful bank of the Jahnavi
having stayed for some time, He touches the cool water and goes to His own home.
With what a graceful, slow gait
Hari takes away the pride of the elephant! [Even] Manmatha keeps no composure.
Coming near His own house,
glancing unseen on all sides, He saw the Beloved of His life.
He holds firm, [yet] composure cannot be kept:
His eyes — thirsty chakora birds — fly up and alight on the moon of [her] face.
What new love is Vishnupriya's!
Not seeing the Lord of her life for even half a moment, she cannot hold [herself in her] body.
Unseen, meeting [His] eyes with her eyes,
she thrills in body; in incomparable, dense bliss her heart surges.
Narahari does not grasp the secret:
the Lord, enchanter of the world, the jewel of qualities, enters the house.[^30]

*Raga gauri*

**17.30.** Gora, full of rasa, in the delight of His heart completed the evening rites.
The associates, absorbed in prema, went each to his own home.
Shri Shachi Devi, having carefully served Vishnu, joining her palms, speaks [these] words [to Him]:
"I pray to You: keep my Vishvambhara in well-being by Your own grace.
And I say more: my beautiful Nitai obeys no one — He is very restless.
What more shall I say? I am afraid at heart: rid [Him] of these boyish ways.
My daughter-in-law Vishnupriya — sweet is her nature; I hold her dearer than life.
May no misfortune ever befall her. To whom but You shall I make this known?"
Hearing His mother's words, Gora, the jewel of qualities, standing at a distance, laughs softly.
He joyfully bows at His mother's feet; Shachi Devi gives [Him] many blessings.
When her daughter-in-law bows, [Shachi], speaking honeyed words, embraces her again and again.
May Narahari be offered up! What a new loveliness of love at the evening hour![^31]

*Now — pradosha.*

*Raga amra-panchama (?)*

**17.31.** At the hour of pradosha Gora, sitting in solitude — how sweetly! —
watches the road [by which] His dear associates [will come]; He cannot keep His composure.
At that time Gadadhara, Shrivasa and the others come with quick steps;
Advaita and Nitai — the Two have arrived; incomparable are the ways of the Two.
They sat down in rows, surrounding Gorachanda — with what happiness of heart!
Gazing at the incomparable beauty of the assembly, the eyes do not blink.
The former tales of Narahari's Lord they tell to one another —
as if a stream of nectar flows. Who can grasp such a play?[^32]

*Raga dhanashi*

**17.32.** Lord Gora sports in solitude; with His associates He floats in an ocean of happiness.
Shachi, hearing that all had come — what arose in her heart cannot be told.
Looking at the road, she says: "When will Sita Devi and Malini come?
In the afternoon they went home; the evening is passing — why have they still not come?"
At that time Sita Devi came; seeing [her] — what joy! — [Shachi] bows at [her] feet.
Seeing Malini coming, [Shachi] went forward and brought her, taking her by the hand.
When her daughter-in-law Vishnupriya bows to the Two, the Two embrace her with what tenderness!
Shachi Devi, in the delight of her heart, says softly, softly to her daughter-in-law Vishnupriya:
"The time for the meal has come." Hearing [this], Vishnupriya, smiling sweetly, says:
"All is ready — come and see." Hearing [this], all, with gladness of heart, go and see.
Sita Devi says again and again: "The time for the meal has come — why delay any longer?
Come to the meal with everyone." Hearing [this], Gadadhara goes and tells [it to] all.
All, in great delight of heart, entering the dining hall, sat down to the meal.
Sita Devi together with Malini serves [the dishes] with supreme bliss in her heart.
Having finished the merry meal and performed achamana, all go there [where they had sat] and sit down.
They partook of tambula, pleasing to the heart; how many merry plays arose — who can grasp [these] deeds?
Shachi Devi, very eager at heart, [wishes] to feed her friends Sita Devi and Malini.
The Two with their [companions], absorbed in prema, eat — what nectar! — Vishnupriya serves.
Having eaten and spoken many gentle words, Malini fed her daughter-in-law Vishnupriya.
The merry meal is charming: no one can leave Shachi's house to go home.
Sita Devi and Malini, as they gaze at Gora's face, have no composure in their hearts.
Taking leave of Shachi — as soon as they set off, Shachi's heart grew agitated.
What can Narahari say? All went to their homes with stricken hearts.[^33]

*Raga suhai*

**17.33.** What shall I say? Nothing like it is seen anywhere: Gora's happiness is the happiness in the hearts of all.
All the associates, in a lovely way, say softly, softly to the Moon of Nadiya:
"You have eaten — now drowsiness has come; without lying down the drowsiness will not pass.
If You do not lie down, discomfort will grow in the body. We will go and lie down in our own homes."
However many times they all said [this], they go, gazing at Gora's face.
Shrivasa, taking Nitai Thakura with him, went to his own home, agitated.
Advaita and the others go to their homes: without Gora they cannot hold on to life.
Some reproach the Creator: "Why did You make so wretched a night?"
Some say: "Make the sun rise — take away our sorrow right now!"
Speaking thus, absorbed in prema, they lie down and in their dreams see Gora.
Lord Gora, having sent everyone off — what became of Him! — seeing it, the heart breaks.
What will Narahari understand of this love? What a charming way at the hour of pradosha![^34]

*Now — night.*

*Raga kanada*

**17.34.** At the hour of night Gora, full of rasa, entering the lovely bedchamber —
what a sweet way! — His composure gone, fixed His eyes on the path by which Vishnupriya would come.
That young jewel among women, with joy in her heart, sitting in seclusion, adorns herself.
Gracefully she binds her hair; how [lovely] is the moon of her face, anointed with kunkuma!
Sindur on her forehead, designs in sandal and the like; how beautifully a dot of musk [shines] on her chin!
In ornaments of various jewels — what beauty! There is none like it in the world.
Putting on a blue sari — what delight grows! — she overflows with incomparable love.
She goes to the Lord, the life of Narahari; her body flashes like lightning.[^35]

*Raga dhanashi*

**17.35.** Vishnupriya [goes] to her Beloved, dear as life;
in [her] gait — what new grace! — by it the pride of many Madanas is destroyed.
Coming near — how sweetly! —
covering her face with her veil, she watches the Moon of Nadiya from the corner of her eye.
Gora, looking at His Beloved, dear as life,
comes forward, slowly, slowly, carefully taking [her] hand, tenderly leads [her].
Bringing [her] to the lovely bed of flowers,
He speaks words full of rasa; hearing [them], the fair one says nothing out of shyness.
When [They] gaze unblinking, eye to eye,
she, absorbed in that rasa, laughs softly, softly — how much merriment in her heart!
Like a very greedy bee
drinking the honey of the lotus of [her] face, He became intoxicated, His composure lost.
His body filled with abundant thrills of ecstasy;
touching the pair of golden lotuses of [her] breasts, He trembles like lightning.
Embracing [her] again and again —
Narahari offers his life to the Lord; the heart surges with bliss.[^36]

*Raga dhanashi*

**17.36.** Gora, the jewel of qualities — in how many plays! — sports with Vishnupriya.
Weariness came, Their bodies were moist with sweat; the Two fan each Other with the ends of Their cloth.
With languid bodies, with delight in Their hearts, the Two lay down in joy, in love.
The two became one body; incomparable in the world is this new love.
The radiance of the bodies of the Two flashes beautifully; ceaselessly it lights up the chamber.
Most wondrous — there is no comparison. May Narahari be offered up to the Two.[^37]

*Thus the eight periods [of the day].*

*Raga vibhasha*

**17.37.** What a wondrous play!
Whoever tells [of it, or] hears [it] even once — can he, for happiness, keep hold of [himself in his] body? *(Refrain)*
The Suradhuni, immersed in the qualities of the Lord of Vishnupriya's life,
having told much in this way, considers her own life blessed.
Hearing from the lips of the Suradhuni those lovely deeds of the Moon of Nadiya,
the Yamuna, overpowered by absorption in rasa, cannot for happiness keep her composure.
Softly, softly she says to the Suradhuni: "This Moon of Nadiya —
the life of the loving young women of Vraja, the snare of their hearts.
Ever subject to their prema and rasa, He is absorbed in the plays of rasa.
Who else will know that He is that very One — this thief of the lives of the nagaris of Nadiya?
The crown of nagaras sports in Nadiya, having become hidden."
Ah, ah! Narahari takes upon himself the misfortunes of the Lord's pastimes.[^38]

*Raga lalita-vibhasha*

**17.38.** The Yamuna, intoxicated with supreme prema, says again and again to the Jahnavi:
"O, you are most experienced! What shall I say? I have entrusted my life to you.
The words of the lovely deeds of Gauranga you have told me, regarding me as your own."
Hearing this, the Suradhuni, with sweet speech, makes a request of the Yamuna.[^39]

*The Ganga says — raga vibhasha*

**17.39.** "Listen, listen, O Yamuna, I entreat you: dispel the longing of my heart.
Be kind and tell of the former pastimes, full of rasa, of Gaurangachanda.
The young maidens of Vrajapura — with whom shall I compare them? I see no others like them.
Which of them is above all? And with whom is His love — tell me.
Shyama is the crown of the highest rasikas; His body is incomparable, [incomparable] are all His ways.
I know that He loves many; tell me, for which of them does He hold the greater love.
How do They meet each other by day and by night, in the order [of the periods], and where do They stay?
I long very much to hear all this; in this Narahari is the witness of my heart."[^40]

*Sri Yamuna answers the Ganga — raga vibhasha*

**17.40.** "Listen, listen, O fair-faced one, dear to me as life! In the three worlds you are charming and wise.
Of Her who is above all in Vrajapura I shall tell you something.
Her father is King Vrishabhanu, Her mother Kirtida, renowned in the world.
Her mother-in-law is Jatila, Her sister-in-law Kutila; Her husband is Abhimanyu, he is generous.
Her friends are Lalita, Vishakha, Chitra, Champakalata, Rangadevi, Sudevi,
Tungavidya, Indulekha and others: I see none equal to them among the wives of gods and men.
Her maidservants are Rupa-manjari, Rati-manjari and others; devoted to service, they ever float in bliss.
A stream of nectar is far [behind]: seeing Her beauty, Uma and Rati feel great shame.
Playful, the jewel among women, knowing rasa — who can tell the host of Her virtues?
Her, performing the abhisheka, the daughter of Yashoda made Queen of Vrindavana.
The daughter of the ocean and all the others long to become Her attendants.
Narahari is sold at Her feet. What shall I tell you? Above all is Radha."[^41]

*Raga vibhasha*

**17.41.** "He with whom Radha's love is — how could I know how to tell of His virtues and deeds?
His form enchants the world and puts to shame the husband of Rati; He is love embodied.
The crown of rasika nagaras, He is happy having gathered [in a heap] lovely garments (?);
at the song of His flute stones melt, trees and creepers weep, beasts and birds are distraught.
He stays fallen at His beloved's feet; He carries [her] on His shoulders — with what care!
The scolding words of His beloved He drinks as a stream of nectar — with what relish, filled with bliss!
It is known that He is wholly subject to love; in the three worlds there is nothing to compare Him with.
What more shall I say? Radha, full of rasa, has sunk [in love] for such a Shyama.
Casting off shame, [the honour of] family and fear, disregarding Her husband, She dwells in the forest.
People's slander, a terrible poison, She holds in Her heart to be nectar.
Lying down or dreaming she knows nothing [else]; without [Him] for half a moment, Her heart breaks.
The Lord of Her life is [Her] all; how, [says] Narahari, can this be conveyed through comparisons?"[^42]

*Again [the Yamuna] says — raga vibhasha*

**17.42.** "O friend, dear as life, full of joy! I am sold to your virtues.
Now listen: I shall tell with whom Shyamasundara has the greater love.
Chandravali is a beauty renowned in Vraja: by her wondrous beauty Rama is put to shame.
In the bloom of youth, a beauty who knows rasa — in virtue and good conduct she has no equal.
In a charming new kunja-temple she plays merrily with Shyama every day.
Shyama, full of rasa, is likewise enraptured [by her], subject to her love — who can grasp [this] way?
When that jewel of beauties touches [Him], He floats amid the ocean of the highest bliss;
absorbed in drinking the nectar of the moon of her face, the nagara is then aware of nothing [else].
But if at that time even a little of the fragrance of Radha's body reaches His nostrils, —
at once, leaving her, the Dark One, most restless, runs off as if gone mad.
What more shall I say? You [yourself] know in your heart for whom Kanu's love is greater.
Narahari is an offering [for the sake of] such love; however one counts [its] virtues, who can find their bottom?"[^43]

*Again [the Yamuna] says — raga todi*

**17.43.** "What shall I say, O [friend]? Incomparable in the world is the love of Radha and Madhava.
The soul is one, the bodies different: who fashioned [Them] — with what joy!
If you say, 'If the Two are one, why then is the colour of the Two different?' —
of this I shall tell you something; listen to it attentively.
There are many colours, but dark and golden have a special beauty.
Showing the world its utmost, [They] shine with a smile, captivating the eyes of the world's people.
I say further: when the restless Dark One sees playful Radha,
He, impatient, opens both arms and eagerly takes [Her] into His embrace.
If at that time adversaries suddenly see such a thing close by,
they, deceived, [take it] for a cloud with lightning: none can make it out — what fun!
I say further: that skilled poet, the Creator, to give great joy to many,
beholding the lovely radiance of dark and golden, composes (?) comparisons in many ways.
Therefore, why say much: [They] are not different — Shyama's body is fashioned of Rai's prema,
Radha's body consists of Kanu's prema: thus is the love of the Two known in the world.
What do others know of the ways of These Two? Only the rasikas know.
Whoever is deprived of this rasa, Narahari counts him the equal of a beast."[^44]

*Suradhuni (matra-vritta)*

**17.44.** "O Suradhuni! What you asked about — the beginning of that account I have told you.
Now listen, listen to the account of days and nights: I shall tell how and where the nagara met [Her]."[^45]

*On a certain day — raga paurabi*

**17.45.** The best of nagaras, the excellent one, who steals the composure of Vraja, with joy in His heart, the beloved, in the fullness of rasa,
having prepared flowers and taken the guise of a flower-seller, enters Yavatapura.
Looking at Himself again and again, covering His face with the edge of His cloth, smiling,
in a most sweet voice He calls from house to house: "Who will take a necklace? Come and take!"
Hearing a voice surpassing [the song of] the kokila, Vinodini speaks with Her friend Vishakha:
"The necklace must be wondrous; lest someone [else] take it, quickly bring the flower-seller here."
Vishakha, hearing these words, in the highest joy says to the flower-seller: "Hey, come here!"
Turning back, she brings the flower-seller into the house and says affectionately: "Sit right here."
Radhika, looking at the flower-seller, hurries [to her] (?); filled with bliss, She thinks to Herself:
"Such a flower-seller I have never seen anywhere; she must be a dweller of the city of the gods."
With such a thought in Her heart She sits near the flower-seller and says: "Come, let me see your necklace."
Hearing [this], she shows the necklace, which has no compare: its beauty enchants the minds of all.
Radhika, full of rasa, sorely afflicted by Madana, — and again Her heart thrills.
Looking at the necklace, She reflects within: "Only the one dear to me as life strings [flowers] like this."
The beauty cannot keep still and says to the flower-seller: "I feel like giving you my life.
Listen, what shall I say? Blessed, blessed are you! Whatever the price is, tell me."
The flower-seller says: "Listen, I do not say things twice, and I never lie to anyone:
I shall put this necklace on [you] and take that gajamoti pearl; and what you give when you are adorned, that I shall take afterwards."
The beauty says kindly to the flower-seller: "Take whatever you want, as you yourself say."
Hearing [this], floating in rasa, smiling a little, [the flower-seller] put the necklace round Radhika's neck.
With what care, arranging [it] on the lovely hills of [Her] breasts, she covers [them] with her hand!
At the flower-seller's touch, with delight in Her heart, the beauty at once trembles all over.
Understanding [this], [says] Narahari, all the companions, glad at heart, keep at a distance.
In the secluded chamber the Two cannot stay still: both are enraptured in the play of Ananga.[^46]

*And on another [day] — raga paurabi*

**17.46.** The best of nagaras, the moon of Vraja, taking the lovely guise of a woman, smiling,
in the fullness of rasa enters Yavatapura.
Surpassing a mass of rain clouds is the charming radiance of every limb [of His];
the garments and ornaments He has put on steal life with [His] beauty.
Radhika, seeing Him from afar, cannot turn Her eyes away even for a moment
and says to Her friend, taking [her] by the hand:
"Where has this beauty come from? Look — with her beauty she has filled [everything] with light!
Bring her here with great care."
Hearing Vinodini's agitated words, the friend, knowing the secret,
quickly goes to where that beauty is coming.
Cleverly drawing near and speaking the sweetest words,
she joyfully leads her [into the house] and brings [her] in with honour [to Radha].
The beauty comes close with delight; the jewel of women, seating her [beside Her],
says affectionately: "I have never seen you.
Today I have found boundless happiness. Tell me truly, giving up guile:
on what business and where are you going alone? Tell me."
Hearing words like nectar, the beauty is immersed in great happiness,
like a poor man who has found the greatest jewel.
Looking at the lovely moon-like face, with cunning in her mind she says:
"Listen: since (?) you have asked, I must say something.
With great eagerness I learned, as I wished, all the arts of adornment;
being young, I put boundless labour into it.
To display all this, I go about this Vrajapura,
[but] I find no such expert who could judge [my] skill.
Then a woman said to me: 'In vain you go about the town:
here [lives] the most accomplished wife of Abhimanyu.
What is Rama [beside Her] in beauty and virtue? In the world there is none equal to Her;
by the touch of Her feet the earth considers itself blessed.
There are many heroines here — how much could one tell of them! —
[but] He whom [none] can keep under her sway even for a moment,
that Shyama-moon, the most graceful (?), above whom there is no one —
He, subject to Her love, constantly goes about [around Her].
Go there, heed my words: there your skill will be honoured.'
Hearing this, I was very glad at heart.
What more shall I tell you? I came, heeding her words,
and found [everything] as she said."
Hearing these words, lovely Rai, filled in Her heart with great bliss,
says: "Adorn me, O [dear one], regarding [me] as your own."
Having received permission, the fragrant (?) [guest] rises eagerly and sits beside [Her];
a maidservant brings all the materials for the adornment.
The beauty, carefully holding her composure, beholding the sweet charm of [Her] back,
braids [Her] hair, shaming a serpent, enchanting the sages.
Joy in the hearts of both; lining [Her] eyes with lovely kajal,
she wipes [Her] face with the edge of her cloth with relish — and the beauty grows still greater.
Putting a lovely champaka [flower] on Her ear, she considers herself blessed;
placing sindur in the parting, she draws lovely designs on the forehead.
Bringing a beshar and putting [it] in [Her] nose, swinging [it], she speaks sweet words:
"There is no compare [for you]: at this, surely, Madana himself will faint."
As [she] places a lovely dot of musk on the chin, an ocean of bliss surges;
seeing this, forgetting to blink, [she] cannot turn her eyes away.
Touching the most lovely breasts to put on the bodice, [her] hand is unsteady:
the Upholder of the world cannot hold even a trace of composure.
[His] incomparable body trembles again and again; with effort [He] covers His face, turning away.
Seeing this, the friend says, putting a finger to her chin:
"What a marvel, never heard of: being a woman, with a woman
you act like this, O [dear one] — how can this be?
A wondrous adornment you have made — but what shall I say of my own friend?
Without discerning, She regards anyone as Her own.
Luckily there is no one here — is this not a great shame?
To whom shall I tell this sorrow? She never heeds any prohibition."
Hearing [this], Rai, with a smiling face, abashed, looking at Shyama,
says: "O restless one! Who would not laugh at this?"
The nagara says: "Do what is fitting: quickly bind [me] in the noose of [your] arms —
then that Ghanashyama will float in the ocean of happiness."[^47]

*And on another [day] — raga todi*

**17.47.** The Shyama-moon, avid for rasa, entering Yavata, floats in an ocean of happiness.
Under many pretexts, with a restless heart, He wanders about in the hope of seeing the king's daughter.
Not seeing [Her], His heart distraught, saying, "Where shall I find [Her]?", He is wet with the water of His eyes.
At that time a parrot, sitting on the branch of a tree, says: "Rai is on the bank of the Yamuna."
Hearing these words, feeling bliss in His heart, He swiftly goes there, where the fair one is.
Staying at a distance, He sees: Radha with Her sakhis playfully enters the water to bathe.
The beauty at that moment is wondrous — what comparison is there? — it illumines the three worlds.
Under the guise of smiles [She] rains down nectar, and Kala makes the chakoras of His eyes drink it.
He cannot steady His mind; reflecting, looking all around once more,
unseen, behind the trees and creepers, He goes and stands at another ghat upstream.
Joyfully entering the water, quickly hiding His whole body in it,
the mass of happiness floats [downstream]; only the lotus of [His] face shows above the water.
With great amusement coming near the ghat where the playful one is bathing —
suddenly seeing it, the beautiful one in Her joy cannot be still: [Her] heart flutters.
To Lalita and the other sakhis She says: "Look, a wondrous lotus has floated here!
I have never seen such a one — look, it has lit up the water of the Yamuna!
I understand in my heart: today this Kalindi, being gracious to me,
has plucked this lotus and brought it from a far country, playfully, with her own wave-hands."
Saying this, leaving the midst of Her friends, She rushes and seizes the lovely lotus with both hands.
The Nagara, slowly, slowly stretching out His arms, seized the fair one's hands, having Her near.
Pressing face to [Her] face, laughing, Shyamaraya quickly rises, taking [Her] in His arms.
The radiance of [Their] bodies glitters, as if lightning were swinging upon a cloud.
All around, the sakhis say with playful gestures: "A fine lotus the Yamuna has brought [You]!"
Hearing [this], Ghanashyama laughs again and again and gazes; the jewel of women lowers Her face in shyness.[^48]

*Now — at night. Raga gauri*

**17.48.** Shyama, the fine nagara, giver of the highest happiness, having devised a plan with Kundalata,
puts on the wondrous guise of a woman, joyful, in the hope of seeing Rai.
The chaste Kundalata, sent by Yashoda, goes to Yavata with incomparable gait;
with her goes the Beautiful One, carrying in a lovely hand a plate with some provisions.
Entering Yavata, they bow at Jatila's feet; seeing [them], with joy in her heart,
Abhimanyu's mother, taking [them] by the hand, speaks sweet words in many ways.
Kundalata, showing her cleverness there, displaying the provisions, sitting close by,
having delivered Yashomati's message and obtained permission, goes to where Rai abides.
The one full of rasa, overjoyed, smiling, looking at Kundalata,
in many ways playfully seats [her] beside Herself, eagerly holding [her] hand.
Having carefully asked for news of Her Beloved, dear as life, Rai, looking at that [companion], speaks again:
"Where did you find this new merry [girl], and why has she come with you?"
Hearing [this], Kundalata, floating in joy, says: "She lives in our neighbourhood;
this young bride is very dear [to me]; later I will introduce [her to you].
Hearing from my lips about your virtues, she longs every day to come here;
seeing her distraught, I brought [her] today; regard her as your own.
The Creator fashioned her with many virtues — I can compare her to no one in the world;
she always keeps very much hidden; she herself is skilled in work; her ways are lovely.
What more shall I say of her skill? By her touch the pains of the body and the like are dispelled.
O you who are full of happiness! Among your sakhis no one has such soft hands."
Hearing [this], Vinodini, Her heart delighted, wished that [the guest] would touch Her body;
understanding [this], Kundalata says to the dark young bride with a playful gesture, smiling slightly:
"The wish that was in your heart has borne fruit: Sri Radha has accepted you as Her own.
So with your lovely lotus hands press Her feet and sprinkle [Her] with streams of nectar."
Hearing [these] words, feeling great happiness in [His] heart, gazing to the eyes' content at the face anointed with nectar,
sitting beside the couch, smiling, [Shyama], in the fullness of rasa, touches the soft feet.
Startled, Rai's restless body trembles, like a golden creeper troubled by the wind, like lightning;
displaying incomparable skill, laughing, the Shyama-moon cannot remain steady.
Wondrous, the Two captivate each other's hearts; in the play of prema the beauty of the Two greatly increases;
there is no pause: new embraces, frequent kisses, abundant thrills of ecstasy in [Their] bodies.
At a distance the sakhis, with great happiness in their hearts, laughing, keep their faces covered with the ends of their cloth;
winking at one another from the corners of their eyes, they joke together with Kundalata.
Choosing the moment, Kundalata again, laughing, coming there beside Vinodini,
looking at Shyama, says to Rai: "What an improper thing you have done!
Embracing a young bride — [that is] your beloved's share; I do not know what he will think of it.
Had I known you would act like this, I would not have brought her."
Rai playfully says, Her face lowered: "I understand: bidding farewell to shame for good,
dressing [him] up like this, you play every day with your own devara.
Until now this was hidden; whatever it may be, now it has come out."
Thus the two say much; hearing it, Ghanashyama's mind is utterly absorbed [in it].[^49]

*And on another [day] — raga gauri*

**17.49.** Shyama, the fine nagara, the ocean of rasa, overwhelmed [with longing] in the hope of seeing Rai,
seeing the moonrise, put on the guise of a brahmana and went to Yavata, to Jatila.
Seeing the excellent twice-born, Jatila, joining both her palms, bowed to him and says:
"Today I consider myself blessed. Let me hear your words: tell me, why have you come to a cowherd's house?"
Hearing [this], the king of the twice-born says: "There is a reason: worshipping the moon, I have eaten nothing today.
Knowing your daughter-in-law to be devoted to her husband, I have come to receive something from her hand."
Jatila, hearing [this], delighted, speaks sweet words to Vishakha:
"Take [him] there, where Radha is; whatever he asks for, let her give it, deeming it a pious deed.
Tell her to join her palms, clasp [his] feet and receive [his] blessing.
Misfortune will go and good fortune will come — cows and wealth — by the boon of this twice-born."
Hearing this, [Vishakha], taking the twice-born along, came to where the jewel of women was
and conveyed the mother-in-law's words; hearing [them], [Radha] felt supreme joy.
Seating the brahmana on a wondrous seat, bowing, speaking humble words,
She carefully brought [him] curd, milk, ghee and all such things.
The twice-born, looking again and again at Rai, says to Vishakha: "Listen, sakhi!
The moon that I worship every day in various ways — that moon I see in her face.
I have found [it] near at hand — how could I neglect [it]? First I must drink the nectar."
Saying this, laughing, floating in the rasa of prema, He kissed the moon of Rai's face.
Vinodini says: "What are you doing, you there? You are a brahmana — why [act] like this?"
The twice-born says: "My hunger and the sorrow of my heart are gone; I find happiness drinking the nectar of [your] face."
In anger the one full of rasa says to Vishakha: "I do not understand your game."
Vishakhika says: "I have understood in my heart — an unearthly pastime of mother-in-law and daughter-in-law!"
Hearing [this], the moon-faced One smiles with lowered eyes; seeing it, the beloved Ghanashyama, laughing,
taking Rai in His arms, trembles all over — what bliss that is! — floating in delight.[^50]

*Raga lalita*

**17.50.** "Shyama met with Radhika: something [of this] I have told — keep it in your heart.
Radha, who enchants Shyama, the beautiful one, meeting with Shyama, fulfilled [Her] wish.
How [They meet] by day and by night — [each] separately — of that I shall tell with joy in my heart.
In this Narahari is very happy. Listen, O Suradhuni, friend dear as life!"[^51]

*On a certain day — raga todi*

**17.51.** Radhika, the jewel of women, hearing that the Beloved has gone to the forest, sends a most skilful messenger — she goes quickly.
Reaching Shyama, seeing [Him], radiant as a fresh cloud, and announcing the beauty's coming, she quickly returns to [Her] home.
The Moon-faced One, greatly delighted, asks the messenger about the doings and ways of the Beloved, dear as life; she says with joy:
"There is no one else beside [Him]; watching for your path, the Shyama-moon stays in the forest, within a mountain cave."
Hearing [this], with Her whole mind immersed [in love], leaving Her home, devising a pretext, the Restless-eyed One goes to the forest.
Endless fun: with many gestures, merrily talking, a friend with a smiling face goes along with Her.
Coming near the best of mountains, She sees: there is a tall banyan, dense shade, a cool, light breeze is blowing.
Seeing [this], stopping, the beauty speaks again: "Make me again a dress like Subala's — what more is there to say? — so that the deception holds."
Understanding, the friend arranged a fine [dress]; the beauty full of rasa, smiling, then at once with great care concealed Her breasts.
Leaving the friend, joyful, She goes quickly to the Beloved; every lovely limb [of Her body] sparkles with radiance.
There, immersed in the rasa of prema, the restless son of the king of Vraja watches the path and, with frequent glances (?), waits in hope.
Who can tell [His] eagerness? Seeing [Her] approach, He says to "Subala": "Tell me, is all well?" — and takes [Her] by the hand.
Hearing this, with lowered eyes, with a troubled face, [She] says: "The excellent jewel of women was coming here —
but then her sister-in-law, at once, in anger, speaking harsh, coarse words, held her back [at home]; hearing that, I came."
Hearing words like a thunderbolt, the jewel of rasikas, powerless, exhausted (?), reproaching the Creator, falls swooning to the ground.
Without Rai, His mind distraught, heaving long sighs again and again, He sheds streams of tears unceasingly.
Understanding the [agreed] signal, then all the friends came; covering their faces with the ends of their cloth, they rain rasa from their eyes.
Seeing the friends with joyful hearts, but not seeing Rai, Ghanashyama is bewildered: not a word comes from [His] mouth.
Lalita and the others, with a charming air, laughing, say to Shyama: "How have you become so clever? Tell — we understand nothing!
How much shall we tell of the virtues of Rasavati's good friend! Seeing new cleverness every day, we are all always delighted."
Hearing [this], the jewel of the clever is abashed; again seeing [His] ways, the beauty, speaking words full of rasa, laughs with the friends.
Kanu, speaking the sweetest words, taking the hand of the Fair One, takes [Her] in His arms, trembles, and, smiling, seats [Her] with care.
Cruel sorrow has gone far away; in the hearts of Both — still greater happiness; [He] kisses [Her] face, immersed in the play of Madana.
All the friends' bodies thrill, as if [they too] had met in the beauty's meeting; Narahari is beside himself, seeing the incomparable union.[^52]

*And on another [day] — raga mayura*

**17.52.** The jewel of women, the beauty Radhika, having adorned Herself, leaving home, with friends dear as life,
goes to the forest — to meet the beautiful Shyama — on the pretext of worshipping Gauri.
Incomparably lovely, conquering the people of the world — the grace of Her steps, swaying at every [step];
the lovely necklace of jewels sparkles, swinging as if dancing.
She sings songs of the Beloved's deeds: with grama, murchhana, tana, shruti and yati
the harmonious, sweet-toned [voice], full of rasa, sounds forth, surpassing hosts of kinnaris.
On Her restless hands — conch-shell [bangles] (?); the kankana bracelets jingle "jhanana-jhanana,"
the belt of little bells — "rini, jhinini," the anklets sound "runu-runuruna."
Seeing such a gait, full of manifold fun, millions of husbands of Rati faint away.
Who can describe the way of the rasa of prema? [All] are immersed in a deep ocean of happiness.
Leaving the friends in a lovely kunja, the beauty again, smiling, duly becoming Her own messenger,
slowly approaches Dhira-samira and sees the nagara.
The playful sari says to Shyama [about Her]; hearing of the coming of Rai's messenger, His heart in turmoil,
He, seeing [Her] near, at once says to the messenger: "Speak, speak: is all well?"
The messenger says: "How much shall I tell, O nagara! You are her all, her life;
for news of where you are, she watches for my path [back] and remains distraught."
Hearing the nectarean words, again and again He is filled with thrills, body and mind melt;
and again He says, joining His palms in entreaty: "Bring [me to her], O messenger of playful glances!"
Saying so, He carefully places His own necklace on the messenger's neck and entreats [Her] many times.
At that very moment, from all four sides, the friends who delight people surround the beautiful Shyama.
The graceful moon of Vraja is left bewildered; Rasavati shines, laughs softly, softly,
with the corners of Her restless lotus eyes revealing all kinds of play.
Understanding [all], Shyama displays great cleverness: "How long [will you keep hiding]?" [He] says (?);
the friend with the friends, and the Lord, in the eagerness of Madana, smile softly, softly.
With a joyful heart Vrinda comes there: how much skill she has! In mastery she has no equal.
She places a lotus with a bee in the friend's hand and says softly, softly:
"This forest of mine bears juicy fruit: I have seen many creepers entwining trees;
but if now the tree entwines the creeper — then [my] wish is fulfilled."
Hearing [this], the graceful young prince, maddened, embraces [Her] and gives many kisses.
Rai says: "I am the messenger! What are you doing? Keep your composure, O moon, Holder of the mountain!"
Seeing [this] play, Narahari rejoices in his heart; together with Vrinda all the companions,
uttering lovely words, laugh on all sides, as if raining honey.[^53]

*And on another [day] — raga deshapala*

**17.53.** The beauty, having made a plan with the friends, merrily goes to meet Shyama.
Entering the nikunja, She sits alone, happily, covering Her body with a lovely veil.
The best of nagaras, restless, beneath a tree watches Rai's path, filled with prema.
Glancing at that beauty in the kunja, He runs and quietly asks Vrinda-devi:
"Tell me, tell me: who is this sitting alone in the new nikunja in wondrous attire?
I have never seen such beauty on earth: Uma, Rama, Rati — what comparison are [they]?"
Hearing [this], Vrinda says to the son of the king of Vraja: "This is she, the goddess of this nikunja (?).
All my power — know that it comes from serving her feet."
Hearing [these] words, Vidagdha goes [to Her] in the hope of beholding [Her] with the greatest reverence;
with a restless heart, entering the lovely kunja, He stands beside the new goddess.
Joining His palms, He says: "Today all my wishes will be fulfilled, [if I] serve you.
Do not deceive [me], be merciful, take away [my] sorrow — I entreat, listen, O goddess!
Dear to me as life, the darling of my heart, is the daughter of Vrishabhanu, the jewel of women.
My life cannot bear not seeing her: a moment I count as hundreds of yugas.
She is of a noble family, [our love] is most secret; her life always trembles in fear of the elders.
O beautiful one! Arrange it so that no one can say anything to her."
Saying this, Kanu bows [to Her], touching [Her] feet, offering a handful of flowers.
Seeing this, Lalita and the others, staying hidden, laugh, their hearts thrilling greatly.
Vrinda-devi says: "What are you doing, Dark One? What fruit will you gain from such worship?
With every limb [of yours] worship every limb [of hers] — then this goddess will be pleased."
Hearing [this], the Moon-faced One, keeping Her face under the veil, laughs softly, floating in bliss;
with the corner of Her eye She restrains Vrinda — and again gives [Herself] away with a slight smile.
Intoxicated with the intoxication of Madana, the best of nagaras, seeing [this], laughing, floating in waters of bliss,
[says]: "Come, come, O goddess, dear to me as life!" — and, lifting [Her], takes [Her] in His arms.
Lalita, leaving [her hiding place] among the creepers, coming close, says: "How much must I admonish [you]?
Having called [her] the Goddess of the nikunja, you are not afraid: you do everything the other way round, you who are greedy for love-play!
You see no fault in this!" Hearing [this], [He] says: "The fault will go, [when I] obtain your touch."
Hearing this, Narahari [and] the companions laugh, putting the ends of their cloth to their mouths.[^54]

*And on another [day] — raga mayura*

**17.54.** Radha, nectar-faced, happy, leaving Her friends a little way off, with great fun,
goes alone to the Beloved, dear as life — She [in the guise of] Chandravali's messenger.
In the nikunja the nagara, overwhelmed [with longing], in the hope of seeing Rai, beside Himself,
forming many wishes in His mind, keeps looking towards the Beloved's path.
There swarms of bees hover with many gestures, merrily, and under the guise of humming say:
"Chandravali's messenger is wandering in the forest — why, we do not know." Hearing [this], Kala starts.
At that very moment that messenger quickly arrives near [Him]; looking at Her,
His face troubled and darkened, He, understanding [that this is] grave trouble, is anxious at heart.
Becoming steady, again displaying cleverness, He speaks kindly to the messenger:
"Go, I shall quickly follow behind you." The messenger says: "I cannot go and leave [you].
Without you Chandravali cannot live; what can I say? — go and see her state.
Get up, get up: she can bear no more delay." Saying this, rushing forward, [She] seizes [His] feet.
At [that] touch the messenger is overjoyed: with what effort She keeps composure of mind (?)!
Receiving the messenger's lovely touch, the Shyama-moon loses control of Himself, as if a serpent had bitten Him.
Glancing with restless eyes at Vrinda, He says: "Tell me, what has happened to me?"
Vrinda says: "Why do you worry? All will be well: embrace the messenger once."
Hearing [this], the jewel of the clever, irresistibly embracing the messenger, floats in bliss.
Seeing this from afar, all the friends, looking at Vrinda, smile slightly.
Lalita, leaving [her hiding place] among the lovely mallika creepers, speaks angrily, knitting her brows:
"Go, go there! There is no point in staying here: your incomparable ways I cannot bear.
What would I not say, O you greedy for love-play! — [but] let all those words be set aside.
What [your dealings] with Chandravali are like — this I have learned through this messenger.
Ah, ah! [What] love of yours! I have never seen such thrills in a body.
Why be shy of any of us? Drink Chandravali's nectar to your heart's content."
Hearing [this], Kanu says: "Surpassing Chandravali, in this moonlike face is a treasure of nectar.
I have received permission — now I shall drink." Saying this, He, laughing, kisses [Her] face.
Holding [Her] by the chin with His hand like a tender shoot, He jests, full of rasa;
placing [Her] on His thigh, arranging [Her] lovely attire, He sports on a new couch.
Knowing the moment is right, the dear friends carefully relieve the weariness of Both;
receiving a sign, Narahari merrily fans [Them] with a chamara, gazing at that beauty.[^55]

*Raga dhanashi*

**17.55.** So many of the sweetest ways of Rai and Kanu did Yamuna tell to Suradhuni!
Yamuna — what joy was in her heart! — again speaks to Suradhuni in gentle words:
"O Suradhuni! Such are [Their] ways: you asked with your heart's wish — I have told a little.
Now listen: of the charming pastimes of the Two I shall tell something, in the order of day and night."[^56]

*First — nishanta.*

*Raga bhairava*

**17.56.** At the end of the night the nagara of the new nikunja, proud, breaker of [the gopis'] composure,
with Rasavati, in an excess of rasa, intoxicated in the battle of rasa, is greatly wearied.
In the lovely grove-house, a mass of beauty, the Two sleep sweetly in the fullness of happiness,
as if a dense mass of cloud entwined with lightning were shining upon the wide bed.
Vrinda, knowing the time, gives instructions to the beasts and birds dwelling in Vrindavana;
at her bidding, very alert, rejoicing, they display their skills.
Near the nikunja the beasts roam with many playful gestures, intoxicated with play;
sharis, shukas, kokilas, peacocks — flocks of birds — raise a fresh chorus in various ways.
Swarms of bees, utterly intoxicated with honey, hum in the lovely kunja;
there is no equal comparison on earth: who, drinking that honey, would not lose composure?
Hearing the sweetest sounds, the Two — the heaviness of sleep goes far away, their hearts thrill with rapture —
in a happy rush of joy remain lying again, pressing face to lovely face.
Reared by the Two, in their cages the shari and the shuka, birds playing charmingly,
skilfully, beautifully recite [the tales of] the glorious deeds of the Two — as if steeped in nectar.
Dhani, hearing the glory of the Holder of the mountain, praises the shuka with the greatest affection;
hearing of the qualities of the Beloved, dear as life, the dear Ghanashyama is enchanted in heart.[^57]

**17.57.** The bee and his mate, circling and circling, speak under the guise of an incomparable lovely humming:
"Get up, Shyama-moon! The night is over — how long will You sleep at daybreak?
You do not heed [our] words: if anyone from anywhere should see, then there will be trouble:
You will be put to shame, a stain of disgrace will remain, the love of many days will be gone in a moment."
Having said this, they say to the sakhis: "O Lalita and the rest, tell us, what happiness is there here?
You are women of good families; staying in the forest, why do you needlessly bring blame upon yourselves?
Radhika has no fear — I know what her nature is:
leaving love for her own husband, she has clung to another — in this [is her] great boldness.
The Dark One is an actor, a rogue, shameless, fickle, victorious in Vraja — who does not know it?
Whatever shame before [her] family there was, that fear she has forgotten — such are the qualities of Nanda's [son]!
Therefore I fear: the cruel sister-in-law, [her] enemy, may no longer keep Radhika at home —
Fate has made [her] a forest-dweller!" Hearing this, Ghanashyama laughs in the fullness of happiness.[^58]

**17.58.** Rasavati, very joyful, with a stirred heart, thrilling with rapture all over,
looking with the corner of Her eye at the face of the Shyama-moon, makes so many gestures in incomparable play.
Keeping composure, slowly, slowly the Holder of the mountain looks at Dhani's face;
speaking playful words, He leaves the bed, pained that the touching of [Their] bodies is broken off.
Again, overwhelmed with the fullness of rasa, taking that lovely chin with His sprout-like hand,
He seats Radhika on His left thigh, with great tenderness.
Seeing the beauty of the loosened fine hair and dress, He smiles softly, counting [it] great good fortune,
and with His own hands skilfully arranges [Her] attire — the beautiful jewel of the clever.
He braids a plait that surpasses a serpent, sets jewels [in it], places a dot of sindura in the parting;
carefully He adorns [Her] with nose-ring and necklace, anointing the moon of [Her] face with kunkuma;
putting fine bracelets on both hands, placing a dot of musk on the chin,
He puts the bodice on [Her] breast; the restless heart, thrilling again and again with rapture, sways.
He cannot be still; quickly, with great longing of heart, feeding [Her] betel,
placing mouth to mouth, taking [it], He says: "In this I find more sweetness, Radhe!"
Seeing Shyama's playful gesture, smiling, Rasavati playfully covers Her face with the edge of Her cloth;
seeing this, the dear Ghanashyama, enchanted, embraces [Her] closely in the highest happiness.[^59]

**17.59.** The Nectar-faced One, happy, happily speaking some words of reproach to the Beloved, dear as life,
seeing that the moon of [His] face is like the moon [with its mark], brings a mirror and holds it before Shyama.
The Nagara, seeing His own face, is abashed; Dhani, quickly taking the edge of [Her] cloth,
wipes away the kunkuma, the dot of musk, the sindura, in the greatest joy.
Smiling, Rasavati in the fullness of rasa arranges Shyama's attire, floating in bliss.
Making many jests with each other, They seem to rain down streams of nectar.
The beauty of the faces of the Two is unprecedented, humbling the pride of countless moons;
putting their faces to the window, the sakhis, unseen, watch happily, filling their eyes.
At the incomparable perfection of prema all are mad — no memory of anything remains.
Playful Vrinda, knowing this, gives an instruction to Kakkhati, skilled in cunning;
she, sitting on a tree, under the guise of a cry playfully calls out the name "Jatila!"
Hearing this, Their restless hearts startled, They deliberate much with the sakhis.
Vrinda-devi, seeing that fun, says by a sign: "Do not worry — go quickly."
Hearing [this], in alarm, having exchanged garments, [They] leave the bed (?); all are full of merriment.
The Two, holding hands, keeping composure, set out — abodes of supreme bliss;
They go, lighting up the forest paths; on all four sides the incomparable sakhis shine.
Alongside [go] the peacocks: having gazed their fill at the abundant beauty of the ornaments, happy,
with love, dancing, they go on ahead — restless, lovely, a delight to the eyes.
Flocks of kokilas on the mango trees sing charming songs in the panchama note;
in the vanjula groves bees hum, swarm upon swarm; on the lotuses lovely rows of khanjanas [play] merrily.
The buck, in the play of Ananga, runs together with the doe, unable to keep composure;
seeing this, the dear Ghanashyama, speaking playful words, laughs in the fullness of rasa.[^60]

**17.60.** Seeing the sign from the corner of the Nectar-faced One's eye, the happy sakhis, lovely, skilled in cleverness,
displaying many jests with Shyama, laugh and hurry one another to go.
Rupa, Rati, Rasa, Kasturi, Lavanga, Manjulali — the manjaris and others — merrily,
taking the water-jug (?), the broken necklaces, the golden cage and the rest, go, their bodies thrilling with rapture.
The Two — each on His and Her own way — their composure gone, look at Each Other from afar;
ceaselessly water streams, streams from [Their] lotus eyes; [Their] hearts are in distress.
No word comes from the mouth; touching the chin with a lotus hand, kissing,
[He] goes two or four steps, turns back again and embraces [Her], gazing at the sweetness of [Her] face.
Thus, again and again, They cannot go, distraught with the fullness of prema.
A companion, keeping composure, consoling Radhika, takes [Her] home.
Unseen, each going to Their own home, lying down, They meet Each Other in dreams.
[Such are] the wondrous pastimes of Ghanashyama, dear as life, with the Beloved at the time of nishanta.[^61]

*Raga vibhasha*

**17.61.** In the morning Paurnamasi, smiling and smiling, floating in rasa, coming to Yashomati,
speaks many words of blessing for Kanu, [her] life; the queen, listening, floats in an ocean of happiness.
Bowing her head to Paurnamasi's feet, slowly, slowly she goes to where her son is sleeping;
seeing her son on the moon-like bed, she wakes [Him], saying many things.
Hearing the queen's words, that Kanu, feigning sleep, stretches His body a little —
as if an emerald-dark creeper, the colour of dense collyrium, were swaying at the touch of the wind.
Seeing the blue cloth and other things on Shyama's body, the queen is much alarmed in her mind;
Bhagavati, very fond of fun, says many things to dispel her fear.
Drinking the nectar of mother's love, He leaves the bed and rises — with new grace, in lovely radiance;
smiling very softly, happily bowing to the feet of both, He goes to where the white cows are (?).
In many, many ways, the knower of fun gets ready with Subala and His other friends;
taking the milking pail and the hobbling rope, the dear Ghanashyama is busy with the milking.[^62]

*Raga vibhasha*

**17.62.** Paurnamasi, seeing on the bed the moon-faced [son] of Vraja, the treasure of rasa, rejoicing,
goes home with very swift steps, having again said so much to the queen of Vraja.
Ah, ah! Who knows the secret — what ways there are at the time when night turns to dawn!
Leaving her bed, with happiness in her heart, Mukhara goes to see her granddaughter.
Entering the house, she meets Jatila — that simple-minded mother of Abhimanyu;
[Jatila] very slowly tells Mukhara what Paurnamasi had said:
"'Honouring Yashomati's word, carefully send your daughter-in-law to worship the sun:
by [his] grace there will be cows, wealth, much welfare' — so said the Devi.
You yourself, too, tell your granddaughter, so that misfortune may be removed from the cows and wealth."
Having said this, [Jatila] together with Mukhara quickly goes to wake the daughter-in-law.
Entering the lovely bedchamber, she says again and again in sweet words:
"Today you have forgotten the worship of Bhanu! Wake up, wake up, daughter-in-law, the night is over!"
Having said this, [she] grew much alarmed, seeing yellow cloth on the daughter-in-law's body;
explaining [it] as the glow of the red rays [of dawn], a clever sakhi dispelled the alarm.
Jatila, having joyfully woken the daughter-in-law, quickly went off where she had work to do;
Mukhara, showing her merriment, went home, having gazed there at her granddaughter to her eyes' fill.
Surrounded by the sakhis, that beauty leaves the bed and sits — [Her] beauty has no comparison.
Merrily they rain rasa on one another; seeing [this], Narahari remains, offering himself [as a ransom].[^63]
