# Kirana 15. The Deeds of the Morning: The Description of the Manifestation of the Joy of the Hosts of Nagas and So On[^1]

*Morning: the nagas, coming out unseen from the clefts of the earth, behold Gaura in Shachi's courtyard; one after another they tell each other of His play on the bank of the Ganga, of the beauty before which the people of Nadiya lose their composure, of His mercy to the lame, the blind and the mute, and of the kirtan with Vasu Ghosha, Mukunda and Madhava — and they long to live in Nadiya among His associates and serve Him*

*Again, in the morning period — the manifestation of the joy of the hosts of nagas and so on.*

*Raga bhairava*

**15.1.** A wondrous play — the night has turned to dawn.
In Shachi's courtyard, putting to shame a multitude of suns, shines Gaura's form — it cannot be described. *(Refrain)*
The lovely moon-like face, before which a multitude of autumn moons perform arati, ceaselessly gleams;
the devotees, like chakoras, filling their eyes again and again, drink the nectarean stream of the rasa of [His] lovely smile.
What could [even] Shesha describe? [His] hair is rare for the eyes: the beautiful curling locks are like a fearsome mass of dense clouds;
rejoicing, the whole world gazes intently [at them] like chatakas; and more than this — the telling has no end.
The nagas, the hosts of nagas, rejoice, reflecting in countless ways on their great good fortune;
unseen, [coming out] from the clefts of the earth, beyond [every] state, overflowing [with feeling], they behold the Lord of Narahari.[^2]

*Chitravalli-chhandah (matra-vritta)*

**15.2.** Today, at the end of the night, auspicious, fortunate, all the nagas,
each deeming his fortune fulfilled, greatly stirred in heart —
who could describe their conduct? — seeing the beautiful, accomplished Gaurasundara,
overpowered by the rasa of prema, again say something to one another.[^3]

*One says — raga suhai*

**15.3.** "Gaurahari sports with His dear associates on the bank of the Suradhuni.
Seeing [this], the heart rejoiced, the body was filled with unbroken thrills of ecstasy.
How sweet, how sweet is His sweetness, which takes away all of Madana's pride!
The moon of His face is the abode of joy; a soft, soft smile pours out nectar.
Taking someone's hand, He seems to fall — the curves of His body cannot be described;
regaining composure, Hari listens again and again to talk of the city of Vraja.
Overflowing with prema, looking [at them], day after day He removes the sorrows of the fallen;
counting the qualities of the Lord of servant Narahari, the hosts of immortals pine at heart."[^4]

*And another — raga kamoda*

**15.4.** "Hearing of Gaura's qualities, [I] rejoiced in all my body with thirst to see [Him].
On the broad road I saw Him walking with His accomplished associates.
Ah, what a wondrous form! Flashing, it subdues garlands of lightning;
from the fragrance of the lovely curling locks how many Kamas fall in a swoon!
On the spotless face, surpassing a multitude of moons — what a sweet smile!
The composure and dharma of the young women are shattered; softly, softly they say [to one another]:
'What spell has suddenly come upon [us]!' — the young women of the city of Nadiya,
surrounding the Lord of Narahari, do not leave [Him]; their eagerness cannot be told."[^5]

*And another — raga suhai*

**15.5.** "Beneath a young nipa tree — what play Gaurahari displays!
Surging forward, men and women come; incomparably beautiful is the Lord — [they are] on all four sides.
The lame, gaining feet, run back and forth in great eagerness;
those blind from birth cannot contain their great joy, seeing the moon of [His] face.
He from whose mouth no word came forth speaks His name;
fools compose many poems, the learned give up their pride.
The sorrow of the poor and the destitute goes far away; in the city of the gods resound cries of 'Victory, victory!'
The hope of servant Narahari is fulfilled: [He] has taken away Yama's authority."[^6]

*And another — raga gurjari*

**15.6.** "What shall I say? How sweet is the form of Gaura the nagara — it cannot be described!
With His companions, in great merriment, He begins water play in the Jahnavi.
There the noble brahmanas, each absorbed in the worship of his chosen Deity,
forgot it all; their bodies overpowered, they gaze intently toward the Lord.
The young women, intoxicated at heart, became chakoris to the moon of His face:
their garments slip — there is no [strength] to hold them, not a little composure can they keep.
All the fine young men of the city, seeing [His] beauty, bowed their heads
and lost their wits and their [accustomed] ways — the Lord of Narahari enchants the whole world."[^7]

*And another — raga malava*

**15.7.** "Today I saw Gaura — the best of dancers — lost in the rasa of dance.
The curves of [His] body take away all of Ananga's pride; His attire is radiant.
How sweetly He utters with His moon-like mouth the name 'Hari, Hari'!
Hearing [it], people's hearts lose their calm; again and again streams of tears flow from [their] eyes.
Gathering His associates — Vasu Ghosha, Mukunda, Madhava and others —
He performs kirtan; drinking that nectar, the world went mad.
The cymbals ring 'jhanu-jhanu', the murajas and mridangas thunder in endless beats;
the beautiful sound pierces the sky — what more can Ghanashyama say?"[^8]

*Raga kamoda*

**15.8.** Ceaselessly, in so many ways, the nagas tell of Gaura's beautiful deeds.
Tears flow from [their] eyes as from rain clouds; intoxicated, they hold on to that rasa.
They cherish so many wishes: "Will we have a dwelling here in Nadiya?
Will we carefully hold in the heart the pair of lotus feet that grant fearlessness?
Will we live together with the Lord's associates and serve [Him] abundantly?
Knowing [us] as His own, will He give [us] bhakti? Will all sorrows go far away?
Will we ceaselessly sing the qualities of the Lord and His associates?"
So much [do they think] in their hearts, the hosts of nagas; Ghanashyama offers himself [to them].[^9]

*Thus [ends] the fifteenth kirana of the Shrimad Gaura-charita-chintamani, called "The Description of the Manifestation of the Joy of the Hosts of Nagas and So On", in the description of the deeds of the morning period.*[^10]

The subheading "Again, in the morning period — the manifestation of the joy of the hosts of nagas and so on" (*punaḥ prātaḥkāle nāga-gaṇollāsa-prakāśādikaṁ*) stands on pdf 155 (printed p. 150) right after the colophon of kirana 14. The nagas are semi-divine serpent beings dwelling in the subterranean worlds (Patala); their lord is Shesha (Ananta). The speakers are nagas (the headings "কোঽপ্যাহ", "কোঽপি চ" are masculine). Kirana 15 consists of stanzas 15.1–15.8 (pdf 155–158), entirely within this portion.
[^2]: Raga bhairava is a morning raga (cf. 12.1). pdf 155 (printed p. 150). Lines 1–2 are the refrain (*dhru*; the mark "॥ধ্রু॥" stands after line 2). The author's words. "A wondrous play" — *aparūpa raṅga*. "Putting to shame a multitude of suns" — *dinamaṇi-bṛnda nindi*. "Form" — *chabi*. "Before which a multitude of autumn moons perform arati" — *śarada-niśākara-nikara-nirājita*: *nīrājita* — "honoured with the waving of lamps" (arati); one could also understand "surpassed". "The devotees, like chakoras" — *bhakata-cakora*: the chakora is a bird said to feed on moonlight. "Drink" — *paripibata*, literally "drink their fill". "What could [even] Shesha describe?" — *racaba ki śeṣa*: Shesha (Ananta), lord of the nagas, who ceaselessly glorifies the Lord with a thousand mouths — fitting in a kirana about nagas. "Rare for the eyes" — *dṛśi dulaha*. "A fearsome mass of dense clouds" — *ghaṭā ghana ghora*. "Like chatakas" — *janu cātaka*: the chataka bird drinks only rainwater and gazes intently at the clouds. "The telling has no end" — *kahai na ora*. "The nagas, the hosts of nagas" — *nāga-nāgagaṇa*: a doubling; possibly "the lords of the nagas and the hosts of nagas". "Reflecting on their great good fortune" — *bhūri nija bhāga bicāri*. "Unseen" — *alakhita*. "From the clefts of the earth" — *bhūmi-bibara-gata*: the abode of the nagas is beneath the earth. "Beyond [every] state" — *gati para*: the understanding is uncertain. "Overflowing [with feeling]" — *garagara*. Signature: "the Lord of Narahari" (*narahari pahũka*).

"All the nagas" — *sa[ba] nāgagaṇa*: the second letter is covered by a blot in print; *saba* is adopted — the reading is unclear. "Deeming his fortune fulfilled" — *māni nija nija saphala bhāga*. "Greatly stirred in heart" — *ati tarala mana*. "Conduct" — *carita*. "Accomplished" — *sughara*. "Overpowered by the rasa of prema" — *prema-rasa-bibaśa*.
[^4]: The heading is "কোঽপ্যাহ—(সুহই)" ("someone says"; raga suhai). pdf 156 (printed p. 151). One of the nagas speaks. "Suradhuni" — the Ganga. "Which takes away all of Madana's pride" — *madana-mada-bhara-hārī*. "The abode of joy" — *muda-sadana*. "Pours out" — *ubhāri*. "Taking someone's hand, He seems to fall" — *kāhu kara gahi girata janu*: Gaura in ecstatic self-forgetfulness. "The curves of His body" — *tanu-bhaṅgi*. "Hari" — *hari*: the first letter is damaged in print (the OCR reading is adopted). "Talk of the city of Vraja" — *brajapura-bāta*: Gaura, Krishna in the mood of Radha, listens to talk of Vraja. "Overflowing with prema" — *preme uchali*. "The sorrows of the fallen" — *patita-duḥkha*. "The hosts of immortals pine at heart" — *amaragaṇa mana jhura*: probably the gods long because they cannot be near Him. Signature: "the Lord of servant Narahari" (*dāsa narahari pahũka*).
[^5]: The heading is "কোঽপি চ—(কামোদ)" ("and another"; raga kamoda). pdf 156 (printed p. 151). "Rejoiced in all my body" — *ulasala aṅga*. "With thirst to see" — *daraśa-piyāse*. "On the broad road" — *parisara panthe*. "Accomplished" — *sughara*. "Flashing, it subdues garlands of lightning" — *damakata damai dāminī-dāma*. "Curling locks" — *cā̃cara cikura*. "How many Kamas" — *kata kāma*: Kama (Madana) as the epitome of perfect beauty (cf. 12.1). "The composure and dharma of the young women are shattered" — *taruṇigaṇa dhṛti dharama dhvaṁsala*; "softly, softly they say" — *bhaṇata lahu lahu bhāṣa*: the subject of the second half is not named; one could also understand that Gaura, speaking soft words, shattered the composure and dharma of the young women. "Spell" — *ceṭaka*: enchantment, bewitchment. "Suddenly" — *jhaṭa*. "The young women of the city of Nadiya" — *nadiyā-nagara-naba-nārī*. "Surrounding" — *beṛhi*. "Eagerness" — *uchāha*. Here, in the nagas' words, the mood of the previous kiranas appears again: the women of Nadiya, seeing Gaura, lose their composure. Gaudiya Vaishnavas, following Shrila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati and the Chaitanya-bhagavata (Adi 15: in His lila the Lord did not jest with women and did not even look at them), reject gaura-nagari-vada — the worship of Gaura as an enjoying lover (*nāgara*) and of oneself as His nagari: Gaura is Krishna in the mood of Radha, tasting Her love, not an enjoyer. The nagas' words convey only how the women's hearts are drawn to Him; Gaura Himself in these stanzas is engaged in kirtan, in play with His associates and in mercy to the fallen (15.3, 15.5, 15.7). Cf. the main notes of kiranas 11 (note 2), 12 (note 10) and 14 (note 2). Further notes in this kirana refer to this note. Signature: "the Lord of Narahari".
