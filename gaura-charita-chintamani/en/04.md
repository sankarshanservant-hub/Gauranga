# Kirana 4. The Deeds of the Morning: Description of [Gaura] Surrounded by Rows of Devotees, and Other Matters[^1]

*Morning in Navadvipa: Gaura, now awake, sits in the courtyard of Shachi's house, surrounded by countless associates, like the golden mountain Sumeru by the gods; the jubilation of the devotees; Nityananda, intoxicated with love for His younger Brother, gazes at Gaura; the beauty of Nityananda*

*Again, in the morning period — the description of the beauty and so on of [Gaura], seated in His own courtyard.*

*Chaturbhangi(?)-chhandah*

**4.1.** Today, [when] the night has ended, … Gaura(?) … in joyful play(?) … — in fine order; in the midst of it shines a vast assembly of associates:[^2]
Sri Gadadhara Pandita, Shrinivasa, Haridasa, Raghunandana, Mukunda, Narahari, Gadadhara, Pundarika Vidyanidhi, Shrigarbha, Vijaya — [like] a splendour of sun rays;
Shriman Pandita, Hiranya, Jagadisha, Vakreshvara, Sri Gauridasa Pandita, Vasudeva Dattacharya(?), Bhagavata, Sanjaya, Dhananjaya, Jagannatha — renowned in the world;
Sri Chandrashekhara, Purandara, Sadashiva, Shriman Sena, Shuklambara, Shridhara, Sri Krishnadasa, Bhagavan Pandita, Purushottama, Murari Gupta — delighted with abundant prema;
Sri Vanamali Pandita, Garuda, Buddhimanta Khan, Govinda, Gangadasa, Nandana, Narayana, Shivananda, Nandanacharya, Ratna(?), Sri Mukunda Datta — bringers of blessing;
Sri Satyaraja, Ramananda, Rama, Vidyananda, Shankara, Srila Yadunatha, Vaninatha Mishra(?), Madhusudana, Jagannatha Acharya — devoted to serving Gaura;
Srila Kavichandra, Shashthivara, Jagannatha Mishra, Gopikanta, Mahesha Pandita, Govinda, Madhava, Vasudeva, Ishana, Gopala, Narada — charming [in their] service;
Shrinatha, Ramadasa, Sri Shubhananda, Krishnadasa, Shrinidhi, Sri Jagannatha Dasa, Kashishvara, Sri Gopinatha and others — of matchless beauty, says Ghanashyama.[^3]

*Raga lalita*

**4.2.** Today in the house of Sri Shachi there is great happiness:
on a matchless seat (padapitha) shines beautiful Gaurachandra, king of the twice-born. *(Refrain)*
Around the Lord is a circle of dear associates, most wondrous and lovely,
like Mount Sumeru encircled by the gods: even Shesha cannot describe [this] beauty.[^4]
Someone, leaning his hand on another's hand, stands like a painted doll;
someone's cloth slips down — he does not set it right: what feeling he has cannot be grasped.
Someone, with a start, has left his bed and come — his eyes droop in deep drowsiness.
Says Narahari: [he] is like a bee drunk on the honey of that lotus face.[^5]

*Raga yatharaga*

**4.3.** Today is a joyful dawn in Shachi's courtyard — unbroken love, fresh delight in many ways!
Someone comes and goes, someone sings the wondrous raga lalita, [someone] dances, drunk with rasa;
someone, [leaning] to another's ear, softly says [something] — and, laughing and laughing, falls: he cannot hold his body up;
someone, seizing another, embraces [him], and someone bows at another's feet.
Someone asks another whether the night passed well; someone says: "Now [all] is well — [we] see the beautiful Lord!"
Someone says to another: "Blessed are you, blessed are you! Sorrow will vanish at the touch of the breeze [wafting] from your body";
someone recites new verses, prose and so on; someone, crying aloud(?), holds a blade of grass between his teeth.
The associates are countless — like a surging ocean: how can Narahari describe this with a single mouth?[^6]

*Raga yatharaga*

**4.4.** What shall I say of today's wondrous play?
In the midst of the spacious courtyard is Gaurahari; [around Him] His dear associates shine unceasingly. *(Refrain)*
Is [this] a clear sky without stars, where a host of moons shines brightly?
Surely they will destroy the dreadful, impenetrable darkness of the world's threefold misery.
Is [this] an arena that removes the burden of the fear of samsara — a lovely gathering of mighty wrestlers?
Surely, defeating the invincible asuras who have turned away from the Lord's feet, they will accomplish their task.
Is [this] a field without a fence, where blossoming wish-fulfilling trees have appeared?
Says Narahari: surely they will distribute matchless, priceless fruits — and no one will be left deprived.[^7]

*Trivikrama-chhandah*

**4.5.** In the lovely courtyard, laid out spaciously, countless people have gathered — and still there is much [room]: [such is] the inconceivable power, the beauty of [His] manifestation(?).
Gaura's beauty is a vast, dense cloud, unceasingly pouring down the lovely rasa of bliss(?), producing abundant joy in the rasikas, ever incomparable.
How many kinds of feelings arise [in them]! Unable to keep their composure, greatly intoxicated, like an irresistible elephant, they cherish various longings — who can describe [them]?
The hypocrite Narahari, fiercest of the fierce, like a bull without [the herdsman's] stick — what can he say? He only longs greatly to hold that lovely time in meditation.[^8]

*Sudhamukhi-chhandah*

**4.6.** The terrace of Shachi's courtyard; in its midst — the beloved [associates].
[The Lord] plays in great delight, shattering people's composure.[^9]

*Chitra-chhandah*

**4.7.** The Lord, Srila Nityananda the moon, root of bliss, dispeller of many doubts, lotus-eyed, worshipped by hosts of gods, eminent, steadfast, renowned —
in the midst of the circle of dear devotees, gazing at the moon of the face of Gaura-Govinda, in utmost rapture softly, softly laughs, overjoyed.
A cloud of joy, an auspicious abode — His love-filled body is full of fresh thrills [of ecstasy]; wondrous are [His] movements, He waves His lovely arms.
He shines and trembles, like a figure of the purest gold; [His] form sways with deep anuraga — how could the insignificant fool Ghanashyama describe [it]?[^10]

*Belavali*

**4.8.** Nityananda rejoices in His heart.
Gazing at His younger Brother, He forgets everything and plunges into that ocean of beauty. *(Refrain)*
In His mind He reflects: "Before, I saw a wondrous dark body,
[but] Gaura's golden body steals the heart even more; how am I to understand [this]? Therefore this is a hidden rasa.
This is most rare; now, somehow propitiating [the Creator], I shall ask a boon(?):
may there never be separation — may this [form] always remain as if fastened to my eyes."
So many such hopes arise in [His] heart — the way of prema is matchless, boundless.
Narahari's Lord again and again irresistibly longs from the Creator for a body made wholly of eyes.[^11]

*Veloara*

**4.9.** Intoxicated with His Brother's mood, the son of Padmavati stands motionless, deeply absorbed.
Again and again He trembles like a garland of lightning; His lovely body shines with hairs standing on end.
The necklace slips from His chest; His lovely hair, ornaments, clothing — He does not set them right:
surely the great golden-hued thief, unseen, swiftly steals everything away.
How much joy arises in His heart! Tears stream in torrents from His beautiful eyes —
surely a greedy chakora, having drunk too much of the nectar of that moon face, pours [it] out again.
Rising on the tips of His toes, He stands and lifts His incomparable arms —
says Ghanashyama: as if a golden mountain, leaving the earth, were going up into the sky.[^12]

*Veloara*

**4.10.** Wondrous is this prema — may I be offered up to it!
His heart overflows, His body is unsteady, His gait faltering — His feet cannot hold [Him] up.
Seeing the beautiful, charming [Gaura] from afar, He came near [Him] swiftly;
utterly overcome, not in control [of Himself], not [setting right] His cloth, He placed His left hand on His shoulder.
Gazing at the moon of Gaurachandra's face, He laughs again and again, softly, softly — His lips shine.
Matchless is the grace, vast the beauty — neither Shesha nor Sharada can describe even a little [of it].
Whoever hopes to attain Gaura's pure lotus feet without this Nitai —
he is the one fool in the three worlds: all [his efforts] are surely in vain, says Narahari Dasa.[^13]

*Raga Veloara*

**4.11.** Nitai-chand, king of rasa, shines;
[His] form surpasses a heap of kunkuma on a golden mountain lit by the dawn — a form that deceives the world(?). *(Refrain)*
The wondrous curves of [His] limbs glitter — far softer than the shirisha flower;
the fragrance of His body, which shatters the dharma and fear of young women of good families, has filled the ten directions.[^14]
On sweet lips a sweet soft smile; the moon face shines, pouring nectar.
The radiance of His teeth puts strings of pearls to shame; His lips are lovely, His chin steals the heart.
Pure, wide eyes — lotus petals, full of merriment — [cast glances] in so many ways;
the curved, lovely brows — Ananga's bow — put to shame serpents and rows of bees.
The forehead adorned with tilaka, earrings swaying in the ears, the nose lovely [like] Garuda's beak;
finely shaped cheeks, a proud neck, mighty arms that take away the pride of elephants' trunks.
Broad is the lovely chest that conquers the three worlds: can firm door panels be compared to it?
In the lake of the navel — hair like water-moss; the three folds shine — [like] Triveni: who, seeing [this], can keep his composure?
Who with such patience fashioned the lovely slender waist that humbles the pride of the lion?
Captivating the minds and eyes of people is the wondrous blue garment of finest silk that He wears.
Firm shanks, soft and beautiful; mighty knees appear, giving joy;
the reddish feet, the lovely rays of the nails — may [they] destroy the darkness of this Narahari's heart![^15]

*Again — Veloara*

**4.12.** At the dawn of night Advaita Mahashaya, [radiant] like the sun, in supreme jubilation,
ceaselessly slaps both His armpits, seeing the moon of Gaura's face shine forth.
His corpulent body, victorious in all directions(?), is most beautiful; on it fresh horripilation shines.
He wondrously walks to and fro, here and there, more intoxicated than the king of elephants.
Laughing softly, He flashes the rows of His teeth — white rays issue ceaselessly,
wondrously, — as if kunda flowers were raining in all four directions; the connoisseurs' longing and delight grow still more(?).
The tears trembling in both His eyes brim over and flow beautifully, heeding no restraint;
they sparkle like strings of pearls, — Narahari understands: the Lord is overcome [by prema](?).[^16]

*Raga yatharaga*

**4.13.** The Lord of Sita is immersed in great happiness.
In His mind He reflects, smiling softly: "Thus even Madana's pride did not survive(?)." *(Refrain)*
"Most wondrous is this golden complexion — an excellent intoxicating nectar: having drunk only a little of it,
the three worlds became intoxicated, forgot everything and made the son of Shachi the essence of their life.
The deliverer from the burning heat and darkness of people's minds, the abode of mercy, the moon [among] His fine associates —
[He has come, and] the word 'sorrow' on earth has passed beyond hearing; the world is filled with great bliss.
The opposite of joy has vanished; now Kali and sin with [all] their retinue are put to shame.
Hari, Hari! Who [now] is left without the right?" — says Narahari. — O Lord, [such is] Your might![^17]

*Raga yatharaga*

**4.14.** The Father of Achyuta, the refuge of people, renowned in the world as generous, the remover of the sorrows of the wretched,
cherishes in His mind so many, many wishes; [now] He loses His composure, [now] again He restrains Himself.
With powerful longing, like a pauper, without hesitation — the king of the twice-born in His night attire
He took by the path of the eyes and with great care seated on the seat of His heart.
Offering kadamba flowers — [His] feelings — He worships [Him] and offers body and mind to Him in nirmanchhana;
crying "Jaya, jaya!", unnoticed, He dances softly — stealing people's minds;
every moment saying "I have won, I have won!" He rejoices, by Himself immersed in sweet rasa.
Seeing this incomparable sight, — who can tell the limit of the happiness of Narahari, Haridasa and the others?[^18]

*Raga yatharaga*

**4.15.** Behold the excellent form of Lord Advaita: who fashioned [Him] — no one can understand.
Is it not a heap of champaka and shona(?) flowers? In each of [His] limbs, it seems, Ananga has taken shelter.
His lovely face shatters the pride of groves of blossoming lotuses; a soft, sweet smile;
lips deep red, bestowing delight; an incomparable golden effulgence, a radiance beyond compare.
His eyes are pure, wide, full of rasa; [their] expressiveness conquers the world; the brows are charming;
the nose is lovely, the forehead and ears graceful; the cheeks steal the pride of golden mirrors.
The neck is well formed, like a conch; the beautiful arms reach to the knees, lovely;
seeing the gleaming, full, broad chest, no one has the strength to keep composure.
A wondrous deep navel; the fine hairs — like a camphor creeper — shine endlessly.
Gracefully clad in Chinese silk, most resplendent is the waist that humbles [the pride of] the lion.
The thighs — [like] inverted plantain trunks — are supremely charming; the pair of fine ankles, giving joy, is incomparable.
The red soles [crush] the hosts of lotuses(?); to the radiance of the jewel-like nails Ghanashyama offers himself.[^19]

*Rangamala-chhandah*

**4.16.** Beside Gaurachandra wondrously shines the root of joy — the son of Madhava;
his charming radiance cannot be described even a little.
His beautiful, excellent grace intoxicates the world; his night attire is spotless;
rows of horripilation sparkle on [his] body, [tender as] a shirisha flower.
His lotus eyes are full of languor and slightly reddish; a lovely face;
the lips are clear — as if he were always softly smiling.
His charming garment shines; his thick locks are slightly loosened;
his composure lost, Narahari meditates [on this] every morning.[^20]

*Raga yatharaga*

**4.17.** Gadadhara — the foremost connoisseur, the abode of rasa.
Charming is [his] golden body; to the delightful radiance of [that] body how many Kamas offer themselves in nirmanchhana! *(Refrain)*
His lotus face conquers forests of lotuses; his lovely soft smile is like flower nectar;
again and again filling and filling the cups of their eyes, [all] drink [it] — great jubilation in the heart.
His soft, sweet, fresh words put to shame the excellent mantra that subdues the world;
hearing [them], the eager ears — [and even] the shrutis — long [for them] more and more; forgotten is the hearing of the Vedas and the teachings of the shruti(?).
Recalling his former deeds in his heart, he grows restless, loses composure and movement, and floats in great happiness.
Gold is left far behind; incomparable is [his] excellent prema; Narahari, seeing it — hidden [and yet] manifest — smiles.[^21]

*Raga yatharaga*

**4.18.** Sri Narahari is the king among the skilful.
He shines beside the son of Madhava; his bearing is a fitting match [for him], unseen in the world. *(Refrain)*
The moon of Gaura's face with its sweet smile — on it both [his] eyes are fixed with great delight;
his nose on the fragrance of [His] body, his fine ears on hearing the nectar of [His] words: [he] wishes for no interruption.
Seeing again and again the most charming, slightly loosened night attire, [he] feels great jubilation in his heart.
The way of prema is most wondrous, beyond comprehension: [to him] it seems the former pastimes in the forests of Vraja.
How many efforts he makes to keep his composure — [but] composure does not stay; [he is] ceaselessly restless.
His most tender body is overflowing with love; to [his] incomparable character Ghanashyama offers himself.[^22]

*Raga yatharaga*

**4.19.** Today's happiness cannot be described at all.
The rasika, steadfast, refined Srivasa, seeing the Lord's play, smiles very softly. *(Refrain)*
His stately body trembles, overflowing with love; graceful is [his] bearing, incomparable [his] great beauty.
Dense horripilation, [like] kadamba flowers, shines — as if he had put on a most charming jacket.
He trembles like a creeper in the wind, unable to steady himself — helpless, full of rasa.
Again and again, ceaselessly, he says: "Friend of the wretched!" — his heart melts, composure is far away.
He sings of [the Lord's] beautiful qualities, dances lightly, lightly; streams of tears flow from his eyes.
Narahari says: one cannot perceive how many, many transformations of bhava arise in his heart.[^23]

*Raga yatharaga*

**4.20.** Beautiful and refined is Gadadhara Dasa.
A jewel of virtues, he shines beside Gaura — like the moon's radiance beside the moon. *(Refrain)*
His most tender body is full of love; his sweet charm diminishes the pride of the champaka;
his bearing, which shatters composure, delights the world; his lotus feet stand motionless(?).
Both his eyes, full of languor, most charming, tremulous, — do not blink even for a moment;
his pure cheeks sparkle; how graceful the smile with his deep red lips!
Imperceptibly, ceaselessly, former feelings of many kinds arise in [his] heart;
however much he tries to conceal them — they cannot be concealed; Narahari, seeing [this], laughs, intoxicated with happiness.[^24]

*Raga yatharaga*

**4.21.** What shall I say? The associates are supremely magnanimous:
the nectar of the incomparable moon — Gaura's face — they drink ceaselessly. *(Refrain)*
So many, many efforts they make to keep their composure — [yet] every moment they grow restless, helpless, intoxicated with rasa.
Adorned with wondrous bhavas — abundant excellent ornaments — [they display] auspicious beauty of many kinds.
In someone the body bristles with horripilation, no word comes out — the beautiful(?) voice falters [with feeling];
in someone there is a trembling that makes people's minds tremble; from someone's eyes flow streams of tears;
someone whirls about, making gestures with his arms; someone utters the sweet name again and again;
someone laughs softly and dances again and again. — When will Narahari, seeing [this], spend [his life] fruitfully(?)?[^25]

*Shobha-chhandah*

**4.22.** Thus the refined associates
gaze again and again at the moon of Gaura's face.
Joyful delight arises; their minds are supremely glad;
[they] cherish various wishes, — says Narahari.

*Thus [ends] the fourth kirana of the Shrimad Gaura-charita-chintamani, called "The Description of [Gaura] Surrounded by Rows of Devotees, and Other Matters", in the description of the deeds of the morning period.*[^26]

[^1]: The name of the kirana is taken from its colophon at the end of the kirana (after 4.22, pdf 60, printed p. 55; read from the scan under strong magnification): "…pratah-kaliya-charitra-varnane bhaktavali-veshtitadi-varnanam nama chaturthah kiranah" — "the fourth kirana, called 'The Description of [Gaura] Surrounded by Rows of Devotees, and Other Matters', in the description of the deeds of the morning period". The OCR has *veṣṭitādri* ("the mountain surrounded…"), but the scan clearly reads *veṣṭitādi* (see note 26). The picture of Gaura among His associates as the golden mountain Sumeru surrounded by the gods is in 4.2. The subheading "Again, in the morning period…" (*punaḥ prātaḥkāle nijāṅganopaviṣṭasya saundaryādi-varṇanaṁ*) stands in the edition immediately after the colophon of kirana 3 (pdf 51, printed p. 46).
[^2]: The name of the metre is badly faded: it reads *ca…ṅgī chandaḥ*, probably *caturbhaṅgī*. The first line of the stanza (pdf 51) is almost effaced: only *āju niśi bhora …* ("today, [when] the night has ended"), *gaura(?)*, *kautuka* ("joyful play") and single letters can be read; this line is translated by sense, with ellipses for the illegible words. The stanza is a dandaka (prose-like); the printed lines wrap arbitrarily, and in the translation it is divided at the marks "।" and "॥". Each segment ends with a rhyming epithet that refers to all those named in it: *ravi-karacchaṭā*, *jagata-viditā*, *prema-muditā*, *śrīkarā*, *gaurasevana-tatparā*, *dāsābhirāmā*, *śobhānupamā*.
[^3]: A list of the Lord's associates in Navadvipa; the division into names and the identifications are tentative (cf. note 68 of kirana 2). Shrinivasa is Srivasa Pandita (cf. note 15 of kirana 1); Gadadhara is named twice (Gadadhara Pandita and, probably, Gadadhara Dasa); Mukunda twice (Mukunda and Mukunda Datta); Narahari — Narahari Sarakara (or the author's signature); Pundarika Vidyanidhi; Shriman Pandita; Hiranya (Pandita); Gauridasa Pandita; *vāsudeva dattācārya* — probably Vasudeva Datta; Bhagavata — probably Bhagavata Acharya; Chandrashekhara Acharya; Shuklambara Brahmachari; Shridhara (the seller of banana-leaf cups); *śrīkṛṣṇadāsa paṇḍita bhagavān* — possibly "Krishnadasa Pandita, Bhagavan"; Buddhimanta Khan; Gangadasa Pandita (Nimai's teacher); Nandana Acharya; Shivananda (Sena); *ratna* (between Nandanacharya and Mukunda Datta) — unclear, possibly Ratnagarbha Acharya or an epithet; Satyaraja Khan and Ramananda (Vasu) of Kulinagrama; *vāṇīnātha miśra madhusūdana* — possibly "Vaninatha, Mishra Madhusudana"; Jagannatha Mishra — probably the Lord's father (the scene takes place in His house); *gopīkānta paṇḍita maheśa* — possibly "Gopikanta Pandita, Mahesha"; Govinda, Madhava and Vasudeva (Ghosha); Ishana — Shachi's servant; Kashishvara (Pandita). The names Govinda, Jagannatha and Krishnadasa occur twice in the list — so in the edition. Signature: Ghanashyama.
[^4]: "King of the twice-born" (*dvijarāja*) also means "the moon": Gaurachandra is the moon among the brahmanas. Padapitha — a low seat (cf. 3.11, 3.13). Shesha — the thousand-mouthed serpent who sings the Lord's glory (cf. 4.10: "neither Shesha nor Sharada").
[^5]: "A painted doll" (*citraka putari*) — motionless with wonder. Signature: Narahari.
[^6]: Raga lalita is a morning raga. "Dances" — *nirata kirata* (*nirata* = *nṛtya*; *kirata* probably for *karata*). The second line has no closing mark in the edition. "Crying aloud" — *phutakāri* (meaning unclear; possibly "sobbing"); grass between the teeth is a sign of humility. "Surging" — *su-uthalala*. Signature: Narahari.
[^7]: The moons are Gaura and His associates. The threefold misery (*tāpatraya*) comes from one's own body and mind, from other beings and from the forces of nature. Arena (*raṅga-bhūmi*) — the place of the wrestlers' contest; the asuras are those who have turned away from the Lord. "A field without a fence" — *bā̐dha kari rahita* (lit. "devoid of dam/enclosure"): the fruits of the wish-fulfilling trees are open to all. Signature: Narahari.
[^8]: Trivikrama is the name of the metre; the stanza is a dandaka. Reading and meaning unclear: *prakāśāja(?)-suṣamā* is taken as "the beauty of [His] manifestation"; *bara-barada-ānanda(?)* as "pouring down bliss" (possibly *baraṣad*); *dara na nirata* as "unceasingly". The thought of the first line: the courtyard holds countless people and yet remains spacious — by the inconceivable power of the Lord. "Hypocrite" (*bhaṇḍa*), "a bull without a stick" — the author's self-deprecating signature (Narahari).
[^9]: Sudhamukhi is the name of the metre. *alinda* — the terrace (veranda) in front of a house.
[^10]: Chitra is the name of the metre; the stanza is a dandaka. "Gaura-Govinda" — Gaura, Krishna Himself. "A cloud of joy" — *haraṣa meha* (*meha* < *megha*). "Waves His arms" — *kuruthe* (probably for Skt. *kurute*). "The purest gold" — *daśabāṇa susvarṇa* (*daśabāṇa* — gold of the highest assay). Anuraga — the ever-increasing attachment of love. "The insignificant fool Ghanashyama" — the author's self-deprecating signature.
[^11]: Belavali (in print *belābalī*) is a raga. Nityananda is Balarama, Krishna's elder brother; Gaura is His younger Brother. "A wondrous dark body" — the form of Krishna in Vraja; "a hidden rasa" — Gaura is Krishna in the mood and lustre of Radha. Nityananda's speech about himself: "my" in lower case (by the project's rule). Reading and meaning unclear: *prasanna baraṇe abanāgi* is taken as "propitiating [the Creator], I shall ask a boon". "A body made of eyes" — cf. the lament over the lack of eyes in 3.4. "Narahari's Lord" (*narahari pahũ*) — Nityananda as the author's Lord; signature.
[^12]: Veloara (in print *beloyāra*) is a raga (cf. kirana 1). The son of Padmavati is Nityananda (Padmavati is His mother). "The golden-hued thief" — Gaura, who steals Nityananda's ornaments and clothing, that is, His composure. The chakora is a bird that drinks moonlight. Signature: Ghanashyama.
[^13]: "May I be offered up to it" — *balihārī*, an exclamation of admiration. Shesha and Sharada (Sarasvati) are paragons of eloquence. The last lines express a well-known thought: Gaura's mercy is attained by Nityananda's mercy. Signature: Narahari Dasa.
[^14]: Here Nityananda's beauty is described through its effect on women — the same device of poetic hyperbole of beauty as in the descriptions of Gaura (cf. kirana 3, 3.3, 3.12). Gaudiya Vaishnavas, following Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati and the *Chaitanya-bhagavata* (Adi 15: in His lila the Lord did not jest with women or even look at them), reject *gaura-nagari-vada*, the worship of Gaura as an enjoying lover: Gaura is Krishna in the mood of Radha, tasting Her love, not an enjoyer. "Deceives the world" (*jaga-vañcana*, refrain) — meaning unclear: possibly "concealing His [divine] nature from the world" or "surpassing [everything] in the world". Shirisha is a tree with very delicate flowers.
[^15]: Ananga (Kama) is the god of love; his bow is a usual comparison for the brows. Garuda's beak — a comparison for a straight, handsome nose. Triveni — the confluence of three rivers (Ganga, Yamuna and Sarasvati) at Prayaga; here, the three folds of the abdomen. The blue garment is the garment of Balarama. *cīna* — fine (Chinese) silk. "Knees" — *uru parva* (cf. 3.3). The stanza begins on pdf 55 and ends on pdf 56 (printed p. 51) with "॥১১॥"; in print *barikhe* ("pouring") looks like *baribe*. Signature: Narahari.
[^16]: Veloara is a raga (as in 4.9–4.11; "again" — *punaḥ*). "Slaps both His armpits" — *kakṣa-yuga-vādya*, lit. "playing on both armpits": a gesture of jubilation with which Advaita, according to the *Chaitanya-bhagavata*, expressed His joy. "Corpulent" — *tundila*. Reading and sense unclear: *diśā(!) jayakṛta* (after *diśā* there is a mark resembling "!") — taken as "victorious in all directions"; *lahu bahu hasata* — perhaps for *lahu lahu* ("very softly"); *sughara lobha rijha āra* — taken as "the connoisseurs' longing and delight grow still more". Kunda — white jasmine: a comparison for the radiance of the teeth. "The Lord is overcome" — *pahuka parābhava jāni*: taken as "Advaita is overcome by prema" (possibly: "[the pearls] are defeated by the Lord['s tears]"). Signature: Narahari.

Sense unclear: *aiche madana-mada na rahala pora* — taken as "thus even the pride of Madana (Kama) did not survive" (before Gaura's golden beauty). Lines 3–8 are understood as Advaita's reflection. "Deliverer" — the first letter of the word is damaged: *?āraṇa* (*tāraṇa* / *vāraṇa* / *hāraṇa*); in any case the sense is "delivering, dispelling". "Passed beyond hearing" — *śravaṇa-gata*, taken as "is no longer heard". "The opposite of joy" — sorrow. "Who [now] is left without the right?" — *ko adhikāra hīna karu*: taken as "no one is left without the right [to prema]". Signature: Narahari.
[^18]: The Father of Achyuta — Advaita (Achyutananda is His son). "The king of the twice-born" — Gaura (*dvija-rāja*, also "the moon"; cf. note 4). "Like a pauper" — *raṅka-sama*: like a poor man eagerly seizing a treasure he has found. Nirmanchhana — see note 3 of kirana 3. "I have won!" — probably Advaita's triumph, whose calls brought the Lord into the world. "Immersed" — in print *bhora* (also resembles *tora*). Haridasa — probably Haridasa Thakura, a close companion of Advaita. Narahari — the author's signature woven into the list.
[^19]: Shona (*śoṇa*, print unclear) — cf. 3.3 (perhaps *sona*, "gold"). Ananga (Kama) — the "bodiless" god of love: his beauty seems to have found shelter in Advaita's limbs. "[Crush] the hosts of lotuses" — reading and sense unclear: *kamala kuladala laye* (or *naye*); taken as *kamala-kula dalaye* ("crush the multitude of lotuses"). "Offers himself" — *nichani* (the nichhani rite). Signature: Ghanashyama.

Shirisha — a tree with very tender flowers (cf. 4.11). "Charming garment" — *sarasāṃśuka* (perhaps "moist garment"). Signature: Narahari.
[^21]: Gadadhara Pandita; "golden body" — *gaura-tanu*. Kama — the god of love: even many Kamas offer themselves in nirmanchhana to his radiance. Reading and sense unclear: *bhari bhari pari pīyata* (*pari* — "thus, like"?); *śunata lubadha śruti śruti bāñchata bahu bisarita beda śrabaṇa śrutitantra* — taken as "the ears, [and even] the shrutis (the Vedas), long [for his words]; the hearing of the Vedas is forgotten". "Former deeds" — the lila of Vraja: in Krishna's lila Gadadhara is Srimati Radhika (*Gaura-ganoddesha-dipika*). "Gold is left far behind" — *dūre rahu hema*: gold cannot compare [with his complexion]. Signature: Narahari.
