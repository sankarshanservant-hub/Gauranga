# Kirana 3. The Deeds of the Morning: Description of the Waking (Shayyotthana)[^1]

*Pratah — morning in Navadvipa: Gaura is still asleep and speaks in His sleep; the associates, hearing His words, rise from their beds and go to His bedchamber to wake Him*

*Now — the deeds of the morning period.*

*Kamala-chhandah*

**3.1.** Gaura the moon, best of rasikas, accomplished, the crown of my head —
at the close of night, in happy sleep, absorbed in rasa —
on a lovely bed, the supreme abode of beauty,
all full of rasa, languid, shines beyond compare.
[His] deeds are most lovely; matchlessly deep is [His] sleep;
as if in a dream He speaks many words — there is no end to them.
What poet could compose all those streams of nectar?
Filling their ears [with them], the associates drink [them] unceasingly.
Each leaving his own bed, in great joy,
every limb covered with abundant fresh thrills [of ecstasy],
absorbed in rasa, smiling, speaking the sweetest words,
[they] go to the Lord's bedchamber, — [says] the servant Narahari.[^2]

*Kamala-chhandah*

**3.2.** Gaura's associates — the greatest rasikas, excellent, the crown of the steadfast.
Who can fathom their feelings? Their hearts, [full of] fresh joy, know no rest.
Walking slowly, they all came near to the abode of bliss.
Many stand leaning against the lovely walls, — in a matchless way;
gracefully [bending], pressing their eyes to the window, with great eagerness,
they plunge into the ocean of the beauty of Gaura, shining on the bed.
At once, [as if] offering their bodies [to Him] in nirmanchhana, — the longings of the heart have no end, —
they again say something to one another, — [says] Narahari, — immersed in joyful delight.[^3]

*Kumari-chhandah (someone says)*[^4]

**3.3.** Look, look: beautiful, excellent young Gaura,
who crushes the pride of Manmatha, the thief of the hearts of young maidens![^5]
[He is] steady lightning, the king of gold(?), of ketaki and champaka;
[His] form surpasses thick kunkuma and the golden mountain.[^6]
The curves of [His] limbs are wondrous, full of rasa, abounding in grace;
adorned with a host of various jewelled ornaments, [He] steals composure.
[His] face puts to shame heaps of lotuses — a lovely, sweet radiance;
the lips subdue the [redness of the] bimba, the teeth are kunda and pearls.
Matchless are the corners of the mouth, lovely the chin — so [He] shines;
the ears adorned with earrings and flowers, the charming cheeks;
the nose shines, lovely and excellent are the eyes — the delight of young maidens(?);
the brows are serpents, bees; the forehead gleams, an abode of beauty,
like the moon; bright are the sandal tilaka and the curls;
the hair — rare(?) hair; the fresh attire slightly loosened.[^7]
The neck with three lines, the high shoulders — exceedingly lovely;
the arms long, [like] door-bars, dispel the pride of elephants' trunks;
the chest, marked with barely visible [auspicious] signs, is very broad;
the navel is deep, the slender waist wrapped in blue cloth.
The shanks — as if the plantain trunk, [which strove to rival them], lost its pride;
the wondrously formed knees put golden caskets to shame.
The tenderest ankles and heels are more lovely still;
the soles are land-lotuses, [like] the young dawn.
The star-like toenails gleam, adorning [the feet] like jewelled mirrors.
Narahari, offering [himself to Him in] nirmanchhana, always meditates on [Him] in his heart.[^8]

*And another — in raga vibhasha*

**3.4.** Ah, ah! Wondrous is Gaura's form —
the world-enchanting king Manmatha.
What am I to do? I was not given countless eyes:
cruel fate has brought [me] sorrow in beholding.[^9]
I keep this longing, filling my heart with it,
and, like a priceless jewel, I do not reveal it.
Who fashioned such shapely curves of [His] body?
[He] is my all in all, matchless in this world.
Every day at the close of night I see [Him],
but such sleep I have never seen.
So I understand: a new sleep reigns [now].
Narahari: will [anyone] wake [Him] today in such [a sleep]?

*(And another — raga bhairava)*

**3.5.** Blessed, blessed — I count tonight blessed:
such sleep of the Lord, who thirsts for the rasa of sankirtana, I have never seen! *(Refrain)*
He, maddened by the fullness of His former mood, ceaselessly speaks of beautiful Vraja;
the lids of His eyes never close even for a moment — staying awake all night, He meets the dawn.
Now He is sunk in matchless sleep — will He wake? This is a play more wondrous still.
Wondrous is the way of [His] sleep: as if in a dream, He utters words like nectar.
The night has gone, morning has come, — and still He who sports in Nadiya does not wake.
How to fathom these deeds of Narahari's Lord? Whether this sleep will break — I cannot say.[^10]

*And another — raga lalita*

**3.6.** Behold the wondrous play of the Lord!
[His] graceful, deep, sweet sleep gives rise to the joy of body, mind and eyes. *(Refrain)*
He whose bodily radiance does not appear even a little in the hearts of the gods, however much they strive, —
that mass of radiance, by [our] good fortune(?), here in the chamber flashes, surpassing rows of suns.
The sages, to picture [Him] on the lovely bed of their hearts, perform sacrifices kalpa after kalpa;
unattainable to them, He is attainable to this prepared bed, — what poet could describe its fortune?
The praises of Brahma and Shiva do not delight [Him], yet now He is pleased, hearing the parrot in the new cage speak at length.
How long will Narahari's Lord stay hidden? That former way [of His] is plainly manifest.[^11]

*And another — raga lalita*

**3.7.** Look, gaze at Gauranga!
Who knows such sleep?
What postures of hands and feet!
Is it like this in sleep? — I never knew.
The eyes are lovely, and [what] grace in them!
Why should it be so in drowsiness?
The beauty of the moon-face has so increased —
a gentle smile, as if nectar were dripping.
Sleep or no sleep — I cannot tell;
what is in my mind — I cannot say.
Narahari: how much can one say of this?
I think it will be hard to wake [Him].

*And another — raga lalita*

**3.8.** Listen, listen, oh! I understand nothing: by what rasa is He [so] absorbed?
The night is over, and still the world-enchanting Gora sleeps(?).
And look: Gadadhara, his eyes fixed on the face of Gauranga the moon,
sits at [His] feet and, smiling, happily presses the feet.
Narahari, gazing at Gauranga, floats in an ocean of happiness
and, [bending] wondrously, says something into Gadadhara's ear.
Some, intoxicated with the rasa of Gora, sway and fall — [all are] bewildered;
some earnestly try to wake Narahari's Lord of life.[^12]

*Raga yatharaga*

**3.9.** At the night's sleep of Gauranga the moon
all gaze and gaze, refreshing their eyes.
With one another, their hearts full of joy,
they merrily talk of many things.
Can a poet describe this —
the matchless virtues of Gauranga?
Again and again, filling their eyes, they behold
the sweetness of the sleep of Narahari's Lord.

*Raga yatharaga*

**3.10.** Beautiful young Gaura still lies [asleep].
The sun has come to the east, people have woken and gone [about their tasks]; night has turned to dawn. *(Refrain)*
Someone, wholly absorbed, recites the sweetest verses, stanza after stanza, in a most wondrous way;
someone, together with many instruments, beautifully sings songs of the Lord's loving deeds;
someone composes lovely [verses]; someone, as is due, loudly utters the Name;
someone, intoxicated with bhakti-rasa out of humility(?), — cannot utter words clearly(?).
The cows low, full [of milk], anxious to suckle their calves;
hearing their voices, the Lord at once awoke and sat up on the bed.
Then, stretching, covering both eyes with His lovely hands, He yawned a little;
leaving the chamber, He goes with a restless heart and keeps asking(?) for the milking rope.
Seeing this, Gauridasa and the others, one after another, joyfully call [Him] by His former name.
Narahari says: wondrous is this lovely deed — is it deep sleep, or a play of prema?[^13]

*Raga yatharaga*

**3.11.** Gaurachandra shines — the jewel of virtues.
He reveals a wondrous former mood, — His heart is joyful, and again shyness arises. *(Refrain)*
Smiling softly, softly, He glances(?) — matchless is [His] grace(?);
He speaks words full of supreme nectar, and with a hundred stratagems hides [them].
The lotus eyes are motionless(?); greatly abashed, He stands with head bowed, looking at the ground.
He steals the hearts of His dear ones; ever-new beauty — more than can be described.
The loosened lovely hair, the attire, the garments, the garland slip as He gently walks;
quickly He sat on the seat (padapitha) — Narahari's sight, beholding [this], bore its fruit.[^14]

[^1]: The name of the kirana is taken from its colophon (from the OCR; at the end of the kirana, after 3.13, ≈ pdf 51; not checked against the image): "…pratah-kaliya-charitra-varnane shayyotthana-varnanam nama tritiyah kiranah" — "the third kirana, called 'The Description of the Waking (lit. rising from the bed)', in the description of the deeds of the morning period". Pratah (morning) is the second of the eight periods of the ashtakaliya-lila (cf. 1.59, 1.61). The subheading "Now — the deeds of the morning period" (*atha prātakālīya caritra*) stands in the edition immediately after the colophon of kirana 2 (pdf 45, printed p. 40).
[^2]: Kamala is the name of the metre. *sughara* — "accomplished"; *śira mora* — "the crown of my head". For Gaura's words in His sleep see 2.25a. "Compose" — in print *viracana* ("composition"), probably for *viracaba*. The subject of the last lines is the associates; "the servant Narahari" is the author's signature (perhaps he too goes with them, cf. 2.15a).
[^3]: "Stand leaning against the walls" — *ṭhāṭi … bhīta kṛta avalamba* (*bhīta* < *bhitti*, "wall"). Nirmanchhana is the rite of waving a lamp or other offering before a revered person to ward off misfortune; "to offer oneself in nirmanchhana" is to give oneself wholly out of love. In the edition the fifth line ends with "॥" instead of "।".
[^4]: "Someone says" — in the edition in brackets *(ko 'py āha)*; the headings of 3.4–3.8 then begin with *ko 'pi ca* — "and another [says / sings]": each stanza is spoken by one of the associates gazing at the sleeping Lord.
[^5]: Here and below (3.3: "steals composure", "the delight of young maidens") Gaura's beauty is described through its effect on women — a poetic hyperbole of beauty (cf. "who crushes the pride of Manmatha", i.e. Kama). Gaudiya Vaishnavas, following Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati and the *Chaitanya-bhagavata* (Adi 15: in His lila the Lord did not jest with women or even look at them), reject *gaura-nagari-vada*, the worship of Gaura as an enjoying lover: Gaura is Krishna in the mood of Radha, tasting Her love, not an enjoyer.
[^6]: "Steady lightning" (*vidyut-thira*) is a usual comparison for Gaura's golden body. Reading unclear: *śoṇa* (distinct; "red") is taken as *sona* ("gold"). Ketaki and champaka are golden-yellow flowers. *nandita* — perhaps for *nindita* ("put to shame"). The golden mountain is Sumeru.
[^7]: The bimba is a red fruit, the kunda a white jasmine. *sṛkkaṇi* — the corners of the mouth. Reading and meaning unclear: *taruṇi-rāma* is taken as "the delight of young maidens" (the editor marks the place as doubtful in a note); *keśa keśa-dulabha* is taken as "rare hair" (perhaps *keśa* = *ka-īśa*, Brahma and Shiva: "[a beauty] unattainable even to Brahma and Shiva"). In the edition the line "the brows … an abode of beauty" ends without punctuation.
[^8]: Three lines (folds) on the neck, long arms and a broad chest are marks of a great personality (mahapurusha). "Door-bars" (*argala*) are a usual comparison for long, strong arms. The plantain trunk (*rambhā*) is a traditional comparison for shanks and thighs. "Knees" — *uru-parva* (lit. "the joints of the thighs"); "ankles" — in print *gulka* (= *gulpha*). The land-lotus (*sthala-padma*) is a red flower. Signature: Narahari.
[^9]: The lament that the Creator did not give countless eyes with which to behold the Lord to the full is a well-known motif (cf. the gopis, who reproach the Creator for making eyelids). *mari mari* is an exclamation of wonder. The ragas vibhasha (in print *vibhāṣa*) and lalita are morning ragas.
[^10]: In the edition the heading is in brackets; with a mark the editor notes that this pada is missing in one of the manuscripts. *saṅkīrtana-rasa-lampaṭa* — "who thirsts for the rasa of sankirtana". "Former mood" — the mood of the Vraja lila: Gaura is Krishna, who has come to taste Radha's love; whole nights He speaks of Vraja and does not close His eyes. "He who sports in Nadiya" — *nadīyā-vihārī*. "Break" — *bhāgaba*. The first two lines are the refrain (*dhru*).
[^11]: Reading and meaning unclear: *sukṛti* is taken as "by good fortune" (perhaps "a fair creation"). A pun: *kalapayite* ("to picture, imagine") and *kalapa* ("kalpa", a world age). "This prepared bed" — *talapa parikalapana*. "Brahma and Shiva" — *vihi bhava* (*vidhi* — Brahma, *bhava* — Shiva). The parrot — cf. 2.15a. "Former way" — the way of Krishna in Vraja (cf. 3.5).
[^12]: Reading unclear: *ghumā?yā* ("sleeps"; the middle letter is indistinct). Gadadhara — cf. 2.14–2.16. Narahari speaking into Gadadhara's ear is the author's signature woven into the narrative (perhaps Narahari Sarkara, the Lord's associate; cf. note 51 of kirana 2). *anubandha* — "earnest effort". The last line also contains the signature: "Narahari's Lord of life" (*narahari prāṇanāthe*).
[^13]: "As is due" — *niyamita* (perhaps "a fixed number of times"). Reading and meaning unclear: *dainya-kṛta* (the first letter is worn away) is taken as "out of humility"; *śabada ghaṭāpaṭatara nāhi hoya* is taken as "words do not come out clearly". "The milking rope" — *chā̐dana ḍora*: the rope used to hobble a cow's legs during milking; waking to the lowing of the cows, Gaura, in the mood of Krishna the cowherd boy, sets out to milk. "Asking" — in print *nāgata*, taken as *māgata*. Gauridasa Pandita is Subala, Krishna's friend, in Krishna's lila (*Gaura-ganoddesha-dipika*); "by His former name" — probably by a name of Krishna from the Vraja lila.
[^14]: Reading and meaning unclear: *vadanaku?saṅga cāyata* (a blot between *ku* and *saṅga*; OCR *vadanakupasga*) is taken as "glances"; *anupama mahi bisāi* (OCR *mati*) — meaning unclear; *agola* — perhaps for *alola* ("motionless"). Padapitha — a low seat (in 3.12 Gaura sits on it like Indra in Amaravati). The stanza begins on pdf 50 and ends on pdf 51 (printed p. 46) with "॥১১॥".
