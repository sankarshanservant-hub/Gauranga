# Kirana 7. The Deeds of the Morning: The Description of the Conduct of the Nagaris of Shri Navadvipa[^1]

*Morning in Navadvipa: the young women of Nadia (the nagaris), filled with prema for Gaura, gather in secret at dawn on the pretext of household tasks and tell one another how, the day before, having outwitted their mothers-in-law, sisters-in-law and aunts, they managed to see Gaura on the road, if only for a moment*

*Again, in the morning period — the conversations with one another and so on of the nagaris of Shri Navadvipa, absorbed in prema for Him.*

*Vibhasha*

**7.1.** The joy there is in Shachi's house at the dawn of the night —
even in hundreds upon hundreds of yugas, with a thousand mouths, not the smallest part of it could be described.
How many playful ways Gaurachandra knows to give happiness to His own!
Surely, in the middle of the courtyard He has spread a snare that enchants the worlds:
leaving their beds, running, running, all the people come, full of joy,
sink into that ocean of beauty — and cannot turn back home again.
A most wondrous love arises unceasingly in everyone's heart;
at those crimson feet they offer body and mind, says Dasa Narahari.[^2]

*Raga yatharaga*

**7.2.** Shachi's darling is love embodied; His fame fills the world.
There is no one who, having once seen the sweetness of that form, would not be enchanted.
Most wondrous is [His] rasikata — one cannot understand what quality is in Him:
no one in the world can live without loving Gaurahari.
And the tenderness these nagaris of Nadia have for Gauranga —
is there anyone who keeps composure while telling or hearing of it?
Gora is their japa, tapas, meditation and contemplation; their minds know nothing else.
Without the sight of Gorachandra for even half a moment, they regard everything as empty.
Gora is their life, wealth, being and [the honour of their] birth; that Gora is the pupil of their eyes.
Lying down and in dreams, saying "Gora, Gora!", they have become like madwomen.
To composure, dharma, modesty and fear for [the honour of] the family they have given tilanjali;
they always wish for happiness only in Gora's happiness, sings Dasa Narahari.[^3]

*Raga yatharaga*

**7.3.** Ah, ah! Let me take upon myself the misfortunes of such nagaris of Nadia:
this night they all spent in great longing.
Some stayed awake all night, singing the deeds of Gorachandra;
some, falling happily asleep, found in dreams the moon — Gaura.
And again, leaving their beds, they rose at the dawn of the night
and came out of this house and that house on the pretext of tasks.
The nagaris are supremely clever — I cannot understand their conduct at all:
whatever task pleases their elders, that task they do with care;
with their permission they come and go — what more can I say?
Such is their custom day after day — and there is no limit to [their] happiness.
Unnoticed, in a most secluded place, the lovely young [women], the allure of the world, sit down —
little by little they all gathered there; Narahari beholds [that] beauty.[^4]

*Raga yatharaga*

**7.4.** What can I say of the love the young women have for one another?
Their bodies are different — their mind is one; who has the power to understand this tenderness?
Some rasika, laughing and laughing, throws her arms around someone's neck;
someone teases someone else, using all kinds of tricks.
Someone says: "Leave off pretending, tell us about yesterday — let us hear:
who had what obstacle, and how did each one see Gaura the jewel?"
Someone says: "Come now, tell us how you spent this night."
Narahari says: it would be good to tell all these stories in detail.[^5]

*A certain [woman] says — yatharaga*

**7.5.** What are you asking, friend, about yesterday? When I start to tell it, I feel like laughing.
Casting off modesty, I shall tell how I saw the moon of Nadia.
At the close of day my mother-in-law, my sister-in-law and some others [were at home];
I was sitting beside them, making a show of my good behaviour.
At that very moment the Lord of my life came along our road.
Hearing [of it], I started, looked all around — and became utterly restless.
I fell into a terrible predicament: not a single thought came into my mind;
my heart was in turmoil — what to do? — tears flowed secretly from my eyes.
Seeing me so distracted, my mother-in-law speaks sweet words:
"Why are you like this, child? I don't know — do you have some pain?"
As soon as she said this, I told her: "I feel somehow unwell."
Hearing that, she gave permission: "Go and lie down in the house."
On the pretext of lying down, I quickly went and stood outside the house;
seeing the sweetness of that face just once, I cooled my eyes and heart.
No one could notice anything, and my whole body was filled with bliss.
Narahari says: who will understand a rasika's cleverness?[^6]

*And a certain [other] — yatharaga*

**7.6.** What can I say about yesterday, friend? When I start to tell it, my heart weeps.
I saw — and yet could not see enough of the moon of Nadia, my life and soul.
Listen to that story: I was sitting alone, quite in seclusion;
suddenly I hear from people's mouths that Gaura is passing by.
Going out quickly, I saw Him together with His dear associates;
flashing like lightning, He went on His way.
Casting off modesty, having seen that face only once, I lost myself completely;
for fear of the elders I hurried home, my heart all but breaking.
I don't know who told it to my mother-in-law;
hearing it, with a fierce face she rushed at me in anger.
How many harsh words she spoke! Hearing them, I trembled with fear.
"I did not see [Him]!" I said, and, swearing an oath, touched her feet.
Telling so many untruths, I came out the good girl — she believed it.
Narahari says: there is a fault in this, and you do not admit it — that is not good.[^7]

*And a certain [other] — yatharaga*

**7.7.** Shamelessly I'll tell it, friend — listen to my words:
pitiless Providence yesterday gave me cruel pain.
After many days my aunt came to my house;
with joy in my heart I went and sat beside her, leaving everything.
At that very moment I hear that Gaura-nagara is passing by —
and I became most eager to go out of the door.
But whenever I think of getting up — oh! — she looks at me again,
takes hold of the edge of my sari and carefully seats me, conjuring me by her head.
Her guileless heart understands nothing of all this:
in every way, with concern, she asks about the household affairs.
But my heart is in turmoil — I hear it and do not hear with my ears;
what I mean to say, what I do say — neither good nor bad stays in my mind.
She shows me love, as is fitting, but to me it is like poison;
I cannot show it outwardly — from constraint my insides burn.
Seeing such a plight, I thought: better I should leave this body.
Narahari says: don't you know a trick to get round your aunt?[^8]

*And a certain [other] — yatharaga*

**7.8.** Listen, friend: as I was coming alone from the ghat of the Suradhuni,
I met the moon of Nadia on the road.
What wondrous sweetness! His sweet gait surpasses an elephant's;
I don't know who fashioned this form, love embodied, or how.
What comparison can I give? — I thought: Gora in a new attire…
I would keep Him in the depth of my heart, or make Him the pupil of my eyes!
At the sight of that face, composure, dharma and my inmost [feeling] were left far behind;
the pot on my hip fell to the ground and broke into a hundred pieces.
What could I do? Having joyfully given my life and youth to the Beloved of my heart,
for fear of the elders I came home and sat with sorrow in my heart.
I don't know who told my sister-in-law about the broken pot;
in terrible anger, like a forest fire, she came rushing at me.
Seeing her fearsome look, [I knew] no excuse would work;
clasping my head with both hands, I wept so much that my eyes swelled.
Seeing my extraordinary weeping, her anger left her;
growing calm, she asks again and again — but I gave her no answer.
After a while, having thought it over, she took me by the hand
and says softly: "Why won't you tell me what is in your heart?"
With great effort, in a choking voice, I spoke to her:
"I am weeping from grief of heart — why ask about all this in vain?
Those whom you hold dear as life — they do dreadful things:
at the ghats, in the fields, on the roads they slander you; hearing it, I am ashamed.
I thought of tying the pot to my neck and entering the waters of the Ganga —
but I could not do it, lest a stain fall on the family.
What am I to do? I cannot quarrel with all of them!
Hearing all the disgrace that has come upon you, I was utterly bewildered.
Whom could I tell? Not one friend was with me there —
in anger at them all I broke the pot on the road."
Hearing this, she was greatly delighted at heart and, showing me love,
consoling me in every way, wiped my face with her own hand.
Thus yesterday, by being bold, I escaped a terrible plight.
Narahari says: let me take upon myself the misfortunes of your cleverness![^9]

*And a certain [other] — yatharaga*

**7.9.** What shall I tell you, friend, about my sister-in-law! When I start to tell it, I cannot help laughing:
she is a faithful wife, and by her reckoning all the women of Nadia are unfaithful.
And, worse still, she will not let me exchange a word with anyone;
she scolds me all the time; I dare not go anywhere alone for fear.
In heartache I languish day and night, hearing her reproaches.
I prayed to the Creator: may her pride be brought low!
I do not know by what merit the kind Creator heard my plea:
what I had in mind came true at once, as it were.
Listen, then, to what happened: yesterday she set out, taking a water pot,
and I, having got her permission, followed close behind her.
On the road by which the two of us go to the ghat of the Suradhuni —
on that very road Gora was standing with His dear associates.
Seeing that sweetness of His form, my sister-in-law could not keep her composure:
a violent trembling seized her, her body shook under the onslaught of Madana.
The pot fell from her hip to the ground, the hair on her head came loose;
her garment slipped from her body of itself — and not a trace of self-awareness remained.
With great effort she regained her composure and, deeply ashamed,
took me by both hands and said very softly, looking into my face:
"Know for certain, virtuous sister-in-law: you are dearer to me than life.
Whatever I have said to you — bear me no grudge: I am in your power.
Whatever you do at any time, tell me about it without hesitation:
I will help you, giving my life and my wealth — I say this on oath.
Do not tell others about all this — keep it secret.
I am caught in this rasa — what can I say? — Gaurahari has driven me mad!"
Much like this she said; hearing it, I was filled with boundless joy,
but when I thought back on what had gone before, great bitterness welled up.
I thought to myself: what is the use of disclosing all this?
Narahari says: one whose ways are those of the virtuous guards the honour of others.[^10]

*And a certain [other] — yatharaga*

**7.10.** Listen, listen, friend of my heart: knowing that you feel for me, I tell you.
The ways of my household are unheard of anywhere in the land: when I begin to tell of them, my heart weeps.
If I so much as sigh, saying "Gora!", the old woman blazes with anger on hearing it;
my sister-in-law is like a deadly poison: through her "virtues" my very life is scorched;
the neighbours are nothing but thorns to the family: day and night they taunt me with this.
Whom, friend, can I call as witness to this? I live in the house like a bird in a cage.
What more can I say of all that? In the telling, my sorrow has no shore.
Yesterday the Creator was kind to me: He placed the moon of the sky in my hand.
At the close of day Gaura-raya was walking along our road;
hurrying to the window, I gazed at Him unseen.
How sweet is that moon of His face — a snare for the hearts of young women!
The pair of brows, curved in an exquisite arch: what woman could keep her composure?
The eyes dance, surpassing the khanjana birds — I understood: from them no one escapes.
On His neck a garland of flowers sways — at the sight of it how many Kamadevas swoon!
What more can I say of that wondrous beauty? His gait enchants the worlds.
I was able to see Him there only for a moment — and the ache of my heart was doubled.
Narahari says: the sorrow will not remain — all will be as your heart wishes.[^11]

*And a certain [other] — yatharaga*

**7.11.** What shall I tell you, friend, about our household? If you hear it all, it will pain you.
Yesterday my night turned into a happy dawn: in solitude I saw the moon Gaura.
To blazes with it — what is shame to me now? I will tell you something of that story.
Leaving me with my sister-in-law, my mother-in-law went off to that [far] quarter of the village.
Here my sister-in-law picked a quarrel and said many hurtful things to me;
to have her own way she dropped everything and went off angrily to someone else's house.
I stayed at home alone; I went and sat by the window;
thinking in my mind of Gaura's beauty and virtues, I kept gazing at the road.
At that very moment Gauranga appeared alone on our road.
Unseen, beholding that moon-face, I forgot a little of my heart's pain;
quickly I dropped a withered kumuda bud through the window.
Seeing it, Gora — so quick-witted! — took it in His hand and said to the kumuda:
"Do not grieve: the moon will rise, and the sun's heat will go far away."
Having said this, He smiled and glanced at me once from the corner of His eyes.
At once my body went limp, as if a deadly snake had bitten me;
try as I might, I could not keep my composure: I longed to go and touch Him.
Again and again I trembled, my body broke out in sweat; I stood up, but my feet would not walk.
What can I say? Consoling my heart, I stayed there in great distress.
Just then my mother-in-law came home and asked me: "Why are you like this?"
Very piteously I said to her: "My sister-in-law will not let me live in the house.
When you yourself are at home she says nothing, but inside she burns like fire.
You left the house and went there — and here she started a quarrel with me.
There is no end to the telling of it; in this I was not at all to blame.
If you have any doubt in your mind, ask these neighbours.
What can I say? Leaving me alone, my sister-in-law went off to someone else's house —
from this you can see how much sense she has. And why I am like this — listen:
first, being alone, there was fear in my heart; and on top of that I was worrying about the housework;
what could I do — I had to work very hard, and that is why I became giddy."
Hearing my faltering words, she lovingly laid her own hand on my head;
fanning me with the end of her own cloth and holding my hand, she comforted me at length;
and she called my sister-in-law and scolded her — hearing that, I rejoiced.
Narahari says: blessed are you! Does anyone else know such a clever trick?[^12]

*And a certain [other] — yatharaga*

**7.12.** Listen, friend, I will tell you: I do not know what has happened to me.
Hastily putting on a new sari, I set out alone for my brother's house.
On the way I met Gora — what can I say? His beauty is beyond description.
Once He glanced at me — I do not know what He did with the corner of His eyes:
composure, dharma, modesty — all of it was struck down at that very moment, as it were.
My heart is all astir; I console it, yet cannot calm it.
Restless, I want to walk, but my feet will not move; my body trembles and trembles;
again and again my garment slips from my body; seeing all this, He smiles once more.
What could I do? For fear of my elders I somehow made my way home.
And again my body is restless without the darshan of that Gaurasundara.
At that very moment my mother-in-law came, sat down beside me and asked:
"Why do I see you like this today? Your eyes are brimming with tears;
you speak so sorrowfully — I do not know what pain has come to you."
Hearing this, I said to her: "I had gone out to the outer gate,
and there I saw a terrible snake: my heart trembled, my courage left me.
It came darting at me again — in panic I ran away;
then it slid off into the ditch — I was saved by the power of your feet."
Hearing this, she was greatly alarmed and herself splashed water on my eyes and face;
laying her own hand on my head, in her heart's fear she made many vows to Shiva.
Narahari says: nothing daunts you — you certainly know how to fool a mother-in-law![^13]

*And a certain [other] — yatharaga*

**7.13.** Listen, listen, friend: at my mother-in-law's bidding I went to do the cooking.
In great haste, with much care, I lit the stove.
On my wrist was my golden bangle — suddenly I caught sight of it,
and at once golden-hued Gaura-raya came to my mind.
No awareness remained: falling to the ground, I closed my eyes.
"What has happened?" crying, my mother-in-law, distraught at the sight of me, caught hold of me.
At that very moment my aunt arrived, and [my mother-in-law] spoke with her:
"The daughter-in-law I longed for so much — and seeing this, I suffer terrible pain.
Now and then she becomes like this — oh, it frightens me.
How much money has been spent — how many more times must I go to the exorcist's house?"
Hearing all this, my aunt says: "Do not worry.
No expense will be needed — I will tell you a simple remedy.
The other day I saw Nimai, the son of Shachi, to my eyes' content.
What power is in His feet I cannot understand at all:
many people, afflicted by many ailments, came to Him,
and with the touch of His feet He took away their suffering — what more can I say!
If somehow you can have Him touch her with His feet,
she will recover without trouble — be sure to do it."
My mother-in-law says: "Good, good that you reminded me — I too have heard of it.
But how can we go there now? Tell me a way."
So the two of them, seeing no way out, ponder, clutching their heads.
At that very moment the moon Gaura appeared on the road.
Hearing of it from people, my mother-in-law ran, took Gauranga by the hand,
and pleaded: "Come to my house, just for a moment!"
Hearing this, He came quickly; my mother-in-law entreated Him again:
"Be merciful — touch these crimson feet to my daughter-in-law's body.
Do not hesitate in the least, Nimai: good fame will be Yours in the world.
By the touch of Your feet all her troubles will go."
Hearing this, Gora, the ocean of virtues, covering His face with His cloth,
very slowly placed His lovely foot upon my breast.
At once I regained consciousness and opened both my eyes;
beholding the sweetness of that beautiful form, I floated on an ocean of happiness.
Rising quickly and setting my clothes in order, I went and sat down to the cooking.
My mother-in-law and my aunt, looking at me, were filled with joy;
how many blessings they gave Gorachanda — their hearts could not contain their delight!
And He, joining His associates, went on to the house of Nandanacharya.
Again and again the two of them praised [Him] and made Gauranga their all.
Hearing all that talk — what was in my heart, how can I say more?
Narahari says: you are very clever, and besides, your mother-in-law is fond of you;
had your sister-in-law been there, there would have been trouble — none of this would have happened.[^14]

*And a certain [other] — yatharaga*

**7.14.** However much I tell of yesterday, I cannot tell it all; listen, friend of my heart.
My sister-in-law is dreadful; I will tell you about her ways.
Having prayed to the Creator, at daybreak, gathering her own [friends],
she holds long counsel on how to pick a quarrel with me.
Finding no fault in me, she speaks to my mother-in-law in private:
"Know for certain: thanks to the 'exploits' of your daughter-in-law we shall not be able to stay in this land.
Whenever I send her on some errand, she sits there and stays on;
she is forever speaking ill-omened words about her own husband to everyone;
she plots your ruin and mine — she wants to be [mistress] alone.
The quarrels she picks with me are beyond counting.
Dressing herself up, she goes strutting about wherever she pleases;
so many people say so much about her — hearing it, my whole body burns as if on fire.
And there is one more thing I must tell — I cannot keep it to myself:
your daughter-in-law will surely bring disgrace [on the family] with a brahmana's son."
Hearing all this, my mother-in-law says: "I understand; [but] how shall I know [the truth] without looking [myself]?
When I see it with my own eyes, then I will believe."
My sister-in-law fell silent; my mother-in-law went off about her own work.
And [my sister-in-law], put to shame, again gathered her own and laid a plan:
"Good — now her mother-in-law is not at home.
Let her stay alone; I will leave her and go for water.
On the road I shall find some young man
and send him quickly to the house in mother's name.
He will sit waiting in the house, and I, having fetched water, will hurry back;
seeing him, I will angrily call the neighbours together,
show him to them all, and quarrel with the daughter-in-law;
I will throw the man out, again saying many bad things [about her].
To the old woman I will say: 'When you went over there, I went for water —
and here your daughter-in-law entertained a strange man in the house.'
If she will not believe it, then I will say in anger:
'Oh, she is very virtuous indeed, your daughter-in-law — just ask the neighbours!'
Hearing so much from the neighbours, she will question them and believe;
she will make the daughter-in-law's life unbearable — what joy that will be for me!"
Having reasoned thus, haughtily putting on a new sari,
she went to fetch water, leaving me alone at home.
Glad at heart, in great haste — by the very road my sister-in-law takes,
by that road comes Gaura-raya with His associates.
Seeing the sweetness of that form, my sister-in-law lost her senses again and again;
every scheme in her heart — she forgot it all.
Along that road came my mother-in-law and, looking, sees her close by:
with the water pot on her hip, [my sister-in-law] is wandering along behind the moon Gauranga.
"Fine, fine!" saying, in great anger [my mother-in-law] snatched the pot away.
What could my sister-in-law say, and to whom? At once she was mortified to the core.
Meanwhile I, finding Gauranga-sundara, my very life, on our own road,
soothed the ache of my heart gazing at that moon-face.
After a while my mother-in-law came and praises me at length;
and my sister-in-law's shame — what can I say? She is in the house as if she were not there.
Narahari says: when one is a fool, one can see nothing at all:
tying one's own fault in the end of one's sari, one wants to blame another.[^15]

*And a certain [other] — yatharaga*

**7.15.** What can I say, friend? Never has the wish of my heart been fulfilled:
to keep me from tasting happiness, the merciless Creator has raised many obstacles.
Yesterday the Beloved of my heart passed along our road.
Hearing of it from people, I took courage, went upstairs, and stood on the gallery.
Seeing the sweetness of that form, both my eyes were drowned [in it];
I longed to become a bird and fly close to Him.
The fragrance of His graceful body came and reached my senses;
I lost all composure — what can I say? — my happiness knew no bounds.
Gora turned and looked my way — and at that very moment the old woman
began calling [me] again and again; my heart trembled, and I came [down], leaving that happiness.
She gave me leave to go for water — and I floated on waters of bliss.
Narahari says: such a mother-in-law is found only by great good fortune.[^16]

*And a certain [other] — yatharaga*

**7.16.** Friend! However much I tell the tale of my sorrows, I cannot tell it all.
But yesterday the kind Creator eased my pain a little.
Leaving me at the cooking, my mother-in-law was out of the house;
hearing that Gaura was coming, she hurried back to me.
Looking at me again, she bolted the doors of the house
and, sitting in the middle of the courtyard, kept watching warily in every direction.
And here my heart was in turmoil — I could see no way out.
There was a small window — I pressed both my eyes to it.
Among His associates — the crown of rasikas: who can understand His ways?
Quite unnoticed, glancing in all four directions, He walks with an elephant's gait.
Once I beheld the sweetness of that form — eyes met eyes;
what state I was in — I made it known to Him, as it were, catching a sign.
Coming my way, with a slight smile, the Jewel of the clever spoke [a word].
And I sat down again to the cooking; my one-eyed mother-in-law unbolted the doors;
looking at me askance with her left eye, she was reassured.
Narahari says: had that eye gone too, it would have been just as well![^17]

*And a certain [other] — yatharaga*

**7.29.** My husband is a very good man, friend; listen to what his ways are like.
Yesterday, sitting alone [with his father], he said to his father:
"In the town of Nadia [lives] Nimai Pandita — in Him is the power of Ishvara.
Who created [Him] I do not know; there is no end to such virtues.
Such a master of the senses, so righteous a [man], I have never seen with my own eyes.
He is very merciful to people of evil mind; He bathes in the joy of kirtana.
Therefore I say: never, not even by oversight, forbid your daughters-in-law [to see Him];
I know for certain: by His darshana all that is inauspicious is destroyed.
Fortunate are all [our] daughters-in-law — what more can I say? I cannot even express it.
Therefore blessed is this woman — I would take upon myself the misfortunes of her whole life!
Drunk with false pride, day and night we live like the blind:
such a wonder in the midst of Nadia — and we cannot recognize it.
In Vraja the brahmanas did not know the Lord of Vraja — the brahmanas' wives attained [Him].
So it is here too, there is no doubt of it — I have understood this on reflection."
Thus father and son both spoke in many ways;
standing in hiding, I listened and listened — and my heart rejoiced.
I thought: if at this moment I could see Gorachanda,
with the corner of my eyes I would tell Him this whole story.
At that very moment joyful cries rose again and again in our quarter;
I hurried out and stood by the road, knowing that Gaura was coming.
Standing at a distance, I gazed, filling my eyes: what wondrous beauty!
The radiance of His body, surpassing gold, lights up everything around.
On His left — Gadadhara, on His right — Nityananda, the abode of bliss;
all around — other associates too: they gaze at that moon of a face.
With His own [companions] comes the Crown of rasikas, full of rasa.
Is there anyone who, seeing that lovely glance, could stand firm?
Smiling, smiling, under the guise of conversation the moon of Nadia showers streams of nectar;
the grace of His movements has utterly enchanted the world — as if it were the snare of Madana.
Knowing that the Lord of life was coming, the young women of the quarter came running;
their mothers-in-law and sisters-in-law sternly held them back, saying many [things].
But no one held me back — I fulfilled my heart's wish.
Narahari says: for one whose husband is so gracious, what obstacles can there be?[^18]

*And a certain [other] — yatharaga*

**7.30.** Listen, listen, friend: the Creator has no taste for rasa — I have understood how things stand.
Otherwise why would He give [me] such sorrow day and night?
If He had settled one of Gaura's associates here,
then the moon of Nadia would come to this quarter and dispel [my] pain.
So let me tell you, oh, about yesterday: leaving all [the work] in the house,
I went with my aunt to that quarter, to the house of Murari Gupta.
There the women of the house were overjoyed, their happiness had no bounds:
for the Beloved of their life they were grinding sandal paste and stringing garlands of flowers.
From their lips I heard that Gauranga would come there.
Having finished her business, my aunt came [for me] — I could not stay there.
Thinking and thinking [of Him], having gone a little way, I looked at the road:
the young crown prince of Nadia, adorned, was coming with His own [companions].
What wondrous beauty of the lips! The teeth — a radiance of stars;
the smile showers streams of nectar; the face — the splendour of the autumn moon.
What a curve of the brows! Long curving eyes, glances in so many ways;
on the forehead lovely sandal paste — at the sight of it all young women are immersed [in that beauty].
At His neck sways a necklace of gold and jewels — it lights up the world;
His gait is of a captivating cast: who in the world, seeing it, would not be enchanted?
In that ocean of beauty I bathed happily, staying beside my aunt.
Looking back, I saw: my husband's elder brother was coming behind with some students.
By good fortune he did not see me — he was looking at Gora.
Covering my face with my veil, lowering my eyes, I carefully hurried away.
Narahari says: what shame there is before a husband's elder brother — do I not know it?
If all this were disclosed, you could not live in this land![^19]

Murari Gupta is a physician from Shrihatta, a close associate of the Lord in Navadvipa (*Chaitanya-bhagavata*, *Chaitanya-charitamrita*). "Aunt" — *māsaisa* (cf. note 8). "That quarter" — *sepāṛā*. "The women of the house" — *badhūgaṇa*, lit. "daughters-in-law" (in Murari's house). "For the Beloved of their life" — the print seems to read *prāṇapriyā lāgi*; understood as *prāṇa-priya* (Gaura); see note 3. "The young crown prince of Nadia" — *nadīyāra naba-yubarāja*. "A radiance of stars" — *tāraka-chaṭā*. "The splendour of the autumn moon" — *śarada śaśīra ghaṭā*. "All young women are immersed [in that beauty]" — *majaye yubatī-jāti*; on the mood see note 3. "Of a captivating cast" — *manohara chānde*. "I bathed" — *sināinu* (*snāna*). "Husband's elder brother" — *bhāsura*: by Bengali custom a young woman does not appear before him with her face uncovered. "With some students" — *paṛuyāra saha*. "Veil" — *ghũghaṭa*. "Lowering my eyes" — *ā̃khi sambari*. The last lines are Narahari's jest: the shame before the brother-in-law is great, and disclosure of the secret would bring disgrace on her throughout the land. Signature: Narahari.
[^20]: The verse number in the edition is "॥৩৯॥", printed directly after "॥৩০॥"; probably a misprint for "৩১"; the edition's numbering is kept (there are no 7.31–7.38). The metre is "সুরত সুরতী ছন্দঃ"; the reading of the second word is uncertain: *suratī* (or *sumatī*). The language of the verse is Brajabuli (*bhaṇata*, *samujhata*, *tahi*). The verse sums up the nagaris' stories of this kirana. "As the heart prompts" — *mana māne*. "Charming" — *rucikārī*. Line 3 — *ati madhuratara tarala hima pulaka bharu aṅga*: understood as a thrill of the utmost sweetness and a "chill" that made their bodies break into gooseflesh (*pulaka*, one of the sattvika-bhavas); the understanding is uncertain. "Supreme delight" — *parama raṅga*. Signature: Narahari.
