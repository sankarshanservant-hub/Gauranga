# Kirana 11. The Deeds of the Morning: The Description of the Quarrels Born of Prema and So On among the Heavenly Women[^1]

*Morning: the heavenly women, gazing from the sky at Gaura in Shachi's courtyard, dispute among themselves about the nagaris of Nadiya and about Gaura Himself; one heavenly woman angrily defends the women of Nadiya as faithful wives, others answer her mockingly and tell how the women of Nadiya seek Gaura's glance on the roads and by the Ganga, while He, abashed, lowers His head and goes away; at the end Narahari prays to the Lord to keep him in Nadiya*

*Again, in the morning period — the quarrels born of prema and so on among the heavenly women.*

*Raga lalita*

**11.1.** Night has turned to dawn — what joy today! In the courtyard the moon of Nadiya sports.
The host of heavenly women, staying in the sky, drink the abundant nectar of Gora's beauty.
One says: "O, look, behold the young nagaris of my Nadiya:
gone mad for the Beloved, dear as life, they wander about, with nothing else in their minds."
One says: "Nadiya is a perilous [place], I have understood: it seems disgrace awaits all [there]."
One says: "It is fitting to do good — why does Gora do such deeds?"
One says: "Why, do you not know Him? What did He not do with the women of Vraja!"
One says: "In this business alone He finds joy: never has He spared shame — neither His own nor another's."
One says: "Why do the women of Nadiya act so, not knowing the fear of disgrace?"
One says: "She whose heart is inclined to love — will she reckon with such trifling disgrace?"
One says: "She whose cunning is strong — will ill fame ever spread about her?"
One says: "Narahari knows well: if you love, disgrace cannot be escaped."[^2]

*Yatharaga*

**11.2.** Many such things the heavenly women say.
In what wondrous attire they sport in the midst of the sky!
Their bodies are filled with the thrill of rapture — they cannot hold their composure.
How much rises in their hearts — can others understand this?[^3]

*Yatharaga*

**11.3.** The heavenly women are most virtuous — who can find a comparison for them?
Seeing the perfection of the prema of the young women of Nadiya, they are enchanted at heart.
Telling one another of the deeds of the nagaris of Nadiya, they grew unsteady at heart;
friends and companions float in tears — who can console and restrain [them]?
All are as if gone mad — what can be said of it! Becoming calm again, the women,
exclaiming, "Blessed, blessed are the young nagaris!", keep gazing toward the city of Nadiya.
Narahari says: at that moment a heavenly woman came with an impetuous gait,
and, swinging her arms, with many gestures, she angrily says to all:[^4]

*Yatharaga*

**11.4.** "Living in the city of the gods, why have you grown so proud?
Have you no fear at heart of reviling the women of Nadiya?
Alas, alas! Hearing such perverse words, how can I bear it?
Not knowing [them] — what wrong have all these women of Nadiya done to you all?
You judge others by your own ways, and do not know what lies deep in others;
you understand nothing — what comes from what; that is why I say: in vain do you bear a body.
What you speak of is possible only among the young women in the city of Vraja;
all the young women of Nadiya are excellent faithful wives: the people of the whole world know it.
They do not see the face of another's husband even in a dream; they never walk the wrong path;
they know no trickery, deceit or guile — all of them always have simple hearts.
They are steadfast, judicious in their work, their lovely inclination is on the path of the highest dharma;
guarding beyond compare the modesty and honour of their families, never, even by oversight, do they sit with wicked people.
They all love their elders as their life; their auspicious virtues I cannot count.
In my heart there is one thing: always to keep them all with care within my eyes.
That is why I speak [to you], knowing you as friends — you will surely accept my words; to others I do not say [this].
Whoever sings of another's disgrace is herself disgraced — this Narahari knows."[^5]

*Yatharaga*

**11.5.** Hearing these words, a certain rasika
with her own [friends] laughs inwardly.
Who can fathom her cunning? She exchanges winks [with them]
and, looking at that one, speaks with a [sly] gesture:[^6]

*Yatharaga*

**11.6.** "Fine, fine! There is no need to teach us this — as if I did not know all this!
On earth the faithful wives are the women of Nadiya, and in the city of the gods — you alone!
Ceaselessly singing of others' disgrace, we shall all become disgraced;
do not worry about it: we shall never make you a sharer in it.
But you, so wise a woman — how will you live alone in the city of the gods?
If you live with the unchaste, you will easily become unchaste yourself.
So I say: go to this city of Nadiya, taking with you your dharma, modesty and the rest —
Narahari is glad at this — and live always with care, in the company of the chaste."[^7]

*And another — yatharaga*

**11.7.** "Look, come here, O: why are you saying all this to [that] faithful wife?
Between equals happiness arises; with one who is unequal, pain increases.
Never think this in your mind: 'Since they are women of the gods, are they all excellent?'
As the owl does not see the sun — know that many people are like this.
All the young women of Nadiya, youthful and skilful — who in the world is their equal?
Their very secret stories I cannot reveal for shame.
Here, look, look: the life of our life, the beautiful, noble Gora:
He will not raise His face and speak to anyone — a wondrous nature, wholly absorbed within;
always careful on the path of dharma — what shall I say! — He knows nothing of all this.
And such a Lord of Narahari they have captivated, making signs with the corners of their eyes!"[^8]

*And another — yatharaga*

**11.8.** "What shall I say, O, of the ways of the young women of Nadiya!
What is in their hearts they do not reveal; outwardly they are always most gracious.
Beside their mothers-in-law and sisters-in-law they always keep well-mannered;
whatever everyone praises, that they do, taking great pains.
In how many ways they accomplish their own aims — no one can ever detect it;
to bring the moon of Nadiya under their sway, they go about most secretly.
With my own eyes I saw that day with how many gestures they enchanted [Him].
Who would have restrained the women? Narahari was not with Gauranga."[^9]

*And another — yatharaga*

**11.9.** "How many, many such amusements there are in Nadiya — how much of it will you tell!
What all these young women are like — chaste, excellent faithful wives — that I know.
That day I saw with my own eyes, standing under a young kadamba tree:
Gora was walking alone toward Murari Gupta's quarter in the afternoon.
At that time the faithful wives come with their stern mothers-in-law and sisters-in-law.
And still she stops and, on some pretext, with a gesture, finding the moon Gora on the nearby road,
making signs again and again, she entices Him, not keeping even half the end of her garment over her breast.
Narahari's Lord, His face lowered in shame, stands far off, to one side."[^10]

*And another — yatharaga*

**11.10.** "What shall I say, O? I feel much hesitation to speak of all these things.
For fear of young women the noble, beautiful moon of Nadiya is always trembling.
I do not know on what errand that day Gora went alone with the gait of an elephant.
He does not look at anyone even once — exceedingly gentle, wholly absorbed within.
On that road the faithful women, stopping, look toward Gauranga;
unnoticed, again and again they shoot the sharpest arrows from the corners of their restless eyes.
[One], with a gesture, letting her own shadow touch Gora's body, smiles slightly.
Another, as if by chance, lets the sound of the bracelet on her hand be heard — and floats in an ocean of joy.
Another, taking a fine pomegranate in her hand, says: 'To whom shall I give this wonder?'
Another says: 'He whose body is new gold will take it Himself, unasked.'
Such words they speak to one another — who, hearing that, could remain steady?
The Lord of Narahari, collecting Himself, in embarrassment did His errand and quickly went home."[^11]

*And another — yatharaga*

**11.11.** "Of the chaste young women of Nadiya — to whom shall I tell the story of their conduct?
Ai, ai, ai! Who could keep composure before the signs of their eyes?
That day at the ghat of the Suradhuni, at bathing time, they found Gauranga, [dear to them] as life.
Unnoticed, laughing, holding on to one another, they splash water near Gora as if by chance.
One, rubbing turmeric on another's body, says: 'Golden-fair one! Listen to what I say:
to Him whose body is gold I will give you — then the pain of your heart will go.'
Washing their clothes, rubbing them with their hands, placing a lovely fine silk cloth over the breast,
some, turning half aside, wring out their hair, looking toward Gora.
At this Narahari, his heart full of shame, seeing it from afar
and knowing it was untoward, quickly finished bathing and, taking Gauranga, went home."[^12]

**11.12.** …Having bathed in the Suradhuni, [He] hurries along the road with bowed head.
\* \* \* \*
"There the most faithful wives, ever-new young women, intoxicated with the pride of youth,
gazing at Gora, smile softly, softly, [making] all kinds of gestures with the corners of their eyes.
One says to another: 'O, if you obtain this new golden form,
then day and night worship this God deep in your heart — and be content.'
'Look, see: the lotus of my breast, without the worship of the God, has closed in sorrow.'
Hearing these words, the moon Gaura covers His face with the end of His cloth for shame.
Seeing that, another says: 'Let this simple one hide like this — what of it?
I shall hide Him in the temple of my heart — no one will be able to see.'
Saying this, playfully, with a restless heart she devises many pretexts to touch His body.
The Lord of Narahari's life, realizing this, trembles; His cloth is soaked with heavy sweat."[^13]

*And another — yatharaga*

**11.13.** "Even to speak of the ways of all the faithful wives of Nadiya brings shame to the heart.
On various pretexts they go out on the road and, finding the moon Gora, make Him hear — in how many ways!
One says to another: 'This is Nanda's son, dark Kanu.
I have heard from people's lips: there — a dark form; here — a body that surpasses gold.'
Another says: 'I do not think so: if He were the dark nagara,
would the jewel of youth of the young maidens of the town of Nadiya have remained [intact]?'
Another says: 'I have considered it well: He is surely the son of Dasharatha;
otherwise why, at a tender youthful age, would He be so devout?'
Another says: 'The son of the king of brahmanas knows austerity and japa — where would He learn this?'
Another says: 'By staying long with Him I shall easily teach [Him] this here.'
Another says: 'Friend, [He] is entrusted to you — teach [Him] right now, and let the heart be soothed.'
Hearing this, the Lord of Narahari, in fear, goes home and performs atonement."[^14]

*And another — yatharaga*

**11.14.** "Listen, listen: I shall tell you yesterday's story, casting off shame.
Many young women, in great joy, take counsel with great care.
One says: 'O, do not delay — take your pitchers, let us go for water:
the moon of Nadiya will come to the ghat of the Suradhuni — the time for His coming has arrived.'
Another says: 'Why go like this? Dress yourselves in all sorts of ways,
so that by a mere trace of that splendour the young Kishora cannot keep His composure even for half a sesame seed.'
Another says: 'Braid your hair and adorn your heads with all kinds of flowers,
so that by their fragrance even the Conqueror of the senses cannot turn His nose away even once.'
Another says: 'Rub your faces with kunkuma, brighten your eyes with kajal,
so that Gauranga's eyes are charmed, seeing a graceful new charm.'
Another says: 'Put on your necks various jewelled necklaces, like a lovely noose,
so that by it the moon of Nadiya, beautiful Gora, is easily caught.'
Another says: 'Bring and put on jewelled nupuras and belts of little bells — choose ringing ones —
so that Gaura's two ears are charmed, hearing the sweet, sweet sound.'
Saying much like this to one another, they all adorned themselves in delight;
each one looked at her face in a mirror and quickly chewed tambula.
Wearing colourful garments, all very lively, with pitchers on their hips,
they came out from this house and that — with what joy in their hearts!
Laughing and laughing, all of the same age, full of rasa — that assembly of faithful wives —
they go along the bank of the Suradhuni, lighting it up — what shall I say of the wondrous splendour of their beauty!
In the rapture of rasa, holding hands, glancing with slight, slight gestures,
on many pretexts they tell stories of rasa, having found Gaura in the middle of the road.
Then the excellent Gaura, the supreme pandita, stands with bowed head, keeping His composure.
Suspecting something most untoward, He turned once and looked toward them.
At that moment they all rain thick arrows of sidelong glances from the corners of their eyes.
At once abashed, the Jewel of virtues again ponders in fear of disgrace.
What happened — what shall I say! Only because Narahari was with Gauranga
did His modesty and dignity remain; otherwise there would have been trouble on the road."[^15]

*And another — yatharaga*

**11.15.** "What shall I say? [She] spoke rejecting all this — how much can one bear?
What the ways of the women of Nadiya are — with that the whole world is filled.
Their mothers-in-law, sisters-in-law, husbands and the rest all scold them constantly;
every day [they] worship Old Shiva with such fervour, [fearing] that there will be disgrace;
all the time they carefully keep them at home and do not let them go out on the road;
if [one of them] wishes to bathe in the Suradhuni, her sister-in-law goes with her.
The neighbour women ceaselessly hold [them] back; no one trusts [them] in what they do.
What more shall I say? Hearing those reproaches, Narahari is consumed with shame every day."[^16]

*Yatharaga*

**11.16.** All the playful women
spoke much in this way.
Hearing it, that young woman was abashed —
no word comes from her lips.
Seeing this, a certain playful one
cannot contain herself for joy
and, looking toward her own [companions],
speaks with cunning:[^17]

*Yatharaga*

**11.17.** "In the city of the gods, who does not know what the ways of the nagaris of Nadiya are?
Why then are you so needlessly angry with her?
What can I say? All that she said is most secret — none of you knows it.
Have patience, all of you, and listen carefully to what I say."
Having thus stopped her own [friends], she quickly took the other by the hand
and, praising [her] in many ways, speaks very softly, brimming with rasa:
"What the ways of all the young women of Nadiya are — who can tell?
On how many days, how many amusing things I have seen with my own eyes — and I tell no one of them.
But let that be: no one looks at the bracelet on her own hand in a mirror.
Just look: why does a woman come running in the morning to Ai's house?
If you say: at the auspicious word of an astrologer she came to Ai on her own business —
then why does she, unblinking, keep gazing at Gora, making her brows dance?
And her body trembles again and again, her garment and ornaments slip, the knot of her hair comes loose;
a wave of the thrill of rapture, sweat trickles, the champaka at her ear sways without pause.
Who acts like this — tell me, tell me? Why should anyone not believe it?
Narahari's Lord is the Master (*pati*) of all: have no doubt of this in your heart."[^18]

*Yatharaga*

**11.18.** The beauty, with words full of rasa dispelling all the doubts of [her] heart —
the supremely skilful rasika — made her her own once more.
All, becoming one, staying in the sky, were deeply immersed in the rasa of Gauranga;
holding on to one another, jesting, they float in an ocean of happiness.
In Shachi's courtyard the moon Gaurahari sports amid [His] dear devotees.
Gazing at Him, their hearts thrilled with rapture, distraught, they bade farewell to shame;
intoxicated, as if utterly mad — no one listens to anyone;
ceaselessly they sing songs of the lovely deeds of the Lord of Narahari's life.[^19]

*Modaka-chhandah*

**11.19.** The host of heavenly women is immersed in the greatest delight and rasa.
The incomparable course of the new prema — how can it be described? There is no end to it.
Joining both palms, Narahari entreats Him:
"O Lord full of rasa, keep [me] in Nadiya together with Your associates!"[^20]

*Thus [ends] the eleventh kirana of the Shrimad Gaura-charita-chintamani, called "The Description of the Quarrels Born of Prema and So On among the Heavenly Women", in the description of the deeds of the morning period.*[^21]

Here a new kind of the heavenly women's mood begins — a dispute (*prema-kalaha*) about the nagaris of Nadiya and about Gaura Himself: some speak of Him as a nagara who "did everything" with the women of Vraja (lines 7–8). Gaudiya Vaishnavas, following Shrila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati and the Chaitanya-bhagavata (Adi 15: in His lila the Lord did not jest with women and did not even look at them), reject gaura-nagari-vada — the worship of Gaura as an enjoying lover (*nāgara*) and of oneself as His nagari: Gaura is Krishna in the mood of Radha, tasting Her love, not an enjoyer. The heavenly women's words are their own conjectures: in 11.4 one of them angrily rejects them (the women of Nadiya are faithful wives who do not see the face of another's husband even in a dream; what her friends speak of "is possible only in the city of Vraja"), and further on in the kirana, on meeting the women of Nadiya, Gaura lowers His head in shame and stands aside (11.9, pdf 140) or hurries home (11.10–11.11).
