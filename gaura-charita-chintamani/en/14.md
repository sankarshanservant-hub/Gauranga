# Kirana 14. The Deeds of the Morning: The Description of the Gandharvis and Kinnaris Revealing Their Heart's Wishes and So On[^1]

*Morning: the gandharvis and kinnaris, unseen, keep their eyes on Gaura's face and are filled with prema; one after another they confide their heart's wishes to their friends — to watch from afar as Gora walks along the road of Nadiya, to be born a young wife in Nadiya and go with the nagaris of Nadiya to Vishnupriya, to become Vishnupriya's maidservant and serve Gaura and Her*

*Again, in the morning period — the gandharvis and kinnaris revealing their heart's wishes, and so on.*

*Raga bhairava*

**14.1.** What can I say! Today the blessed night has turned to dawn.
The hosts of gandharvis are absorbed [in bliss], full of joy; the throng of kinnaris shines together [with them]. *(Refrain)*
Unseen, they gaze at Gaurachandra's face without blinking — endlessly thirsting.
Their garments slip, they are overpowered by rasa; their loosened braids gleam; not a trace of composure remains.
Glittering, covered with abundant thrills of rapture, their bodies, utterly unstrung, tremble ceaselessly.
However hard they try, the secret is not kept: the feeling of the heart becomes manifest.
Deeming their lot wholly fulfilled, they give up the jewel of their fresh youth at the Lord's feet.
A pure, mighty, incomparable course of prema! Seeing [it], Narahari, overcome with joy, sings.[^2]

*Rasakala-chhandah (matra-vritta)*

**14.2.** The gandharvis are wholly absorbed in joy,
they shine together with the throng of young kinnaris.
Who can describe their incomparable attachment to Gaura?
Absorbed in prema, they say something to one another.[^3]

*One [of them] says — raga lalita*

**14.3.** "Listen, listen, O playful friend!
What arises in my heart I do not tell out of shame — [but,] becoming shameless, I will tell it.
This Gora, the delight of Nadiya,
a mine of the jewel of love, a jewel of virtues, the thief of women's lives —
throwing His curly hair back over His shoulders,
with how many graceful movements He will walk along the road, captivating the young women!
Seeing such a radiance of beauty,
throngs of young women, losing their composure, will run like madwomen.
Gora, looking at them all,
smiling ever so gently, raining nectar, will strike with the arrows of His eyes.
And I, overwhelmed by Madana,
will watch this play of the Lord of Narahari's life, staying apart."[^4]

*And another — raga todi*

**14.4.** "Friend, what arises in my heart,
finding [us] alone, casting off shame, I will tell you.
This Gora is the moon of Nadiya;
with what yearning in their hearts the wives of [good] families will run to see Him!
I, staying with them all,
laughing and laughing, with so much fun, will tell amusing stories full of rasa.
Hearing that, the Lord of Narahari,
His head bowed, will say so much to me with the corner of His eyes!"[^5]

*And another — raga vibhasha*

**14.5.** "Friend! Hear what is in my heart.
I will become a young wife in the town of Nadiya and live with those playful [women].
The young nagaris of Nadiya, joyfully,
will go to Vishnupriya, delighted, taking me with them.
Together with them I will go to Shachi's house;
seeing Gora with Vishnupriya, I will be lost in delight full of rasa.
The Lord of Narahari's life,
looking at me, smiling ever so gently, will shatter [my] dharma and composure."[^6]

*And another — raga ramakeli*

**14.6.** "Listen, I will tell you what is in my heart.
Devi Vishnupriya, making me her maidservant, will keep me near.
She, taking the hand of the Lord of [her] life,
will sit with such graceful movements — I will watch it, filling my eyes.
Receiving Vishnupriya's permission,
I will joyfully place a garland of malati around Gorachanda's neck.
Gorachanda, placing that malati garland
around Vishnupriya's neck, will say so much to me on the pretext of a jest!
I, smiling the sweetest smile,
having prepared tambula, will lift [it] to the full-moon faces of the Two.
I will fan [Them] with a lovely chamara,
[and] the Beloved — the life of Narahari — will recline on the couch with His beloved."[^7]

*And another — raga asavari*

**14.7.** "Friend! Listen to what is in my heart.
I will seat Gauranga with Lakshmi — Vishnupriya — where it is secluded.
They, sitting on a throne of jewels,
will sprinkle each other with heaps of nectar in all kinds of playful words.
Seeing that beauty, filling my eyes,
I will stand before [Them] with my own [friends], striking so many graceful poses!
Dense thrills of ecstasy will pervade [my] body;
joyfully I will play the mridanga, mandira, vina, pinaka and other instruments.
I will dance in many ways,
in many ragas I will sing of His qualities and deeds — [They] will listen, intoxicated with bliss.
The beloved of Narahari's Lord, [dear to Him as] life,
keeping me always near herself, will soothe my heart."[^8]

*Raga sindhura*

**14.8.** The joyful gandharvis and the hosts of kinnaris, gathered together,
playfully tell one another their wishes, full of rasa.
In the midst of the vault of the sky they ceaselessly sing of Gaura's qualities;
[the song] flows as if with fresh nectar, enters the cups of the ears and steals away the mind.
Softly, softly dancing, sounding "dhidhikat", they strike countless poses;
descending to the earth, they regain their composure and speak special words.
One says: "This Gaura, the Lord of Narahari, is the Lord of Gokula.
He will not be apart [from us]: day and night He will sport in the temple of [our] heart!"[^9]

*Thus [ends] the fourteenth kirana of the Shrimad Gaura-charita-chintamani, called "The Description of the Gandharvis and Kinnaris Revealing Their Heart's Wishes and So On", in the description of the deeds of the morning period.*[^10]

The gandharvis and kinnaris are the women of the heavenly singers and musicians (cf. kirana 13). [^2]: Raga bhairava is a morning raga (cf. 12.1). The stanza begins on pdf 151 (printed p. 146) and ends on pdf 152 (printed p. 147) "॥১॥". Lines 1–2 are the refrain (*dhru*; the mark "॥ধ্রু॥" stands after line 2). The author's words. "Blessed" — *subhaga*. "Shines together [with them]" — *birājata jora* (the first letter of the verb in print resembles *n*: *nirājata*). "Unseen" — *alakhita*. "Endlessly thirsting" — *tṛṣita aśeṣa*. "Their garments slip" — *khasata basana*. "Overpowered by rasa" — *rasa-bibaśa*. "Loosened braids" — *ati śithila kabarī*. "Utterly unstrung" — *sugalita* (OCR: "গুগলিত"). "The secret is not kept" — *na rahata gupata rīti*. "Give up the jewel of their fresh youth" — *naba yaubana-ratana nichai pahũ pāya*: *nichai* — "offer up, sacrifice". The frank passages (lines 4, 7) are translated exactly, without softening. Here a new kind of mood begins: heavenly women — the gandharvis and kinnaris — are filled with prema for Gaura, and further on (14.3–14.6) they dream of seeing Him as the nagara before whom the women of Nadiya lose their composure, and of serving Him and Vishnupriya. The Gaudiya Vaishnavas, following Shrila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati and the Chaitanya-bhagavata (Adi 15: in His lila the Lord did not jest with women and did not even look at them), reject gaura-nagari-vada — the worship of Gaura as the enjoying lover (*nāgara*) and of oneself as His nagari: Gaura is Krishna in the mood of Radha, tasting Her love, not an enjoyer. Even in these dreams Gaura Himself, on meeting women, bows His head (14.4; cf. 11.9). Cf. the main notes of kirana 11 (note 2) and kirana 12 (note 10). Further in this kirana there are references to this note. Signature: "Narahari".
