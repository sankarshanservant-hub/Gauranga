# Kirana 13. The Deeds of the Morning: The Description of the Longings and So On of the Gandharvas and Kinnaras[^1]

*Morning: the gandharvas and kinnaras, rejoicing, behold Gaura in Shachi's house and confide their longings to one another — to fall at His feet on the bank of the Ganga, to hear His kirtana, to live in Nadiya, to drink His beauty and to serve His feet; then, standing in circles in the sky, they sing of His plays in Nadiya, and their tears humble the pride of the clouds*

*Again, in the morning period — the longings and so on of the gandharvas and kinnaras.*

*Raga lalita*

**13.1.** The house of Shri Shachi-devi, rich in adornments — there is great merriment there: night has turned to dawn.
Amid the host of skilled, excellent associates the Lord shines, delighting the young women of the world.
His tender, spotless body, adorned with beautiful curves — charming, full of rasa, lovely, filled with radiance;
[surpassing] garlands of lightning, heaps of gold and champakas, He crushes the pride of Kandarpa.
His eyes are broad lotuses; His forehead a net of beauty; His lips beautifully red, [like] the rising sun;
His teeth [like] kunda flowers; from the fair moon of His face nectar-rasa gently flows — His lovely smile.
His hair, [darker] than water-laden clouds, puts crushed kajal to shame; glossy fresh curls are [His] ornament.
His glittering attire — not a trace of an equal to it; the world is enchanted, says Ghanashyama.[^2]

*Chanchala-chhandah (matra-vritta)*

**13.2.** Today the hosts of gandharvas and kinnaras are in great joy;
their bodies are filled with the thrill of rapture, their wondrous course of prema cannot be hidden.
Beholding their own Lord Gauranga, the best of Shachi's sons,
again and again they speak nectarean words to one another.[^3]

*Someone says — raga vibhasha*

**13.3.** "Hear my longing: when that Gaura, the Moon of Gokula,
goes to the bank of the Suradhuni, surrounded by His companions, with a very slow gait,
then, beholding from afar the sweet charm of the Lord's face,
I will come nearer, not blinking, holding on to what is left of my composure.
I will fall, rolling on the ground, at [His] feet, and bathe [His] feet with the tears of my eyes.
Saying, 'Rise!' and taking [me] by both arms, that Lord will comfort me.
Hearing, 'Worship Nanda-kishora', I will speak again, clinging to [His] feet:
'You are He — the life of Narahari, manifest in this [age of] Kali by [our] good fortune.'"[^4]

*And another — raga lalita*

**13.4.** "Gaura is the Lord of Gokula, the king of nagaras, the king of brahmanas;
He puts to shame the lovely gait of an elephant; His attire enchants [even] Manoja.
His face gladdens the minds of the people of the world and robs the autumn moon of its pride;
His teeth [like] kunda flowers, a beautiful smile, speech full of rasa, overflowing with nectar.
On the bank of Jahnu's daughter He will shine amid His associates,
He will perform the lovely play of kirtana — hearing [it], I will not keep my composure.
He will seek out the fallen; then, as soon as the Lord of Ghanashyama looks at me,
I will run, clasp [His feet] and sprinkle [them] with the tears of my eyes. — I have told you my longing."[^5]

*And another — raga bhairava*

**13.5.** "You ask again and again — so I will tell [you] my longing:
may my dwelling be in this Nadiya, revered by Shesha, Shiva and Vidhi.
The incomparable beauty of Gaurasundara is a stream of nectarean rasa:
I will drink it, filling the cups of my eyes again and again, carefully plunging [into it].
I will sing His most charming deeds — my chest will swell with joy;
I will forget eating, sleeping — everything; there will be no awareness of day or night.
'Keep [me] near, take away my sorrow,' I will say again and again in words of entreaty;
then the servant Narahari will serve the Lord's two feet."[^6]

*Raga todi*

**13.6.** Thus the gandharvas and kinnaras together voice many longings;
singing Gaura's qualities, they were carried away in supreme rapture.
In the sphere of the sky, standing in circles, they sing the alap of sweet ragas
and play many kinds of instruments — the sound, like nectar, pervades the world.
When in sweet voices they proclaim [Gaura's] wondrous plays in Nadiya,
all the wise were enchanted: then no one kept even a trace of composure.
Tears flow ceaselessly from [their] eyes, humbling the pride of the host of clouds.
The servant Narahari gives up his body — he cannot describe [their] anuraga.[^7]

*Thus [ends] the thirteenth kirana of the Shrimad Gaura-charita-chintamani, called "The Description of the Longings and So On of the Gandharvas and Kinnaras", in the description of the deeds of the morning period.*[^8]

The subheading "Again, in the morning period — the longings and so on of the gandharvas and kinnaras" (*punaḥ prātaḥkāle gandharva-kinnara-abhilāṣādikaṁ*) stands on pdf 149 (printed p. 144) immediately after the colophon of kirana 12. Gandharvas are heavenly singers and musicians, kinnaras heavenly musicians; here the speakers are men (the headings "কোঽপি আহ", "কোপি চ" are masculine; kirana 14 is devoted to the women of these races, the gandharvis and kinnaris). Kirana 13 comprises stanzas 13.1–13.6 (pdf 149–151).
[^2]: Raga lalita is a morning raga (cf. 11.1). pdf 149 (printed p. 144). The author's words: a description of Gaura's form. "Rich in adornments" — *bhuri-bhūṣaṇa*. "Great merriment" — *bhuri kutuka*. "Delighting the young women of the world" — *jagata yubati hulasāta*. Here and in 13.4 ("the king of nagaras" — *nāgara-bhūpa*) Gaura is described in words used of Krishna of Vraja. For the Gaudiya Vaishnavas Gaura is Krishna Himself; but Shrila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati, following the Chaitanya-bhagavata (Adi 15: in His lila the Lord did not jest with women and did not even look at them), rejected gaura-nagari-vada — the worship of Gaura as the enjoying lover (*nāgara*) of women: Gaura is Krishna in the mood of Radha, tasting Her love, not an enjoyer (cf. the main notes of kirana 11 — note 2, and of kirana 12 — note 10). The gandharvas and kinnaras themselves in this kirana long only to fall at Gaura's feet, to hear His kirtana and to serve Him. "Adorned with beautiful curves" — *su-balani-balita*: the meaning is unclear; possibly "endowed with beautiful folds" (*bali*) or "with a beautiful gait"; the understanding is uncertain. "Filled with radiance" — *rucipūra*. "Garlands of lightning, heaps of gold and champakas" — *dāminī-dāma-bharma-bhara-campaka*; the next word is *-kara* (the print is heavy; possibly "making", i.e. "[eclipsing]") — the reading is unclear. "Crushes the pride of Kandarpa" — *kandarpa-darpaka karu cūra*: literally "crushes Kandarpa [and his] pride"; Kandarpa is Kama. "His forehead a net of beauty" — *bhāla chabi-jāla*. "The rising sun" — *bālarabi*. "Kunda flowers" — white jasmine. "Puts crushed kajal to shame" — *dalitāñjana-gañji*. "Ornament" — *lalāma*. "Not a trace of an equal" — *leśa naha samatula*. "The world is enchanted" — *bhulala bhubana*: the word *bhulala* is smudged (OCR: "রতন ভুর্ন") — the reading is unclear. Signature: "Ghanashyama" — Narahari's other name.
