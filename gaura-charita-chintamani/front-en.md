# Gaura-charita-chintamani

*"The Wish-fulfilling Gem of Gaura's Life"*

*A poem by Narahari Chakravarti (Ghanashyama Dasa), Bengali, 18th century. Kiranas 1–17 (up to where the manuscript breaks off)*

*Translated from the Bengali into English*

---

## Translator's Note

Narahari Chakravarti, also known as Ghanashyama Dasa, is the author of the *Bhakti-ratnakara* and the *Narottama-vilasa*, a master of music and prosody (he wrote a treatise on metre, the *Chhandah-samudra*). The *Gaura-charita-chintamani* is not a biography but a lila-smarana: remembrance of Sri Gaura's pastimes in Navadvipa through the hours of the day (ashtakaliya-lila), in "kiranas" ("rays"), each stanza in its own metre or raga.

**Source.** The translation follows the edition of Haridasa Dasa (Navadvipa, 1947), who printed the first part from a single manuscript that breaks off; one leaf is missing in the scan (stanzas 7.17–7.28). Stanza numbers are those of the edition ("kirana.stanza"); where the edition restarts its numbering, a letter is added. The editor's preface and notes were not used.

**The author and Gaudiya Vaishnava teaching.** In several kiranas Narahari describes the women of Navadvipa drawn by Gaura's beauty, in the manner of later "nagari" poetry. Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati rejected gaura-nagari-vada — the idea of the Lord as a "Nagara" enjoying the maidens of Navadvipa: in Mahaprabhu's lila He is a devotee tasting Radha's mood, not an enjoyer. The translation renders the text exactly, and in such places the notes state the Gaudiya Vaishnava position.
