# Pata-nirnaya · Pata-paryatana · Shakha-nirnaya of Abhiramachandra

*Ramagopala Dasa; Abhirama Dasa. Bengali, 17th century*

*Translated from the Bengali into English*

---

## Translator's Note

Three short Bengali lists of the holy places ("patas") and branches of Sri Chaitanya's associates. The **Pata-nirnaya** of Ramagopala Dasa (chronogram 1597 Shaka, c. 1675) names the dhamas of the Lord and the shripatas of His associates — the places of their birth and pastimes in Bengal and Orissa. The **Pata-paryatana** of Abhirama Dasa is an extract from it: five dhamas, the twelve shripatas of the gopalas with their names in Vraja, the villages of other associates. The **Shakha-nirnaya of Abhiramachandra** by the same author lists the disciples of Abhirama Thakura. The translation follows the edition of Kishori Dasa Babaji and the 1911 publication in the *Sahitya-parishat-patrika*; the editors' notes are not translated.

## The *Sri Pata-nirnaya* of Ramagopala Dasa

*A list of the Lord's dhamas, of the shripats and great shripats of His associates, and of places of birth and pastimes in Bengal and Orissa; the fruit of pilgrimage; chronogram and the author's signature (couplets and verses 1–79)*

*Obeisance to Sri Krishna Chaitanya-chandra.*

## Introduction

**1.** Sri Krishna Chaitanya is a descent for pastimes;
with His limbs, sub-limbs and associates He spread throughout the world.[^PN-1]

**2.** The places of perfection, the eternal places, cannot be counted;
I write only a little — merely pointing out the direction.

**3.** [I write of] His own eight dhamas[^PN-2] and the shripats[^PN-3] of the mahantas,
and also [the places of] the offshoots and all the establishments of service.

**4.** Throughout the whole world there are dwellings of Vaishnavas;
in my own land I write of two or four [places] that are renowned.

**5.** Where a Vaishnava stays even half a moment, half the blink of an eye,
that the Puranas call a tirtha and a forest of austerity.

Thus [it is said]:

**6.** *"Where sadhakas stay even half a moment, half the blink of an eye,*
*that place should be known as a place of perfection: it is a tirtha, it is a forest of austerity."*[^PN-4]

**7.** Where Vaishnavas live, drinking in talks about Krishna,
there the Ganga and the other tirthas take up their abode.

Thus [it is said]:

**8.** *"There is the Ganga and the Yamuna, there the Godavari, there the Sarasvati too;*
*all the tirthas dwell there, where talk of the magnanimous Achyuta goes on."*[^PN-5]

**9.** Vaishnava gosanis make a tirtha of what is no tirtha;
therefore there is no fault in writing of those places.

Thus [it is said]:

**10.** *"[Such devotees] make the holy places themselves holy, by the Bearer of the club who dwells within their hearts."*[^PN-6]

**11.** First I shall write of the dhamas of Sri Krishna Chaitanya,
then I shall write of the villages of the gopala mahantas;[^PN-7]

**12.** where the birth and other pastimes of the Vaishnavas [took place] —
I shall tell briefly how it stands with those villages.

**13.** Vrindavana, Mathura, Dvaraka, Nilachala,
Navadvipa, Khardaha, the place Shantipura,

**14.** together with Kantakanagara[^PN-8] — [these are] the dhamas of Krishna Chaitanya:
here He always abides with His devotees.

**15.** First I shall write of twenty-four shripats,
and also twelve great shripats (mahapats) — these I shall compose.[^PN-9]

**16.** Where one or two mahantas [live], that is called a shripat;
where many Vaishnavas [live], that I record as a great shripat.

**17.** There is no consideration of what comes before and what after:
I write in the order of writing, as it appears [to me].

## The twelve shripats

**18.** In Radha-desha is the village of Sri Vaidyakhanda:[^PN-10]
the dhama of Mukunda, Narahari and Sri Raghunandana.

**19.** Chiranjiva, Sulochana, Kaviraja, Mahananda;[^PN-11]
the service of Sri Krishna [and] the Vaishnavas is supreme bliss.

**20.** Across the Suradhuni is the village named Agradvipa,[^PN-12]
where Gopinatha is manifest — Bhagavan Himself.

**21.** Govinda Ghosha, Vasu Ghosha and Madhava Ghosha —
to see that place is the highest delight.

**22.** Across from Navadvipa is Kulia-Paharpur,
where Vamshivadana Dasa is — the city of the flute's rasa.[^PN-13]

**23.** Kavidatta Mahashaya, Thakura Saranga;[^PN-14]
the place of Mahaprabhu — waves of pastimes and play.

**24.** To its south is the village of Ambuya-muluk:[^PN-15]
to see the service of Chaitanya and Nityananda is great happiness.

**25.** Gauridasa Pandita, his younger brother Krishnadasa,[^PN-16]
Hridaya Chaitanya Dasa — many manifestations [are there].

**26.** To its west is the village named Kulinagrama,
where the incomparable Vasu family is — Ramananda and the others.

**27.** Many dear to Mahaprabhu lived there;
much service of Krishna, and Haridasa stayed there.

**28.** Across from Triveni is the village of Kanchrapara,[^PN-17]
where Krishna-raya Thakura is, incomparable, as one hears of Him.[^PN-18]

**29.** Shivananda Sena and Sena Shrikanta,
Kavi Karnapura and others — wholehearted devotees.

**30.** To its south is the village of Kumarahatta:
Srivasa Pandita Thakura, [the Deity] named "Gauranga-raya".

**31.** The dwelling of many — Shivananda Pandita[^PN-19] and others;
Mahaprabhu's beloved place — the Deity "Gopala-raya".

**32.** To the south of Khardaha is the village of Ariadaha,
the very own dhama of Gadadhara Dasa Thakura.

**33.** To the north is Purandara Pandita, to the south Raghava;
many Vaishnavas gather — the greatest festival.

**34.** Near it is the village named Panihati,
the dhama of Raghava Dasa Thakura[^PN-20] and Damayanti.

**35.** There Sri Ramadasa Thakura is manifest,[^PN-21]
who without effort lifted the log of sixteen [bearers].

**36.** [It is] the abode of Mahaprabhu's love alone;
to see Raghava's bags (jhali) is the highest joy.[^PN-22]

**37.** Halda-Maheshpur and Bodhkhana[^PN-23] —
two villages in one land, counted as one.

**38.** There the service of Thakura Sundara is performed;[^PN-24]
[the place of] Sadashiva Kaviraja is determined to be Bodhkhana.

**39.** His son is Thakura Purushottama:[^PN-25]
a great tree, a great fruit, the best of the best.

**40.** In Khanakul-Krishnanagar is Thakura Abhirama;[^PN-26]
his wife is she whose name is Malini.

**41.** In that very place is the Gaurangapur of Vasu Ghosha;[^PN-27]
the navaratna of Yadava Simha — to see it is a wonder.

**42.** Chatra-Vallabhapur is across from Khardaha:[^PN-28]
Kashishvara, Shankararanya and Shrinatha Pandita.

**43.** The service of Rudra Pandita is named Radhavallabha:
a form that enchants the worlds — a new Kamadeva.

**44.** Thus I have written of the twelve great shripats;
now I tell the names of thirteen more shripats.[^PN-29]

## The thirteen shripats

**45.** In Akai Hat lived Thakura Krishnadasa,
whose joy was the nupura of Raghunandana that he obtained.[^PN-30]

**46.** In the village of Anadiha lived Thakura Gangadasa;[^PN-31]
in Badagachhi [and] Shaligrama is the dwelling of Krishnadasa.[^PN-32]

**47.** In Belun is Ananta Puri, great is [his] glory;[^PN-33]
in Baghnapara are Sri Vamshi [and] Ramai Thakura.[^PN-34]

**48.** In Bharatpur is Mahashaya Sri Mishra Thakura:[^PN-35]
his glory, full of the pastimes of Radha and Krishna, is great.

**49.** In Guptipara is Satyananda Sarasvati:[^PN-36]
the service of Vrindavanachandra with the highest love.

**50.** In Jirat are Madhavacharya and Ganga-devi;[^PN-37]
in Jashra[^PN-38] is Jagadisha, who bears the title "Dancer".

**51.** Halisahar [and] Dendudi are the two places
of Vrindavana Dasa, the son of Narayani.[^PN-39]

**52.** Varahanagara is [the place] of Bhagavata Acharya;[^PN-40]
in Saptagrama is the house of Uddharana Datta [and] Sugriva Mishra.[^PN-41]

**53.** Sanchra-Panchra, Karanda, the village of Shitala[^PN-42] —
the service of Dhananjaya Pandita is manifold.

**54.** Thus I have made known the twenty-five shripats;
now I shall write of the birthplaces, and also of [the places of] pastimes and play.

## Places of birth and pastimes

**55.** In the village of Benapole is the dwelling of Haridasa;
in Phulia the great soul stayed for some days.

**56.** The village of Raghunatha Dasa is Chandapur;[^PN-43]
[it is] a village near Hugli, so all people say.

**57.** The dwelling of Kalidasa Thakura is Saptagrama;
the birthplace of Sanatana [and] Rupa is Bakla.[^PN-44]

**58.** In Shrihatta, in Chattagrama, is the home of Vidyanidhi;[^PN-45]
in the village of Ekachakra[^PN-46] Nityananda was born.

**59.** Ramakeli and Kanai-Natashala[^PN-47] are [places of] the Lord's rest;
and in Radha-desha there are many more places.

**60.** Under a jivaputri tree[^PN-48] He rested for a moment:
that village is called Naopara-Atkuri.

**61.** Across the Damodar is the village of Barasat:[^PN-49]
one day the Lord accepted alms there.

**62.** Lokanatha Gosani was born in the land of Yashohar;
the village of Nagara Purushottama is called Nakhachhara.[^PN-50]

(The dwelling of Nagara Purushottama is in Vanakunda.[^PN-51])

**63.** In Sardanga-Sultanpur is the house of Mahesha Pandita;[^PN-52]
in the village of Dogachhia is Balarama, best of the twice-born.[^PN-53]

**64.** [The place] of Suryadasa Sarakhela is determined to be Khana;[^PN-54]
in Uttaranapur is the brother, Jagannatha Dasa Mahashaya.[^PN-55]

**65.** Within Gauda is a village named Pokhuria:
the service of Nrisimha Chaitanya Dasa, named Vrindavanachandra.[^PN-56]

**66.** In Tamoluk is the temple of Madhava Ghosha:
the shelter of Hari-Vishnu, Jagannatha and Gauranga.[^PN-57]

**67.** Pandita Gosvami [and] Vakreshvara lived in Nilachala;[^PN-58]
Gopinatha's Tota is the dwelling of Gopala Guru.

**68.** The land of Udra: Remuna, Alalanatha, Nilagiri,
Cuttack, Bhubaneshvara, Konark, Vidyanagari.[^PN-59]

**69.** To the west of Sonakanta, across the Suvarnarekha,[^PN-60]
in the village of Paharajpur, is the Lord's water-tank.

**70.** Beyond it, to the east, at two kroshas,
is the place where the Lord's staff was broken,[^PN-61] — so all say.

**71.** In the village of Amaradai is the Vidyadhara pond;[^PN-62]
in that place is a resting place of Mahaprabhu.

**72.** How many more places there are in Utkala!
How can I write of them without seeing them with my own eyes?

**73.** Vraja-bhumi, Navadvipa and Nilachala —
places of the gopala mahantas are in all [of them].[^PN-63]

## Conclusion

**74.** Whoever, having seen all these places, bows [to them] and remembers [them],
soon attains the feet of Radha-Krishna.

**75.** The desires of the heart are fulfilled, prosperity [comes] without end;
with a pure body [he] becomes a servant of the Vaishnavas.

**76.** In Nilachala, at Shvetaganga, at the place of Gangamata,[^PN-64]
this writing of the shripats of the mahantas was done.

**77.** Seven, anka, arrow, Brahma — [the year] of the Shaka king;
the month of Madhu, Monday, the lunar day of Rama-navami.[^PN-65]

**78.** In the fullness of love's rapture — [such is] the description of the book;
I offer [it] at the feet of Radha-Krishna [and] the Vaishnavas.

**79.** He whose longing is the feet of Sri Ratipati,[^PN-66]
Ramagopala Dasa, tells the *Pata-nirnaya*.

## The *Pata-paryatana* of Abhirama Dasa

*An extract from the Pata-nirnaya: the five dhamas of the Lord, Nityananda and Advaita; the twelve shripats of the gopalas with their names in Vraja; the villages of other associates and the great shripats Agradvipa and Shrikhanda; the author's signature (couplets 1–57)*

## Introduction

**1.** The shripats one should go round (parikrama)[^PP-1] —
briefly, merely pointing the way, I write them down truly.

**2.** Five dhamas[^PP-2] and twelve shripats — that makes seventeen;
together with the seventeen [shripats] of the devotees, thirty-four shripats, it is said[^PP-3].

**3.** In which villages the thirty-four shripats are — I will name them;
hear all the names in order, [and] be sure of them.

**4.** In which village each one lived — that is established;
writing the names and villages, I humbly beg [your indulgence][^PP-4].

## The five dhamas

**5.** Sri Navadvipa is the dhama [where] the Lord was born;
Katwa is a dhama of the Lord — know this for certain[^PP-5].

**6.** Ekachakra is the birthplace, Khardaha the residence:
the two dhamas of Sri Nityananda — know it truly[^PP-6].

**7.** The dhama of Sri Advaita is at Shantipura;
know these five dhamas, all of you, for certain.

## The twelve shripats

**8.** Abhirama, formerly Sudasa[^PP-7], dwelt at Khanakul;
the village is famed by the name Khanakul-Krishnanagar.

**9.** Halda-Maheshpur is the residence of Sundarananda;
Sundarananda was formerly Sudama — know this for certain[^PP-8].

**10.** Kanchrapara is the birthplace, Jalandi the residence:
Dhananjaya is Vasudama — know it truly[^PP-9].

**11.** At Ambika is the residence of Gauridasa Pandita;
Gauridasa was formerly Subala — know it truly[^PP-10].

**12.** Born at Akna-Mahesh, dwelling at Jageshvara:
such is Kamalakara Pippalai, this is certain[^PP-11].

**13.** Kamalakara's former name is Mahabala.
The residence of Uddharana Datta is said to be Krishnapur.

**14.** Near Hugli is the village of Krishnapur;
Uddharana's former name is Subahu — know this[^PP-12].

**15.** Saguna [and] Sardanga, near Sukhasagar:
[there] is the residence of Mahesha Pandita, I say with joined palms[^PP-13].

**16.** Mahesha was formerly Mahabahu — know this name.
At Badagachhi lived [a devotee] named Sri Krishnadasa[^PP-14].

**17.** Parameshvara Dasa was formerly Stoka-Krishna[^PP-15];
at Bodhkhana Nagara Purushottama was born.

**18.** Bodhkhana is in the pargana of Halda, know this, all;
Purushottama was formerly called the friend Sudama[^PP-16].

**19.** At Sanchra is the residence of Parameshvara Dasa;
Parameshvara was formerly the friend Arjuna — such is his fame[^PP-17].

**20.** This [Arjuna] is Madhava's friend, not the Pandava.
Hiranga, Sanchra-Panchra — so all people call [the village][^PP-18].

**21.** At Akai Hat is the residence of Kala Krishnadasa,
formerly famed by the name of the friend Lavanga[^PP-19].

**22.** Shridhara the Kholavecha[^PP-20] lived in Navadvipa;
formerly he was Madhumangala — know it truly.

**23.** Thus the twelve shripats have been written down.
[Now] hear the account of the villages where devotees lived.

## The villages of the devotees

**24.** Sri Gadadhara Pandita was born in Shrihatta;
coming to the Lord, he lived in Navadvipa[^PP-21].

**25.** The Pandita's nephew is his branch,
by name Nayanananda Mishra; he lives at Bharatpur[^PP-22].

**26.** At Ariadaha is the residence of Gadadhara Dasa;
Svarupa Gosvami always stayed in Navadvipa.

**27.** Svarupa was formerly Lalita — know this name;
Ramananda is Vishakha — know this, all[^PP-23].

**28.** Ramananda Raya lived on the bank of the Godavari —
in the southern country, at Sri Vidyanagara.

**29.** [That place] is not counted in the round of the shripats;
if you go to Nilachala, perhaps you will visit [it too][^PP-24].

**30.** At Kanchrapara [and] Kumarahatta Shivananda stayed;
formerly he is famed by the name Suchitra[^PP-25].

**31.** At Kulinagrama stayed Vasu Ramananda,
formerly famed by the name Champakalatika.

**32.** Agradvipa is a great shripat (mahapat)[^PP-26] — know this, devotees:
because two or three devotees lived there, it is called a mahapat.

**33.** In Agradvipa the three Ghoshas took birth;
for this reason the devotees call [it] a mahapat.

**34.** Govinda Ghosha is Ranga-devi, Vasu is Sudevi, it is said;
Madhava Ghosha is Tungavidya — know this for certain[^PP-27].

**35.** At Kongarhatta is the residence of Govindananda Thakura;
formerly he was the sakhi Indurekha — know it truly[^PP-28].

**36.** Names with an identification (anuvada and vidheya) are only these;
from here on, identifying names (vidheya) are not written[^PP-29].

## Shrikhanda and the other shripats

**37.** The [places] one should go round —
those villages I write down; know this for certain.

**38.** Writing down the villages and the names of the devotees, [I pray]:
forgive my offences, all you devotees.

**39.** The glorious great shripat — know this, all:
in Shrikhanda many devotees took birth[^PP-30].

**40.** Sri Mukunda, Narahari, Sri Raghunandana,
Chiranjiva Kaviraja and Sulochana.

**41.** The devotees glorify Sarakara Thakura[^PP-31];
because many devotees were born [here], [Shrikhanda] is called a mahapat.

**42.** Kulia and Paharpur — two [villages], this is established:
Vamshivadana, Kavidatta, Saranga Thakura[^PP-32].

**43.** In these two villages the three [of them] always live;
[so] the names of Kulia and Paharpur are famed.

**44.** Hear the account of Kanchrapara [and] Kumarahatta:
[there] appeared Shrikanta Sena, Kavikarna, Sri Rama Pandita[^PP-33].

**45.** In the village of Panihati is the abode of Raghava [and] Damayanti[^PP-34];
there is a story called "Raghava's bags".

**46.** At Bodhkhana is the residence of Sadashiva Kaviraja;
Sadashiva's son is Nagara Purushottama Dasa[^PP-35].

**47.** Four incomparable services are at Vallabhapur[^PP-36];
I will tell the names of the devotees who were [there]:

**48.** Kashishvara, Shankararanya and Shrinatha,
Sri Rudra Pandita and others — the residence of them all[^PP-37].

**49.** At Belun is Ananta Puri, great is [his] glory;
the dweller of Baghnapara is Sri Ramai Thakura[^PP-38].

**50.** At Guptipara Satyananda Sarasvati
serves Vrindavanachandra with love[^PP-39].

**51.** At Jirat are Madhava Acharya and Ganga-devi;
at Jashra is Jagadisha, ever rejoicing[^PP-40].

**52.** Halisahar, Natigrama — Narayani's son,
named Thakura Vrindavana, known to the world.

**53.** Natigrama is his birthplace, his residence is at Dendura;
he spread the *Sri Chaitanya-bhagavata*[^PP-41].

**54.** At Varahanagara is the residence of Bhagavatacharya;
at Naihati truly lived Rupa [and] Sanatana[^PP-42].

## Conclusion

**55.** The villages where one should perform parikrama —
those villages are written down here, truly.

**56.** In the book *Pata-nirnaya* [this is given] in detail;
having looked at it, I have made this extract[^PP-43].

**57.** Thus the *Pata-paryatana* is complete;
Abhirama Dasa composed it.

*Thus ends the "Pata-parikrama", the* Pata-paryatana.

[^PP-2]: "Dhama" (ধাম) — an abode of the Lord (here also of Nityananda and Advaita). The five dhamas are named in vv. 5–7.

## The *Shakha-nirnaya of Abhiramachandra* by Abhirama Dasa

*The disciples of Abhirama Thakura — twenty-four and a half "branches" — and their villages; the author's signature (couplets 1–17)*

**1.** However many disciples Abhiramachandra[^ASN-1] had —
their names and villages I write down truly.

**2.** At Khanakul is the residence of Krishnadasa Thakura;
in the village of Kaiyar Vedagarbha appeared[^ASN-2].

**3.** At Budhana is the residence of Haridasa[^ASN-3];
at Helagrama stays Pakhiya Gopaladasa[^ASN-4].

**4.** At Pakmalyati lives Gumphya-Narayana[^ASN-5];
at Sitanagar lives Thakura Mohana.

**5.** Everyone calls him Dariya-Mohana[^ASN-6]:
for what a handsome beard he has — quite extraordinary!

**6.** At Mahinnamuri lives [a disciple] named Satyaraghava[^ASN-7];
at Salika, one called Rajanikara Pandita.

**7.** At Bhajamora lives [a disciple] named Sundarananda[^ASN-8] —
a supremely learned brahmana, called Pandita.

**8.** At Dvipagrama stays Krishnananda Avadhuta;
at Sonatola, in Ranga-desha, Rangana Krishnadasa — this is certain[^ASN-9].

**9.** At Maldah lives Murari Dasa[^ASN-10];
at Panihati stays Thakura Mohana[^ASN-11].

**10.** At Radhanagar lives Yadu Haldar;
Hira Madhava Dasa stays at Anantanagar.

**11.** At Maheshagrama lives [a disciple] named Gopaladasa;
at Kotra lives one called Achyuta Pandita.

**12.** At Patlagrama is Lakshminarayana the doorkeeper[^ASN-12];
at Nilachala stays one called Gopinathadasa.

**13.** A dweller of Chunakhali is Dasa Nandakishora;
at Patagrama Vidura Brahmachari constantly roams[^ASN-13].

**14.** A dweller of Vishnupara, named Ramakrishnadasa[^ASN-14];
at Gaurangapur stays one called Kamalakaradasa[^ASN-15].

**15.** The disciple of Gopala Bhatta is Acharya Shrinivasa:
the Acharya is a limb-branch (anga-shakha) — know it truly[^ASN-16].

**16.** At Vishvagrama lives Thakura Balarama.
[Thus] I have told the names and villages of the twenty-four and a half branches[^ASN-17].

**17.** Meditating on the lotus feet of Sri Ratneshvara,
Dasa Abhirama composed [this] in brief[^ASN-18].

*Thus ends the* Shakha-nirnaya of Abhiramachandra.

[^ASN-1]: Abhiramachandra — Abhirama Thakura (Ramadasa; @abhirama), one of the twelve gopalas, whose shripat is Khanakul (#khanakul; cf. *Pata-paryatana*, v. 8). "Shakha" (branch) — a disciple.

[^ASN-2]: Kaiyar (কৈয়ড়) is a new place. Krishnadasa Thakura and Vedagarbha are new names.

[^ASN-3]: The text has বুঢ়নগ্রাম. Cf. Budhana (#budhana), the birthplace of Haridasa Thakura (CB Adi 2); whether this is the same village and the same Haridasa is unclear from the text — here he is named as a disciple of Abhirama.

[^ASN-4]: Helagrama is a new place. "Pakhiya" (পাখীয়া) — apparently a nickname (from পাখী, "bird"); the interpretation is uncertain.

[^ASN-5]: "Pakmalyati" (পাকমাল্যাটি) — reading unclear; a new place. "Gumphya-Narayana" (গুম্ফ্যানারায়ণ) — apparently "moustached Narayana" (গুম্ফ, "moustache"), a nickname like the "bearded Mohana" of the next verse; the reading is uncertain.

[^ASN-6]: "Dariya-Mohana" (দাড়িয়ামোহন) — "bearded Mohana". Sitanagar is a new place.

[^ASN-7]: The reading of the name is unclear (মহিন্নামুড়ি?); a new place. Salika (সালিকা) is a new place.

[^ASN-8]: The reading of the name is unclear (ভজমোড়া? ভঞ্জমোড়া?); a new place. This Sundarananda is a disciple of Abhirama, not Sundarananda of the twelve gopalas (*Pata-paryatana*, v. 9).

[^ASN-9]: The line is not quite clear: সোনাতোলা রঙ্গাদেশে রঙ্গনকৃষ্ণ দাসনিশ্চিত. Understood as "at Sonatola, in Ranga-desha, Rangana Krishnadasa"; "Ranga-desha" and "Rangana" are uncertain readings. Dvipagrama and Sonatola are new places.

[^ASN-10]: Maldah (#maldah).

[^ASN-11]: Panihati (#panihati). Another Thakura Mohana (cf. v. 4).

[^ASN-12]: "Doorkeeper" — দ্বারী; possibly a nickname or an office. Patlagrama is a new place.

[^ASN-13]: Chunakhali and Patagrama are new places. "Roams" — বিহার, literally "spends [his time], sports".

[^ASN-14]: The journal has বিন্নুপাড়া (?); probably Vishnupara (বিষ্ণুপাড়া). Reading unclear; a new place.

[^ASN-15]: Gaurangapur is a new place; cf. *Pata-nirnaya*, v. 41 (a Gaurangapur near Khanakul?). Kamalakaradasa is a disciple of Abhirama, not Kamalakara Pippalai.

[^ASN-16]: Shrinivasa Acharya (@srinivasa-acharya), disciple of Gopala Bhatta (@gopala-bhatta). "Anga-shakha" (অঙ্গশাখা) — so the journal; the reading of the image is clear. It may be a corruption of "ardha-shakha" ("half-branch"): then Shrinivasa would be the "half" in the count "twenty-four and a half" (v. 16) — without him the list contains exactly twenty-four disciples.

[^ASN-17]: Vishvagrama is a new place. The count: vv. 2–16 name twenty-four disciples and Shrinivasa.

[^ASN-18]: Ratneshvara — apparently the author's guru; a new name. The author, Abhirama Dasa, is not Abhirama Thakura (v. 1).
