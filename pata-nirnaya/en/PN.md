# The *Sri Pata-nirnaya* of Ramagopala Dasa

*A list of the Lord's dhamas, of the shripats and great shripats of His associates, and of places of birth and pastimes in Bengal and Orissa; the fruit of pilgrimage; chronogram and the author's signature (couplets and verses 1–79)*

*Obeisance to Sri Krishna Chaitanya-chandra.*

## Introduction

**1.** Sri Krishna Chaitanya is a descent for pastimes;
with His limbs, sub-limbs and associates He spread throughout the world.[^1]

**2.** The places of perfection, the eternal places, cannot be counted;
I write only a little — merely pointing out the direction.

**3.** [I write of] His own eight dhamas[^2] and the shripats[^3] of the mahantas,
and also [the places of] the offshoots and all the establishments of service.

**4.** Throughout the whole world there are dwellings of Vaishnavas;
in my own land I write of two or four [places] that are renowned.

**5.** Where a Vaishnava stays even half a moment, half the blink of an eye,
that the Puranas call a tirtha and a forest of austerity.

Thus [it is said]:

**6.** *"Where sadhakas stay even half a moment, half the blink of an eye,*
*that place should be known as a place of perfection: it is a tirtha, it is a forest of austerity."*[^4]

**7.** Where Vaishnavas live, drinking in talks about Krishna,
there the Ganga and the other tirthas take up their abode.

Thus [it is said]:

**8.** *"There is the Ganga and the Yamuna, there the Godavari, there the Sarasvati too;*
*all the tirthas dwell there, where talk of the magnanimous Achyuta goes on."*[^5]

**9.** Vaishnava gosanis make a tirtha of what is no tirtha;
therefore there is no fault in writing of those places.

Thus [it is said]:

**10.** *"[Such devotees] make the holy places themselves holy, by the Bearer of the club who dwells within their hearts."*[^6]

**11.** First I shall write of the dhamas of Sri Krishna Chaitanya,
then I shall write of the villages of the gopala mahantas;[^7]

**12.** where the birth and other pastimes of the Vaishnavas [took place] —
I shall tell briefly how it stands with those villages.

**13.** Vrindavana, Mathura, Dvaraka, Nilachala,
Navadvipa, Khardaha, the place Shantipura,

**14.** together with Kantakanagara[^8] — [these are] the dhamas of Krishna Chaitanya:
here He always abides with His devotees.

**15.** First I shall write of twenty-four shripats,
and also twelve great shripats (mahapats) — these I shall compose.[^9]

**16.** Where one or two mahantas [live], that is called a shripat;
where many Vaishnavas [live], that I record as a great shripat.

**17.** There is no consideration of what comes before and what after:
I write in the order of writing, as it appears [to me].

## The twelve shripats

**18.** In Radha-desha is the village of Sri Vaidyakhanda:[^10]
the dhama of Mukunda, Narahari and Sri Raghunandana.

**19.** Chiranjiva, Sulochana, Kaviraja, Mahananda;[^11]
the service of Sri Krishna [and] the Vaishnavas is supreme bliss.

**20.** Across the Suradhuni is the village named Agradvipa,[^12]
where Gopinatha is manifest — Bhagavan Himself.

**21.** Govinda Ghosha, Vasu Ghosha and Madhava Ghosha —
to see that place is the highest delight.

**22.** Across from Navadvipa is Kulia-Paharpur,
where Vamshivadana Dasa is — the city of the flute's rasa.[^13]

**23.** Kavidatta Mahashaya, Thakura Saranga;[^14]
the place of Mahaprabhu — waves of pastimes and play.

**24.** To its south is the village of Ambuya-muluk:[^15]
to see the service of Chaitanya and Nityananda is great happiness.

**25.** Gauridasa Pandita, his younger brother Krishnadasa,[^16]
Hridaya Chaitanya Dasa — many manifestations [are there].

**26.** To its west is the village named Kulinagrama,
where the incomparable Vasu family is — Ramananda and the others.

**27.** Many dear to Mahaprabhu lived there;
much service of Krishna, and Haridasa stayed there.

**28.** Across from Triveni is the village of Kanchrapara,[^17]
where Krishna-raya Thakura is, incomparable, as one hears of Him.[^18]

**29.** Shivananda Sena and Sena Shrikanta,
Kavi Karnapura and others — wholehearted devotees.

**30.** To its south is the village of Kumarahatta:
Srivasa Pandita Thakura, [the Deity] named "Gauranga-raya".

**31.** The dwelling of many — Shivananda Pandita[^19] and others;
Mahaprabhu's beloved place — the Deity "Gopala-raya".

**32.** To the south of Khardaha is the village of Ariadaha,
the very own dhama of Gadadhara Dasa Thakura.

**33.** To the north is Purandara Pandita, to the south Raghava;
many Vaishnavas gather — the greatest festival.

**34.** Near it is the village named Panihati,
the dhama of Raghava Dasa Thakura[^20] and Damayanti.

**35.** There Sri Ramadasa Thakura is manifest,[^21]
who without effort lifted the log of sixteen [bearers].

**36.** [It is] the abode of Mahaprabhu's love alone;
to see Raghava's bags (jhali) is the highest joy.[^22]

**37.** Halda-Maheshpur and Bodhkhana[^23] —
two villages in one land, counted as one.

**38.** There the service of Thakura Sundara is performed;[^24]
[the place of] Sadashiva Kaviraja is determined to be Bodhkhana.

**39.** His son is Thakura Purushottama:[^25]
a great tree, a great fruit, the best of the best.

**40.** In Khanakul-Krishnanagar is Thakura Abhirama;[^26]
his wife is she whose name is Malini.

**41.** In that very place is the Gaurangapur of Vasu Ghosha;[^27]
the navaratna of Yadava Simha — to see it is a wonder.

**42.** Chatra-Vallabhapur is across from Khardaha:[^28]
Kashishvara, Shankararanya and Shrinatha Pandita.

**43.** The service of Rudra Pandita is named Radhavallabha:
a form that enchants the worlds — a new Kamadeva.

**44.** Thus I have written of the twelve great shripats;
now I tell the names of thirteen more shripats.[^29]

## The thirteen shripats

**45.** In Akai Hat lived Thakura Krishnadasa,
whose joy was the nupura of Raghunandana that he obtained.[^30]

**46.** In the village of Anadiha lived Thakura Gangadasa;[^31]
in Badagachhi [and] Shaligrama is the dwelling of Krishnadasa.[^32]

**47.** In Belun is Ananta Puri, great is [his] glory;[^33]
in Baghnapara are Sri Vamshi [and] Ramai Thakura.[^34]

**48.** In Bharatpur is Mahashaya Sri Mishra Thakura:[^35]
his glory, full of the pastimes of Radha and Krishna, is great.

**49.** In Guptipara is Satyananda Sarasvati:[^36]
the service of Vrindavanachandra with the highest love.

**50.** In Jirat are Madhavacharya and Ganga-devi;[^37]
in Jashra[^38] is Jagadisha, who bears the title "Dancer".

**51.** Halisahar [and] Dendudi are the two places
of Vrindavana Dasa, the son of Narayani.[^39]

**52.** Varahanagara is [the place] of Bhagavata Acharya;[^40]
in Saptagrama is the house of Uddharana Datta [and] Sugriva Mishra.[^41]

**53.** Sanchra-Panchra, Karanda, the village of Shitala[^42] —
the service of Dhananjaya Pandita is manifold.

**54.** Thus I have made known the twenty-five shripats;
now I shall write of the birthplaces, and also of [the places of] pastimes and play.

## Places of birth and pastimes

**55.** In the village of Benapole is the dwelling of Haridasa;
in Phulia the great soul stayed for some days.

**56.** The village of Raghunatha Dasa is Chandapur;[^43]
[it is] a village near Hugli, so all people say.

**57.** The dwelling of Kalidasa Thakura is Saptagrama;
the birthplace of Sanatana [and] Rupa is Bakla.[^44]

**58.** In Shrihatta, in Chattagrama, is the home of Vidyanidhi;[^45]
in the village of Ekachakra[^46] Nityananda was born.

**59.** Ramakeli and Kanai-Natashala[^47] are [places of] the Lord's rest;
and in Radha-desha there are many more places.

**60.** Under a jivaputri tree[^48] He rested for a moment:
that village is called Naopara-Atkuri.

**61.** Across the Damodar is the village of Barasat:[^49]
one day the Lord accepted alms there.

**62.** Lokanatha Gosani was born in the land of Yashohar;
the village of Nagara Purushottama is called Nakhachhara.[^50]

(The dwelling of Nagara Purushottama is in Vanakunda.[^51])

**63.** In Sardanga-Sultanpur is the house of Mahesha Pandita;[^52]
in the village of Dogachhia is Balarama, best of the twice-born.[^53]

**64.** [The place] of Suryadasa Sarakhela is determined to be Khana;[^54]
in Uttaranapur is the brother, Jagannatha Dasa Mahashaya.[^55]

**65.** Within Gauda is a village named Pokhuria:
the service of Nrisimha Chaitanya Dasa, named Vrindavanachandra.[^56]

**66.** In Tamoluk is the temple of Madhava Ghosha:
the shelter of Hari-Vishnu, Jagannatha and Gauranga.[^57]

**67.** Pandita Gosvami [and] Vakreshvara lived in Nilachala;[^58]
Gopinatha's Tota is the dwelling of Gopala Guru.

**68.** The land of Udra: Remuna, Alalanatha, Nilagiri,
Cuttack, Bhubaneshvara, Konark, Vidyanagari.[^59]

**69.** To the west of Sonakanta, across the Suvarnarekha,[^60]
in the village of Paharajpur, is the Lord's water-tank.

**70.** Beyond it, to the east, at two kroshas,
is the place where the Lord's staff was broken,[^61] — so all say.

**71.** In the village of Amaradai is the Vidyadhara pond;[^62]
in that place is a resting place of Mahaprabhu.

**72.** How many more places there are in Utkala!
How can I write of them without seeing them with my own eyes?

**73.** Vraja-bhumi, Navadvipa and Nilachala —
places of the gopala mahantas are in all [of them].[^63]

## Conclusion

**74.** Whoever, having seen all these places, bows [to them] and remembers [them],
soon attains the feet of Radha-Krishna.

**75.** The desires of the heart are fulfilled, prosperity [comes] without end;
with a pure body [he] becomes a servant of the Vaishnavas.

**76.** In Nilachala, at Shvetaganga, at the place of Gangamata,[^64]
this writing of the shripats of the mahantas was done.

**77.** Seven, anka, arrow, Brahma — [the year] of the Shaka king;
the month of Madhu, Monday, the lunar day of Rama-navami.[^65]

**78.** In the fullness of love's rapture — [such is] the description of the book;
I offer [it] at the feet of Radha-Krishna [and] the Vaishnavas.

**79.** He whose longing is the feet of Sri Ratipati,[^66]
Ramagopala Dasa, tells the *Pata-nirnaya*.

[^1]: *Sangopanga-parishada* — cf. SB 11.5.32 ("krishna-varnam tvishakrishnam sangopangastra-parshadam"): angas and upangas, "limbs and sub-limbs", i.e. the Lord's closest associates.
[^2]: "Dhama" (ধাম) — an abode of the Lord. The eight dhamas are named in vv. 13–14.
[^3]: "Pat" (পাট) — shripat, the residence and place of service of an associate of the Lord; a "mahanta" is the head of such a place, a revered Vaishnava.
[^4]: The source of the verse is not named in the text.
[^5]: The source of the verse is not named in the text. "Achyuta" — "the Infallible", a name of Krishna.
[^6]: SB 1.13.10, second half of the verse (Yudhishthira's words to Vidura). The separate couplet before it (v. 9) is Bengali.
[^7]: "Gopala mahantas" (গোপাল মহান্ত) — apparently mahantas from among the gopalas (cowherd boys of Vraja), associates of Nityananda: many of those named below are among the twelve gopalas (Abhirama, Sundarananda, Gauridasa, Purushottama, Kaliya Krishnadasa, Uddharana, Mahesha, Dhananjaya). It may also be taken more broadly — "mahantas, [associates] of Gopala". Cf. v. 73.
[^8]: Kantakanagara is Katwa (#katwa), the place of the Lord's sannyasa.
[^9]: "Mahapat" (মহাপাট) — a great shripat, where many Vaishnavas lived (v. 16). The count in the text does not match what is promised here: below the author names twelve great shripats (v. 44) and thirteen more, twenty-five in all (v. 54), not 24 + 12.
[^10]: So in the print (শ্রীবৈদ্যখণ্ড). From the persons named (Mukunda Dasa, Narahari Sarakara, Raghunandana) this is Shrikhanda (#shrikhanda), the author's home. "Radha-desha" (রাঢ়দেশ) is Rarh (#radha-desha).
[^11]: Chiranjiva Sena and Sulochana were residents of Shrikhanda. "Kaviraja" may refer to Chiranjiva's sons, Ramachandra and Govinda Kaviraja. We do not know Mahananda from other sources; "mahananda" may here be not a name but "great bliss". The reading is uncertain.
[^12]: Suradhuni, "the river of the gods", is the Ganga. Agradvipa (#agradvipa) is the shripat of Govinda Ghosha; Gopinatha is his Deity.
[^13]: Kulia-Paharpur is now part of Navadvip (#kulia). "Vamshirasapura" (বংশীরসপুর), "the city of the flute's rasa", seems to be a play on the name Vamshivadana ("He whose mouth is a flute"); it is not attested as the name of a village.
[^14]: "Kavidatta" — so in the print (কবিদত্ত); the person is unclear. Possibly a corruption of the name of Vasudeva Datta, who by tradition lived at Mamgachhi (Modadruma-dvipa) together with Saranga Thakura; but this is only a guess.
[^15]: "Ambuya-muluk" (অম্বুয়ামুলুক, lit. "the land of Ambuya") — from the persons (Gauridasa Pandita, Hridaya Chaitanya, the Deities Gaura-Nitai) this is Ambika-Kalna (#ambika-kalna).
[^16]: Cf. @krishnadasa-poet (according to Majumdar, Gauridasa Pandita's younger brother).
[^17]: Kanchrapara is a new place. Triveni — #saptagrama.
[^18]: "Thakura" here and below (vv. 30–31) means the Deity: Krishna-raya, Gauranga-raya, Gopala-raya. According to *Prema-vilasa* 24, Krishna-raya is the Deity of Srinatha Chakravarti at Kumarahatta (@srinatha-chakravarti); here He is placed at Kanchrapara.
[^19]: Possibly Shivananda Sena (v. 29; @shivananda-sena of Kumarahatta); the text has "Shivananda Pandita".
[^20]: I.e. Raghava Pandita of Panihati (@raghava-pandita); Damayanti was his sister.
[^21]: Ramadasa is Abhirama Thakura (@abhirama; v. 40). Cf. CC Adi 11.13: he lifted like a flute a log that sixteen men carried.
[^22]: "Raghava's bags" — the delicacies Raghava Pandita and Damayanti sent every year to the Lord in Puri.
[^23]: Halda-Maheshpur is the shripat of Sundarananda (#maheshpur; "Halda" is new). Bodhkhana is a new place.
[^24]: Sundarananda (@sundarananda).
[^25]: Purushottama, the son of Sadashiva Kaviraja, is Nagara Purushottama (@purushottama-nagara; cf. v. 62).
[^26]: Khanakul (#khanakul). Krishnanagar here is part of the name of Abhirama's shripat, not the town of Krishnanagar in Nadia (#krishnanagar).
[^27]: Gaurangapur is a new place; where it is is not clear from the text ("in that very place" — apparently near Khanakul). Vasu Ghosha's shripat is usually said to be Tamoluk (#tamluk); cf. v. 66. A "navaratna" is a temple with nine towers; Yadava Simha (a new name) was apparently its builder.
[^28]: Chatra and Vallabhapur are new places (cf. "Ballabhpur" in the glossary). Shankararanya — cf. @shankararanya (identification unclear). Shrinatha Pandita — the person is not identified (cf. @srinatha-chakravarti).
[^29]: The thirteen shripats are vv. 45–53; v. 54 gives the total as twenty-five (cf. note to v. 15).
[^30]: Akai Hat (#akai-hat) is the shripat of Kaliya (Kala) Krishnadasa (@kaliya-krishnadasa). The pond Nupura-kunda — by tradition the nupura (ankle-bell) of Raghunandana fell into it.
[^31]: "Anadiha" (অনাড়িহা) — the reading is unclear (print: অনাডিহা). Gangadasa is not identified (cf. @baru-gangadasa, @gangadasa-chaturbhuja). This line and the next half-line are printed as separate centred lines, as one couplet.
[^32]: Badagachhi (#badagachhi), Shaligrama (#shaligrama-village); Krishnadasa is @krishnadasa-badagachhi.
[^33]: Belun is a new place. Ananta Puri — @ananta-puri.
[^34]: Baghnapara is a new place. "Sri Vamshi Ramai" — apparently Vamshivadana (@vamshivadana) and Ramai (Ramachandra) Gosvami (@ramai-jahnava), his grandson; it may also be read "Ramai, [descendant] of Vamshi".
[^35]: Bharatpur is a new place. "Mishra Thakura" is not named; possibly Nayanananda Mishra (@nayanananda-mishra), but the text does not say so.
[^36]: Satyananda Sarasvati is a new name (not to be confused with @satyananda-bharati). Guptipara — #guptipara; Vrindavanachandra is a Deity.
[^37]: Jirat is a new place. Madhava Acharya, husband of Ganga (@madhava-acharya-ganga), and Ganga, Nityananda's daughter (@ganga-nityananda).
[^38]: The text has যশোড়া (Yashora) — Jashra (#jashra), the shripat of Jagadisha Pandita (@jagadisha-pandita), the famous dancer (GGD 143).
[^39]: Halisahar — cf. #kumarahatta (Kumarahatta/Halisahar). "Dendudi" (দেন্দুড়ি) — so in the print; possibly Denur (দেনুড়), but we do not identify it. Vrindavana Dasa — @vrindavana-dasa; Narayani — @narayani.
[^40]: Bhagavatacharya (@bhagavatacharya); Varahanagara — #varahanagara.
[^41]: Uddharana Datta — @uddharana-datta; Sugriva Mishra — @govindananda (Govindananda).
[^42]: Sanchra-Panchra — #sanchra-panchra; Karanda is a new place; "Shitala" is Shitalagrama (#shitalagrama).
[^43]: The text has চাঁদপুর; cf. #chandapur-saptagrama (Chandapur near Saptagrama, CC Antya 3.164).
[^44]: Bakla — cf. #chandradvipa (Bakla-Chandradvipa).
[^45]: Chatigrama (চাটিগ্রাম) is Chattagrama (#chattagrama), the home of Pundarika Vidyanidhi (@pundarika-vidyanidhi); Shrihatta — #shrihatta. Whether "Shrihatta" is linked to Vidyanidhi or stands separately is not clear from the text.
[^46]: The text has এক চাকা (Eka-chaka) — Ekachakra (#ekachakra).
[^47]: #kanai-natashala; Ramakeli — #ramakeli.
[^48]: "Jivaputri" (জীব পুত্রি) — apparently the putranjiva tree (Bengali "jiyaputi"); reading and interpretation uncertain. Naopara-Atkuri is a new place.
[^49]: The Damodar is a river; Barasat is a new place.
[^50]: Yashohar (Jessore) — cf. #talagadi (Lokanatha's birthplace). Nakhachhara is a new place. Nagara Purushottama — @purushottama-nagara.
[^51]: This line stands in brackets in the print, without a pair — apparently a variant inserted by the editor; translated without a number. The name reads বনকুণ্ডাতে (Vanakunda) — the reading is unclear.
[^52]: Sardanga-Sultanpur is a new place. Mahesha Pandita — @mahesha-pandita.
[^53]: Cf. #dogachhia (in *Prema-vilasa*, the village of Krishnadasa Hoda). "Balarama Dvija" is not identified (cf. @balarama-dasa-poet).
[^54]: "Khana" (খানায়) — the reading of the place is unclear: possibly a village Khana; we do not identify it. Suryadasa Sarakhela — @suryadasa-pandita.
[^55]: Uttaranapur (উত্তরণপুর) is a new place. "The brother" — apparently Suryadasa's brother; we do not know a Jagannatha Dasa as Suryadasa's brother from other sources.
[^56]: Gauda — #gauda; Pokhuria is a new place. "Nrisimha Chaitanya Dasa" can be read either as one person (cf. @nrisimha-chaitanya) or as two — Nrisimha and Chaitanya Dasa. Vrindavanachandra is a Deity.
[^57]: Tamoluk — #tamluk. Here the temple is called that of Madhava Ghosha (@madhava-ghosha); usually Tamoluk is considered the shripat of Vasu Ghosha (cf. v. 41). The second line is apparently a list of the temple's Deities.
[^58]: Pandita Gosvami is Gadadhara Pandita; Vakreshvara — @vakreshvara; Tota — #tota (Tota-Gopinatha); Gopala Guru — @gopala-guru.
[^59]: Udra (উড্রদেশ) is Orissa. Remuna — #remuna; Alalanatha — #alalanatha; Nilagiri is a new place; "Chataka" (চটক) in the print is Cuttack (#cuttack); Bhubaneshvara — #bhubaneshvara; Konark — #konark; Vidyanagari — cf. #vidyanagara.
[^60]: "Sonakanta" (সোনাকান্তার?) — the reading is unclear. Suvarnarekha — #suvarnarekha. Paharajpur is a new place. "Water-tank" (জলাধার) — apparently a pond or well connected with the Lord.
[^61]: Dandabhanga — the place where Nityananda broke the Lord's staff (danda) (CC Madhya 5).
[^62]: "Amara-dai" (অমর দই) — the reading is unclear; we do not identify it. "The Vidyadhara pond" (পুষ্কণি বিদ্যাধর) — so in the print.
[^63]: Or: "in Vraja-bhumi, Navadvipa and Nilachala are the places of all the gopala mahantas". See note to v. 11.
[^64]: Shvetaganga is a pond in Puri (a new place); "the place of Gangamata" — cf. @gangamata-gosvamini (the Ganga-mata Math in Puri). That is, the book was written in Puri.
[^65]: The chronogram. সাত "seven" = 7; অঙ্ক "digit" = 9 (there are nine digits); শর "arrow" = 5 (the five arrows of Kama); ব্রহ্ম "Brahma" = 1. By the rule "digits go leftward" (অঙ্কস্য বামা গতিঃ) the number is read from the end: 1597 of the Shaka era. The month of Madhu is Chaitra; Rama-navami is the ninth day of the bright half of Chaitra. 1597 Shaka + 78 = 1675 CE (spring). We have not checked the weekday (Monday).
