# Act 9. The Journey to Mathura (Śrī-mathurā-gamana)

*A pair of Kinnaras tell of the three kinds of the Lord's mercy; Prataparudra learns from messengers of Chaitanya's journey through Gauda to Vrindavana, of the meetings with Rupa and Sanatana, and receives Him on His return to Nilachala.*

*(Enter a pair of Kinnaras.)*

**Man.** My dear! This year the Gundicha festival of Jagannatha appeared to us supremely beautiful — more beautiful than in all the years gone by.

**Woman.** How so?

**Man.** This year the Lord Sri Krishna Chaitanya Himself — bliss embodied, golden like a golden mountain, wearing the garb of a king of ascetics, descended in the form of a devotee — made this great festival supremely beautiful in all its fullness.[^1]

**Woman.** Alas, alas! Why was I not taken along? Why was it not my lot to see it?

**Man.** My dear! Next year you will be able to see Him.

**Woman.** Next year — if only it will be so.

**Man.** My dear! From now on He will abide right here.

**Woman.** And what is certain about that?

**Man.** I know how matters stand.

**Woman.** How do you know it?

**Man.** From the account that His people pass on to one another — those who know His wondrous deeds.

**Woman.** What account is that?

**Man.** Listen, my dear: the ways in which He shows mercy to people are three.

**Woman.** What are these three kinds?

**Man.** The first — by appearing in person; the second — by entering the heart of another; the third — by appearing at the mere thought of Him.

**Woman.** Tell it in detail.

**Man.** To those who are able to come to the land of Purushottama He appears in person. For every year from various countries thousands upon thousands of beings come swiftly flocking, who have never before seen Him or heard of Him — and all of them long to see Him even more than to see Jagannatha.

**Woman.** And then, and then?

**Man.** Then: among them the Gaudiyas are dear to Him, and among the Gaudiyas there are some especially dear — those who have seen Him hundreds of times, and even those who have not seen Him but possess good fortune.[^2] Such as these:

> **1.** Certain natives of Khanda, headed by Narahari and Raghunandana — though from "Khanda", yet of undivided (*akhaṇḍa*) good fortune:[^3] never having seen Him before, every autumn they attain Purushottama.[^4]

> **2.** And also the people of Kulina-grama, descendants of Gunaraja — Ramananda and the others, friends of the Lord;[^5] and these great scholars, Nyaya Acharya and the rest, full of growing love — every autumn they come here.

And the Nyaya Acharya named Bhagavan[^6] has stayed in Purushottama itself for life, wishing to see the Lord Chaitanya. Such is His mercy shown in person. To those who are unable to come He shows mercy by rising in the heart of another. And worthy to have Him rise in their hearts are Advaita, Nakula Brahmachari and those like them.

**Woman.** Tell me at least something.

**Man.** The story of how He entered into Advaita is too long — it would take much time. Listen, then, how He entered the heart of Nakula Brahmachari.

**Woman.** Tell it, I am all attention.

**Man.** In Ambu-grama[^7] lives a supreme Vaishnava, a brahmachari from birth, named Nakula. Once a certain state came upon him, as if he were possessed by a spirit; he was overflowing with tears of bliss and trembling, and by his mere appearance he produced in the depths of everyone's heart the conviction: "Sri Chaitanya has entered into him." So several days and nights passed.

**Woman.** And then, and then?

**Man.** After that —

> **3.** colouring all the quarters around with a golden radiance, having lost the outward signs of consciousness in the enjoyment of bliss, he became an object of worship for hundreds of thousands of loving people — from children to old men and youths.

**Woman.** And then, and then?

**Man.** Then by the will of fate there came there at that time Shivananda, an associate of the Lord Chaitanya. Hearing this news, he doubted it strongly and, though he wished to see him,[^8] thought: "Ah, what is it to me to see him? For I have seen the Lord Himself face to face. What happiness at the sight of this man could equal the happiness of beholding Him? None." Having turned back, he thought again: "Ah, if he himself calls me, standing outside the whole crowd, brings me near to him and declares my chosen mantra — then truly the Lord abides in him." With this thought Shivananda stood outside the spreading crowd. And the other, though he had kept silent all the time the descent lasted, said only: "Ho, who is here? Call Shivananda, who is standing far off!" — and at this single command several people rushed off running, searched for him here and there, calling his name, and, finding him standing very far away, brought him to him. Then he declared: "Shivananda! Listen to what you have conceived in your mind: your chosen mantra has four syllables, its deity is Gaura-Gopala." Understanding this, Shivananda concluded: "This report is true."[^9]

**Woman.** Noble husband! And what is the third?

**Man.** Hear about the third too, the appearance at a mere thought. Once that same Shivananda's nephew, Shrikanta, came alone to Sri Purushottama for the first time and beheld the feet of the Lord Chaitanya. And at that very time the Lord, the only friend of the world, jestingly said something to him in the presence of the Puri master, Svarupa and the others: "Shrikanta! This year tell Advaita and the others, burning with compassion, not to come: I Myself shall go there." And further: "Tell Shivananda too, your maternal uncle: in the month of Pausha I shall be there with him. Jagadananda is there — I shall accept alms from him." When the returning Shrikanta delivered the Lord's message, Advaita and all the others gave up their preparations for the journey. But Shivananda, longing for the Lord's coming and reasoning, "This will be useful for the Lord's meal", began to store up what the Lord loves: vastuka leaves, the heart of the plantain stem, ears of grain and the rest.[^10]

**Woman.** And then, and then?

**Man.** Then by the will of fate Raya Ramananda came from the Godavari, and, held back by his entreaties, the Lord could not set out.

**Woman.** And then, and then?

**Man.** Then Shivananda in due time said to the renowned Sri Nrisimhananda Brahmachari — the highest of the lords of yoga, like Nrisimha Himself — who was formerly known as Pradyumna Brahmachari but had received the name Nrisimhananda from the Lord Himself, since he had attained perfection in the worship of Nrisimha: "Teacher! The Lord said, 'I shall come', and He has not come. I look at the vastuka greens — and there is only grief in my heart."

**Woman.** And then, and then?

**Man.** Then he said: "I myself shall bring Him — wait two days." Knowing his power, Shivananda believed him. And Nrisimhananda — the joy of Nrisimha and one who rejoices in Nrisimha[^11] — from that very time sank into samadhi, and within two days called Shivananda: "Ho! The Lord Chaitanya has been brought to the house of Raghava. In the morning He will come here. I myself shall cook, and I shall give the alms." Hearing this, Shivananda too set to work, while he himself bathed at dawn, became especially pure and began cooking. He cooked as he himself wished, whatever he pleased. Then at that very time, full of rasa, he divided and prepared three separate offerings: for Sri Chaitanya, for Jagannatha and for Nrisimha. Then, having offered them — each under its own name — he went outside and, closing his eyes, saw with his inner eye that all three offerings were being eaten by one and the same Lord. Then, filled with great bliss, with streams of tears flowing, he cried out loudly, weeping, in loving anger and with gracious reproach: "Good, good! You are one with Jagannatha — so let You eat Jagannatha's offering. But how can You eat the offering of my Nrisimha? Today through my fault Nrisimha has been left hungry!" When he wept so loudly, Shivananda asked: "Teacher! Why are you crying out so?"

**Woman.** And then, and then?

**Man.** Then he said: "Your Gosvami Chaitanya has eaten all three offerings. Nrisimha has had to fast."

**Woman.** And then, and then?

**Man.** Then Shivananda said: "Teacher! Another offering must be prepared for Nrisimha." When this was done, he calmed down. There the matter rested, but in Shivananda a doubt arose: "Did he say this only in a state of possession, or is it true?" With this thought he came to Purushottama the next year and went to the Lord Chaitanya. And when, in the course of conversation, the Lord began to speak of the greatness of Nrisimhananda and incidentally said that he cooks very well — and all were surprised — "Last year, in the month of Pausha, I accepted alms from him; there I learned his skill in cooking" — all remained in doubt, but Shivananda was completely convinced. So I have explained the three kinds of mercy to you.

**Woman.** A wonder, a wonder! Then tell me about this too: why did Ramananda prevent Him from going there?

**Man.** My dear! He loves the Lord so much that he cannot bear separation from Him. Therefore, yielding to his entreaties, the Lord, though He wished to go to Mathura, delayed for two years, saying "today", "tomorrow".

**Woman.** So from now on He will abide here. Or else He will go to Mathura.

**Man.** My dear! Now, after long persuading that same Ramananda and obtaining his consent, He has set out to go — by way of Gauda.

**Woman.** Noble husband! And will He come back here again?

**Man.** Certainly.

**Woman.** That is just the doubt: for Mathura is His beloved place.

**Man.** Be that as it may, still —

> **4.** to lighten the heavy burden taken up by the Moon of Nilachala, who wishes to deliver all beings, down to the last simpleton, this Purushottama will again abide in Purushottama.[^12]

**Woman.** Yes, so it will be, so it will be.

**Behind the scenes.** Bhattacharya! How could Ramananda give his consent to this matter?

**Man.** My dear! Do you hear — just what I was saying: here is the Gajapati too, grieved by separation from Him, conversing with Sarvabhauma. Let us too go and serve the Lord, the Moon of Nilachala, with song. *(Exeunt both.)*

*— End of the introductory scene (praveśaka) —*

*(Enter the King, seated on the throne, and Sarvabhauma.)*

**King.** Bhattacharya, the Lord has set out only because the knot of the noose of Ramananda's insistent entreaties has loosened.

**Sarvabhauma.** What persistence can one show against the Lord? And still He delayed a whole two years.

**King.** Bhattacharya, Ramananda has done me a great kindness. For —

> **5.** he brought the All-merciful One onto the road from the capital and arranged that I should see Him;
> he granted me the touch of the lotuses of His feet — inaccessible, yet bringing happiness in full;
> he gave me to drink the nectar of His speech — merciful, sweetest, drunk by the ear.
> What could not be achieved by great efforts came about suddenly — and still there is emptiness in my heart.

**Sarvabhauma.** Maharaja! Ramananda is truly the best of Bhagavatas. For it is said: "He who holds the lotuses of His feet with the rope of love is called the foremost of Bhagavatas" [*Bhāg.* 11.2.55]. Therefore the Lord is in his power. That is why, yielding to his request, the Lord showed you such mercy.

**King.** How far is Ramananda to accompany Him?

**Sarvabhauma.** I have heard — as far as Bhadraka.

**King.** How many people have gone with the Master?

**Sarvabhauma.** Only five or six: the Puri master, Damodara, Jagadananda, Gopinatha, Govinda and others.

**King.** Alas —

> **6.** although the Lord of the world, the Lord of the Blue Mountain, shines, seated on the throne, in all His manifest supreme majesty,
> yet when the Lord Sri Krishna Chaitanya-deva goes off again to the north — alas, the three worlds are empty!

**Sarvabhauma.** King! Such is the way[^13] of love free of self-interest.

**King.** Did none of our people follow the Master?

**Sarvabhauma.** King, that is said only out of love. What need has He of your people! And still, as far as the borders of your dominions, some have already gone ahead with your letter — they will arrange everything properly.

> **7.** At every halting place they set up a new lodging beforehand, fill every house with abundant food,
> and the people appointed for it everywhere arrange everything carefully[^14] — and, entering there, the travellers shake off the fatigue of the journey on foot.

And the Lord thinks only that this is Ramananda's doing.

*(Enter the doorkeeper.)*

**Doorkeeper.** Sire! Ramananda and others are standing at the gate.

**King.** Bring them here quickly.

**Doorkeeper.** As you command. *(Goes out and returns with them.)*

**King** *(respectfully seating him)*. Tell me, how far did you accompany the Lord?

**Ramananda.** Although at every step I was told, "From here, from here go back!", I accompanied Him as far as Bhadraka. Maharaja, it is hard to leave the path of worldly duties, for —

> **8.** even Him, the ocean of compassion, the saviour of the most wretched — O Shiva, Shiva! — I left and came back only for fear of you.
> Ah, why did my body not fall there? Alas, I, whose frame is hard as a diamond thunderbolt — I have come back!

*(Sheds tears.)*[^15]

**Sarvabhauma.** Ramananda! You are so firm of spirit — why do you torment yourself so? Such are the Lord's pastimes: He left the people of Vraja and went to Mathura, from there to Dvaraka, and from there somewhere else. How did the people there bear separation from Him? Although separation from Him is unbearable, He Himself gives the strength to bear it. So enough of lamenting. You should now console the King, not sadden him by showing your sadness.

**King.** Tell it.

**Ramananda.** As far as the borders of your dominions your people are going. Beyond that, along the road, my knowledgeable people: some will go all the way to the kingdom of Gauda, some of them will return from no great distance, and some will go still further.

**Doorkeeper** *(entering)*. Sire! Of the Raya's people who went to escort Mahaprabhu, some have returned.

**King.** Let them in without delay.

*(The doorkeeper does so.)*

**Man** *(entering)*. Glory to the King!

**Ramananda.** Tell it, ho! How far has the Lord gone?

**Man.** As far as Kuliya-grama.

**King** *(looks into Sarvabhauma's face)*.

**Sarvabhauma.** Sire! Opposite Navadvipa, on the other bank of the Ganga, there is a village of that name.

**King.** Tell it from the beginning.

**Man.** Sire, from here to the borders of the King's dominions everyone went on foot, and the comfort of the journey was ensured by your power alone. To enter the territory of Gauda there are three ways: two are blocked, and the third is a water fortress. When they moved towards it, the governor of the frontier there, a Turk — like an ulcer-abscess in the heart,[^16] tormenting everyone to the very quick, a great drunkard, the crown of villains — was heard to say: "It will go badly for those who come from that country." Fear arose in everyone. No one told Mahaprabhu of it. Our frontier chief said: "Stay here a little, until I conclude an agreement with him." And at the very time this was said, a certain man came from that same governor to our frontier chief.[^17]

**King.** And then, and then?

**Man.** Then ours said: "If he comes with three or four people, let him come." Hearing this, he came so, fell to the ground near the Lord's feet and bowed for a long time. Then all said: "Lord! Only with his help can one go safely. Cast a merciful glance on him." Yielding to their request, the Lord looked at him — and this Yavana, with his hair standing on end, in tears, his voice breaking, became as if possessed. Then Gopinatha Acharya said: "Ho! How could Mahaprabhu go safely?"

**King.** And then, and then?

**Man.** Then he asked: "How far must you go?" Gopinatha answered: "For now — as far as Panihati."

**King.** And then, and then?

**Man.** Then —

> **9.** with hair standing on end, with streams of tears flowing, he said in a choked voice:
> "Oh, how great is my good fortune — that I can serve the Lord with help!"

Then a new boat with a cabin in the middle, manned by boatmen who were all good people,[^18] was washed again and in the twinkling of an eye brought to the river. Then he himself got into another boat and said: "Let the Lord board this one" — and all boarded it together with the Lord. Then he himself, to ward off danger from river pirates, went ahead and, passing Mantreshvara, reached the village of Picchhanada. When he was turning back, the Lord bestowed on him mahaprasada of Jagannatha — sweet balls called "manohara". Receiving them, he cried "Say 'Hari'!", went into ecstasy and attained the state of a great Bhagavata.

*(The King acts amazement.)*

**Sarvabhauma.** Such is the Lord's play. For —

> **10.** this independent Lord shows mercy even where it is out of place, and where it is in place, He produces, indeed, only indifference.
> The divine Rama made Guha[^19] His bosom friend, while Krishna, when the Creator praised Him with hymns and bowed to Him — alas, remained silent.[^20]

**King.** And then?

**Man.** By his command the boatmen, singing of the Lord, drove the boat so fast that in a single day we landed at the village of Panihati.

**King.** Who lives there?

**Sarvabhauma.** Raghava Pandita.

**Man.** What happened there is a wonder.

**King.** How so?

**Man.** Sire!

> **11.** The Lord had not yet reached the edge of the bank of the River of the gods when it all — alas, what is there to say! — became a crowd of people.
> Had the dust of the earth, ah, become people there? Or had the stars in human bodies fallen from the sky to the earth?

So the Lord with difficulty made His way from the bank of the Ganga to his house.

**King.** And then, and then?

**Man.** Spending that night there, the next day He moved on by the same waterway.

> **12.** And so the line of people became long like the Ganga: unbroken is its auspicious stream, there are no barriers to it, its waves are moving arms,
> ceaselessly it descends to the foot of Vishnu.[^21]

Then He came to Kumarahatta, to the house of Shrivasa Pandita. And there, when He was walking from the bank of the Ganga to the house —

> **13.** wherever the Lord stepped, there, to gather the dust of His feet, people's hands went down —
> and the road, alas, was all covered with pits.[^22]

As soon as the Lord landed there, Jagadananda, unnoticed by the Lord, went off to Shivananda's house. Since he had lived there a long time, out of attachment to that house he reasoned, "The Lord must be brought here", and arranged everything with special care.

And so —

> **14.** when people had filled the tops of the walls, all the branches of the trees, the ground, every street and every road,
> loudly crying, "Louder, louder say 'Hari'!" — the Lord at the end of the night, led by Shivananda, set out by boat.

Then Jagadananda, on both sides of the road all the way to Shivananda's house, adorned the way with special skill with plantain trunks, full pitchers, buds and rows of lamps. Setting foot on it, the Lord, with a smile on His sweetest lips, thought: "Jagadananda has arranged this." But seeing that on the left the road to Vasudeva's house was decorated in the same way, He hesitated: "Should I go on from here, or there?" And Vasudeva said: "Lord! First grace the house of Shivananda with Your presence." So He did: Jagadananda washed the lotuses of His feet, and He entered his house-temple. Then Jagadananda sprinkled a little of the water from His feet on the roof of the house, and part he himself distributed to the household in the inner apartments. Staying there a *muhūrta*, the Lord came to the house of Vasudeva, stayed there a moment and again boarded the boat. When the Lord set out, the people, to obtain the water of His feet, went into the water up to their necks, and, seeing their agitation, the Lord was filled with such compassion that all obtained the water of His feet. Then all the people moved along the bank, and no one turned back.

**King.** And then, and then?

**Man.** Then He arrived at Advaita's house, where Haridasa bowed to Him, and by the same way, by boat, He landed on the bank opposite Navadvipa, in the village of Kuliya, at the house of Madhava Dasa. Out of mercy to the people of Navadvipa He stayed there seven days. And in crossing from Navadvipa to the other bank —

> **15.** the ferry fee, which before had been only one *kākiṇī* from each person, gradually,
> even on the largest boats, became more and more — already in *kārṣāpaṇas*.[^23]

And further —

> **16.** the fence of the strongest thick bamboos that Madhava erected at night, by morning the crush of the throngs of people gradually turned to dust.
> So all seven days passed: such was the press of those longing to see Him that the Lord, though He is the Lord, could not Himself go to bathe in the River of the immortals.

Staying there seven days so, He again moved on by the road along the bank.

> **17.** Wherever the Lord was heading, there people, hearing of it beforehand, flocked together;
> and so the very earth here and there sagged greatly — astonishing especially the King of serpents.[^24]

And it is also heard: when the Lord was walking along the bank of the Ganga opposite the capital of the lord of Gauda, with throngs of people moving after Him on both sides, the lord of Gauda, seated in a pavilion pitched on the bank of the Ganga, saw it and asked in amazement: "What is this?" Then his minister named Keshava Vasu said: "Shuratrana,[^25] it is a certain great man named Sri Krishna Chaitanya going from Purushottama to Mathura. These people are moving, wishing to see Him." Then he said: "He who draws people so is God." And this too is heard: going a little further from there, He turned back and will go to Mathura not by that way but, coming to Purushottama, by the forest road. We do not know whether the report speaks the truth or not.

**Behind the scenes.** True, sirs, true!

> **18.** He suddenly came alone, saw the ornament of the Blue Mountain,
> and, for fear of the throngs of people, secretly went to Mathura by the forest road.

**King** *(with amazement)*. It sounds like the voice of Kashi Mishra. Call him.

**Kashi Mishra** *(entering hurriedly)*. Sire, here I am — come even uncalled.

**King.** Tell me how matters stand.

**Kashi Mishra.** It is true: for fear of the people He turned back from there. And from here He left unnoticed by anyone.

**King.** Sarvabhauma! For us it is one and the same road — for its fruit is one: the grief of separation. But how will He manage alone?

**Kashi Mishra.** Several brahmins worthy of giving Him alms have been sent. The Lord, however, does not know it.

**King.** Good, Mishra, good! Did He say anything as He was leaving?

**Kashi Mishra.** "Consider that I have already returned."

**King.** When will that day come? Bhattacharya! Let some runners[^26] be dispatched to bring news of the Lord.

**Sarvabhauma.** That is truly fitting.

**King.** Kashi Mishra! Convey my order to the mahapatra Chandaneshvara, that he do so.

**Kashi Mishra.** Sire! I have already informed Pariksha Mahapatra and have given orders for it myself.[^27] Some have almost returned already.

**Doorkeeper** *(entering)*. Sire! At the gate are messengers with news of Mahaprabhu.

**King.** Let them in.

*(The doorkeeper does so.)*

**Messengers** *(entering)*. Glory, glory to the King!

**King.** Tell what you know.

**Messengers.** We know everything.

**King.** Tell it.

**Messengers.**

> **19.** The Lord has returned from Madhupura, having beheld the beauty of Vrindavana, sporting in every grove along the banks of the Daughter of the Sun;
> having visited the best of mountains, Govardhana, and wandering, wandering from forest to forest for several days — He has been seen on the road.

**King.** Ho! And do you know what the Lord did in Vrindavana?

**Messengers.** Of course. We heard it from the lips of certain fortunate ones who came back together with the Lord.[^28]

**King.** Tell it.

**Messengers.**

> **20.** He, the single root of the whole multitude of His own blisses, from His very appearance, it is true, revealed all their manifestations ever more strongly;
> but when He came to Vrindavana — ah, these waves grew so high that neither word nor thought can reach beyond them.

For —

> **21.** now, gazing at the beauty of the forest borders by the Yamuna, the Lord of all, stretching out His longing arms,[^29]
> embraces every creeper, every tree, and weeps aloud with love, not restraining His voice.

And further —

> **22.** mad with joy at the sight of herds of beautiful cows, He, with streaming tears, fell —
> like a slope of Meru broken by the wind, down which the River of the gods flows.

**King.** And then, and then?

**Messengers.** Then they told:

> **23.** Suddenly catching sight somewhere of a lustre like the neck of a peacock intoxicated with joy,[^30]
> the Lord stumbles, rolls on the ground, trembles, cries out, melts, falls into despondency — ah! — and loses consciousness.

And further —

> **24.** Somewhere in the forest, seeing a running herd of calves with tails raised,
> the Lord at once rushes after them along a path strewn with thorns — and falls, His whole body wounded.[^31]

**King.** And then, and then?

**Messengers.** Then —

> **25.** when at times, losing consciousness by chance, He fell to the ground within the bounds of a grove,
> the deer licked the foam from His lips, and the birds drank the water of His eyes.

At times —

> **26.** the Lord fell even at the foot of Mount Govardhana, but, immersed in the very depths of the nectar ocean of love,
> though He was bruised, He knew no outward pain.[^32]

> **27.** When the Lord, gazing at every forest and every grove, wept aloud with love, not restraining His voice,
> the creepers and trees too seemed to weep, and the flocks of birds and beasts lost consciousness.

And further —

> **28.** When the Lord sobbed piteously — though His voice was firm and deep like the thunder of a cloud —
> somewhere the blue-throated peacocks, with tears in their throats, abandoning their dance, sobbed long after Him.[^33]

Seeing that from the excess of love He was so beside Himself and His body held together only by the power of His divinity, the pious Balabhadra Bhattacharya and others thought, "Ah, a calamity has happened!" — and as if by force led the Lord away from Vrindavana. That is why He stayed there only a short time. And then —

> **29.** him who even before, though tightly bound by the multitude of virtues dear to him, was free of attachment to home — like the supreme rasa, embodied and at the same time bodiless —
> that Sri Rupa together with Anupama the Lord favoured in Prayaga with conversations full of love and the rapture of the firmest embraces.[^34]

**King.** Sarvabhauma! Why did He, who so loves Vrindavana, stay there so short a time?

**Sarvabhauma.** It is as if Jagannatha, unable to bear separation from Him, drew Him back. And further —

> **30.** into Rupa — dear by his own nature, beloved by his own nature, love by his very nature, beautiful from birth,
> like unto Himself — the Lord, one in His form, placed the form of His own pastimes.[^35]

**King.** And then, and then?

**Messengers.** And then in Varanasi —

> **31.** in the house of a god of the earth[^36] known by the name of Chandrashekhara, the Lord of the world, the king of ascetics,
> then stayed — by the former accumulations of good deeds of the master of the house.

**King.** And then, and then?

**Messengers.** And then —

> **32.** Vishveshvara as it were commanded the whole world: "Go and see Him!" — commanded with love.
> Otherwise how would all people at one and the same time have done one and the same thing?

And further —

> **33.** to Him came brahmacharis, householders, mendicants and hermits, priests and keepers of vows;
> only a few envious heads of sannyasis did not go there — and did not see Him.

**King.** Sarvabhauma! Why is there such envy in such people?

**Sarvabhauma.** Maharaja! As long as the mind is not conquered, so long the venerable Lady Envy[^37] leaves no one.

**King.** And then, and then?

**Messengers.** And then —

> **34.** he who was the jewelled ornament of the assembly of the lord of Gauda — Rupa's elder brother — leaving his flourishing wealth, took as his wife the young Lakshmi of renunciation.
> His heart is full within of the rasa of bhakti, while outwardly he has the form of an avadhuta; like a great lake covered with duckweed, he gives joy to those who know his worth.

**King.** And then, and then?

**Messengers.**

> **35.** As soon as He saw with His own eyes Sanatana, who had come, He, golden like a champaka, boundlessly softened by compassion,
> compassionately embraced him with His arms, long as bolts.

**King.** How did their meeting come about?

**Messengers.** We heard it from his own lips.

**King.** Tell it.

**Messengers** *(conveying Sanatana's words)*.

> **36.** "Those who, led by eagerness alone, go first to the Lord come out from Him absorbed in the name of the Lord, in tears, with hair standing on end.
> But I stood outside, counting the series of those coming and going and taking up the dust of their feet — and the all-knowing Lord commanded someone to bring me."

**King.** And then, and then?

**Messengers.** And then, saying, "Not dear to Me is one who knows the four Vedas…" [*Itihāsa-samuccaya*][^38] and so on, He hurriedly came down and clasped him in His arms.

**King.** And then, and then?

**Messengers.** And then, there too, we heard a certain report.

**King.** What was it?

**Messengers.**

> **37.** Coming to Purushottama and spending some days there, gladdened by the Lord Himself,
> they will then, at His word, go to Vrindavana.

**King.** Together with the Lord, or afterwards?

**Messengers.** Afterwards, for it was seen that the Lord left Varanasi alone. And we heard something else there.

**Sarvabhauma.** What was it?

**Messengers.**

> **38.** Since in the course of time the accounts of the pastimes in Vrindavana had been lost, in order to proclaim them especially,
> the Lord there sprinkled with the nectar of mercy both Rupa and Sanatana.

**Ramananda.** That is quite fitting.

*(Behind the scenes, from afar, a loud noise: "Glory, glory!")*

**All** *(having listened)*. Oh! It seems the Lord Sri Krishna Chaitanya has come — for an unprecedented noise is heard.

**Again behind the scenes.**

> **39.** Today our birth has borne fruit, our eyes have reached their goal; every fever has at once subsided, the heart has found peace.
> What more is there to say? Look — for us this is a new birth: the king of ascetics has returned[^39] from Vrindavana to the Blue Mountain!

**King.** Why do you still delay? Let us go there. *(Exeunt all.)*

*(Enter Sri Krishna Chaitanya, and Paramananda Puri and Svarupa, who have met Him at this time; around them Kashi Mishra and others who have gathered to see Him.)*

**Sri Krishna Chaitanya** *(to the Puri master)*. Teacher!

> **40.** Although these two tirthas — the great souls and Kashi and the other holy places — are equal and equally wash away sins,
> yet bliss is bestowed, indeed, only by the great-souled — for to us happiness is the joy of seeing you.[^40]

Therefore association with such as you is more beautiful even than serving other tirthas — that is why I have come back so soon.

**Puri master.** Such is our good fortune, that in so long a time we were not burnt[^41] in the forest fire of separation from the Lord.

*(Hurriedly approaching, Sarvabhauma and Ramananda fall prostrate like rods; Sri Krishna Chaitanya embraces them.)*

**Kashi Mishra.** Master! After the offering of the "vallabha-bhoga"[^42] Sri Jagannatha, though He is about to rest, is waiting for the Lord. Hurry, then, to see Him.

**Sri Krishna Chaitanya.** Come, Puri master, come! *(Goes out together with everyone.)*

*(Exeunt all.)*

*Thus ends the ninth act of the* Sri Sri Chaitanya-chandrodaya, *called "The Journey to Mathura".*

[^14]: The source has *kṛta-suvacanam uccais tatra tathābhiyuktaiḥ*; the Kāvyamālā reading *kṛta-suracanam uccais tatra tatrābhiyuktaiḥ* ("carefully arranged everywhere by those appointed") is adopted.
[^15]: The stage direction *(ity aśrūṇi muñcati)* is found only in the Kāvyamālā.

[^24]: Shesha, the King of serpents, who holds the earth on his hoods.
[^25]: *Śūra-trāṇa*, a Sanskritisation of the word "sultan" (literally "protection of heroes"). The lord of Gauda is Sultan Husain Shah; Keshava Vasu is Keshava Khan, his official.

[^29]: *Lobhya-bāhuḥ* (so too in the Kāvyamālā), "with arms longing (for embrace)": the commentary in M. Guha's edition explains *ākāṅkṣita-bāhuḥ*, his translation has "embraces with eager impulse". *Anurakti-mukta-kaṇṭham*, literally "with a throat released by love", i.e. aloud.
[^30]: The source has *-kaṇṭha-kāṇḍa-dyutim*, "the lustre of the stalk of the neck"; the Kāvyamālā *-kaṇṭha-kānta-dyutim*, "the lovely lustre of the neck". The colour of a peacock's neck reminds Him of Krishna's dark colour.
[^31]: The source has *vīkṣitāṅgam*, a slip: M. Guha's edition reads *vikṣatāṅgam*, "with wounded body" (Bengali translation: "wounded His whole body"), and so it is translated. In the Kāvyamālā the end of the line is damaged.

[^33]: A pun: the Lord is "dark" like a cloud (by His inner nature Krishna), and the peacocks, which usually dance at the thunder of clouds, here abandon their dance and weep.

[^36]: *Kṣmāsura*, "a god on earth", i.e. a brahmin. Chandrashekhara is a scribe (a vaidya) in Varanasi, a friend of the Lord; here he is called a brahmin.
[^37]: *Bhagavatī matsaratā*, ironically "the venerable Lady Envy"; Kāvyamālā: *bhagavatī matsara-latā*, "the venerable creeper of envy".

[^39]: The source has *apagataḥ*, "went away (from Vrindavana)"; the Kāvyamālā *punar upagataḥ*, "has come again". The sense is the same.

[^42]: *Jagannātha-vallabhāvakāśa*, the interval after the "vallabha-bhoga", one of the daily food offerings to Jagannatha, before His midday rest; so Krishnadasa Baba's Hindi translation understands it ("after the vallabha-bhoga").
