# Act 2. The Vision of All the Avataras (Sarvāvatāra-darśana)

*Viraga, wandering over the earth seized by Kali, meets Bhakti-devi, who tells him of the deeds of Gaurachandra in Navadvipa; then the Lord reveals to Advaita the form of Shyamasundara.*

*(Enter Viraga.)*

**Viraga** *(looking all around)*. Ah, the world is full of those turned away from God![^1]

> **1.** No purity, no truthfulness, neither peace of mind nor control of the senses, no self-discipline either,
> no tranquillity, no forbearance — Shiva, Shiva! — neither friendliness (Maitri) nor compassion.
> Ah, these friends of mine, who love without any self-interest — have the people of Kali torn them up by the roots?
> Or do they live somewhere in secret, unrecognised by anyone?[^2]

Alas, how can one suppose that they live in secret? For there is no fit place for it. Look:

> **2.** The twice-born think only of the sixth of their duties, and their sole mark is the sacred thread;
> those born of the arms are distinguished by name alone, and the vaishyas are like Buddhists;
> the shudras, deeming themselves pandits, are eager to teach dharma as gurus —
> to such a state, alas, alas, has Kali brought the varnas![^3]

And further:

> **3.** Some here remain in the first ashrama only because they are unfit for marriage;
> the householders are devoted only to feeding wife, sons and their own belly;
> ah, the vanaprasthas are dear only to the ear — one only hears of them;
> and the wandering renunciants are recognised, at best, by their dress.[^4]

*(Walking a few steps.)* Ah, here is a fine region! Many scholars shine here, whose talent sparkles with swiftness.[^5] I must test them — whether they are outsiders[^6] or not. *(Observing for a long time.)* Ah!

> **4.** From their very birth they practise the string of words "upādhi", "jāti", "anumiti", "vyāpti" and the rest;
> they are endlessly, endlessly far from any talk of God.
> Whoever is the most skilful in inventions is, among them, the most learned;
> they take their own inventions for shastra — ah, these logicians![^7]

Enough of talk with them. I shall go from here to another place. *(Going some distance.)* Ah, these seem free of ailments[^8] and as if intent on welfare. Let me examine them. *(Does so.)* Ah, these are Mayavadis! Look:

> **5.** "We are pure being, without distinctions, consciousness free of limitations, beyond differentiation, without desire;
> we are Brahman alone" — with such words, Shiva, Shiva, they are at enmity with the form of God;
> rejecting all the attributes of God famed in the shruti — His inconceivable power and the rest —
> they cast away love (*rati*). Alas, obeisance to you — from afar![^9]

*(Leaving them, he again goes in another direction and looks about.)* Ah, these are disputing with one another; I must find out what the matter is. *(Gazing.)* Ah, these are experts in the doctrines of Kapila, Kanada, Patanjali and Jaimini, disputing with one another. None of them knows the truth about God. I must leave this place. *(Going a few more steps.)* Ah, I have strayed into the southern quarter![^10] For these are Arhatas, Saugatas and Kapalikas — fierce heretics. And these Pashupatas, cursed wretches,[^11] will even kill me. So the only refuge is to flee from here. *(Moving off, going some distance and noticing something.)* Ah, this must be a saint: comfortably seated on a flat stone that can be seen right by the river bank, free of suffering, he passes his time as if meditating on something beyond the gunas. Let me examine him. *(Does so.)* Ah!

> **6.** He displays great skill, blocking with the tip of his tongue the path of the nectar that flows from the moon in the forehead;
> closing his eyes and sitting in a posture, he meditates here on the river bank —
> why, then, has his samadhi been broken?

*(Reflecting with surprise.)* Ah, I understand!

> …Because he heard the jingle of the conch-shell bracelets of the young women who have come for water![^12]

So all this is merely a show for filling his belly. *(Again going in another direction and looking about.)* Ah, this one looks like a man without any possessions. He must be a pilgrim. I shall learn his heart from his talk — he is just conversing with a traveller who has happened by. *(Listening, he repeats it himself.)*

> **7.** "Gangadvara, Gaya, Prayaga, Mathura, Varanasi, Pushkara,
> Sri Ranga, Northern Koshala, Badarika, Setu, Prabhasa and the rest —
> going round this row of tirthas three or four times a year,
> how many hundreds of years I have spent so! Who can compare with the likes of us?"[^13]

*(Having repeated it.)* Excellent, sir, excellent! Truth, harassed by Kali, has found a safe hiding place in you![^14] Let us move away from here. *(Doing so and looking ahead.)* Ah, this ascetic, it seems, will prove worthy. Let me look closely. *(Does so.)* Alas, alas, this one is even worse than that! Look:

> **8.** Fiercely shouting "hum! hum! hum!" in a sharp, harsh voice, with a savage glance
> driving people far away, flinging his feet wide at every step,
> with clay on his forehead, arms, sides, neck, throat, belly and chest,[^15] his palm gleaming with kusha grass —
> who is this approaching: hypocrisy embodied, or pride?

Ah, here is a marvel now!

> **9.** Without unconditional devotion to Vishnu, firmness in concentration and meditation,
> skill in the labours of studying the shastras, in japa, austerity and rites —
> all these are only particular arts of superior dexterity, as with actors,[^16]
> various ways of filling the whirlpool of the pot of the belly.

Ah, Kali, well done! You have brought the whole earth under a single parasol.[^17] Look:

> **10.** Tranquillity, self-control and the rest are banished; and somewhere, tightly fettered,
> they are turned — alas! — into servants for earning money;
> the tree of dharma has been torn up completely, by the roots —
> and with it friendliness (Maitri) and the rest; what more could one wish?

*(Reflecting a moment.)* I am in great distress — from the pain of separation from such kinsmen, from weariness after searching for them so long, and from grief at seeing the earth become empty and worthless. I must rest somewhere, if only for a moment. *(Doing so, with tears.)*

> **11.** I have seen all this: everything is thrown into confusion by one discord alone — between mind and speech, between intention and act —
> from the exhaustion of the accumulated filth of Kali.
> When, alas, shall we see Vaishnavas who sing of Krishna and serve Him likewise,
> with tears, with hair standing on end — the same outside and inside?

*(Listening to the sky.)* What do you say? "Where bhakti is, there you must look for them"? *(Reflecting a moment.)* Ah, so Bhakti-devi is somewhere! *(Again fixing his gaze on the sky.)* What do you say, sir?

> **12.** "Glory to a certain land of Gauda, almost the crown of holy tirthas,
> which bears on itself the city named Sri Navadvipa.
> In it is the descent of the Lord who shines like a heap of gold,[^18]
> and there in every house[^19] Bhakti-devi stirs in bodily form."

*(Joyfully.)* Ah, ah! What will the living not see! I shall go from here straight there. *(Walks a few steps.)*

*(Then enters Bhakti-devi.)*

**Bhakti-devi** *(looking ahead)*. Oh, who is this? His mind is worn out by ceaseless heavy anxiety, his face has faded from the loss of honour and happiness; I seem to recognise him, and he, seeing me, falls into a state unlike the usual, shows joy and as if casts off his pain of himself — and is coming this way! Alas, now that all is well with us and we have found prosperity, the company of brother Viraga has been beyond our reach.[^20] I do not know whether he survived when misfortune overtook him from the wicked people of Kali, or not.

**Viraga** *(looking)*. Why, this is Bhakti-devi! For:

> **13.** She makes the heart serene, purifies the senses,
> makes liberation itself insignificant — what to speak of wealth and pleasures;
> as soon as she draws near, she at once brings living beings to their goal,
> plunging them into the depths of the ocean of bliss.

I shall approach her. *(Approaching.)* Goddess, I, Viraga, bow to you.

**Bhakti-devi** *(tenderly)*. Brother, you are alive! You are the life of the self-controlled.[^21] Come here, come! *(Touches him with her hand.)*

**Viraga** *(bowing at her feet)*. Goddess, tell me: how is it that Kali has not overcome you[^22] as he overcame Truth and the others?

**Bhakti-devi.** Viraga, do you not know? Listen. For our sake there has descended a certain greatly merciful Lord — Gaurachandra, whose deeds cut the bonds of worldly existence.

**Viraga.** Lady, tell me, what is the secret here?

**Bhakti-devi.** Viraga, in this age of Kali there is no other dharma — not even a particle of a bud;[^23] nothing here is lasting. Only the dharma of the Lord adorns this age of Kali and repels both bondage and delusion; therefore it alone is the true dharma, both the means and the goal. And so the Lord has made His descent in the form of a devotee, bringing with Him the yoga of pure bhakti — which carries away sins, crushes the filth of Kali and destroys, down to the chandala, the deep imprints of evil desires that even a chandala shuns[^24] — and together with it such as I, Bhakti-devi, with all our limbs and subsidiary limbs.[^25]

**Viraga.** That I have already understood from the heavenly voice, which spoke of it briefly. But:

> **14.** What do you desire, and what does that God desire?
> And will He become a refuge for me, who have no refuge?[^26]

**Bhakti-devi** *(changing to Sanskrit)*. Viraga, listen:

> **15.** We purify even a chandala, shake off every impurity,
> cut away in his heart even the firmest imprints of the past;
> and if the goddess Mercy (Kripa-devi) of this Lord casts her glance here,
> then we make grow in their hearts a certain indescribable experience of rasa as well.

**Viraga.** So without His goddess Mercy you yourselves have no such power?

**Bhakti-devi.** Without the ever-new mercy of Him or of His people we do not exist at all — what to speak of acting so!

**Viraga.** And what is the answer to the second question?

**Bhakti-devi.** Viraga, listen. *(In Sanskrit.)*

> **16.** Ah, there was no one in Navadvipa in whose house there was not a temple of Hari;
> there was no temple without a Deity of the Lord;
> there was no Deity that was not served; there was no service devoid of rasa;
> and there was no rasa in which sankirtana and dance were not the chief.[^27]

That is His desire.

**Viraga.** Does He Himself command it so, or do they themselves, grasping His intention, act so by their own understanding, in accordance with the essence?[^28]

**Bhakti-devi.** Such is His greatness: because of this greatness people, as soon as they see Him, become as if possessed — so that all know His intention of themselves and act accordingly. Together with His descent Kamala descended too:[^29] for no one there was poor. And what He Himself has been doing since His very childhood[^30] — listen.

> **17.** Now in the house of Shrivasa, now in the dwelling of Acharya-ratna,
> now in the abode of Sri Vidyanidhi, and now in the house of Murari —
> while His dear associates sing, God, filled with dense bliss,
> with trembling, stupor, tears, sweat and the rest, dances wildly day after day.

**Viraga.** Lady, does He always show only the conduct of a devotee? Or sometimes divine power as well?

**Bhakti-devi** *(in Sanskrit)*. Viraga, although

> **18.** this humanlike play of Hari is in a certain way more attractive
> than the superhuman —
> just as the Ganga, descending from the head of Mahesha into the midst of the earth,
> brings still greater joy[^31] —

yet sometimes He reveals the superhuman too. Thus, once in the abode of Shrivasa, when the Lord Vishvambhara was circumambulating the shrine, He was seen by a certain mleccha who was in the southern courtyard: by the will of fate he lived by the needle,[^32] a great drunkard and a sinner;[^33] he was sewing clothes for amusement and had been drunk for a very long time. Seeing the Lord, he was at once overwhelmed by the intoxication of this sight, which intoxicated more than the drunkenness of wine: his eyes opened wide — "hee-hee! I have seen, I have seen something!" — his whole body thrilled, his chest was drenched by unceasing streams of tears; throwing aside his clothes and the rest, he raised his arms and broke into a dance. Then, seeing him so, the Lord, concealing nothing, said to Shrivasa: "Shrivasa, what is this? How has he suddenly become like this, with a festival in his eyes?" And Shrivasa answered, as if giving an example of the rasa of humour: "Lord, unheard-of is the greatness of this intoxicant of Yours! For this man, though he is even now addicted to jugs of wine, never lost his senses from them; but from this glance at You — the size of a sesame seed — he has become sinless, has been freed from the torments born of ignorance,[^34] and, becoming a benefactor of others, is utterly intoxicated."

**Viraga.** And then, and then?

**Bhakti-devi.** And so from that time, taking refuge in the name of the Lord alone, he has left all his household, put on the dress of an avadhuta,[^35] and, dancing and singing, though he is beaten by the acharyas of the Yavanas,[^36] unfailingly performs the sankirtana of the Lord's name. And if anyone asks him, he answers: "Vishvambhara alone is God, and no one else." All the Bhagavatas take care of his food, and he wanders like a siddha.

**Viraga.** Goddess, in what form did he see Him?

**Bhakti-devi.** Viraga, the form of the Lord is bliss itself. That form in which the greatest bliss is born is His form. Thus bliss and forms depend on each other, and so the degrees of beholding the form correspond to the degrees of bliss.

**Viraga.** How did this man of low birth become the vessel of such happiness?

**Bhakti-devi** *(in Sanskrit)*.

> **19.** The mercy of Hari does not look to birth, character, ashrama, dharma, learning,
> lineage and the rest;
> it is self-willed — alas, it has no notion
> of the difference between the worthy and the unworthy.

**Viraga.** So it is. And then, and then?

**Bhakti-devi.** Then, on another day, in the courtyard of Murari's house, on a night bathed in the light of the full moon, all the Bhagavatas sitting there saw that same God in the form of Sankarshana.

**Viraga.** Goddess, tell it in more detail.

**Bhakti-devi.** Before them the sky was veiled in darkness: swarms of intoxicated bees, circling, were drinking the streams of moonlight, and the sky grew dark at that moment;[^37] and then all the people perceived a certain fragrance of *kādambarī*.[^38]

**Viraga.** And then, and then?

**Bhakti-devi.** And so, perceiving this fragrance, all asked the Lord: "Lord! How is it that this sudden, pure fragrance of *kādambarī*, able to blind the swarms of flower-drinkers, awakens amazement in us? And further on a plough appears, rousing curiosity, and a club that crushes the enemy's host!"[^39]

**Viraga.** And then, and then?

**Bhakti-devi.** Then Sri Vishvambhara-deva said: "Oh! Today the Lord, the protector Sankarshana, who draws all hearts to Himself, must appear.[^40] Here before Him have appeared the excellent *kādambarī*, dear to His friends, and His weapons, the plough and the club, gleaming even without a battle."

**Viraga.** And then, and then?

**Bhakti-devi.** And before He had finished saying this, that God — Gaurachandra Himself — with eyes rolling and reddened, joyful from the fragrance of the wine, with an earring dancing at the edge of one ear, pale as the orb of the moon, in the highest degree[^41] became Talanka[^42] Himself, sporting to His heart's content. Then they, singing ever new songs of His deeds, made Him dance and glorified Him long, and for a moment He threw everyone into amazement.[^43]

**Viraga.** And then, and then?

**Bhakti-devi.** So, having portrayed in turn the chief avataras — Buddha, Varaha, Nrisimha[^44] — after some days, out of causeless mercy, He revealed to Nityananda the six-armed form.

**Viraga.** What was it like?

**Bhakti-devi** *(in Sanskrit)*.

> **20.** With two hands He held a lovely flute,
> with four — the club, conch, disc and lotus;
> He wore a crown, necklaces, armlets,
> the Vaijayanti garland and the Kaustubha gem.

And further:

> **21.** Foremost in natural, not borrowed, beauty and sweetness,
> in great magnanimity, dexterity, depth and valour,
> in unshakeable steadfastness, in unchanging tenderness —
> that noble Fourth radiance[^45] was a wonder.

**Viraga.** And then, and then?

**Bhakti-devi.** Then, seeing this form, Nityananda, stunned with supreme bliss, in an armour of hair standing on end, offered praise for a long time. Thus:

> **22.** You are Hari, You are Hara, You are Virinchi,
> You are the waters, You are fire, You are the moon, You are the sun,
> You are the sky, You are the earth, You are the wind, O Murari;
> obeisance to You, obeisance — to the Lord of all!

And further:

> **23.** From these six arms someone calls You
> "the slayer of the six, fierce by nature";[^46]
> but we say: O magnanimous One, with them You
> bestow the four goals of life, bestow bhakti and bestow prema.

**Viraga.** Ah, this is truly wonderful! And then, and then?

**Bhakti-devi.** So I have told of His divine state. Now hear of His absorption in prema. In this city people are of three kinds: some are devoted to Him, others moderately devoted, and others not devoted but not hostile either. And so the students, coming out into the city street and seeing Him walking after bathing in the heavenly river,[^47] in His wet clothes — one recites to Him the sweetest name of the Lord, another a juicy verse of the *Bhāgavatam*, and another a graceful song full of love. And as soon as He hears it, He falls to the ground in the middle of the road; His whole body is covered with mud from falling on the ground soaked by the water running from His wet clothes; on His mud-covered chest tears trace a parting; and He rolls about as if bound by the creeper of blissful ecstasy,[^48] like a heap of garlands of lightning. Seeing Him so, His companions, guilty of frivolity, laugh and make merry. After a moment His household lift Him up, lead Him again to the heavenly river and bathe His whole body. And His mother, hearing of it, though versed in the rules of worldly life, grieves that her son is losing all control of Himself.[^49]

**Viraga.** Ah, He is truly in the power of the bliss of prema!

**Bhakti-devi.** On another day, in the rapture of great bliss like a thousand suns, He danced before the pearl of houses, the house of Acharya-ratna, and, returning home, was seen in the middle of the road by a certain unworthy brahmin[^50] — He, the Lord of His kinsmen, the God of His own people. Seeing Him, that man, all oozing with pus,[^51] seeing no other remedy, said in a piteous voice: "Ho, ho, son of Shachi, Vishvambhara! Everyone calls You the mighty Supreme Purusha. Much is told of the greatness of Your gait — now royal, like that of the best of elephants, now borne by a bird.[^52] If You free wretched me from this disease,[^53] then truly You are God, the lotus-eyed, the very blessing of the earth."

**Viraga.** And then, and then?

**Bhakti-devi.** Then the Lord, moved with compassion, said, addressing that wretched brahmin, whose body was shaking and covered with gooseflesh — one who had already fallen into the noose of death:[^54] "Ho, He who is God is unattainable for all. Why do you reproach Me? But there is one means to relieve your disease. If you carry it out, the disease will go. Of this there is no doubt. This body of yours may become even better than before."

**Viraga.** And then, and then?

**Bhakti-devi.** Then the unworthy brahmin, his eyes brightening, said: "Lord, what is this means?" Then the Lord said, as if setting an example: "Ho, unworthy twice-born! Of those who are devoted in love to the Lord, the friend of all the worlds, and are freed from a multitude of sins, the foremost is this Advaita Acharya. If you drink the water that has washed his feet, this disease caused by sin will go away, for its cause will vanish." Then the brahmin said: "It will go from the mere sight of You. Why then the special means You have spoken of?" And yet, when he did so, by his[^55] power his body became better than before.

**Viraga.** What wonder is that for the Lord! But tell me, where are you going and what do you seek, lady — why are you hurrying so, accompanied only by your own shadow?[^56]

**Bhakti-devi.** Noble one, my lord[^57] in the abode of Shrivasa is holding a certain secret conversation — without separation[^58] — with Advaita-deva, who has come. I am hurrying to follow Him.

**Viraga.** Lady, the answer to the third question remains; I am your disciple.[^59] So instruct me: will He become my refuge, will He become my protector?

[^1]: *Bahirmukha*, literally "with faces turned outwards", i.e. turned away from God, worldly.
[^2]: The virtues (purity, truthfulness, *śama*, *dama* and others) are conceived as friends and kin of Viraga; Maitri is an allegorical character of the drama. *Ajñāta-vasati*, "living unrecognised", like the Pandavas in the thirteenth year of their exile.
[^3]: Of the six duties of a brahmin (studying and teaching the Vedas, sacrificing for oneself and for others, giving and receiving gifts) the sixth is receiving gifts. "Those born of the arms" (*bhuja-bhuvaḥ*) are the kshatriyas, according to the hymn Purusha-sukta. The vaishyas are "like Buddhists", i.e. they reject the Vedas (there were many Jains and Buddhists among the merchants).
[^4]: The vanaprasthas are "dear only to the path of hearing" (*śravaṇa-patha-mātra-praṇayinaḥ*): one only hears of them; in reality they do not exist.
[^5]: A play on words: *bhūrayo rayojjvala-pratibhāḥ pratibhānti*.
[^6]: *Bāhya*, "outsiders", i.e. strangers to bhakti (cf. *bahirmukha* above). Another assonance: *avagāhyā bāhyā abāhyā*.
[^7]: Terms of Navya-nyaya: *upādhi*, a limiting condition; *jāti*, a generic class; *anumiti*, inference; *vyāpti*, invariable concomitance. The famous school of logic of Navadvipa is meant.
[^8]: The assonance *amī amīva-rahitāḥ hitābhiyogāḥ* ("these, free of ailments, intent on welfare").
[^9]: The Mayavadis are followers of Advaita Vedanta, who hold Brahman to be devoid of attributes (*nirviśeṣa*). "Obeisance to you" is an ironic bow from afar, as to something one wishes to keep away from. The electronic text has *hanti* ("kills"), the Kāvyamālā *hanta* ("alas"); the latter is followed.
[^10]: The south is the quarter of Yama, the god of death; at the same time the Jains (Arhatas) and Shaiva sects were strong in South India. The Saugatas are Buddhists; the Kapalikas and Pashupatas are Shaiva ascetics.
[^11]: *Hatāyuṣaḥ*, literally "whose life is ruined" (an abusive "accursed").
[^12]: The *khecarī-mudrā* of hatha-yoga is described: the yogi turns his tongue back to the palate, retaining the "nectar of the moon" which, according to the yogis, trickles down from the region of the forehead. The verse is broken by a stage direction; the last line is the solution. *Śaṅkha*: the conch-shell bracelets worn by Bengali women.
[^13]: Gangadvara is Haridvar; Northern Koshala is Ayodhya; Setu is Rameshvaram. The last words (*asmādṛśān etu kaḥ*) can also be understood as "who will come up to the likes of us?". The pilgrim's boasting is implausible: "hundreds of years" is an obvious lie, hence Viraga's irony.
[^14]: Irony: truth has "hidden" with a liar, i.e. it is not in him at all.
[^15]: The electronic text has the corrupt *dos taṭa*; the reading by the sense is followed: "arms (*dos*), sides (*taṭa*)". Clay (tilaka) is applied to twelve parts of the body.
[^16]: Electronic text: *śailūṣāṇām* ("of actors"); Kāvyamālā: *śeṣāṇām*, with the editor's gloss "i.e. *naṭānām*" (of actors). The sense is the same.
[^17]: The "single parasol" is the sign of a sole sovereign: Kali has become the absolute master of the world.
[^18]: The electronic text is corrupt (*cāmīkara-camaru-cerīśvarasya*); the Kāvyamālā reading *cāmīkara-caya-ruceḥ īśvarasya*, "of the Lord with the lustre of a heap of gold", is followed.
[^19]: *Puri puri*, "in every town" or "in every house"; in Bengali Sanskrit *pura* often means "house".
[^20]: A play on words: *saṅgo saṅgocaro na bhavati*. Bhakti-devi is not yet sure that it is Viraga before her: renunciation has lost its strength in the age of Kali.
[^21]: *Vaśinām*, "of the self-controlled": renunciation is the life of ascetics.
[^22]: Bhakti-devi together with her "sisters". Viraga compares her with the banished virtues (Truth and the others, see v. 1). The electronic text has *vayaṁ satyādaya iva* ("we, like Truth"); the Kāvyamālā reading *kathaṁ satyādaya iva* is followed.
[^23]: A play on words: *kali-kāle kalikā-leśa-mātram*, "in the age of Kali — not even a particle of a bud (*kalikā*)".
[^24]: A difficult passage (*ā-caṇḍālaṁ caṇḍāla-laṅghanīya-durvāsanā-vāsanā-nāśena*); we understand: bhakti destroys even in chandalas those evil tendencies which are unworthy even of a chandala. The word of the *chāyā* *ena-sāmavāharakeṇa* is read as *enasām apahārakeṇa*, "which carries away sins".
[^25]: *Sāṅgopāṅgā*, "with limbs and subsidiary limbs": cf. *Bhāg.* 11.5.32 on the God of the age of Kali, who appears *sāṅgopāṅgāstra-pārṣadam*, "with limbs, subsidiary limbs, weapons and associates".
[^26]: Viraga's three questions, which Bhakti-devi then answers in turn. The electronic text has *bhavatāśayaḥ*; the Kāvyamālā reading *bhavitāśrayaḥ* is followed.
[^27]: The verse is built in the figure "chain" (*ekāvalī*), as the Kāvyamālā editor notes. The electronic text has *sajano*; *sa jano* is read.
[^28]: The assonance *maty-anusāreṇa sāreṇa*. The Kāvyamālā proposes instead of *te ta eva* the reading "*tattvataḥ*" ("in truth").
[^29]: Kamala (Lakshmi), the goddess of prosperity.
[^30]: *Ā-śiśu-kālam*, "from infancy"; in the Kāvyamālā the verse is preceded by the stage direction "(changing to Sanskrit)".
[^31]: An example (*dṛṣṭānta*): the Ganga flowing from Shiva's head is holy, but the Ganga flowing over the earth gladdens people more. So too the Lord's "earthly" play is sweeter than the divine.
[^32]: *Bhāgadheya-sūci-vṛtti*, i.e. a tailor. The Muslim tailor who, seeing the Lord, went mad with love is a well-known episode from the life of Mahaprabhu.
[^33]: Electronic text: *mahā-madyapena amalecchena mlecchena*; Kāvyamālā: *mahā-madyapena amalākṣeṇa mlecchena*, Prakrit *abhakkheṇa*. M. Guha's Bengali translation renders the word as "sinful" ("a great drunkard, a sinner"); it is translated accordingly. The Kāvyamālā *chāyā* *amalākṣeṇa* ("clear-eyed") and the Prakrit *abhakkheṇa* (probably "eating forbidden food") remain unclear; Krishnadasa Baba's Hindi translation omits the word.
[^34]: *Jāto 'jāto-dara-rahito hito bhavann atimamāda*: *dara* is "the torment (thorn) of ignorance" (so both the Kāvyamālā and M. Guha's commentary gloss it: "free of the suffering born of ignorance"); *hita*, "beneficial to others": Guha's Bengali translation has "became a benefactor of others", Krishnadasa Baba's Hindi translation "a benefactor of the world"; the Kāvyamālā "beneficial to both high and low". *Anenā*, "sinless" (*an-enas*), with the assonance *anenānenā*.
[^35]: An avadhuta is an ascetic who has cast off all outward rules.
[^36]: "The acharyas of the Yavanas" are Muslim clerics and judges (qazis). Kāvyamālā: singular *yavanācāryeṇa*.
[^37]: The bees swarm so thickly, "drinking" the moonlight, that the sky grows dark. The bees were drawn by the scent of wine, a forerunner of the appearance of Balarama.
[^38]: *Kādambarī*, an intoxicating drink dear to Balarama (Sankarshana); the Kāvyamālā gloss: "the smell of wine".
[^39]: The plough (*hala*) and the club (*muṣala*) are the weapons of Balarama. A play on words: *kautūhalaṁ halam*. The words "that crushes the enemy's host" and the second *muṣalam* follow the Kāvyamālā (*vipakṣa-pakṣa-muṣalaṁ muṣalam*); they have dropped out of the electronic text.
[^40]: A pun on the name: *saṅkarṣaṇa*, "the one who draws, attracts"; *bhagavatāvatā* = *bhagavatā avatā*, "by the Lord, the protector".
[^41]: Prakrit *aisaaṁ* = *atiśayam*, "in the highest degree"; the *chāyā* in both editions gives *aviṣayam* ("inconceivably", "beyond perception").
[^42]: Talanka, "marked with the palm" (on His banner), a name of Balarama.
[^43]: The Prakrit of the electronic text *bikkhaṇāṇaṁ bimhaakāri*, "caused amazement among the discerning (*vicakṣaṇānām*)"; the Kāvyamālā and the *chāyā*: *kṣaṇaṁ*, "for a moment". The latter is followed.
[^44]: The Kāvyamālā gloss: Buddha is the ninth, Varaha the third, Nrisimha the fourth of the ten avataras.
[^45]: *Turya*, "the fourth", transcending the three states of consciousness (waking, dream and deep sleep) or the three gunas.
[^46]: "The six enemies" (*ṣaḍ-varga*): lust, anger, greed, delusion, pride and envy. Nityananda gives his own "etymology" of the six arms: the four goals of life (dharma, artha, kama, moksha) + bhakti + prema = six gifts.
[^47]: The heavenly river is the Ganga. *Ātmānam avatārya* (Kāvyamālā: *uttārya*), literally "bringing Himself down" into the street; we understand "coming out".
[^48]: A play on words: *vihvalatayā latayā*, "by the creeper of ecstasy".
[^49]: In the electronic text (*nija-tanayasya nayasya varivasitam anubhāvyate*) and in the Kāvyamālā (*…anubhāvya*, where the sentence breaks off) the sense is unclear. The reading of M. Guha's edition is followed: *nija-tanayasya nayasya vaivaśyam anutapati*, "grieves at the helplessness (self-forgetfulness) of her son"; so both Guha and Krishnadasa Baba's Hindi translation render it ("though prudent, she weeps in distress"). Viraga's reply takes it up ("blissful self-forgetfulness", *vaivaśya*). A play on words: *tanayasya nayasya*, *jananī jana-nīti*.
[^50]: *Brāhmaṇa-bandhu*, "a kinsman of a brahmin", i.e. a brahmin by birth alone. A play on words: *bandhunā bandhu-nāthaḥ*.
[^51]: *Sarvāṅga-galana-siktaḥ*, literally "drenched with the discharges of his whole body": the brahmin is suffering from leprosy. Cf. the story of the leper brahmin who offended Shrivasa (*Chaitanya-charitamrita*, Adi 17).
[^52]: The Kāvyamālā gloss analyses *danti-vara-vikrama-vikrama*: "might like that of the best of elephants (Airavata)" and "a gait (*krama*) on a bird (*vi*, Garuda)", i.e. the greatness of Vishnu. An allusion to Trivikrama is also possible.
[^53]: Electronic text: *gato gato* ("the going will go"); Kāvyamālā: *gado gataḥ* ("the disease is removed"). The latter is followed: so read both M. Guha's edition (*eṣa gado gataḥ kriyate*, "if this disease of mine is removed") and his Bengali translation; the play *gado gataḥ* is intended.
[^54]: A play on words: *brāhmaṇa-pāśaṁ pāśaṁ mṛtyoḥ*, "the wretched brahmin, (caught in) the noose of death". By the grammar the trembling and gooseflesh belong to the brahmin, but they may also imply the ecstasy of the Lord Himself.
[^55]: That is, by Advaita's power (or that of the remedy).
[^56]: A play on words: *gacchantīcchantī*; *carīkarti tvarām* is an intensive form, "you hurry so".
[^57]: *Ārya-putra*: so in a drama a wife addresses her husband; Bhakti-devi sees in the Lord her husband.
[^58]: A play on words: *nirviraha-rahasya*.
[^59]: A play on words: *avaśiṣyate śiṣyate 'yam*, "there remains… — this one (I) is instructed".
