# Act 8. Mercy to Prataparudra (Pratāparudrānugraha)[^1]

*The Lord settles in Puri with Kashi Mishra; Paramananda Puri, Svarupa Damodara, Govinda and Brahmananda Bharati come to Him; He refuses to see the King; the Gaudiya devotees headed by Advaita arrive; at the chariot festival King Prataparudra in simple dress clasps the Lord's feet.*

*(Enter Sri Krishna Chaitanya, accompanied by Sarvabhauma and others, and also by Damodara, Jagadananda and others.)*[^2]

**Sri Krishna Chaitanya.** Sarvabhauma! I have gone round so many lands — and have met no one like you. Only Raya Ramananda. But he is truly not of this world.

**Sarvabhauma.** Master! That is why I told You that he must certainly be seen.

**Sri Krishna Chaitanya.** I have seen many Vaishnavas, but they too are merely worshippers of Narayana. Others are Tattvavadis, and they are of the same kind: their doctrine is not flawless.[^3] Many others are Shaivas. And the heretics, the pashandas, are a great multitude, and very strong. But, Bhattacharya, only the doctrine of Ramananda has pleased My heart.

**Sarvabhauma.** He has only entered into Your own doctrine; he is not himself the creator of a doctrine. Master! From now on for us too this doctrine is the most honoured, and it is established by all the scriptures.

**Gopinatha Acharya.** Bhattacharya! Has thought been given to the place where the Lord will live?

**Bhattacharya.** Acharya! The King himself has thought of that.

**Gopinatha Acharya** *(softly, to him alone)*. And what place is that?

**Bhattacharya.** The house of Kashi Mishra.

**Gopinatha Acharya.** Splendid, splendid! It is near the Lion Gate; from there it will be easy to go for the darshan of Jagannatha.

*(With these words they enter the settlement of Purushottama from the seashore. Then enter the pashupalas — servants of the god Jagannatha — with garlands of the prasada of the Lord Jagannatha in their hands, and also Kashi Mishra and Pariksha Mahapatra.)*[^4]

**Sarvabhauma.** Here He is, here He is — the master, Sri Krishna Chaitanya! *(All approach eagerly.)* Master! These are the pashupalas of the Lord. This is the one called Kashi Mishra. And this is the chief superintendent of everything, the judge at the Lord's court.

*(Kashi Mishra and Pariksha Mahapatra, approaching, fall prostrate like rods. The pashupalas put a garland round His neck and bow.)*

**Bhagavan.** Ah, what is this? You are intimates of the Lord, and it is for Me to honour you. Why do you do what is not fitting? *(Bows to all and embraces them.)*

**Pashupalas.** Sarvabhauma! Now it is the time of the Lord's midday sleep. Should the master go straight there and wait, or rest, bathe and so on, and then come?

**Sarvabhauma.** Let Him bathe, and then visit the god.

**Kashi Mishra.** Then let them come this way. *(Leads them to his house.)*

**Sarvabhauma.** Master! This is the Mishra's own dwelling; it has been cleaned and prepared for Your holy feet. So let the Lord enter here.

*(They act entering. The pashupalas and the others bow and go out. Then enter several notable persons from among the people of Utkala.)*

**The people of Utkala.**

> **1.** Then such good fortune did not fall to us, and so the supreme Compassion did not come onto the path of our eyes.
> But now our luck has come together: ah, we catch with our gazes this very Lord of the Blue Mountain — moving![^5]

*(With these words they approach.)*

**Sarvabhauma.** Master! This is Janardana, a close servant who serves the Lord's body during the *anavasara*.[^6] This is Krishnadasa, an intimate who bears the golden staff. This is Shikhi Mahiti, in charge of the records, and these are his two brothers. This is the one called Dasa Mahasoara, in charge of the kitchen.[^7] All of them are natural devotees of the god Sri Jagannatha. These are Chandaneshvara, Murari and Hamseshvara,[^8] the best of brahmins, royal mahapatras, Vaishnavas by nature. This is Praharajya Mahapatra,[^9] a very great devotee of the Lord. This is Pradyumna Mishra, this is Vishnudasa. These are the brothers of Raya Ramananda, and among them, here, is Vaninatha Pattanayaka. And this is their father, Bhavananda Raya. And all these others, people of Gauda and Utkala, whose minds are in You and whose life is in You, fall prostrate like rods. Favour them with Your mercy as Your own.

**Bhagavan.** They are My very self; how could they be merely "Mine"? All the more the servants of Jagannatha.

**Sarvabhauma.** Master! In Jagannatha and in You the being of Krishna-Chaitanya is one and the same, without difference; and yet there is a certain distinction: He is Brahman in wood, and You are Brahman in a man.

**Bhagavan** *(covering His ears)*.

> **2.** This exaggeration of yours, Sarvabhauma, grates harshly on the ear:
> for if the juice of molasses is boiled down too much, it turns bitter and does not set.[^10]

**Sarvabhauma.** Master! The juice of the land of Gauda, however long it is boiled, is always sweet — for the Lord appeared there.

**Sri Krishna Chaitanya.** Enough, enough. Now it is the time for the darshan of Sri Pundarikaksha.[^11]

**All.** So be it. Let the Lord go ahead. *(Letting the Lord go first, they go round the stage.)*

**Behind the scenes.**

> **3.** Here ahead is the elixir of the eyes, the lord of Paramananda-puri:
> holding the staff to chastise those who heap up sins, he comes out at his time.[^12]

**All** *(having listened)*. Oh, it is the time of the god's coming out, since it is being announced.

**Sri Krishna Chaitanya** *(to Himself)*. Ah, Paramananda Puri the master is a disciple of the lord of sages Madhavendra Puri, into whom, they say, all the divine power of My elder brother Vishvarupa entered.[^13] Has he himself not come? Well, after seeing the god, I shall learn the truth of this.

**Sarvabhauma.** Here before us is the Lord's Paramananda-puri, the City of Supreme Bliss. Enter it, then.

*(All go out with the Lord to see Sri Jagannatha. Then enters Paramananda Puri.)*

**Paramananda Puri** *(eagerly)*.

> **4.** "When shall I see the Lord who has taken the body of a devotee?" — ah, by this ripe thirst my mind is stirred.
> After long waiting, today the season of fruit has come for me; but I do not know what fruit the tree of my fate will bear.

*(Going round the stage.)* Lord Sri Jagannatha, forgive me! That I go to Him first without looking at You — You, the all-knowing, know Yourself what this thirst is.

*(Looking around again.)* Ah, that Lord must be somewhere here, for —

> **5.** Even from the gate of Jagannatha a crowd of people flows here with loud noise and does not go back.
> Therefore I think: O blessed Earth, by your merit that very golden-shining God has appeared here — He will be here.

So let me approach. *(Approaches.)*

*(Then enters Sri Krishna Chaitanya with His companions, motionless with the bliss of the darshan of Sri Jagannatha.)*

**Sri Krishna Chaitanya** *(acting as if remembering)*. Ah, it seems Paramananda Puri the master will come now, for —

> **6.** Following the happiness of beholding the Lord, some further happiness must come now:
> a sudden clarity of mind announces a joy near at hand.

*(Stands in eager expectation.)*

**Puri** *(looking ahead)*. Ah, here He is —

> **7.** Glory to Him — motionless, without a single wave, from the rapture of relishing the rasa of beholding the Moon of the Blue Mountain, which He has drawn into Himself;
> down His chest, gleaming like a slab of gold and jewels, tears flow, and the hair on His body ceaselessly stands on end!

**Sri Krishna Chaitanya** *(looking ahead)*. It is he — here is the cause of this sudden joy: he has come here. *(Rises and bows.)* Svamin! Are you the Puri master?

**Puri** *(in confusion)*. Master! I have come from Varanasi, pining only with the desire to see You.

*(Approaches.)*[^14]

**Sri Krishna Chaitanya.** Svamin, this way, this way. *(Offers him rest.)*

**Sarvabhauma.** O svamin! There is nothing surprising in this.

> **8.** Whatever rivers there are on earth — masculine or feminine — that carry their currents,
> not one of them anywhere finds rest and refuge except in the ocean, the treasury of pearls.[^15]

**Behind the scenes.**

> **9.** Ah, as if to become a teacher of rasa for the Lord, the knower of the arts of rasa, a body in the garb of a mendicant monk has taken form —
> for throughout the earth everyone calls him "Damodara-svarupa", that is, "the very form of Damodara", for love inseparable from Him.[^16]

**Sarvabhauma.** Ah, what natural love (*rati*) the people here have for the Lord! Even in His absence all sing of His divinity.

**Sri Krishna Chaitanya** *(acting as if listening)*. Ah, I heard the name "Damodara-svarupa". It seems he too will soon come.

**Sarvabhauma.** Svamin! As a rule, when You descend, all Your associates descend — some before, others after. In due time all must surely meet. That is why it was said: "that carry their currents".

*(Enter Damodara-svarupa.)*[^17]

**Damodara-svarupa** *(fixing his gaze on the sky)*.

> **10.** O Sri Chaitanya, treasury of mercy! May Your mercy increase beyond measure —
> playfully shaking off grief, clear, revealing fragrance,
> stilling the disputes of the scriptures, bestowing rasa, instilling madness in the heart,
> ever finding joy in bhakti, intoxicating — the limit of sweetness![^18]

*(With these words he approaches.)*

**Gopinatha Acharya** *(looking ahead, to himself)*. Ah, I have heard of a certain Damodara-svarupa, a disciple of Chaitanyananda — a very great renunciant, a devotee of the Lord, very learned — who, though his guru asked him many times, "Study Vedanta and teach it", never did so. On the contrary —

> **11.** He accepted the fourth ashrama only from the longing to give up everything;
> but out of passion for the pollen of the lotus feet of Sri Krishna, ah, he held even that as nothing, though he wore it.[^19]

That is he. I shall report to the Lord. *(Approaches.)* Master! Here, here is that Damodara-svarupa of whom You have heard.

**Bhagavan.** Where is he? Where is he? *(Eagerly goes to meet him.)*

*(Damodara-svarupa approaches and bows at His feet. Sri Krishna Chaitanya raises him with His hands and embraces him.)*

**Behind the scenes.**

> **12.** Devoted to the service of Ishvara Puri, and in himself a devotee of Krishna,
> here comes this man of pure heart, detached from all objects of the senses.

**Sarvabhauma** *(having listened)*. Oh, some attendant is coming from the Lord's town. Who can it be?[^20]

**Sri Krishna Chaitanya.** Ah, it seems someone is coming from the Puri master.[^21]

**Sarvabhauma** *(looking)*. We shall soon know. *(Gazes.)*

*(Then enters one named Govinda.)*

**Govinda** *(to himself)*. Ishvara Puri sent me: "Go straight to Mahaprabhu." Here I am close to Him, and yet I do not grasp the greatness of my good fortune. *(Approaches, bows and joins his palms.)* Svamin!

> **13.** "That form of His which I saw first — ah, in that alone is my bliss; in another garb I do not wish to see Him. So go to Him" —
> so the Puri master sent me to the border of Your feet.[^22]

**Sri Krishna Chaitanya.** So it is: their love for Me is unchanging.

**Sarvabhauma.** You are his servant?

**Govinda.** Of course.

**Sarvabhauma.** Svamin! How did he accept a non-brahmin as a servant?

**Sri Krishna Chaitanya.** Bhattacharya, do not speak so.

> **14.** Hari is independent, and so is His mercy: it looks neither to caste nor to lineage and the like.
> Rejecting the food of Suyodhana, God joyfully accepted only the food of Vidura.[^23]

**Sarvabhauma.** Master, so it is.

**Sri Krishna Chaitanya.** Well, although it is not fitting to make the servant of one's venerable elders serve oneself, since such is his command, so be it. *(Accepts him.)*

**Mukunda** *(entering hurriedly)*. Svamin! Brahmananda Bharati has come, wishing to see You. If You command, we shall bring him straight here.[^24]

**Sri Krishna Chaitanya.** God forbid! For they are venerable. So it is I who must go.

**All.**

> **15.** Even the worldly conduct of the transcendental surely serves to reveal their transcendental nature:
> for the course of Vishnupadi over the earth leads the embodied to heaven.[^25]

*(Letting the Lord go first, they go round the stage. Then enters Brahmananda, dressed in a skin.)*

**Brahmananda** *(looking ahead)*. Here He is, Sri Krishna Chaitanya. And truly —

> **16.** With arms long, long like golden bolts, shining like a petal of a bright golden ketaka,
> whose lustre is caressed by a garland of fresh *damanaka*, with a charming gait — He comes out to meet me.[^26]

**Sri Krishna Chaitanya** *(approaches and, though He sees him, concealing His feelings, lets it be understood that the garment of skin does not please Him)*. Mukunda, where is he?

**Mukunda.** Why, here he is, here.

**Sri Krishna Chaitanya.** No, no. If it were he, would he, putting on a skin, have become a slave of outward garb?[^27]

**Brahmananda** *(hearing, to himself)*. Ah, evidently it does not please Him that I am dressed in a skin. And indeed —

> **17.** A garment of skin and the like serves only to display hypocrisy, not as a means of reaching the Real.
> One who walks the earth by the straight path easily reaches the goal he strives for.

What is this for? *(Wants to throw off the skin.)*

*(Sri Krishna Chaitanya glances at Damodara. Damodara, understanding the hint, quickly offers an upper cloth. Brahmananda puts it on. Sri Krishna Chaitanya approaches and bows to him.)*

**Brahmananda** *(with fear and reverence)*. Svamin! Though for the instruction of people it befits You to act so, to us it gives great fear. Do not do it again. Look, look —

> **18.** The greatness of Nilachala is beyond the power of such as I to describe — so transcendental is it:
> for here now two Brahmans appear — the moving and the unmoving, the golden and the blue.

**Sri Krishna Chaitanya.** Since the word "now" denotes the present time, the "golden Brahman" is just you, who have recently come; besides, the word "brahma" forms part of your name.[^28]

**Brahmananda.** That is inferred from the relation of the pervaded and the pervader. And that I am the pervaded, let it be seen at least from my having left the skin at Your sign.[^29]

**Sarvabhauma.** The holy man speaks rightly.

**Brahmananda.** Sarvabhauma, look, look —

> **19.** "Golden in colour, with a body of gold, with beautiful limbs, wearing sandal armlets" —
> these names have attained their literal sense in Him alone.[^30]

And it is the Lord Sri Jagannatha Himself who makes Him "wearing sandal armlets" — with a cord anointed with the sandal of His prasada. Look, look: even a mere Deity of the Lord bestows supreme bliss — what then to speak of Bhagavan Sri Krishna Himself! Ah, a wonder —

> **20.** Ah, the form — dense bliss and consciousness — the sole means of tasting bliss: in those who gaze at it, it stills the agitation of the outer and inner senses.
> And those who, rejecting it, meditate in the heart on the formless for the sake of attaining bliss — I think, ah, some mighty evil tendency leads them astray.

And further —

> **21.** If the essence of the Lord is formlessness, why then, ah, are pride, envy and the rest, also formless, not counted as deity?
> There is no rule that He is necessarily with form or without form; but supreme bliss, and that from which it comes — that is the Lord: such is my view.

**Sarvabhauma.** Svamin, so it is. This is explained also at the sutra "Made of bliss, on account of repetition".[^31] He Himself is bliss and bestows bliss on others too, as one very rich gives wealth to others as well: the suffix *-maya* here denotes abundance. And if His mercy comes, then even from meditation on the formless one falls again into the sweetness of the divine form. As the knowing have said:

> **22.** We, who were worshipped by travellers on the path of non-duality, who were consecrated on the throne of the bliss of our own self,
> have been forcibly turned into slave-girls by a certain cheat — the lover of the cowherds' wives.[^32]

**Damodara** *(to Brahmananda)*. Holy one, I have invited you. So go now to what is to be done next.

**Sri Krishna Chaitanya.** Svamin, that is fitting.

**Brahmananda.** As you please. *(Goes out together with Damodara and several others.)*

**Sri Krishna Chaitanya.** Sarvabhauma! It is time for you too to go.

**Sarvabhauma.** Master, I must report something.

**Sri Krishna Chaitanya.** What is it?

**Sarvabhauma.** Svamin, if You promise not to be angry, then I shall report it.

**Sri Krishna Chaitanya.** Speak without fear.

**Sarvabhauma.** The King is pining with the desire to see Your holy feet. If You allow it, we shall bring him.

**Sri Krishna Chaitanya** *(covering His ears)*. Sarvabhauma, you too say this!

> **23.** For one who has nothing, who is intent on the service of the Lord and wishes to reach the far shore of the ocean of existence,
> to see people devoted to the senses, and women — alas, alas! — is worse than eating poison.[^33]

**Sarvabhauma.** Svamin! That is truly so; but he is a servant of Jagannatha.

**Sri Krishna Chaitanya.**

> **24.** One should fear even the form of women and of people devoted to the senses:
> as a serpent agitates the mind, so does its image too.

If this is spoken of once more, I shall no longer be seen here.

*(Sarvabhauma stands silent.)*

**Sri Krishna Chaitanya.** Bhattacharya, it is already very late.

**Sarvabhauma.** As Svami pleases. *(Goes out.)*

**Sri Krishna Chaitanya.** Mukunda, where did Shripada Nityananda go when I went to the south?

**Mukunda.** To Gauda. And he said this: "Reckoning the time of the Lord's return, I shall come here again with everyone, headed by Advaita."

**Gopinatha Acharya.** Now there is neither war between two kingdoms nor other troubles;[^34] the road is easy, and the Gundicha festival is near — everything is ready for their coming. Only — has the news of Svami's return reached so far? But enough of doubts.

> **25.** Who in the world spreads the news of the sun, risen and dispelling the darkness with its rays, or of the moon?
> Such is the way of a thing that transcends the world: it reveals itself everywhere of itself.[^35]

So, svamin, it is the time of the evening incense offering (*dhūpa*) of the god Jagannatha, if You allow… *(Without finishing, he acts shyness.)*

**Sri Krishna Chaitanya.** Acharya, go and see the dhupa. And I shall go to converse with the Puri master and Svarupa. *(Bhagavan goes out.)*

**Gopinatha Acharya.** Ah, the Lord has already gone. Then I too, having seen the dhupa, shall join Him there again. *(Takes a few steps.)*

**Behind the scenes.**

> **26.** The victorious drive of the chariot of the Lord of all is near — and here the ruler of the earth, Prataparudra, has arrived.
> Let me once more make efforts that he may see with his own eyes the bull among ascetics in His golden form.

**Gopinatha Acharya.** Oh, the Bhattacharya's voice is heard; so the ruler of the earth, the Gajapati, has arrived. And the great chariot festival is already near. So I shall go and look at Jagannatha and come back. *(Goes out.)*

*(Then enters the Bhattacharya.)*

**Bhattacharya.** Since the Gajapati summoned me the very moment he arrived, he must be pining to see Sri Krishna Chaitanya. *(Goes round the stage and looks.)* Oh, here is the King. Let me approach. *(Approaches.)*

*(Then enter the King, seated on the throne, and the mahapatras.)*

**King** *(longingly, to himself)*.

> **27.** My cares for the kingdom have ceased to be my cares, the enjoyment of happiness has become a disease.
> If He does not look at me hereafter — alas, I shall not keep even my life.[^36]

**Sarvabhauma.** It seems the great King is troubled in his heart by something: I have approached, and he does not notice me. I shall announce myself. Victory, victory to the great King!

**King** *(acting as if coming to himself)*. What, is it you, Bhattacharya! Come near, come near! *(Bows.)*

*(Sarvabhauma, greeting him with blessings, sits down.)*

**King.** Bhattacharya, have you reported to the Lord Sri Krishna Chaitanya?

**Sarvabhauma.** Of course.

**King.** And what did He command?

**Sarvabhauma.** What shall I say?

**King** *(sorrowfully)*. I understood that already when you did not come to me yourself, joyfully, to tell me. Alas, woe is me!

> **28.** He looks even on the low-born, who should not even be looked at — but not on me.
> Can it be that this God has descended resolving: "I shall show mercy to all except him alone"?

*(Reflecting a moment.)* Listen, then —

> **29.** Well known is the vow of Him whose word is true; so, ah, now I too take a vow:
> either I shall part with my life, or — or, what am I to do? — I shall make the pair of His lotus feet a traveller on the road of my eyes.[^37]

*(Stands in tears.)*

**Sarvabhauma** *(to himself)*. The excess of his love has passed all measure. What is to be done?

> **30.** To go and tell Him again? Ah, no, that will not do: His resolve is dense — firm, heavy, long-drawn.
> And this one's great passion is almost invincible — broad, keen, mature: neither of them is able to conquer the other.[^38]

So now a stratagem is needed. *(Aloud.)* Great King! Be comforted, be comforted: there is a certain means by which the tree of your desire may bear fruit.

**King.** If you know it, tell it.

**Sarvabhauma.** Although the one who will arrange the meeting with Him is this messenger — your love itself — still our stratagem too may serve as a road.

**King.** Tell me, then, what stratagem?

**Sarvabhauma** *(softly, to him alone)*. Making love alone your messenger, all by yourself, putting off your royal garb, recognised by no one, on the day of the chariot festival of the god Jagannatha, when the Lord, to relieve the fatigue of the dancing play, goes into a secluded garden and His outer senses fade away from relishing bliss — approach suddenly and behold the Lord. Otherwise it will not come to pass.

**King** *(reassured, aside)*. So we shall do. But arrange it so that no one except you knows of it.

**Sarvabhauma.** So it shall be.

**Doorkeeper** *(entering)*. Sire! A spy has arrived in haste from the capital.

**King.** Let him enter.

**Doorkeeper** *(goes out quickly, brings him in and enters again)*. Sire, here he is.

**King.** Spy, tell me, why such alarm?

**Spy.** Sire!

> **31.** Many thousands of people have suddenly gathered beyond the Chitrotpala.
> Whether they are pilgrims or from an enemy army I do not know: as soon as I heard the noise I hurried here.[^39]

**Sarvabhauma.** Certainly pilgrims; otherwise the news would have come earlier. From this we conclude that they are Gaudiyas — the beloved associates of the Lord Sri Krishna Chaitanya. Well, the Lord will have a great gathering of those who surpass all in sweet conversations.

*(Behind the scenes, noise.)*

**Sarvabhauma.** Great King! They have indeed already come to the bank of the Narendra-sarovara — for this joyful clamour is heard.

**King.** So it is.

**Sarvabhauma.** Great King, if you please, go up to the upper terrace and look at the spectacle.

**King.** As you please. *(Goes up to the terrace and looks at the road.)* Sarvabhauma! Who is that running hurriedly to meet the pilgrims with a garland of the remnants of the Lord's offerings?

**Sarvabhauma.** That is Damodara, a beloved associate of the Lord Chaitanya. Evidently the Lord, hearing of the arrival of Advaita and other dear friends, has sent him to meet them with honour, offering a garland of prasada, and to bring them.

**King.** So there are some there who have been favoured with the mercy of the Lord Chaitanya?

**Sarvabhauma.** Of course! Otherwise would it be so? Have Gopinatha Acharya called: he knows them all.

**Gopinatha Acharya** *(entering, tossing aside the curtain)*. Here I am. Let the King command what is to be done.

**King.** Sarvabhauma, give the orders.

**Sarvabhauma.** You know them all; so name each one for us.

**Gopinatha Acharya.** Very well. *(Approaches.)*

*(Behind the scenes, the sankirtana of the names of Hari is heard.)*

**Sarvabhauma** *(having listened)*.

> **32.** This sound of sankirtana at first, while the words and meaning were not yet distinguished, only gladdened the ear;
> then, when the words began to be made out, it became something else, and when the meaning was grasped, it became something else again.[^40]

**King** *(listening intently)*. Such art of kirtana I have never seen anywhere.

**Sarvabhauma.** It is the creation of the Lord Krishna Chaitanya.

**King.** Acharya! And who is the one to whom he offered the Lord's garland?

**Gopinatha Acharya.** I shall name each one. This is Advaita, this is Nityananda.

**Sarvabhauma.** Him I recognise.

**King.** Why does he walk apart, with only a few people?

**Sarvabhauma.** He is indifferent to everything and does not seek the company of others.[^41]

**Gopinatha Acharya.** This is Shrivasa. Here, this is Vakreshvara. This is Acharya-ratna. Here, this is Vidyanidhi.

**Sarvabhauma.** These two I saw in my childhood.

**Gopinatha Acharya.** This is Haridasa. Here, this is Gadadhara. This is Murari. These are Shrivasa's brothers. This is Gangadasa. This is Nrisimha Acharya.[^42] And these others are people of Navadvipa; I have not seen them before either. If you allow me, I shall find out and come back.

**King.** Do so.

**Gopinatha Acharya.** As the King pleases. *(Quickly goes round the stage, goes out behind the scenes and at once enters again.)* Sarvabhauma! This is Acharya Purandara. This is Hari Bhatta. This is Raghava. This is Narayana. This is Kamalananda. This is Kashishvara. This is Vasudeva, Mukunda's elder brother. This is Shivananda. And this is another Narayana. This is Vallabha. This is Shrikanta. Why say more? All of them are associates of Sri Chaitanya; there is not a single ordinary pilgrim among them.

**King.** How is it that they, leaving the temple of Jagannatha behind, go first of all to the abode of Sri Chaitanya-Krishna?

**Sarvabhauma.** In this lies the greatness of natural love.

**King** *(again looking in another direction)*. Oh, why is Vaninatha, Ramananda's younger brother, hurrying to them with an abundance of mahaprasada?

**Sarvabhauma.** He knows the heart of the Lord Sri Chaitanya. Without a word from Him he is going to treat them to mahaprasada.

**King.** Bhattacharya! There is the injunction: "Shaving of the head and fasting — such is the rule at all holy places." Will they, transgressing it, take mahaprasada today?

**Sarvabhauma.** Sire! That is another path. That is the Lord's command given in His absence, while this is face to face. Moreover, the food of Jagannatha's prasada the Lord makes His own prasada with His own hand — what is there to dispute? And it is said thus:

> **33.** When the Lord, on whom one meditates in the heart, shows someone mercy,
> he abandons the mind fixed on the worldly and on the Vedic.[^43]

And further: according to the saying "That is action which pleases Hari", their aim is only His pleasure, not the fruit of pilgrimage.[^44]

**King.** So it is. But tell me, when is the chariot festival? The counsel you gave me has sunk into my heart, and now even a moment drags on for me like a kalpa.

**Sarvabhauma.** The day after tomorrow.

**King.** Ho, who is there! Call Pariksha Mahapatra and Kashi Mishra.

**Someone** *(entering and bowing)*. Sire, as you command. *(Goes out, brings both and enters again.)* Sire, they have come.

**King.** Mahapatra! In everything concerning the festive drive of the god Jagannatha, carry out whatever this Kashi Mishra, who knows the heart of the Lord Chaitanya, commands — as my own command.

**Mahapatra.** As the King commands.

**King.** Mishra! And you, day after day, fulfil the wishes of the Lord Chaitanya.

**Mishra.** That is my cherished desire.

**King.** And further: however many Gaudiyas come, arrange it so that they too may see the Lord without hindrance.

**Both.** As the King commands. *(Exeunt.)*

**King.** Bhattacharya! Go nearer and watch the joy of their mutual conversations. If I too had the right to it, would I remain deprived of such supreme bliss?[^45] And I shall see to what must and must not be done for the coming victorious drive of the chariots. *(Goes out.)*

**Sarvabhauma.** The King has commanded just what I desired. I shall do so at once. *(Walking a few steps with Gopinatha Acharya.)* Ah, ahead —

> **34.** Deeply rumbling with blissful cries of "hum!", with waves of tandava raised by the wind of rapture,
> bearing beauty — the moving ocean of devotion to Hari surpasses the unmoving ocean.[^46]

Let us approach. *(They approach.)*

*(Then enter all who have been mentioned, headed by Advaita.)*

**Advaita** *(looking ahead)*. Damodara! Who is this coming with yet another garland?

**Damodara.** That is Govinda, who attends on the Lord.

*(Govinda, entering hurriedly, offers the garland. Advaita receives it respectfully.)*

**Damodara.** Here, here is the abode of Kashi Mishra. Enter.

*(Advaita and the others act entering.)*

**Sarvabhauma.** Ah, a wonder!

> **35.** As at the end of the yuga, in the womb of God, light as a leaf, all these hosts of universes find room,
> so here, in the small abode of the Mishra, hundreds and thousands of people, finding a place, fit in, each in his own place.[^47]

*(Looking ahead.)* Oh, here He is —

> **36.** Surpassing the limit of the jubilation born of the rising of the moon Advaita, the ocean of nectar, Sri Chaitanya, seems to heave with high waves.
> Full of bliss, unchanging, ever lofty, indivisible — how, ah, does He receive still further increase even from partial joys?[^48]

*(Then enter, as indicated, Sri Chaitanya, the Puri master, Svarupa and the others. Sri Krishna Chaitanya, approaching, bows to Nityananda and embraces Advaita. Advaita, bowing, embraces Him in return.)*

**Sarvabhauma** *(gazing)*.

> **37.** Glory to this mutual embrace of Advaita and Chaitanya — two royal elephants in the forest of love,
> intoxicated with love for each other, nimbly clashing their trunk-arms, loudly trumpeting,
> abundantly sprinkling each other with streams of tears as with musk-fluid![^49]

*(All here and there fall prostrate on the ground like rods. The Lord favours all — embraces, converses, bestows His glance. Advaita introduces those whom the Lord has not seen before; but Sri Krishna Chaitanya Himself calls by name even those whom He has never seen.)*

**Gopinatha Acharya.** Ah, here is a marvel!

> **38.** "Are you well, Raghava? Is all well, Vasudeva? Is all dear to you?
> Ho, Narayana! Is all in order, Shivananda? Are you prosperous, Shankara?
> Kamalananda and Kashishvara, is all good with you? Shrikanta, is all in order? Peace to Narayana!"[^50]

And further —

> **39.** With such affectionate words, sweet and tender, the Lord softly addresses even those He has not seen before, as old acquaintances.
> What is this — His divine power, or His former, all-knowing love?

**Sri Krishna Chaitanya.**

> **40.** Today I have a great festival; and tomorrow or the day after, another festival of the Moon of Nilachala, called Gundicha.
> Although both, ah, equally cause bliss to flow in the heart, this festival, where Advaita is revealed, is especially delightful to Me.[^51]

*(With these words He adorns each one — Advaita and the others — with garlands and sandal of the prasada of the Lord Jagannatha, and with His own hand gives each a little of the prasada food.)*

**Sarvabhauma.** Now I should not approach: seeing me, they may pass into another mood. These two days have almost passed, and the King has charged me with gathering everything needed for the festival. So I shall go there now. Acharya, you stay here. *(Goes out.)*

**Gopinatha Acharya** *(approaching)*. Victory, victory to Mahaprabhu!

**Sri Krishna Chaitanya.** Ah, it is you, Acharya! Come near, bow to Advaita.

*(Gopinatha Acharya does so.)*

**Advaita** *(embracing him)*. I know you — you are Visharada's son-in-law.[^52]

**Sri Krishna Chaitanya.** That is why he is so very distinguished.[^53] Acharya, together with Vaninatha arrange lodging for everyone wisely.

**Gopinatha Acharya.** As the Lord commands. *(Goes out.)*

**Sri Krishna Chaitanya.** Vasudeva! Although Mukunda is My old companion, you, though I see you today for the first time, have long been dear to Me.

**Vasudeva** *(humbly)*. Master! What am I, insignificant! But Mukunda has long been favoured by You. The time of the Lord's mercy is the time of birth; therefore, though he is younger, he is my elder brother.

**Sri Krishna Chaitanya.** Shivananda! I know that you are very attached to Me.

**Shivananda.**

> **41.** For me, long drowning in the ocean of existence, You, O Infinite One, have been found like a shore;
> and You, Lord, have now found this unsurpassed vessel for Your mercy.[^54]

*(With these words he falls to the ground.)*

**Sri Krishna Chaitanya.** Raghava! You are the object of My great love.

*(Raghava, answering without words, falls prostrate.)*

**Sri Krishna Chaitanya.** Svarupa! Although this Shankara is Damodara's younger brother, yet to Me… *(Without finishing, He looks at Damodara.)*[^55]

**Damodara.** Master! For me this is great happiness. Finish the rest of Your words.

**Sri Krishna Chaitanya.** Svarupa! For Damodara I have an attachment mixed with reverence, but for this one a love without any admixture. So let him live here, with you. Govinda, you too be kind to him.

**Both.** As the master commands.

**Gopinatha Acharya** *(entering hurriedly)*. Svamin! Everything has been arranged as You commanded. Especially for Gadadhara a fine place has been found near Yameshvara — for all time.[^56]

**Sri Krishna Chaitanya.** Advaita! This Puri master is like a second Munindra;[^57] for he is the beloved disciple of your guru. Bow to him, then.

*(Advaita does so. All bow too.)*

**Sri Krishna Chaitanya.** Acharya Advaita! And this here is Svarupa. By the interpretation "he who has his own form" he is always unchanging; know that he is My very heart.[^58]

**Advaita.** So it is. *(Bows. All bow.)*

**Gopinatha Acharya.** Master, allow them to go and rest.

**Sri Krishna Chaitanya.** Acharya, tell them yourself. How can I say "go"?

*(Advaita and the others, understanding the hint, go out.)*

**Sri Krishna Chaitanya.** Svamin Puri master! Loving Svarupa! Today I am full.

**Svarupa.** Svamin!

> **42.** The Lord, though full in Himself, is filled by His associates:
> the full moon too, the lord of the night, is empty without the stars.[^59]

Come, then: evening has come. Without You the Puri master has not yet accepted alms either.

**Sri Krishna Chaitanya.** So it is. *(Exeunt.)*

**Behind the scenes.**

> **43.** Shatadhriti too has lost his firmness, and the thousand-eyed has become quite blind —
> from the thirst to behold the drive of the chariot of the Moon of the Blue Mountain.[^60]

**Gopinatha Acharya** *(having listened)*. Ah, two days have flown by like a moment: here is the Bhattacharya starting to speak of the chariot festival. I shall go and look. *(Walking a little.)* Ah, a wonder!

> **44.** Like the three Vedas embodied, like the three eyes of Shambhu,
> like the three streams of the heavenly river — ahead gleam three chariots.[^61]

**Again behind the scenes.**

> **45.** Today the day of the chariot festival of the god, the lord of Nilachala, has come; today before Him the golden Hari will dance in His own bliss;
> today, when He has finished dancing, He will rest in the jasmine grove — ah, today this desire of one such as I will be fulfilled!

**Gopinatha Acharya.** Oh, it is like the voice of the great King Gajapati; I must listen carefully. *(Gazing.)* And indeed: the great King, conversing with the Bhattacharya, is pining with impatience. Let me see why they delay in raising Jagannatha onto the chariot. *(Looks.)*

*(Behind the scenes, the thunder of kahala trumpets and the rest.)*

**Gopinatha Acharya** *(gazing)*. Ah!

> **46.** Like the light in the heart of those absorbed in samadhi, like the hot sun on the peak of the mountain of sunrise,
> the Lord of the Blue Mountain mounts the chariot — a beauty that is an elixir for all eyes.

*(Gazing again.)*

> **47.** Surrounded by hosts of all His friends headed by Advaita, and by other singers headed by Sri Svarupa,
> with many intimates immersed in nectar, headed by Sri Vakreshvara —
> this ocean of love, the golden king of ascetics, dances here and dances without end.[^62]

*(Behind the scenes, noise.)*

**Gopinatha Acharya** *(joyfully)*.

> **48.** The throngs of chariot-pullers, called Gaudas, hold it with their left hands, as if playfully dragging a bundle of thick ropes —
> and the chariot, ah, obedient only to its own will, now stands and stands, and now suddenly rushes at full speed,
> now runs and runs, and now, ah, stands more motionless than before.[^63]

*(Again behind the scenes, the roar of sankirtana.)*

**Gopinatha Acharya** *(gazing with curiosity)*.

> **49.** When Jagannatha moves off, Gaura draws back before Him; when Jagannatha stops, Gaura steps forward before Him.
> So the two Gods, full of playfulness and longing for each other, seem to start a game — the Moon of Nilachala and the jewel Lord.[^64]

*(Gazing again.)* Ah —

> **50.** When the Lord, the Lord of the world, has stopped at the pavilion of Balagandi, the king of ascetics, having finished the dance,
> goes into the garden with His loving associates to rest from His extreme fatigue. Glory to Him![^65]

From the Bhattacharya's sign I have understood that now the King too must come there secretly, in disguise. So I too shall hurry. *(Approaches.)*

*(Then enters Sri Krishna Chaitanya — motionless, experiencing the bliss of the dance, with closed eyes, a delight to the eyes; He sits in a garden bower, stretching out the trembling pair of His lotus feet with shanks like lotus stalks; His chest is bathed in tears flowing from His eyes; He is like the very embodiment of the bliss of love. His associates sit silently, each at the foot of a tree.)*[^66]

**Bhagavan.** "And therefore the swans take refuge at Your lotus feet, pouring forth bliss, O Lotus-eyed One…"[^67] *(With closed eyes He repeats only this half-verse again and again.)*

**Gopinatha Acharya** *(looking)*. Ah, how great is the relishing of the bliss of love! Savouring, the Lord relishes the fact that the bliss He experienced during the dance — the bliss of directly beholding the Lord Krishna — is more wondrous even than the bliss of Brahman. "And" (*atha*) — that is, upon completing the understanding of all that the scriptures, high and low, teach. "Therefore" (*ataḥ*) — because it is more wondrous even than the bliss of Brahman. "The swans" — skilled in discriminating essence from non-essence — "take refuge at the lotus feet". Why? "Pouring forth bliss" — by this the greatness of the bliss He Himself has tasted is indicated. *(Looking around.)* Ah, here too are all the greatest devotees. And truly —

> **51.** Motionless, with bright radiance, with lovely flames, full of the oil of love, dispelling darkness,
> at the foot of every tree these great souls shine in a beautiful state — like lamps at an auspicious great festival in a windless place.[^68]

Well, I shall sit quietly somewhere here and wait for the King to enter. *(Does so.)*

*(Then enters the King, having taken off his royal garb, dressed in a pair of washed garments.)*

**King** *(agitated)*.

> **52.** Longing, overpowering the two strong ones — fear and deliberation — shakes me greatly; and my feet — alas, woe! — how they stiffen!
> Ho, fate! Today your test will surely be a test of my very life; but to my life I am not attached at all.[^69]

*(Very, very slowly goes round the stage.)*

**Gopinatha Acharya** *(looking at the King)*. Ah, a wonder!

> **53.** All that is royal in him is his majesty alone; he is ahead, like the heroic rasa asleep;
> seized by bliss, by doubt, by fear and by deliberation, he sets down his lotus foot with difficulty.[^70]

*(All around — to themselves.)* Ah, it is King Prataparudra, his hand sealed with the auspicious cord![^71] How is it that he, having taken the guise of an ascetic, suddenly approaches here? Svami will be disturbed. Let us see what he will do.

*(The King, slowly looking about, suddenly approaches and with his arms, long like iron bars, tightly clasps the trembling lotus feet of the Lord.)*

**Sarvabhauma** *(looking)*.[^72] Ah, here is great trouble! The Lord, with His lotus eyes closed, helpless in the rapture of His own bliss, did not notice him — and he has seized the Lord's lotus feet. We do not know what will happen now.

**Bhagavan** *(still in His bliss, with closed eyes, without looking, tightly embracing him)*.

> **54.** Who, O King, among those possessing senses, surrounded on every side by death, would not serve the lotus feet of Mukunda,
> worshipped by the best of the immortals?[^73]

*(Repeats this again and again.)*

**Gopinatha Acharya.** Ah, a marvel!

> **55.** Boldness sometimes serves for good and sometimes turns into a vice;
> but what the King has accomplished by boldness is not attained even by all austerities.

*(Gazing again.)*

> **56.** He by whom great wrestlers, squeezed by his broad arms and chest, were left powerless, as if with broken bones —
> he himself, squeezed by the Lord's arms, has become like a plantain trunk encircled by the trunk of an intoxicated royal elephant.[^74]

*(Behind the scenes, noise. The Lord, leaving the King and hurrying, since from the noise He understands that the chariot is moving off, again goes out, wishing to see Jagannatha, still in the same bliss. All, each as is fitting, go out after Him.)*

**Gopinatha Acharya** *(approaching)*. Great King! God has gone to see Jagannatha. Now it is time for you too to go.

*(With these words he goes out, leading away the King, motionless with bliss. Exeunt all.)*

*Thus ends the eighth act of the* Sri Chaitanya-chandrodaya, *called "Mercy to Prataparudra".*

[^3]: The Tattvavadis are the followers of Madhva (cf. the meeting with them during the southern pilgrimage, Act 7).
[^4]: *Paśupāla* (literally "herdsman") is here apparently a class of temple servants of Jagannatha. Pariksha Mahapatra (Oriya "Paricha") is the chief superintendent of the temple. In the Kāvyamālā edition the pashupalas and Kashi Mishra with Pariksha are separated by the stage direction differently, with no difference in sense.
[^5]: "Then", i.e. during the Lord's first stay in Puri, before the southern pilgrimage. *Jaṅgama*, "moving", in contrast to the unmoving wooden form of Jagannatha (the "Lord of the Blue Mountain", Nilachala).
[^6]: *Anavasara*, the period after the bathing festival (Snana-yatra), when Jagannatha is hidden from view "by illness" and is served only by special servants.
[^7]: *Dāsa-mahāsoāra*: Oriya *mahāsuāra*, the "head cook" of the temple kitchen; "Dasa" is a name or by-name.
[^8]: In the Kāvyamālā edition, Simheshvara (*siṁheśvara*).
[^9]: *Prahara-rājya-mahāpātra* (Kāvyamālā *prahara-rāja-*) is apparently the title of a royal official; the interpretation is unclear.
[^10]: A play on words: *guḍa* is "molasses, cane sugar", while *gauḍa* is both "of molasses" and "Gaudiya, Bengali". The Kāvyamālā edition has plainly *gauḍasya rasasya*, "the juice of molasses / the 'rasa' of Gauda"; the end of the line there is *na caiti baddham* ("does not set"), the electronic text has *randham*. Sarvabhauma replies, playing on the sense "Bengali".
[^11]: Pundarikaksha, "the Lotus-eyed", i.e. Jagannatha.
[^12]: A double meaning: "the lord of the City of Supreme Bliss" (*paramānanda-purīśvara*) is Jagannatha, who comes out to the people at the hour of the temple's opening and chastises sinners; but the sannyasi Paramananda Puri with his monastic staff (*daṇḍa*) is also so called. Chaitanya understands the cry in the second sense, and Sarvabhauma then calls the temple "the Lord's Paramananda-puri".
[^13]: The electronic text has *agajasya*, an obvious misprint; the Kāvyamālā edition *agrajasya*, "of the elder brother". Vishvarupa is Chaitanya's elder brother, who accepted sannyasa.
[^14]: In the Kāvyamālā edition here: "**Sri Krishna Chaitanya.** I am favoured. *(Approaches.)* **Jagadananda.** Svamin, this way, this way. *(Offers him rest.)*", i.e. the last words belong to Jagadananda.
[^15]: *Nada* and *nadī* are rivers of masculine and feminine gender; an image of the associates flowing together to Chaitanya, the ocean. Below, Sarvabhauma interprets the word *sa-pravāhāḥ*, "with their currents", as a hint at the associates descending, some before, others after.
[^16]: A play on the name: *dāmodara-svarūpa*, "the very form of Damodara (Krishna)". Sarvabhauma understands the verse as a glorification of Chaitanya Himself, while Chaitanya hears in it the name of an expected associate. The electronic text has *bādhita*, the Kāvyamālā edition *vyadhita* ("created"); the latter is adopted.
[^17]: The stage direction is in the Kāvyamālā edition; it is missing in the electronic text.
[^18]: The verse is built on a continuous rhyme in *-dayā* ("mercy" and an instrumental ending), including *rasa-dayā*, "bestowing rasa", and *sa-madayā*, "intoxicating".
[^19]: Svarupa, according to tradition, took sannyasa without formal initiation, keeping the brahmachari name "Svarupa". The Kāvyamālā edition has *vairāgya-vaśena*, "under the sway of renunciation", instead of *vaiyāgrya-veśena*, and *vadann api*, "though he spoke of it", instead of *vahann api*.
[^20]: The Kāvyamālā edition adds: "Is it not Pariksha Mahapatra's deputy? No, that one is not so detached."
[^21]: Here "the Puri master" is Ishvara Puri, Chaitanya's guru (not Paramananda Puri).
[^22]: Ishvara Puri, who had given Chaitanya initiation, saw Him still as a householder; he does not wish to see Him in the garb of a sannyasi and therefore sends his servant. In the Kāvyamālā edition Govinda's first words ("Ishvara Puri sent me…") are set out as a separate quoted verse.
[^23]: Suyodhana is Duryodhana. Krishna, coming as envoy to Hastinapura, refused Duryodhana's hospitality and ate at Vidura's (*Mahābhārata*, Udyoga-parva).
[^24]: In the electronic text Mukunda's speech breaks off at the address; it is restored from the Kāvyamālā edition; the same text is in M. Guha.
[^25]: Vishnupadi is the Ganga, flowing from the foot of Vishnu; descending to earth, she raises people to heaven.
[^26]: *Damanaka*, a fragrant plant (artemisia) from which garlands were woven.
[^27]: In the Kāvyamālā edition: *bāhya-veśāveśa-vaśatvam*, "subjection to infatuation with outward garb".
[^28]: Chaitanya deflects the praise: "the newly arrived golden Brahman" is Brahmananda himself, whose name contains the word "brahma".
[^29]: *Vyāpya-vyāpaka*, the logical relation of "pervaded and pervader" (e.g. smoke and fire): Brahmananda acknowledges himself as subordinate and Chaitanya as the all-pervading Brahman, and as proof refers to his having obeyed His sign.
[^30]: Names from the *Viṣṇu-sahasra-nāma* (*Mahābhārata*, Anushasana-parva): *suvarṇa-varṇo hemāṅgo varāṅgaś candanāṅgadī*. *Sānvayatva*, "agreement with the etymology".
[^31]: *Brahma-sūtra* 1.1.12 (*ānandamayo 'bhyāsāt*). There follows a grammatical interpretation of the suffix *mayaṭ* in the sense of "abundance" (and not of "modification").
[^32]: The verse is cited as a well-known quotation; it is found also in Rupa Gosvami's *Bhakti-rasāmṛta-sindhu* (3.1.44, in the common numbering). The speakers are former Advaitins; "slave-girls" (*dāsī*) is feminine in the original.
[^33]: Verses 23 and 24 are quoted in connection with this same episode in the *Chaitanya-charitamrita* of Krishnadasa Kaviraja (Madhya 11.8 and 11.11); there too (11.47) verse 28 is quoted.
[^34]: *Dvairājya*, "the rule of two kings", i.e. war between two kingdoms (Orissa and Gauda), which made the road dangerous.
[^35]: The electronic text has *vidhura*, the Kāvyamālā edition *vidhūya* ("dispelling"); the latter is adopted.
[^36]: In the electronic text the negative is missing (*dhārayiṣye*); according to the Kāvyamālā edition, *na dhārayiṣye*, which the metre also requires.
[^37]: *Pakṣa* is both "vow, side" and a logical "thesis": the King, as it were, puts forward his own condition in a dispute with Chaitanya.
[^38]: A piling up of abstract nouns (*draḍhiman, gariman, drāghiman; prathiman, paṭiman, prauḍhiman*) — a deliberate play of sounds. The Kāvyamālā edition has *prathita-*, "famed", instead of *prathima-*. The last words (*kaścit kam api na vijetuṁ prabhavati*) we understand as "neither can overcome the other".
[^39]: The Chitrotpala is the river Mahanadi, the boundary of Prataparudra's dominions on the side of Bengal (on the way from Gauda to Puri). *Paracakra*, "a foreign army".

[^40]: M. Guha's edition reads *'vibhakta-sarvārtha* (so too the electronic text: *avibhakta-*) and *śravaṇa-pramodī*, "gladdening the ear"; in Guha's commentary: at first neither words nor tune nor rhythm are distinguishable, and his Bengali translation understands the verse in the same way. The Kāvyamālā edition: *'vibhakta-śabdārtha*, the same in sense. *Śaraṇa-pramodī* of the electronic text is a misprint for *śravaṇa-*.
[^41]: *Sarvādṛtatvāt* can be understood both as "being honoured by all" (*sarva-ādṛta*) and as "caring for nothing" (*sarva-adṛta*); the second agrees better with the image of Nityananda the avadhuta and with his "not seeking the company of others".
[^42]: Probably Nrisimhananda (Pradyumna Brahmachari) or another Nrisimha; the identification is unclear.
[^43]: *Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 4.29.46 (the electronic text gives the reference 4.29.47; the numbering of verses differs between editions).
[^44]: *Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 4.29.49: *tat karma hari-toṣaṁ yat*.
[^45]: *Sati tādṛśe 'dhikāre mayaiva tādṛśa-paramānanda-bhogād avañcitena kathaṁ bhavitavyam*: an unreal condition — the King does not yet have such a right (of access to the Lord). So M. Guha's Bengali translation and Krishnadasa Baba's Hindi translation understand it; that is why the King sends Sarvabhauma in his place.
[^46]: *Lāvaṇya*, "beauty" and "saltiness" (from *lavaṇa*, "salt"): the ocean of bhakti "bears salt" just as the real ocean does. The unmoving ocean is the ordinary sea (at Puri; in M. Guha's commentary *sindhum* = *samudram*), the "moving" one is the kirtana procession: Guha explains *calaḥ* as "flowing with a stream of beauty".
[^47]: An allusion to the vision of Markandeya (*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 12.9): at the end of the yuga he sees an infant on a banyan leaf and enters its womb, in which all the worlds abide. The Kāvyamālā edition has *brahmāṇḍaka-samudayād eva vapuṣaḥ … mānti*, "find room"; the electronic text has *yānti sam*; *mānti* is adopted.
[^48]: A play on words: *advaita* is the name of Advaita and "non-duality"; *akhaṇḍa*, "indivisible", is contrasted with *khaṇḍa-ānanda*, "partial joys" (possibly also a hint at *khaṇḍa*, "sugar", and at the devotees from Shrikhanda). The ocean heaves at the rising of the moon.
[^49]: *Dāna* is both "gift" and the musk-fluid (*mada*) flowing from an elephant's temples in rut.
[^50]: All the greetings are synonyms of "well-being" (*kṣema, śiva, priya, kalyāṇa, bhavya, bhadra, kuśala, svasti*); *śivam* plays on the name of Shivananda.
[^51]: A play on words: *advaita-prakaṭana*, "the manifestation of Advaita" and "the manifestation of non-duality". The second half of the verse is corrupt in the electronic text (*hetu-garbha-viśeṣāṇaṁ*); translated according to the Kāvyamālā edition (M. Guha reads and understands it likewise): *tulyau yadyapy ahaha hṛdayānanda-niḥsyanda-hetū yatrādvaita-prakaṭanam asāv utsavo me pramodī*.
[^52]: Maheshvara Visharada is the father of Sarvabhauma; Gopinatha Acharya was married to his daughter.
[^53]: In the Kāvyamālā edition: *svata eva mahattaro 'yam*, "he is very great in himself as well".
[^54]: A verse from the *Stotra-ratna* of Yamunacharya (21 in the electronic text; in other editions the numbering may differ).
[^55]: Damodara here is Damodara Pandita, the elder brother of Shankara Pandita, not Svarupa Damodara (to whom the speech is addressed).
[^56]: Yameshvara is a temple of Shiva in Puri; near it, according to tradition, Gadadhara settled (the site of Tota-Gopinatha). In the Kāvyamālā edition (OCR) *mameśvarasya*, apparently an error.
[^57]: "The lord of sages" is Madhavendra Puri, the guru of Advaita and of Paramananda Puri.
[^58]: A play on the name "Svarupa": *svaṁ rūpam asyāsti*, "he has his own form", i.e. he does not change.
[^59]: In the Kāvyamālā edition *aṁśubhir vinā*, "without rays", instead of *uḍubhir vinā*, "without stars".
[^60]: Shatadhriti ("Hundredfold firm") is Brahma; "the thousand-eyed" is Indra. A play: the firm one loses his firmness, the many-eyed one goes blind.
[^61]: The three chariots are those of Jagannatha, Balarama and Subhadra; "the heavenly river" is the Ganga, flowing in the three worlds in three streams.
[^62]: In the Kāvyamālā edition *sukhāviṣṭa*, "immersed in happiness", instead of *sudhāviṣṭa*, "immersed in nectar".
