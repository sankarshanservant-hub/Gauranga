# Act 4. The Acceptance of Sannyasa (Sannyāsa-parigrahaḥ)

*Shachi is anxious about her son's inclination to sannyasa; Advaita comes from Shantipura to the sankirtana at Shrivasa's; the Lord secretly leaves for Katva and accepts sannyasa from Keshava Bharati.*

*(Enter the lady Shachi, followed by the wife of Acharya-ratna.)*

**Shachi.** Sister! Why does Sri Vishvambhara have such reverence for sannyasis? For a sannyasi's inclinations are of quite another kind.[^1]

**Sister.** And how did you learn of His reverence?

**Shachi.** Because that day, full of faith, He asked me for alms for a certain sannyasi named Keshava Bharati. And He Himself showed him great devotion and attachment.

**Sister.** Evidently he too is a devotee.

**Shachi.** My heart trembles at the very name "sannyasi". His elder brother has already taught me that lesson.[^2] That is why I shall ask Him Himself about this matter.

**Sister.** That is right.

**Shachi.** Then find out where He is, my sandal tree that gladdens the heart.

**Sister** *(looking ahead)*. Lady! Look, look — here is your son rising like the full moon in the east.

*(Shachi gazes tenderly. Then enters the Lord Vishvambhara.)*

**Vishvambhara** *(joining His palms)*. Mother, I bow to you.

**Shachi** *(saying "Live long!", she smells the crown of His head)*. My son! This is the wife of Acharya-ratna; bow to her.

*(The Lord does so. She shrinks back in embarrassment.)*

**Shachi.** My son! I want to ask You something.

**The Lord.** Command Me.

**Shachi.** My little son! Why do You have such reverence for sannyasis that that day You showed such devotion to Keshava Bharati?

**The Lord.** Mother! Why, they are the greatest devotees of Bhagavan.

**Shachi.** Tell the truth: are You not intending to take sannyasa?

**The Lord** *(laughing)*. Mother! Whence this delusion of yours? Can such a thing be?

**Shachi.** Child, the book that that very elder brother of Yours gave You — I threw it into the hearth while cooking and burnt it.

**The Lord.** What book? And why did you burn it?

**Shachi.** Vishvarupa said to me: "Mother! When Vishvambhara becomes learned, give Him this book." I kept it only until he himself went off wandering. And when he had gone, I burnt it — I was afraid that You, getting that book, would also go off into sannyasa.

**The Lord** *(saddened for a moment, then laughing)*. Mother! Though you yourself are knowledge embodied, still, out of love for your son, you did this, yielding to the play of ignorance.[^3]

**Shachi.** My son! Do not count this as my fault.

**The Lord.** What fault can a mother have towards her sons? But if there is a fault on My part, be merciful: may my mother's venerable feet surely forgive it.

**Shachi.** Child! Never shall I count any fault against You — You simply have none.

**The Lord.** Mother! For a few days I shall have to go somewhere. Do not grieve over it in your heart.

**Shachi.** Where must You go?

**The Lord.** Mother! To seek that by which you and all our kinsfolk will always be happy.[^4]

**Shachi.** Child! Act so that I may have no sorrow.

**The Lord.** Mother!

> **1.** Sri Krishna is your protector,[^5] your father, mother and son,
> your kinsman and wealth, your friend who grants eternal happiness, and your deity.
> Always, day and night, remembering in your heart Him with whom your bond is eternal,
> you have attained fullness; know, then: now the whole circle of the quarters is full of happiness for you.

**Shachi.** Child! You are everything to me; by Your mercy I have no sorrow. But act so that I may always see You.

**The Lord.** Always look upon Krishna. He alone will destroy all your sorrows.

**Shachi.** So be it. You are my Krishna. So get up: it is already midday. You too perform Your bathing, worship and the rest, and I shall go and cook. Sister, you too go home: for you too the time has come to prepare food for the service of Bhagavan.

*(Exeunt all. Then enters Advaita.)*

**Advaita.** One whose senses are all helplessly subjected to possession by a spirit, and one who has lost his steadiness through the entry of Bhagavan into him, show one and the same state. For how I danced that day when Bhagavan entered into me, I hear only now from people's lips — and I both believe and doubt. So the power of this Lord Vishvambhara is in no way restricted. But in truth it is simply the play of Bhagavan, skilled in the endless series of performances in which He moulds and breaks the pots of the universal eggs — myriads upon myriads — and who with the nectar of the lovely fame of His deeds has washed away the bitter impurity of the darkness lurking in the wells of the hearts of the people of the world.[^6] It truly cannot be measured by any means of knowledge — perception, inference, comparison, verbal testimony, presumption, tradition and the rest — without the special knowledge born of His mercy alone. Therefore that dancing play of the other day, the cause of a transcendental wonder, though it took place in me, did not become accessible to my experience — it was performed by Him. Therefore:

> **2.** Some will be bewildered, others will dispute with them,
> but certain knowers of the connection of events will understand: "This is one of the secrets."

*(Looking up.)* Oh, here is the divine sun in its crown of rays bending down to kiss the summit of the Western Mountain. For:

> **3.** "You are mine in name only, but in fact a resting place for all the planets"[^7] —
> so her husband accused her; and, to prove her fidelity,
> the Western Quarter, the wife of Varuna, as if holds red-hot iron
> in the guise of the orb of the evening sun.

Or else:

> **4.** From the West, whose mind has lost itself in the bliss of union with Evening,
> the garment of red clouds has slipped from her hips —
> and the sun, the jewel of the day, which had taken the form of the chief ruby of her girdle,
> has in its turn begun to fall.[^8]

So the weariness of the road has passed; now, having performed the evening worship, I must see the most beautiful of all who are worth seeing — the Lord Vishvambhara. *(Prepares to do so.)*

**Behind the scenes.** Ah, Advaita went away saying that, after visiting his own town, he would return at once — why has he not yet come?

**Advaita** *(acting as if listening)*. Oh, seeing that I delay, the Lord Himself reproaches me. I had better go quickly. *(Walks about the stage.)*

**Shrirama**[^9] *(entering, tossing aside the curtain)*. O Advaita! The Lord, Bhagavan, commands: "I am going from here to the house of Shrivasa; you too go there."

**Advaita.** As the Lord commands. *(Walks about the stage with him and, looking ahead.)* Here is the house of Shrivasa; let me enter. *(Acting entering and looking to the east.)* Oh, how beautiful:

> **5.** Gladdening the eyes of the people of the whole world, with cool rays for feet,
> pouring forth the nectar of love, making the night-lilies — the joy of the earth[^10] — open,
> both the moon and the moon Vishvambhara are rising.

**Vishvambhara** *(rising to meet him)*. Welcome, oh, welcome!

**Advaita.** It is a blessing to me — the sight of Your beautiful moon-face.

**The Lord** *(bowing respectfully and embracing him)*. Venerable sir, sit here.

**Advaita.** As You command. *(All act sitting down comfortably.)*

**The Lord** *(to Advaita)*. We have all already eaten and drunk. Only you are tired from the road and hungry, so there must be no delay. Shrivasa, you are the master of the house: show them hospitality.[^11]

**Advaita.** Do not worry about that: we, like you, have already fulfilled all the day's duties.

**The Lord** *(joyfully)*. Then now, in the courtyard of Shrivasa's house, washed with the silver water of the moonbeams, accept the auspicious festival of the sankirtana of Bhagavan.

**All** *(joyfully)*. Lord! Rise up Yourself too.

**The Lord.** Here I come. *(All go out to the place of sankirtana.)*

**Gangadasa** *(entering)*. They say that the venerable Advaita has arrived from Shantipura. I do not know whether he is staying in the house of the Lord Vishvambhara or in the house of Shrivasa; I must find out how matters stand. *(Takes a few steps. Behind the scenes, the noise of sankirtana.)*

**Gangadasa** *(listening)*. Oh, I hear near the house of Shrivasa the roar of sankirtana, born of joy and gladdening all the devotees who have gathered there in time.[^12] So Advaita must be there too. Let me stand here and watch. *(Gazes.)* Oh, all of them, singing, are making the Lord Vishvambhara dance — and are dancing themselves. Therefore I think this:

> **6.** Although the heavy burden of the earth, made up of the hosts of invincible Daityas,
> was removed by Bhagavan Himself,
> now the Lord, dancing with His devotees, shakes off from her the remnant of her weariness
> with the heavy blows of His feet, wild in the dance.[^13]

*(Gazing again.)* Oh!

> **7.** Is this not the supreme bliss that has taken form? Is it not love itself that has taken a body?
> Faith embodied, or compassion itself, appearing on earth in its own form?
> Sweetness that has taken a body? Or ninefold bhakti come together in one body? —
> It is Vakreshvara dancing, a festival of bliss equal in rapture to the Lord Himself.

*(Again behind the scenes, a united thunder of bliss and the roar of cries of "Jaya! Jaya!")*

**Gangadasa** *(gazing)*. Oh, a great marvel!

> **8.** When Vakreshvara dances, Gaurachandra sings loudly, clapping His hands;
> when Vakreshvara sings, Gaurachandra dances — and their joy is one.

*(Again behind the scenes, the same roar of cries of "Jaya! Jaya!" and jubilant cries of "ulu-ulu".[^14])*

**Gangadasa** *(gazing for a long time)*. Oh, now the Lord Vishvambhara Himself has broken into a dance. For:

> **9.** With deep peals of roaring He turns His people into peacocks,[^15]
> with streams of tears He ceaselessly makes the world a rainy day,
> with floods of radiance He girds all the quarters with lightning —
> gladdening the universe, there dances before me the cloud Vishvambhara.[^16]

And further:

> **10.** In all the quarters and intermediate quarters scattering with His gaze a garland of lotuses,
> with the tears of His eyes pouring honey into them,
> with His brows a swarm of bees —
> in a dance whirling like a wheel, Gaurachandra is victorious here.[^17]

And further:

> **11.** Victorious be the wheel-dance of the Lord Vishvambhara —
> who with the blows of His feet causes a flood of bliss in the city of the serpents,
> who with the waving of His arms seems skilled in the tandava in the city of the gods,
> who seems to turn the circle of the quarters with whirling rods of radiance.[^18]

*(Gazing again.)* Oh! Next the venerable Advaita has broken into a dance.

> **12.** Shrivasa with his three brothers — Rama and the others, with the loveliest voices —
> sings sweetly; the Lord Himself sings too, and ardent Vakreshvara;
> and Advaita — with anklets, armlets, necklace, bracelets, a girdle of little bells and the rest —
> like the bliss of service embodied, dances and dances.

> **13.** His head is beautiful with an elegant large turban, in both ears
> pearl earrings sway in the tandava, on his chest a splendid golden garland swings,
> at his feet anklets jingle, the hair on his body stands on end, his face is bathed in tears —
> and now magnanimous Nityananda is dancing in great rapture.

*(Looking at the sky.)* Oh, only one watch of the night is left. No wonder my eyes are closing: the lady Drowsiness has overcome me. Let me sleep here a little. *(Acting sleep, he speaks in his sleep.)* O Lord Vishvambhara, where are You, where are You? *(Having said this in his sleep and waking again.)* Oh, what an evil dream I have had! *(Meditating for a moment in his heart on the Lord's feet, he again looks towards the back of the stage.)* Oh, no one is to be seen. It seems the Lord and all the others, at the end of the sankirtana, have gone off to sleep here and there. So be it — I too shall go home. *(Taking a few steps.)* Oh, the night has already dawned! *(Looks to the east.)*

> **14.** Having only just stepped over the crest of the rampart of the Eastern Mountain,
> holding on to the edge of the sky-garment of the eastern quarter —
> though he still clumsily stretches out his ray-feet —
> look: the child sun is rising, obedient to time.[^19]

*(Walking a few steps and looking ahead.)* Oh, who is this hurrying this way, as if wanting to ask something?

*(Enter a certain man, alarmed.)*

**Man.** Hey, Gangadasa! Is the Lord Vishvambhara in your garden?

**Gangadasa** *(joyfully)*. Oh, what good fortune for me! He whom I was going to see has come to my garden Himself!

**Man.** No, no, I am asking: has He come to your garden?

**Gangadasa** *(dejected)*. Why do you ask so?

**Man.** On other days He comes home in the morning and performs His duties. But today He has not come, and so the lady Shachi has sent me to look for Him. *(Having said this, he goes off to look elsewhere. Again, from another side, an alarmed man hurriedly enters, asks in the same way and goes out. And again from another side, and again from another, and again from another — they ask and ask in the same way and go out.)*

**Gangadasa** *(dejected)*. Oh! It seems the evil dream has come true. What am I to do? I shall go to where Advaita and the others are. *(Takes a few steps.)*

*(Then enter Advaita and the others, despondent, acting deep thought.)*

**Advaita.** Shrivasa, what is this?

> **15.** We thought the Lord had gone home in the morning,
> and His mother thought He had stayed in the house of Shrivasa or with others —
> so we were all deceived, suspecting nothing. How could we know
> that today this thunderbolt would suddenly strike?

**Shrivasa.** Of all who were sent to look for Him, not one has returned.

**Advaita.** They will return when they have found and seen Him. But no one has learned anything — how can that be?

> **16.** Who in this village could hide Him who reveals Himself?
> And could He hide Himself?
> People cannot conceal the sun with its blazing rays;
> how, then, will it hide itself in the sky by day?

**Shrivasa** *(looking ahead)*. Here comes Gangadasa. We must question him.

**Gangadasa** *(approaching)*. O venerable ones, O greatly fortunate ones! What is this sudden commotion?

**All.** Oh! He too is searching for Him with all his might — since he is asking us himself.

**Advaita** *(with tears)*.

> **17.** O Lord Vishvambhara, O treasury of virtues, O ocean of love,
> O Bhagavan, who descended to raise the wretched, O wish-fulfilling gem of the devotees!
> Blinding our eyes, plunging the quarters into pitch darkness,
> emptying the minds of all living beings — for what fault are You leaving us?

**Murari.** O venerable Advaita! You are inconceivably deep — how can you lament so without having found anything out yet? From such remorse of people like you, poor lady Shachi will waste away all the more.

**Shrivasa.** Murari speaks the truth. For:

> **18.** Alas, He is her only son, her only eye,
> the only thing in which she feels her happiness;
> though she is His mother, she sees in Him her guru and her god,
> and without Him she will not live a moment.

Therefore now only one thing befits us — to preserve her life. So, O Gangadasa, she trusts your words: speak to her so that she does not part with her life.

**Gangadasa.** As you command. *(Goes out.)*

**Gadadhara** *(with compassion)*. O Master!

> **19.** One watch has passed, ah, two watches have passed, alas, these watches too
> have passed; woe, and the day too has almost passed;
> little by little, alas, ah, together with the breath of life the noose of hope is breaking —
> and still no news of You has stepped onto the road of our ears.

*(Faints.)*

**Vakreshvara.**

> **20.** "Will You really go away and leave us?" — O ocean of mercy, why, last night
> You showed us so many mercies, juicy with the abundant radiance of love!
> What then is this mercy of Yours — and what is this great neglect?
> Alas, O lord of my heart, O Master — this duality of Yours is beyond the world![^20]

*(Faints.)*

**Murari** *(in agitation)*.

> **21.** The steadiness one puts on outwardly
> is undermined by the weight of tears hidden within —
> so a swollen stream of water washes away a dam of sand,
> however often one builds it up again.

*(Sobbing and sobbing, he falls to the ground weeping.)*

**Shrivasa** *(looking at him)*. Oh, even he, so deep, has become irrepressibly tremulous with love. So it is:

> **22.** A stream of water remains calm only
> until it breaks the dam;
> but when the dam is broken, it is irresistible
> and able to flood everything.

O Lord Vishvambhara! Where are You, where are You?

> **23.** Before, I was dead — why, alas, was I brought back to life?
> Having revived me, are You now killing me again?
> Your cruel play, O Almighty, is beyond the grasp of the mind;
> truly the Lord has nothing but childish games.[^21]

*(Weeps.)*

**Mukunda.**

> **24.** Will our eyes no longer see Your face?
> Will our ears no longer hear Your speech?
> O lord of our life, Bhagavan — what use to us, abandoned by You,
> is this tormenting, accursed life?

**Jagadananda** *(with tears)*.

> **25.** We never thought that such as we, deprived of nearness to Your lotus feet,
> would live even a moment;
> that is why we are ashamed, O beloved, O Lord —
> for we go on living whole days without seeing You.

*(Loses consciousness.)*

**Damodara.** O lord of my life! Where are You, where are You?

> **26.** Breaths of life, why do you not depart? Cast off your torpor altogether:
> the lord of life is wandering now all alone.
> Go to His lotus feet and truly serve them —
> ah, let no stain fall on the family of those whose soul is love![^22]

*(Loses consciousness.)*

**Haridasa.** Oh, woe!

> **27.** If the breaths of life did not depart at once together with the lord of life,
> now they can no longer depart in a single moment:
> ceaselessly they endure the bitterness of myriads of reproaches;
> they themselves do not weaken, but wear out the one who bears them.[^23]

So be it — let me think a little.

> **28.** If that Lord does not come onto the road of my eyes,
> if in mercy He does not cast a glance at one such as I,
> then you, breaths of life, hard as the diamond thunderbolt, were you a thousand,
> I shall cast off in an instant like a straw, in my longing to reach His feet.

*(Collecting himself, he acts deep thought.)*

**Vidyanidhi**.[^24]

> **29.** O love, obeisance to you — for nowhere are you born
> without pretence! Alas, alas, what shall I say?
> Even towards Him whose mercy is without pretence you are pretended —
> otherwise how could I still remain bound to life?

*(Falls to the ground and, losing control of himself, sobs.)*

**Murari** *(collecting himself)*. Hey, venerable ones! Consider this: has the Lord gone somewhere alone, or has someone else gone with Him?

**Advaita.** How can that be determined?

> **30.** No one on the road saw the moon Gaura departing:
> like a mass of lightning, He suddenly vanished from sight.[^25]

**Murari.** There is a way to determine it.

**All.** What is it?

**Murari.** Let us check among ourselves: who is not here?

**All.** Well said. *(They confer among themselves.)*

**Murari.** Oh, I understand!

**All.** What is it?

**Murari.** Magnanimous Nityananda and Acharya-ratna are not here.

**All.** What does that signify?

**Murari.** If they were here, in such a calamity they would surely have come here.

**All** *(somewhat reassured)*. Ah, whatever becomes of us — if He is not alone, it is already a little easier for us.

**Advaita.** Mukunda! Console His mother with this news. Say this: "Mother, do not worry about Him. The Lord has gone somewhere with Nityananda and Acharya-ratna on a special errand and will soon return."

**Mukunda.** As you command. *(Goes out.)*

**Advaita.** Ho, favourites of the Lord Vishvambhara! Some steadiness seems to have come into my heart, since these two faithful ones are with Him. As long as they are with Him, the Lord, though independent, will not act wilfully. But, alas, what is His purpose, for which He has conceived this?

> **31.** If it is a pilgrimage, why hide?
> We could all have gone with Him.
> And if it is a desire arising from special love for those two,
> it could quite well have been fulfilled here.

*(All, after reflecting a moment, stand silent.)*

**Behind the scenes.** Ah, alas, woe!

> **32.** Woe, alas, oh, three days have passed, and they have not heard even a word;
> are they alive — or already dead? Or have they found refuge in a swoon?
> If they are still alive — having seen their beloved Lord like this,[^26] O fate,
> how shall I, returning, appear before them?

So better to remain here and try to part with the body.

**All** *(acting as if listening)*. Oh, it sounds like the voice of Acharya-ratna. For tears and sobbing one cannot make it out properly. Let us listen attentively. *(All act attention.)*

**Again behind the scenes.** Ah, ah, why did I, wretch, not go with Him? Or rather:

> **33.** Shiva, Shiva! The Lord cannot be overcome by force:
> the Lord Himself places His will in the hearts of His own.
> The sun displays its radiance in the sunstone —
> and the stone cannot prevent it, though it burns the stone itself.[^27]

**All** *(having listened)*. It really is Acharya-ratna. It seems he has come back after parting from the Lord — for he says: "Shiva, Shiva! The Lord cannot be overcome by force." Ah, so somehow we have obtained a seed of consolation — but one as if roasted by the forest fire of evil fate.[^28]

**Murari.** I think this: Nityananda is with Him, and this one seems to have been sent here on some other errand.

**Advaita.** What errand could He have here? He needs no money, to send for it; nor has He such attachment to His mother as to send him to console her; nor have we had such good fortune that He should send him to bring us.[^29] Enough of these doubts! Only I do not know what further fruit the poison tree of evil fate has borne for such as I. *(Stands absorbed in thought.)*

**Again behind the scenes.** Ah, woe! I am truly wretched.

> **34.** Why did I not follow Him, following and following? Alas, ah, having seen
> Him in such a form, why were my eyes not burnt with pain?
> When the Lord said "Go!", why did life not leave me?
> Ah, ah, Vishvambhara, I am deceived by that maya of Yours!

**All** *(having listened, turning towards the back of the stage)*. Ho! We are all eagerly waiting to see your face — delay no longer.[^30] *(They stand watching for him.)*

*(Then enters Acharya-ratna.)*

**Acharya-ratna.**

> **35.** Where are those glossy dark curling locks? And where is this rite of shaving the head?[^31]
> Where the weight of the hips in fine clothing? And where — Shiva, Shiva! — this loincloth?

*(Pausing a moment and reflecting.)*

> But this is only what appears to the onlookers; in reality it is not so:
> for all things shine forth in consciousness (*cit*), which is the support of all.

**All** *(approaching, eagerly)*. Acharya! Speak, speak, where is the Lord?

**Acharya-ratna.** Ah, ah, what can this wretch tell?

**Advaita.** Speak, what has happened?

**Acharya-ratna** *(in his ear)*. This.

**Advaita.** Ah, can such a matter be covered with the palm of one's hand? Say it plainly, let everyone hear.

**Acharya-ratna** *(aloud, with tears)*.

> **36.** All those skilful sports of kirtana and dance — alas, woe! — have come to an end;
> all those words, sweet with the play of love and laughter, remain only in our hearts;
> that love and that compassion — Shiva, Shiva! — have become a mere memory:
> by Your sannyasa, O Lord, we have been deprived of all we had.

*(All, hearing it, act fainting.)*

**Gangadasa** *(entering hurriedly)*. Acharya! The Lord's mother asks: "Tell me, is the blessed Lord well?"

*(The Acharya stands, his throat choked with tears.)*

**Advaita.** Tell her in my name:

> **37.** Rama's life in the forest, Krishna's departure to Mathura,
> and this one's rite of sannyasa — three lots that three mothers were destined to bear.[^32]

**Gangadasa.** Ah, she herself guessed it long ago. And she said this:

> **38.** "What are you hiding? Why, He has gone the way of His elder brother.
> For those whose deeds transcend the world, harshness and compassion are equal."

**Advaita.** So it is — she is steadfast. Otherwise would she have had such a son? *(Pausing a moment and reflecting.)* So it is:

> **39.** "Maker of sannyasa, Peace, the Tranquil, Steadfastness, the Supreme Refuge of the world" —
> these names the Lord has now made true.[^33]

And further:

> **40.** In this Bhagavan alone has the great saying come true in the direct sense:
> it applies to Him in its primary meaning, and an interpretation that discards its own sense is not needed here.[^34]

Acharya! Tell everything from the very beginning.

**Acharya-ratna.** Ah, it is only for this that I have stayed alive. Well, at the end of the night, just when the dancing had ended, He took me by the hand and walked a few steps; seeing Nityananda ahead, He said: "You come too," took him along, crossed the heavenly river[^35] and went on. I said: "Lord! Tell me, where are You going alone?" Not listening, He went on in silence; the two of us followed Him step by step, and, coming to a village called Katona,[^36] He approached Keshava Bharati, the king of renunciants. Then the two of us thought to ourselves: "The Lord wishes to accept the fourth ashrama." But even thinking so, we, overpowered by the Lord's might, could not utter a word. The next day He said to me: "Acharya-ratna! Perform the preliminary rites for this ceremony." I asked the Lord: "Lord, what ceremony?" Then the Lord said: "I must do this." At that I, not knowing what to do, was struck dumb, answered nothing and only wept. And then, without my taking part, the whole ceremony was performed according to the rules. What happened afterwards cannot be told in words.

**All** *(having heard, despondently)*. O Lord! How could You decide such a thing? Or is it simply the season when the fruits ripen on the tree of grief for such as we — why reproach the Lord's feet? Ah, ah, even remembered, it cuts our hearts. How could you, Acharya, bear to see it? *(They act agitation.)*

**Advaita.** What name befitting that ashrama did the Lord receive?

**Acharya-ratna.** "Sri Krishna Chaitanya."

**Advaita** *(in amazement)*. Oh, that is just what befits Him!

> **41.** Chaitanya (consciousness) is the essence of Krishna; He is named "Krishna Chaitanya";
> that is why the meaning of the great saying bears fruit here.[^37]

For Keshava Bharati is the Shruti herself, since she is the speech (*bhāratī*) of Keshava, as it is said: "…which I first spoke to Brahma, in which is the dharma whose essence is Me."[^38] So what Keshava Bharati taught is what the Shruti teaches. Speak, then, Acharya, speak:

> **42.** **Advaita.** Is He, Bhagavan, there, or in some other place?
> **Acharya-ratna.** At once the Lord,
> taking on the form of a staff-bearing ascetic,[^39] set out on His way.
> **Advaita.** And said nothing to you?
> **Acharya-ratna.** Blinded by love, stumbling, His chest bathed in streams of tears,
> He does not remember even Himself — ah, alas, what could He have said to me?

**Advaita.** And why did you not follow Him?

**Acharya-ratna.**

> **43.** Nityananda said this: "I shall follow the Lord,
> and then, leading Him from road to road by various devices,
> I shall bring Him to Advaita's house too. But you go, take this news
> to those who suffer, and give comfort to Advaita and the others."

**Advaita.** Blessed are you, Nityananda, blessed! You have conquered by your guileless friendship. Come, let us console the lady Shachi with this news and ourselves act as is fitting.

*(Exeunt all.)*

*Thus ends the fourth act of the* Sri Chaitanya-chandrodaya, *called "The Acceptance of Sannyasa".*

[^1]: *Vijātīya-vāsana*, literally "with impressions (inclinations) of another kind", i.e. alien to family life.

[^4]: In the Kāvyamālā there is a further exchange here. Shachi: "But that is You Yourself." The Lord: "So be it, but still I shall strive for that from which I too shall gain the greatest glory."
[^5]: The reading *paripālakaḥ*, "protector, guardian", is adopted: so in M. Guha, in the edition of J. Vidyasagara (commentary: *rakṣakaḥ*, "defender") and in Krishnadasa Baba's Hindi translation. The electronic text has *paripākaḥ* ("ripened fruit"), the Kāvyamālā OCR the corrupt *pariṣāṅkas*.

[^7]: *Graha*, "planet" and "seizer, one who takes possession": the husband accuses the West of being the haunt of all lovers. The red-hot iron (*paritaptam ayaḥ*) is the image of an ordeal (*divya*): it does not burn the innocent.
[^8]: *Sāyāhna* ("evening", masculine) is represented as the lover of the Western Quarter (feminine). There is a play on words: the sun is called *dinamaṇi*, "the jewel of the day" (Kāvyamālā *dinapati*, "lord of the day"); the evening glow is the red garment slipped from her hips.
[^9]: So in the Gaudiya Grantha Mandira edition (Shrirama, Shrivasa's brother). In the Kāvyamālā the speech is spoken by Shrivasa himself, and before verse 5 there is the stage direction "Then enters the Lord Vishvambhara, accompanied by Shrivasa and the others".
[^10]: *Pāda*, "ray" and "foot"; *kaumuda*, "night-lilies" (*kumuda*) and, by the etymology *ku-mud*, "joy of the earth".
[^11]: In the Kāvyamālā the words "You are the master of the house… show them hospitality" are given to Shrivasa himself (apparently an error in the distribution of speeches).
[^12]: A play of repetitions (*yamaka*): *samayā samayāsādita-… pramodī-pramodīrṇa-…*, "near… who gathered in time…, gladdening… born of joy…".
[^13]: An allusion to the descent of Krishna, who removed from the earth the burden of the demon-kings; here the blows of the feet in the dance are like a massage removing the remnant of her fatigue.
[^14]: *Ulu-nināda*, the jubilant cries of "ulu-ulu" that the women of Bengal raise at auspicious moments.
[^15]: The Kāvyamālā reading *barhiṇayati* ("makes peacocks" — peacocks dance at the thunder of clouds) is followed; the GGM edition has *bahir nayati* ("leads outside").
[^16]: *Viśvambhara*, "the one who sustains (nourishes) the universe": this is also an epithet of the cloud that nourishes the world with rain.
[^17]: The Kāvyamālā reading: *dṛśā … cakra-bhrami-naṭane*; corrupt in GGM (*dṛśo … cakraṁ bhrami naṭane*). The gaze is a garland of lotuses, the tears honey, the brows bees.
[^18]: "The city of the serpents" is the underworld of the Nagas; "the city of the gods" the heaven of Indra; *tāṇḍava*, the wild dance (of Shiva). "Rods of radiance" are the upraised arms.
[^19]: *Pāda*, "ray" and "foot"; *bāla*, "young (morning)" and "child": the infant sun cannot yet walk.
[^20]: The sense: the generous mercies of the past night did not let them even think of His departure; the Lord's mercy and neglect are at the same time inconceivable.

Guha's Bengali translation ("before I had died — why did You revive me?") and Krishnadasa Baba's Hindi translation ("when I was going to my death before, why did You save me?") understand it: the Lord once restored the speaker to life, and now, by departing, kills him again. *Kevala-bāla-līla*, "whose games are nothing but childish sport"; both translations render the last line as a bitter question.
[^22]: The Kāvyamālā readings: *sāmpratam eka eva* ("now alone") and *bhajadhvam addhā* ("truly serve"); corrupt in GGM (*eva eva*, *adhvā*).
[^23]: The Kāvyamālā reading *punar jhaṭiti naiva bhavanti gantum*; corrupt in GGM (*jhaṭitenaiva*).
[^24]: Vidyanidhi is Pundarika Vidyanidhi, a devotee from Chittagong, a close associate of the Lord.
[^25]: The Kāvyamālā reading *pathi kenāpi* ("by no one on the road"); GGM has *pathikenāpi* ("even by a traveller"), almost the same sense.
[^26]: "Like this", i.e. having already accepted sannyasa. The speaker is Acharya-ratna (Chandrashekhara), returning from Katva.
[^27]: The sunstone (*sūrya-kānta*) emits fire under the sun's rays. The Kāvyamālā reading *sva-kānta-maṇau mahaḥ* ("radiance in its own stone"); GGM has *matvā*.
[^28]: A roasted seed does not sprout: there is hope, but it is not destined to grow.
[^29]: The Kāvyamālā has *asmān lālayitum*, "to caress us"; GGM *ānayitum*, "to bring us".
[^30]: *Mātaḥ-paraṁ* in GGM is apparently a misprint for *ataḥ paraṁ* ("any longer, further").
[^31]: *Kva sa vidhiḥ*, literally "where is that rite". According to M. Guha's commentary this is the *muṇḍana-vidhiḥ*, the rite of shaving the head at initiation into sannyasa; his Bengali translation has: "where are those curls — and where is this shaven-headed form of a sannyasi". The line, like the next one, is built on the contrast of former and present.
[^32]: Kaushalya, the mother of Rama; Yashoda, the mother of Krishna; Shachi.
[^33]: Names of Vishnu from the "Thousand Names" (*Mahābhārata*, Anushasana-parva): *sannyāsa-kṛc chamaḥ śānto niṣṭhā śāntiḥ parāyaṇam*.
[^34]: The great saying (*mahā-vākya*) of the Upanishads, for example "You are That" (*tat tvam asi*). The Advaitins interpret it through *jahad-ajahal-lakṣaṇā*, a figurative meaning in which part of the words' own sense is discarded and part retained. This is the Kāvyamālā reading; GGM has *jahat-svārtha-lakṣaṇā*, "with the discarding of the own sense". The irony: the Lord is God who has taken on the form of a sannyasi, and to Him the identification applies literally.
[^35]: *Suradhunī*, the Ganga.
[^36]: Katona (*kaṭoṅā*, Kāvyamālā *kāṭoyā*) is Katva (Katoya), where the Lord accepted sannyasa.
[^37]: *Caitanya*, "consciousness". The great saying (*prajñānaṁ brahma* and the like) identifies consciousness with Brahman; here it comes true literally: consciousness (Chaitanya) is Krishna Himself.
[^38]: *Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 11.14.3. The name *Keśava-bhāratī*, "the speech of Keshava", is played upon.
[^39]: *Maskarin*, a mendicant ascetic with a bamboo staff, i.e. a sannyasi. Verse 42 is divided between Advaita and Acharya-ratna.
