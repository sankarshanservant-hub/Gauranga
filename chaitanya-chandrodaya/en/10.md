# Act 10. The Great Festival (Mahā-mahotsava)

*The pilgrimage of the Gaudiya devotees headed by Advaita and Shivananda to Purushottama; the bathing festival of Jagannatha seen through the eyes of King Prataparudra and the Queen; Mahaprabhu's meeting with the devotees, and the benediction at the close of the drama.*

*(Enter a certain traveller.)*

**Traveller.** I have heard that every year at the time of Gundicha Advaita Acharya and all the others set out to see Sri Krishna Chaitanya. They are looked after by one named Shivananda, an associate of that same Lord: he removes the obstacles raised by the guards of the crossings, those thorns of the road — the ferry tolls and other exactions[^1] — and leads everyone with him, even chandalas, taking care of them. I too shall seek him out, so as to go along with him. *(Walking a few steps.)* Ah, a good man is to be seen ahead; I must ask him. *(Approaches.)*

*(Enter a servant of Advaita named Gandharva.)*

**Gandharva.** Well, my master Advaita has charged me with this:

> **1.** "The auspicious time to go to Purushottama has come; we are setting out
> at once. Tell Shivananda: let him fix the day of departure
> and write where all of us, travelling quickly, are to
> join together — whether he turns out to be behind us or ahead."

So I am going to him. *(Walks about the stage.)*

**Traveller** *(approaching)*. Brother! Where are you going?

**Gandharva** *(recites the verse "The auspicious time…")*.

**Traveller** *(joyfully, to himself)*. So what I heard is true. And still I shall ask. *(Aloud.)* Brother, does he take a stranger too under his care and lead him along?

**Gandharva.** Brother! He took even a dog under his care and brought it along — what to speak of a man!

**Traveller.** Brother! Tell me how that was.

**Gandharva.** Even before the Lord went to Mathura,[^2] once, when many thousands of people had set out, a certain dog attached itself to Shivananda of its own accord. Shivananda, seeing that it walked beside him step by step, took care of it with respect: at every halting place he gave it unpolluted food at the proper time, and wherever a river or the like had to be crossed, he paid for it separately whatever was required for the ferry[^3] and put it in the boat. And every day it followed at his heels. So they went two-thirds of the way. And then one day a servant forgot and did not give it its food. Afterwards, not seeing it, Shivananda repented: "Ah, today the dog has been given no food!" — he himself called it by name here and there, but never saw it. From that day all the way to Purushottama it was not to be seen, and his heart grew very heavy.

And then, by the will of fate, when Shivananda came to Purushottama, he saw that very dog on the shore of the ocean beside the Lord Chaitanya, who was sitting alone. Shivananda bowed, as one who has offended, and, standing at a distance, watched. The Lord was throwing it bits of coconut pulp — Jagannatha's prasada — and saying: "Say 'Krishna'!" And it ate piece after piece and also uttered: "Krishna, Krishna, Krishna!" Seeing this unheard-of thing, Shivananda bowed to it again and begged forgiveness for his offence. And from that time no one saw it any more. I think that in that same body it took on another form and attained another world.

**Traveller.** Brother! Today is an auspicious day for me: this story of Sri Krishna Chaitanya has become a guest of my ears. The God who made even a dog utter the name of the Lord — what mercy will He not show to the world of men! So tell me, brother, how do the guards of the crossings — those thorns of the way — treat these travellers on the road?

**Gandharva.** Brother, listen.

> **2.** By the power of this God alone, all who follow Him every year
> surely meet with great happiness on the way there and back.

But in some year there may be trouble.

**Traveller.** What trouble?

**Gandharva.** One year, when many thousands of people headed by my master had set out, Shivananda, the guardian of all the travellers, went too with his household. At every crossing he himself stood surety for the toll of all who were going, except my master's retinue, and, himself paying for each one as much as was due by the fixed rate, went on; and the people went on in peace. So they went, and then in the region of Remuna[^4] there appeared a certain official of the Gajapati, the chief over the guards of the crossings, who was acting wilfully there while the great King Gajapati had gone to the southern lands. This villain, passing all bounds, raised the tax, counted the people head by head, listed in order even the past years and, to take the accumulated toll, put Shivananda in wooden stocks. Because of his fetters my master and all the others were in the greatest distress and sat on without performing the day's rites.

**Traveller.** And then?

**Gandharva.** Then, when two watches of the night had passed, that scoundrel sent a servant with a stick for Shivananda. Shivananda then was in great distress: "Will he not beat me?" — and, offering praise[^5] to the feet of Chaitanya, went to him together with his escort. Seeing him just risen from sleep, surrounded by many torch-bearers, he was frightened. Then the official asked him: "Hey, have you come with your household?" He answered: "Yes." He asked again: "Whose man are you?" He answered: "Sri Krishna Chaitanya's." The official again: "You are Chaitanya's, and I am Jagannatha's. Who is greater — Jagannatha or Chaitanya?" He answered: "For me Krishna Chaitanya is greater." Hearing this, the official, brightening with goodwill, said as one at fault: "Hey, I had a dream: Sri Krishna Chaitanya said to me, 'You have put My man in fetters — release him at once.' Forgive me this offence. You owe nothing. In the morning rise in peace and go with everyone." Having said this, he told two torch-bearers: "Take him to where his people are." When he returned, my master and all the others then began the day's rites.

**Traveller.** Ah, truly wondrous are the mercy, power and might of Chaitanya-deva!

**Gandharva.** And where are you from?

**Traveller.** I am from Northern Radha.[^6]

**Gandharva.** Why alone?

**Traveller.** Narahari Dasa and the others sent me.

**Gandharva.** What for?

**Traveller.** To find out when he will go to Purushottama.

**Gandharva.** Then wait here, by my master's abode, until I find out and come back. Some ten more people going there, like you, my master himself has left here, saying: "You will go with me."

**Traveller.** How have they deserved such favour from your master?

**Gandharva.** One of them is supremely lovable, like an elixir for people's eyes, young, beautiful in form, full outside and inside of the juice of love for Sri Krishna, born in him together with himself — a moon of the brahmin race named Shrinatha. Seeing this captivating youth, my master was greatly pleased. And he said to him: "I myself shall show you Sri Krishna Chaitanya in private; do not go with others."[^7] For his sake he has been providing for the needs of these ten for a month already, ever since leaving home.

**Traveller.** Very well, I shall wait for you here.

**Gandharva.** And I shall go for Shivananda. *(Exeunt both.)*

*— Interlude (vishkambhaka) —*

*(Enter Shivananda, and with him several others who wish to go.)*

**Shivananda** *(to one of them)*. Hey, where are you from?

**He.** Great soul! Govardhana Dasa has sent me to you.

**Shivananda.** Ah, I see. You will go to seek out Raghunatha Dasa.

**He.** Just so.

**Shivananda.** And why seek him out?

**He.**[^8] Venerable sir! Are you acquainted with him?

**Shivananda.** Listen, then:

> **3.** The preceptor Yadunandana,[^9] sweetest, beloved of Sri Vasudeva —
> his disciple Raghunatha, rich in virtues, dearer than life to such as I,
> ever anointed with the generous mercy of Sri Chaitanya, a follower of Svarupa,
> the sole treasury of renunciation — to whom among those living on Nilachala is he not known?

And further:

> **4.** He is a certain blessed soil, the one delight of the hearts of all people,
> that bears fruit without ploughing; on it the tree of love for that Lord,
> as soon as it was planted, at once became fruitful, having no equal.

And still let us go; I myself shall take you under my care and lead you. The delay is only until the command of Advaita-deva has been received. *(Ponders.)*

*(Enter Gandharva.)*

**Gandharva.** Ah, here is Shivananda. Let me approach. *(Approaches.)*

**Shivananda** *(looking ahead)*. Ah, ahead, it seems, is a servant of the Acharya Gosvami. Good: what I was thinking of has come to pass. *(Approaches.)*

**Gandharva.** Venerable sir! My master Advaita Gosvami commands you.

**Shivananda.** I am all attention. Speak.

**Gandharva.** "The auspicious time…" *(and so on; recites the verse again)*.

**Shivananda.** I myself am waiting for his command.

**Gandharva.** There is something special too.

**Shivananda.** What is it?

**Gandharva.** This year the bathing festival (*snāna-yātrā*) too must be seen.[^10]

**Shivananda.** That is just what everyone desires. Go and inform the masters: here I am, having fixed the days, setting out for the Lord's feet. And meanwhile I shall go there to settle the matter with Shrivasa Pandita and the others. You go too. *(Exeunt.)*

*(Enter Sarvabhauma, without drawing the curtain.[^11])*

**Sarvabhauma.** Although the Lord did not give His consent, still I am setting out on my own for Varanasi, to make them accept the Lord's teaching[^12] — so on my own I am going there. I do not know what will come of it. Although the Lord's mercy depends on His will, yet He Himself is subject to mercy, so that at times mercy too becomes independent. What will happen with the help of mercy itself — that will be. *(Walks about the stage and looks ahead.)* Ah, ahead are seen many people from various lands gathered in one place — they must be pilgrims. *(Gazing again.)* Ah, they are all Gaudiyas! *(Looking more closely.)* Here he is, here is Advaita Acharya! Here he is, here is Nityananda the avadhuta! Here he is, here is Shrivasa! Here he is, here is Haridasa! Here he is, here is Gadadhara Dasa! These are Govinda Ghosha and the rest. This is Makaradhvaja. This is Kashinatha. This is Narahari and his companions. These are Ramananda and the others from Kulina-grama. These are Nityananda's companions, Gauri Dasa and the rest. What more is there to say — all the associates of Sri Chaitanya to the last one are coming! Good: I shall stop here today and converse with each of them. *(Approaches.)*

*(Enter Advaita and the others, who have set out to see the Lord.)*

**Advaita.** Ah, ahead seems to be Sarvabhauma. What does this mean?

**Sarvabhauma** *(approaches and bows to Advaita and to the others too; seeing Haridasa at a distance)*. Haridasa, who looks not to lineage and caste — obeisance to you!

*(Haridasa, stepping further back, bows in embarrassment.)*

**Advaita.** Sarvabhauma! How did you consent to separation from the lotus feet of Sri Krishna Chaitanya?

**Sarvabhauma.** This is how it was. *(Tells everything.)*

**Advaita.** Well, then, let everyone rest here today. We must converse with the Bhattacharya.

**All.** As you please. *(Each settles in his place.)*

**Shrikanta** *(to Shivananda)*. Venerable uncle! If you allow me, I shall go ahead.

**Shivananda.** Go, as you please.

*(Shrikanta bows and goes out.)*

**Advaita.** Come, Bhattacharya, come! Let us settle down, and in due time we shall hear everything. *(Exeunt.)*

*(Enter Svarupa and Govinda.)*

**Svarupa.** I have heard that Advaita and all the others are coming from Gauda.

**Govinda.** Yes. Shrikanta has just come, having left them halfway.

**Svarupa.** Where is he?

**Govinda.** He is sitting and talking with Mahaprabhu.

**Svarupa.** Then we too shall listen. *(They approach.)*

*(Enter Mahaprabhu, sitting comfortably together with the Puri master;[^13] at a distance, Shrikanta.)*

**Mahaprabhu.** Shrikanta! Tell Me, who all is coming?

**Shrikanta.** Lord! All are Yours. This year no one has stayed behind there. And some have not yet seen Your beautiful feet.

**Svarupa** *(approaching)*. Glory, glory to Mahaprabhu!

**Mahaprabhu.** Come here, come, Svarupa! *(Seats him beside Himself.)*

*(Shrikanta bows to Svarupa.)*

**Mahaprabhu.** Shrikanta! Well, and then? Who are they — those whom I have not seen before?

**Shrikanta.** Lord! Advaita Acharya's sons — Vishnu Dasa, Gopala Dasa and others. And also with Advaita comes one named Shrinatha, dear to all people.

**Mahaprabhu.** Why does he come with him, leaving the company of Shivananda?

**Shrikanta.** He said to him: "I shall bring you to Mahaprabhu in private and obtain His special mercy for you" — and so he relied on this promise.

**Mahaprabhu** *(smiling, to Svarupa)*. Listen:

> **5.** "This is Advaita's offering; it will prove very sweet" — therefore
> some love must be shown to him; and you, Svarupa, be a friend to him;
> and you, O Shankara,[^14] awaken the sweetest feeling for him:
> for the union of those alike in nature is sweet to all.

**Both.** As the Lord commands.

**Mahaprabhu.** Who else?

**Shrikanta.** The son of Vasudeva[^15] and my uncle's two sons.

**Mahaprabhu.** Those two I have already seen.

**Shrikanta.** But the youngest[^16] has not yet seen Your beautiful feet.

**Mahaprabhu** *(to the Puri master)*. Svami, this is your servant.[^17]

**Shrikanta.** Lord, so it is.

**Mahaprabhu.** Well, and then?

**Shrikanta.** The son of Ramananda Basu. And others likewise.

**Mahaprabhu.** Svami Puri master! Ho, Svarupa! This year, for the sake of these, those too will get the chance to see Me.[^18]

**Both** *(to themselves)*. Ah, what can be the meaning of these words? Well, it will become clear of itself.

**Mahaprabhu.** This year the Acharya will meet the King.

**Shrikanta.** Master! I have come from afar, so I know nothing of this.

**Puri master and Svarupa** *(to themselves)*. Ah, I see! Last year Advaita Acharya conversed with the King — that reproach still lives in the Lord's heart.

**Mahaprabhu.** Puri master! It is just the conduct of Vasudeva that pleases Me.[^19]

**Puri.** Truly he is fortunate: the Lord praises him even in his absence.

*(Behind the scenes, noise.)*

**Puri** *(having listened)*. Master! They are already very near — this clamour is heard.

**Mahaprabhu.** Govinda! Take a garland — the Lord's prasada — and go out to meet them.

**Govinda.** As You command. *(Goes out.)*

**Vaninatha** *(enters, bows and, joining his palms)*. Master! Here is mahaprasada — rice and other dishes of the Lord Sri Jagannatha.

**Mahaprabhu.** Vaninatha! You know well when what is needed: you have done this, realising that Advaita and the others are arriving now. Until Govinda comes, put it somewhere yourself. *(Vaninatha does so.)*

**Kashi Mishra** *(entering)*. Lord! Tomorrow is the great bathing festival of the Lord.

**Mahaprabhu.** Yes, I know. But, Mishra, see to it that this year My Gaudiyas, all of them down to the last simple man,[^20] may watch the great bathing festival in peace.

**Kashi Mishra.** Master! The King has commanded me: "This year none of mine — neither the queens nor the others — will watch the bathing of God. Where they formerly watched the bathing of God, standing on the circular enclosure[^21] — there let all the Gaudiyas go up."

**Mahaprabhu.** May all good be his!

*(Again behind the scenes, the sounds of sankirtana.)*

**Puri.** Master! They have already reached Chara-Ganesha.[^22]

**Mahaprabhu.** Svarupa! You go ahead to meet them, and then I shall come too.

**Svarupa.** As You command. *(Goes out.)*

**Puri** *(to himself)*.

> **6.** A great reproach was shown — and now the Lord, loving Advaita,
> shows him still greater honour.
> Such is the greatness of friendship: though friends, by the nature of the body,[^23] have both virtues and faults,
> the eye sees only the virtues and does not catch on the faults.

**Mahaprabhu.** Puri master, rise, let us too go to meet them.

**Puri.** Rise, Gosvami! *(They go out of the town to meet them.)*

*(Enter Advaita, doubly gladdened at having received the garland, the Lord's prasada, overflowing with the bliss of the dance; around him Shivananda and the others, singing kirtana. Advaita, seeing Mahaprabhu coming from afar, dances and falls to the ground.)*

**Shivananda** *(addressing his son, but so that all may hear)*. Look, look, here He is, here!

> **7.** In lustre a garland of lightning, in gait the playful stride of the king of lions full of eager longing,
> His arms long and mighty like golden bolts;
> His neck like a lion's, His garment gleaming with the lustre of the rising sun —
> Sri Gauranga shines before us. Bow, bow down!

*(All bow to the ground, and when they rise, their eyes again seek the Lord, who has entered the circle of Advaita.)*

**Shivananda** *(gazing)*.

> **8.** When Advaita and Chaitanya embraced tightly,
> no one could distinguish who was who:
> for a moment people see Advaita in Chaitanya,
> and Chaitanya in Advaita.[^24]

Look, then:

> **9.** The Lord, putting Advaita in front, came forward, wishing to see him,
> and now leads him into His abode.
> Wait — let everyone enter in order.

*(Holding everyone back, he lets them in in order. Mahaprabhu sits down and, having gladdened each one — Advaita and the others — as is fitting, with embraces, conversation, glances and the rest, with His own hand gives each a full handful of rice, the Lord's prasada. Advaita and the others receive it in order.)*

**Bhagavan.** Ho, today eat nothing more; tonight, at the coming of evening, all of you go up onto the circular enclosure so as to watch the great bathing festival in peace.

**All.** As You command. *(Exeunt.)*

**Svarupa.** Lord! You Yourself should perform the day's rites at the proper time.

**Bhagavan.** As you please. *(Goes out together with Puri Gosvami.)*

**Svarupa.** Kashi Mishra! The King must now be arriving from the capital — not for Gundicha?

**Behind the scenes.** He has arrived!

**Kashi Mishra.** Gosvami! Judging by this cry, he seems to have arrived.

**Svarupa.** Then it will be inconvenient for him to watch the bathing himself.[^25]

**Kashi Mishra.** I know how matters stand. *(Goes out.)*

**Svarupa.** Govinda! Let us follow the Lord. *(Exeunt.)*

*(Enter the King, on the terrace of the palace,[^26] and the priest.)*

**King.** Priest! This year I shall watch the bathing while staying here. Otherwise the Lord Gauranga will be inconvenienced.

**Priest.** That is quite fitting.

**King.** Ho, who is there? Call Kashi Mishra!

**Kashi Mishra** *(entering)*. Here I am. Command, Sire.

**King.** Mishra!

> **10.** The Gaudiyas who are here — the associates of the Lord or their kinsmen,
> or those who follow them, or those with servants —
> let them all sit where my wives, sons and friends, settling down,
> watch the bathing with great comfort.

**Kashi Mishra.** Great King! By your[^27] command all this has already been done.

**King.** Well arranged.

**Chamberlain** *(entering)*. Sire! The queens report: "We came to watch the bathing of God, and it has not worked out."

**King.** How can it not work out? Let them too watch while staying here. Look: everything has been prepared for it.

**Kashi Mishra.** Sire! Look, look:

> **11.** Having climbed the freshly whitewashed tower opposite the bathing place,
> covered by the moonbeams, all these devotees
> shine like gods seated in the midst of heaven.

**King.** It is good that you have placed these associates of Sri Krishna Chaitanya there. And now go: the time of Jagannatha's coming out seems already near. Do not delay the proper duties.

**Kashi Mishra.** As you command. *(Goes out.)*

**Queens.** Glory, glory to the great King!

**King** *(to the Queen)*. Come here, Queen, come! Make your birth fruitful. *(Praising her, he seats her.)* Queen, look, look:

> **12.** Here are the associates of Chaitanya-deva, who purify the universe;
> very soon you will see Him too.

Bow to them, then. *(The Queen bows.)*

**King.** Look, look, what a great wonder!

> **13.** "At the great conjunction of Jyaishtha[^28] the flag on the temple of the Lord
> flies upward" — such is the well-known report;
> and here all of them, with eyes raised in faith, see at once how it
> seems to lick with its tongue the body of the cool-rayed moon.

**Queen.** Sire! This popular report is indeed true.

**King.** Look for yourself. *(Shows her the flag flying upward.)*

*(Behind the scenes, the sound of kahala horns.)*

**King** *(having listened)*. Queen, look, look! The time of Jagannatha-deva's coming out has come. So let me see whether Sri Krishna Chaitanya-deva is coming. *(Doing so, joyfully.)* Here He is, here is Sri Krishna Chaitanya! Look, look:

> **14.** Above the dense crowd of people, higher than all heads,
> shines this orb of the Lord's beautiful face —
> behold: as if high above a water surface where swans of many kinds are swimming,
> a certain golden lotus on a high-raised stalk.

**Queen.** Noble husband! Another festival has come to us at our festival: we wished to see Jagannatha — and we have seen Gaurachandra! *(In Sanskrit.)*

> **15.** The stream of His radiance, spreading, is like the fierce touch of the sun
> that dries up the taste for the objects of the senses;
> His beauty is inseparable from Him, He scatters the nectar of love for the Lord —
> who is this, majestic of stature, the golden treasure that has appeared on the path of my eyes?

And further:

> **16.** I would wave moons around His face,[^29]
> I would perform arati to His radiance with golden lamps,
> I would worship His lotus feet with the flowers of my life-breaths,
> and repay His mercy with hundreds of thousands of bodies at least!

*(Bows.)*

**King.** Your feeling is true. But look: Sri Jagannatha too has mounted the bathing platform.

*(The Queen looks and bows. Behind the scenes, again the sound of kahalas.)*

**King** *(having listened)*. Queen, look, look — the bathing of Sri Jagannatha!

**Queen** *(turning her gaze now to one, now to the other)*. Noble husband! This is truly a marvel!

**King.** Queen, what is it?

**Queen** *(in Sanskrit)*.

> **17.** Standing opposite each other, unblinking, gazing at each other,
> bathed by an irresistible stream — of the bathing water and of the tears from their eyes —
> two great treasures of compassion alone, the sole medicine that destroys the fear of existence,
> two Gods equal in radiance shine before us, though one is dark and the other golden.

**King.** That is true.

**Queen.** Noble husband! The great bathing festival of God is over: for as soon as He[^30] turns His gaze to the south, Gaurachandra goes off in another direction.

**King.** Just so.

> **18.** Before Him the crowd thins out,
> and behind Him it thickens again;
> and His associates, joining hands,
> reverently form a circle around Him.

**Behind the scenes.**

> **19.** When the Lord of the universe entered the period of *anavasara*,[^31]
> the lord of ascetics, alas, fell into a state full of the torments of separation.
> For He is the embodiment of the pure bliss of love, and when
> He becomes absorbed in anything, He at once becomes that.

**King** *(having listened)*. Ah, it is undoubtedly Kashi Mishra speaking. Let me hear the end of his speech. *(Stands listening attentively.)*

**Again from there.**

> **20.** Neither bathing, nor the rite of watering the tulasi, nor beholding the chakra,[^32]
> nor repeating the names, nor the series of obeisances — alas, He does not even accept alms;
> under the pretext of the *anavasara* of the Moon of Nilachala,[^33] of His own will
> taking upon Himself the torment of separation from His own Self, He ceaselessly, motionless, weeps.

**King.** Ah, trouble! If it is so the whole time of the *anavasara*, what will happen then?

**Again from there.**

> **21.** There is no other means than the bliss of sankirtana
> glorifying the Beloved; and so that the rasa may change,[^34]
> it is just this that Svarupa is undertaking.

**King.** Blessings on you, blessings! *(To the Queen.)* My dear, go away from here: meanwhile I shall call this Kashi Mishra.

**Queen.** As you command. *(Goes out.)*

**Kashi Mishra** *(entering)*. Glory, glory to the King!

**King.** Mishra! Tell me, what was it you were saying?

**Kashi Mishra.** What I said — so it is.

**King.** Tell me what Svarupa Gosvami has conceived.

**Kashi Mishra.** When the Lord became absorbed in such an experience of the distress of separation, Svarupa, to dispel it, consulted with all the friends: "If this evening, after beholding the procession of Gopinatha,[^35] the Lord hears the sweetest kirtana about the Lord, the rasa will change; and when that happens, the possession by separation will weaken." Having consulted thus, some of His closest, who know His heart, began near the Rohini-kunda[^36] the sweetest of sweet kirtanas.

**King.** Mishra! How could one see it?

**Kashi Mishra.** If you go up onto the temple terrace.[^37]

**King.** So I shall. *(Acts going up there together with him.)*

*(Behind the scenes, the roar of the sweetest sankirtana.)*

**Kashi Mishra** *(gazing)*. O great King, look, look:

> **22.** He who by day was like the pain of separation embodied,
> the rasa of compassion (*karuṇa*) embodied —
> now, from the sounds of kirtana, has become quite different.

**King.** So it is:

> **23.** His body, sprouting with bliss, whatever feeling (*bhāva*) it touches,
> that very feeling it shows outwardly:
> a crystal vessel filled with whatever juices
> takes on their colour, displaying them.

*(Again behind the scenes, the sounds of singing.)*

**King** *(having listened)*. What song is this?

**Kashi Mishra.** It describes the sweetness of the sound of the Lord's flute and is composed in the Gauda language;[^38] that is why the King does not understand it.

**King.** Ah, a marvel! For He —

> **24.** Gaura, who Himself is reflected in the minds of the virtuous as Krishna,
> dances here on Niladri and spreads the rasa of Vrindavana —
> a certain Primeval Person relishing the torment of love for Krishna
> of a young, pining bride. Wonder, oh, marvel, ah, ah — the pastimes of Chaitanya!

*(Gazing again.)* Ah! For a long time now they have been singing the refrain (*dhruva*) of one and the same line of the song.

**Kashi Mishra.** Into whatever pastime (lila) His mind sinks first, from that He does not come back.

**King.** So it is. *(Looks again, joyfully.)* Ah, how great is His sweetness! Look, look:

> **25.** He flings up His knees, waves His arms, steps, casts glances,
> oh, gladdening the hearts of His friends and stunning the universe —
> and His face, charmingly smiling with great bliss, shines covered with the foam of saliva,
> like a golden lotus covered with foam, or the moon with thickened frost.

*(Looks again, with amazement.)* Ah!

> **26.** Who is this, without any timidity, gathering the saliva from His face,
> drinking it and exulting? Ah, it is a chakora
> drinking the sparkling foam of the nectar-liquid
> as if poured out from the moon![^39]

**Kashi Mishra.** It is a Vaishnava named Shubhananda.

**King.** Ah, still the same single refrain of the song, the same high pitch, the same fervour of the singers — for two watches now! And in the Lord the same wonder of the manifestation of bliss. Yes, with this other rasa they have driven back the waves of the possession by separation; but with what will they now interrupt this new rasa? One way or another the Lord has missed the day's rites.

**Kashi Mishra.** And yet the possession by separation is unbearable for the devotees. *(Gazing again.)* Sire! The festival of dance is over: here they are leading the Lord to His abode.

**King.** Good, good! Perhaps He will perform the day's rites too. And now, Mishra, go to the Lord. And I shall sleep here a little.

**Kashi Mishra.** As you command. *(Goes out.)*

**King.** The Mishra has gone — I shall sleep here. *(Acting sleep, he rises again.)* Ah, this night has already dawned. For:

> **27.** The cool-rayed and the hot-handed, drawing near to the slopes
> of the mountain of sunset and the mountain of sunrise, equal in radiance through mildness,
> at dawn for a moment become as it were the eyes
> of this Most Ancient One.[^40]

*(Looks all around.)*

**Behind the scenes.** Ah, what a great wonder!

> **28.** Every morning, when Advaita, whose hair stands on end from inner bliss,
> worships the Lord with arghya, sandal, tulasi, flowers and the rest,
> God with a smile forcibly takes them away and with great rapture worships Advaita with them,
> and with His fingers at His mouth, gurgling, imitates the playing of musical instruments.[^41]

And further:

> **29.** He whom Advaita-deva, wishing to perform puja,
> placing in the hollow of the lotus of his palms the articles for the worship of the Lord,
> sends every day with joy — Shrinatha then
> was filled in heart by even a little of the Lord, the treasury of virtues, seeing him, touching him,
> speaking to him with love and casting on him a merciful glance.[^42]

**King.** Ah, what is Tulasi Mishra, the pariksha-mahapatra,[^43] about to report? I must hear it: he is hurrying straight here.

**Tulasi Mishra** *(entering)*. Sire! The Ratha-yatra of Jagannatha is already near,[^44] and Mahaprabhu Himself has gone there to sweep and wash the temple of Gundicha.

**King.** It pleases me, it pleases me! Was there any command?

**Tulasi Mishra.** Whatever was commanded, Kashi Mishra has already carried out.

**King.** What was it?

**Tulasi Mishra.** As many people as He has — so many brooms, and even more pitchers.

**King.** Only that?

**Tulasi Mishra.** What else would He need?

*(Behind the scenes, noise.)*

**Tulasi Mishra.** Look, look:

> **30.** Having anointed with His own hand the body of each of them with sandal juice[^45]
> and put on their necks garlands — remnants from the Lord,
> handing them brooms like clusters of blossoms of the tree of jubilation,
> Gaura goes out, stepping unhurriedly like a tall intoxicated elephant.

And further:

> **31.** Joyfully they go out — on chariots of desires, on elephants of satisfaction,
> on horses of jubilation — like warriors, victors in the battle with existence,
> their bodies clad in armour of hair standing on end; with ceaselessly flowing
> tears they bear, as it were, the spell of Varuna,[^46] and in rapture thunder cries of "hum!".

**King.** Shame on royal rank! When shall I, becoming at least someone among them, follow the Lord? Tulasi Mishra! How could I hear about this wonder from beginning to end?

**Tulasi Mishra.** I have a certain man; he has found out everything and will come at any moment.

**King.** It pleases me, it pleases me!

**A certain man** *(entering hurriedly and bowing to the King)*. Great King! I have seen everything and have come.

**King.** Tell it.

**Man.** Sire, attend:

> **32.** Taking a broom in His tender, lovely hand, higher and higher
> He Himself, together with everyone, inside the pavilion of Gundicha
> sweeps away the cobwebs[^47] and dirty dust — and, covered with them,
> Gaura became like the moon with its mark clearly showing.

Then:

> **33.** Lifting someone onto another's shoulders — to where a hand cannot reach —
> ah, saying in a voice deep as the thunder of clouds, "Do not fear!" —
> raising His eyes, His body covered with dust, cleaning everything higher and higher,
> God cleaned the walls, then the throne, then the floor too.

And further:

> **34.** Gathering the rubbish with a broom into the edge of His upper cloth,
> He fills it and carries it out Himself;
> and here and there, where His hand can reach, He nimbly scrubs off stains;
> with the throng of His friends He both sings and, sporting, makes them sing.

**King.** And then, and then?

**Man.** So, having cleaned the main pavilion, the Jaganmohana[^48] and the bhoga-mandapa —

> **35.** Some draw water from the wells, someone hands a pitcher into another's hands,
> he into the hands of the next, and that one to yet another, and the water ends up in someone's hands;
> so, passing pitchers along a chain, carrying the full ones on and leaving the empty ones,
> the people taught a lesson on how to accept the full and reject the empty.[^49]

And further:

> **36.** Some, heeding Gaura's word, with joy in their hearts pour water on the throne,
> others on the walls, and someone hands water into His hands;
> some from pitchers joyfully pour water on His lotus feet,
> and that water some drink from cupped hands, and others pour on their heads.

**King.** And then, and then?

**Man.** Then, having washed everything in order as before, all, with their own feet washed, drove off the water with their garments in order. Having done so, they went down into the courtyard and, when they began to clean it —

> **37.** when His people had sat down in rows, He, jesting, sitting in their midst,
> gathered a full lap of His cloth of the pebbles that had long accumulated in the courtyard
> and said: "Let us see who gathers how many!" — and then
> all of them joyfully, vying with one another, set about gathering.

So, having made the whole courtyard on every side faultlessly clean like their lives and smooth like their hearts,[^50] they began the kirtana of the Lord. Namely:

> **38.** Causing a thrill in the doe-eyed Earth, a halt[^51] in the sun, trembling in the maidens of the quarters,
> stupor in the wind, tears in the thousand eyes of the lord of the immortals,
> sweat in the assembly of the seven rishis, jubilation full of supreme rasa in the son of Uttanapada,[^52]
> and the collapse of meditation in Brahma — victorious be the sound of the bliss of the Lord's kirtana!

And then —

> **39.** Having danced for a moment, charmingly sweet, Gaura Hari
> made Advaita's son dance, the uniquely dear one named Gopala Dasa.
> He, dancing, lost consciousness from bliss, as if passing into another body;
> and when Advaita lamented, God revived him, passing His lotus hand over him.

And then the cry "Hari!" rang out loudly.

**King.** Ah, my evil lot — I did not see this either!

**Man.** Then, having cleaned the mandapa of Narasimhanatha as well, all hurried to the lake of Indradyumna, sported in the water and rested in the flower garden beside it. And then all partook of the rice and other prasada of the Lord brought by Vaninatha Pattanayaka.

**Behind the scenes.**

> **40.** "Tomorrow all people will celebrate a festival of the eyes,
> beholding the beautiful face of Shripati" — as if from this thought
> this festival of the heart was born —
> the great festival even of the great festival.[^53]

**King.** Mahapatra! Judging by how loudly this Kashi Mishra is speaking, I think he wants just you to hear it.[^54] So go now and see to what has been done and what has not for the Netrotsava.

**Tulasi Mishra.** As you command. *(Goes out.)*

*(Enter Kashi Mishra.)*

**Kashi Mishra.** Ah, how sweet it will be!

> **41.** In front, Kashishvara, parting the throngs of people,
> behind, Govinda, guarding His playful gait,
> on both sides, the Puri master and Svarupa —
> so Gaurachandra will go to the festival of the eyes.

**King.** Ah, the Mishra is conversing with his own mind and does not even notice me.

**Kashi Mishra** *(glancing to the right)*. Ah, all three chariots are already fully adorned! Especially this chariot of Sri Jagannatha:

> **42.** Its beauty is set off by thousands of gleaming mirrors,
> it is surrounded by heaps of lovely chamara whisks and fine Chinese silk;
> all made of radiance, shining as the hour approaches,
> gladdening the eyes, the chariot gleams.

*(Looking straight ahead.)* Ah, the King is here! *(Approaches.)* Glory, glory to the great King! Great King, you should, staying here, watch the Lord mount the chariot, and then, after bathing, perform your service.[^55]

**King.** The service at the chariot procession, though it is prescribed for me, does not draw me as much as the desire to see Mahaprabhu's dance.

**Kashi Mishra.** When the Lord has mounted the chariot, after half a watch Mahaprabhu's dance will begin.

**King** *(reflecting)*.

> **43.** If I watch from here, my heart will not be satisfied;
> if I watch from there, that too is not at all easy:
> to enter His circle is impossible for me, for He is surrounded by them;
> and since He is inside the circle, how am I to see His dance?

And still, while the dance lasts, I must stay there; my only refuge is the goddess of His mercy.

**Kashi Mishra.** Great King! The queens, through the chamberlain, have asked permission to stay here, so as to see the dance of the Lord Gaurachandra.

**King.** Let them watch in peace and make their eyes and their birth fruitful.

**Kashi Mishra.** Then hurry: the time when the Lord will mount the chariot is already near.

**King.** So it is.

**Kashi Mishra.** I too am going to the Lord. *(Exeunt both.)*

*(Enter the queens, who have gone up to the same terrace; the chamberlain approaches them.)*

**Chamberlain.** Queens, look:

> **44.** The Moon of Nilachala has reached the chariot[^56] — together with the eyes and minds of all embodied beings;
> Gaura Hari has reached the chariot's path —
> and their eyes and minds, seized by feeling, have left — no, not the former;
> but of the former and the latter, truly, the latter is the stronger.[^57]

And further:

> **45.** Surrounded by three circles of His people,
> He triumphs, golden-fair —
> like the seed-cup of a lotus
> that has opened a thousand shining petals.

*(The queens gaze longingly and bow.)*

**Chamberlain.** Queens, look, look!

> **46.** Kashishvara has become the chief in the outer ring,
> Govinda, most excellent, in the middle one,
> in the inner one, like a precious gem, Svarupa triumphs,
> and the Puri master is the spectator before the Lord Himself.

*(Gazing a moment.)* Ah, what a great marvel!

> **47.** Out of modesty he does not push aside the people who have taken refuge at Chaitanya's feet,
> and because they have surrounded Gaura closely, without a gap, he does not see Him;
> longingly casting glances here and there, seeking a gap,
> the King wanders about, leaning with his hand on the shoulder of Harichandana.[^58]

**Queens.** As is his longing, so is his conduct.

**Chamberlain.** Ah, a mishap![^59] Look, look:

> **48.** Here Harichandana, to open a view for the King from afar,
> slightly pushes Shrivasa aside with his hand;
> he in anger struck him — and when the other grew angry in return,
> the King himself pacifies him, gently and wisely.

**Queens.** What is wretched Harichandana to such as these, who need nothing?

**Chamberlain.** Look, look:

> **49.** During the wild tandava of the Lord of the world,
> all, joining hands with one another
> and stretching out their arms, ceaselessly widen the circle,
> fearing lest He, falling again and again to the ground, should hurt His body.

**Queens.** Now it can be seen well.

**Chamberlain.** Look, look:

> **50.** Now He leaps like the king of beasts,
> now rushes like an intoxicated elephant,
> now whirls like a wheel of fire —
> the king of renunciants on the waves of bliss.

And further:

> **51.** When Svarupa, first among the generous-hearted, who knows the inner feelings,
> indicates what to sing, all sing just that with love,
> and Gaura, like the meaning of the song embodied, reflecting it, dances wildly
> with stupor, tears, faltering voice, trembling, gooseflesh, sweat, swooning and smiles.

**Queens.** Truly wondrous is this marvel of the bliss of love!

**Chamberlain.** Look, look:

> **52.** I do not know by what high and low waves of the ocean of bliss —
> but when, from the rapture of the madness of the dance, the Lord Gaura fell into a swoon of bliss,
> His saliva congealed, His tears ceased to flow, His breath is not perceived;
> only His radiance, as bright as before, becomes for His friends a pledge of consolation.

**Queens.** Only the awareness that He is Ishvara keeps us alive;[^60] otherwise could one bear to watch this?

**Chamberlain** *(gazing a moment)*. Ah, He lives, He lives!

> **53.** Again the gooseflesh flashes forth, the eyes again fill with tears,
> again the saliva appears, again the breath runs to the lips;
> all around, on every side, a joyful clamour rises —
> God, leaving the swoon of His bliss, has awakened our hearts.

**Queens.** Ah, we live!

**Chamberlain** *(gazing again)*. Ah, a wonder!

> **54.** From that very song from which the swoon came,
> from it again came the awakening.
> Can it be that it is one and the same certain mantra,
> with power both to launch and to recall?[^61]

And further:

> **55.** The whirlwind of bliss gradually raised the mighty rivers of the madness of the dance,
> and there a series of waves and billows arose:
> one was quelled by another, that by a third, and that by yet the next —
> so the various states too were born of the waves of bliss, and not of the meaning of the song.[^62]

Ah, how great is Svarupa's loving tenderness! For:

> **56.** When the Lord, slowly rising and sitting down again, struck by the onrush of the waves of happiness,
> began to draw lines on the ground with His forefinger,[^63]
> Svarupa, fearing lest He hurt Himself,
> compassionately held back God's hand with his own.

*(Gazing again.)* Ah, woe!

> **57.** Here the Lord of the world rides on the chariot, Himself stretching out His arms,
> as if lovingly wishing to raise to Himself onto the chariot Sri Gaurachandra, who goes in front;
> but He, dancing, only moves further and further away out of His own wilful pride[^64] —
> both have vanished from the path of our eyes; ah, our good fortune is exhausted!

Let us go down from here: here comes the King this way.

*(He helps the queens down and goes out with them. Then enter the King and Kashi Mishra.)*

**King.** Ah, the chariot festival has ended!

> **58.** One has remained in the house of Gundicha,
> the other has gone to another garden —
> and this divine pair at once
> has entered the hearts of the people.

Kashi Mishra! See to it that at the Hera-panchami[^65] the procession of the goddess Shri is wonderful on every side. However many parasols, whisks and the like there are in the Lord's storerooms and however many there are in my treasuries — have them all brought, so that, delighting the eyes even more than the chariot festival, the rasa of wonder may become as it were embodied.

**Kashi Mishra.** As you command.

**King.** Begin it from today; and I too shall return to the city. *(Goes out.)*

**Kashi Mishra** *(to himself)*. Let me see what is happening with the Lord,[^66] Advaita and all the other great ones who have come to another garden. *(Entering the garden called Jagannatha-vallabha and looking around joyfully.)* Ah, it is the Lord Sri Krishna Chaitanya Himself!

> **59.** "Tomorrow is my day!" — "The day after is mine!" — "And the third day is mine!" —
> "The next is mine!" — "And the one after that is mine!" —
> so, sharing out the days of feeding the Lord,[^67]
> Advaita and the others were sporting.

It is not fitting for me to go in here now; I shall go and prepare everything needed for the approaching great festival of Hera-panchami. *(Goes out.)*

*(Enter Sri Krishna Chaitanya, surrounded by Advaita and the others.)*

**Sri Krishna Chaitanya.** Svarupa! Although Sri Jagannatha imitates the pastimes in Dvaraka, still every year, under the pretext of Gundicha, He leaves Nilachala and comes to Sundarachala[^68] to sport in these gardens that recall Vrindavana. How then does He leave the goddess Shri?

**Svarupa.** Master! You Yourself said "that recall Vrindavana" — in that lies the answer. For in Vrindavana He sports not with Shri but only with the cowherd maidens, the gopis.

**Sri Krishna Chaitanya.** And still she is angry.

**Svarupa.** Such is the nature of those in love: they do not notice their own impropriety.

*(Behind the scenes, the din of many musical instruments.)*

**All** *(having listened)*. Ah, four days have flown by like a moment: here the great festival of Hera-panchami is already coming back.

**Sri Krishna Chaitanya.** Just in time for our conversation — this is the great festival of the angry procession of the goddess Shri. We must watch it.

**All.** Just so. *(They approach a place convenient for the spectacle.)*

*(Again behind the scenes, cries of "Jaya! Jaya!")*

**Svarupa** *(looking)*. Lord!

> **60.** This is not the way of *māna*[^69] at all — that she herself, oh marvel, with many
> divine accoutrements proclaiming her majesty, comes out against God!
> Clearly this is the rasa of fury (*raudra*): a great transformation
> of the abiding feeling of anger in the daughter of the Ocean — her "refinement" is well known![^70]

**Puri master.** Svarupa, that is true; but for the spectators it is the rasa of wonder. For:

> **61.** With her flags the goddess, following the quarrel, seems at once to have licked all the ten quarters
> with the two thousand tongues of the king of serpents;[^71]
> and the sky — as if with swans of a pond, with gently swaying chamara whisks,
> and with white parasols, as if with many full-blown white lotuses — is covered.

And further:

> **62.** When the smoke of incense billowing in all directions has thickened
> like a cloud, when the mingled roar of mridangas and other drums thunders deeply,
> when the line of white festive arches moves like a flock of cranes —
> the peacocks, as if gone mad, break into dance.

**Advaita.**

> **63.** In front are courtesans who have surpassed in their accomplishments Rambha and the other apsaras,
> again and again performing all around the graceful *lāsya* dance;
> on every side she is surrounded by maidservants nimbly carrying
> whisks, betel boxes, jewelled pitchers and the rest.

And further:

> **64.** Seated on a golden palanquin adorned with jewels,[^72]
> which by its enchanting lustre seems to eclipse a celestial chariot,
> though blind with great anger, yet proudly thrusting out her bosom,[^73]
> the daughter of the Ocean moves along, as if proud of her father.

**Svarupa** *(mockingly)*. Ah, how great is her refinement in *māna*!

**Sri Krishna Chaitanya.** Svarupa, and what is refinement in loving anger?

**Svarupa.** As the heroine is, so is her refinement.

**Sri Krishna Chaitanya.** And still let us hear it.

**Svarupa.** Once, when the beloved — the young son of the king of Vraja — having offended, approached and spoke as was fitting:

> **65.** "Why do you fall at my feet? I am not angry, and you are guilty of nothing:
> for without cause there is neither anger nor offence among the wise.
> The worthy are those who become the objects of your enjoyment; what is unworthy me to you?
> So from now on, son of the lord of Gokula, be free!"

That is the refinement of anger. And here is another kind:

> **66.** "When I was still far off, she rose; when I came near, she offered a seat with her hand;
> when I spoke, she answered softly with a smile in a word tender and pouring nectar;
> when I sat on half her seat, she showed joy; when I embraced her, she embraced me in return,
> contradicting me in nothing — and by just this she revealed the wilfulness of her heart."[^74]

**Sri Krishna Chaitanya.** This is juicier still than the former.

**Shrivasa** *(jestingly)*. Where would cowherd girls have such majesty? And majesty is the cause of pride.

> **67.** Look how great is the majesty of her pride: the throng of maidservants,
> unbridled from the haughtiness and arrogance of their mistress,
> binding round the waist these chief servants of the Lord of the world and dragging each one,
> throw them at the feet of their mistress like thieves!

We have heard that for a servant's offence the master is punished;[^75] but here it is all the other way round — ah, a great marvel!

**Svarupa** *(to Shrivasa)*. Ho, Pandita! Look, look how great is the refinement of your goddess:

> **68.** What is the fault of this insensible chariot?
> Why is it beaten by the servants?[^76]
> As soon as the Lord said, "I am not going far",
> how did this long anger subside all at once?[^77]

**Shrivasa.** Master! Such is the custom of the Lady.

**Sri Krishna Chaitanya.** Shrivasa, you, like Narada,[^78] love the pastimes in Dvaraka, and so the side of majesty (*aiśvarya*) pleases you. But Svarupa, since Vrindavana is dear to him, prefers the side of such refinement of bliss.

**Advaita.** Lord!

> **69.** By the mercy of Your lotus feet
> such as we have been granted such, such great
> happiness: ah, these great festivals
> have as it were entered in bodily form onto the path of our eyes.

**Sri Krishna Chaitanya.** What further pleasure shall I do for you?[^79]

**Advaita.** What further pleasure will You do?

> **70.** You have playfully, in sport, crushed Kali, proclaimed the path of bhakti,
> and in it revealed the most hidden principle, called prema;
> and here and there You have scattered the boundless good for the sensitive of heart,
> and in it the faultless science of the mahabhava of the crest-jewel of the cowherd maidens.

And for us —

> **71.** for dharma, artha and kama — the highest aversion,
> and of longing for liberation we have none, ever;
> only, O God, together with all these people of Yours,
> may we live together in the other world too.

**Sri Krishna Chaitanya.** So it shall be.

> **72.** Abiding in Vrindavana, ever rejoicing,
> having revealed by sweet play My very Self — He whose mind is captivated by streams of bliss[^80] —
> I shall make you devoted solely to Vrindavana, in bodies equal to Me in radiance[^81] —
> this is the great work that still remains to Me, and it I shall accomplish.

And further:

> **73.** Some devoted in service, others loving in friendship, others in both,
> some firmly devoted to Radha and Madhava, some to the lord of Dvaraka,
> some in friendship and the rest towards both, or those who in another incarnation
> have bound their hearts to Me — all of them I shall make attached to Vrindavana.[^82]

**Advaita.** So be it.

> **74.** By Your own will bring us to whatever
> other place or other body You will —
> but may we, remembering our former births, long, long
> remember only this wondrous life of Yours.

And still let this be:[^83]

> **75.** To the end of the kalpa let poets compose the series of Your pastimes,
> let hosts of actors present it, let people hear and see it;
> let the good give up envy, and let the wicked be ever content;
> let kings, with devotion to Your feet, protect their subjects.

**Bhagavan.** So be it.

*(Exeunt all.)*

*Thus ends the tenth act of the* Sri Chaitanya-chandrodaya, *called "The Great Festival".*

## The Author's Concluding Verses

> **1.** By the mercy shown in His remnants, my maturity was born in the form of poetry,
> and by it here, glorifying His descent at the proper time, I have made the goddess of speech attain her goal.
> What it was mine to do, I have done. Let the wise, to whom this is dear,
> listen; to the others we bow; and let them not think this account an invention.

> **2.** As far as I could, according to my understanding, as I saw and as I heard,
> I, a child, by His mercy have composed this account of Sri Chaitanya.
> Now that the circle of His beloved ones — O Shiva, Shiva! — remains only in memory,
> who will understand it, who will listen? So let Krishna Himself be pleased with it.

> **3.** We saw the Bhagavatas, received their mercy and lived among them,
> came to know the truth and were assured of it, spent some time there with love;
> while they lived, we did not die — but if, now that they are gone, such as we must die after all,
> why did we not die at once, on being born? Alas, Fate, to you, the contrary one — obeisance![^84]

> **4.** In the Shaka year fourteen hundred joined with the horses of the sun,[^85]
> Gaura Hari appeared in the circle of the earth;
> and when ninety-four had been added to that fourteen hundred,
> this book of His pastimes appeared — from whose lips?

*Here ends the drama called* Chaitanya-chandrodaya.
