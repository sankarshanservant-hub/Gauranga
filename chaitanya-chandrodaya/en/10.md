# Act 10. The Great Festival (Mahā-mahotsava)

*The pilgrimage of the Gaudiya devotees headed by Advaita and Shivananda to Purushottama; the bathing festival of Jagannatha seen through the eyes of King Prataparudra and the Queen; Mahaprabhu's meeting with the devotees, and the benediction at the close of the drama.*

*(Enter a certain traveller.)*

**Traveller.** I have heard that every year at the time of Gundicha Advaita Acharya and all the others set out to see Sri Krishna Chaitanya. They are looked after by one named Shivananda, an associate of that same Lord: he removes the obstacles raised by the guards of the crossings, those thorns of the road — the ferry tolls and other exactions[^1] — and leads everyone with him, even chandalas, taking care of them. I too shall seek him out, so as to go along with him. *(Walking a few steps.)* Ah, a good man is to be seen ahead; I must ask him. *(Approaches.)*

*(Enter a servant of Advaita named Gandharva.)*

**Gandharva.** Well, my master Advaita has charged me with this:

> **1.** "The auspicious time to go to Purushottama has come; we are setting out
> at once. Tell Shivananda: let him fix the day of departure
> and write where all of us, travelling quickly, are to
> join together — whether he turns out to be behind us or ahead."

So I am going to him. *(Walks about the stage.)*

**Traveller** *(approaching)*. Brother! Where are you going?

**Gandharva** *(recites the verse "The auspicious time…")*.

**Traveller** *(joyfully, to himself)*. So what I heard is true. And still I shall ask. *(Aloud.)* Brother, does he take a stranger too under his care and lead him along?

**Gandharva.** Brother! He took even a dog under his care and brought it along — what to speak of a man!

**Traveller.** Brother! Tell me how that was.

**Gandharva.** Even before the Lord went to Mathura,[^2] once, when many thousands of people had set out, a certain dog attached itself to Shivananda of its own accord. Shivananda, seeing that it walked beside him step by step, took care of it with respect: at every halting place he gave it unpolluted food at the proper time, and wherever a river or the like had to be crossed, he paid for it separately whatever was required for the ferry[^3] and put it in the boat. And every day it followed at his heels. So they went two-thirds of the way. And then one day a servant forgot and did not give it its food. Afterwards, not seeing it, Shivananda repented: "Ah, today the dog has been given no food!" — he himself called it by name here and there, but never saw it. From that day all the way to Purushottama it was not to be seen, and his heart grew very heavy.

And then, by the will of fate, when Shivananda came to Purushottama, he saw that very dog on the shore of the ocean beside the Lord Chaitanya, who was sitting alone. Shivananda bowed, as one who has offended, and, standing at a distance, watched. The Lord was throwing it bits of coconut pulp — Jagannatha's prasada — and saying: "Say 'Krishna'!" And it ate piece after piece and also uttered: "Krishna, Krishna, Krishna!" Seeing this unheard-of thing, Shivananda bowed to it again and begged forgiveness for his offence. And from that time no one saw it any more. I think that in that same body it took on another form and attained another world.

**Traveller.** Brother! Today is an auspicious day for me: this story of Sri Krishna Chaitanya has become a guest of my ears. The God who made even a dog utter the name of the Lord — what mercy will He not show to the world of men! So tell me, brother, how do the guards of the crossings — those thorns of the way — treat these travellers on the road?

**Gandharva.** Brother, listen.

> **2.** By the power of this God alone, all who follow Him every year
> surely meet with great happiness on the way there and back.

But in some year there may be trouble.

**Traveller.** What trouble?

**Gandharva.** One year, when many thousands of people headed by my master had set out, Shivananda, the guardian of all the travellers, went too with his household. At every crossing he himself stood surety for the toll of all who were going, except my master's retinue, and, himself paying for each one as much as was due by the fixed rate, went on; and the people went on in peace. So they went, and then in the region of Remuna[^4] there appeared a certain official of the Gajapati, the chief over the guards of the crossings, who was acting wilfully there while the great King Gajapati had gone to the southern lands. This villain, passing all bounds, raised the tax, counted the people head by head, listed in order even the past years and, to take the accumulated toll, put Shivananda in wooden stocks. Because of his fetters my master and all the others were in the greatest distress and sat on without performing the day's rites.

**Traveller.** And then?

**Gandharva.** Then, when two watches of the night had passed, that scoundrel sent a servant with a stick for Shivananda. Shivananda then was in great distress: "Will he not beat me?" — and, offering praise[^5] to the feet of Chaitanya, went to him together with his escort. Seeing him just risen from sleep, surrounded by many torch-bearers, he was frightened. Then the official asked him: "Hey, have you come with your household?" He answered: "Yes." He asked again: "Whose man are you?" He answered: "Sri Krishna Chaitanya's." The official again: "You are Chaitanya's, and I am Jagannatha's. Who is greater — Jagannatha or Chaitanya?" He answered: "For me Krishna Chaitanya is greater." Hearing this, the official, brightening with goodwill, said as one at fault: "Hey, I had a dream: Sri Krishna Chaitanya said to me, 'You have put My man in fetters — release him at once.' Forgive me this offence. You owe nothing. In the morning rise in peace and go with everyone." Having said this, he told two torch-bearers: "Take him to where his people are." When he returned, my master and all the others then began the day's rites.

**Traveller.** Ah, truly wondrous are the mercy, power and might of Chaitanya-deva!

**Gandharva.** And where are you from?

**Traveller.** I am from Northern Radha.[^6]

**Gandharva.** Why alone?

**Traveller.** Narahari Dasa and the others sent me.

**Gandharva.** What for?

**Traveller.** To find out when he will go to Purushottama.

**Gandharva.** Then wait here, by my master's abode, until I find out and come back. Some ten more people going there, like you, my master himself has left here, saying: "You will go with me."

**Traveller.** How have they deserved such favour from your master?

**Gandharva.** One of them is supremely lovable, like an elixir for people's eyes, young, beautiful in form, full outside and inside of the juice of love for Sri Krishna, born in him together with himself — a moon of the brahmin race named Shrinatha. Seeing this captivating youth, my master was greatly pleased. And he said to him: "I myself shall show you Sri Krishna Chaitanya in private; do not go with others."[^7] For his sake he has been providing for the needs of these ten for a month already, ever since leaving home.

**Traveller.** Very well, I shall wait for you here.

**Gandharva.** And I shall go for Shivananda. *(Exeunt both.)*

*— Interlude (vishkambhaka) —*

*(Enter Shivananda, and with him several others who wish to go.)*

**Shivananda** *(to one of them)*. Hey, where are you from?

**He.** Great soul! Govardhana Dasa has sent me to you.

**Shivananda.** Ah, I see. You will go to seek out Raghunatha Dasa.

**He.** Just so.

**Shivananda.** And why seek him out?

**He.**[^8] Venerable sir! Are you acquainted with him?

**Shivananda.** Listen, then:

> **3.** The preceptor Yadunandana,[^9] sweetest, beloved of Sri Vasudeva —
> his disciple Raghunatha, rich in virtues, dearer than life to such as I,
> ever anointed with the generous mercy of Sri Chaitanya, a follower of Svarupa,
> the sole treasury of renunciation — to whom among those living on Nilachala is he not known?

And further:

> **4.** He is a certain blessed soil, the one delight of the hearts of all people,
> that bears fruit without ploughing; on it the tree of love for that Lord,
> as soon as it was planted, at once became fruitful, having no equal.

And still let us go; I myself shall take you under my care and lead you. The delay is only until the command of Advaita-deva has been received. *(Ponders.)*

*(Enter Gandharva.)*

**Gandharva.** Ah, here is Shivananda. Let me approach. *(Approaches.)*

**Shivananda** *(looking ahead)*. Ah, ahead, it seems, is a servant of the Acharya Gosvami. Good: what I was thinking of has come to pass. *(Approaches.)*

**Gandharva.** Venerable sir! My master Advaita Gosvami commands you.

**Shivananda.** I am all attention. Speak.

**Gandharva.** "The auspicious time…" *(and so on; recites the verse again)*.

**Shivananda.** I myself am waiting for his command.

**Gandharva.** There is something special too.

**Shivananda.** What is it?

**Gandharva.** This year the bathing festival (*snāna-yātrā*) too must be seen.[^10]

**Shivananda.** That is just what everyone desires. Go and inform the masters: here I am, having fixed the days, setting out for the Lord's feet. And meanwhile I shall go there to settle the matter with Shrivasa Pandita and the others. You go too. *(Exeunt.)*

*(Enter Sarvabhauma, without drawing the curtain.[^11])*

**Sarvabhauma.** Although the Lord did not give His consent, still I am setting out on my own for Varanasi, to make them accept the Lord's teaching[^12] — so on my own I am going there. I do not know what will come of it. Although the Lord's mercy depends on His will, yet He Himself is subject to mercy, so that at times mercy too becomes independent. What will happen with the help of mercy itself — that will be. *(Walks about the stage and looks ahead.)* Ah, ahead are seen many people from various lands gathered in one place — they must be pilgrims. *(Gazing again.)* Ah, they are all Gaudiyas! *(Looking more closely.)* Here he is, here is Advaita Acharya! Here he is, here is Nityananda the avadhuta! Here he is, here is Shrivasa! Here he is, here is Haridasa! Here he is, here is Gadadhara Dasa! These are Govinda Ghosha and the rest. This is Makaradhvaja. This is Kashinatha. This is Narahari and his companions. These are Ramananda and the others from Kulina-grama. These are Nityananda's companions, Gauri Dasa and the rest. What more is there to say — all the associates of Sri Chaitanya to the last one are coming! Good: I shall stop here today and converse with each of them. *(Approaches.)*

*(Enter Advaita and the others, who have set out to see the Lord.)*

**Advaita.** Ah, ahead seems to be Sarvabhauma. What does this mean?

**Sarvabhauma** *(approaches and bows to Advaita and to the others too; seeing Haridasa at a distance)*. Haridasa, who looks not to lineage and caste — obeisance to you!

*(Haridasa, stepping further back, bows in embarrassment.)*

**Advaita.** Sarvabhauma! How did you consent to separation from the lotus feet of Sri Krishna Chaitanya?

**Sarvabhauma.** This is how it was. *(Tells everything.)*

**Advaita.** Well, then, let everyone rest here today. We must converse with the Bhattacharya.

**All.** As you please. *(Each settles in his place.)*

**Shrikanta** *(to Shivananda)*. Venerable uncle! If you allow me, I shall go ahead.

**Shivananda.** Go, as you please.

*(Shrikanta bows and goes out.)*

**Advaita.** Come, Bhattacharya, come! Let us settle down, and in due time we shall hear everything. *(Exeunt.)*

*(Enter Svarupa and Govinda.)*

**Svarupa.** I have heard that Advaita and all the others are coming from Gauda.

**Govinda.** Yes. Shrikanta has just come, having left them halfway.

**Svarupa.** Where is he?

**Govinda.** He is sitting and talking with Mahaprabhu.

**Svarupa.** Then we too shall listen. *(They approach.)*

*(Enter Mahaprabhu, sitting comfortably together with the Puri master;[^13] at a distance, Shrikanta.)*

**Mahaprabhu.** Shrikanta! Tell Me, who all is coming?

**Shrikanta.** Lord! All are Yours. This year no one has stayed behind there. And some have not yet seen Your beautiful feet.

**Svarupa** *(approaching)*. Glory, glory to Mahaprabhu!

**Mahaprabhu.** Come here, come, Svarupa! *(Seats him beside Himself.)*

*(Shrikanta bows to Svarupa.)*

**Mahaprabhu.** Shrikanta! Well, and then? Who are they — those whom I have not seen before?

**Shrikanta.** Lord! Advaita Acharya's sons — Vishnu Dasa, Gopala Dasa and others. And also with Advaita comes one named Shrinatha, dear to all people.

**Mahaprabhu.** Why does he come with him, leaving the company of Shivananda?

**Shrikanta.** He said to him: "I shall bring you to Mahaprabhu in private and obtain His special mercy for you" — and so he relied on this promise.

**Mahaprabhu** *(smiling, to Svarupa)*. Listen:

> **5.** "This is Advaita's offering; it will prove very sweet" — therefore
> some love must be shown to him; and you, Svarupa, be a friend to him;
> and you, O Shankara,[^14] awaken the sweetest feeling for him:
> for the union of those alike in nature is sweet to all.

**Both.** As the Lord commands.

**Mahaprabhu.** Who else?

**Shrikanta.** The son of Vasudeva[^15] and my uncle's two sons.

**Mahaprabhu.** Those two I have already seen.

**Shrikanta.** But the youngest[^16] has not yet seen Your beautiful feet.

**Mahaprabhu** *(to the Puri master)*. Svami, this is your servant.[^17]

**Shrikanta.** Lord, so it is.

**Mahaprabhu.** Well, and then?

**Shrikanta.** The son of Ramananda Basu. And others likewise.

**Mahaprabhu.** Svami Puri master! Ho, Svarupa! This year, for the sake of these, those too will get the chance to see Me.[^18]

**Both** *(to themselves)*. Ah, what can be the meaning of these words? Well, it will become clear of itself.

**Mahaprabhu.** This year the Acharya will meet the King.

**Shrikanta.** Master! I have come from afar, so I know nothing of this.

**Puri master and Svarupa** *(to themselves)*. Ah, I see! Last year Advaita Acharya conversed with the King — that reproach still lives in the Lord's heart.

**Mahaprabhu.** Puri master! It is just the conduct of Vasudeva that pleases Me.[^19]

**Puri.** Truly he is fortunate: the Lord praises him even in his absence.

*(Behind the scenes, noise.)*

**Puri** *(having listened)*. Master! They are already very near — this clamour is heard.

**Mahaprabhu.** Govinda! Take a garland — the Lord's prasada — and go out to meet them.

**Govinda.** As You command. *(Goes out.)*

**Vaninatha** *(enters, bows and, joining his palms)*. Master! Here is mahaprasada — rice and other dishes of the Lord Sri Jagannatha.

**Mahaprabhu.** Vaninatha! You know well when what is needed: you have done this, realising that Advaita and the others are arriving now. Until Govinda comes, put it somewhere yourself. *(Vaninatha does so.)*

**Kashi Mishra** *(entering)*. Lord! Tomorrow is the great bathing festival of the Lord.

**Mahaprabhu.** Yes, I know. But, Mishra, see to it that this year My Gaudiyas, all of them down to the last simple man,[^20] may watch the great bathing festival in peace.

**Kashi Mishra.** Master! The King has commanded me: "This year none of mine — neither the queens nor the others — will watch the bathing of God. Where they formerly watched the bathing of God, standing on the circular enclosure[^21] — there let all the Gaudiyas go up."

**Mahaprabhu.** May all good be his!

*(Again behind the scenes, the sounds of sankirtana.)*

**Puri.** Master! They have already reached Chara-Ganesha.[^22]

**Mahaprabhu.** Svarupa! You go ahead to meet them, and then I shall come too.

**Svarupa.** As You command. *(Goes out.)*

**Puri** *(to himself)*.

> **6.** A great reproach was shown — and now the Lord, loving Advaita,
> shows him still greater honour.
> Such is the greatness of friendship: though friends, by the nature of the body,[^23] have both virtues and faults,
> the eye sees only the virtues and does not catch on the faults.

**Mahaprabhu.** Puri master, rise, let us too go to meet them.

**Puri.** Rise, Gosvami! *(They go out of the town to meet them.)*

*(Enter Advaita, doubly gladdened at having received the garland, the Lord's prasada, overflowing with the bliss of the dance; around him Shivananda and the others, singing kirtana. Advaita, seeing Mahaprabhu coming from afar, dances and falls to the ground.)*

**Shivananda** *(addressing his son, but so that all may hear)*. Look, look, here He is, here!

> **7.** In lustre a garland of lightning, in gait the playful stride of the king of lions full of eager longing,
> His arms long and mighty like golden bolts;
> His neck like a lion's, His garment gleaming with the lustre of the rising sun —
> Sri Gauranga shines before us. Bow, bow down!

*(All bow to the ground, and when they rise, their eyes again seek the Lord, who has entered the circle of Advaita.)*

**Shivananda** *(gazing)*.

> **8.** When Advaita and Chaitanya embraced tightly,
> no one could distinguish who was who:
> for a moment people see Advaita in Chaitanya,
> and Chaitanya in Advaita.[^24]

Look, then:

> **9.** The Lord, putting Advaita in front, came forward, wishing to see him,
> and now leads him into His abode.
> Wait — let everyone enter in order.

*(Holding everyone back, he lets them in in order. Mahaprabhu sits down and, having gladdened each one — Advaita and the others — as is fitting, with embraces, conversation, glances and the rest, with His own hand gives each a full handful of rice, the Lord's prasada. Advaita and the others receive it in order.)*

**Bhagavan.** Ho, today eat nothing more; tonight, at the coming of evening, all of you go up onto the circular enclosure so as to watch the great bathing festival in peace.

**All.** As You command. *(Exeunt.)*

**Svarupa.** Lord! You Yourself should perform the day's rites at the proper time.

**Bhagavan.** As you please. *(Goes out together with Puri Gosvami.)*

**Svarupa.** Kashi Mishra! The King must now be arriving from the capital — not for Gundicha?

**Behind the scenes.** He has arrived!

**Kashi Mishra.** Gosvami! Judging by this cry, he seems to have arrived.

**Svarupa.** Then it will be inconvenient for him to watch the bathing himself.[^25]

**Kashi Mishra.** I know how matters stand. *(Goes out.)*

**Svarupa.** Govinda! Let us follow the Lord. *(Exeunt.)*

*(Enter the King, on the terrace of the palace,[^26] and the priest.)*

**King.** Priest! This year I shall watch the bathing while staying here. Otherwise the Lord Gauranga will be inconvenienced.

**Priest.** That is quite fitting.

**King.** Ho, who is there? Call Kashi Mishra!

**Kashi Mishra** *(entering)*. Here I am. Command, Sire.

**King.** Mishra!

> **10.** The Gaudiyas who are here — the associates of the Lord or their kinsmen,
> or those who follow them, or those with servants —
> let them all sit where my wives, sons and friends, settling down,
> watch the bathing with great comfort.

**Kashi Mishra.** Great King! By your[^27] command all this has already been done.

**King.** Well arranged.

**Chamberlain** *(entering)*. Sire! The queens report: "We came to watch the bathing of God, and it has not worked out."

**King.** How can it not work out? Let them too watch while staying here. Look: everything has been prepared for it.

**Kashi Mishra.** Sire! Look, look:

> **11.** Having climbed the freshly whitewashed tower opposite the bathing place,
> covered by the moonbeams, all these devotees
> shine like gods seated in the midst of heaven.

**King.** It is good that you have placed these associates of Sri Krishna Chaitanya there. And now go: the time of Jagannatha's coming out seems already near. Do not delay the proper duties.

**Kashi Mishra.** As you command. *(Goes out.)*

**Queens.** Glory, glory to the great King!

**King** *(to the Queen)*. Come here, Queen, come! Make your birth fruitful. *(Praising her, he seats her.)* Queen, look, look:

> **12.** Here are the associates of Chaitanya-deva, who purify the universe;
> very soon you will see Him too.

Bow to them, then. *(The Queen bows.)*

**King.** Look, look, what a great wonder!

> **13.** "At the great conjunction of Jyaishtha[^28] the flag on the temple of the Lord
> flies upward" — such is the well-known report;
> and here all of them, with eyes raised in faith, see at once how it
> seems to lick with its tongue the body of the cool-rayed moon.

**Queen.** Sire! This popular report is indeed true.

**King.** Look for yourself. *(Shows her the flag flying upward.)*

*(Behind the scenes, the sound of kahala horns.)*

**King** *(having listened)*. Queen, look, look! The time of Jagannatha-deva's coming out has come. So let me see whether Sri Krishna Chaitanya-deva is coming. *(Doing so, joyfully.)* Here He is, here is Sri Krishna Chaitanya! Look, look:

> **14.** Above the dense crowd of people, higher than all heads,
> shines this orb of the Lord's beautiful face —
> behold: as if high above a water surface where swans of many kinds are swimming,
> a certain golden lotus on a high-raised stalk.

[^1]: *Ghaṭṭa-pāla*, the guards of the crossings (*ghaṭṭa*, "ghat"), who collected the toll (*ghaṭṭa-deya*) from travellers.
[^2]: That is, before Chaitanya's pilgrimage to Vrindavana by way of Mathura (Act 9).
[^3]: *Yatheṣṭa-mātaraṁ*: in KM *yatheṣṭam āteraṁ*, with the editor's explanation "the boat fare and the like"; *ātara*, the ferry fee.
[^4]: Remuna is a village in Orissa (near Balasore), known for the temple of Kshirachora-Gopinatha; it lay on the way from Bengal to Puri.
[^5]: So in GGM (*stutvā*); KM has *smṛtvā*, "remembering".
[^6]: Northern Radha (*uttara-rāḍha*) is a region in western Bengal, north of the Ajay; Narahari Dasa (Sarakara) is from Shrikhanda.
[^7]: *Uktaṁ ca: mayā rahasi śrī-kṛṣṇa-caitanyaṁ bhavān darśayiṣyate, mānya-saṅge gantavyam*, Advaita's words to Shrinatha: "by me Sri Krishna Chaitanya will be shown to you in private"; *mānya-saṅge* is understood as *mā anya-saṅge*, "do not (go) with others". So M. Guha's Bengali translation understands it ("I shall show you Sri Krishna Chaitanya in private; do not go with others").
[^8]: In KM the speech is spoken by "Another" (*anyaḥ*).
[^9]: So in GGM (*yadunandanaḥ*); KM has *raghunandanaḥ*. By tradition the preceptor of Raghunatha Dasa is held to be Yadunandana Acharya, a disciple of Vasudeva Datta. In the same place KM has *vairāgyasya nidhir* instead of *vairāgyaika-nidhir*.
[^10]: According to KM (*snāna-yātrā ca draṣṭavyā*); GGM has *snāna-yātrā na draṣṭavyā*, "need not be seen", which contradicts Shivananda's answer and the subsequent action. The Snana-yatra is the bathing of Jagannatha on the full moon of the month of Jyaishtha, two weeks before the Ratha-yatra.
[^11]: *Apaṭī-kṣepeṇa*, a hurried entrance onto the stage, without drawing aside the curtain.
[^12]: That is, to preach the teaching of Chaitanya in Varanasi (the stronghold of the Mayavadis).
[^13]: The Puri master here is Paramananda Puri (*purīśvara*, "lord of Puri", an honorary address to a sannyasi of the Puri order).
[^14]: *Śaṅkara*, the address of an individual, apparently Shankara Pandita (brother of Damodara Pandita), who lived with the Lord in Puri: so in M. Guha ("and you, Shankara, receive him with tenderness") and in Premadasa, in whom the "both" who answer below are Svarupa and Shankara. (The Sanskrit explanation in Guha's edition names the "both" as Svarupa and Govinda.) Line 2 in Guha and Premadasa: *bhavatāpy atra maitrī svarūpa*, "and you, Svarupa, (be) a friend". The word *tylyena* in GGM is a misprint for *tulyena*.
[^15]: *Vāsudevāpatyam*, the son of Vasudeva Datta (Mukunda's brother): so M. Guha's Bengali translation ("the son of Vasudeva") and Premadasa ("the son of Vasudeva Datta") understand it. KM has *vāsudevāpatye*, dual, "two children of Vasudeva".
[^16]: According to KM (*kanīyāṁs tu yaḥ*); GGM has *kanīyāṁs turyaḥ*, "the youngest, the fourth". The youngest son of Shivananda is meant — the author of the drama himself, Kavi Karnapura.
[^17]: Karnapura bore the name Puri Dasa (Purai Dasa), "servant of Puri": the jest alludes to his childhood name, connected with Paramananda Puri.
[^18]: The allusion is unclear even to the listeners themselves; below it is partly revealed — it concerns Advaita's meeting with the King.
[^19]: In KM run together: *purīśvara-vāsudeva-caritam*. The praise of Vasudeva (Datta?) is apparently contrasted with Advaita's conduct; in KM the next speech is spoken by Puri and Svarupa together.
[^20]: According to KM (*āpāmaram api*); GGM has the corrupt *āpādayam apip*.
[^21]: *Cakra-veṣṭa*, a circular enclosure (wall) around the temple or the bathing platform, from which spectators watched the festival.
[^22]: *Cāra-gaṇeśa*, a locality (a shrine of Ganesha) at the entrance to Puri; M. Guha translates "as far as the place of Chara-Ganesha".
[^23]: GGM and M. Guha have *deha-svabhāvāt*, "by the nature of the body"; KM *deva svabhāvāt* ("O God, by nature…"). Understood according to Guha's Bengali translation: friends, by the nature of the body, inevitably have both virtues and faults, but the eye falls only on the virtues.
[^24]: A play on words: *advaita*, "non-duality", *caitanya*, "consciousness"; in the embrace the "non-dual" becomes indistinguishable.
[^25]: So in GGM (*tasya … sukhena na bhāvi*: for the King, who has given up his places to the Gaudiyas). KM: *tarhi snāna-darśanaṁ sukhena bhāvī*, "then the watching of the bathing will be convenient".
[^26]: *Baḍabhī* (= *valabhī*), a turret or covered terrace on the roof of the palace.
[^27]: KM has *bhagavad-ājñayā*, "by the Lord's command".
[^28]: The Mahajyaishthi is the especially auspicious full moon of the month of Jyaishtha (in conjunction with the constellation Jyeshtha and the like), the day of the Snana-yatra.
