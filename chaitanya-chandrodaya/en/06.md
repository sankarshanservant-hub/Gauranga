# Act 6. Mercy to Sarvabhauma (Sārvabhaumānugraha)

*The Ganga tells the Ocean of the Lord's journey from Shantipura to Purushottama; the meeting with Sarvabhauma Bhattacharya and his conversion.*

*(Enter Ratnakara.[^1])*

**Ratnakara.** Ah, why did my beloved, the river of the gods, seem to me so suddenly dejected today? I must ask her the cause of her heart's grief. *(Looking ahead.)* What, is the goddess still in the same state? I shall approach and ask. *(Approaches.)*

*(Enter the Ganga in the state described.)*

**Ganga.** Alas, alas! That Lord, thanks to the water that washed whose feet such good fortune fell to my lot — for a long time I enjoyed the touch of His body. What lot could be higher? And now, having had Him, it seems I shall have Him no more. What grief could be greater? What am I to do, unfortunate that I am? *(Stands for some time, lost in thought.)*

**Ratnakara** *(approaching)*. Bhagirathi,[^2] why are you so sad?

**Ganga.** What, is it you, noble husband? What are you asking, my husband? For I am unfortunate.

**Ratnakara.** Bhagirathi, why is that?

**Ganga** *(in Sanskrit)*.

> **1.** I, whose fame resounded through the whole world only because I am the water that washed His feet —
> me this very Lord, eager for rasa, by the art of His daily bathing filled with relish[^3] —
> and today He is leaving me; alas, that is why I suffer.

**Ratnakara.** Yes, I know. I have heard that He, turning back from the road to Mathura, has now come to the house of Advaita. Why, then, do you suffer?

**Ganga.** That is so. But when His intimates — Shrivasa and the others — came from Navadvipa, one day the Lord, after eating with all His own, amid laughter and merry sport, the food cooked by Sri Shachi-devi, sat down with them and began to say something.

**Ratnakara.** What was it?

**Ganga.**

> **2.** "O Advaita and the rest, hear this: at the bidding of My mother
> and of you, loving friends, I did not go.
> Because of this a hindrance arose, and so I cannot go to Mathura.
> So all of you, in your mercy, give Me leave — let Me go now!"[^4]

**Ratnakara.** And then, and then?

**Ganga.** Then Advaita Acharya said:

> **3.** "If all the people say to You, 'Go, since it pleases You' —
> then, before Your departure, the breath of life will depart from all.
> For, alas, we have no strength to bear such reproach of ourselves[^5] —
> and so these souls will depart first, having tasted calamity."

**Ratnakara.** Well said, Advaita, well said! And then, and then?

**Ganga.** Then the Lord lovingly spoke again: "O Advaita and the rest!

> **4.** Since I have accepted this garb, to remain here, near My native land,
> together with those who love Me, is now improper.
> You are wise and learned, and there is the mother of this body too —
> consider it yourselves; ah, what more should I say!"

**Ratnakara.** The Lord too spoke rightly. And then, and then?

**Ganga.** Then the Acharya, together with all the Lord's kinsmen, went to His mother, Shachi-devi, and they held counsel.[^6]

**Ratnakara.** And then, and then?

**Ganga.** Since no one could agree in this matter, His mother said: "Oh, if [His staying here] is a sin against dharma, how can I, for my own happiness, bring on Him the gossip of the wicked? Let happen to me what may. So let Him rather go to the very field of Jagannatha[^7] — that is good. There is hope that news of Him will reach us sometimes."

**Ratnakara.** Splendid, mother, splendid! You have surpassed Devahuti.[^8] And then, and then?

**Ganga.** Then all, deeply moved, said again: "Mother! How could you say such a thing? From now on we cannot dispute your word, as if it were established by the shruti."

**Ratnakara.** And then, and then?

**Ganga.** Then she said: "Let happen to me what may. But if the wicked find fault with Him — that is unbearable. If He goes to Jagannatha, you will be able to go there from time to time, and I shall get news."

**Ratnakara.** And then, and then?

**Ganga.** Then all came to the Lord and told Him what the goddess had said.

**Ratnakara.** And then, and then?

**Ganga.** Then the Lord said joyfully: "As the mother of the universe commands, so do you command. I am going."

**Ratnakara.** And then, and then?

**Ganga.** Then all said: "Master! Stay a few days. Let us gaze at Your lotus feet."

**Ratnakara.** And then, and then?

**Ganga.** Then, to gladden His mother and them, He stayed there three days; as before, He ate together with everyone the food cooked by His venerable mother and the mother of Achyutananda,[^9] and gladdened all. On the fourth day, when He was about to leave, all took counsel and gave Him as companions Nityananda, Jagadananda, Damodara and Mukunda. Then, when the people of Navadvipa had gone and the Lord had set out with them, Advaita Acharya wept bitterly. The Lord left without taking leave of him.

**Ratnakara.** Alas! Now, when the Yavana king, the lord of Gauda, is at war with the Gajapati,[^10] no one goes back and forth. How then does He go with only four companions?

**Ganga** *(changing to Sanskrit)*. Noble husband, there is nothing surprising in that. Look, look:

> **5.** He is the inner ruler of the worlds, the disinterested friend;
> there is no man in the world to whom He is hateful — who would hate Him?
> In this realm of two kings,[^11] right between two armies, fearsome and skilled,
> He — mark it — passed unhindered with five or six friends.

And further:

> **6.** In every village there are crafty cheats,[^12] guards of the crossings,
> roamers of forests and mountains, highway robbers
> who terrify travellers — as soon as they saw Him with their own eyes,
> with tears, trembling all over, they rolled on the ground.

**Ratnakara.** Why, the Lord does not gladden only those whose hearts are steeped in an innate demonic nature; all others, even the lowest, He purifies with a single glance and colours with the rasa of bhakti. And then, and then?

**Ganga.** Just so.

**Ratnakara.** And then, and then?

**Ganga.** Then, leaving the royal road, impassable because of the many huge elephants, tall horses and foot soldiers of the endless train of the great vassals of the Gajapati moving along it without a break, He went deep into the forest and took a forest path.

**Ratnakara.** And then, and then?

**Ganga** *(in Sanskrit)*. Then —

> **7.** Behold a wonder! Once, ah, when the lord of the Raghus[^13] entered the thicket,
> leopards, elephants, buffaloes and rhinoceroses of gigantic bodies,
> struck with terror of His bow, fled; but now these same creatures,
> receiving a drop of the moisture of His sweetness, only stood stock-still.

So for several days He went from forest to forest, bestowing His mercy on the beauty of various trees and creepers, and then came out again onto the royal road.

**Ratnakara.** Why?

**Ganga** *(in Sanskrit)*.

> **8.** In the district of the town of Remuna there is a Deity (*pratimūrti*) of the Lord with a flute in hand;
> it is "ancient", and so, out of great reverence,
> to bow to it, He then went by the royal road.

**Ratnakara.** What does "ancient" mean?

**Ganga.** Some say that this God, though in four-armed form, is in truth the two-armed Gopinatha, who is the one the devotees must worship. Others say that everywhere only four-armed Deities are seen to be ancient, while the two-armed Deity of Gopinatha is not ancient.[^14]

**Ratnakara.** They are mistaken: in Kataka and other places Sakshi-gopala and other [two-armed Deities] are very ancient. And then, and then?

**Ganga** *(in Sanskrit)*.

> **9.** He fell to the ground like a rod and bowed to Him, and He honoured Him highly:
> on His head suddenly fell a garland, slipping from the head [of the Deity].[^15]

**Ratnakara.** And then, and then?

**Ganga** *(in Sanskrit)*. Then the Lord lovingly composed a few verses in His honour. Namely:

> **10.** The left arm — the elbow lowered, the shoulder bent, the hand raised,
> the forearm lying across slightly covering the broad chest,
> the bracelet on it reddening — reveals a certain indescribable beauty
> of Him at whose lips is the flute.[^16]

And further:

> **11.** And the right arm: from the tightly bent elbow and other joints, as from behind a dam,
> a stream of the nectar of sweetness has burst forth[^17] —
> and, flooding the earth with it, it eclipses
> the beauty of Him at whose lips is the flute.

**Ratnakara.** And then, and then?

**Ganga.** Taking the forest path once more, He went to the capital called Kataka to see Sakshi-gopala.

**Ratnakara.** He is truly worth seeing: only in appearance is He a *pratimā* (a Deity), but in truth He is Bhagavan Himself. For —

> **12.** Chosen by a brahmin as witness, He slowly walked behind him,
> stepping with His beautiful tender lotus feet, the ankle-bells jingling close by;
> but when the brahmin, turning his neck, looked at Him at the boundary of the land of Mahendra,[^18]
> the Lord, taking on the form of a *pratimā* (a Deity), unhurried, remained standing on that very spot.

And then, a long time afterwards, the great king Gajapati Purushottama-deva[^19] brought Him and installed Him in his capital.

**Ganga.** So it is.

**Ratnakara.** And then, and then?

**Ganga.** Then, looking at Him, the Lord, as though considering that He had come out of His own heart and was standing before Him, for a moment seemed to enter into Him again. *(In Sanskrit.)* And then He composed this verse:

> **13.** Its petals are the toes, reddish and tender; its pollen sparkles
> in the broken lines of saffron designs from the breasts of the intoxicated cowherd women;
> its honey is pure consciousness; its filaments are the clusters of radiance of the jewel-nails;
> its stalk is the shank: may the lotus foot of the Enemy of Putana protect us!

**Ratnakara.** And then, and then?

**Ganga.** Then all saw this.

**Ratnakara.** What was it?

**Ganga** *(in Sanskrit)*.

> **14.** He, though engaged in playing the flute, for a moment lowered the flute from His lips,
> and it seemed as if, filled with pure faith,
> He wished to enter into conversation with Him.

**Ratnakara.** And then, and then?

**Ganga.** Staying there that day, on the next day, with the words "I must see the lotus-eyed Sri Jagannatha!", His heart as if whirling with great longing, He hurriedly set out.

**Ratnakara.** And then, and then?

**Ganga.** Then, coming to the village called Kamalapura and bathing in the river, the Lord went ahead to see the temple of Bhagavan, while Nityananda-deva, saying, "What is this staff — a fragment of unexpected calamity?",[^20] broke the Lord's staff, which was in his hand, and threw it into the middle of the river.

**Ratnakara.** And then, and then?

**Ganga.** Then Mukunda, seeing the temple of Bhagavan Jagannatha, said to the Lord: "Master! Look, look:

> **15.** Is it not an arm raised by the earth to draw the sun to herself?
> Or is it Shesha risen from Patala to ascend to Satyaloka?
> Or is it the mass of radiance of the royal jewels on the serpent's hoods ascending to heaven? —
> It is, it is, O Master, the wondrous temple of the Lord shining forth!"

**Ratnakara.** Goddess, I have understood everything. Why do you grieve here? My happiness is your happiness too. Look, look:

> **16.** For whose sake — O Shiva, Shiva! — I was bound, for whose sake I was churned,
> that Lord who took no account of my being the father [of His consort][^21] —
> ah, what good fortune is mine! — that very Hari, leaving even that beloved,
> has settled, like one of the family, by the banyan on our shore!

So come, let us go nearer and look.

**Ganga.** As my noble husband pleases.

*(Exeunt both.)*

*End of the introductory scene (praveśaka).*

*(Enter the Lord in deep self-forgetfulness, surrounded by Nityananda and the others.)*

**The Lord.**

> **17.** Though it touches the clouds, it has entered the hearts of the worlds;
> though it is huge, it fits between two eyes;
> though built of stones, it pours down a rain of rasa —
> this palace of the Lord shines before us.

*(Walks about the stage with longing.)*

**All.** Ah, this path, which can be covered in a moment, seems to the Lord longer than long! What are we to think of here? To see the Lord, the Moon of Nilachala, is easy only for His servants, not for others. It is especially hard for us, foreigners: without the help of the king's people it is not easy.

**Mukunda.** There is a way.

**The others.** What is it?

**Mukunda.** Here lives Gopinatha Acharya, the son-in-law of Visharada and brother-in-law of Sarvabhauma,[^22] a very close friend of the Lord; like you, he knows well the special pastimes of the Lord in Navadvipa.

**The others.** And what of it?

**Mukunda.** Through him, by means of Sarvabhauma, everything can be arranged.

**All** *(acting joy)*. Well said! Then first of all we must seek out his dwelling. *(They walk about the stage.)*

*(Enter Gopinatha Acharya.)*

**Gopinatha Acharya** *(to himself)*.

> **18.** My right eye throbs,[^23] my mind is clear and serene —
> I do not know what happiness the sight of the Lord of the universe will bring today.

*(Goes to see Jagannatha.)*

**Mukunda.** Here he is, Gopinatha Acharya!

**Nityananda.** Mukunda, go, go quickly, before he enters the Lion Gate!

*(Mukunda does so.)*[^24]

**Gopinatha Acharya** *(looking ahead)*. Ah, who is this? Probably some Gaudiya. *(Looking closely.)* Ah, from Navadvipa! *(Looking still more closely.)* Ah, it is Mukunda, the beloved servant of the Lord Vishvambhara! The omen has come true! *(Approaches.)* Ho, is it you, Mukunda?

**Mukunda.** Acharya, I bow to you.

**Gopinatha Acharya.** Is the Lord well?

**Mukunda.** The feet of Sri Prabhu have come right here.

**Gopinatha Acharya** *(joyfully)*. What are you saying? Where, where? *(Embraces him again.)*

*(Mukunda leads him back with him.)*

**Gopinatha Acharya** *(looking ahead)*. Mukunda, who is this lord of ascetics?

*(Mukunda tells him everything.)*

**Gopinatha Acharya** *(with amazement)*.

> **19.** What was then the pure rasa of love is now mixed with the rasa of renunciation;
> to the eyes its taste is still the same, but to our heart
> it has become sweet and sour.

**Mukunda** *(approaching)*. O Master! This is Gopinatha Acharya, sent by Sri Jagannatha-deva Himself, as if His representative, to meet the Lord.

**The Lord** *(acting the return to outward consciousness)*. Where is he, where?

**Gopinatha Acharya.** Here I am.

*(Falls at His feet. The Lord embraces him. Gopinatha Acharya bows to Nityananda, then bows to Jagadananda and Damodara.)*

**Mukunda.** Acharya, how can it be arranged that the Lord may see Bhagavan Jagannatha without hindrance, as much as He wishes?

**Gopinatha Acharya.** Only if such good fortune dawns for Sarvabhauma.

**All.** Then let it be reported to the Lord Himself.

**Gopinatha Acharya.** Svami! We think that without a talk with Sarvabhauma it is not easy to behold Sri Jagannatha. What is the Lord's will?

**The Lord.** Your will is My will.

**Gopinatha Acharya.** Then the tree of Sarvabhauma's good deeds has borne fruit. Master, be pleased to direct Your feet this way, this way.

**The Lord.** Show the way.

**Gopinatha Acharya.** This way, this way.

*(All walk about the stage. Then enters Sarvabhauma Bhattacharya, teaching his students, together with them.)*

**Sarvabhauma.** Ho, who is there? Find out whether the midday incense offering to Sri Jagannatha has been made or not.

**Gopinatha Acharya.** Here, here, the Bhattacharya's lessons are over. Now he will go inside [the temple]. Let me approach quickly. *(Reflecting.)* Master, please rest here a little until I return. *(Approaching hurriedly.)* Bhattacharya, a certain great soul has arrived. Go out to meet him and bring him in.

**Sarvabhauma.** Is he far?

**Gopinatha Acharya.** Quite near.

*(Sarvabhauma Bhattacharya rises and goes to meet Him, the students following him.)*

**Nityananda.** Ah, here he is, Bhattacharya Sarvabhauma. Since he has come himself, so much the better.

**Sarvabhauma** *(approaching)*. Obeisance to Narayana. *(Bows.)*[^25]

**The Lord.** Love for Krishna, thought of Krishna!

**Sarvabhauma** *(to himself)*. Ah, what an unusual blessing! So in His former ashrama He must have been a Vaishnava.

*(The students smirk.)*

**Sarvabhauma.** Svami, this way. *(Leads the Lord, seats Him in a fitting place and sits down himself. All act sitting down.)*

**Sarvabhauma.** Acharya, in His former ashrama He was evidently a Gaudiya?

**Gopinatha Acharya.** Bhattacharya, in His former ashrama He is the daughter's son of Nilambara Chakravarti of Navadvipa and the son of Jagannatha Mishra Purandara.

**Sarvabhauma** *(with love and respect)*. Ah, why, Nilambara Chakravarti studied together with my father, and Mishra Purandara was highly respected by my venerable father.

**Gopinatha Acharya.** Their aim is that it should be easy for them to see Sri Jagannatha.

**Sarvabhauma.** So it shall certainly be. Ho, who is there? Call Chandaneshvara.

*(Enter Chandaneshvara hurriedly; he bows to the Lord, then to his father.)*

**Sarvabhauma.** Chandaneshvara, follow the Shripada.[^26] [Let everyone arrange it so that every day, as much as He wishes, He may calmly see the holy face and no one may hinder Him. He is most honoured by me.][^27]

**Chandaneshvara.** As your holy feet command.

**Sarvabhauma.** Svami, stay here [with us].

*(The Lord with Nityananda and the others goes out, taking Chandaneshvara with Him.)*

**Sarvabhauma.** Acharya, stay.

*(Gopinatha Acharya, already halfway out, takes Mukunda by the hand, comes back and sits down.)*

**Sarvabhauma.** Acharya, looking at Him, I am stirred with love and sorrow. Because of His kinship with Nilambara Chakravarti He is very dear to me. How is it that at so young an age He has accepted the fourth ashrama? Who instructed Him in the great saying?[^28]

**Gopinatha Acharya.** Keshava Bharati.

**Sarvabhauma.** Alas! How did He enter the tradition of the Bharatis?

**Gopinatha Acharya.** He does not look much to externals. He values only renunciation itself.

**Sarvabhauma.** What is external here?

**Gopinatha Acharya.** The superiority of a tradition and the like.

**Sarvabhauma.** Wrongly said. The lustre of an ashrama is not external.

**Gopinatha Acharya.** That is only for the sake of honour, and therefore external.

**Sarvabhauma.** What fault is there in honour? That is why I say: He should take the *yoga-paṭṭa*[^29] again from a monk of a better tradition, and then be refined by hearing Vedanta.

**Gopinatha Acharya** *(as if vexed)*. Bhattacharya, you do not know His greatness. But I, from what I have seen, conclude that He is God Himself.

**Mukunda** *(to himself)*. Splendid, Acharya, splendid! You have cooled my burning life.

**Students.** By what means of valid knowledge[^30] have you learned that He is God?

**Gopinatha Acharya.** By a special knowledge born of the Lord's mercy — that is, by a transcendental means of knowledge. The essence of the Lord cannot be grasped by a worldly means of knowledge, for it is transcendental.

**Students.** That is not the sense of the shastras. Why should God not be proved by inference?

**Gopinatha Acharya.** Let God be proved by inference, if you like; but His essence cannot be proved so. It [is grasped] only by knowledge born of His mercy, for that alone is the means of its valid knowledge.

**Students.** It is not seen to be a means of valid knowledge.

**Gopinatha Acharya.** On this there are the words of the Purana.

**Students.** Recite them.

**Gopinatha Acharya.**

> **20.** And yet, O God, only he who has been touched by even a particle of the mercy of Your lotus feet
> knows the essence of the greatness of Bhagavan — no other, though he search for long, knows it.[^31]

"Though he search" — that is, by the paths of the shastras and the rest.

**Students.** So, then, His mercy is not obtained through the shastras?

**Gopinatha Acharya.** Of course not. Otherwise why is it said "though he search"?

**Students** *(laughing)*. Why, then, did you study in vain for so many days?

**Gopinatha Acharya.** That is simply a particular craft.

**Sarvabhauma** *(laughing)*. Evidently His mercy has descended on you. Since you know this essence, tell us at least something.

**Gopinatha Acharya.** It is not a subject for telling; it is known only by experience. If His mercy descends on you, you will experience it yourself.

**Students** *(to themselves)*. How unabashedly he chatters something irrelevant to him! Or perhaps, as brother-in-law, he is jesting a little.[^32]

**Gopinatha Acharya.** Bhattacharya, you said something unbefitting about Him, about God, and I, unable to bear it, answered something. It does not befit such deep people as you to speak so. But there is no fault of yours in this:

> **21.** To Him whose powers become the ground both of dispute and of agreement among disputing talkers,
> and again and again plunge them into self-delusion — to the Infinite, the possessor of infinite qualities — obeisance![^33]

**Sarvabhauma** *(laughing)*. I see: you are a Vaishnava.

**Gopinatha Acharya** *(humbly)*. If He is merciful, you too will become one.

**Sarvabhauma.** Enough of idle talk. After the darshan of the Lord, go to the house of my maternal aunt:[^34] let your "God" with His companions stay there in my name, and invite Him [to a meal] with the prasada of Bhagavan.

**Gopinatha Acharya.** As you command.

**Sarvabhauma.** And I shall go to perform the midday rites. *(Goes out with the students.)*

**Gopinatha Acharya.** Mukunda, come, come. *(Walks about the stage.)* O Mukunda! The thunderbolt of the Bhattacharya's words still cuts my heart. Only if the all-merciful Lord removes it will my mind be at peace.

**Mukunda.** What is impossible for this Lord?

**Gopinatha Acharya.** Come, let us follow the Lord, who has gone to behold Sri Jagannatha. *(Exeunt both.)*

[^1]: Ratnakara ("the mine of jewels") is the Ocean, the husband of the rivers, the Ganga among them.
[^2]: Bhagirathi is a name of the Ganga, brought down to earth by King Bhagiratha.
[^3]: A play on words: *rasa* is both "moisture, water" and "taste, aesthetic feeling"; *rasa-kautukī*, "eager for rasa" (or "fond of water"); *rasayāṁ cakāra*, "relished / made me relish".
[^4]: That is, the Lord stayed at the request of His mother and friends, and as a result His journey to Mathura (Vrindavana) was interrupted. The reading *vyajani* ("arose") follows the Kāvyamālā; GGM has *vyajati*.
[^5]: *Dhikkāra*, the exclamation "shame!", reproach of themselves for letting Him go.
[^6]: GGM has the run-together *ācārio-jaṇaṇīe* ("to the Acharya's mother"), which gives an unclear sense. M. Guha reads *saddhaṁ ācārieṇa jaṇaṇīe* (Sanskrit *sārdham ācāryeṇa jananyāḥ*), "together with the Acharya … to the mother"; the Acharya is Advaita. So Guha's Bengali translation and Krishnadasa Baba's Hindi translation understand the passage.
[^7]: Shri-kshetra is Puri, the abode of Jagannatha.
[^8]: Devahuti, the mother of the sage Kapila, let her son go to the forest after his instruction (*Bhāg.* 3.33). Shachi surpasses her, letting her son go for the sake of His good name.
[^9]: Achyutananda is Advaita's son; his mother is Sita-devi, Advaita's wife.
[^10]: The Muslim sultan of Bengal (Gauda) and the king of Orissa (Gajapati, "lord of elephants", the title of the kings of Utkala), i.e. Prataparudra.
[^11]: According to the note of the Kāvyamālā edition, "between two kingdoms, Gauda and Utkala".
[^12]: *Paṭu-kapaṭin*, "skilful cheats" (so we divide *paṭukapaṭino*); *ghaṭṭa-pāla*, toll-collectors at the crossings; *vāṭa-pāṭac-cara*, according to the Kāvyamālā, "highway robbers".
[^13]: Rama, when He entered the Dandaka forest.
[^14]: That is, the dispute is whether the worship of the two-armed Krishna with the flute (Gopinatha) is ancient, in contrast to that of the four-armed Vishnu. Remuna (near Balasore) is the site of the temple of Kshira-chora-Gopinatha.
[^15]: *Śekhara*, a garland or head-ornament of the Deity.
[^16]: *Muralī-mukha*, "He at whose lips is the flute", i.e. Krishna the flute-player (here the form of Gopinatha in Remuna); so M. Guha's Bengali translation ("Murali-vadana") and Krishnadasa Baba's Hindi translation ("Krishna with the flute") understand it.
[^17]: The reading of GGM and M. Guha, *-bādho labdha-srutā* (in the Kāvyamālā, by the OCR, possibly *-bādhoruddha-srutā*). The sense according to Guha's Bengali translation: the stream of the nectar of sweetness, bursting from behind the "barrier" of the bent elbow and other joints, floods the earth, and the right arm eclipses the beauty of Krishna with the flute. *Vilakṣayati* literally means "puts to confusion, shames"; Guha and Krishnadasa Baba's Hindi translation render it more mildly as "reveals".
[^18]: Mahendra is the mountains and land in the south of Utkala (Orissa).
[^19]: So according to the Kāvyamālā and M. Guha (*gajapati-mahārājena*), and likewise in Krishnadasa Baba's Hindi translation; *gaṅgā-pati* in GGM is apparently a corruption. Gajapati ("lord of elephants") is the title of the kings of Orissa. Purushottama-deva is a fifteenth-century king of Orissa who, according to tradition, brought Sakshi-gopala from Vijayanagara (Vidyanagara) to Cuttack.
[^20]: *Akāṇḍopaplava-khaṇḍa*, "a fragment of unexpected calamity"; a play *daṇḍa*, "staff" — "punishment" is possible. Kamalapura is a village by the river (Bhargi) near Puri.
[^21]: The Ocean was bound by Rama's bridge and churned by the gods for the nectar; from it Lakshmi, the consort of Vishnu, was born, so the Ocean is Hari's father-in-law. Here Hari (Chaitanya) has left Lakshmi too (has accepted sannyasa) and settled on the shore of the Ocean. *Taṭa-vaṭa-kuṭumbī*, literally "a householder of the banyan on the shore"; M. Guha's Bengali translation understands *kuṭumbī* as "by right of kinship, as one of the family" (the Ocean being His father-in-law), Krishnadasa Baba's Hindi translation simply as "a dweller on my shore". Neither translation explains the banyan; probably the sacred banyan of Puri is meant.
[^22]: *Āvutta*, according to the Kāvyamālā note citing the *Amarakośa*, "sister's husband". Visharada is the father of Sarvabhauma.
[^23]: For a man, a throbbing of the right eye is an auspicious omen.
[^24]: In GGM the speeches from verse 18 to this stage direction are repeated twice (an obvious typesetting error); in the Kāvyamālā, once.
[^25]: "Namo nārāyaṇāya" is the usual greeting of Advaitin sannyasis; the Lord answers with a Vaishnava blessing, which surprises Sarvabhauma.
[^26]: Shripada, a respectful address to a sannyasi.
