# Act 1. Absorption in His Own Bliss (Svānandāveśa)

*Prologue: at the command of King Prataparudra the stage-director produces a drama about Chaitanya; Kali and Adharma anxiously discuss the appearance of Vishvambhara and His associates; the great bathing ceremony (abhisheka) in the house of Shrivasa; the Lord's mercy to Shrivasa, Murari, Mukunda, Shuklambara and Shachi.*

> **1.** To the treasures — Kumuda, Padma, Shankha and the rest — He brings distaste with the fresh moonstones of bhakti;
> He has driven a thorn of grief into Kali, as into the chakravaka bird —
> may Gaurachandra destroy the darkness of the objects of the senses![^1]

*(At the end of the nandi, the benediction.)*

**Stage-director.** Enough of lengthy speech! Ho there, ho! Listen! Today, at the festival of the journey to Gundicha of the Lord, Sri Purushottama-deva — the fresh shoot of a great sapphire, gleaming like ground collyrium, sprung up on the shore of the ocean, the mine of jewels; the grove of tamala trees with dense foliage, seen again and again in the caves of the Blue Mountain; the exultant elephant who has appeared beside the great banyan with its deepest of hollows[^2] — at the festival when the hollow of the vessel of the universe resounds with cries of "Jaya! Jaya!" ringing from the eloquent mouths of the jubilant throngs of charming people come from all the quarters and intermediate quarters of the world; when the host of maidens of the quarters rejoices at the tumult raised by the delight of the Lord's kirtana, sung by devotees whose hearts exult with joy and who are undivided in rasa; when the crowds are endlessly deafened, unable to hear any other sound for the din of the pataha drums, eager to keep company with the thundering mardalas, of the precious panava drums, of the countless booming dhakkas, the deepest roar of the bheris and the thunder of the dundubhis — at such a moment I received a command from Gajapati Prataparudra. He drew near with love, by right of his service, and yet he was dejected: his heart was wounded by grief at the disappearance which the Lord, Sri Krishna Chaitanya, is spreading,[^3] and, turned away from joy by his sorrowful plight — for that chief reason — he paid no honour even to such supreme bliss. This protector of the earth, with undisguised longing, spoke thus: "O master of the bards!

> **2.** Here is the Lord of the Blue Mountain, here the same splendour, the same journey to Gundicha;
> the same virtuous people come from all quarters, the same thirst of theirs to see;
> the same gardens that eclipse the beauty of the Nandana grove —
> yet all of it, alas, without Mahaprabhu, we count as empty.

Therefore you must now gladden me with some auspicious performance, full of rasa, whose essence is the outpouring fragrance of the qualities of that very Lord, the king of renunciants, rich in the good fortune of divine love. For:

> **3.** There is no means of bearing the pain born of not seeing the beloved face to face
> except the portrayal of his qualities — told by friends or else enacted on the stage."

So here I must make every effort. And this is a wonder:

> **4.** The fire of his valour, though smokeless, is inferred in every quarter;
> it gives happiness to the virtuous and is eager to burn the swarm of moths, his enemies.
> Fearing lest the world should burst from the onrush of its heat,
> the Lord of Shri plastered the egg of the universe on the outside beforehand with many coverings.[^4]

He is like valour embodied and gathered into one, and he is also like tranquillity embodied: immersed in shanta-rasa, which arose of itself from the nature of his steadily growing devotion to the Lord, he has shaken off rajas and tamas, cast off ignorance, and does not let the thirst for sense objects take root even in the hearts of others. And so now, casting off doubt and making this body of mine fruitful, I shall fulfil the cherished good of this king by staging the drama named *Sri Chaitanya-chandrodaya* ("The Rising of the Moon of Chaitanya"), which dispels the darkness of the heart's impurity. It was composed by the poet Paramananda Dasa, favoured by Srinatha, the son of Shivananda Sena, a dear associate of that same Lord, Sri Krishna Chaitanya, who protects [the world] with His mercy.[^5] *(Looking ahead.)* Ho there, ho! This way, this way!

*(Enter the assistant.)*

**Assistant.** Sir! A wonder, a wonder!

**Stage-director.** My friend, what is it?

**Assistant.** Even amid this supreme bliss of the chariot festival of the Lord — the moving fountain of joy on Nilachala — some people, bereft of happiness and utterly downcast, regard the universe as a pot broken before its time, and they weep. Like this:

> **5.** "Ah, here is the goal of the journey of the Ornament of Nilachala, here the rows of new gardens,
> here the very road of the victorious procession —
> yet all this burns the eyes like a cruel bilious fever, cuts the mind like the speech of the wicked,
> and torments the body like an ulcer in the heart."

Tell me, then, what is the secret here?

**Stage-director.** My friend, you are fortunate: today there have come before these eyes of yours those very great devotees who make sight fruitful, the saviours of the world.[^6]

**Assistant.** Sir, who are they?

**Stage-director.** The dear associates of Sri Krishna Chaitanya.

**Assistant.** Sir, and who is this Chaitanya Gosvami?

**Stage-director.** My friend, you must still be sitting hidden in the pot of your mother's womb, if you have not heard even the name of this Sri Mahaprabhu! Listen, then:

> **6.** Behold a wonder! Its root is the crest-jewel of renunciants, the lord of sages named Madhava;
> its shoot is Srila Advaita; its trunk, renowned in the three worlds, is the Avadhuta;
> the branches of the trunk are Srimad Vakreshvara and others whose bodies are made of rasa;
> its spreading growth is bhakti-yoga, and its flower and fruit are prema free of all deceit.[^7]

And further:

> **7.** Its summit shines, piercing even the bliss of Brahman, and there has built its nest
> a pair of birds woven of the pastimes of Radha and Krishna, devoid of all separateness;
> its shade relieves the weariness of the road of samsara; it is the cause of the fulfilment of the devotees' desires —
> such a wish-fulfilling tree, Chaitanya, has appeared here in the world.[^8]

**Assistant.** Sir, what is the purpose of this descent, which has come so near to people?

**Stage-director.** My friend, attend! Learned men possessed by the demon of attachment to their own doctrine think that the supreme goal of man is merely dissolution — the extinction of the mind in the attributeless, all-pervading supreme Brahman — and that the only means to it, the only wealth, is meditation on non-duality; and they hold this to be primary and established by all the shastras. The truth has remained beyond their grasp, though it abides as the highest in those very shastras, only in hidden form: that Bhagavan Sri Krishna alone, whose form is condensed being, consciousness and bliss, whose pastimes are eternal, who is full of every good fortune, is Brahman with attributes; that worship of Him, sung by Sanandana and others, faultless, is the complete goal of man; and that the means, the wealth for attaining it, is bhakti-yoga of many kinds, chief among which is the congregational chanting of the Name. To reveal this, Bhagavan Sri Krishna, becoming Chaitanya, has come into the world.

**Assistant.** Sir, and has this Hari composed here a book setting forth His own doctrine?[^9]

**Stage-director.** Who does not know, indeed, that the Lord is the author of the Vedas? Yet the impulse which the Inner Ruler wishes to give cannot be limited by outward instruction, nor by place, nor by time.

**Assistant.** Sir, why then does not everyone delight in this generous teaching?

**Stage-director.** My friend, people in the world are subject to various *vāsanās* (latent tendencies) — how could all of them set foot on the path that transcends the world? For faith bound by *vāsanās* gives rise to differences of understanding.

**Assistant.** Sir, bhakti-yoga, which is beyond the reach of the authors of the shastras,[^10] also produces nothing but knowledge, however full of wonder; its effective fruit is *kaivalya* (isolation) in Brahman. Where, then, is the difference?

**Stage-director.** My friend,

> **8.** "One who has taken such a vow, chanting the name of his Beloved, becomes full of attachment, and his heart melts;
> he laughs loudly, weeps, cries out, sings, and dances like one mad, indifferent to the ways of the world."[^11]

By these and other words [it is declared that] the state which gives rise to *rati* (attraction) — a property of bhakti-yoga, unshakeable as a mountain, in the form of the congregational chanting of the Lord's name and the rest — leads to the position of an associate [of the Lord].[^12] And likewise in the words "with their beautiful limbs…" and so on:

> **9.** "The virtuous, O mother, behold My beautiful, divine forms, bestowers of blessings,
> with gracious faces and reddish eyes, and converse with them in words they long for."[^13]

Thus their liberation lies in that very form [of an associate], not in *kaivalya*. That is why Kapila and others have said: "Bhakti is greater than *siddhi* (liberation)."[^14] Therefore in the age of Kali it is the congregational chanting of the Name — which surpasses the power of [all other] means of attaining the goals of man — that brings to the fore the bhava called *rati*.

**Assistant.** Sir! This heartfelt utterance is very astonishing: for the shastra says that the Lord's name produces only liberation. And if you say otherwise — in fact it is said: "Uttering 'Narayana!' at the time of death, he attained liberation."

**Stage-director** *(smiling)*. The word "liberation" here means the form of an associate, for in the same place it is said: "At once he assumed the form of those who abide beside the Lord."[^15] This very teaching of Sri Krishna Chaitanya sweeps away other doctrines. And it is followed by the virtuous and the wise. Therefore even this age of Kali has been blessed by His descent.

**Assistant.** How can that be? For we hear the censure:

> **10.** "In the age of Kali, O king, people whose minds are split by heresy will for the most part not worship
> the Lord Achyuta, the supreme teacher of the worlds, at whose lotus feet the lords of the three worlds bow."[^16]

**Stage-director.** That refers to former ages of Kali, before the descent of Sri Krishna. Otherwise such sayings as these, which point to the future descent of Chaitanya, would contradict one another:

> **11.** "Out of mercy for the devotees who will be born in the age of Kali, He spread His most holy fame,
> which dispels suffering, grief and darkness";[^17]

> **12.** "People of the Krita and other ages, O king, desire to be born in the age of Kali:
> for in the age of Kali devotees of Narayana will appear."[^18]

**Assistant.** And how is it that Kali does not harm them?

**Stage-director.**[^19]

> **13.** He who in the dark fortnight wanes unfailingly day after day —
> how can this *doṣā-kara* harm those who have taken refuge in the *viṣṇu-pada*?[^20]

**Behind the scenes.** Hey, who are you, that, reviling me as a *doṣā-kara*, you put me on a level with the moon?

**Stage-director** *(looking intently)*. My friend, as was to be expected, this [Kali] — his heart blazing with excess of wrath, merciless in his ascendancy — is approaching, together with his dear friend Adharma. Let us leave this place.

*(Exeunt both.)*

*End of the prologue.*

*(Then enters Kali, attended by Adharma.)*

**Kali.** Friend Adharma! The master of the bards spoke the truth.

**Adharma.** What exactly?

*(Kali again recites "He who in the dark fortnight…" and so on.)*

**Adharma.**[^21] My friend, king of the age! Why, that scoundrel has called you yourself a *doṣā-kara*! Ah, you sinner, you wretched actor! Listen, then!

> **14.** He who, together with his vassals — purity, good conduct, austerity, forbearance, tranquillity, self-control,
> and discrimination and the rest — tore King Dharma up by the roots;
> by whom even Dharma's favourites, who purify by a mere glance, were blinded in an instant —
> I, the sole doer of all this, am in his service: and this is the Kali they revile![^22]

Stand, sinner! Stand, stand!

> **15.** Where there is dharma, there is Krishna; where Krishna is, there is victory.
> Where there is no dharma, how can Krishna be with him on whose side is Kali himself?[^23]

**Kali.** My friend, do not revile him. Understand: for

> **16.** My time is past, my friend: now my power has been crushed by a boy —
> as the might of Takshaka, king of serpents, fades the moment a shoot of the great healing herb breaks through.

**Adharma.** King of the age! Who is this boy (*kumāraka*)? A vile killer (*ku-māraka*)? Or a destroyer of *ku*, the earth?

**Kali.** Neither (*nobhayam*) — he cannot inspire fear (*no bhayam*).[^24] But:

> **17.** In Navadvipa, of Purandara Mishra named Jagannatha,
> this boy was born to Shachi — and He cleaves my very heart.

**Adharma** *(laughing)*. Ho there, king of the age!

> **18.** For fear of the wrath of your mighty, club-like arms, blazing like the great sun,
> the Bull [of Dharma], with but a single leg left, has grown dumb and, like an owl, has hidden in a mountain cave.
> And you, whose feet are served by servants such as I, are afraid of a brahmin brat?
> Ah, what unheard-of confusion of mind is this of yours![^25]

**Kali.** Friend, He is not merely the child of a "god of the earth" (*bhū-deva-bāla*); He is the God of the god Bala (*bāla-deva-deva*).[^26] For

> **19.** Hari, Hari![^27] Hari, whose heart is full of sweet feeling, in order to teach the bhakti-yoga of Hari,
> purifying the whole world, with the lovely lustre of a golden lotus,
> has descended here, in a brahmin's house, and plays His childhood pastimes.

And so:

> **20.** He, being born at the full moon, on the pretext of an eclipse
> made all the people of the world chant the name of Hari at once.[^28]

**Adharma.** This too is your delusion: you are interpreting otherwise what happened by the maxim of "the crow and the palm fruit".[^29] Listen, listen then:

> **21.** Where are you — great in might, with the mightiest of allies, long and firmly rooted —
> and where is this sprout born in a family of brahmins?
> And you fear Him? Ah, what a delusion!

**Kali.** Friend, consider it as it really is:

> **22.** The self-luminous do not depend on time, place, age, lineage and the like:
> the young sun, as soon as it rises, dispels the dense darkness.

Nor is He without allies: for even before His own descent He caused hosts of His dear associates to appear on earth. Like this:

> **23.** Advaita, best of acharyas — manifestly the abode of Shambhu (Shiva), not inferior to Bhagavan Himself;[^30]
> Nityananda the Avadhuta — the glorious splendour of Sankarshana;
> and one named Shrivasa — victorious together with Shrikanta and Shripati, and with Srila Rama too —
> ornament of the brahmin race, the very power of Narada.[^31]

And further:

> **24.** Acharya-ratna, Haridasa, Murari, Gangadasa and others, together with Gadadhara Pandita and the rest,
> Vidyanidhi and those with him, Vasudeva Acharya and others, and Mukunda at the head of the others.

And further:

> **25.** With them Sri Vakreshvara, with Nrisimha, with Shuklambara,
> with them Sri Damodara and Shankara, and Jagadananda, one of the chief —
> a host of His beloved ones, connoisseurs of the dance and play of manifold feelings,
> His kinsmen from childhood, has descended to earth for the deliverance of the world.[^32]

**Adharma.** King of the age, tell me: how was it established that He is Ishvara, the Lord?

**Kali.** My friend, the power to attract the hearts of all people is the exclusive sign of Bhagavan, because He is made of bliss. One made of bliss is able to gladden living beings, just as a very rich man makes another rich as well. And He has this very sign of the Supreme: even as a child He astonished the hearts of all people. Like this:

> **26.** Shiva, Shiva! Even in infancy He had depth, steadfastness,
> memory, intelligence, extraordinary learning, sweetness, tenderness and other
> qualities that especially attract all people — who, seeing them,
> would not come here to the conviction: "This is Vishnu!"?[^33]

**Adharma.** That is no proof (*anaikāntika*):[^34] let Him be merely some excellent soul.

**Kali.** Not so.

> **27.** "Know that whatever is glorious, beautiful or mighty
> is born of a spark of My splendour."[^35]

So the Lord spoke in general [of each single excellence]; and since He has hosts of such qualities by the tens of thousands, His divinity is all the more proven. And we ourselves are the evidence of it: for we are not afraid of any living being.

**Adharma.** King of the age, they say He has married?

**Kali.** Indeed!

> **28.** Ah, when the Lord descends into the world, there descends with Him
> His own particular potency — Shri herself in bodily form.
> Accepting her, who imitated the play of human beings, and spending a few days with her,
> God caused her to disappear.[^36]

And so her human nature [is explained by the words]: "When He is a god, she is a goddess; when He is a man, she is a woman."[^37] Moreover:

> **29.** Taking as His wife another beloved, known as Vishnupriya, the embodiment of a portion of Bhu (the Earth),
> He, ever new, wishing to reveal the lesson of renunciation, will leave her too, His new bride.

And further:

> **30.** His elder brother, taking no wife — Bhagavan Sankarshana, who appeared on earth as Vishvarupa —
> having passed his splendour on to a lord of the Puris (Paramananda Puri), took up the wandering life first and disappeared.[^38]

**Adharma** *(reflecting a moment; aside)*. Ah, it looks like trouble! It was from an excess of fancy that I pictured everything otherwise. But

> **31.** The great Name wearies my heart, loosens limb after limb,
> throws into disorder all the obedient servants, the senses;
> oh, it takes away memory, ah, it shakes off steadfastness —
> there is no other calamity for me than the mention of the Great One.

**Kali.** My friend! What [I] have experienced, now experience, experience [you too]!

**Adharma.** My friend! There is a means, sure and without flaw, to overcome Him and to prosper ourselves.

**Kali.** Friend, what is it?

**Adharma.** My friend,

> **32.** These six ministers — Lust and the rest — there is nothing beyond their power:
> by the pride of their invincible mighty arms the whole world is under your single parasol.

Just now all six have returned from their campaign of conquering the quarters of the world. Each, having conquered one quarter and rid the world of every thorn, will now, casting off doubt, present himself at the foot of the feet of his master — you. All of them together we shall send to defeat Him. Let me describe their valour:

> **33.** From the pride of his arms Brahma, born of the lotus, himself approached his own daughter,
> and Pashupati (Shiva), though content in himself, ran after Mohini —
> this is Kama (Lust), whose loud fame in conquering the three worlds is like sport with women.
> And what of the others — wretched creatures![^39]

**Kali.** My friend, you do not know what divinity is:

> **34.** Once he himself went to overcome Narayana — and was defeated by Him.[^40]
> Embodied beings cannot delude Hari, the enchanter of the enchanter of the world.

And yet they have already been appointed by me. They gave their promise: "When His childhood is past, we shall overcome Him." But that too is impossible. For

> **35.** At the very beginning of His youth He left His new wife, radiant like Lakshmi,
> and, fulfilling His own duty, went to Gaya and performed the funeral rite for His father.[^41]

And further:

> **36.** There, by the will of providence, He accepted Ishvara Puri, a lord of sannyasis whom He met;
> the Lord of the self-controlled received from this preceptor, as from a guru,
> the knowledge of ten syllables that had been in the keeping of Madhava Puri.[^42]

And further:

> **37.** Returning to His home, surrounded by dear associates headed by Shrivasa, Rama and Haridasa,
> He sings, dances, enacts [feelings] and weeps bitterly — and plunges the three worlds into oceans of bliss.[^43]

How could wretched Kama find his chance here?

**Adharma.** Friend, do not say so!

> **38.** Those who were devoted to the severest austerities, to tranquillity, self-control, vows, concentration, meditation and yoga,
> whose wisdom rejected, like vomited food, everything up to the seat of Brahma and the riches of the three worlds,
> who conquered even the enemies — Kandarpa and the rest, invincible by nature —
> fell the moment he touched them: tell me, who can conquer him, Anger, and how?[^44]

**Kali.** My friend, what can wretched Anger do to Him? Here is what happened. There lived two, named Jagannatha and Madhava:[^45] ministers in the sport of every kind of lawlessness; their hearts were baked in the five great sins with all their ramifications; their whole measure was nothing but the oppression of all the people; arrant plunderers, some sort of brahmins by dress alone, and shabby dress at that; zealous in evil deeds, miserly, brothers born of one womb, in appearance drums of deceit; from day to day the filth in their minds grew ever greater. Of His own accord, with the insistence of mercy, He called them and had them brought before Him, saying: "Whatever sin you have committed, greedy for the poison of sin — give it all to Me with attention." Hearing this, they were for a moment somehow stunned with amazement and astonishment, and then they said: "We give it!" — and, taking water from His hand,[^46] at once began to shine; a thick thrill of the hair, rising swiftly, covered them like armour, tears of joy flowed from their eyes like the water of consecration into bliss, their throats were choked, and stammering they repeated: "Krishna, Krishna!" Through the purity of a mind long restrained, through bhakti-yoga long attained, lust and the other unbridled vices left them, and they ascended to the rank of great devotees. By such a transformation, born of bliss, He without any hindrance dispelled the doubts of all the onlookers and made them motionless like figures in a painting. So what is surprising if the Lord, who lightens every sin, who with a single sidelong glance cuts away from the hearts of others the band of the six enemies, lust and the rest, brings anger under His control?

*(Behind the scenes, a joyful uproar.)*

**Adharma** *(acting as if listening)*. My friend! Do you hear? Today this din and clamour of joyful excitement comes from the house of Shrivasa. From this I infer that some immeasurably great, venerable, heart-astonishing deed of His is unfolding.

*(Again behind the scenes, the sounds of various musical instruments together with cries of "ulu-lu".)*

**Kali** *(looking intently)*. Ah! We inferred rightly — as if we were seeing it with our own eyes. Look: to the unrestrained ringing of conches and bells, which swells the thunder of the many instruments echoing the cries of "Jaya! Jaya!" born of supreme joy together with the auspicious cries of "ulu-lu"[^47] from the mouths of throngs of young brahmin wives, lovely as goddesses, some great festival is unfolding, like a shower of nectar-juice pouring into the hollows of the ears. This must certainly be observed carefully. *(Does so.)*

*(Again, there, behind the scenes.)*

> **39.** Rama, quickly bring the arghya and the rest! Shripati, good fellow, go faster,
> set out a hundred and eight new pitchers! Shrikanta, send the women out in every direction,
> and let other fortunate brahmin wives bring water from the river of the immortals (the Ganga)!

**Kali.** Friend, I have noted it well: it is Shrivasa giving orders to his brothers. I think the festival of the great bathing of that very Vishvambhara-deva is beginning, whose supreme power blossoms in the manifest possession of Him by His own nature.[^48]

**Adharma.** If He is Ishvara Himself, governed by none but Himself, where does this present possession come from?

**Kali.** Friend, listen:

> **40.** Ah, though the divine nature of the Lord is eternal and mighty,
> He, being independent, does not always manifest it.
> Out of playful whim He takes on just the ways of the world —
> and those who know call this His supreme, sweetest lila (play).

*(Again looking towards the back of the stage.)* Friend, look, look! He has entered the Lord's room[^49] — as if making the abode of Shrivasa, best of brahmins, visible to the eyes of all people like a corner of Ilavrita-varsha, with a radiance that pours in clusters of waves of rays from the summit of the golden mountain wholly embraced by the rays of the newly risen sun;[^50] like bliss gathered into one and intoxicated with great rapture; like a mass of garlands of lightning flashing all at once and breaking loose. He has mounted the great couch of the shalagrama and the other [Deities], moved the shalagrama and the rest aside and seated Himself. And all the others, in a flurry running hither and thither, covered with a thick thrill of hair and with tears, brought the articles of worship that had been named beforehand — as if their senses had lost all their skill and their hearts had been cleansed of thirst for the various objects of the senses — and surrounded Him on every side.

*(Again behind the scenes.)*

> **41.** Rama, prepare fragrant, purified water here;
> Mukunda, gather yourself all that is needed for the bathing;
> Gadadhara, ho, arrange the garments, garlands, ornaments and the rest:
> I shall bathe Hari right here, on the couch.

**Kali** *(looking all around, towards the back of the stage)*. Friend, look, look:

> **42.** Ah, the women, their hands busy with auspicious pitchers,
> go and come — from all ends of the town to the heavenly river, and from its bank to the town;
> on their lips are His deeds, in their eyes tears, their bodies tremble,
> their braids have come loose, on their cheeks the hair stands on end — a wonder!

**Adharma.** Friend, better say: "their braids have come loose" — for all of this is the flashing of Kama. For

> **43.** Wherever the doe-eyed go, there strides the valour of Madana:
> does a general conquer alone, without the army that gives him his superiority?

**Kali.**[^51] That too is not so.

> **44.** A mind struck by [amorous] feeling throws both into turmoil;
> but in those who are free of such feeling, the outward appearance is in no way at fault.

*(Again behind the scenes, a din swelling the loud uproar, sweet with the recitation of the "Purusha-sukta".[^52])*

**Kali** *(gazing)*. Ah, the greatest wonder!

> **45.** As streams from the kamandalu of Brahma, as from Sumeru, ceaselessly bathed
> by the torrents of the waters of the heavenly Ganga — so from the pitchers, pouring again and again,
> falling from the body of Gauranga and reviving the circle of the earth,
> the streams of the waters of the great bathing flowed out to the four quarters of the world.[^53]

And further: look — after the bathing, the bathing water that ran from Sri Vishvambhara like rivers has been wiped away; He is beautiful, clad in garments of His liking; some devotees, beside themselves with joy, have anointed Him all over, others have adorned Him, others have washed His lotus feet; arati has been performed to Him with lamps in vessels of the purest, fire-refined gold, and the golden radiance of His body has flashed forth; His majesty is envied by radiance itself. And all, filled with the weight of love, at the foot of His lotus feet, setting out their wealth as their means allow, putting Advaita at their head and banishing duality,[^54] offer Him gold, jewels, garments and the rest.

**Adharma.** Friend, here is a chance for Greed. For

> **46.** Greed — destroyer of steadfastness, hater of happiness in the end, opponent of modesty —
> ah, can anyone at all overcome it?
> Even the god Vishnu coveted the Kaustubha, born of the churning of the Milk Ocean,
> the jewel among jewels — and Shri (Lakshmi).

**Kali.** He is not like that. *(Gazing.)* Look, look! He

> **47.** does not speak, does not look and hears nothing;
> He is stilled in His own bliss — and only His radiance keeps increasing.

**Adharma.** Friend, this too is the usual way of Mada (Intoxication):

> **48.** Ah, the dumb he makes dumb, the sightless blind, the not-deaf deaf;
> this strong one robs the sensible of their sense:
> tell me, whom does the intoxication of wealth not ruin?

So there is no need to worry here. When one has his chance, it must be supposed that all have it: [for it is said that] "those named in a single rule" act together or abstain together.[^55] And so:

> **50.** In whom, from intolerance of another's excellence, there arises an impurity of mind that breeds cruelty and cunning,
> him it burns in an instant, as fire in a hollow burns a tree;
> because of it the name "villain", troubler of the world, becomes fixed on those whose hearts it has invaded —
> this is your chief minister named Matsarya (Envy), and he is secretly present here.[^56]

*(Again behind the scenes.)* Ho, Advaita!

> **51.** He for whom eighteen watches[^57] have passed like a moment —
> how can we, insignificant as we are, serve Him who is full of bliss?

Therefore now all of you glorify Him so that, out of love for His devotees, He may lessen His natural, dense bliss and turn to outward awareness.

**Kali.** Do you hear, friend, do you hear? The devotees simply cannot bear His majesty — that is why Shrivasa speaks so.

**Adharma.** I hear. But these very words betray His inner intoxication, and after it delusion (*moha*) as well. Like this:

> **51a.** If the bliss were natural, He could not give it up;
> so, swollen with inner intoxication, out of great cunning He regards the universe as a straw.
> And if He has no attachment to His own, why then, ah, does He heed their words?

**Kali.** Out of tender affection.

**Adharma.**
> In an insignificant person it is called "delusion"; in another, people call the very same thing "tender affection".[^58]

**Kali.** Ignoramus! This reasoning concerns the living soul: for insignificance and its opposite belong to the living soul.[^59] *(Again looking towards the back of the stage.)* Ah, how suddenly

> **52.** Advaita and all the others fall at once to the ground like rods,
> and all the women, first among them the wives of the four brothers, Shrivasa and the rest.

Ah, I understand!

> It is the Lord who has opened His eyes — like garlands of full-blown lotuses,
> pouring the honey of mercy, awakened by the waves of His own bliss.

*(Again, gazing for a long time.)* Ah, the greatest wonder!

> **53.** When the Lord, in a voice deep and firm as the thunder of the clouds, said: "Fix your minds on Me!" —
> and placed His lotus feet on their heads,
> they sank into the slumber of bliss — in tears, in longing, trembling,
> sighing, with hair standing on end, exulting and marvelling.[^60]

After this they will all come here. So come, let us think of some other place.

**Adharma.** Friend, where then should I seek a dwelling?

**Kali.** It has already been thought out. Listen:

> **54.** Those who, though endowed with learning, character, austerity, noble birth and an ashrama of life, and wholly self-controlled,
> yet revile the sinless deeds of this Lord — live among them.
> And let your wife Mrisha (Falsehood) dwell in the mouths of those turned away [from God],
> and your son Dambha (Hypocrisy) among those devoted to dry rituals alone. Do not grieve over this.

**Adharma.** As you please.

*(Exeunt both.)*

*End of the vishkambhaka (introductory scene).*

*(Then enters the Lord Vishvambhara, seated on the couch, in divine dress and ornaments, as if awakened from the sleep of His own bliss; around Him His retinue, Advaita and the others;[^61] at a distance, other devotees singing kirtana.)*

**The Lord.** Advaita!

> **55.** It was you who brought Me down from Goloka by the irresistible,
> supreme flood of the nectar of your love, unequalled in humility and the rest.

**Advaita** *(joining his palms)*.
> Who am I, the most insignificant? It is You Yourself who, by the will of Your own play,
> eager to show mercy to the world, have revealed Yourself on earth.

How else could it be? Kunti said:

> **56.** "How can we women see You, [who have appeared] in order to establish bhakti-yoga
> for the paramahamsas, the sages of pure heart?"[^62]

According to her word, in order to make juicy the hearts of such sages, dried up for want of rasa, Bhagavan Himself, the son of the king of Vraja — the auspicious descent who revealed the good of the world — performed His pastimes in Gokula, Mathura and Dvaraka, full of hearing, chanting and the rest, which can render all other goals of man meaningless — pastimes more astonishing even than the bliss of Brahman. And, according to the word of Uddhava:

> **57.** "Fortunately you have established unsurpassed bhakti
> for Bhagavan Uttamashloka, hard to attain even for sages"[^63] —

in the abode of Sri Vrindavana, otherwise called Gokula, which lies beyond the ocean of all rasas, in the land of boundless, unsurpassed bliss, the timid cowherd maidens — embodied potencies of bliss, whose portion is the highest share of ever-increasing love of the heart — established such a truth of divine love; and it then remained there alone. And now You, the Lord rich in good fortune, have taken a fitting body, longing to taste its flavour, which is now being attained; You ceaselessly sip mouthfuls of the nectar broth of its sweetest secret rasa — how else could we too taste the drops that seem to trickle through the gaps between Your fingers?

**The Lord.** Shrivasa! Do you remember how here, with a deft slap, I held back your departing life-breaths?

**Shrivasa** *(acting as if remembering)*.
> **58.** Ah, I know, best of lords! I remember
> that someone forcibly snatched me from death.

*(All act amazement.)*

**The Lord.** Shrivasa, tell it from the beginning. Let everyone listen.

**Shrivasa.** Before the appearance of the Lord, from infancy to the age of sixteen, this creature was very sharp-toothed and in mad arrogance constantly offended brahmins, gurus and others; my heart had reached the utmost limit of restlessness and become merciless; my mind, intoxicated and confused, was occupied with idle waves of quarrels, evil talk and the like; not even in a dream did I hear or chant the qualities of the Lord. But once, by virtue of piety accumulated before, when sleep had robbed me of consciousness, it seemed to me as if someone merciful instructed me in a dream thus: "Hey you, a brahmin in name only![^64] Who would speak words of instruction to you, fickle heart? And yet we shall say it: you have one more year to live, and then your life will end. Do not waste your life." When the night had passed, I remembered these words of instruction, lost heart because so little life was left, gave up all frivolity and fasted that day, nourished only by the nectar of that instruction. To decide what is the highest good of man, I found, as was proper, this verse of the *Nārada Purāṇa*:

> **59.** "The name of Hari, the name of Hari, the name of Hari alone!
> In the age of Kali there is no other way, no other way, no other way."[^65]

Then, regarding it as if it were the instruction of the Subduer of the Danavas Himself, I gave up all duties enjoined by the Veda and, taking refuge in the name of Hari alone, forgot even my home. Though everyone laughed at me, I, at peace, not distracted for a moment by anything else, kept remembering that day of death, held grief in my heart and counted the months. When the year was over and the day of death had come, I, with what remained of my life, came to the house of Devananda Pandita, the teacher of the *Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam*, wishing to hear him. While I was listening to the deeds of Prahlada, that very hour arrived: my consciousness clouded over, I fell into the power of death and dropped from the veranda of his house into the courtyard. Then, regarding myself as snatched by someone from the jaws of death and given life once more, I rose as if helpless. At that very moment everyone lifted me up and carried me home.

**The Lord.** It was I who showed you that dream at night; and you, whose life had already gone, I saved, giving you life again.

*(All act amazement.)*

**The Lord.**
> **60.** As iron, by the touch of the philosopher's stone, is turned into gold,
> so your body too is the same, yet, through the entry of Narada's power, as if another.

**Advaita.** So it is: otherwise the dead do not come back to life. But, Lord, all of them are associates of Your own intrinsic nature; and yet the Lord, inexhaustibly blissful, so ordained it as a lesson: "A man who worships Me obtains as it were a new body." In truth this Shrivasa, whose greatness has been sung, is Shri-vasa, the abode (*vāsa*) of the beauty (*śrī*) of devotion to You.[^66]

**The Lord.** Advaita, that is true.

**Advaita.** Lord! And Murari, Mukunda and the others — they too impart the mood of service to You and give joy to the eyes.

**The Lord.** Advaita, between these two there is [in each] a great lawlessness.

*(Then both tremble with fear, as if struck by lightning.)*

**Advaita.** Master, what is it?

**The Lord.** In Murari's mind the rasa of bhakti does not take root: in him there is only a possession by the demon of attachment to contemplation of the "supreme Self" (*adhyātma*), spreading a pungency like the stench of garlic — for even now he celebrates [the study of] the "Vasishtha" every moment.[^67]

**Advaita.** But what fault is there in adhyatma-yoga?

**The Lord.** How can you say so?

> **61.** "For one who has devotion to the Lord Hari, the master of the highest good,
> who sports in the ocean of nectar — what is the water of little ditches to him?"[^68]

**Advaita.** And what fault has Mukunda committed?

**The Lord.** He declares — it pleases him[^69] — that only the four-armed form of Bhagavan is to be worshipped.

**Advaita.** Ah, what kind of opinion is this — no opinion at all?

**The Lord.** Bhagavan's four-armed form is by His wish; His natural form is two-armed alone. For it is said: "The Supreme Brahman in human form"; "the hidden Supreme Brahman with the marks of a man";[^70] "the Supreme Self in human form".[^71] The human form is the two-armed form.

**Advaita.** "You Yourself know Yourself by Yourself, O Supreme Person."[^72] So be pleased to tell us the truth about Your own form.

**The Lord** *(graciously)*. That will be shown to you.

**Advaita.** I am favoured. But, Lord, be merciful to these two: they are already pining. Enough of anger in the heart for a grave offence.[^73]

> **62.** O parasol of Your feet — which takes away the grief caused by the poison of evil tendencies, grants cool shade,
> pours the honey of abundant mercy, and skilfully puts lotuses to shame! —
> place it on their heads: show them this mercy.

**The Lord** *(doing so)*.
> **63.** "This Bhagavan, the son of the cowherd woman, is not so easily attained by the embodied,
> nor by the learned, nor by those identified with the Atman, as by the devotees."[^74]

*(Having recited this, compassionately.)* Henceforth do not be possessed by alien inclinations.

**Both** *(falling prostrate like rods)*.
> **64.** "O Hari, may I become again a servant of the servants of those whose only refuge is Your feet;
> may my mind remember Your qualities, O Lord of my life, may my speech sing them, and my body labour [for You]."[^75]

*(So they recite.)*

**The Lord.** So be it.

**Shuklambara** *(humbly)*. Master!

> **65.** I have performed many austerities, O master, visited many holy places,
> and still my mind has found no peace. Cast a merciful glance on me, be merciful!

*(Having recited this, without timidity, with dignity and without embarrassment he lays his head on the Lord's lotus feet.)*

**Shrivasa** *(looking; aside, to Gadadhara)*.[^76] Gadadhara, look, look!

> **66.** The heart of this brahmin, hard as a thunderbolt from the proud zeal grown out of the severest austerities,
> suddenly, as soon as it touched the feet of the Lord, has as it were melted
> and is pouring out through his eyes and the hairs of his body.

And now there is this: Shachi-devi, seeing in Him only her son, has scolded us many times for sporting with Him in the bliss of kirtana: "It is they who by their evil influence have driven my son mad; his path of worldly affairs has been cut off." But if she now sees her son — who has set aside the thought that He is [her] son, helpless in the possession of the bliss born of Himself — then she will give up her motherly feeling for Him in favour of [revering Him as] God. How can this be brought about?

**Gadadhara.** If the Acharya were to tell [her]…

**Advaita.** What are you talking about?

**Shrivasa** *(in his ear)*. Just this.

**Advaita.** Very well. Let her be brought now. Why worry? Let her delusion be dispelled.

**Shrivasa.** As you command. *(Goes out, brings her and enters again.)* Master Acharya! After due thought, present this mother of the world [to the Lord]; let the mercy and favour of the Lord rejoice here.

**Advaita** *(joining his palms)*. Master! Devahuti, they say, was blessed by Kapila with the yoga of knowledge and the yoga of bhakti.[^77] But now let this mother of Vishvambhara, surrounded by the art of the good conduct of Vishvambhara's people,[^78] be blessed by nothing but the ever-increasing bliss of divine love. *(Taking her by the tips of her fingers, with the greatest joy he leads her to the Lord.)*

**Shachi** *(gazing with amazement and wonder at her son, lovely in His absorption in His own bliss, wondrous in His acts; filled with compassion for Him, her body trembling, as if illumined by inspiration — the descent of the goddess of speech — she recites something).*

> **67.** "You are the Supreme Person who at the end of the night [of the world] holds in Your body
> this whole universe, as much room as there is in it —
> and You have come to be in my womb! Ah, this is a mockery of the world of men!"[^79]

*(Glorifying Him thus, beside herself, as if possessed by a spirit, she tries to seize His feet by force.)*

**Advaita** *(holding her back, with amazement)*.
> **68.** Whence has this illumination come to her? For in her is that very natural motherly feeling:
> a former, unconditioned feeling passes over into another body too.[^80]

**The Lord.** Lady! Though you are the mother of the world, still, when your offence committed against Shrivasa and the others has ceased, the mercy of the Supreme will be yours. For offence against a devotee is the enemy of the waves of bhava.[^81]

**Advaita.** Lord, not so!

> **69.** This mother of the world is in no way at fault,
> since You, the Lord, were born from her womb.
> Ah, sons may commit offences against their mother,
> but a mother against her sons — never.

*(Turning to Shrivasa.)* Ho, Shrivasa!

> **70.** Although the daughter of Devaka[^82] saw the Lord in her son,
> yet there was not born in her such bhakti
> as in this one, who wished here to seize the feet of her son.

**Shrivasa.** Now we are at peace.

**Advaita.** Ho, ho, Shrivasa and the others! In His possession by ever-increasing majesty alone, even His filial feeling for His mother and the mother's motherly feeling have been cast aside. If so, glorify Him, so that this possession may cease.

**All.** Master, so we shall. *(Together with Advaita they do so.)* O Lord!

> **71.** Eternal is the divinity of Bhagavan, O Lord, eternal His nature of knowledge, bliss and consciousness;
> and yet, ah, whatever body He assumes, He assumes its nature too.

Therefore now, protecting us, be pleased to show this mercy: the form You have worn from childhood, revealing Your descent as a devotee, in keeping with the good fortune of such as we — the form by which You have gladdened us and cut away all our sufferings — reveal that now. As Arjuna said:

> **72.** "Seeing this gentle human form of Yours, O Janardana,
> I have now come to myself and returned to my own nature."[^83]

But in essence:

> **73.** In a transcendental object the worldly does not contradict the transcendental:
> the presence of the properties of other gems does not diminish the chintamani stone.[^84]

**The Lord** *(remembering the mood of a devotee, entering it and returning to His ordinary nature)*. Ho, Advaita and the others! Have I been all this time as if in a dream? Why did you not wake Me?

**All.** Lord, we were afraid to interrupt Your sleep of bliss.

**The Lord.** Alas, alas! So much time I have wasted, bereft of understanding. Come, then, let us sing of Sri Krishna!

**All** *(joyfully)*. As the master commands.

*(Exeunt all.)*

*Thus ends the first act of the* Sri Chaitanya-chandrodaya, *called "Absorption in His Own Bliss".*

The "moonstones" (*candra-kānta*) of bhakti rob worldly riches of their charm. The chakravaka (*koka*) is a bird which, by poetic convention, is parted from its mate at night and grieves; the rising of the moon, Gaura, is grief for Kali.
