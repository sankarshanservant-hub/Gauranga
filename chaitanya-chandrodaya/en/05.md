# Act 5. Pastimes in the Town of Advaita (Śrīmad-advaita-pura-vilāsa)

*Seized by ecstasy, the Lord wanders towards Vrindavana; Nityananda by a stratagem brings Him to Shantipura, to Advaita, where the people of Navadvipa, headed by mother Shachi, flock together.*

*(Enter Sri Krishna Chaitanya, followed by Nityananda.)*

**Sri Krishna Chaitanya.**

> **1.** Firmly fixed in this devotion to the Supreme Self,
> held by the great sages of the most ancient times,
> I shall cross over the darkness, whose far shore is hard to reach —
> by service to the feet of Mukunda alone.[^1]

*(Acts stumbling.)*

**Nityananda** *(to himself)*. Oh, a marvel!

> **2.** The nectar of love is of course the same, but, thickened on the fire of the grief of renunciation[^2]
> and as if stirred without pause, it is turning into a lump;
> in time it will, I fear, become as painful as an ulcer in the heart.

What am I, alone, to do? Well, let me think. *(Gazing again.)* Oh, a marvel!

> **3.** The dance is its waves, the loud roar of joyful cries of "hum!" its sound,
> sweat, stupor and other flashing feelings its strings of pearls:
> in this ocean of the Lord's bliss a deep current has arisen —
> and we do not know, ah, what its outcome will be!

Ah, He moves like a heap of flower-pollen driven by a strong wind! Even I, hurry as I may, cannot keep up with Him. All His senses have ceased to function: He goes now here, now there, but not at all towards any goal. Look:

> **4.** Road or roadlessness — nothing becomes an object of His gaze:
> what is high, what is low, where there is water, where forest.
> Like a wild elephant in rut He goes on and on,
> noticing neither what is ahead, nor what is behind, nor even Himself.

So it is:

> **5.** The self-satisfied[^3] attain a certain state in which the senses are without function;
> and those intoxicated with love are absorbed in the one form of Bhagavan alone.
> But if the Lord too abides in His own bliss, where then is the difference?
> Ah, I understand:
> in Bhagavan bliss is subject to Him, while the living being is subject to bliss.

What am I to do now? *(Pausing a moment.)*

> **6.** For three days now He has been without food, not even drinking water or milk — what to speak of the rest;
> a loincloth is all His clothing, and He follows only His absorption in His own joy;
> He goes on and on, noticing neither day nor night — what are we to do?
> O Gauranga, treasury of mercy, have mercy, be gracious to me who suffer!

*(Pausing a moment.)* But at this thought my heart grows a little calmer:

> **7.** This state of Mahaprabhu, helpless with bliss,
> has now become for us the healing medicine of life:
> leading Him along a road He cannot distinguish,
> I shall bring Him to the house of the lord Advaita.

*(Taking heart, he follows Him.)*

*(Behind the scenes, a united noise of voices: "Say 'Hari'! Say 'Hari'!")*

**Nityananda** *(looking ahead)*. Oh, here is a marvel: these cowherd boys, seeing the Lord, with curiosity and respect, with devotion, faith, joy and amazement, are loudly crying: "Say 'Hari'! Say 'Hari'!"

*(The Lord, by His old habit, hearing the sound of the name of Hari, as if slightly awakened from a blissful sleep, opens the lotuses of His eyes and looks in the direction from which the name of Hari comes.)*

**Nityananda** *(looking closely)*. Ah, how these cowherd boys have helped! Hearing their cry of "Hari", He, as if slightly awakened from a blissful sleep, is drawn along this very road, like one bitten by a snake who is drawn by the great mantra of a charmer.[^4]

**The Lord** *(approaching)*. Say it, say "Hari"! *(Repeats this again and again.)*

*(Enter cowherd boys; on every side they fall prostrate like rods and, clapping their hands, sing the sankirtana of the name of Hari. The Lord, listening eagerly, halts His march for a moment.)*

**Nityananda** *(joyfully)*.

> **8.** The madness caused by bliss is of different kinds: one produces restlessness,
> another a deep stupor, and yet another produces restlessness and stupor together,
> and at times it is like possession by an evil spirit.

And now the Lord's madness of bliss seems to have reached its last stage. Look:

> **9.** Opening His eyes, He looks, but makes nothing an object of His perception;
> like one half-deaf, He hears something but does not grasp the meaning.

**The Lord** *(touching their heads with His lotus hand)*. Ah, you have sung the Lord's name well, and I have obtained the fulfilment of My desires. Tell Me, then, which road leads to Vrindavana?

**Nityananda** *(joyfully)*. Here is my chance! *(Leads one of them out of the crowd.)* My boy, say: "Here is the road to Vrindavana."

**Boy.** As the master commands. *(Approaches.)* O master, here is the road to Vrindavana. *(He points out the desired road that Nityananda had pointed out to him.)*

*(The Lord, in blissful ecstasy, goes along that very road. The boys bow and go out.)*

**Nityananda.** Well, I am saved! Now my wish will be fulfilled: by this road one can reach Advaita's homestead. *(Follows Him along this road; having gone some distance — reflecting.)* Ah, why does He not recognise me? However, the ability to recognise others seems to be returning to Him little by little. Well, let me try my luck. *(Comes nearer.)*

**The Lord** *(again reciting "Firmly fixed in this devotion to the Supreme Self…" and so on)*. Ah, well sang the mendicant monk! Only by service to Mukunda shall I cross over the darkness, not by this devotion to the Supreme Self: one need only be fixed in it, but it is not the goal.[^5] So I shall go to Vrindavana and make service to Mukunda my service of the mind. *(Fixing His gaze on the sky.)* Ho, is it far to Vrindavana?

**Nityananda** *(approaching)*. Master, to Vrindavana is a day's journey.

**The Lord** *(as if between sleep and waking, with amazement)*. What? Ah, is it you, Shripada Nityananda?

**Nityananda.** Master, it is I. *(Unable to finish, he falls silent: tears choke his throat.)*

**The Lord.** Shripada, tell Me, where have you come from?

**Nityananda.** Hearing of the master's wish to go to Vrindavana, I, wishing to see it myself, set out and joined the Lord.

**The Lord.** Good, ah, good! Let us go together, then. *(Walks on joyfully.)*

**Nityananda.** Master, this way, this way! *(Having led Him some distance.)* Master, not far from here flows the divine Yamuna. It would be good to bathe in her.

**The Lord.** What, today I shall see the Yamuna?

**Nityananda.** Of course!

**The Lord** *(acting joy)*.[^6] Shripada, where is she, where is she?

**Nityananda.** This way, this way! *(Having led Him some distance, he comes to the Ganga.)* Master, here she is, here is the Yamuna!

**The Lord** *(bowing joyfully, He glorifies her)*.

> **10.** Vessel of the highest love for the Sun of consciousness and bliss, the eternal son of Nanda,[^7]
> whose body is liquid Brahman,
> cutter-away of sins,[^8] bestower of good on the world —
> may the daughter of Mitra purify our bodies![^9]

**Nityananda.** Master, immerse Yourself in her.

**The Lord.** As you please. *(Acts bathing.)*

**Nityananda** *(to himself)*. Ah, I am happy: a huge, maddened wild elephant has been tamed by a mere mantra![^10] But something of this business still remains — I shall arrange that too. *(Looking around, he calls someone.)*

*(Enter a certain man, who bows.)*

**Nityananda** *(aside, to him alone)*. Listen, here, not far off, on the other bank of the Ganga, is the town of the venerable Advaita. Go there quickly and report: "Nityananda is here nearby with a certain other sannyasi and is waiting for you. So make haste."

**Man.** I shall do so at once. *(Goes out hurriedly.)*

**Nityananda** *(to himself)*. Ah, today it is three days since I have touched even water. I too shall bathe. *(Does so.)*

*(Behind the scenes.)*

> **11.** Our lives, bound by His virtues as by ropes[^11] twisted double with the fetters of hope,
> though worn out in separation from Him, could not — alas! — depart.
> But now they have done us a service, by revealing His face —
> for when fate is favourable, even the unfavourable suddenly becomes favourable.[^12]

**Nityananda** *(listening from afar)*. Oh, it is the Acharya himself beginning to sing! So fate has arranged everything well. Now my heavy burden has become light. *(Looking at the Lord.)* Ah, woe!

> **12.** His body is drenched by streams of water; from shyness, being unaccustomed,
> He has not even taken off His loincloth and upper cloth to wring the water out of them;[^13]
> His head is covered with a heap of red lotus petals —
> I see the Lord, golden-fair, like the best of elephants coming out after bathing.

Let me see whether Advaita will come soon. *(Looks ahead.)*[^14]

*(Enter Advaita, acting impatience, surrounded on every side by a countless retinue.)*

**Advaita** *(looking ahead)*. Ah, here He is, here is the Lord!

> **13.** Although from afar, without His tuft of hair and in reddish garments, He seems another,
> yet by His radiance like that of a golden image and by the abundance of His beauty
> He appears to us the very same.

Ah, how beautiful!

> **14.** Look: this red garment makes that same body of the Lord,
> yellow as gold, like the beauty of a mango fruit —
> the essence of renunciation, golden-red,
> filled to the brim with great juice.[^15]

*(Hurriedly approaching and standing before the Lord, whose eyes are closed in meditation, he sobs aloud.)*

**The Lord** *(quickly opening His eyes)*. What? Is it magnanimous Advaita Acharya?

**Nityananda.** Master, so it is.

**The Lord** *(embracing him tightly)*. Tell Me, how did you learn that I am here? Or how did you, following Me, come to be in Vrindavana too? Or am I dreaming this?

**Advaita** *(with tears, to himself)*. Ah, the Lord is really convinced that He is in Vrindavana! *(Aloud.)* Master, this is no dream: it is I, the same wretched man. *(Acts falling down.)*

**The Lord** *(raising him in His embrace, with tears)*. So be it. O Advaita, you yourself are Vrindavana, for the Lord's lotus feet abide in you constantly. Tell Me, then, where have I come?

**Advaita.** Here is the divine Bhagirathi,[^16] and here, on the other bank, is my town.

**The Lord** *(acting the return to outward consciousness)*. Shripada, but you said that this was the Yamuna.

**Nityananda.** Whether the Yamuna flows in her or not — let the master know for Himself.[^17]

**The Lord.** Shripada by his play has made Me act a part.[^18]

*(Advaita again recites the verse "Our lives, bound…" (5.11), substituting at will the word "Your" for the words "His" and "that".)*[^19]

**Nityananda.** O Advaita, ever since He took up the staff, the staff has become a punishment[^20] for me: three days without food! But the Lord is satisfied merely by tasting His own bliss. So enough of lofty talk here.

**Advaita** *(taking from a servant's hands a new loincloth and upper cloth, he bathes the Lord again and, full of compassion, has Him put them on)*.

> **15.** Before, I clothed You in garments worthy of God,
> and now I clothe You in the garb worthy of a mendicant monk.
> Your beauty is the same, and the same the serenity of Your face —
> alas, alas, only to our eyes it is unbearable![^21]

My house is quite near. May the Lord's feet deign to adorn it.

**The Lord.** So that is why Shripada deceived Me!

**Advaita.** No one can deceive the Lord. However:

> **16.** "The Lord can be constrained by nature — but only by His own maya;
> being independent, He, like crystal, takes colour from contact with what is near" —
> so some of us say here. But in truth everything in the Lord — ah! —
> is the flashing of a play like a child's game.

In any case, You have not been deceived. And there is no fault of his in this, for he is Shripada: "*śrīpa*" is the one who bestows Shri, that is, Krishna; and "*śrīpāda*" is the one who takes Him away.[^22] That is just what he did. So let the venerable ones go ahead. Today let the Lord accept His first alms in my house.

**The Lord.** As you please. Show the way, then.

**Advaita.** This way, this way. *(Seats them in a boat.)*

**Nityananda** *(having listened)*. O Advaita, have you sent anyone to Navadvipa?

**Advaita.** Of course. Everyone has almost arrived.

**The Lord.** O Advaita, to come to your house is something unprecedented for us.

**Advaita.** How can I compare with the good fortune of Shrivasa, in whose house the Lord played every day!

**Nityananda.** O Advaita, now a great throng of people will begin. For news of the Lord spreads of itself, and all the more has the news spread that the Lord is going to Mathura. Now, when the blessed Lord is gracing your house,[^23] all the people — children, old men and young — will gather at once to see this ocean of compassion. So while they have not yet found out, let us enter your house unnoticed.

**Advaita.** Let us do so. *(Walking a few steps.)* O Lord, here, here is our house. Enter, then. *(All act entering.)*

*(Behind the scenes.)*

> **17.** That very Lord Vishvambhara, having deceived His mother
> and taken the form of an ascetic, wanted to go to Mathura,
> but Nityananda by a stratagem has brought Him to Shantipura —
> ho, come, ho, come and see Him!

**Nityananda** *(having listened and looked)*. Advaita, do you hear and see? Many thousands of the local people have suddenly gathered here. A little more — and there will be hundreds of thousands. So let doorkeepers stand at the doors.

*(Advaita, placing doorkeepers at every door and letting the Lord go first, acts entering together with Nityananda and goes out. Then enter people longing to see the Lord.)*

**Some.**

> **18.** Although that He is now in another form than the one in which He appeared to our eyes in Navadvipa
> does trouble the heart,
> yet our thirst — Shiva, Shiva! — only grows,
> for the true essence, which cannot be taken away, is equally sweet in its natural and in its altered state.

Let us find out now where the Lord is. *(They walk about.)*

**Others** *(from another side)*.

> **19.** The great sweetness that this Lord had in His former ashrama
> did not become a guest of our eyes;
> if we do not see it now either, then shame on our birth,
> shame on our body, ah, shame on our life, shame on our eyes!

**Yet others.** Hey, this way, this way! We have heard that the Lord has just entered the town of Advaita. Let us go in there. *(Approaching impatiently.)* Ah, these doorkeepers let no one in. Well, we must persuade them and give them something. *(They come nearer.)*

*(Enter the doorkeepers, canes in hand, standing at the gates.)*

**Doorkeeper.** Hey, people, wait a little. Since the Lord accepted the fourth ashrama He has eaten nothing. Today He will accept alms. So do not make a noise now. Sit down and wait: after the meal you will be able to see the Lord.

*(Behind the scenes.)* Master, this way, this way!

**Doorkeeper.** Let me have a look. *(Climbing onto a raised place and stretching his neck.)* Ah, the Lord has already accepted alms, for here He is:

> **20.** His body anointed with sandal paste, wearing a new red garment,
> a white garland shining on His chest, golden in lustre,
> He triumphs like a jewelled mountain[^24] that has taken on the beauty
> of the snows, of the evening glow and of the stream of the Ganga.

How will these eager people see Him? *(Gazing again.)* Ah, the lord Advaita has arranged things well: he has seated the Lord in the middle of a high pavilion. Now all will see Him without difficulty, and this pavilion has justified its name.[^25]

*(All the people impatiently cry in chorus: "Say 'Hari'!")*[^26]

*(Then enter the Lord, seated in the said pavilion, Advaita and the others.)*

**Advaita.**

> **21.** What play is this You have staged: You have accepted the fourth ashrama,
> which is so dear to the adherents of *advaita*?[^27]

**The Lord** *(laughing)*.

> Remember, O Advaita — are We not, alas, adherents of Advaita?
> For the whole difference between it and you lies only in form and gender.[^28]

**Advaita.** Is it fitting for the lord of speech to play with words?

**The Lord.** Let them understand it in essence:

> **22.** Without renouncing everything, worship of the Lord of life is impossible —
> that is why I have made the renunciation; what has talk of *advaita* to do with it?
> "This staff is a very strong punishment[^29] for the beast that is the mind" —
> with this thought alone I, without special deliberation,[^30] took up the staff.

**Advaita.** All this is merely a pretext. In reality it is only to reveal the meaning of such names as "Maker of sannyasa, the Restrained, the Tranquil, the Refuge of steadfastness and peace".[^31]

**Doorkeeper** *(looking over everyone in front)*. Ah, a wonder!

> **23.** Even in such an impenetrable crush, uneven with the jostling of the throngs of people,
> this Lord is seen without difficulty by all, though they are past counting:[^32]
> unhurried, glad to have found a little place, and comfortably settled,
> they gaze at Him to their heart's content, honoured with His glance full of mercy.

And these are no idle words, for:

> **24.** "Today the ocean of existence has been crossed, today the gates of Yama too are closed,
> the fruit of human birth is attained and all the feats of austerity are accomplished,
> for I have seen God, full of the sweetness of merciful glances" —
> so all, each one separately, proclaim, ah, their happiness.

*(Behind the scenes, noise on every side.)*

**People.** Ah, all the people of the Lord's birthplace are coming! Now there will be a great crush. Let us get out of here. *(Exeunt all.)*

*(Then enter all the people of Navadvipa.)*

**They.**

> **25.** Today blindness has left our eyes, today the quarters have become clear,
> today the dried-up creepers of the thirst for life are putting forth shoots,
> and into the ruined heart someone has placed consciousness (*caitanya*) —
> ah, so that is it: today for us the Moon Chaitanya will rise![^33]

*(They approach impatiently.)*

**Advaita** *(seeing them)*.

> **26.** Letting the Lord's mother go first, there have come
> these friends headed by Shrivasa Pandita,
> and others — children, youths, old men. What more is there to say?
> It is the same land arisen anew, it is the same time![^34]

*(The Lord hurriedly acts descending. The doorkeepers respectfully let the newcomers through. The Lord bows to His mother.)*

**Mother** *(with tears, trembling and with a faltering voice, in which fear, reverence, motherly tenderness and joy are mingled — in Sanskrit)*.[^35]

> **27.** Who are You — renunciation itself, or realisation,
> or bhakti, or the highest rasa that has taken a body?
> My son, I see in You only the infant at my breast.
> Now that I have found You, I shall never let You go again!

*(Impatiently embraces Him.)*

**Sri Krishna Chaitanya.**

> **28.** Divine mother of the universe, O mother! Can the creeper called motherly love
> bear[^36] any fruit higher and better than this?
> You, lady, love the whole world without self-interest —
> surely the Lord has given a body to Forbearance herself![^37]

*(He recites it again. His mother embraces Him again and bows. Advaita raises her and, himself leading her into the inner apartments, goes out. The Lord gladdens everyone, each in his own way, with embraces, touches, glances, questions and the rest.)*

**Advaita** *(hurriedly entering again)*. Ho, who is there? Serve everyone — children, old men, youths, down to the chandalas — with whatever they wish: lodging, food, drink and the rest.

**One of the servants** *(entering, tossing aside the curtain)*. Here I am, here, I shall do it all at once. *(Goes out.)*

**Advaita** *(approaching the Lord)*.

> **29.** I am the same, they are the same, You are the same, and their love is the same,
> and Your compassion is the very same;
> all is as before, the single source of happiness;
> only Your other form is the cause of our sorrow.

**The Lord.** Advaita, Advaita, not so!

> **30.** My body has been cast into the stream of dark nectar[^38]
> and is borne along swiftly by its high wave;
> whatever state it comes into — auspicious or inauspicious —
> each only kindles love in Me more and more.

Come, let us sit in private with those whom we have not seen for so long.

*(Exeunt all.)*

*Thus ends the fifth act of the* Sri Chaitanya-chandrodaya, *called "Pastimes in the Town of Advaita".*

[^1]: *Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 11.23.57 (11.23.58 in GGM), the words of the Avanti brahmin, a mendicant monk (bhikshu) who had taken up renunciation. Chaitanya repeats them, having just accepted sannyasa.

[^2]: *Nirveda* is both "despondency, grief" and "renunciation of the world"; *śṛta*, "boiled": the image of milk which, after long boiling and stirring, thickens into a lump (*piṇḍa*). Nityananda fears that the Lord's love, "boiled down" in the grief of sannyasa, will become a torment to Him.

[^3]: *Ātmārāma*, those who delight in the Atman (cf. *Bhāg.* 1.7.10). In the last line there is the play *ānanda-nighna*: Bhagavan is the one to whom bliss is subject (*nighnānanda*, so the Kāvyamālā; GGM has the misprint *nighānando*), while the jiva is itself subject to bliss.

[^4]: *Mahā-mantrākṛṣṭa iva phaṇi-daṣṭas*, literally "like one bitten by a snake, drawn by a great mantra"; cf. the belief that a charmer by his mantra makes the snake that has bitten return. The verb *anusarpati* ("creeps after") plays on the image of the snake. *Phaṇi-daṣṭa*, "bitten by a snake" (a man), not the snake itself: so M. Guha's commentary and Bengali translation (*phaṇinā daṣṭaḥ*) and Krishnadasa Baba's Hindi translation explain it; the "great mantra" is here at once a charm and the cry of the name of Hari.

[^5]: A play on words: in verse 5.1 *samāsthāya* ("firmly fixed"); the Lord interprets: for *parātma-niṣṭhā* only *āsthā* (support, adherence) is needed, while the goal is service to Mukunda. GGM has the corrupt *mukundasyevaiva mānasī*; translated according to the Kāvyamālā, *mukunda-sevaiva mānasīkartavyā*.

[^6]: So according to the Kāvyamālā (*harṣaṁ nāṭayitvā*); GGM has *iyaṁ nāṭayitvā*, an obvious corruption.

[^7]: A play: *sadā nanda-sūnoḥ* can also be read *sadānanda-sūnoḥ*, "of the son of eternal bliss".

[^8]: GGM has *lavitrī* ("cutter, reaper"), the Kāvyamālā *khanitrī* ("digger, underminer").

[^9]: The Yamuna is the daughter of the Sun god (Mitra, Surya). In GGM this verse is wrongly numbered 11 (the number 10 is missing), and Advaita's verse 5.13 is missing altogether; here the numbering of verses 10–13 follows the Kāvyamālā; GGM's numbers 11–13 correspond to 10–12. The verse is quoted in the *Chaitanya-charitamrita* (Madhya 3.28; there, as in GGM, *lavitrī*).

[^10]: *Mantra* is both a charm and "secret counsel, stratagem": the elephant has been tamed by Nityananda's device.

[^11]: *Guṇa* is both "virtue" and "thread, rope". The verse is sung behind the scenes by Advaita, approaching the bank.

[^12]: Following GGM, *diṣṭe hīṣṭe* (= *hi iṣṭe*); the Kāvyamālā has *diṣṭe dṛṣṭe*, "when fate is revealed".

[^13]: Translated according to the Kāvyamālā (*na nirgālya*); GGM has *nirgīlya* without the negative. The sannyasi, unaccustomed to a mere loincloth, is too shy to take it off to wring it out.

[^14]: This sentence, Advaita's entrance and verse 13 are found only in the Kāvyamālā; in GGM they have dropped out, and the next verse (5.14) is wrongly attributed there to Nityananda. Verse 13 is also in the edition of Jivananda Vidyasagara; its second quarter reads *dūrād asau ced anasāv ivekṣyate*, "if from afar He seems not Himself [but someone else]" (Jivananda's commentary: *anasau = itara*); Krishnadasa Baba's Hindi translation understands the passage the same way.

[^15]: *Rasa* is both "juice" and aesthetic/loving experience: the Lord is the "ripe fruit" of renunciation, full of great rasa. The saffron garment of the sannyasi is compared to the red skin of a ripe mango.

[^16]: The Bhagirathi is the Ganga (the name of its western branch in Bengal). Shantipura lies on its left bank.

[^17]: An ambiguous answer: below Prayaga the Yamuna does flow in the waters of the Ganga, so Nityananda has not, formally, lied.

[^18]: *Nāṭya*, "acting, theatrical play": the Lord says He has been "played a trick on".

[^19]: So according to the Kāvyamālā (*tvac-chabdaṁ dattvā*: "inserting the word 'You'"), that is, "by Your virtues", "in separation from You", "Your face". GGM has *yac-chabde tac-chabde ca svacchandaṁ dattvā*, "freely changing the words 'which' and 'that'".

[^20]: A play on words: *daṇḍa* is both the sannyasi's "staff" and "punishment".

[^21]: *Viṣama* is both "unequal, incongruous" and "hard, unbearable": the beauty is the same, but the eyes of His friends cannot bear this change.

[^22]: A jesting etymology: *śriyaṁ prāti iti śrīpaḥ*, "the bestower (or 'filler') of wealth/Lakshmi", is Krishna; *śrīpaṁ ādadāti iti śrīpādaḥ*, "the one who takes the Shripa". The honorary title of a sannyasi, "Shripada", is interpreted as "the one who carries Krishna off".

[^23]: *Tad-bhavana-subhagaṁbhāvuka-bhāvuke bhagavati* (in M. Guha *bhavad-bhavana-…*), "when the Lord is inclined to bless your house"; *subhagaṁbhāvuka* in Guha's commentary means "inclined to bestow good fortune" (*saubhāgya-bhāvana-śīla*), and in his Bengali translation: "when your house is sanctified by the touch of the Lord's feet".

[^24]: That is, Mount Meru; the snow is the white garland, the evening glow the red garment, the stream of the Ganga the sandal paste (or the other way round).

[^25]: *Upakārikā*, "pavilion, marquee", but literally "the helper": the pavilion has helped the people to see the Lord.

[^26]: This and the next stage direction are found only in the Kāvyamālā.

[^27]: A play on words: *advaita-bhāj* means both "adherents of *advaita*" (non-dual Vedanta, among whom sannyasa is held in honour) and "devotees of Advaita".

[^28]: That is, *advaitam* (the doctrine, neuter) and *advaitaḥ* (Advaita Acharya, masculine) differ only in the form of the word and grammatical gender (*liṅga*, also "outward mark"). The Lord jests that He is an "advaitist" only in the sense of devotion to Advaita.

[^29]: See note 20: *daṇḍa*, staff and punishment.

[^30]: *Aviśeṣāt*, "without special deliberation": in M. Guha's commentary *viśeṣa-vivecanā-rahitatvāt*, in his Bengali translation "I had no time to consider further"; Krishnadasa Baba's Hindi translation: "simply, in the ordinary way". Another possible understanding, "not as an adherent of any school" (i.e. not for the sake of Mayavada), is not supported by the translations.
