# Act 7. Pilgrimage (Tīrthāṭanaḥ)

*King Prataparudra questions Sarvabhauma about the Lord, who has gone to the south; the brahmins returning from the Godavari tell of the healing of Vasudeva and of the meeting with Raya Ramananda; Malla Bhatta, envoy of the king of Karnata, relates the wonders of the Lord in the southern lands; behind the scenes the Lord's return to Purushottama is announced.*

*(Enter Sarvabhauma.)*

**Sarvabhauma.** Ah, why has the Gajapati[^1] summoned me today at an unusual hour? Well, I must go to him. *(Walks.)*

*(Enter the King, and around him his retinue.)*

**King.** Ho, who is there? Call the Bhattacharya.

**Bhattacharya** *(approaching)*. Here I am — I have come without waiting to be called.[^2]

**King** *(bowing)*. Here is a seat, sit down. *(The Bhattacharya, pronouncing a blessing, sits down.)* Bhattacharya, I have heard that a certain king of ascetics, of great power and supreme compassion, has now come from Gauda.

**Bhattacharya.** Just so.

**King.** How could I bow to His feet?

**Bhattacharya.** That is extremely difficult. Those who rarely show themselves among people are within sight only of one who has nothing.[^3] Besides, it is now several days since He went to the southern quarter.

**King.** Why did He go, leaving the nearness of the great lord Jagannatha?

**Bhattacharya.** "They make holy places holy, bearing within them the Holder of the club"[^4] — such is the nature even of ordinary great souls. And He is Bhagavan Himself.

**King** *(with amazement)*. Bhattacharya! Since you say so, it must truly be so. Why did you not try to keep Him here?

**Bhattacharya.** Maharaja!

> **1.** He at the movement of whose brows Brahma and the other guardians of the worlds rise and fall like waves —
> without His goddess Mercy He yields to no one's power.

And yet:

> **2.** What praises did I not offer! What piteous entreaties did I not devise!
> What threats did I not act out — even that I would part with my life!
> How I wept, clasping His feet! — and still He went.
> In the great, by nature, mercy and refusal are one and the same.

**King** *(longingly)*. Will He return here?

**Bhattacharya.** Certainly. For His companions have stayed here.

**King.** How then did He go alone?

**Bhattacharya.** Can such a one as He ever be alone?[^5] And still I gave Him worthy brahmins as companions.

**King.** How far?

**Bhattacharya.** They will go as far as the Godavari. And the Lord Himself, I think, will go all the way to Setubandha.[^6]

**King.** Why were the brahmins not sent with Him to the end?

**Bhattacharya.** He did not allow it. And He accepted their company as far as the Godavari only for the sake of Ramananda.

**King.** What is this consideration concerning him?

**Bhattacharya.** When He had firmly decided to go, I said: "On the bank of the Godavari lives Ramananda. He must certainly be favoured."

**King.** Whence such good fortune for him?

**Bhattacharya.** Maharaja! He is a born Vaishnava. Before, he was an object of mockery among us; but now, since the Lord's mercy has descended on us, we have come to know his greatness.

**King.** I have heard what mercy has descended on you yourself.

**Bhattacharya.** The Lord's power reveals itself.

**Doorkeeper** *(entering)*. Bhattacharya! The brahmins you sent have returned from the Godavari and are waiting for you.

**Bhattacharya.** The King is pining with impatience. Let them be brought straight here.

**Doorkeeper.** As you command. *(Goes out and enters again, leading them.)*

**Bhattacharya.** Oh, come in, come in!

*(The brahmins approach, honour the King with blessings and bow to Sarvabhauma.)*

**King.** Sit down and tell everything from the very beginning. *(All sit down.)*

**Bhattacharya.** Let one of you tell it.

**One of the brahmins.** From here He first went to the god Alalanatha,[^7] beheld Him and offered Him praise. He walked, skilled in His gait like an irresistible leader of intoxicated elephants who knows no goad — so that even we, hurrying with all our might, could barely keep up with Him — moving like a mass of Mount Sumeru split off and driven by a mighty north wind. Ceaselessly chanting the names of the Lord, with a voice broadened by sankirtana, deep and firm, He drowned the rumbling of a train of rain clouds; and the people along the road could not take their eyes off Him, all their thoughts fixed on Him. So in a very short time He reached Kurma-kshetra.[^8]

**Sarvabhauma.** And on the way did He take no food?

**Brahmin.** No, He took none.

**Bhattacharya.** And what did you do?

**Brahmin.** We snatched a bite of whatever came to hand and followed without losing sight of Him. There, in Kurma-kshetra, He bowed to the god Kurma,[^9] offered Him praise and stayed in the house of the best of the twice-born named Kurma. He at once invited Him, and when the Lord had accepted food from him, He did something else — utterly astonishing.

**Bhattacharya.** What was it?

**Brahmin.** There lived a brahmin named Vasudeva, sick with running leprosy: his whole body was wet with thick blood and pus, swarming with a multitude of worms. When the worms fell off him, he picked them up and put them back in the same place — without any disgust, as if his mind were absorbed in the Lord. No one told the Lord about him, but the Lord found him Himself. And at once, as if he were the closest of friends found again after a long separation, He tightly embraced him with His long arms — just as he was. And then this brahmin,

> **3.** "Who am I — a pauper, most sinful — and who is Krishna, the abode of Shri?
> Yet me, 'an unworthy brahmin', He has clasped in His arms!"[^10] —

had scarcely uttered this when his body at once became radiant and beautiful, as it had been by nature. Then he said: "Lord! Why have You punished me so? As long as I was like that — diseased — I lived without grief[^11] and without agitation. But now, when honour comes to me, all the movements of the mind will rise up again." The Lord said: "Brahmin! From now on, apart from remembrance of the Lord, your mind will turn to no outward affairs. So give up your worry."

**King.** Bhattacharya! Truly He is Ishvara. Otherwise such compassion is impossible for a jiva — while curing leprosy is something even a great yogi can do.

**Sarvabhauma.** And then, and then?

**Brahmin.** And then He, whose power is inconceivable, came to Nrisimha-kshetra,[^12] beheld the Lord Nrisimha, offered Him praise, bowed, circumambulated Him and went on.

> **4.** With waves of rays like those of a golden mountain He seemed to make the southern quarter golden (*gaura*),[^13]
> and by His appearance — a river surging with mercy — He melted people's hearts everywhere.

And further:

> **5.** "Krishna, Krishna, glory, Krishna, Krishna, oh!
> Krishna, Krishna, glory, Krishna, Krishna, oh!
> Krishna, Krishna, glory, Krishna, Krishna, oh!
> Krishna, Krishna, glory, Krishna — save us!"

> **6.** So with streams of the nectar of words — tender, captivating, in a voice broadening like a cloud —
> He delighted both ears of all who had ears, and stole their hearts as well — and went on.

**Sarvabhauma.** You speak the truth. Such is His nature.

**Brahmin.** Then He came to the bank of the Godavari and settled down to rest — He who gladdens the hearts of the people of the whole world, charming with matchless mercy, rich in the good fortune of prema, the Lord — like a grove of golden ketakas filling the whole circle of the quarters with fragrance, the abode of all virtues.[^14] And through His fame, which spread of itself, a throng of "gods of the earth" — brahmins — gathered there, conjecturing: "Who is this? Who is this?"[^15] Someone invited Him to a meal. At once the news of Him, full of endless mystery, a delight to the ears of many people, spread everywhere — and Raya Ramananda came, as if drawn by a mantra, as if possessed by a spirit, as if he had lost his balance from the shock of amazement and rapture.

**King.** Ramananda! Blessed are you, blessed! Those feet have been revealed to your eyes!

**Brahmin.** At once, on arriving, he himself fell at the lotus feet, bathed in tears.[^16] "Ah, so you are Ramananda!" the Lord said graciously, and he answered: "Yes." Then the Lord said: "At Sarvabhauma's request I wished to see you, and so I have stayed here a little while. You have done very well to come yourself. So tell Me something." And although Ramananda had no special knowledge of the Lord — who He was, what His name, what His greatness, what His purpose, what His essence — still, filled with the bliss of an old, constant friendship, like a man of feeling (*bhāvuka*),[^17] without any timidity he began to recite something:

> **7.** If the mind is not conquered, what use are austerity and the rest?
> But what victory over the mind is there if one does not think of Madhava?
> And what thinking is that, if — alas! — the heart does not melt?
> And how can it melt if the *vāsanās*, the traces of former impressions, have not been washed away?

**The Lord.** This is external.

> **8.** **The Lord.** What is knowledge?
> **Ramananda.** Devotion to Hari alone, not skill in the Vedas and the rest.
> **The Lord.** What is fame?
> **Ramananda.** The report "He is devoted to the Lord", not that born of gifts and the like.
> **The Lord.** What is wealth?
> **Ramananda.** To be dear to Him, not an abundance of money, people, villages and the rest.
> **The Lord.** What is sorrow?
> **Ramananda.** Separation from one who is dear to the Lord, not the pain of heart-wounds and the like.[^18]

**The Lord.** Good. Who is liberated?

**Ramananda.**

> **9.** Those whose nearness is to the feet of Hari, full of love (*anurāga*), not to passion (*rāga*);
> whose fondness is for the bhakti-yoga of Hari, abounding in prema, not for yoga;
> whose support is in the fragrant anointing of the surge of love, not in the body —
> they, juicy by nature, are the liberated (*muktā*) — not the pearls (*muktā*).[^19]

**The Lord.** So be it.

> **10.** **The Lord.** What should be sung?
> **Ramananda.** The deeds of the pastimes of Vraja.
> **The Lord.** What is the highest good here?
> **Ramananda.** The company of saints.
> **The Lord.** What should be remembered?
> **Ramananda.** The name of the Enemy of Agha.[^20]
> **The Lord.** On what should one meditate?
> **Ramananda.** On the feet of Murari.
> **The Lord.** Where should one dwell?
> **Ramananda.** In Vraja alone.
> **The Lord.** What is a delight to the ears?
> **Ramananda.** The pastimes of Vrindavana alone.
> **The Lord.** What should be worshipped?
> **Ramananda.** Here — the two radiances called Sri Krishna and Radha.

**The Lord.** Good. Speak, speak further.

**Ramananda** *(to himself)*. What can be said beyond this? What has been said already answers the questions. And what must now be said — whether it will please Him or not, I do not know. *(Reflecting a moment, aloud.)*

> **11.** Let those who do not know rasa suck the bitter nimba fruit, nirvana;
> but we, knowers of the essence of rasa, drink the dark nectar
> that remains after it has been sipped by the cupped hands of the eyes
> of the cowherd maidens, languid with love.[^21]

**The Lord.** Say something more. This is about the same thing.

**Ramananda.** Beyond this there is nothing more to expound. What more can be said? *(Reflecting, aloud.)*

> **12.** Those that make the roads seem to have been licked by flocks of young chakoras;[^22]
> those that in an instant turn into crystal the rows of footstools adorned with jewels;
> those that on feet washed and wiped give rise to the suspicion that drops of water are trickling from them —
> may these moonbeams of the jewel-nails of Krishna's lotus feet protect you[^23] for ever!

**The Lord.** This is mere poetry. Say more.

**Ramananda** *(reflecting a moment)*.

> **13.** The juice of the red lac from Radha's lotus feet on Hari's chest,
> putting to shame the Shrivatsa, the Kaustubha and the goddess Rama[^24] —
> like the orb of the radiance of the morning sun taken captive by darkness under a veil,
> like a red lotus in full bloom in the waters of the Kalindi — may it protect us!

**The Lord.** This too is the same.

**Ramananda** *(clasping His feet)*.

> **14.** Friend! There is no difference between us: "He is the beloved, I am the beloved one";
> it is as if Madana himself had forcibly ground both our hearts together in the juice of love.[^25]

Or else:

> **15.** Then there was no thought: "I am the beloved, You are the lover";
> all the movements of the mind had gone, and even the notion of "you" and "I" had vanished for us.
> But now there is the conviction: "You are the husband, I am the wife" —
> and still life holds on in me. What could be more astonishing?[^26]

**Sarvabhauma.** And what did the Lord say to that?

**Brahmin.** Hear what was seen then:

> **16.** Like a serpent with raised hood listening to the song of a snake-charmer,
> He listened to what was said with great delight and attention;
> and then the Lord — whether because what was said was out of place, or from helplessness in bliss —
> covered his mouth with His lotus hand.[^27]

**King.** Bhattacharya! What is the meaning here?

**Bhattacharya.**[^28] Maharaja! Love free of all conditions (*upādhi*) does not tolerate the slightest condition. Hearing in the first half of the unconditioned love of the divine couple, Krishna and Radha, the Lord acknowledged it as the supreme goal of man. And that He covered Ramananda's mouth shows that it is a most intimate secret.

**Brahmin.** Then Ramananda parted his hair in two, wound it round both His feet, fell prostrate and spoke:

> **17.** Crest-jewel of the great knowers of rasa, preceptor of the pastimes in the drama full of rasa —
> You are the lord of my heart: how then can we praise You?
> To take on various roles — that too is natural to You;
> therefore the role of an ascetic is not so astonishing to us.[^29]

*(And for a long time he wept, holding the lotus feet, and from time to time said something.)*

> **18.** Has not fate unexpectedly brought me a treasure?
> Has not the moon of nectar broken, that it pours forth so?
> For I have beheld Your lotus feet, O God —
> the beautifully ripened fruit of the tree of bliss![^30]

"Lord, as I saw You today in a dream, so I see You awake." *(And again, clasping His feet, he weeps. The Lord embraces him tightly.)* After this that best of brahmins who had invited the Lord came and said: "Master, it is already past noon." And the Lord went to perform the midday rites. And we, bowing to His lotus feet, set out on the return journey that same day.

**Sarvabhauma.** Then rest now.

**Brahmin.** As you command.

*(The King gives them a reward. The brahmins accept it respectfully and go out. Then enters the doorkeeper.)*

**Doorkeeper.** Sire! The minister of the king of Karnata, the great maharaja, a king of pandits named Malla Bhatta, has brought a gift sent by him and stands at the door.

**Sarvabhauma.** I know him: he is a great pandit.

**King.** Let him enter.

**Doorkeeper.** As the King commands. *(Goes out and enters, leading him.)*

**Sarvabhauma.** Welcome, venerable Bhatta! *(Acts rising.)*

**Malla Bhatta** *(honouring the King with blessings)*. Bhattacharya! What are you doing? It is not for you to rise before me. However —

> **19.** Ever lofty is the golden mountain, ever deep are the rain clouds,
> ever humble are the wise, whose sole ornament is modesty:
> greatness in people is bound up with their very nature.[^31]

**King.** Here is a seat.

**Malla Bhatta.** I sit, as bidden. *(Sits down.)*

**King.** Is the king of Karnata well?

**Malla Bhatta.** One who has friends like you is always well. And now even more so.

**King.** How so?

**Malla Bhatta.** Maharaja!

> **20.** Since the time when from this land, on the pretext of a holy pilgrimage,
> a certain unique king of ascetics, shining like molten gold —
> ah! — came to the southern quarter, since then he too has found peace.

**Sarvabhauma** *(with eager impatience)*. Venerable Bhatta, tell it, tell it!

**King.** In what way?

**Malla Bhatta.** In the southern quarter, in ascending order: a few people are devoted to rites, very few to knowledge, the Sattvatas[^32] can be counted on one's fingers, the Pashupatas[^33] are more numerous, and most numerous of all are the heretics. And because of association with them — constantly confused by mutual disputes, foremost in the art of every kind of interpretation[^34] — our sovereign was almost always in great anxiety.

**King.** And then, and then?

**Malla Bhatta.** And then, as soon as this lord of ascetics unexpectedly appeared, people began to flock together from all the quarters and intermediate quarters — old and young, youths — with joyful amazement; and the hosts of pandits who had come out of curiosity also experienced His greatness, revealed by the mere beauty of His body, the highest delight for the eyes — and even without any instruction, from a special impression arising at that moment: "Where have we been until now!"[^35] — with trembling hair and tears, every one of them fell away from his own doctrine and entered upon His path. Hearing of this from one to another, our protector of the world of men attained the highest bliss and, to learn the details of His deeds, sent brahmins in disguise — as far as Setubandha and until His return from there. Hearing from their lips of the wonders of His transcendental deeds, he forgot the flame of the forest fire of worldly existence.

**King.** Minister, blessed is your sovereign!

**Sarvabhauma.** What, then, did you hear? Tell us something.

**King.** Minister, tell it.

**Malla Bhatta.** Once the Lord was walking, absorbed in His own nectar: His whole golden body was bathed in the tears streaming from His eyes; with the hairs standing on end in multitudes, He looked like a kadamba tree covered with buds; His voice was broken by sobs from chanting the Lord's name; with no knowledge of the road He walked by His own impulse, helpless with His own bliss — and yet He walked straight along the road. Seeing Him, the heretics said: "This is a Vaishnava, a mendicant monk; this at least he will accept under the name of 'the Lord's prasada'. Let us feed him this."[^36] Putting on a plate the most impure food, fit only for dogs, they came out to meet Him and, crying "Svami, accept this prasada of the Lord!", handed it to Him. The Lord, though all-knowing, unable to reject what had been called the Lord's prasada, took it in His hand and walked on, raising His hand with it. And at once some huge bird snatched the food in its beak from the Lord's palm and flew away.

**Sarvabhauma.** Oh, how great is their blindness!

> **21.**[^37] Him by whose maya even the lords of the worlds wander deluded —
> Him these insignificant ones thought to delude here!

**Malla Bhatta.** On another day He happened to enter the house of a certain brahmin in another place and, seeing that the brahmin was devoted solely to repeating the name "Rama, Rama, Rama", went away. On the way back He went in there again to see him and, seeing the same brahmin repeating "Krishna, Krishna, Krishna", asked: "Brahmin! Why, when I was going there, did I see you repeating 'Rama, Rama, Rama', and now I see you repeating 'Krishna, Krishna, Krishna'? Tell Me, what is the matter." Then he said: "Lord! This is Your own power. From my very childhood I was devoted to repeating only the name of Rama, but since I saw You, Lord, only 'Krishna, Krishna, Krishna' comes from my lips. Even by force I cannot stop it. So the fault is that of Your glance, not mine."[^38]

**King.** Bhattacharya, what is the meaning here?

**Bhattacharya.** Maharaja! Although both names are equal in meaning:

> **22.** "Yogis delight (*ramante*) in the Infinite, in the Real, in the Soul that is bliss and consciousness" —
> so by the word "Rama" the Supreme Brahman is denoted.[^39]

And likewise:

> **23.** The root *kṛṣ* denotes being, and *ṇa* bliss;
> their union, the Supreme Brahman, is called "Krishna"[^40] —

so in that both denote the Supreme Brahman, they are equal. But still, in those who have love (*rati*), at the word "Rama" the form of the Lord of the Raghus appears, and at the word "Krishna" the form of the prince of Vraja. And the secret is that Sri Krishna Chaitanya is Krishna Himself, and therefore at the sight of Him it is the form of Krishna that appears. And further:

> **24.** "The name of Rama, O fair-faced one, is equal to a thousand names";[^41]

> **25.** "The fruit that three recitations of the thousand holy names give
> is bestowed by a single repetition of the one name of Krishna"[^42] —

so the name of Krishna is higher than the name of Rama.

**Malla Bhatta.** That is true. We too came to this conclusion in our sovereign's assembly. Thus all the worshippers of Vishnu in the southern quarter come to Him through devotion to the Lord of the Raghus: seeing the beautiful places of the deeds of the Lord of the Raghus during His exile in the forest — Panchavati[^43] and the rest — they are naturally attached to them. But now, having seen this king of ascetics, they have all become partisans of Krishna.[^44] Also, in one place there was a brahmin who, out of great ignorance, understanding neither the words nor the meaning, read the *Bhagavad-gītā* with mistakes, and almost everyone laughed at him; but while he read, he was overcome by trembling of the hair and tears. Seeing him, the Lord thought: "Oh, here is the highest of those entitled (*adhikārī*)!" — and said to him: "Brahmin! What does what you are reading mean?" He answered: "Svami, I know no meaning at all. But while I read, I see Sri Krishna, dark as a tamala, goad in hand, on the chariot of Partha." Then the Lord said: "You are the highest of those entitled to read the *Gītā*," and embraced him. Experiencing the bliss of this touch, he attained a bliss even greater than from reading the *Gītā* and, with the words "Svami, You are He!", fell to the ground bowing, utterly beside himself.

**Sarvabhauma.** Such knowledge befits him. To one whose heart has been purified by the constant manifestation of the Lord, the true form appears.

**Malla Bhatta.** So it was discussed in our sovereign's assembly too. Thus the wondrous stories brought back by those secret envoys are endless — how many of them can be retold?

**Sarvabhauma.** So it is.

**King** *(longingly)*. Ah, when shall I be able to see the Lord?

**Behind the scenes.** Ah, here is the time for beholding! So do not delay![^45]

**King** *(joyfully)*. Bhattacharya! Since this voice, as if on purpose for our talk, announces the time of beholding Jagannatha, I think that Sri Krishna Chaitanya too will soon come.

**Bhattacharya.** Sire, just so.

**King.** Bhattacharya! This Purushottama will become as it were the seed of Purushottama-kshetra.[^46] From Him, surely, many shoots of bliss will spring. There will also be a special happiness in the service of the Moon of Nilachala.

**Sarvabhauma.** That is true. Righteous kings are portions of the gods. Therefore what unfolds in your mind is the truth.

**Behind the scenes.** Ah! True, true!

**King** *(joyfully)*. Again the same omen in words! Look, find out who it is.

**Bhattacharya.**[^47] It is a pilgrim, together with another pilgrim, pouring out his eagerness to see Jagannatha.

**Doorkeeper** *(entering)*. Sire! Some people are running here at full speed.

**King.** Find out whether they are unarmed or armed.

**Doorkeeper** *(goes out and enters again)*. Sire! All are unarmed.

**Sarvabhauma.** Probably that very God is returning.

*(Again behind the scenes, all together, a noise: "I praise Hari! I praise Hari!")*

**Sarvabhauma.** It is true — the Lord has returned!

**Behind the scenes.**

> **26.**[^48] In all those holy places He found no such satisfaction,
> and so He has hurried back to Purushottama.
> Look: by the Abode of Jewels are two moving jewel mountains![^49]
> Fate is favourable to us.

**Sarvabhauma.** Maharaja! Since it is Gopinatha Acharya crying out so joyfully, the Lord has really come. I shall go to Him.

**King.** Hurry, hurry! Malla Bhatta, you too rest now. And we shall go on a special matter.

*(Exeunt all.)*

*Thus ends the seventh act of the* Sri Chaitanya-chandrodaya, *called "Pilgrimage".*

[^1]: *Gajapati* ("lord of elephants") is the title of the kings of Orissa; here Prataparudra.
[^2]: Literally "came without a summons" (*anāhvānena*): Sarvabhauma was already on his way to the King when the latter ordered him to be called.
[^3]: *Niṣkiñcana*, "one who has nothing", a renunciant; possibly a hint that for a king, a possessor of wealth, it is especially hard to see a sannyasi (cf. the plot of Act 8).
[^4]: *Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 1.13.10 (the words of Yudhishthira to Vidura).
[^5]: That is, the Lord, who bears everything within Himself, is never "alone".
[^6]: *Setubandha* ("the Bridge", Rameshvaram), the southernmost point of the pilgrimage, the place of Rama's bridge to Lanka.
[^7]: *Ālālanātha*, a temple of Vishnu (Jagannatha) south of Puri, at Brahmagiri.
[^8]: *Kūrma-kṣetra*, the holy place of Kurma (the incarnation of Vishnu in the form of a tortoise), today Srikurmam in Andhra.
[^9]: A play: both the deity and the master of the house bear the name Kurma.
[^10]: *Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 10.81.16, the words of Sudama (Kuchela) after his meeting with Krishna.
[^11]: A play on words: *āmayāvinā vinā duḥkham*, "being diseased — without grief". Further: *samprati sampratipadyamāna-mānatayā*, "now, when honour comes".
[^12]: *Nṛsiṁha-kṣetra*, the holy place of Nrisimha; according to the *Chaitanya-charitamrita* (Madhya 8.3), after Kurma-kshetra the Lord came to Jiyada-Nrisimha-kshetra, the temple of Nrisimha on the hill of Simhachalam near Vishakhapatnam. M. Guha and Krishnadasa Baba do not specify the place.
[^13]: *Gaurayan*, "making white/golden": an allusion to the name Gaura.
[^14]: Translated according to the Kāvyamālā: "…*nirupama-kṛpābhirāme prema-saubhagavati bhagavati kanaka-ketakopavana iva*…". The electronic text of GGM has a repetition (*vihita-viśrāme*… twice) and omits the word *bhagavati*.
[^15]: A play on words: *kṛta-samūhaḥ samūhaḥ*, "a throng (*samūha*) that made conjectures (*samūha*)". Cf. also *samanantaram ananta-rahasye*, "at once… full of endless mystery".
[^16]: GGM: *vevīyamānāśru* ("trembling, in tears"); KM (by the OCR): *sarerīyamāṇāśru*, apparently *sa rerīyamāṇāśru*, "with flowing tears".
[^17]: A play: *subhagambhāvuka* ("becoming fortunate") — *bhāvuka* ("sensitive, full of bhava").
[^18]: In GGM the stanza is broken up into speeches without a number (the number 8 is missing); in KM it is marked ॥८॥. We keep the number 8.
[^19]: *Yamaka* (assonance): *sānurāge na rāge*, *bhakti-yoge na yoge*, *upadehe na dehe*; *muktā na muktāḥ*, "the liberated, not pearls"; *prakṛti-sarasa*, "juicy, full of rasa by nature" (and of a pearl, "moistly lustrous"). GGM *prayāsattiḥ*, KM *pratyāsattiḥ* ("nearness"): translated according to KM.
[^20]: *Aghāri*, "the enemy of (the demon) Agha", i.e. Krishna.
[^21]: That is, Krishna (*śyāmāmṛta*, "the dark nectar"), "drunk" by the gazes of the gopis. GGM *cuṣantu*, KM (OCR) *cuṣyantu*.
