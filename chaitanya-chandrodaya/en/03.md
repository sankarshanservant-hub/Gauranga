# Act 3. The Sport of the Toll (Dāna-vinoda)

*Prema-bhakti takes Maitri to see Mahaprabhu, in the house of Acharya-ratna, play Radha in a drama of the "collection of the toll": Haridasa is the stage-director, Shrivasa Narada, Nityananda the old woman Yogamaya, and in Advaita Krishna Himself appears.*

*(Enter Maitri.)*

**Maitri.** Alas, alas! I have heard that our only kinsman[^1] is alive — Viraga, who has nothing left but his life — but I do not know where he is. And I too live in name only — and he does not even know that. I shall go and look for him. *(Looking all around.)* Ah, who is this coming towards me?

*(Changing to Sanskrit.)*

> **1.** The embodiment of bliss, she scatters all around, here and there, the juicy radiance of her body like flowing nectar,
> and, generously bestowing inner purity on all by her merciful glances, she is drawing near to me.

*(Gazes with amazement. Enter Prema-bhakti.)*

**Prema-bhakti** *(looking ahead)*. Ah, who is this?

> **2.** Her body is a mere name, her lustre has faded, there is no contentment in her;
> gazing longingly into my face, she is approaching me very, very slowly.

**Maitri** *(looking attentively)*. Ah, by the marks that have been described, this is mother Prema-bhakti! I shall approach and bow. *(Approaches.)* Lady! I, named Maitri, bow to you.

**Prema-bhakti** *(with surprise)*. What, you are Maitri? Come here, child, come! *(Embraces her.)* Maitri, tell me, why do you wander alone, in such distress?

**Maitri.** When the servants of Kali overcame all our partisans, I, mad with fear, fled to save my life. Why do you ask about my distress?

**Prema-bhakti.** Child, from now on abide without fear — with me. I am the sister of your maternal grandfather.

**Maitri.** How so?

**Prema-bhakti.** Hear it from the beginning.

> **3.** The father, named Mercy of Bhagavan, the mother, Attachment to the people of Bhagavan:
> to them in due time many children were born.

And further:

> **4.** One son named Viveka (Discrimination) and many daughters called bhaktis;
> and this Viveka had by Mati (Understanding)[^2] a daughter named Anasuya (Freedom from Malice).

And further:

> **5.** Anasuya, obtaining as her husband the pure Samabhava (Equal Regard for All), born of nature itself,
> gave birth to a daughter — you, Maitri, who bring me joy.

And further:

> **6.** Those daughters were first divided into two kinds — by the presence or absence of rasa;
> those without rasa, from union with the gunas, became manifold, and those partaking of rasa took on ten forms.[^3]

> **7.** The radiant (amorous), wonder, tranquillity, laughter,
> friendly affection and parental tenderness — here are six rasas;
> the bhaktis that rest on them are called the highest — these six are the most worthy.

**Maitri.** So the last is the bhakti of the rasa of love?

**Prema-bhakti.** Of course! For it is said:

> **8.** That in which all rasas and bhavas, like waves in the ocean,
> rise and sink — that is called prema.

> **9.** All the rasas are partial bliss, but she is called undivided bliss;
> in the undivided the properties of the partial abide as if each separately.

So I have explained your lineage to you.

**Maitri.** Lady! Where are you going now, alone?

**Prema-bhakti.** Maitri, with the permission of our refuge — a certain Bhagavan, Sri Vishvambhara-deva, who has performed the pastimes of all the avataras and now wishes to re-enact the bhava of the Queen of Vrindavana — I am going to purify the hearts of all people.

**Maitri.** Where?

**Prema-bhakti.** To where He — who, filled with Her bhava, becomes beautiful and thereby dear to all the worlds — will imitate Her dance.

**Maitri.** What place is that?

**Prema-bhakti.** The courtyard of Acharya-ratna's house.

**Maitri.** But how can He, being the Lord, dance in a woman's bhava?

**Prema-bhakti.** Girl, do you not know? The Lord, who contains all the rasas, performs the most varied pastimes in accordance with the wishes of all the devotees, and the devotees imitate now one, now another of them according to their inclinations. Therefore, in order to instil this bhava in the hearts of certain intimate Bhagavatas, He will perform the highest of the high pastimes — the imitation of Her — for there is nothing fuller of rasa.[^4]

**Maitri.** Tell me, will this dance be in the form of an act (*aṅka*) or of scattered scenes (*prakīrṇaka*)?[^5]

**Prema-bhakti.** In the form of an act.

**Maitri.** Tell me, who will take whose part?

**Prema-bhakti.** Listen, child. Considering that Advaita, as Rudra, is identical with Himself, and that to take on the form of Sri Radha is beyond the power of anyone else and, as the greatest secret, befits no one else —

> **10.** "He made Advaita assume the form of the Lord, and He Himself assumed the guise of Radha" —
> so it seems; but in truth this God Himself became twofold.

> **11.** Advaita attained his purpose by the costume alone,
> but there Hari Himself appeared.

And further:

> **12.** Haridasa is the stage-director, Mukunda his assistant,
> and the one called Vasudeva Acharya the arranger behind the scenes (the costumer).

> **13.** She who unites Sri Radha and Krishna, the goddess Yogamaya,
> has entered the body of Nityananda in the form of an old woman.

**Maitri.** And who are the spectators?

**Prema-bhakti.** The worthy, of whom Bhagavan Himself spoke beforehand in this matter. Like this:

> **14.** "O Shrivasa, today you yourself must be watchful in this:
> let him enter who is worthy, and no other — so it must be arranged."

When He had spoken half the verse, Shrivasa asked: "Lord, in what matter must one sort out who is worthy and who is not? And where should they be let in?" And God spoke again:

> "Sri Radha Herself, oh, will surely appear here before our eyes,
> from the fullness of rasa, on the ground of the courtyard in the dwelling of Acharya-ratna."

**Maitri.** And then, and then?

**Prema-bhakti.** Then Shrivasa, though doubtful at heart, firmly decided that this was the word of Bhagavan and appointed as doorkeeper the best of brahmins named Gangadasa, the most trusted man of Bhagavan.

**Maitri.** And then, and then?

**Prema-bhakti.** Then Bhagavan again said to Shrivasa: "Shrivasa, you will be Narada. Shuklambara will be your *snātaka* (brahmachari disciple)." As singers God Himself appointed three — Shrirama and the others[^6] — and also Acharya-ratna and Vidyanidhi. Apart from them no one enters there; but the wives of Shrivasa and his brothers, and with them the wives of Acharya-ratna, Murari and others, went in beforehand and are there. They too are worthy.

*(Behind the scenes, the sounds of mridanga, karatalas and the rest.)*

**Prema-bhakti.** Child, do you hear? The *pratyāhāra* has begun — a part of the preliminary performance (*pūrva-raṅga*).[^7]

*(Again behind the scenes.)*

> **15.** Glory to Him — the refuge of all living beings, of whom it is said "born of Devaki",
> surrounded by the best of the Yadus, casting down lawlessness with His own arms,
> destroying the sins of the moving and the unmoving, who with His beautiful, gently smiling face
> increases love in the women of Vraja and of the Cities (Mathura and Dvaraka)![^8]

And further:

> **16.** With a face like the full moon, eyes like lotuses, breasts like chakravaka birds, a smile like white lilies,
> a neck like a conch that crushes the pride of the ocean-born (Lakshmi),
> as if made of auspicious things alone — like the nandi of a drama of the sports and pastimes of Vrindavana —
> may Sri Radhika grant you blessings![^9]

**Prema-bhakti.** Just so. As I had concluded: since this Haridasa, in the role of stage-director, has recited the nandi, sanctifying it with a verse of the *Śrī Bhāgavatam*, I think[^10] he will present here a one-act play — a *bhāṇa* or a *vyāyoga* — for their mark is this: "the nandi is sung behind the scenes" and so on.[^11] Well, child, do you want to see it?

**Maitri.** Where would I find such fortune?

**Prema-bhakti.** Why should you worry? Stay with me: by my power no one will notice you. And for your sake I too, becoming the same (invisible), will stand beside you.

**Maitri.** You have shown me mercy.

**Prema-bhakti.** Then let us go.

*(Exeunt both.)*

*End of the introductory scene (praveśaka).*

*(Enter Haridasa, who has taken on the role of the stage-director. Nearby the invisible Prema-bhakti and Maitri sit down.)*

**Stage-director** *(holding a handful of flowers in his joined palms)*.

> **17.** Lighting up the quarters with pure radiance, bearing a lustre born of rows of *dvijas*,
> spreading fragrance all around — the ground of boundless joy —
> pure, increasing the beauty of the moons of the toenails at the lotus feet of Bhagavan,
> like the smile of the secret of the drama, this handful of flowers is scattered.[^12]

*(Does so.)*

**Prema-bhakti** *(looking)*. Well done! Although the nandi was recited behind the scenes, still, on the occasion of the worship of the stage, the handful of flowers has been scattered at the very feet of Bhagavan. Look, child, look:

> **18.** With a necklace on his neck, with earrings and ornaments in both ears,
> with a garland on his broad chest, with bracelets on his arms and wrists,
> in a turban on his head, with ankle-bells on his feet —
> by the beauty of the drama, before us is the form of Haridasa, like an embodied centre of radiance.

**Maitri.** Lady! But this is not the way of the shastras.[^13]

**Prema-bhakti.** Listen:

> **19.** The way of the shastras is one thing, the way of *anurāga* (passionate attachment) is another:
> the first requires rules, the latter lives without rules.

**Maitri.** One who goes by a way without rules reaches the goal only late.

**Prema-bhakti.** That is not always so. For:

> **20.** In a flood the boat's path, even without a fixed channel, quickly brings it to the goal;
> not so in the currents of rivers, winding by nature, even when strictly following the channel.

Enough of this talk. Let us hear what he proposes.

**Stage-director.** Enough of prolixity. Ho there, ho! Today, after performing my daily obeisances and the rest at the foot of the lotus feet of the lord Kamala-yoni (Brahma), as I was returning, I received with respect a command[^14] from Narada, exalted in honour, who by the will of fate had just come there. It was this: "Ho, king of the Gandharvas! For a long time a shining desire has lived in me: that you should enact in dance some moonlight of the sports of Bhagavan playing in Sri Vrindavana, and that I should see it with my own eyes. So be skilful today, that this may come to pass." So I shall make the effort. *(Looking ahead.)* Ho there, ho! This way, this way!

*(Enter the assistant.)*

**Assistant.** What does the honourable sir command?

**Stage-director.** Marisha! Today the venerable lord Narada has bound me with his request.

**Assistant.** How so?

**Stage-director** *(repeats)*. "At the foot of the lotus feet of the lord Kamala-yoni…" and so on.

**Assistant.** Sir! How can he — the son of the self-born lord, the younger brother of Sanaka, Sanandana and the others, content in himself, become Brahman, eternally and happily immersed in the experience of the bliss of Brahman — have come to thirst for the worldly sports of Sri Krishna and asked you for this?

**Stage-director.** There is a secret here.

> **21.** Even sages content in themselves, free of bonds,
> serve Urukrama with causeless bhakti — such are the qualities of Hari.

So says a verse of the *Bhāgavatam*.[^15]

**Assistant.** Let it be so: let them serve with bhakti. But how can they be captivated by worldly affairs?

**Stage-director.** Do not speak so.

> **22.** This worldly play of Hari becomes an elixir more than the unworldly:
> the tale of the lilas of the avataras is the most tender, but the tale of the creation of the universe and the rest is a grey-haired old woman.[^16]

Therefore it is said: "He performs such pastimes that one who hears of them devotes himself to Him"[^17] — but that is said for ordinary people. And this Sri Narada especially loves the sports of Sri Vrindavana; he is also the rishi of the great mantra of Sri Gopala.[^18] So it is quite fitting. Therefore let the troupe, without delay, sort out the roles.

**Assistant.** Sir, wait a little, until that best of sages comes.

**Stage-director.** He is almost here already: for such wanderers in the sky there are no delays on the road.

**Assistant.** If so, what performance shall we stage? Let me know that too.

**Stage-director.**

> **23.** That very "play of the toll" of Radha and Mukunda,
> which is brought about by the goddess Yogamaya, who has assumed the form of an old woman.

**Assistant.** But how can it be staged quickly?

**Stage-director.** Why, what is the matter?

**Assistant.** Listen:

> **24.** For this performance your daughters —
> all especially skilled, the first among all…

**Stage-director** *(anxiously)*. Tell me, are they well?

**Assistant.** They are well. But —

> …they have gone to Vrindavana, joyfully,
> all together — to honour Gopishvara.[^19]

**Stage-director.** What shall I do? How shall I persuade Narada of this? Ah, his curse is to be feared!

**Assistant.** Enough, enough of worry! They too have almost returned.

**Stage-director.** Alas, Marisha, do you not know?

> **25.** These girls do not know the way, and there is no fitting kinsman with them,
> and there roams a fearsome elephant the colour of a cloud, his trunk wet with flowing musk (*dāna*).[^20]

**Assistant.** Sir, with them is the old woman, your mother-in-law, mighty with the power of Yogamaya.

**Stage-director** *(laughing)*. Well, then I am completely at ease!

> **26.** She who does not see the road and does not hear words —
> how will she help, she who is like old age itself in bodily form?

**Assistant.** Do not speak so.

> **27.** She is a yogini of great power; the years do not throw her mind into confusion:
> the orb of the moon, gradually growing (ageing), does not grow dim but shines to the full.[^21]

*(Behind the scenes.)* Ho, lord of the Gandharvas! Why do you delay the performance?

**Stage-director** *(acting as if listening)*. Marisha, look: this best of sages is impatient and joyfully hurrying here to see what will be presented. And we have nothing ready. Let us go from here to look for those girls.

**Assistant.** As you please.

*(Dancing a little — "tena-tena"[^22] — exeunt both.)*

*End of the prologue (prastāvanā).*

*(Enter Narada, followed by the snataka.)*

**Narada.** Ah, king of the Gandharvas! Why do you delay further? *(Looks for him.)*

**Prema-bhakti.** Child Maitri, look, look:

> **28.** Resting the Mahati[^23] on his shoulder and making it ring,
> with a rosary on his right wrist like a bracelet,
> with a mass of matted hair spread out, flashing like lightning,
> shining like Kailasa — here comes before us the divine sage.

Child, bow to him: this best of sages is the greatest of the great Bhagavatas. For there is this verse about him:

> **29.** Oh, blessed is this divine sage who, singing the glory of the Lord of the bow Sharnga,
> in rapture gladdens the suffering world on his vina.[^24]

**Maitri** *(bowing)*. Lady! You said that Shrivasa would be Narada. How is it that Narada himself has come now?

**Prema-bhakti.** Since Narada naturally abides in him, his form as Narada is evident; but on Advaita and the others these roles are merely put on. So understand it as you see it.

**Narada.** Ho, snataka! Why is no one to be seen here?

**Snataka.** Divine sage! The king of the Gandharvas decided that the dance must be held in Vrindavana itself and went there with all his equipment. Let us go there too.

**Narada.** But is this not Vrindavana?

**Snataka.** O great soul! From excess of joy you have forgotten even yourself: though first in the knowledge of Vrindavana, you do not recognise Vrindavana.

**Narada.** Ho, snataka! You have spoken the truth.

> **30.** Who among those whose actions of the inner and outer senses have been stolen by the madness of bliss
> is able to know even himself properly, what to speak of another?

So show me the way there.

**Snataka.** Ho, this way, this way!

*(Both, acting walking, go round the stage.)*

**Prema-bhakti** *(gazing)*. Ah, what innate love for Vrindavana this great Bhagavata has!

**Narada** *(acting as if he has gone some distance)*. Oh, ho!

> **31.** That which shines beyond the Viraja and is sung of as the supreme sky,
> ever charming with spiritual earth, spiritual creepers, bowers and the rest,
> surrounded on every side by flocks of birds and beasts woven of the light of dense bliss —
> this Vrindavana I behold! What other fruit for the eyes could be imagined?

Here even Svayambhu, my father, desired some birth, any birth at all. On this there is a verse of the Purana:

> "The greatest good fortune is any birth here, in the forest,
> in Gokula, where (one may receive) a bath in the dust from the feet of any of its dwellers…"[^25] and so on.

*(He dances, accompanying this very verse on the vina.)*

**Snataka.** If at every step you are thrown into turmoil by the bliss of love, how shall we ever get there?

**Narada** *(collecting himself)*. Show the way.

**Snataka.** This way, this way!

*(Both again, acting walking, go round the stage. Behind the scenes, the sound of a flute.)*

**Snataka.** Divine sage! Here is Vrindavana: the sweet melody of Bhagavan's flute is heard.

**Narada** *(acting as if listening)*. Ah, you speak the truth. For:

> **32.** The babbling of an intoxicated she-swan in the pond of the rasa of sweetness,
> the melodious hum of bees in the flower garden of love,
> the roll of the war-drum in the battle of love — victorious
> is the indescribable sound of the flute of the Enemy of Putana, which stings the heart![^26]

**Prema-bhakti.** Child, now Bhagavan Sri Krishnachandra will appear. Forget now the grief of your whole life, and let your eyes bear fruit.

**Maitri.** Everything is only by the mercy of your feet.

**Narada** *(listening attentively)*. Snataka! This is indeed the sound of the flute of the prince of Vraja. For:

> **33.** Even the mountain ranges seem to pour forth streams of hot tears,
> the trees and creepers have their hair (buds) standing on end because of it,
> even the rivers raise their currents high —
> ah, ah[^27] — it is the sound of the flute that is rising!

*(He dances, accompanying this very verse on the vina, as before.)*

**Snataka.** This dance is quite fitting. For:

> **34.** That which even the shrutis seek, which even the possessors of the treasure of Brahman drink to their fill,
> the embodied essence of bliss — ah! — today there will come before our eyes
> the lotus feet worshipped by glorious Shambhu, Svayambhu and the others.

Therefore, divine sage, we had better stand aside for a while: whether He comes with His companions or with the cowherd maidens, timid and deep in the rasa of their happiness — to approach suddenly would be unfitting.

**Narada.** You are right. Let us do so.

*(They take up their positions.)*

*(Enter Sri Krishna: He leans against the trunk of a kadamba tree, charming in the pose bent in three places, playing the flute, together with several friends.)*

**Sri Krishna.** Friend, how charming is the happiness of Vrindavana! For:

> **35.** The vasanti laughs (blossoms), the young bakula has opened its buds,
> the ashoka has truly become "griefless", the heaps of champaka ask to be taken in hand,
> the punnaga is flawless, captivating with its clusters — look: the flower
> bower, fragrant with the sandal breeze — spring is shining![^28]

**Friends.** Friend! How could this pleasure grove of Yours not be charming?

**Prema-bhakti** *(gazing)*. Ah, what is this?

> **36.** This is not Advaita at all, nor is it the art of costume and deceit —
> no, here Hari Himself has appeared!
> Only the real thing displays such a great wonder;
> but the semblance of the real both gladdens and gives rise to doubt.

*(Looking again, reflecting.)*

> **37.** One who is not Krishna cannot become Krishna;
> but Krishna Himself is certainly able to create various forms.
> The whole can take on the totality of the parts;
> but how could a separate part obtain the nature of the whole?[^29]

So this is not Advaita at all, nor the art of costume: it is Krishna Himself who has descended.

**Narada** *(gazing from afar, with bliss)*. Ah, what is this?

> **38.** Obtained by churning the ocean of the rasa of dense bliss, in the assembly of true devotees
> served by Mohini-Mercy to the close-packed throng of lovers sitting around there,
> drunk again and again with various accompaniments to taste, always full —
> it neither runs out nor changes: a certain dark nectar![^30]

And further:

> **39.** In colour a new rain cloud, more beautiful than millions of Kamadevas,
> tender and charming in the beauty of His face like the ripened autumn moon,
> His eyes long and reddish like the cup of a petal of a fresh lotus,
> His lips a bimba fruit on which the lustre of the flower-teeth reposes.[^31]

He is coming straight here. Come, let us hide in the bower and watch.

**Snataka.** Let us do so.

*(They do so.)*

**Sri Krishna.** Friend Subala! Friend Shridama! Friend Sudama! Why is the brahmin boy named Kusumasava, My dear friend, not to be seen? Look for him.

**Friends.** As You command, we are looking. *(They act searching.)*

*(Tossing aside the curtain, enter the vidushaka, alarmed.)*

**Vidushaka.** O friend! Save me, save me!

**Sri Krishna.** Why do you look so frightened?

**Vidushaka.** Friend! I saw a certain yogini, an old woman to look at: she has led into the middle of the forest five or six lovely young beauties, as if sent by fate,[^32] and has begun the worship of Gopishvara. Thanks to Your merits I escaped; otherwise I was afraid she would seize me and certainly offer me as a sacrifice.

**Sri Krishna** *(laughing)*. Friend Subala, what is this?

**Subala.** I know, though I was here. Today, for the worship of Gopishvara, despite the prohibition of the elders, Radha's venerable maternal grandmother, Mukhara, will hastily send Her to the forest, and Radha, in a surge of jubilation, will go there of Her own will.[^33] There this boy saw that same old woman and took fright, taking her for a yogini. For by her very nature she is like the divine Maya.

**Vidushaka.** Hee-hee! If so, then all of them will fall into the hands of my dear friend. For to the doe-like women living in Gokula and fond of play, the virtues of my dear friend are a real hunting net.

**Narada.** Ho, snataka! It is not fitting for us to remain here any longer. Come, let us rise into the sky by the power of yoga and watch from there.

*(Exeunt both.)*

*(Behind the scenes.)* Alas, venerable one! By what road can we make our way to Gopishvara? For —

> **40.** Setting up toll posts (for *dāna*) everywhere, this rogue — the forest elephant —
> seizes (the girls) together with their friends: his hand-trunk is itching for play.[^34]

**Subala.** Friend, my words have come true.

**Kusumasava.** And will what I said not come true? You carry your pride for nothing! *(To Krishna.)* Friend, and You too keep up the same zeal.

**Sri Krishna.** What zeal is that?

**Kusumasava.** Did You not hear what they sang: "Setting up toll posts everywhere…" and so on? That they called You "the forest elephant" — that is right. But "rogue" — that word is bitter to me.[^35]

**Sri Krishna** *(laughing, concealing His feeling)*. Why is it bitter to you that "the forest elephant is a rogue"?

**Kusumasava.** And who else in this forest is the forest elephant?

*(Again behind the scenes.)*

> **41.** He has again taken over the road and roams the forest with his companions, the elephant calves,
> sporting with the collection of the toll (*dāna*) — ah, how are we to go there?

**Kusumasava.** Friend, let us hide in the bower for a while, until they too, calmed down, come here.

**All.** Let us do so. *(Together with Sri Krishna they act entering the bower.)*

*(Enter Sri Radha with Her friends, who carry in their hands vessels with the articles of worship; the old woman follows Her. Surrounded by them, Radha is like the beauty of a fresh shoot amid faded leaves.[^36])*

**Sri Radha.** Friends, has everything needed for the worship of Gopishvara been brought?

**Friends.** Of course! Only we did not take flowers — we thought they would wither. We shall pick them here.

**Sri Radha.** That pleases Me, pleases Me. Come, then, let us pick them.

*(They act picking flowers.)*

**Prema-bhakti** *(gazing)*. Ah, a wonder! It is He, that very God: nothing is impossible for Him. For —

> **42.** He who by His portion became Mohini, enchanting the enemies of the gods
> and captivating even the self-content lord of lords, Sri Shankara —
> it is no marvel at all for Him, that this God Vishvambhara, being the avatara of Krishna,
> has taken on in His own body the form of Sri Radha.

Or else:

> **43.** This Hari in His play, by His potency (*śakti*),
> like a pea of two halves, without being divided,
> has as it were become separately man and woman —
> uniting in Himself the equal forms of both parts.[^37]

*(Again looking in another direction.)*

> **44.** And this is Lalita herself, Radhika's friend,
> and not at all this Gadadhara, the chief of brahmins:[^38]
> or else this Hari by His own *śakti*
> has become threefold — Himself, the friend and Radhika.

*(Again looking in another direction.)* Ah, and this one is Yogamaya: ancient, in her tenth decade, subduer of samsara,[^39] calm, free of rajas, lotus-eyed, white-faced — bearing her mass of hair as though she had turned darkness itself into pure sattva — she has entered, taking on the form of an old woman. This is not the body of Nityananda: it is Bhagavan, whose body is eternal bliss (*nitya-ānanda*), who has brought here His own Yogamaya.[^40] For Him that is no marvel, for

> **45.** "He who for Your sake became dwelling, bed, seat, sandals, garment,
> pillow, umbrella against rain and heat and the rest — by these various bodies,
> having become Your remnant (servant), is rightly called Shesha among people"[^41] —

so the devotees of old have said.

**Sri Krishna** *(looking at Radha)*. Friend!

> **46.** Did not Kama, chief of craftsmen, sculpt Her beautifully? Did not the painter Prema paint Her?
> Did not Tvashtar himself turn Her loveliness on his lathe?
> Did not this Lakshmi of sweetness rise from the churning of the ocean of beauty?
> Ah, though I see Her day after day, She astonishes Me as if never seen before!

Or:

> **47.** The valour of King Smara, the intoxication of the Lakshmi of loveliness, her pride,
> the playground of happiness, the smile of the beauty of the rapture of sweetness,
> the matchlessness (*advaita*) of treasures of virtues, the upanishad of the series of love-sports —
> who is She, a wonder of a multitude of moonbeams for the eyes, with eyes like a chakora's?[^42]

*(He gazes with longing.)*

**Sri Radha.** Come, Lalita, let us pick lavanga flowers. *(Goes round the stage.)*

**Old woman.** This lavanga garden is also very dear to Krishna. Do not go near it: afterwards we shall not be able to rescue You.

**Lalita.** Venerable one, we shall leave you as a pledge and rescue ourselves. Why worry?

*(All, merrily, act picking flowers.)*

**Sri Radha.** Lalita, save Me, save Me! This wicked bee gives Me no peace.

**Friends.**

> **48.** Leaving the lavanga creeper, this fickle Madhusudana,[^43]
> dear friend, with inconstant love, blinded by the fragrance of Your mouth, is circling (round You).

**Krishna** *(gazing with longing)*. Friend, look, look:

> **49.** Driving away with Her hand the bee that swoops towards Her face,
> with eyes trembling with fright, She has lowered Her face;
> and when the bee attacked Her hand too, She, knitting Her brows, shook it —
> and it seemed to express its pain by the jingling of Her bracelets.[^44]

**Kusumasava.** Friend, here is our chance! She is picking our lavanga flowers: take by force what She has gathered and keep it for Yourself.[^45]

**Sri Krishna.** Merely to look at such a sight is already the greatest sweetness. But, yielding to the words of the dear boy, let us do so. *(Approaches and speaks haughtily.)* Hey, Lalita, you spoilt good-for-nothing! Who taught you this science of impudence? What arrogance is this of yours in My abode, in Vrindavana? Whence this wilfulness of yours? Time and again you come into My forest, wander here and there to no purpose and, like strangers, in your pride break the creepers and branches with their lovely fruits and flowers — you scurry about everywhere and cause Me every kind of displeasure.[^46] Scorning propriety, you do not even honour Me. Good that I have caught you today! Now taste the fruit of this.

**Old woman.** Ho, Krishna! They need only flowers: they have not come to the forest to feast on fruit.

**Kusumasava.** Venerable one! Your wits have gone with your years: you do not know that "fruit" here is the penalty for an offence.

**Old woman.** You brahmin brat! The milk is not yet dry on your lips — what do you know? Judge for yourself, what offence? There is a penalty only for an offence — and not for us, who are with Radha (worshipping the god).[^47]

**Lalita.** Hey, boy! What is your friend to this forest?

**Kusumasava.** Lalita, He is the master (*adhikārī*) here.

**Lalita.** Is that so? If He is not the "worst enemy" (*adhika ari*), why does the forest of our dear friend look like this?[^48]

**Kusumasava.** Lalita, you are showing off your learning. So be it, so be it: my friend is the worst enemy of this forest. But how did this forest become the forest of your dear friend?

**Lalita.** The proof is the use itself. Otherwise how could we pick the flowers without fear?

**Old woman.** Lalita has spoken the truth: this forest belongs to my granddaughter. It is She who set up here as its deity Vrinda, who is Her maidservant.

**Sri Krishna** *(laughing)*. Venerable one, so Vrinda is your granddaughter's maidservant?

**Old woman.** Ho, Krishna! What doubt is there? Ask her yourself.

**Kusumasava** *(in His ear)*. Friend, she is on their side. Do not ask her as a witness.[^49]

**Subala.** Kusumasava, do not fear. Lalita, the proof here is the seal with my friend's name that adorns every tree.

**Lalita.** So be it, but we are not at fault either: all the creepers are marked with the letters of the names of my dear friends. What right has your friend to the creepers? We are picking flowers only from the lavanga creepers.[^50]

**Old woman.** Lalita, may I take every misfortune of yours upon myself![^51] Well said. Krishna, why do You quarrel with them when they are within their rights? If You ask — ask: I myself will give You lavanga flowers. To whom are You not dear?

**Sri Radha** *(with longing, to Herself, thinking of Krishna, in Sanskrit)*.

> **50.** With His body He makes the whole world dark blue, with His face He fills the bounds
> of the quarters with orbs of the full moon, with His speech
> He fills the ears with the rasa of nectar, with His glance
> He fills the sky with lotuses — what is this?

**Old woman.** Krishna, take the flowers! *(Taking the flowers from the edges of their garments, she scatters them before Krishna.)*

**Sri Radha** *(covering Her face with the edge of Her garment, with a slight smile)*. Venerable one, what have you done? Flowers gathered for the worship of the god — and such a fate for them?[^52]

**Sri Krishna** *(looking at Radha, to Himself)*. Ah, how extraordinarily lovely She is, with Her face covered by Her garment! For:

> **51.** The painted corner of the doe-eyed one's eye
> shines like a wagtail in a cage,
> and this trace of a smile can be seen
> like moonlight strained through cloth.

**Lalita.** Venerable one, you were too frightened! Why did you ruin the flowers gathered with long labour? Who is He to this Vrindavana?

**Old woman.** Lalita, you are a mistress of quarrelling: your heart is itching from the weight of empty pride. So quarrel with these insolent fellows. Come, granddaughter, come! *(Takes Radha and is about to leave.)*

**Sri Radha.** Venerable one, where are we to go? Must we not worship Gopishvara?

**Kusumasava.** Venerable one, here is my friend's toll post. The theft of the flowers, so be it, we shall overlook — out of regard for you.[^53] But let everyone pay the toll and then go.

**Old woman.** Ho, little brahmin, what toll is this?

**Kusumasava.** Friend Subala, tell her.

**Subala.** Venerable one, listen:

> **52.** Look: King Smara, deeming Him worthy, handed Him a flower
> and diligently appointed this glorious friend of mine
> head of the toll post over the throngs of young wives of good families in the forest of Vrinda.
> Pay the toll and go, fair ones — and no need for idle disputes.[^54]

**Old woman.** Let your friend be the toll-collector — what is that to us? We are not subject to King Smara.

**Kusumasava.** That is true too: how could you, an old woman, be subject to him?

**Old woman** *(angrily)*. Ho! If there were goods subject to toll — that would be another matter.

**Subala.** Dear friend, answer her Yourself.

**Sri Krishna** *(gravely)*. Ah, so that is how it is! Listen:

> **53.** Whether or not you lotus-eyed ones have jewels and the like,
> for this swinging of your creeper-arms[^55] a share is due —
> such is My law, proclaimed to you. But you are carrying jewels too:
> show what is inside the golden casket!

**Friends.** It is a casket with the articles for the worship of Gopishvara.

**Kusumasava.** Hey, silly girls! Here He is — Gopishvara (the Lord of the gopis)! Worship Him!

**Friends.** Gopishvara is Mahakala![^56]

**Kusumasava.** Hey, and is He not "very dark" (*mahā-kāla*), since by His radiance He has made the whole forest the colour of tamala?

**Friends.** It is Chandrashekhara (the Moon-crested) who is to be worshipped.

**Kusumasava.** Look, look! Is He not adorned with "moons" on His head?[^57] *(Points to the crown of peacock feathers.)*

**Friends.** Chatterbox! We shall worship the husband of Gauri.

**Kusumasava.** And are you not *gaurī* (fair)?[^58]

**Old woman.** Hey, boy! So you know that your friend is their husband?! Wait, wait: shall I not meet you in the village?

**Friends.** Hey, chatterbox! It is Pashupati (the Lord of creatures) who is to be worshipped.

**Kusumasava.** Ha! And He who herds so many cows — is He not Pashupati?

**Friends.** Say rather: is He whose creatures we are not Pashupati?[^59]

**Subala.** So be it: we are all creatures. So, by your own words, He is Pashupati. Worship Him then. And besides, what is it you are carrying in the caskets? Show it — and go your way in peace. Why this idle dispute?

**Radhika.** Hey, friends, show it, show it!

*(The friends do so.)*

**Kusumasava** *(examining)*. Here is musk, here is kumkuma, here is black aguru, here is sandal, here is camphor, here is a pearl necklace skilfully made in the shape of a serpent (*phaṇi-hāra*).[^60]

**Old woman.** Hey, serpent-necklaces, bite this boy!

**Kusumasava.** Venerable one, I, the companion of the subduer of Kaliya — whence should I fear a serpent-necklace? So pay the duty on all this and go.

**Friends.** All right, all right. We shall perform the worship of the god and go home; and afterwards, if your friend comes there, we shall give what we can.

**Kusumasava.** Hey, you slave-girls! Should my friend leave his domain and go to your homes to demand it? Stop, stop! *(Tries to snatch the articles of worship.)*

**Lalita.** Hey, son of the king of the cowherds! This is the property of the god: it is not fitting to defile it so.

**Sri Radha.** Lalita, since he has touched them, how can they now be offered to the god?[^61] Leave them, leave them. Let us go home, bring other articles and worship the god. So, venerable one, let us go home. *(Is about to leave.)*

**Krishna** *(blocking the way)*. Hey, You who think Yourself clever, where are You going?

**Sri Radha** *(concealing Her indignation)*. The principal (*mūla*) has already been given — what further toll do You demand on it?[^62]

**Sri Krishna** *(with laughter and anger)*. What principal has been given? Now listen to what the principal is:

> **54.** Here is a golden lotus, above it lovely dark-blue lotuses,
> behind them, in the cup of a ruby bud, a string of pearls;
> all this is in plain sight — but what is hidden, a pair of golden pitchers,
> or whatever else You are carrying unlawfully (with her),[^63] — that, girl, I must examine!

**Sri Radha.** Who are You to examine? One who is himself without judgement cannot examine a case.[^64]

**Old woman** *(sitting down between the two of them, in Sanskrit)*. Hey you, son of Yashoda,[^65] do not be flighty! Why with a greedy heart do You behave so insolently? I tell You the truth: if the rumour spreads that You trouble carefree wives of good families, no good will come of it for You.

**Lalita** *(angrily approaching, in Sanskrit)*.

> **55.** "Who are you, hey?" — "Why, I am Madhava." — "How, oh, has the month of Vaishakha taken on a form?"
> — "Silly girl, know that I am Janardana." — "That is just what your living in the forest says (that you are a robber)."
> — "Who on earth does not know Me, the Holder of Govardhana?" — "Hm, *vardhana* means slaying;
> you, O slayer of the bull, do bear it: so through that sin — *go-vardhana* (the slaying of a cow)!"[^66]

**Prema-bhakti.** Ah, what fun!

> **56.** If the play of Hari, even when presented by (ordinary) actors,
> becomes an elixir — what then to say of that
> which is presented by the Lord Himself with His own?

And further:

> **57.** Rasa belongs to the spectators, not to the actors — such is the way known to some;
> but here, when they are both, they know the rasa: in a transcendental object, what contradiction is there?[^67]

And further:

> **58.** The worldly conduct of Shauri is more wondrous than the transcendental — that is lila;
> and that it attracts the people of the world — that is a certain reason for its transcendental nature.

*(She gazes in amazement.)*

**Kusumasava.** Ah, you cunning, ill-bred Lalita! You have made my friend out to be a villain! Stop, stop!

**Subala** *(with mock anger)*. Ah, so that is how it is! Well, this is the truth:

> **59.** Your faces destroy the king of the twice-born (the moon), your eyes are thirsty for intoxicants,
> your appearance steals gold by its lustre, you do not shun intercourse with the wife of a guru (with the broad-hipped),
> and the fifth accomplice — here he is, the five-arrowed (Kama); and still you are pure,
> while He whose very name destroys all sins — our friend — He is the worst of villains![^68]

So, friend, the guards of a toll post will not succeed in their business without showing boldness. Show Your valour!

*(Krishna, stepping between them, pushes aside with His hand the old woman, who was standing shielding Radha, and acts as if forcibly seizing the edge of Radha's garment. The old woman forcibly frees Her, makes Radha invisible and vanishes herself as well. Nityananda, left in his own form, dances.)*

**Maitri.** Lady, what is this? Where has Nityananda suddenly come from? Where has that old woman gone?

**Prema-bhakti.** This is the power of Yogamaya. She herself entered him and became the old woman, and now, judging that a rasa left unfinished becomes especially sweet,[^69] she has vanished at the proper time. That is why Nityananda remains in his own form. For

> **60.** Innate nature is stronger than anything from outside: it overcomes an artificial nature;
> the warmth of water produced by fire or the sun does not last long.

So now the performance has stopped here. For this is the lila of the Lord Himself, not the custom of actors. Look: Advaita is again only Advaita.

**Maitri.** I do not know what Bhagavan is like now.

*(Behind the scenes.)* Ho, ho! A sannyasi! A sannyasi![^70]

**Prema-bhakti.** Ah, what are these sudden ill-omened words? Let me look. *(Looking.)* Ah, some sannyasi is entering the house of Bhagavan, and someone, seeing him, is crying out. Come, let us go out together.

*(Exeunt both.)*

*(Exeunt all.)*

*Thus ends the third act of the* Sri Chaitanya-chandrodaya, *called "The Sport of the Toll".*
