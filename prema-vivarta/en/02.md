# Chapter 2. The Writing of the Book

*Why and how the author writes: a conversation with Svarupa Damodara; the Lord's jest about the "queen of Dvaraka"; a childhood quarrel at school and the reconciliation; the author's love for Sri Chaitanya; the parrot on the bank of the Alakananda that sings "Gaura"; Navadvipa is Vrindavana (pp. 7–13)*

*[p. 7]*

**1.** The form and qualities of Chaitanya always come to my mind:
they make my heart weep and my body tremble again and again.

**2.** While I wept, a thought arose in my mind:
taking up the pen, I write, casting off shame and fear.

**3.** I am a "pandita" in name only — there is nothing in my head;
and still I wish to write down the lila of Chaitanya.

**4.** Gosai Svarupa says: "What are you writing, Pandita?"
I say: "I write what I love.

**5.** Whatever stories of Chaitanya's lila come to my mind
I shall write down and keep in great secrecy."

**6.** Svarupa says: "Then describe the Lord's deeds —
reading them, the world will gain great benefit."

**7.** I say: "Of the world's benefit I know nothing:
whatever pleases my heart, that I write down."

**8.** Svarupa left me, calling me a madman;
sitting alone, I write, meditating on the Lord.[^1]

**9.** Many lilas have I seen, living with the Lord;
some of them I write down — for the joy of my own heart.

*[p. 8]*

**10.** My mind weeps, my heart weeps, both my eyes weep;
whatever I remember, I write down then and there.

**11.** One day the Lord, laughing, said to me:
"You are the experienced one — the chief queen of Dvaraka.

**12.** I am a beggar; why serve Me?
You will find hundreds of sannyasis like Me."[^2]

**13.** I said: "Keep Your tricks to Yourself:
I am a maidservant of Radha's feet — don't speak so.

**14.** You have stolen my Radha's complexion;
I'll catch You like a thief and take You to Vraja.

**15.** I want Radha's feet, and You push me away,
sending me off to Dvaraka — that is Your game.

**16.** I know Your sannyasi-ship well:
cheating us, You will serve Radha Yourself."[^3]

**17.** Ah, those feet of Chaitanya, the wealth of bhajana —
where have they gone now, leaving me?
He has gone, abandoning me, and death has not come to me —
in grief I roll upon the ground.[^4]

*[p. 9]*

**18.** One day in childhood, we two at school
quarrelled and came to blows.
At Mayapura, on the bank of the Ganga, under the weight of grief,
I wept the whole night through.[^5]

**19.** My merciful Lord, before dawn had come,
came together with Gadadhara
and called: "Jagadananda! Sulking is very bad;
give up your stubbornness and speak to Me."

**20.** Seeing the Lord's face, I put away my sulk
and asked: "Why so late at night?
The ground of Nadia is hard — walking on it You have suffered;
for my sake You suffer so."

**21.** The Lord says: "Come, come, the night is over;
come home and eat.
Knowing your sorrow in My heart, I fasted,
and, leaving My bed, lay on the ground.

**22.** Just then Gadadhara came to My house,
and the two of us came looking for you.
Good that your sulk is gone; now come to your own home,
and tomorrow we will play joyfully."

**23.** Clasping the feet of both, I slowly rose:
I could not refuse the Lord's command.
Going to the Lord's house, I ate a little, drank some water
and slept for an hour or two.[^6]

*[p. 10]*

**24.** In the morning Shachi and Jagannatha gave me milk and rice
and sent me to study with the Lord.
And when, after lessons, I came home,
my Lord came to my house and ate there.

**25.** After a quarrel love becomes like pure gold —
how much joy there was in my heart!
The Lord says: "That is why you get angry and I get angry:
so our love for each other grows."

**26.** Such is Gaurangachand: not to serve Him is calamity,
to serve Him is the highest happiness.
He is the Lord of mercy; who could forget Him,
when He is so merciful to His servant?

**27.** Chaitanya is my Lord; Chaitanya I will never leave;
He is the Lord of my life.
Whoever calls "Chaitanya!" — I rise and embrace him:
he is my own brother in very life.

**28.** Whoever has not said "O Chaitanya, treasure of my life!" —
I will not even look at his face.
Whoever has forgotten Chaitanya, be he a god or goddess —
to see him is an ill-omened morning.

*[p. 11]*

**29.** Whoever, leaving Chaitanya, honours another sannyasi —
him I will beat with a stick.
Whoever, leaving the stories of Chaitanya, idly tells other tales —
fire in his mouth![^7]

**30.** If what gives Chaitanya happiness should turn to sorrow —
let me suffer sorrow forever.
But if He gives up His own comfort and follows the stern duty of a sannyasi —
I am drowned in grief at that.[^8]

**31.** One day my Lord, while playing,
went into a dense forest on the bank of the Alakananda.[^9]

**32.** Gadadhara and I were with Him;
in a bakula tree He playfully caught a parrot.

**33.** Holding the parrot, He says: "You are Vyasa's son:[^10]
say 'Radha-Krishna', increase our bliss."

**34.** The parrot does not say that; it says "Gaurahari".
The Lord, feigning anger, flings it away.

**35.** Still the parrot dances, repeating "Gaura, Gaura";
in the parrot's weeping prema awakens.

*[p. 12]*

**36.** The Lord says: "Hey, parrot, this is Vrindavana:
say 'Radha-Krishna' here, let everyone hear."

**37.** The parrot says: "Vrindavana has become Navadvipa;
Radha and Krishna have appeared in the form of Gaurahari.

**38.** I, the parrot, sing Gaura's name in this forest:
You are my Krishna, and Radha is this Gadai here.

**39.** Gadai and Gauranga are the Lords of my life;
from now on nothing else comes to my lips."

**40.** The Lord says: "I worship Radha and Krishna;
hearing another name, I grieve."

**41.** Saying this, He took Gadai by the hand
and returned to Mayapura, leaving the parrot.

**42.** The parrot says: "Sing what You like;
I shall do my own bhajana forever."[^11]

**43.** In whose heart the sweet lila of Chaitanya awakens —
my prostrate obeisance, brother, at his feet.

**44.** In Gadai and Gauranga I know "Radha and Shyama";
the Navadvipa of sixteen kroshas I hold to be Vrindavana.[^12]

**45.** Whoever sees Yashoda's son and Shachi's son as different —
why does he not die?

*[p. 13]*

**46.** Whoever has not found Vrindavana in Navadvipa
is an empty logician — what is the use of his living?

**47.** The name of Gaura, the abode of Gaura, the deeds of Gauranga —
whoever serves them, for him I have unfeigned love.

**48.** The form of Gaura, the name of Gaura, the lila of Gaura, the abode of Gaura —
whoever, born in Gauda, does not serve them,
his heart the name and form of Radha and Krishna,
Their wondrous abode and lila, will never touch.

[^1]: Svarupa Damodara — the Lord's closest associate in Puri (CC Adi 10, Antya 6 and elsewhere). This conversation is known only from the *Prema-vivarta*.
[^2]: The "chief queen of Dvaraka" (*pateshvari*) is Satyabhama: according to GGD 51 Jagadananda is her manifestation; CC Adi 10.21 and Antya 7.138 call his love like Satyabhama's, with *vamya-bhava* — a contrary, jealous temper.
[^3]: Jagadananda answers as a maidservant of Radha (cf. ch. 1, n. 8): the Lord, a sannyasi in appearance, secretly tastes Radha's mood. This is a jesting quarrel of love, not a reproach.
[^4]: The verse is written after the Lord's departure — the author himself thus indicates when the book was composed.
[^5]: The childhood quarrel and reconciliation are known only from the *Prema-vivarta*; the CC and CB tell nothing of Jagadananda's childhood. The name "Mayapura" for a part of Navadvipa is one of the features that made B. Majumdar doubt Jagadananda's authorship (see the preface).
[^6]: Literally "two or four dandas" (a danda is 24 minutes).
[^7]: The vehemence of these lines expresses the author's zealous, "contrary" (*vamya*) love for the Lord; cf. n. 2.
[^8]: Cf. CC Antya 12–13: Jagadananda cannot bear that the Lord sleeps on the bare ground and refuses oil and a pillow.
[^9]: Alakananda — here a name of the Ganga at Navadvipa.
[^10]: Shuka ("parrot") — Shukadeva, the son of Vyasa, the speaker of the *Bhagavatam*.
[^11]: The parrot episode is known only from the *Prema-vivarta*. Its sense: the Lord hides His divinity by calling Himself a worshipper of Radha and Krishna, while the parrot reveals that Gaura and Gadadhara are Krishna and Radha.
[^12]: The Navadvipa-mandala, sixteen kroshas in circumference (≈ 50 km; cf. Bhaktivinoda Thakura's *Navadvipa-dhama-mahatmya*); the non-difference of Navadvipa and Vrindavana is a teaching Bhaktivinoda developed at length.
