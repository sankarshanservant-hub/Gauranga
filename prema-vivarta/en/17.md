# Chapter 17. Conduct According to the Kind of Devotee

*Svarupa's instructions to Raghunatha: dharma without bhajana is deceit; the conduct of the householder Vaishnava and the renunciant Vaishnava; self-styled "Krishnas" condemned; the chief injunction — to remember Krishna; the "Achyuta-gotra"; three stages — pravarta, sadhaka, siddha; three kinds of devotion — aropa-siddha, sanga-siddha and svarupa-siddha (pp. 55–64)*

*[p. 55]*

**1.** On another day Sri Svarupa says to Raghunatha:
"I shall tell you something deeply hidden.

**2.** In whatever varna one is born, in whatever ashrama one stands —
by the dharma of that one maintains the body: this is the pure rule.

**3.** Maintaining the body in this way,
he serves Krishna unceasingly and wholeheartedly —

**4.** he is wise, truly religious, a good Vaishnava;
dharma without bhajana is mere deceit (kaitava).[^1]

**5.** Whoever does not serve Krishna but practises dharma —
his human life goes downward.

**6.** Householder, brahmachari, vanaprastha or sannyasi —
without devotion to Krishna he is not fit to be spoken to, day or night.

**7.** All should take refuge in yukta-vairagya
and serve Krishna, having firmly understood sambandha.[^2]

**8.** When sambandha is ascertained, the support (alambana) of love is recognized;
when the support is pure, prema awakens.

*[p. 56]*

**9.** Whoever serves Krishna with prema is worthy of honour even from his father;
a jiva without prema is but a clay dog from a mould.[^3]

**10.** Whoever has devotion to Krishna is a Vaishnava;
but one who has left home and begs yet performs no bhajana
is not counted as a Vaishnava.

**11.** Do not accept the remnants of offerings to other gods,
and never accept invitations to the rites of karma-kanda.

**12.** Vaishnavas are distinguished as householders and renunciants:
both are entitled to devotion, but their conduct differs.

**13.** Both need the rule of yukta-vairagya;
true knowledge and true devotion are equal in both.

**14.** Let the householder Vaishnava always earn by his own dharma
and, as far as he can, practise hospitality and service.

**15.** Living with a lawful wife does no harm to devotion;
nor do I see any fault in using mustard oil or good oil.

**16.** Yogurt and milk, which the smartas class as "non-vegetarian",
are pure food for the yukta-vairagi.[^4]

**17.** The householder Vaishnava always keeps far from offences to the Name:
he accepts the favourable and rejects the unfavourable.

*[p. 57]*

**18.** The glory of exclusive refuge in the Name —
for the householder Vaishnava its greatness has no limit.

**19.** To do no harm to others, to be zealous in helping others,
to be compassionate to all beings — this is the householder's only vow.

**20.** The renunciant Vaishnava, accepting only what is needed for life,
storing nothing, not conversing with women, serves Hari.

**21.** Thus, with differing conduct, all Vaishnavas,
serving Krishna, attain Krishna's transcendent riches.

**22.** Householder or renunciant — among devotees there is no difference:
one who makes a difference goes to the Kumbhipaka hell.

**23.** The main point: whoever is crooked in conduct
is the great disgrace of the Vaishnava family.

**24.** Shape your conduct guilelessly:
know devotion to Krishna as the essence in life and in death.

**25.** Whoever serves Hari without giving up crookedness, pretence, cunning and duplicity —
his days pass in vain.

**26.** Such people, twisting the meaning of the *Bhagavatam*,
roam about giving their senses free rein and seducing women.

*[p. 58]*

**27.** *As the verse of the Bhagavatam says: "To show favour to His devotees He takes a human body and performs such pastimes that, hearing of them, one becomes wholly devoted to Him."*[^5]

**28.** The lecherous sinner, thinking himself Krishna,
imitates Krishna's lila and destroys dharma.

**29.** Pure devotees, in the mood of devotees, having attained a spiritual nature,
serve Radha and Krishna in Vraja, taking the mood of the sakhis.

**30.** The scoundrel bent on posing as Krishna
goes after death to Kumbhipaka.

**31.** Intimate devotion is in the mind, not in the body;
foolish behaviour is what is called crookedness.

**32.** Avoiding all such bad company far away,
the pure devotee serves Krishna, taking the siddha-deha.

**33.** All devotees, becoming "prakriti", are absorbed in Krishna's feet:
the only "purusha" is Krishna, Dasa Mahashaya![^6]"

**34.** Then Raghunatha Dasa humbly,
with folded palms, addresses Svarupa:

*[p. 59]*

**35.** "Tell me, master, I have a question:
devotion without svadharma is the essence of all devotion;

**36.** why then should a householder remain in his svadharma?
He can surely give up svadharma and practise devotion."

**37.** Svarupa says: "Listen, brother, to the meaning of this:
I shall tell you the dharma of pure devotion.

**38.** In one's own dharma life goes on easily;
in another's dharma there are hardships — it is unnatural.

**39.** Whatever in one's own dharma is favourable to devotion,
that the devotee accepts.

**40.** And whatever becomes unfavourable to devotion —
giving that up, he attains pure devotion.

**41.** Therefore, giving up in the heart attachment to svadharma
and fixing oneself in devotion, one follows the dharma of the sadhus.

**42.** 'Giving up svadharma' means giving up attachment to it:
when clinging to rules goes, the conduct of a Vaishnava appears.

**43.** Unceasing remembrance of Krishna is the root injunction, brother;
whatever makes one forget Sri Krishna is the root prohibition."[^7]

**44.** Then Raghunatha says: "There is one more thing —
permit me to hear about Vaishnava custom:

*[p. 60]*

**45.** Vaishnavas are called the 'Achyuta-gotra' —
what is the meaning of this, what is special about it?"

**46.** Svarupa says: "Householder and renunciant alike are in every way
entitled to this gotra — it cannot be otherwise.

**47.** All the pure devotees who belong to the Achyuta-gotra
never cling to svadharma.

**48.** Giving up the worldly gotra, he serves Krishna's gotra:
that is his eternal gotra; he lives in Vraja.[^8]

**49.** Some live in their own land, having become [at heart] gopis of Vraja;
others take it up in the mind as 'aropa-siddha'.

**50.** Pravarta, sadhaka, siddha — three stages:
whoever understands this understands the essence of the dharma of devotion.

**51.** The 'kanishtha-adhikari' is counted as a 'pravarta';
the 'madhyama-adhikari' is a 'sadhaka', a great devotee;

**52.** the 'uttama-adhikari' is a 'siddha', a great soul:
in his heart he never clings to svadharma.

**53.** The madhyama-adhikari and the uttama-adhikari —
all belong to the Achyuta-gotra: consider it and see."

*[p. 61]*

**54.** Raghunatha says: "Now I wish to understand 'aropa':
understanding its meaning, I shall give up all doubts."

**55.** Damodara says: "Listen to the inquiry into aropa:
for this, knowledge of the nature of devotion is required.

**56.** Consider the three kinds of Vaishnava devotion:
'aropa-siddha', 'sanga-siddha' and 'svarupa-siddha'.[^9]

**57.** First I shall speak of aropa-siddha:
be calm and understand, restraining the mind.

**58.** The conditioned, averse jiva, worldly above all,
lives only in association with matter.

**59.** Material happiness and material sorrow are its law;
apart from worldly ties it has nothing.

**60.** Of anything transcendent it has no knowledge:
the transcendent truth finds no place in its mind.

**61.** It does not even know that it is itself a transcendent entity:
like an unprotected child, it is always ignorant.

**62.** By some fortune, in some birth, as the fruit of piety,
faith awakens in its impure heart.

*[p. 62]*

**63.** At the first inquiry it hears: 'I am Krishna's servant' —
and hopes to be delivered from samsara.

**64.** The guru says: 'Listen, child, worship Krishna' —
and the desire to worship Krishna takes shape in it.

**65.** It only hears that Krishna is the transcendent Lord,
but does not know that Krishna's form is transcendent.

**66.** Among the things it sees around it
it picks out in its mind what seems desirable,

**67.** and with desirable articles it worships the form of its Lord:
here its thinking by 'superimposition' (aropa) arises.

**68.** Making an image in human form,
it worships it with scents, flowers, incense and lamps;

**69.** thinking by superimposition, it considers all this a transcendent treasure;
thinking by superimposition, at times it sees in it its own, the transcendent.

**70.** Here the offering of the fruits of action is the domain of aropa:
through aropa one gradually gains strength in the truth of devotion.

**71.** This is the mark of aropa-siddha-bhakti:
this is how the kanishtha-adhikari worships.[^10]

**72.** But when, having understood the truth, he worships the Sri Murti,
then the stage of the madhyama awakens.

*[p. 63]*

**73.** At the stage of the uttama there is no room for aropa:
in his mind he finds the transcendent truth.

**74.** Prema awakens; seeing with the eyes of prema,
he serves the Lord of his life, having removed the former aropa.

**75.** Such an offering of the fruits of action is not by nature devotion:
it is counted as aropa-siddha-bhakti.

**76.** The root principle of aropa-siddha is this:
material things and material acts are taken up in the mood of devotion.

**77.** What among material things and acts is contemptible —
even if it be offered, it never becomes devotion.

**78.** The offering of action considered valuable and desirable —
that I shall call 'aropa-siddha-bhakti'.

**79.** For the Mayavadi, worship of the Deity is a mark of aropa;
in the teaching of devotion it is seen as svarupa-siddha-bhakti.[^11]

**80.** Now hear what 'sanga-siddha-bhakti' is like:
pure knowledge and proper renunciation are the nature of sanga-siddha.

**81.** Where there is devotion, there are yukta-vairagya and pure knowledge:
accompanying it, they are 'sanga-siddha' — grasp this.

**82.** Humility, compassion and tolerance are devotion's companions:
know them henceforth as parts of sanga-siddha-bhakti.

*[p. 64]*

**83.** Where the direct acts of devotion take place,
there are the acts of 'svarupa-siddha-bhakti'.

**84.** Hearing, chanting and the other nine kinds of bhajana
are called svarupa-siddha-bhakti — and the chanting of His name.

**85.** Their chief course is directly toward Krishna;
aropa-siddha and sanga-siddha abide as secondary.

**86.** The self-existent function of the soul is the essence of pure devotion:
in the conditioned jiva it awakens in the functions of the mind

**87.** and extends to the material body turned toward Krishna:
thus the goddess Bhakti abides in this world.

**88.** This devotion is 'svarupa-siddha' in direct action,
'sanga-siddha' with the help of its companions in every way,

**89.** 'aropa-siddha' like material, bound action:
it is practised in a transcendent mood, destroying the material."

**90.** By Svarupa's instruction Raghunatha understands
the truth of the nature of piriti — together with Jagai.

[^1]: "Dharma without bhajana is deceit": cf. *Bhagavatam* 1.1.2 ("here all deceptive dharma is rejected") and 1.2.8.
[^2]: *Sambandha* — knowledge of the relationship of the jiva, Krishna and the world; the first of the three stages (sambandha, abhidheya, prayojana) according to CC Madhya 20.
[^3]: Bengali sayings: "a master even to his father" — a person of the highest honour; "a dog from a mould" — an empty, lifeless figure.
[^4]: Under smarta rules, milk products on certain fasts are reckoned as "non-vegetarian" (*amisha*); for the devotee who accepts them as prasada they are pure food.
[^5]: *Bhagavatam* 10.33.36. The verse speaks of the Lord's transcendent pastimes for the sake of His devotees, not of justifying imitation of them.
[^6]: Krishna is the only enjoyer (purusha); all jivas are His energy (prakriti). "Dasa Mahashaya" — an address to Raghunatha Dasa. Directed against sahajiyas who think themselves "Krishnas" (cf. ch. 16).
[^7]: Cf. the *Padma Purana* (quoted in CC Madhya 22): Vishnu should always be remembered and never forgotten; all injunctions and prohibitions are servants of these two.
[^8]: *Achyuta-gotra* — "the lineage of Achyuta": a Vaishnava, on receiving initiation, is considered to belong to the lineage of the Lord Himself, not to a worldly lineage.
[^9]: The division of devotion into aropa-siddha, sanga-siddha and svarupa-siddha goes back to Jiva Gosvami (*Bhakti-sandarbha*) and is explained at length by Bhaktivinoda Thakura (*Jaiva-dharma*). For Jiva, aropa-siddha is activity (karma) "attributed" to devotion by being dedicated to the Lord; sanga-siddha is the knowledge and renunciation that accompany devotion; svarupa-siddha is devotion itself (hearing, chanting and so on).
[^10]: Verses 64–71 describe the mind of the neophyte, not the nature of the Deity. According to Gaudiya Vaishnava teaching (and Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati), the Sri Murti installed according to scripture is the Lord Himself, not a "statue" or an imagined "superimposition"; only the vision of the kanishtha is limited — he does not yet see the Lord in the Deity and in the devotees. The *Prema-vivarta* itself says in verses 72 and 79 that worship of the Sri Murti with understanding of the truth is the stage of the madhyama, and that to see Deity worship as "aropa" is the Mayavadi view.
[^11]: That is, for the Mayavadi Deity worship is merely an auxiliary "superimposition" upon the impersonal Brahman; for devotees arcana is svarupa-siddha-bhakti, one of the nine direct forms of devotion.
