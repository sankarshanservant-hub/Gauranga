# Chapter 12. The Greatness of the Vaishnava

*A devotee purifies more quickly than holy places; the fruit of the company of sadhus; three grades of devotees — prakrita (kanishtha), madhyama and uttama; the marks of the uttama-bhagavata — a rendering of Bhagavatam 11.2 (pp. 32–35)*

*[p. 32]*

**1.** Holy places made of water, Deities of clay and stone
awaken in the jiva's heart the memory of dharma only after a long time;

**2.** but at the sight of a devotee of Krishna all anarthas go far away
and the supreme goal awakens — devotion to Krishna.[^1]

**3.** When, wandering in samsara, the jiva nears the end of his worldly existence,
by good fortune the company of sadhus will fall to his lot.[^2]

**4.** Through the company of sadhus, toward Krishna, the Lord of lords,
bhava awakens, brother, in the jiva's heart.

**5.** The prakrita-bhakta, having received initiation,
worships Krishna keeping to the path of rules;

**6.** he does not distinguish uttama and madhyama devotees
and does not give due honour to pure devotees.[^3]

**7.** Love for Krishna, friendship with devotees, mercy to the ignorant,
and avoidance of those hostile to pure devotees —

*[p. 33]*

**8.** such is the true madhyama, a sadhaka of devotion:
very soon, by Krishna's power, he will become uttama.[^4]

**9.** He sees in all beings the presence of Ishvara
and beholds all beings in Bhagavan;

**10.** toward enemy and friend, toward worldly things he has neither attachment nor aversion:
he is the uttama-bhagavata — such is Gaura's teaching.[^5]

**11.** One who, accepting objects through the senses,
lives by a devotion free of attachment and aversion,
and sees the whole world as Vishnu's maya —
that great soul is the best of the bhagavatas.[^6]

**12.** All beings endowed with body, senses, life, mind and intelligence
are subject to birth, death, hunger, thirst and fear;

**13.** but, undeluded by the passing laws of samsara,
the true devotee spends his time remembering Krishna.[^7]

**14.** In whose heart Yashoda's son dwells unceasingly,
who undertakes action only to maintain the body —

*[p. 34]*

**15.** in his heart the desires that are seeds of karma for enjoyment do not arise:
such is the essence of the truth of devotion.[^8]

**16.** Jnana, karma, varnas and ashramas are attributes of the body;
from attachment to them arises the sense of "I and mine".

**17.** One who has no sense of "I and mine" toward the body
is a person dear to Hari; consider it yourself.[^9]

**18.** One who, as regards wealth, has given up distinguishing "mine and another's",
and the division "you and I" that breeds thoughts of friend and foe,

**19.** who looks evenly on all beings and is at peace —
he is counted among the best of the bhagavatas.[^10]

**20.** The treasure at Krishna's lotus feet, sought even by the gods —
whoever will not give it up even for the glory of the three worlds

**21.** and does not abandon remembrance of Krishna's feet for half a moment —
he, foremost of Vaishnavas, is immersed in supreme bliss.[^11]

**22.** One whose heart, by the moonlight of the jewel-like nails
on the toes of Krishna's feet, has been freed of all burning —

**23.** why should he seek the sun-heat of worldly objects?
His heart will always remain cool.[^12]

*[p. 35]*

**24.** Whoever has bound Krishna's lotus feet with the rope of love —
his guileless heart Hari does not leave;

**25.** even if Krishna's name comes to his lips involuntarily —
he is the best of the bhagavatas, all his desires fulfilled.[^13]

**26.** Whoever, understanding the merits and faults of his own duty (svadharma),
has given up all dharmas and serves Krishna's feet —

**27.** he is the uttama-bhakta: none is his equal,
there is no other best of the bhagavatas in the world.[^14]

**28.** The nature of Krishna and the nature of the Name,
the nature of the devotee and the nature of devotion —
having understood these, the great soul serves:
none among good Vaishnavas is his equal.

**29.** And whoever, not knowing these natures, yet with undivided feeling
serves Sri Krishna Himself in the form of the Name —
know him, brother, as the best of devotees:
such is the command Chaitanya Gosai has given.[^15]

[^1]: Cf. *Bhagavatam* 10.84.11: holy places of water and Deities of clay and stone purify only in time, whereas saints purify by their very sight.
[^2]: Cf. *Bhagavatam* 10.51.53.
[^3]: Verses 5–6 render *Bhagavatam* 11.2.47 (the marks of the prakrita or kanishtha devotee). The "Deities of clay and stone" of verse 1 and the kanishtha's worship are not the worship of a "statue": the Deity is a manifestation of the Lord Himself, but the neophyte does not yet see the Lord in His devotees.
[^4]: *Bhagavatam* 11.2.46.
[^5]: Verses 9–10: *Bhagavatam* 11.2.45.
[^6]: *Bhagavatam* 11.2.48. "Vishnu's maya" — the Lord's energy: the uttama sees the world as belonging to the Lord, not as an object of his own enjoyment.
[^7]: *Bhagavatam* 11.2.49.
[^8]: *Bhagavatam* 11.2.50.
[^9]: *Bhagavatam* 11.2.51.
[^10]: *Bhagavatam* 11.2.52.
[^11]: *Bhagavatam* 11.2.53.
[^12]: *Bhagavatam* 11.2.54.
[^13]: *Bhagavatam* 11.2.55: Hari does not leave the heart of one who has bound His feet with the rope of love — Hari, whose name, uttered even involuntarily, destroys sins. "All his desires fulfilled" — the reading *sarvakam* is unclear.
[^14]: Cf. *Bhagavatam* 11.11.32 (quoted in CC Madhya 22).
[^15]: Cf. *Bhagavatam* 11.11.33: whether they know Me or not, those who serve Me exclusively are the best of devotees.
