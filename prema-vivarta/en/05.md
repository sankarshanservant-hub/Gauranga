# Chapter 5. Service in the Play of Vivarta

*The "vivarta" of love — its transformations and paradoxes: the author's quarrels with the Lord; the incident with Sanatana in Vrindavana; the broken pot of sandalwood oil; the Lord does not let him go to Vrindavana and sends him to Navadvipa (pp. 16–17)*

*[p. 16]*

**1.** All the wondrous varieties of love, all its transformations (vivarta),
dance unceasingly in my mind.

**2.** Day after day I quarrel with Gaura:
"Jagai the quarrelsome" is my name.

**3.** I went to see Vraja, stayed in Sanatana's house
and quarrelled with him:
he came calmly, the red cloth of a sannyasi tied on his head —
and I made up my mind to strike him with the rice-pot.

**4.** Seeing Sanatana's humility, I let him be at once
and sat down to one side in shame.
My Gaura knows all: He sends me to Vrindavana
and, staying far away Himself, watches the fun.[^1]

**5.** Well, let Him be happy and let me be unhappy forever:
in His happiness will be my happiness.
I weep day and night, thinking of separation from Gaura,
and Gaura laughs to see my tear-stained face.

**6.** He is a sham sannyasi; I love His lila
and His honeyed words.
The mood He cherished in Vraja is His again now;
understanding, again I do not understand.

*[p. 17]*

**7.** I brought oil with sandalwood and other scents — hearing barbed words,
I broke the pot of oil with force.
Offended, I lay down in my place and fasted —
and He broke my sulk with all sorts of tricks.

**8.** He makes me cook: rice, vegetables, unsalted greens[^2] —
and says: "Cooking done in anger is very sweet."
He fans my anger — in that is His pleasure;
His pleasure is my goal.[^3]

**9.** Sanatana asked; I set out for Vrindavana —
and He made a simpleton of me.
Seeing His childlike mind, my heart is amazed,
and I clasp His lotus feet.[^4]

**10.** I want to go to Vrindavana — I do not get leave:
He plays all sorts of tricks with me.
When there is a quarrel, He tells me to go to Navadvipa —
that, I know in my heart, is His mercy.

**11.** On the pretext of His mother's command He stays in Vaikunthapuri,
leaving His own abode now;
and so to His own town He sends whomever He favours —
as if a cowherd were granted the sight of Goloka.[^5]

**12.** Thus I serve Gaura day and night —
such is the nature of Gaura's companions.
The feet of Gaura and Gadadhara are my wealth;
Damodara knows this mood.

[^1]: Cf. CC Antya 13.50–61: in Vrindavana Jagadananda saw on Sanatana's head a red cloth (a gift of Mukunda Sarasvati), took it for the Lord's gift, and on learning that it was a sannyasi's cloth seized the rice-pot to strike him; Sanatana explained that he had wished to see this love for the Lord. In the CC Jagadananda goes to Vrindavana with the Lord's permission.
[^2]: *Abona shak* — probably "unsalted greens"; the reading is unclear.
[^3]: Cf. CC Antya 12: Jagadananda breaks the pot of sandalwood oil brought from Bengal when the Lord refuses it, and lies two days without eating; the Lord reconciles him by coming to dine with him and praises his cooking, done in anger.
[^4]: The stanza is unclear: it may refer to Sanatana (in CC Antya 13 Jagadananda is his guest) or to the Lord, who does not let the author go.
[^5]: Vaikunthapuri — Puri (Nilachala). Mother Shachi asked the Lord to live in Puri so that she could have news of Him (CC Madhya 3). "His own town" — Navadvipa.
