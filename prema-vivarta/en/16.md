# Chapter 16. What Is "Piriti"?

*Raghunatha Dasa's question to Svarupa Damodara: how is the love in the songs of Vidyapati and Chandidasa to be understood? The answer: there is no worldly kama in them — the descriptions of man and woman are only similes; prema for Krishna is pure and spiritual; the right to learn it belongs to one free of the bodily conception; the "sahajiyas" and "monkey renunciation" are condemned; the Lord's instruction to Raghunatha (pp. 46–54)*

*[p. 46]*

**1.** One day Raghunatha asks Svarupa:
"What is 'piriti' (love)? Give me at least a hint.

**2.** The love that Vidyapati and Chandidasa described —
I have not had the power to understand it.

**3.** The love people understand outwardly from their words
is only the custom of love between man and woman.

**4.** How then is it counted within the supreme goal?
Why is worldly kama called transcendent?

**5.** Mahaprabhu always sings those songs with you —
and I cannot find the key to them.

**6.** The Lord has entrusted me into your hands
and commanded: 'Reveal to him now the hidden treasure of truth.'

**7.** Mercifully explain to me the truth of love —
and I, casting off doubt, shall reach my goal."[^1]

**8.** Svarupa said: "Brother Raghunatha Dasa!
In private I shall reveal the truth to you.

*[p. 47]*

**9.** What am I, or Ramananda, or the Pandita?
No one will understand the truth revealed by the Lord.

**10.** But if Gaurachandra, seated on my tongue,
mercifully makes me speak His own truth —

**11.** then know that genuine revelation has come,
and, hearing it, Raghunatha Dasa will find joy.

**12.** Chandidasa, Vidyapati, the *Karnamrita*, the songs of Raya[^2] —
know these all as priceless scriptures.
In them there is no kama; they are the abode of prema;
their law is transcendent.

**13.** Whatever description of man and woman there is in them
is in essence only a simile.
Where worldly kama is described, there Krishna is absent:
consider them transcendent.

**14.** Whether man or woman — whoever, unable to grasp this truth,
plays at rasa in a material body,
that "guru" posing as Krishna does not know pure rati:
his bhajana is the play of maya.[^3]

**15.** Prema for Krishna is perfectly pure, like pure Ganga water:
that prema is an ocean of nectar.
Spotless is that anuraga; there is no material stain on it:
a white cloth without a single drop of ink.

*[p. 48]*

**16.** The ocean of the happiness of pure prema — get but a drop of it,
and that drop will flood the world.
To love with a material body is only the custom of kama:
the pure body does not then awaken.

**17.** Far from him are the bonds of pure prema; blinded by feigned love,
by such love one does not attain Krishna.
And if he weeps, it is to display his own good fortune —
know this for certain.

**18.** In one who has prema for Krishna the vibhavas are spiritual,
and the anubhavas manifest in the body;
the sattvika-bhavas, the vyabhicharis and the rest, taking spiritual form,
play within the spiritual self.[^4]

**19.** Blessed is Lilashuka: Krishna, appearing before him,
gave him the transcendent rasa of Vraja;
he gave up the play of this body, broke with the worldly object of love —
and thereby gave Krishna supreme pleasure.[^5]

**20.** Vidyapati and Chandidasa, giving up their former shadow of rasa (rasabhasa),
attained the transcendent rasa:
formerly there was a petty rasa — giving it up, overcome by prema,
they attained the service of Krishna.[^6]

*[p. 49]*

**21.** One intoxicated by petty rasa does not attain the essence of Krishna's rasa:
his object of love is not the One with the flute at His lips.
He adorns a material body — may a thunderbolt strike his head! —
and merely drags on his insect of a life.

**22.** Giving up that petty rasa, serving Sri Nanda-nandana,
he sees Krishna, Sri Vamshivadana;
he himself attains a gopi's body, hastens to the forests of Vraja,
and leaves his former company.

**23.** *As Mahaprabhu's verse says: "There is not even a scent of prema for Hari in Me: I weep only to display the abundance of My good fortune. Without seeing the face of the One who plays the flute, I bear in vain a life like that of a moth."*[^7]

**24.** "Piriti, piriti, piriti", they say — but who has understood piriti?
Whoever can understand piriti becomes a gopi of Vraja.

**25.** The three syllables "pi-ri-ti" are known throughout the world:
whoever enters into it is lost — what is shame or gossip to him?

**26.** Becoming a gopi of Vraja, remembering his spiritual body, he gives up connection with matter;
in the object and the abode of love there is a pure support, and the rasa of parakiya grows.[^8]

**27.** Nowhere but in Vraja is there the mood of parakiya:
in the Lakshmis of Vaikuntha it is never found.

*[p. 50]*

**28.** All the men and women in the world,
through a flaw in the object of love,
attribute [the divine] to bodies of blood and flesh —
and always become dwellers in hell.

**29.** Therefore, when in "sahaja"-sadhana
they gain Krishna's mercy,
then, leaving every scent of the material body,
they hasten to the rasa of spiritual bliss.[^9]

**30.** The real "sahaja" — the service of Sri Krishna —
is performed by Ramananda Raya:
in proper sadhana according to the rules, in this material body,
his yukta-vairagya shines.

**31.** In a pure body he serves Krishna in Vraja,
having received Mahaprabhu's mercy;
staging his drama and teaching the devadasis,
he is free from the fault of association.[^10]

**32.** Apart from Ramananda, no one else
will gain the right to this.
Whoever has in his heart the thought of seeing, touching, serving another's wife —
he, know for certain, has no right to learn piriti.

*[p. 51]*

**33.** Never in this world, in dealings between man and woman,
is the treasure of piriti to be had:
all pleasures of the skin are passing and limited —
it is no eternal union.

**34.** Whoever, taking the mood of a gopi and following the spiritual dharma,
will cultivate piriti —
he has no dealings of man and woman:
within he is a gopi.

**35.** Outwardly a good man, practising dharma,
keeping to the rules until death;
inwardly a gopi, serving Krishna in the heart:
only such a one has piriti.

**36.** "Yah kaumara-harah" and verses like it
are only places of simile:[^11]
hero and heroine, becoming spiritual in nature,
serve Krishna in perfect purity.

*[p. 52]*

**37.** If someone says that it is a means of serving Krishna
to "superimpose" Him in thought on another man —

**38.** by Chaitanya's command I do not accept this:
such a thought about the material I consider hell.

**39.** To superimpose spirit on a material body — a most contemptible association;
to bring the mood of love for Krishna into it — utter folly.

**40.** Know for certain: this is the deception of Kali;
through it Vaishnava dharma goes down the wrong path.[^12]

**41.** Every pious person, understanding it as a simile,
serves Krishna in his own transcendent body.

**42.** Chandidasa, Vidyapati and the other mahajanas
served, keeping their former notions far away.

**43.** The last word of that service is spiritual piriti:
it is there, but the impious do not understand it.

**44.** Raghunatha! Consider this matter:
a devotee like you will preach good conduct.

**45.** Tell the Lord about this once;
strengthen your mind and make your heart firm."

**46.** Then Raghunatha came to the Lord's feet
and humbly, by hints, asked Him.

*[p. 53]*

**47.** The Lord gave him a command in my presence;
receiving the command, Raghunatha serves with a happy heart:

**48.** "Do not listen to worldly talk, do not speak of worldly things,
do not eat well and do not dress well.

**49.** Seeking no honour and honouring others, always take Krishna's name;
in your mind serve Radha and Krishna in Vraja."[^13]

**50.** Receiving this command, Raghu then understood:
piriti is never cultivated in matter.

**51.** Contemplating the siddha-deha in his mind,
in that body he will serve Radhanatha;

**52.** humble, honouring others, possessing nothing,
tolerant as a tree,

**53.** with his outer body he sings Krishna's name all the time,
while his inner body remains in the service of Radha and Krishna.[^14]

**54.** Giving up good food and fine clothes,
he maintains the material body with the bare necessities of life.

**55.** Whoever attributes Radha and Krishna to this material body
is a "monkey renunciant" who destroys his own dharma.

*[p. 54]*

**56.** The Lord has said: "He is a 'monkey renunciant'
who, living as a renunciant, converses with women."[^15]

**57.** The pure renunciant performs nama-sankirtana
and lives by eating what he begs.

**58.** "Whoever, having become a renunciant, depends on others
does not reach the goal, and Krishna disregards him.

**59.** Whoever, having become a renunciant, indulges the greed of the tongue
loses the supreme goal and falls under the sway of taste.

**60.** The renunciant should always perform nama-sankirtana,
filling his belly with greens, leaves, fruits and roots.

**61.** Whoever, out of greed of the tongue, wanders among people,
devoted to belly and lust — he will not attain Krishna."[^16]

[^1]: Cf. CC Antya 6: the Lord entrusts Raghunatha Dasa to Svarupa Damodara. The conversation about "piriti" is known only from the *Prema-vivarta*.
[^2]: The *Karnamrita* — Bilvamangala's (Lilashuka's) *Krishna-karnamrita*; "the songs of Raya" — Ramananda Raya's *Jagannatha-vallabha-nataka*. According to the CC (Madhya 2, Antya 17 and elsewhere) Mahaprabhu relished these books with Svarupa and Ramananda.
[^3]: Against those who imitate Krishna's pastimes in material relationships. Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati firmly rejected such "sahajiya" practices; the *Prema-vivarta* agrees with him here.
[^4]: Terms of the *Bhakti-rasamrita-sindhu*: vibhava (the causes of rasa), anubhava (outward manifestations), sattvika-bhava (ecstatic bodily symptoms), vyabhichari (transitory feelings).
[^5]: Lilashuka — Bilvamangala Thakura, author of the *Krishna-karnamrita*. According to tradition he was once attached to the courtesan Chintamani and, admonished by her, left her for Krishna.
[^6]: On the "former rasabhasa" of Vidyapati and Chandidasa — cf. the traditions about them (Chandidasa and Rami); the *Prema-vivarta* says that they gave it up and came to pure service.
[^7]: CC Madhya 2.45 (by the numbering of our CC text): a verse Mahaprabhu speaks in humility.
[^8]: *Vishaya* — the object of love (Krishna); *ashraya* — its abode (the gopis); *alambana* — their "support". On parakiya see ch. 11, n. 2: it is the transcendent prema of Vraja, unrelated to worldly love.
[^9]: The stanza is unclear: it seems to say that even followers of the "sahaja" path, when they receive Krishna's mercy, leave the bodily behind and come to spiritual rasa. Cf. n. 3.
[^10]: Cf. CC Antya 5: Ramananda Raya himself trained the devadasi girls for his drama, remaining, as the Lord says, untouched by worldly agitation; the Lord stresses that this right is his alone.
[^11]: A verse from the *Padyavali* that Mahaprabhu recited at the Ratha-yatra (CC Madhya 1.58): a heroine longing for the one "who stole her maidenhood". Its meaning, according to the CC, was known only to Svarupa: it is Radha's longing for Krishna at Kurukshetra, not worldly love poetry.
[^12]: Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati exposed such practices of "aropa" (superimposition) among the sahajiyas at length; the *Prema-vivarta* expresses the same idea in the name of Svarupa Damodara.
[^13]: Verses 48–49 are almost word for word CC Antya 6.236–237.
[^14]: *Siddha-deha* — the spiritual body in which, following the guru's instruction, inner service is performed; according to Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati it is revealed as the heart is purified, not imagined at will.
[^15]: Cf. CC Antya 2.120.
[^16]: Verses 58–61 are almost word for word CC Antya 6.224–227 (the Lord's instruction on the conduct of a vairagi).
