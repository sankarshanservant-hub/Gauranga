# Chapter 1. Mangalacharana

*The auspicious invocation: obeisance to the Supreme Truth, manifest now as Radha and Krishna, now as the one Sri Chaitanya; the hladini-shakti and the "transformation of love"; the transcendent Truth beyond time; Sri Chaitanya is neither the impersonal Brahman nor the Paramatma but the original Truth (pp. 1–6)*

*[p. 1]*

**1.** *The love of Radha and Krishna is a transformation (vikriti) of the Lord's hladini-shakti. Therefore, though one soul, They took two separate bodies on earth from time immemorial; and now They have appeared, united again into one, under the name Chaitanya. I bow to Him — Krishna Himself, adorned with Radha's mood and lustre.*[^1]

**2.** The undivided, nondual knowledge, the essence of all truths —
to that Truth, prostrate obeisance again and again.

**3.** That Truth is sometimes two — in the form of Radha and Krishna;
sometimes one — the Highest of the high, in the form of Chaitanya.

**4.** The Truth, the Substance, is ever one, without a second;
between the Substance and its energy there is no difference at all.

**5.** There is no difference, true — and yet there is always difference in it:
"inconceivable oneness and difference" — so sing all the Vedas.[^2]

**6.** The energy of the Substance is spiritual by nature; in being it is sandhini,
in action, hladini: thus it bears three aspects.[^3]

*[p. 2]*

**7.** Through its energy the Substance makes itself known;
by the energy of the Substance joined with action everything is accomplished.

**8.** In the undivided Substance being and action are eternal;
the energy and the possessor of energy, the Substance, are still not separate.

**9.** Hladini, giving the Substance two forms,
brings about the wondrous lila of Radha and Krishna in Vraja.

**10.** Know: Radha is the transformation of love, hladini;
Radha is the inconceivable energy who maddens Krishna with love.

**11.** To accomplish the impossible she wields a great power:
in the Changeless she has wrought a change — the attachment of love.[^4]

**12.** And now a remarkable objection arises,
which the logician will not grasp though he ponder a hundred thousand years.

**13.** Only he to whom Krishna is merciful knows it;
another, though he think it over a hundred thousand years, will not understand.

**14.** Hladini is the transformation of Radha and Krishna's love;
in the fullness of love is born that which intoxicates the heart.

**15.** When Radha and Krishna are two, then there is love;
when there is love, then transformation takes place.

**16.** Before there were two bodies there was no transformation —
how then did the one form become two?

*[p. 3]*

**17.** From hladini comes the distinction of the two bodies —
where then was hladini, how did the division come about?

**18.** To this question there is only one answer:
the Truth of Krishna is ever beyond place and time.

**19.** See: in material nature time holds sway;
the notion of past and future is its property.

**20.** In the transcendent Truth there is no past or future:
there is the eternal present. I marvel at it!

**21.** The transcendent Truth is beyond speech and mind;
as soon as one describes it a flaw creeps in — this alone is true.

**22.** In the transcendent Truth itself I never find a flaw:
all is by inconceivable energy. Be careful, brother!

**23.** Talk of "before" and "after" never applies to it:
there all is ever new and intoxicates with bliss.

**24.** Therefore, that in the Truth the undivided and the divided
are seen at the same time — that too is its nature.

**25.** The Truth is the abode of contrary attributes; wondrous are its qualities:
hladini was never born — and yet she is skilled in action.

**26.** Before being born she makes Radha and Krishna two;
becoming the transformation of love in Them both, she herself is born.

*[p. 4]*

**27.** The Truth is the eternal present, free from the flaw of time;
reasoning about the flaw of time fits only the material.

**28.** The nondual Truth and the Truth of Radha and Krishna
are simultaneous, real, eternal and pure being (shuddha-sattva).

**29.** Therefore Radha and Krishna, the two, becoming one,
are now manifest as my Chaitanya Gosai.

**30.** In saying "now", bring in no distinction of time:
remember that in the transcendent there is no distinction of time.[^5]

**31.** "Radha and Krishna were, and then became Chaitanya Gosai" —
say this, and the flaw of time creeps in: we lose the Reality.

**32.** If by the words "one soul" you understand Sri Chaitanya alone,
then, brother, Radha and Krishna will seem something later.

**33.** Who comes first — Radha and Krishna or the son of Shachi? —
do not waste your time on such reasoning.

**34.** I have said: in the transcendent all is present;
about Chaitanya and Krishna be careful in argument.

**35.** Simultaneity and eternity — both truths are real;
the undivided, nondual lila is the greatness of the Truth.

**36.** Hladini, the power of love's transformation,
holds both truths in simultaneity — know this.

*[p. 5]*

**37.** That same Chaitanya, now appearing in the world,
roams with sankirtana along the banks of the Ganga and the ocean.

**38.** This lila of Sri Chaitanya is the lila of Krishna,
in which the transformation of love has grown intense.

**39.** Grown intense, the chaste hladini,
in the excess of love, anointed Krishna with Radha's mood and lustre.

**40.** A greater happiness than in Vraja is in Navadvipa-dhama:
Krishna obtained it, coming golden by His own desire.[^6]

**41.** The form of Chaitanya is an unprecedented form of Krishna;
the form of Krishna is the wondrous own-form of Chaitanya.

**42.** The two attires of hladini are supremely sweet:
sweeter than the sweet — and this one sweeter still.

**43.** The sweetest form of Krishna is the figure of Chaitanya;
unceasingly I bow down to it to the ground.

**44.** If you say that "one soul" means the changeless Brahman,
from which arise the forms of Radha and Krishna, endowed with form —

**45.** such a conclusion does not follow from the sense of the verse:
the two, one soul, are in the manifestation of Chaitanya.

**46.** Chaitanya is never the changeless Brahman:
He is full of the transformations of bliss, pure, possessed of form.

*[p. 6]*

**47.** Brahman is the impersonal effulgence of His body;
the foundation of Brahman is the person Krishna Chaitanya.[^7]

**48.** Therefore by "one soul" Sri Chaitanya is understood
by the learned — the blessed Svarupa and others.

**49.** Bow down to that Truth of "one soul" —
you will gain the service of Radha and Krishna, and your desire will be fulfilled.

**50.** If you say that "one soul" is the Paramatma,
from which Radha and Krishna become two souls —

**51.** by the sense of the verse this is in no way established:
it flatly contradicts the words "under the name Chaitanya".

**52.** Know Sri Chaitanya to be the original Truth;
always understand the Paramatma to be His part.

**53.** The oneness of Radha and Krishna is that very form of "one soul":
Sri Chaitanya, my wondrous Lord of life.

**54.** A maidservant of Radha's feet am I, a maidservant of Radha's feet:
I love the form adorned with Radha's lustre.[^8]

**55.** To the feet of the Highest of the high, the son of Shachi,
my prostrate obeisance — taking shelter in Him alone.

[^1]: The verse of Svarupa Damodara quoted in CC Adi 1.5 (and repeated in Adi 4); the rest of the chapter comments on it: the words "one soul" (*ekatmanau*), "now" (*adhuna*), "under the name Chaitanya" (*chaitanyakhyam*).
[^2]: *Achintya-bhedabheda* — the "inconceivable oneness and difference" of the Lord and His energies; the Gaudiya Vaishnava teaching as formulated by Jiva Gosvami and accepted by Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati.
[^3]: The Lord's internal energy (svarupa-shakti) is threefold: sandhini (being), samvit (knowledge) and hladini (bliss); cf. CC Adi 4 and Madhya 8. "Spiritual by nature" (*chit*) here corresponds to samvit.
[^4]: The "change" (*vikara*) in the Changeless is no material alteration: it is the play of the internal energy, hladini, which makes the Lord Himself taste love. There is no worldly "passion" here.
[^5]: A play on the word *adhuna* ("now") in verse 1.
[^6]: Cf. CC Adi 4: Krishna accepts Radha's mood and lustre in order to taste Her love. "Golden" (*purata*) — Gaura's golden form.
[^7]: Cf. CC Adi 2: Brahman is the effulgence of the Lord's body, the Paramatma His partial manifestation, Bhagavan the complete Truth.
[^8]: The poet speaks of himself in the feminine, as a maidservant of Sri Radha. According to GGD 51, Jagadananda Pandita is a manifestation of Satyabhama (in Dvaraka); here the author expresses the mood of service to Radha in Vraja.
