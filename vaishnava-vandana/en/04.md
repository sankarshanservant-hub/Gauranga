# Part 4. The "First vandana" ascribed to Vrindavana Dasa

*A Bengali vandana (payar), entitled "First vandana" in the 1910 edition: benediction; Nityananda and His associates and their forms as cowherd boys (the "Gopala-nirnaya"); Vrindavana Dasa; the devotees go to Nilachala for the Chariot festival; conclusion (pp. 8–20 of the 1910 edition; two verses and couplets 3–144)*[^1]

*[p. 8]*

## Benediction

**1.** I praise the two whose arms reach to their knees, who shine like gold, the sole source of sankirtana, with eyes wide as lotuses, who sustain the universe, the best of the twice-born, guardians of the dharma of the age, doers of good to the world, descents of compassion.[^2]

**2.** Obeisance to You, eternally true in all three times, son of Jagannatha — to You together with Your sons, servants and wives, obeisance.[^3]

**3.** Glory, glory to Vishvambhara, glory to the king of the twice-born!
Glory to the assembly of those dear to Vishvambhara!

**4.** Glory to Gaurachandra, the bridge of dharma, great and steadfast!
Glory to Him whose beautiful body is sankirtana itself!

**5.** Glory to the friend, the treasure and the life of Nityananda,
glory to the abode of the prema of Gadadhara and Advaita!

**6.** Glory to Him to whom Sri Jagadananda is exceedingly dear!
Glory to the heart of Vakreshvara and Kashishvara!

**7.** Glory, glory to the Lord of Srivasa and all His beloved ones!
Cast, O Lord, Your auspicious glance upon the living beings![^4]

*[p. 9]*

**8.** With all my heart I praise Thakura Chaitanya;
I praise the worshipful Deity and all His beloved ones.

**9.** I praise Thakura Nityananda together with Advaita:
He is golden of form, His arms reach to His knees.

**10.** Listen, listen, brothers, to an auspicious account:
hearing it, sin departs and the world is purified.

**11.** The greatness of Nityananda's servants
cannot be exhausted even if one speaks for a hundred years.

**12.** And yet I shall name those whom I know:
all of them are of Nanda's community, descended cowherd men and women.[^5]

**13.** Nityananda Raya, the jewel of a thousand crowns —
He, the Lord, sings Gora's qualities with a thousand mouths.

**14.** It is He who, bearing a thousand hoods in various pastimes,
holds on His own body His own form.[^6]

**15.** Who has the power to tell of Nityananda's greatness?
Those with whom Nityananda played —

**16.** all of them are of Nanda's community, descended cowherd men and women;
all of them are eternal, pure, forms of the Lord Himself.

**17.** Be merciful, Lord, grant me this grace:
may my mind dwell at the feet of Your servants.

**18.** All of them were born at the command of Lord Chaitanya;
Nityananda Prabhu wanders through the world of samsara, [bound] by maya.[^7]

**19.** Vedavyasa and others — all have spoken [of them];
each speaks according to his own love and understanding.[^8]

*[p. 10]*

## Nityananda's associates and their forms as cowherd boys

**20.** Because of Nityananda's prohibition
their former names were not openly written down.[^9]

**21.** A dear associate is Ramadasa Mahashaya:
he always speaks in the mood of the Lord.

**22.** No one can quickly understand his words;
Gaurachandra is always in his heart.

**23.** More than all is Ramadasa seized by feeling:
for three months Krishna dwelt in his heart.[^10]

**24.** The one whom the *Bhagavata* calls Shridama —
know for certain: it is Ramadasa.[^11]

**25.** He is the manifest form of prema, beyond [prema itself]:
ninefold prema-bhakti is his play.

**26.** The ocean of prema named Sri Sundarananda —
the great abode of Nityananda's prema.

**27.** He is counted first among the associates;
he is Nityananda's treasure and life.[^12]

**28.** The one whom the Puranas extol as Sudama —
all know: it is Sundarananda.[^13]

**29.** Kamalakara Pandita, free in love —
the one to whom Nityananda gave Saptagrama.

**30.** The one whom the Puranas call Vasudama —
all know: it is Kamalakara.[^14]

**31.** Uddharana Datta, a great Vaishnava, generous:
to him was given the right to serve Nityananda.

*[p. 11]*

**32.** The one whom the *Bhagavata* calls Mahabala —
know for certain: it is Uddharana.[^15]

**33.** Sri Parameshvara Dasa, whose life is Nityananda:
Nityananda plays in his form.

**34.** The one whom the Puranas call Subahu —
all know: it is Parameshvara.[^16]

**35.** Purushottama Dasa has no awareness of the external:
Nityananda-chandra plays in his heart.

**36.** The one whom the Puranas extol as Stokakrishna —
all know: it is Purushottama.[^17]

**37.** Gauridasa Pandita, greatly fortunate:
in body, mind and word his life is Nityananda.

**38.** The one whom the Puranas call Subala —
all know as Gauridasa Pandita.[^18]

**39.** Purushottama Pandita was born in Navadvipa;
he is the innermost core [of the heart] of Nityananda.

**40.** The one whom the *Bhagavata* calls Arjuna —
know for certain: it is Purushottama.[^19]

**41.** All call him the son of Ratai;
this noble one is an associate of Nityananda.

**42.** The one whom the Puranas extol as Lavanga —
all know: it is Purushottama.[^20]

**43.** Kaliya Krishnadasa is famed in the three worlds:
at the sight of him Gaurachandra appears in the heart.

*[p. 12]*

**44.** The one whom the *Bhagavata* calls Mahabahu —
know as settled: it is Kaliya Krishna.[^21]

*(Thus ends the "Gopala-nirnaya".)*

**45.** Gadadhara Dasa, full of the rasa of prema-bhakti:
at the sight of him all sins are destroyed.

**46.** All know him as Radhika;
at the sight of him Gaurachandra is attained.[^22]

**47.** Sadashiva Kaviraja, greatly fortunate:
Gaurachandra and Nityananda are his treasure and life.

**48.** The one whom the Puranas extol as Chandravali —
all know as Sadashiva Kaviraja.[^23]

**49.** Dhananjaya Pandita, an extraordinary mahanta:
Nityananda is always in his heart.

**50.** Mahesha Pandita is the greatest of mahantas;
it is said that he is especially dear to Nityananda.[^24]

**51.** Balarama Dasa, full of prema and intoxicated by it:
from the breeze [that comes] from him all sins are destroyed.

**52.** The noble brahmana Krishnadasa, born in Radha:
his play is among Nityananda's associates.

**53.** Jagadisha Pandita, abode of the highest radiance:
I name him among Nityananda's associates.

**54.** The pious Krishnadasa, whose home is in Badagachhi:
in his village Nityananda played.[^25]

*[p. 13]*

**55.** Jagannatha Kavichandra, full of the rasa of prema:
Nityananda is always in his heart.[^26]

**56.** The famous Chaitanya Dasa — Murari Pandita:
his play is with huge snakes and tigers.[^27]

**57.** The wise Raghunatha Vaidya Upadhyaya:
from his mere glance love for Krishna and attraction to Him arise.

**58.** Chaturbhuja Pandita, Nandana, Gangadasa:
formerly Nityananda played in his house.[^28]

**59.** Acharya Vaishnavananda, most generous:
formerly he was known by the name Raghunatha Puri.[^29]

**60.** Paramananda Upadhyaya, a wholly devoted Vaishnava:
in love for Nityananda he became a mahanta.

**61.** The famous Paramananda Gupta Mahashaya:
formerly Nityananda found shelter in his house.

**62.** The mahanta Acharyachandra, endowed with every power:
he has no other refuge than Nityananda.

**63.** Krishnananda and Devananda, two pure servants:
their play is among Nityananda's associates.[^30]

**64.** Acharya Vaishnavananda, most generous:
he has no other refuge than Nityananda-chandra.

**65.** The singer Madhavananda Ghosha Mahashaya;
Vasudeva Ghosha, full of the rasa of prema.

**66.** Very dear to Nityananda are Manohara, [Narayana],
Krishnananda and Devananda — these four.[^31]

*[p. 14]*

**67.** Greatly fortunate are these four generous panditas:
in their houses Nityananda played.[^32]

## Other devotees of the Lord

**68.** With joy I praise Pundarika Vidyanidhi:
remembering him, Gaurachandra weeps aloud.[^33]

**69.** With joy I praise Thakura Haridasa,
and I praise the other Haridasa, who lives on the seashore.[^34]

**70.** I praise Mukunda Datta, Krishna's singer,
Shivananda Sena and the others close [to the Lord].

**71.** With a joyful heart I praise Sri Govinda:
he led the singing in kirtana together with the Lord.[^35]

**72.** I praise Sri Vijaya Dasa, the copyist,
whom the Lord blessed [with the name] "Ratnabahu".[^36]

**73.** I praise the wise Sadashiva Pandita,
in whose house Nityananda formerly lived.

**74.** With a joyful heart I praise Purushottama Sanjaya:
Nityananda came first to his house.[^37]

**75.** I praise Achyutananda, whose deeds are wonderful,
Brahmananda Bharati, and Sanatana and Rupa.[^38]

**76.** With joy I praise Garuda Pandita:
snake poison does not take one who utters his name.[^39]

**77.** How many servants and dear friends are with Nityananda —
who can describe them? It is like [counting] the waves of the ocean.

**78.** How many companions are with Nityananda-chandra —
even in a hundred years I could not describe them.[^40]

*[p. 15]*

**79.** One [of His] servants has thousands upon thousands of [his own] servants;
by Nityananda's grace they too are gurus.

**80.** All of them are free in the rasa of Chaitanya;
the treasure and life of them all are Chaitanya and Nityananda.

## Vrindavana Dasa

**81.** The last servant is Sri Vrindavana Dasa:
he dwelt in the womb of Narayani, who partook of [the Lord's] remnants.[^41]

**82.** Having gained a servant's place, he became learned;
birth after birth [may] I sing the qualities of Vrindavana-chandra.[^42]

**83.** Nityananda and Sri Chaitanya are the two great Masters:
birth after birth [may] I serve the feet of the two Lords.

**84.** In nine ways I shall serve [Them] —
grant this gift, Lord: so Your servant prays.

**85.** Glory, glory to Gaurachandra, glory to Nityananda!
Glory, glory to all the Lord's devotees!

**86.** Glory, glory to Advaita and the others, glory to Haridasa!
Glory, glory to Gadadhara, glory to Srinivasa!

## The devotees go to Nilachala

**87.** Now hear how all the Vaishnavas came —
Acharya Gosani and all the beloved [of the Lord].[^43]

**88.** The time of the festival when [Jagannatha] mounts the chariot had come:
the host of devotees set out for Nilachala.

**89.** The Lord's command: every year
all are to come to see the Chariot festival.

**90.** Acharya Gosani and with him all the devotees —
all set out for Nilachala.

*[p. 16]*

**91.** Thakura Pandita Srinivasa went,
in whose house Chaitanya revealed Himself.

**92.** Then Sri Pandita Vakreshvara went,
in whose house Gaurangasundara danced.[^44]

**93.** With joy Thakura Haridasa went:
in his form is Brahma, a manifestation of devotion.[^45]

**94.** Pradyumna Brahmachari Mahashaya went,
with whom Nrisimha converses face to face.

**95.** Vasudeva Datta Mahashaya went,
to whom Krishna sells Himself.

**96.** Acharyachandra Sri Chandrashekhara went,
in whose house the Lord danced in the form of the Devi.[^46]

**97.** Joyfully Pandita Gangadasa went:
remember him, and all sins are destroyed.

**98.** With a jubilant heart Purushottama Sanjaya went,
Mahaprabhu's chief pupil in the former days of study.

**99.** Crying "Hari!", Pandita Shriman went,
who carefully holds the torch during the Lord's dancing.

**100.** Nandana Acharya and other beloved ones went:
Nityananda came first to his house.

**101.** The scribe went — the fortunate pandita
in whose body Krishna dwelt.[^47]

**102.** Gopinatha Pandita and Sri Garbha Pandita
went — both truly forms of Krishna.[^48]

*[p. 17]*

**103.** Joyfully Shuklambara Brahmachari went,
whose food Lord Gaurahari ate.

**104.** The penniless Shridhara, Krishna's servant, went,
whose water Sri Gaurasundara drank.

**105.** Sri Vanamali Pandita, the auspicious, went,
who saw Nityananda's plough and pestle.[^49]

**106.** Jagadisha Pandita and Hiranya Bhagavata
went joyfully — both intoxicated with the rasa of Krishna.

**107.** Formerly, in the form of an infant, [the Lord]
ate in the houses of these two the offering brought on Hari's day.[^50]

**108.** Buddhimanta Khan Mahashaya went:
from his very birth his business is Chaitanya's commands.

**109.** Crying "Hari!", Acharya Purandara went,
whom Sri Gaurasundara calls "father".

**110.** The generous Pandita Sri Raghava went,
in whose house Chaitanya secretly played.

**111.** Gopinatha Simha Mahashaya went,
whom Gaurachandra calls Akrura.

**112.** Murari went — a lion among physicians for the disease of worldly existence,
who foresaw the advent of Gaurachandra.[^51]

**113.** Pandita Shriman, greatly beloved of the Lord,
went together with Vanamali Pandita.[^52]

**114.** Pandita Damodara, who had come to see Ai,
having seen Ai, quickly went [with all].

*[p. 18]*

**115.** The devotees of Chaitanya are countless — how many names shall I give?
All went, becoming abodes of bliss.

**116.** Having honoured Ai and taken leave of her,
the lion Advaita went, leading the host of devotees.

**117.** Whatever dishes they knew the Lord had liked before —
those dishes they took to feed Him.

**118.** All along the way performing kirtana in bliss,
they went, sanctifying every path.

**119.** Exulting, the devotees cry "Hari!" —
hearing it, the three worlds are purified.

**120.** With wives, sons, menservants and maidservants
they came in the highest bliss to see Chaitanya.

**121.** Wherever they stopped and settled for the night,
that place became like the city of Vaikuntha.

**122.** Of which Mahaprabhu sings, that very Bhagavan, […][^53]

## The glory of the Lord

**123.** In the form of Bhagavan the Lord is in endless pastimes;
by the Lord's mercy Bhagavan is one in form.[^54]

**124.** Ananta in the form of Madhava sports in play;
Ananta serves in the house of Mukunda.

**125.** Mahaprabhu honours devotion with reverence,
as if a gem were hidden within a stone.[^55]

**126.** In the form of the original energy is Mahi, the Vaishnavi mother;
her child is Ganga, who holds the great jewel.

*[p. 19]*

**127.** All these original forms give birth to devotion;
devotion is dear to the Lord — this is the inner meaning.

**128.** This is the secret, unfathomable narrative of the Vedas:
a good to the pious, a hindrance to the deeds of the wicked.

**129.** Listen, all, to these secret accounts:
their support is Mahaprabhu, Bhagavan.

**130.** Glory, glory to Bhagavan, glory to Haladhara!
Glory, glory to Bhagavan, the beautiful Trimalla![^56]

**131.** Glory, glory to Harihara, glory to Balarama!
Glory, glory to Jagannatha, the abode of bliss!

**132.** Glory, glory to Mahaprabhu Sri Krishna Chaitanya!
Glory, glory to Nityananda Prabhu, blessed is He, blessed!

## The meeting at Nilachala

**133.** Thus in joy all the great souls
safely came to Nilachala.

**134.** Seeing at Kamalapura the flags and banners [of the temple],
all fell down weeping, [prostrate] like sticks.[^57]

**135.** Mahaprabhu, hearing of the arrival of the host of devotees,
wished to go out to meet them.

**136.** What wondrous love — it has no end:
His mahaprasada reaches as far as Kataka!

**137.** "For Advaita's sake is this descent of Mine" —
so Mahaprabhu says again and again.

**138.** "I lay asleep in the Ocean of Milk —
My sleep was broken by Nada's roar."[^58]

*[p. 20]*

**139.** "Nada! Nada!" crying, the Lord roars:
"For Advaita's sake is this descent of Mine."

**140.** No one can understand the reason [for His love of] Nada:
for Nada's sake I descended into the three worlds.

**141.** Because of Nada [came] Lord Chaitanya and Nitai,
whose qualities I sing in endless universes.

## Conclusion

**142.** Endless are Chaitanya's servants — how many names do I know?
With joined palms I praise the feet of them all.

**143.** Whoever, rising at dawn, reads and hears [this]
attains Advaita and Chaitanya — this cannot be refuted.

**144.** Know Sri Nityananda and Sri Chaitanya-chandra —
Vrindavana Dasa sings at Their feet.[^59]

*Thus ends the "Sri Vaishnava-vandana" composed by Srimad Vrindavana Dasa Thakura.*

### Translator's notes

[^1]: Attribution. The 1910 editor called this vandana the "First"; its colophon ascribes it to Vrindavana Dasa Thakura. In substance it is a compilation of couplets from the *Chaitanya-bhagavata* (CB): the verses Adi 1.1–2; the list of Nityananda's associates (Antya 5.718–757) and the account of the devotees going to Nilachala (Antya 8.3–52), with few changes. To these are added an insertion, the "Gopala-nirnaya" (identifications of the associates with the cowherd boys of Vraja), and several linking couplets not found in CB (12–19, 25, 75, 123–132 and others). Vrindavana Dasa himself declined to give the associates' former names (see v. 20), so the insertions are the work of a later compiler. The name of Vrindavana Dasa in the colophon is probably due to the borrowed lines, including his closing signature (v. 144). CB verse numbers follow the Gaudiya Math edition (`chaitanya-bhagavata/src/`).
[^2]: CB Adi 1.1 (the opening verse of the *Chaitanya-bhagavata*): Gaura and Nityananda.
[^3]: CB Adi 1.2.
[^4]: Chapters of CB begin this way (cf. Antya 5.2, 9.2).
[^5]: Vv. 11–12 — CB Antya 5.718–720. "Nanda's community" is Vraja: Nityananda's associates are cowherd boys of Vraja (cf. GGD 14).
[^6]: Nityananda is Ananta-Shesha, thousand-mouthed, eternally singing the Lord's glory. As Shesha He serves the Lord with His bodies (as couch, umbrella and so on) and bears upon Himself the Lord's form, which is non-different from Him.
[^7]: That is, Nityananda moves through this world to deliver conditioned souls. He is not subject to maya: maya rules only the world of samsara.
[^8]: Cf. CB Antya 5.756: all will become known "through Vedavyasa". Here the reference is probably to those who indicated the associates' former forms (cf. GGD 5).
[^9]: CB Antya 5.721. In Vrindavana Dasa this couplet explains why the former names are not given. Here those names are given immediately after it (the "Gopala-nirnaya", vv. 24–44): one more sign that the insertion is not Vrindavana Dasa's.
[^10]: CB Antya 5.722–724. Ramadasa is Abhirama (cf. Pt. 1, 95; Pt. 2, 83). In CB, Nityananda is always in his heart (here, Gaurachandra), and Krishna dwelt for three months "in his body". The word *grihastha* ("householder") in the edition is probably a corruption of *bhava-grasta* ("seized by feeling"), as in CB; translated accordingly. "The mood of the Lord" (*ishvara-bhava*): when Krishna manifested in him, Ramadasa spoke as the Lord. This is no claim to divinity.
[^11]: GGD 126: Abhirama is Shridama; it agrees. References to the *Bhagavata* and the Puranas here and below concern the names of the cowherd boys (Shridama, Subala, Stokakrishna, Arjuna and others are named in Bhag. 10.22.31), not the identifications themselves.
[^12]: CB Antya 5.728 ("chief among Nityananda's associates"). The last word of the couplet (বাণা) is unclear.
[^13]: GGD 127: Sundarananda is Sudama; it agrees.
[^14]: CB Antya 5.729: Nityananda gives Saptagrama to "Kamalakanta Pandita"; here the reading is "Kamalakara". According to GGD 127–128, Vasudama is Dhananjaya Pandita, and Kamalakara Pippalai is Mahabala. Here it differs from GGD.
[^15]: CB Antya 5.743. GGD 129: Uddharana Datta is Subahu. It differs.
[^16]: CB Antya 5.732 ("Parameshvari Dasa"). GGD 132: Parameshvara Dasa is Arjuna. It differs.
[^17]: CB Antya 5.742. According to CB (5.741), Purushottama Dasa is the son of Sadashiva Kaviraja (Devakinandana's guru, Pt. 2, 87–94). GGD distinguishes Purushottama Dasa, who is Stokakrishna (130), from Nagara Purushottama, son of Sadashiva, who is Dama (131). The identification with Stokakrishna agrees with GGD 130.
[^18]: CB Antya 5.730. GGD 128: Gauridasa Pandita is Subala; it agrees.
[^19]: CB Antya 5.737–738; cf. Pt. 2, 100. According to GGD 132, Arjuna is Parameshvara Dasa; Purushottama Pandita is not named among the cowherd boys in GGD.
[^20]: The "son of Ratai" is not in CB; he may be the same Purushottama, son of Ratnakara (Pt. 2, 97; Pt. 1, 101 and note). According to GGD 132, Lavanga is Kala Krishnadasa.
[^21]: CB Antya 5.740. According to GGD 132, Kaliya (Kala) Krishnadasa is Lavanga, and Mahabahu (GGD 129) is Mahesha Pandita. The "Gopala-nirnaya" in sum: ten cowherd boys are named (Dama and Kusumasava are missing). Shridama, Sudama, Subala and Stokakrishna agree with GGD; the other six identifications differ. Cf. CC Adi 11, which lists the same associates of Nityananda, but without former names.
[^22]: CB Antya 5.727. According to GGD 154–155, Gadadhara Dasa is Chandrakanti, a manifestation of Radha's splendour, and Purnananda; the vandanas call him Radha herself (cf. Pt. 1, 83, "the daughter of Vrishabhanu"; Pt. 5, 54). The understanding accepted among Gaudiya Vaishnavas follows GGD: Gadadhara Dasa carries Radha's radiance (*kanti*).
[^23]: CB Antya 5.741. GGD 156: Sadashiva Kaviraja is Chandravali; it agrees.
[^24]: Vv. 49–53 — CB Antya 5.733, 744, 734, 739, 736.
[^25]: CB Antya 5.748. On Krishnadasa of Badagachhi see Pt. 1, 125–128 and Pt. 5, 104–109.
[^26]: CB Antya 5.735 has "Yadunatha Kavichandra"; "Jagannatha" here is probably a corruption.
[^27]: Vv. 56–57 — CB Antya 5.725–726. Murari Chaitanya Dasa — cf. Pt. 1, 120; Pt. 2, 121.
[^28]: CB Antya 5.745: "Gangadasa, son (নন্দন) of Chaturbhuja Pandita". The edition's commas make one person into three, reading the word "son" as the name "Nandana". The translation follows the edition.
[^29]: CB Antya 5.746; this couplet (with a different second line) is repeated in v. 64.
[^30]: Vv. 60–63 — CB Antya 5.744, 747, 749. CB 5.749 and 5.752 have "Krishnadasa and Devananda"; here, "Krishnananda".
[^31]: CB Antya 5.752: "Manohara, Narayana, Krishnadasa, Devananda". In the edition a corrupt word (বাএন) stands where "Narayana" should be; restored from CB.
[^32]: Cf. CB Antya 5.751, which concerns one person, Jiva Pandita, "in whose house Nityananda-chandra played".
[^33]: Vv. 68–76 — couplets from the list of devotees going to Nilachala (CB Antya 8.10, 13, 15, 17–20, 34). The verb "went" is replaced here by "I praise".
[^34]: CB Antya 8.13. Who this second Haridasa "who lives on the seashore" is remains unclear.
[^35]: CB Antya 8.17: Govinda Datta, the lead singer in the Lord's kirtana.
[^36]: CB Antya 8.18. Vijaya was a copyist (cf. Pt. 1, 49; Pt. 2, 33). The Lord showed him His greatness, and thereafter he was called "Ratnabahu" ("jewel-arm").
[^37]: CB Antya 8.20, 22. In CB, Nityananda first comes to the house of Nandana Acharya (cf. v. 100), while Purushottama Sanjaya is the Lord's chief pupil in His student days (cf. v. 98). Here the two lines are joined.
[^38]: This couplet is not in CB Antya 8. Achyutananda is Advaita's son; Brahmananda Bharati — CC Madhya 10; Sanatana and Rupa are the Gosvamis.
[^39]: CB Antya 8.34: by the power of the holy name, snake poison did not overcome Garuda Pandita. Here the power is transferred to one who utters his name.
[^40]: Vv. 77–80 — CB Antya 5.753–755 (77 has no parallel).
[^41]: CB Antya 5.757–758: Narayani is "the vessel of Chaitanya's remnants" (cf. Pt. 1, 36–37; Pt. 2, 19, 126).
[^42]: The sense of the first line is unclear; who speaks in the second — Vrindavana Dasa or the compiler — is also unclear. "Vrindavana-chandra" is Krishna (Gaura); perhaps there is also a play on the name of Vrindavana Dasa.
[^43]: Vv. 87–90 — CB Antya 8.3–6. "Acharya Gosani" is Advaita.
[^44]: CB Antya 8.11: Vakreshvara, "for whose dancing Gaurasundara became the kirtaniya". Cf. Pt. 1, 80.
[^45]: CB Antya 8.13 reads differently. *Brahma-vastu* here is Brahma (GGD 93–95; Pt. 1, 38, "the abode of Brahma"), not the impersonal Brahman.
[^46]: CB Antya 8.8: "Acharyaratna Sri Chandrashekhara". The Lord's dance in the form of the Devi (Lakshmi, Rukmini) in Chandrashekhara's house — CB Madhya 18. Vv. 91–114 repeat CB Antya 8.7–37 in a somewhat different order.
[^47]: CB Antya 8.25: "the scribe — Bhagavan Pandita"; the edition has *bhagyavan* ("fortunate").
[^48]: CB Antya 8.26. Gopinatha Pandita is probably Gopinatha Acharya. Shrigarbha — cf. Pt. 1, 47.
[^49]: CB Antya 8.27: Vanamali saw the golden plough and pestle (Balarama's emblems, revealed by the Lord).
[^50]: CB Antya 8.28–29: as a child the Lord, on Ekadashi, demanded the food prepared for Vishnu in the houses of Jagadisha and Hiranya (CB Adi 6).
