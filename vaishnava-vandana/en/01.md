# Part 1. The Sanskrit *Vaishnava-vandana* attributed to Jiva Gosvami

*Introduction; Gaura and His family; Nityananda, His consorts and descendants; Madhavendra, Advaita and His line; the associates at Navadvipa; the sannyasis; the Gosvamis of Vrindavana; associates in Gauda and Orissa; Nityananda's followers; conclusion: the succession from Madhavendra Puri (verses 1–153)*

## Introduction

**1.** He whose elder brother is the glorious Sanatana, like unto the Eternal, and whose younger brother is Sri Vallabha — that Sri Rupa is the true refuge of Jiva.[^1]

**2.** Bhagavan, the son of Sri Shachi — Krishna — descended in the age of Kali with all the avataras and their devotees: the Lord absorbed in their various moods.

**3.** "He who utters the syllables 'Krishna' and whose lustre is not dark, accompanied by His parts, intimates, weapons and associates — in the age of Kali the intelligent worship Him with sacrifices consisting chiefly of sankirtana."[^2]

**4.** The one God is the supreme Krishnachandra; He is the One who bears the name Krishna Chaitanya; and this God Nityananda is His own form (svarupa). Dividing Himself in two […], may He do [good] to us.[^3]

**5.** His dear ones, Advaita and the others, are His very self; the second [after Him is Nityananda]; His many principal energies are glorious Rupa and the others. The Lord born of Shachi is a wide-spreading tree of prema: may It give shade to those scorched by the heat [of worldly existence].

**6.** To praise them and to remember them bestows every perfection. And this [praise] is made by a certain jiva — Jiva, who does not know which of them comes first and which after.[^4]

**7.** Forgive my offences, O great souls whose hearts are with Krishna — saints who see no faults in others, eager to show mercy to the wretched.

**8.** As you are here, as [the knowers] truly know you, so may Bhagavan make [me] speak, impelled by His command.

## Gaura and His family

**9.** I praise Shachi and Jagannatha, who are Yashoda and Nanda;[^5] both their sons are Vishvarupa and the divine Vishvambhara.

**10.** Then I praise Vishvarupa, king among sannyasis, called Shankararanya — the wondrous elder brother of Chaitanya.[^6]

**11.** I praise Sri Gaurachandra, whose body is made of rasa, the abode of a treasury of compassion: taking the mood of Radhika in order to relish Sri Hari, to deliver the hosts of souls stained by the filth of Kali and devoid of every [good] disposition, He joyfully appeared with throngs of His associates in Sri Navadvipa.[^7]

**12.** I praise the goddess Lakshmipriya, and then Vishnupriya; then the divine Gadadhara, who is truly the Lord's second body.[^8]

**13.** He is the disciple of Vidyanidhi, a mine of the rasa of devotion to the Lord; this is the steadfast Gadadhara, beloved of all the devotees.

## Nityananda and His line

**14.** I praise Padmavati and her husband Haddipa Pandita:[^9] the merciful Nityananda became their son.

**15.** I praise the divine Nityananda — Balabhadra, the Lord Himself, the root of bliss, the fearless one, the deliverer of the world, the guru.

**16.** He is both purusha and prakriti, by the distinction of outer and inner: in different bodies He serves Sri Krishna.[^10]

**17.** I praise Sri Vasudha-devi, beloved of Nityananda Prabhu, daughter of Sri Suryadasa, awakened by the power of the Lord.[^11]

**18.** I praise Sri Jahnavi-devi, the disciple of Sri Puri-Ishvara, whom the knowers of secrets call Ananga-manjari.[^12]

**19.** When the Lord had taken sannyasa and gone away, by His command she serves Nityananda — His very own form — with supreme love, firm in her vow.[^13]

**20.** Ever drawn by separation, the mistress went to Vrindavana; when she wished to see Gopinatha, He pulled the knot of her garment.

**21.** And the goddess whose knot had been pulled said to Him — the dawning of rasa: "Soon I will come to You — to the place beside Your feet."[^14]

**22.** I praise Lord Virachandra — Hari, the Lord Sri Chaitanya, appearing a second time, the deliverer of the three worlds,[^15]

**23.** devoted to the Vedic dharma and yet detached, free from egotism, without pride, self-controlled — the servant of Jahnavi here.[^16]

**24.** The daughter of Nityananda Prabhu, whose nature is melted in Radha and Krishna, the wife of Madhava Acharya, whose form is being, consciousness and bliss,

**25.** the chief — Sri Prema-manjari, the excellent mother of the worlds — I praise Ganga, the giver of prema, the purifier of the three worlds.[^17]

**26.** This Ganga, the disciple of Jahnavi, together with the purifying lords […], purifies the three worlds.[^18]

**27.** [I praise] Madhava, the ornament of the twice-born, who appeared wishing to take as wife Ganga, who had descended [to earth] — like Madhava in form, his body full of rasa — named Prema.[^19]

**28.** A disciple of Ishvara Puri, gone to the far shore of all the darshanas, foremost among the devotees of Vishnu, adorned with a host of good qualities,

**29.** the wise one examined them and, setting aside karma and knowledge, established the truth of love for Krishna — an ocean of mercy.

## Madhavendra, Advaita and His line

**30.** The ornament of the ancient line of ascetics, lord of sages, devoted to the first guru-Lord — I praise Sri Madhavendra, who made devotion to Hari manifest.[^20]

**31.** I praise Advaita, the merciful, the supremely compassionate — the very abode of Shambhu in person: by His austerities the son of Sri Shachi was brought here together with His associates.[^21]

**32.** [I praise] His beloved named Sita — the primal energy of Kailasa, mother of the three worlds: pleased with her, Sri Jagannatha bestowed His grace on the three worlds.[^22]

**33.** Among His sons, the one called Achyutananda — him I praise, supreme bliss, the beloved of Krishna Chaitanya;

**34.** the one called Achyuta, who knows the truth of Sri Krishna Chaitanya, servant of the steadfast Sri Gadadhara, an ocean of good qualities.

**35.** Those followers of Sri Advaita and [His] sons who wholly worshipped with devotion the merciful Sri Chaitanya-Hari, the son of Shachi, as the Lord of all — [are worthy of praise]. But many others were struck down by God — them we do not honour; save Achyuta, they are abandoned: I pass them over.[^23]

## The associates at Navadvipa

**36.** I praise Srivasa — Narada; Malini — the [Lord's] second mother; then Narayani-devi, who partook of the nectar of His lips.[^24]

**37.** I praise Narayani's son — Vrindavana Dasa, the excellent, who described the qualities of Sri Nityananda and Chaitanya.[^25]

**38.** I praise Haridasa — the abode of Brahma, who made the holy name of Hari manifest — and Jagadananda Pandita, another form of Vani.[^26]

**39.** Then I praise Gopinatha, who offered praise to Chaitanya;[^27] then Murari Gupta, the great soul, Hanuman.[^28]

**40.** I praise Sri Chandrashekhara, ever cool as the moon; Acharyaratna; Govinda and Garuda, whose minds are with Gaura.[^29]

**41.** I praise also Mukunda Datta, the great soul, maddened by singing the pure qualities of Sri Krishna, praised by the kinnaras.[^30]

**42.** I praise Vasudeva Datta, full of greatness: by the touch of the breeze from his body one at once attains prema.[^31]

**43.** Damodara and Pitambara, Jagannatha, Shankara and Narayana — these five, free from desires, I praise — saints, great souls.[^32]

**44.** I praise Nilambara Chakravarti, renowned as the Lord's maternal grandfather, who cast the horoscope that contained what was to come.[^33]

**45.** I praise Sri Rama Pandita, devoted to the welfare of all beings,[^34] and Narayana Gupta, the great soul, the one abode of virtues.[^35]

**46.** I praise Gangadasa, who lived in Navadvipa, the supreme guru, and Sri Vishnudasa, and the one called Sri Sudarshana.[^36]

**47.** I praise Sadashiva, Vidyanidhi and Shrigarbha, Shrinidhi, Buddhimanta and the excellent Srila Shuklambara —

**48.** these six great souls, brahmacharis full of love;[^37] and Sri Ramadasa, and Kavichandra, an ocean of mercy.[^38]

**49.** I praise Vijaya the scribe,[^39] and the pure Acharya Ratneshvara,[^40] and the generous Shridhara, the renowned,[^41] and Vanamali together with his son.[^42]

**50.** I praise Halayudha and Vasudeva, the pure, whose hearts are with Sri Chaitanya, and Ishana Dasa, the object of Shachi-devi's affection.[^43]

**51.** With humility I praise Shriman and Sanjaya, the merciful: their mark is supreme bliss, their hearts are given to Chaitanya.[^44]

**52.** Garuda, Kashishvara, Jagadisha and Gangadasa, both, the sweet Krishnananda I praise, and the excellent Rama-Mukunda.[^45]

**53.** I praise Vallabha Acharya, whose daughter Lakshmi captivates the heart: rich in virtues, he gave her to the son of Shachi, the bridegroom.[^46]

**54.** Then I praise Sanatana Pandita, rich in virtues, who gave his daughter Vishnupriya to the son of Shachi.

**55.** I praise the brahmana Kashinatha, and Vanamali Acharya, who conceived and arranged the marriage of Lakshmi-devi.[^47]

## The sannyasis

**56.** Then I praise Ishvara Puri: accepting him as His guru, the Lord, called Chaitanya, considered Himself blessed.[^48]

**57.** I praise also Sri Keshava Bharati, honoured by the sannyasis, from whom Mahaprabhu took the renunciation of dharma.[^49]

**58.** Then I praise Ramachandra Puri, [he] to whom the Lord always yielded;[^50] [I praise] Sri Puri Paramananda, called Uddhava, dear to Hari.[^51]

**59.** Then I praise Damodara Puri, like unto Satyabhama;[^52] I praise Narasimha Tirtha, then Sukhananda Puri;

**60.** then the one called Govindananda, and Brahmananda Puri; the one called Nrisimhananda, and Satyananda Bharati.

**61.** I praise Garuda Avadhuta, endowed with wondrous prema; then I praise Vishnu Puri, who composed the *Bhakti-ratnavali*;[^53]

**62.** and Brahmananda Svarupa, then Krishnananda Puri; Sri Raghava Puri I praise with devotion and supreme joy;

**63.** I praise Vishveshvarananda, then Sri Keshava Puri; I praise then Anubhavananda and Chidananda, pure of heart.[^54]

## The Gosvamis of Vrindavana

**64.** I praise the two masters — Rupa and Sanatana, supreme bliss, detached and merciful, dwelling in Vrindavana.

**65.** Perfumed by even a trace of the fragrance of their lotus feet, may I, named Jiva, serve them here, birth after birth.[^55]

**66.** Sri Rupa, endowed with the Lord's power, examining all the shastras, established the supreme truth — love for Krishna — an ocean of mercy.[^56]

**67.** Sanatana, seeking nothing for himself, composed the *Hari-bhakti-vilasa* and other works — what devotees ought to do — under the name of Gopala Bhatta.[^57]

**68.** And the wise Gopala Bhatta, living near Sanatana, devoted to the qualities of Hari, happily spent his days and nights here;

**69.** constantly hearing what he told of the Lord's form and qualities, he truly considered himself blessed in every way.[^58]

**70.** I praise Raghunatha Dasa, who dwells at Radha-kunda, who knows the whole truth of Chaitanya, the best, who has given up every other feeling.[^59]

**71.** I praise the Gosvami named Raghava, who sports at Govardhana, a great soul, wandering [there] in a special mood.[^60]

**72.** With humility I praise Raghunatha Bhatta, teacher of the *Sri Bhagavata*,[^61] Lokanatha Gosvami and the pure Bhugarbha Thakura.[^62]

**73.** With joy I praise the pure Prabodhananda Sarasvati, by whom the *Chandramrita* was composed, whose disciple is Gopala Bhatta.[^63]

**74.** Then I praise Kashishvara,[^64] then Shuddha Sarasvati,[^65] and then Raghavananda, imbued with the mood of Nityananda,[^66]

**75.** in whose house the son of Padmavati, in play, joined a kadamba flower to a pomegranate tree.[^67]

## The associates in Gauda and Orissa

**76.** I praise Purandara — here truly equal to Angada: a certain best of brahmanas saw his tail in his house.[^68]

**77.** I praise the best of Mishras — Kashi, dwelling in Utkala, the spotless: in his abode lived Gaurahari, worshipped by his devotion.[^69]

**78.** Then I praise Vaninatha, whose life is Sri Jagannatha;[^70] then I praise Ramananda, full of all the marks of devotion:

**79.** from the cloud of his mouth the merciful Chaitanya rained upon the earth the nectar — a multitude of truths about devotion to Him.[^71]

**80.** Then I praise Vakreshvara, the heart of the Lord, who is attained with the greatest difficulty: in his presence the Lord performed kirtana in the bliss of prema.[^72]

**81.** I praise that Sugriva Mishra — Govinda, the best of the twice-born, whose greatness in bhakti-yoga is renowned on earth:[^73]

**82.** so that the Lord might go to the forest [of Vrindavana], he built from the land of Sri Navadvipa across the whole land of Gauda a bridge made of mind.[^74]

**83.** I praise here Gadadhara Dasa — the daughter of Vrishabhanu, whose body is non-different from Sri Krishna, the embodiment of mahabhava.[^75]

**84.** I praise Sadashiva the vaidya: at his mere touch even a stone at once melts — what then of the living![^76]

**85.** I praise Shivananda Sena, devoted to firm faith and peace, who knows nothing but the Lord's feet.[^77]

**86.** I praise that Mukunda Dasa whose son is Raghunandana — truly Kama, the husband of Rati — the one who fed Gopala.[^78]

**87.** The devotion of Sri Mukunda Dasa is sung by people to this day: seeing a peacock feather, drawn away by love for Krishna,

**88.** he at once lost his senses, immersed in supreme bliss, and, unaware of the outer world, fell down from his high seat.[^79]

**89.** With devotion I praise Narahari Dasa, who gave the play of his feelings to Chaitanya, called Madhumati — pure, blessed, seeing nothing but Krishna.[^80]

**90.** And this Raghunandana is most worthy, the disciple of Narahari, righteous, honoured; saintly in character from childhood, pure, his heart cleansed by devotion.

**91.** I praise then Raghunatha Dasa,[^81] Purandara Acharya, generous in deeds, and Sri Krishnadasa, slave of Hari's feet, gentle, merciful, loving the people of Bhagavan.[^82]

**92.** I praise Paramananda Pandita, the Lord's fellow student,[^83] and Devananda Pandita, the reciter of the *Sri Bhagavata*.[^84]

**93.** I praise Acharyaratna, who knows the inmost essence of prema,[^85] and Govinda, Madhavananda and Vasu Ghosha — treasuries of virtue.[^86]

**94.** I praise the Dasa named Purushottama, endowed with majesty, who made the karavi flower in his ears smell like a lotus.[^87]

**95.** I praise Abhirama Dasa, who on earth is called Shridama: a log that only many could lift he playfully made into a flute.[^88]

**96.** I praise Sri Sundarananda, the form of the cowherd Sudama: his disciple gave the name of Hari here even to tigers.[^89]

**97.** I praise also Sri Gauridasa — the cowherd called Subala: led by him, Advaita Thakura [came] to Utkala, to supreme bliss.[^90]

**98.** By him the Deities of Sri Chaitanya and Nityananda were made manifest in person: by the sight of these Deities the bonds of karma are at once destroyed.[^91]

**99.** Then I praise Parameshvara Thakura, the self-luminous: dancing, he made the jackals hear the name of Hari.[^92]

**100.** Then I praise Pippalai, overwhelmed by a childlike mood — I praise Kamalakara Dasa, whose bliss is sankirtana.[^93]

**101.** I praise the one called Purushottama Tirtha, the crown of rasikas;[^94] then I praise Kaliya Krishnadasa, overwhelmed by prema alone.[^95]

**102.** I praise Sharnga Thakura, who revealed his greatness: he gave snakes places in his own dwelling.[^96]

**103.** Then I praise Makaradhvaja, beautiful, the one abode of virtues, who always sings kirtana to Krishna near the Lord.[^97]

**104.** Then Bhagavatacharya,[^98] Sri Kaviraja Mishra,[^99] and then Ananta Acharya, who lived at Navadvipa.[^100]

**105.** I praise the Pandita named Madhu[^101] and the one called Govinda Acharya, who zealously described the secret of Radha and Krishna.[^102]

**106.** Then I praise Sarvabhauma Bhattacharya — Brihaspati;[^103] then Prataparudra, by whom the Lord's six arms were seen.[^104]

**107.** I praise the brahmana Raghunatha, the physician Sri Vishnudasa; I praise also his brother — Vanamali Dasa;[^105]

**108.** Vipradasa of Utkala and then the brahmana Haridasa: by them Hari, the son of Sri Shachi, was won over by love.[^106]

**109.** I praise Kanai Khutiya, a treasury of the rasa of love for Krishna, whose two auspicious sons are Jagannatha and Balarama.[^107]

**110.** I praise Jagannatha, at whose singing the trees here weep, losing themselves, and the compassionate Balarama the Oriya, to whom Baladeva and Jagannatha are subject.[^108]

**111.** With devotion I praise the Thakura named Govindananda: for the Lord's sake he built a bridge made of mind.[^109]

**112.** Then I praise Kashishvara, the one called Sri Simheshvara, and Shivananda Pandita, and then Chandaneshvara.[^110]

**113.** With the highest feeling I praise Madhava Pattanayaka; then I praise Hari Bhatta and Baladeva Mahiti;

**114.** then Subuddhi Mishra and Shrinatha, the best of Mishras; I praise Sri Tulasi Mishra and Kashinatha Mahiti.[^111]

**115.** Ramananda, first in the Vasu line, together with his family;[^112] both Purushottama Brahmachari and the Pandita named Madhu,[^113]

### Translator's notes

[^1]: This verse is Jiva's own signature: Rupa Gosvami, his uncle and teacher, stands between his elder brother Sanatana and his younger brother Vallabha (Anupama), Jiva's father. "Refuge of Jiva" is a pun: "the refuge of living beings (jivas)" and "the refuge of Jiva". According to Majumdar, the same verse closes Jiva's commentary on the *Ujjvala-nilamani*; this and the other places where "Jiva" is named (vv. 6, 65, 153) are the basis for attributing the vandana to Jiva Gosvami. The *Bhakti-ratnakara* does not list it among Jiva's works, and one of the two known manuscripts (according to Majumdar) lacks the first seven verses. The authorship remains disputed (see the preface).
[^2]: *Bhagavata Purana* 11.5.32.
[^3]: The second half of the verse is corrupt (the edition marks it with a question mark); the translation is approximate. The sense seems to be that Krishna appearing as Chaitanya and Nityananda are one Lord in two forms.
[^4]: A pun: "jiva", "a living being", and the name Jiva. "Not knowing the order" is an apology for not listing the associates by seniority (cf. GGD 212).
[^5]: GGD 37: Yashoda and Nanda became Shachi and Jagannatha Purandara.
[^6]: Shankararanya is Vishvarupa's sannyasa name (CC Madhya 9). According to GGD 58–62 Vishvarupa is Sankarshana, who on his departure merged with Nityananda.
[^7]: Cf. CC Adi 1.5–6 and 4: Krishna took Radha's mood and lustre in order to relish Himself. The word rendered "joyfully" is corrupt in the text.
[^8]: According to GGD 147–153 Gadadhara Pandita is Radha (the "energy of the devotee" in the pancha-tattva, GGD 11). He received mantra from Pundarika Vidyanidhi (CB Madhya 7).
[^9]: Haddipa (Hadai) Pandita, Nityananda's father, of Ekachakra; GGD 40 calls him Mukunda and identifies Nityananda's parents with Rohini and Vasudeva.
[^10]: That is, Nityananda-Balarama serves Krishna both in the outer, male form (as Balarama and Shesha — cf. GGD 64; CC Adi 5) and in an inner, female form. The vandana does not say which female form is meant; below (v. 18) it calls Jahnava Ananga-manjari (cf. GGD 66).
[^11]: Vasudha and Jahnava were daughters of Suryadasa Sarakhela (GGD 65: Varuni and Revati). "Awakened by the power of the Lord" may allude to Nityananda's healing of Vasudha, described in the *Nityananda-vamsha-vistara* (ch. 1).
[^12]: "Puri-Ishvara" is Ishvara Puri: below (v. 142) the vandana plainly calls Gaurachandra and Jahnavi his disciples. This is found only in the vandanas; it is not in the CC or the CB, and chronologically it is difficult (Ishvara Puri left the world soon after the Lord's sannyasa). Ananga-manjari is Radha's younger sister; GGD 66 gives both opinions — that she is Vasudha and that she is Jahnavi.
[^13]: That is, Nityananda married at Mahaprabhu's command (cf. *Nityananda-vamsha-vistara*, ch. 1).
[^14]: Gopinatha is Madhu Pandita's Deity in Vrindavana. The tradition that Jahnava in Vrindavana was united with Gopinatha (He drew her to Himself, and to this day her Deity stands beside Him) is told here briefly: "He pulled the knot of her garment (nivi)." This is a lila of the Deity and of Ananga-manjari, Radha's sister — a sign of His intimacy, nothing worldly. Jahnava's journeys to Vrindavana are described in the *Bhakti-ratnakara* and the *Nityananda-vamsha-vistara*.
[^15]: According to GGD 67 Virachandra is the One who lies on the ocean of milk, non-different from Chaitanya; tradition regards him as Mahaprabhu's second appearance (cf. *Nityananda-vamsha-vistara*, ch. 1).
[^16]: Here Virachandra is called not Nityananda's son (as in CC Adi 11 and in Devakinandana, Part 2, v. 12) but only "the servant of Jahnavi", i.e. her disciple; Majumdar too draws attention to this.
[^17]: Ganga is Nityananda's daughter; GGD 69: she is the river Ganga, and her husband Madhava is King Shantanu. "The chief — Sri Prema-manjari" may identify Ganga with Prema-manjari (according to GGD 187 Prema-manjari is Bhugarbha); the reading is unclear.
[^18]: The second half of the verse is corrupt (something about Brahma-Viranchi: the Ganga, born from Vishnu's foot, purifies the three worlds).
[^19]: Madhava Acharya, Ganga's husband (GGD 69). A pun: Ganga is both the river and Nityananda's daughter; "Madhava" is both his name and a name of Krishna. Not to be confused with Madhava Acharya, the author of the *Krishna-mangala* (v. 135). That he was a disciple of Ishvara Puri (v. 28) is reported only by the vandanas.
[^20]: Madhavendra Puri is "the root of the tree of devotion" (CC Adi 9); GGD 22–25 — a link in the succession from Madhva. "To the first guru-Lord" — the reading is unclear.
[^21]: GGD 76: Advaita is Sadashiva; "the abode of Shambhu" (Shiva) points to the same. Advaita called the Lord by worshipping with Ganga water and tulasi leaves (CC Adi 3; CB Adi 2).
[^22]: According to GGD 86 Sita is Yogamaya; the vandana calls her "the primal energy of Kailasa" as the consort of Advaita-Sadashiva. The end of the verse is unclear; the translation is approximate.
[^23]: Cf. CC Adi 12: Advaita's followers divided; those who accepted the view of Achyutananda (that Chaitanya is the Supreme Lord) are recognised as true, the rest as fallen away. Achyutananda, a disciple of Gadadhara Pandita — GGD 87–88. The verse is in a long metre; its second half is partly corrupt in the text.
[^24]: GGD 90: Srivasa is the sage Narada; GGD 42: Malini is Krishna's nurse Ambika. To Narayani, Srivasa's niece, the Lord gave the remnants of His food (CB); GGD 43: she is Kilimbika.
[^25]: Vrindavana Dasa, author of the *Chaitanya-bhagavata* (GGD 109: Vedavyasa).
[^26]: GGD 93: Haridasa is Brahma together with Prahlada. Jagadananda here is "another form of Vani" (Sarasvati, goddess of speech); so too in Devakinandana (Part 2, v. 62: "Sarasvati herself"). According to GGD 51 he is a manifestation of Satyabhama.
[^27]: Probably Gopinatha Acharya: in Puri he argued the Lord's divinity before Sarvabhauma (CC Madhya 6); Devakinandana (Part 2, v. 21) has "counted as Brahma for his praise of the Lord", which agrees with GGD 75 (Gopinatha Acharya is Brahma).
[^28]: GGD 91.
[^29]: GGD 112: Chandrashekhara is Chandra (the Moon). Acharyaratna is, according to CC Adi 10, the title of this same Chandrashekhara; but GGD 103 and both vandanas name Acharyaratna separately (he is mentioned again below, v. 93). Govinda and Garuda — according to GGD 116 favourites of the Lord, in Vaikuntha Pundarikaksha and Kumuda.
[^30]: Mukunda Datta, the singer (GGD 140: the singer of Vraja Madhukantha).
[^31]: Vasudeva Datta, Mukunda's brother (GGD 140: Madhuvrata); cf. CC Madhya 15 on his compassion for all souls.
[^32]: Five brothers: Damodara Pandita (GGD 159: Shaibya), Pitambara (GGD 168: Kaveri), Jagannatha, Shankara Pandita (GGD 157: Bhadra) and Narayana. In Devakinandana (Part 2, vv. 27–28) Pitambara is Damodara's elder brother, and all five are "great renunciants".
[^33]: GGD 104: Nilambara foretold Gaura's birth and is therefore called Garga. On the newborn's horoscope — CC Adi 13–14.
[^34]: Sri Rama Pandita, Srivasa's younger brother (GGD 90: the sage Parvata).
[^35]: The reading is unclear: "Sri Gupta Narayana"; Devakinandana (Part 2, v. 30) has "Gupta Narayana", the *Vaishnavabhidhana* (Part 3, v. 12) simply Narayana after Murari Gupta. Possibly Narayana Pandita (CC Adi 10).
[^36]: Young Nimai's teachers: Gangadasa (GGD 53, together with Sudarshana — Vasishtha), Vishnu (Pandita) and Sudarshana (CB Adi).
[^37]: That these six were brahmacharis is reported only by the vandanas (Majumdar too points this out). Sadashiva is Sadashiva Pandita (in whose house at Navadvipa Nityananda lived); Vidyanidhi is apparently Pundarika Vidyanidhi; Shrigarbha and Shrinidhi — GGD 103 (the nine "nidhis"); Buddhimanta Khan, according to CB Adi 15, was a wealthy townsman who bore the cost of the Lord's marriage to Vishnupriya; Shuklambara — GGD 191.
[^38]: Ramadasa and Kavichandra — in Devakinandana (Part 2, v. 33) "Ramadasa Kavichandra"; in the *Vaishnavabhidhana* (Part 3, v. 15) two persons. Kavichandra — GGD 171 (Manohara).
[^39]: Vijaya Dasa, the copyist of books, to whom the Lord gave the name Ratnabahu (CC Adi 10).
[^40]: Ratneshvara Acharya is unknown from other sources; at this place Devakinandana has Nandana Acharya (Part 2, v. 33; Majumdar notes this too).
[^41]: Shridhara Kholaveca (GGD 133).
[^42]: The poor brahmana Vanamali, who came with his son and saw Krishna in Gaura (GGD 114: the brahmana Sudama). Not to be confused with Vanamali Acharya (v. 55).
[^43]: Halayudha — GGD 134 (Balarama's friend Prabala). Vasudeva — in Devakinandana (Part 2, v. 36) "Vasudeva Bhadra". Ishana — the servant in the Lord's household (CC Adi 10; CB).
