# Pashchima-vibhaga. Lahari 9. The Taking of Refuge by the Subahdar Ahmed Beg and the Gajapati Nrisimhadeva

*A song in glory of Shyamananda; Rasika sets forth the truth to the subahdar Ahmed Beg with the evidence of the scriptures, and he, falling to the ground, recognizes him as a portion of Narayana; seeing the subahdar's humility, the rajas take shelter of Rasika, and in Banapur a multitude of people become disciples; Raja Harinarayana, the lord of Panchati, persuades the Gajapati Nrisimhadeva to see Rasika; Rasika himself comes to the Gajapati, the king prostrates himself before him and asks him to play the flute, and at the sound of the flute the king and the rajas swoon; declining to sit beside the king, Rasika sets forth the essence of the shastras — service to sadhus, the punishment of those who persecute sadhus, compassion for all beings — and asks the king as alms to give up the killing of living beings; the king gives it up, surrenders wholly to Rasika and honours his command like the command of Jagannatha; following him, the rajas take shelter of Rasika, and the subjects of Utkala become Vaishnavas*

*The ninth lahari.*

*Raga Varadi.*[^1]
*Ghosha: "O treasury of mercy, O compassionate Shyama!*
*Turn Your attention to the fallen, afflicted people."*

**1.** Glory, glory to Shyamananda, the deliverer of the fallen!
Be merciful, so that I may compose [a song of your] glory.[^2]

**2.** Thus [Rasika] set forth to the subahdar Ahmed Beg
the whole truth with the evidence of the scriptures.[^3]

**3.** Hearing the nectarean words from Rasika's mouth,
the subahdar fell to the ground in bliss.

**4.** He offered much praise with humble words;
in his mind he firmly understood [him] to be a portion of Narayana.[^4]

**5.** Seeing the subahdar's humility and devotion,
the rajas all took shelter at Rasika's feet.

**6.** A multitude [of people] became disciples in the settlement of Banapur;
the rajas and subjects became intoxicated with prema-bhakti.

**7.** Seeing the manifestation [of power], the rajas became disciples;
Raja Harinarayana came to see [him].[^5]

**8.** [He is] the lord of Panchati, greatly blessed by fortune;
all the rajas praise him.[^6]

**9.** Seeing Rasika's form, the raja was enchanted;
with his whole being he honoured [his] holy feet.

**10.** Harinarayana says to the Gajapati:
"Behold Rasika's feet!

**11.** [He is] a partial incarnation, [come] to destroy the horrors of Kali;
by Utkala's good fortune Rasika has appeared.[^7]

**12.** Quickly behold [his] lotus feet:
by seeing [them] sins are destroyed — [this is] the highest good."

**13.** Harinarayana knows Rasika's greatness;
in many ways he told the Gajapati [of him].[^8]

**14.** Hearing this, Nrisimhadeva, glad at heart,
resolved to go and see [his] feet.[^9]

**15.** Rasikendra, the indwelling lord, who knows all people,
himself went to the Gajapati and granted [him] his sight.[^10]

**16.** A sweet form, a gait like that of a king of elephants,
a gentle, most sweet smile, a beautiful moonlike face;

**17.** clad in fine garments, [with] a fine wrap;
in the hand of the Lord of [our] life — a flute that enchants the world.[^11]

**18.** All the singers and musicians who [were] with him
[and] the companions go along [with him], crowd after crowd.[^12]

**19.** Prabhu entered the Gajapati's presence;
the raja saw [him] as if he were a second Narayana.[^13]

**20.** Agitated, the raja quickly rose
and right [before him] fell prostrate like a rod.[^14]

**21.** That raja is the adhikari of Sri Jagannatha;
in many ways he honoured Rasikendrachandra.[^15]

**22.** The raja was very happy with Rasikendra;
standing, he gazes [at him] with eyes full of tears.

**23.** With joined palms the raja [says] to Rasika:
"Play the flute — hearing it, I will soothe my ears."[^16]

**24.** Hearing this, Rasikachandra, glad at heart —
Prabhu played the flute with his sweet lips.

**25.** The three gramas, the seven svaras, the murchhanas and the rest,
the six ragas that are renowned — [all] he played.[^17]

**26.** Hearing it, the raja swooned and lost consciousness;
all the rajas, as many as there were, swooned.[^18]

**27.** All say: "Such a flute we have never heard anywhere!
Krishna Himself, the Lord of Vrindavana, has come!"

**28.** As we hear in the Puranas of that sweetness of sound,
such is the flute we hear [from] Rasika-Murari.[^19]

**29.** Hearing the flute, all were astonished;
motionless, all listened on.

**30.** Not a word comes from the mouth of anyone in the assembly;
their eyes roll, tears flow unceasingly.[^20]

**31.** Hearing the flute, all were immersed in bliss;
having listened a while, the Gajapati makes a request:

**32.** "Sit on the seat, O Rasika-shekhara!"
Hearing this, the king of music replied:[^21]

**33.** "It is not fitting to sit beside the Gajapati;
such is the custom at court, [handed down] by succession."[^22]

**34.** Having said this, Rasika-chand, on the pretext of these words,
poured forth the nectar of talks on the rasa of Krishna.[^23]

**35.** The six shastras, the eighteen Puranas and the rest, as many as there are,
shruti, smriti, the four Vedas and the tantras — [of all] he spoke.[^24]

**36.** He speaks of the dharma of firm devotion to Krishna according to all the shastras;
[as] the essence of the shastras he speaks of service to sadhus with a firm heart.[^25]

**37.** "The gods, the holy places and the rest bring deliverance after a long time;
by the favour of sadhus — the highest good [at once].[^26]

**38.** The guru, Krishna and the sadhus are one and equal;
the heart of the guru and the sadhu is Krishna's own abode.[^27]

**39.** Sadhus should be honoured more than Krishna;
when sadhus are served, Krishna rejoices in His heart.[^28]

**40.** Narayana always wanders in the company of sadhus;
the dust of the sadhus' feet He makes an ornament for His body.[^29]

**41.** Whoever, turned away from Krishna, reviles sadhus —
to him Krishna gives great suffering birth after birth.

**42.** Kamsa, Keshi, Hiranyakashipu, Duryodhana,
Durvasa and others, Kumbhakarna, King Ravana,

**43.** Dantavakra, Shishupala, Naraka and the rest, as many as there were —
reviling sadhus, all lost their lives.[^30]

**44.** Devaki, Yashoda, Nanda, Vasu[deva], the line of Yadu —
persecuting them, Kamsa perished.[^31]

**45.** Keshi, Agha, Baka, Trina[varta], Putana and the rest, as many as there were —
persecuting the Vrajavasis, they lost their lives.[^32]

**46.** King Hiranya, who persecuted Prahlada,
was destroyed by the Husband of Kamala.

**47.** Together with [Hiranya]kashipu, all his wicked followers,
persecuting the sadhu, parted with their lives.

**48.** The five Pandava brothers [were] under Krishna's shelter;
persecuting them, Duryodhana perished.

**49.** Bhishma, Drona, Karna, Ashvatthama, Duhshasana,
the ninety-nine brothers perished through enmity toward the Pandavas.[^33]

**50.** Durvasa, best of rishis, having assailed Ambarisha —
Sudarshana caused him great suffering.[^34]

**51.** Having offended at Sita's feet, Ravana
perished with all his line, and Kumbhakarna too.

**52.** Dantavakra, Shalva, Shishu[pala] showed enmity [to Him];
Naraka and the rest the god Narayana destroyed.[^35]

**53.** Thus whoever persecutes sadhus,
Narayana destroys him together with all his line.

**54.** The Life of the world bears reviling of Himself,
[but] the Beloved of Kamala does not bear reviling of sadhus.[^36]

**55.** Such service to sadhus, O raja, perform with firmness —
then [even] the dread at life's end will be destroyed.[^37]

**56.** Associating with sadhus, worship the Son of Nanda —
you will surely attain Krishna, the jewel among persons.[^38]

**57.** Constantly serve brahmanas [and] Vaishnavas;
be compassionate to all living beings — listen, O best of kings!

**58.** In all beings dwells Krishna, the one Bhagavan;
with your whole being worship the Treasury of compassion.[^39]

**59.** Worship Krishna's feet with firm feeling in your heart;
know that all is false — the Son of Nanda alone is true."[^40]

**60.** [Thus] Rasika-shekhara explained the six shastras;
hearing, the raja was overwhelmed in every limb with love for Krishna.[^41]

**61.** Rasikendra begged as alms [the giving up of] violence to living beings;
hearing [this], the raja rejoiced in his mind.[^42]

**62.** From then on he no longer killed living beings;
apart from Jagannatha there was nothing else in his mind.

**63.** He gained great happiness from seeing Rasika;
he heard all [the teaching] from Rasika.

**64.** Giving up everything, he firmly gave his mind to Krishna;
seeing Rasika, he became one wholly surrendered.[^43]

**65.** The king gave up the killing of living beings and all the rest;
apart from guru, Krishna [and] the sadhus he has no other goal.

**66.** The maharaja became compassionate to all living beings;
he worshipped Rasika like Narayana.

**67.** Great faith arose [in him] toward Rasikendra;
the king honoured [his] command like [the command] of Jagannatha.[^44]

**68.** Seeing the gajapati's devotion, all the rajas
took refuge at Rasika's feet.

**69.** Seeing the [power] manifested by Rasika, the rajas
recognized Rasika as a second portion of Narayana.[^45]

**70.** Seeing on earth [this] wondrous manifestation —
Rasika gave prema to the maddened Gopala Dasa —[^46]

**71.** [seeing him] whose command even the yavana carries out at the risk of his life,
whose command the gajapati [and] the rajas carry out,[^47]

**72.** all began to serve the sadhus;
taking up beads and tilaka, all were adorned [with them].[^48]

**73.** Countless rajas [and] subjects became Vaishnavas;
giving up everything, all took refuge in Krishna.[^49]

**74.** Rasika's greatness is beyond the reach [even] of Indra and the other gods;
there is nothing to compare with it in the world.[^50]

**75.** Of him by whose command the raja serves the sadhus
I have made known a little.[^51]

**76.** The abode of Utkala was flooded with prema;
the Treasury of compassion showed mercy to all beings.

**77.** Rasika-shekhara — boundless greatness [and] virtues;
bestowing prema-bhakti, he overwhelmed everyone.

**78.** Even speaking with a hundred mouths one does not reach the limit of [his] qualities;
by his grace I have composed a little.

**79.** "Rasika-mangala" is supremely full of rasa:
hearing it, all beings, cross [the ocean of] the age of Kali![^52]

**80.** Adorning himself with Shyamananda's feet,
the son of Rasamaya joyfully composed [this].

*Thus ends the ninth lahari in the Pashchima-vibhaga of "Sri Rasika-mangala," called "The Taking of Refuge by the Subahdar Ahmed Beg and the Gajapati Nrisimhadeva."*[^53]

[^2]: "So that I may compose [a song of your] glory" — *yaśaḥ yena kariẏe racana*.
[^3]: "Thus" — with Rasika's speech (lahari 8, 80–102). Ahmed Beg — here *ahammadabega*; the subahdar of Banapur (lahari 6, 17; lahari 7, 27). "With the evidence of the scriptures" — *nigama-pramāṇe*.
[^4]: "A portion of Narayana" — *nārāyaṇa-aṃśa*; likewise "a partial incarnation" (11), "as if a second Narayana" (19), "the indwelling lord" (15) — rapturous words of the author and his characters. According to Gaudiya Vaishnava teaching, Rasika is a devotee, a disciple of Shyamananda, not an incarnation (Pashchima 2, note 28; Pashchima 7, note 36; Pashchima 8, note 29); on how the author exalts Shyamananda and Rasika, see Purva, lahari 1, note 2; Purva, lahari 14, note 11. The subahdar's submission — cf. BR 15, 85: "That villain, the yavana king, bowed down [before him]."
[^5]: "The manifestation [of power]" — *prakāśa* (lahari 8, 66, 71; note 39 there): the taming of the elephant. Raja Harinarayana (*harinārāẏaṇa*; in 10, *hari nārāẏaṇa*) is unidentified.
[^6]: Panchati — *pañcaṭī* (so in the scan); the domain is unidentified. "Blessed by fortune" — *bhāgyabān*. "Praise" — *bākhāna*.
[^7]: Gajapati ("lord of elephants") is the title of the kings of Orissa; here Nrisimhadeva (14), apparently Narasimhadeva of the Bhoi dynasty, who ruled from Khurda in the first half of the 17th century (the identification is tentative). BR 15 says nothing of Rasikananda meeting the Gajapati; the author exalts Rasika with an account of the king's reverence for him (cf. note 4). "The horrors of Kali" — *kalighora*. "Has appeared" — *haila pracāra*, "became known, manifested himself".
[^8]: "Knows" — *jāne*: Harinarayana apparently already knew Rasika's greatness.
[^9]: Nrisimhadeva — *nṛsiṃhadeba* (cf. note 7).
[^10]: "The indwelling lord" — *antaryāmī*; "who knows all people" — *jñāta sarbajana*: Rasika, knowing the king's intention, goes to him himself (cf. note 4).
[^11]: "A gait like that of a king of elephants" (16) — *gajendra-gamana*, a traditional comparison for a stately gait. "Fine" — *jhina* (*jhīna*). "Wrap" — *ācchādana*. "The Lord of [our] life" — *prāṇapati*: here apparently Rasika (cf. 23–24, where he plays the flute).
[^12]: "Singers and musicians" — *saṅgīta-sāhitya* (lahari 6, note 19; lahari 7, note 31).
[^13]: "Second" — *dbitī* (*dvitīya*).
[^14]: "Agitated" — *āsta byasta*, "hurriedly, in agitation". "Like a rod" — *daṇḍabata*: stretched full length.
[^15]: "Adhikari" — *adhikārī*, "one who holds the right [of service], the superintendent": the Gajapati is revered as the foremost servant of Jagannatha of Puri.
[^16]: "Play the flute" — *muralī bājāo*. "Soothe my ears" — *juṛāi śrabaṇe*, lit. "cool [my] ears".
[^17]: Gramas (*grāma*), svaras (*sbara*), murchhanas (*mūrcchanā*) are terms of Indian music theory: the basic scales, the seven degrees of the scale, and modal sequences. "The six ragas" — the six principal ragas. Rasika's flute-playing is not in BR 15 (cf. note 4).
[^18]: "Swooned" — *mūrcchita*: an echo of *mūrcchanā* (25).
[^19]: "In the Puranas" — on Krishna's flute in the Bhagavata (10.21; 10.35).
[^20]: "Not a word comes from the mouth" — *bāṇī nāhi sare*. "Roll" — *ghūrṇita*, "whirl".
[^21]: "The king of music" — *saṅgīta rāẏa*: apparently an epithet of Rasika (cf. 24–25); it may also be understood as a name (Sangita-raya) — unclear.
[^22]: "By succession" — *paramparā*. Out of humility Rasika does not sit beside the king.
[^23]: "On the pretext of these words" — *ei kathā chale*. "Poured forth" — *udgāre*.
[^24]: "The six shastras" — *ṣaṛ-śāstra*: the six philosophical systems (darshanas). "The four Vedas and the tantras" — *cāribeda-tantra*.
[^25]: "Firm devotion to Krishna" — *kṛṣṇaniṣṭhā*. "Service to sadhus" — in the scan *sādhuselā*, apparently a misprint for *sādhusebā*. 37–58 are apparently Rasika's words (note the address "O raja" in 55, 57).
[^26]: Cf. Bhagavata 10.48.31 (*na hy am-mayāni tīrthāni na devā mṛc-chilā-mayāḥ / te punanty uru-kālena darśanād eva sādhavaḥ*). "Bring deliverance" — *uddhāraẏe*; "after a long time" — *cirakāle*. "By the favour" — *prasannete*.
[^27]: Cf. Bhagavata 9.4.68 (*sādhavo hṛdayaṃ mahyaṃ sādhūnāṃ hṛdayaṃ tv aham*).
[^28]: Cf. Padma Purana (*ārādhanānāṃ sarveṣāṃ viṣṇor ārādhanaṃ param / tasmāt parataraṃ devi tadīyānāṃ samarcanam*; CC Madhya 11) and Bhagavata 11.19.21 (*mad-bhakta-pūjābhyadhikā*).
[^29]: Cf. Bhagavata 11.14.16 (*anuvrajāmy ahaṃ nityaṃ pūyeyety aṅghri-reṇubhiḥ*).
[^30]: 42–53 list the enemies of the sadhus and of the Lord from the Puranas and itihasas; "lost their lives" — *prāṇe haila hata*, "were struck down". Durvasa (42, 50) is not an asura but a great sage who offended King Ambarisha (note 34).
[^31]: "Vasu[deva]" — *basu*. "Persecuting" — *hiṃsana kari'* (likewise 45–53: *hiṃsā*). "Perished" — *kṣaẏa haila*.
[^32]: Keshi, Agha, Baka, Trinavarta (*tṛṇā*), Putana — asuras sent by Kamsa to Vraja (Bhagavata, Canto 10).
[^33]: "The ninety-nine brothers" — *ūnaśata bhāi*: the sons of Dhritarashtra, Duryodhana's brothers. Bhishma is revered as a devotee (one of the twelve mahajanas), and Ashvatthama, according to the Mahabharata, survived (cursed by Krishna); the author lists them among those who perished in the war against the Pandavas.
[^34]: Bhagavata 9.4–5: the Sudarshana disc pursued Durvasa until he begged Ambarisha's forgiveness.
[^35]: Shalva, Shishupala (*śiśu*), Dantavakra — enemies of Krishna (Bhagavata 10.74–78); Naraka is Narakasura (Bhagavata 10.59).
[^36]: "The Life of the world" — *jagata-jībana*; "the Beloved of Kamala" — *kamalā-ramaṇa*, the Husband of Lakshmi (likewise 46, "the Husband of Kamala").
[^37]: "The dread at life's end" — *maraṇe udbege* (in the scan *marane*). "Will be destroyed" — *dhbaṃsana habe*.
[^38]: "The jewel among persons" — *puruṣaratana*.
[^39]: "With your whole being" — *sarbātmabhābete* (likewise 9). pdf 153 ends with a complete ॥ 58 ॥; Rasika's instruction continues in 59 (pdf 154).
[^40]: The portion begins on pdf 154 (printed p. 114); pdf 153 ends with a complete ॥ 58 ॥. 59 is the end of Rasika's instruction to the gajapati (begun at 37). "Son of Nanda" — *nandasuta*. "All is false" — *saba mithyā*: everything other than Krishna is transient.
[^41]: "Six shastras" — *ṣaṛ śāstra* (cf. 35, note 24). "Overwhelmed" — *jara jara*, "broken, overcome" (likewise 77).
[^42]: "Begged as alms" — *bhikṣā māgilena*: as a Vaishnava, Rasika asks the king not for gifts but for the renunciation of *jībahiṃsā* — the killing of animals (hunting, sacrifices). This vow of the gajapati and his meeting with Rasika are not mentioned in BR 15 (note 7); the author exalts Rasika with an account of the king's submission to him.
[^43]: "Wholly surrendered" — *ananyaśaraṇa*, "having no other refuge" (likewise 73: "took refuge").
[^44]: "Like Narayana," "like [the command] of Jagannatha" — *nārāẏaṇa sama*, *jagannātha sama*: the author's rapturous words on the king's reverence for Rasika (note 4).
[^45]: "[Power] manifested" — *prakāśa* (note 5). "A second portion of Narayana" — *dbitīẏāṃśa nārāẏaṇa*; cf. 19 ("as though a second Narayana," note 13). The rajas' rapturous acclamation; according to Gaudiya Vaishnava teaching Rasika is a devotee, a disciple of Shyamananda (note 4; Purva, lahari 1, note 2; Purva, lahari 14, note 11).
[^46]: "Manifestation" — *parakāśa* (= *prakāśa*). Gopala Dasa is the elephant initiated by Rasika (lahari 8, note 34). "Maddened" — *matta*, "in musth," the elephant's standing epithet (lahari 8); here the word can also mean "intoxicated [with prema]". 70–72 form one sentence.
[^47]: "The yavana" — the subahdar Ahmed Beg (lahari 8, 73–103). "At the risk of his life" — *prāṇapaṇe*, "with all his might". The second line can also be taken as "whose command the gajapati [has] the rajas carry out" (*kare rājāgaṇe*).
[^48]: "Beads and tilaka" — *mālā se tilaka*: tulasi beads and the Vaishnava tilaka on the forehead, the marks of a Vaishnava. "Were adorned" — *haila śobhā*.
[^49]: "Countless" — *ananta* (so the scan; garbled in the OCR).
[^50]: "Beyond the reach" — *agocara*; "Indra and the other gods" — *debendrādi*.
[^51]: "Him by whose command" — Rasika; "the raja" — the gajapati. "Made known" — *karilũ pracāre*.
[^52]: "Full of rasa" — *rasāla*, "juicy, delightful". "Cross" — *tara*: imperative; it can also be taken as "cross" in the indicative.
