# Dakshina-vibhaga. Lahari 3. The Manifestation of Sri Gopivallabhapur

*A song in glory of Shyamananda; by Shyamananda's mercy prema grows in Rasika's heart; he cares nothing for the household, serves the Vaishnavas and guests, drinks the water from their feet and partakes of their remnants without regard to caste; after Achyuta's departure the brothers quarrel, and the relatives revile Rasika and blaspheme the Vaishnavas; unable to bear this, Rasika, together with Shyamadasi, who resolves to follow him, leaves home and on the bank of the Suvarnarekha finds a beautiful hidden village — "the hidden Vrindavana"; he settles there, at Kashipur, taking with him only the family Deity, whom a raja of the Bhanja line had earlier taken by force, and serves the Vaishnavas; Shyamananda comes, and Rasika and Shyamadasi serve him; Shyamananda names the Deity Gopivallabha-raya and the village Sri Gopivallabhapur, foretells the manifestation of Govinda there, and makes Shyamadasi the overseer of the service*

*The third lahari.*

*Raga Karuna-shri.*[^1]
*Ghosha: "Where shall I go to find Shyama, my life?"*

**1.** Glory, glory to Shyamananda, the life of all [the world]!
Be merciful to me: [let] me sing [your] virtues every moment.

**2.** Thus day by day the jubilation of prema
became manifest in Rasika's heart.

**3.** By Shyamananda's mercy prema-bhakti arose [in him];
apart from Krishna, Rasika knows neither day nor night.

**4.** From [his time in] the womb Rasika meditated on prema-bhakti;
then he found [its] confirmation in the Vedas, the shastras [and] the Puranas.[^2]

**5.** Then, hearing the truth from the guru's lips,
night and day he became mad with love for Krishna.[^3]

**6.** The sixty-four angas of bhakti, confirmed by the shastras —
all of them constantly dwell in Rasika's heart.[^4]

**7.** For household affairs he takes no care at all;
for this reason all the household suffer.

**8.** Moreover, after Achyuta's departure to Vaikuntha,
brother against brother — each against each — they quarrel.[^5]

**9.** But Rasika [stays] constantly in the company of sadhus,
constantly speaking of Krishna with joy.

**10.** Apart from Krishna's affairs he knows nothing else;
whatever is found in the house — with [that] he feeds guests.[^6]

**11.** If there is [nothing] in the house, he begs alms himself;
night and day Rasika serves guests.

**12.** He feeds the Vaishnavas [dishes] of six tastes;
he worships the sadhus as equal to Krishna.[^7]

**13.** Every day he drinks the water [that has washed] the sadhus' feet,
every day with devotion he partakes of [their] remnants.[^8]

**14.** He clears away the leaf-plates himself, with his own hands;
he gives no thought to caste — a mala on the neck is enough.

**15.** Whatever caste [the Vaishnava] may be, he does not consider;
Thakurani [and] Rasika take his remnants.[^9]

**16.** After everyone, husband and wife take their meal;
seeing such signs, the household burn with anger.

**17.** All say: "This son has fallen;
because of him disgrace will come upon the family.

**18.** Whose son he is — and what he does!
He will shame [us] in the society of our kin."

**19.** Thus all revile Rasika;
some to his face, some behind his back — they speak constantly.

**20.** All those words he does not value even as a straw:
the son of Achyuta does twice as much.

**21.** He instructs everyone, [citing] the views of various shastras,
[that] serving sadhus is serving Krishna, in many ways.

**22.** Hearing these words, some gain a pure mind;
[but] among them a few are evil-minded pashandas.[^10]

**23.** Seeing this conduct, they constantly burn [with spite];
on various pretexts, in various ways they speak harsh words.

**24.** Hearing words blaspheming the sadhus, Rasika —
Prabhu — cannot bear [it], becoming filled with anger.[^11]

**25.** First he deliberated with Thakurani:
"The reproaches of the kinsmen cannot be borne.

**26.** However much they say about me [and] about you — I can bear it;
blasphemy of the sadhus I cannot bear.

**27.** The Vaishnava thakura is greater to me than Krishna;
because of me he is abundantly reviled.[^12]

**28.** I cannot serve the sadhus without fear;
why then should I waste time in vain at home like this?

**29.** You are my faithful wife, very dear;
if you want [to be with] me — come along yourself.[^13]

**30.** I shall certainly not stay with all of them;
if not — you stay, and I shall live freely in joy."[^14]

**31.** Hearing these words, Shyamadasi Thakurani [says]:
"Wherever you go, there I too shall go with you.

**32.** What happiness would I have staying here without you?
With you, even at the foot of a tree there is beauty.

**33.** With you, even a grove I shall consider good;
be sure to take me with you, and soon.[^15]

**34.** Wealth without you — may lightning strike it!
Without you, what have I to do in this house?"[^16]

**35.** Hearing these words from Thakurani,
he set out, looking for a lovely place.

**36.** He roams, looking at both banks of the Suvarnarekha;
eagerly he saw a delightful place.

**37.** He saw a beautiful, captivating place —
is it not Vrindavana he sees before him?[^17]

**38.** The bank of the Suvarnarekha is very beautiful;
on all four sides groves of mango [and] jackfruit shine.

**39.** Beautiful is the sandy bank, the river is lovely to see;
its water is like [the water] of the Yamuna — I see [it] fragrant.[^18]

**40.** The place is so soft that it cannot be described;
however much it rains, there is no mud.

**41.** In the pargana of Mallabhumi is the tappa of Chorchita;
within it is Nuyabasan, a very beautiful [land].[^19]

**42.** Near it lies this captivating village;
it had been hidden, visible to no one.

**43.** That place is devoutly worshipped by Indra and [the other gods];
a place equal to Vaikuntha, [its] soil is chintamani.[^20]

**44.** On all four sides I see forests, fragrant;
fresh, dense kunjas, beautiful to see.

**45.** Various trees shine with various blossoms, fruits [and] flowers;
always [they] abide in the village — within and without.[^21]

**46.** The beauty of that village cannot be described at all;
all people sing of [it], calling it "the hidden Vrindavana."

**47.** Rasikendra-chandra made his abode there;
even with a hundred mouths his virtues cannot be told.

**48.** I shall tell how it came about — listen, everyone:
how Rasika went there.

**49.** Rasika's elder brother, Kashinatha Dasa,
made known [the village] by the name Kashipur.[^22]

**50.** By providence the lord of the land, of his own wish,
made his abode in the village of Kashipur.[^23]

**51.** Seeing that village, Rasika, joyful at heart,
went there together with his family.

**52.** From long ago [the family] had had an ancestral Deity;
the Bhanja raja took Him away by force.

**53.** [Rasika] himself went there and brought the Deity;
binding Him to his breast, Rasika set out.[^24]

**54.** He had great wealth, equal to Kubera's —
[yet] he took nothing of it, not so much as a sesame seed.

**55.** Husband and wife, the two of them, and with them the Deity,
left home in only the clothes they wore.

**56.** In Kashipur Rasika-shekhara remained;
in the middle of the village he built a fine house.

**57.** With Rasika are the eight siddhis [and] nine nidhis;
wherever he stays, there they serve [him] — this is well known.[^25]

**58.** Here all the brothers grieve without Rasika;
the property was split up, [passing] from hand to hand.

**59.** Rasika Murari is the dear devotee of Lakshmikanta:
all the wealth went along with him to Kashipuri.[^26]

**60.** Thus Rasika-shekhara lives there;
hundreds upon hundreds of sadhus he serves constantly.

**61.** As his heart desires, he serves the Vaishnavas:
food [and] water, [dishes of] six tastes, clothes, ornaments.

**62.** With his own hands he washes the sadhus' feet;
he himself carries away the leaf-plates, [lifting them] onto his head.

**63.** Fearlessly, joyfully he partakes of [their] remnants,
casting far away all fear for [the honour of] his family, and shame.[^27]

**64.** Day by day Rasika became more renowned;
hearing [of him], all the servants of Krishna come there.[^28]

**65.** Thus Rasikendra lives [there] for some time;
after some time Shyamananda arrives.

**66.** Seeing [him], Rasika could not contain his joy;
with his body [outstretched] like a rod he fell on the ground at [his] feet.[^29]

**67.** Prabhu Shyamananda lifted [him] up and embraced [him];
joyfully he spoke of Krishna.

**68.** Day and night Rasika serves [his] two feet;
I have come to know for certain: Shyamananda is dear to Krishna.[^30]

**69.** He worships Prabhu more than Krishna;
Rasika himself serves [him] constantly.[^31]

**70.** Shyamadasi Thakurani cooks herself —
Rasika's wife, descended as a portion of Lakshmi.[^32]

**71.** She cooks all the dishes like nectar;
with [dishes of] six tastes she feeds Shyamananda.

**72.** Afterwards the two of them accept the remnants;
apart from serving Shyamananda they know nothing else.

**73.** In body, mind and speech [they] take shelter of Shyamananda;
without guile the two serve constantly.

**74.** Whatever command Shyamananda gives, pleased at heart —
[Rasika], staking his very life, carries it out without delay.

**75.** When Shyamananda speaks an inviolable word,
Rasikendra-chandra unfailingly carries it out.[^33]

**76.** In Shyamananda's presence he has no awareness of the body;
constantly he serves [his] holy feet.

**77.** Lying down, dreaming, falling asleep [or] awake —
constantly he meditates on Shyamananda.

**78.** Apart from Shyamananda he has no other refuge;
Rasika always worships [him] with a pure mind.

**79.** With his whole being — at Shyamananda's feet
he has sold himself together with his whole family, and knows nothing else.[^34]

**80.** Such a devotee of the guru there has never been, nor will be —
[save] as formerly Krishna [and] Baladeva served [Their] guru.[^35]

**81.** Thus the guru, Krishna [and] the Vaishnavas
he worships as non-different, firm of mind.[^36]

**82.** Great mercy [shows] Shyamananda-raya to Rasika:
wherever he goes, he takes [him] there and wanders with him.

**83.** One day Rasikendra, in Shyamananda's presence,
told [him] about the Sri Murti in [his] house:

**84.** "The Sri Murti has been in [our] house since long ago;
command [us] His name — whatever comes to your heart."[^37]

**85.** Hearing [this], Shyamananda speaks in sweet words:
"Everyone will call [Him] Gopivallabha-raya.

**86.** The name of this village is Sri Gopivallabhapur;
here the service of the sadhus [and] of Krishna will be abundant.

**87.** There will be great joy in this village;
it will become renowned as equal to Vrindavana.

**88.** Of the glory of this village I cannot tell even a little:
here Govinda Himself will become manifest.

**89.** The form in which I behold [Him] in meditation —
that very form all will see before their eyes.

**90.** After some time Krishna in such a form, suddenly —
Govinda — will become manifest in this village.[^38]

**91.** The overseer of this village is Mother Shyamadasi."
From then on he appointed her to the service.[^39]

**92.** "Rasika is detached: he, with me,
will constantly wander to deliver the living beings."[^40]

**93.** Sri Gopivallabhapur, the service of the sadhus [and] the service of Krishna
he entrusted to Shyamadasi.

**94.** From that day the service grows day by day;
by [his] command the place came to shine brilliantly.[^41]

**95.** Shyamananda [and] Rasikendra entered Utkala;
from then on prema-bhakti increases especially.

**96.** Even with a hundred mouths it cannot be told;
who has the power to fathom that bhakti?

**97.** Only a little have I briefly proclaimed —
what the son of Achyuta told me.[^42]

**98.** Learned and good people, do not count this as a fault;
and do not take to heart any faults in the order [of events].[^43]

**99.** Listen to the Rasika-mangala, all friends:
without delay you will obtain the treasure of prema-bhakti for Krishna.

**100.** Adorning himself with Shyamananda's feet,
the son of Rasamaya joyfully composed [this].

*Thus ends the third lahari in the Dakshina-vibhaga of the Sri Rasika-mangala, called "The Manifestation of Sri Gopivallabhapur."*[^44]

[^1]: The lines before the raga are the lahari's heading ("তৃতীয়-লহরী"), in italics without a number. Lahari 3 begins on the lower half of pdf 95, after the colophon of lahari 2. Raga Karuna-shri — as in laharis 1 and 2 of the Dakshina-vibhaga (Dakshina, lahari 1, note 1). The ghosha (refrain) and couplet 1 are the same as in lahari 2 (notes 1, 2), and their translation is repeated; but in the ghosha there it has "Shyamananda," here "Shyama" (*śyāma*): apparently Shyamananda in short; it can also be taken as a name of Krishna. There is no mangala-shloka: after the raga and ghosha the song begins with ॥ 1 ॥. The title follows the colophon on pdf 100 (checked against the scan): "ইতি শ্রীরসিকমঙ্গল দক্ষিণ-বিভাগে শ্রীগোপীবল্লভপুর-প্রকাশ-নাম তৃতীয়লহরী সম্পূর্ণা" (*śrīgopīvallabhapura-prakāśa*). Gopivallabhapur is a village on the Suvarnarekha (now Jhargram district, West Bengal), the chief place of Rasikananda's service (BR 15, 81).
[^2]: "From [his time in] the womb" — *garbha haite*, i.e. from birth (cf. Purva, lahari 4). "Confirmation" — *pramāṇa*, proof from the shastras.
[^3]: The guru is Shyamananda (Dakshina, lahari 1, the initiation). "Mad" — *unamattā*: a feminine form, apparently for the rhyme.
[^4]: The sixty-four angas (limbs) of bhakti are listed in Rupa Gosvami's Bhakti-rasamrita-sindhu (1.2) and in CC, Madhya 22.
[^5]: Achyuta is Rasika's father (Purva, lahari 3, note 5). "Departure to Vaikuntha" — *baikuṇṭha-gamana*: so the author speaks of Achyuta's departure from this world. "Brother against brother" — *bhāi bhāi*: apparently Rasika's brothers are dividing the inheritance (cf. pdf 99, 58: "the property was split up").
[^6]: "Krishna's affairs" — *kṛṣṇa-byabahāra*, in contrast to "household affairs" (*gṛha byabahāra*, 7).
[^7]: "Of six tastes" — *ṣaṛ-rasa*: sweet, sour, salty, bitter, pungent and astringent, i.e. a full, refined meal.
[^8]: "Remnants" — *abaśeṣa*, the remnants of the sadhus' meal. Drinking the water that has washed the devotees' feet and partaking of their remnants are traditional forms of honouring Vaishnavas (cf. CC, Antya 16: *bhakta-pada-dhūli āra bhakta-pada-jala, bhakta-bhukta-avaśeṣa*).
[^9]: "Leaf-plates" — *patrābali*: after the meal Rasika himself clears away the leaves from which the Vaishnavas ate. "A mala on the neck is enough" — *mālā mātra gale*: he counts as a Vaishnava anyone who wears beads (a tulasi-mala) on the neck, of whatever caste.
[^10]: Pashandas (*pāṣaṇḍa*) — the godless, hostile to Vaishnavas. "Evil-minded" — *durmati*; the edition gives in a footnote the variant *durgati*, "wretched."
[^11]: Prabhu here is Rasika (cf. lahari 2, note 6); in lahari 1 Shyamananda is so called (Dakshina, lahari 1, note 3).
[^12]: Cf. Shiva's words in the Padma Purana (CC, Madhya 11.31): of all kinds of worship the highest is the worship of Vishnu, but higher still is honouring His devotees. "Thakura" — the respectful "master." "He" — *tā̃ra* (respectful), i.e. the Vaishnava.
[^13]: "Very dear" — *priyasvinī*: so in the scan and in the 1997 ed.; an unusual form (apparently from *priyā*, "dear").
[^14]: "I shall live freely" — *kheliba*, lit. "I shall play, frolic," i.e. live free of household cares.
[^15]: "A grove" — *upabana*, i.e. even life in the forest. "Soon" — *sakāla*, "early, without delay."
[^16]: "May lightning strike it" — *tāhe paṛu bāja*, a curse.
[^17]: The Suvarnarekha is the river on which Gopivallabhapur stands (note 1). The place he finds is the future Gopivallabhapur: the village of Kashipur (pdf 99, 49–50), which Shyamananda will name Sri Gopivallabhapur (pdf 100, 85–86); in 46 (pdf 99) people call it "the hidden Vrindavana." "Is it not Vrindavana" — *kibā bṛndābana hena*, "as if Vrindavana."
[^18]: "Sandy bank" — *pulina*: the word also used for the banks of the Yamuna in Vraja, the place of Krishna's pastimes. "Fragrant" — *parimala*, "fragrance": so in the scan and in the 1997 ed.; the rhyme with *sundara* is loose (cf. 44, where *parimala* rhymes with *sundara* in the same way), so the reading *nirmala*, "pure," is not assumed. The comparison with the Yamuna continues the thought of 37 and 46 ("the hidden Vrindavana").
[^19]: A pargana (*paragaṇā*) is a revenue district; a tappa (*tapā*, *tappā*) is a smaller unit within it. Mallabhumi is the land of Rasika's native village (Purva, lahari 3, note 6). "The tappa of Chorchita" — *coracitā tapā* (crop ×2; written as one word "চোরচিতাতপা"); Nuyabasan — *nuyābasāna*. Apparently these are the present villages of Chorchita and Nayabasan near Gopivallabhapur on the Suvarnarekha (Jhargram district); the identification is provisional.
[^20]: Chintamani — "the wish-fulfilling gem": so the soil of the spiritual world is described (cf. Brahma-samhita 5.29: *cintāmaṇi-prakara-sadmasu*). "Indra and [the other gods]" — *debendrādi*.
[^21]: "Abide" — *thākena* (respectful form); the subject is not named: apparently the trees, blossoms and fruits (45a), cf. 44; it can also be taken of the gods of 43. "Without" — *bāhāre* (= *bāhire*).
[^22]: Kashinatha Dasa is Rasika's elder brother; nothing more is said of him here or in BR 15 (the Kashinatha in Purva, lahari 1, 129 — in the list of Vaishnavas to whom the author bows — is apparently another person). The village was apparently named after him.
[^23]: "The lord of the land" — *rājya-adhipati* (1997 ed.: *rājā adhipati*); apparently Kashinatha himself, who after Achyuta's departure became lord of his land (cf. note 5; Achyuta was earlier called *adhipati* — Purva, lahari 3, 54). "By providence" — *daive*, "by the will of Providence, by chance."
[^24]: The ancestral Deity — *baṃśābali ṭhākura*: later Shyamananda will name Him Gopivallabha-raya (pdf 100, 85). The Bhanja raja — apparently the raja of Mayurbhanj (cf. Purva, lahari 1, note 49). "Himself went there" — apparently to the raja; the author does not say how Rasika recovered the Deity. "Binding Him to his breast" — *hṛde bā̃dhi*.
[^25]: The eight siddhis are the mystic perfections (anima and others; cf. Dakshina, lahari 1, note 13); the nine nidhis are Kubera's treasures. The sense: though Rasika took nothing from home (54), wealth and power serve him of themselves. Cf. the Narada-pancharatra (quoted in Bhakti-rasamrita-sindhu 1.1): all the siddhis follow bhakti like maidservants. "Serve" — *khāṭena*, lit. "work, labour."
[^26]: Lakshmikanta — "the beloved of Lakshmi," i.e. Vishnu, Narayana. The sense: since Rasika is dear to Lakshmi's consort, wealth (Lakshmi) went after him, while at home the property was split up (58). Kashipuri is Kashipur.
[^27]: The leaf-plates and remnants — cf. 13–15 and notes 8–9 (here the remnants are *śeṣa*). "Fear for [the honour of] his family" — *kula-bhaya*: fear of losing caste standing, cf. the reproaches of the kinsmen in 17–18.
[^28]: "The servants of Krishna" — *kṛṣṇadāsa*, i.e. Vaishnavas (not a name). "Became more renowned" — *haila parakāśa*, "became manifest, famous."
[^29]: "With his body like a rod" — *daṇḍabata-kāya*: the dandavat obeisance, full prostration.
[^30]: "I have come to know" — *jāninu* (1st person): apparently the author's words; it can also be taken as Rasika's conviction. "Dear to Krishna" — *kṛṣṇa-priya* (masculine), ordinary praise of a devotee, without exalting Shyamananda as "the beloved of Krishna" (cf. Dakshina, lahari 1, notes 6 and 33; on the theological side — Purva, lahari 1, note 2, and Purva, lahari 14, note 11).
[^31]: "Prabhu" is Shyamananda. Honouring the guru above Krishna — cf. note 12 (the Vaishnava above Krishna). According to Gaudiya Vaishnava teaching the guru is honoured as Hari Himself because he is the Lord's dear servant (Vishvanatha Chakravarti's Gurv-ashtaka, 7: *sākṣād-dharitvena samasta-śāstrair… kintu prabhor yaḥ priya eva tasya*); Shyamananda is a devotee and servant of Radha and Krishna (Purva, lahari 1, note 2; Purva, lahari 14, note 11).
[^32]: Shyamadasi is Rasika's wife, formerly Ichchha-devi (Dakshina, lahari 1). "Descended as a portion of Lakshmi" — *lakṣmī-aṃśe abatīrṇa*: thus the author exalts her (cf. 59: Rasika is a devotee of Lakshmikanta); BR 15 says nothing of the kind.
[^33]: "Inviolable" — *alaṅghya*, "that which may not be transgressed." Rasikendra-chandra — "the moon Rasikendra," i.e. Rasika.
[^34]: "With his whole being" — *sarbātma-bhābete*. "Has sold himself" — *bikāila* (in the scan "বিকা'ল"), i.e. has given himself into [his] power; "together with his whole family" — *sabaṃśe*: with his family and household.
[^35]: Krishna and Baladeva served Their guru Sandipani Muni in Avanti (Bhagavatam 10.45). Understood thus: no one but They ever served the guru as Rasika did.
[^36]: "As non-different" — *abheda-rūpe*. Cf. note 31: according to Gaudiya Vaishnava teaching the guru is honoured as Hari Himself because he is the Lord's dear servant; on Shyamananda as a devotee and servant of Radha see Purva, lahari 1, note 2; Purva, lahari 14, note 11.
[^37]: The Sri Murti is the family Deity whom Rasika recovered from the Bhanja raja and brought to Kashipur (note 24). "Command [us] His name" — *tā̃ra nāma ājñā kara*: Rasika asks Shyamananda to name the Deity. Gopivallabha — "the beloved of the gopis," a name of Krishna; -raya is the respectful "lord." The village is Kashipur (note 17), henceforth Sri Gopivallabhapur (86).
[^38]: 88–90 are Shyamananda's words: he foretells the manifestation (*prakāśa habena*, "will become manifest") of the Deity Govinda in the village, in the form he beholds in meditation. "Suddenly" — *ācambite*. "Renowned" (87) — *paracāre* (= *pracāre*); "abundant" (86) — *paracura* (= *pracura*).
[^39]: "Overseer" — *adhikārī*: the one to whom the village and the service are entrusted. "Mother" — *mātā*, respectfully of Shyamadasi. The second line is the author's; "he appointed" — *karila niyojitā*, apparently Shyamananda (cf. 93).
[^40]: Shyamananda's words again. "Detached" — *udāsīna*: free from household cares; hence the service in the village is entrusted to Shyamadasi (93).
[^41]: "Shine brilliantly" — *mahādīpta*. "By [his] command" — *ājñā paramāṇe* (= *pramāṇe*), i.e. as Shyamananda had foretold.
[^42]: The son of Achyuta is Rasikananda: the author says he heard this account from Rasika himself. The author's father Rasamaya and his family were Rasika's disciples (Purva, lahari 1, 135–141, note 56; cf. Dakshina, lahari 4).
[^43]: "Faults in the order [of events]" — *anukrama doṣa*: the author admits that the sequence of events may be inexact.
[^44]: The colophon is on pdf 100 (checked against the scan): "ইতি শ্রীরসিকমঙ্গল দক্ষিণ-বিভাগে শ্রীগোপীবল্লভপুর-প্রকাশ-নাম তৃতীয়লহরী সম্পূর্ণা"; on the title see note 1. Lahari 4 begins below on the same page.
