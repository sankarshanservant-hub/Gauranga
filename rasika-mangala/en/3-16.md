# Pashchima-vibhaga. Lahari 16. The Description of the Great Festival of Separation from Sri Sri Shyamananda Prabhu

*A song in glory of Shyamananda and Rasikendra; having heard from the brothers about the Shyamanandis, Rasika writes to everyone — kings, bhuiyans, Vaishnavas, sannyasis, brahmanas — inviting them to the great festival, and himself sets out to collect alms; mountains of rice, beans, flour, sugar, ghee and oil are stored, sweets, fruits, coconuts, spices, vegetables and utensils, and lodgings are built for the sadhus — four times as much as for the Great Rasa; the festival ground is adorned, and the circle of the rasa is built with a throne, canopies, banners and auspicious pots; on the full moon of Jyeshtha, the day of the Deity's bathing, Rasika performs the adhivasa; communities, mahantas, Vaishnavas, Vrajavasis, kings and subjects gather, and Rasika honours everyone with gifts, first of all Tulasi, who began the sankirtana; with the onset of the first day of the dark fortnight the festival begins: hundreds of brahmanas cook and serve, thousands at once honour prasada, and the demigods, unrecognised, feast and play with Rasika; after the meal with the mahantas and the brothers Rasika dances in the kirtana, showing all the signs of ecstasy, amid lights and the sounds of many instruments, as if Vrindavana had appeared before the eyes; the author asks the learned not to count his faults and concludes the Pashchima-vibhaga*

*The sixteenth lahari.*

*Raga Varadi.*[^1]
*Ghosha: "O ocean of mercy, O merciful Shyama!*
*Turn Your attention to the fallen and wretched people."*

**1.** Glory, glory to Shyamananda, the ocean of compassion!
Glory to Rasikendra-chandra, the treasury of all virtues!

**2.** Hearing from the mouths of the brothers about the community of the Shyamanandis,
Thakura Rasika rejoiced greatly.[^2]

**3.** To everyone, as was fitting [for each], he wrote:
"Come [and] behold the greatest festival."[^3]

**4.** To kings, subjects, bhuiyans, mahajanas, sadhus,
elders, Vaishnavas, sannyasis, brahmanas,

**5.** to the Shyamanandi Vaishnavas — as was fitting [for each] —
Rasika-Murari wrote, one by one.[^4]

**6.** He himself set out to collect alms;
for the festival he quickly [gathered] all the provisions.[^5]

**7.** First of all he [stored] rice beyond measure:
bringing [it] in mountains, they pour it into the granaries.[^6]

**8.** Much mung [and] biri they stored, filling baskets,
and much chona, chickpeas, khariya, khesari;

**9.** wheat, maida, barley, roasted-grain flour — in abundance;
gur, sugar, khanda-pheni, lovely misri.[^7]

**10.** Filling house after house, they stored [everything] in its place;
areca nuts [and] ghee — carefully, in hundreds and hundreds of jars;

**11.** mustard [and] sesame oil — in hundreds and hundreds of pots;
khai, chida, hudumba, ukhra — beyond measure.[^8]

**12.** Sweets fried in ghee, cakes of many kinds —
filling hundreds and hundreds of pots, they stored each [separately].

**13.** Luchi, puri, curd, milk, palo, cream, chhana
the household prepares at the proper time.[^9]

**14.** Very soft laddus, chida fried in ghee,
ripe mangoes [and] jackfruits — hundreds and hundreds of baskets;

**15.** bananas of many kinds, ripe [and] soft;
narengas, kamalas, fragrant mau-tabas.[^10]

**16.** Young coconuts, ripe coconuts, bana-pairas and dry coconuts
the disciples bring in hundreds and hundreds of loads.[^11]

**17.** Lime, khadira, areca nuts, betel leaves — beyond measure;
nutmeg, cloves, mace, bay leaves;

**18.** fennel, cumin, and also pepper, camphor,
ginger and the other pungent [spices] they quickly stored.[^12]

**19.** All [that is needed] for cooking people bring, one after another:
greens of many kinds, badi, bananas, eggplants,

**20.** tender lau, maja, kushmanda gourds, karelas,
palata, patara-phalas — they brought them in good time.[^13]

**21.** All this in many ways, in due order, the followers
brought and, filling the houses, stored.

**22.** Pots, jars, earthen lids, sarais, oil vessels,
spatulas, bontis, areca-nut cutters, brooms;[^14]

**23.** new kanduis [and] large baskets they made in plenty;
and sieves for washing rice, and winnowing fans they had brought in plenty.[^15]

**24.** Salt, methi, hingu, turmeric, ground mustard [they stored],
[and] flour of atapa rice to adorn the pavilion.[^16]

**25.** For the sadhus they built excellent lodgings;
blankets, bhota, mats — of many kinds.[^17]

**26.** Thus he quickly [gathered] various provisions.
As before, for the Great Rasa, [he had gathered] loads of provisions, —

**27.** compared with that the provisions were fourfold.
Prabhu proclaimed the greatest festival.[^18]

**28.** All made the festival ground radiant:
arches, hanging tassels, beautiful chamaras.

**29.** Many canopies they stretched on firm ropes;
on [all] four sides — banana trees [and] handsome banners.[^19]

**30.** They enclosed the place in a circle of many-coloured cloths;
in its midst — a throne, most beautiful.

**31.** Around it — a fence of many-coloured cloths;
in every direction hang tassels [and] chamaras.

**32.** Various flower garlands hang all along [the fence];
seeing the arrangement of the pavilion, [all] are amazed.[^20]

**33.** Around the pavilion shine jewelled kundas,
[and] auspicious pots with coconuts [and] mango leaves.[^21]

**34.** Thus they arranged the place of the rasa;
the beauty of the circle of the rasa enchants all people.

**35.** In the month of Jyeshtha, on the full-moon day of the Deity's bathing —
on that day the mahashaya performed the adhivasa.[^22]

**36.** Many communities came to that place,
many Vaishnavas, mahantas, sadhus.

**37.** Thousands and thousands of Vrajavasis — beyond count;
kings [and] subjects by hundreds of thousands came in haste.[^23]

**38.** With garments, ornaments, rings, garlands, sandal and the rest
he honoured everyone, according to the rules, as was fitting [for each].

**39.** Hundreds of people [carried] hundreds of cups of sandal;
the brahmanas gave [it] to all the mahantas [and] Vaishnavas.[^24]

**40.** First Tulasi performed the sankirtana:
him the son of Achyuta honoured before [all].

**41.** According to [each one's] rank he honoured the shantas:
garlands, sandal and the rest, garments he gave to each.[^25]

**42.** At the auspicious moment, with the onset of the first lunar day of the dark fortnight,
in the morning he began the festival.[^26]

**43.** Hundreds and hundreds of people entered the storerooms;
hundreds and hundreds of brahmanas set to the cooking.

**44.** Hundreds and hundreds of people prepare [everything] for the cooking;
hundreds and hundreds of water-carriers all go [and] bring water.

**45.** Hundreds and hundreds of people stitch leaf cups [and] leaf plates;
hundreds and hundreds joyfully sweep [and] sprinkle [the ground].

**46.** Hundreds and hundreds of brahmanas serve [the food];
five to ten thousand [people] sit at one time.[^27]

**47.** Ten or twenty dishes, excellent shali rice,
khir, pitha, fried delicacies — the offering to Krishna;

**48.** ghee, curd, milk, chhana — of many kinds;
around the leaf plates — the leaf cups: a delight to see.

**49.** Cream, sugar, bananas — gifts of six tastes —
the brahmanas of pure conduct offer before Krishna.[^28]

**50.** At the fragrance of the prasada the demigods are enchanted;
if one partakes of [it], the three [kinds of] miseries do not remain.

**51.** The world of the gods and the world of men, coming together,
merrily honour prasada, dancing [and] singing.

**52.** No one recognises anyone; they play in many ways;
the demigods sport together with Rasika.[^29]

**53.** First the Vrajavasis ate;
after everyone [Rasika] sits down [to the meal], taking the mahantas with him.

**54.** What a market of moons — the host of mahantas!
He himself sits down, taking the brothers with him.

**55.** The principal disciples, the throng of disciples' disciples,
[like] stars, surround Rasikendra-chandra in the middle.[^30]

**56.** Having honoured prasada, all went to the kirtana;
the son of Achyuta himself dances.

**57.** What a sweet dance, what a gait!
What streams of tears — dancing eyes![^31]

**58.** What beauty of pulaka — kadamba buds!
What a faltering voice — the eight sattvika-bhavas![^32]

**59.** With the tears from his eyes the cloth on his body is drenched;
unrestrained streams flow — they cannot be held back.

**60.** Casting [his] Sri-anga on the ground, he rolls about, over and over;
the place of the rasa floats away on waves of tears.[^33]

**61.** The dark, beautiful body is grey with dust;
on it sweat, drop by drop — beautiful to see.

**62.** On his neck sways a flower garland, flower bracelets [on his arms];
on [his] Sri-anga shines a fine cloth.

**63.** Sweet-sounding nupuras adorn both feet;
the beauty of the murali in his hand cannot be told.

**64.** What beauty of the body's poses, what a gait!
What a gentle, gentle smile, what a sweet form![^34]

**65.** Chandrodayas, lamps, deutis, chandrabanas,
bhumichampas and the rest — all blaze on the place of the rasa.

**66.** The holy circle of the rasa became [brighter] than day;
there — Rasika's dance, most joyful.[^35]

**67.** Vina, venu, mridanga, mandiras, karatalas,
sarangi, kinnari, sweet-sounding svara-mandala;

**68.** pinaka, khamaka, kapinash — delightful;
some play pakhavaj [and] muchung;

**69.** rabab, tambura, saptasvara, the sound of the bamshi;
ayuja, dampha, dholaki — they are heard in unison.

**70.** All the musical [instruments] there are, created by the Creator —
hundreds and hundreds of people play [them] in concert.[^36]

**71.** What a blending of music — all in one tune!
And there — Rasika's incomparable dance.

**72.** Vrindavana became visible before the very eyes;
in this happiness the son of Achyuta spent the night.[^37]

**73.** Night and day in this happiness Rasikendra plays;
throngs of mahantas — on all sides, crowd upon crowd.

**74.** Even with a hundred mouths the greatness of that happiness cannot be told;
briefly I have composed a little beautiful praise.

**75.** In the Pashchima-vibhaga I have described this;
may the learned, the good, not hold faults in it against me.[^38]

**76.** Adorning himself with Shyamananda's feet,
the son of Rasamaya joyfully composed [this].

*Thus in the Pashchima-vibhaga of the Sri Rasika-mangala ends the sixteenth lahari, called "The Description of the Great Festival of Separation from Sri Sri Shyamananda Prabhu."*[^39]

[^1]: The lines before the raga are the heading of the lahari ("ষোড়শ-লহরী"), in italics without a number. Lahari 16 begins on the lower half of pdf 175 (printed p. 131), under a rule after the colophon of lahari 15 (files RM-3-15-076-086). Raga Varadi (*barāṛī*; Pashchima, lahari 1, note 1); the ghosha is the same as in Pashchima, laharis 1 and 9, and its translation is repeated (Pashchima, lahari 9, note 1: "Shyama" here is apparently Shyamananda). There is no mangala-shloka: after the raga and the ghosha the song begins with ॥ 1 ॥. The title follows the colophon on pdf 177 (printed p. 133), checked against the scan: "ইতি শ্রীরসিকমঙ্গল-পশ্চিম-বিভাগে শ্রীশ্রীশ্যামানন্দপ্রভুর বিরহ-মহোৎসব-বর্ণন-নাম ষোড়শ-লহরী সম্পূর্ণা" (*biraha-mahotsaba-barṇana*). "The great festival of separation" (*biraha-mahotsaba*) is the festival in memory of Shyamananda's departure, held on the anniversary of his disappearance (notes 22, 26; cf. lahari 13, 49–50, 69; lahari 14, 3–5). Lahari 16 comprises 1–76 (pdf 175–177) and is the last lahari of the Pashchima-vibhaga; the Uttara-vibhaga begins on a new page — pdf 178 (printed p. 134).
[^2]: "About the community of the Shyamanandis" — *śyāmānandī goṣṭhī*: the list of Shyamananda's disciples, their disciples and their disciples' disciples (lahari 14, 57–170; lahari 15). "The brothers" — *bhāigaṇa*: Shyamananda's disciples, Rasika's spiritual brothers (lahari 14, note 8). "Thakura Rasika" — *ṭhākura rasike*.
[^3]: "Wrote" — *karila lekhana*: he sent out invitations (cf. lahari 14, 18). "The greatest festival" — *mahā-mahotsaba* (likewise in 27).
[^4]: Bhuiyans (*bhuñā*) are landholders (lahari 14, note 69). Mahajanas (*mahājana*) are respected people, probably merchants and moneylenders. "Elders" — *gurujana*. "Sannyasis" — *nyāsī*. Rasika-Murari is Rasikananda.
[^5]: "To collect alms" — *bhikṣā karibāre*: to collect provisions for the festival, as before the Rasa-yatra (Pashchima, lahari 1, 19, 27, note 15). "Quickly [gathered]" — *karilā satbare*.
[^6]: "Beyond measure" — *apramite*. "Granaries" — *marāi*: round wicker grain stores (Pashchima, lahari 1, note 21).
[^7]: Mung and biri (urad) are beans (Pashchima, lahari 1, note 21); "baskets" — *ḍola* (ibid., note 24). Chona (*conā*) and khariya (*khāṛiẏā*) are apparently kinds of pulses; not identified. "Chickpeas" — *cholā*; khesari is grass pea (Pashchima, lahari 1, note 22). Maida is finely milled flour; "roasted-grain flour" — *chātu*. Gur is unrefined cane sugar; khanda-pheni (*khaṇḍapheṇī*) — according to the editor's note, *bātāsā*, airy sugar drops; misri is crystal sugar.
[^8]: "Areca nuts" — *guẏā* (Pashchima, lahari 1, note 22). Khai is puffed rice; chida is flattened rice; hudumba (*huṛumba*, so in the scan, crop ×2) is apparently the same as hudum (Pashchima, lahari 1, note 24); ukhra (*ukhaṛā*) — according to the editor's note, *muṛakī*, puffed rice in sugar syrup.
[^9]: "Cakes" (12) — *piṣṭaka*, pitha. "Each [separately]" — *pratyeke*. Luchi and puri are flatbreads fried in oil (Pashchima, lahari 1, note 24). Palo (*pālo*) is a starch flour (from tubers); the sense is approximate. "Cream" — *sara*, the skin of boiled milk; chhana is fresh curd cheese. "The household" — *parijanā*.
[^10]: "Baskets" (14) — *jhoṛā*. Narengas and kamalas are oranges (Pashchima, lahari 1, note 26); mau-taba (*mauṭābā*, so in the scan, crop ×2) is apparently a sweet (*mau*, "honey") taba lemon (ibid., note 26). "Fragrant" — *parimala*.
[^11]: 16a — *paiṛa nāṛiẏā bāna paiṛa se jhunā*: apparently coconuts of different ripeness — paira (cf. Oriya *paiḍa*), a young green coconut; nariya (cf. Oriya *naḍiā*), a ripe coconut; jhuna (*jhunā*), an old dry coconut; bana-paira (*bāna paiṛa*) is not identified. "Loads" — *bhāra*: burdens carried on a shoulder pole (Pashchima, lahari 1, note 22).
[^12]: Lime, khadira, areca nuts, betel leaves — everything for pan (Pashchima, lahari 1, note 27). "Betel leaves" — *pāga* (so in the scan): apparently a misprint for *pāṇa*. "Mace" — *jāi*: taken as *jaẏitrī*; the reading is unclear. "Bay leaves" — *tejapatra*. "Fennel" — *pāṇamauri*. "Ginger and the other" — *ādrakādi*; the editor explains: *ādā prabhṛti*, "ginger and the like."
[^13]: Badi, bananas (*rambhā*), eggplants, maja, kushmanda, karela, palata — Pashchima, lahari 1, notes 25–26. Lau (*lāu*) is the bottle gourd. Patara-phala (*patarphala*) — according to the editor's note, *jhiṅgā*, ridge gourd (luffa).
[^14]: Kitchen utensils. "Earthen lids" — *sarā*, shallow earthen lids or dishes; sarai (*saṛai*) is not identified, apparently a kind of earthenware; "oil vessels" — *telāni*. "Spatulas" — *cāṭu*. Bontis — *panakhī*: according to the editor's note, *bãṭhī*, a curved blade fixed on a stand. "Areca-nut cutters" — *khilikāti*: according to the editor's note, *jā̃ti*. "Brooms" — *sammārjanī*.
[^15]: Kandui (*kaṇḍui*) — according to the editor's note, *ṭucani* (so in the scan); apparently a kind of basket; not identified. "Large baskets" — *cāṅgāṛī*. "Sieves for washing rice" — *cāladhuẏā*. "Winnowing fans" — *kulā*.
[^16]: "Ground mustard" — *sariṣā guṛī*. "Flour of atapa rice" — *ābātaṇḍulera guṛī*: the editor explains *ābātaṇḍula* as *ātapa taṇḍula*, rice husked without parboiling (from sun-dried grain); with such flour auspicious patterns are drawn on the ground (cf. Dakshina, lahari 10, note 10). "To adorn the pavilion" — *maṇḍapa maṇḍite*.
[^17]: "Lodgings" — *bāsā*. Bhota (*bhoṭa*) — apparently woollen blankets from Bhota (Tibet, Bhutan). "Mats" — *maśinā* (cf. Oriya *masiṇā*).
[^18]: "Before, for the Great Rasa" — *pūrbbe … baṛa rāse*: for the Rasa-yatra at Sri Gopivallabhapur (Pashchima, lahari 1, 19–43). "Fourfold" — *caturguṇa*. "Prabhu" is Rasika. "Proclaimed" — *karilā pracāra*.
[^19]: Arches — *toraṇā*; "hanging tassels" — *lambita jhārā*; chamaras are whisks of yak tails; "canopies" — *candrātapa* (Dakshina, lahari 10, note 9; Pashchima, lahari 1, note 36). "On firm ropes" — *subandhane*. Banana trees are set up at entrances as an auspicious decoration.
[^20]: "Enclosed in a circle" (30) — *maṇḍalī karila*; it may also be understood as "made the circle (the place of the rasa)" (cf. 34). "Fence" (31) — *bāṛa*. "Tassels" — *thupanā* (cf. Oriya *thupa*, "tuft, tassel"); the sense is approximate. "All along [the fence]" (32) — *tā'ra lāge lāge*, lit. "adjoining it."
[^21]: "Jewelled kundas" — *ratnakuṇḍa* (so in the scan, crop ×2): *kuṇḍa* is a pool, bowl or vessel; possibly a misprint for *ratnakumbha*, "jewelled pots" (cf. the rhyme *kumbhe*). "Auspicious pots" — *maṅgala kumbha*: water pots crowned with a coconut and mango leaves (purna-kumbha).
[^22]: "On the full-moon day of the Deity's bathing" — *debasnāna pūrṇimā-dibase*: the Snana-yatra on the full moon of Jyeshtha (lahari 13, note 23). Shyamananda departed on the first lunar day of the dark fortnight, at the onset of Ashadha, after the Snana-yatra (lahari 13, 49–50); the festival begins on the same lunar day (42), that is, on the anniversary of his departure. The adhivasa is the preliminary rite on the eve of a festival (Pashchima, lahari 2, note 19). "The mahashaya" is Rasika.
[^23]: "Communities" — *sampradā* (= *sampradāẏa*). Vrajavasis are residents of Vraja (Pashchima, lahari 12, note 15). "Beyond count" — *apramite*.
[^24]: "Rings" (38) — *mudi* (= *mudrikā*). "Honoured" — *pūjilena*: presented gifts and honours. 39 — *śata śata candanera donā śata jane / mohānta baiṣṇaba sabe dila dbijagaṇe*: the sense is approximate; "cups" — *donā*, cups of leaves; it may also be understood as "[Rasika] gave [sandal] to all the mahantas, Vaishnavas [and] brahmanas."
[^25]: Tulasi (*tulasī*) is taken as a name — apparently the leader of the kirtana (cf. Tulasi, lahari 15, 47); at Vaishnava festivals the kirtana singers are honoured first with garlands and sandal. It may also be understood as "first they performed sankirtana [before] Tulasi," the sacred plant, and then "honoured her before [all]." "The son of Achyuta" is Rasika. "According to rank" (41) — *pada anusāre*. "The shantas" — *śāntagaṇe* (so in the scan): "the peaceful" — renunciants, sannyasis; possibly = *santagaṇe*, "the saints."
[^26]: "With the onset of the first lunar day of the dark fortnight" — *kṛṣṇa-pratipada parabeśa*: the first day of the dark fortnight of Ashadha (note 22). "In the morning" — *bihāne*. The subject is not expressed; understood as Rasika.
[^27]: "Water-carriers" (44) — *bhārī*. "Stitch" (45) — *śiñe*: they pin leaves together with slivers, making cups (*donā*) and plates (*patrābalī*). "Sweep [and] sprinkle" — *jhā̃ṭī chaṛā deẏa*: they sweep the ground and sprinkle it with water for purification. "Serve [the food]" (46) — *prabeṣaṇa* (= *pariveśana*). "Five to ten thousand" — *daśa pā̃ca sahasra*.
[^28]: "Shali rice" (47) — *śāli anna*, the finest rice. Khir is rice cooked in thickened milk; pitha are cakes; "fried delicacies" — *pakvānna*. "The offering to Krishna" — *kṛṣṇera nibedana*: food is first offered to Krishna and so becomes prasada (50). "A delight to see" (48) — *dekhite susāra*. "Of six tastes" (49) — *ṣaṛarasa* (Dakshina, lahari 3, note 7). "The brahmanas of pure conduct" — *dbija sadācāra*. "Before Krishna" — *kṛṣṇera sannidhe*: before the Deity.
[^29]: "The three [kinds of] miseries" (50) — *tina tāpa*: those caused by one's own body and mind, by other beings, and by the forces of nature. "Honour prasada" (51) — *prasāda pāẏena*. Unrecognised demigods at Rasika's festivals — cf. Pashchima, lahari 2, note 29.
[^30]: "After everyone" (53) — *sarbba pāche*; the subject is not expressed; understood as Rasika (cf. 54). "A market of moons" (54) — *cā̃dera hāṭa*: a figure for a radiant assembly of beautiful persons. "The brothers" — note 2. "Disciples' disciples" (55) — *anuśiṣya* (lahari 14, note 11). Rasikendra-chandra ("moon") amid the stars — a play on the epithet.
[^31]: "Gait" — *calani*. "Dancing eyes" — *nayana nācanī*; 57b may also be understood as "what streams of tears from the dancing eyes."
[^32]: Pulaka is the standing of the hairs on end from ecstatic love — "kadamba buds" (*kadamba kalikā*; lahari 14, note 45). "A faltering voice" — *gada gada kaṇṭha*. "The eight sattvika-bhavas" — *aṣṭa sāttvikā*: being stunned, perspiration, pulaka, faltering of the voice, trembling, change of colour, tears, and fainting (Bhakti-rasamrita-sindhu 2.3).
[^33]: "Sri-anga" — *śrīaṅga*, "holy body" (likewise in 62). "Rolls about, over and over" — *gaṛi gaṛi bule*. "On waves of tears" — *lohera hillole* (*loha*, "tears"; Pashchima, lahari 3, note 24).
[^34]: "Fine" (62) — *jhīna*: according to the editor's note, *pātalā*, "thin." "Gentle, gentle" (64) — *lohu lohu* (= *lahu lahu*, "softly, gently"; Dakshina, lahari 12, note 14). 61–64: the dark body, the nupuras, the murali in his hand, the poses of the body — the author describes the dancing Rasika with features recalling the form of Krishna, thus exalting his guru (Purva, lahari 1, note 2; Purva, lahari 14, note 11). According to Gaudiya Vaishnava teaching Rasika is a devotee of Krishna, a disciple of Shyamananda. The murali may also be understood literally: Rasika dances with a flute in his hand.
[^35]: Chandrodaya, deuti, chandrabana, bhumichampa are festive lamps and fireworks (Purva, lahari 12, note 17). "Lamps" — *dīpaka*. "Became [brighter] than day" (66) — *dibasa adhika haila*. "The holy circle of the rasa" — *śrīrāsamaṇḍalī* (cf. 34).
[^36]: The instruments — Pashchima, lahari 3, note 17; Dakshina, lahari 5, note 23: vina, kinnari, kapinash are kinds of vina; venu and bamshi are flutes; the mridanga is a clay drum; mandiras and karatalas are hand cymbals; the sarangi is a bowed instrument; the svara-mandala is a kind of zither; the pinaka is a stringed instrument; the pakhavaj is a double-headed drum; the muchung is a jaw harp; the rabab is a stringed instrument; the dampha is a frame drum; dholakis are small drums; the tambura (*tāmburā*) is a stringed drone instrument. Khamaka (*khamaka*) is apparently a one-string plucked percussion instrument; saptasvara (*saptasbarā*, "of seven notes") and ayuja (*āẏuja*, so in the scan, crop ×1.3) are not identified. "In unison" — *ekamila*, *ekamela*. "Play" (70) — *bāẏa*: according to the editor's note, *bājāẏa*. "Created by the Creator" — *bidhātā sṛjana*.
