# Purva-vibhaga. Lahari 12. Description of the Wedding

*A song in glory of Shyamananda and Rasikananda; Rasika's wedding procession, with brahmanas, kinsfolk, gifts and music, goes to Hijli, and on the way all marvel at the bridegroom's beauty; Sadashiva receives the guests, adorns the house and the wedding pavilion and lights countless lamps; Rasika is dressed as a bridegroom and goes in procession to the pavilion, where the brahmanas bless him; the adorning of the bride Ichchha-dei, the wedding rite and the dowry; the return home, Achyuta's joy and the feasting of the kinsfolk*

*The twelfth lahari.*

*Raga Suhi.*[^1]
*Ghosha: "What shall I say of the beauty of Gopala's moon-like face?*
*It captivates the hearts of all the women of Vraja."*

**1.** Glory, glory to Shyamananda, the friend of the afflicted!
Glory, glory to Rasikananda, the ocean of mercy!

**2.** At that time, by Achyuta's order, the kinsfolk —
all set out, taking Rasika with them.

**3.** Having reckoned an auspicious day and an auspicious moment,
Achyuta's son set out to be married.

**4.** Brahmanas versed in the Vedas they all took along;
the astrologer, the barber, the washerman and others go with [them].[^2]

**5.** All the kinsfolk, mounted on horses and in litters —
near ones, friends, bhattacharyas, mahatas and others.[^3]

**6.** With them, hundreds upon hundreds of loads with various gifts:
fried dishes, sweets, laddus — of many kinds.[^4]

**7.** Cloths of many sorts, ornaments of many kinds;
with them music and dundubhis of many kinds.

**8.** Dhol, dhak, pahara, mridanga, karatalas,
upanga, muraja, dampha — delightful music.

**9.** Tamaka, dogiri, gijighosha in abundance;
madal, murali, banshi, beautiful shanais.

**10.** Muchanga, karatalas, venu — instruments [sounding] in many ways;
at the sound of the music the earth quakes and trembles.[^5]

**11.** Having decked out palanquins in many ways,
Rasikendra set out to be married.[^6]

**12.** In royal attire all the companions go,
again and again [raising] the thunder of music and dundubhis.

**13.** Hearing, hundreds upon hundreds of people go to see;
seeing the moon-like face, all forget themselves.

**14.** Hearing [his] sweet words, all are enchanted;
no one has the heart to go away.

**15.** All say: "In what village did this man live?
Seeing all [his] marks, [he] is equal to Narayana.[^7]

**16.** In a human being we have never seen such a form;
once one sees [this] sweet form, the eyes cannot slip away."

**17.** Thus all go along the road praising [him];
seeing Rasika's form, all are enchanted.

**18.** At that time [the procession] entered the neighbourhood of Hijli;
a messenger went and quickly told Sadashiva.[^8]

**19.** Hearing [this], Sadashiva summoned the kinsfolk
and sent them all to fetch the bridegroom.

**20.** Going some distance, all saw Rasika;
seeing Rasika's form, they are charmed at heart.

**21.** All say: "Ichchha-devi is most fortunate:
the bridegroom in beauty and virtues is as if equal to Vishnu."[^9]

**22.** Praising [him], they quickly came, bringing the bridegroom;
all entered the town of Hijli.

**23.** Hearing of the greatness of Sri Rasika's beauty,
all run to see — there is no limit to it.

**24.** Seeing [his] sweet form, [people] forget themselves;
all the people praise Balabhadra:

**25.** "Blessed is Balabhadra, blessed is your daughter:
by great austerities [she] has obtained Achyuta's son!"[^10]

**26.** Seeing Rasika, all are in an ocean of bliss;
no one even thinks of going away.

**27.** At that time Sadashiva, delighted,
gave all of them lodging, bringing [them] to a fine house.

**28.** Preparing all the provisions as prescribed,
he sent [them] with hundreds of porters.

**29.** Calling astrologer brahmanas, [he] fixed the auspicious lagna
and adorned the house like a city of the gods.[^11]

**30.** Making the house, courtyard and pavilion beautiful,
they decked [them] with patanet of many colours.[^12]

**31.** Diamonds, sapphires, corals, pearls hang from it in tassels;
and hundreds upon hundreds of chamaras sway [in the air].[^13]

**32.** Flags, arches and the like shine on all sides;
they adorned the house on all sides with various paintings.

**33.** Golden pitchers shine on the verandas;
the place, like Vaikuntha, is beautiful to see.[^14]

**34.** Setting up a pitcher in the middle of the pavilion,
the brahmanas versed in the Vedas sat on the four sides.

**35.** Hundreds upon hundreds of kinsmen sat on all sides;
the notables sat right beside them.[^15]

**36.** On spreads of Bhot blankets, coloured carpets and mats
[the guests] sat in great assemblies.[^16]

**37.** Of lamps and torches there is no counting;
millions upon millions of blazing chandrodayas burn there.

**38.** Of lamps, lanterns and rockets there is no number;
millions upon millions of chandrabanas, millions of bhumichampas.

**39.** Kanakachampas and others shine in so many ways;
in the markets, on the roads, in the courtyards they burn in rows.[^17]

**40.** All around the pavilion there burn, beyond counting,
trees of white wax, most brilliant.

**41.** For four praharas those trees burn;
night became day in all those places.

**42.** Thus hundreds upon hundreds of wax trees burn;
they made [everything] brighter than day.[^18]

**43.** The supreme beauty [that met] the eye cannot be described:
pavilion, house and courtyard are arrayed with flowers.

**44.** Stringing various fragrant flowers into garlands,
on all sides — moon-garlands, flowers hanging in tassels.[^19]

**45.** With flowers they adorned the pavilion and the courtyard;
[all] surpassed ten million moons — it became [like] moonlight.

**46.** The extraordinary beauty cannot be told;
it seems as if Vaikuntha had risen.

**47.** Quickly adorning the place of the wedding,
everywhere they perform the family customs as prescribed.

**48.** All those festivities cannot be told even with a hundred mouths;
in brief I have given some account of the wedding.

**49.** At that time, fixing the lagna at the auspicious moment,
Sadashiva, taking all the kinsfolk with him,

**50.** having completed the proper rites — the adhivasa and the rest,
as many as there are according to the Vedas — came quickly.[^20]

**51.** Beside the wedding pavilion Sadashiva Dasa,
with all his kin, [is] in joy and jubilation.

**52.** All the kinsfolk sat in their places;
all accepted betel with camphor.

**53.** At that time the astrologer quickly announces:
"The lagna is most auspicious [now] to bring the bridegroom."[^21]

**54.** Hearing [this], Sadashiva, greatly delighted,
ordered everyone: "Go and bring the bridegroom."

**55.** Hearing, the whole kin, rejoicing,
taking music, dundubhis and the rest with them,

**56.** all came to where Rasika [was]
and said: "In the auspicious lagna, please set out."

**57.** Hearing, the companions quickly dressed [him]
and put various ornaments on every limb.[^22]

**58.** On the long cheeks they applied kunkuma and sandal;
in the midst of it, a dot of phagu, most beautiful.

**59.** The finely curling hair, which conquers the town-ladies, they tie up;
they adorn it with a fragrant garland of flowers.[^23]

**60.** With care they tie a turban of tansuk;
on it they fastened a crown, a golden ornament.[^24]

**61.** Diamonds, sapphires, corals, pearls — in the middle of the crown;
rubies, little mirrors — the radiance sparkles and glitters.

**62.** Various fragrant flowers are arranged in rows;
seeing the crown, all people are enchanted.

**63.** That captivating crown they placed on [his] head;
the beauty of [his] Sri moon-like face has no comparison.

**64.** Millions upon millions of moons are given as nichhani for that face;
by beauty, virtues and words he enchanted all living beings.[^25]

**65.** The jewelled pendants of the crown swing at the sides;
in the rays of the jewels the face shines like the moon.

**66.** In the eyes, lines of kajal, beautiful to see;
the two eyes are more restless than the wagtail.[^26]

**67.** The nose, surpassing the sesame flower, is beautiful to see;
the teeth surpass [pomegranate seeds], the lips are of a lovely red.

**68.** On them the colour of betel, most captivating;
a face with a gentle, gentle smile, a beautiful glance.

**69.** The brows are shaped surpassing Kama's bow;
the fine hairs on them shine like bees.[^27]

**70.** In both ears shine strings of gold and pearls;
on them shine various gems, [like] bright lightning.

**71.** On the neck, [mighty as] an elephant's shoulder, shines a double golden necklace;
on the chest shines a pendant, most captivating.[^28]

**72.** Various precious stones, gems and pearls, strung in rows,
encircle the pendant on the chest, shining beautifully.

**73.** On the arms reaching to the knees — keyuras and bracelets;
the arms, like lotus stalks, are most beautiful.[^29]

**74.** On both arms — armlets studded with various gems;
[his] body makes the ornaments shine.

**75.** The deep, lovely navel graces the belly;
the line of fine hairs and the three folds shine upon it.

**76.** On the slender waist shines fine cloth;
they wrapped [him] in a silk shawl — a yellow cloth.[^30]

**77.** At the waist they tied a silk garment tightly;
those hips and the pair of thighs enchant the three worlds.

**78.** On the very tender feet shines the row of nails;
the lines of red lac on them shine in various ways.[^31]

**79.** Seeing that form, the people of the world are enchanted in mind;
a silk cloth encircling the body shines on the left shoulder.

**80.** On the dark, beautiful body — kunkuma and sandal;
by the radiance of [his] body the three worlds were lit up.

**81.** On the feet — finely painted wooden sandals, captivating to see;
Rasika-shekhara was arrayed in a bridegroom's attire.[^32]

**82.** Holding a karajagya in his hand, with the gait of a king of elephants,
Rasikendra set out to be married.[^33]

**83.** Quickly seating himself in a palanquin at [his] lodging,
all in royal attire go slowly, slowly.

**84.** At the thunder of music and dundubhis the ground trembles;
with chandrodayas and torches the night is bright.

**85.** In one place — various instruments of various kinds;
in another place they recite poetry again and again.

**86.** In one place the brahmanas chant the Vedas;
in another — the "Bharata," the Puranas, the "Ramayana."[^34]

**87.** Somewhere there is sankirtana, and cries of "Hari!" resound;
somewhere the sound of shinga and vishana horns is heard.[^35]

**88.** Somewhere cowherds twirl their staffs;
somewhere women dance to various instruments.[^36]

**89.** Somewhere [they perform] raut-sharana in various ways;
somewhere they set horses galloping, herd after herd.[^37]

**90.** Somewhere all the people watch various amusements;
somewhere wrestlers wrestle in various ways.

**91.** Somewhere shield-bearers clash with one another;
the young men all jostle in the crowd.[^38]

**92.** Musicians from various kingdoms came at [his] order;
with music [playing] in rivalry the earth surged.[^39]

**93.** As many people as live in the town of Hijli —
rulers and subjects — all came to see the wedding.

**94.** In the markets, on the roads, in the courtyards, on the roofs of houses —
the people are beyond counting; it is very crowded.[^40]

**95.** Drop a mustard seed — it would never fall to the ground;
all say, "We have never seen such a wedding!"[^41]

**96.** With various festivities [they] arrived at the wedding pavilion;
in various ways chandrodayas light it up.

**97.** Surrounding the pavilion sit brahmanas versed in the Vedas;
all perform homa and yajna with care.

**98.** At this time Rasikendra, from the palanquin,
in beautifully painted wooden sandals stepped down onto the ground.[^42]

**99.** Standing beneath the pavilion, Rasikendra
joyfully bowed to the twice-born.

**100.** All the twice-born gave [him] their blessing:
"Bestowing devotion to Krishna, deliver the three worlds!"

**101.** Seeing Rasika's form, all were filled with bliss —
the rulers and the subjects and the host of twice-born;

**102.** children and old men and throngs upon throngs of women —
seeing Rasika's form, all [were] amazed.

**103.** All say, "Where has this man been living?
We see that by all [his] marks [he] is equal to Narayana."[^43]

**104.** How [lovely] that dark body, the soft, soft smile!
Tens of millions of moons offer themselves in ransom for this face.[^44]

**105.** How [lovely] the curve of these brows, the dance of the eyes!
How [lovely] that sweet smile that colours the lips!

**106.** The arms reaching to the knees — how [lovely] their swaying!
By the radiance of [his] body the earth was enchanted.

**107.** Such a form of the best of dancers we have never seen;
to see that form hundreds of eyes come running.

**108.** Blessed, fortunate is Balabhadra's daughter:
as the fruit of many austerities she has obtained such a husband.

**109.** Thus all the people, seeing Rasika,
like bees, surrounded [him] by the hundreds.[^45]

**110.** Seeing [his] Shri moon-like face, they gladden their eyes;
all those places came to surpass Vaikuntha.

**111.** Seeing [him], all the people are amazed;
all say, "What avatara is this man?"

**112.** Around the pavilion all sit on four sides:
kinsmen, close ones [and] Sadashiva Dasa.

**113.** Beside them sat all the twice-born;
setting up the auspicious pot, they perform the puja in due order.[^46]

**114.** Homa, yajna, service — all according to the injunctions;
[they keep] the family customs, whichever are known for each.[^47]

**115.** Swiftly performing one after another according to the rules,
after the varana of the bridegroom they seat the bridegroom on [his place] in the pavilion.[^48]

**116.** On a coloured mat — a bed of a coloured blanket;
over it they spread a covering of fine cloth.[^49]

**117.** On it sat the bridegroom Rasika-shekhara;
on all four sides — cries of "Jaya!" and loud music.

**118.** The women [raise] hulahuli; the conches sound again and again;
amid the roar of the music and the dundubhis nothing can be heard.

**119.** Hundreds of shahnai players perform the auspicious wedding songs;
cries of "Hari!", the sound of the Vedas — a mighty din.[^50]

**120.** The priest, gladly taking the book in hand,
performs one after another the Vedic injunctions and the family customs.[^51]

**121.** The Vedic injunctions, the family customs, homa, yajna and the rest;
setting up the auspicious pot, [they perform] the puja according to the injunctions.

**122.** The brahmanas ordered that the bride be brought;
hearing [this], the close ones rose and went to the inner apartments.[^52]

**123.** They said, "Quickly dress the bride!
Rasika is seated in the pavilion — he will not suffer delay."[^53]

**124.** Hearing [this], the women began to dress [her];
they anointed the body of the golden-complexioned [girl] with kunkuma and sandalwood.[^54]

**125.** On her head, at the parting, a braid beautifully adorned with jewels;
seeing the moon of [her] face, the full moon was put to shame.

**126.** The line of musk tilaka on her forehead
glitters, surpassing the new moon.

**127.** Her brows are lovely, surpassing Kama's bow;
on them — curls that attract swarms of bees.[^55]

**128.** The "chakshu-rakhi" placed in her eyes flashes like lightning;
in her nose, [like] a sesame flower, a pearl glitters.[^56]

**129.** The beauty of her two lips surpasses the bandhuli;
her row of teeth — kunda buds, its gleam [like] a creeper of lightning.[^57]

**130.** Surpassing [gold] of tenfold purity, golden earrings shine in her ears;
on her chin a dot of musk, on her neck ornaments.[^58]

**131.** On her neck various jewels blaze — diamonds, corals, pearls;
on her breast a necklace sways, its radiance shimmering.

**132.** On her arms golden taras, on her wrists golden bangles,
armlets, golden bracelets, golden rings.[^59]

**133.** Her breasts, [like] pitchers, are lovely; the line of fine hair — [a row of] bees;
slender-waisted, on her waist three folds shine.

**134.** On her, a yellow garment and a precious veil;
her knees and shanks are lovely, shapely to behold.[^60]

**135.** On her feet golden anklets, golden pashulis;
in the nails of her feet a hundred thousand moons glitter.[^61]

**136.** Dressing her in various ways, [adorning her] with various flower garlands,
her friends arrayed Balabhadra's daughter.[^62]

**137.** Descended as a portion of Lakshmi, Ichchha of the pata line —
therefore birth after birth [she is] Rasika's beloved.[^63]

**138.** Having thus dressed the bride, the close ones
brought [her] to the place in the pavilion and performed the varana.[^64]

**139.** Taking [her] on their laps, the elders quickly sat down;
the twice-born [perform] homa and yajna according to the Vedic injunctions.

**140.** Performing one after another the Vedic injunctions and the family customs,
they then joyfully gave the bride to Rasika.

**141.** Joining their hands and so on, they seated [her] beside [him];
it seemed as if [they were] Lakshmi and Narayana — the portion and [He who] possesses the portions.[^65]

**142.** To behold [it] was supreme beauty, most enchanting;
seeing [their] form, all the people rejoiced at heart.

**143.** Divine food, clothes and the like, loads of jewels
he gave as dowry — of very many kinds.[^66]

**144.** Thus, having celebrated the wedding with many joys,
having stayed there some days, having asked leave to depart,

**145.** having performed there the ashtamangala and the rest with many joys,
at an auspicious moment Rasika departed for home.[^67]

**146.** Rasika-shekhara came to his own house;
unceasingly [his] body is overwhelmed with love for Krishna.

**147.** In the bliss of Krishna [tears] flow from his eyes in a hundred streams;
unceasingly he chants the name of Hari in seclusion.[^68]

**148.** Seeing Rasika-chandra's face
[and] seeing the bride, Achyuta rejoiced greatly.

**149.** Greeting the kinsmen, the mahashaya
fed them all with dishes of six tastes.[^69]

**150.** Keeping them all some days, he bade [them] farewell;
to all he gave food, clothes, ornaments.

**151.** Such is the joy of Rasika's wedding;
whoever hears [it] with faith, the bonds of worldly existence fall away.

**152.** Listen to "Rasika-mangala", all you friends:
Achyuta's son is the friend of all, hard to attain.[^70]

**153.** Listen to the nectar of his lilas, leaving other talk:
the one who hears, Krishna delivers without fail.

**154.** Adorning himself with Shyamananda's feet,
the son of Rasamaya joyfully composed [this].[^71]

*Thus in the Purva-vibhaga of the Sri Rasika-mangala ends the twelfth lahari, called "Description of the Wedding".*

[^1]: The raga in the scan is *śuhī*, apparently the same as *suhī* (Suhi) in lahari 6 (note 2); there too is the same ghosha (refrain), whose translation is repeated here. Gopala ("the little cowherd") is Krishna. There is no mangala-shloka at the beginning of this lahari: after the heading, the raga and the ghosha, the song begins with ॥ 1 ॥. Lahari 11 ends in the upper half of the same page (pdf 76).
[^2]: "To be married" (3; cf. 11, 82) — *vibhā haite*. The astrologer (*daivajña*) fixes the auspicious moment of the rite; the barber and the washerman (*nāpita*, *rajaka*) by custom take part in the wedding rites.
[^3]: "Litters" — *dolā*. Bhattacharyas are learned brahmanas; mahatas (*mahata*) are respected men, headmen (in Utkala and Medinipur also a hereditary title). "Near ones, friends" — *iṣṭa mitra*.
[^4]: "Loads" — *bhāra*: a load on a carrying pole borne by a porter (*bhārī*, 28). "Fried dishes" — *pakvānna*, food cooked in ghee; laddus are sweet balls.
[^5]: 8–10 are a list of instruments. The dundubhi is a large kettledrum (lahari 3, note 26); the dhol and dhak are large drums; the mridanga and muraja are clay drums; karatalas (*karatāla*, *karttāla*) are hand cymbals; the dampha is a frame drum; the madal is a double-headed drum of Utkala and Medinipur; murali, banshi and venu are flutes; the shanai (*sāhāni*) is a wind instrument like an oboe (lahari 7, note 4); the upanga (*upāṅga*) is a wind instrument known from descriptions of kirtana; the muchanga (*mucaṅga*) is apparently a jew's harp. Pahara (*pahaṛā*), tamaka (*ṭamaka*), dogiri (*dogiṛī*), gijighosha (*gijīghoṣa*): the reading in the scan is clear, but the instruments cannot be identified with certainty (apparently various drums). "Delightful music" — *saṅgīta rasāla*.
[^6]: "Palanquins" — *sukupāla*, which the edition's gloss explains as *pālkī*, palanquin (also 83, 98).
[^7]: "Marks" — *lakṣaṇa*: bodily signs by which a great personality is recognised (cf. 73: arms reaching to the knees). On the rapturous comparisons of Rasika with Narayana and Vishnu (15, 21) see lahari 10, note 35: according to Gaudiya Vaishnava teaching Rasikananda is a pure devotee and associate of Shyamananda, not Narayana Himself.
[^8]: "A messenger went and quickly told Sadashiva" — *sadāśiva dūta giyā kahila satvare*; possibly also "Sadashiva's messenger went and told [him]." Sadashiva is Balabhadra's brother (lahari 10, note 21), who after Balabhadra's passing gives his daughter in marriage to Rasika (lahari 11).
[^9]: Ichchha-devi (*icchādevī*) is Ichchha-dei, Balabhadra's daughter (lahari 10, note 26; lahari 11, 8–12).
[^10]: By this time Balabhadra had already departed from this world (lahari 11, 12); the people praise him and address him (25) — so in the author. "By great austerities" — *bahu tapasyāya*, literally "by many austerities" (cf. lahari 10, 99–102: from childhood the girl prayed to Krishna for such a husband).
[^11]: The lagna is the auspicious time of a rite, reckoned by the rising sign. "Adorned" — *maṇḍalī karila*: decked out, painted with patterns (cf. 45, 47).
[^12]: Patanet (*pāṭaneta*) — a fine silk cloth (lahari 10, note 24).
[^13]: "Sapphires" — *līlā*: so in the scan (also in 61, and in the 1997 edition), apparently for *nīlā*, "sapphire." "In tassels" — *jhārā*. Chamaras are yak-tail whisks.
[^14]: "Verandas" — *piṛā*, which the edition's gloss explains as *bārāndā*. Vaikuntha is the spiritual abode of Narayana.
[^15]: "Notables" — *mahājana*: eminent, respected men (also "merchants, moneylenders").
[^16]: "Bhot blankets" — *bhoṭa kambala*, woollen blankets from Bhot (Tibet, Bhutan). "Mats" — *masinā*, which the edition's gloss explains as *machalanda mādura*, a fine mat (masland). "In great assemblies" — *baṛa baṛa jamā*: *jamā* (Persian) means "gathering, assembly"; possibly "men of rank"; the meaning is unclear.
[^17]: 37–39: the deuti is a lamp on a stand; the chandrodaya, by the edition's gloss, is "a particular kind of lamp" (lahari 3, note 26); haui (*hāui*) are rockets; the chandrabana, by the edition's gloss, is "a particular kind of firework"; bhumichampa and kanakachampa (*bhūmicampā*, *kanakacāmpā*) are apparently ground fireworks that scatter sparks resembling champaka flowers. "In rows" — *pā̃ti pā̃ti*, glossed by the edition as *sāri sāri*.
[^18]: 40–42: "trees of white wax," "wax trees" (*momera vṛkṣa*, *mombṛkṣa*) — apparently tree-shaped candelabra holding white wax candles. A prahara is a watch of about three hours.
[^19]: "Into garlands" — *kerā kerā*: the reading in the scan is clear, the meaning conjectural ("in bunches, in clusters"). "Moon-garlands" — *cā̃da-mālā*, apparently round wreaths of flowers.
[^20]: The adhivasa is a preliminary rite on the eve or on the day of the wedding (anointing and blessing of the bride and bridegroom).
[^21]: "The astrologer" — in the scan *doibajña*, a variant spelling of *daivajña* (as in lahari 5, note 2; cf. 29). "To bring the bridegroom" — to the bride in the wedding pavilion.
[^22]: "Put on" — *khũjilena*: so in the scan (and in the 1997 edition), literally "searched"; apparently for *guṃjilena*, "inserted, fastened." Possibly also "chose various ornaments for every limb."
[^23]: "Which conquers the town-ladies" — *nāgarī dalana*, literally "crushing the nagaris (town-ladies, beloveds)"; the sense is conjectural.
[^24]: Tansuk (*tanasuka*, cf. Persian *tansukh*) — a fine cloth (muslin); the edition's gloss has *cūṛā*, "crest, crown [of the turban]." "Turban" — *pāga*.
[^25]: "Given as nichhani" — *nichāni*: the nichhani custom (lahari 9, note 21; lahari 10, note 25).
[^26]: Kajal is the collyrium used to line the eyes. The wagtail (*khañjana*) is in Indian poetry a standard comparison for lively, restless eyes; the sesame flower (67), for a beautiful nose.
[^27]: Kama is the god of love (Kandarpa). "Like bees" — *ali paramāṇa*, literally "of the measure of bees": the dark hairs of the brows are compared to rows of bees.
[^28]: "An elephant's shoulder" — *gaja-skandha*: mighty shoulders and neck are a sign of a great man. "Double necklace" — *dosarī*, of two strands (cf. lahari 10, note 32); "pendant" — *padaka*.
[^29]: Arms "reaching to the knees" (*ājānulambita*) are a sign of a great personality. Keyuras are bracelets worn on the upper arm; "armlets" (74) — *bājubandha*; "studded" — *jhā̃pā*.
[^30]: "Fine cloth" — *jhinabāsa* (lahari 10, note 32); "shawl" — *pāchaṛi*, a wrap, a covering cloth.
[^31]: "Red lac" — *alakā*, i.e. *alakta* (cf. lahari 10, note 33).
[^32]: "Wooden sandals" — *kaṭhā̃u*, which the edition's gloss explains as *khaṛama* (cf. lahari 6, note 3). "Finely painted" — *suraṅga*, "beautifully coloured." Rasika-shekhara ("the crown of rasikas") is Rasikananda.
[^33]: "Karajagya" — *karajāgya*: the reading in the scan is clear, the meaning has not been established; apparently an object that the bridegroom by custom holds in his hand (in the 1997 edition the line is illegible). The gait of a king of elephants is stately and slow.
[^34]: The "Bharata" is the Mahabharata. The description of the wedding procession continues on the next page (pdf 79, 87–).
[^35]: Sankirtana — congregational singing of the holy names. Shinga and vishana are horns (lahari 4, note 19).
[^36]: "Twirl their staffs" — *laguṛī phirāya*: the cowherds (*gopa*) display their skill with the staff.
[^37]: Raut-sharana (*rāuta śaraṇa*) — according to the edition's gloss, "songs or praises of a particular caste"; the rautas (*rāuta*) of Utkala and Medinipur are a caste of horsemen and soldiers. "Set galloping" — *dhābāyena*; "herd after herd" — *yūthe yūthe*.
[^38]: "Shield-bearers" — *ḍhāli*, men with shields who give display fights (*melāmeli*, "clash with one another"). "Young men" — *bayesiyā*, "those of the same age"; "jostle" — *pelāpeli*.
[^39]: "In rivalry" — *bādābādi*, "competing, outdoing one another".
[^40]: "Beyond counting" — *samuccaya nāhi*, lit. "there is no reckoning"; "crowded" — *gahala*, "thronged, a press of people".
[^41]: A mustard seed that cannot fall to the ground is a common figure for a dense crowd.
[^42]: The palanquin (*sukupāla*) and the wooden sandals (*suraṅga kaṭhā̃u*) — notes 6 and 32 (the OCR is garbled here; read from the scan and the 1997 edition).
[^43]: Cf. 15 and note 7: according to Gaudiya Vaishnava teaching Rasikananda is a pure devotee, a companion of Shyamananda, not Narayana Himself; so also the rapturous question "what avatara" in 111.
[^44]: "Offer themselves in ransom" — *nichāni*: the nichhani rite (note 25).
[^45]: "Surrounded [him] by the hundreds" — *beṛi śata pure*: so in the scan and in the 1997 edition; *pure* may be understood as "fill" or as "from hundreds of villages"; the rendering is tentative.
[^46]: The auspicious pot (*maṅgala-ghaṭa*) — a water-filled pitcher set up for the rite (cf. 34); also 121.
[^47]: "Yajna" — the scan has *sajña*, a misprint for *yajña* (so in the 1997 edition). "Service" — *paricaryā*. "Family customs" — *kulācāra*.
[^48]: "After the varana" — *bariyā*: varana is the rite of honourably receiving the bridegroom (gifts, washing of the feet, etc.), after which he is led to his place; cf. 138.
[^49]: "Mat" — *masinā* (note 16). "Fine cloth" — *jhinapatri*, according to the edition's gloss, "fine cloth" (cf. *jhinabāsa*, note 30).
[^50]: Hulahuli — the women's auspicious cry (lahari 4, note 12). "Shahnai players" — *sāhaniyā*, apparently players of the shahnai (*sāhāni*, note 5); the verb is *gāya*, "sing". "Auspicious wedding songs" — *vibhāra maṅgala*. "A mighty din" — *ghana utarola*.
[^51]: The priest — *purohita*, the family priest conducting the rite; "the book" — *pũthi*, the manuscript of the ritual manual.
[^52]: "The brahmanas ordered" — *vipragaṇe ājñā dila*. "Inner apartments" — *antaḥpura*, the women's quarters of the house.
[^53]: "Delay" — *biyāja*.
[^54]: "Golden-complexioned [girl]" — *gaurāṅgī*, "of golden (fair) body", said of the bride; the 1997 edition has *gaurāṅgī āge*.
[^55]: "Curls" — *alakāvalī*, ringlets above the brows; "that attract swarms of bees" — *alikula-lobhā*.
[^56]: "Chakshu-rakhi" — *cakṣu rākhi*: so in the scan (enlarged) and in the 1997 edition; apparently kajal (collyrium), lit. "guardian of the eyes"; possibly also "placing [kajal] in the eyes, [they] flash like lightning". The reading is unclear. The sesame flower is a figure for a beautiful nose (note 26).
[^57]: Bandhuli (*bandhuli*, Skt. *bandhūka*) — according to the edition's gloss, the "chauddaghari" flower and the [red] telakucha fruit; a red flower to which lips are compared. Kunda — white jasmine. "Creeper of lightning" — *vidyullatā*.
[^58]: "[Gold] of tenfold purity" — *daśabāṇa*: *bāṇa*, *bāṇī* is a measure of the purity of gold; the rendering is tentative. "Golden earrings" — *svarṇakāpa*, according to the edition's gloss, "an ear ornament".
[^59]: Taras (*tāṛa*) — bracelets worn on the upper arm; "armlets" — *bājubandha* (note 29); "bracelets" — *bālā*; "rings" — *mudarī*, according to the edition's gloss, "ring".
[^60]: "Veil" — *uṛayāni*, an upper wrap or veil. "Shapely" — *suṭhāni*.
[^61]: Pashulis (*pāśuli*) — foot ornaments, apparently toe-rings.
[^62]: "Her friends" — *sakhāgaṇe*, lit. "friends"; garbled in the OCR, read from the scan.
[^63]: "Of the pata line" — *pāṭa vaṃśī*: so in the scan (enlarged) and in the 1997 edition; the meaning is unclear: possibly "of the chief (noble) line" (*pāṭa* — "throne, chief seat") or the name of a family. Ichchha-dei, Rasikananda's wife, is called "Ichchha" in the "Bhakti-ratnakara" as well (taranga 15). That she is a portion of Lakshmi and Rasika's eternal beloved birth after birth is the author's view, as is the comparison of the couple with Lakshmi and Narayana (141); cf. note 43.
[^64]: "Varana" — *baraṇa*: here the rite of honourably receiving the bride in the pavilion (cf. 115).
[^65]: "Gave the bride" (140) — *kanyā samarpila*, the kanyadana. "Joining their hands" — *hāta joṛa*, the rite of joining the hands of bride and bridegroom. "The portion and [He who] possesses the portions" — *aṃśī aṃśe*: Lakshmi is Narayana's energy and Narayana the source; a rapturous comparison by the onlookers, cf. notes 43, 63.
[^66]: "He" — apparently Sadashiva, who gives the bride away in place of his departed brother. "Dowry" — *yautuka*. "Loads" — *bhāra*, lit. "burdens".
[^67]: Ashtamangala (*aṣṭa-maṅgala*) — the wedding rite of the eighth day, after which the couple returns to the bridegroom's house. "Departed" — *bije karila*, the honorific "was pleased to set out".
[^68]: "In seclusion" — *nigame*: so in the scan and in the 1997 edition (also lahari 13, 3, 10); the meaning is taken from the context.
[^69]: The mahashaya — here Achyuta. Six tastes — lahari 3, note 13.
[^70]: "The friend of all, hard to attain" — *sabāra durlabha bandhu*; possibly also "the rare friend of all".
[^71]: The author's signature is rendered by the standard formula; here the verb is *kautuke racilā*, "joyfully (with delight) composed".
