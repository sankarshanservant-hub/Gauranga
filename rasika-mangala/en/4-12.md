# Uttara-vibhaga. Lahari 12. The Deliverance of the Lord of the Kols

*A song in glory of Shyamananda; year after year Rasika travels through every land and floods gods and men with love for Krishna; on the way to Nagpur, going through the forest, he and his companions come into the domain of the cruel lord of the Kols, who with a huge host of Kols slays brahmanas, sannyasis, kings and subjects; scouts tell the lord that a wealthy rajaguru is coming with loads of silk and jewels, and he orders the roads blocked, the travellers surrounded with seven rings, and all of them killed at night and their goods taken; Rasika seats his companions around his palanquin and bids them chant the Hari-nama; at night four invisible great beings come to the lord, beat him and bid him fall at Rasika's feet and accept initiation into the Hari-nama; bleeding, he tells this to his kin, comes to Rasika with his whole clan and falls at his feet; Rasika embraces him, he gains divine knowledge and offers praise; Rasika initiates his clan into the Hari-nama, and all sing kirtan and dance; the lord feasts Rasika for several days, gives him gifts and escorts him through the forest; the forest tribes who sacrificed brahmanas to their goddess give up their wicked deeds, build temples and install Deities; Rasika enters Nagpur, and thousands become his disciples*

*The twelfth lahari.*

*Raga Varadi.*[^1]
*Ghosha: "[My] life is Radhanatha, O, [my] soul is Gopinatha."*

**1.** Glory, glory to Shyamananda, the life of the world,
whose feet's servant is Achyuta's son!

**2.** Thus, in [all his] array, Rasikendra year after year
travels through every land at his own pleasure.

**3.** Nothing has remained [undone] in the deliverance of living beings:
he has flooded gods and men with love for Krishna.[^2]

**4.** At this time he went for some days to Nagpur;
I shall tell of it — listen eagerly.

**5.** He goes through the midst of the forest with his companions;
unexpectedly he arrived in the land of a villain.[^3]

**6.** The lord of the Kols is very wicked [and] evil;
he slays brahmanas, sannyasis, kings [and] subjects.

**7.** Twenty kahans of Kols go about with him;
whomever he finds there he kills with great delight.

**8.** At his name the forest country trembles;
before him there is no escape for anyone.[^4]

**9.** Rasikendra arrived in his domain;
before him the scouts said:

**10.** "With him are hundreds upon hundreds of sadhus, loads of all kinds of goods;
silk, silk cloth, bhot — with him, load upon load.

**11.** He is a great rajaguru in Orissa;
all kinds of jewels, diamonds and gems go with him."

**12.** With these words the scouts persuaded the villain;
hearing it, the villain joyfully says to his own men:[^5]

**13.** "All of you, block the roads and the crossings and hold him;
at night I will take all the goods [and] slaughter [them].

**14.** Detain them all quickly,
so that not one of them manages to flee."

**15.** Receiving the order, hundreds upon hundreds of men went in;
surrounding [the travellers] with seven rings, all kept awake.[^6]

**16.** Rasikendra heard the whole account;
joyfully he spoke to his companions:

**17.** "All of you, sit down around me;
do not go anywhere, remember Krishna."

**18.** Taking their seats, all sit on [all] four sides;
terror seized all, a great anxiety.

**19.** All say: "Today we have all lost our lives!
In the land of this asura there is no escape for anyone."

**20.** In the Prabhu's heart some unease arose;
he told everyone: "Chant the Hari-nama."[^7]

**21.** In the middle, in the palanquin, the Prabhu himself sat,
[and all] around on [all] four sides — like the stars surrounding the moon.

**22.** Again and again they sound the mantra of the Hari-nama;
hearing [this], the men and women were distraught:

**23.** "That Prabhu whose commands are carried out by tigers [and] elephants —
that Prabhu the villain will come and bind!

**24.** That Prabhu whose commands are carried out by gods, asuras [and] men —
that Prabhu has come and fallen into the power of an asura!"[^8]

**25.** Women, the old, children [and] youths were distraught;
they remember Krishna, anxious at heart.

**26.** Seated, all joyfully chant the Hari-nama;
a watch of the night passed — [they] did not drink [even] water.[^9]

**27.** In his settlement the wicked lord was sitting;
unexpectedly four great beings entered.

**28.** Seizing the villain by the hair, they beat [him] —
with all the punishments there are in the Creator's creation.[^10]

**29.** His life rose to his throat; he cries out in terror;
he sees no one — no one is with him.

**30.** He only hears words with his own ears:
"You [meant to] kill Rasika-shekhara!

**31.** [Him,] in fear of whom gods [and] asuras tremble,
whom the people of the three worlds serve, ever [remaining] with him;

**32.** the Prabhu who gave bhakti — love for Krishna,
who expounded bhakti hidden in the Vedas;

**33.** whose glory is renowned among the people of the three worlds,
the giver of prema-bhakti to the poor [and] lowly, down to the chandalas —

**34.** at the feet of such a Prabhu you have committed an offence!
Boundless are [his] beauty [and his] qualities, fathomless his greatness.[^11]

**35.** Now, evil one, go [and] fall at [his] feet;
for that reason we have spared your life.

**36.** At the Prabhu's place, all of you become servants of Krishna;
taking initiation into the Hari-nama, become [his] followers."

**37.** As soon as he heard these words with his own ears,
[he was] beaten with all [the punishments] the Creator made.

**38.** Falling to the ground, the wicked evil one cries out;
blood flows ceaselessly over his body [and] from his mouth.[^12]

**39.** Kinsmen, subjects and others, wives [and] sons
came and surrounded [him], all amazed at heart.

**40.** He has no strength to speak — he speaks by signs of the hand:
"I do not see [them], yet they beat [and] beat, taking my life."[^13]

**41.** Regaining consciousness for a moment, he says to all:
"Many punishments [they] inflicted [on me]; my life burns with pain.

**42.** All that they said I heard with my own ears:
Rasika-Murari Dasa is a second Narayana."[^14]

**43.** And all the rest about [his] greatness he told his household:
[that] the three worlds serve Rasika's feet.

**44.** "At such feet I committed an offence;
come, all, let us fall at the Prabhu's lotus feet.

**45.** Not knowing, I committed an offence at those feet —
[the feet] of Rasika-Murari Dasa, the second Narayana."

**46.** Thinking thus, the wicked one went to Rasika;
coming with his whole clan, he fell at [his] feet.

**47.** All around burn ten or twenty torches;
in the bliss of prema [Rasika] is seated, full of attachment to Krishna.

**48.** At that time all say to Rasika:
"The lord of the village has come [and] fallen at [your] feet."

**49.** That greatly fortunate one prostrated himself many times;
[Rasika], rising, embraced him with attachment to Krishna.[^15]

**50.** By Rasika's touch divine knowledge came to him;
clasping [his] feet, that fortunate one weeps:

**51.** "Not knowing, in my foolishness I committed an offence;
I am a great sinner, you are a fathomless [ocean] of mercy.

**52.** You, Prabhu, are a sure refuge, the possessor of all virtues;
boundless [are your] beauty [and] virtues, you are an ocean of compassion.

**53.** With my whole clan I have entered the shelter of your feet;
granting devotion to Krishna, Prabhu, protect [us].

**54.** You are the lord of the three worlds, the friend of all;
you are the soul of all people, an ocean of mercy."[^16]

**55.** With many [words] the lord of the kingdom offered praise;
hearing the praise, Shyamapati was pleased.[^17]

**56.** Granting the Hari-nama, [Rasika] made him a disciple with his whole clan;
all, joyful, danced in the kirtan.

**57.** For five or seven days [the lord] kept [him], falling at [his] feet,
feeding [him] with the nectar of six tastes and more.

**58.** He took leave of the Prabhu, offering many gifts,
[and], going [with him] with his whole clan, led [him] across the forest.[^18]

**59.** Thus hundreds of thousands of wicked ones, by the touch of [his] feet,
giving up their wicked deeds, float in the bliss of prema.

**60.** All the low-born, as many as there are in the forest land,
who killed brahmanas for the worship of their chosen goddess —

**61.** the ignorance of all went away by the sight of Rasika;
all people serve the guru, Krishna, the sadhus [and] the brahmanas.

**62.** All built temples, [installed] Sri-murtis and the like;
all began to dance in the sweetness of sankirtana.

**63.** All the forest land sways in love for Krishna;
all became firmly settled [in this, that] Krishna is their own Lord.[^19]

**64.** Rasika-shekhara entered Nagpur;
thousands upon thousands became [his] disciples [and] followers.

**65.** King [and] subjects — all were won over by love for Krishna;
day after day [he] held sankirtana in every house.

**66.** All became intoxicated with the sweetness of sankirtana;
all float in the sweetness of prema, finding no end [to it].

**67.** All the pastimes he performed on the circle of the earth,
all the manifestations he showed with delight —

**68.** if one speaks [of them], not even a small part of their account is told.
Listen to the "Rasika-mangala," all friends.

**69.** Without delay will obtain the treasure of prema-bhakti for Krishna
whoever hears this with faith.[^20]

**70.** Adorning himself with Shyamananda's feet,
the son of Rasamaya joyfully composed [this].

*Thus in the Uttara-vibhaga of the "Sri Rasika-mangala" ends the twelfth lahari, called "The Deliverance of the Lord of the Kols."*[^21]

[^2]: "The life of the world" (1) — *jagatajībana*. "In [all his] array" (2) — *hena sāje*: with companions, music and festivals, as described in lahari 11. "At his own pleasure" — *icchāsukhe*. "Nothing has remained [undone]" (3) — *abaśeṣa nāi kichu*.
[^3]: Nagpur (4) — apparently Chota Nagpur, the forest plateau west of Medinipur (Dakshina, lahari 2, note 29). "For some days" — *kata dina*. "Eagerly" — *kutūhale*. "In the land of a villain" (5) — *khaṇṭera bhubane*: the editor glosses "খণ্ট—দুষ্ট," "wicked."
[^4]: The Kols (*kola*; the editor glosses "কোল—জাতি," "a people, a tribe") — the forest tribes of Chota Nagpur and the neighbouring lands (Munda, Ho and others). "The lord of the Kols" (6) — *kolā adhipati*; his name is not given. "Brahmanas, sannyasis" — *dbijanyāsī* (so on the scan, cut-out ×2). "Twenty kahans" (7) — *biṃśati kāhana*: a kahan is a count of 1280 (16 pans of 80), i.e. about twenty-five thousand — apparently hyperbole: "a countless host." "With great delight" — *mahāraṅge*. "Trembles" (8) — *kampamān*.
[^5]: "In his domain" (9) — *tā'ra nijasthāne*: in the domain of the lord of the Kols. "Scouts" — *dūtagaṇa*. "Silk, silk cloth, bhot" (10) — *pāṭa paṭṭāmbara bhoṭa*: *bhoṭa* — woollen cloth or blankets from Bhot (Tibet, Bhutan). "Load upon load" — *bhāre bhāra*. "Rajaguru" (11) — *rājaguru*, preceptor of kings. "Persuaded" (12) — *bujhāila*.
[^6]: "Block the roads and the crossings" (13) — *patha ghāṭa rundhe*; 13 — on the scan ॥ ১৩ ॥ (the OCR has ১৪, a false repetition). "Slaughter" — *kariba saṃhāre*. "Manages to flee" (14) — *palā'bāra tare*. "With seven rings" (15) — *sātapurā*: the editor glosses "সাতপুরা—সাতবেড়া," "seven cordons." "Went in" — *prabeśilā*.
[^7]: "Sit down" (17) — *karaha āsana*. "Asura" (19) — the lord of the Kols. "Some unease" (20) — *kichu saṅkoca*, lit. "some constraint": understood as concern for his companions.
[^8]: "Like the stars surrounding the moon" (21) — *candra beṛi' … yena tārāgaṇe*. "The men and women" (22) — *naranārīgaṇa*: apparently Rasika's companions (cf. 25). 23–24 are understood as their sorrowful words; they may also be taken as the author's. "Fallen into the power of an asura" (24) — *ṭhekila … asuragocara*. The praise of Rasika, whose commands beasts, gods and men obey, is the author's enthusiastic praise (lahari 11, 73–74 and notes 17, 25 there; lahari 10, note 14); on the author's attitude to Shyamananda and Rasika — Purva, lahari 1, note 2; Purva, lahari 14, note 11.
[^9]: "Women" (25) — *strīri* (so on the scan). "A watch of the night" (26) — *praharek*: a prahara is about three hours. "Did not drink [even] water" — *nāhi jala pāna*.
[^10]: "In his settlement" (27) — *nigame* (so on the scan, cut-out ×2; the sense is unclear): *nigama* — "town, market settlement"; understood as the lord's seat. "Four great beings" — *cāri mahāmati*, lit. "four of great mind": beings who are invisible (29); the author does not name them. Cf. lahari 10, 54: "in secret the hosts of the gods stay with Rasika." "With all the punishments there are in the Creator's creation" (28) — *yata daṇḍa āchaẏe bidhātāra sṛjana*; likewise 37: *bidhātā sṛjana yata*.
[^11]: "His life rose to his throat" (29) — *kaṇṭhagata haila prāṇa*. "You [meant to] kill" (30) — *karibi nidhane*, lit. "you will kill." 31–34 are the words of the invisible beings: enthusiastic praise of Rasika; see note 8. "Ever [remaining] with him" (31) — *saṅge nirantara*. "Hidden in the Vedas" (32) — *bedagopya*. "Down to the chandalas" (33) — *ācaṇḍāla*. "Offence" (34) — *aparādha*: an offence against a Vaishnava.
[^12]: "For that reason we have spared your life" (35) — *se kāraṇe tomāra rākhinu nija prāṇe*. "Servants of Krishna" (36) — *kṛṣṇera kiṅkara*. "Initiation into the Hari-nama" — *harināma dīkṣā*. "Followers" — *anucara*. "Blood" (38) — *rudhira* (so on the scan; the OCR is garbled).
[^13]: "Wives" (39) — *stīri* (so on the scan; = *strī*, cf. note 9). "By signs of the hand" (40) — *hāta ṭhāri* (so on the scan; the OCR is garbled). "I do not see [them]" — the beaters are invisible (29; note 10). "Punishments" (41) — *daṇḍa* (cf. 28, 37). "My life burns" — *prāṇa dahe*.
[^14]: "Rasika-Murari Dasa" (42, 45) — *rasikamurāri dāsa*: Rasika; *dāsa* is part of the name ("servant") or "servant [of Krishna]." "A second Narayana" — *dbitī nārāẏaṇa*: so the invisible ones said (30–36), and the lord of the Kols repeats it; cf. lahari 10, 36 and note 14 there; lahari 13, 3, 19. Rapturous praise; see note 8. "His household" (43) — *parijane*. "Let us fall" (44) — *paṛimu*.
[^15]: "Torches" (47) — *deuṭī*. "The lord of the village" (48) — *grāma-adhipati* (so on the scan; the OCR is garbled): the lord of the Kols (6); in 55, "the lord of the kingdom" (*rājya-adhipati*). "That greatly fortunate one" (49) — *sei mahābhāge*. "Prostrated himself" — *daṇḍabata kailā*. "Embraced" — *kola dila*.
[^16]: "In my foolishness" (51) — *mūṛhapaṇe*. "A sure refuge" (52) — *śaraṇapañjara*, literally "a sheltering cage." "Have entered the shelter" (53) — *paśinu śaraṇa*. The lord's praise (52–54: "lord of the three worlds," "the soul of all people") is the rapturous praise of a convert; see note 8.
[^17]: "The lord of the kingdom" (55) — *rājya-adhipati*, the same lord of the Kols. Shyamapati (*śyāmāpati*, so on the scan, crop ×2) — "the lord (husband) of Shyama": apparently Rasika as the husband of Shyamadasi (Dakshina, lahari 1); the epithet is unusual in the poem.
[^18]: "Kept [him]" (57) — *rākhe*. "The nectar of six tastes" — *ṣaṛarasa amṛtādi* (so on the scan; the OCR is garbled). "Took leave" (58) — *bidāẏa kailā*. "Led [him] across the forest" — *araṇya karilā pāra*, literally "brought across the forest" (so on the scan; the OCR is garbled).
[^19]: "The low-born" (60) — *antyajāti*. "Their chosen goddess" — *iṣṭadebī*: apparently a local goddess of the forest tribes; "killed brahmanas" — *brāhmaṇahiṃsana*: according to the author, for her worship (cf. 6: the lord of the Kols kills brahmanas). "Ignorance" (61) — *abidyā*. "Built temples, [installed] Sri-murtis" (62) — *debālaẏa śrīmūrttyādi … karilā*. "Sways" (63) — *ḍhalāḍhali*. "Became firmly settled" — *dārḍhya karila* (so on the scan; the OCR is garbled).
[^20]: Nagpur (64) — Chota Nagpur (note 3). "Were won over" (65) — *baśa haila*. "Finding no end [to it]" (66) — *nāhi pāẏa ora*. "Manifestations" (67) — *parakāśa*. "A small part of their account" (68) — *kichu bibaraṇa*. "With faith" (69) — *śraddhā kari'*.
