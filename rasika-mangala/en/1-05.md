# Purva-vibhaga. Lahari 5. The Name-Giving

*A song in glory of Shyamananda and Rasikananda; the name-giving rite: the astrologers, the horoscope and the name Rasika, and, at Achyuta's request, Murari; Rasika's infant play and ornaments; hearing songs about Krishna the infant weeps, and the household, suspecting the evil eye, calls in exorcists; the blessings of the brahmanas*

*The fifth lahari.*

*Raga Shri — panchali metre.*[^1]

**1.** Glory, glory to Shyamananda! Glory to the moon Rasikendra!
Glory, glory [to him whose] glory is fathomless!
Be so merciful to me that your virtues may arise [in me] —
[that I may] compose [a song of] Rasika's fair fame.

**2.** Thus day by day he grew very strong —
the time for the name-giving came.
Bringing brahmana astrologers, Rasika's father and mother
at an auspicious moment give [him] a name.[^2]

**3.** All the astrologers, gathering, draw their chalk lines at an auspicious moment,
marking out on the ground the houses [of the horoscope].[^3]
Brahmanas versed in the Vedas ceaselessly intone [mantras];
some sing the Sama[-veda].

**4.** Vina, venu, various instruments; the sound of rabab and murali,
mridanga, mandira, karatalas;
dhol, dhak and all the rest — all sound wildly;
all the goyalas (cowherds) dance with delight.[^4]

**5.** Thus in various merriments, all in great joy,
they spend days and nights in dancing and singing.
The brahmanas perform homa according to the Vedic rule,
as if having taken counsel [together].[^5]

**6.** The women, with cries of victory, all the various family customs —
they performed them all.
Bhavani, having bathed, put on divine garments,
having bowed to the elders.

**7.** Having finished the boy's bath, having performed all the auspicious rites,
she sat down, taking the son on her lap.
Manuscripts of the Gita and the Bhagavatam the brahmanas and sannyasis read there;
there are discourses on the Bharata and the Puranas.

**8.** Placing the son in the middle, all sat on the four sides,
pronouncing Vedic mantras.
Endowed with all auspicious signs, the horoscope is most wondrous:
everyone is amazed.[^6]

**9.** Rashi — Vishakha, Tula; the principal name — Sri Rasika:
[this] they quickly wrote in the birth record.
The brahmana astrologers, having calculated, glad at heart,
say: "The horoscope is the most excellent of all."[^7]

**10.** The brahmanas versed in the Vedas again and again
all bless Achyuta:
"O, your son is the treasure of life of the world:
he will give prema-bhakti even to the chandalas.

**11.** His signs cannot be told even with a hundred mouths:
fathomless, boundless [is his] glory.
To prema-bhakti, to sankirtana he will lead all people;
its limit cannot be told."

**12.** Thus the brahmanas, having praised that son,
began to leave for their own places.
Achyuta, joining his palms, says: "Listen, O best of the twice-born,
I make one request.

**13.** The principal name is Sri Rasika, as the birth horoscope attests;
it will be known in the world.
[But] in my heart [there is] a wish — fulfil my hope:
let all people call [him] Murari.

**14.** Incomparable in all the shastras is the name Sri Murari Dasa:
let the whole world call [him] so."
The brahmanas, hearing [these] words and accepting this name as true,
went each to his own home.[^8]

**15.** The name Rasika-Murari was established:
[such was] the auspicious moment written by the Creator.
Taking the boy on her lap, joyfully within the house
[the mother] converses with everyone.

**16.** Everyone, according to the rules and in due order, he honoured with worship and obeisances,
and according to his means [rewarded and] took leave of them.
Seeing his son's face, he knows no sorrow at all:
the Mahashaya floats in joy.

**17.** Thus after some days, heaving himself on knees and chest,
he plays on the bed.
Day by day he moves about the house on his knees:
Rasika-shekhara crawls on all fours.

**18.** Wherever he finds some object, he breaks it and throws it right there;
he mixes curd, milk and ghee in one place.
Breaking the pots, to his heart's content he eats some and smears himself with some;
neither snake nor fire does he fear in the least.

**19.** Thorns, stones and the like — he regards them all alike;
enemy and friend he does not distinguish.
Night and day, at play, he roams the house and courtyard;
of good and bad he knows nothing.

**20.** Dust and mud he gleefully smears over his body —
[on him] they shine like aguru and sandal.
How sweet that smile! His lovely face defeats the moon;
those two eyes are very long.

**21.** At his waist a belt of little bells is fitting, on his neck fine pearls shine,
on his hands golden bracelets glitter;
on both arms two armlets, made of gold;
on his chest an ornament — a tiger's claw.[^9]

**22.** On his feet, jewelled anklets: their beauty cannot be told;
you look — as if [you saw] the form of Gopala.
On his head a lovely garland; with it he looks so tender:
that majesty cannot be told.

**23.** Thus crawling, now rising, now falling,
he constantly roams the courtyard.
Father and mother, seeing his face, cannot contain the joy in their chests;
shaking off the dust, they take him in their arms and carry him away.

**24.** Washing his body with scented water,
the mother gives him milk to drink.
Joyfully she sits on the swing, taking her son on her lap,
thinking [it is time] for sleep.

**25.** The fair fame and deeds of Krishna that fortunate [mother] sings,
saying: "So that [my] child may fall asleep."
Hearing Krishna's name, Rasika cannot contain himself:
he started up, crying aloud.

**26.** Sweat, trembling, a faltering voice, the hairs on all his limbs standing on end;
from his eyes streams of tears flow.
Sobbing, he weeps in the bliss of prema [at the song] of Krishna's fame:
where has his sleep gone![^10]

**27.** Hearing her son cry, the grieved mother
gives him the breast again and again.
More and more she sings, so that her son may sleep —
hearing the song, [he] weeps twice as hard.

**28.** Whatever means she tries to break that weeping —
plaintively [she sings] Krishna's name.[^11]
The fortunate [mother] summoned a few young women
and told them all: "Sing!"

**29.** A few women, gathering, sing with zest —
lovely and charming to hear.[^12]
Lamenting loudly, Rasika-Murari weeps:
his whole body is drenched through with streams [of tears].

**30.** [He is full of] longing under the weight of prema; when love for Krishna overflows,
he constantly floats in that rasa of prema.
As soon as he hears Krishna's name, [his] body is all full of prema for Krishna;
hearing Krishna's qualities, [he] exults.

**31.** All take [him] from his mother's lap, each in his own way,
yet Achyuta's son still weeps.
All say, guessing: "We know what this is:
some wicked person has cast the evil eye on him at some time."

**32.** Someone ties a protective charm on his head; with various mantras, in various ways,
all the ojhas (exorcists) began to sweep [the evil] off him.[^13]
Hearing the weeping, the mother — the poor soul's heart breaks with anxiety —
vows various pujas to the gods.

**33.** All those who are singing — [they sing] streams of Sri Krishna's fair fame:
by whose word, then, will the weeping stop?
In prema at moments he becomes stunned (stambha), meditating on Krishna's qualities;
with humility and shyness he looks at everyone.

**34.** Then, after a while, the tired women fell silent —
and then Prabhu stops weeping.
[Showing] his own childlike nature, restraining all [the feelings of] bhava,
he sucks the breast on his mother's lap.

**35.** The mother, joyful, thinking her son calmed,
treats the brahmanas to sweets.
The host of the twice-born blesses: "May the son remain free from obstacles:
this boy is under Krishna's shelter."

**36.** Hear the Rasika-mangala, all friends, people of Krishna —
the account of Rasika's childhood.
Adorning himself with Shyamananda's feet,
the son of Rasamaya joyfully composed [this].[^14]

*Thus in the Purva-vibhaga of the Sri Rasika-mangala ends the fifth lahari, called "The Name-Giving".*

[^1]: Raga Shri (*śrī*) is one of the six principal ragas. "Panchali metre" (*pāñcālī-chanda*) here is the tripadi: each line has three parts (two short rhyming ones and a long one); the edition sets each stanza in four lines, and so it is translated. No ghosha (refrain) is given. The stanzas are numbered as in the edition (1–36).
[^2]: The name-giving (*nāma-karaṇa*) is one of the purificatory rites (samskaras) of childhood. Astrologers — *daivajña*; the scan has the misprint "দোইবজ্ঞ" (below, in 9, "দৈবজ্ঞ").
[^3]: "Astrologers" — *khaṛikāra*, lit. "those who work with chalk": the horoscope was calculated by drawing the squares of the "houses" in chalk (*khaṛi*) on the ground or on a board. For the line "marking out on the ground the houses" the edition gives in a footnote the variant "installing the auspicious pot" (*śubha ghaṭa kariyā sthāpana*).
[^4]: The vina is a stringed instrument; venu, rabab, murali — see lahari 4, note 19; the mandira are small cymbals; the dhol and dhak are large drums. Goyalas (*goyāla*) are cowherds, milkmen; the rejoicing recalls Nanda's festival at Krishna's birth (Bhag. 10.5).
[^5]: "As if having taken counsel [together]" — *kāro yena kariyā yukati*; the reading on the scan is clear, the sense conjectural (lit. "as if having consulted someone").
[^6]: "Horoscope" — *koṭī*; so on the scan (and in the 1997 ed.), understood as *koṣṭhī*, "horoscope" (cf. 9, 13); "millions [of signs]" is also possible.
[^7]: The rashi is the sign of the Moon at birth: the Moon in Vishakha, in Tula (Libra), as in lahari 4, 39. "Principal name" (*mūla nāma*) — the name according to the birth sign; in one system names for the sign Tula begin with the syllable "ra" — hence "Rasika." Birth record — *jāta-patra*.
[^8]: Murari ("the enemy of [the demon] Mura") is a name of Krishna; "Murari Dasa" means "servant of Murari." Hence the double name Rasika-Murari, by which Rasikananda is also known in BR 15. In 15 the scan has the misprint "মুবারি" for "মুরারি".
[^9]: A tiger's claw (*vyāghra-nakha*) set in gold is a traditional amulet for children; in Vaishnava poetry the infant Krishna wears one too. "Belt of little bells" — *kiṅkiṇī*; "armlets" — *tāṛ*.
[^10]: "Sobbing" — *usaśī usaśī*; the reading on the scan is clear, the word is not found in dictionaries and is understood from the context (an onomatopoeia for sobbing).
[^11]: The line is understood tentatively: *karuṇā kariyā kṛṣṇa-nāma*, lit. "having made Krishna's name plaintively," i.e. the mother, trying to stop the crying, herself sings Krishna's name, which only makes the crying worse (cf. 27). "Entreating [by] Krishna" is also possible.
[^12]: "Lovely" — *suśaṅka*, as in lahari 4, 58 (note 13 there); the reading on the scan is clear, the sense from the context.
[^13]: The evil eye (lit. "a wicked person saw") is the folk explanation of an infant's crying. Protective charm — *rakṣā*, a protective thread or amulet. An ojha (*ojhā*) is a village exorcist-healer; "sweep off" — *jhāṛite*: to drive out the evil with mantras, brushing the patient with a twig or the edge of a cloth. The author shows that the household does not understand the ecstatic symptoms of prema in the infant.
[^14]: The author's signature is given by the common formula; here it is in the tripadi metre and reads slightly differently: "Making the holy feet of Shyamananda an ornament on his head, the son of Rasamaya sings."
