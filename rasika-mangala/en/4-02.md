# Uttara-vibhaga. Lahari 2. The Determination of the Annual Festivals

*A song in glory of Shyamananda; in Dharenda Rasika, with astrologer brahmanas and his kinsmen, establishes the annual festivals: twelve days of Shyamananda's aradhana after the full moon of the Bathing of the Deity, a festival for the Ratha-yatra, Hera-panchami and the appearance of Sri Chaitanya, for which he himself begs alms of cowries and handfuls of rice, five days of the aradhana of Subala-chandra in the month of gamha, the birth of Krishna, the birth of Thakurani — Radha, Kumara-purnima, the rasa festival on the full moon after Utthana-ekadashi and eight days in memory of Hridayananda before Dola-purnima — thirty festivals in all; Rasika wanders with hundreds of sadhus, mahantas, Rasamaya's community, Thakura Tulasi and bhattacharyas, crowds stream to see him, and in the houses where he stays provisions appear from no one knows where; for the festival days he returns home; after Shyamananda's festival at Govindapur he announces to the community Shyamananda's order to settle the three mothers, the Thakuranis, with the Deities in one house at Shyamasundarpur; Shyamapriya agrees to follow Rasika, and he remains in that village*

*The second lahari.*

*Raga Shri.*[^1]
*Ghosha: "O Hari, this time be merciful to me:*
*I hope to obtain the shade of Your feet."*

**1.** Glory, glory to Shyamananda, the ocean of mercy,
at whose feet the servant is Rasika-shekhara![^2]

**2.** At that time in Dharenda the excellent Rasikendra
established festivals for every year.[^3]

**3.** Taking the astrologer brahmanas [and] his kinsmen,
he sat [with them] and determined on which lunar day which festival [should be held].[^4]

**4.** The festival of Shyamananda's aradhana —
at the close of the full moon of the Bathing of the Deity, on the first lunar day.

**5.** Sometimes in Jyeshtha, sometimes in the month of Ashadha,
he firmly established twelve festivals.[^5]

**6.** At Sri Ratha-yatra, on the day of Hera-panchami,
on the day of the appearance of Sri Chaitanya Mahaprabhu —

**7.** for this he firmly established one festival:
begging alms of cowries [and] handfuls, he holds the festival.[^6]

**8.** He himself begs cowries [and] handfuls from house to house;
hearing [of it], all rejoice, charmed in their hearts.

**9.** One person brings hundreds of loads of provisions:
Rasika's lila astonishes the three worlds.[^7]

**10.** On the thirteenth day of the bright half [before] the gamha full moon —
on that day is the aradhana of Subala-chandra.[^8]

**11.** Five festivals he firmly established on that day;
without cease Prabhu floats in the rasa of festivals.[^9]

**12.** In the month of Bhadra, on the day of the constellation of Krishna's birth,
he firmly established one festival on that day.

**13.** The birth of the Thakurani — on the eighth day of the bright half of Bhadra:
for this he openly established one festival.[^10]

**14.** On the day of Kumara-purnima — one festival:
Rasika-chand holds [it] by his own inspiration.[^11]

**15.** On the full moon after Utthana-ekadashi
he firmly established the festival of the rasa on that day.[^12]

**16.** On the twelfth day of the bright half [before] Dola-purnima
he carefully established the festival of Hridayananda.

**17.** Eight festivals he firmly established on that day;
every year the son of Achyuta holds [them].[^13]

**18.** He chose these thirty festivals:
every day with Rasika, life is a festival.

**19.** Every year thus he plays:
Achyuta's boy is intoxicated with the rasa of festivals.[^14]

**20.** For the sake of the festivals he wanders from land to land;
hundreds and hundreds of sadhus are always with him.

**21.** Ten or twenty mahantas [are with him] day and night;
with him are Rasamaya's kin [and] Thakura Tulasi.[^15]

**22.** All perform sankirtana in great joy
with Rasika night and day, without pause.

**23.** All the singers and musicians there are on earth
constantly roam with Rasika.[^16]

**24.** Five or ten bhattacharyas [are with him] at every moment,
[to] seek talks of Krishna with Rasika.[^17]

**25.** In a divine palanquin sits the abode of all virtues;
he reads the Bhagavata, always [chants] the Harinama.[^18]

**26.** Ceaselessly streams of tears flow from [his] holy eyes;
seeing [his] form, all people float in bliss.

**27.** Hearing those sweet words, all in bliss
forget themselves, gazing at Sri Rasikananda.

**28.** North, south, east, west — always,
always Prabhu Rasikendra-raya plays.[^19]

**29.** Thousands and thousands of people come to see [him],
raising a clamour of the cry "Hari!" on every road.

**30.** Women, the old, children, youths run in joy
to see Prabhu Rasikendra, the crest-jewel [of devotees].

**31.** Pregnant women — all run with faces raised;
in the joy of seeing [him] they do not tidy their hair.[^20]

**32.** With tears of bliss they go, with longing hearts;
their voices choke with prema, pulaka on their bodies.

**33.** When they see the moon of [his] face, the darkness is dispelled;
the son of Achyuta steals the hearts of all.

**34.** Thus Rasikendra conquers the quarters of the world;
at [his] darshana the hosts of gods and asuras rejoice.[^21]

**35.** Be it a king, be it a subject, be it the saints —
[whoever] has Prabhu, the son of Achyuta, staying in his house,

**36.** seeing [his] moon-face, gains as it were ten million jewels;
even if he is poor, he gains the happiness of great bliss.

**37.** The eight siddhis [and] the nine treasures appear in his house:
all these constantly attend on Rasika.[^22]

**38.** [He] in whose house there is not even one ser of rice —
when Rasikendra comes to his dwelling,

**39.** [he] with a pure heart feeds thousands and thousands of sadhus
with fried delicacies, sweets, curd, ghee [and] panchamrita.

**40.** Where the provisions come from no one knows:
such is the wondrous lila of the son of Achyuta.[^23]

**41.** He always travels from land to land;
for the six seasons, the twelve months, he does not stay at home.

**42.** In whatever month falls the lunar day of anyone's aradhana —
on that day Prabhu goes home.[^24]

**43.** For the adhivasa of the festival he arrives;
having completed the festival, he again wanders from land to land.[^25]

**44.** Again the festival-aradhana of Shyamananda
the son of Achyuta held in Govindapur.

**45.** Two [festivals] of twelve [days] took place in that village.
Having completed the festival, all sat in assembly:

**46.** the host of mahantas and the Shyamanandis,
kings, subjects, mahajanas, Vaishnavas, brahmanas.[^26]

**47.** To all Prabhu Rasika-shekhara says:
"Shyamananda's order has been made unalterable for me:

**48.** 'Your three mothers you shall settle in one house;
if [they] live apart, [they] will not be honoured [as they should].[^27]

**49.** Vrindavanachandra [and] Vrajamohana Thakura
you shall ceremonially bring to Sri Shyamasundarpur.[^28]

**50.** All will serve together in that place' —
three times he ordered me in the depth of my heart.

**51.** 'In three places you will not be able to serve the saints' —
this order Prabhu gave me in person.[^29]

**52.** So that Shyamananda's order is not transgressed,
all [of you] make a request to the Thakurani.

**53.** Shyamapriya Thakurani [and] Vrindavanachandra
will go there — [such is] Shyamananda's order.

**54.** Then I shall go [and] bring Yamuna Thakurani:
in one house the three Thakuranis will live."[^30]

**55.** Hearing Rasika's words, all joyfully
firmly accepted those words in their hearts.

**56.** All went to the Thakurani
[and] conveyed the whole matter in Rasika's words.

**57.** Hearing everyone's words, mother Shyamapriya [said]:
"Whatever [gives] Rasika happiness, that is fitting for me.

**58.** He is the shining moon of the lineage of Shyamananda's community;
and all authority Shyamananda gave [him].[^31]

**59.** How shall I break his word?
Come, I shall go to that place at Rasika's word.

**60.** He is father, he is son: whom else do I have?
Wherever he takes [me], there I shall go, following behind him."[^32]

**61.** Hearing [this], Rasika was very glad;
having taken leave of all, he stayed in that village.

**62.** Of [that] journey I shall tell [later];
listen, all, attentively, with joyful hearts.[^33]

**63.** Listen to the Rasika-mangala, all friends:
without delay you will obtain the treasure of prema-bhakti for Krishna.

**64.** Adorning himself with Shyamananda's feet,
the son of Rasamaya joyfully composed [this].

*Thus in the Uttara-vibhaga of the Sri Rasika-mangala ends the second lahari, called "The Determination of the Annual Festivals."*[^34]

[^1]: The lines before the raga are the heading of the lahari ("দ্বিতীয়-লহরী"), in italics without a number. Lahari 2 begins on pdf 180 (printed p. 136), under a rule after the colophon of lahari 1 (files RM-4-01-001-065). Raga Shri (*śrī*; Purva, lahari 5, note 1); the ghosha is the same as in Purva, laharis 4 and 15 (*tuẏā pada-chāẏā*), and its translation is repeated. There is no mangala-shloka: after the raga and ghosha the song begins with ॥ 1 ॥. The title follows the colophon on pdf 182 (printed p. 138), checked against the scan: "ইতি শ্রীরসিকমঙ্গল উত্তর-বিভাগে বার্ষিক মহোৎসব-নির্ণয়-নাম দ্বিতীয়-লহরী সম্পূর্ণা" (*bārṣika mahotsaba-nirṇaya*; on the scan "বাষিক", without the reph). Lahari 2 — 1–64 (pdf 180–182). The summary line covers the whole lahari (12–64 — from the OCR of pdf 181–182, provisionally).
[^2]: "At whose feet the servant" — *yā̃ra caraṇera bhṛtya*: Rasika is Shyamananda's disciple.
[^3]: "At that time" — *henakāle*: after the festival in memory of Damodara (lahari 1, 58–59). Dharenda — lahari 1, note 31. "The excellent Rasikendra" — *rasikendra-bara*. "Established festivals" — *mahotsaba-niṣṭhā kaila*, lit. "established a firm [order] of festivals". "For every year" — *prati sambatsara*.
[^4]: "Astrologer brahmanas" — *daibajña brāhmaṇa*: experts in the calendar who determine the lunar days (*tithi*). "His kinsmen" — *ātmagaṇa*. "Festival" — *yātrā*, a festival (often with a procession).
[^5]: Shyamananda's aradhana is the festival in memory of his departure: on the first lunar day of the dark half after the full moon of the Snana-yatra (*deba-snāna pūrṇamī*, "the full moon of the Bathing of the Deity") — the day of Shyamananda's departure (Pashchima, lahari 13, 49–50, note 23). "Sometimes in Jyeshtha, sometimes in Ashadha" — depending on the reckoning of the lunar months. "Twelve festivals" — *duẏādaśa mahotsaba*: apparently twelve days of festival (lahari 1, 3, 23, notes 3, 11): counted thus, the festivals of this lahari add up to the "thirty festivals" (18): 12 + 1 (7) + 5 (11) + 1 (12) + 1 (13) + 1 (14) + 1 (15) + 8 (17). "Firmly" — *niścite*.
[^6]: Ratha-yatra — the chariot festival of Jagannatha (month of Ashadha). Hera-panchami (*horā pañcamī*, so on the scan, crop ×2) — the fifth day after the Ratha-yatra, when Lakshmi goes out to see Jagannatha at Gundicha (CC, Madhya 14). The day of Sri Chaitanya's appearance — the full moon of the month of Phalguna (Gaura-purnima). 6–7 can be understood both as one festival for these days and as a festival for each; the count of "thirty" (note 5) implies one. "Cowries" — *kaṛā*, cowrie shells, small change; "handfuls" — *muṭhā*, a handful [of rice]: the humblest alms. "Firmly established" — *kaila niścaẏa*.
[^7]: "One person brings hundreds of loads" — *śata śata bhāra drabya āne ekajana*: apparently, by Rasika's mercy abundance comes even from a single giver; it can also be understood as "each one brings". "Astonishes the three worlds" — *camatkāra tribhubana*.
[^8]: Gamha-purnima (*gamā pūrṇimī*; the editor explains: *gamā* — Shravana) — the full moon of the month of Shravana (July–August; in Utkala *gamhā pūrṇimā* is the festival of Balarama's appearance). "The thirteenth day of the bright half" — *śukla-trayodaśī*. Subala-chandra — apparently Gauridasa Pandita (lahari 1, 44, note 22). Aradhana — a festival of remembrance (Pashchima, lahari 13, note 8).
[^9]: "Five festivals" — *pañca mahotsaba*: apparently five days of festival (note 5). "On that day" — *se dibase*. Pdf 180 (printed p. 136) ends with a complete ॥ 11 ॥; the continuation (12 onward) is on pdf 181.
[^10]: "On the day of the constellation of Krishna's birth" (12) — *kṛṣṇa-janma-nakṣatra dibase*: apparently Janmashtami — Krishna's birth under the nakshatra Rohini in the dark half of Bhadra (August–September). The Thakurani (13) is Sri Radha (so also in the summary line of the lahari): Radhashtami, the eighth day of the bright half of Bhadra. "Openly" — *bidite*, "publicly, for all to know."
[^11]: Kumara-purnima (*kumāra pūrṇimā*; the editor explains: *kojāgara*) is the Kojagari-purnima, the full moon of the month of Ashvina (September–October). Rasika-chand is Rasika. "By his own inspiration" — *ātma-anubhaba*, lit. "[by] his own experience"; the sense is approximate.
[^12]: Utthana-ekadashi is the ekadashi of the bright half of Karttika, when Vishnu awakens from sleep; the full moon following it is the Karttika-purnima, when the Rasa-yatra is celebrated (cf. Pashchima, laharis 1–5: the Great Rasa in Gopiballavpur).
[^13]: Dola-purnima (*dola pūrṇamī*) is the full moon of Phalguna (February–March), the swing festival (Dola-yatra). "The twelfth day of the bright half" — *śukla-dbādaśī*. "The festival of Hridayananda" — *hṛdaẏānandera yātrā*: apparently the festival in memory of Hridayananda, Shyamananda's guru (aradhana — Pashchima, lahari 13, note 8). "Eight festivals" (17) — *aṣṭa mahotsaba*: apparently eight days of festival (notes 5, 9); this is how the count of "thirty festivals" (18) works out. "The son of Achyuta" is Rasika.
[^14]: "Chose" (18) — *abhīṣṭa karila*, "made [them] desired, chosen." 18b — *niti rasikera sane jībana utsaba*: the sense is approximate; it can also be understood as "[for those who are] with Rasika, life is a daily festival." "Achyuta's boy" (19) — *acyutera bālā*: Rasika.
[^15]: "From land to land" (20) — *deśāntara*. "Ten or twenty" (21) — *daśa biśa*. Rasamaya's kin (*rasamaẏa goṣṭhī*) and Thakura Tulasi (Tulasidasa), the singer — Dakshina, lahari 5, note 24; Pashchima, lahari 16, note 25.
[^16]: "Singers and musicians" — *saṅgīta-sāhitya* (Pashchima, lahari 6, note 19). "Roam" — *bihare*, "stroll, spend their time."
[^17]: Bhattacharya is the title of a learned brahmana (Purva, lahari 8, note 5). "Five or ten" — *daśa pā̃ca*. "At every moment" — *anukṣaṇe*.
[^18]: "Palanquin" — *sukapāla* (so in the scan, crop ×2; = *sukhapāla*, a covered litter). "Sits" — *bije*. "The abode of all virtues" is Rasika. 25b — *bhāgabata paṛena sadāi harināma*; it can also be understood as "he always reads the Bhagavata [and] the Harinama."
[^19]: "Plays" — *khelena*: he wanders, manifesting his pastimes (cf. 19). Rasikendra-raya is Rasika.
[^20]: "The crest-jewel [of devotees]" (30) — *cūṛāmaṇi*. "With faces raised" (31) — *ūrddhamukhe*: to see him from afar. "They do not tidy their hair" — *nā sambare keśapāśa* (checked against the scan, crop ×2): they forget decorum.
[^21]: Pulaka (32) — the hairs standing on end from ecstatic love (Pashchima, lahari 16, note 32). "Conquers the quarters of the world" (34) — *digbijaẏa*: usually a king's victorious campaign or the tour of a scholar who wins debates; here, the wanderings of Rasika, who conquers hearts. "At [his] darshana" — *darśane*.
[^22]: "The saints" (35) — *santagaṇa*. "Ten million jewels" (36) — *koṭiratna*. The eight siddhis (37) are the mystic perfections of yoga (anima, laghima and others); the nine treasures (*nabanidhi*) are the treasures of Kubera. 37b — *satata rasika saṅge e saba bihare*: the siddhis and treasures, like servants, always follow Rasika.
[^23]: A ser (38) is a measure of weight, about 0.9 kg. "Comes" — *bije*. "Dwelling" — *mandira*. "Feeds" (39) — *sebe*, "serves." "Fried delicacies" — *pakkānna*; panchamrita, the "five nectars," is milk, curd, ghee, honey and sugar.
[^24]: "The six seasons, the twelve months" (41) — *ṣaṛṛtu bāramāsa*: the whole year round. 42a — *yā̃ra yei māse haẏa tithi ārādhana*: apparently the days of remembrance of saints and the other festivals listed above (4–17). "Aradhana" is a festival of remembrance (note 8). "Home" — *bāṛīte*: apparently to Dharenda (2), where the festivals were established.
[^25]: The adhivasa is the preliminary rite on the eve of a festival (Pashchima, lahari 2, note 19). "Arrives" — *haẏena prabeśa*, "enters."
[^26]: "The festival-aradhana of Shyamananda" (44) — *śyāmānanda-mahotsaba ārādhana*: the annual festival in memory of Shyamananda (4–5). Govindapur is Sri Govindapur, the site of the first festival of separation (lahari 1, 23, note 11). "Two [festivals] of twelve [days]" (45) — *dui duẏādaśa*: apparently this was already the second twelve-day festival in memory of Shyamananda in Govindapur (the first — lahari 1, 3, 23; "twelve festivals" — note 5; cf. the "third" festival of twelve days in lahari 4, 12, pdf 184). The number 45 is missing in the edition: the couplet is printed without a number (checked against the scan), and the next is ॥ ৪৬ ॥; the numbering is restored by count. "Sat in assembly" — *basilā samāje*. The Shyamanandis are Shyamananda's disciples (Pashchima, lahari 15); mahajanas — Pashchima, lahari 16, note 4.
[^27]: "Has been made unalterable for me" (47) — *more karila niścala*. "Your three mothers" (48) — *tina mātā tomāra*: the three Thakuranis (54) — apparently Shyamananda's wives, who for his disciples are mothers: Shyamapriya (53, 57), Yamuna (54) and one more, not named here (Gaurangadasi? — Pashchima, lahari 12, 11, note 11; Dakshina, lahari 12, note 8). "You shall settle" — *rākhibe*. 48b — *sbatantra rahile nā karibe ādare*: the sense is approximate; it can also be understood as "if [you] live apart, [they] will not show [you] affection." 48–51 are Shyamananda's words as reported by Rasika; the quotation marks follow the sense.
[^28]: Vrindavanachandra is Shyamananda's Deity (Pashchima, lahari 11, note 9). Vrajamohana Thakura (*brajamohana ṭhākura*) is apparently another Deity of Krishna (*ṭhākura* — "Deity"); not identified; it can also be understood as one Deity, "Vrindavanachandra Vrajamohana." "You shall ceremonially bring" — *bijaẏa karābe*: of a Deity's procession. Sri Shyamasundarpur is the village given to Shyamananda (Pashchima, lahari 12, 7, note 8); in lahari 3, 3 — "Sri Shyamanandapur."
[^29]: "In the depth of my heart" (50) — *marame*: apparently an inner command; it can also be understood as "insistently." "Serve the saints" (51) — *santasebā* (so in the scan; OCR "সম্তসেবা"). "In person" — *sākṣāte*, "face to face": during Shyamananda's lifetime. 51a is taken as Shyamananda's direct speech; the sense: living in three places, they could not feed the saints.
[^30]: The Thakurani (52, 56) is apparently Shyamapriya (cf. 57). "Will go" (53) — *bijaẏa karibe* (note 28). Shyamapriya is Shyamananda's wife (Pashchima, lahari 11, 26–29). Yamuna Thakurani — Dakshina, lahari 16, 37, note 22. "Thakurani" is a respectful title of the wife of a Vaishnava teacher.
[^31]: "Conveyed the whole matter in Rasika's words" (56) — *kahila sakala tattba rasika-bacane*. "Mother Shyamapriya" (57) — *śyāmapriyā mātā*. "Fitting" — *ucitā*. "The shining moon of the lineage" (58) — *kuladīpta-candra*. "All authority Shyamananda gave [him]" — *sarbba adhikāra dila*: Shyamananda handed over to Rasika the leadership of the community (Pashchima, lahari 13, note 21).
[^32]: "He is father, he is son" — *pitā sei putra sei*: for her Rasika is both a support, like a father, and a son (as her husband's disciple). "Takes" — *la'be*, "will take [with him]."
[^33]: "In that village" (61) — in Govindapur (44). "Having taken leave" — *bidāi kari*, "having sent off." "Of [that] journey" (62) — *gamanera bibaraṇa*: of the moving of the Thakuranis and the Deities (laharis 3–4).
[^34]: The author's signature (64) and the colophon are on pdf 182 (printed p. 138), checked against the scan; the title matches the heading (note 1). Lahari 2 comprises 1–64 (pdf 180–182). Below come a rule and the heading of lahari 3 (files RM-4-03-001-022).
