# Dakshina-vibhaga. Lahari 13. The Establishment of Krishna's Worship through the Examination of All the Scriptures

*A song in glory of Shyamananda; Raja Vaidyanatha asks Rasika to instruct the three brothers, and Rasika promises to initiate them and teaches them to worship Krishna alone, giving up the worship of various gods; the court bhattacharyas demand a learned debate, and Rasika, examining Sankhya, Mimamsa, Patanjali's yoga, logic, Vaisheshika, Vedanta, the four Vedas, the thirty-six smritis, poetry and grammar, shows that the essence of all the shastras is prema-bhakti for Krishna; the panditas admit that they cannot answer him, call him an avatara of Vyasa and Shuka and equal to Narayana; the raja asks about the truth of the living being, and Rasika tells how the jiva, not knowing Krishna, wanders by his karma in the cycle of births, suffers in the womb, remembers his past births and prays to Narayana for deliverance; the brothers ask why, once born, he forgets Krishna*

*The thirteenth lahari.*

*Raga Kaushika.*[^1]
*Ghosha: "Glory to Rama and Krishna, O Murari!*
*O Murari, O Murari!"*

**1.** Glory, glory to Shyamananda, purifier of the worlds!
Be merciful, so that I may always sing [your] glory.

**2.** Thus the king speaks at Rasika's feet:
"Carefully instruct the three brothers.[^2]

**3.** Birth after birth we all are your servants;
seeing [us as] servants, be merciful, ocean of mercy."

**4.** Hearing the king's words, Prabhu says:
"I shall certainly give initiation to [you] three brothers.[^3]

**5.** Listen attentively, I shall say one thing:
taking exclusive shelter, worship Narayana.

**6.** Do not worship various gods any more;
worship wholeheartedly the son of Nanda.[^4]

**7.** From Krishna all bodies have their origin;
Krishna is the life of all, Krishna is the goal of all.

**8.** If one worships Krishna, whose mind [still holds] fear?
By Krishna's qualities all are intoxicated, rejoicing in bliss."

**9.** According to various shastras he explained the essence to him:
"All is false, Krishna is true — this is the conclusion of the shastras."[^5]

**10.** Hearing Rasika's words, the king rejoiced:
"Whatever you command, Prabhu, that word is true."

**11.** Hearing the talk of initiation, all the bhattacharyas present [said]:
"Listen, maharaja, what are you doing?

**12.** Here are hundreds upon hundreds of bhattacharyas [and] chakravartis;
we shall hold a learned debate with him.[^6]

**13.** The dharma that is established in all the shastras —
in that dharma we too shall take shelter."

**14.** Hearing [this], Rasika, greatly delighted,
with joined palms, says to the brahmanas with a smile:

**15.** "The word you have spoken is the very essence;
I shall examine the truth of the Vedas [and] the six shastras."[^7]

**16.** The king [and his brothers], the three, sat down joyfully;
all began to examine the shastras.[^8]

**17.** Rasika sat down joyfully, remembering Krishna,
like Brihaspati, Vyasa [and] Shuka embodied.[^9]

**18.** First he examined Sankhya [and] Sankhyayana:
in the truth of Sankhya he established the worship of Krishna.[^10]

**19.** Prabhu tells everyone the essence of the truth, drawn out [of the shastras];
no one knows any other reasoning about it.[^11]

**20.** No one says a word — they sit with heads bowed;
inwardly they go over their books: "That is indeed the meaning."[^12]

**21.** Then he examined the Mimamsa-shastra:
in it he established Krishna as the essence drawn out.

**22.** Then he examined the shastra of Patanjali:
in it he showed the worship of Krishna as the dharma of firm faith.[^13]

**23.** Then he expounded all the Tarka-shastra:
in it steadfastness in Krishna's dharma is enjoined by scripture.

**24.** Then he revealed the Vaisheshika-shastra:
in it he established steadfastness in the dharma of Shrinivasa.[^14]

**25.** Then he expounded the Vedanta-shastra:
in it the worship of Krishna was ascertained.

**26.** He explained all the truths of the four Vedas:
in them the proof of bhakti for Krishna is ascertained.

**27.** The thirty-six smritis and the other [books] that exist on earth —
in them he established bhakti for Krishna unshakeably.[^15]

**28.** Rasikendra established the conclusion of all the shastras;
no one in the host of panditas can refute it.

**29.** Poetry and drama, all the upashastras and the rest —
in all the shastras he explains bhakti for Krishna as the accepted [truth].

**30.** He explains the roots [and] sutras in their accepted form,
he explains the commentaries and glosses one by one.[^16]

**31.** With many words he draws conclusions in many ways —
the meaning of words, the meaning of the Vedas — in agreement with Shuka [and] Vyasa.

**32.** Drawing out the essence of all the shastras [and] the truth of the Vedas,
he made prema-bhakti for Krishna the essence of all the shastras.

**33.** In all the shastras, he says, firm faith is the worship of Krishna;
not knowing [this], the panditas wander in vain.

**34.** One shloka Rasika explains in many ways:
the worship of Krishna is true — such is the truth of the shastras.

**35.** The host of panditas does not understand the truth of the shastras;
[he] establishes bhakti for Krishna in all the shastras.

**36.** Hearing Rasika's explanation, all were amazed;
the brahmanas say: "An avatara of Vyasa and Shuka!"[^17]

**37.** "We have all studied the four Vedas [and] the six shastras,
[but], not knowing the truth, we wander in anxiety of mind.

**38.** Hearing from a boy's lips the settled meaning of the shastras,
now we have understood the worship [that is] firm faith in Krishna."[^18]

**39.** No one could refute Rasika's explanation;
what Rasikendra says, that alone is right.

**40.** All the bhattacharyas told the king:
"Rasika is not a boy: he is equal to Narayana."[^19]

**41.** The king [and his brothers], the three, say: "Listen, best of brahmanas:
all of you, answer the boy."

**42.** Hearing [this], all the brahmanas say to the king:
"He is not a boy — he is equal to Narayana.

**43.** Either Vyasa, Shuka, Narada and the other sages,
or Brihaspati himself has taken birth.

**44.** The very shastras we studied at the cost of our lives —
those same shastras Rasika explains in so many ways!

**45.** One shloka he explains in many ways —
the meaning of the Vedas, the meaning of words, the truth of the shastras [with] proof.

**46.** He explains the roots [and] sutras, all that is accepted;
this boy's intelligence is equal to [that of] Vyasa [and] Shuka.

**47.** We could not set this boy [a single] problem;
what Rasika says, that alone is proof."[^20]

**48.** Hearing [this] from the brahmanas' lips, the king rejoiced
and with faith asks Rasika about everything.

**49.** He raised before Rasika [the question of] the truth of the living being;
Rasika tells everyone in detail:[^21]

**50.** "The jiva, subordinate to Ishvara, wanders under the sway of karma;
not knowing the truth of Krishna, he roams in samsara.[^22]

**51.** Wandering through various species, the jiva becomes senseless
and does not worship his own Lord, God Narayana.[^23]

**52.** I shall tell of the course of the jiva; listen, everyone:
rajas and virya are a single substance, arranged by the Creator.[^24]

**53.** From water are born blood, flesh, bones, skin;
[the jiva] enters the womb, taking his own karma with him.[^25]

**54.** In the seventh month the jiva becomes manifest in the womb;
much suffering does the jiva undergo, dwelling in the womb.[^26]

**55.** Pungent, bitter, salty and other things that the mother eats —
all of it pricks [him] in every pore, and it cannot be borne.

**56.** In distress the jiva seeks shelter;
in the womb he takes shelter of God Narayana.[^27]

**57.** All the births he has had under the sway of karma,
one by one — all of it comes to his mind in the womb.[^28]

**58.** Then, seized with terror, he calls to Narayana:
'Deliver me, Prabhu, I take shelter of You!

**59.** Blinded by sense objects, I did not worship You;
for that reason, Prabhu, You gave me the torments of the womb.

**60.** The five pranas [and] the twenty-five tattvas dwell in the body;
kama, anger, greed, delusion are especially enemies;

**61.** pride and envy dwell [there too] — these are [my] six riches;
sound, touch, form, taste, smell — this is [my] retinue.[^29]

**62.** All these enemies are with me without cease;
they did not let me worship Your lotus feet.

**63.** Thus birth after birth I undergo much suffering:
I did not take Hari's name in Hari-sankirtana,

**64.** I did not serve the sadhus, did not travel to holy places,
I showed no mercy to living beings — [my] life has been fruitless.

**65.** Brahmanas, kshatriyas, vaishyas, shudras — Narayana is [their] father;
not recognising the father, they wander, bewildered.[^30]

**66.** Now be merciful to me, Prabhu Bhagavan,
that birth after birth I may meditate on Your feet.

**67.** Be merciful, Narayana, brother of those who take shelter [of You],
that birth after birth I may worship Your feet.[^31]

**68.** Without worshipping You the jiva is not delivered;
thus he ever wanders through various species.

**69.** Brahma, Shiva [and] Purandara, by Your maya,
wander on the wheel of samsara by Your lila.[^32]

**70.** The one whom You accept [as Your own], showing mercy,
that person easily attains Your feet.'

**71.** Thus in yoga and meditation within the womb
the jiva offers various prayers, having come to know the Lord."

**72.** Hearing all these words, the three brothers [asked]:
"Why then, once born, does [the jiva] forget Krishna?"[^33]

**73.** Rasika tells everything as it is set forth in the shastras;
who could relate all those words even with a hundred mouths?

**74.** In brief I shall compose something of it;
listen to the Rasika-mangala, all you sadhus.

**75.** Adorning himself with Shyamananda's feet,
the son of Rasamaya joyfully composed [this].

*Thus in the Dakshina-vibhaga of the Sri Rasika-mangala ends the thirteenth lahari, called "The Establishment of Krishna's Worship through the Examination of All the Scriptures."*[^34]

[^1]: The lines before the raga are the heading of the lahari ("ত্রয়োদশ-লহরী"), in italics without a number. Lahari 13 begins on the lower half of pdf 119 (printed p. 81), after the colophon of lahari 12 (files RM-2-12-031-079). Raga Kaushika; the ghosha — *jaẏa re rāmakṛṣṇa murāre / o murāre o murāre* — is almost the same as in the Purva-vibhaga, laharis 3 and 16 (note 1 there): Rama and Krishna are Balarama and Krishna; Murari ("enemy of Mura") is a name of Krishna, which the author also applies to Rasika-Murari. There is no mangala-shloka: after the raga and ghosha the song begins with ॥ 1 ॥. The title follows the colophon on pdf 121 (checked against the scan): "ইতি শ্রীরসিকমঙ্গল-দক্ষিণবিভাগে সর্ব্বশাস্ত্রবিচারে কৃষ্ণভজন-স্থাপন-নাম ত্রয়োদশ-লহরী সম্পূর্ণা" (*sarbaśāstrabicāre kṛṣṇabhajana-sthāpana*). The summary line covers the whole lahari (1–50 from the scans of pdf 119–120; 51–75 from the scan of pdf 121 and the OCR, provisionally).
[^2]: "Instruct" — *upadeśa karaha*: give instruction, i.e. initiation (Dakshina, lahari 1, note 8; cf. 4). The OCR of the 1997 edition has "শুন ভাই" ("listen, brother") instead of "তিন ভাই," apparently a recognition error. Servants (3) — *kiṅkara*.
[^3]: Initiation — *dīkṣā* (Dakshina, lahari 1, note 8; lahari 10, note 26). "Prabhu" — Rasika.
[^4]: "Do not worship various gods any more; worship wholeheartedly the son of Nanda" — cf. the Bhagavad-gita (9.23–24; 18.66). According to Gaudiya Vaishnava teaching the demigods are respected as servants of the Lord but are not worshipped as independent lords; the devotee worships Krishna alone (*ananya-śaraṇa*, 5).
[^5]: "All is false, Krishna is true" — *saba mithyā kṛṣṇa satya*. According to Gaudiya Vaishnava teaching the world is a real, though temporary, energy of the Lord, not an illusion (CC, Madhya 6, the debate with Sarvabhauma on vivarta-vada); "false" here means everything apart from its relation to Krishna is futile and transient.
[^6]: Bhattacharya, chakravarti — titles of learned brahmanas. "We shall hold a learned debate" — *bidyāra bibāda kari*; "with him" — *uhāra saṅgati*.
[^7]: "The very essence" — *sei sāroddhāra*, lit. "the essence drawn out": Rasika accepts the brahmanas' condition (13). The six shastras are the six philosophical systems (lahari 12, note 12); Rasika then examines them one by one (18–25).
[^8]: "The king [and his brothers], the three" — *rājā tina bhāi* (likewise in 41).
[^9]: "Joyfully" — *raṅge*. "Remembering" — *samariẏā* (= *smariẏā*). Brihaspati, Vyasa, Shuka — lahari 12, notes 12 and 23. "Embodied" — *mūrttimanta*.
[^10]: Sankhya, Sankhyayana — lahari 10, note 28; lahari 12, note 27. According to Gaudiya Vaishnava tradition the true Sankhya is the teaching of Kapila, the son of Devahuti, which leads to bhakti ("Bhagavata," Canto 3, chs. 25–33). "He established the worship of Krishna" — *niṣṭhā kaila kṛṣṇera bhajana*: he showed that the conclusion of Sankhya is the worship of Krishna; likewise below (21–27).
[^11]: "No one knows any other reasoning about it" — *keha jāne nāhi āra ihāra bicāra*; the sense is approximate: no one could object.
[^12]: "With heads bowed" — *heṭa māthe*. "Inwardly they go over their books: 'That is indeed the meaning'" — *mane mane pũthi cinte sei artha haẏe*; the sense is approximate.
[^13]: The shastra of Patanjali is the Yoga-sutras. "The dharma of firm faith" — *niṣṭhā-dharma*. The Tarka-shastra (23) is logic (Nyaya); "enjoined by scripture" — *śāstrapraṇihita*.
[^14]: Shrinivasa (*śrīnibāsa*), "the abode of Shri (Lakshmi)," is a name of Vishnu. "He established steadfastness in the dharma of Shrinivasa" — *karila niṣṭhā dharma śrīnibāsa* (so in the scan and in the 1997 edition); it can also be understood as "he established the firm [conviction]: dharma is Shrinivasa."
[^15]: The thirty-six smritis are the traditional count of the dharma-shastras (codes of sacred law). The OCR of the 1997 edition has "ত্রিশ" ("thirty"), apparently a recognition error.
[^16]: Upashastras (29) — the auxiliary sciences. "Roots [and] sutras" — *dhātu sūtra*: grammar (verbal roots and rules, sutras). "Commentaries and glosses" — *ṭīkā*, *ṭippanī*.
[^17]: "An avatara of Vyasa and Shuka" — the rapturous words of the brahmanas, as is "equal to Narayana" (40, 42–43; lahari 12, note 19).
[^18]: "A boy" — *bālaka*: Rasika was young at the time. "The worship [that is] firm faith in Krishna" — *kṛṣṇa-niṣṭhāra bhajana*.
[^19]: "Equal to Narayana" — *nārāyaṇa sama*: the words of the panditas (cf. 42); on the theological side see lahari 12, note 19 (Purva, lahari 1, note 2; Purva, lahari 14, note 11).
[^20]: "Problem" — *samasyā*: a difficult question, a riddle to test learning. "Proof" — *paramāṇa* (*pramāṇa*), i.e. beyond dispute. The second line is missing in the OCR of the 1997 edition; it is read from the scan.
[^21]: "He raised … [the question of] the truth of the living being" — *jībatattba pracārila*: apparently the king (48); it can also be understood as "Rasika expounded." Next (50–71) Rasika describes the life of the jiva in the womb.
[^22]: "Subordinate to Ishvara" — *īśbara adhīna*. According to Gaudiya Vaishnava teaching the jiva is the eternal servant of Krishna; having forgotten Krishna, he wanders under the sway of karma in the cycle of births (CC, Madhya 20). Rasika's speech continues on pdf 121 (51–71).
[^23]: "Senseless" — *acetana*, "devoid of consciousness": having forgotten himself and God. Rasika's speech, begun in 50, continues to 71; the jiva's prayer in the womb (58–70) is a speech within the speech. The teaching on the jiva in the womb (51–71) is close to Kapila's teaching in the Bhagavatam (3.31).
[^24]: "Rajas and virya" — *rajobīryya*: the female and male seed. "Arranged by the Creator" — *bidhātā-ghaṭana*; Vidhata is the Creator, Brahma.
[^25]: "From water" — *jala haite*: apparently from the fluids of conception. "His own karma" — *ātmakarmma*, the fruits of past actions that determine the new body.
[^26]: "Becomes manifest" — *haẏa parakāśa*: in the seventh month he gains consciousness (cf. Bhagavatam 3.31).
[^27]: "Seeks shelter", "takes shelter" — *śaraṇa*; in Middle Bengali *śaraṇa* may also stand for *smaraṇa* — then: "remembers God Narayana."
[^28]: "All of it" — *saba tattva*, lit. "all the truths (circumstances)": in the womb the jiva remembers his former births.
[^29]: The five pranas are the vital airs (prana, apana, vyana, udana, samana); the twenty-five tattvas are the principles of existence according to Sankhya. The six enemies (*ṣaṛ ripu*) are kama (*kāma*, worldly desire), anger, greed, delusion, pride and envy; "six riches" (*ṣaṛ sampatti*) is bitter irony. "Retinue" — *saṅgati*: the five objects of the senses.
[^30]: "Brahmanas, kshatriyas" — *brahmakṣetrī* (= *brahma-kṣatriya*). Cf. lahari 14, 22–24.
[^31]: "Brother of those who take shelter [of You]" — *śaraṇa sodara* (cf. lahari 12, 67).
[^32]: Purandara is Indra. "By Your lila" — *tomāra līlāẏa*: by Your will, in Your play. Thus the author speaks of the Lord's power over all the demigods; according to Gaudiya Vaishnava teaching, Brahma and Indra are jivas in the highest posts of the universe, while Shiva is a special category distinct from ordinary living beings (CC, Madhya 20; Brahma-samhita 5.45).
[^33]: "Forget" — *pāśare* (= *pāsare*). Rasika's answer is in lahari 14.
[^34]: The colophon is on pdf 121 (checked against the scan): "ইতি শ্রীরসিকমঙ্গল-দক্ষিণবিভাগে সর্ব্বশাস্ত্রবিচারে কৃষ্ণভজন-স্থাপন-নাম ত্রয়োদশ-লহরী সম্পূর্ণা" (*sarbbaśāstra-bicāre kṛṣṇabhajana-sthāpana*); the 1997 edition reads the same; on the title see note 1. Lahari 14 begins on the same page (files RM-2-14-001-068).
