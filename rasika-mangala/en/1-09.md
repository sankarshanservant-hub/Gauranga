# Purva-vibhaga. Lahari 9. Description of the Delights of Learning

*A shloka in glory of Rasikananda and a song; the rite of beginning to write, performed by the astrologer Vasudeva; Rasika quickly masters the letters and the phalas, then studies grammar and the shastras with five teachers in the places where his father, the adhikari of Mallabhumi, takes him along, and surpasses all in exposition; Jagannatha Mishra reads the Bhagavata with him and, amazed at his explanations, weeps and blesses him; study with Haridasa Dube, their discussions of prema-bhakti and Dube's praise of the boy; the fruits of hearing*

*The ninth lahari.*

**S1.** *What [use] is thinking of the chintamani? What [use] are the praisers of the tree of the gods?*
*Or service of the gods, austerity, or attachment to meditation and the like?*
*There is not only suffering there: [there] the mind is again and again gripped by great fear.*
*Lead your mind to Rasikananda, manifest before our eyes for the welfare of the worlds.*[^1]

*Raga Sangada.*[^2]
*Ghosha: "In Nanda's house the crown-jewel of the gods*
*sports in the guise of a boy."*

**1.** Glory, glory to Krishna's qualities! I bow to the holy feet.
Glory, glory to the son of Achyuta, the treasure of [my] life![^3]

**2.** Thus day by day Rasika-shekhara
hears without pause of Krishna's qualities, of Krishna's pastimes.

**3.** After some time Achyuta considers in his mind
placing the chalk in Rasika's hand according to the rite.

**4.** Quickly summoning all the brahmana astrologers,
he fixed an auspicious day according to the shastras.

**5.** Setting up the auspicious pot, he worships Sarasvati —
that wise astrologer named Vasudeva.

**6.** He placed the chalk in [his] hand, saying "Sri Krishna!" —
and Prabhu sat down to study, full of joy.

**7.** With his beautiful hand, [tender as] a young shoot, he took the chalk,
wrote "siddhir astu" and prostrated himself.[^4]

**8.** Quickly bowing at the feet of his teacher,
he then offered obeisance to the brahmanas.

**9.** Bowing at the feet of his father and mother,
Rasika then bowed to all his elders.

**10.** With joyful hearts all bless [him]:
"May Krishna make you like Brihaspati!"[^5]

**11.** Setting up an excellent school,
Vasudeva teaches the son of Achyuta.

**12.** He learns every letter as soon as he sees it;
the teacher joyfully teaches [him], [wholly] absorbed [in it].

**13.** Prabhu recites all the phalas in a sweet voice —
hearing [it] is as if nectar were sprinkled into the ears.[^6]

**14.** One longs to hear the sweetness of those words;
when he speaks, [the words] come from his sweet mouth in a childish half-lisp.

**15.** Hearing the nectar of those words, a stone melts.
Thus, as if at play, he studies with the children.

**16.** All the phalas he learned in a few days
and with a joyful heart began to spell out words.

**17.** After some time, having mastered the letters,
he resolved in his mind to study grammar.

**18.** He told his father of [his] wish to learn;
[the father] brought a teacher — Mimamsa-mandana.

**19.** Fixing an auspicious day, he took a manuscript in hand;
Mimamsa-mandana teaches Rasika-shekhara.[^7]

**20.** Hearing only once from the teacher's mouth,
Rasika quickly explains the roots and sutras.

**21.** Seeing the son's explanations, [all] are amazed.
The bhattacharya says: "This boy is no human being.

**22.** What we learn after studying a year or two —
all those explanations I hear from this mouth.

**23.** Truly this great soul is Krishna's associate;
he will crush the Shaivas, the Shaktas and the pashandas."[^8]

**24.** For some days he studied with him;
then the vaidya Balabhadrasena taught [him].

**25.** For some days he studied with Balabhadra —
he was highly accomplished in the science of grammar.

**26.** [He studied] for some time with Anukula Chakravarti,
and at last learned something with Kavichandra.

**27.** For some time with Sri Yadunandana Chakravarti
he studied with eagerness.

**28.** Alone, with five teachers, great men,
Sri Rasika studies, honouring [them].[^9]

**29.** Hearing once, he explains the roots and sutras,
poetry, drama, grammar, commentaries and glosses.

**30.** The son himself explains, himself refutes —
and no one is able to re-establish [what was refuted].

**31.** Hundreds of students study with all [these teachers];
when Rasika refutes, no one can re-establish.

**32.** By the mercy of Krishna, the Lord of Sarasvati,
Rasika himself establishes [the point] again.[^10]

**33.** The exposition [follows] the proper order, without a single fault;
hearing [it], that teacher is supremely pleased.

**34.** The students, hearing the exposition of the sutras, are astonished:
"How has a child come to such understanding of the shastras?

**35.** We have studied for so long, with all our strength —
[yet] no one could answer the child's refutation.

**36.** And he himself has re-established the roots and sutras.
This is no child — this is a person endowed with all virtues."

**37.** Thus all are amazed,
[while] the son of Achyuta studies with his teachers.

**38.** Achyuta is the adhikari in the land of Mallabhumi;
on royal business he travels from region to region.[^11]

**39.** Taking with him the son dearer than life,
wherever he goes, there he takes Rasika.

**40.** Choosing a dwelling in one place or another,
he stays there for many days.

**41.** In whatever place whatever teacher lives,
in that place, with him, [go on] the delights of learning.

**42.** Therefore with five teachers, in different places,
he studies day and night with diligence.

**43.** Absorbed in learning, Prabhu knows neither day nor night;
he became a knower of the six shastras, keen of understanding.

**44.** His heart is ever enchanted by prema for Krishna;
for the deliverance of living beings he studies with diligence.

**45.** In debate the opponents [are]: tarka, sankhya, sankhyayana,
mimamsa, [the teaching of] Patanjali and all the other [subjects of] study.[^12]

**46.** To crush their pride,
he quickly studied all the shastras and the essence of the Vedas.

**47.** He became a great pandita, equal to Brihaspati —
he by whose touch the earth rejoiced.

**48.** Having thus studied all the shastras,
he wished to study the Bhagavata.

**49.** The teacher Jagannatha Mishra, the fortunate,
set the Bhagavata Purana in song metre.[^13]

**50.** Fixing an auspicious moment, [Rasika] began his study;
the son of Achyuta studies with love.

**51.** Day by day he studies [it], beginning with the first canto;
hearing once from the teacher's mouth, he explains.

**52.** He explains the commentaries and glosses in accordance with Svami;
he explains in various ways — who will grasp [this] truth?[^14]

**53.** A single shloka he explains in so many ways:
the mood, the nature, the meaning of the words, the intent of Vyasa.

**54.** Joining prema with the conclusions of Vedanta,
he explains bhakti like Shuka embodied.[^15]

**55.** His voice choked with prema, he explains;
the hair stands on end all over his body, tears flow from his holy eyes.

**56.** Hearing that explanation, dry wood melts;
he explains as one who experiences prema-bhakti for Krishna.

**57.** Hearing the exposition of the Bhagavata, the teacher is amazed;
in joy he embraces [him] again and again.

**58.** Mishra says: "Blessed is the father, blessed is that mother!
Has Vyasa or Shukadeva himself been born?

**59.** Through the boy's exposition knowledge has come to me."
Pressing Rasika to his chest, he began to weep.

**60.** The eight sattvika [bhavas] arose in Mishra;
he says: "This great person is a dear devotee of Krishna.[^16]

**61.** By seeing him I shall surely attain Krishna;
by his touch prema-bhakti awakens.

**62.** By seeing him [one] is freed from all sins."
Saying these words, all cry: "Glory, glory!"

**63.** "Hearing his words, the ears are soothed;
this person will deliver the whole world.

**64.** We have studied the Bhagavata for so long —
[yet] never knew anything of the truth of the Bhagavata.

**65.** Hearing from this boy's mouth, I have gained knowledge;
whatever Rasika explains — that is the truth.[^17]

**66.** To make known to the world the deep meaning of the Bhagavata,
Krishna's servant has been born as the son of Achyuta."[^18]

**67.** Having said this, Jagannatha Mishra Mahashaya
gives [him] his blessing with joy in his heart.

**68.** After studying with him for some time,
Prabhu then studied with Hari Dube.[^19]

**69.** The fortunate Hari Dube, Krishna's servant,
without pause explains prema-bhakti for Krishna.

**70.** Hearing [him], Prabhu rejoices; with kindly words
they constantly expound prema-bhakti to each other.

**71.** He found great happiness with Hari Dube;
without pause, together with him, [he was engaged] in research among manuscripts.[^20]

**72.** Hearing Sri Rasika's explanations, Hari Dube
in joy [shows] horripilation and tears — in the mood of prema for Krishna.

**73.** He regards Rasika as dearer than his own self;
without pause the two sit discussing the shastras.

**74.** The two know nothing of eating, resting or sleep;
at every moment they expound prema-bhakti for Krishna.

**75.** The most steadfast and learned Haridasa Dube,
hearing from the boy's mouth, experiences Krishna.

**76.** In joy he takes Rasika on his lap and embraces him:
"May [all I have] be offered up for [your] face, son of Achyuta![^21]

**77.** Blessed, blessed is that land, blessed is that mother!
Has Brihaspati himself come and been born?

**78.** Or Vyasa, Shuka, Narada and the other gods,
or Aja, Bhava, Purandara, Narayana?[^22]

**79.** Such intelligence in a child [I] have never seen;
to the greatness of [his] learning Brihaspati and Shuka are witnesses.[^23]

**80.** [He] knows Vedanta, siddhanta, the meanings of words, the six shastras,
the eighteen Puranas, the Sri Bhagavata, the Gita.[^24]

**81.** The great ocean of the nectar of the rasa of prema-bhakti for Krishna
he expounds [so that it appears] embodied — such is the fame [of him].[^25]

**82.** Different people give a single shloka different meanings;
the child refuted the explanations of them all.

**83.** However Rasika explains [it] — that is the true evidence;
of this Vyasa, Shuka, Narada and others are the warrant.

**84.** By our good fortune [this] boy has come into the world;
this great-minded one will deliver all living beings."

**85.** Past, future [and] present Dube knows.
Blessing [Rasika], he proclaims [his] glory before everyone.

**86.** Thus, in love for Krishna, [went on] the delights of learning;
[Rasika] lived constantly in Dube's company.

**87.** Whoever hears of these delights of learning
[gains] prema-bhakti for Krishna [and] release from bondage.[^26]

**88.** Hear the Rasika-mangala, all you devotees of Krishna:
soon you will attain the Sri feet of Rasika.[^27]

**89.** Adorning himself with Shyamananda's feet,
the son of Rasamaya joyfully composed [this].

*Thus ends the ninth lahari, called "Description of the Delights of Learning," in the Purva-vibhaga of the Sri Rasika-mangala.*

[^1]: The shloka is unnumbered in the edition (after it come the raga, the ghosha and the couplets from ॥ 1 ॥); it is marked S1, like the mangala-shlokas of lahari 1 (note 1), 6 and 8 (notes 1). The metre is shardula-vikridita. The chintamani is the wish-fulfilling gem; "the tree of the gods" — *sura-kṣauṇīruha*, the wish-fulfilling tree (kalpa-vriksha) of the heavenly world. "Great fear" — *guru-bhaya*; the edition's shlokartha takes it as fear of the cycle of worldly existence (*saṁsṛti*). "Manifest before our eyes" — *pratyakṣam*. The thought is the same as in the mangala-shloka of lahari 8: not worldly boons, austerity or meditation, but devotion to a pure Vaishnava leads to bhakti (cf. lahari 8, note 1). The source is not established — apparently the author's own composition.
[^2]: Raga Sangada (*sāṅgaṛā*) — so in the scan and in the 1997 edition; we have not found this raga in other collections of padas. The ghosha (refrain) is *nandera mandire deva-śiromaṇi vihare bālaka-veśe*. Unlike lahari 8, there is no "song" (*gīta*) mark before ॥ 1 ॥.
[^3]: "Glory to Krishna's qualities" — *jaya jaya kṛṣṇa-guṇa* — so in the scan and in the 1997 edition; in the opening couplets of laharis 6, 8 and 10 Shyamananda's name stands here. "I bow to the holy feet" — *banda śrīcaraṇa*; "bow [all of you]" is also possible; whose feet is not said. The son of Achyuta is Rasikananda.
[^4]: 3–7 — the rite of beginning instruction (*hāte khaṛi*, lit. "[giving] chalk into the hand"): on an auspicious day fixed by astrologers (*daivajña*), an auspicious pot (*maṅgala-ghaṭa*) is set up, Sarasvati, the goddess of learning, is worshipped, and the child writes letters in chalk for the first time. "Siddhir astu" ("may there be success") are the customary first words written by a pupil. Vasudeva, the astrologer and Rasika's first teacher, is not mentioned in other sources.
[^5]: Brihaspati is the preceptor of the gods, the model of learning.
[^6]: Phalas (*phalā*) are the special (subscript) forms of consonants in Bengali script (*ya-phalā, ra-phalā*, etc.), which a child learns after the letters. "Spell out words" (16) — *bānāite*: to join letters into words, to learn spelling.
[^7]: Mimamsa-mandana (*mīmāṁsā-maṇḍana*, "ornament of mimamsa") — cf. lahari 8, 11 and note 5, where the learned brahmana who read the Bhagavata to Rasika is so called; here (apparently the same) teacher of grammar is so called — probably his honorific title or name; in 21 he is called the bhattacharya. "Roots and sutras" (*dhātu sūtra*) — the verbal roots and rules (sutras) of Sanskrit grammar.
[^8]: The Shaivas, the Shaktas (worshippers of Shiva and of Shakti-Durga) and the pashandas — opponents of Vaishnava bhakti. According to BR 15, Shyamananda and Rasikananda converted many pashandas in Utkala.
[^9]: 24–28: the five teachers are Mimamsa-mandana, the vaidya (of the physician caste) Balabhadrasena, Anukula Chakravarti, Kavichandra ("moon of poets," apparently a title) and Sri Yadunandana Chakravarti; BR 15 does not name them (it says only: "from his early years he was versed in all the shastras"). "Alone" (28, *ekā*) — apparently Rasika studied with them by himself, without fellow pupils. "With eagerness" (27) — *kariyā ārati*.
[^10]: 30–36: "to refute" (*khaṇḍana*) and "to re-establish, to affirm" (*sthāpana*) are moves in scholarly debate: Rasika himself refutes an explanation and himself re-establishes it. The Lord of Sarasvati is Krishna (Sarasvati is the goddess of speech and learning).
[^11]: Mallabhumi — lahari 3, note 6; there too (54) Achyuta is called the lord of the region. Adhikari (*adhikārī*) — a steward, an official.
[^12]: The six shastras (43, *ṣaḍ-śāstra*) are the six philosophical systems (darshanas). 45: tarka (nyaya, logic), sankhya, mimamsa, the [yoga] of Patanjali; *sāṅkhyāyana* — so in the scan and in the 1997 edition; probably Shankhayana (a Vedic school named after the rishi Shankhayana), but possibly just an echo of "sankhya"; the reading is unclear.
[^13]: Jagannatha Mishra is Rasika's teacher — not to be confused with Jagannatha Mishra, the father of Sri Chaitanya. "Set in song metre" — *gīta-chande bāndhilena*: apparently he composed a rendering of the Bhagavata in the vernacular. Cf. the Oriya Bhagavata of Jagannatha Dasa (16th c.); any connection with it is not established.
[^14]: Svami is Shridhara Svami, author of the commentary *Bhavartha-dipika* on the Bhagavata. Sri Chaitanya honoured this commentary and said that one who does not accept Svami should be counted as unfaithful, like a harlot (CC, Antya 7).
[^15]: Shuka is Shukadeva Gosvami, the speaker of the Bhagavata. "Joining prema with the conclusions of Vedanta": according to Gaudiya Vaishnava teaching, the Bhagavata is the natural commentary on the *Vedanta-sutra*, and its highest conclusion is prema (CC, Madhya 25).
[^16]: The eight sattvika bhavas — lahari 6, note 13; cf. 55 and 72.
[^17]: "Knowledge" — *giyāna* = *jñāna* (so also the edition's gloss). "The truth" — *pramāṇa*, "proof, the measure of truth."
[^18]: "Krishna's servant" — *kṛṣṇera kiṅkara*: Mishra sees in Rasika a devotee sent by Krishna (cf. 23, 60).
[^19]: Hari Dube is Haridasa Dube (75); Dube (Dvivedi) is a brahmana surname common in North India. He is not mentioned in other sources.
[^20]: "Research among manuscripts" — *puṅthi anveṣaṇe*: perhaps searching for and collating manuscripts, or simply the study of books.
[^21]: "May it be offered up for your face" — *nichāni mukhera yāu*: nichhani is the custom of waving a gift (or oneself) around a beloved's face to ward off misfortune; that is, "let all that is mine be sacrificed for you." Dube's speech continues on the next page (pdf 70, 79–85).
[^22]: Aja is Brahma, Bhava is Shiva, Purandara is Indra. The comparisons in Dube's praise are rapturous hyperbole; the author himself calls Rasika Krishna's servant (66) and His associate (23). According to Gaudiya Vaishnava teaching, Rasikananda is a pure devotee, an associate of Shyamananda, and not Narayana Himself.
[^23]: 79–84 continue Haridasa Dube's speech (76–78, note 21); the quotation closes in 84. Brihaspati is the preceptor of the gods, the model of learning; Shuka is Shukadeva (note 15).
[^24]: Siddhanta — the established conclusions of a teaching; "the meanings of words" — *śabdārtha* (grammar and lexicon); the six shastras are the six philosophical systems (note 12).
[^25]: "The great ocean of the nectar of the rasa of prema-bhakti" — *rasāmṛta-mahodadhi*; possibly an echo of the title of Rupa Gosvami's *Bhakti-rasamrita-sindhu* ("The Ocean of the Nectar of the Rasa of Bhakti"). The second line is elliptical: *mūrttimanta bākhānaya ye āche prasiddhi* — "he expounds [it] embodied, as is well known"; "he expounds all that is renowned" is also possible.
[^26]: "Release from bondage" — *bandhana-mocana*, release from the bonds of material existence. According to Gaudiya Vaishnava teaching, liberation comes to the devotee of itself, as a by-product of bhakti, and is inferior to it (cf. lahari 8, note 1). The edition notes a variant reading of the first line, close in meaning.
[^27]: "The devotees of Krishna" — *kārṣṇa-jana*; read from the scan (a superscript repha), garbled in the OCR (cf. lahari 1, note 66; lahari 3, note 19).
