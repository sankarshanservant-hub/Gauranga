# Uttara-vibhaga. Lahari 1. The Description of the Farewell of the Mahantas and Vaishnavas at the End of the Festival of Separation, and of the Attainment of Vrindavana by Kishora and Chintamani

*A song in glory of Shyamananda; the author begins the Uttara-vibhaga; Rasika dances for twelve days at the festival of separation, and at its close a dadhi-kada is held amid the thunder of mridangas, with turmeric, curd and abir, and the dancing lasts until evening; Rasika generously gives gifts and sends off the rajas, the subjects, the saints, his kinsmen and the kirtaniyas, and all call him a partial incarnation; from then on he institutes the festival of twelve days; the elders Sri Kishora and Chintamani Dasa, brothers by birth, throw down the garments offered to them and revile Rasika, but he rejoices that the cloth has fallen on his head, as Shyamananda once tied a cloth on it, and humbly begs them for mercy; that same night the brothers fall ill and in anger leave for Kashiyari; Rasika follows them alone through the forests, serves them and calls physicians; the brothers admit that they have offended Rasika, in whose heart dwell Chaitanya, Nityananda, Advaita, their associates, Krishna and Shyamananda, and call him a portion of Narayana; after some days they depart for Vaikuntha; Rasika mourns them, holds a festival in their memory and goes to Dharenda, where a brahmana celebrates the days of the aradhana of Damodara Gosani; Rasika wanders, absorbed in festivals; the author promises the listeners deliverance from the age of Kali*

*Glory to Sri Sri Radha and Govinda!*
*Sri Sri Rasika-mangala.*
*Uttara-vibhaga.*
*The first lahari.*

*Raga Narayani-gauda.*[^1]
*Ghosha: "My Krishna, treasury of virtues,*
*refuge of the helpless, the utmost limit of mercy."*

**1.** Glory, glory to Shyamananda, the friend of the wretched and destitute,
the benefactor of all people, the friend of the whole [world]!

**2.** Now I shall proclaim the Uttara-vibhaga —
[that] which Prabhu, the son of Achyuta, makes me speak.[^2]

**3.** Thus Rasikendra, [absorbed in] the rasa of the great festival,
dances in great bliss for twelve days.[^3]

**4.** The happiness of the great festival cannot be described even in part:
Rasikendra-raya floats in the happiness of supreme bliss.

**5.** Having completed the great festival, they hold a dadhi-kada;
in heaven, on earth [and] in the nether world the dundubhis resound.[^4]

**6.** Hundreds of mridangas thunder deafeningly;
together with the rajas — countless instruments of every kind.[^5]

**7.** The sounds of instruments, the clamour of the crowd, the sounds of sankirtana —
the earth trembles with the uproar of the cries of "Hari!"

**8.** Chuya, sandal and the rest, phagu together with flowers —
they throw them by hundreds of pots, each as he can.[^6]

**9.** Hundreds of people throw turmeric with curd;
all the people have their bodies adorned with abir.

**10.** Holding hands, all the people dance;
in their midst dances the son of Achyuta.

**11.** Until the fourth prahara [lasts] the dance in this bliss;
as evening comes on, the sankirtana is completed.

**12.** Having sported in the water, all finished the meal and the rest;
Prabhu sent [the guests] off, [honouring each] as was fitting.

**13.** Silk, silken cloths, various garments, ornaments,
tankas, gold and the rest the son of Achyuta gives;[^7]

**14.** camphor, sandal, nutmeg, pepper and the rest,
ghee, oil, gur, areca nuts, pan, cloves and the rest.

**15.** Whatever anyone desires within his mind —
knowing [their] minds, he gives parting gifts to each one.

**16.** Whatever thing the saints desire,
that thing he gives them and fulfils [their] wish.

**17.** Such a great festival has never been and never will be;
seeing [it], the rajas and the subjects are amazed.

**18.** All rejoiced seeing the great festival:
[such is] the power of Rasika, the friend of the three worlds.[^8]

**19.** All say: "Such happiness we have never seen at any time:
Rasikendra-chudamani is a partial incarnation."[^9]

**20.** All — the rajas and the subjects — took their leave;
[Prabhu] honoured all, each one.

**21.** Then Prabhu honoured his kinsmen;
having taken leave, all went to their own places.

**22.** Prabhu took leave of the kirtaniyas:
garments, ornaments, money he gave to each one.[^10]

**23.** Thus [passed] the great festival at Sri Govindapur;
from then on he instituted the [festival of] twelve [days].[^11]

**24.** To the elders — Sri Kishora [and] Chintamani Dasa —
to these two Prabhu gave splendid garments.[^12]

**25.** The two did not accept them — they threw [them] down before him;
the cloth, [flying back], fell on Prabhu's head.

**26.** Then the two reviled [him] — with whatever came to their lips;
hearing [it], Prabhu rejoices [and] laughs with happiness in his heart:

**27.** "Today, after so long, my good fortune has come:
reviling [me], they have placed a cloth on my head.

**28.** Formerly Shyamananda tied a cloth on my head;
now the two brothers have given [it] out of great mercy."[^13]

**29.** Saying this, Rasikendra fell at [their] feet;
in great anger, reviling [him], they rose abruptly.

**30.** Smiling, the son of Achyuta speaks gently:
"I am an offender; I am not worthy [even of your] punishment.[^14]

**31.** You two brothers by birth are an ocean of mercy;
regarding [me] as a servant, be merciful, [be my] refuge and protection."[^15]

**32.** Much praise he offered to the two of them,
yet they were not appeased, firm in their anger.

**33.** That same night both fell ill;
in anger both went to Kashiyari.[^16]

**34.** After them went Prabhu with tears in his eyes;
remembering the qualities of Krishna, he weeps without cease.

**35.** And [in him was] love in separation from Shyamananda:
alone he entered the forest — no one could catch up with him.[^17]

**36.** Passing from forest to forest, he enters that abode;
having come, Prabhu stayed with these two.

**37.** Gravely ill were the two brothers;
Rasika-shekhara served [them] in many ways.

**38.** He brought many physicians from various lands;
[they] were treated in many ways, as the physicians judged.[^18]

**39.** Sri Kishora [and] Chintamani understood in their minds
that surely the two of them had stumbled before Rasika.[^19]

**40.** Before everyone the two said:
"We two have stumbled at Rasika's feet.

**41.** Surely the son of Achyuta is a portion of Narayana;
not knowing [his] glory, we reviled [him] without cause.[^20]

**42.** He in whose heart Sri Chaitanya dwells without cease,
in whose heart is Nityananda's own home;

**43.** in whose heart dwells Sri Advaita Mahashaya,
in whose heart dwells Sri Abhirama;[^21]

**44.** in whose breast is Sri Subala-chandra,
in whose heart dwell Sri the twelve gopalas;[^22]

**45.** in whose heart dwell the eight Giris [and] the eight Puris,
in whose heart dwell especially the eight Bharatis;

**46.** the sixty-four mahantas dwell in his heart,
in whose heart dwell together the eight boys;[^23]

**47.** [they] dwell within his heart with [their] associates and followers,
in whose heart dwells the whole society of Vaishnavas;[^24]

**48.** in whose heart dwells Hridayananda Chaitanya,
whose union with the Vrajavasis is undivided;[^25]

**49.** Krishna, in whose heart [He] abides without cease,
in whose heart is Sri Shyamananda's own home;

**50.** whose meditation, waking and dreaming, is Shyamananda,
for whom Shyamananda Prabhu is caste, wealth [and] life —[^26]

**51.** at the feet of such a Prabhu we have committed an offence:
what [further] desire [is left] to us in life?

**52.** Why do you give medicines in vain in so many ways?
The black serpent of offence has truly bitten [us]."[^27]

**53.** Very wise, the two understood [it all] truly;
before everyone they told [these] truths one by one.

**54.** Hearing of Rasika's glory, all were amazed:
all the people recognised [in him] the very form of Narayana.[^28]

**55.** In many ways Rasikendra treated the two,
yet the two brothers did not recover.

**56.** After some days both went to the world of Vaikuntha;
[Rasika] weeps much because of the separation.[^29]

**57.** Then Prabhu held a festival [in memory] of the two;
to that village many loads of provisions were brought.[^30]

**58.** Having completed the festival, Prabhu set out;
coming to Dharenda, Prabhu settled [there].[^31]

**59.** [Then came] the day of the aradhana of Damodara Gosani:
a brahmana held two or three festivals.[^32]

**60.** Intoxicated with the rasa of festivals, Rasika-shekhara
wanders in the bliss of Krishna through lands near and far.

**61.** [His] boundless, ocean-like lilas cannot be described;
Rasikendra-raya was born for the deliverance of living beings.

**62.** By his mercy I have made known a little —
something of the qualities, fame [and] praise of Rasika-deva.[^33]

**63.** This is the first lahari in the Uttara-vibhaga:
[about] the lilas that Sri Rasika-deva performed.

**64.** Hear the Rasika-mangala, all the world:
for one who hears [it], the dreadful age of Kali is destroyed.

**65.** Adorning himself with Shyamananda's feet,
the son of Rasamaya joyfully composed [this].

*Thus in the Uttara-vibhaga of the Sri Rasika-mangala ends the first lahari, called "The Description of the Farewell of the Mahantas and Vaishnavas at the End of the Festival of Separation, and of the Attainment of Vrindavana by Kishora and Chintamani."*[^34]

[^1]: The lines before the raga are the opening lines of the vibhaga and the lahari ("শ্রীশ্রীরাধাগোবিন্দৌ জয়তঃ / শ্রীশ্রীরসিকমঙ্গল / উত্তর-বিভাগ / প্রথম-লহরী"), in italics without a number, as at the beginning of the Purva-, Dakshina- and Pashchima-vibhagas (the line "Glory to Sri Sri Rasikananda-deva!", found in the Purva, is absent here). The Uttara-vibhaga ("northern part") is the last, fourth part of the poem; it begins on a new page — pdf 178 (printed p. 134; the page number is not printed), after the colophon of Pashchima, lahari 16 on pdf 177. Raga Narayani-gauda (*nārāẏanī-gauṛā*, so on the scan; Purva, lahari 7, note 1); the ghosha is the same as in Pashchima, lahari 13 (note 1), and its translation is repeated. There is no mangala-shloka: after the raga and ghosha the song begins with ॥ 1 ॥. The title follows the colophon on pdf 180 (printed p. 136), checked against the scan: "ইতি শ্রীরসিকমঙ্গল-উত্তর-বিভাগে বিরহ-মহোৎসবান্তে মোহন্ত ও বৈষ্ণব-বিদায় এবং কিশোর-চিন্তামণির বৃন্দাবনপ্রাপ্তি-বর্ণননাম প্রথম-লহরী সম্পূর্ণা" (*biraha-mahotsabānte mohanta o baiṣṇaba-bidāẏa ebaṃ kiśora-cintāmaṇira bṛndābanaprāpti-barṇana*). "The festival of separation" is the festival in memory of Shyamananda's departure at Govindapur (Pashchima, lahari 16). Lahari 1 — 1–65 (pdf 178–180). For the Uttara-vibhaga there is no second reading (the 1997 edition).
[^2]: "The friend of the wretched and destitute" (1) — *dīnahīna-bandhu*; "the friend of the whole [world]" — *akhilera bandhu*. "Proclaim" — *kariba pracāra*. "Makes me speak" — *more bolāna*: the author ascribes his words to Rasika (cf. Pashchima, lahari 13, note 34). "The son of Achyuta" — *acyuta-kumāra*: Rasika.
[^3]: "Thus" — *henarūpe*: the narrative continues the festival of separation (Pashchima, lahari 16). "For twelve days" — *dbādaśa dibase*; it can also be understood as "on the twelfth day". Cf. 23; lahari 2, 5; Pashchima, lahari 14, note 4.
[^4]: Dadhi-kada (*dadhikādo*, so on the scan) — a festive drenching with curd mixed with water and turmeric (Dakshina, lahari 16, note 37; cf. 9). The subject of "hold" is not expressed. Dundubhis — great kettledrums (here, celestial ones). "In heaven, on earth [and] in the nether world" — *sbaraga marttya pātāla*. "Resound" — *abatare*, lit. "descend".
[^5]: "Together with the rajas" — *rājāgaṇa saṅge*: apparently musicians who came with the rajas' retinues. "Countless" — *agocara*, lit. "beyond perception".
[^6]: Chuya (*cuẏā*) — a fragrant mixture; phagu (*phāgu*) — the red powder people throw at one another at the spring festival; abir (9) — a coloured fragrant powder. "Each as he can" — *ye yemate*.
[^7]: "Silk, silken cloths" — *pāṭa paṭāmbara*. Tankas (*ṭaṅkā*) — coins. Gur (14) — unrefined cane sugar; areca nuts — *guẏā*; pan — betel leaf.
[^8]: "Power" — *anubhaba*. "The friend of the three worlds" — *trijagata-bandhu*.
[^9]: "A partial incarnation" — *aṃśa-abatāra*: the rapturous words of the people at the festival (as also in Pashchima, laharis 2 and 3). According to Gaudiya Vaishnava teaching, Rasikananda is a devotee and associate, a disciple of Shyamananda, not an incarnation of the Lord (Pashchima, lahari 4, note 37). On the author's attitude to Shyamananda and Rasika — Purva, lahari 1, note 2; Purva, lahari 14, note 11.
[^10]: Kirtaniyas (*kīrtanīẏā*) — kirtana singers. "To each one" — *gāẏa gāẏa*, lit. "body by body", i.e. to each personally (perhaps clothing them in the garments).
[^11]: Sri Govindapur — the place of the festival of separation (Pashchima, lahari 13, note 8; Pashchima, lahari 14, note 5). "Instituted" — *kaila paracāre*, "proclaimed, spread". "The [festival of] twelve [days]" — *duẏādaśa* (= *dbādaśa*), lit. "twelve": apparently the twelve-day festival in memory of Shyamananda, which became annual (3; lahari 2, 4–5); it can also be understood as "the festival of the twelfth day" (Pashchima, lahari 14, note 4).
[^12]: "The elders" — *baṛa bāpā*, lit. "elder fathers" (*bāpā* — "father"; *baṛa bāpā* — also "father's elder brother"): a respectful form of address to elders. The editor explains in a footnote: "Shyamananda Prabhu's senior disciples". Sri Kishora and Chintamani Dasa are brothers by birth (*sahodara*, 31, 37, 55); it can also be read as "Sri Kishora-Chintamani Dasa", but further on the text always speaks of two (the colophon: *kiśora-cintāmaṇira*). Kishora is apparently Kishoradasa, one of Shyamananda's early disciples (Dakshina, lahari 1, note 37; Pashchima, lahari 1, note 10; BR 15, 41); a Chintamani is named among Shyamananda's disciples in BR 15 (63–65) and by the author (Purva, lahari 1, 131; Dakshina, lahari 11, 17); the identity with them is not established. "Splendid garments" — *dibya dibya bāsa*. The author does not say why the brothers are angry; apparently they, as senior disciples, do not accept the primacy of Rasika, to whom Shyamananda handed over authority in the community (Pashchima, lahari 13, 45–47).
[^13]: "Cloth" — *śāṛī*, a length of cloth: Shyamananda tied a cloth on Rasika's head with his own hand, entrusting the community to him (Pashchima, lahari 13, 43, note 20). Rasika sees in the garment that fell on his head a repetition of that mercy. "After so long" (27) — *eta kāle*.
[^14]: "Offender" — *aparādhī*. "Not worthy [even of your] punishment" — *yogya nahi tāṛanāra*: the reproof and punishment of elders is a mercy of which Rasika considers himself unworthy; it can also be understood as "I am at fault, [but] do not deserve [such] reviling".
[^15]: "Refuge and protection" — *śaraṇa-pañjara* (Purva, lahari 1, note 62).
[^16]: "Fell ill" — *haila asusthe*. Kashiyari — Pashchima, lahari 1, note 4; Dakshina, lahari 12, note 4.
[^17]: "Love" — *anurāga*, loving attachment. 35b — *ekalā paśilā bane nāhi pāẏa lāga*: understood as "no one could catch up with him" (*lāga pāoẏā* — "to overtake, to find"); it can also be understood as "[he] cannot overtake them". "Abode" (36) — *dhāma*: apparently Kashiyari.
[^18]: "Treated" — *cāhila*, lit. "looked after, cared for" (likewise in 55). "As the physicians judged" — *baidya abhimate*.
[^19]: "Stumbled" — *ṭhekila* (40 — *ṭhekilũ*), "ran into, came to grief": the brothers understand that they have fallen ill for offending Rasika (cf. 51–52). "Before Rasika" — *rasikera sthāne*.
[^20]: "A portion of Narayana" (41) — *nārāẏaṇa-aṃśa*; "the very form of Narayana" (54) — *nārāẏaṇa-sbarūpe*: the words of the repentant brothers and the author's rapturous praise. According to Gaudiya Vaishnava teaching, Rasikananda is a devotee and associate, not an incarnation of the Lord (Pashchima, lahari 4, note 37; cf. 19); on the author's attitude to Shyamananda and Rasika — Purva, lahari 1, note 2; Purva, lahari 14, note 11. "Reviled" — *nindinu*.
[^21]: 42–50 form one enumerating sentence, continued in 51: "at the feet of such a Prabhu…". Abhirama (*abhirāma*) — apparently Abhirama Thakura (Ramadasa), an associate of Nityananda, one of the twelve gopalas.
[^22]: "Breast" — *bakṣaḥsthala*. Subala-chandra — apparently Gauridasa Pandita, who in Krishna's pastimes is the cowherd boy Subala (Purva, lahari 1, note 16), the guru of Hridayananda; cf. the aradhana of Subala-chandra (lahari 2, 10). The twelve gopalas — associates of Nityananda (Purva, lahari 1, note 18).
[^23]: The eight Giris, eight Puris, eight Bharatis (45) — apparently sannyasis of the Giri, Puri and Bharati orders connected with Sri Chaitanya (such as Ishvara Puri and Keshava Bharati); the exact list is not established. The sixty-four mahantas — Purva, lahari 1, note 20. "The eight boys" — *aṣṭa bālaka*: possibly the eight boys who enacted the sakhis at the Rasa-yatra (Pashchima, lahari 2, 47, notes 31–32); not established. "Together" — *yugate* (= *yukte*?); the reading is unclear. "In his heart" (46a) — *yā̃ra hṛdgate*.
[^24]: "With [their] associates and followers" — *sāṅgopāṅga saha*; the subject is not expressed — apparently all those named above.
[^25]: Hridayananda Chaitanya — Hridayananda (Hridaya Chaitanya), Shyamananda's guru. "Whose union with the Vrajavasis is undivided" — *brajabāsī saṅge yā̃ra milana abhinna*: Rasika is at one with the inhabitants of Vraja.
[^26]: 49 is understood thus: Krishna abides without cease in his heart (*kṛṣṇa yā̃ra hṛdaẏe thākena nirantara*). "Waking and dreaming" — *śaẏane sbapane*, lit. "lying down and in dreams". "Caste, wealth [and] life" — *jāti dhana prāṇa* (cf. Pashchima, lahari 15, 83).
[^27]: "The black serpent" — *kālasarpa*, the venomous cobra. "Truly" — *sbarūpe* (likewise in 53).
[^28]: See note 20. "Were amazed" — *camatkāra*.
[^29]: "Went to the world of Vaikuntha" — *gelā baikuṇṭha-bhubana*: so the author speaks of the departure of Vaishnavas from this world; the colophon has "the attainment of Vrindavana" (*bṛndābana-prāpti*). The subject of "weeps" is not expressed — apparently Rasika.
[^30]: "A festival [in memory] of the two" — *mahotsaba … dõhāra*: an aradhana festival in memory of the departed (Pashchima, lahari 13, note 8). "Were brought" — *ānāila*, "had brought". "That village" — apparently Kashiyari.
[^31]: Dharenda (*dhārandā*) — Shyamananda's birthplace (Purva, lahari 2, note 6). "Settled" — *upasana* (so on the scan, crop ×2): apparently *upasanna*, "arrived, settled"; the sense is approximate.
[^32]: Damodara Gosani — the learned yogi from Chakulia, a disciple of Shyamananda who departed before him (Pashchima, lahari 13, 16, note 9). "The day of the aradhana" — *ārādhana dina*: the day of remembrance. "A brahmana" — *dbija*, "twice-born"; who this is is not said. "Two or three festivals" — *dui tina*: apparently two or three days of festival (cf. lahari 2, note 5).
[^33]: "By his mercy" — *tā̃ra anugrahe*: by the mercy of Rasika. "Praise" — *kīrtta* (so on the scan; = *kīrti*). "Made known" — *karilũ bidita*. 63 — *karila yateka līlā*, "all the lilas that he performed".
[^34]: The author's signature (65) and the colophon are on pdf 180 (printed p. 136), checked against the scan; the title is as in the heading (note 1). Lahari 1 — 1–65 (pdf 178–180), completed. Below on the same page, under a rule, lahari 2 begins (files RM-4-02-001-011).
