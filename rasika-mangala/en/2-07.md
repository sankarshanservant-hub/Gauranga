# Dakshina-vibhaga. Lahari 7. The Manifestation of the Sixty-four Limbs of Bhakti

*A song in glory of Shyamananda; Rasika lives in his own home, and day by day prema-bhakti grows, and service is rendered to thousands of Vaishnavas; he honours the sadhus more than Krishna and himself dresses Sri Gopivallabha-raya with Thakurani; the author lists how Rasika firmly practises the sixty-four limbs of bhakti — from taking shelter of the guru to residing in Mathura in his mind — and, practising them himself, teaches them to others; a mere glance from him is enough for a person to surrender exclusively to Krishna; Rasika never transgresses the orders of the guru, Krishna and the sadhus, is ready to give himself with his whole family to the sadhus, and honours anyone on whom he sees the mark of a Vaishnava as equal to Krishna, be he a brahmana or a Huna or a mleccha; by his mercy all become pure in heart*

*The seventh lahari.*

*Raga Varadi.*[^1]
*Ghosha: "O ocean of mercy, O merciful Shyama!*
*Pay heed to fallen and wretched people."*

**1.** Glory, glory to Shyamananda, the life of the world,
the dear treasure of the life of Rasika-deva!

**2.** Thus Rasika remains in his own home;
day by day he awakened prema-bhakti.

**3.** Day by day [his] radiance grew ever greater:
to thousands upon thousands of Vaishnavas daily service is rendered.

**4.** He honours the sadhus more than Krishna;
apart from service to the sadhus he knows nothing else.[^2]

**5.** Day by day the service grew greatly;
apart from Krishna, Achyuta's son knows [nothing].

**6.** Sri Gopivallabha-raya together with Thakurani
he himself, sitting down, joyfully dresses.[^3]

**7.** On different days he arranges different dresses in different ways;
apart from service to Krishna he knows [nothing], day or night.

**8.** The sixty-four limbs of bhakti he performs without cease;
practising [them] himself, he diligently teaches [them] to all.[^4]

**9.** One by one he always performs all [the limbs of] bhakti;
Rasika's deeds cannot be described.

**10.** Firmly he took shelter of Sri Guru;
firmly [he took] initiation into [the service of] Krishna and so on [and] certainly learned [from the guru].[^5]

**11.** With firm faith he served Sri Guru;
whatever path the sadhus speak of — that he firmly follows.

**12.** Firmly he inquires about the true dharma from the sadhus;
firmly in mind he renounces enjoyment for Krishna's sake.[^6]

**13.** Firmly Rasika-ramana wanders through holy places —
in Dvaraka, on the Ganga, in Vraja, near Krishna.[^7]

**14.** Firmly [keeping] all the customs of which all people speak,
he does only what is needed for the purpose — beyond that nothing for himself.[^8]

**15.** Firmly he honours the day of Hari;
[he honours] the ashvattha, tulasi, dhatri, all holy places.[^9]

**16.** Living beings averse to Krishna he avoids from afar;
he has no desire to make many disciples.[^10]

**17.** Great undertakings and the like he never begins;
by his very nature [he is such] an auspicious undertaking as is not found in the three worlds.[^11]

**18.** The many books [and] arts that turn one away from Krishna —
knowing their nature, he does not study them.

**19.** Avoiding disputes, he delights everyone [with his] explanation;
in his dealings he shows no miserliness at all.[^12]

**20.** From grief and the like he is free;
other gods he never disdains.

**21.** He causes no distress to any living being on earth;
never are there offences in his service or toward the [holy] name.

**22.** Haters of Krishna, haters of the devotees, all who revile [them] —
with them he never keeps company.[^13]

**23.** All the marks of a Vaishnava are on Rasika's body:
he writes the syllables of Hari's name on every part of the body.[^14]

**24.** Nirmalya and the like he [accepts] before Krishna;
dancing, prostrating [in obeisance] like a rod — he performs them one by one.[^15]

**25.** Firmly he rises to greet the sadhus on seeing them;
seeing the sadhus, he accompanies [them] and brings [them to his home].[^16]

**26.** Temples, the Sri Murti and the like, all holy places —
the fortunate Rasika circumambulates them.

**27.** Firmly Rasikendra worships Krishna;
firmly Rasika-shekhara performs service.[^17]

**28.** With a sweet voice he sings songs before Krishna,
now sankirtana, now japa.

**29.** Joyfully Rasikendra recites hymns;
he tastes the naivedya, the water [that bathed the] feet, the nectar.[^18]

**30.** He inhales the fragrance of incense, garlands, sandal and the like;
he touches the Sri Murti — [as] the shastras enjoin.

**31.** He firmly beholds Krishna
[and] the aratis and all of Krishna's festivals.

**32.** Firmly he hears of Krishna's virtues and glory;
humbly he entreats Krishna, crying out [to Him].[^19]

**33.** Firmly he remembers Krishna's virtues and names;
firmly Rasika meditates on Krishna.

**34.** Sometimes he [is] with Krishna in the mood of a servant,
sometimes in the love of a friend for Krishna.

**35.** Sometimes with Krishna — in the rasa of prema-bhakti:
in those eight sattvika-bhavas he floats in the bliss of prema.[^20]

**36.** Rasika surrenders himself to Krishna;
all that is dear to him he offers to Krishna.

**37.** He makes various endeavours for Krishna's sake;
with his whole being Rasika is under Krishna's shelter.

**38.** Ceaselessly he serves Krishna's devotees;
he worships the feet of a devotee, honouring [him] as equal to Krishna.

**39.** As equal to Krishna he worships according to the rules
tulasi, the shastras, Mathura, the Vaishnavas and so on.[^21]

**40.** According to [his] means gathering all that is needed for this,
Rasika holds festivals together with his circle.

**41.** In Karttika he performs special service to Krishna;
[he celebrates] the yatras, the [festival of His] birth and all the rest, dedicating [them to Him].[^22]

**42.** The feet of the Sri Murti, with special affection,
Rasika-chandra worships with firm feeling in his heart.

**43.** Discourses on the Bhagavata, together with his circle,
Rasika-chandra relishes in every way.[^23]

**44.** Seeing excellent sadhus alike [to himself] in all aspirations,
with such sadhus he keeps company without cease.[^24]

**45.** Rasika always performs nama-sankirtana;
in his mind Achyuta's son dwells in Mathura.[^25]

**46.** Rasika's bhakti cannot be described:
that great soul is bhakti for Krishna embodied.[^26]

**47.** To whomever Rasika-shekhara shows mercy,
he becomes devoted to the sixty-four [limbs of] bhakti.

**48.** By the mere sight [of him one] surrenders exclusively [to Krishna]:
apart from Krishna, no one knows anything else.

**49.** He manifested the feeling of love for Krishna in all directions;
by Rasika's mercy [people] came to love Krishna.

**50.** Day by day prema-bhakti arose:
[this] Shyamananda [and] Rasika-raya brought about.[^27]

**51.** In meditation, in shelter and the like, in sleeping [and] eating,
Rasika knows nothing apart from Shyamananda.[^28]

**52.** Whether at home, within, or in other lands,
Rasikendra serves the guru, Krishna [and] the sadhus.

**53.** Practising [this] himself, he teaches all people;
seeing [his] bhakti, the three worlds are amazed.

**54.** The orders of the guru, Krishna [and] the sadhus he does not transgress:
Rasika-chandra carries [them] out, staking [his very] life.

**55.** Whatever a sadhu orders — feasible [or] unfeasible —
Achyuta's son unfailingly does it with joy.[^29]

**56.** Should a sadhu [wish to] sell [or] buy Rasika with his whole family,
Rasika with his whole family gives himself up for sale with a joyful heart.[^30]

**57.** In whomever he sees even a mere mark of a Vaishnava,
him he firmly worships as equal to Krishna.

**58.** Be it a brahmana, be it a sannyasi, be it a shudra and others,
Hunas, Pulindas, mlecchas, antyajas, Pukkashas and others —[^31]

**59.** all are filled with bliss at Rasika's touch:
apart from Krishna, the lord of their life, they care for nothing [else].[^32]

**60.** Thus at home Rasika Mahashaya
with an unshakable heart serves the guru, Krishna [and] the sadhus.

**61.** To all living beings he gave prema-bhakti,
explaining the truth and meaning of the Vedas and shastras.

**62.** Day by day prema-bhakti kept growing;
by Rasika's mercy all became pure in mind.

**63.** Rasika's greatness cannot be described:
he is endowed with all virtues, [he is] the very majesty of loveliness.

**64.** By his grace I have made known a little
of the holy fame and glory of Shyamananda [and] Rasika.[^33]

**65.** Hear the Rasika-mangala, all friends:
without delay you will obtain the treasure of prema-bhakti for Krishna.

**66.** Adorning himself with Shyamananda's feet,
the son of Rasamaya joyfully composed [this].

*Thus in the Dakshina-vibhaga of the Sri Rasika-mangala ends the seventh lahari, called "The Manifestation of the Sixty-four Limbs of Bhakti."*[^34]

[^1]: The lines before the raga are the lahari's heading ("সপ্তম-লহরী"), in italics without a number. Lahari 7 begins on the lower half of pdf 106, after the colophon of lahari 6 (files RM-2-06-014-023). Raga Varadi (*barāṛī*; cf. Dakshina, lahari 6, note 1). There is no mangala-shloka: after the raga and ghosha the song begins with ॥ 1 ॥. "Pay heed" — *kara abadhāna*; the 1997 edition has *anudhāna*. The title follows the colophon on pdf 108 (checked against the scan): "চতুঃষষ্টি-ভক্ত্যঙ্গ-প্রকাশ-নাম সপ্তম-লহরী" (*catuḥṣaṣṭi-bhaktyaṅga-prakāśa*).
[^2]: "More than Krishna" — *kṛṣṇake ādhikya kari'*, lit. "making [them] higher than Krishna." Cf. the Padma Purana (CC, Madhya 11.31): of all kinds of worship the highest is the worship of Vishnu, but higher still is the worship of His devotees (*tadīyānāṃ samarcanam*).
[^3]: Thakurani — apparently the Deity of Sri Radha, whose "wedding" with Gopivallabha-raya is described in lahari 6 (note 3). In Dakshina 3 Rasika's wife Shyamadasi is called Thakurani (Dakshina, lahari 3, 15); then one could read: "together with Thakurani [Shyamadasi] he himself… dresses Sri Gopivallabha-raya." "Dresses" — *beśa karāẏena*, a causative form: "has [Them] dressed" or "attires."
[^4]: The sixty-four angas (limbs) of bhakti — Dakshina, lahari 3, note 4. Below (10–45) the author lists them in almost the same order as the Bhakti-rasamrita-sindhu (1.2.74–92) and CC (Madhya 22.115–128), repeating "firmly" (*dṛṛhe*, *dṛṛhabhābe*); the notes below mark only departures and unclear places. "Diligently" — *tatpara*; it can also be read "teaches all [to be] devoted [to them]."
[^5]: Cf. in the list of angas: taking shelter of the guru's feet, initiation (*dīkṣā*) and instruction (*śikṣā*) from the guru, serving the guru with trust, following the path of the sadhus. "Certainly learned" — *śikhila niścaẏa*.
[^6]: "Inquires about the true dharma" — *saddharma-pṛcchā* (on the scan *-pucchā*), as in the Bhakti-rasamrita-sindhu (*sad-dharma-pṛcchā*). "Renounces enjoyment for Krishna's sake" — *bhoga-tyāga kṛṣṇera nimitte*.
[^7]: "Rasika-ramana" — *rasika-ramaṇa*, apparently an epithet of Rasika ("delightful Rasika"; cf. "Rasika-shekhara," "Rasika-chandra"). The anga is residing in Krishna's holy places (Dvaraka and others) and by the Ganga. "Near Krishna" — *kṛṣṇa-sannidhāne*.
[^8]: The couplet corresponds to the anga *yāvad-artha-anuvartitā* — "accepting only as much as is needed" for life and service. Reading by the scan (crop ×2): *dṛṛhe sarbajana kaẏe saba byabahāra / sei kare ye arthe byatīta āpanāra* (the 1997 edition has *artha*); the sense of the first line is unclear, the translation is tentative.
[^9]: The day of Hari (*haribāsara*) is Ekadashi. Dhatri (*dhātrī*) is the amalaki tree. In the Bhakti-rasamrita-sindhu the anga is honouring the ashvattha (sacred fig), tulasi, dhatri, cows, brahmanas and Vaishnavas; the author adds "all holy places."
[^10]: 16–22 are the angas of "rejection": giving up the company of those averse to Krishna, not making many disciples, not undertaking great enterprises, not studying many books, avoiding disputes, not being miserly, not yielding to grief, not disdaining the demigods, not distressing living beings, avoiding offences in service and to the holy name, not tolerating blasphemy of Krishna and His devotees.
[^11]: "Great undertakings" — *mahā ārambha*: elaborate worldly enterprises. The second line is understood tentatively: *sbabhābe ye śubhārambha nāi tribhubane*; it can also be read "by nature [he needs no] auspicious undertakings [such as abound] in the three worlds."
[^12]: The Bhakti-rasamrita-sindhu has "avoiding the study of many books, commentaries and disputes" (*bahu-granthakalābhyāsa-vyākhyā-vāda-vivarjanam*); in the author's version, on the contrary, Rasika's explanation (*byākhyā*) delights everyone. "Shows no miserliness in his dealings" — *byabahāre akārpaṇya*.
[^13]: In the Bhakti-rasamrita-sindhu the anga is not tolerating blasphemy of Krishna and His devotees; the author renders it as avoiding the company of blasphemers.
[^14]: The marks of a Vaishnava — tilaka, tulasi beads and so on. "The syllables of Hari's name" — *harināmākṣara*: names of Hari that Vaishnavas write on the body with gopi-chandana clay.
[^15]: Nirmalya (*nirmālya*) — the remnants of what was offered to the Deity: garlands, flowers and so on; the anga is wearing them. "Prostrating like a rod" — *daṇḍa-paraṇāma*, dandavat. "One by one" — *eke eke*, i.e. he performs each of the angas.
[^16]: "Rises to greet" — *abhyarthanā* (cf. *abhyutthāna*); "accompanies" — *anubraje*. In the Bhakti-rasamrita-sindhu both angas concern the Deity (rising at His appearance, following His procession); the author applies them to the sadhus.
[^17]: "Rasika-shekhara" — *rasikaśekhare*, apparently the subject form (agentive in *-e*); it can also be read "serves Rasika-shekhara," but the whole list speaks of Rasika. The angas are worship (*pūjā*) and service (*paricaryā*) of the Deity.
[^18]: Naivedya — food offered to the Deity. "The water [that bathed the] feet" — *pādya* (so on the scan; the 1997 edition has *pānya*): charanamrita. "Nectar" — *makaranda*, lit. "flower nectar," apparently also charanamrita, or honey offered to the Deity.
[^19]: "Humbly entreats, crying out" — *binaẏa… kariẏā kākuti*: the anga *vijñapti*, humble supplication.
[^20]: In the list of the Bhakti-rasamrita-sindhu, dasya and sakhya are followed by self-surrender (*ātma-nivedana*, 36); couplet 35 is the author's own addition. The eight sattvika-bhavas — becoming stunned, perspiration, hair standing on end, faltering voice, trembling, change of complexion, tears, fainting (cf. Dakshina, lahari 6, note 7).
[^21]: The anga is serving what is connected with Krishna (*tadīya*): tulasi, the shastras, Mathura, the Vaishnavas.
[^22]: Karttika (*kārttika*, *ūrja*; October–November) is the month of special service to Krishna. "Yatras" are festive processions of the Deity; "the [festival of His] birth" is Janmashtami. "Dedicating" — *kariẏā uddeśa*, lit. "with [Him] in view, on the occasion."
[^23]: The Bhakti-rasamrita-sindhu has relishing the meaning of the Bhagavata together with *rasikas*, connoisseurs of rasa; the author says "together with his circle" (*sagoṣṭhī*).
[^24]: The anga is association with sadhus who are like-minded, affectionate and more advanced (*sajātīyāśaye snigdhe sādhau saṅgaḥ svato vare*). The word on the scan (crop ×2) and in the 1997 edition is *kāmpe*; understood as *kāmye*, "in aspirations"; the reading is unclear.
[^25]: The anga is residing in Mathura-mandala; the author says "dwells in Mathura in his mind" (*mane mathurāte sthiti*).
[^26]: "Embodied" — *mūrtimanta*, "having taken form." Mahashaya — respectful "great soul."
[^27]: "Shyamananda [and] Rasika-raya brought about" — *karilena śyāmānanda rasika rāẏa*; it could also be read "Rasika-raya made [manifest the bhakti of] Shyamananda," but there appear to be two subjects (cf. lahari 4, 2, note 2).
[^28]: "In shelter" — *śaraṇa*; possibly for *smaraṇa*, "remembrance." Rasika is wholly absorbed in thoughts of his guru.
[^29]: "Feasible [or] unfeasible" — *sughaṭana durghaṭana*, "easy [or] difficult, possible [or] impossible."
[^30]: "Sell [or] buy" — *bice kine*; "gives himself up for sale" — *bikāẏa*, "is sold": Rasika with his whole family is entirely at the sadhus' disposal, like a purchased servant.
[^31]: Sannyasi — *nyāsī*. Hunas, Pulindas, mlecchas, Pukkashas — foreigners and the lowest classes; antyajas — untouchables (Dakshina, lahari 4, note 4; Dakshina, lahari 5, note 18).
[^32]: "Care for nothing" — *nāi bāse*, lit. "feel [attraction] for nothing."
[^33]: "By his grace" — *tā̃ra anugrahe*: apparently by Rasika's grace (cf. Dakshina, lahari 5, 65, note 28). "I have made known" — *karila bidita*, by the sense about himself (the author).
[^34]: The colophon is on pdf 108 (checked against the scan); on the title see note 1. Lahari 8 begins on the same page.
