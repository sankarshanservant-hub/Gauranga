# Dakshina-vibhaga. Lahari 8. The Display of Devotion to the Guru

*A song in glory of Shyamananda and Rasika; the author praises Rasika's virtues — his beauty, learning, eloquence, humility and mercy to all living beings — and blesses Utkala, his parents, his village and all who see his feet; Shyamananda comes to Balarampur, where he gains many disciples, and sends Rasika a letter: "Come to me quickly"; the letter reaches Rasika at his meal, and without finishing he rises at once and goes alone, at dusk and in the rain, through a forest where tigers, rhinoceroses and elephants live; Shyamananda, learning from the servants how he travelled, is grieved, tells him to convert the forest country (Vanabhumi), and plans the Panchama-dola festival in the month of Vaishakha at Barakola; the author urges that their pastimes not be considered human*

*The eighth lahari.*

*Raga Kaushika.*[^1]
*Ghosha: "Having beheld the moon-like face — not seeing [that] beauty, I die:*
*how can a woman keep her life?"*

**1.** Glory, glory to Shyamananda, the moon of the gopa clan!
Glory to Rasikendra-chandra, the beloved of Krishna![^2]

**2.** Thus day by day the awakening of bhakti
Achyuta's son brought about in all lands.

**3.** Rasika's deeds are exceedingly, supremely deep:
all his virtues and deeds cannot be described.

**4.** Like Vyasa, Narada and others, [as] equal to Narayana,
the people of the world worship [him], calling [him] Ishvara.[^3]

**5.** All the virtues that Krishna, the life of the world, possesses —
all those marks abide in Rasika's body.[^4]

**6.** Exceedingly lovely is the body of Rasika-shekhara;
endowed with every auspicious mark is the best one, Rasikendra.

**7.** He is so lovely that it cannot be told;
wholly full of radiance is the form of Achyuta's son.

**8.** Mighty with the strength of bhakti, youthful — [immersed] in bhajana,
in bhajana his form is wholly absorbed [in it], ever young.[^5]

**9.** In various lands, in various languages — wondrous speech
Rasika-chand utters, with rare skill.[^6]

**10.** Of all truths speaks the son of Achyuta;
like nectar are the words he speaks.

**11.** A great orator, an excellent scholar, he has no equal;
no one has an intelligence equal to Rasika's.

**12.** If [he] is answered, he gives a reply to the answer;
expert in every virtue is Rasika, the jewel of virtues.

**13.** Rasika-shekhara is full of great resourcefulness;
the son of Achyuta is perfect in the refined arts.

**14.** The crest-jewel of the clever is Achyuta's son;
adept in all tasks, discerning is the great soul.

**15.** All the dharmas of good deeds he knows directly;
constantly he keeps firmly the vow [of service] to Krishna.[^7]

**16.** Rasikendra is renowned [for knowing] place, time and the worthy;
the scriptures are his constant sight: [for this] he is famed in the world.[^8]

**17.** Exceedingly pure is Prabhu, the life of the world;
by love of Krishna he has won over these three worlds.

**18.** Supremely steady is Rasika-shekhara;
surpassing the hosts of Indras, he is an ascetic [in his very] body.[^9]

**19.** Immensely wondrous forgiveness he shows to all living beings;
such goodness of character no one has had or will have.

**20.** Most profound and steadfast is Rasika Murari;
equal-minded to all living beings, possessed of every virtue.

**21.** A great giver is Rasika, there is none [like him];
there is no one in the world to compare him with.

**22.** In all dharmas righteous is Rasika Mahashaya;
mighty with the strength of bhakti, [he] destroys the sins of the world.

**23.** A wondrously compassionate form, mercy to all living beings;
he honours those worthy of honour, being kind.

**24.** Every day Rasikendra-chandra is very happy;
he is the friend of all, he is the joy of all.

**25.** Very much subject to love is the son of Achyuta;
Rasikendra does good to these three worlds.

**26.** Mighty in bhakti to Krishna is Rasika-chudamani,
by whose might false learning has left the earth.[^10]

**27.** The fame of Rasika-deva is known to the world;
all people are devoted [to him] for his life.

**28.** The shelter of all people [and] sadhus is Rasikendra;
by the power of bhakti he steals the hearts of multitudes.

**29.** Most fortunate are all the people of the world
who see Rasika: [they] honour [him] as equal to Krishna.[^11]

**30.** Worshipful to all is Rasika Mahashaya;
[his] wealth is highly esteemed, [he is] the dawn of great happiness.[^12]

**31.** Of most courteous character is Rasika-shekhara;
his soul is renounced, [in his whole] body he is most humble.

**32.** Most modest is Rasikendra, the great one;
he is surely the protector of those who take shelter [of him].

**33.** Supremely great [are his] virtues, equal to [those of] Ishvara;
not even with a hundred mouths can the host of his virtues be told.[^13]

**34.** Blessed is the earth, blessed is Utkala, blessed is the holy abode!
Blessed is the father, blessed the mother in whose womb [he] rested![^14]

**35.** Blessed is that village where he took birth;
blessed is that place where those feet tread.

**36.** Blessed is that village where he dwells;
blessed is that place where the pastimes of love [take place].

**37.** Blessed are the companions with whom he sports;
blessed are [his] kin, friends and all his household.

**38.** Blessed are all the men and women of Utkala
who behold Rasika's feet.

**39.** By [his] darshana all sins are dispelled;
hearing Rasika's words, the ears are refreshed.

**40.** Soft, deep, gentle, sweet is his speech;
nowhere is this sweetness heard mixed [with anything].[^15]

**41.** Every syllable is sprinkled with nectar;
hearing those words, all forget themselves.

**42.** A gentle smile ever rains from [his] lips;
seeing [his] form, all people float in the bliss of love.

**43.** He destroyed all evil desires in people's minds;
all people became embodiments of prema-bhakti for Krishna.

**44.** Thus Rasika at home, night and day,
constantly serves the guru, Krishna and the sadhus.

**45.** Thus after some days Shyamananda-raya
made his way to Greater Balarampur.[^16]

**46.** Prabhu delighted [all] with the rasa of talks about Krishna;
he especially took all under Shyamananda's shelter.[^17]

**47.** Many men and women in that village became [his] disciples:
Gopinatha, Jagannatha, Akrura, Sri Hari,

**48.** the charming boy Radhavallabhadasa,
Shyamadasa and others — all followers of Shyamananda.[^18]

**49.** The raja [and] the subjects all became [his] followers;
all received initiation into [the mantra of] Krishna, giving up their own [former] views.

**50.** In the forest country he showed mercy to all the people;
to all Shyamananda gave the shade of [his] feet.[^19]

**51.** After some days, from there Shyamananda-raya
sends a messenger to bring Rasika.

**52.** With his own hand he wrote a letter:
"Come quickly yourself and see me."

**53.** Having done the service of Krishna [and] the service of the sadhus, Rasika
then sat down to his meal, taking prasada.

**54.** He had only just taken the first mouthful
when at that very moment the letter arrived.

**55.** In the letter, the order: "Come quickly."
The second mouthful of prasada is [still] in his hand.[^20]

**56.** Rasikendra rose, hearing the guru's order;
he himself washed his hands in the Suvarnarekha.

**57.** Having done achamana, he set off at once in that direction;
the day came to an end, darkness lay ahead.[^21]

**58.** Tigers, rhinoceroses, elephants — all live in the forest parts;
[even] by day [people] do not go alone — it is so fearsome.

**59.** Along that road Rasika went alone;
a light rain fell, clouds covered the sky.

**60.** In the darkness one cannot see oneself;
at such an hour, alone, disregarding his meal,

**61.** bearing the order on his head, chanting the name "Hare Krishna,"
Rasikendra entered Balarampur.[^22]

**62.** Seeing [him], Shyamananda was very pleased;
embracing him, he seated him before himself.

**63.** Seeing his face withered by the fatigue of the road and by fasting,
he asked how he had come so quickly.

**64.** [Rasika] says not a word, his head bowed in shyness.
After a while all the servants arrived there.

**65.** They told how everything had gone on the way;
hearing it, Prabhu was boundlessly grieved.[^23]

**66.** Having bathed, eaten and so on, sitting down and gathering an assembly,
he said: "Listen, child, Rasika Murari.

**67.** I have heard that you made Dharenda Vaishnava;
now instruct the whole forest country.

**68.** There is one desire in my heart:
I will hold the Panchama-dola in the month of Vaishakha.[^24]

**69.** The place Barakola is very beautiful to see:
dense forest, mango [groves], a charming river.[^25]

**70.** I will begin the great festival at that place;
come in good time with all the provisions.

**71.** I will go there first and proclaim [it];
and you, there in Dharenda, make everything ready."[^26]

**72.** From there Shyamananda departed.
Listen to the Rasika-mangala, all friends!

**73.** In which places which pastimes the two of them performed,
of that I will briefly describe something.

**74.** Do not think [these are] human pastimes:
from yuga to yuga, descending, [they perform] pastimes in different ways.[^27]

**75.** Adorning himself with Shyamananda's feet,
the son of Rasamaya joyfully composed [this].

*Thus [ends] in the Sri Rasika-mangala, in the Dakshina-vibhaga, the eighth lahari named "The Display of Devotion to the Guru."*[^28]

[^1]: The lines before the raga are the lahari's heading ("অষ্টম-লহরী"), in italics without a number. Lahari 8 begins on the lower half of pdf 108, after the colophon of lahari 7 (files RM-2-07-001-066). Raga Kaushika (*kauśika*; cf. Purva, lahari 3, note 1). The ghosha (refrain) is the words of a gopi longing for Krishna. There is no mangala-shloka: after the raga and ghosha the song begins with ॥ 1 ॥. The title follows the colophon on pdf 110 (checked against the scan): "গুরুভক্তিপ্রদর্শন-নাম অষ্টম-লহরী" (*gurubhakti-pradarśana*). The summary line covers the whole lahari (1–4 from the scan; 5–75 from the OCR of the full edition, provisionally): Balarampur (*baṛa balarāmapura*), Vanabhumi (*banabhūmi*), Barakola (*baṛakolā*) should be checked when translating.
[^2]: "The moon of the gopa clan" — *gopakula-śaśī*: Shyamananda was of the gopa caste (Purva, lahari 1, note 9); it is also an epithet of Krishna. "The beloved of Krishna" — *kṛṣṇera preẏasī* (feminine): in this way the author exalts Rasika, speaking of him as a gopi. On the same word applied to Shyamananda, and on Gaudiya Vaishnava teaching that Shyamananda is a devotee and servant of Radha, not Radhika Herself, see Purva, lahari 1, note 2; Purva, lahari 14, note 11; on Rasikananda — Purva, lahari 1, note 69 (a devotee and associate, not an incarnation of the Lord). The line can also be read as three praises: "Glory to Rasikendra-chandra, [glory] to the beloved of Krishna [Sri Radha]!"
[^3]: "Calling [him] Ishvara" — *īśbara baliẏā*: "Lord, master." These words describe how people honour him; according to Gaudiya Vaishnava teaching Rasikananda is a devotee and associate, not an incarnation of the Lord (Purva, lahari 1, note 69; Dakshina, lahari 5, note 26). Cf. Vishvanatha Chakravarti's Gurv-ashtaka (7): all the shastras call the guru Hari Himself, and the sadhus regard him so, because he is dear to the Lord. Vyasa and Narada are great sages and devotees, honoured as empowered manifestations of the Lord; Narayana is the Lord.
[^4]: Verses 5–33 follow, almost in order, the list of the fifty virtues of Krishna as hero in the Bhakti-rasamrita-sindhu 2.1 (cf. CC, Madhya 23): a lovely body, auspicious marks, radiance, strength, youth, knowledge of languages, eloquence, learning, resourcefulness, refinement, adroitness, firmness in vows, knowledge of place, time and the worthy, seeing through the scriptures, purity, steadiness, forgiveness, profundity, equanimity, generosity, righteousness, compassion, humility, modesty, protection of the surrendered, subjection to love, might, fame, being the shelter of sadhus, being worshipful to all, wealth, greatness and, finally, "Ishvara." In this way the author praises Rasikananda (cf. lahari 7: the sixty-four limbs of bhakti). According to the Bhakti-rasamrita-sindhu (2.1), these virtues are present in a small measure, "in drops," in living beings as well, but in fullness only in Krishna; according to Gaudiya Vaishnava teaching Rasikananda is a devotee and associate, not an incarnation of the Lord (Purva, lahari 1, note 69).
[^5]: "Youthful — [immersed] in bhajana" — *kiśora bhajana*: apparently "youthful" (*kiśora*, cf. "youth" in the list of the Bhakti-rasamrita-sindhu); it can also be understood as "[his] bhajana is [worship of] Kishora," that is, of the youthful Krishna. "Wholly absorbed" — *tanmaya-mūrti*.
[^6]: "In various languages" — *nānā-bhāṣā* (so in the scan; garbled in the OCR of the 1997 edition). Rasika-chand (*rasika-cā̃da*) is Rasikananda.
[^7]: "All the dharmas of good deeds" — *sukṛti-sakala dharma*; at this point the list of the Bhakti-rasamrita-sindhu has "grateful" (*kṛtajña*). "The vow [of service] to Krishna" — *kṛṣṇa-brata*.
[^8]: "Place, time and the worthy" — *deśa kāla supātra*: he knows what is fitting in which place, at which time and with which person (*deśa-kāla-supātra-jña*). "The scriptures are his sight" — *śāstra-dṛṣṭi* (*śāstra-cakṣus*).
[^9]: "The hosts of Indras" — *indra-gaṇa* (so in the scan and in the 1997 edition); in view of the sense (at this point the list of the Bhakti-rasamrita-sindhu has "one who has subdued the senses"), the reading *indriya-gaṇa* is possible: "having conquered the senses." "An ascetic" — *tapobanta*.
[^10]: "False learning" — *kubidyā*: in the scan the ligature looks like "কুবিত্তা," taken as কুবিদ্যা; garbled in the OCR of the 1997 edition. Rasika-chudamani — "the crest-jewel of rasikas," Rasikananda.
[^11]: "Honour [him] as equal to Krishna" — *mānī kṛṣṇa-sama*. These are the author's words; cf. notes 3 and 4.
[^12]: "[His] wealth is highly esteemed" — *bahumāna sampatti*: at this point the list of the Bhakti-rasamrita-sindhu has "wealthy" (*samṛddhimān*); the sense of the line is approximate.
[^13]: "Ishvara" — *īśvara*, the Lord. The author's rapturous words; cf. verse 4 and notes 3 and 4. "With a hundred mouths" — cf. Ananta-Shesha, who sings the Lord's glory with a thousand mouths.
[^14]: Utkala is Orissa. "The holy abode" — *puṇya-dhāma*. Rasikananda's father and mother are Achyuta and Bhavani (Purva-vibhaga).
[^15]: The line is unclear: *kothāo miśrita nahe se madhura śuni* — apparently "nowhere is such pure (unmixed) sweetness heard"; it can also be understood as "his sweet speech is mixed with nothing."
[^16]: Greater Balarampur — *baṛa balarāmapura* (*baṛa*, "big"): Balarampur, where Shyamananda preached (BR 15, 59: "what prema-bhakti merciful Shyamananda made manifest in Balarampura cannot be told"); in 61 — *balarāmapurī*. "Made his way" — *karilā bijaẏa*, a respectful expression for the arrival of a saint.
[^17]: "Prabhu" here is Shyamananda. "Under shelter" — *āśre* (= *āśraẏe*; so in the scan, cut-out ×2). The line can also be understood as "all [of them] especially took Shyamananda's shelter."
[^18]: Gopinatha, Jagannatha, Akrura, Sri Hari, Radhavallabhadasa, Shyamadasa are Shyamananda's disciples in Balarampur; Akrura and Jagannatha are also named in BR 15 (64) among Shyamananda's disciples. Not identified.
[^19]: "The forest country" — *banabhūmi* (Dakshina, lahari 2, note 10): the wooded lands west of Medinipur; likewise in 67.
[^20]: 53–55: at the guru's order Rasika leaves his prasada, having barely begun the meal — hence the name of the lahari (colophon). "The letter arrived" — *likhā āsi haila upasanna*, lit. "the letter came and was at hand."
[^21]: Achamana (*ācamana*) — rinsing the mouth and hands with water after eating. The Suvarnarekha is the river on whose bank Gopivallabhapur stands; apparently Rasika is at home at this time (44). "In that direction" — *sei mukhe*; it can also be understood as "right then."
[^22]: Balarampur — *balarāmapurī* (cf. 45 and note 16).
[^23]: "Prabhu" is Shyamananda. "How everything had gone on the way" — *gamanera saba byabahāra*.
[^24]: Panchama-dola (*pañcama-dola*) — the swing festival (dola) of Krishna; apparently the Pancha-dola celebrated in Utkala after the Dola-purnima (the spring full moon). The author places the festival in the month of Vaishakha (April–May) and calls its night "the spring full moon" (lahari 9, 18). "Vaishakha" — *boiśākha*, so in the scan.
[^25]: Barakola (*baṛakolā*) — the village where Shyamananda held the festival (laharis 9–10). "Mango [groves]" — *āmra*, so in the scan (garbled in the OCR of both editions); it can also be taken as the name of the river.
[^26]: "In good time" — *sakāle*, "in time, beforehand" (or "in the morning"). "Make everything ready" — *karaha susāra*, "arrange properly."
[^27]: "The two of them" (73) — Shyamananda and Rasikananda. "Descending" — *abatari'*: in this way the author exalts them (cf. Purva, lahari 1, notes 2 and 69; Purva, lahari 14, note 11). According to Gaudiya Vaishnava teaching, Shyamananda is a devotee and servant of Radha, a disciple of Hridaya Chaitanya, and Rasikananda is his disciple, a devotee and associate; neither is an incarnation of the Lord, but their pastimes, like those of all pure devotees, are not ordinary human doings.
[^28]: The name is given by the colophon (checked against the scan): "গুরুভক্তিপ্রদর্শন-নাম অষ্টম-লহরী" (*guru-bhakti-pradarśana*). The colophon ends the lahari on pdf 110 (cf. note 1); lahari 9 begins on pdf 111 (files RM-2-09-001-016).
