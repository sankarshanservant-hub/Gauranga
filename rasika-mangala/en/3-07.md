# Pashchima-vibhaga. Lahari 7. The Preaching of the Holy Name and the Description of the Wild Elephant's Ravages

*A song in glory of Shyamananda; Rasika, intoxicated with love for Krishna, travels to Banapur, staying nowhere longer than two days, and everywhere thousands become his disciples; he commands his disciples Ramakrishna and Dinashyama, and also Vamshidasa, to give the Hari-nama to all, from rajas to chandalas, and countless low-born people in the forest country and in Utkala are converted; at Banapur is camped the cruel governor Ahmed Beg, who has ruined the rajas and bhunyas of Orissa and keeps them about him in fear; Rasika stays with Raja Vaidyanatha, rajas, subjects, officials and hundreds of yavanas flock to him, and Shyamananda's affair concerning the village is settled by a document; the governor, seeing the crowds going to Rasika with the cry of "Hari," grows angry, reviles him as a cheat and sends for him, demanding a miracle like Kabir's; the rajas are afraid and his companions urge Rasika to flee, but he goes to the governor; meanwhile a wild, maddened elephant is ravaging Banapur, and when Rasika arrives the elephant bursts into the town, all flee in terror to the rooftops, and Rasika resolves to save everyone and make the elephant a sadhu — that elephant will become his servant*

*The seventh lahari.*

*Raga Mallar.*[^1]
*Ghosha: "Crusher of the Daityas, enemy of the Daityas.*
*Glory to the Son of Yadu, worshipped in the three worlds,*
*Slayer of invincible asuras!"*

**1.** Glory, glory to Shyamananda, the treasury of mercy!
At this time Rasikendra set out on his way.[^2]

**2.** In great intoxication with the bliss of Krishna [his] body trembles;
his whole body is wet with the tears of his eyes.[^3]

**3.** Unceasingly — conversations about Krishna;
on the roads and at the landings — an unceasing search for Krishna.

**4.** Not for a moment does he know anything but Krishna;
lying down, eating, sleeping — always remembrance of Krishna.[^4]

**5.** Wherever he sits for a moment, tired from the road,
thousands upon thousands of people come to see [him].

**6.** Seeing [his] sweet form, they forget themselves;
hearing the words from [his] holy mouth, they rejoice within.

**7.** [His] half-spoken words are like nectar;
hearing [them], all people find their hearts at peace.[^5]

**8.** As soon as they hear those words the three kinds of suffering depart;
wholly surrendered, [people] serve Krishna with bhakti.[^6]

**9.** By Rasika's touch [they] become embodiments of prema;
day after day hundreds of thousands become disciples.[^7]

**10.** In one place he halts, in another he bathes,
somewhere [he takes] food — [but] he does not rest.

**11.** Though hundreds of thousands of people [came to him] on the road,
he did not stay two days [anywhere], [following] his guru's command.[^8]

**12.** In village after village there arose a multitude of disciples;
[and] disciples' disciples, servant-disciples — they cannot be counted.[^9]

**13.** Rasika's disciples Ramakrishna [and] Dinashyama —
both are very great treasuries of prema-bhakti.[^10]

**14.** Seeing the pure hearts of those two,
Rasika-shekhara commanded [them] again and again:

**15.** "You two, give the Hari-nama to all people;
deliver the poor, the fallen — down to the chandalas.

**16.** Give the Hari-nama to all the rajas and subjects;
give prema-bhakti to everyone [in] the forest country.[^11]

**17.** Shyamananda-raya begged alms of me:
'Deliver living beings by my command.'[^12]

**18.** In the same way I beg alms of you two:
give the Hari-nama to everyone in Utkala."

**19.** Hearing this, both fell at [his] feet in obeisance:
"What paltry power have I, O son of Achyuta!"[^13]

**20.** Earlier Shyamananda had commanded these two:
"Whomever you touch will have Vishnu-bhakti."[^14]

**21.** By that command, through the sight [and] touch of these two,
millions upon millions in the land of the forest country became disciples.

**22.** All manner of low-born castes were delivered;
apart from service to sadhus they know nothing else.[^15]

**23.** They serve the guru, serve Krishna, serve the brahmanas;
all, wholly surrendered, [became] servants of Krishna.

**24.** Thus Rasikendra says to his disciples:
"Wherever anyone may be — give everyone the Hari-nama."[^16]

**25.** Rasikendra commanded Vamshidasa:
"Giving the Hari-nama, accept disciples in crowds."[^17]

**26.** Thus for some days Rasika-Murari,
joyfully playing [on the way], went on and reached Banapur.[^18]

**27.** Listen, everyone, I will tell how it was:
Ahmed Beg was a very wicked yavana.[^19]

**28.** All the rajas [and] bhunyas who live in the land of Orissa —
he especially ruined all their homes.[^20]

**29.** He destroyed houses [and] homesteads, cut down all the groves;
the yavana seized them all and took them along with him.[^21]

**30.** Very mighty is the wicked yavana ruler;
all the bhunyas [and] rajas tremble.

**31.** All serve [him] there in Banapur;
from fear a single day passes like a yuga.[^22]

**32.** Every day he kills two or four;
very wicked deeds that yavana does.[^23]

**33.** Everyone's heart is anxious, [all are] distraught with fear;
within their minds [they] remember Krishna.

**34.** At this time Rasikendra entered that village;
he stayed there with Raja Vaidyanatha.[^24]

**35.** Seeing [him], the raja fell at [his] feet with reverence;
washing [his] feet, he seated [him] on a seat.

**36.** Rasika-shekhara speaks the nectar of talks about Krishna;
hearing the words from [his] holy mouth, all are overwhelmed [with bliss].

**37.** The raja was very happy at seeing Rasika;
in his own palace he gave [him] lodging in a splendid place.[^25]

**38.** Every day all the rajas go there;
the Prabhu tells everyone of the truth of Krishna.

**39.** Day and night rajas [and] subjects go to that lodging;
an enormous crowd of people — beyond counting.[^26]

**40.** Following one another, all, hearing of it, run quickly
to see Rasikendra, the crest-jewel [of devotees].[^27]

**41.** Hearing [his] sweet words, all in bliss
took refuge in Sri Rasikananda.

**42.** All the rajas became wholly surrendered [to him];
following their example, all their people came as well.

**43.** All the Hindu clans, his kayasthas [and] karanas —
all took refuge in Rasikendra.[^28]

**44.** By Rasika's touch [they] became wholly surrendered [to Krishna];
remaining in worldly affairs, all are intoxicated with the taste of Krishna.[^29]

**45.** That village of Banapur was stirred to its depths;
in the taste of sankirtana [people] do not know night from day.[^30]

**46.** Unceasingly [he] plays with singing and music;
Achyuta's son gave prema to all people.[^31]

**47.** [In] the matter for which Shyamananda-raya had sent [him] —
as soon as he heard [of it], he had everyone [draw up] a document.

**48.** The document was sent to Shyamananda;
for some days Rasikendra remained in that village.[^32]

**49.** In Banapur [was] the subahdar, and with him a crowd of yavanas;
every day they come and see Rasika.

**50.** Only the subahdar does not go to that place;
rajas, subjects, Hindus and others, all the yavanas —

**51.** all go every day to see Rasika;
hundreds upon hundreds of yavanas and others easily became disciples.[^33]

**52.** A great festival arose in that village of Banapur;
people beyond counting come night and day.[^34]

**53.** The wicked subahdar sits on his terrace;
he saw with his own eyes: hundreds of thousands of people standing there.[^35]

**54.** Every day, constantly, thus [they] come and go;
seeing [this], the villain grew angry [and] asks everyone:

**55.** "Where are you all going, on what business?
You go by hundreds of thousands, crying 'Hari!' on the road."

**56.** Hearing this, all the eminent people say:
"Sri Rasika-Murari is Krishna's very own form.[^36]

**57.** [He is] the greatest mahanta in all this Orissa,
with whom Jagannatha converses constantly.[^37]

**58.** All the rajas [and] subjects who live in Orissa —
all have become disciples at Rasika's feet.

**59.** All these people who are [now] around you —
all have become Rasika's disciples.

**60.** Hundreds upon hundreds of yavanas have become his disciples;
he is not a man — he is a partial incarnation."

**61.** In many ways everyone told [of his] greatness;
the subahdar says: "I will see him — bring [him] before me."[^38]

**62.** That yavana ruler was very wicked;
hearing of [his] greatness, he speaks evil words in anger:

**63.** "Let him make Hindus his disciples — that is no concern of mine;
[but] it is not fitting for him to make yavanas his disciples.[^39]

**64.** With false show he deceives people;
he performs tricks and shows to take [their] goods.[^40]

**65.** When he shows me a miracle,
then I will acknowledge him as Narayana."[^41]

**66.** He quickly sent a messenger to bring Rasika:
"When he shows me a miracle —[^42]

**67.** [for] formerly divine great souls — Kabir and others,
a host of mahantas — have shown miracles.[^43]

**68.** Then I will acknowledge all [of them] as equal to Ishvara,
if I see any miracle at all with these eyes."[^44]

**69.** The yavana said to all the Hindu rajas:
"Bring Rasika-Murari-dasa here."[^45]

**70.** All the rajas were greatly afraid on hearing [this]:
"Why has Prabhu come to where the yavana is?[^46]

**71.** We do not know what miracle he wants to see;
if he does not see [one] — who knows what [this] villain will do."

**72.** Great sorrow arose in the hearts of the rajas;
the son of Achyuta heard [of it] from everyone.[^47]

**73.** Prabhu declared: "I shall go to him."
In many ways [his] companions tried to dissuade [him]:[^48]

**74.** "Why, Prabhu, will you go to the yavana?
Come, let us all flee — from forest to forest.

**75.** Saving our lives, come, let us flee home.
Why [deal] with a yavana — what is there to deliberate so much?"[^49]

**76.** At that time in Banapur, day after day,
a forest elephant was wreaking havoc.[^50]

**77.** Many houses it has smashed; men and women,
horses [and] elephants it kills day after day.

**78.** [People] do not go along the roads or to the landings for fear and terror of it;
the subahdar and everyone else were especially afraid.[^51]

**79.** By night or by day the king of elephants comes
[and] every day wreaks havoc in Banapur.

**80.** Prabhu went beyond everyone's words
[and] quickly set out to see the subahdar.[^52]

**81.** When the ruler was sitting on his terrace
[and] all the Hindu rajas were sitting around [him], —[^53]

**82.** at that time he set out for the subahdar;
[his] companions go [with him], delighting in various rasas.

**83.** Overwhelmed with love for Krishna, [his] heart rejoices.
Now listen attentively to the greatness of Rasika.[^54]

**84.** At that time, suddenly, that king of elephants
came [and] burst into Banapur.

**85.** A maddened forest elephant, most terrible,
of furious might, vicious, of huge body.

**86.** The elephant, crazed by great mada, heeds nothing;
all the houses [and] doors it smashes [and] breaks to pieces.[^55]

**87.** It lets out a most dreadful roar incessantly;
the earth trembles, and [even] the clouds.

**88.** With its breath the dust flies up into the vault of heaven;
under the weight of its steps the earth sinks into Rasatala.[^56]

**89.** Seizing them with its trunk, it uproots all the trees by force;
seizing horses [and] elephants, it kills [them] for sport.[^57]

**90.** The maddened elephant killed many people;
in confusion all fled to other lands.

**91.** Some onto the tops of houses, some onto terraces —
they hastily climbed up to high places.[^58]

**92.** In great fear all Banapur trembles;
thinking [it was] the end of the world, all were in turmoil.

**93.** All say: "Protect us, Lord Narayana!
For nothing we have all lost our lives!"[^59]

**94.** Thus everyone cries out "alas!";
the prince of Achyuta knew the minds of all.[^60]

**95.** He resolved in his mind: "Today I shall save everyone;
I shall make the elephant a supreme, wholly devoted sadhu."

**96.** Such a forest elephant [became] the servant of Rasika —
I shall tell how it happened; listen attentively.[^61]

**97.** Listen, listen, all, to the Rasika-mangala —
without delay you will obtain the wealth of prema-bhakti for Krishna.[^62]

**98.** Adorning himself with Shyamananda's feet,
the son of Rasamaya joyfully composed [this].

*Thus in the Pashchima-vibhaga of the Sri Rasika-mangala ends the seventh lahari, called "The Preaching of the Holy Name and the Description of the Wild Elephant's Ravages."*[^63]

[^1]: The lines before the raga are the lahari's heading ("সপ্তম-লহরী"), in italics without a number. Lahari 7 begins on a new page — pdf 146 (printed p. 106; no page number or running head printed), after the colophon of lahari 6 on pdf 145 (files RM-3-06-004-020). Raga Mallar (*mollāra*, so in the scan; garbled in the OCR); the ghosha is as in Dakshina, lahari 4 (note 1): names of Krishna, "Crusher of the Daityas" (*daitya-dalana*), "Enemy of the Daityas" (*daityāri*); here it is continued by a tripadi line: *jaẏa yadunandana, tribhubana-bandana, durjjaẏa asura-saṃhārī*. There is no mangala-shloka: after the raga and ghosha the song begins with ॥ 1 ॥. The title follows the colophon on pdf 148 (printed p. 108), checked against the scan: "ইতি শ্রীরসিকমঙ্গল পশ্চিম-বিভাগে হরিনাম-প্রচার ও বন্যহস্তীর উপদ্রব-বর্ণন-নাম সপ্তম-লহরী সম্পূর্ণা" (*harināma-pracāra o banyahastīra upadraba-barṇana*) — "The Preaching of the Holy Name and the Description of the Wild Elephant's Ravages". The summary line covers the whole lahari (67–98 from the scan of pdf 148, provisionally).
[^2]: "Set out on his way" — *prayāṇa karila*: to Banapur (lahari 6, 18–20).
[^3]: "Trembles" — *jara jara*: "is overcome, all aquiver".
[^4]: "Not for a moment" — *eka tila*, lit. "[not for] a sesame seed". "Remembrance" — *dhyāna*, "meditation".
[^5]: "Half-spoken words" — *ādha ādha kathākhāni*: soft, broken speech (cf. lahari 6, 6). "Find their hearts at peace" — *juṛāẏa parāṇa*, lit. "life is cooled (comforted)".
[^6]: "The three kinds of suffering" — *tina tāpa*: those caused by one's own body and mind, by other beings, and by the forces of nature. "Wholly surrendered" — *ananyaśaraṇa*, "having no other refuge".
[^7]: "Embodiments of prema" — *premamaẏa mūrti*. "Hundreds of thousands" — *lakṣa lakṣa*, "lakhs and lakhs" (a lakh is a hundred thousand); so also below.
[^8]: "Though… [came]" — *tabe … pathete yāite*: the sense of the line is approximate; understood as: although crowds flocked to him on the road, he stayed nowhere longer than two days. "His guru's command" — Shyamananda's (lahari 6, 18–19).
[^9]: "Disciples' disciples, servant-disciples" — *anu-śiṣya bhṛtya-śiṣya*.
[^10]: Ramakrishna and Dinashyama (*dinaśyāma*) are disciples of Rasika (Purva, lahari 1, 132; Pashchima 1, note 19). "Treasuries" — *nidhāna*.
[^11]: "The forest country" — *banabhūmi* (Dakshina, lahari 2, note 10).
[^12]: On the alms Shyamananda begged of Rasika, see Dakshina, lahari 4, 3 ff.
[^13]: "What paltry power have I" — *mui kona chāra śakti*: singular — understood as the words of each of the two. "O son of Achyuta" — addressed to Rasika (*acyuta-nandane*).
[^14]: "Vishnu-bhakti" — *biṣṇubhakti*, devotion to Vishnu (Krishna).
[^15]: "Low-born castes" — *antyaja jāti*.
[^16]: "Wherever anyone may be" — *yathāẏa ye yāhā*.
[^17]: Vamshidasa — the author's uncle, Rasamaya's brother (lahari 6, 15, 19). "In crowds" — *bṛnda bṛnda*.
[^18]: "Joyfully playing [on the way]" — *kheli kheli*, lit. "playing, playing": apparently with sankirtana. "Banapur" — here *bāṇapurī* (lahari 6, 18, 20).
[^19]: Ahmed Beg — the subahdar (lahari 6, 17). "Yavana" — a Muslim. "How it was" — *tā'ra bibaraṇa*. BR 15 (83–85) says briefly that Rasikananda made a disciple of the elephant a villain had set upon him, and that "that villain, the yavana king," bowed before him.
[^20]: Orissa — *uṛiṣyā*. Bhunyas (*bhuñā*) — bhuiyans, local landholders and chiefs (Dakshina, lahari 10, note 16). "Especially" — *biśeṣe*.
[^21]: "Seized them all and took them along with him" — *saṅge dhari' laila*: apparently he forced the rajas and bhunyas to stay in attendance on him (cf. 31).
[^22]: "Serve [him]" — *sebā kare*: apparently they were in attendance on the governor. A yuga is a world-age, an immense span of time.
[^23]: "Kills" — *saṃhāraṇa kare*.
[^24]: Raja Vaidyanatha (*baidyanātha*) — not identified (cf. Purva, lahari 1, 132 and note 49: Rasika's disciple Vaidyanatha). "Village" — *grāma*: Banapur. "Stayed" — *utarila*.
[^25]: "Palace" — *mandire*: here a house or palace. "Lodging" — *bāsā*.
[^26]: "Beyond counting" — *nāhi samucaẏa* (cf. lahari 4, note 44).
[^27]: "Following one another" — *dekhā dekhi*, lit. "seeing [one another]" (so also in 42). "Crest-jewel" — *cūṛāmaṇi*, "the jewel in a crown".
[^28]: Kayasthas and karanas (*kāẏeta karaṇa*) — castes of scribes and officials (Purva, lahari 3, note 23). "His" — *tā'ra*: apparently the Hindu officials in the governor's service; unclear.
[^29]: "Remaining in worldly affairs" — *biṣaẏe thākiẏā*: without leaving home and duties. "The taste of Krishna" — *kṛṣṇarase*.
[^30]: "Was stirred to its depths" — *ṭalaṭala hailā*. "Do not know night from day" — *niśi diśi nāi jāna*.
[^31]: "Singing and music" — *saṅgīta sāhitya* (lahari 6, note 19). "Achyuta's son" — *acyutera bālā*.
[^32]: 47–48: "Document" — *patra*, "letter, deed". Understood as the matter of the village of Radhanagar (lahari 6, 12–17): as soon as he hears of it, [the rajas and officials] draw up a document at Rasika's word, and it is sent to Shyamananda. Literally "by mere hearing he makes all [do] a document" (*śrabaṇa mātreke patra karāẏa sabāẏa*); the sense is approximate.
[^33]: "Easily" — *hele*, "lightly, effortlessly".
[^34]: "A great festival" — *mahāyātrā*.
[^35]: "On his terrace" — *jagatīte*: *jagatī* — a raised platform or terrace (cf. 81). "Standing" — *ubhā* (cf. Purva, lahari 10, note 30).
[^36]: 56–60 are the words of the eminent people to the subahdar. "Krishna's very own form" — *kṛṣṇera svarūpa*; "partial incarnation" (60) — *aṃśa-abatāra*: rapturous praise. According to Gaudiya Vaishnava teaching, Rasika is a devotee, a disciple of Shyamananda, not an incarnation (Pashchima 2, note 28). On how the author exalts Shyamananda and Rasika, see Purva, lahari 1, note 2; Purva, lahari 14, note 11.
[^37]: "Mahanta" — *mohānta* (*mahānta*), a revered teacher, the head of a community. Jagannatha is the Lord, the Deity of Puri. "With whom Jagannatha converses constantly" — a tradition expressing Rasika's intimacy with the Lord; it is not in BR 15.
[^38]: "Bring [him] before me" — *āna bidyamāne*, lit. "bring into [my] presence".
[^39]: "That is no concern of mine" — *tā'ra nāhi dāẏa*. "It is not fitting" — *nā yuẏāẏa*.
[^40]: "Show" — *āṛambari*. "Deceives" — *bhāṇḍibāre*, "in order to deceive". "Tricks and shows" — *caṭaka nāṭaka*. "Goods" — *drabya*, "property".
[^41]: "Miracle" — *keramati* (Arabic-Persian *karāmat*, a saint's miracle). The author exalts Rasika with accounts of miracles (cf. lahari 4, note 49).
[^42]: The second line of 66 repeats 65; the subahdar's speech continues in 67–68 (pdf 148), so it is left open in the translation.
[^43]: 67–68 conclude the subahdar's speech (65–66; note 42). Kabir (*kabira*) — the fifteenth-century poet-saint of Varanasi, revered by Hindus and Muslims alike. "Divine great souls" — *deba mahājana*. "A host of mahantas" — *mohāntera gaṇa* (note 37). "Miracles" — *keramati* (note 41). "Have shown" — *dekhā'lena*.
[^44]: "Acknowledge as equal to Ishvara" — *māni īśvara-samāne*, "honour on a par with God". "All [of them]" — *sabā*: apparently Kabir and the other mahantas who showed miracles (and so Rasika too, if he shows one — cf. 65). "With these eyes" — *e-naẏane*.
[^45]: "The yavana said" — *kahila yabane*: *yabane* is taken as the subject ("the yavana said to all the Hindu rajas"); grammatically "all the Hindu rajas said to the yavana" is also possible, but from 70 and lahari 8, 5 the order comes from the subahdar. Rasika-Murari-dasa (*rasika-murāridāsa*) is Rasika.
[^46]: Prabhu is Rasika (cf. 38; so the author calls him). "To where the yavana is" — *yabanera sthāne*, lit. "to the yavana's place": to Banapur.
[^47]: "From everyone" — *sabāsthāne*. The son of Achyuta (*acyuta-nandana*) is Rasika (note 13).
[^48]: "Declared" — *ājñā karilena*, lit. "commanded". "In many ways" — *bahurūpe*.
[^49]: "From forest to forest" — *bane bane*. "Saving our lives" — *prāṇa laiẏā*, lit. "taking [our] lives". "What is there to deliberate so much" — *kena eta ki bicāre*; the sense of the line is approximate: understood as "why have anything to do with a yavana at all".
[^50]: The wild elephant of the lahari's title (note 1). "Was wreaking havoc" — *pīṛane karaẏe*, "oppresses, torments". "Forest" — *araṇyera*.
[^51]: "Do not go along the roads or to the landings" — *patha ghāṭa nāhi cale*. "For fear and terror" — *bhaẏa-trāse*. The subahdar is Ahmed Beg (note 19).
[^52]: "King of elephants" — *gajarāja*. 80: "Went beyond everyone's words" — *karilā laṅghana*: he did not heed their dissuasion (73–75).
[^53]: 81–82 form one sentence. "The ruler" — *nṛpati*: the subahdar. "On his terrace" — *jagatīte* (note 35; cf. 91). "Around" — *cāribhiti*, "on four sides". 82: "Delighting in various rasas" — *nānārasa-binode*: apparently with singing and talk of Krishna.
[^54]: "Overwhelmed" — *jara jara* (note 3); understood as Rasika's heart. "Now" — *ibe* (garbled in the OCR, checked against the scan).
[^55]: *Mada* — the season of frenzy in bull elephants (musth), when fluid flows from the temporal glands; "crazed by great mada" — *mahāmada-matta*. "Heeds nothing" — *kichu nāi māne*. "To pieces" — *khāne khāne*.
[^56]: "The vault of heaven" — *gagana-maṇḍala*, "the circle of the sky". Rasatala is one of the lower worlds. "Under the weight of its steps" — *padabhare*.
[^57]: "For sport" — *kutūhale*.
[^58]: 90: "In confusion" — *āste byaste*, "in haste". "To other lands" — *deśāntare*. 91: "Onto the tops of houses" — *aṭṭālikā upare*: onto the tops of tall (masonry) houses. "Onto terraces" — *jagatī* (note 53; garbled in the OCR, checked against the scan). "Hastily" — *tvarati*.
[^59]: 92: "The end of the world" — *pralaẏa*, the dissolution of the universe. 93: "For nothing" — *akāraṇe*, "without cause, without fault". "We have lost our lives" — *hārāinu*: the past tense of what is felt to be inevitable.
[^60]: "Cries out 'alas!'" — *hāhākāra*. "The prince of Achyuta" — *acyuta-kumāra*: Rasika (cf. Pashchima, lahari 5, note 60).
[^61]: 95: "A supreme, wholly devoted sadhu" — *parama ananya sādhu*. 96: "Servant" — *bhṛtya*; cf. BR 15, 84 (note 19): Rasikananda made the elephant a disciple and engaged it in the service of Krishna and the Vaishnavas. The account of how it happened (*tā'ra bibaraṇa*) is in lahari 8.
[^62]: "Without delay" — *abilambe*. "The wealth of prema-bhakti for Krishna" — *kṛṣṇapremabhaktidhana*.
[^63]: The colophon is on pdf 148 (printed p. 108), checked against the scan (note 1). Lahari 7 ends with the author's signature (98); lahari 8 begins on a new page, pdf 149 (files RM-3-08-001-066).
