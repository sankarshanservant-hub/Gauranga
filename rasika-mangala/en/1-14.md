# Purva-vibhaga. Lahari 14. The Vision of Sri Krishna and the Hearing of His Command

*A song in glory of Shyamananda; absorbed in love for Krishna, Rasika wanders through Achyuta's domains, settles with his family at Ghantashila and reads the Bhagavata in the Jagannatha pavilion; in the forests on the Suvarnarekha, where the Pandavas once rested, he reads the Bhagavata, sings the name and feeds the Vaishnavas; as he sits in meditation at dusk, Krishna appears to him and declares that his teacher will be Shyamananda, in whose form His beloved has taken birth; Krishna vanishes, and Rasika weeps in separation until morning; the household find him in the forest and bring him home; Rasika, heedless of the joys of home, longs for Krishna and Shyamananda; the author promises to tell how Shyamananda came from Vraja to Utkala, and speaks of his own service to both since childhood*

*The fourteenth lahari.*

*Raga Narayani-gauda.*[^1]
*Ghosha: "My Krishna is a treasury of virtues,*
*the refuge of the helpless, the utmost limit of mercy."*

**1.** Glory, glory to Shyamananda, the friend of all,
the benefactor of all people, the ocean of mercy!

**2.** Be merciful to me, Prabhu, son of Durika:
I shall describe a little of Rasika's glory.[^2]

**3.** How [his] meeting with Shyamananda came about —
of that I shall tell something in detail;

**4.** how [Shyamananda] was united with Rasika,
and how both, giving instruction, delivered living beings.

**5.** At that time Rasikendra, absorbed in Krishna,
freely, in the bliss of Krishna, wanders from land to land.[^3]

**6.** Achyuta had houses in various places;
in each of those places he stays for many days.

**7.** There is a most holy place called Ghantashila;
there he settled together with his family.[^4]

**8.** There stands the incomparable Jagannatha pavilion;
sitting there, he reads the Bhagavata without pause.[^5]

**9.** Rasika-shekhara is intoxicated with the rasa of the Bhagavata;
his whole body is drenched with the tears of [his] eyes.

**10.** On the bank of the Suvarnarekha is a most wondrous place:
very dense groves, wonderfully formed.

**11.** Formerly the Pandavas and others rested there;
[that it is] so holy a place — there is proof of it.[^6]

**12.** Seeing all these places, Rasika-shekhara
roams the forest alone, with love [for them].

**13.** In one place — [he reads] the Bhagavata, in another — [chants] the name,
in another he performs sankirtana without pause.

**14.** In one place, joyfully holding a meal in the forest,
Rasika feeds the Vaishnavas there.[^7]

**15.** At that time to the place where the Pandavas and others [once] were
Rasikendra went.

**16.** A most enchanting place, beautiful to behold:
dense forest, river, water, fragrance.

**17.** Rasikendra, taking his seat in that place,
sat in meditation, [chanting] the name of Hari, with closed eyes.

**18.** Now sweat, now trembling, now tears flow;
his heart is in great longing from separation from Krishna.

**19.** At that time a certain foremost of great personalities
appeared before Rasika.

**20.** A dark, beautiful, most enchanting body;
with the radiance of [His] limbs He lights up the forest.

**21.** Bent in three places, graceful; the flute adorns [His] lips;
curling locks [and] crown glitter.

**22.** His peacock feather is beautiful to behold;
armlets, anklets, little bells; He wears yellow garments.[^8]

**23.** On His neck sway various jewels and the Kaustubha gem;
in His ears are earrings; in His nose a pearl sparkles.

**24.** His ankle-bells are most beautiful to behold;
[His] unhurried, languid gait surpasses [that of] an elephant.[^9]

**25.** At the hour [when] the cows [return], Krishna came to that place
and gave darshana to His servant Rasika.[^10]

**26.** Standing before him, He speaks profound words;
the speech [seems to] melt on His lips, it delights the ear:

**27.** "Hear these words, Rasika-mahashaya:
your teacher [will be] Shyamananda-raya.

**28.** My beloved has taken birth in the form of Shyamananda;
bestowing prema-bhakti, he will deliver all people.[^11]

**29.** Serving him, you will attain My feet;
in your heart I shall sport constantly."

**30.** Hearing these words with his ears, Rasika-Murari
came out of his meditation and looked with tear-filled eyes.

**31.** Before him he saw Krishna, the Lord of [his] life,
whose enchanting form surpasses millions upon millions of Kamas.

**32.** Seeing [Him], Rasika fell joyfully at [His] feet;
placing his head on [His] holy feet, [he is] in deep bliss.

**33.** Right there Sri Krishna vanished;
rising, [Rasika] looked — there is no one in that place.

**34.** "O Krishna, where have You gone, my life?" —
[and] Rasika fell face down on that spot.[^12]

**35.** Fallen on the ground, he weeps, sobbing, sobbing;
streams of tears flow ceaselessly from his eyes.[^13]

**36.** His body grey with dust, he rolls on the ground;
his body is overwhelmed by the waves [of tears] from his eyes.

**37.** In a choked voice he speaks sweet words:
"Leaving me, where have You gone, Krishna, treasure of my life?

**38.** As the fruit of some merit I was granted the sight of You;
and now, Krishna, You have abandoned me and left me alone.

**39.** I saw Your form with these sinful eyes;
and now, leaving [me] without support, You have vanished.[^14]

**40.** How shall I pass my days without seeing You?" —
remembering, remembering, he weeps without end.

**41.** However much he lamented in his mood [of love] for Krishna —
what power have I to tell of those expressions [of feeling]?

**42.** Nothing can be told of that longing;
hearing of that love (anuraga), [even] a stone would melt.[^15]

**43.** Rasika weeps in love for Krishna, unaware of the outer world;
he lies right there, and morning came.

**44.** Meanwhile all the household go searching:
they search from village to village, look from forest to forest.

**45.** Looking [everywhere], they search in Panduya, at that place
where the Pandavas and others rested.[^16]

**46.** A most fearsome forest, very remote,
on the bank of the Suvarnarekha, adorned with hills.[^17]

**47.** Tigers, bears, elephants, lions, rhinoceroses — in [those] parts;
all [these] creatures live there by hundreds of thousands.

**48.** In the ecstasy of prema Prabhu [Rasika] is aware of nothing;
by Krishna's power none [of them] is anywhere near.

**49.** At that time all the people, searching in haste,
came and saw Rasika-shekhara there:

**50.** he lies on the ground, his eyes full of tears,
his curling locks scattered, his hairs ceaselessly standing on end.[^18]

**51.** Seeing [this], all the people, distraught,
lifted Rasika, seated him and brought him to his senses.

**52.** All wiped [his] holy face with fine cloth,
brushed [the dust] off his whole body and carefully tied up his hair.

**53.** Taking him by the hands, all raised [him]
and slowly, slowly came home.

**54.** The form that Rasika had seen with his own eyes
lives on unceasingly in [his] heart.

**55.** Having heard the command [that] his teacher is Shyamananda, [he thinks]:
"When shall I see that moon-like face?"

**56.** To no one does he speak of the thoughts of his heart;
ceaselessly he worships Shyamananda.

**57.** His heart is always in longing, intent on Krishna;
neither worldly affairs nor the joys of home please him at all.

**58.** His kinsfolk constantly surround him;
at times he sits with his [young] wife.[^19]

**59.** Various goods, various garments, various ornaments
they place before Rasika again and again.

**60.** He casts no glance at any of the heaps of goods;
Krishna [and] Shyamananda are always within his mind.

**61.** Worship of Krishna, meditation on Krishna, remembrance of Krishna;
Krishna is the truth, and all else — false aims.

**62.** Night and day he sees the three worlds full of Krishna;
in Rasika's eyes there is no hunger, no thirst, no sleep.

**63.** Having seen Krishna, [he was filled with] a most firm anuraga;
day and night, sitting, he weeps, thinking of that form.

**64.** Rasika gives no thought to books and does not stay at home;
in the ocean of separation from Krishna he roams the forest.

**65.** The secret thoughts of his heart he tells no one;
apart from that form, nothing [else] appears [to him].[^20]

**66.** Thus, when for many days he had been thinking and thinking,
Shyamananda, won over by [his] devotion, came in haste.

**67.** How he, leaving Vraja, came to Utkala —
of that I shall tell something.

**68.** What do I know of the inner meaning of these accounts?
[I tell them only] by the mercy of Shyamananda and Rasika.

**69.** Serving the two prabhus from childhood,
[I pass on] what was related without guile;

**70.** all the lilas of the two that I saw from childhood —
all of this I shall proclaim in brief.[^21]

**71.** Let no one hold against me any faults in the order [of the narrative]:
it is Rasikendra, the crest-jewel [of rasikas], who makes me speak.

**72.** Listen to "Rasika-mangala", all you devotees of Krishna:
merely by hearing [it] one obtains the treasure of love for Krishna.[^22]

**73.** Adorning himself with Shyamananda's feet,
the son of Rasamaya joyfully composed [this].

*Thus in the Purva-vibhaga of the Sri Rasika-mangala ends the fourteenth lahari, called "The Vision of Sri Krishna and the Hearing of His Command".*

[^1]: In the scan the raga is *nārānī gauṛā* (1997 ed.: *nārāṇī gauṛā*), as in lahari 10 (note 1), that is, the *nārāyaṇī gauṛā* of lahari 7; the ghosha (refrain) is the same as in laharis 7 and 10, and its translation is repeated. There is no mangala-shloka at the beginning of the lahari: after the heading, the raga and the ghosha, the song begins with ॥ 1 ॥. The title follows the colophon on pdf 84 (checked against the scan): "śrīkṛṣṇa-darśana o tad-ādeśa-śravaṇa-nāma caturdaśa-laharī". Lahari 13 ends in the upper half of the same page (pdf 82).
[^2]: The son of Durika is Shyamananda (Durika is his mother; lahari 1, note 11; lahari 10, note 2); "Prabhu" here is Shyamananda.
[^3]: "Absorbed in Krishna" — *kṛṣṇera āveśe*. "Freely" — *icchāmaya*, lit. "following [his own] will".
[^4]: Ghantashila (*ghaṇṭaśilā*; Ghantashila in BR 15) is apparently present-day Ghatshila on the Suvarnarekha (Jharkhand); cf. BR 15, 30–31: Rasikananda lived for some time at Ghantashila, where the Pandavas rested on the bank of the Suvarnarekha. "Settled" — *viśrāma karilā*, lit. "stayed, rested". Achyuta's houses in various places (6) were apparently on his estates: Achyuta is a man of rank who lives "in royal splendour" (lahari 4, 2).
[^5]: The Jagannatha pavilion (*jagannātha-maṇḍapa*) is apparently a mandapa (a roofed hall for gatherings and readings) attached to a shrine of Jagannatha.
[^6]: The Pandavas are the five sons of Pandu, the heroes of the Mahabharata; according to local tradition, they lived in the forests on the Suvarnarekha during their exile (cf. BR 15, 31). "There is proof of it" — *āchaye pramāṇa*.
[^7]: "A meal in the forest" — *banabhuji*, according to the edition's gloss *banabhojana*. "Feeds the Vaishnavas" — *vaiṣṇava-bhojana karaye*.
[^8]: "Peacock feather" — *mayūra-candrikā*, lit. "the moon-like eye of a peacock feather". "Armlets" — *tāṛ* (lahari 5, note 9); "anklets" — *khāṛu*; "little bells" — *kṣudraghuṇṭi*, according to the edition's gloss *ghuṅur*, *kiṅkiṇī* (bells worn on the waist or ankles). Kaustubha (23) is the jewel on Krishna's chest.
[^9]: "Ankle-bells" — *nūpura*. "Unhurried, languid" — *madana-manthara*: so in the scan and in the 1997 edition, lit. "slow with love (Madana)"; possibly for *mada-manthara*, "slow as one intoxicated", the usual epithet of an elephant's gait.
[^10]: "At the hour [when] the cows [return]" — *godhūli*, lit. "cow-dust": dusk, when the herds come home from the pastures. Darshana is the sight of a saint or of the Deity.
[^11]: "My beloved" — *preyasī* (feminine), that is, apparently, Sri Radha: in this way the author exalts Shyamananda (cf. lahari 1, note 2; lahari 2, note 5). According to Gaudiya Vaishnava teaching, Shyamananda is a devotee and servant of Radha, a disciple of Hridaya Chaitanya, and not Radhika Herself. In BR 15 (32–39) Rasikananda at Ghantashila hears a voice from the sky saying that here he will become Shyamananda's disciple, and then Shyamananda himself appears to him in a dream; there is no appearance of Krishna there.
[^12]: "Face down" — *mukha māṛi*, lit. "pressing his face [to the ground]".
[^13]: "Sobbing" — *usasi usasi*, as in lahari 5 (note 10).
[^14]: "Without support" — *nirimākhi*, according to the edition's gloss *nirāśraya*, "without shelter".
[^15]: "Expressions [of feeling]" (41) — *anubhāva*, the outward signs of an inner emotion (bhava). "Longing" — *ārati* (= *ārti*), "yearning, anguish". Anuraga is deep, ever-increasing attachment to Krishna.
[^16]: "In Panduya" — *pāṇḍuyā*: clear in the scan (1997 ed.: *pāṇḍayā*); apparently the name of a locality associated with the Pandavas; another interpretation is possible (e.g. a corruption of a verb meaning "having found").
[^17]: "Very remote" — *nirgama-vidita*: clear in the scan (and in the 1997 edition); the meaning is unclear. Possibly for *nigama-vidita*, "known from the scriptures" (cf. 11), or connected with *nigama* in the sense of "seclusion", which the author uses in laharis 12–13 (lahari 13, note 4).
[^18]: "His hairs ceaselessly standing on end" — *pulakāvirāma* (*pulaka* + *avirāma*).
[^19]: "With his [young] wife" — *priyā saṅge*, that is, with Ichchha-dei (lahari 12).
[^20]: "Appears" — *sphure*; the edition gives in a footnote the variant *smare*: "apart from that form, he remembers nothing".
[^21]: "Without guile" — *niramāyāte*, according to the edition's gloss *akapaṭa-bhāve*, "sincerely". Who related it (*kaila*, 3rd person) is not stated: possibly the two prabhus themselves, Shyamananda and Rasikananda, or the author's father Rasamaya; the rendering is tentative. In 70 the author says that he himself saw the lilas of both from childhood.
[^22]: "Devotees of Krishna" — *kārṣṇa-jana* (lahari 10, note 13). "The treasure of love for Krishna" — *kṛṣṇa-prema-dhana*; the edition gives in a footnote the variant *kṛṣṇa-prāṇa-dhana*: "Krishna, the treasure of life".
