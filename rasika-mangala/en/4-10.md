# Uttara-vibhaga. Lahari 10. The Immovable Chariot of Sri Sri Jagannathadeva in Nilachala Set in Motion by the Touch of Sri Sri Rasikananda Prabhu

*A song in glory of Shyamananda; Rasika crosses the river without the loss of a single thing and, refusing to sit in a palanquin, goes on foot so fast that his companions cannot keep up, though his tender feet have cracked and are bleeding; on the day of the festival he arrives at Tulasi-chaura; the three chariots of Jagannatha come to a halt at Baligandi, and neither hundreds of thousands of pilgrims, nor the king with his ministers, nor elephants and horses can move them; Jagannatha bids Mudiratha tell the king that the chariot will move only when His dear devotee Murari comes; the gajapati meets Rasika and falls at his feet; the crowds leave the chariots to see Rasika; he offers gifts to the chariots and dances in the sankirtana of Rasamaya's community with Tulasidasa, while the people call him a second Narayana; Rasika pushes the chariot with his head, and the chariots, set in motion, swiftly reach their place; the gajapati glorifies Rasika and gives him lodging and prasada; Rasika visits the tirthas and mathas of Nilachala, receives land from the king, lays out a flower garden and a matha, makes gifts to the brahmanas and residents, and promises to come to every Ratha-yatra*

*The tenth lahari.*

*Raga Shri.*[^1]
*Ghosha: "O Hari, this time be merciful to me:*
*I hope to obtain the shade of Your feet."*

**1.** Glory, glory to Shyamananda, the deliverer of the world!
Be merciful, so that I may compose [his] glory.[^2]

**2.** At this time Rasikendra crossed the river;
no one lost a single thing any more.

**3.** Prabhu goes along the road on tender feet;
both feet have cracked, and blood falls again and again.

**4.** All said many [times]: "Sit in the palanquin," —
[but] Rasika-shekhara heeded no one's words.[^3]

**5.** Tearing cloth, he binds the joints of his toes;
as he walks, blood drips from his tender feet onto the road.

**6.** And still he goes on with the speed of the wind;
the followers [who are] with him all cannot keep up [with him].[^4]

**7.** On the day of the festival he arrived at Tulasi-chaura;
weary from the road, Prabhu bathes there.[^5]

**8.** Meanwhile, there, Jagannatha-raya mounted the chariot;
the three chariots halted at Baligandi.

**9.** From Baligandi the chariots move no further;
thousands upon thousands of kalapithyas [set to] pull [them].[^6]

**10.** And still the chariot does not move; it stayed there;
[then] all the pilgrims began to pull.

**11.** Hundreds of thousands of people pull, gripping the chariot ropes;
and still the chariot does not move; it stays [as if] sunk to the ground.

**12.** Growing angry, the king [himself] began to pull the chariot,
[and with him] the officials, the ministers, all the people who were with him.

**13.** Together with the brahmanas, everyone pulls;
whatever strength each had, he pulls with all his might.

**14.** Carters [and] ploughmen pulled by the hundreds;
elephants [and] horses pull — and still the chariot does not move.[^7]

**15.** Seeing [this], the maharaja was greatly astonished.
At this time the Lord gave a command to the Mudiratha:

**16.** "My dear one, My devotee Murari has come;
coming to Tulasi-chaura, he has entered [the town].

**17.** Rasika will come [and] see the chariot —
only then will the chariot move; do not exert yourselves [in vain].

**18.** Rasika-shekhara himself will pull the chariot —
then the chariot will go quickly; tell [this] to the best of kings."[^8]

**19.** Hearing the command, the Mudiratha told the king;
hearing [it], the king hurried to see Rasika.

**20.** Messengers, hurrying, say to Rasika:
"The king has come out to meet you, to take you [with him]."

**21.** Hearing [this], Prabhu set out with the speed of the wind;
at Atharanala the king saw [him].[^9]

**22.** Seeing [him], the king in reverence fell at [his] feet;
Rasikendra embraced [him] with joy in his heart.

**23.** In many ways the king, the gajapati, offered praise;
all came for darshan, holding one another's hands.

**24.** Hearing Rasika's name, all the pilgrims,
kings [and] subjects — all came for darshan.

**25.** There is no counting the people [who came] to see Rasika;
all the people came — [only] the three chariots remained.[^10]

**26.** Hari-dhvani, [cries of] "Glory, glory!", music of every kind;
one after another, all have darshan of Rasika.

**27.** Then Prabhu, coming to the chariots, had darshan;
he offered cloth [and] ornaments with the five jewels.

**28.** To each of the three chariots he gave its own load of gifts;
seeing the gifts, everyone was amazed.[^11]

**29.** Seeing the beautiful moon-like face, the son of Achyuta [weeps]:
hundreds and hundreds of streams flow from his two eyes.

**30.** His body, its hair standing on end, is like a kadamba bud;
the eight sattvika-bhavas appear on his holy body.

**31.** Rasamaya's party, together with Sri Tulasidasa,
began sankirtana in great joy.

**32.** Rasika-shekhara himself danced;
from the manifestation of mahabhava his holy body is overwhelmed.[^12]

**33.** How charming [his] body, how [lovely] the movement of his dance!
How, absorbed in ecstasy, he roams like an intoxicated elephant!

**34.** Hundreds and hundreds of garlands sway on Rasika's neck;
everyone, with one another, starts a play with prasada.[^13]

**35.** Seeing Rasika's form, everyone was enchanted;
leaving the chariots, all came and began to gaze [at him].

**36.** All say: "This Prabhu is a second Narayana:
his union with Jagannatha is without difference.[^14]

**37.** Because of him the chariot moved no further;
it is he who preached devotion to Krishna.

**38.** This man has freed everyone from ignorance,
has preached sankirtana in every home.

**39.** In Utkala he has awakened prema-bhakti;
at his command everyone serves the sadhus.

**40.** It is he who established the festival procession of the rasa,
by whose mercy all are intoxicated with love for Krishna.[^15]

**41.** We do not know how to tell his endless qualities;
with him Jagannatha Himself enjoys His pastimes."

**42.** All speak with one another about Rasika.
At this time a pratihari delivered a message:

**43.** "Because of you the chariot has stayed here;
now you yourself pull the chariot rope."[^16]

**44.** Hearing [this], Rasika rejoiced in great bliss;
placing his head against a pillar of the chariot, he pushes [it] from one side.[^17]

**45.** At Rasika's touch the chariot [sped] with the speed of the wind:
the three chariots arrived at the place Balinara.[^18]

**46.** Seeing Rasika's manifestation [of power], all were amazed;
all say: "Rasikendra is a partial incarnation."

**47.** The gajapati came to believe in every way:
he praised Rasika as the very form of Narayana.[^19]

**48.** He gave [him] lodging in his own palace, in a splendid place,
[and] with care gave many provisions.

**49.** Whatever is offered to Jagannatha —
all that prasada the king sends [him] with care.[^20]

**50.** For nine days he stayed at the place Balinara,
conversing with all the mahantas.

**51.** Ceaseless play in the sweetness of music [and] poetry:
the son of Achyuta is intoxicated with the sweetness of sankirtana.

**52.** All the pilgrims, the residents of the Kshetra, kings [and] subjects,
Vaishnavas, sannyasis, brahmanas, all people of merit,

**53.** the poor, the lowly, the suffering, scholars of the six darshanas —
all stay with Rasika day after day.

**54.** To all Rasikendra gives food [and] water;
in secret the hosts of the gods stay with Rasika.

**55.** Having satisfied everyone, Rasikendra
only then, afterwards, sits down [to eat] with his own people.[^21]

**56.** With Rasika it is always a festival of life;
many days he stayed joyfully in Nilachala.

**57.** In all the holy places there are in Nilachala
he bathes, going round [to them] night and day.

**58.** All the mathas of the mahantas that are there —
going round night and day, he visits them everywhere.[^22]

**59.** Asking the king for land on the southern side,
he made a flower garden [and] a matha with joy in his heart.

**60.** Every day there are garlands twelve cubits [long], three dhondas;
he appointed ten or fifteen servitors.

**61.** He established a bhoga — ten or twenty abadas;
assigning villages, he gave [them] to brahmanas.[^23]

**62.** He gave many gifts to all the brahmanas,
cloth [and] ornaments to the residents of the Kshetra.

**63.** Rasikendra satisfied everyone;
Prabhu took his leave with joy in his heart.

**64.** With everyone Rasikendra made a firm promise:
"Every year I will come and see the moon-like face.

**65.** At the time of the Ratha-yatra I will always come" —
so he settled with the king, the subjects [and] the sadhus.[^24]

**66.** Many pastimes he performed in the abode of Nilachala —
they cannot be described even by speaking with a hundred mouths.

**67.** By his mercy I have made known a little —
a little of the qualities, glory [and] fame of Rasikadeva.[^25]

**68.** Listen to the *Rasika-mangala*, all friends:
without delay you will attain the treasure of prema-bhakti for Krishna.

**69.** Adorning himself with Shyamananda's feet,
the son of Rasamaya joyfully composed [this].

*Thus in the Uttara-vibhaga of the Sri Rasika-mangala ends the tenth lahari, called "The Immovable Chariot of Sri Sri Jagannathadeva in Nilachala Set in Motion by the Touch of Sri Sri Rasikananda Prabhu."*[^26]

[^1]: The lines before the raga are the title of the lahari ("দশম-লহরী"), in italics without a number. Lahari 10 begins on pdf 195 (printed p. 151) below a rule after the colophon of lahari 9 (files RM-4-09-001-074). Raga Shri (*śrī*; Purva, lahari 5, note 1); the ghosha is the same as in Uttara, lahari 2 (note 1) and lahari 5, and its translation is repeated. There is no mangala-shloka: after the raga and the ghosha the song begins with ॥ 1 ॥. The title follows the colophon on pdf 197 (printed p. 153), checked against the scan: "শ্রীশ্রীরসিকানন্দ প্রভুর স্পর্শে নীলাচলে শ্রীশ্রীজগন্নাথদেবের অচল রথ সচল হওন" (*śrīśrīrasikānanda prabhura sparśe nīlācale śrīśrījagannāthadebera acala ratha sacala haona*), literally "How the immovable chariot … became movable by the touch of …". Lahari 10 comprises 1–69 (pdf 195–197); this file has 1–4 (pdf 195 ends with the complete couplet 4); 5–69 and the colophon are on pdf 196–197, the next portion. The summary line covers the whole lahari (5–69 from the OCR of pdf 196–197, provisional).
[^2]: "The deliverer of the world" — *jagata-tāraṇa*. The second line is as in lahari 9, 1 (note 2).
[^3]: "No one lost a single thing any more" (2) — *kāro eka drabya naṣṭa nāhi gela āra*: after the rescue of the Bhagavata (lahari 9, 54–70). "Have cracked" (3) — *phāṭi'*. "Palanquin" (4) — *sukapāla* (lahari 9, note 6): Rasika gave it up at Dhamnagar (lahari 9, 11).
[^4]: "The joints of his toes" (5) — *aṅguli-sandhi*: Rasika's feet have cracked on the way and are bleeding (3–4, pdf 195). "Cannot keep up" (6) — *nāi pāẏa lāge*.
[^5]: Tulasi-chaura (*tulasī caurā*; also in 16) — apparently a place at the entrance to Puri; not identified (*caurā* — a raised platform with a Tulasi plant). "On the day of the festival" — *yātrā-dine*: on the day of the Ratha-yatra. "Weary from the road" — *pathaśrānte*.
[^6]: "Mounted the chariot" (8) — *rathe bije kaila*. Jagannatha-raya — Jagannatha. The three chariots — those of Jagannatha, Baladeva and Subhadra. Baligandi (*bāligaṇḍī*) — Balagandi, the place on the great road from the temple to the Gundicha where the chariots halt for offerings (CC, Madhya 13). "Kalapithyas" (9) — *kālāpiṭhyā* (so on the scan; the OCR is garbled): apparently *kālābeṭhiā*, the hereditary servitors of Puri who pull the chariots; the interpretation is tentative.
[^7]: "Stays [as if] sunk to the ground" (11) — *rahe bhūme paṛi'*. "The officials, the ministers" (12) — *pātra mantrī*. "With all his might" (13) — *prāṇapaṇe*. "Carters [and] ploughmen" (14) — *gāṛibahā hāliẏā*: understood as cart drivers and ploughmen (with their bullocks?); the sense is approximate. Cf. CC, Madhya 14: when the chariot stopped, King Prataparudra had elephants harnessed, but even they could not move it.
[^8]: "The Lord" (15) — *prabhu*: here Jagannatha. The Mudiratha — lahari 7, note 13. "Murari" (16) — Rasika (Rasika-Murari). "Has entered [the town]" — *parabeśa hailā*. "Do not exert yourselves [in vain]" (17) — *nā kara yatana*.
[^9]: "Has come out to meet you" (20) — *pāchoṭī* (so on the scan; Oriya, "going out to meet a guest"; the editor glosses "পাছোটী—অনুব্রজে," "going out to meet"). "To take" — *nibāre*. Atharanala (*āṭhāra nālā*, 21) — the bridge of eighteen arches at the entrance to Puri (CC, Madhya 5). "With the speed of the wind" — *pabanagamane*.
[^10]: "Holding one another's hands" (23) — *dhari' hātāhāti*; perhaps "jostling, hand to hand." "There is no counting" (25) — *samuccaẏa nāi*. 25b — *sarbbaloka āilā rahilā tina rathe*: understood to mean that all the people went off [to Rasika] and the chariots were left alone (cf. 35); it may also be understood as "all the people came [and] stayed at the three chariots."
[^11]: "Offered" (27) — *bheṭilena*. "With the five jewels" — *pañcaratne*: pancharatna, the five precious things — a customary offering of honour. "Its own load of gifts" (28) — *bhinna bhinna drabya bhāra*.
[^12]: "Moon-like face" (29) — *śrīcandrabadana*: the face of Jagannatha. The eight sattvika-bhavas (30) — lahari 6, note 26. "Rasamaya's party" (31) — *rasamaẏa goṣṭhī*: understood as the kirtan party of Rasamaya, the author's father; it may also be understood as "a party full of sweetness (rasa)." Sri Tulasidasa — perhaps the same "guru of sankirtana" whom Rasika revered from childhood (Purva, lahari 1, notes 34, 63). "Is overwhelmed" (32) — *jara jara*.
[^13]: "Roams" (33) — *bule*. "A play with prasada" (34) — *prasādera khelā*: apparently they toss prasada garlands and food to one another.
[^14]: "A second Narayana" (36) — *dbitīẏa nārāẏaṇa*; "his union with Jagannatha is without difference" — *abheda milana*; in 46 — "a partial incarnation" (*aṃśa-abatāra*), in 47 — "the very form of Narayana" (*nārāẏaṇa-sbarūpe*); in 54 — "the hosts of the gods" with Rasika. So speak the crowd and the king — rapturous praise. According to Gaudiya Vaishnava teaching, Rasika is a devotee and associate, a disciple of Shyamananda, not an incarnation (Pashchima, lahari 2, note 28; lahari 7, note 36); the Lord Himself exalts His devotee. On the author's attitude to Shyamananda and Rasika — Purva, lahari 1, note 2; Purva, lahari 14, note 11. "With him Jagannatha Himself enjoys His pastimes" (41) — cf. Pashchima, lahari 7, note 37.
[^15]: "Ignorance" (38) — *abidyā*. "The festival procession of the rasa" (40) — *rāsa mahotsaba-yātrā*: the rasa festival at Gopivallabhapur (Pashchima, laharis 2–3).
[^16]: Pratihari (*pratihārī*, 42) — temple servitors of Jagannatha, guards and messengers. "The chariot rope" (43) — *rathadaṛī*.
[^17]: "Placing his head against a pillar of the chariot, he pushes" — *rathastambhe māthā diẏā ṭhele*. Cf. CC, Madhya 14: at Balagandi the chariot stopped, and Sri Chaitanya moved it by pushing it with His head.
[^18]: Balinara (*bālinara sthāna*; so on the scan, crop ×2; likewise in 50) — not identified; apparently the place the chariots arrive at, the Gundicha, where Jagannatha stays for the nine days of the Ratha-yatra (CC, Madhya 14; cf. 50, "nine days"). The reading and sense are unclear.
[^19]: See note 14. "Came to believe" — *biśbāsa haila*.
[^20]: "In his own palace" (48) — *āpanā mandire*: understood as the king's palace. "Is offered" (49) — *upahāra haẏa*.
[^21]: "The residents of the Kshetra" (52) — *kṣetrabāsī*, the residents of Puri (Sri Kshetra). "Scholars of the six darshanas" (53) — *ṣaṛdarśane paṇḍita*: of the six philosophical schools. "In secret … the hosts of the gods" (54) — *gupate … debabṛnda* (note 14). "Sits down [to eat]" (55) — *boïsena*; "with his own people" — *ātmabṛnda*.
[^22]: "A festival of life" (56) — *jībana-mahotsaba*; it may also be understood as "all of life is a great festival." "Night and day" (58) — *niśi diśi* (so on the scan; = *niśi dine*, as in 57). "The mathas of the mahantas" — *mohāntera maṭha*. "Everywhere" — *sarbbathā*.
[^23]: "On the southern side" (59) — *dakṣiṇa pāraśe*; of what is not said (probably of the temple or the town). "A flower garden [and] a matha" — *phulatoṭā maṭha*; this matha of Rasika in Puri is not identified. "Three dhondas" (60) — *tina dhoṇḍā* (so on the scan, crop ×2): the word is unclear — perhaps "bunch, bundle" (?). "Ten or fifteen servitors" — *daśa pā̃ca sebāita*, i.e. "several." "Abadas" (61) — *ābaṛā*, Oriya *abaḍā*: cooked rice offered to Jagannatha (mahaprasada); "ten or twenty abadas" — apparently that many [pots] of daily offering. "Established" — *nirṇe* (= *nirṇaya*). "Assigning villages, he gave [them] to brahmanas" — apparently he granted villages to brahmanas for the upkeep of this service.
[^24]: "Took his leave" (63) — *bidāẏa karila*; it may also be understood as "sent [everyone] off [with gifts]." "Made a firm promise" (64) — *nirabandha kaila* (= *nirbandha*). "The moon-like face" — the face of Jagannatha (cf. 29). "Always" (65) — *sarbba dine*, "on all days." "So he settled" — *karila e nirṇe*.
[^25]: "By his mercy" (67) — by Rasika's mercy. "Fame" — *kīrtta* (so on the scan, crop ×2; = *kīrtti*).
[^26]: The colophon is on pdf 197 (printed p. 153), checked against the scan: "ইতি শ্রীরসিকমঙ্গল-উত্তর-বিভাগে শ্রীশ্রীরসিকানন্দ প্রভুর স্পর্শে নীলাচলে শ্রীশ্রীজগন্নাথদেবের অচল রথ সচল হওন-নাম দশম-লহরী সম্পূর্ণা" (*śrīśrīrasikānanda prabhura sparśe nīlācale śrīśrījagannāthadebera acala ratha sacala haona*).
