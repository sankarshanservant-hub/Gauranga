# Pashchima-vibhaga. Lahari 1. Preparations for the Spring Rasa at Sri Gopivallabhapur

*A song in glory of Shyamananda; Shyamananda comes with the Deity Shyama-raya to Kashiyari, manifests Thakurani, celebrates the wedding of Thakura and holds three festivals, and there Purushottama, Damodara, Mathuradasa, Haraghosha Mahapatra and the brahmana Haridasa receive the mantra from him; in Dharenda, at an assembly with Rasika, Damodara and other devotees, Shyamananda orders a great Rasa-yatra to be proclaimed for the spring full moon of Vaishakha and entrusts Rasika with gathering the provisions; Rasika's followers spend a whole year clearing the thorny thickets from the site at Sri Gopivallabhapur and build a pavilion, while Rasika himself in one year gathers mountains of grain, sweets, fruits, vegetables and spices; an octagonal circle of the rasa is built with paintings, a fence, four gates and high platforms, with the gods, instruments, banners and indrajala; the circle is adorned with cloths, garlands and canopies, and by the wish-fulfilling tree at the eight corners eight Deities are installed with eight Thakuranis; the author says that he cannot describe it and writes by the mercy of Rasika, whom he saw with his own eyes from childhood*

*Glory to Sri Sri Radha and Govinda!*
*Sri Sri Rasika-mangala.*
*Pashchima-vibhaga.*
*The first lahari.*

*Raga Varadi.*[^1]
*Ghosha: "O ocean of mercy, O merciful Shyama!*
*Turn Your attention to the fallen and wretched people."*

**1.** Glory, glory to Shyamananda, the purifier of the fallen!
Glory, glory to the treasure of the life of Rasika-deva![^2]

**2.** Thus, after some days, Shyamananda-raya
left that place, taking Shyama-raya with him.[^3]

**3.** Coming to the town of Kashiyari, he stayed [there];
manifesting Thakurani for Shyama-raya,[^4]

**4.** joyfully arranging the wedding of Thakura,
in great bliss he held three festivals.

**5.** Great joy came to the town of Kashiyari:
men and women look on with eyes brimming, happy.[^5]

**6.** Sri Purushottama, Damodara, Mathuradasa,
Haraghosha Mahapatra, the brahmana Haridasa[^6]

**7.** received instruction in the mantra from Shyamananda:
giving up their family dharma, [they came] to Shyamananda's shelter.[^7]

**8.** Staying there some days, Shyamananda-raya
entered Dharenda together with Shyama-raya.

**9.** Bringing Thakurani in procession together [with Him],
for some days he joyfully spoke there of Krishna.[^8]

**10.** One day Shyamananda-raya held an assembly;
with him there were Rasikendra, Damodara,[^9]

**11.** Netrananda, Kishora, Thakura Haridasa,
Bhima Shirikara, Rasamaya, Vamshidasa,[^10]

**12.** Chintamani and all the other devotees of Krishna.
Shyamananda took counsel with Rasika:[^11]

**13.** "Proclaim one great Rasa-yatra,
so that the people of the three worlds may be amazed.

**14.** [It is] spring, and [soon] the full moon of Vaishakha:
a bright moon, [as in] autumn, a clear night.[^12]

**15.** Carefully entrust everyone with duties;
from today turn your mind to gathering provisions.

**16.** Give the order and the duties to all — the raja and the subjects:
let each, as far as his strength allows, prepare provisions."[^13]

**17.** In Rasamaya's house he tells [this] to everyone;
in great joy he fixed the Rasa-yatra.[^14]

**18.** Prabhu went to Sri Govindapur;
he ordered Rasika: "Turn your mind to the provisions."[^15]

**19.** Making Shyamananda's feet a necklace upon his heart,
Rasika went out to collect alms.

**20.** The order was given: "At Sri Gopivallabhapur
there will be a Great Rasa-yatra, shining over the three worlds."[^16]

**21.** Rasika ordered all [his] followers:
"All of you, clear the site for the rasa."[^17]

**22.** At the order hundreds and hundreds of followers set to work:
cutting down the thorns and the rest, they cleared [the site].

**23.** For about a year hundreds and hundreds of people laboured;
only then were the thorny thickets uprooted.[^18]

**24.** The whole site became even, like flower pollen;
in its midst they built a pavilion.

**25.** Dinashyama, Ramakrishna, Narayanadasa,
Shyamagopala, Sri Rasamaya, Vamshidasa —[^19]

**26.** leaving all of them at Sri Gopivallabhapur
and saying, "Do all the work here," —

**27.** he himself went out to gather provisions:
in one single year he gathered provisions in many ways.[^20]

**28.** First [gathering] all the provisions for the festival,
he carefully brought rice and the rest, mung [and] biri.[^21]

**29.** Putting [them] into granaries, they stored [them] like mountains:
for the festival, in great joy, all this is the chief [supply].

**30.** Wheat, maida, chickpeas, khesari — without end;
ghee, oil, gur, guya — hundreds and hundreds of loads.[^22]

**31.** Fried dishes [and] sweets were made of many kinds;
bananas of various sorts, well ripened — without end.[^23]

**32.** Very soft laddus of many, many kinds —
filling hundreds and hundreds of pots, they quickly stored [them].

**33.** They filled hundreds and hundreds of baskets with saliya-ukhra,
filled hundreds and hundreds of baskets with fine, thin chida.[^24]

**34.** Hundreds and hundreds of baskets of khai, making [it] into hudum;
luchis [and] puris, filling hundreds and hundreds of pots.

**35.** Various fried dishes — hundreds and hundreds of loads;
curd, milk, cream, sugar — of many kinds.

**36.** At the proper time the household people bring
greens, badis, mangoes, maja, rambha, eggplants,[^25]

**37.** kushmanda gourds, bananas and the rest, palata, karela;
taba, jambira, nembu — hundreds of loads, kamala,[^26]

**38.** shatakara, pomegranates, lovely narenga:
all bring many different provisions in abundance.

**39.** Much khadira, tambula and lime arrived;
hingu, methi, nutmeg, mahuri and also jira,[^27]

**40.** camphor and pepper were brought in abundance:
for the festival — beautiful dishes of the six tastes.

**41.** And all the other subsidiary provisions they carefully brought —
all the provisions the Creator made in creation.[^28]

**42.** Countless different provisions all the people bring;
all say: "Mahalakshmi has appeared."

**43.** At the proper time all these loads of provisions
the household people brought and stored in [their] places.

**44.** Then they built the circle of the rasa:
in the shape of an eight-petalled [lotus], octagonal in its measure.[^29]

**45.** All around it they made various paintings;
seeing the site of the rasa, all the people are seized with awe.[^30]

**46.** Quickly bringing all the huge logs,
they planted the logs beautifully around the circle.

**47.** Even if a maddened elephant pushes, it cannot be moved —
such a firm fence shines on all sides.[^31]

**48.** On the four sides are four gates of supreme beauty;
on all four sides are very high, charming platforms.[^32]

**49.** Brahma, Shiva, Purandara [and] all the [other] gods
they carefully set up in [their proper] places.[^33]

**50.** Various instruments [and] banners they set up in various places;
at the sound the earth trembles, [there is] a rain of flowers.[^34]

**51.** Thus, on high, are great platforms, beautifully adorned;
on them indrajala shines — it cannot be described.[^35]

**52.** From them hang chamaras [and] various pendants of gems;
[all is] colourful — in cloths of many kinds, in clusters.[^36]

**53.** Colourful flower pendants of shola — of how many kinds!
Various fragrant flower pendants — in rows.[^37]

**54.** The circle [of the rasa] was adorned with indrajala in various styles;
on tall, tall poles they tied all the banners.[^38]

**55.** On them golden pitchers shine in rows;
from the arches hang pendants — captivating.

**56.** They wrapped all the pillars in colourful cloths;
everywhere they decorated [with designs of] rice flour.[^39]

**57.** To behold — supreme beauty, brilliantly shining:
[as if] Vrindavana had appeared before the eyes.[^40]

**58.** In the midst of the rasa circle a pavilion shines;
within it, a throne blazing with splendour.

**59.** In the midst of the pavilion — a beautiful wish-fulfilling tree
[with] pendants of various gems and jewels, wondrously skilful.[^41]

**60.** The tree is overlaid with gold, various gems blaze [on it];
by the radiance of that tree all four directions are lit.

**61.** At the foot of the tree — an octagonal jewelled throne;
at the eight corners [Rasika] placed eight Deities,[^42]

**62.** with eight Thakuranis, full of splendour,
in various ornaments — it cannot be described.

**63.** On all sides they stretched canopies of many kinds;
on the arches chamaras [and] pendants hang in clusters.[^43]

**64.** Seeing the circle [of the rasa], one marvels at [its] beauty;
Rasika's greatness cannot be described.[^44]

**65.** The qualities that Vyasa, Shuka, Narada and others glorify —
what power have I to describe them?[^45]

**66.** Yet by the mercy of Rasika's remnants
[I tell what] Rasikendra, dwelling in [my] heart, makes me speak.[^46]

**67.** With my own eyes I saw all his qualities [and] pastimes,
all the play [I] had with him from childhood.[^47]

**68.** In brief I have composed a certain description of [his] nature.
Hear the Rasika-mangala, all you people who long [for it].[^48]

**69.** This is the first description in the Pashchima-vibhaga:
I have composed [an account] of something of the great festival of the rasa.[^49]

**70.** Rasika held a great rasa, glorious [and] renowned;
in part I have made its description known.[^50]

**71.** Adorning himself with Shyamananda's feet,
the son of Rasamaya joyfully composed [this].

*Thus in the Pashchima-vibhaga of the Sri Rasika-mangala ends the first lahari, called "Preparations for the Spring Rasa at Sri Gopivallabhapur."*[^51]

[^1]: The lines before the raga are the opening lines of the vibhaga and the lahari ("শ্রীশ্রীরাধাগোবিন্দৌ জয়তঃ / শ্রীশ্রীরসিকমঙ্গল / পশ্চিম বিভাগ / প্রথম-লহরী"), in italics without a number, as at the beginning of the Dakshina-vibhaga. The Pashchima-vibhaga ("western part") begins on a new page — pdf 131 (printed p. 93; the page number is not printed), after the colophon of Dakshina, lahari 16 on pdf 130 (files RM-2-16-056-072). Raga Varadi (*barāṛī*); the ghosha is the same as in Dakshina, laharis 7, 10 and 14 (Dakshina, lahari 7, note 1), and its translation is repeated. There is no mangala-shloka: after the raga and ghosha the song begins with ॥ 1 ॥. The title follows the colophon on pdf 135 after ॥ 71 ॥ (checked against the scan, crop ×4): "ইতি শ্রীরসিকমঙ্গল-পশ্চিম-বিভাগে শ্রীগোপীবল্লভপুরে বাসন্তরাসোত্থমনাম প্রথম-লহরী সম্পূর্ণা" — *bāsantarāsotthama*: understood as *bāsanta-rāsa-udyama*, "the preparation (undertaking) of the spring rasa" (which also fits the content of the lahari); possibly *-rāsottama*, "the excellent spring rasa"; the reading is unclear. The summary line covers the whole lahari (52–71 — from the scan of pdf 135 and the OCR, provisionally). For the Pashchima-vibhaga there is no second reading (the 1997 edition).
[^2]: "The purifier of the fallen" — *patitapābana*. "Rasika-deva" — *rasikadeba*.
[^3]: "Thus" — the narrative of Dakshina, lahari 16 continues: Shyamananda and Rasika are with Uddanda in Nrisimhapur, where Shyama-raya had been brought from Dharenda (Dakshina, lahari 16, 57, note 36). Shyama-raya — a Deity of Krishna (Dakshina, lahari 10, note 12). "Taking with him" — *saṅge kari*.
[^4]: Kashiyari — *kāśīẏāṛi nagara*; in 5 — *kāśīẏāṛī purī* (Dakshina, lahari 12, note 4). "Stayed" — *utarila*. Thakurani is the Deity of Sri Radha; "manifesting" (*prakāśa kariyā*) is how the author speaks of making and installing a Deity, and "the wedding of Thakura" (4) is the installation of the Deity of Radha beside the Deity of Krishna (cf. Dakshina, lahari 6, note 3). The line can also be understood as "manifesting Shyama-raya [and] Thakurani".
[^5]: "Look on with eyes brimming, happy" — *naẏana bhariẏā sukha dekhe*, lit. "filling their eyes, they see happiness".
[^6]: 6–7 — new disciples of Shyamananda in Kashiyari; not identified. "Mathuradasa" — *mathurā dāsa*; it can also be understood as two names. Whether this is the same Damodara as in 10 (Shyamananda's disciple from Chakulia, Dakshina, lahari 1) is unclear. "Haraghosha Mahapatra" — *hāṛaghoṣa mahāpātra* (so in the scan; crop ×2); Mahapatra is an Oriya title or surname. "The brahmana Haridasa" — *dbija haridāsa*.
[^7]: "Received instruction in the mantra" — *mantra upadeśa kaila*: initiation (Dakshina, lahari 15, note 28). "Family dharma" — *kuladharma*: family customs and the worship of family gods (cf. Dakshina, lahari 13, note 4).
[^8]: "Bringing in procession" — *bijaẏa kariẏā*: said respectfully of a Deity's procession (Dakshina, lahari 16, note 31). "Together [with Him]" — *eka saṅge*: Thakurani together with Shyama-raya. "Joyfully" — *raṅge*.
[^9]: Rasikendra — Rasika. Damodara — Shyamananda's disciple from Chakulia (Dakshina, lahari 1). "There" — *tāẏa*.
[^10]: Netrananda, Kishora, Thakura Haridasa — apparently disciples of Shyamananda; it can also be understood as "Kishora Thakura, Haridasa". Bhima Shirikara — the ruler of Dharenda who became a Vaishnava (Dakshina, lahari 5, note 4); Rasamaya and Vamshidasa (Vamshi) are his grandsons, the author's father and uncle (Dakshina, lahari 4, note 14).
[^11]: Chintamani — apparently a disciple of Shyamananda (Purva, lahari 1, note 38; Dakshina, lahari 10, note 13). "Took counsel with Rasika" — *bicārila rasikera sthāne*. 13–16 are Shyamananda's words to Rasika.
[^12]: The Rasa-yatra (*rāsaẏātrā*) is the festival of the rasa, Krishna's dance with the gopis (Bhagavatam 10.29–33); it is usually celebrated on the autumn full moon (Kartika), but here on the spring (*basanta*) full moon of the month of Vaishakha (April–May; cf. the spring rasa in the Gita-govinda, canto 1). "A bright moon, [as in] autumn" — *śarada ujjbala candra*; *śarada* can also be understood as "autumnal". "Full moon" — *pūrṇimī* (= *pūrṇimā*). "A clear night" — *nirmmala-yāminī*. The spring full moon of Vaishakha was also the time of the Panchama-dola at Dharenda (Dakshina, lahari 10).
[^13]: "Entrust with duties" — *bhāra deha*. "Gathering provisions" (15) — *drabya karibāre*. "As far as his strength allows" — *yāra yata śaktirūpe*; "prepare provisions" — *karaha sambhāra*.
[^14]: "In Rasamaya's house" — *rasamaẏa-gṛhe*: in Dharenda (cf. Dakshina, lahari 10). "Fixed" — *nirṇaẏa karila*: Shyamananda (or Rasika) fixed the time of the festival.
[^15]: Sri Govindapur — Shyamananda's village (Dakshina, lahari 12, note 7; lahari 16, note 21). "Went out" (19) — *bāhāra* (= *bāhira*). "To collect alms" — *bhikṣā karibāra*: to gather provisions for the festival (cf. 27).
[^16]: Sri Gopivallabhapur — Rasika's village on the Suvarnarekha (Dakshina, lahari 3). "Shining over the three worlds" — *trailokya birāje*.
[^17]: "Followers" — *anucara*: Rasika's disciples and servants. "The site for the rasa" — *rāsasthala*. "Clear" — *pariṣkāra* (in the scan *parikṣāra*, also in 22).
[^18]: "About a year" — *batsareka*. "The thorny thickets" — *kaṇṭakera bana*, lit. "the forest of thorns". "Even, like flower pollen" (24) — *parāga dhūli samāna*: *samāna* means both "even" and "like"; it can be understood as "[soft] like flower pollen". "Pavilion" — *maṇḍapa*.
[^19]: Dinashyama, Ramakrishna, Shyamagopala — disciples of Rasika (cf. Purva, lahari 1, 131–132); Narayanadasa is a new name; Rasamaya and Vamshidasa — note 10. Ramakrishna is apparently the brahmana Ramakrishna(dasa) of Dakshina, lahari 16, 10. "Do all the work here" (26) — *ekhānera sarbbakāryya karaha*.
[^20]: "Himself" — *āpani*: Rasika (cf. 19). "In many ways" — *nānārūpe*. "In one single year" — *ekai batsare*.
[^21]: "Mung [and] biri" — *muga biri*: mung beans and biri (urad, black gram). "Granaries" (29) — *marāi*: round woven bins for grain. "The chief [supply]" — *pradhāna*: apparently the main provisions for the festival. After the first line of 29 the scan has "॥" instead of "।".
[^22]: Maida (*maẏadā*) — finely milled wheat flour; "chickpeas" — *cholā*; khesari (*khesārī*) — grass pea, a pulse. Gur and guya — unrefined cane sugar and areca nut (Purva, lahari 11, note 9). "Loads" — *bhāra*: a load carried on a shoulder pole (Purva, lahari 12, note 4).
[^23]: "Fried dishes" — *pakvānna*; "sweets" — *miṣṭānna*. "Bananas" — *kadalī*; "well ripened" — *supakka*. "Laddus" (32) — *lāṛu*; "pots" — *hā̃ṛī*; "quickly" — *tvarati*.
[^24]: "Baskets" — *ḍola*: large woven baskets. Saliya-ukhra (*sāliẏā ukhaṛā*) — according to the editor's note, "the best murki", puffed rice in sugar syrup; *sāliẏā* apparently means "of shali rice". Chida — flattened rice; "thin" — *saru*. Khai (34) — puffed rice; hudum (*huṛuma*) — according to the editor's note, a kind of roasted chida; "making [it] into hudum" — *huṛuma kariẏā*, the sense is approximate. Luchis and puris — wheat breads fried in oil or ghee. The editor's notes are not translated.
[^25]: "Cream" (35) — *sara*: the skin of boiled milk. "Household people" — *parijana* (in 43 — *paribāra*). Badis (*baṛi*) — sun-dried balls of pulse paste. Maja (*mājā*) — according to the editor's note, the pith of the banana stem (*thoṛ*). Rambha (*rambhā*) — bananas (plantains). "Eggplants" — *bāigana*.
[^26]: Kushmanda — a gourd (ash gourd). Palata (*palatā*) — apparently patol (pointed gourd) or its leaves. Karela (*karalā*) — bitter gourd. Taba (*ṭābā*), jambira (*jāmbira*), nembu (*nembu*) — kinds of lemon; kamala — oranges. Shatakara (38) — according to the editor's note, pomelo (*bātāpi nebu*); narenga (*nāreṅga*) — oranges; "pomegranates" — *dāṛimba*.
[^27]: Khadira (*khadira*) — the astringent extract of catechu acacia, tambula — betel leaves, "lime" — *cūrṇa*: all for pan. Hingu — asafoetida; methi — fenugreek; "nutmeg" — *jāẏaphala*; mahuri (*mahurī*) — fennel (in Dakshina, lahari 10, *mahuri* is a musical instrument); jira — cumin. "Pepper" (40) — *marica*, black pepper. "Dishes of the six tastes" — *ṣaṛarasa byañjana*.
[^28]: "Subsidiary provisions" — *upadrabya*. "The Creator" — *bidhātā*. "Countless" (42) — *apramita*. Mahalakshmi — the goddess of prosperity; "has appeared" — *haila upasana* (cf. Dakshina, lahari 16, note 26). "Stored in [their] places" (43) — *yathāsthāne … purila*.
[^29]: "The circle of the rasa" — *rāsa-maṇḍalī* (Dakshina, lahari 15, note 41). "In the shape of an eight-petalled [lotus]" — *aṣṭadalākṛti*; "octagonal in its measure" — *aṣṭakoṇa parimāṇa*. The inset on pdf 133 (after this page) is a photograph of the rasa-mancha (rasa temple) of Sri Sri Radha-Govindaji at Gopivallabhapur — octagonal, with nine spires ("Kadamkhandi"); the caption of the inset is not translated.
[^30]: "Paintings" — *nānācitra*. "Are seized with awe" — *pāẏa trāse*, lit. "feel fear": reverent awe.
[^31]: "It cannot be moved" — *ṭhelā nāhi haẏa*. "A firm fence" — *goṛā bāṛa* (so in the scan): understood as *bāṛa* (= *beṛā*), "fence", and *goṛā*, "base, root", i.e. firmly planted; the reading and sense are approximate.
[^32]: "Platforms" — *mañca*: high stands around the circle.
[^33]: Purandara — Indra. "Set up" — *sthāpilena*: apparently figures of the gods on the platforms, as if watching the rasa (cf. Bhagavatam 10.33.3: the gods in their celestial chariots watch the rasa).
[^34]: "Banners" — *niśāna*: banners; *niśāna* is also a large drum (kettledrum). "Trembles" — *tharahara*. "A rain of flowers" — *puṣpa bariṣaṇe*.
[^35]: Indrajala (*indrajāla*, lit. "Indra's net, magic") — apparently patterned net-like canopies and hangings (cf. 54 on pdf 135: "they adorned the circle with indrajala of various kinds"). "On high" — *ucce*. Couplet 51 is the last on pdf 132 and is complete; pdf 133 and 134 are inset plates, and the lahari continues on pdf 135 with ॥ 52 ॥.
[^36]: The description of the festive decoration of the rasa circle begins in 44–51 (RM-3-01-001-051). "From them" — *tāhe*: from the tall platforms (51). Chamaras are whisks of yak tails. "Pendants" — *jhārā*: hanging clusters, fringes (Dakshina, lahari 15, notes 36, 38). "In clusters" — *kerā kerā* (so in the scan): the meaning is unclear, understood as in Dakshina, lahari 15, note 38.
[^37]: Shola (*śolā*) is the light white pith of a marsh plant, from which festive decorations are carved in Bengal. "Of how many kinds" — *kata bhānti* (= *bhā̃ti*). "In rows" — *pā̃ti pā̃ti*.
[^38]: Indrajala (*indrajāla*) — apparently patterned net-like awnings and pendants (note 35). "In various styles" — *nānā chā̃de*. "Poles" — *kāṣṭhete*, lit. "on wooden [posts]".
[^39]: "[Designs of] rice flour" — *taṇḍulera cūrṇe*: patterns (alpana) of rice flour with which the festival ground is decorated.
[^40]: "Before the eyes" — *dṛggocara*, "visible to the eye".
[^41]: "Pavilion" — *maṇḍapa*. "Wish-fulfilling tree" — *kalpataru* (Dakshina, lahari 15, note 36). "Wondrously skilful" — *ati bicakṣaṇa*. Cf. the description of Vrindavana in Rasika's speech (Dakshina, lahari 15, 101–110): the wish-fulfilling tree and the jewelled throne at its foot.
[^42]: "Octagonal" — *aṣṭakoṇe*, lit. "at the eight corners" (cf. 44: the rasa circle is eight-petalled, octagonal). "Eight Deities" — *aṣṭa śrīmūrati*, with eight Thakuranis (62): apparently eight Deities of Krishna, each with a Thakurani — a Deity of Radha (cf. Dakshina, lahari 11, note 17: Gopivallabha-raya with the Thakurani); which ones, the author does not say. "Placed" — *dila bāre*: the sense of *bāre* is unclear (so in the scan, checked at ×4; "in [due] turn"? or a misprint). The subject is not named; understood as Rasika.
[^43]: "Canopies" — *cā̃duẏā*: cloth awnings stretched over the festival ground. "In clusters" — *yutha yutha*, lit. "in flocks, in throngs".
[^44]: "Greatness" — *anubhabe*: it can also be understood as Rasika's "spiritual insight (experience)" (cf. lahari 2, 67).
[^45]: Vyasa is the compiler of the Bhagavata; Shuka (Shukadeva) narrated it to Parikshit; Narada is Vyasa's teacher. "Qualities" — *guṇa*: apparently the qualities [of Krishna and His pastimes], including the rasa that Rasika re-enacted.
[^46]: "Remnants" — *abaśeṣa* (Dakshina, lahari 3, note 8). Rasikendra is Rasikananda. The author ascribes his inspiration to Rasika dwelling in his heart (cf. Dakshina, lahari 5, note 28).
[^47]: The subject is not named (*dekhila*, *kaila* — third person, often used of oneself in this language); understood as the author: he saw Rasika with his own eyes and was near him from childhood (the author is the son of Rasamaya, Rasika's disciple; cf. lahari 2, 45: "Gopijana-vallabha, the son of Rasamaya" among the boys at the festival). It can also be understood as "all the play that [Rasika] had from childhood with [his companions]".
