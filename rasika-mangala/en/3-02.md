# Pashchima-vibhaga. Lahari 2. The Description of the Adhivasa of the Rasa

*A song in glory of Shyamananda; having gathered everything needed over a year, Rasika opens the Rasa-yatra on the spring full moon of Vaishakha; there arrive Shyamananda, Hridayananda, Auliya Thakura, Vidyutmala Thakurani, Subaladasa, the descendants of Nityananda and Advaita, the followers of the twelve gopalas and the sixty-four mahantas, kings and countless people; in the evening the adhivasa of the festival is performed, and Rasika worships Shyamananda, Hridayananda and the mahantas; hundreds of thousands partake of prasada, the demigods serve unrecognized together with the people, and all call Rasika a partial incarnation; eight boys, among them Rasamaya's son Gopijana-vallabha, are dressed as the eight sakhis, and the boy Raghunatha as Krishna; all forget themselves in bliss, as though Vrindavana had appeared*

*The second lahari.*

*Raga Varadi.*[^1]
*Ghosha: "[My] life is Radhanatha, O, [my] soul is Gopinatha."*

**1.** Glory, glory to Shyamananda, the sweet form!
Be merciful, so that I may sing [your] glory day and night.[^2]

**2.** At that time Rasikendra, going round all four directions,
gathered everything [needed] within one year.[^3]

**3.** In spring, when the month of Vaishakha had become full,
on the full-moon day he opened the festival.[^4]

**4.** First came Shyamananda-raya;
Sri Hridayananda [Rasika] invited with care.[^5]

**5.** Auliya Thakura came rejoicing;
[there came] Vidyutmala Thakurani — Lakshmi herself.[^6]

**6.** They came to see the Rasa-yatra —
great mahajanas with their followers.[^7]

**7.** Thakura Subaladasa, a great mahajana,
performs kirtana together with Jagatvallabha.[^8]

**8.** Shyama, Mathuradasa, Vallabha the drummer —
with Hridayananda came all his own servants.[^9]

**9.** Bada Balaramadasa Thakura came;
the sons and grandsons of Nityananda came and entered.[^10]

**10.** All the sons and grandsons of Advaita arrived,
the disciples and grand-disciples of the twelve gopalas.[^11]

**11.** The servants of the sixty-four mahantas and the servants of their servants
hastily entered in time for the Rasa-yatra.[^12]

**12.** Ramadasa Thakura, Krishnadasa the renunciant,
Sri Prasada Thakura, Sri Jagannathadasa —[^13]

**13.** Dvaraka, Mathura, Vrindavana, Nilachala:
as many companions of Krishna as there were in each of these places,

**14.** all came to see the Rasa-yatra;
hundreds and hundreds of maharajas came there.[^14]

**15.** People without count from all four directions:
brahmanas, sannyasis, sadhus, kings, subjects — crowd upon crowd.[^15]

**16.** Women, men, and children, the old and the rest —
all came, having locked up their houses and doors.

**17.** The countless people cannot be described even with a hundred mouths:
throw a mustard seed, and it would never fall to the ground.[^16]

**18.** To perform sankirtana, a great many parties
came crowd upon crowd from all four directions.[^17]

**19.** They performed the auspicious adhivasa of the festival:
with clothes and ornaments they honour all the Vaishnavas.[^18]

**20.** Setting up the auspicious pot, [performing] the auspicious arati,
[placing] turmeric, rice, durva grass, grain, mango leaves,[^19]

**21.** setting a coconut on top of that pot —
around the pot burn four lamps of jewels;

**22.** together with curd, milk, ghee, honey, sugar,
and with garlands, sandal, clothes and ornaments as well;

**23.** kunkuma, musk, chuya, abir, saffron,
camphor, sandal — hundreds of pots together.

**24.** Thus, having joyfully performed the adhivasa,
first of all Rasika worshipped Shyamananda.[^20]

**25.** Then he worshipped the feet of Sri Hridayananda;
then at the sankirtana he honoured Tulasidasa.

**26.** To adorn [them] with clothes, ornaments, garlands, sandal,
Rasikachandra worships [them] with his own hands.

**27.** Rasika's god-brothers [and] worthy disciples —
one person to each mahanta —

**28.** camphor, sandal, garlands, clothes, ornaments
were offered, as befitting, by hundreds and hundreds of people.[^21]

**29.** Thus, having performed the adhivasa in the evening,
Rasikendra-raya began the festival.[^22]

**30.** Hundreds and hundreds of people entered the storerooms
and began to give out cooling provisions [and] sida.[^23]

**31.** Hundreds and hundreds of kings, subjects, Vaishnavas, brahmanas —
all were satisfied and served food.[^24]

**32.** Hundreds and hundreds of brahmanas do the cooking;
a hundred elderly brahmanas serve out the food.[^25]

**33.** Hundreds and hundreds of people carry water on their shoulders;
hundreds and hundreds of people, seated, sprinkle the leaf-plates.

**34.** Hundreds and hundreds of leaf-plates are before Krishna:
shali rice, hundreds of dishes of six tastes,[^26]

**35.** khira, pitha, fried delicacies — various gifts;
brahmanas of righteous conduct offer [them] to Krishna.

**36.** Then, taking the prasada, they give it to all;
five hundred thousand sit down at one time.[^27]

**37.** Thus they sit down in rows again and again;
hundreds and hundreds of servants clear away the leaf-plates.

**38.** The splendour of the festival seems a wonder;
all say: "Rasikendra is a partial incarnation."[^28]

**39.** The world of the gods [and] the world of men, joined together,
make merry in every way in the great bliss of the Rasa-yatra.

**40.** Someone cooks, someone serves out the food;
no one recognizes anyone: the demigods are [there] in secret.[^29]

**41.** What power have humans to accomplish such a work?
Seeing that assembly, gods [and] men are astonished.

**42.** A most wondrous lila [he], the friend of the three worlds,
Rasikendu, manifested on earth.[^30]

**43.** Having sought out people one by one for all these tasks,
they then dressed the boys close by.[^31]

**44.** They settled on eight sakhis [and] one Krishna;
beforehand they had taught them all to dance.

**45.** Devakidasa and Gokula Mahashaya,
Gopijana-vallabha, the son of Rasamaya,[^32]

**46.** Gaura, Gopaladasa, the boy Gokula,
Narayanadasa, Gopijivana of Bhupura.

**47.** These eight boys — to the number of the eight sakhis;
in the dress of Krishna — one boy named Raghunatha.[^33]

**48.** With care they dressed them all;
these young boys are most skilled in dance.

**49.** They plaited the hair on their heads into braids;
to them they deftly bound knots with pendants of jewels.[^34]

**50.** Stringing various jewels [and] pearls on double threads,
they tied [them] beautifully and neatly, encircling the head.[^35]

**51.** On everyone's forehead they put fragrant sandal;
in the midst of the gorochana — a lovely dot of phagu.[^36]

**52.** On the eyes they put kajal — most charming;
on the nose shine a row of jewels [and] the finest pearl.

**53.** On the lips is the red of tambula, beautiful to see;
in the ears jewelled ornaments sparkle.[^37]

**54.** The neck is beautifully adorned with [necklaces] of various jewels and gems;
on the breast [necklaces] hang in tiers, dazzlingly bright.

**55.** Golden bajubandhas grace both arms;
on them golden pendants shine beautifully.[^38]

**56.** Beside them golden bracelets look lovely;
wondrous patterned conch-shell bangles shine on both hands.[^39]

**57.** Above the conch-shell bangles — golden kankanas on the hands;
beside them — bajubandhas, golden ornaments.

**58.** All the fingers are adorned with jewelled rings;
on everyone's breast — wondrous bodices.

**59.** Blue, yellow, silken, fine, wondrous garments
they put on all the boys in various styles.

**60.** Over that — a golden wrap;
at the waist they tied to it [a girdle] of inlaid little bells.[^40]

**61.** On the feet — double rows of bells [and] sweet-sounding nupuras:
they made everyone's form one of supreme sweetness.[^41]

**62.** It is as if one sees the young women of Vraja in person.
One boy they took care to make [like] Krishna:

**63.** every limb they adorned with ornaments in various styles;
in various styles they put [on him] yellow garments;

**64.** [he] is coated with sandal, kunkuma [and] musk;
on his head they set a most beautiful crown:

**65.** it sparkles, various gems blaze in it,
and in it — peacock feathers, most charming.

**66.** Thus they dressed Krishna and the young women of Vraja:
seeing [this], all the men and women are enchanted.

**67.** Rasika's power cannot be told:
it is as if Vrindavana itself had come and appeared [here].[^42]

**68.** All were immersed in bliss, with no awareness of the outer world;
there are no enemies [or] friends — all have one life.

**69.** They know no sorrow, all are drunk with happiness;
all think themselves young immortals.

**70.** No one has anger, pride, conceit;
no one has strangers — all are their own.

**71.** They know neither night nor day — [only] jubilation in bliss;
seeing what Rasika has manifested, they are amazed.

**72.** Whatever provision they think of comes there;
the eight siddhis [and] the nine nidhis follow in attendance.[^43]

**73.** Such is [Rasika] — an ocean of mercy, a sweet form,
for whom Krishna is the treasure of life, family, character and caste.

**74.** Rasika's greatness cannot be told at all:
by remembering him one attains Krishna, the lord of life.[^44]

**75.** Hear the Rasika-mangala, all friends:
without delay you will gain the treasure of prema and bhakti for Krishna.

**76.** Adorning himself with Shyamananda's feet,
the son of Rasamaya joyfully composed [this].

*Thus ends the second lahari in the Pashchima-vibhaga of the Sri Rasika-mangala, called "The Description of the Adhivasa of the Rasa".*[^45]

[^1]: The lines before the raga are the lahari's heading («দ্বিতীয়-লহরী»), in italics without a number. Lahari 2 begins on pdf 135 (printed p. 95) after the colophon of lahari 1 (files RM-3-01-052-071). Raga Varadi (*barāṛī*) — Purva, lahari 8, note 2. The ghosha reads in the scan: *jībana rādhānātha re parāṇa gopīnātha*: Radhanatha ("the Lord of Radha") and Gopinatha ("the Lord of the gopis") are names of Krishna; *re* is an exclamation. There is no mangala-shloka: after the raga and ghosha the song begins with ॥ 1 ॥. The title follows the colophon on pdf 137 (printed p. 97; checked against the scan): «ইতি শ্রীরসিকমঙ্গল-পশ্চিম-বিভাগে রাসাধিবাস-বর্ণন-নাম দ্বিতীয়-লহরী সম্পূর্ণা» (*rāsādhibāsa-barṇana*) — "The Description of the Adhivasa of the Rasa". Adhivasa is the rite of consecration on the eve of a festival (19–29). The summary line covers the whole lahari (1–6 from the scan of pdf 135; 7–76 from the OCR of pdf 136–137, provisionally).
[^2]: "The sweet form" — *madhura mūrati*.
[^3]: Rasikendra is Rasikananda. "Going round all four directions" — *phiri cāridike*: gathering provisions for the festival (lahari 1, 26–43). "Gathered everything" — *saba karila saṃyoge*.
[^4]: "When the month of Vaishakha had become full" — *baiśākha pūrṇa māsa*: understood as the full moon of Vaishakha (April–May); cf. "the spring full moon, the night of Vaishakha" — the time of the Panchama-dola (Dakshina, lahari 8, note 24; lahari 9, note 14). "Opened the festival" — *yātrā karila prakāśa*, lit. "manifested the festival (festive procession)". The spring rasa — colophon of lahari 1.
[^5]: "Came" — *bīje karilā* (*bijaẏa*): respectful, of a ceremonial arrival (Dakshina, lahari 16, note 31); the editor glosses it below the line as *āgamana*, "arrival" (gloss not translated). Hridayananda is Hridaya Chaitanya, Shyamananda's guru (Purva, lahari 1, note 13). "Invited with care" — *ānāilā yatane*, lit. "had [him] brought with care"; the subject is not named, understood as Rasika.
[^6]: Auliya Thakura (*āuliẏā ṭhākura*) and Vidyutmala Thakurani (*bidyutmālā ṭhākurāṇī*, "garland of lightning") — not identified. *Āuliẏā* means "free, ecstatic [in love of God]", a byname of wandering Vaishnavas (cf. Chaitanya Dasa the Auliya in the Prema-vilasa). "Rejoicing" — *kautuke*. "Lakshmi herself" — *lakṣmīra sbarūpe*, lit. "in the form of Lakshmi": of her beauty and grace.
[^7]: The Rasa-yatra (*rāsa-yātrā*) is the festival of the rasa-lila, the circle dance of Krishna with the gopis headed by Sri Radha (Bhag. 10.29–33; Purva, lahari 7, note 29). According to Gaudiya Vaishnava teaching, the rasa-lila is the summit of Krishna's pastimes, a manifestation of the gopis' pure prema, in which there is not a trace of mundane desire (kama); whoever hears of it with faith attains the highest devotion and is freed from the heart's disease of mundane desires (Bhag. 10.33.39). The Bhagavata describes the autumn rasa; the spring rasa is sung in Jayadeva's Gita-govinda (canto 1). At the festival the Deities are placed on the rasa-mancha in the middle of the decorated circle (lahari 1, 44–63). "Mahajanas" — *mahājana*: great Vaishnavas. "With their followers" — *saṅge anucare*.
[^8]: Thakura Subaladasa (*ṭhākura subaladāsa*) and Jagatvallabha (*jagataballabha*) are not identified; *jagataballabha* may also be taken as an epithet, "beloved of the world". "A great mahajana" — *baṛa mahājana*: a great Vaishnava (note 7).
[^9]: "Shyama, Mathuradasa, Vallabha the drummer" — *śyāma mathurā dāsa bāẏena ballabha*; the names may also be divided otherwise (e.g. "Shyama-Mathuradasa"). *Bāẏena* is a musician who plays the drum (khol). Whether this Mathuradasa is the one in lahari 1, 6 is unclear. "His own servants" — *nija bhṛtya*: Hridayananda's disciples.
[^10]: Bada Balaramadasa Thakura — *baṛa balarāma dāsa ṭhākura* (*baṛa*, "elder, great"); cf. Purva, lahari 1, 38 (note 21) and 62 (Balaramadasa, note 33); not identified. "Sons and grandsons" — *putra pautra*: the descendants of Nityananda (of Virachandra and his line); likewise of Advaita in 10.
[^11]: The twelve gopalas are associates of Nityananda (Purva, lahari 1, note 18). "Disciples and grand-disciples" — *śiṣya praśiṣya-gaṇa*: their spiritual descendants.
[^12]: The sixty-four mahantas — Purva, lahari 1, note 20. "The servants of their servants" — *tadbhṛtya*. "Hastily" — *tvarita*.
[^13]: Ramadasa Thakura, Krishnadasa, Sri Prasada Thakura (cf. Purva, lahari 1, 62, note 33) and Sri Jagannathadasa are not identified. "The renunciant" — *bairāgī*: a Vaishnava who has renounced the world (cf. Purva, lahari 1, 63: "renounced, devoted to Krishna").
[^14]: Nilachala is Puri. "Companions of Krishna" — *kṛṣṇa-sahacara*: Vaishnavas. "Maharajas" — *mahārājā*: ruling princes.
[^15]: "Without count" — *samuccaẏa nāi*, lit. "there is no reckoning". "Sannyasis" — *nyāsī*. "Crowd upon crowd" — *yūthe yūthe*.
[^16]: "Cannot be described even with a hundred mouths" — *śatamukhe kahā naẏa*. A mustard seed would not fall to the ground — so tightly packed were the people.
[^17]: "Parties" — *sampradā* (= *sampradāẏa*): kirtana groups. "A great many" — *bahuta*.
[^18]: Adhivasa is the rite of consecration on the eve of a festival (note 1). "Auspicious" — *maṅgala*.
[^19]: 20–23 describe the rite of adhivasa. The auspicious pot (*maṅgala ghaṭa*) is a pot of water crowned with a coconut and mango leaves, a sign of the divine presence in the rite. "Grain" — *dhānya*: unhusked rice. "Mango leaves" — *āmrapatrī*. Chuya (*cuẏā*) is a fragrant oil (perfume); abir is a fragrant red powder; "saffron" — *keśara*. "Pots" — *hāṇḍī*; "together" — *ekatara*.
[^20]: Rasika first worships Shyamananda, his guru, then Hridayananda, Shyamananda's guru (Purva, lahari 1, note 13). Tulasidasa (25) is the "guru of sankirtana" whom Rasika revered from childhood (Purva, lahari 1, 64, note 34). "Honoured" — *bandilā*. Rasikachandra (26) is Rasika.
[^21]: "God-brothers" — *guru-bhāi*: Shyamananda's disciples. "One person to each mahanta" — *eka eka mohāntera eka eka jane*: understood as each mahanta being presented with gifts by a separate person. "As befitting" — *yathāyogya*.
[^22]: "Began the festival" — *mahotsaba juṛilena*: opened the festive meal (the distribution of prasada). Rasikendra-raya is Rasika.
[^23]: "Cooling provisions" — *śītala sāmagrī*: meaning unclear; possibly raw (uncooked) foodstuffs. Sida (*sidā* = *sidhā*) is a dry ration (rice, dal and so on) given to brahmanas and sadhus so that they may cook their food themselves.
[^24]: "Served food" — *prabeṣaṇa kaila*: according to the editor's footnote, *prabeṣaṇa* = *paribeṣaṇa*, "serving out (distributing) food" (footnote not translated); so also in 32, 40.
[^25]: "Elderly" — *baẏesiẏā* (so in the scan; crop ×2): understood as "advanced in years" (from *baẏasa*, "age"); reading unclear. "Leaf-plates" (33) — *patra*: leaves used as plates; "sprinkle" — *śiñcena*: wash them by sprinkling with water.
[^26]: "Before Krishna" — before the Deity, for offering. "Shali rice" — *śāli anna*. "Dishes of six tastes" — *ṣaṛarasa byañjana* (cf. lahari 1, 40). Khira (35) is rice cooked in thickened milk; pitha are sweet cakes; "fried delicacies" — *pakvānna*. "Of righteous conduct" — *sadācāra*.
[^27]: "Five hundred thousand" — *pā̃ca śata sahasra*, lit. "five hundreds of thousands"; it may also be taken as "five hundred, a thousand". "Rows" (37) — *maṇḍalī*.
[^28]: "A partial incarnation" — *aṃśa abatāra*: so the people at the festival say; this is the author's exalted praise. According to Gaudiya Vaishnava teaching, Rasika is a devotee, a disciple of Shyamananda. On how the author exalts Shyamananda and Rasika — Purva, lahari 1, note 2; Purva, lahari 14, note 11.
[^29]: "In secret" — *gupte*: the demigods, unrecognized, served on a par with the people. "Gods [and] men" (41) — *sura nara*.
[^30]: "The friend of the three worlds" — *tribhubana-bandhu*; Rasikendu, "the moon of the rasikas", is Rasika. "Manifested" — *prakāśa karilā*.
[^31]: 43–66 describe the preparation for a performance of the rasa-lila: boys portray the sakhis and Krishna (cf. Purva, lahari 7, 107–111, note 29); on the rasa-lila — note 7. "Having sought out people one by one" — *loka khũji eke eke*. "Dressed" — *beśa karila*; "close by" — *samīpe*. "Settled on" (44) — *karila niścaẏa*; "beforehand" — *pūrbba haite*.
[^32]: Gopijana-vallabha, the son of Rasamaya, is the author of the poem himself (Dakshina, lahari 4, note 14). The other boys are not identified. "Mahashaya" is a respectful title. In 46 the names may also be divided otherwise: "Gaura-Gopaladasa" as one name, in which case the eighth is Bhupura (*bhupura*, so in the scan, crop ×2). Here *bhupura* is taken as a place name ("of Bhupura"); reading unclear.
[^33]: "To the number of the eight sakhis" — *aṣṭa sakhī paramāṇa*, lit. "eight sakhis as the measure". The eight sakhis are the principal companions of Sri Radha (Lalita, Vishakha and the others). Raghunatha is not identified. "Young" (48) — *naba*; "most skilled" — *baṛa … bicakṣaṇa*.
[^34]: "Knots with pendants of jewels" — *maṇi-khopa-jhārā*: understood as a knot of hair (*khõpā*) with pendants (*jhārā*) of gems; meaning approximate. "Deftly" — *susāra*.
[^35]: "On double threads" — *dosati* (taken as *do-sūti*); "neatly" — *susañca* (so in the scan, crop ×2): meaning unclear, rendered approximately.
[^36]: Gorochana is a yellow pigment for decorating the forehead; phagu (*phāgu*) is red powder (abir). "Kajal" (52) — collyrium. "A row of jewels [and] the finest pearl" — *ratna pā̃ti muktābara*: a nose ornament.
[^37]: Tambula is betel. "Jewelled ornaments" — *ratnakāpa*: understood as earrings (Oriya *kāpa*, "earring"). "Sparkle" — *jhalamala*. "In tiers" (54) — *thare thare*; "hang" — *lambe*.
[^38]: A bajubandha (*bājubandha*) is an armlet worn on the upper arm. "Golden pendants" — *khopa soṇā jhā̃pā*: meaning approximate.
[^39]: "Bracelets" — *tāṛa*: heavy bracelets. "Patterned conch-shell bangles" — *khãjā śā̃khā*: *khãjā* taken as "notched, patterned"; reading unclear. A kankana (57) is a bracelet. Bajubandhas are mentioned twice (55, 57) — so in the text. "Bodices" (58) — *kāñculī*. "Silken, fine" (59) — *pāṭa neta*.
[^40]: "Wrap" — *uṛaẏāni*: a veil or wrap (cf. *oṛhanī*). "At the waist" — *ā̃ṭāẏa*: according to the editor's footnote, "at the waist" (*kaṭite*; footnote not translated). "Inlaid little bells" — *khañjita kiṅkiṇī*.
[^41]: "Double rows of bells" — *ghughũrī dosarī*. Nupuras are anklets with little bells. "Sweet-sounding" — *rasāla*.
[^42]: "Power" — *anubhaba*: also "realization, experience"; it may be taken as "what Rasika experienced (manifested)". "As if" — *kibā*.
[^43]: The eight siddhis are mystic perfections (the ability to become minute and the rest); the nine nidhis are the nine treasures of Kubera. The sense: everything needed comes to Rasika of itself. "Follow in attendance" — *saṅgete beṛāẏa*.
[^44]: "The treasure of life, family, character and caste" (73) — *prāṇadhana kulaśīla jāti*: Rasika has no family or standing other than Krishna. "By remembering him one attains Krishna" — the author's exalted praise; on how he exalts Rasika — note 28.
[^45]: The title is "The Description of the Adhivasa of the Rasa" (*rāsādhibāsa-barṇana*; checked against the scan). The colophon stands on pdf 137 (printed p. 97) below ॥76॥ in the right column; below it are a rule and the editor's footnote to 60. Lahari 3 begins on a new page, pdf 138 (files RM-3-03-001-031).
