# Dakshina-vibhaga. Lahari 9. Preparations for the Panchama-dola

*A song in glory of Shyamananda; Rasika stays at Balarampur; twenty Vaishnavas come to him, and at midnight, when there is no ghee to feed them, he himself goes into the town for it and in the dark enters the house of a mleccha, a Mughal; the man beats him, and Rasika says with a smile that he will hurt his hand on his hard body; the astonished Mughal falls at his feet, but within a few days loses his horses, elephants, wealth and family; terrified, he seeks Rasika's shelter, and Rasika tells him to worship Krishna and be merciful to all living beings; the yavana becomes a sadhu and is rich again; at Shyamananda's order Rasika gathers provisions, comes to Dharenda, to Rasamaya's house, and tells everyone to prepare for the Panchama-dola on the spring full moon of Vaishakha; all joyfully bring offerings*

*The ninth lahari.*

*Raga Kamoda — panchali metre.*[^1]

**1.** Glory, glory to Shyamananda, revered by the people of the three worlds,
in the garb that purifies the world!
O life of the world, treasure of Rasika's life,
kind one, compassionate to living beings![^2]

**2.** At Balarampur Rasika-shekhara
stayed for some days.
At that time twenty Vaishnavas
came there one day.[^3]

**3.** Having greeted [them], they gave sweet food,
gave all the raw provisions and so on.
Only ghee there was none; midnight came;
a servant told Rasika [of it].[^4]

**4.** Hearing this, at once, to fetch ghee,
[he] went into the town.
The night was dark, the road could not be made out —
he entered the house of a mleccha.[^5]

**5.** On a couch the ill-behaved mleccha
sits together with his wife.
Rasika went there;
in anger the wicked man seized [him] by the hand.

**6.** The wicked, ill-behaved man struck a blow
on Rasika's tender body.
Rasika, seeing [this], says with a smile,
playfully holding him by the hand:

**7.** "Listen, good sir, why do you strike?
There is no need for that at all.
Your hand, I know, will be hurt:
this body of mine is hard."[^6]

**8.** Hearing this, the Mughal was astonished
and let go of Rasika's hand.
Pleading, clasping [his] feet,
he at once fell to the ground.

**9.** Rasika quickly brought that ghee
[and] gave it to the assembly of Vaishnavas.
Within two or three days that very yavana
met with misfortune.[^7]

**10.** His horses, his elephants — all suddenly perished;
his wealth went who knows where.
His wife and the rest — all were struck down;
[he himself] barely held on to life.[^8]

**11.** Seeing Rasika's greatness, all the people
were filled with wonder.
In terror the Mughal came:
"Prabhu, [I seek] your shelter!

**12.** I am an offender; what good sense have I?
Fathomless, great is [your] greatness.
[You are] a sure stronghold of refuge, the possessor of all virtues:
be merciful to me."[^9]

**13.** Hearing his words, [Rasika] himself says:
"Listen, listen, good sir:
go and worship Krishna, [be] merciful to all living beings —
and wealth will surely come."[^10]

**14.** Accepting those words, that yavana became a sadhu
and took Rasika's shelter.
Once more he had boundless wealth,
by Rasika's mercy.

**15.** Rasika's greatness has no limit;
it is famed in this world.
Then Rasikendra, at Shyamananda's order,
gathered many provisions there.[^11]

**16.** Taking all the provisions, coming to Dharenda,
Rasika entered [the village].
In Rasamaya's house Rasika-shekhara
stayed there that day.[^12]

**17.** All who seek the highest good [he] had quickly brought,
[and] told [them] everything in detail:
"Shyamananda-raya has given me an order —
for the sake of the Panchama-dola.[^13]

**18.** All the preparations — each whatever [he can] — make,
many things in various ways.
On the spring full moon, on the night of Vaishakha,
[there will be] a festival, wondrous beyond words.[^14]

**19.** I shall go ahead, prepare the place,
build the pavilion,
tell Shyamananda everything, —
and all of you come after."[^15]

**20.** Hearing [this], everyone, with joyful hearts,
made up many loads of provisions.
[For this] first meeting everyone, happy,
made many preparations.[^16]

**21.** Of Rasika's virtues hear, all people;
worship Rasika's feet.
Adorning himself with Shyamananda's feet,
the son of Rasamaya joyfully composed [this].[^17]

*Thus in the Dakshina-vibhaga of the Sri Rasika-mangala ends the ninth lahari, called "Preparations for the Panchama-dola."*[^18]

[^1]: The lines before the raga are the heading of the lahari ("নবম-লহরী"), in italics without a number. Lahari 9 begins on pdf 111 (printed p. 73; no running head), after the colophon of lahari 8 on pdf 110 (files RM-2-08-005-075). Raga Kamoda (*kāmoda*). "Panchali metre" (*pañcālī chanda*) is tripadi; in the edition each stanza is set in four lines and is translated likewise (cf. lahari 6, note 1). There is no ghosha and no mangala-shloka; the stanzas are numbered as in the edition. The name is given by the colophon on pdf 112 (checked against the scan): "পঞ্চমদোল-আয়োজন-নাম নবম-লহরী" (*pañcamadola-āẏojana*). Stanza 16 begins at the bottom of pdf 111 and ends at the top of pdf 112.
[^2]: "In the garb that purifies the world" — *bhubana-pābana-bānā*: *bānā* is taken as "garb, attire" (the Vaishnava appearance); the sense is approximate. "Treasure of Rasika's life" — *rasikera prāṇa-dhana*: Shyamananda is Rasika's guru.
[^3]: Balarampur — lahari 8, 45 and 61. "Twenty" — *biṃśete* (so in the scan); the OCR of the 1997 edition has "তিংশেতে" (possibly *triṃśe*, "thirty").
[^4]: "Raw provisions" — *śidā* (= *sidhā*): uncooked foodstuffs given to guests for preparing their meal; the editor's gloss below the line is not translated. Ghee is clarified butter.
[^5]: Mleccha (*mleccha*) — a non-Hindu, "barbarian"; here a Muslim, further on called a Mughal (8, 11) and a yavana (9, 14; cf. Purva, lahari 15, note 21).
[^6]: "Good sir" — *mahājana*, a polite address. "There is no need for that at all" — *tāra nāhi kichu dāẏa*; it can also be understood as "there is no fault [on my part]."
[^7]: "That very yavana met with misfortune" — *haila tā'ra akāje*, lit. "it turned to his harm." The author links the Mughal's misfortunes with the offence done to Rasika: according to Gaudiya Vaishnava teaching, offence against a Vaishnava (*vaiṣṇava-aparādha*) is ruinous (CC, Madhya 19), although Rasika himself, as 6–7 show, bears his assailant no anger.
[^8]: "Were struck down" — *haila hata*, "were slain (stricken)." "Barely held on to life" — *prāṇa laiẏā ṭānāṭāni*, lit. "[his] life was pulled this way and that."
[^9]: "Offender" — *aparādhī*. "A sure stronghold of refuge" — *śaraṇa pañjara*, lit. "a cage (enclosure) of refuge," i.e. a safe protection.
[^10]: "Good sir" — *mahāśaẏa*. Rasika promises the Mughal grandee the return of his wealth; according to Gaudiya Vaishnava teaching, devotional service is not performed for worldly gain, but the Lord is merciful also to those who come to Him with desires (Bhagavata 2.3.10: "whether free of desires or full of desires… one should worship the Supreme Person"), and purifies them.
[^11]: "Has no limit" — *dite nāhi sīmā*, lit. "there is no limit [for one] to give [its description]." "Gathered" — *kaila*, lit. "made." Shyamananda's order — lahari 8, 70–71.
[^12]: Rasamaya's house at Dharenda — Dakshina, laharis 4 and 6. "Entered [the village]" — *prabeśa hailā*.
[^13]: "All who seek the highest good" — *saba paramārthi* (*paramārthī*), i.e. pious people, devotees; apparently the people of Dharenda (16). "Had brought" — *ānā'ẏe*. "Me" — *moẏa* (an Oriya form; in the 1997 edition *mora*). Shyamananda's order — lahari 8, 68–71. Rasika's words run from 17 (second half) to 19.
[^14]: "On the spring full moon, on the night of Vaishakha" — *basanta-pūrṇamī, boiśākha yāminī*: on the time of the festival see lahari 8, note 24. "Festival" — *yātrā*, a festive procession (the dola-yatra). "Each whatever [he can]" — *kara ye yāhāra*, lit. "make, each whatever [he has]".
[^15]: "Tell Shyamananda everything" — *śyāmānanda-sthāne kahi bibaraṇe*; it can also be taken as "[there] where Shyamananda is, having told everything — and all of you come after." Pavilion — *maṇḍapa* (cf. lahari 10, 13–14).
[^16]: "Made up many loads" — *kaila bahu drabya bhāra*, lit. "made many burdens of things." "First meeting" — *prathama milana*: apparently the first joint festival of Shyamananda and Rasika (cf. lahari 10, 43: "the first pastime").
[^17]: The author's signature is given by the common formula; here, in the tripadi metre, it reads differently: "Shyamananda's feet are all the wealth of the son of Rasamaya" (*śyāmānanda-pada, sakala sampada, rasamaẏera nandana*; cf. Purva, lahari 11, note 15).
[^18]: The colophon is on pdf 112 (checked against the scan): "ইতি শ্রীরসিকমঙ্গল-দক্ষিণ-বিভাগে পঞ্চমদোল-আয়োজন-নাম নবম-লহরী সম্পূর্ণা"; on the title see note 1. Lahari 10 begins on the same page (files RM-2-10-001-073).
