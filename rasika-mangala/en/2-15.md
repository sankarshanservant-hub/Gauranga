# Dakshina-vibhaga. Lahari 15. The Instruction to Maharaja Vaidyanatha Bhanja on Meditation on Vrindavana and on Worship

*A song in glory of Shyamananda; before the king and the bhattacharyas Rasika speaks of the greatness of the sadhus and of their association: one who does not worship Krishna lives like a tree, breathes like a blacksmith's bellows, and in intelligence is like a hog, a dog, a camel and an ass, while a brahmana averse to Krishna is lower than a devoted shvapacha; the gods are Krishna's servants, and one may worship Him while remaining at home; subjects of every class surrender to Krishna; Rasika asks the three brothers for alms — the giving up of the killing of living beings — and names the eight who fall into hell for killing an animal; the brothers and all the people take exclusive shelter, the bhattacharyas acknowledge Rasika as an associate of Krishna and bless him, the king worships his feet, and the brothers receive the Krishna-mantra; Rasika teaches them to meditate on Krishna, the Lord of Vrindavana, in the mood of madhurya and describes Vrindavana — the Kalindi, the twelve forests, the wish-fulfilling trees, the jewelled pavilion and temple, the throne, the form of Radha and Krishna, the playing of the flute and the circle of the gopis; the brothers firmly devote themselves to the worship of Krishna*

*The fifteenth lahari.*

*Raga Kaushika.*[^1]
*Ghosha: "Glory to Rama and Krishna, O Murari!*
*O Murari, O Murari!"*

**1.** Glory, glory to Shyamananda, renowned in [all] the world,
the shining moon of the family among all the cowherd lineages![^2]

**2.** At that time Rasika speaks to the king,
[and] the great bhattacharyas listen.[^3]

**3.** With tears, with thrills [of joy] they hear Rasika's words;
giving up ignorance, they entered Krishna's shelter.

**4.** Then he speaks firmly of the greatness of the sadhus;
hearing [it], everyone was freed from the anxiety of the mind.

**5.** "Listen, listen, everyone, to the greatness of the sadhus:
even Brahma cannot tell its limit.

**6.** If by good fortune association with a sadhu happens even for a moment,
by [his] sight the sin that binds one to births is destroyed.[^4]

**7.** All the holy places of the gods he purifies forever;
the mere sight of a sadhu is the highest blessing.[^5]

**8.** Keep the company of such sadhus, giving up all [other] talk:
in the company of sadhus all the pains of birth in the world are ended.

**9.** For the good of the sadhus Krishna is always [with them];
the heart of a sadhu is Sri Krishna's own home.[^6]

**10.** Keep the company of such sadhus, worship Lord Krishna:
without the company of sadhus one never attains Krishna.

**11.** Keeping the company of sadhus, worship Krishna's feet;
why waste the human birth in vain?

**12.** If one does not worship Krishna, the living being suffers great misery:
he always sinks in the most terrible hell.

**13.** The rod of Yama constantly strikes the jiva;
now rising, now falling, he perishes in hell.[^7]

**14.** When, giving up everything, he takes shelter of Narayana,
then he is delivered in this round of worldly existence.[^8]

**15.** The living being who does not worship Krishna lives like a tree;
like a blacksmith's bellows, he [merely] breathes.[^9]

**16.** Whatever a being not devoted to Krishna eats —
whatever he eats is called impure.

**17.** His intelligence is like a hog's: he does not discriminate;
his intelligence is like a dog's: such is that wicked one.

**18.** His intelligence is like a camel's: he does not worship Krishna,
he eats thorns and the like, filling his belly.

**19.** Like an ass, the being carries various burdens
all the days that he does not hear the nectarean words about Krishna.

**20.** Even if he is the best of brahmanas, endowed with twice six qualities —
if he does not worship Krishna, he is called a shvapacha.[^10]

*Thus [it is said] in the Srimad-Bhagavatam:*

**21.** *"Than a brahmana endowed with twice six qualities but averse to the lotus feet*
*of the Lotus-naveled [Lord], better, I consider, is a shvapacha*
*who has offered to Him his mind, speech, activities, wealth*
*and life: he purifies [his] family, but not one [full] of pride."*[^11]

**22.** All the gods and goddesses are Krishna's servants;
all of them are constantly enchanted by the taste of Krishna's happiness.

**23.** If one worships Krishna, the gods are not angry:
if one waters the root of a tree, the leaves [too] are satisfied.[^12]

**24.** Gods, asuras, humans, yakshas, kinnaras, gandharvas and others —
all are blessed if [one] worships Krishna.

**25.** The supreme bliss of all is Lord Narayana;
giving up everything, all of you, worship Krishna's feet.

**26.** By no means waste this rare birth in vain:
if one worships Krishna, all the pains of worldly existence are ended.

**27.** Wife, sons and the rest, one's own household affairs,
wealth, people, kinsmen [and] family are false.[^13]

**28.** Offering [them] to Krishna with body, mind and speech,
all of you, with one mind, worship Krishna abundantly.

**29.** Remaining amid all this, worship Krishna firmly:
if you worship Krishna exclusively, you will quickly attain [Him].

**30.** Fix your mind unceasingly on Krishna's feet;
with body, mind and speech take shelter of Krishna.

**31.** Rejecting nectar, do not drink poison:
Krishna is nectar, and the rest is like poison."

**32.** Thus, expounding the truths of all the shastras,
Rasikendra spoke to the three brothers.

**33.** All the subjects who were in the king's assembly —
kshatriyas, vaishyas, brahmanas, shudras — gave their minds to Krishna.[^14]

**34.** For all who heard Rasika's words
suffering was ended: [they took] Krishna's shelter.

**35.** The sixty-four [kinds of] bhakti come at once to one
to whom the son of Achyuta is gracious.[^15]

**36.** A supreme Vaishnava, with a perfectly pure mind, becomes [one]
who hears Rasika's words with faith.

**37.** Then Rasika-shekhara commanded:
"Give me one alms, three brothers."[^16]

**38.** Hastily the three brothers joined their palms:
"Wealth, people, life — all are at your disposal.

**39.** Whatever you wish, command, considering [us] servants:
I have offered everything to your feet."

**40.** Hearing [this], the son of Achyuta says joyfully:
"Give me as alms [the giving up of] the killing of living beings.[^17]

**41.** If one does violence to living beings, great sin results:
at the end [of life] the being goes and falls into the terrible [hell] Raurava.[^18]

**42.** Eight persons fall into terrible hell;
I shall tell — listen attentively, with care.

**43.** The one who, seeing the animal, gives consent [to the killing];
the one who is the ruler of that village;[^19]

**44.** and the one who catches the animal and leads [it] away;
the one who buys, the one who sells, deluded by maya;

**45.** that lowest of brahmanas who offers the animal in sacrifice;
the one who cuts, the one who eats — these are the eight persons.

**46.** These eight persons fall into the most terrible hell —
[all] who, bereft of sense, kill an animal.[^20]

**47.** As many hairs as there are on the animal's body,
so many years they lie in these hells.

*Thus [it is said] in the Padma Purana:*

**48.** *The one who consents, the overseer, the slayer, the buyer [and] the seller,*
*the one who prepares it, the one who serves [and] the eaters — the eight killers.*[^21]

**49.** *He dwells in terrible hell for [as many] years [as] the animal's hairs —*
*so measured — that wicked one who kills animals contrary to the injunctions.*

**50.** For the destroyer of his own soul, the wicked one, there is no deliverance:
thus birth after birth passes for him in vain.[^22]

**51.** In all living beings Narayana dwells in a subtle form:
be it a worm or Brahma — [in each] He dwells in His own form.[^23]

**52.** All beings who thus do violence to living beings
fall into the Maharauravas and wander in births among the lowborn.

**53.** Thus, giving up everything, give your mind to Krishna;
offer home, sons, wealth to Krishna.

**54.** Krishna is mother, Krishna is father, Krishna is destroyer [and] creator;
Krishna is the Lord of all, Krishna is the protector.

**55.** Krishna is wealth, Krishna is kin, Krishna is friends;
knowing that Krishna is the soul, enter Krishna's shelter.[^24]

**56.** Seeing this suffering of the jivas, I am moved to compassion;
therefore I have told [it all] in order.

**57.** Heed my words, give your mind to Krishna,
make this human birth fruitful."

**58.** Seeing one another, all took exclusive shelter;
all the bhattacharyas say to the king:[^25]

**59.** hearing Rasika's words, the great best of kings —
the three brothers — became exclusive Vaishnavas.

**60.** Such wicked [men] became sadhus by the word of Rasika —
the son of Achyuta, whose vow is to crush the asuras.[^26]

**61.** When the king [and his brothers], the three noble ones, became sadhus,
hearing [of it], all the people took shelter of Krishna.

**62.** Whether women, children or the old —
seeing one another, all took exclusive shelter.

**63.** All the bhattacharyas say to the king:
"What Rasika says is the word of Shuka [and] Vyasa.

**64.** Much did [we] debate according to the doctrines of the six shastras,
[but] could not answer Rasika.

**65.** By the truth of the shastras he established the worship of Krishna;
no one had the power to refute [it].

**66.** We wandered so long, not knowing [the truth];
in vain did we all study, not knowing Krishna.

**67.** He has proclaimed this secret [hidden] in the Vedas:
this son of Achyuta is an associate of Krishna.[^27]

**68.** Blessed are King Vaidyanatha Bhanja [and his brothers], the three,
to whom Rasika Gosani has shown mercy!"

**69.** Thus the brahmanas, praising Rasika,
blessed [him], having found joy in their minds.

**70.** Seeing Rasika's power, the maharaja
worshipped Rasika's feet in many ways.

**71.** The three brothers received instruction in the Krishna-mantra;
[Rasika] began to speak [to them] of Krishna according to the nigama.[^28]

**72.** Prabhu explained to them what [true] worship is;
the three brothers listen, motionless.

**73.** "[It is] one's own mood: prema-bhakti for Krishna,
the mood in which the cowherd men and gopis of Vraja attained Krishna.[^29]

**74.** Krishna, the Lord of Vrindavana, is Bhagavan Himself;
meditate on Krishna in the mood of madhurya."

**75.** Rasika says: "King, listen with a firm mind:
I shall tell briefly something of the beauty of Vrindavana."

**76.** The three brothers hear all that account
which Rasikendra-chudamani related.[^30]

**77.** "The greatness of Vrindavana cannot be expressed —
[that Vrindavana] on which Devendra and all the other gods meditate.

**78.** In Vrindavana the trees are in flowers and [young] leaves;
the trees are adorned with flowers, [there are] various birds.

**79.** In swarms the bees come, maddened;
all of them, circling, [sing] with a resounding hum.[^31]

**80.** Around Vrindavana is the lovely Kalindi;
[her] water is very cool, the waves are beautiful.

**81.** In Vrindavana trees, creepers, various flowers shine;
the trees adorned with flowers enchant the gods.

**82.** In that abode the moon and the sun rise at the same time;
kindled by [their] radiance, that place always shines.[^32]

**83.** Lotuses — kamala, utpala, kahlara;
the ground everywhere is adorned with pollen.[^33]

**84.** All the forest creatures always play there;
various deer always serve there.

**85.** There are no enemies or friends [there] — all play in bliss,
always beholding the lotus feet of Radha and Krishna.

**86.** In it are places where the twelve forests shine,
and also eighteen upavanas, as [the shastras] attest.[^34]

**87.** The beautiful forests shine, full of great beauty;
their beauty, surpassing Vaikuntha, cannot be described.

**88.** The ground, [made] of jewels — chintamani — is beautiful;
that world always shines [like] hosts of suns.[^35]

**89.** Meditate on such wish-fulfilling trees in Vrindavana:
pendants of gems hang [from them] — they cannot be described.[^36]

**90.** Those trees constantly shower various jewels;
by the rays of the jewels the place is full of splendour.

**91.** On all sides that place is inlaid with rubies;
in its midst is a beautiful pavilion of gems.

**92.** The pavilion is built, adorned with various jewels;
the place is all full of radiance — it has no comparison.

**93.** All the paintings of the pavilion shine [like] the moon;
the place is beautiful to behold, full of fragrance.[^37]

**94.** On all sides hang chamaras [and] jewelled pendants;
on the arches hang various gems in clusters.[^38]

**95.** The altar, adorned with rubies, shines brightly;
[its] light is ever new, [it] is skilfully built to behold.[^39]

**96.** On all sides strings of various pearls sway;
rubies blaze, surpassing millions and millions of suns.

**97.** The temple is adorned with various gems and rubies;
a single ruby [there] holds the radiance of a million suns.

**98.** The pavilion is adorned with an eight-petalled lotus;
by the rays of the gems the place blazes with bright radiance.

**99.** Four doors grace that beautiful temple;
eight panels of gems and rubies are enchanting.[^40]

**100.** How many, how many jewelled lamps burn there!
They always illumine the circle of the rasa.[^41]

**101.** In its midst is a wish-fulfilling tree, beautiful to behold;
a jewelled palace, a jewelled altar are most enchanting.[^42]

**102.** On all sides in that place there is a rain of jewels;
the six seasons abide in that forest without cease.[^43]

**103.** Nectar always rains down in the midst of Vrindavana;
thus the wish-fulfilling tree always abides [there] in splendour.

**104.** The leaves of the wish-fulfilling tree are in rapturous delight;
coral stalks all shine beside it.[^44]

**105.** The wish-fulfilling tree is adorned with jewelled shoots;
gems, pearls [and] various jewels shine.

**106.** [Its] fruits are padmaraga rubies, in clusters;
various gems and rubies hang down in streams.[^45]

**107.** All the gems and rubies are dazzling to behold;
that best of trees takes away the heat of samsara.

**108.** The shade of that tree [brings] blessing to all;
the wondrous best of trees takes away the threefold heat.[^46]

**109.** At the root of such a tree shines a jewelled palace;
in its midst is a sweet jewelled throne.

**110.** The radiance of millions and millions of suns, brightly shining,
the eight-petalled throne is made of gems.

**111.** In such a Vrindavana, on the throne beneath the wish-fulfilling tree,
always behold Krishna, meditate [on Him].[^47]

**112.** Krishna wearing yellow garments, Radha-jiu at [His] left;
both hands are rosy, the feet are red.[^48]

**113.** Both eyes are tinged with red, the lips rosy;
on [His] chest [He] wears the Kaustubha gem [and] various jewels.[^49]

**114.** On the neck are a double string of pearls, a necklace of various jewels;
the ornaments of His body are set with various jewels.

**115.** The crown sparkles with various jewels and rubies;
on the neck a necklace shines, flashing [like] lightning.

**116.** Earrings, armlets, bracelets, little bells shine;
the form glitters and shimmers, the glance is sweet.[^50]

**117.** On the heart is the mark of Shrivatsa, most enchanting;
the two lotus feet are beautiful to behold.[^51]

**118.** On both feet shine anklets of gems;
a supremely sweet sound — [they] sing the panchama.[^52]

**119.** That sound steals the minds of the three worlds [when they] hear it:
so sweetly [the anklets] ring, resting on the feet.

**120.** Gorochana [and] kunkuma shine very brightly;
on the forehead shines a tilaka like a bee.[^53]

**121.** On the beautiful, sweet face is a sweet smile;
revealing [His] glance, [He] has conquered the three worlds.

**122.** Enchanting, surpassing millions and millions of Kandarpas,
the form of supreme loveliness is beautiful to behold.

**123.** On the beautiful belly shine three folds, as [befits];
the waist surpasses a lion's, the thighs [like plantain stems] are most shapely.[^54]

**124.** In such a form Krishna, taking the flute in His hand
and putting the flute to His lips, began to play.

**125.** In what divine ragas all began to sing
[in] Vrindavana, on the sandy bank of the Yamuna, beneath the trees![^55]

**126.** In the middle are Radha and Krishna, on all sides the gopis;
thousands and thousands of groups — who can count [them]?[^56]

**127.** As the filaments of a lotus are surrounded by the petals,
so the gopis surround Radha and Krishna on all sides.[^57]

**128.** The form that enchants the three worlds is beyond the three worlds;
according to your own nature contemplate [Him] without cease.[^58]

**129.** If you worship, you will attain Radha and Krishna, the lords of [your] life;
worship [Them] firmly, O raja, with firm resolve."

**130.** [Citing] the testimony of various tantras [and] various shastras,
he set forth the truth of the Vedas, explaining [it].[^59]

**131.** Hearing Rasika's words, the three brothers
firmly and unceasingly worshipped Krishna.

**132.** Who can describe the boundless ocean of [his] lilas?
In brief I have proclaimed something.[^60]

**133.** This is proclaimed in the Dakshina-vibhaga;
all of you, listen attentively, without disputing.[^61]

**134.** The Rasika-mangala is supremely sweet:
hearing [it], all living beings, cross over the [age of] Kali.[^62]

**135.** Adorning himself with Shyamananda's feet,
the son of Rasamaya joyfully composed [this].

*Thus in the Dakshina-vibhaga of the Sri Rasika-mangala ends the fifteenth lahari, called "The Instruction to Maharaja Vaidyanatha Bhanja on Meditation on Vrindavana and on Worship."*[^63]

[^1]: The lines before the raga are the heading of the lahari ("পঞ্চদশ-লহরী"), in italics without a number. Lahari 15 begins at the top of pdf 124 (printed p. 86; the page number is not printed), after the colophon of lahari 14 on pdf 123 (files RM-2-14-001-068). Raga Kaushika; the ghosha is the same as in lahari 13 (note 1 there): Rama and Krishna are Balarama and Krishna; Murari is a name of Krishna, which the author also applies to Rasika-Murari. There is no mangala-shloka: after the raga and ghosha the song begins with ॥ 1 ॥. The title follows the colophon on pdf 128, after ॥ 135 ॥ (checked against the scan): "ইতি শ্রীরসিকমঙ্গল-দক্ষিণবিভাগে মহারাজা বৈদ্যনাথ ভঞ্জের প্রতি বৃন্দাবন-ধ্যান-ভজনোপদেশ-নাম পঞ্চদশ-লহরী সম্পূর্ণা" (*mahārājā baidyanātha bhañjera prati bṛndābana-dhyāna-bhajanopadeśa*). The summary line covers the whole lahari (94–135 from the OCR and the scans of pdf 127–128, provisionally).
[^2]: "Cowherd lineages" — *gopabaṃśa-samūha*; "the moon of the family" — *kulacandra*. According to tradition, Shyamananda came from the Sadgopa ("good cowherd") caste.
[^3]: Bhattacharya — lahari 13, note 6. "Ignorance" (3) — *abidyā*; "entered the shelter" — *paśilā śaraṇa*. "Anxiety of the mind" (4) — *manera udbega*.
[^4]: "A moment" — *laba* (lava, the smallest unit of time). Cf. CC, Madhya 22: "'Association with sadhus, association with sadhus' — [so] say all the shastras: even by a moment's association with a sadhu every perfection is attained." "The sin that binds one to births" — *janmabandha-pāpa*.
[^5]: "The holy places of the gods" — *debatīrtha*. Cf. Bhagavatam 1.13.10: great devotees by their presence make the holy places holy.
[^6]: "For the good of the sadhus" — *sādhujana-hite*. Cf. Bhagavatam 9.4.68: "The sadhus are My heart, and I am the heart of the sadhus."
[^7]: "The rod of Yama" — *yamadaṇḍa*. "Now rising, now falling" — *uṭhu paṛu haññā*; "perishes" — *mare*.
[^8]: "In this round of worldly existence" — the scan has *prabhabasaṃsāre*; the 1997 edition (OCR) has *e bhabasaṃsāre*; the latter is adopted.
[^9]: 15–19 — cf. Bhagavatam 2.3.18–19: "Do the trees not live? Do the bellows not breathe? … A man praised by those like dogs, hogs, camels and asses is [himself] an animal," if he does not hear of the Lord. "Lives" — *jīẏe* (the scan has "জীএে"). "A blacksmith's bellows" — *kāmārera yā̃tā*. "Impure" (16) — *amedhya*.
[^10]: "Twice six qualities" — *dbiṣaṛ guṇa*: the twelve virtues of a brahmana (truthfulness, self-control, austerity and others). A shvapacha, "dog-eater," is a person of the lowest station.
[^11]: Bhagavatam 7.9.10 (the prayer of Prahlada); checked against the scan. "The Lotus-naveled" — *aravinda-nābha*, Vishnu. Shlokas 21, 48 and 49 have their own numbers in the edition's common count of couplets. Below the shloka (and below 48–49) the edition gives its Bengali prose translation (*ślokārtha*) — the editor's apparatus, not translated.
[^12]: Cf. Bhagavatam 4.31.14: as watering the root of a tree nourishes the trunk, branches and leaves, so the worship of Achyuta satisfies all. According to Gaudiya Vaishnava teaching the gods are Krishna's servants (22), and the worship of Krishna includes respect for them (lahari 13, note 4).
[^13]: "False" — *asatya*: transient when not related to Krishna (lahari 13, note 5). "Remaining amid all this" (29) — *e sabāte thāki'*: without leaving home and family (cf. lahari 14, 37). "Abundantly" (28) — *atireke*.
[^14]: "Kshatriyas" — *kṣetrī* (= *kṣatriẏa*). "Subjects" — *prajāgaṇa*.
[^15]: The sixty-four kinds of service (*catuḥṣaṣṭi bhakti*) — the list of sadhana-bhakti in the Bhakti-rasamrita-sindhu (1.2) and CC, Madhya 22. The son of Achyuta (*acyuta-nandana*; 40 *acyuta-tanaẏa*, 67 *acyuta-kumāra*) is Rasika (lahari 12, note 24).
[^16]: Rasika-shekhara — lahari 12, note 40. "Alms" — *bhikṣā*. "Considering [us] servants" (39) — *sebaka-giẏāne* (*giẏāna* = *jñāna*). "I have offered everything" (39) — *saba samarpinu mui*: apparently the king speaks for the three.
[^17]: "[The giving up of] the killing of living beings" — *jībahatyā bhikṣā*, lit. "alms — the killing of living beings": Rasika asks that the killing of animals cease, above all sacrifices (45); at the beginning of lahari 16 (by the OCR) it is said that in the land of the Bhanjas the Shaivas and Shaktas gave up the killing of living beings. Vaishnavas do no violence to living beings and do not eat meat.
[^18]: Raurava, Maharaurava (52) — hellish worlds to which those who do violence to living beings go (Bhagavatam 5.26.10–12).
[^19]: 43–45 — the eight participants in killing; cf. shloka 48 and the Manu-smriti (5.51), which lists the same eight. "Gives consent" — *anumāna kare* (cf. *anumantā*, 48). "The ruler of that village" — *grāmera adhipati* (cf. *adhiṣṭhātā*, 48). "Sells" (44) — *bice* (= *bece*). "The lowest of brahmanas" (45) — *dbijādhama*.
[^20]: "Kill" — the scan has *baddha kare* ("বদ্ধ"; so also the 1997 edition, by the OCR); understood as *badha kare* ("kills"). "Bereft of sense" — *haiẏā acetana*.
[^21]: The source is given by the edition as the Padma Purana; not verified. The first half is close to the Manu-smriti (5.51); on years in hell by the number of the animal's hairs cf. the Manu-smriti (5.38). "Overseer" — *adhiṣṭhātā*; "the one who prepares" — *saṃskartā*; "the one who serves" — *upahartā*. "Contrary to the injunctions" (49) — *avidhinā*.
[^22]: "The destroyer of his own soul" — *ātmaghātī*: one who, having obtained a human birth, does not worship the Lord and so destroys himself (cf. Bhagavatam 11.20.17).
[^23]: "In a subtle form" — *sūkṣmarūpe*: the Paramatma, who dwells in the heart of every being (cf. Bhagavad-gita 18.61). "In His own form" — *sbarūpe*.
[^24]: "Kin" — *jana*; the mark at this word refers to the editor's note: the variant reading "প্রাণ" (*prāṇa*, "life"). "Enter" — *paśa*.
[^25]: Couplet 58 repeats the second line of 62 and the first line of 63 (so in the scan and in the 1997 edition); apparently a copying slip. Translated as it stands; the bhattacharyas' speech is in 63–68.
[^26]: "Wicked" — *duṣṭa*. "Whose vow is to crush the asuras" — *asuradalanabānā* (the 1997 edition, by the OCR, has *anuradalanabāna*); *bānā* is understood as "vow, habit" (Bengali বানা); the reading and sense are unclear.
[^27]: "Secret [hidden] in the Vedas" — *beda-gopya kathā*. "An associate of Krishna" — *kṛṣṇapāriṣada*: the words of the panditas; on the position of Rasika and Shyamananda see Purva, lahari 1, note 2; Purva, lahari 14, note 11 (cf. lahari 12, note 19).
[^28]: "Received instruction in the Krishna-mantra" — *kṛṣṇamantre upadeśa*: initiation (Dakshina, lahari 1, note 8; lahari 13, 4). "According to the nigama" — *nigamete*: according to the Vedas.
[^29]: "One's own mood" — *nijabhāba*: the sense is approximate — the "intimate" or "true" mood (possibly Rasika's own mood). The cowherd men and gopis of Vraja attained Krishna in the moods of sakhya, vatsalya and madhurya; according to Gaudiya Vaishnava teaching this is the path of raganuga-bhakti, following the residents of Vraja (CC, Madhya 22). "Bhagavan Himself" (74) — *sbaẏaṃ bhagabān*; cf. Bhagavatam 1.3.28. Madhurya is conjugal love, the mood of the gopis. "Meditate" — *dhiẏāna* (= *dhyāna*).
[^30]: "Related" — *karila bikhyātā*, lit. "made known." Chudamani, "the jewel on the crest": Rasikendra-chudamani is an epithet of Rasika.
[^31]: "With a resounding hum" — *mukharaba-dhbani*. "[Sing]" — the scan has *pāẏena* ("obtain, find"); apparently a misprint for *gāẏena* ("sing"); the reading is unclear.
[^32]: "At the same time" — *ekakāle*. "Kindled by [their] radiance" — *teje uddīpana*.
[^33]: Kamala — lotus; utpala — blue water lily; kahlara — white water lily. "Pollen" — *parāgadhūli*.
[^34]: The twelve forests of Vraja (Madhuvana, Talavana, Kumudavana and others); upavanas are lesser groves. Usually twenty-four upavanas are named; here eighteen (*aṣṭādaśa*; so in the scan). "As [the shastras] attest" — *paramāṇa* (*pramāṇa*); it can also be understood as "in number."
[^35]: "The ground of jewels — chintamani" — *ratnamaẏa bhūmi-cintāmaṇi*; cf. the Brahma-samhita 5.29, 5.56 (ground of chintamani, wish-fulfilling trees). "Hosts of suns" — *sūryya yūtha yūtha*.
[^36]: Wish-fulfilling tree — *kalpataru*. "Pendants of gems" — *maṇimaẏa jhārā*: hanging clusters, fringes. "Cannot be described" — *nā yāẏa bākhāna* (*bākhāna* = *byākhyāna*).
[^37]: "Shine [like] the moon" — *candrake ujjbala*; it can also be understood as "brighter than the moon." "Fragrance" — *parimala*. The description of Vrindavana continues on pdf 127 (from ॥ 94 ॥).
[^38]: The description of Vrindavana in Rasika's speech continues from 77 (RM-2-15-001-093). Chamaras are whisks of yak tails. "Pendants" — *jhārā* (cf. 89, note 36; 106). "Arches" — *toraṇa*, decorated gateways. "In clusters" — *kerā kerā* (so in the scan; cf. 106, of the fruits): the meaning is unclear, understood as "in clusters, in bunches".
[^39]: "Altar" — *bedī*, a raised platform. "Skilfully built" — *subandhāna*, "well-formed, shapely" (cf. 123).
[^40]: "Four doors" — *caturtha duẏāra*, lit. "the fourth door" (so in the scan and in the 1997 edition); understood as "four doors", each with two panels, eight in all.
[^41]: "The circle of the rasa" — *rāsa-maṇḍalī*: the place of the rasa dance.
[^42]: "In its midst" — *tā'ra madhye*: in the midst of the temple or of the rasa circle. On the wish-fulfilling trees and the ground of chintamani see note 35.
[^43]: "The six seasons" — *ṣaṛ-ṛtu*: in Vrindavana all the seasons abide at once.
[^44]: "Stalks" — *dāṇḍi*, "stems, stalks".
[^45]: Padmaraga is the "lotus-coloured" ruby. "In clusters" — *kerā kerā* (note 38). "In streams" — *jhārā jhārā*.
[^46]: The threefold heat (*tribidha tāpa*) is the three kinds of suffering: from one's own body and mind, from other living beings, and from the forces of nature.
[^47]: "Behold" — *dekhaha*, lit. "look"; "meditate" — *karaha dhiẏāne* (*dhyāna*). The raja is addressed in the singular, as in 75 and 89.
[^48]: "Radha-jiu" — *rādhājīu*: the respectful "Radha-ji". "Rosy" — *aruṇima*; "red" — *rātula*.
[^49]: "Tinged with red" — *rātula*. Kaustubha is the jewel on the chest of Vishnu-Krishna. The scan has "কোউস্তভ".
[^50]: "Armlets" — *keẏūra*; "little bells" — *kiṅkiṇī*, bells on the girdle.
[^51]: Shrivatsa is the auspicious mark (a curl) on the chest of Vishnu-Krishna.
[^52]: "Anklets" — *mañjira*. Panchama (*pañcama*) is the fifth note of the Indian scale, traditionally the voice of the cuckoo.
[^53]: Gorochana is a bright yellow pigment (Purva, lahari 6, note 8); kunkuma is a red powder. "Like a bee" — *ali paramāṇa* (checked against the scan): *ali* is a bee, *paramāṇa* (= *pramāṇa*) "measure, likeness"; understood as comparing the tilaka to a bee (in shape or colour); the sense is approximate.
[^54]: "Three folds" — *tribalī* (Purva, lahari 16, note 31). "As [befits]" — *pramāṇa*, "in due measure". "Thighs" — *rambhā*, "plantain": thighs like plantain stems (cf. lahari 12, 26). "Shapely" — *subandhāna* (cf. 95).
[^55]: "All began to sing" — *saba gāite lāgilā*: at the sound of the flute everything around sings; it may also be understood as "[He] began to sing of everything". "Sandy bank" — *pulina* (lahari 3, note 18). "Beneath the trees" — *tarutalā*.
[^56]: "Groups" — *yūtha*: bands of gopis, each with its own leader.
[^57]: The number "॥ ১২৭" is printed without the closing "॥" (so in the scan). "The filaments … surrounded by the petals" — *padmera keśara dala yena-i beṣṭita*.
[^58]: "Beyond the three worlds" — *trailokyera para*. "According to your own nature … contemplate" — *āpanāra sbabhābete bhāba*: it may also be understood as "meditate in your own mood (bhava)"; cf. 73 (*āpanāra nijabhāba*, note 29). Rasika's speech, begun in 77, ends in 129.
[^59]: "Testimony" — *pramāṇa*. "Tantras" — *tantra*: here, sacred scriptures (agamas, pancharatra).
[^60]: "The boundless ocean of [his] lilas" — *apāra samudra-līlā*; it may also be understood as "lilas boundless [as] the ocean". The author's words.
[^61]: "Is proclaimed" — *karilā pracāra* (a third-person form; in sense, the author's words). "Without disputing" — *nā kara bicāra*: without doubting.
[^62]: "Cross over the [age of] Kali" — *tara kalikāla*.
