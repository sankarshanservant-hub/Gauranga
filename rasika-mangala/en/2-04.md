# Dakshina-vibhaga. Lahari 4. The Meeting of Rasamaya and Tulasi

*A song in glory of Shyamananda; Shyamananda asks Rasika for alms — to deliver all living beings by giving everyone the sixteen names "Hare Krishna" and prema-bhakti, from the raja to the chandala — and Damodara receives the same command; Rasika begins to accept disciples — the first ten are named — and kirtana and the service of Vaishnavas spread throughout Utkala; Rasika goes first to Dharenda, ruled by the impious Bhima Shirikara, who sacrifices animals and mocks the Vaishnavas; Rasika stays with Bhima's grandsons Rasamaya and Vamshi, early Shyamanandis and disciples of Damodara, who receive him with honour, drink the water from his feet and beg mercy for Bhima and his brother; during the meal the young Tulasidasa, son of Gopaladasa, a disciple of Hridayananda, sings a song about Krishna, and Rasika listens to it in ecstasy all night; from that day Rasika takes Rasamaya's family, together with Tulasi, into his company*

*The fourth lahari.*

*Raga Mallar.*[^1]
*Ghosha: "Crusher of the Daityas, enemy of the Daityas."*

**1.** Glory, glory to Shyamananda! I bow to [his] holy feet.
Be merciful: [let] me describe [your] glory.

**2.** Thus day by day Rasika [and] Sri Shyamananda-raya
awakened bhakti.[^2]

**3.** One day Shyamananda says to Rasika:
"Give me one alms with joy in your heart.

**4.** This is the alms: deliver all living beings,
give everyone the sixteen names 'Hare Krishna.'[^3]

**5.** Brahmanas, kshatriyas, vaishyas, shudras — as many people as there are,
chandalas, pukkashas, Hunas — as many people as there are —

**6.** to all of them give prema-bhakti for Krishna:
this is the alms I beg of you without fail.[^4]

**7.** Be it a raja, be it a subject, be it a sadhu,
be it a child, be it an old man, be it women —

**8.** to all of them you yourself will go constantly
[and] make them take up the name of Hari in every house."

**9.** Hearing Shyamananda's words, Rasika-shekhara
bowed down and rose, joining his palms.

**10.** After some time Shyamananda-raya [left] that place;
to deliver the living beings he wanders as he pleases.[^5]

**11.** To Damodara he diligently gave the same command:
"Quickly make all living beings hear the name of Hari."[^6]

**12.** From then on the son of Achyuta accepts disciples;
to all he gave the treasure of prema-bhakti for Krishna.

**13.** Day by day bhakti spread;
the whole world became filled with love for Krishna.

**14.** Brahmanas, kshatriyas, vaishyas, shudras or other people —
by Rasika's touch they become exclusively surrendered [to Krishna].

**15.** As iron, touching the philosopher's stone, becomes gold,
so by Rasika's touch all people became Krishna's.[^7]

**16.** The whole world became filled with prema-bhakti:
in Utkala the moon — Rasika — rose.

**17.** He began sankirtana in every house,
and everywhere established the service of Vaishnavas.

**18.** The first disciples — Kalandi, Bhaktadasa the yavana,
then Shyamagopala, humble Shyamanarayana,

**19.** then Ramakrishna, Paramananda, Bhudhara,
Goura-gopala, Gopinatha, Sri Gokula.

**20.** At first these ten people became disciples;
how many became disciples after that — who can count?[^8]

**21.** Briefly I shall tell something of this —
in which village what pastime the son of Achyuta performs.

**22.** There is a holy place called Dharenda;
to that village Rasika set out first.

**23.** A most captivating place, beautiful to see;
the lord of that village is Bhima Shirikara.

**24.** Both have great wealth, [they are] great men,
the family of the pure gopa caste, highly esteemed.[^9]

**25.** They worship various gods and goddesses, installing [them],
and slaughter goats and buffaloes beyond count.[^10]

**26.** Bereft of sense, they kill various living beings;
they do not know who He is that is called Krishna.

**27.** On seeing Vaishnavas, they mock:
"Unable to support your family, you have left home!

**28.** Since you all cannot fill your bellies,
I shall give you all food — do this service [for me]."[^11]

**29.** Seeing sadhus, they mock in various ways;
the sadhus cannot say [anything] — they call on Krishna as witness.

**30.** Most perverse, wicked Bhima Shirikara;
the subjects [and] the sadhus constantly fear [him].

**31.** Giving the raja bribes of thousands upon thousands of rupees,
vying [with one another], they slaughter goats and buffaloes in frenzy.[^12]

**32.** They do not listen to kirtana, do not take the name of Hari;
apart from wicked deeds, they have no other business.

**33.** Whether Ajamila or Jagai and Madhai —
these two brothers are greater asuras than they.[^13]

**34.** From the womb of Bhima's daughter were born
three brothers — Sri Rasamaya, Vamshi [and] Mathura.

**35.** He became renowned as one of the first Shyamanandis;
with their families they lived in that village.[^14]

**36.** Earlier they had received instruction from Damodara:
the two brothers became true Vaishnavas.[^15]

**37.** At their house Rasika-shekhara arrived,
[coming] quickly, knowing [them] to be his own dear servants.

**38.** Seeing [him], Rasamaya [and] Vamshi, filled with joy,
fell to the ground with their bodies like sticks, rolling at [his] feet.

**39.** Preparing an excellent seat, they seat Rasika,
and joyfully wash [his] feet with scented water.

**40.** With their whole family they drank the water of [his] holy feet;
with their whole family they deemed that today their birth had borne fruit.

**41.** Most fortunate are Vamshi [and] Rasamayadasa:
Prabhu, seating [them] beside [him], asked about everything.[^16]

**42.** "Shyamananda firmly commanded me:
'Awaken prema-bhakti in Utkala.'

**43.** Taking that command on my head, I set out,
to make [people] give up wicked deeds and make [them] virtuous.[^17]

**44.** I have heard that Bhima Shirikara is a great asura;
how can all these wicked ones become Vaishnavas?

**45.** If these wicked ones become Vaishnavas, it will be a great deed:
seeing [them], the whole kingdom will become Vaishnava."

**46.** Then Rasamaya [and] Vamshi tell everything:
"Both are the greatest of asuras, famous in [all] the world.

**47.** If you show mercy to them,
then they will have prema-bhakti for Krishna.

**48.** Whomever you diligently favour,
however wicked he may be, will become a sadhu."

**49.** Vamshi [and] Rasamaya know Rasika's greatness;
they understood: these two will surely become sadhus.

**50.** In Rasamaya's house there is full contentment in everything;
they feed the best of the twice-born [with dishes] of six tastes.[^18]

**51.** Milk, curd, ghee, excellent shali rice,
fried dishes [and] sweets they offered [to Krishna] as bhoga.[^19]

**52.** Forming a circle for the meal, Rasika sat down
[and] began to honour prasada together with the Vaishnavas.

**53.** At that time, with [his] father, Sri Tulasidasa
was living together with Rasamaya [and] Vamshi.

**54.** Rasamaya's eldest son, with Tulasi,
from childhood lived in inseparable union.[^20]

**55.** At first [he was] a youth, beautiful to see:
Tulasi sings like a sweet-voiced cuckoo.[^21]

**56.** At that time he entered the place
where Rasika-chandra was taking his meal.

**57.** Joyfully, fearing no one, he began to sing:
"Not only is Kanu dark — he is also bent in three places."[^22]

**58.** [A song] surpassing the cuckoo, most captivating to the ear —
hearing the song, Rasika's heart breaks.

**59.** All his clothes were soaked with the tears from [his] eyes;
Rasikendra floated on the waves of prema.

**60.** Fondly taking [the youth], he seated [him] beside [him],
and again and again especially had [him] sing this pada.

**61.** From evening they sat to perform bhajana;
where the night went — [they did not notice], singing this pada.[^23]

**62.** His heart overwhelmed by feeling, the weeping does not stop;
seeing [his] ecstasy, all are astonished.

**63.** Composing himself for a moment, he asks: "Whose son is this?"
Rasamaya told everything as it was:

**64.** "A disciple of Hridayananda, living on the Ganga —
father and son — manifested kirtana here.

**65.** Thakura Gopaladasa is a great mahajana,
a disciple of Subala, devoted to the name of Hari.[^24]

**66.** Seeing [their] sankirtana, Shyamananda-raya
carefully kept father and son here."

**67.** Hearing [this], Rasika, joyful, in love for Krishna, [said]:
"Surely this family will wander with me."[^25]

**68.** From that day Rasamaya's family, together with Tulasi,
Rasika joyfully took into his company.

**69.** I have told something of the first jubilation;
listen to the Rasika-mangala, all sadhus.[^26]

**70.** Adorning himself with Shyamananda's feet,
the son of Rasamaya joyfully composed [this].

*Thus ends the fourth lahari in the Dakshina-vibhaga of the Sri Rasika-mangala, called "The Meeting of Rasamaya and Tulasi."*[^27]

[^1]: The lahari's heading ("চতুর্থলহরী") is in italics without a number. Lahari 4 begins on the lower half of pdf 100, after the colophon of lahari 3. Raga Mallar (*mollāra*, so in the scan; garbled in the OCR). The ghosha (refrain) consists of names of Krishna: "Crusher of the Daityas" (*daitya-dalana*), "Enemy of the Daityas" (*daityāri*). Couplet 1 is marked "ধ্রু" (*dhru*, *dhruvā* — "the fixed [verse]," the song's refrain). There is no mangala-shloka: after the raga and ghosha the song begins with ॥ 1 ॥. The title follows the colophon on pdf 102 (checked against the scan): "ইতি শ্রীরসিকমঙ্গল দক্ষিণ-বিভাগে রসময়-তুলসী-মিলন-নাম চতুর্থ-লহরী সম্পূর্ণা" (*rasamaya-tulasī-milana*).
[^2]: "Awakened bhakti" — *bhaktira udaya karilena*, lit. "brought about the rising of bhakti." The subject is twofold: Rasika and Shyamananda.
[^3]: "Alms" — *bhikṣā*: Shyamananda, like a beggar, asks his disciple for the gift of delivering all living beings. The sixteen names are the maha-mantra "Hare Krishna, Hare Krishna, Krishna Krishna, Hare Hare / Hare Rama, Hare Rama, Rama Rama, Hare Hare."
[^4]: Chandalas and pukkashas are the lowest castes; the Hunas are foreign "barbarians." Cf. Bhagavatam 2.4.18: *kirāta-hūṇāndhra-pulinda-pulkaśāḥ… śudhyanti*, "the Kiratas, Hunas, Andhras, Pulindas, Pulkashas… are purified" by taking shelter of the Lord's devotees. "Without fail" — *nidāna*, "in the end, by all means."
[^5]: "As he pleases" — *āpanā līlāya*, lit. "by his own play."
[^6]: Damodara is Rasika's disciple (Dakshina, lahari 1, "The Deliverance of Damodara"). The command was apparently given by Shyamananda (the subject of 10); it can also be understood that Rasika gave Damodara the command. "Diligently" — *yugate*, following the edition's gloss ("যত্ন সহকারে"); cf. Dakshina, lahari 1, note 25.
[^7]: The philosopher's stone — *paraśa* (*sparśa-maṇi*), which turns iron into gold. "Krishna's" — *kārṣṇa* (so in the scan; garbled in the OCR); cf. *kārṣṇa-jana* (Dakshina, lahari 1, note 40).
[^8]: The division of the list into ten names is not obvious; understood thus: Kalandi (*kālandī*), Bhaktadasa the yavana, Shyamagopala, Shyamanarayana, Ramakrishna, Paramananda, Bhudhara, Goura-gopala, Gopinatha, Gokula; "humble" (*dīna*) is taken as an epithet. If Dina and Goura are separate names, there are twelve disciples. A yavana (*yabana*) is a Muslim or other non-Hindu by birth: Rasika accepts him too as a disciple. Shyamagopala, Ramakrishna, Bhudhara and Gokula are also named among the associates in the Purva list (lahari 1, 81, 88, 110, 131–132; there also "Dinashyama"). The persons are not identified.
[^9]: Dharenda is a village in Utkala, Shyamananda's birthplace (Purva, lahari 2, notes 6, 29). Shirikara is apparently a surname (Purva, lahari 1, note 39). "Both" — *dohāra* (so in the scan): apparently Bhima and his brother, whom the author does not name (cf. 33, 46, 49: "these two"). Shyamananda (Purva, lahari 1, note 9) and the author's family (Purva, lahari 1, 139) were also of the gopa caste. "Highly esteemed" — *baṛai calana*, "of great standing."
[^10]: "Goats" — *bodā* (cf. Oriya *boda*, "he-goat"): blood sacrifices to gods and goddesses. "Installing" — *sthāpanā*, i.e. setting up images of the gods for worship.
[^11]: Mockery of the poor Vaishnavas: "do this service" — i.e. work for me for your keep.
[^12]: "Bribes" — *nāca diyā*, following the edition's gloss ("উৎকোচ দিয়া," "giving a bribe"). "Vying" — *bādābādi*. "Goats and buffaloes" — *bodā-poṛa*: *bodā* is a he-goat (note 10), *poṛa* is taken as *pāṛā*, "young buffalo"; the reading is unclear. The sense is apparently that by bribing the raja they obtain leave for mass sacrifices.
[^13]: Ajamila is the sinner delivered by uttering the name of Narayana (Bhagavatam 6.1–2); Jagai and Madhai are the villainous brothers delivered by Nityananda and Chaitanya (CB, Madhya 13; CC, Adi 17). "These two brothers" are Bhima Shirikara and his brother (note 9).
[^14]: Rasamaya is the author's father, Vamshi (Vamshi Mathuradasa) his paternal uncle (Purva, lahari 1, 135–137, notes 56–57). Here a third brother, Mathura, is also named; apparently "Vamshi Mathuradasa" in the Purva is two persons. Bhima Shirikara is thus Rasamaya's maternal grandfather. "One of the first Shyamanandis" — *ādya śyāmānandī* (so in the scan): this confirms the reading *ādya* in Purva, lahari 1, 137 (note 57). "He" — *tĩha* (singular): apparently Vamshi (as in the Purva, 137) or Rasamaya.
[^15]: "Instruction" — *upadeśa*, i.e. initiation (cf. Dakshina, lahari 1, note 8). The two brothers are Rasamaya and Vamshi (cf. 38–49). "Vaishnavas" — *boiṣṇaba*, an Oriya form.
[^16]: Prabhu is Rasika (Dakshina, lahari 3, note 11). "Rasamayadasa" is Rasamaya (Purva, lahari 1, note 46). 42–45 are Rasika's words.
[^17]: "Virtuous" — in the scan "সন্থশীল" (*santhaśīla*), likewise in the 1997 ed.; apparently a misprint for *sattvaśīla*. The rhyme (*bāhāra* / *-śīla*) is imperfect.
[^18]: "The best of the twice-born" — *dvijabara*, i.e., apparently, Rasika, the son of Achyuta (or the brahmana guests in general). "Of six tastes" — Dakshina, lahari 3, note 7.
[^19]: Shali is a choice variety of rice. "Offered as bhoga" — *bhoga kaila nibedana*: the food is first offered to Krishna, and Rasika and the Vaishnavas then honour the prasada (52).
[^20]: The line can be understood in two ways. Adopted: "Rasamaya's eldest son" (*rasamaya jyeṣṭha putra*, genitive without ending, as in 50 and 68) was inseparable from Tulasi from childhood; in the Purva list the first of Rasamaya's five sons named is the author himself (Purva, lahari 1, 140), but seniority is not stated there. Alternatively: "Rasamaya, with the eldest son — Tulasi — was inseparable from childhood" (Tulasi being Gopaladasa's eldest son; cf. the lahari's title "The Meeting of Rasamaya and Tulasi"). Tulasidasa is apparently the "guru of sankirtana" to whom the author bows (Purva, lahari 1, 64–66, note 34; the conjecture there that he is Tulasi Thakura, Rasika's uncle, is not borne out here: Tulasidasa is Gopaladasa's son, 64–65).
[^21]: "At first" — *prathame*: apparently of that time — Tulasi was then a youth (*kiśora-mūrti*).
[^22]: Kanu is an endearing name of Krishna. "Bent in three places" — *tinu ṭhā̃i bā̃kā*: Krishna in the tribhanga pose. The words are taken as the opening of the pada Tulasi sings; the pada is not identified.
[^23]: "Bhajana" — here the singing of kirtana. "Where the night went" — *kon dike rātra gela*, lit. "in which direction the night went."
[^24]: Hridayananda (Hridaya Chaitanya) is Shyamananda's guru. "Living on the Ganga" — *gaṅgāte nibāsa*: apparently of Gopaladasa, who came from Bengal, from the banks of the Ganga (Hridayananda lived at Ambika-Kalna on the Ganga). "A disciple of Subala" — apparently in the sense of succession: Subala is Gauridasa Pandita, the guru of Hridayananda (Purva, lahari 1, note 16; Hridayananda is "the son of Subala," Purva, lahari 2, note 12). Father and son are Gopaladasa and Tulasidasa.
[^25]: "Will wander with me" — *biharibe*, lit. "will roam, spend time." "Family" — *goṣṭhī*: Rasamaya's family (68), together with Tulasi.
[^26]: "The first jubilation" — *prathama pramoda* (*pramoda*, "joy, festivity"): apparently the first festival of Rasika's preaching — his arrival in Dharenda (22); cf. at the beginning of lahari 5 (pdf 103): *prathama pramode*.
[^27]: The colophon is on pdf 102 (checked against the scan); on the title see note 1. Lahari 5 begins on pdf 103.
