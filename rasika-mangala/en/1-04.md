# Purva-vibhaga. Lahari 4. Description of the Pastime of Rasika's Birth

*A song in glory of Shyamananda and Rasikananda; the devotees' prayers for Utkala and Rasika's descent; Bhavani's wondrous pregnancy, conjectures and blessings; the birth of Rasikananda on the night of Dipavali in Shaka 1512, the position of the planets and the beauty of the infant; gifts and rites, the women's blessings; the infant weeps on hearing Krishna's name; the rejoicing of the Vaishnavas in Achyuta's house*

*The fourth lahari.*

*Raga Karuna-shri.*[^1]
*Ghosha: "O Hari, this time be merciful to me:*
*I hope to obtain the shade of Your feet."*

**1.** Glory, glory to Shyamananda, the abode of all virtues!
Glory, glory [to him who is] the dear life of Rasika-shekhara![^2]

**2.** Thus in that land Achyuta Mahashaya
lives in royal splendour, fearing no one.

**3.** Constantly with his beloved Bhavani
he passes his time at home in manifold delights.

**4.** Here all the devotees at the lotus feet [of the Lord]
incessantly pray for the deliverance of Utkala:

**5.** "Prabhu, send a devotee and deliver [them]:
this burden of the living beings' suffering is unbearable."

**6.** At the devotees' word Prabhu became merciful:
He sent His own devotee Rasika to earth.

**7.** All Rasika's dear associates, his companions,
took birth in fitting places in Utkala.[^3]

**8.** Hear all this in detail with joy —
how he was born to deliver the living beings.

**9.** At that time, on an auspicious day with every good sign,
Achyuta came together with Bhavani.

**10.** Spending that night together in their chamber,
in joy in Krishna, in manifold rasas, they stayed awake all night.[^4]

**11.** At that very time [he] took his rest in the womb:
by the good fortune of Utkala the abode of virtues appeared.

**12.** Husband and wife, the two of them, and all the household
count the months — one, two.

**13.** Day by day that faithful wife grows more radiant:
bearing Rasika in her womb, she became the mother of the world.

**14.** Seeing this, all the household were astonished:
"From where has Bhavani gained such beauty?"

**15.** The family members say to one another:
"Bhavani's beauty and splendour cannot be described.

**16.** Is it Brahma, is it Shambhu, is it Narayana,
is it Vyasa, Shukadeva, Narada and others,

**17.** is it Parikshit, is it King Janaka —
what great soul has taken birth in the womb?"

**18.** Such various conjectures the household make;
the extraordinary pregnancy cannot be described.

**19.** All the neighbours whisper together:
"Bhavani has taken on a form that enchants the world."

**20.** From mouth to mouth everyone hears —
subjects, kinsmen, Vaishnavas, brahmanas.

**21.** Hearing of the glory of the pregnancy, all the townspeople
came to see with joyful hearts.

**22.** Seeing the pregnancy, all are amazed:
"What mahapurusha has appeared here?"

**23.** All the devotees of Krishna, hearing this, are in an ocean of joy:
"This person will deliver everyone."

**24.** All the astrologer brahmanas give their blessings:
"From this womb will come a chakravarti, a king of the earth."

**25.** Hearing everyone's blessings, Achyuta,
seeing the form of the expectant mother, was struck with wonder.

**26.** Reflecting, Achyuta rejoiced at heart:
"A great mahapurusha is within this womb."

**27.** Thus ten months were counted;
the great month of Kartika arrived.

**28.** The new moon of the Dipa-yatra came;
people come from land after land to see it.[^5]

**29.** That day there was service to the Deity (Thakura) in Achyuta's house
and [in the houses of] all the other officials in the town of Rauni.

**30.** Many Vaishnavas and brahmanas came there;
on all four sides the name of Hari resounds again and again.

**31.** Hundreds upon hundreds of lamps burn, torches, deutis,
chandrodayas of many kinds, festive patis.[^6]

**32.** Darkness went far away, [all became] brilliantly radiant;
all those places became brighter than day.

**33.** At that time the mother of Sri Rasika-deva
herself lets everyone know of her labour pains.

**34.** Hearing this, Achyuta called all the brahmanas
and set in place excellent astrologers and dandatamis.[^7]

**35.** All the brahmanas who know the Vedas recite the Vedas;
on all sides the name of Hari and sankirtana are heard.

**36.** At this time came Rasika's arrival on earth;
the record is the Shaka year 1512,

**37.** the eighteenth day of Tula, the new moon of the dark fortnight:
the new moon waning, the pratipad setting in.[^8]

**38.** The bright pratipad, a Sunday, an auspicious moment,
the third watch of the night, the deepest darkness.[^9]

**39.** The Sun in Svati, in Tula; the Moon in Vishakha, in Tula;
and Mars in Uttara-phalguni, in Kanya.[^10]

**40.** Mercury in Svati, in Tula; Jupiter in Svati, in Tula;
Venus in Hasta, in Kanya: all the benefic planets were gathered.

**41.** Saturn in Ardra, in Mithuna: a most auspicious moment;
Rahu in Pushya, in Karkata: supremely excellent.

**42.** Ketu in Uttara-ashadha: everything excellent;
the lagna Kanya: at an auspicious moment he took birth.

**43.** That mahashaya was endowed with every auspicious mark:
all the signs [on him] were as of a chakravarti king.

**44.** Such a mahapurusha — Rasika mahashaya —
rose to destroy the blinding darkness of Utkala.

**45.** All the devotees of Krishna are in an ocean of joy;
knowing of a devotee's birth, the earth is in boundless joy.

**46.** In heaven the gods pour down a rain of flowers:
"He it is who will uphold all dharma."

**47.** Thus all give their blessings —
Vaishnavas, brahmanas, sannyasis and sadhus.

**48.** On all four sides the Vedas resound without pause:
here the *Bharata*, the *Gita*, there the Puranas,

**49.** here the *Ramayana*, there the study of the Vedas;
only they do not know what a treasure sankirtana is.[^11]

**50.** So that Utkala would take up the dharma of sankirtana,
Rasikendra-chandra took birth on earth.

**51.** Cries of "Hari!", the sound of conches and many instruments resound;
at the festival of Dipavali there is joy in the whole kingdom.

**52.** The world of the gods and the world of men, joined together,
[rejoice] in joy in Krishna in Achyuta's house in manifold ways.

**53.** At this time Rasika took birth;
everyone raises the hulahuli and cries of victory.[^12]

**54.** Come down upon the ground, he cries as all newborns cry;
by the radiance of his body the house shone bright.

**55.** Having given birth, the lady looks at her son's face:
the beautiful moon-like face lights up the darkness.

**56.** Curling locks of hair, a beautiful head;
long cheeks and nose — most charming.

**57.** The pair of brows is like the bow of Kama;
the beauty of the two eyes surpasses lotus petals.

**58.** The two ears, finely shaped, adorn their proper places;[^13]
seeing that beauty, everyone is enchanted.

**59.** The two lips look most tender:
their redness surpasses the bimba fruit.

**60.** Shoulders like an elephant's, very handsome; the neck most beautiful;
the cheeks and the roots of the arms captivate the eye.

**61.** The beauty of the long arms is like a lotus stalk;
on the reddish hands like young shoots, the nails [are like] a garland of kunda flowers.

**62.** Seeing his chest, the three worlds are enchanted;
a beautiful belly, a deep and lovely navel.

**63.** Beautiful upon it are the three folds — the beauty of millions of lions;
the knees and shanks — seeing them, the rama-kadali [is envious].[^14]

**64.** Seeing the marks on the lotus feet, [all] are amazed;
by the radiance of the moon-like nails darkness is dispelled.

**65.** The dark (shyamala) beautiful body, supremely beautiful —
seeing [it], the lady at once fell into a swoon.

**66.** Rising again, she looked at the moon-like face:
at that sight the misery of the bondage of birth was destroyed.

**67.** Seeing her son's beauty, she thinks within herself:
"Is this a king, a chakravarti, or [one of] the gods?

**68.** Such beauty in an infant I have never seen."
Seeing that beauty, millions upon millions of eyes are enchanted.

**69.** The whole account is told in full to Achyuta;
the townspeople tell [the news] to one another.

**70.** Hearing it, Achyuta cannot contain his joy;
he hurried to see his son.

**71.** Having cut the cord and taken the son in her arms,
the midwife (dhai) brought the son and shows him to Achyuta.

**72.** Seeing his son, Achyuta, most fortunate,
[gives] in charity sesame, rice, cloth, gold and cows.

**73.** He called all the brahmanas who know the Vedas
and gave [to them] as befitted his means.

**74.** Giving the Vaishnavas and brahmanas all that was needed,
he satisfied [them], honouring [them] with humility.

**75.** Satisfied, all give their blessings:
"May your son live long!

**76.** This son of yours is a devotee dear to Krishna;
he has taken birth to deliver Utkala.

**77.** He will preach prema-bhakti for Krishna to all" —
so say to one another all the learned devotees.

**78.** Then, when the Vaishnavas and brahmanas had been satisfied,
the women take the son and perform the customs of the family.

**79.** Seeing the son, everyone was immersed in joy;
again and again they pass him from lap to lap.

**80.** The townspeople say: "Bhavani is fortunate:
by the fruit of austerities this son was born from her womb."

**81.** All the elders of the family say: "The rise of the family
this man will bring about — so it seems to us.

**82.** He it is who will look after us all;
because of him everyone will live happily.

**83.** Achyuta's son is endowed with every auspicious mark;
may Krishna always protect this boy!"

**84.** Thus all give their blessings;
in joy the women raise the hulahuli.

**85.** All joyfully cry, "Jaya, jaya!"
Many days passed in this joy.

**86.** The day of the auspicious rite arrived;
messengers were quickly sent to bring all that was needed.

**87.** A great many brahmanas who know the Vedas came,
all the kinsmen, and women in throngs.

**88.** Young women adorned with splendid clothes and ornaments,
brahmanas, kshatriyas, vaishyas, shudras and all the other castes —

**89.** all came to see Achyuta's son;
cries of "Hari!" and the hulahuli sound again and again.

**90.** All the observances prescribed by the rules
the women, gathering together, performed according to family custom.

**91.** Installing Shashthi in the chamber of the sixth day (chhati-ghara),
all worshipped with many kinds of offerings.[^15]

**92.** The whole family, bowing, ask for a blessing:
"May my [dear] son of Achyuta live long!"

**93.** Then the lady Bhavani, taking her son on her lap,
having completed all the auspicious rites, at once sat down.

**94.** Taking in their hands turmeric, rice, durva grass and akshata [grains],
the women, blessing him, place [them] on his head.

**95.** One says: "Mahesha, Parvati, grant a boon:
may this boy live eight hundred years!"

**96.** Another says: "By Shashthi's mercy may [this] son live!"
In many ways the throngs of women give their blessings.

**97.** Yet another says: "Protect [him], Krishna Bhagavan!
Markandeya! Grant him long life!"[^16]

**98.** As soon as he heard the name of Krishna, on his mother's lap
he, in tears and with his hair standing on end, began to cry.

**99.** At whatever woman utters the name of Krishna
he gazes with eyes full of tears.

**100.** Hearing him cry, his mother gives him the breast,
[but] nothing pleases him [when] he hears the name of Krishna.

**101.** As once Prahlada [while still] in his mother's womb
heard the name of Krishna that came from Narada's lips,[^17]

**102.** so [too], when the mother of Rasikendra carried [him] in her womb,
Dayaladasi told the mother about Krishna.[^18]

**103.** Dwelling in the womb, Rasikendra heard [it];
[and now] Achyuta's son is overwhelmed with joy in Krishna.

**104.** Come down upon the ground, he is absorbed in contemplation of the Lord (bhagavata-dhyana);
guru, Krishna and the sadhus are Rasika's wealth and life.

**105.** Keep no doubt at all about this in your mind:
an associate of Krishna has taken birth to deliver the living beings.

**106.** Thus the women cried "Jaya!";
[it was time] for all to take leave and go home.

**107.** Then Bhavani, honouring everyone,
put sindura on [their] heads and kajal on [their] eyes.

**108.** To all she gave splendid fragrant garlands,
adorning [their] bodies with kunkuma and sandal.

**109.** Preparing all kinds of dishes cooked in ghee,
she feeds the women with sweets.

**110.** Then she gave everyone camphor and betel;
taking leave, all went each to her own home.

**111.** On the way all talk together about Rasika:
"This boy is by no means [an ordinary] human being."

**112.** Seeing the boy's beauty, all are enchanted:
seeing his lotus face, the moon is put to shame.

**113.** "The sweetness of that beauty cannot be told;
has not an associate of Krishna been born here?"

**114.** Thus conjecturing in many ways, the young women
went home, leaving their hearts with Rasika.

**115.** Hearing of the auspicious rite, all the brahmanas came;
[Achyuta] gave many gifts and fed [them] with sweets.

**116.** He gave camphor and betel, adorning [their] bodies,
and satisfied the twice-born, giving dakshina and the rest.

**117.** The dust of the twice-born's feet was placed on Rasika's head;
in joy the twice-born give their blessings.

**118.** Having taken leave of the twice-born, the mahashaya
brings all the Vaishnavas there [were] in the town.

**119.** Calling "Hari!", they all came in a throng —
with murali, rabab, venu, shinga, beta and vishana.[^19]

**120.** Bowing to all, [Achyuta] seats [them] on their seats;
with pleasure he feeds everyone with sweets.

**121.** The devotees of Krishna began to sing the name of Krishna —
there was no way to keep Achyuta's son on anyone's lap.[^20]

**122.** He cries as he hears the song of Krishna's qualities;
the midwife, taking [him] in her arms, brought [him] to that place.

**123.** Hearing the name of Krishna, seeing the Vaishnavas feasting,
the [beautiful] moon-faced one, in joy, has his hair standing on end.

**124.** All the devotees, seeing [this], are in an ocean of joy:
"This boy will deliver Utkala."

**125.** Having thus pleased everyone,
the midwife entered the house with the boy.

**126.** All the kinsmen joyfully took their meal;
Achyuta's courtyard became the world of Vaikuntha.

**127.** From that day all people come and go;
the world of the gods and the world of men met in one place.

**128.** From that day his wealth became abundant:
the eight siddhis, the nine treasures (nidhis), [he was] endowed with all virtues.[^21]

**129.** Constantly they accompany Rasika:
they all appeared in Achyuta's house.

**130.** Thus day by day [this] became manifest;
seeing [it], all the people exult with joy.

**131.** [This is] the composition in the Purva-vibhaga on the birth;
hear the Rasika-mangala, all good people.

**132.** Adorning himself with Shyamananda's feet,
the son of Rasamaya joyfully composed [this].

*Thus in the Purva-vibhaga of the Sri Rasika-mangala ends the fourth lahari, called "Description of the Pastime of Rasika's Birth".*

[^1]: Raga Karuna-shri — as in lahari 1 (note 8) and lahari 2 (note 1). The ghosha is the refrain. There are no mangala-shlokas at the beginning of this lahari: after the heading, raga and ghosha the song begins with ॥ 1 ॥.
[^2]: Rasika-shekhara ("crown of rasikas") is Rasikananda (cf. lahari 2, note 3). The compound *rasika-śekhara-priya-prāṇa* may also be understood as "[he] whose dear life is Rasika-shekhara."
[^3]: "Companions" — *sāṅgopāṅga*, literally "with [all] limbs and minor limbs," i.e. Rasikananda's associates and helpers in his future service.
[^4]: "Chamber" — *vāsara* (the bridal chamber). "In joy in Krishna" — *kṛṣṇa-sukhe*; a variant noted in the edition, and the 1997 ed., have *krīḍā-sukhe*, "in the joy of play."
[^5]: Kartika is the month of October–November. The new moon of the Dipa-yatra (*dīpa-yātrā*) is the new moon of Kartika, the festival of lamps (Dipavali, Dipanvita).
[^6]: Deuti and chandrodaya — see lahari 3, note 26. Pati (*paṭī*) — the reading on the scan is clear; apparently festive cloths or canopies.
[^7]: Dandatami (*daṇḍatāmī*) — the reading on the scan is clear; apparently those who count the dandas (divisions of the day of 24 minutes) with a copper water-bowl (*tāmrī*), so as to fix the exact hour of birth for the horoscope; possibly also "tantrikas."
[^8]: Shaka 1512 is 1590 CE. Tula is the solar month corresponding to Kartika. That is, Rasikananda was born in the last night of the dark fortnight of Kartika, the night of Dipavali, as the new moon gave way to the first day of the bright fortnight (pratipad); the description of the date continues in 38.
[^9]: The date from 36–37 continues. The bright pratipad is the first day of the bright fortnight of Kartika (the day after Dipavali, on which Govardhana-puja, Annakuta, is celebrated). The third watch (*prahara*) of the night is roughly from midnight to three o'clock (the night from sunset is divided into four praharas).
[^10]: 39–42 give the positions of the planets at the birth: the nakshatra (lunar mansion) and the sign (rashi). Tula is Libra, Kanya Virgo, Mithuna Gemini; Karkata is Cancer (Bengali *kāṁkaṛā*, "crab," in the text). The lagna is the ascendant. Ketu's sign is not named (Uttara-ashadha falls in Dhanu and Makara). "Gathered" — *melā*, literally "an assembly."
[^11]: The author speaks of his own region — northern Utkala (cf. lahari 3: Mallabhumi, Rohini), where, as he says, the Vedas, the epics and the Puranas were known, but not sankirtana. In Puri the sankirtana of Sri Chaitanya had resounded half a century earlier (CC, Madhya and Antya); according to BR 15, it was Shyamananda and Rasikananda who spread it in Utkala and Medinipur.
[^12]: Hulahuli (*hulahulī*, also ulu-dhvani) is the auspicious trilling cry of Bengali and Oriya women at a birth, a wedding or a puja.
[^13]: "Finely shaped" — *suśaṅka*; the reading on the scan is clear, the 1997 ed. has *gaṭhita*; apparently "well-formed" (possibly from *śaṅkha*, "conch," a figure for the ear).
[^14]: Traditional marks of beauty (cf. the signs of a mahapurusha): shoulders like an elephant's; trivali, the three folds on the belly; "the beauty of lions," a waist slender as a lion's; rama-kadali, a variety of banana whose smooth trunk is a poetic figure for rounded legs.
[^15]: "The auspicious rite" (86) is apparently the rite of the sixth day after birth (*chaṭī*, *ṣaṣṭhī*), when the goddess Shashthi, protectress of children and mothers, is worshipped. "Chamber of the sixth day" — *chaṭī ghara*; the reading on the scan is clear, the sense conjectural (apparently the lying-in room where the rite is performed); the 1997 ed. has *chaṭā*.
[^16]: Markandeya is the sage famed for his long life (Bhag. 12.8–10). The line may also be understood as "grant him Markandeya's span of life"; the exclamation mark follows the edition.
[^17]: Prahlada heard Narada's instructions while in his mother's womb (Bhag. 7.7). "That came from the lips" — *mukha-cyute*; a variant noted in the edition reads *mukhāśrite*, "that dwelt on the lips."
[^18]: Dayaladasi (*dayāla-dāsī*) — apparently a name (it could also be understood as "a kind maidservant"). In lahari 7 (couplet 22) she is called "Dayaladasi Thakurani," a devotee of Chaitanya living in that region at her own temple; she whispered the holy name into the infant's ear, and later Rasikananda recalls that he heard the name from her in childhood.
[^19]: Murali and venu are flutes; the rabab is a stringed instrument; the shinga (*śiṅgā*) is a curved horn; the vishana (*viṣāṇa*) is a horn. "Beta" — *beta-viṣāṇa*, the reading on the scan is clear; possibly a reed (*vetra*) pipe or a horn with a cane; unclear.
[^20]: "The devotees of Krishna" — *kārṣṇa-jana*; read from the scan (a superscript repha over *ṣṇa*); garbled in the OCR and in the 1997 ed. That is, hearing the kirtana, the infant strains out of everyone's arms.
[^21]: The eight siddhis (*aṣṭa-siddhi*) are the mystic perfections of the yogis (anima, laghima, etc.; Bhag. 11.15); the nine nidhis (*nava-nidhi*) are the nine treasures of Kubera, an emblem of inexhaustible wealth. Here they stand for the prosperity that came to the house with Rasika's birth: pure devotees do not seek siddhis (Bhag. 11.14.14). "His" (*tā̃ra*) apparently refers to Achyuta (cf. 129).
