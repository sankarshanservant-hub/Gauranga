# Purva-vibhaga. Lahari 3. Description of the Glory of Rohini

*A song in glory of Shyamananda and Rasikananda; the sinfulness of Utkala and the devotees' prayers to Krishna for the deliverance of living beings; Rohini in Mallabhumi, Rasikananda's native village, its beauty and wealth; Achyuta, Rasikananda's father, and his marriage to Bhavani*

*The third lahari.*

*Raga Kaushika.*[^1]
*Glory, O glory to Rama and Krishna!*
*O Murari, O Murari, O Murari!*

**1.** Glory, glory to Shyamananda, the abode of all virtues!
Be merciful, so that I may sing the glory of your name.

**2.** Listen, listen, everyone, to the Rasika-mangala:
I shall describe the glory of Rasika-deva.

**3.** Who can comprehend [his] supremely wonderful pastimes?
Briefly I shall proclaim only a little.

**4.** I shall make known the book in four vibhagas;
listen, all, attentively and joyfully.[^2]

**5.** For what reason Sri Chaitanya, sending [His] servant,
delivered Utkala by bestowing prema-bhakti —

**6.** of all that I shall tell something in detail;
learned and good people, do not find fault with me.

**7.** In Utkala all the people are firmly set on sin:
they do not take the name of Hari, do not hear of Hari's glory.

**8.** They constantly commit exceedingly wicked deeds,
endlessly revile brahmanas and Vaishnavas.

**9.** Drunk on wine, they do violence
to sannyasis bearing the danda, to Vaishnavas and brahmanas.

**10.** Out of greed for wealth they do violence to sadhus;
this is how they behave in the forest lands.

**11.** Whether king or subjects — all are wicked-minded;
[so are] all the castes, as many as dwell in the land of Utkala.

**12.** All of them, bereft of sense, kill living beings;
vying with one another, everyone slaughters and roasts goats.[^3]

**13.** As many mahatas and others as there are among them —
all are engaged in various kinds of ignorance; it cannot be told.[^4]

**14.** Merely out of greed for paltry goods they do violence to living beings,
they kill hundreds and hundreds of brahmanas, Vaishnavas and sadhus.

**15.** All the goods they bring by doing violence to sadhus
[they spend:] they eat meat, [drink] wine, and give to harlots.

**16.** Setting up [their own objects of worship], they perform various pujas;
they do not hear topics of Hari, do not hear kirtana.

**17.** Hearing sankirtana, all run to beat [the singers]:
"Because of the noise of these [people], Lakshmi leaves the land!"

**18.** Seeing a Vaishnava, they say, "These are thieves!" —
and, driving [them] out of the village, keep [them] in the far wilderness.

**19.** Thus I cannot tell all [their] various sins:
the land of Utkala was seized by great sin.

**20.** Such servants of Krishna as there were among them
at every moment prayed to [His] lotus feet:

**21.** "Prabhu, give good sense to all these living beings;
destroying all sins, give [them] devotion to Krishna."

**22.** Thus all the devotees of Krishna, day and night,
for the sake of the living beings prayed at Krishna's feet:

**23.** "Prabhu, send [Your] servant and deliver [them]:
this burden of the living beings' suffering is unbearable.

**24.** Prabhu, protect these suffering living beings;
bestowing prema-bhakti, preserve them all."

**25.** The Lord, affectionate to His devotees, at the word of the devotees
caused His dear devotee to be born as the son of Achyuta.[^5]

**26.** I shall tell of this in detail — listen, everyone —
how Rasika was born in the land of Utkala.

**27.** In Utkala there is [a region] called Mallabhumi;
in it is the incomparable village of Rohini,

**28.** a village equal to Cuttack — all people know this —
on the bank of the Suvarnarekha, a most holy place.[^6]

**29.** Near the village is a river called the Dolanga;
[its] water is like the water of the Ganga, a true well of sweetness.[^7]

**30.** Near Rohini is the great holy place of Barajit,
where Sita, Rama and Lakshmana rested.[^8]

**31.** [There is] Rameshvara of the twelve lingas, the excellent Shambhu;
the moon of the Raghu dynasty worshipped [him] abundantly.[^9]

**32.** [There] the stream of the Suvarnarekha flows northward;
millions of people come there to take its water.[^10]

**33.** Such a holy river and holy places [lie] on all sides;
all around Rohini people live by hundreds of thousands.

**34.** The place is beautiful to see, most delightful:
dense forests, groves of mango and jackfruit.

**35.** Taba, jamir, lemons, shatakara, kamala,
naranga, pomegranates — on every tree in clusters.[^11]

**36.** Many pandava trees, beautiful to see,
splendid, splendid banana groves, enchanting.[^12]

**37.** All kinds of flowers shine on all sides;
all the gods are enchanted, craving the six tastes.[^13]

**38.** Splendid, splendid betel, splendid, splendid rice;
much grain grows, and the place is enchanting.

**39.** Such a place, a well of sweetness, is beautiful to see:
there are very many ponds, embankments and fields.

**40.** There is a capital, a fort, beautiful to see;
around the fort lies the town of Rauni.[^14]

**41.** Hundreds upon hundreds of Deities and brahmanas dwell there;
[there is] Vedic learning, [and according to] the smriti-shastras, sandhya and tarpana.[^15]

**42.** All joyfully devote themselves to study;
the sound of the Vedas [rises] on all sides incessantly.

**43.** Sannyasis bearing the danda stay there at all times;
all the brahmanas who know the Vedas serve [them].

**44.** The navashakha castes live there, townspeople;
all carry on trade; there is no sorrow or grief.[^16]

**45.** The high platforms are very handsome, the houses splendid, splendid;
at the doors are tulasi-manchas, beautiful to see.[^17]

**46.** Whatever each one's livelihood, all buy and sell;
hundreds of thousands, thousands, hundreds — who can count them?

**47.** The townspeople live in royal splendour;
only one wealth is lacking — krishna-prema-bhakti.

**48.** All the other castes live at a distance;
no one is unhappy, all pass their time in joy.

**49.** I cannot describe the glory of Rauni at all:
is it Navadvipa, Mathura, or the city of the Raghu dynasty?

**50.** As the abode of Sri Krishna is Vaikuntha-dhama,
so the place where a devotee lives is equal to it.

**51.** Wherever devotees abide age after age,
all those places become equal to Vaikuntha.

**52.** Have no doubt at all about this in your minds:
the Vedas and Puranas speak of these signs.

**53.** Therefore I bow to Rauni:
blessed is that place of Rasikachandra's birth.

**54.** The lord of that land is Achyuta Mahashaya,
peaceful, self-controlled, forbearing, most kind-hearted.

**55.** It is known that he was born in a noble family of karanas;
round about live his kinsmen and all his servants.[^18]

**56.** Everyone's way of life is like royal splendour:
all have very large houses and walls.

**57.** Among them Achyuta's house is outstanding:
its supremely beautiful assembly hall is famous to all.

**58.** He knows nothing but service to the brahmanas;
all praise him, calling him a brahmana.

**59.** All know him as a benefactor of others;
Achyuta's glory cannot be told at all.

**60.** That Mahashaya is devoted to the name of Hari,
tender with compassion toward all beings, humble with all.

**61.** Birth after birth he performed much austerity;
for that reason Rasikendra was born [to him] as his son.

**62.** I shall tell something of all this in detail;
listen, listen attentively, all you people of Krishna.[^19]

**63.** Thus that Achyuta lived there;
he had several wives and many sons.[^20]

**64.** In Cuttack lived one named Haladhara;
because of the oppression of the Yavanas he left his home.[^21]

**65.** That Mahashaya was a pure, noble karana;
[asking,] "Rauni, Rauni?" he came there.

**66.** Hearing Achyuta's name, he went to that land;
arriving, he stayed in the house of Gopi Mandala.[^22]

**67.** Husband and wife, the two of them, and one daughter —
fortunate in beauty and virtues, most lovely in form.

**68.** The name of that mother of the world was Bhavani;
by her austerities she became the mother of Rasika.

**69.** One day Achyuta, most fortunate,
went to the house of Gopi Mandala.

**70.** Seeing that fortunate girl, Achyuta
inquires of Mandala about her:

**71.** "From where has this worthy man come?
Make an effort that this girl be given to me."

**72.** Then Gopi disclosed to the mother and father:
"Give your daughter to the Pattanayaka."[^23]

**73.** Hearing this, the mother and father were overjoyed;
briefly they told Mandala everything:

**74.** "Giving my daughter, I take his shelter.
There is only one thing I humbly submit:

**75.** through the oppression of the Yavanas I am bereft of my estate and without property;
I shall give him the girl alone.[^24]

**76.** Whatever shame and trouble there is in this, the burden is yours;
afterwards do not hold any fault against me."

**77.** Hearing such humility from the girl's father, [Gopi said:]
"The burden of this matter is mine, be assured."

**78.** Gopa told Achyuta everything in detail;
hearing it, [Achyuta] diligently sends messengers.[^25]

**79.** From kingdom after kingdom he had all the provisions brought;
no one can transgress Achyuta's command.

**80.** Fixing an excellent hour, he performed the wedding;
telling cannot convey the splendour of the wedding.

**81.** As at the wedding of a great king or of the gods,
such joy was there in the assembly of Rauni.

**82.** Music, the roar of dundubhis of many kinds;
hundreds of thousands upon hundreds of thousands of chandrodayas, deutis and torches.[^26]

**83.** Seeing the wedding, all the people were in an ocean of joy;
taking the bride, the Mahashaya came home.

**84.** Who can tell all that joy and happiness?
Briefly I have proclaimed a little.

**85.** Now I shall make known the birth of Rasika;
hearing it, the devotees will rejoice at heart.

**86.** The Rasika-mangala is a most excellent secret,
the supreme object of worship for the people of Krishna, [the followers] of Shyamananda.[^27]

**87.** Adorning himself with Shyamananda's feet,
the son of Rasamaya joyfully composed [this].

*Thus ends the third lahari, called "Description of the Glory of Rohini," in the Purva-vibhaga of the Sri Rasika-mangala.*

[^1]: Raga Kaushika. The two italic lines after the raga are the refrain; the word "ghosha" does not appear here in the edition (cf. lahari 2, note 1). Rama and Krishna are Balarama and Krishna; Murari ("enemy of Mura") is a name of Krishna, which the author also applies to Rasika-Murari, i.e. Rasikananda (cf. lahari 1, note 7).
[^2]: "The book in four vibhagas" — *caturtha-vibhāga puṁthi*, i.e. the whole Rasika-mangala (Purva, Dakshina, Pashchima, Uttara).
[^3]: The reading on the scan is clear: "বাদাবাদি বোদাপোড় কাটে" (*bādābādi bodā-poṛa kāṭe*); the sense is conjectural: Oriya *boda*, "he-goat"; *poṛa*, "to roast"; *bādābādi*, "vying with one another".
[^4]: Mahatas (*mahata*) are apparently village headmen and landholders (cf. *mahato*, *mahanti*); it may also be understood as "the notables".
[^5]: The son of Achyuta is Rasikananda; Achyuta is his father (cf. BR 15).
[^6]: Mallabhumi is the region to which BR 15 also assigns Rasikananda's native village (BR 15: "In Mallabhumi there is a village named Rayani"). Rohini is Rasikananda's native village on the Suvarnarekha (today in Jhargram district); in BR 15 it is called Rayani, and later in this lahari Rauni. Cuttack was the capital of Utkala under the Gajapatis.
[^7]: The Dolanga is a river near Rohini (cf. BR 15: "the Dolanga river"). "Well of sweetness" — *rasa-kūpa*; possibly a local name (cf. "the place Rasakupa" later in this lahari).
[^8]: Barajit (*bārājita*) — in BR 15 "Barayita" (*bārāyita*); local tradition connects it with the wanderings of Rama, Sita and Lakshmana. Its location has not been identified.
[^9]: "The twelve lingas" (*duyādaśa liṅga*) — apparently the twelve jyotirlingas of Shiva, among which Rameshvara is counted; here it is Shiva Rameshvara near Barajit, worshipped by Rama, "the moon of the Raghu dynasty" (cf. 30; BR 15: "In Barayita Rama, son of Dasharatha, installed the Shiva named Rameshvara").
[^10]: A river's northward course (*uttara-vāhinī*) is considered especially holy; cf. BR 15: "There the Suvarnarekha flows northward."
[^11]: The names of the citrus fruits are given as in the edition: taba (*ṭābā*, a large lemon), jamir (*jāmira*, a kind of citron), shatakara (*śatakarā*, unidentified), kamala (*kamalā*, mandarin), naranga (*nāreṅga*, orange). "In clusters" — *jhārā jhārā*.
[^12]: "Pandava" (*pāṇḍava-vṛkṣa*) — the reading on the scan is clear; which tree is meant has not been identified.
[^13]: "The six tastes" — *ṣaḍ-rasa* (sweet, sour, salty, bitter, pungent, astringent); the scan has "যড়রস," the same word in an older letter form (so also the 1997 ed.: "ষড়রস").
[^14]: Rauni (*rauni*) is the same village of Rohini (cf. note 6); in BR 15, Rayani.
[^15]: "Deities and brahmanas" — *devatā brāhmaṇa*; it may also be understood as "brahmanas [honoured as] gods." Sandhya is the daily prayer at dawn, noon and dusk; tarpana is the offering of water to the ancestors and the gods.
[^16]: Navashakha ("nine branches") — a group of clean Shudra castes of Bengal and neighbouring lands: artisans and traders.
[^17]: Platforms (*piṇḍā*) — high earthen verandas along the houses. A tulasi-mancha is a raised stand with a Tulasi plant in front of a Vaishnava's house.
[^18]: Karanas — the caste of scribes and officials in Utkala (corresponding to the Kayasthas of Bengal); Achyuta belonged to it, as did Haladhara below (65).
[^19]: "People of Krishna" — *kārṣṇa-jana*, i.e. Vaishnavas.
[^20]: "Several" — literally "two or four" (*dui cāri*). Rasikananda's mother is Bhavani (68); the author says nothing here about Achyuta's other wives and sons.
[^21]: Haladhara is Bhavani's father (67–68). Cuttack — see note 6. The Yavanas here are apparently the Muslim rulers who took possession of Utkala after the fall of the Gajapatis (1568).
[^22]: Gopi Mandala (*gopī-maṇḍala*) — a resident of Rohini with whom Haladhara stayed; Mandala is a family title (usually of a village headman). Below he is called simply Mandala (70, 73), Gopi (72, 77) and Gopa (78).
[^23]: Pattanayaka (*paṭṭanāyaka*) — an honorific title of the karanas of Utkala; here, Achyuta.
[^24]: "The girl alone" (*kanyā-mātra*), i.e. without a dowry. "Bereft of my estate" — *rājya-hṛta*.
[^25]: "Gopa" — so on the scan; the 1997 ed. has "Gopi." Apparently the same Gopi Mandala.
[^26]: The dundubhi is a large kettledrum. Chandrodaya (*candrodaya*, "moonrise") — apparently a kind of festive lamp or firework; deuti (*deuṭi*) — a lamp on a stand.
[^27]: "[The followers] of Shyamananda" — *śyāmānandī*, the Shyamanandis, i.e. Shyamananda's line; "people of Krishna" — see note 19. "The supreme object of worship" — *parama upāsya*.
