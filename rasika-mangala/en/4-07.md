# Uttara-vibhaga. Lahari 7. The Glorification of the Greatness of Sri Sri Rasikananda Prabhu and the Description of Jagannathadeva's Command concerning the Journey to Nilachala

*A song in glory of Shyamananda; the great festival at Sri Gopivallabhapur grows day by day ten times more splendid than the earlier ones — the circle of the rasa, the procession of Sri Govinda-raya, the sankirtana, the feeding of the Vaishnavas, the lights, the kadamba groves, the bank of the Suvarnarekha — a hidden Vrindavan; when the river's stream moves away to the far bank, Rasika bids the Suvarnarekha, if she is a servant of Sri Chaitanya, to come to this bank, and that same night a flood brings the water back; a storm gathers over the feast of thousands of sadhus, Rasika bids Indra not to wet the assembly of Vaishnavas — and the rain pours all around the village without dropping a single drop in it; in the midst of the festival the elephant Gopala Dasa suddenly arrives, falls at Rasika's feet and, at his command, from then on comes only at night to hear kirtan and take prasada; at night in Nilachala Jagannatha bids the Mudiratha tell the maharaja to send for Rasika and give him the cloth from His holy body; the raja, receiving the same command, sends two brahmanas with the cloth; Rasika, keeping vigil at the festival, hears a voice: "Come quickly and see My lotus feet," and calls everyone to the Ratha-yatra; the brahmanas hand him the cloth and the raja's request, and Rasika ties it on his head; the author promises to tell of the pastimes in Nilachala that he saw while with him*

*The seventh lahari.*

*Raga Varadi.*[^1]
*Ghosha: "[My] life is Radhanatha, O, [my] soul is Gopinatha."*

**1.** Glory, glory to Shyamananda, the treasury of compassion,
the dear life of those who have nothing, the life of the world![^2]

**2.** In Sri Gopivallabhapur such a great festival
Rasikachandra, renowned in the world, celebrates.

**3.** However many provisions [and] how much splendour were seen before
at the rasa festivals and other aradhanas, [held] with diligence, —

**4.** ten times [more] than that comes about day by day;
the details of the festival cannot be described.[^3]

**5.** The beauty of the circle of the rasa cannot be told!
How in great bliss Sri Govinda-raya goes in procession!

**6.** How the sound of sankirtana touches the sky!
How [arranged are] the seats of the assembly of sadhus on all four sides!

**7.** And the kings, subjects, pilgrims, traders!
And the cries of "Glory, glory!" [and] the name of Hari — again and again!

**8.** And the bhoga [and] offerings to Sri Govinda-raya!
And the provisions that hundreds and hundreds of people bring!

**9.** And the order of the feeding of the Vaishnavas!
And the chandrodayas, lamps, torches, deutis!

**10.** And all the various provisions that stand in rows!
And the kadamba groves surrounding [all] on all four sides![^4]

**11.** And the beauty of the village — a hidden Vrindavan!
And the beauty of the riverbank [and] the dense forest!

**12.** And the charming waters of the Suvarnarekha!
And the wonderful pastimes of Rasika-shekhara![^5]

**13.** How wonderful the festival is cannot be told;
the son of Achyuta is intoxicated with the sweetness of the festival.

**14.** At the time of the festival the river's stream,
going far away, began to flow by the other bank.

**15.** Seeing the water far off, Rasika-shekhara in anger
said something to the Suvarnarekha:

**16.** "If you are truly a servant of Sri Chaitanya,
then by tomorrow let [your] stream flow by this bank.

**17.** Without water my sadhus suffer;
you must come to this bank."

**18.** And so, unexpectedly, that very night
a flood suddenly came and flowed by this bank.

**19.** Seeing [this], all the men and women were astonished;
from that time the river's water flows by this bank.[^6]

**20.** From that time various festivals are held in that place;
Rasika's glory [became] renowned throughout the world.

**21.** One day he seated the Vaishnavas for a meal;
two or four thousand sadhus sat down.[^7]

**22.** At that time terrible clouds covered the sky;
a fearsome wind, lightning — flash upon flash.

**23.** Thunderbolts strike, the clouds roar, the earth trembles.
Seeing this, Rasikendra himself speaks:

**24.** "All my sadhus have sat down to the meal;
in Gopivallabhapur there will be no rain.

**25.** If you are truly [like] Prahlada, O King Indra,
then you will not wet the assembly of my Vaishnavas."[^8]

**26.** Hearing Rasika's words, Indra, the lord of the gods,
did not rain on the village — it pours on all four sides.

**27.** Around the village he poured the heaviest rain,
[but] he did not let a single drop fall within the village.

**28.** The Vaishnavas joyfully took their meal;
all the men and women, seeing [it, marvelled at] the wonder.

**29.** Such is Rasika's command, inviolable [his] word:
Brahma, Shiva, the gods [and] men do not transgress [it].[^9]

**30.** One day, at the time of the great festival,
unexpectedly the elephant Gopala Dasa came.

**31.** Seeing [him], all the people trembled with fear;
hearing [of it], Rasika-shekhara came out.[^10]

**32.** He came up to the elephant —
the elephant fell at [his] feet, [stretched out] like a stick.

**33.** Placing his hand on its head, Rasika-shekhara
commanded: "Listen, intoxicated best of elephants!

**34.** Seeing you, people are greatly afraid.
Come to hear the kirtan at night.

**35.** Every day, coming here, you will receive prasada."
Bowing down, the elephant went away.[^11]

**36.** From then on it comes for darshana at night:
all carry out Rasika's command.

**37.** Devas, asuras, men, beasts do not transgress
[his command]; in the three worlds his word is not broken.[^12]

**38.** Rasikendra-chandra is intoxicated with the sweetness of the festival;
together with him his associates always delight.

**39.** At that time in Nilachala Jagannatha-deva
at night, appearing in person, gave a command to the Mudiratha:[^13]

**40.** "My dear one, My devotee, is Rasika-shekhara;
great is My desire to see him.

**41.** Go quickly to the maharaja [and] tell him:
let him send messengers and bring him here.

**42.** I will give him the neta-shari from My holy body;
at every Ratha-yatra he will come and see Me."[^14]

**43.** Hearing the command, the Mudiratha told the king;
hearing it, the king rejoiced in his heart.

**44.** That same night a divine command came to the king:
"Send messengers and bring Rasika-shekhara."

**45.** The divine command [confirmed his] faith in the Mudiratha's words:
the worthy king floats in an ocean of bliss.[^15]

**46.** He sent two brahmanas to Rasika;
with care he sends the neta-shari from the holy body.

**47.** Meanwhile here, keeping awake at night in the sweetness of the festival,
Rasika-shekhara was sitting alone.[^16]

**48.** Unexpectedly Rasikendra heard a command:
"Come quickly and see My lotus feet."

**49.** Startled, he looks around — there is no one in that place;
[yet] the soft, deep voice he heard with his own ears.

**50.** To his own people he told everything in detail:
"I have heard Jagannatha's command.

**51.** Let us all go now and see the Ratha-yatra:
the chariot that enchants the world is renowned throughout the world."[^17]

**52.** Prabhu speaks these words to everyone —
at that time two brahmanas arrived.

**53.** They began to say: "Listen, Rasika-shekhara!
Your glory has no comparison.

**54.** Jagannatha Himself gave the cloth from His body;
He gave the command to the Mudiratha, taking him by the hand:

**55.** 'My very dear devotee is Rasikendra-chandra;
to see him is a great joy for Me.'

**56.** He commanded the king: 'Bring [him] quickly,
so that he may come soon and have darshana at the chariot.'

**57.** Here, take the garment from Jagannatha's [holy] body;
hear the king's humble letter.

**58.** Set out quickly to see the chariot:
the maharaja asked us [to say this] in many ways.

**59.** Be sure to have darshana of Sri Jagadbandhu."
Hearing [this], the son of Achyuta floats in bliss.[^18]

**60.** In many ways he served the king's messengers;
on his head he tied the neta — the garment from the holy body.[^19]

**61.** Now I will tell of the pastimes in Nilachala —
whatever of [his] lila I saw while staying with him.[^20]

**62.** A description of [his] nature I have composed a little.
Listen to the Rasika-mangala, all you sadhus!

**63.** Adorning himself with Shyamananda's feet,
the son of Rasamaya joyfully composed [this].

*Thus in the Uttara-vibhaga of the Sri Rasika-mangala ends the seventh lahari, called "The Glorification of the Greatness of Sri Sri Rasikananda Prabhu and the Description of Jagannathadeva's Command concerning the Journey to Nilachala."*

[^1]: The lines before the raga are the title of the lahari ("সপ্তম-লহরী"), in italics without a number. Lahari 7 begins on a new page — pdf 190 (printed p. 146; the number and running head are not printed), after the colophon of lahari 6 on pdf 189 (files RM-4-06-001-061). Raga Varadi (*barāṛī*; Purva, lahari 8, note 2). The ghosha on the scan — *jībana rādhānātha he parāṇa gopīnātha* — is the same as in Pashchima, lahari 2 (there *re*; note 1), and its translation is repeated: Radhanatha ("Lord of Radha") and Gopinatha ("Lord of the gopis") are names of Krishna. There is no mangala-shloka: after the raga and ghosha the song begins with ॥ 1 ॥. The title follows the colophon on pdf 191 (printed p. 147), checked against the scan: "শ্রীশ্রীরসিকানন্দপ্রভুর মহিমাকীর্ত্তন ও নীলাচল-গমনে জগন্নাথদেবের প্রত্যাদেশ-বর্ণন" (*śrīśrīrasikānandaprabhura mahimākīrttana o nīlācala-gamane jagannāthadebera pratyādeśa-barṇana*). "Command" — *pratyādeśa*: a command of the Deity revealed from above (in a dream or by a voice; 44–45, 48). Lahari 7 — 1–63 (pdf 190–191), all in this file.
[^2]: "The dear life of those who have nothing" — *akiñcanapriya prāṇa*; cf. Uttara, lahari 3, 1 ("dear to those who have nothing").
[^3]: "Great festival" (2) — *mahāyātrā*; apparently the same twelve-day festival at Gopivallabhapur spoken of in laharis 5–6. "The rasa festivals and other aradhanas" (3) — *rāsamahotsaba ādi ārādhane*: the rasa festival — Pashchima, laharis 2–3; aradhana — a festival of worship (Uttara, lahari 2). "Were seen" — *dekhila*, the subject not expressed. "Ten times" — *daśaguṇa*.
[^4]: Sri Govinda-raya — the Deity whom Rasika served at Gopivallabhapur (Pashchima, lahari 11). "Goes in procession" (5) — *bije*, respectfully of the procession of the Deity. "Traders" (7) — *pasāriẏā*, stall-keepers at the festival market (on the scan there is a blot between *yātrī* and *pasāriẏā*). "Again and again" — *ghane ghana*. Chandrodaya, deuti — festive lamps (Purva, lahari 12, note 17). "Stand in rows" (10) — *sāri sāri baise*. "Kadamba groves" — *kadambakhaṇḍi*.
[^5]: "A hidden Vrindavan" (11) — *gupta-bṛndābana*: the author likens Gopivallabhapur and its surroundings to Vrindavan. "Riverbank" — *pulina*, the sandy bank of a river (like the bank of the Yamuna in Vrindavan). The Suvarnarekha — the river on which Gopivallabhapur stands.
[^6]: 14–19 — the Suvarnarekha changes its course at Rasika's word. "Began to flow by the other bank" (14) — *āra taṭe lāge bahibāra*. "A servant of Sri Chaitanya" (16) — *śrīcaitanyera sebaka*. "By tomorrow" — *kālai*. "Flood" (18) — *banyā*. On the author's view of Rasika — note 9.
[^7]: "Two or four thousand" — *dui cāri sahasra*: "several thousand." "Sadhus" (21, 24) — *santa* (so on the scan; garbled in the OCR).
[^8]: The line is clear on the scan (crop ×2): *yabe tumi niścaẏe prahlāda indrarāja*, literally "if you are truly Prahlada, King Indra"; understood as an address to Indra: "if you are [devoted like] Prahlada" — cf. 16 ("if you are truly a servant of Sri Chaitanya"). Prahlada is a great devotee of the Lord (Bhag. 7); according to a tradition of the Mahabharata, he himself once held the throne of Indra. Another understanding of *prahlāda* — "gracious, gladdened" (?) — is possible. Cf. the Govardhana lila: Krishna protected Vraja from Indra's downpour (Bhag. 10.25).
[^9]: "Inviolable" — *alaṅghya*. Indra, Brahma and Shiva submit to Rasika's word — the author's rapturous praise (cf. Uttara, lahari 4, 8, note 5). According to Gaudiya Vaishnava teaching, Rasikananda is a devotee and associate, a disciple of Shyamananda, not an incarnation of the Lord; the Lord, who loves His devotees, Himself fulfils their word. On the author's attitude to Shyamananda and Rasika — Purva, lahari 1, note 2; Purva, lahari 14, note 11. 15–19 and 37 are to be understood in the same way.
[^10]: The elephant Gopala Dasa (*gopāladāsa hātī*) — the mad elephant whom Rasika tamed and initiated into the holy name (Pashchima, lahari 8, note 34; on its devotion to the guru and service of the sadhus — Pashchima, lahari 10). "Came out" — *bāhāra hailā*.
[^11]: "Came up" (32) — *upasana* (Uttara, lahari 4, note 3). "[Stretched out] like a stick" — *daṇḍabat haiẏā*: a full prostration. "Intoxicated best of elephants" (33) — *matta karibara*: in the state of musth (Pashchima, lahari 8, note 10). "Prasada" (35) — *parasāda*. On the scan 35 is followed by a single "।".
[^12]: The same in Uttara, lahari 4, 8 (note 5); see note 9.
[^13]: Nilachala — Puri, the abode of Jagannatha. The Mudiratha (*mudiratha*) — a servant of the Jagannatha temple who stands in for the gajapati in the temple rites (the editor glosses "মুদিরথ—সেবক," "servant"). "In person" — *sākṣāta*. The maharaja (41) — the king of Utkala, the gajapati; his name is not given.
[^14]: Neta-shari (*netaśāṛī*; in 54, 60 — *neta*) — a fine cloth that has been on the Deity of Jagannatha: prasada cloth, a sign of special favour. "From My holy body" — *mora śrīaṅgera*. "Will come and see Me" — *āsi' dekhibe more*.
[^15]: "Rejoiced" (43) — *ānanda … hailē* (so on the scan; the ending is for the rhyme). "Divine command" (44–45) — *pratyādeśa* (note 1). 45a — *pratyādeśa mudiratha-bāṇīte pratyaẏa*, literally "the command — confidence in the Mudiratha's words": understood to mean that the command received by the king himself confirmed the Mudiratha's words.
[^16]: "Keeping awake at night" — *niśi ujāgare*. "Alone" — *nigame* (so on the scan, crop ×3): "in private, in seclusion," as in lahari 4 (note 19) and lahari 5 (note 22).
[^17]: "Startled" (49) — *camakite*. "To his own people" (50) — *ātmagaṇe*. "The chariot that enchants the world" (51) — *bhubanamohana ratha*: Jagannatha's chariot at the Ratha-yatra (Uttara, lahari 2, note 6).
[^18]: "Has no comparison" (53) — *dite nāi paṭāntare*: *paṭāntara* — "comparison, likeness." "Taking him by the hand" (54) — *dhari' tā'ra hāta*. "Humble letter" (57) — *binaẏa-patra*. "Sri Jagadbandhu" (59) — *śrījagabandhu* (so on the scan, crop ×2), "Friend of the world" — Jagannatha.
[^19]: "Served" — *karila sebana*: received the messengers with honour. "The neta — the garment from the holy body" — *neta śrīaṅga basana* (note 14). "Tied on his head" — a sign of reverence for the prasada cloth.
[^20]: "The pastimes in Nilachala" — *nīlācala khelā*: they are told in the following laharis. 61b — *ye kichu dekhila līlā saṅge thāki' tā̃ra*: understood as the author speaking of himself (forms in *-ila* are not rare in the poem for the first person); it can also be understood as "what [people] saw while staying with him." "A description of [his] nature" (62) — *sbabhāba-barṇanā* (lahari 6, 60, note 29).
