# Purva-vibhaga. Lahari 7. Description of the Childhood Pastimes

*The ear-piercing ceremony: Rasika faints on hearing the musicians sing of Krishna; Dayaladasi Thakurani comes to see the child, foretells his service in Utkala and gives him the name of Hari in his ear; the boy chants a hundred thousand names daily; with the children he enacts Krishna's pastimes after the Bhagavata*

*The seventh lahari.*

*Raga Narayani-gauda.*[^1]
*Ghosha: "My Krishna is a treasury of virtues,*
*the refuge of the helpless, the utmost limit of mercy."*

**1.** Glory, glory to Shyamananda, the lord of Rasika's life!
Be merciful, so that I may sing your glory and renown.[^2]

**2.** Day by day the joyful Rasika-shekhara
wanders from house to house, playing as he pleases.

**3.** At that time the hour for the karnavedha came;
the townspeople informed Achyuta [of it].[^3]

**4.** Having calculated an auspicious day and an auspicious moment,
[Achyuta] set up the mangala-ghata in the middle.

**5.** The brahmanas, rejoicing, perform the homa;
quickly they summoned that excellent barber.

**6.** Having bathed the son and dressed [him] handsomely,
they seated [him] on a seat, placing a laddu in [his] hand.

**7.** Music and the thunder of the dundubhi sound without pause;
two muhuriyas sing of Krishna's virtues.[^4]

**8.** ("Listen, friend, to what lies deep in my heart:
Shyama-nagara comes to my mind day and night.")[^5]

**9.** This pada the two [play] on the shanai;
hearing [it], Achyuta's son became motionless.[^6]

**10.** The eight sattvika-[bhavas] arose in his body:
the hairs stood on end all over his body, streams of tears flow from his eyes.

**11.** Sitting on the pindi, he lost consciousness;
hearing the shanai, Prabhu fell to the ground.[^7]

**12.** By degrees his mind [came] into a state of madness with Krishna-prema;
not understanding [the cause], [people] say, "What did he see, and when?"[^8]

**13.** Sobbing and sobbing, he weeps in distress,
saying, "When will I attain Krishna, the Lord of my life?"

**14.** He does not open his eyes, the weeping does not cease;
seeing [this], all the townspeople were frightened.

**15.** Running, everyone came there;
the relatives tend [him] in various ways.

**16.** In great fear the brahmanas do not perform the homa;
the musicians do not play at all.

**17.** [The players of] the shanai fell silent, seeing the boy;
everyone, stunned, comes and looks.

**18.** As soon as he no longer heard the sound of the shanai,
Achyuta's son sat up in his ordinary state.

**19.** Everyone raised cries of "Hari!" and "Jaya!";
joyfully the barber sits down to pierce the ears.

**20.** With care he pierced the beautiful, shapely ears;[^9]
[and the boy,] saying "Krishna," eats the laddu again and again.

**21.** The homa, the yajna and the auspicious rites were performed in due order;
the brahmanas utter the sound of the Vedas.

**22.** At that time Dayaladasi Thakurani —
all praise [her] as a devoted servant of Chaitanya —[^10]

**23.** having set up a temple, lives in this land;
Achyuta serves [her] at all times.

**24.** At that time, hearing of Rasika's appearance,
the mother came joyfully to see [him].[^11]

**25.** Having quickly blessed Achyuta,
she went into the chambers and saw the son.

**26.** Seeing Rasika's beauty, she became unconscious;
all the women, sprinkling water on her face, lift [her] up.

**27.** Everyone says, "To begin with she is old, and on top of that she came in the heat of the sun —
that is why she fainted and fell to the ground."

**28.** People make fun [of her] one to another;
rising, that mother tells the reason for her fainting:

**29.** "You do not know the glory of this child:
I saw as it were the form of Gopala.[^12]

**30.** Seeing [his] enchanting beauty, I lost consciousness.
This son is not [merely] a child: he is the life of the world.

**31.** He will deliver all living beings;
he will preach prema-bhakti in Utkala.

**32.** He will uphold all dharma:
service to the ashvattha, Tulasi, Vaishnavas [and] brahmanas.

**33.** He will show mercy to the poor and lowly;
by his signs [one sees that he is] the protector of the surrendered.

**34.** His glory cannot be told at all:
this great soul is Krishna's own associate.

**35.** Achyuta's son is endowed with every auspicious sign;
no one will transgress his word.

**36.** Seeing [his] dark, beautiful, enchanting body,
I know for certain: he is a servant of Krishna.

**37.** This great soul will be a knower of all the shastras;
this son will be deeply steadfast in all dharma.

**38.** He will preach the sixty-four limbs of bhakti;
he has descended (avatara) to deliver all beings.[^13]

**39.** I do not know how to tell of his endless virtues.
By great merit Bhavani obtained this son."

**40.** That mother is the eldest of the clan, the mother of the world:
she herself knows past, future and present.[^14]

**41.** In detail she told everything to Achyuta:
"This son is the moon that lights up the family."

**42.** Hearing all those words, humbly
Achyuta, bowing down, speaks to her:

**43.** "Bless [him], mother, that the son may live!
Birth after birth this boy is the servant of you all."

**44.** Hearing [this], the mother joyfully gives her blessing:
"Krishna, protect, Krishna, protect this boy!"

**45.** Hearing Krishna's name, he became joyful;
clasping that mother by the neck, he began to weep.

**46.** Joyfully taking the son on her lap, Dayaladasi
mercifully let [him] hear the name in his ear.[^15]

**47.** She gave the name "Hare Krishna" in Achyuta's presence
and openly told all about it:

**48.** "The true meaning of the mantra that I spoke into the boy's ear
a certain person will tell [him].

**49.** That great soul is his own lord of life;
the two will deliver living beings — this I say for certain.[^16]

**50.** Together they will deliver Utkala,
preaching prema-bhakti by Chaitanya's order.

**51.** They will distribute the wealth of Krishna-prema from house to house;
they will deliver all beings, [even] the chandalas.

**52.** Do not neglect him, thinking him a child:
this great soul is a dear devotee of Sri Krishna."

**53.** Having told everything in detail, she asked leave to go;
Achyuta [and] Bhavani gave [her] many gifts.

**54.** All took the dust of her feet on their heads;
following [her] some distance, they bade [her] farewell.

**55.** Thus, taking Rasika-shekhara in their arms,
the two came home, joyful at heart.

**56.** Day by day — a most wondrous account! —
Achyuta's son performs pastimes of Krishna-prema.

**57.** Whatever human childhood games there are —
those he does not touch: [all he] does is full of Krishna's pastimes.

**58.** Some days, spreading an asana, he sits in meditation
and remembers the Hare Krishna maha-mantra.

**59.** For two or three praharas he meditates on Krishna:[^17]
the hairs stand on end all over his body, tears flow unceasingly.

**60.** Seeing [this], his mother says, "Listen, my darling:
milk, laddu, cream, sugar — wish for something!"

**61.** [But] Prabhu does not hear anyone's words
until the count of a hundred thousand names is complete.[^18]

**62.** From that day he chants a hundred thousand names;
on his neck is a Tulasi necklace, beautiful beyond compare.

**63.** Seeing [this], all the people say to Achyuta:
"Surely Krishna's mercy has come upon this son.

**64.** In such a little one such understanding has appeared:
he has begun to chant Krishna's name unceasingly!"

**65.** He cares nothing for eating, resting or sleep;
[his] body, overwhelmed with Krishna-prema, shines.

**66.** Thus all the people praise the son;
day by day he enacts all the pastimes of Krishna there are.

**67.** Taking along children of the same age,
he plays those games in which there is talk of Krishna.

**68.** With his own hands he dresses the children;
he himself costumes [them] according to the pastime.[^19]

**69.** Some become the Earth in the form of a cow,
someone becomes Brahma — they go and lay [their plight] before him.[^20]

**70.** Someone becomes Narayana in the Ocean of Milk;
some [become] the demigods and with Brahma [offer] their plea.

**71.** Someone [becomes] Vasudeva, someone Devaki,
someone Kamsa, someone keeps [them] in prison.

**72.** Some are Nanda [and] Yashoda, others gopis [and] cowherds,
some become the cows, some the little ones.[^21]

**73.** Someone becomes Nanda's son, someone Putana:
he sucks her breast, tormenting her.

**74.** Someone becomes the cart and the rest, someone Trinavarta;
day by day he does [the pastimes] thus in various ways.

**75.** As [he] dresses the children, such is their appearance;
seeing the children's costumes, [he] sheds streams of tears.

**76.** Day by day they all play this pastime as a game;
seeing [it], Achyuta's child is immersed in bliss.[^22]

**77.** Apart from the Bhagavata he knows nothing else;
lying on the ground, he meditates on the Bhagavata.

**78.** In childhood he knows no other game:
he constantly plays the games of Krishna and Balarama.

**79.** All the boys take on those forms;
Rasika plays this game day and night.

**80.** Seeing [this], all the people are amazed:
"This son of Achyuta is not human."

**81.** Not with a child's understanding does he [enact] Krishna's pastimes:
he plays all the games in the order of the Bhagavata.

**82.** One day they stage the name-giving:
one is Garga, another Nanda, others the cowherd men.

**83.** One day some boy eats earth;
[his] mouth is opened, and someone sees within it the whole play.[^23]

**84.** One day, tying [someone] to a mortar,
[he] drags [it] between [the trees] and breaks the yamala-arjunas.

**85.** One day some child was finely dressed,[^24]
[and] someone kills the demon Vatsasura.

**86.** One day, making some child Bakasura,
[someone] slays [him] in fun; Rasika-Murari watches.

**87.** One day, dressing [someone] as Aghasura,
someone kills [him] in play; [Rasika] himself watches.

**88.** One day some [become] calves, [others] boys,
and someone, becoming Brahma, steals [them] and carries [them] off.

**89.** Someone becomes Krishna [and] creates calves and boys;
[someone], becoming Brahma, offers prayers in many ways.

**90.** One day [someone] takes the form of Dhenukasura;
having killed him, the child goes about dancing.

**91.** One day [they enact] with delight the subduing of Kaliya;
someone, [as] the wives of the naga, offers prayers together with the children.

**92.** One day [someone] puts out the forest fire;
someone kills the demon Pralamba.

**93.** One day they again put out the forest fire;
one day all together describe nature.[^25]

**94.** One day the child describes autumn;
one day he tells the glory of the Venu-gita.

**95.** One day, setting up Katyayani,
all the children together steal the clothes.

**96.** One day someone dresses as the wives of the sacrificing brahmanas;
all together they ask for food and accept [it].

**97.** One day they put a stop to the worship of Indra;
one day in play they lift Govardhana.[^26]

**98.** One day Indra, taking Surabhi with him,
offers prayers in many words, calling [Krishna] "Govinda."

**99.** On the day the child played the Holder of Govardhana,
Rasika-Murari, seeing [it], fainted.

**100.** His whole body overwhelmed, he rolls on the ground;
every day, seeing [these] pastimes, he weeps aloud.

**101.** Seeing the boy's understanding, the pandits praise [him]:
"This great soul is a dear devotee of Sri Krishna.

**102.** We [ourselves] know nothing of the truth of the Bhagavata,
[yet he], a child, plays [it] in exactly that form!"

**103.** Thus every day [people] praise the child;
thus the child, dressing [the children], plays constantly.[^27]

**104.** Day and night he knows nothing but the Bhagavata;
taking up childhood games, he spends nights and days.

**105.** One day someone, [as] Nanda, observes Ekadashi;
[another], becoming Varuna, seizes [him] and takes [him] away.[^28]

**106.** Someone, becoming Krishna, rescues him and brings him back.
One day, forming a circle at the place of the rasa —

**107.** some as gopis, some as Krishna: dressing up the children,
giving [them] exactly that appearance, taking all along —

**108.** someone at the foot of the wish-fulfilling tree imitates the sound of the flute with his singing;
hearing that sound, all the gopis set out.[^29]

**109.** Meeting Krishna, they sport, [dancing] the rasa;
someone disappears — [and he] is searched for again and again.

**110.** Seeing that Krishna has disappeared, Murari [enters such a state] —
those eight sattvika bhavas I am unable to describe.

**111.** Again the child-gopis, having found Krishna,
joyfully dance the rasa in Vrindavana.

**112.** One day someone delivers Sudarshana;
one day they sing the "Gopi-gita."[^30]

**113.** One day someone becomes the demon Arishta,
someone slays him — in great joy.[^31]

**114.** One day someone takes the form of Keshi,
and another child makes an end of him.

**115.** One day some boy becomes Akrura
and at Kamsa's command goes to fetch Krishna.

**116.** Some, becoming Akrura, offer prayers;
together with Krishna [they] enter Mathura.

**117.** One day a child, in fun, becomes the washerman;
slaying him, [Krishna] hands out the garments on all sides.

**118.** Someone becomes the garland-maker, [calling himself] Sudama;
dressing all the children, he puts flower garlands on their necks.

**119.** Someone becomes Kubja with a box of scents;
some child, taking her scents, makes her well.

**120.** Some child, taking up the bow, breaks [it];
some child slays the elephant Kuvalaya.

**121.** On some days [they] slay Chanura and Mushtika;
one day the children slay Kamsa.

**122.** Thus day and night, without pause, plays
Rasika-shekhara — the Sri Bhagavata embodied.[^32]

**123.** Listen, listen to the Rasika-mangala, all people:
Rasika's plays [follow] the order of the Bhagavata.

**124.** Adorning himself with Shyamananda's feet,
the son of Rasamaya joyfully composed [this].

*Thus ends the seventh lahari, called "Description of the Childhood Pastimes," in the Purva-vibhaga of the Sri Rasika-mangala.*

[^1]: Raga Narayani-gauda (*nārāyaṇī gauṛā*) — so in the scan. The ghosha is the refrain. There is no mangala-shloka at the beginning of this lahari (unlike lahari 6): after the heading, the raga and the ghosha the song begins with ॥ 1 ॥.
[^2]: "Lord of life" — *prāṇa-pati*. "Your" — *tuyā* (a Brajabuli form): the address is to Shyamananda. "Glory and renown" — *yaśaḥ-kīrtti*.
[^3]: Karnavedha (*karṇa-vedha*) — the samskara of piercing a child's ears. Couplet 3 is built like the opening of the account of the annaprashana in lahari 6, 10; the rite itself is described further on (4–21).
[^4]: The dundubhi is a large kettledrum. Muhuriya (*muhuriyā*) — according to the edition's gloss, a *shanaidar*, a player of the shanai (sanai), a wind instrument like an oboe (see 9, 11, 17–18).
[^5]: Lines of the pada played by the musicians (9) — printed in the edition in parentheses but with the number ॥ 8 ॥. They are the words of a gopi (Radha) to a friend; Shyama-nagara ("Shyama the lover") is Krishna. "Listen, friend" — *śuna lo sajani*. The source of the pada has not been identified.
[^6]: "Became motionless" — *aceṣṭa*; according to the edition's gloss, "lost consciousness"; the edition also notes a variant *āviṣṭa*, "became absorbed [in ecstasy]" (so too in the 1997 edition).
[^7]: The pindi (*piṁṛā*) is a low wooden seat (cf. lahari 6, note 10). Prabhu — here Rasika (so also in lahari 6, 7–8).
[^8]: The sense of the second line is unclear: *nā jāniyā bale kibā dekhila kakhane*. It may also be understood as "without knowing [it], [he] speaks of what [he once] saw," i.e., the boy, hearing the song about Shyama, seems to recall something seen before.
[^9]: "Shapely" — *susañca*: so in the scan and in the 1997 edition; the word is unclear and the translation conjectural; cf. the infant's "finely shaped" (*suśaṅka*) ears in lahari 4, 58, note 13.
[^10]: Dayaladasi — see lahari 4, note 18 (she told Rasika's mother about Krishna during her pregnancy). Thakurani is a respectful title of a Vaishnava woman. She has not been identified in other sources.
[^11]: "The mother" — *mātā*: a respectful way of referring to an elderly Vaishnava woman (Dayaladasi), not Rasika's mother; so also in 28, 40, 42–45.
[^12]: "The form of Gopala" — *gopāla-pratimā*; *pratimā* means both "form, likeness" and "Deity (murti)": "as it were the Deity of Gopala." Gopala is Krishna as a child, the little cowherd.
[^13]: The sixty-four limbs (*aṅga*) of bhakti are listed in the Bhakti-rasamrita-sindhu 1.2 and in the CC, Madhya 22. On the word "avatara" applied to Rasikananda see lahari 6, note 11: in the Gaudiya Vaishnava understanding he is an eternal associate and dear devotee (cf. 34, 36, 52), not an avatara of the Lord.
[^14]: "Eldest of the clan" — *kula-vṛddha*; "mother of the world" — *jagata-jananī*: an honorific; knowledge of past, present and future is ascribed to her as a saintly Vaishnavi.
[^15]: That is, she whispered the holy name into his ear. This is not diksha: Rasikananda later receives diksha from Shyamananda (BR 15); Dayaladasi herself says that the meaning of the mantra will be revealed to him by another — his "lord of life" (48–49).
[^16]: "Lord of life" — *prāṇa-pati*: Shyamananda, Rasikananda's future guru (cf. 7, 1: "Shyamananda, the lord of Rasika's life"). "The two" (*doṁhe*) — Shyamananda and Rasikananda; cf. lahari 2, 13 on how "the two" won over Utkala.
[^17]: A prahara is a quarter of the day or night, about three hours.
[^18]: "A hundred thousand names" (*lakṣa-nāma*, cf. 62) — the vow to chant a lakh of holy names daily (about 64 rounds of the maha-mantra on beads of 108); cf. Haridasa Thakura, who chanted three lakhs of names (CC, Adi 10; Antya 3).
[^19]: "Dresses" — *kāchani karena*; *kāchani* — according to the edition's gloss, "dress, attire"; cf. 75, 85, 87, 103 (*kāchaya*, *kāchiyā*).
[^20]: 69–106 follow the order of the tenth canto of the Bhagavata (cf. 81): 69–71 — the Earth in the form of a cow, Brahma and the demigods at the Ocean of Milk, Vasudeva, Devaki and Kamsa (10.1–3); 72 — Nanda's festival (10.5); 73 — Putana (10.6); 74 — Shakatasura and Trinavarta (10.7); 82–83 — the name-giving by Garga and the universe in Krishna's mouth (10.8); 84 — Krishna bound to the mortar and the yamala-arjunas (10.9–10); 85–86 — Vatsasura and Bakasura (10.11); 87 — Aghasura (10.12); 88–89 — Brahma steals the calves and boys, Krishna becomes them, Brahma's prayers (10.13–14); 90 — Dhenukasura (10.15); 91 — Kaliya and the prayers of the naga's wives (10.16); 92–93 — the forest fire and Pralamba (10.17–19); 93–94 — the rains and autumn, the Venu-gita (10.20–21); 95 — the Katyayani vow and the stealing of the clothes (10.22); 96 — the wives of the brahmanas (10.23); 97 — Govardhana (10.24–25); 98 — Indra and Surabhi, "Govinda" (10.27); 105–106 — Nanda carried off by Varuna's servant after Ekadashi (10.28); 106 — the rasa (10.29 ff.).
[^21]: "Cowherds" — *gopāla*, the cowherd boys. "Little ones" — *chāoyāla*, "child, young one"; next to the cows it may also mean "calves."
[^22]: "Achyuta's child" — *acyutera bālā*; *bālā* here means "child," as in 83 (*kona bālā*, "some boy"); it could also be understood as "Achyuta's wife" (Bhavani).
[^23]: "Within it" — *garbhe*, lit. "in the womb, inside." According to the Bhagavata (10.8), Yashoda, opening Krishna's mouth, saw the whole universe in it; "the whole play" — *saba khelā*; possibly "the whole [universe as] play."
[^24]: "Finely" — *susāra*, glossed in the edition as *sundara*. Perhaps the one who is to play Krishna is dressed up.
[^25]: "Nature" — *svabhāva*: apparently the description of the rainy season and of Vrindavana's nature, which in the Bhagavata (10.20) precedes the description of autumn (94).
[^26]: "Put a stop to" — *bhañjana karaye*, lit. "break": Krishna persuaded Nanda and the cowherds to give up the sacrifice to Indra and honour Govardhana instead (Bhag. 10.24).
[^27]: "Dressing [the children]" — *kāche* is taken as the verb "dresses" (cf. the edition's glosses on 75, 85); it could also be understood as "plays near, together with the children."
[^28]: In the Bhagavata (10.28) Nanda, having observed Ekadashi, entered the waters of the Yamuna before dawn and was led away by a servant of Varuna; Krishna went to Varuna and brought his father back.
[^29]: 107–111 — the rasa-lila (Bhag. 10.29–33; cf. note 20): the call of the flute, the rasa dance, Krishna's disappearance, the search and His return. "Imitates the sound of the flute with his singing" — *vaṁśī-dhvani gāna*: the boy playing Krishna imitates the flute with his voice. The eight sattvika bhavas (110) — see lahari 6, note 13.
[^30]: Sudarshana — the vidyadhara who by a curse had become a serpent and was delivered by the touch of Krishna's foot (Bhag. 10.34); the editor of the edition in his note identifies him with Shankhachuda, who is slain in the same chapter. "Gopi-gita" ("the song of the gopis") is the name of Bhag. 10.31; by the order of the narrative (after 10.34) the gopis' song in their daytime separation from Krishna (10.35, the "Yugala-gita") may also be meant.
[^31]: 113–121 — Bhag. 10.36–44: Arishtasura, Keshi, Akrura sent by Kamsa and his prayers (10.39–40), Kamsa's washerman, Sudama the garland-maker, Kubja, the broken bow, the elephant Kuvalayapida, the wrestlers Chanura and Mushtika, Kamsa. "Box" — *peṛī*, a basket or box; "makes her well" — *bhāla kare*, lit. "makes good": Krishna straightened the hunchbacked Kubja.
[^32]: "The Sri Bhagavata embodied" — *śrī-bhāgavata-murati*: his whole play is the Bhagavata in living forms. Rasika-shekhara ("crown of the rasikas") is Rasikananda (lahari 4, note 2). 123 — cf. 81 (*bhāgavata-anukrame*, "in the order of the Bhagavata").
