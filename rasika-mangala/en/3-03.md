# Pashchima-vibhaga. Lahari 3. The Description of the Rasa

*A song in glory of Shyamananda; at Sri Gopivallabhapur on the bank of the Suvarnarekha, on the spring full moon of Vaishakha, Rasika opens the rasa: amid the thunder of instruments and sankirtana, amid hosts of people and gods, boys enact the rasa-lila following the five chapters of the Bhagavata — the call of the flute, the coming of the gopis, the words of Krishna sending them home to their husbands, the gopis' weeping and their reply that they will not leave His feet; Krishna smilingly calls the gopis, and the boys dance the rasa in the midst of the circle of sankirtana; the bliss of Vrindavana appears, the people are amazed and call Rasika a second Narayana; the author writes by the grace of Rasika, the son of Achyuta*

*The third lahari.*

*Raga Kamoda.*[^1]
*Ghosha: "Blessed, O blessed is the union of Radha and Shyama!*
*How many waves have risen in the ocean of nectar!"*[^2]

**1.** Glory, glory to Shyamananda, the refuge of the helpless,
the friend of Vallabha's life, the son of Durika![^3]

**2.** Sri Gopivallabhapur is a charming place
on the bank of the Suvarnarekha, beautifully laid out.

**3.** On all four sides are forests, most charming;
a very soft place — a beautiful river sandbank.[^4]

**4.** In such a place Rasikendra began the rasa;
all the hosts of the gods came to see.[^5]

**5.** People without count — they became innumerable;
various instruments and dundubhis sound on all four sides.

**6.** Cries of "Hari!", the sound of conches, flutes and horns;
with the sound of the various instruments the earth shakes.[^6]

**7.** The sankirtana [and] the dundubhis are most tremendous;
with the sound "dulu-dulu" the earth trembles.

**8.** At that time Rasikendra manifested the rasa;
the people of the three worlds were immersed in bliss.

**9.** The full moon of Vaishakha, a very bright moon;
that night is [like] eternity, there is no limit to it.[^7]

**10.** In springtime the wish-fulfilling tree is beautiful;
in the middle of the circle of the rasa is a throne of jewels.

**11.** Krishna went up onto it and remained [there];
bent in three places, [He plays] a most sweet song on the flute.[^8]

**12.** He calls the gopis by name, one after another;
hearing the murali, the gopis came [to Him].[^9]

**13.** Seeing Krishna's face, [they] lost their senses;
with the tears from their eyes the gopis' garments grew wet.

**14.** Seeing the gopis, Krishna, bowing His head,
stood still for a moment, leaning on the wish-fulfilling tree.[^10]

**15.** Seeing Krishna displeased, all the gopis,
their heads bowed, drawing on the ground, weep.

**16.** Sobbing and sobbing they weep, unaware of their bodies.
[Then] the Lord — God, Bhagavan — began to speak.[^11]

**17.** In the order of the Bhagavata, according to its every rule,
Sri Rasika, the treasury of virtues, manifested the rasa.[^12]

**18.** Krishna speaks sweet words to the gopis:
"Leaving your homes, on what business have you come to the forest?[^13]

**19.** At home your relatives are anxiously searching for you;
why have you come to the forest at this dreadful midnight?

**20.** The blue waters of the Yamuna, the beautiful sandbank,
the trees, [their] shoots — are most charming:

**21.** you have seen all these places — go home;
the children are crying bitterly.

**22.** If you have come to see Me out of love for Me —
you have seen Me; now go home quickly.

**23.** Serve your husbands at home, filled with joy;
worship [them] firmly as Vishnu.

**24.** Even if a husband is poor, unhappy, sick —
know him for certain to be equal to Vishnu.

**25.** Serve your husbands thus with firm attachment;
why do you wander in the forest with anxious minds?"[^14]

**26.** All [the rasikas] who are like Rasika, together with Rasika,
on various instruments, with various music, sing in various delightful ways.[^15]

**27.** Pakhoyaj, mridanga, and dampha [and] dhol,
and all the other various instruments raised a thunder.[^16]

**28.** Vina, venu, murali, upanga — delightful;
someone joyfully plays the svara-mandala.

**29.** Kapinasha, saranga, and pinaka, kinnari,
rabab, madol, kansi, muchung, mahuri,

**30.** kartal, mandiras and the rest, the sweet panchama;
surrounding the gopis, all sing in many ways.[^17]

**31.** Someone sings the five chapters on the rasa from the Sri Bhagavata;
following their order, they have the boys enact the lila.[^18]

**32.** That gait, that posture, that conduct,
those tears of bliss — someone [among them] presents.[^19]

**33.** The children are all skilled in music and poetry;
one after another [they] enact all the pastimes in various ways.[^20]

**34.** With dance, song [and] movement they enchant the people of the world;
choked with prema, the children all speak.

**35.** The children dance most charmingly;
seeing the dance, men and women are spellbound.[^21]

**36.** Having danced, turning round, they speak sweet words:
they enact that pastime in the order of the Bhagavata.[^22]

**37.** Then, hearing Krishna's harsh words,
heads bowed, sighing, [the gopis] speak.[^23]

**38.** Their lips dried up; all weep;
their garments were soaked with tears, [the tears] washed their feet.[^24]

**39.** Falling at [His] feet, they speak sweet words:
"Hear, Prabhu, ocean of mercy, O Bhagavan!

**40.** Your flute calls all of us by name;
we could not stay at home — [our] hearts were distraught.

**41.** Leaving household affairs, sons, property [and] wealth,
we came to the forest at night to see [Your] feet.

**42.** Your flute called us and brought [us here];
and now You say: 'Go home, all of you, return.'

**43.** All the rules of the Vedas that You have told us —
[I] shall tell their explanation; listen, O Vamshidhara.[^25]

**44.** The four Vedas meditate on Your feet,
and yet they cannot see this treasure — the feet.

**45.** The feet on which Indra and the other gods, the best of sages and others, meditate —
those unattainable feet we have today obtained by good fortune.[^26]

**46.** Leaving such feet, what have we to do at home?
Without You, may a thunderbolt strike every other happiness![^27]

**47.** You are wealth, You are kin, You are friend [and] husband;
apart from these feet I know nothing.

**48.** All the rules of the Vedas that You have told us —
service to the husband, service to the guru, the consideration of one's own dharma —[^28]

**49.** [yet] You are the husband of all, the life of the world;
You abide in all beings, divine Narayana.

**50.** You are the destroyer of all, the sustainer of all;
knowing [this], the gopis do not leave Your feet.[^29]

**51.** Many austerities I diligently performed;
that is why the gopis have obtained Your feet.[^30]

**52.** The ill-fated gopis are utterly wretched;
the one hope of all [of them] is Your two feet.

**53.** Without these feet the gopi does not live even a moment;
we have entered [Your] shelter, leaving all delusion.[^31]

**54.** Without You the gopi has no hope of home;
leaving all happiness, I have made You my support.

**55.** If in this Bhagavan will not show mercy,
the gopi will surely perish — of this there is no doubt."[^32]

**56.** Hearing the gopis' exceedingly firm words,
the Lotus-eyed One, smiling, called the gopis.[^33]

**57.** Rejoicing, the gopis surrounded Gopala;
in that same mood the children all dance in the rasa.[^34]

**58.** Around the circle, on all four sides, is sankirtana;
in the middle the children dance in various ways.

**59.** Whatever music there is on earth —
all, joined together, sounds in merriment.[^35]

**60.** All the happiness of Vrindavana became manifest;
seeing [it], all the people are seized with great awe.[^36]

**61.** Rasika's glory is altogether a wondrous tale;
all say: "Rasika is a second Narayana."[^37]

**62.** The pastimes that he displayed at every moment
could not be described even with ten million mouths.[^38]

**63.** By his grace I have described a little —
what the son of Achyuta, abiding in [my] heart, tells.[^39]

**64.** The Rasika-mangala is supreme, full of sweetness;
hearing it, all living beings, cross over [the age of] Kali.[^40]

**65.** Adorning himself with Shyamananda's feet,
the son of Rasamaya joyfully composed [this].

*Thus in the Pashchima-vibhaga of the Sri Rasika-mangala ends the third lahari, called "The Description of the Rasa."*[^41]

[^1]: The lines before the raga are the lahari's heading («তৃতীয়-লহরী»), in italics without a number. Lahari 3 begins on a new page, pdf 138 (printed p. 98; page number and running head not printed), after the colophon of lahari 2 on pdf 137 (files RM-3-02-007-076). Raga Kamoda (*kāmoda*) — Dakshina, lahari 9, note 1. There is no mangala-shloka: after the raga and ghosha the song begins with ॥ 1 ॥. The title follows the colophon on pdf 139 (printed p. 99; checked against the scan): «ইতি শ্রীরসিকমঙ্গল-পশ্চিম-বিভাগে রাস-বর্ণন-নাম তৃতীয়-লহরী সম্পূর্ণা» (*rāsa-barṇana*) — "The Description of the Rasa". The summary line covers the whole lahari (32–65 from the OCR and scan of pdf 139, provisionally).
[^2]: The ghosha reads in the scan: *dhanya re dhanya rādhāśyāma saṅga / amiya-sāgare kata bāṛila taraṅga*; "union" — *saṅga*, lit. "company, togetherness"; "nectar" — *amiya* (= *amṛta*). This lahari describes the rasa of Radha and Krishna. According to Gaudiya Vaishnava teaching, the rasa-lila is a pastime of the Lord's internal energy (the hladini-shakti), pure prema, having nothing in common with mundane love: kama is the desire to please one's own senses, prema is the desire to give joy to Krishna; they differ as iron differs from gold (CC, Adi 4). Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati taught that the rasa-lila must not be understood with the mundane mind or imitated; one hears of it with faith under the guidance of pure devotees (cf. Pashchima, lahari 2, note 7). Here the rasa is enacted by boys as a sacred performance following the text of the Bhagavata (17, 31).
[^3]: "The refuge of the helpless" — *anātha-śaraṇa*. "The friend of Vallabha's life" — *ballabha-jībanabandhu*: Vallabha ("the beloved") is apparently Krishna, Gopivallabha (Dakshina, lahari 10, note 2); it may also be taken as "the beloved, the friend of [our] life". Durika is Shyamananda's mother (Purva, lahari 1, note 11). On how the author exalts Shyamananda — Purva, lahari 1, note 2; Purva, lahari 14, note 11.
[^4]: "Beautifully laid out" (2) — *subandhāna*. "Sandbank" — *pulina*: a sandy riverbank; cf. the sandbank of the Yamuna, the place of the rasa in the Bhagavata (10.29.45; 20 below). The Suvarnarekha is the river on whose bank Gopivallabhapur stands (Pashchima, lahari 1, note 16).
[^5]: Cf. Bhag. 10.33.3: the demigods in their celestial chariots watch the rasa (Pashchima, lahari 1, note 33). Rasikendra is Rasika.
[^6]: "Horns" — *biṣāṇa*: according to the editor's footnote, a shinga horn made of horn (the editor's footnotes on this page are not translated). "With the sound" — *cahale*: according to the editor's footnote, "with sound". "Shakes" — *kampabān*. Dundubhis (5, 7) are large drums, kettledrums (Dakshina, lahari 10, note 14). "Dulu-dulu" (7) is onomatopoeia; "trembles" — *tharahara*.
[^7]: The full moon of Vaishakha — the spring rasa (Pashchima, lahari 1, note 12). "That night is [like] eternity, there is no limit to it" — *cirakāla sei rātra nāhi tā'ra sīmā*; cf. Bhag. 10.33.38: the night of the rasa lasted a night of Brahma.
[^8]: The wish-fulfilling tree (10) — *kalpataru*: apparently the tree in the middle of the circle of the rasa (cf. Pashchima, lahari 1: the Deities by the wish-fulfilling tree at the eight corners of the circle). "Went up onto it" — *tā'ra madhye … giẏā*, lit. "going into its midst". "Remained" — *haila sannidhāna*. "Bent in three places" — *tribhaṅgima chānde*: the form of Krishna bent at the neck, waist and knee. In the performance Krishna is played by a boy (Pashchima, lahari 2, 47).
[^9]: "Murali" — the flute. "Gopis" — in the scan *gopā* (= *gopī*). Cf. Bhag. 10.29.3–4.
[^10]: "Bowing His head" — *heṭa māthā haiẏā*. "Leaning" — *āujiẏā*: according to the editor's footnote, "resting against". "Displeased" (15) — *birasa*. "Drawing on the ground" — *bhūmi lekhi*: cf. Bhag. 10.29.29 (the gopis, heads bowed, draw on the ground with their feet).
[^11]: "Sobbing" — *usasi usasi*. "Unaware of their bodies" — *nāhi deha-jñāna*. "The Lord — God, Bhagavan" — *prabhu deba bhagabān*: Krishna (in the performance, the boy playing Krishna).
[^12]: "In the order of the Bhagavata, according to its every rule" — *bhāgabata-anukrame yathā prati bidhi*: Rasika staged the rasa as a performance, following the text of the Bhagavata in order (10.29–33; cf. 31). "The treasury of virtues" — *guṇanidhi*.
[^13]: 18–25 are Krishna's words, a retelling of Bhag. 10.29.18–27. "To the forest" — *araṇa* (= *araṇya*). "Anxiously" (19) — *utkaṇṭhite*.
[^14]: "The children are crying" (21) — cf. Bhag. 10.29.22. 23–24 — cf. Bhag. 10.29.24–25. "As Vishnu" — *biṣṇu sama*. "Wander" — *bhrami bula*. According to the Bhagavata and Gaudiya Vaishnava teaching, with these words Krishna tests the gopis and reveals the depth of their prema, which has left everything for His sake (note 2).
[^15]: "All [the rasikas] who are like Rasika" — *rasika samāna saba*: understood as Rasika's companions, akin to him in their taste for the rasa; it may also be taken as "all, like Rasika, together with Rasika". 26–30 describe the kirtana and music around the performance.
[^16]: Pakhoyaj (*pākhoẏāja*) is the pakhavaj, a double-headed drum; the dampha is a frame drum (Dakshina, lahari 5, note 23); the mridanga and dhol are drums. "Raised a thunder" — *karila tumula*. After the first line of 27 the scan has «॥» instead of «।». Vina, venu, murali — a stringed instrument and flutes; the upanga (*upāṅga*) is apparently a wind instrument; the svara-mandala is a kind of zither (Dakshina, lahari 5, note 23).
[^17]: The kapinasha is a kind of vina; the saranga is a bowed instrument; the pinaka is a stringed instrument (Dakshina, lahari 5, note 23); the kinnari (*kinnarī*) is a kind of vina; the rabab is a stringed instrument; the madol (*mādola*) is a drum; the kansi (*kā̃si*) is a bronze gong (cymbal); the muchung (*mucuṅga*) is a jew's harp; the mahuri is a reed wind instrument of Utkala (Dakshina, lahari 10, note 14); kartal and mandiras are small cymbals. "The sweet panchama" — *pañcama rasāla*: understood as the fifth note of the scale (panchama), sounding especially sweet; it may also be taken as the name of an instrument. "Surrounding the gopis" — *gopī beṛi*: around the boys playing the gopis. "In many ways" — *bahu parakāra*.
[^18]: The five chapters on the rasa are the rasa-panchadhyaya, Bhagavata 10.29–33. "Someone sings" — *ke gāẏa*. "They have the boys enact the lila" — *līlā śiśure karāẏa*. Couplet 31 is the last on pdf 138 and complete; pdf 139 begins with ॥32॥.
[^19]: Continuation of lahari 3 (its beginning is on pdf 138, files RM-3-03-001-031); pdf 139 (printed p. 99) begins with ॥ 32 ॥. "Gait… posture… conduct" — *gati… bhaṅgī… ācaraṇa*: the boys reproduce the bearing and movements of Krishna and the gopis, following the one who sings the five chapters on the rasa from the Bhagavata (31; Bhag. 10.29–33). "Presents" — *nibedaẏa*, lit. "offers, presents". "Someone" — *kona jana*: it can also be understood as "each one". On the rasa as pure prema and on the boys' performance, see note 2 (cf. Dakshina, lahari 5, note 22).
[^20]: "Skilled" — *parabīṇa* (= *prabīṇa*). "In music and poetry" — *saṅgīta-sāhitye*: in singing and in the poetic (dramatic) art.
[^21]: "Are spellbound" — *moha pāẏa*, lit. "fall into a swoon".
[^22]: "Turning round" — *phiri*. "In the order of the Bhagavata" — *bhāgabata anukrama*: in 18–56 the author retells Bhag. 10.29.18–42 — Krishna's speech to the gopis (18–25; 10.29.18–27) and their reply (37–55; 10.29.29–41).
[^23]: "Harsh words" — *niṣṭhura bākya*: Krishna tells the gopis to return home to their husbands (18–25). "Heads bowed" — *heṭa māthe*.
[^24]: "With tears" — *lohe* (*loha*, "tears", = *lora*).
[^25]: Vamshidhara (*baṃśīdhara*) — "the Holder of the flute", a name of Krishna. "The rules of the Vedas" — *beda bidhi*: Krishna's instruction on a wife's duty (23–25). "Explanation" — *bibaraṇa*. "[I] shall tell" — *kahi*: the gopis speak now in the plural, now in the singular (cf. 47, 51, 54).
[^26]: "Indra and the other gods, the best of sages and others" — *debendrādi munīndrādi*. "Meditate" — *bhābe*. "Unattainable" — *durlabhe*, "hard to obtain".
[^27]: "May a thunderbolt strike" — *paṛu bāja*, lit. "may thunder fall": a curse.
[^28]: 48–50 form one thought: cf. Bhag. 10.29.32–33 — the gopis reply that the instruction on a wife's duty towards her husband and relatives is fulfilled in service to Krishna Himself, the Soul and the beloved of all. "The consideration of one's own dharma" — *sbadharmma bicāre*.
[^29]: "Destroyer" — *harttā*: it can also be understood as "the One who takes away [everything]". "Sustainer" — *pālana*, lit. "maintenance".
[^30]: "I performed" — *sādhinu*, first person.
[^31]: "Does not live" — *jiẏe* (so in the scan: «জিএে»). "Even a moment" — *tileka*. "Leaving all delusion" — *chāṛi saba mohe*: it can also be understood as "leaving all attachments".
[^32]: "The gopi will surely perish" — *abaśya maribe gopī*: cf. Bhag. 10.29.35 — the gopis say they will give up their bodies in the fire of separation. "There is no doubt" — *ithe nāhi āna*, lit. "in this there is nothing else".
[^33]: Cf. Bhag. 10.29.42. The Lotus-eyed One (*kamalalocana*) is Krishna.
[^34]: Gopala is Krishna. "In that same mood" — *sei bhābe*: the children represent how the gopis surround Krishna and dance with Him.
[^35]: "Joined together" — *eka mela kari*. "In merriment" — *kutūhale*.
[^36]: "Great awe" — *mahātrāsa*, lit. "great fear": understood as reverent trembling at the sight of a wonder.
[^37]: "A second Narayana" — the rapturous words of the onlookers (cf. Pashchima 2, 38: "a partial incarnation", and note 28 there). According to Gaudiya Vaishnava teaching, Rasikananda is a devotee and associate, not an incarnation of the Lord (Dakshina, lahari 5, note 26; Purva, lahari 1, note 69); his guru Shyamananda is a servant of Radha and Krishna (Purva, lahari 1, note 2; Purva, lahari 14, note 11).
[^38]: "Displayed" — *karilena*: the subject is not named; understood as Rasika (61). "With ten million mouths" — *koṭī mukhe*. The second line lit. "even describing with ten million mouths, that play would not come out [fully]".
[^39]: "By his grace" — *tā̃ra anugrahe*. The son of Achyuta is Rasika (Achyuta is his father; Purva, lahari 3, note 5). Cf. Pashchima 1, 66 and note 46 there: the author ascribes his inspiration to Rasika in his heart. "I have described" — *karilu barṇana*.
[^40]: "Full of sweetness" — *rasāla*, "juicy, full of rasa". "Cross over" — *tara*: understood as an imperative; it can also be understood as "cross over" in the third person ("all living beings cross").
[^41]: The colophon is on pdf 139 (printed p. 99), checked against the scan: «ইতি শ্রীরসিকমঙ্গল-পশ্চিম-বিভাগে রাস-বর্ণন-নাম তৃতীয়-লহরী সম্পূর্ণা» (*rāsa-barṇana*) — "The Description of the Rasa".
