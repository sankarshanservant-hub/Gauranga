# Pashchima-vibhaga. Lahari 8. The Deliverance of the Maddened Elephant

*A song in glory of Shyamananda; the subahdar, sitting on his terrace, orders the rajas to bring Rasika: let him give the Holy Name to the elephant ravaging Banapur, and if he survives he will be acknowledged as Narayana; the rajas despair, but Rasika, relying on Krishna's shelter, goes out to meet the elephant; his companions flee, and the subahdar repents of having summoned a sadhu to his doom; Rasika instructs the elephant to serve Krishna, reminding it of Indra and Gajendra; the elephant, gaining divine knowledge, falls at his feet, receives in its ear the sixteen names of "Hare Krishna" and the name Gopala Dasa, and, intoxicated with prema, goes off into the forest; all see Rasika's power, and Ahmed Beg falls at his feet and begs forgiveness; Rasika explains that Narayana, the maintainer of countless universes, dwells in the heart of every being, and so he did no violence to the elephant but gave it Krishna's name; the elephant gives up its wicked deeds, and the king of the yavanas rejoices*

*The eighth lahari.*

*Raga Kaushika.*[^1]
*Ghosha: "Glory, O glory to Rama and Krishna!*
*O Murari, O Murari, O Murari!"*

**1.** Glory, glory to Shyamananda, the treasury of compassion,
the supremely merciful Prabhu, the life of the world![^2]

**2.** At that time that wicked lord of the yavanas,
sitting on his terrace, summoned all the rulers.[^3]

**3.** To all the rajas the yavana ruler says:
"This elephant has today brought calamity on everyone.

**4.** Horses, elephants, men — it has killed many.
Make an effort so that it is stopped."[^4]

**5.** Then that yavana says to all the rajas:
"Bring Rasika-Murari-dasa here.

**6.** All the Hindus call him Narayana —
let him today give this elephant the Holy Name.[^5]

**7.** If the mahashaya comes through unharmed from the elephant,
then I shall surely acknowledge [him] as Narayana."[^6]

**8.** Hearing this, all the people were stunned;
great sorrow arose in everyone's heart.

**9.** All say: "Well, today [has come] utter ruin!
Why did Prabhu come to this villain?"[^7]

**10.** All remembered Krishna within;
among the rajas, in no one [, it seemed,] was there life left.[^8]

**11.** At that time Rasika, hearing these words,
declared: "I shall go to the subahdar without fail.

**12.** If I have truly taken shelter of Krishna, the Lord,
then what can a forest elephant do?"[^9]

**13.** Prabhu set out to see the subahdar;
with tears in his eyes he remembers Krishna.

**14.** Prabhu drew near the village
[and] saw: along the road comes an elephant crazed with mada.[^10]

**15.** Whomever it catches nearby it deprives of life;
seeing [its] fury, no one comes near.

**16.** Hearing the roar — a most dreadful sound —
[people think]: "What calamity has befallen the land of Banapur!"[^11]

**17.** They saw [it] coming along the road, like a mountain;
houses [and] doors it destroyed, broke to pieces.

**18.** Many people [and] horses it kills, mighty [as] a lion;
swinging its trunk, crazed, it goes unhurriedly.[^12]

**19.** All the people flee in fear of it;
today the elephant has destroyed many.

**20.** At that time Rasika comes along that road;
swiftly the untamable [elephant] came and stood face to face with him.[^13]

**21.** Seeing [this], the companions in fear say to Rasika:
"Come, let us flee into the town!

**22.** This elephant's ferocity cannot be told;
in an instant it will take the lives of all at once."[^14]

**23.** Prabhu did not listen to anyone's words;
standing, he remembers the name of Krishna.

**24.** The companions fled, [saving] their lives in panic;
stumbling and falling, all rushed far off into the wilds.[^15]

**25.** Prabhu remained alone, with joy in his heart;
without timidity, without fear he chants the Holy Name.[^16]

**26.** Staying on his terrace, the fierce yavana watches;
within he prays: "Protect him, Narayana!

**27.** Without cause I summoned a sadhu here;
I have become a partaker in the killing of a sadhu — this I fully understand.[^17]

**28.** The mahashaya has fallen into the clutches of this untamable [elephant];
today I shall surely know [whether] that promise of Krishna [holds]."[^18]

**29.** Thus that yavana ruler thinks within himself;
all the Hindu rajas are in turmoil:

**30.** most wicked is this yavana ruler:
by force he has summoned the son of Achyuta;

**31.** most mighty is the wicked, untamable elephant, —
[and] within [they] remember Krishna.[^19]

**32.** Everyone weeps with one another;
seeing this, the companions are barely alive.[^20]

**33.** With joy in his heart, with tears in his eyes, Prabhu
again and again remembers: "Krishna, Krishna."[^21]

**34.** When the maddened elephant came face to face [with him],
the elephant saw with its own eyes the beautiful Rasika.

**35.** Seeing Rasika, the elephant at once stops;
looking into its face, Rasika-shekhara says:[^22]

**36.** "Listen, listen, O maddened elephant!
Serve Krishna, serve the sadhus unceasingly.

**37.** Why do you ruin yourself in vain, doing all kinds of wicked deeds?
Everything apart from Krishna is wasted labour.[^23]

**38.** Chant [the name of] Krishna, meditate on Krishna, give your mind to Krishna;
becoming wholly devoted, serve Krishna's feet.

**39.** All the scriptures in essence speak of the service of Krishna;
giving up ignorance, serve Krishna's feet.[^24]

**40.** All that you see apart from Krishna is not your own;
from today do not do wicked deeds any more.

**41.** Krishna is mother, Krishna is father, Krishna is life itself;
not serving Krishna, why perish for nothing?

**42.** To serve Krishna, do not be intoxicated [and] proud:
the pride of Airavata [and] Indra [He] swiftly crushed.[^25]

**43.** Once a crocodile seized the best of the kings of elephants;
destroying the crocodile, [the Lord] delivered the elephant.[^26]

**44.** Prabhu, the divine Bhagavan, is an ocean of mercy;
giving up intoxication [and] pride, meditate on Prabhu Krishna.

**45.** Hearing the words of a sadhu, my child, give your mind to Krishna;
the Son of Nanda will be merciful to you."[^27]

**46.** As the fruit of former austerities [came] the sight of Rasika;
at the sight [its] heart melted, [and] it gazes intently [at him].[^28]

**47.** It saw Rasika as if a second Narayana;
with tears in its eyes the elephant thinks within itself:

**48.** "This person is not a man, [but] a portion of Narayana;
the truth he has told me is the conclusion of the scriptures."[^29]

**49.** Divine knowledge dawned in the elephant's heart;
it knew for certain that Krishna is the truth.[^30]

**50.** Hearing Rasika's words, the maddened elephant
quickly fell at Rasika's feet.

**51.** Laying its head at [his] holy feet, with joy in its heart,
it washed Rasika's feet with tears.

**52.** Into the elephant's right ear Rasika-shekhara
made the name of Krishna heard, placing his hand on its head.

**53.** The sixteen names of "Hare Krishna," thirty-two syllables,
Rasika-shekhara made heard in the elephant's ear.[^31]

**54.** Hearing Krishna's name, the elephant quickly rose
[and] prostrated its body on the ground at [his] feet.

**55.** Circumambulating [him], it bows down;
in hundreds of streams [tears] flow from the elephant's eyes.[^32]

**56.** Seeing Rasika's form, enchanted at heart,
the maddened elephant gazes intently.

**57.** In the waves of [his] beauty [its] eyes sank into an ocean;
intoxicated with prema, it forgets itself.[^33]

**58.** Then [Rasika] gave it the name Sri Gopala Dasa;
hearing it, the elephant falls at [his] feet again and again.[^34]

**59.** With tears it washed [his] two feet;
it itself saw [him] in the form of Narayana.[^35]

**60.** It has no wish to go away;
with firm faith it took shelter of Rasika.

**61.** Apart from Rasika's feet nothing else pleases it;
it knew for certain: all is false, Krishna is the truth.[^36]

**62.** Staying a moment, the elephant, having bowed,
all in tears [and] trembling, slowly goes away.

**63.** Placing Rasika's lotus feet in its heart,
having gained divine knowledge, it goes, intoxicated with prema.[^37]

**64.** The king of elephants, wholly devoted, went [and] entered the forest;
with its whole soul it took shelter of Rasika.[^38]

**65.** Everyone comes and sees Rasika —
rajas, subjects, Hindus and others, the host of yavanas.

**66.** All the people saw [the power] manifested by Rasika;
seeing [these] signs, all deemed [it] a wonder.[^39]

**67.** Seeing [this], the men and women were astonished;
all recognized Rasika as a portion of Narayana.[^40]

**68.** All the people say to one another:
"The maddened elephant fell at Rasika's feet!"

**69.** Rasika-shekhara gave a name to the maddened elephant;
this news spread widely in all four directions.[^41]

**70.** Hearing it, all the people went to see Rasika:
rajas, subjects, children, the old, women — crowd after crowd.[^42]

**71.** All the crowds of yavanas come and look;
the subahdar too came quickly, seeing the manifestation [of power].[^43]

**72.** In his heart the ruler of the yavanas was greatly afraid:
"For no reason at all I have offended Rasika!"[^44]

**73.** Coming, he presented himself before Rasika;
Ahmed Beg, coming, fell at [his] feet.[^45]

**74.** With joined palms the subahdar says to Rasika:
"I bring one plea to your feet.

**75.** I did not know that you are Ishvara Himself, in person;
that is why I sent for you, to have [you] brought.[^46]

**76.** Such a wicked deed I did in ignorance;
forgive [my] offence — [I] take refuge in you.

**77.** You are an ocean of mercy, a treasury of compassion;
you are the stronghold of refuge, the life of the world.[^47]

**78.** Bewildered by your maya, O Prabhu,
I offended [you], not knowing your greatness."

**79.** Hearing such humble words from the subahdar,
Prabhu, with tears in his eyes, began to speak:

**80.** "Listen, listen, O ruler of the yavanas:
I came in order to see you.

**81.** On the road [I] saw an untamable elephant;
the elephant rushed swiftly [at me] to kill me.[^48]

**82.** The elephant rushed swiftly to kill;
seeing it untamable, I remembered Krishna.

**83.** In all bodies dwells the one Lord, Bhagavan;
in the hearts of all living beings dwells the Treasury of compassion.[^49]

**84.** Patala, Sutala, Vitala, Rasatala,
the abode of Mahakurma, Mahatala, Atitala;

**85.** beneath them is the place of Padma, such-and-such a place;
[thus] the seven lower worlds — there is evidence for it.[^50]

**86.** Bhurloka, Bhuvarloka, Svarloka — three places,
Janaloka, Tapoloka — [so] the shastras testify;

**87.** above them are Maharloka, Brahmaloka and the rest:
these seven worlds above — [so] it is known.[^51]

**88.** An ordinary universe is these fourteen worlds;
in it are one Brahma, Indra [and] all the gods.[^52]

**89.** Be it seven worlds or a thousand worlds —
thus creating the universes,

**90.** hundreds of thousands, endless worlds and more —
each and every universe Hari creates in this way.[^53]

**91.** Four-faced, hundred-faced, ten-million-faced Brahmas,
and likewise the Indras — who can set [them] a limit?[^54]

**92.** Countless millions of universes exist in this way;
all of them arise in the pores of Narayana's skin.[^55]

**93.** Such is Narayana — the original light, the spotless one;
the Lord maintains all that moves and does not move.[^56]

**94.** In countless millions of universes, the unmoving and the moving:
insects, beasts, birds, worms, those born as humans —

**95.** all the moving, the unmoving and the rest, as many as there are in the endless worlds —
in the heart of all, in a subtle form, dwells Narayana.[^57]

**96.** Dwelling in the hearts of all, He maintains dharma;
the Destroyer of adharma is the god Narayana.

**97.** Age after age He establishes dharma;
all the wicked, devoid of dharma, He destroys.[^58]

**98.** Whoever attacks someone is attacked by him in turn;
whoever attacks one who does no harm will never be delivered.[^59]

**99.** So in the elephant's heart too is Narayana,
the Indwelling Lord of all, the Lord, one and spotless.

**100.** I never did it any harm;
seeing [him], I said: 'Remember Krishna.'

**101.** Hearing Krishna's name, the elephant fell at [my] feet;
with its tears it washed my feet.

**102.** Into the elephant's ear I spoke Krishna's mantra [and] Name;
circling [me], it went off to its own place."[^60]

**103.** That king of elephants gave up its wicked deeds;
hearing [this], the king of the yavanas rejoiced.[^61]

**104.** Listen, listen, all people, to the Rasika-mangala:
Rasika's pastimes are a wonder [to] the three worlds.

**105.** [His] pastimes are a boundless ocean: who can know [them]?
[Only] by Rasika's mercy is something revealed to me.[^62]

**106.** Adorning himself with Shyamananda's feet,
the son of Rasamaya joyfully composed [this].

*Thus in the Pashchima-vibhaga of the Sri Rasika-mangala ends the eighth lahari, called "The Deliverance of the Maddened Elephant."*[^63]

[^2]: "Treasury of compassion" — *karuṇānidhāna*. "The life of the world" — *jagatera prāṇa*.
[^3]: 2–10 repeat and develop the account of lahari 7, 65–75 (Rasika summoned to the subahdar). "Lord of the yavanas" — *yabana-adhipati*: the subahdar Ahmed Beg (Pashchima, lahari 7, 27). "On his terrace" — *jagatīte*: on a raised platform or terrace (Pashchima, lahari 7, note 35). "Rulers" — *nṛpati*: the Hindu rajas (3).
[^4]: "Make an effort" — *karaha yatana*. "So that it is stopped" — *kemane nibṛtta haẏa*, lit. "how [it might] cease".
[^5]: Rasika-Murari-dasa is Rasika. "Call him Narayana" — cf. Pashchima, lahari 7, 56–60, 65. "Let him … give" — *dibena*, honorific future: taken as a demand and a test.
[^6]: "Comes through unharmed from the elephant" — *hātī haite uturena*, lit. "comes across (gets through) from the elephant": remains unharmed. "Mahashaya" (*mahāśaẏa*, "great soul") is an honorific: Rasika.
[^7]: "Stunned" (8) — *camakita*. "Utter ruin" — *sarbbanāśa*. Prabhu is Rasika.
[^8]: "In no one [, it seemed,] was there life left" — *kāhāra dehete prāṇa nāi*, lit. "in no one's body is there life": the rajas were half dead with fear.
[^9]: "Declared" (11) — *ājñā kaila*, lit. "commanded". "Without fail" — *sarbbathā*. 12: "Truly" — *niśce* (= *niścaẏa*). "Krishna, the Lord" — *kṛṣṇapati*. Cf. Krishna's promise (note 18).
[^10]: "Drew near the village" — *parabeśa hailā … grāmera samīpe*, lit. "entered the vicinity of the village": Banapur. "Crazed with mada" — *mada-matta* (Pashchima, lahari 7, note 55). "Elephant" — *kari*.
[^11]: "Calamity" — *pramāda*. The subject of the second line is not expressed; understood as the people's exclamation.
[^12]: 17: "Coming" — *āse* (so in the scan; OCR *bhāse*). "Like a mountain" — *parbbata samāna*. 18: "Mighty [as] a lion" — *bikramī keśarī*, lit. "a mighty lion". "Swinging its trunk" — *śuṇḍa phirāiẏā*. "Unhurriedly" — *dhīri dhīri*.
[^13]: "Untamable" — *duranta*, "unruly, ferocious" (so throughout). "Stood face to face with him" — *karila sākṣāte*.
[^14]: "In an instant" — *ekatile*, lit. "in [one] sesame seed". "At once" — *eka ṭhā̃ẏa*, lit. "in one place".
[^15]: "In panic" — *prāṇera bikale*. "Stumbling and falling" — *uṭhu paṛu hañā*, lit. "rising [and] falling" (checked against the scan). "Far off into the wilds" — *tepāntare*: into distant open, deserted country.
[^16]: "Without timidity" — *nā saṅkoca*. "Chants" — *laẏa*, lit. "takes".
[^17]: 26–28 are the subahdar's thoughts. "Fierce" — *duranta* (note 13), here of the subahdar. "Protect" — *rakṣa*. 27: "Here" — *hethā*. "A partaker in the killing of a sadhu" — *sādhu-badhabhāgī*: one who bears the sin of killing a sadhu. "Fully" — *sarbbathā*.
[^18]: "Has fallen into the clutches" — *hāte ṭhekilena*, lit. "got caught in the hand"; *hāta* ("hand") may also hint at the trunk. "That promise of Krishna" — *kṛṣṇa-pratijñā*: cf. Bhagavad-gita 9.31: "O son of Kunti, declare it boldly: My devotee never perishes" (*kaunteya pratijānīhi na me bhaktaḥ praṇaśyati*).
[^19]: 29–31 are taken as one sentence: 30–31 are the thoughts of the Hindu rajas. "Thinks within himself" — *mane mane kare*. 30: "By force" — *haṭha kari'*. The son of Achyuta is Rasika. 31: "Elephant" — *kuñjara* (garbled in the OCR, checked against the scan).
[^20]: "With one another" — *eka āre*. "Barely alive" — *nā dhare parāṇe*, lit. "cannot hold on to life".
[^21]: "Again and again" — *ghane ghane*. "Remembers" — *sumaraṇa kare* (= *smaraṇa*).
[^22]: "Stops" — *dā̃ṛāẏa*, "stands, stops" (garbled in the OCR, checked against the scan). Rasika-shekhara is Rasika.
[^23]: 36–45 are Rasika's instruction to the elephant. 37: "Ruin yourself" — *mara*, lit. "perish" (so also in 41). "Wasted labour" — *byartha pariśrama*.
[^24]: "In essence" — *tattve*. "Ignorance" — *abidyā*: avidya, the ignorance that binds the soul to the material world.
[^25]: "Do not be intoxicated [and] proud" — *mada garbba nā kariha*: *mada* is both the intoxication of pride and the elephant's mada (note 10); a play on words. Airavata is Indra's elephant. Krishna crushed Indra's pride by lifting Govardhana Hill (Bhag. 10.24–27); when taking the parijata tree Krishna defeated Indra, who fought mounted on Airavata (Bhag. 10.59). "Crushed" — *nāśilā*.
[^26]: The deliverance of Gajendra, the king of elephants, seized in the water by a crocodile: the Lord, on whom the elephant called, killed the crocodile and saved him (Bhag. 8.2–4). "The best of the kings of elephants" — *gajarājabare*.
[^27]: 44: "The divine Bhagavan" — *deba bhagavān*. 45: "My child" — *bāpu*: an affectionate address. The Son of Nanda is Krishna.
[^28]: "The fruit of former austerities" — *pūrbba-tapasyāra phale*: apparently the piety of the elephant's past lives (cf. Gajendra, in his past life King Indradyumna; Bhag. 8.4). "Melted" — *drabila*.
[^29]: 47–48 give the elephant's perception: "as if a second Narayana" (*dbitīẏa nārāẏaṇa*), "a portion of Narayana" (*aṃśa nārāẏaṇa*). On how the author exalts Rasika, and on the Gaudiya Vaishnava view of Rasika as a devotee and disciple of Shyamananda, see Pashchima, lahari 7, note 36; Purva, lahari 1, note 2; Purva, lahari 14, note 11. "Person" — *puruṣa*. "The conclusion of the scriptures" — *śāstra nirūpaṇa*: what the scriptures establish.
[^30]: "Divine knowledge" — *dibyajñāna*. "Knew that Krishna is the truth" — *kṛṣṇa satya kariẏā … jānila*.
[^31]: Making the name of Krishna heard in the right ear is the rite of initiation into the Holy Name. 53: the maha-mantra "Hare Krishna Hare Krishna Krishna Krishna Hare Hare / Hare Rama Hare Rama Rama Rama Hare Hare" — sixteen names, thirty-two syllables (Purva, lahari 2, note 11). Cf. CC, Madhya 17: in the forests of Jharikhanda, by Sri Chaitanya's mercy, tigers, deer and elephants chanted the name of Krishna. "Placing his hand on its head" — *muṇḍe diẏā kara*.
[^32]: 54: "Prostrated its body on the ground" — *daṇḍabata kāẏa kṣiti* (Pashchima, lahari 5, note 45). 55: "Circumambulating [him]" — *parikramā kari'*: a reverent circling. "Bows down" — *paraṇāme* (= *praṇāma*). "Streams" — *dhārā*.
[^33]: "Enchanted at heart" — *mugudha antara*. 57: "In the waves of [his] beauty" — *rūpera hillole*; "sank into an ocean" — *paṛilā pāthāre*, lit. "fell into the boundless waters". "Intoxicated with prema" — *premamaẏe matta haiẏā*; it may also be understood as "intoxicated with the One full of prema", i.e. Rasika.
[^34]: Sri Gopala Dasa (*śrīgopāla dāsa*) is the name given to the elephant at initiation; in BR 15, 84 (Pashchima, lahari 7, note 19) the elephant's name is not given. The subject of the first line is not expressed; understood as Rasika. "Again and again" — *katabāra*, "how many times".
[^35]: "Two feet" — *caraṇa-dukhāni*. "It itself saw" — *dekhila āpani*: the elephant saw Rasika in the form of Narayana (note 29).
[^36]: 60: "Has no wish" — *nāhi laẏa mana*. "With firm faith" — *dṛṛha biśbāsete*. 61: "Pleases" — *bhāẏa*. "All is false, Krishna is the truth" — *saba mithyā kṛṣṇa satya*: everything transient without Krishna is worthless.
[^37]: 62: "All in tears [and] trembling" — *aśru-pulakita*: tears and trembling (hair standing on end) are signs of prema. 63: "Having gained divine knowledge" — *dibyajñāna haiẏā*.
[^38]: "The king of elephants, wholly devoted" — *gajendra ananya*. "With its whole soul" — *sarbbātmabhābe* (= *sarvātma-bhāve*; abbreviated in the scan, garbled in the OCR).
[^39]: "[The power] manifested by Rasika" — *rasikera prakāśa*, "Rasika's manifestation". "Signs" — *lakṣaṇa*: marks [of his power]. "Deemed [it] a wonder" — *adbhuta mānila*.
[^40]: "A portion of Narayana" — *aṃśa nārāyaṇa*: the crowd's rapturous acclamation (cf. 48 and note 29: on how the author exalts Rasika, and on the Gaudiya Vaishnava view of him as a devotee and disciple of Shyamananda; Pashchima, lahari 7, note 36; Purva, lahari 1, note 2; Purva, lahari 14, note 11). Likewise 75–78.
[^41]: "Gave a name" — *nāma dila*: the name Sri Gopala Dasa (58, note 34). "News" — *śabada* (*śabda*), "word, report".
[^42]: "Women" — *strīri* (so in the scan). "Crowd after crowd" — *yūthe yūthe*.
[^43]: "Manifestation [of power]" — *prakāśa*, "manifestation": the taming of the elephant (66, note 39).
[^44]: "For no reason at all" — *akāraṇe*. "Offended" — *biṛambana* (likewise 78, *biṛambinu*): "humiliation, harassment".
[^45]: "Presented himself" — *upasana* (so in the scan; apparently *upasanna*, "having approached"). Ahmed Beg is the subahdar of Banapur (lahari 6, 17; lahari 7, 27). The subahdar's submission — cf. BR 15, 85: "That villain, the yavana king, bowed down [before him]." The author exalts Rasika with accounts of miracles (lahari 7, note 41).
[^46]: 75–78 are the subahdar's words: "Ishvara Himself, in person" (*īśbara sākṣāte*), "your maya" (78, *tomāra māẏāte*) — the yavana ruler's rapturous avowal; cf. note 40.
[^47]: "Stronghold of refuge" — *śaraṇa-pañjara*, lit. "enclosure (cage) of refuge", i.e. sure protection.
[^48]: "Untamable" — *duranta* (note 13; likewise 82; in 76 "wicked deed" — *duranta karmma*). "Elephant" — *kuñjara*.
[^49]: "In all bodies" — *saba ghaṭe*, lit. "in all vessels". Cf. BG 18.61 (*īśvaraḥ sarva-bhūtānāṃ hṛd-deśe 'rjuna tiṣṭhati*).
[^50]: 84–87 are the fourteen worlds. The usual list (cf. Bhagavata 5.24): lower — Atala, Vitala, Sutala, Talatala, Mahatala, Rasatala, Patala; upper — Bhur, Bhuvar, Svar, Mahar, Jana, Tapa, Satya (Brahmaloka). The author lists them differently: instead of Talatala, "the abode of Mahakurma" (*mahākūrmma sthāna*, the Great Tortoise) and "the place of Padma" (*padma sthāna*, 85); *atitala* is apparently Atala. "Such-and-such a place" — *sei kona sthāna*: the sense is unclear. "Lower worlds" — *bhubanatala*.
