# Uttara-vibhaga. Lahari 3. The Description of the Crushing of the Pride of Uddanda the Bhuiyan and of the People of Hijli

*A song in glory of Shyamananda; Rasika stays in the village and at his pleasure composes thirteen padas on prema for Krishna; he announces to Uddanda the bhuiyan Shyamananda's order to settle the three mothers together in Sri Shyamanandapur, but the bhuiyan in his pride will not give up Vrindavanachandra; the angered Rasika vows not to drink water in his land, foretells that Uddanda will be no more on earth, and leaves home alone, but Shyamananda's voice stops him: "I have struck him down; take Vrindavanachandra and unite [everyone]"; in the guise of a Vrajavasi Rasika lives unrecognised in Mayna, initiates the brothers Chandrabhanu and Shyamarasika and others, and astonishes the learned, until Vamshidasa reveals to all who he is; in Hijli the scholars led by Mimamsa-mandana cannot answer him and revile Shyamananda; by a letter Rasika vows not to drink water in Hijli and foretells that they will all leave the body and, born again, receive the Harinama from him; the mimamsaka who ordered the letter tied to a dog's neck perishes, torn by a dog, and within half a year the others perish too; born again, they receive the Harinama from Rasika, and all Hijli turns to Krishna*

*The third lahari.*

*Raga Kamoda — panchali metre.*[^1]

**1.** Glory, glory to Shyamananda, revered in all the worlds,
the life of the world, the enchanter of hearts!
Dear to those who have nothing, the treasure of Rasika's life,
the abode of compassion, descended [into the world]![^2]

**2.** Thus for some days in that village
Rasikendra stayed, at his pleasure.
There thirteen padas — songs of the virtues of prema for Krishna —
Rasika composed in play.[^3]

**3.** Thus after some days to Uddanda the bhuiyan
he spoke of the reason for [his] departure:
"On my head is an order: the three mothers — together,
in the place Sri Shyamanandapur."[^4]

**4.** Hearing, the bhuiyan grew angry, disregarded Prabhu's order
[and] uttered proud words with his mouth:
"Is there anyone so able [as to] take Vrindavanachandra
while I am on the earth?"[^5]

**5.** Hearing the bhuiyan's words, Rasikendra-mani in anger
rose, remembering Krishna:
"I will not drink water here — as far as this land extends," —
[and] Prabhu left, overcome by anger.[^6]

**6.** "If Shyamananda-raya truly gave me the order,
Vrindavanachandra will come [to me].
If there is mercy upon me, the Lord will not remain there,
[and] Uddanda will not remain on the earth."[^7]

**7.** Having declared [this], Prabhu left; great grief arose [in him]:
"No longer will I stay in this land:
this pashanda has broken Shyamananda's order!" —
[and] in his strong emotion he went far away.[^8]

**8.** Leaving all his household, he went out alone,
[keeping] in his heart Shyamananda's lotus feet.
Prabhu had gone some distance [when] suddenly a voice arose:
Shyamananda began to speak.[^9]

**9.** "Over what trifle have you become so stirred?
I have destroyed him.
Taking Vrindavanachandra, go [and] unite [everyone] together;
go no further, my child, no further."[^10]

**10.** Hearing the order with his ears, Prabhu went no further;
turning back, he stopped at Mayna.
Prabhu went about alone, in the guise of a Vrajavasi —
no one could recognise [him].[^11]

**11.** Beginning with Chandrabhanu, [many] became disciples of Sri Murari;
he gave initiation to Shyamarasika.
Seeing the two brothers, Prabhu became very happy
[and] poured forth talks of Krishna.[^12]

**12.** Hearing the words from his holy mouth, [even] Brihaspati falls silent;
all are amazed.
The host of scholars says: "Is this not Narayana?
Who has the power to challenge him?"[^13]

**13.** Thus night and day, in search of talks of Krishna, —
no one in that village recognised [him].
After some days Vamshidasa came [and] met [him]
[and] bowed down to Rasika.

**14.** He tells everyone: "The one [living] here alone is Prabhu,
Rasika-shekhara-chudamani."
Hearing, all in joy, with heads bowed from shame [and] fear,
fell to the ground at [his] feet.[^14]

**15.** From there Prabhu went, taking Vamshidasa with him,
[and] arrived at the town of Hijli.
Sadashiva, Uddhava and others, Mimamsa-mandana and others
brought that Shyamasundara there.[^15]

**16.** Rasikananda held talks of Krishna with all,
expounded the six shastras.
All the scholars, as many as there were, were astonished:
they could not challenge [him].[^16]

**17.** Unable [to do so], the brahmanas reviled Shyamananda.
Hearing [this], Prabhu quickly rose:
"In all the villages of Hijli I will not drink water," —
[and] he wrote a letter to all:

**18.** "All will leave this body [and], coming again, will be born;
taking [them] on my lap, [I] will give [them] the Harinama."
A servant brought [and] delivered the letter; [it] was read out in the midst of the assembly.
Hearing [it], the ignorant mimamsaka says:[^17]

**19.** "Tie the letter to a dog's neck — that is my opinion."
Hearing [this], all stopped their ears.
The mimamsaka rose [and] went; suddenly a dog met [him]
[and] fiercely began to tear at his eyes [and] face.[^18]

**20.** Howling like a dog, the "lion among brahmanas" took to his heels
[and] right there gave up his life.
Sadashiva, Uddhava and others, that Shyamasundara and others —
within six months all perished.[^19]

**21.** Having clashed with Rasika, no one finds protection:
fathomless, boundless is that glory.
[His] vow is to crush the asuras; to fallen people he shows mercy;
in the three worlds he has no equal.[^20]

**22.** Seeing [this], all are amazed [and] say: "A partial avatara!"
As he had ordained in play, [so it came to pass]:
all were born again, [and] then he gave [them] the Harinama,
held talks of Krishna with all.[^21]

**23.** All the people of Hijli turned to Krishna,
seeing the manifestation [of the power] of Rasika.
All had devotion in love; all turned their minds to Krishna:
the firm hope of all was fulfilled.[^22]

**24.** Such is Prabhu Rasikendra, the unyielding Nagara-chandra:
to all living beings he gives prema.
He is the life [and] soul dear to those who have nothing, the deliverance from the terrible [age of] Kali:
not even with a hundred mouths can [he] be described.[^23]

**25.** Listen to the "Rasika-mangala," devotees of Krishna of the three worlds —
the word of Rasika's good fame.
Adorning himself with Shyamananda's feet,
the son of Rasamaya joyfully composed [this].[^24]

*Thus in the Uttara-vibhaga of the Sri Rasika-mangala ends the third lahari, called "The Description of the Crushing of the Pride of Uddanda the Bhuiyan and of the People of Hijli."*

"Panchali metre" (*chanda pā̃cālī*) is the tripadi; a stanza is set in four lines in the edition and is translated likewise (Pashchima, lahari 6, note 1). There is no ghosha and no mangala-shloka; the stanzas are numbered as in the edition. Lahari 3 comprises stanzas 1–25 (pdf 182–184); the summary line covers the whole lahari (23–25 from the scan of pdf 184, provisionally). Stanza 5 begins on pdf 182 and ends at the top of pdf 183.
[^2]: "Revered in all the worlds" — *akhila bhubanabandya*. "Dear to those who have nothing" — *priẏajana akiñcana*; it can also be understood as "whose dear ones are those who have nothing." "Descended [into the world]" — *abatari*: the author speaks of Shyamananda as one who descended from above; on the author's attitude to Shyamananda and Rasika — Purva, lahari 1, note 2; Purva, lahari 14, note 11.
[^3]: "In that village" — apparently in Govindapur (lahari 2, 44, 61). "At his pleasure" — *āpanāra sukhe*. "Thirteen padas" — *trayodaśa pada*: songs by Rasika; not identified. "Songs of the virtues of prema for Krishna" — *kṛṣṇaprema-guṇa-gāthā*. "In play" — *kautuke*.
[^4]: Uddanda the bhuiyan (*uddaṇḍa bhū̃ñā*) is apparently Uddanda-raya, the bhuiyan of Nrisimhapur, a disciple of Shyamananda (Dakshina, lahari 16, note 24; Pashchima, lahari 13, note 15). "The reason for [his] departure" — *gamana-kāraṇe*. "On my head is an order" — *ājñā āche mora māthe*: "I carry an order" (lahari 2, 47–54). "The three mothers" — the three Thakuranis, apparently Shyamananda's wives (lahari 2, note 27). Sri Shyamanandapur (*śrīśyāmānandapura*, so in the scan) is apparently the same as Sri Shyamasundarpur (lahari 2, 49, note 28; so also in lahari 4, 11, pdf 184).
[^5]: "Disregarded Prabhu's order" — *prabhu ājñā helā kailā*: Prabhu here is Shyamananda. "Proud words" — *sagarbba bāṇī*. From 4 it appears that Vrindavanachandra was with Uddanda; where and since when is not said. "While I am on the earth" — *pṛthibīte mui se thākite*.
[^6]: Rasikendra-mani is Rasika. "Remembering" — *saṅariẏā* (= *smariẏā*). "I will not drink water here" — *nā khāiba ethā jala*: refusing to drink water in someone's domain means breaking off all dealings (cf. 17). "As far as this land extends" — *yatadūra ihā sthala*. "Overcome by anger" — *krodhābeśa haiẏā*.
[^7]: 6 is Rasika's speech. Shyamananda-raya is Shyamananda. "Will come [to me]" — *āsibena*: the Deity Himself will come to Rasika. "The Lord will not remain there" — *prabhu tathā nā rahibe*: apparently of Vrindavanachandra (the Deity will not remain with Uddanda); it can also be understood of Shyamananda. "Uddanda will not remain on the earth" is a prediction of his end (cf. 9; lahari 4, 3–4, pdf 184).
[^8]: "Having declared" — *ājñā kari*, lit. "having commanded." Pashanda (*pāṣaṇḍa*) — an unbeliever, a blasphemer. "In his strong emotion" — *anurāge*; cf. *anurāgī* (9): *anurāga* usually means loving attachment; here it is apparently strong feeling — grief and resolve to leave the land. "Went far away" — *calilā biśeṣe*; the sense is approximate.
[^9]: "All his household" — *saba paribāra*; it can also be understood as "all his family, all his retinue." "Went out" — *hailā bāhāra*. By this time Shyamananda had already left this world (Pashchima, lahari 13); the voice that suddenly arose is his (cf. Dakshina, lahari 16, note 28: Shyamananda appears at night to Uddanda). In the Vaishnava understanding, the Lord's associates continue to guide their disciples even after their disappearance; on the author's attitude to Shyamananda — Purva, lahari 1, note 2.
[^10]: "Over what trifle" — *kona chāra kathā lāgi*. "So stirred" — *anurāgī* (note 8). "I have destroyed him" — *tā're mui karinu saṃhāra*: of Uddanda (cf. lahari 4, 3, pdf 184: Uddanda perished with all his line). "Unite [everyone] together" — *ekatra karaha*: the Deities and the three Thakuranis in one place (lahari 2, 48–54). "My child" — *bāpu*, an affectionate address.
[^11]: "Turning back" — *phiriẏā*. Mayna (*maẏanā*, checked against the scan, crop ×2) is apparently Moyna in Medinipur, north of Hijli. "Stopped" — *uttarilā*. "Alone" — *ekeśbara*. "In the guise of a Vrajavasi" — *brajabāsī beśa*: as a wanderer from Vraja.
[^12]: Chandrabhanu (*candrabhānu*) and Shyamarasika (*śyāmarasika*) are apparently "the two brothers" (11b); persons not identified; cf. "the renunciate Shyamarasika" among the disciples (Pashchima, lahari 14, 128) — whether he is the same is unclear. Sri Murari is Rasika (Rasika-Murari). 11a — *candrabhānu ādi kari, śiṣya hailā śrīmurāri*: it can also be understood as "…became disciples; Sri Murari gave initiation…". "Poured forth" — *udgāre*, lit. "pouring out."
[^13]: Brihaspati is the preceptor of the gods, the model of learning and eloquence. "Falls silent" — *tūṇī* (the editor explains: *tuṣṇī*, "silent"). "Is this not Narayana?" — the scholars' rapturous words (cf. 22). "Challenge" — *samasyā karite*: *samasyā* is a hard question, a problem posed in a learned dispute (cf. 16).
[^14]: "In search of talks of Krishna" (13) — *kṛṣṇakathā anbeṣaṇe* (lahari 2, 24). Vamshidasa is the author's uncle, Rasamaya's brother (Pashchima, lahari 7, note 17). "Bowed down" — *karaẏe paraṇāme*. "The one [living] here alone" (14) — *ekeśbara prabhu ethā*; it can also be understood as "Prabhu is here alone [without companions]." Rasika-shekhara-chudamani, "the crest-jewel of Rasika-shekharas," is Rasika. "With heads bowed" — *hẽṭamāthe*: from shame at not having recognised him.
[^15]: Hijli is a coastal region and town in south-eastern Medinipur (Purva, lahari 10, note 21). Sadashiva — cf. Sadashiva, brother of Balabhadra, the ruler of Hijli (ibid.); whether he is the same is not said. Uddhava is not identified here. Mimamsa-mandana (*mīmāṃsā maṇḍana*, "ornament of Mimamsa") is apparently a Mimamsa scholar (in 18–19 — *mīmāṃsā*); cf. Purva, lahari 8, note 5, where Rasika's teacher is so called. 15b — *tathā lailā se śyāmasundare*: the sense is approximate; Shyamasundara is apparently a scholar of Hijli (20), brought to dispute with Rasika; it can also be understood as "[Rasika] took Shyamasundara there with him."
[^16]: "Expounded the six shastras" — *ṣaṭśāstra prakāśa karilā*: the six philosophical systems (Purva, lahari 9, note 12). "Were astonished" — *camakita*. "Could not challenge" — *samasyā dibāre nā pārilā* (note 13).
[^17]: "Reviled Shyamananda" (17) — *nindā kaila śyāmānande*: according to Vaishnava teaching, blasphemy of a Vaishnava, and all the more of the guru, is the gravest offence (vaishnava-aparadha; CC, Madhya 19). "A letter" — *ekapatra*, "one letter." 18a is the text of the letter: Rasika foretells that they will leave the body and that in their next birth he will accept them as his children and give them the Harinama (fulfilled — 22). "Read out" — *prakāśila*. "The ignorant mimamsaka" — *mīmāṃsā-ajñāna*: Mimamsa-mandana (note 15).
[^18]: "Tie the letter to a dog's neck" — *śbāna gale bāndha patra*: a sign of utter contempt. "Stopped their ears" — *hāta dila karṇe*: so as not to hear the offence. "Met" — *melā*. 19b — *dṛṛhe tā'ra ā̃khi mukha jhune* (so in the scan, crop ×2): the sense of *jhune* is unclear; taken as "tear, maul."
[^19]: 20a — *kukura raba rā kāṛi, diẏe dbija siṃha raṛī*: "howling like a dog" (*rā kāṛā*, "to give voice"); "took to his heels" — *raṛī diẏe* (*raṛa deoẏā*, "to break into a run"); "lion among brahmanas" (*dbija siṃha*) is ironic. "Gave up his life" — *chāṛila parāṇe*. "Perished" — *binaśane*. The author presents the fulfilment of 18 ("will leave this body") as retribution for the blasphemy of Shyamananda (17).
[^20]: "Having clashed with Rasika" — *ṭheki rasikera ṭhāñe*. "[His] vow is to crush the asuras" — *asura-dalana-bānā*: *bānā* — a vow, a banner, a distinguishing mark; the sense is approximate. "Shows mercy" — *kara karuṇā*.
[^21]: "A partial avatara" — *aṃśa-abatāra*: the people's rapturous words; according to Gaudiya Vaishnava teaching, Rasika is a devotee, a disciple of Shyamananda, and not an avatara (Pashchima, lahari 2, note 28; Purva, lahari 1, note 2; Purva, lahari 14, note 11). "As he had ordained in play" — *yei ājñā karilena raṅge*: what was said in the letter (18) came to pass. Rasika's punishment of the offenders and his subsequent mercy to them accord with the Vaishnava understanding that even a Vaishnava's chastisement purifies and leads to bhakti. Stanza 22 is the last on pdf 183 (printed p. 139); the continuation of the lahari (23–25) and the colophon are on pdf 184.
[^22]: Continuation of lahari 3 (stanzas 1–22 — pdf 182–183, files RM-4-03-001-022); pdf 184 (printed p. 140) begins with the complete stanza ॥ 23 ॥. "Turned to Krishna" — *haila kṛṣṇa parāẏaṇa*. "Manifestation" — *parakāśa*: the power of Rasika made manifest (18–22). "Devotion in love" — *premabhakti*. 23c — *sabākāra premabhakti, kṛṣṇe dilā sabe mati*: it can also be understood as "[Rasika] gave all devotion in love, turned the minds of all to Krishna."
[^23]: "The unyielding Nagara-chandra" — *haṭhiẏā nāgaracandra*: *haṭhiẏā* — "stubborn, unyielding"; *nāgara-candra* — "the moon among nagaras" (*nāgara* — the refined, skilful lover, an epithet of Krishna), here of Rasika (cf. lahari 4, 25). "Dear to those who have nothing" — *akiñcanapriẏa-prāṇa* (cf. 1, note 2). "The deliverance from the terrible [age of] Kali" — *kalighora-paritrāṇa*. "Cannot be described" — *nā yāẏa bākhāna*.
[^24]: "Devotees of Krishna" — *kārṣṇa-jana* (Purva, lahari 10, note 13; Purva, lahari 11, note 15): on the scan the repha is printed to the right of the conjunct (crops ×3–×4); garbled in the OCR. "The word of good fame" — *suyaśaḥ-bacana*. The author's signature (the second half of the stanza) is given in the standard formula; in the tripadi metre it runs: "Making the holy feet of Shyamananda the ornament on his head, the son of Rasamaya sings" (Purva, lahari 11, note 15).
