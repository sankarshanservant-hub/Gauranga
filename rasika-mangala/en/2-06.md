# Dakshina-vibhaga. Lahari 6. Description of the Wedding of Sri Sri Gopivallabha-raya

*A song in glory of Shyamananda; while living at Dharenda, Rasika plans the wedding of Gopivallabha-raya: craftsmen manifest the Deity of Thakurani, and in the house of Rasamaya and Vamshi provisions and gifts are prepared; sadhus and brahmanas are invited, Rasamayadasa performs the adhivasa and has the Deity brought, and over three festivals the Divine Couple are united; Rasika in ecstasy beholds Them, feeds the sadhus and sends them off with honour, and returns home with the Deity; the people of Dharenda, like the maidens of Vraja, pine in separation from him, while Rasika weeps remembering his pastimes there and sends them a letter describing those pastimes; at the reading of the letter all weep, and Rasika's elder brother Pattanayaka Jagannatha loses consciousness; the author extols the fortune of those who played with Rasika*

*The sixth lahari.*

*Raga Varadi — panchali metre.*[^1]

**1.** Glory, glory to Shyamananda, the bliss of love for all,
the giver of prema to the whole world!
Be merciful to me, Prabhu: may your virtues become manifest [in me],
may I sing the song of your glory.

**2.** Thus at Dharenda he stayed some days,
performing sankirtana with various joys.
For Sri Gopivallabha-raya in his own abode
he set his mind on a wedding.[^2]

**3.** Having craftsmen called, manifesting Thakurani,
he prepared there everything for the wedding.
In the house of Rasamaya [and] Vamshi provisions [and] gifts were prepared;
he tells everyone about the wedding:[^3]

**4.** "Two or three festivals — all of you, give your hearts to this —
I will hold in Rasamaya's house."
Hearing this, all joyfully came with every preparation,
with various provisions [and] various gifts.[^4]

**5.** Wherever there were sadhus — sending them invitations,
Rasika-shekhara had [them] brought.
Having brought the brahmanas, [fixing] the lagna at an auspicious moment —
the best of the brahmanas sounded the Vedas.[^5]

**6.** Having performed the adhivasa of the festival, Rasamayadasa
had the Deity brought there.
Holding three festivals, uniting the Two,
[he] set everyone afloat in joy.[^6]

**7.** [Amid] the clamour of various instruments the wedding was auspiciously performed;
night and day [people] know nothing [else] for happiness.
All, giving up household cares, are intoxicated with this rasa;
all the people float in love for Krishna.

**8.** Seeing the Divine Couple, Rasika found happiness:
from [his] eyes flowed a hundred streams.
Perspiration, trembling, a faltering voice, hair standing on end all over the body —
again and again, many times, he gazes [at Them].[^7]

**9.** In the great joy of the festival Rasikananda is absorbed,
intoxicated with love for Krishna day and night.
Various kinds of dishes, foods of six tastes —
the sadhus dine together.[^8]

**10.** Having held three festivals, he sent everyone off,
[giving them] as was fitting loads of clothes and goods.
Satisfying all, lovingly [asking pardon] with humility,
he showed the sadhus due honour.[^9]

**11.** Thus, having completed the wedding, Prabhu went to his own town,
taking the Deity with him.
In Dharenda all the people, their hearts distraught with separation,
repeat [the name of] Rasika day and night.[^10]

**12.** Children, old men, women weep and cannot compose their hearts;
night and day they meditate on Rasika.
As formerly the maidens of Vraja pined in separation from Krishna —
[so he] stole away everyone's senses.[^11]

**13.** And here Rasika-raya, remembering them all,
weeps in Krishna's mood:
all the pastimes he performed in love for Krishna — which one in which place —
remembering them again and again, he weeps on and on.[^12]

**14.** After some time he wrote a letter, as it pleased him —
in what place he had performed what pastime;
in a deeply intimate mood, describing everything as he had experienced it —
with whom he had played what games.[^13]

**15.** Where Achyuta's son had performed what pastime of Krishna —
of all this [he] wrote in detail:
that beautiful lake, the most fragrant places,
the dense forest, the trees.[^14]

**16.** As many people as live there — he named them all in the letter,
those with whom he had spoken of Krishna.
Having described everything one by one, he sent [the letter] with his own men —
Narayana [and] Ramakrishna — joyfully.[^15]

**17.** Having completed the sankirtana, [all] sat down in a circle
to hear Rasika's letter.
Hearing [it] verse by verse, everyone's heart breaks;
[all] began to weep aloud.[^16]

**18.** All, overwhelmed, hearing that letter of love,
no one can hold back [his] heart.
All float in love for Krishna, in hope of Rasika's feet;
all the people lost consciousness in prema.[^17]

**19.** Rasika's elder brother, Pattanayaka Jagannatha,
hearing the letter, lost his senses.
In the mood of Krishna [and] the maidens of Vraja, in the loving anguish of separation —
[such] were all those details in the letter.[^18]

**20.** Hearing [it], all the people weep in the bliss of Rasika's love;
some roll on the ground.
Hearing the letter, Jagannatha fell to the ground in ecstasy —
his weeping cannot be described.

**21.** As once the maidens of Vraja, in love for Krishna, in loving anguish,
hearing all those words of love —
[so] all, overwhelmed, remembering Rasika,
sing the song of Rasika's virtues.[^19]

**22.** Thus all the people, night and day, every moment,
meditate on Rasika's holy feet.
Blessed is the fortune of them all, [it is] the fruit of their austerities:
he plays [with them] every moment.[^20]

**23.** The Rasika-mangala, the song of [his] glory — sing it, all of you;
worship Rasika's holy feet.
Adorning himself with Shyamananda's feet,
the son of Rasamaya joyfully composed [this].[^21]

*Thus in the Dakshina-vibhaga of the Sri Rasika-mangala ends the sixth lahari, called "Description of the Wedding of Sri Sri Gopivallabha-raya."*[^22]

[^1]: The lines before the raga are the heading of the lahari ("ষষ্ঠ-লহরী"), in italics without a number. Lahari 6 begins on the lower half of pdf 105, after the colophon of lahari 5 (files RM-2-05-001-067). Raga Varadi (*barāṛī*). "Panchali metre" (*pāñcālī chanda*) is tripadi; in the edition each stanza is set in four lines and is translated likewise (cf. Purva, lahari 11, note 1). There is no ghosha and no mangala-shloka; the stanzas are numbered as in the edition. The title is from the colophon on pdf 106 (checked against the scan): "শ্রীশ্রীগোপীবল্লভ-রায়-বিবাহ-বর্ণন-নাম ষষ্ঠলহরী" (*śrīśrīgopīvallabha-rāya-bibāha-barṇana*). Stanza 13 begins at the foot of pdf 105 and ends at the top of pdf 106.
[^2]: Gopivallabha-raya is Rasika's family Deity, whom Rasika brought to Kashipur and to whom Shyamananda gave this name (Dakshina, lahari 3, note 24; pdf 100). "Wedding" — *bibhā* (*bibāha*). "In his own abode" — *āpanāra nijālaẏe*: apparently of the Deity residing in Rasika's home (at Gopivallabhapur; cf. 6 and 11); it may also be understood as "[remembering] his own home".
[^3]: Thakurani is the Deity of Sri Radha, consort of Gopivallabha-raya. "Manifesting" — *prakāśiyā*: so the author speaks of the Deity's being made by craftsmen (*kārikara*). The "wedding" of Deities is the installation of Radha's Deity beside Krishna's Deity, performed with the rites of marriage. Vamshi is Rasamaya's brother (lahari 4, 34, note 14). "Were prepared" — *kaila*: the subject is not named (Rasika or the hosts).
[^4]: "With every preparation" — *sarbārambhe*, lit. "with all undertakings".
[^5]: Lagna (*lagna*) — the moment astrologically auspicious for a rite.
[^6]: Adhivasa (*adhibāsa*) — the preliminary rite of consecration on the eve of a festival (anointing with fragrances, etc.). Rasamayadasa is Rasamaya, the author's father. "Had the Deity brought" — apparently Gopivallabha-raya, from Gopivallabhapur to Dharenda (cf. 11). "The Two" — *dõhāra*: Gopivallabha and Thakurani.
[^7]: The Divine Couple — *yugala-rūpa*, "the form of the Couple", Radha and Krishna. Perspiration, trembling, a faltering voice, hair standing on end, tears are the sattvika-bhavas, bodily signs of ecstatic love (Bhakti-rasamrita-sindhu 2.3).
[^8]: "Six tastes" — Dakshina, lahari 3, note 7. "Dine together" — *bhojana saṅgati*, "a common meal".
[^9]: "[Asking pardon] with humility" — *binaẏa kariẏā*: so guests are customarily seen off, with a request to forgive the shortcomings of the hospitality. "Showed due honour" — *karila byabahāra*, lit. "dealt [as was proper]".
[^10]: Prabhu is Rasika (Dakshina, lahari 3, note 11). "His own town" — *nija-purī*: apparently Gopivallabhapur. "Repeat [the name of] Rasika" — *rasika japai*.
[^11]: The gopis pining in separation from Krishna when He left for Mathura (Bhagavata 10.39; 10.47). "Stole away" — *hari' nilā*: the subject is not named — apparently Rasika (or the separation itself).
[^12]: "Here" — *ethā*: apparently at Gopivallabhapur. "In Krishna's mood" — *kṛṣṇa-bhābe*: apparently like Krishna longing in Mathura for Vraja (cf. 12); it may also be understood as "in love for Krishna". "Which one in which place" — his pastimes at Dharenda (lahari 5, 43–59).
[^13]: "As it pleased him" — *abhimata*, "as desired, at [his own] discretion." "In an intimate mood" — *rahasya-bhābe*. "As he had experienced it" — *anubhabe*. "Games" — *khelā*: Rasika's pastimes with the boys and people of Dharenda (lahari 5, 43–59).
[^14]: "Achyuta's son" — *acyuta-bālā* (lahari 5, note 27). "Wrote" — on the scan *lekhilũ* (a first-person form, "I wrote"; crop ×2), in the 1997 edition *lekhila*, "wrote"; translated by the sense (the subject is Rasika, cf. 14, 16). The lake and groves of Dharenda are not identified.
[^15]: Narayana and Ramakrishna (*nārāẏaṇa rāmakṛṣṇa*) — apparently two disciples of Rasika sent as messengers; Ramakrishna is named among his first disciples (lahari 4, 19, note 8). "Joyfully" — *raṅge*.
[^16]: "Verse by verse" — *eka eka pada*: *pada* is "line, verse" (or "song"; the letter was perhaps written in verse). "Heart" — *prāṇī* (= *prāṇa*, "life, soul").
[^17]: "In hope of Rasika's feet" — *rasika-caraṇa āśe* (so on the scan, crop ×4, and in the 1997 edition; the OCR of the full edition has *āge*). "Lost consciousness" — *agiẏāna*; the editor glosses it in a footnote as "অজ্ঞান" (the editor's note is not translated).
[^18]: Rasika's elder brother — *rasikera jyeṣṭha bhrātaḥ* (so on the scan, = *bhrātā*); "Pattanayaka" (*paṭṭanāeka*) is an honorific title also borne by Rasika's father Achyuta (Purva, lahari 3, note 23). In Dakshina 3 (49) Rasika's elder brother is named Kashinatha Dasa (Dakshina, lahari 3, note 22); whether Jagannatha is another elder brother or this is a discrepancy is unclear. Where he heard the letter — in Dharenda or in Rasika's family home — the author does not say. The second half of the stanza is understood as describing the content of the letter: Rasika writes of his pastimes in the mood of separation of Krishna and the gopis (cf. 13, note 12).
[^19]: Cf. the gopis hearing Krishna's message brought by Uddhava (Bhagavata 10.47). "In love for Krishna" — *kṛṣṇa-bhābe*. "Remembering" — *samariẏā* (= *smariẏā*).
[^20]: "Austerities" — *tapasyā*. The last line is understood thus: the fortune of those with whom Rasika played (14) is the fruit of their past austerities; it can also be understood as "[in meditation] he plays with them every moment."
[^21]: The author's signature is given in the standard formula (§ 4); here it is in tripadi metre and reads slightly differently: "Placing Shyamananda's feet joyfully on his head, the son of Rasamaya sings" (cf. Purva, lahari 5, note 14).
[^22]: The colophon is on pdf 106 (checked against the scan); on the title see note 1. Lahari 7 begins on the same page.
