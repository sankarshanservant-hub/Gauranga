# Uttara-vibhaga. Lahari 6. The Description of the Twelve-Day Festival in Sri Gopivallabhapur

*A song in glory of Shyamananda; Rasika resolves to hold the festival in Gopivallabhapur on the Suvarnarekha, which all call the hidden Vrindavana; in the kadamba grove, beneath trees adorned with canopies, strings of jewels, chamaras and garlands, is the circle of the rasa with a pavilion and a jewelled throne; around it are Vrajavasis, Gaudiyas, people of Utkala, Shyamanandis, rajas and subjects, and hundreds of traders bringing goods and beads from Vraja, and everything is sold; Rasika buys everything and lavishes sweets, provisions, clothes and ornaments beyond measure on hundreds of thousands of people; amid lights and fireworks, kirtana and music, Sri Govinda-raya in twelve attires goes in procession to the place of the rasa, Rasika sprinkles the way with sandal water, the rajas pelt one another with powders, and for twelve days Rasika dances, overcome by the eight sattvika-bhavas, and embraces everyone; calling himself ignorant, the author says he writes by the inspiration of the son of Achyuta*

*The sixth lahari.*

*Raga Varadi.*[^1]
*Ghosha: "O ocean of mercy, O merciful Shyama!*
*Turn Your attention to the fallen and wretched people."*

**1.** Glory, glory to Shyamananda, the life of those who have nothing,
the friend who purifies the fallen, the treasury of compassion![^2]

**2.** Thus in the place Sri Gopivallabhapur
he resolved in his mind to hold the festival.[^3]

**3.** A most charming place, beautiful to behold,
on the bank of the Suvarnarekha, most charming.

**4.** The glory of the village cannot be told at all:
all people sing [of it], calling [it] the hidden Vrindavana.

**5.** The beauty of the kadamba grove cannot be told;
beneath it the Sri circle of the rasa shines forth.[^4]

**6.** A dense forest — a wood of kadambas;
its wondrous beauty to behold cannot be described.

**7.** Row upon row the trees shine on every side;
in the shade of the thick leaves the [sun's] rays do not reach.

**8.** Beneath them, wondrous canopies tier upon tier;
from them hang strings of jewels [and] wondrous chamaras.

**9.** In their midst a wondrous pavilion was built,
adorned with various jewels, most skilfully made.[^5]

**10.** With various kinds of cloth it was adorned on every side;
tier upon tier hang various kinds of strings of gems.

**11.** In its midst is a most beautiful jewelled throne;
year after year [its] beauty is ever more extraordinary.[^6]

**12.** Strings of various jewels and gems hang from all the trees;
gateways [and] banners row upon row on all four sides.

**13.** The beauty of the circle of the rasa is extraordinary:
gods, asuras and humans are astonished.

**14.** From every tree hang hundreds and hundreds of strings,
*jhāna*, cloths, chamaras, tassels, garlands of flowers.[^7]

**15.** Every tree is adorned with various flowers;
the trees glitter, the night is bright.

**16.** The kadamba trees themselves are a wondrous adornment;
by the radiance of the trees the earth was lit up.[^8]

**17.** Hundreds and hundreds of such trees shine all around;
thousands and thousands of Vrajavasis are beside them.

**18.** Gaudiyas, people of Utkala, the Shyamanandis —
there is no counting them; who can count the sadhus?

**19.** Rajas, subjects, well-to-do folk — hundreds of thousands of people;
all stay around the kadamba grove.[^9]

**20.** Beside them sit hundreds and hundreds of traders;
day and night they sell [and] buy various goods.[^10]

**21.** Whatever goods there are of the Creator's making —
at the time of the festival all bring [them].

**22.** Various goods all bring, like mountains;
everything is sold — not a single grain remains.

**23.** Thousands and thousands of people bring divine beads:
from Mathura-mandala the traders gather.[^11]

**24.** Tulasi, *āṭhela*, wood, lovely strands —
from Vraja they bring hundreds and hundreds of sacks full.[^12]

**25.** Mountains of beads lie all around;
all the beads are sold in twelve days.

**26.** Whatever goods come from hundreds and hundreds of kroshas —
everything is sold, nothing remains at the end.

**27.** As was the abundance of goods before at the festival of the rasa, —
four times as much as that there is each time.[^13]

**28.** The goods are past counting, [they are] beyond measure;
all the goods Rasika buys up with joy.

**29.** Hundreds of thousands of people he honours [with gifts]:
sweets, refreshments, uncooked provisions, clothes, ornaments.[^14]

**30.** Where has there been, [where] will there be such a festival?
Sri Rasika-deva holds [it] with firm resolve.

**31.** As much chuya [and] sandal as he distributes to all —
who would be able to give out that much [even] of mud from a pond?[^15]

**32.** As many clothes as the son of Achyuta distributes —
who would be able to give out that much [even] of banana peel?

**33.** As many sweets [and] fried delicacies as he gave to all —
[no one] would be able [to give out that much even] of broken rice.

**34.** Shali rice with the six tastes he gives to the sadhus —
[no one] would be able [to give out that much even] of banana roots.

**35.** As much curd, milk, cream, chhana as he gave away,
ghee, honey, sugar, pana drinks, bananas — lavishly arranged —

**36.** and as many other various provisions as he gave to all —
no one would be able [to give out that much even] of buttermilk and water.[^16]

**37.** Seeing Rasika's festival, all people —
all, rajas and subjects — were astonished.

**38.** What beauty that circle has — it cannot be told!
What a procession of Sri Govinda-raya [there]![^17]

**39.** Hundreds and hundreds of chandrodayas, deutis, torches,
bhuichampas, chandrabanas, rockets without number.[^18]

**40.** The night becomes brighter than day;
with the music of the five sounds the earth trembles.

**41.** The earth sways with the sound of the kirtana;
shingas, venus, vishanas sound — various instruments.[^19]

**42.** Hundreds and hundreds of rajas [go] with staffs in hand,
and still hundreds of thousands of people push and jostle.[^20]

**43.** As if in rapture Prabhu Sri Govinda-raya moves;
by the effulgence of [His] body the four quarters were lit up.[^21]

**44.** What a sweet face, what a sweet glance!
What a gentle, gentle smile, the swaying of [His] body!

**45.** In twelve days there are twelve attires:
on whichever day, whichever attire — that one is resplendent.

**46.** What wondrous attire Sri-charana creates!
Seeing the attire, all people are astonished.[^22]

**47.** When Prabhu Sri Govinda goes in procession,
Rasikendra sprinkles sandal water ahead.[^23]

**48.** Some with phagu, some with chuya, some with argaja —
all the rajas pelt one another.[^24]

**49.** All float in the ocean of bliss night and day;
in great bliss Prabhu goes in procession with sankirtana.

**50.** Having brought Govinda in procession to the place of the rasa,
in great bliss Rasika-shekhara dances.[^25]

**51.** The eight sattvika-bhavas manifest in [his] holy body;
hundreds and hundreds of streams flow from the waves of [his] eyes.

**52.** Like kadamba buds, [his] body [bristles] with pulaka;
in the rapture of bhava [his] holy body rolls on the ground.[^26]

**53.** With a faltering voice he speaks soft, soft words;
with the tears of [his] eyes the earth turned to mud.

**54.** What a sweet smile, what sweet speech!
Clasping everyone by the neck, he floats in the bliss of prema.

**55.** To all Prabhu gives embraces;
in the rapture of bhava Prabhu has no outer awareness.

**56.** Thus for twelve days he dances,
intoxicated with prema for Krishna, Rasika-shekhara.

**57.** All that happiness cannot be told at all —
all the pastimes [the son of Achyuta displayed] moment by moment.[^27]

**58.** By his grace I have made known a little —
a little of the virtues, fame [and] glory of Rasika-deva.

**59.** I am without learning, without intelligence, a wretch;
[I write] whatever the son of Achyuta speaks, dwelling [in my] heart.[^28]

**60.** A description of [his] nature I have composed a little;
the learned and the good will not count this a fault.[^29]

**61.** Adorning himself with Shyamananda's feet,
the son of Rasamaya joyfully composed [this].

*Thus in the Uttara-vibhaga of the Sri Rasika-mangala ends the sixth lahari, called "The Description of the Twelve-Day Festival in Sri Gopivallabhapur."*[^30]

[^1]: The lines before the raga are the title of the lahari ("ষষ্ঠ-লহরী"), in italics without a number. Lahari 6 begins on a new page — pdf 188 (printed p. 144; no running head or page number), after the colophon of lahari 5 on pdf 187 (files RM-4-05-024-057). Raga Varadi (*barāṛī*; Purva, lahari 8, note 2); the ghosha is the same as in Pashchima, laharis 1 and 9 (note 1), and its translation is repeated; "Shyama" here is apparently Shyamananda (cf. 1); it may also be understood as Krishna. There is no mangala-shloka: after the raga and ghosha the song begins with ॥ 1 ॥. The title follows the colophon on pdf 189 (printed p. 145), checked against the scan: "ইতি শ্রীরসিকমঙ্গল উত্তর-বিভাগে শ্রীগোপীবল্লভপুরে দ্বাদশ মহোৎসব-বর্ণননাম ষষ্ঠ-লহরী সম্পূর্ণা" (*śrīgopīballabhapure dbādaśa mahotsaba-barṇana*), lit. "The Description of the Festival of Twelve [Days] in Sri Gopivallabhapur." Lahari 6 comprises 1–61 (pdf 188–189).
[^2]: "The life of those who have nothing" — *akiñcana-prāṇa*: dear as life to those who have nothing (cf. lahari 3, note 2). "The friend who purifies the fallen" — *patitapābana-bandhu*. "The treasury of compassion" — *karuṇānidhāna*.
[^3]: "Thus" — *henarūpe*: after the events of lahari 5, where Rasika decided that the festivals would be in Gopivallabhapur (lahari 5, 36, 50–51). "The festival" is the twelve-day festival in memory of Shyamananda (Uttara, lahari 2, 4–5, note 5; lahari 5, notes 28–29). Gopivallabhapur is Rasika's village on the Suvarnarekha (now in Jhargram district, West Bengal; BR 15, 81).
[^4]: "The hidden Vrindavana" (4) — *gupta-bṛndābana*: Gopivallabhapur is revered as a concealed Vrindavana. The kadamba grove (5) — *kadamba-khaṇḍi*: the place of the rasa in Gopivallabhapur, where the octagonal rasa-mancha ("Kadamkhandi") now stands (Pashchima, lahari 1, note 29). The Sri circle of the rasa — *śrīrāsamaṇḍalī* (ibid.; Pashchima, lahari 16, note 35).
[^5]: "Do not reach" (7) — *nā lāge*. "Canopies" (8) — *cā̃duẏā*. "Strings of jewels" — *ratna-jhārā*: hanging strings, pendants. Chamaras are yak-tail whisks. "Pavilion" (9) — *maṇḍapa*. "Most skilfully made" — *ati subandhāna* (so in the scan, crop ×2; likewise in 35): understood as "well arranged"; the sense is approximate.
[^6]: "Various kinds" (10) — *nānā bhānti* (= *bhā̃ti*). "Year after year" (11) — *batsare batsare*: the festival is annual (lahari 5, 51).
[^7]: *jhāna* — so in the scan (crop ×2); the sense is unclear (perhaps a kind of pendant). "Cloths" — *bāsa*. "Tassels" — *thopanā*.
[^8]: "The night is bright" (15) — *ujala yāminī*: from the festive lights (cf. 39–40). 16a — *ekete kadamba bṛkṣa bicitra maṇḍanī*: the sense is approximate.
[^9]: Gaudiyas (18) are Vaishnavas from Gauda (Bengal; lahari 4, note 9). The Shyamanandis are the disciples of Shyamananda. "There is no counting them" — *samuccaẏa nāi*, lit. "there is no total." "Well-to-do folk" (19) — *sukhabāsī*, lit. "those living in comfort"; the sense is approximate.
[^10]: "Traders" — *pasārī*: shopkeepers, hawkers. "Sit" — *boise*. "Sell [and] buy" — *bicā kinā* (= *becā kenā*). "Day and night" — *diśi niśe*.
[^11]: "Not a single grain" (22) — *eka dhāna*, "one grain of paddy." "Beads" (23, 25) — *mālā*: rosaries and necklaces, apparently of tulasi (24); it can also be understood as "garlands." Mathura-mandala is Vraja. "The traders gather" — *byāpāra se melā*: *byāpāra* is trade, traders; *melā* is a gathering, a fair; the sense is approximate.
[^12]: Tulasi is the sacred plant from whose stems rosaries and neck beads are made. *āṭhela* — so in the scan (crop ×2); the sense is unclear. "Wood" — *kāṣṭha*: apparently wooden beads. "Strands" — *jhurī*. "Sacks" — *chālā*. A krosha (26) is a measure of distance, about 3 km.
[^13]: "The festival of the rasa" is the Great Rasa in Gopivallabhapur (Pashchima, laharis 1–5). "Abundance of goods" — *drabyabhāra*, "load of goods." "Each time" — *bāre bāra*: with every festival.
[^14]: "Beyond measure" (28) — *apramite*. "Honours" (29) — *sambhāṣaṇa*: greets and presents with gifts. "Refreshments" — *śītala*: apparently cooling drinks and refreshments. "Uncooked provisions" — *sidā*: rice, dal and other supplies given to a guest to cook for himself.
[^15]: 31–36 are a hyperbole: Rasika gave away so much that no one could give away as much even of the cheapest things — mud, banana peel, broken rice, banana roots, buttermilk. "Would be able" — *bhājana haibe*, lit. "will become a vessel, be worthy." Chuya (*cuẏā*) is a fragrant mixture (Uttara, lahari 1, note 6).
[^16]: "Fried delicacies" (33) — *pakkānna*. "Shali rice with the six tastes" (34) — *ṣaṛarasa śāli anna*: rice of the finest kind with all the seasonings. "Chhana" (35) — *chānā*, curd cheese. "Pana drinks" — *pānā*: sweet cooling drinks. "Lavishly arranged" — *subandhāna* (note 5). "Buttermilk and water" (36) — *takrapāni*.
[^17]: "That circle" (38) is the circle of the rasa (5, 13). "Procession" — *bijaẏa*: the solemn going forth of the Deity (Uttara, lahari 2, note 28). Sri Govinda-raya is the Deity of Gopivallabhapur (Pashchima, lahari 10, note 31; lahari 11, note 12).
[^18]: Chandrodaya, deuti, chandrabana, bhuichampa (*bhũicampā*; cf. bhumichampa) are festive lamps and fireworks (Purva, lahari 12, note 17; Pashchima, lahari 16, note 35). "Rockets" — *hāui*.
[^19]: "The music of the five sounds" (40) — *pañcaśabda bājanā*: apparently an ensemble of five kinds of instruments. "Sways" (41) — *dulu dulu kare*. The shinga (*siṅgā*) is a curved horn, the venu a flute, the vishana a horn (Purva, lahari 4, note 19).
[^20]: "With staffs in hand" — *chaṛi kari hāte*: apparently the rajas themselves keep order in the crowd, as stewards of the procession. "Push and jostle" — *ṭhelāṭheli kare*.
[^21]: "As if in rapture" — *madamatte*: with a stately gait, like an elephant in rut. Prabhu Sri Govinda-raya is the Deity (note 17); in the Vaishnava understanding the Deity is the Lord Himself (the archa-avatara), and the author describes His procession as the living movement of the Lord.
[^22]: "Attire" (45–46) — *beśa*: the Deity's dress, a different one for each day of the festival. "Sri-charana" — *śrīcaraṇa*, "the holy feet": an honorific designation; apparently Rasika, who dresses the Deity (cf. lahari 5, 46, note 27); it can also be understood of Govinda Himself: "what wondrous attire [the Deity] assumes."
[^23]: "Goes in procession" — *bijaẏa kare*. "Sprinkles sandal water" — *candanera chaṛā deẏa*: sprinkles the Deity's path.
[^24]: Phagu (*phāgu*) is a red powder; chuya is a fragrant mixture (note 15); argaja (*aragajā*) — the editor explains in a footnote: "water with abir" (fragrant coloured powder). "Pelt one another" — *paraspara mārāmāri kare*, lit. "beat one another": playful festive sport.
[^25]: "Prabhu goes in procession" (49) — *prabhu bije kare*: apparently of Sri Govinda (cf. 47, 50); it can also be understood of Rasika. "Having brought in procession" (50) — *bije karāiẏā*. "The place of the rasa" — *rāsasthala*.
[^26]: The eight sattvika-bhavas (51) — *aṣṭa sāttbika bhāba*: being stunned, perspiration, pulaka, faltering of the voice, trembling, change of colour, tears, fainting (Bhakti-rasamrita-sindhu 2.3; Pashchima, lahari 16, note 32). Pulaka (52) is the standing of the hairs on end from ecstatic love — "kadamba buds" (*kadamba-kalikā*; ibid.). "In the rapture of bhava" — *bhābera ābeśe*.
[^27]: "Prabhu" (55) is Rasika. "Outer awareness" — *bāhyajñāna*. "Moment by moment" (57) — *tile tile*. The son of Achyuta (*acyuta-tanaẏa*) is Rasika.
[^28]: "By his grace" (58) — *tā̃ra anugrahe*: by Rasika's grace. "Glory" — *kīrtta* (= *kīrti*). "A wretch" (59) — *durācāra*: the author's self-abasement. The son of Achyuta (*acyuta-kumāra*) is Rasika; the author ascribes his inspiration to Rasika dwelling in his heart (lahari 5, 55).
[^29]: "A description of [his] nature" — *sbabhāba barṇanā* (lahari 5, note 31). 60b — *ithe doṣa nāi la'be paṇḍita sujana*: understood as "will not count [it] a fault"; it can also be understood as "there is no fault in this — [so] the learned and the good will accept [it]."
[^30]: The author's signature (61) and the colophon are on pdf 189 (printed p. 145), checked against the scan; the title is as in note 1. Below the rule is an editor's footnote to 48 (not translated; used in note 24). Lahari 6 comprises 1–61 (pdf 188–189) and is complete; lahari 7 begins on a new page, pdf 190.
