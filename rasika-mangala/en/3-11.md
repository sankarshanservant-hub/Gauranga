# Pashchima-vibhaga. Lahari 11. The Manifestation of the Deity of Sri Govinda in Sri Gopivallabhapur

*A song in glory of Shyamananda; Rasika comes to Shyamananda in Thuriya, who embraces him and asks about the events in Banapur; the companions tell of the elephant and all the rest, Rasika offers Shyamananda clothes, ornaments and jewels, and they spend days and nights in talk of Krishna; Rasika presents the craftsman brothers Raghu and Ananda, Shyamananda manifests a Deity and names Him Vrindavanachandra, and bids Rasika manifest Sri Govinda in Gopivallabhapur with the craftsmen; on an auspicious day Govinda-raya becomes manifest, Rasika rewards the craftsmen, installs the Deity in the temple and holds a great festival; thereafter he lives at home in the service of Govinda, in sankirtana and in service to guru, Krishna and the sadhus*

*The eleventh lahari.*

*Raga Kamoda — panchali metre.*[^1]

**1.** Glory to Shyamananda, conqueror of the world,
lord of all the worlds!
Life of the world, life of Rasika —
may I sing [his] virtues every day.[^2]

**2.** After some days the son of Achyuta went
to see Shyamananda.
In the town of Thuriya Rasika-shekhara
saw the feet of Prabhu.[^3]

**3.** Seeing [him], Shyamananda — great was Prabhu's joy —
lifted [him] up and embraced [him].
He seated [him] at his side and asked about everything —
all the pastimes in Banapur.[^4]

**4.** Hearing Shyamananda's words, Rasikendra
[sits] with head bowed from shyness.
The companions [who were] with him tell in detail
of the pastimes as they were, as is fitting.[^5]

**5.** They told of the naming of the king of elephants and the rest, of the renowned [deed];
hearing it, [Shyamananda] was delighted.
Having finished their meal and so on, they sit as an assembly;
Sri Shyamananda took his seat.[^6]

**6.** Clothes, ornaments, many jewels and riches
Rasika offered there.
In the rasa of talks about Krishna they sat absorbed,
knowing neither night nor day.[^7]

**7.** Quickly bringing Raghu [and] Ananda,
[Rasika] presented [them] to Prabhu:
"These two are truly the form of Vishvakarma," —
he tells everything in detail.[^8]

**8.** Hearing this, Prabhu Shyamananda, delighted,
manifested a shri-murti.
"Vrindavanachandra" — with joy in his heart
he gave Him this name.[^9]

**9.** Shyamananda says to Rasikendra-chandra:
"Go quickly.
Take the craftsmen and have a shri-murti made
in Sri Gopivallabhapur.[^10]

**10.** His name [will be] Sri Govinda, the incomparable;
He will become manifest in the world."
Hearing the command with his own ears, [Rasika] asked leave to depart —
with him the two brothers.[^11]

**11.** Reckoning an auspicious day, [when] all the good omens [were present],
Govinda-raya became manifest.
Seeing each of [His] limbs, Ananga is enchanted;
all float in bliss.[^12]

**12.** Seeing that form, Rasika in bliss —
[so] the command in the dream was confirmed —
gave the craftsmen many goods by the load
as a nichhani offering for Govinda's form.[^13]

**13.** Bringing Govinda and installing [Him] in the temple,
he began a great festival.
Bringing brahmanas, [having them] recite the shruti,
he did everything according to rule.[^14]

**14.** A great, great festival, a festival of bliss —
who can tell the limit of that happiness?
The virtues and pastimes of Krishna — that is his play;
Rasika is lost in happiness.[^15]

**15.** Thus in manifold delights, in great bliss —
Rasikendra, the crest-jewel [of devotees],
ever [immersed in] the *Bhagavata*, intoxicated with the rasa of Krishna,
the jewel among all virtues.[^16]

**16.** For many, many days in his own home
he remains in the rasa of serving Krishna;
always in sankirtana, raining tears,
knowing neither night nor day.[^17]

**17.** On the form of Govinda, the embodiment of bliss,
he gazes constantly;
at the base of [His] lotus feet, with tulasi leaves,
Rasika-shekhara worships [Him].[^18]

**18.** Whether at home or in other lands —
[always] service to guru, Krishna and the sadhus.
Rasika's characteristics are wondrous [even] to tell;
who in the world compares [with him]?[^19]

**19.** Rasika's true nature is the very form of prema-bhakti;
seeing [it], all are amazed.
The people of the world, filled with prema,
held worldly existence to be false.[^20]

**20.** The *Rasika-mangala* — billows of bliss;
listen, all people!
Adorning himself with Shyamananda's feet,
the son of Rasamaya joyfully composed [this].[^21]

*Thus in the Pashchima-vibhaga of the Sri Rasika-mangala ends the eleventh lahari, called "The Manifestation of the Deity of Sri Govinda in Sri Gopivallabhapur."*[^22]

[^1]: The lines before the raga are the title of the lahari ("একাদশ-লহরী"), in italics without a number. Lahari 11 begins on pdf 157 (printed p. 117), below a rule after the colophon of lahari 10 (files RM-3-10-078-083). Raga Kamoda (*kāmoda*; Dakshina, lahari 9, note 1). "Panchali metre" (*pā̃cālī chanda*) — tripadi; in the edition each stanza is set in four lines and is translated so (Pashchima, lahari 6, note 1). There is no ghosha and no mangala-shloka; the stanzas are numbered as in the edition. The title follows the colophon on pdf 158 (printed p. 118), checked against the scan: "ইতি শ্রীরসিকমঙ্গল-পশ্চিম-বিভাগে শ্রীগোপীবল্লভপুরে শ্রীগোবিন্দ-বিগ্রহ-প্রকাশ-নাম একাদশ-লহরী সম্পূর্ণা" (*śrīgopīballabhapure śrīgobinda-bigraha-prakāśa*) — "The Manifestation of the Deity of Sri Govinda in Sri Gopivallabhapur". Lahari 11 comprises 1–20 (pdf 157–158) and is entirely in this file.
[^2]: "Conqueror of the world" — *bhubana bijaya*; it may also be understood as "[whose] coming [graces] the world" (*bijaya* is also the "arrival, procession" of a holy person). "Lord of all the worlds" — *akhila bhubana-pati*, "life of the world" — *jagata-jībana*: the author's rapturous titles for Shyamananda; according to Gaudiya Vaishnava teaching Shyamananda is a devotee, a servant of the Lord (Purva, lahari 1, note 2; Purva, lahari 14, note 11). "Life of Rasika" — *rasika-parāṇa*: Shyamananda is Rasika's guru. "May I sing" — *guṇa gāi yena niti*, literally "that I may sing [his] virtues daily".
[^3]: "After some days" — *kata dine*: after the events in Banapur (laharis 7–10). "The son of Achyuta" — *acyutera bālā*, literally "the boy of Achyuta" (Pashchima, lahari 5, note 30). Thuriya (*thuriyā*; "town" — *nagara*) is unidentified; Shyamananda lives there (lahari 12, 2). Prabhu is Shyamananda.
[^4]: "Great was Prabhu's joy" — *prabhura ānanda*; Prabhu is Shyamananda. "Embraced" — *kole karila*, literally "took onto his lap, pressed to his breast". Banapur — Pashchima, lahari 6, note 1; "the pastimes in Banapur" are the meeting with the subahdar Ahmed Beg, the taming of the elephant and the Gajapati (laharis 7–10).
[^5]: "With head bowed" — *heṭamāthe*. "The companions [who were] with him tell" — *saṅge saṅgigaṇe kahe bibaraṇe*: understood as Rasika keeping silent from shyness while his companions tell the story; it may also be read "together with his companions [Rasika] tells". "As is fitting" — *yathocite*.
[^6]: "The king of elephants" — *gajendra*: the elephant Sri Gopala Dasa (lahari 8, 58, note 34; lahari 10). "The naming and the rest" — *nāmādi*. "The renowned [deed]" — *prasiddhi*, "fame, renown"; the sense is approximate. The subject of "was delighted" is not expressed; understood as Shyamananda. "They sit as an assembly" — *baise sabhā kari'*.
[^7]: "Offered" — *dila*, "gave": apparently Rasika presents to Shyamananda the gifts he received in Banapur from the rajas and the Gajapati (lahari 9). "There" — *sesthāne* on the scan (= *se sthāne*). "Absorbed" — *ābeśe*. "Night nor day" — *niśi diśi*.
[^8]: Raghu and Ananda (*raghu ānanda*) are the brothers Raghunatha and Ananda, craftsmen from Nilachala whom Rasika met on the way (lahari 10, 75–77). "Presented" — *upadeśa*, literally "information, instruction"; understood as Rasika presenting them to Prabhu (Shyamananda). Vishvakarma is the architect of the gods; "the form of Vishvakarma" praises their skill (cf. lahari 10, 76). "Truly" — *sbarūpa*, "in their very nature".
[^9]: "Manifested a shri-murti" — *śrīmūrti prakāśa kailā*: apparently Shyamananda had the brothers fashion a Deity (cf. 9: "take the craftsmen"). The author speaks of the Deity as "manifested", "became manifest" (*prakāśa*, 8, 10–11): in the Vaishnava understanding a Deity installed according to rule is non-different from the Lord and manifests Himself. Vrindavanachandra (*bṛndābanacandra*, "Moon of Vrindavana") is a Deity of Shyamananda; BR 15 does not mention Him.
[^10]: "Craftsmen" — *kārikara* (*kārigar*), "artisan, craftsman". "Have made" — *banāha*. This is the Deity of Sri Govinda who was to become manifest by Jagannatha's command in the dream (lahari 10, 70–73). Cf. BR 15, 81: "he entrusted the service of Sri Govinda to Sri Rasika".
[^11]: "The incomparable" — *anupama*. "Asked leave to depart" — *māgila melāni*, the usual formula of farewell. The two brothers are Raghu and Ananda (7).
[^12]: "Reckoning an auspicious day" — *gaṇi' śubhadina*, by astrological calculation. "All the good omens" — *sarba sulakṣaṇa*. Govinda-raya (*gobinda rāẏa*) is Sri Govinda (10; lahari 10, 71). Ananga (*anaṅga*, "the Bodiless") is Kamadeva, the god of love: a common motif of Vaishnava poetry — Krishna's beauty enchants the god of love himself.
[^13]: "The command in the dream was confirmed" — *sbapna-ājñā paramāṇi* (*pramāṇa*, "proof"): the form of the Deity turned out as Jagannatha had commanded in the dream (lahari 10, 70–73). "By the load" — *bhāre*. "As a nichhani offering for Govinda's form" — *gobindarūpe nichāni*: nichhani is the custom of waving a gift around a beloved to ward off misfortune (Purva, lahari 9, note 21; Purva, lahari 10, note 25); here the gifts to the craftsmen are given as nichhani for the beauty of the Deity.
[^14]: "In the temple" — *mandire*. "Recite the shruti" — *śruti uccāraṇa*: Vedic mantras at the installation of the Deity. "According to rule" — *yathābidhi*.
[^15]: "Limit" — *ora*. "That is his play" — *sei kare khelā*, literally "that [he] makes his play"; the sense is approximate: Rasika is wholly absorbed in glorifying Krishna's virtues and pastimes. "Lost" — *bibhora*.
[^16]: "Manifold delights" — *nānā raṅge*. "Crest-jewel [of devotees]" — *cūṛāmaṇi*, "the jewel in a crown". "Ever [immersed in] the *Bhagavata*" — *sadā bhāgabata*; it may also be understood as "ever a bhagavata", i.e. a devotee. "The jewel among all virtues" — *sarbaguṇe guṇamaṇi*. The stanza has no main verb.
[^17]: "In his own home" — *āpanā sadane*: in Gopivallabhapur. "Raining tears" — *aśrubariṣaṇe*.
[^18]: "The embodiment of bliss" — *ānanda-sbarūpa*. "With tulasi leaves" — *tulasīra dale*.
[^19]: "Other lands" — *deśāntare*. "Characteristics" — *lakṣaṇa*. "Wondrous [even] to tell" — *adbhuta kathana* (garbled in the OCR, checked against the scan).
[^20]: "Rasika's true nature" — *rasika-sbarūpa*, "Rasika's own nature". "Worldly existence" — *saṃsāra*.
[^21]: "Billows of bliss" — *ānanda-kallola*. The author's signature (the second half of the stanza) is given in the standard formula; in the tripadi metre it reads: "Shyamananda's feet are [his] whole wealth — so sings the son of Rasamaya" (*śyāmānanda-pada sakala sampada gāẏa rasamaẏera nandana*; cf. Purva, lahari 5, note 14).
[^22]: Colophon on pdf 158 (printed p. 118), checked against the scan (note 1). Lower on the same page, below a rule, lahari 12 begins (files RM-3-12-001-006).
