# Pashchima-vibhaga. Lahari 6. The Departure for Banapur

*A song in glory of Shyamananda; after the Rasa-yatra Shyamananda stays some days in the village, and Rasika with his family serves him; at Shyamananda's bidding the bashful Rasika expounds Krishna-katha in the assembly, so that the learned are amazed and people of all classes, even mlecchas, take shelter of Krishna; Raghunatha sends his brother with news that a Yavana is oppressing Sri Radhanagar; Shyamananda hastens with Rasika to Dharenda, Raghunatha Pattanayaka asks that men be sent to the subahdar Ahmed Beg, and Rasika with attendants and Vamshidasa sets out for Banapur*

*The sixth lahari.*

*Raga Varadi — panchali metre.*[^1]

**1.** Glory, glory to Shyamananda, the supreme bliss of all,
the giver of happiness to the whole world!
The great rasa of Rasika became manifest in the world,
renowned among all people in the three worlds.[^2]

**2.** Having sent everyone off, Shyamananda Raya then
stayed some days in that village.
Worshipping the Sri-murti more zealously still, Rasika Murari
knows nothing other than service.[^3]

**3.** Husband, wife, kinsfolk — [all] serve Shyamananda's holy feet
with firm devotion.
When Rasika-deva sits before Shyamananda,
[he sits] with his feet covered by his cloth.[^4]

**4.** With head bowed, full of bashfulness, with a faint, most sweet smile,
he speaks with no one.
Fixing both his holy eyes on Shyamananda's holy feet,
[he sits, and his] body floats in waves of tears.[^5]

**5.** Having offered himself [to his guru], Achyuta's son remains
the crest-jewel of composure and steadiness.
When Shyamananda says eagerly:
"Child, tell everyone about Krishna, explaining [it all]," —[^6]

**6.** faint with bashfulness, smiling softly and sweetly,
he utters a word or two very slowly;
as if [afraid] that someone might hear, he speaks in sweet words —
hearing them, [even] stone melts.

**7.** On one side words press forward, on the other — bashfulness, head bowed:
he is shy before Shyamananda.
Understanding [this], Shyamananda-raya rises on some pretext and goes away,
having given his command to Achyuta's son.[^7]

**8.** Receiving the command, Shyama-pati — [like] Shuka, Vyasa, Brihaspati —
seated in the assembly in great splendour,
joyfully speaks of Krishna, [unfolding] one meaning in many ways,
weeping and weeping in love for Krishna.[^8]

**9.** Hearing Rasika's words, [even] Brihaspati falls silent;
those assembled are amazed.
All the learned pandits say: "In vain have we studied all this time!
Rasika speaks the very essence."[^9]

**10.** The truth that Rasika speaks is the view of Vyasa [and] Shuka;
establishing [it] with the Svami's assent, [he says:]
"The Vedas, the shastras, the Bhagavata, the Puranas and all there is —
all say: set your mind on Krishna."[^10]

**11.** Saying this, the assembly of pandits praises Rasikendra,
and all began to follow it.
Hunas, Pulindas and others, mlechchhas, antyajas, Pukkashas and others —
all took refuge in Krishna.[^11]

**12.** Thus for some days Shyamananda in that place
remained in supreme bliss.
At that time Raghunatha sent [his] brother;
[he] tells everything at [Shyamananda's] lotus feet.[^12]

**13.** The brother says to the Prabhu: "In the village of Sri Radhanagar
a certain yavana has caused oppression.
Kindly come there quickly and, having heard, Prabhu, the whole account,
judge the whole matter."[^13]

**14.** Hearing [this], troubled at heart, together with Achyuta's son
Shyamananda-raya set out.
Very quickly [they] came, entered Dharenda
[and] stayed at Rasamaya's house.[^14]

**15.** The Prabhu says: "Listen, Vamshi: quickly bring everyone
[and] Raghunatha Pattanayaka."
Receiving the command, Vamshidasa went to them all
[and] promptly brought them all.[^15]

**16.** All, [coming] to Shyamananda, bowed to [his] holy feet
[and] told all the news:
"Hear, Prabhu, attentively; we will tell everything in detail:
a villain has seized the village.[^16]

**17.** Let ten or twenty devotees of Krishna quickly go
to the subahdar Ahmed Beg —
then [you] will hold that village undisturbed;
this we say at your feet for certain."[^17]

**18.** Hearing this, Shyamananda-raya looks at Rasika's face;
Rasikendra understood the hint:
"Let there be a command for me, Prabhu, to go to Banapur;
give me a company of followers."[^18]

**19.** Hearing Rasika's words, Shyamananda, delighted,
gave him all the companions;
all the singers and musicians — as he wished —
and with them gave the servant Vamshi.[^19]

**20.** Having made the auspicious setting-out, they went a little way and halted,
[and then] proceeded to Banapur.
Adorning himself with Shyamananda's feet,
the son of Rasamaya joyfully composed [this].[^20]

*Thus in the Pashchima-vibhaga of the Sri Rasika-mangala ends the sixth lahari, called "The Departure for Banapur."*[^21]

[^1]: The lines before the raga are the lahari's heading («ষষ্ঠ-লহরী»), in italics without a number. Lahari 6 begins on the lower half of pdf 144 (printed p. 104), below a rule after the colophon of lahari 5 (files RM-3-05-001-084). Raga Varadi (*barāṛī*; Purva, lahari 8, note 2). "Panchali metre" (*pā̃cāli chanda*) is the tripadi; each stanza is set in four lines in the edition and is translated likewise (cf. Purva, lahari 11, note 1). There is no ghosha or mangala-shloka; the stanzas are numbered as in the edition. The title follows the colophon on pdf 145 (printed p. 105; checked against the scan): «ইতি শ্রীরসিকমঙ্গল-পশ্চিম-বিভাগে বানপুরোদ্দেশ্যে গমন-নাম ষষ্ঠ-লহরী সম্পূর্ণা» (*bānapuroddeśye gamana*, "going with the aim of [reaching] Banapur") — "The Departure for Banapur". Banapur (*bānapura*; in stanza 20 *bāṇapura*) is not identified; possibly Banpur in Utkala (now Khurda district). The summary line covers the whole lahari (stanzas 4–20 from the scan of pdf 145, provisionally). Stanza 3 begins on pdf 144 and ends at the top of pdf 145.
[^2]: "The supreme bliss of all" — *sabāra paramānanda*. "The great rasa of Rasika" — *rasikera baṛa rāsa*: the Rasa-yatra held by Rasika (Pashchima, laharis 2–5). On the rasa as pure prema, having nothing in common with mundane love (CC, Adi 4) — Pashchima, lahari 5, note 3; lahari 3, note 2. "Renowned" — *khyātā*.
[^3]: Shyamananda Raya is Shyamananda (Pashchima, lahari 5, note 35). "In that village" — *se grāme*: apparently Gopivallabhapur, where the Rasa-yatra took place. "Sri-murti" — *śrīmūrti*: usually the Deity; in connection with stanza 3 (Rasika and his family serve Shyamananda) it may also mean the "sacred person" of Shyamananda himself. "More zealously still" — *adhika kari'*, lit. "making more"; the meaning is approximate. Rasika Murari is Rasika.
[^4]: "Husband, wife" — *pati patnī*: apparently Rasika and his wife Shyamadasi. "Kinsfolk" — *goṭhī jana*: members of the clan, the household. "With firm devotion" — *dṛṛhabhābe*. "Before Shyamananda" — *śyāmānanda-sthāne*, lit. "at Shyamananda's place". "With his feet covered by his cloth" — *caraṇete ḍhākiẏā basana*: out of respect for the guru one does not uncover one's feet before him.
[^5]: 4–7 — Rasika's humility and bashfulness before his guru. "With head bowed" — *heṭa māthe*. "A faint smile" — *lahu hāsya*. "Tears" — *lohera* (*loha* = *lora*, "tears"). "[He sits, and]" — added for the connection; in the original the subject is "body".
[^6]: "Having offered himself" — *ātmanibedana kari'*: having surrendered himself wholly to his guru. "Crest-jewel of composure and steadiness" — *gāmbhīryya dhairyya śiromaṇi*. "Eagerly" — *kutūhale*. "Child" — *bāchā*, an affectionate address. "Explaining" — *bākhāni*.
[^7]: "On one side… on the other" — *ekamukhe … āre*: understood thus — he is drawn to speak, but bashfulness holds him back; it can also be taken as "on his lips — words". "Is shy" — *saṅkucita*. "On some pretext" — *kona chale*. "Before Shyamananda" — *śyāmānanda-sthāne*, lit. "in Shyamananda's presence".
[^8]: Shyama-pati (*śyāmāpati*), "husband of Shyama" — Rasika, husband of Shyamadasi (Purva, lahari 1, note 47); he is so named in the Uttara-vibhaga as well. Shuka (Shukadeva) is the speaker of the Bhagavata; Vyasa its compiler; Brihaspati the preceptor of the gods, a model of eloquence and learning. "In great splendour" — *mahāteje*. "One meaning in many ways" — *eka artha nānāchande*; cf. CC, Madhya 24, where Sri Chaitanya explains one verse in many ways.
[^9]: "Falls silent" — *tūṇi* (so in the scan; = *tūṣṇīm*, "silently"). "In vain have we studied" — *byartha paṛi*. "The very essence" — *sāroddhāra*, lit. "extraction of the essence".
[^10]: The Svami is Shridhara Svami, author of the Bhavartha-dipika commentary on the Bhagavata (Purva, lahari 9, note 14). "Establishing" — *thāpiẏā*. "Set your mind on Krishna" — *kṛṣṇe deha mati*.
[^11]: "Saying this" — *hena bali'*: understood as the pandits, having said this (9–10), praising Rasika; it can also be taken as "Rasikendra said this". "Began to follow it" — *kaila sei ācaraṇa*. Hunas, Pulindas, Pukkashas — foreign and forest peoples and low castes; mlechchhas — those outside Vedic practice; antyajas — the lowest castes. Cf. Bhagavatam 2.4.18 (Dakshina, lahari 4, note 4): *kirāta-hūṇāndhra-pulinda-pulkaśāḥ… śudhyanti* — they are purified by taking shelter of the Lord's devotees.
[^12]: "In that place" — *sei sthāne*: apparently Gopivallabhapura, where the Rasa-yatra took place (cf. 2: "in that village"). Raghunatha is Raghunatha Pattanayaka (15); not identified; apparently the holder of the village of Radhanagar (13, 16–17). "Brother" — *bhrāta* (so in the scan), i.e. his brother. "Tells everything" — *kahe sabe* (so in the scan), lit. "tells all"; understood as *saba*, "everything".
[^13]: Radhanagar — the village where Shyamananda first set up his home (Dakshina, lahari 11, note 16); not identified. "Yavana" — a Muslim. "Kindly come" — *bije haibe*: respectful of a holy person's going. "Having heard… judge" — *śuni prabhu saba bibaraṇa*, *bicāribe saba kathā*: the last half-line is taken as continuing the brother's speech.
[^14]: "Troubled at heart" — *ucāṭa mana*. Dharenda (*dhārandā*, so in the scan) — the village of Rasamaya and Vamshi (Dakshina, lahari 4, 22). "Stayed at Rasamaya's house" — *utarilā gṛhe rasamaẏa*, lit. "stayed at the house — Rasamaya": understood thus.
[^15]: Vamshi (Vamshidasa) — Rasamaya's brother, the author's uncle (Dakshina, lahari 4, note 14). Pattanayaka (*paṭṭanāyaka*; here *paṭṭanāẏeka*) — an honorific title of the Karanas of Utkala (Purva, lahari 3, note 23). "Went to them all" — *prabeśilā sabā pāśa*.
[^16]: "A villain" — *durācāra*: the yavana of 13. "Has seized the village" — *grāma harila*.
[^17]: "Devotees of Krishna" — *kārṣṇajana* (so in the scan; Purva, lahari 10, note 13). Ahmed Beg (*āhammada bega*) — apparently Ahmad Beg Khan, the Mughal governor of Orissa in the 1620s; the identification is tentative. Subahdar (*subā*) — governor (Purva, lahari 10, note 28). "Will hold" — *bhoga karibe*, lit. "will enjoy (have the use of)": the subject is not named — understood as Shyamananda. "For certain" — *niścaẏa*.
[^18]: "Understood the hint" — *iṅgita bujhena*. Banapur (*bānapura*, so here and in the colophon; *bāṇapura* in 20 and in lahari 7) — see note 1: possibly Banpur in Orissa (now Khurda district); according to lahari 7 the governor Ahmed Beg was staying there, which fits this identification, but it remains tentative.
[^19]: "Singers and musicians" — *saṅgīta-sāhitya*, lit. "singing and accompaniment": apparently the whole kirtan party (cf. lahari 7, 46). "As he wished" — *āpanāra manomata*: understood as Rasika's wish. "The servant Vamshi" — *dāsa baṃśī*: Vamshidasa (15).
[^20]: "Having made the auspicious setting-out" — *śubha anukūla kailā*: apparently the custom of setting out at an auspicious moment — going a little way and halting (*kata dūra giẏā railā*) before continuing the journey; the sense is approximate. The author's signature (the second half of the stanza) is given by the common formula; in the tripadi metre it reads: "Making the holy feet of Shyamananda an ornament on his head, the son of Rasamaya sings" (cf. Purva, lahari 5, note 14).
[^21]: Colophon (checked against the scan): "ইতি শ্রীরসিকমঙ্গল-পশ্চিম-বিভাগে বানপুরোদ্দেশ্যে গমন-নাম ষষ্ঠ-লহরী সম্পূর্ণা" (*bānapuroddeśye gamana*) — "The Departure for Banapur".
