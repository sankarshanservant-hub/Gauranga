# Purva-vibhaga. Lahari 6. Description of the Childhood Pastimes

*A verse in glory of Prabhu Murari and a song in glory of Shyamananda; the infant Rasika crawls about the courtyard and carries out everyone's requests; the rite of the first feeding with rice — among the objects laid out he chooses the Bhagavatam; games and kirtana with the children, ecstasy at the name of Krishna; honouring Tulasi, the ashvattha, the Vaishnavas and the brahmanas, cleaning the holy places; the townspeople's love for Achyuta's son*

*The sixth lahari.*

**S1.** *When the elephant of Kali has grown furious on the earth — him who makes known the meaning of the Vedas,*
*who is devoted to attentive meditation on the pair of lotus feet of Sri Vishnu,*
*who by the study of the shastras and reflection [on them] has become the rising of the root of bliss for the worlds —*
*O fools, worship him on earth, the glorious Prabhu Murari!*[^1]

*Raga Suhi.*[^2]
*Ghosha: "What shall I say of the beauty of Gopala's moon-like face?*
*It captivates the hearts of all the women of Vraja."*

**1.** Glory, glory to Shyamananda, the abode of all virtues!
Glory, glory [to him who is] the dear life of Rasikachandra!

**2.** Glory, glory to all the associates with their followers,
by hearing of whom one quickly attains Krishna!

**3.** Thus day by day Achyuta's son
crawls on all fours about the courtyard.

**4.** Now he rises, now he falls, now he rolls;
everyone lifts [him] up, crying: "Ah, ah!"

**5.** He constantly roams the courtyard at play;
all the things lying on the ground he handles as he goes.

**6.** "Bring the shoes, child, the wooden sandals!" says someone;
"The jug, the cup, the broom!" say others.[^3]

**7.** Joyfully toddling, Prabhu brings [the thing] and puts it on someone's lap;
no one's words does he disregard.

**8.** Prabhu carries out everyone's words:
rising and falling, he does [it] — he never disobeys.

**9.** Seeing [this], all the people are in an ocean of joy;
again and again they pass him from arms to arms.

**10.** Thus the time of the annaprashana came;
the townspeople informed Achyuta [of it].[^4]

**11.** Hearing this, he respectfully sends out messengers:
"From [all the] lands bring all kinds of provisions quickly!"

**12.** Summoning learned brahmanas who knew the Vedas,
he has the Vedas and [sacred] learning recited on all sides.

**13.** Inviting them, he called together his kinsfolk,
throngs upon throngs of women, and his dear ones and friends.

**14.** Respectfully greeting everyone, Achyuta says:
"Give your permission: [my] son will undergo the annaprashana."

**15.** Hearing this, all the panditas say: "Good, good!
Arrange the homa, the auspicious rites and the rest, set up the ghata — everything [as is fitting]."[^5]

**16.** They adorned the house with colourful cloths;
chamaras and pendants hang down — most beautiful.

**17.** Grinding rice into powder, all the young women
decorated the place of the meal in many different ways.[^6]

**18.** In its midst they placed in due order
grain, cow dung and the like, a conch, silver and gold,

**19.** a stylus, palm leaves, paper, a reed pen —
and they placed the book of the Srimad-Bhagavatam.[^7]

**20.** Having completed all the auspicious rites, they adorned
Rasika-shekhara's whole body with ornaments.

**21.** Dusted with sandal, kunkuma and musk,
his beautiful face is enchanting, surpassing the autumn moon.

**22.** On his lovely forehead shines a fine [line] of gorochana;[^8]
seeing that beauty, everyone is enchanted.

**23.** Thus, taking the boy on her lap, the mother
[and] Rasika's father joyfully went and sat down.

**24.** All the kinsfolk and brahmanas sit on all sides;
the brahmanas on their part began the Vedic mantras and the homa.[^9]

**25.** Music and the roar of the dundubhi sound without pause;
the women cry "Jaya, jaya!" and raise the hulahuli.

**26.** Adorning the middle of that room,
they set out kshira, pithas and pakkanna.

**27.** Seating Rasika on a pindi,
the young women say to him again and again:[^10]

**28.** "Listen, listen, child, Rasika-shekhara:
whatever pleases you, bring that first, quickly!"

**29.** Hearing everyone's words, he looked around;
seeing the Srimad-Bhagavatam, [his] eyes [filled] with tears.

**30.** Drawing the book with both hands, he brings it
and clasps it firmly to his heart.

**31.** Holding the Bhagavatam to his breast, he began to weep:
sweat, trembling, tears and hair standing on end appeared.

**32.** Absorbed in Krishna, in the rasa of prema, he weeps;
all the men and women see a wonder.

**33.** Some say: "This boy is no human being:
surely a dear devotee of Sri Krishna has been born."

**34.** Some say: "He will deliver all living beings."
Others say: "[He has] descended (avatara) to uphold dharma."[^11]

**35.** Some say: "Achyuta is supremely fortunate."
Each praises [him] as it comes to his mind.

**36.** Thus having respectfully performed the annaprashana,
[Achyuta] promptly bade the brahmanas farewell.

**37.** Then that same day, gathering all the kinsfolk,
he feeds [them] with all kinds of [dishes] of the six tastes.[^12]

**38.** The young women, with great respect,
the lady herself, sitting [with them], feeds.

**39.** Having fed them, she gave [them] camphor and betel,
[put] sandal and kunkuma on their bodies and sindur on their heads.

**40.** All the women, taking the son on their laps
and praising Bhavani, took their leave and went.

**41.** Thus after some days the son of Achyuta
incessantly roams about the whole village.

**42.** With ten or twenty children of the same age
he incessantly plays many games in many ways.

**43.** One day all the children, clapping their hands,
hold sankirtana, and he moves in their midst, dancing.

**44.** [Seeing] the children's play, the townspeople —
[all] among them who were devotees of Krishna —

**45.** seeing the children's kirtana, [were] in an ocean of joy:
"Such understanding in a child we have never seen!"

**46.** Together with the children all sing Krishna's name;
saying, "Dance, child!", all clap their hands.

**47.** As soon as he heard Krishna's name, he lost consciousness:
his voice choked, tears on his beautiful moonlike face.

**48.** With the hair standing on end all over his body, he fell to the ground;
rolling [on the ground], he began to weep loudly.

**49.** Hearing [this], all the people came there;
with still greater joy they sing of Krishna's qualities.

**50.** Some cry "Hari!" again and again;
hearing [it], the son of Achyuta dances with joy.

**51.** From whosesoever mouth he hears Krishna's name,
with the dust of that person's feet he adorns [his] body.

**52.** Seeing the child's deeds, people are amazed;
all say: "This boy is no human being."

**53.** The boy's feelings cannot be described at all:
hearing Krishna's name, [he displays] the eight sattvika [bhavas].[^13]

**54.** Saying this, all lift [him] to their breasts;
snatching [him] from one another, they take [him] again and again.

**55.** All the men and women say to Achyuta:
"What can be told of your son is beyond telling.

**56.** What understanding has an infant [to know] whom they call Krishna?
[Yet] as soon as he hears [of] Krishna, tears [flow] and his hair stands on end.

**57.** Whoever utters 'Krishna, Krishna' —
with the dust of that person's feet he adorns himself.

**58.** [He] who wept on hearing Krishna's name —
this boy is no human being, we tell [you] plainly."

**59.** Hearing everyone's words, Achyuta says:
"May [my] son live, receiving the dust of the feet of you all!"

**60.** For his son's sake he humbly entreats everyone:
"All of you, bless this boy!"

**61.** Taking the boy in his arms, he came home.
In this way every day [Rasika] wanders about the village.

**62.** With him are ten or twenty boys of the same age,
and five or seven devotees of Krishna stay around [him].

**63.** From childhood he keeps all dharma:
[he honours] the ashvattha, Tulasi, the dhatri, Vaishnavas and brahmanas.[^14]

**64.** Day by day his understanding is ever more kindled;
apart from establishing dharma he knows nothing [else].

**65.** Not for a moment does he stay quietly at home:
in the joy of Krishna he wanders from village to village.

**66.** All know [him] as the son of the ruler of the land;[^15]
moreover, that enchanting form enchants everyone's heart.

**67.** Affectionately, vying with one another,
all carry Achyuta's son off to their own homes.

**68.** Seeing [his] moonlike face, they feel as if they had found millions of jewels;
clasping [him] to their breasts, they carry [him] off — great happiness in their hearts.

**69.** Bringing him home, setting out an excellent seat,
they seat the son of Achyuta on it.

**70.** Laddus, sandesh, cream from milk, fine sugar,
many offerings — ripe [fruits], water [sweet as] nectar —[^16]

**71.** all bring and give to Rasika;
seeing those offerings, he is glad at heart.

**72.** He takes all those things to Tulasi
and, in his own play, offers [them] to Krishna.

**73.** Circling Tulasi, he dances and claps his hands;
with the children — sankirtana, with many merry games.

**74.** Incessantly he passes his time in this joy;
seeing [it], the townspeople marvel.

**75.** After a moment, taking all those things himself,
he goes and gives the first portion to the brahmanas and Vaishnavas.

**76.** All respectfully accept [it], holding out their hands;
regarding [it] as prasada, they praise the boy:

**77.** "This boy's character cannot be described;
it seems [this] son will establish dharma."

**78.** Then he gives the offerings to all the children,
and afterwards takes a little [of it] himself.

**79.** In this way he wanders about the village day and night;
the joy of all the people cannot be contained.

**80.** Wherever [there is] a place of Krishna, wherever a sadhu dwells —
joyfully visiting them all, [he] wanders day and night.

**81.** A temple of Krishna or a devotee's dwelling,
the ashvattha, Tulasi, the dhatri, [the abodes of] brahmanas, holy places —

**82.** if he sees that these places are unclean,
he goes there with the children.

**83.** Quickly bringing clay, cow dung and water,
he cleanses the place thoroughly.

**84.** With his own hands he smears all those places:
in a moment they shine like Vaikuntha.[^17]

**85.** In this way, on the roads, at the ghats, in village after village,
he goes about constantly, cleansing the holy places.

**86.** Thus in his childhood the son of Achyuta
leads all people to keep dharma.

**87.** Seeing this deed of the child, [people] are in an ocean of shame;
from the very next day all [themselves] keep things clean.

**88.** Bringing soft grass with his own hands,
he serves the cows, giving [them] grass and water.

**89.** If on the road he sees a Vaishnava [or] a brahmana,
with the greatest respect he bows to [his] feet.

**90.** Joining his palms, with humility,
he greets everyone, smiling slightly.

**91.** How sweet [his] smile, [how] light [his] words!
In joy all the people take [him] in their arms.

**92.** All say: "Ah, little one, [may] your misfortunes [fall on us]![^18]
Where did you learn all these ways?

**93.** How did you learn thus to keep dharma —
to bow to Vaishnavas, sannyasis and brahmanas,

**94.** to clean the places of the gods and the Tulasi platforms?[^19]
Where, child, did you learn all these deeds?"

**95.** Thus in many ways the townspeople,
questioning [him], take [him] in their arms again and again.

**96.** Hundreds upon hundreds of kisses they give [him] on the face;
no one has a mind to set [him] down on the ground.

**97.** In their arms they carry [him] to Achyuta's house,
all snatching [him] from one another again and again.

**98.** All joyfully say to Achyuta:
"This son of yours is surely no human being.

**99.** Seeing his signs, [we] are amazed:
as soon as he hears Krishna's name, [tears] pour in a hundred streams."

**100.** Hearing Krishna's name, with eyes full of tears,
he came [and found himself] on Achyuta's lap.

**101.** Taking Rasika-shekhara on his lap and brushing off the dust,
[Achyuta] promptly has [him] bathed, fed and so on.

**102.** Thus day by day, growing older,
the son of Achyuta performs various pastimes.

**103.** The Rasika-mangala is very sweet to hear:
joyfully hearing [this] fair glory, cross over [the age of] Kali.

**104.** Adorning himself with Shyamananda's feet,
the son of Rasamaya joyfully composed [this].

*Thus ends the sixth lahari, called "Description of the Childhood Pastimes," in the Purva-vibhaga of the Sri Rasika-mangala.*

[^1]: The verse is unnumbered in the edition (after it come the raga, the ghosha and the song beginning with ॥১॥); it is marked S1, like the mangala-shlokas of lahari 1 (lahari 1, note 1). "The elephant of Kali" — *kali-vāraṇa*; *vāraṇa* means both "elephant" and "warding off, restraining," so "[who appeared] on earth to restrain the blazing [age of] Kali" is also possible. Prabhu Murari is Rasikananda (Rasika-Murari, lahari 5, 13–15). The source of the verse is not identified — apparently the author's own composition.
[^2]: Raga Suhi (*suhī*, also Suhai) — a raga frequently found in Vaishnava padas; cf. BR 15 ("raga Suhai").
[^3]: "Shoes" — *pāṇḍoi*, "wooden sandals" — *kaṭhāu*: the meanings follow the edition's glosses (*jutā*, "footwear"; *khaṛama*, "wooden sandals"). That is, the household playfully gives the infant errands.
[^4]: Annaprashana (*anna-prāśana*, "eating of rice") — the samskara of a child's first feeding with rice (usually in the sixth to eighth month of life).
[^5]: Homa — a fire sacrifice. Ghata (*ghaṭa*) — a water-pot (mangala-ghata) set up at the beginning of a rite as an auspicious seat of the deities.
[^6]: That is, they drew auspicious designs (alpana) in rice paste on the spot where the child would be fed.
[^7]: 18–19 and 27–30 describe a custom of the annaprashana: grain, earth or cow dung, a conch, silver and gold, writing materials and a book are laid out before the child; what he takes first is considered a sign of his future (wealth, learning, piety). A stylus (*lekhanī*) is for writing on palm leaves; a *kalam* is a reed pen.
[^8]: Gorochana — a bright yellow pigment used for auspicious marks on the forehead.
[^9]: "On their part" — *dvija-bhāge*; the reading in the scan is clear; it may also be understood as "in the part [of the assembly] where the brahmanas [sat]."
[^10]: Kshira — thickened milk; pitha — rice cakes; pakkanna — dishes fried in ghee. Pindi (*piṁṛā*) — a low wooden seat.
[^11]: So the townspeople say. In the Gaudiya Vaishnava understanding Rasikananda is not an avatara of the Lord but an eternal associate and dear devotee (cf. 33 and lahari 4, 105: "an associate of Krishna has been born for the deliverance of living beings"); *avatāra* here is in the broad sense of "one who has descended [into the world]."
[^12]: The couplet number is defectively printed in the scan (it looks like "৩০"); by sequence it is 37. "The six tastes" — see lahari 3, note 13.
[^13]: The eight sattvika bhavas (*aṣṭa-sāttvika*) — involuntary bodily manifestations of love for Krishna: stupefaction, perspiration, hair standing on end, faltering of the voice, trembling, change of complexion, tears and fainting (*Bhakti-rasamrita-sindhu* 2.3). Cf. 31, 47–48.
[^14]: Ashvattha — the sacred fig (pipal); dhatri — the amalaki (emblic myrobalan). Together with Tulasi they are honoured by watering, circumambulation and obeisances.
[^15]: "The son of the ruler of the land" — *rājya-adhipati-suta*; cf. lahari 4, 2: Achyuta "lives in royal splendour."
[^16]: Laddus and sandesh are sweets; "cream" — *sara*, the skin of boiled milk. "Ripe [fruits], water [sweet as] nectar" — *supakka amṛta pāni*; it may also be understood as "well-cooked [food], nectarean water."
[^17]: Smearing the floor and courtyard with a mixture of clay, cow dung and water is the traditional way of purifying a place.
[^18]: "[May] your misfortunes [fall on us]" — *nichāni tomāra*: an affectionate exclamation; *nichāni* is the custom of "warding off" misfortune from a loved one by taking it upon oneself.
[^19]: "The Tulasi platforms" — *tulasī cauṛā*; corrected in the edition's list of errata to *caurā*: a raised platform for the Tulasi plant (cf. lahari 3, note 17: tulasi-mancha).
