# Uttara-vibhaga. Lahari 8. The Manifestation of His Own Glory at Muktapur on the Way to Nilachala

*A song in glory of Shyamananda; hearing the message from the brahmanas' lips, Rasika sets out for Nilachala with singers and men of letters and countless sadhus, kings, subjects and pilgrims; in every village people marvel at the thousands of his companions and bring sida, and Rasika has the gifts distributed first to the elders, Vaishnavas and brahmanas, and then to all in turn; the eight siddhis and nine treasures follow him unfailingly; at Muktapur the wicked headman of the village refuses shelter to thousands of Vaishnavas: "Stay under the trees," — and Rasika, reproaching him in jest, gladly settles with everyone under the trees; a north wind rises, fire flares up and burns hundreds of large houses; in fear the villagers say: "Rasika has brought ruin," fall at his feet and beg for protection; Rasika, hearing the praise, looking with nectarean eyes, his voice faltering with the bliss of Krishna, asks the fire to spare the wretched, the fire is at once put out, and all the people of the village become Rasika's servants*

*The eighth lahari.*

*Raga Varadi — panchali metre.*[^1]

**1.** Glory to Shyamananda, revered in the three worlds,
whose vow is to purify the world!
The life of the world, the life of Rasika,
mercy to suffering people![^2]

**2.** Hearing the message from the brahmanas' lips, Rasikendra
set out on the journey, in order to go.
Singers and men of letters, as many as there were,
he took with him as his companions.[^3]

**3.** With him — countless sadhus, crowd upon crowd;
he set out [on the way] joyfully.
In whatever village he halts — [people] are astonished,
[seeing] thousands upon thousands with him.

**4.** Kings and subjects, Vaishnavas, brahmanas,
with him — pilgrims without number.
Wherever the son of Achyuta stays,
[they] bring [him] sida of every kind.[^4]

**5.** The lord of the village [places] before Rasika
provisions of every kind.
Seeing [this], Rasikendra-chandra in joy
gives a command to his companions:

**6.** "First distribute to the elders, the Vaishnavas, the brahmanas —
as befits [each];
then — to the pilgrims, the kings and the subjects,
to all, one after another."[^5]

**7.** Wherever Rasika-shekhara halts,
the eight siddhis [and] nine treasures —
all these with him
unfailingly travel; splendour — as befits.[^6]

**8.** Going along the way, at Muktapur
Prabhu stopped.
The headman there, a wicked villain,
spoke harsh words:[^7]

**9.** "For a thousand Vaishnavas, kings, subjects — all,
brahmanas, sannyasis and the rest
no house will be found: stay under the trees," —
so firmly said [this] "wise man."[^8]

**10.** Hearing [this], the servants say to Prabhu:
"No house will be found here.
All [are to stay] under the trees, together, —
so says the headman."

**11.** Hearing this, Rasika says in jest:
"Many houses [you] have enjoyed;
my sadhus will stay outside —
for what purpose [then] did [you] build houses?

**12.** Very well, let us all go and stay under the trees,
gladly arranging our seats."
Surrounding Rasika, joyfully remain
with him all the mahajanas.[^9]

**13.** Soon a north wind began to blow —
unexpectedly fire rose up.
Blazing fiercely, on all sides burn
the large houses, by the hundreds.

**14.** From one side much caught fire —
goods of every kind, clothing, all there was.
Distraught with fear, [people] say to one another:
"Rasika has brought ruin!"[^10]

**15.** In fear all quickly went
[and] fell at Prabhu's feet:
"O stronghold of refuge, possessor of all virtues,
protect [us] all, Prabhu!

**16.** Who will know the limit of your glory?
[Your] vow is to crush the proud.
I am a villain, I disregarded [you] —
[be] merciful to suffering people!"[^11]

**17.** Hearing the words of praise, Rasikendra-mani
looks with nectarean eyes.
Immersed in the rasa of Krishna's bliss, in a faltering voice
he speaks sweet words:[^12]

**18.** "O Brahma, servant of Hari, do not destroy
all these wretched people!
Forgive the living beings, burn no more —
listen to my words."[^13]

**19.** Hearing Rasika's words, Agni
saw Prabhu with [his own] eyes.
The fiercely blazing, boundless heat [of his]
he put out at once.[^14]

**20.** Seeing [this], all the people deemed a wonder
the manifestation [of the power] of Rasika.
All the people of the village fell at [his] feet
[and] became Rasika's servants.

**21.** To Rasika's greatness there is no limit —
listen, all people!
Adorning himself with Shyamananda's feet,
the son of Rasamaya joyfully composed [this].[^15]

*Thus in the Uttara-vibhaga of the Sri Rasika-mangala ends the eighth lahari, called "The Manifestation of His Own Glory at Muktapur on the Way to Nilachala."*

[^2]: "Whose vow is to purify the world" — *bhubanapābana-bānā*: *bānā* — "vow, mark, garb" (Dakshina, lahari 9, note 2, where it is understood as "garb"; Uttara, lahari 3, note 20); cf. 16. "Mercy to suffering people" — *duḥkhita jane karuṇā*; the same line ends 16.
[^3]: "The message from the brahmanas' lips" — of the two brahmanas sent by the king (lahari 7, 46, 52–59). "Set out on the journey" — *yātrā kaila*. "Singers and men of letters" — *saṅgīta-sāhitya*, literally "[all there was of] music [and] letters": understood as people versed in song and poetry. "Companions" — *anucara*.
[^4]: "Countless" (3) — *apramita*. "Halts" — *utare*. "Sida" (4) — *sidā*, uncooked provisions offered to a guest (Pashchima, lahari 2). "Of every kind" — *nānāmate*.
[^5]: "The elders" — *gurujana*: venerable persons, those senior in standing. "As befits [each]" — *yathāyogya-rūpe*. "Distribute" — *diẏe*.
[^6]: The eight siddhis — mystic perfections (the power to become smallest, lightest and so on; Bhag. 11.15); the nine treasures (*naba nidhi*) — the treasures of Kubera. The author's praise: according to the Bhagavata (11.14.14), a pure devotee does not desire siddhis; here they themselves follow Rasika like servants. "Splendour — as befits" — *baibhaba yathābidhi* (on the scan *baïbhaba*).
[^7]: Muktapur (*mukutāpura*; in the colophon *muktāpura*) is not identified; on the way from Gopivallabhapur to Puri, before Dhamnagar and Jajpur (lahari 9, from the OCR of pdf 193). "Headman" — *pradhāna*, the village headman. "A wicked villain" — *duṣṭa durajana*.
[^8]: "A thousand" — *sahasra* (so on the scan; OCR *sahaja*; likewise in 3). "Wise man" — *sudhī*, "wise": apparently ironical, of the headman; the sense is approximate.
[^9]: "In jest" (11) — *kautuke* (on the scan *koütuke*; likewise in 12). "Have enjoyed" — *bhoga kailā*. "For what purpose did you build houses" — *ki kāryye ghara tulilā*. "Mahajanas" (12) — *mahājana*, venerable Vaishnavas of Rasika's retinue.
[^10]: "North wind" (13) — *uttarā pabana*. "Has brought ruin" (14) — *kailā nipāte*. On how anger at or contempt for Rasika turns into calamity — cf. lahari 5 (Raja Ramachandra Dhal) and lahari 3; on the author's attitude to Rasika — lahari 7, note 9.
[^11]: "Stronghold of refuge" (15) — *śaraṇa-pañjara*, literally "cage (enclosure) of refuge." "Vow to crush the proud" (16) — *sagarbba-dalana-bānā* (note 2). "Disregarded" — *helana karilu*. The villagers' speech, apparently in the person of the headman ("I am a villain"). pdf 192 ends with a complete stanza 16; Rasika's words to the fire and the extinguishing of the blaze are in 17–21 on pdf 193.
[^12]: Pdf 193 (printed p. 149) begins with stanza 17 — a continuation of RM-4-08-001-016 (pdf 192 ends with the complete stanza 16; there is no stanza running over). At the top of the page there are two columns of tripadi (17–19a | 19b–21); the stanza is set in four lines and is translated likewise. "Rasikendra-mani" — *rasikendra-maṇi*, "the jewel among rasikas." "With nectarean eyes" — *amṛtanaẏane*. "In a faltering voice" — *gada gada bhāse*: *gadgada* is speech choked by overflowing feeling, one of the signs of prema.
[^13]: Rasika addresses the fire. *brahmā haridāsa* — clear on the scan (cut-out ×2; the OCR is garbled), the sense is unclear: apparently an address to Agni, the deity of fire, called "Brahma" (in the Vedic hymns Agni is the brahmana priest of the sacrifice) and "servant of Hari" (the demigods serve the Lord); another understanding is possible. "Do not destroy" — *nā kara bināśa*. "The living beings" — *jībe*.
[^14]: Agni (*agni*) is the deity of fire. "Saw Prabhu with [his own] eyes" — *dekhila prabhu naẏane*: the fire, as a living being, beheld Rasika. "Put out" — *nibhāila*; the subject is apparently Agni (it could also be understood as "[Prabhu] put out"). On how the author exalts Rasika, see lahari 7, note 9.
[^15]: "The manifestation [of the power]" (20) — *parakāśa*. "There is no limit" (21) — *dite nāhi sīmā*, literally "a limit cannot be set." Purva, lahari 5, note 14).
