# Purva-vibhaga. Lahari 10. Description of the Pastimes of Adolescence

*A song in glory of Shyamananda and Rasikananda; reading the Tenth Canto of the Bhagavata with Hari Dube, Rasika hears that Krishna did not return to Vraja and, weeping like the gopis in separation, wanders the forests for seven days without food or water; Achyuta and the townspeople find him and carry him home; Dube, citing Rupa's Bhagavatamrita, explains to him that Krishna returned to Vraja and never leaves it, and Rasika comes back to himself; Achyuta entrusts his son to Dube; Rasika becomes a master of all the shastras, composes songs and shlokas, and his detachment grows; Achyuta looks for a bride for him; Balabhadra Dasa, the ruler of Hijli, and his daughter Ichchha-dei; Achyuta frees Balabhadra, detained by the subadar in Medinipur over arrears, and Balabhadra, seeing Rasika, offers him his daughter in marriage*

*The tenth lahari.*

*Raga Narayani-gauda.*[^1]
*Ghosha: "My Krishna is a treasury of virtues,*
*the refuge of the helpless, the utmost limit of mercy."*

**1.** Glory, glory to Shyamananda, the son of Durika!
Glory to the son of Bhavani, Rasikendra-chandra![^2]

**2.** Thus together with Dube, [immersed] in the rasa of the Bhagavata,
he reads the Tenth [Canto] with love and with special [care].

**3.** One day he reads the Tenth [Canto] with Hari Dube —
[of how] the wives of Vraja, in separation, [pine] with love for Krishna.[^3]

**4.** Krishna went to Mathura and did not come back to Vraja —
hearing [this], Rasika fell senseless to the ground.

**5.** Hearing with his own ears that Krishna did not come back to Vraja,
[he thinks]: "How did the gopis keep their lives?

**6.** Parted from Krishna, the lord of [their] lives, how did they survive?" —
saying this again and again, he began to weep.

**7.** Sobbing and sobbing, he weeps and rolls on the ground;
the eight sattvika-[bhavas] arose in him.[^4]

**8.** Down [his] Sri moonlike face flow hundreds and hundreds of streams;
torrents of tears pour from his eyes without cease.

**9.** All over his body the hairs stand on end, his voice falters, [he is drowning in tears]:
"Krishna, lord of my life, to what land have You gone?"

**10.** He weeps, distraught with love for Krishna;
hearing the weeping, all the people came.

**11.** Everyone says: "Why is [this] best of children weeping,
whose father is the lord of the realm in Mallabhumi?[^5]

**12.** What thing does [he] not obtain, what task is beyond [him]?
Perhaps some wicked person has done [him] harm?"

**13.** What is in his heart, no one knows;
different mouths say different things without end.

**14.** Prabhu does not bathe, does not eat,
does not ponder his books — he weeps incessantly.

**15.** Prabhu does not stay at home; he is always [as if] mad;
everyone laments, [but] they do not know the truth [of it].

**16.** Day and night, wandering and wandering, he roams from forest to forest;
alone, weeping, he roams in the dense thicket.

**17.** In the forest he has no fear of tigers or bears;
in love for Krishna he has no awareness of the outer [world] and wanders all the time.

**18.** Coming into a terrible forest, he falls face down;
rolling and rolling [on the ground], he weeps and sighs heavily:

**19.** "O lord of life, why have You become so cruel?
For what fault did You leave the unfortunate gopis, and where have You gone?[^6]

**20.** For Your sake they gave up husbands, sons and homes;
leaving such beloveds, where have You gone, O best of the Yadus?

**21.** Family, good conduct, shame, fear — they know nothing of these;
like a shadow they always go about with You.

**22.** In hunger — food, in thirst — water the gopis did not take;
You left them all and went, leaving them without shelter.[^7]

**23.** Day and night they see You, lying [awake] and in dreams.
How did the cowherd boys and the cows survive?

**24.** How did Nanda and the grieving Yashoda survive?
Without You, how did they keep their lives?

**25.** The bank of the Yamuna weeps, remembering You;
the trees and creepers, the deer and the birds cannot steady their hearts.[^8]

**26.** How could You become so heartless, leaving them all?" —
remembering and remembering, he weeps, rolling on the ground.

**27.** Thus [his] absorption lasted seven days;
Achyuta's son gave up food and water.[^9]

**28.** He wandered from forest to forest, not knowing day from night;
meanwhile the townspeople go about searching [for him] on all sides.

**29.** Achyuta came home from the royal court
and heard that his son had gone into the forest.

**30.** "Where has [my] boy gone?" — saying this, he fell to the ground;
everyone rushed quickly to lift him up.

**31.** Getting up, he weeps, calling and calling:
"Into which forest has [my] son gone? Go and search!"

**32.** Hundreds and hundreds of people went at Achyuta's command;
in distress they all rush to search.

**33.** Together with Achyuta they went, weeping and weeping;
from forest to forest they all search with anxious hearts.

**34.** [They] saw Rasika rolling on the ground;
the radiance of [his] body was lighting up the forest.

**35.** [His] form, surpassing Kandarpa, captivates the heart;
his Sri moonlike face is beautiful to see.[^10]

**36.** His curling locks of hair lie spread on the ground.
Seeing his son, Achyuta [feels his] heart break.

**37.** "Alas, alas, my son!" — saying this, he lifted [him] and took him in his arms;
Prabhu does not open his eyes; tears flow.

**38.** Gladdened, they all came home,
[carrying] the beautiful Rasika on their chests.

**39.** At home they all saw the son's face:
on his Sri moonlike face — streams [of tears], his eyes closed.

**40.** Whatever they do, the weeping does not cease;
seeing [this], all the townspeople were amazed.

**41.** Some say: "[It is] because of the glance of an evil person";
some say: "A strong wind has overcome the son."[^11]

**42.** Thus different people [propose] different remedies;
whatever anyone says, that they do again and again.

**43.** By no means does the child say a word;
he does not look, does not eat, his head hung low.

**44.** Prabhu weeps incessantly, distraught;
seeing all this, Achyuta can barely hold on to life.

**45.** Humbly he speaks to Hari Dube:
"My son has given up food — how will he live?"

**46.** Dube says: "Do not worry at all in your mind:
he is intoxicated with feeling for Krishna and is aware of nothing [else].

**47.** This son of yours is a great soul;
this child will deliver the whole world."

**48.** Then Hari Dube speaks to Rasika,
speaking with care what accords with the shastras:

**49.** "Gathering together the words of all the shastras,
Rupa composed the book Bhagavatamrita.

**50.** In it he established [that] the separation [lasted] three months:
the great Krishna came to Vraja again.

**51.** Krishna never leaves Vraja —
this essential truth of the shastras no one knows.[^12]

**52.** He to whom Krishna in His mercy gives prema-bhakti
gives this explanation — the evidence of the shastras.

**53.** This is the meaning hidden in the Vedas; the devotees of Krishna know it;
[only] one who has taken exclusive refuge knows this secret."[^13]

**54.** [Rasika] heard from Dube's mouth that Krishna came to Vraja:
[he] told Rasika the essence of all the shastras.

**55.** Hearing Dube's words, filled with joy,
the lord of [our] life rose, [exclaiming]: "Sri Krishna!"[^14]

**56.** Dube told all this in private;
hearing those words, [Rasika] was rid of the turmoil of his mind.

**57.** Joyfully and humbly Achyuta says:
"Dube Mahashaya has made [my] son well.

**58.** I cannot repay you this debt:
today you have given Murari to me."[^15]

**59.** Joyfully Achyuta pays his respects to Dube:
[offering him] a bath, worship [and] feeding him sweet dishes,

**60.** giving [him] many clothes and much wealth, he humbly said:
"Mahashaya, do not leave Rasika even for a moment.

**61.** Having given the boy to you, I am free of care:
you will look after the child, [and] I have no fear."

**62.** Bowing to him, [Achyuta] took his son on his lap,
and they joyfully bathed, ate and so on.

**63.** Seeing [his] moonlike face, the fortunate Achyuta
goes about day and night [with him], holding Rasika as [his] wealth and life.

**64.** Thus, in various joys, [Rasika] lives in his own home;
day by day he became a great scholar.

**65.** The best of children became endowed with all virtues;
no one is able to answer [him] face to face.

**66.** Rasikendra-chandra is expert in all the shastras;
the host of pandits cannot establish [their own] conclusions [before him].

**67.** All the things people long to hear —
hearing [them] in that nectarean speech, they forget themselves.

**68.** Alone he examines all the shastras;
stunned, [people] by hundreds and hundreds listen without cease.

**69.** All the great scholars listen to Rasika's expositions;
no one has the power to refute [them].

**70.** Hearing the sweet explanations from that sweet mouth,
all living beings then float in bliss.

**71.** Thus day and night in the delights of learning
Rasika-chandra engages in his own home.[^16]

**72.** [He is] utterly detached; [his] mind does not stay at home;
from forest to forest he wanders constantly in solitude.[^17]

**73.** With love for Krishna [his] body is always overwhelmed;
from childhood he constantly composes songs.

**74.** In childhood he composes shlokas with love;
no one in the world can find fault [with them].

**75.** On some days, sitting alone in solitude,
he weeps incessantly with love for Krishna.

**76.** Thus in childhood bhava arises in him;
day by day his love for Krishna, full of rasa, grows.

**77.** Such is Prabhu's conduct in childhood and early boyhood:
constantly absorbed in Krishna, he weeps.[^18]

**78.** Sometimes he reads books, sitting in solitude;
sometimes he performs worship, absorbed in meditation.

**79.** Sometimes he composes songs in various languages;
sometimes he composes shlokas with various poetic meanings.[^19]

**80.** Sometimes [he holds] discussions of the shastras with everyone.
Thus [a good] while passed in childhood and early boyhood.

**81.** Adolescence, youth, maturity, old age and so on —
[through these] I shall describe a little of the nature of Rasika-Murari.

**82.** On entering adolescence, [his] form captivates the heart;
[his] beauty, surpassing Kandarpa, is lovely to see.

**83.** [His] body is always [as if] maddened by detachment;
in love for Krishna he wanders the forests without cease.

**84.** Household affairs and dealings do not please him at all;
Achyuta understood in his heart that detachment had arisen in him.

**85.** Thinking to himself of [his] marriage,
[Achyuta] carefully looks for a suitable family to ally with.[^20]

**86.** At that time the ruler of the land of Hijli,
Sadashiva's brother, bearing the name Balabhadra,[^21]

**87.** — his paternal uncle, Vibhishana Mahapatra,
always serves there in the royal retinue —

**88.** was the lord of a domain and very wealthy besides:
in the land of Hijli there was no one so fortunate.

**89.** Of valuables of many kinds — gems, diamonds, pearls, corals,
cloths surpassing gold, money — beyond counting.[^22]

**90.** His cows cannot be counted, his grain is beyond measure;
seeing [his] wealth, [even] the maharaja is amazed.

**91.** Thus Balabhadra Dasa lives there;
he dwells [in splendour] in the land of Hijli.

**92.** He has a daughter, most fortunate;
she is the darling of Lakshmi, very beautiful.[^23]

**93.** Endowed with all auspicious marks, supremely lovely;
in beauty and virtues there is no equal to her in the world.

**94.** The beauty of [her] lotus face cannot be described at all;
should Manasija see that form, [even] he is bewildered.

**95.** The beauty of every limb is most enchanting;
her gait, slow as a royal she-elephant's, is exceedingly lovely.

**96.** On all her limbs are ornaments — divine adornments;
apart from patanet silk she wears nothing else.[^24]

**97.** Her body is most tender, her speech soft and soft;
there is no comparison to give [for her] — [even] Ananga [is fit only] as a nichhani offering [for her].[^25]

**98.** Her name is renowned as Ichchha-dei Thakurani;
the Creator fashioned a girl equal to Rasika.[^26]

**99.** Balabhadra's daughter is endowed with every virtue;
from childhood she serves Krishna — she who [will be] devoted to her husband.

**100.** Taking along girls of her own age,
she installs a Deity of Krishna and worships [Him] with many delights.[^27]

**101.** At the end of the worship, bowing down, she begs a boon:
"Give me such a husband — one equal to Krishna.

**102.** Birth after birth I [will be] his maidservant always —
this is my plea at Your feet, O Lord."

**103.** Such is the renowned daughter of Balabhadra.
The account of her wedding — listen attentively.

**104.** At the order of the king of that country, Balabhadra
goes, taking the revenue and various goods.[^28]

**105.** In Medinipur, to the Padishah's subadar,
he came for an audience with the revenue and goods.

**106.** A lakh of rupees in arrears stands against the land of Hijli:
as soon as [the subadar] saw him, he had him taken into custody.

**107.** Balabhadra's messenger, in distress, quickly
tells everything in detail to Achyuta:

**108.** "Giving some money, [he] came for an audience with the subadar;
at the audience [the subadar] took [him] into custody because of the arrears."[^29]

**109.** Hearing all this, Achyuta quickly,
astonished, met with the subadar.

**110.** The subadar cannot break Achyuta's word:
once he stands [before him], he forgives tens of millions of faults.[^30]

**111.** He spoke to the subadar about Balabhadra's case:
"I will pay this money — release him altogether."

**112.** Hearing Achyuta's words, [the subadar] released [him] at once;
taking Balabhadra along, [Achyuta] went home.

**113.** Hand in hand the two go, [enjoying] many conversations,
and then arrived at Achyuta's abode.

**114.** He was honoured in many ways:
[served] sweet dishes to eat, [given] fine clothes to wear.

**115.** Seated on a seat, he chews betel with camphor.
At that moment Rasika comes to that place.

**116.** His curling locks of hair are tied up gracefully;
his cheeks are long, his face surpasses the full moon.

**117.** A finely shaped nose shines [between] those two eyes;
pearls glitter and shine in both ears.[^31]

**118.** Surpassing lightning, [gleams] the row of teeth [like] pomegranate seeds;
on his Shri face is a gentle, gentle smile — [how much] radiance!

**119.** His voice surpasses the kokila, at his beautifully red lips;
steeped in nectar are [his] half-spoken (lisping) words.

**120.** A double gold kanthi on his neck;
a coral and pearl necklace sways upon his chest.[^32]

**121.** On arms reaching to the knees bracelets shine;
lovely is his belly, his navel deep and radiant.

**122.** On his waist, [slimmer than] a lion's, fine cloth shines;
his two thighs appear [like] emerald pillars.

**123.** His tender feet are lovely to see;
the row of his nails glitters, enchanting.

**124.** His two lotus feet are red, redder than red dye;
with a book in hand he walks like the best of dancers.[^33]

**125.** A cloth, folded double, on his shoulder;
his gait slow as a lordly elephant's, his bearing beautiful.

**126.** Achyuta's son, intoxicated with love for Krishna,
went to Balabhadra and came [before him].

**127.** Seeing Rasika's form, [Balabhadra] was struck with wonder;
gazing [at him], Balabhadra fell into an ocean [of amazement].[^34]

**128.** Losing consciousness, he falls upon the ground;
his attendants lifted him and sprinkled water.

**129.** Coming to his senses, Balabhadra says to everyone:
"This boy is not human; he is equal to Narayana.

**130.** Nowhere in the world have I seen such a form;
a very great person — Narayana is [my] witness!"[^35]

**131.** "Whose son is this jewel among men?"
Everyone says, "This is Achyuta's son."

**132.** Hearing [these] wonderful words, Balabhadra Dasa
disclosed something to Achyuta:

**133.** "Listen, sir: if you consent,
I will give my daughter to your son.

**134.** A very beautiful girl, named Ichchha-dei;
in beauty and virtues she has no comparison in the world.

**135.** Your son is worthy to be that girl's husband;
she is a girl worthy of him: this is Providence's arrangement.

**136.** Seeing your son, I lost my senses:
this jewel among men is equal to Narayana.

**137.** Giving [my] daughter, I have entered your shelter;
your son is the treasure of the life of the world."

**138.** Hearing all of Balabhadra's words, Achyuta
said, "Good," and felt great joy.

**139.** Taking leave of the ruler right then,
Achyuta, coming home, showed [him] honour.[^36]

**140.** I will tell in detail of Rasika's wedding,
and compose some description of [his] nature.

**141.** Listen to the Rasika-mangala, all you devotees of Krishna:
Rasikendra is the lord of [our] life, the life of all.[^37]

**142.** Adorning himself with Shyamananda's feet,
the son of Rasamaya joyfully composed [this].

*Thus ends the tenth lahari, called "Description of the Pastimes of Adolescence," in the Purva-vibhaga of the Sri Rasika-mangala.*

[^1]: In the scan the raga is *nārānī gauṛā* (1997 ed.: *nārāṇī gauṛā*), apparently the same as *nārāyaṇī gauṛā* in lahari 7 (note 1); the ghosha (refrain) there is also the same. There is no mangala-shloka at the beginning of this lahari: after the heading, the raga and the ghosha the song begins with ॥ 1 ॥.
[^2]: Durika is Shyamananda's mother (lahari 1, note 11), Bhavani is Rasikananda's mother (lahari 3). Rasikendra-chandra ("the moon among the kings of rasikas") is Rasikananda.
[^3]: "Reads with Hari Dube" — *paṛena haridube*; "Hari Dube reads [to him]" is also possible. Hari Dube (Haridasa Dube) is Rasika's teacher spoken of in lahari 9 (note 19). The second line is elliptical; it refers to the gopis' separation from Krishna after His departure for Mathura (Bhag. 10.39, 10.46–47).
[^4]: "Sobbing" — *uśasi uśasi*. The eight sattvika bhavas — see lahari 6, note 13.
[^5]: Mallabhumi is the region of Rasikananda's native village (lahari 3, note 6). "The lord of the realm" — *rājyera īśvara*: so the townspeople call Achyuta; in lahari 9 (38) he is called the *adhikārī* (ruler, administrator) in Mallabhumi.
[^6]: The number ॥ 19 ॥ is missing in the edition (the couplet stands between ॥ 18 ॥ and ॥ 20 ॥); it has been restored. In 19–26 Rasika speaks in the words of the gopis, addressing Krishna.
[^7]: "In hunger", "in thirst" — *bhokhe*, *śose*; "without shelter" — *nirmmāthī*: so these words are explained by the editor of the edition in his glosses at the foot of the page.
[^8]: "Cannot steady their hearts" — *buka nāhi bāndhe*, literally "do not bind their breasts".
[^9]: "Absorption" — *āveśa*: absorption in a mood (here, in the gopis' separation from Krishna). "Son" — *bālā*, "boy".
[^10]: Kandarpa is the god of love (Kamadeva). "Moonlike face" — the scan has the misprint *candra-bandana* for *candra-vadana* (cf. 8, 39).
[^11]: "Because of the glance of an evil person" — *duṣṭa loka darśana-kāraṇe*, i.e. the evil eye. "A strong wind" — *vāyu prabala*: in popular and Ayurvedic notions, a disorder of the "wind" (vata) in the body, a cause of madness, or possession by a spirit.
[^12]: 49–51: the "Bhagavatamrita" is Rupa Gosvami's *Laghu-bhagavatamrita*, which establishes on the evidence of the Puranas that Krishna returned to Vraja and eternally abides there. According to Gaudiya Vaishnava teaching, Krishna in His original form never leaves Vrindavana (CC, Antya 1); it is His expansion Vasudeva who goes to Mathura and Dvaraka, while the pastimes in Vraja are eternal. The term of separation, "three months" (*trimāsi viraha*), is as the author has it. "The great Krishna" — *kṛṣṇa mahāśaya*.
[^13]: "The devotees of Krishna" — *kārṣṇa-jana*; read from the scan (a superscript repha); garbled in the OCR and in the 1997 ed. (cf. lahari 1, note 66; lahari 3, note 19). "Taken exclusive refuge" — *ananya-śaraṇa*.
[^14]: "The lord of [our] life" — *prāṇanātha*: so the author calls Rasika (cf. 1: "Rasikendra-chandra").
[^15]: Murari is Rasika-Murari, i.e. Rasikananda. "Made well" (57) — *bhāla karilena*, literally "did good".
[^16]: "The delights of learning" — *vidyāra vilāsa*, as in lahari 9 (41, 86–87; its title is "Description of the Delights of Learning").
[^17]: "In solitude" — *nigame*; from the context (cf. 75, 78), in a secluded place.
[^18]: Childhood (*bālya*, up to 5 years) and early boyhood (*paugaṇḍa*, 5–10 years); then (81) adolescence (*kaiśora*, 10–15 years), youth (*yauvana*), maturity (*prauṛha*) and old age (*jarā*). "Absorbed in Krishna" — *kṛṣṇāveśe*.
[^19]: "Composes songs" — *gīta kare* (cf. 73); "sings" is also possible. "In various languages" — *nānā bhāṣā-mate*.
[^20]: "A suitable family to ally with" — *yathāyogya bandhu*: a family with which to become related by marriage. Next (86 ff.) comes Balabhadra Dasa, the ruler of Hijli, and his daughter Ichchha-dei.
[^21]: Hijli (*hijalī maṇḍala*) is a coastal tract in the south-east of Medinipur, near the mouth of the Hooghly; *adhikārī* means a ruler or administrator (as Achyuta in Mallabhumi, lahari 9, 38). Sadashiva is Balabhadra's brother; in lahari 11, after Balabhadra's passing, he gives Balabhadra's daughter in marriage to Rasika. 86–88 form one sentence; 87 is a parenthesis about Balabhadra's uncle. Vibhishana Mahapatra is not mentioned in other sources; *mahāpātra* is a title of high officials in Utkala. "Serves in the royal retinue" — *rāja-paricchade*; possibly also "in royal attire."
[^22]: "Valuables" — *pāṇidravya*, literally "hand-goods" (so in the scan and in the 1997 edition); apparently movable valuables. "Beyond counting" — *asaṅkhyalā*. The maharaja (90) is not named — perhaps the king mentioned in 104.
[^23]: "The darling of Lakshmi" — *lakṣmīra preyasī*: Lakshmi is the goddess of fortune and beauty, the consort of Narayana. "No equal" (93) — *nāhika paṭāntarī* (cf. 134). Manasija (94, "born of the mind") and Ananga (97, "the bodiless") are names of the god of love, Kandarpa (note 10).
[^24]: Patanet (*pāṭanet*) — a fine silk cloth.
[^25]: "As a nichhani offering" — *nichāni*: in the nichhani custom a gift is waved around a beloved's face to ward off misfortune (lahari 9, note 21); that is, even the god of love is fit only to be offered up for her beauty.
[^26]: Ichchha-dei (*icchādei*; the 1997 edition has *icchādevī*) — Rasikananda's future wife; in BR 15 (30) she is called Ichchha, in lahari 1 (107) Shyamadasi Thakurani (lahari 1, note 47). Thakurani is a respectful address to a lady.
[^27]: "A Deity of Krishna" — *kṛṣṇa-mūrti*. The girl's prayer (101–102) is addressed to Krishna; "O Lord" — *prabhu*. Cf. the gopis' worship of Katyayani to obtain Krishna as their husband (Bhag. 10.22).
[^28]: 104–106: "revenue" — *kaṛakaṛi*, as the editor explains it (*khājanā*, land tax). The king "of that country" is not named. The Padishah is the Mughal emperor; "subadar" — *subā*, here the Padishah's governor or revenue officer in Medinipur. A lakh is one hundred thousand; "rupees" — *ṭākā*. "Had him taken into custody" — *bandī karilā*.
[^29]: "In distress" — *ārata*, which the editor explains as *ārta*, "afflicted, alarmed." 108 gives the messenger's words; the subjects are omitted.
[^30]: "Once he stands [before him]" — *haile se ubhā*; *ubhā* means "standing" (the editor's gloss), that is, once Achyuta appears in person. Possibly also "if he himself stands up [for someone]." "Money" (111) — *taṅkā*.
[^31]: 116–125 describe Rasika's form in the traditional order "from head to foot" (*nakha-śikha*; cf. descriptions of Krishna). "Finely shaped" — *susañca*: so in the scan and in the 1997 edition; the meaning is unclear, translated from context. "Half-spoken words" (119) — *ādha ādha bola*, lisping, like a child's.
[^32]: Kanthi — a necklace fitting closely around the neck; "double" — *dosari*, of two strands. "Coral" — *pahalā*, which the editor explains as coral (*prabāla*, *palā-kā̃ṭhi*). "Fine cloth" (122) — *jhinabāsa*, explained by the editor as *sūkṣma-basana*.
[^33]: "Red dye" — *alakā*; apparently *alakta*, the red lac used to colour the feet. "The best of dancers" — *naṭavara*, an epithet of Krishna. "Bearing" (125) — *balani*, explained by the editor as *bhaṅgi*.
[^34]: "Came [before him]" (126) — *upasana*, i.e. *upasanna*, "having approached." "Fell into an ocean" — *paṛilā pāthāra*: was overwhelmed, lost his footing.
[^35]: 129–130 (cf. 136): Balabhadra's rapturous praise; see lahari 9, note 22: according to Gaudiya Vaishnava teaching Rasikananda is a pure devotee and associate of Shyamananda, not Narayana Himself.
[^36]: The reading is clear (*nṛpa-sthāne bidāi kariyā tatakṣaṇe / gṛhe āsi' acyuta karila sanamāne*), but the sense is not: "the ruler" (*nṛpa*) is apparently Balabhadra; perhaps Achyuta sent him off with honours (gifts) according to custom. Cf. lahari 11, 2–3, where Achyuta tells his kin of the proposal.
[^37]: "Devotees of Krishna" — *kārṣṇa-jana* (note 13). Rasikendra is Rasikananda (notes 2, 14). "Description of [his] nature" (140) — *svabhāva varṇanā*; cf. 81.
