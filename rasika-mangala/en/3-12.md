# Pashchima-vibhaga. Lahari 12. The Manifestation of Shyamasundarpur

*A song in glory of Shyamananda; Shyamananda, living in Thuriya, calls Rasika to him; so many sadhus and guests arrive that he tells Rasika to beg for a few more homesteads, and the raja of Ghantashila gives them the village of Satuti, which is named Sri Shyamasundarpur; Shyamananda, while living in Ayodhya, builds a house there, lives in Little Govindapur and leaves three Thakuranis there; rajas and subjects give many villages, and the sadhus are served throughout Utkala; Shyamananda playfully tells everyone to call Rasika "Thakura Gosani"; the disciples of Shyamananda and Rasika number hundreds of thousands; in Thuriya, at the Dola festival, Shyamananda hears a call to go to Vraja and, in separation from Krishna, remains three days under a tree; Rasika, refusing food and water, and the rajas beg him to stay in Utkala; Shyamananda falls gravely ill, and Rasika brings from Harichandana in Balarampur the "Hemasagara" oil, which cures him; in Kashiyari a wicked Yavana official, enraged by the sankirtana, seizes everyone — apparently Shyamananda himself with his companions, soon loses everything and takes refuge with Shyamananda; in Narayanagarh Shyamananda tells the bhuiyan Shyamapala not to keep a Yavana guard at his door and, seeing his regard for the Yavanas, departs; from then on no Yavanas live in Narayanagarh*

*The twelfth lahari.*

*Raga Narayani-gauda.*[^1]
*Ghosha: "My Krishna, treasury of virtues,*
*refuge of the helpless, the utmost limit of mercy."*

**1.** Glory, glory to Shyamananda, the sure shelter!
Glory, glory to Rasikendra, the prince of Achyuta![^2]

**2.** Shyamananda dwells in his house in Thuriya;
he had Rasika brought to his place.[^3]

**3.** Crowd upon crowd of sadhus come there;
the throng of guests is so great it cannot be described.[^4]

**4.** Prabhu took counsel with Rasika:
"Beg, my child, for a few more homesteads as alms."[^5]

**5.** Then the two Prabhus went to the village of Ghantashila
and spoke to the raja about the service of sadhus.[^6]

**6.** That raja gave a village called Satuti;
there in many ways they settled people [and] tenants.[^7]

**7.** He gave it the name Sri Shyamasundarpur;
there service to the sadhus [and] yatras took place in abundance.[^8]

**8.** There Shyamananda-raya built a house;
for some days he made his abode in Ayodhya.[^9]

**9.** Many yatras [and] great festivals he held in that village;
all — the raja [and] the subjects — were immersed in bliss.

**10.** Little Govindapur he made another place of his;
for many, many days he stayed there.[^10]

**11.** Three Thakuranis he settled in that place;
in all those villages the sadhus are served abundantly.[^11]

**12.** All the rajas [and] subjects gave a great many villages;
in many ways the sadhus were served throughout Utkala.

**13.** Apart from service to the sadhus, [people] know nothing else:
in every house — service to the sadhus, by rajas [and] subjects.

**14.** Taking Rasika with him, Shyamananda-raya
wanders everywhere through the forest land, from region to region.[^12]

**15.** Playfully laughing, Shyamananda-raya says:
"All shall call [him] 'Thakura Gosani.'

**16.** Call Rasika 'Thakura Gosani.'"
From then on all people call [him] by this name.[^13]

**17.** To all Prabhu gave prema-bhakti for Krishna;
all, down to the chandalas, became pure in mind.[^14]

**18.** Hundreds upon hundreds of Vrajavasis come constantly;
the Gaudiyas of the guru's line — they cannot be counted.[^15]

**19.** Shyamananda [and] Rasika serve the sadhus,
giving food, cloth [and] various jewels to each one.

**20.** The towns of Utkala became the world of Vaikuntha;
all the people were overwhelmed with love for Krishna.[^16]

**21.** Many became disciples of Shyamananda;
Rasika's disciples cannot be counted.

**22.** Disciples of disciples, servant-disciples, their servants —
hundreds of thousands became disciples: it cannot be told.[^17]

**23.** One after another, mahantas who had conquered all the quarters became disciples;
throughout the forest land, on all four sides, they spread joy.[^18]

**24.** All are servants of Krishna, seeking no other refuge;
abandoning false knowledge, [they] serve the sadhus without cease.[^19]

**25.** Seeing [this] community, Shyamananda, with joy in his heart,
commanded: "All of you, accept disciples on all four sides."[^20]

**26.** Thus after some time Shyamananda-raya
went by his own will to Thuriya.

**27.** Rasika was with [him] at the house in Thuriya;
there they joyfully held the great Dola festival.[^21]

**28.** With them is Damodara Gosani;
ten, twenty brothers, servant-disciples — crowd upon crowd.[^22]

**29.** In that place they held a great festival-yatra.
Suddenly Shyamananda heard with his ears:

**30.** "Come to Vraja, making every effort."
Hearing the command, Shyamananda wept.[^23]

**31.** A great longing seized Shyamananda-raya;
in his mercy he entrusted everything to Rasika.

**32.** He declared: "I shall certainly go to Vrindavan."
Setting out on the journey, he went [and stopped] beneath a tree.[^24]

**33.** His heart was in great agitation from separation from Krishna;
night and day the streams of tears could not be held back.

**34.** For the third day he remained beneath the tree;
hearing [of it], the raja [and] the subjects quickly gathered.[^25]

**35.** Rasikendra gave everyone a sign:
"Gather, all of you, and make your plea before those feet.

**36.** Gather, all of you, [and] serve at [his] holy feet;
keep Prabhu in Utkala for at least a few days."[^26]

**37.** Receiving the instruction, the rajas fell at [his] feet;
in many ways they spoke to Prabhu:

**38.** "Rasika-shekhara has given up food and water;
hearing of separation from Prabhu, [his] heart is torn.

**39.** The tears of his eyes cannot be stopped;
night and day he weeps, thinking of you.

**40.** Rasikendra-mani has offered his life at [your] feet;
without you Rasika's life will not remain.

**41.** Rasikendra-chandra has offered his life at [your] feet;
why do you go, leaving him, Prabhu Shyamananda?"[^27]

**42.** Hearing the words of all, Shyamananda-raya,
seeing Rasika's grief, remained there.

**43.** Shyamananda was tormented by a severe wind disorder;
great physicians came in crowds.

**44.** Having examined [him], all said to Rasika:
"This wind will not subside without the 'Hemasagara' [oil].

**45.** With great labour this oil would be prepared;
quickly fetch [it] from the house of Harichandana."[^28]

**46.** Hearing this, Rasika went to Balarampur;
arriving, he met [him] at Harichandana's house.[^29]

**47.** In many ways [he] worshipped Rasika's feet;
with all his family he honoured [him] as a second Narayana.[^30]

**48.** Rasika told him everything:
"Bring that oil and relieve the illness."

**49.** Joyfully [he] went, taking the 'Hemasagara' oil;
hastening, he arrived at Shyamananda's place.

**50.** As soon as the oil was applied to the head, [Shyamananda] was at once made well;
for some days all stayed in that place.[^31]

**51.** In the bliss of the rasa of Krishna they pass the days and nights;
all are always absorbed in the sankirtana of Krishna.

**52.** At that time Rasika returned to his own place,
and Shyamananda went to Kashiyari.[^32]

**53.** At the sight of Prabhu all rejoiced;
in many ways all began to serve [him].

**54.** In every house the sankirtana of Krishna began;
raja [and] subjects reel [as if drunk] with love for Krishna.[^33]

**55.** The official of that place was a wicked Yavana;
hearing the sankirtana, he burned with anger without cease.

**56.** He asked everyone about Shyamananda;
all the people told [him] in many ways of [his] greatness.[^34]

**57.** Hearing this, the Mughal said in anger to everyone:
"The subjects of the forest land are his disciples.

**58.** If he is seized, all will come [and] present themselves [to me]."
Saying this, he sent many men.[^35]

**59.** Having brought [them], he held them all in confinement;
all the people [pleaded with] him in many ways.[^36]

**60.** That Yavana heeded no one's words;
within two or three days calamity befell him.

**61.** [He] lost his holdings, his wife [and] sons perished;
horses, wealth, people — all were ruined.[^37]

**62.** Great suffering came upon his body; the Yavana understood [the cause]
[and] addresses Shyamananda with humble words:[^38]

**63.** "Listen, listen, great Prabhu: I am wicked-minded;
not knowing you, [I brought on myself] so much misfortune.

**64.** Your greatness is beyond the reach of Indra and [the other] gods;
I did not know that you are the refuge, a brother born of the same womb."[^39]

**65.** In many ways that very Yavana praised [him];
he took refuge at Shyamananda's feet.

**66.** Having shown him mercy, Prabhu went from there;
coming to Narayanagarh, he entered [it].

**67.** He met with Shyamapala Bhunya,
[and] saw that the doorkeeper at his door was a Yavana.[^40]

**68.** Shyamananda commanded: "Listen, Shyamapala,
keep a Yavana doorkeeper inside no longer.

**69.** Where service to guru, Krishna [and] the sadhus takes place,
there brahmanas constantly come and go.

**70.** To see a Yavana [and] to touch [him] is needless;
from today keep only Hindus at the door."[^41]

**71.** Disregarding the command, [his] attendants speak [their mind];
Shyamananda, smiling, says to Shyamapala:[^42]

**72.** "I see you are much inclined towards the Yavanas;
it is not fitting for me to stay here.

**73.** In this place there are always many Yavanas."
Saying this, Shyamananda departed.

**74.** From then on no Yavanas remained in that place:
his command, unfailing, brings about the impossible.[^43]

**75.** He whose command Brahma [and] Shiva cannot break,
whose command Indra and [the other] guardians of the quarters accept,[^44]

**76.** in this way delivered the world;
even with millions upon millions of mouths [his] virtues cannot be described.

**77.** Listen, listen to the "Rasika-mangala," all people:
without delay you will obtain the treasure of prema-bhakti for Krishna.[^45]

**78.** Adorning himself with Shyamananda's feet,
the son of Rasamaya joyfully composed [this].

*Thus in the Pashchima-vibhaga of the Sri Rasika-mangala ends the twelfth lahari, called "The Manifestation of Shyamasundarpur."*[^46]

[^1]: The lines before the raga are the title of the lahari ("দ্বাদশ-লহরী"), in italics without a number. Raga Narayani-gauda (*nārāẏanī-gauṛā*, so on the scan; Purva, lahari 7, note 1). Ghosha (refrain): *mora kṛṣṇa guṇanidhi / anātha-śaraṇa baṛa daẏāra abadhi*. There is no mangala-shloka: after the raga and ghosha the song begins with ॥ 1 ॥. The title follows the colophon on pdf 163 (printed p. 121), checked against the scan: "ইতি শ্রীরসিকমঙ্গল-পশ্চিম-বিভাগে শ্যামসুন্দরপুর-প্রকাশ-নাম দ্বাদশ-লহরী সম্পূর্ণা" (*śyāmasundarapura-prakāśa*) — "The Manifestation of Shyamasundarpur". Lahari 12 comprises 1–78 (pdf 158, 161–163; pdf 159 is an inserted plate, pdf 160 is blank). The summary line covers the whole lahari (7–78 from the OCR of pdf 161–163, provisionally).
[^2]: "The sure shelter" — *śaraṇa-pañjara*, literally "cage (enclosure) of refuge", i.e. secure protection for those who take refuge. "The prince of Achyuta" — *acyuta-kuṅara* (*kuṅara* = *kumāra*): Rasika (Pashchima, lahari 5, note 60).
[^3]: Thuriya (*thuriyā*) is unidentified (lahari 11, note 3). "In his house" — *sadane*. "Had brought" — *ānāilā* (so on the scan; OCR "নাইলা").
[^4]: "Crowd upon crowd" — *yūtha yūtha*. "The throng of guests" — *atithera bhīṛa*.
[^5]: Prabhu is Shyamananda. "Took counsel" — *karilā bicāra*, "deliberated, discussed". "A few homesteads" — *daśa pā̃ca ghara*, literally "ten [or] five houses": apparently land with households to support the service of sadhus (cf. 5–6). "My child" — *bāpu*, an affectionate address to a younger person. "As alms" — *bhikṣā*: Shyamananda asks not for himself but for the service of sadhus (cf. 5).
[^6]: "The two Prabhus" — *dui prabhu*: Shyamananda and Rasika. Ghantashila (*ghaṇṭaśilā*) — Purva, lahari 14, note 4; Rasika formerly lived there with his family. The raja of Ghantashila is not named here (cf. Purva, lahari 16, note 4). "About the service of sadhus" — *sādhu-sebā-prasaṅga*.
[^7]: Satuti (*sātuṭī*, so on the scan) is unidentified; in 7 (pdf 161) the village is given the name Sri Shyamasundarpur (the title of the lahari). "Settled" — *basāilā*: the subject is apparently Shyamananda and Rasika (or the raja). "People [and] tenants" — *jana prajā*: peasant tenants. pdf 158 ends with a complete ॥ 6 ॥; the continuation (7–78) is on pdf 161–163.
[^8]: Sri Shyamasundarpur (*śrīśyāmasundarapura*, "the town of Shyamasundara," i.e. Krishna) is the new name of the village of Satuti (6, note 7); the lahari is named after it. "Yatras" — *yātrā*: festivals with a procession of the Deity; the edition notes a variant reading *tathā*, "there." "Took place in abundance" — *hailā pracura*.
[^9]: "There" — *tathā*: apparently in Shyamasundarpur. "House" — *bāṛi*, a homestead. Ayodhya (*ayodhyā*) is apparently a local village; not identified (cf. Dakshina, lahari 2, note 10). "Made his abode" — *karilā ālaẏa*.
[^10]: Little Govindapur — *sānā se gobindapura*; *sānā* means "small" (so the editor also explains). Whether this is the Sri Govindapur given to Shyamananda by the bhuiyan Bhimadhana (Dakshina, lahari 12, note 7) is unclear. "Another place of his" — *kaila āra sthāna*. "Stayed" — *biśrāma karila*, lit. "rested, halted."
[^11]: The three Thakuranis — *tina ṭhākurāṇī*: apparently the three women of Shyamananda's household — Shyamapriya, Gaurangadasi and Yamuna (Dakshina, lahari 12, 12, note 8; "Thakurani" is an honorific title). It may also be understood as three Deities of Sri Radha (Pashchima, lahari 1, note 4). "Settled" — *rākhilena*, "placed, left."
[^12]: The forest land — *banabhũi* (= *banabhūmi*; Dakshina, lahari 2, note 10); likewise in 23 and 57.
[^13]: Thakura Gosani — *ṭhākura gõsāi*: an honorific ("lord-gosvami"; *gõsāi* is the same as *gosāñi*, Gosani). "Playfully" — *kautuke*. "All shall call [him]" (15) — *ḍākibe sabāẏa*; the object is Rasika, as 16 shows.
[^14]: "Down to the chandalas" — *ācaṇḍāla-ādi*: the chandalas are the lowest class. "Became pure in mind" — *haila śuddhamati*.
[^15]: Vrajavasis are the residents of Vraja. "The Gaudiyas of the guru's line" — *gurukula gauṛīẏā*: apparently Gaudiya Vaishnavas (from Gauda, Bengal) of the line of Shyamananda's guru, Hridaya Chaitanya; the sense is unclear. "Cannot be counted" — *gaṇana nā yāẏa*.
[^16]: "The towns of Utkala" — *utkala nagara*. "The world of Vaikuntha" — *baikuṇṭha-bhubana*. "Overwhelmed" — *jara jara*.
[^17]: "Disciples of disciples" — *anuśiṣya*; "servant-disciples" — *bhṛtya-śiṣya*; "their servants" — *tadbhṛtyagaṇa*. "Hundreds of thousands" — *lakṣa lakṣa*.
[^18]: "Mahantas who had conquered all the quarters" — *digbijaẏī mohānta*: renowned teachers, victors in learned debate. The line may also be understood: "each of the disciples became a mahanta, a conqueror of all the quarters." "Spread joy" — *pramoda karilā*.
[^19]: "Seeking no other refuge" — *ananya-śaraṇa* (so the scan; OCR "অনন্তাশরণ"). "False knowledge" — *kubidyā*: apparently sorcery and false doctrines.
[^20]: "Community" — *goṣṭhī*: the circle of followers. "Accept disciples" — *śiṣya kara*. "On all four sides" — *cāribhite*.
[^21]: 26: "went" — *bije kaila* (*bijaẏa*), said respectfully of a saint's going; "by his own will" — *āpanā līlāẏa*, "in his own play." Thuriya — note 3. The great Dola festival — *dola-mahotsaba*, the spring swing festival (Dakshina, lahari 10, note 5).
[^22]: Damodara Gosani — *dāmodara gosāñī* (so the scan; OCR "গোসাঞা"): apparently Damodara, Shyamananda's disciple from Chakulia (Dakshina, lahari 1, note 21). "Brothers" — *bhāi*: apparently godbrothers. "Crowd upon crowd" — *yūthe yūthe*.
[^23]: The author does not say who calls Shyamananda to Vraja: he hears the voice "suddenly" (*ācambite*), and it is called a "command" (*ājñā*) — apparently a call from above (cf. 33: "from separation from Krishna"). "Making every effort" — *kariẏā yatana*. This episode is not mentioned in BR 15.
[^24]: "Longing" — *anurāga*: loving yearning (for Krishna and Vraja). "Entrusted everything to Rasika" — *saba samarpaṇa kaila rasike*: apparently the service and the disciples. "Setting out on the journey" — *yātrā kari'*: one leaving on a long journey goes out of the house at the appointed hour and halts outside it — here beneath a tree.
[^25]: "For the third day" — *tṛtīẏa dibasa*. "Quickly" — *satbare*.
[^26]: "Gave a sign" — *kahilā iṅgite*, "spoke by hint." "Before those feet" — *se caraṇa-agrete*: Shyamananda's feet.
[^27]: 38–41 are taken as the rajas' words to Shyamananda: 39–40 have *tuẏā*, "you." 38 may also be read as the author's narration. Rasika-shekhara, Rasikendra-mani, Rasikendra-chandra — "crown of the rasikas," "jewel [among] the lords of the rasikas," "moon…" — Rasika. "Has offered his life at [your] feet" — *prāṇa nibedila pāẏa*. "Thinking of you" — *tuẏā bhābe*, "in feeling for you." "His life will not remain" — *nā rahe parāṇī*.
[^28]: "A severe wind disorder" — *mahābāẏu*: in Ayurveda, a grave disturbance of vata, the "wind" in the body. "Physicians" — *boidya* (= *baidya*; so the scan, crop ×4; OCR "বোইগ্য"). "In crowds" — *bṛnda bṛnda*. "Hemasagara" (*hemasāgara*, "ocean of gold") is a medicinal oil (*taila*, 45, 49); not identified. "With great labour this oil would be prepared" — *bahu kaṣṭe … haibe randhana*: medicinal oils are cooked at length with herbs; understood to mean that Harichandana has it ready. Harichandana is an Oriya title of landholders (Dakshina, lahari 5, note 13); he is not named here. Shyamananda's illness and the cure by the oil are not mentioned in BR 15.
[^29]: Balarampur — Dakshina, lahari 8, note 16.
[^30]: "With all his family" — *sabaṃśe*. "As a second Narayana" — *yena dbitīẏa nārāẏaṇe*: cf. Pashchima, lahari 10, 80. According to Gaudiya Vaishnava teaching the guru and the Vaishnava are honoured as the Lord Himself because they are His dearmost servants (Vishvanatha Chakravarti, Gurv-ashtaka 7); cf. Purva, lahari 1, note 2; Purva, lahari 14, note 11.
[^31]: The subject of 49 is not expressed; understood as Rasika (it may also be Harichandana). "Arrived" — *utarilā*. "To the head" — *māthe*. The instant cure is as the author tells it.
[^32]: "His own place" — *nija sthāne*: apparently Gopivallabhapur. Kashiyari — *kāśīẏāṛī* (Pashchima, lahari 1, note 4).
[^33]: "Reel" — *ḍhalāḍhali*: sway and tumble against one another as if drunk.
[^34]: 55: "official" — *adhikārī*, an officer administering the locality; not named. Yavana — a Muslim; below he is called a Mughal (57; cf. Dakshina, lahari 9, note 5). "Asked" (56) — *sudhāila*.
[^35]: "All will come [and] present themselves" — *sabe milibe āsiẏā*: apparently the official's calculation: if Shyamananda is seized, all his disciples will come to him [in submission or with a ransom].
[^36]: "In confinement" — *nigama sthāne* (so the scan, crop ×2): the sense is unclear; taken as "in prison" (perhaps *nigaṛa*, "fetters"). "Them all" — *sabāre*: apparently Shyamananda and his companions. "[Pleaded]" — *kahila*, "spoke": the people interceded for them.
[^37]: "Calamity" (60) — *aghaṭana*. "Lost his holdings" — *biṣaẏa chuṭila*: his post and property. The ruin of his family and property is as the author tells it: according to Gaudiya Vaishnava teaching, offence against a Vaishnava is ruinous (Dakshina, lahari 9, note 7).
[^38]: "Great suffering came upon his body" — *aṅge mahāduḥkha haila*: illness or grievous sorrow. "Understood" — *jānila*.
[^39]: "Great Prabhu" — *mahāprabhu*: here the Yavana's address to Shyamananda. "Beyond the reach of Indra and [the other] gods" — *debendrādi-agocara*. "The refuge, a brother born of the same womb" — *śaraṇa sodara* (so the scan): apparently "a protector, close as a brother"; the sense is unclear.
[^40]: Narayanagarh (*nārāẏaṇagaṛa*) is apparently Narayangarh in Paschim Medinipur, the seat of an estate of that name. Shyamapala Bhunya (*śyāmapāla bhuñā*) is a bhuiyan, a landholder (Dakshina, lahari 12, note 7), apparently the lord of Narayangarh; not identified. "Entered" — *parabeśa hailā*. "Doorkeeper" — *duẏāri* (68: *dbāri*).
[^41]: "Inside" — *bhitare*: in the house. "Brahmanas" — *dbijagaṇa*. "To see … [and] to touch … is needless" — *daraśe paraśe akāraṇa*: the concern is the ritual purity of the brahmanas who come where guru, Krishna and the sadhus are served; this is as the author tells it and reflects the customs of his time. "Hindus" — *hindugaṇa*.
[^42]: The line is unclear: *helana kariẏā ājñā saṅgīgaṇa bale* — understood as: Shyamapala's attendants, disregarding the command, raise objections; it may also be read "[Shyamapala] disregarded the command, the companions say." "Smiling" — *kutuhale*, "playfully."
[^43]: "Unfailing … brings about the impossible" — *aghaṭana ghaṭāẏa se ājñā paramāṇe*, lit. "by the measure (power) of that command the unheard-of comes to pass." The departure of the Yavanas from Narayanagarh is as the author tells it.
[^44]: Such things are usually said of the Lord; the author exalts Shyamananda (cf. Purva, lahari 1, note 2; Purva, lahari 14, note 11). According to Gaudiya Vaishnava teaching Shyamananda is a servant of Radha, and the power of his word is the power the Lord grants to His devotee. "Accept" — *dhare*, "hold [on their heads]." The guardians of the quarters (*dikpāla*) are Indra and the other gods.
[^45]: "Without delay" — *abilambe*. pdf 162 ends with a complete ॥ 77 ॥; the continuation (78 — the author's signature, colophon) is on pdf 163.
[^46]: The author's signature (78) and the colophon are on pdf 163 (printed p. 121), checked against the scan: "ইতি শ্রীরসিকমঙ্গল-পশ্চিম-বিভাগে শ্যামসুন্দরপুর-প্রকাশ-নাম দ্বাদশ-লহরী সম্পূর্ণা" (*śyāmasundarapura-prakāśa*; note 1).
