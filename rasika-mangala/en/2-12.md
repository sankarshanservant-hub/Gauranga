# Dakshina-vibhaga. Lahari 12. The Meeting with Raja Vaidyanatha Bhanja

*A song in glory of Shyamananda; Shyamananda travels through the country with Rasika: at Naihati, at Arjuni's, he holds three festivals and accepts disciples, Rasika accepts many disciples at Kashiyari, Shyamananda shows mercy to Haridasa at Jhatiyara and to Bhimadhana at Murura, who gives him the village of Sri Govindapur, where Shyamananda builds a house and where Shyamapriya, Gaurangadasi and Yamuna come; Shyamananda orders Rasika to deliver the king and subjects of Utkala; Rasika comes to Rajgarh, to the learned assembly of Raja Vaidyanatha Bhanja and his two brothers, versed in the shastras but ignorant of devotion to Krishna; the author describes Rasika's beauty, with the Bhagavata in his hands; women, scholars, friends, servants, sadhus and men of knowledge see in him a jewel among men, like Narayana; the three brothers fall at his feet, wash them, glorify him as Narayana and entertain him with honour; the author promises to tell next of the instruction*

*The twelfth lahari.*

*Raga Dhanashri.*[^1]
*Ghosha: "Sweet words, O enchanting the mind!"*

**1.** Glory, glory to Shyamananda, fathomless [his] greatness,
friend of all the worlds, compassion [incarnate] for living beings![^2]

**2.** Having stayed at Dharenda, after some days Shyamananda
set out, taking Rasika with him.

**3.** Coming there, to Arjuni of Naihati,
he joyfully held three festivals.

**4.** Prabhu accepted many disciples there:
Jagannatha, Damodara and the women of the family,[^3]

**5.** Shyamadasa, Arjuni's son, and others.
From there Prabhu went to the village of Kashiyari.[^4]

**6.** Rasika accepted many disciples in that village:
Vrajamohana, Shyamadasa and Narayana,

**7.** Radhamohana the bhakta and Yadavendradasa;
day by day he brought forth many [new] disciples.[^5]

**8.** From there [they] came and stayed in the village of Jhatiyara;
there Prabhu showed mercy to Haridasa.[^6]

**9.** From there [they] entered Murura;
[there] Shyamananda showed mercy to Bhimadhana.

**10.** That bhuiyan gave [him] the village of Sri Govindapur;
in that village Shyamananda Thakura built a house.[^7]

**11.** For some days Shyamananda stayed there,
without pause delighting in absorption in Krishna.

**12.** Shyamapriya Thakurani came there,
Gaurangadasi Thakurani, Yamuna — all.[^8]

**13.** Glory, glory to Shyamananda, the purifier of the fallen:
giving bhakti, he conquered every land.[^9]

**14.** Shyamananda-raya gave an order to Rasika:
"Save all living beings, O Mahashaya.

**15.** Deliver the king and the subjects of Utkala;
spread the rasa of prema-bhakti for Krishna."

**16.** Receiving the order, Rasikendra set out
and, coming to Rajgarh, arrived [there].

**17.** Raja Vaidyanatha Bhanja; Chhota-raya Sena [and]
Rautra are his younger brothers: [all] three are fortunate.[^10]

**18.** The three brothers are of great splendour, very mighty;
born in the pure Solar line, very mighty.[^11]

**19.** Hundreds and hundreds of learned pandits stay in [their] assembly;
they always study Vedic learning [and] the Bhagavata.

**20.** They know the six shastras, they are equal to Brihaspati;
devotion to Krishna they do not know — [their] labour is in vain.[^12]

**21.** At that time Raja Vaidyanatha, having convened the assembly —
the three brothers were seated, having finished the fivefold worship.[^13]

**22.** At that time Rasikendra went [there];
coming into the midst of the assembly, he arrived [before them].

**23.** A dark, beautiful body, a sweet form,
a softly, softly smiling face, that unhurried gait.

**24.** Curling locks, very long cheeks;
from beautiful lips — soft, gentle, gentle words.[^14]

**25.** Arms reaching to the knees, eyes beautiful;
a nose [like] a sesame flower, a row of teeth charming.

**26.** A broad chest, a navel deep [and] lovely;
a waist [like] a lion's, thighs [like] plantain [trunks], a bright-hued cloth.[^15]

**27.** Most tender are those two feet;
the row of nails glitters, surpassing the moon.

**28.** A fine cloth, [folded] double, adorns his left shoulder;
seeing that sweet form, the people of the world are enchanted.[^16]

**29.** Holding in his hand the manuscript of the Bhagavata,
he entered the assembly like the sun.[^17]

**30.** Having bowed to all the brahmanas,
he came near the king and sat [before him].[^18]

**31.** Seeing [him], the three brothers were greatly amazed:
with their own eyes they saw a form like Narayana.[^19]

**32.** He is the embodiment of all dharma, all radiant with splendour;
this Mahashaya is the destroyer of adharma.

**33.** The three brothers saw this form;
the women in the house, seeing [him], are enchanted — [the whole] world [is enchanted].[^20]

**34.** All say: "Where were you, jewel among men?
Your body surpasses Kandarpa — it enchants the world."[^21]

**35.** Shaivas, Shaktas, debaters [and] opponents — all say:
"He will easily crush the pride of us all."[^22]

**36.** The brahmanas versed in the six shastras, averse to Krishna —
they all saw [him] as if in the form of Vyasa [and] Shuka.[^23]

**37.** "He it is who will destroy the pride of us all;
he will reveal the essential truth of the Vedas and shastras."

**38.** All the wives of the family say: "The son of Achyuta,
this Mahashaya, is a moon illuminating the family.[^24]

**39.** Through him all our kin will live in happiness;
by the good fortune of us all he was born like this."

**40.** All the elders say: "A son of [our] family!
May he live long — protect [him], Narayana!

**41.** We shall see his sons [and] grandsons with our own eyes!" —
such parental affection all the elders show.[^25]

**42.** All the friends say: "He is our very own friend;
apart from him there is no dearer friend in the three worlds."

**43.** The companions say: "He is my dear brother;
with him I roam the world without fear.

**44.** With him one never knows any sorrow;
the eight siddhis [and] nine nidhis — [that] happiness is with him."[^26]

**45.** All the servants say: "He is the foremost of men;
even with ten million mouths his qualities cannot be described."

**46.** All the sadhus say: "He is the crown of men:
he will distribute prema-bhakti for Krishna from house to house.

**47.** This Mahashaya will deliver all living beings;
his greatness cannot be expressed even in part."

**48.** The learned brahmana panditas speak affectionate words:
"This person is not a man — we all know [it].

**49.** This Mahashaya is expert in all the shastras;
he will make prema-bhakti for Krishna arise.

**50.** Mimamsa, Patanjala [yoga] and the rest, Sankhya, Sankhyayana —
this person will crush the pride of them all."[^27]

**51.** All the jnanis say: "He is like Narayana,
Whom the yogis call the Param Brahman.

**52.** In this boy we see that very form present before us;
by his darshan my ignorance has been dispelled.[^28]

**53.** Such a sweet form we have never seen;
seeing the enchanting form, the eyes cannot turn away.[^29]

**54.** Truly this Mahashaya is like Narayana:
he has risen to destroy the blinding darkness of dreadful Kali."

**55.** Thus he gave darshan to all:
as each one sees [his] form, so everyone speaks.[^30]

**56.** One moon filled the hearts of all;
seeing [his] moon-face, all are enchanted by his darshan.

**57.** Everyone fell into Rasika's snare:
the son of Achyuta brought all under his sway.

**58.** At this time the king, seeing that form —
the three brothers fell to the ground at [his] feet.

**59.** They seated Rasikendra-chandra on a seat;
the king washes [his] feet with joy in his heart.[^31]

**60.** One brother drew water and joyfully handed [it];
the king himself washed [his] lotus feet.

**61.** Another brother wiped the holy feet with a cloth:
birth after birth these three are Rasika's servants.

**62.** The three brothers sat on the ground with joined palms;
seeing [his] manifest [glory], the king trembled at heart.[^32]

**63.** Bowing down, he says to Rasika:
"By the good fortune of us all you have come to [our] home.

**64.** Today my birth has become fruitful:
I have seen your feet with my own eyes.

**65.** For millions upon millions of births I performed austerities —
that is why, Prabhu, I have seen your feet.

**66.** You have appeared to deliver us all;
age after age, for the sake of your servants, you accept dwelling in a womb.[^33]

**67.** You take form by your own will — who can know [you]?
Prabhu, affectionate to devotees, brother to those who take shelter![^34]

**68.** You create [and] maintain the entire universe;
you are the universal form, Prabhu, the god Narayana.

**69.** By your maya the moving and the unmoving come and go;
you are the soul of all, the enclosure of shelter.[^35]

**70.** Seeing the living beings in the dreadful Kali-yuga,
you took birth to deliver [them], bringing your associates and companions."

**71.** In many ways King Vaidyanatha offered praises;
he worshipped [his] holy feet as [one worships] Narayana.[^36]

**72.** He offered [him] a seat in his own palace
and with care [served] a meal of six tastes and so on.[^37]

**73.** The king, sitting [there] himself, serves [him] tambula;
he reveals all the pain of his heart at [his] feet.[^38]

**74.** Now I shall tell the account of the instruction;
listen, all, attentively to the "Rasika-mangala."[^39]

**75.** Who can comprehend [these] most wondrous pastimes?
What Rasika-shekhara told me —[^40]

**76.** in that order I have described something;
learned and good people, do not count this as a fault.

**77.** This I composed in the Dakshina-vibhaga,
making Rasika's feet the ornament of my head.

**78.** I shall make known something of the "Rasika-mangala":
listen, all, attentively to the description of [his] nature.[^41]

**79.** Adorning himself with Shyamananda's feet,
the son of Rasamaya joyfully composed [this].

*Thus in the Dakshina-vibhaga of the "Sri Rasika-mangala" ends the twelfth lahari, called "The Meeting with Raja Vaidyanatha Bhanja."*[^42]

[^1]: The lines before the raga are the heading of the lahari ("দ্বাদশ-লহরী"), in italics without a number. Lahari 12 begins on pdf 117 (printed p. 79; no running head), after the colophon of lahari 11 on pdf 116 (files RM-2-11-008-069). Raga Dhanashri (*dhānaśrī*). The ghosha is *madhura bacana mana mohanā re*: "enchanting the mind" (*mana mohanā*) can also be taken as the name-address "Manamohana" ("Enchanter of the heart," Krishna): "Sweet words, O Manamohana!" There is no mangala-shloka; the numbering is that of the edition. The name is given by the colophon on pdf 119 (checked against the scan): "রাজা বৈদ্যনাথ-ভঞ্জ-মিলন-নাম দ্বাদশ-লহরী" (*rājā baidyanātha-bhañja-milana*). The summary line covers the whole lahari (1–30 from the scan; 31–79 from the OCR of the full edition and the scan of pdf 119, provisionally). Couplet 30 is the last on pdf 117; pdf 118 begins with ॥ 31 ॥.
[^2]: "Compassion [incarnate] for living beings" — *jībera karuṇā*, lit. "the compassion of living beings."
[^3]: Naihati (*naihāṭī*) — here apparently a village in Utkala or Medinipur, not Naihati on the Ganges (glossary); not identified. Arjuni (*arjjunī*) — apparently the master of the house where Shyamananda stayed (his son is Shyamadasa, 5); not identified. "The women of the family" — *badhūgaṇa*: the wives (daughters-in-law) of the house. Jagannatha and Damodara (4) are not identified (not Damodara of Chakulia).
[^4]: Kashiyari — *kāśīẏāṛī purī*; possibly present-day Keshiary (West Medinipur); not identified.
[^5]: Vrajamohana, Shyamadasa, Narayana, Radhamohana, Yadavendradasa — Rasika's disciples; Radhamohana is also named in the Purva-vibhaga (lahari 1, 80). "The bhakta" — *bhakta*: possibly Radhamohana's byname or the epithet "devotee." "Brought forth" — *kailā parakāśa*, lit. "made manifest."
[^6]: Jhatiyara (*jhāṭīẏāṛā*; so in the scan) is not identified. Haridasa is not identified.
[^7]: Murura (*muruṛā*; so in the scan, the OCR of the 1997 edition has "মুকড়া") and Sri Govindapur are not identified. Bhimadhana is "that bhuiyan" (*bhuñā*): a local landholder, lord of villages.
[^8]: Shyamapriya is Shyamananda's wife (lahari 11, 26–29). Gaurangadasi Thakurani and Yamuna are apparently women of Shyamananda's household; not identified. "Thakurani" is a respectful title of a Vaishnava teacher's wife.
[^9]: "Conquered" — *karila dalana*, lit. "crushed": he conquered by bhakti (cf. lahari 10, 66).
[^10]: Rajgarh (*rājagaṛa*) is not identified. Vaidyanatha Bhanja — apparently a raja of the Bhanja dynasty (cf. Purva, lahari 1, 113 and note 49). "Chhota-raya Sena [and] Rautra" — *choṭa rāẏa sena, rāutrā* (so in the scan; the OCR of the 1997 edition has "রাঢত্রা"): the reading and division of the names are unclear; "Chhota-raya" ("the younger raya") and "Rautra" (the Odia title *rāutarāẏa*) may be titles. Understood as the raja's two younger brothers, three brothers in all (18, 21).
[^11]: "The Solar line" — *sūryabaṃśa*, the dynasty of the Sun (the line of Ikshvaku and Rama). "Very mighty" — *baṛai pratāpī*, repeated in both lines, as in the original.
[^12]: "The six shastras" — *ṣaṛśāstra*: the six philosophical systems (darshanas). Brihaspati is the preceptor of the demigods, a model of learning.
[^13]: "Having finished the fivefold worship" — *sāri pañcapūjā*: following the editor's gloss below the line (*sāri*, "having finished"; *pañcapūjā*, worship of the Lord with five offerings, panchopachara; the gloss is not translated). "Having convened the assembly" — *sabhā kari*.
[^14]: "Curling locks" — *cā̃cara cikura keśa*. "Very long cheeks" — *sudīrgha kapola*. "Gentle, gentle" — *lahu lahu*.
[^15]: "Thighs [like] plantain [trunks]" — *rambhā jānu*: *rambhā*, the plantain tree, a traditional simile in descriptions of beauty; *jānu*, knees (thighs).
[^16]: "A fine cloth" — *jhinabāsa*; "[folded] double" — *dosarā*: apparently an upper wrap (chadar) of two layers; the sense is approximate.
[^17]: "The manuscript of the Bhagavata" — *bhāgabata pũthikhāni*: Rasika was famed for his exposition of the Srimad-Bhagavatam (BR 15, 120). "The sun" — *dinamaṇi*, lit. "the jewel of the day."
[^18]: "Sat [before him]" — *haila upasana*, lit. "drew near, came to be beside" (the same word as in 16, 22).
[^19]: "Like Narayana" — *nārāyaṇa sama*; the panditas and jnanis say the same (51, 54), and the king himself glorifies Rasika as Narayana (65–71). These are the rapturous words of the onlookers: thus the author exalts Rasika. According to Gaudiya Vaishnava teaching Rasikananda is a devotee and associate, not an incarnation of the Lord (Purva, lahari 1, note 69; Dakshina, lahari 8, note 3); his guru Shyamananda is a servant of Radha and Krishna (Purva, lahari 1, note 2; Purva, lahari 14, note 11). The guru and the great devotee are honoured as Hari Himself because he is dear to the Lord (Vishvanatha Chakravarti's Gurv-ashtaka, 7).
[^20]: "The women in the house" — *gṛhe nārīgaṇa*. "[The whole] world [is enchanted]" — *mohita dharaṇī*, lit. "the earth is enchanted"; it can also be understood as "[fell] enchanted to the ground" (cf. 58: *caraṇe paṛilā dharaṇī*). The 1997 edition reads the same.
[^21]: "Jewel among men" — *puruṣa-ratana*. Kandarpa is Kamadeva, the god of love, the model of beauty.
[^22]: "Debaters [and] opponents" — *bādī bibādī* (lahari 10, note 28). "Easily" — *e hele* (*helāẏa*, "playfully, without effort"); "এ" is taken as "he" ("this one").
[^23]: "The six shastras" — note 12. "Averse to Krishna" — *kṛṣṇere bimukha*. Vyasa is the compiler of the Vedas and Puranas, the author of the "Bhagavata"; Shuka is Shukadeva Gosvami, the speaker of the "Bhagavata."
[^24]: "The wives of the family" — *kulabadhū*: the wives and daughters-in-law of the royal house. The son of Achyuta is Rasika (Achyuta is his father; Purva-vibhaga). "A moon illuminating the family" — *kuloddīpana candra*: the women, and then the elders (40–41), see Rasika as though he were a son of their own family. The scene recalls the "Bhagavata" (10.43.17): Krishna, entering Kamsa's arena, appears to each according to his relationship with Him — to the wrestlers as a thunderbolt, to the women as Kamadeva embodied, to the cowherds as their own kinsman, and so on; so here (33–55) each sees Rasika in his own way (cf. 55).
[^25]: "Parental affection" — *bātsalya* (vatsalya).
[^26]: "The companions" (43) — *saṅgī jana*. "Without fear" — *nirbhaẏete* (in the 1997 edition *nirbharete*, "relying [on him]"). The eight siddhis are the mystic perfections of yoga (anima and the others); the nine nidhis are the nine treasures of Kubera.
[^27]: The list of doctrines is as in lahari 10, 65–66 (note 28): *mīmāṃsā* — Mimamsa; *pātañjala* — the yoga of Patanjali; *sāṃkhya* — Sankhya; *sāṃkhyāẏana* (so in the scan and in the 1997 edition) — apparently Shankhayana (a Vedic school); the reading is unclear.
[^28]: Jnanis (*jñānī*) — followers of the path of knowledge. Param Brahman (*paraṃbrahma*) — the Supreme Absolute Truth. "Present before us" — *bidyamāna*. "My ignorance has been dispelled" — *harilā ajñāna*, lit. "[he] has stolen [my] ignorance."
[^29]: "The eyes cannot turn away" — *nā pichale ā̃khi*, lit. "the eyes do not slip off."
[^30]: "As each one sees [his] form, so everyone speaks" — *yei jana rūpa dekhe bale sarbajana*; understood as a summing-up of 33–54 (cf. note 24).
[^31]: Rasikendra-chandra — "the moon Rasikendra," an affectionate name of Rasika (cf. Rasika-chandra, lahari 11, 54). Washing the feet is the first of the offerings to an honoured guest and to the guru.
[^32]: "Manifest [glory]" — *prakāśa*. "Trembled at heart" — *ḍarilā antara*, lit. "was afraid at heart": reverential awe.
[^33]: "For the sake of your servants" — *bhṛtya lāgi*; "you accept dwelling in a womb" — *laha garbhabāsa*: the words of the king, who sees in Rasika the Lord descending for the sake of His devotees (note 19). The 1997 edition reads *lai* ("[I] accept"): "age after age, to be [your] servant, I accept dwelling in a womb."
[^34]: "You take form by your own will" — *sbecchāmaẏa rūpa* (the 1997 edition: "স্বেচ্ছাময় প্রভু তুমি কে কহিতে পারে," "…who can describe [you]?"). "Brother to those who take shelter" — *śaraṇa sodara*, lit. "brother [giving] shelter"; the same in lahari 13, 67.
[^35]: "The enclosure of shelter" — *śaraṇa-pañjara*, lit. "cage of shelter": a protecting enclosure for those who take refuge.
[^36]: "As [one worships] Narayana" — *nārāẏaṇa sama* (note 19).
[^37]: "In his own palace" — *āpanā mandire*: *mandira* is a palace, living quarters (or a temple). "Of six tastes" — *ṣaṛrasa*: sweet, sour, salty, pungent, bitter, astringent.
[^38]: Tambula — betel, served after a meal. "The pain of his heart" — *manera bedanā*: apparently spiritual longing; next the king asks for instruction (lahari 13, 2–3).
[^39]: "The account of the instruction" — *upadeśa-kathā*: the instruction and initiation of the three brothers, in lahari 13.
[^40]: Rasika-shekhara — "crown of the rasikas," an epithet of Rasika (Dakshina, lahari 2, note 5): the author says he heard the account from Rasika himself (cf. lahari 3, 97, note 42).
[^41]: "The description of [his] nature" — *sbabhāba-barṇanā* (lahari 10, note 31; lahari 11, note 31). "Composed" (77) — *karila racana* (in the 1997 edition *kahila*, "told").
[^42]: The colophon is on pdf 119 (checked against the scan): "ইতি শ্রীরসিকমঙ্গল-দক্ষিণবিভাগে রাজা বৈদ্যনাথ-ভঞ্জ-মিলন-নাম দ্বাদশ-লহরী সম্পূর্ণা." Lahari 13 begins on the same page (files RM-2-13-001-050).
