# Uttara-vibhaga. Lahari 11. The Sending of Wild Elephants at the Request of Shah Shuja and the Giving of Hari-nama to Two Wild Tigers

*A song in glory of Shyamananda; for the sake of the festivals Rasika constantly travels with his companions, serving the guru, Krishna and the sadhus; kings and subjects vie with one another to invite him, everywhere the eight siddhis and the nine treasures are with him, and he floods every land with love for Krishna; wicked people tell the Padishah of the great mahanta of Orissa who gives Hari-nama to the forest elephants and with whom Jagannatha converses, and Shah Shuja sends a khoja with a thousand horses and elephants to Gopivallabhapur to demand a miracle: let Rasika by his command bring elephants out of the forest; the Hindu community is alarmed, but Rasika answers with a smile that he knows no tricks, his Master is Krishna, and let the khoja keep the yavanas away while the twelve-day festival lasts and make his preparations; the khoja builds an enclosure, and at night, at Rasika's command, the elephant Gopala Dasa leads fourteen forest elephants into it; the khoja falls at Rasika's feet and acknowledges him as Ishvara, the elephants are taken to the Padishah, and the yavanas acknowledge Rasika as Narayana; in Varahabhumi the companions, losing their way at night, enter a dense forest where two tigers block the path; Rasika steps forward and gives them Hari-nama, and the tigers, bowing, lead everyone out of the forest to a village; people gather to see this, and Rasika bids his companions keep his manifestations secret; the author glorifies Rasika, who gave prema-bhakti to all of Utkala*

*The eleventh lahari.*

*Raga Narayani-gauda.*[^1]
*Ghosha: "My Krishna, treasury of virtues,*
*refuge of the helpless, the utmost limit of mercy."*

**1.** Glory, glory to Shyamananda, the treasury of compassion,
whose wealth [and] life is Rasikendra-chudamani!

**2.** For the sake of the festivals Sri Rasikendra-chandra
always wanders over the earth; with him is a host of Krishna's devotees.[^2]

**3.** He constantly serves the guru, Krishna and the sadhus;
in great bliss he always goes from town to town.

**4.** Kings [and] subjects, vying with one another [to] serve the sadhus,
invite Rasika and take [him to their homes].[^3]

**5.** Sending messengers ten or twenty days ahead
[and] watching for where Prabhu will arrive,

**6.** with their whole families they go joyfully for darshan;
falling at [his] feet, they bring [him], begging him to come.[^4]

**7.** He fulfils the desires of all;
Prabhu wanders in [all] four directions for the sake of living beings.

**8.** Whatever music [and] poetry there is on earth —
all of it always abides with Rasika.

**9.** Wherever Rasikendra-mani stays,
the eight siddhis [and] the nine treasures are present of themselves.

**10.** One who has not even a ser of rice in his house —
as soon as [Rasika] arrives, the eight siddhis at once supply [everything].[^5]

**11.** In the bliss of sankirtana Sri Rasika-chudamani
always himself sings sankirtana [and] dances.

**12.** The eight sattvika [bhavas] appear on his holy body;
with the streams from his eyes [everything] around is drenched.

**13.** How [lovely] the movement of his dance [and] his poses, how [lovely] his gentle smile!
How [sweet] the speech of his holy mouth always is!

**14.** With a special glance he won over all the pilgrims;
he flooded every land, one after another, with love for Krishna.[^6]

**15.** At this time wicked people say to the Padishah:
"There is a very great mahanta in the land of Orissa.

**16.** He gives Hari-nama to the forest elephants;
Jagannatha speaks with him — [this is] certain."[^7]

**17.** Hearing [this], the Padishah says, looking at the khoja:
"Go and tell Rasika: let him catch elephants [and] give [them to us].

**18.** The forest elephants are his servants.
Ask humbly; do not speak harsh words."[^8]

**19.** Hearing [this], the evil-minded khoja quickly set out,
taking with him a thousand horses [and] elephants.

**20.** He arrived in the vicinity of Sri Gopivallabhapur
[and] sent eminent people to Prabhu.

**21.** They submitted to Sri Rasika:
"This is why Shah Shuja has sent me:

**22.** [so that] he may grant me this miracle —
by his command bring elephants out of the forest.

**23.** When ten or twenty elephants come at [his] command,
[we] will surely acknowledge him as Ishvara."[^9]

**24.** [This] reached the whole Hindu community there;
deeming [it] a very great misfortune, they all go [to Rasika].[^10]

**25.** All made it known at Rasika's feet;
with a gentle smile on his face he says to all:

**26.** "I know nothing of tricks [and] shows;
with my whole being, my Master is Lord Krishna.[^11]

**27.** When Lord Krishna shows me mercy,
then the matter will be accomplished — tell the khoja.

**28.** The twelve-day great festival every year
I celebrate with great joy in Sri Gopivallabhapur.

**29.** As long as I am celebrating the festival here,
let not a single one of the yavanas come.

**30.** Tell him: staying at a distance, let him make his preparations;
Krishna will give the elephants if [he] takes pains."[^12]

**31.** All went and told [this] to the evil-minded khoja;
hearing it, he joyfully made many obeisances.[^13]

**32.** On [all] four sides he laid the foundation of a wall;
inside he carefully placed bananas, rice and so on.

**33.** He left there only one entrance with gate-leaves;
and he put his faith in Rasika's words.

**34.** At this time [Rasika] began the festival;
that [very] elephant, Gopala Dasa, came and entered.[^14]

**35.** At night he bowed down to Rasika;
before the elephant Rasika-shekhara spoke:

**36.** "Listen, listen, dear one, maddened best of elephants!
The king of the yavanas has sent servants.

**37.** He has wished to see my miracle:
[that] ten or twenty elephants be brought [and] given [to him] before his eyes.

**38.** At my command, dear one, do one task:
bring ten or twenty elephants [and] put [them] into the enclosure.

**39.** You yourself stay behind, do not go in through the entrance;
show me this very miracle."[^15]

**40.** Receiving the command, the maddened Gopala Dasa quickly went;
in the forest he entered among the elephants.

**41.** The king of elephants, leading the elephants along with him,
brings them inside the enclosure.

**42.** At the entrance he himself turned back [and] went off into the forest;
fourteen elephants went into the enclosure.[^16]

**43.** The servants closed the gate-leaves of the entrance;
seeing [this], that evil-minded yavana was amazed.

**44.** Seeing Rasika's greatness, he quickly came;
falling down like a rod, the khoja falls at [his] feet.

**45.** In many ways he offered praise before Rasika;
he firmly acknowledged [him] as Ishvara.

**46.** He quickly told everything to the Padishah;
[he] brought the elephants [and] gave [them] to him before his eyes.

**47.** Seeing all these signs, the yavanas
from that time acknowledged him as Narayana in person.[^17]

**48.** See, then: at Rasika's command
the forest elephants come to Rasika.[^18]

**49.** In the bliss of Krishna Rasika-shekhara wanders;
constantly, absorbed in Krishna, [his] eyes are full of tears.

**50.** From land to land he travels with a happy heart,
overwhelmed by sankirtana, in the happiness of supreme bliss.

**51.** To Varahabhumi Rasikendra once
journeyed, the Prabhu, taking his own people with him.

**52.** His companions are full of [the arts of] music and poetry;
the Prabhu, Achyuta's son, rides in a palanquin.[^19]

**53.** At this time, losing their way, they entered a forest;
the night is dark, clouds cover the sky.

**54.** Leaving the road, all wander from forest to forest;
all went on and entered an impenetrable thicket.

**55.** At this time two tigers blocked the way;
in fear all said to Rasika:

**56.** "Two very fearsome, ferocious tigers
have blocked [the way] ahead — the wicked ones do not let us pass."[^20]

**57.** Putting everyone behind him, the Prabhu went ahead;
going up to the tigers, he began to speak:

**58.** "Listen, listen, you two tigers!
Both of you, give up wicked deeds, give your minds to Krishna.

**59.** From past sins you were born in a wicked race;
if you do not worship Krishna, you will gain toil birth after birth.

**60.** Worship Krishna's lotus feet with firm resolve;
do not trouble any of the sadhus."[^21]

**61.** Hearing Rasika's words, the two tigers
both fell at [his] feet with tearful eyes.

**62.** Placing his hand on [their] heads, he gave the Hari-nama into [their] ears;
hearing the Name, in great bliss they fell at [his] feet.

**63.** Again and again — circumambulation, again — obeisance;
by seeing Rasika they gained divine knowledge.

**64.** Seeing [all] wandering from forest to forest, the two tigers,
clearing the way, go ahead — the two brothers.

**65.** All walk behind, the two in front;
crossing the forest, they came near a village.[^22]

**66.** Morning came; in the village everyone saw [them] —
hundreds of thousands of people come to see the tigers.[^23]

**67.** Rasika gave a command to the two tigers:
"Go, dear ones, go [and] worship Krishna with care."

**68.** Receiving the command, the two bow down;
circumambulating [him], both went off into the forest.

**69.** All the people saw Rasika's manifestation [of power];
all came, together with [the whole] town, for darshan.

**70.** Such is the greatness of Rasikendra-chudamani:
in the forest country he showed mercy to all the people.

**71.** Rasika gave a command to all his companions:
"All of you, keep all these manifestations secret."

**72.** And all the other manifestations he made in the same way —
receiving the command, the companions kept [them] secret.[^24]

**73.** Inviolable is the word of Sri Rasika-chudamani;
his commands are carried out by the best of gods and men.

**74.** He whose commands are carried out by tiger, bear, elephant, serpent,
whose commands are carried out in heaven by Indra, the great king!

**75.** Hearing of [his] greatness, all became stirred;
children [and] the old — all come [and] receive darshan.[^25]

**76.** In all lands the Prabhu gave prema-bhakti;
the poor, the lowly, down to the chandalas, he delivered.

**77.** The utter fools who lived in the forest country —
all became Vaishnavas by the touch of [his] feet.

**78.** Not even with a hundred mouths can [all] his qualities be told;
he flooded the land of Utkala with love for Krishna.

**79.** By his mercy I have made known a little
of the qualities, glory [and] fame of Shyamananda [and] Rasika.[^26]

**80.** [His] pastimes are a boundless sea, known in the world;
all of you, listen to the Rasika-mangala, giving [it your] heart.

**81.** All the pastimes he performed in the circle of the earth,
all the manifestations he made in waves of bliss —

**82.** even speaking with a hundred mouths, they cannot be described;
the Prabhu is an ocean of qualities, a treasury of compassion.

**83.** In brief I have composed [a song] of [his] fair glory;
listen with joy, people of the three worlds.

**84.** Good people [and] scholars, do not hold [my] faults [against me];
setting faults aside, all of you listen with joy.[^27]

**85.** Adorning himself with Shyamananda's feet,
the son of Rasamaya joyfully composed [this].

*Thus in the Uttara-vibhaga of the Sri Rasika-mangala ends the eleventh lahari, called "The Sending of Wild Elephants at the Request of Shah Shuja and the Giving of Hari-nama to Two Wild Tigers."*[^28]
