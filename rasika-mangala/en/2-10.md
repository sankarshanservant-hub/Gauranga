# Dakshina-vibhaga. Lahari 10. The Description of the Panchama-dola

*A song in glory of Shyamananda; on the way from Dharenda Rasika stays at Vasantapur with three disciples of Shyamananda, accepts many disciples there and calls everyone to the Dola-yatra; at Barakola he meets Shyamananda and promises that provisions will be brought from every land; at Shyamananda's order a pavilion for the rasa is built and the place adorned like Vaikuntha, and Rasika brings the Deity Shyama-raya from Dharenda; on the full moon of Vaishakha the Dola-yatra is celebrated: kirtan parties, rajas and bhunyas, the subadar of Medinipur and hundreds of thousands of people gather and throw phagu and abir — this is the first joint pastime of Shyamananda and Rasika; at the festival Vishvanatha Bhunya, brother of Raja Harichandana, skilled in music and letters, at Vamshi's request receives initiation from Rasika and the name Shyama-manohara, gives up worldly life, defeats the adherents of all doctrines in debate and accepts many disciples; Shyamananda sends the Vaishnavas off with gifts*

*The tenth lahari.*

*Raga Varadi.*[^1]
*Ghosha: "O ocean of mercy, O merciful Shyama!*
*Pay heed to fallen and wretched people."*

**1.** Glory, glory to Shyamananda, the life of Vallabha,
friend of the whole world, the source of mercy![^2]

**2.** Thus Rasikendra set out;
that day he rested at Vasantapur.

**3.** Madhava, Sri Haridasa, Madanamohana —
these three are disciples of Shyamananda Prabhu.[^3]

**4.** In their house Rasika-shekhara stayed,
with him ten or twenty young companions.[^4]

**5.** Two or three days he stayed in that village;
Rasika accepted many disciples there.

**6.** He told everyone about the festival:
"Let us all go to see the Dola-yatra!

**7.** Whatever each wishes — take loads of various provisions;
proclaim these words to everyone everywhere."[^5]

**8.** From there Rasikendra set out [further];
in the village of Barakola he met Prabhu.[^6]

**9.** Shyamananda asked Rasika:
"What provisions have you brought, having procured [them]?"[^7]

**10.** Rasikendra said to Shyamananda:
"Do not worry about any provisions.

**11.** By the time of the great festival the loads of provisions will come:
everyone will bring as much as he can.

**12.** From land to land I have proclaimed everything;
many provisions will come, various gifts."

**13.** Hearing this, Shyamananda-raya rejoiced;
he ordered everyone to build a pavilion.

**14.** Receiving the order, everyone built the pavilion:
a pavilion — a place for the rasa — they raised.[^8]

**15.** In various ways they stretched canopies, set up arches;
of various cloths [and] garlands of flowers there was no counting.[^9]

**16.** On the four sides they planted banana trees;
a place beautiful to see — a dense forest.

**17.** Mango, jackfruit, lemon, jambira, kamala,
tabha, shatakara — on every tree cluster upon cluster.[^10]

**18.** A most charming place, beautiful to see,
it became like Vaikuntha, supremely radiant.

**19.** In various ways it was adorned with silks [and] chamaras;
the moon of the Vaishakha full moon — bright was that night.[^11]

**20.** From all lands came rajas [and] subjects —
women, men, children, hundreds of thousands of people.

**21.** Shyamananda-raya gave Rasika an order:
"Bring Thakura Shyama-raya from Dharenda."[^12]

**22.** Receiving the order, Rasikendra set out;
going to Bhima's house, he arrived [there].

**23.** He said to Rasamaya, Chintamani [and] Vamshi:
"Bring Shyama-raya in procession to Barakola.

**24.** The holy Panchama-dola-yatra will be there:
quickly bring Shyama-raya there in procession."[^13]

**25.** Hearing this, all set out joyfully;
taking the Thakura, everyone went there.

**26.** Conches, mahuri, various instruments, rabab, vina;
crying "jaya, jaya," they beat the dundubhis.[^14]

**27.** All entered Barakola;
that same day they performed the adhivasa with fragrances.[^15]

**28.** On the full moon they joyfully arranged the great festival —
the great festival of the Dola-yatra, in great happiness and bliss.

**29.** Many parties came to sing kirtan;
a great many Vaishnavas came from all sides.

**30.** Countless people came — they could not be counted;
rajas [and] bhunyas came with music.[^16]

**31.** The subadar of Medinipur came there;
there were hundreds of thousands of people — it cannot be told.

**32.** Lamps, torches, chandrodayas of various kinds;
in their joy people do not know day from night.[^17]

**33.** Many provisions came, various gifts;
everyone gave provisions of various kinds.

**34.** Be they rajas, be they subjects, be they pilgrims —
all of them, one by one, he satisfied.

**35.** Sankirtana, dundubhis, music of various kinds;
shinga, venu, vishana, songs of many sorts.[^18]

**36.** Heaven, earth and the nether world were filled with cries of "jaya";
for the roar of the dundubhis nothing else could be heard.

**37.** The world of the gods [and] the world of men, coming together,
dance in joy [and] happiness, forming circles.

**38.** All were immersed in bliss, unaware of the body;
all those places became greater than Vaikuntha.[^19]

**39.** Seeing that happiness, all are amazed:
hundreds of thousands of maunds of phagu, chuya — load upon load.[^20]

**40.** Camphor, sandal, fragrant garlands of flowers —
who brings, who gives, no one knows.

**41.** All became many-coloured, adorned with abir;
phagu fell on the ground a hand deep.

**42.** All say: "Never have we seen such happiness!"
All the men and women were immersed in bliss.

**43.** Not even with a hundred mouths can that happy pastime be told —
the first pastime of Shyamananda [and] Rasika.[^21]

**44.** At this time Vishvanatha Bhunya Mahashaya,
the youngest son of Shashadhara Bhunya,[^22]

**45.** brother of Harichandana, lord of the realm,
skilled in music and letters, very pure in mind,

**46.** endowed with every virtue, [eminent] in family, character and honour, —
set out to go there to see the festival.

**47.** With Rasamaya [and] Vamshi [he had] an inseparable friendship;
he beheld Shyamananda [and] Rasika.[^23]

**48.** Vamshi tells Rasika everything:
"Be gracious, Prabhu, take care [of him].

**49.** He is very accomplished in music and letters;
quickly give him prema-bhakti.

**50.** [He is] the brother of Harichandana, lord of the realm:
be merciful to him, Rasika Gosani.

**51.** When such a worthy disciple becomes yours,
he will deliver many living beings."

**52.** Hearing Vamshi's words, Rasika-shekhara says:
"Quickly make [him] a disciple of Shyamananda."[^24]

**53.** Hearing these words, Vishvanathadasa says:
"Birth after birth I am your own servant, your slave.

**54.** Apart from your feet I have no other goal;
you are the lord of my life, my family, character and caste.

**55.** Apart from your feet I know nothing else:
grant me prema-bhakti for Krishna."[^25]

**56.** Hearing his very firm words,
Rasika spoke to Vishvanatha about Krishna.

**57.** Rasika-shekhara gave [him] instruction in the mantra;
with love he gave him the name Shyama-manohara.[^26]

**58.** All the tantras [and] mantras he gave to Shyama-manohara;
he ordered: "Deliver all living beings."

**59.** From that day Shyama-manoharadasa
gave up all endeavours [and] worldly enjoyments.

**60.** By Rasika's touch he became exclusively surrendered [to him];
he accepted many disciples in every land.

**61.** By virtue of much austerity, birth after birth,
he with his whole family took shelter at Rasika's feet.

**62.** Apart from Rasikendra-chandra he knows nothing else;
from his very birth he meditates on Rasika.[^27]

**63.** The master of music, Shyama-manohara,
by Rasika's mercy is prema embodied.

**64.** Very eloquent [and] learned is that mahashaya:
no one could answer him face to face.

**65.** Debaters [and] opponents — [adherents of] logic, of Patanjali's [teaching] and others,
of Sankhya, Sankhyayana, Mimamsa — all the famous ones,[^28]

**66.** Shaivas, Shaktas, Sauras, Ganapatyas — all of them:
Shyama-manohara crushed them all.

**67.** By Rasika's mercy he became the knower of all the shastras,
the expounder of the essence of the four Vedas.

**68.** Swaying in prema for Krishna, [he walks] with the gait of a king of elephants;
in the bliss of Krishna he sports throughout the world.[^29]

**69.** Such is the unfathomable greatness of Rasika:
in the three worlds there is no comparison for it, no limit.

**70.** Thus, having joyfully celebrated the Dola-yatra,
Prabhu bade farewell to the Vaishnavas.[^30]

**71.** Giving cloths [and] ornaments, he bade [them] farewell;
all that happiness cannot be told.

**72.** Briefly I have described something, as it was;
hear the Rasika-mangala, all friends.[^31]

**73.** Adorning himself with Shyamananda's feet,
the son of Rasamaya joyfully composed [this].

*Thus in the Dakshina-vibhaga of the Sri Rasika-mangala ends the tenth lahari, called "The Description of the Panchama-dola."*[^32]

[^1]: The lines before the raga are the lahari's heading ("দশম-লহরী"), in italics without a number. Lahari 10 begins on the lower half of pdf 112, after the colophon of lahari 9 (files RM-2-09-017-021). Raga Varadi (*barāṛī*); the ghosha is the same as in lahari 7 (note 1). There is no mangala-shloka: after the raga and ghosha the song begins with ॥ 1 ॥. The title follows the colophon on pdf 114 (checked against the scan): "পঞ্চমদোল-বর্ণন-নাম দশমলহরী" (*pañcamadola-varṇana*); in the OCR of the 1997 edition — "পঞ্চমদোল-নাম-দশমলহরী". On the Panchama-dola see lahari 8, note 24. The whole lahari is on pdf 112–114; the summary line follows the scan.
[^2]: "The life of Vallabha" — *ballabhera prāṇa*: Vallabha ("the beloved") is apparently Krishna, Gopivallabha ("the beloved of the gopis"; cf. Rasika's Deity Gopivallabha-raya, Dakshina, lahari 6). Thus the author exalts Shyamananda, as when he calls him "Krishna's beloved"; according to Gaudiya Vaishnava teaching Shyamananda is a devotee and servant of Radha, not Radhika Herself (Purva, lahari 1, note 2; Purva, lahari 14, note 11). "The source of mercy" — *karuṇā-nidāna*, lit. "the prime cause of compassion".
[^3]: Vasantapur (*basantapura*) — a village on the way from Dharenda to Barakola; not identified. Madhava, Sri Haridasa, Madanamohana — disciples of Shyamananda; not identified (cf. Madhava and Madanamohanadasa in the list of disciples, Purva, lahari 1, 94 and 101).
[^4]: "In their house" — *tāra ghare* (singular): apparently in the house of one of the three, or of all three. "Young companions" — *bālaka … sahacara*: young disciples accompanying Rasika.
[^5]: "Festival" — *yātrā* (6), i.e. the dola-yatra, the swing festival (lahari 8, note 24). "Loads of various provisions" — *nānādrabya bhāra*: gifts for the festival (cf. 11).
[^6]: "He met Prabhu" — *prabhu karilā darśana*: in Barakola Rasika met Shyamananda, who had gone there before him (lahari 8, 71). In the 1997 edition the first line reads *karila gamana*, the second *kahilā darśana* (OCR). The first line is missing from the OCR of the full edition; the reading follows the scan.
[^7]: "Having procured" — *kariẏā saṅgati*, "having arranged, gathered".
[^8]: "A place for the rasa" — *rāsasthalī*: a pavilion arranged like the place of the rasa dance — for the Deity at the festival.
[^9]: Canopies — *candrātapa* (awnings over the festival ground); arches — *toraṇā* (*toraṇa*), festive gateways. "Garlands of flowers" — *phula-jhārā*, hanging tassels of flowers.
[^10]: Banana trees (*rambhā-bṛkṣa*, 16) are set up around a festival ground as an auspicious sign. The names of the fruits are given as in the edition: jackfruit (*panasa*), jambira (*jambira*, a kind of citron), kamala (*kamalā*, mandarin orange), tabha (*ṭābhā*, a large lemon), shatakara (*śatakarā*, not identified) — cf. Purva, lahari 3, note 11. "Cluster upon cluster" — *jhārā jhārā*.
[^11]: "Silks" — *pāṭa-neta* (*pāṭa*, silk; *neta*, fine cloth). Chamaras are yak-tail whisks. "The Vaishakha full moon" — *boiśākha pūrṇimā* (April–May).
[^12]: Shyama-raya (*śyāmarāẏa*) — a Deity of Krishna in Dharenda; Thakura (*ṭhākura*) — the Deity. Apparently this is Shyamananda's Deity, served in Dharenda (later in the poem Shyama-raya is mentioned together with Thakurani).
[^13]: "To Bhima's house" — *bhīmera mandire* (*mandira*, house or temple): Bhima Shirikara, the ruler of Dharenda who had become a Vaishnava (Dakshina, lahari 5, note 4); apparently Shyama-raya was with him. Rasamaya is the author's father, Vamshi his uncle (Dakshina, lahari 4, note 14); Chintamani is apparently a disciple of Shyamananda (Purva, lahari 1, note 38). "Bring in procession" — *bije karāiha*, a respectful expression for the procession of a Deity.
[^14]: Mahuri (*mahurī*; so in the scan, garbled in the OCR of the 1997 edition) — a reed wind instrument of Utkala, a kind of shehnai. Rabab — a stringed instrument (Purva, lahari 4, note 19); vina — a stringed instrument; dundubhi — a large drum, kettledrum (Purva, lahari 3, note 26).
[^15]: Adhivasa with fragrances — *gandha adhibāsa*: the preliminary rite on the eve of a festival (Dakshina, lahari 6, note 6).
[^16]: "Parties" — *sampradā* (*sampradāẏa*): kirtan groups. Bhunyas (*bhuñā*) — bhuiyans, local landholders and chiefs in Utkala and Medinipur. "Countless" — *apramita*, so in the scan.
[^17]: Lamps — *deuṭī*, oil lamps on stands; torches — *maśāla*; chandrodaya — apparently a large lamp with many wicks, or a firework (Purva, lahari 3, note 26; Dakshina, lahari 5, note 25). Subadar (*subā*, 31) — a governor or administrator (Purva, lahari 10, note 28).
[^18]: Shinga and vishana — horns; venu — a flute (Purva, lahari 4, note 19).
[^19]: Vaikuntha — the spiritual abode of Lord Narayana. "Greater than Vaikuntha" (*baikuṇṭha adhika*) — the author's rapturous comparison (cf. 18).
[^20]: Phagu (*phāgu*) — red powder that people throw on one another at the dola festival; abir (*ābira*, 41) — fragrant coloured powder; chuya (*cuẏā*) — fragrant oil. A maund (*maṇa*) is a measure of weight (about 37 kg): "hundreds of thousands of maunds" is hyperbole. "A hand deep" (41) — *hātek pramāṇa*, lit. "to the measure of a hand".
[^21]: "The first pastime" — *prathama bihāra*: apparently the first festival that Shyamananda and Rasika celebrated together (cf. lahari 9, 20, note 16).
[^22]: Vishvanatha Bhunya (*biśvanātha bhuñā*), afterwards given the name Shyama-manohara (57); not identified in other sources. "The youngest son of Shashadhara Bhunya" — *śaśadhara bhuñā āra kaniṣṭha tanaẏa* (so in the scan and in the 1997 edition): *āra* is taken as the genitive ending (*bhuñāra*); it can also be understood as "and [his] youngest son", i.e. Vishvanatha and Shashadhara's youngest son came. Harichandana (45) — an Oriya title of landholders and rulers (Dakshina, lahari 5, note 13); "lord of the realm" — *rājya-adhipati*, a raja; which principality is not stated. Mahashaya — "great soul", a respectful form of address.
[^23]: "Inseparable friendship" — *abheda milana*, lit. "undivided union".
[^24]: "Quickly make [him] a disciple of Shyamananda" — *śyāmānanda sthāne śiṣya karaha satbara*, lit. "with Shyamananda": apparently Rasika out of humility refers the petitioner to his own guru; Vishvanatha, however, asks for shelter from Rasika himself (53–55), and Rasika initiates him (57).
[^25]: "My family, character and caste" — *kula śīla jāti*: all that a person holds dear in the world is, for Vishvanatha, in Rasika.
[^26]: "Instruction in the mantra" — *mantra upadeśa*, i.e. initiation (Dakshina, lahari 1, note 8). Shyama-manohara (*śyāma manohara*), "Shyama who captivates the heart" — the initiation name; in 59 — Shyama-manoharadasa. "Tantras [and] mantras" (58) — *tantra mantra*: the rules of worship and the mantras.
[^27]: "From his very birth" — *garbha haite*, lit. "from the womb": according to the author, by virtue of the austerities of past lives (61) Vishvanatha was devoted to Rasika from birth.
[^28]: 65–66 list the learned opponents: *bādī bibādī* — debaters and opponents; *tarka* — logic (Nyaya); *pātañjala* — the yoga of Patanjali; *sāṅkhya* — Sankhya; *sāṅkhyāẏana* (so in the scan; the line is missing in the 1997 edition) — apparently Shankhayana (a Vedic school) or a distortion of the name of Vatsyayana, the commentator on Nyaya; the reading is unclear; *mīmāṃsā* — Mimamsa. Shaivas, Shaktas, Sauras (worshippers of Surya), Ganapatyas (worshippers of Ganesha) — followers of non-Vaishnava doctrines. "Crushed" (*dalana*) — defeated in debate.
[^29]: "Swaying" — *ḍagamagī*, "rocking, staggering" with prema. "With the gait of a king of elephants" — *karīndra gamana*, a majestic walk. The second line is *kṛṣṇānande khele se sakala bhubana*; it can also be understood as "in the bliss of Krishna the whole world sports".
[^30]: "Prabhu" — apparently Shyamananda (cf. lahari 11, 2: "Mahashaya … bade farewell to all the Vaishnavas").
[^31]: "As it was" — *sbabhāba*: "a description of [their] nature", or "simply, as it is" (cf. lahari 11, 67: *barṇilu sbabhābe*).
[^32]: The colophon is on pdf 114 (checked against the scan); on the title see note 1. Lahari 11 begins on the same page (files RM-2-11-001-007).
