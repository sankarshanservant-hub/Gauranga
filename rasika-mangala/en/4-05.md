# Uttara-vibhaga. Lahari 5. The Description of the Failure of Gaurangadasi's Conspiracy through the Power of the Glory of Sri Sri Rasikananda Prabhu

*A song in glory of Shyamananda; at the dadhi-kada Rasika, grey with the dust of the rasa and spattered with drops of curd, sits among his own with Krishna and Shyamananda in his heart, and the five sons of Rasamaya, as though sensing the plot, sit down around him, resolved to strike down the wicked and give their lives for Rasika; Padmanabha and Gopidasa read the letter, whose edge the elder mother holds — and in it appear verses of the Bhagavata from all twelve cantos, the siddhanta of the six shastras, the meaning of the Vedas, songs of Jayadeva and at the end three shlokas in glory of Rasika, threatening his revilers with hell; the conspirators, whispering, run off one after another, the Thakurani throws down the letter and weeps, and all acknowledge Rasika's glory; because of the incessant troubles Rasika resolves to hold the festivals at Gopivallabhapur and goes to Raja Ramachandra Dhal, and when the raja disregards his words, he leaves his land in anger, accepting not even water there, and foretells his ruin; the raja soon loses his life and many of his domains, and his son — a great Vaishnava, a disciple of Rasika — is placed on the throne; one festival of twelve days Rasika celebrates at Kusharada, in the kingdom of Patna; five such festivals took place in three villages, and twenty at Gopivallabhapur; the author promises to tell of them*

*The fifth lahari.*

*Raga Shri.*[^1]
*Ghosha: "O Hari, this time be merciful to me:*
*I hope to obtain the shade of Your feet."*

**1.** Glory, glory to Shyamananda, the ocean of mercy,
at whose feet the servant is Rasika-shekhara![^2]

**2.** Thus Rasikendra, performing sankirtana,
danced much, celebrating the dadhi-kada.[^3]

**3.** His charming holy body is grey with the dust of the rasa,
and drops of curd are on his beautiful holy body.

**4.** Sandal paste, kunkuma, phagu, the pollen of flowers —
how [they] glitter on his long cheeks![^4]

**5.** His face, surpassing the moon, is exceedingly charming;
from his holy eyes tears flow in love for Krishna.

**6.** Holding Sri Nandanandana [and] the holy feet of Shyamananda
in his heart, the son of Achyuta remained.[^5]

**7.** Rasika's associates and followers together with his own people —
like a host of stars surrounding the moon.[^6]

**8.** As though knowing by a sign the plan of the wicked,
the five sons of Rasamaya sit down around Prabhu.

**9.** Within their minds the five brothers resolve:
"If anyone makes a disturbance against Prabhu,

**10.** we will strike down all these wicked ones,
and then give our lives before Sri Rasika."[^7]

**11.** Thus all together think within their minds;
all sat down carefully around Rasika.

**12.** Kings, subjects, brahmanas, sannyasis, gentle sadhus —
all the people sat around on all four sides.[^8]

**13.** At that time, bringing the letter, [they] presented [it].
Listen, all, attentively to this account.[^9]

**14.** Padmanabha [and] Gopidasa began to read;
reading the letter, they proclaimed [it] before all.

**15.** The elder mother holds the letter by one edge,
and they here, at the other edge, read [and] let [all] hear.[^10]

**16.** From the first canto to the twelfth —
ten shlokas [from each] were at the beginning of the letter.

**17.** Then — the siddhanta of the six shastras in subtle form:
they read ten or twenty shlokas, one after another.

**18.** Then in the letter the meaning of the truths of the Vedas appeared;
Padmanabha [and] Gopidasa went on reading.

**19.** Then — two or four songs of Sri Jayadeva;
on the four edges of the letter — narratives of the Bhagavata.[^11]

**20.** At the end, after that, three shlokas were written:
"Whoever reviles Murari is a sinner in this world.

**21.** Blameless is Rasika, known to all people;
the beloved of his life is Krishna; [he is] renowned in the world.[^12]

**22.** Whoever without cause lays blame on such a person
goes with all his line to a terrible hell.

**23.** One who is inseparably united with Krishna —
whoever reviles such a person is a sinner [and] a villain.[^13]

**24.** Rasikendra-raya is born of a portion of Narayana;
whoever reviles him, that sinner goes to ruin.[^14]

**25.** Rasika-shekhara is wholly devoted to every dharma;
whoever lays blame on such a person is a sinner, a base wretch.[^15]

**26.** By whose touch Krishna-bhakti [awakens] in all —
such a person the evil-minded revile, not knowing [him].

**27.** By whose darshana all bonds [fall away],
hearing whose words the ears are soothed;

**28.** by whose might the earth was rid of sin —
such a person the fool, the wicked creature reviles."[^16]

**29.** All heard these words in the letter;
the villains, gathering together, whisper to one another.[^17]

**30.** All heard in the letter narratives of the Bhagavata;
not knowing [how], all were thrown into confusion: [where were] those false words?[^18]

**31.** One by one, rising, the villains flee;
who goes in which direction cannot be told.

**32.** Suddenly the pride of all was crushed;
throwing down the letter, the Thakurani began to weep.

**33.** All people came to know Rasika's glory;
astonished, all went to their places.[^19]

**34.** Thus after some days Prabhu, [thinking of leaving] that place,
held counsel with his own people, as was fitting:[^20]

**35.** "Here troubles arise constantly;
my heart no longer consents to stay here.

**36.** The festival will be in Sri Gopivallabhapur;
the community of the Shyamanandis will not come here any more."[^21]

**37.** Having considered all this, Prabhu went to the raja;
sitting in private, he told him the whole matter.

**38.** Ramachandra Dhal, hearing [it], treated [it] with disregard;
in anger [Prabhu] departed from there.[^22]

**39.** He declared: "I will not come to your land again;
in a few days your pride will be brought to ruin."

**40.** From there Rasikendra departed;
in the Dhal land he no longer accepted water.[^23]

**41.** He declared: "Utter ruin will befall him:
in drunken pride this wretch has broken [my] word.

**42.** In his conceit he has wrecked Shyamananda's seat;
his life will hang by a thread, [he] will have to leave the country."[^24]

**43.** Rasika's word is never transgressed, on any day:
whatever order he gives to anyone is never overturned.

**44.** By his word durva grass becomes like wood,
[and] wood like durva: irresistible is [his] word.[^25]

**45.** From then on misfortune came upon the raja:
he lost his life, and many domains were lost.[^26]

**46.** Then after some days Sri-charana carefully
installed his son on the royal throne.

**47.** A great Vaishnava was [this] raja, known in the world;
a disciple of Rasika was the raja, honoured throughout the universe.[^27]

**48.** Seeing Rasika's power, all people,
deeming [it] a wonder, offer [him] praise.

**49.** Then one [festival] of twelve [days] — in the village of Kusharada:
in the kingdom of Patna he stayed some days.[^28]

**50.** These five [festivals] of twelve [days] took place in three villages,
and all the rest in Sri Gopivallabhapur.

**51.** Twenty [festivals] of twelve [days] took place in that village:
the son of Achyuta held [them] at his pleasure.[^29]

**52.** Of them I shall tell something;
listen, listen, all devotees of Krishna, with attention.[^30]

**53.** Who can know [Rasika's] boundless greatness and virtues?
Whatever by Rasika's mercy dawns upon me even a little —

**54.** [those] human pastimes [which] Prabhu, having descended upon the earth,
[displayed] in waves of play —

**55.** describing [his] nature, I have described something —
whatever the son of Achyuta speaks, dwelling [in my] heart.[^31]

**56.** Listen to the Rasika-mangala, all friends:
without delay you will obtain the treasure of prema-bhakti for Krishna.

**57.** Adorning himself with Shyamananda's feet,
the son of Rasamaya joyfully composed [this].

*Thus in the Uttara-vibhaga of the Sri Rasika-mangala ends the fifth lahari, called "The Description of the Failure of Gaurangadasi's Conspiracy through the Power of the Glory of Sri Sri Rasikananda Prabhu."*[^32]

Lahari 5 — 1–57 (pdf 186–187); the summary line covers the whole lahari (24–57 from the scan of pdf 187, provisional).
[^2]: As in Uttara, lahari 2, 1 (note 2): Rasika is Shyamananda's disciple.
[^3]: "Celebrating the dadhi-kada" — *dadhikādā sāri*, literally "having performed, completed the dadhi-kada": the festive drenching with curd (lahari 4, 36, 47, note 23); this is the very dadhi-sankirtana at which Gaurangadasi calls the assembly (lahari 4, 47–55). On the scan there is a stray punctuation mark after *saṃkīrttana*.
[^4]: "Grey with the dust of the rasa" (3) — *rāsadhūli-dhūsara*: with the dust of the circle of the dance. Phagu (*phāgu*) — the red powder people throw at each other at the festival (abir). "The pollen of flowers" — *phulera keśara*. "Long cheeks" — *sudīrgha kapole*.
[^5]: Sri Nandanandana — Krishna, the son of Nanda. "The son of Achyuta" — Rasika (Purva, lahari 3, note 5).
[^6]: "Associates and followers" — *sāṅgopāṅga* (Uttara, lahari 1, note 24). "His own people" — *nijagaṇa*.
[^7]: "The five sons of Rasamaya" — *rasamaẏa pañcaputra*: the author of the poem is a son of Rasamaya, a disciple of Rasikananda (the author's signature), apparently one of these five; the brothers' names are not given. "By a sign" — *iṅgite*. 9b — *yabe prabhu diẏā kare keha gaṇḍagola*: *diẏā* — "through, concerning"; understood as "against Prabhu." "Give our lives" — *prāṇa diba*: readiness to give their lives for the guru.
[^8]: "All together" (11) — *paraspara*. "Sannyasis" — *nyāsī* (so on the scan; OCR "স্যাসী"). "Gentle sadhus" — *śānta sādhujana*. "Sat" — *basilena* (so on the scan; OCR "বলিলেন").
[^9]: "Bringing the letter, presented" — *likhā āni kaila upasana*: the subject is not expressed — apparently the elder mother (lahari 4, 47–55) or her people; *upasana* — "arrival" (lahari 4, note 3), here apparently "presented."
[^10]: Padmanabha and Gopidasa — lahari 4, 55, note 30. "Proclaimed before all" (14) — *sabāsthāne karilā prakāśa*. "Holds by one edge" — *ekadike*: Gaurangadasi does not let the letter out of her hands (lahari 4, 54–55). 15b — *paṛiẏā śunaẏe ethā tā̃rā āra dike*: the sense is approximate.
[^11]: Instead of what the conspirators had written (lahari 4, 33–35, 39), the letter turns out to contain verses of the Bhagavata: "canto" — *skandha*, one of the twelve cantos of the Bhagavata; "ten shlokas" — *daśa daśa śloka*. "The siddhanta of the six shastras" — *ṣaṛśāstrera siddhānta*: of the six philosophical systems (lahari 3, note 16); "in subtle form" — *sūkṣmarūpe*. "The meaning of the truths of the Vedas" (18) — *bedatattba artha* (so on the scan; garbled in the OCR). Jayadeva — the author of the Gita-govinda; "songs" — *gāthā*. "On the four edges" — *cāridike*: where the conspirators had written the slander (lahari 4, 35, 39). This story is not in BR 15.
[^12]: "Three shlokas" — the author gives their sense in Bengali (20b–28, continuing on pdf 187); the Sanskrit text is not given. Murari — Rasika-Murari. "Blameless" — *adoṣita*. 21b — *prāṇera ballabha kṛṣṇa jagata-bikhyātā*: understood as "the beloved of his life is Krishna"; "renowned in the world" apparently refers to Rasika (the endings *-khyātā*, *-bikhyātā* are for the rhyme). "With all his line" (22) — *sabaṃśe*.
[^13]: "Inseparably united with Krishna" — *kṛṣṇera sahita yā̃ra abheda milana*, literally "whose union with Krishna is non-different": praise of Rasika. According to Gaudiya Vaishnava teaching, the devotee is eternally distinct from Krishna, and "non-difference" is understood as complete oneness of heart and will in love and service, not as merging; Rasikananda is a devotee, a disciple of Shyamananda (Pashchima, lahari 4, note 37; on the author's attitude to Shyamananda and Rasika see Purva, lahari 1, note 2; Purva, lahari 14, note 11). "Villain" — *durjjana*. pdf 186 (printed p. 142) ends with a complete ॥ 23 ॥; the words of the shlokas continue on pdf 187 (24–28).
[^14]: 24–28 continue the "three shlokas" found at the end of the letter (20–23, note 12); the quotation mark opened in 20 closes in 28. Rasikendra-raya is Rasika. "Born of a portion of Narayana" — *nārāẏaṇa-aṃśe janma*: so say the miraculously changed words of the letter; according to Gaudiya Vaishnava teaching Rasikananda is a devotee, a disciple of Shyamananda, not an incarnation of the Lord (note 13; Uttara, lahari 4, note 5; on the author's view of Shyamananda and Rasika — Purva, lahari 1, note 2; Purva, lahari 14, note 11). "Goes to ruin" — *kṣaẏa yāẏa*.
[^15]: "Wholly devoted to every dharma" — *sarbbadharmma-parāẏaṇa*. "Lays blame" — *doṣe*. "A base wretch" — *adhama pāmara*. "By whose touch" (26) — *yā̃hāra paraśe*: by contact, association with him. "Not knowing [him]" — *nā jāniẏā*. "The evil-minded" — *duṣṭamati*.
[^16]: "All bonds" (27) — *sarbba bandha*: the bonds of worldly existence (cf. lahari 4, 57). "The ears are soothed" — *juṛāẏa śrabaṇa*, lit. "the ears are cooled, comforted." "Might" (28) — *pratāpe*. "The wicked creature" — *duṣṭa se parāṇī*.
[^17]: "Whisper to one another" — *kāṇākāni kare*: the conspirators (lahari 4, 29–30, 43–46).
[^18]: "Narratives of the Bhagavata" — *bhāgabata-kathā*: verses of the Bhagavata in place of the slander (16–19, note 11). 30b — *nā jāni bhulila sabe hena mithyā kathā*: the sense is approximate; understood as the confusion of the conspirators, who could not find their slander in the letter; it can also be understood as "no one knows how, all [in the letter] forgot those false words."
[^19]: The Thakurani (32) is Gaurangadasi, the "elder mother" (lahari 4, note 18). "The pride of all" — *sabākāra garbba*: of the conspirators. "To their places" (33) — *yathāsthāne*.
[^20]: "That place" — *tathā haite*, "from there": Shyamasundarpur, where the three Thakuranis lived (lahari 4, 11). "His own people" — *ātmagaṇa*. "Held counsel" — *yukti kaila*.
[^21]: "Troubles" (35) — *upadraba*, "disturbances, vexations." "My heart no longer consents" — *nāhi laẏa mane*. 36 is the decision to move the festivals to Gopivallabhapur (50–51; lahari 6); Gopivallabhapur is a village on the Suvarnarekha, Rasika's main place of service (Dakshina, lahari 3). "The community of the Shyamanandis" — *śyāmānandī goṣṭhī*.
[^22]: "To the raja" (37) — *rājāsthāne*. "In private" — *nigame* (lahari 4, note 19). "The whole matter" — *sakala tattba*. Ramachandra Dhal (*rāmacandra dhala*) is apparently the ruler of Dhalbhum ("the Dhal land," 40), a region on the Suvarnarekha around Ghantashila (Ghatshila; Purva, lahari 14, note 4), neighbouring Gopivallabhapur; *dhala* is the family name of the rulers of Dhalbhum; the identification is provisional. Among Rasika's disciples a "Raja Ramachandra" is named (Pashchima, lahari 14, 136) — whether this is the same person is not said. What exactly Rasika asked of the raja is not said (cf. 42). "Treated [it] with disregard" — *karila helana*. 38b — the subject is not expressed; apparently Rasika (cf. 40).
[^23]: "Declared" (39, 41) — *ājñā kaila*, lit. "ordered." "Brought to ruin" — *chārakhāra haibe*. "No longer accepted water" (40) — *jala āra nā kaila grahaṇa*: a severance of all relations (lahari 3, note 6). "The Dhal land" — *dhala deśa* (note 22).
[^24]: "Utter ruin" (41) — *sarbbanāśa*. "In drunken pride" — *madagarbbe*. "Has broken [my] word" — *bacana bhāṅgila*: has disregarded Rasika's request. "Shyamananda's seat" (42) — *śyāmānanda-pāṭa*: *pāṭa* is a place of service, a seat; the sense of 42a is approximate: apparently by his refusal the raja wrecked what had been arranged by Shyamananda's order (the three Thakuranis and the Deities together in Shyamasundarpur — lahari 2, 47–54; lahari 4, 10–11). "In his conceit" — *ātmagarbbe*. "His life will hang by a thread" — *prāṇa laiẏā ṭānāṭāni*, lit. "a tug-of-war over his life." "Will have to leave the country" — *deśatyāga ha'be*.
[^25]: "On any day" (43) — *sarbba dine*: always. "Is never overturned" — *nā haẏa khaṇḍane*. Durva (44) is a soft grass used in rites; the figure of speech: Rasika's word can make the soft hard and the hard soft. "Irresistible" — *alaṅghya*.
[^26]: "Misfortune came" — *hailā akāye* (so in the scan, crop ×2): understood as *akāja*, "misfortune, ill"; the sense is approximate. "He lost his life" — *prāṇa hata hailā*: what was said in 39, 41–42 came to pass. 45b — *āra tyāga bahu rājye*, lit. "and the abandonment of many kingdoms"; it can also be understood as "[his house] had to leave many domains" (cf. 42: "will have to leave the country").
[^27]: "Sri-charana" (46) — *śrīcaraṇe*, "the holy feet": an honorific designation; apparently Rasika (cf. lahari 6, 46); it can also be understood as "[Rasika] installed his son, [who had fallen] at [his] holy feet, on the throne." The raja's son is not named. "Installed on the royal throne" — *sthāpilena rājapade*. "Known in the world" (47) — *jagata-bidita*; "honoured throughout the universe" — *bhubana-pūjita*.
[^28]: Kusharada (*kuśaradā*, so in the scan) and the kingdom of Patna (*pāṭanā rājya*) are unidentified; apparently not Patna in Bihar but a local principality. "One [festival] of twelve [days]" — *eka duẏādaśa*: the annual twelve-day aradhana of Shyamananda (Uttara, lahari 2, 4–5, note 5). 49b is understood as an explanation: for the sake of the festival Rasika stayed there some days.
[^29]: "Five [festivals] of twelve [days] in three villages" (50) — *pā̃ca duẏādaśa haila tina grāme*: apparently two in Govindapur (Uttara, lahari 1, 23; lahari 2, 44–45, note 26), two in Shyamasundarpur (lahari 4, 19, note 12) and one in Kusharada (49); the count is conjectural (cf. the "third" festival — lahari 4, 12, 28, note 8). "In that village" (51) — in Gopivallabhapur: apparently twenty annual festivals in memory of Shyamananda held there by Rasika. The son of Achyuta is Rasika. "At his pleasure" — *āpana sukhe* (lahari 3, note 3).
[^30]: "I shall tell" — *kariba bidita*, "I shall make known": the account of the festival in Gopivallabhapur is lahari 6. "Devotees of Krishna" — *kārṣṇa-bṛnda* (so in the scan, crop ×2; garbled in the OCR; cf. Purva, lahari 1, note 66).
[^31]: "Greatness and virtues" (53) — *garima-guṇa*. "Dawns upon me" — *sphure*, "flashes [in the mind]." "Having descended upon the earth" (54) — *abatīrṇa haiẏā abanīmaṇḍale*: the author speaks of Rasika as one descended from above (note 14; Purva, lahari 1, note 2; Purva, lahari 14, note 11). "Human pastimes" — *mānabika līlā*. "In waves of play" — *kautuka-kallole*. "Describing [his] nature" (55) — *sbabhāba barṇanā* (cf. Purva, lahari 1, note 68). 53–55 form one sentence; the connection of the parts is approximate. The author ascribes his inspiration to Rasika dwelling in his heart (cf. lahari 6, 59).
