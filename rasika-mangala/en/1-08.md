# Purva-vibhaga. Lahari 8. Hearing the Srimad-Bhagavatam

*A shloka on the superiority of bhakti over liberation and a song in glory of Shyamananda; the boy Rasika plays Bhagavata pastimes with the children day and night and forgets to eat; at his request Achyuta invites a learned brahmana to recite the Bhagavata, and Rasika, sitting on his father's lap, hears in order of Krishna's deeds in Mathura and Dvaraka, and faints at the account of the gopis' separation; he shapes a Deity and worships Him, holds sankirtana with the children and plays at pilgrimage; people call him Krishna's companion and bless him*

*The eighth lahari.*

**S1.** *Those whose understanding is formed by the study of the Vedas and reflection [on them] praise liberation as supreme,*
*but they, with an understanding made certain by guru and shastra, know nothing.*
*In my view bhakti is greater; and therefore, O fool,*
*lead your mind to him — Murari-deva Rasikananda, the shelter of peace.*[^1]

*Raga Varadi.*[^2]
*Ghosha: "O king of the Yadus! O beautiful one, darling, ah!"*

**1.** *Song.* Glory, glory to Shyamananda, the son of Durika!
Glory, glory to the treasure of Rasika-deva's life![^3]

**2.** Thus day by day he grows cleverer;
day and night he plays [the pastimes] in the order of the Bhagavata.

**3.** Putting aside [all care for] bed, food and sleep — everything,
he gathers the children and plays with eagerness.

**4.** Nothing pleases him but the Bhagavata.
Achyuta, taking [him] on his lap, asks again and again:

**5.** "You eat nothing, child — you play all the time,
gathering ten or twenty children around you.

**6.** You eat no rice, drink no water — you play without pause.
From tomorrow sit at home and play every day."

**7.** Hearing his father's words, he answers very softly —
the words barely leave his lips, [such is] the sweetness of his speech:

**8.** "Then I will not play [, running] all over the town,
if you will listen to the Bhagavata with reverence.

**9.** Sit still and listen to the narrations of the Bhagavata —
then I will by no means go out to play."[^4]

**10.** Hearing his son's words, [Achyuta] rejoiced in his heart:
"Good, good! So I have decided."

**11.** With care he invited a teacher —
the best of brahmanas, a bhattacharya, an ornament of Mimamsa.[^5]

**12.** Achyuta speaks these words to the teacher:
"My son wishes to hear the Sri Bhagavata.

**13.** Every day recite [to him this book], full of Krishna's pastimes."
Saying "good," the venerable [teacher] began [to read] the manuscript.

**14.** Sitting on his father's lap, Prabhu hears
[how Krishna] in childhood played eagerly with the children.[^6]

**15.** All this he heard with delight for some days;
the pastimes of the slaying of Kamsa and others he hears one after another.

**16.** Now one day he hears with love [how]
[Krishna] made Ugrasena king in Mathura.[^7]

**17.** One day he hears with delight of the schooling:
[how Krishna], bringing back his teacher's son, gave [him] to him.

**18.** One day he hears how Uddhava goes to Vraja,
[and] of the gopis' hurt pride [voiced] under the pretext [of speaking to] the bee.[^8]

**19.** Hearing of the gopis' separation, Rasika
falls senseless from his father's lap.

**20.** The hairs stand on end all over his body, streams [of tears] flow from both eyes;
seeing [this], Achyuta remembers Sri Krishna.

**21.** Lifting [him], alarmed, he wiped [his] face,
saying, "Krishna, protect this child!"

**22.** Thus every day on Achyuta's lap
he listens with love and great eagerness.

**23.** One day he hears of the meeting in Kubja's house,
one day — of how [Krishna] went to Akrura's house.

**24.** One day he hears how Akrura entered Hastina
to learn about [His] own servants, the Pandavas.

**25.** One day — [how] Kamsa's two wives, Asti and Prapti,
went to their father Jarasandha and complained [to him].[^9]

**26.** One day he hears of Jarasandha in [all his] might:
[how he] besieges Mathura in a terrible battle.

**27.** Again and again, seventeen times he fights,
[and] the king of Magadha goes away defeated.

**28.** One day he hears how [Krishna], leaving Madhupuri,
settled in Dvaraka with His kinsmen and friends.

**29.** One day he hears the story of Kalayavana:
[how he], breaking Muchukunda's sleep, was turned to ashes.

**30.** One day he hears Muchukunda's prayers;
of the burning of the mountain [and] the flight of the two brothers.[^10]

**31.** One day he hears of the abduction of Rukmini:
[how she], sending a brahmana to Dvaraka, summons Narayana;

**32.** [how He], binding Rukmi and shaving [his head],
fought a great battle with all the kings.

**33.** One day he hears of the abduction of Pradyumna
and of the deliverance of Pradyumna, who slew Shambara.

**34.** One day he hears of the theft of the Syamantaka jewel:
[how Krishna] fought with Jambavan,

**35.** [how He], because of a slander, brought the Syamantaka jewel
and married Jambavati Thakurani.

**36.** One day he hears of the marriage to Satyabhama:
Krishna slew Shatadhanva in battle.

**37.** One day he hears of the journey to Indraprastha
to see His servants, the Pandavas.

**38.** One day he hears of the marriage to Kalindi,
of the marriage to Nagnajiti and how [He] bound the seven bulls.

**39.** One day he hears of the slaying of Narakasura:
[Krishna] took his sixteen thousand one hundred maidens [as wives].[^11]

**40.** One day he hears of the taking of the parijata:
[Krishna] defeated the lord of the gods in battle.

**41.** One day he hears of Rukmini's bewilderment:
[how she], her voice choked with prema, offers prayers.[^12]

**42.** One day — of the number of the sons of the eight queens;
one day he hears of Aniruddha's marriage.

**43.** One day he hears of Pradyumna's marriage
and of how the king of Kalinga's teeth were knocked out.

**44.** One day he hears of the slaying of Rukmi,
of the abduction of Usha, of that unceasing battle with Bana.[^13]

**45.** One day he hears of the deliverance of King Nriga;
[of how] Balarama, coming to Vraja, sees His friends,

**46.** drags the Yamuna with His plough;
one day he hears of the slaying of Pundarika.[^14]

**47.** One day — [how] Sudarshana burns Varanasi;
[how] the fire, [driven] by the splendour of the disc, rushed to seek shelter.[^15]

**48.** [How] Shyama-kumara was bound in the land of Hastina —
for that reason Balarama went [there].[^16]

**49.** One day he hears how [Balarama] drags Hastina;
[how] Narada [comes] to Dvaraka to see Krishna.

**50.** In every palace Narada sees Bhagavan
and in supreme bliss offers prayers unceasingly.

**51.** One day he hears [how] Jarasandha
put ninety-six thousand kings in fetters.[^17]

**52.** Their messenger comes and tells Krishna [of it];
[and] again Narada comes to Dvaraka.

**53.** One day, [taking] Uddhava's counsel,
Krishna enters Hastina, to the Pandavas.

**54.** One day he hears of the slaying of Jarasandha:
[Krishna] freed the kings from captivity.

**55.** One day he hears the account of the Rajasuya sacrifice,
one day — the news of the slaying of Shishupala.

**56.** One day he hears of Duryodhana's humiliation,
one day he hears with great delight of the slaying of Shalva.

**57.** One day he hears of the slaying of Dantavakra,
one day — of Balarama's pilgrimage to the holy places;

**58.** one day he hears of [His] coming to Naimisharanya:
Balarama slays Suta the Pauranika.[^18]

**59.** [How He] visited all the holy places there are on earth.
Constantly he hears how Sudama's poverty was removed.[^19]

**60.** One day he hears [how] at the time of a solar eclipse
the king of the Yadus went to Kurukshetra,

**61.** met with Nanda and the other cowherds;
[how] Draupadi questions the eight queens.

**62.** One day — the prayers of Vasudeva and Devaki;
of the abduction of Subhadra he hears with undivided attention.

**63.** One day he hears of the journey to the land of Videha;
one day he hears [how] the four Vedas offer prayers.[^20]

**64.** One day — of the cause of the sages' dispute;
one day he hears of Bhrigu's coming to Dvaraka:

**65.** [how] Krishna, [receiving] a kick, bears [on His chest the mark of] Shrivatsa —
[this] Rasika hears with joy and attention.[^21]

**66.** One day he hears of Arjuna's vow:
[how Krishna] keeps [him] from entering the fire and restores the children.

**67.** One day he hears the count of the sons.
Thus he hears the Bhagavata unceasingly.[^22]

**68.** Day after day he hears the twelve cantos of the Bhagavata
[and] all the glory of Krishna that there is in the Puranas.

**69.** He hears those shastras in which [is told] the glory of Krishna,
and, hearing, he weeps, filled with tender feeling.

**70.** One day, bringing clay at an auspicious moment,
with his own hands he makes a Sri-murti (Deity).[^23]

**71.** Installing [Him], he dresses [Him] in various ways;
incense, lamps, naivedya — the sixteen articles of worship.

**72.** One day, taking the children, [he holds] sankirtana:
some sing, some play [instruments], some dance.

**73.** One day, taking up renunciation in childish play,
out of love for Krishna he goes off to wander to the holy places.

**74.** The children bring him back from far away;
day and night he plays this game with the children.

**75.** Apart from Krishna he knows nothing else even for a moment:
those plays, those qualities — hearing [of them], meditation [on them].

**76.** Without pause his eyes are wet with tears;
Krishna is [his] life, Krishna his wealth, Krishna his kin.

**77.** Seeing all these signs in Rasika in his childhood,
the men and women are amazed.

**78.** All say: "This boy is Krishna's companion;
day and night his body shines with absorption in Krishna.

**79.** Because of him the father listens to the Bhagavata;
apart from the Bhagavata he knows nothing, day or night.[^24]

**80.** O Krishna, always protect this boy!
This great soul will deliver all living beings."

**81.** Thus all the people bless [him] —
brahmanas, kshatriyas, vaishyas, shudras — all who see [him].

**82.** All who see the beauty of [his] holy moon-like face,
forgetting themselves, gaze [at him] intently.

**83.** A gentle, gentle smile, tender, soft speech —
hearing [it], all living beings are enchanted.

**84.** Thus in the mood of childhood Rasika-shekhara
without pause enacts Krishna's pastimes with the children.

**85.** I am unable to tell [of it] in detail;
briefly I have described this [nature of his].

**86.** The narrative of the Purva-vibhaga is supremely full of rasa;
hearing the Rasika-mangala, cross over the age of Kali.[^25]

**87.** Adorning himself with Shyamananda's feet,
the son of Rasamaya joyfully composed [this].

*Thus ends the eighth lahari, called "Hearing the Srimad-Bhagavatam," in the Purva-vibhaga of the Sri Rasika-mangala.*

[^1]: The shloka is unnumbered in the edition (after it come the raga, the ghosha and the song with ॥ 1 ॥); it is marked S1, like the mangala-shlokas of lahari 1 (note 1) and 6 (note 1). The metre is shardula-vikridita. Second line: *guru-śāstra-niścita-dhiyā* — "with an understanding made certain by guru and shastra," i.e. for all their learning. Murari-deva Rasikananda is Rasikananda (Rasika-Murari, lahari 5, 13–15). According to Gaudiya Vaishnava teaching, liberation (mukti) is inferior to bhakti: the pure devotee does not accept even liberation without service to the Lord (Bhag. 3.29.13; Bhag. 1.1.2 and CC, Adi 1: the desire for moksha is a "cheating" rejected by the Bhagavata). The source of the shloka has not been identified — apparently the author's own composition.
[^2]: Raga Varadi (*barāṛī*) — a raga frequent in Vaishnava padas. The ghosha (refrain) reads in the scan: *yadurājā nārere sundara yādumaṇi āhāre*. *Yādumaṇi* is an endearment, "darling, treasure" (said to a child), echoing "Yadu"; *āhā re* is an exclamation; the sense of *nāre re* (possibly a mere sung filler) is unclear.
[^3]: "Song" — *gīta*, the edition's marker before ॥ 1 ॥, as in lahari 1. Durika is Shyamananda's mother (lahari 1, note 11). Rasika-deva is Rasikananda.
[^4]: 8–9: "listen" (*śuna*) is addressed to the father: the boy asks that his father hear the Bhagavata [with him]; then (11–14) Achyuta invites a reciter, and Rasika listens sitting on his father's lap.
[^5]: "Ornament of Mimamsa" — *mīmāṁsā-maṇḍana*: an expert in Mimamsa (the school of Vedic exegesis); possibly also an honorific title or the teacher's name. In the edition's list of errata the reading "মীমাংম" is corrected to "মীমাংসা". Bhattacharya is a title of a learned brahmana.
[^6]: Prabhu is Rasika (lower case for him, as is the rule in this translation). The second line is elliptical; it is understood as the account of Krishna's childhood play (Bhag., Canto 10, up to ch. 44); "Kamsa and others" in 15 — Bhag. 10.36–44 (cf. lahari 7, 113–121).
[^7]: From 16 to 67 the list follows the order of the second half of the Tenth Canto of the Bhagavata (10.45–90); only some episodes and divergences are noted below. 17 — the schooling with Sandipani Muni and the return of his son (10.45).
[^8]: Bhag. 10.46–47: the "Bhramara-gita" — the gopis address a bee (*bhramara*), seeing it as Krishna's messenger. "Hurt pride" — *abhimāna*, the wounded feeling of a lover.
[^9]: Asti and Prapti — Kamsa's wives, daughters of Jarasandha (Bhag. 10.50; so also the edition's gloss). "Complained" — *gohārī karila* (the edition's gloss: "complaint"). 26 — *māhātme*, probably = *māhātmye*, "in [his] greatness, might."
[^10]: Bhag. 10.52: Jarasandha set fire to Mount Pravarshana, where Krishna and Balarama had taken refuge; They leapt down and went away to Dvaraka. "Flight" — so in the text (*palāyana*); the Bhagavata (10.52) says that the brothers fled only feigning fear.
[^11]: The sixteen thousand one hundred princesses whom Naraka held captive (Bhag. 10.59). *Kanyā naila* — "took the maidens."
[^12]: "Rukmini's bewilderment" — *rukmiṇīra mohana*: Krishna jokingly suggested that Rukmini choose another husband, and she fainted; then come her prayers (Bhag. 10.60).
[^13]: The scan reads *rukmiṇīra saṁhāra*, "the slaying of Rukmini"; the edition's list of errata corrects this word (the corrected form is illegible in the OCR) — by sense "Rukmi": Rukmini's brother, slain by Balarama at Aniruddha's wedding (Bhag. 10.61). Usha and Bana — Bhag. 10.62–63.
[^14]: Pundarika (*puṇḍarīka*) — in the Bhagavata, Paundraka, the false Vasudeva (10.66).
[^15]: Bhag. 10.66: the fiery demon (kritya) sent from Varanasi against Dvaraka fled back, pursued by Sudarshana, and burned those who had raised it; Sudarshana burned Varanasi.
[^16]: *Śyāma-kumāra* — so in the scan; by sense Samba, Jambavati's son, whom the Kauravas bound in Hastinapura for carrying off Duryodhana's daughter; Balarama dragged the city with His plough (Bhag. 10.68; cf. 49).
[^17]: According to the Bhagavata (10.70), twenty thousand eight hundred kings; the author has *chiyānabbai sahasra*, "ninety-six thousand."
[^18]: Bhag. 10.78: Balarama slew Romaharshana Suta, who had not risen before Him in the assembly of sages at Naimisharanya.
[^19]: Sudama — the poor brahmana, Krishna's childhood friend (Bhag. 10.80–81); not to be confused with Sudama the garland-maker (lahari 7, 118).
[^20]: 60–63: Bhag. 10.82–87: the meeting at Kurukshetra, Draupadi's questions to the queens, the prayers of Vasudeva and Devaki, the abduction of Subhadra, the visit to Mithila (the land of Videha), the prayers of the personified Vedas (the "Shruti-gita," 10.87).
[^21]: Bhag. 10.89: the sages disputed which of the three deities is greatest; Bhrigu, testing them, kicked Vishnu on the chest — in the Bhagavata this happens in Vaikuntha, in the author's account in Dvaraka. The author identifies the mark of Bhrigu's foot on the Lord's chest with the Shrivatsa.
[^22]: 66: Bhag. 10.89: Arjuna vowed to protect the children of a brahmana of Dvaraka and, having failed, wished to enter the fire; Krishna restrained him and brought the children back from the abode of Maha-Vishnu. 67: "the count of the sons" — Krishna's descendants (10.90).
[^23]: Sri-murti — the form of the Lord for worship, the Deity. The sixteen articles of worship (*ṣoḍaśa-upacāra*) — the full order of puja: offering of a seat, water, garments, scents, a lamp, food (naivedya) and so on.
[^24]: Cf. 8–14: at his son's request Achyuta invited a reciter and listens to the Bhagavata together with him. The second line refers to the boy.
[^25]: "Cross over the age of Kali" — *tara kali-kāla*: by hearing this book, overcome [the miseries of] the Kali age. Cf. the call to listen at the close of lahari 7 (123).
