# Purva-vibhaga. Lahari 16. The Meeting with Sri Shyamananda

*A song in glory of Shyamananda; at Ghantashila Rasika listens to the "Bhagavata" in the raja's assembly and questions the brahmanas about its meaning; Shyamananda enters, and the raja and everyone receive him with reverence; Rasika and Shyamananda recognize each other in their hearts and weep; in private Rasika falls at his feet, Shyamananda embraces him and says that he has come from Vrindavana by Krishna's command to see him; spending the chaturmasya together, they discuss the scriptures, and Shyamananda reveals to Rasika the path of worshipping Radha and Krishna in Vrindavana in the mood of madhurya and the power of prema, by which Krishna is conquered; Shyamananda embraces and blesses Rasika; thus the Purva-vibhaga concludes*

*The sixteenth lahari.*

*Raga Kaushika.*[^1]
*Ghosha: "Glory, O glory to Rama and Krishna!*
*O Murari, O Murari!"*

**1.** Glory, glory to Shyamananda, the life of all [the world]!
Be merciful, so that I may describe [your] glory.[^2]

**2.** How the meeting of Shyamananda with Rasika came about —
of that I tell in detail.

**3.** Rasika lives joyfully in the village of Ghantashila;
day and night he sees Shyamananda in meditation.[^3]

**4.** One day all are sitting in the raja's assembly;
before the raja a brahmana began [to recite] the "Bhagavata".

**5.** Rasika, sitting there, listens to the "Bhagavata";
Raja Vaikuntha listens to the narration of the "Bhagavata".[^4]

**6.** The assembly in the capital is large and beautiful to see;
the great brahmanas are like the foremost [knowers] of the Vedas.[^5]

**7.** Rasika joyfully questions them all:
one after another he asks about the inner meaning of the "Bhagavata".[^6]

**8.** At that time Shyamananda came;
entering the midst of the assembly, he presented himself [there].[^7]

**9.** A body beautiful to see, a golden form,
arms reaching to the knees, a captivating face.

**10.** A face with a gentle, gentle smile, a beautiful glance,
a gait unhurried like that of a king of elephants — most captivating.[^8]

**11.** Seeing great power in [his] form, all are amazed;
the raja with all his courtiers quickly rose.

**12.** Stretched out like a stick, he fell at [his] feet;
all saw [him] as if he were a second Narayana.[^9]

**13.** Seeing [that] wondrous form, the raja, leaving [his own] seat,
carefully seats [him] upon [that] seat.

**14.** Seeing Shyamananda, Rasika [was filled with] blissful jubilation:
Prabhu, the giver of prema-bhakti, has appeared.[^10]

**15.** Shyamananda sat down with a joyful heart;
looking around on all sides, he observes the people one by one.

**16.** Seeing Rasika's form, he is enchanted at heart:
"This man must be Rasika-shekhara."

**17.** Neither knows the other [by sight], [yet] both know in their minds;
seeing each other's form, both wept.[^11]

**18.** Soon, having heard the "Bhagavata", the raja-mahashaya
and all [with him] withdrew into the palace.

**19.** The brahmanas all went away, each to his own place;
Rasika remained alone, knowing for certain [who had come].[^12]

**20.** In that assembly Shyamananda settled down;
Shyamananda, the jewel among men, sat there.[^13]

**21.** In private Rasika went and fell at [his] feet;
streams of bliss flow from Rasika's eyes.

**22.** Rising, Shyamananda-raya took [him] into his embrace
[and] asked everyone: "Whose son is this man?

**23.** What is this youth's name? Tell me.
His form is sweet to see, a gentle smile is on his face."[^14]

**24.** All give [his] name — Murari,
the son of Achyuta, the lord of Mallabhum.[^15]

**25.** Hearing [this], Shyamananda seated him by his side
[and] asked about everything in detail.

**26.** He asked about the worldly affairs of [his] people, one by one,
and then carefully spoke of the highest goal.[^16]

**27.** "Leaving Vraja, I have come to see you,
by the command of Krishna and of all the dwellers of Vraja.[^17]

**28.** You, the son of Achyuta, are an associate of Krishna;
to see [you] I have come, leaving Vrindavana."[^18]

**29.** Hearing [this], Rasika speaks with embarrassment:
"Birth after birth I am the servant of your feet.

**30.** Regarding [me] as your own servant, be gracious [to me] in your heart:
you have granted me the sight of you by [your] grace."[^19]

**31.** Thus the two conversed with each other;
taking leave, Rasika came home.

**32.** For the [period of] chaturmasya Shyamananda stayed there,
joyfully holding discussions with Rasika.[^20]

**33.** The two ceaselessly [hold] talks about Krishna, [forgetting] food;
night and day the two sit in seclusion.[^21]

**34.** First they examined all the shastras:
Mimamsa, Patanjali's [yoga] and the others — the essence of the truth of the Vedas.

**35.** Sankhya, Sankhyayana and the truth of the Bhagavata —
all [this] Rasika explains in agreement with Svami.[^22]

**36.** He explains prema-bhakti in agreement with the shastras:
all the shastras call loving devotion to Krishna the essence.

**37.** Hearing Rasika's explanation — the greatness of bhakti —
Shyamananda took [him] into his embrace and showed [him] mercy.

**38.** Thus day after day the examination of the shastras
the two carry on, sitting [together], and know nothing else.

**39.** Thus Shyamananda, in seclusion, to Rasika
explains one by one everything about what bhajana should be.

**40.** Citing all the shastras and all the tantras as authority,
Shyamananda spoke to Rasika.

**41.** "The Fish, the Tortoise, the Boar, Sri Nrisimha, Vamana,
Parashurama, Rama, Bala — the son of Rohini,

**42.** Buddha, Kalki — [and] all the avataras,
as many as are proclaimed on the authority of the shastras —

**43.** whoever desires whichever form worships that very form;
[but I] speak of the true nature of the worship of Chaitanya.[^23]

**44.** Once the sages questioned Narada;
he expounded the truth of the shastras, keeping [it] hidden.

**45.** Those who heard Narada's words
began to worship [Krishna] in the mood of madhurya.[^24]

**46.** Krishna, the Lord of Vrindavana, is Bhagavan Himself;
[worship Him] as the Shrutis meditated [on Him].[^25]

**47.** The bank of the Yamuna, charming Vrindavana;
at the root of a wish-fulfilling tree — the beautiful circle of the rasa.

**48.** Various jewels and gems shine on the jewelled throne;
the rays of the gems are [like] the radiance of millions of suns.

**49.** The ground [there] is chintamani; it rains nectar;
the earthly world became blessed by touching it.[^26]

**50.** The women of that abode are Lakshmis indeed;
all the men there are truly Vishnus.[^27]

**51.** Within and beyond millions of universes
[this] abode shines eternally, exceedingly charming.

**52.** The city of jewels and gems is splendidly adorned;
it cannot be told in words; Indra and the other gods worship it.

**53.** In such an abode, [beneath] a wish-fulfilling tree, on a jewelled throne —
dark, beautiful Krishna, [and] Radha-jiu on [His] left.

**54.** A graceful, thrice-bent form, a youth in fresh kishora age;
curling hair gathered in a topknot, crowned with a peacock feather.

**55.** Around the topknot are strings of various jewels and gems,
jati, yuthi, mallika, malati, kera-kera.[^28]

**56.** On the beautiful forehead shines a line of gorochana;
the fine hairs above it look like a line of bees.[^29]

**57.** The pair of brows looks like the bow of Kama;
His two eyes surpass the petals of the lotus.

**58.** The nose surpasses the tila flower; a pearl sways [on it];
on the crimson lips a row of teeth glitters.

**59.** On the face is a gentle smile, the speech is sweet;
the face shines, surpassing the autumn moon.

**60.** Earrings adorn the ears, swaying at the cheeks;
seeing that beauty, the people of the world lose their minds.

**61.** A necklace of gems and pearls adorns the neck;
on the chest is the Kaustubha gem; [He] is marked with the Shrivatsa.

**62.** On the shapely arms are armlets, golden bracelets;
rings of gems and jewels are the ornaments of the fingers.[^30]

**63.** A beautiful deep navel; three folds [of the belly] — [like] the Triveni;
the beautiful waist shines, surpassing millions of lions.[^31]

**64.** He wears a yellow dhoti with a swaying end;
on the waist, row upon row, little bells shine.

**65.** The beauty of the two thighs surpasses pillars of emerald;
this form, when they see it, captivates the hearts of the women of Vraja.

**66.** The two lotus feet are very soft —
[like] young shoots, the lotus, the newly risen sun.

**67.** Anklets of gems adorn both feet;
at the radiance of the nails millions of moons are put to shame.

**68.** The flag [and] the upward line are on His right foot,
the lotus, the thunderbolt, the svastika, the wheel — beautiful;

**69.** the umbrella and the goad adorn that same right foot;
on the left — the cow's hoofprint, the sky, the pot, the conch, the fish;

**70.** the rainbow [and] the triangle adorn the left foot;
the jambu fruit [and] the half-moon adorn that same place.[^32]

**71.** Such, in the attire of the best of dancers, is the Wearer of the forest garland;
on [His] left is beautiful Radhika, exceedingly enchanting.

**72.** At the eight corners of the throne are eight dear friends:
eight moon-faced ones serve Radhika and Krishna.

**73.** At the eight gates eight sakhis sit there;
in the four groups the group leaders play various instruments.[^33]

**74.** Such is the sweet worship of Radha and Krishna;
in this mood worship Krishna, taking pains."

**75.** And all that was in the storehouse of his own prema
he revealed to Rasika one by one.

**76.** "Being a man, becoming prakriti, abide with the son of Nanda:
worshipping in this mood, you will quickly attain Krishna.[^34]

**77.** This is a secret of the Vedas — no one else knows it;
only by Krishna's mercy does knowledge of the truth of prema come.

**78.** Without this prema Krishna is never attained:
Krishna, Sri Nandananda, is subject to prema.

**79.** Out of prema a gopi fed [Him] what she had chewed in her mouth;
out of prema He carried garments on His shoulder — [this] is well known.[^35]

**80.** Bhagavan is subdued by prema — [so] say all the shastras;
worship the son of Nanda in the mood of prema-bhakti.

**81.** Taking refuge in Him alone, worship Bhagavan —
and you will surely attain Krishna, the life of all."

**82.** Hearing Shyamananda's words, Rasika-shekhara
is drenched from head to foot by the streams [of tears from his] eyes.[^36]

**83.** Rasika fell at Shyamananda's lotus feet
and washed [his] holy feet with the water of [his] eyes.

**84.** Shyamananda-raya in prema embraced [him]
and with the greatest mercy blessed [him]:

**85.** "Ceaselessly Krishna sports in your heart;
you are the embodiment of love for Krishna, son of Achyuta.

**86.** With you I shall deliver all living beings."
Thus he accepted Rasika.

**87.** [Thus] took place the meeting of Shyamananda and Rasika;
now I shall tell of the instruction — listen, everyone.[^37]

**88.** The Rasika-mangala is exceedingly, supremely full of rasa;
hearing [it], O people, cross over [the ocean of] the age of Kali.

**89.** Adorning himself with Shyamananda's feet,
the son of Rasamaya joyfully composed [this].

**90.** Thus in the Purva-vibhaga [the author] has brought [the account] to completion —
[of] how Rasika met with Shyamananda.[^38]

*Thus in the Purva-vibhaga of the Sri Rasika-mangala ends the sixteenth lahari, called "The Meeting with Sri Shyamananda".*

[^1]: Raga Kaushika — as in lahari 3 (note 1); the ghosha (refrain) is almost the same as there, and its translation is repeated: here it is "মুরারে ও মুরারে" (*murāre o murāre*, "O Murari" twice; in lahari 3, three times), and the word "ঘোষা" (ghosha) is present. There is no mangala-shloka at the beginning of the lahari: after the heading, the raga and the ghosha, the song begins with ॥ 1 ॥. Lahari 15 ends with its colophon on pdf 86; lahari 16 begins on a new page (pdf 87; the heading is printed "যোড়শ" for ষোড়শ). The title follows the colophon on pdf 89 (checked against the scan): "ইতি শ্রীরসিকমঙ্গল-পূর্ব্ববিভাগে শ্রীশ্যামানন্দ-মিলন-নাম ষোড়শ-লহরী সম্পূর্ণা" (*śrīśyāmānanda-milana*). This is the last lahari of the Purva-vibhaga.
[^2]: "The life of all [the world]" — *akhila-jīvana*. "So that I may describe" — *yena karihe varṇana*.
[^3]: Ghantashila — lahari 14, note 4; Rasika had settled there with his family (lahari 14, 7; lahari 15, 64–65). "Joyfully" — *kautuke*. In BR 15 (37) Rasika sees Shyamananda in a dream before their meeting.
[^4]: "Assembly" — *melā*, "gathering, meeting"; here the raja's audience hall (cf. 20). "Raja Vaikuntha" — *baikuṇṭharājā*: apparently the name of the raja of Ghantashila; the reading is tentative. "A raja [like the Lord] of Vaikuntha" is also possible. This episode is not in BR 15.
[^5]: "The assembly in the capital" — *rājadhānī sabhā*, i.e. at the raja's residence. "The foremost [knowers] of the Vedas" — *veda-vara*.
[^6]: "Inner meaning" — *tattvārtha*, "true meaning, essence".
[^7]: "Presented himself" — *upasana* (= *upasanna*, "having approached, presented himself"): clearly so on the scan.
[^8]: Arms reaching to the knees are one of the marks of a great personality (mahapurusha). "Unhurried like that of a king of elephants" — *gajendra-manthara-gati* (cf. lahari 14, note 9).
[^9]: "A second Narayana" expresses the onlookers' admiration, as elsewhere in the poem (lahari 12, note 43). According to Gaudiya Vaishnava teaching, Shyamananda is a pure devotee, a servant of Radha and a disciple of Hridaya Chaitanya, not an incarnation of Narayana (cf. lahari 1, note 2; lahari 14, note 11).
[^10]: "Has appeared" — *prakāśa haila*, "has revealed himself, become manifest". Prabhu here is Shyamananda.
[^11]: "Knows by sight" — *nāhi cine*, "does not recognize". In the "Rasika-mangala" Shyamananda comes to the raja's assembly and recognizes Rasika by his form; in BR 15 (37–44) Shyamananda first appears to Rasika in a dream and tells him that in the morning he will find him there, and at dawn Rasika sees Shyamananda coming along the road with his disciples and falls at his feet.
[^12]: "Soon" — *kṣaṇe*, literally "after a moment". "Palace" — *mandira*, "apartments, palace". "Each to his own place" — *yathā yā̃ra sthāna*. "Knowing for certain" — *jāniyā pramāṇa*.
[^13]: "In that assembly" — *se melāte*, i.e. in the same hall (note 4). "Jewel among men" — *puruṣa-ratana* (lahari 15, note 8).
[^14]: "This man" — *e puruṣa*; "this youth" — *e bālaka*, literally "boy". "Form" — *mūrti*.
[^15]: "Mallabhum" — *mallabhūma* = Mallabhumi (lahari 3, note 6): clearly so on the scan (cropped), distorted in the OCR. "Lord" — *adhipati*: Achyuta was so called before (lahari 3, 54; lahari 9, 38 — *adhikārī*).
[^16]: "Of [his] people, one by one" — *jane jane*, literally "of each person"; "about the worldly life of each [of his household]" is also possible. "Spoke of the highest goal" — *paramārtha-kathā*, a conversation on spiritual matters.
[^17]: "And of all the dwellers of Vraja" — *āra brajabāsī yate*; cf. lahari 15, 7–28: Krishna's command was conveyed to Shyamananda by Sri Jiva Gosvami.
[^18]: "Associate of Krishna" — *kṛṣṇa-pāriṣada*, an eternal associate; cf. lahari 15, 10 and note 6.
[^19]: "Be gracious [to me] in your heart" — *anugraha kara mane*. "Granted me the sight of you" — *daraśana dilā*, granted darshan (lahari 14, note 10).
[^20]: Chaturmasya (*cāturmāsya*) — the four months of the rainy season, when Vaishnavas and sannyasis do not travel but stay in one place. "Holding discussions" — *goṣṭhī kariyā*.
[^21]: "[Forgetting] food" — *anaśane*, lit. "fasting, without eating." "In seclusion" — *nigame*, as in lahari 10 (note 17), 12 (note 68) and 13; likewise in 39.
[^22]: Mimamsa, the yoga of Patanjali and Sankhya are philosophical systems (darshanas). "Sankhyayana" — *sāṅkhyāyana*: so in the scan and in the 1997 edition; what the author means is unclear (possibly the Vedic school of Shankhayana, or simply a pair echoing "Sankhya"). Svami is Shridhara Svami, the commentator on the Bhagavata (lahari 9, note 14).
[^23]: 41–42 list the ten avataras in the order of the Gita-govinda (canto 1), where Balarama stands in Krishna's place: Matsya (the Fish), Kurma, Varaha, Nrisimha, Vamana, Parashurama, Rama, Balarama, Buddha, Kalki. "Bala — the son of Rohini" — *bali rohiṇī-nandana*; it can also be read "called the son of Rohini." In 43 "true nature" — *svarūpa*. 41–81 are Shyamananda's speech; where it ends is not marked in the text (75 is the author's words).
[^24]: The source of the account of Narada (44–45) has not been identified. Madhurya is the sweet, conjugal mood of love for Krishna, as of the gopis.
[^25]: "Bhagavan Himself" — *svayaṁ bhagavān*, cf. Bhag. 1.3.28 (*kṛṣṇas tu bhagavān svayam*). The Shrutis are the personified Vedas; according to the Brihad-vamana Purana, wishing to serve Krishna as the gopis do, they took birth as gopis in Vraja (cf. Bhag. 10.87.23). The word "worship" is added from the sense.
[^26]: Cf. Brahma-samhita 5.29 and 5.56 (the ground made of chintamani, the water nectar, the trees wish-fulfilling trees). Chintamani is the wish-fulfilling gem. "It" — *yā̃hāra* (the respectful "whose [touch]"): possibly "by touching Him," i.e., Krishna.
[^27]: Cf. Brahma-samhita 5.56: *śriyaḥ kāntāḥ kāntaḥ parama-puruṣaḥ* — "the beloveds [there] are goddesses of fortune (Lakshmis), and the lover is the Supreme Person." The words "all the men are Vishnus" are the author's own formulation; apparently he speaks of the spiritual nature of the abode's inhabitants, not of each of them being the Lord Himself.
[^28]: Jati, yuthi, mallika and malati are kinds of jasmine. "Kera-kera" — *kerā kerā*: so in the scan and in the 1997 edition; meaning unclear (possibly *keyā*, ketaki, or "bunch upon bunch"). "Strings of jewels" — *ratna-jhārā*, lit. "showers (streams) of jewels."
[^29]: Gorochana is a yellow pigment used for tilaka. "Fine hairs" — *romāvalī*; apparently the curls above the forehead.
[^30]: "Shapely" — *susañca*: so in the scan (the 1997 edition has a garbled form of the same word); meaning unclear, rendered from the context. "Armlets" — *tāṛ* (lahari 5, note 9).
[^31]: The Triveni is the confluence of three rivers (the Ganga, Yamuna and Sarasvati) at Prayaga; three folds on the belly (*tribalī*) are a mark of beauty. "Waist" — *udara*, lit. "belly": a slender waist like a lion's is a usual comparison.
[^32]: The marks on Krishna's feet (68–70): on the right — the flag, the upward line, the lotus, the vajra (thunderbolt), the svastika, the wheel (*rathāṅga*), the umbrella, the goad (*aṅkuśa*); on the left — the cow's hoofprint (*gopada*), the sky (*ambara*), the pot, the conch, the fish, the rainbow (*indradhanu*; in other lists, the bow), the triangle, the jambu fruit, the half-moon. In the usual Vaishnava list of nineteen marks the jambu fruit is on the right foot, and so are the barleycorn and the octagon, which are absent here.
[^33]: The eight friends (*aṣṭa-sakhī*) are Radha's principal sakhis (Lalita, Vishakha and the others). "Group leaders" — *yūtheśvarī*; the author does not say who these four are.
[^34]: "Becoming prakriti" — *prakṛti haiyā*, i.e., taking on the feminine (subordinate, serving) mood of the gopis. According to Gaudiya Vaishnava teaching (Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati), this refers to the devotee's inner, spiritual identity — a maidservant of Radha under the guidance of the gopis — which is revealed by the mercy of guru and Krishna at the mature stage of pure bhakti; external imitation of a woman's appearance and behaviour (as among the sakhi-bhekis) is rejected by this teaching. Cf. Krishna as the one *puruṣa*, and all jivas as His energy (*prakṛti*).
[^35]: Line 79a apparently refers to chewed betel (*carvita*) with which a gopi feeds Krishna from her own mouth; 79b apparently to the stealing of the gopis' garments (Bhag. 10.22): Krishna carried their garments up a tree. The subject of the second line is not named; the reading is tentative.
[^36]: "Drenched" — the scan has *jaya jara*, apparently a misprint for *jara jara* (as in the 1997 edition; cf. lahari 14, 9).
[^37]: "Instruction" — *upadeśa*: here the initiation (diksha), told at the beginning of the Dakshina-vibhaga (lahari 1, 9–31).
[^38]: "Has brought to completion" — *kaila samādhāna*. The Purva-vibhaga is the first of the poem's four parts; it ends with lahari 16, and the Dakshina-vibhaga begins on the next page (pdf 90). The lahari's title follows the colophon on pdf 89 (checked against the scan): "শ্রীশ্যামানন্দ-মিলন-নাম ষোড়শ-লহরী" (*śrīśyāmānanda-milana*).
