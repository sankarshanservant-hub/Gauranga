# Dakshina-vibhaga. Lahari 5. The Deliverance of Bhima Shrikara

*A song in glory of Shyamananda; for four months Rasika stays in Rasamaya's house at Dharenda and holds sankirtana everywhere; Bhima Shirikara, the lord of Dharenda, rebukes Achyuta's young son for abandoning his studies and becoming a Vaishnava; Rasika proposes to summon the pandits and decide by the shastras which dharma is highest; in the debate he crushes the pride of all the brahmanas, expounding the shastras according to Svami, and Bhima with his brother and his whole family take shelter of him and give up the killing of living beings; Rasika instructs people of every caste, and Dharenda becomes like Vraja; with boys dressed as the maidens of Vraja and as Krishna he enacts Krishna's forest pastimes and returns to the village with music and sankirtana, and the people call him an incarnation*

*The fifth lahari.*

*Raga Narayani-gauda.*[^1]
*Ghosha: "My Krishna is a treasury of virtues,*
*the refuge of the helpless, the utmost limit of mercy."*

**1.** Glory, glory to Shyamananda! I bow to [his] holy feet:
may I sing [his] glory in sankirtana without cease.

**2.** Then Rasika-shekhara, in Rasamaya's house,
was absorbed in love for Krishna [and] in sankirtana.[^2]

**3.** For four months Rasika remained in that village;
he held sankirtana of love for Krishna in place after place.

**4.** From then on he gave his heart to the first jubilation:
he delivered king and subjects — the whole world.[^3]

**5.** To all living beings Rasikendra gave the shade of [his] feet;
I shall tell of it — listen attentively.

**6.** How he made Bhima Shirikara a Vaishnava —
I shall tell of it; listen, everyone.[^4]

**7.** One day Bhima Shirikara, having called an assembly,
was sitting within his own house.

**8.** There Rasika, taking his companions with him,
went and cheerfully greeted Bhima Shirikara.

**9.** Seeing that Rasikendra-chandra in the dress of a Vaishnava,
[Bhima] out of embarrassment said nothing, [but] grew blind with anger.

**10.** Unable to bear it, Bhima speaks reproachfully:
"What have you done, being Achyuta's son?[^5]

**11.** You are only twenty years old —
for what pleasure have you become a Vaishnava, child?[^6]

**12.** Who gave you such an idea — to give up your studies?
You ran away wearing rags, knowing of the quarrel [in your family].[^7]

**13.** On whose word have you become a Vaishnava at this age?
How will you support your family?

**14.** Achyuta is the lord of the land of Mallabhumi,
and in his line has been born such a worthless son!

**15.** You will wander from door to door begging alms;
because of you there will be shame on Achyuta's line.

**16.** It is good that I have met you, my boy:
go back and study at home.

**17.** All this talk of dhaturvada does not become you;
I say all this to you out of affection for Achyuta."[^8]

**18.** Hearing Bhima's words, so harsh,
Rasika, smiling, speaks sweet words:

**19.** "Listen, Bhima Shirikara, to my words:
all the reciters of the Puranas there are here with you —

**20.** call them all quickly into the assembly:
[let them say] which highest dharma the shastras proclaim.

**21.** The six shastras, the Vedas, the smritis, the Gita, the Bhagavata,
the views of Vyasa, Shuka, Janaka and [others], Narada and [others] —[^9]

**22.** by [all] the various shastras I will hold a discussion today:
which dharma is the highest by the reasoning of the shastras.[^10]

**23.** If all the shastras declare Krishna [the highest] established [truth],
then, giving up [all else], meditate on Krishna alone."[^11]

**24.** Bhima Shirikara had [good] tendencies from the past,
and besides, upon him was Rasika's great mercy.[^12]

**25.** Hearing, he said: "These words are the very essence,"
and had pandits of that realm brought, without number.

**26.** At Bhima's command all the brahmanas came —
knowers of all the shastras, devoted to the four Vedas.

**27.** Janaki Harichandana [and] all [the rest] came;
king, subjects, bhattacharyas — all entered.[^13]

**28.** All sat in a circle for the discussion;
Rasika-shekhara delights them all.[^14]

**29.** No one can overturn Rasika's exposition:
[it is] a discussion of the Vedas and shastras in accord with Vyasa.

**30.** Rasika crushed the pride of all;
no one could give [another] true meaning of the shastras.

**31.** A single shloka Rasika expounds in many ways;
he establishes the conclusion on the meaning of the words, [taking] Svami as the authority.[^15]

**32.** All the brahmanas were unable to answer;
without guile they said to Bhima Shirikara:

**33.** "What Rasika speaks is the word of Vyasa."
Everyone accepted Rasika's words [and abided by them].

**34.** Hearing [this] from their own mouths, Bhima Shrikara joyfully
took shelter, with [all] his family, of Sri Rasikananda.

**35.** Those two brothers took exclusive shelter;
all [of them] — and both [brothers] — began to worship Krishna's feet.[^16]

**36.** The killing of living beings and all [such things] they promptly gave up;
taking exclusive shelter, [they became] servants of Krishna.[^17]

**37.** Rasika gave instruction to all;
day by day [people] began to come in throng after throng.

**38.** Whether brahmana, shudra or outcaste —
receiving instruction, all gave their hearts to Krishna.[^18]

**39.** Rasika gave prema-bhakti to all;
by Rasika's touch all became pure in mind.

**40.** The town of Dharenda became like Vrajapura:
in every house there is abundant sankirtana.[^19]

**41.** All float in joy, in love for Krishna;
seeing that prema, everyone is amazed.

**42.** In that village Prabhu remained, caring [for the people];
the place is beautiful to see, most charming.

**43.** Seeing all the other people, too, full of love for Krishna,
he constantly performs the pastime of roaming the forest.

**44.** As formerly Krishna, taking all the boys,
roamed the forest in play, —[^20]

**45.** following that, Prabhu performs various pastimes:
from childhood he has played the games of Krishna's pastimes.[^21]

**46.** Gathering boys of youthful age,
he dresses each according to his looks.

**47.** Stringing various flowers, he brings [them] in various ways:
[the boys] are dressed just like the young maidens of Vraja.

**48.** Among them he makes someone Krishna;
the wondrous beauty of the sight cannot be described.[^22]

**49.** Clad in divine garments, with ankle-bells [and] belts of small bells —
thus Rasika dresses them himself.

**50.** He himself also puts on costume along with them all;
with dancing and singing they joyfully come back from the forest.

**51.** Vina, venu, rabab, mridanga, karatalas,
pakhavaj, dampha, bamshi, sweet-sounding mandiras;

**52.** some play the kapinasha, saranga, pinaka;
they tune the svara-mandala and various [other] instruments together.[^23]

**53.** The boys dance, striking various poses;
Tulasi [and] Rasamaya's family [sing] sankirtana together [with them].[^24]

**54.** From that day all these sport together [with him];
birth after birth all these are Rasika's servants.

**55.** As in Vraja Krishna comes home from the forest,
in that very way Rasika-shekhara performs his pastimes.

**56.** Lamps, torches, chandrodayas burn in plenty;
hundreds and hundreds of people come to see.[^25]

**57.** Seeing [this], all the people are amazed;
all say: "Rasikendra is a partial incarnation."[^26]

**58.** With various songs, various music, in the rasa of sankirtana,
in great bliss they enter the village.

**59.** Day after day thus performs various pastimes
Achyuta's son — in sankirtana in every house.[^27]

**60.** With a form of supreme sweetness he enchants the people of the world;
to all he speaks of Krishna constantly.

**61.** Hearing those words, all rejoice;
by the mere sight [of him] all become pure in heart.

**62.** Thus at Dharenda, having found great happiness,
he remained for some time, absorbed in prema.

**63.** All those joyful [pastimes] cannot be told;
in brief I have composed a little by Rasika's mercy.

**64.** All the pastimes Murari performed moment by moment —
not even with ten million mouths could I tell those pastimes.

**65.** So the little of [his] nature that I have described —
[it is] Achyuta's son who speaks it, dwelling [in my] heart.[^28]

**66.** Listen, whole world, to the Rasika-mangala:
singing [it] joyfully, cross over the terrible age of Kali.

**67.** Adorning himself with Shyamananda's feet,
the son of Rasamaya joyfully composed [this].

*Thus in the Dakshina-vibhaga of the Sri Rasika-mangala ends the fifth lahari, called "The Deliverance of Bhima Shrikara".*

[^1]: The lines before the raga are the heading of the lahari ("পঞ্চম-লহরী"), in italics without a number. Lahari 5 begins on a new page (pdf 103) after the colophon of lahari 4 on pdf 102. The raga in the scan is *nārānī gauṛā*, i.e. Narayani-gauda (Purva, lahari 7, note 1; lahari 10, note 1); the ghosha (refrain) is the same as there and in Purva, lahari 14, and its translation is repeated. There is no mangala-shloka: after the raga and the ghosha the song begins with ॥ 1 ॥. The title is from the colophon on pdf 105 (checked against the scan): "ভীম-শ্রীকর-উদ্ধার-নাম পঞ্চম-লহরী" (*bhīma-śrīkara-uddhāra*); within the lahari he is almost everywhere Bhima Shirikara (*śīrikara*; note 4).
[^2]: Rasamaya (Rasamayadasa) is the author's father ("the son of Rasamaya", 67); how Rasika came to his house at Dharenda and met Tulasi is told in lahari 4. "That village" (3) is Dharenda (40, 62), Shyamananda's native village in Utkala (Purva, lahari 1, note 9; Purva, lahari 2, note 6).
[^3]: "The first jubilation" — *prathama pramoda*: apparently the author's name for Rasika's first preaching journey — his coming to Dharenda, the first festival of his preaching (lahari 4, 69, note 26); the exact sense of the word here is not clear (cf. 28, note 14). "Delivered" — *uddhārila*: turned to bhakti.
[^4]: Bhima Shirikara (*bhīma śīrikara*) is the lord (*adhipati*) of Dharenda, of the gopa caste, formerly a persecutor of Vaishnavas (lahari 4, 23–33); of his daughter were born Rasamaya, Vamshi and Mathura (lahari 4, 34, note 14), so he is Rasamaya's maternal grandfather. Shirikara is apparently a surname (cf. Purva, lahari 1, note 39); in 34 and in the colophon it is Shrikara (*śrīkara*).
[^5]: "Reproachfully" — *dhikkāriyā*, "reviling, censuring". Achyuta is Rasika's father (Purva, lahari 3, note 5), lord of Mallabhumi (14). "Unable to bear it" — *sahite nā pāri*; the 1997 ed. reads *kahite nā pāri*.
[^6]: "Child" — *śiśubara*, lit. "best of children", mockingly. The age — *biṃśati baṯsara*, twenty years.
[^7]: "Rags" — *kānā* (cf. Oriya *kanā*, "rag, piece of cloth"); "wearing" — *pĩdhi* (Oriya *pindhi*). Cf. Dakshina, lahari 3: after the brothers' quarrel Rasika left home in nothing but the clothes he wore (pdf 99, 55). "You ran away" — *pālāile*; the 1997 ed. reads *bolāile*.
[^8]: "Talk of dhaturvada" — *dhāturbādya kathā* (crop ×2; 1997 ed. *dhātubādya*); reading and sense unclear. *Dhātuvāda* is "alchemy" (the art of transmuting metals), and possibly also "the doctrine of verbal roots", i.e. learned disputation; apparently Bhima mockingly so calls Rasika's theological talk.
[^9]: The six shastras are the six philosophical systems (darshanas; cf. Purva, lahari 16, note 22). Vyasa, Shuka, Janaka, Narada are authorities who know the true dharma; cf. the list of twelve mahajanas in the Bhagavata (6.3.20), which includes Narada, Janaka and Shuka (*vaiyāsaki*).
[^10]: "By the reasoning" — *yugate*, taken as *yukti-te*, "by reasoning"; in lahari 4, 11 the editor glosses the same word as "with diligence" (lahari 4, note 6) — then: "which dharma is highest, [examining] the shastras diligently".
[^11]: "Established" — *paramāṇa* (= *pramāṇa*), "proof, authority". The sense: if by all the shastras the highest truth is Krishna, give up everything and meditate on Him alone.
[^12]: "Tendencies from the past" — *pūrbera bāsanā*: impressions (pious merits) of past births. "And besides" — *āre*.
[^13]: Janaki Harichandana (*jānakī haricandana*) — apparently a local ruler or notable: Harichandana is an Oriya title of landholders (further on the poem mentions "the raja, the lord, Harichandana's brother"). It may also be read as two names — Janaki [and] Harichandana. "Bhattacharyas" — learned brahmanas.
[^14]: "Delights" — *pramoda kare* (so in the scan and in the 1997 ed.); it may also be understood as "prevails in the debate". Cf. note 3.
[^15]: Svami is Shridhara Svami, author of the commentary Bhavartha-dipika on the Bhagavata (Purva, lahari 9, note 14; Purva, lahari 16, note 22). "Expounds in many ways" — cf. CC, Madhya 24, where Sri Chaitanya explains the single *ātmārāma* verse in sixty-one ways.
[^16]: "Those two brothers" — *yei dui bhāi*: Bhima Shirikara and apparently his brother (lahari 4, 24 and 33, notes 9 and 13; the brother's name is not given). "Exclusive shelter" — *ananya śaraṇa*.
[^17]: "The killing of living beings" — *jībahatyā*: the sacrifices of goats and buffaloes to various gods and goddesses (lahari 4, 25–26, 31, notes 10 and 12).
[^18]: "Outcaste" — *antyaja*, "last-born", outside the castes. Cf. CC, Madhya 8.128: whether a brahmana, a sannyasi or a shudra — whoever knows the science of Krishna is a guru.
[^19]: Vrajapura is Vraja, Krishna's abode.
[^20]: "Roaming the forest" — *bana-behārana* (= *bana-bihāra*): the pastimes of Krishna and His cowherd friends in the forests of Vrindavana (Bhagavata 10.11–12 and elsewhere).
[^21]: "Following that" — *sei anveṣaṇe*, lit. "in pursuit of that"; the editor's note gives the variant *anukrame*, "in order, following [that]" (the editor's note is not translated). "From childhood" — cf. the Purva-vibhaga, where the boy Rasika plays at Krishna's pastimes.
[^22]: With the boys Rasika enacts the pastimes of Krishna and the maidens of Vraja as a sacred performance (cf. the drama Sri Chaitanya staged in Chandrashekhara's house — CB, Madhya 18). According to Gaudiya Vaishnava teaching this is a glorification of the Lord's pastimes for the pleasure of the devotees, not an imitation (*anukaraṇa*) of Krishna.
[^23]: Rabab — a bowed or plucked string instrument; pakhavaj — a double-headed drum; dampha (*ḍampha*) — a frame drum; bamshi — a bamboo flute (like the venu); mandiras — small cymbals; kapinasha (*kapināśa*) — a kind of vina; saranga — the sarangi, a bowed instrument; pinaka — a bowed string instrument; svara-mandala — a kind of zither. "Sweet-sounding" — *rasāla*.
[^24]: Tulasi (Tulasidasa) is a singer, the son of Thakura Gopaladasa, a disciple of Hridayananda; Rasika met him in Rasamaya's house and took him into his company together with Rasamaya's family (lahari 4, 53–68, notes 20 and 24). "Rasamaya's family" — *rasamaẏa goṣṭhī*.
[^25]: Deuti (*deuṭī*) — oil lamps on stands; mashal — torches; chandrodaya (*candrodaya*) — apparently a large lamp with many wicks (or fireworks).
[^26]: "A partial incarnation" — *aṃśa abatāra*: the rapturous words of the onlookers. According to Gaudiya Vaishnava teaching Rasikananda is a devotee and associate, not an incarnation of the Lord (cf. Purva, lahari 1, note 69); his guru Shyamananda is a servant of Radha and Krishna (Purva, lahari 1, note 2; Purva, lahari 14, note 11).
[^27]: "Achyuta's son" — *acyutera bālā* (cf. Purva, lahari 7, note 22).
[^28]: "[His] nature" — *sbabhāba*; the 1997 ed. reads *prabhāba*, "[his] power, glory". "Achyuta's son speaks it, dwelling [in my] heart" — the author ascribes his inspiration to Rasikananda.
