# Pashchima-vibhaga. Lahari 10. The Guru-Devotion and Sadhu-Service of the Elephant Gopala Dasa

*A song in glory of Shyamananda; after some days in Banapur, Rasika sets out to see Shyamananda, but the ruler of Hijli carries off his disciples, the singers Shyamadasa and Mohana; Rasika refuses food and returns to Banapur, and the rajas secure their release; the elephant Gopala Dasa comes to Rasika, hears Krishna-katha and goes off to wander among the holy places; when Rasika and his companions lose their way in the forest and are left without food, the elephant brings them rice from a householder; Rasika instructs him to serve the sadhus, and the elephant becomes a great Vaishnava, while Rasika's touch purifies people of every class; a description of Rasika's form; at night Jagannatha commands Rasika to manifest Him as Sri Govinda, and Rasika meets two brother craftsmen from Nilachala, Raghunatha and Ananda; convinced of Jagannatha's command, he longs in bliss to see Govinda*

*The tenth lahari.*

*Raga Dhanashri.*[^1]
*Ghosha: "Blessed, blessed is the glory of Gopala!"*

**1.** Glory, glory to Shyamananda, the friend of all,
the deliverer of the poor and lowly, the ocean of compassion![^2]

**2.** Having stayed some days in Banapur,
Rasikendra set out to see Shyamananda.

**3.** Having seen Jagannatha, he goes along the roads
together with his disciples — Shyamadasa, Mohana and others.[^3]

**4.** Formerly Shyamadasa [and] Mohana had been with the ruler of Hijli:
[they were] excellent singers.

**5.** On the road the ruler of Hijli saw them
and, turning both back, took them away with him.[^4]

**6.** Going back, [he] arrived at Banapur;
where [Rasika was, there came] a companion [and] reported at [his] feet.[^5]

**7.** Hearing [this], Rasika took no more food;
Prabhu turned back and went to Banapur.

**8.** Seeing [him], all the rajas rejoiced;
[but,] hearing of the fast, all were alarmed.

**9.** By many means [they] obtained the release of the two disciples
and brought both brothers to Rasika.[^6]

**10.** Having got both back, Prabhu was delighted;
finishing his meal and so on, he stayed there that day.

**11.** That night he spent in bliss in sankirtana;
the elephant Sri Gopala Dasa heard [of it] with his own ears.[^7]

**12.** Rasikendra again set out on his way;
[the elephant] resolved in his mind: "I shall see Prabhu."[^8]

**13.** The maddened best of elephants came, leaving the forest;
slowly, slowly he went, full of eagerness.

**14.** Prabhu, sitting in seclusion, is chanting the Hari-nama;
the maddened elephant came [and] entered that place.

**15.** Seeing Rasika, the elephant fell at [his] feet;
with his tears he washed both [his] feet.

**16.** Again prostrating like a staff, again circling [him],
again and again that maddened elephant gazes [at him].[^9]

**17.** Rasika spoke Krishna-katha [to him] in many ways;
he explained [to him] the essence of the shastras — the "Bhagavata" [and] the "Gita".[^10]

**18.** Hearing [this], the elephant gained divine knowledge:
he knew for certain that all is false, Krishna is the truth.

**19.** In tears [and] thrilling [at] Rasika's feet,
taking his leave, the elephant went into the forest.

**20.** The maddened best of elephants set off to wander among the holy places;
[his] body and heart were overwhelmed with the bliss of Krishna.[^11]

**21.** Going some distance ahead, he stayed in the forest,
in meditation on Rasika's feet, in [their] shelter [and] in bhajana.

**22.** At that time Rasikendra went on from there;
in the forest evening fell, [and they] lost their way.

**23.** All around there are no villages — a vast wasteland;
they stopped beneath a tree in the forest.[^12]

**24.** He remains without food together with many Vaishnavas;
to that place the elephant came in great joy.

**25.** He saw that all had lain down to sleep without food;
at once the elephant went off with the speed of the wind.

**26.** In his mind he knows where what provisions are to be found;
he came to a certain householder.

**27.** From his house he brought a bundle of rice;
the other provisions were with Rasika.

**28.** Knowing there was no rice, the best of elephants took [it]
[and] came to Rasika's feet.[^13]

**29.** Tired from the road, everyone was asleep;
the elephant bows at Rasika's feet.

**30.** Seeing the elephant, Rasikendra rose;
alarmed in his mind, he remembered Krishna.

**31.** Leaving sleep, all meditate on Krishna in their hearts:
"Today the elephant will take everyone's life!"[^14]

**32.** Knowing everyone's thoughts, the maddened best of elephants
placed the bundle of rice before Rasika.

**33.** He himself, going into the forest, stayed some distance away;
seeing the rice, Rasika-shekhara says —

**34.** he gave the command to the brahmanas to cook:
"All the sadhus are sorely tormented by hunger."[^15]

**35.** Hearing the command, the companions quickly set to [work];
cooking [the food], they feed the sadhus.

**36.** Afterwards Prabhu Rasika-shekhara sat [to eat];
to that place came the maddened best of elephants.[^16]

**37.** Seeing for himself the sadhus eating,
he fell to the ground, his body stretched out like a staff.

**38.** Circling [him], the fortunate king of elephants,
gazing at Rasika, meditates [on him].[^17]

**39.** He has no wish to go away [from him];
his whole body thrills, his eyes are full of tears.

**40.** Rasika was delighted seeing the elephant;
placing his hand on his head, he speaks, praising [him]:

**41.** "With firm faith serve the sadhus constantly;
wander from holy place to holy place.

**42.** Never do harm to the sadhus;
always serve the Vaishnavas [and] brahmanas."

**43.** [Thus] he told the maddened best of elephants the whole of dharma;
from then on the elephant always roams from land to land.[^18]

**44.** The story of Gopala Dasa cannot be told:
the mahashaya became prema-bhakti embodied.[^19]

**45.** Constantly he remembers the name of Krishna;
happy at heart, he wanders from forest to forest.

**46.** A place of Krishna, a place of a brahmana, a place of a Vaishnava —
he circles them from afar and [so] goes [on].[^20]

**47.** If good people are staying in the dense forest,
the elephant brings [and] gives [them] all provisions in that place.[^21]

**48.** Thus he constantly serves the sadhus;
the maddened best of elephants became a great Vaishnava.

**49.** When he comes into someone's field —
whoever [there] speaks, uttering the name of Rasika,

**50.** bowing to him, he leaves that place.
Even with a hundred mouths the elephant's qualities cannot be described.[^22]

**51.** By the power of the feet of such [a] Rasika
the elephant gladly serves hundreds and hundreds of sadhus.

**52.** Hunas, Pulindas, mlecchas, various low castes —
by Rasika's touch all became pure in mind.[^23]

**53.** Seeing [him], all gave up their [former] nature;
rajas [and] subjects became great Vaishnavas.[^24]

**54.** All the lands overflowed with love for Krishna;
by Rasika's touch all became wholly surrendered.

**55.** There is nothing to compare with Rasika's greatness:
he possesses all virtues, [he is] the splendour of beauty.

**56.** There is no one equal to him in the world;
[he is] the friend, refuge [and] brother of the poor, the lowly, the suffering.[^25]

**57.** Rasika-shekhara, whose emblem is love for the devotees,
[is] a great ocean of mercy, the son of Achyuta.[^26]

**58.** How [wondrous] that sweet smile, those soft, soft words!
How [wondrous] that glance of eyes [like] lotus petals![^27]

**59.** How [wondrous] that unhurried gait, the gait of a king of elephants!
How [wondrous] that enchanting form that enchants the three worlds!

**60.** How [wondrous] the swaying of the arms, surpassing an elephant's trunk!
How [wondrous] those exceedingly tender two feet!

**61.** How [wondrous] the beautiful book in that tender hand!
How [wondrous] the remembrance of Krishna's name on those lips![^28]

**62.** How [wondrous] that sweet form, enchanting the world!
That face, once seen, steals the minds of the three worlds.

**63.** How [wondrous] those streams from the eyes that flow ceaselessly!
How [wondrous] that thrill in the body — beyond words!

**64.** How [wondrous] the radiance of that body — it enchants the people of the world!
How [wondrous] the colour of those lips, [how] the rows of teeth shine!

**65.** How [wondrous] that high forehead adorned with tilaka!
How [wondrous] that nose, [how] beautiful the conch-like neck![^29]

**66.** How [wondrous] that broad, broad chest!
How [wondrous] the charming garment shining on that waist!

**67.** How [wondrous] that dark body — it enchants the people of the world!
Hearing those sweet words, the heart rejoices.[^30]

**68.** Hundreds and hundreds of people come and look [at him];
no one wishes to go away.

**69.** In all the lands he manifested prema-bhakti;
[people,] becoming wholly surrendered, turned their minds to Krishna.

**70.** At that time Rasikendra is coming along the road;
at night Jagannatha unexpectedly gave [him] a command:

**71.** "Manifest Me there —
[as] Sri Govinda-raya, the graceful form bent in three places.

**72.** In His heart I shall constantly sport;
the three worlds will worship My feet.

**73.** As in Nilachala everyone serves [Me],
such faith [in Me] will there be in that place of yours."[^31]

**74.** Hearing these words with his own ears,
Rasika told everyone of Krishna's command, heard with his ears.[^32]

**75.** At that time the craftsmen Raghunatha [and] Ananda
unexpectedly met with Rasika.

**76.** They are two brothers born of one mother, residents of Nilachala,
[like] Vishvakarma, skilled in the shilpa-shastra.[^33]

**77.** Seeing [them], the son of Achyuta was delighted;
taking both brothers with him, he went on his way.[^34]

**78.** In his mind he understood for certain the command of Jagabandhu:
"The Friend of the three worlds will surely become manifest."[^35]

**79.** Rasikendra-chandra floats in an ocean of bliss:
"When will such good fortune come — that I shall see Govinda?"[^36]

**80.** The account of Rasikachandra cannot be told:
the world honoured [him] as equal to Narayana.[^37]

**81.** The blinding darkness of sin was destroyed on earth:
Rasikendra-chandra shone forth in Utkala.[^38]

**82.** The *Rasika-mangala* is a song of Rasika's virtues;
hearing it, destroy the pain of the bonds of worldly existence.[^39]

**83.** Adorning himself with Shyamananda's feet,
the son of Rasamaya joyfully composed [this].

*Thus in the Pashchima-vibhaga of the Sri Rasika-mangala ends the tenth lahari, called "The Guru-Devotion and Sadhu-Service of the Elephant Gopala Dasa."*[^40]

[^1]: The lines before the raga are the lahari's heading ("দশম-লহরী"), in italics without a number. Lahari 10 begins on the lower half of pdf 154 (printed p. 114), under a rule after the colophon of lahari 9 (files RM-3-09-059-080). Raga Dhanashri (*dhānaśrī*; Dakshina, lahari 12, note 1). The ghosha is *dhanya dhanya gopālera yaśa nāre*; Gopala is apparently the elephant Gopala Dasa, the hero of this lahari (lahari 8, note 34); it may also be taken as Krishna. *nāre* is unclear: possibly a sung particle (*nā re*; cf. *mana mohanā re* in Dakshina, lahari 12) or "cannot" (*nāre* = *nā pāre*: "Gopala's glory cannot [be told]"). There is no mangala-shloka: after the raga and ghosha the song begins with ॥ 1 ॥. The title follows the colophon on pdf 157 (printed p. 117), checked against the scan: "ইতি শ্রীরসিকমঙ্গল-পশ্চিম-বিভাগে গোপালদাস-হস্তীর গুরুভক্তি ও সাধুসেবা-নাম দশম-লহরী সম্পূর্ণা" (*gopāladāsa-hastīra gurubhakti o sādhusebā*) — "The Guru-Devotion and Sadhu-Service of the Elephant Gopala Dasa". Lahari 10 runs 1–83 (pdf 154–157). The summary line covers the whole lahari (78–83 from the scan of pdf 157, provisionally).
[^2]: "The friend of all" — *akhilera bandhu*. "The deliverer of the poor and lowly" — *dīnahīna-tāraṇa*.
[^3]: "Set out" — *bijaẏa karilā*, said respectfully of a holy man's departure. "Having seen Jagannatha" — apparently in Puri (Nilachala); the author says nothing more about this. Shyamadasa (*śyāmadāsa*) and Mohana (*mohana*) are disciples of Rasika; not identified. "Along the roads" — *pathe pathe*.
[^4]: "Had been with the ruler of Hijli" — *chilā hijalī-adhipati sthāne*: apparently they had served as singers at his court. On Hijli see Purva, lahari 10, note 21; the ruler is not named here. "Excellent singers" — *baṛa sugāẏana*. "Turning back" — *phirāiẏā*: making them turn round. Rasika's dealings with rulers are as the author tells them; this episode is not mentioned in BR 15.
[^5]: The subject of the first line is not expressed; taken as the ruler of Hijli, returning with the singers to Banapur (where the rajas of Orissa were kept with the subahdar; Pashchima, lahari 7, note 21). It may also be read "[they] arrived". "Where [Rasika was]" — *yathāsthāne*, "at the place". "A companion" — *anucara*: one of Rasika's companions.
[^6]: 7: "Took no more food" — *āra nā kaila bhojana*: Rasika fasts until his disciples are returned. "Were alarmed" — *tarāsa* (*trāsa*). "Obtained the release" — *ānila chāṛāñā*, "brought, having freed". "By many means" — *bahurūpe*. "Brothers" — *bhāi*: apparently spiritual brothers (disciples of one guru).
[^7]: Gopala Dasa is the elephant initiated by Rasika (lahari 8, note 34); the author speaks of him in the respectful form (*śunilena*, "heard"). "Heard with his own ears" — *śunilena karṇe*: the elephant heard the sankirtana.
[^8]: The subject of the second line is not expressed; taken as the elephant (cf. 13).
[^9]: "Prostrating like a staff" — *daṇḍabata*: lying full length. "Circling" — *parikramā*, in token of reverence (lahari 8, note 32).
[^10]: The "Bhagavata" and the "Gita" — the "Srimad-Bhagavatam" and the "Bhagavad-gita". "The essence of the shastras" — *śāstratattva*.
[^11]: "To wander among the holy places" — *tīrtha-paryaṭane*. "Overwhelmed" — *jara jara*.
[^12]: "A vast wasteland" — *baṛa tepāntara*: an uninhabited plain. The subject of 22–24 is Rasika and his companions; "he" in 24 is Rasika.
[^13]: The elephant bringing rice to the hungry Vaishnavas is a miracle told by the author; cf. BR 15, 84: Rasikananda "engaged [the elephant] in the service of Krishna and the Vaishnavas" (Pashchima, lahari 7, note 19). "A bundle of rice" — *taṇḍulera puṛā*: a parcel (usually of straw or leaves) of husked rice. "The other provisions" — *upadrabya*. "Took" — *naila* (so the scan; apparently *laila*, "took"). "Came" — *uttarila*, "arrived".
[^14]: "Alarmed" — *saṅkucita haiẏā*, lit. "shrinking [with fear]". "Remembered" — *saṅariẏā*. 31: the companions' thought: this is the same elephant that had once ravaged Banapur (laharis 7–8).
[^15]: "The brahmanas" — *dbijagaṇe*: the brahmanas among the companions, who cook the food. Lines 33–34 form one sentence.
[^16]: "Afterwards" — *paścāte*: after the sadhus had eaten.
[^17]: "King of elephants" — *gajendra*; "fortunate" — *bhāgyabān*.
[^18]: "The whole of dharma" — *sarba dharma*: all that it befits him to do. "Roams from land to land" — *phire deśāntare*.
[^19]: "Embodied" — *mūrtimanta*. "Mahashaya" — a respectful form of address; here, of the elephant.
[^20]: "A place of Krishna" — a temple; "a place of a brahmana, a place of a Vaishnava" — their dwellings; "circles them from afar" — *dūra haite parikramā kari'*: in token of reverence, without coming near. "Goes" — *yāna*, respectful form.
[^21]: "Good people" — *sujana*: sadhus. "All provisions" — *sarbadrabya*.
[^22]: "Into a field" — *kṛṣite*: wild elephants commonly ravage crops. 49–50: if anyone utters Rasika's name, the elephant bows and leaves the field.
[^23]: Hunas, Pulindas, mlecchas, low castes — *hūna se pulinda mleccha nānā antyajāti* (Pashchima, lahari 6, note 11; Dakshina, lahari 4, note 4). "By the touch" — *paraśe* (*sparśe*) (likewise 54).
[^24]: "Seeing [him]" — *darśane*, "at the sight". "Their [former] nature" — *āpanāra sbabhāba*.
[^25]: "The splendour of beauty" (55) — *lābaṇya-garimā*. "Equal" — *paṭāntara*, lit. "difference", i.e. one who could compare with him. "Brother" — *sodara*, "brother born of the same mother".
[^26]: "Whose emblem is love for the devotees" — *bhakata-batsala-bānā*: *bānā* — "sign, vow, garb"; "loving to His devotees" (*bhakta-vatsala*) is an epithet of the Lord, here applied to Rasika. "The son of Achyuta" — *acyuta-kumāra*: Rasika.
[^27]: 58–67 describe Rasika's form; *kibā se* is an exclamation: "how [wondrous] that…". Such descriptions are usual for the form of Krishna and of Sri Chaitanya; the author transfers them to Rasika (cf. note 26).
[^28]: "Book" — *grantha*: apparently the "Bhagavata" (cf. 17). "Remembrance" — *saṅaraṇa* (*smaraṇa*): Krishna's name on his lips.
[^29]: "Conch-like neck" — *kambukaṇṭha*: a neck with three folds like the whorls of a conch, a mark of beauty.
[^30]: "Dark" — *śyāmala*: dark like a cloud, the colour of Krishna.
[^31]: Jagannatha's command is as the author tells it; according to BR 15, 81, it was Shyamananda who entrusted Rasika with the service of Sri Govinda in Gopivallabhapur; in the next lahari (11) Shyamananda likewise gives Govinda His name and orders the Deity to be made. "At night" — *rātre*: apparently in a dream. "Manifest Me" — *āmāra prakāśa tumi karaha*: install My Deity. "There" — *tathāẏa*: in Gopivallabhapur (cf. 73: "in that place of yours"). Sri Govinda-raya is the Deity of Gopivallabhapur (BR 15, 81); *tribhaṅga*, "bent in three places," is the form of Krishna with the flute; *lalita*, "graceful". "In His heart" — *tā'ra hṛde*: apparently in the heart of [the Deity] Govinda; Jagannatha promises to dwell in Him (according to Vaishnava teaching the Deity is the Lord Himself, the archa-avatara). It may also be taken as "in his heart" — Rasika's. "Sport" — *biharibe*. Nilachala is Puri.
[^32]: "Krishna's command" — *kṛṣṇa-ājñā*: Jagannatha is Krishna.
[^33]: Raghunatha and Ananda (*raghunātha ānanda*; in lahari 11, 7 — *raghu ānanda*) are master craftsmen who carve Deities; not identified. "Craftsmen" — *kāmilā* (so the scan): apparently from Arabic-Persian *kāmil*, "perfect, skilled"; the sense is unclear. Vishvakarma is the architect of the gods; by tradition he carved Jagannatha. The shilpa-shastra is the science of sculpture and architecture, including the making of Deities.
[^34]: "The son of Achyuta" — *acyutanandana*: Rasika. pdf 156 ends with a complete ॥ 77 ॥; the lahari continues (78–83, colophon) on pdf 157.
[^35]: The subject is Rasika (77: "the son of Achyuta"). "For certain" — *niśce* (*niścaẏa*). Jagabandhu (*jagabandhu*, "friend of the world") is Jagannatha (Purva, lahari 2, note 28), who commanded Rasika to manifest the Deity of Govinda (70–73). "The Friend of the three worlds" — *trailokyera bandhu*: Sri Govinda, who is to become manifest in Gopivallabhapur (lahari 11).
[^36]: "Such good fortune" — *hena bhāgya*.
[^37]: "As equal to Narayana" — *nārāẏaṇa sama*: the author's words of rapture; according to Gaudiya Vaishnava teaching Rasika is a devotee, a disciple of Shyamananda, not an incarnation (Pashchima, lahari 9, note 4; Purva, lahari 1, note 2; Purva, lahari 14, note 11).
[^38]: "The blinding darkness of sin" — *pāpa-timirāndha*. "Shone forth" — *prakāśila*, "appeared, became manifest". Utkala — *utakala*.
[^39]: "A song of virtues" — *guṇagāthā*. "Destroy" — *dhbaṃsana kara*; "the pain of the bonds of worldly existence" — *bhababandha-byathā*.
[^40]: Colophon on pdf 157 (printed p. 117), checked against the scan: "ইতি শ্রীরসিকমঙ্গল-পশ্চিম-বিভাগে গোপালদাস-হস্তীর গুরুভক্তি ও সাধুসেবা-নাম দশম-লহরী সম্পূর্ণা" (note 1). Lahari 10 comprises 1–83 (pdf 154–157). Lower on the same page, below a rule, lahari 11 begins (files RM-3-11-001-020).
