# Dakshina-vibhaga. Lahari 16. The Deliverance of Uddanda

*A song in glory of Shyamananda; through Rasika's instruction the three brothers become wholly devoted, and in Bhanjabhum all the people, Shaivas and Shaktas, give up the killing of living beings; when during a reading of the Bhagavata the raja is distracted by his steward, the brahmana Ramakrishna, Rasika's disciple, slaps him; the raja restrains the enraged courtiers, falls at Ramakrishna's feet and thanks him for his deliverance, and Rasika is convinced that the raja has become a Vaishnava; taking leave of the raja, Rasika returns to Shyamananda at Govindapur; Shyamananda goes with Rasika to Nrisimhapur, to the bhuiyan Uddanda-raya, who robs and beats Vaishnavas; at night a mahapurusha appears to Uddanda and bids him take shelter of Shyamananda, and, seeing Shyamananda, he takes his shelter with his whole family; Shyamananda has Shyama-raya brought from Dharenda and holds three festivals; Uddanda serves the sadhus, repents of having destroyed thousands of sadhus and hands over the gudris taken from the Vaishnavas — seven hundred and eighteen — and Shyamananda distributes them to the Vaishnavas; seeing him, everyone becomes a sadhu; thus the Dakshina-vibhaga ends*

*The sixteenth lahari.*

*Raga Narayani-gauda.*[^1]
*Ghosha: "My Krishna is a treasury of virtues,*
*the refuge of the helpless, the utmost limit of mercy."*

**1.** Glory, glory to Shyamananda, the life of the whole [world]!
Be merciful, so that I may compose [a song of your] glory.

**2.** Thus Vaidyanatha received instruction;
day by day prema-bhakti began to grow.[^2]

**3.** The three brothers became wholly devoted;
the towns of Utkala became filled with love for Krishna.[^3]

**4.** Rasika accepted many disciples there;
for some days he stayed in that village of the raja.

**5.** The three brothers became wondrous Vaishnavas;
there is none to compare [them] with in all the world.[^4]

**6.** In Bhanjabhum all the people became Vaishnavas;
Shaivas [and] Shaktas — all gave up the killing of living beings.[^5]

**7.** One day Rasika-shekhara, having gathered an assembly,
was sitting in the raja's chambers.[^6]

**8.** The three brothers listen to the narration of the Bhagavata;
with joy in his heart Rasika Gosani speaks.

**9.** At that time the raja's steward [came] to that place
and, making obeisance with an offering, stood before [him].[^7]

**10.** As soon as [he] made a sign, [the raja] looked at him;
in anger Ramakrishna, the best of brahmanas, rose.[^8]

**11.** He is Rasika's disciple, wholly surrendered [to Krishna];
all people praise him, calling him the blessing of the world.[^9]

**12.** Rising, he said: "Raja, you have lost your senses!
Leaving the nectarean words about Krishna, you are listening to [other] talk."[^10]

**13.** With force he struck the raja on the face with a slap;
the raja fell senseless, everyone was grieved.

**14.** All the great men, seized with anger,
drawing their swords, rush roaring to kill [him].[^11]

**15.** Seeing this, the raja in agitation rose quickly
and fell at the feet of Ramakrishnadasa.

**16.** Joining his palms, he says before the assembly:
"For [my] offence the punishment was in due measure.[^12]

**17.** Rasika speaks the nectarean words about Krishna,
and I myself, leaving them, looked the other way.

**18.** In all the shastras it is said: the worship of Krishna is true;
everything apart from Krishna is the eating of poison.

**19.** Whoever, beside the narration about Krishna, listens to other talk
is a great sinner; he falls into dense darkness.

**20.** Know: his mind is like a dog's [or] a hog's —
leaving the rice on a jewelled plate, to eat leavings.[^13]

**21.** Leaving nectar, he partook of poison;
leaving the narration about Krishna, he turned his mind elsewhere.[^14]

**22.** This punishment is just, in measure with the offence;
today brother Ramakrishna has delivered [me].

**23.** I have understood in my heart: [he] bears me great love;
he has delivered me from the eighty-four [lakhs of births]."[^15]

**24.** Embracing Ramakrishna by the neck, [the raja] began to weep;
with the tears of his eyes the raja bathed [his] body.[^16]

**25.** Seeing [this], all in the assembly were amazed:
the saintliness of Vaidyanatha is wondrous to tell.[^17]

**26.** Taking Ramakrishna by the hand, the raja says playfully:
"Your hand has been hurt by this hard body."

**27.** With a rebuke he restrained all the people,
took Ramakrishnadasa and seated him beside [himself].[^18]

**28.** Without guile he showed [him] much affection;
Rasika knew for certain: [the raja] had become a Vaishnava.

**29.** Rising, Rasika Gosani embraced the raja [and] the three brothers,
without guile he showed [them] mercy.[^19]

**30.** Such sadhus — Vaidyanatha [and] the three brothers —
unceasingly worship Rasikendra-chudamani.[^20]

**31.** Lying down, eating, in sleep, night and day [theirs is] meditation:
apart from Rasika's feet they know nothing else.

**32.** Seeing the raja's prema-bhakti, all the people,
following one another's example, became wholly surrendered [to Krishna].

**33.** Day by day bhakti was kindled;
Achyuta's son showed mercy to all.

**34.** Then, having stayed there for some days,
[Rasika], telling the raja, went to Shyamananda.

**35.** Because of the separation the raja became very sad;
giving much wealth [and] cloth, he fell at [his] feet.

**36.** From there Rasikendra set out
and at Govindapur saw Shyamananda.[^21]

**37.** In Utkala Yamuna Thakurani was born;
Rasikendra brought her [and] gave [her] to Shyamananda.[^22]

**38.** All the wealth [and] cloth he gave to Shyamananda;
at [his] feet he told of the raja's instruction.[^23]

**39.** Hearing [this], Shyamananda-raya rejoiced:
"There is one more matter; I shall tell you.

**40.** The bhuiyan of Nrisimhapur, that Uddanda-raya,
is always hostile to Vaishnavas [and] brahmanas.[^24]

**41.** Hundreds and hundreds of gudris he takes away;
greedy for goods, intoxicated, he beats the Vaishnavas.[^25]

**42.** If such a man becomes a sadhu, it will be good;
let us go to him, you and I."

**43.** Saying this, Shyamananda set out;
coming to Narasimhapur, he arrived [there].[^26]

**44.** That night Raja Uddanda was lying [in bed]:
he had only just lain down and was still awake.[^27]

**45.** At that time a certain great, supreme mahapurusha
came and appeared before the bhuiyan.[^28]

**46.** In a soft, sweet voice he spoke [to him] face to face:
"Take shelter of Shyamananda with a firm heart.[^29]

**47.** Surrender everything to Shyamananda."
Having spoken these words to him, he vanished.

**48.** [Uddanda] himself saw the form, heard the words;
rising, he saw that no one was there.

**49.** Having received this vision, he gained divine knowledge:
"When shall I behold the feet of Shyamananda?"[^30]

**50.** At that time Prabhu came there;
the two Prabhus arrived at Uddanda's house.[^31]

**51.** Shyamananda, taking Rasika-Murari with him,
came and entered into Uddanda-raya's presence.

**52.** Seeing the two, the raja rejoiced greatly:
the Prabhu whose words he had heard with his ears [stood] before him.[^32]

**53.** In many ways he worshipped [their] feet;
firmly he took shelter of Shyamananda.

**54.** Without guile [Shyamananda] bestowed prema-bhakti on him;
the fortunate bhuiyan took shelter with his whole family.[^33]

**55.** Very mighty, a great asura he had been;
by Shyamananda's touch he became a supreme sadhu.[^34]

**56.** Seeing [this], all the people are amazed:
apart from guru, Krishna and sadhu they know nothing else.[^35]

**57.** He had Shyama-raya brought from Dharenda;
Shyamananda-raya held three festivals.[^36]

**58.** From that day [Uddanda] became a great Vaishnava;
he began to serve sadhus by the hundreds and hundreds.

**59.** Having completed the dadhi-kada, Shyamananda sat down;
the bhuiyan, with joy in his heart, makes a humble request:[^37]

**60.** "I am a great villain, a great sinner, of evil conduct:
thousands upon thousands of sadhus I have destroyed.[^38]

**61.** A whole room is filled with their gudris;
if you order it, I will bring [them] before you."[^39]

**62.** Hearing [this], Shyamananda at once gave the order;
the gudris were brought and piled up like a mountain.[^40]

**63.** On counting, they came to seven hundred and eighteen;
seeing [this], all the devotees of Krishna are amazed.[^41]

**64.** Then Prabhu gave [them] one by one to the Vaishnavas;
receiving the gudris, all give [him] their blessings.[^42]

**65.** Many clothes, much wealth he gave to the sadhus;
firmly he took shelter of Shyamananda.

**66.** Following his example, all the people became sadhus;
of Uddanda's saintliness nothing can be told.[^43]

**67.** Such is the might of Shyamananda [and] Rasika,
by whose touch the three sins of worldly existence are destroyed.[^44]

**68.** Thus, day by day, prema dawns:
giving up wicked deeds, all worship Krishna.

**69.** All the greatest sinners, giving up wicked deeds,
became supreme Vaishnavas, wholly surrendered [to Krishna].[^45]

**70.** Even if described with millions of mouths, it could not be described;
of [his] nature I have briefly composed something.[^46]

**71.** In the Dakshina-vibhaga I have revealed this;
listen to the Rasika-mangala, filled with delight.

**72.** Adorning himself with Shyamananda's feet,
the son of Rasamaya joyfully composed [this].

*Thus in the Dakshina-vibhaga of the Sri Rasika-mangala ends the sixteenth lahari, called "The Deliverance of Uddanda."*[^47]

[^1]: The lines before the raga are the heading of the lahari ("ষোড়শ-লহরী"; the scan has "যোড়শ"), in italics without a number. Lahari 16 begins on pdf 128 (printed p. 90) after the colophon of lahari 15 (files RM-2-15-094-135). The raga in the scan is *nārāni gauṛā*, i.e. Narayani-gauda (Dakshina, lahari 5, note 1); the ghosha is the same as there, and its translation is repeated. There is no mangala-shloka: after the raga and ghosha the song begins with ॥ 1 ॥. The title follows the colophon on pdf 130 (checked against the scan): "ইতি শ্রীরসিকমঙ্গল-দক্ষিণবিভাগে উদ্দণ্ড-উদ্ধার-নাম ষোড়শ-লহরী সম্পূর্ণা" (*uddaṇḍa-uddhāra*) — "The Deliverance of Uddanda"; the 1997 edition reads the same. This is the last, sixteenth lahari of the Dakshina-vibhaga. The summary line covers the whole lahari (1–55 from the scans of pdf 128–129; 56–72 from the scan of pdf 130, provisionally).
[^2]: "Received instruction" — *upadeśa hailā*: received initiation and instruction (lahari 15, 71).
[^3]: "Wholly devoted" — in the scan *ananya śaraṇa* (the OCR of the full edition has "অনন্ত"). "The towns of Utkala" — *utkalanagara*; it may also be understood as "the whole land of Utkala".
[^4]: The number "॥ ৫ ॥" is not printed in the edition (only "॥" after the couplet); the edition's count 4 → [5] → 6 is unbroken.
[^5]: Bhanjabhum (*bhañjabhūma*) is "the land of the Bhanjas", the domain of the rajas of the Bhanja line (lahari 12, note 10). Shaivas and Shaktas are worshippers of Shiva and of Shakti-Durga (Purva, lahari 9, note 8). "The killing of living beings" — *jībahatyā*: animal sacrifice (lahari 14, 66, note 33; lahari 15, 40–52).
[^6]: "In the chambers" — *mandira-bhitara*: *mandira* is a house, palace or temple (cf. lahari 10, note 13).
[^7]: "Steward" — *bebarttā*: so the editor explains below the line (*byabasthāpaka*; the gloss is not translated). "Obeisance with an offering" — *niuchāni*: according to the editor's explanation, an obeisance with an offering of cloth (*bastrabheṭa diẏā praṇāma*; the gloss is not translated).
[^8]: "As soon as [he] made a sign" — *iṅgita karibāmātra*: the subjects are not named; understood as: the steward attracted the raja's attention with a sign, and the raja, distracted from the Bhagavata, looked at him. Ramakrishna (in 15, 27 — Ramakrishnadasa) is a disciple of Rasika (lahari 4, 19, note 8; lahari 6, note 15). "The best of brahmanas" — *dbijabara*.
[^9]: "The blessing of the world" — *bhubanamaṅgala*.
[^10]: "You are listening to [other] talk" — *kathā kara pāna*, lit. "you drink talk".
[^11]: "Drawing" — *yoṛi*: so the editor explains below the line (*bāhira kariyā*, "taking out"; the gloss is not translated). "To kill" — *māribāre*: it may also be understood as "to strike".
[^12]: "In due measure" — *paramāṇe* (= *pramāṇe*): justly, in proportion (cf. 22).
[^13]: "On a jewelled plate" — *ratnasthāli*. "Leavings" — *jhuṭā*. "Know" — *jānaha*: the raja addresses the assembly.
[^14]: The subject of 21 is not named: understood as continuing 19–20 (about one who listens to other talk); the raja says this of himself.
[^15]: "From the eighty-four" — *caurāśī*: from the cycle of eighty-four lakhs of births (lahari 14, 12–19, note 7).
[^16]: "Embracing by the neck" — *galā dhari*. "Bathed [his] body" — *aṅga pākhālilā*; it may also be understood as "bathed [his own] body": the subject and object of lines 24a–b allow both readings.
[^17]: "Wondrous" — the scan has "অদ্ভুন" (*adbhuna*; checked against a crop), understood as *adbhuta*, "wondrous"; the OCR of the 1997 edition is illegible. "Saintliness" — *sādhu kathā*, lit. "the account of the sadhu (of the saintly one)".
[^18]: "With a rebuke he restrained" — *nibārila tāṛanā kariyā*: understood as: the raja, with a shout, stopped the people who had rushed at Ramakrishna (14).
[^19]: "The raja [and] the three brothers" — *rājā tina bhāi*: the raja and his brothers, all three (lahari 12, 17–18). "Embraced" — *karila kole*, lit. "took onto his lap (to his breast)".
[^20]: "Worship" — *bhaja* (an imperative form; understood as indicative; it may also be understood as the author's call: "worship…"). Chudamani — "the jewel on the crown" (lahari 15, note 30). The guru is honoured as Hari Himself, since he is the Lord's dear servant (Dakshina, lahari 3, note 31); on how the author exalts Shyamananda and Rasika, see Purva, lahari 1, note 2, and lahari 14, note 11.
[^21]: Govindapur is Sri Govindapur, the village given to Shyamananda by the bhuiyan Bhimadhana; there Shyamananda built a house (lahari 12, 10, note 7).
[^22]: Yamuna Thakurani — cf. lahari 12, 12 and note 8 (Yamuna among the women of Shyamananda's household). The sense of the couplet is unclear: "was born" (*janmechilā*) — apparently Yamuna came from Utkala, and Rasika brought her to Shyamananda; the person is not identified.
[^23]: "At [his] feet" — *caraṇe*: respectfully. "Of the raja's instruction" — *rājā upadeśa kathā*: of how the raja received instruction.
[^24]: Nrisimhapur (*nṛsiṃhapura*; in 43 *narasiṃhapura*) is a village in Utkala; in BR 15 (80) it is "Sri Nrisimhapura", where Shyamananda held sankirtana; Uddanda is not named there. Bhuiyan (*bhuñā*) — a local landholder (lahari 12, note 7; in lahari 10, note 16 — "bhunya"). Uddanda-raya (*uddaṇḍa rāẏa*): *uddaṇḍa* means "unruly, fierce"; in 44 and 52 he is called a raja.
[^25]: "Gudris" — *gudhaṛi* (= *gudaṛi*): the patchwork quilt or cloak of a wandering Vaishnava, serving him as both bedding and bag. "Takes away" — *laẏa chāṛāiẏā*. "Intoxicated" — *matta*.
[^26]: "Narasimhapur" — so in 43 (*narasiṃhapura*); the same village as Nrisimhapur in 40. "Arrived" — *haila upasana* (= *upasanna*).
[^27]: "Had only just lain down and was still awake" — *śaẏana kariẏā mātra jāgrata āchilā*, lit. "had just lain down, was waking": the vision was in waking.
[^28]: Mahapurusha (*mahāpuruṣa*), "great personage": the author does not say who this is; according to 52 the voice heard at night is that of Shyamananda himself, i.e., it was Shyamananda who appeared to Uddanda at night. On how the author exalts Shyamananda, see Purva, lahari 1, note 2, and lahari 14, note 11. "Appeared" — *haila adhiṣṭhāna*.
[^29]: "Sweet" — *susbara*; the editor notes below the line the variant *gabhīra*, "deep" (the editor's note is not translated). "Face to face" — *sākṣāte*.
[^30]: "Divine knowledge" — *dibya jñāna*. "Shall I behold" — *pāmu … darśana*, lit. "shall I obtain the sight" (*pāmu* is a first-person future form).
[^31]: "Prabhu" is Shyamananda; "the two Prabhus" are Shyamananda and Rasika. "Arrived" — *bīje kaila* (*bijaẏa*): a respectful word for a procession.
[^32]: "The Prabhu whose words he had heard" — *ye bāṇī śunila karṇe se prabhu sākṣāte*: understood as: the one whose voice the raja had heard at night now stood before him (note 28).
[^33]: "With his whole family" — *sabaṃśe*. "Fortunate" — *bhāgyabān*.
[^34]: "Asura" — *asura*: a villain, a man of demonic nature. "Mighty" — *pratāpī*. "By the touch" — *paraśe*.
[^35]: Numbering: in the scan this couplet (the first on pdf 130, printed p. 92) is numbered "॥ ৫৭ ॥", and the next one is also "॥ ৫৭ ॥"; there is no 56. By the count this is 56 (a misprint of the edition), and it is given so; the next is 57 (checked against a crop ×2). The running head of pdf 130, "দক্ষিণ বিভাগ-পঞ্চদশ-লহরী" ("fifteenth lahari"), is also an error of the edition. "Apart from guru, Krishna and sadhu" — *guru kṛṣṇa sādhu bine*.
[^36]: Shyama-raya — a Deity of Krishna in Dharenda (Dakshina, lahari 10, note 12). "He had … brought" — *ānāilā*: the subject is not named; apparently Shyamananda (cf. Pashchima, lahari 1, 2); it can also be understood as "[Uddanda] had … brought". "Dharenda" — in the scan *dhārandā*. "Three festivals" — *tina mahotsaba* (cf. Dakshina, lahari 7). "To serve sadhus by the hundreds and hundreds" (58) — *śata śata sādhusebā*; in the 1997 edition (OCR) — "সাধু-সব".
[^37]: Dadhi-kada (*dadhi kādā*, "curd [and] mud") is a festive game in which the participants douse one another with curd mixed with water and turmeric and dance in this "mud" (so Nandotsava, the celebration of Krishna's birth in Nanda's house, is kept); here it is apparently the conclusion of the festival. "Having completed" — *sāri*. "Sat down" — *basilena*; in the 1997 edition (OCR) — *balilena*, "said". "Makes a humble request" — *nibedana kare*. The bhuiyan is Uddanda (note 24).
[^38]: "Destroyed" — *saṃhāra karinu*: killed or ruined (cf. 41: "he beats the Vaishnavas"). "Of evil conduct" — *durācāra*.
[^39]: Gudri — note 25. "Their" — *tāhāra* (singular): apparently the gudris of those sadhus. "A whole room" — *eka ghara*, lit. "one house (one room)".
[^40]: "At once" — *anibāre*, lit. "unhindered, irresistibly". "Piled up like a mountain" — *kaila parbbata ākāre*, lit. "made [them] in the shape of a mountain".
[^41]: "Seven hundred and eighteen" — *sāta śata aṣṭādaśa* (so in the scan); in the 1997 edition (OCR) — *śata śata aṣṭādaśa*, "hundreds and hundreds, eighteen". "The devotees of Krishna" — *kārṣṇa jane*: in the scan "কাষ্ঞ´" with the repha shifted to the right (cf. lahari 14, note 29); in the 1997 edition (OCR) — "কার".
[^42]: "Prabhu" — Shyamananda. "Give [him] their blessings" — *āśīrbāda kare*: the Vaishnavas bless Shyamananda or Uddanda; whom is not said.
[^43]: "Following his example" — *tā'ra dekhādekhi*. "Saintliness" — *sādhutā*.
[^44]: "Might" — *paratāpa* (= *pratāpa*). "The three sins of worldly existence" — *bhaba tina pāpa*: the meaning is unclear; apparently the three kinds of suffering (*tāpa-traẏa*: from one's own body and mind, from other beings and from the forces of nature), or the sins of body, speech and mind. "Are destroyed" — *khaṇḍe*. On how the author exalts Shyamananda and Rasika, see Purva, lahari 1, note 2; Purva, lahari 14, note 11.
[^45]: "The greatest sinners" — *mahā mahā pāpā* (so in the scan; understood as *pāpī*, as in the 1997 edition, by the OCR). "Wholly surrendered" — *ananyaśaraṇa*.
[^46]: "Described" — *baṇile* (= *barṇile*). "Of [his] nature" — *sbabhāba*: the meaning is unclear; cf. Pashchima, lahari 1, 68: *sbabhāba-barṇana*, "a description of [Rasika's] nature (character)". It can also be understood as "according to my own understanding (my own nature) I have briefly…". "Composed" — *karinu racana*.
[^47]: The title is "The Deliverance of Uddanda" (*uddaṇḍa-uddhāra*; checked against the scan). The colophon stands on pdf 130 below ॥72॥; below it is a rule, and the rest of the page is blank. With this lahari the Dakshina-vibhaga ends; the Pashchima-vibhaga begins on a new page (pdf 131).
