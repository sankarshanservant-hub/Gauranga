# Vilasa 18. Gopala Bhatta; the Deliverance of Chand Raya

*On the followers of the Gosvamis of Vrindavana — Kashishvara, Lokanatha, Raghunatha Dasa and Krishnadasa Kaviraja; Mahaprabhu's meeting with Gopala Bhatta at Srirangam, his journey to Vrindavana and the Hari-bhakti-vilasa; the disciples of Gopala Bhatta and the fall of Harivamsha; Narottama drives a brahma-daitya out of the sinner Chand Raya and initiates him, his brother Santosha and his father Raghavendra; Chand Raya at Kheturi; captivity under the Nawab, the subduing of the elephant and release; the author's closing words*

**1.** Glory, glory to Mahaprabhu Sri Krishna Chaitanya!
Glory, glory to Nityananda, whose heart is compassion!

**2.** Glory, glory to Advaitachandra, the utmost of mercy,
who, bringing down Gaurachandra, fulfilled his desire!

**3.** Glory, glory to Gadadhara, the ocean of rasa!
Glory, glory to the devotees of Gaura, possessed of every virtue!

**4.** Of all the Gosanis who live in Vrindavana,
whose branches and sub-branches they are, I have not written.

**5.** Whoever has written of it wrote by consulting the shastras;
but I write following the command of Prabhu[^1].

**6.** My Thakurani holds within her the shakti of Sri Gauranga;
all that I have written I wrote on hearing the command from her blessed lips.

**7.** As Gauranga spoke, so [speaks] the Thakurani;
I know no other view — that alone I expound.

**8.** My Thakurani is she who sports in Vrindavana;
of that I have not written, knowing her heart[^2].

**9.** To write disputes on siddhanta is an offence;
[but here are] words from Prabhu's blessed lips — in this there is no fear.

**10.** Two brothers born of one mother — Rupa and Sanatana;
Prabhu placed his own shakti in them.

**11.** Rupa and Sanatana are devoted to Prabhu's feet;
Sanatana and Rupa are shown the highest honour and esteem.

**12.** In the land of Mathura, Kashishvara Pandita is renowned;
his devotion to Rupa and Sanatana is very deep.

**13.** I write the reason for it, I write it once more:
he is a disciple of Ishvara Puri — such is [his standing][^3].

**14.** [Ishvara Puri] entrusted him to Sri Krishna Chaitanya,
[and Prabhu] wove him [into his tree] as one of his chief branches.

**15.** Gadadhara Pandita is Prabhu's own shakti;
not seeing Vidyanidhi, Prabhu wept bitterly[^4].

**16.** Gadadhara Pandita is the disciple of that same Pundarika,
and Bhugarbha is his disciple, especially dear to Prabhu.

**17.** Rupa and Sanatana honour him and show him kindness;
to some he shows love and devotion, to others compassion.

**18.** Lokanatha Gosani is the vessel of Prabhu's mercy;
he delivers souls — compassion incarnate.

**19.** Rupa and Sanatana revere him as the foremost [of devotees];
there is no other so renounced in the three worlds.

**20.** He takes no thought for food and lives in a secluded place;
no one knows in what sadhana his time passes.

**21.** Rupa and Sanatana hold him a worthy perfected soul;
when they ask him, he answers, and they accept [his word][^5].

**22.** His servant [and disciple] is Thakura Mahashaya;
how many are his virtues — how could I write them?

**23.** Kashishvara has a disciple, a resident of Vraja,
born in a brahmana family, named Bhaktakashi.

**24.** Govinda Gosani and Yadava Acharya,
leaving their household affairs, took shelter at his feet.

**25.** These two brahmana youths, natives of Gauda,
live with their master and serve him.

**26.** Krishna Pandita Thakura is a pure Vrajavasi;
Rupa and Sanatana show him great honour.

**27.** The glory of Kashishvara and Krishnadasa is boundless;
Sri Rupa Gosani knows their glory[^6].

**28.** Keli-kala and Kunkuma — such are the svarupas of these two[^7];
the two, the life of all, came together.

**29.** Raghunatha Bhatta, dear [to Prabhu], whose life is Gauranga,
stays constantly with Rupa and Sanatana.

**30.** Sri Yadunandana is a disciple of Acharya Gosani;
Raghunatha Dasa is [his] disciple, wholly surrendered[^8].

**31.** By the strength of Nityananda's mercy he gave up worldly wealth,
went to Nilachala and saw Prabhu.

**32.** With utmost renunciation he lived with [Prabhu] in the Kshetra;
seeing him, Prabhu was filled with joy and delight.

**33.** After some days [Prabhu] entrusted him to Svarupa,
who trained him in body, speech and mind.

**34.** Only Gauranga himself understood the reason:
why he did so — who can understand?

**35.** [Svarupa] is most skilled in the charming rasa of shringara;
night and day he assists [Prabhu], [endowed with] the qualities of Lalita[^9].

**36.** The former word is fulfilled — who can understand it?
As one dear to Svarupa, [Prabhu] shows [Raghunatha] mercy[^10].

**37.** After some more days, that Raghunatha Dasa
[Prabhu], giving him a garland of gunja, placed in Radha's hands.

**38.** He gave him the stone of Govardhana to serve
and ordered him to go to Vrindavana.

**39.** [There] he surrendered himself to Rupa and Sanatana
and so was united with his own perfected yutha[^11].

**40.** They became very kind to him, [holding him] dear as life:
this one shows devotion, those embrace him.

**41.** He chose to dwell by the kunda of Radhika;
fifty-six dandas of the day and night are his bhajana[^12].

**42.** Who else is so renounced and so dear to Radhika?
The Kaviraja, his disciple, lived beside him[^13].

**43.** For the fulfilment of his own cherished goal [he chose] such a mahashaya —
[so] writes Yadunandana himself, my guru[^14].

**44.** When Krishnadasa Kaviraja [lived] in the land of Gauda,
he performed bhajana to Krishna, absorbed in bliss.

**45.** One day, in a village called Jhamatpur,
Nityananda, the abode of virtues, appeared to him,

**46.** with his companions, in a captivating dress.
Seeing his form, Krishnadasa rejoiced at heart.

**47.** Bowing down, he offered him much praise.
The command came: "All perfection [is yours] — go to Vrindavana!"

**48.** In his own book he writes of himself as Prabhu's disciple:
"Not regarding that I am lowly and wretched, he showed me mercy"[^15].

**49.** Then he went again to Vrindavana
and took shelter at the feet of Raghunatha.

**50.** Why he writes so, why he takes [another] shelter —
only one of great realization understands.

**51.** Such is the conduct of the perfected, utterly pure:
when one takes shelter in bhava, all things are revealed.

**52.** For that virtue Rupa and Sanatana showed him mercy.
Thus I have described [their] views.

**53.** Hear now about Gopala Bhatta:
how he went to Vrindavana and how he found shelter.

**54.** When Mahaprabhu travelled to the south,
wandering, he came to Ranga-kshetra[^16].

**55.** Bathing in the Kaveri and seeing Ranganatha,
he took his alms in the house of Trimalla Bhatta[^17].

**56.** Out of love for the Bhatta, Prabhu spent the chaturmasya there[^18];
day and night he spoke of Krishna with the Bhatta.

**57.** Formerly [the family] worshipped Lakshmi-Narayana;
in jest Prabhu raised [this] question with him:

**58.** "Lakshmi, who dwells on her Lord's chest, though a faithful wife,
desires the company of Krishna — so the shastras say.

**59.** Why, being a faithful wife, does she long for Krishna's company?"
Having said this, Mahaprabhu smiled softly.

**60.** Hearing this, the Bhatta was bewildered:
he could not grasp the inner meaning.

**61.** Afraid at heart, he said to Prabhu:
"Whatever you say, my mind cannot enter into it.

**62.** I know nothing of the goal and the means;
I know that you are that very Lakshmi-Narayana.

**63.** Showing me mercy, you have come here
and appeared to me in the dress of a sannyasi.

**64.** What praise can I offer? Nothing comes to mind.
Knowing me ignorant, show me mercy, O Merciful One!"

**65.** Hearing this, Mahaprabhu was moved to mercy;
embracing him, he imparted shakti to him.

**66.** At that moment the Vraja-lila was revealed in his mind;
his body swelled with prema and he began to dance.

**67.** Prabhu showed him his own form
and commanded: "All the people in your house —

**68.** bring them all, let them see me now."
Hearing Prabhu's command, the Bhatta went

**69.** and brought before Prabhu the whole household —
two brothers, his son and all who lived in the house.

**70.** Prabhu in his mercy purified their minds;
seeing Prabhu's form, all shed tears.

**71.** All fell to the ground, prostrating themselves;
in his mercy he placed his feet on everyone's head.

**72.** All went home; three remained.
Mercifully Prabhu spoke sweet words:

**73.** "This son of yours, named Gopala Bhatta —
my great mercy rests upon him.

**74.** Have him taught and make him a fine pandita;
do not have him married — this I tell you."

**75.** Smiling, Prabhu looked at Prabodhananda:
"Your disciple will be a treasury of virtues in all the shastras"[^19].

**76.** Gopala Bhatta was then studying the Sri Bhagavata;
Prabhu told him his own inner view.

**77.** He said to him: "You will remain at home for some time;
when you are parted from your mother and father, you will go to Vrindavana.

**78.** There you will find great happiness — this I tell you."
Having said this to him, he said to Prabodhananda:

**79.** "Be sure to send him to Vrindavana:
I have need of him — this I tell you."

**80.** Saying this, Prabhu took his leave;
in separation from Prabhu, weeping arose in the Bhatta's house.

**81.** That Prabodhananda was dear to Prabhu as life;
in his mercy Prabhu made him the best of bhagavatas.

**82.** Thus I have described such mercy of Prabhu;
in passing I have written all this account.

**83.** Here is the account of what I have written;
now I write of Gopala Bhatta's going to Vrindavana.

**84.** At the end of his days Prabodhananda remembered [this]
and, calling the Bhatta, told him Prabhu's words.

**85.** [Gopala] remembered the command [Prabhu] had spoken
and resolved in his mind: "I will go to Vrindavana."

**86.** Prabodhananda Sarasvati showed him mercy;
[Gopala] himself wrote [of this] in the book Hari-bhakti-vilasa[^20].

**87.** At the end of his days Sarasvati said:
"Go and take shelter of Rupa and Sanatana."

**88.** When his mind had turned away from samsara,
[Prabodhananda] wrote a letter with his own hand.

**89.** He wrote a fitting letter to the two Gosanis:
"I have sent Gopala Bhatta to you."

**90.** With that letter [Gopala] set out by the road through Jharikhanda
and after some days arrived at Mathura.

**91.** The next day in Vrindavana he saw Rupa,
bowed down and offered him much praise.

**92.** He gave the letter; the two brothers read it and understood all,
kept him near them and showed him great mercy.

**93.** The two brothers loved the Bhatta as their life;
after some days the two brothers gave him a command —

**94.** [to compose] the book Hari-bhakti-vilasa on Vaishnava conduct,
on the rites of Vaishnavas, mudras, rules and the rest.

**95.** The book was completed and he offered it to Sanatana;
he accepted it, making it his own book.

**96.** In it he wrote a praise of his own guru —
the first shloka of the book, the auspicious invocation[^21].

**97.** He is perfected, his deeds cannot be understood;
whoever thinks of them otherwise brings harm on himself[^22].

**98.** Taking into account each one's virtues and nature,
he shows mercy accordingly, knowing his mind.

**99.** What were the names of Gopala Bhatta's disciples,
and in what land each lived — hear the account.

**100.** Srinivasa Acharya, Harivamsha the Vrajavasi,
Gopinatha Pujari — a great treasury of virtues[^23].

**101.** And two more disciples of the Bhatta, a great treasury of prema —
Shambhurama and Makaranda, natives of Gujarat.

**102.** The service of Sri Radha-ramana he entrusted to Gopinatha[^24].
These are the disciples of the Bhatta told of in the account.

**103.** Not heeding his guru's command, Harivamsha fell:
all the virtues he had came to ruin.

**104.** For what reason Harivamsha fell
I shall describe, expanding on it a little.

**105.** Harivamsha the Vrajavasi was very learned;
he constantly served the Bhatta Gosvami.

**106.** The Bhatta Gosvami had great love for him:
[he was] a supreme devotee, the abode of all virtues.

**107.** [But] by fate he transgressed his guru's command.
Listen, listen, O hearers, with minds attentive!

**108.** One day, on the day of Sri Ekadashi, Harivamsha
came to Prabhu chewing betel.

**109.** Seeing betel in his mouth, the Gosani asked him [about it];
he answered: "It is Sri Radha's prasada betel."

**110.** The Gosani said: "On the day of Sri Ekadashi
one must abstain even from Hari's prasada.

**111.** As it is said:
*'Hari's prasada is always to be accepted, except on Ekadashi.'"*[^25]

**112.** The Gosani said: "Do not do such a thing again:
by transgressing the shastras you will commit an offence."

**113.** Bowing to the Gosani, Harivamsha went away from there,
but, fond of betel, he could not give it up.

**114.** Again on Ekadashi, having chewed Sri Radha's prasada betel,
he went to the Gosvami.

**115.** Harivamsha bowed to the Gosani,
and he saw his lips reddened with betel.

**116.** The Gosani said: "Harivamsha, you are a pandita —
why do you act contrary [to the rules]?

**117.** Chewing betel on the day of Sri Ekadashi —
[by this] you have taken all sins upon yourself.

**118.** Being a pandita, you have broken the command;
for this offence I reject you."

**119.** Harivamsha said: "Taking betel is my service;
I cannot refuse this prasada.

**120.** I have committed an offence at your lotus feet,
[but] I could not disregard Sri Radha's prasada."

**121.** Hearing these words, the Gosani became angry,
and Harivamsha quickly went away from there.

**122.** Harivamsha lost the service of Radha-ramana
and manifested the image of Sri Radha-vallabha[^26].

**123.** In that offending body two sons were born to him,
named Vanachandra and Vrindavanachandra.

**124.** Earlier Harivamsha had had two other sons,
who were given the names Krishnadasa and Suryadasa.

**125.** Entrusting the service to his sons, he went to the forest,
immersing his mind in the feet of Sri Radha-vallabha.

**126.** Strange are the ways of fate — they cannot be understood:
robbers cut off Harivamsha's head and threw it into the Yamuna.

**127.** Crying "Radha, Radha!", the head floated upstream
to where Gopala Bhatta Gosani was bathing.

**128.** At that ghat the head came to rest
and, crying "Radha", began to shed tears.

**129.** At that time the Bhatta Gosani was at that ghat
and was amazed that a severed head was saying "Radha".

**130.** Looking closely, the Gosani saw Harivamsha's head;
he said, "Come, come!" and felt great pain at heart.

**131.** The severed head came and touched Prabhu's feet:
"Tell me, will you forgive the offence of an offender?"

**132.** The Gosani said: "I have forgiven your offence" —
and with these words he placed his foot on its head.

**133.** Receiving [the touch of] his foot, Harivamsha was liberated.
Gopala Bhatta told everyone all that had happened.

**134.** When the one against whom the offence was committed forgives,
then Sri Krishna's mercy comes — let all know this.

**135.** One whose offence is not removed,
even if a great devotee, will not obtain Krishna's mercy.

**136.** The offence of an offender does not leave even his descendants;
for this reason Vaishnavas should shun them.

**137.** Sri Jiva Gosani is the disciple of Sri Rupa;
know this in due order — there is no other[^27].

**138.** Their comings and goings are for Gauranga's happiness;
their bhajana and remembrance are for Prabhu's own happiness.

**139.** Whatever bhajana and shelter each has had from the first —
as the devotee is in each place, so is the way.

**140.** The wish-fulfilling tree named Chaitanya bears five fruits[^28];
know, all of you — [they are tasted] in the company of those devotees.

**141.** Everyone's inclination of mind and dharma are preserved;
and the accompanying disciples, too, they blessed with mercy.

**142.** Srinivasa and Narottama are the two empowered ones;
both have countless branches — I cannot tell them all.

**143.** The number of branches and virtues of these two I cannot write;
through that door all the lowly and wretched are delivered.

**144.** [This is] the description of this virtue of Thakura Mahashaya;
hear also another wondrous word.

**145.** He in whose heart Gauranga himself dwells
serves outwardly there too that image of prema.

**146.** Yatras, great festivals, service [of the deity], service of Vaishnavas,
bhajana and remembrance — so he spends his time.

**147.** Those who became his disciples he instructs:
"Serve Krishna and perform bhajana to Krishna"[^29].

**148.** "The word of my Prabhu is within me and without;
[all is] that Prabhu, that command — if he shows mercy."

**149.** Narottama considers himself unworthy:
"Listen, disciples and friends, I tell you:

**150.** one whose aim from the very first is to attain Krishna's feet
should take for himself a count of a lakh of names"[^30].

**151.** His branches multiplied in his own land and in other lands.
I shall write one more account, full of bliss.

**152.** Raghavendra Raya, a brahmana, a resident of a certain land —
I shall tell of him, [beginning] from Garerhat[^31].

**153.** He had two sons, Santosha and Chand Raya;
all people spoke of Chand Raya as mighty.

**154.** A great warrior, strong, valiant in battle —
hearing his name, [people] trembled for their lives.

**155.** He was the zamindar [of an estate] of eighty-four thousand [rupees][^32].
After some time it came about thus:

**156.** [The land] passed to Garhi-dvara: there [sat] a faujdar;
setting up a thana at Rajmahal, he governed [the land][^33].

**157.** [Chand Raya], seeing himself strong, reasoned in his mind:
he pays no tax to the Padshah and sets up his own posts in the villages.

**158.** He keeps five thousand horse and some foot;
by force of arms he seized many lands.

**159.** He gave battle — the people in fear abandoned the thana;
he plundered and carried off all the money and wealth.

**160.** The land became the domain of his fort — so he lives[^34]:
robbing, stealing, killing people, heeding no one.

**161.** His sins cannot be written;
people stop their ears and run away.

**162.** Worshipping Shakti, he constantly drinks wine and eats meat;
he plunders others' wives, homes and goods and carries them off.

**163.** At the festival of Durga he worships the image,
and there is no end to the animals he slaughters.

**164.** In the abode of Yama, Chitragupta cannot write down
all his sins — hundreds of bundles have piled up[^35].

**165.** One day Chitragupta says to the king [Yama]:
"What do these two brahmana sons not do!

**166.** Having committed so many sins, where will they stay?
How long will the two of them suffer hell?

**167.** I remember the two of old, Jagai and Madhai;
these two brothers are greater sinners still.

**168.** Those were great sinners, but did not commit so many sins."
King Yama said: "Shame on them!"

**169.** Thus Chand Raya lived for some time.
[Then] a brahma-daitya came and seized him[^36].

**170.** That brahmana son was exceedingly wicked;
[the spirit], entering his body, beats him.

**171.** It binds his body; [Chand Raya] raves incessantly;
his body has withered — he is about to give up his life.

**172.** His father brings many physicians from land after land;
many remedies were tried — the spirit leaves for none of them.

**173.** He summoned an astrologer[^37], who made his reckoning:
"The brahma-daitya will not leave — hear, this is certain."

**174.** Reckoning again, he said: "Listen, sir:
there is no remedy — [there is] one, but it is impossible.

**175.** The zamindar of the land of Kheturi
has a son — Narottama, Thakura Mahashaya.

**176.** If he in his mercy comes [here],
then the spirit will leave" — so he declared.

**177.** Hearing this, his father summoned a pandita
and had a fitting letter written by his hand.

**178.** Separately the Raya wrote [to Krishnananda] with a request:
"By my good fortune may your son come [to us]."

**179.** [Setting out] the reason he wrote, having considered everything,
he sent [for him] a sukhpal and bearers[^38].

**180.** All those men went to Kheturi,
gave the letter to the Majumdar and made their request.

**181.** Having read it, he pondered the matter;
with the letter he went to where Thakura Mahashaya was.

**182.** Holding the letter he had read in his hand, he said [to himself]:
"Why did they send the letter? They have troubled themselves in vain.

**183.** Who has the courage to tell him and send him there?"
To Narottama he said nothing of this matter[^39].

**184.** Out of fear the Raya told [him] nothing; going outside,
he wrote a reply and sent it off.

**185.** The men came and reported everything to him;
hearing it, [Chand Raya's] father began to weep:

**186.** "Mother Durga, save my son this time!
Besides you, who has the power to protect?"

**187.** At night the Thakurani, in the guise of a brahmana woman[^40],
spoke to Chand Raya, smiling softly:

**188.** "What good can there be, son? Your body is full of sin;
I have no power to do this.

**189.** Of all the sinful deeds and sinful ways in the world —
who but you does such things?

**190.** You did not worship Krishna's feet; you did such things;
you worshipped me — my heart breaks with grief.

**191.** Whoever leaves Krishna and worships me, the world becomes his enemy;
I destroy him — I cannot bear it.

**192.** Whoever worships me out of greed, his next life is ruined:
[I] take away his wealth and livelihood, and then comes utter ruin.

**193.** My lord (Shiva) is intoxicated with Krishna's qualities;
offering everything to Him, he remains in meditation[^41].

**194.** The Three-eyed, Five-faced one [lives] for His sake,
and I am his maidservant, I tell you[^42].

**195.** You two brothers took shelter of me,
[but] on what you have done there is no mercy of mine.

**196.** When I am worshipped in sattva-guna, I am glad;
at rajo-guna and tamo-guna my breast is torn.

**197.** Krishna is the maker of the world, the shastras say;
who else on earth grants liberation and bhakti?

**198.** You have reached the limit of sin — there is no talking of it;
King Yama and Chitragupta are in great distress.

**199.** Having sinned, how will you two endure the reckoning?
In the records there are bundles as high as a mountain.

**200.** My lord will be pleased [only] then:
without delay, son, worship the feet of Govinda.

**201.** Thakura Mahashaya, of whom the astrologer spoke —
bring him and take shelter at his feet.

**202.** Srinivasa's disciple Sri Govinda Kaviraja
worshipped me, leaving all else aside[^43];

**203.** he begged me much for liberation,
but to give it was not in my power.

**204.** Taking shelter at the Acharya's feet,
he gained bhakti to Krishna and dispelled the fear of samsara.

**205.** That Srinivasa and Narottama are one life;
for the sake of the lila two bodies exist.

**206.** To deliver the souls of the Kali age, Chaitanya and Nitai
came to earth together with their associates and attendants.

**207.** They delivered all souls by giving them Krishna's name;
from the prema of those two are Srinivasa and Narottama.

**208.** Whoever worships these two knowing them to be one
will surely attain the feet of Govinda.

**209.** Whoever, seeing them as different, reviles or honours them [apart][^44],
know for certain, sinks into Yama's noose and perishes."

**210.** Having said this, the Thakurani vanished;
in [Chand Raya's] heart an astonished understanding arose.

**211.** In the morning he told everything to his father and brother:
"Bring the Thakura — then my life will be spared."

**212.** In the morning [they sent] two brahmanas with a letter:
"You are merciful — show mercy to me, your servant.

**213.** When I see those two feet with my own eyes,
then this wicked brahmana will tell you everything."

**214.** With the letter the two brahmanas go to the village of Kheturi;
setting down the letter, the two brahmanas bowed.

**215.** [Narottama] honoured them and [asked] whence they had come.
"Here is the letter — what more need we say?"

**216.** He gave the brahmanas food and gave them lodging;
reading the letter, he considered its words.

**217.** He told the Kaviraja all the news:
"Consider this and tell me your opinion."

**218.** [He replied:] "This is a very hard matter, beyond people's understanding;
what can I say? You possess all virtues,

**219.** you hold every power, the manifest image of prema:
to see you with one's eyes is joy and delight."

**220.** Thus, deliberating, part of the night passed.
Gaura-raya himself, sitting on his seat, speaks[^45]:

**221.** "Listen, Narottama, I tell you what to do:
this is no great wonder — go to him.

**222.** Those two brahmanas are the greatest of sinners;
for the sake of seeing you [Chand Raya] keeps hold of life.

**223.** Show him mercy, let him be delivered;
the brahma-daitya will leave him — this is my command.

**224.** You have appeared for the deliverance of sinners:
many, receiving salvation, will become servants of Krishna.

**225.** Take the Kaviraja with you and go to his house:
how many hearts will be filled with joy!"

**226.** Morning came; Prabhu's command became [his] strength;
he told the Kaviraja everything that had happened.

**227.** Having taken their morning bath, the two are setting out;
at that moment the Majumdar arrives.

**228.** [Narottama] told him all about the letter:
"I think I shall go to his house."

**229.** The Raya said: "It is his fortune [earned] over many births
that he will see those feet of yours with his own eyes.

**230.** I, luckless one, shall not see it;
how the mercy is given I shall hear afterwards."

**231.** He arranged [a party], giving many men as escort;
[Narottama] sets out with the Kaviraja, taking Vaishnavas with him.

**232.** Bowing to Gauranga, he went out;
all the people weep, unable to restrain themselves.

**233.** Honouring everyone, he set off;
behind him walk those two brahmanas.

**234.** That day, seeing a village [on the way], they halted on the road;
one brahmana went [ahead] with the news.

**235.** He told the Raya everything, the reason for the journey;
he rejoiced at heart, and his eyes overflowed.

**236.** With brahmanas, worthy men and a great crowd,
joyful at heart, he went out on the road to meet him.

**237.** How many instruments sound — who can count?
Going a little way, all of them saw [the Thakura].

**238.** Seeing his form, with tears they fell at his feet;
smiling, he greeted them all.

**239.** When he arrived and entered the village,
the people gaze at him, absorbed in bliss.

**240.** Full pitchers have been set here and there along the road,
hundreds of banana trees have been planted.

**241.** Flower garlands at every house, along every street;
thousands of people are following him.

**242.** All the women raise the auspicious ululation[^46];
people count themselves blessed and their lives fulfilled.

**243.** They gaze at his form — streams of tears flow;
hearing [of him], people from other villages run with loud cries.

**244.** Then he went to the Raya's house;
[the Raya], joyful at heart, washed his feet.

**245.** Gazing at his form, [the Raya] shed streams of tears;
he washed the Thakura's feet with water.

**246.** [The Thakura] sat on the seat; the Raya says to the Thakura:
"There are no words for the limit of my fortune"[^47].

**247.** "Good, good," the Thakura said to him,
"I shall see your son. Come, in which room is he?"

**248.** To where Chand Raya lies on his bed
the Thakura goes with all the people.

**249.** The Raya went and raised him, taking him in his arms.
The Thakura entered that room.

**250.** The Thakura's companions stood in front;
Chand Raya sees [the Thakura] with his own eyes.

**251.** The brahma-daitya that was in his heart
began to speak, shouting aloud:

**252.** "Having committed many sins, I became a brahma-daitya;
such a sinner as I am, such a sinner I found.

**253.** For so many days I have enjoyed myself in his body;
now I am commanded — where am I to go?"

**254.** In the midst of all the people he spoke again:
"I have been granted the sight of you — may I be delivered!

**255.** You are the purifier of the fallen: by the mere sight of you
even a brahma-daitya is delivered — I have understood the reason.

**256.** Kheturi is not a village, it is Gupta Vrindavana;
when [my] reckoning is spent, [may I] be born in that land,

**257.** and, being born, take shelter at your feet —
then mercy will surely come to this lowly one."

**258.** Thakura Mahashaya said: "Listen, king of spirits:
leave his heart at once and go."

**259.** The room's door faced east — he went out towards the west[^48];
passing among the people, he attained the other world.

**260.** Seeing this, all the people fall at [his] feet;
all the people cry out: "Glory, glory!"

**261.** Chand Raya rose and came with [them] to his lodging,
and with joined palms bowed and fell to the ground:

**262.** "There is no other such sinner in the three worlds:
one look at me, and all merit is used up.

**263.** However many kinds of sin there are in the shastras —
I have committed them all, none is left.

**264.** With such sins, how shall I, a sinner, be saved?"
Saying this over and over, he weeps, rolling at [his] feet.

**265.** "Bearing a brahmana's body, to carry so many sins!
I fell into the intoxication of worldly things, into such delusion of maya."

**266.** Santosha, weeping, said: "Listen, O Merciful One,
let me tell you a little about myself.

**267.** We were born of one womb, two brothers,
and both of us sinned alike.

**268.** There is nothing more to tell Prabhu:
our only hope is your feet.

**269.** Make these two brahma-daityas your own;
be the lord of the life of Chand and Santosha."

**270.** Raghavendra came and fell, rolling at [his] feet:
"With all my line I am sold [to you] — in life and in death."

**271.** The Thakura called them, seated them beside himself
and placed his right hand on all their heads:

**272.** "Bathe, come quickly and hear Krishna's name;
soon Gaura Bhagavan will show you mercy."

**273.** Having bathed and put on new clothes,
they came at once before Prabhu.

**274.** He seated all three on his left
and one by one gave the Hari-nama into their ears.

**275.** Ramachandra Kaviraja, sitting on the left,
rolls on the ground, his whole body in ecstasy:

**276.** "Is there anyone so merciful in the three worlds?"
Saying this, he strikes his own head with his hand.

**277.** Seeing [this], all the Vaishnavas weep, beside themselves;
seeing it, all the people shed tears.

**278.** The two brothers and the father prostrate themselves;
calling them, he placed his feet upon their heads.

**279.** Such bhava at that time [no one] had seen or heard:
everywhere only the sound of weeping is heard.

**280.** The next day, when the auspicious moment came,
at that very moment he imparted to them the mantra of Radha and Krishna.

**281.** Another wonder also happened — hear the account:
King Yama and Chitragupta sing [his] praises.

**282.** "I have learned that there is a purifier of the fallen in the world;
otherwise who would deliver such sinners?

**283.** Ho, Chitragupta, see to it:
bring here the bundles of his sins."

**284.** Chitragupta brought them and threw them into the water[^49]:
"I know my authority is wholly gone."

**285.** Clutching his head, the king [Yama] laments:
"Coming to earth, [he] has spread prema[^50].

**286.** Everyone has gained hope of worshipping Krishna;
over whom now shall I hold authority?

**287.** As the deliverance of the two, Jagai and Madhai, was,
[the deliverance] of these two brahmana brothers is greater still."

**288.** When they had taken shelter at Prabhu's feet,
they brought and offered [him] many gifts:

**289.** they gave villages, gave clothes, much gold and silver,
vessels and the like, horses, cows, [oxen][^51] — by the hundred.

**290.** From early morning there are sweets and fried dishes,
vegetable dishes, thickened milk, baras and fragrant rice.

**291.** How great their fortune is cannot be told:
they receive the remnants of [his] plate and the water of [his] feet.

**292.** Their fame as Vaishnavas spread through the world;
every virtue was born [in them], entering their hearts.

**293.** Many, [coming] with them, took shelter at [his] feet;
he saved many sinners, and they found release.

**294.** He taught them dharma according to [his] former rule;
they sit continually [at] bhajana and remembrance.

**295.** The dharma, conduct and teaching I spoke of before [they adopted];
I shall write further of their other virtues, praising [them].

**296.** Together with all their line they surrendered to him alone;
that they had become Vaishnavas became known everywhere.

**297.** [Chand Raya], gathering everyone together, began to say:
"Let us go and see Gaura-raya — give your consent"[^52].

**298.** Hearing this, [all] wept bitterly;
[Chand Raya's] eyes quivered and tears streamed down.

**299.** One day, sitting [with him], the Thakura says to him:
"Listen, son Chand Raya, keep this in your heart:

**300.** what is [rightly] yours to enjoy, enjoy;
all else — [whence come] sin and oppression — give up"[^53].

**301.** Thus I have told of the deliverance of the three;
whoever hears it will attain Krishna's feet.

**302.** Now I write the account of Chand Raya's virtues —
hearing it, people attain salvation.

**303.** Carrying out the command, he summoned a vakil
and sent a written letter to the Nawab[^54].

**304.** On receiving the letter, that hakim was afraid at heart;
all the mutsuddis began to say to him[^55]:

**305.** "What need is there of him?
Whoever goes to that place will lose his life."

**306.** For fear of him, the Padshah's men did not travel that road:
they would [rather] wish for death than go there.

**307.** One day the Thakura says in the assembly:
"It would be good for me to go home once.

**308.** Without seeing Gaura-raya I cannot live;
I shall stay [there] some days and come back again."

**309.** All began to consider: what is there [to say]?
What Prabhu's will is — who can say?

**310.** They adorned ten boats with gold and jewels;
one boat was for the Thakura and his companions.

**311.** In one boat were the two brothers, with their father among them;
in all the other boats provisions were stowed.

**312.** They took much rice, mung and masakalai[^56],
and loaded many clothes and many goods.

**313.** Many men went aboard —
all the men who take the oars.

**314.** All the Vaishnavas who were with the Thakura
set out joyfully in the boats.

**315.** All the household, the dwellers of the inner apartments,
all the menservants and maidservants began to weep.

**316.** The two Raya brothers went aboard;
all together they set out by water.

**317.** They travel by boat, talking of Krishna;
that day they halted halfway at a certain place.

**318.** The next day, when one prahara had passed[^57],
they arrived and with joy at heart saw Gaura.

**319.** Seeing him, all roll on the ground in bhava:
someone clasps [his] feet, someone is unaware of his body.

**320.** Coming to their senses, all came and sat in their places;
the Thakura, smiling, calls [everyone] to the meal.

**321.** Chand Raya rose and went to see the Raya[^58];
the Raya came out; [Chand Raya] fell at his feet.

**322.** He, showing him honour, embraces him
and asks after everything, whether all is well.

**323.** [Chand Raya] says: "I am a sinner, but by seeing you,
by seeing [your] feet, I have gained every blessing."

**324.** The two came together in great love;
all together they proceeded to the Thakura.

**325.** Having seen the arati, all, to partake of prasada,
began to sit, each in his fitting place.

**326.** All partook of prasada in blissful rapture;
how many dishes they eat, how many [more] are served!

**327.** The fragrance fills the nostrils, surpassing amrita;
one whispers into another's ear.

**328.** Now and then in the midst [of the meal] they speak of Krishna:
in the middle the moon, all around the host of stars.

**329.** Having rinsed their mouths, all sat in their places;
prasada betel was brought and given there.

**330.** Joyfully they chewed the betel;
the devotees talk of intimate matters.

**331.** Each performs his own sadhana in his mind.
Chand Raya says: "Fortune [is mine] — this life is praiseworthy"[^59].

**332.** They brought and unloaded all the provisions from the boats
and stored them separately in the storerooms.

**333.** At night Devidasa and his kirtaniyas[^60]
began sankirtana before Gauranga.

**334.** How sweet the singing, [how sweet] the sound of the mridanga!
One feels like offering up[^61] one's very life.

**335.** Sri Thakura Mahashaya listens to the kirtana;
on his left is the Kaviraja, beautiful of form.

**336.** Krishnananda Raya with all his family
relishes the song in blissful rapture.

**337.** To his left are [Chand Raya], his father and brother;
as they listen, prema rises in their hearts.

**338.** *"Trembling and sweetness, and the artfulness of love —
seeing them, the heart breaks: I cannot forget!*

**339.** *Matchless sweetness, the artfulness of love —
not for a moment can I forget [them]"* (refrain)[^62].

**340.** *"When he, so shapely, goes off [with] the cows,
seeing and hearing [it], the soul longs to leave the body."*

**341.** Sri Thakura Mahashaya relishes [the song];
at that moment his soul weeps, and he sobs.

**342.** His body is seen to tremble like a palm [in the wind];
now his body swells, now it shrinks.

**343.** Tears flow from his eyes — what can I say of their measure?
Now and then he falls to the ground, lost to himself.

**344.** Krishnananda Raya and the others roll on the ground;
how much gold, silver, clothes and shawls they gave them!

**345.** Ramachandra Kaviraja was as if mad:
tears seemed to burst [from his eyes][^63].

**346.** The hair on his body [stood up] like the thorns of the shimul;
he lay there, [only] his heart pounding.

**347.** Chand Raya's father and brother, hearing this,
weep for a long while, rolling on the ground:

**348.** "O Creator, for so many days you kept [us] from this!
The soul pines — to whom shall we tell it?"

**349.** Saying this, they weep with bitter cries:
"Only now have we come to know [this] by Prabhu's grace!"

**350.** Trembling and trembling, they fall, not coming to themselves;
no words leave their lips, the soul is leaving [the body].

**351.** They do not know where they are or where they go;
overwhelmed by prema, now and then they faint.

**352.** What they say, what they do — [they only] cry, "Alas, alas!";
clutching the feet of father and brother, they roll [on the ground].

**353.** What can I say of the measure of what they gave?
They bow to the Thakura many, many times.

**354.** The moon of bhava rose in the palace[^64];
in the turmoil of bhava no one says anything to anyone.

**355.** The kirtana ended, and all sat in their places;
the Thakura was sunk in bhava and aware of nothing.

**356.** That night he remained in bhava, his heart overflowing;
the next day he came to himself somewhat.

**357.** Thus for ten nights, in the rasa of talk of Krishna,
beside themselves, they knew neither day nor night.

**358.** The next day Chand Raya took his leave
and with much humility said to the Thakura:

**359.** "What shall I, worthless, say? What else have I?
My only hope is your two feet.

**360.** It may seem strange, but [my] word is firm:
if I do not see Prabhu's feet, I shall give up my life"[^65].

**361.** The Thakura said: "Son, [here is] the strength of my mercy:
Sri Krishna's feet are true, and all else is false."

**362.** Saying this, he mercifully sent him off;
weeping and weeping, he goes to the Kaviraja.

**363.** He, embracing him, says: "Blessed is this life!
You have gained all perfection — you have the shelter of [those] feet."

**364.** [Chand Raya] gave [him] a hundred rupees and two cloths:
"Be very merciful to me, lowly one;

**365.** [grant] the mercy that I may never forget those two feet;
regard me as your own servant."

**366.** To all the Vaishnavas who had gone with Prabhu
he gave parting gifts, to each as was fitting.

**367.** He went and bowed at the feet of Gauranga,
took leave of everyone and set off.

**368.** The three boarded the boats and went to their home,
speaking of Prabhu's virtues and weeping.

**369.** The next day they reached home in great joy;
[in] sadhana and remembrance they float ever in prema.

**370.** Thus I have told of Prabhu's greatness;
by writing and telling, can I find its limit?

**371.** This wondrous event is beyond people's understanding;
whoever hears of it is overwhelmed with rapture.

**372.** Thus the two brothers live with care;
they never go against the command from Prabhu's blessed lips.

**373.** One day, at the time of a pilgrimage to bathe in the Ganga,
Chand Raya fearlessly set out.

**374.** Taking up to a hundred horsemen and four hundred men,
he set out with his father's consent.

**375.** Arriving, they all bathed in the Ganga together;
there he took care to feed [everyone].

**376.** At that time the Pathan's footmen were there[^66];
they went and reported everything as it was.

**377.** Then many sepoys came and surrounded [him];
they seized Chand Raya and bound him.

**378.** Seating him in a palanquin, they took him to the durbar;
all the way he said not another word.

**379.** To where the Nawab sat in anger,
all the men, surrounding him, brought him.

**380.** He went up and made his salaam; [the Nawab], seeing [him], laughed:
"What sort of hakim are you, to have plundered so much of the realm?"

**381.** Saying this, he had him whipped[^67] many times;
[Chand Raya] said nothing — in his heart was joy.

**382.** Smiling, he says: "This is a just punishment;
whatever fine[^68] is fitting, impose it, sir."

**383.** He was not killed; the order was: "Keep him in the cellar"[^69].
On reflection, this is death while still alive.

**384.** They took him and kept him there, [and he] fasted.
People told those at home how it had happened.

**385.** Father, mother and kin grieved at heart:
"Find a way for him to eat."

**386.** He writes a letter of entreaty to Prabhu;
hearing [of the trouble], the Thakura was much saddened at heart.

**387.** The man sent [made] friends with the jamadar[^70];
three knew [of it], and no one else knew.

**388.** Thus Chand Raya lives in the prison;
and here Raghavendra was utterly distraught:

**389.** "Is there anyone who will bring me back Chand Raya?
I will give him much wealth, [even] my very life"[^71].

**390.** At that time a certain man said to him:
"I will soon bring him to you," so he said.

**391.** [Raghavendra] said: "I will give a village, a horse and a robe of honour[^72];
if I do not see Chand Raya, my life will leave me."

**392.** He possessed a perfected mantra, which he knew in secret;
digging the earth and making a tunnel, he makes his way there.

**393.** To the place where Chand Raya was kept
he made his way and came up right before him.

**394.** Chand Raya said: "Brother, tell me,
how did you get here without coming to harm?"

**395.** He said: "Your father told me to:
by the power of my science I will take you home."

**396.** "How will you take me? What science do you have?"
"I will go in front, and you will go behind me.

**397.** I have a mantra of Mother Kali;
a mantra of two and a half syllables I will speak into your ear[^73].

**398.** By its power you will go — there is nothing more to fear;
come at once, do not hesitate."

**399.** The Raya said: "Brother, how much longer shall I live?
How many offences I have committed — what is my lot!

**400.** Thakura Mahashaya placed his foot on my head;
he is Prabhu and I am his servant, I tell you.

**401.** In his mercy he gave the mantra of Radha and Krishna into my ears;
shall I listen to another mantra? Shame on such a life!

**402.** Where else is there [such] a dwelling in hell?
Tell my father this request of mine:

**403.** that Prabhu, that mantra, the hope of those feet —
by that command may I dwell wherever it may be.

**404.** My mind is free of cares, to worship Krishna;
all the household duties — those are the heavy burden[^74].

**405.** Why does my father grieve at heart?
[My] only grief is that I do not see Prabhu's feet.

**406.** Let him not worry: my mind has become good;
the fortune is that my evil mind has not returned."

**407.** Saying this, he chants the Hari-nama, keeping count,
and at times, sitting, sings of Krishna's virtues.

**408.** He takes no thought for food; his thirst has left him;
at times he calls out with a bitter cry.

**409.** As Prabhu commanded, [he practises] the sadhana of remembrance;
his mind sank into it, and there are no other thoughts.

**410.** Whatever lila Radha and Krishna perform at whatever time,
so, in accordance with it, he contemplates in his heart.

**411.** Now he serves, gazing on [their] faces,
now he anoints [their] bodies with kunkuma,

**412.** now he fans [them], now he massages [their] feet —
thus his mind was absorbed in service.

**413.** [He prays:] "Lalita, Vishakha, Chitra, Champakalatika[^75],
show mercy to such a one as me — [give me] more service!

**414.** Together with your own [friends], cast a merciful glance on me,
that I may always serve and my mind remain in that.

**415.** Rupa, Rati, Lavanga, Guna Manjari, Manjulali[^76],
show such mercy that I may serve together with you.

**416.** My Prabhu Narottama — may I stay with him in that company,
and always, at [your] sign, be eager for service[^77].

**417.** Wherever your dwelling is, there is my service;
keep me there always as your companion."

**418.** Thus time passes in the sadhana of remembrance;
it turned out well — thus the net of maya fell away.

**419.** He does not notice the days and nights pass, remaining in ecstasy.
After two or four days — what happened at last?

**420.** One day the Nawab Sahib had [him] brought
and, filled with anger, questioned Chand Raya:

**421.** "You, Raya, pay no money; you have plundered the whole land;
what do you have left now? Only your life.

**422.** If you are killed, all the land's calamity will go" —
[so] resolving in his mind, he called the mahout[^78]:

**423.** "Bring the elephant here, maddened"[^79].
Many people sat down to watch the execution.

**424.** [Chand Raya's] feet are tightly fettered; he stands.
At that moment they brought that elephant, surrounding [it].

**425.** They brought the elephant before him — it will not stand still.
"Set the elephant on him, take his life!"

**426.** Then [Chand Raya] remembered Prabhu Narottama:
"No more shall I see those feet that grant fearlessness!"

**427.** They set the elephant on him; it seized him with its trunk
and at first threw him a little way off.

**428.** When the elephant in rage seized him again,
he then grasped its trunk with both hands.

**429.** Striking and pulling, he tore out the trunk;
with a scream the elephant crashed to the ground.

**430.** Seeing that the elephant had died, all the people,
pressing a hand [to their mouths], began to ponder[^80].

**431.** With fetters on his feet, Chand Raya stands in front;
the Nawab himself took him by the hand.

**432.** Sitting in the durbar, he asked him:
"How much strength do you have — you killed the elephant!"

**433.** Chand Raya said: "What strength have I?
I hold my Prabhu's command in my heart."

**434.** "Come, tell me how it was — I wish to hear."
He told him everything from beginning to end:

**435.** "When the Sahib had me seized and brought,
whipped and thrown into the cellar,

**436.** then I thought of the feet of my Prabhu;
my mind decided: this is not suffering, but great happiness[^81].

**437.** You yourself never inquired [about me] again;
dying of hunger, I fed on the name of Krishna.

**438.** My father, out of love for his son, sent a man
and wrote to the mirdha to have me fed[^82].

**439.** He secretly gives me a little to eat —
how much life can one keep with that?[^83]

**440.** For so many days I was a prisoner and did not know it as suffering:
the prison was no prison — a greater happiness than home.

**441.** Now that I have been brought to be killed —
what strength have I? Prabhu holds the strength.

**442.** When the elephant, not killing me, threw me aside,
at that moment I remembered Prabhu's feet.

**443.** When the elephant came and seized me [again],
I gripped its trunk tightly with both hands.

**444.** Knowing this, I pulled with all my might: why should I die?
Prabhu knows this — who else knows it?

**445.** Hear one more request, sir:
if you will not take my life, if you permit [me to speak]…"

**446.** "Speak, have no fear in your mind."
All the people say [the same] and look into his face.

**447.** "My father sent a man to me;
by the power of perfected science he made a tunnel underneath

**448.** and came out where I was.
Seeing him, I asked him something:

**449.** 'How did you get here, brother, without coming to harm?'
He told me he had a perfected science:

**450.** 'I have a mantra of Mother Kali; by its power I came;
by this way I will take you — such is the stratagem.

**451.** I will give that mantra into your ear;
I will go in front, and you will go behind.'

**452.** Hearing those words, I nearly lost my life.
Here is what I have to say:

**453.** Prabhu gave [me] one mantra for deliverance;
giving that mantra into my ears, he bought me.

**454.** What [else] should I hear with my ears? Shame on such a life!
After how many sins I obtained his feet!

**455.** 'Tell my father this request of mine:
I know only Prabhu's feet and nothing else.'

**456.** This, sir, hear — it is what my heart has resolved;
at your command I have spoken it without fear."

**457.** Softened, the Nawab embraced him;
all who were there prostrated themselves [before him].

**458.** At once a horse was brought and a robe of honour bestowed:
"Go home right now — no one has any claim on you.

**459.** Enjoy your own domain; I renounce everything;
I have no jurisdiction[^84] over it at all, I tell you."

**460.** At once [they issued] a dastak and the Padshah's writ;
reading the paper, [Chand Raya] rejoiced at heart.

**461.** The order was given to the munshi: "Your land,
which the Padshah had especially taken under [his] control —

**462.** [it] was given [to you] under the panja seal together with a parwana";
all the mutsuddis came to carry it out[^85].

**463.** Taking leave, the Raya sets off for home:
until he goes home, [his family's] worry will not depart.

**464.** [But he thought:] "He at whose feet I took shelter gave me this state;
to see those feet — that is my hope."

**465.** He sent a man, wrote a letter to his father,
and wrote to his brother to come quickly:

**466.** "I have been released; I would have gone home,
[but first] I shall see Prabhu with joy at heart.

**467.** You two, taking many gifts,
come at once to see Prabhu.

**468.** We shall all meet before Prabhu;
come quickly, so that there is not a danda's delay."

**469.** The man came, gave the letter and told the Raya [everything];
as soon as he had read the letter, he rose in haste.

**470.** Hearing [this], Santosha Raya was overjoyed;
with many gifts and men he set out quickly.

**471.** Meanwhile Chand Raya went to Kheturi;
leaving his horse, he then went on foot.

**472.** The men whom [the Thakura] had earlier given him [as escort]
ran and reported to Prabhu.

**473.** The Thakura was sitting there with the Kaviraja;
the Raya came near — [the Thakura] saw him with his own eyes.

**474.** The Thakura, together with the Kaviraja, rejoiced:
"Such is Gauranga's play — who can understand it?"

**475.** At that time, [while] Chand Raya [was] at the Sri Rasa-mandala[^86],
all the household came and told the Thakura.

**476.** Then Chand Raya bows down;
his body thrills, tears flow from his eyes.

**477.** He bowed many times, saying nothing;
the Thakura rose, came and embraced him.

**478.** Seating him, he asked about everything.
"My release is only [by the mercy of] Prabhu's feet."

**479.** He tells everything as it happened, from beginning to end;
having heard, the Thakura placed his foot on Chand's head.

**480.** After a while people came and say:
"An army[^87] has come to the village," so they reported.

**481.** [It was], as it turned out, Raghavendra Raya with his son,
who, having heard [everything], had come to see Prabhu.

**482.** At once they went to the Thakura;
father and son bow down and offer much praise.

**483.** The Thakura showed them mercy, placing his hand on their backs;
[there] Chand Raya saw [them] before Prabhu.

**484.** Father and son, brother and brother greeted one another;
embracing, they wept much.

**485.** Chand Raya said to his father's face:
"Why did you bring misfortune on yourself by this [deed]?

**486.** Why did you send a man to take me away?" —
and he told before all how everything had happened.

**487.** The Thakura, smiling, says, looking at Chand Raya:
"Such delightful words — for the ear [they are] life and death"[^88].

**488.** Ashamed, Raghavendra bows down:
"Forgive the offence, be merciful!"

**489.** The father is afraid of Chand Raya at heart:
"Forgive the offence, look kindly on me!"

**490.** Father and son, speaking, took each other's hands and wept:
"We have sold ourselves to these feet — he has bought [us] with all our line."

**491.** For five days they beheld [the Thakura], the kirtana and dancing;
the next day they made their request at Prabhu's feet,

**492.** took leave and went to their own land, home.
[Chand Raya] rules, keeping Prabhu's command in his heart.

**493.** After some days there came from the Nawab
an order: "Where is Chand Raya?" — so he commanded[^89].

**494.** Runners went and brought Chand Raya;
with a large army he came to the meeting.

**495.** Arriving, he met the Nawab,
who handed over to him the Ahidi pargana[^90].

**496.** That day he stayed there, and in the morning [received] leave:
"In body, mind and speech no one has any claim on you."

**497.** Having received Ahidi, the Raya goes home;
an army goes with him, music plays.

**498.** Hear, brothers, how bhajana to Sri Krishna is done;
seeing and hearing it all, realize [it].

**499.** Of the virtues of Sri Thakura Mahashaya [I have told] only a trifle;
I have no ability to describe them in detail.

**500.** How he brought prema from Vrindavana,
so that all the souls on earth were flooded!

**501.** Such bhakti of those who possess nothing as the shastras describe —
such a one who possessed nothing was Thakura Mahashaya.

**502.** Wherever there is the business of reproach,
pride and envy are mixed into it[^91].

**503.** As the guru is, so is his disciple:
by mere touch virtue is born — the essence of great jewels.

**504.** Such is the way of sadhana, such the bhajana of the disciples;
seeing and hearing [it], the mind is amazed.

**505.** [Through] the Acharya Thakura and Thakura Mahashaya
the prema of Chaitanya and Nitai arose.

**506.** How many [souls] were saved through them all —
[yet] it has not touched me: grief rises in my heart.

**507.** The Acharya Thakura showed mercy to Vira Hambira;
Thakura Mahashaya delivered Chand Raya.

**508.** Singing [their] virtues, I bow to all
whose bhajana is the two feet of Radhika,

**509.** on whose neck the doctrine of Sri Rupa is a necklace,
whose bhajana is the cherished desire of Gauranga's heart.

**510.** Of all who became the Acharya Thakura's disciples
I shall describe some, the most prominent.

**511.** The branches of Thakura Mahashaya I shall describe briefly;
little by little all the branches will become [known][^92].

**512.** One whose all is the feet of Sri Chaitanya, Nityananda and Advaita —
he obtains this wealth.

**513.** I write this at Prabhu's command;
as Prabhu's command was, so I wrote it down in her presence.

**514.** Whose virtues Prabhu named with her blessed lips —
those I too write down, to purify my mind.

**515.** Sri Gopala Bhatta and Sri Lokanatha — these two;
Srinivasa and Narottama — purifiers of the fallen.

**516.** However many their virtues — how many can be written?
I have written a little, according to my realization.

**517.** I make my appeal to all the Vaishnava hearers:
he will find happiness whose treasure of life is Gaura.

**518.** Let no one count what is said here as an offence of mine:
[this] was told in [my] presence by a certain Sri Guru-Vaishnava[^93].

**519.** I write it at command; if anyone reviles it,
let him know: there is no offence on my part.

**520.** Whoever accepts it incurs no offence;
but if the Gosani's command is broken, the work is in vain.

**521.** He whose hope is the feet of Sri Jahnava and Virachandra,
Nityananda Dasa, tells the Prema-vilasa.

*Thus ends the eighteenth vilasa of the Prema-vilasa.*
