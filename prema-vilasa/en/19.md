# Vilasa 19. The Six Deities of Kheturi; Rupa Narayana; Virachandra's Travels

*Ramachandra Kaviraja brings Srinivasa back from samadhi (Radha's nose-ring); Shyamananda converts Sher Khan and the yogi Damodara; the passing of Gadadhara Dasa and Narahari, the memorial festivals at Katwa and Khanda; Jahnava's return from Vrindavana; the vanishing of the Kheturi deities, the finding of Gauranga's image in Vipradasa's granary and the abhisheka of the six deities on Phalguna Purnima; Mahaprabhu and his companions appear in the kirtana; Madhava Acharya, author of the Krishna-mangala, and Madhava Acharya, husband of Ganga; wonders on Jahnava's journey to Vrindavana; the service at Kheturi; the deliverance of Gurudasa Bhattacharya, Jagannatha Acharya and Chand Raya's former soldiers; the story of the pandita Rupa Narayana and the conversion of Raja Narasimha; Narottama reveals the sacred thread in his heart; Virachandra heals the blind Ramai, travels to Nilachala and Vrindavana and returns to Khardaha*

**1.** Glory, glory to Sri Chaitanya! Glory to Nityananda!
Glory to Advaitachandra! Glory to the devotees of Gaura!

**2.** Glory, glory to Srinivasa Acharya Thakura!
Glory, glory to Shyamananda, full of the rasa of prema!

**3.** Glory, glory to Narottama Thakura Mahashaya!
Glory, glory to Ramachandra, the abode of virtues!

**4.** Now I shall tell a little of the greatness of Ramachandra,
whose bhajana and grasp of truth have no equal.

**5.** One day Srinivasa Acharya Mahashaya
was staying in Vana-Vishnupur, in the king's house.

**6.** His two wives were with him;
understanding [his] signs, they did the work at once[^1].

**7.** Having bathed and so on, he sat on his seat
and began the worship of his chosen deity.

**8.** His own siddha name is Sri Mani Manjari;
in his mind he contemplated the blessed abode of Vrindavana.

**9.** Absorbed in meditation, he entered samadhi,
and then the lila of Radha and Krishna became visible to him.

**10.** He sees Radha and Krishna with all the sakhis
merrily sporting in the waters of the Yamuna.

**11.** In the water play Sri Radhika became quite carried away:
the ring fell from her nose, and she did not notice[^2].

**12.** When they had finished playing, [all] came up onto the bank,
and each began to put on her own clothes and ornaments.

**13.** Then Sri Rupa Manjari looked at Radha
and did not see the ring in her nose.

**14.** Sri Rupa Manjari signalled to Guna Manjari
and said, "Find the nose-ring and bring it quickly."

**15.** Sri Guna Manjari, understanding the hint,
said to Mani Manjari with a smile:

**16.** "Search in the waters of the Yamuna
and bring back Srimati's ornament."

**17.** Having said this, all the sakhis went to the kunja,
while Sri Mani Manjari began to search.

**18.** She searched for a long time and cannot find it;
she looks here and there, and her heart is troubled.

**19.** Here meanwhile the two wives of Acharya Thakura,
seeing that his meditation did not break, grew anxious.

**20.** The day went by, evening came, night fell;
however loudly they called out the Hari-nama —

**21.** there is no breath in or out, the body does not stir;
seeing this, both were seized with fear[^3].

**22.** The day went by, night came, and there is no consciousness;
seeing this, both weep aloud.

**23.** The king heard of all this
and came in haste to see his master.

**24.** Hearing of it, Vyasa Acharya, Sri Krishnavallabha
and the other devotees also came to see.

**25.** Looking closely at Acharya Thakura's body,
they called to mind the states of Mahaprabhu[^4].

**26.** The night passed, day came, the third prahara arrived[^5] —
and still Prabhu's body did not stir.

**27.** Seeing this, the two wives of the Acharya
began to weep loudly.

**28.** The king and all the devotees grew downcast;
"What has happened? What has happened?" they say, and their minds are not at rest.

**29.** The devotees examined Prabhu's body at length
and understood that there was no cause to fear harm.

**30.** All of them comforted the two wives of the guru;
then a thought arose in the Ishvari's mind[^6]:

**31.** "Ramachandra Kaviraja is Prabhu's power;
if he saw him, he would understand what state Prabhu is in."

**32.** The Ishvari said, "O, listen, Maharaja:
bring Ramachandra quickly, do not delay."

**33.** [The king] arranged to have Ramachandra brought;
meanwhile the night ended and morning came.

**34.** And Ramachandra, [wishing] to see Prabhu,
came to the king's house at daybreak.

**35.** His arrival was announced to the Ishvari,
and the king took the Kaviraja into the inner apartments.

**36.** From a distance he bowed to the feet of his Prabhu
and touched his head to the feet of both of Prabhu's wives.

**37.** Looking at Prabhu, Ramachandra said, "There is nothing to worry about:
after a while the Gosani will regain outer consciousness."

**38.** Having said this, Ramachandra sat down in meditation
and in his siddha body contemplated his chosen deity.

**39.** His own siddha name is Sri Karuna Manjari;
in that body he went to the abode of Radha and Krishna.

**40.** Bowing to Radha and Krishna and to all the sakhis,
he went to the bank of the Yamuna.

**41.** He sees Sri Mani Manjari standing in the water;
without delay he too went down into the Yamuna.

**42.** He sees the nose-ring lying covered by a lotus leaf;
he picked it up and quickly gave it into Mani Manjari's hand.

**43.** Getting the nose-ring, Sri Mani Manjari was delighted
and said, "Friend, let us go to the kunja, quickly!"

**44.** From there they went to the kunja
and handed the nose-ring to Guna Manjari.

**45.** Guna Manjari gave it into Rupa Manjari's hand,
and Rupa Manjari put it on Radha's nose.

**46.** A lovely form, and on it clothes and ornaments —
whoever sees the Divine Couple has his mind stolen.

**47.** Having beheld the sweet form of the Divine Couple,
Ramachandra regained outer consciousness and then rose.

**48.** Calling out the name of Hari, he began to recite praises;
and, regaining outer consciousness, Srinivasa rose and sat up.

**49.** "What a form I have seen!" he says, and weeps;
embracing Ramachandra, he opened his eyes.

**50.** Ramachandra fell at the feet of his Prabhu,
and all the devotees together cried, "Hari! Hari!"

**51.** Then Sri Ishvari and Sri Gauranga-priya
went with glad hearts and cooked.

**52.** Many kinds of rice and dishes were cooked;
having offered them [to the deity], the Acharya ate.

**53.** From Prabhu's plate Ramachandra took prasada;
afterwards all the devotees ate prasada.

**54.** Having rinsed their mouths, all came to rest
and spent the night in talk of Krishna.

**55.** The greatness of Ramachandra Kaviraja is boundless;
the little I have described is according to Prabhu's words.

**56.** Now I shall write a little of the greatness of Shyamananda,
to whom even the gods cannot find a limit.

**57.** From Vraja, Shyamananda, by way of Gauda,
passing through Garerhat, came to Ambika.

**58.** With great joy he beheld Mahaprabhu
and prostrated himself before Hridaya Chaitanya[^7].

**59.** He related everything that had happened in Vrindavana;
hearing it, he was greatly gladdened.

**60.** Hearing of the theft of the books, he was grieved[^8];
for some time Shyamananda stayed there.

**61.** After a while he received a letter
and, seeing the news that the books had been found, rejoiced.

**62.** Then, taking leave of his Sri Guru,
he went to his own country, Utkala.

**63.** Coming to his native village of Dharenda[^9],
he revealed prema-bhakti in all its fullness.

**64.** He spread the nama-sankirtana
and delivered many robbers and pashandis.

**65.** One day Shyamananda with the sankirtana
was going about to various places with a joyful heart.

**66.** There was a deputy of the king, a Pathan named Sher Khan;
hearing the sankirtana, he burned with ceaseless anger.

**67.** He forbids the performance of sankirtana —
Shyamananda does not listen and carries on the sankirtana.

**68.** In anger that robber yavana, taking [other] yavanas with him,
smashed the khols and karatalas and threw them away.

**69.** Then Shyamananda in anger gave a terrible roar,
and fear entered the hearts of all the yavanas.

**70.** The beards and moustaches of the yavanas were all burnt off;
they vomited blood, and all were prostrated.

**71.** Shyamananda then went to his own place,
and all [of them] too went off to their own places.

**72.** The next day Shyamananda, with great pomp,
gathered many bands of sankirtana.

**73.** Performing kirtana through various places,
all went along full of joy.

**74.** Seeing this, the robber yavana Sher Khan came in haste
and bowed many times at Shyamananda's feet:

**75.** "O Shyamananda Prabhu, have mercy on me!
I have committed an offence — give me shelter at your feet."

**76.** What befell him for breaking up the sankirtana
he began to relate in brief:

**77.** "My beard was burnt, blood came from my nose and mouth;
[but] when I tell of the dream, my heart weeps.

**78.** First I saw a terrible form;
it struck me on the cheek and said, 'Hey, you wretched yavana!

**79.** I am your Allah, Ahad incarnate[^10]' —
and with these words it showed a golden form:

**80.** 'My name is Sri Chaitanya, the refuge of all;
Shyamananda is my great devotee.

**81.** From him you will take the mantra of Krishna,
or else you are bound for hell.'

**82.** I saw a wondrous form — my eyes cannot contain it;
the tears of my eyes cannot be stopped.

**83.** You, Prabhu, are the teacher of the world; have mercy on me:
there is none so low as I — give me the shade of your feet."

**84.** Thus he pleaded humbly in many ways;
seeing his humility, Shyamananda showed him favour.

**85.** All this I heard from the lips of my Prabhu
and, taking her command upon my head, have described it[^11].

**86.** Having delivered the yavana, Shyamananda went to Rayani
and, going there, spread prema-bhakti.

**87.** That village lies on the bank of the river Suvarnarekha;
there lives a raja named Achyutananda.

**88.** He has two sons, named Rasika and Murari[^12];
Shyamananda showed them great mercy.

**89.** In Balarampur, in Sri Nrisimhapur
and in Gopivallabhapur he made many disciples.

**90.** In Gopivallabhapur he gave out prema abundantly
and entrusted the service of Sri Govinda to Rasika[^13].

**91.** The greatness of Rasikananda is boundless;
he delivered many robber yavanas.

**92.** His many disciples cannot be counted;
[let] whoever is fortunate describe them.

**93.** One day Shyamananda in Gopivallabhapur
was sitting together with the devotees.

**94.** At that time an eminent sannyasi came,
and he had a long debate with Shyamananda.

**95.** This yogi, a Vedantist, knew many shastras;
the debate with Shyamananda went on for many days.

**96.** [Shyamananda] refuted the yogi's advaita-vada in debate
and by the teaching of the Gosvamis established dvaita.

**97.** In the debate the yogi was defeated
and inwardly praised Shyamananda highly.

**98.** At night the yogi had a dream:
Shyamananda is a jewel among great personalities.

**99.** He had been a cowherd, a gopa, and became a brahmana[^14] —
such is the power of bhajana, as everyone knows.

**100.** The next day the yogi, rising in the morning,
came and fell at Shyamananda's feet:

**101.** "There is no sinner in the world as low as I;
have mercy and deliver me, a sinner."

**102.** Then Shyamananda, a jewel among great personalities,
placed his feet on the yogi's head.

**103.** In his mercy he gave him initiation into the mantra of Krishna
and explained all the rules of sadhana.

**104.** That yogi's name is Damodara;
he became devoted to the bhajana of Sri Krishna.

**105.** One day, when Shyamananda was in seclusion,
Damodara came and prostrated himself before him.

**106.** He sees Shyamananda's form shining brilliantly,
and a radiant sacred thread glittering on his body.

**107.** At that moment Rasika and the other devotees came;
prostrating themselves, they offered many praises.

**108.** Shyamananda, hiding the sacred thread
and concealing his radiance, began the nama-sankirtana.

**109.** This Mahashaya is filled with the avesha of Advaita Prabhu[^15];
in many ways he distributes prema-bhakti to people.

**110.** Thus he delivered a multitude of pashandis
and gave [them] prema-bhakti.

**111.** Shyamananda's bhajana has no equal;
his siddha name is Kanaka Manjari.

**112.** What do I know of Shyamananda's many deeds?
And the little I have written, I wrote in obedience to the guru's command.

**113.** Listen, listen, listeners, be attentive:
give heed to what I shall now relate.

**114.** The great festivals that took place at Katwa and at Khanda
I did not describe before — now I shall describe them all[^16].

**115.** The Thakurani commanded me to describe them;
the guru's command is the strongest — I took it to heart.

**116.** When they heard that Vishnupriya Thakurani had disappeared [from the world],
the grief of the devotees was beyond words[^17].

**117.** Then Gadadhara Dasa and Narahari Sarakara —
how much both of them grieved I cannot say.

**118.** Little by little Gadadhara Dasa grew very weak
and within a short time vanished from the world.

**119.** He disappeared on the krishna-ashtami of the month of Karttika;
Yadunandana and the other devotees grieved greatly.

**120.** Hearing that Prabhu Gadadhara Dasa had disappeared,
Narahari Sarakara lamented long.

**121.** Raghunandana, Sulochana — all the devotees there were —
with the tears of them all the earth was soaked.

**122.** Thus Narahari, stricken with grief,
one day vanished from everyone's sight —

**123.** on the krishna-ekadashi of the month of Agrahayana;
seeing his departure, all weep.

**124.** How Raghunandana and Sulochana grieved
I cannot describe even in small part.

**125.** By Prabhu's will Raghunandana recovered
and resolved to go to Katwa.

**126.** Taking Lochana with him, Sri Raghunandana
came to the town of Katwa[^18].

**127.** Sri Yadunandana Chakravarti Mahashaya
is a disciple of Gadadhara Dasa, very dear to him.

**128.** To him Sri Raghunandana went;
seeing Sri Gauranga, he rejoiced greatly[^19].

**129.** Many times he prostrated himself in obeisance,
[and then] went to Yadunandana.

**130.** Embracing, both bowed down full length
and, speaking of the departure [of their masters], wept long.

**131.** By Prabhu's will both grew calm
and, sitting together, fixed the day of the festival.

**132.** Then, having arranged everything for the festival here,
Raghunandana went to Khanda, remembering Prabhu's feet.

**133.** There too preparations for the festival began,
and letters of invitation were sent everywhere.

**134.** Gadadhara Dasa and Thakura Narahari —
for both there will be a great memorial festival[^20].

**135.** All the mahantas received two invitations
and went to the town of Katwa in great joy.

**136.** Some days beforehand Raghunandana gladly
came to Katwa together with Lochana and others.

**137.** Raghunandana and the others set to work
and put everything in particular order.

**138.** Now I shall tell of the arrival of the mahantas;
I give only an indication — it cannot all be described.

**139.** As many branches of Sri Mahaprabhu as came,
I shall only name them, each one separately.

**140.** Sripati, Srinidhi, Vaninatha, Vasu, Kavichandra,
Ramadasa, Sanjaya and Vidyananda came[^21];

**141.** Kamalakanta, Vishnudasa, Sri Chandrashekhara;
Chaitanya Dasa and Sridhara the kirtaniya came[^22];

**142.** Nayana Pandita and Kavi Karnapura,
Janakinatha, Gopala Dasa, Acharya Purandara.

**143.** All the branches of Sri Nityananda Prabhu came too;
I shall name a few according to my understanding.

**144.** Murari, Chaitanya Dasa, Raghunatha Vaidya,
Upadhyaya Narayana — and I, the unfortunate[^23];

**145.** Sanatana, Krishnadasa and Manohara,
Nakadi, Gopala Dasa and Mahidhara;

**146.** Ramachandra Kaviraja[^24], Vasanta, Lavani,
Hariharananda, Kanu Thakura, a jewel of virtues;

**147.** Ramasena, Jnanadasa and Damodara;
Sri Kumuda and Pitambara came;

**148.** Nrisimha Chaitanya and Vrindavana Dasa,
who brought forth the Sri Chaitanya-mangala[^25];

**149.** Prabhu Virachandra; Madhava Acharya, a jewel of virtues,
whose wife is Ganga, the daughter of Nityananda;

**150.** two Mahashayas came, Jagannatha and Madhava,
who are called Jagai and Madhai.

**151.** Thus I have named the companions of Nityananda Prabhu;
now I shall tell of the arrival of the companions of Advaita.

**152.** Vanamali Dasa, Vijaya, Lokanatha Pandita,
Bholanatha, Hridayananda Sena, Murari Pandita;

**153.** Kanu Pandita, Sri Haridasa Brahmachari,
Krishnadasa, Janardana Dasa, worthy of bhakti;

**154.** Ananta Dasa, Narayana, the excellent Yadava Dasa,
Haricharana, Raghunatha, Sri Rama Acharya.

**155.** Sri Madhava Acharya came, full of the rasa of bhakti,
whose singing of the Krishna-mangala is supremely sweet[^26];

**156.** Achyutananda, Krishna Mishra, Prabhu Sri Gopala —
the sons of Advaita Prabhu, full of compassion.

**157.** All the branches of Gadadhara Pandita Gosani came;
I shall name a few according to my understanding[^27]:

**158.** Chaitanya-vallabha Dasa, Bhagavata Acharya,
Pushpa Gopala, Gopala Dasa, Sri Hari Acharya,

**159.** Sri Harsha, Raghu Mishra and Lakshminatha,
Jagannatha of Kashthakata and Raghunatha[^28].

**160.** The Pandita Gosani's brother is Vaninatha;
his son is Nayanananda Mishra Mahashaya,

**161.** the Pandita Gosani's disciple and his power;
he too came to Katwa with gladness in his heart.

**162.** As many devotees came — who can count them?
I have given only a general indication.

**163.** All the mahantas and adhikaris there were in various places —
all came, filled with great delight.

**164.** All beheld the place where Prabhu took sannyasa[^29],
and tears flowed ceaselessly from their eyes.

**165.** From there they went to the courtyard of the temple
and, seeing the image of Sri Gauranga, rejoiced at heart.

**166.** With glad hearts they prostrate themselves
and, full of delight, began the sankirtana.

**167.** All the mahantas dance in boundless bliss;
tears of prema stream unceasingly from their eyes.

**168.** Then, having seen the food offering and the arati,
all took prasada with joyful hearts.

**169.** After staying some days at Katwa,
they set out for Khanda in boundless bliss.

**170.** Raghunandana, having come to Khanda some days earlier,
had gladly put the arrangements in order.

**171.** All the mahantas came to Khanda,
and each was given lodging in a fitting place.

**172.** All the mahantas, staying some days at Khanda,
held a great festival and were very happy.

**173.** One day in the sankirtana all the mahantas
dance and sing in perfect bliss.

**174.** At that time a blind man came there
and by the mercy of Prabhu Virachandra received his sight.

**175.** Crying, "Blessed! Blessed!" all rejoiced;
I shall relate it in detail further on.

**176.** For some days the mahantas stayed there,
and then, taking leave, went to their homes.

**177.** Having seen off the mahantas, Sri Raghunandana —
how much sorrow he felt cannot be told.

**178.** What shall I write? What comes first and what after, I cannot judge;
I only write, taking the Thakurani's command upon my head[^30].

**179.** Listen, listen, listeners, with one mind:
now I shall describe the deeds of Narottama.

**180.** The festival held at the manifestation of Sri Gauranga and Sri Vallavikanta
I have described in detail[^31].

**181.** Afterwards I only named the six deities;
[now] the guru has commanded me to describe the second abhisheka.

**182.** What was the order of the abhisheka of the six deities
I shall now describe — all will be pleased.

**183.** O listeners, all give heed:
[I shall tell] all the reasons for the second abhisheka.

**184.** All this I shall describe by the Ishvari's command,
contemplating her feet in the sky of my heart.

**185.** All that I saw with my own eyes I shall describe —
that which pleased all the mahantas.

**186.** For the second time the Ishvari with her companions
came back from Vrindavana again to Kheturi[^32].

**187.** And I was with the Ishvari at every moment:
never did I leave these feet.

**188.** The Mahashaya, hearing of the Thakurani's arrival,
came with the Kaviraja to meet her.

**189.** Seeing the Thakurani, Narottama and Ramachandra
fell to the ground, prostrating themselves.

**190.** Having bowed, they asked after her welfare
and carried her in a palanquin to their home.

**191.** The Thakurani, before Sri Gauranga and Sri Vallavikanta-raya,
prostrates herself with a joyful heart.

**192.** Looking at the sacred images, she was overcome with prema;
tears ran down her face.

**193.** After a while the Devi grew calm
and, having bathed and performed her daily rites, took prasada.

**194.** Sri Ishvari rested a while,
washed her face and sat on her seat.

**195.** Ramachandra and Narottama came;
having bowed, both sat on their seats.

**196.** Talk of Vrindavana began;
she gave Narottama Lokanatha's blessing.

**197.** Hearing his own Prabhu's blessing, the Mahashaya,
remembering his feet, wept bitterly.

**198.** She gave Ramachandra the blessing of Gopala Bhatta;
he, remembering his feet, began to weep.

**199.** Having conveyed the blessings of Jiva Gosani and others,
Sri Ishvari showed favour to them both.

**200.** Having stayed happily at Kheturi two to four days,
the Ishvari went from there to Yajigrama.

**201.** Hearing of the Ishvari's arrival, Srinivasa
came out in great joy to meet her.

**202.** Bowing at Sri Ishvari's feet,
he gladly brought her to his house.

**203.** When bathing, eating and the rest were done,
the talk of Vrindavana began.

**204.** She gave Srinivasa the blessing of Bhatta Gosani;
remembering Prabhu's feet, he began to weep.

**205.** Learning all about Jiva Gosvami and the others,
he rejoiced at heart, and tears of joy flowed from his eyes.

**206.** Having stayed some two days at Yajigrama, the Ishvari
soon came to Khardaha.

**207.** Hearing that the Ishvari had returned from Vrindavana,
the people of Khardaha rejoiced.

**208.** So the Thakurani went to Khardaha;
and here a certain feeling arose in Narottama.

**209.** One day the Mahashaya, having finished the evening arati,
was gazing at the two sacred images.

**210.** Looking at the images without the Beloved[^33],
he felt a divine feeling awaken in his heart:

**211.** "Will such a blessed day ever come for me,
when I shall see with these eyes the images of the Divine Couple?

**212.** How great will be the joy if I see the Divine Couple —
I shall not be able to put it into words.

**213.** Today there has arisen in my heart [the wish]
to install further images of Krishna together with the Beloved.

**214.** Seeing the household of Sri Krishna[^34],
let the body be refreshed, let eyes and mind be purified.

**215.** When will my Prabhu bring about such a day for me,
that, seeing Krishna's household, I may be immersed in bliss?"

**216.** Thinking thus, the Mahashaya became distraught;
bereft of outer consciousness, he passed the greater part of the night.

**217.** By Prabhu's will sleep overcame him,
and in a dream Bhagavan appeared to him.

**218.** Gauranga and Vallavikanta, smiling, said,
"O Narottama, your desire is fulfilled.

**219.** You have resolved to install further images —
seeing Krishna's household, you will float in bliss[^35].

**220.** To fulfil the desires of devotees is my task;
you are a supreme devotee, your desire will be fulfilled.

**221.** Behold, O Narottama:
I have assumed six forms, together with the Beloved.

**222.** These six forms you are to install;
I shall tell you their names — listen.

**223.** Gauranga, Vallavikanta, and also Sri Krishna,
Vraja-mohana, Radha-ramana, Radha-kanta — these are the six.

**224.** O Narottama, in the forms of Gauranga and Vallavikanta
I have dwelt in your house with great joy.

**225.** [Now] these two forms of mine have vanished,
and the temple now stands empty.

**226.** Quickly bring forth the six deities again —
seeing them, all people will rejoice.

**227.** At the time of the abhisheka of the six deities
these two forms of mine will be united [with them]:

**228.** Gaura-raya will merge into Gauranga,
Vallavikanta will become one with Vallavikanta[^36].

**229.** In these six forms I shall reside;
whoever sees them — all living beings will be saved."

**230.** Having said this, Bhagavan vanished,
and at that very moment Narottama woke and sat up.

**231.** At the sight of Bhagavan he was beside himself with bliss,
and the grief of losing sight of him had no limit.

**232.** Then came the time of the mangala-arati,
and the Mahashaya came to the temple doors.

**233.** Ramachandra Kaviraja met him there;
the pujari opened the doors with a glad heart.

**234.** They see there are no sacred images in the temple!
"What has happened? What has happened?" they cry, deeply pained.

**235.** Seeing the empty shrine, the Mahashaya began to weep,
and Ramachandra Kaviraja was grief-stricken.

**236.** Then the sound of weeping arose:
all weep in distress, and the earth was soaked [with tears].

**237.** By Prabhu's will the Mahashaya grew calm
and little by little calmed everyone[^37].

**238.** He told Ramachandra his dream:
"Make arrangements for the abhisheka of the six deities.

**239.** Have Acharya Thakura brought from Vishnupura
and gladly do all that is fitting."

**240.** Having said this, he then told the pujari,
"Worship the two deities in the shalagrama[^38].

**241.** Until the deities appear again,
until then worship [them] in the shalagrama."

**242.** Having said this, he was sitting with Ramachandra —
and at that moment a letter from the Acharya came.

**243.** Receiving the letter, Narottama was glad;
in the letter is written: "The Acharya is going to Vrindavana."

**244.** Learning that the Acharya had gone to Vrindavana,
he was ever anxious, and his heart had no rest.

**245.** One day Narottama said to Ramachandra,
"Go to Vrindavana and bring back the Acharya."

**246.** Then Ramachandra Kavi[raja] went to Vrindavana,
and Narottama set out for Nilachala.

**247.** He beheld Jagannatha and the places of Mahaprabhu's lila,
and then went on to Shyamananda.

**248.** Having stayed [there] some time, he went to Gauda
and travelled through Khardaha, Shantipur, Ambika.

**249.** By way of Navadvipa, Khanda, the town of Katwa
and Ekachakra he came home[^39].

**250.** Coming home, he resolved to install the deities,
and at night Narottama saw a dream:

**251.** Gauranga, Vallavikanta, and also Sri Krishna,
Vraja-mohana, Radha-kanta, Radha-ramana — these six.

**252.** How his heart rejoiced on seeing
the six deities together with the Beloved cannot be described.

**253.** Having seen the dream, Narottama woke and sat up
and, shedding tears of joy, passed [the rest of] the night.

**254.** At daybreak, having done his morning duties,
he made great preparations for fashioning the deities.

**255.** Stone was brought, craftsmen were summoned,
and he had six deities carved together with the Beloved[^40].

**256.** The five images of Krishna turned out excellent,
but the image of Gaura would not take shape properly.

**257.** However hard they try, the image will not form;
seeing this, Thakura Mahashaya grew very anxious.

**258.** "Gauranga! Gauranga!" he cries aloud;
and in a dream Sri Chaitanya appeared to him.

**259.** At night the Mahashaya saw a dream:
Sri Chaitanya, sitting at his head, says softly:

**260.** "Listen attentively, child Narottama:
however hard you try, my image will not take shape.

**261.** In this image I shall not reside;
I shall tell you of an image that I made myself.

**262.** Before my sannyasa I fashioned an image of myself
and, without anyone knowing, sank it in the Ganga.

**263.** You are the embodiment of my prema; to you I show favour:
I have placed that deity in Vipradasa's granary[^41]."

**264.** Having said this, Sri Chaitanya vanished;
waking, Narottama sees that day has dawned.

**265.** Rising and doing his morning duties, the Mahashaya
asks people where Vipradasa's house is.

**266.** A man came up to Narottama and said,
"Vipradasa is a rich man who lives in these parts.

**267.** He has much grain — paddy, mustard and the rest;
he is always trading in grain."

**268.** Hearing this, Narottama went to his house;
seeing the Mahashaya, Vipradasa bows down.

**269.** He says, "Why have you come here?"
The Mahashaya says, "There is a particular need."

**270.** Narottama says, "I shall go into your granary."
Vipradasa says, "That must not be done:

**271.** there is the danger of many kinds of snakes;
when they see a man, they hiss loudly.

**272.** For fear of the snakes no one can go in there;
there is much paddy [lying] there from long ago."

**273.** Narottama says, "Do not worry at all:
when I go in, all the snakes will flee."

**274.** Having said this, Narottama went into the granary,
and the snakes then vanished.

**275.** From the granary he lifted out the image of Chaitanya;
seeing it, all the people were rid of every distress.

**276.** From then on the fear of snakes ceased,
and joy blossomed in Vipradasa's heart.

**277.** Then Vipradasa with his whole family came
and took refuge at the feet of Thakura Mahashaya.

**278.** Narottama installed the image of Gauranga[^42];
seeing its form, all were filled with joy.

**279.** The image of Gauranga that [people] had seen with their eyes before —
"This is the very same," they say, "it is no different at all."

**280.** The Mahashaya, receiving no letter from Srinivasa Acharya,
was ever anxious, and his heart was restless.

**281.** At that time a letter was put into the Mahashaya's hand:
"The Acharya has come to Vishnupura with Ramachandra."

**282.** Now Ramachandra, together with Acharya Prabhu,
went by way of Khardaha and Shantipur to Ambika.

**283.** By way of Navadvipa and Khanda they came to Yajigrama,
and from there set out for Katwa.

**284.** There they beheld Mahaprabhu
and, staying a few days, went to Teliya Budhari.

**285.** Hearing that they had come to Budhari, the Mahashaya
went with a few companions to Ramachandra's house.

**286.** Hearing from afar of Narottama's arrival,
the Acharya came out with Ramachandra to meet him.

**287.** When Narottama bowed to Srinivasa Acharya,
he embraced him and could not let him go.

**288.** Ramachandra bowed to Narottama;
he, bowing in return, embraced him.

**289.** Govinda came and bowed to Narottama;
he, embracing him, held him to his heart.

**290.** Then all went into the house
and, sitting down, talked of Vrindavana.

**291.** [They told] how the Gosvamis had shown favour to Ramachandra,
and conveyed Lokanatha's blessing to Narottama.

**292.** Narottama, crying "Prabhu!", wept
and with great difficulty steadied his mind.

**293.** He told all about the fashioning of the deities
and related the whole story of how Gauranga was found.

**294.** Hearing this, the Acharya and all the others were glad at heart
and all cried out together, "Blessed! Blessed!"

**295.** Srinivasa said, "Take Ramachandra and the others,
go to Kheturi and begin the preparations for the abhisheka.

**296.** I shall come soon; you set out."
Hearing this, he took everyone and came to Kheturi.

**297.** Coming to Kheturi, he made all the preparations
and appointed a separate person to each task.

**298.** Wherever the companions of Sri Mahaprabhu were,
he sent letters of invitation everywhere.

**299.** On the lunar day of Phalguna Purnima
[it was resolved] to perform the abhisheka and seat the deities on the throne.

**300.** Day and night the sankirtana went on;
hearing it, the pashandis felt as if struck by a thunderbolt.

**301.** Now I shall tell of the arrival of the mahantas;
listen, all of you, attentively.

**302.** Srinivasa, Ramachandra and Sri Govinda,
Vyasa Acharya, Krishnavallabha, Divyasimha, Premananda;

**303.** Karnapura, Vamshidasa and Shyamadasa;
from Budhuipara came Sri Gopala Dasa;

**304.** Sri Gokula Vidyavanta of Kanchana-Nagariya[^43];
of all the people who came there was no end.

**305.** With Rasika, Murari and other devotees
Shyamananda came from Utkala to Kheturi.

**306.** From Khardaha came Jahnava Ishvari;
I shall name some of the devotees who came with her:

**307.** her son, Prabhu Virachandra, rare to find in the world[^44];
her son-in-law Madhava Acharya, the husband of Ganga;

**308.** Krishnadasa, Suryadasa and Raghupati,
Murari, Chaitanya Dasa, Sri Jiva Pandita;

**309.** Nrisimha, Gauranga Dasa, Kamalakara Piplai,
Minaketana Ramadasa, Shankara, Kanai;

**310.** Narayana, Sanatana, Nakadi, Manohara,
Gopala, Vrindavana, Ramasena, Damodara;

**311.** Jnanadasa, Kumuda and Pitambara;
Ramachandra, Nrisimha and Haladhara came.

**312.** As many devotees came — how many shall I name?
I shall name a few according to my understanding[^45].

**313.** In the village of Halisahar lived Nayana Bhaskara;
he came to Kheturi together with Raghunatha Acharya.

**314.** Hridaya Chaitanya with his own devotees
came to Kheturi in great joy.

**315.** From Shantipur came two Mahashayas,
Gopala and Achyutananda, the sons of Advaita.

**316.** As many devotees came with them —
now I shall name some of them:

**317.** Kanu Pandita, Vishnudasa, Acharya Janardana,
Kamadeva, Vanamali Dasa, Narayana,

**318.** Purushottama, Shyamadasa, Madhava Acharya,
whose singing of the Krishna-mangala robs all of their composure —

**319.** the dearest disciples of Sri Chaitanya and Advaita:
by Chaitanya's mercy the bonds of worldly life fell [from them].

**320.** From Navadvipa Sripati, Srinidhi and others
came to Kheturi full of delight.

**321.** Yadunandana of Katwa, bringing devotees with him,
came to Kheturi together with Krishnadasa of Akaihat.

**322.** From Khanda came Sri Raghunandana,
bringing with him Lochana Dasa and other devotees[^46].

**323.** Shivananda, Vaninatha, Sri Hari Acharya,
Jita Mishra, Kashinatha, Bhagavata Acharya,

**324.** the Pandita Gosani's nephew Sri Nayanananda,
Pushpa Gopala, Gopala Dasa and Dhruvananda,

**325.** Raghu Mishra, Sri Uddhava, Jagannatha of Kashthakata —
as many came, how many shall I name?

**326.** Wherever the devotees of Sri Krishna Chaitanya were,
little by little all came and gathered at Kheturi.

**327.** Narottama showed great honour to all
and gave each lodging in a fitting place.

**328.** Sri Govinda, Sri Santosha and some others
were appointed to the service of all [the guests].

**329.** Having finished their meal and the rest, all the mahantas
went to sleep at night in perfect joy.

**330.** In the night Narottama sees a dream:
Sri Chaitanya comes and speaks these words to him:

**331.** "Tomorrow in the great sankirtana with the devotees
I shall dance — all will see it with their own eyes."

**332.** Having said this, he placed his feet on Narottama's head,
and Gauranga, Sri Hari, vanished.

**333.** In great bliss Narottama woke quickly
and saw that the night had almost turned to dawn.

**334.** Thakura Mahashaya and the others, having done their morning duties,
hastily began the great abhisheka.

**335.** Srinivasa Acharya went to Jahnava
and, bowing, received her permission.

**336.** Narottama made many obeisances
and one by one received the permission of all the mahantas.

**337.** Having received the permission of all the mahantas,
he gladly began the work.

**338.** Narottama Thakura, immersed in prema,
with a joyful heart sheds tears unceasingly.

**339.** The names of the deities that he had received in the dream —
all those names he then began to pronounce:

**340.** "Gauranga, Vallavikanta, and also Sri Krishna,
Vraja-mohana, Radha-ramana, Radha-kanta — these six."

**341.** Thus in the verse composed by Sri Thakura Mahashaya:
*"Gauranga, Vallavikanta, Sri Krishna, Vraja-mohana,
Radha-ramana, O Radha, Radha-kanta — obeisance to You!"*

**342.** According to the rules of the great abhisheka of Sri Krishna
he performed the abhisheka of the six deities with a joyful heart.

**343.** On the lunar day of Phalguna Purnima, having performed the abhisheka,
he seated the sacred deities on the throne.

**344.** Taking various clothes and ornaments,
Srinivasa with gladness of heart dresses the deities.

**345.** Then, looking at the sacred deities, all the mahantas —
streams flow from their eyes, and their joy has no end.

**346.** The gods from heaven shower flowers;
cries of "Glory! Glory! Glory!" resound unceasingly.

**347.** The sounds of various instruments captivate everyone's mind;
the brahmanas recite the Vedas in sweet voices.

**348.** Phalguna Purnima is the great festival of the Dola-yatra
and the birthday of Mahaprabhu: the celebration has no bounds[^47].

**349.** According to the rules of the ten-syllable mantra of Sri Gopala
he worshipped the six deities with a joyful mind[^48].

**350.** Having completed the puja, he performed the arati;
seeing it, all the people rejoiced.

**351.** When the arati was over, all the mahantas
in supreme joy prostrate themselves on the ground.

**352.** Narottama, swimming in an ocean of happiness,
rolling on the ground, bows down with this mantra.

**353.** Thus in his own verse:
*"Gauranga, Vallavikanta, Sri Krishna, Vraja-mohana,
Radha-ramana, O Radha, Radha-kanta — obeisance to You!"*

**354.** Srinivasa in great joy bowed down
and has many kinds of foods brought.

**355.** Arranging the offerings separately [for each deity],
Srinivasa with a glad heart offers them.

**356.** After some time he gave [water] for rinsing the mouth,
offered tambula and opened the doors.

**357.** Jahnava Ishvari, coming and seeing the deities,
bows again and again with fervour in her joy[^49].

**358.** Then Srinivasa Acharya, coming out into the courtyard,
falling to the ground, bows again and again.

**359.** He bows again and again to the companions of Mahaprabhu;
all embrace him, with tears of joy [streaming] from their eyes.

**360.** Srinivasa bows at the feet of Sri Jahnava,
and she showed him great favour.

**361.** Sri Jahnava asked Srinivasa,
"How did you perform the worship of Sri Gauranga?"

**362.** He said, "By the command of the Gosvamis
I worshipped Chaitanya with the mantra of the Divine Couple, Radha and Krishna.

**363.** The Gosvamis commanded that Chaitanya be worshipped
according to the rules of worship with the ten-syllable Gopala mantra."

**364.** "Good!" said Jahnava, and praises [this] before all:
"Chaitanya Gosani is Radha and Krishna, the Divine Couple in one form"[^50].

**365.** Having said this, Sri Jahnava fell silent;
Narottama came and bowed at her feet.

**366.** Sri Ishvari showed favour to Narottama;
Narottama bowed to the companions of Chaitanya.

**367.** The companions of Chaitanya with joyful hearts
embraced Narottama and cannot let him go.

**368.** Sri Ishvari commanded Srinivasa,
"Give the sacred garlands and sandal paste to all the devotees there are."

**369.** Srinivasa, bringing garlands and sandal paste as prasada,
gave them to Prabhu's companions, to each separately.

**370.** Then he gave them to all the devotees,
and all were adorned with the sacred garlands and sandal paste.

**371.** All the mahantas gave Sri Narottama
permission to begin the sankirtana.

**372.** Then Narottama, bowing to all,
began the sankirtana with delight.

**373.** First Devidasa plays on the khol;
in rhythm Gauranga Dasa plays the karatalas.

**374.** Vallabha, Gokula and all the other devotees
began the sankirtana in sweet voices.

**375.** All the devotees of Chaitanya, coming to the kirtana,
dance with arms raised, crying, "Gauranga!"

**376.** Sri Gaurangachandra is immersed in the mood of Sri Radha —
of that mood he sings songs, filled with bliss.

**377.** Narottama's voice, supremely sweet,
drew Gorachanda — he could not hold back.

**378.** By the power of the bhakti of the great devotee Narottama
Gaura-raya appeared together with his companions:

**379.** Nityananda, Advaita, Srivasa, Gadadhara,
Sri Murari, Haridasa, Svarupa Damodara;

**380.** taking with him Rupa, Sanatana, Gauridasa and others,
he dances in the sankirtana, filled with joy.

**381.** At that moment all forgot themselves;
streams flow from their eyes, all dance in joy.

**382.** Taking with him Sri Achyutananda and all the devotees,
the Son of Shachi dances.

**383.** Of all the devotees there, none has outer consciousness;
in bliss Advaita, Gauranga and Nitai dance.

**384.** Who can understand Prabhu's transcendent lila?
As he appeared, so he vanished.

**385.** No longer seeing Prabhu with his companions in the sankirtana,
all, regaining outer consciousness, weep bitterly.

**386.** Narottama, Shyamananda and Srinivasa
roll on the ground, weep and heave deep sighs.

**387.** Again and again, swooning, they fall to the ground;
their faces are bathed in the tears of their eyes.

**388.** Srinivasa Acharya and all the others grew calm,
but the Mahashaya, crying "Gora!", weeps and finds no rest.

**389.** Sri Achyutananda and all the devotees of Gaura,
consoling Narottama, calmed his heart:

**390.** "Gaura-raya, with the companions of Nityananda and Advaita,
subject to your prema, let us all see him."

**391.** All, embracing, bow at one another's feet;
what joy there was cannot be described.

**392.** Srinivasa and Narottama, bowing at Achyuta's feet,
say, "Sprinkle coloured powder on Prabhu's body"[^51].

**393.** Having said this, they had much powder brought here,
and Sri Jahnava sprinkled the powder on the body of the sacred image.

**394.** Achyuta, Gopala, Narottama, Srinivasa,
Virachandra, Shyamananda, Ramachandra Dasa,

**395.** Hridaya Chaitanya and Sri Raghunandana —
and how many [other] devotees there were, who can count? —

**396.** all come and sprinkle powder on the body of the sacred image;
what joy there was cannot be written.

**397.** Having sprinkled powder on the deity, all the mahantas
sprinkle powder on one another — their happiness has no end.

**398.** They sing of Krishna's lila and ceaselessly throw powder —
in all ten directions water and land turned crimson.

**399.** Having finished the kirtana, all the mahantas
eat prasada with great delight.

**400.** Many foods — those that are chewed, sucked, licked and drunk[^52] —
all ate with joyful hearts.

**401.** Evening came; all saw the arati
and for some time performed nama-sankirtana.

**402.** To perform the abhisheka on Mahaprabhu's birthday,
they brought Gaura-raya into the courtyard.

**403.** At Sri Ishvari's command Acharya Srinivasa
began the abhisheka with delight in his heart.

**404.** According to the rules of [the festival of] Sri Krishna's birth
he worships Gaurangachand with gladness in his heart.

**405.** Meditating on the Divine Couple, Sri Radha and Krishna, as described in the Padma[-purana],
he worshipped [him] with the sixteen offerings, glad at heart[^53].

**406.** Krishna and Gaura are one; whoever thinks of a difference here
goes to hell, and for him there is no deliverance.

**407.** Having offered food, they laid the deity to rest,
and all the mahantas ate prasada[^54].

**408.** Resting with gladness of heart,
all spent the night rapt in songs of Krishna's lila.

**409.** Having seen the mangala-arati,
all went off to their own affairs.

**410.** Staying here that day and taking prasada,
on the next day all took leave and departed.

**411.** What grief Narottama felt at that time
I am unable to write even in part.

**412.** Narottama's order of service is wonderful indeed;
who is able to describe how everything was arranged?

**413.** Sri Lochana Dasa is born of a vaidya family;
he is a disciple of Sri Narahari and lives in Srikhanda[^55].

**414.** The songs of the Chaitanya-mangala composed by him
are always sung in Narottama's house.

**415.** First the Sri Chaitanya-mangala is sung,
and after that the Krishna-mangala is sung.

**416.** The singing of the Sri Krishna-mangala is wonderful indeed:
whoever hears it, his heart melts and tears of joy flow.

**417.** The Tenth Canto of the Srimad Bhagavata
Madhava Acharya rendered [in verse] in many metres.

**418.** I shall describe a little of the virtues of Madhava Acharya,
whose character and virtues are known throughout the world.

**419.** Durgadasa Mishra, a mine of all virtues,
a Vedic brahmana, lived in the town of Nadia.

**420.** His wife's name was Sri Vijaya;
she bore two sons, abodes of virtue.

**421.** The elder was Sanatana, the younger Parashara Kalidasa[^56];
supreme panditas, abodes of all virtues.

**422.** Sanatana's wife was named Mahamaya;
she bore an only daughter, Vishnupriya.

**423.** An only daughter, and no other children;
she was given in marriage to Sri Krishna Chaitanyachandra.

**424.** Kalidasa Mishra's wife was named Vidhumukhi;
she bore a jewel of a son, an abode of all virtues.

**425.** Leaving his only son, Kalidasa
left the earth and took up his abode in heaven.

**426.** Vidhumukhi, with her son named Madhava in her arms,
became a widow at a young age.

**427.** In the eighth year from conception Madhava was invested with the sacred thread[^57],
and he began to study various shastras.

**428.** Having studied various shastras, he became a pandita
and became known by the title "Acharya".

**429.** At the time of the abhisheka of Sri Gauranga Mahaprabhu
Madhava Acharya went to Srivasa's house[^58].

**430.** Seeing the form of Gauranga, he went mad [with love],
and from then on he became a devotee of Chaitanya.

**431.** On the day when Sri Chaitanya
loudly instructed the devotees in his own Hari-nama —

**432.** on that day Madhava was in that place,
and the sound of the maha-mantra entered his ears.

**433.** Hearing the Name, he felt prema awaken
and prostrated himself at Chaitanya's feet.

**434.** Sri Chaitanya Prabhu, showing him favour,
lifted his foot and placed it on his head.

**435.** Madhava asked Prabhu about the rules of [chanting] the Name;
Prabhu told him to chant the Name keeping count.

**436.** Keeping count, he lovingly chants a hundred thousand names;
from then on detachment from worldly life arose in him.

**437.** Many days after Sri Mahaprabhu's sannyasa
he rendered the nectar of Krishna's lila into the common tongue[^59].

**438.** The Tenth Canto of the Srimad Bhagavata
he set out in songs in many metres[^60].

**439.** Taking something from other Puranas as well,
he included it in the Krishna-mangala.

**440.** He named the book Sri Krishna-mangala
and offered it at Sri Chaitanya's feet.

**441.** Sri Krishna Chaitanya showed him favour,
and all the devotees grew fond of him.

**442.** Mahaprabhu commanded Advaita,
"Instruct Madhava in the initiation mantra."

**443.** Sri Advaita Prabhu, at Mahaprabhu's command,
began to speak the mantra into Madhava's ear.

**444.** First he gave him the Hari-nama together with its meaning,
then he spoke the mantra of Radha and Krishna into his ear.

**445.** He instructed him in the kama-gayatri and the kama-bija[^61]
and, explaining the meaning, revealed to him the whole truth.

**446.** From then on Madhava became skilled in bhajana,
and he no longer wished to live in the world.

**447.** Madhava's mother, seeing him indifferent,
was afraid that he would leave home.

**448.** Madhava's mother, to get him married,
in her fear hastily began the preparations.

**449.** Seeing his mother's preparations, Madhava then
ran away and went to Vrindavana.

**450.** Coming to the feet of Sri Rupa, he surrendered himself
and learned the whole truth of bhajana.

**451.** Having taken sannyasa, he stayed in Vrindavana
and performs bhajana in the madhurya-bhava of Vraja.

**452.** Madhava Acharya is the sakhi Sri Madhavi[^62];
by Sri Rupa's mercy [this awareness] awoke in him.

**453.** Later Madhava became famed as Kavivallabha Acharya[^63];
all say, "This great-minded one is the Vyasa of the age of Kali."

**454.** He, a great devotee of Krishna, while wandering in Vrindavana,
heard of his mother's death.

**455.** Hearing of his mother's death, he came to Shantipur
and together with Achyuta went to Sri Kheturi.

**456.** Having seen the abhisheka of the sacred deities at Kheturi,
he quickly returned to Vrindavana.

**457.** I went to Vrindavana with the Ishvari
and wandered with great joy together with Madhava Acharya.

**458.** He instructed me in the truth;
to his lotus feet is my special obeisance.

**459.** Now I shall tell of the splendour of the service under Narottama,
seeing which the pashandis were laid low in the dust.

**460.** He built a most excellent temple
and in six chambers installed six deities:

**461.** Gauranga, Vallavikanta, and also Sri Krishna,
Vraja-mohana, Radha-ramana, Radha-kanta — six.

**462.** According to the rules of the service of the eight periods[^64]
he performs the daily service with a joyful heart.

**463.** All year round the sankirtana goes on unceasingly;
seeing it, the pashandis are amazed.

**464.** In one place the Srimad Bhagavata is expounded,
in another the Chaitanya-bhagavata and the Chaitanya-charitamrita are read.

**465.** The Chaitanya-bhagavata used to be called the Chaitanya-mangala;
the mahantas in Vrindavana gave it the name "Bhagavata".

**466.** Seeing that it resembles the Bhagavata,
all gladly call it the Chaitanya-bhagavata.

**467.** In another place many sadhus and mahantas, sitting together,
joyfully converse about Krishna.

**468.** How much shall I tell of the Sri Sankirtana? —
hearing it, the pashandis melted at heart.

**469.** First they sing the Chaitanya-mangala,
after that the Sri Krishna-mangala is sung.

**470.** Then Govinda's songs of the lilas of Gaura and Krishna are sung[^65];
by Narottama's singing everyone's mind and soul are refreshed.

**471.** Whoever listens to the songs of Vidyapati and Chandidasa on Krishna's lila,
they steal his mind and soul.

**472.** Every year on the day of Sri Phalguna Purnima
there is a great festival at Kheturi.

**473.** All the Vaishnavas come there;
what joy there is cannot be written.

**474.** What my Thakurani did
on the way from Kheturi to Vrindavana[^66] —

**475.** of how we travelled on the road I now write;
listen, listeners, to the wonders that happened [there].

**476.** I went to Vrindavana with the Thakurani
and saw all the events with my own eyes.

**477.** There was a robber chief named Qutbuddin[^67];
taking with him many yavanas,

**478.** he came to rob us of our money and the rest —
but cannot find the way and wanders about.

**479.** As they wandered about, the night passed;
seeing the dawn, all were frightened out of their wits.

**480.** In fear all fell at Jahnava's feet:
"Protect us, Mother, we have come to you for refuge!

**481.** We came to rob you of your money and everything,
[but] we wandered and wandered, and the night passed.

**482.** We look in all four directions and see huge snakes:
they are rushing at us to bite.

**483.** Then from somewhere came a voice:
'This Thakurani has subdued you.'

**484.** Hearing this, we were seized with great fear
and, coming to your feet, take refuge."

**485.** Hearing this, the Thakurani, with great joy in her heart,
showed mercy to all the yavanas.

**486.** Then the cry of Hari's name arose there:
all the yavanas dance and sing of Krishna's qualities.

**487.** Hear what happened another day — it is most wonderful:
with the Ishvari we entered a certain village.

**488.** That day we stayed in that village;
the pashandis of the village jeered at us greatly.

**489.** At night they see a dream:
the goddess Chandika in anger speaks these words:

**490.** "You have mocked Jahnava Devi;
for this offence great misfortune will befall you.

**491.** If you take refuge at Jahnava's feet,
then you will be saved; otherwise — ruin."[^68]

**492.** Next morning all the pashandis
came and fell at the Thakurani's feet.

**493.** Jahnava Ishvari, my [mistress], is an ocean of compassion:
she showed them mercy, and all became [her] followers.

**494.** Returning from Vrindavana, Jahnava Ishvari
came to Sri Kheturi and stayed some days.

**495.** With her is always Madhava Acharya[^69];
by singing and playing he robs everyone of composure[^70].

**496.** Madhava Acharya is a Varendra brahmana,
a beloved devotee of Nityananda, of the highest [brahmana] lineage[^71].

**497.** A disciple of Nityananda, he knows none but Nitai
and always meditates on Nitai's feet.

**498.** Nityananda Prabhu has a daughter named Ganga;
Prabhu gave her in marriage to Madhava Acharya.

**499.** Madhava married at the guru's command:
the scriptures say that the guru's command is strongest of all[^72].

**500.** The greatness of the Lord cannot be understood at all:
by the Lord's will the impossible comes to pass.

**501.** Think no ill of a marriage between Radhi and Varendra:
Radhi and Varendra are the offspring of the same ancestors[^73].

**502.** There have been many marriages between Radhi and Varendra;
the difference of names is only from the difference of regions.

**503.** Five twice-born came to the sacrifice of Adishura;
their descendants are the Radhi and Varendra communities[^74].

**504.** Madhava Acharya, having married Ganga,
by the guru's command became a Radhi[^75].

**505.** Madhava Acharya is called Shantanu:
this Ganga Devi is the Ganga herself, become water[^76].

**506.** I learned to play instruments from Madhava Acharya:
in his mercy he taught me to play.

**507.** At his lotus feet are my countless obeisances;
how much mercy he showed me has no limit.

**508.** When Phalguna Purnima drew near,
the festival at Sri Kheturi began.

**509.** Little by little all the mahantas came;
how many people came cannot be counted.

**510.** All came — Srinivasa, Shyamananda;
Virachandra and Achyutananda came with many Vaishnavas.

**511.** On the day of the full moon in the morning there was nama-sankirtana;
they performed the abhisheka of the deities and sprinkled them with coloured powder.

**512.** All the devotees, sprinkling powder on the bodies of the deities,
gladly sprinkle powder on one another.

**513.** Having played with the powder, all took prasada;
having seen the evening arati, they began the kirtana.

**514.** First, Vasu Ghosha's songs of Gaura's lila:
whoever hears them, his heart melts and tears flow.

**515.** Devidasa and Madhava Acharya play the mridanga,
Gauranga [Dasa] and Govinda Dasa play the karatalas.

**516.** Santosha, Govinda, Gokula — all sing songs;
Krishna's lila in the songs of Chandidasa captivates everyone's heart.

**517.** Achyutananda, Virabhadra and Srinivasa,
Shyamananda, Narottama, Ramachandra Dasa,

**518.** with arms raised, dance and sing of Krishna's lila;
what joy there was cannot be written.

**519.** Drawn by the power of Narottama's bhakti and his song,
Krishna could not hold back and came together with his Beloved.

**520.** In all ten directions water and land were lit up;
a spotless radiance, like lightning in a cloud.

**521.** Then all saw the form of Radha and Krishna;
what joy they felt cannot be told.

**522.** A fragrant breeze blew, wonderful indeed;
anklets and belts of little bells tinkled sweetly.

**523.** Above the sankirtana, in the vault of the sky,
Bhagavan appeared and vanished.

**524.** Narottama fell to the ground senseless;
Ramachandra and the others weep, rolling on the ground.

**525.** Sri Krishna's lila cannot be understood at all;
by Krishna's will all grew calm.

**526.** A cry rose in the sky: "Blessed is Narottama!"
All embrace one another and bow.

**527.** Narottama is an avesha-avatara of Bhagavan;
by his mercy we were delivered.

**528.** Narottama's bhajana and his sport are supremely excellent;
in his mercy he delivers all people.

**529.** One day Narottama, fixing his mind,
contemplates the rasa-lila of Radha and Krishna.

**530.** He remains in samadhi, his body motionless;
friends, kinsfolk and devotees, seeing this, grew alarmed[^77].

**531.** Ramachandra says, "Do not worry at all:
when the samadhi breaks, he will regain consciousness."

**532.** Two days passed, and all were in distress;
Srinivasa Acharya came and calmed everyone.

**533.** Srinivasa Acharya with effort brought him to consciousness;
then the cry arose: "Hari! Hari! Hari!"

**534.** Regaining outer consciousness, Narottama bowed to the Acharya,
and Srinivasa Acharya embraced him.

**535.** Listen, listen, listeners, with one mind:
now I shall describe the deliverance of a pashandi.

**536.** In Gopalpur lived a Vedic brahmana;
he taught pupils and knew many shastras.

**537.** His name was Gurudasa Bhattacharya;
this wicked man reviled Narottama in every way.

**538.** From his reviling he was struck with leprosy;
neither propitiatory rites nor treatment rid him of the disease.

**539.** He constantly meditates on Bhavani:
"For what offence has such misery befallen me?"

**540.** At night Bhavani showed herself to him in a dream:
"You suffer so for reviling Narottama.

**541.** You have always regarded Narottama as a shudra —
for this offence you are suffering greatly.

**542.** Narottama is the embodiment of Sri Chaitanya's prema;
whoever looks on him with bhakti, the distress of his mind departs.

**543.** He is an avesha-avatara of Nityananda Prabhu;
in his mercy he will deliver the world.

**544.** The sinner who regards Narottama as an ordinary man
sinks into hell after death.

**545.** The wretch who calls Narottama a shudra
goes to hell with his whole family, there is no doubt.

**546.** Whoever is devoted to Vishnu
has the sacred thread within his heart, know this for certain.

**547.** A devotee of Krishna is greater than a brahmana;
a brahmana without devotion to Krishna is truly the lowest of shudras.

**548.** Thus it is said:
*'Even a chandala devoted to Vishnu is the best of the twice-born;
but one without devotion to Vishnu, though twice-born, is lower than a dog-eater.'*[^78]

**549.** Having said this, Bhagavati vanished;
waking, the brahmana sees that the night has passed.

**550.** In the morning the brahmana, coming from there to Kheturi,
prostrated himself at Narottama's feet.

**551.** He related his dream in detail:
"Have mercy, Prabhu, give me the boat of your feet.

**552.** There is none in the world, Prabhu, as low as I;
if you deliver me, your fame will spread everywhere."

**553.** Hearing this, Narottama in mercy placed his foot on his head;
he was freed from the disease — all saw it.

**554.** Thakura Mahashaya is an ocean of compassion:
in his pity he made him his servant.

**555.** From then on many people, fearful at heart,
came and took refuge at Narottama's feet.

**556.** There was a Vedic brahmana named Jagannatha Acharya,
a great pandita, a resident of Budhari.

**557.** Seeing that [Narottama] was initiating brahmanas, this brahmana Jagannatha
became furious with Narottama[^79].

**558.** [Wishing] to enter into debate with Sri Narottama,
he began inwardly to meditate on the feet of Kali.

**559.** At night Jagannatha saw a dream:
Narottama is an avesha of Sri Bhagavan.

**560.** Greatly afraid at heart, Jagannatha
quickly came to the village of Sri Kheturi.

**561.** He came and took refuge at Narottama's feet,
and Narottama in his mercy gave him the initiation mantra.

**562.** Narottama made many brahmanas his disciples —
and the pashandi brahmanas all burned [with spite] like fire.

**563.** Wicked brahmanas, robber chiefs from Bengal,
were delivered by the mercy of Thakura Mahashaya.

**564.** I shall give the names of some of them;
listen, listen, listeners, with one mind.

**565.** Govinda Banerji and Lalita Ghoshal,
Kalidasa Chatterji, a most wicked robber,

**566.** Nilamani Mukhuti and Ramajaya Chakravarti,
Harinatha Ganguli and Shiva Chakravarti[^80].

**567.** Formerly they were soldiers of Chand Raya
and committed much robbery together with Chand Raya.

**568.** They are Chand Raya's kinsmen and friends;
fighting, they defeated the yavanas.

**569.** They plundered many lands and extended their domain;
out of fear the yavana king did not advance against them.

**570.** On the day when Chand Raya was taken captive,
all of them fled in fear.

**571.** Knowing Thakura Mahashaya's power and its essence,
all became [his] disciples, giving up their former trade[^81].

**572.** [Narottama] has a kinsman, Raja Narasimha Raya;
he lives very far away, at Pakkapalli[^82].

**573.** His city on the bank of the Ganga is most delightful;
he cares for his subjects with love, as for his sons.

**574.** Many brahmana panditas live near him;
once, by fate, a great pandita came there.

**575.** The pandita's name was Rupa Narayana;
nowhere had he been defeated in debate.

**576.** His life is supremely sweet;
I heard much of it from Narasimha Raya.

**577.** I shall describe something of it here in brief —
hearing of his life, all will be very glad.

**578.** In Bengal there was the kingdom of Kamarupa, very pure;
the Pathans took it after a great war.

**579.** The capital of that country is Egarasindur,
a most beautiful [town] on the bank of the Brahmaputra.

**580.** Egarasindur and Mirjafarpur,
Dagdaga, Kutishvara and Hosenpur —

**581.** all these places lie on the bank of the Brahmaputra;
people from various lands trade there.

**582.** Egarasindur and Dagdaga
are famous for trade — everyone knows it.

**583.** Merchants from all quarters live here;
all buy and sell with joyful hearts.

**584.** Near Egarasindur there is a village
named Bhitadiya, where kulinas live.

**585.** There lives the brahmana Lakshminatha Lahiri;
his virtuous wife is Kamala Devi, exceedingly beautiful.

**586.** He is a Varendra brahmana, foremost among the kulinas,
honoured by all brahmanas, revered everywhere.

**587.** A son was born to him, like Indra himself;
they named him Sri Rupachandra.

**588.** As a child Rupachandra was very naughty
and even at his father's bidding did not learn to read and write.

**589.** However hard Lakshminatha Lahiri tried,
he would by no means study.

**590.** One day his father in anger threw ashes on his rice;
in distress he got up and left without eating.

**591.** Bowing to his mother, he left home
and in a few days reached the village of Panditbari[^83].

**592.** Having studied grammar, he received the title Chakravarti;
his fame grew from his studies at Navadvipa.

**593.** Having studied many shastras, he became very learned
and gained fame as an Acharya.

**594.** From there he went to Nilachala
and saw Mahaprabhu in the sankirtana.

**595.** Bowing to Sri Chaitanya from afar,
he joyfully beheld Jagannatha.

**596.** From there he went to the city of Puna in Maharashtra
to study the Vedas and the rest, with a glad heart.

**597.** Rupachandra had a great memory for what he heard;
he studied the Vedas, the Vedangas, the Vedanta — everything.

**598.** Seeing his profound learning in many shastras,
the teachers gave him the title Sarasvati.

**599.** Making a digvijaya, he goes to various places[^84]:
wherever he sees a pandita, he defeats him in debate.

**600.** Having wandered through many places, he came to Vrindavana
and hears that two supreme panditas live there —

**601.** two Gosanis named Rupa and Sanatana:
in no land are there panditas equal to these two.

**602.** Rupachandra came to the two Gosanis,
boundlessly glad that he would debate.

**603.** Coming, he bowed to the Gosvamis;
the Gosanis respectfully gave him a seat.

**604.** Asking after his welfare, they say, "Why have you come?"
Rupachandra says, "I have come for a debate.

**605.** I have studied many shastras and become a pandita;
a debate with the two of you is what I desire."

**606.** The Gosvamis say, "What fruit comes of debate?"
The pandita says, "A test in the shastras, victory and defeat."

**607.** The Gosanis say, "There is no need for debate:
we both accept defeat."

**608.** Aggrieved, Rupachandra rose from there:
"The Gosvamis would not debate with me out of fear,"

**609.** saying this, he goes towards the bank of the Yamuna;
on the way he met Sri Jiva Gosvami.

**610.** Sri Jiva, questioning him, learned everything,
and [Rupachandra] in anger began the debate right there.

**611.** Sri Jiva said, "Rupa and Sanatana are my teachers;
if you defeat me, I shall call you the victor."

**612.** Jiva said, "The two Gosanis are supreme panditas;
if you debate with me, this will become clear to you."

**613.** The debate between Jiva and Rupachandra went on for five days;
who won and who lost could not be known.

**614.** On the seventh day the debate went on for a long time:
Jiva was victorious, and Rupachandra was crushed.

**615.** Sri Jiva, refuting Rupachandra's advaita-vada,
established dvaita-vada with reasons and proofs.

**616.** He showed the pre-eminence of the Vaishnava teaching:
bhakti was acknowledged above jnana, karma and yoga.

**617.** The defeated Rupachandra, at Sri Jiva's feet,
prostrated himself gladly.

**618.** With joined palms he offers praise to Sri Jiva:
"By your mercy my mind has been purified."

**619.** Sri Jiva in his mercy placed his foot on his head,
embraced him and seated him beside himself.

**620.** Rupa said, "Prabhu, you have shown me mercy:
you have dispelled all my darkness of ignorance.

**621.** I have committed countless offences against you;
have mercy, purify my wicked mind."

**622.** Jiva said, "Before me you bear no offence:
it is Chaitanya Gosani who has shown you mercy."

**623.** Hearing this, Rupachandra, touching his head
to Sri Jiva's feet, sheds tears of prema.

**624.** Rupachandra said, "Prabhu, Sri Jiva Gosani,
my offences have no end.

**625.** How much tamas I showed
before Sri Rupa and Sanatana Gosvami cannot be told.

**626.** Remembering it, my heart burns;
I see no way out, Prabhu, I see no way out!"

**627.** Having said this, Rupachandra lamented bitterly;
Sri Jiva Gosvami carefully consoled him.

**628.** Sri Jiva Gosvami, taking Sri Rupachandra with him,
gladly goes to the Gosvamis.

**629.** Rupachandra at the feet of Sri Rupa and Sri Sanatana
falls to the ground and rolls in great sorrow:

**630.** "There is no sinner in the three worlds as low as I;
my offences cannot be counted.

**631.** Drunk with tamas, with a mind gripped by delusion,
I came to debate with you.

**632.** Forgive my offences, Prabhu, have mercy on this low one,
deliver the fallen, give the shade of your feet.

**633.** By the mercy of Sri Jiva Gosvami I have gained a little knowledge;
by the strength of his mercy I have beheld your feet."

**634.** Saying this and much else, Rupa rolls on the ground;
in distress he weeps, his heart groans.

**635.** Seeing Rupachandra's humility, Rupa and Sanatana
in their mercy placed their feet on his head.

**636.** Rupa and Sanatana said to Rupachandra,
"You bear no offence; your mind has become pure.

**637.** Sri Krishna Chaitanyachandra has shown you mercy;
you have become blessed in all the world."

**638.** Having said this, the two Gosanis embraced him;
tears of prema flowed from his eyes.

**639.** Rupachandra humbly says to the Gosvamis,
"Have mercy, give this low one initiation into [the mantra of] Krishna."

**640.** Hearing this, both Gosvamis pondered;
at that moment a voice rose in the sky:

**641.** "Give Rupachandra the Hari-nama, both of you;
initiation into the Gopala mantra he will receive from Narottama."

**642.** Hearing the voice from the sky, the two Gosvamis
speak into his ear the maha-mantra of the Hari-nama:

**643.** "Keeping count, you will always chant the Hari-nama;
initiation into [the mantra of] Krishna you will receive from Narottama.

**644.** In the village of Sri Kheturi at Gopalpur, in the land of Garerhat,
Narottama has been born — so we tell you[^85].

**645.** At twelve years of age he will come to Vrindavana
and become a disciple of Lokanatha Gosani, a treasury of virtues."

**646.** Having said this, Sanatana fell silent;
Rupachandra touched his head to the Gosvamis' feet.

**647.** At that very moment a wonder took place:
Narayana entered into Rupachandra.

**648.** Rupa and Sanatana, seeing the power of his bhakti,
embraced him and felt prema.

**649.** The Gosanis said, "Narayana has entered your body;
from today your name is Rupa Narayana."

**650.** Having said this, they infused shakti into him,
and Rupa Narayana bowed at the Gosanis' feet.

**651.** For some time he lived in Vrindavana
and studied the shastras of bhakti under Sri Jiva.

**652.** He studies the Bhagavata with the commentaries of the Svami and the Toshani[^86],
gladly studies the Laghu- and Brihad-bhagavatamrita,

**653.** studies the [Bhakti-]rasamrita, the Ujjvala, all the Sandarbhas;
studying the dramas and the rest, he found great delight.

**654.** Having seen the whole of the Mathura-mandala,
immersed in bliss, he performs nama-sankirtana.

**655.** To Sri Rupa, Sanatana, Raghunatha Bhatta,
Sri Jiva, Gopala Bhatta, the devotee Kashinatha,

**656.** and to the two Gosanis, Lokanatha and Bhugarbha,
he bowed with great joy in his heart.

**657.** To Brahmachari Krishnadasa and Kashishvara —
he bowed at the feet of all the Vaishnavas.

**658.** All the Vaishnavas showed him favour;
taking leave, he went to Nilachala.

**659.** There he heard of Mahaprabhu's disappearance;
in great grief he lost consciousness.

**660.** By Prabhu's will sleep overcame him,
and in a dream Gaurachandra appeared to him.

**661.** Prabhu said, "Listen, Rupa Narayana:
you will meet Narasimha Raya.

**662.** Staying with him, you will receive from Narottama
the Gopala mantra together with him."

**663.** Having said this, Gaura placed his feet on his head
and, showing him favour, went away.

**664.** Having seen the dream, Rupa Narayana
woke, sat up and sheds tears of prema.

**665.** By Prabhu's will he found peace
and came to the house of Gadadhara Pandita.

**666.** Bowing, he related everything,
and Gadadhara placed his feet on his head.

**667.** Then he went to Sri Svarupa Gosvami
and humbly told him everything.

**668.** He bowed at Svarupa's feet,
and Svarupa in his mercy placed his foot on his head.

**669.** Showing him favour, he infused shakti into him
and told him many hidden truths.

**670.** He bowed to Sri Dasa Gosvami,
and he showed him boundless favour.

**671.** With joy in his heart he beheld Sri Jagannatha
and freely offered praises of his own composing.

**672.** Bowing, he left that place,
and on the way he met Ramananda.

**673.** Learning who he was, he bowed to the Raya,
and Raya Ramananda showed him favour.

**674.** Having thus met all the devotees of Gaura,
after some time he came to the land of Gauda.

**675.** For some days he wandered through various places —
and heard that Nityananda Prabhu had disappeared.

**676.** Hearing of his disappearance, he grieved greatly,
and in a dream Nityananda appeared to him.

**677.** Seeing Prabhu, he fainted with bliss;
[Prabhu] placed his foot on his head, and his heart grew calm.

**678.** Nitai said, "Listen, O Rupa Narayana:
soon you will meet Narasimha.

**679.** For some time you will stay here,
and after some days you will see Narottama."

**680.** Having said this, Nityananda vanished;
waking, he sees that night has turned to morning.

**681.** What joy he felt on seeing Prabhu cannot be described;
what grief at losing sight of him cannot be written.

**682.** By Prabhu's will he recovered somewhat;
and some days later he heard of Advaita Prabhu's departure.

**683.** He grieved greatly, and in a dream he had darshana:
Prabhu said, "You will meet Raja Narasimha."

**684.** Having said this, Prabhu placed his foot on his head
and, showing him favour, went away.

**685.** Waking, Rupa Narayana was filled with grief;
after some time the night turned to dawn.

**686.** By Prabhu's will he recovered somewhat
and, having done his morning duties, went to bathe in the Ganga.

**687.** To that same ghat a raja came,
with many brahmana panditas and countless people.

**688.** He learned from people that this was Narasimha Raya;
he bathed in the Ganga with a joyful heart.

**689.** Raja Narasimha, seeing Rupa Narayana,
came up to him and asked who he was.

**690.** Rupa Narayana was exceedingly handsome;
deep reverence arose in Narasimha's heart.

**691.** Raja Narasimha Raya with great insistence
took Rupa Narayana to his own palace.

**692.** Many brahmana panditas came to the palace,
and Rupa Narayana defeated them all in debate.

**693.** Rupa Narayana's fame spread everywhere:
in no land is there a pandita equal to him.

**694.** The raja loves Rupa Narayana dearly;
by his counsel the raja's fame grows greatly.

**695.** Rupa Narayana knows the yoga-shastra deeply;
I myself studied some yoga-shastra with him.

**696.** Certain kinds of yoga I learned from him,
and I honoured him as my guru in yoga.

**697.** To write of his life is the Ishvari's command;
I have written briefly and shall not write in detail.

**698.** One day Narasimha, together with Rupa Narayana,
was sitting in assembly with his courtiers.

**699.** At that time some brahmana panditas came,
distressed: "Ruin has come!

**700.** Narottama Dasa, the son of Krishnananda Datta[^87],
by giving the mantra to brahmanas has ruined everything.

**701.** It seems the dreadful [age of] Kali has now arrived:
hearing that a shudra has brahmana disciples, we tremble.

**702.** From somewhere he brought and spread the Vaishnava teaching
and abolished the worship of all the gods and goddesses.

**703.** There are no more sacrifices, no slaying of animals[^88];
he has ruined the country, the rites have gone to waste.

**704.** Giving up fish and meat, they eat only vegetable food;
in the sankirtana they dance and weep like madmen.

**705.** All the Vedic and Tantric rites have vanished;
with the sankirtana he has beguiled all the people.

**706.** What sorcery does this Narottama Dasa know? —
many learned brahmanas have become his disciples.

**707.** Protect the caste of brahmanas, Mahashaya:
lead us all to his house.

**708.** We shall defeat him in a debate on the shastras —
and he will flee in fear."

**709.** Hearing this, Narasimha Raya said to Rupa Narayana,
"What do you say, brother? Speak now."[^89]

**710.** Rupa Narayana said, "Listen, Maharaja:
go with your whole retinue, do not delay.

**711.** It seems our good fortune has dawned at last:
Thakura Mahashaya has drawn [us] by his virtues."[^90]

**712.** Rupa Narayana said to the brahmana panditas,
"Let us go with your pupils; we shall defeat Narottama in debate."[^91]

**713.** But inwardly Rupa says, "Such is the greatness [of his] I have heard of —
by the Mahashaya's mercy all people will be delivered."

**714.** Taking the teachers and Rupa Narayana with him,
the Raya set out for Kheturi.

**715.** Near Kheturi, in the village of Kumarpur,
the Raya one day stopped to rest.

**716.** Meanwhile Thakura Mahashaya heard all about it:
Narasimha Raya had come with many panditas.

**717.** Ramachandra Kaviraja said to Narottama,
"We shall go in disguise and defeat them there."

**718.** Having said this, they took the Mahashaya's permission
and gladly went to Kumarpur.

**719.** Ramachandra, Govinda and Ganganarayana,
Harihara, Ramakrishna, Jagannatha — these few

**720.** dressed up as an oilman, a liquor-seller, a barui and a potter[^92]
and, taking various goods, set up a market there.

**721.** Some pupils came to buy goods;
when they asked the price, they were answered in Sanskrit.

**722.** The pupils haughtily spoke in Sanskrit —
and after a little conversation all were defeated.

**723.** The oilman and the liquor-seller say, "Fools, what do you know?
If you have any shame, bring your teachers."

**724.** Ashamed, the pupils say to their teachers,
"An oilman, a liquor-seller, a barui and a potter have defeated us all.

**725.** We asked, 'Where did you learn the shastras?' —
and they told us everything in detail:

**726.** 'We keep shops at the holy seat of Kheturi[^93];
the shastras are much studied there — we have retained a little.'"

**727.** Hearing this, the teachers flared up like fire,
and all went to the market to debate.

**728.** For a long time all debated —
and the panditas were utterly defeated.

**729.** The panditas came to the raja's camp
and told him how they had been defeated.

**730.** The panditas say, "We shall go no further to Kheturi;
come, let us quickly flee from here."

**731.** Rupa Narayana said, "There is nothing to worry about:
Narottama Gosani will show mercy to all.

**732.** What is the use of fleeing?
Take refuge at Narottama's feet.

**733.** All the shastras say that the Vaishnava dharma is the highest dharma;
becoming Vaishnavas, people attain bhakti and liberation.

**734.** Thus it is said:
*'The Vaishnava is the highest dharma, the Vaishnava is the highest austerity,
the Vaishnava is the highest object of worship, the Vaishnava is the highest guru.'*

**735.** *'Let one seek health from the Sun, wealth from Fire,
knowledge from Shankara, and liberation from Janardana.'*[^94]"

**736.** Meanwhile all the traders from the market,
calling the pupils, gave them the goods.

**737.** They said, "We make our profit in various places;
[this] we all give to the brahmanas."

**738.** Having said this and handed over the goods,
the traders went off to their own places.

**739.** Meanwhile all ate and went to sleep;
towards morning the panditas saw a dream:

**740.** Bhagavati, sword in hand and with an angry face, says,
"You have reviled Narottama, you wicked ones!

**741.** You studied, yet nothing was born in you;
by reviling a Vaishnava you have fallen into the abyss.

**742.** Even if I hack your heads to pieces,
the grief of my heart will not end.

**743.** Narottama is an avesha-avatara of the Lord[^95];
in his heart is a dazzlingly radiant sacred thread.

**744.** He in whose heart is Brahman is a brahmana;
the outer thread is only the mark of the brahmana caste.

**745.** Tomorrow you will take initiation from Narottama;
if Narottama shows you favour, you are saved."

**746.** Having said this, Bhagavati vanished,
and all the teachers woke and sat up.

**747.** Having seen the dream, they tremble with fear, utterly subdued,
and told the dream to the raja in detail.

**748.** The raja said, "Before, you did not heed the warning
and regarded Narottama as an ordinary man.

**749.** What he does is beyond the power of men:
Sri Thakura Mahashaya is worshipped by the gods."

**750.** Having said this, he calmed the teachers,
and all, having bathed and so on, went to Kheturi.

**751.** They bowed to the deities, rolling on the ground,
and prostrated themselves before Narottama:

**752.** "There is no sinner in the world as low as we are;
forgive our offences, have mercy, Gosani."

**753.** Narottama, showing great mercy to all,
lifted his foot and placed it on their heads.

**754.** Then he gave all the brahmanas initiation into [the mantra of] Krishna;
what mercy he showed cannot be told.

**755.** Those of them who were the chief panditas —
I shall now proclaim their names:

**756.** Yadunatha Vidyabhushana and Kashinatha[^96],
known everywhere by the title Tarka-bhushana;

**757.** Haridasa Shiromani and Chandrakanta,
known everywhere by the title Nyaya-panchanana;

**758.** Shivacharana and Durgadasa — these two,
whom all call by the titles Vidya-vagisha and Vidyaratna[^97].

**759.** The names of the panditas I have written here,
but the names of the pupils I could not write.

**760.** Meanwhile Raja Narasimha and Rupa Narayana,
having bowed to the six deities and offered them praise,

**761.** came to Narottama's feet and prostrated themselves;
what humility they showed cannot be described.

**762.** Narottama, showing favour to both[^98],
lifted his foot and placed it on their heads.

**763.** Learning who Raja Narasimha was,
in great mercy he gave him the mantra of Krishna.

**764.** Then Narasimha Raya told Thakura Mahashaya
in detail who Rupa Narayana was:

**765.** what had happened in Vrindavana,
and how they had met him —

**766.** all this he described in detail;
hearing it, Ramachandra and the others were glad.

**767.** Hearing it, Thakura Mahashaya in his mercy
gave him the Hari-nama with its meaning into his ear,

**768.** gave the ten-syllable Gopala mantra,
and then gave the kama-gayatri and the kama-bija.

**769.** Rupa Narayana, prostrating himself,
placed the Mahashaya's feet on his head.

**770.** Then he bowed to Ramachandra and the others;
what joy there was cannot be described.

**771.** Having seen the rajabhoga and the arati[^99],
all gladly ate prasada.

**772.** Sri Thakura Mahashaya, having eaten prasada,
had the remnants from his plate distributed to the disciples.

**773.** On another day Narasimha brought his wife,
and Narottama Gosani gave her the mantra.

**774.** Listen, listeners, to the story of yet another day;
I shall describe what took place.

**775.** One day two brahmanas, having seen a dream,
came gladly to Narottama.

**776.** Bowing, both say, "We have seen a dream
that we took the sacred mantra from you."

**777.** Hearing this, Narottama gave both the mantra of Krishna,
and both brahmanas were utterly overwhelmed with prema.

**778.** They are two brothers, Radhi brahmanas of the Savarna gotra:
Sri Balarama and Rupa Narayana[^100].

**779.** The prema-bhakti of both is extraordinary;
their home is in the village of Sri Kheturi.

**780.** Narottama, seeing the prema-bhakti of both,
appointed them to the service of the sacred deities.

**781.** Both are qualified for the bhajana of Sri Krishna;
at Kheturi everyone calls them Pujari.

**782.** Their zeal in bhajana cannot be described;
great is Narottama Thakura's mercy upon them.

**783.** Narottama Thakura's disciples are countless;
in describing the branches I shall give only a general indication.

**784.** I shall describe yet another day,
by hearing of which sins are destroyed.

**785.** Little by little Sri Phalguna Purnima came,
and all the mahantas came here.

**786.** To subdue all the pashandis,
[the Prabhus] displayed one great wonder of power[^101].

**787.** On the third day after Sri Phalguna Purnima
they held a great assembly with delight in their hearts.

**788.** A great many people gathered at the assembly;
I shall record the names of Chaitanya's companions.

**789.** Shyamananda came with Rasika and other devotees;
full of enthusiasm came Hridaya Chaitanya and others;

**790.** Achyutananda, Krishna Mishra, Gopala, Yadava,
Shyamadasa, Yadunatha, Madhava Acharya and all the rest;

**791.** Vasudha, Jahnava, Ganga and Virachandra,
Madhava Acharya and others, and Sundarananda;

**792.** with Yadunandana and others, Sri Raghunandana,
Sridasa, Gokulananda and Sulochana;

**793.** Raja Vira Hambira, Krishnavallabha, Vyasa —
all came to Kheturi, and Srinivasa too.

**794.** A great many people gathered at the assembly;
a great many pashandis also entered the assembly.

**795.** Srinivasa expounded the Srimad Bhagavata,
and Virabhadra Gosvami delivered a discourse[^102]:

**796.** the Vaishnava dharma is greater than all dharmas —
take up this dharma, all of you, making your minds firm.

**797.** Thus it is said:
*"Ganapatya, and likewise Saura, Shaiva, Shakta, in order;
of all these dharmas the Vaishnava [is held to be] the chief."*

**798.** *"The Vaishnava is the highest dharma, the Vaishnava is the highest austerity,
the Vaishnava is the highest object of worship, the Vaishnava is the highest guru."*[^103]

**799.** Never, brothers, take a non-Vaishnava as guru;
leave such a guru and serve a Vaishnava Gosani.

**800.** The mantra of Krishna surpasses all mantras;
take this mantra, all of you, and become foremost among men.

**801.** Thus in the Gautamiya[-tantra]:
*"Among the mantras of the Ganapatyas, Sauras, Shaivas and Shaktas, O keeper of vows,
and among all the Vaishnava ones, the mantras of Krishna yield the greatest fruit."*

**802.** This mantra should be taken from a Vaishnava belonging to a sampradaya,
and the mantra of one outside a sampradaya should be rejected.

**803.** Thus in the Gautamiya[-tantra]:
*"For those instructed through a sampradaya perfection will surely come;
but mantras outside a sampradaya are held to be fruitless."*

**804.** And in the Padma[-purana]:
*"Therefore in the age of Kali there will be four [founders of] sampradayas —
Sri, Brahma, Rudra and Sanaka: Vaishnavas who purify the earth."*

**805.** *"Mantras that are outside a sampradaya are held to be fruitless:
they bring no perfection by sadhana even in hundreds of millions of kalpas."*[^104]

**806.** A mantra [that has come] from Krishna through the succession of gurus —
that is what the panditas call a mantra of the sampradaya.

**807.** But a mantra of Krishna received from a non-Vaishnava
is everywhere known as a mantra outside the sampradaya.

**808.** Ganapatyas, Sauras, Shaktas, Shaivas,
offenders and the like — all are called non-Vaishnavas.

**809.** Whoever has received the mantra of Krishna from a non-Vaishnava
certainly goes to hell.

**810.** Therefore, obeying the command of the shastras,
one should take the sacred mantra anew from a Vaishnava.

**811.** Thus in the Hari-bhakti-vilasa, [in the section] on the greatness of the guru:
*"With a mantra given by a non-Vaishnava one goes to hell;
let him therefore receive it again, properly and according to the rules, from a Vaishnava guru."*

**812.** Whoever has received the mantra of Krishna is called a Vaishnava;
a devotee of Vishnu is certainly greater than a brahmana.

**813.** Thus in the Hari-bhakti-vilasa:
*"A man who has received initiation into [the mantra of] Vishnu and is devoted to Vishnu's service
is called a Vaishnava by the knowing; any other is a non-Vaishnava."*

**814.** And elsewhere:
*"One who is devoted to the Hari-nama, intent on the worship of Krishna,
who receives the mantra of Krishna and knows Vishnu — he is a Vaishnava."*

**815.** *"Even a chandala devoted to Vishnu is the best of the twice-born;
but one without devotion to Vishnu, though twice-born, is lower than a dog-eater."*

**816.** In the Bhakti-sandarbha:
*"Even a dog-eater, O lord of the earth, if he is a devotee of Vishnu, is higher than a twice-born;
but one without devotion to Vishnu, even a sannyasi, is lower than a dog-eater."*

**817.** One who is devoted to Krishna is not a shudra;
a twice-born without devotion to Krishna is called the lowest of shudras.

**818.** Thus it is said:
*"The devotees of Bhagavan are not shudras: they are the best of Bhagavatas;
the shudras in all varnas are those who are not devoted to Janardana."*

**819.** As bronze by the action of rasa attains the nature of gold,
so a man by initiation from Krishna attains twice-born status.

**820.** Thus in the Hari-bhakti-vilasa, [in the section] on the greatness of initiation:
*"As bronze by the rules of [alchemical] treatment becomes gold,
so by the rules of initiation twice-born status arises in men."*[^105]

**821.** This Narottama is born of a Kayastha family;
some, calling him a shudra, treat him with contempt.

**822.** A devotee of Krishna is greater than a brahmana:
whoever knows the shastras firmly accepts this.

**823.** Attaining twice-born status through initiation from Krishna is the word of the shastras;
whoever disbelieves this goes to the abode of hell.

**824.** He who is called Brahman, Paramatma, Bhagavan —
that Supreme Lord is Krishna, know this for certain.

**825.** One in whom Krishna always dwells within and without —
he is a brahmana, I say with certainty.

**826.** The thread on a brahmana's neck all people see,
but the thread in the heart of a sadhaka is always hidden.

**827.** Whoever, tearing open his breast, shows the sacred thread —
him I count among the brahmanas.

**828.** Narottama is the embodiment of Mahaprabhu's prema,
an avesha-avatara of Nityananda Prabhu.

**829.** He is a part of Nityananda; honour him as the Lord;
tearing open his breast, he will show the sacred thread."

**830.** Having said this, Virachandra fell silent
and bade [Narottama] show the sacred thread to all.

**831.** As in olden times Hanuman in the assembly,
tearing open his breast, showed Sita and Rama[^106] —

**832.** so Narottama Gosani, at the bidding of the assembly,
tearing open his breast, showed the sacred thread.

**833.** The shining thread is like a ray of the sun;
the pashandis cannot bear to look at it.

**834.** Whoever is a devotee looks on with delight in his heart;
seeing it, the pashandis tremble all over in great terror.

**835.** The devotees and all the pashandis,
bowing, offer him many praises.

**836.** Then Narottama, hiding the thread,
showed the pashandis great mercy.

**837.** Then cries arose: "Blessed! Blessed! Blessed!"
All embraced one another.

**838.** Narottama bowed to the companions of Gaura
and, taking their permission, began the sankirtana.

**839.** Having sung for a while, they rest;
Narasimha and Rupa Narayana came and bowed.

**840.** Then Rupa Narayana began to sing,
and Narasimha Raya hung the khol on his shoulder.

**841.** What singing, what playing, what a sweet voice! —
everyone's heart melted beyond measure[^107].

**842.** With his sweet voice he captivated the hearts of the assembly,
and all, raising their arms, began to dance.

**843.** Prabhu Virabhadra, firmly embracing
Sri Rupa Narayana, dances.

**844.** Then Rupa Narayana fell at Prabhu's feet,
and Virachandra in his mercy placed his foot on his head.

**845.** What joy there was cannot be written;
after a while the sankirtana came to an end.

**846.** Prabhu Virabhadra, the abode of all virtues,
asked Rupa Narayana who he was.

**847.** He learned the whole story from beginning to end
and was convinced that this was the shakti of Sri Rupa.

**848.** Virachandra said, "Listen, Rupa Narayana:
your bhakti has melted my heart.

**849.** You are the shakti of Sri Rupa Gosvami;
I bestow on you the title 'Gosvami'."

**850.** Hearing this, Rupa Narayana rejoiced
and clasped the Gosvami's feet with both hands.

**851.** Achyutananda, Krishna Mishra and Sri Gopala,
Srinivasa, Shyamananda and Raghunandana,

**852.** Ramachandra, Santosha Datta, Sri Gokulananda,
Vasudha, Jahnava, Ganga and Sri Govinda —

**853.** all the companions of Gauranga — how many shall I name? —
all showed him fitting favour.

**854.** Rupa Narayana bowed at the feet of all,
and all embraced him with love.

**855.** The mahantas took leave and went to their own places,
and Rupa Narayana stayed here for some time.

**856.** One day Sri Rupa Narayana
asked the Mahashaya for his siddha name.

**857.** Thakura Mahashaya, showing him favour,
gave him the siddha name "Sri Narayani Manjari".

**858.** The greatness of Narottama Thakura is boundless;
what can I, wretched and without bhakti, know how to write?

**859.** My Thakurani in Sri Vrindavana
had promised to send a sacred image.

**860.** She had an image of Sri Radha made for Madanamohana[^108]
and sent it to Vrindavana.

**861.** Seeing it, the Gosvamis were filled with great joy
and placed Sri Radha to the left of Sri Madanamohana.

**862.** The Ishvari's story I do not write further here:
I have described it in detail in the Virachandra-charita[^109].

**863.** My Sri Thakurani had eight sons;
seven gave up their lives at the obeisance of Abhirama[^110].

**864.** The last son is Virabhadra, named Virachandra;
I have recorded his story in the Virachandra-charita.

**865.** At Srikhanda, at the memorial festival of Narahari,
all came and performed a great sankirtana.

**866.** At that time a blind man named Ramai
came to see the kirtana and the dancing[^111].

**867.** He hears the singing but cannot see the dancing at all;
clutching both his eyes, he only cries, "Alas! Alas!

**868.** I cannot see the dancing in Krishna's sankirtana:
for what offence have my eyes been taken from me?"

**869.** Saying this, he weeps bitterly;
Prabhu Virachandra gave him sight.

**870.** Holding his eyes, Prabhu said, "Look, Ramai:
in this sankirtana everyone is dancing."

**871.** Having received his sight, Ramai fell at Prabhu's feet,
and Prabhu placed his foot on his head.

**872.** Then cries of "Blessed! Blessed!" rose in the sky;
all embrace one another with love.

**873.** How Prabhu in his compassion gave him sight
I have written in detail in the Virachandra-charita.

**874.** My Prabhu Virachandra is a merciful Gosani;
there is no end to the disciples he made.

**875.** In the village of Kadra there is a certain Jayagopala;
for disregarding the guru's grace he excommunicated him[^112].

**876.** To Srinivasa and all the mahantas
Virabhadra made it known by letter.

**877.** Here I have written a little, in brief;
in the Virachandra-charita I have described it in detail.

**878.** One day Virachandra, with his mother's leave,
set out for Nilachala with a joyful heart.

**879.** Going there, he beheld Jagannatha
and saw all the places of Mahaprabhu's lila.

**880.** Meeting all of Prabhu's companions who were there,
he came to Gopivallabhapur[^113].

**881.** Having met Shyamananda there,
after some time he arrived at Khardaha.

**882.** Here I have told a little, in brief;
I have described it in detail in the Virachandra-charita.

**883.** Having stayed some time at Khardaha, my Prabhu
set out for Vrindavana with a happy heart.

**884.** From Khardaha by way of Ambika and Shantipur
he came with a joyful heart to Navadvipa.

**885.** Having seen the places of Mahaprabhu's lila,
he went by way of Khanda to Yajigrama.

**886.** Staying there two to four days,
he went by way of Katwa and Budhari to Kheturi.

**887.** Staying some time in the village of Sri Kheturi,
after some days he arrived in Vrindavana.

**888.** Here I have described nothing of what happened on the way:
I have told it in detail in Prabhu's life.

**889.** He met the Gosvamis
and toured the whole Mathura-mandala.

**890.** All this I have recorded in great detail
and with joy in the Virachandra-charita[^114].

**891.** Sri Govinda, Gopinatha, Madanamohana,
Radha-Damodara and Sri Radha-ramana,

**892.** Radha-vinoda and others he beheld;
what joy Prabhu felt cannot be written.

**893.** All that concerns these deities —
how each of them was found — setting it out clearly,

**894.** I have recorded in detail in the Virachandra-charita;
whoever hears it, his heart melts.

**895.** My Prabhu Virachandra, from Vrindavana,
after some days came to Sri Ekachakra.

**896.** He beheld the place of Ekachakra,
where Nityananda Prabhu was born.

**897.** Seeing the places of Nitai's childhood lilas,
with a joyful heart he shed streams of prema from his eyes.

**898.** This I have described in detail in the Virachandra-charita;
from there my Prabhu came to Kheturi.

**899.** Seeing him, Narottama fell at Prabhu's feet,
and Prabhu embraced him with great delight.

**900.** Having beheld the sacred deities, Prabhu
performed nama-sankirtana for some time.

**901.** Having eaten prasada, Prabhu together with Narottama
spoke for some time of what had happened in Vrindavana.

**902.** Hearing the blessing of Lokanatha Gosvami,
Narottama shed tears from both his eyes.

**903.** Staying some time at Sri Kheturi,
he set out from there for Yajigrama.

**904.** The Acharya, hearing of Virachandra's arrival,
came out to meet him and brought him to his own house.

**905.** Srinivasa bowed at Virachandra's feet,
and Prabhu Virachandra embraced him.

**906.** The Ishvari and Gauranga-priya were there too;
they came and bowed at Prabhu's feet[^115].

**907.** Telling of his journey to Vrindavana,
he conveyed the blessing of Sri Bhatta Gosvami.

**908.** Hearing the blessing of his own Prabhu, Srinivasa —
who had not [again] seen his holy feet — heaves deep sighs.

**909.** Staying some time at Yajigrama, Prabhu
came by way of Khanda to Khardaha.

**910.** He bowed at the feet of Vasudha and Jahnava,
and both, laying a hand on him, blessed him.

**911.** Prabhu Virachandra described to everyone
what had happened in Vrindavana.

**912.** Here I have set it out only in brief —
I have recorded it in detail in the Virachandra-charita.

**913.** Something of how Sri Virachandra Prabhu lived in renunciation
I have written in Prabhu's life.

**914.** All the events that I write of in this book —
[I write] in constant remembrance of Prabhu's feet.

**915.** All the shastras say that the guru's command is strongest:
whatever I have written, I wrote at the guru's command.

**916.** He whose hope is the feet of Sri Jahnava and Virachandra,
Nityananda Dasa, tells the Prema-vilasa.

*Thus ends the nineteenth vilasa of the Prema-vilasa.*
