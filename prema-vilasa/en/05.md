# Vilasa 5. Khardaha and Abhirama; the Journey to Vrindavana

*Srinivasa at Khardaha with Jahnava and Virachandra; the test at Abhirama's and the three strokes of the whip; farewell to Narahari and to his mother; Rupa in Vrindavana awaits Srinivasa; the journey by Varanasi and Prayaga; at Mathura Srinivasa learns of the passing of Rupa, Sanatana and Raghunatha Bhatta*

**1.** Glory, glory to Sri Chaitanya, glory to the Merciful One!
Grant me bhakti, that I may write this book and my desire be fulfilled.

**2.** Listen, listen, O hearers, behold this vilasa:
at the mere sight Srinivasa was filled with joy.

**3.** The moment he entered Khardaha,
Srinivasa, maddened with prema, began to dance.

**4.** Virachandra Prabhu was beside his mother;
suddenly Virachandra began to tremble.

**5.** The Thakurani[^1] said: "My son, be attentive:
it seems some bhagavata[^2] has made his presence here.

**6.** I think Srinivasa of Chakhandi has come;
otherwise why should the body so rejoice?

**7.** I hear the clamour of people calling the names of Radha and Krishna;
he was born as the very form of prema — this is his power.

**8.** I hear there is joy everywhere — why should it be so?
Because joy is being born in everyone's heart.

**9.** I have remembered my Lord's command;[^3]
I think that boy has come to the village.

**10.** Find out, my son, be attentive;
then I shall be at ease and my heart will be soothed."

**11.** At that moment Ishana came and said in haste:
"A wondrous boy has come and is weeping at the gate."

**12.** "Go, go, my son, take Ishana with you;
when you see him you will know his virtues — [bring him] before me."

**13.** Crying "Nityananda!", the Prabhu went out,
and seeing the boy's beauty he embraced him.

**14.** At the name "Srinivasa" he remembered [Srivasa] of Navadvipa;[^4]
at the name and the form he was seized by prema and trembled again and again.

**15.** Srinivasa prostrated himself many times and fell at his feet;
taking him by the hand, he raised him and asked his name.

**16.** "What is your name? Seeing you, I am glad."
"My name is Srinivasa; my lot is very poor."

**17.** "Come, come, friend, you have given me great joy;
without any effort fate has brought me a jewel."

**18.** Taking Srinivasa by the hand into the house,
he led him to the inner apartments where Ishvari was.

**19.** With that longing with which Ishvari was longing —
without any effort fate gave her the sweetness of prema.

**20.** Seeing the boy, she was filled with great prema
and cried aloud: "Chaitanya! Nityananda!"

**21.** Saying "Navadvipa!", she heaved deep sighs,
and in separation from Nityananda a great surge of feeling arose in her.

**22.** Taking [Srinivasa] by the hand, Virachandra, in Ishvari's presence,
placed him in her hands: "Give Srinivasa prema.

**23.** He is going to Vrindavana — give him your command quickly;
this is my plea again and again: infuse him with power."

**24.** "If he makes haste and goes to Vrindavana,
then he will have the sight of Sri Rupa's feet.

**25.** If he delays on the way, he will not see him;
if he goes quickly, he will have that sight with joy.

**26.** Srinivasa will be commanded to go quickly;
he will tell all the wonders of the books of the lila.[^5]

**27.** Delay no longer, go to Vrindavana,
and take shelter at the feet of Gopala Bhatta."

**28.** She commanded: "Feed the boy,
and send a letter to Abhirama with Ishana.

**29.** [Abhirama,] all blessing and all perfection lie in your whip:
be sure to strike Srinivasa three times with the whip."[^6]

**30.** Ishvari's remnants filled a vessel;
Virachandra brought them and placed them in his hands.

**31.** Receiving the remnants from her lips, he was flooded with prema:
he laughed, wept, danced and sang like a madman.

**32.** Taking him by the hand, Virachandra seated him beside him,
and at the touch of his hand he came back to outward awareness.

**33.** "Quickly, Srinivasa, go to Vrindavana;
if you delay, you will not meet Rupa."

**34.** Prostrating himself, the Mahashaya took his leave
and then arrived at Abhirama's.

**35.** Ishana handed him the letter and bowed:
"Ishvari's command: grant prema to the boy."

**36.** "Tell me, tell me the good news of Ishvari;
I shall carry it out according to her command.

**37.** Seeing Srinivasa, my heart greatly rejoices:
I have seen prema blossoming in Gauda."

**38.** He gave Ishana a seat to sit on,
and hearing mention of the whip he rejoiced within.

**39.** "Let me see what kind of vessel Ishvari has sent" —
and to test him he gave him eight karas of cowries.[^7]

**40.** "How will the boy manage on this?
When I know his renunciation, I shall strike him with the whip."

**41.** Taking the cowries in his hand, [Srinivasa] pondered long:
"How am I to eat, what provisions should I buy?

**42.** To test me he has put eight karas into my hand;
I mean to cook and eat."

**43.** Going to a merchant's shop he looked over all the provisions
and bought them in due order.

**44.** Having paid, he went into a banana grove
and went down to the water with all his provisions.

**45.** Thakura Sri Abhirama said to two Vaishnavas:
"Go and be guests at Srinivasa's door.

**46.** Knowing the time when he cooks, go to him,
and, [asking him] for food, make plenty of jests and banter."

**47.** Taking leave, they went to where Srinivasa was,
at the time when he was offering [the food] to Radha and Krishna.

**48.** At the end, when he was rinsing his mouth, the two arrived;
seeing Vaishnavas, Srinivasa's heart was glad.

**49.** "We are hungry — feed the two of us."
"This is my good fortune!" he said in humble words.

**50.** "You are devotees of Krishna, and I a wretched soul;
in your mercy accept [me]."

**51.** That offered food he divided into three equal portions
and, with joined palms, said: "Come and eat."

**52.** Having eaten, they rinsed their mouths contentedly,
and the two Vaishnavas went and said before the Gosani:

**53.** "There were no side dishes, yet the rice was like nectar;
as we ate, our hearts were filled with joy.

**54.** Radha and Krishna partook of that food;
as we ate, trembling and tears came over us."[^8]

**55.** Hearing this himself, his heart rejoiced,
and he quickly summoned Ishana before him.

**56.** "Bring Srinivasa before me:
to the one sent by Ishvari I shall give prema."

**57.** He sent Ishana to where Srinivasa was,
and quickly had the whip brought and kept it at his left.

**58.** With Ishana came the brahmana Srinivasa
and, approaching, bowed with joy in his heart.

**59.** He wept in prema with joined palms;
the Gosani rose and struck him with the whip.

**60.** "I have flooded you, flooded you!" he cried, striking with the whip;
Srinivasa was in great joy, his breast heaving with prema.

**61.** He struck three strokes of the whip there before his eyes;
then Malini[^9] came out and caught hold of his hand.

**62.** "You have flooded him with prema, Gosani, strike no more:
this brahmana boy is Chaitanya's power."

**63.** Taking him by the hand, she led him into her inner apartments;
the Thakurani showed him abundant compassion.

**64.** That night he spent happily at the Gosani's;
[in the morning he] called Ishana and gave him to Srinivasa as a companion.

**65.** "Srinivasa, go quickly to Vrindavana,
take shelter at the feet of Gopala Bhatta.

**66.** You will see Sanatana, Rupa Gosani and Lokanatha;
go and behold Raghunatha's renunciation in person.

**67.** Chaitanya's mercy cannot be fathomed at all:
through you he will flood everyone with prema.

**68.** Narahari is watching the road for you;
see him and then go to Vrindavana."

**69.** At the parting he wept long:
"Command me to remain at your feet always."

**70.** "I am utterly small, yet you have shown me mercy;
my desire is fulfilled — there is no illusion in this.

**71.** How shall I spend my time? I am only a child;
give me a command, so that my time is not wasted on a wrong path."

**72.** "Listen, boy, you do not know yourself:
Chaitanya has shown mercy to you.

**73.** You are Chaitanya's power to make prema manifest;
do not delay, set out quickly.

**74.** I too have placed power upon you:
on the way no one will oppose you."

**75.** With a joyful heart he prostrated himself
and took his leave, saying "Gaurahari!"

**76.** In this way he went in due course to Narahari,
prostrated himself and told him everything in detail.

**77.** Seeing his body radiant, he rejoiced:
"Go quickly to Vrindavana — you have obtained everything."

**78.** He came and took prasada; afternoon came on,
and Sarakara Thakura embraced Srinivasa.

**79.** He prostrated himself many times, falling to the ground;
his body quivered with prema, his eyes brimmed with tears.

**80.** "Delay is unbearable, my son; go to Vrindavana;
go quickly and your desire will be fulfilled.

**81.** Go to your mother, ask her humbly,
and set out on the journey with her leave."

**82.** In the evening he came and bowed at his mother's feet
and told her everything from beginning to end.

**83.** At the mention of going to Vrindavana she was stunned;
the grief of separation from her son swelled in her breast.

**84.** "The Lord took my husband; I have one son, Srinivasa;
how shall I, helpless and alone, go on living?

**85.** O cruel fate, what shall I say to you?
My son is going — I think you mean to blind me.

**86.** Leaving your mother alone,[^10] have you no fear?
How can you go, my son, and become so heartless?

**87.** How shall I stay at home — I do not know at all;
in the three worlds there is no one so forsaken."

**88.** Seeing his mother weep, he was grieved at heart
and, entreating her humbly, consoled her at length.

**89.** "My body was nurtured by you, I was born of you;
in no way can I repay my debt to you.

**90.** What am I to do? I cannot steady my heart;
give me leave quickly to go to Vrindavana.

**91.** Be merciful and command me to go to Vrindavana,
otherwise life will not remain in my body."

**92.** So both spent the night with separation in their hearts:
there was no sleep; only their souls tossed in anguish.

**93.** Towards the end of the night, after seeing a dream, he came to himself[^11]
and with a joyful heart rose and set out on the journey.

**94.** That night in Vrindavana Sri Rupa Gosani,
seeing Srinivasa's delay, was much grieved.

**95.** From separation from Sanatana illness had come upon his body;
rising at dawn, he went to see the samadhi.[^12]

**96.** He wept long, calling "Srinivasa!":
"Still the sweetness of prema has not come!"

**97.** Full of anxiety he came to Jiva;
all were together on the bank of the Yamuna.

**98.** Seeing Sri Rupa, all prostrated themselves,
and he greeted and embraced each as was fitting.

**99.** "You all sit at ease on the bank of the Yamuna,
while Srinivasa has not come — he has fallen into trouble.

**100.** He set out to come to Vrindavana;
why has he been so delayed on the way?

**101.** As the form of prema he was born in a brahmana family;
somehow we would have met, had he come earlier.

**102.** You are renunciants, none of you will go to Gauda;
so there was no meeting, and now it is very late."

**103.** As he spoke, he took Sri Jiva by the hand:
"I have no other recourse — what am I to do?

**104.** Listen, listen, Jiva, I tell you for certain:
he set out at night from Yajigrama.

**105.** Be all of you on the watch for his arrival,
until he comes to Sri Vrindavana."

**106.** Hearing this command, all felt boundless joy
and became attentive to carry it out.

**107.** I cannot describe the journey on the road in full;
what is needful, that I write in its order.

**108.** Ever joyful at heart he goes along the road;
in five days he reached Rajmahal.

**109.** A very young boy travels the road
and at times weeps, crying "Ha Chaitanya!"

**110.** "Where are Rupa, Sanatana, Raghunatha Bhatta?
When shall I have such fortune as to see them in person?"

**111.** Seeing Garhi-dvara,[^13] he reached Patna;
sometimes he fasts, sometimes he eats a little.

**112.** Once in two or three days he eats a roti or two
and spends the nights in gardens.

**113.** His golden body has dried up; he walks without food
and at times weeps in a choking voice.

**114.** Twice a day he unfailingly chants Hari-nama;
that day he crossed the Ganga and reached Varanasi.

**115.** At the ghat where Prabhu Chaitanya had bathed,
he went up onto the ghat and bowed.

**116.** North of the ghat is the house of Chandrashekhara;[^14]
to the left of the door there is a lovely spot,

**117.** where Sanatana Gosani, in the garb of a dervish,
sat longing for the sight of the Prabhu.

**118.** There is a tulasi platform there; he bowed to it,
and, leaving it behind, went into the inner part of the house.

**119.** Going in, he saw an aged Vaishnava
and bowed to him in deep humility.

**120.** He rose, embraced him and honoured him:
"Where have you come from, and what is your name?"

**121.** He told him: "My name is Srinivasa;
my birthplace is Chakhandi, near the bank of the Ganga."

**122.** Seeing him, the old man rejoiced
and began to tell everything from beginning to end.

**123.** "Sri Chandrashekhara Acharya is my guru;
by his command I live here — I tell you truly.

**124.** See, this is the place where Mahaprabhu sat;
I live here and serve — his command is binding on me."

**125.** Walking around it, he bowed;
now he wept, now he rolled on the ground.

**126.** "O pitiless fate, what shall I say to you?
You had me born like this only to give me sorrow.

**127.** Why was this sinner born so late?
I could not see Mahaprabhu's devotees."

**128.** He lamented long and wept long;
his grief grew and he lost consciousness.

**129.** Then at length Srinivasa came to himself;
seeing the signs of mahabhava, [the old man] grew very fond of him.

**130.** He fed him with great affection:
"My great good fortune today is beyond words."

**131.** The two spent the night in the delight of talk about Krishna,
and at dawn he took leave of him.

**132.** On the second day he arrived at Prayaga,
bathed at the Triveni and stayed there.

**133.** The next day he walked on and on along the highway;
a pious man put four paisa into his hand.

**134.** On that he managed for two days;
from the toil of the road his body grew thin and weak.

**135.** He asked how far Vrindavana was;
people said: "Four days' journey."

**136.** The next day, having bathed at a well,
he lay down to rest under a tree.[^15]

**137.** From Vrindavana came five Vrajavasis
and sat down under a tree near the water.

**138.** Srinivasa saw that they were very tired
and gave them water: "Wash your feet, Thakuras."

**139.** Having bathed and done their remembrance, at the time for a light meal
they put chickpeas and gur into the end of Srinivasa's cloth.[^16]

**140.** Sitting down, he ate, and his weariness went away;
they talked a great deal with one another.

**141.** All the good news of Nilachala and Gauda
he told, and all the Vaishnavas felt boundless joy.

**142.** "Tell us, Thakuras, in your kindness, about Vrindavana:
who lives where, who is staying where?"

**143.** They name them, and he bows:
"There live those named Rupa and Sanatana,

**144.** the two Bhattas, and one named Lokanatha Gosani,
Bhugarbha, Sri Jiva" — so they named them all.

**145.** "What shall I tell you, brother, if you would hear it all:
when Sanatana withdrew from sight, we felt great pain.

**146.** It is now four months since he withdrew;
just to hear of it, the soul writhes."

**147.** Bowing to them all, he goes on along the road,
as though mountains had broken and fallen on his head.

**148.** "Never since my birth have I known such sorrow:
where I was going to find happiness, fate has given me sorrow."

**149.** That day, sustained by that food, he walked very fast,
and the next day he reached Agra.

**150.** As he walked, his heart grew restless;
leaving the highway on his left, he went to Gokula.

**151.** Crossing the Yamuna, he goes to Nanda's abode
and with great humility beholds it and bows.[^17]

**152.** At dawn he came to Mathura, the birthplace of Krishna,
offered prayers there and bowed.

**153.** Every place that is there he saw;
his body trembled and tears flowed from his eyes.

**154.** Seeing the beauty of Mathura, he reflects in his heart:
"I have heard from the scriptures that it is higher even than Vaikuntha."

**155.** A great hubbub, singing, someone dancing;
in this way he came to the Vishrama ghat, where Krishna rested.

**156.** He beholds it, touches it, sprinkles the water on his head,
and knows in his heart the good fortune of many births.

**157.** He sits gazing towards the east,
and three Vrajavasis go by, talking among themselves.

**158.** One speaks and another listens: "What will become of us?
Three have withdrawn from sight — there is great pain in our hearts.

**159.** First Sanatana withdrew;
some days after him, Raghunatha Bhatta;

**160.** and now Sri Rupa Gosani has withdrawn;
life will not stay in the body, the soul writhes."

**161.** Srinivasa hears what they are saying:
"Fate has turned my misfortune into utter hopelessness."

**162.** With joined hands he addressed them:
"What were you three saying to one another?"

**163.** They said: "Brother, what are you asking?
What shall we tell you of the pain in our hearts?

**164.** Vrindavana is empty, yet death does not come;
in separation from Rupa life cannot be sustained."

**165.** As soon as he heard this, Srinivasa rose from the spot:
"What blame shall I lay on fate? My heart is bursting.

**166.** My eyes cannot see the road — where shall I go?
Fate has drowned me in an ocean of sorrow."

**167.** Until midnight[^18] he walks along the road;
weeping and weeping he goes, clutching his head with his hands.

**168.** Going some way in the direction of his homeland,[^19]
he came to a tree and lay fallen beneath it.

**169.** All the torments that came into his heart at that time —
how much can I write? He alone knows them.

**170.** Steeling my hard heart, I have written this;
hearing it, the heart of this wicked one does not burst.

**171.** I write only because my guru's command is binding on me;
apart from that, what do I know? I am ignorant.

**172.** He whose hope is the feet of Sri Jahnava and Virachandra,
Nityananda Dasa, tells the Prema-vilasa.

*Thus ends the fifth vilasa of the Prema-vilasa.*

[^1]: The Thakurani, Ishvari: here Jahnava, Virachandra's mother.
[^2]: That is, a devotee.
[^3]: Nityananda's, Jahnava's husband.
[^4]: Srivasa (Srinivasa) Pandita, Mahaprabhu's companion in Navadvipa; the likeness of the names reminded Virachandra of him.
[^5]: The books of the Gosvamis of Vrindavana about Radha and Krishna.
[^6]: Abhirama's whip: according to tradition, anyone he struck with it was filled with prema.
[^7]: A kara is a group of four cowrie shells; eight karas (32 cowries) is a trifling sum.
[^8]: Couplets 54–55 are found only in the 1913 edition.
[^9]: Malini here is apparently Abhirama's wife (not the wife of Srivasa).
[^10]: Literally "making [yourself] motherless".
[^11]: It is unclear whether Srinivasa or his mother is meant; taken as Srinivasa.
[^12]: The tomb of Sanatana Gosvami in Vrindavana.
[^13]: Garhi (Teliagarhi), the pass between Bengal and Bihar. This line is found only in the 1913 edition.
[^14]: Chandrashekhara, a resident of Varanasi with whom Mahaprabhu stayed (not to be confused with Chandrashekhara of Navadvipa).
[^15]: This couplet is found only in the 1999 edition.
[^16]: Gur: unrefined cane sugar.
[^17]: This couplet is found only in the 1999 edition; "Nanda's abode" (nandālaya) is apparently Nandagrama or Nanda's house in Gokula.
[^18]: Literally "until two praharas of the night".
[^19]: Literally "facing his country"; the sense is unclear, perhaps "wherever his feet took him".
