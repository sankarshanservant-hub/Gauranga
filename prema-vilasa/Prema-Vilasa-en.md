# Prema-vilasa

*"The Pastimes of Love"*

*A poem by Nityananda Dasa*

*Translated from the Bengali*

---

## Translator's note

The *Prema-vilasa* is a Bengali poem by Nityananda Dasa (Balarama Dasa), a disciple of Jahnava Devi,
on the lives of Srinivasa Acharya, Narottama Thakura and Shyamananda: their training in Vrindavana under
Jiva Gosvami, the bringing of the Gosvamis' books to Bengal, and their preaching in Gauda, Orissa and
Vishnupura.

**Source.** The edition of 1913 (Calcutta, Bagbazar; public domain), collated from two independent OCR
copies (the 1913 edition and its 1999 reprint). **Only the poem itself** is translated: the editors'
prefaces and notes are not. Vilasas 1–23 are translated; the twenty-fourth vilasa (a late genealogical
addition) and the appendix are not included.

**Principles.** The payar couplets are translated line by line, without rhyme, and numbered within each
vilasa (the original has no numbering); tripadi stanzas, songs and Sanskrit verses are numbered as single
units. Corrupt passages are noted in the translator's notes at the end of each vilasa. Names follow
`GLOSSARY.md`.

# Vilasa 1. The Description of Srinivasa Acharya's Birth

*Mahaprabhu's distress that Gauda has lost bhakti; counsel with Sarvabhauma; Jagannatha's command to grant prema to the son of the childless brahmana Chaitanya Dasa; the Earth gives prema to Lakshmipriya; Srinivasa's birth at Chakhandi*

**1.** *I did not worship your lotus feet in the Kali age,*
*I did not see your golden form in the Kali age,*
*I did not hear the tales of your truth in the Kali age —*
*O Chaitanya-chandra, you have left me deprived!*[^01-1]

**2.** Glory, glory to Sri Chaitanya, glory to Nityananda!
Glory to Advaita-chandra, glory to the host of Gaura's devotees!

**3.** Glory, glory to Sri Jahnava, glory to Virachandra!
Glory, glory to the mantra of Hari-nama in the Kali age![^01-2]

**4.** Glory, glory to Srinivasa Acharya Thakura,
whose disciple Ramachandra is the sprout of prema!

**5.** Glory, glory to Kaviraja Thakura Govinda,
by whose virtues the beings of the seven islands rejoice!

**6.** Glory, glory to the listeners — give your attention,
you whose very life the pastimes of Radha and Krishna will become.

**7.** How the birth of Acharya Thakura came about —
listen, brothers, with devotion and a steady heart.

**8.** Mahaprabhu sent Nityananda Prabhu to Gauda,
and he flooded Gauda with prema-bhakti.

**9.** All the Vaishnavas who came from Gauda
Mahaprabhu questioned in every detail.

**10.** Some said: "In Gauda there is no Hari-nama;
there is no deliverance for good people or for bad."

**11.** Some said: "Acharya Gosani has left bhakti
and everywhere makes people accept mukti as the chief thing."

**12.** Some said: "He has no word but mukti;
speaking of mukti, Gosani has flooded the world."

**13.** As he listened, the Lord's anger arose,
and the grief of separation from Nityananda grew in him.

**14.** At this time Svarupa and Ramananda Raya came to the Lord;
they wished to speak, and there was joy in their hearts.

**15.** "Come in, come in, it is good that you two have come!
Gauda has become empty of bhakti — hear the reason.

**16.** Advaita Acharya, the very form of Ishvara,
has left bhakti and expounds the five kinds of mukti.[^01-3]

**17.** I cannot understand Advaita's mind.
Tell me, both of you, how bhakti is to be kept.

**18.** I cannot believe that he teaches mukti,
for he is a chief participant in these pastimes.[^01-4]

**19.** I hear it from people's mouths, but I am not convinced:
to expound mukti, leaving bhakti, is not his intent."

**20.** At this time a letter came from Nityananda:
"Leaving the path of bhakti, the Acharya preaches mukti."

**21.** On receiving the letter the Lord was greatly alarmed;
holding the letter in his own hand he went for darshan.

**22.** With his devotees the Lord went into the temple
and beside Garuda[^01-5] beheld the Lord with joy in his heart.

**23.** At that time Sarvabhauma Bhattacharya came;
seeing him, the Lord was overcome with ecstasy.

**24.** Crying "Bhakti, bhakti!" the Lord was filled with prema
and, rejecting the explanations of mukti, began to extol bhakti.

**25.** Embracing the Bhattacharya, he came out
and, reaching Mishra's residence,[^01-6] grew somewhat calmer.

**26.** Nityananda Prabhu's letter was in his hand.
"Read the letter, Bhattacharya," the Lord commanded.

**27.** Reading the letter, the Bhattacharya flew into a great rage:
"It seems no one in Gauda has any understanding!

**28.** Who is this who rejects bhakti and preaches mukti?
The three of us will go to that place.

**29.** In debate, Prabhu, I will silence him,
and if he is obstinate I will bind him and bring him here."[^01-7]

**30.** At the Bhattacharya's words the Lord's heart rejoiced:
"There will be no hindrance to bhakti — listen, Mahashaya.

**31.** One letter under my own signature will go to Advaita,
and another he will write to Sri Nityananda Prabhu."

**32.** Saying "Good, good," they settled this plan firmly
and sent the letters to Gauda by the hand of Vaishnavas.

**33.** Hearing this, the high-minded Bhattacharya
joined his palms and told of his own former plight:

**34.** "Studying logic, I knew not a speck of bhakti;
now, leaving the explanations of mukti, I find a special joy in bhakti.

**35.** How long I spent chewing the dry oil-cake of logic!
Then I came to talk with Gopinatha Acharya.

**36.** He is not ill-minded nor under maya — he is the Lord's devotee;
how should he not know the truth of the Lord's own nature?

**37.** Through my bond with him the Lord showed me mercy:
all my wrong thinking went, and bhakti was born in my heart.

**38.** He is very dear to the Lord, a king among devotees;
his work is to bring the world to understanding."

**39.** All went off to their own places,
yet joy did not arise in the Lord's mind.

**40.** The more he thought, the more his anxiety grew:
"Living beings have become empty of bhakti!" — and fear arose in him.

**41.** "How will bhakti remain upon the earth?
I want to send some prema and the Name to Gauda.

**42.** While Nityananda is here, how is this to be done?
And when he is gone, how will bhakti remain among the beings?

**43.** To reveal the scriptures of bhakti, Rupa and Sanatana,
the two brothers, went to Vrindavana.

**44.** That bhakti I want to reveal in Gauda;
I want a vessel to be born who is prema in form."

**45.** "Avani! Avani!"[^01-8] the Lord called out,
and the Earth with joined palms came before the Lord.

**46.** "Listen, listen, Earth, and be attentive:
bring a vessel who is prema in form and give him a dwelling."

**47.** "The prema that you, my Lord, have left in my keeping —
give the command, and I will make it manifest in a form."

**48.** Delighted, the Lord gave the Earth his command,[^01-9]
but of who was worthy to be the vessel, and of his name, nothing was said.[^01-10]

**49.** At this time Svarupa and Ramananda Raya came to the Lord;
bowing to the Lord, they wished to speak to him.

**50.** "What am I to do, what will happen? It is good that you have come" —
and he told them all that had passed with the Earth.

**51.** Crying "Prema, prema!" the Lord was overcome
and, calling "Nityananda!", began to weep.

**52.** The Lord fell senseless; the third watch[^01-11] went by;
in a sweet voice Svarupa sang Hari-nama to him.

**53.** On hearing Hari-nama the Lord came to himself:
"Come, Svarupa, let us go and see the Lord."

**54.** At this time Sarvabhauma appeared before the Lord;
seeing Sarvabhauma, the Lord was very glad.

**55.** "It is good that you have come; sit here.
There is a special matter — listen carefully.

**56.** Advaita Acharya has put away the path of bhakti.
What am I to say, what am I to do — tell me, Bhattacharya."

**57.** Hearing of this hindrance to bhakti, the Bhatta was much grieved,
and then he spoke at Mahaprabhu's feet:

**58.** "Advaita Acharya is the lord of the world;
such words will never come from his mouth.

**59.** Some insolent people[^01-12] came and told this to the Lord.
Bring those people before me!"

**60.** They searched, but those people could not be found,
and the Bhatta declared them to be great ignoramuses.[^01-13]

**61.** "Listen, listen, Bhattacharya, I will tell you of the past:
ever since I left Navadvipa, he has been in great sorrow.

**62.** I cannot tell what is in his mind in that sorrow:
to frighten me he acts as he pleases.

**63.** He has the power to do anything:
what he does comes to pass and is never in vain.

**64.** I have faith in his word,
and I cannot go against his command.

**65.** Devise a plan, then, so that his command is not slighted
and that, by this plan, prema is preserved hereafter."

**66.** The Bhattacharya said: "Prabhu, while Nityananda is here,
while he is present, prema will meet no obstacle.

**67.** What will happen when he is gone — what can I say?
Do whatever is in your mind.

**68.** Of this, Prabhu, my own Mayavada is witness:
leaving mukti, I expound bhakti by your grace.

**69.** By seeing you, Prabhu, my bondage was destroyed,
and leaving mukti I became a servant on the path of bhakti.

**70.** The people of the Kali age are very wicked,
and the chief cause of it is the rule of the yavana kings.[^01-14]

**71.** All that the kings' rule had brought, he did away with,
and he revealed the great medicine, the mantra of Hari-nama.

**72.** By the mere shadow of the Name he destroyed sins;
Nityananda, your own portion, established bhakti.

**73.** Such a Nityananda Prabhu you sent to Gauda —
why then do you begin to worry about the future?"

**74.** "All that is true; but listen with attention:
I sent Nityananda off together with the devotees,

**75.** I myself parted from Nityananda.
What am I to do — give me your counsel."

**76.** "No one can counsel you better
than One — in Nilachala there is Jagannatha Raya."

**77.** "The Bhatta Mahashaya has given a fine solution:
without Jagannatha this cannot be settled."

**78.** Having so resolved, they all went for darshan
and prayed that prema might be preserved hereafter:

**79.** "You are an ocean of compassion, most merciful.
We pray to you, Lord — be sure to answer.

**80.** In the Kali age you descended in the form of Jagannatha;
seeing you and believing, people have been delivered.

**81.** With the sweetness and fragrance of your prasada you flooded the land,
making the varnas and ashramas and even the chandalas one.

**82.** In this way let the pastimes of Radha and Krishna spread,
and in granting favour or punishment let no one weigh who is worthy."[^01-15]

**83.** The fourteen-cubit swinging garland[^01-16] broke from the Lord's neck;
the pujari brought it and held it out before the Lord.

**84.** Delighted, the Lord came back to his lodging,
and his heart was filled with joy beyond measure.

**85.** Free from worry, he lay down to rest,
and Jagannatha came to his bedside.

**86.** Smiling, Jagannatha said to him:
"Is my power so very much greater than yours?

**87.** For a long time now there has been a brahmana;
childless, he came to pray for a son.

**88.** Whenever he came for darshan he begged for the boon of a son,
always weeping, with an anguished heart.

**89.** Seeing the brahmana so distressed, I was moved to great compassion
and, being pleased, granted him the boon of a son.

**90.** His name is Chaitanya Dasa Acharya;
from him will come that most worthy vessel, the embodiment of prema.[^01-17]

**91.** You shall entrust the prema to him,
so that the brahmana grieves no more.

**92.** Lakshmipriya is his wife, the daughter of Balarama[^01-18] —
virtuous, devoted to her husband, most blessed."

**93.** At that moment Mahaprabhu awoke,
and calling "Jagannatha!" he wept a long while.

**94.** Calling Kashi Mishra, the Lord asked him:
"Show me the Gaudiya Chaitanya Dasa.

**95.** Jagannatha has given me a command concerning him;
take pains and bring him quickly."

**96.** Mishra said: "Prabhu, many days have passed;
weeping, the brahmana went back to his own country."

**97.** The Lord said: "Do you know where his home is?"
Mishra said: "I will find it out for certain."

**98.** At this time Jagadananda came from Vrindavana,
and the Lord began to ask him about Sanatana's welfare.

**99.** He said: "All are in perfect fulfilment and bliss,"
and told the Lord how each one was.

**100.** "After seeing Mother's feet I came to Navadvipa,
and then I came to Shantipur, to the Acharya.

**101.** At my leave-taking Gosani gave me a command:
'What I say, repeat it to Him.'[^01-19]

**102.** And he spoke a riddle."[^01-20] Hearing this, Mahaprabhu said:
"Words such as he spoke I have never heard."

**103.** Svarupa and others were there together with Mahaprabhu;
hearing the riddle, they all began to laugh.

**104.** From that day the Lord's condition changed:
the malady of separation from Krishna grew twofold.

**105.** "What was said has come to pass[^01-21] — you two have heard it.
Consider how prema may be preserved.

**106.** Jagannatha has commanded me to see the brahmana;
take pains to bring him — I wish to send him to his country."

**107.** Meanwhile the Earth could not bear the weight of prema,
and an earthquake struck all of Nilachala-puri.

**108.** Day and night Nilachala shook.
"This is no earthquake, brothers — it is Chaitanya doing all this!

**109.** Earlier Chaitanya gave prema to the ocean
and flooded Nilachala-puri with prema.

**110.** The ocean, it seems, could not hold that prema;
taking the prema from it, he gave it to the Earth.

**111.** The Earth cannot hold it and is shaking;
houses may collapse and people may die!

**112.** All this time we have lived in Nilachala, brothers —
and now Chaitanya-chandra has come and does such things!"

**113.** They all took counsel together on what should be done:
"Let us go to a land where everyone's life will be safe."

**114.** One says: "There is no land apart from the earth;
come what may, I shall stay right here."

**115.** One says: "You have no sons or children,
and besides, you are poor and own no property —

**116.** for fear of what would you leave Nilachala?
Either way we shall all die.

**117.** If Jagannatha protects us in this calamity,
then easily, brothers, we shall remain in the world."

**118.** One says: "Brother, what will Jagannatha do?
Chaitanya's rasa, brother, will only grow twofold!"

**119.** One says: "Let us all gather together,
go to Sri Krishna Chaitanya and lay it before him."

**120.** Saying "Good, good," they all gathered together
and arrived at Mishra's door.

**121.** The crowd made a great uproar at the door,
and the Lord came out with Svarupa and the others.

**122.** Seeing the Lord, the distressed people of Nilachala —
children, old men, young men, householders and ascetics — cried out:

**123.** "When the city was floating in the water, you saved it;
now the city is shaking — a calamity has come.

**124.** This time save us, Lord Gaurachandra!
With what mantra have you made the earth unsteady?

**125.** There is none but you to save us in calamity;
in fear we have come to plead with you.

**126.** You are the purifier of the fallen, most merciful;
do not forsake us who have taken refuge in Jagannatha."[^01-22]

**127.** At this time the pujari, bringing Jagannatha's prasada,
came to where the Lord was.

**128.** Seeing the prasada, Mahaprabhu rose,
bowed down and placed the prasada beside him.

**129.** The pujari said: "Prabhu, I cannot perform the service:
whenever I lay my hands on Jagannatha, my whole body spins.[^01-23]

**130.** What am I to do? Save us, Prabhu — the service has stopped!
In fear I have come to tell you."

**131.** Then the Lord called the Earth:
"For some days stay still."

**132.** Hearing what people were saying, the pujari was afraid:
"It is Advaita Mahashaya who has landed us in this calamity!"

**133.** With joined palms the pujari addressed the Lord.
"Do your service," said the Lord, and he laid his hands on Jagannatha's body.

**134.** Dismissing the pujari, the Lord said before the people:
"Go, go, brothers, all of you go home in peace.

**135.** There will be no earthquake; I will pray to Jagannatha,
and I will beg it of the Earth as alms."

**136.** Humbly dismissing all the people,
he began to think about the brahmana Chaitanya Dasa.

**137.** Thinking so, he called the Earth to mind;
the Earth came and bowed to the Lord.

**138.** "What is your command, Prabhu?" the Earth asked.
The Lord asked where Chaitanya Dasa lived.

**139.** The Earth said: "Prabhu, many bear that name;
how shall I know for certain?"

**140.** The Lord said: "For the sake of a son, before Jagannatha,
for a whole year he prayed with body and mind.

**141.** That Chaitanya Dasa you must seek out;
the father of his wife Lakshmipriya is Balarama Dasa."[^01-24]

**142.** "As you command," said the Earth and departed,
and on the third day she came and reported to the Lord:

**143.** "He lives at Chakhandi, very pure in conduct;
in his body there is no trace of sin.

**144.** For the sake of a son he began a purashcharana,[^01-25]
and soon he left, entrusting it to Jagannatha."

**145.** The Lord said: "Earth, you have been a great help:
Jagannatha has kept his word about prema firm.

**146.** Listen, listen, Earth, listen with care:
take the prema and give it to Lakshmipriya.

**147.** Give her all the prema, keep nothing back.
My word is true — you must carry it out."[^01-26]

**148.** Overjoyed, the Earth began to dance,
brought the prema and laid it before Lakshmipriya.

**149.** And here the Lord, free from worry, began kirtana
and started to dance in the courtyard of Jagannatha's temple.

**150.** Before Jagannatha, with joined palms, the Lord
wept aloud, crying "Srinivasa! Srinivasa!"

**151.** Seeing this, Jagannatha laughed with delight
and sent prema to Chaitanya Dasa.

**152.** Seeing Jagannatha laugh, the Lord laughed too,
and by his command sent prema to Chaitanya Dasa.

**153.** "From this a son named Srinivasa will be born,
and through him there will be a great play of prema.[^01-27]

**154.** To reveal the various scriptures I sent Rupa and Sanatana,
the two brothers, to Sri Vrindavana.

**155.** In Rupa's scriptures the pastimes of Radha and Krishna will be revealed,
and by command they will be entrusted to Srinivasa."

**156.** Having prayed to Jagannatha, he came back to his lodging
and joyfully summoned Kashi Mishra.

**157.** He took secret counsel with Svarupa and Ramananda;
having received Jagannatha's command, his mind was at ease.

**158.** "Tell us, tell us, Prabhu, give us the news!" —
"In Chaitanya Dasa's house prema will be made known.

**159.** In Gauda Nityananda Raya is anxious;
send him a letter and set his mind at rest."

**160.** "Good, good," and the Lord wrote in his own hand:
"Hari-nama sankirtana will be in every house.

**161.** You shall send a letter to Advaita Acharya,
that he should count the state of mukti without bhakti as worthless.

**162.** Do not worry about me hereafter;
the world will float in Hari-nama sankirtana.

**163.** By Jagannatha's command a son will be born by a boon,
who will flood the world with the pastimes of Radha and Krishna.

**164.** Near the bank of the Ganga is a place called Chakhandi;
there lives a worthy brahmana named Chaitanya Dasa.

**165.** To him a son, Srinivasa, will be born, prema in form;
he will appear in the form of a Vaishnava."

**166.** Having written the letter thus, he sent it to Gauda,
and having made prema manifest he remained at peace.

**167.** At this time a letter came from Sanatana;
he wrote in full about the arrival of Gopala Bhatta.

**168.** Hearing of Gopala's arrival in Vrindavana,
the Lord was overjoyed together with his devotees.

**169.** "Listen, listen, Svarupa and Ramananda, to the news:
Gopala Bhatta has come to Vrindavana."

**170.** The Lord spoke much of the Bhatta's greatness,
and all rejoiced to hear it from the Lord's mouth.

**171.** The Lord said: "Consider now what I should do:
what thing of unprecedented sweetness shall I send?

**172.** I am a poor sannyasi; I have no wealth;
all I have is my cord[^01-28] and a seat to sit on.

**173.** In them is my power — hear the reason;
these two things I will give to the Bhatta,

**174.** so that he may sit on it in Rupa's company.
I will have Svarupa write the letter."

**175.** But to Sanatana the Lord himself wrote in his own hand:
"Let Rupa take up the describing of the pastimes in scriptures.

**176.** This letter of mine, read it out to Rupa;
hearing it, your hearts will rejoice.

**177.** In Gauda I will cause a jewel of a vessel to be born;
when his body is grown and fit, I will send him to you later.

**178.** His name is Srinivasa, born in a brahmana family;
he will make known in Gauda the very heart of Radha and Krishna's pastimes.

**179.** When I am no longer present, he will go to Vrindavana;
you shall entrust your books to him.

**180.** To Gauda I have sent Nityananda,
and with him Ramadasa, Gadadhara and Sundarananda.

**181.** For a son Chaitanya Dasa stayed in Nilachala;
Jagannatha gave prema, and he accepted it.

**182.** I too am coming soon to see you all;
make a secluded place for me in some grove.

**183.** Only Svarupa and Ramananda Raya are with me;
it is by the kindness of these two that my life is kept.

**184.** I am sending you the seat by a Vaishnava's hand,
and through Ramananda I have given him money for the road."

**185.** Taking the cord and the seat, the Vaishnava went to Vrindavana.
That day Rupa and Sanatana were together.

**186.** On receiving the letter the two brothers rejoiced,
and seeing the cord and the seat they swooned in prema.

**187.** Placing the cord round their necks, they wept a long while
and fell to the ground, crying "Gaurahari!"

**188.** "Shall we ever again see, Lord, the face of Gora-chand?
Not hearing your sweet words, our breasts are breaking!"

**189.** Rolling on the ground, they wept, clasping the seat to their breasts,
and perceived the sweet fragrance of his holy body.

**190.** At that moment Bhugarbha and Lokanatha arrived
and fell to the ground, a hand pressed to the breast.

**191.** In passing I will write something — listen, hearers —
a mere particle about Lokanatha's renunciation.

**192.** With no companion at all, he sits in seclusion;
with eyes closed, now he weeps and now he laughs.

**193.** Lokanatha Gosani is most deeply dear to the Lord;
Rupa and Sanatana honour him constantly.

**194.** Such will be his disciple Narottama;
he will make prema manifest on earth.

**195.** Narottama by name, a resident of Garerhat,[^01-29]
the son of Krishnananda Raya, a treasury of prema.

**196.** As Rupa and Sanatana are one body,
so, know for certain, are Narottama and Srinivasa.

**197.** Narottama, whose name Gauranga himself gave —
what shall I say of his virtues? They are all beyond compare.

**198.** That same power, those same pastimes he spread abroad;
who can be compared with such a chosen one?

**199.** The virtues of the two Mahashayas cannot be written —
of those who made prema manifest in Gauda.

**200.** The virtues of the two Mahashayas that are recorded
I shall write later, following in their wake.

**201.** Now I shall write of the pain of separation that befell;
seeing it, how could a poor, wretched person keep alive?

**202.** Seeing Sanatana's state, Rupa was stunned:
"If you become like this, everyone will die!

**203.** You are the Lord's second body, Mahashaya;
seeing you so distraught, who can keep his senses?"

**204.** With many efforts Rupa brought him back to consciousness,
but the dreadful trembling of separation grew twofold.

**205.** From that day Sanatana had no rest:
the malady of separation from Gauranga grew twofold.

**206.** Seeing Sanatana, Rupa grew anxious
lest Govinda leave this Vrindavana empty.[^01-30]

**207.** Regaining his composure, Rupa took the seat
and went reverently to the Bhatta.

**208.** The two brothers, carefully holding the two things to their breasts,
went in great happiness to the Bhatta's lodging.

**209.** Prostrating themselves, they gave him the seat and the cord,
and reading the letter they made him hear the sweetness of prema.

**210.** Hearing the honour the letter did him, the Bhatta swooned,
and then, clasping the seat to his breast, he began to weep.

**211.** With care Sri Rupa steadied him somewhat,
and on seeing Sanatana the Bhatta grew calm.

**212.** Sanatana said: "Listen, Bhatta Gosani:
at the time of discourse sit on this seat — there is no fault in it."

**213.** "How can I sit on the Lord's seat?" —
"The Lord has commanded it — how can you disregard it?"

**214.** "The Lord's command is mightiest," said Sri Rupa;
and placing the cord round his neck, the Bhatta sat on the seat.

**215.** All their hearts were filled with joy,
and they went off each to his own grove.

**216.** That night, as Sanatana slept, in the guise of a dream
Gaurangachandra, embracing his neck, said:

**217.** "There is a brahmana youth named Srinivasa,
endowed with the greatest steadiness and other virtues.

**218.** You are my second body, Sanatana;
through Srinivasa you shall accomplish my purpose."

**219.** Having seen the dream, Sanatana rejoiced,
and in the morning, sitting in the assembly, he began to tell it.

**220.** Sanatana said: "Listen to a wondrous account:
the Lord is going to depart, and there is a reason for it.

**221.** The pastimes of Radha and Krishna, through sankirtana,
the Lord savours together with Svarupa and the others.

**222.** The pastimes that he will describe by infusing his power into Rupa
he will make known by sending a vessel.

**223.** There is a brahmana youth named Srinivasa;
through him the Lord will spread these pastimes in Gauda.

**224.** As prema in form he will cause him to be born in Gauda,
and Srinivasa will come here when the Lord's pastimes are ended.

**225.** You, staying in Vrindavana, will see him;
whether I am alive or not, he will be seen.

**226.** To see one chosen by Chaitanya's mercy is a matter of fortune,
and I suppose that such fortune is not mine.

**227.** If Chaitanya's mercy rests on everyone,
then in due time you will all see him in your midst."

**228.** The Bhatta said: "The Lord will send such a treasure —
if fortune allows, I shall see him with my own eyes."

**229.** Rupa said: "I have laboured by the Lord's power;
my labour will bear fruit if he comes soon.

**230.** While I live I will entrust everything to him,
and teaching him all the books, I will make him a pandit."

**231.** Thus all rejoiced together,
knowing that he would deliver the poor, the wretched and the wicked.

**232.** From then on Gopala Bhatta made it his rule
to keep all his observances with the cord tied round his neck.

**233.** One day in the assembly he raised the matter
of what he had seen of Srinivasa in a dream that night:

**234.** "In Chaitanya Dasa's house, in Lakshmipriya's womb...
As soon as he is born he will proclaim the name of Radha and Krishna,

**235.** and joyfully he will deliver all, down to the chandalas."
It turned out that they had all seen this in a dream.

**236.** All at once it dawned on them all:
they had seen a joyful dream and understood its reason.

**237.** Yet all were anxious about the Lord:
"We cannot understand what he intends by this."

**238.** So Sanatana and Rupa remained anxious:
when would Srinivasa come, the embodiment of prema?

**239.** And in Nilachala Svarupa's mind was uneasy:
night and day he saw inauspicious dreams.

**240.** One day Svarupa found the Lord alone
and asked him: "Who is this Srinivasa?

**241.** Tell me his virtues in detail, Prabhu;
merely to hear of his virtues gladdens the heart."

**242.** On hearing the name, Svarupa's joy grew,[^01-31]
and he wrote a letter to Sanatana once more.

**243.** A wondrous letter he wrote to Sanatana:
"Reading it, he will understand the whole matter."

**244.** Meanwhile, in his own house, the brahmana Chaitanya Dasa
was performing purashcharana for the sake of a son.

**245.** Seven purashcharanas he performed near the Ganga,
and in a dream a command came to him from One of golden form:

**246.** "A wondrous son named Srinivasa will be born,
and through him prema will be made manifest."

**247.** To Lakshmipriya the command was given with a hand laid on her head:
"A wondrous son will be born — remain joyful."

**248.** At the mere touch of the Lord's hand she became mad with prema;
coming to herself, Lakshmipriya began to weep.

**249.** Tears, trembling, hair standing on end — and she lost all composure:
Lakshmipriya's body was filled with prema.

**250.** Lakshmipriya said: "Acharya, pay heed:
see, a great being dwells within my body.

**251.** I laugh, I weep, I dance, I sing — such is my state;
all sorrow is gone, I tell you.

**252.** Do not go anywhere and leave me;
perform nama-sankirtana at home day and night."

**253.** The Acharya said: "How shall we get by?[^01-32]
There is no wealth in the house — what shall we eat?"

**254.** Lakshmipriya said: "I have gained great wealth:
your poverty is at an end, your life is fulfilled."

**255.** The village had suffered oppression from the ruler and many troubles;
now it had all calmed down, and the ruler had become well-disposed.

**256.** The zamindar had left the village and was living in another,
but now that the trouble was over he was to return to his own place.

**257.** As soon as he[^01-33] entered the womb, hearts were filled with prema and joy,
and all fear of the yavanas went away without effort.

**258.** Until then people had kept saying "Durga, Shiva";
now no one uttered any name but Krishna's.

**259.** There was an old brahmana there of wicked ways;
he whispered slander into the zamindar's ear:

**260.** "The village will be ruined, brother, by this name;
forbid the people!" — so he came and said.

**261.** "They have left Shiva and Durga and keep saying...[^01-34]
I will plunder their homes — by what strength will they keep them?"

**262.** Calling the kotal[^01-35] and a drummer, Durgadas said:
"Let them say 'Shiva, Durga!' — or else it is ruin for all!"

**263.** The drummer struck his drum for the first time —
and the drum began to sound "Radha-Krishna"!

**264.** Children dance in prema, saying "Radha-Krishna";
women dance, with longing in their hearts.

**265.** At the sound of the drum all the people became intoxicated
and, saying "Radha-Krishna," began to dance.

**266.** The drummer, possessed by prema, dances, weeps and laughs;
the boys dance and tumble down.

**267.** At the sound of the drum all the people were intoxicated
and danced merrily along with the children.

**268.** Dancing, they came to Chaitanya Dasa's house;
seeing the zamindar, the brahmana grew anxious at heart.

**269.** With honour he seated him on a seat,
and in his flurry knew not what to do or say.

**270.** Courteously he dismissed the people[^01-36]
and with honour had Durgadas bathe.

**271.** A great deal of food came together,
and the Acharya brought it and set it before Durgadas.

**272.** Seeing the provisions, Durgadas was pleased,
and seeing such things in a poor man's house he smiled gently.

**273.** Having eaten, the Raya stayed in the Acharya's house
and lay down to rest with joy in his heart.

**274.** In the night came the sound of khol and kartals;
heavy with sleep, at the mere hearing of the sound

**275.** he awoke — but could hear nothing more;
and the Raya fell senseless where he was.

**276.** Lakshmipriya says: "Acharya, attend:
two golden children are dancing in sankirtana!"

**277.** The two golden children together,
laughing and laughing, laid their feet upon her head.[^01-37]

**278.** And the command came: "For ten months take care of yourself;
once again I shall dance in your courtyard."[^01-38]

**279.** Durgadas, sitting up on his bed, beheld it;
he heard every word — he saw it in a dream.

**280.** Intoxicated with prema, the Raya cried out and wept,
fell to the ground and could not be calmed.

**281.** In haste the Acharya lifted him and took him in his arms:
"Be steady, Raya," he said to him gently.

**282.** "I do not know what sound I heard ringing in my ears;
when I came to, I do not know where it had gone."

**283.** The Acharya said: "In a dream I saw what those two were like;
my sleep broke — where has such a treasure gone?"

**284.** The Raya said: "It was no dream — why do you dissemble?
Be kind and tell me; it will make me very happy."

**285.** The Acharya said: "Raya, you are a very sober man;
why should seeing a dream unsettle you so?"

**286.** The Raya said: "It was no dream; I saw it with my own eyes:
I found the Creator, and he left me bereft."

**287.** The Raya said: "Acharya, I will confess to you:
I have forgotten my own chosen deity, and I do not know why.

**288.** Having seen the dream I have forgotten my chosen deity:
the name of Radha and Krishna has entered my body.

**289.** To abandon one's chosen deity is death, as the shastras attest;
I have heard this word from the shastras; it cannot be otherwise."

**290.** The Acharya said: "Raya, you are very learned;
consider, and do whatever your mind accepts."

**291.** The Raya said: "I have heard people say
that in Navadvipa the Creator was born in the form of Gaura.

**292.** That same Creator has entered my heart,
and entering in the night, he made me forget my own deity.

**293.** That same Creator danced in your courtyard:
I saw two golden ones in my dream.

**294.** What am I to do? Give me your counsel."
The Acharya said: "You are the ruler, and I am under your protection."

**295.** The Raya said: "I have told you everything as it was;
it is in my mind to take the mantra of Radha and Krishna."

**296.** So saying, the Raya went to his residence,
and set his mind on taking a guru in a worthy place.

**297.** Finding a worthy place, the Raya received instruction.
Merely from his entering the womb, such was the fruit!

**298.** To such a Srinivasa's feet I bow:
from the womb he flooded the world with the name of Radha and Krishna.[^01-39]

**299.** In Navadvipa the Lord could not make every being take the Name,
but Srinivasa from the womb made them take it in Chakhandi.[^01-40]

**300.** Even pashandis openly take the name of Krishna —
so great a dawning of the Lord through Srinivasa!

**301.** There has been no such avatara, brothers, in any yuga;
yet we, not honouring the two brothers, feed upon poison.

**302.** Worship, worship, O brothers, Chaitanya and Nitai:
such a merciful Thakura I have never seen.

**303.** Meanwhile Lakshmipriya and the Acharya rejoiced:
the bodies of both were filled with prema.

**304.** Whoever got anything anywhere brought it and gave it;
poverty vanished, and everyone was glad.

**305.** When ten months and ten days were complete,
at an auspicious moment the boy was born.

**306.** On the full moon of Vaishakha,[^01-41] on an auspicious day at an auspicious moment,
Lakshmipriya beheld her son's face.

**307.** The Acharya entered the room,
and seeing his son's face he was filled with great joy.

**308.** Brahmanas recite the Vedas, bards their songs of praise;[^01-42]
in the sky the gods utter blessings.

**309.** The women raise the auspicious ulu-ulu cry;
old men and children dance and clap their hands.

**310.** Lowing, the cows with their calves
roam about with tails held high and grass in their mouths.

**311.** The villagers bring gifts, filling their plates;
everyone gives, rejoicing greatly.

**312.** Durgadas Raya, bringing musicians and instruments,
came to the Acharya's house uttering blessings.

**313.** Coming into the courtyard, he began to dance at length
and distributed many gifts to the brahmanas.

**314.** No sound was heard but "Radha-Krishna,"
and a voice from the sky said: "Sing, sing!"

**315.** *Today there is no end to my joy:
after so many days Madhava is in my house!* (refrain)[^01-43]

**316.** Having this song sung, he began to dance;
there was no bound to the joy, and so the day ended.

**317.** With his people the Raya went back to his home,
and brahmanas and good folk came thronging.

**318.** For the son's welfare they made offerings to the brahmana:
whatever wealth they had at home they brought and laid before him.

**319.** He whose hope is the feet of Sri Jahnava and Virachandra,
Nityananda Dasa, tells the Prema-vilasa.

*Thus ends the first vilasa, called "The Description of the Birth of Srinivasa Acharya Thakura".*

# Vilasa 2. The Description of the Festival of Srinivasa Acharya's Birth

*Chakhandi rejoices at Srinivasa's birth: the father gazes on his son's face; music, dancing and crowds of people who forget their sorrows*

**1.** Glory, glory to Sri Chaitanya Acharya, glory, glory!
Glory, glory to Lakshmipriya of compassionate heart!

**2.** Glory, glory to the listeners — listen with care,
you whose life's treasure is the pastimes of Radha and Krishna's prema.

**3.** Hearing of the son's birth, people forgot their sorrow and grief;
men and women set out to see him.
The lame, the dull-witted and the blind run along, singing the virtues of Radha and Krishna,
forgetting home and sons.[^02-1]

**4.** The Acharya went into the room, his eyes brimming with joy:
he beholds his son's moon-like face.
Tears flow from his eyes; gazing, he is beside himself,
again and again offering up his life for him.[^02-2]

**5.** Having seen, he cannot come away; tears stream from both his eyes:
"Blessed is the mother who bore him in her womb!"
Whether gandharva or kinnara — there is none to compare him with;
and he sank into the ocean of prema.

**6.** The dancing girls dance, and all the dancers
dance and sing in the sweetest voices.
The bhats recite many wondrous pastimes of Krishna,
their bodies thrilling with delight.

**7.** Mridanga, jhanjh and dhol — the music is a tumult;
kartals and pakhoajs are played.
Mahuri and pinaka sound, damph and saptasvara thunder;[^02-3]
the sound pierces the sky.

**8.** The blind and the deaf count themselves blessed,
yet they too reproach Providence:
"I would have gazed my fill — in such sorrow I die!
O Fate, you are pitiless!"

**9.** So saying they dance and sing, weep and roll on the ground,
exulting and crying "Radha-Krishna!"
Hundreds of thousands of people run, casting off fear, sorrow and grief;
many worldly men and pandits run too.

**10.** Bodies were filled with joy, homes were filled with wealth and grain,
and all swooned in prema.[^02-4]

**11.** Such is the birth of Srinivasa — I have told it to you, brothers;
whoever hears it, his life is fulfilled.
Nityananda Dasa in his song will distribute the wealth of prema,
to purify his own body.

**12.** He whose hope is the feet of Sri Jahnava and Virachandra,
Nityananda Dasa, tells the Prema-vilasa.

*Thus ends the second vilasa, called "The Description of the Festival of Srinivasa Acharya Thakura's Birth".*

[^02-1]: Stanzas 3–11 are in tripadi metre.
[^02-2]: "Nichhiya nichhiya": the nichhani rite, in which one circles the hand round a child as though giving up one's own life for it, to ward off misfortune.
[^02-3]: Mahuri, a reed wind instrument like the oboe; pinaka, a stringed instrument; damph, a frame drum; saptasvara, an instrument "of seven notes".
[^02-4]: The stanza survives only as a half; in the 1913 edition its opening words ("ānande purila deha") have been displaced to the top of the page.

# Vilasa 3. The Interruption of Srinivasa's Studies

*The author's address on the cause of his writing; Srinivasa's childhood — the first rice and the sacred thread; the break in his studies and the boy's grief; his teacher Dhananjaya admits that the pupil has surpassed him; Sarasvati, by Chaitanya's command, bestows all learning on Srinivasa*

**1.** Glory, glory to Sri Chaitanya Acharya, glory, glory!
Glory, glory to Lakshmipriya of compassionate heart!

**2.** Glory, glory to the listeners — listen with care,
you whose life's treasure is the pastimes of Radha and Krishna's prema.

**3.** Of one shortcoming of mine I will write once:[^03-1]
at the feet of Krishna's devotees I bow.

**4.** I have not studied, and there is not a trace of bhakti in me;
yet I write by the command of the Ocean of compassion:

**5.** "All my devotees who wander upon the earth
descended along with me and bear the same virtues.

**6.** Some have described the pastimes of Radha and Krishna,
others have revealed scriptures about Gaura's pastimes.

**7.** Whoever writes of the virtues of Krishna's devotees,
to him I show mercy with a joyful heart.

**8.** After my departure there will be prema upon the earth;
I bid you write and describe their virtues.

**9.** Srinivasa and Narottama are the two Mahashayas;
write of the virtues of these two abundantly.

**10.** Write of the virtues of both and of their way of bhajana —
how the earth spread prema in their form."

**11.** I have never known a speck of the gift of description;
yet I write by the strength of the command of the two Lords.

**12.** Sri Jahnava and Virachandra, at whose feet my hope rests —
from them came the command to reveal these virtues.

**13.** Srinivasa is my breath, Narottama my life;
with care I write of the virtues of these two.

**14.** By that command I write whatever words come to my mind;
O Vaishnava Gosanis, do not count it against me in any way.

**15.** For six months the Acharya did not go out anywhere;
seeing his son's power, he remained at peace.

**16.** Both rejoiced as they looked on their son's face,
and raised their son with happy hearts.

**17.** The time of the annaprashana[^03-2] came;
they brought an astrologer and fixed the day firmly.

**18.** At an auspicious moment they put prasada into the son's mouth,
and both rejoiced, clasping their son to their breasts.

**19.** The time of the chudakarana[^03-3] came;
performing the rites according to rule, they gave him the sacred thread.

**20.** A crimson cloth gleams upon his body;
seeing him, his father and mother rejoice at heart.

**21.** On the third day the Thakura grew restless:
his lessons had stopped — he stayed at home and began to weep.[^03-4]

**22.** At this time the pandit Vidyanidhi came by;[^03-5]
hearing of the break in the lessons, he was greatly pleased:

**23.** "The boy has such a longing for learning —
it seems Srinivasa will become an expert in the sciences."

**24.** One night Srinivasa saw in a dream:
"Study quickly, Srinivasa — you will go to Vrindavana.

**25.** Gauda is very dear to Chaitanya;
through you he will make books of the pastimes arise here.

**26.** Why have you stopped studying for three days?
At your parents' word I will teach you.

**27.** But if you act against this word,
what you have learned will not come back to your mind."[^03-6]

**28.** He constantly uttered the name of Radha and Krishna with his tongue,
and so his learning went — he did not go to study.

**29.** Lakshmipriya and the Acharya were astonished:
they could not understand what kind of birth this was.[^03-7]

**30.** He constantly uttered the name of Radha and Krishna with his tongue,
and so his learning went — yet there was joy in his heart.

**31.** Sitting at home, what does Srinivasa say?
"I have not become a pandit" — such is the pain in his mind.

**32.** "I cannot understand Krishna's mercy;
scholarship comes from study — such is its way.[^03-8]

**33.** Therefore I will not live in Yajigrama:
for the sake of learning I will go to another land."

**34.** After ten days his mother bade him:
"Go and study, my son; your lessons have been interrupted."

**35.** "As you command," he said, and taking a book in his hand,
Srinivasa went to his guru's place.

**36.** Dhananjaya Vidyanivasa said a wondrous thing:
"Looking at your form, I feel joy.

**37.** Listen, listen, Srinivasa, I will tell you:
learning no longer comes alive in me — why have you come?

**38.** You have stolen all my learning;
I am an empty body, I tell you.

**39.** I cannot understand your nature;
it seems Sarasvati has turned against me."

**40.** Ashamed, Srinivasa bowed to his guru,
and Dhananjaya started up in fear, crying "Alas, alas!"

**41.** Srinivasa's shame grew all the more, doubling;
dejected, he tied up his book and went away.

**42.** His father and mother knew nothing of all this;
grieving over the break in his studies, he lay down alone.

**43.** The meal was ready, but the boy was not at home;
his father went to look for him with an anxious heart.

**44.** The pandit said: "He left a long time ago."
Finding no trace of him, the father was greatly distressed.

**45.** Going into the house, he entered the room
and saw him asleep with a book in his hand.

**46.** Hearing his father's voice, out of shame he said nothing,
and saying "Give me food, Mother," began to laugh.

**47.** Having eaten, Srinivasa rinsed his mouth
and, laughing, lay down again.

**48.** Suddenly a voice from heaven was heard in the room —
the sound came: "All learning shall awaken in you!

**49.** I am Sarasvati; by Chaitanya's command
I have come in the guise of a dream to give you learning."

**50.** Opening his eyes, he looks — there is no one in the room.
"By the goddess's boon I shall have great learning!"

**51.** Laughing, he came out happily
and stood before his father and mother.

**52.** "Come, come, my son, let me take you on my lap;
do not grieve over the break in your studies," and she kissed his cheek.[^03-9]

**53.** From then on there was no further break in his studies,
and beyond that, the name of Radha and Krishna awoke in him.

**54.** He whose hope is the feet of Sri Jahnava and Virachandra,
Nityananda Dasa, tells the Prema-vilasa.

*Thus ends the third vilasa, containing the description of the interruption of Srinivasa Acharya Thakura's studies.*

# Vilasa 4. Srinivasa's Wanderings: Srikhanda, Puri, Navadvipa, Shantipur

*Srinivasa meets Narahari Sarakara; longing for Mahaprabhu and a voice from the sky; the death of Chaitanya Dasa; the move to Yajigrama; Srikhanda and Raghunandana; the journey to Puri and Gadadhara's passing; Navadvipa and Vishnupriya's grace; Shantipur: the vision of Advaita and Sita's account*

**1.** Glory, glory to Sri Chaitanya, glory to Nityananda!
Glory to Advaita-chandra, glory to the host of Gaura's devotees!

**2.** One morning, as Srinivasa was going to bathe,
he met Sarakara Thakura[^04-1] on the road.

**3.** He was on his way from Gajipur to Khanda,
and he saw a wondrous form of golden hue.

**4.** Suddenly the Lord's feet came to his mind:
it seemed to him that he had seen that very form in person.

**5.** "Srinivasa is his name, born in a brahmana family" —
seeing the boy's radiance, the Thakura grasped the secret.

**6.** "If I ask his name, I shall learn who he is," he thought;
and the boy, prostrating himself, stood before him.

**7.** In a sweet voice he began to ask him:
"What is your name, boy? Tell me truly."

**8.** Respectfully Srinivasa answered:
"Your own servant, born at Chakhandi."

**9.** Hearing the name Srinivasa, the Thakura was delighted
and became firmly convinced that this was Chaitanya's power.

**10.** "Come, come, my son, let me embrace you!" —
and holding him to his breast, he bathed him in tears.

**11.** "Nityananda is anxious about you.[^04-2]
I had a wish, and now I have met you.

**12.** I had heard nothing of you from anyone, nor seen you;
I cannot tell how much hidden treasure lies in this.

**13.** Virachandra called me in Jahnava's presence:
'Send Srinivasa to Vrindavana quickly.'

**14.** He was born on the bank of the Ganga and is still a mere child —
that is why I have not met him.

**15.** Without any effort Chaitanya has brought us together on this road;
through you he will reveal the pastimes in Vrindavana.

**16.** Now there is no need to ask anything more;
meeting you in Khanda will make me happy."

**17.** The Thakura went on to Khanda, and Srinivasa crossed the Ganga;
and at home his father and mother were sorely anxious.[^04-3]

**18.** On reaching home the boy lost all composure in prema:
he laughs, weeps, dances, sings and falls to the ground again and again.

**19.** Crying out, he weeps very loudly,
and a great lamentation arose in the Acharya's house.

**20.** "Why has this happened to him? We know nothing;
when we ask, he weeps the more," and their hearts were torn.

**21.** The Acharya heard the weeping inside the house
and saw his son weeping with an anguished heart.

**22.** He asked: "My son, why are you weeping?
You went to bathe — why do you weep? I do not understand."

**23.** One by one the villagers gathered,
and seeing the boy's behaviour they cried out in dismay.

**24.** Among them was an old brahmana:
"Be patient; listen, I will tell you the cause of this.

**25.** Narahari Thakura Mahashaya, who lives in Khanda,
met the boy on the road at the time of his bath.

**26.** From seeing him the boy has come into this state;
do not worry, be patient," he said truly.

**27.** Hearing the name of Narahari, the boy laughed,
and at the brahmana's words regained some outward awareness.

**28.** But from that day his state was changed:
the malady of separation from Chaitanya grew twofold.

**29.** "I never had the sight of Chaitanya Prabhu,
I never saw the feet of Nityananda Prabhu,

**30.** I did not see the form of Advaita Acharya,
I did not meet Svarupa, Ramananda Raya, Sanatana or Rupa,

**31.** I did not hear sankirtana together with the devotees —
sinner that I am, I was not born at that time!"

**32.** Lifting his face to the sky, he cried out long:
"By giving me a later birth, Fate has barred my happiness!"

**33.** Then a voice sounded from the sky:
"Your birth is in the form of prema — why do you worry?

**34.** Through you the pastimes of Radha and Krishna will be spread;
with what Chaitanya savoured you will flood the world.

**35.** In Vrindavana Rupa and Sanatana,
the two brothers, have written scriptures of rasa for your sake.

**36.** Foreseeing the future, Chaitanya Gosani, for your sake,
sent the two brothers to compose these books.

**37.** The two brothers are anxious in Vrindavana;
if you go quickly, you will see them.

**38.** If you delay, you will not see the two brothers,
and when you reach Vrindavana your sorrow will be all the greater."

**39.** "The grief in my father's and mother's hearts — this is my great dilemma;
if only the Mahashaya would help me in this!"

**40.** For a while he grew calm, and the people went home;
seeing him well, his father and mother were glad at heart.

**41.** Understanding his father's heart, Srinivasa smiled
and asked to eat: "I am very hungry."

**42.** At their son's words they were overjoyed
and lovingly fed him many dishes.

**43.** "While my father and mother live, how can I leave them?
Besides, I am only a boy — how shall I go to Vrindavana?"

**44.** But Chaitanya's mercy is very deep;
all sorrow vanished, and there was joy in his heart.

**45.** Suddenly a fever came upon Chaitanya Dasa,
and within seven days he attained the Ganga.[^04-4]

**46.** Seeing this, Srinivasa wept much in his grief,
and then performed the prescribed rites with special care.

**47.** At the loss of his father he suffered great sorrow,
and seeing his mother weep, his face withered.

**48.** "To the childless the Lord gave a son, Srinivasa,
and now bereavement has come, and our hope is unfulfilled.

**49.** O cruel Fate, what shall I say to you?
So soon you have given the boy such sorrow!

**50.** My diamond of a boy[^04-5] — how will his days pass?
We have no one to call our own — what will become of me?

**51.** O Srinivasa, where has your father gone?
How shall we pass our days? We are orphaned!"

**52.** Seeing his mother's grief, Srinivasa was stricken
and wept long, crying "Father, father!

**53.** With whom have you left me, where have you gone?[^04-6]
You loved me so, Thakura, and yet you have left me!"

**54.** Thus, after lamenting long on the bank of the Ganga,
he performed the rites according to rule and gave the bones to the water.

**55.** Coming home, he wept a great deal,
and women came to console Lakshmipriya.

**56.** "Listen, listen, Thakurani, why do you grieve?
Look at your son and restrain yourself.

**57.** What comfort can we give? Listen, steady your heart;
look at your son and forget your sorrow; do not weep."

**58.** At this time a voice sounded from the sky:
"Why do you grieve, mother, why are you anxious?

**59.** You know nothing of the boy's virtues;
when you go to Yajigrama, you will learn everything later.

**60.** For the sake of these two[^04-7] Sri Chaitanya and Nityananda
began the books in Vrindavana through Rupa.

**61.** Take care of your son — listen, Lakshmipriya;
do not grieve in vain; be steady of heart."

**62.** All her grief for her husband went away,
but in her breast remained a sprout of sorrow over Srinivasa.

**63.** Having duly performed all the customary observances,
Srinivasa visited Yajigrama and saw Narahari.[^04-8]

**64.** A great longing arose in him: "I will leave this village;
settling my mother in Yajigrama, I will go elsewhere."

**65.** At night, as he slept in the village,
the command of Chaitanya came in a dream: "Go to Vrindavana."

**66.** Having seen the dream, he awoke:
"How can I go so soon and leave her?

**67.** Besides, I have not yet received initiation;
I have no right to go to Vrindavana.

**68.** Delay is not good; I will go and settle there,
and then follow whatever counsel Thakura Narahari gives me."

**69.** After living some days in Chakhandi,
he left that place and came to Yajigrama.

**70.** On the fifth day of the month of Phalguna they settled there,
and at that time he met the zamindar of the village.

**71.** Seeing his radiance, the zamindar showed him honour:
"Live in this village — I will build you a house."

**72.** Seeing his wondrous form, he counted himself fortunate:
"We too are blessed; our life is fulfilled!"

**73.** Thus he stayed some days in that village,
and then the wish arose in him to go to Khanda.

**74.** He chose and made ready a very charming place —
a beautiful dwelling on the western side of the village.[^04-9]

**75.** Leaving his mother in that village, he went to Khanda.
At the outer gate, under a tree, sat Sri Raghunandana.

**76.** Seeing his radiance, he asked: "What is your name?
Where have you come from? Tell me your news."

**77.** "I have just come from Yajigrama for darshan;
my name is Srinivasa," he answered respectfully.

**78.** Hearing the name Srinivasa, Raghunandana was overcome with prema;
stretching out his arms, he came and embraced him.

**79.** "From the Thakura's holy lips I have heard all about you;
at the mere sight of you all my distress has gone.

**80.** Come, come, brother, to the Thakura;
we two will have our talk later."

**81.** Taking him by the hand, he led him to the Thakura.
"Come, come, my son, sit down, Srinivasa!

**82.** A letter has come from Virachandra about you:
'Send Srinivasa to Vrindavana quickly.'"

**83.** Kindly he passed his holy hand over his body,
and at the touch of that holy hand Srinivasa was deeply absorbed in prema.

**84.** Nearby was Nayana Sena Mahashaya;[^04-10]
supporting Srinivasa, he took him to his own house.

**85.** That day he was holding the worship of his guru and his father's memorial rite;
in the afternoon the Thakura came to his house with Raghunandana.

**86.** "Tell me, tell me, Nayana, where is Srinivasa?
Bring him — I will ask him about going to Vrindavana."

**87.** Then Srinivasa, seeing Narahari,
bowed to him, and he was glad to see his smiling face.

**88.** "Tell me, Srinivasa, about your going to Vrindavana:
how will you manage it, my son? Tell me in detail."

**89.** "Listen, Thakura, I must tell you:
without a refuge,[^04-11] how can I do this?"

**90.** "Concerning you, Chaitanya gave the Bhatta a command:
he will give you initiation in every way."

**91.** Weeping, Srinivasa said:
"Why do you deceive me and send me to Vrindavana?

**92.** Coming from Chakhandi I received your darshan,
and at that very moment I surrendered myself to you."

**93.** The Thakura said: "What you say is true;
but Gopala Bhatta is your guru, I tell you.

**94.** I cannot go against the Lord's command.
For now live here and serve.

**95.** Hari-nama is Mahaprabhu's own power;
understanding this, you shall receive it."

**96.** Having heard this, he went to his lodging
and began to ponder what Sarakara Thakura had said.

**97.** "From whom shall I receive Hari-nama?" —
turning this over in his mind, he lay awake all night.

**98.** Towards the end of the night his sleep broke, and he was half awake,[^04-12]
and Gaurachandra mercifully said to him:

**99.** "Listen, listen, Srinivasa, why are you troubled?
You were born as prema in form for my purpose.

**100.** Why, then, do you wait?[^04-13]
I have told you: you were born as prema in form.

**101.** Go to Vrindavana; do not delay;
take refuge at the feet of Gopala Bhatta.

**102.** He was born in the land of Telanga; he is my very life;
and Sanatana and Rupa are one soul in two bodies.

**103.** All the books that Rupa and Sanatana have written
they will entrust to you when you go.

**104.** They are anxious at your delay;
the work is done — go quickly."

**105.** In ecstasy he bowed to the Lord,
who laid a hand on his head and said: "May your heart's desire be fulfilled."

**106.** The Lord vanished, and his sleep was broken;
waking, Srinivasa reflected:

**107.** "The Lord has commanded me to go to Vrindavana."
And he went and told Sarakara Thakura.

**108.** Thinking thus, Srinivasa came to Narahari,
bowed to him and offered him praise:

**109.** "Listen, Mahashaya, to what I saw in a dream:
a golden-bodied boy came and spoke to me..."

**110.** All that he had seen in the dream he told;
the Thakura said: "Mahaprabhu's mercy has come."

**111.** Laying a hand on his head, he blessed him:
"May Vrindavana show you mercy without delay.

**112.** I have sent a letter to Virachandra:
'I have kept Srinivasa with me — give your command.'

**113.** Whatever answer comes, I will act on it;
until then stay here, close to me."

**114.** Thus he lived some days in Khanda,
and a wish arose in him to see Jagannatha.

**115.** "I greatly wish to study the Bhagavata;
I will see Jagannatha and stay with the Pandita.[^04-14]

**116.** Going to him I will study the Bhagavata,
and having studied it with commentary I will go to Vrindavana."

**117.** With this in mind he went to Narahari.
"What you have said, my son, is right.

**118.** I will give you a Vaishnava as a companion;
delivering a letter, he will give my news."

**119.** He put a respectful letter into the Vaishnava's hand,
and setting out, the two went along the road to Jagannatha.

**120.** Travelling on, they reached Jagannatha-puri,
and having seen Jagannatha they came to Gopinatha's abode.[^04-15]

**121.** The Pandita Gosani was stricken in separation from Chaitanya:
now he swooned, now he laughed, and a stupor lay within him.[^04-16]

**122.** Saying "Chaitanya! Nityananda!" Srinivasa prostrated himself;
hearing Chaitanya's name, Gosani was stirred.

**123.** "Who are you? Who are you?" he said, opening his eyes.
"Come, come, my son, let me hold you to my breast.

**124.** What is your name, my son? Tell me.
What wondrous words I have heard from your lips!

**125.** By letting me hear the Name you have bought me.[^04-17]
With separation in my heart I saw a dream:

**126.** 'From Gauda will come one named Srinivasa,
born at Chakhandi as prema in form,

**127.** to his father Chaitanya Dasa, from the womb of Lakshmipriya,
so that the pastimes of Radha and Krishna may be spread.'

**128.** Are you that one, my son? Tell me who you are;
soothe my body — tell me for certain."

**129.** "I am he," he said, and smiled gently.
"You are the master, and I am wretched, luckless and low."

**130.** "It is good that you came, my son, and told me who you are.
The Lord commanded me to teach you the Bhagavata.

**131.** At the time of his last pastimes the Lord told me:
'When Srinivasa comes, let him hear Krishna's pastimes.

**132.** For his sake you shall stay at Gopinatha,
and send him to Vrindavana with a letter in his hand.

**133.** Sri Rupa and Sanatana, the two brothers,
have revealed the Lord's heart through their scriptures.

**134.** Those scriptures you[^04-18] will bring to Gauda,
and reveal the scriptures of the pastimes in every particular.'

**135.** The Lord's command is to teach you the Bhagavata,
but the letters of my book have all been washed away by my tears.

**136.** Give my letter into Narahari's hands;
he will send a new book with you.

**137.** The Lord's command concerning you is mighty;
do not delay; settle everything.

**138.** In the pastimes of Radha and Krishna there is Sri Guna-manjari —
she is none other than Gopala Bhatta, of equal sweetness."

**139.** "I will become his disciple, Prabhu!" — a great longing was in Srinivasa's heart,
and on hearing the name Guna-manjari his ears rejoiced.

**140.** "I see the Lord's command has been given to the Manjari;
in Navadvipa you will receive a witness from Ishvari-ji.[^04-19]

**141.** Partake of the remnants of Gopinatha's meal;
today is an auspicious day — set out for Gauda.

**142.** If you delay on the road, you will not find them."
With closed eyes Srinivasa listened to these words.

**143.** "Where has Prabhu Chaitanya gone, where is Nityananda?" —
for a moment he weeps, then gently laughs.

**144.** Bearing the pain of separation, he loses all memory:
it does not occur to him that Gopinatha is before him.

**145.** The raving of separation, all kinds of changes in his body;
lifting his face, now and again he cries out.

**146.** Seeing these changes, Srinivasa was stunned:
"If I go to Gauda, I shall not see him again."

**147.** Taking the reply, he prostrated himself.
"Set out for your country, if you would study the Bhagavata."

**148.** Taking the road, he came to Narahari;
that day Virachandra was in the house, and many people had gathered.[^04-20]

**149.** Then the Mahashaya prostrated himself,
and the word was given: "Srinivasa, it is good that you have come.

**150.** Listen: this letter has come from Vrindavana —
study the Bhagavata and set out for Vrindavana."

**151.** He made known the Pandita Gosani's command in the letter
and told everything just as he had seen it.

**152.** He read the letter out to Virachandra Gosani
and asked leave to go and study the Bhagavata.

**153.** "If you delay, you will not find him;
go to the Kshetra without delay."

**154.** The Thakura again gave him the same Vaishnava as a companion,
and saying "Gadadhara! Chaitanya!" Srinivasa set out.

**155.** In anxiety Srinivasa went as far as Jajpur,
and as he entered the village he received the news that the Pandita had passed away.

**156.** Receiving the news, he swooned on the spot,
and the frightened Vaishnava clasped his feet.

**157.** Coming to himself, he bowed down many times:
"My purpose is not fulfilled — Fate has turned against me!"

**158.** That night he fasted there;
seeing how wasted he was, the Vaishnava was greatly afraid:

**159.** "How shall I bring him to Gauda?
The Thakura will punish me!" — and his heart sank.

**160.** With much care he got him to eat,
and entreating him, they went on to Gauda.

**161.** Weeping and weeping, he came back to Gauda,[^04-21]
and in the afternoon entered the village of Srikhanda.

**162.** Prostrating himself, he told the whole story
and, crying "Alas!", wept long.

**163.** Who can describe that lament of separation —
the lament of one whose heart holds the grief of separation from guru and Vaishnavas?

**164.** From that day another state came over him again:
"How shall I get to Vrindavana?" — and his longing grew.

**165.** In the morning, leaving Srikhanda, he came to Navadvipa,
and living as a renunciant he stayed near the Lord's house.

**166.** Crying "Pandita Gosani!", he weeps aloud;
for two or four days at a time he takes no food.

**167.** When his hunger becomes unbearable,
he gathers a chhatak[^04-22] of rice,

**168.** cooks it on the bank of the Ganga,
but what he truly eats is the pain of separation.

**169.** By the eighth day his body was utterly wasted;
and he met Vamshivadana Dasa.

**170.** "What is your name? Where do you live? I have never seen or heard of you." —
"In separation from Gadadhara I am staying here.

**171.** My name is Srinivasa; my home is in Yajigrama;
I could not study the Bhagavata, and my heart is in pain.

**172.** Gadadhara Pandita had the Lord's command —
I would have studied with him, but by my misfortune it did not come to pass."

**173.** As he spoke, a great weeping overcame him.
At that moment Ishana arrived.

**174.** Such is Ishana's nature: he is compassionate to living beings,
and on seeing a great bhagavata, prema rises in him.

**175.** Seeing him so wasted, he questioned him,
and seeing him entirely alone, he was much pleased.[^04-23]

**176.** He understood that Chaitanya's power was in the boy:
"I ought to tell Ishvari of this."

**177.** He went back home to Ishvari:
"I have seen a wondrous boy at the bathing ghat on the Ganga.

**178.** He is always weeping, calling on Gadadhara Pandita;
he has no companion, and his eyes are full of tears.

**179.** Seeing him, I was moved to compassion:
without food his body has become utterly wasted.

**180.** If you permit, I will give him some food,
and later bring him here, and you may show him mercy."

**181.** "Go and give him rice, as much as is fitting;
but now that Chaitanya is gone, his renunciation troubles me."[^04-24]

**182.** Ishana took excellent provisions
and went joyfully to Srinivasa.

**183.** "Listen, brahmana: take these provisions,
go to the bank of the Ganga, cook them and eat."

**184.** "As you command," he said, and bowing he took them.
And here Ishvari herself understood everything.

**185.** Having given him rice, Ishvari rejoiced at heart:
"I see the boy has been born as prema in form."[^04-25]

**186.** When the brahmana took the rice and cooked it,
she sent ten vairagis to him.

**187.** When the food was ready, they came in the guise of vairagis
and appeared before him just at the time of the meal.

**188.** Seeing the Vaishnavas, he was overjoyed,
and receiving them all, he showed them great honour.

**189.** They said: "We are very hungry;
give us food, Mahashaya, and then we shall be pleased."

**190.** "Most kindly you have come and given me darshan;
the prasada is ready — sit down and eat."

**191.** "You have cooked very little, and we are many;
our hunger will not be satisfied — that is plain to see."

**192.** "Your hunger will be satisfied — such is the nature of prasada."
And the ten Vaishnavas sat down in a circle.

**193.** In this way he served them all,
filling plate after plate with great joy.

**194.** Half a ser of rice, made into prasada,
was partaken of by eleven Vaishnavas, and they were content.

**195.** Ishvari heard of this through Ishana
and understood in her heart that he had been born as prema in form.

**196.** "How delightful it is to hear of such a boy's virtues!
I must certainly see the boy's face."[^04-26]

**197.** At night, going with a maid to bathe in the Ganga,
she saw the boy, full of the sweetness of prema.

**198.** Having bathed, while it was still night, she went back to the inner apartments;
having seen the boy, she was glad at heart.[^04-27]

**199.** "How can I bring him here and question him?
How can I ask, looking at the face of a strange man?

**200.** If he is the Lord's power, my shyness will go far away;
then I shall know that abundant mercy rests on him."

**201.** Ishvari bade Ishana bring the boy:
"Why does he weep day and night?"

**202.** Ishana came and said: "Listen, Srinivasa:
Ishvari is calling you — come to the Lord's house."

**203.** Raising his arms, Srinivasa began a long dance:
it seemed that the Pandita Gosani's state had come upon him.

**204.** Weeping, he went along behind Ishana;
on entering the inner chamber he grew shy.

**205.** Trembling, he went into the inner apartments,
but did not come close and stayed at some distance.

**206.** Ishana said: "Here is Srinivasa;
he bows down to you; he is your dear servant."

**207.** Drawing aside the inner curtain, she looked at him
and understood the reason: it is my Lord's power.

**208.** Setting her shyness aside, she called him herself:
"Why do you weep and wander about alone?"

**209.** He told her the Pandita Gosani's words:
"By his kindness I would have gone to Vrindavana.

**210.** What I heard from his lips in Nilachala...
Not having studied the Bhagavata, my birth has been in vain.

**211.** I heard the Lord's command to go to Vrindavana,
but it has not been fulfilled," he said at her feet.

**212.** "Now I weep without ceasing for Gadadhara;
therefore the Lord's power rests with you."

**213.** "I see you are still very young and tender.
Have you taken up renunciation? Go home, brahmana boy.

**214.** Renunciation is hard — it needs very great strength."[^04-28]
With joined palms he pleaded long:

**215.** "Let me stay near your feet;
my soul will be soothed, and I shall escape from danger.

**216.** I have no one in the world; only my mother is living;
how can I go to Vrindavana and still keep my life?"

**217.** "Without Chaitanya's power there is no such mercy.[^04-29]
When you are mature, then you will go; it is not fitting now.

**218.** Having received this command, live outside the house,
eat prasada, and let your mind grow steady."[^04-30]

**219.** Vishnupriya, in separation from Gauranga, was sorely afflicted:
her grief doubled, and she lost herself.

**220.** Ishvari called Ishana and said: "Listen, Ishana:
the night has gone by, and it is dawn."

**221.** Ishana said: "He spends the night weeping;
crying 'O Pandita Gosani!', he stays awake."

**222.** That day they did not meet again;
in longing for darshan, night and day went by.

**223.** Hear, brothers, how Ishvari took the Name —
hearing it, one comes to realise the pastimes.

**224.** She brings new clay pots and sets them on either side:
one pot empty, the other full of rice.

**225.** Each time she chants the sixteen names of thirty-two syllables,[^04-31]
she places one grain in the pot, with joy in her heart.

**226.** Until the third watch she chants Hari-nama,
and whatever grains have been gathered she takes and goes to cook.

**227.** Cooking only those grains,
she feeds them to the Lord with tears.

**228.** Day and night, Hari-nama, so many rounds for the Lord!
I cannot fathom that endeavour — my mind is utterly helpless.

**229.** What can be said of her who is the Lord's beloved?
Day and night she ever chants Hari-nama.

**230.** What is impossible for her, with such longing for the Name?
She chants the Name, and in it she plants the Lord's power.

**231.** By the mere shadow of the Name all sins are destroyed,
the heart's desire grows, and prema is made manifest.

**232.** The Name is a wish-fulfilling tree — this is certain;
whoever chants the Name by count attains every perfection.

**233.** "The Name is the truth in the Kali age" — so it is said three times;[^04-32]
Gauranga accepts those who are free from attachment.

**234.** It is the best of all means of sadhana;
only by the fortune of many births does love for it arise.

**235.** Thus, when the third watch of the night had passed,
crying "O Chaitanya!", she lay down on the ground.

**236.** At the end of the night the two brothers together, in sankirtana,
dancing, say: "Where is my mother?

**237.** Your daughter-in-law keeps my Srinivasa outside the gate,
while she herself is at ease in her own house!

**238.** All my work is to be done through Srinivasa;
send him to Abhirama, with Ishana as his companion."[^04-33]

**239.** In separation from Chaitanya she does not sleep at night;
having heard the nectar words of the dream, she fell silent.

**240.** "Ishana! Ishana!" the maids call out,
but Ishana is fast asleep and does not stir.

**241.** After a long while Ishana awoke,
and in fear counted himself most unfortunate.

**242.** With joined palms he came to Ishvari,
and she commanded: "Bring Srinivasa to me."

**243.** Srinivasa was weeping on a mat of kusha grass.
"Get up, get up, lad, come quickly!"[^04-34]

**244.** Standing in the courtyard, he bowed down many times,
and Ishana was told: "Take him to see Abhirama."[^04-35]

**245.** So saying, with her toes wrapped in cloth,
she called Srinivasa and raised her foot onto his head.

**246.** At the touch of her foot he was overcome with prema
and, rolling on the ground, began to weep.

**247.** "Listen, listen, my son, you are fortunate:
Chaitanya's power is in you — it cannot be otherwise.

**248.** Go to Shantipur, and then you will go to Khardaha;
on seeing Acharya Gosani, you will be recognised.

**249.** Going to Khardaha, you will see Nityananda;
finding you, Jahnava will rejoice.

**250.** Do not delay long; go quickly —
you will hear much and see the sweetness of beauty.

**251.** Having met everyone, go to Vrindavana;
all will be fulfilled — keep this in mind on the way."

**252.** Prostrating himself, he went to Shantipur,
and fell into great perplexity: "Where shall I stay?

**253.** Ishvari bade me see Advaita —
how is that command to be understood?

**254.** It is now the third year since Gosani passed away;
yet this command cannot be disobeyed" — doubt cast him into a quandary.

**255.** At that moment one appeared with arms reaching to his knees and a huge body;
seeing his radiance, Srinivasa trembled and lost all composure.

**256.** Unable to open his eyes, he fell to the ground.
"Come, come, Srinivasa, I have heard your words."

**257.** So Advaita Gosani had willed it;
prostrating himself there, Srinivasa addressed him.

**258.** Sighing, Gosani wept a long while:
"Where have Chaitanya and Nityananda gone in their bodies?

**259.** Where have their companions gone — Svarupa, Ramananda Raya?" —
and lovingly he laid his hand on Srinivasa's head.

**260.** "Come, come, Srinivasa, let my life be soothed;
let me embrace you and let my heart be comforted.

**261.** For your sake the Lord sent Gopala Bhatta;
you will become his servant — I tell you for certain.

**262.** Being angry with me, the Lord caused you to be born,[^04-36]
and here he has revealed all his own work.

**263.** He sent Rupa and Sanatana to Vrindavana,
and to make that known he caused you to be born.

**264.** For your sake he sent Gopala Bhatta;
take instruction there, in order to make prema manifest.

**265.** Calling 'Come, come!', with the Lord's power infused in you,
you[^04-37] will flood the world, spreading prema.

**266.** For your sake I have appeared here;
never speak of it anywhere else.

**267.** Going to Khardaha, you will find joy;
having seen Jahnava, you will go to Vrindavana.

**268.** There you will see Sri Rupa;
go and worship the feet of Gopala Bhatta.

**269.** By Chaitanya's mercy you will flood the land with prema;
cry 'Advaita! Govinda!' and do not dwell on sorrow.

**270.** Your Lord, the best of lovers,
has by prema destroyed the corruption among my followers.[^04-38]

**271.** Whoever among my followers takes up this doctrine on his lips
will, cut off from Chaitanya and Nityananda, meet with great sorrow."

**272.** So saying, Advaita-chandra vanished,
and Srinivasa, parted from the vision, fell senseless.

**273.** At this time Mother Sita was going to bathe in the Ganga,
and she sees a jewel of a boy weeping.

**274.** "Child, child!" she took the boy on her lap
and comforted him with the sweetest words.

**275.** She asked: "Who are you? Why are you weeping?
It seems you have had the darshan of my lord.

**276.** Tell me, boy, where is your home?
Why have you come here, weeping so bitterly?"

**277.** "My name is Srinivasa; I was born at Chakhandi;
by Ishvari-ji's command I have come to see you."

**278.** Hearing the name Srinivasa, she rejoiced at heart:
she already knew of him from Achyutananda's letter.

**279.** "I have greatly wished to see you, my son;
by Chaitanya's great mercy we have met on the road."

**280.** Gopala Gosani[^04-39] was coming from his bath;
seeing him, Srinivasa fell prostrate.

**281.** "Until I come back from bathing in the Ganga,
look after him carefully."

**282.** Leaving them together, Mother Sita went to bathe in the Ganga,
and meanwhile Gosani sat with him on one seat.

**283.** Having bathed, she soon came back
and saw Srinivasa and Gopala together.

**284.** So she took him into the inner apartments,
glad at heart to have found a wondrous Vaishnava.

**285.** She gave him the remnants of Advaita's meal to eat:
"I am going to cook; you sit by the door."

**286.** Having cooked, she offered the bhoga,
gave Krishna water to rinse his mouth and laid him down to rest.

**287.** Gopala was told to take prasada:
"Sit down quickly together with Srinivasa.

**288.** I will serve the wondrous Vaishnava myself;
sit with him, my son — it will make me very happy."

**289.** They sat down together to eat;
at the touch of the prasada from the Lord's lips their hair stood on end again and again.

**290.** Food cooked by Sita's hand, the remnants of Krishna's lips —
and the transformations of prema in all their variety.

**291.** Having rinsed their mouths, both in great delight
freshened their mouths and sat together in one place.[^04-40]

**292.** The day ended, and evening came;
seeing Krishna's joy, their joy knew no bounds.

**293.** That night he spent joyfully in Shantipur,[^04-41]
and in the morning went to Sita to take his leave.

**294.** "I have one request — listen carefully:
on coming to this village I heard the words 'Advaita, Govinda'.[^04-42]

**295.** Tell me, Mother, the true account of this,
and then give me leave to go and see Khardaha."

**296.** "What need have you to hear this, boy?
Attend to your own business and go on your travels."

**297.** "Permit me, Mother — I long to hear it;
be gracious and kindly tell me."

**298.** Such is a boy's nature — he holds fast to what he has said;
and Mother Sita could not refuse him.

**299.** "Listen with a steady mind, Srinivasa;
when you hear it, you will believe with a calm heart.

**300.** At the time when Jagai and Madhai were delivered,
Gosani[^04-43] in anger said to Haridasa:

**301.** 'If Gosani does not give me the union of prema,
I will dry up all prema — and the fault will not be mine.'

**302.** Angry with Nityananda, he came home
and sent a riddle, a tarja, written through Jagadananda.

**303.** From that day anger arose in the Lord:
to Nityananda he gave companions — Ramai, Sundarananda and others,

**304.** and to my Thakura he gave Kamadeva Nagara,
and in his anger made the Nagara speak.[^04-44]

**305.** My lord came to Gauda with the Nagara in his company,
and amid the waves of prema the Nagara set a doctrine going.

**306.** As soon as I heard it, anger arose in me,
and I never looked on the Nagara's face again.

**307.** I separated my maidservant Nandini from me,[^04-45]
and I no longer listen to those words.

**308.** Some of my sons hold to Achyuta's view,
and some, following the Nagara, have gone astray.

**309.** I rejoice in the sons who hold to Achyuta's view;
and Nityananda, coming to Gauda, flooded it with prema.

**310.** Gosani could not restrain the Nagara,
and for that reason this circle became hostile.

**311.** Listen, Srinivasa, my heart is sorely pained:
having quarrelled with my sons, I keep to my house.[^04-46]

**312.** Whoever stands with Achyuta, the son of Chaitanya's maidservant,[^04-47]
does not speak this doctrine; whoever speaks it has no tie with me."

**313.** Hearing this, he rejoiced greatly at heart,
and Srinivasa prostrated himself again and again.

**314.** The Mother dispelled all the doubts in his mind,
and prostrating himself, he took leave of Sita.

**315.** He whose hope is the feet of Sri Jahnava and Virachandra,
Nityananda Dasa, tells the Prema-vilasa.

*Thus ends the fourth vilasa of the Prema-vilasa.*

# Vilasa 5. Khardaha and Abhirama; the Journey to Vrindavana

*Srinivasa at Khardaha with Jahnava and Virachandra; the test at Abhirama's and the three strokes of the whip; farewell to Narahari and to his mother; Rupa in Vrindavana awaits Srinivasa; the journey by Varanasi and Prayaga; at Mathura Srinivasa learns of the passing of Rupa, Sanatana and Raghunatha Bhatta*

**1.** Glory, glory to Sri Chaitanya, glory to the Merciful One!
Grant me bhakti, that I may write this book and my desire be fulfilled.

**2.** Listen, listen, O hearers, behold this vilasa:
at the mere sight Srinivasa was filled with joy.

**3.** The moment he entered Khardaha,
Srinivasa, maddened with prema, began to dance.

**4.** Virachandra Prabhu was beside his mother;
suddenly Virachandra began to tremble.

**5.** The Thakurani[^05-1] said: "My son, be attentive:
it seems some bhagavata[^05-2] has made his presence here.

**6.** I think Srinivasa of Chakhandi has come;
otherwise why should the body so rejoice?

**7.** I hear the clamour of people calling the names of Radha and Krishna;
he was born as the very form of prema — this is his power.

**8.** I hear there is joy everywhere — why should it be so?
Because joy is being born in everyone's heart.

**9.** I have remembered my Lord's command;[^05-3]
I think that boy has come to the village.

**10.** Find out, my son, be attentive;
then I shall be at ease and my heart will be soothed."

**11.** At that moment Ishana came and said in haste:
"A wondrous boy has come and is weeping at the gate."

**12.** "Go, go, my son, take Ishana with you;
when you see him you will know his virtues — [bring him] before me."

**13.** Crying "Nityananda!", the Prabhu went out,
and seeing the boy's beauty he embraced him.

**14.** At the name "Srinivasa" he remembered [Srivasa] of Navadvipa;[^05-4]
at the name and the form he was seized by prema and trembled again and again.

**15.** Srinivasa prostrated himself many times and fell at his feet;
taking him by the hand, he raised him and asked his name.

**16.** "What is your name? Seeing you, I am glad."
"My name is Srinivasa; my lot is very poor."

**17.** "Come, come, friend, you have given me great joy;
without any effort fate has brought me a jewel."

**18.** Taking Srinivasa by the hand into the house,
he led him to the inner apartments where Ishvari was.

**19.** With that longing with which Ishvari was longing —
without any effort fate gave her the sweetness of prema.

**20.** Seeing the boy, she was filled with great prema
and cried aloud: "Chaitanya! Nityananda!"

**21.** Saying "Navadvipa!", she heaved deep sighs,
and in separation from Nityananda a great surge of feeling arose in her.

**22.** Taking [Srinivasa] by the hand, Virachandra, in Ishvari's presence,
placed him in her hands: "Give Srinivasa prema.

**23.** He is going to Vrindavana — give him your command quickly;
this is my plea again and again: infuse him with power."

**24.** "If he makes haste and goes to Vrindavana,
then he will have the sight of Sri Rupa's feet.

**25.** If he delays on the way, he will not see him;
if he goes quickly, he will have that sight with joy.

**26.** Srinivasa will be commanded to go quickly;
he will tell all the wonders of the books of the lila.[^05-5]

**27.** Delay no longer, go to Vrindavana,
and take shelter at the feet of Gopala Bhatta."

**28.** She commanded: "Feed the boy,
and send a letter to Abhirama with Ishana.

**29.** [Abhirama,] all blessing and all perfection lie in your whip:
be sure to strike Srinivasa three times with the whip."[^05-6]

**30.** Ishvari's remnants filled a vessel;
Virachandra brought them and placed them in his hands.

**31.** Receiving the remnants from her lips, he was flooded with prema:
he laughed, wept, danced and sang like a madman.

**32.** Taking him by the hand, Virachandra seated him beside him,
and at the touch of his hand he came back to outward awareness.

**33.** "Quickly, Srinivasa, go to Vrindavana;
if you delay, you will not meet Rupa."

**34.** Prostrating himself, the Mahashaya took his leave
and then arrived at Abhirama's.

**35.** Ishana handed him the letter and bowed:
"Ishvari's command: grant prema to the boy."

**36.** "Tell me, tell me the good news of Ishvari;
I shall carry it out according to her command.

**37.** Seeing Srinivasa, my heart greatly rejoices:
I have seen prema blossoming in Gauda."

**38.** He gave Ishana a seat to sit on,
and hearing mention of the whip he rejoiced within.

**39.** "Let me see what kind of vessel Ishvari has sent" —
and to test him he gave him eight karas of cowries.[^05-7]

**40.** "How will the boy manage on this?
When I know his renunciation, I shall strike him with the whip."

**41.** Taking the cowries in his hand, [Srinivasa] pondered long:
"How am I to eat, what provisions should I buy?

**42.** To test me he has put eight karas into my hand;
I mean to cook and eat."

**43.** Going to a merchant's shop he looked over all the provisions
and bought them in due order.

**44.** Having paid, he went into a banana grove
and went down to the water with all his provisions.

**45.** Thakura Sri Abhirama said to two Vaishnavas:
"Go and be guests at Srinivasa's door.

**46.** Knowing the time when he cooks, go to him,
and, [asking him] for food, make plenty of jests and banter."

**47.** Taking leave, they went to where Srinivasa was,
at the time when he was offering [the food] to Radha and Krishna.

**48.** At the end, when he was rinsing his mouth, the two arrived;
seeing Vaishnavas, Srinivasa's heart was glad.

**49.** "We are hungry — feed the two of us."
"This is my good fortune!" he said in humble words.

**50.** "You are devotees of Krishna, and I a wretched soul;
in your mercy accept [me]."

**51.** That offered food he divided into three equal portions
and, with joined palms, said: "Come and eat."

**52.** Having eaten, they rinsed their mouths contentedly,
and the two Vaishnavas went and said before the Gosani:

**53.** "There were no side dishes, yet the rice was like nectar;
as we ate, our hearts were filled with joy.

**54.** Radha and Krishna partook of that food;
as we ate, trembling and tears came over us."[^05-8]

**55.** Hearing this himself, his heart rejoiced,
and he quickly summoned Ishana before him.

**56.** "Bring Srinivasa before me:
to the one sent by Ishvari I shall give prema."

**57.** He sent Ishana to where Srinivasa was,
and quickly had the whip brought and kept it at his left.

**58.** With Ishana came the brahmana Srinivasa
and, approaching, bowed with joy in his heart.

**59.** He wept in prema with joined palms;
the Gosani rose and struck him with the whip.

**60.** "I have flooded you, flooded you!" he cried, striking with the whip;
Srinivasa was in great joy, his breast heaving with prema.

**61.** He struck three strokes of the whip there before his eyes;
then Malini[^05-9] came out and caught hold of his hand.

**62.** "You have flooded him with prema, Gosani, strike no more:
this brahmana boy is Chaitanya's power."

**63.** Taking him by the hand, she led him into her inner apartments;
the Thakurani showed him abundant compassion.

**64.** That night he spent happily at the Gosani's;
[in the morning he] called Ishana and gave him to Srinivasa as a companion.

**65.** "Srinivasa, go quickly to Vrindavana,
take shelter at the feet of Gopala Bhatta.

**66.** You will see Sanatana, Rupa Gosani and Lokanatha;
go and behold Raghunatha's renunciation in person.

**67.** Chaitanya's mercy cannot be fathomed at all:
through you he will flood everyone with prema.

**68.** Narahari is watching the road for you;
see him and then go to Vrindavana."

**69.** At the parting he wept long:
"Command me to remain at your feet always."

**70.** "I am utterly small, yet you have shown me mercy;
my desire is fulfilled — there is no illusion in this.

**71.** How shall I spend my time? I am only a child;
give me a command, so that my time is not wasted on a wrong path."

**72.** "Listen, boy, you do not know yourself:
Chaitanya has shown mercy to you.

**73.** You are Chaitanya's power to make prema manifest;
do not delay, set out quickly.

**74.** I too have placed power upon you:
on the way no one will oppose you."

**75.** With a joyful heart he prostrated himself
and took his leave, saying "Gaurahari!"

**76.** In this way he went in due course to Narahari,
prostrated himself and told him everything in detail.

**77.** Seeing his body radiant, he rejoiced:
"Go quickly to Vrindavana — you have obtained everything."

**78.** He came and took prasada; afternoon came on,
and Sarakara Thakura embraced Srinivasa.

**79.** He prostrated himself many times, falling to the ground;
his body quivered with prema, his eyes brimmed with tears.

**80.** "Delay is unbearable, my son; go to Vrindavana;
go quickly and your desire will be fulfilled.

**81.** Go to your mother, ask her humbly,
and set out on the journey with her leave."

**82.** In the evening he came and bowed at his mother's feet
and told her everything from beginning to end.

**83.** At the mention of going to Vrindavana she was stunned;
the grief of separation from her son swelled in her breast.

**84.** "The Lord took my husband; I have one son, Srinivasa;
how shall I, helpless and alone, go on living?

**85.** O cruel fate, what shall I say to you?
My son is going — I think you mean to blind me.

**86.** Leaving your mother alone,[^05-10] have you no fear?
How can you go, my son, and become so heartless?

**87.** How shall I stay at home — I do not know at all;
in the three worlds there is no one so forsaken."

**88.** Seeing his mother weep, he was grieved at heart
and, entreating her humbly, consoled her at length.

**89.** "My body was nurtured by you, I was born of you;
in no way can I repay my debt to you.

**90.** What am I to do? I cannot steady my heart;
give me leave quickly to go to Vrindavana.

**91.** Be merciful and command me to go to Vrindavana,
otherwise life will not remain in my body."

**92.** So both spent the night with separation in their hearts:
there was no sleep; only their souls tossed in anguish.

**93.** Towards the end of the night, after seeing a dream, he came to himself[^05-11]
and with a joyful heart rose and set out on the journey.

**94.** That night in Vrindavana Sri Rupa Gosani,
seeing Srinivasa's delay, was much grieved.

**95.** From separation from Sanatana illness had come upon his body;
rising at dawn, he went to see the samadhi.[^05-12]

**96.** He wept long, calling "Srinivasa!":
"Still the sweetness of prema has not come!"

**97.** Full of anxiety he came to Jiva;
all were together on the bank of the Yamuna.

**98.** Seeing Sri Rupa, all prostrated themselves,
and he greeted and embraced each as was fitting.

**99.** "You all sit at ease on the bank of the Yamuna,
while Srinivasa has not come — he has fallen into trouble.

**100.** He set out to come to Vrindavana;
why has he been so delayed on the way?

**101.** As the form of prema he was born in a brahmana family;
somehow we would have met, had he come earlier.

**102.** You are renunciants, none of you will go to Gauda;
so there was no meeting, and now it is very late."

**103.** As he spoke, he took Sri Jiva by the hand:
"I have no other recourse — what am I to do?

**104.** Listen, listen, Jiva, I tell you for certain:
he set out at night from Yajigrama.

**105.** Be all of you on the watch for his arrival,
until he comes to Sri Vrindavana."

**106.** Hearing this command, all felt boundless joy
and became attentive to carry it out.

**107.** I cannot describe the journey on the road in full;
what is needful, that I write in its order.

**108.** Ever joyful at heart he goes along the road;
in five days he reached Rajmahal.

**109.** A very young boy travels the road
and at times weeps, crying "Ha Chaitanya!"

**110.** "Where are Rupa, Sanatana, Raghunatha Bhatta?
When shall I have such fortune as to see them in person?"

**111.** Seeing Garhi-dvara,[^05-13] he reached Patna;
sometimes he fasts, sometimes he eats a little.

**112.** Once in two or three days he eats a roti or two
and spends the nights in gardens.

**113.** His golden body has dried up; he walks without food
and at times weeps in a choking voice.

**114.** Twice a day he unfailingly chants Hari-nama;
that day he crossed the Ganga and reached Varanasi.

**115.** At the ghat where Prabhu Chaitanya had bathed,
he went up onto the ghat and bowed.

**116.** North of the ghat is the house of Chandrashekhara;[^05-14]
to the left of the door there is a lovely spot,

**117.** where Sanatana Gosani, in the garb of a dervish,
sat longing for the sight of the Prabhu.

**118.** There is a tulasi platform there; he bowed to it,
and, leaving it behind, went into the inner part of the house.

**119.** Going in, he saw an aged Vaishnava
and bowed to him in deep humility.

**120.** He rose, embraced him and honoured him:
"Where have you come from, and what is your name?"

**121.** He told him: "My name is Srinivasa;
my birthplace is Chakhandi, near the bank of the Ganga."

**122.** Seeing him, the old man rejoiced
and began to tell everything from beginning to end.

**123.** "Sri Chandrashekhara Acharya is my guru;
by his command I live here — I tell you truly.

**124.** See, this is the place where Mahaprabhu sat;
I live here and serve — his command is binding on me."

**125.** Walking around it, he bowed;
now he wept, now he rolled on the ground.

**126.** "O pitiless fate, what shall I say to you?
You had me born like this only to give me sorrow.

**127.** Why was this sinner born so late?
I could not see Mahaprabhu's devotees."

**128.** He lamented long and wept long;
his grief grew and he lost consciousness.

**129.** Then at length Srinivasa came to himself;
seeing the signs of mahabhava, [the old man] grew very fond of him.

**130.** He fed him with great affection:
"My great good fortune today is beyond words."

**131.** The two spent the night in the delight of talk about Krishna,
and at dawn he took leave of him.

**132.** On the second day he arrived at Prayaga,
bathed at the Triveni and stayed there.

**133.** The next day he walked on and on along the highway;
a pious man put four paisa into his hand.

**134.** On that he managed for two days;
from the toil of the road his body grew thin and weak.

**135.** He asked how far Vrindavana was;
people said: "Four days' journey."

**136.** The next day, having bathed at a well,
he lay down to rest under a tree.[^05-15]

**137.** From Vrindavana came five Vrajavasis
and sat down under a tree near the water.

**138.** Srinivasa saw that they were very tired
and gave them water: "Wash your feet, Thakuras."

**139.** Having bathed and done their remembrance, at the time for a light meal
they put chickpeas and gur into the end of Srinivasa's cloth.[^05-16]

**140.** Sitting down, he ate, and his weariness went away;
they talked a great deal with one another.

**141.** All the good news of Nilachala and Gauda
he told, and all the Vaishnavas felt boundless joy.

**142.** "Tell us, Thakuras, in your kindness, about Vrindavana:
who lives where, who is staying where?"

**143.** They name them, and he bows:
"There live those named Rupa and Sanatana,

**144.** the two Bhattas, and one named Lokanatha Gosani,
Bhugarbha, Sri Jiva" — so they named them all.

**145.** "What shall I tell you, brother, if you would hear it all:
when Sanatana withdrew from sight, we felt great pain.

**146.** It is now four months since he withdrew;
just to hear of it, the soul writhes."

**147.** Bowing to them all, he goes on along the road,
as though mountains had broken and fallen on his head.

**148.** "Never since my birth have I known such sorrow:
where I was going to find happiness, fate has given me sorrow."

**149.** That day, sustained by that food, he walked very fast,
and the next day he reached Agra.

**150.** As he walked, his heart grew restless;
leaving the highway on his left, he went to Gokula.

**151.** Crossing the Yamuna, he goes to Nanda's abode
and with great humility beholds it and bows.[^05-17]

**152.** At dawn he came to Mathura, the birthplace of Krishna,
offered prayers there and bowed.

**153.** Every place that is there he saw;
his body trembled and tears flowed from his eyes.

**154.** Seeing the beauty of Mathura, he reflects in his heart:
"I have heard from the scriptures that it is higher even than Vaikuntha."

**155.** A great hubbub, singing, someone dancing;
in this way he came to the Vishrama ghat, where Krishna rested.

**156.** He beholds it, touches it, sprinkles the water on his head,
and knows in his heart the good fortune of many births.

**157.** He sits gazing towards the east,
and three Vrajavasis go by, talking among themselves.

**158.** One speaks and another listens: "What will become of us?
Three have withdrawn from sight — there is great pain in our hearts.

**159.** First Sanatana withdrew;
some days after him, Raghunatha Bhatta;

**160.** and now Sri Rupa Gosani has withdrawn;
life will not stay in the body, the soul writhes."

**161.** Srinivasa hears what they are saying:
"Fate has turned my misfortune into utter hopelessness."

**162.** With joined hands he addressed them:
"What were you three saying to one another?"

**163.** They said: "Brother, what are you asking?
What shall we tell you of the pain in our hearts?

**164.** Vrindavana is empty, yet death does not come;
in separation from Rupa life cannot be sustained."

**165.** As soon as he heard this, Srinivasa rose from the spot:
"What blame shall I lay on fate? My heart is bursting.

**166.** My eyes cannot see the road — where shall I go?
Fate has drowned me in an ocean of sorrow."

**167.** Until midnight[^05-18] he walks along the road;
weeping and weeping he goes, clutching his head with his hands.

**168.** Going some way in the direction of his homeland,[^05-19]
he came to a tree and lay fallen beneath it.

**169.** All the torments that came into his heart at that time —
how much can I write? He alone knows them.

**170.** Steeling my hard heart, I have written this;
hearing it, the heart of this wicked one does not burst.

**171.** I write only because my guru's command is binding on me;
apart from that, what do I know? I am ignorant.

**172.** He whose hope is the feet of Sri Jahnava and Virachandra,
Nityananda Dasa, tells the Prema-vilasa.

*Thus ends the fifth vilasa of the Prema-vilasa.*

# Vilasa 6. The Vision of Rupa and Sanatana; Srinivasa's Initiation

*Srinivasa's despair under the tree; Rupa and Sanatana appear to him; the meeting with Jiva at Govinda's temple; Gopala Bhatta; initiation before Radha-ramana and instructions on sadhana*

**1.** Glory, glory to Gaurachandra, glory to Nityananda!
Glory to Advaita-chandra, glory to the host of Gaura's devotees!

**2.** Glory, glory to Sri Jahnava, glory to Virachandra!
Those two pairs of lotus feet are my shelter.

**3.** Now hear what I write, O hearers,
you whose life's treasure is the lila of Radha and Krishna.

**4.** To whoever tells and whoever hears, my obeisance.
[Here is] the full account of Srinivasa's state beneath the tree.

**5.** He weeps and rolls on the ground like a mad baul,
crying "Rupa! Sanatana!" and moaning "Alas, alas!"

**6.** "Whatever I long for turns out fruitless;
all the commands given me have proved vain.

**7.** In Purushottama I did not get to see Mahaprabhu;
with the Pandita[^06-1] I did not get to read the Sri Bhagavata.

**8.** Sarakara Thakura commanded: 'Go to Vrindavana,
go quickly and see Rupa and Sanatana.'

**9.** Ishvari commanded: 'Go to Vrindavana,
see Rupa together with Sanatana.'

**10.** So Sri Jahnava Thakurani told me.
My life is leaving me — to whom shall I tell this sorrow?

**11.** How many offences have I committed through how many births!
I endure the pain of separation, and yet I do not die.

**12.** Leaving my own land I have come — to Mathura, or where?
I have not seen Vrindavana; my birth has been in vain.

**13.** I would have taken shelter at the Bhatta Gosani's feet,
but in separation from the two Gosanis how can life remain?

**14.** What is the use of going home? I did not get to see them;
my body is in vain — I have not gained shelter at their feet.

**15.** If Srinivasa dies, who will come to see him?
There remains the hope of attaining those feet in another birth.

**16.** Taking refuge in this dharma, how many, many people
float in an ocean of happiness, leaving sorrow and grief behind!

**17.** But all sorrows [fate] has laid upon me;
with what consolation can I console myself? My soul is shattered.

**18.** O Prabhus Rupa and Sanatana, masters of Srinivasa!
I never saw your forms with my own eyes."

**19.** In this way he lies on the ground beneath the tree;
his body is motionless, only breath remains.

**20.** Seeing Srinivasa weeping and crying out,
Rupa and Sanatana came and appeared to him in person.

**21.** "Rise, rise, Srinivasa, look — we are close by;
you are the Prabhu's image of prema, you are my very life.

**22.** All these days I have been watching the road for you —
how can life remain in separation from the Prabhu?

**23.** Why are you turning back, my son? Go to Vrindavana;
may your desire be fulfilled and your longing satisfied.

**24.** Take shelter at the feet of Sri Gopala Bhatta;
know for certain that through him my mercy will come to you.

**25.** I have told Sri Jiva about you;
read the books with him, keep his company."

**26.** He had not slept — Srinivasa rose at once,
and, rising, prostrated himself, offering obeisance and praise.

**27.** Rising, he gazes at their forms, the craving of the eyes;
standing, he beholds the splendour of the two brothers' bodies.

**28.** Their bodies golden and full, a thin shikha on the head,
tilaka on the brow, kanthi beads gracing the neck.

**29.** All over their bodies the two names "Radha" and "Krishna" are written;
over the kaupina an outer cloth is worn.

**30.** They count Hari-nama on the hand, uttering it with the tongue,
and now and then the names of Radha and Krishna sound forth.

**31.** The fragrance of their bodies is like kunkuma and other scents;
the rows of their teeth are lovely as kunda flowers, their smile sweet.

**32.** All sorrow went far away — [there was] an ocean of joy;
Srinivasa's heart became utterly intoxicated.

**33.** His mind overcome with ecstasy, he fell to the ground;
they themselves lifted him and placed their feet on his head.

**34.** Both vanished and went to their own place;
Srinivasa came to outward awareness and reflected in his mind:

**35.** "What more is there to do? Go, my mind, to Vrindavana!
The masters of the masterless are Prabhus Rupa and Sanatana.

**36.** Sri Gopala Bhatta Prabhu is my life;
may Sri Jiva Gosani show me boundless mercy."

**37.** Overwhelmed with feeling, he goes along the road,
not knowing whether it is night or morning has come.

**38.** Meanwhile Rupa and Sanatana themselves commanded Sri Jiva:
"Srinivasa has come.

**39.** In the evening, at the time of Govinda's arati,
he will surely come for darshan.

**40.** Seeing Govinda's form, he will be overcome with ecstasy
and in a frenzy fall on the left side of the doorway.

**41.** When you go for Govinda's darshan at that time,
search for him to the right and left of the doorway.

**42.** Console him and keep him in your own house,
and then take him to Sri Gopala Bhatta,

**43.** so that he may show mercy to Srinivasa;
and you, take him with you and read the books of bhakti with him.

**44.** Teach him those books for the sake of Gauda,
so that people may practise them, abandoning jnana and karma."

**45.** In the same way they went to the Bhatta Gosani
and told him in detail of Srinivasa's coming:

**46.** "He has come to Mathura and today will come to Vrindavana;
he will take shelter at your feet.

**47.** Show him special, boundless mercy,
so that he may go to Gauda with the books of bhakti."

**48.** Having said this, Sri Rupa vanished.
Now I write the account of Srinivasa's arrival.

**49.** Rapt in prema he goes along, dancing as he goes,
walking the road and looking to right and left.

**50.** With his eyes he sees a golden Vrindavana;
with his mouth he says: "Sri Govinda! Gopinatha!"

**51.** He saw the enclosing wall around Govinda's temple;[^06-2]
at the sight his heart overflowed and could find no rest.

**52.** Streams of tears flow unceasingly from his eyes:
"I shall behold Govinda's lotus feet!"

**53.** Saying this, he arrived there in the evening;
flutes, vinas, pakhawajs and gongs resounded.

**54.** Standing behind the people, he gazes at the form
and sees tears raining from everyone's eyes.

**55.** Prostrating themselves, all went inside,
and Srinivasa came into the jagamohana.[^06-3]

**56.** He sees Govinda's beauty, with joy in his heart:
as is the form, so are the qualities — just as it was described.[^06-4]

**57.** Rupa composed an ashtaka[^06-5] on what he had seen,
and wove prema into it syllable by syllable.

**58.** Govinda's body surpasses Manmatha himself;
Srinivasa sank into it and found no bottom.

**59.** Rapt in feeling, he lies to the left of the doorway,
and people whisper to one another: what are they saying?

**60.** At this moment Sri Jiva arrived;
prostrating himself, he took darshan of Govinda.

**61.** Many people were with him, carrying lit torches;
the Prabhu's[^06-6] command had been given, and his heart was glad.

**62.** He went and saw him lying to the left of the doorway;
sitting down, he calmed him, laying a hand on his body.

**63.** He saw the intense feeling in the depths of his heart,
and with the people took him through the gate to his own house.

**64.** When the second prahara of the night came,
he said nothing; only his throat rattled.

**65.** Then Jiva knew that the ecstasy was coming to an end;
sitting near him, he laid a hand on his body.

**66.** After a while he cried out "Govinda!",
and tears flowed from his eyes, streaming down his breast.

**67.** Sri Jiva asks him: "What is your name?
Tell me, let me hear it; let my heart rejoice."

**68.** Prostrating himself, he gave his name as Srinivasa:
"I was born in a twice-born family; my village is Chakhandi."

**69.** "Friend, friend!" [Jiva] cried, and embraced him:
"Gauranga, the treasure-house of mercy, has brought you to me.

**70.** Such an ocean of compassion I have never seen:
Rupa and Sanatana have given wealth to a pauper."

**71.** The next day, rising, he said: "Listen, Srinivasa:
it is the Prabhu's command — come, go to the Bhatta Gosani.

**72.** Go and take shelter at his feet."
"As you command," said Srinivasa.

**73.** So saying, the two went to the Gosani,
and from afar prostrated themselves.

**74.** "I was born in a brahmana family; my name is Srinivasa;
if you command, I shall take shelter at your feet."

**75.** "Come, come, Srinivasa, come, my son!
How much affliction has fallen upon me in my old age!"

**76.** Approaching his feet, he prostrated himself;
in his mercy he placed his hand on his back.

**77.** Placing his feet on his head, he told him everything:
in separation from the two Gosanis he had suffered great pain.

**78.** "See, in this body of mine only bones are left;
to whom shall I go now for comfort?

**79.** Why have you come so late, my son?
[You were] needed — I would have gone along with you.[^06-7]

**80.** Why did you not come all this time, Srinivasa?
Everyone hoped to see you."

**81.** "Prabhu, I beg you: forgive my fault.
I had a great longing to read the Sri Bhagavata.

**82.** Because of my fault my heart was troubled;
I went once again to the Pandita Gosani.

**83.** I saw that book — it was in the Prabhu's own hand,
but all the letters were rubbed away, and I was much grieved.

**84.** The Pandita Gosani said to me:
'Bring a new book from Sarakara Thakura's house.'

**85.** With his letter I came to Khanda-grama;
I was given the book and came back to Purushottama.

**86.** While still on the way I heard that the Pandita Gosani had withdrawn from sight;
my heart was stricken and I was in dire straits.

**87.** Then in Navadvipa I saw the feet of Ishvari,[^06-8]
and with her leave I went to Shantipur.

**88.** At Khardaha I saw the feet of Jahnava;
she commanded: 'Go and see Thakura Abhirama.'

**89.** Everyone commanded: 'Go to Vrindavana.'
All that happened I lay before you, Prabhu."

**90.** Hearing his words, the Gosani wept long
and fell senseless to the ground.

**91.** "My son, you are fortunate, and I am luckless;
for all those faults I suffer so much sorrow.

**92.** I never had the sight of Nityananda's feet,
I never saw Advaita-chandra — my life is fruitless.

**93.** Nor did I ever again see the feet of Ishvari,[^06-9]
and Sarakara Thakura never once showed me mercy.

**94.** Fate has thwarted all these wishes of mine;
with all these sorrows life will not stay within.

**95.** And now both Rupa and Sanatana have withdrawn;
fate has fashioned my heart out of wood and stone."

**96.** Jiva was present, sitting on his seat.
"Fate has deprived me of everything good.

**97.** I am a sinner: they have all withdrawn from sight,
and through all these separations I cling to this worthless life."

**98.** Srinivasa weeps, fallen at the feet of both;
how many displays of that feeling can I write down?

**99.** In a changed mood Sri Jiva goes to his lodging;
Srinivasa bowed and took his leave [of the Gosani].

**100.** So the two[^06-10] remain in the delight of talk about Krishna;
knowing neither night nor day, they float ever in prema.

**101.** A good day and an auspicious hour were calculated,
and Srinivasa went with the Gosani.[^06-11]

**102.** They took a garland of tulasi manjaris and sandal paste;
carrying them in his hands, Srinivasa followed behind.

**103.** The two came to Sri Gopala Bhatta;
Srinivasa bowed and offered humble praise.

**104.** "Rise, my son; I shall speak — hear the word that is the essence."
Having heard him, Srinivasa spoke again:

**105.** "Mahaprabhu, the guru of the world — the dharma he practised
is all set out in the books of Sri Rupa.

**106.** The instructor is [guru] to the servant birth after birth;
so is the mood of following established — of the follower and the one followed."[^06-12]

**107.** At that moment Sri Jiva said:
"As you have said, so accept him."

**108.** "Good, good," said the Gosani, and rose quickly,
and went with Srinivasa, joy in his heart.

**109.** To where Sri Radha-ramana disports himself
the two went for darshan.

**110.** Washing their feet, they bowed,
and, prostrating themselves again, entered the temple.

**111.** Knowing the time, Srinivasa bows:
"Come, come, Srinivasa, come near me."

**112.** He sat at the guru's left, facing east,
and with a joyful face beholds the Deity's form.

**113.** Holding his feet, he surrendered himself;
accepting him as his own, the Gosani said:

**114.** "Wash both hands once more,
and with joined palms meditate on the son of the King of Vraja.

**115.** At his left is Sri Radhika, enchanting beyond measure,
and Lalita, the manjaris and others, beautifully adorned."

**116.** He had him worship each of them separately,
offering tulasi manjaris, garlands, sandal paste and the rest.

**117.** Hand to hand he joined them all into their groups,
and made Srinivasa the maidservant[^06-13] of them all.

**118.** "Worship Sri Radha-ramana once more,
at whose feet all desires are fulfilled."

**119.** He put fragrant sandal paste on his breast,
and many tulasi manjaris were laid at the [Deity's] feet.

**120.** Placing his right hand on his head, he spoke the Hari-nama,
and then the rule of the five names of Radha and Krishna.[^06-14]

**121.** Holding both his hands, he imparted the mantra of Radha and Krishna
and let him hear the kama-bija, counting on the fingers.

**122.** "These mantras you will remember
whenever you meditate, taking shelter in her.

**123.** Under the shelter of Guna Manjari you are Mani Manjari;
I shall tell you all about your group.

**124.** Rupa, Guna, Rati, Rasa and Manjulali[^06-15] —
these are the companions you are with; they are favourable to you.

**125.** The service follows the bhajana of raga that follows the ragatmika;
the words of Sri Rupa Gosani agree with this.

**126.** "Service" is the name of the sadhaka's great yearning;
when it is perfected, all this is attained.

**127.** The body performs sadhana, and so it is called "sadhaka";
know for certain that perfection lies in the shelter of a sakhi.

**128.** I have named many of the sixty-four limbs of sadhana;
understand clearly what is favourable and what is unfavourable.

**129.** Whatever is unfavourable, reject;
whatever is favourable is the cause of attainment.

**130.** [Guard against] all offences in service and against the Name:
entering the heart, they ruin bhajana.

**131.** The limbs of devotion to Krishna are the attainment through sadhana;[^06-16]
if one does otherwise, sadhana flies off who knows where.

**132.** The mind fixed on Krishna is the cause of attaining Krishna;
by practising those limbs, attainment is assured.

**133.** Listen, Srinivasa, to how offence arises;
I shall tell and explain it in detail.

**134.** One who does not practise the limbs of bhakti himself and reviles [them],
[…][^06-17]

**135.** The banyan seed is tiny, but the tree is enormous —
so offence grows day by day.

**136.** Reviling the gods, causing suffering to living beings and the like —
the heart not drawn to these has bhakti in that measure.

**137.** When you see the scriptures, then you will know;
at that moment you will accept my words as true.

**138.** Having set out on this path, be careful;
bhajana of Krishna — the saints and scriptures are its proof."

**139.** The mercy shown to Srinivasa — in it is all perfection;
fate did not give me a hundred thousand mouths and a hundred thousand ears.[^06-18]

**140.** He whose hope is the feet of Sri Jahnava and Virachandra,
Nityananda Dasa, tells the Prema-vilasa.

*Thus ends the sixth vilasa of the Prema-vilasa.*

# Vilasa 7. Lokanatha and Bhugarbha

*The descent of Mahaprabhu's companions: Vishvarupa and Nityananda; Lokanatha comes to Mahaprabhu in Navadvipa; a confidential talk on Radha's mood and the reason for sannyasa; teachings on raga and the mission to Vrindavana; the journey of Lokanatha and Bhugarbha*

**1.** Glory, glory to Sri Chaitanya, glory to Nityananda!
Glory to Advaita-chandra, glory to the host of Gaura's devotees!

**2.** Glory, glory to Sri Jahnava, glory to Virachandra!
Those lotus feet are my shelter.

**3.** Glory to the hearers! Listen with one mind,
give ear to a most wondrous account.

**4.** Whatever I have written here is all true;
I write at the command of the Prabhu, my shelter.

**5.** I write all this about the cause of the descent;
hearing it, you will find joy, and your pain will leave you.

**6.** When Krishna descended in Vraja,
the scriptures proclaimed it as Vraja and Vrindavana.

**7.** It is written that the circle of Vraja is eighty-four kroshas;
everywhere in it dwell Krishna's companions.

**8.** That same Krishna descended as the Lord of Navadvipa,
with mother, father, servants, friends and girl companions.

**9.** First of all Vishnu himself descended:[^07-1]
in Shantipur appeared Advaita, the crest-jewel [of devotees].

**10.** He is the crest-jewel of devotees, and so he is called Acharya;
through him [all the Prabhu's work] was accomplished.[^07-2]

**11.** Madhavendra and others — twenty-four sannyasis:
eight, eight and eight more[^07-3] — these are treasures of prema.

**12.** All of them are Krishna's family in Vraja;
all who came with him I shall describe in detail.

**13.** Four kinds of friends and servants, five kinds of girl companions —
all this I write by command from the Prabhu's holy mouth.

**14.** Who was who before and after, and what each one's true identity was —
to avoid controversy I have written no more of it.

**15.** As I was commanded by the Prabhu to write,
so with deep faith I write it fully.

**16.** Shachi Thakurani, wife of Jagannatha Mishra —
know that her first son was Vishvarupa.

**17.** His beauty had no equal, and he was very learned;
seeing and hearing him, his mother and father rejoiced.

**18.** The house of Shachi's father was at Belpukhuria;
it is needed for the story, and so I write of it.

**19.** Yogeshvara Pandita was her father's eldest son,
[then came] Ratnagarbha Pandita; Shachi was younger than they.

**20.** His son, Lokanatha Pandita, rich in virtues,
went along with Vishvarupa wherever he went.[^07-4]

**21.** They studied in one place with great delight;
what became of him I shall write a little at the end.

**22.** Both mother and father held [Vishvarupa] as dear as life;
after some days he came into Advaita's company.

**23.** He expounded the scriptures and spoke of much learning,
and in a short time plainly became a great scholar.

**24.** Weary of worldly life, he went off to distant lands;
his father and mother weep, their life nearly at an end.

**25.** He gave up shikha and sacred thread and took the staff,
and wore a kaupina and ruddy cloth.

**26.** His name became Shankararanya Puri;
what shall I say of all his kinds of virtue?

**27.** Pandita Lokanatha became his disciple;
he visits the holy places with him and serves him constantly.

**28.** After two years he attained perfection;
this, it is said, was the doing of Yogamaya herself.[^07-5]

**29.** In Rarh there is a village called Ekachakra;
there lived a brahmana rich in virtues —

**30.** Hadai Pandita; his wife was Padmavati;
from her womb came the birth [of the Lord].

**31.** On the day of Rama-navami she conceived,
and the joy in the hearts of mother and father grew boundless.

**32.** Day by day the child grew in the womb, and ten months passed;
the joy of the brahmana and his wife kept growing.

**33.** In the month of Magha, in the bright fortnight, on the thirteenth day,
with every auspicious sign he was born at that moment.

**34.** They named him Nityananda, and all rejoiced;
at times he would stay stock-still, at times laugh aloud.

**35.** For fourteen years he played from house to house;
one day a sannyasi came to the house.

**36.** [Hadai] fed him with a joyful heart;
pleased, the sannyasi spoke these words:

**37.** "Hadai Pandita, hear my request:
give me one alms — I have need of it."

**38.** "As you command," he agreed.
"Give me as alms this son of yours.

**39.** In my old age he will take me round the holy places;
you will have every happiness — do not grieve."

**40.** Stricken by the parting, he placed his son in his hands,
and the sannyasi at once took Nityananda away with him.

**41.** He made him his disciple, but he did not take the staff,
and wandered with him in the guise of an avadhuta.

**42.** His heart was overflowing with the mood of Nanda's son;
what he did, where he stayed — he had no outward awareness.

**43.** That great soul was Ishvara Puri himself.[^07-6]
One day he smiled and said to Nityananda:

**44.** "We have wandered through all the holy places there are;
do this one thing, my son, and all will be accomplished:

**45.** the son of Nanda has descended in Navadvipa;
seek him out with a joyful heart."

**46.** I write the simple account — [elsewhere] it is told at length;
just to hear it brings happiness and boundless joy.

**47.** Sankarshana and Balarama are one and the same being;
Vishvarupa and Shankararanya are a difference of form in different kalpas.[^07-7]

**48.** At home his name was Nityananda, in the ashrama Avadhuta;
in the same way the son of Nanda is the son of Shachi.

**49.** All of Mahaprabhu's own companions descended [before him];
I write of them as I heard from the Prabhu's mouth.

**50.** After them he descended in Shachi's womb,
and the devotees descended in various lands.

**51.** On the full-moon day of Phalguna, at an auspicious moment, was the birth;
thus Mahaprabhu grew day by day.

**52.** As on the earth all the rivers and streams
come and meet together in the ocean,

**53.** so to Gaurachandra, the ocean of prema,
[the devotees] come one by one and join him with joy in their hearts.

**54.** East of Navadvipa is a land called Yashohara;[^07-8]
listen further, I shall tell of it.

**55.** In it is a village called Talagadi;
there was born one named Lokanatha.

**56.** His father was named Padmanabha Chakravarti,
his mother was named Sita, the abode of every virtue.

**57.** They were high kulinas, as everyone in the land knew;
with great care they had their son educated.

**58.** In that land there was no scholar to equal him;
day by day more virtues entered into him.

**59.** His mother and father were anxious, seeing his state:
"If our son stays at home — that is our great hope."

**60.** They prayed especially to God
that Lokanatha should suffer no hardship.

**61.** A great fear constantly gripped his mother and father:
"If only by some good fortune our son would stay at home!"

**62.** They wished to get him married with all care;
seeing his parents' efforts, he reflected in his mind.

**63.** He thinks: "How am I to leave worldly life?" —
and every impulse of renunciation arose in his heart.

**64.** Constantly he remembers Chaitanya's feet;
his mind longs: "I shall go and see him."

**65.** In the month of Agrahayana, in the cold, having lain down to sleep,
he took counsel within his own heart.

**66.** At midnight he left the house and went out,
and went eight kroshas; morning came.

**67.** Rising, his mother and father did not see him
and wept long with stricken hearts.

**68.** That pain, that sorrow cannot be told;
he alone knows it in whose heart it arose.

**69.** Meanwhile he reached Navadvipa
and walked slowly, pondering.

**70.** Asking people the way, he goes to the Prabhu,
thinking in his mind: "What shall I do, what shall I say?

**71.** When I see the Prabhu I shall tell him who I am;
but who knows whether the Prabhu will be kind to me?"

**72.** Saying this, at times he weeps and walks slowly on:
"Accept me!" he says, "Lord of my life!"

**73.** The Prabhu is seated, with devotees all around —
Gadadhara, Srivasa, Murari and many more.

**74.** Beholding the Prabhu's form, he weeps
and bows, his heart overflowing with prema.

**75.** He joins his palms, but what is he to say? No words leave his mouth;
at that moment the Prabhu rushes to embrace him.

**76.** "O Lokanatha, you have forgotten me!
How have you spent your time, to what lands did you go?"

**77.** Saying this, Gaura weeps as he embraces him:
"It seems fate has brought me a treasure.

**78.** I had been as one blind, forgetting everything."
Lokanatha weeps, clasping the Prabhu's feet.

**79.** Taking Lokanatha by the hand, he seated him beside him;
now he gazes at his face, now he laughs.

**80.** There too he met the Pandita;[^07-9]
bowing, the two embraced.

**81.** "Having seen with my own eyes such a jewel as you,
after so many days my eyes have been blessed with good fortune."

**82.** All were in great joy, rapt in talk about Krishna;
no one had outward awareness, all floated in the ocean of prema.

**83.** He met Nityananda, Advaita and all the others;
he bowed to them and they embraced him.

**84.** Thus for five nights his meeting with the Prabhu went on;
they relished much talk about Krishna and kirtana.

**85.** One day the Prabhu said: "Listen, Lokanatha:
how did you leave worldly life and come here?"

**86.** He told in every detail how he had come:
"All sorrow is unreal; these feet alone are real.

**87.** How I could break free I do not know:
you put the rope of mercy around my neck and drew me here."

**88.** Thus Mahaprabhu, sitting in seclusion,
called Lokanatha and gave him a command.

**89.** Holding his hand, he said: "Listen, Lokanatha:
to whom shall I tell the sorrow that rises in my heart?

**90.** Why have I come, and where are you all?
That work is not being accomplished, and my heart aches.

**91.** To Nityananda, Advaita and all the devotees
I shall speak to each according to his realization.

**92.** But the realization of my own heart — to whom shall I tell it?
Seeing me, some revile me, some go away laughing.

**93.** Taking on Radhika's mood I have come to Gauda,
[…] a joy boundless and special.[^07-10]

**94.** For my sake Radha gave up caste, family and wealth;
leaving everything, she surrendered her very self.

**95.** She made me the lord of her life; in separation from me
day and night she pines for my beauty and my virtues.

**96.** Her body has become like a lotus-stalk fibre,
her clothing soiled, she wanders like a madwoman.

**97.** Hundreds and hundreds of times she blames the Creator:
'He did not give me a hundred thousand eyes, as I wished.'

**98.** With her eyes she looks on no other man's face;
she hears of my virtues and speaks of them with her mouth.

**99.** She is ever restless for union with me,
and speaks of it so much from grove to grove on the Yamuna's bank.

**100.** I am a rogue, impudent, an utter libertine,
turning truth into untruth, a cheat and a deceiver.

**101.** And yet, if she sees me face to face,
her pique departs and she feels a hundred thousand joys.

**102.** And if on some day there is no meeting with me,
a moment's separation she reckons as a hundred yugas.

**103.** She whose love is such, whose longing is such —
even the scriptures cannot tell such virtues of hers.

**104.** She who sports in Vrindavana is my beloved;
she is my life, and I am her life.

**105.** For her sake I dwell in Vrindavana,
and day and night I think of her play.

**106.** That rasa of which friends, servants, father and mother are deprived,
that rasa which enchants [me] — only the sakhis know it.

**107.** For her virtues and her love I remain in her debt;
this I have told you, Lokanatha.

**108.** That is one sorrow of my heart; hear another thing:
seeing me, a certain brahmana went away reviling me in every way.

**109.** Earlier he committed an offence against me;
it has borne fruit, and he does not know it.

**110.** Not knowing that Krishna is the guru of the world,
he wanders about drunk with false pride.

**111.** He says that Krishna's body is [nothing…],[^07-11]
and, leaving such feet of Krishna, clings to another shelter.

**112.** From his[^07-12] mouth [the brahmanas] were born, yet he does not accept it;
he worships one thing, says another, and drinks wine.

**113.** Holding Krishna's power, he is mighty in the world,
yet what Vyasadeva wrote he twists into something else.

**114.** He will not call Krishna guru, but worships a maidservant;[^07-13]
for this offence how many will go to Yama's abode!

**115.** Having left Krishna, his mind has lost its strength;
he does not know that such a person is lower than a shudra.

**116.** That is one sorrow, and there are other matters too;
I shall tell — hear a little of the reason for this.

**117.** Between lies the month of Pausha; in the bright fortnight of Magha,
on the third day, I shall take sannyasa, for all to see.

**118.** All brahmanas accept the staff-bearer as their guru;
I have told you this firm resolve of my heart.

**119.** Truly, for the sake of these brahmanas I shall take sannyasa,
leave home and wander from land to land.

**120.** That is the outward reasoning; and now the intent of my heart —
listen, Lokanatha, I tell it to you for certain.

**121.** The whole purpose is to take on Radhika's mood;
who will understand, who will hear what is in my heart?

**122.** The colour of my body Radha wears as her clothing —
that is why blue garments give her such delight.

**123.** In separation from me she wears ruddy cloth
and ever regards herself as my own maidservant.

**124.** For my sake Radha and her companions,
distraught in separation, were ready to give up their lives.

**125.** I too shall give up my life for her sake;
that state will come — you will remain and hear of it.

**126.** I shall take on her lustre and wear ruddy cloth,
and with a joyful heart become her servant.

**127.** That is why I shall clothe my body in ruddy cloth;
I shall chant her virtues — so I have told you.

**128.** All her virtues I can never repay,
even if I had a lifespan of a hundred births on earth.

**129.** For her virtues and her love I have become her debtor;
this I have told you, Lokanatha.

**130.** I shall flood the world with her fame and glory —
then I shall know that she has shown me such mercy.

**131.** I shall attain her prema, my eyes will weep,
and, grey with dust, I shall dance in sankirtana."

**132.** Saying this, Gaura-raya weeps aloud
and, crying "Radha! Vrindavana!", rolls on the ground.

**133.** Lokanatha held the Prabhu in his arms and calmed him;
but as he spoke of Radha's virtues, he began to tremble.

**134.** "How much sorrow, how much joy — my heart knows;
only the groves of Vrindavana are witness to it.

**135.** Rise at dawn and go to Vrindavana;
after you will come Rupa and Sanatana,

**136.** and those named Sri Gopala Bhatta and Raghunatha Bhatta,
and then Raghunatha Dasa, a treasury of virtues.

**137.** All gathering together in Vrindavana,
they will describe the lila in books and perform their own bhajana.

**138.** As I have told you, so I shall tell Rupa,
and send him off, infusing him with power.

**139.** I shall say more — hear what is in my mind:
as that shelter is, so is the attainment, and so the bhajana.

**140.** To make it firmer still I say it again:
when one hears from the guru's mouth, everything becomes certain.

**141.** The lila that is my cherished goal — that is the worship;
can other people come to know it?

**142.** You are the crest-jewel of the wise, you know every secret;
still I shall tell you the essence of that dharma.

**143.** The scriptures speak of full, fuller and fullest;
the difference of forms points to a difference in substance.[^07-14]

**144.** There are two kinds, svakiya and parakiya;
hear also the views on this.

**145.** All the women of Dvaraka are called svakiya;
among the parakiya, know that the gopis are the best.

**146.** The maidens devoted to the vow of Katyayani —
know for certain that they are in that same Vraja.

**147.** Among them there are two chief group-leaders in Vraja:
Radha and Chandravali are there.

**148.** The natures of the two are of two kinds:
Radha and hers are vama, Chandravali dakshina."[^07-15]

**149.** As he spoke, another state came over the Prabhu;
holding Lokanatha's hand, he began to speak:

**150.** "There is one desire of my heart, a kind of corollary;
in that which is of gain to me you shall be my helper.

**151.** In another body the perfected devotee forgets the lila
and regards himself as entirely like prakriti.[^07-16]

**152.** Chaitanya himself instructs him,
and as he listens he remembers everything.

**153.** Such is the Prabhu's mercy to the perfected devotee;
only he whose mind is firm knows it.

**154.** What I shall do, what I shall say, the word of my heart —
that will become the renowned scripture in every way.

**155.** When I send Rupa and Sanatana to Vrindavana,
they will reflect on many books and treatises.

**156.** All of you together will approve it well;
let no one slight it — that would grieve me."

**157.** Lokanatha said: "Prabhu, I beg of you:
dispel my doubt and purify my mind.

**158.** Vyasadeva wrote many books
and set forth in them the bhajana of Krishna.

**159.** But not all of that accords with this way of bhajana;
give your command, Prabhu, I bow before you."

**160.** "Kalas, amshas, vilasas and the rest are one self, one form;
hear what is the nature of each one's lila.

**161.** Descriptions of all this in the scriptures are endless,
but the worship of Vraja is not set out in detail there.

**162.** Dasya, sakhya, vatsalya, madhura — the essence of the moods;
here no one accepts aishvarya.[^07-17]

**163.** And to compose on the mood of madhurya —
no one's mind enters into that.

**164.** The way of madhura no one knows;
madhura is known to him to whom its account is [revealed].

**165.** One qualified for another rasa does not know this love —
in it is the lila of the hero with his beloved.

**166.** Radha's dear attendants know its bounds;
no one else knows its greatness.

**167.** This parakiya lila is most profound;
no one else knows its inner heart.

**168.** The Bhagavata, the Puranas and the rest are Vyasa's descriptions;
in them power and majesty are made manifest.

**169.** All this bhajana he did not set forth;
what is set forth there is aishvarya and the like, mixed with knowledge.

**170.** So that people might be careful in entering [the path],
he wrote in them of the excellence of Krishna's bhajana.

**171.** Where each one dwells, with whom each stays —
who has the power to describe it?

**172.** Sri Rupa saw Krishna's lila with his own eyes;
still I shall infuse [him] with power.

**173.** For firmness, what he hears from his guru's mouth
he will describe with joy and delight.

**174.** From the scriptures and converse with the saints deep prema arises;[^07-18]
one surely leads to the company of the other.

**175.** He will bring many scriptures, and their intent will be shown;
when the events of the lila are set forth, the meaning will be understood.

**176.** He will place established proofs among them;
seeing firm statements, all will accept them.

**177.** Where that rasa is not found in those scriptures,
he will write the word of his heart, full of rasa.

**178.** Just now he[^07-19] is in the king's presence;
in my mercy I shall send him after you.

**179.** All will be together in that Vrindavana
and spend their time together relishing the lila.

**180.** They will propagate the worship of Vraja in the scriptures,
by which the son of Nanda is attained.

**181.** What should and should not be done, what is gain and what is loss —
hearing this, all people will take shelter.

**182.** In this is my joy; this is the word of my heart;
and then whatever I say will fully agree with that.

**183.** Age after age people worship Krishna,
but who attains Radhika's feet?

**184.** All those firm scriptures of many kinds —
hearing them, I shall have boundless joy.

**185.** I myself shall be intoxicated and intoxicate[^07-20] the people of the world —
for this my heart pines every moment —

**186.** so that people may attain Radhika's two feet
and practise bhajana and remembrance, leaving sorrow and grief behind.

**187.** For your sake I shall beg alms of her;
to whom else shall I tell the sorrow of my heart?

**188.** Wherever the lord of Radha's life performs his lila,
there, together with all the sakhis —

**189.** I have no power to describe it.
I am a poor sannyasi, and I have a need:

**190.** I shall eat myself and distribute to all the people of the world;
let them know that you have made me rich with your wealth.

**191.** In my sorrow you will be sorrowful, in my joy joyful;
whenever anything happens, write and send me news.

**192.** And I shall write and send to all of you,
and through those letters you will know what is well and what is ill.

**193.** Go to that place, your own Vrindavana;
if I have the good fortune, I shall get to see [it]."

**194.** I am ignorant and foolish — how much of this can I write?
I write what I have heard; devotees, do not find fault.

**195.** "Hear something more, O steadfast one;
what I say, hear with a steady mind.

**196.** If one gives up everything — the dharma of varna and ashrama —
he alone knows the inner essence of that dharma.

**197.** One who holds to varna and ashrama does not have exclusive shelter,
and Krishna himself does not accept him.

**198.** After staying some days in Nilachala I shall come to Gauda,
and, having renounced everything, wander in the garb of a pauper."

**199.** Lokanatha said: "Prabhu, I beg of you:
hearing from your holy mouth, doubt is dispelled.

**200.** Hearing this, my heart was amazed;
I have a request — please accept it.

**201.** If such followers of varna and ashrama go beyond varna,
then all will take shelter at Krishna's feet.

**202.** Whoever worships whomever, he accepts him;
having taken shelter, where will the soul go?"

**203.** The Prabhu said: "Lokanatha, listen once more:
what you have asked — hear it to the end.

**204.** [People of] the four varnas and ashramas took shelter in Krishna;
[hear] by what bhajana Krishna shows them mercy.

**205.** Hear it carefully, with a steady mind:
the scriptures to be read and the intent of the saints are deep.

**206.** He who can understand gains Krishna's company;
whoever transgresses [this order] is broken by it.

**207.** The people of Vraja do not regard Krishna as God:
they always serve him in love and float in prema.

**208.** For that joy they gave up everything;
and they have one more mighty saying:

**209.** the mark of raga is that it does not take the reasoning of scripture;
he who does thus attains that treasure.

**210.** He who, abandoning karma, performs bhajana turned towards raga,
attains such feet.

**211.** He who in body, speech and mind holds another view
does not attain Vraja — that is another aim.[^07-21]

**212.** If one does this, all those moods are attained in this body;
if not, the nature of sadhana is attained in another body.

**213.** Lokanatha, you have forgotten your own nature:
who you are, where you dwell, what your mood is,

**214.** in which group you are, what your name is,
whom you serve, wholly absorbed.

**215.** You are the sakhi Manjulali, of old a companion of Radha;
your service is to anoint her body and put on her girdle of bells.

**216.** You are ever at play with Radhika,
and, regarding yourself as a maidservant, you serve her constantly.

**217.** Happy in Radhika's happiness, sorrowful in her sorrow —
such is the renowned sakhi devoted to service."

**218.** As he heard this from the Prabhu's mouth, everything was revealed to him;
gazing at his face, he began to weep.

**219.** "Intoxicated with that rasa, stay in that place;
save my life — go to Vrindavana.

**220.** Girikunda, Govardhana, Javata, Varshana,[^07-22]
Sanketa, the secluded groves, all the places of the lila:

**221.** live in those places and your heart will be happy;
leave your attachment to me and set out on the road.

**222.** Dwell there where your birthplace is,[^07-23]
perform bhajana and remember Kishora and Kishori.

**223.** Chir-ghat, Rasa-sthali, the rows of kadambas,
and to the east of them a grove of supreme sweetness.

**224.** Tamala, bakula and banyan trees grow there;
live in that place and your heart will be happy.

**225.** Rasa-sthali, Vamshivata, Nidhuvana —
rest in the midst of Dhira-samira.

**226.** Bathe in the Yamuna, live on alms you do not ask for,
perform bhajana and remembrance, and teach living beings.

**227.** Become perfected — from you a branch will spring,
and its followers will be past counting.

**228.** Rupa and the others of your circle will soon come together;
then you will know [everything], when all are gathered."

**229.** The night passed; in the morning the Prabhu was seated;
saying something to Lokanatha, he seated him beside him.

**230.** The Prabhu said: "Lokanatha, go to Vrindavana;
all sorrow will leave you and you will find your own happiness.

**231.** I have told you the pain of my heart only as instruction."[^07-24]
Rising, the Prabhu embraced him with love.

**232.** He prostrated himself, and [the Prabhu] placed his feet on his head,
and then began to weep, holding his hand.

**233.** "I have given you my own land, Vrindavana, as a gift."
Lokanatha lost outward awareness and began to weep.

**234.** Who but the master and the servant can understand all this?
Who knows what the two of them experienced?

**235.** Gadadhara Pandita was there;
his disciple Bhugarbha made a request:

**236.** "Prabhu, command me too to go to Vrindavana;
I have had this wish for many days — be merciful."

**237.** Mahaprabhu said: "Gadai, give your leave:
let Lokanatha and Bhugarbha go together."

**238.** Gadadhara said: "Bhugarbha, go with him;
the two of you will go happily, delighting in talk about Krishna."

**239.** Bowing, they set out for Vrindavana,
and the devotees joyfully raised the cry of Hari.

**240.** When Lokanatha Gosani went to Vrindavana,
the Prabhu wept in distress.

**241.** Gadadhara weeps for his Bhugarbha;
why he sent him and why he weeps — who can understand?

**242.** The master and the servant know, but no one else knows
in what manner the two departed.

**243.** So [the Prabhu] joyfully disports himself in Navadvipa
together with Nityananda, Advaita and the other devotees.

**244.** Now hear attentively what I shall write,
for the words of the Prabhu's heart are of great import.

**245.** The two go along the road rejoicing;
in their feeling for Gaura, their hair standing on end, they fall to the ground.

**246.** Now they walk along rapt in talk of Krishna,
now they sing aloud of Gauranga's lila.

**247.** In humility, weeping, they say: "Prabhu, ocean of mercy,
show me even a drop of mercy, Prabhu!"

**248.** Step by step they arrived at Rajmahal,
and the two considered how to go on.

**249.** At that time, for fear of robbers, people did not travel;
[fearing that] the Prabhu's command would go unfulfilled, they lamented much.

**250.** The two deliberate long: which road should we take,
by which road shall we reach the sight of Vrindavana?

**251.** They ask people: "Brothers, which road should we take?"
And they reply: "You will not be able to get to Vrindavana."

**252.** The two consider: "Tell me, brother:
if we go by the Tajpur road, we shall fare well."

**253.** At dawn they set out, remembering their Prabhu,
and so they came to the village of Purnia.

**254.** Hope rose in their hearts, and they go by that road;
after some days they arrived at Ayodhya.

**255.** "Will such a day come that we reach Vrindavana
and see with our own eyes all its places and groves?

**256.** The Prabhu's command will be kept and our desire fulfilled" —
thinking of all this, they weep.

**257.** The two love each other as friends, each the other's companion —
that is why Gaura, the ocean of mercy, gave them this command.

**258.** Then in some days they went to Lucknow,
and on the third day they arrived at Agra.[^07-25]

**259.** The Yamuna flows there; they bathed and drank,
and, counting themselves blessed, went on their way.

**260.** At the end of the second day they reached Gokula;
seeing Krishna's birthplace, they were overwhelmed.

**261.** "O friend, what great fortune — we have seen the birthplace!
There is no friend as merciful as Gauranga."

**262.** Gauranga had instructed them about all the places;
the next day they entered Vrindavana.

**263.** I make my plea at the feet of the Vaishnava Gosanis:
hear a most wondrous account.

**264.** I wish to make known what else I have heard;
let it pain someone's heart — for me it is joy.

**265.** Gadadhara, dear to Gauranga, is his own shakti;
let no one admit disbelief in this.

**266.** I do not know — Gauranga himself knows;
what loss or gain there is in it, he knows.

**267.** Gadadhara descended in the form of Krishna's beloved;
only he knows it to whom his mercy is great.

**268.** Bhugarbha Mahashaya, whose name is Nandimukhi,
has great love for Lokanatha.

**269.** Manjulali and Nandimukhi love each other dearly;
Gauranga let them be together — know this for certain.

**270.** Chaitanya-chandra himself is the guru of the world,
merciful to living beings, a wish-fulfilling tree.

**271.** He is qualified for every rasa, he is the goal and what is attained;
that is why he is worshipped by all.

**272.** He took on the mood of a devotee and established dharma;
the Prabhu, the treasure of my life, knows his own devotees.

**273.** As many virtues as Gaura-raya has, so many has [his] devotee;
why then does he give instruction to such a devotee?

**274.** Speaking in dreams, he reveals everything to the devotees;
they can hold the Prabhu's virtues but cannot hold his power.

**275.** When Lokanatha Gosani wandered through Vrindavana,
the Prabhu's power was infused into him in the course of it.[^07-26]

**276.** Like a madman he roams about, looking at everything,
and seeing the places of the lila, at times rolls on the ground.

**277.** Seeing the beauty of Govardhana, he goes to the bank of the kundas;
seeing fields on the two kundas,[^07-27] he weeps aloud.

**278.** He saw with his own eyes barley and wheat sown there,
and recognized in his heart which lila had been at which place.

**279.** All the groves of the girl companions' kundas had become [fields];
now his body trembles, now he laughs.

**280.** The next day he went to Javata, Radhika's abode,
and, recognizing one thing after another, weeps over all the pastimes.

**281.** He recognized where each of the girl companions had lived,
and there he knew in his heart his own house.

**282.** All the sorrow that arose within him —
he stands as if transfixed and says nothing to anyone.

**283.** Then he went to Nanda's abode and saw all the places;
what tale of virtues there is, he alone knows.

**284.** Then he went to Sanketa and wandered through the groves,
now bowing, now crying "Alas, alas!"

**285.** Taking Bhugarbha by the hand, he says:
"Tell me, in which place was which lila?"

**286.** As they spoke, both lost consciousness in ecstasy
and, crying "Radha, Radha!", weep and fall to the ground.

**287.** That day they stayed in that place;
seeing them, the people of Vraja rejoiced.

**288.** Having considered, all took them for great siddhas
and brought them wonderful things to eat.

**289.** The next day, on the hill of Varshana,
the two view the places, their bodies filled with prema.

**290.** In the morning they go to bathe in the lake,
and, deep in thought, come to the bank of the kunda.

**291.** Having walked round again, they stay in that village;
the people of Vraja showed the two great love.

**292.** The next day in Vrindavana they go to the Kaliya-hrada;
he says to Bhugarbha: "Do you remember, brother?"

**293.** Recognizing the places, they go along the road,
wander through the settlement and come to Rasa-sthali.

**294.** Seeing it, they knew that Nidhuvana lay ahead,
and, heaving deep sighs, they weep and fall to the ground.

**295.** Going on, they reached the Chir-ghat
and saw the forest at that ghat, gazing at it.

**296.** "Where shall we settle?" — they can find no place;
looking everywhere, they wander through all the forests.

**297.** They saw that place, those trees and creepers,
and, settling there, they remained there.

**298.** "Gauranga, I shall not see your feet again;
I have stayed here holding only to your command.

**299.** The lila that the Prabhu will perform with his devotees —
of that he has deprived me and sent me here.

**300.** When shall I see Rupa and Sanatana with my own eyes?
Then I shall count my life blessed."

**301.** With cries of anguish he pleads at the Prabhu's feet:
"When will the Prabhu send Rupa and Sanatana?

**302.** Then my life will hold — there is no other way;
who knows my sorrow? To whom shall I tell it?

**303.** I have stayed, making your command my support;
fulfil my desire quickly."

**304.** Not far from each other the two perform sadhana;
day and night they perform the sadhana that is in their hearts.

**305.** All the people of Vraja, brahmanas and good folk,
seeing them, ponder in their minds:

**306.** "Hear of one more wonder:
two brahmacharis have come here.

**307.** Going to Dhira-samira we saw them,
and we understood in our hearts that they are not human.

**308.** Sacred thread on the shoulder — how handsome they are!
What brahmachari forms, like Madana himself!

**309.** All these days we never knew or saw the like;
are they gods or gandharvas who have descended?"

**310.** All the people of Vraja who go hoping to see them
meet with respect and kind words from them.

**311.** To all they say: "Follow the teaching about Krishna,"
and hearing it, all are filled with joy.

**312.** What a manner of bhajana is theirs, all see;
whoever sees them carries out their bidding.

**313.** People bring so many provisions from distant villages
that a hundred thousand could not eat them.

**314.** Utterly detached, they say nothing to anyone;
all the people of Vraja hold them dear as life.

**315.** Without seeing them for a moment, people cannot live;
whatever they command, people carry out.

**316.** Of all the days the two lived in Vrindavana —
how much can I write, setting it forth?

**317.** I write this account at the command of Sri Jahnava and Virachandra;
hearing all this, you will feel no pain.

**318.** When Srimati Thakurani[^07-28] went to Vrindavana,
I, a wretch, was with her and saw it.

**319.** Brother Ramachandra Dasa and many Vaishnavas
who were with the Thakurani saw it all.

**320.** [They saw too] how Ishvari herself, at Rupa Gosani's place,
met all the Gosanis as they came.

**321.** He whose hope is the feet of Sri Jahnava and Virachandra,
Nityananda Dasa, tells the Prema-vilasa.

*Thus ends the seventh vilasa of the Prema-vilasa.*

# Vilasa 8. The Prema Left for Narottama

*The glory of sankirtana; Mahaprabhu turns back from Kanai Natashala; in kirtana he calls Narottama; his talk with Nityananda about Garerhat; Mahaprabhu entrusts prema to the river Padmavati for the future Narottama*

**1.** Glory, glory to Sri Chaitanya, glory to the Merciful One!
Glory, glory to Nityananda, the shelter of devotees!

**2.** Glory, glory to Vishvambhara, the embodiment of compassion!
Glory, glory to Advaita-chandra, grace to the world!

**3.** Glory, glory to Virachandra, the ocean of prema!
Glory, glory to Gaura's devotees, crest-jewels of the connoisseurs of rasa!

**4.** Glory, glory to Gauranga with the host of his devotees!
Whoever hears the story of Chaitanya obtains bhakti.

**5.** Listen, listen, O hearers, attentively;
hear with faith a tale of prema.

**6.** The ornament of Gauda is the great sankirtana:
at the mere hearing prema arises — this I say firmly.

**7.** Hari-nama-sankirtana is this great power;
in the Kali age there is nothing else — all else is false.

**8.** From a single name of Hari comes every perfection;
by sankirtana prema is born in one's body.

**9.** In the body of one who has no attraction to Hari's name,
there is no prema — it flies off who knows where.

**10.** Whoever has the desire to attain Krishna,
let him take Hari's name with great joy.

**11.** Whatever one's attachment, let all take Hari's name;
taking the Name by count fulfils the heart's desire.

**12.** Now hear the account of Narottama's birth;
hearing it, you will find joy, and your heart will turn to kirtana.

**13.** The Prabhu was to go from Gauda to Vrindavana,
but he did not go to Vrindavana — he turned back from Kanai Natashala.

**14.** That story is told at length in earlier writings;[^08-1]
[here] I only describe the virtues of Narottama.

**15.** At the ghat of Tartipur[^08-2] he crossed the Padma,
and seeing the beauty of the Padmavati he rejoiced.

**16.** Clasping Nityananda round the neck, he sat down right there:
"I shall not go to Vrindavana; I shall stay here."

**17.** Hearing the Prabhu, Nityananda laughed:
"Leaving Navadvipa, you took sannyasa,

**18.** and now you have conceived the intention [to live] on the bank of the Padmavati!"
"Good, good," said the Sripada,[^08-3] and began to laugh.

**19.** The Prabhu said: "Sripada, pay attention:
where one sits happily, that is one's birthplace.

**20.** The reason for which I left Nilachala —
when you meet him, you will hear it all."[^08-4]

**21.** The Prabhu said: "That is the truth, this is no lie;
I shall explain in detail, listen if you wish.

**22.** When I am together with Sanatana and Rupa,
then all the Acharyas will hear all of this."

**23.** "Good, good," and the Prabhu rose quickly
and came to a village near Gauda,

**24.** named Chaturpura, not far [from there];
there was a meeting with Sanatana, full of prema.

**25.** Having accomplished the purpose of that meeting,
from there he went on and arrived at Natashala.

**26.** Hearing the village's name, "Krishna's Natashala,"
prema surged in his body, mistaking it for Vrindavana.

**27.** Nityananda said: "Prabhu, ever since you left the Padmavati,
you have had a mind to live on a river bank."[^08-5]

**28.** Listen, listen, O hearers, attentively:
the Prabhu's intentions cannot be understood at all.

**29.** One day Mahaprabhu, dancing in kirtana,
suddenly called out the name "Narottama!"

**30.** Leaning his whole body against Nityananda,
he wept, and hundreds of streams flowed from his eyes.

**31.** Seeing these transformations of prema, [Nityananda] reflected
and, seized with fear, stopped the kirtana.

**32.** Surrounding the Prabhu, all the kirtaniyas
sing Krishna's name in sweet voices.

**33.** "Sing, sing!" cried the Prabhu, and fell to the ground,
and Nityananda Prabhu could no longer hold him.

**34.** Crying "Mathura, Mathura!" he weeps;
hearing it, the devotees' hearts break.

**35.** At the name of Mathura he lost all sense of direction;
the village of Natashala quaked with prema.

**36.** The Prabhu weeps aloud, calling "Mathura!";
Nityananda sat down, holding the Prabhu round the neck.

**37.** Everyone cried out, a great tumult arose;
the housewives and others came to see.

**38.** Crying "Mathura, Mathura!" he rolls on the ground;
the Prabhu's golden body lies sprawled on the earth.

**39.** Seeing the Prabhu's beauty, the sweetness of prema,
men and women gaze at his form without blinking.

**40.** He roars "hum, hum," saying "Mathura!";
the Prabhu became intoxicated with prema like a mad baul.

**41.** "Where is Radha? Radha!" he says again and again,
"My body burns when I do not see you!

**42.** Where are Lalita and Vishakha, where is Champakalata?
Ah, show me — where is my friend, dear as life?

**43.** Show yourself and save my life — why do you give me sorrow?
I shall throw myself into the Yamuna; I cannot go on living.

**44.** Come quickly, friend Lalita, let us go to Madhupuri;
why can I no longer see the lord of my life?"

**45.** Seeing him distraught, the Prabhu[^08-6] sat holding him
and began to think: "What am I to do, what will happen?

**46.** Let us go — why did we come to the village of Natashala?
We have lost Gorachand!" he thinks to himself.

**47.** The Sripada found a means in sankirtana:
he loudly raised the cry "Jagannatha!"

**48.** At the name of Jagannatha the Prabhu regained consciousness
and for a while wandered here and there.

**49.** Calling "Narottama!", the Prabhu weeps unceasingly;
he looks in every direction, but does not see Narottama.

**50.** All say: "Let us take the Prabhu to Nilachala;
then all my good fortune will be complete.

**51.** If somehow we can turn the Prabhu's mind,
once we have crossed the Padmavati we shall have everything."

**52.** At that moment he again calls out "Narottama!"
[They thought:] "It seems some best of bhagavatas is to come."

**53.** Clasping the Sripada, the Prabhu embraced him,
and Nitai's body was drenched with tears.

**54.** With effort they calmed the Prabhu,
and, knowing the time had come, Nityananda too grew composed.

**55.** Nityananda said: "Prabhu, I beg of you:
there is great need to go to Jagannatha.

**56.** Krishna forbade [this road] through Sanatana's mouth:
he told of the crowds of people and the dangers of the way."

**57.** "Whatever you think best, do that;
whatever you do will be — I am not my own master."

**58.** The Prabhu said: "Sripada, listen attentively:
there is a reason why we came to Natashala."

**59.** "What business is there, Prabhu? Tell me, let me hear;
if it seems right to me, I shall take [you] — then I shall agree."

**60.** Nityananda said: "Prabhu, I beg of you:
in the sankirtana you remembered Narottama.

**61.** So shall I take you, or shall I not go with you?[^08-7]
I have no strength to hold you in the waves of feeling.

**62.** Seeing the pain of separation, I cannot bear to look;
I think: better that I should die this very moment."

**63.** The Prabhu said: "Garerhat is a most delightful place;
as soon as you see it, you will want to stay.

**64.** Listen, listen, Sripada, I shall explain in detail:
I wish to preserve here the sankirtana, the treasure of my life.

**65.** In Navadvipa the sankirtana was made manifest,
but I have left Gauda and live in Nilachala.

**66.** From now on I wish to preserve the sankirtana:
I shall lay up prema at Garerhat — so I tell you."

**67.** "How will you keep prema at Garerhat, Prabhu?
Who is the vessel to whom you will give such prema?"

**68.** The Prabhu said: "As long as you are present [in the world],
so long my prema will not disappear.

**69.** If this prema is kept with a worthy vessel, it remains;
if it falls on an unworthy one, prema is wasted.

**70.** The earth, drunk with prema, does not know proper place from improper;
give prema to such a person that all may drink of it.

**71.** So come, brother, let us go to Garerhat:
giving prema to such a person, [I] shall make the ghats and the roads weep."

**72.** "Good, good," said the Sripada, and fell silent,
and began to think how such a vessel would be born.

**73.** The Prabhu said: "Sripada, I see you are pondering;
you yourself do not know your own virtues.

**74.** All that you wept on the way to Nilachala —
that prema I have gathered and bound up day by day.

**75.** That prema I shall lay up on the bank of the Padmavati
and give it to a vessel named Narottama.

**76.** His birth will be through prema while I am still [in the world];
for now I shall leave it in the keeping of the Padmavati."

**77.** Nityananda said: "Prabhu, where is Garerhat?
Take me with you and go there."

**78.** "Both banks of the Padmavati are very cool,
and between them the Padmavati flows in a pure stream."

**79.** Hearing this, Nityananda's heart rejoiced:
"Make haste, Prabhu, and go there."

**80.** On the pretext of [the journey to] Vrindavana he came to Garerhat;
thus he turned back from Natashala.

**81.** Holding Nityananda's hand, laughing and laughing,
he began to speak, seeing the beauty of the Padmavati.

**82.** So he came to the village of Kudodarapura,[^08-8]
and, seeing the beauty of the bank, was full of joy.

**83.** There he stayed, in talk about Krishna,
and at dawn the Prabhu went to the Padmavati.

**84.** Having bathed, the Prabhu began kirtana on the bank;
with roars of "hum, hum," overflowing with prema, he shook violently.

**85.** How shall I behold that prema, what is its meaning?
A thousand people were not able to hold him.

**86.** At that moment he cried out "Narottama!"
The Sripada said: "[He is] stealing the play of prema.[^08-9]

**87.** Listen, listen, devotees, be on your guard:
at this moment, I surmise, he is going to take the prema."

**88.** At Nityananda's words the devotees were startled,
and Nityananda stopped the kirtana.

**89.** With the kirtaniyas the Prabhu began his bath,
and the Padmavati, drunk with prema, began to rise.

**90.** At the touch of the Prabhu's body the current stood still,
and all the water was filled with prema.

**91.** Rising and rising, the water flooded the village;
we understood that in this way he flooded [it] with prema.

**92.** Houses and homesteads floated away; there was an uproar in the village;
"This is not the rains," but no one could understand.

**93.** The Sripada said: "Prabhu, keep the prema properly;
the village is being laid waste — I have never seen the like."

**94.** The Prabhu said: "Padmavati, hold it, take the prema;
give this prema to a vessel named Narottama.

**95.** In Nityananda's presence I have placed prema with you;
keep it carefully and in secret."

**96.** The Padmavati said: "Prabhu, I beg of you:
how shall I know who is named Narottama?"

**97.** "At whose touch you overflow the more,
he is Narottama — give him the prema."

**98.** The Prabhu said: "All is as you have said;
keep the prema at this ghat — I command it."

**99.** The joyful Padmavati kept it by the bank:
in seclusion she hid the prema at a secluded ghat.

**100.** The Prabhu stood up to take leave of the Padmavati,
and with Nityananda gazed in that direction.

**101.** The current moved on, flooding [the nearby] villages,[^08-10]
then let go of the water, and the people rejoiced.

**102.** The Sripada said: "Prabhu, seeing such beauty,
I too have been tempted to stay here.

**103.** You will cause Narottama to be born and give him prema;
I think you will [abide] near Narottama."[^08-11]

**104.** The Prabhu said: "Sripada, it is as you have said:
near Narottama alone do I abide."

**105.** Then the Prabhu crossed the Padmavati,
and, going on step by step, the Prabhu came to Nilachala.

**106.** All say: "What sort of thing is this prema?
Who would call dancing and singing prema?

**107.** Weeping and falling down too we do not call prema;
who can describe it, who has the capacity?"

**108.** In the form of prema is Krishna's very self;
this Sri Rupa himself has explained.

**109.** I write only a particle of it, that it may be known;
by the strength of the Prabhu's command I write this with my hand.

**110.** […][^08-12] —
and how many more tales there are of her virtues!

**111.** Thus I have told the sweetness of Garerhat;
with great longing I shall tell of the prema of kirtana.

**112.** The Vaishnava who hears of this prema with faith
will soon obtain this treasure of prema.[^08-13]

**113.** He whose hope is the feet of Sri Jahnava and Virachandra,
Nityananda Dasa, tells the Prema-vilasa.

*Thus ends the eighth vilasa of the Prema-vilasa.*
