# Prema-vilasa

*"The Pastimes of Love"*

*A poem by Nityananda Dasa*

*Translated from the Bengali*

---

## Translator's note

The *Prema-vilasa* is a Bengali poem by Nityananda Dasa (Balarama Dasa), a disciple of Jahnava Devi,
on the lives of Srinivasa Acharya, Narottama Thakura and Shyamananda: their training in Vrindavana under
Jiva Gosvami, the bringing of the Gosvamis' books to Bengal, and their preaching in Gauda, Orissa and
Vishnupura.

**Source.** The edition of 1913 (Calcutta, Bagbazar; public domain), collated from two independent OCR
copies (the 1913 edition and its 1999 reprint). **Only the poem itself** is translated: the editors'
prefaces and notes are not. Vilasas 1–23 are translated; the twenty-fourth vilasa (a late genealogical
addition) and the appendix are not included.

**Principles.** The payar couplets are translated line by line, without rhyme, and numbered within each
vilasa (the original has no numbering); tripadi stanzas, songs and Sanskrit verses are numbered as single
units. Corrupt passages are noted in the translator's notes at the end of each vilasa. Names follow
`GLOSSARY.md`.

# Vilasa 1. The Description of Srinivasa Acharya's Birth

*Mahaprabhu's distress that Gauda has lost bhakti; counsel with Sarvabhauma; Jagannatha's command to grant prema to the son of the childless brahmana Chaitanya Dasa; the Earth gives prema to Lakshmipriya; Srinivasa's birth at Chakhandi*

**1.** *I did not worship your lotus feet in the Kali age,*
*I did not see your golden form in the Kali age,*
*I did not hear the tales of your truth in the Kali age —*
*O Chaitanya-chandra, you have left me deprived!*[^01-1]

**2.** Glory, glory to Sri Chaitanya, glory to Nityananda!
Glory to Advaita-chandra, glory to the host of Gaura's devotees!

**3.** Glory, glory to Sri Jahnava, glory to Virachandra!
Glory, glory to the mantra of Hari-nama in the Kali age![^01-2]

**4.** Glory, glory to Srinivasa Acharya Thakura,
whose disciple Ramachandra is the sprout of prema!

**5.** Glory, glory to Kaviraja Thakura Govinda,
by whose virtues the beings of the seven islands rejoice!

**6.** Glory, glory to the listeners — give your attention,
you whose very life the pastimes of Radha and Krishna will become.

**7.** How the birth of Acharya Thakura came about —
listen, brothers, with devotion and a steady heart.

**8.** Mahaprabhu sent Nityananda Prabhu to Gauda,
and he flooded Gauda with prema-bhakti.

**9.** All the Vaishnavas who came from Gauda
Mahaprabhu questioned in every detail.

**10.** Some said: "In Gauda there is no Hari-nama;
there is no deliverance for good people or for bad."

**11.** Some said: "Acharya Gosani has left bhakti
and everywhere makes people accept mukti as the chief thing."

**12.** Some said: "He has no word but mukti;
speaking of mukti, Gosani has flooded the world."

**13.** As he listened, the Lord's anger arose,
and the grief of separation from Nityananda grew in him.

**14.** At this time Svarupa and Ramananda Raya came to the Lord;
they wished to speak, and there was joy in their hearts.

**15.** "Come in, come in, it is good that you two have come!
Gauda has become empty of bhakti — hear the reason.

**16.** Advaita Acharya, the very form of Ishvara,
has left bhakti and expounds the five kinds of mukti.[^01-3]

**17.** I cannot understand Advaita's mind.
Tell me, both of you, how bhakti is to be kept.

**18.** I cannot believe that he teaches mukti,
for he is a chief participant in these pastimes.[^01-4]

**19.** I hear it from people's mouths, but I am not convinced:
to expound mukti, leaving bhakti, is not his intent."

**20.** At this time a letter came from Nityananda:
"Leaving the path of bhakti, the Acharya preaches mukti."

**21.** On receiving the letter the Lord was greatly alarmed;
holding the letter in his own hand he went for darshan.

**22.** With his devotees the Lord went into the temple
and beside Garuda[^01-5] beheld the Lord with joy in his heart.

**23.** At that time Sarvabhauma Bhattacharya came;
seeing him, the Lord was overcome with ecstasy.

**24.** Crying "Bhakti, bhakti!" the Lord was filled with prema
and, rejecting the explanations of mukti, began to extol bhakti.

**25.** Embracing the Bhattacharya, he came out
and, reaching Mishra's residence,[^01-6] grew somewhat calmer.

**26.** Nityananda Prabhu's letter was in his hand.
"Read the letter, Bhattacharya," the Lord commanded.

**27.** Reading the letter, the Bhattacharya flew into a great rage:
"It seems no one in Gauda has any understanding!

**28.** Who is this who rejects bhakti and preaches mukti?
The three of us will go to that place.

**29.** In debate, Prabhu, I will silence him,
and if he is obstinate I will bind him and bring him here."[^01-7]

**30.** At the Bhattacharya's words the Lord's heart rejoiced:
"There will be no hindrance to bhakti — listen, Mahashaya.

**31.** One letter under my own signature will go to Advaita,
and another he will write to Sri Nityananda Prabhu."

**32.** Saying "Good, good," they settled this plan firmly
and sent the letters to Gauda by the hand of Vaishnavas.

**33.** Hearing this, the high-minded Bhattacharya
joined his palms and told of his own former plight:

**34.** "Studying logic, I knew not a speck of bhakti;
now, leaving the explanations of mukti, I find a special joy in bhakti.

**35.** How long I spent chewing the dry oil-cake of logic!
Then I came to talk with Gopinatha Acharya.

**36.** He is not ill-minded nor under maya — he is the Lord's devotee;
how should he not know the truth of the Lord's own nature?

**37.** Through my bond with him the Lord showed me mercy:
all my wrong thinking went, and bhakti was born in my heart.

**38.** He is very dear to the Lord, a king among devotees;
his work is to bring the world to understanding."

**39.** All went off to their own places,
yet joy did not arise in the Lord's mind.

**40.** The more he thought, the more his anxiety grew:
"Living beings have become empty of bhakti!" — and fear arose in him.

**41.** "How will bhakti remain upon the earth?
I want to send some prema and the Name to Gauda.

**42.** While Nityananda is here, how is this to be done?
And when he is gone, how will bhakti remain among the beings?

**43.** To reveal the scriptures of bhakti, Rupa and Sanatana,
the two brothers, went to Vrindavana.

**44.** That bhakti I want to reveal in Gauda;
I want a vessel to be born who is prema in form."

**45.** "Avani! Avani!"[^01-8] the Lord called out,
and the Earth with joined palms came before the Lord.

**46.** "Listen, listen, Earth, and be attentive:
bring a vessel who is prema in form and give him a dwelling."

**47.** "The prema that you, my Lord, have left in my keeping —
give the command, and I will make it manifest in a form."

**48.** Delighted, the Lord gave the Earth his command,[^01-9]
but of who was worthy to be the vessel, and of his name, nothing was said.[^01-10]

**49.** At this time Svarupa and Ramananda Raya came to the Lord;
bowing to the Lord, they wished to speak to him.

**50.** "What am I to do, what will happen? It is good that you have come" —
and he told them all that had passed with the Earth.

**51.** Crying "Prema, prema!" the Lord was overcome
and, calling "Nityananda!", began to weep.

**52.** The Lord fell senseless; the third watch[^01-11] went by;
in a sweet voice Svarupa sang Hari-nama to him.

**53.** On hearing Hari-nama the Lord came to himself:
"Come, Svarupa, let us go and see the Lord."

**54.** At this time Sarvabhauma appeared before the Lord;
seeing Sarvabhauma, the Lord was very glad.

**55.** "It is good that you have come; sit here.
There is a special matter — listen carefully.

**56.** Advaita Acharya has put away the path of bhakti.
What am I to say, what am I to do — tell me, Bhattacharya."

**57.** Hearing of this hindrance to bhakti, the Bhatta was much grieved,
and then he spoke at Mahaprabhu's feet:

**58.** "Advaita Acharya is the lord of the world;
such words will never come from his mouth.

**59.** Some insolent people[^01-12] came and told this to the Lord.
Bring those people before me!"

**60.** They searched, but those people could not be found,
and the Bhatta declared them to be great ignoramuses.[^01-13]

**61.** "Listen, listen, Bhattacharya, I will tell you of the past:
ever since I left Navadvipa, he has been in great sorrow.

**62.** I cannot tell what is in his mind in that sorrow:
to frighten me he acts as he pleases.

**63.** He has the power to do anything:
what he does comes to pass and is never in vain.

**64.** I have faith in his word,
and I cannot go against his command.

**65.** Devise a plan, then, so that his command is not slighted
and that, by this plan, prema is preserved hereafter."

**66.** The Bhattacharya said: "Prabhu, while Nityananda is here,
while he is present, prema will meet no obstacle.

**67.** What will happen when he is gone — what can I say?
Do whatever is in your mind.

**68.** Of this, Prabhu, my own Mayavada is witness:
leaving mukti, I expound bhakti by your grace.

**69.** By seeing you, Prabhu, my bondage was destroyed,
and leaving mukti I became a servant on the path of bhakti.

**70.** The people of the Kali age are very wicked,
and the chief cause of it is the rule of the yavana kings.[^01-14]

**71.** All that the kings' rule had brought, he did away with,
and he revealed the great medicine, the mantra of Hari-nama.

**72.** By the mere shadow of the Name he destroyed sins;
Nityananda, your own portion, established bhakti.

**73.** Such a Nityananda Prabhu you sent to Gauda —
why then do you begin to worry about the future?"

**74.** "All that is true; but listen with attention:
I sent Nityananda off together with the devotees,

**75.** I myself parted from Nityananda.
What am I to do — give me your counsel."

**76.** "No one can counsel you better
than One — in Nilachala there is Jagannatha Raya."

**77.** "The Bhatta Mahashaya has given a fine solution:
without Jagannatha this cannot be settled."

**78.** Having so resolved, they all went for darshan
and prayed that prema might be preserved hereafter:

**79.** "You are an ocean of compassion, most merciful.
We pray to you, Lord — be sure to answer.

**80.** In the Kali age you descended in the form of Jagannatha;
seeing you and believing, people have been delivered.

**81.** With the sweetness and fragrance of your prasada you flooded the land,
making the varnas and ashramas and even the chandalas one.

**82.** In this way let the pastimes of Radha and Krishna spread,
and in granting favour or punishment let no one weigh who is worthy."[^01-15]

**83.** The fourteen-cubit swinging garland[^01-16] broke from the Lord's neck;
the pujari brought it and held it out before the Lord.

**84.** Delighted, the Lord came back to his lodging,
and his heart was filled with joy beyond measure.

**85.** Free from worry, he lay down to rest,
and Jagannatha came to his bedside.

**86.** Smiling, Jagannatha said to him:
"Is my power so very much greater than yours?

**87.** For a long time now there has been a brahmana;
childless, he came to pray for a son.

**88.** Whenever he came for darshan he begged for the boon of a son,
always weeping, with an anguished heart.

**89.** Seeing the brahmana so distressed, I was moved to great compassion
and, being pleased, granted him the boon of a son.

**90.** His name is Chaitanya Dasa Acharya;
from him will come that most worthy vessel, the embodiment of prema.[^01-17]

**91.** You shall entrust the prema to him,
so that the brahmana grieves no more.

**92.** Lakshmipriya is his wife, the daughter of Balarama[^01-18] —
virtuous, devoted to her husband, most blessed."

**93.** At that moment Mahaprabhu awoke,
and calling "Jagannatha!" he wept a long while.

**94.** Calling Kashi Mishra, the Lord asked him:
"Show me the Gaudiya Chaitanya Dasa.

**95.** Jagannatha has given me a command concerning him;
take pains and bring him quickly."

**96.** Mishra said: "Prabhu, many days have passed;
weeping, the brahmana went back to his own country."

**97.** The Lord said: "Do you know where his home is?"
Mishra said: "I will find it out for certain."

**98.** At this time Jagadananda came from Vrindavana,
and the Lord began to ask him about Sanatana's welfare.

**99.** He said: "All are in perfect fulfilment and bliss,"
and told the Lord how each one was.

**100.** "After seeing Mother's feet I came to Navadvipa,
and then I came to Shantipur, to the Acharya.

**101.** At my leave-taking Gosani gave me a command:
'What I say, repeat it to Him.'[^01-19]

**102.** And he spoke a riddle."[^01-20] Hearing this, Mahaprabhu said:
"Words such as he spoke I have never heard."

**103.** Svarupa and others were there together with Mahaprabhu;
hearing the riddle, they all began to laugh.

**104.** From that day the Lord's condition changed:
the malady of separation from Krishna grew twofold.

**105.** "What was said has come to pass[^01-21] — you two have heard it.
Consider how prema may be preserved.

**106.** Jagannatha has commanded me to see the brahmana;
take pains to bring him — I wish to send him to his country."

**107.** Meanwhile the Earth could not bear the weight of prema,
and an earthquake struck all of Nilachala-puri.

**108.** Day and night Nilachala shook.
"This is no earthquake, brothers — it is Chaitanya doing all this!

**109.** Earlier Chaitanya gave prema to the ocean
and flooded Nilachala-puri with prema.

**110.** The ocean, it seems, could not hold that prema;
taking the prema from it, he gave it to the Earth.

**111.** The Earth cannot hold it and is shaking;
houses may collapse and people may die!

**112.** All this time we have lived in Nilachala, brothers —
and now Chaitanya-chandra has come and does such things!"

**113.** They all took counsel together on what should be done:
"Let us go to a land where everyone's life will be safe."

**114.** One says: "There is no land apart from the earth;
come what may, I shall stay right here."

**115.** One says: "You have no sons or children,
and besides, you are poor and own no property —

**116.** for fear of what would you leave Nilachala?
Either way we shall all die.

**117.** If Jagannatha protects us in this calamity,
then easily, brothers, we shall remain in the world."

**118.** One says: "Brother, what will Jagannatha do?
Chaitanya's rasa, brother, will only grow twofold!"

**119.** One says: "Let us all gather together,
go to Sri Krishna Chaitanya and lay it before him."

**120.** Saying "Good, good," they all gathered together
and arrived at Mishra's door.

**121.** The crowd made a great uproar at the door,
and the Lord came out with Svarupa and the others.

**122.** Seeing the Lord, the distressed people of Nilachala —
children, old men, young men, householders and ascetics — cried out:

**123.** "When the city was floating in the water, you saved it;
now the city is shaking — a calamity has come.

**124.** This time save us, Lord Gaurachandra!
With what mantra have you made the earth unsteady?

**125.** There is none but you to save us in calamity;
in fear we have come to plead with you.

**126.** You are the purifier of the fallen, most merciful;
do not forsake us who have taken refuge in Jagannatha."[^01-22]

**127.** At this time the pujari, bringing Jagannatha's prasada,
came to where the Lord was.

**128.** Seeing the prasada, Mahaprabhu rose,
bowed down and placed the prasada beside him.

**129.** The pujari said: "Prabhu, I cannot perform the service:
whenever I lay my hands on Jagannatha, my whole body spins.[^01-23]

**130.** What am I to do? Save us, Prabhu — the service has stopped!
In fear I have come to tell you."

**131.** Then the Lord called the Earth:
"For some days stay still."

**132.** Hearing what people were saying, the pujari was afraid:
"It is Advaita Mahashaya who has landed us in this calamity!"

**133.** With joined palms the pujari addressed the Lord.
"Do your service," said the Lord, and he laid his hands on Jagannatha's body.

**134.** Dismissing the pujari, the Lord said before the people:
"Go, go, brothers, all of you go home in peace.

**135.** There will be no earthquake; I will pray to Jagannatha,
and I will beg it of the Earth as alms."

**136.** Humbly dismissing all the people,
he began to think about the brahmana Chaitanya Dasa.

**137.** Thinking so, he called the Earth to mind;
the Earth came and bowed to the Lord.

**138.** "What is your command, Prabhu?" the Earth asked.
The Lord asked where Chaitanya Dasa lived.

**139.** The Earth said: "Prabhu, many bear that name;
how shall I know for certain?"

**140.** The Lord said: "For the sake of a son, before Jagannatha,
for a whole year he prayed with body and mind.

**141.** That Chaitanya Dasa you must seek out;
the father of his wife Lakshmipriya is Balarama Dasa."[^01-24]

**142.** "As you command," said the Earth and departed,
and on the third day she came and reported to the Lord:

**143.** "He lives at Chakhandi, very pure in conduct;
in his body there is no trace of sin.

**144.** For the sake of a son he began a purashcharana,[^01-25]
and soon he left, entrusting it to Jagannatha."

**145.** The Lord said: "Earth, you have been a great help:
Jagannatha has kept his word about prema firm.

**146.** Listen, listen, Earth, listen with care:
take the prema and give it to Lakshmipriya.

**147.** Give her all the prema, keep nothing back.
My word is true — you must carry it out."[^01-26]

**148.** Overjoyed, the Earth began to dance,
brought the prema and laid it before Lakshmipriya.

**149.** And here the Lord, free from worry, began kirtana
and started to dance in the courtyard of Jagannatha's temple.

**150.** Before Jagannatha, with joined palms, the Lord
wept aloud, crying "Srinivasa! Srinivasa!"

**151.** Seeing this, Jagannatha laughed with delight
and sent prema to Chaitanya Dasa.

**152.** Seeing Jagannatha laugh, the Lord laughed too,
and by his command sent prema to Chaitanya Dasa.

**153.** "From this a son named Srinivasa will be born,
and through him there will be a great play of prema.[^01-27]

**154.** To reveal the various scriptures I sent Rupa and Sanatana,
the two brothers, to Sri Vrindavana.

**155.** In Rupa's scriptures the pastimes of Radha and Krishna will be revealed,
and by command they will be entrusted to Srinivasa."

**156.** Having prayed to Jagannatha, he came back to his lodging
and joyfully summoned Kashi Mishra.

**157.** He took secret counsel with Svarupa and Ramananda;
having received Jagannatha's command, his mind was at ease.

**158.** "Tell us, tell us, Prabhu, give us the news!" —
"In Chaitanya Dasa's house prema will be made known.

**159.** In Gauda Nityananda Raya is anxious;
send him a letter and set his mind at rest."

**160.** "Good, good," and the Lord wrote in his own hand:
"Hari-nama sankirtana will be in every house.

**161.** You shall send a letter to Advaita Acharya,
that he should count the state of mukti without bhakti as worthless.

**162.** Do not worry about me hereafter;
the world will float in Hari-nama sankirtana.

**163.** By Jagannatha's command a son will be born by a boon,
who will flood the world with the pastimes of Radha and Krishna.

**164.** Near the bank of the Ganga is a place called Chakhandi;
there lives a worthy brahmana named Chaitanya Dasa.

**165.** To him a son, Srinivasa, will be born, prema in form;
he will appear in the form of a Vaishnava."

**166.** Having written the letter thus, he sent it to Gauda,
and having made prema manifest he remained at peace.

**167.** At this time a letter came from Sanatana;
he wrote in full about the arrival of Gopala Bhatta.

**168.** Hearing of Gopala's arrival in Vrindavana,
the Lord was overjoyed together with his devotees.

**169.** "Listen, listen, Svarupa and Ramananda, to the news:
Gopala Bhatta has come to Vrindavana."

**170.** The Lord spoke much of the Bhatta's greatness,
and all rejoiced to hear it from the Lord's mouth.

**171.** The Lord said: "Consider now what I should do:
what thing of unprecedented sweetness shall I send?

**172.** I am a poor sannyasi; I have no wealth;
all I have is my cord[^01-28] and a seat to sit on.

**173.** In them is my power — hear the reason;
these two things I will give to the Bhatta,

**174.** so that he may sit on it in Rupa's company.
I will have Svarupa write the letter."

**175.** But to Sanatana the Lord himself wrote in his own hand:
"Let Rupa take up the describing of the pastimes in scriptures.

**176.** This letter of mine, read it out to Rupa;
hearing it, your hearts will rejoice.

**177.** In Gauda I will cause a jewel of a vessel to be born;
when his body is grown and fit, I will send him to you later.

**178.** His name is Srinivasa, born in a brahmana family;
he will make known in Gauda the very heart of Radha and Krishna's pastimes.

**179.** When I am no longer present, he will go to Vrindavana;
you shall entrust your books to him.

**180.** To Gauda I have sent Nityananda,
and with him Ramadasa, Gadadhara and Sundarananda.

**181.** For a son Chaitanya Dasa stayed in Nilachala;
Jagannatha gave prema, and he accepted it.

**182.** I too am coming soon to see you all;
make a secluded place for me in some grove.

**183.** Only Svarupa and Ramananda Raya are with me;
it is by the kindness of these two that my life is kept.

**184.** I am sending you the seat by a Vaishnava's hand,
and through Ramananda I have given him money for the road."

**185.** Taking the cord and the seat, the Vaishnava went to Vrindavana.
That day Rupa and Sanatana were together.

**186.** On receiving the letter the two brothers rejoiced,
and seeing the cord and the seat they swooned in prema.

**187.** Placing the cord round their necks, they wept a long while
and fell to the ground, crying "Gaurahari!"

**188.** "Shall we ever again see, Lord, the face of Gora-chand?
Not hearing your sweet words, our breasts are breaking!"

**189.** Rolling on the ground, they wept, clasping the seat to their breasts,
and perceived the sweet fragrance of his holy body.

**190.** At that moment Bhugarbha and Lokanatha arrived
and fell to the ground, a hand pressed to the breast.

**191.** In passing I will write something — listen, hearers —
a mere particle about Lokanatha's renunciation.

**192.** With no companion at all, he sits in seclusion;
with eyes closed, now he weeps and now he laughs.

**193.** Lokanatha Gosani is most deeply dear to the Lord;
Rupa and Sanatana honour him constantly.

**194.** Such will be his disciple Narottama;
he will make prema manifest on earth.

**195.** Narottama by name, a resident of Garerhat,[^01-29]
the son of Krishnananda Raya, a treasury of prema.

**196.** As Rupa and Sanatana are one body,
so, know for certain, are Narottama and Srinivasa.

**197.** Narottama, whose name Gauranga himself gave —
what shall I say of his virtues? They are all beyond compare.

**198.** That same power, those same pastimes he spread abroad;
who can be compared with such a chosen one?

**199.** The virtues of the two Mahashayas cannot be written —
of those who made prema manifest in Gauda.

**200.** The virtues of the two Mahashayas that are recorded
I shall write later, following in their wake.

**201.** Now I shall write of the pain of separation that befell;
seeing it, how could a poor, wretched person keep alive?

**202.** Seeing Sanatana's state, Rupa was stunned:
"If you become like this, everyone will die!

**203.** You are the Lord's second body, Mahashaya;
seeing you so distraught, who can keep his senses?"

**204.** With many efforts Rupa brought him back to consciousness,
but the dreadful trembling of separation grew twofold.

**205.** From that day Sanatana had no rest:
the malady of separation from Gauranga grew twofold.

**206.** Seeing Sanatana, Rupa grew anxious
lest Govinda leave this Vrindavana empty.[^01-30]

**207.** Regaining his composure, Rupa took the seat
and went reverently to the Bhatta.

**208.** The two brothers, carefully holding the two things to their breasts,
went in great happiness to the Bhatta's lodging.

**209.** Prostrating themselves, they gave him the seat and the cord,
and reading the letter they made him hear the sweetness of prema.

**210.** Hearing the honour the letter did him, the Bhatta swooned,
and then, clasping the seat to his breast, he began to weep.

**211.** With care Sri Rupa steadied him somewhat,
and on seeing Sanatana the Bhatta grew calm.

**212.** Sanatana said: "Listen, Bhatta Gosani:
at the time of discourse sit on this seat — there is no fault in it."

**213.** "How can I sit on the Lord's seat?" —
"The Lord has commanded it — how can you disregard it?"

**214.** "The Lord's command is mightiest," said Sri Rupa;
and placing the cord round his neck, the Bhatta sat on the seat.

**215.** All their hearts were filled with joy,
and they went off each to his own grove.

**216.** That night, as Sanatana slept, in the guise of a dream
Gaurangachandra, embracing his neck, said:

**217.** "There is a brahmana youth named Srinivasa,
endowed with the greatest steadiness and other virtues.

**218.** You are my second body, Sanatana;
through Srinivasa you shall accomplish my purpose."

**219.** Having seen the dream, Sanatana rejoiced,
and in the morning, sitting in the assembly, he began to tell it.

**220.** Sanatana said: "Listen to a wondrous account:
the Lord is going to depart, and there is a reason for it.

**221.** The pastimes of Radha and Krishna, through sankirtana,
the Lord savours together with Svarupa and the others.

**222.** The pastimes that he will describe by infusing his power into Rupa
he will make known by sending a vessel.

**223.** There is a brahmana youth named Srinivasa;
through him the Lord will spread these pastimes in Gauda.

**224.** As prema in form he will cause him to be born in Gauda,
and Srinivasa will come here when the Lord's pastimes are ended.

**225.** You, staying in Vrindavana, will see him;
whether I am alive or not, he will be seen.

**226.** To see one chosen by Chaitanya's mercy is a matter of fortune,
and I suppose that such fortune is not mine.

**227.** If Chaitanya's mercy rests on everyone,
then in due time you will all see him in your midst."

**228.** The Bhatta said: "The Lord will send such a treasure —
if fortune allows, I shall see him with my own eyes."

**229.** Rupa said: "I have laboured by the Lord's power;
my labour will bear fruit if he comes soon.

**230.** While I live I will entrust everything to him,
and teaching him all the books, I will make him a pandit."

**231.** Thus all rejoiced together,
knowing that he would deliver the poor, the wretched and the wicked.

**232.** From then on Gopala Bhatta made it his rule
to keep all his observances with the cord tied round his neck.

**233.** One day in the assembly he raised the matter
of what he had seen of Srinivasa in a dream that night:

**234.** "In Chaitanya Dasa's house, in Lakshmipriya's womb...
As soon as he is born he will proclaim the name of Radha and Krishna,

**235.** and joyfully he will deliver all, down to the chandalas."
It turned out that they had all seen this in a dream.

**236.** All at once it dawned on them all:
they had seen a joyful dream and understood its reason.

**237.** Yet all were anxious about the Lord:
"We cannot understand what he intends by this."

**238.** So Sanatana and Rupa remained anxious:
when would Srinivasa come, the embodiment of prema?

**239.** And in Nilachala Svarupa's mind was uneasy:
night and day he saw inauspicious dreams.

**240.** One day Svarupa found the Lord alone
and asked him: "Who is this Srinivasa?

**241.** Tell me his virtues in detail, Prabhu;
merely to hear of his virtues gladdens the heart."

**242.** On hearing the name, Svarupa's joy grew,[^01-31]
and he wrote a letter to Sanatana once more.

**243.** A wondrous letter he wrote to Sanatana:
"Reading it, he will understand the whole matter."

**244.** Meanwhile, in his own house, the brahmana Chaitanya Dasa
was performing purashcharana for the sake of a son.

**245.** Seven purashcharanas he performed near the Ganga,
and in a dream a command came to him from One of golden form:

**246.** "A wondrous son named Srinivasa will be born,
and through him prema will be made manifest."

**247.** To Lakshmipriya the command was given with a hand laid on her head:
"A wondrous son will be born — remain joyful."

**248.** At the mere touch of the Lord's hand she became mad with prema;
coming to herself, Lakshmipriya began to weep.

**249.** Tears, trembling, hair standing on end — and she lost all composure:
Lakshmipriya's body was filled with prema.

**250.** Lakshmipriya said: "Acharya, pay heed:
see, a great being dwells within my body.

**251.** I laugh, I weep, I dance, I sing — such is my state;
all sorrow is gone, I tell you.

**252.** Do not go anywhere and leave me;
perform nama-sankirtana at home day and night."

**253.** The Acharya said: "How shall we get by?[^01-32]
There is no wealth in the house — what shall we eat?"

**254.** Lakshmipriya said: "I have gained great wealth:
your poverty is at an end, your life is fulfilled."

**255.** The village had suffered oppression from the ruler and many troubles;
now it had all calmed down, and the ruler had become well-disposed.

**256.** The zamindar had left the village and was living in another,
but now that the trouble was over he was to return to his own place.

**257.** As soon as he[^01-33] entered the womb, hearts were filled with prema and joy,
and all fear of the yavanas went away without effort.

**258.** Until then people had kept saying "Durga, Shiva";
now no one uttered any name but Krishna's.

**259.** There was an old brahmana there of wicked ways;
he whispered slander into the zamindar's ear:

**260.** "The village will be ruined, brother, by this name;
forbid the people!" — so he came and said.

**261.** "They have left Shiva and Durga and keep saying...[^01-34]
I will plunder their homes — by what strength will they keep them?"

**262.** Calling the kotal[^01-35] and a drummer, Durgadas said:
"Let them say 'Shiva, Durga!' — or else it is ruin for all!"

**263.** The drummer struck his drum for the first time —
and the drum began to sound "Radha-Krishna"!

**264.** Children dance in prema, saying "Radha-Krishna";
women dance, with longing in their hearts.

**265.** At the sound of the drum all the people became intoxicated
and, saying "Radha-Krishna," began to dance.

**266.** The drummer, possessed by prema, dances, weeps and laughs;
the boys dance and tumble down.

**267.** At the sound of the drum all the people were intoxicated
and danced merrily along with the children.

**268.** Dancing, they came to Chaitanya Dasa's house;
seeing the zamindar, the brahmana grew anxious at heart.

**269.** With honour he seated him on a seat,
and in his flurry knew not what to do or say.

**270.** Courteously he dismissed the people[^01-36]
and with honour had Durgadas bathe.

**271.** A great deal of food came together,
and the Acharya brought it and set it before Durgadas.

**272.** Seeing the provisions, Durgadas was pleased,
and seeing such things in a poor man's house he smiled gently.

**273.** Having eaten, the Raya stayed in the Acharya's house
and lay down to rest with joy in his heart.

**274.** In the night came the sound of khol and kartals;
heavy with sleep, at the mere hearing of the sound

**275.** he awoke — but could hear nothing more;
and the Raya fell senseless where he was.

**276.** Lakshmipriya says: "Acharya, attend:
two golden children are dancing in sankirtana!"

**277.** The two golden children together,
laughing and laughing, laid their feet upon her head.[^01-37]

**278.** And the command came: "For ten months take care of yourself;
once again I shall dance in your courtyard."[^01-38]

**279.** Durgadas, sitting up on his bed, beheld it;
he heard every word — he saw it in a dream.

**280.** Intoxicated with prema, the Raya cried out and wept,
fell to the ground and could not be calmed.

**281.** In haste the Acharya lifted him and took him in his arms:
"Be steady, Raya," he said to him gently.

**282.** "I do not know what sound I heard ringing in my ears;
when I came to, I do not know where it had gone."

**283.** The Acharya said: "In a dream I saw what those two were like;
my sleep broke — where has such a treasure gone?"

**284.** The Raya said: "It was no dream — why do you dissemble?
Be kind and tell me; it will make me very happy."

**285.** The Acharya said: "Raya, you are a very sober man;
why should seeing a dream unsettle you so?"

**286.** The Raya said: "It was no dream; I saw it with my own eyes:
I found the Creator, and he left me bereft."

**287.** The Raya said: "Acharya, I will confess to you:
I have forgotten my own chosen deity, and I do not know why.

**288.** Having seen the dream I have forgotten my chosen deity:
the name of Radha and Krishna has entered my body.

**289.** To abandon one's chosen deity is death, as the shastras attest;
I have heard this word from the shastras; it cannot be otherwise."

**290.** The Acharya said: "Raya, you are very learned;
consider, and do whatever your mind accepts."

**291.** The Raya said: "I have heard people say
that in Navadvipa the Creator was born in the form of Gaura.

**292.** That same Creator has entered my heart,
and entering in the night, he made me forget my own deity.

**293.** That same Creator danced in your courtyard:
I saw two golden ones in my dream.

**294.** What am I to do? Give me your counsel."
The Acharya said: "You are the ruler, and I am under your protection."

**295.** The Raya said: "I have told you everything as it was;
it is in my mind to take the mantra of Radha and Krishna."

**296.** So saying, the Raya went to his residence,
and set his mind on taking a guru in a worthy place.

**297.** Finding a worthy place, the Raya received instruction.
Merely from his entering the womb, such was the fruit!

**298.** To such a Srinivasa's feet I bow:
from the womb he flooded the world with the name of Radha and Krishna.[^01-39]

**299.** In Navadvipa the Lord could not make every being take the Name,
but Srinivasa from the womb made them take it in Chakhandi.[^01-40]

**300.** Even pashandis openly take the name of Krishna —
so great a dawning of the Lord through Srinivasa!

**301.** There has been no such avatara, brothers, in any yuga;
yet we, not honouring the two brothers, feed upon poison.

**302.** Worship, worship, O brothers, Chaitanya and Nitai:
such a merciful Thakura I have never seen.

**303.** Meanwhile Lakshmipriya and the Acharya rejoiced:
the bodies of both were filled with prema.

**304.** Whoever got anything anywhere brought it and gave it;
poverty vanished, and everyone was glad.

**305.** When ten months and ten days were complete,
at an auspicious moment the boy was born.

**306.** On the full moon of Vaishakha,[^01-41] on an auspicious day at an auspicious moment,
Lakshmipriya beheld her son's face.

**307.** The Acharya entered the room,
and seeing his son's face he was filled with great joy.

**308.** Brahmanas recite the Vedas, bards their songs of praise;[^01-42]
in the sky the gods utter blessings.

**309.** The women raise the auspicious ulu-ulu cry;
old men and children dance and clap their hands.

**310.** Lowing, the cows with their calves
roam about with tails held high and grass in their mouths.

**311.** The villagers bring gifts, filling their plates;
everyone gives, rejoicing greatly.

**312.** Durgadas Raya, bringing musicians and instruments,
came to the Acharya's house uttering blessings.

**313.** Coming into the courtyard, he began to dance at length
and distributed many gifts to the brahmanas.

**314.** No sound was heard but "Radha-Krishna,"
and a voice from the sky said: "Sing, sing!"

**315.** *Today there is no end to my joy:
after so many days Madhava is in my house!* (refrain)[^01-43]

**316.** Having this song sung, he began to dance;
there was no bound to the joy, and so the day ended.

**317.** With his people the Raya went back to his home,
and brahmanas and good folk came thronging.

**318.** For the son's welfare they made offerings to the brahmana:
whatever wealth they had at home they brought and laid before him.

**319.** He whose hope is the feet of Sri Jahnava and Virachandra,
Nityananda Dasa, tells the Prema-vilasa.

*Thus ends the first vilasa, called "The Description of the Birth of Srinivasa Acharya Thakura".*

# Vilasa 2. The Description of the Festival of Srinivasa Acharya's Birth

*Chakhandi rejoices at Srinivasa's birth: the father gazes on his son's face; music, dancing and crowds of people who forget their sorrows*

**1.** Glory, glory to Sri Chaitanya Acharya, glory, glory!
Glory, glory to Lakshmipriya of compassionate heart!

**2.** Glory, glory to the listeners — listen with care,
you whose life's treasure is the pastimes of Radha and Krishna's prema.

**3.** Hearing of the son's birth, people forgot their sorrow and grief;
men and women set out to see him.
The lame, the dull-witted and the blind run along, singing the virtues of Radha and Krishna,
forgetting home and sons.[^02-1]

**4.** The Acharya went into the room, his eyes brimming with joy:
he beholds his son's moon-like face.
Tears flow from his eyes; gazing, he is beside himself,
again and again offering up his life for him.[^02-2]

**5.** Having seen, he cannot come away; tears stream from both his eyes:
"Blessed is the mother who bore him in her womb!"
Whether gandharva or kinnara — there is none to compare him with;
and he sank into the ocean of prema.

**6.** The dancing girls dance, and all the dancers
dance and sing in the sweetest voices.
The bhats recite many wondrous pastimes of Krishna,
their bodies thrilling with delight.

**7.** Mridanga, jhanjh and dhol — the music is a tumult;
kartals and pakhoajs are played.
Mahuri and pinaka sound, damph and saptasvara thunder;[^02-3]
the sound pierces the sky.

**8.** The blind and the deaf count themselves blessed,
yet they too reproach Providence:
"I would have gazed my fill — in such sorrow I die!
O Fate, you are pitiless!"

**9.** So saying they dance and sing, weep and roll on the ground,
exulting and crying "Radha-Krishna!"
Hundreds of thousands of people run, casting off fear, sorrow and grief;
many worldly men and pandits run too.

**10.** Bodies were filled with joy, homes were filled with wealth and grain,
and all swooned in prema.[^02-4]

**11.** Such is the birth of Srinivasa — I have told it to you, brothers;
whoever hears it, his life is fulfilled.
Nityananda Dasa in his song will distribute the wealth of prema,
to purify his own body.

**12.** He whose hope is the feet of Sri Jahnava and Virachandra,
Nityananda Dasa, tells the Prema-vilasa.

*Thus ends the second vilasa, called "The Description of the Festival of Srinivasa Acharya Thakura's Birth".*

[^02-1]: Stanzas 3–11 are in tripadi metre.
[^02-2]: "Nichhiya nichhiya": the nichhani rite, in which one circles the hand round a child as though giving up one's own life for it, to ward off misfortune.
[^02-3]: Mahuri, a reed wind instrument like the oboe; pinaka, a stringed instrument; damph, a frame drum; saptasvara, an instrument "of seven notes".
[^02-4]: The stanza survives only as a half; in the 1913 edition its opening words ("ānande purila deha") have been displaced to the top of the page.

# Vilasa 3. The Interruption of Srinivasa's Studies

*The author's address on the cause of his writing; Srinivasa's childhood — the first rice and the sacred thread; the break in his studies and the boy's grief; his teacher Dhananjaya admits that the pupil has surpassed him; Sarasvati, by Chaitanya's command, bestows all learning on Srinivasa*

**1.** Glory, glory to Sri Chaitanya Acharya, glory, glory!
Glory, glory to Lakshmipriya of compassionate heart!

**2.** Glory, glory to the listeners — listen with care,
you whose life's treasure is the pastimes of Radha and Krishna's prema.

**3.** Of one shortcoming of mine I will write once:[^03-1]
at the feet of Krishna's devotees I bow.

**4.** I have not studied, and there is not a trace of bhakti in me;
yet I write by the command of the Ocean of compassion:

**5.** "All my devotees who wander upon the earth
descended along with me and bear the same virtues.

**6.** Some have described the pastimes of Radha and Krishna,
others have revealed scriptures about Gaura's pastimes.

**7.** Whoever writes of the virtues of Krishna's devotees,
to him I show mercy with a joyful heart.

**8.** After my departure there will be prema upon the earth;
I bid you write and describe their virtues.

**9.** Srinivasa and Narottama are the two Mahashayas;
write of the virtues of these two abundantly.

**10.** Write of the virtues of both and of their way of bhajana —
how the earth spread prema in their form."

**11.** I have never known a speck of the gift of description;
yet I write by the strength of the command of the two Lords.

**12.** Sri Jahnava and Virachandra, at whose feet my hope rests —
from them came the command to reveal these virtues.

**13.** Srinivasa is my breath, Narottama my life;
with care I write of the virtues of these two.

**14.** By that command I write whatever words come to my mind;
O Vaishnava Gosanis, do not count it against me in any way.

**15.** For six months the Acharya did not go out anywhere;
seeing his son's power, he remained at peace.

**16.** Both rejoiced as they looked on their son's face,
and raised their son with happy hearts.

**17.** The time of the annaprashana[^03-2] came;
they brought an astrologer and fixed the day firmly.

**18.** At an auspicious moment they put prasada into the son's mouth,
and both rejoiced, clasping their son to their breasts.

**19.** The time of the chudakarana[^03-3] came;
performing the rites according to rule, they gave him the sacred thread.

**20.** A crimson cloth gleams upon his body;
seeing him, his father and mother rejoice at heart.

**21.** On the third day the Thakura grew restless:
his lessons had stopped — he stayed at home and began to weep.[^03-4]

**22.** At this time the pandit Vidyanidhi came by;[^03-5]
hearing of the break in the lessons, he was greatly pleased:

**23.** "The boy has such a longing for learning —
it seems Srinivasa will become an expert in the sciences."

**24.** One night Srinivasa saw in a dream:
"Study quickly, Srinivasa — you will go to Vrindavana.

**25.** Gauda is very dear to Chaitanya;
through you he will make books of the pastimes arise here.

**26.** Why have you stopped studying for three days?
At your parents' word I will teach you.

**27.** But if you act against this word,
what you have learned will not come back to your mind."[^03-6]

**28.** He constantly uttered the name of Radha and Krishna with his tongue,
and so his learning went — he did not go to study.

**29.** Lakshmipriya and the Acharya were astonished:
they could not understand what kind of birth this was.[^03-7]

**30.** He constantly uttered the name of Radha and Krishna with his tongue,
and so his learning went — yet there was joy in his heart.

**31.** Sitting at home, what does Srinivasa say?
"I have not become a pandit" — such is the pain in his mind.

**32.** "I cannot understand Krishna's mercy;
scholarship comes from study — such is its way.[^03-8]

**33.** Therefore I will not live in Yajigrama:
for the sake of learning I will go to another land."

**34.** After ten days his mother bade him:
"Go and study, my son; your lessons have been interrupted."

**35.** "As you command," he said, and taking a book in his hand,
Srinivasa went to his guru's place.

**36.** Dhananjaya Vidyanivasa said a wondrous thing:
"Looking at your form, I feel joy.

**37.** Listen, listen, Srinivasa, I will tell you:
learning no longer comes alive in me — why have you come?

**38.** You have stolen all my learning;
I am an empty body, I tell you.

**39.** I cannot understand your nature;
it seems Sarasvati has turned against me."

**40.** Ashamed, Srinivasa bowed to his guru,
and Dhananjaya started up in fear, crying "Alas, alas!"

**41.** Srinivasa's shame grew all the more, doubling;
dejected, he tied up his book and went away.

**42.** His father and mother knew nothing of all this;
grieving over the break in his studies, he lay down alone.

**43.** The meal was ready, but the boy was not at home;
his father went to look for him with an anxious heart.

**44.** The pandit said: "He left a long time ago."
Finding no trace of him, the father was greatly distressed.

**45.** Going into the house, he entered the room
and saw him asleep with a book in his hand.

**46.** Hearing his father's voice, out of shame he said nothing,
and saying "Give me food, Mother," began to laugh.

**47.** Having eaten, Srinivasa rinsed his mouth
and, laughing, lay down again.

**48.** Suddenly a voice from heaven was heard in the room —
the sound came: "All learning shall awaken in you!

**49.** I am Sarasvati; by Chaitanya's command
I have come in the guise of a dream to give you learning."

**50.** Opening his eyes, he looks — there is no one in the room.
"By the goddess's boon I shall have great learning!"

**51.** Laughing, he came out happily
and stood before his father and mother.

**52.** "Come, come, my son, let me take you on my lap;
do not grieve over the break in your studies," and she kissed his cheek.[^03-9]

**53.** From then on there was no further break in his studies,
and beyond that, the name of Radha and Krishna awoke in him.

**54.** He whose hope is the feet of Sri Jahnava and Virachandra,
Nityananda Dasa, tells the Prema-vilasa.

*Thus ends the third vilasa, containing the description of the interruption of Srinivasa Acharya Thakura's studies.*

# Vilasa 4. Srinivasa's Wanderings: Srikhanda, Puri, Navadvipa, Shantipur

*Srinivasa meets Narahari Sarakara; longing for Mahaprabhu and a voice from the sky; the death of Chaitanya Dasa; the move to Yajigrama; Srikhanda and Raghunandana; the journey to Puri and Gadadhara's passing; Navadvipa and Vishnupriya's grace; Shantipur: the vision of Advaita and Sita's account*

**1.** Glory, glory to Sri Chaitanya, glory to Nityananda!
Glory to Advaita-chandra, glory to the host of Gaura's devotees!

**2.** One morning, as Srinivasa was going to bathe,
he met Sarakara Thakura[^04-1] on the road.

**3.** He was on his way from Gajipur to Khanda,
and he saw a wondrous form of golden hue.

**4.** Suddenly the Lord's feet came to his mind:
it seemed to him that he had seen that very form in person.

**5.** "Srinivasa is his name, born in a brahmana family" —
seeing the boy's radiance, the Thakura grasped the secret.

**6.** "If I ask his name, I shall learn who he is," he thought;
and the boy, prostrating himself, stood before him.

**7.** In a sweet voice he began to ask him:
"What is your name, boy? Tell me truly."

**8.** Respectfully Srinivasa answered:
"Your own servant, born at Chakhandi."

**9.** Hearing the name Srinivasa, the Thakura was delighted
and became firmly convinced that this was Chaitanya's power.

**10.** "Come, come, my son, let me embrace you!" —
and holding him to his breast, he bathed him in tears.

**11.** "Nityananda is anxious about you.[^04-2]
I had a wish, and now I have met you.

**12.** I had heard nothing of you from anyone, nor seen you;
I cannot tell how much hidden treasure lies in this.

**13.** Virachandra called me in Jahnava's presence:
'Send Srinivasa to Vrindavana quickly.'

**14.** He was born on the bank of the Ganga and is still a mere child —
that is why I have not met him.

**15.** Without any effort Chaitanya has brought us together on this road;
through you he will reveal the pastimes in Vrindavana.

**16.** Now there is no need to ask anything more;
meeting you in Khanda will make me happy."

**17.** The Thakura went on to Khanda, and Srinivasa crossed the Ganga;
and at home his father and mother were sorely anxious.[^04-3]

**18.** On reaching home the boy lost all composure in prema:
he laughs, weeps, dances, sings and falls to the ground again and again.

**19.** Crying out, he weeps very loudly,
and a great lamentation arose in the Acharya's house.

**20.** "Why has this happened to him? We know nothing;
when we ask, he weeps the more," and their hearts were torn.

**21.** The Acharya heard the weeping inside the house
and saw his son weeping with an anguished heart.

**22.** He asked: "My son, why are you weeping?
You went to bathe — why do you weep? I do not understand."

**23.** One by one the villagers gathered,
and seeing the boy's behaviour they cried out in dismay.

**24.** Among them was an old brahmana:
"Be patient; listen, I will tell you the cause of this.

**25.** Narahari Thakura Mahashaya, who lives in Khanda,
met the boy on the road at the time of his bath.

**26.** From seeing him the boy has come into this state;
do not worry, be patient," he said truly.

**27.** Hearing the name of Narahari, the boy laughed,
and at the brahmana's words regained some outward awareness.

**28.** But from that day his state was changed:
the malady of separation from Chaitanya grew twofold.

**29.** "I never had the sight of Chaitanya Prabhu,
I never saw the feet of Nityananda Prabhu,

**30.** I did not see the form of Advaita Acharya,
I did not meet Svarupa, Ramananda Raya, Sanatana or Rupa,

**31.** I did not hear sankirtana together with the devotees —
sinner that I am, I was not born at that time!"

**32.** Lifting his face to the sky, he cried out long:
"By giving me a later birth, Fate has barred my happiness!"

**33.** Then a voice sounded from the sky:
"Your birth is in the form of prema — why do you worry?

**34.** Through you the pastimes of Radha and Krishna will be spread;
with what Chaitanya savoured you will flood the world.

**35.** In Vrindavana Rupa and Sanatana,
the two brothers, have written scriptures of rasa for your sake.

**36.** Foreseeing the future, Chaitanya Gosani, for your sake,
sent the two brothers to compose these books.

**37.** The two brothers are anxious in Vrindavana;
if you go quickly, you will see them.

**38.** If you delay, you will not see the two brothers,
and when you reach Vrindavana your sorrow will be all the greater."

**39.** "The grief in my father's and mother's hearts — this is my great dilemma;
if only the Mahashaya would help me in this!"

**40.** For a while he grew calm, and the people went home;
seeing him well, his father and mother were glad at heart.

**41.** Understanding his father's heart, Srinivasa smiled
and asked to eat: "I am very hungry."

**42.** At their son's words they were overjoyed
and lovingly fed him many dishes.

**43.** "While my father and mother live, how can I leave them?
Besides, I am only a boy — how shall I go to Vrindavana?"

**44.** But Chaitanya's mercy is very deep;
all sorrow vanished, and there was joy in his heart.

**45.** Suddenly a fever came upon Chaitanya Dasa,
and within seven days he attained the Ganga.[^04-4]

**46.** Seeing this, Srinivasa wept much in his grief,
and then performed the prescribed rites with special care.

**47.** At the loss of his father he suffered great sorrow,
and seeing his mother weep, his face withered.

**48.** "To the childless the Lord gave a son, Srinivasa,
and now bereavement has come, and our hope is unfulfilled.

**49.** O cruel Fate, what shall I say to you?
So soon you have given the boy such sorrow!

**50.** My diamond of a boy[^04-5] — how will his days pass?
We have no one to call our own — what will become of me?

**51.** O Srinivasa, where has your father gone?
How shall we pass our days? We are orphaned!"

**52.** Seeing his mother's grief, Srinivasa was stricken
and wept long, crying "Father, father!

**53.** With whom have you left me, where have you gone?[^04-6]
You loved me so, Thakura, and yet you have left me!"

**54.** Thus, after lamenting long on the bank of the Ganga,
he performed the rites according to rule and gave the bones to the water.

**55.** Coming home, he wept a great deal,
and women came to console Lakshmipriya.

**56.** "Listen, listen, Thakurani, why do you grieve?
Look at your son and restrain yourself.

**57.** What comfort can we give? Listen, steady your heart;
look at your son and forget your sorrow; do not weep."

**58.** At this time a voice sounded from the sky:
"Why do you grieve, mother, why are you anxious?

**59.** You know nothing of the boy's virtues;
when you go to Yajigrama, you will learn everything later.

**60.** For the sake of these two[^04-7] Sri Chaitanya and Nityananda
began the books in Vrindavana through Rupa.

**61.** Take care of your son — listen, Lakshmipriya;
do not grieve in vain; be steady of heart."

**62.** All her grief for her husband went away,
but in her breast remained a sprout of sorrow over Srinivasa.

**63.** Having duly performed all the customary observances,
Srinivasa visited Yajigrama and saw Narahari.[^04-8]

**64.** A great longing arose in him: "I will leave this village;
settling my mother in Yajigrama, I will go elsewhere."

**65.** At night, as he slept in the village,
the command of Chaitanya came in a dream: "Go to Vrindavana."

**66.** Having seen the dream, he awoke:
"How can I go so soon and leave her?

**67.** Besides, I have not yet received initiation;
I have no right to go to Vrindavana.

**68.** Delay is not good; I will go and settle there,
and then follow whatever counsel Thakura Narahari gives me."

**69.** After living some days in Chakhandi,
he left that place and came to Yajigrama.

**70.** On the fifth day of the month of Phalguna they settled there,
and at that time he met the zamindar of the village.

**71.** Seeing his radiance, the zamindar showed him honour:
"Live in this village — I will build you a house."

**72.** Seeing his wondrous form, he counted himself fortunate:
"We too are blessed; our life is fulfilled!"

**73.** Thus he stayed some days in that village,
and then the wish arose in him to go to Khanda.

**74.** He chose and made ready a very charming place —
a beautiful dwelling on the western side of the village.[^04-9]

**75.** Leaving his mother in that village, he went to Khanda.
At the outer gate, under a tree, sat Sri Raghunandana.

**76.** Seeing his radiance, he asked: "What is your name?
Where have you come from? Tell me your news."

**77.** "I have just come from Yajigrama for darshan;
my name is Srinivasa," he answered respectfully.

**78.** Hearing the name Srinivasa, Raghunandana was overcome with prema;
stretching out his arms, he came and embraced him.

**79.** "From the Thakura's holy lips I have heard all about you;
at the mere sight of you all my distress has gone.

**80.** Come, come, brother, to the Thakura;
we two will have our talk later."

**81.** Taking him by the hand, he led him to the Thakura.
"Come, come, my son, sit down, Srinivasa!

**82.** A letter has come from Virachandra about you:
'Send Srinivasa to Vrindavana quickly.'"

**83.** Kindly he passed his holy hand over his body,
and at the touch of that holy hand Srinivasa was deeply absorbed in prema.

**84.** Nearby was Nayana Sena Mahashaya;[^04-10]
supporting Srinivasa, he took him to his own house.

**85.** That day he was holding the worship of his guru and his father's memorial rite;
in the afternoon the Thakura came to his house with Raghunandana.

**86.** "Tell me, tell me, Nayana, where is Srinivasa?
Bring him — I will ask him about going to Vrindavana."

**87.** Then Srinivasa, seeing Narahari,
bowed to him, and he was glad to see his smiling face.

**88.** "Tell me, Srinivasa, about your going to Vrindavana:
how will you manage it, my son? Tell me in detail."

**89.** "Listen, Thakura, I must tell you:
without a refuge,[^04-11] how can I do this?"

**90.** "Concerning you, Chaitanya gave the Bhatta a command:
he will give you initiation in every way."

**91.** Weeping, Srinivasa said:
"Why do you deceive me and send me to Vrindavana?

**92.** Coming from Chakhandi I received your darshan,
and at that very moment I surrendered myself to you."

**93.** The Thakura said: "What you say is true;
but Gopala Bhatta is your guru, I tell you.

**94.** I cannot go against the Lord's command.
For now live here and serve.

**95.** Hari-nama is Mahaprabhu's own power;
understanding this, you shall receive it."

**96.** Having heard this, he went to his lodging
and began to ponder what Sarakara Thakura had said.

**97.** "From whom shall I receive Hari-nama?" —
turning this over in his mind, he lay awake all night.

**98.** Towards the end of the night his sleep broke, and he was half awake,[^04-12]
and Gaurachandra mercifully said to him:

**99.** "Listen, listen, Srinivasa, why are you troubled?
You were born as prema in form for my purpose.

**100.** Why, then, do you wait?[^04-13]
I have told you: you were born as prema in form.

**101.** Go to Vrindavana; do not delay;
take refuge at the feet of Gopala Bhatta.

**102.** He was born in the land of Telanga; he is my very life;
and Sanatana and Rupa are one soul in two bodies.

**103.** All the books that Rupa and Sanatana have written
they will entrust to you when you go.

**104.** They are anxious at your delay;
the work is done — go quickly."

**105.** In ecstasy he bowed to the Lord,
who laid a hand on his head and said: "May your heart's desire be fulfilled."

**106.** The Lord vanished, and his sleep was broken;
waking, Srinivasa reflected:

**107.** "The Lord has commanded me to go to Vrindavana."
And he went and told Sarakara Thakura.

**108.** Thinking thus, Srinivasa came to Narahari,
bowed to him and offered him praise:

**109.** "Listen, Mahashaya, to what I saw in a dream:
a golden-bodied boy came and spoke to me..."

**110.** All that he had seen in the dream he told;
the Thakura said: "Mahaprabhu's mercy has come."

**111.** Laying a hand on his head, he blessed him:
"May Vrindavana show you mercy without delay.

**112.** I have sent a letter to Virachandra:
'I have kept Srinivasa with me — give your command.'

**113.** Whatever answer comes, I will act on it;
until then stay here, close to me."

**114.** Thus he lived some days in Khanda,
and a wish arose in him to see Jagannatha.

**115.** "I greatly wish to study the Bhagavata;
I will see Jagannatha and stay with the Pandita.[^04-14]

**116.** Going to him I will study the Bhagavata,
and having studied it with commentary I will go to Vrindavana."

**117.** With this in mind he went to Narahari.
"What you have said, my son, is right.

**118.** I will give you a Vaishnava as a companion;
delivering a letter, he will give my news."

**119.** He put a respectful letter into the Vaishnava's hand,
and setting out, the two went along the road to Jagannatha.

**120.** Travelling on, they reached Jagannatha-puri,
and having seen Jagannatha they came to Gopinatha's abode.[^04-15]

**121.** The Pandita Gosani was stricken in separation from Chaitanya:
now he swooned, now he laughed, and a stupor lay within him.[^04-16]

**122.** Saying "Chaitanya! Nityananda!" Srinivasa prostrated himself;
hearing Chaitanya's name, Gosani was stirred.

**123.** "Who are you? Who are you?" he said, opening his eyes.
"Come, come, my son, let me hold you to my breast.

**124.** What is your name, my son? Tell me.
What wondrous words I have heard from your lips!

**125.** By letting me hear the Name you have bought me.[^04-17]
With separation in my heart I saw a dream:

**126.** 'From Gauda will come one named Srinivasa,
born at Chakhandi as prema in form,

**127.** to his father Chaitanya Dasa, from the womb of Lakshmipriya,
so that the pastimes of Radha and Krishna may be spread.'

**128.** Are you that one, my son? Tell me who you are;
soothe my body — tell me for certain."

**129.** "I am he," he said, and smiled gently.
"You are the master, and I am wretched, luckless and low."

**130.** "It is good that you came, my son, and told me who you are.
The Lord commanded me to teach you the Bhagavata.

**131.** At the time of his last pastimes the Lord told me:
'When Srinivasa comes, let him hear Krishna's pastimes.

**132.** For his sake you shall stay at Gopinatha,
and send him to Vrindavana with a letter in his hand.

**133.** Sri Rupa and Sanatana, the two brothers,
have revealed the Lord's heart through their scriptures.

**134.** Those scriptures you[^04-18] will bring to Gauda,
and reveal the scriptures of the pastimes in every particular.'

**135.** The Lord's command is to teach you the Bhagavata,
but the letters of my book have all been washed away by my tears.

**136.** Give my letter into Narahari's hands;
he will send a new book with you.

**137.** The Lord's command concerning you is mighty;
do not delay; settle everything.

**138.** In the pastimes of Radha and Krishna there is Sri Guna-manjari —
she is none other than Gopala Bhatta, of equal sweetness."

**139.** "I will become his disciple, Prabhu!" — a great longing was in Srinivasa's heart,
and on hearing the name Guna-manjari his ears rejoiced.

**140.** "I see the Lord's command has been given to the Manjari;
in Navadvipa you will receive a witness from Ishvari-ji.[^04-19]

**141.** Partake of the remnants of Gopinatha's meal;
today is an auspicious day — set out for Gauda.

**142.** If you delay on the road, you will not find them."
With closed eyes Srinivasa listened to these words.

**143.** "Where has Prabhu Chaitanya gone, where is Nityananda?" —
for a moment he weeps, then gently laughs.

**144.** Bearing the pain of separation, he loses all memory:
it does not occur to him that Gopinatha is before him.

**145.** The raving of separation, all kinds of changes in his body;
lifting his face, now and again he cries out.

**146.** Seeing these changes, Srinivasa was stunned:
"If I go to Gauda, I shall not see him again."

**147.** Taking the reply, he prostrated himself.
"Set out for your country, if you would study the Bhagavata."

**148.** Taking the road, he came to Narahari;
that day Virachandra was in the house, and many people had gathered.[^04-20]

**149.** Then the Mahashaya prostrated himself,
and the word was given: "Srinivasa, it is good that you have come.

**150.** Listen: this letter has come from Vrindavana —
study the Bhagavata and set out for Vrindavana."

**151.** He made known the Pandita Gosani's command in the letter
and told everything just as he had seen it.

**152.** He read the letter out to Virachandra Gosani
and asked leave to go and study the Bhagavata.

**153.** "If you delay, you will not find him;
go to the Kshetra without delay."

**154.** The Thakura again gave him the same Vaishnava as a companion,
and saying "Gadadhara! Chaitanya!" Srinivasa set out.

**155.** In anxiety Srinivasa went as far as Jajpur,
and as he entered the village he received the news that the Pandita had passed away.

**156.** Receiving the news, he swooned on the spot,
and the frightened Vaishnava clasped his feet.

**157.** Coming to himself, he bowed down many times:
"My purpose is not fulfilled — Fate has turned against me!"

**158.** That night he fasted there;
seeing how wasted he was, the Vaishnava was greatly afraid:

**159.** "How shall I bring him to Gauda?
The Thakura will punish me!" — and his heart sank.

**160.** With much care he got him to eat,
and entreating him, they went on to Gauda.

**161.** Weeping and weeping, he came back to Gauda,[^04-21]
and in the afternoon entered the village of Srikhanda.

**162.** Prostrating himself, he told the whole story
and, crying "Alas!", wept long.

**163.** Who can describe that lament of separation —
the lament of one whose heart holds the grief of separation from guru and Vaishnavas?

**164.** From that day another state came over him again:
"How shall I get to Vrindavana?" — and his longing grew.

**165.** In the morning, leaving Srikhanda, he came to Navadvipa,
and living as a renunciant he stayed near the Lord's house.

**166.** Crying "Pandita Gosani!", he weeps aloud;
for two or four days at a time he takes no food.

**167.** When his hunger becomes unbearable,
he gathers a chhatak[^04-22] of rice,

**168.** cooks it on the bank of the Ganga,
but what he truly eats is the pain of separation.

**169.** By the eighth day his body was utterly wasted;
and he met Vamshivadana Dasa.

**170.** "What is your name? Where do you live? I have never seen or heard of you." —
"In separation from Gadadhara I am staying here.

**171.** My name is Srinivasa; my home is in Yajigrama;
I could not study the Bhagavata, and my heart is in pain.

**172.** Gadadhara Pandita had the Lord's command —
I would have studied with him, but by my misfortune it did not come to pass."

**173.** As he spoke, a great weeping overcame him.
At that moment Ishana arrived.

**174.** Such is Ishana's nature: he is compassionate to living beings,
and on seeing a great bhagavata, prema rises in him.

**175.** Seeing him so wasted, he questioned him,
and seeing him entirely alone, he was much pleased.[^04-23]

**176.** He understood that Chaitanya's power was in the boy:
"I ought to tell Ishvari of this."

**177.** He went back home to Ishvari:
"I have seen a wondrous boy at the bathing ghat on the Ganga.

**178.** He is always weeping, calling on Gadadhara Pandita;
he has no companion, and his eyes are full of tears.

**179.** Seeing him, I was moved to compassion:
without food his body has become utterly wasted.

**180.** If you permit, I will give him some food,
and later bring him here, and you may show him mercy."

**181.** "Go and give him rice, as much as is fitting;
but now that Chaitanya is gone, his renunciation troubles me."[^04-24]

**182.** Ishana took excellent provisions
and went joyfully to Srinivasa.

**183.** "Listen, brahmana: take these provisions,
go to the bank of the Ganga, cook them and eat."

**184.** "As you command," he said, and bowing he took them.
And here Ishvari herself understood everything.

**185.** Having given him rice, Ishvari rejoiced at heart:
"I see the boy has been born as prema in form."[^04-25]

**186.** When the brahmana took the rice and cooked it,
she sent ten vairagis to him.

**187.** When the food was ready, they came in the guise of vairagis
and appeared before him just at the time of the meal.

**188.** Seeing the Vaishnavas, he was overjoyed,
and receiving them all, he showed them great honour.

**189.** They said: "We are very hungry;
give us food, Mahashaya, and then we shall be pleased."

**190.** "Most kindly you have come and given me darshan;
the prasada is ready — sit down and eat."

**191.** "You have cooked very little, and we are many;
our hunger will not be satisfied — that is plain to see."

**192.** "Your hunger will be satisfied — such is the nature of prasada."
And the ten Vaishnavas sat down in a circle.

**193.** In this way he served them all,
filling plate after plate with great joy.

**194.** Half a ser of rice, made into prasada,
was partaken of by eleven Vaishnavas, and they were content.

**195.** Ishvari heard of this through Ishana
and understood in her heart that he had been born as prema in form.

**196.** "How delightful it is to hear of such a boy's virtues!
I must certainly see the boy's face."[^04-26]

**197.** At night, going with a maid to bathe in the Ganga,
she saw the boy, full of the sweetness of prema.

**198.** Having bathed, while it was still night, she went back to the inner apartments;
having seen the boy, she was glad at heart.[^04-27]

**199.** "How can I bring him here and question him?
How can I ask, looking at the face of a strange man?

**200.** If he is the Lord's power, my shyness will go far away;
then I shall know that abundant mercy rests on him."

**201.** Ishvari bade Ishana bring the boy:
"Why does he weep day and night?"

**202.** Ishana came and said: "Listen, Srinivasa:
Ishvari is calling you — come to the Lord's house."

**203.** Raising his arms, Srinivasa began a long dance:
it seemed that the Pandita Gosani's state had come upon him.

**204.** Weeping, he went along behind Ishana;
on entering the inner chamber he grew shy.

**205.** Trembling, he went into the inner apartments,
but did not come close and stayed at some distance.

**206.** Ishana said: "Here is Srinivasa;
he bows down to you; he is your dear servant."

**207.** Drawing aside the inner curtain, she looked at him
and understood the reason: it is my Lord's power.

**208.** Setting her shyness aside, she called him herself:
"Why do you weep and wander about alone?"

**209.** He told her the Pandita Gosani's words:
"By his kindness I would have gone to Vrindavana.

**210.** What I heard from his lips in Nilachala...
Not having studied the Bhagavata, my birth has been in vain.

**211.** I heard the Lord's command to go to Vrindavana,
but it has not been fulfilled," he said at her feet.

**212.** "Now I weep without ceasing for Gadadhara;
therefore the Lord's power rests with you."

**213.** "I see you are still very young and tender.
Have you taken up renunciation? Go home, brahmana boy.

**214.** Renunciation is hard — it needs very great strength."[^04-28]
With joined palms he pleaded long:

**215.** "Let me stay near your feet;
my soul will be soothed, and I shall escape from danger.

**216.** I have no one in the world; only my mother is living;
how can I go to Vrindavana and still keep my life?"

**217.** "Without Chaitanya's power there is no such mercy.[^04-29]
When you are mature, then you will go; it is not fitting now.

**218.** Having received this command, live outside the house,
eat prasada, and let your mind grow steady."[^04-30]

**219.** Vishnupriya, in separation from Gauranga, was sorely afflicted:
her grief doubled, and she lost herself.

**220.** Ishvari called Ishana and said: "Listen, Ishana:
the night has gone by, and it is dawn."

**221.** Ishana said: "He spends the night weeping;
crying 'O Pandita Gosani!', he stays awake."

**222.** That day they did not meet again;
in longing for darshan, night and day went by.

**223.** Hear, brothers, how Ishvari took the Name —
hearing it, one comes to realise the pastimes.

**224.** She brings new clay pots and sets them on either side:
one pot empty, the other full of rice.

**225.** Each time she chants the sixteen names of thirty-two syllables,[^04-31]
she places one grain in the pot, with joy in her heart.

**226.** Until the third watch she chants Hari-nama,
and whatever grains have been gathered she takes and goes to cook.

**227.** Cooking only those grains,
she feeds them to the Lord with tears.

**228.** Day and night, Hari-nama, so many rounds for the Lord!
I cannot fathom that endeavour — my mind is utterly helpless.

**229.** What can be said of her who is the Lord's beloved?
Day and night she ever chants Hari-nama.

**230.** What is impossible for her, with such longing for the Name?
She chants the Name, and in it she plants the Lord's power.

**231.** By the mere shadow of the Name all sins are destroyed,
the heart's desire grows, and prema is made manifest.

**232.** The Name is a wish-fulfilling tree — this is certain;
whoever chants the Name by count attains every perfection.

**233.** "The Name is the truth in the Kali age" — so it is said three times;[^04-32]
Gauranga accepts those who are free from attachment.

**234.** It is the best of all means of sadhana;
only by the fortune of many births does love for it arise.

**235.** Thus, when the third watch of the night had passed,
crying "O Chaitanya!", she lay down on the ground.

**236.** At the end of the night the two brothers together, in sankirtana,
dancing, say: "Where is my mother?

**237.** Your daughter-in-law keeps my Srinivasa outside the gate,
while she herself is at ease in her own house!

**238.** All my work is to be done through Srinivasa;
send him to Abhirama, with Ishana as his companion."[^04-33]

**239.** In separation from Chaitanya she does not sleep at night;
having heard the nectar words of the dream, she fell silent.

**240.** "Ishana! Ishana!" the maids call out,
but Ishana is fast asleep and does not stir.

**241.** After a long while Ishana awoke,
and in fear counted himself most unfortunate.

**242.** With joined palms he came to Ishvari,
and she commanded: "Bring Srinivasa to me."

**243.** Srinivasa was weeping on a mat of kusha grass.
"Get up, get up, lad, come quickly!"[^04-34]

**244.** Standing in the courtyard, he bowed down many times,
and Ishana was told: "Take him to see Abhirama."[^04-35]

**245.** So saying, with her toes wrapped in cloth,
she called Srinivasa and raised her foot onto his head.

**246.** At the touch of her foot he was overcome with prema
and, rolling on the ground, began to weep.

**247.** "Listen, listen, my son, you are fortunate:
Chaitanya's power is in you — it cannot be otherwise.

**248.** Go to Shantipur, and then you will go to Khardaha;
on seeing Acharya Gosani, you will be recognised.

**249.** Going to Khardaha, you will see Nityananda;
finding you, Jahnava will rejoice.

**250.** Do not delay long; go quickly —
you will hear much and see the sweetness of beauty.

**251.** Having met everyone, go to Vrindavana;
all will be fulfilled — keep this in mind on the way."

**252.** Prostrating himself, he went to Shantipur,
and fell into great perplexity: "Where shall I stay?

**253.** Ishvari bade me see Advaita —
how is that command to be understood?

**254.** It is now the third year since Gosani passed away;
yet this command cannot be disobeyed" — doubt cast him into a quandary.

**255.** At that moment one appeared with arms reaching to his knees and a huge body;
seeing his radiance, Srinivasa trembled and lost all composure.

**256.** Unable to open his eyes, he fell to the ground.
"Come, come, Srinivasa, I have heard your words."

**257.** So Advaita Gosani had willed it;
prostrating himself there, Srinivasa addressed him.

**258.** Sighing, Gosani wept a long while:
"Where have Chaitanya and Nityananda gone in their bodies?

**259.** Where have their companions gone — Svarupa, Ramananda Raya?" —
and lovingly he laid his hand on Srinivasa's head.

**260.** "Come, come, Srinivasa, let my life be soothed;
let me embrace you and let my heart be comforted.

**261.** For your sake the Lord sent Gopala Bhatta;
you will become his servant — I tell you for certain.

**262.** Being angry with me, the Lord caused you to be born,[^04-36]
and here he has revealed all his own work.

**263.** He sent Rupa and Sanatana to Vrindavana,
and to make that known he caused you to be born.

**264.** For your sake he sent Gopala Bhatta;
take instruction there, in order to make prema manifest.

**265.** Calling 'Come, come!', with the Lord's power infused in you,
you[^04-37] will flood the world, spreading prema.

**266.** For your sake I have appeared here;
never speak of it anywhere else.

**267.** Going to Khardaha, you will find joy;
having seen Jahnava, you will go to Vrindavana.

**268.** There you will see Sri Rupa;
go and worship the feet of Gopala Bhatta.

**269.** By Chaitanya's mercy you will flood the land with prema;
cry 'Advaita! Govinda!' and do not dwell on sorrow.

**270.** Your Lord, the best of lovers,
has by prema destroyed the corruption among my followers.[^04-38]

**271.** Whoever among my followers takes up this doctrine on his lips
will, cut off from Chaitanya and Nityananda, meet with great sorrow."

**272.** So saying, Advaita-chandra vanished,
and Srinivasa, parted from the vision, fell senseless.

**273.** At this time Mother Sita was going to bathe in the Ganga,
and she sees a jewel of a boy weeping.

**274.** "Child, child!" she took the boy on her lap
and comforted him with the sweetest words.

**275.** She asked: "Who are you? Why are you weeping?
It seems you have had the darshan of my lord.

**276.** Tell me, boy, where is your home?
Why have you come here, weeping so bitterly?"

**277.** "My name is Srinivasa; I was born at Chakhandi;
by Ishvari-ji's command I have come to see you."

**278.** Hearing the name Srinivasa, she rejoiced at heart:
she already knew of him from Achyutananda's letter.

**279.** "I have greatly wished to see you, my son;
by Chaitanya's great mercy we have met on the road."

**280.** Gopala Gosani[^04-39] was coming from his bath;
seeing him, Srinivasa fell prostrate.

**281.** "Until I come back from bathing in the Ganga,
look after him carefully."

**282.** Leaving them together, Mother Sita went to bathe in the Ganga,
and meanwhile Gosani sat with him on one seat.

**283.** Having bathed, she soon came back
and saw Srinivasa and Gopala together.

**284.** So she took him into the inner apartments,
glad at heart to have found a wondrous Vaishnava.

**285.** She gave him the remnants of Advaita's meal to eat:
"I am going to cook; you sit by the door."

**286.** Having cooked, she offered the bhoga,
gave Krishna water to rinse his mouth and laid him down to rest.

**287.** Gopala was told to take prasada:
"Sit down quickly together with Srinivasa.

**288.** I will serve the wondrous Vaishnava myself;
sit with him, my son — it will make me very happy."

**289.** They sat down together to eat;
at the touch of the prasada from the Lord's lips their hair stood on end again and again.

**290.** Food cooked by Sita's hand, the remnants of Krishna's lips —
and the transformations of prema in all their variety.

**291.** Having rinsed their mouths, both in great delight
freshened their mouths and sat together in one place.[^04-40]

**292.** The day ended, and evening came;
seeing Krishna's joy, their joy knew no bounds.

**293.** That night he spent joyfully in Shantipur,[^04-41]
and in the morning went to Sita to take his leave.

**294.** "I have one request — listen carefully:
on coming to this village I heard the words 'Advaita, Govinda'.[^04-42]

**295.** Tell me, Mother, the true account of this,
and then give me leave to go and see Khardaha."

**296.** "What need have you to hear this, boy?
Attend to your own business and go on your travels."

**297.** "Permit me, Mother — I long to hear it;
be gracious and kindly tell me."

**298.** Such is a boy's nature — he holds fast to what he has said;
and Mother Sita could not refuse him.

**299.** "Listen with a steady mind, Srinivasa;
when you hear it, you will believe with a calm heart.

**300.** At the time when Jagai and Madhai were delivered,
Gosani[^04-43] in anger said to Haridasa:

**301.** 'If Gosani does not give me the union of prema,
I will dry up all prema — and the fault will not be mine.'

**302.** Angry with Nityananda, he came home
and sent a riddle, a tarja, written through Jagadananda.

**303.** From that day anger arose in the Lord:
to Nityananda he gave companions — Ramai, Sundarananda and others,

**304.** and to my Thakura he gave Kamadeva Nagara,
and in his anger made the Nagara speak.[^04-44]

**305.** My lord came to Gauda with the Nagara in his company,
and amid the waves of prema the Nagara set a doctrine going.

**306.** As soon as I heard it, anger arose in me,
and I never looked on the Nagara's face again.

**307.** I separated my maidservant Nandini from me,[^04-45]
and I no longer listen to those words.

**308.** Some of my sons hold to Achyuta's view,
and some, following the Nagara, have gone astray.

**309.** I rejoice in the sons who hold to Achyuta's view;
and Nityananda, coming to Gauda, flooded it with prema.

**310.** Gosani could not restrain the Nagara,
and for that reason this circle became hostile.

**311.** Listen, Srinivasa, my heart is sorely pained:
having quarrelled with my sons, I keep to my house.[^04-46]

**312.** Whoever stands with Achyuta, the son of Chaitanya's maidservant,[^04-47]
does not speak this doctrine; whoever speaks it has no tie with me."

**313.** Hearing this, he rejoiced greatly at heart,
and Srinivasa prostrated himself again and again.

**314.** The Mother dispelled all the doubts in his mind,
and prostrating himself, he took leave of Sita.

**315.** He whose hope is the feet of Sri Jahnava and Virachandra,
Nityananda Dasa, tells the Prema-vilasa.

*Thus ends the fourth vilasa of the Prema-vilasa.*
