# Junior Haridasa
*Младший Харидас · @chhota-haridasa*

## The Lord's Singer
While Sri Chaitanya lived in Nilachala, two kirtana singers with sweet voices stayed by His side, and both were named
Haridasa: the Senior Haridasa and the Junior. Krishnadasa Kaviraja mentions them in his list of the branches of the
Chaitanya tree:

“Bada Haridasa and Chota Haridasa, two kirtana singers, stayed by Mahaprabhu's side.” (CC, Adi 10.147)

Of the Junior Haridasa — in Bengali, Chhota Haridasa — Kaviraja tells in the second chapter of the Antya-lila, where he
calls him “the Lord's singer”; there too, on the Lord's lips, he is simply “Haridasa.” Kavi Karnapura in the
“Gaura-ganoddesha-dipika” calls him “Haridasa the Younger,” and Ishana Nagara in the “Advaita-prakasha” “the singer Sri
Haridasa.” The biographies call him Chhota Haridasa, Haridasa and Sri Haridasa.[^1] <!-- F1 F4 -->

Where he came from, who his parents were and when he appeared in this world, the sources do not say; the year of his
departure is not known either, and the Vaishnava calendar of Sri Chaitanya Saraswat Math has no days for him. All we
know of him belongs to the years when the Lord lived in Puri, and the chief event of his life to the last of those years.
<!-- F3 -->

He lived together with Govinda, the Lord's personal servant. Having told how Govinda came to Mahaprabhu, Kaviraja adds:

“The two Haridasas, the junior and the senior, both kirtana singers, and Ramai and Nandai stayed with Govinda. Together
with Govinda they served the Lord.” (CC, Madhya 10.149–150) <!-- F5 -->

He was a renunciant, a *vairagi*: this is exactly what the Lord calls him in the story told below.[^2] He was
indifferent to worldly things and served the Lord by singing, each time choosing a kirtana suited to His mood.
Mahaprabhu loved him dearly, and so all the Lord's devotees treated him with great tenderness; Svarupa Damodara would
later say of him that he was dear to the Lord. <!-- F6 F7 F9 -->

One of the biographies opens its account of him with a reflection on music. Singing, playing instruments and dancing are
condemned by the shastras as unworthy of a noble person when they serve worldly enjoyment; yet the same arts are highly
praised when they are offered to the Lord and awaken attraction to Him. The Junior Haridasa's service was of this kind.
<!-- I1 -->

## Patraka
Who the two singers are in Krishna's pastimes, Kavi Karnapura tells:

“The two servants who were formerly in Vrindavana, Raktaka and Patraka, now serve Gauranga: they are Haridasa the Elder
and Haridasa the Younger.” (“Gaura-ganoddesha-dipika” 138)

By the order of the names, the Junior Haridasa is Patraka, one of Krishna's servants in Vrindavana, and the Senior is
Raktaka.[^3] <!-- F2 -->

## At the Ratha-yatra
At the festival of the chariots the Lord divided the devotees into seven kirtana groups. In the fourth the lead singer
was Govinda Ghosha; Haridasa, Vishnudasa, Raghava and Govinda's brothers Madhava and Vasu Ghosha sang the response, and
Vakreshvara danced (CC, Madhya 13.42). Whether this Haridasa was our singer Kaviraja does not specify; one of the
biographies says that he was, others say so with doubt.[^4] <!-- F8 -->

## The Rice from Madhavi-devi
In Puri, so as to be near the Lord, lived Bhagavan Acharya. He was so close to the Lord that from time to time he would
invite Him to an especially fine meal, and Mahaprabhu could not refuse him. Once, preparing such a meal, he called the
Junior Haridasa. The “Charitamrita” relates:

“One day the Acharya invited the Lord. At home he cooked rice and made various vegetable dishes. The Lord had a singer
named Chota Haridasa. The Acharya himself called him, brought him over and said, ‘Go in my name to Shikhi Mahiti's
sister, ask her for one *mana* of white rice and bring it.’” (CC, Antya 2.101–103)

A *mana* was a measure of grain used in Utkala. Although the Acharya had brought much prasada from the temple of
Jagannatha, he wished to cook many dishes for the Lord at home as well, and for this he needed good rice.[^5]
<!-- F10 F11 F12 -->

Shikhi Mahiti's sister was named Madhavi-devi. Kaviraja says of her:

“She was elderly, given to austerity, and a supreme Vaishnavi. The Lord counted her among Radhika's associates. In all
the world there are only three and a half such worthy persons: Svarupa Gosani, Ramananda Raya and Shikhi Mahiti are the
three, and his sister is the half.” (CC, Antya 2.104–106)

Madhavi and her brothers were among the Lord's most intimate companions. <!-- F13 Q3 -->

Haridasa begged the rice from her and brought it to the Acharya, who, much pleased, cooked it with love together with the
Lord's favourite dishes, and set out with them prasada from the temple, slices of ginger, and lime with salt. At midday
the Lord came to the Acharya and, seeing the rice, asked:

“‘Such excellent rice! Where did you get grain like this?’ The Acharya said, ‘It was begged from Madhavi and brought
here.’ The Lord said, ‘Who went and begged it?’ The Acharya named Chota Haridasa.” (CC, Antya 2.110–111)

The Acharya had not said that he went himself, and that is why the Lord asked who had brought it. Praising the rice,
Mahaprabhu finished His meal.[^6] <!-- F14 F15 F44 -->

## “Do Not Let Him Come Here”
Returning to His residence, the Lord said to Govinda, His constant servant:

“‘From today you must keep this order of Mine: do not let Chota Haridasa come here.’” (CC, Antya 2.113)

When Haridasa learned that the Lord's door was barred to him, he was deeply grieved, and no one around could understand
the reason for the order.[^7] For three days he ate nothing.[^8] Then Svarupa Damodara and the other close devotees came
to the Lord and asked what offence Haridasa had committed and why he was not allowed in, since he had been fasting for
three days. The Lord answered: <!-- F16 F17 F18 F19 -->

“‘A renunciant who converses with women — I cannot bear to see his face. The senses are hard to restrain and seize their
objects; even a woman made of wood steals the mind of a sage. “One should not sit in a secluded place even with one's
mother, sister or daughter, for the powerful senses drag away even a learned man.” Petty souls adopt a monkey's
renunciation and wander about, letting their senses graze and conversing with women.’” (CC, Antya 2.117–120)

The words about mother, sister and daughter the Lord quotes from the “Srimad-Bhagavatam” (9.19.17) and the
“Manu-samhita” (2.215).[^9] Having said this, Mahaprabhu went into His inner rooms so that the conversation would go no
further, and the devotees, seeing how stirred He was, fell silent.[^10] <!-- F20 F21 Q1 Q2 -->

The next day they all came to the Lord again to plead for Haridasa: his offence was small, he had learned his lesson and
would not do such a thing again. Srila Bhaktivinoda Thakura in the “Amrita-pravaha-bhashya” explains why the devotees
could call his fault slight: in begging rice from Madhavi, Haridasa wanted only to please the Lord by his service. For a
renunciant to converse with a woman is a grave fault, but it was done for the Lord's service, not for himself. The Lord
answered: <!-- F22 -->

“‘My mind is not under My control: it will not look upon a renunciant who converses with women. Go, all of you, to your
own duties and give up this useless talk. If you speak of it again, you will not see Me here.’” (CC, Antya 2.124–125)

This is no admission of weakness but a teacher's sternness: Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati in the “Anubhashya” calls
it a strictness harder than a thunderbolt. The Lord, the teacher of the world, was setting an example for sannyasis to
follow. Hearing such words, the devotees covered their ears with their hands and went off to their own duties, and
Mahaprabhu went to perform His midday duties. His actions are very hard to understand. <!-- F23 F24 -->

## Intercessor and Comforter
The next day the Vaishnavas went to Paramananda Puri, a disciple of Madhavendra Puri. The Lord honoured him deeply as a guru,
since he was a godbrother of Ishvara Puri, the Lord's guru, and the devotees hoped that Puri could soften the Lord. He
came to Mahaprabhu; the Lord bowed to him with reverence, seated him beside Himself and asked why he had come. Puri asked
Him to show grace to Haridasa.[^11] <!-- F25 -->

The Lord, displeased, replied: “Listen, Gosani: stay here with all the Vaishnavas, and give Me leave to go to
Alalanatha. I will stay there alone, with only Govinda.” Alalanatha is a secluded place a few miles from Puri. Saying
this, the Lord called Govinda, bowed to Puri and set off. Puri hurried to stand before Him, humbly persuaded Him to come
back into the room, and said:

“‘Do whatever You wish: You are the independent Lord. Who can tell You anything? All Your dealings are for the good of
the people. None of us can know Your deep heart.’” (CC, Antya 2.135–136)

So the Lord did not forgive Haridasa. And in the way the devotees turned to an elder for intercession, and in the way the
Lord honoured His guru's godbrother, the biographies see a lesson in honouring the guru and all who are close to him.[^12]
<!-- F26 I4 Q5 -->

When Puri had gone home, the Vaishnavas came to Haridasa's house. Svarupa Damodara said to him:

“‘Listen, Haridasa: we all wish you well — believe this. The Lord is the independent Supreme Lord, and He has become
unbending; but in time He will show you mercy, for His heart is compassionate. If you are stubborn, His stubbornness will
only grow. If you bathe and eat, His anger will pass of itself.’” (CC, Antya 2.138–140)

Haridasa obeyed: he bathed and ate, and Svarupa, comforting him with the hope of seeing the Lord's feet again, went
away. He who would not tolerate the slightest deviation from siddhanta always strove ardently to draw the Lord's mercy to
a devotee who had stumbled for a time; so he laboured for the Junior Haridasa too. <!-- F27 F28 -->

From then on Haridasa did not show himself to the Lord: when Mahaprabhu went to the temple to see Jagannatha, he only
watched Him from a distance. Kaviraja writes:

“Mahaprabhu is an ocean of mercy — who can understand Him? He punishes His own devotee to make people understand dharma.
Seeing this, all the devotees were struck with fear, and even in their dreams they gave up talking with women.” (CC,
Antya 2.143–144)

This refers, as Srila Bhaktivinoda Thakura explains, to sadhakas who wore the renunciant's garb. So a whole year
passed, but the Lord did not relent towards Haridasa. <!-- F29 F30 F31 -->

## At the Triveni
A year later, on the anniversary of his punishment, Haridasa left Puri:

“At the end of the night he offered prostrate obeisances to the Lord and went to Prayaga without saying anything to
anyone.” (CC, Antya 2.146)

There, where the Ganga meets the Yamuna and the Sarasvati, at the Triveni, Haridasa, firmly resolved to attain the
Lord's feet, entered the waters and gave up his body.[^13] Kaviraja continues:

“At that very moment he came to the Lord in a divine body; and, having received His mercy, he remained invisible. In the
body of a Gandharva he sang invisibly. At night he would let the Lord hear his songs, and no one else knew.” (CC, Antya
2.148–149)

This body is no phantom shell but a divine, non-material body given to him by the Lord's mercy, and in it he did what he
had done all his life: he sang for the Lord. Srila Bhaktisiddhanta writes in the “Anubhashya” that in his divine body
Haridasa served the Lord invisibly with kirtana. <!-- F32 F33 -->

Haridasa's departure cannot be measured by a worldly yardstick, nor taken as a pattern to follow. It was not suicide in
the worldly sense, nor the act of a man in despair: the “Charitamrita” speaks of a resolve to attain the Lord's feet; Svarupa, whose judgement the Lord
always accepted, flatly rejected the guess of suicide; and the Lord Himself, as we shall see, accepted His singer's
service. This is the pastime of an eternal associate, enacted by the Lord's will to teach the world, not a rule for the
sadhaka. <!-- F32 -->

## A Voice by the Sea
One day Mahaprabhu asked the devotees, “Where is Haridasa? Bring him here.” They answered that on the anniversary of his
punishment Haridasa had gone away in the night and no one knew where. Hearing this, the Lord only smiled a little, and
the devotees were filled with wonder. <!-- F34 -->

Another day Jagadananda, Svarupa, Govinda, Kashishvara, Shankara, Damodara and Mukunda went to bathe in the sea, and
some distance away they heard Haridasa singing, as if calling out at the top of his voice. They saw no singer; they only
heard his wondrous voice. And Govinda and the others began to guess: “Haridasa must have taken poison or something like
it and killed himself, and by that sin he has become a *brahma-rakshasa*. We see no form; we only hear his song” (CC, Antya 2.155–157).[^14]
Svarupa objected:

“‘This guess is false. From his birth he sang of Krishna and served the Lord; he was a recipient of the Lord's mercy,
and he left his body in a holy place. No evil end can be his; his end is a blessed one. This is the Lord's way — later
you will know it for certain.’” (CC, Antya 2.157–159)

By the holy place Svarupa means the Triveni at Prayaga.[^15] His judgements were always sound, and the Lord accepted
them. <!-- F35 F36 F37 -->

## “A Man Reaps the Fruit of His Own Deeds”
Soon a Vaishnava came from Prayaga to Navadvipa and told about Haridasa: with what resolve he had entered the Triveni and
how he had carried out his purpose. Srivasa Pandita and the other devotees were amazed. The next year, as usual, the
Bengali devotees led by Shivananda Sena came to the Lord in Puri, and Srivasa asked where Haridasa was. Mahaprabhu
answered:

“‘A man reaps the fruit of his own deeds.’” (CC, Antya 2.163)

In Bengali the Lord said this in the words of scripture: *sva-karma-phala-bhuk puman*, “a man enjoys the fruit of his
own deeds.” <!-- F38 F39 F40 Q4 -->

Then Srivasa told all that he had heard. The Lord smiled and, His heart well pleased, said: “For one who looks at women,
this is the atonement” (CC, Antya 2.165). These are the words of the Lord as the guru of the world, Who, as Srila
Bhaktisiddhanta says, is establishing a rule. Bhaktivinoda Thakura explains to whom it is addressed: to a sadhaka who
wears the renunciant's garb and yet deliberately looks at women — by leaving his body at the Triveni, such a person will
in future become faultless. This is not a prescription to be imitated: the Lord shows how grave such a fault is for a
renunciant. And the Lord rejoiced not at Haridasa's death but at his destiny: by the power of the Triveni His singer had
come to Him. <!-- F41 -->

The devotees came to the same conclusion:

“Then Svarupa and the others came together and reflected: by the power of the Triveni, Haridasa had come to the Lord.”
(CC, Antya 2.166)

Srila Bhaktisiddhanta sees in this the fruit of leaving the body at the Triveni and other holy places of Vishnu; one of
the biographies adds that thus the Junior Haridasa attained the position of Mahaprabhu's eternal associate.[^16]

## The Lesson
Krishnadasa Kaviraja briefly mentions this story twice more: in the summary of the Lord's pastimes — “Then the Lord
punished Chota Haridasa, and Damodara Pandita ‘punished’ the Lord with words” (CC, Madhya 1.259) — and in the summary of
the Antya-lila: the second chapter, he says, tells of “the lesson the Lord gave Chota Haridasa” (CC, Antya 20.105).
Hence the title of the chapter itself: “The Lesson Taught by the Punishment of Sri Haridasa.” <!-- F45 -->

Mahaprabhu's severity is neither cruelty nor worldly anger. Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati explains in the
“Anubhashya”: the Lord is an ocean of mercy, and He punished His devotees to show people what true devotion is, how high
the Vaishnavas stand above the three modes of nature, and how far from pure Vaishnava dharma are the customs of the
pseudo-Vaishnavas to come. Madhavi-devi was a maha-bhagavata, and for the Lord's associate to accept rice from her was
not forbidden; there is no fault in her at all, and in Haridasa's request there was not a shadow of worldly feeling. But
the Lord foresaw that deceivers, citing such an example, would pass off prakrita-sahajiya as Vaishnava dharma. Therefore
He, the teacher of the world, enacted the pastime of a beloved associate's severe punishment and, knowing the weakness of
the souls of the age of Kali, gave so harsh an order. In this severity lies His highest mercy. <!-- I2 -->

“In this pastime the Lord showed His compassion, taught the world renunciation and revealed the deep love of His own
devotee. He showed the glory of the holy place, and He made His devotee His own. Through a single pastime the Lord
accomplishes five or seven purposes.” (CC, Antya 2.168–169)

Following these verses and the “Anubhashya,” one of the biographies lists the fruits of this pastime: the Lord revealed
His boundless mercy, taught the value of true renunciation, showed His devotees' deep attachment to Him and the glory of
the holy place, and accepted His devotees as His own. He also showed how faultless a Vaishnava's life and character must
be, and how poisonous is the fruit of even the subtlest touch of worldly attachment: one who wishes to become His
devotee must give up worldly enjoyment to the very last trace. Outwardly rejecting Haridasa to teach the people, the Lord
nevertheless accepted the service — the kirtana — of this pure devotee. <!-- I3 -->

The Junior Haridasa was a pure and supremely saintly devotee of Mahaprabhu. He sang for the Lord in his life, and he
sang for Him afterwards, when no one but the Lord could hear him; and his very story became the lesson Mahaprabhu taught
all who seek pure devotion. <!-- F43 -->
