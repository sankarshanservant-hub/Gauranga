# Srila Bhakti Pramoda Puri Goswami Maharaj
*Srila Bhakti Pramoda Puri Goswami Maharaj · @bhakti-pramoda-puri*

## A Companion of Srila Bhaktisiddhanta
Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati gathered great devotees around him, and one of the biographies compares that circle
to the associates of Sri Chaitanya in the sixteenth century. One of them was Srila Bhakti Pramoda Puri Goswami
Maharaj. His life is inseparable from the Gaudiya Math: he had no aim but to please his guru and Mahaprabhu.

## Appearance and Youth
According to one biography, he appeared on 8 October 1898 in the village of Ganganandapur in Jessore district (now in
Bangladesh)[^1]. His father was Tarini Charan Chakravarti, his mother Srimati Ram Rangini Devi, and his worldly name
was Pramod Bhushan Chakravarti. <!-- F1 F2 -->

While still a boy he met the one who first showed him the way — his vartma-pradarshaka-guru, Srila Bhakti Ratna
Thakura, a godbrother and shiksha-disciple of Srila Bhaktivinoda Thakura. Through him he came to know Srila
Bhaktivinoda’s journal “Sajjana-toshani”, and then the “Sri Chaitanya-charitamrita”, the “Sri Chaitanya-bhagavata”
and the “Srimad-Bhagavatam”; from him, too, he first heard of his future guru — Srila Bhaktisiddhanta. <!-- F3 F4 -->

## At the Feet of Srila Bhaktisiddhanta
He was still a university student when in 1915 he first came to Srila Bhaktisiddhanta at Yogapitha in Mayapur — on
the day after the departure of Srila Gaurakishora Dasa Babaji. As he bowed before him for the first time, he
recognised his guru in his heart, and later he often told of that meeting. On Janmashtami 1923 Srila Bhaktisiddhanta
gave him harinama and mantra-diksha and the name Pranavananda Brahmachari. <!-- F5 F6 -->

For thirteen years he stayed near his guru and took down his lectures and talks; most of Srila Bhaktisiddhanta’s
words that have come down to us are transcripts of his notes. At his guru’s instruction he wrote articles for the
journal “Gaudiya”, and for seven years was its proofreader and one of its chief editors. In 1926 he was entrusted with
the “Dainika Nadiya Prakasha”, the world’s only daily Vaishnava newspaper; for two years he ran it, printing his
guru’s daily talks and the articles of other writers. <!-- F7 F8 F9 -->

Srila Bhaktisiddhanta gave him the titles “maha-mahopadeshaka”, great teacher, and “pratna-vidyalankara”, keeper of
the wisdom of the ancient scriptures, and said of him: he loves his guru, and his life is one with his words. The key
to success in bhakti is to listen attentively to the guru; “lend me your ears”, Srila Bhaktisiddhanta would ask, and
Pranavananda followed this in everything. He ascribed all his success to his guru’s mercy, and one disciple said of
him that he set forth the whole siddhanta of Srila Bhaktisiddhanta without words. <!-- F10 F11 I3 I4 -->

## After His Guru’s Departure
When Srila Bhaktisiddhanta left this world[^2], he continued preaching through the “Gaudiya” — first from the Bagbazar
Gaudiya Math, then from the Sri Chaitanya Math in Mayapur. In 1942 his guru appeared to him in a dream, gave him the
sannyasa mantra and told him to accept renunciation. In August 1946, at Champahati, he took tridandi-sannyasa from
his godbrother Srila Bhakti Gaurava Vaikhanasa Maharaj, and then travelled across India with Srila Bhakti Hridaya Bon
and Srila Bhakti Dayita Madhava. <!-- F12 F14 F15 -->

At the request of his godbrother Bhakti Vilasa Tirtha he served for seven years as chief pujari of the Yogapitha
temple, the birthplace of Sri Chaitanya. In the 1950s he chose solitary worship and settled in a humble hut on the
bank of the Ganges at Ambika-Kalna. The Maharaja of Burdwan, moved by his austerity, gave him the ancient temple of
Ananta-Vasudeva in Kalna on Radhashtami 1958. <!-- F16 F17 F18 -->

In 1964 Srila Bhakti Dayita Madhava invited him to head the editorial board of the journal “Sri Chaitanya Vani”, and
for thirty-three years he served as chairman of its editorial board. <!-- F13 -->

## Sri Gopinath Gaudiya Math
In 1989, in his ninety-first year, he founded the Sri Gopinath Gaudiya Math at Ishodyan in Mayapur — for the service
of Sri Sri Gaura-Gadadhara, Jagannatha, Radha-Gopinatha and Lakshmi-Nrisimha; his temples in Puri, Vrindavan, Kolkata
and Medinipur followed. In 1995 he was elected president of the World Vaishnava Association. For his delicacy and
precision in worship he was often called to be chief priest at Deity installations and at the ceremonies of the
Gaudiya Math; it was he who performed the samadhi rite of Srila Bhakti Dayita Madhava on 27 February 1979.

## Word and Pen
For more than sixty years he wrote on Vaishnava philosophy and theology — hundreds of poems, essays, stories, diary
pages, editorials and letters. In his foreword to the Sri Chaitanya Gaudiya Math edition of the “Sri
Chaitanya-charitamrita” he writes about Krishnadasa Kaviraja and retells a tradition about Vishvanatha Chakravarti.
Vishvanatha could not understand why Kaviraja’s kama-gayatri has twenty-four and a half syllables, and vowed to die at
Radha-kunda; but Srimati Radhika told him in a dream that Krishnadasa is Her friend and writes correctly: according to
the “Varnagama-bhasvata”, the syllable “ya” before “vi” counts as a half. <!-- F22 F23 -->

In the collection “Sri Sarasvati-jayashri”, published by the Bagbazar Gaudiya Math in 1948, appeared his article on the
works of the vaishnava-sarvabhauma Srila Jagannatha Dasa Babaji. Of Srila Sridhar Maharaj’s “Gayatri-nigudhartha” he
wrote that this explanation of the gayatri as centred on Srimati Radharani was revealed to its author by the mercy of
Srila Bhaktisiddhanta, and that it is the best path to Krishna’s mercy. <!-- F24 F25 -->

He was shiksha-guru and editor to Srila Bhakti Ballabha Tirtha Maharaj when the latter prepared the English edition
of his biographies of the Lord’s associates: he inspired him, edited the text and here and there added new
information; the book is dedicated to him as well. In his foreword, dated 9 September 1992, he recalls that Srila
Bhaktisiddhanta founded the Sri Chaitanya Math in Mayapur, and Srila Bhakti Dayita Madhava the Sri Chaitanya Gaudiya
Math, leaving Bhakti Ballabha Tirtha as his successor; he praises the author, who even at seventy[^3] edits a journal,
writes books and manages the maths, and especially thanks his dear Bhakti Varidhi Parivrajaka Maharaj, who printed
the Bengali edition. The lives of devotees soften the heart, he writes, and the Lord’s mercy comes in the wake of the
Vaishnavas’ mercy; so it is good to read them on the appearance and departure days of the associates. <!-- F26 F27 I5 -->

He supports his thought with words from the “Sri Chaitanya-charitamrita”, the “Sri Chaitanya-bhagavata” and the
“Srimad-Bhagavatam”, and with songs of Srila Bhaktivinoda; among them are the Lord’s words: “Know the acharya to be
Me”. (Srimad-Bhagavatam, 11.17.27) <!-- Q1 -->

## Godbrothers and Senior Vaishnavas
He regarded Srila Sridhar Maharaj as his shiksha-guru. His close companions throughout life were Srila B. R. Sridhar
Maharaj, Bhakti Prajnana Keshava and Akinchana Krishnadasa Babaji; he was known for his love for his godbrothers and
his gift for glorifying them. Srila Bhakti Dayita Madhava, in one of his lectures, recalls his stories about Srila
Gaurakishora Dasa Babaji. Srila Bhakti Ballabha Tirtha received his blessings and association and, from 1997, at his
request, has travelled the world preaching. <!-- F28 F29 F31 F32 -->

He had seen Vamshidasa Babaji in person and told stories about him. Once, at night, a thief carried off the utensils
from Babaji’s hut, and in the morning a cow ate the fruit set aside for the Deities; Babaji only laughed and clapped
his hands: one thief gives, another takes away. Babaji did not let anyone touch his feet, but on the day after
Phalguna Purnima, in ecstasy, he allowed it — and so he received the dust of his feet, and another time his prasad
remnants. He also told an incident connected with Srila Bhaktivinoda’s song “tyajiya shayana-sukha…”, and of the ban
on invented “mantras”. <!-- F30 -->

## His Nature
He rested little: even in old age he wrote at night and chanted the Holy Name, and his servants always found him
awake in the morning. A knower of the scriptures, he also looked after the printing press and sang kirtan beautifully.
Humble and simple, he thought little of himself and made everyone feel needed and valued. <!-- F33 F34 F35 -->

## Departure
Before his departure he appointed his close disciple, Sripad Bhakti Bibudha Bodhayan Maharaj, as his successor and
president-acharya of the Sri Gopinath Gaudiya Math. According to the biography, Srila Bhakti Pramoda Puri Goswami
Maharaj left this world in the predawn hours of Narayana Chaturdashi, the day before Rasa Purnima, on 21 October
1999[^1]. His body was brought from Jagannatha Puri to the Gopinath Gaudiya Math at Ishodyan and laid in samadhi.
