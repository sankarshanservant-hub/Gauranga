# Swami Bhakti Ballabha Tirtha Maharaj
*Swami Bhakti Ballabha Tirtha Maharaj · @bhakti-ballabha-tirtha*

## Appearance and Youth
Srila Bhakti Ballabha Tirtha Maharaj was a disciple and the successor of Srila Bhakti Dayita Madhava Goswami Maharaj,
and acharya of the Sri Chaitanya Gaudiya Math. He appeared in 1924 in Assam, on Rama Navami, the day on which
Lord Ramachandra appeared[^1]. His worldly name was Kamakhya Charan Guha-Ray, his father’s Dhirendra Guha-Ray; at initiation
he became Krishna Ballabha Brahmachari, and on accepting sannyasa, Tridandi Swami Bhakti Ballabha Tirtha Maharaj.
<!-- F1 F2 -->

He grew up in a pious setting; his search for truth led him to Calcutta University and to philosophy, and in 1947 he
took his master’s degree in philosophy. <!-- F3 -->

## Meeting His Guru
That same year, 1947, in Goalpara in Assam, in the house of Radha Mohan, he first saw Srila Bhakti Dayita Madhava,
and the sadhu’s divine and powerful presence drew him at once. He came with a friend, Devavrata (Rabi), with questions
about the path to the Lord; when he bowed before the sadhu for the first time, his hair stood on end — he felt
blessed. He confessed that while chanting the Name he felt he might see the Lord at any moment — and would then have
to leave home and those he loved; this worry made him stop chanting. The guru praised the question to encourage him
and answered with the parable of the geese and the swan. <!-- F4 -->

Radha Mohan — “Ram Mohan-da”, a disciple of Srila Bhaktisiddhanta who worked as a clerk for the young man’s uncle —
became his vartma-pradarshaka-guru: he brought him to the Gaudiya Math and bore opposition and reproach for it.
Gurudeva’s letters to Kamakhya were sent care of Radha Mohan, and Radha Mohan’s kind wife and whole family did much
for him. On his guru’s advice he read Srila Bhaktivinoda’s “Jaiva-dharma”, and his doubts dispersed. The guru wrote
to him: on the path of nivritti government service is not needed; on the path of pravritti, take it. Understanding
that pure service was impossible at home, he resolved to leave home. <!-- F5 F6 -->

## Serving His Guru
From then on he devoted himself wholly to serving his guru, and soon became secretary of the Sri Chaitanya
Gaudiya Math, which had more than twenty ashrams in India. In 1961 he accepted sannyasa, becoming one of Srila Bhakti
Dayita Madhava’s first sannyasi disciples. Shortly before 1965 Srila A. C. Bhaktivedanta Swami asked him to come with
him to America; but he was serving his guru as secretary of the math and humbly declined. <!-- F7 F8 F9 -->

The journal “Sri Chaitanya Vani” printed an eyewitness account, by a disciple of Srila Bhakti Dayita Madhava, of the
first festival at Jashra after the temple of Jagadisha Pandita passed to their guru: thousands were fed with
mahaprasad, and the narrator admits that he still cannot recall that day without a thrill. The biography does not
name him; perhaps it was Tirtha Maharaj himself, the journal’s editor. From his guru’s mother he heard stories of his guru’s childhood. On 30 December 1978 he
asked Gurudeva to give instructions to a Western devotee from Chandigarh. In those last instructions the guru said
that Tirtha Maharaj could not always be on the spot, and so entrusted the management of the math to Jagamohan
Prabhu. <!-- F10 F11 F12 F13 -->

## Acharya of the Sri Chaitanya Gaudiya Math
By a letter of 12 December 1976 Srila Bhakti Dayita Madhava appointed him president and acharya of the Sri Chaitanya
Gaudiya Math after himself, and on 30 December 1978, after consulting his senior godbrothers, again announced him as
his successor. After his guru’s departure in 1979 he became acharya of the math. <!-- F14 F15 -->

He was blessed by, and closely associated with, many prominent disciples of Srila Bhaktisiddhanta — Srila Bhakti
Pramoda Puri, Srila Bhakti Hridaya Bon, Srila B. R. Sridhar Maharaj, Akinchana Krishnadasa Babaji and others. He named
Srila Bhakti Pramoda Puri Maharaj as his shiksha-guru, and honoured all the disciples of Srila Bhaktisiddhanta as
shiksha-gurus too. <!-- F16 F17 -->

## Preaching
For some five decades he preached the teaching of Sri Chaitanya — against violence and cruelty, for the unity of
hearts beyond differences of race, creed and religion. From 1997, at Srila Bhakti Pramoda Puri’s request, he
spent several months a year travelling the world and preached in Great Britain, Holland, France, Spain, Italy,
Austria, Germany, Slovenia, Russia, Ukraine, Australia, Singapore, Malaysia, Indonesia, Hawaii and throughout the
United States. <!-- F18 F19 -->

In 1997 in New York he spoke at the World Peace Prayer Society at the United Nations and at the World Conference on
Religion and Peace, setting out a path to peace on Vedic principles. He held conversations with theologians — Catholic and Protestant,
Jewish, Hindu and Baha’i — — at universities, interfaith gatherings, churches, temples and centres; he spoke
on radio and television, and in 2000 gave an interview to the BBC that was broadcast worldwide. In 1997 he founded
GOKUL — the Global Organization of Krishnachaitanya’s Universal Love — and became its acharya; he was also
vice-president of the World Vaishnava Association. <!-- F20 F21 F22 F23 -->

He worked eighteen hours a day, tirelessly bringing people Vedic wisdom and the teaching of bhakti-yoga. Srila Bhakti Pramoda Puri Maharaj wrote in 1992 that even at seventy[^2] he edited a
journal, wrote books and managed the Chaitanya Gaudiya Maths throughout India, “from the Himalayas to Cape Comorin”;
in his tirelessness Puri Maharaj saw a sign of mercy from the Lord, from the guru and from the Vaishnavas, and prayed for his
long life. People were drawn by his gentle and loving nature, his firm faith in guru, Vaishnavas and Bhagavan, his
extraordinary kirtan and his heartfelt talks. <!-- F24 F31 I2 I1 -->

## Writings
In Bengali he composed a commentary on Dhruva’s life, a Vraja-parikrama guidebook, an account of the rules of Deity
worship, and a two-volume life of his guru. He edited the Sri Chaitanya Gaudiya Math’s monthly “Sri Chaitanya Vani”
(its chief editor and president was Srila Bhakti Pramoda Puri); there the life of his guru and his sketches of the
Lord’s associates appeared in instalments, the latter later coming out in Bengali as two volumes. The Bengali edition
was printed by Bhakti Varidhi Parivrajaka Maharaj; Srila Bhakti Pramoda Puri edited the text and added information. He
dedicated the book to his “beloved gurudeva”, Srila Bhakti Dayita Madhava, and to his “deeply revered shiksha-guru”,
Srila Bhakti Pramoda Puri, who also inspired the English edition, for devotees everywhere. He wrote articles and books
in English as well as in his mother tongues: in English appeared his books “Suddha Bhakti”, “Sages of Ancient India” and
“A Taste of Transcendence”, and in 2001 the English edition of those same biographies (on the title page, “Swami
B. B. Tirtha”). <!-- F25 F26 F28 F29 F31 F27 -->

In his foreword to these biographies Srila Bhakti Pramoda Puri wrote that he had collected information on the devotees’ lives from many
sources and made it sweeter still by his own insight; he tells with delight of Jagannatha Mishra, of Madhavendra Puri and
Ishvara Puri, of Advaita Acharya, Shrivasa Pandita, Chandrashekhara Acharya, Pundarika Vidyanidhi, Gadadhara Pandita,
Vakreshvara Pandita, Gadadhara Dasa, Shivananda Sena, Paramananda Puri, Murari Gupta and a great many others, and in these
stories, Puri Maharaj says, every letter is soaked in devotion’s nectar. Srila Bhaktisiddhanta called the
printing press the “brihat-mridanga”, the great drum; and Puri Maharaj wrote that Bhakti Varidhi Parivrajaka Maharaj
and Tirtha Maharaj, by giving book publishing a large place in the math’s service, win the affection of their gurus
and of all pure devotees — those of the past and those yet to come. <!-- F32 F33 -->

In his introduction to them he writes that the articles were gathered into one book in the hope of helping devotees
relish the deeds of the Lord’s associates. Mahaprabhu’s associates are transcendental and are understood only by
their mercy; to one who has not surrendered to the Lord these mysteries are closed, and even among the surrendered
the capacity to see the devotees’ glory varies. A soul covered by anarthas cannot grasp the glory of Vishnu and His
devotees, and its praise turns into disrespect; yet if one glorifies them humbly and prayerfully, aware of one’s
own unworthiness, the Vaishnavas will forgive the flaws, and their pleased glance frees one from anarthas. A pure
devotee is rare, and to meet one is great good fortune. He recalls the words of the “Sri Chaitanya-charitamrita”:
“Whoever constantly remembers the Guru, God and the holy Vaishnavas will easily attain the fulfilment of his innermost
desires and overcome every difficulty and obstacle”. (CC, Adi 1.21) And further: every perfection comes by the
Vaishnavas’ mercy, and a soul that glorifies the qualities of devotees is — as he heard from his guru and from
sadhus — liberated. <!-- I3 I5 Q1 -->

The Six Goswamis, he recalls, bowed to the Vaishnavas thousands of times each day, and every day one should give time
to remembering the Vaishnavas; an important part of service is to observe their days of appearance and departure — to
remember them, to pray for their mercy and to sing their glories in kirtan. The Sri Chaitanya Gaudiya Math publishes a
Vaishnava calendar with these days every year, and his book is meant to help those who remember and glorify the
Vaishnavas, though for many associates neither the tithi, nor the year, nor the birthplace, nor the parents’ names are
known. We glorify them to be rid of all that leads away from service and to gain attachment to Krishna; and even if
we see no change in ourselves, the Vaishnavas’ feet remain the fallen souls’ sole shelter. He allows that some
readers may find the book’s language not poetic enough, thanks Srila Bhakti Pramoda Puri, whose blessings encouraged
him in this service, and all who helped, and at the end bows to his Gurudeva, “who purifies the fallen”, to his
parama-gurudeva Srila Bhaktisiddhanta, to his shiksha-guru Srila Bhakti Pramoda Puri and to all the disciples of
Srila Bhaktisiddhanta, signing himself “servant of the servants of the Vaishnavas”. <!-- F30 I4 F34 -->

[^1]: Our calendar (Sri Chaitanya Saraswat Math) does not list his appearance day; the date follows one biography.
[^2]: According to the same biography he was born in 1924, so in 1992 he was sixty-eight.
