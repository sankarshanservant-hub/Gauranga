# Shachi Devi
*Шачи-деви (Шачимата) · @shachi*

Shachi Devi is the mother of Sri Chaitanya Mahaprabhu, the Lord's eternal mother. In Vraja she is called Yashoda, the
queen of Vraja; in Navadvipa she became the one at whose breast the Lord fell asleep, whom He obeyed and before whom He
was wilful, like any child. Krishnadasa Kaviraja says it in a single line:

“Thus I have briefly told of the descent of Lord Chaitanya. As Krishna became the son of Yashoda, so Sri Chaitanya
became the son of Shachidevi.” (CC, Adi 14.3)

The fullest account of her life is given by Vrindavana Dasa Thakura in the “Chaitanya-bhagavata”; much is added by the
“Chaitanya-charitamrita”, by the Sanskrit life written by Murari Gupta and by Lochana Dasa's “Chaitanya-mangala”.

## Daughter of Nilambara Chakravarti
Shachi was the daughter of Nilambara Chakravarti, a learned brahmana well versed in the science of the stars. Her
husband was Jagannatha Mishra, who had come from Shrihatta, settled in Navadvipa and was known by the title Purandara.
Krishnadasa Kaviraja introduces her thus:

“His wife, Srimati Shachidevi, was a chaste woman, wholly devoted to her husband. Her father was Nilambara
Chakravarti.” (CC, Adi 13.60) <!-- F1 F2 F172 Q3 -->

Shachi had a sister, Sarvajaya, the wife of Chandrashekhara Acharyaratna, who was thus the Lord's uncle on His
mother's side. Even before Mahaprabhu's appearance Chandrashekhara and his wife were constant visitors at Mishra's
house and helped to run the household.[^1] Other dear ones lived close by. Ratnavati, the mother of Gadadhara Pandita,
looked upon Shachi as an elder sister, and the two were hardly ever apart. Malini, Srivasa's wife, was Shachi's
inseparable friend: they were always happy together, and when the time of the birth came, Malini tended Shachi like the
most devoted of nurses. <!-- F46 F67 F93 -->

Shachi did not come into the world by chance. Before appearing Himself, the Lord sends down to the earth those whom He
will honour as His parents and elders, and Krishnadasa Kaviraja names her among them twice:

“Madhavendra Puri, Ishvara Puri, Srimati Shachimata and Jagannatha Mishra appeared together with Advaita Acharya.”
(CC, Adi 3.94)

“Sri Shachidevi and Jagannatha Mishra, Sri Madhavendra Puri, Keshava Bharati, Ishvara Puri, Advaita Acharya, Srivasa
Pandita, Acharyaratna, Vidyanidhi and Thakura Haridasa.” (CC, Adi 13.54–55) <!-- F25 F172 -->

The first years of the marriage were years of loss. One after another eight daughters were born to Shachi, and not one
of them lived:

“Before the appearance of Sri Chaitanya eight daughters were born to Shachi and Jagannatha Mishra. But they died
immediately after birth.” (CC, Adi 13.72)

Jagannatha began to worship Vishnu, praying for a son, and Vishvarupa was born — a manifestation of Baladeva, the
Sankarshana of the spiritual world (CC, Adi 13.73–74). The boy was beautiful, detached from worldly things from early
childhood, and while still a child grasped the meaning of the scriptures; His parents could not look at Him enough (CB,
Adi 2.140–142). So in the house where the Lord was to appear, His elder brother came to live before Him.

## Mother of the Universe
Vrindavana Dasa, beginning his account of the Lord's appearance, names her chief quality:

“His faithful wife, Srimati Shachi Devi, was the embodiment of pure devotion to the Supreme Lord. She is the Mother of
the universe.” (CB, Adi 2.139)

Jagannatha Mishra and Shachi Devi are eternal companions of Krishna, never bound by this world. Their hearts and bodies
are of pure goodness, shuddha-sattva, which is called “vasudeva”: in it alone does the Lord appear. Vrindavana Dasa
says that Shachi beheld what is hidden from others because “the Vedas call her the embodiment of service to Lord
Vishnu” (CB, Adi 12.230), and he calls her tenderly “Ai”, “mother”: if anyone merely utters this word, it frees him from
all suffering (CB, Madhya 13.374). <!-- F5 F123 F142 F154 -->

Who she is in the eternal lila is told by Krishnadasa Kaviraja and by Kavi Karnapura:

“The king of Vraja, Nanda Maharaja, has now become the father of Sri Chaitanya, and Yashoda, the queen of Vraja, has
become His mother, Sri Shachidevi.” (CC, Adi 17.294)

“Yashoda and the king of Vraja, Nanda, who once in Vrindavana were mines of the rasa of love, became Shachi and
Jagannatha Purandara — of this there is no doubt. Into them also entered the divine Aditi and Kashyapa, Sri Kaushalya
and Dasharatha, and Sri Prishni and her husband.” (GGD 37–38)

Thus all who in earlier descents had been mothers of God were united in Shachi;[^2] yet she herself is Yashoda, the very
essence of motherly love. In this lies the secret of her life. The Mother of the universe fears for her Son, scolds Him,
runs after Him with a stick, weeps for Him and does not remember His majesty — not because maya has power over her: it
is yogamaya, the Lord's internal potency, that veils His majesty so that nothing may constrain pure parental love,
vatsalya. Only His eternal associate can treat the Lord as a son; the Lord Himself, sitting in her arms, is never under
the sway of maya, and neither is His mother. <!-- F7 F8 Q1 Q2 I1 -->

## The Lord in Shachi's Womb
In the month of Magha of the year 1406 of the Shaka era (January 1485) the Lord Himself entered the bodies of Shachi
and Jagannatha (CC, Adi 13.80). The couple were the first to notice the change:

“One day Jagannatha Mishra said to Shachidevi: ‘I have witnessed an astonishing wonder! Your form radiates such
effulgence, as if the goddess Lakshmi herself had come to live in our house. Wherever I go, people show me respect and
of their own accord give me wealth, clothes, rice and much else.’ Shachidevi replied: ‘I too see in the sky the forms
of divine beings offering prayers.’” (CC, Adi 13.81–83) <!-- F172 -->

The “Chaitanya-bhagavata” relates that when the Lord entered Shachi and Jagannatha, the couple heard Ananta's cries of
“Victory! Victory!” and thought they were dreaming. Brahma and the other gods, remaining unseen, came every day to
glorify Him who, though the shelter of countless universes, had entered Shachi's womb, and they bowed to her house (CB,
Adi 2.144–154, 193–195). Murari Gupta gives their hymn, addressed to her herself: “We bow to you who ever bear Him in
your womb! You are Aditi, the mother of Hari… You bear in your womb Him who will spread the holiest of sacrifices —
kirtana” (“Kadacha” 1.5.8–10). <!-- F123 F202 -->

Lochana Dasa adds that day by day Shachi's body shone more brightly and people wondered: “We do not know who has come
to Shachi's house.” In the sixth month Advaita Acharya came to them. Shachi brought Him water to wash His feet, and He,
with tears in His eyes, walked around her and bowed down to her; Shachi started at such a breach of custom, the couple
asked in bewilderment what it meant, and Jagannatha received only the answer: “Later you will know everything.” The
gods came too; Shachi felt their presence and rejoiced, though at times she was frightened to see their host. And her
heart in those days became like this:

“Shachi's heart was filled with compassion for all beings: she is kind [to all] as to herself, not distinguishing her
own from others.” (CM, Adi-khanda, part 1, v. 46) <!-- F72 F73 F186 -->

The Lord remained in her womb until the full moon of the month of Phalguna (CB, Adi 2.195). On the full moon of
Phalguna in the year 1407 of the Shaka era (February 1486), at dusk, during an eclipse of the moon, when all over Nadia
people were singing the holy names on the pretext of the eclipse, Gaurachandra rose from the ocean of her womb like a
golden moon. Thus in Mayapur the Lord accepted Jagannatha and Shachi as His father and mother. The gods, in the guise
of ordinary people, came to Shachi's house and sang the names of Hari aloud (CB, Adi 2.226–233). In Lochana Dasa,
Shachi, beside herself with happiness, beckons to her husband:

“‘Our life has borne fruit — look at our son's face!’” (CM, Adi-khanda, part 1, v. 55)

Seeing the Child's face, both forgot themselves for joy. Nilambara Chakravarti hurried to his son-in-law's house, cast
his grandson's horoscope and, turning to Shachi, said that the Child would deliver both families for ever — the
father's and the mother's; hearing her father's words, she was filled with joy (“Kadacha” 1.5.23–28). Gods and men
hastened to Mishra's house, and the house became Vaikuntha. For eight days, as Bengali custom required, mother and
child did not leave the birth chamber, and on the ninth a festival was held. Narahari sings of those days: happy Shachi
holds her son on her lap, and crowds stream in to see Him. <!-- F6 F9 F74 F123 F186 F202 Q9 -->

Heavenly women also came to Shachi's house. Aditi, the mother of the gods, touched the Child's head with rice and durva
grass and wished Him long life, and other goddesses were with her:

“Srimati Shachi Devi and the assembled guests were astonished to see the unearthly beauty of the goddesses, but did not
dare to ask the strangers who they were and where they came from. The goddesses reverently took the dust from Shachi
Devi's feet, and mother Shachi lost the power of speech for happiness.” (CB, Adi 3.37–38) <!-- F124 -->

Malini did not come alone — with her was Chandrashekhara's wife, Shachi's sister:

“Malini, the wife of Srivasa Thakura, came with the wife of Chandrashekhara and many other women. With great joy they
honoured the infant, offering Him vermilion, turmeric, oil, puffed rice, coconuts, bananas and other fruits.”
(CC, Adi 13.110) <!-- F110 F173 -->

Soon, with Advaita's consent, His wife Sita Thakurani came from Shantipura in a covered palanquin with rich gifts.
Entering Shachi's house, she was amazed: the Child was exactly like Krishna of Gokula, differing only in colour — and
her heart melted with motherly love (CC, Adi 13.111–116). When mother and son first left the birth chamber, Shachi went
with the women to the Ganga, worshipped the Ganga and the goddess Shashthi and, as custom required, gave the women
puffed rice, bananas, oil, kumkum and betel, and they bowed down to her (CB, Adi 4.18–22). On that day Sita presented
mother and child with clothes and ornaments, Shachi and Jagannatha honoured her, and she went home happy.
<!-- F88 F125 F173 -->

From then on Sita often came to Mayapur and taught Shachi how to bring up the Child; on His birthday the parents gave
her the honour of being the first to offer Him new clothes. Sita and Shachi were of one heart — Nimai was their life,
and day after day Sita helped to bring Him up. The Lord always treated Sita with the same reverence and love as His own
mother, and therefore Vaishnavas honour Sita equally with Shachi, as a mother of the universe. Krishnadasa Kaviraja sums
up those days:

“Thus Shachimata and Jagannatha Mishra obtained a son who was the husband of Lakshmi herself. All their desires were
fulfilled, and from then on their house was always full of wealth and every kind of provision. And as they looked at
their beloved child, Nimai, their happiness grew day by day.” (CC, Adi 13.119) <!-- F89 F90 F92 F87 F173 -->

## The Name “Nimai”
When the time came for the name-giving, the pandits led by Nilambara Chakravarti, friends and relatives gathered in
Mishra's house. Opinions were divided: the pandits proposed one name, the venerable women another:

“[The women said:] ‘Since you have lost many daughters, let this child of yours bear the name Nimai.’” (CB, Adi 4.45)

The pandits reasoned that with the Child's birth the drought had given way to long-awaited rain and the whole world had
found prosperity, and they named Him Vishvambhara, “He who sustains the universe”; His horoscope too pointed to this
name. But the women's voice was not set aside:

“Let His second name be ‘Nimai’, as the venerable women advised.” (CB, Adi 4.50)

The nim is a bitter tree; witches and evil spirits, as people in Bengal believe, dare not approach it, and the
bitterness of its leaves drives death away. So the mother who had lost eight daughters, and the women around her,
protected the Child and wished Him long life.[^3] <!-- F126 F10 F88 Q13 -->

## The Infant in His Mother's Arms
Shachi nursed the Child herself (CC, Adi 14.10). And already at four months He began His pranks: as soon as His mother
went out, rice, grain and peas lay scattered all over the house and the pots of milk and yoghurt were broken; but the
moment He sensed her coming, He climbed into His cot and began to cry. Anxious Shachi soothed Him by singing the names
of Hari and only then noticed the havoc. No one could suspect the Child: they decided that an evil spirit had crept into
the house, but the protective mantras had kept it from harming the Child, and in its vexation it had smashed the pots.
The losses were considerable, but as soon as the parents looked at their son's face they forgot everything (CB, Adi
4.32–40). There were many wonders in the house, but the parents, fearing for the Child, told no one about them.
<!-- F125 F11 F112 F174 -->

Once a snake crawled into the house, and the Child caught hold of it, lay down on its coils and smiled serenely. The
grown-ups shouted, “Garuda! Garuda!” — “and the Lord's parents wept with fear” (CB, Adi 4.70). The snake — it was
Ananta Himself — crawled away, while the Boy kept trying to catch it again; the women snatched Him up and wished Him
long life.[^4] When He crawled about everywhere, Shachi was often frightened, thinking that His reddish soles were
bleeding. The couple were poor, but unearthly happiness never left their hearts (CB, Adi 4.82–83). <!-- F127 F12 F128 -->

Once Jagannatha asked his son to bring him his book from the house. Nimai ran off — and a gentle tinkling of ankle-bells
was heard, though He wore no bells. And when the couple went into the house, they saw everywhere the prints of small
feet marked with the flag, the thunderbolt, the goad and the pennant. Their hair stood on end and tears poured from
their eyes:

“Bowing down to these footprints, they said: ‘We are liberated! We shall not have to be born again.’” (CB, Adi 5.11)

Yet love would not let them admit that these were their son's footprints: Jagannatha decided that their household
Deity, the Shalagrama Damodara, was walking about the house, asked “the mother of Vishvarupa” to prepare an offering
for Him, and next morning they performed His bathing and puja, while Nimai smiled to Himself (CB, Adi 5.12–15). On
another occasion Shachi, having taken her son to bed with her, saw that the house was full of celestial beings; she
sent the Boy to call His father — and again the bells rang at His every step (CC, Adi 14.76–80; “Kadacha” 1.6.32–34).
Lochana Dasa relates that one night the gods took the Child from her arms, seated Him on a jewelled throne and
performed His abhisheka; Shachi called out in fear, “My son, my son!” — she had no fear for herself: her whole life
was in her son. <!-- F129 F13 F175 F186 F203 -->

Lochana Dasa also preserves her lullaby:

*“Moon, moon, moon up in the sky — who will pluck it and bring it to me? Wiping off its stain, I will paint a design on
my Gora's forehead. Come, come, come! My golden little son Nimai is crying — He wants to sleep.”* (CM, Adi-khanda,
part 2)

At times she saw gods and goddesses come to her son and glorify Him, and she marvelled; at times He danced with the gods,
crying “Radha-Govinda!”, and she fell senseless; at times she heard the bells on His bare little feet; at times fear
seized her that some misfortune threatened Him — but the next moment, looking at His face, she forgot everything and
kissed Him again and again.[^5] When Nimai grew bigger and began to play in the courtyard, she carried Him in her arms
and never took her eyes off Him, so that He would not break everything around. <!-- F186 F75 F76 -->

Once a wandering brahmana came to the house. He cooked an offering for his Deity, and the Child ate it; the brahmana
agreed to cook again, and so that the little rascal would not interfere once more, Shachi carried Him off to the
neighbours (CB, Adi 5.46–52). Krishnadasa Kaviraja adds that the Child three times partook of the guest's offering and
then, in private, granted him liberation (CC, Adi 14.37). <!-- F130 -->

Nimai often cried — now for birds, now for the stars, now for the moon (“Pluck the moon and give it to Me!” — “Oh, You
silly child! Who can catch the moon in the sky?” — CM, Adi-khanda, part 3, vv. 267–268) — and there was only one way to
quiet Him: Shachi and the neighbouring women clapped their hands and sang the names of Hari. But once, on Ekadashi, the
singing did not help. When they asked Him what He wanted, He answered: “If you want to save My life, go to Jagadisha and
Hiranya: today they are fasting, but they have prepared an offering for Vishnu; when I eat it, I will stop crying.”
Shachi threw up her hands and struck her forehead:

“‘What You ask is permitted neither by men nor by the Vedas.’” (CB, Adi 6.24)

The neighbours first gasped and then laughed. Jagadisha and Hiranya, learning of His wish, brought the whole offering
themselves, for they understood that Krishna Himself was acting in this Child (CB, Adi 6.16–33).
<!-- F14 F15 F131 F69 F187 -->

Once Shachi gave her son a tray of puffed rice and sweets, and on returning saw that He was eating earth. In tears the
Boy explained to her that sweets are the same earth, only in another form, and that He was being scolded for nothing.
Shachi answered Him with a simple housewife's wisdom:

“‘When we eat earth in the form of food, it nourishes the body and gives us strength. But if we eat raw earth, the body
will fall ill and perish. In a jug made of clay I can easily carry water, but if the water is poured out on the ground,
it simply soaks in and all is wasted.’ The Lord replied: ‘Mother, why did you not tell Me this before? Now I understand
and will not eat earth any more. When I am hungry, I would rather drink milk from your breast.’” (CC, Adi 14.31–34)
<!-- F174 -->

The men complained of His mischief to His father, and the girls and women to Shachi. The neighbours' daughters came to
tell her how at the Ganga He splashed them with water, threw itching okada seeds into their hair and behaved exactly
like the son of Nanda. Shachi smiled, embraced them tenderly and promised: “As soon as Nimai comes home, I will tie Him
up and thrash Him with a stick”; the girls took the dust of her feet and went off to bathe (CB, Adi 6.72–85). And the
parents, looking at their son, asked each other: “Who is this Child? Has the son of Nanda Himself been secretly born to
us?” When Shachi scolded Him, He would run off in a temper and smash the pots at home, and then she would take Him on
her lap, and Nimai, ashamed, would ask forgiveness (CC, Adi 14.41–44). Murari Gupta sees in this the same mystery as in
Vraja:

“He whom Yashoda once bound with cattle ropes for a broken pot — Him Shachi now feared: looking at His face, she
trembled.” (“Kadacha” 1.6.12) <!-- F16 F132 F175 F203 -->

Once, when His mother was scolding Him, the Boy struck her — according to the “Chaitanya-charitamrita” with His soft
little hand, according to Lochana Dasa and Murari with a brick He threw. Shachi fell down as if unconscious — Lochana
Dasa calls it a “feigned swoon”, for it was a play — and Nimai wept, “Mother, mother!” The neighbouring women advised
Him: “Bring your mother two coconuts and she will come back to life,” and He at once brought two fresh coconuts, just
picked; where a child had found them in a moment no one could understand (CC, Adi 14.45–47; “Kadacha” 1.6.20–30).
Lochana Dasa says that Shachi then grasped for a moment who her son was:

“‘No one can measure my good fortune — not Brahma, nor Maheshvara, nor the other gods: this son of mine is the One whom
all worship!’ Saying this, she took Gauraraya into her arms. But as soon as Shachi took Gaurahari into her arms, in her
love for her son Shachi-devi forgot His majesty.” (CM, Adi-khanda, part 3, vv. 252–254)

So too Yashoda, after seeing the universe in Krishna's mouth, by His will once again saw in Him only her son: this is
the working of yogamaya, which nourishes vatsalya, not worldly ignorance. And Shachi, who had just recognized Narayana
in her son, ties a protective thread on Him and whispers over Him the names of Vishnu, praying them to guard His head,
His eyes, His breast and His feet. <!-- F187 F77 F203 -->

Once Nimai made friends with a puppy; Shachi scolded Him and told Him to give the dog away, and He wept before her in
hurt. Then, singing the holy name, He granted the puppy a spiritual body, and it went to Vaikuntha; Shachi marvelled at
her son's deeds, and little by little they revealed to her who He was. Another time she prepared an offering for the
vow of the goddess Shashthi, the protectress of children, and Nimai intercepted her on the way and ate it. To her tears
He replied that He is the essence of the three worlds and that, as watering the root nourishes the whole tree, so
worship of Achyuta nourishes all the gods. Shachi, astonished, still went to Shashthi and, holding a blade of grass
between her teeth in sign of humility, begged all the elder women to bless her son (CM, Adi-khanda, part 4, vv.
358–378):

“‘I have not many — this treasure Nimai you gave me out of mercy; let no misfortune befall my son — this boy is yours,
O goddess!’” (CM, Adi-khanda, part 4, v. 375) <!-- F78 F188 -->

When Nimai grew older, He went with His friends to bathe in the Ganga, and on coming back He would call out: “Mother,
mother! Give Me scented oil, I want to go and bathe” — and Shachi was glad, seeing no trace of bathing on Him (CB, Adi
6.114–115). When He began to go to school, His six hours of lessons were like two yugas to His father and mother (CB,
Adi 6.135). The same love that Shachi had for Nimai was felt by the neighbours: by Jagadisha and his wife Duhkhini, who
was like a second mother to Him and sometimes even nursed Him, and by Srivasa and Malini, whom He accepted as a second
father and mother. For Shachi's son is Yashoda's son Himself, Krishna. <!-- F132 F22 F93 I1 -->

## Vishvarupa
The elder brother spent His days at Advaita's house, in discussions of the scriptures. When she had cooked the meal,
Shachi would send the younger one to call Him:

“‘Dear brother, come and eat! Mother is calling You!’ Then Vishvambhara caught hold of the edge of Vishvarupa's dhoti,
and the brothers went home.” (CB, Adi 7.40)

Advaita could not take His eyes off the beautiful Boy covered with dust, not yet recognizing in Him the One whom He had
called with His weeping.[^6] <!-- F133 F27 -->

When Vishvarupa reached youth — according to one biography, He was twelve — His father began to look for a bride for
Him, and Vishvarupa secretly left home and took sannyasa (CC, Adi 15.11–12). The parents' hearts were broken: day and
night they called, “Vishvarupa! Vishvarupa!” (CB, Adi 7.74–80). In Lochana Dasa, Shachi, hearing the news, falls
senseless and laments: how will He, so tender, walk the roads on foot, to whom will He now be wilful? — and Jagannatha
consoles her: “If one in a family takes sannyasa, millions of generations are delivered.” And the mother replies:

“‘What did you say? Tell me again, my lord. “If one takes sannyasa, millions of generations are delivered”? Then my son
has done well.’” (CM, Adi-khanda, part 5, v. 452)

Murari Gupta says the same: the parents wept bitterly, but, wishing their son well, blessed Him: “May our son remain
true to the dharma of sannyasa!” (“Kadacha” 1.7.7). So the mother's grief turned into a blessing. All the Vaishnavas of
Navadvipa grieved, and Advaita and Sita consoled the parents and helped to bring up Nimai, repeating that Krishna
Himself would soon come. <!-- F17 F133 F189 F203 -->

Nimai Himself consoled them:

“‘My brother has become a sannyasi, and this is a great blessing. For by this he will deliver his mother's family and
his father's. In His place I will serve you.’ These words gladdened the hearts of His parents.” (CC, Adi 15.14–15)

Once He fell unconscious, and on coming to Himself related that Vishvarupa had called Him to take sannyasa, but He had
reminded His brother that their parents needed help, and Vishvarupa had brought Him back and asked Him to convey
“thousands of obeisances to mother Shachi” (CC, Adi 15.16–21). From then on Nimai was always beside His father and
mother, dispelling their sorrow (CB, Adi 7.114). <!-- F176 F133 F189 -->

Vishvarupa, who received in sannyasa the name Shankararanya, left this world in Pandharpur in 1509 (1431 of the Shaka
era). The Lord learned of it many years later when, travelling in the South, He met in Pandharpur Sri Ranga Puri, a
disciple of Madhavendra. Ranga Puri told Him that he had once been a guest with his guru at Jagannatha Mishra's house
and still remembered the taste of the banana-flower curry the mistress of the house had cooked:

“Jagannatha Mishra's wife remained in his memory as a most devoted and chaste woman. She showed them such care, as if
she were the mother of the whole universe.” (CC, Madhya 9.297)

“It seemed to him then that in all the three worlds no one could cook better than she. When she fed sannyasis, she
treated them with the same love as her own sons.” (CC, Madhya 9.298)

Ranga Puri added that one of her worthy sons, Shankararanya, had attained perfection here in Pandharpur, and the Lord,
overcome by separation, replied that before taking sannyasa Shankararanya had been His brother, and Jagannatha Mishra
His father (CC, Madhya 9.299–301).[^7] <!-- F4 F23 F24 -->

## Learning
Nimai learned with astonishing speed, and all Navadvipa vied in praising Him. Shachi heard this praise with joy, but
Jagannatha grew gloomy: “Vishvarupa studied the scriptures, understood that the world is transient — and left. If
Vishvambhara becomes learned, He too will leave home. Better that He remain unlettered but live with us.” Shachi
objected:

“‘But how then will He be able to make a living? And who will give his daughter in marriage to an ignoramus?’” (CB,
Adi 7.128)

Jagannatha replied that it is Krishna who feeds everyone, not learning, and insisted: the boy was forbidden to go to
school (CB, Adi 7.121–148).[^8] Lochana Dasa has another story too: when the father, angered by Nimai's pranks, took a
switch in his hand, Shachi seized his arm, shielded her son with her body and hid Him in her lap:

“‘Do not beat my son — He is dying of fright!’” (CM, Adi-khanda, part 6, v. 492) <!-- F134 F19 F190 -->

Left without schooling, the Boy grew more mischievous than ever and one day sat down on a heap of discarded pots in
which food had been cooked. His friends ran to Shachi, and she cried out: “Nimai, Nimai, what are You doing? One who
touches these pots must bathe!” And He, like the sage Dattatreya, answered her from the top of this mound: I, an
ignoramus, cannot tell good from bad; where I am, nothing is impure — the Ganga and all holy places come there; pots in
which food was cooked for Vishnu cannot be defiled, and worldly notions of purity are mere figments of the imagination
(CB, Adi 7.166–179; cf. CC, Adi 14.73–75; “Kadacha” 1.6.13–17). People laughed, and His mother pleaded:

“Shachimata said: ‘Come down and go and bathe.’ But the Lord did not move and went on sitting. ‘Nimai!’ said
Shachimata. ‘Go quickly, before your father finds out about Your pranks!’” (CB, Adi 7.181–182)
<!-- F18 F135 F175 F203 F77 -->

“I will not come down until you promise that I shall go to school again,” He answered, and the onlookers began to
reproach Shachi: who had put it into her head to forbid her son to study when he himself wished to? At last Shachi
pulled Him down from the pots, took Him to the Ganga, bathed Him and bathed herself, and when Jagannatha returned she
told him everything:

“‘Our son is sad that He is not allowed to study.’” (CB, Adi 7.193)

All the neighbours supported her: “What Krishna wills will happen — let Him study,” and Jagannatha gave way: “As you
say, so I shall do” (CB, Adi 7.183–197), and Nimai joyfully returned to His studies (7.202). <!-- F19 F135 -->

While still a child, Nimai asked His mother for a gift:

“Once, bowing at His mother's feet, Sri Chaitanya asked her for a single gift. His mother said: ‘I will do whatever You
ask.’ Then Nimai said: ‘Please, mother, do not eat grain on Ekadashi.’ Shachi said: ‘Your words are true. I promise
that I will no longer eat grain on Ekadashi.’ From then on she fasted on Ekadashi.” (CC, Adi 15.8–10)

Lochana Dasa says that He said it in a voice deep as thunder, and His mother's heart was filled with shyness, awe and
love: “I shall keep Your command,” she said softly. Thus, by instructing His mother, the Lord taught everyone.

## The Passing of Jagannatha
One day Jagannatha woke in tears and, bowing his head to the ground, began to pray to Govinda that Nimai might remain at
home. Astonished, Shachi asked what he was praying for, and he told her his dream: Nimai with a shaven head, in the
garb of a sannyasi, laughing, weeping and dancing with Krishna's name on His lips; the Vaishnavas, led by Advaita,
singing around Him; He seated on the throne of Vishnu, Brahma and Shiva glorifying Him; and then He goes to Nilachala,
and millions of people follow Him. Shachi calmed her husband:

“‘Do not worry,’ said Shachimata, ‘it is only a dream. Nimai will not leave home.’” (CB, Adi 8.106)

She tried to convince him that their son was too attached to His books to leave home, but she could not dispel her
husband's anxiety (CB, Adi 8.93–108). <!-- F20 F136 -->

Soon Jagannatha left this world. Vrindavana Dasa says of him:

“But only a few days passed, and Jagannatha Mishra, whose body is eternal and transcendental, left this world.” (CB,
Adi 8.109)

Lochana Dasa describes his last hours: when the fever began and Shachi wept, the Lord began to explain the truth to
her — death is certain for all, even for Brahma and Rudra, time destroys Indra, Varuna and Agni — and told her to call
the relatives so that all together might help His father remember Krishna's name. Mother and son carried Jagannatha to
the bank of the Ganga, and to the sound of sankirtana he departed for Vaikuntha. Shachi, clasping her husband's feet,
wept: “I shall go with you! Take me with you!” The Lord grieved bitterly for His father, as Ramachandra did for
Dasharatha, and then performed the prescribed rites for him (CM, Adi-khanda, part 8, vv. 657–691; CB, Adi 8.110; CC,
Adi 15.23–24). <!-- F21 F137 F176 F190 -->

Only her love for her son helped Shachi to bear the loss. If she lost sight of Him even for a little while, her eyes
grew dim, and He never left her side:

“‘Mother, do not grieve so,’ He would say, ‘as long as I am with you, you will want for nothing! Very soon I shall give
you what is unattainable even for Brahma and Shiva.’ Seeing the beautiful face of Sri Gaurachandra, Shachi forgot not
only her sufferings but herself.” (CB, Adi 8.117–119)

“Looking at Vishvambhara, you will forget everything,” the women consoled her. And she thought of one thing only: that
her fatherless son should not fall into despondency; let His heart be immersed in learning — then He will be happy. She
herself took Him to His teachers and humbly begged: “My son has lost His father — be kind to Him, regard Him as your own
son”; the pandits, abashed, replied that it was they who would learn from Him (CM, Adi-khanda, part 8, vv. 687,
694–695; part 9, vv. 697–705). Chandrashekhara Acharyaratna took on the management of the widow's household.
<!-- F137 F190 F45 -->

They lived in poverty, but Nimai demanded of His mother all that only a King of kings could wish for, and when He did
not get what He wanted, He grew angry. Once, about to go to the Ganga, He asked for a garland and sandal paste for the
worship of the Ganga; His mother asked Him to wait a little — and He, flaring up like Rudra, smashed all the pots in the
house, tore the cloth, broke the doors and began to beat with a stick the walls, the tree in the courtyard and the very
ground. Shachi hid in a corner in fear: against His mother, says Vrindavana Dasa, He would not have raised His hand even
in anger. Then He fell down in the courtyard and slept in the dust — He who reclines on Ananta. Shachi made the
garland, gently brushed the sand from her son and raised Him up: “Get up, my son, go and worship the Ganga. It is good
that You broke everything — now all misfortunes are behind us.” Abashed, He went silently to the river, while she
tidied the house and sat down to cook:

“Although the Lord had destroyed everything in the house, there was no anxiety in Shachi's heart. She meekly bore all
her son's outbursts, just as mother Yashoda bore Krishna's pranks in Gokula. Thus Shachi Devi, the mother of the
universe, bore all the wilful outbursts of Sri Gauranga.” (CB, Adi 8.160–162)

“Mother Shachi, like mother Earth, patiently endured everything. Body, mind and speech were subject to her will.” (CB,
Adi 8.164)

In the evening she only asked Him tenderly: “Why did You break so many things? All this is Yours, not mine. What will
You eat tomorrow?” — and He answered with a smile: “Krishna takes care of everyone” (CB, Adi 8.123–171). <!-- F138 -->

And indeed: returning one day from the Ganga, He called His mother aside and gave her two tolas of gold:

“‘Mother, look what Krishna has given us!’ said Nimai. ‘Go and exchange it for what we need.’” (CB, Adi 8.176)

This happened whenever want came to the house. Shachi marvelled and worried: where did her son get the gold — had He
borrowed it? — and every time she exchanged it with great misgiving (CB, Adi 8.175–181). <!-- F139 -->

## Two Daughters-in-law
When Nimai had become a renowned pandita, Shachi resolved to see Him married. By the Lord's will, on the very day when
He met at the Ganga Vallabhacharya's daughter Lakshmi, His eternal consort, the matchmaker Vanamali Acharya came to
Shachi's house and proposed this girl as a wife for her son. But the mother replied:

“‘My son is growing up without a father. Let Him first complete His education. When the time comes, I will think of His
marriage.’” (CB, Adi 10.57)

Vanamali went away saddened and on the road met Nimai Himself. Having heard him, the Lord smiled and went home:

“Entering the house, the Lord asked His mother: ‘Why did you turn down the brahmana's proposal?’ Mother Shachi was
delighted to hear her son's hint. The next day she called the brahmana to the house.” (CB, Adi 10.63–64)

So also Lochana Dasa tells it: the Lord told His mother that He had seen the matchmaker looking sad, and “the very
quick-witted Shachi understood the hint” (CM, Adi-khanda, part 9, vv. 750–753). At her bidding Vanamali went to
Vallabha; the poor brahmana could give with his daughter only “five haritaki fruits”, but this troubled no one (CB, Adi
10.65–77; CC, Adi 15.29–30). Shachi invited relatives and friends, gave the women rice, bananas, kumkum, betel and oil,
and in the evening, after the ceremony at Vallabha's house, she received the bride at the threshold together with the
brahmanas' wives and lavishly rewarded the brahmanas and musicians (CB, Adi 10.87, 116–118).
<!-- F28 F80 F140 F176 F190 F203 -->

With Lakshmi's coming Shachi's house became like Vaikuntha. It was flooded with such light that at times Shachi could
not see anything clearly; beside her son she saw tongues of flame, and the fragrance of the lotus spread through the
house:

“Shachi thought: ‘I can guess the reason for this. Evidently my daughter-in-law is an incarnation of Lakshmi Devi. That
is why I see this radiance and smell the fragrance of the lotus. Now our poverty will come to an end. How did I come to
have such a daughter-in-law? Surely she is Lakshmi herself!’” (CB, Adi 10.124–126)

And indeed, the former poverty departed, and all that was needed appeared from no one knew where. When Nimai sat down
to eat, His mother could not look enough at Lakshmi serving the rice and Him eating it (CB, Adi 12.102).
<!-- F29 F115 F140 F142 -->

In those days Ishvara Puri came to Navadvipa, recognized by no one. Nimai invited him home for a meal, and Shachi cooked
an offering for Krishna; having honoured the prasada, the guest sat in the Deity room and became absorbed in talk of
Krishna (CB, Adi 11.92–94). Her son's studies, the happy days of His learning, were incomparable happiness for Shachi
(CB, Adi 11.69). <!-- F26 F68 F141 -->

Vrindavana Dasa relates how one full-moon night Nimai sat at the door of the Vishnu room and, remembering
Vrindavana-chandra, began to play the flute. Only Shachi heard this melody: she fainted with rapture, and on coming to
herself went out — and no longer heard the flute, but saw the full moon on her son's breast. At night she heard the
singing of hundreds of voices, as though a rasa-lila were going on close by; she saw a light that filled the house,
divine women with lotuses in their hands, radiant gods who vanished the next moment:

“There was nothing surprising in Shachi beholding all these wonders — the Vedas call her the embodiment of service to
Lord Vishnu. Anyone on whom mother Shachi casts a merciful glance will be able to see the same wonders.” (CB, Adi
12.230–231) <!-- F142 -->

Sometimes the Lord invited ten or twenty sannyasis and sent word to His mother to cook at once a meal for twenty. “What
shall I feed them with?” Shachi would worry if there was nothing in the house, but as soon as she thought of it, someone
brought everything needed, and Lakshmi joyfully cooked (CB, Adi 14.14–19). Shachi saw how meek and diligent her
daughter-in-law was, and her happiness grew day by day. Lakshmi rose at dawn, managed the house herself, decorated the
floor of the Deity room with designs, prepared everything for the worship of Vishnu; she served Tulasi tirelessly, but
still more gladly the Lord's mother. And when Lakshmi held her husband's feet in her hands for hours, Shachi at times saw
around those feet a radiance like tongues of flame and smelt the fragrance of the lotus throughout the house (CB, Adi
14.38–47). “Her daughter-in-law's goodness makes Shachi forget herself,” says Lochana Dasa (CM, Adi-khanda, part 12,
v. 107). <!-- F116 F117 F143 F191 F203 -->

To support His mother and wife, the Lord went to teach in East Bengal. On leaving He said to His mother: “Mother, I am
setting out on a journey,” and told Lakshmi to serve His mother constantly (CB, Adi 14.50–51). Lakshmi served “Ai” so,
almost ceasing to eat; but separation from her husband she could not bear: “the snake of separation from Lord Chaitanya
bit her, and the poison of that snake sent her to the supreme abode” (CC, Adi 16.21). On the bank of the Ganga, placing
the Lord's feet in her heart, she left this world (CB, Adi 14.100–105). Shachi's grief, says Vrindavana Dasa, would have
melted stones (CB, Adi 14.106). Lochana Dasa gives her lament: “My daughter-in-law Lakshmi was like Lakshmi in all her
virtues… My son, having appointed you to serve me, went to foreign lands without a care — how shall I, unfortunate one,
look at His face?” — and her relatives consoled her that the one eternal Reality is Krishna (CM, Adi-khanda, part 13,
vv. 122–136). The neighbours and all the devotees helped to perform the last rites. <!-- F118 F143 F30 F191 -->

Returning with rich gifts, the Lord bowed to His mother and offered her all He had brought; Shachi, sorrowful, at once
began to cook. The relatives, not wishing to grieve Him, kept silent. In the evening He came to His mother:

“In reply to her son's words mother Shachi bowed her head and wept bitterly. Overcome by grief, she could not speak.
The Lord said: ‘Dear mother, I know everything. Has some misfortune befallen your daughter-in-law?’” (CB, Adi
14.176–177)

The relatives told Him that Lakshmi had departed.[^9] For a while the Lord was silent, grieving like an ordinary man,
and then He consoled His mother: all creation is in the power of the Supreme — who but He unites and separates? And who
is more fortunate than a woman who leaves her body before her husband? (CB, Adi 14.158–188). Krishnadasa Kaviraja says
that He returned for this very purpose:

“As the Supreme Soul, Sri Chaitanya learned of Lakshmidevi's departure. He at once returned home to console His
grieving mother.” (CC, Adi 16.22)

In Lochana Dasa and Murari Gupta the Lord consoles her with the story of an apsara cursed by Indra to be born on earth,
who became the wife of an incarnation of God[^10] — and Shachi, having listened to her son, “ceased grieving and thought
of it no more” (CM, Adi-khanda, part 13, v. 157; “Kadacha” 1.12.8–18). <!-- F30 F143 F177 F191 F203 -->

For a long time pain tormented Shachi's heart, but in time she began to think of her son's happiness and searched all
Navadvipa for a new bride for Him. Vishnupriya, the daughter of the raja-pandita Sanatana Mishra, she had known long:
from childhood the girl bathed in the Ganga several times a day and each time bowed humbly to Shachi.

“And mother Shachi lovingly blessed her: ‘May Lord Krishna send you a worthy husband.’ While bathing, Shachi thought:
‘How good it would be if this girl became my son's wife.’” (CB, Adi 15.48–49)

Having made sure that her son did not object, Shachi called the brahmana Kashinatha and, opening her heart to him in
private, sent him to Sanatana: “Tell him: if he wishes, let him give his daughter to my son” (CB, Adi 15.38–52; CM,
Adi-khanda, part 13; “Kadacha” 1.13.2–3).[^11] It is told that she worried lest the noble Sanatana refuse his daughter
to the son of poor parents, who moreover passed for an eccentric, but the matchmaker only answered: “Thakurani, if not
to Nimai Pandita, then to whom?” — and when he came back from Sanatana and told her everything, he heard: “I have done
what I could; the rest is in the hands of Sri Hari.” Sanatana and his family gladly agreed, and the mother, delighted,
at once began to prepare for the wedding (CB, Adi 15.60–67). And when Sanatana sent his messenger to her, Shachi
replied:

“‘Good, good!’ said Shachi in great joy. ‘I agree; do everything quickly.’” (CM, Adi-khanda, part 13, v. 38)

The brahmana messenger added that Vishnupriya would justify her name — she would obtain Vishvambhara as Rukmini
obtained Krishna — and the mother rejoiced still more (CM, Adi-khanda, part 13, vv. 39–42; “Kadacha” 1.13.10–15).

On the eve of the wedding Shachi with the women performed puja to the Ganga, went with music to worship the goddess
Shashthi, visited the houses of relatives and lavishly gave the women puffed rice, bananas, oil, betel and kumkum:

“By the presence of the Supreme Lord the quantity of prepared gifts kept increasing. Thus mother Shachi was able to
present each of the women five or seven times.” (CB, Adi 15.118)

Setting out for the bride's house, the Lord reverently walked around His mother and bowed to her and to the brahmanas
(CB, Adi 15.140). When the newly-weds were returning in a palanquin through the streets of Navadvipa, the women
compared them, some to Shiva and Parvati, some to Lakshmi and Hari, some to Kama and Rati, some to Indra and Shachi,
some to Rama and Sita; and at the house Shachi was waiting with the virtuous women — she warmly greeted her
daughter-in-law and led her into the house (CB, Adi 15.205–213; “Kadacha” 1.14.33). From then on, says Murari, the Lord
lived at home, gladdening His mother and wife (“Kadacha” 1.15). <!-- F98 F144 F99 F107 F203 -->

Then the Lord performed the shraddha for His father and prepared to go to Gaya. He asked His mother's permission (CB,
Adi 17.12), and her heart was set aflame:

“‘You are going to foreign lands — listen, Vishvambhara: without You there is darkness in my house. You are the blind
woman's staff, the pupil of my eyes, the soul of this body — I have no one but You.’” (CM, Adi-khanda, part 15,
vv. 11–12)

He consoled her with sweet words and left. And when He returned and bowed at her feet, Shachi embraced her son, and
tears of love and joy streamed from her eyes (CM, Adi-khanda, part 15, vv. 14, 105–106). But it was a different Nimai
who had come back from Gaya. <!-- F145 F193 -->

## The Son Returned from Gaya
Shachi looked at her son and forgot herself for joy (CB, Madhya 1.18). But what had happened to Him she could not
understand. He wept aloud, calling for Krishna, flooding the courtyard with His tears, and asked everyone, “Where is
Krishna? Where is Krishna?” With folded palms the mother begged Govinda to protect her son (1.43–46), and then went to
worship the Ganga and Vishnu:

“Mother Shachi prayed: ‘O Krishnachandra! You have taken my husband and my children. Only one son is left to me. O
Krishna! There is no one to protect me. Please grant me the blessing that Vishvambhara may go on living at home, in His
right mind.’” (CB, Madhya 1.135–136)

She would bring Vishnupriya and seat her beside her son, but He seemed not to see even His wife; when He began to cry
out, Vishnupriya would run away in fright and Shachi would be numb with fear (1.137–139). One day at the meal she set
before Him rice adorned with a tulasi leaf and, like any mother, asked what He had been reading that day and whether He
had quarrelled with anyone. Vrindavana Dasa, describing that moment, calls her “Shachi, the mother of all creation”: in
answer the Lord, as Kapila once spoke to His mother Devahuti, began to tell her of the glory of devotional service to
Krishna and of the torments of the soul that forgets Krishna — in the mother's womb, at birth and after it — and that
only one who worships Krishna in the company of His devotees can live and die without fear. Listening to Him, Shachi
felt great bliss (CB, Madhya 1.189–241). <!-- F146 F147 -->

But the change in her son kept growing. He laughed and wept, fell unconscious, talked to Himself, and Shachi decided it
was some unknown illness. “By the will of Providence I have lost my husband and my children; I have no one but my son,”
she told everyone, “and I cannot understand what is wrong with Him: now He laughs, now He weeps.” The neighbours advised
her to tie Him up, to give Him the juice of green coconuts, to rub oil into His head, and “the magnanimous Shachi, the
Mother of the universe”, not knowing what to do, tried to follow their advice and surrendered herself wholeheartedly to
Govinda. She sent word of everything to the Vaishnavas headed by Srivasa, and one day Srivasa came. The Lord was then
circumambulating tulasi; on seeing the devotee He fell unconscious, and when He came to Himself He sobbed. “Is this
madness?” thought Srivasa. “This is the highest devotion to Krishna.” To the mother he said:

“‘Do not worry about anything. I assure you, this is not madness but true devotion to Krishna. No one is able to
understand it. If you wish to see still more of Krishna's wonders, tell nothing to outsiders.’” (CB, Madhya 2.120–122)

Shachi gave up the thought of madness, but anxiety did not leave her: would not this son, too, leave home? (CB, Madhya
2.88–124) <!-- F148 F33 -->

Once the Lord asked Gadadhara where that dark Krishna in yellow garments was, and on hearing, “Krishna is always in Your
heart,” began to tear at His chest with His nails. Gadadhara held His hands and calmed Him: “Be patient, Krishna will
come soon.” Shachi saw it all. “I have never met so sensible a youth,” she thought. “I myself am afraid even to go near
my son, and he has calmed Him so skilfully,” and she asked him, “Dear boy, stay always by my son; do not leave Him for a
moment.” Seeing such love of God in Him, she could no longer think of Him as her son: “An ordinary man cannot shed such
tears. What great soul has appeared in our family?” — and in awe she did not dare to show herself before Him (CB,
Madhya 2.202–214). Thus for a moment the Lord's majesty veiled her motherly love; but each time vatsalya, guarded by
yogamaya, prevailed, and to her He was again only her son. <!-- F149 F34 -->

Soon the mother herself received what He had brought from Gaya. Krishnadasa Kaviraja says it in a single line:

“Then He bestowed love of Krishna upon His mother Shachidevi.” (CC, Adi 17.10)

Lochana Dasa tells how it happened. Once, lying at home, the Lord wept in the mood of Radha separated from Krishna, who
had gone away to Mathura, and gave no answer to His mother's questions. Shachi reflected — and by the signs understood
that this was prema, Krishna's grace. Then she said:

“‘Listen, listen, my son, my golden son: I see in You a wondrous [treasure], rare in the world. Wherever You went,
whatever wealth You obtained, You brought it and gave it to me. In Gaya You obtained such wealth — love for Krishna, a
thing rare even for the gods, a priceless jewel. If there is any compassion for me in Your heart, give me the wealth of
love for Krishna — this I ask.’ When Shachi-devi spoke such words, the Lord's heart melted, and He looked [at her]: ‘By
the mercy of the Vaishnavas you will obtain prema, mother — know it for certain: I am telling you.’” (CM,
Madhya-khanda, part 1, vv. 26–31)

At once Shachi obtained prema-bhakti: her body trembled, tears streamed from her eyes, and she called, “Krishna,
Krishna!” (vv. 32–34). Murari Gupta tells it the same way: the mother saw her son weeping in His sleep, in time
recognised the signs of prema and humbly begged Him for it; the Lord answered, “Mother, by the mercy of the Vaishnavas
this will come to be for you,” and Shachi, “devotion personified”, obtained firm devotion to Hari (“Kadacha”
2.1.9–18).[^n12] <!-- F194 F204 F82 F177 -->

Lochana Dasa tells of another night too. In the third watch Vishvambhara suddenly began to weep, and Shachi, waking,
asked, “Why are You weeping, child? Hearing Your weeping, I burn all over.” He was long silent, and then, sitting up on
the bed, told her His dream: He had seen a Youth shining like a fresh raincloud, with a crest of peacock feathers, in
yellow garments and with a flute in His hand. “Do not tell anyone,” He asked, and the mother rejoiced at these
nectarean words (CM, Madhya-khanda, part 6, vv. 5–13). <!-- F83 F195 -->

## Two Sons
In those years a joy came to dwell in Shachi's house such as she had not known before: once more she had two sons. At
the Vyasa-puja in Srivasa's house, when Nityananda and Gaurachandra, intoxicated with dancing, forgot Themselves, Shachi
watched Them from a secluded place:

“When mother Shachi looked at Vishvambhara and Nityananda, she thought: ‘These are my sons.’” (CB, Madhya 5.159)
<!-- F150 F102 -->

Nityananda often came running to her, and she welcomed Him with great love, while He, like a mischievous child, tried to
catch hold of her feet, and she would run away (CB, Madhya 8.26–27). Once, towards morning, she had a dream and told it
to her son in private: He and Nityananda, both five years old, ran into the Deity room, carried out Krishna and
Balarama and began to quarrel with Them over the offerings. “The time when You stole butter is past,” Nityananda was
saying, “now is the time of the brahmanas. My Master is Gaurachandra Vishvambhara.” All four snatched the food from one
another, and then Nityananda called to her, “Mother, give Me something to eat, I am hungry.” The Lord laughed: “Mother,
this is a most auspicious dream, only do not tell anyone about it. I Myself have noticed that half of what is offered to
our Deities disappears somewhere.” And He proposed that they invite Nityananda to a meal at once (CB, Madhya
8.28–51).[^n13] <!-- F151 F103 -->

Shachi gladly set about cooking, the Lord Himself went to fetch Nityananda, and Ishana, the servant of the house,
brought water to wash Their feet (8.52–59). The brothers sat side by side, and Vrindavana Dasa compares Them to Rama and
Lakshmana in the house of Kaushalya:

“Mother Shachi joyfully served Them, and when by mistake she brought Them three plates instead of two, They laughed
merrily. And when she brought Them more, she saw Them in the form of five-year-old boys. She saw two beautiful boys, one
fair-skinned and the other dark. They wore no clothes, and both had four arms. One held in His hands the conch, the
disc, the club and the lotus, and on His chest were the mark of Shrivatsa and the Kaustubha gem. The other held the club
and the plough. In the ears of both were earrings shaped like makaras.” (CB, Madhya 8.62–65)

On her son's chest she saw her daughter-in-law too, like Lakshmi on the chest of Narayana — and the vision vanished.
Shachi fell unconscious, the food scattered about the room; the Lord lifted her up, and she, saying nothing, went away
into another room all in tears (8.66–71). <!-- F151 F48 -->

The remnants of that meal were cleared away and reverently eaten by Ishana:

“He is the most fortunate in all the fourteen worlds, for all his life he served mother Shachi.” (CB, Madhya 8.74)

Ishana did whatever she needed, and Shachi loved him dearly. Serving her who is non-different from Yashoda, he knew that
service to a devotee is higher than worship of the Lord Himself, and that the Lord's mercy follows the mercy of His
devotee. “With folded palms I bow to Ishana, whom Shachi Thakurani loved so dearly,” sings the “Vaishnava-vandana”.

Shachi regarded Nityananda as her own Vishvarupa: more than once she had heard Him speak exactly like her elder son, and
had even seen Him in the form of Vishvarupa, but she told no one of this. When Nityananda, like a child, stood
unclothed in the middle of the courtyard and the Lord Himself dressed Him, the mother beamed with smiles. Once she gave
Him five sandesh; He ate one and threw the rest away. “Why did You throw them away? What will You eat now?” Shachi
lamented. “If you want, you will find them,” He replied. Going into the room, she found there the same four sandesh,
and on coming back she saw Nityananda eating them:

“Shachi said: ‘O Nityananda, why do You bewilder me? I know that You are the Supreme Lord, and so I pray You to free me
from the veil of illusion.’” (CB, Madhya 11.92)

And, like a child, He reached again for her feet, and she ran away (CB, Madhya 11.78–93). Lochana Dasa relates that the
Lord Himself revealed to her, “This is Vishvarupa, your own son; under the name of Nityananda He has come to
Navadvipa,” and the mother, embracing Nityananda, said, “You have become my son… from today you two are my sons.” “All
that you have said, mother, is true: I am your son — know it for certain,” answered Nityananda, and “seeing her two
sons, Shachi was comforted at heart” (CM, Madhya-khanda, part 5, vv. 81–90).[^n14] <!-- F153 F104 F195 -->

At home the Lord spent time with Vishnupriya, for He knew that this gladdened His mother (CB, Madhya 11.67–68). But more
and more the house became for Him a place of ecstasy. His mother would say, “Son, go and bathe in the Ganga,” and He
would answer, “Mother, chant the names of Rama and Krishna”;

“Whatever Shachi said to her son, Vishvambhara answered only, ‘Krishna.’” (CB, Madhya 8.94)

Watching this, she was immersed in bliss, but in the depths of her heart she thought that this son too might leave home
(8.92). When in kirtana His tender body fell heavily to the ground, she would close her eyes, call on Govinda and pray,
“O Krishna, let me not see Vishvambhara falling to the ground.” The Lord felt His mother's wish — and immersed her in
such bliss that while the kirtana went on she was unaware of the outer world (8.124–132). When the Lord delivered Jagai
and Madhai, Shachi and her daughter-in-law watched His pastimes at home, sunk in an ocean of nectar (13.308, 372), and
Vrindavana Dasa exclaims:

“Who can describe the good fortune that fell to mother Shachi? Only the thousand-headed Lord Ananta is able to, and even
He only if He receives inspiration from above.” (CB, Madhya 13.373)

“Gazing at the lotus face of her son, Shachi, the Mother of the universe, forgot where she was.” (CB, Madhya 13.375)

Together with her daughter-in-law she prayed to Govinda as she beheld her son, and was wholly filled with bliss (CB,
Madhya 19.270). <!-- F152 F154 F156 -->

## In the House of Chandrashekhara
When the Lord resolved to play the role of Lakshmi in the house of Chandrashekhara Acharya, her sister's husband, Shachi
and her daughter-in-law came to watch, and with them the families of all the Vaishnavas (CB, Madhya 18.28–30). Srivasa
came out in the guise of Narada — the same form, the same words and manners. “Is that Srivasa?” Shachi asked Malini.
“Yes, it is he,” she replied. And Shachi, “a great Vaishnavi and the mother of the whole universe”, fainted with bliss,
so that no sign of life could be found in her body; the women whispered the names of Krishna in her ear, and on coming to
herself she remembered Govinda (18.62–68). And when the Lord Himself came out, no one recognised Him, not even those who
had known Him from birth:

“What to speak of others, when even mother Shachi did not recognise Him. She said: ‘Can it be that Lakshmi herself has
come to dance?’” (CB, Madhya 18.131)

And when the play was over and all were weeping with love of God, the pious women fell at Shachi's feet (18.197).

## Shachi and Advaita
Even in Nimai's childhood Shachi used to send Him to Advaita's house to call Vishvarupa to the meal, and Advaita, looking
at the beautiful Child, would think, “He has stolen My heart — so this is My Lord” (CB, Madhya 22.93–102). But for the
mother Advaita's house became a house of loss: there Vishvarupa had found the love for Krishna with which He left for
sannyasa. “It was Advaita who induced my son to leave,” she would think when the pain subsided, but, fearing to offend a
Vaishnava, she told this to no one, and looking at Vishvambhara she forgot her sorrows (22.107–110). Now Vishvambhara too
spent nearly all His time with Advaita, leaving His young wife at home,[^n15] and one day her grief broke out:

“When mother Shachi saw that her son was hardly ever at home, she said: ‘Acharya Gosani will take this son away too.’ In
deep sorrow she went on: ‘Why do they call him “Advaita”? In truth this Gosani is “Dvaita”. He has already induced one
son of mine to leave home, and now he wants to deprive me of my other son. I am a widow. No one will take pity on
me.’” (CB, Madhya 22.113–117)

No one learned of this except her Son, who dwells in the heart of everyone. <!-- F157 F37 F38 Q4 F158 -->

One day the Lord sat on Vishnu's throne in Srivasa's house and began to give the devotees blessings. Srivasa asked Him
to grant love of Krishna to mother Shachi — and heard: “Do not ask this. She has offended a Vaishnava, and that keeps
her from attaining prema.” “Your words wound us to death,” cried Srivasa. “How can she who gave birth to You not attain
love of Krishna?” The Lord answered that it was not in His power to destroy an offence against a Vaishnava — only that
Vaishnava himself could do so, as Ambarisha forgave Durvasa; let His mother place on her head the dust of Advaita's feet
(22.13–36).[^n16] The devotees went to Advaita, and He exclaimed:

“‘Do you all want to kill Me? My Lord came forth from her womb, and therefore she is My mother and I am her son. I Myself
long to obtain the dust of her feet. I do not even know the limits of My mother's glory. She is the mother of the whole
universe and the embodiment of loving service to Vishnu. So how can you say such a thing? Even if a person utters “ai”
as an ordinary word, the divine power of that sound will free him from all suffering. Mother Shachi is exactly like the
Ganga. In truth, she is equal to Devaki and Yashoda.’” (CB, Madhya 22.38–43)

Glorifying her, Advaita lost consciousness, and at that moment Shachi took the dust of His feet — and herself fell
unconscious with bliss. The Vaishnavas began to sing the names of Hari, and the Lord, pleased with His mother, said to
her from the throne:

“‘Now you have attained devotional service to Vishnu, freed from the offence you committed against Advaita.’” (CB,
Madhya 22.52) <!-- F39 F40 F157 -->

Vrindavana Dasa himself adds a caution: “In truth, it was not even an offence, but the Lord took it so” (CB, Madhya
22.58). The mother's words were the lament of vatsalya, not abuse; and the Lord enacted this pastime for the sake of
others. He knew that there would be people who, exalting one Vaishnava, would disregard the rest, and through the
example of His own mother, “as the instructing spiritual master”, He warned everyone against vaishnava-aparadha (22.54,
118–131). If He did not pardon even His mother — the very essence of motherly love, Yashoda — what can others expect?
One can be freed from an offence against a Vaishnava only by asking forgiveness of that Vaishnava himself. But Shachi too
set an example: having given birth to Gaurahari, she was not in the least proud, and on understanding her fault she went
to Advaita without delay. <!-- I2 I3 F157 -->

## Before the Sannyasa
The anxiety that Shachi had carried in her heart ever since her son's return from Gaya was gradually taking shape.
Lochana Dasa relates that once a certain brahmana, who had not been let in to watch the Lord's dancing, cursed Him in
anger: “Be outside samsara!” Hearing of it from people, Shachi fell unconscious, and when her son came home she asked
Him in tears, “How did you wrong him? This whole house of mine is only for your sake… if harm comes to you, I shall
die.” “I have done him no wrong,” the Lord consoled her, “and a curse without fault cannot take effect.” He bathed in
the Ganga, worshipped Krishna, watered tulasi and sat down to eat, while Shachi tenderly gazed at His face (CM,
Madhya-khanda, part 10, vv. 69+1–21).[^n17] But for Him this curse was a blessing: He Himself wished to leave samsara.
Soon, with a quiet smile, He told those close to Him that in the night a brahmana had whispered the mantra of sannyasa
in His ear, and since then His heart was no longer His own (CM, Madhya-khanda, part 12, vv. 49–53).[^n18]
<!-- F196 F197 F84 -->

The first to learn of the Lord's decision was Nityananda, and Shachi was the first of the five to whom the Lord allowed
Him to disclose His intention (CB, Madhya 28.11–12). Thinking of how she would bear this separation, Nityananda went
off to a solitary place and wept bitterly (CB, Madhya 26.153–156). Shachi herself wept at home, seeing her son in ecstasy
embracing the devotees and sobbing (26.84). And when the news reached her, she forgot where she was, fell unconscious
again and again and could not stop her tears (CB, Madhya 27.18–20), and one day, when her son was sitting at home, she
said to Him:

“‘My dear son, do not leave home. Do not abandon Your mother. This sinful soul lives only because she sees Your
face.’” (CB, Madhya 27.22)

“‘You have descended to establish religious principles, but is leaving one's mother a religious precept? You are the
embodiment of all religious rules, so how will You leave Your mother? How then will You preach religious precepts in
this world?’” (CB, Madhya 27.27–28)

“Your elder brother left me, Your father has gone to Vaikuntha; stay, sing kirtana at home with Your devotees —
Nityananda will help You,” she pleaded (27.30–34), and Vrindavana Dasa compares her to Kaushalya instructing Rama
(27.35). The Lord was silent, His head bowed. Shachi grew pale, stopped eating, and from grief her body became “skin and
bones”. Then, seeing that His mother could not go on living like this, the Lord took her aside to a solitary place:

“The Lord said: ‘My mother, restore peace to your soul and understand that I have been your son for many lives. Hear
attentively of your own glory. In the past your name was Prishni. You were My mother. Later you appeared in the heavens
as Aditi. I descended into this world as Vamana, and you were My mother. Then you came as Devahuti, and I was born as
your son Kapila. Then you became Kaushalya, and I came again as your son Ramachandra. Then you were born as Devaki in
Mathura and were imprisoned by the demon Kamsa. At that time too you became My mother. You are that same Devaki, and I
am your son. Beginning this movement of sankirtana, I shall soon take two more births as your son. My mother, in My form
as the Deity you appear as the earth. And in My form as the holy name you are My mother as the tongue. You are My mother
birth after birth. Nothing will ever separate you and Me.’” (CB, Madhya 27.39–49)

Thus the Lord revealed to her that He would be her son hereafter as well — in the Deity, which is made from earth, and in
the holy name, which the tongue sings: the name, the form and the very being of the Lord are non-different from one
another. Hearing this secret, Shachi found peace (27.36–51). <!-- F159 F105 F160 F109 Q12 I5 F161 -->

Lochana Dasa tells the same in his own way. Shachi begged her son not to take sannyasa in His youth, and He told her of
Dhruva and of the instruction of his mother. “I too will go with you,” said Shachi then. “If such a golden son goes off
with a shaven head, I will shave my head, put on red garments and become a yogini.” The Lord began to console her: other
people's sons bring their mothers gold and silver, which pass away, but He would bring her prema for Krishna,
imperishable in this world and the next. And for a moment the Lord of the fourteen worlds lifted the veil from her: in
her son she saw Krishna of Vrindavana, dark as a fresh cloud, with His flute, among the cowherd boys and gopis. Yet even
then the mother's love did not yield to His majesty:

“[Yet] in her tenderness Shachi does not give up her own relation [to Him]: ‘Krishna has become my son — so my good
fortune has decreed. Krishna, unattainable for the world, is my son; He is subject to no one's control — what can my
strength do?’ Reasoning thus, Shachi spoke: ‘You are the independent Lord, the jewel among men. By my good fortune You
were so long in my power — now, as it pleases You, take sannyasa.’” (CM, Madhya-khanda, part 14, vv. 212–215)

“Why is such a treasure leaving me?” she asked and wept aloud, and the Lord, raising His face, answered:

“‘…on any day when you long with love to see Me — at that very moment you will be able to see Me.’” (CM,
Madhya-khanda, part 14, v. 220)

The veil lifted from her at that moment Lochana Dasa calls maya, but it is no veil of ignorance: it is yogamaya, the
Lord's internal potency, which hides His majesty for the sake of a mother's love — and, having seen that majesty, Shachi
still remained His mother. <!-- F198 F85 -->

On the eve of His departure the pious Shridhara brought the Lord a gourd. “Tomorrow I shall leave,” thought the Lord,
“but nothing Shridhara brings must go to waste,” and He asked His mother to cook it, and when another devotee brought a
pitcher of milk, He said with a smile, “Very good, cook them together.” Shachi set about the cooking with great joy (CB,
Madhya 28.33–40). Vrindavana Dasa places this departure beside others: Kapila left His mother Devahuti, Shuka went away
from his father, the great Vaishnava Vyasa, without looking back, and the best of sannyasis left His mother Shachi; but
“in truth all these examples of renunciation are not a forsaking of one's obligations. At their root lies the attainment
of the highest good, but only great souls can understand this” (CB, Madhya 3.101–104). <!-- F36 F161 F101 -->

## The Night of Departure
That night Shachi did not sleep but only wept. The Lord woke in the brahma-muhurta, and when she understood that He was
about to leave, she came and sat by the door. The Lord took her by the hands:

“‘You cared for Me wonderfully. Only thanks to you did I master the sciences and receive an education. Forgetting your
own happiness, you live only for My pleasure. Every moment you gave Me such love that I cannot repay you even in a
million kalpas.’” (CB, Madhya 28.51–53)

“‘My dear mother, please hear Me. All creation is governed by the Supreme Lord. No one has the power to be independent.
Who can understand the will of the Supreme Lord, by which living beings meet and part? Whether I leave now or in ten
years, you should not grieve in any case. I take full responsibility for both your worldly and your spiritual
needs.’” (CB, Madhya 28.55–58)

“Mother Shachi listened quietly to what the Lord was saying. She answered nothing; only endless tears streamed from her
eyes. Thus Shachi, the mother of the whole universe, became as sorrowful and silent as mother Earth. Can anyone
comprehend the inconceivable pastimes of Krishna? The Lord placed the dust of His mother's feet on His head, walked
around her and left without delay.” (CB, Madhya 28.60–62)

In the morning the devotees came to bow to the Lord and found His mother at the door. “Why are you sitting here,
mother?” asked Srivasa. For a long time she could not speak, and then she said:

“After some time she said: ‘Listen to me. Only the Vaishnavas have the right to share the Lord's possessions. Divide among
yourselves all that He has left. The shastras say that all this now belongs to you. So take it, do as I ask, and let me
go away from here.’” (CB, Madhya 28.70–72)

The Lord's house was filled with weeping, and when the devotees had calmed down a little, they sat around mother Shachi
(28.65–88).

This parting was no rupture in the worldly sense. The Lord did not leave His mother as people leave their families for
their own sake: He went for the deliverance of the fallen (28.63), and in leaving took upon Himself the care of her. And
her grief is vipralambha, separation, in which a mother's love is revealed most fully; so Yashoda wept for Krishna when
Akrura took Him away to Mathura. <!-- F162 -->

## Twelve Days
When the news reached Navadvipa, “mother Shachi turned to stone with grief. She stood motionless, like a doll” (CB, Antya
1.38). Lochana Dasa says that Shachi and Vishnupriya kept falling unconscious; Shachi wept, holding on her lap
Vishnupriya, who lay as if dead, and called, “Nimai!” — “On every side it is empty, all is wrapped in darkness… no one
will call me ‘mother’ any more” (CM, Madhya-khanda, part 16, vv. 8–15). As He left to follow the Lord to Katva,
Nityananda consoled them both (v. 44). Then, remembering His words, “Whoever chants the name will attain Me,” all of
them — Shachi and Vishnupriya, children and the old, young men and girls — sat down to chant the holy name, making
Gauranga their refuge. The devotees, finding consolation only in the names and qualities of the Lord, did not leave
Shachi's side (CB, Antya 1.50). For four days, while the Lord wandered half-mad through Radha-desha, yearning for
Vrindavana, Sita was with her: Shachi lay on the ground like one dying — as if the sun had left her sky for ever. And
Vamshivadana composed a song of separation in which the poet rolls on the ground, hearing the weeping of the Lord's
young wife and her mother-in-law. <!-- F163 F199 F86 F108 F91 F54 -->

When Chandrashekhara came back from Katva, Shachi ran out to meet him with her hair unbound:

“‘Where have you left my Nimai and come back? How did He shave His head, in what land? What wretched sannyasi with a
cruel heart felt no pity when giving the mantra to Vishvambhara!’” (CM, Madhya-khanda, part 18, vv. 6–7)

“No more shall I cook rice to serve you… Who will know when you are hungry?” she lamented (vv. 12–13). These are the
words of a mother's love, not abuse of the guru of the Lord's sannyasa: so speaks the heart of a mother for whom her
Son is her whole life. <!-- F199 -->

Meanwhile the Lord had sent Nityananda to Navadvipa to tell the devotees that He awaited them in Shantipur, in
Advaita's house (CB, Antya 1.127–133). Nityananda, intoxicated with prema, swam down the Ganga to the Lord's favourite
ghat and went straight to His house:

“On arriving, He saw that mother Shachi had been fasting for twelve days already. Only by the strength of her devotion
to Krishna did she remain alive. Mother Shachi was overflowing with the parental love of Yashoda. Tears of love streamed
endlessly from her eyes. Whomever she met, mother Shachi asked: ‘Are you from Mathura? Please, tell me, how are Krishna
and Balarama there?’ Asking this, she would fall unconscious to the ground. Sometimes mother Shachi said: ‘Are those
the sounds of the flute and the horn? Has Akrura at last come back to Vrindavana?’ Thus mother Shachi was sinking in the
ocean of separation from Krishna. Not a trace of outer consciousness remained in her body.” (CB, Antya 1.146–151)

This is not bewilderment but prema itself: in separation from Gaura there was revealed in Shachi who she eternally is —
Yashoda waiting for Krishna to come back from Mathura. Nityananda fell at her feet, and with a cry of “My son!” she lost
consciousness. He gave everyone the good news and began to console her: “Your son is the Supreme Lord Himself, whom the
Vedas seek. He has promised always to protect you and to care for all your needs, both material and spiritual”
(1.152–168). And He added:

“‘Mother, let us go quickly. You will cook for Krishna and so satisfy all the devotees. Everyone wishes to taste the
food cooked by you. But when you fast, Krishna fasts too. Cook some dish and offer it to Krishna. I am very
hungry.’” (CB, Antya 1.169–171)

“Hearing these words of Nityananda, mother Shachi forgot her sorrows and went to cook. The pious mother Shachi first
offered the dish she had cooked to Krishna, and then served it to Nityananda. Then mother Shachi served all the
Vaishnavas, feeding them prasada. All were happy to taste it. When all the devotees were satisfied, mother Shachi
herself sat down to eat, breaking her twelve-day fast.” (CB, Antya 1.172–175) <!-- F164 -->

## Shantipur
Lochana Dasa describes her meeting with Nityananda thus: with a cry of “O Avadhuta, where have you left my Nimai?”
Shachi, mad with grief, ran to meet Him, and Nityananda answered, “Do not grieve: He has sent me to bring you all. The
Lord will be in the house of Advaita Acharya — let us go!” At once everyone set out — from children to the old — and
Shachi ran ahead of them all: strength had returned to her body (CM, Madhya-khanda, part 18, vv. 56–64). Murari Gupta
writes that Nityananda bowed at Shachi's feet, consoled her, ate the food she had cooked, and next morning led her and
the Vaishnavas to Advaita's house (“Kadacha” 3.4.8–10). With her went Murari Gupta, Nandana Acharya, Shridhara,
Damodara Pandita and many others; Krishnadasa Kaviraja says that it was Chandrashekhara who went to fetch her:[^n19]

“On the morning after Mahaprabhu's arrival Chandrashekhara brought mother Shachi in a palanquin. Many other devotees
came with them too.” (CC, Madhya 3.137) <!-- F200 F205 F42 F120 F178 -->

“The Lord fell before her in obeisance. Mother Shachi laid His head on her lap and burst into sobs. The meeting shook
them both. Seeing her son's smoothly shaven head, Shachimata was deeply agitated. With great love she stroked her son and
kissed His face. She tried to look at Him, but tears blinded her eyes. Sobbing, Shachi lamented: ‘My dear son, Nimai! Do
not become as cruel as Vishvarupa, Your elder brother. I have not seen him since he took sannyasa. You will kill me if You
do the same.’” (CC, Madhya 3.140–144)

“Sri Chaitanya answered with tears in His eyes: ‘O My mother, hear Me. My body is all I have, and it belongs to you. It
was born and nurtured by you. Even in millions of births I could not repay you this debt. I shall never become
indifferent to you, whatever aims I pursued in taking sannyasa. I shall live wherever you say and fulfil your every
wish.’” (CC, Madhya 3.145–148)

Seeing her son in the garments of a sannyasi, Shachi and all the people of Navadvipa felt joy mingled with the pain of
separation. Murari recalls His words: “Mother, I am always near you” (“Kadacha” 3.4.21); according to Lochana Dasa, the
Lord fell before her and said, “Weep no more: your weeping pains My heart” (CM, Madhya-khanda, part 18, vv.
76+3–4).[^n20] <!-- F178 F43 F205 F200 -->

From that day it was Shachi herself who cooked for the Lord and His companions (CC, Madhya 3.160), and He ate what she
had prepared, to soothe at least a little the pain His departure had caused her. At night, when He fell to the ground in
kirtana, the mother wept and prayed:

“‘O Narayana! From childhood I have done my every act and every deed as service to You. Please grant my request.
Each time my Nimai falls to the ground, let Him feel no pain at all.’” (CC, Madhya 3.165–166)

Krishnadasa Kaviraja says that in her at that time happiness, fear and humility were joined together (3.167). When
Srivasa and other brahmanas also wished to invite the Lord to their homes for a meal, Shachi asked them:

“‘Who knows where and when I shall see Nimai again? You will always be able to meet Him in another place. But my lot is
sad — for I have only this meeting. Therefore I beg you all, let me cook for Nimai while He stays in the house of Sri
Advaita.’” (CC, Madhya 3.169–171) <!-- F179 F43 -->

A sannyasi ought not to live among his relatives, but the Lord said that He would leave neither the devotees nor His
mother, and asked how He might fulfil both dharmas. Advaita and the devotees came to Shachi with this question, and the
Mother of the universe decided it herself:

“‘If He stays here, He will make me happy. But if people censure Him, I shall be unhappy. So I have a fine idea. Let Him
settle in Jagannatha Puri. In this way He will attain both aims at once. Navadvipa is closely connected with Jagannatha
Puri. People are always going back and forth, and so I shall always have the latest news. You will easily be able to go
there, and sometimes He too will come to bathe in the Ganga. It does not matter to me at all whether I am happy or not.
But whatever brings Him happiness gives happiness to me too.’” (CC, Madhya 3.181–185)

“O mother, your words are like the injunctions of the Vedas,” the devotees replied (3.186).[^n21] The Lord, delighted,
asked everyone at home to perform Krishna-sankirtana unceasingly (3.187–191). The festival at Advaita's house went on for
several days more: by day they spoke of Krishna, by night they sang, the mother cooked in bliss, and her son and the
devotees partook of her cooking, and her happiness grew day by day (3.198–205); in all the Lord stayed ten days in
Shantipur (3.136). Advaita asked Nityananda, Jagadananda, Damodara Pandita and Mukunda Datta to accompany Him to
Puri.[^n22] <!-- F179 F44 F121 -->

Lochana Dasa describes the farewell. The devotees pleaded, “Look at Your defenceless mother… How will You take leave of
Shachi?” Shachi, her strength gone, rose, holding on to the hands of two people:

“‘Where will you go, having become pitiless? Not seeing you, my son, I shall die. Everyone else will see your face many
times — but I, unfortunate, shall see it no more. You yourself, child, have consoled everyone — but who, my son, will
console me?’” (CM, Madhya-khanda, part 19, vv. 56–58)

“You are dying of vain grief, forgetting what you knew before,” the Lord answered, reminding her of His promise to
appear whenever she called, and, prostrating Himself at her feet, consoled her (vv. 60–62). Krishnadasa Kaviraja says
that on leaving the Lord offered prayers at His mother's feet and reverently walked around her, and Advaita's house was
filled with weeping (CC, Madhya 3.210–211). So in 1510 He left by way of Chhatrabhoga for Nilachala. <!-- F201 F44 -->

## The Years of Kirtana
From Gaya the Lord returned changed. He wept, called for Krishna, fainted, and people began to talk of a “wind
disorder”. Shachi, beside herself with anxiety, sent for Srivasa and other Vaishnavas. Nimai was circumambulating
Tulasi at the time; on seeing Srivasa He fell unconscious. Srivasa understood that this was no illness but the highest
love, mahabhava — Krishna's mercy — comforted Shachi and told her to say nothing to anyone, for the present, of what
had been revealed in her son. Lochana Dasa says that after Gaya the Lord praised His mother's good fortune and showed
her His mercy; one night, weeping for Krishna, He told her His dream. <!-- F33 F82 F83 -->

Seeing how skilfully Gadadhara had calmed her son when, longing for Krishna, He wanted to tear open His chest, Shachi
thought, “This boy is very clever — with him I need not fear for Gaurahari,” and asked him, “Gadadhara, stay always by
my son's side; then I shall have nothing to worry about.” <!-- F34 -->

In those years Shachi's house became a home for Nityananda too. She dreamt that Gaura and Nitai were Kanai and Balai of
Vraja Themselves, and that Nitai was calling her “Ma” and asking for something to eat. So she invited Nitai and fed Him
together with Nimai. Even before this the Lord had begged His mother to let Him invite Nitai, and when she agreed He
went to call Him Himself, while Ishana, the servant of the house, brought water to wash Their feet before the meal.
When Nityananda wandered about unclothed like a child and the Lord Himself dressed Him, Shachi laughed at His childlike
ways. At the close of the Vyasa-puja in Srivasa's house she sat apart, watching the play of the two brothers and
bathing Them in her motherly love. <!-- F103 F48 F104 F102 -->

Ishana, the servant in Mishra's house, served the Lord's mother all his life, doing whatever she needed, and Shachi
Thakurani loved him dearly. He knew that serving a devotee is higher than worshipping the Lord Himself, and he served
Shachi, who is non-different from Yashoda; her love became for him the Lord's own mercy. The “Vaishnava-vandana” sings:
“With folded hands I bow to Ishana, whom Shachi Thakurani loved so dearly.” <!-- F47 I4 Q7 -->

When the Lord danced as Lakshmi in Chandrashekhara's house, Shachi and Vishnupriya came to watch. Seeing Srivasa in the
guise of Narada, Shachi fainted in amazement, and the women revived her by repeating Krishna's name. And in the guise of
the goddesses Nimai was not recognised even by those who had known Him from birth — not even by Shachi. <!-- F35 -->

## Shachi and Advaita
Vishvarupa's departure had left in Shachi's heart a quiet grievance against Advaita, for it was at His house that her
elder son had come to see the world as passing. She was careful not to offend Advaita, knowing Him to be a Vaishnava,
and found comfort in having Nimai at home. But when Nimai too began to spend whole days at Advaita's, leaving His young
wife at home,[^12] Shachi thought to herself: “He is called Advaita, but Dvaita would suit Him better: He does not unite,
He divides. Because of Him one son of mine, beautiful as the moon, has gone, and now the other is not allowed to stay
at home. No one pities me; I am alone in the world.” She did not say it aloud — she only thought it, out of love for
her son. But her Son dwells in every heart. <!-- F37 F38 Q4 -->

Once, when the Lord was revealing His divine nature to the devotees in Srivasa's house, Srivasa asked Him to grant His
mercy to Shachi as well. The Lord replied that His mother had offended Advaita and therefore would not receive it.[^13]
“If those words reach our ears again, we shall not survive them,” cried Srivasa, and the Lord explained how to be freed
from an offence against a Vaishnava: only the one offended can forgive. Learning of this, Shachi went at once to
Advaita to beg forgiveness. But as soon as He heard her, He began to glorify the one who had carried the Supreme Lord
in her womb — how could she whom the Lord had chosen as His mother be at fault before Him? — and He went on glorifying
her until He fell unconscious. Then Shachi could touch the dust of His feet, and the offence was lifted. Mahaprabhu was
pleased with His mother. <!-- F39 F40 -->

So the Lord taught everyone: if He did not forgive even His mother — the very essence of motherly love, the incarnation
of Yashoda — what can others expect? One can be freed from an offence against a Vaishnava only by begging forgiveness
of that Vaishnava himself. But Shachi too set an example: though she had given birth to Gaurahari, she was not in the
least proud, and on realising her fault she went without delay to ask forgiveness. <!-- I2 I3 -->

## Her Son's Sannyasa
Lochana Dasa relates that even before the sannyasa Shachi saw in a dream her son receiving the mantra from Keshava
Bharati. And when the news of His intention reached her, she wept and begged Him to remain a householder. The Lord
comforted her in many ways, taught her to worship Krishna and revealed to her the divine form of Krishna. According to
the “Chaitanya-bhagavata”, He promised her:

“Two more births, at the beginning of sankirtana, I shall soon be your son.” (CB, Madhya 27.47)

These words are understood as a promise to appear in the holy name and in the Deity, for the Lord's name, form and
very being are non-different. <!-- F84 F85 F109 Q12 I5 -->

On the eve of His departure Shridhara brought the Lord a lau gourd, and Shachi cooked it with milk — one of His
favourite dishes. Even Nityananda, hearing of the Lord's decision, stood stunned at the thought of what would become of
Shachi. The “Chaitanya-bhagavata” places the Lord beside Kapila, who left his mother Devahuti, and Shuka, who left his
father Vyasa: the jewel of sannyasis went away, leaving alone such a mother as Shachi. Yet a leaving for the highest
goal is no true parting. <!-- F36 F105 F101 -->

When they learned that He had gone, Shachi and Vishnupriya fainted. Lochana Dasa describes how Shachi wept holding
Vishnupriya on her lap and lay as if dead; then, remembering His words, “Whoever chants the name will attain Me,”
everyone — Shachi, Vishnupriya, children and old people, young men and girls — sat down to chant the holy name, making
Gauranga their refuge. Vamshivadana too described in a song the weeping of the young wife and her mother-in-law: on
hearing it, the poet rolls on the ground. <!-- F86 F108 F54 -->

For four days, while the Lord wandered half-mad, pressing towards Vrindavana, Sita stayed with Shachi: Shachi lay on the
ground like one dying — as if the sun had left her sky for ever. At last Nityananda brought the Lord by a ruse to
Shantipur, and Chandrashekhara Acharyaratna was sent to Navadvipa for Shachi and the devotees.[^14] The news brought her
almost lifeless body back to life, and the next morning he brought her by palanquin to Advaita's house; with her came
Murari Gupta, Nandana Acharya, Shridhara, Damodara Pandita, Gangadasa and others. <!-- F91 F42 F113 -->

Seeing her son in the robes of a sannyasi, Shachi felt joy mingled with the pain of separation. The Lord prostrated
Himself at her feet with prayers. She took the cooking upon herself and prepared many of His favourite dishes, and He
ate them to soothe at least a little the pain His leaving had caused her. The festival went on for days, and no one
knew where the provisions came from. After ten days Shachi herself asked her son to settle in Puri: it was not so far,
the devotees could see Him every year, and those who could not would receive news. Having comforted His mother, the
Lord took leave of her and in 1510 went to Nilachala by way of Chhatrabhoga with Nityananda, Mukunda, Jagadananda and
Damodara. <!-- F43 F44 -->

## The Mother in Navadvipa
When the Lord returned from His journey through the South, Nityananda and Jagadananda sent Krishnadasa to Bengal with
prasada to inform Shachi. The news seemed to give life back to her, to Advaita, Srivasa and Haridasa, and the devotees,
with her permission, began to prepare for Puri. Paramananda Puri was staying at her house then; Shachi fed him from her
own hands with great love, and on hearing the news he hurried to the Lord ahead of the others. And when the Bengali
devotees set out for Nilachala for the third time, Advaita first went to Shachi to take leave of her. <!-- F57 F58 F41 -->

She was not forgotten. Srivasa and his brothers, having moved to Kumarahatta, went every year both to Puri and to
Mayapur to see Shachi, and then stayed several days in their own house in Navadvipa. Gadadhara Pandita, setting out once
for Bengal, told the Lord, “I am going to see Shachimata, not for Your sake.” After the Chaturmasya Vasudeva Datta,
Murari Gupta and Jagadananda came to her with news of the Lord, and she joyfully kept them with her and would not let
them go for a long time. <!-- F55 F56 F114 -->

The Lord sent her Damodara Pandita, the strictest of His companions:

“Besides you I see no one who could look after her and protect her. You have taken care even of Me.” (CC, Antya 3.22)

Damodara was told to bow to her again and again on her son's behalf, to tell her about His life, and to remind her of
one secret:

“Tell her: ‘O My dear mother! Again and again I come to your house and eat all the sweets and vegetable dishes that
you offer Me.’” (CC, Antya 3.30)

At the last Magha-sankranti, the Lord went on, she had cooked various dishes, cakes, sweet rice and thickened milk,
offered them to Krishna and sat down to meditate. Suddenly He came and hastily ate everything, and her eyes filled with
tears of joy; but when she wiped them, she saw an empty plate and decided she had imagined it. Then, in the bewilderment
of separation, she thought she had not made the offering at all, went to the kitchen, found the pots full, cleaned the
place again and offered once more.

“Thus again and again I ate all the offerings you made Me, drawn by your pure love.” (CC, Antya 3.38)

“I live in Jagannatha Puri only in obedience to your order. But the power of your pure love draws Me so irresistibly
that I cannot resist.” (CC, Antya 3.39)

Damodara also brought her Jagannatha's prasada for her and the devotees of Navadvipa. <!-- F59 F60 Q8 -->

Later Jagadananda told her the same, naming the very days, and Shachi came to believe that these visits had been no
dream but reality. Every year the Lord sent Jagadananda to Navadvipa with a cloth He had worn, with prasada and sweets,
and he would talk with her at length about her son, easing her separation at least a little. In sending His mother
Jagannatha's prasada, the sannyasi Lord showed not worldly attachment but His transcendental love for His devotees.
Krishnadasa Kaviraja says:

“The Lord is always drawn by divine love, for such is His nature. Therefore Sri Chaitanya Mahaprabhu always manifested
Himself in four places: in the house-temple of mother Shachi, wherever Nityananda Prabhu danced, during the kirtana of
Srivasa Thakura, and in the house of Raghava Pandita.” (CC, Antya 2.34–35) <!-- F61 F62 F111 I6 F63 Q5 -->

Five years after His sannyasa the Lord Himself came to Bengal to see His mother and the Ganga; at that time He also
stayed seven days in Kuliya and bestowed His mercy on Devananda Pandita. <!-- F94 -->

Nityananda, preaching in Bengal, would come to Mayapur to see Shachi and, unable to bear separation from Gaura, would
take leave of her and go to Nilachala again. Once, at the festival for the anniversary of Madhavendra Puri's departure
in Advaita's house, all the cooking was taken on by Shachi — “Ai”, as the “Chaitanya-bhagavata” calls her — surrounded
by the wives of the Vaishnavas, and the Lord with all His devotees partook of what she had prepared. After His wedding
Nityananda brought His wives, Vasudha and Jahnava, to her; Shachi embraced them again and again, seated them on her lap
and fondly pinched their cheeks. At her wish Nityananda then lived nearby for some time before settling in
Khardaha.[^15] <!-- F106 F100 Q11 F70 F64 -->

Ishana and Vamshivadana were always with her, servants and protectors of Shachi and Vishnupriya; later, according to
one biography, their affairs were also managed by Gadadhara Dasa, who moved to Katwa when they were gone. Every morning
Shachi went with Vishnupriya to bathe in the Ganga. <!-- F49 F52 F53 -->

## Departure and Remembrance
When and how Shachi left this world the biographies do not say. We know only that Vamshivadana, after the departure of
the Lord and of Shachimata, served Vishnupriya and the aged Ishana, and that Jahnava, coming to the Lord's house after
the festival at Kheturi, did not find Shachi there and grieved that she had missed her.[^16] Srinivasa Acharya, who came
to Navadvipa to see the Lord's family, was blessed by Shachi in a dream. <!-- F51 F50 F71 F65 -->

Songs keep her memory. Vasudeva Ghosha sings of the Boy who whirls about Shachi's courtyard, hides and teases His mother,
and when she calls, “Vishvambhara, where are You?” runs to her and catches the hem of her sari. And Narottama Dasa sums
it up: the son of the king of Vraja has become the son of Shachi, and Balarama has become Nitai. When the Lord's
birthplace was rediscovered at the end of the nineteenth century, Jagannatha Dasa Babaji proposed that the Deities of
Jagannatha and Shachi be installed there in one temple, and Mahaprabhu with Lakshmipriya and Vishnupriya in another.

## Queen of Vraja
Who, then, was this brahmana woman of Navadvipa? Krishnadasa Kaviraja answers:

“The king of Vraja, Nanda Maharaja, has now become the father of Sri Chaitanya, and Yashoda, the queen of Vraja, has
become His mother, Sri Shachidevi.” (CC, Adi 17.294)

“Now the son of Nanda Maharaja is Sri Chaitanya, and Baladeva is Sri Chaitanya's brother, Nityananda.” (CC, Adi 17.295)

And Kavi Karnapura:

“Yashoda and Nanda, the king of Vraja, who once in Vrindavana were mines of the rasa of love, have become Shachi and
Jagannatha Purandara — of this there is no doubt. Into them also entered the gods Aditi and Kashyapa, Kaushalya and
Dasharatha, and Prishni and her husband.” (GGD 37–38)

Thus all who in earlier descents had been mothers of God were united in Shachi.[^17] She is the very essence of
motherly love, and in that love — not in majesty — lies her secret: the Lord for whose sake sages fast cried in her
arms, asked her for food, and secretly came from Puri to her hearth. <!-- F7 F8 Q1 Q2 -->
