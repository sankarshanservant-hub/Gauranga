# Kalidasa
*Калидас · @kalidasa*

## Raghunatha Dasa's Uncle
In the sixteenth chapter of the Antya-lila of the “Sri Chaitanya-charitamrita,” Krishnadasa Kaviraja tells of a Bengali
devotee who knew nothing but the name of Krishna and who all his life sought one thing alone — the remnants of the
Vaishnavas' meals. This is Kalidasa. The “Charitamrita” calls him simply “a devotee named Kalidasa” (Antya 16.5); the
biographies call him Sri Kalidasa, the sadhu Kalidasa, the Vaishnava sadhu, and in the “Prema-vilasa” he is “Kalidasa
Mahashaya” (16.262). <!-- F1 -->

He was Raghunatha Dasa Gosvami's uncle within the clan (*jnati-khura* — not a father's brother, but an uncle by kinship;
one of the biographies calls him a distant uncle):

“He was Raghunatha dasa's uncle within the clan, and he had grown old honouring the remnants of Vaishnavas.” (CC, Antya 16.8)

<!-- F3 Q2 --> By birth, the biographies tell us, he was a kayastha; the “Charitamrita” does not name his varna, but Jhadu
Thakura, of whom we shall hear below, calls him “a most honoured guest” and “a noble gentleman” (Antya 16.18, 16.23). His
family, like Raghunatha's, was of high standing, yet he himself, says one of the biographies, sought no honour on account
of his birth. <!-- F2 F4 -->

He lived in the Hooghly district, not far from Saptagrama, Raghunatha's home. Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati writes in
the “Anubhashya” that the shripats of Kalidasa and of Jhadu Thakura were in the same village — Bhedo, or Bhaduya: about
three miles south of Krishnapura, where Srila Dasa Gosvami appeared, and about a mile west of Bandel junction; the post
office is Devanandapura.[^1] <!-- F5 F6 --> There too Srila Bhaktisiddhanta speaks of their Deities. Sri Madana-gopala,
whom Jhadu Thakura served, is still served in Bhedo by a Ramayat named Ramaprasada Dasa. The Deity whom Kalidasa served
was until recently served in Shankha-nagara on the bank of the Sarasvati; a little over twenty years earlier (before the
“Anubhashya” was written), Motilala Chattopadhyaya, a resident of Triveni, took Him and serves Him in his own home. The
“Anubhashya” does not give this Deity's name.[^2] <!-- F7 -->

Of the rest of his life in this world the sources are silent. Neither the primary sources nor the biographies speak of his
birth or family (apart from his kinship with Raghunatha), of disciples, or of the years of his life; when and where he
left this world, and where he rests, is unknown. His days of appearance and departure are not found in the Vaishnava
calendar of the Sri Chaitanya Saraswat Math either. <!-- F36 -->

## Malli, the Pulinda's Daughter
Who he is in Krishna's eternal pastimes, Kavi Karnapura tells us:

“Malli, the daughter of a Pulinda, has now become Kalidasa.” (“Gaura-ganoddesha-dipika” 190)

The Pulindas are a forest people of Vraja; their girls, full of love for Krishna, are His companions in the forests of
Vrindavana. The biographies take this verse to speak of our Kalidasa, Raghunatha's uncle, and so does our translation of
the “Gaura-ganoddesha-dipika.”[^3] <!-- F8 Q9 -->

## Krishna's Name in Everything
The “Charitamrita” introduces him thus:

“With them came a devotee named Kalidasa, who knew nothing but the name of Krishna. He was a great devotee, simple and generous, and even in his everyday dealings the name of Krishna served him as a sign.” (CC, Antya 16.5–6)

A great devotee of Sri Krishna Chaitanya, a *maha-bhagavata*, he spoke the name even when dealing with people about
worldly matters: “Hare Krishna” sounded in all that he did, and Krishna's name was for him both word and sign
(*sanketa*).[^4] <!-- F9 F10 Q1 --> Kaviraja adds:

“When he played at dice for fun, he would cast them saying, ‘Hare Krishna! Hare Krishna!’” (CC, Antya 16.7)

Srila Bhaktisiddhanta warns that this pastime of Kalidasa, who was devoted to Vishnu and the Vaishnavas alone, misleads
the outward eye. Whoever, lacking his firm devotion to the name, imitates him for the sake of his own senses — at dice, for
instance — will only increase his inclination to sin, and his imitation of chanting the name will turn into an offence
against the name (“Anubhashya” to Antya 16.7). <!-- F39 -->

One of the biographies writes that Mahaprabhu spoke especially to His intimate companions of Kalidasa's firm and peerless
devotion.[^5] <!-- F11 -->

## The Remnants of the Vaishnavas
Kalidasa did not look at a Vaishnava's caste: every Vaishnava, however low his birth, he honoured as a guru. So Srila
Bhaktisiddhanta writes in the headings of the “Anubhashya”: “Kalidasa is free of notions of caste with regard to
Vaishnavas” (to Antya 16.13, 16.20). <!-- F12 --> All his life, into old age, he joyfully honoured the remnants of the
Vaishnavas' meals — often in secret, so that they did not even know it:

“There was no Vaishnava in Gauda whose remnants he had not taken. He would go to every Vaishnava of brahmana birth, great and small, bringing the finest things as gifts. He would beg them for the plates with their remnants, and where he was refused, he would hide nearby. When a Vaishnava had eaten and thrown his plate away, Kalidasa would secretly take it up and lick it clean. He went with gifts to the homes of Vaishnavas of shudra birth as well, and in the same way secretly took their remnants.” (CC, Antya 16.9–13)

<!-- F13 F14 F15 --> Brahmanas and shudras here are by birth, “by seed,” Srila Bhaktisiddhanta explains: for Kalidasa all of
them were Vaishnavas first and foremost. He went with the best of gifts to each — to one born a brahmana, to one born in a
low family, even to an untouchable — and after the meal he asked for the remnants. They were seldom given; then he waited
aside, and when the leaf-plate was thrown away, even onto a rubbish heap, he honoured what was left on it.

In this there is neither eccentricity nor defiance of social custom. A Vaishnava, in whatever family he is born, is worthy
of worship; whoever judges a Vaishnava by caste goes, as the scriptures say, to hell. A Vaishnava is above the material
qualities of nature, and neither nation nor caste defines him. The remnants of a devotee's meal are no ordinary food but,
in Kaviraja's words, *maha-maha-prasada*, and Kalidasa took them “seeing them as transcendental” (“Anubhashya,” heading
to Antya 16.37). <!-- I3 -->

[^5]: In the sixteenth chapter of the Antya-lila, to which the biography refers, there are no such words of the Lord: there

    Kalidasa's devotion and praised his virtues with His own lips.
