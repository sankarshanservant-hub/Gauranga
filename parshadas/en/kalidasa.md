# Kalidasa
*Калидас · @kalidasa*

## Raghunatha Dasa's Uncle
In the sixteenth chapter of the Antya-lila of the “Sri Chaitanya-charitamrita,” Krishnadasa Kaviraja tells of a Bengali
devotee who knew nothing but the name of Krishna and who all his life sought one thing alone — the remnants of the
Vaishnavas' meals. This is Kalidasa. The “Charitamrita” calls him simply “a devotee named Kalidasa” (Antya 16.5); the
biographies call him Sri Kalidasa, the sadhu Kalidasa, the Vaishnava sadhu, and in the “Prema-vilasa” he is “Kalidasa
Mahashaya” (16.262). <!-- F1 -->

He was Raghunatha Dasa Gosvami's uncle within the clan (*jnati-khura* — not a father's brother, but an uncle by kinship;
one of the biographies calls him a distant uncle):

“He was Raghunatha dasa's uncle within the clan, and he had grown old honouring the remnants of Vaishnavas.” (CC, Antya 16.8)

<!-- F3 Q2 --> By birth, the biographies tell us, he was a kayastha; the “Charitamrita” does not name his varna, but Jhadu
Thakura, of whom we shall hear below, calls him “a most honoured guest” and “a noble gentleman” (Antya 16.18, 16.23). His
family, like Raghunatha's, was of high standing, yet he himself, says one of the biographies, sought no honour on account
of his birth. <!-- F2 F4 -->

He lived in the Hooghly district, not far from Saptagrama, Raghunatha's home. Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati writes in
the “Anubhashya” that the shripats of Kalidasa and of Jhadu Thakura were in the same village — Bhedo, or Bhaduya: about
three miles south of Krishnapura, where Srila Dasa Gosvami appeared, and about a mile west of Bandel junction; the post
office is Devanandapura.[^1] <!-- F5 F6 --> There too Srila Bhaktisiddhanta speaks of their Deities. Sri Madana-gopala,
whom Jhadu Thakura served, is still served in Bhedo by a Ramayat named Ramaprasada Dasa. The Deity whom Kalidasa served
was until recently served in Shankha-nagara on the bank of the Sarasvati; a little over twenty years earlier (before the
“Anubhashya” was written), Motilala Chattopadhyaya, a resident of Triveni, took Him and serves Him in his own home. The
“Anubhashya” does not give this Deity's name.[^2] <!-- F7 -->

Of the rest of his life in this world the sources are silent. Neither the primary sources nor the biographies speak of his
birth or family (apart from his kinship with Raghunatha), of disciples, or of the years of his life; when and where he
left this world, and where he rests, is unknown. His days of appearance and departure are not found in the Vaishnava
calendar of the Sri Chaitanya Saraswat Math either. <!-- F36 -->

## Malli, the Pulinda's Daughter
Who he is in Krishna's eternal pastimes, Kavi Karnapura tells us:

“Malli, the daughter of a Pulinda, has now become Kalidasa.” (“Gaura-ganoddesha-dipika” 190)

The Pulindas are a forest people of Vraja; their girls, full of love for Krishna, are His companions in the forests of
Vrindavana. The biographies take this verse to speak of our Kalidasa, Raghunatha's uncle, and so does our translation of
the “Gaura-ganoddesha-dipika.”[^3] <!-- F8 Q9 -->

## Krishna's Name in Everything
The “Charitamrita” introduces him thus:

“With them came a devotee named Kalidasa, who knew nothing but the name of Krishna. He was a great devotee, simple and generous, and even in his everyday dealings the name of Krishna served him as a sign.” (CC, Antya 16.5–6)

A great devotee of Sri Krishna Chaitanya, a *maha-bhagavata*, he spoke the name even when dealing with people about
worldly matters: “Hare Krishna” sounded in all that he did, and Krishna's name was for him both word and sign
(*sanketa*).[^4] <!-- F9 F10 Q1 --> Kaviraja adds:

“When he played at dice for fun, he would cast them saying, ‘Hare Krishna! Hare Krishna!’” (CC, Antya 16.7)

Srila Bhaktisiddhanta warns that this pastime of Kalidasa, who was devoted to Vishnu and the Vaishnavas alone, misleads
the outward eye. Whoever, lacking his firm devotion to the name, imitates him for the sake of his own senses — at dice, for
instance — will only increase his inclination to sin, and his imitation of chanting the name will turn into an offence
against the name (“Anubhashya” to Antya 16.7). <!-- F39 -->

One of the biographies writes that Mahaprabhu spoke especially to His intimate companions of Kalidasa's firm and peerless
devotion.[^5] <!-- F11 -->

## The Remnants of the Vaishnavas
Kalidasa did not look at a Vaishnava's caste: every Vaishnava, however low his birth, he honoured as a guru. So Srila
Bhaktisiddhanta writes in the headings of the “Anubhashya”: “Kalidasa is free of notions of caste with regard to
Vaishnavas” (to Antya 16.13, 16.20). <!-- F12 --> All his life, into old age, he joyfully honoured the remnants of the
Vaishnavas' meals — often in secret, so that they did not even know it:

“There was no Vaishnava in Gauda whose remnants he had not taken. He would go to every Vaishnava of brahmana birth, great and small, bringing the finest things as gifts. He would beg them for the plates with their remnants, and where he was refused, he would hide nearby. When a Vaishnava had eaten and thrown his plate away, Kalidasa would secretly take it up and lick it clean. He went with gifts to the homes of Vaishnavas of shudra birth as well, and in the same way secretly took their remnants.” (CC, Antya 16.9–13)

<!-- F13 F14 F15 --> Brahmanas and shudras here are by birth, “by seed,” Srila Bhaktisiddhanta explains: for Kalidasa all of
them were Vaishnavas first and foremost. He went with the best of gifts to each — to one born a brahmana, to one born in a
low family, even to an untouchable — and after the meal he asked for the remnants. They were seldom given; then he waited
aside, and when the leaf-plate was thrown away, even onto a rubbish heap, he honoured what was left on it.

In this there is neither eccentricity nor defiance of social custom. A Vaishnava, in whatever family he is born, is worthy
of worship; whoever judges a Vaishnava by caste goes, as the scriptures say, to hell. A Vaishnava is above the material
qualities of nature, and neither nation nor caste defines him. The remnants of a devotee's meal are no ordinary food but,
in Kaviraja's words, *maha-maha-prasada*, and Kalidasa took them “seeing them as transcendental” (“Anubhashya,” heading
to Antya 16.37). <!-- I3 -->

## At Jhadu Thakura's Home
What this practice of Kalidasa's was like, Kaviraja shows by a single example:

“There was a Vaishnava named Jhadu, of the *bhuinmali* caste. Kalidasa went to his home, taking mangoes with him. He presented the mangoes, bowed at his feet, and then paid his respects to his wife.” (CC, Antya 16.14–15)

The bhuinmalis are one of the lowest castes of Bengal, a caste of sweepers akin to the haddis; but Jhadu was a true
Vaishnava, and the Vaishnavas called him Jhadu Thakura. Having heard of his greatness, Kalidasa came to him with the best
ripe, sweet mangoes to be had at that season. <!-- F16 F17 --> Jhadu was abashed: how could a man of high birth bow to him
and bring him gifts? He could not believe his good fortune and felt he had no right to accept such honour — and yet he
welcomed his guest with love:

“Jhadu Thakura was sitting with his wife; seeing Kalidasa, he received him with great respect. After talking with him a while, Jhadu Thakura said to him sweetly: ‘I am of low birth, and you are a most honoured guest. How shall I serve you? Give me leave to take provisions to a brahmana's house. There you can take prasada, and that will give me life.’” (CC, Antya 16.16–19)

<!-- F18 F19 --> Kalidasa answered him:

“Kalidasa said, ‘Thakura, be merciful to me! I, a fallen sinner, have come to see you. By seeing you I have become pure. My purpose is fulfilled, and my life has borne its fruit. I have one desire, if you will be merciful: give me the dust of your feet, and place your foot on my head.’” (CC, Antya 16.20–22)

One of the biographies adds a thought on the greatness of a Vaishnava: the Ganga purifies a sinner only when he touches
her waters, but a Vaishnava purifies him merely by being seen.[^6] <!-- F20 I4 --> At these words Jhadu trembled: for him
even to hear such things was a sin leading to hell. With folded hands he begged:

“The Thakura said, ‘It is not fitting to say such things. I am of low birth, and you are a noble gentleman.’” (CC, Antya 16.23)

In the headings of the “Anubhashya” Srila Bhaktisiddhanta calls these the humble words of Jhadu Thakura, “free of pride
and giving honour to others” (*amani*, *manada*). <!-- F21 --> Then Kalidasa recited to him verses on the glory of a
devotee:

“Even a knower of the four Vedas is not dear to Me if he is not devoted to Me, but My devotee is dear to Me even if he was born a dog-eater. One should give to him and accept from him; he is to be worshipped just as I am.” (“Itihasa-samuchchaya,” quoted in the “Hari-bhakti-vilasa,” 10.91; CC, Antya 16.25)

“I consider a dog-eater whose mind, words, deeds, wealth and very life are offered to the Lord of the lotus navel better than a brahmana endowed with the twelve qualities who has turned away from His lotus feet. Such a devotee purifies his whole family; the brahmana swollen with pride cannot.” (“Srimad-Bhagavatam,” 7.9.10; CC, Antya 16.26)

“Oh, how wonderful! Even a dog-eater is worthy of the highest honour if Your name is on the tip of his tongue. Those who utter Your name have already performed austerities and offered sacrifices; they have bathed in all the holy waters, they are truly noble, and they have studied the Vedas.” (“Srimad-Bhagavatam,” 3.33.7; CC, Antya 16.27)

The first verse is spoken by the Lord Himself; the second is Prahlada's prayer to Lord Nrisimha; the third, Devahuti's
words to Kapila.[^7] <!-- F22 Q3 Q4 Q5 --> Jhadu heard them with joy, but would not apply them to himself:

“Hearing this, the Thakura said, ‘What scripture says is true: one in whom there is devotion to Krishna is not low. But I am of low birth, and I have no devotion to Krishna. Others may be so blessed, but I have no such power.’” (CC, Antya 16.28–29)

From the lips of a great soul, he said, such words would rather cast him into hell. Srila Bhaktisiddhanta explains this
passage with many testimonies from the “Mahabharata,” the “Srimad-Bhagavatam” and the “Padma” and “Garuda Puranas”: the mark
of a brahmana is not birth but nature and conduct, and “transcendental brahmanhood belongs eternally to a Vaishnava.
Therefore, if even one born in a low family has gained devotion to Krishna, he can no longer be of low caste.” And the
words “I am not a Vaishnava” “only show Jhadu Thakura's magnanimity, worthy of a Vaishnava, and his freedom from pride.
Maha-bhagavatas are free from identifying themselves with the body, and by their very nature they speak with the humility
worthy of pure devotees” (“Anubhashya” to Antya 16.28–29). So speaks not one who lacks devotion, but one who has risen
above every thought of his own greatness. <!-- F23 -->

Kalidasa bowed and asked leave to go. Jhadu, honouring his guest, walked a little way with him and returned home. But
Kalidasa did not leave:

“When the Thakura had seen him off and returned home, Kalidasa went to the spot where Jhadu's footprints lay. He gathered up that dust, smeared it all over his body, and then hid near Jhadu's house.” (CC, Antya 16.31–32)

He smeared himself with the dust, Srila Bhaktisiddhanta explains, because he saw a Vaishnava in Jhadu Thakura.[^8]
<!-- F24 --> Meanwhile, in the house:

“Entering the house, Jhadu Thakura saw the mangoes and offered them all to Krishnachandra within his mind. His wife took the mangoes out of their wrappings of banana leaf and bark and handed them to him, and he ate them, sucking out the juice. He sucked the stones and threw them into the wrappings; and when his wife had fed him, she ate after him. She filled the wrappings with the sucked stones, carried them outside and threw them into the pit for remnants.” (CC, Antya 16.33–36)

“Offered within his mind” does not mean “thought of”: Jhadu Thakura, says Srila Bhaktisiddhanta, “the guru of all
brahmanas,” worshipped within his mind the Deity manifest in the mind (*manomayi-archa*), and so ate the mangoes as
Krishna's remnants; and his wife, a Vaishnavi, honoured the remnants of her Vaishnava husband (“Anubhashya,” headings to
Antya 16.33, 16.35). <!-- F25 --> Then Kalidasa came out of hiding:

“Kalidasa sucked those wrappings, stones and skins, and as he went on, he grew ever more jubilant with divine love.” (CC, Antya 16.37)

The joy of love for Krishna awoke in him, and he felt himself purified. <!-- F26 -->

So he did with everyone: “In this way Kalidasa honoured the remnants of all the Vaishnavas who lived in the land of
Gauda” (CC, Antya 16.38). He was following the word of scripture which Kaviraja repeats at the end of the story: the dust
of a Vaishnava's feet, the water that has washed his feet, and the remnants of his meal help one in bhakti (16.60).

## In Nilachala
Every year the Bengali devotees came to Puri for the Ratha-yatra to see Mahaprabhu. A year after their previous visit to
the Lord, Kalidasa came with them:[^9]

“The next year all the devotees of Gauda came again and, as before, met the Lord.” (CC, Antya 16.4)

<!-- F28 --> The Lord, who dwells in every heart, at once understood who stood before Him, and was pleased with him:

“When Kalidasa came to Nilachala, Mahaprabhu showed him great mercy.” (CC, Antya 16.39)

<!-- F29 --> Every day, before going in to see Jagannatha, the Lord washed His feet at the Lion Gate:

“Every day, when the Lord went to see Lord Jagannatha, Govinda accompanied Him with His waterpot. On the north side of the Simha-dvara, sheltered behind the gate, at the foot of the twenty-two steps, there was a small pit. In that pit the Lord would wash His feet, and then He would go to see Lord Jagannatha. Mahaprabhu had given Govinda a rule: ‘See that no one takes the water that washes My feet.’ No living being could take that water; only intimate devotees contrived to get it, on one pretext or another.” (CC, Antya 16.40–44)

The pit lay outside the temple courtyard; now, writes Srila Bhaktisiddhanta, houses have been built on the spot.[^10]
<!-- F30 --> This prohibition is neither a whim nor strictness for its own sake. The “Anubhashya” calls it “the strict rule
of the Lord, who in the role of acharya teaches the world”: no one but the most intimate devotees may take the water that
has washed His feet (headings to Antya 16.43–44). <!-- F31 --> And then one day:

“One day, as the Lord was washing His feet there, Kalidasa came and held out his cupped hands. He drank one palmful, two palmfuls, three palmfuls, and then Mahaprabhu stopped him: ‘From now on, do not do this again. With this much I have fulfilled your desire.’” (CC, Antya 16.45–47)

Kalidasa held his hands right beneath the Lord's feet, and no one hindered him.[^11] In stopping him the Lord was not
angry: He Himself gave him the water from His feet and said that He had fulfilled his desire — and He forbade him to take
it again so that the acharya's rule should stand. <!-- F32 --> Why then was Kalidasa given what was withheld from others?
Kaviraja answers:

“Lord Chaitanya, crest jewel of the all-knowing, knew Kalidasa's heart and his faith in the Vaishnavas. Pleased with him for that quality, the Lord showed him a mercy rarely granted to others.” (CC, Antya 16.48–49)

Srila Bhaktisiddhanta says that for his transcendental faith in the Vaishnavas Kalidasa received a mercy unattainable
even by Brahma and the rest (“Anubhashya,” heading to Antya 16.49). Having honoured the remnants and the foot-dust of
Vaishnavas all his life, he had gained a store of spiritual merit (*sukriti*), and by its power he received the water from
the feet of the Lord Himself. <!-- F33 Q6 -->

But the mercy did not end there. Returning from darshan to the house of Kashi Mishra, the Lord took His midday meal, while
Kalidasa waited at the gate:

“Kalidasa was waiting hopefully at the outer door. Knowing this, the Lord gave Govinda a sign. Govinda understood all the Lord's signs and gave Kalidasa the plate with the Lord's remnants. Such is the greatness of the remnants of Vaishnavas: by taking them Kalidasa received the Lord's mercy in its fullest measure.” (CC, Antya 16.55–57)

By honouring the remnants of the Vaishnavas, Kalidasa came to the remnants of Mahaprabhu Himself. <!-- F34 Q7 -->
Kaviraja ends the story with the words: “In this way Mahaprabhu lived in Nilachala, and without anyone noticing He showed
Kalidasa great mercy” (CC, Antya 16.64). No one else knew how deep that mercy was.[^12] <!-- F35 -->

The “Prema-vilasa” too remembers Kalidasa. Sri Jahnava, instructing Nityananda Dasa, says: by the Lord's command no one
took the water from His feet, and He forbade it to Govinda; but Kalidasa Mahashaya, full of faith, took the foot-water of
Vaishnavas from every land and the remnants of everyone's meals, and the Lord, knowing this, praised his virtues with His
own lips. One day at the Lion Gate he drank three handfuls, and the Lord let him drink no more (“Prema-vilasa”
16.260–266). The poet says that Gaura, the ocean of prema, was then “afraid”: it is not fitting to give the water of His
own feet to a sadhaka. This is no fear in the worldly sense, but the care of the Lord as a sannyasi, who in the role of
acharya guards both the rule and the true good of the devotee himself.[^13] <!-- F38 -->

## The Three Powers of Sadhana
Having told of Kalidasa, Kaviraja turns to his readers:

“Therefore, casting off disgust and shame, take the remnants (*jhuta*) of Vaishnavas; through them you will obtain all you desire. The remnants of Krishna are called *maha-prasada*; when they become the remnants of a devotee, they are called *maha-maha-prasada*. The dust of a devotee's feet, the water that has washed his feet, and the remnants of what he has eaten — these three are the strength of spiritual practice. By serving these three one attains love for Krishna; all the scriptures declare this loudly again and again. Therefore I tell you over and over: listen, O devotees! Serve these three with faith. These three bring the joy of Krishna's name and of love for Him, and through them one gains Krishna's grace. Kalidasa is the witness to this.” (CC, Antya 16.58–63)

<!-- Q8 --> The dust of a Vaishnava's feet, the water from his feet and the remnants of his meal are three means that bestow
bhakti. Whoever serves them sincerely and with faith attains the name of Krishna free of offences, and through the name,
prema, the highest goal of life. Kalidasa is the living witness to this. <!-- I2 --> So too, in the review of the chapters
at the end of the “Charitamrita,” Kaviraja says of this chapter:

“In the sixteenth the Lord showed mercy to Kalidasa and revealed the fruit of eating the remnants of Vaishnavas.” (CC, Antya 20.128)

## Significance
Kalidasa is an example of faith in the Vaishnavas that stands above birth. He sought not honour but the dust of the feet
and the remnants of devotees, whatever their birth, and in humility called himself fallen before a Vaishnava of the
sweepers' caste. And the Lord, who is pleased with one who has faith in His devotees and honours their remnants, showed
him a mercy rarely granted to others: He gave him the water of His feet and the remnants of His own meal. There is nothing
the Lord would not give to such a devotee. <!-- I1 I3 F33 -->

One of the biographies remarks that Krishnadasa Kaviraja recorded this example for our sake — for those who seek the
highest good on the path of pure devotion. Yet Kalidasa is not to be imitated outwardly: his deeds were born of faith in
the transcendental nature of the Vaishnava, and without that faith, as Srila Bhaktisiddhanta warns, imitation becomes only
an offence. <!-- I5 -->
