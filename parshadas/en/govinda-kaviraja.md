# Govinda Kaviraja
*Говинда Кавираджа (Говиндадас) · @govinda-kaviraja*

## A Poet of Chiranjiva's Line
Among the disciples of Srinivasa Acharya were two brothers whom the Vaishnavas of Gauda called kavirajas, "kings of
poets": the elder, Ramachandra Kaviraja, and the younger, Govinda Kaviraja. The biographies call him Sri Govinda
Kaviraja, Sri Govindadasa Kaviraja, Govinda Sena, and in verse Sri Govinda-kavindra, "lord of poets"; he himself signs
the closing lines of his songs "Govinda dasa" or "Dasa Govinda." The "Bhakti-ratnakara" calls him Govinda and Sri Govinda
Kaviraja, and Srila Jiva Gosvami, in a letter to him, "Sri Govinda Kaviraja, the mahabhagavata"; the "Prema-vilasa" calls
him Govinda Kaviraja, Govinda Dasa and Govindadasa.[^1] <!-- F1 -->

His father, Sri Chiranjiva Sena, was a resident of Shrikhanda and a companion of Mahaprabhu; Krishnadasa Kaviraja names
him among the branches of Sri Chaitanya's tree:

"Mukunda dasa, Sri Raghunandana, Narahari dasa, Chiranjiva and Sulochana, all of Shrikhanda, are great branches, abodes
of Sri Chaitanya's mercy. Everywhere they give away the fruits and flowers of love." (CC, Adi 10.78–79)

Chiranjiva came of a vaidya family, a family of physicians; one of the biographies adds that he first lived in
Kumaranagara and moved to Shrikhanda only after his marriage.[^2] In Krishna's eternal pastimes he is, according to Kavi
Karnapura's "Gaura-ganoddesha-dipika," Chandrika, one of Sri Radha's maidservants. Govinda's mother was named Sri
Sunanda. Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati, in his "Anubhashya" on these verses, writes that Chiranjiva had two sons, the
younger of whom was Govinda dasa Kaviraja, the composer of padas. His elder brother was Sri Ramachandra Kaviraja. One of
the biographies also counts Govinda among the prominent devotees of Shrikhanda.[^3] <!-- F2 F3 F45 Q7 -->

Govinda's maternal grandfather was Sri Damodara Kaviraja, a poet whose name was known throughout the region and, as the
"Bhakti-ratnakara" says, a worshipper of Shakti.[^4] It was in his house that Govinda was born. <!-- F4 -->

## Birth
According to one of the biographies, Govinda was born in the year 1459 of the Shaka era, that is, in 1537 or 1538; the
original sources do not give the year, and the day of his appearance is not in the Vaishnava calendar of the Sri
Chaitanya Saraswat Math.[^5] <!-- F5 -->

The "Bhakti-ratnakara" tells of the birth itself. The child stayed in his mother's womb longer than was due, and Sunanda
suffered greatly. A maidservant went to tell Damodara. He was at that moment performing puja to the Goddess and
told her by a glance and a sign to take the yantra of the Devi to the woman in labour and show it to
her. The maidservant did not understand the sign: she quickly washed the yantra and gave Sunanda the water to drink
("Bhakti-ratnakara" 9.149). And the mother was easily delivered of a son. The author of the "Bhakti-ratnakara" sees in this
one of the two reasons why Govinda in his youth was devoted to the Goddess: he was born after his mother had drunk the water of
her yantra. <!-- F6 Q1 -->

Soon after Govinda's birth his father left this world, and the brothers grew up in the house of their grandfather,
Damodara. <!-- F7 -->

## A Worshipper of the Goddess
The second reason, says the "Bhakti-ratnakara," was the company in which he grew up: as one's companion is, so one
becomes. Under their grandfather's influence both brothers inclined to the worship of Shakti. Ramachandra later, by the
mercy of Srinivasa Acharya, received Vaishnava initiation, but Govinda remained devoted to the Goddess: he spoke only of
Bhagavati, honoured no one else, described her alone in his verses and songs, and instructed everyone around him to
worship her. One of the biographies notes with regret that this happened in a family whose father had been a
mahabhagavata and a personal companion of the Lord. <!-- F8 -->

But when the mercy of Srinivasa came upon Ramachandra, the younger brother's mind too was drawn to the Vaishnava
dharma. Govinda began to reflect: "Will worship of the Goddess not free me from the bonds of existence?" And then
Bhagavati herself spoke, unseen:

"Without serving Krishna, no one is freed from misfortune." ("Bhakti-ratnakara" 9.159)

Govinda understood that no path and no worship loosens the bonds except service to Krishna. Thus the one he worshipped
herself directed him to Krishna, for she too serves His feet.[^6] From that day Govinda ardently longed to serve Krishna.
He considered himself fortunate that his elder brother had taken shelter at Srinivasa's feet, for by his brother's
mercy he himself had been blessed. <!-- F9 F10 F11 Q2 -->

He longed to reach the Acharya's feet and was about to set out for Yajigrama — but learned that Srinivasa had already
gone to Vrindavana. Then Govinda began to reproach himself: the Vaishnavas had wished him well and told him about his
father, and he had not listened. The "Bhakti-ratnakara" gives his lament:

"My father, Chiranjiva Sena, is learned, a devotee of Chaitanya-chandra, a treasury of virtues. And I, born as such a
man's son, am utterly lost. This is only the fault of my karma — to whom can I tell it?" ("Bhakti-ratnakara"
9.165–166)[^7] <!-- F12 Q3 -->

The Devi herself, out of causeless mercy, had told him to serve Krishna, and some inclination to that service had
already arisen in him; but where was he to find a true guru? When this sorrow seized him again, a voice once more rang
out from the sky:

"Your desire will be fulfilled in a few days." ("Bhakti-ratnakara" 9.172)

From that day, says the "Bhakti-ratnakara," attachment to Krishna was born in Govinda, and Ramachandra, seeing it,
rejoiced greatly. <!-- F13 Q4 -->

## The Move to Teliya-Budhari
Meanwhile the Vaishnavas of Gauda were longing for Srinivasa and sent Ramachandra to Vrindavana to bring the Acharya
back. Before leaving, Ramachandra spoke with his brother. He rejoiced at his thirst to serve Krishna and promised that as
soon as the Acharya came, all his desires would be fulfilled. But to live in Kumaranagara, he said, was now dangerous:
there was unrest over their land. Let Govinda settle in Teliya-Budhari — a holy place between the Ganga and the
Padmavati, a large village of noble people, where their grandfather had stayed before. Govinda agreed and moved there a
few days later; the villagers warmly arranged a dwelling for him, and he built his house there.[^8] Budhari, in the
Murshidabad district, became his shripat, the place of his bhajana, and later Ramachandra's shripat as well: the
brothers lived there together. <!-- F14 F15 -->

## Initiation
At the devotees' wish Srinivasa returned from Vrindavana to Gauda. Narottama and Shyamananda were with him, and a flood
of Hari-kirtana spread over all Gauda. The Acharya travelled with Ramachandra through Vana-Vishnupura, Yajigrama,
Kantaknagara, Khanda and Kanchanagadiya; and there a messenger came running to him from Budhari: "Sri Govinda and the
others are waiting for you." When the Acharya arrived at Teliya-Budhari, Govinda and the whole village received him with
great solemnity. <!-- F16 -->

Govinda with deep devotion invited the Acharya to his house and begged for mercy. In tears he fell at his elder
brother's feet, asking him to give him over to the Acharya, and Ramachandra told Srinivasa what was in his brother's
heart. The Acharya, filled with joy,

"gave Govinda initiation into the mantra of Radha-Krishna." ("Bhakti-ratnakara" 10.171)

Thus Govinda became a disciple of Srinivasa Acharya in the line of Gopala Bhatta Gosvami.[^9] <!-- F17 F18 F34 Q5 -->

After the initiation Srinivasa stayed for some time in Budhari, and Govinda was absorbed in serving him. Later, as the
"Prema-vilasa" relates, Durga herself, instructing Chand Raya, held Govinda up as an example: Srinivasa's disciple Govinda
Kaviraja had once worshipped her, leaving everything else, and had begged her much for liberation, but to give
liberation is not in her power; by taking shelter at the Acharya's feet he gained bhakti for Krishna and was freed from
the fear of the round of existence ("Prema-vilasa" 18.202–204). <!-- F35 F34 -->

Govinda's life shows that even the firmest other faith gives way when the mercy of a pure devotee arrives. The Goddess
herself bears witness that there is no deliverance without service to Krishna, and the way to Him opens through shelter
with a true guru. And further: mercy that comes upon one member of a family comes upon the whole family — the elder
brother, having taken shelter with Srinivasa, became the channel of that mercy for the younger. <!-- I1 I2 -->

## King of Poets
In Gauda Govinda was known as a great poet. The "Bhakti-ratnakara" says of him:

"Govinda, Sri Ramachandra's younger brother, is full of bhakti; learned in all the shastras, he is praised by all as a
poet." ("Bhakti-ratnakara" 1.445)

People marvelled at both his learning and his gift: he could compose songs and sing them as well — he had a beautiful
voice. One of the biographies calls him a padakarta, a composer of padas, famed for this gift.[^10] <!-- F20 F23 -->

He received the title "Kaviraja" from more than one person. His guru was the first to value his poetry. While Srinivasa
was staying in Budhari, he joyfully told Govinda to describe the pastimes of Sri Krishna Chaitanya; Govinda began to
compose prose, verses and songs, and the Acharya, delighted, praised him and gave him the name "Kaviraja"
("Bhakti-ratnakara" 10.292–296). Later, in Vrindavana, Parameshvari dasa asked the Gosvamis to hear Govinda's poetry, and
all, delighted, said, "The title kaviraja befits him," and began to praise him as Sri Govinda Kaviraja (ibid.
11.145–147). The "Bhakti-ratnakara" also says that Sri Jiva, Sri Lokanatha and others in Vrindavana drank the nectar of
his songs, together gave him the title Kaviraja, and praised him greatly in verse (1.446–447). Srila Bhaktisiddhanta, in
the "Anubhashya" on CC, Adi 10.85, writes that the name "Kaviraja" was given to Ramachandra Sena and his younger brother
Govinda by Jiva Gosvami. According to one of the biographies, the title was given to him by Jiva Gosvami, Srinivasa
Acharya, Jahnava-mata and others, delighted by his songs full of bhakti.[^11] <!-- F24 Q6 -->

One of the Gosvamis' tributes has been preserved in the "Bhakti-ratnakara":

"The fragrance of the garland of verses connected with the moon Krishna, brought from the sandal mountain — Sri Govinda,
the king of poets — by the playful spring breeze, has intoxicated the bees that take shelter at the feet of the
celestial tree, the illustrious Jiva, and has caused wonder to all in the forests of Vraja. What could be higher?"
("Bhakti-ratnakara" 1.448)

Jiva Gosvami, in his letters from Vraja, asked again and again for the nectar of his songs, and Govinda, at the
Gosvami's bidding, sent them to Vrindavana (ibid. 1.449–450). When, after living with Ramachandra and Narottama in
Kheturi, he returned to Budhari and in solitude was gathering his songs, a letter came from Jiva addressed to "Sri
Govinda Kaviraja, the mahabhagavata," praising his songs about Krishna (ibid. 14.30–41). A letter that Ramachandra,
Narottama and Govindadasa wrote together to Jiva has also been preserved ("Prema-vilasa," appendix, letter 4).
<!-- F26 F32 F33 Q8 -->

Among Srinivasa's disciples eight kavirajas were especially honoured: Ramachandra, Govinda, Karnapura, Nrisimha,
Bhagavan, Vallabha Dasa, Gopiramana and Gokula. The "Karnananda" lists them thus: "The eldest of the kavirajas is
Ramachandra Kaviraja… His younger brother is Sri Kaviraja Govinda" ("Karnananda" 6.181–182).[^12] <!-- F25 -->

## Songs
With the permission of Santosha Datta, the king of Kheturi, Govinda composed the drama "Sangita-madhava," in which the
purvaraga, the dawning of love between Radha and Krishna, is wondrously depicted; Santosha, hearing it, was filled with
supreme joy ("Bhakti-ratnakara" 1.461–462). Verses of this drama about Ramachandra and Narottama are quoted in the
"Bhakti-ratnakara."[^13] Santosha and Govinda were great friends: "in everything they are one, only their bodies differ"
(ibid. 1.480). <!-- F21 F39 Q8 -->

Govinda is best known for his padas, songs for kirtana. One of the biographies mentions his song collections, such as the
"Gitamrita," as well known in Bengali literature.[^14] He composed his songs in Brajabuli, after the model of Vidyapati's
padas: from there came their language and form, while their content is the pastimes of Sri Gaura and Sri Radha-Krishna.
According to one of the biographies, his songs embody all the rasas described by Sri Rupa Gosvami in the
"Bhakti-rasamrita-sindhu" and the "Ujjvala-nilamani": shanta, dasya, sakhya, vatsalya and madhurya. The "Prema-vilasa"
tells of this too: Govinda, having taken shelter, asked permission to describe Gauranga's pastimes, and Srinivasa replied
that Vasudeva Ghosha had already sung Gaura's pastimes, and told him to describe freely the pastimes of Radha and Krishna
and to read the "Rasamrita-sindhu" and the "Ujjvala"; on merely hearing an outline of the book, Govinda grasped both the
rasa and the teaching, and from that day composed songs about the pastimes of both Gaura and Krishna ("Prema-vilasa"
14.186–200). The "Karnananda" says the same: Srinivasa with great mercy ordered the Kaviraja to compose songs on the
pastimes of Radha-Krishna in the manner of verses on rasa, and the earth floated in the nectar of prema of those songs
(1.111–112). The madhurya-rasa of his padas is the prema of Vraja, not worldly passion: Govinda's songs are built on Sri
Rupa's teaching on the rasas, and that is why they so move the hearts of their hearers. <!-- F22 F27 F28 I3 -->

The biographies quote several of his padas. One is a song of self-surrender to the Lord, "Bhajahu re mana
Sri-Nanda-nandana…": O mind, serve the son of Nanda; a rare human birth and the company of sadhus have been given to you —
cross the ocean of existence; wealth, youth and sons are unreliable, and Govinda dasa's desire is the nine kinds of
bhakti. The "Prema-vilasa" says he composed this song when he came to Srinivasa (14.185). Two padas about Gaura were composed for
kirtana: "Kandala kanaya kalevara kanti…" — of the Lord whose body shines like gold and is bathed in streams of tears of
love: He Himself chants His name and has others chant it, dances and makes even the deaf, the dull and the blind dance,
and embraces all without distinction; and "Sabahu gayata sabahu nachata…" — all sing and dance, and Gaura takes the
fallen in His arms, crying "Hari, Hari!", and Himself, without waiting to be asked, gives away prema in full. And,
finally, the song of separation "Madhava, tuhu rahila Madhupura…": Madhava, You have stayed in Mathura, and all Vraja is in
turmoil — Yashoda and Nanda sit as if blind, friends, flute and cows are abandoned, and the Yamuna is full to the brim.
This is vipralambha, the love of the residents of Vraja in separation from Krishna, the highest expression of their
prema. <!-- F29 Q9 -->

Four hundred and sixty of his songs were included in the collection "Pada-kalpataru." Govindadasa also sang of his guru,
in the song "Jaya jaya Srinivasa…," in whose signature he calls himself "helpless Govinda dasa." He also composed a song
about Narottama Thakura: "Glory, glory, glory to Thakura Narottama, the great king of prema-bhakti, whose minister, one
with him in body, is Ramachandra Kaviraja!"; it ends with the words: "To the poor and lowly he gave the treasure of
bhakti — only Govindadasa is left deprived" ("Bhakti-ratnakara" 1.439–444). And when King Harinarayana, "foremost among
Vaishnavas," asked him, Govinda composed for him a song about Sri Rama, signing it: "Govindadasa is firmly convinced in his
heart: Rama is Harinarayana's chosen Lord" (ibid. 1.456–460). <!-- F30 F31 F32 -->

## In the Circle of Srinivasa and Narottama
When the great festival at Kheturi was being prepared, the news reached Srinivasa and his companions at Budhari, in
the house of Govinda Kaviraja, and from there they all went together to Kheturi. Srinivasa, his disciple Ramachandra and
Ramachandra's brother Govinda actively helped to arrange the festival. The "Narottama-vilasa" relates that Srinivasa sent
Govinda Kaviraja to Shyamananda, and he showed Shyamananda the Deities and the stores for the festival (6.165–171); and
when the guests were assigned their lodgings, Govinda Kaviraja was put in charge of the lodging of Sri Raghunandana and
his people (6.320). <!-- F36 -->

Jahnava-devi, the consort of Nityananda, on her way to Kheturi came with her companions to Teliya-Budhari, and Govinda
Kaviraja respectfully received her and performed puja to her; after spending the night there, all went on to
Kheturi.[^15] Later, at the Acharya's bidding, Govinda accompanied Jahnava to Vrindavana, bowed there to the Gosvamis, and,
on returning, passed on their words to Narottama together with the book "Gopala-virudavali"; and when Jahnava was on her
way back, he took care of her reception in Budhari ("Narottama-vilasa" 9.23–297). He also received in his home
Virachandra, Nityananda's son: Virachandra came to Budhari, to the house of Govinda Kaviraja, and Govinda welcomed him
with great devotion and performed puja to him; pleased, Virachandra stayed with him two days and then went on to
Kheturi. <!-- F37 F38 -->

Govinda knew well the hearts of Ramachandra and Narottama. The "Karnananda," having said that Ramachandra and Narottama are
equal in virtue and there is no difference between them, adds: "The inner essence of both is known to Kaviraja Govinda,
and Govinda Chakravarti knows it too" (3.245). <!-- F40 -->

## Family and Departure
Govinda's wife was named Mahamaya; Srinivasa Acharya bestowed his mercy on the wives of both kavirajas ("Prema-vilasa"
20.15–16; "Karnananda" 1.113). His son Divyasimha also became a disciple of Srinivasa; one of the biographies names
Govinda's grandson as well — Ghanashyama, Divyasimha's son. <!-- F41 F42 F43 -->

Having taken shelter at Srinivasa's feet, Govinda, according to the "Prema-vilasa," spent thirty-six years in sadhana and
composition (14.198–199).[^16] According to one of the biographies, he left this world on the first day of the bright
fortnight of the month of Ashvina in the year 1535 of the Shaka era (1613), having lived some seventy-six years. The
biographies do not name the place of his departure, and the day of his departure is not in the calendar of the Sri
Chaitanya Saraswat Math. <!-- F19 F44 -->

## Significance
Govinda Kaviraja is one of the eight kavirajas among the disciples of Srinivasa Acharya. His path began far from Krishna,
but Bhagavati herself pointed him to Krishna, his elder brother's mercy brought him to a true guru, and the guru opened
to him the gift of singing the pastimes of Gaura and Radha-Krishna. Govindadasa's songs, composed according to Sri
Rupa's teaching on the rasas, were loved both by the Gosvamis of Vrindavana and by the Vaishnavas of Gauda, and hundreds
of them are gathered in the "Pada-kalpataru." <!-- F25 F28 F30 I1 I2 I3 -->
