# Nrisimhananda Brahmachari
*Нрисимхананда Брахмачари · @nrisimhananda*

## Pradyumna, Whom the Lord Named Nrisimhananda
His earlier name was Pradyumna Brahmachari. He worshipped Lord Nrisimhadeva, and Sri Chaitanya, seeing how devoted he
was to his Deity, gave him a new name — Nrisimhananda, “the joy of Nrisimha.” Listing the branches of Sri Chaitanya's
tree, Krishnadasa Kaviraja names him as the thirteenth branch and says of him:

“Pradyumna Brahmachari was a worshipper of Sri Nrisimha, and the Lord gave him the name Nrisimhananda.” (CC, Adi 10.35)

And once more, in the Antya-lila:

“His own name was Pradyumna Brahmachari; it was Gaura, the golden Lord, who named him Nrisimhananda.” (CC, Antya 2.53)
<!-- F1 F2 F3 Q1 Q2 -->

So the original sources call him: Vrindavana Dasa — Pradyumna Brahmachari; Murari Gupta — now Nrisimhananda, now
Pradyumna Brahmachari; Kavi Karnapura in his drama “Chaitanya-chandrodaya” calls him “the famous Sri
Nrisimhananda-brahmachari, formerly known as Pradyumna-brahmachari,” says that the Lord Himself gave him the new name for
his worship of Nrisimha, and explains the name in two ways: “the joy of Nrisimha” and “he who gives joy to Nrisimha”
(act 9). Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati writes in the “Anubhashya”: “Pradyumna, or Nrisimha Brahmachari.” Nrisimha was
his chosen Deity (*ishta-deva*); the “Charitamrita” says so plainly — “his worshipful Deity, Nrisimha” (Antya 2.61).
<!-- F1 F2 -->

Of his worldly life the sources say almost nothing: neither his birthplace, nor his parents, nor the year and day of his
appearance or departure. Nor are his days found in the Vaishnava calendar of the Sri Chaitanya Saraswat Math. Only Srila
Bhaktisiddhanta remarks in the “Anubhashya” that Pradyumna Brahmachari formerly lived in the village of Piyariganj near
Kalna.[^1] <!-- F7 -->

## The Servant of Nrisimha
Vrindavana Dasa speaks of him twice, and both times of his closeness to Nrisimhadeva. When, in Puri, after the arrival
of Svarupa Damodara, the Lord's associates were gathering around Him, he came too:

“Sri Pradyumna Brahmachari was the servant of Lord Nrisimha; Nrisimha Himself was manifest in his body. He came and
joined the Lord, understanding that Nrisimha was enjoying the pastimes of kirtana in the form of a sannyasi.” (CB, Antya
3.186–187)

And telling how the devotees of Gauda, led by Advaita, went to Nilachala for the Ratha-yatra, Vrindavana Dasa names him
among them:

“Pradyumna Brahmachari also went, with whom Nrisimha Himself speaks face to face.” (CB, Antya 8.12)[^2]
<!-- F4 F5 F6 Q4 Q5 -->

Murari Gupta, too, sees him among Advaita's companions on the way to Puri: “Vakreshvara Pandita and Pradyumna
Brahmachari” (Murari Gupta's “Karcha,” 4.17.4). That Nrisimha spoke with him face to face and was manifest in his body
is no worldly trance and no possession by an alien power. It is the Lord's mercy to His servant: Nrisimha Himself reveals
Himself to one who serves Him with love, and the devotee even then remains “the servant of Nrisimha.” And he recognized
his Lord in the sannyasi singing the names of Krishna in Nilachala — recognized that Nrisimha and Sri Chaitanya are one
and the same Lord — and stayed with Him. <!-- F4 F5 F6 F33 -->

Year after year he came to Puri with the Bengali devotees. On their first visit Gopinatha Acharya, standing on the palace
roof, pointed them out to King Prataparudra by name: “This is Hari Bhatta, this is Sri Nrisimhananda; this is Vasudeva
Datta, and this is Shivananda” (CC, Madhya 11.87). In another year Krishnadasa Kaviraja names him in a long list of those
who went to Puri, next to Shuklambara Brahmachari (CC, Antya 10.9–11). And Govinda Dasa's “Karcha” mentions Pradyumna
Brahmachari among the sannyasis and devotees who gradually gathered around the Lord in Nilachala — together with
Shankara Bharati, Paramananda Puri, Damodara Svami and others (3.502).[^3] <!-- F32 F33 F34 -->

## The Road of Jewels
After His sannyasa Nityananda cleverly brought the Lord to Shantipura, to Advaita's house, and from there the Lord went
to Puri; with Him went Nityananda, Jagadananda, Damodara Pandita and Mukunda Datta. On returning from His journey
through South India, He resolved to go to Vrindavana by way of Gauda. In Bengal He stayed in the house of
Vidya-vachaspati, Sarvabhauma's brother; then He went to Kuliya, where He showed His grace to Devananda Pandita and
forgave Gopala Chapala; and then He reached Ramakeli and met Rupa and Sanatana (CC, Madhya 1.150–153, 166). <!-- F12 -->

When Nrisimhananda heard that the Lord was going to Vrindavana, he began to serve Him in the way he knew — in
meditation. Krishnadasa Kaviraja tells it:

“When Nrisimhananda heard that the Lord would go to Vrindavana, he joyfully decorated the road in his mind. From the town
of Kuliya he paved the road with jewels and spread over it a bed of flowers with their stems removed. On both sides of
the road stood rows of flowering *bakula* trees, and here and there on either side lay divine ponds. Their bathing steps
were set with jewels, and lotuses bloomed in them; many birds chattered, and the water was like nectar. A cool breeze
blew, bearing many fragrances. Thus he paved the road as far as Kanai's Natashala.” (CC, Madhya 1.155–159) <!-- F13 -->

Kanai-natashala is a village on the Ganges near Gauda; according to one of the biographies, it lies some two hundred
miles north of Calcutta, five miles from Rajmahal, in what is now the Santal Parganas district. Beyond that place the
road would not go:

“Beyond that point his mind could not go, nor could he pave any further; the road came to a halt, and Nrisimhananda was
astonished. ‘I tell you with certainty — listen, O devotees: this time the Lord will not go to Sri Vrindavana. He will
turn back from Kanai's Natashala. You will see it later; I have said this with certainty.’” (CC, Madhya
1.160–162)[^4] <!-- F14 F15 -->

And so it happened. The Lord set out from Kuliya, and thousands of people followed Him (Madhya 1.163); but from
Kanai-natashala He turned back to Shantipura, and went to Vrindavana only later. Telling of this return, Krishnadasa
Kaviraja again recalls “how Nrisimhananda decorated the road for Him” (Madhya 16.214). <!-- F14 F15 F16 -->

Other original sources also remember this road. Murari Gupta writes that Nrisimhananda, absorbed in thought, paved the
way to Madhupuri with gold, silver, coral and gems, spread it with the finest Chinese silks and with flowers without
stems, and adorned it with ponds of lotuses and water lilies, with jewelled steps, cygnets and water hens; bringing the
road to Krishna's “place of dancing,” he enacted there Krishna's forest pastimes, and then, laughing and dancing,
announced to the devotees that from there the Lord would turn back. The devotees walked around him and fell prostrate
before him, and he lovingly bowed to them in return (Murari Gupta's “Karcha,” 3.17.6–13); and later Murari recalls “the
excellent road laid down by Sri Nrisimhananda” (4.25.30). Govinda-deva in the “Gaura-krishnodaya” says that the Lord
came to Shantipura “along the road adorned by Nrisimha, who knew beforehand of His coming” — with trees full of fruit
and flowers and the ground strewn with blossoms (13.37). Lochana Dasa, too, sings of this road in the
“Chaitanya-mangala,” but tells of it in his own way.[^5] <!-- F16 -->

This is no daydream and no play of fancy. Service in the mind (*manasi-seva*) is as real as service with physical
articles, and even higher; one of the biographies recalls that Rupa Gosvami in the “Bhakti-rasamrita-sindhu” gives the
example of a brahmana of Pratishthanapura who by service in the mind attained the company of Narayana in
Vaikuntha.[^6] And that Nrisimhananda's meditation could go no further than Kanai-natashala was a revelation of the
Lord's will: serving in his mind, the devotee learned where the Lord would go — and the prediction came true.

## The Lord Appears to Him
Krishnadasa Kaviraja says that Sri Gaura, who descended for the good of all the worlds, delivered souls in three ways:

“To some He granted the sight of Himself in person; in some places He entered a worthy devotee (‘avesha’), and in
others He appeared (‘avirbhava’). By being seen in person He delivered almost everyone; and He ‘entered’ the body of
Nakula Brahmachari. Before Pradyumna Nrisimhananda He appeared (‘avirbhava’). ‘I shall deliver the people’ — such is
the Lord's nature.” (CC, Antya 2.4–6) <!-- F9 Q7 -->

The first way is *sakshat*, when the Lord is present with the devotees in person; the second is *avesha*, when He enters
a devotee with His power, as He entered Nakula Brahmachari; the third is *avirbhava*, when He reveals Himself where He is
not present in person. As the example of the third Kaviraja names Nrisimhananda, already in the Adi-lila:

“His earlier name was Pradyumna Brahmachari; afterwards the Lord gave him the name Nrisimhananda. In him Sri Chaitanya
manifested Himself (*avirbhava*). The Lord has many such wonderful, unearthly ways.” (CC, Adi 10.58–59)

All three kinds of this mercy were tasted by Shivananda Sena (Adi 10.60). <!-- F9 Q8 -->

Srila Bhaktisiddhanta explains in the “Anubhashya” on this passage: when the Lord is present in person, the devotees all
appear alike — as servants of one kind; but Sriman Mahaprabhu Himself entered Nrisimhananda, and therefore Shivananda
Sena and others saw him exactly like Sri Gaurasundara: full of love for Krishna and performing more wonderful,
unearthly deeds than all the other devotees. They saw him so not because he himself had become God, but by the power of
the Lord who had entered His servant. <!-- F10 Q3 -->

And Kavi Karnapura writes in the “Gaura-ganoddesha-dipika”:

“The appearance (*avirbhava*) of Gaurahari was in Nakula Brahmachari; and His entering (*avesha*) is likewise to be
known in the Mishra named Pradyumna.” (“Gaura-ganoddesha-dipika” 73–74)

Literally the verse speaks of Pradyumna Mishra, the Orissan devotee of Puri. But Srila Bhaktisiddhanta in the
“Anubhashya” refers it not to the Mishra but to Pradyumna Brahmachari; so too does the “Gaudiya-vaishnava-abhidhana,”
which calls Nrisimhananda “an avesha of Gaura.”[^7] <!-- F8 Q6 -->

Neither avesha nor avirbhava means possession. The Lord does not seize a devotee with an alien power: He who is drawn by
love comes Himself to one who loves Him. “The Lord is always drawn by prema, for such is His nature” (CC, Antya 2.34) —
and therefore, Kaviraja continues, Sri Chaitanya constantly manifested Himself in four places: in mother Shachi's
household shrine, where Nityananda dances, at Shrivasa's kirtana and in the house of Raghava Pandita (Antya 2.34–35,
79–80). So the Lord delivers even those who cannot come to Him themselves. <!-- F11 I4 -->

## The Three Offerings
How the Lord appeared to Nrisimhananda, Krishnadasa Kaviraja tells in the second chapter of the Antya-lila:

“Appearing before Nrisimhananda, He took a meal. Hear of it attentively.” (CC, Antya 2.36) <!-- F17 Q9 -->

Once Shrikanta Sena, Shivananda's nephew, came to Puri alone. For two months he lived near the Lord, and then the Lord
sent him back to Bengal with a message: this year let no one come — He Himself would come to Gauda and meet Advaita and
all the devotees; and in the month of Pausha He would be Shivananda's guest, and Jagadananda, who lived with him, would
cook for Him (Antya 2.37–43). The devotees rejoiced and stayed at home. Shivananda and Jagadananda made everything ready
and throughout Pausha waited for the Lord every day until evening. But the month passed, the Lord did not come, and
both grew sorrowful (Antya 2.44–47). The “Charitamrita” does not name the year of these events: it was already in the
Antya-lila. <!-- F18 F31 -->

Then Nrisimhananda came to them. Seeing their sorrow, he asked its cause, and Shivananda replied: the Lord promised to
come — why has He not come? “Hearing this, the brahmachari said, ‘Be content. I shall bring Him on the third day’” (CC,
Antya 2.51).[^8] Both knew his power and his love for the Lord, and believed him without a moment's doubt (Antya 2.52).
<!-- F19 F20 -->

For two days he remained in meditation, and then he said to Shivananda:

“I have brought the Lord to the village of Panihati. Tomorrow at noon He will come to your house. Bring the provisions
for cooking; I shall offer Him bhiksha. Then I shall quickly bring Him here. I tell you this with certainty — have no
doubt at all.” (CC, Antya 2.54–56)[^9]

“Brought” does not mean that the devotee commands the Lord, as a yogi might by his powers. The Lord yields to love by His
own will, and Nrisimhananda's power is the power of his love. Shivananda brought everything he asked for (Antya
2.57–58). <!-- F21 F22 -->

At dawn Nrisimhananda himself cooked many dishes — soups, vegetable dishes, sweet rice, cakes (*pitha*) and various
delicacies — and divided them into three offerings: for Jagannatha, for Sri Chaitanya and for his own Deity,
Nrisimhadeva. Having offered them, he sat outside and became absorbed in meditation (Antya 2.59–61). Karnapura adds that
before cooking he bathed and was perfectly pure, and that he offered each plate in its own name, went out and closed his
eyes (“Chaitanya-chandrodaya,” act 9). <!-- F23 F24 -->

“He saw Chaitanya Gosani quickly come and sit down. He ate all three offerings, and nothing was left.” (CC, Antya 2.62)

This was no vision born of the mind: the Lord truly accepted his food, and it became prasada; later, in Puri, the Lord
Himself confirmed it. And Nrisimhananda, beside himself with happiness, with tears of bliss in his eyes, cried out:

“Alas, alas! What are You doing? You and Jagannatha are one, so You may eat His offering. But why do You take
Nrisimha's offering? I see that today Nrisimha has had to fast. If the master goes without food, how can his servant
live?” (CC, Antya 2.63–65) <!-- F25 F26 Q10 -->

This “reproach” is a play of love. “Although his heart rejoiced at seeing the Lord eat, outwardly, for Nrisimha's sake,
he made some show of sorrow” (Antya 2.66). In Karnapura he weeps aloud, streaming with tears — “in loving anger and with
gracious reproach”: “Today, through my fault, Nrisimha has been left hungry!” (act 9). He did not doubt the Lord, nor
was he offended with Him. The biographies explain: he knew that Mahaprabhu, Nrisimha and Jagannatha are not different on
the highest level, and he spoke so in order to show his fidelity to his chosen Deity. <!-- F26 -->

And Kaviraja discloses the meaning of this pastime:

“Krishna Chaitanya Gosani is Svayam Bhagavan, the Supreme Lord Himself: between Him and Jagannatha and Nrisimha there is
no difference at all. Pradyumna had a secret wish to know this, and the Lord showed it to him by eating the offerings.”
(CC, Antya 2.67–68)

This is not the impersonal “all gods are one.” Sri Krishna Chaitanya is Bhagavan Himself, the original Lord, and
Jagannatha and Nrisimha are His own forms, Vishnu-tattva; by tasting the three offerings at once, the Lord showed
Pradyumna that He is not different from all the forms of Vishnu. <!-- F27 Q11 -->

Pleased with the skilfully cooked dishes, the Lord went to Panihati (Antya 2.69). Nrisimhananda lamented, “Alas, alas!”
Shivananda asked the reason, and he answered:

“He alone has eaten the offerings meant for all three, and Jagannatha and Nrisimha have been left fasting.” (CC, Antya
2.71)

Shivananda began to doubt: had this really happened, or was Nrisimhananda speaking so, overflowing with divine love
(Antya 2.72)? Karnapura renders his thought with the words “in a state of avesha” (act 9); here too it is not
possession, but the same ecstasy of love. Nrisimhananda asked for provisions once more, and Shivananda brought them;
according to Karnapura, it was Shivananda himself who proposed it, and Nrisimhananda was pacified. And he cooked a second
time and offered the food to Nrisimhadeva (Antya 2.73–74). <!-- F28 F29 Q12 -->

The following year Shivananda, as usual, brought the Bengali devotees to Nilachala.

“One day, in the assembly, the Lord brought up the subject and began to speak of Nrisimhananda's virtues: ‘Last year, in
Pausha, he fed Me. Never have I eaten such sweets and vegetable dishes.’ Hearing this, the devotees were filled with
wonder, and then conviction was born in Shivananda's mind.” (CC, Antya 2.76–78)

In Karnapura the Lord says that last Pausha He accepted alms from Nrisimhananda and came to know how skilfully he cooks.

## Significance
When and where Nrisimhananda left this world the original sources do not say; the day of his departure, like the day of
his appearance, is not in our Vaishnava calendar. Only Lochana Dasa writes that, without finishing the road in his mind,
he attained Vaikuntha; but the “Charitamrita” shows him in this world after that as well (see note 5). <!-- F7 F16 -->

Krishnadasa Kaviraja counts him among the branches of Sri Chaitanya's tree, and the Lord Himself appeared to him and
tasted his cooking. His life is a story of the love that draws the Lord. Serving in his mind, he paved a road of jewels
for the Lord, and by where it ended learned His will. Having promised the sorrowing devotees to bring the Lord to them,
he kept his word, and the Lord Himself later praised his meal before everyone. And by the three offerings the Lord
revealed to him, and to all who hear this story, that Sri Krishna Chaitanya, Bhagavan Himself, is not different from
Jagannatha and Nrisimha; while Nrisimhananda's “complaint” is a lesson in fidelity to one's chosen Deity.
<!-- F3 F27 I3 I4 -->

One of the biographies warns: one who considers the Lord's body a product of material nature is deceived by the
illusory energy; he mocks such accounts as fables and passes this off as learning; bereft of faith in the Lord, he is a
pauper, deprived of all that is truly valuable, and gains only births and deaths. But for one who has faith, the story
of Nrisimhananda bears witness that the Lord still comes to those who serve Him with love. <!-- I2 -->
