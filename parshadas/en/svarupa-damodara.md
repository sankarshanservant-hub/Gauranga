# Svarupa Damodara Gosvami
*Svarupa Damodara Gosvami · @svarupa-damodara*

## The Lord's “Second Self”
Of all who surrounded Sri Chaitanya in Puri, none was as close to His heart as Svarupa Damodara. When Krishnadasa
Kaviraja Gosvami first brings him into the story, he speaks of him thus:

“The next day Svarupa Damodara arrived, whose heart was like a boundless ocean of rasa. He was a very close friend
of Sri Chaitanya.” (CC, Madhya 10.102)

And a little further on:

“He had deeply realized the essence of Krishna-rasa and had become the very embodiment of that divine love. At times
he was called a manifestation of the spiritual nature of Mahaprabhu Himself.” (CC, Madhya 10.111)

That is why the Vaishnavas call him Gaurasundara's “second self” (dvitiya-svarupa): the Lord's most intimate
confidant, the keeper of His secret moods, prema-rasa itself in the form of a devotee. <!-- F3 Q1 -->

## Purushottama Acharya of Navadvipa
Before sannyasa he was called Purushottama Acharya; he is sometimes also called Purushottama Bhattacharya. Little is
known of his family. His father was Padmagarbha Acharya, a learned pandita; his mother, whose name has not come down
to us, was the daughter of Jayarama Chakravarti. Jayarama lived in Bhitadia on the Brahmaputra (now in Bangladesh);
later he moved with his family to Navadvipa, gave his daughter in marriage to Padmagarbha and brought his son-in-law
to live with him. Soon after the birth of the son Padmagarbha left his wife and child in his father-in-law's house
and went away to study in Mithila and then in Kashi, so Purushottama grew up in Navadvipa, in the home of his
maternal grandparents. The “Prema-vilasa” adds that in Kashi Padmagarbha received initiation from Lakshmipati, the
guru of Madhavendra Puri, and that by other wives he had further sons, among them Lakshminatha Lahiri, Svarupa's
half-brother. <!-- F1 F102 F104 -->

The same “Prema-vilasa” relates that Purushottama, a treasury of every virtue, lived with his mother in Navadvipa
and became a beloved devotee of Chaitanya; he was well versed in the shastras, and everyone called him Acharya
(“Prema-vilasa,” 24.536–537). While Gaurasundara was performing His pastimes in Navadvipa, Purushottama Acharya
lived at His feet. Already then Mahaprabhu, seeing his extraordinary gift as a singer, gave him the name
“Damodara.” <!-- F5 F103 Q38 F4 -->

## Sannyasa in Kashi
When Mahaprabhu accepted sannyasa, Purushottama, seeing it, became as if mad with separation. He went to Kashi
(Varanasi)[^1] and there he too took sannyasa, from a sannyasi named Chaitanyananda Bharati. His guru taught him
Vedanta and told him to explain it to everyone. Sannyasa gave him the name “Svarupa,” and together with the name the
Lord had given him he came to be called Damodara-Svarupa, or Svarupa Damodara. The “Prema-vilasa” calls him “the
Lord's most intimate devotee, an ocean of rasa” (24.539). <!-- F6 F7 F4 F103 F106 -->

This sannyasa was not a step taken for standing in an ashrama. His heart was full of separation, and he wanted only
one thing — to serve Krishna free of hindrance and care:

“A strong longing arose in him to worship Krishna without any disturbance or obstacle, and therefore, guided by
this half-mad feeling, he accepted sannyasa. Though, following tradition, he gave up the shikha and the sacred
thread, he did not accept saffron cloth, but only changed his name to ‘Svarupa.’” (CC, Madhya 10.107–108) <!-- F8 I1 -->

According to the rules of the Dashanami sannyasis he went through the rites of renunciation: he performed the eight
shraddhas and the viraja-homa and gave up the shikha and the sacred thread. But he did not accept the yogapatta, nor
the rite of invoking the guru (gurv-ahvana), nor a sannyasa name and danda. That is why the brahmachari name
“Svarupa” stayed with him; had he accepted the yogapatta, it would have been replaced by the sannyasa title
“Tirtha.” Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati explains that in the Tirtha and Ashrama lines of the Dashanamis candidates
receive a brahmachari title with a vow of lifelong celibacy; “Svarupa” was such a title, and brahmacharis bearing it
usually become “Tirthas” once they accept saffron or the yogapatta[^2]. So he stopped at outward renunciation,
without taking on the marks of sannyasa. No wonder the “Bhakti-ratnakara” says that the Lord “through Ramananda
revealed the crushing of Kandarpa's pride, through Damodara revealed impartiality, through Haridasa showed
forbearance, through Sanatana and Rupa revealed humility” (“Bhakti-ratnakara,” 1.630–631). <!-- F9 F105 I1 F143 Q37 -->

He was an eternally perfect devotee, peerless in the science of music and in the shastras alike; supremely
detached and learned, he took shelter of Krishna with body and mind. He wrote a separate treatise on music, the
“Sangita-damodara.” <!-- F10 F11 -->

## Return to the Lord
The Lord accepted sannyasa on the full moon of Magha, came to Nilachala in Phalguna, and in Vaishakha set out on His
pilgrimage to the south. When He returned, the first to come to Him from Bengal were Paramananda Puri and the
brahmana Kamalakanta, and soon after them Svarupa Damodara. With his guru's permission he left Kashi and came to
Purushottama. <!-- F107 F12 -->

Coming before Mahaprabhu, he prostrated himself, embraced His feet and recited a verse — a prayer for Sri
Chaitanya's mercy:

“O ocean of mercy, Sri Chaitanya! The manifestation of Your compassion easily frees the heart from the dust of
mental agitation, and so the heart is very quickly cleansed of all impurity. Then, in the purified heart, the
rapturous birth of the highest devotion to Sri Krishna takes place. <…> O boundless kindness, Sri Chaitanya! When
will Your all-auspicious mercy touch me?” (CC, Madhya 10.119; “Chaitanya-chandrodaya,” 8.10) <!-- F13 Q2 -->

Gaurasundara raised His eternal “second self” from the ground and embraced him, and at that touch both fell
unconscious in loving rapture. Coming to Himself, the Lord said, “Today I saw in a dream that you would come. It is
good that you have come: I was as if blind, and with you I have regained My sight.” Svarupa answered with
repentance:

“Prabhu! Forgive me my offense. I went away and left You, and that was a great mistake. There is not a trace of
love for Your lotus feet in me. Were it otherwise, how could I have gone away? I am sinful and have no excuse. I
left You, but You did not forget me. With the rope of Your mercy You pulled me by the neck back to Your feet.” (CC,
Madhya 10.123–125)

The Lord embraced him again and said, “Krishna is merciful: by His mercy we have met again.” <!-- F14 F15 F16 F109 -->

Then Svarupa bowed at the feet of Nityananda, who embraced him with love; he bowed to Paramananda Puri and met
Jagadananda, Mukunda, Shankara, Sarvabhauma and the other devotees. Mahaprabhu gave him a secluded dwelling and a
servant to carry water and help with other needs. From then on he settled in Nilachala for good, to serve the
Lord. <!-- F17 F18 F37 -->

Soon, at the Lord's wish, Ramananda Raya too gave up the king's service and came to Nilachala; it was then that
Svarupa met him[^3]. Svarupa and Sarvabhauma, both former residents of Navadvipa, now lived in Purushottama as
kshetra-sannyasis, and both were bound to Ramananda by an eternal friendship; Svarupa and Ramananda were even
called “one soul.” <!-- F19 F108 F137 -->

## Singer and Guardian of Siddhanta
The Lord regarded Svarupa as His intimate and entrusted him with a special service: to examine everything brought
for Him to hear.

“If anyone wished to present his book, poem or song to Mahaprabhu, Svarupa Damodara first examined it, and only then
would Sri Chaitanya agree to hear it. Sri Chaitanya did not wish to hear anything that contradicted the conclusions
of Vaishnava siddhanta or contained rasabhasa, an incompatibility of spiritual moods.” (CC, Madhya 10.112–113)

Rasabhasa and deviation from siddhanta pained the Lord and roused His anger; so only what Svarupa had approved
reached His ears. <!-- F53 Q1 -->

Svarupa himself delighted the Lord with the songs of Vidyapati and Chandidasa and Jayadeva's “Gita-govinda.”
Krishnadasa Kaviraja continues:

“In singing and playing musical instruments he was like the heavenly gandharvas. In knowledge of the scriptures he
was like Brihaspati himself, the guru of the gods. Svarupa Damodara possessed such outstanding qualities that no one
could compare with him. He was very dear to Advaita and Sri Nityananda, and the very life of the whole community of
devotees headed by Srivasa Thakura.” (CC, Madhya 10.116–117) <!-- F54 -->

Vrindavana Dasa Thakura too glorifies him above all as a singer. As Gadadhara was the master of the “Bhagavatam,” so
Svarupa was the master of kirtana; he sang alone while the Lord danced, and hearing his loud singing the Lord would
lose outer consciousness and fall to the ground. “Whenever Svarupa Damodara sang the qualities of Krishna, who has
no equal, Lord Gauranga began to dance in great bliss” (CB, Antya 10.37). Among the Lord's sannyasi companions none
equalled him, and the Lord loved him just as He loved Paramananda Puri. These two — Svarupa and Paramananda Puri —
were inseparable from Mahaprabhu and took up the danda for the sake of His pastimes in sannyasa; Puri was absorbed
in meditation, Svarupa in kirtana, and “these two sannyasis were like the arms of Lord Chaitanya” (CB, Antya
10.49). <!-- F115 Q34 -->

Krishnadasa Kaviraja, listing the branches of Chaitanya's tree in Puri, names the same two first: “two — Paramananda
Puri and Svarupa Damodara — were Sri Chaitanya's very life and breath” (CC, Adi 10.124). When Sarvabhauma invited the
Lord and ten sannyasis for a whole month, he gave the Lord five days, Paramananda Puri five, Svarupa four and the
rest two each, and asked the Lord to come alone or with Svarupa. In the list of these ten sannyasi companions —
Paramananda Puri, Svarupa, Brahmananda Puri, Brahmananda Bharati, Vishnu Puri, Keshava Puri, Krishnananda Puri,
Nrisimha Tirtha, Sukhananda Puri and Satyananda Bharati — he is named second. <!-- F112 F124 -->

## Welcoming the Bengalis and Cleaning the Gundicha
Before the Ratha-yatra the devotees came from Gauda, and the Lord sent Svarupa together with His servant Govinda to
meet them with garlands and prasada. When the Lord Himself served them prasada and would not eat, and the devotees
would not eat without Him, Svarupa persuaded Him to sit down to the meal with Nityananda; then Svarupa, Jagadananda,
Damodara Pandita and others did the serving. <!-- F20 F123 -->

Before the festival the Lord Himself washed the Gundicha temple together with Nityananda, Advaita, Bharati, Puri and
Svarupa Damodara, while the others carried water. Then a simple-hearted devotee from Gauda poured water on the
Lord's feet and drank it. The Lord, inwardly pleased, outwardly grew angry in order to uphold proper conduct in the
temple; He called Svarupa and said, “Look what your Gaudiya has done: in the Lord's temple he washed My feet and
drank the water!” Svarupa took the devotee by the neck and led him out of the Gundicha; the man came back, humbly
begged forgiveness, and the Lord was pleased[^4]. So a Vaishnava may seem stern, yet within he is always full of
compassion. <!-- F21 F22 F118 I8 -->

The Vaishnavas understood the words “your Gaudiya” as a proclamation: all the Gaudiya Vaishnavas are under Svarupa,
and he is their leader. Hence his title “Sri Madhva-Gaudiyeshvara,” “lord of the Madhva-Gaudiyas,” by which he is
honored in the Vaishnava community. <!-- F23 F2 -->

When the cleaning was over, a great kirtana began. “Mahaprabhu dearly loved Svarupa Damodara's singing. When he sang,
Sri Chaitanya always danced in bliss and leapt high” (CC, Madhya 12.141). In Puri Svarupa also took part in the
water pastimes — at Narendra-sarovara during the Chandana-yatra and at Indradyumna after the cleaning of the
Gundicha; there he and his friend Pundarika Vidyanidhi, laughing, splashed water on each other (CB, Antya 8.124).
<!-- F24 Q4 F116 Q35 -->

## The Ratha-yatra
At the Ratha-yatra the chief lead singers of the kirtana groups were Svarupa Damodara and Srivasa Pandita. At first
there were four groups; in the first Svarupa sang and Advaita was the chief dancer. With the groups of Shantipura,
Shrikhanda and Kulina there were seven, each with two drummers, and the Lord appeared in all of them at once — just
as, Srila Bhaktivinoda Thakura explains, Krishna appeared in the rasa dance. When the Lord wished to dance in full
abandon, He brought the groups together and set Svarupa at the head of nine singers; Damodara Pandita too sang in his
choir. <!-- F25 F119 -->

The Lord danced to his singing, and Svarupa understood His every sign and sang following the movements of His
heart. The Lord was especially delighted by the song with the refrain “sei ta parana-natha painu / jaha lagi
madana-dahane jhuri genu”:

“At last I have found again the Lord of My life! In separation from Him I pined with grief, burning from the arrows
of love.” (CC, Madhya 13.113)

This song of meeting the Beloved after long separation, says Bhaktivinoda Thakura, came of itself to Svarupa's lips
when at the end of the dance the Lord was absorbed in Radha's mood at Kurukshetra. For the Lord the temple of
Jagannatha was Kurukshetra and the Gundicha was Vrindavana, and Svarupa understood everything He was experiencing at
the Ratha-yatra. <!-- F26 Q5 F120 -->

Before Jagannatha the Lord, dancing, acted out the song with His hands. When Gaura stepped back, Shyama stopped;
when He went forward, the chariot slowly moved forward: Gaurasundara, as it were, seated Shyamasundara on the
chariot of His heart. Raising His arms, He recited again and again the verse “yah kaumara-harah….” This verse comes
from the books on poetics (“Kavya-prakasha,” “Sahitya-darpana”; it is also in the “Padyavali,” 380) and on the
surface speaks of earthly lovers, but in the Lord's mouth it expressed the feeling of Radha meeting Krishna at
Kurukshetra. No one but Svarupa knew its meaning, and day and night the Lord relished it alone with Svarupa.
<!-- F27 Q6 F28 F121 -->

Krishnadasa Kaviraja finds no words for Svarupa's happiness:

“Who can describe the great fortune of Svarupa Damodara, who in body, heart and words was wholly devoted to Sri
Chaitanya? Their feelings were truly fused into one, and so the Lord listened to Svarupa Damodara's singing in
self-forgetfulness. Sometimes Sri Chaitanya, overcome by intense prema, would sit on the road and, bending down,
write something on it with His finger. Then Svarupa, fearing that the Lord might hurt Himself, would carefully
examine His hand. He was so sensitive to the slightest change in Sri Chaitanya's feelings that he could at once
foresee their subtlest shade and instantly embody it in song.” (CC, Madhya 13.163–167)

Through Svarupa's senses the Lord Himself relished the singing, and Svarupa's kirtana gave form in the Lord's heart
now to one, now to another feeling of Radha suffering in separation from Krishna. <!-- F29 Q7 F30 -->

## Hera-panchami
On Hera-panchami, having returned from the Gundicha, the Lord asked Svarupa with a smile why Jagannatha, going to
Sundarachala, whose groves resemble Vrindavana, does not take Lakshmi with Him. Svarupa answered that in the
pastimes of Vrindavana there is no place for Lakshmi: there Krishna's companions are the gopis alone, and no one but
they can steal His mind. “Then why is she so angry?” “Such is the nature of a loving woman: she cannot bear even a
shadow of indifference from her beloved, and from this is born mana — the anger of loving pique.” <!-- F31 -->

At that moment Lakshmi, in her anger, came out of the inner chambers to the Lion Gate — on a golden palanquin,
under an umbrella, with chamaras and flags, with betel boxes and pitchers, to music, with dancing devadasis and
hundreds of maidservants. Her maids seized the chief servants of Jagannatha and began to beat them, while the
devotees laughed, covering their mouths. Svarupa, the first among the intimate devotees, said: “Such mana we have
never heard of or seen in the three worlds. A piqued beloved loses her ardor, takes off her ornaments, sits on the
ground with darkened face and draws lines on the earth with her nail. Such is the mana of the gopis of Vraja and
even of Satyabhama, the lady of the city. But Lakshmi does the opposite: she has put her riches on show, gathered an
army and marches to war against her beloved.” <!-- F32 F33 -->

The Lord began to question him about the kinds of mana in Vraja and heard from his lips the secret truths of
Vraja-prema. Listening to Svarupa's explanation of Radha's feelings, He embraced him in rapture, and then asked
again — about the vilasas and her other moods. <!-- F34 -->

Here Srivasa Pandita joined in. He extolled the wealth of his Lakshmi and teased Vrindavana for its poverty and
“your mistress” for churning butter. The Lord remarked that each has his own particular way of bhajana, and Svarupa
answered with the natural wealth of Vrindavana: all the majesty of Maha-Vaikuntha is but a small part of it; there
Krishna sets aside His majesty; there the trees are wish-fulfilling kalpa-vrikshas, the earth is chintamani, the
water nectar, speech song, every step a dance, the flute a dear friend, and the Lakshmis there are countless and full
of sweetness. This dispute is told more fully in the article on Srivasa Pandita. <!-- F35 Q8 -->

Hearing from Svarupa's lips of the highest rasa of service to Radha, the Lord danced for joy. When Svarupa sang songs
of Vraja-rasa, He kept saying, “Go on, go on!” and bent His ear to his lips, and a wave of divine love flooded all
Purushottama. <!-- F36 -->

## Nilachala and the Road to Vrindavana
So in Nilachala the Lord lived with Puri Gosvami (Paramananda Puri), Svarupa, Jagadananda, Damodara Pandita, Govinda
and Kashishvara, and every morning went for darshana of Jagannatha, while Gadadhara He had appointed to serve
Gopinatha in Yameshvara-tota. <!-- F38 -->

Svarupa was bound by friendship to Pundarika Vidyanidhi. Whenever Vidyanidhi came to Nilachala he stayed with
Svarupa, and they talked of Krishna; Krishnadasa Kaviraja calls him Svarupa's close friend (CC, Madhya 16.77). Once,
on Odana-shashthi, Pundarika disapproved that the pujaris dressed Jagannatha in starched cloth and asked Svarupa's
opinion. Svarupa replied, “The Lord is independent and not subject to the rules of smriti.” “Jagannatha is
independent, but the pujaris are not,” Pundarika objected. That night Jagannatha and Balarama came to him in a dream
and slapped his cheeks. Seeing his friend's swollen cheeks, Svarupa rejoiced at his good fortune:

“Listen, brother. I have never seen such an amazing punishment, nor heard of anything like it. The Lord came to you in
a dream and punished you personally.” (CB, Antya 10.175–176) <!-- F39 F117 Q35 -->

On Vijaya-dashami the Lord set out for Vrindavana, and His chief companions were Puri Gosvami and Svarupa Damodara.
That year He did not reach Vrindavana; He returned to Nilachala and began to think of going alone. The next year,
after the Ratha-yatra, He made up His mind; Ramananda and Svarupa pleaded with Him at length and gave Him Balabhadra
Bhattacharya and one servant as companions. In the morning the devotees, not finding the Lord, rushed in anguish to
search for Him, but Svarupa held them back. And when the Lord came back from Vrindavana, Svarupa and others met Him
at Narendra-sarovara, and he sent word of His return to Gauda. <!-- F40 F41 F42 F43 -->

## Rupa's Verse
The secret meaning of the verse “yah kaumara-harah” was known to Svarupa alone, and he sang padas in the same mood,
delighting the Lord. But in Puri Rupa Gosvami too caught this mood. He wrote on a palm leaf a verse revealing the
true meaning of the verse and tucked the leaf under the thatch of his hut. The Lord came while Rupa was bathing in
the sea, read it and was overwhelmed with ecstasy; when Rupa returned and prostrated himself, the Lord slapped him:
“How did you know what is hidden in My heart?” <!-- F44 F45 -->

Then the Lord showed the verse to Svarupa and, as if not knowing the answer, asked how Rupa had entered into His
thoughts. Svarupa replied:

“As I understand it, You are especially favorable to him. No one could grasp this secret meaning. I think You have
fully endowed him with Your causeless mercy.” (CC, Antya 1.86–87)

And he added that the moment he saw the verse he understood it was anointed with the Lord's special mercy, for the
cause is known by its effect. <!-- F46 -->

Another time the Lord came with Svarupa, Ramananda and Sarvabhauma to Rupa, who was staying with Haridasa near the
Siddha-bakula, and told him to recite his verse “priyah so 'yam…” (it is also given in the “Padyavali,” 383). Rupa
was silent out of reverence, so Svarupa recited the verse, astonishing everyone, and then explained that Rupa had
understood the Lord's heart by His special mercy. Rupa became Svarupa's dear friend; later, speaking of worldly
poets, Svarupa would hold up as an example the way Rupa had begun his two plays — and from this it is concluded that
Rupa's plays were largely written while the Lord was still present. <!-- F47 Q9 F122 F139 -->

In Puri Sanatana Gosvami too was introduced to Svarupa, as to all the devotees, and later Raghunatha Bhatta, when he
first came to the Lord. <!-- F125 -->

## Against Mayavada
In Puri, near the Lord, lived Bhagavan Acharya — a learned Vaishnava, simple-hearted, in the mood of friendship, like
a cowherd boy, though his father, Shatananda Khan, was a worldly man. He was Svarupa's friend. Bhagavan Acharya's
younger brother, Gopala Bhattacharya, had studied Vedanta in Kashi, and Bhagavan Acharya suggested that Svarupa
hear Shankara's “Shariraka-bhashya” from him. Svarupa answered with loving anger:

“In Gopala's company you have lost your reason. Forgetting the highest goal of life, you wish to hear the deceptive
philosophy of Mayavada. Any Vaishnava who decides to study Shankaracharya's Mayavada commentary gradually breaks off
his relationship with God, founded on the feeling of servant and Master, and begins to think himself the Supreme.
Hearing Shankara's Mayavada commentary defiles the heart even of the greatest Vaishnava, for whom Krishna is dearer
than life itself, and as a result he will surely change all his convictions.” (CC, Antya 2.94–96) <!-- F48 F49 Q10 -->

Bhagavan Acharya objected that a heart given to Krishna will not be shaken by any commentary. But Svarupa replied
that one would still have to hear that Brahman is only impersonal spirit, the world a false illusion, the soul a
fiction and God subject to ignorance — and such words break a devotee's heart. Bhagavan Acharya, abashed and
frightened, fell silent, and the next day sent Gopala back to his home. So Svarupa, “the guru of all Vaishnavas,”
showed how one who seeks pure service to Krishna should regard the Mayavada commentary of Shankaracharya, the avatara
who came to bewilder the asuras: the poison of Mayavada is so strong that once it enters the ears it inevitably ruins
the soul. <!-- F50 F51 Q10 -->

He who would not yield a hair's breadth in siddhanta was gentle with devotees who had stumbled. When the Lord, to
teach living beings, rejected His devotee Junior Haridasa (Chhota Haridasa), Svarupa and others asked the Lord the
reason. Haridasa fasted for three days in grief, and Svarupa comforted him with the hope of seeing the Lord's feet
again: “The Lord is independent, and now He is holding firm; later He will be merciful. If you persist, His
persistence will only grow. Break your fast.” Haridasa obeyed, but the Lord did not relent, and a year later
Haridasa went to Prayaga and left his body there in the waters of the Triveni. Later Svarupa and other intimates
heard his singing from afar without seeing the singer. Krishnadasa Kaviraja writes of this:

“Then Svarupa Damodara and all the devotees gathered and concluded that, since Junior Haridasa had left his body at
the holy confluence of three rivers, he had certainly attained the shelter of Sri Chaitanya Mahaprabhu's lotus
feet.” (CC, Antya 2.166) <!-- F52 F129 -->

## The Bengali Poet's Play
Once a brahmana poet from East Bengal, an acquaintance of Bhagavan Acharya, wrote a play about the Lord; the
Vaishnavas praised it, and Bhagavan Acharya asked Svarupa to hear it before it was read to the Lord. Svarupa
answered: “You are a cowherd boy, generous and ready to hear anything. In the words of an uninitiated poet there is
rasabhasa. One who cannot tell rasa from rasabhasa and does not know grammar, rhetoric and the science of drama
cannot describe Krishna's pastimes, still less the unfathomable pastimes of Chaitanya. Only one whose life is the
feet of Gaura can describe the pastimes of Krishna and Gaura. A worldly poet's verses are painful to hear, while the
words of a knowing devotee give joy — as with the way Rupa began his two plays.” <!-- F55 Q11 -->

For two or three days they pleaded with him, and at last he agreed. The poet read the opening nandi-shloka:

“Golden Sri Chaitanya has become the soul of the body of Sri Jagannatha, whose eyes are wide open like lotuses. He
appeared in Jagannatha Puri and brought this lifeless nature to life. May this Sri Chaitanya Mahaprabhu grant you the
highest fortune.” (CC, Antya 5.112)

Svarupa told him to explain it, and the poet said: Jagannatha is the body and Sri Chaitanya the soul within that
body; Jagannatha is merely a wooden image and Chaitanya His life: the Lord appeared in Nilachala to bring inert wood
to life[^5]. <!-- F56 Q12 -->

All the listeners were pleased; only Svarupa grew sad and said angrily:

“Fool! You have brought upon yourself a curse that will utterly ruin you. You have faith neither in Lord Jagannatha
nor in Sri Chaitanya Mahaprabhu. Lord Sri Jagannatha is wholly spiritual and full of supreme bliss. Yet in your
poetry you have presented Him as a lifeless, perishable body made of the Lord's external energy (prakriti). And Sri
Chaitanya is the Supreme Himself, endowed with all six divine opulences. He is like the original supreme fire. But
you have described Him as a mere spark — one of the multitude of ordinary souls. By offending Them both you have
condemned yourself to hell! <…> In your verse you make a distinction between the body and the soul of God. And that is
a grave offense! The soul and the body of God are one truth! There is no difference between them. Both His person and
His body are the direct manifestation of the spiritual bliss of the Supreme Lord.” (CC, Antya 5.117–122)

Where is Krishna, the fullness of bliss and of all six opulences — and where the tiny soul, suffering in the service
of maya! <!-- F57 Q13 -->

The assembly was struck dumb. The poet stood ashamed, frightened and astonished, like a heron among swans. Seeing
his distress, Svarupa softened and instructed him:

“Go and hear the message of the ‘Srimad-Bhagavatam’ from a true Vaishnava. You can do this only if you give yourself
wholly to the feet of Sri Chaitanya Mahaprabhu. Try to associate every day with Sri Chaitanya's devotees. Thus you
will be able to feel and understand the waves of the ocean of the highest truths of devotion to God. Only then will
your learning bear the fruits of true wisdom. Then you will soon be able to describe Krishna's divine pastimes without
any inventions or fancies of your own.” (CC, Antya 5.131–133) <!-- F58 Q14 -->

And then he showed how pure Sarasvati serves Gaura-Krishna even through the words of a fool or an enemy: thus she
interprets as praise both Indra's reproaches and the abuse of Jarasandha and Shishupala. And Svarupa gave the poet's
nandi-shloka a glorifying interpretation:

“There is no difference between Krishna and Jagannatha. They are one and the same Personality of Godhead. But here,
in Sri Jagannatha Puri, the Supreme Lord appears before all as Daru-Brahman — God in a wooden and immovable form.
Though Sri Chaitanya and Lord Jagannatha look different, Their identity is wholly one, and this is Krishna Himself,
the one indivisible Absolute.” (CC, Antya 5.148–149)

Daru-brahma is the Lord Himself, appearing in a form of wood, not a piece of wood that needs to be “brought to life”;
and the same Krishna, to save people, came as the moving (jangama) Brahman — Sri Chaitanya — and, traveling through
the lands, saves all who cannot come to Jagannatha. For even one who utters Krishna's name while reviling Him attains
liberation. <!-- F59 Q15 -->

The poet accepted this instruction with bowed head. He prostrated himself before the devotees, surrendered to them,
by their mercy received the Lord's darshana and mercy, gave up everything and stayed on in Nilachala among the
devotees. Such is Svarupa as an acharya: without mercy he rejects the Mayavadis and rasabhasa, yet mercifully
corrects one who has gone astray, and whoever accepts his merciful chastisement (kripa-shasana) surely attains the
good. <!-- F60 I2 -->

## “Svarupa's Raghu”
When Raghunatha Dasa, having finally left his worldly father and uncle, came to Nilachala, the Lord, touched by his
zeal, entrusted him to Svarupa:

“I give Raghunatha Dasa wholly into your care. Please accept him as your son or servant. From now on there are three
Raghunathas in My circle. So let this Raghunatha be known as Svarupa Damodara's Raghu.” (CC, Antya 6.202–203)

Saying this, the Lord took Raghunatha by the hand and placed his hand in Svarupa's. From then on he was called just
that — “Svarupa's Raghu.” <!-- F61 Q16 F126 -->

Raghunatha often put his questions to the Lord through Svarupa and Govinda. When Svarupa asked the Lord what
Raghunatha's duty was, the Lord told Raghunatha to accept Svarupa himself as his shiksha-guru: “He will tell you in
full detail about the goal and the method of spiritual practice. I Myself do not know as much as he knows about all
this” (CC, Antya 6.234). Having given Raghunatha His instructions — not to speak or hear of worldly things, not to eat
tasty food or dress finely, to chant the Name without seeking honor, and to serve Radha-Krishna in the mind in
Vrindavana — the Lord concluded, “You will learn the details from Svarupa.” By this the Lord declared Svarupa the
eternal master-guru of the Madhva-Gaudiyas and the acharya of the whole truth of the goal and the path (sadhya and
sadhana). Following him, Raghunatha attained the intimate service of Gaura-Krishna. <!-- F62 Q17 F126 F63 -->

Srila Bhaktivinoda Thakura explains that this “intimate service” is service in Vraja performed in the mind, in one's
own spiritual form; and Dasa Gosvami performed it by entering the group of that sakhi whom Svarupa is in the Vraja
pastimes[^6]. <!-- F64 Q31 -->

Svarupa watched over the whole life of his charge. When Raghunatha stopped inviting the Lord, Svarupa explained to
the Lord that he had understood that food bought with his worldly father's money did not please Him. And when
Raghunatha stopped standing at the Lion Gate waiting for alms and began going to the place where alms were
distributed, Svarupa explained that he had come to regard standing at the gate as a waste of time. The stone from
Govardhana and the gunja garland given him by the Lord Raghunatha worshiped as Gandharvika and Giridhari; at
Svarupa's suggestion he offered Them sandesh that Govinda gave him. <!-- F127 F65 -->

At night Raghunatha would gather the rotten rice mahaprasada that the merchants at the Lion Gate threw out for the
cows — not even the Tailanga cows would eat it — wash it and eat it as Krishna's spiritual remnants. Once Svarupa
took a little and ate:

“Every day you relish this nectar, yet you never even offer it to us! How can you behave like this?” (CC, Antya 6.320)

Hearing of it from Govinda, the Lord came Himself and ate a handful: “What delicious treats you eat! But why don't
you share them with Me?” When He took a second handful, Svarupa caught His hand and snatched the rice away: “It is
not fitting for You to eat this.” <!-- F66 Q18 F67 -->

From this comes Svarupa's lesson: a sadhaka may, for the sake of his own love for Krishna, live in severe
renunciation, but Hari, guru and the Vaishnavas are to be served with the best spiritual offerings, as masters. One
must not “train in renunciation” Hari, guru and the Vaishnavas — keeping the Deities of Guru and Gauranga without
food on Ekadashi, or worshiping them in the way they displayed austerity in their manifest pastimes to teach
people — that is a great offense. The sadhaka's renunciation concerns himself alone. <!-- F68 I3 -->

In the Lord's final pastimes Svarupa was constantly at His side, and his “right hand” was Raghunatha Dasa. For
sixteen years Raghunatha lived in Puri under Svarupa's care, and after the departure of the Lord and of Svarupa he
went in grief to Vrindavana. <!-- F111 F128 -->

## Vallabha Bhatta and Gadadhara
When Vallabha Bhatta came to Nilachala and began to glorify the Lord, the Lord, concealing Himself, began to praise
His devotees, and of Svarupa He said:

“In Svarupa Damodara all the rasas of divine love for Krishna are embodied. Through association with him I was able to
understand madhura-rasa — the sweetness of the transcendental relationships of the lovers in Vrindavana. Unmixed and
pure is the love of the cowherd girls of Vrindavana, the gopis. In it there is not a trace of worldly lust. The only
aim of such love is to give happiness to Krishna. <…> The love of the cowherd girls of Vrindavana surpasses all kinds
of devotion to God. In it the highest peak of service to Krishna is revealed. And Krishna Himself admits this when He
calls Himself their eternal debtor. <…> Pure, unmixed love for Krishna is far higher than the love that arises from
understanding His majesty and power. In the whole world there is no devotee better than Uddhava. Yet Uddhava himself
longs to sprinkle his head with the dust of the feet of these cowherd girls of Vraja. All these truths I learned from
Svarupa Damodara.” (CC, Antya 7.38–39, 42, 44–45) <!-- F69 Q19 -->

Elsewhere Krishnadasa Kaviraja says that the Lord relished parental love with Paramananda Puri, friendship with
Ramananda, service with Govinda and others, and the chief, conjugal rasa with Gadadhara, Jagadananda and Svarupa, and
for that love felt Himself in their debt (CC, Madhya 2.78). Srila Bhaktisiddhanta in the “Anubhashya” confirms that
they served the Lord in madhura-rasa. And Svarupa himself, according to Kaviraja, indicated that Gadadhara is the
Lakshmi of Vraja. <!-- F113 Q33 F140 -->

When the Lord, testing the love of Gadadhara, who was well disposed toward Vallabha Bhatta, made a show of anger
toward him and Gadadhara grew afraid, the Lord sent Svarupa and others for him. Svarupa said to Gadadhara: “The Lord
was only testing you. Why didn't you come and tell Him everything, instead of suffering in fear?” Gadadhara replied
that his duty was to bear any severity from his beloved Lord. <!-- F70 -->

## Advocate and Servant
Intolerant of deviations from siddhanta and of offenses, Svarupa always tried to draw the Lord's mercy to those who
had stumbled for a time and to fulfil the devotees' requests. He interceded for Junior Haridasa; when the family of
Bhavananda Raya was taken into custody because of his son Gopinatha Pattanayaka, Ramananda's kinsman, he and others
begged the Lord to give up His indifference and intervene (CC, Antya 9.36); he urged the Lord to lie on the bedding
Jagadananda had prepared. For all his strictness he was great in kindness. <!-- F71 F130 I4 -->

Once in the Jagannatha temple the Lord held a kirtana in seven groups, revealing great majesty. Wishing to dance
Himself, He told Svarupa to sing a line of an Oriya song, “jagamohana pari munda yau” — “let my head fall in the
Jagamohana” — and, listening to it, displayed wondrous spiritual transformations. <!-- F72 Q20 -->

When Namacharya Haridasa Thakura left his body before the Lord, Svarupa, the first of the intimate servants,
surrounded him with nama-sankirtana and together with the Lord held a festival in honor of his departure. The Lord,
spreading the end of His cloth, Himself begged mahaprasada from the merchants, and they were ready to give whole
baskets; but Svarupa, knowing it would be hard for the Lord to carry, stopped them, sent the Lord home and took the
festival upon himself. And when the Lord began to serve the prasada Himself, Svarupa held Him back and served it
together with Jagadananda, Kashishvara and Shankara[^7]. <!-- F73 F74 F131 -->

Jagadananda sewed a mattress and pillow for the Lord in saffron covers, gave them to Govinda and asked Svarupa too
to see to it. Svarupa and Govinda made up the bed, but the Lord angrily asked who had done it, threw the bedding aside
and lay down on banana leaves. Svarupa said that Jagadananda would be hurt, and the Lord in His “anger” pleaded that
He was a renounced sannyasi. Then Svarupa, expert in service, gathered dry banana leaves, split them with his nails
until they were soft and stuffed the Lord's outer cloths with them — and so there was a mattress, and after long
persuasion the Lord agreed to lie on it. This story shows more vividly than any other how well he knew how to serve
the Lord. Jagadananda, hurt, wished to go to Mathura and asked Svarupa to obtain the Lord's permission; at Svarupa's
request the Lord called Jagadananda and gave him instructions for the journey. <!-- F75 I7 F76 -->
