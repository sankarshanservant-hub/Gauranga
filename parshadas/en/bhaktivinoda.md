# Srila Sacchidananda Bhaktivinoda Thakura
*Шрила Саччидананда Бхактивинода Тхакур · @bhaktivinoda*

## Mahaprabhu's envoy
Srila Sacchidananda Bhaktivinoda Thakura (1838–1914) is the acharya with whom, in modern times, the preaching of Sri
Chaitanya's pure bhakti began to flow once more. He revealed to the world the place of Mahaprabhu's appearance in
Mayapur, left more than a hundred books, and became the father and shiksha-guru of Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati. His
parents named him Kedarnatha; in government service he was known as Kedarnatha Datta, and one of his early works, the
"Sri Chaitanya-gita," he signed with the pen-name Sacchidananda Premalankara. The title "Bhaktivinoda" — "he whose joy
is bhakti" — was conferred on him by Vaishnavas, and ever since the Vaishnava world has called him Sri Sacchidananda
Bhaktivinoda Thakura. Srila Bhaktisiddhanta's followers honour him as Om Vishnupada Srila Thakura Bhaktivinoda Gosvami
Prabhu, as Bhaktivinoda Prabhu and as Bhaktivinoda Vibhu. <!-- F1 -->

The pranama-mantra with which he is saluted says: "I bow to Bhaktivinoda, whose name is Sacchidananda, the embodiment
of Gaura's potency, the best of the followers of Sri Rupa." And the daily prayer of the
Brahma-Madhva-Sarasvata-Gaudiya line says of him: "Sri Bhaktivinoda-deva, the root of the preaching of pure bhakti,
the best here, is renowned as the Lord's beloved." <!-- F2 Q1 F3 Q2 -->

Sri Chaitanya Math, the Gaudiya Maths, Sri Chaitanya Gaudiya Math and their kindred missions owe him everything: he is
the root of their daily life. "All that we do is for Sri Krishna-Chaitanya, Sri Rupa, Srila Bhaktivinoda Thakura and
our gurudeva," Srila Bhaktisiddhanta wrote in one of his letters. On his father's books and teaching he built his
mission and began to fulfil Mahaprabhu's prophecy: "In every town and village throughout the world My holy name will be
sung" (CB, Antya 4.126). And the Lord's command — "Whomever you meet, instruct him in the science of Krishna. I order
you: become guru and deliver this land" (CC, Madhya 7.128) — Srila Bhaktisiddhanta repeated to everyone. <!-- F13 F14 F10 Q6 Q9 -->

## Darkness over Gauda
When Mahaprabhu concealed His pastimes, and after Him Svarupa Damodara, Ramananda Raya and the six Gosvamis departed,
and then Srinivasa, Narottama and Shyamananda, darkness gathered over the Lord's teaching. Communities arose that had
strayed from His path (apa-sampradayas); Totaram Dasa Babaji counted thirteen of them: the auls, bauls, kartabhajas,
neras, darveshes, sais, sahajiyas, sakhibhekis, smartas, jata-gosanis, atibaris, chudadharis and gauranga-nagaris.
Seeing them, educated Bengalis came to regard Mahaprabhu's faith as a religion of low and immoral people. Then the Lord
in His mercy sent into the world His eternal associate, Bhaktivinoda, to bring such people back to the truth.
<!-- F7 Q5 F8 -->

Srila Bhaktisiddhanta writes in his preface to the "Jaiva-dharma": Bhaktivinoda is a very intimate associate of Sri
Chaitanya-chandra. When the preachers of Chaitanya's cherished desires had left this world, the sky of Bengal was
overcast with clouds of sensuality and false renunciation; and almost three hundred and fifty years after Mahaprabhu's
appearance Bhaktivinoda came to light up the sky of Gauda once more. All the qualities of a Vaishnava of which the
"Chaitanya-charitamrita" speaks were perfectly manifest in his life: "Vaishnavas are merciful, humble, always truthful,
impartial, faultless, magnanimous and soft-hearted" (CC, Madhya 22.75). And the mercy that Gaura-Hari distributed in
nine ways can be seen in his life and in his works. <!-- F11 F12 Q7 -->

Outwardly he was a householder and a government official — and yet he wrote more than a hundred books in several
languages. In them he refuted views opposed to Mahaprabhu's teaching, and through them many throughout the world came
to recognise the worth of that teaching; Vaishnavas accept his every word as shastra. Vyasa found peace only when he
had described Krishna's pastimes in the "Bhagavata," and Mahaprabhu preached the religion of the "Bhagavata"; but after
the departure of His associates the path of bhakti became overgrown with thorns — until Bhaktivinoda, by his books,
laid bare the emptiness of the false doctrines. Such preaching is impossible without power given by Krishna: he is a
direct associate of Gauranga and of Krishna. <!-- F9 F71 F70 I1 -->

## Lineage
The Lord's eternal associates, descending for the sake of the fallen, behave like human beings: they may even live as
householders, yet maya does not touch or bind them, for their hearts are attached to the Lord. Only those surrendered
to Vishnu and the Vaishnavas can discern this. So too Bhaktivinoda's life in family and in service was not worldly.
<!-- F19 I2 -->

His family traces its descent to the kayastha Purushottama — one of the brahmanas and noble Hindus whom King Adishura
invited to Bengal. Purushottama's descendants in the seventh and eighth generations, Vinayaka and Narayana, were
ministers, and in the fifteenth generation lived a contemporary of Mahaprabhu, Raja Krishnananda Datta, a devotee of
Krishna. Sri Nityananda Prabhu stayed in his house with His party and blessed him generously[^1]. <!-- F20 -->

A descendant of Krishnananda, Govinda Sharan Datta, founded the village of Govindapur; Kalighat, Sutanuti and
Govindapur became the core of Calcutta. Govinda Sharan's grandson was Ramachandra; Ramachandra's grandson was Madan
Mohan Datta, who gave Calcutta the Hedua-pukur tank and in 1774 built at his own cost the steps at Pretashira in Gaya and
on Chandranath hill. His grandson was Rajavallabha Datta, and Rajavallabha's son was Ananda Chandra Datta,
Bhaktivinoda's father, a deeply pious and detached man. His mother, Jaganmohini Devi, was the daughter of Ishvara
Chandra Mustaufi, a well-known zamindar of the village of Ula in the Nadia district. <!-- F21 F22 -->

## Appearance and childhood
Srila Bhaktivinoda appeared in 1838, on the Gaura-trayodashi of the month of Hrishikesha, in Ula-Birnagar (Nadia
district), in the house of his maternal grandfather[^2]. <!-- F23 F122 -->

He composed his first poem at the age of two. His songs were not the fruit of worldly talent: in an eternally perfect
associate of the Lord they manifested of themselves. At six he knew the "Mahabharata" and the "Ramayana" in every
detail. <!-- F24 F25 -->

In his "Autobiography" he recalls that at nine he took up astrology, but at ten gave it up to seek spiritual truth and
began to ask people about their convictions — without ever offending anyone. For from birth he was established in the
truth and only enacted these pastimes to show what is special about human life. <!-- F26 -->

As a boy he went to festivals; he also visited a tantric brahmachari who kept a human skull — and gave the skull water
to see whether it would "smile." The skull did not smile. He asked the old craftsman who painted the backdrop for an
image of Durga when the goddess enters the image; neither at the "painting of the eyes" nor at the rite of
consecration did the boy notice it, and the craftsman admitted that he himself believed only in the one Supreme,
Parameshvara[^3]. The treasury guard, a Muslim named Golam Khan, told him the Muslim account of creation — "Rama and
Rahim are one, it is Khoda" — and taught him a charm against ghosts. His maternal uncle Parashuram Mustaufi, a student
of law, lost his faith in God and called Rammohan Roy his guru; other uncles, Raghu and Nashu, followed him. This
troubled the boy, and the answer of a certain bhattacharya confused him still more; but he did not give up the name of
Rama. <!-- F27 F28 F29 F30 -->

The lesson of these childhood searchings is simple: instead of speculating about God and the world, one should chant
the Holy Name with faith. So too Mahaprabhu, tying up His books, told His students only to sing the Name. <!-- F31 I4 -->

## Studies and marriage
At six he was sent to learn Sanskrit at the tol of Vidyavachaspati, and at seven his grandfather sent him to the
Krishnanagar College: its principal was Captain D. L. Richardson, its chief Indian professor Ramtanu Lahiri, and one of
his fellow students the young Raja of Cooch Behar. A year later an English school opened in Ula, and the boy moved
there. <!-- F33 -->

When he was eleven his father died. As was the custom of the time, his mother married her twelve-year-old son to a
five-year-old girl from neighbouring Ranaghat. He himself later called it a "doll's wedding": when he visited his
mother-in-law, a nurse was sent with him. <!-- F32 -->

After his maternal grandfather's death he and his mother moved to Calcutta, to the family house at the corner of Beadon
Street and Hedua[^4]. There he studied at the Hindu Charitable Trust school, and four years later, in 1856, entered the
Hindu School. He was one of the first pupils of Ishvara Chandra Vidyasagara, who loved him. Once the teacher said that
it was better not to speak of God, Whom no one has seen. Kedarnatha objected: why then is it written in the "Bodhodaya"
that God is formless? The Almighty can also have form; the Lord is the eternal master and I am His eternal servant, and
natural love for Him is bhakti, the highest knowledge, brahma-vidya and para-vidya. <!-- F34 F35 -->

In 1856 the University of Calcutta was founded and the first entrance examinations were held. Among his fellow students
were Satyendranath and Ganendranath Tagore, Taraknath Palit and Navagopal Mitra; his English was admired by the
principal, Clint, by the Reverend Duff, George Thompson and Keshab Chandra Sen. At the end of that year he published an
English poem, "The Poriade"; his English verses appeared in the "Literary Gazette," and at the British Indian Society
he lectured on the evolution of matter. He studied Brahmo-dharma, Christianity, the Bible, the Koran and other
teachings; he placed Christianity above Brahmoism, because it acknowledges the eternal personality of God. In the year
of the Sepoy Rising, 1857, he travelled and lectured. <!-- F36 F37 F38 F39 -->

## A grandfather's prophecy and first employment
In 1858 he set out from Bengal for Nilachala. At Chhutigram he visited his grandfather, Krishnavallabha Datta; the old
man foretold that his grandson would become a great Vaishnava — and at once left his body, the life-air departing
through the crown of his head. In Puri Kedarnatha attended the Chandana-yatra of Jagannatha, stayed on for a few more
days, and returned to Calcutta by way of Cuttack, Bhadrak and Midnapore[^5]. <!-- F40 F41 -->

On Vidyasagara's recommendation he became a teacher at the government secondary school in Cuttack, and soon, in 1860,
headmaster of the school at Bhadrak. In those years he wrote a book on the monasteries of Orissa, "Maths of Orissa,"
which Sir William Hunter drew on extensively in his work "Orissa," and the "Sri Chaitanya-gita," signed with the name
Sacchidananda Premalankara. <!-- F42 F43 -->

He also taught in Midnapore. There a speech of his on true religion, given at a literary society, deeply moved a member
of the Brahmo Samaj, Rama Narayana Basu. In Midnapore his first wife died, and in a second marriage he wed Bhagavati
Devi. <!-- F45 F46 -->

From Midnapore he moved to Burdwan and preached there too. In 1863 his English book "Our Wants" came out. In Burdwan the
Brahmos and the Christians were in dispute, and he tried to reconcile them: in two lectures he showed the weakness of
both sides' arguments. He founded a circle, the "Bhratri-samaja," where his talk on the soul attracted a certain
Mr. Haley. Then service took him to Chuadanga and Ranaghat, and for a time to Chhapra in Bihar; from there he made his
first journey to Vrindavana, by train, through Kashi, Mirzapur, Prayag and Agra. In Chhapra he learned Urdu and Persian
and gave a lecture on Gautama. <!-- F47 F48 -->

From Chhapra he was transferred to Purnea, and in 1868 to Dinajpur as Deputy Magistrate. There the Hindus were in
dispute with the Brahmos, and he answered both sides with a speech, "The Bhagavata: Its Philosophy, Its Ethics and Its
Theology." In June of that year he visited the house of Sri Rupa and Sri Sanatana at Ramakeli, and Rajmahal. Returning
to Calcutta, he searched long for copies of the "Chaitanya-charitamrita" and the "Srimad-Bhagavatam" and at last found
them with the publishers of Bat-tala. <!-- F49 F50 -->

## Five years in Puri
With these books he went to Purushottama-dhama: the government entrusted him with supervising the management of Lord
Jagannatha's temple. From 1869 to 1874 he lived in Puri without a break. It is said of a Vaishnava that he is "softer
than a flower and harder than a thunderbolt" — and such was Bhaktivinoda: kind to all, he would not tolerate deceit
cloaked in the name of religion. <!-- F51 F52 -->

In 1871, on the edge of the forest near Bhubaneswar, there lived with his disciples a yogi named Bishikishan, of the
Khandait caste and the Atibari sect. Having gained mystic powers, he proclaimed himself an incarnation of Mahavishnu and
announced in Oriya verses that on the 14th of the month of Chaitra (in the verses — the thirteenth month, Mina) he would
assume a four-armed form and destroy the foreign mlecchas. He cured incurable diseases and gathered a great many
followers. At last he announced that at the full moon he would perform a "rasa-lila," and summoned the girls of a
neighbouring village. The Chaudhuris of the Bhringar clan, whose women he had defiled, complained to the district
commissioner, Mr. Ravenshaw, and he entrusted the investigation to Bhaktivinoda. <!-- F53 F54 -->

Bhaktivinoda went into the forest himself. Bishikishan called himself the living Mahavishnu and blasphemed Lord
Jagannatha, while trying to flatter Bhaktivinoda. Seeing that the impostor would not give up his deceit, Bhaktivinoda
arrested him and brought him to Puri. He gathered evidence in the surrounding villages and in the Buddhist viharas near
Khandagiri. During the trial the yogi sent illnesses upon him and his family, but could not intimidate him: for
conspiracy to rebellion Bhaktivinoda sentenced him to eighteen months' imprisonment. For twenty-one days Bishikishan
neither ate nor drank — and left his body. In the months that followed Bhaktivinoda put an end to the deceit of two more
impostors: an "incarnation of Brahma" in Jajpur and a "manifestation of Balarama" in Khurda. <!-- F55 F56 F57 -->

## The "Bhagavata-samsad"
In Puri he immersed himself in the shastras: he studied the "Bhagavata," the six "Sandarbhas" of Srila Jiva Gosvami,
the "Govinda-bhashya," "Siddhanta-ratna" and "Prameya-ratnavali" of Srila Baladeva Vidyabhushana, and — in particular
detail — Srila Rupa's "Bhakti-rasamrita-sindhu"; thus he showed how necessary the study of scripture is. He also
studied the "Chaitanya-charitamrita" and Narahari Chakravarti's "Bhakti-ratnakara," while he did not accept Jayananda's
"Chaitanya-mangala" as authoritative. Here he wrote the Sanskrit "Datta-kaustubha" and began the Sanskrit verses of the
"Sri Krishna-samhita." <!-- F58 F63 F65 -->

For the regular study of the "Bhagavata" he founded an assembly, the "Bhagavata-samsad," in the Jagannatha-vallabha
garden; prominent Vaishnavas of Puri came to hear him — Nityananda Dasa, Paramananda Dasa, Narayana Dasa Mahanta, and
Harihara Dasa Mahanta of Uttara-parshva. For with Mahaprabhu hearing the "Bhagavata" is one of the five principal limbs
of bhakti, and Srila Jiva calls it the best of them. As Mahaprabhu heard the "Bhagavata" from Gadadhara Pandita, so
Bhaktivinoda heard it from Sri Gopinatha Pandita. <!-- F59 F60 -->

Not everyone was pleased. A babaji, Raghunatha Dasa of the Hati-akhra, objected to his readings — and fell ill; but
Lord Jagannatha told him in a dream to go to Bhaktivinoda and beg forgiveness, and when he had done so he recovered. In
Jagannatha's temple Bhaktivinoda would not sit in the Mukti-mandapa, where the mayavadis debated, but spoke near the
temple of Lakshmi, where the footprints of Mahaprabhu are kept. His talks drew many mayavadis too, and the place came to
be called the Bhakti-mandapa — the Bhakti-prangana. He often visited the siddha Vaishnava Svarupa Dasa Babaji,
discussing the shastras with him. <!-- F61 F62 F64 -->

The family lived near Grand Road, beside Narayana-chhata, not far from the temple, in a house on land that a wealthy
family leased from the Dakshina-parshva Math. In 1974 Srila Bhakti Dayita Madhava Gosvami Maharaja recovered this house,
and today Sri Chaitanya Gaudiya Math stands there with a beautiful temple. <!-- F66 -->

## The birth of Bimala Prasada
In this house, on Friday 6 February 1874, on the krishna-panchami of the month of Magha, at half past three in the
afternoon, a son was born to Bhagavati Devi — the future Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati, the fourth child of the
family. The Thakura and the household were singing the holy names at that time. The umbilical cord was wound about the
infant's body like a sacred thread. His father named him Bimala Prasada, after Vimala Devi, the potency of Lord
Jagannatha; the child's first solid food was maha-prasada[^6]. <!-- F67 F144 F180 -->

Six months later, at the Ratha-yatra, Lord Jagannatha's chariot by His will stopped before Bhaktivinoda's house at
Narayana-chhata and stood there three days; for all three days, at the Thakura's direction, kirtana never ceased.
Bhagavati Devi held the baby up to the chariot, and, stretching out his little hand, he touched Jagannatha's feet and
received His garland; the pandas foretold a great future for the boy. As he grew, he listened attentively to the
"Bhagavata" from his father's lips[^7]. <!-- F145 F181 -->

Bhaktivinoda was "Jagannatha's own man": by His arrangement he was brought to Puri to set His service in order. And
after Srila Bhaktisiddhanta's birth Mahaprabhu's message spread throughout the world; thus are interpreted the words of
the "Padma Purana," "from Purushottama in Utkala." Ten months after his son's birth Bhaktivinoda was transferred to
Ranaghat, and the family returned to Bengal overland, in palanquins. <!-- F68 F69 -->

## Travels and preaching
From 1877 to 1910 Bhaktivinoda travelled all over India as a pilgrim and preacher. First he visited Amta (Uluberia
subdivision), the shripata of Abhirama Thakura at Khanakul-Krishnanagar, Shyampur, Bhadrak, Naral (Jessore district),
Calcutta and Prayag, and then Vrindavana, Radha-kunda and Govardhana. Near Govardhana he scattered a gang of Kanjhar
robbers who were plundering pilgrims. The way back led through Mathura, Lucknow, Faizabad, Guptar-ghat, Ayodhya and
Benares. <!-- F73 F74 -->

It was then, in Vrindavana, in 1880, that he first bowed at the feet of Srila Jagannatha Dasa Babaji and received from
him many instructions on hari-bhakti[^8]. From 1885 he often received spiritual guidance from the Babaji; their mutual
attraction, Vaishnavas say, was "from time immemorial." <!-- F133 F167 F175 -->

In Calcutta he began building his house, the "Bhakti Bhavana." When the foundations were being dug, a Deity of Sri
Kurmadeva emerged from the earth; Bhaktivinoda gave Him to his seven-year-old son and taught him His worship — the
mantra of Kurma — and from then on the boy wore tilaka[^9]. At this time too Bhaktivinoda first visited Mayapur.
<!-- F75 F147 F76 -->

In his service he was Deputy Collector in the Barasat subdivision, and then he was transferred from place to place:
Srirampur (1884), Vaidyanath, Bankipur, Gaya — where he saw the steps at Pretashira built by his ancestor Madan Mohan
Datta[^10] — again Naral and Barasat, Memari, Kulinagrama, Bandel and Saptagram. In Kulinagrama he lectured on the Holy
Name, distinguishing the pure name, namabhasa — the shadow of the name — and namaparadha, offensive chanting. There too
he gave his son initiation into harinama and the Nrisimha-mantra[^11]. Once, returning from Puri, he gave his son, then
a pupil in the seventh class, a tulasi-mala of Lord Jagannatha, and read to him the "Sri Chaitanya-shikshamrita," which
he was writing at the time. <!-- F77 F78 F146 -->

In 1885 he founded in Calcutta the "Chaitanya-yantra" press, and in the "Bhakti Bhavana" a publishing house, the
"Vaishnava Depository." His son helped him with the printing and proof-reading and travelled with him to the shripatas
— to Kulinagrama and Saptagram — hearing his lectures on the Holy Name. In those same years the Sri Gaudiya
Gosvami-sangha, for the four-hundredth anniversary of Mahaprabhu's appearance, conferred on him the title
"Bhaktivinoda"[^12]. <!-- F79 F148 F44 -->

In 1886 he helped found the "Vishva-vaishnava-raja-sabha" — the "royal assembly of the Vaishnavas of the whole world"
— in the Durga-mandapa of Rama Gopala Basu in Krishna-Singher Gali off Bethune Road, and lectured there on the
"Chaitanya-charitamrita" and the "Bhakti-rasamrita-sindhu." This sabha, tradition says, was first founded by Srila Jiva
Gosvami eleven years after Mahaprabhu's departure; Bhaktivinoda gave it new life, and Srila Bhaktisiddhanta revived it
in 1919 (of which more below). In those years Bhaktivinoda also met the famous Ramakrishna Paramahamsa, refuted his
impersonalism and explained to him the truth of pure bhakti. <!-- F80 F81 -->

## The mentor
Srila Jagannatha Dasa Babaji, the "general of the Vaishnavas," Srila Bhaktivinoda honoured as his mentor. In his song on
the chain of teachers Srila Bhaktisiddhanta remembers "Jagannatha, whose dear disciple is Bhaktivinoda." The Babaji,
like him, was a goshthyanandi — one who rejoices in the company of devotees and makes the community grow; and his wish
to see the sampradaya and its preaching flourish blossomed and bore fruit in Bhaktivinoda. The Babaji inspired him to
preach the name and the dhama of Gaura, and verses in the Babaji's praise say that he invested all his power in his
devotee Bhaktivinoda and through him sent the stream of harinama over the world; the blessed line of Bhaktivinoda grew
from his body and his labours. <!-- F132 F189 F141 -->

## Where the Lord appeared
In 1887 Bhaktivinoda visited Tarakeshwar, the chief Shiva-linga of Bengal. There Lord Shiva said to him in a dream:
"You long for Vrindavana, but close to you is Navadvipa-dhama, where so much remains to be done. What have you done
there?" In the same year he was transferred to Krishnanagar, a few miles from Navadvipa. He began to visit Kulia — the
present town of Navadvip — and stayed at the Ranir Dharmashala above the Ganga. One evening at about ten o'clock,
standing on the roof, he saw across the river a shining building; the light was seen also by his son Kamala Prasada,
who was with him. <!-- F82 F83 -->

Bhaktivinoda learned that the light came from a place by the Ballal-dighi, and the old men of that village confirmed:
here Mahaprabhu was born. Having studied old maps and documents, he established firmly that the place of the Lord's
appearance lies by the Ballal-dighi. He examined the "Bhakti-ratnakara," the government's old statistical and
topographical maps and the records of the district court, and laid the evidence before an assembly of the district's
leading men and the famous pandits of Navadvipa — and they approved it. <!-- F84 F177 -->

In 1888 he bought a plot in Godruma-dvipa and named it Surabhi-kunja; from Krishnanagar he also visited his native Ula.
In 1889 he was transferred to the Netrokona subdivision of Mymensingh district, in East Bengal; he visited
Narayanganj, the town of Mymensingh, the Garo hills, where he blessed the Hajong people, and Goalundo. Then came
transfers to Tangail and Burdwan, and journeys to Shantipur, Kalna, Bagnapara, Koigram and Denur, the shripata of Srila
Vrindavana Dasa Thakura. <!-- F86 F87 -->

In May 1890 he was again in Kulia and visited Jagannatha Dasa Babaji in his bhajana-kutira in Koladvipa, where the
Babaji spent his last years. In service to him Bhaktivinoda added a concrete veranda to the kutira[^13]. From Burdwan he
went to sing kirtana with the devotees of the village of Amlajora, and visited Gopalpur, Raniganj and Barakar. In 1890
he served at Raniganj, in 1891 at Dinajpur. <!-- F88 F136 F168 F89 -->

In Calcutta he met Shishir Kumar Ghosh, a newspaper publisher. Ghosh regarded him as his guru and called him "the
seventh Gosvami," was a constant visitor to the "Bhakti Bhavana," and at his word began to chant japa and wear a
tulasi-mala, but was never able to adopt the Vaishnava way of life completely. Bhaktivinoda visited Midnapore, where he
spoke with Sitanatha Mohapatra and others, Ghatal and Kayapat-Badna-ganj in Hooghly, and returned to Krishnanagar, to
Surabhi-kunja. In Krishnanagar he convened large meetings, regularly attended by Englishmen too — Malrow, Wallace,
Butler. <!-- F90 F91 -->

On 9 March 1892, at Amlajora, in the presence of Jagannatha Dasa Babaji, he observed Hari-vasara — ekadashi; all night
long there was kirtana and talk of Krishna. A "Prapanna-ashrama" was being established there — a place for hearing
hari-katha and singing kirtana. The Babaji, then over a hundred and fifty years old, led a great nagara-sankirtana,
danced, and for a long time lost external consciousness, while the Thakura in his hari-katha spoke of how necessary such
abodes are and declared the ashrama open; the Babaji rejoiced at his zeal in preaching the name[^14].
<!-- F92 F175 F167 -->

From Amlajora Bhaktivinoda went via Buxar and Prayag to Vrindavana, and from 21 to 29 March went round Vraja:
Bilvavana, Bhandiravana, Mathavana, Man-sarovara, Mathura, Gokula, Madhuvana, Talavana, Kumudavana, Bahulavana,
Radha-kunda, Govardhana and other places; he returned through Cawnpore and Allahabad. In Calcutta he preached in the
"Bhakti Bhavana" and continued the meetings in Krishnanagar. In the same year, 1892, the Babaji with the Vaishnavas
sang sankirtana in Godruma, and then in Mayapur. <!-- F92 F93 F134 -->

In the month of Magha 1299 of the Bengali era (February 1893) the Babaji came from Kulia to Godruma, to Surabhi-kunja:
with his companions he crossed the Ganga, and the whole neighbourhood resounded with kirtana. On Wednesday, 27 Magha,
Bhaktivinoda held a kirtana festival, and the Babaji was its guest of honour. The Babaji visited the Thakura both in
Godruma and in Calcutta. <!-- F93 F134 F176 -->

And on Thursday, 20 Phalguna of the same year (March 1893)[^15], the Thakura came to the Babaji in the morning with
kirtana, and at eleven o'clock the devotees crossed with him to Mayapur in three boats. The Babaji was carried in a
palanquin; usually the old man was carried in a basket by his servant, the Vrajavasi Bihari Dasa. At the place of
appearance Dvarika Babu awaited them with kirtana and flags. The Babaji led everyone to the birthplace of the Lord — the
Yogapitha — began to dance and pointed it out exactly, and then struck that very spot several times hard with his
staff, to dispel all doubt. He also pointed out Khola-bhangar-danga — Srivasa-angana. They danced there as no one had
danced in Navadvipa for almost four hundred years. It was decided to begin the service of Deities at the place of
appearance and at Srivasa-angana: the Babaji proposed to install Jagannatha Mishra and Shachi in one building, in
another Mahaprabhu with Lakshmi and Vishnupriya at His sides, and at Srivasa-angana the Pancha-tattva.

At about that time Bhaktivinoda argued with a descendant of a gosvami family who regarded one of Mahaprabhu's closest
associates as a shudra. Grieved, the Thakura answered him with a verse, "vaishnava-charitra sarvada pavitra…": the
character of a Vaishnava is always pure, and with one who out of envy reviles a Vaishnava Bhaktivinoda does not speak
— he keeps silent. He wrote out his chain of teachers (guru-parampara) and hung it outside the "Bhakti Bhavana." He
also preached in Bihar — at Sasaram, Nasirganj and Dehri. <!-- F95 Q10 F96 F97 -->

In January 1894, at a large meeting in the A.B. School in Krishnanagar, it was resolved to install Deities at the place
of appearance, and the "Navadvipa-pracharini-sabha" was founded to spread word of this place far and wide; its
secretary was Naphar Chandra Pal Bhaktibhushana, zamindar of Natudaha (Nadia). Dvarika Babu, Naphar Babu and others
decided to put up a thatched hut there for the Deities of Gaura and Vishnupriya. <!-- F98 -->

The Deities were installed on 21 March 1894 (9 Chaitra 1300 of the Bengali era), on Phalguni-purnima — Gaura-purnima —
during a lunar eclipse, amid loud kirtana[^16]. Thus Bhaktivinoda, following Srila Narottama, who installed Gaura and
Vishnupriya at Kheturi, with the permission of Jagannatha Dasa Babaji manifested the service of Gaurasundara with
Vishnupriya in Gaura's house in Mayapur. By this he answered the teachings which, calling themselves worship of
Vishnupriya-Gauranga, reject the path of the rupanugas: the "gaura-bhajis," "gauravadis," gauranga-nagaris and blends
with theosophy. Srila Bhaktisiddhanta sang of it in a song: Bhaktivinoda, servant of Gaura, remembering Narottama's
feet, installed Priya and Hari in Mayapur. Through him the Babaji also revealed to the world once more the day of
Srimati Vishnupriya's appearance — Sri-panchami, the shukla-panchami of the month of Magha; from Bhaktivinoda this
custom spread throughout the Vaishnava world. <!-- F99 F170 F131 -->

For the service of the Deities the "Sri Mayapur-seva-samiti" was formed. Its members were Shyamlal Gosvami,
Shashibhushan Gosvami, Radhikanatha Gosvami, Bipina Bihari Gosvami, Mahamahopadhyaya Ajitanatha Nyayaratna,
Mahendranatha Bhattacharya Vidyaranya, Satya Jivan Lahiri, Raja Vanamali Ray Bahadur of Tarash (Pabna), Shishir Kumar
Ghosh, Matilal Ghosh, Yatindranatha Chaudhuri, Mahendranatha Majumdar, the advocate Kishorilal Sarkar, Nalinaksha Datta,
Kanailal Dey Bahadur, the Deputy Magistrate Navin Chandra Sen and Jagatchandra Ray. <!-- F99 -->

The Babaji also visited Bhaktivinoda in the Calcutta "Bhakti Bhavana," in Ram-Bagan; the Thakura always invited him for
prasada, but the detached old man came only rarely. There the Babaji met the twelve-year-old Bimala Prasada and, learning
that the boy was versed in jyotisha, told him to compose a Vaishnava calendar reckoned in years from Chaitanya's
appearance. So arose the "Navadvipa-panjika," which Sri Chaitanya Math publishes to this day. The Babaji allowed no one
to photograph him, but Bhaktivinoda arranged for a photograph when he was a guest at the "Bhakti Bhavana": this picture
— the Babaji on a wooden seat, with Giridhari on his breast — now stands on the altars of the Gaudiya Maths. The Babaji
loved Bhaktivinoda deeply and, it is said, gave him his own Giridhari; the Thakura installed this shila in his Calcutta
house, and He is served there to this day. <!-- F168 F137 F178 F138 F139 F85 -->

Srila Jagannatha Dasa Babaji entered the eternal pastimes on the shukla-pratipat of the month of Phalguna, Monday 25
February 1895[^17]. Bhaktivinoda wrote in the "Sajjana-toshani": at ten in the morning, in his bhajana-kutira in
Koladvipa, the old general of the Vaishnavas departed for the eternal abode. <!-- F140 -->
