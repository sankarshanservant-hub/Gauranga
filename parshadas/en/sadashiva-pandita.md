# Sadashiva Pandita
*Садашива Пандит · @sadashiva-pandita*

## The Twelfth Branch
Among the residents of Navadvipa who surrounded Sri Chaitanya in the first years of His kirtana there was a devotee of
whom the original sources say little, but always with love: Sadashiva Pandita. Krishnadasa Kaviraja, listing the
branches of Sri Chaitanya's tree, names him thus:

“Sadashiva Pandita set all his hope on the Lord's feet, and at first Nityananda lived in his house.” (CC, Adi 10.34)

In the “Anubhashya” on this verse Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati calls him the twelfth branch of the tree, a
resident of Navadvipa and the Lord's companion in kirtana. And in his commentary on the “Chaitanya-bhagavata” (Adi 2.31)
he names “Pandita Sadashiva” among the many devotees of the Lord who were born in Navadvipa. <!-- F2 F3 F4 Q1 F22 -->

Vrindavana Dasa calls him now Sadashiva Pandita, now simply Sadashiva, and once Sri Sadashiva; elsewhere he calls him
“pure-hearted” (*shuddha-mati*). Murari Gupta writes “Sri Sadashiva Pandita,” and the biographies likewise call him Sri
Sadashiva Pandita, Sadashiva Pandita and Sadashiva. <!-- F1 -->

Nothing more is known of his life. Neither the original sources nor the biographies name any other birthplace of his, his family or his caste, nor the year and day of his appearance, nor when and where he left this world;
nor are his days found in the Vaishnava calendar of the Sri Chaitanya Saraswat Math. Nor do they say who he is in
Krishna's eternal pastimes: Sadashiva Pandita does not appear in the “Gaura-ganoddesha-dipika,” where the name Sadashiva
belongs to Sri Advaita and to Sadashiva Kaviraja, a different person.[^1] <!-- F5 -->

## Nityananda Lived in His House
A special fortune fell to Sadashiva Pandita when Sri Nityananda Prabhu came to Navadvipa. Krishnadasa Kaviraja says
that at first (*prathamei*) Nityananda lived in his house, and Vrindavana Dasa that Nityananda formerly (*purve*) lived
in his house. According to the biographies, on coming to Navadvipa Nityananda stayed with him before anyone else, and
Sadashiva's house became Nityananda's first dwelling in Navadvipa.[^2] <!-- F6 -->

Nityananda is not a wanderer in need of lodging but Balarama Himself, the Lord's eternal companion and servant;
to receive Him in one's home is not to show hospitality but to be blessed with service to Him. It is above all for this
mercy that Vaishnavas remember Sadashiva.

## After Gaya
When the Lord returned from Gaya, His companions saw a change in Him: Nimai Pandita, until lately the foremost of
debaters, who used to meet the Vaishnavas with mockery, had now become the topmost Vaishnava. This change astonished
them all — Shrivasa, Shriman, Gadadhara, Sadashiva, Shuklambara.[^3] <!-- F11 F12 I3 -->

Then, Vrindavana Dasa relates, the Lord told Shriman Pandita that He wished to reveal His heart's pain to
those close to Him, and bade him:

“Come early in the morning, the two of you — you and Sadashiva — to the house of Shuklambara Brahmachari.” (CB, Madhya
1.40) <!-- F7 Q3 -->

Shriman passed on the Lord's words to the devotees:

“Coming back to Himself, He told us: ‘Let us meet tomorrow early in the morning in Shuklambara's house. You, Sadashiva
Pandita and Murari — to you all I shall pour out My grief.’” (CB, Madhya 1.69–70)[^4]

*Gohari*, one of the biographies explains, is an entreaty, an outpouring of grief before one from whom sympathy and help
are hoped for. <!-- F8 Q4 -->

Next morning Shriman Pandita and Gadadhara Pandita came to Shuklambara's house, and “soon
all the Lord's intimate companions, such as Sadashiva, Murari, Shriman and Shuklambara, gathered there” (CB, Madhya
1.81). Then Vishvambhara Himself came and revealed to them the prema of separation from Krishna. <!-- F9 Q5 -->

The biographies say that there the Lord told Sadashiva and the other devotees of His service to Krishna — Srila
Bhaktisiddhanta's “Anubhashya” says the same — and that Sadashiva was among the first to see prema manifest in the Lord
after Gaya. Vrindavana Dasa writes:

“All the devotees, such as Gadadhara, Sadashiva, Shriman Pandita and Shuklambara, were struck to the depths of their
hearts.” (CB, Madhya 1.108)

Seeing and hearing of the unheard-of transformations of love in the Lord, they marvelled, and afterwards, meeting one
another, they shared what each thought of what they had seen (CB, Madhya 1.109–110). <!-- F10 -->

That the Lord called Sadashiva among the few to whom He would reveal the secret pain of separation from Krishna shows
how dear he was to Him. <!-- I1 -->

## The Night Kirtanas
When the Lord began sankirtana, the devotees sang with Him every night in the house of Shrivasa, and sometimes in the
house of Chandrashekhara, and Sadashiva was among them: Vrindavana Dasa names him in the long list of those who took
part in these kirtanas, beside Vakreshvara, Shrigarbha and Shuklambara (CB, Madhya 8.111–116). The “Bhakti-ratnakara”
repeats the same list (12.1853). Thus he was among those with whom the sankirtana movement began. <!-- F13 Q6 -->

One of the biographies observes: worldly people are absorbed in their affairs by day and sleep by night, but the
devotees who had taken shelter of the Lord did not sleep at night, spending the nights in kirtana — and Sadashiva was
among them. <!-- I2 -->

He was with the Lord too when, after the deliverance of Jagai and Madhai, the Lord sported with His companions in the
waters of the Ganga: among the countless servants of the Lord present there Vrindavana Dasa names “Sri Sadashiva” (CB,
Madhya 13.336–339). <!-- F14 -->

## Garments for the Lord's Pastime in Chandrashekhara's House
A special service fell to him when the Lord wished to manifest the pastimes of Vraja in the house of Chandrashekhara
Acharya and to dance Himself. Vrindavana Dasa relates:

“The Lord called Sadashiva and Buddhimanta Khan and said to them: ‘Go and prepare the garments. Find conch bangles,
bodices, silk saris and ornaments, and make everything ready for everyone — for each what befits him.’” (CB, Madhya
18.7–8)

Then and there the Lord Himself named which companion would take which guise, and urged them on: “Buddhimanta, go
quickly and prepare the garments: I shall dance” (CB, Madhya 18.9–13).[^5] <!-- F15 F16 -->

“Taking the Lord's command on their heads, Sadashiva and Buddhimanta went home in boundless bliss.” (CB, Madhya 18.14)

The biographies say that Sadashiva was overflowing with the bliss of love and delighted with this commission. Together
with Buddhimanta he put up a large awning and prepared the garments, and Buddhimanta brought them to the Lord (CB,
Madhya 18.15–16). <!-- F17 Q7 -->

This was neither a performance nor a dressing-up. That day the Lord appeared in the form of His own energy — “in the
form of Prakriti, My energy,” as He Himself says — and, by His word, only one who has conquered his senses is entitled
to see this pastime (CB, Madhya 18.18). The garments prepared by Sadashiva and Buddhimanta were service to this lila.

The “Bhakti-ratnakara” too preserves the memory of this service. Following Vrindavana Dasa, Narahari Chakravarti tells
how the son of Shachi, absorbed in sankirtana, called Sadashiva and Buddhimanta Khan: that day He would go to
Chandrashekhara's house and dance with everyone in the guise of Lakshmi and others, and they were to make ready conch
bangles, saris, bodices, gold and other ornaments — for each the garments befitting him (12.1949–1952). <!-- F21 -->

And when Narahari leads Shrinivasa through Navadvipa, he shows him Chandrashekhara's house and says:

“Here Sadashiva and Buddhimanta Khan together prepared many garments. Gaura-raya will dance in the guise of Lakshmi and
others, and there will be a kirtana that maddens the world.” (“Bhakti-ratnakara” 12.2903–2904)[^6] <!-- F18 Q8 -->

## The Journey to Nilachala
When the Lord, having accepted sannyasa, settled in Nilachala, the devotees of Gauda led by Advaita went to Him for the
Ratha-yatra. Sadashiva Pandita went with them. Vrindavana Dasa, listing those who went, says:

“There came also the pure-hearted Sadashiva Pandita, in whose house Nityananda formerly lived.” (CB, Antya 8.19)

Murari Gupta, too, names “Sri Sadashiva Pandita” among the people of Gauda who set out for Nilachala to see Gauranga
(4.17.7).[^7] <!-- F19 Q2 -->

## In the Vandanas
The Vaishnava-vandanas glorify Sadashiva beside Vidyanidhi, Shrigarbha, Shrinidhi, Buddhimanta and Shuklambara. The
Sanskrit vandana ascribed to Jiva Gosvami says:

“I praise Sadashiva, Vidyanidhi and Shrigarbha, Shrinidhi, Buddhimanta and the excellent Srila Shuklambara — these six
great souls, brahmacharis full of love.” (“Vaishnava-vandana” 47–48)

The Bengali vandana in tripadi likewise glorifies these six as “heirs to the treasure of prema.” That Sadashiva was a
brahmachari only the vandanas say; neither the “Charitamrita” nor the “Chaitanya-bhagavata” nor the biographies report
it, and the vandanas themselves are a late source. Devakinandana Dasa and his “Vaishnavabhidhana” also name him in their
lists, and the “First Vandana” praises “the wise Sadashiva Pandita, in whose house Nityananda formerly lived.” <!-- F20 -->

## Significance
Only a few lines about Sadashiva Pandita have survived, but each of them speaks of service. Nityananda lived in his
house; the Lord called him into the close circle where He revealed the pain of separation from Krishna; night after
night he sang kirtana with the Lord; the Lord's command to prepare garments for His pastime he took on his head and went
to fulfil it in boundless joy. Of when and how he left this world the sources are silent; but the
“Charitamrita” has said the main thing about him: all his hope was on the Lord's feet. <!-- F2 F6 I1 I2 -->

    Panihati (CC, Antya 6.61). In Vrindavana Dasa's accounts of the Lord's Navadvipa pastimes (CB, Madhya 1, 8, 13, 18)

    here, and we do not smooth them over: according to the “Charitamrita,” Nityananda at first lived in Sadashiva's
    house; according to the “Chaitanya-bhagavata,” He first came to Nandana Acharya, and lived with Sadashiva as well.
