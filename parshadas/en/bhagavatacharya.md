# Raghunatha Bhagavatacharya
*Рагхунатха Бхагаватачарья · @bhagavatacharya*

## A Name Given by the Lord
Among those whom Sri Chaitanya blessed with His mercy on the banks of the Ganga was a brahmana of Varahanagara, learned
in the “Srimad-Bhagavatam.” Vrindavana Dasa does not name him: in the “Chaitanya-bhagavata” he is only “a fortunate
brahmana” in whose house the Lord stayed. The name by which all Vaishnavas know him — Bhagavatacharya, “the teacher of
the ‘Bhagavata’” — was given to him by Mahaprabhu Himself, on hearing him read the “Bhagavatam” full of bhakti-rasa. So
he is called by Krishnadasa Kaviraja in the “Chaitanya-charitamrita” and by Kavi Karnapura in the
“Gaura-ganoddesha-dipika” — “Srimad Bhagavatacharya.” <!-- F2 -->

His own name, Raghunatha, we know from Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati. In his “Gaudiya-bhashya” on the
“Chaitanya-bhagavata” he writes of the verse in which this brahmana appears: “The brahmana indicated by the words *eka
brahmanera ghare* (in the house of a brahmana) is Sri Raghunatha Bhagavata Acharya. His description can be found in the
‘Anubhashya’ on the ‘Chaitanya-charitamrita’ [Adi 10.113]” (on CB, Antya 5.110). And the “Anubhashya” itself says: “His
name is said to have been ‘Raghunatha.’” The biographies also call him Sri Raghunatha Pandita, Sri Bhagavatacharya and
Srila Bhagavatacharya Prabhu.[^1] <!-- F1 F7 -->

Of his family and the years of his life the sources say nothing: we do not know when he was born, nor when and where he
left this world; nor are his days found in the Vaishnava calendar of the Sri Chaitanya Saraswat Math. We know only that
he was a brahmana and lived in Varahanagara. <!-- F5 -->

## The Sripat at Varahanagara
Varahanagara (today Baranagar) lies on the Ganga, about three and a half miles north of Calcutta; it is now the northern
edge of the great city. There, in the quarter of Malipara, is Bhagavatacharya's sripat.[^2] Srila Bhaktivinoda Thakura
writes in the “Amrita-pravaha-bhashya” that Bhagavatacharya lived in Varahanagara and that his abode is still called
“the pata of Bhagavatacharya.” Srila Bhaktisiddhanta in the “Anubhashya” names the same place: “His pata is at Malipara
in Varahanagara (about three and a half miles north of Calcutta), on the bank of the Ganga.” <!-- F3 -->

By the early twentieth century the pata had fallen into disrepair; Srila Bhaktisiddhanta reports that its servitor then
was Dhirendra Ganguli, son of the late Rajachandra Ganguli. One of the biographies adds that a manuscript in
Bhagavatacharya's own hand — his book, of which more below — is still shown at the sripat. <!-- F4 F16 -->

## The Lord Listens to the “Bhagavatam”
This was when Mahaprabhu, already a sannyasi, came from Nilachala to Gauda and went along the Ganga from one village of
devotees to the next. After spending some days at Panihati with Raghava Pandita, He came to Varahanagara. Vrindavana
Dasa relates:

“Then the Lord came to Varahanagara, to the house of a most fortunate brahmana. That brahmana was thoroughly learned in
the ‘Bhagavata’; seeing the Lord, he began to read the ‘Bhagavata.’ Hearing him read of bhakti-yoga, Gaurachandra
Narayana was overwhelmed with ecstasy.” (CB, Antya 5.110–112)

On seeing the Lord the brahmana began to read — and that reading was his service to the Lord. And the Lord, who is
Himself the subject of the “Bhagavatam,” listened to him in order to glorify the reader and show the world what the
“Bhagavatam” read with bhakti is worth:

“‘Read on! Read on!’ cried Sri Gauranga-raya, roaring and thundering again and again. The brahmana read, immersed in the
highest bliss, and the Lord danced, forgetful of the external world. Hearing verse after verse on the glory of bhakti,
He fell to the ground again and again. Such was the love the Lord revealed, and seeing Him fall, everyone was afraid. In
this way, for three praharas of the night, the ocean of virtues danced, listening to the ‘Bhagavata.’” (CB, Antya
5.113–117)

The loss of “external consciousness” here is no swoon and no worldly intoxication: it is prema, which the Lord tastes in
the mood of His devotee, and the fear of those around Him is the fear of those who love Him lest He be hurt. <!-- F5 -->

When the Lord came back to Himself, He joyfully embraced the brahmana and said to him:

“Never have I heard anyone else read the ‘Bhagavata’ like this. Therefore your name is ‘Bhagavatacharya.’ Do no other
work but this.” (CB, Antya 5.119–120)[^3]

Hearing what title the Lord had bestowed on the brahmana, everyone loudly sang “Hari! Hari!” (Antya 5.121). The Lord's
praise is not a judgment of “skill,” nor an academic degree: it is mercy toward a service and, with it, a commission.
Mahaprabhu named him for his service and told him to know no other work, and Bhagavatacharya gave the rest of his life
to the “Bhagavatam” — to reading it, explaining it and rendering it into Bengali. The reader of the “Bhagavatam” whom
the Lord acknowledges is one whose reading awakens love of God, not admiration for learning. <!-- F6 I1 Q1 -->

## A Disciple of Gadadhara Pandita
Bhagavatacharya names Sri Gadadhara Pandita Gosvami as his guru. In the opening verse of blessing of his book he bows to
his true guru — Gadadhara, the best of the twice-born, wholly devoted to bhakti, whose form is the form of a servant of
his Master; by the Master, as one of the biographies explains, he means Sri Krishna-Chaitanya.[^4] This does not make
Gadadhara a “mere servant”: service to Mahaprabhu is his eternal nature, for Gadadhara is a manifestation of Sri Radha
Herself. <!-- F8 Q3 -->

Krishnadasa Kaviraja, listing the branches of Gadadhara's tree, places him among the first:

“The finest of them is Dhruvananda; after him come Shridhara Brahmachari, Bhagavatacharya and Haridasa Brahmachari.”
(CC, Adi 12.79)[^5] <!-- F9 -->

In the “Charitamrita” he is also named among the branches of the tree of Mahaprabhu Himself, beside Thakura Saranga:

“Ramadasa, Kavidatta, Sri Gopala dasa, Bhagavatacharya, Thakura Saranga dasa.” (CC, Adi 10.113)

A little further on the name is repeated, and it is the same person (Adi 10.119). And in the list of the branches of Sri
Advaita Acharya we read:

“Other branches are Bhagavata Acharya, Vishnudasa Acharya, Chakrapani Acharya and Ananta Acharya.” (CC, Adi 12.58)

Srila Bhaktisiddhanta comments on this verse: “Bhagavatacharya was at first among Advaita's associates and later joined
Gadadhara's associates” (“Anubhashya” on Adi 12.58). So in the “Charitamrita” one and the same Bhagavatacharya is named
in three places: he was with Advaita Acharya and afterwards came to Gadadhara.[^6] <!-- F10 Q2 Q4 -->

## The “Krishna-prema-tarangini”
The Lord's commission — “do no other work” — Bhagavatacharya fulfilled as a writer too. His book, the “Sri
Krishna-prema-tarangini” (“The River of Love for Krishna”), is a rendering of the “Srimad-Bhagavatam” in Bengali verse.
Kavi Karnapura says of him in the “Gaura-ganoddesha-dipika”: “Srimad Bhagavatacharya, the author of the booklet
‘Krishna-prema-tarangini,’ exceedingly dear to Gauranga” (GGD 203). And Yadunandana Dasa in the “Shakha-nirnayamrita”
honors him thus: “I praise Bhagavatacharya, dear to Gauranga, who composed the great book named ‘Prema-tarangini’”
(verse 6; cited by Srila Bhaktisiddhanta in the “Anubhashya” on CC, Adi 12.58). <!-- F12 -->

He undertook the book, according to one of the biographies, only after he had received the darshan of Sri Chaitanya
Mahaprabhu. It was not written for scholarly fame: the author himself says that, having studied the “Bhagavatam,” he
composed it for the joy of those not versed in learning — so that they too might receive the “Bhagavatam” and devotion
to Sri Krishna. This is mercy, not simplification. <!-- F15 -->

The first nine cantos of the “Bhagavatam” are set out in it briefly, yet the meaning of each is unfolded so fully and
skillfully that the biography calls it the greatest of wonders. In many places the author sings the incomparable glory
of Sri Gaurasundara: he glorifies Krishna — the complete Brahman, whose pastimes are wondrous — Jagannatha, who appeared
in Nilachala, and Sri Gauranga, the Lord in the form of Chaitanya, who bestows prema-bhakti and gives shelter to His
devotees (so in the Tenth Canto, chapter 1, and in the Eleventh, chapter 5).[^7] A rendering of the “Bhagavatam” made by
a disciple of Gadadhara is the “Bhagavatam” read through the eyes of a devotee of Gauranga. The manuscript in the
author's own hand is kept, as we have said, at his sripat in Malipara. <!-- F13 F14 I2 -->

## Shveta-manjari
In the eternal pastimes of Vraja Bhagavatacharya is Shveta-manjari. Kavi Karnapura in the “Gaura-ganoddesha-dipika”
lists twenty-seven maidservants whom Sri Radha has appointed each to her own service, and names those as whom they came
into Gaura's pastimes. Shveta-manjari is the eleventh in the list of gopis, and the eleventh in the list of Gaura's
associates is “Srimad Bhagavatacharya, the author of the booklet ‘Krishna-prema-tarangini’” (GGD 195, 203). So too
Srila Bhaktisiddhanta understands it: “he is Shveta-manjari of Vraja” (“Anubhashya” on CC, Adi 12.58).[^8] <!-- F11 -->

## In Later Traditions
Bhagavatacharya's name lives on in the Vaishnava prayers of homage (vandanas). The “Bhakti-ratnakara” of Narahari
Chakravarti opens by glorifying the Lord's associates, and among them: “Glory to Bhagavatacharya, Madhava and
Shridhara!” (1.34); various “Vaishnava-vandanas” also name him (“Srimat Bhagavatacharya”). <!-- F18 -->

Later books also mention a Bhagavata Acharya at the great festival in Kheturi: the “Prema-vilasa,” the
“Bhakti-ratnakara” and the “Narottama-vilasa” name him among the mahantas of Gadadhara Pandita's branch who came there —
beside Vaninatha Brahmachari, Chaitanya-vallabha Dasa, Pushpa-Gopala and Hari Acharya. Whether this is the same
Raghunatha Bhagavatacharya who read the “Bhagavatam” to the Lord, the sources do not say.[^9] <!-- F17 -->

## Significance
Almost the whole life of Raghunatha Bhagavatacharya is contained in one night and one word of the Lord. Listening to
him, Mahaprabhu danced for three praharas, named him for his service and told him to know no other work — and he knew
none: he explained the “Bhagavatam,” rendered it for simple people, and in that rendering glorified Gaura. That is why
the “Gaura-ganoddesha-dipika” calls him “exceedingly dear to Gauranga,” and why for Vaishnavas his name sounds like the
Lord's commission itself: the true reader of the “Bhagavatam” is one whose reading awakens prema. <!-- F2 F11 F12 I1 I2 -->
