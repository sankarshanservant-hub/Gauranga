# Sarvabhauma Bhattacharya
*Сарвабхаума Бхаттачарья · @sarvabhauma*

## Vasudeva Sarvabhauma
Listing the branches of Sri Chaitanya's tree, Krishnadasa Kaviraja names him among the greatest:

“One great branch is Sarvabhauma Bhattacharya, and another is his sister's husband, Sri Gopinatha Acharya.”
(CC, Adi 10.130)

His own name was Vasudeva; so writes Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati, too, in his “Anubhashya” on this verse.
“Sarvabhauma,” “lord of all the earth,” is not a name but the highest title of a scholar, given to one who stood first in
several sciences at once; he received it on reaching the summit in Shankara's Vedanta as well. Murari Gupta calls him
Vasudeva Sarvabhauma, Lochana Dasa Sarvabhauma Vasudeva Bhattacharya, Vrindavana Dasa “Sarvabhauma Mahashaya,” and
Krishnadasa Kaviraja most often simply “the Bhattacharya.” The biographies also call him Srila Vasudeva Sarvabhauma and
“Sarvabhauma Pandita.” <!-- F1 F2 Q2 -->

The foremost scholar of eastern India, a master of logic and Vedanta at whose feet impersonalist sannyasis studied — and
at the same time the first of the learned whom the Lord in Puri turned to pure devotion, and beside whom He then lived
through all His years in Nilachala. Krishnadasa Kaviraja places his conversion at the head of a whole chapter and opens it
with a bow to the Lord “who turned Sarvabhauma, his heart hardened by false arguments, into a great abode of devotion”
(CC, Madhya 6.1). <!-- F22 -->

## Family and Birthplace
His father was Maheshvara Visharada of Vidyanagara, a village near Navadvipa.[^1] Visharada's house stood by an
embankment, and Devananda Pandita lived nearby; his school, then, was somewhere in Kuliya. Vrindavana Dasa
tells how the Lord, walking through Navadvipa with His devotees, came to this place:

“So Lord Vishvambhara came near the embankment that guarded the house of Maheshvara Visharada, the father of Sarvabhauma
Bhattacharya. There too lived a brahmana named Devananda Pandita.” (CB, Madhya 21.6–7)

Sarvabhauma's family were noble kulina brahmanas of Radha. The sources give neither the day nor the year of his
appearance; one late reference work places his birth in the first half of the fourteenth century of the Shaka era, but
cites no original source. Nor is the day of his appearance found in the Vaishnava calendar of the Sri Chaitanya Saraswat
Math. <!-- F3 F4 F5 F6 Q13 -->

His brother was Vidya-vachaspati. The “Charitamrita” names them together — “the two brothers, Sarvabhauma and
Vidya-vachaspati” (Madhya 15.133); when the Lord came from Puri to Bengal, “He stayed in the house of Vidya-vachaspati”
(Madhya 1.150), and according to one of the biographies this was in that same Vidyanagara. One of them adds that
Vidya-vachaspati taught philosophy and scripture to Rupa and Sanatana. Sarvabhauma's sister was married to Gopinatha
Acharya, likewise a native of Navadvipa and a devotee of the Lord of long standing; the “Charitamrita” calls Gopinatha
“Visharada's son-in-law” (Madhya 6.18), and Sanatana Gosvami in the “Vaishnava-toshani” names both brothers among his
teachers. <!-- F8 F9 -->

Sarvabhauma's wife, whom everyone called “the mother of Shathi,” was devoted to Mahaprabhu with all her soul and loved Him
as a mother. Their daughter Shathi was married to Amogha, a brahmana of a noble kulina family, whose story is still to
come. Sarvabhauma also had a son, Chandaneshvara.[^2] An aunt of his lived in Puri; in her house, in a quiet spot, the Lord
was lodged at his request in the first days. Sarvabhauma's own house in Puri, with its room for the shalagrama, later
became the Gangamata Math, and the shalagrama Damodara whom Sarvabhauma served is worshipped there to this day.

## Brihaspati
Who he is in the eternal pastimes, Kavi Karnapura tells us:

“Sarvabhauma Bhattacharya was formerly the Lord of Speech (Brihaspati) in heaven.” (“Gaura-ganoddesha-dipika” 119)

Brihaspati is the preceptor of the demigods and the patron of eloquence. The Lord Himself, having heard Sarvabhauma's
explanations, exclaimed: “Bhattacharya, I know that you are Brihaspati himself. No one else has the power to explain
scripture like this” (CC, Madhya 6.191); and when Sarvabhauma composed a hundred verses in a moment, Krishnadasa Kaviraja
remarked that they were “verses such as even Brihaspati could not compose” (Madhya 6.206). One of the biographies
explains that the guru of the demigods was born as Sarvabhauma to serve a pastime of the Lord — the deliverance of a
Mayavadi scholar. This is not a learned dweller of heaven who happened to become a man, but a servant of the Lord who came
with Him in order, at the appointed hour, to be turned by Him and to show the world the power of His mercy. <!-- F7 Q1 -->

## The Scholar of Navadvipa
In his student years nyaya was not taught in Bengal: the higher books were lacking. Tradition tells that, having finished
his studies at home, Vasudeva went to Mithila, then the centre of nyaya, whose school was headed by the great logician
Pakshadhara Mishra. The scholars of Mithila would not let students from elsewhere copy their books, lest they lose the
glory of being its only masters; and Vasudeva, gifted with a matchless memory, learned them all by heart. On his return he
wrote them down at home, opened a school of nyaya and taught many; so the centre of the science moved from Mithila to
Navadvipa, which to this day is famed as the foremost school of nyaya.[^3] “In the opinion of some,” as Srila
Bhaktisiddhanta writes, the famous logician Raghunatha Shiromani, author of the “Didhiti,” also studied under Sarvabhauma.
<!-- F14 F15 F16 F17 F18 -->

No less did he attain in the Vedanta of Shankara's school: there too he took the highest place. Remaining a householder,
he became the teacher of many impersonalist sannyasis. Sanskrit hymns in praise of Advaita Acharya are also ascribed to
him — the “Advaita-dvadasha-nama-stotra,” the “Advaitashtaka” and a prayer of Advaita's hundred and eight names. One of the
biographies remarks that his having been a teacher of Mayavada did not prevent him from later becoming a companion of Sri
Gaura worthy of the title *jagad-guru*, teacher of the world. <!-- F19 F22 F23 F24 -->

## Pandita at the Royal Court
Sarvabhauma's fame spread throughout India, and Prataparudra, king of Odisha and Telinga, invited him to Puri as head of
his court scholars (*sabha-pandita*). Srila Bhaktisiddhanta writes in the “Anubhashya” that Sarvabhauma, while a
householder, took *kshetra-sannyasa* — the vow never to leave Jagannatha Puri — and taught Vedanta there.[^4] So by the
time Mahaprabhu came he was the foremost teacher of Vedanta at Jagannatha's temple; he taught it according to Shankara's
commentary — and set about teaching the Lord Himself, until by His mercy he grasped its true meaning. <!-- F20 F21 -->

## In the Temple of Jagannatha
Mahaprabhu came to Puri in spring, in the month of Phalguna, a month after taking sannyasa. At Atharanala He saw the
pinnacle of the temple and, leaving His companions far behind, hastened on in His longing to see Jagannatha, Balarama and
Subhadra:

“Absorbed in ecstasy, the Lord went to the temple of Jagannatha, and when He saw Jagannatha, He grew restless with love. He
ran forward to embrace Jagannatha, but, overwhelmed with love, He fell down in the temple. By providence Sarvabhauma saw Him
there, and he stopped the temple superintendent (*padichha*) from striking Him.” (CC, Madhya 6.3–5)

To attendants who did not know Who stood before them, an unknown sannyasi rushing at the altar was an affront to the holy
place; but by providence Sarvabhauma was close by, and they drew back in fear. Vrindavana Dasa records what the scholar
thought then:

“Sarvabhauma Mahashaya thought: ‘No human being has such extraordinary power. This loud cry, this roar, these wonderful
signs of divine love cannot belong to this world. I think this person is Sri Krishna Chaitanya.’” (CB, Antya 2.432–434)
<!-- F25 F26 F27 -->

He had heard that the young scholar of Navadvipa had taken sannyasa, and now, struck by the Lord's beauty and by the signs
of love on His body, he understood that this was no ordinary man. For a long time the Lord did not come to Himself, and the
hour of Jagannatha's offering was drawing near; so Sarvabhauma, with the help of his disciples and the temple attendants,
had Him carried to his house. The task of carrying Him fell to those very attendants who carry Jagannatha to His chariot,
and they bore Mahaprabhu on their shoulders, loudly chanting the names of Hari (CB, Antya 2.446–448). At home, seeing no
breath, Sarvabhauma grew anxious; he held a wisp of cotton to the Lord's nostrils and was calmed only when he saw it stir
slightly. <!-- F28 F29 -->

Sitting beside Him, he reflected:

“This is a sattvika transformation of great love for Krishna. This swoon, called *pralaya*, is *suddipta sattvika*, ecstasy
at its most blazing, and such blazing ecstasy (*suddipta bhava*) is found only in eternally perfect devotees.” (CC, Madhya
6.11–12)

So, before any discussion at all, the Advaitin scholar recognised in the young sannyasi a devotee of the very highest
order. <!-- F35 -->

Meanwhile Nityananda, Mukunda, Jagadananda and Damodara reached the Lion Gate and heard that some sannyasi had fallen
senseless in the temple and that Sarvabhauma had carried Him home. There Gopinatha Acharya, who knew Mukunda from
Navadvipa, met them and led them all to his brother-in-law. Sarvabhauma received the Lord's companions with love, made sure
Who He was, and bowed to Nityananda as the leader of the party — according to Vrindavana Dasa, he took the dust of His feet
(CB, Antya 2.458). Then he sent his son Chandaneshvara to take them for darshana, asking the guests to restrain themselves
before the Deity and not to behave as their Master had. <!-- F30 F31 F32 -->

On their return they found the Lord still unconscious and began to chant the holy names loudly; only after noon, in the
third watch of the day (in Vrindavana Dasa, after more than nine hours), did He rise with a cry of “Hari! Hari!” and ask
how He had come there. Nityananda told Him, and the Lord embraced Sarvabhauma:

“Jagannatha is so merciful that He has brought Me to Sarvabhauma's house. I was anxious, not knowing how to meet you; but
Krishna has fulfilled My desire with ease.” (CB, Antya 2.480–482) <!-- F33 F34 -->

Gopinatha Acharya joined them — a devotee of the Lord since Navadvipa, who believed in His divinity. Sarvabhauma had the
guests bathe quickly in the ocean and fed them all with Jagannatha's maha-prasada, serving it himself on golden plates; and
he forbade the Lord to go to the temple alone: “Go with me or with my men” (CC, Madhya 6.62). The Lord replied that He would
not go inside again and would see Jagannatha from beside the Garuda pillar. Of this day Vrindavana Dasa says: “Sarvabhauma
is the Lord's companion birth after birth; how else could he have gained such fortune?” (CB, Antya 2.497). <!-- F36 F37 F133 -->

Murari Gupta begins the story differently: the Lord, he says, first came to Sarvabhauma's house, and Sarvabhauma, struck by
His form, sent his younger brother with Him to the temple; and Kavi Karnapura says that it was Sarvabhauma's son who took
the Lord to the temple. <!-- F134 -->

## Guardian of a Young Sannyasi
Learning from Gopinatha that the Lord was the son of Jagannatha Mishra and the grandson of Nilambara Chakravarti,
Sarvabhauma rejoiced: “Nilambara Chakravarti is known as a fellow student of Visharada” (CC, Madhya 6.53). He told the Lord
that, as a sannyasi, He was worthy of honour and that he himself was His servant. But the Lord answered with great humility:

“You are the teacher of the whole world and the benefactor of all people. You teach Vedanta, and you are a helper of
sannyasis. I am a young sannyasi who does not know good from bad. I have taken shelter of you, and I regard you as My Guru.
I have come here for your company alone. Please take care of Me in every way. Today I fell into great danger, and you
rescued Me from it.” (CC, Madhya 6.58–61) <!-- F38 F39 -->

Sarvabhauma was much older and took these words in earnest: he felt himself the young sannyasi's guardian. The next day he
asked Gopinatha in which sampradaya He had taken sannyasa, and hearing that His name was Sri Krishna Chaitanya and His guru
Keshava Bharati, he said: “His name is excellent, but the Bharati sampradaya is of middling rank” (CC, Madhya 6.72). He was
anxious how the Lord, so young, would keep His vows, and he resolved to explain Vedanta to Him constantly so as to establish
Him in renunciation, and, should He wish, to initiate Him anew into sannyasa of a more honoured line (6.74–76). <!-- F40 F42 -->

Gopinatha and Mukunda were grieved. Gopinatha replied that in Sri Chaitanya the signs of Bhagavan were fully manifest and
that He had no need of any sampradaya; the devotees of Navadvipa had had direct evidence of it. Sarvabhauma's disciples
demanded proof, and Gopinatha said the essential thing:

“The Lord is not known by inference. Inference is no proof where the truth of the Lord is concerned; without His mercy no
one can know Him.” (CC, Madhya 6.81–82)

He told his brother-in-law plainly: you are the greatest of scholars, but not a particle of the Lord's mercy has come to
you, and so the truth about Him is closed to you; you yourself have seen in the Lord a love such as is not found among men,
and still “you take all this for the Lord's maya, mere worldly behaviour” (6.85–91). One of the biographies calls this error
“the maya of knowledge” (*jnana-maya*): pride of learning keeps one from feeling what one's eyes behold. The manifestations
of prema in the Lord are not maya; maya lies in the outlook that takes them for something worldly. <!-- F41 F43 -->

Sarvabhauma smilingly objected that he was arguing only for the sake of truth:

“Chaitanya Gosani is a great devotee, but in this age of Kali there is no avatara of Vishnu. That is why Vishnu is called
Triyuga, ‘He who appears in three ages’. The scriptures teach that there is no avatara in the Kali-yuga.” (CC, Madhya
6.94–95)

Gopinatha answered that the name Triyuga means only that in Kali there is no *lila-avatara*; but in every age Krishna comes
as the *yuga-avatara*. He cited the “Bhagavatam” (10.8.13; 11.5.31–32) and the verse of the “Mahabharata” found in the
“Vishnu-sahasranama”: “He is of golden colour, His limbs are like gold… He has taken sannyasa, He is self-restrained and
tranquil” (Dana-dharma 149; CC, Madhya 6.104).[^5] As the husband of Sarvabhauma's sister he could afford to speak to him
freely: “he would instruct his brother-in-law with censure, praise and jest” (6.112). <!-- F44 F45 Q3 F9 -->

When Gopinatha and Mukunda bitterly told the Lord how Sarvabhauma, denying His divinity and wishing to be His teacher, had
found fault with Him, the Lord stopped them:

“Do not speak like that. The Bhattacharya is kind to Me. He wants to protect My vows of sannyasa, and out of fatherly
affection he shows Me compassion. What fault is there in that?” (CC, Madhya 6.116–117) <!-- F46 -->

Vrindavana Dasa shows this pastime from another side. In his account the Lord Himself, alone with Sarvabhauma, asks him to
teach Him what rules a sannyasi must keep so as not to fall into the well of worldly life; and Sarvabhauma, praising His
devotion, reproaches Him for having taken sannyasa so early, and in defence of bhakti even quotes the words of Shankara
himself (CB, Antya 3.10–64). Vrindavana Dasa says that thus “Sri Gaurahari displayed His maya” and that Sarvabhauma, “under
the influence of maya,” did not understand Him (3.17, 3.75). This is the yogamaya of His pastime, not worldly illusion: the
same Vrindavana Dasa calls Sarvabhauma the Lord's eternal companion. Sarvabhauma's former Advaita was part of the lila and a
lesson to the world — but Mayavada does not thereby become true, and the Lord conceded nothing to it. <!-- F133 -->

## Seven Days of Vedanta
The next morning, after Jagannatha's darshana, Sarvabhauma seated the Lord in his house and began to explain Vedanta to Him:
“To hear Vedanta is the duty of a sannyasi” (CC, Madhya 6.121). For seven days the Lord listened in silence like an
obedient student. On the eighth day the teacher asked why He said nothing — one could not even tell whether He understood.
The Lord replied that the meaning of the sutras was clear to Him, but Sarvabhauma's explanations obscured it:

“A commentary (*bhashya*) should bring the meaning of a sutra to light, but your commentary only covers it. <…> The meaning
of Vyasa's sutras is like the rays of the sun, and you cover it with the clouds of a commentary of your own invention.” (CC,
Madhya 6.131, 6.138) <!-- F47 F48 -->

The direct meaning of the Upanishads, the Lord said, is the very essence of Vedanta, while Sarvabhauma abandons the direct
sense of the words (*abhidha*) for an indirect one (*lakshana*). The “attributelessness” of Brahman in the shruti denies only
material qualities; Brahman is Bhagavan Himself, full of countless perfections, with an eternal spiritual form. Citing the
Upanishads and Puranas, the Lord explained the sutras, refuted Advaita and established the eternal distinction between
Brahman and the jiva, the teaching of the Lord's energies and of the transformation of energy (*parinama-vada*).
Sarvabhauma defended his position with “captious wrangling, quibbling, points of defeat and the like” (6.177),[^6] but the
Lord refuted them all. Of Shankara He said:

“The Acharya (Shankara) is not at fault: it was the Lord's order. That is why he invented an atheistic scripture.” (CC,
Madhya 6.180)

— and in support He cited two verses of the “Padma Purana” (6.181–182).[^7] <!-- F49 F50 F51 -->

## Eighteen Meanings of “Atmarama”
Sarvabhauma sat stunned. And then the Lord recited a verse of the “Bhagavatam”:

“Even sages who delight in the atma and are free from the knots of the heart render causeless devotion to Urukrama, for
such are the qualities of Hari.” (“Srimad-Bhagavatam” 1.7.10; CC, Madhya 6.186)

Sarvabhauma asked Him to explain it, but the Lord wished first to hear him. The scholar gave nine explanations, each
supported by scripture — and the Lord, smiling, called him Brihaspati. Then, without touching any of his nine meanings, He
revealed eighteen, joining each word of the verse with the word “atmarama”: Bhagavan, His energies and His qualities, He
said, are inconceivable and steal the hearts of the perfected and of seekers alike (6.187–198).[^8] Later the Lord called this
“the madness in My words,” which Sarvabhauma took to be the truth (Madhya 24.7). <!-- F52 Q4 -->

## Mercy
Hearing these explanations, Sarvabhauma understood Who stood before him:

“Hearing this, the Bhattacharya was struck with wonder. Knowing the Lord to be Krishna, he began to reproach himself: ‘He
is Krishna Himself, and I, not knowing it, have committed a great offence in my pride.’ Reproaching himself, he took
shelter of the Lord, and the Lord then resolved to show him mercy.” (CC, Madhya 6.199–201)

One of the biographies says that, “utterly defeated,” he reproached himself for having neglected the path of bhakti until
then. But it was not the debate that conquered Sarvabhauma. Gopinatha had said from the very beginning that God is known
only by His mercy, and the “Charitamrita” stresses exactly this: the Lord “resolved to show him mercy,” “by the Lord's
mercy all truths were revealed to him” (6.201, 6.205). The talks on Vedanta and on “atmarama” were only the occasion; what
melted Sarvabhauma's heart was mercy. <!-- F53 -->

And the Lord revealed Himself to him:

“The Lord showed him His own form: He became four-armed. First He showed him the four-armed form, and then His very own
form — dark, with the flute at His lips.” (CC, Madhya 6.202–203)

Vrindavana Dasa describes this revelation differently: with a loud cry the Lord assumed a six-armed form, “wonderfully
beautiful and shining like millions of suns,” and at the sight of it Sarvabhauma fell senseless (CB, Antya 3.100–108). The
biographies describe the six-armed form of Gaurachandra thus: two arms of Sri Krishna, two of Sri Ramachandra and two His
own.[^9] This was a darshana the Lord granted by His own will and mercy, not a “vision” in the worldly sense of the word: He
Himself told Sarvabhauma to keep what he had seen secret as long as He remained in this world (3.148–149). The Deity of
Mahaprabhu in His six-armed form, according to one of the biographies, is worshipped to this day in the Jagannatha temple
in Puri. <!-- F54 F61 Q5 -->

Sarvabhauma fell flat in obeisance, and by the Lord's mercy the whole truth awoke in him: he sang the power of the holy
name and the greatness of the gift of prema, and in a moment, within less than half an hour, composed a hundred verses in
the Lord's praise — “verses such as even Brihaspati could not compose” (CC, Madhya 6.206).[^10] The Lord embraced him, and
Sarvabhauma lost consciousness in bliss; the eight sattvika transformations appeared on his body — tears, stupor, thrill,
sweat, trembling and the rest — and he danced, sang, wept and fell down, longing to touch the Lord's feet (6.207–208). In Vrindavana Dasa
the Lord places His hand on his head and His foot on his chest, and Sarvabhauma, clasping His feet, cries out: “Today I
have caught the thief who stole my heart” (CB, Antya 3.109–113). <!-- F55 F56 F57 -->

Gopinatha Acharya rejoiced: “It is that very Bhattacharya whom You have brought to such a state!” The Lord replied: “You
are a devotee, and through association with you Jagannatha has shown him full mercy” (CC, Madhya 6.210–211). And
Sarvabhauma, coming to himself, prayed:

“That You have delivered the world is a small deed; that You have delivered me — that is a wondrous power! Logic had made
me as hard and dull as a lump of iron, yet You have melted me — mighty is Your power!” (CC, Madhya 6.213–214)

Vrindavana Dasa gives the Lord's answer as well:

“Listen, Sarvabhauma: you are My eternal companion. That is why you can see My glory and greatness. It is for your sake
alone that I have come here, so that you may worship Me without restraint.” (CB, Antya 3.142–143)

The next day, when Gopinatha reminded him that his prediction had come true, Sarvabhauma bowed to him:

“You are a great devotee (*maha-bhagavata*), and I am blinded by logic; the Lord has shown me mercy through my connection
with you.” (CC, Madhya 6.246)

Gopinatha danced for joy, clapping his hands, and Sarvabhauma glorified him as the kinsman through whom he had received
mercy. <!-- F58 F133 -->

## Two Verses
Of the hundred verses, two have been preserved. After Jagannatha's darshana Sarvabhauma returned home with Jagadananda and
Damodara, gave them much maha-prasada for the Lord, and wrote two verses on a palm leaf. Mukunda Datta took the leaf from
Jagadananda's hands and copied them onto the wall at the entrance to the Lord's room; and when the Lord, having read it,
tore the leaf up, the devotees had already learned the verses from the wall:[^11]

“I take refuge in the one primeval Person, the ocean of mercy, who has assumed the form of Sri Krishna Chaitanya to teach
detachment, knowledge and devotion to Himself.

He appeared under the name Krishna Chaitanya to revive His own devotional yoga, which had been lost through time. May the
bee of my mind sink deep, deep into His lotus feet.” (CC, Madhya 6.254–255; “Chaitanya-chandrodaya” 6.74–75)

These two verses, says Krishnadasa Kaviraja, “are a jewelled necklace on the devotees' necks, and like the beat of a great
drum they proclaim Sarvabhauma's glory” (6.256). Murari Gupta, too, gives the same two verses as Sarvabhauma's prayer: in
his account Sarvabhauma addresses the Lord after He, before many brahmanas, has set forth to him the hidden meaning of
Vedanta — shelter at His feet (3.12.12–18). Vrindavana Dasa paraphrases verses close to them among those Sarvabhauma
uttered while holding the Lord's feet (CB, Antya 3.123–127). <!-- F59 F60 Q6 F134 -->

## Faith in Maha-prasada
The next morning, just before sunrise, the Lord Himself came to Sarvabhauma's house with Jagannatha's prasada and garland.
Sarvabhauma had just woken and, rising, cried out, “Krishna! Krishna!” — and the Lord rejoiced to hear it. Without having
brushed his teeth or bathed, Sarvabhauma joyfully honoured the prasada, reciting two verses of the “Padma Purana,” the
first of which runs:

“Even if it is dry, stale or brought from a distant land, it should be eaten as soon as it is received. Here there is no
consideration of time.” (CC, Madhya 6.225)

Seeing this, the Lord embraced him, and both danced in tears, trembling and sweat. The Lord said:

“Today I have conquered the three worlds without effort. Today I have ascended to Vaikuntha. Today all My desires are
fulfilled, for Sarvabhauma has come to have faith in maha-prasada.” (CC, Madhya 6.230–231) <!-- F62 Q7 -->

“From that time the Bhattacharya's pride was broken. He knew nothing but Chaitanya's feet, and he explained scripture as
teaching nothing but bhakti.” (CC, Madhya 6.236–237)

The next day Sarvabhauma came to the Lord before he had even seen Jagannatha, repenting of his former frame of mind, and
asked what is most important in the practice of bhakti. The Lord answered: nama-sankirtana — and explained the verse
“*harer nama harer nama harer namaiva kevalam*” (6.239–243). From then on Sarvabhauma constantly repeated the names of Sri
Krishna Chaitanya, the son of Shachi; this became his meditation and the essence of his practice (6.257–258). <!-- F123 F124 -->

## “Bhakti-pade”
Once, bowing to the Lord, Sarvabhauma recited Brahma's prayer from the “Bhagavatam” (10.14.8) — but as he recited it he
changed two syllables at the end: instead of *mukti-pade* (“at the feet of liberation”) he said *bhakti-pade*. The Lord
asked why he had changed the verse. Sarvabhauma answered that the fruit of bhakti is higher than liberation, that merging
into the impersonal Brahman is given as punishment to those hostile to God's service, and that devotees do not accept it.
The Lord objected that the word *mukti-pada* has another meaning too: He at whose feet liberation dwells — that is,
Krishna Himself. But Sarvabhauma stood his ground:

“When I say the word ‘mukti’, my mind feels disgust and dread; when I say the word ‘bhakti’, it fills with delight.” (CC,
Madhya 6.276)

The Lord laughed and embraced him firmly. So the fearless champion of Advaita became a steadfast champion of bhakti: “The
Bhattacharya had once studied and taught Mayavada, yet by Chaitanya's grace such words now sprang from his lips” (6.278).
One of the biographies says that through his association with the Lord he could not even bring himself to say
“liberation” and said “devotion” instead. <!-- F63 F64 F65 Q8 -->

The conversion of the foremost scholar of Puri became a sign to all: “seeing the Bhattacharya become a Vaishnava, everyone
knew the Lord to be the son of the king of Vraja Himself,” and Kashi Mishra and all the residents of Nilachala came to take
shelter at His feet (CC, Madhya 6.280–281). The scholars of Puri took His shelter, and after them many nobles of Odisha and
the king himself came to the Lord. <!-- F66 -->

Kavi Karnapura in his mahakavya adds one more story. Once, when the Lord was leaving Puri, Gopinatha showed
Him a manuscript, and in a verse of Sarvabhauma's the Lord saw the name “Krishna”; He fell down in ecstasy and next
morning, thinking He had offended Sarvabhauma, returned to Puri to serve him (mahakavya 12.35–58). In Karnapura, too, the
Lord shows Sarvabhauma a four-armed form (12.33). <!-- F134 -->

## Farewell before the South
Krishnadasa Kaviraja gives the dates precisely: the Lord took sannyasa in the month of Magha, came to Puri in Phalguna,
and after the Dola-yatra, in Chaitra, delivered Sarvabhauma; and at the beginning of Vaishakha He resolved to go south
(CC, Madhya 7.4–6). When He came to Sarvabhauma to ask leave, Sarvabhauma fell at His feet:

“By the piety of many births I obtained Your company, and now Providence is breaking it off. If a thunderbolt struck my
head or my son died, I could bear it; but separation from You I cannot bear.” (CC, Madhya 7.47–48)

The Lord put off His departure and spent five days in his house; the mother of Shathi cooked for Him (7.50–54).
<!-- F67 F129 -->

When at last the Lord set off along the seashore towards Alalanatha, Sarvabhauma sent four sets of kaupinas and outer
cloths after Him with a brahmana and, taking leave, earnestly begged Him:

“Ramananda Raya lives on the bank of the Godavari; he is the governor of Vidyanagara. Do not neglect him, thinking him a
shudra and a worldly man (*vishayi*). At my request, be sure to meet him. He is the one man worthy of Your company; in all
the world there is no devotee who relishes rasa (*rasika*) as he does. <…> Not understanding his extraordinary words and
actions, I used to mock him, knowing him to be a Vaishnava. By Your grace I now understand who he truly is.” (CC, Madhya
7.62–67) <!-- F68 F69 Q9 -->

Srila Bhaktisiddhanta explains in the “Anubhashya” that Sarvabhauma had noticed Vaishnava qualities in Ramananda even
before, and, having become a devotee, recognised in him an *adhikari-rasika-bhakta*, a true connoisseur of rasa. One of the
biographies adds that by the Lord's mercy Sarvabhauma's divine eyes were opened, and together with the Lord's nature he
came to know Ramananda's as well. Even the greatest learning fails, without mercy, to recognise either the Lord or His pure
devotee: Sarvabhauma at first recognised neither Mahaprabhu nor Ramananda. In Nilachala a special friendship bound the
two; both, like Purushottama Acharya, later Svarupa Damodara, had come from Navadvipa. <!-- F71 F72 I2 -->

The Lord embraced him and said, “Worship Krishna at home and bless Me, so that by your grace I may return to Nilachala”
(7.69) — and departed, while Sarvabhauma fell senseless to the ground. The Lord did not look back: such are the hearts of
the great — “harder than a thunderbolt, softer than a flower” (7.72–73). Nityananda lifted Sarvabhauma up and sent him
home. <!-- F129 -->

On the Godavari the Lord told Ramananda that He had come at Sarvabhauma's request, and Ramananda replied:

“Sarvabhauma regards me as his servant; even in my absence he is careful of my welfare. By his mercy I have seen You.”
(CC, Madhya 8.32–33)

And the Lord told Ramananda how, purified in Sarvabhauma's company, He had asked him to explain the truth of devotion to
Krishna — and Sarvabhauma had answered that he could not tell it himself, but that Ramananda Raya knew it (8.124–125).
<!-- F70 F128 -->

## The Lord's Return
When Mahaprabhu returned from the south, Sarvabhauma, after the long separation, met Him on the seashore beside himself
with joy; he wept, fallen at His feet. He brought the Lord home, fed Him maha-prasada and himself massaged His feet, and the
Lord, to please him, spent the night in his house telling of His journey:

“The Lord said, ‘I have travelled to so many holy places, yet I have not seen a single Vaishnava equal to you. Ramananda
Raya alone gave Me great happiness.’ The Bhattacharya said, ‘That is why I asked You to meet him.’” (CC, Madhya 9.356–357)
<!-- F73 -->

Even before the Lord's return the king, at Sarvabhauma's request, had set aside for Him Kashi Mishra's quiet house near
the temple. After Jagannatha's darshana Sarvabhauma took the Lord there and, seated at His right, began to introduce to
Him the devotees of Puri: Janardana, Krishnadasa of the golden staff, the scribe Shikhi Mahiti, Pradyumna Mishra —
“foremost among the Vaishnavas,” Murari Mahiti, Chandaneshvara, Simheshvara and others; and when Bhavananda Raya came with
his sons, he said, “This is Bhavananda Raya. His eldest son is Ramananda Raya” (CC, Madhya 10.29–50). <!-- F75 F76 -->

Here too the Lord told Sarvabhauma how His companion in the south, Kala Krishnadasa, had behaved, and sent him away
(10.62–63). Svarupa Damodara, on coming to Puri, met Sarvabhauma and the others (10.127); and Jagadananda, who had come with the
Lord from Navadvipa, had known him since the very first days in Puri. <!-- F74 F77 -->

On another day the Lord was speaking of Krishna with Sarvabhauma and other devotees when Govinda arrived, the servant of
Ishvara Puri, sent by his guru to serve the Lord. Sarvabhauma asked why Ishvara Puri had had a servant from a shudra
family. The Lord replied that Ishvara Puri, like the Lord Himself, is independent, and his mercy is not bound by rules:
Krishna ate in Vidura's house, and His mercy waits only for loving service. And then He asked in His turn:

“Bhattacharya, consider this: My guru's servant is worthy of My respect. It is not proper for Me to have him serve Me, yet
My guru has ordered it. What am I to do?” The Bhatta said, “The guru's order is mighty, and one must not transgress it.”
(CC, Madhya 10.142–144)

And the Lord accepted Govinda; according to one of the biographies, on Sarvabhauma's advice He accepted Kashishvara too at
that time. The Lord Himself needs no one's service; having accepted a guru, He showed how a disciple honours the guru's will
and all that is connected with him. <!-- F78 -->

At that time Brahmananda Bharati, a godbrother of Keshava Bharati, also came to Puri. He said that two Brahmans had appeared
in Puri — the “moving” golden one, Mahaprabhu, and the “unmoving” dark one, Jagannatha — and when the Lord disputed it, he
left the dispute to Sarvabhauma's judgement, citing the verse of the “Vishnu-sahasranama” on the golden Lord who is a
sannyasi. Sarvabhauma declared him the winner (CC, Madhya 10.167–172), and when Bharati told how, on seeing the Lord, he had
felt the presence of Krishna Himself, Sarvabhauma confirmed it: without prema there is no seeing Krishna — it was only by
the Lord's mercy that Bharati had received His darshana. The Lord exclaimed: “Vishnu! Vishnu! What are you saying,
Sarvabhauma? Such excessive praise is a mark of censure” (10.180–182).[^12] <!-- F79 -->

## Mediator of Mercy to the King
One of the biographies calls Sarvabhauma “the Lord's eternal companion, who brought Mahaprabhu's mercy to King
Prataparudra.” The king was very dear to him — as to Ramananda and to Kashi Mishra — and through him Prataparudra came
little by little to know Sri Chaitanya and became His great devotee. Vrindavana Dasa records the words the Lord spoke to
Ramananda: “I have come here for the sake of three: you, Sarvabhauma and Ramananda Raya” (CB, Antya 5.202); and Murari
Gupta says that King Gajapati, wishing to see the Lord, summoned Sarvabhauma together with Ramananda. <!-- F80 I6 -->

While the Lord was still in the south, the king called Sarvabhauma to the palace and asked him to arrange a meeting.
Sarvabhauma warned him that this would be very hard: a renounced sannyasi, “not even in a dream would He meet a king” (CC,
Madhya 10.8) — but he promised to try. And asked why he had not kept the Lord back, he answered: “He is the Lord Himself,
fully independent — Sri Krishna in person. He depends on no one” (10.15). And the king said:

“Bhatta, you are the crest-jewel of the learned. Since you call Him Krishna, I accept it as the truth.” (CC, Madhya 10.17)
<!-- F81 F82 -->

When the Lord returned, Sarvabhauma, hesitantly, told Him of the king's longing. The Lord covered His ears with His hands,
uttered the name of Narayana and said that for a renounced sannyasi to see a king is like swallowing poison; “if you speak
of this once more, you will not see Me here any longer” (CC, Madhya 11.6–12). This severity concerned not the Vaishnava
king but the duty of a sannyasi, which the Lord kept for the world's instruction. Returning from Cuttack, the king summoned
Sarvabhauma again and, learning of the firm refusal, said that without the Lord's mercy he needed neither kingdom nor life.
Sarvabhauma found himself between two fires — and found a way out:

“He is subject to love, and your love is very deep; He will surely show His mercy to you. <…> On the day of the
Ratha-yatra the Lord, with all His devotees, will dance before the chariot, overwhelmed with love. In that ecstasy of love
He will enter the flower garden. At that time you must lay aside your royal dress and come alone.” (CC, Madhya 11.52,
11.54–55)

There, he said, the king should clasp the Lord's feet and recite the verses on the rasa dance — the five chapters of the
“Bhagavatam” (10.29–33) — and the Lord, unaware of the outer world, hearing Krishna's name, would embrace the king as a
Vaishnava (11.56–57). <!-- F83 F84 F85 -->

He also told the king that the Snana-yatra was three days off; and when after it, in the days of anavasara, the Lord in
separation from Jagannatha went away to Alalanatha, Sarvabhauma brought Him back to Puri and informed the king (CC, Madhya
11.60–65). <!-- F130 -->

When some two hundred devotees arrived from Gauda, the king went up to the palace roof with Sarvabhauma and Gopinatha and
asked to have each one named. Sarvabhauma named Svarupa Damodara — “Mahaprabhu's second body” — and Govinda, and the
others, whom he himself did not yet know, were named at his request by Gopinatha, who knew them all from Navadvipa:
Advaita, Srivasa, Gangadasa, Acharya Nandana, the residents of Kulina-grama and of Khanda — Narahari, Mukunda Dasa,
Raghunandana and others (CC, Madhya 11.70–93).[^13] The king asked why they were going to the Lord before going to the
temple, and Sarvabhauma answered: “This is the natural way of love: their hearts long to meet Mahaprabhu. First they will
all meet Him; then they will go with Him to see Jagannatha” (11.106–107). And when the king marvelled at their radiance,
Sarvabhauma explained that this sankirtana of love is Chaitanya's own creation, and that only one who has received a
particle of His mercy can accept Him as Krishna (11.97–103). <!-- F86 F87 Q13 -->

From Cuttack the king wrote to Sarvabhauma, begging him to have all the Lord's close companions plead for him. Sarvabhauma
showed the letter to the devotees, who marvelled at the king's devotion but doubted whether such a thing could be spoken of
to the Lord. Then Sarvabhauma proposed: “Let us all go to Him, just this once. We will not ask Him to meet the king; we will
only tell Him how the king is behaving” (CC, Madhya 12.15). Nityananda stepped forward; the Lord did not agree to a
meeting, but Nityananda obtained His outer cloth from Govinda, and it was sent to the king through Sarvabhauma (12.37).
<!-- F88 F89 -->

At the Ratha-yatra the king, who was sweeping the road before the chariot, by the Lord's mercy understood the secret of His
dancing in seven groups of devotees at once and gave Sarvabhauma a sign of it: “The king and Sarvabhauma silently exchanged
knowing signs; no one else knew Chaitanya's secret” (CC, Madhya 13.58). Sarvabhauma and Kashi Mishra, the king's guru, were
astonished at the grace shown to the king (13.62). And when the king caught the falling Lord and He, outwardly displeased,
exclaimed that a worldly man had touched Him (13.182), the king trembled, but Sarvabhauma reassured him:

“Do not worry. The Lord is very pleased with you. He is only making you an example in order to teach His own people.” (CC,
Madhya 13.186–187)

At heart the Lord was already won by the king's love; His displeasure was outward and served as a lesson, not worldly
disdain. Sarvabhauma promised to choose his moment and present the king's request to the Lord. <!-- F90 F91 -->

And the moment came in the garden at Balagandi. As Sarvabhauma had advised, the king laid aside his royal dress and,
alone, in the simple guise of a Vaishnava, with the devotees' leave, clasped the feet of the resting Lord, massaged them
and recited the verses on the rasa dance. When he reached the verse “*tava kathamritam*” (“Bhagavatam” 10.31.9), the Lord
rose and embraced him (CC, Madhya 14.5–10) — just as Sarvabhauma had foretold. At the Nanda-utsava, when the Lord, dressed
as a cowherd boy with a yoke of milk and yoghurt, danced with the devotees, the king too was there with Kashi Mishra and
Sarvabhauma (15.17–20); one of the biographies says that the king and Sarvabhauma played His cowherd companions in this
lila. <!-- F92 F93 Q11 -->

## The Feast in Sarvabhauma's House
When after the Chaturmasya the Gaudiya devotees had gone home, Sarvabhauma came to the Lord and with folded hands asked Him
to take His meals at his house for a whole month. The Lord refused: it was against the dharma of a sannyasi. Sarvabhauma
asked for twenty days, fifteen, ten — the Lord would agree to only one; at last they settled on five. And so that every day
of the month one of the Lord's sannyasis would dine with him, Sarvabhauma invited Paramananda Puri for five days, his close
friend Svarupa Damodara for four, and eight other sannyasis for two days each; the Lord he asked to come alone or with
Svarupa, lest he offend one of the sannyasis by a lack of due honour (CC, Madhya 15.186–198). <!-- F94 -->

The cooking was done by the mother of Shathi, a devotee of the Lord and a skilled cook, and Sarvabhauma helped her. For the
Lord he set aside a separate room in the quietest part of the house, next to the room where food was offered to the
shalagrama, and on a whole leaf of a huge banana tree he heaped a mountain of rice drenched in ghee, surrounding it with
countless dishes of Bengal and Odisha (15.199–221). At noon the Lord came and marvelled: how could so much be cooked in six
hours? All this had already been offered to Krishna — so Radha and Krishna had tasted the feast, and the remnants would be
His. Sarvabhauma replied:

“O Lord, do not be astonished. By the potency of Him who will eat it, the feast was made ready. My wife did not have to
labour at the cooking. He by whose potency the feast was made ready knows how it was done.” (CC, Madhya 15.232–233)
<!-- F95 F96 -->

And when the Lord objected that no one could eat so much, Sarvabhauma said:

“At Nilachala You eat fifty-two times, and the food of each offering comes in hundreds upon hundreds of loads. In Dvaraka
You eat in the palaces of the sixteen thousand queens, in the homes of Your eighteen mothers and in the houses of the
Yadavas. In Vraja You eat twice a day, morning and evening, in the houses of all the cowherds — Your uncles on Your father's
and mother's side, the husbands of Your aunts and others — and of all Your friends. At the Govardhana sacrifice You ate
heaps upon heaps of food. Compared with that, this food is not even one mouthful. You are the Lord Himself, and I am a
tiny, wretched soul. Please accept this one mouthful of *madhukari*.” (CC, Madhya 15.239–243)[^14]

The Lord smiled and sat down to eat. One of the biographies sees in this service the model of a Vaishnava householder, like
Advaita and Srivasa, who gave his home, his family and his social duties to the service of the Lord. <!-- F97 I5 -->

## Amogha
Sarvabhauma's son-in-law Amogha, Shathi's husband, came of a noble kulina family but loved to find fault with others.
Knowing his ways, Sarvabhauma stood at the door with a stick in his hand; but while he was serving, Amogha slipped in and,
seeing the feast, said: “This food would satisfy ten or twelve people, and one sannyasi eats so much all by himself!” (CC,
Madhya 15.248). Sarvabhauma ran after him with the stick, but he escaped; and the mother of Shathi, hearing what had
happened, beat her head and breast in grief and cried again and again, “Let Shathi become a widow!” (15.252). The Lord only
laughed, calmed them both, ate the prasada and was very pleased. <!-- F98 F99 -->

This is neither worldly cruelty nor an outburst of temper. For Sarvabhauma and his wife, blasphemy against the Lord who had
come into their home was unbearable. One of the biographies explains: a devotee living in society as a householder does
not belong to it; his concern is Bhagavan's pleasure, and one who reviles the Lord or His devotees is given up, be he guru,
father or mother — so Bali gave up Shukra, Prahlada Hiranyakashipu, Bharata Kaikeyi (cf. “Bhagavatam” 5.5.18,
10.74.40).[^15] To the mother of Shathi her son-in-law was dearer than to Sarvabhauma himself — and yet she wished him dead;
and Srivasa, for the same reason, seeing the Lord's displeasure, had his own mother-in-law dragged out of the house by her
hair (CB, Madhya 16.4–17). The acts of such devotees cannot be measured by a worldly yardstick. <!-- F100 I3 -->

Having seen the Lord home, Sarvabhauma repented at His feet of the offence done to Him in his house, and on returning told
his wife that he did not wish to see that blasphemer's face again, and had word sent to Shathi: “Tell Shathi to leave him:
he has fallen (*patita*)” (CC, Madhya 15.264). That same night Amogha fled, and in the morning he fell ill with cholera.
Sarvabhauma saw in this the direct fruit of the offence: “An offence against the Lord bears fruit at once” (15.268) — and
recalled the words of Shukadeva: “Offence against great souls destroys a man's life, fortune, fame and dharma, his higher
worlds and blessings — all that is good for him” (“Bhagavatam” 10.4.46; CC, Madhya 15.270). He and his wife fasted;
Gopinatha told the Lord of it. <!-- F101 F102 F127 -->

And the Lord hastened to Amogha. Placing His hand on his chest, He said that a brahmana's heart is the seat of Krishna and
no place for envy:

“Through Sarvabhauma's company your impurity has been destroyed, and when the taint is gone, a soul takes the name of
Krishna. Get up, Amogha, chant the name of Krishna, and the Lord will at once show you His mercy.” (CC, Madhya 15.276–277)

Amogha sprang up and in the frenzy of prema began to cry, “Krishna! Krishna!”; trembling, tears, thrill and sweat appeared
on his body. He fell at the Lord's feet, begging forgiveness, and slapped his own cheeks until they swelled, until Gopinatha
held him back. The Lord consoled him:

“Because you are related to Sarvabhauma, you are dear to Me. Even the servants, maids and dogs in Sarvabhauma's house are
dear to Me — to say nothing of others.” (CC, Madhya 15.283–284) <!-- F103 F104 -->

Then the Lord came to Sarvabhauma, embraced him and told him to bathe, go to Jagannatha and break his fast: “Amogha is a
child; he is your son. A father does not hold his child's faults against him, for he is the child's protector. He has
become a Vaishnava now, and his offence is gone; so show him your favour” (15.291–292). The Lord consoled the mother of Shathi
too. And Amogha from then on became the Lord's exclusive devotee and constantly chanted the names of Krishna (15.296). So,
says Krishnadasa Kaviraja, the Lord took His meal in Sarvabhauma's house, and in it Sarvabhauma's love became known
(15.299). <!-- F105 Q10 -->

## At the Lord's Side
From then on Sarvabhauma was beside the Lord in all the important events of His life in Puri. The Lord entrusted the two
brothers with the service of two of His manifestations:

“As the Daru-brahma, Brahman in the form of wood, He is Sri Purushottama Himself; and the Bhagirathi is the Jala-brahma,
Brahman in the form of water. Sarvabhauma, worship the Daru-brahma. Vachaspati, serve the Jala-brahma.” (CC, Madhya
15.135–136) <!-- F126 -->

At the feast in the garden near Gundicha the Lord seated Sarvabhauma at His left and asked again and again that the best
food be served to him, and Gopinatha, serving his brother-in-law, joked about his former life. Sarvabhauma replied: “I was
a logician of crooked intellect. It is by your grace that I have gained this wealth” (CC, Madhya 12.177–181). In the water
sports on the lake he and Ramananda “lost all their gravity and became like children” (14.82). On fixed days of the month
the Lord dined at his house or at Gadadhara Pandita's, and in their homes He ate as they wished: there He “had no
independence” (Antya 10.153; 8.88–89). When the Lord returned from Vrindavana, Sarvabhauma came out to meet Him and together
with Gadadhara brought maha-prasada for Him and His companions (Madhya 25.227–230); the Lord gave him His own prasada (Antya
16.106) and introduced Sanatana Gosvami to him (Antya 4.108–110). <!-- F106 F107 F131 F132 -->

He was present, too, at the examination of Rupa Gosvami's dramas, when the Lord came to Rupa with him, Ramananda and Svarupa
and they relished the rasa, poetic beauty and diction of his verses (CC, Antya 1.103–115); and in the
“Chaitanya-chandrodaya” (act 9) Karnapura puts a verse about Rupa into Sarvabhauma's mouth. Before him and Ramananda the
Lord glorified the holy qualities of Haridasa (Antya 11.50), and one of the biographies says that Sarvabhauma was present
also when the Lord consoled Sanatana, who wished to take his own life.[^16] He was among the devotees of Puri who met the
Lord on His return from Bengal (Madhya 16.254). <!-- F110 F112 F113 -->

The highest praise Sarvabhauma heard from the Lord's own lips, before Vallabha Bhatta:

“Sarvabhauma Bhattacharya knows the six systems of philosophy (*darshanas*). In these six systems he is the guru of the
world, and he is the best of bhagavatas. He showed Me the farthest limit of bhakti-yoga; by his grace I came to know that
the yoga of devotion to Krishna is the essence.” (CC, Antya 7.21–22)

Deep knowledge of the six philosophical systems together with the highest devotion — the rarest of combinations.
<!-- F111 Q12 -->

## “Stay until the Ratha-yatra”
The king, fearing that the Lord would leave Puri, called Sarvabhauma and Ramananda to him and begged them to keep Him there
by every means. When the Lord told them that He wished to go to Vrindavana, they asked Him to stay until the Ratha-yatra; in
Kartika they said it was too cold — and so, afraid of separation, they found one pretext after another to put off His
departure (CC, Madhya 16.4–10). At last, two years later, the Lord embraced them both and said: “I long very much to go to
Vrindavana, but because of your insistence I have not gone for two years” (16.88) — and they, understanding that it is not
good to press one's will upon the Lord, let Him go after the rainy season, on the day of Vijaya-dashami (16.92–94).
<!-- F108 -->

When the Lord was leaving Puri, Gadadhara Pandita, His friend from childhood, wished to go with Him; the Lord sent him back,
and when the boat put off, Gadadhara fell senseless. The Lord charged Sarvabhauma to take care of him, and Sarvabhauma
consoled him:

“Get up — such is the Lord's *lila*. You know how Krishna gave up His own promise: moved by mercy for His devotee, He kept
Bhishma's promise.” (CC, Madhya 16.143–144)

In the same way, Sarvabhauma said, the Lord had protected Gadadhara's vow, though it was not easy for Him either to part
from him; and the two of them, stricken with the grief of separation, returned to Nilachala (16.146–147). <!-- F109 -->

## The Journey to Kashi
Of one later event both the “Charitamrita” and Kavi Karnapura tell. Krishnadasa Kaviraja, in his brief summary of the
Lord's pastimes, mentions that the Gaudiya devotees on their way to Puri met Sarvabhauma, who was going to Kashi:

“On the way they all met Sarvabhauma Bhattacharya, who was going on to Kashi.” (CC, Madhya 1.141)

In Karnapura's drama (“Chaitanya-chandrodaya,” act 10) Sarvabhauma says that he is going to Varanasi without the Lord's
consent, to bring the scholars there to His teaching: “Though the Lord's mercy depends on His will, He Himself is subject
to mercy, so that at times mercy too acts on its own.” Meeting Advaita and the other Gaudiyas, he bows to them and talks
with them; Karnapura's mahakavya says the same (14.42–56). How this journey ended, and whether Sarvabhauma returned to Puri,
the sources do not say.[^17] <!-- F125 -->

## After the Lord's Disappearance
Sarvabhauma outlived the Lord's disappearance. When, soon after it, young Srinivasa came to Puri, he found the Lord's
companions in Nilachala — Ramananda, Sarvabhauma, Vakreshvara, Paramananda Puri, Shikhi Mahiti, Govinda, Shankara,
Gopinatha Acharya — sunk in separation from Gaura: they had forgotten food and sleep. The “Bhakti-ratnakara” tells:

“All long to see Srinivasa; Srinivasa went to the house of Sarvabhauma. Sri Raya Ramananda came there too; sitting
together, the two sing the virtues of Gaurachandra. <…> Both of them are drowning in an ocean of great grief, but seeing
Srinivasa, they felt joy in their hearts.” (“Bhakti-ratnakara”, 3.155–156, 158) <!-- F114 Q14 -->

When, where and how Sarvabhauma departed from this world is not known; nor is the day of his departure found in the
calendar of the Sri Chaitanya Saraswat Math. His grief in his last years was not worldly sorrow but the separation of an
eternal companion from his Lord.

## Remembrance
After him Sarvabhauma's house in Puri stood empty for a long time and fell into ruin; only the shalagrama Damodara whom he
had served remained. Later Haridasa Gosvami sent Shachi Devi, the future Gangamata Gosvamini, to live there and perform
her bhajana, and the place became the Gangamata Math; Sarvabhauma's shalagrama is worshipped there to this day. And in his
birthplace, Vidyanagara, Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati on 5 March 1936 opened the Sarvabhauma Gaudiya Math and installed
Deities there; during the parikrama of the islands of Navadvipa, on Gaura-chaturdashi, the pilgrims of the Sri Chaitanya
Saraswat Math pass through the Vidyanagar Sarvabhauma Gaudiya Math as well. <!-- F121 F122 -->

Tradition also links Sarvabhauma with the handing down of the teaching. It is said that the conclusions of Vedanta which
he heard from the Lord in Nilachala he passed on to his disciple Madhusudana Vachaspati, and he to Jiva Gosvami; Srila
Bhaktivinoda Thakura writes in the “Jaiva-dharma” (ch. 39) that the Lord entrusted the preaching of siddhanta to
Sarvabhauma, and he, through one of his disciples, passed this task on to Sri Jiva. According to one of the biographies,
Baladeva Vidyabhushana too learned from Radha-Damodara Gosvami, among other things, Gaurasundara's instructions to
Sarvabhauma. Sanatana Gosvami in the introduction to the “Vaishnava-toshani” (v. 5) glorifies his teachers — Sarvabhauma
Bhattacharya, Vidya-vachaspati and others. <!-- F115 F116 F117 -->

Narahari Sarakara in the “Bhajanamrita” (9–11) tells that in a dream Gaurachandra appeared to him holding Sarvabhauma by
the hand and told him to make known in waking life the conclusions he had seen. And Narahari Chakravarti in the
“Namamrita-samudra,” calling upon the Lord's companions, addresses him too:

“O Vasudeva Sarvabhauma Bhattacharya! Make me mad with the virtues of your Gauranga!” (“Namamrita-samudra” 11)

— and next his wife and daughter: “O mother of Shathi! O Shathi! I pray at your feet: may I not see, even in a dream,
those who have turned away from Sri Gaura” (12). <!-- F118 F135 -->

## Significance
Sarvabhauma is Brihaspati, the Lord's eternal companion “birth after birth,” who came with Him to become a living witness
of His mercy. The greatest of scholars, the teacher of Advaitin sannyasis, became the humble servant of a young sannyasi who
could not bear even the word “mukti” — and the Lord Himself called him the guru of the world, the best of bhagavatas, who
had shown Him the farthest limit of bhakti-yoga. <!-- F7 F133 F111 -->

One of the biographies says that the deliverance of Sarvabhauma, like that of Prakashananda Sarasvati, is a still greater
miracle of mercy than the deliverance of Jagai and Madhai. Mayavada is the chief obstacle on the path to love of Krishna:
by denying the eternity of the Lord's name, form, qualities and pastimes it bars the way to prema, and that is why all the
Vaishnava acharyas opposed the doctrine of *vivarta-vada*. The stronger the fortress, the brighter the mercy that melted
it. <!-- F119 I4 -->

His story also teaches how this mercy comes. Even the greatest learning without it fails to recognise either the Lord or
His pure devotee; and it comes through a devotee: Sarvabhauma himself acknowledged that he received it only through his
kinship with Gopinatha Acharya, who convinced the foremost scholar of eastern India that Sri Chaitanya is Krishna Himself.
And through Sarvabhauma in his turn King Prataparudra and many nobles of Odisha came to the Lord. <!-- F120 I2 I6 -->

The biographies see in his transformation — from a proud Advaitin scholar to a fervent Vaishnava and great saint — an
example for scholars to come: by becoming, like him, devotees of Mahaprabhu, they will attain the highest good. <!-- I1 -->
