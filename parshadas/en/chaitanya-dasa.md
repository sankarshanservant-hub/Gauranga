# Bhatta Chaitanya Dasa (Gangadhara Bhattacharya)
*Бхатта Чайтанья Дас (Гангадхара Бхаттачарья) · @chaitanya-dasa*

## A Brahmana of Chakhandi
On the bank of the Ganga, not far from Katwa, lies the village of Chakhandi (also called Chakhundi). There lived a
brahmana named Gangadhara Bhattacharya, whom, after a single day of his life, the whole district came to call by another
name: Sri Chaitanya Dasa, “the servant of Chaitanya.” By this name he is known to the “Bhakti-ratnakara” and the
“Narottama-vilasa”; one of the biographies calls him Sri Bhatta Chaitanya Dasa, while the “Prema-vilasa” calls him
Chaitanya Dasa Acharya, simply Acharya, and Sri Chaitanya Acharya. In its first wave the “Bhakti-ratnakara” calls him simply
“the brahmana Chaitanya,” in whose house Srinivasa Acharya was born: Chaitanya Dasa is Srinivasa's father. That book begins
its account of him thus:

“On the bank of the Bhagirathi is the village of Sri Chakhandi; there lives a brahmana named Sri Chaitanya Dasa.
Formerly he was called Gangadhara Bhattacharya; listen to how he received this name.” (“Bhakti-ratnakara” 2.21–22)
<!-- F1 -->

Gangadhara was a Vaishnava brahmana of high family; one of the biographies specifies that he was a Rarhi brahmana. His
village stands on the Ganga near Kantaka-nagara, that is, Katwa; today one reaches it from Daihat station on the
Bardhaman–Katwa railway.[^1] <!-- F2 F3 -->

His wife was Lakshmipriya Devi, daughter of the brahmana Balarama Vipra of Yajigrama, near Srikhanda; the
“Bhakti-ratnakara” calls his father-in-law “very pure in conduct,” while the “Prema-vilasa” calls him Balarama Dasa. The
couple lived in poverty and long remained childless: according to one of the biographies, Gangadhara was already about
fifty years old and still had neither a son nor any other child.[^2] Yet this did not darken his life: he lived joyfully,
absorbed in loving service to the Lord, Sri Hari. The sources give no years of his life, and the Vaishnava calendar of
the Sri Chaitanya Saraswat Math has neither a day of his appearance nor a day of his departure. <!-- F4 F5 -->

## Youth in Navadvipa
Of Gangadhara's youth we know from his own lips: years later he told his son about it, and the “Bhakti-ratnakara” has
preserved these accounts. When Gaura was still playing as a child in Navadvipa, Gangadhara was studying logic and other
sciences there:

“Not understanding the essence of bhakti, we studied logic and the rest and always kept company with those turned away
[from God].” (“Bhakti-ratnakara” 2.231)

News of the Lord's wondrous pastimes reached him, and more than once he meant to go and see them for himself, but he was
held back by the company of fellow students intoxicated with their learning: at the mere mention of the Lord they
laughed. Their arrogance pained him, and he prayed to the Creator without cease to crush their pride soon. <!-- F6 -->

The prayer was answered. An invincible digvijayi came to Navadvipa, and Nimai Pandita defeated him; hearing of it,
Gangadhara's teacher and the other scholars ceased to regard Nimai as a human being. Later Gangadhara would tell his son
also of the deliverance of Jagai and Madhai and the subduing of the Kazi — as a man of that time, for whom all this was
lived experience: “With the deliverance of those two we were all freed from fear” (“Bhakti-ratnakara” 2.294). <!-- F7 -->

His affairs sometimes took him to Yajigrama and to Kantaka-nagara. In Kantaka-nagara lived the sannyasi Keshava Bharati,
who was very kind to him; Gangadhara used to visit him and at times stayed with him. <!-- F9 -->

Once his teacher, the foremost scholar of Chakhandi, was invited to Ramakeli, and his students went with him. There Rupa
and Sanatana received the teachers of Navadvipa and other places with great honour. Gangadhara saw both brothers with
his own eyes: ministers of the king of Gauda, seated in the assembly of pandits “like Indra,” they were humble, without a
trace of pride, themselves great masters of the shastras, and begged of everyone only bhakti to Krishna's feet. “When I
saw the two of them, at that time I did not have such understanding” (“Bhakti-ratnakara” 2.362), he admits to his son:
who they were, he understood only later. They returned home from Ramakeli a few days before the Lord's sannyasa.
<!-- F8 -->

## “Chaitanya! Chaitanya!”
At last Gangadhara resolved to go to Navadvipa and see Gaura. On the way he came to Kantaka-nagara — and saw people
running from every side toward Bharati's house. The news had spread like a forest fire: Gaurasundara, the Lord whom His devotees
worship, had come to Katwa to accept sannyasa from Keshava Bharati. People hurried to see Him and be
purified, and Gangadhara found himself among them. <!-- F10 -->

Everyone marvelled at the Lord's beauty and, learning that His hair was now to be shaved, was overcome with grief; this
sorrow wounded Gangadhara deeply. The barber in his anguish could not bring himself for a long time to take up the
razor, and kept falling at Mahaprabhu's feet; and when he placed his hand on the Lord's head, he burst into tears.
Everyone wept, a wail rose over the crowd, the ground turned to mud with tears, and people struck their foreheads and
reproached the Creator. One of the biographies adds that the barber Madhu could not see for his tears and set to work
only at Mahaprabhu's word, then cried out, “What have I done!” and fell senseless; and even the birds fell silent.
<!-- F11 -->

Gangadhara fainted so deeply that people despaired of his life; the “Bhakti-ratnakara” says that he survived only by
the Lord's will. Long afterwards he came to himself — and could not stop weeping. <!-- F12 -->

Then he heard that Keshava Bharati had given the Lord the name Sri Krishna Chaitanya:

“The Lord received the name Sri Krishna Chaitanya, and the name ‘Sri Chaitanya’ entered the brahmana's ears. The
brahmana repeats the name of Sri Chaitanya again and again, and tears flow from his eyes without cease. He cannot stay in
Kantaka-nagara and, like a madman, walks along the bank of the Ganga. He calls out constantly, ‘Chaitanya! Chaitanya!’ —
and neither bathing nor eating nor anything else appeals to him.” (“Bhakti-ratnakara” 2.40–43)

Forgetting bathing and food and saying nothing but this name, he walked along the Ganga until he was brought to his home
village; and there this state of absorption (*avishta-bhava*) lasted several days more. Some biographies give his cry as
“Ha Chaitanya! Ha Chaitanya!”, others say that he repeated softly, to himself, “Sri Krishna Chaitanya.” This was neither
derangement nor an illness of grief. The Lord's name alone, entering his ears, awakened prema in him, and he became, as
the biographies say, mad with love, intoxicated by it — swimming in a Ganga of his own tears. <!-- F13 -->

In Chakhandi he was met with amazement. One brahmana, looking at him from afar, told another that Gangadhara had lost
his mind; the other explained what had happened at Katwa and did not know how he might be helped. But someone among them
spoke a word that proved prophetic:

“‘He is Chaitanya's servant; Chaitanya will make him well, so I think.’ Saying such things, many [people] call the
brahmana Gangadhara ‘Sri Chaitanya Dasa’ again and again. Hearing himself called ‘Sri Chaitanya Dasa,’ he responds, and
his heart rejoices without cease. No one any longer uses Gangadhara's former name: everyone calls him ‘Sri Chaitanya
Dasa.’” (“Bhakti-ratnakara” 2.56–59)

The villagers saw in him a sick man in need of a “cure”; but the word proved truer than its speaker knew: Gangadhara
had indeed become Chaitanya's servant, and only the Lord Himself could heal — that is, satisfy — his longing. The
discerning, according to one of the biographies, understood just this: he was “mad for Chaitanya.” He rejoiced in his new
name, and it stayed with him all his life.[^3] <!-- F14 -->

The Lord's sannyasa brought grief to Gangadhara's home as well. One of the biographies says that his faithful wife, on
hearing of it, was bathed in tears, and all the brahmanas of the village and their wives spent their days in bitter
weeping. <!-- F15 -->

After some time Chaitanya Dasa came to himself and resumed his life in the village. All this the author of the
“Bhakti-ratnakara” heard from a very old brahmana of Chakhandi. <!-- F16 -->

From then on Chaitanya Dasa and Lakshmipriya became great devotees of Sri Chaitanya Mahaprabhu. “Extraordinary is the
bhakti of Chaitanya Dasa, and so too is his chaste wife Lakshmipriya” (“Bhakti-ratnakara” 2.62). Thus a single word —
the name “Chaitanya” — changed his whole life and even his name: Gangadhara became Chaitanya Dasa, the servant of
Chaitanya. <!-- F17 I1 -->

## A Desire Born of the Lord's Will
For many years the couple lived without children and complained of nothing. And then, as the “Bhakti-ratnakara” tells,
something unexpected happened:

“They were childless, yet they had no desires at all; by the Lord's will the desire for a son arose. The brahmana Sri
Chaitanya Dasa says to his wife: ‘Why has the desire for a son suddenly arisen? My heart is troubled on account of a son;
think it over and tell me how I may find peace.’ Lakshmipriya says: ‘Let us go quickly to Nilachala: by the darshan of
the Lord everything will be fulfilled.’” (“Bhakti-ratnakara” 2.63–66)

This desire was not a worldly one. In a man indifferent to the world it arose “by the Lord's will,” and he himself did
not understand where it came from. The Lord Himself placed it in His servant's heart: through Chaitanya Dasa's son He
wished to give the world the books of Rupa and the other Gosvamis.[^4] <!-- F18 I2 -->

Lakshmipriya advised her husband to go quickly to Puri, to Nilachala: Mahaprabhu was there, and His darshan would bring
complete fulfilment. Without delay, at an auspicious moment, the couple set out. <!-- F19 -->

Their road led through Yajigrama. For two to four days they stayed with Balarama Vipra, and as he saw his daughter and
son-in-law off, he humbly asked them to bow at the Lord's feet on his behalf. Further on they travelled with fellow
pilgrims, of whom many were going to Nilachala in those days; the journey was long. <!-- F20 -->

One night husband and wife wept bitterly together:

“‘We have wasted such a human birth, we never once remembered the Lord's lotus feet! Will we have the good fortune to
see our fill of the sweetness of Sri Krishna Chaitanya and Jagannatha?’” (“Bhakti-ratnakara” 2.74–75)

This is the humility of a devotee speaking, not the memory of a godless life: even before, as we have seen, he had
served Sri Hari with love. A true Vaishnava always feels that his service has not yet even begun. <!-- F21 -->

That night a revelation came to Chaitanya Dasa in a dream:

“… youthful, in the form of beautiful Shyama, in the threefold-bending pose — the king of millions of Kandarpas; a
peacock feather on His head, clad in yellow garments, the beauty of His face surpassing millions of moons; His body
adorned with ornaments, anointed with sandal, He plays the flute, enchanting the whole world. Having seen Him thus, he
sees Him again golden: He shines, surpassing pure gold, wearing cloth the colour of a cloud with a red border, and all
else as before — a treasury of rasa. And again he sees the golden form in another attire: with staff and water-pot, His
head shaven. And again he sees Him in a captivating dark form, with most beautiful eyes like lotus petals, together with
Balabhadra and Subhadra; Brahma and others with joyful hearts offer praise.” (“Bhakti-ratnakara” 2.77–84)

Shyamasundara with His flute; Gaura shining like gold in a householder's dress; the same Gaura as a sannyasi with staff
and wooden water-pot; and finally Sri Jagannatha with lotus eyes, beside Balarama and Subhadra. This was no play of the
imagination: the Lord revealed to His servant that all these forms are one Person, Krishna Himself, who has come as
Gaura and abides in Nilachala as Jagannatha. <!-- F22 -->

Chaitanya Dasa woke at once, deeply stirred. His pious wife comforted him in every way, and, heartened, they went on
their way in the morning with light hearts. <!-- F23 -->

## At the Lion Gate
A few days later they reached Nilachala, longing to see Mahaprabhu. And as they approached the Lion Gate (Simha-dvara)
of Jagannatha's temple in this mood, the all-knowing Lord, knowing their hearts beforehand, appeared there with His
associates at that very time: “The all-knowing Lord, by that very road at the Lion Gate, comes with His dear associates”
(“Bhakti-ratnakara” 2.88). <!-- F24 -->

Husband and wife gazed at His wondrous beauty and awe-inspiring radiance and could not take their eyes away. The
“Bhakti-ratnakara” describes in detail the Lord's form as the couple saw it — the golden body, the moon-like face, the
eyes reaching to the ears, the garland of tulasi, the cloth the colour of dawn — and says that wherever their gaze fell,
there it remained, while tears of joy flowed from their eyes. Weeping with happiness, they fell down before the Lord.
<!-- F25 -->

Before they could speak, the Lord Himself answered their unspoken prayer:

“Prabhu Sri Krishna Chaitanya, glancing from the corner of His eye, rained the nectar of mercy on the fortunate
brahmana. In sweet words He speaks to the brahmana, comforting him: ‘Jagannatha has gladly brought you here. Go, go,
have the darshan of Jagannatha: Sri Padmalochana will fulfil your desire.’” (“Bhakti-ratnakara” 2.102–104)[^5]
<!-- F26 -->

Chaitanya Dasa fell to the ground and lay a long while in obeisance. “Body, mind and life he gave to the Lord's feet, and
the all-knowing Lord accepted the brahmana as His own” (“Bhakti-ratnakara” 2.106). <!-- F27 -->

The Lord gave them His servant Govinda as their guide — “This brahmana is simple-hearted: arrange for him the darshan of
Jagannatha without hindrance” (“Bhakti-ratnakara” 2.107) — and Himself went to Jagannatha with His associates, while the
brahmana and his wife went along with His companions. Chaitanya Dasa humbly bowed to each of them, and all, seeing his
meekness, took him as one of their own. <!-- F28 -->

In the temple there was revealed to them what he had prayed for on the road:

“Seeing both in one place — the moving and the unmoving Brahman — the brahmana felt joy without end.”
(“Bhakti-ratnakara” 2.112)

The moving Brahman is Mahaprabhu; the unmoving one is Jagannatha. What is meant here is not the impersonal Brahman but
Para-brahman, the Supreme Person: Krishna Himself stood before them in two manifestations — Gaura who walks and
Jagannatha who stands still. Offering body, mind and life at the feet of both Lords, the couple wept tears of prema and
silently, within themselves, offered prayers and hymns from the scriptures. <!-- F29 -->

The Lord entrusted the brahmana to Jagannatha's feet and gave him to understand by a hint that he should return to
Gauda. Then He went back to His own quarters in the house of Kashi Mishra, and Chaitanya Dasa to the lodging assigned to
him; for several days the couple lived happily in Puri. <!-- F30 -->

## The Secret Revealed to Govinda
The devotees wondered what the brahmana had prayed for and what desire the Lord had promised to fulfil. The next day
they asked Govinda about it. “There is a secret here; by the Lord's will it will surely be revealed,” Govinda answered.
And at that very moment the Lord, who as the Paramatma knows every heart, called him:

“At that very time the Lord called Govinda and, absorbed in bhava, speaks in a deep voice: ‘This brahmana came desiring
a son; a son named Srinivasa will be born to him. Through Sri Rupa and others I shall reveal the shastras of bhakti, and
through Srinivasa I shall distribute the jewels of the books. Srinivasa is the embodiment of My pure prema; seeing him,
all will rejoice at heart. Let the brahmana go quickly to Gauda.’ Having said many such things, He restrained His
bhava.” (“Bhakti-ratnakara” 2.120–124)

So even before Srinivasa's birth both his name and his service were decided: through him the books that Sri Rupa and the
other Gosvamis were to compose would go out into the world.[^6] <!-- F31 F32 Q1 -->

That same night Jagannatha Himself appeared to Chaitanya Dasa in a dream:

“‘Do not delay, brahmana, go to Gauda. A son full of prema will be born to you; in a short time he will be victorious in
all the shastras.’ Having seen such a dream, the brahmana thinks to himself: ‘How can I leave this happiness and go? The
son of the king of Vraja, Gaurachandra, Jagannatha, has accepted such a wretch as me as His own.’”
(“Bhakti-ratnakara” 2.125–128)

For him Nanda's son, Gaurachandra and Jagannatha are one Lord. And he calls himself a wretch only out of humility: so a
devotee, having received mercy, sees himself unworthy of it. The news gladdened husband and wife — the Lord had
fulfilled their desire and accepted them as His servants; but the thought of leaving Sri Chaitanya and Jagannatha and
going away from Puri saddened them. <!-- F33 -->

Govinda carefully brought the brahmana to the Lord, and Mahaprabhu, smiling, gave him a parting instruction:

“‘Jagannatha is pleased with you; soon your desire will be fulfilled. Go quickly to Gauda and perform the sankirtana of
the holy name without cease.’” (“Bhakti-ratnakara” 2.132–133) <!-- F34 -->

How the Lord and His servant parted, the “Bhakti-ratnakara” does not venture to describe. Chaitanya Dasa bowed at the
feet of the Lord's associates, and the devotees' hearts were stirred; at the Lion Gate he bowed to Patita-pavana, the
“Purifier of the fallen,” and with a heavy heart, weeping, set out with his wife on the way home. <!-- F35 -->

Having received Mahaprabhu's blessing, Chaitanya Dasa returned to Gauda in great joy. Again they went through Yajigrama;
there he told Balarama everything, and Balarama accompanied his daughter and son-in-law all the way to Chakhandi and
stayed with them five to seven days. <!-- F36 -->

## Nama-sankirtana in Chakhandi
On their return the couple carried out the command of the two Lords — Mahaprabhu and Jagannatha. The
“Bhakti-ratnakara” says:

“What unrestrained prema Sri Chaitanya Dasa has! He is always enraptured with the rasa of talk of Krishna. Having given
his mind to the feet of Sri Gaurachandra, in solitude he performs nama-sankirtana daily.” (“Bhakti-ratnakara” 2.146–147)

From Chaitanya Dasa's home the sankirtana spread to his neighbours, and soon the chanting of the holy name became the
custom both in his village and in the villages around. Thus a poor and childless brahmana, at whom the villagers had
laughed, by carrying out the Lord's word became the cause of nama-sankirtana for a whole district. <!-- F37 I3 -->

Not all the villagers understood him at once. Some rejoiced at his devotion; others said, “All this is useless — that is
why he is poor and childless.” Husband and wife answered no one and only smiled to themselves. But when Lakshmipriya
conceived, even those who had shunned the childless couple began to praise their good fortune and brought fitting
gifts. And Balarama Vipra, versed, according to one of the biographies, in jyotisha, understood that a mahapurusha would
be born to his daughter. <!-- F38 -->

In due time, “on the full-moon day of Vaishakha, under the nakshatra Rohini” (“Bhakti-ratnakara” 2.156), a son was born
in Chakhandi, to the joy of all the villagers.[^7] At the hour of his birth Chaitanya Dasa saw various mysteries in a
dream. <!-- F39 Q3 -->

In great joy father and mother offered the newborn at the feet of Gaurachandra. At the birth celebration brahmanas and
their wives were generously given gifts, and when Lakshmipriya took her son on her lap, everyone began to sing the names
of Gaura; with this kirtana the boy grew up. <!-- F40 -->

A few days later Chaitanya Dasa held the ceremony of name-giving and first feeding with rice and, remembering
Gaurasundara's words, named his son Srinivasa. “Everyone says: ‘His name is Srinivasa’; they do not know that this name
was proclaimed beforehand” (“Bhakti-ratnakara” 2.168). <!-- F41 -->

## Father of a Future Acharya
In this family Srinivasa received all that made him an acharya. From his earliest years his mother taught him the names
of the Lord and His associates — “Come, my son, say: ‘Gaura Vishvambhara… Sri Krishna Chaitanya, Nityananda-Haladhara’”
(“Bhakti-ratnakara” 2.176–177) — and from her he learned devotion to Chaitanya, Nityananda, Advaita and Their associates.
From his father he heard stories of Mahaprabhu and His devotees, of the life of Rupa and Sanatana and their wondrous love
for Gaura-Krishna, of the Vrindavana pastimes of Radha and Krishna; often father and son were lost in ecstasy together,
and the boy came to love sankirtana about Gauranga. <!-- F42 Q2 I4 -->

Once the son asked his father to tell him about Gauranga. Chaitanya Dasa replied:

“‘He whose end Brahma and the others do not find — how shall I, a wretched soul, tell of His pastimes? Listen, listen,
Srinivasa, I shall tell you: Vishvambhara-raya is Krishna, the Moon of Vrindavana.’” (“Bhakti-ratnakara” 2.228–229)
<!-- F46 -->

And he told him — of the Lord's childhood pastimes in Navadvipa and of his own student years, of the digvijayi and of
Gaya, of Jagai and Madhai, of the Kazi. But when he came to what he had seen at Katwa — how the hair vanished from the
Lord's head — he fainted, and tears flowed from him without cease; and when he came to himself, he sighed and repeated,
“The beautiful curling hair!” Then he told how the Lord plays in Nilachala, of Nityananda, Advaita and the Lord's
associates, of the Vraja-lila and of why the Lord descended in Navadvipa; of Rupa and Sanatana, whom he had seen in
Ramakeli; of Vrindavana, Govinda-deva and Gopinatha. Srinivasa listened, unable to stay on his feet, and day after day
asked to hear more. <!-- F43 -->

One day Chaitanya Dasa told his son that he was now able to look after his mother, and confessed that he was thinking
of leaving them both in Yajigrama and going soon to Vrindavana. At the end of a long account of Vraja he exclaimed:

“‘O Srinivasa, how my soul longs! Will there be such a day when I go to Vrindavana?’ Saying this, Sri Chaitanya Dasa
sheds streams of tears and cannot stop them. Holding his father's feet, Srinivasa weeps… Father and son grew calm only
after some time; who knows how wondrous the might of prema is! Apart from talk of Krishna nothing pleases them; such a
father and son have no comparison.” (“Bhakti-ratnakara” 2.485–489) <!-- F44 -->

That Chaitanya Dasa and Lakshmipriya really were with Mahaprabhu in Puri is attested also by the “Narottama-vilasa”:

“In the same way, in Nilachala, Prabhu, the enchanter of the worlds, wept, repeating the name ‘Srinivasa.’ In whose
house Srinivasa would appear, that Mahaprabhu revealed to the world. His father Sri Chaitanya Dasa and his mother
Lakshmipriya both went to Nilachala and saw Prabhu, and then by Prabhu's order the two returned to Gauda; and I with
great joy saw them both there.” (“Narottama-vilasa” 1.157–160)

The author of the “Narottama-vilasa” lived in the eighteenth century; “I saw” is presumably the word of an eyewitness
whose account he passes on. <!-- F45 -->

## The Account of the “Prema-vilasa”
The “Prema-vilasa” tells of the gift of a son differently and with other details. Its account must not be merged with
that of the “Bhakti-ratnakara”: these are two separate traditions, and we take the “Bhakti-ratnakara” as the main one,
giving the account of the “Prema-vilasa” separately.[^8]

According to the “Prema-vilasa,” Jagannatha in a dream tells Mahaprabhu of a childless brahmana who has long prayed to
Him for a son — Chaitanya Dasa Acharya, husband of Lakshmipriya, Balarama's daughter; and Jagannatha has granted him a
son, a vessel of prema. The Lord asks Kashi Mishra to find “Chaitanya Dasa the Gaudiya,” but he has already gone home in
tears. <!-- F47 -->

Then the Lord sends the Earth, the goddess Prithivi, to find him: for the sake of a son, He says, this brahmana has
prayed before Jagannatha for a whole year. The Earth finds him in Chakhandi: he is very pure in conduct, in his body there
is not a trace of sin, and he has begun a purashcharana for the sake of a son. At the Lord's word the Earth gives all
prema to Lakshmipriya. And the Lord, in kirtana before Jagannatha, weeps and calls out, “Srinivasa!” — and writes to
Nityananda that in Chakhandi, in the house of the brahmana Chaitanya Dasa, Srinivasa will be born. Here too the prayers
for a son are a petition to the Lord for what He Himself desired. <!-- F48 -->

At home, on the bank of the Ganga, Chaitanya Dasa performs seven purashcharanas for the sake of a son. In a dream the
Golden Lord announces to him the birth of Srinivasa. Lakshmipriya, touching the Lord's hand in a dream, is filled with
prema and asks her husband to go nowhere any more but to perform nama-sankirtana at home. “How shall we live? There is no
wealth in the house,” he replies — and this is not lack of faith but the simple concern of a poor brahmana householder;
Lakshmipriya answers that she has gained great wealth — prema. <!-- F49 -->

With Srinivasa's conception the persecutions ceased. Durgadasa Raya, the zamindar of Chakhandi, ordered it proclaimed by
drum that Shiva and Durga be glorified, but the drum sounded “Radha-Krishna!” The dancing people came to Chaitanya
Dasa's house; he received the zamindar with honour and fed him. At night Durgadasa heard kirtana and dreamed of two
golden children dancing in the courtyard; Chaitanya Dasa calmed him, and Durgadasa resolved to accept the mantra of
Radha and Krishna. <!-- F50 -->

After ten months and ten days, on the full moon of Vaishakha, the son was born; the father could not look at him
enough. Durgadasa came with musicians, gifts were brought, and the second chapter of the “Prema-vilasa” opens with the
words, “Glory to Sri Chaitanya Acharya!” For six months the father did not leave the house; then came the first feeding
with rice on a day fixed by the astrologer, the first tonsure, the sacred thread. Once the boy went missing, and his
father, after searching everywhere, found him asleep with a book. <!-- F51 -->

When young Srinivasa met Narahari Sarakara and came home in tears, weeping arose in the house of the “Acharya”; the
father questioned his weeping son, and only when he grew calm were the parents glad. <!-- F52 -->

## Departure from This World
His dream of going to Vrindavana was not fulfilled: Chaitanya Dasa left this world while Srinivasa was still young. The
“Bhakti-ratnakara” says briefly:

“After some time his father left this world; looking at her son's face, his mother forgot her grief.”
(“Bhakti-ratnakara” 3.18)

According to the “Prema-vilasa,” he was suddenly seized by fever and in seven days “attained the Ganga” — as one has
long said of a departure on the bank of the sacred river. Srinivasa performed the rites on the bank of the Ganga and
consigned his father's bones to its waters, and a voice from the sky consoled Lakshmipriya. The son was inconsolable and
fasted for some time; the devotees comforted both him and his mother. The sources give neither the year nor the day of
the departure. <!-- F53 -->

Then Srinivasa and his mother moved from Chakhandi to Yajigrama, to his grandfather's house.[^9] <!-- F54 -->

The “Nityananda-vamsha-vistara” records how Srinivasa, asked about himself in Tailanga, replied: “I am the son of
Chaitanya Dasa; my home is in Jajigrama.” <!-- F55 -->

## The Servant of Chaitanya
Sri Chaitanya Dasa is a wholly devoted servant of Sri Chaitanyadeva, worthy to be counted among the foremost of His
devotees. Twice he saw the Lord: at Katwa, on the day the Lord accepted sannyasa and the name that made Gangadhara
Chaitanya Dasa, and in Puri, at the Lion Gate, where the Lord accepted him as His own. <!-- F56 -->

But the heart of his life is the home in which Srinivasa appeared, the vessel of prema foretold by the Lord Himself. The
desire for a son came to him by the Lord's will, and the Lord fulfilled it, for through this son He wished to give the
world the books of Sri Rupa and the other Gosvamis. The “Prema-vilasa” adds that Srinivasa, while still in his mother's
womb, made all of Chakhandi chant the names of Radha and Krishna. And Chaitanya Dasa himself, having received from the
Lord the command to sing the holy name without cease, carried it out so that nama-sankirtana became the life of the
whole district. <!-- F57 I2 I3 -->

    Agradvipa and Daihat lie on opposite sides of the Ganga between Navadvipa and Katwa; the reference to Nadia probably
    follows an older administrative division or is mistaken.
[^2]: The age of “about fifty” is given by only one biography, with no reference to a primary source; the years of

[^3]: The biographies differ as to who gave him this name: the people of the surrounding villages, discerning people of
    Chakhandi, the devotees, “the people of Nadia.” We follow the “Bhakti-ratnakara” (2.56–59): it was his fellow
    villagers who called him so.
[^4]: According to some biographies, Chaitanya Dasa went to Nilachala only to see the Lord's feet, and the Lord Himself

    while still at home, and his wife advised him to go to Mahaprabhu for a blessing.
[^5]: According to one of the biographies, these words were spoken by “someone, as if in a clear voice”; according to

[^7]: The year of Srinivasa's birth, 1530, is given by only one biography.

[^9]: In a late chapter of the “Prema-vilasa” (16.295) Srinivasa, meeting Jahnava, says that his mother and father live
