# Narottama Dasa Thakura
*Нароттама Дас Тхакур · @narottama-dasa*

## Thakura Mahashaya
Narottama Dasa Thakura is a companion of Sri Chaitanya Mahaprabhu, although he came into this world only after the Lord
had withdrawn from sight. He is called Narottama Dasa, Srila Narottama Dasa Thakura, Paramahamsa Sri Narottama Dasa
Thakura; but in Bengal he was known above all as Thakura Mahashaya, or simply Sri Thakura, so that his name hardly needed
to be spoken. The title was given to him in Vrindavana by his teacher Sri Jiva Gosvami, in the same days in which he named
Srinivasa “Acharya” and Dukhi Krishnadasa “Shyamananda.”[^1] The “Bhakti-ratnakara” says that Jiva, delighted at how
quickly his pupil mastered many shastras, “taking everyone's consent,” named him “Sri Mahashaya” (4.422–424).
<!-- F1 F2 F115 -->

The “Bhakti-ratnakara” quotes an old verse about him:

“A brahmachari from boyhood, who has seen all the tirthas, the best of the highest bhagavatas — Srila Narottama Dasa.”
(“Bhakti-ratnakara” 1.279)

So he remained: all his life he kept celibacy, visited the holy places of Vraja, Gauda and Orissa, and became for the
Vaishnavas a pattern of pure devotion. <!-- F4 Q1 -->

Together with Srinivasa Acharya and Shyamananda he is one of the three great preachers who, in the generation after the
Lord, carried the prema-dharma through nama-sankirtana, along the path shown by Sri Chaitanya Mahaprabhu Himself. When the
Lord's associates in Vrindavana withdrew one after another, these three preserved their heritage and widened the movement
in Bengal; they are called the threefold power of the Lord's mercy, and of them it is said that they are eternal
associates of Gaurasundara who came into the world to carry His message. <!-- F5 F6 F110 -->

## Called Before His Birth
Mahaprabhu called Narottama long before he was born. At Ramakeli, in kirtana, the Lord turned His face towards Kheturi —
Narottama's future birthplace — and began to call him; His companions did not then understand whom He was calling. That
is why the “Bhakti-ratnakara” says that Narottama “was born drawn by Krishna Chaitanya” (1.280). <!-- F7 -->

The “Prema-vilasa” tells of this at greater length. On the way back from Kanai-Natshala the Lord, in ecstatic kirtana,
began to call out, “Narottama!” Nityananda asked whom He meant, and Mahaprabhu answered: “Your own glory is hidden from you.
In Puri you shed tears of love day after day — this prema I gathered and kept. Now I shall leave it in the keeping of the
Padmavati, for Narottama.” Then the Lord came to Kutubpur on the bank of the Padmavati, bathed, sang and danced in ecstasy,
and told the river to keep His prema and give it to Narottama when he came to bathe; he would be easy to recognise — as
soon as he entered the water, the river would overflow its banks. The place was later named Prematali, “the place of
prema.”[^2] <!-- F8 F9 F10 -->

## The Prince of Kheturi
Narottama took birth at Kheturi in North Bengal, twelve miles from Rampur-Boalia, in the subdivision of Gopalpur in the
district of Rajshahi (now in Bangladesh). His father, Raja Krishnananda Datta, held the zamindari of the Gopalpur region on
the bank of the Padmavati and a very wealthy man, so that his son was spoken of as a prince. His mother was named Narayani
Devi. The “Bhakti-ratnakara” says of her: “His mother, most virtuous, was named Narayani; her life was in her son” (1.289).
His father's brother, Purushottama Datta, shared with him a wealth and fame that had no equal. Purushottama's son
Santosha, Narottama's cousin, later became the raja of Kheturi and his disciple; the “Bhakti-ratnakara” says of him: “He is
Sriman Narottama's brother and disciple, pure in serving the guru, Krishna and the Vaishnavas” (1.471).[^3] Narahari
Sarakara Thakura of Srikhanda was very fond of Krishnananda. <!-- F12 F13 F14 F15 F112 -->

The family belonged to the kayastha community. One of the biographies explains that Krishna let His associate be born in
such a family to make it plain that His associates may appear in any station of life. Later the smartas would call Narottama a
“shudra”; how his own life answered them, and how Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati judges the matter, will be told below.
<!-- F16 I3 -->

His appearance was on the full moon of the month of Magha — according to one of the biographies, around the middle of the
fifteenth century of the Shaka era:

“Narottama was born on the full-moon day of the month of Magha; day by day he grew like the moon.” (“Bhakti-ratnakara”
1.281)

The “Narottama-vilasa” also names the Magha purnima and specifies the hour — the sixth danda. According to our calendar
(Sri Chaitanya Saraswat Math), the Appearance Day of Srila Narottama Dasa Thakura is the purnima at the end of the month of
Madhava (≈ Magha); in 2026 it fell on 1 February.[^4] <!-- F17 Q2 -->

At his birth there were auspicious signs. In his joy the raja gave generous gifts to the brahmanas, and they foretold that
the boy was a great soul through whom many would be delivered; the “Prema-vilasa” also tells of Narayani's prophetic dream
and of the astrologer's prediction. Day by day the child grew as the moon grows: his body had the lustre of melted gold, his eyes the shape of lotus petals, his arms reached his knees, his navel was deep — all marks of a mahapurusha — and the townspeople
gathered to look at the beautiful holy child. The rite of first feeding, the anna-prashana, was performed with lavish
gifts; the “Narottama-vilasa” adds that from then on the boy ate only the prasada of Vishnu. His mother, floating in bliss,
would not let him out of her sight and cared for him unceasingly; in the women's quarters he was petted with great joy,
and he himself was so quiet that wherever he was laid, there he lay. <!-- F18 F19 F20 F21 -->

When the time came for study, his intelligence amazed everyone: he remembered a thing after hearing it once, and before long Sanskrit grammar, rhetoric, poetry and many shastras were his. But soon he came to regard as useless any
knowledge that does not lead to Hari-bhajana, and he thought of the sages of old who went to the forest to serve Hari.
From early childhood he was drawn to bhakti and indifferent to games and pleasures. Hearing from devotees of the glory of
Nityananda and Gauranga, he seemed to come alive anew: day and night he repeated Their names and meditated on Their
qualities, and in dreams the Lord would often come to him with His associates. The “Narottama-vilasa” says that a brahmana
named Krishnadasa told him about the Lord, and that in a dream Gaura, Nityananda, Advaita and Their associates told him to
go to Vrindavana, to Lokanatha. <!-- F22 F23 -->

The “Bhakti-ratnakara” says:

“To Sri Krishna Chaitanya, Nityananda, Advaita and Their associates he offers his prayers, and tears stream from both his
eyes. In the guise of a dream the Lord with His associates appeared to him, comforted His beloved Narottama and calmed
him.” (“Bhakti-ratnakara” 1.285–286) <!-- F24 Q3 -->

## Prema from the Hands of the Padmavati
When Narottama was twelve years old, Nityananda, in a dream, bade him bathe in the Padmavati and receive the prema kept
for him. The next morning, as soon as he stepped into the water, the river overflowed its banks, and the Padmavati,
remembering the Lord's words, gave him what had been entrusted to her. This wonder is not the power of a river: the
Padmavati only returned what the Lord had given into her keeping — the mercy of Mahaprabhu and Nityananda, Nityananda's tears of
love. Having bathed at Prematali, Narottama was at once filled with prema and resolved to leave his family and go to
Vrindavana. <!-- F11 -->

## Leaving Home
From that time he was completely changed — in temper, in looks, in everything. His parents tried by every means to bring
him back to what he had been, but in vain: one intoxicated with divine love could no longer be held by family ties.
Fearing that their son would suddenly leave for Vrindavana, the raja and Narayani set guards over him, while he begged
Gaura and Nityananda to show him the way and sang: “Hari, Hari, when shall I become a resident of Vrindavana?” <!-- F25 -->

The opportunity came of itself. The “Bhakti-ratnakara” relates:

“Suddenly a man came from the king of Gauda, and his father and uncle went to the court in Gauda. Taking this opportunity
he deceived the guards and somehow took leave of his mother. [...] The mother lives carefree, cherishing her son, and does
not know that her son will leave home. Narottama, meanwhile, in deep secrecy set out, meditating on the feet of the Lord.
How fresh his youth, how handsome he is! On the full-moon day of Kartika he left home.” (“Bhakti-ratnakara” 1.287–288,
1.290–292)

He deceived the guards not out of guile: this is how one leaves whom the Lord has called. Young and handsome, he went away
without a thought for youth, or beauty, or a kingdom.[^5] <!-- F26 F27 F28 Q4 -->

The son of a wealthy zamindar, for the sake of meeting the Lord he gave up every bodily comfort in an instant. He walked
day and night barefoot and in tears, with no thought of food or drink, until at last he collapsed senseless beneath a tree. A brahmana with a golden complexion then approached, offered him a bowl of milk and spoke gently: “Narottama, drink — your wounds will
heal; be calm, all will be well,” and vanished. And that night in a dream Rupa and Sanatana placed their hands upon his breast and fed him milk brought by Mahaprabhu Himself, and his weariness left him. Pilgrims, seeing that he was a raja's son, grew fond of him. He lived now on milk, now on roots and fruits: his longing to see Vrindavana
drowned his hunger. On the way he heard from devotees about Gaura and Nityananda and prayed to Nityananda, “When,
Nitai-chand…” At Mathura, on the bank of the Yamuna, remembering the six Gosvamis headed by Rupa and Sanatana, he wept in
ecstasy. <!-- F29 -->

Keeping the Lord's feet in his heart, he visited many tirthas and came to Vrindavana (“Bhakti-ratnakara” 1.293).
<!-- F30 -->

## Lokanatha's Only Disciple
Of the six Gosvamis, three still remained in Vrindavana at that time — Gopala Bhatta, Jiva and Raghunatha Dasa; Rupa,
Sanatana and others had already withdrawn from sight.[^6] The senior Gosvamis of Vraja took the young man to their hearts: “Sri
Gopala Bhatta and all the other wise ones regard Narottama as their second life” (“Bhakti-ratnakara” 1.347). <!-- F31 -->

Jiva Gosvami sent him to serve the feet of Lokanatha Gosvami; according to the “Prema-vilasa,” Sri Rupa told Jiva to do
so in a dream. Lokanatha accepted no disciples — such was his vow. But Narottama was drawn by his severe renunciation and
vowed to take initiation from him alone. So he began to serve him in secret, at night: he cleaned the place where
Lokanatha went to relieve himself, brought sifted earth and water for cleansing, and hid the broom. This went on for a
long time — according to one of the biographies, a whole year — until Lokanatha, catching the raja's son at this work
one night, softened his resolve. The “Bhakti-ratnakara” says it briefly:

“At that very time Narottama came there and joyfully served his guru as is fitting. Pleased with his service, he gave him
the mantra of initiation and showed Narottama boundless mercy.” (“Bhakti-ratnakara” 1.345–346)

And of the day of initiation:

“On the full moon of the month of Shravana, at an auspicious moment, Lokanatha accepted Narottama as his disciple.”
(“Bhakti-ratnakara” 1.294)

According to the “Prema-vilasa,” he received initiation in the month of Vaishakha.[^7] Thus Narottama became Lokanatha's
only disciple; the story is told in detail in the article on Lokanatha Gosvami. <!-- F32 F33 Q4 -->

Narottama's initiation is a lesson for the whole world: how to serve one's spiritual master, and how selfless, sincere
service can move to mercy even one bound by a vow never to accept a disciple. This is not “menial work” but a disciple's model
service; Lokanatha and Narottama enacted this pastime to show its worth, above all for the people of North Bengal.
Centuries later it was repeated by Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati and Srila Gaurakishora Dasa Babaji: the Babaji too had
vowed to accept no disciples, and Bhaktisiddhanta by persistent service won his acceptance — as his only disciple.

## At Jiva's Feet
In Vrindavana Narottama lived by madhukari, on alms, and studied the writings of the Gosvamis under the guidance of Sri
Jiva. Here, with Jiva, he met Srinivasa. Srinivasa had only heard his name before — and the name alone filled him with joy
and longing to meet him; in a dream Sri Rupa told him, “Tomorrow you will see him.” The next day they met, and tears
flowed from the eyes of both. “Providence has been kind,” said Srinivasa, “it has brought me such a jewel as Narottama,”
and Narottama bowed to him and humbly begged his mercy. From their first meeting they were friends for life and studied
together; to Jiva the two were “like his two arms” (“Bhakti-ratnakara” 4.405–426). With Jiva's permission Srinivasa and Narottama went round the whole of
Vraja-mandala with Raghava Gosvami, who showed them the places of Krishna's pastimes; Narahari Chakravarti gives five
waves of the “Bhakti-ratnakara” to this circuit, and the “Anuraga-valli” (6) also tells of their farewell circuit of
Vraja.[^8] <!-- F35 F37 F108 F115 -->

Then Dukhi Krishnadasa, the future Shyamananda, came from Gauda. That very day Srinivasa and Narottama sensed that a new Vaishnava friend was about to join them — and he came. From then on the three studied with Jiva together, and
he taught each of them closely, opening to them the philosophy and scriptures of Mahaprabhu and a
devotion full of bliss, and the three studied deeply the works of the six Gosvamis. All three were accomplished
musicians, skilled in *kalavati*, the music of Delhi, and with Jiva's permission they held kirtanas. One heart, one life
and one aim united them in their bhajana in Vrindavana, and yet they felt that their innermost longing was still
unfulfilled. <!-- F36 -->

Krishnadasa Kaviraja wrote the “Chaitanya-charitamrita” while Jiva was still living, and soon afterwards the three future
acharyas came to Vrindavana. That, says one of the biographies, is why the “Charitamrita” names neither Srinivasa nor
Narottama, Lokanatha's disciple: the book was finished before they began to preach (and Lokanatha, moreover, had forbidden
any mention of himself). Narottama regarded Krishnadasa Kaviraja himself as the centre of the circle of devotees after the
six Gosvamis and drew inspiration from him. <!-- F38 -->

## The Gosvamis' Books to Gauda
Jiva grieved over the wretched state of Gauda and wished to establish his disciples there as acharyas; the Gosvamis of
Vraja decided the same. The “Prema-vilasa” (12) says the decision was taken at Jiva's festival at the end of the month of
Kartika. For their deep learning and devotion the three were charged with preaching bhakti according to the Gosvamis'
teaching, and Jiva told them: “From now on carry Mahaprabhu's message everywhere. Take the books of the Gosvamis and go
quickly to Bengal.” Lokanatha too told Narottama to return to his homeland and preach. <!-- F39 -->

The books were packed in a wooden chest protected from the rain and loaded on an ox-cart; a wealthy man of Mathura paid
for the cart and for guards on foot, and Jiva walked beside the chest up to Mathura. They set out in the bright half of
the month of Agrahayana — according to one of the biographies, on the shukla-panchami, in the year 1504 of the Shaka era
(1582–1583). Bowing to Govinda, Gopinatha and Madana-mohana, the three followed the cart along the road to Mathura,
spending the nights in villages and holding sankirtana and preaching there. Among those who saw them off was Uddhava Dasa
(“Bhakti-ratnakara” 6.514). The “Prema-vilasa” (12–13) also tells of this journey. <!-- F40 -->

In Vana-Vishnupura the books were stolen at night by the men of King Vira Hambira, who took the chest for treasure. The
next morning the grief of the three was so great that life lost all meaning for them. Learning that the thief was the
king himself, Srinivasa stayed in Vishnupura and sent Narottama to Kheturi and Shyamananda to Orissa.[^9] Srinivasa knew
Lokanatha's intention and said to Narottama: “Hurry to Kheturi and fulfil Prabhu Lokanatha's order”
(“Bhakti-ratnakara” 7.119). Srinivasa recovered the books and informed Narottama and Shyamananda; how this came about and
how Vira Hambira was converted is told in the article on Srinivasa Acharya. <!-- F41 Q6 -->

Why did Lokanatha let his only disciple go? Narottama's former position as a wealthy zamindar made him especially fit to
preach in North Bengal: he above all could teach the people of that land the norms of a renounced life. Lokanatha saw in
him not only a noble upbringing but also zeal and a liking for being among people, and out of compassion for them he told
him to return to Kheturi. Hermits absorbed in intimate service are usually not inclined to work for the “good” of people:
such good, while it remains on the bodily level, is valued only by those who have not understood the true aim of life.
But when a devotee of Narottama's stature takes up such work, the work itself is ennobled. So, through his disciple, Lokanatha was instructing the world — as a mother-in-law, instructing her daughter, teaches her daughter-in-law. <!-- F42 I2 -->

Narottama himself, tormented by separation from his guru, obeyed and set about preaching pure devotional service in North Bengal. His humility and sorrow sound in the song of the “Prarthana” “Aneka duhkhera pare…”: after many
sorrows he was brought to Vraja — and now he is far from it once more. <!-- F42 -->

## In the Lord's Footsteps
While Srinivasa was recovering the books, Narottama travelled through Gauda. First of all he came to Mahaprabhu's
birthplace, Mayapur-Navadvipa. On the bank of the Ganga he cried out hundreds of times, “O Gaurahari!” Sitting under a
palm tree and wondering where the Lord's birthplace might be, he met an old brahmana. Learning who he was, the old man
burst into tears: “Every day Nimai Pandita would sit with His students on this
ghat and explain the shastras, and I would gaze from afar at His wonderful form. Today I remember how He sat under this tree.” Narottama touched his feet with his head, and
the brahmana blessed him: “Before long devotion to Govinda's feet will be yours, and will preach the glory of
Gaura-Govinda everywhere,” and showed him the way to the house of Jagannatha Mishra. <!-- F43 F44 -->

At the door of that house Narottama fell down in tears and offered prayers. Inside he was met by Shuklambara
Brahmachari; recognising by the signs on his body that here was a bearer of the Lord's mercy, he embraced him and asked
him endlessly about the Gosvamis of Vrindavana. Then Ishana Thakura, Shachi-mata's old servant, blessed and embraced
Narottama; in the house of Srivasa he was embraced by Shripati and Shrinidhi. The “Bhakti-ratnakara” says that on seeing
Damodara Pandita, a direct associate of the Lord, he fell at his feet in great emotion (8.93). All these aged associates
of the Lord were sunk in grief over His withdrawal.[^10] <!-- F45 Q10 -->

Narottama spent several days in Mayapur, then came to Shantipura, to Advaita's house. Advaita had already withdrawn; his
son Achyutananda asked the guest about the Gosvamis of Vrindavana, and together they sang the glory of Mahaprabhu.[^11] In
Ambika-Kalna, in the house of Gauridasa Pandita, he met his disciple Hridaya Chaitanya, and they embraced. In Saptagrama,
at the confluence of the Ganga, Yamuna and Sarasvati, a village blessed by Nityananda, he visited Uddharana Datta's home; its master had already left this world, and the devotees lived in the anguish of separation from their guru.
<!-- F46 F47 F48 -->

In Kharadaha Parameshvari Dasa led him into the inner apartments, to Jahnava and Vasudha. Seeing that he bore the mercy of Lokanatha and Jiva, the two mistresses granted him incomparable mercy, and for four days he spoke with them
about Krishna. Parameshvari showed him the way to Puri; Narottama was granted darshana of Mahesha Pandita's feet, and
Mahesha, at parting, could not calm himself for love (“Bhakti-ratnakara” 8.220). In Khanakul-Krishnanagara Narottama came to Abhirama Gopala Thakura. He lived in separation
from Gaura and Nityananda, hardly returning to outer consciousness; Narottama wept much, beheld the wonderful Deity of
Abhirama's Gopinatha and offered Him prayers, and the next morning, by an inner prompting, set out for Puri.
<!-- F49 F50 F106 -->

In Puri Gopinatha Acharya and the devotees came out to meet him. “I was just hoping you would come today,” said Gopinatha.
Narottama fell at the feet of the Lord's associate, and they spoke long of Krishna, Gauranga and the Gosvamis. On
Gopinatha's advice he visited the holy places: he had darshana of Jagannatha, Baladeva and Subhadra, drowned in an ocean
of prema at the samadhi of Haridasa Thakura, visited the Gambhira, and in the house of Gadadhara Pandita cried aloud, “O
life of Gauranga, O Gadadhara!” He had darshana of Tota-Gopinatha and honoured Mamu Gosvami, who served Gopinatha. At
Gopinatha he was told that here the Lord had entered and had not come out again (“Bhakti-ratnakara” 8.357) — and hearing
of the Lord's withdrawal he fell unconscious, and everyone wept. Everywhere in Puri he was honoured as the one whom the
Lord had called before his birth. <!-- F51 Q9 -->

In the house of Kashi Mishra Gopala Guru received him with great love. He showed Narottama the Lord's bed and seat and
the places of His ecstasies, and both wept; he told him about Vakreshvara and entrusted him to the feet of Sri
Radhakanta (“Bhakti-ratnakara” 8.382–392). Narottama had darshana of the Deities of the Radhakanta Matha, then visited the
Gundicha, the garden of Jagannatha-vallabha and the Narendra-sarovara. The “Narottama-vilasa” (4) relates that in Puri he
dreamed of the Lord's kirtana before the chariot. <!-- F52 F103 -->

From Puri he went to Nrisimhapura, to Shyamananda. After the long separation Shyamananda embraced him and would not let
him go for many days; Narottama flooded the town with sankirtana, and day and night they spoke of Krishna. In Srikhanda he
bowed to Narahari Sarakara and Raghunandana. Narahari, who had loved his father, blessed him again and again, touching his
head; Raghunandana embraced him. His hosts asked about the devotees of Puri, and kirtana and dancing went on for many
days. Raghunandana showed him the Deity of Gaura installed by Narahari, and Narottama in ecstasy bowed down in the
courtyard (“Bhakti-ratnakara” 8.431–432). <!-- F53 F54 Q10 -->

In Katwa, Kantaka-nagara, he spent two days with Gadadhara Dasa, who lived in separation from Gaura and Nityananda; he
embraced Narottama and bathed him in tears. Seeing the Deity of Gaura installed by Gadadhara Dasa, Narottama could not
contain his feelings. The “Narottama-vilasa” (5) relates that in Yajigrama and Srikhanda he learned of the withdrawal of
almost all the Lord's associates in Puri. In Ekachakra an old brahmana showed him the places of Nityananda's pastimes;
remembering Hadai Pandita and Padmavati, Nityananda's parents, he fell unconscious. According to the
“Narottama-vilasa,” this brahmana was Nityananda Himself, who then appeared to him in the form of Balarama. <!-- F55 F56 -->

At last, by his guru's will, he returned home. Asking the way, he came to the Padmavati, crossed it and arrived in
Kheturi, where the people gave him a welcome. His father and uncle had by then left this world. Santosha, the best of the
saintly people of the village, ran out onto the road, prostrated himself before him and with tears took the dust of his
feet, and Narottama embraced him. The “Prema-vilasa” (13) tells of his return and of his instructions to Shyamananda.
<!-- F57 -->

## The Six Deities of Kheturi
The estate passed to Santosha Datta, and he became raja. Narottama had long since renounced the world and laid claim to
nothing, and Santosha became his disciple: after a few days Narottama initiated him into the mantra of Radha-Krishna. The
“Bhakti-ratnakara” calls him just that: “Sri Santosha Datta, the disciple of Mahashaya” (10.205). Santosha had long wished
to build a temple and install a Deity, and he asked for permission. Within a few months he raised a large temple, and with
it a storehouse for provisions, a hall for kirtana, dwellings and an ashrama for devotees, a tank, a garden of flowers and a house for guests. <!-- F58 F59 -->

While still in Vrindavana, Lokanatha had told Narottama to install Deities of Radha-Krishna and Sri Gauranga. The
“Bhakti-ratnakara” says:

“When Narottama came to Gauda, Prabhu Lokanatha gave the command: the service of the Deities of Sri Gauranga and Krishna,
service to the Sri Vaishnavas, and the Lord's sankirtana. As he was commanded, so he applied himself: he established the
service of six beautiful Deities. With all earnestness he is always absorbed in their service; the very sound of those
names soothes the heart.” (“Bhakti-ratnakara” 1.422–425)

On his return Narottama prepared five pairs of Radha-Krishna Deities — the “Bhakti-ratnakara” (10.191) speaks of five
Krishnas with Their beloveds; the sixth was Gauranga. By Lokanatha's command Shyamananda too was invited to the festival.
<!-- F60 F61 Q5 -->

This is how Gauranga appeared. One night Narottama received an instruction in a dream. In the morning he went to a small
village near Gopalpur and asked to be let into the granary of a certain Vipradasa — “fortunate Vipradasa,” as the
“Bhakti-ratnakara” calls him — a granary full of grain and mustard. They politely tried to dissuade him: venomous snakes
had settled there, hissing terribly, and snake-charmers could not drive them out either with mantras or with herbs — the
snakes only grew angrier. Narottama insisted. The granary was opened — the snakes, to everyone's amazement, had vanished,
and the Deities of Sri Gaura and Vishnupriya came into his hands of Their own accord: Gaurasundara with Vishnupriya rose
onto his lap (“Bhakti-ratnakara” 10.202). A mighty sankirtana broke out on the spot. Narottama brought Them to Kheturi and
established Their service; today these Deities are served in Gambhila. According to the “Bhakti-ratnakara” (10.191–206),
this was still before the great festival, during the preparations for it.[^12] The “Prema-vilasa” (19) says that the form
of Gauranga was manifested by the Lord Himself before He took sannyasa. <!-- F62 Q8 -->

So six Deities appeared in Kheturi: Sri Gauranga, Sri Vallavikanta, Sri Vrajamohana, Sri Krishna, Sri Radhakanta and Sri
Radharamana.[^13] The very sound of these names gladdens the hearts of the devotees; Narottama composed a pranama to Them:

“Gauranga, Vallavikanta, Sri Krishna, Vrajamohana, Radha-ramana, O Radha, Radha-kanta — obeisance to You!”
(“Bhakti-ratnakara” 1.426)

After the arati he would worship Them, calling each Deity by name (“Bhakti-ratnakara” 10.492, 495–496). Srila Vamshidasa
Babaji spoke of Them with a loving jest: “Six thieves in the house of Narottama Thakura, and one thief in Mayapur.”
<!-- F63 Q5 Q13 -->

By revealing Vishnupriya together with Gaura, Narottama gave the world a lesson in siddhanta. Some held Gaurasundara to be
not Vishnu but merely a jiva within varnashrama, a “great man” or the founder of a sect; Narottama dispelled this error by
revealing, together with Gaura-Narayana, Vishnupriya — the bhu-shakti, one of His three energies. Following him as “his
first teacher,” Srila Bhaktivinoda Thakura installed Gaura and Vishnupriya in the Lord's house in Mayapur. But this
worship is on the pure path of the followers of Rupa, walked by Rupa, Raghunatha, Jiva, Krishnadasa Kaviraja and
Narottama himself: Krishna is worshipped together with His ashraya-vigraha. Where this path is abandoned and Gaura with
Vishnupriya are “worshipped” as separate from Krishna, in a worldly fashion of one's own, there is neither Vishnupriya nor
Gaurasundara. The installation of the six Deities is described in the “Bhakti-ratnakara” (10, 12), the “Prema-vilasa”
(19) and the “Narottama-vilasa” (6). <!-- F64 I4 -->

## The Great Festival at Kheturi
Santosha Datta resolved to mark the installation of the six Deities with a festival such as Gauda had never seen. It was
set for the Phalguna Purnima, the appearance day of Mahaprabhu. In its splendour it was compared only with Yudhishthira's
rajasuya. Messengers were sent many miles to invite kings and landholders, poets and pandits, Vaishnavas, the best
singers and reciters. The “Narottama-vilasa” (6) tells how Narottama and Srinivasa met in Budhari and sent out the
invitations; the “Prema-vilasa” (14) speaks of a first festival, when Gauranga and Vallavikanta were installed.
<!-- F65 -->

At Narottama's invitation there came all who still remained of Sri Chaitanya's associates, and their disciples — from
Navadvipa, Orissa, Kharadaha and other places. From Kharadaha came Jahnava, Nityananda's shakti, with her retinue; with
her were Parameshvari Dasa, her uncle Krishnadasa Sarkhel, Nityananda's son-in-law Madhava Acharya, Raghupati Vaidya,
Minaketana Ramadasa, Murari Chaitanya Dasa, Jnana Dasa, Mahidhara, Shankara, Kamalakara Pippalai, Gauranga Dasa, Nakari,
Krishnadasa, Damodara, Balarama Dasa, Mukunda and Vrindavana Dasa Thakura. From Srikhanda came Raghunandana and others,
from Navadvipa Shripati and Shrinidhi, from Shantipura Advaita's sons Achyutananda, Gopala and Krishna Mishra, from
Ambika-Kalna Hridaya Chaitanya, from Nrisimhapura Shyamananda with his people. Srinivasa with the devotees set out from
Budhari, from the house of Govinda Kaviraja. Achyutananda was received with great reverence: the festival took place
almost half a century after the Lord's withdrawal. Srinivasa, with his disciple Ramachandra and Govinda, Ramachandra's younger brother, laboured to make the festival a success. <!-- F66 -->

Santosha provided a huge boat for the crossing, palanquins and carts. Srinivasa, Narottama and the raja met the guests
with garlands; when Jahnava entered Kheturi with kirtana, Srinivasa, Narottama and Shyamananda, walking ahead of all,
prostrated themselves before her in the dust of the road. Each guest was given a separate house and servants. Gates and houses were decorated with banana leaves, pots,
mango leaves, flowers and auspicious signs; there rose mountains of clay and silver vessels of milk and ghee, thousands
of pots of yogurt, mountains of vegetables and fruits. <!-- F67 F104 -->

On the eve, at Jahnava's word, the preparations were completed. Narottama honoured Jahnava with sandal paste and garlands;
at the request of Narottama and Srinivasa, Raghunandana Thakura sang the mangalacharana, the sankirtana went on until
midnight, and thousands of people received maha-prasada. <!-- F68 -->

On the day of the festival the rites of installation and the abhisheka of the six Deities were performed by Srinivasa
Acharya as chief priest, and the whole installation took place under Jahnava's guidance, according to her instructions.
An hour before the abhisheka the Deities were carried into the temple; then came the dressing and ornaments, thousands of
dishes, tambula, sandal paste, garlands, a great arati and dandavats by all. Srinivasa, Narottama and Shyamananda gave
everyone sandal paste and garlands, and at Jahnava's word Nrisimha Chaitanya Dasa garlanded the three of them. Jahnava sat
on a high seat in the kirtana hall. <!-- F69 F70 -->

At the word of Jahnava and Achyutananda, Narottama began the kirtana. Gauranga Dasa, Gokula Dasa and Vallabha Dasa sang
the responses, and Devidasa played the mridanga — the finest masters of song and music. Narottama's sweet kirtana, with
its distinctive melody, filled all directions, and everyone wept tears of prema. And then in this kirtana Mahaprabhu
Himself appeared with His associates. The “Bhakti-ratnakara” compares His appearance to lightning in a cloud (10.572).
With Him were Nityananda and Advaita, Haridasa and Srivasa, Narahari, Mukunda, Gauridasa Pandita, Madhava, Vasu and
Govinda Ghosha, Acharya Purandara, Mahesha, Shankara, Shridhara, Jagadisha Pandita, Yadunandana, Kashishvara and many
others; among them danced Achyutananda, Raghunandana, Shripati and Shrinidhi. Those who had long since withdrawn from the
world's sight appeared again and sang in the sankirtana as in the Lord's days, and the devotees in their joy forgot that
They had ever withdrawn. After a long kirtana the Lord and His associates suddenly withdrew again; everyone was
stricken with grief, and the festival was not resumed at once. <!-- F71 F72 Q7 -->

This was no vision of the dead and no ghosts: the Lord's eternal associates do not die — they only withdraw from the
world's sight and by Their own will appear again. The “Bhakti-ratnakara” says that on that day Srinivasa, Narottama and
their companions saw every desire of theirs fulfilled (10.607). The “Prema-vilasa” (19) adds that Radha and
Krishna appeared above the kirtana, and Narottama was plunged into deep ecstasy. <!-- F72 Q7 -->

Then Jahnava sprinkled the Deities with red powder — phagu — and the devotees played with it. In the evening Srinivasa
performed the abhisheka of Gauranga in honour of His appearance, to Narahari's song of the Lord's appearance on the
Phalguna Purnima; the kirtana went on all night, and at dawn the mangala-arati was performed. Early in the morning
Jahnava bathed and with great enthusiasm cooked innumerable dishes and herself served prasada to the mahantas
(“Bhakti-ratnakara” 10.686–687).[^14] She asked Narottama, Srinivasa and Shyamananda to eat after the mahantas, and she
herself ate last; outside the hall Santosha fed thousands of people and also ate last. <!-- F73 F74 Q12 -->

On the second day Santosha asked the devotees to cook for the Deities in their own huts, and on the third he tearfully
presented everyone with gold coins, cloth, ornaments and vessels. Jahnava with her retinue left for Vrindavana; Srinivasa
and Shyamananda stayed in Kheturi a few days more. The festival was truly unprecedented, and Vaishnavas still remember it today. The “Narottama-vilasa” (8) tells of the farewell to the mahantas and of robbers who on the
road took shelter of the Vaishnavas. <!-- F75 -->

Afterwards Srinivasa and Shyamananda returned to Yajigrama, and soon Narottama came there. Shyamananda went to Utkala,
while Srinivasa, Narottama and Ramachandra Kaviraja set out for Navadvipa. The Lord's old servant Ishana embraced them and
the next day took them round the holy places — the nine islands of Navadvipa; Srinivasa took Narottama with him, “one
with him in soul.” From there they went to Srikhanda and there learned that Ishana had left this world. Returning from
Vrindavana, Jahnava came first of all to Kheturi, and she was met by the devotees headed by Narottama and Ramachandra
Kaviraja (“Narottama-vilasa” 9; “Prema-vilasa” 15). And Shyamananda stayed on in Kheturi with Narottama for some months.
Virachandra, Nityananda's son, also came to Kheturi — “to increase Sri Narottama's joy,” as the “Bhakti-ratnakara” says;
Thakura Mahashaya came out to meet him with great joy, Virachandra danced in the sankirtana, and the festival went on for
several days. <!-- F76 F77 F102 -->

## Brahmanas at the Thakura's Feet
After the festival Narottama's fame spread through the whole Vaishnava world. Eminent brahmanas became his disciples —
Ramakrishna Acharya and Ganganarayana Chakravarti — and many learned devotees of the region; these two became the most
important preachers among his disciples. The “Narottama-vilasa” (12) lists the branches and shoots of Narottama's tree.
<!-- F78 -->

Not everyone was pleased. A smarta pandita insulted Narottama before his own students, calling him a shudra — and his body
began to rot with leprosy. In despair he went to drown himself in the Ganga, but that night Durga-devi came to him in a dream: “Fool, you took a mahabhagavata for a shudra… Go to his feet and ask his pardon.” The next morning, with a cloth
round his neck as a sign of humility, he fell in tears at Narottama's feet. The Thakura's company gradually healed him; on
Narottama's advice he began to serve Krishna and became deeply attached to him. The disease was not a “punishment of the
Goddess” but the fruit of offending a Vaishnava; and Durga, Mahamaya, is the Lord's maidservant, and so she herself sends
the offender to the feet of His devotee. The “Narottama-vilasa” (9) tells the same. <!-- F79 -->

Once Narottama and Ramachandra Kaviraja were on their way to bathe in the Padmavati when they met two young brahmanas, Harirama and
Ramakrishna, sons of Shivananda Acharya, a zamindar of the village of Goyasgaram. At their father's order they were
leading goats and a buffalo to be sacrificed to the Goddess at the Durga-puja in their home. The radiance of the two
Vaishnavas captivated the youths. Narottama explained to them the meaning of the “Bhagavata”: the rites of karma-kanda in
the modes of passion and ignorance, with violence to animals, do not elevate — even one who reaches heaven falls from it,
and one who kills animals destroys his own soul; and every soul is Krishna's energy. Give up such worship, he said,
and serve the Supreme Lord without selfish motive. The brothers set the animals free, bathed in the Padma, came to the
temple of Mahaprabhu, and the next morning, with shaven heads, received the mantra of Radha-Krishna: Harirama from
Ramachandra Kaviraja, Ramakrishna from Narottama.[^15] <!-- F80 -->

Shivananda was enraged. Finding his sons in Narottama's ashrama at Kheturi — with tilaka, a tulasi-mala round the neck and
a shikha — he summoned from Mithila the smarta pandita Murari. But by their guru's mercy the sons refuted all his arguments
from scripture, showing that a Vaishnava stands above a brahmana, and the pandita fled. And that night the Goddess
reproached Shivananda in a dream for his enmity towards Vaishnavas: “Sri Hari is the master of all… Go to Narottama and
beg forgiveness.” <!-- F81 -->

Ganganarayana Chakravarti, a famous learned brahmana of Gambhila, listened attentively to the Gosvamis' siddhanta from
Narottama, took shelter at his feet, and then studied the Gosvamis' books deeply. Jagannatha Acharya, a worshipper of
Durga, also came to him: Durga herself told him in a dream to go to Narottama, saying, “Krishna is my master and my guru.”
He came to Kheturi and bowed down; Narottama smiled and said, “Great is your fortune — Krishna's mercy is upon you,” and
initiated him into the mantra of Radha-Krishna. Jagannatha became one of his dearest and most trusted disciples; the
“Prema-vilasa” (19) also tells of him. <!-- F82 F83 -->

The smartas complained to Raja Narasimha, the ruler of Pakkapalli: “Narottama is a shudra, yet he makes brahmanas his
disciples. Let this continue, and we shall perish like the Yadus.” With the raja and the digvijayi pandita Rupa Narayana they
set out for Kheturi.[^16] Ramachandra Kaviraja and Ganganarayana learned that they had stopped for the night in the bazaar
at Kumarapura, and opened stalls there — a potter's and a pan-seller's. They addressed the smartas in pure Sanskrit and
defeated them all in debate, Rupa Narayana included. Learning that these were Narottama's disciples, the raja said: “If
even his ordinary disciples defeat you, there is no point in going to him himself.” That night Durga appeared to the raja
and Rupa Narayana in a dream with a sword and told them to take shelter of Narottama. In the morning they came and found
him in the temple of Gauranga, absorbed in chanting the name. Narottama received them respectfully and humbly: “I am
insignificant, while you are famed for your learning, your wisdom and a king's riches. How fortunate I am that such learned and noble men have come to me; what service can I do for you?” They fell at his feet, begging forgiveness, and after a few days he
initiated them into the mantra of Radha-Krishna. The “Prema-vilasa” (19) also tells of the raja's conversion; it also
contains the tradition of a sacred thread that appeared in Narottama's heart. <!-- F84 -->

How, then, could a kayastha be a guru to brahmanas? This is no violation and no “protest” against social order. Srila
Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati explains in the “Anubhashya”:

“Sri Thakura Narottama, Shyamananda and other gurus who were true brahmanas were in fact pure brahmanas themselves. That
is exactly why brahmanas by birth such as Sri Ganganarayana and Ramakrishna recognised them as pure ‘brahmanas’ fit to be
gurus.” (“Anubhashya” on CC Madhya 24.325)

Mahabhagavatas, he says elsewhere, are nishkinchana paramahamsas beyond varnashrama who take shelter of Krishna alone —
and at once he quotes Narottama's words from the “Prarthana”: “One who regards the associates of Gauranga as eternally
perfect goes to the son of the king of Vraja” (“Anubhashya” on CC Madhya 11.67). <!-- F98 Q17 -->

Nor was there any worldly entanglement in Narottama's accepting many disciples. Of those who think so, Srila
Bhaktisiddhanta writes:

“Sri Rupa, Sri Sanatana, Sri Jiva, Sri Raghunatha Dasa and the Lord's other associates, the great souls, as well as
Sriman Narottama, Srila Madhva, Ramanuja and others, accepted many disciples and gave instruction by writing books. Many
foolish people imagine that all this is an obstacle to bhakti and a wave of worldly objects, and so they become offenders
at the feet of genuine akinchana devotees.” (“Anubhashya” on CC Madhya 7.130)

An acharya's power is not measured by the number of his disciples: in one line there are both hermits who delight in
solitary service (viviktanandi) and preachers who enlarge the community (goshthyanandi). <!-- F100 I5 Q17 -->

## Kirtana and Songs
Narottama's ashrama, according to the late reference work “Gaudiya-vaishnava-abhidhana,” was at Bhajantuli, about two
miles from the capital of Kheturi. He preached above all through kirtana. He introduced his own style into
sankirtana — *garanhati* — and this style helped to spread bhakti through the land; Srinivasa had the *manohara-shahi*
style and Shyamananda the *reneti*; today all three are lost. Srinivasa preached both through the “Bhagavata” and through
kirtana, Narottama and Shyamananda mainly through kirtana. According to the “Narottama-vilasa” (6), Narottama created his
style before the Deity of Gauranga, and gandharvas came to listen. <!-- F85 F86 Q19 -->

Narottama's songs are collected in the “Prarthana” and the “Prema-bhakti-chandrika”; one of the biographies also names a
book of kirtanas, the “Nama-sankirtana.” A pranama to his guru Lokanatha is also his. These books have been reprinted
countless times and have influenced all who walk the path of bhakti: in them is the life of the devotees and songs for
every mood. His influence reached even distant Manipur: there it is acknowledged that Vaishnavism spread by his spiritual
power, and his songs are heard in every household. The “Prema-vilasa” (17) tells of the “Prema-bhakti-chandrika.”[^17]
<!-- F85 F87 -->

The biographies quote much from his songs. In his songs he prays to Nityananda (“Ara kabe nitai chand…”) and to
Advaita, remembers his guru (“Ha ha prabhu Lokanatha…”: only by your mercy will Narottama's longing for the service of
Radha-Govinda and Chaitanya be fulfilled), laments bitterly that he did not serve the feet of the nine
Gosvamis, among whom he names Bhugarbha and Lokanatha (“Hari hari, ki mora karama-gati manda…”), sings of Rupa and Sanatana
and of Gauranga (“Bane te thake ha Gauranga…”; “Vrajendra-nandana yei, shachi-suta haila sei” — “He who is the son of the
king of Vraja has become the son of Shachi”). The songs about Sri Rupa-manjari — “Sri rupa-manjari-pada sei mora
sampada…,” “Shuniyachhi sadhu-mukhe…,” “Ei nava-dasi bali…,” “Sri-rupa-pashchate ami…” — speak of service in Rupa's
footsteps. The “Prema-bhakti-chandrika” opens with a
mangalacharana to Rupa, and from it too comes “Shuniya govinda-raba…”; the biographies also quote the lines “Jaya
sanatana rupa prema-bhakti-rasa-kupa…” and “Ana katha ana vyatha….” Srila Bhaktisiddhanta in the “Anubhashya”
quotes his lines on the devotee's only wealth:

“Thakura Narottama says: ‘My wealth is Nityananda’; ‘The feet of Sri Radha and Krishna are the wealth of my life’; ‘Glory
to You, Purifier of the fallen! Give me this wealth: apart from You nothing is dear to me’; ‘The feet of Sri
Rupa-manjari are my treasure, my service and worship, the wealth of my life.’” (“Anubhashya” on CC Adi 13.124)
<!-- Q14 F98 F101 F105 -->

And the “Bhakti-ratnakara” quotes his verse on the Lord's two shaktis:

“Through one shakti, headed by Sri Rupa, the Lord reveals these books; through another shakti, named Sri Srinivasa, He
spreads them. When will He who mercifully manifested these two shaktis on earth — the ocean of mercy Sri Chaitanya —
come before my eyes?” (“Bhakti-ratnakara” 1.234) <!-- F114 -->

In these songs is his soul. He calls himself a “chandala” and by other self-abasing names, just as Mahaprabhu called
Himself “a Mayavadi sannyasi without prema”: devotees hide their greatness. Narottama is the best follower of Rupa
Gosvami, “the first among the followers of Rupa,” the servant of Rupa, Raghunatha and Jiva, who accepted Rupa as his
treasure of prema. In one of his songs he draws a ladder of mercy: without shelter at Jahnava's feet there is no mercy of
Nityananda; without it the desires for enjoyment do not leave; while they remain there is no mercy of Rupa and
Raghunatha, and without that no attachment to the youthful Couple of Vraja. <!-- I6 I7 I8 -->

Srinivasa was Narottama's friend, his equal in spirit, and yet Narottama in a song asked him for the company of his
disciple: “Prabhu Srinivasa Acharya, be merciful — Narottama Dasa begs for the company of Ramachandra.” This shows how he
loved Srinivasa himself. In the “Prarthana” he prayed for Srinivasa's mercy also when detractors condemned the Acharya's
second marriage. And Ramachandra Kaviraja was the closest companion of his preaching and bhajana, his second self; Narottama
held him dearer than his own life, and Srinivasa, Narottama and Ramachandra were of one heart and mind. The “Bhakti-ratnakara” says of Ramachandra:

“Ramachandra Kaviraja, full of all virtues, is the one with whom Narottama Mahashaya is one in soul.”
(“Bhakti-ratnakara” 1.269)

Narahari also quotes a verse from Govinda Kaviraja's drama “Sangita-madhava,” where Ramachandra is called “truly one” with
the renowned Narottama (1.270), and another about the two “who became a pair through great mutual love” (1.277). Srila
Bhaktisiddhanta writes in the “Anubhashya” that Thakura Narottama prayed for Ramachandra's company birth after birth, and
that Ramachandra was the chief and dearest companion of Thakura Mahashaya and Srinivasa Acharya in preaching and in
bhajana (on CC Adi 11.53). The “Prema-vilasa” (17) describes their friendship and the order of their bhajana. As once
Rupa, Sanatana and Raghunatha, as Lokanatha and Bhugarbha, so Narottama and Ramachandra showed the bhajana of like-minded
friends, without a trace of rivalry — though they did not necessarily live in one hut. <!-- F88 Q11 F98 I9 F109 -->

When Srinivasa came to Gauda, Narottama and Shyamananda were with him, and a flood of Hari-kirtana arose. Srinivasa,
Narottama and the other acharyas spread sankirtana by the mercy and under the guidance of Jahnava, and also of Vishnupriya;
Jahnava they accepted as jagad-guru and considered they had fulfilled the purpose of life by hearing Hari-katha from her.
<!-- F89 -->

The “Bhakti-ratnakara” pictures the Thakura's life in Kheturi: he served the Vaishnavas with body, mind and words, his
festivals of sankirtana never ceased, and in that ocean of happiness all bathed, down to the lowliest; Gauranga's
associates in Gauda rejoiced on seeing him, and Jahnava was pleased at the very name “Sri Thakura Mahashaya” and
mercifully came to Kheturi herself (1.427–434). By the Lord's will he accepted many disciples, and whomever he favoured
attained every perfection (1.435–437). Ramachandra's brother Govinda Kaviraja sang of him:

“Glory, glory, glory to Thakura Narottama, the great king of prema-bhakti, whose minister, one with him in body, is
Ramachandra Kaviraja! Prema is the jewel of his crown, his ornaments the succession of bhavas, with which every limb of
his shines; on the royal throne in Kheturi he sits, and with him the assembly of devotees.” (“Bhakti-ratnakara”
1.439–440) <!-- F113 -->

## Separation and Withdrawal
Ramachandra, with the blessing of Narottama and Srinivasa — by one of the biographies, at their bidding — went again to Vrindavana and some months later entered the eternal
pastimes; Srinivasa, unable to bear separation from his beloved disciple, also left this world. Narottama first learned of
Ramachandra's departure, and soon after, of Srinivasa's. In great separation he composed the song “Ye anila prema-dhana
karuna prachura…” — “He who brought the treasure of prema, boundlessly merciful…” — on his separation from Srinivasa; all
the devotees sing it. The “Narottama-vilasa” (11) gives another of his laments as well — “Gaurangera sahachara…” — about
the associates of Gauranga. <!-- F90 F107 -->

It was the same sorrow that lived in his songs all his life: he had come into the world when the Lord had already
withdrawn. Vrindavana Dasa Thakura grieves that he did not see the pastimes of the Lord as a scholar:

“I am a fallen sinner — it was not my lot to be born at that time and see those divine pastimes.” (CB Madhya 1.399)

And Srila Bhaktisiddhanta in the “Gaudiya-bhashya” on this verse quotes Narottama's “Prarthana”: “Not having obtained the
company of those associates, Narottama Dasa weeps,” and: “When Gaura, Nityananda, Advaita and the devotees appeared in
Nadia, I was not born.” <!-- F99 Q18 -->

Soon his own hour came. Almost drowning in the ocean of separation, Narottama took leave of the Deities and went with
Govinda and others to Budhari; he spent a day there in kirtana and then came to Gambhila on the Ganga. There he entered
the temple of Mahaprabhu and told the devotees to sing kirtana. After the sankirtana, bowing to the Ganga in tears, he
entered the water and, going in a little way, asked that all directions be filled with loud chanting of the holy name.
Ramakrishna Acharya and Ganganarayana Chakravarti led two kirtanas in two places. He asked them to wash his body with
Ganga water — and as they were about to do so, he, absorbed in the chanting of the name, withdrew from worldly eyes into
the waters of the Ganga. The “Narottama-vilasa” says:

“From Budhari he went quickly to Gambhila, bathed in the Ganga and sat in the Ganga's water. He ordered Ramakrishna and
Ganganarayana, ‘Both of you, wash my body.’ How could they wash him? As soon as they touched him, he dissolved like milk
into the waters of the Ganga. Before everyone's eyes he quickly disappeared — this is utterly unfathomable: who could
understand it?” (“Narottama-vilasa” 11.330–333)

The waves of the Ganga surged up, the gods showered flowers from heaven, and from all sides rose the cry, “Hari! Hari!”
Narahari calls it Sri Mahashaya's hidden withdrawal. Thakura Mahashaya left this world as the Lord's eternal associates
leave it — he withdrew. <!-- F91 -->

Earlier, according to the same book, there had been another pastime. In Gambhila Narottama had a funeral pyre made ready,
stayed silent for three days and, as Narahari says, “in the eyes of people, separated from his body” (11.232). The brahmana
detractors were already rejoicing: here, they said, was the price of brahmana disciples. But Ganganarayana, taking pity on
them, prayed at the pyre: “Prabhu, save them,” — and the Thakura at once returned to his body and, crying “Radha-Krishna!
Chaitanya!”, rose from the pyre shining like the sun. The detractors repented and fell at Ganganarayana's feet, and
Narottama gave them bhakti. The Lord's eternal associate is not subject to the laws of the body: he showed this pastime
for people's eyes — for the sake of those very detractors. <!-- F91 -->

The day of his departure is the krishna-panchami of the month of Kartika; according to our calendar (Sri Chaitanya
Saraswat Math), the krishna-panchami of the month of Damodara (≈ Kartika); in 2026 it fell on 30 October. The sources do
not give the year of his departure.[^18] One of the biographies says that Vishvanatha Chakravarti was active some sixty
years after Narottama's withdrawal. <!-- F92 -->

After his departure his disciples held festivals in Gambhila and in Kheturi. In the kirtana at Kheturi everyone suddenly
saw Narottama dancing; after dancing a little he slipped away unseen, and later, appearing in dreams, consoled those close
to him (“Narottama-vilasa” 11.338–355). <!-- F93 -->

## Legacy
Narottama's disciplic line reached Srila Vishvanatha Chakravarti Thakura: Narottama — Ganganarayana Chakravarti —
Krishnacharana Chakravarti — Radharamana Chakravarti — Vishvanatha; he is the fourth from Narottama. Vishvanatha named his
line in an auspicious verse:

“Bowing to the feet of my gurus, full of great love — Rama, Krishna and Ganga — I praise Srila Narottama, Natha and Sri
Gauranga Prabhu.”

“Rama” here is Radharamana, “Krishna” is Krishnacharana, “Ganga” is Ganganarayana, “Natha” is Lokanatha. Vishvanatha wrote
his “Sankalpa-kalpadruma” “under the guidance of Sri Lokanatha and Sri Narottama” of his line, and among his minor works is
the “Sri Narottamashtaka,” a hymn to Narottama in nine stanzas. And in the line of shiksha — the handing down of truth
itself — as Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati gives it, Narottama, together with Srinivasa and Shyamananda, stands between
Krishnadasa Kaviraja and Vishvanatha: Mahaprabhu — Svarupa Damodara — Rupa and Sanatana (with Raghunatha Dasa, Raghunatha
Bhatta and Gopala Bhatta) — Jiva — Krishnadasa Kaviraja — Narottama — Vishvanatha. <!-- F94 Q15 Q16 F111 -->

With the departure of Srinivasa, Narottama, Shyamananda and Rasikananda, the third of its “dark ages” began for Bengal
Vaishnavism: darkness descended on Gaudiya Vaishnavism, and apasampradayas multiplied. <!-- F95 -->

But the Gaudiya Vaishnavas of later generations kept Narottama's songs as something intimate. Srila Gaurakishora Dasa
Babaji considered the “Prarthana” and the “Prema-bhakti-chandrika” his everything and often sang Narottama's song “Keno va
achhaye prana…”; Srila Vamshidasa Babaji quoted the “Prema-bhakti-chandrika” (“Shuniya govinda-raba…”). Srila
Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati as a schoolboy read only the “Prarthana,” the “Prema-bhakti-chandrika” and his father's books. In
Bogra he spoke of North Bengal, once blessed by Narottama. And on his last day in this world he asked Srila B. R. Sridhara
Maharaja to sing “Sri rupa-manjari-pada,” a song he dearly loved, and said: “Narottama Dasa Thakura accepted the
understanding of Rupa and Raghunatha — better to live by it.” The songs of Srila Bhaktivinoda Thakura are of the same
essence as Narottama's; and Srila Akinchana Dasa Babaji said that Sridhara Maharaja's writings too are not different from
Narottama's. Srila Krishnadasa Babaji himself gave no one initiation, yet, seeing the countless people blessed by
Narottama, Bhaktivinoda and other great souls, he never presumed to say that their company did those people no good: he
understood that this was their calling. <!-- F96 I10 I7 -->

He is remembered also by Narahari Chakravarti, the author of the “Bhakti-ratnakara” and the “Narottama-vilasa.” Near the
end of the “Namamrita-samudra,” his prayers to the Lord's associates, he calls on him together with Srinivasa and
Shyamananda:

“O Srinivasa! Narottama! Shyamananda! Be merciful to me together with your followers — I am very wicked.”
(“Namamrita-samudra” 270) <!-- F97 Q18 -->

## Champaka-manjari
In Krishna's eternal pastimes Narottama is Champaka-manjari, who always assists Sri Rupa-manjari in the service of Radha
and Krishna; out of mercy for conditioned souls she came into this world. According to the “Prema-vilasa” (11), Sri Radha
Herself bestowed on him this service and this name.[^19] <!-- F3 -->

So Thakura Mahashaya, called by the Lord before his birth, received prema from the hands of the Padmavati, served his
guru as a servant of servants, brought the Gosvamis' books to Gauda, revealed in Kheturi six Deities and in his kirtana
Mahaprabhu Himself with His associates, and in his songs left to the Gaudiya Vaishnavas the path of the followers of
Rupa. He is called one of the three acharyas who preserved the Gosvamis' heritage — and the Gaudiya Vaishnavas sing his
songs as something intimate to this day. <!-- F5 F6 F64 F94 F96 I1 I2 I4 I7 I10 -->
