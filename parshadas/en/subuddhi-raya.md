# Subuddhi Raya
*Субуддхи Рай · @subuddhi-raya*

## A Former Governor of Gauda
Among those whom Sri Chaitanya favoured with special mercy is a man who met Him only once:
Subuddhi Raya. The “Charitamrita” calls him Subuddhi Raya, and more often simply Raya; the biographies likewise call him
Sri Subuddhi Raya.[^1] His life is remembered not for the worldly position he once held, but because Mahaprabhu received
him, heard him out and gave him what no rules could give — and for the way he then served the Gosvamis and the
Vaishnavas of Vraja. <!-- F1 F4 -->

By birth he was a brahmana, known for his learning in the scriptures. The “Charitamrita” does not name his varna
outright; the story shows only that he was a Hindu of high caste, since the Shah's wife demands that he be deprived of
his Hindu caste. The sources name neither his parents nor his birthplace; the year and day of his appearance and of his
departure are unknown, and his days are not in the Vaishnava calendar of the Sri Chaitanya Saraswat Math. <!-- F2 F3 -->

In earlier days Subuddhi Raya was a governor — an *adhikari*, an official holding authority — in Gauda.[^2] Gauda was the
name of the ancient capital of Bengal, and after it of the whole region.[^3] In his service at that time was one Hussain
— Saiyad Hussain Khan, who was destined to become the Badshah of Gauda, Hussain Shah:

“Formerly, when Subuddhi Raya was a governor (*adhikari*) in Gauda, Saiyad Husain Khan was in his service.” (CC, Madhya
25.180)

One of the biographies calls Hussain his protégé; another in one place says the opposite — that Subuddhi served under
Hussain — but this contradicts both the “Charitamrita” and that biography's own account.[^4] <!-- F5 F6 F7 Q1 -->

## The Whip and the Water Pot
Krishnadasa Kaviraja tells the story that brought Subuddhi to Mahaprabhu as follows:

“Raya put him in charge (*munsiph*) of digging a large pond; finding a fault in his work, Raya struck him with a whip.
Later, when Husain Khan became king of Gauda, he greatly advanced Subuddhi Raya. His wife saw the marks of the whipping
on his body and urged the king to have Subuddhi Raya killed. The king said, ‘Raya has been my patron; he is a father to
me. That I should kill him — such talk is not right.’ His wife said, ‘If you will not take his life, then take his
caste.’ The king said, ‘If his caste is taken, he will not survive.’ His wife threatened to die, and the king was caught
in a dilemma. At last he had water from a Muslim's water pot (*karoya*) put into Subuddhi Raya's mouth.” (CC, Madhya
25.181–186)

The pond was a large one (*dighi*); it was Subuddhi himself who undertook to have it dug, setting Hussain to supervise
the work (*munsiph*), and the fault for which Hussain felt the whip across his back was no small one. In time Hussain
became Badshah of Gauda,[^5] but he did not forget his former benefactor: out of gratitude he honoured Subuddhi highly
and, as one of the biographies remarks, bore him no grudge for the whipping. The scars, however, remained on the
Shah's back. Seeing them, the Begum questioned her husband and learned that it was Subuddhi who had beaten him in the
days when he was governor. The Shah refused to execute his benefactor; he refused, too, to strip him of his caste,
knowing that for Subuddhi this would be as good as death.[^6] Only when his wife threatened to kill herself did the
Shah, seeing no way out, have water from a *karoya* — the vessel in which Muslims keep water — poured into his mouth:
water that Muslims had touched. By the smarta custom of the time this alone was enough for a Hindu to lose his caste.
For a Vaishnava, however, such a loss is nothing before the holy name, and, as we shall see, the Lord Himself would
prescribe for Subuddhi no purification other than the Name. <!-- F8 F9 F10 F11 F12 F13 F14 F15 F16 -->

## In Varanasi: the Name Instead of Atonement
Subuddhi had long wished to give up worldly affairs. He took the loss of caste as a pretext (*chadma*): he left his
family and all worldly concerns and went to Varanasi. It was not resentment or humiliation that made him renounced — he
merely used the loss of caste as an occasion to leave a world to which his heart had long grown cold. <!-- F17 I2 -->

In Varanasi he asked the smarta panditas how he should atone for his sin. They prescribed a prayashchitta: to drink
boiling ghee and so give up his life.[^7] Other brahmanas objected that the fault was not so grave as to be paid for
with his life. The disagreement of the learned left Subuddhi confused, and he hesitated:

“He asked the panditas what atonement he should perform. They said, ‘Drink boiling ghee and give up your life.’ Another
said, ‘Not so: the fault is slight.’ Hearing this, Raya was left in doubt.” (CC, Madhya 25.188–189)

This very disagreement shows the futility of the smartas' prayashchittas: some of the learned demand death, others
reject it, and neither can give the soul peace. <!-- F18 F19 F20 -->

Then Mahaprabhu came to Kashi. Subuddhi came to Him and told Him everything in order. The Lord did not choose between the
panditas' opinions, nor did He give him another, milder prayashchitta — He gave him the Name:

“Then, when Mahaprabhu came to Varanasi, Raya met Him and told Him his story. The Lord said, ‘Go from here to Vrindavana
and always chant the name of Krishna in sankirtana. By the mere semblance of the holy name (*namabhasa*) your sin and its
taint will go; and by chanting the Name itself you will attain Krishna's feet. Chanting Krishna's name further, you will
dwell in Krishna's abode. This is the atonement even for the greatest sins.’” (CC, Madhya 25.190–193)

Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati in the “Anubhashya” heads these verses thus: the Lord gives an injunction better than
all others; the fruits of namabhasa and of the pure Name are different. Sin departs at the mere semblance of the Name,
its passing shadow; the Name itself leads further, to Krishna's feet and a place in His abode. The Lord speaks the word
“atonement” to a man who had come to Him seeking atonement, but for Him the Name is not a rite for cancelling sins, nor a
means, but the goal itself: where the Name is chanted, the question of atonement simply falls away. Thus Mahaprabhu freed
Subuddhi from the search for a prayashchitta, and rejected the counsel of suicide — just as later He would hold Sanatana
Gosvami back from it (CC, Antya 4). <!-- F21 F22 Q2 -->

## The Woodcutter of Mathura
Having received the order, Subuddhi set out for Vrindavana by way of Prayaga and Ayodhya and stayed some days in
Naimisharanya. Meanwhile the Lord, having visited Vrindavana, had left it for Prayaga. Coming to Mathura, Subuddhi learned
that he had missed Him and was deeply grieved (CC, Madhya 25.194–196). In this separation true renunciation (*vairagya*)
and indifference to everything worldly awoke in him; one of the biographies writes that in the grief of deep separation
he took up severe renunciation. <!-- F23 F24 F25 -->

This is how he lived:

“Raya would gather dry wood and sell it in Mathura, earning five or six *paisa* a bundle. He himself lived on one
*paisa*'s worth of chickpeas and kept the rest of his money with a merchant. Whenever he saw a poor Vaishnava, he fed
him; and when Gaudiyas came, he gave them yogurt and rice and had them massaged with oil.” (CC, Madhya 25.197–199)

The dry wood he gathered in the forest himself, with great labour. What he earned he did not spend on himself alone: with
these few coins he fed other Vaishnavas too, and to the Gaudiyas who came to Vraja he gave yogurt with rice and
oil to anoint their bodies.[^8] His life shows that it is not only the rich who can serve Vishnu and the Vaishnavas:
where there is a sincere wish to serve, then even in poverty, by the Lord's will, there is no lack of what service
requires. <!-- F26 F27 F28 Q3 I1 -->

## Rupa Gosvami's Companion
Meanwhile Rupa Gosvami, who had received the Lord's teachings at Prayaga, came to Mathura:

“Meanwhile, when Rupa Gosani came to Mathura, Subuddhi Raya met him at Dhruva-ghata.” (CC, Madhya 25.179)

They had known each other since Gauda. Rupa rejoiced to see the renunciation of his old acquaintance and his eagerness to
serve the Vaishnavas, and Subuddhi received him lovingly and became his guide:

“When Rupa Gosani came, Raya welcomed him with great affection, took him along and showed him the twelve forests.” (CC,
Madhya 25.200)

Srila Bhaktisiddhanta heads this verse the same way: Subuddhi shows Sri Rupa the twelve forests. Govinda-deva in the
“Gaura-krishnodaya” writes that Rupa with his brother Anupama, “taking the steadfast Subuddhi Raya as companion,” entered
Vraja (15.28); and one of the biographies says that he guided both brothers through the forests of Vraja.[^9] After a
month Rupa and Anupama left by the road along the Ganga in search of Sanatana. <!-- F29 F30 F31 F32 Q4 -->

## Meeting Sanatana Gosvami
Sanatana Gosvami, having received the Lord's teachings in Kashi, came to Prayaga and from there went on to Mathura by the
main, royal road.[^10] There, in Mathura, Subuddhi Raya met him:

“In Mathura Subuddhi Raya met him and told him all the news of Rupa and Anupama. The two brothers went by the Ganga path
and Sanatana by the royal road; that is why he did not meet them. Subuddhi Raya showed Sanatana great affection, but
Sanatana would not accept an affection rooted in worldly relationships (*vyavahara-sneha*).” (CC, Madhya 25.204–206)[^11]
<!-- F33 F34 -->

Subuddhi, like Sanatana, had come to Vrindavana at Mahaprabhu's bidding.[^12] He had known Sanatana as a minister at the
court of Gauda, loved him dearly and wished to serve him, and so he met him with all the warmth their former relations
called for. But Sanatana, the great renunciant, would not accept an attachment grown out of worldly ties and remained
unmoved by it; one of the biographies explains this by his humility: Sanatana did not wish to accept service from
him.[^13] Govinda-deva relates that on coming to Mathura Sanatana first of all met the people of Mathura and Subuddhi
Raya and, hearing that his brothers had gone by the road to Prayaga, stayed there, content in their company
(“Gaura-krishnodaya” 16.41). <!-- F35 F36 F37 F38 F39 -->

## In Vraja to the End
Subuddhi Raya spent the rest of his life in Vraja. He lived in great humility together with the Gosvamis and, with
renunciation and steadfastness, sang the name of Hari — thus fulfilling the order Mahaprabhu had given him in Kashi. When
and where he left this world is not known. <!-- F40 F3 -->

It seems that the books of the next generation remember him too, under the name Subuddhi Mishra.[^14] The
“Narottama-vilasa” tells how Lokanatha Gosvami, wandering through Vrindavana, “suddenly heard from someone that Sri
Subuddhi Mishra had come to Vrindavana,” and then Sri Rupa Gosvami came (1.87–88); there too Subuddhi Mishra is named
among the Lord's companions whose accounts were told to Narottama (2.67). The “Bhakti-ratnakara” twice glorifies him in
its lists of blessing: “Glory to Sri Subuddhi Mishra and Sri Jiva Gosani!” (1.33) and “Glory to Sri Bhugarbha, Lokanatha
and Shashthidhara! Glory to Sri Subuddhi Mishra and Sri Chandrashekhara!” (2.12). And Narahari, in a pada of prayer to
Gaura's companions, sings:

“Subuddhi with Raghava, Bhugarbha, Sri Lokanatha, who wander in Vraja in the joy of prema — may I have attachment to
these names, may the evil mind go far away, may a thrill pervade my whole body.” (pada of Narahari “Ei abhilasha
mane…,” 18.9) <!-- F41 -->

## Significance
Subuddhi Raya saw Mahaprabhu only once, but that one meeting sufficed him for a lifetime. A worldly misfortune — the loss
of caste, for which the experts in smriti could find no atonement but death — became a door for him: the Lord freed him
from the search for a prayashchitta by giving him the name of Krishna, and separation from the Lord, whom he did not
find in Mathura, made him truly renounced. <!-- F22 I2 -->

Yet his renunciation was not dry. The penniless woodcutter of Mathura fed poor Vaishnavas, gave hospitality to pilgrims
from Gauda, lovingly served Rupa Gosvami by showing him the forests of Vraja, and met Sanatana with the same love; and he
spent the rest of his life beside the Gosvamis, in humility and in chanting the Name. So his life, made memorable only by
Mahaprabhu's mercy, teaches that anyone with a sincere wish to serve can serve the Lord and His devotees: the rest the
Lord Himself provides. <!-- F4 F40 I1 Q5 -->
