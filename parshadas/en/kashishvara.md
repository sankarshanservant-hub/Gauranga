# Kashishvara Pandita
*Кашишвара Пандит · @kashishvara*

## The Lord's Mighty Servant
When Sri Chaitanya Mahaprabhu went in Nilachala to see Jagannatha, Kashishvara walked ahead of Him, parting the crowd —
a disciple of Ishvara Puri, a brahmachari, whom Krishnadasa Kaviraja calls “the mighty.” Behind, carrying a water pot,
walked his godbrother Govinda. So these two, sent to the Lord by their guru, served Him day after day. Later Mahaprabhu
sent Kashishvara to Vrindavana and gave him to take along His very Self — the Deity of Gaura-Govinda; Kashishvara
installed Him beside Sri Govindadeva. <!-- F20 F38 -->

The primary sources call him by various names. In the “Charitamrita” he is “Brahmachari Kashishvara” (Adi 10.138),
“Kashishvara Gosani” (Madhya 10.185; Adi 8.66) and “the mighty Kashi” (*kashi-balavan*, Adi 10.142); in Vrindavana Dasa
he is “Kashishvara Pandita” (CB, Antya 8.57); in Murari Gupta and in the “Gaura-ganoddesha-dipika” (166), “Sri Kashishvara
Gosvami”; in the “Bhakti-ratnakara,” “Kashishvara Pandita” and “Kashishvara Gosvami”; in the “Prema-vilasa,” “Kashishvara
Pandita”; in Devakinandana's vandana, “Kashishvara Gosani.” The biographies call him Sri Kashishvara Pandita, Brahmachari
Kashishvara Pandita, and also “the mighty” Srila Kashishvara Gosvami Prabhu.[^1] <!-- F1 -->

Kashishvara, the Lord's servant in Puri, and Kashishvara Gosani (Gosvami) of Vrindavana, the teacher of Govinda Gosani,
are one and the same person. Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati states this plainly in the “Anubhashya” on CC, Adi 8.66:
“Sri Kashishvara (Pandita) Gosvami is a disciple of Sri Ishvara Puri,” and there describes his family, birthplace, Deities
and service to the Lord in Puri. So too the “Bhakti-ratnakara,” which tells how the Lord sent him from Puri to
Vrindavana, and the “Prema-vilasa”: “In the land of Mathura, Kashishvara Pandita is renowned… he is a disciple of
Ishvara Puri.” The word “brahmachari” speaks of his ashrama, not of another person.[^2] <!-- F44 -->

## Bhringara
Who he is in Krishna's eternal pastimes, Kavi Karnapura tells us:

“The two servants in Vrindavana, Bhringara and Bhangura, were born as the Lord's servants Sri Kashishvara and Govinda.”
(“Gaura-ganoddesha-dipika” 137)

By the order of the names Kashishvara is Bhringara and Govinda is Bhangura; so Srila Bhaktisiddhanta understands the
verse in the “Anubhashya” (on Adi 8.66 and 10.138), and so do the biographies. Bhringara is one of Krishna's serving boys
(*cheta*) in Vraja. The eternal servant of Krishna came into Gaura's pastimes to serve Him here as well, and in that
service his extraordinary strength found its use.[^3] <!-- F2 Q1 -->

Elsewhere in the same list Karnapura writes: “Sri Kashishvara Gosvami is the former Shashirekha in Vraja”
(“Gaura-ganoddesha-dipika” 166). One of the biographies gives this as the opinion of some, while Srila Bhaktisiddhanta,
comparing the two verses, writes with caution: he who in Vrindavana was Krishna's servant Bhringara, or she who was
Shashirekha, is Kashishvara in Gaura's avatara — and adds a question mark. The “Prema-vilasa” (18.27–28) names him and
Krishnadasa — apparently Krishna Pandita — as the manjaris Keli-kala and Kunkuma. We follow verse 137.[^4] <!-- F3 F4 -->

## Family and Birthplace
Kashishvara's shripata is the village of Chatra (Chatara) in the Hooghly district, a mile from Shrirampur station. His
father, Sri Vasudeva Bhattacharya, was a brahmana of the Kanjilal Kanu family, of the Vatsya gotra.[^5] The family bore
the hereditary title “Chaudhuri,” and therefore the part of Chatra where Kashishvara's temple stands is called
Chaudhuripara. All this Srila Bhaktisiddhanta records in the “Anubhashya” on CC, Adi 8.66. <!-- F5 F6 F7 -->

That Kashishvara came from Bengal, Mahaprabhu Himself says. In Nilachala, naming His Bengali devotees to Vallabha
Bhatta, He said:

“Acharyaratna, Acharyanidhi, Gadadhara Pandita, Jagadananda, Damodara, Shankara, Vakreshvara, Kashishvara, Mukunda,
Vasudeva, Murari and all the other devotees who descended in Gauda have preached the name of Krishna and prema throughout
the world, and whatever devotion to Krishna I have, I have gained in their company.” (CC, Antya 7.48–50)

Murari Gupta, too, opens with his name the list of “residents of Gauda” who came to Nilachala (4.1.4). <!-- F8 F32 Q9 -->

His sister's son was Sri Rudra (Rudrarama) Pandita of Vallabhapura. Vallabhapura lies a mile north of Mahesha; there
Rudra Pandita installed the Deity of Sri Radha-vallabha. The “Charitamrita” names him a twig of Shankararanya's branch
(Adi 10.106), and Karnapura writes: “Krishnachandra's friend in Vraja named Varuthapa is Rudra Pandita, dear to
Gauranga” (“Gaura-ganoddesha-dipika” 135). <!-- F9 -->

The service of Kashishvara's Deities in Chatra was carried on by his brother's descendants. Srila Bhaktisiddhanta writes
that in his day the manager was Sri Shivachandra Chaudhuri of that family; nine seers of rice were allotted daily for the
service; the lands long set aside for the Deities the brother's descendants had lost in the courts, and the service had
fallen into decline. One of the biographies, quoting another's commentary, says on the contrary that the Deities have
land enough.[^6] <!-- F10 -->

## The Deities of Chatra
In the temple in Chaudhuripara reside to this day the Deities of Sri Gauranga and Sri Radha-Govinda installed by
Kashishvara Pandita. Every year Dola-yatra — Dol-purnima — is celebrated there. <!-- F11 F12 -->

One of the biographies, citing the commentary of Srila Bhaktivedanta Swami Maharaja on CC, Adi 8.66, adds that Srila
Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati visited this temple; that the commentator himself, while still a householder, went there more
than once and took prasada; that the Deities of Radha-Govinda and Gauranga are extraordinarily beautiful; and that a
temple of Jagannatha stands nearby. <!-- F13 -->

## Disciple of Ishvara Puri
Kashishvara took shelter of Sri Ishvara Puri — whom the Lord chose as His guru — and remained a brahmachari. Govinda
served Ishvara Puri together with him. Krishnadasa Kaviraja writes, listing the branches of the Chaitanya tree:

“Brahmachari Kashishvara was a disciple of Ishvara Puri, and Sri Govinda was his dear follower. At the time of his
departure both received his order, came to Nilachala and met the Lord.” (CC, Adi 10.138–139)[^7]

Both served their guru as long as he remained in this world, and received much mercy from him. Before his departure
Ishvara Puri told them: when he was gone, they should go to Nilachala and serve Sri Chaitanya Himself. Srila
Bhaktivinoda Thakura writes the same in the “Amrita-pravaha-bhashya”: on their guru's order they came to Mahaprabhu in
Nilachala. The “Prema-vilasa” says that Ishvara Puri entrusted Kashishvara to Sri Krishna Chaitanya, and the Lord wove
him into His tree as one of its chief branches (18.13–14). <!-- F14 F15 F16 F17 -->

## Coming to the Lord
Govinda went to the Lord at once and reached Puri when Mahaprabhu had already returned from His journey through South
India. Introducing himself, he said:

“I am the servant of Ishvara Puri, and my name is Govinda. On Puri Gosani's order I have come to You. At the time of his
passing, Gosani ordered me, ‘Go to Krishna Chaitanya and serve Him.’ Kashishvara will come after visiting all the holy
places, but on my master's order I have hurried to Your feet.” (CC, Madhya 10.132–134)

Kashishvara first went round the holy places — according to one of the biographies, he travelled all over India — and
came later.[^8] In the same account the “Charitamrita” says:

“The next day Kashishvara Gosani came, and the Lord received him with honour and kept him with Himself. He would take
the Lord for the darshana of Jagannatha, walking ahead and keeping back the crowds.” (CC, Madhya 10.185–186)

In the summary of the pastimes at the beginning of the Madhya-lila, too, Kaviraja mentions his coming: “Paramananda
Puri, Govinda and Kashishvara arrived” (Madhya 1.129). <!-- F18 Q3 -->

To accept their service was not simple for the Lord, and He Himself disclosed His difficulty to Sarvabhauma
Bhattacharya:

“The Lord said, ‘Bhattacharya, consider this: My guru's servant is worthy of My respect. It is not proper for Me to have
him serve Me, yet My guru has ordered it. What am I to do?’ The Bhatta said, ‘The guru's order is mighty, and one must
not transgress it. Scripture bears witness to this.’” (CC, Madhya 10.142–144)

And Sarvabhauma gave two examples. Lakshmana, knowing that Parashurama on his father's order had stopped short of nothing,
accepted his elder brother's will, for the orders of the guru and of elders are carried out without deliberation
(“Raghuvamsha” 14.46); and Sri Rama Himself says that He will carry out His guru's order without deliberation: in this
lies Sita's good, and His own above all (“Ramayana,” Ayodhya-kanda 22.9). <!-- F19 Q4 -->

Then the Lord accepted both disciples of His guru:

“Out of regard for their connection with His Guru, the Lord honoured them both, yet in obedience to the Guru's order He
gave them His service.” (CC, Adi 10.140)

One of the biographies says: the Lord honoured them as His equals and yet, on His guru's order, made them His personal
servants. The Lord Himself, the Master of all, needs no one's service; He accepted a guru in His lila and accepted the
service of His guru's disciples out of mercy — to Ishvara Puri, whose wish He fulfilled, and to them. So Mahaprabhu
taught the world a lesson in obedience to the guru: all that is connected with the guru is worthy of honour, and the
guru's word is not debated. <!-- I2 -->
