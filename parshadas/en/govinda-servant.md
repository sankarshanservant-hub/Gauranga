# Govinda, Mahaprabhu's Servant
*Говинда — слуга Махапрабху · @govinda-servant*

## A Servant Named Govinda
Among Sri Chaitanya's companions in Nilachala there was one who, day after day, attended the Lord's very person: His
personal servant Govinda. He served the Lord His meals and kept His stores, massaged His feet, followed Him to
Jagannatha with a vessel of water, slept at the door of His room, and through him the Lord spoke to the devotees and the
devotees to the Lord. Krishnadasa Kaviraja calls him simply Govinda, Sri Govinda and “the servant Govinda”; Vrindavana
Dasa calls him “the doorkeeper Govinda,” “the Lord's faithful doorkeeper” and “the supremely fortunate servant”;
Govinda-deva in the “Gaura-krishnodaya” calls him “the servant of the best of Puris,” that is, of Ishvara Puri. The
biographies also name him Sri Govinda Dasa and “the Lord's personal servant.”[^1] <!-- F1 -->

By birth Govinda was a shudra. When he came to the Lord, Sarvabhauma Bhattacharya wondered that Ishvara Puri had kept a
servant from a shudra family, and Kavi Karnapura's drama repeats his question: “How could he accept a non-brahmana as
his servant?” The Lord's answer we shall give below; here let us say only that mercy does not look at birth, and that
Govinda's position is defined not by caste but by his being an eternal servant of Krishna. Nothing more is known of his
worldly life. The sources name neither his birthplace nor his parents, nor the year and day of his appearance, nor when
and where he left this world; nor are his days found in the Vaishnava calendar of the Sri Chaitanya Saraswat Math. One
of the biographies says only that after the Lord's disappearance Govinda did not live long. <!-- F4 F5 -->

## Bhangura
Who he is in Krishna's eternal pastimes, Kavi Karnapura tells us:

“The two servants in Vrindavana, Bhringara and Bhangura, were born as the Lord's servants Sri Kashishvara and Govinda.”
(“Gaura-ganoddesha-dipika” 137)

By the order of the names Govinda is Bhangura, and his godbrother Kashishvara is Bhringara; so Srila Bhaktisiddhanta
Sarasvati, too, understands the verse in his “Anubhashya.” Bhangura is one of Krishna's serving boys (*cheta*) in Vraja.
The servant of Krishna descended into Gaura's pastimes together with the Lord to serve Him here as well; birth in a
shudra family does not change his eternal nature.[^2] <!-- F3 Q8 -->

One of the biographies sees in him also an associate from Vaikuntha, citing another verse of the same list:

“Pundarikaksha and Kumuda, famous in the realm of Vaikuntha, were born in Gauda as Govinda and Garuda, dear to the
Lord.” (“Gaura-ganoddesha-dipika” 116)

And so that biography names him: Sri Pundarika, an eternal serving associate of Vaikuntha.[^3] <!-- F2 -->

## A Disciple of Ishvara Puri
Before he came to Mahaprabhu, Govinda served Sri Ishvara Puri — the one whom the Lord chose as His guru. With him served
Ishvara Puri the brahmachari Kashishvara, his godbrother. Krishnadasa Kaviraja says of them:

“Brahmachari Kashishvara was a disciple of Ishvara Puri, and Sri Govinda was his dear follower. At the time of his
departure both received his order, came to Nilachala and met the Lord.” (CC, Adi 10.138–139)[^4]

Both served their guru as long as he remained in this world. And before his departure Ishvara Puri instructed them:
when he was gone, they were to go to Nilachala and serve Sri Chaitanya Mahaprabhu Himself — his disciple. Govinda-deva
tells it this way:

“When the Acharya and the others were already preparing to set out, the disciple of Madhavendra [Ishvara Puri], having
completed his austerities, attained perfection; and his servant named Govinda came, resolutely casting off grief; out
of respect for his guru's word the Lord kept him by Him.” (“Gaura-krishnodaya” 11.20)[^5] <!-- F6 F7 F8 -->

## The Guru's Servant Becomes the Lord's Servant
Govinda came to Puri when the Lord had already returned from His journey through South India. The “Charitamrita”
relates:

“The next day Mahaprabhu sat down to discuss Sri Krishna's pastimes with Sarvabhauma Bhattacharya and other devotees.
Just then Govinda came, offered his obeisances and humbly addressed the Lord: ‘My name is Govinda, and I am the servant
of Ishvara Puri. By my guru's order I have come to You. Just before leaving this world Ishvara Puri told me to find Sri
Krishna Chaitanya and become His servant. Kashishvara will also come here when he has finished his pilgrimage; but,
following my guru's command, I came to You at once.’” (CC, Madhya 10.130–134)

Kashishvara came later, after first visiting the holy places (Madhya 10.185); Govinda went straight to the Lord.[^6] In
Karnapura's drama “Chaitanya-chandrodaya” the Lord guesses even before Govinda has spoken: “Ah, it seems someone is
coming from Puri-ishvara.” And in the “Charitamrita” He answers the newcomer:

“Sri Ishvara Puri always regarded Me with a father's love. Therefore in his sending you to Me I see his causeless
mercy.” (CC, Madhya 10.135)

In Karnapura's drama, too, the Lord says that Ishvara Puri's love for Him never changes. <!-- F9 F10 Q2 -->

Then Sarvabhauma asked how Ishvara Puri could have a servant from a shudra family. The Lord answered:

“‘Like the Supreme Lord Himself, Ishvara Puri is fully independent; therefore his mercy is not bound by the rules and
injunctions of the Vedas. Like the Supreme Lord Himself, Ishvara Puri does not confine his mercy to any caste or
ancient lineage. Vidura was a shudra on his mother's side, yet Krishna took food in his house. Krishna's mercy is
moved by love alone; only pure love can draw the mercy of the fully independent Lord. A relationship of love brings
millions of times more happiness than one steeped in fear and reverence.’” (CC, Madhya 10.137–140)

In Karnapura's drama the Lord answers in the same way: Hari is independent, and His mercy looks neither at caste nor at
lineage; rejecting the food of Suyodhana, He gladly accepted the food of Vidura. The mercy of the Lord and of His pure
devotees is not bound by birth, and service out of love is incomparably higher than service out of duty and reverence.
<!-- F11 I4 -->

Yet the Lord had a difficulty, and He Himself revealed it to Sarvabhauma:

“Sri Chaitanya said: ‘Bhattacharya, consider this yourself. I must always honour My guru's servant. Tell Me, what am I
to do? It is not proper to accept service from one's guru's servant, yet it is the guru himself who has given this
order.’ Sarvabhauma replied: ‘The guru's command is very powerful, and it must not be disobeyed. So the scriptures
declare.’” (CC, Madhya 10.142–144)

And Sarvabhauma gave two examples: Parashurama, without deliberating, carried out even the most terrible order of his
father, and Lakshmana, following his example, devoted his life to carrying out the will of his elder brother, Ramachandra,
for the instructions of the guru and the elders are carried out without deliberation (“Raghuvamsha” 14.46); and Sri
Rama Himself tells Sita that the command of the guru is one's first duty and a source of great fortune (“Ramayana,”
Ayodhya-kanda 22.9). In Karnapura's drama the Lord concludes: though it is not fitting that the servant of revered
elders should serve Him, since such is the guru's order, so be it. <!-- F12 Q3 -->

And the “Charitamrita” concludes the story:

“Then Mahaprabhu embraced Govinda, and he became the personal servant of Lord Sri Chaitanya. All the Vaishnavas honoured
Govinda as one of Sri Chaitanya's dearest devotees, and he was always ready to serve them. So Govinda came to live with
the other devotees: the elder Haridasa and the younger Haridasa, both skilful singers, and Ramai and Nandai. All of them,
together with Govinda, constantly served Sri Chaitanya. Therefore the great fortune Govinda attained cannot be
described.” (CC, Madhya 10.147–150)

Krishnadasa Kaviraja says the same in his list of the branches of the Chaitanya tree:

“Out of regard for their connection with His Guru, the Lord honoured them both, yet in obedience to the Guru's order He
gave them His service.” (CC, Adi 10.140) <!-- F13 Q1 -->

Srila Bhaktisiddhanta puts the meaning of this verse into a heading of his “Anubhashya”: honouring everything connected
with the guru, and following the rule that “the guru's orders are not to be questioned,” the Lord accepts their service.
The Lord Himself, the Master of all, needs no one's service. He accepted a guru in His lila to teach the world that
without taking shelter of a true guru there is no real good — and for the same reason He accepted initiation from Ishvara
Puri in Gaya. Therefore His guru's servant, whom He honoured as an elder, He accepted only on the guru's order. Two
lessons meet in this event: everything connected with the guru is worthy of honour, and the guru's word is carried out
without question. And in it there is also the Lord's mercy both to Ishvara Puri, whose wish He fulfilled, and to
Govinda, to whom He granted service to Himself. <!-- F14 I3 -->

## The Chief Servant
From then on Govinda served the Lord Himself:

“The Lord gave Govinda the service of His person, and when He went to see Jagannatha, Kashishvara walked ahead of Him.
Through the dense crowd the Lord passed untouched, for the mighty Kashishvara pushed the people aside and cleared the way.
Ramai and Nandai, both servants of the Lord, served constantly alongside Govinda. Every day Ramai filled twenty-two jars
of water, and Nandai served as Govinda directed.” (CC, Adi 10.141–144) <!-- F16 -->

Among the Lord's servants in Puri Govinda was the chief: he served the Lord's meals, kept the stores and saw to
everything else, while Kashishvara, Ramai and Nandai worked under his direction. Ramai and Nandai carried water and did
other work; and they too had served Krishna before, in Vrindavana:

“The two, Payoda and Varida, who formerly prepared the water, are now the servants known as Ramai and Nandai.”
(“Gaura-ganoddesha-dipika” 139)

Govinda bathed the Lord and massaged His body; everything concerning Mahaprabhu was entrusted to him, and at the proper
time he served the Lord what the devotees had brought. One should not see in this the worldly work of a domestic: it is
the intimate service of an eternal associate, for whom nothing is higher than caring for the Lord's body.
<!-- F15 F17 F26 -->

The “Charitamrita” names four rasas that the Lord tasted with His companions in Nilachala:

“With Paramananda Puri Sri Chaitanya tasted the mood of parental love, with Ramananda Raya friendship, with Govinda and
others service, and the rasa of conjugal love with Gadadhara, Jagadananda and Svarupa Damodara. These four rasas gave
Him indescribable joy, and for this He felt Himself forever indebted to these devotees.” (CC, Madhya 2.78)

Srila Bhaktisiddhanta explains that Govinda and others were devoted to the Lord's service and possessed pure *dasya* —
a servant's love, free of any other motive; it was by this pure dasya-rasa of Govinda, he writes in the “Anubhashya,”
that the Lord was conquered. <!-- F45 Q4 -->

When the Lord went for darshan of Jagannatha, Kashishvara cleared the way for Him and Govinda followed:

“Kashishvara went first, clearing a path through the crowd, and behind Sri Chaitanya, holding a vessel of water, came
Govinda.” (CC, Madhya 12.207)

With this water the Lord washed His feet before entering the temple. So it was every day:

“Every day, when Sri Chaitanya went for darshan of Lord Jagannatha, Govinda accompanied Him with a jug of water. To the
north of the Lion Gate, behind a leaf of the gate, beneath the twenty-two steps, there was a small hollow. There Sri
Chaitanya washed His feet and then went for darshan of Lord Jagannatha. Mahaprabhu instructed Govinda: ‘See that no one
takes the water with which I wash My feet.’” (CC, Antya 16.40–43)

In his drama Karnapura says: behind the Lord comes Govinda, “guarding His playful gait” (“Chaitanya-chandrodaya,” act 10,
verse 41), and in his mahakavya he sees him with a water jug in hand beside Damodara. <!-- F18 Q9 -->

Yet to Kalidasa, Raghunatha Dasa's uncle, the Lord, knowing his faith in the Vaishnavas, allowed three handfuls of this
water, and only then stopped him: “That is enough — I have fulfilled your desire” (Antya 16.45–49). That same day,
returning from darshan and having taken His meal, He gave him still greater mercy:

“Kalidasa was waiting at the outer gate. Knowing this, Mahaprabhu made a sign to Govinda. Govinda understood all of
Mahaprabhu's signs, and he gave Kalidasa the plate with the remnants of the Lord's meal. Such is the greatness of
honouring the remnants of the Vaishnavas: by it Kalidasa obtained Sri Chaitanya's highest mercy.” (CC, Antya 16.55–57)

In the same chapter the Lord, having tasted prasada, ties the rest into the end of Govinda's cloth (16.92), and later at
the Lord's sign Govinda brings prasada, which the Lord sends to Paramananda Puri and Brahmananda Bharati (16.105).
<!-- F19 Q10 -->

## The Lord's Remnants for the Vaishnavas
Through Govinda the Lord sent His remnants every day to Haridasa Thakura, who lived in seclusion nearby. As soon as the
Gauda devotees arrived, the Lord Himself placed prasada in Govinda's hands and told him to take it to Haridasa (CC,
Madhya 11.206); and when, on the day of the cleaning of the Gundicha temple, the Lord called Haridasa to the common meal,
Haridasa answered, “Govinda will bring me prasada later” (Madhya 12.162). And so it was:

“Govinda set aside the remnants of Sri Chaitanya's meal and kept them, and later took part of this mahaprasada to
Haridasa. All the devotees begged for even a little of this food. The very last remnants Govinda honoured himself.” (CC,
Madhya 12.201–202) <!-- F44 -->

He also brought Haridasa the mahaprasada of Jagannatha. On Haridasa's last day Govinda came to him as usual:

“One day Govinda went to Haridasa Thakura and with great joy gave him the mahaprasada of Lord Jagannatha. There he saw
Haridasa lying on his back, very slowly chanting Krishna's holy names. ‘Please get up and eat,’ Govinda asked. ‘Today I
am fasting,’ Haridasa answered. ‘I have not yet completed my vowed number of Krishna's names — how can I eat? But you
have brought mahaprasada; how can I neglect it?’ With these words he offered his respects to the mahaprasada and ate a
single grain.” (CC, Antya 11.16–20) <!-- F21 -->

When Sri Rupa came to Puri, Govinda brought him and Haridasa prasada from the Lord at the feast in the Aitota garden
after the cleaning of the Gundicha temple (CC, Antya 1.64), and, according to one of the biographies, the Lord sent His
remnants to them both through Govinda every day. To Sanatana Gosvami, too, the Lord sent prasada through Govinda (Antya
4.50), and in the Yameshvara garden Govinda gave Sanatana a plate with the remnants of the Lord's meal (4.121).
<!-- F46 F48 -->

Govinda fed the poor as well. After the Hera-panchami festival the Lord had prasada distributed to the needy:

“At Sri Chaitanya's order the compassionate Govinda called together all the destitute and the beggars and fed them all
with prasada.” (CC, Madhya 14.44)

The Lord moved them to chant the name of Hari, and, repeating “Haribol,” they attained love of God (14.45–46) — the
greatest fortune, bhakti. <!-- F22 -->

He also kept the stores that Raghava Pandita sent every year from Panihati. Govinda stacked the bags in a corner of the
dining room, clearing away those of the year before (CC, Antya 10.55–56), and the Lord ate from them all year long. In
the same way, according to one of the biographies, he carefully kept the stores of Raghava's sister Damayanti and gave
the Lord a little of them at each meal. <!-- F25 -->

## Welcoming the Devotees from Gauda
When in the first year Advaita Acharya and the other devotees came from Bengal for the Ratha-yatra, the Lord sent two
to meet them — Svarupa Damodara and Govinda. King Prataparudra was watching the procession from the palace roof, and
Sarvabhauma named the newcomers for him:

“Svarupa Damodara and Govinda met them with prasada and flower garlands. Seeing these two messengers of Mahaprabhu, the
king asked their names. The Bhattacharya said: ‘This is Svarupa Damodara; he is called a manifestation of Mahaprabhu's
own body. And the other is Govinda, Sri Chaitanya's personal servant. Mahaprabhu has sent them both to welcome the
devotees who have arrived and to give them prasada and flower garlands.’” (CC, Madhya 11.74–77)

Svarupa was the first to place a garland on Advaita Acharya, and then Govinda:

“Then Govinda fell flat before Advaita. Advaita had not met him before, and so he asked Svarupa Damodara about him.
Damodara said: ‘His name is Govinda. He has many good qualities and formerly served Ishvara Puri. His guru sent him to
Puri to serve Sri Chaitanya, and so the Lord has kept him by His side.’” (CC, Madhya 11.79–81)

In Karnapura's drama, too, Damodara introduces him to Advaita: “This is Govinda, who attends upon the Lord,” and
Govinda offers Advaita a garland; and, entrusting young Shankara, Damodara Pandita's brother, to Svarupa, the Lord says:
“Govinda, you too be kind to him” (act 8). In his mahakavya Karnapura tells how Govinda brings the arriving devotees
garlands from the Lord and His instruction at the Narendra lake (14.67–82). Govinda-deva also writes that Govinda and
Damodara led the Bengalis along the road while Gopinatha Acharya pointed them out to the king one by one
(“Gaura-krishnodaya” 11.37). And Vrindavana Dasa, among those who joyfully went out with the Lord to meet the Gaudiya
devotees, names “Govinda, the Lord's faithful doorkeeper” (CB, Antya 8.56–61). <!-- F20 F60 -->

He welcomed the Gaudiya devotees in other years too. In Karnapura's drama Svarupa and Govinda announce Shrikanta's
arrival to the Lord, and the Lord sends Govinda with a prasada garland to meet the Bengalis; at the Ratha-yatra, when
three rings of singers circle round the Lord, “the most excellent Govinda” is in the middle one (“Chaitanya-chandrodaya,”
act 10, verse 46). And in the third year Govinda went to meet them alone:

“Learning that the devotees had already reached the Atharanala bridge, Sri Chaitanya sent His servant Govinda to meet
them with two garlands. Govinda adorned Advaita Acharya and Sri Nityananda with the flowering garlands, and both of Them
felt great happiness.” (CC, Madhya 16.38–39) <!-- F60 -->

## To Gauda and Back
When the Lord set out from Puri for Vrindavana by way of Gauda, Govinda went with Him. Krishnadasa Kaviraja names him
among the Lord's principal companions on this journey — with Paramananda Puri, Svarupa Damodara, Jagadananda, Mukunda,
Kashishvara, Haridasa Thakura and others (CC, Madhya 16.127–129). In Karnapura's drama Sarvabhauma says that only five or
six went with the Lord: Puri-ishvara, Damodara, Jagadananda, Gopinatha and Govinda (act 9); and in the mahakavya the
Lord is accompanied by Govinda, Jagadananda, Damodara Pandita and Paramananda Puri (19.6–9). <!-- F23 -->

When the Lord, having visited Vrindavana, returned to Nilachala by way of Kashi, the devotees ran out to meet Him at the
Narendra-sarovara lake; Govinda was among them (CC, Madhya 25.218–221), and together with the others he received the
Lord's embrace. <!-- F24 -->

## Stepping over the Lord
Once, when the Bengali devotees had come to Nilachala as every year for the four months of the rains, the Lord danced
in their kirtan at the temple of Jagannatha. Seven groups sang around Him, and He danced till noon; when Nityananda told
Him that all the singers and dancers were very tired, the Lord ended the kirtan, bathed in the ocean with His companions
and took prasada with them (CC, Antya 10.66–81). And then:

“The Lord lay down right in the doorway of the Gambhira, a small room without windows. Then Govinda came, wishing to
massage His feet. Over a long time a fixed rule had been established between them: after taking prasada Sri Chaitanya
rested, and Govinda massaged His feet and afterwards honoured the remnants of the Lord's meal. This time Sri Chaitanya lay
so that He blocked the whole doorway. Unable to enter the room, Govinda made a request to the Lord: ‘Please turn on Your
side and let me into the room.’ But Sri Chaitanya answered him: ‘I have no strength even to move.’” (CC, Antya
10.82–86) <!-- F27 F28 -->

One of the biographies says that the Lord merely pleaded great pain. Here lies the key to the whole story: “no strength
to move” is not worldly weakness but the play of the Lord, who Himself arranged this test.

“Again and again Govinda repeated his request, but each time Sri Chaitanya answered that He could not move. Then Govinda
said again: ‘O Prabhu, let me massage Your feet.’ Lord Chaitanya replied: ‘Do as you like. You may do it or not.’ Then
Govinda covered Sri Chaitanya with His own outer cloth and entered the Lord's room, stepping over Him. Govinda massaged
first Sri Chaitanya's legs and then His back. By this gentle massage all the Lord's fatigue went away. While Govinda
massaged Sri Chaitanya, the Lord slept peacefully and woke after about fifty minutes.” (CC, Antya 10.87–91)
<!-- F29 F30 -->

On waking the Lord saw that Govinda was still sitting beside Him and had not eaten, and He reproached him — not for
having stepped over Him, but because His servant had gone hungry:

“When Sri Chaitanya saw Govinda beside Him, He asked him angrily: ‘Why are you sitting here so long today? Why did you
not go to eat after I fell asleep?’ Govinda answered: ‘You were blocking the way, and I could not get out.’ The Lord
asked: ‘How then did you come in? And why did you not go out the same way to take prasada?’ Govinda thought: ‘My duty is
to serve. I will do all I must, even if I have to commit something forbidden and go to hell for these offences. In
serving You I pay no heed even to millions of such offences; but I fear even the shadow of an offence that I might
commit for my own sake.’ So Govinda thought, but aloud he gave no answer to Sri Chaitanya's question.” (CC, Antya
10.92–97)

Usually, when the Lord fell asleep, Govinda went to eat; but that day, seeing how tired the Lord was, he stayed on
massaging His body, and there was no way out: to step over the Lord for his own sake he considered a great offence
(Antya 10.98–99). Krishnadasa Kaviraja concludes:

“Such are the subtleties of Vaishnava etiquette described in the scriptures of bhakti. All these matters can be
understood only by the mercy of Chaitanya Mahaprabhu. The Lord always wishes to show the exalted qualities of the
Vaishnavas; that is why He arranged this incident.” (CC, Antya 10.100–101)[^7]

And in the summary of pastimes in the last chapter he names this story so: “the Lord tested Govinda” (Antya 20.118).
<!-- F31 Q5 -->

Srila Bhaktisiddhanta in his “Anubhashya,” speaking of Govinda, singles out this incident: for the Lord's service
Govinda did not hesitate to step over His body, yet for his own sake he feared to offend.[^8] There is neither audacity
nor familiarity in this act: Govinda reverently covered the Lord with His own cloth and stepped over Him only because
there was no other way to serve. One of the biographies explains the meaning of the story in the words of the
“Charitamrita” itself on the nature of pure love:

“The bliss of the ashraya of love lies in the bliss of its vishaya; there is no connection with the desire for one's own
happiness. Wherever love is unconditional, this is the law: the joy of the ashraya lies in the happiness of the vishaya.
If the bliss of one's own love hinders the bliss of serving Krishna, the devotee is filled with great anger at that
bliss.” (CC, Adi 4.199–201)

The ashraya is the one who loves, the vishaya the beloved. Krishnadasa Kaviraja says this of the love of the gopis; the
biography applies the same law to Govinda. The true devotee seeks only the Lord's joy and does not prize even his own
pleasure in serving. Therefore for the sake of service he does not dread even an offence, but for his own sake he fears
even its shadow. This intimate subtlety of bhakti will not be understood by those who seek enjoyment in this world or in
heaven; only pure devotees understand it — and here, says the biography, one sees how holy was Govinda's nature.
<!-- F32 Q6 I1 -->

## The Food Kept for a Month
The Lord gladly accepts whatever His devotees bring Him with love. In Dvaraka Krishna snatched a handful of flat rice
from His childhood friend, the poor brahmana Sudama, with whom He had studied under Sandipani Muni, and ate it; He Himself
says:

“A leaf, a flower, a fruit or water — whatever one pure of heart offers Me with devotion, that offering brought with love
I eat.” (“Srimad-Bhagavatam” 10.81.4; “Bhagavad-gita” 9.26)[^9]

So too Mahaprabhu in Navadvipa snatched from Shuklambara Brahmachari a handful of raw rice gathered by begging and ate
it, saying: “I eat your broken rice, and I will not even look at the nectar of a non-devotee” (CB, Madhya
16.124–127).[^10] The Lord is ready to repay His devotees for all they bring Him. Of this the “Charitamrita” tells in
the same tenth chapter of the Antya-lila. <!-- F33 I2 Q7 -->

“When the Vaishnavas first arrived from Bengal in Jagannatha Puri, each of them wanted to treat Sri Chaitanya
Mahaprabhu with something of his own. Bringing some prasada, each gave it to Govinda and said: ‘Let Sri Chaitanya be sure
to taste this offering of mine.’ <…> Govinda brought this prasada to Sri Chaitanya, telling who had sent what. As a rule
the Lord did not eat those dishes, but simply asked that they be kept for later.” (CC, Antya 10.107–110)

So much food accumulated that it could have fed a hundred people. And the devotees kept asking Govinda whether the Lord
had liked their offering:

“Not knowing how to answer them, Govinda was forced to tell untruths. Unable to go on doing so, in despair he turned to
Sri Chaitanya: ‘Advaita Acharya and the other devotees send You through me so many different dishes. But You do not
even taste them, and the devotees keep asking me about it. How long must I go on deceiving them? Please free me from
this responsibility.’” (CC, Antya 10.113–115) <!-- F34 F35 -->

One of the biographies says that Govinda answered evasively, to please the givers. This is the anguish of a servant
standing between the Lord's will and the devotees' love: he can neither disobey the Lord nor grieve the Vaishnavas, and
so he himself begs the Lord to free him from this burden. And the Lord answered:

“Sri Chaitanya replied: ‘You have lived with Me so long — why then do you grieve now? Quickly bring here everything they
have given you.’ Saying this, Sri Chaitanya sat down. Then Govinda began to bring Him all these dishes, naming those who
had brought them.” (CC, Antya 10.116–117)

Govinda named the givers one after another: here are the dishes of Advaita Acharya, here those of Shrivasa Pandita,
Acharyaratna and Acharyanidhi, here those of Vasudeva Datta, Murari Gupta and Buddhimanta Khan, of Shrimana Sena,
Shrimana Pandita and Nandana Acharya, here those of the people of Kulinagrama and Khanda (10.118–123). And the Lord with
delight ate everything that had accumulated over the month — food for a hundred people:

“Though the coconut sweets, the drinks and the sweet balls had been kept for more than a month, by Sri Chaitanya's mercy
none of them had lost its freshness or its wonderful taste.” (CC, Antya 10.125–126)

Only Raghava Pandita's bags remained untouched; these the Lord opened the next day (10.128–129). <!-- F36 -->

Govinda-deva retells this incident in his own way. Over the month the offerings filled the Lord's inner room:

“Then the servant of the best of Puris [Govinda], troubled again and again by this accumulation, said to the Lord in
private with a smile: ‘Lord, what is to be done with these stale foods?’ Hearing his words, Hari replied: ‘No, gentle one
— like Brahman, it undergoes no change: it is food cooked by Indira [for] Achyuta and gathered for the meal of the
brahmins.’ Saying this, He — ah! — ate it all — rice, cakes, sweet rice, drinks, vegetables, frothy sweets and the rest —
the Great-bellied One, asking Govinda, ‘Whose is this? What is this?’” (“Gaura-krishnodaya” 17.38–40)

One of the biographies remarks that such a thing is possible only for God. It is not a miracle for its own sake:
prasada is spiritual and does not spoil, and the Lord, conquered by His devotees' love, tastes their love together with
their offerings. <!-- F62 -->

## “Never Leave Me”
Govinda always kept close to Mahaprabhu, to give Him whatever He needed at the right moment, and went with Him
everywhere. Once the Lord was going to the temple of Yameshvara and heard a devadasi, a servant of Jagannatha, singing
Jayadeva's “Gita-govinda.” Absorbed in Radha's feelings, He was not aware who was singing and, hearing only a song about
Krishna, rushed straight toward the sound through the thickets:

“The Lord did not notice the thorns piercing His body mercilessly. Behind Him ran Govinda at full speed. Running fast,
Sri Chaitanya had almost reached the singing girl, but Govinda caught Him firmly in his arms and cried: ‘It is a woman
singing!’ Hearing the word ‘woman,’ Sri Chaitanya came to Himself and turned back to His former path. Then Sri Chaitanya
said: ‘O My Govinda! Today you have saved My life, for to touch a woman would have been like death to Me. From now on I am
in your debt, and I do not know how I can ever repay it.’ But Govinda answered: ‘It was not I who saved You, but Lord
Jagannatha.’ Sri Chaitanya said: ‘My dear Govinda, please never leave Me. Everywhere and always you must take care of Me
and protect Me from every danger.’” (CC, Antya 13.82–87) <!-- F41 -->

There is nothing worldly in this story — neither in the Lord nor in the singer, whose song was a service to Jagannatha.
The Lord, absorbed in Radha's separation, ran toward a song about Krishna; and Govinda guarded His pastime as a
sannyasi, which the Lord Himself kept strictly to teach the world. The rescue of the Lord he humbly ascribed to
Jagannatha, and the Lord in return entrusted to him what had always been his life: never to leave Him. <!-- F37 -->

Once at darshan a woman from Orissa, unable to see Jagannatha in the crowd, climbed onto the Garuda pillar, setting her
foot on the Lord's shoulder. Govinda wanted to pull her down, but the Lord rebuked him:

“Sri Chaitanya said to him: ‘You are behaving like a barbarian! Why do you not let her see Lord Jagannatha? Let her look
to her heart's content.’” (CC, Antya 14.26)

Govinda was protecting the Lord, but the Lord saw in this woman such a thirst for darshan as, in His humble words,
Jagannatha had not granted Him Himself, and He offered prayers to her feet (14.27–30). <!-- F40 -->

In His last years the Lord was almost always, day and night, in divine madness (*divyonmada*) and was unaware of His
body. Now He rushed toward the sand hill Chataka-parvata, taking it for Govardhana in Vraja; now He ran to the sea,
seeing in it the Yamuna of Vrindavana. Govinda followed Him unfailingly, and when the Lord disappeared he searched for
Him with Svarupa Damodara and others. Once the Lord saw the rasa dance in a dream, and Govinda, seeing that He had not
risen at the usual time, woke Him (CC, Antya 14.21). <!-- F39 -->

When the Lord, repeating the “Bhagavatam” verse on Govardhana, ran toward Chataka-parvata, Govinda was the first to rush
after Him:

“Repeating this verse again and again, Sri Chaitanya ran to the dune with the speed of the wind. Govinda ran after Him
but could not catch up.” (CC, Antya 14.87)

And when the Lord fell to the ground in ecstasy, Govinda was at once beside Him:

“Shaking with this violent trembling, Sri Chaitanya fell to the ground. Govinda ran to Him at once. He sprinkled Sri
Chaitanya's whole body with water from his vessel and then began to fan Him with His outer cloth.” (CC, Antya 14.96–97)

At night Govinda slept at the door of the Gambhira. Once Svarupa and Govinda lay down at the door while the Lord loudly
chanted Krishna's names all night; when His voice fell silent they opened the door — the Lord was gone, though all three
doors remained locked. He was found by the Lion Gate, unconscious, His body elongated (CC, Antya 14.58–64). Another night
the Lord, hearing Krishna's flute, likewise found Himself outside the locked doors:

“Meanwhile Govinda, no longer hearing Mahaprabhu's voice, opened the doors and called Svarupa Damodara.” (CC, Antya
17.13)

The Lord was found among the cows from Telinga by the Lion Gate. And once, having put the Lord to rest, Svarupa and
Govinda lay down at the door of the Gambhira, and in the night, lighting a lamp, they went in and, seeing His face, which
in separation He had rubbed against the walls, were deeply grieved (CC, Antya 19.56, 19.61). <!-- F38 -->

## Through Govinda
Through Govinda the Lord gave His orders, and through him the devotees approached the Lord. When Kamalakanta Vishvasa,
Advaita's servant, had slighted his guru's honour in a letter to the king, the Lord said:

“He ordered Govinda, ‘From today on, do not let that madcap Vishvasa come here.’” (CC, Adi 12.36) <!-- F42 -->

When the Lord refused to see King Prataparudra, Nityananda asked Govinda for one of the Lord's outer cloths and sent it to
the king through Sarvabhauma (CC, Madhya 12.36). And when after the Ratha-yatra the Lord fell before the king, neither
Nityananda, lost in bliss, nor Kashishvara, nor Govinda and the others were beside Him: they were elsewhere (Madhya
13.183).[^11] In Lochana Dasa, Prataparudra in the Lord's garden implores Govinda: “How may I see the Gosani's feet?” —
and he answers: “King, do not be distressed: you will not see Him here — now is not the time” (CM, Shesha-khanda, part 6,
verses 87–89). <!-- F43 F63 -->

When the junior Haridasa begged rice for the Lord from a venerable woman, the Lord, on returning home, told Govinda:

“‘From now on My house is closed to the junior Haridasa. Do not let him come here any more.’” (CC, Antya 2.113)

One of the biographies adds that this order troubled and saddened Govinda, who knew how dear Haridasa was to the
Lord.[^12] When the devotees began to plead for Haridasa, the Lord wished to go to Alalanatha and live there in
seclusion “with Govinda alone,” and He called him (2.132–133). And later Govinda, Svarupa, Jagadananda, Kashishvara and
others, coming to the ocean, heard Haridasa singing without seeing him, and Govinda and the others wondered whether he had
become a brahma-rakshasa (2.153–156). <!-- F47 -->

When Raghunatha Dasa came to Puri, the Lord entrusted him to Svarupa and told Govinda to take loving care of him:

“Govinda gave him a plate with the remnants of Sri Chaitanya's food, and Raghunatha honoured this mahaprasada with great
joy. So Raghunatha remained in Svarupa Damodara's care. For five days Govinda fed him with Sri Chaitanya's
mahaprasada.” (CC, Antya 6.212–213)

On the sixth day Raghunatha stopped taking the prasada and began to beg at the Lion Gate, and it was “the servant
Govinda” who told the Lord (6.221). Raghunatha himself never addressed the Lord directly — only through Svarupa or Govinda
(6.230); from Govinda the Lord learned of the changes in his austerities (6.282, 6.321), and it was Govinda who, at
Svarupa's instruction, gave Raghunatha sweets worth eight kauris for the Govardhana-shila (6.305). <!-- F49 -->

On the day Vallabha Bhatta invited the Lord, the Lord sent Svarupa Damodara, Jagadananda and Govinda to fetch Gadadhara
Pandita (CC, Antya 7.150). <!-- F50 -->

To invite the Lord to a meal usually cost four panas (three hundred and twenty) kauris, since Govinda and Kashishvara ate
with Him (CC, Antya 8.38). But when Ramachandra Puri reproached the Lord for eating much, the Lord called Govinda:

“‘From now on I shall limit My food to a quarter pot from the temple of Jagannatha and vegetables worth only five
gandas. If you take any other food for Me, you will not see Me here any more.’” (CC, Antya 8.51–52)

Govinda told the devotees, and for them it was like a thunderbolt. From then on the Lord ate only half of this small
portion and gave the rest to Govinda, and both ate half of what they had before; Govinda and Kashishvara the Lord allowed
to take prasada elsewhere (8.53–59). <!-- F51 -->

When young Shrikanta Sena, Shivananda's nephew, bowed to the Lord without removing his upper shirt, Govinda told him that
it should be taken off before bowing; but the Lord stopped him: Shrikanta had come in distress — let him do as he
pleased (CC, Antya 12.37–38). And after the meal the Lord gave Govinda this instruction:

“‘As long as Shivananda Sena's wife and children stay here in the town, they must be given a plate of the remnants of
My meal every day.’” (CC, Antya 12.53) <!-- F52 -->

Jagadananda Pandita's gifts, too, passed through Govinda. He brought a jar of fragrant sandalwood oil from Gauda and left
it with Govinda; Govinda told the Lord that the oil was a remedy for disorders of bile and wind. The Lord told him to give
it to the temple for the lamps; ten days later Govinda reminded Him again, and the Lord answered in anger that for this
He would have to hire a masseur — and Govinda fell silent (CC, Antya 12.104–115). When Jagadananda, having broken the jar,
stopped eating, the Lord Himself came to him and asked him to cook Him a meal; after eating, the Lord left Govinda
behind to tell Him whether Jagadananda had eaten. Jagadananda sent Govinda to massage the Lord's feet, fed Ramai,
Raghunatha Bhatta, Govinda and Nandai, and himself ate the Lord's remnants; Govinda reported this — and the Lord fell
asleep (12.144–151). <!-- F53 Q11 -->

Another time Jagadananda made a quilt and a pillow for the Lord, gave them to Govinda and asked Svarupa to persuade the
Lord to lie on them:

“Sri Chaitanya asked Govinda, ‘Who made this?’ And hearing Jagadananda's name, the Lord became a little afraid. Sri
Chaitanya at once asked Govinda to take away the quilt and pillow, and lay down as before on dry banana bark.” (CC,
Antya 13.11–12) <!-- F54 -->

When Raghunatha Bhatta came to Puri, the Lord asked Govinda to find him lodging (CC, Antya 13.104); according to one of
the biographies, He told Govinda both to give him prasada and to put away carefully the gifts his mother had sent.
<!-- F55 -->

The “Bhakti-ratnakara” tells how the childless brahmana Chaitanya Dasa came to the Lord at the Lion Gate with his wife
Lakshmipriya. The Lord accepted him and told Govinda to arrange darshan of Jagannatha for him without hindrance. The
devotees could not understand what the brahmana had prayed for:

“The next day they all said to Govinda: ‘We cannot understand what this brahmana prayed for.’ Govinda says: ‘There is a
mystery in this; by the Lord's will it will surely be revealed.’ At that very time the Lord called Govinda and, absorbed
in bhava, speaks in a deep voice: ‘This brahmana came desiring a son; a son named Srinivasa will be born to him. Through
Sri Rupa and others I shall reveal the shastras of bhakti, and through Srinivasa I shall distribute the jewels of the
books. Srinivasa is the embodiment of My pure prema; seeing him, all will rejoice at heart.’” (“Bhakti-ratnakara”
2.118–123) <!-- F56 Q13 -->

## In Other Original Sources
Vrindavana Dasa Thakura, opening chapters of the “Chaitanya-bhagavata,” more than once glorifies the Lord as “the Master
of the doorkeeper Govinda” (CB, Adi 10.2, 13.2; Madhya 6.6; Antya 7.5) — the doorkeeper who guards the Lord's rest. In
the Antya-khanda “the supremely fortunate servant Govinda” informs the Lord that Vaishnavas are waiting at the door and,
with His permission, lets them in (CB, Antya 9.195–196).[^13] <!-- F59 -->

Karnapura in his mahakavya shows him in many scenes: with Kashishvara and Kashi Mishra he drinks in the Lord's dance with
his eyes (16.33); after the bath he offers the Lord fresh clothing (17.43–52); in the evening the Lord goes with him once
more to see Jagannatha (17.59–62); and in the description of the Lord's daily round Govinda, with Svarupa, accompanies Him
to darshan, carries the jug, and on his report the Lord receives visitors (18.17–45). <!-- F61 -->

Murari Gupta mentions how Govinda with Kashishvara and others serves the Lord in His divine madness with kirtan
(4.24.13). Raghunatha Dasa Gosvami in the “Shachisunu-ashtaka” glorifies the Lord's feet, which are worshipped by “the
pure service arising from Govinda” (2). In a pada of Narahari, Govinda is with the Lord at sankirtan by the sea
(“Padakalpataru” 2241). The late “Prema-vilasa” recalls that the Lord forbade Govinda to give anyone the water from His
feet (16.261). In Lochana Dasa the Lord sends Govinda to see who is standing at the garden door — and there is Vibhishana
in the form of a brahmana (CM, Shesha-khanda, part 6, verses 48–51); Lochana also names “Sri Govinda” among those
weeping on the day of the Lord's disappearance (verse 94+30).[^14] <!-- F64 -->

Krishnadasa Kaviraja names him, too, among those who stayed to live with the Lord in Puri:

“Gadadhara Pandita, Vakreshvara, Damodara, Shankara and Haridasa Thakura remained to live with the Lord in Puri. Also
there came to live Jagadananda, Bhagavan, Govinda, Kashishvara, Paramananda Puri and Svarupa Damodara.” (CC, Madhya
1.252–253)

With them, and with Govinda among them, the Lord went every morning for darshan of Jagannatha (Madhya 15.184).

## After the Lord's Disappearance
When the Lord withdrew from the world, Govinda lived in the deep sorrow of separation. Soon young Srinivasa came to
Nilachala, sent by Narahari Thakura from Shrikhanda; he had learned of the Lord's disappearance on the way. In Puri he
found the devotees in an ocean of grief:

“Receiving leave, Srinivasa, with tears in his eyes, went to see Govinda and Shankara. Arriving, he sees the two sitting
in solitude, withered in separation from Gauranga — swaying in the wind. Srinivasa falls to the ground before both; both
lifted Srinivasa and embraced him. With troubled hearts they said many things; on hearing of those sorrows stones melt.”
(“Bhakti-ratnakara” 3.188–191)

Seeing Srinivasa weep, they comforted him and told him to go and see everyone (3.192–193). One of the biographies adds
that Govinda embraced Srinivasa with great love, seeing in him the future chief preacher of Mahaprabhu's teaching, and
that after this he did not live long.[^15] When and how he entered the Lord's eternal pastimes is not known. His sorrow
is not worldly grief but the separation of an eternal servant from his Master, that same vipralambha in which Mahaprabhu
spent His last years. <!-- F57 -->

The memory of his service was kept by later generations. According to one of the biographies, Gopala Guru as a child
served Mahaprabhu under Govinda's direction;[^16] and the “Bhakti-ratnakara” tells how Gopala Guru, showing Narottama the
house of Kashi Mishra, said: “Here Mahaprabhu slept, and Sri Govinda massaged His feet” (8.382–392). <!-- F58 -->

## Significance
Govinda is Bhangura, an eternal servant of Krishna, and in Gaura's pastimes the one to whom the Lord entrusted the care
of His body. The Lord Himself acknowledges that with him He tasted the sweetness of service (*dasya*), and Srila
Bhaktisiddhanta calls his dasya pure. <!-- F3 F45 -->

In his coming to the Lord, Mahaprabhu taught a lesson about the guru. Accepting His guru's servant, He honoured him as
an elder and yet accepted his service, because the guru had so ordered: everything connected with the guru is worthy of
honour, and the guru's orders are not to be questioned. And in the same event the Lord showed that His mercy and the
mercy of His pure devotees do not look at birth: service out of love is incomparably higher than service out of duty and
reverence. <!-- F12 I3 I4 -->

In his service there is a model of pure love. For the Lord's service he did not fear to step over Him, while for his own
sake he feared even the shadow of an offence; he sought only the Lord's joy and did not prize even his own pleasure in
serving. The Lord Himself arranged this incident to reveal to the world the qualities of a Vaishnava. <!-- F31 I1 -->

And the month's store of food, which the Lord ate at one sitting while Govinda named the givers, shows something more:
the Lord gladly accepts whatever is brought to Him with love, and answers love with love. Govinda, standing between the
Lord and His devotees, was for them the one through whom their love reached the Lord, and the Lord's mercy reached them.
