# Madhu Pandita
*Мадху Пандит · @madhu-pandita*

## The Servant of Gopinatha
Of the three Lords of Vrindavana whom the Gaudiya Vaishnavas worship, Sri Gopinatha is the One who stands at Vamshivata
and with the sound of His flute calls the gopis to the rasa dance. Madhu Pandita served Him, and in that service lies all
we know of his life. The sources call him Sri Madhu Pandita and Srila Madhu Pandita; Narahari Chakravarti in the
“Bhakti-ratnakara” calls him “the adhikari of Sri Gopinatha,” that is, the one entrusted with the Deity's service and
care, and in the “Narottama-vilasa” “Sri Madhu Pandita Gosvami.”[^1] <!-- F1 -->

Of his worldly life almost nothing is known. The sources name neither his family and lineage, nor the year and day of
his appearance, nor when and where he left this world; nor are his days found in the Vaishnava calendar of the Sri
Chaitanya Saraswat Math. Neither the “Chaitanya-charitamrita” nor the “Chaitanya-bhagavata” speaks of him; the Vaishnava
vandanas call him a servant of Lord Sri Chaitanya, but whether he met Mahaprabhu in person the sources do not say.
All that we know of him belongs to the Vrindavana of Jiva Gosvami's time.[^2] <!-- F2 -->

He was a disciple of Gadadhara Pandita. The “Bhakti-ratnakara” says so plainly: “Sri Madhu Pandita, the adhikari of
Sri Gopinatha, is known as the disciple of Gadadhara Pandita” (13.319). Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati, in his
“Anubhashya” on the “Charitamrita” (Adi 12.87), following the “Shakha-nirnaya” of Yadunandana Dasa, names him fifth
among the additional branches of Gadadhara's tree; his shripat, he writes, is the village of Saimbona, two miles east of
Khardaha, and it was he who installed the famous Deity of Gopinathadeva in Vrindavana and served Him.[^3] The same bond
with Gadadhara is remembered in the “Shakha-nirnayamrita” of Yadunandana Dasa: Madhu Pandita is a disciple of Sri
Gadadhara Pandita and a resident of Vrindavana; “I bow to Sri Madhu Pandita, the great soul, full of sweet love,
devoted to prema, intoxicated with rasa in Vrindavana” — so the “Gaudiya-vaishnava-abhidhana” quotes its stanza (34).

## The Three Lords of Vrindavana
According to tradition, Vajranabha, Krishna's great-grandson, with the help and by the grace of the sage Shandilya,
installed three Deities in Vrindavana — Madana-gopala, Govinda and Gopinatha — and with Them Vrinda-devi and the
Shiva-linga of Gopishvara. In time They disappeared together with the dhama itself: the Lord is not subject to time, and
His forms appear and withdraw by His own will. The “Narottama-vilasa” passes on another tradition as well: Jiva Gosvami
explained to Narottama that the face of Govinda, the chest of Gopinatha and the feet of Madana-mohana, seen together,
make up a single form of Krishna (2.269–270). <!-- F6 -->

Later, empowered by Sri Chaitanya and at His direct command, Sanatana Gosvami manifested Madana-gopala from Mahavana,
and Rupa Gosvami manifested Sri Govindadeva from the Yogapitha near Goma-tila in Vrindavana.[^4] The third to appear was
Gopinatha, and He appeared to Madhu Pandita. <!-- F7 F7a -->

Krishnadasa Kaviraja begins the “Chaitanya-charitamrita” with obeisances to these three Deities:

“All glory to the merciful Sri Sri Radha and Madana-mohana! I am lame and dull of mind, and They are my only refuge;
Their lotus feet are all I have.” (CC, Adi 1.15)

“I remember Sri Sri Radha and Sri Govindadeva, seated on a throne in a beautiful temple of jewels beneath a
wish-fulfilling tree in the radiant forest of Vrindavana, served by Their dearest companions.” (CC, Adi 1.16)

“May the beautiful Sri Gopinatha, who begins the sweet rasa dance, be our good fortune. Standing on the bank by
Vamshivata, He draws the gopis to Himself with the sounds of His flute.” (CC, Adi 1.17)

“These three Deities have made the Gaudiya devotees Their own. I worship Their feet: these Three are my Lords.”
(CC, Adi 1.19)

The words about lameness and dullness of mind are the humility of Kaviraja Gosvami himself, not a statement about Madhu
Pandita.[^5] Madana-mohana is the Lord of *sambandha*, knowledge of the soul's relationship with Krishna; Govindadeva is
the Lord of *abhidheya*, the path of service; Gopinatha is the Lord of *prayojana*, the highest goal, the prema of the
gopis whom He calls to the rasa with His flute. So Srila Bhaktisiddhanta explains these verses in the “Anubhashya.” By
granting the Gaudiya Vaishnavas the right to serve Them, these three Deities made them Their intimates, as the
“Amrita-pravaha-bhashya” of Srila Bhaktivinoda Thakura explains: service is the sign that the Lord has accepted the
servant as His own. To Madhu Pandita fell the service of the Deity of the highest goal.

## Gopinatha at Vamshivata
How Gopinatha appeared, the “Bhakti-ratnakara” tells in the words of Chaitanya Dasa addressed to his son Srinivasa.
Having spoken of Madana-gopala, he says:

“My son Srinivasa, what more is there to say? The Lord revealed Himself through His devotees. Paramananda Bhattacharya
Mahashaya and Sri Madhu Pandita are abodes of virtue. Krishna, the son of the king of Vraja, is subject to the love of
both; their doings are unfathomable — who is able to tell of them? Near Vamshivata there is a most beautiful place;
there Gopinatha plays in great merriment.” (“Bhakti-ratnakara” 2.473–476) <!-- F10 Q2 -->

And he cites a stanza of the “Sadhana-dipika”:

“By him (Madhu Pandita) was manifested Gopinatha, the ocean of mercy, at Vamshivata, on the auspicious bank of the
beautiful Yamuna.” (“Bhakti-ratnakara” 2.477) <!-- F9 Q1 -->

“Suddenly [Gopinatha] mercifully gave him darshan, and Sri Madhu Pandita received the right to serve [Him].” (2.479)

Madhu Pandita did not “find” the Deity by his own power: Krishna, conquered by the love of His devotees, of His own
mercy revealed Himself to him and entrusted him with His service.[^6] Beside him was Paramananda Bhattacharya — the two
of them conquered Krishna by their love, and, as the narrator goes on, Paramananda loved Madhu Pandita dearly. “O
Srinivasa,” says Chaitanya Dasa, “what joy there is in Vrindavana at the darshan of Gopinatha — who can tell?” With the
radiance of His body Gopinatha steals the hearts of all, and hundreds of thousands of people run to have His darshan;
beautiful and sweet is the life of Sri Gopinatha, and whoever hears of it even once, his sufferings go far away
(2.480–483).[^7] <!-- F8 F11 F12 F13 F17 Q3 I1 -->

## With Radha and Ananga-manjari
In Vraja Madhu Pandita manifested the service of Gopinatha, the Lord of the highest goal, together with Sri Radha, the
daughter of Vrishabhanu, and Sri Jahnava — Ananga-manjari, Radha's younger sister. This is the eternal lila of the Lord
and His eternal companions, and there is nothing mundane in it. Other associates of Gaura manifested the same service
of Gopinatha: Abhirama at Khanakula-Krishnanagara in Gauda-mandala, and Gadadhara Pandita, Madhu Pandita's guru, in
Purushottama-dhama in Kshetra-mandala, likewise with Radha and Ananga-manjari.[^8] <!-- F16 -->

## In the Vrindavana of Jiva's Time
When Srinivasa came to Vrindavana, Jiva Gosvami brought him to Gopala Bhatta and Radha-ramana and introduced him to
Lokanatha, Bhugarbha, Paramananda and Madhu Pandita. The “Bhakti-ratnakara” describes their meeting before Gopinatha:

“Then Sri Jiva, taking Srinivasa, went for the darshan of Gopinatha. Srinivasa, at the darshan of Sri Gopinatha, lost
his composure; streams flowed from both his eyes. There Sri Paramananda and Sri Madhu Pandita — all rejoiced on seeing
Srinivasa. The affection they showed cannot be described.” (“Bhakti-ratnakara” 4.361–364) <!-- F18 -->

Later Srinivasa was to carry the books of the Gosvamis to Gauda; the departure was set for the shukla-panchami of the
month of Agrahayana. At Jiva's word he received the command and blessing of Raghunatha Dasa, Gopala Bhatta, Bhugarbha,
Lokanatha, Madhu Pandita and other acharyas. He came to take leave of Gopinatha as well. Jiva said to Madhu Pandita and
the others, “May Srinivasa's journey be free of obstacles.” Sri Madhu Pandita made this known to Gopinatha and brought
Srinivasa a garland, the sign of the Lord's permission (*ajna-mala*; “Bhakti-ratnakara” 6.431–432). And when the
mahantas gathered in the temple of Govinda to see Srinivasa off, among them were “Sri Madhava, Sri Paramananda
Bhattacharya, Sri Madhu Pandita, whose life is wondrous” (6.511); Srila Bhaktisiddhanta, too, cites this stanza in the
“Anubhashya” (on CC, Madhya 18.49–52). <!-- F19 F19a F19b -->

In the same way Jiva brought Narottama to Gopinatha. The “Narottama-vilasa” relates:

“Thus, speaking of many things in prema, they entered the temple of Sri Gopinatha. [Sri Jiva] informed Sri Madhu Pandita
Gosvami: ‘Narottama has come from Gauda today.’ Narottama fell at the Gosvami's feet, and he, greatly delighted, took
him on his lap. Drenching Narottama with the streams from his eyes, he spoke to him with such tenderness as I cannot
tell. He gave him darshan of Radha-Gopinatha and, bringing a garland-prasada, gave it to Narottama.”
(“Narottama-vilasa” 2.271–275)[^9] <!-- F20 -->

When Jahnava-devi came to Vrindavana, the heads of the Vaishnavas went out to greet her — Jiva, Gopala Bhatta,
Lokanatha, Bhugarbha and Madhu Pandita. At Akrura-ghata, at her request, Parameshvari Dasa named those who approached,
pointing to each with his hand: “…this is Sri Madhu Pandita, this is the renowned Jiva” (“Bhakti-ratnakara”
11.100–106).[^10] <!-- F21 -->

He also met Nityananda's son Virachandra (Virabhadra) when he came to Vrindavana: among the mahajanas of Vraja who went
out to receive him, the “Bhakti-ratnakara” names Madhu Pandita — “the adhikari of Sri Gopinatha” — and his godbrother
Bhavananda, “whose great joy is the service of Gopinatha” (13.319–320).[^11] Madhu Pandita was joined to Bhavananda and
to Paramananda Bhattacharya by friendship and by their shared service of Gopinatha. <!-- F22 -->

## In the Prayers of the Vaishnavas
Madhu Pandita's name entered the vandanas, the hymns of praise to the Lord's associates. The vandana attributed to Jiva
Gosvami says, “I praise the Pandita named Madhu,” and “Purushottama Brahmachari and the Pandita named Madhu, servants of
Lord Sri Chaitanya, merciful great souls”; Devakinandana writes, “I praise Sri Madhu Pandita”; the vandana in the
tripadi metre says, “Purushottama Brahmachari and Sri Madhu Pandita, the two noble servants of Chaitanya, I praise.”[^12]
Narahari Dasa, in his song listing the Lord's associates, strings his name together with others: “Kavichandra,
Vidyanidhi, Sri Madhu Pandita and all the others dear to Gaura — of these jewel-names I shall make a necklace.”
<!-- F23 F24 -->

Narahari Chakravarti, the author of the “Bhakti-ratnakara,” in the “Namamrita-samudra,” his prayers to the Lord's
associates, calls on him together with Kashi Mishra and Gangadasa:

“O Sri Madhu Pandita, Kashi Mishra, Gangadasa! I place my hope in your feet — do not leave me without hope.”
(“Namamrita-samudra” 34)

## Significance
Madhu Pandita's life is almost wholly hidden from us, and one thing in it stands open: his service. A Deity is not found
by one's own will: the Lord Himself reveals Himself to those whose love has conquered Him and entrusts them with His
service. Gopinatha at Vamshivata, the Lord of the highest goal of Mahaprabhu's teaching, appeared to Madhu Pandita, the
disciple of Gadadhara Pandita, and so accepted him as His own. And in the accounts of the Vrindavana of Jiva's time we
see him before Gopinatha: tenderly he takes Narottama on his lap, on the Lord's behalf he blesses Srinivasa with a
garland, together with the heads of the Vaishnavas he receives Jahnava and Virachandra. <!-- I1 I2 I3 F11 F15 F16 -->
