# Rasikananda Deva Gosvami
*Rasikananda Deva Gosvami · @rasikananda*

## Shyamananda's Foremost Disciple
Shyamananda Prabhu — a disciple of Hridaya Chaitanya of Ambika, trained in Vrindavana under Srila Jiva Gosvami —
returned to Utkala (Orissa) and in the lands bordering Bengal gained countless disciples. The most eminent and the
closest of them all was Rasikananda. His biographers honour him as Rasikananda Deva Gosvami or Sri Rasikananda Deva.
<!-- F1 F4 -->

He also bore a second name, Murari. Narahari Chakravarti in the “Bhakti-ratnakara” (15.27–28) says that in Rayani
lived the illustrious son of Achyutananda, who had two names, Rasikananda and Murari, and was learned in the
scriptures from childhood. Hence the name by which he is most often known — Rasika-Murari.[^1] <!-- F2 F3 Q2 Q5 -->

## Homeland and Family
Rasikananda appeared in the year 1512 of the Shaka era (1590), in the village of Rayani (Rohini, Raini) in the
district of Medinipur, where the Suvarnarekha meets the Dolanga. According to one biography, it was the 18th of
Kartika, on the night of the Divali festival.[^2] The Suvarnarekha winds along the present border of Bengal and
Orissa, and in those days Medinipur belonged to the kingdom of Orissa. The land is ancient: according to the
“Bhakti-ratnakara,” in the village of Barajita, not far from Rayani, Ramachandra, the son of Dasharatha, lived for a
time in exile with Lakshmana and Sita, and there He installed the Shiva-linga Rameshvara. <!-- F5 F6 F9 F12 -->

His father, Raja Achyutananda (Achyutadeva), was the zamindar of Rohini; Rayani is called his capital. He was a
pious and generous landholder who kept strictly to dharma and cared for his subjects. The mother, Bhavani Devi, was
renowned as a faithful and devoted wife, and the whole family lived in bhakti. For a long time the raja had no son,
and he regarded his only child as a jewel granted by the mercy of the Lord of the universe, Lord Jagannatha. The boy
grew up a capable and loving servant of his parents and was their delight. <!-- F7 F8 F11 -->

By birth Achyutananda was an Oriya of the karana caste, which corresponds to the kayasthas among Bengalis. But a
Vaishnava is beyond the modes of material nature and is not judged by caste: as one biographer explains, father and
son were born among the karanas in order to bless that caste. <!-- F10 I4 -->

## Childhood
From his earliest years Rasikananda was an example of devotion to Krishna. His father invited learned pandits to
teach him, and the boy, as handsome as he was gifted, excelled in every subject. Yet worldly learning held no
attraction for him: whatever he studied, he found in it one conclusion — service to Hari. <!-- F13 -->

When he reached youth, his father arranged his marriage. According to the “Bhakti-ratnakara,” his wife was named
Ichchhamayi Devi (Ichchhadei); she was devoted to him and came from the village of Ghatshila (Ghantshila), also on
the Suvarnarekha — a place that, by tradition, remembers the Pandavas, who lived there in exile.[^3]

## A Prayer for the Guru
The young man's heart was seeking a true guru who would lead him on the confidential path. Even before they met,
Srila Jiva Gosvami, seeing off the cart of books from Vrindavana, foretold to Shyamananda that Murari Dasa, the son
of Achyutananda, would take shelter of him (“Prema-vilasa,” 13). <!-- F16 F53 -->

Once, while Rasikananda was living in Ghatshila, his wife's home, he withdrew to a solitary place to meditate and
fell into a deep trance. Then an unseen voice spoke to him: “Murari! Do not be anxious. Your guru is Shyamananda, a
great devotee; soon you will meet him here. Take shelter of him, and your life will be crowned with success.”[^4]
From that hour he joyfully chanted Shyamananda's name on his beads, kept his eyes on the road, and wept all night in
impatient longing. Toward the end of the night Shyamananda appeared to him in a dream: “Do not be anxious. Today
you will see me.” <!-- F17 F18 F19 F20 -->

At dawn Shyamananda himself arrived, travelling to preach. Tall, radiant as the sun, beads in hand on which he
ceaselessly counted the names of Krishna, his eyes full of tears of love, he was dancing in the ecstasy of prema and
singing the names of Chaitanya and Nityananda, surrounded by disciples (among them Kishora Dasa). Rasikananda fell
at his feet, prostrate in obeisance, and Shyamananda lovingly raised him and embraced him. <!-- F21 F22 -->

Rasikananda brought his guru to the royal house, washed his feet, honoured him with fragrances and flowers, and
surrendered himself, his family and his children at his feet. After the preliminary rites, on an auspicious day,
Shyamananda gave him — together with his wife — the mantra of Radha and Krishna and entrusted him to Sri Chaitanya
and Nityananda. <!-- F23 F24 -->

Why was the initiation into the worship of Radha and Krishna? Shyamananda's own guru, Hridaya Chaitanya, served the
Lord in the mood of friendship (sakhya-bhava). But in Vrindavana, in the company of the devotees of Vraja headed by
Srila Jiva Gosvami, Shyamananda embraced the mood of conjugal love (madhurya-bhava) — and in that mood he instructed
his disciple. <!-- F25 -->

This whole story, one biographer remarks, shows that sincere prayer itself brings one to the guru. <!-- I1 -->

## Beside His Guru
In Rasikananda's house a sankirtana was held, and all his friends and relatives were invited. The whole village sang
the holy names, came for Shyamananda's darshana and, struck by his explanation of the message of Gaura and
Nityananda, took shelter of him. Many received initiation, among them the Vedantist yogi Damodara; the
“Bhakti-ratnakara” (15.55–58, 15.62–66) tells of his conversion and of Shyamananda's other disciples. The festival
that Shyamananda held in Dharenda together with Rasika-Murari and Damodara is remembered to this day.
<!-- F26 F31 Q6 -->

Rasikananda himself travelled for a long time with his guru, never leaving his feet. He gave body, mind and soul to
serving him — and so he was soon recognised as Shyamananda's foremost disciple, and later became a mighty preacher
and a guru who gave initiation himself. One who becomes a good disciple becomes a good teacher: a guru may have
many “disciples” in name, but he passes his power only to one who has surrendered to him completely. Such was
Rasikananda. <!-- F27 I2 -->

The “Prema-vilasa” (19) preserves a touching detail: Shyamananda wore an invisible sacred thread and hid it whenever
Rasika came. Other early sources say that Narottama Thakura showed mercy to Rasika-Murari in Nrisimhapura
(“Narottama-vilasa,” 4), and that when messengers came from Vrindavana to Gauda, Murari Dasa washed their feet and
gave the narrator a hundred coins for the journey back (“Prema-vilasa,” 17). He is remembered in Narahari's songs
about Shyamananda as well, and the “Bhakti-ratnakara” (1) and the “Narottama-vilasa” (3) name him among
Shyamananda's disciples in Utkala. <!-- F53 F54 F55 F56 -->

According to the same sources, Rasikananda came from Utkala with Shyamananda to the great festival at Kheturi, and
Narottama put him in charge of caring for the guests; he was also present at the great assembly where Virabhadra
spoke, and at the farewell of the mahantas.[^5] <!-- F57 -->

## Service to Govinda
Shyamananda installed the Deity of Sri Govindadeva — Radha-Govinda — in Gopivallabhapura, near the border of Orissa
and Bengal, on the Bengal side. After the installation festival he left for Utkala, and the service of the Deity was
entrusted to Rasikananda.[^6] Rasikananda accepted it with the greatest care and gave himself to it entirely; the
devotees were captivated by this extraordinary service, which seemed to increase the beauty and joy of the Deities.
His followers serve Govinda in the proper way to this day. Only when he had set the service on a firm footing did
Rasikananda go out to preach. <!-- F28 F29 F30 -->

## Preaching
The fame of his preaching lives on in the villages of Orissa to this day. At the end of the sixteenth and the
beginning of the seventeenth century, the preaching of Gaudiya dharma by Shyamananda and Rasikananda-Murari
transformed the entire district of Balasore. At his guru's command Rasikananda carried the message of Sri
Gaurachandra for nearly forty-six years, and under his influence many agnostics and unbelievers became devotees of
Gaura and Nityananda. <!-- F32 F39 -->

Shyamananda empowered him so that he turned robbers, atheists, Muslims and other fallen souls to bhakti and gave
all of them the jewel of prema. The “Bhakti-ratnakara” (15.81–86) portrays him thus: with his followers he went
from town to town, accepting disciples — there is no counting the homes he visited; regardless of caste or creed he
delivered countless beings — robbers, blasphemers, unbelievers, yavanas and outcastes; ever intoxicated with
harinama-sankirtana, he was almost like a madman — and who, singing his virtues, would not be moved? The
“Prema-vilasa” (19) also says that he delivered many robbers and Muslims. <!-- F33 F34 F35 Q3 Q4 -->

It is told that once a wicked Muslim zamindar, wanting to silence him, set a maddened elephant upon him. By the
Lord's mercy Rasikananda made the elephant itself his disciple and engaged it in the service of Vishnu and the
Vaishnavas. The onlookers were amazed, and the zamindar himself came to him and surrendered.[^7] It is said that he
also helped tigers give up their predatory nature and turned them to devotion to Krishna. This was no display of
power: it was the Lord's mercy at work and the power with which his guru had invested him. <!-- F36 F37 -->

Kings, too, took shelter of him. His disciples included the Raja of Mayurbhanja (Orissa), Vaidyanatha Bhanja, drawn
by his spiritual power; Ganapati, the zamindar of Patashpura (Medinipur); Bhima and Shrikara, zamindars of Dharenda;
and Ahmad Beg, son-in-law of Ibrahim Khan, the governor of Orissa.[^8] <!-- F38 -->

## Writings
Rasikananda left several works. The chief of them is the “Shyamananda-shataka,” a life of his guru; later Baladeva
Vidyabhushana wrote a commentary on it. Also known are his “Bhakta-bhagavatashtaka” and
“Kunjakeli-dvadashaka.”[^9] <!-- F40 F41 -->

## Departure
Shortly before his departure, it is said, Rasikananda came with seven disciples to the village of Bansdaha
(Vamshadaha) near Jaleshvara. Along this very road Mahaprabhu Himself had once travelled to Puri with Nityananda:

“Having spent the night in Jaleshvara, the Lord set out at dawn together with the devotees. On the way to Vamshadaha
the Lord met a shakta sannyasi, who tried to instruct Mahaprabhu.” (CB, Antya 2.263–264)

From there Rasikananda and his companions went on to Remuna, singing the holy names all the way.[^10]
<!-- F43 Q1 -->

In Remuna he spoke of Krishna with the devotees there and instructed everyone always to worship Him. When they
entered the courtyard of the famous temple of Kshirachora-Gopinatha, Rasikananda approached the Lord, touched His
lotus feet — and disappeared into the Deity of Gopinatha, entering the Lord's eternal pastimes at His feet.[^11]
His seven disciples left their bodies at once. In the temple courtyard of Kshirachora-Gopinatha the pushpa-samadhi
(flower samadhi) of Rasika-Murari and his seven companions is honoured to this day. <!-- F44 F45 -->

This took place in 1652.[^12] According to our calendar, the disappearance day of Srila Rasikananda Deva Gosvami is
observed on Gaura-pratipad of the month of Govinda, the day after Shivaratri; in Remuna his departure is celebrated
every year for twelve days.[^13] Our calendar does not give the day of his appearance. <!-- F42 F46 -->

With Rasikananda's departure — together with those of Srinivasa, Narottama and Shyamananda — one biographer
associates the beginning of the third of the four “dark periods” of Bengal Vaishnavism. <!-- F47 -->

## Descendants and Successors
Rasikananda had three sons: Radhananda, Krishna-Govinda and Radha-Krishna. Some of his descendants still live in
Gopivallabhapura (Gopijana-vallabhapura). His preaching was carried on by his son Radhananda, his grandson
Nayanananda and others. Among his descendants was also Vishvambharananda Deva Gosvami, author of the well-known
“Astikya-darshana.” <!-- F48 F49 F50 -->

Through Rasikananda runs the disciplic succession of Baladeva Vidyabhushana in the line of Nityananda: Gauridasa
Pandita — Hridaya Chaitanya — Shyamananda — Rasikananda — Nayanananda — Radha-Damodara — Baladeva. Nayanananda was
Baladeva's parama-guru, while his direct guru was Sri Radha-Damodara, then head of the “Shyamasundara-kunja” matha
founded by Shyamananda in Vrindavana (“Siddhanta-ratna,” commentary on 8.35).[^14] <!-- F51 F52 -->

## Significance
Rasikananda is the model of a disciple who gave his whole being to his guru and so became the channel of his
power. His prayer brought his guru to him, and his service to Govinda and tireless preaching turned an entire region
to Gaura and Nityananda — from forest villages to royal courts. One biographer suggests that in Krishna's eternal
pastimes he serves as a manjari — but this is only the author's conjecture, not based on the
“Gaura-ganoddesha-dipika.” <!-- I3 -->
