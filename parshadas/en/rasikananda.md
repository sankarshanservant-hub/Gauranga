# Rasikananda Deva Gosvami
*Rasikananda Deva Gosvami · @rasikananda*

## Shyamananda's Foremost Disciple
Shyamananda Prabhu — a disciple of Hridaya Chaitanya of Ambika, trained in Vrindavana under Srila Jiva Gosvami —
returned to Utkala (Orissa) and in the lands bordering Bengal gained countless disciples. The most eminent and the
closest of them all was Rasikananda. His biographers honour him as Rasikananda Deva Gosvami or Sri Rasikananda Deva.
<!-- F1 F4 -->

He also bore a second name, Murari. Narahari Chakravarti writes in the “Bhakti-ratnakara” (15.27–28): in the village
of Rayani dwelt the renowned son of Achyuta, who bore two names, Sri Rasikananda and Sri Murari; people knew him as
Rasika-Murari, and from an early age he was versed in all the scriptures. It is as Rasika-Murari that he is most
often remembered.[^1] <!-- F2 F3 Q2 Q5 -->

## Homeland and Family
Rasikananda appeared in the year 1512 of the Shaka era (1590), in the village of Rayani (Rohini, Raini) on the
Suvarnarekha River. According to one biography, the village lies in the district of Medinipur, where the
Suvarnarekha meets the Dolanga, and he was born on the 18th of Kartika, on the night of the Divali festival.[^2] The
“Bhakti-ratnakara” (15.21–23) places Rayani in Mallabhumi and says that the Suvarnarekha flows northward there. The
river winds along the present border of Bengal and Orissa, and in those days Medinipur belonged to the kingdom of
Orissa. The land is ancient: according to the “Bhakti-ratnakara” (15.23–25), in Barajita, a village beside Rayani on
the bank of the Dolanga, Ramachandra, the son of Dasharatha, stayed for a time in exile with Lakshmana and Sita, and
there He installed the Shiva-linga Rameshvara. <!-- F5 F6 F9 F12 -->

His father, Raja Achyutananda (Achyutadeva), was the zamindar of Rohini; Rayani is called his capital. The
“Bhakti-ratnakara” (15.26) says that he, the ruler of that land, delighted in caring for his subjects and was pure in
conduct: he held strictly to dharma. The mother, Bhavani Devi, was renowned as a truly faithful wife, and the whole
family lived in bhakti. For a long time the raja had no son, and he regarded his only child as a jewel granted by the
mercy of the Lord of the universe, Lord Jagannatha. The son, in the words of the same book (15.29), was “most skilled
in serving his mother and father,” and he was their delight. <!-- F7 F8 F11 -->

By birth, as one biography says, Achyutananda was an Oriya of the karana caste, which corresponds to the kayasthas
among Bengalis; the “Prema-vilasa” (20.191), however, calls Rasikananda and Murari “two brahmanas.”[^3] But a
Vaishnava is beyond the modes of material nature and is not judged by caste: as the same biographer explains, father
and son were born among the karanas in order to bless that caste. <!-- F10 I4 -->

## Childhood
From his earliest years Rasikananda was an example of devotion to Krishna. His father invited learned pandits to
teach him, and the boy, as handsome as he was gifted, excelled in every subject. Yet worldly learning held no
attraction for him: whatever he studied, he found in it one conclusion — service to Hari. <!-- F13 -->

When he reached youth, his father arranged his marriage. The “Bhakti-ratnakara” (15.30–31) names his wife Ichchha
and says that she was rich in virtues; for a time he lived in the village of Ghatshila (Ghantshila) near the
Suvarnarekha — the place where, by tradition, the Pandavas rested during their forest exile.[^4] <!-- F14 F15 F58 -->

## A Prayer for the Guru
The young man's heart was seeking a true guru who would lead him on the confidential path. Even before they met,
Srila Jiva Gosvami, seeing off the cart of books from Vrindavana, said to Shyamananda: “Achyutananda's son, named
Murari Dasa, has set his heart on your shelter” (“Prema-vilasa,” 13.47). <!-- F16 F53 -->

The fullest account of their meeting is given in the “Bhakti-ratnakara” (15.32–39). One day in Ghatshila Murari sat
alone, wondering: whose disciple am I to become? And a voice came from the sky: “Do not worry: here you will become a
disciple of Sri Shyamananda.” Hearing this, Rasika-Murari rejoiced and began to repeat Shyamananda's name as a
mantra. His longing grew from moment to moment, and at the name of Shyamananda tears streamed from his eyes. He spent
almost the whole night in anxiety, and only toward morning did sleep overtake him. In a dream he saw Shyamananda,
wondrous in the beauty of every limb, who said to him with a smile, “At daybreak you will find me here,” and
vanished. Rasikananda was filled with great joy.[^5] <!-- F17 F18 F19 F20 -->

At daybreak the wise Rasika, saying nothing to anyone, began to watch the road. And then, a little way off,
Shyamananda appeared, travelling to preach. He came joyfully, surrounded by disciples — Sri Kishora Dasa and others —
shining like the sun, a smile on his face, holding beads on which he kept ceaseless count of Krishna's names, and,
beside himself with prema, swaying as he went, he sang the names of Krishna-Chaitanya and Nityananda. Rasika-Murari
fell to the ground, touching his feet, and Shyamananda lovingly raised and embraced him and bathed him with his own
tears. Rasikananda felt himself blessed and could not take his eyes from the beauty of his Prabhu (15.40–46).
<!-- F21 F22 -->

Rasikananda brought his guru to the royal house, washed his feet, honoured him with fragrances and flowers, and
surrendered himself, his family and his children at his feet. After the preliminary rites, on an auspicious day,
Shyamananda initiated him — according to one biography, together with his wife — into the mantra of Radha and
Krishna and, accepting him as a disciple, offered him at the feet of Nityananda and Chaitanya; and Rasika-Murari was
overwhelmed with prema, tears pouring ceaselessly from his eyes (15.47–49). <!-- F23 F24 -->

Why was the initiation into the worship of Radha and Krishna? Shyamananda's own guru, Hridaya Chaitanya, worshipped
the Lord as a friend (sakhya-bhava). But in Vrindavana, in the company of the devotees of Vraja headed by
Srila Jiva Gosvami, Shyamananda embraced the mood of conjugal love (madhurya-bhava) — and in that mood he instructed
his disciple. <!-- F25 -->

This whole story, one biographer remarks, shows that sincere prayer itself brings one to the guru. <!-- I1 -->

## Beside His Guru
Rasikananda brought his Prabhu to Rayani, and there all were plunged into an ocean of sankirtana joy. Friends and
relatives were invited; the whole village sang the holy names, came for Shyamananda's darshana and, struck by his
explanation of the message of Gaura and Nityananda, took shelter of him. Shyamananda stayed there some days and made
many disciples. At that time or soon after, the yogi Damodara received his mercy; the “Bhakti-ratnakara”
(15.55–58, 15.62–66) tells of his conversion and of Shyamananda's other disciples.[^6] The great festival that
Shyamananda held in the village of Dharenda together with Kishora, Murari, Damodara and others (15.60) is remembered
to this day. <!-- F26 F31 Q6 -->

“Who could describe with a single mouth the guru-bhakti of Sri Rasika-Murari?” exclaims Narahari Chakravarti.
Shyamananda tested Murari and, seeing how he conducted himself, was filled with great joy (15.51–52). Rasikananda
himself travelled for a long time with his guru, never leaving his feet. He devoted himself to serving him with body, mind and heart —
and so he was soon recognised as Shyamananda's foremost disciple, and later became a mighty preacher and a guru who
gave initiation himself. Whoever becomes a true disciple becomes a true teacher: a guru may have many “disciples” in
name, but he passes his power only to one who has surrendered to him completely. Such was Rasikananda.
<!-- F27 I2 F59 -->

The “Prema-vilasa” (19.105–109) preserves this incident. Once, when Shyamananda was in seclusion, Damodara came to
him and saw his form shining brilliantly and a radiant sacred thread on his body. Just then Rasika and other devotees
arrived and began to offer praises, and Shyamananda, concealing both the thread and his radiance, began
nama-sankirtana. Other early sources tell that Narottama Thakura showed mercy to Rasika-Murari in Nrisimhapura: at
the parting he stood to one side weeping, and Narottama embraced him with great affection (“Narottama-vilasa,”
4.229–230). When messengers came from Vrindavana, Murari Dasa washed their feet, and the guests said to each other,
“As the guru, so the disciple — we have never seen the like”; guru and disciple wept, rolling on the ground, and at
parting Murari Dasa gave the narrator a hundred coins for the journey back. “What a disciple of Shyamananda is Murari
Dasa! Nowhere have I seen such faith in the service of the Vaishnavas,” the narrator writes (“Prema-vilasa,”
17.126–143). <!-- F53 F54 F55 F56 F64 -->

He is remembered in Narahari's songs about Shyamananda as well, and the “Bhakti-ratnakara” (1.411) and the
“Narottama-vilasa” (3.96) name him among Shyamananda's disciples in Utkala. When Shyamananda returned from
Vana-Vishnupura to Utkala, “Sri Rasikananda and the others were filled with great joy” (“Bhakti-ratnakara,” 9.258).
<!-- F62 -->

According to the same sources, Rasikananda came from Utkala with Shyamananda to the great festival at Kheturi.
Narottama brought him and Shyamananda's other disciples together with everyone and said to him affectionately: “My
child, you will see to everything, so that no one is in any way slighted”; Rasikananda stood silent with joined
palms, deeming himself blessed. With Purushottama, Kishora and others he had the provisions brought from their own
land placed in Gauranga's storeroom and saw to the people who had come with them. When the Lord and His associates
manifested at the festival, Rasikananda lay unconscious on the ground with the others; and before the departure
everything that was to go with Shyamananda was entrusted to him (“Narottama-vilasa,” 6.157–174; 7.146; 8.352). He was
also present at the great assembly where Virabhadra spoke, and at the farewell of the mahantas.[^7]
<!-- F57 F63 -->

## Service to Govinda
The “Bhakti-ratnakara” (15.81) says that in Gopivallabhapura Shyamananda “poured down a rain of prema and entrusted
the service of Sri Govinda to Sri Rasika”; the “Prema-vilasa” (19.90) says the same. Shyamananda installed the Deity
of Sri Govindadeva — Radha-Govinda — in Gopivallabhapura, near the border of Orissa and Bengal, on the Bengal side.
According to one biography, after the installation festival he left for Utkala, and the service of the Deity was
entrusted to Rasikananda.[^8] Rasikananda accepted it with the greatest care and gave himself to it entirely; the
devotees were captivated by this extraordinary service, which seemed to increase the beauty and joy of the Deities.
Followers still serve Govinda there in the proper way. Only when he had set the service on a firm footing did
Rasikananda go out to preach. <!-- F28 F29 F30 -->

## Preaching
The fame of his preaching lives on in the villages of Orissa to this day. At the end of the sixteenth and the
beginning of the seventeenth century, the preaching of Gaudiya dharma by Shyamananda and Rasikananda-Murari
transformed the entire district of Balasore. At his guru's command Rasikananda carried the message of Sri
Gaurachandra for nearly forty-six years, and through him many agnostics and unbelievers became devotees of
Gaura and Nityananda. <!-- F32 F39 -->

Shyamananda empowered him so that he turned robbers, the godless, Muslims and other fallen souls to bhakti and gave
all of them the jewel of prema. The “Bhakti-ratnakara” (15.82–86) says: great was Rasikananda's influence; in his
mercy he delivered many pashandis and gave the jewel of bhakti to the yavanas; with his disciples he wandered from
village to village — and how many beings he delivered! Sri Rasikananda was ever intoxicated with sankirtana — and
who, singing his virtues, would not be moved?[^9] The “Prema-vilasa” (19.91–92) also says that Rasikananda's
greatness is boundless: he delivered many robber yavanas, and his disciples cannot be counted. <!-- F33 F34 F35 Q3 Q4 -->

It is told that once a wicked Muslim, wanting to silence him, set a maddened elephant upon him. The
“Bhakti-ratnakara” (15.84–85) tells it briefly: the elephant sent by the wicked man Rasikananda made his disciple and
engaged in the service of Krishna and the Vaishnavas, and the wicked yavana raja himself bowed before him. The
onlookers were amazed, and that man came to him and surrendered.[^10] This was no display of power: it was the Lord's
mercy at work and the power with which his guru had invested him. <!-- F36 F37 -->

Kings, too, took shelter of him. His disciples included the Raja of Mayurbhanja (Orissa), Vaidyanatha Bhanja, drawn
by his spiritual power; Ganapati, the zamindar of Patashpura (Medinipur); Bhima and Shrikara, zamindars of Dharenda;
and Ahmad Beg, whose father-in-law Ibrahim Khan governed Orissa.[^11] <!-- F38 -->

## Writings
Rasikananda left several works. The chief of them is the “Shyamananda-shataka,” a life of his guru; later Baladeva
Vidyabhushana wrote a commentary on it. Narahari Chakravarti quotes from it a verse that glorifies Shyamananda “as
the beloved disciple of Hridayananda and as the dear disciple's disciple of Sri Subala, the Lord's friend”
(“Bhakti-ratnakara,” 1.381; he does not name the author). Also known are his “Bhakta-bhagavatashtaka” and
“Kunjakeli-dvadashaka.”[^12] <!-- F40 F41 F61 -->

## Departure
Shortly before his departure, it is said, Rasikananda came with seven disciples to the village of Bansdaha
(Vamshadaha) near Jaleshvara. Along this very road Mahaprabhu Himself had once travelled to Puri with Nityananda:

“Having spent the night in Jaleshvara, the Lord set out at dawn together with the devotees. On the way to Vamshadaha
the Lord met a shakta sannyasi, who tried to instruct Mahaprabhu.” (CB, Antya 2.263–264)

From there Rasikananda and his companions went on to Remuna, singing the holy names all the way.[^13]
<!-- F43 Q1 -->

In Remuna he spoke of Krishna with the devotees there and instructed everyone always to worship Him. When they
entered the courtyard of the renowned temple of Kshirachora-Gopinatha, Rasikananda approached the Lord, touched His
lotus feet — and disappeared into the Deity of Gopinatha, entering the Lord's eternal pastimes at His feet.[^14]
His seven disciples left their bodies at once. In the temple courtyard of Kshirachora-Gopinatha the pushpa-samadhi
(flower samadhi) of Rasika-Murari and his seven companions is honoured to this day. <!-- F44 F45 -->

This took place in 1652.[^15] According to our calendar, the disappearance day of Srila Rasikananda Deva Gosvami is
observed on Gaura-pratipad of the month of Govinda, two days after Shivaratri; in Remuna his departure is celebrated
every year for twelve days.[^16] Our calendar does not give the day of his appearance. <!-- F42 F46 -->

With Rasikananda's departure — together with those of Srinivasa, Narottama and Shyamananda — one biographer
associates the beginning of the third of the four “dark periods” of Bengal Vaishnavism. <!-- F47 -->

## Descendants and Successors
Rasikananda had three sons: Radhananda, Krishna-Govinda and Radha-Krishna. Descendants of his live to this day in
Gopivallabhapura (Gopijana-vallabhapura). His preaching was carried on by his son Radhananda, his grandson
Nayanananda and others. Among his descendants was also Vishvambharananda Deva Gosvami, author of the well-known
“Astikya-darshana.” <!-- F48 F49 F50 -->

At Sripat Gopivallabhapura Vishvambharananda kept a collection of old manuscripts — among them copies of Srila Rupa
Gosvami's works and records of the Gosvamis' dates; in February 1925, following the direction of their gurus,
Vaishnavas searched for manuscripts there together with him — so the author of one biography recalls. <!-- F65 -->

Through Rasikananda runs the disciplic succession of Baladeva Vidyabhushana in the line of Nityananda: Gauridasa
Pandita — Hridaya Chaitanya — Shyamananda — Rasikananda — Nayanananda — Radha-Damodara — Baladeva. Nayanananda was
Baladeva's parama-guru, while his direct guru was Sri Radha-Damodara, then head of the “Shyamasundara-kunja” matha
founded by Shyamananda in Vrindavana (“Siddhanta-ratna,” commentary on 8.35).[^17] <!-- F51 F52 -->

## Significance
Rasikananda is the model of a disciple who gave his whole being to his guru and so became the channel of his
power. His prayer brought his guru to him, and his service to Govinda and tireless preaching turned an entire region
to Gaura and Nityananda — from forest villages to royal courts. Narahari Chakravarti sings of him in a song: the
merciful Rasika-Murari destroys the defilement of Kali, his spotless virtues captivate all hearts, his mind rests at
Shyamananda's feet, all his rasa is Gaurachandra, and his fame fills the three worlds; “there is no end to the deeds
of Sri Rasikananda; he is ever beside himself with the virtues of Prabhu Shyamananda” (“Bhakti-ratnakara,”
15.87–91). One biographer suggests that in Krishna's eternal pastimes he serves as a manjari — but this is only the
author's conjecture, not based on the “Gaura-ganoddesha-dipika.” <!-- I3 F60 -->
