# Gopinatha Pattanayaka
*Гопинатха Паттанаяк · @gopinatha-pattanayaka*

## One of Bhavananda's Five Sons
Gopinatha Pattanayaka was an Orissan devotee, a son of Bhavananda Raya and a brother of Ramananda Raya. Vaishnavas
remember his name through a single story, which Krishnadasa Kaviraja Gosvami tells in the ninth chapter of the
Antya-lila: a prince ordered him put to death, and Mahaprabhu, while seeming to do nothing at all, saved him from death.
Kaviraja calls him “Gopinatha Pattanayaka” (Adi 10.133), “Gopinatha Pattanayaka, Ramananda's brother” (Madhya 1.265;
Antya 9.17) and “the noble Gopinatha, Ramananda's brother” (Antya 9.73); the biographies call him Sri Gopinatha
Pattanayaka. The later “Vaishnava-vandana” of Devakinandana also has the line: “I praise Kalanidhi, Sudhanidhi and
Gopinatha.”[^1] <!-- F1 -->

Listing the branches of Sri Chaitanya's tree, Kaviraja recalls the words the Lord spoke to his father:

“Embracing him, the Lord said, ‘You are Pandu, and your sons are the five Pandavas: Ramananda Raya, Gopinatha
Pattanayaka, Kalanidhi, Sudhanidhi and Vaninatha Nayaka. These five sons of yours are dear to Me, and between Ramananda
and Me there is a difference of body alone.’” (CC, Adi 10.132–134)[^2] <!-- F2 F4 F5 F6 Q1 Q2 -->

Gopinatha is named here second, right after Ramananda, and the biographies conclude from this that he was the second of
the five brothers; Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati in the “Anubhashya” calls him Ramananda's younger brother. Together
the five brothers form a single branch of the tree — in the “Anubhashya,” the eighth: “the five brothers headed by Sri
Raya Ramananda.” <!-- F3 F6 -->

The Lord repeated the words “you are Pandu” at His first meeting with Bhavananda (CC, Madhya 10.53). This is not an
account of a former birth in the worldly sense: the Lord Himself revealed the eternal nature of His associates. He who
in Krishna's pastimes was King Pandu came into Gaura's pastimes together with his sons, and the five Pandavas became
the five sons of Bhavananda. Which of the Pandavas Gopinatha is, neither the “Chaitanya-charitamrita” nor the
“Gaura-ganoddesha-dipika” nor the biographies say; of the brothers, Kavi Karnapura identifies only Ramananda, with
Arjuna. <!-- F5 -->

## Family and Office
Bhavananda's family belonged to the karanas, the service community of Orissa, akin to the kayasthas of Bengal; the
“Anubhashya” names it more precisely: “shaukra-karana,” karana by birth. Bhavananda lived near the ancient shrine of
Alalanatha, or Brahmagiri, six kroshas (about twelve miles) west of Puri, and was a landholder in the neighbouring
village of Bentpur. His descendants live there to this day and bear the family title “Chaudhuri Pattanayaka.”
<!-- F7 F8 -->

Gopinatha served Maharaja Prataparudra. He was, as the “Chaitanya-charitamrita” says, a *raja-vishayi*, a keeper of the
king's property: he collected the revenue of the district of Malajathya-Dandapata and delivered it to the royal
treasury (CC, Antya 9.17–18); Srila Bhaktivinoda Thakura in the “Amrita-pravaha-bhashya” calls this office that of a
tahsildar. According to the biographies, Malajathya-Dandapata is present-day Medinipur.[^3] <!-- F9 -->

Nothing more is known of his worldly life. Neither the biographies nor the original sources give the year or day of his
appearance, nor when or where he departed from this world, nor his wife or children; nor are his days in the Vaishnava
calendar of the Sri Chaitanya Saraswat Math. <!-- F10 -->

## At the Lord's Feet
Gopinatha first saw Mahaprabhu when the Lord returned to Puri from His pilgrimage through South India:

“Just then Bhavananda Raya arrived and, with his four sons, fell at Mahaprabhu's feet.” (CC, Madhya 10.49)

Ramananda was still in Vidyanagara, and the four, according to the “Anubhashya,” were Vaninatha, Gopinatha, Kalanidhi
and Sudhanidhi. The “Gaura-krishnodaya” (11.6) likewise has him come “with four sons.” Bhavananda surrendered to the
Lord's feet himself, his house, his wealth, his servants and his five sons (Madhya 10.55), and the Lord replied: “You
are no stranger. Birth after birth you and your family have been My servants” (Madhya 10.58). The sons placed the
Lord's feet on their heads, and the Lord sent them home, keeping only Vaninatha with Him (Madhya 10.60–61).
<!-- F11 -->

Kavi Karnapura also saw that meeting. In the drama “Chaitanya-chandrodaya,” Sarvabhauma, presenting the devotees of Puri
to the Lord, says: “These are the brothers of Ramananda Raya” (act 8); and in his poem on the Lord, Karnapura writes:
“And his five sons too — Ramananda and the rest — remaining by the side of the great Merciful One and serving Him,
became truly most dear to Him” (“Chaitanya-charita-mahakavya” 13.135). The word “birth after birth My servants”
Gopinatha was soon to prove in his own life. <!-- F11 -->

## The Horses and the Prince
Once, when the accounts were settled, Gopinatha was found to owe arrears of two lakhs of kahanas of cowries. A kahana,
Srila Bhaktivinoda Thakura explains, is 1,280 cowrie shells, so that the debt came to more than two hundred and fifty
million shells.[^4] The king demanded the whole sum (CC, Antya 9.19). Gopinatha could not pay so much at once and
promised to clear the debt little by little by selling his property; and in part payment he offered horses: “I have ten
or twelve horses; take them at a fair valuation,” and he led them to the palace gate (CC, Antya 9.20–21).[^5]
<!-- F12 F13 -->

The king sent one of the princes, a judge of horses, to value them, together with ministers and companions. The prince
set the price far below the true one, and Gopinatha flared up (CC, Antya 9.22–23). The prince had a habit of turning
his neck again and again and glancing upward, and Gopinatha jibed: “My horse raises its neck, but it does not look
upward. So it is not right to set its price low.” Srila Bhaktivinoda gives the sense of the jibe plainly: my horse is
worth no less than you. He said it without fear, Kaviraja tells us, for the king had always favoured him (CC, Antya
9.24–26). <!-- F14 F15 -->

Stung, the prince went to his father and slandered Gopinatha: the man makes excuses and has no intention of paying; let
me raise him onto the *changa*, and the debt will be recovered. The king replied: “Go and do whatever is best. Use any
means by which I may get the money” (CC, Antya 9.27–29). The changa was a scaffold of execution: naked swords were set beneath it, and the
condemned man was thrown down onto them. The prince had Gopinatha raised onto the scaffold and the swords set below (CC,
Antya 9.30). This was Prataparudra's eldest son, the heir to the throne, Purushottama Jana, who was called Bara-Jana (CC,
Antya 9.13, 9.99, 9.104).[^6] The king himself, as it later turned out, knew nothing of the scaffold and the swords, and
the prince, by the king's account, only meant to frighten Gopinatha; but in the eyes of all who saw it, the threat was
mortal.[^7] <!-- F16 F17 F18 -->

## “What Can I Do?”
People well disposed to Gopinatha — his friends and servants — ran to Mahaprabhu, for he had no other shelter:

“The Bada Jana, the eldest prince, has raised Gopinatha onto the *changa*, the execution platform. They have set swords
beneath it and will throw him down onto them from above. Only if You, Lord, protect him will he be saved. Bhavananda
Raya and his whole family are Your servants. His son is Your servant, and it is fitting that You protect him.” (CC,
Antya 9.13–15) <!-- F19 -->

The Lord asked why he was being put to death, and they told Him the whole story (CC, Antya 9.16). Having heard it,
Mahaprabhu answered “with a show of affectionate anger,” taking no one's side: what fault is there in the king, if his
money is not paid? For he, the Lord said, collects the revenue in the king's name and, without fear of him, squanders it
on harlots and dancers; a sensible servant first pays the king what is due and spends on himself only what is left.
“The king himself will collect the money due on his accounts. I am a detached sannyasi; what can I do in this?” (CC,
Antya 9.31–35). Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati explains that here the Lord displays detachment and shows what the
dharma of a sannyasi is. <!-- F20 F21 -->

Then another messenger came running: Vaninatha and the other brothers had been bound and taken away with their
families (CC, Antya 9.34).[^8] Svarupa Damodara and the other devotees fell at the Lord's feet: “The family of Ramananda
Raya are all Your servants. Such indifference is not fitting for You” (CC, Antya 9.37). But the Lord answered angrily:
am I to go to the king, spread out the end of My cloth and beg him for money? A sannyasi is fit to receive at most five
gandas, twenty cowries; who would give Me two lakhs of kahanas? And when word came that Gopinatha was about to be thrown
onto the swords, He said: “I am a beggar; I can do nothing” (CC, Antya 9.38–42). A renunciant should not take up worldly
affairs, the “Anubhashya” explains — and the Lord, to teach people this, remains unbending. <!-- F22 F23 -->

And yet He showed them a way:

“So if you all have it in mind to save him, go together to the feet of Jagannatha. Jagannatha is Ishvara, the Lord
Himself, and all things are in His hands. He is able to do, not to do, or to do otherwise.” (CC, Antya 9.43–44)[^9]
<!-- F24 -->

This “indifference” is not coldness toward a devotee. The Lord Himself, as Kashi Mishra will later say, cannot bear it
when His own suffer (CC, Antya 9.97), and Bhavananda's family is under His protection. But, setting the example of a
sannyasi, He does not petition kings or meddle in worldly affairs, and His mercy works hidden, through others.
<!-- I1 -->

## Taken Down from the Scaffold
At the very hour when Mahaprabhu was saying this, Harichandana Patra, one of the king's men, went to Prataparudra and
told him everything in order.[^10] Gopinatha is your servant, he said; to put a servant to death is unworthy, and his
death will not bring your money back — better take the horses at their true price, and he will pay the rest little by
little (CC, Antya 9.45–48). The biographies see in this the prompting of Mahaprabhu Himself, who dwells in every heart
as the Supersoul; the “Chaitanya-charitamrita” says only that Harichandana went to the king while the Lord was
answering the devotees, but later Kaviraja says plainly that the Lord, without entreating either the king or Kashi
Mishra, effortlessly saved Gopinatha from death (CC, Antya 9.150).[^11] The king replied:

“I know nothing of this. Why should I take his life? It is his money I want. Go there and settle everything, so that the
money comes in and his life is spared.” (CC, Antya 9.49–50) <!-- F25 F26 -->

Harichandana conveyed the king's will to the prince, and Gopinatha was at once taken down from the scaffold (CC, Antya
9.51). Asked how he would pay the revenue, Gopinatha answered: “Take the horses at their proper value. The rest I will pay
little by little, whatever I can.” The horses were taken at a fair price, a date was fixed for the rest of the debt,
and he was sent home (CC, Antya 9.52–54). Thus by Mahaprabhu's mercy Gopinatha was saved, and Kaviraja himself, early
in the Madhya-lila, listing the Lord's pastimes, mentions this one:

“Gopinatha Pattanayaka, Ramananda's brother, was about to be put to death by the king, and the Lord became his
saviour.” (CC, Madhya 1.265)[^12]

In the “Anubhashya” this verse is headed: “The deliverance of Gopinatha, who had fallen under the king's wrath.”
<!-- F26 F27 F28 Q3 -->

Meanwhile Mahaprabhu asked the messenger what Vaninatha had been doing as they led him away bound. The man told Him that
Vaninatha was fearlessly chanting “Hare Krishna, Hare Krishna,” counting the names on the fingers of both hands and
marking each thousand with a line on his body. The Lord was supremely joyful:

“Who can fathom the mercy of Gaura, veiled beneath another guise?” (CC, Antya 9.58)

Mercy veiled beneath another guise — *kripa-chhadma*: the Lord seemed to refuse His help, yet deliverance came — through
the prayer to Jagannatha, through Harichandana and through the king. <!-- F29 Q4 I1 -->

## Kashi Mishra and the King
That same day Kashi Mishra, the royal priest and Prataparudra's guru, came to Mahaprabhu, and the Lord shared His
anxiety with him. Bhavananda's family, He said, serve the king and spend the king's money, and when the king demands
what is his, they come to Me; four times people have run to Me with news of the scaffold. “Today Jagannatha has
protected him; but tomorrow who will protect him if he does not pay the king's money?” Worldly news robs Me of peace,
and I shall go to Alalanatha to live there in solitude (CC, Antya 9.59–66). The Lord speaks thus as a
sannyasi, who should not listen to worldly talk: they teach detachment, but do not condemn His servants. <!-- F30 -->

Kashi Mishra fell at His feet and begged Him not to leave the Kshetra. One who serves You for worldly ends, he said, is
blind; but Gopinatha is not such a man:

“The noble Gopinatha, Ramananda's brother, has no wish for worldly gain from You. Seeing his distress, his servants and
others told You of it, for he has no shelter but You.” (CC, Antya 9.73–74)

Srila Bhaktisiddhanta heads these verses accordingly: Gopinatha is no merchant seeking his own profit from the Lord; he
has surrendered to Him completely. And Kashi Mishra concluded: “If You have a mind to protect him, He who protected him
today will protect him still” (CC, Antya 9.79). <!-- F31 -->

At midday, when Prataparudra came as usual to Kashi Mishra's house and was massaging his feet, Mishra told him that the
Lord meant to leave for Alalanatha, and explained why (CC, Antya 9.80–93). The king was pained to the heart:

“I give up all the money, if only the Lord will stay here. If I can have the Lord's darshana for even a moment, not even
millions of *chintamani* gems can equal it. And what are these two lakhs of kahanas? A paltry thing! My life and my
kingdom I offer at the Lord's feet in worship.” (CC, Antya 9.94–96) <!-- F32 Q8 -->

Kashi Mishra objected: the Lord does not wish the debt remitted — what He cannot bear is only that the whole family is
suffering; and if the debt is remitted for His sake, He may well be pained (CC, Antya 9.97, 9.101). Then the king asked
that the Lord be told that he was remitting it not for His sake, but because he loved Bhavananda's whole family in any
case and honoured Bhavananda himself (CC, Antya 9.102–103).[^13] <!-- F32 -->

Bara-Jana summoned Gopinatha, and the king said to him: your whole debt to the treasury is remitted; go on collecting
the revenue of Malajathya-Dandapata, but do not spend the king's money on yourself; from this day your salary is
doubled; go to Mahaprabhu, and with His leave, go home. And the king himself dressed him in the *neta-dhati*, a silk
cloth of honour (CC, Antya 9.104–107).[^14] Not only was the execution cancelled: Gopinatha's whole debt was remitted,
his salary doubled and his post restored. <!-- F32 F33 -->

Kaviraja Gosvami pauses here to say what matters most. Leave aside the Lord's highest mercy — its fruits are endless;
the royal wealth that Gopinatha obtained is the fruit of a mere glimmer of Chaitanya's mercy, and even that cannot be
reckoned. A moment before, Gopinatha had been raised onto the scaffold to be stripped of life and all he owned — and
suddenly his debt was remitted and his salary doubled. Yet the Lord had no wish at all that his debt be remitted, his
pay raised or his post restored: He had only been asked to save Gopinatha from death, and that request alone bore such
fruit. The Lord's intentions cannot be fathomed even by Brahma and Shiva (CC, Antya 9.108–115). <!-- F35 -->

When Kashi Mishra came to the Lord and told Him of the king's decision, Mahaprabhu exclaimed: “Kashi Mishra, what have
you done? Have you made Me accept a gift from the king?” (CC, Antya 9.117). Srila Bhaktisiddhanta explains that for a
sannyasi to accept the king's wealth (*rajartha-pratigraha*) is not permissible. But Mishra conveyed the king's words to
Him: all Bhavananda's sons are dear to me, I regard them as my own family and so have given them posts; Gopinatha, in
handling the king's business, usually kept two to four lakhs of kahanas, and I did not call him to account; this time
he fell out with the heir, and I did not know that my son meant to destroy him (CC, Antya 9.118–126). Hearing this, the
Lord was delighted by the king's humility (CC, Antya 9.127). These are the words not of a judge about a negligent
official, but of a sovereign about those close to him. <!-- F34 F36 -->

## The Shadow of the Fruit
Then Bhavananda Raya came to Mahaprabhu with his five sons, and all fell at His feet. The Lord raised Bhavananda and
embraced him, and Bhavananda said:

“My whole family are Your servants. Saving us in this danger, Lord, You have bought us again for a price. Now You have
shown Your affection for Your devotees, as long ago You delivered the five Pandavas from danger.” (CC, Antya 9.130–131)

He who in Krishna's pastimes was King Pandu here recalled how the Lord had protected his sons then too (CC, Antya
9.127–131). <!-- F37 -->

Then Gopinatha bowed at the Lord's feet, the king's silk cloth still on his head, and told how kind the king had been to
him:

“He remitted the money I owed and doubled my salary; he gave me my post again and dressed me in silk. On one hand, that
peril of death on top of the *changa*! On the other, silk cloth again — all this is Your grace! On the *changa* I
meditated on Your feet, and by the power of remembering Your feet I obtained this fruit. Seeing all this happen to me,
people are amazed; they sing and praise the glory of Your mercy.” (CC, Antya 9.133–136) <!-- F38 -->

Facing death, Gopinatha had remembered the Lord's feet. And now, when
life, honour and wealth had come back to him twofold, he was not beguiled by them:

“But this is not the chief fruit of remembering You. It is only a semblance of fruit (*phalabhasa*), for worldly things
are fickle. You made Ramananda Raya and Vaninatha free from worldly affairs. But I have not received the mercy that
brings this about! Show me pure mercy, Gosani: take away my worldly affairs. I have grown weary of them (*nirvinna*);
worldly affairs are not for me.” (CC, Antya 9.137–139)[^15] <!-- F39 Q5 -->

Srila Bhaktivinoda Thakura explains: the chief fruit of remembering the Lord's feet is love for Him, while life and
property preserved are only the shadow of that fruit, for everything worldly is unstable. Srila Bhaktisiddhanta adds:
seeing his worldly possessions grow and fearing to lose the happiness of service, Gopinatha prays for genuine mercy —
*amaya-kripa*. He asks not for wealth, but for freedom from it. <!-- F39 I2 -->

## “You Are My Eternal Servants”
Mahaprabhu answered him with a question: “If all five of you become sannyasis, who will maintain your large household?”
(CC, Antya 9.140).[^16] The outward dress of a sannyasi, Srila Bhaktisiddhanta explains, the Lord does not value highly:
He knew what Gopinatha was fit for and told him to serve Hari while remaining a householder. <!-- F40 -->

And the Lord spoke words addressed to all five brothers:

“Whether you manage great worldly affairs or live detached and indifferent to the world, birth after birth you five are
My own servants. But keep one order of Mine: do not spend any of the king's principal. After paying the king his
principal, spend whatever profit remains on various pious works. Do not spend on sinful things, by which both worlds
are lost.” (CC, Antya 9.141–144) <!-- F41 Q6 -->

“Birth after birth My servants” is not a chain of karmic births but the eternal service of an associate who comes with
the Lord whenever He comes. Srila Bhaktisiddhanta explains: one who knows himself to be the eternal servant of Bhagavan
is harmed neither by outward renunciation nor by great worldly affairs; having dedicated everything to Krishna, he
lives even at home in great renunciation — maha-sannyasa. Trouble comes only when the soul forgets this and begins to
enjoy for itself: then it loses both goods. <!-- F41 I3 -->

In parting, Mahaprabhu embraced them all, and the devotees went home calling out loudly, “Hari!” (CC, Antya 9.146).
<!-- F42 Q7 -->

Kaviraja calls this whole story the Lord's *kripa-vivarta* in the house of Bhavananda Raya, in which His affection for
His devotees was revealed (CC, Antya 9.145). Srila Bhaktivinoda explains the word: mercy in the form of worldly benefit
is not yet true mercy; it is only to the worldly mind that the one appears as the other. And Srila Bhaktisiddhanta sees
in this pastime the evidence that the Lord is conquered by His devotees. People were amazed and could not understand the
Lord's conduct: when they asked Him for help, He said He could do nothing, only censured Gopinatha and spoke of His own
detachment —

“He did not entreat Kashi Mishra, nor did He entreat the king; yet without any effort He gave all this fruit.” (CC,
Antya 9.147–150) <!-- F43 -->

Srila Bhaktisiddhanta unfolds the meaning of this pastime: the Lord does not agree to save; He reproves the dishonest
spending of money; He seeks solitude, setting an example for renunciants; Gopinatha's worldly possessions nonetheless
multiply; and Gopinatha fears those possessions, and the Lord teaches him to serve Krishna in any situation. Worldly
benefit is only a secondary mercy; and had the renunciant Gaurasundara busied Himself with the affairs of worldly
people, imitators would have degraded the ideal of the detached Vaishnava. <!-- F43 -->

## Significance
We know the life of Gopinatha Pattanayaka almost entirely from a single day, but that day held a whole teaching. A son
of Pandu in Krishna's pastimes and an eternal servant of Gaura, he found himself on a scaffold above the swords — and the
Lord, outwardly refusing to help, saved him in such a way that no one saw His hand: through the prayer to Jagannatha,
through Harichandana, through Kashi Mishra and the king's love. So was revealed the “mercy veiled beneath another guise”
of which the “Chaitanya-charitamrita” speaks. <!-- I1 F27 F35 -->

Gopinatha himself showed how a devotee receives mercy. Facing death he meditated on the Lord's feet and ascribed
his whole deliverance to Him alone; and when life, office and wealth came back to him twofold, he called them only the
shadow of the fruit and prayed for freedom from worldly things — such as Ramananda and Vaninatha had. <!-- I2 -->

The Lord's answer is addressed to all who serve Him in the world. One need not leave home to be His servant: the eternal
servant remains a servant both in great affairs and in renunciation, if he honestly pays what is due and spends the rest
on service and dharma, not on sin. <!-- I3 -->
