# Srivasa Pandita
*Шриваса Пандит · @srivasa*

## Foremost Among the Devotees
Srivasa Pandita, also called Srivasa Thakura, is one of the five who make up the Pancha-tattva, and within it he is the
first of the pure devotees. His name is renowned as that of a herald, the very “mouth” of the truth of pure devotion to
Gaura-Hari. Krishnadasa Kaviraja opens his “Chaitanya-charitamrita” with obeisances to the Lord's associates, and of
Srivasa he says:

“There are many saintly devotees of the Lord, but the chief among them is Srivasa Thakura. I humbly offer him thousands
of obeisances.” (CC, Adi 1.38) <!-- F117 F118 Q14 -->

Srivasa's house in Navadvipa was the stage for most of Mahaprabhu's Navadvipa pastimes. Here the Lord first revealed
His divine form to a devotee, here for a whole year the kirtana went on behind closed doors night after night, here took place
the Mahaprakasha and Nityananda's Vyasa-puja. And Srivasa himself, with his family, his servants and even the animals of
his household, shows to what kind of home the Lord's mercy comes: to a home given over to Him entirely. <!-- I1 -->

## Brothers from Shrihatta
Srivasa's family came from Shrihatta (Sylhet). There, as the “Chaitanya-bhagavata” says, his brother Srirama,
Chandrashekhara and Murari Gupta also appeared. There were four brothers: Srivasa; Srirama, that is, Rama Pandita,
affectionately called Ramai; Shripati; and Shrinidhi, who is also called Shrikanta. All four took part in the Lord's
pastimes, and the three younger ones lived with Srivasa in Navadvipa. Of their father we know only from a single
tradition: his name was Jaladhara Pandita, he was a Vedic brahmana, and the eldest of his sons was Nalina Pandita.[^1]
At first the brothers lived in Shrihatta; later, seeking the company of devotees, they moved to the bank of the Ganga, to
Navadvipa. <!-- F3 F4 F9 Q18 -->

Srivasa built a house by the Ganga, not far from the house of Jagannatha Mishra in Navadvipa-Mayapur. His wife's name
was Malini Devi. With them lived Narayani, the daughter of one of Srivasa's brothers.[^2] Widowed before her son was
born, she left the home of her husband, Vaikuntha Dasa Vipra, in Kumarahatta (Halisahar) and settled with Srivasa in
Navadvipa. Here her son grew up — born in 1507, the future Vrindavana Dasa Thakura, author of the
“Chaitanya-bhagavata” — and here from childhood he received the Lord's special mercy.[^3] Srivasa himself had a son, an
only son, who died as a child; of him we shall speak below.[^4] <!-- F6 F7 F8 F10 F12 F134 Q21 -->

The brothers, their servants and all their household gave themselves forever to the feet of Mahaprabhu. Gaurasundara was
everything to them, and they knew no other gods:

“These four brothers, their whole family and household served Lord Sri Chaitanya. Apart from Gaurachandra they worshipped
no other gods or goddesses.” (CC, Adi 10.11) <!-- F5 Q1 -->

Srivasa's appearance day is Krishna-ashtami, the eighth day of the dark fortnight of the month of Chaitra; so our
calendar also marks it, in the month of Vishnu. His disappearance day is Krishna-dashami of the month of Vamana, that is,
Ashadha. The biographies name neither the year of his appearance nor the year of his departure.[^5] <!-- F1 F2 -->

## Kirtana Before the Lord's Appearance
Before coming Himself, the Lord sends to earth His senior associates — those who will be His father, mother and
teachers. Krishnadasa Kaviraja names Srivasa among them (CC, Adi 13.52–55), and so it is said that Chaitanya, Advaita and
Nityananda came together with Srivasa and others to give the world hari-bhakti. Srivasa, Haridasa and others were sent
ahead of the Lord. <!-- F18 Q18 F125 -->

The world at that time was languishing without Krishna. No one understood who a Vaishnava was or why sankirtana was
needed; people attached to body and home, slaves of their senses, hungry for sons and wealth — the pashandis — mocked the
Vaishnavas. The four brothers grieved over this and did not sleep at night: they sang the name of Hari loudly in their
home. Three times a day, at the three sandhyas, they worshipped Krishna, and every day they bathed in the Ganga. In the
house by the Ganga, Srivasa, together with kindred souls — Advaita Acharya, Haridasa Thakura and others — sang the name of
Krishna, calling for the coming appearance of Gaura-Hari. <!-- F10 F13 F15 -->

The leader of the Navadvipa Vaishnavas then was Advaita Acharya, who had returned to Bengal: around him gathered Srivasa,
Chandrashekhara and Gangadasa. Advaita also had a house in Navadvipa, not far from Srivasa's, and there he would speak of
Krishna until dawn with the devotees headed by Srivasa. The brothers went to him to hear the “Bhagavatam,” to sing
nama-sankirtana and to help in the Vaishnavas' affairs; when Haridasa came to Navadvipa, Advaita, Srivasa and all the
others welcomed him with joy. All of them were close friends of Jagannatha Mishra, and by their devotion they sensed
that Krishna Himself would soon appear in his home. <!-- F14 F125 -->

Hindus hostile to the Vaishnavas decided that Srivasa would ruin the whole village: should the Muslim ruler, an enemy of
sanatana-dharma, learn of his kirtana, all Navadvipa would suffer. They conspired to tear down his house and drive the
brahmana out of town at once. Hearing of this, Advaita grew angry and made a firm vow: soon he would bring Krishna down to
Navadvipa to crush the pashandis. “Listen, Shuklambara, Gangadasa, Srivasa,” he said, “I shall bring Krishna before
everyone's eyes.” <!-- F16 F125 -->

Seeing the sufferings of souls in the age of Kali, the devotees prayed that God Himself would come. And Hari appeared —
in the house of Jagannatha Mishra in Mayapur, on the full moon of the month of Phalguna in the year 1407 of the Shaka era
(1486). Advaita understood Who had come, went to Srivasa and the others, and revealed to them the meaning of this
appearance. <!-- F17 -->

## Nimai's Childhood
When a son was born to Shachi, Srivasa in ecstasy bathed in the Ganga, arranged hari-sankirtana and gave away many
gifts. Malini, together with the wife of Chandrashekhara Acharyaratna, brought to Shachi's house sindura, turmeric, oil,
khai (puffed rice), bananas and other fruits, and performed the auspicious rites. <!-- F19 -->

Malini was Shachi's constant friend: they delighted in each other's company, and after the birth Malini tended Shachi
like a nurse. She always raised and cared for the boy tenderly, and in their love for Him she and Shachi were of one
heart. Srivasa, for his part, was at Jagannatha Mishra's house so often that he was called his host's shadow. Srivasa and
Malini helped to raise Nimai and taught Him much, and Gaurasundara honoured them as a second father and mother. <!-- F20 F21 -->

Then came the time of the pastimes of learning. Nimai would put tricky questions of logic to Srivasa and other
devotees, and Srivasa, seeing Him from afar, would hurry off another way. Mukunda, Srivasa and others who knew the taste
of bhakti avoided idle debates with Nimai Pandita; listening to Him argue with Gadadhara and Mukunda, the devotees only
sighed: “If only such a scholar would become a devotee!” They could not recognise their Lord in Nimai: what concealed
Him from them was the Lord's yogamaya, His lila-shakti, which for the sake of His pastimes enchants even those turned
toward Him.[^6] And Srivasa prayed to Krishna that love for Krishna would awaken in Nimai. <!-- F22 F23 -->

Nimai Himself, the Lord of the devotees, would bow to the ground on meeting Srivasa and other Vaishnavas, honouring the
devotees, and they would bless Him: “May You have devotion to Krishna.” The “Chaitanya-bhagavata” explains why He did so:

“The Lord respectfully accepted the blessings of the devotees, knowing that devotional service to Lord Krishna can be
attained only by the power of the Vaishnavas' blessings.” (CB, Adi 12.46) <!-- F24 Q4 -->

Srivasa often said to Him: “Why waste time on worldly learning? The essence of all knowledge is service to Krishna.”
Nimai would reply that He was fortunate, since the Vaishnavas wished Him well: He would teach a little longer, and then
go to a pure Vaishnava to learn devotion to Krishna. Once they met in the street. Nimai was walking with His students,
finely dressed, with tilaka on His forehead, His lips red with betel and a book in His hand. Srivasa could not hold back
a smile: “People study in order to know devotion to Krishna; without it, what is learning for? Do not waste time —
serve Krishna.” Nimai smiled in reply: “By your mercy I shall certainly serve Krishna” (CB, Adi 12.250–252). Thus, even in
the days of Nimai's schoolboy pranks, Srivasa instructed Him: the object of knowledge is Krishna and devotion to
Krishna. <!-- F25 F26 Q5 -->

## “I Would Wish Such a Disorder for Myself”
From Gaya, where Nimai received the mantra from Ishvara Puri, He returned changed: signs of intoxication with love of God
began to appear in Him. Shachi was frightened that her son had lost his mind — from outside it looked like an illness, a
“disorder of the airs” (vayu-vyadhi) — and sent for Srivasa and other Vaishnavas. Nimai was circling the tulasi; seeing
Srivasa, He remembered Krishna and fell unconscious. <!-- F27 -->

Srivasa understood at once that this was no illness but transcendental mahabhava: the desire of his heart had been
fulfilled. He comforted Shachi and asked her to tell no one what great love had manifested in her son. And Nimai asked
him: “Everyone says My humours are disordered. What do you think?” Srivasa laughed: “I would wish such a disorder for
myself! Strong feelings of devotion have awakened in You — this is Krishna's mercy.” The Lord embraced him: “Had you too
said that I was mad, I would have drowned Myself in the Ganga” (CB, Madhya 2.113–117). <!-- F28 Q6 -->

Now Nimai sang the name loudly day and night in His own house and in Srivasa's. The pashandis complained that they
could not sleep and reviled the devotees, and Srivasa most of all: “That brahmana Srivasa is behind it all! Four brothers
shouting for their bread.” They spread a rumour that by the king's order two boats were coming to seize the singers;
they proposed throwing Srivasa's house into the Ganga and binding him and handing him over to the authorities (CB,
Madhya 2.229–238). According to the “Chaitanya-mangala” of Lochana Dasa, a brahmana who wanted to frighten Srivasa and
stop the kirtana was bewildered by the Lord's maya. <!-- F29 Q6 -->

The biographies see a lesson in this: Srivasa as if deliberately drew the pashandis' abuse upon himself to teach that a
devotee endures the reproaches of society and neighbours, humbler than a blade of grass and more tolerant than a tree,
and does not give up kirtana, while the Lord always protects one who has surrendered to Him. <!-- I2 -->

## “Whom Are You Worshipping?”
Srivasa shut the door of his temple and began to worship Nrisimhadeva.[^7] The Lord, knowing his heart from within, came
to his house and began kicking the door of the room where the household Deity stood — the door flew open — and roared:

“Whom are you worshipping? On Whom are you meditating? Know that the One you worship now stands before you!” (CB, Madhya
2.258)

Srivasa's absorption was broken. Before him sat Gaura-Hari holding conch, disc, club and lotus in four hands, slapping
His left side and roaring. One biography says that He sat in the hero's posture (virasana) and showed the form of
Nrisimha with the four emblems of Vishnu; another, that He entered the room of the Vishnu Deity, boldly sat on the altar
and revealed His four-armed form. Srivasa was struck dumb with wonder. <!-- F30 F31 F32 Q7 F127 -->

“Your loud kirtana and Advaita's roaring,” said the Lord, “have brought Me from Vaikuntha together with My associates.
Because the devotees are persecuted I have left Vaikuntha. I shall protect the righteous and destroy the wicked — do not
worry. Have you still not understood that I have come? Recite My praises.” And again: “If you want fearlessness, sing My
sankirtana.” <!-- F33 F127 -->

Srivasa rose in love, joined his palms and glorified Vishvambhara to his heart's content with the prayer of Brahma from
the “Bhagavatam.” By one account, he fell prostrate and bowed to Vishvambhara, the son of Shachi, in yellow cloth, with a
garland of gunja and a peacock feather — “to Nimai, the student of Gangadasa” — and said that now his ancestors were
delivered and his life fulfilled. The Lord, pleased, wished to show His form to Srivasa's wife, his sons and all his
household, and told him to serve His feet with his wife and ask for blessings. Srivasa and his family worshipped
Vishvambhara's feet with everything they had prepared for the worship of Vishnu — fragrances, flowers, incense, lamps —
and surrendered themselves to Him. The whole household received mercy. <!-- F34 F35 F127 -->

Vishvambhara granted Srivasa complete fearlessness and promised that He would drive mad with love for Krishna even the
Muslim king, hostile to bhakti, together with his whole retinue. Then Srivasa — bold of speech, a “maha-vakta” — raised his
arms and said fearlessly: “I fear not even Time itself, which creates and destroys the world — what have I to fear in a
house where Gaurasundara lives eternally?” <!-- F36 F38 -->

Narayani, Srivasa's four-year-old niece, was there too. The Lord said to her, “Krishna, Krishna!” — and the girl began to
weep and fell senseless to the ground. The Lord gave her His prasada and blessed her with mercy, making her utter the
holy name. Seeing her tears, Srivasa's wife and all the devotees wept with love, and Srivasa's courtyard was filled with
prema. As the devotee is dear to the Lord, so the Lord is dear to the devotee. <!-- F37 I3 -->

That day in Srivasa's house the Lord gave darshana to all His eternal associates and devotees. The
“Chaitanya-bhagavata” concludes:

“That Lord Whom the personified four Vedas long to see revealed Himself even to the servants and maidservants of
Srivasa. What more can be said of Srivasa's magnanimity? The dust of his feet purifies the whole universe. Krishna
appeared in the house of Vasudeva, but displayed His wonderful pastimes in the house of Nanda Maharaja. In the same way,
though the Lord appeared in the house of Jagannatha Mishra, He performed His astonishing pastimes in the house of Srivasa
Pandita.” (CB, Madhya 2.331–334) <!-- F35 Q2 F64 -->

Lochana Dasa in the “Chaitanya-mangala” tells of other pastimes in this house. Once, when the Lord came to Srivasa and
his brothers, the devotees heard Krishna's flute; the Lord became absorbed in the mood of Radhika, laughing and weeping
in the madness of separation, and a divine voice said: “Vishvambhara, You are the Lord Himself, descended to preach love
for Krishna.” Another time the Lord showed here the form of Nrisimha and blessed Srivasa with mercy, and Srivasa
performed His abhisheka. Here too was performed the “gada-puja,” the worship of Vishnu's club, to invoke the Lord's power
against the godless. <!-- F39 -->

## Nityananda in Srivasa's House
One day Vishvambhara, having told of a dream in which He saw a great personality on the chariot of Baladeva, entered
the mood of Balarama and began to roar: “Wine! Wine!” Srivasa replied: “The wine of Hari-rasa is Yours alone — whoever You
give it to will receive it.” Nityananda meanwhile was living secretly with Nandana Acharya. Having learned of His coming
in a dream, Gaura sent Haridasa Thakura and Srivasa to search for Him:

“Go, Haridasa, go, Srivasa Pandita…” (CB, Madhya 3.160)

For three praharas they went round Navadvipa — the homes of Vaishnavas, sannyasis, householders — and did not find the
newcomer. Then Gaura Himself led everyone to Nandana's and revealed Nityananda to them.[^8] <!-- F40 F119 Q21 -->

At the Lord's sign Srivasa recited a verse of the “Bhagavatam” — “barhāpīḍaṁ naṭa-vara-vapuḥ…” (Bhag. 10.21.5): Krishna,
dressed as the best of dancers, with a crown of peacock feathers, enters the forest of Vrindavana. At once Nityananda
displayed wondrous transformations of love — He fell unconscious, and all eight sattvika-bhavas appeared in Him — and
Vishvambhara pressed Him to His chest. Srivasa begged Gaura to recite the verse again and again. Gaura and Nityananda
praised each other and conversed in signs, and Srivasa said: “Just as people cannot understand how Hari and Hara, Madhava
and Shankara, worship each other, so we cannot understand how Gaura and Nityananda glorify each other.” <!-- F41 F42 F119 Q22 -->

Gaura proposed to Nityananda that He perform Vyasa-puja. Nityananda understood His hint, took Srivasa by the hand and
said: “The Vyasa-puja will be in this brahmana's house.” Srivasa gladly took everything upon himself: he prepared the rite
with fervour and obtained the manual “Vyasa-puja-paddhati.” A few days later, behind closed doors, the adhivasa-kirtana
began. On the night of the adhivasa Mahaprabhu entered the mood of Baladeva, revealing Nityananda's oneness with
Balarama, and called to Advaita: “Nara, Nara!” That same night Nityananda broke His staff and kamandalu in Srivasa's house;
in the morning the Lord learned of it from Ramai, Srivasa's brother. <!-- F43 F119 -->

The acharya, the officiating priest, of the Vyasa-puja was Srivasa. He placed in Nityananda's hand a garland of forest
flowers of wondrous fragrance and asked Him, reciting the mantras, to bow to Vyasadeva. Nityananda hesitated and looked
around, and Srivasa complained to Gaura: “Your Shripada is not performing the puja!” Then Nityananda placed the garland
on Gaura Himself,[^9] and Gaura revealed a six-armed form — with Balarama's club and plough and the four emblems of
Vishnu. Afterwards Mahaprabhu asked Srivasa for the offerings of the Vyasa-puja and distributed them Himself to everyone in
the house, down to the servants and maidservants. <!-- F44 F45 F119 -->

After the Vyasa-puja the Lord, in the mood of the Supreme, sent Ramai to Shantipur for Advaita: let him come to
Srivasa-angana, “Srivasa's courtyard,” with his wife and everything needed for worship. Advaita, testing the Lord, first
hid at Nandana's. The Lord ascended the couch of Vishnu in Srivasa's house — Nityananda held an umbrella over Him —
revealed Advaita's intention to all, and sent Ramai again. Advaita came to Srivasa's house and bowed to the Lord; the
“Chaitanya-mangala” also tells of this worship. <!-- F46 F127 -->

Nityananda settled with Srivasa in Mayapur and displayed here the childlike moods of Vraja. Srivasa was a father to Him,
Malini a mother; she treated Him as her own son and fed Him with her own hands, and He was always drinking her milk: at
the sight of Nityananda, transcendental maternal love made milk flow from her breasts. Srivasa-angana became the centre
of the sankirtana of Chaitanya and Nityananda, and Nityananda, the avadhuta, was always in the ecstasy of love. <!-- F47 F119 -->

Once Gaura, wishing to test Srivasa's faith, said to him: “You are a brahmana — why do you keep this avadhuta? Who knows
His caste and family? If you want to keep your caste, send Him away quickly.” Srivasa answered: “It does not befit You to
test me so. Nityananda is Your second body. Whoever has worshipped You for even a day is dear to me as life, whoever he
may be. Let Nityananda drink wine, keep company with a woman of another faith, ruin my caste, my life and my wealth — my
faith in Him will not waver.”[^10] <!-- F48 Q8 -->

Then the Lord, of His own accord, unasked, blessed him: “Even if Lakshmi should go begging from town to town, there will
be no poverty in your house. Even the cats and dogs of your house will attain firm devotion to Me. Nityananda I entrust
to you — take care of Him in every way” (CB, Madhya 8.20–22). The occasion of the blessing was Srivasa's devotion to
Nityananda and his unshakeable faith in Him. <!-- F49 F119 Q8 -->

Once Nityananda, in the mood of a cowherd boy of Vraja, threw off His cloth, tied it on His head and wandered swaying
about the courtyard; Gaura dressed Him and, as if scolding, glorified Him. Another time a crow carried off from Srivasa's
temple the brass cup used in the service of Krishna. Malini wept, knowing how angry her husband would be; Nityananda told
the crow to return the cup, and it at once laid it before Malini.[^11] <!-- F50 F51 F120 -->

Here too, in Srivasa's house, the question of Nityananda's marriage was settled. According to the “Bhakti-ratnakara,” a
brahmana sent by Surya Dasa Sarakhela came to Navadvipa to Srivasa, with whom Nityananda was living; Srivasa rejoiced and
passed the matter of the marriage to Nityananda Himself, and then Srivasa, Advaita, Chandrashekhara, Murari and others
gathered and sang sankirtana. <!-- F52 -->

Once, while Nityananda was wandering about Nadia, the Lord revealed His universal form in Srivasa's house. Nityananda,
knowing it within, came to Srivasa's door and roared, and the Lord showed Him the form of countless universes. <!-- F119 -->
