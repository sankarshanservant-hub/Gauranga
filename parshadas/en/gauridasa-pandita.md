# Gauridasa Pandita
*Гауридас Пандит · @gauridasa-pandita*

## Subala of Ambika
In Krishna's eternal pastimes Gauridasa Pandita is Subala, the best of the Lord's dear friends and one of the twelve
cowherd boys, the gopalas. Kavi Karnapura says so plainly:

“Subala, the best of the dear friends, is Gauridasa Pandita…”. (GGD 128) <!-- F1 Q1 -->

In Gaura-lila these twelve became the companions of Nityananda; they are known precisely as the cowherd friends of Vraja,
and all of them hold places of honour in the assembly of the Vaishnavas of the whole world, the
Vishva-vaishnava-raja-sabha. Krishnadasa Kaviraja describes them thus:

“All the companions of Sri Nityananda were formerly cowherd boys in Vraja. Each of them had a staff and a horn, they wore
the dress of cowherds, and peacock feathers adorned their hair”. (CC, Adi 11.21)

Gauridasa's name never fails to appear in the lists of the twelve gopalas — now fourth, now fifth, for the order of these
lists is not the same; he is named as well where the subject is another of the twelve, Mahesha Pandita. <!-- F3 Q14 -->

On the tree of Sri Chaitanya, Nityananda is the main trunk:

“Sri Nityananda is the principal branch of Sri Chaitanya's wish-fulfilling tree. From this mighty branch grow many other
branches and twigs”. (CC, Adi 11.5)

Among the branches that grow from Him, Gauridasa is one of the most important, and among Nityananda's companions one of
the dearest to Him. <!-- F2 Q15 -->

Narahari Chakravarti in the “Bhakti-ratnakara” (7.322, 327) calls him Subalachandra, the abode of every virtue, full of
prema: the name “Subala — Gauridasa” is known everywhere, he is dear to Nityananda and non-different from Chaitanya[^1].
In another verse (7.329) the poet bows to Gauridasa, who was formerly, in Krishna's pastimes, Subalachandra, and is now
dear to Krishna-Chaitanya and Nityananda. <!-- F5 Q4 Q2 -->

He is honoured by various titles: Srila Gauridasa Pandita, Gauridasa Prabhu, Pandita Prabhu, Gauridasa Pandita Gosvami.
His brother, the poet Krishnadasa, calls him in his pada Thakura Pandita and “king of panditas” — pandita-raja. In some
quotations from the original sources the name is also read “Gauradasa”. <!-- F4 -->

## The Sons of Kamsari Mishra
Gauridasa was born in Shaligrama, a village a few miles from Navadvipa, a few kilometres north of Mayapur, near
Muragachha station on the Eastern Railway. His father was Kamsari Mishra, a brahmana of the Ghoshal family of the Vatsa
gotra; his mother was Kamala Devi. Kamsari had six sons: Damodara, Jagannatha, Suryadasa Sarakhela, Gauridasa — the
fourth — Krishnadasa Sarakhela and the youngest, Nrisimha Chaitanya[^2]. <!-- F6 F7 F8 F9 F10 -->

Gauridasa's elder brother Suryadasa was a man of rare generosity. He served the Muslim rulers, whence his title
“Sarakhela”[^3], and later gave his two daughters, Vasudha and Jahnava, in marriage to Nityananda. Suryadasa and the
younger brother, Krishnadasa, believed in Nityananda with all their hearts:

“Suryadasa Sarakhela and his brother Krishnadasa had unshakable faith in Nityananda. They were filled with love of
God”. (CC, Adi 11.25) <!-- F11 F12 Q13 -->

Gauridasa's constant support was also King Krishnadasa, son of Raja Harihoda of Badagachhi — the same who arranged
Nityananda's marriage. <!-- F18 -->

## The Move to Ambika
With Suryadasa's permission Gauridasa left Shaligrama and settled in Ambika-Kalna on the bank of the Ganga. The
“Bhakti-ratnakara” (7.330–331) also tells of it: the generous Suryadasa Pandita let his younger brother go, and he moved
to Ambika. There he lived in deep renunciation and solitude, giving all his time to the loving service of the Deity of
his heart. <!-- F19 Q6 F20 -->

## The Oar
It was this service that drew Gaurasundara Himself. One day, tradition says, He came to Ambika from Shantipura: He went
as far as the village of Harinadi, got into a boat there and, plying the oar Himself, crossed the Ganga. <!-- F27 -->

Then the Lord held out that oar to Gauridasa. “With this I crossed the river,” He said, “and with it you must carry living
souls across the river of worldly existence.” Having given him the oar, He embraced His devotee[^4]. So it is in the
“Bhakti-ratnakara” (7.333–336): having sailed from Shantipura by way of Harinadi, the Lord bids Gauridasa ferry the
fallen across the river of suffering. The oar became the sign of his service: he is one of those who carry souls to the
farther shore, for he bestows on them love for Krishna. <!-- F28 Q7 I1 -->

In the courtyard where the Lord sat down to rest there grew a tamarind tree. Beneath it, tradition says, Mahaprabhu and
Gauridasa met, and there Gauridasa began to beg the Lord to remain with him in Ambika for ever. The tamarind still stands
at the temple entrance. <!-- F29 F22 -->

Returning to Navadvipa, Mahaprabhu took Gauridasa with Him. He also gave him the “Gitamrita”, written in His own hand, and
to the end of his days Gauridasa read this gift of the Lord with great reverence[^5]. <!-- F30 F31 -->

The oar and the book are kept in the temple to this day. By tradition the book is the “Bhagavad-gita” copied out by
Mahaprabhu; some scholars doubt it, but the “Bhakti-ratnakara” (7.341) already speaks of it: the fortunate still see,
beside the Deities of Ambika, the Gita given by the Lord, and the oar. <!-- F25 Q8 -->

## The Gaura-Nitai Deities
For Gauridasa, Gaura and Nityananda were everything. Separation from Them even for a moment was to him like the soul's
parting from the body, and he sang the qualities of Nitai and Chaitanya ceaselessly, in rapture. <!-- F33 -->

Once Gaura and Nityananda came to take leave of him. This farewell is sung by a contemporary of the events — Gauridasa's
brother, the poet Krishnadasa. Sri Chaitanya dances in the courtyard, Nityananda cries “Hari, Hari!”, and Gauridasa falls
weeping at the Lord's feet: do not leave my house, stay in Ambika, or I shall die; and if You stay, all who look upon You
will be saved. The Lord replies:

“Gauridasa, give up such a wish:
serve My Deity and you will see —
I am present in it, know this for certain;
keep this true word of Mine.” (Krishnadasa, PK 2358)

Gauridasa heaved a heavy sigh and wept again; the brothers consoled him, but his heart found no rest. <!-- F32 F38 Q11 -->

Then, the poet continues in the next pada, the Lord, seeing his despair, said softly: “We have stayed with you. Know that
We two brothers are in your house for ever,” — and They manifested Themselves in two perfect murtis. The astonished
Gauridasa saw four before him: Gaura and Nitai — and Their exact likenesses. “Your wish is fulfilled,” said the Lord.
“These two will remain with you for ever, and We shall for ever ask you for food.” The “king of panditas” cooked a meal
and offered it to all four, then garlands of lotuses, cloth and betel, and anointed Them with sandal paste. Then the two
Lords, leaving two Lords with him, went away to Nilachala, while the Deities ate the rice Gauridasa offered Them, and he
served Them as his heart desired[^6]. <!-- F39 -->

Another contemporary, Murari Gupta, tells of the same in the “Kadacha”: at almost the same time as the Lord's Deity was
installed in Vishnupriya's house, Gaura and Nityananda came to the house of Gauridasa and gave him Their Deities.

“Bound by his love, They each manifested a lovely, auspicious Deity of Themselves, full of Their own rasas and endowed
with every power, and in great delight gave Them to him; dwelling there as They pleased, They partook, together with
those two Deities, of food of many tastes”. (“Kadacha” 4.14.13–14) <!-- F35 Q12 -->

The Gaura-Nitai Deity is not a likeness or a memorial of the Lord, but He Himself. “My murti is the same as I,” the Lord
says in the pada, and in serving the Deities Gauridasa served Gaura and Nitai Themselves. <!-- I3 -->

Later traditions tell it differently[^7]. According to one, Gaurasundara, understanding Gauridasa's heart, Himself
commanded: “Manifest the Deities of Me and of My brother Nityananda — and We shall fulfil the desire of your heart.”
Gauridasa manifested Them; at the sight of Their matchless beauty tears of love streamed from his eyes, and together with
those close to him he performed the abhisheka and the other rites, as the shastras prescribe, and raised both Lords to
the throne. <!-- F34 -->

According to another tradition, in answer to the plea that He stay, Mahaprabhu revealed to Gauridasa that His form and
Nityananda's were hidden in a nim tree growing nearby. Gauridasa understood the sign and had the Gaura-Nitai Deities
carved from that tree; Nityananda Himself, they say, was present at the carving. But when Mahaprabhu and Nityananda were
about to leave, Gauridasa held Them back with all his strength. The Lord calmed him: “Nityananda and I are directly
present in these Deities. Choose: either the Deities go — or We do.” Gauridasa, of course, chose that the “real” Gaura and
Nitai should stay — and so it came to pass[^8]. This tradition sees in what happened a confirmation of the words of the
“Charitamrita”:

“His name, His form and the nature of His personality make up one indivisible truth. Between these three aspects of the
Absolute there is no difference — their nature is equally spiritual and blissful”. (CC, Madhya 17.131)

The name, the Deity and the Lord Himself are one; all three are spiritual. <!-- F36 F37 Q9 I2 -->

From then on, conquered by Gauridasa's love, the Deities truly ate everything he offered Them. Gaura-Nitai, who love Their
devotees, became the captives of his love: he locked Them in the temple of his heart, and there was no way out for Them. <!-- F40 -->

## “Friend Subala”
Once Gaura and Nityananda, smiling tenderly, called him “friend Subala” and reminded him of the cowherd pastimes on the
bank of the Yamuna. And at once They showed Themselves to him as They were in Vraja — as Krishna and Balarama: dark and
fair, in the dress of cowherd boys, with staffs and horns, in garlands of forest flowers, with bells on Their ankles;
Krishna with His flute and peacock feather. The world around was filled with enchanting beauty, and Gauridasa forgot
himself in the bliss of love. For some time They showed him various pastimes, and then by Their own will brought him back
to external consciousness and stood once more upon the altar as Gaura and Nitai. Thus the Lord increased his love and
awakened in him the mood of Vraja. <!-- F41 -->

Gauridasa cooked for the Deities himself, and both Lords ate his rice and vegetables with great relish. He was so absorbed
in this service that he noticed neither bodily comforts nor hardships, and even in old age, grown weak, he would not leave
the hearth. Once, having cooked and offered many dishes, he asked Them to eat, but the Lords were silent and did not touch
the food. Then Gauridasa, like a true friend, said to Mahaprabhu with vexation: “If You are content without food, why do
You make me cook?” — and fell silent himself. The Lord replied: “We asked you not to labour so, yet you still cook so many
dishes with such great toil. It pains Us to see your efforts. Cook only what comes easily — We shall accept it gladly.”
“Then I shall offer You only rice with greens, shak-anna,” said the Pandita, and from then on the Lords, as They ate,
praised his shak[^9]. <!-- F42 F43 -->

Another time Gauridasa wished to adorn the Deities with jewels — he merely thought of it. The Lord, who knows all hearts,
Himself appeared on the throne in wondrous ornaments. Entering the temple and seeing such beauty, Gauridasa fainted with
love, and on coming to himself was struck dumb with delight: never had he seen the like — where had They found these
ornaments? And how could he now find any such for Them? Knowing his heart, the Lord said: “Ornaments of flowers please Me
most of all.” And Gauridasa joyfully decked Them with flowers and, holding a mirror up to Their faces, delighted in Their
beauty. <!-- F44 -->

Thus in Gauridasa's house Gaura and Nityananda revealed many pastimes. Later the young Shyamananda would say: Gaura and
Nitai ever dwell in the house of Gauridasa. A devotee's love binds the Lord: He gladly eats his simple cooking, puts on
ornaments Himself and, as we shall see, goes wherever the love of a disciple calls Him. <!-- F45 I5 -->

## Hridaya Chaitanya
One morning Gauridasa came to Gadadhara Pandita Gosvami. Gadadhara seated his guest respectfully and asked what had
brought him. “I need Hridayananda,” Gauridasa replied. It pained Gadadhara, but he placed the hand of Hridayananda, dear
to him as life, in Gauridasa's hand. Gauridasa brought the youth home, gave him the diksha-mantra and then, pleased with
his service, entrusted to him the service of Gaura and Nityananda. Hridayananda became his dearest disciple. <!-- F46 F47 -->

The day of Mahaprabhu's appearance was approaching. Gauridasa told Hridayananda that he would go round the homes of his
disciples, collect what was needed for the festival and soon return, and that Hridayananda should serve the Deities with
care. He set off with the devotees, absorbed in the bliss of love — and was delayed. Hridayananda grew anxious: plenty of
provisions had been gathered, only a few days remained before the festival[^10], and the devotees who lived far away still
had to be invited. Remembering the feet of his guru, he sent out invitations everywhere himself. <!-- F48 -->

Gauridasa returned on the eve of the festival. Learning of the invitations, he rejoiced at heart but outwardly made a
show of anger: “While I am here, you act on your own? Without asking me, you summon anyone you please — even my
disciples! You cannot stay here.” Hridayananda bowed at the feet of his guru, went to the bank of the Ganga and sat down
under a tree, and Gauridasa began the festival. <!-- F49 -->

Soon a boat carrying a wealthy merchant — a mahajana — put in by Hridayananda, and the merchant handed him a generous
offering for his guru[^11]. Hridayananda told Gauridasa of it, and the guru bade him hold the festival right there on the
bank. With Vaishnavas gathered from all the country round, Hridayananda began a great sankirtana — and in the midst of
the kirtana saw with his own eyes Gaura and Nityananda dancing in love. <!-- F50 F51 -->

Gauridasa heard the roar of the kirtana, and his heart filled with love. He sent the pujari — Bara (the Elder) Gangadasa
Pandita — into the temple to serve Gaura and Nityananda. The pujari came back and reported: the Lords are not in the
temple, the throne is empty. Gauridasa understood: conquered by Hridayananda's love, They had gone to the bank of the
Ganga to kindle his prema still more. With a faint smile he again put on a show of anger and set off there, stick in
hand — “so that the brothers do not run away.” Seeing him, Hridayananda fell flat at his feet. Gauridasa saw the Lords
dancing; noticing his anger, They vanished unseen by all — and Gauridasa saw Them entering Hridayananda's heart[^12]. <!-- F52 F53 -->

The stick fell from his hand without his noticing. Overwhelmed with love, Gauridasa embraced his disciple: “You are
blessed! From now on your name is Hridaya Chaitanya — he in whose heart is Chaitanya.” He bathed him in tears, while
Hridaya lay humbly at his feet. Then Gauridasa led him into the temple courtyard, set him to serve the Deities of Gaura
and Nityananda, and together with the Vaishnavas they sang and danced in sankirtana, filling all ten directions with the
cry of “Hari!”. So ended the festival of Gauranga's appearance, and Gauridasa blessed Hridaya Chaitanya for the service of
the Deities, giving him full right to it — adhikara. <!-- F54 F55 -->

Gauridasa's anger was only an outward play: inwardly he rejoiced, and through this play the glory of the disciple was
revealed, the one into whose heart Gaura and Nitai had entered. <!-- I4 -->

From Gauridasa comes a line of disciplic succession. The chief disciple of Hridaya Chaitanya was Shyamananda, who called
himself the servant of the servant of Subala; after him came Rasikananda, Nayanananda, Radha-Damodara and Baladeva
Vidyabhushana; this line is called the line of Nityananda. Hridaya Chaitanya himself, the disciple of Subala-sakha,
worshipped Gaura-Nitai in the mood of friendship. <!-- F56 -->

## Kinsmen and Guests of Ambika
When in Shaligrama Nityananda married the daughters of Suryadasa and embraced His father-in-law, Gauridasa, looking at
his brother, was also overcome with bliss and could not contain his joy. <!-- F57 -->

Gauridasa's house in Ambika became a place to which the Lord's companions and heirs would come; many of these visits fell
in the years of Hridaya Chaitanya. Virachandra, Nityananda's son, on his way to Vrindavana at his mother's bidding, came
here from Shantipura, and Hridaya Chaitanya received him with warmth and love. Jahnava, on her first pilgrimage, came
first of all to Gauridasa's house; Hridaya Chaitanya welcomed her cordially, she cooked an offering for the Gaura-Nitai
Deities with her own hands, and the festival lasted the whole night. On the way back from Vraja she came to Ambika again
and wept, remembering her uncle, who had by then departed. In a great kirtana Gaura and Nityananda Themselves appeared;
at night Jahnava cooked for Them and distributed prasada, and in a dream she saw Gauridasa together with Gaura and
Nityananda, and all of them blessed her. <!-- F58 F59 F60 -->

Narottama, after visiting Shantipura, came to Ambika-Kalna, to the house of Gauridasa, where Hridaya Chaitanya was then
living. They embraced; Narottama told of the labours of the Gosvamis in Vrindavana and spent a day there. The young
Shyamananda, then called Duhkhi, named as his guru Hridaya Chaitanya of Ambika-Kalna, the disciple of Gauridasa Pandita,
the companion of Gaura-Nitai. Coming to Ambika, he asked where Gauridasa's house was, bowed down at the temple of
Mahaprabhu in that house and there found Hridaya Chaitanya. <!-- F61 F62 -->

## Descendants and Heirs
The accounts of Gauridasa's family differ. According to some, he, like Hridaya Chaitanya, had no offspring: those who are
now counted as his family are a family of disciples, a shishya-shakha, of Gauridasa or of Hridaya Chaitanya. According to
others, Gauridasa's wife was Bimala Devi (so in the “Gaudiya-vaishnava-abhidhana”), and he had two sons: the elder,
Balarama, called “Bara”, that is, “the big one”, and the younger, Raghunatha; Raghunatha's sons were Mahesha Pandita and
Govinda (this Mahesha is not Mahesha Pandita of the twelve gopalas)[^13]. Annapurna is held by some to be Gauridasa's
daughter, by others Raghunatha's daughter, that is, his granddaughter. <!-- F13 F15 F16 -->

Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati, in the “Anubhashya” on the “Charitamrita” (Adi 11.26), does not accept the genealogy with
two sons. According to him, the disciple of Gauridasa was Hridaya Chaitanya Dasa, and Hridaya's disciple was Gopiramana,
son of Annapurna Devi, Gauridasa's daughter; the present owners of the shripata in Ambika are descendants of this
Gopiramana. <!-- F17 -->

Some kinsmen of Kamsari Mishra's family live in Shaligrama to this day[^14]. <!-- F14 -->

## Departure
Gauridasa's samadhi is in Vrindavana, and from this it is concluded that there he completed his journey in this world.
Jahnava, coming to Vraja, saw her uncle's samadhi and burst into tears; the “Bhakti-ratnakara” (11.259) tells of it too.
Gauridasa left the world and entered the eternal, unmanifest pastimes of Gauranga and Nityananda. <!-- F66 Q10 F67 -->

His departure is honoured on the twelfth lunar day of the bright half of the month of Shravana — the Gaura-dvadashi of the
month of Shridhara in the Vaishnava calendar; in 2026 this is 24 August, the same day as the departure of Rupa
Gosvami[^15]. <!-- F70 F71 -->

Narahari Chakravarti set out his whole life and glory in the seventh wave of the “Bhakti-ratnakara”. His memory lives on
centuries later: the twentieth-century Vaishnava babaji Vamshidasa, in one of his last days, sang with tears of joy
“Nitai and Gaura dance in the house of Gauridasa” and spoke of Kalna and its Amli-tala — the place beneath the tamarind. <!-- F69 F68 -->

## The Shripata at Ambika
The shripata of Gauridasa Pandita exists to this day and is well known. Ambika-Kalna lies in Bardhaman district, directly
across the Ganga from Shantipura, two miles east of Kalna station on the Eastern Railway; one can reach it by train from
Howrah on the line towards Navadvipa. The town takes its name from the locality of Ambika in the Kalna area. The shripata
is about one krosha east of Ambika-Kalna. <!-- F21 -->

The temple is faced with white stone; it was built by the zamindar of Bardhaman. At the entrance grows that same tamarind.
In the three chambers of the temple are worshipped the Deities of Gauridasa Pandita, Radha-Krishna, Gaura-Nityananda,
Jagannatha, Balarama, Sita and Rama[^16]. A little to the west of the shripata stand the house and temple of Suryadasa
Sarakhela, and further west the ashrama of Bhagavan Dasa Babaji. <!-- F23 F24 F26 -->

## Significance
Krishnadasa Kaviraja says of Gauridasa:

“Sri Gauridasa Pandita was endowed with great love and devotion for God. He also possessed tremendous power — he could
receive this love and pass it on to others. For the sake of serving Sri Nityananda, Gauridasa gave up the support of his
class and family. Thus he accepted Sri Chaitanya and Nityananda as the Lords of his whole life”. (CC, Adi 11.26–27)

Vrindavana Dasa Thakura echoes him:

“Gauridasa Pandita was supremely fortunate, for he surrendered wholly to Nityananda, dedicating his body, mind and speech
to His service”. (CB, Antya 5.730) <!-- F63 Q5 F64 Q3 -->

As Nityananda, as if intoxicated, gave love for Krishna to all without discrimination, so His close companion Gauridasa
showed great power in preaching. The oar received from Mahaprabhu's hands was the sign of this power: he carried souls
across the river of worldly existence, granting them divine love. His life shows something more: a devotee's love binds
the Lord Himself. Gaura-Nitai, who love Their devotees, ate his cooking, adorned Themselves at his mere wish, went after
the love of his disciple — and remained in his house for ever. <!-- F65 I1 I5 -->

Let us pray, then, for the mercy of Gauridasa Pandita, Subala of Vraja and companion of Nityananda, whose Lords of life
were Sri Chaitanya and Nityananda. <!-- I6 -->
