# Srila Bhakti Sundar Govinda Dev-Goswami Maharaj
*Srila Bhakti Sundar Govinda Dev-Goswami Maharaj · @govinda-maharaja*

## Successor of Srila Sridhar Maharaj
Srila Bhakti Sundar Govinda Dev-Goswami Maharaj was the disciple, servant and successor of Srila Bhakti Rakshaka
Sridhar Deva Goswami Maharaj and the acharya of the Sri Chaitanya Saraswat Math in Navadvip. The books of the Math
style him His Divine Grace and Sevaite-President-Acharya — alongside the Founder-Acharya, Sridhar Maharaj; for short he
is called Srila B. S. Govinda Dev-Goswami Maharaj or Srila Govinda Maharaj, and his Russian disciples call him Srila
Gurudev and Acharyadev. In his youth, at his initiation, his guru gave him the name Gaurendu Brahmachari.
<!-- F1 F2 -->

The whole life of Srila Sridhar Maharaj was an embodiment of sharanagati, surrender to the Lord, and in his mission
his most generous gift is said to be the sharanagata-bhakta in whom that highest ideal became a living example —
Srila Govinda Maharaj. Sripad B. B. Avadhut Maharaj called him “the precious gift of Srila Sridhar Maharaj to the
whole world”. The book “Our Teachers”, in which the Russian mission of the Sri Chaitanya Saraswat Math glorified “the
greatest Vaishnavas of our time”, is dedicated to three teachers, and his name stands first in the dedication — before
those of Srila B. R. Sridhar Maharaj and Srila A. C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupada. The aim of the book is “to give
pleasure to our great guides and eternal well-wishers” and to help their followers unite their efforts in the service
of the mission of Sri Chaitanya Mahaprabhu. Recordings of his lectures and talks are collected in an audio and video
archive (govindamaharaj.ru). <!-- I2 F3 I5 -->

## Bamunpara: Family and Childhood
He appeared on 17 December 1929 in the village of Bamunpara, in the Burdwan district of West Bengal, not far from
Navadvip, the place of Sri Chaitanya Mahaprabhu’s appearance. By our calendar his appearance day is the Krishna-dvitiya
of the month of Narayana[^1]. The house where he was born stands next to the house where Vrindavana Dasa Thakura, the
author of the “Sri Chaitanya-bhagavata”, lived. <!-- F5 F6 -->

For five generations his family had worshipped the Deities of Sri Radha and Sri Krishna; according to family
tradition, They had been installed by Virachandra Prabhu himself, the son of Nityananda Prabhu. The boy grew up in an
atmosphere of traditional Vaishnava culture, surrounded by the love of his relatives. His father was a well-known
singer of Vaishnava songs, and from early childhood the son learned from him to lead kirtana. Even before the son’s
birth, when the father visited a temple of Lord Shiva, it was revealed to him that the boy would be under the special
protection of Lord Shiva and would become a great saint. “Thus from his very birth it was clear that Srila Govinda
Maharaj was an extraordinary personality,” says “Our Teachers”, and the events of his life confirmed it again and
again. <!-- F8 F7 F10 F9 I1 -->

When he was thirteen his father left this world, and the care of the family fell on the young man’s shoulders. He
began to work as a medical assistant in a clinic at Nandaghat, some fifteen kilometres from Bamunpara. <!-- F11 -->

## Navadvip: Gaurendu Brahmachari
In the spring of 1947, when he was seventeen, a sankirtana party from the Sri Chaitanya Saraswat Math came to
Nandaghat. While the sadhus sang, the youth offered to accompany them on the mridanga. He was drawn to them, as “Our
Teachers” puts it, by “some irresistible force”: he delighted in their company and listened eagerly to their
explanations of the “Bhagavata Purana”. At last he asked whether they would take him with them; the sadhus agreed, and
at night he secretly left home and went with them to Navadvip — just as Raghunatha Dasa Gosvami and Narottama Dasa
Thakura had once left their homes for the service of Mahaprabhu. His coming to the Math took place, in the book’s
words, “after events in which the supreme will of Lord Krishna Himself revealed itself”. <!-- F12 I1 -->

He came to the feet of Srila Sridhar Maharaj on the day of Nrisimha Chaturdashi. The guru at once paid him special
attention and, as Srila Govinda Maharaj himself later recalled, asked him two questions: could he stay in the Math and
dedicate his whole life to service — and would he follow the instructions of his spiritual master and not his own
mind? Without a moment’s hesitation the youth answered “yes” — and confirmed it with his whole life. <!-- F13 -->

By a rule that Sridhar Maharaj himself had laid down in the Math, a newcomer passed a probation of at least six months
before initiation. For this youth the guru broke his own rule: soon, at the festival of Ratha-yatra, he gave him diksha
and the name Gaurendu Brahmachari, and only a week later introduced him to his godbrothers as his successor. Among them
was Sripad Krishnadasa Babaji Maharaj; he accepted the youth as his own son and said to Sridhar Maharaj: “Please give
special attention to Sri Gaurendu Brahmachari — he is very intelligent and has outstanding qualities.” Recalling the
early years of the Math, Sridhar Maharaj himself said: “Then came Sripad Govinda Maharaj, in his youth Sri Gaurendu
Brahmachari.” <!-- F14 F2 F15 -->

Other disciples of Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati Thakura also spoke of the youth’s extraordinary gifts — Sripad
Bhakti Prajnana Keshava Maharaj, Sripad Bhakti Saranga Goswami Maharaj, Sakhi Babu, Ram Gopal Babu, Sripad Madhava
Maharaj, Sripad Jajabar Maharaj. Their words strengthened Sridhar Maharaj in his intention to make Gaurendu his
successor and the acharya of the Sri Chaitanya Saraswat Math. He drew him close as his personal servant and himself
taught him the truths of the scriptures, Vaishnava etiquette and Sanskrit poetics — all that was needed to take up the
responsibility for the spiritual guidance of the Rupanuga-sampradaya. For more than forty years Srila Govinda Maharaj
remained his guru’s trusted servant, carried out his most responsible commissions and was his personal physician. “I
was beside Srila Guru Maharaj for about 42 years,” he later said. <!-- F16 F17 -->

Sridhar Maharaj’s choice surprised many: among his followers there were many serious and educated devotees, yet as
the future acharya he named a very young, almost unknown youth. There was unrest in the Math, but Sridhar Maharaj
firmly declared his decision and asked everyone who disagreed with him to leave the society; many sannyasis — his
godbrothers — bore witness to this. <!-- F18 -->

Thus Gaurendu entered a line of succession whose beginning he would later describe from the words of his guru and his
guru’s godbrothers: he himself came too late to see Srila Bhaktisiddhanta. On the eve of his departure Srila
Bhaktisiddhanta called his closest disciples together and, not seeing Sridhar Maharaj among them, had him called. The
disciples kept watch by the guru’s bed two hours each, and Sridhar Maharaj chose the hardest hours — from two to four
in the morning. Srila Bhaktisiddhanta asked him to sing “Sri Rupa-manjari-pada”; and when, in place of the abashed
Sridhar Maharaj, a brahmachari with a beautiful voice began to sing, he stopped him: “I do not need beautiful singing —
I want to hear this bhajan from Sridhar Maharaj.” Having heard the song, he said: “I am happy.” “From that moment all
Guru Maharaj’s godbrothers said many times — and I personally heard it from them: ‘Before, we did not know how the
transmission of authority takes place, but on that day we witnessed it. Srila Prabhupada transmitted the authority of
the Rupanuga-sampradaya to Srila Sridhar Maharaj,’” Srila Govinda Maharaj recalled. <!-- F35 -->

## Calcutta: Beside Srila Bhaktivedanta Swami
For several years Sridhar Maharaj and the young Gaurendu lived in Calcutta, on Sitakanta Banerjee Lane, in a preaching
centre on the upper floor of the pharmaceutical laboratory of Abhay Charan De — the future Srila A. C. Bhaktivedanta
Swami Prabhupada. According to Srila Govinda Maharaj himself, at Swami Maharaj’s request the Calcutta branch of the
Math was housed in his home for many years[^2]; the Swami often came to them and spoke at length with Sridhar Maharaj.
<!-- F19 -->

When leaving Calcutta, Sridhar Maharaj told his disciple to study the “Bhagavad-gita” under Swami Maharaj, who at
once discerned unusual spiritual qualities in the youth. The guru often took Gaurendu with him to preach, and he also
preached by himself in the Bara Bazar, going from stall to stall — only he had no books to give out at that time.
<!-- F20 -->

The books were found in the Swami’s house. He had brought out two or three issues of the journal “Back to Godhead”,
but lacked the means to continue it, and in his room lay huge stacks of journals that no one undertook to distribute.
“Take the journal and distribute it, going from door to door,” the Swami said to the youth. “So I became the first
distributor of that journal,” Srila Govinda Maharaj recalled. Many years later he added: “I did not know a word of
English, but I was very proud of the chance to distribute that journal,” and admitted that today he was prouder of it
still. <!-- F21 -->

Those were troubled times. They lived among Hindus, and the Muslim houses began right next door; clashes flared up
nearby again and again, and at night bandits attacked peaceful homes. “It was very frightening; I had never seen
anything like it,” Srila Govinda Maharaj related. Once the alarmed Swami said to him: “Gaurendu — at that time my name
was Gaurendu Brahmachari — I have a stock of bottles with incendiary mixture in the attic. If we are attacked, take the
bottles and throw them at the attackers.” But by the Lord’s mercy it all passed. <!-- F22 -->

Preaching did not come easily to the Swami. He was then a grihastha, a family man, and Gaurendu a brahmachari in
saffron robes, and at public lectures the Swami would yield the vyasasana to him: he taught him a few shlokas of the
“Srimad-Bhagavatam” and said: “Read these verses… and then I will explain them to the audience.” “His desire to spread
Krishna consciousness was enormous,” Srila Govinda Maharaj recalled. <!-- F23 -->

The Swami loved the youth with a father’s love. Four years of preaching together bound them with ties of love and
tenderness; the Swami himself taught him the Vaishnava scriptures and the mridanga and cared for his health and
well-being. He himself loved food cooked in ghee and scolded the youth: “Why do you eat dry rice, without ghee?! Be
sure to eat it with ghee!” — but Gaurendu could not afford ghee: his usual breakfast was one paisa’s worth of rice.
<!-- F24 F25 -->

Srila Govinda Maharaj also recalled how the Swami said to his sister Bhavini Didi — she was also called Pishima:
“‘Sri Sri Prapanna-jivanamritam’ is a very necessary and wonderful book. You have money. Please help to publish this
book.” And it was she who gave the money for the first edition of this book by Sridhar Maharaj. <!-- F26 -->

[^1]: The date and place of his appearance are given in “Our Teachers”. By calculation, at sunrise on 17 December 1929
    the Krishna-pratipad was still running, and the dvitiya began in the afternoon; the book does not give the hour of
    his birth.
[^2]: “Our Teachers” speaks of this in different ways: in one place it says that Sridhar Maharaj and the young Gaurendu
    lived there “for several years”, in another Srila Govinda Maharaj recalls that the Calcutta branch of the Math was
    housed in the Swami’s home “for ten years”.
