# Govinda Ghosha
*Говинда Гхош · @govinda-ghosha*

## The Kirtaniya Who Stayed with the Lord
Govinda Ghosha is one of the three Ghosha brothers, the lead singer of the kirtana to whose singing Sri Chaitanya
Himself danced. Krishnadasa Kaviraja in the “Chaitanya-charitamrita” calls him now simply Govinda, now Govinda Ghosha;
Vrindavana Dasa in the “Chaitanya-bhagavata” likewise says now Govinda Ghosha, now Govinda; Kavi Karnapura in the
“Gaura-ganoddesha-dipika” calls him Govinda. He himself signed the last lines of his songs "Govinda Ghosha." With
reverence he is called Sri Govinda Ghosha Thakura — so he is named in the subheading of our edition of the
“Charitamrita” as well — and Srila Bhaktivinoda Thakura in the “Amrita-pravaha-bhashya” calls him simply "Ghosha
Thakura"[^1]. <!-- F1 -->

Krishnadasa Kaviraja names the three brothers — Govinda, Madhava and Vasudeva — as branches of Sri Chaitanya's
tree[^2]. Soon after, telling how Mahaprabhu sent Nityananda to Gauda, he says that two of the brothers went with
Nityananda, while Govinda stayed with the Lord and was glad to do so (CC, Adi 10.118). For this reason the biographies
count Govinda in the branch of Sri Gauranga Himself. Of Madhava and Vasudeva the “Charitamrita” says specially that
they belong to both ganas, Gaura's and Nityananda's (Adi 11.15); of Govinda this is not said. <!-- F2 F3 -->

All three brothers are eternal companions of Sri Chaitanya and Nityananda, says one biography; another, in its chapter
on Vasudeva, adds that the other two brothers as well, Govinda and Madhava Ghosha Thakura, were
devotees of Gaura full of the deepest love for Him. <!-- F4 F5 -->

## The Three Brothers
Govinda's brothers were Madhava Ghosha, who is also called Madhavananda, and Vasudeva, or Vasu, Ghosha. Which of the
three was the eldest the sources do not say; Kavi Karnapura names them in the order "Govinda, Madhavananda, Vasudeva."
Their family were kayasthas of northern Rarh, Uttara-Rarhi; the family was prominent and respected and belonged to the
"shaukra," kayasthas by birth. Srila Bhaktisiddhanta, too, speaks of the Uttara-Rarhi kayasthas in the “Anubhashya” on
CC, Adi 10.115[^3]; one biography adds that this is the part of Rarh through which the Ganga does not flow. <!-- F6 F7 -->

The family is linked with Shrihatta (Sylhet) through the village of Buran, or Burangi; the brothers' father settled in
Kumarahatta, and afterwards the brothers settled in Navadvipa. More on this is said in the article on Vasu Ghosha. One
biography, however, gives Govinda's home as Kashipur-Vishnutala near Agradvipa and mentions that some consider
Vishnutala his birthplace[^4]. Neither the year, nor the place, nor the day of Govinda Ghosha's appearance is given in
the sources; nor does the day of his appearance appear in the Vaishnava calendar of Sri Chaitanya Saraswat Math.

## Kalavati
Who the three brothers were in Krishna's eternal pastimes Kavi Karnapura reveals:

“Kalavati, Rasollasa and Gunatunga, who were in Vraja and sang the songs composed by Sri Vishakha, are now considered to
be Govinda, Madhavananda and Vasudeva respectively.” (GGD 188)[^5]

Govinda, then, is in Vraja the sakhi Kalavati, one of those who sang the songs composed by Sri Vishakha. As one
biography puts it, she who in Vraja-lila is Kalavati is in Gaura-lila Sri Govinda Ghosha. This is no rebirth in the
worldly sense: Krishna's eternal companions descend together with the Lord, and in His pastimes as Gaura they continue
their eternal service — for Kalavati, that service is song. <!-- F11 Q1 -->

Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati Thakura in the “Gaudiya-bhashya” on the “Chaitanya-bhagavata” reveals this more
deeply: Madhava, Vasudeva and Govinda are kaya-vyuha, personal expansions of the ashraya-vigraha of the madhura-rasa of
Vraja, that is, of Sri Radha Herself. Ashraya means the "abode" of love: she in whose heart love for Krishna reaches
its summit, while Krishna Himself is its object, the vishaya. Therefore, as one biography relates, the three brothers
abide in madhurya-rasa, among those who have accepted Srimati Radhika as the highest shelter of that love. This is
their eternal spiritual nature, not an allegory and not a worldly "female role." <!-- F12 -->

## The Lord Danced to Their Singing
All three brothers sang with sweet voices and composed padas. As a poet Vasudeva is the best known of them: of
Govinda's and Madhava's songs only a few have survived. But the brothers were loved above all for their kirtana — and
that is how the “Charitamrita” remembers them:

“Govinda, Madhava and Vasudeva were three brothers, and when they sang kirtana, Chaitanya and Nitai danced.” (CC, Adi
10.115)

The biographies repeat this in various ways: no sooner did the three brothers begin their sankirtana than Lord
Chaitanya and Nityananda broke into ecstatic dance; Nityananda Himself danced to their kirtana[^6].

## In Navadvipa
In Navadvipa we see Govinda Ghosha above all at the kirtana in the courtyard of Srivasa Pandita.
On the day of Hari-vasara, when everyone chants the names of Hari, the Lord began to dance at dawn, and the devotees,
dividing into groups, sang. Vrindavana Dasa says:

“In one group the chief singer was Srivasa Pandita, in another, Mukunda. The third group was led by Govinda Ghosha. And
as Gaurachandra danced, all the devotees sang the holy names.” (CB, Madhya 8.141–142)

So even in Navadvipa Govinda Ghosha stood among the Lord's lead singers. <!-- F17 Q6 -->

One biography sees him also in the great nagara-sankirtana, when the Lord went with His devotees to the Kazi's house.
Vrindavana Dasa, listing those who knew perfectly how to perform kirtana, names Govinda among them (CB, Madhya
23.150–152), and among those who danced in the procession — "Murari, Mukunda Datta, Ramai, Govinda, Vakreshvara and
Vasudeva" (CB, Madhya 23.209). The biography concludes from these verses that Govinda Ghosha, too, danced in the Lord's
kirtana; another also names him among those in the procession. In the “Chaitanya-bhagavata” itself the name is simply
"Govinda"[^7]. <!-- F18 Q7 -->

## Songs of Separation
Not many of Govinda's own songs have come down to us. Two of them
tell how Navadvipa learned of the Lord's sannyasa. In the first, Gadadhara Pandita runs to Mukunda Datta with news he
has just heard from the lips of Sri Gauranga Himself:

"O Mukunda, dear as life! What have I suddenly heard today!
To say it, life departs; it will not leave my lips:
Sri Gauranga will leave Navadvipa!
We knew nothing. In the morning I came to Gora —
He sits with bowed head;
tears stream from His eyes and run in rivulets down His chest,
the moon of His face has grown pale.
Seeing this, my heart was at once in turmoil,
but there was no chance to ask.
After a moment He came to Himself; then I asked —
and, hearing me, He gave this answer.
And I, overwhelmed, saying nothing to Him,
ran to you.
So I have told you; do what you can —
I have no hope of living."
Hearing this, Mukunda weeps, his heart finds no rest,
as he looks into Gadadhara's face.
Says this Govinda Ghosha: may this not happen!
Or else I shall die. (Govinda Ghosha, PK 1606) <!-- F35 -->

The second song was composed about the very day the Lord left Navadvipa. The poet calls out to the people of Nadia:

"O people of Nadia! Whose face are you looking to?
Stretch out your arms — bring Gorachand back!
Who else will take you all into His arms?
Who, seeing the afflicted, will offer them prema unasked?
What a spear in the heart, alas, what a spear in the heart!
The darling of our life is leaving Navadvipa.
No more shall we go to Gauranga,
no more shall we delight in kirtana.
The devotees weep, their hearts breaking;
only stony Govinda Ghosha does not die." (Govinda Ghosha, PK 1622)

One biography gives both songs, another only the second, calling it a pada of Sri Govinda Ghosha Thakura. The words
"stony Govinda Ghosha" are not a judgement on his heart but the humility of a Vaishnava poet: in padas the poet often
reproaches himself for still being alive, when separation from the Lord should have taken his life. The stronger the
love, the sterner such self-reproach; and the same Govinda in the first song prays: "may this not happen! Or else I
shall die." <!-- F34 Q10 -->

Other padas with his signature have survived as well. One tells how Nimai's dear ones in Navadvipa pined while He was
travelling in East Bengal; two praise the beauty of Gorachanda; one is about the Lord absorbed in the mood of Sri
Radha and is signed "this lowly Govinda Ghosha"; another was composed after the Lord's abhisheka, another concerns His
decision to accept sannyasa; and there is a song signed jointly by the three brothers: "Govinda, Madhava and Vasu." <!-- F36 F13 -->

## In Nilachala
When the Lord, having accepted sannyasa, settled in Puri, the devotees of Gauda came to Him. On that first visit King Prataparudra watched them from the roof of the palace, and Gopinatha Acharya named each one for him.
He pointed out the brothers too:

“Here are Govinda, Madhava Ghosha and Vasu Ghosha — three brothers whose kirtana delights the Lord.” (CC, Madhya 11.88)
<!-- F40 -->

At the Ratha-yatra Mahaprabhu formed seven groups of singers. The fourth He entrusted to Govinda Ghosha:

“He formed another group with Govinda Ghosha as the leader, in which Haridasa, Vishnudasa and Raghava sang, with the two
brothers Madhava and Vasudeva Ghosha; and Vakreshvara Pandita danced there.” (CC, Madhya 13.42–43)

So Govinda became the mula-gayaka, the lead singer whom the chorus followed, his two brothers among them; and to dance
before his group the Lord appointed none other than Vakreshvara Pandita himself[^8]. <!-- F20 Q4 -->

When the Lord wished to dance Himself, He brought all seven groups together and, for His wild, leaping dance, put
nine devotees together with Svarupa Damodara: Srivasa, Ramai, Raghu, Govinda, Mukunda, Haridasa, Govindananda, Madhava
and Govinda (CC, Madhya 13.72–74). One biography sees Govinda Ghosha in the Govinda of this list[^9]. <!-- F21 Q5 -->

Later Mahaprabhu sent Nityananda to preach in Gauda. Two of the brothers went with Him, but Govinda stayed:

“At the Lord’s command Nityananda went to Gauda, and by the Lord’s command three devotees went with Him: Sri Ramadasa,
Madhava and Vasudeva Ghosha. Govinda stayed with the Lord, and was overjoyed to do so.” (CC, Adi 10.117–118)

To remain with the Lord was a joy for Govinda: his place was at Gaura's feet, and it was before Him that he sang.
<!-- F19 -->

While Mahaprabhu lived in Puri, every year the brothers, together with Shivananda Sena and other Gaudiya Vaishnavas, walked from Gauda
to Nilachala and with their kirtana increased the Lord's joy. Of the third year of these journeys the
“Charitamrita” says:

“Acharyaratna, Vidyanidhi, Srivasa, Ramai, Vasudeva, Murari, and Govinda with his two brothers set out. Raghava
Pandita packed his bags (*jhali*) for the journey, and the residents of Kulina-grama came bringing silk ropes.” (CC,
Madhya 16.16–17)[^10]

Murari Gupta, listing the devotees of Gauda going to the Lord in Puri, also names "Sri Govinda Ghosha and Mukunda, the
best of singers" (“Kadacha,” 4.17.6). And in Kavi Karnapura's drama “Chaitanya-chandrodaya” Sarvabhauma Bhattacharya,
on his way to Varanasi, meets the Gaudiya pilgrims and recognizes among them Advaita, Nityananda, Srivasa, Haridasa:
"This is Govinda Ghosha and the others" (act 10). <!-- F22 Q8 -->

## In Panihati
When by Gaura's command Nityananda went from Nilachala to Gauda, He stayed in Panihati, in the house of Raghava Pandita.
There the finest singers gathered around Him, and Vrindavana Dasa says:

“The pious Madhava Ghosha was the most skilled in singing kirtana. In this he had no equal in all the world. He was
known as the singer of the Lord's pastimes in Vrindavana, and he was very dear to Lord Nityananda. The three brothers —
Madhava, Govinda and Vasudeva — sang while Lord Nityananda danced.” (CB, Antya 5.257–259)

One biography, too, says that Govinda Ghosha took part in the pastimes at Raghava Pandita's house[^11]. <!-- F23 F14 Q2 -->

Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati Thakura in the “Gaudiya-bhashya” on these verses calls all three brothers "singers of
Vrindavana." Their singing had nothing in common with that of worldly performers: the brothers were wholly given to
kirtana and did not judge it in a worldly way, as worldly kirtana-singers do. Here lies the meaning of the name: the
desire to serve Hari grows in the heart only when worldly conceptions have been destroyed completely[^12].
<!-- F16 I1 -->

## Sri Gopinatha in Agradvipa
Govinda Ghosha's principal service, remembered to this day, is connected with Agradvipa. Instructed by Sriman
Mahaprabhu, he manifested there the Deity of Sri Gopinatha from a black stone, a krishna-shila, that had come to him.
Gopinatha is Krishna Himself, the Beloved of the gopis; He appeared in Agradvipa by Mahaprabhu's will and accepted
Govinda's service. One biography calls Govinda Ghosha the founder of Sri Gopinatha's
service in Agradvipa; Srila Bhaktivinoda Thakura in the “Amrita-pravaha-bhashya” writes that Govinda installed the
Deity of Gopinatha there, and Srila Bhaktivedanta Svami in his commentary on the “Charitamrita” — that Govinda Ghosha
established the temple of Gopinatha in Agradvipa and lived there. Gopinatha was his Deity — his Krishna. <!-- F24 -->

Srila Bhaktisiddhanta in the “Anubhashya” on CC, Adi 11.15 says that Sri Gopinatha, "most dear" to Govinda, resides in
Agradvipa to this day, near Daihata and Patuli, in the district of Bardhaman. Agradvipa and Daihata are villages
between Navadvipa and Katwa; a little north of Agradvipa lies Chakhandi, the birthplace of Srinivasa Acharya. Srila
Bhaktivinoda in the “Amrita-pravaha-bhashya” on Madhya 11.88 adds that in his day, too, a fair in memory of "Ghosha
Thakura" was held in Agradvipa, near Katwa. <!-- F25 -->

The service of Sri Gopinatha has long been carried on under the supervision of the royal house of Nadia at
Krishnanagar. Every year in the month of Vaishakha, at the Baradol festival, Sri Gopinatha is brought together
with eleven other Deities to the palace of Krishnanagar, and after the Dola festival He returns to Agradvipa. Of this
too Srila Bhaktisiddhanta writes in the “Anubhashya.” <!-- F26 -->

In the end the brothers parted, and each had his own shripata: Vasudeva in Tamoluk, Madhava in
Daihata, Govinda in Agradvipa. According to one biography, Govinda settled in Agradvipa after Mahaprabhu had withdrawn
from this world[^13]. <!-- F37 -->

## Gopinatha's Promise
It was Sriman Mahaprabhu who told Govinda Ghosha to enter the grihastha-ashrama. This was no concession to worldliness
but the fulfilment of the Lord's will, as in the lives of other householder companions of His, such as Srivasa
Pandita and Shivananda Sena. <!-- F27 -->

According to tradition, Govinda's wife and son left this world before him; their names the sources do not give[^14].
Then, it is said, he began to wonder: who, after his departure, would offer the pinda for him — the memorial offering
(shraddha) that by custom a son performs? This should not be taken as fear for his own fate after death: a devotee
wholly surrendered to Mukunda no longer owes anything to the gods, the sages or the ancestors (SB 11.5.41). The worry
only gave the Lord an occasion to show how He loves His servant. <!-- F28 F29 -->

One night, as Govinda Ghosha slept, Sri Gopinatha spoke to him: "Do not be troubled. I Myself will offer the pinda for
you." One biography gives His words thus: "Do not be anxious: I am your son, I Myself will perform your shraddha."
<!-- F30 -->

When Sri Govinda Ghosha manifested the pastime of his departure, on the very next day Sri Gopinatha offered the pinda
for him — He kept His word. And still, year after year, on the day of his departure, Sri Gopinatha offers the pinda for
him, as a son performs the shraddha for his father. Srila Bhaktisiddhanta, too, writes of this in the “Anubhashya.”
<!-- F31 F32 -->

This account is a tradition, but the biographies and Srila Bhaktisiddhanta's commentary hand it down as living truth:
the Lord is true to the word He gives His devotee. Having promised in a dream, Gopinatha Himself does for Govinda what a
son would do — and does it still, year after year. Such is bhakta-vatsalya, the Lord's love for His servant: for his
sake the Lord gladly takes the role of a son. <!-- I2 -->

## Departure
Govinda Ghosha left this world on Krishna-dvadashi — the twelfth lunar day of the dark fortnight — of the month of
Vishnu (Chaitra). On this day the Vaishnava calendar of Sri Chaitanya Saraswat Math observes "the Disappearance Day of
Srila Govinda Ghosha Thakura"[^15]. The year and place of his departure are not given in the sources; the whole story
of Gopinatha points to Agradvipa, though this is not said outright. His brother Vasudeva departed at another time — on
Shukla-dvitiya of the month of Damodara (Kartika). <!-- F33 -->

## Srinivasa and Kheturi
Near Agradvipa lies Chakhandi; there Srinivasa Acharya spent his childhood. As a boy Srinivasa constantly visited
the homes of Gaura's devotees around Chakhandi and listened to them speak of the Lord. The “Bhakti-ratnakara” says:

"To the houses of the devotees who live near Chakhandi
Srinivasa constantly goes.
Sri Govinda Ghosha and others, losing all composure out of love,
bathe him in the nectar of Sri Gaurachandra's pastimes." (BR 2.203–204)

So Govinda Ghosha told the boy about the pastimes of Sri Gaurasundara. According to one biography, even when Srinivasa
was an infant, and the rites of his first feeding, name-giving and first tonsure were performed, the local companions
of Mahaprabhu — Govinda Ghosha and the devotees of Shrikhanda, Narahari and Raghunandana — showered love and blessings
on him; according to another, Srinivasa as a child was blessed with the mercy of Sri Govinda Ghosha Thakura and Narahari
Sarakara Thakura. <!-- F38 -->

And after the Lord had withdrawn from this world, the brothers appeared together with Him at His devotees' kirtana.
According to the “Bhakti-ratnakara” (10.572 ff.), when Narottama Dasa sang at the great festival in Kheturi, Sri
Chaitanya appeared there with His companions, and among them were Madhava Ghosha, Vasu Ghosha and Govinda Ghosha. There
too the poet exclaims: "…the dancing of Govinda, Madhava, Vasu Ghosha — who has the fortitude to watch it?" (BR 10.549).
<!-- F39 Q9 -->

## In the Memory of the Vaishnavas
Govinda Ghosha's name lives on in the Vaishnavas' salutations. Lochana Dasa at the beginning of the
“Chaitanya-mangala” bows "to Govinda and Madhava Ghosha and to Vasu Ghosha" (Sutra-khanda 34), and, listing the
companions who descended with the Lord, names the three brothers again (Sutra-khanda 629). Devakinandana in the
“Vaishnava-vandana” sings: "With attention I shall praise Sri Govinda Ghosha, whose name the Lord Himself made true"
(part 2, 80); elsewhere in the same vandana he is named beside Gadadhara Dasa, but that line is unclear (part 2, 91).
Other vandanas honour the three brothers too: in the Sanskrit “Vaishnava-vandana” ascribed to Jiva Gosvami, Govinda,
Madhavananda and Vasu Ghosha are called "treasuries of virtue" (part 1, 93), and in the “Sri Vaishnavabhidhana” of the
same Devakinandana, "Govinda, Madhava and Vasudeva, bearing the name Ghosha" (part 3, 30). The “Narottama-vilasa” honours them as well (2.122; 11.280), and in the “Prema-vivarta” a Govinda plays the
mridanga — apparently Govinda Ghosha (15.7). <!-- F40 -->

## Significance
Govinda Ghosha is an eternal companion of Gaura, in Vraja the sakhi Kalavati who sang the songs of Sri Vishakha;
Srila Bhaktisiddhanta sees in the three brothers the kaya-vyuha of Sri Radha Herself. In Gaura's pastimes he descended
to sing for the Lord: in Navadvipa he led one of the kirtana groups in Srivasa's courtyard, in Puri he led the fourth
group before Jagannatha's chariot, and Vakreshvara danced before him. When Nityananda went to Gauda, Govinda stayed
with the Lord and was glad of it. By Mahaprabhu's will he manifested Sri Gopinatha in Agradvipa — and Gopinatha
answered him with such love that, as a son, He offers the pinda for him year after year. The kirtana of one in whom
worldly conceptions have been destroyed completely, and the Lord who keeps the word He gave His servant — this is what
Govinda Ghosha has left us. And he himself, signing as "stony Govinda Ghosha," teaches the humility without which no
one can sing the Lord's glories. <!-- F3 F11 F12 F24 F32 I1 I2 -->
