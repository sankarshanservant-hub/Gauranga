# Balabhadra Bhattacharya
*Балабхадра Бхаттачарья · @balabhadra-bhattacharya*

## A Companion on the Road to Mathura
When Sri Chaitanya Mahaprabhu set out from Nilachala for Vrindavana through the forests of Jharikhanda, only one of His
devotees went with Him as His companion — the Bengali brahmana Balabhadra Bhattacharya. He cooked the Lord's meals,
collected alms for Him, guarded Him in the forest and in foreign lands, drew Him out of the waters of the Yamuna when the
Lord plunged into them in ecstasy, and saw with his own eyes pastimes of which he would later tell the devotees in Puri.
Krishnadasa Kaviraja counts him among the branches of the Chaitanya tree:

“Balabhadra Bhattacarya, a true devotee, served the Lord as His brahmacari on the journey to Mathura.” (CC, Adi 10.146)

In the “Charitamrita” he is called Balabhadra Bhattacharya, simply the Bhattacharya, or Balabhadra, and once Balabhadra
Bhatta; the “Anubhashya,” too, heads this verse “Balabhadra Bhatta.” Kavi Karnapura in his drama
“Chaitanya-chandrodaya” calls him Balabhadra-bhattacharya; Govinda-deva in the “Gaura-krishnodaya” calls him now the
Bhatta, now Balabhadra Bhattacharya, now the Bhattacharya; and Murari Gupta calls him Baladeva.[^1] <!-- F1 F3 Q1 -->

Why Balabhadra's service is called the service of a brahmachari, Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati explains in his
“Anubhashya” on this verse: a sannyasi does not cook or attend to other household matters — such services he accepts
from householders; the sannyasi is the guru, and brahmacharis are his disciples. So on the road Balabhadra performed for
the Lord the duties of a brahmachari. As acharya, the Lord kept the rules of sannyasa to teach the world; yet His
measure was Vaishnavism, not birth, and we shall see that on the way He accepted invitations from great souls of the
lower classes and ate food cooked by a Sanodiya brahmana, following the example of Madhavendra Puri. <!-- I2 -->

Of his worldly life little is known. He was a brahmana, and Svarupa Damodara, proposing him to the Lord as a companion,
said of him: “He is full of love for You, and he is learned, saintly and noble.” The Lord Himself acknowledged his
learning: “You are a learned man,” He told him once in Vrindavana. He was a Bengali, a *Gaudiya*: the Pathans would call
him and his servant “the Bengalis,” and Svarupa says that he came with the Lord from Gauda and wished to visit all the
holy places. He had his own servant, also a brahmana, whose name the sources do not give; he carried the cloth and the
waterpot. Neither Balabhadra's birthplace, nor his parents and disciples, nor the year and day of his appearance and
departure are recorded; nor are his days found in the Vaishnava calendar of the Sri Chaitanya Saraswat Math.

## Madhurekshana
Who he is in Krishna's eternal pastimes, Kavi Karnapura tells us:

“Balabhadra Bhattacharya is Sri Madhurekshana.” (“Gaura-ganoddesha-dipika” 171)

The verse stands in the list of Srimati Radharani's friends: in the “Gaura-ganoddesha-dipika” this is the section on
the gopis who embody the power of devotion (verses 160–172). Srila Bhaktisiddhanta gives the same identity in his
“Anubhashya” on CC, Adi 10.146: “Balabhadra Bhattacharya is Madhurekshana of Vraja.” Thus the Lord's companion on the
forest roads is not merely a pious pandit from Bengal but one of Radha's eternal friends, who descended into Gaura's
pastimes together with the Lord to serve Him here as well. <!-- F2 Q4 -->

## From Shantipura to Nilachala
The “Charitamrita” first names him on the Lord's return from Gauda. That year Mahaprabhu had set out from Nilachala for
Vrindavana but went only as far as Kanai-natashala and turned back. At Shantipura, in the house of Advaita Acharya, He
told the devotees He would take only two with Him to Nilachala, and would meet the rest at the Ratha-yatra:

“With two companions — Balabhadra Bhattacarya and Damodara Pandita — the Lord came to Nilacala.” (CC, Madhya 1.236)

Govinda-deva says the same: the Lord came to Jagannatha “with the Bhatta [Balabhadra], the Acharya, with the brahmin
Damodara Pandita and with His servants” (“Gaura-krishnodaya” 13.59). Svarupa would recall this later: Balabhadra “came
with You from Gauda.” <!-- F9 F5 -->

## The Choice of Svarupa and Ramananda
The next year, after the Ratha-yatra, when autumn came, the Lord resolved to go to Vrindavana. He consulted in private
with Ramananda Raya and Svarupa Damodara: He would rise at night and go through the forest alone, taking no one with Him.
His two intimate companions pleaded with Him for a long time, and at last they said:

“You are the independent Lord. Whatever You wish, You will do; You depend on no one. But hear one request from the two
of us. You Yourself have said, ‘In your happiness is My happiness.’ Our hearts will be very happy, O merciful Lord, if
You grant us one request. We must ask that one good brahmana go with You. He will collect alms and give You Your meals,
and he will carry Your waterpot. On the forest path there is no brahmana whose food You could accept. Permit one brahmana
to go with You.” (CC, Madhya 17.8–12)

The happiness of the devotees is the Lord's joy, and the Lord's joy is the devotees' happiness; so the Lord, who had
wished to go alone, yielded to their request. He made only one condition: He would take none of His old companions, lest
the others be unhappy — let it be someone new, “whose heart is full of love.” Then Svarupa said:

“Here is Balabhadra Bhattacarya. He is full of love for You, and he is learned, saintly and noble. He came with You from
Gauda at the very start, and he wishes to visit all the holy places. With him is a brahmana servant, and on the road the
Bhattacarya himself will serve You and see to Your meals. If You take him with You, everyone will be happy, and You will
meet no hardship on the forest path. That brahmana will carry Your cloth and waterpot, and the Bhattacarya will go
begging alms and give You Your meals.” The Lord accepted Svarupa's words and took Balabhadra Bhattacarya with Him. (CC,
Madhya 17.15–20) <!-- F10 F11 F12 I1 Q5 -->

So the Lord's two closest companions entrusted the Lord Himself to Balabhadra: His care on the road, His meals, His
peace and safety in the forest. One of the biographies says that Ramananda and Svarupa gave the Lord two companions —
Balabhadra and a brahmana servant besides; but the “Charitamrita,” in its summary of the pastimes, says that “only
Balabhadra Bhattacarya stayed with Him” (Madhya 1.238), and the biographies, too, call him the Lord's “only
companion.”[^2] In the evening the Lord received Jagannatha's permission, and at the end of the night He set out in
secret, and Balabhadra went with Him; Govinda-deva writes that the Lord left “secretly … at night, together with
Balabhadra Bhattacharya” (“Gaura-krishnodaya” 14.2). <!-- F13 F14 -->

## Jharikhanda
The Lord went through the lonely forests of Jharikhanda chanting Krishna's name. Herds of tigers, elephants, rhinoceroses
and boars crossed His path, and absorbed in ecstasy He walked straight through their midst:

“Seeing this, the Bhattacarya was terrified, but by the Lord's power the animals stood aside.” (CC, Madhya 17.27)

The companion's fear here is no weakness to be smiled at: it sets off the Lord's fearlessness and power. Srila
Bhaktisiddhanta remarks that a maha-bhagavata, absorbed in love for Krishna, knows no fear — and such is the Lord on this
forest road; while a Bengali pandit in the wild forests of a foreign land plays in His pastime a role natural to him, and
it is through his eyes that we see what followed. A tiger touched by the Lord's foot sprang up and danced, crying
“Krishna!”; maddened elephants, sprinkled with water by the Lord, began to sing and dance —

“Some fell to the ground, and some trumpeted loudly. Seeing this, the Bhattacarya was struck with wonder.” (CC, Madhya
17.33)

Does and five or seven tigers walked together behind the Lord and danced —

“The tigers danced and wept together with the does, and Balabhadra Bhattacarya watched, amazed at this wondrous play.”
(CC, Madhya 17.41) <!-- F15 F16 -->

Every forest seemed to the Lord to be Vrindavana:

“Seeing a forest, He would take it for Vrndavana; seeing a hill, He would think it Govardhana. Wherever He saw a river,
He took it for the Kalindi. Overwhelmed by love, the Lord danced and fell down weeping.” (CC, Madhya 17.55–56) <!-- Q2 -->

Balabhadra meanwhile served. Along the road he gathered greens, roots and fruits. In every village five or seven brahmanas
would invite the Lord, and people brought Balabhadra grain, milk, yogurt, ghee and sugar — everyone served the Lord as he
could.[^3] Where there were no brahmanas, the Lord and His companion were invited by great souls of the lower classes:
Srila Bhaktisiddhanta explains that these were shudra mahajanas, brahmanas by initiation, for the Lord looked at
Vaishnavism, not at birth. From the vegetables gathered in the forest Balabhadra cooked dishes the Lord loved, and for the
empty stretches, where for days there was no dwelling, he kept with him grain for two or four days. The Lord was
perfectly content with the simple forest fare, and happier still with the solitude. <!-- F17 F18 F19 -->

“The Bhattacarya served Him lovingly, like a servant, and his brahmana carried the waterpot and the outer cloth.” (CC,
Madhya 17.65) <!-- F20 -->

Once the Lord spoke to him of His earlier journey — how He had set out for Vrindavana through Gauda and turned back
halfway: there had been crowds all around, but now, in the forest, no trouble had come to Him — by Krishna's mercy He was
very happy. And embracing the Bhattacharya, He said:

“By your kindness I have found so much happiness.” He replied, “You are Krsna! You are merciful! I am a fallen soul, yet
You have been kind to me. I am worthless, yet You have brought me with You. In Your mercy, my Lord, You have taken Your
meals from my hand. You have made a lowly crow equal to Garuda. You are the independent Lord, *Svayam Bhagavan*
Himself.” (CC, Madhya 17.76–79)

The words “You are Krishna” are neither flattery nor exaggeration: Balabhadra knows that his Lord is Krishna Himself, and
so he sees a miracle of mercy in His eating from his hands. And he recited a verse of Sridhara Svami:

“I bow down to Madhava, the supreme bliss, whose mercy makes the mute eloquent and lets the lame cross a mountain.” (CC,
Madhya 17.80; “Bhavartha-dipika” 1.1.1)

“In this way Balabhadra offered praise, and by his loving service he pleased the Lord.” (CC, Madhya 17.81)

So in one conversation both are revealed: the Lord is grateful for loving service and Himself ascribes His happiness to
His servant's kindness, while the servant, seeing Krishna in Him, counts himself as nothing and cannot cease to marvel
at His mercy. <!-- F21 F22 Q3 I3 -->

## Kashi, Prayaga, Mathura
In Kashi the Lord was met by Tapana Mishra. Govinda-deva writes that he saw the Lord from afar “with Balabhadra”
(“Gaura-krishnodaya” 14.10). The “Charitamrita” relates:

“With all his family he drank the water that had washed the Lord's feet, and he honoured the Bhattacarya with worship.
He invited the Lord and fed Him in his home, having Balabhadra Bhattacarya do the cooking.” (CC, Madhya 17.88–89)

Tapana Mishra quickly made everything ready for cooking, and in his house Balabhadra cooked for the Lord; after the meal
the Lord's feet were massaged by the boy Raghunatha, the future Raghunatha Bhatta Gosvami. <!-- F23 -->

From Kashi the road led through Prayaga. There, the moment He saw the Yamuna, the Lord leapt into it in love, and
Balabhadra hurriedly caught hold of Him and lifted Him out (CC, Madhya 17.150). The Lord was not drowning as a man of this
world drowns: He plunged into the Yamuna overwhelmed by prema, and Balabhadra's service was care for the Lord absorbed in
ecstasy. So it would be again later. <!-- F24 -->

In Mathura the Lord was approached by a Sanodiya brahmana, a disciple of Madhavendra Puri. Seeing His love, he guessed
that the Lord was connected with Madhavendra: “Love for Krsna is found where there is a connection with him.”

“Then the Bhattacarya told him of the connection, and hearing it the brahmana began to dance with joy.” (CC, Madhya
17.174)

The brahmana took the Lord to his home and asked Balabhadra to cook His meal. But the Lord smiled and said: “Puri Gosani
has taken his meals in your house. You yourself give Me My meal” (CC, Madhya 17.176–177). Sanodiya brahmanas were
regarded as a community in whose houses sannyasis do not eat; but Madhavendra Puri, seeing this brahmana's Vaishnava
conduct, made him his disciple and ate with him — and the Lord followed the conduct of His great predecessor.[^4]
<!-- F25 F26 -->

From then on the Sanodiya brahmana was the Lord's guide through the twelve forests of Vraja, and together with Balabhadra
he guarded Him. Once the Lord, seeing the necks of peacocks that reminded Him of Krishna, fell unconscious.

“Seeing the Lord unconscious, the brahmana and the Bhattacarya tried to revive Him. Hurriedly they took Mahaprabhu's
outer cloth, sprinkled His body with water and fanned Him with the cloth. They spoke the name of Krsna loudly in the
Lord's ear, and regaining consciousness, the Lord rolled about on the ground. In that forest, impassable with thorns, His
body was scratched and cut. The Bhattacarya took the Lord on his lap and calmed Him. In His absorption in Krsna the
Lord's mind was overwhelmed with love. Crying ‘Say it! Say it!’ He got up and began to dance. The Bhattacarya and the
brahmana sang the name of Krsna, and the Lord went on along the path, dancing all the way.” (CC, Madhya 17.219–224)

Govinda-deva tells of a similar moment: after the conversation of the parrots the Lord fell down “as if lifeless,” and
“the Bhattacharya soon brought Him back to consciousness” (“Gaura-krishnodaya” 14.28). At Radha-kunda the Lord marked
Himself with tilaka of clay from its bank and with Balabhadra's help took some of the clay along; at Brahma-kunda the
Bhattacharya cooked, and the Lord ate after bathing there (CC, Madhya 18.14, 18.21). <!-- F27 F28 -->

## Kaliya's Lake
Once a rumour spread through Vrindavana that Krishna had appeared again at Kaliya's lake: at night people saw Him dancing
on the serpent Kaliya, with jewels flashing on the serpent's hoods. Guileless Balabhadra believed it, and in the evening
he wished to go and see.

“Then the Bhattacarya said at the Lord's feet, ‘Give me permission, and I shall go and have darsana of Krsna!’ At this
the Lord gave him a slap and said, ‘You are a learned man, yet you have become a fool by listening to the words of fools.
Why would Krsna give darsana in the age of Kali? Foolish people are making an uproar out of their own delusion. Do not go
mad. Stay at home and sit quietly. You can go tomorrow night and have darsana of Krsna.’” (CC, Madhya 18.99–102)
<!-- F29 F30 -->

In the morning everything came to light: respectable people told the Lord that at night a fisherman fished from a boat
with a lamp on the lake, and from afar people took the boat for Kaliya, the lamp for his jewels and the fisherman for
Krishna Himself (CC, Madhya 18.103–106). Balabhadra's delusion was dispelled.[^5] <!-- F31 -->

This episode is not the story of a credulous man but a lesson for everyone. The Lord's slap is a teacher's caress: He
corrects His servant, and with him the whole world. In the age of Kali Krishna does not show Himself to people in that
way, for display, and to ascribe Krishna's position to an ordinary jiva is an offence; the Lord says this here too, in
the same chapter of the “Charitamrita” (Madhya 18.111–116). Srila Bhaktisiddhanta, in his chapter heading at Madhya
17.16, writes of Balabhadra that a guileless brahmana in whom the tendency to fruitive work was still strong was given
the chance to be purified in the Lord's company. This is a lesson in the power of the Lord's company: whomever He draws
close, He leads to pure devotion. And who Balabhadra is in the eternal pastimes we already know from Karnapura:
Madhurekshana, Radha's friend — and so we must not see in him a worldly man. <!-- F30 -->

## Leaving Vraja
The Lord stayed at Akrura-tirtha, and all Mathura longed to invite Him.

“All the people of Mathura — brahmanas and respectable folk — came to the Bhattacarya to invite the Lord. In a single day
ten or twenty invitations would come, but the Bhattacarya would accept only one.” (CC, Madhya 18.130–131) <!-- F32 -->

One day the Lord, sitting on the ghata of Akrura-tirtha, remembered that here Akrura had seen Vaikuntha and the people of
Vraja had seen Goloka, and He threw Himself into the Yamuna:

“With these words He leapt into the water and stayed submerged beneath it. Seeing this, Krsnadasa wept and cried out, and
the Bhattacarya quickly came and pulled the Lord out.” (CC, Madhya 18.137–138)

Govinda-deva gives the Bhattacharya's thought at that moment as well: “Alas, what would have happened had I not been
here?” (“Gaura-krishnodaya” 14.52). <!-- F33 -->

Svarupa and Ramananda had entrusted the Lord to Balabhadra so that no harm should come to Him on the road. And now,
anxious for the Lord, absorbed in prema and surrounded by crowds, he took counsel in private with his elder — the
Sanodiya brahmana, the disciple of Madhavendra Puri:

“Today I was here, and I pulled the Lord out. But if He dives in at Vrndavana, who will pull Him out? The crowds of
people, the bother of invitations, the Lord's constant absorption — none of this seems good to me. If we take the Lord
away from Vrndavana, all will be well. This is a good plan.” (CC, Madhya 18.140–142)

The brahmana proposed going to Prayaga by the road along the Ganga, through Soro-kshetra: the month of Magha had begun,
and they would be in time for the bathing during Makara-sankranti.[^6] He advised Balabhadra to tell the Lord of his
distress. Balabhadra did so: he told the Lord he could no longer bear the crowds and would be happy to reach Prayaga in
time (CC, Madhya 18.143–151). <!-- F34 F35 -->

This decision was born of love: neither Svarupa nor Ramananda had told Balabhadra to take the Lord away from Vraja in
particular — he was moved by a servant's anxiety for the Lord entrusted to his care, and he took counsel with his elder,
the disciple of Madhavendra. And the Lord agreed for His devotee's sake:

“Although the Lord had no wish to leave Vrndavana, to fulfil His devotee's wish He spoke sweet words: ‘You brought Me here
and showed Me Vrndavana. I shall never be able to repay this debt. Whatever you wish, that I will do. Wherever you take
Me, there I will go.’” (CC, Madhya 18.152–154)

The Lord calls Himself His servant's debtor — so highly does He value his love. The summary of the pastimes says the same
briefly: seeing the places of the pastimes, the Lord became overwhelmed with love, “and Balabhadra led Him away from
Mathura” (CC, Madhya 1.240). Karnapura writes that the pious Balabhadra-bhattacharya and others, seeing the Lord
senseless from excess of love, thought, “Ah, misfortune has come!” — “and as if by force led the Lord away from
Vrindavana”; and Sarvabhauma in his drama says that the Lord was “as if drawn to Himself by Jagannatha, who could not
bear separation from Him” (“Chaitanya-chandrodaya,” Act 9). In Govinda-deva the Lord leaves “in order to deliver Sanatana
and others” (“Gaura-krishnodaya” 14.59). The Lord's will is fulfilled through His servant's love.[^7] <!-- F36 -->

Next morning, after His bath, the Lord was overcome with love at the thought that He was leaving Vrindavana, though
outwardly He did not show it. Understanding His state, Balabhadra proposed going to Mahavana, that is, Gokula, and they
crossed the Yamuna by boat (CC, Madhya 18.155–157). <!-- F37 -->
