# Ishana Thakura, Servant of the Lord's Household
*Ишана Тхакур — слуга в доме Господа · @ishana-navadvipa*

## A Servant in the Lord's House
In the house of Jagannatha Mishra in Navadvipa, where Sri Gaurasundara was born and grew up, there lived a servant named
Ishana. He had served the Lord's father, carried little Nimai in his arms, served the Lord's mother, Shachi, all his life,
and after Mahaprabhu's sannyasa he remained as the keeper of His house and the support of Shachi and Vishnupriya. He
outlived almost all the Lord's companions in Navadvipa, and the next generation of acharyas — Srinivasa, Narottama,
Ramachandra — found him a very old man in the same house where he had once dandled the little Gaura on his knees.
<!-- F3 -->

Krishnadasa Kaviraja calls him simply Ishana; Vrindavana Dasa also calls him Ishana;
Devakinandana in the “Vaishnava-vandana” calls him Ishana Dasa; Narahari Chakravarti in the “Bhakti-ratnakara” calls him
Ishana, Ishana Thakura, “Sri Ishana Mahashaya,” “Ishana, dear to the Lord,” and “the Lord's Ishana.” The biographies call
him Sri Ishana Thakura, Sri Ishana Prabhu, Ishana Dasa and “Mahaprabhu's family servant.”[^1] <!-- F1 -->

By position he was the household servant (*griha-bhritya*) of Sri Jagannatha Mishra; Srila Bhaktisiddhanta
Sarasvati in the “Gaudiya-bhashya” calls him “the servant in the Lord's house” (*prabhura griha-bhritya*); he is spoken of
also as “the old servant of Sri Shachimata” and as one who served the Lord personally in His own home. But the word
should not mislead us: this was no worldly post of a hired domestic. To serve the Lord in His house — to touch Him, feed
Him and fulfil His wishes — is possible only for one whom the Lord Himself has chosen and who is dear to Him, for the Lord
accepts service only from devotees dear to Him, and that service alone bespeaks Ishana's great fortune. He is a
*parshada*, an eternal associate of the Lord who descended together with Him, and therefore he knew all truths
(*sarva-tattva-jnya*), fully aware of the divine nature of the One he served; “his knowledge of all truths is known
everywhere,” says the “Bhakti-ratnakara” (12.91). Ishana's service in the house of the Lord, of Shachi and of
Vishnupriya is the eternal service of an associate in the Navadvipa-lila. <!-- F3 F4 I1 -->

Of his origins the sources are silent. Neither Ishana's birthplace nor his parents, varna, or the year and day of his
appearance are named anywhere; nor do the primary sources say who he was in Krishna's pastimes: he does not appear in the
“Gaura-ganoddesha-dipika.”[^2] Nor are the days of his appearance and departure in the Vaishnava calendar of the Sri
Chaitanya Saraswat Math. <!-- F5 F42 -->

## A Branch of the Chaitanya Tree
Krishnadasa Kaviraja, listing the branches of Sri Chaitanya's tree, names him too:

“Srinatha Mishra, Shubhananda, Srirama, Ishana, Srinidhi, Sri Gopikanta, Bhagavan Mishra.” (CC, Adi 10.110)

In his “Anubhashya” to this verse (where Ishana is the fifty-seventh branch) Srila Bhaktisiddhanta cites three testimonies
about him: the words of the “Chaitanya-bhagavata” that Ishana served Ai, Shachi-devi, all his life and was the most
fortunate in all fourteen worlds; the couplet of the “Vaishnava-vandana” on how Shachi Thakurani loved him; and the line
of the “Bhakti-ratnakara”: “That Ishana is very dear to Nimai-chandra” (the twelfth wave, 12.95). These three voices — of
Vrindavana Dasa, Devakinandana and Narahari Chakravarti — make up our story. <!-- F2 Q1 F9 -->

## Nimai in Ishana's Arms
Ishana knew the Lord from His first days. When Mahaprabhu was born, Ishana would take Him on his lap and hold Him
tenderly; later he carried the growing Nimai in his arms, bore with all His childish pranks and fulfilled all His whims.
And Nimai grew so attached to him that He could not do without Ishana for a moment (*eka-danda*). An old brahmana of
Navadvipa, whose meeting with Srinivasa we shall describe later, told him:

“How can I describe Ishana's doings? That Ishana is very dear to Nimai-chandra; Ishana's life is Shachinandana Nimai;
without Ishana Nimai goes nowhere; in childhood Nimai was very restless — whatever whim He took up, Ishana would fulfil
it.” (“Bhakti-ratnakara” 12.95–97)

These pranks and whims are no worldly mischief of a child but the sweet *bala-lila*, the Lord's childhood pastimes, and
Ishana took part in them: the Lord Himself, the Master of all, allowed him to carry Him in his arms and to fulfil His
wishes.[^3] <!-- F6 F7 F8 Q5 -->

Ishana served in this house even before Nimai's elder brother, Vishvarupa, left for sannyasa. Many years later, showing
Srinivasa the Lord's house, he recalled how Shachi would send little Vishvambhara to call His brother home from
Advaita's assembly for the meal: “Holding the end of His elder brother's cloth, Vishvambhara, enchanting the hearts of
all, came home. At that very moment I cleaned the place and made it ready; here the two brothers sat down to eat. O dear
Srinivasa, what more is there to say? When I think of all that, my heart breaks” (“Bhakti-ratnakara” 12.1123–1125).[^4]

## Service to the Lord's Mother
Ishana is most renowned for his service to Shachi-devi. Vrindavana Dasa says of him:

“All his life Ishana served Ai; among the fourteen worlds he is supremely fortunate.” (CB, Madhya 8.74)

These words are cited both by Srila Bhaktisiddhanta in the “Anubhashya” and by Narahari Chakravarti in the
“Bhakti-ratnakara” (12.92). <!-- F10 Q2 -->

Ishana knew that service to a devotee is higher than worship of the Lord Himself, and with great love he performed all
the service in Shachi's house. And Shachi is no ordinary woman: she is non-different (*abhinna-svarupa*) from mother
Yashoda, the Lord's eternal mother. This is no belittling of service to the Lord but the very heart of the Gaudiya
Vaishnava path: service to His devotees pleases the Lord more than service to Himself. In serving Shachi, Ishana served
the Lord as well, and the Lord was pleased with him; and the Lord's blessing follows the blessing of His devotee — the
Lord's mercy comes by way of the Vaishnava's mercy. <!-- F11 I2 I3 -->

And Shachi loved him dearly. Devakinandana Dasa sings in the “Vaishnava-vandana”:

“I shall praise Ishana Dasa with joined palms —
Shachi Thakurani loved him dearly.”
(“Vaishnava-vandana,” part 2, v. 37)

The “Bhakti-ratnakara” cites this couplet too (12.94); the biographies say that Ishana was blessed with Shachi-devi's
love.[^5] Other “Vaishnava-vandanas” remember him as well: the Sanskrit one calls him “Ishana Dasa, the object of
Shachi-devi's affection” (part 1, v. 50), the “Sri Vaishnavabhidhana” lists him among the Lord's associates (part 3, v.
16), and the tripadi of the “second” Vrindavana Dasa calls him “the vessel of Ai's mercy, Ishana, whom Ai brought up”
(part 5, v. 37). <!-- F12 Q4 F13 -->

The late “Gaura-charita-chintamani” of the same Narahari Chakravarti, which sings of Gaura's eternal pastimes hour by
hour, remembers Ishana beside Shachi in these pastimes too: at midday, while Gaura plays with His associates in the
flower garden, Shachi tells her daughter-in-law to prepare the meal — “Nimai and Nitai will eat together with everyone”
— and “Shachi Devi, calling Ishana, quickly sent off the various foods” (“Gaura-charita-chintamani” 17.26, a late
source). <!-- F54 -->

The old people of Navadvipa saw that love with their own eyes. That same old brahmana, meeting Srinivasa on the road,
began his account thus: “I have just come from seeing Ishana. What can I say — who does not long for his virtues? His
knowledge of all truths is known everywhere; he served Sri Shachi-devi as was fitting; and how Shachi-devi loved Ishana I
cannot tell: I saw it with my own eyes” (“Bhakti-ratnakara” 12.90–93).[^6] <!-- F14 Q5 -->

## The Meal of Gaura and Nityananda
Ishana was blessed with service to Sri Nityananda as well, who is non-different from Baladeva — Sri Chaitanya's second
self. The “Chaitanya-bhagavata” tells how the Lord proposed to His mother that they invite Nityananda to a meal; Shachi
rejoiced and began to cook, and the Lord Himself went to fetch Nityananda (Madhya 8.51–54).[^7] When the Brothers came
and sat down side by side, Ishana came up to Them:

“Ishana gave water to wash Their feet; then Mahaprabhu and Nityananda sat down to eat.” (CB, Madhya 8.59)
<!-- F15 F16 Q3 -->

Shachi was feeding Them when suddenly she saw both of Them as four-armed boys; she fell senseless, and the
food was scattered about the room (CB, Madhya 8.62–72). It fell to Ishana to set the house in order:

“Ishana tidied the whole house, and all that was left of the meal was his. All his life Ishana served Ai; among the
fourteen worlds he is supremely fortunate.” (CB, Madhya 8.73–74)

Thus all the remnants of the meal of Gaura and Nityananda — the greatest mercy — came to him; he honoured them with
reverence. <!-- F17 -->

In the “Bhakti-ratnakara” it is Ishana himself who tells Srinivasa of this meal, standing on that very spot: “O
Srinivasa, seeing that wondrous beauty, I quickly gave water to wash [Their] feet… Having eaten, the two of Them sat
here; I quickly cleaned the place. Joyfully I gathered all the remnants from the leaf-plates; remembering all that, [my]
heart grows distraught” (12.1830, 1834–1835). <!-- F48 -->

On these verses Srila Bhaktisiddhanta writes in the “Gaudiya-bhashya”: “Ishana, the servant in the Lord's house,
gathered up the scattered food and tidied the house. There is no limit to Ishana's fortune: he spent his whole life in
the service of the Lord's mother; and after Mahaprabhu's sannyasa the servant Ishana, obtaining the service of his
Master's mother and wife, became the most blessed (*dhanyatidhanya*) among the blessed servants of the world.”[^8]
<!-- F18 -->

## Keeper of the House after the Lord's Sannyasa
When Mahaprabhu took sannyasa and left home to live in Puri, the care of His house, of His mother Shachi-devi and of His
wife Vishnupriya-devi fell to Ishana. Together with him Vamshivadana Thakura was blessed with this service: both became
the servants and protectors of Vishnupriya and Shachi. <!-- F19 F22 -->

Of the first morning without the Lord Vasu Ghosha sings. Shachi, waking, learns that her son has gone and lies as if
dead: “striking her head with her hand, she gestures to Ishana: ‘Gora has gone, leaving Nadia’” (pada of Vasu Ghosha,
“Pada-kalpataru” 2222). In her grief she cannot speak.

This is no worldly despair but *vipralambha*, the separation of the Lord's mother from her son — the feeling in which
the eternal associates live when the Lord hides Himself from their eyes. And in that separation it is to Ishana that
Shachi turns. <!-- F20 -->

Yet even after sannyasa the Lord did not abandon His mother. He Himself told in Puri how on the day of Vijaya-dashami He
came unseen to the house in Navadvipa and ate the food she had offered to Gopala. But Shachi saw that the pots were still full,
and wondered:

“She called Ishana, had the place cleaned and plastered again, and once more offered food to Gopala.” (CC, Madhya
15.63) <!-- F21 -->

Ishana remained in the house even after Shachi had departed from this world. After the disappearance of Mahaprabhu and the departure of
Shachimata, Sri Vamshivadana Thakura served Vishnupriya-devi and Sri Ishana Thakura. <!-- F23 -->

## Srinivasa at Vishnupriya's
Soon after Mahaprabhu disappeared in Nilachala, the young Srinivasa came to Mayapur at the bidding of Gadadhara Pandita.
At the Yogapitha, in the Lord's house, he saw Vishnupriya and beside her the old Vaishnava Sri Ishana Prabhu.[^9]
<!-- F24 -->

The “Prema-vilasa” tells in detail of his care for the youth. Ishana, compassionate to living beings and ever filled with
prema at the sight of a great bhagavata, noticed by a ghat on the Ganga an emaciated boy weeping and calling for Gadadhara
Pandita. Ishana understood that Chaitanya's power was in him and told Ishvari — Vishnupriya — about him. With her
permission he brought Srinivasa choice provisions; and when half a ser of rice cooked by the youth satisfied eleven
Vaishnavas, Ishana told her of this too. At her bidding he brought Srinivasa into the inner apartments and presented him:
“Here is Srinivasa; he bows down to you; he is your dear servant.” And afterwards he reported to her that the youth wept
all night long, calling to Pandita Gosani, and never closed his eyes (“Prema-vilasa” 4.173–222). <!-- F25 F43 -->

Towards the end of one night Chaitanya and Nityananda appeared in a dream and commanded that Srinivasa be sent to Abhirama: “Send him
to Abhirama, with Ishana as his companion.” The maids called Ishana, but he was fast asleep and did not hear; waking late, he thought himself
most unfortunate, came with joined palms to Ishvari, at her word brought Srinivasa, and received the order to take him to Abhirama
(4.236–244).
This is the humility of a servant who reproaches himself for the slightest delay in service, not a worldly failing.
<!-- F26 -->

Their way lay through Khardaha. There Ishana announced a wondrous boy weeping at the gate and went out with Virachandra to
fetch him; Jahnava, blessing Srinivasa, ordered a letter to be sent with Ishana to Abhirama. Ishana handed Abhirama the
letter — “Ishvari's command: grant prema to the boy”; Abhirama seated him, sent him to fetch Srinivasa, and the next
morning gave Ishana to Srinivasa as his companion on the way to Vrindavana (“Prema-vilasa” 5.11–64). <!-- F27 -->

## The Last of the Navadvipa Associates
Years passed, and one by one Ishana saw his contemporaries leave this world. When Narottama came to Navadvipa, Ishana,
“dear to the Lord,” met him in the Lord's house: in great love he embraced the youth and said with tears: “It is good
that you have come at such a time; from the Vaishnavas I have heard all about you” (“Bhakti-ratnakara” 8.88–92). He
touched Narottama's head and blessed him. Narottama also saw in Mayapur other aged associates of the Lord, grieving over
His disappearance — Shuklambara Brahmachari, Murari, Damodara and Gadadhara Dasa. When leaving, Narottama asked Ishana's
leave to go to Nilachala, and Ishana lovingly told him to go quickly to Sri Kshetra: “Day by day it grows darker
everywhere. These days I have had all the news from there through people”; and how Gopinatha Acharya and the others
now fare by the Lord's will “cannot be told.” And after that he advised him to go to
Shrikhanda and Katwa (8.103–108). <!-- F28 F29 -->

When Jahnava came back to Navadvipa, Mahaprabhu's birthplace, Ishana was already a very old man. Hardly any of the Lord's
associates were left there; “the few who were there — Sri Ishana and the others — hearing of Ishvari's coming, came
forward to meet [her],” and on seeing her they seemed to regain their life (11.751–753). Before leaving, Jahnava carefully
consoled Ishana and the others and told them that Srinivasa would soon come (11.768). And earlier Srinivasa had told her: “In Navadvipa almost all have
disappeared — only the Lord's Ishana remains,” and added: “Ishana told me to come” (11.721–723). <!-- F30 F31 Q6 F53 -->

In the house of Jagannatha Mishra in Mayapur Ramachandra Kaviraja too, returning from Vraja to Gauda, found the very aged
Ishana Thakura: he introduced himself and bowed at his feet, and Ishana blessed him generously. According to one of the
biographies, Shyamananda (Duhkhi Krishnadasa) as well found the very old Ishana Thakura in Sri Gauranga's house: he bowed
to him, and Ishana asked who he was and, having heard, blessed him.[^10] <!-- F32 F33 -->

## Guide through Navadvipa
Ishana's last great service was to show Srinivasa Acharya, Narottama Thakura and Ramachandra Kaviraja the places of
Mahaprabhu's pastimes — he led them round the nine islands of Navadvipa. One of the biographies calls him here “Sri
Ishana Prabhu, the venerable devotee of Mahaprabhu's household.”[^11] <!-- F34 F36 -->

The “Bhakti-ratnakara” tells of this in detail. On the road to Mayapur the three met that same old brahmana: he said that
he had gone that day to see Ishana and that Ishana had told him beforehand of their coming — he was waiting for them in
the house of Jagannatha Mishra (12.107–108). In the Lord's house they found him alone: he was radiant, tears ran down his
face, and he was calling: “Vishvambhara!” (12.112–115). Ishana embraced all three (12.117–118) and said that he remembered the
places of the Lord's pastimes — what he had seen himself and what he had heard from the old people — and that this was why he had called Srinivasa
through Narottama (12.126–127). “I am old and have lost my strength,” he confessed, “but now my strength has suddenly
returned” (12.131). The next morning he led them through Nadia (12.134–135) and all along the way told them about each
place, and at times sang padas (12.625, 701, 752, 772, 809, 849, 2117, 4031). <!-- F35 -->

The way led over all nine islands: from Mayapur to Antardvipa (Atopur), Simantadvipa (Simuliya), Godrumadvipa
(Gadigachha), Madhyadvipa (Majita), Koladvipa (Kuliya) and Samudragarhi, Ritudvipa (Champahatta), Jahnudvipa (Jannagar)
with Vidyanagara, Modadrumadvipa (Maugachhi), through Vaikunthapura and Mahatpur to Rudradvipa (Rahupur), and by way of
Suvarna-vihara back to Mayapur. At each place Ishana explained where its name came from — which god or sage had once
beheld Gaura there or learnt of His future pastimes — and linked the place with the Lord's pastimes; now he went on “in
joy,” now “floating in tears,” unable to become calm (12.134–850).[^12] <!-- F51 -->

Back in Mayapur, Ishana entered Mishra's house — and “was overwhelmed with prema, remembering the Purusha.” Growing calm,
he said softly to Srinivasa: “This house of Mishra is the Mahayogapitha… unfathomable to others, this house is like a
lotus” (12.849–852). And he began to show where everything had happened: where Jagannatha Mishra and Shachi worshipped
Vishnu, where the Lord appeared, where He crawled and played as a child, how He grew up, studied, married, how He
returned from Gaya and how the kirtans began in this house — up to the last days before sannyasa. “O Srinivasa, in this
house of the Lord I have shown which pastimes He performed in which places,” he concluded, and led the three through
Mayapur and its surroundings: to the kadamba under which little Nimai played, to the house of Vallabha where the Lord
married Lakshmipriya, to Srivasa's house, to Madhai's ghat, along the path of the nagara-kirtana to the Kazi's house —
and so on to the weeping of all Nadia for the departed Lord; he told also of what came after the sannyasa — of
Nityananda's wanderings through Gauda and His marriage (12.851–4021).[^13] The author of the “Bhakti-ratnakara”
himself, in the list of its contents, says just this of the twelfth wave: the three “went round Nadiya with Sri Ishana”
(BR, “Contents of the Book,” 24). <!-- F45 F46 F50 F55 -->

Again and again Ishana speaks of himself as an eyewitness: “O Srinivasa, I saw [it] to my eyes' fill” (12.1359); “All
that I saw rises ever in my mind” (12.1933); “Staying here, I saw the Lord's pastimes” (12.2699); and ending his account
he confesses: “All that I saw I cannot tell; when I think of all that, my heart bursts and I die” (12.4030). Having
said this, “Ishana Mahashaya, in the rapture of prema, utterly lost his composure”; coming to himself, he lay down with
his three guests to sleep at night in the Lord's courtyard (12.4031–4032). That night the eternal Navadvipa was revealed
to Srinivasa in a dream. <!-- F49 F52 -->

His old age and frailty are also a pastime. The Lord kept His eternal associate in this world in order to hand over
Navadvipa through him to the next acharyas, and the strength that returned to the old man at the sight of Srinivasa is
His mercy, not a worldly recovery. Ishana's solitude in the house where Nimai's footsteps once sounded, and his cry of
“Vishvambhara!”, are the separation of an eternal servant from his Master; and his tears at each place of the pastimes are that same prema
of separation, not the weakness of old age. One of the biographies adds that when Ishana
showed them these places they had already fallen into utter ruin — and from this one may judge how long he lived.[^14]
<!-- F37 -->

## Farewell and Departure
Ishana remained long in the Lord's manifest pastimes: he disappeared only after Vishnupriya-devi and almost all of
Mahaprabhu's devotees in Navadvipa.[^15] The three acharyas received his loving blessing and embrace and took leave of
him. Embracing them, Ishana could not hold back his tears; he wished to say something — and could not, and only by a
sign of his hand showed that this was their last meeting (“Bhakti-ratnakara” 13.8–11). <!-- F38 F39 -->

And so it was. The acharyas went to Shrikhanda to Raghunandana Thakura, and there Srinivasa told Raghunandana: “In the
Lord's house only Ishana remained — what power have I to tell of his endless virtues! On the way I heard from people's lips: Ishana Thakura has disappeared”
(“Bhakti-ratnakara” 13.20–21).[^16] The eternal associate had departed from this world into the Lord's unseen
pastimes. <!-- F40 Q7 F44 -->

When exactly this happened is unknown: according to the “Bhakti-ratnakara,” in Navadvipa, soon after he had led the three
acharyas through the places of the pastimes, after the Lord's disappearance. The day of his departure, like that of his
appearance, is not in the calendar of the Sri Chaitanya Saraswat Math. <!-- F41 -->

## Significance
Ishana is one of the branches of Sri Chaitanya's tree and an eternal associate of the Lord. In him is found what rarely
meets in a single life: he held the infant Gaura in his arms, and he it was who blessed the acharyas who carried Gaura's
teaching further. <!-- F2 F4 -->

His life teaches that only one dear to the Lord can serve Him personally — and such service is itself a sign of the
greatest fortune. All his life he served the Lord's mother, seeing in Shachi mother Yashoda, and thereby pleased the Lord
more than he would have by worshipping Him directly, for service to the devotee is higher. That is why Vrindavana Dasa
called him the most fortunate in all fourteen worlds, and Srila Bhaktisiddhanta the most blessed of the blessed servants
of the world. And finally, he shows that the Lord's mercy comes through the mercy of His devotee: the Lord blesses those
whom His Vaishnavas have blessed first. <!-- F10 F18 I1 I2 I3 -->

He remained in the Lord's house when almost all had gone, and preserved for later generations the living memory of
Navadvipa. <!-- F38 -->
