# The Sanodiya Brahmana of Mathura
*Санодия-брахман из Матхуры · @krishnadasa-mathura*

## The Old Brahmana of Mathura
When Sri Chaitanya came to Mathura, the first to fall at His feet and dance with Him was an old brahmana of that
city — a disciple of Sri Madhavendra Puri. He received the Lord in his home and fed Him, showed Him the holy places of
Mathura, went with Him through the twelve forests of Vraja, and later, together with Balabhadra Bhattacharya, led Him
away from Vraja and accompanied Him as far as Prayaga. Krishnadasa Kaviraja never once calls him by name: in the
“Chaitanya-charitamrita” he is “the brahmana,” “the Sanodiya brahmana,” “the disciple of Madhavendra Puri,” and of
himself he says, “I am a brahmana of Mathura” (Madhya 17.179, 17.190, 18.129, 18.169). Govinda-deva in the
“Gaura-krishnodaya” calls him “the brahmana of Mathura, the disciple of Madhavendra,” and Narahari Chakravarti “the
Sanodiya vipra” and “the best of the twice-born, dear to Gaura.” The biographies call him the Sanodiya Vipra, Sri
Sanodiya Vipra, the brahmana of Mathura, the old brahmana, and, together with his Rajput companion, “the two
Vrajavasis.” Murari Gupta and Lochana Dasa call the brahmana who received the Lord in Mathura and guided Him through
Vraja Krishnadasa; our database takes him to be the same person.[^1] <!-- F1 -->

Of his worldly life little is known. The biographies say plainly that we know neither his birthplace, nor his father
and mother, nor even his name; the sources give neither the year and day of his appearance nor when and where he left
this world, and his days are not found in the Vaishnava calendar of the Sri Chaitanya Saraswat Math. One of the
biographies remarks that what matters in his life is not his origin, of which nothing is known, but the lessons of his
example. <!-- F2 F8 I5 -->

He lived in Mathura itself: so he says of himself before the Pathans, and so the Pathans address him — “You two are men
of the west, from Mathura” (CC Madhya 18.169, 18.172). Murari Gupta writes, “There [in Mathura] a certain brahmana, best
of the best”; Lochana Dasa, “There lived [there] a foremost brahmana named Krishnadasa.” When the Lord met him he was
already advanced in years: “noble and simple-hearted, an aged brahmana,” the Lord calls him (Madhya 17.165), and the
biographies call him simply “the old brahmana.” <!-- F3 F4 -->

By birth he belonged to the Sanodiya community: “that brahmana was a Sanodiya” (Madhya 17.179). One of the biographies
adds that he appeared in a family of brahmanas who served as priests to the “Sanovar” caste, that is, to dealers in
gold. What this means, and why it matters for his story, we shall say below, when we come to the Lord's meal in his
home. <!-- F5 -->

One of the biographies calls him the Sanodiya brahmana “who kept himself hidden,” a resident of Sri Mathura. This is not
secrecy but the concealment of a humble Vaishnava: he made no show either of his love for Krishna or of his
discipleship under the great Madhavendra, and it came to light only when the Lord Himself came to Mathura. <!-- F9 -->

## Disciple of Madhavendra Puri
Long before the Lord came to Mathura, Sri Madhavendra Puri had come there in the course of his pilgrimage. He visited
this brahmana's house, gave him mantra, accepted him as his disciple and accepted food from his hands. The brahmana
himself would later tell the Lord:

“The brahmana said, ‘Sripada Madhavendra Puri came to the city of Mathura in the course of his wanderings. Out of
kindness he came to my home. He made me his disciple and accepted his meal (*bhiksa*) from my hands. That great soul
brought Gopala to light and served Him, and to this day Gopala is served at Govardhana.’” (CC Madhya 17.166–168)
<!-- F10 F12 -->

By custom sannyasis did not accept food in the houses of Sanodiyas. But Madhavendra looked not at birth but at the
heart:

“Nevertheless, seeing his Vaisnava conduct, Puri made him his disciple and accepted meals from him.” (CC Madhya 17.180)

One of the biographies puts it thus: Madhavendra recognized him as a Vaishnava, that is, as a pure brahmana, and
therefore, against custom, made him his disciple and ate at his house. Another adds that by this Madhavendra showed how
*daiva-varnashrama* works — the divine order in which a person is judged by his service to the Lord, not by his birth.
<!-- F11 -->

By his guru's mercy, says one of the biographies, love for Krishna awoke in him. It was this that the Lord saw in him
when He asked, “Where did you get this treasure of love?” And he himself, seeing the Lord's love, recognized by it that
the Lord was connected with Madhavendra: “Love for Krsna is found where there is a connection with him. Without him
there is not even a scent of such love anywhere” (CC Madhya 17.173). One of the biographies cites these words in its
chapter on Madhavendra and introduces them thus: wherever there is the *prema* of separation, there is surely a
connection with Madhavendra Puri — and even the brahmana of Mathura “who kept himself hidden” recognized it in the
Lord. <!-- F13 F14 Q3 I4 -->

## The Meeting at Keshava's Temple
This was on the journey when Mahaprabhu, after the Ratha-yatra, left Puri for Vrindavana by the forest road through
Jharikhanda. Ramananda Raya and Svarupa Damodara gave Him Balabhadra Bhattacharya as His companion, and with him a
brahmana servant. Through Kashi and Prayaga the Lord came to Mathura. The sources do not name the year of the journey;
by our chronology the Lord's journey to Vrindavana and Prayaga falls in 1514–1516. <!-- F15 -->

“Arriving in Mathura, He bathed at Visrama-tirtha, and at the birthplace He saw Kesava and bowed down to Him. Rapt in
love, He danced, sang and roared again and again, and the people were astonished to see the Lord's rapture of love.”
(CC Madhya 17.156–157) <!-- F16 -->

And then our brahmana came to the Lord:

“A brahmana fell down and clasped the Lord's feet, and then, overwhelmed with love, he danced with the Lord. Dancing in
love, the two embraced each other, and with arms raised they both called out, ‘Hari! Krsna!’” (CC Madhya 17.158–159)

The whole temple resounded with the name of Hari. Watching the Lord dance, the people said, “Love such as this is never
of this world,” and concluded firmly that He was in every way an avatara of Krishna who had come to Mathura to deliver
the people (Madhya 17.161–163). <!-- F17 F18 -->

Murari Gupta tells of this meeting in his own way. Seeing Hari, the brahmana lost all composure in love: his hair
bristled, his voice faltered, and he fell at the feet of the Lord of the world. The Lord asked, “Who are you, good sir,
whose composure love has shattered? By good fortune I have seen you.” And he replied:

“‘I am Your servant, O Lord, treasury of mercy. Though I am Krishnadasa in name only, yet I have had the good fortune of
seeing You. O treasury of mercy, purify me with the dust of the Vaishnavas' feet, O Gaura, youthful son of Nanda!’”
(Murari Gupta, “Sri Krishna-chaitanya-charitamrita” 4.2.10–11)

Krishnadasa means “servant of Krishna.” In Lochana Dasa, the Lord, at the very sight of Madhupura, cried “Akrura!
Akrura!”, fell, and lay unconscious in separation for three days, while people came to look at this marvel. Krishnadasa,
who lived there, saw Him and thought, “From where has this jewel among men come? Is this Shuka or Prahlada?” When the
Lord asked, he told his name: “My name is Krishnadasa.”[^2] <!-- F19 F20 -->

## “You Are the Guru”
After the dance the Lord took the brahmana aside:

“Then Mahaprabhu took that brahmana aside, sat with him in private and asked him: ‘You are noble and simple-hearted, an
aged brahmana. Where did you get this treasure of love?’” (CC Madhya 17.164–165)[^3]

The brahmana told Him of Madhavendra Puri — how he had come to his house, accepted him as a disciple and brought Gopala
to light (Madhya 17.166–168). And then something happened that the brahmana had never expected:

“Hearing this, the Lord bowed down at the brahmana's feet, and the brahmana in fear fell at the Lord's feet. The Lord
said, ‘You are in the position of a guru, and I am like your disciple. It is not proper for a guru to bow down to a
disciple.’” (CC Madhya 17.169–170)[^4] <!-- F21 Q6 F22 F23 Q2 -->

The Lord, the Supreme Himself, needs no guru. But He keeps *maryada* — the proper order of relationships set for
devotees — and honours this brahmana as the godbrother of His own guru, Ishvara Puri, a disciple of the same
Madhavendra. So, in the words of one of the biographies, He teaches that the godbrothers of the guru are worthy of the
disciple's reverence, and that all that is connected with the guru is to be honoured as the guru. And the brahmana falls
at His feet in fear, for he knows Who stands before him. <!-- I2 -->

He objected:

“Hearing this, the brahmana was astonished and said in fear, ‘You are a sannyasi — why do You speak like this? But
seeing Your love, I infer that You have some connection with Madhavendra Puri; I am sure of it.’” (CC Madhya
17.171–172) <!-- F24 -->

“Then the Bhattacarya told him of the connection, and hearing it the brahmana began to dance with joy.” (CC Madhya
17.174)

According to one of the biographies, the brahmana himself asked Balabhadra the name of the Lord's guru and, his guess
confirmed, rejoiced. Another says more: he realized that Mahaprabhu was the son of the king of Vraja Himself. The same
sounds in Murari — “O Gaura, youthful son of Nanda!” — and in Lochana, where Krishnadasa tells the Lord, “I know for
certain — You are the son of Nanda,” later, “Your sannyasa is only a disguise,” and at parting, “You are that very
Krishna — this I have known for certain.” <!-- F25 F26 -->

## The Meal in the Sanodiya's House
“Then the brahmana took the Lord to his own home and of his own accord rendered Him many kinds of service. He had the
Bhattacarya cook for the Lord's meal, but Mahaprabhu smiled and said: ‘Puri Gosani has taken his meals in your house.
You yourself give Me My meal — this is My instruction. “Whatever the best of men does, others do the same; whatever
standard he sets, the world follows.”’” (CC Madhya 17.175–178)

One of the biographies calls the Lord here the guardian of *daiva-varnashrama-dharma*, which is favourable to pure
bhakti: by the true meaning of the shastras He teaches the people. Another gives His words thus: “This I learned from his
example.”[^5] <!-- F27 F28 F29 F30 -->

To understand why the brahmana at first hesitated to cook himself, we must say something of his community.

“That brahmana was a Sanodiya, and sannyasis do not eat in a Sanodiya's house.” (CC Madhya 17.179)[^6]

In Srila Bhaktisiddhanta's edition the verse is headed: “because of his family by birth (*shaukra*), that brahmana's food
is not accepted.” Srila Bhaktivinoda Thakura explains in the “Amrita-pravaha-bhashya”: in the west the vaishyas are
divided into Agarwals, Kalwars, Sanovars and other communities; the Agarwals are very pure, while the Kalwars, Sanovars
and some others are held to have fallen through the faults of their occupations. The brahmanas who serve as priests to
the Kalwars and Sanovars are called Sanodiya brahmanas; the Sanovars are dealers in gold, and their priests are the
“Sanovar brahmanas.” Because they serve as priests to such communities, they themselves are counted as fallen, and
sannyasis do not eat in their houses. The biographies give this explanation almost word for word.[^7] <!-- F6 F7 Q4 -->

This reasoning concerns the social standing of a family by birth, not a judgment of the brahmana himself. He is a
Vaishnava, a disciple of Madhavendra, and one of the biographies calls him outright “a pure brahmana.” Srila
Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati writes of him that this brahmana was a pure devotee: although by caste rules one was not to
accept even water from his hands, he was established in the truth and in *daiva-varnashrama*, which is favourable to
pure bhakti. And those who judge devotees by worldly caste — by *adaiva-varnashrama* — or who disparage maha-prasada, the
Lord without hesitation called wicked fools. <!-- I3 Q5 -->

The brahmana answered the Lord humbly:

“‘To give You a meal is my great good fortune. You are the Lord; You are not bound by rules of conduct. But foolish
people will censure You, and I will not be able to bear the words of those wicked people.’” (CC Madhya 17.182–183)
<!-- F31 -->

He spoke the truth: the Lord is bound by no rules. And he feared not for himself but for the Lord's good name: by the
custom of the time fools would revile a sannyasi who had eaten in a Sanodiya's house. Srila Bhaktisiddhanta explains why
the brahmana fearlessly calls such people fools and wicked: they are those who oppose the Lord and pure bhakti and see
caste in a Vaishnava. <!-- F32 -->

And yet the Lord, free from all rules, follows for the people's instruction the conduct of the great devotees. He
replied:

“The Lord said, ‘The sruti, the smrti and all the sages are not of one opinion; they teach many different kinds of
dharma. The conduct of a sadhu is what establishes dharma, and the conduct of Puri Gosani is the essence of dharma.’”
(CC Madhya 17.184–185)

And He cited the verse of the “Mahabharata”: the path is the one by which the *mahajanas*, the great souls, have gone
(Madhya 17.186). <!-- F33 -->

Thus Madhavendra, and after him the Lord Himself, by accepting food from a Sanodiya, showed what *daiva-varnashrama* is:
a Vaishnava is judged not by social rank, and dharma is set by the conduct of the mahajanas, not by custom. <!-- I1 -->

“Then the brahmana served the Lord His meal.” (CC Madhya 17.187)

Govinda-deva tells the same briefly:

“Seeing Him as a wonder, a certain brahmin of Mathura, a disciple of Madhavendra in that forest land, joyfully, honouring
[Him] as a guru, brought the Lord to his home and fed Him.” (“Gaura-krishnodaya” 14.14)

And the Lord, he goes on, was pleased with his devotion (14.15). In Murari, having gone round Mathura, the Lord gladly
accepted bhiksha in Krishnadasa's house, and afterwards, while Krishnadasa served His feet, spoke of the sweetness of
Krishna's bliss (4.4.24–25). In Lochana the Lord spent that day in Krishnadasa's house, and all night Krishnadasa told
Him of the Mathura-mandala. <!-- F34 F35 -->

After the meal all Mathura came to the brahmana's house: “people came by the hundreds of thousands, beyond counting.” The
Lord came out to them, raised His arms and called out, “Haribol!” — and the people danced, mad with love, calling out
the name of Hari (CC Madhya 17.187–189). <!-- F36 -->

## Guide Through Mathura and Vraja
From that day the brahmana became the Lord's guide.

“The Lord bathed at the twenty-four ghats of the Yamuna, and the brahmana showed Him the holy places. He saw Svayambhu,
Visrama, Dirgha-visnu, Bhutesvara, Mahavidya, Gokarna and many other places.” (CC Madhya 17.190–191)

Govinda-deva too writes that the Lord saw the many holy places of Mathura “with that brahmin of Mathura”
(“Gaura-krishnodaya” 14.16). <!-- F37 -->

“When the Lord wished to see the forests, He took that same brahmana with Him.” (CC Madhya 17.192)

So he became the Lord's companion in going round the twelve forests of Vraja; one of the biographies recalls how with him
the Lord went round the forests, listening to the conversation of the parrots. Srila Bhaktivinoda Thakura lists these
forests: east of the Yamuna — Bhadra, Bilva, Loha, Bhandira and Maha; west of it — Madhu, Tala, Kumuda, Bahula, Kamya,
Khadira and Vrindavana itself. One of the biographies, too, draws on Madhya 17.164–168 and 17.192 when it speaks of him in
its chapter on Madhavendra. <!-- F38 Q1 -->

Once in the forest the Lord saw the necks of peacocks, which reminded Him of Krishna, and fell unconscious. The brahmana
was at hand:

“Seeing the Lord unconscious, the brahmana and the Bhattacarya tried to revive Him. Hurriedly they took Mahaprabhu's
outer cloth, sprinkled His body with water and fanned Him with the cloth. They spoke the name of Krsna loudly in the
Lord's ear.” (CC Madhya 17.219–221)

Coming to Himself, the Lord sprang up crying “Say it! Say it!” and went on, dancing.

“The Bhattacarya and the brahmana sang the name of Krsna, and the Lord went on along the path, dancing all the way. The
brahmana was amazed to see the Lord's rapture of love, and he grew anxious about how to keep the Lord safe.” (CC Madhya
17.224–225)

The Lord's unconsciousness is not the swoon of a sick man but the depth of His love for Krishna, and the brahmana's
service was care for the Lord immersed in prema. <!-- F42 -->

He was with the Lord further on as well. At Arishtagrama the Lord asked where Radha-kunda was, but neither the local
people nor “the brahmana with Him” knew — and the all-knowing Lord Himself revealed the forgotten holy place (CC Madhya
18.4–5). Returning from Gokula to Mathura, the Lord “stayed in the house of that same brahmana” — at the house of
Madhavendra Puri's disciple, Srila Bhaktisiddhanta explains — and then, because of the crowds, went away to secluded
Akrura-tirtha (Madhya 18.69–70). From there, according to Govinda-deva, He went again in the morning to Vrindavana “with
those two brahmins” — that is, with him and with Balabhadra (“Gaura-krishnodaya” 14.43). <!-- F43 F44 F45 -->

While the Lord stayed at Akrura-tirtha, the brahmana served Him in his own way:

“That brahmana, the disciple of Madhava Puri, arranged invitations for the Lord in house after house in Mathura.” (CC
Madhya 18.129)

Ten or twenty invitations came each day; Balabhadra accepted only one, and those who could not invite the Lord
themselves pleaded with the Sanodiya brahmana to persuade Him (Madhya 18.130–132). Srila Bhaktisiddhanta, in his heading
to these verses, says: the Sanodiya brahmana gives all the pious people of Mathura the chance to serve the Lord and so
delivers them. <!-- F46 -->

## Krishnadasa in Murari Gupta and Lochana Dasa
Murari Gupta and Lochana Dasa tell of the Lord's guide in Vraja at much greater length than Kaviraja Gosvami. In Murari,
hearing his answer, the Lord says: “It is you who are truly a servant of Krishna: you know all the secret pastimes of Sri
Krishna's abode — tell them.” Krishnadasa asks, “Place Your feet in my heart and reveal Your own Madhu-mandala,” and the
Lord answers in a voice as deep as a thundercloud:

“‘By My command may the pastimes of Sri Krishna and the holy places shine forth to you with ease on every side.’”
(Murari Gupta 4.2.14)

Krishnadasa promised to show Him everything, danced and wept, and that night they talked of the pastimes of Vraja.
<!-- F39 -->

In Lochana the Lord asks to be shown the Mathura-mandala “place by place.” The brahmana answers, “I do not know all the
places; I know only the places of the twelve forests.” The Lord laughs in the bliss of prema and manifests His power in
his body — and he says, “I will show everything; from Krishna's birth to the slaying of Kamsa — I will tell it all.” The
Lord embraces him. <!-- F40 -->

Here it matters to understand what is taking place. The Lord “asks” about the holy places and “asks to be shown” them —
but this is the play of One who is Krishna Himself; not for nothing does Lochana say, “Ishvara Himself plays a prank on
Krishnadasa.” And that the Lord “manifested His power in his body” is the bestowal of power, *shakti-sanchara*, by which a
Vaishnava reveals what is hidden: not worldly inspiration nor a guide's know-how, but the Lord's mercy disclosing His own
pastimes.

Further on, in both poets, Krishnadasa describes to the Lord the Yamuna and the twelve forests — seven on the western bank
and five on the eastern; shows Him Mathura — its walls, Kamsa's palace, the prison, the houses of Uddhava and Kubja, the
arena, Gatashrama, the ghats; tells Him of the measure of the Mathura-mandala — eighty-four kroshas — and of the lake
Setubandha; ferries the Lord across the Yamuna to Mahavana and leads Him through Vrindavana, Govardhana, Nandishvara, and
the Khadira, Kumuda and Ambika forests, telling of Akrura and of the slaying of Kamsa. In Murari, at the Lord's
thunderous “hum” “the best of brahmanas was frightened” and, joining his palms, went on with his account; on a sleepless
night the Lord begs him, “Krishnadasa, speak to Me! This night torments Me”; and hearing of the divine Child, the Lord
embraces him with love. In Lochana, seeing a grove of ashokas burst into bloom at the Lord's glance, Krishnadasa says,
“Your sannyasa is only a disguise,” and weeps at His feet. These accounts themselves are about Krishna's pastimes, not
about him, and we do not retell them here. <!-- F41 -->

In both poets the parting takes place in Vraja. In Murari, when Krishnadasa had shown the Lord the whole Vraja-mandala,
the Lord said:

“‘As the nectar of the rasa of Krishna's stories has softened My heart, so may Krishnachandra Himself be gracious to
you.’ He replied, ‘I am Your servant, and You are Krishna, the abode of Shri. Make it so, O Lord, that I know no one but
You.’” (Murari Gupta 4.13.2–3)

The Lord blessed him, embraced him and departed “surrounded by brahmanas.” In Lochana Krishnadasa, falling at His feet,
entreats Him: “I am in great distress — do not go away, deceiving me! You are that very Krishna — this I have known for
certain,” and the Lord answers: “By your grace I have seen the secret places… may Krishna be gracious to you,
Krishnadasa!” According to the “Chaitanya-charitamrita,” however, the Sanodiya brahmana did not part from the Lord in
Vraja but went on with Him as far as Prayaga.[^8] <!-- F47 -->

## Leading the Lord Away from Vraja
In Vraja the Lord's love grew day by day. Once, sitting on the bank at Akrura-tirtha, He remembered that here Akrura had
seen Vaikuntha and the people of Vraja Goloka, sprang up and threw Himself into the water. Krishnadasa the Rajput cried
out, and Balabhadra leapt in after Him and pulled Him out (CC Madhya 18.135–138). Then Balabhadra turned to our
brahmana:

“Then the Bhattacarya took that brahmana aside, and sitting in a secluded place they made a plan. ‘Today I was here, and
I pulled the Lord out. But if He dives in at Vrndavana, who will pull Him out? The crowds of people, the bother of
invitations, the Lord's constant absorption — none of this seems good to me. If we take the Lord away from Vrndavana,
all will be well. This is a good plan.’” (CC Madhya 18.139–142)

The biographies say the same: Balabhadra, alarmed by the Lord's frequent transformations of love, consulted “Sri
Sanodiya brahmana, the disciple of Madhavendra Puripada,” and they decided that the Lord must not be left wandering in
Vraja. Govinda-deva writes that Balabhadra consulted “in private with the brahmin of Mathura,” alarmed by the invasion of
the crowds (“Gaura-krishnodaya” 14.53).[^9] <!-- F48 F49 -->

How to lead the Lord away was the Sanodiya brahmana's own idea:

“The brahmana said, ‘Let us take the Lord to Prayaga. If we go by the road along the bank of the Ganga, the journey will
be pleasant. Let us first go to Soro-ksetra and bathe in the Ganga, and then travel on that road with the Lord. The month
of Magha has begun. If we go now, we shall have some days of bathing at Prayaga during Makara. Tell Him something of your
own distress, and mention the full moon of Makara (*makara-pancasi*) at Prayaga.’” (CC Madhya 18.143–146)[^10]
<!-- F50 -->

Balabhadra told the Lord both of his distress and of this plan. The Lord had no wish to leave Vrindavana, but to fulfil
His devotee's wish He answered, “You brought Me here and showed Me Vrndavana. I shall never be able to repay this debt.
Whatever you wish, that I will do” (CC Madhya 18.152–154). The biographies add that the Lord accepted the counsel also
out of reverence for the Sanodiya brahmana as the godbrother of His guru; one of them says the Lord could not refuse
him. The one does not exclude the other.[^11] <!-- F51 -->

The brahmana knew the road along the Ganga:

“Krsnadasa, full of love, and that brahmana — these two knew the road along the Ganga well.” (CC Madhya 18.158)

So he and Krishnadasa the Rajput went along as guides. Govinda-deva writes that the Lord departed with five companions
— three followers from Gauda and two devotees from Mathura — and that the eminent people of Mathura, stricken with
grief, followed Him (“Gaura-krishnodaya” 14.57).[^12] <!-- F52 F53 -->

## Before the Pathans
On the way, seeing that His companions were tired, the Lord sat down beneath a tree. Cows were grazing nearby; the Lord
felt the pastimes of Vraja, and when a cowherd boy played on his flute He fell unconscious, without breath, foam on His
lips. This was the swoon of love. But ten Pathan horsemen led by Bijli Khan thought otherwise: these four had drugged the
sannyasi, killed Him and robbed Him (CC Madhya 18.159–165). <!-- F54 -->

“Then the Pathans bound the four men and wanted to cut them down. The Bengalis all began to tremble. Krsnadasa was a
Rajput and utterly fearless. That brahmana too was fearless, and very bold of speech.” (CC Madhya 18.166–167)

Balabhadra, who had come from Bengal, and his companion trembled, while the two local men — the Rajput and the brahmana
of Mathura — were not afraid and found what to say. <!-- F55 -->

“The brahmana said, ‘Pathan, I appeal to you in the name of your padsah! Come along — I will go with you before the
sikdar. This sannyasi is my guru, and I am a brahmana of Mathura. I have a hundred men at the padsah's court. Because of
an illness this sannyasi sometimes faints. He will soon regain consciousness and come to His senses. Sit here a moment
and keep us all bound. Ask Him, and then kill me.’” (CC Madhya 18.168–171)[^13] <!-- F56 F57 -->

“Illness” — so the brahmana speaks to strangers, to save his companions and not to reveal to them what is hidden; the
Lord's unconsciousness at the sound of the flute is a manifestation of *mahabhava*, the highest love, not a disease. And
the leader judged:

“The Pathan said, ‘You two are men of the west, from Mathura, and these two Bengali cheats are trembling.’” (CC Madhya
18.172) <!-- F58 -->

Then Krishnadasa the Rajput threatened the Pathans, and at that moment the Lord came to Himself and, loudly calling
“Hari! Hari!”, began to dance. The Pathans in fear released the four, and the Lord never saw His companions bound. He said
to the Pathans:

“‘They are not cheats; they are My companions. I am a mendicant sannyasi and have no wealth at all. Because of the
falling sickness I sometimes lose consciousness, and these four kindly look after Me.’” (CC Madhya 18.183–184)

“The falling sickness” — in the Bengali *mrigi* — is the same veil the brahmana had used before: so before strangers the
Lord conceals His prema. Our brahmana is one of the “four” whom the Lord called His companions. He was present, too, when
the Lord refuted the arguments of the pir and converted Bijli Khan and his men (Madhya 18.185–212); of this we tell in
the account of Bijli Khan. <!-- F59 F60 -->

## As Far as Prayaga
“At Soro-ksetra the Lord bathed in the Ganga, and then He set out for Prayaga by the road along its bank. There the Lord
bade farewell to that brahmana and to Krsnadasa, but the two folded their hands and said: ‘We two will go with You as
far as Prayaga. Where shall we ever again find the company of Your feet? This is a land of mlecchas, and someone
somewhere may make trouble. The Bhattacarya is a scholar; he does not know how to talk to them.’” (CC Madhya 18.214–217)

According to one of the biographies, the Lord, in sending them back, said: “With great trouble you have come all the way
from Mathura to show us the road; I do not wish to trouble you further — go back from here.” <!-- F61 -->

“Hearing this, Mahaprabhu smiled slightly, and the two went on with the Lord.” (CC Madhya 18.218)

In their plea the first thing is the wish to be with the Lord a little longer — “Where shall we ever again find the
company of Your feet?” — and only then their concern: this is a land of mlecchas, trouble may arise on the way, and
Balabhadra would not manage with strangers.[^14] <!-- F62 -->

Reaching Prayaga, the Lord bathed for ten days at the confluence of the Ganga and the Yamuna in the days of Makara (CC
Madhya 18.222). Of the brahmana himself the sources are then silent: neither the “Chaitanya-charitamrita” nor the
biographies tell of his return to Mathura or of when he left this world. <!-- F63 -->

## His Memory
Years later, when Raghava Pandita was guiding Srinivasa and Narottama through Mathura, he first brought them to the place
where the Sanodiya vipra had lived. Narahari Chakravarti writes in the “Bhakti-ratnakara” that this was the one in whose
house Sri Krishna Chaitanya accepted bhiksha — the disciple of Madhavendra Puri Gosvami, who had seen the highest secret
of Gauranga. “Here Gaurachandra danced, rapt in love; countless people came, all went mad in the kirtana of the name…
all knew Him to be the son of the king of Vraja,” Raghava told them; and Srinivasa and Narottama, listening, wept and
rolled in the dust of the brahmana's courtyard (“Bhakti-ratnakara” 5.163–171). <!-- F64 -->

The same Narahari Chakravarti, in the “Namamrita-samudra,” where his prayers call upon the Lord's associates, turns to
him as well:

“O best of the twice-born, disciple of Madhavendra, dear to Gaura! Grant [me] unceasing residence in the
Mathura-mandala.” (“Namamrita-samudra” 215)
<!-- F65 -->

One of the biographies further writes that later Sanatana Gosvami went round the twelve forests of Vraja together with
Subuddhi and the Sanoriya brahmana, and learned from them that Rupa and Anupama had shortly before made the same round and
left by the road along the Ganga.[^15] <!-- F66 -->

## Significance
Of the Sanodiya brahmana we know almost nothing beyond a few weeks spent with the Lord; yet much was contained in those
weeks. His discipleship under Madhavendra and the Lord's meal in his house showed what *daiva-varnashrama* is: a
Vaishnava is judged by his devotion, not by his birth, and dharma is set by the conduct of the mahajanas. The Lord's bow
to him taught that all that is connected with the guru — the guru's godbrothers too — is to be honoured. And he himself,
recognizing the Lord's connection with Madhavendra by His love alone, showed that the *prema* of separation knows its own
source. He kept himself hidden, as befits a humble Vaishnava, and all that is known of him is his service: he danced with
the Lord, fed Him, guided Him to the holy places, watched over Him in the swoons of love, fearlessly stood up for his
companions before the Pathans, and would not part from the Lord while he could still walk at His side.
