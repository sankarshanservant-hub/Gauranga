# Vallabhacharya (Vallabha Bhatta)
*Валлабхачарья (Валлабха Бхатта) · @vallabha-bhatta*

## His Name and Place among the Associates
Vallabha Bhatta, known to later generations as Vallabhacharya, the founder of the path of *pushti* (the Pushti-marga),
was a learned brahmana of a family from the south whose life twice touched the pastimes of Sri Chaitanya. At Prayaga he
received the Lord in his own home, and at Puri the Lord broke his pride of learning and allowed him to take initiation
from Gadadhara Pandita. Krishnadasa Kaviraja calls him Vallabha Bhatta; Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati in his
“Anubhashya” also calls him Vallabhacharya and Vallabha Dikshita, and the “Amrita-pravaha-bhashya” remarks that he is
known as Vallabhacharya. The biographies name him Sri Vallabhacharya, Sri Vallabha and Vallabha Bhatta, and one of them
“the original Vallabhacharya,” to distinguish him from his grandson Gokulanatha, who bore the same name; in the books of
his followers he is “Acharyaji.”[^1] <!-- F1 -->

Who he is in the eternal pastimes, Kavi Karnapura reveals:

“Shuka, the son of Dvaipayana, became the Bhatta named Vallabha.” (“Gaura-ganoddesha-dipika” 110)

Shukadeva, the son of Vyasa, is the one who narrated the “Srimad-Bhagavatam” to King Parikshit. <!-- F2 Q10 -->

Srila Bhaktisiddhanta, in the “Anubhashya” (on CC, Adi 12.82), following the “Shakha-nirnaya” of Yadunandana Dasa,
counts Vallabha Bhatta among the twenty-three branches of Gadadhara Pandita and adds that the Vallabha sampradaya, the
path of *pushti*, is known by his name. One of the biographies also traces the line of teachers: Madhavendra Puri —
Pundarika Vidyanidhi — Gadadhara Pandita — Vallabha; and it calls the Pushti-marga of “the original Vallabha” the path
of a follower of Gadadhara. The “Amrita-pravaha-bhashya” (on CC, Madhya 19.61) speaks of him as a Vaishnava pandita,
later an acharya of the Vishnusvami sampradaya; the books of his followers, too, trace him to Vishnusvami and
Bilvamangala. <!-- F3 F4 -->

## Birth
One of the biographies tells of his birth as follows. In the fifteenth century there lived in Varanasi two people who had
come from Andhra-desha — Lakshmana Bhatta and his wife Ellamma Garu.[^2] Muslims attacked the city again and again, and
the couple left for the south with their three children. Ellamma was then with child. According to tradition, worn out by
the journey through the forest, she gave birth to an infant who seemed to be dead; they left him in the bushes beneath a
tree and, resigning themselves, went on. The next day, troubled by a dream, the parents returned to the place and saw to
their astonishment that the child was alive and that a fire was burning around him, guarding him. He was their fourth
child, and his appearance, the biography says, was lit by a divine radiance.[^3] <!-- F5 F6 F7 F8 F9 -->

Srila Bhaktisiddhanta, in the “Anubhashya” (on CC, Madhya 19.61), gives different information. Vallabha was the son of
Lakshmana Dikshita of the village of Kankadavad (Kakurpad) in the land of Trailanga, sixteen miles from Nidadavolu
station; he came of the Velanati Andhra brahmanas, one of five subdivisions: Velanati, Vegi-nati, Muriki-nati,
Telugu-nati and Kashala-nati. He was born in Shaka 1400 (1478/79). Some say that his father had taken sannyasa and left
home even before his birth, and afterwards returned and obtained a son. According to another opinion, which Srila
Bhaktisiddhanta cites in the same place, Vallabha appeared in Vikrama 1535 (Shaka 1400), on the ekadashi of the dark
fortnight of the month of Chaitra, in Champakaranya, as the son of Lakshmana Bhatta Dikshita of the Velanati family of
Trailanga, which bore the title “Khambhampativaru”; according to yet another, in the village of Champajhar near Rajim
station (B. N. R. line, Madhya Pradesh).[^4] The day of his appearance is not in the Vaishnava calendar of the Sri
Chaitanya Saraswat Math. <!-- F10 F11 -->

## Childhood and Education
After four years in the south the family returned to Varanasi. The boy received the sacred thread (*upanayana*) and was
sent to the gurukula of Sri Madhavananda.[^5] At eleven he lost his father. According to the “Anubhashya,” he lived in
Kashi until the age of eleven; having finished his studies, he was on his way home when, at Sheshadri, he heard of his
father's passing; then, leaving his brother and mother at home, he went to Vidyanagara. <!-- F12 F13 -->

The young man's mind was deeply absorbed in three schools of thought — advaita, dvaita and vishishta-advaita. To find the
truth he resolved to travel the holy places on foot, and he received his mother's permission. From the very beginning he
rejected the Mayavada of Shankaracharya, while fully upholding the teachings of Ramanuja and Madhva. <!-- F14 F15 -->

## Travels
The kingdom of Vijayanagara under Sri Krishnadeva Raya was then flourishing in every way. Vallabha's maternal uncle held
a high office of state there, and so the young man could live in the capital and go deeply into the truths of dvaita and
vishishta-advaita. He debated with scholars of every school, and already at fifteen, refuting the Mayavada of the
advaitins, he firmly maintained his understanding with arguments and quotations from authoritative scripture.
<!-- F16 F17 -->

He dearly loved the south of India, for the Vaishnava dharma had long been established there. He went to Rameshvaram, and
then through Malabar and the Kannada country westward to the river Kaundenya, where he lived in an ashrama and received
the teaching of bhakti preached by the sage Kaundinya. Having thus gone round the whole south, he returned to
Vijayanagara; King Krishnadeva Raya received him with honour, presented him with gold and riches and kept him at court as
his chief priest. The “Anubhashya,” too, says that in Vidyanagara, on the bank of the Tungabhadra, Vallabha gave joy to
Krishnadeva, the grandson of King Bukka. His followers, for their part, tell of a debate at Krishnadeva Raya's court and
of the Madhva acharya Vyasatirtha; their account is cited and examined by one of the biographies (see the article on
Madhavendra Puri). <!-- F18 F19 -->

Taking leave of the king, Vallabha set out for the north and northwest. At Pandharpur he received the mercy of Sri
Krishna; the temple drew him so strongly that he stayed there for some time, rejoicing in the darshana of Vithoba and
Rukmini. With his mother's permission he married; according to the “Anubhashya,” this was in Kashi when he was thirty,
and his wife was the daughter of a brahmana of his own caste, named Mahalakshmi. He travelled the holy places from the
Himalayas to Cape Comorin — the “Anubhashya” speaks of eighteen years, of three *digvijayas* of six years each — and came
to Vraja. There he saw Giriraja, and the holy hill seemed to him a divine temple. <!-- F20 F21 F22 -->

## Gopala on Govardhana
On Giriraja Gopala was being served — Sri Giridhari Gopala, the Deity whom Sri Madhavendra Puripada had found. One of the
biographies says that Gopala, after several thousand years in a cave, was brought out of it and installed in a temple
built for Him, where He was served according to all the rules; later He was taken to Nathdvara near Udaipur.[^6] This
history in detail — how Vallabha installed Gopala on Giriraja (according to different accounts, in 1499 or in 1519), how
he entrusted the temple to his disciple Krishnadasa, how the Bengali disciples of Madhavendra served Gopala and how they
were driven out after Vallabha's departure, how Gopala was taken to Mathura and then to Nathdvara — we have told in the
article on Madhavendra Puri. According to the “Anubhashya,” after installing the Deity on the heights of Govardhana,
Vallabha settled at Adaila near Prayaga. And the “Anuraga-valli” (8.89) says: “Sri Vallabhacharya became the adhikari of
Nathji,” that is, he received the right to serve Sri Nathji. <!-- F23 F24 -->

## A Tradition of a Meal
The biography that tells of his youth calls Vallabha a contemporary of Sri Krishna Chaitanya Mahaprabhu, who had
appeared in Bengal, and says that both were absorbed in the Vaishnava dharma and in love for Sri Krishna — Mahaprabhu in
the service of Radha-Krishna, Vallabha in the service of Bala-Gopala; that they met more than once and showed each other
respect. For a Gaudiya Vaishnava Mahaprabhu does not stand in a row with the great devotees of His time: He is Krishna
Himself, who has taken on the mood and radiance of Radha. So Vallabha himself spoke of Him at Puri: “When I see You, it
is as if I see Bhagavan Himself,” and later, “You are the Lord” (CC, Antya 7.8, 7.123). The Lord's respect for Vallabha
is His mercy to a Vaishnava; Vallabha's respect for the Lord is worship. <!-- F25 F26 -->

The same biography relates a tradition. Once Sri Chaitanya, after a long journey, came late to Vallabha's hut. Vallabha at
once began to serve Him and told his wife to offer the Guest the *naivedya* that had been kept for the evening puja; his
wife did so, though she was embarrassed to see her husband depart from the established order. And it became evident that
Sri Krishna Himself, standing behind Sri Krishna Chaitanya as a silent witness, was accepting this sanctified food; then
Vallabha drew his wife's attention to the greatness of Sri Krishna Chaitanya, who is worthy of worship.[^7]

## A Guest at Adaila
In Shaka 1434 or 1435 (1512–1513) Sri Chaitanya, returning from Vrindavana, came to Prayaga. Vallabha was then living in
the village of Adaila (Arail) beyond the Yamuna, opposite Prayaga, about a mile from the confluence. Krishnadasa Kaviraja
relates:

“At that time Vallabha Bhatta was living in the village of Adaila. Hearing that Mahaprabhu had come, he came to Him. He fell before the Lord in obeisance, and the Lord embraced him. Then the two of them spoke about Krishna for some time. As they spoke of Krishna, great love surged up in the Lord, but out of reserve before Bhatta He held it back. Yet within, His love was seething and could not be restrained, and Vallabha Bhatta was amazed at heart to see it.” (CC, Madhya 19.61–64) <!-- F29 -->

One of the biographies explains that the Lord concealed His prema because He still regarded Vallabha as an outsider
(*bahiranga*); another notes how Vallabha marvelled at the Lord's manifestations of love. With the Lord at that time
were Sri Rupa and his brother Anupama, and the Lord introduced them to Vallabha:

“Then Bhatta invited Mahaprabhu for a meal, and Mahaprabhu introduced the two brothers to him. From a distance the two brothers fell to the ground and, in great humility, offered obeisances to Bhatta. When Bhatta went to embrace them, they both drew far back: ‘I am untouchable and sinful. Do not touch me!’ Bhatta was astonished, and the Lord was pleased at heart. Then the Lord told Bhatta about Rupa: ‘Do not touch him; he is of very low birth! And you are a Vedic scholar, a performer of sacrifices, learned and of noble family!’ Hearing the name of Krishna constantly from Rupa's mouth, and grasping something of the Lord's hint, Bhatta said: ‘The name of Krishna is dancing in the mouths of both. These two are not “lowly” — they are “the best of all.” “Oh, how wonderful! Even a dog-eater is worthy of the highest honour if Your name is on the tip of his tongue. Those who utter Your name have already performed austerities and offered sacrifices; they have bathed in all the holy waters, they are truly noble, and they have studied the Vedas.”’” (CC, Madhya 19.65–72; “Srimad-Bhagavatam” 3.33.7)

This was a test, and, as one of the biographies remarks, Vallabha passed it: he did not see a Vaishnava as an ordinary
mortal, he had faith in the holy name, and he was not merely a ritualist smarta brahmana; the Vaishnava nature had to a
great extent awakened in him, and that is why the Lord accepted his invitation. Mahaprabhu was very pleased with the
Bhatta's words, praised him and, overcome with prema, recited verses from the “Hari-bhakti-sudhodaya”: low birth is burnt
away in the fire of bhakti, while without devotion to the Lord high birth, learning and austerity are only ornaments on a
dead body (CC, Madhya 19.73–75). <!-- F30 Q2 I1 -->

Vallabha seated the Lord and His companions in a boat and took them to his home. In the middle of the Yamuna, seeing its
dark waters, the Lord leapt into the river with a loud cry; they pulled Him out, and He began to dance in the boat, so
that it rocked and began to fill with water. Only when He saw where He was and among whom did Mahaprabhu restrain
Himself, and the boat came safely to the ghat of Adaila (CC, Madhya 19.77–83). Anxious for the Lord, Vallabha stayed by
Him while He took His midday bath, and then brought Him to his house:

“Joyfully Bhatta offered Him a fine seat and himself washed the Lord's feet. With his whole family he placed that water on his head, and he dressed the Lord in a new kaupina and outer cloth. He worshipped the Lord grandly with sandal paste, flowers, incense and lamps, and with due respect he had Balabhadra Bhattacharya do the cooking.” (CC, Madhya 19.85–87)

Vallabha fed the Lord with love, and fed Rupa and Anupama too; the remnants of the Lord's meal Balabhadra gave to Sri
Rupa and to Krishnadasa.[^8] When the Lord lay down to rest, the Bhatta himself massaged His feet; the Lord sent him to
eat, and after his meal he came back to His feet (CC, Madhya 19.88–91). One of the biographies especially remembers that
Vallabha and his whole family drank the water that had washed the Lord's feet. <!-- F30 F31 F35 -->

In Vallabha's house the Lord was also found by Raghupati Upadhyaya, a great Vaishnava and scholar from Tirhut. The Lord
asked him to describe Krishna's pastimes, and he recited verse after verse, until he answered the Lord that the highest
form is Shyama, the highest abode Mathura, the highest age *kaishora*, and the highest rasa *shringara*. Then:

“In His rapture the Lord embraced him, and Upadhyaya, intoxicated with love, began to dance. Seeing this, Vallabha Bhatta was amazed. He brought his two sons and had them fall at the Lord's feet.” (CC, Madhya 19.107–108)

The biographies add that together with Vallabha all his household marvelled at Raghupati's good fortune.[^9]
<!-- F32 F33 Q3 -->

Word of the Guest spread through the village:

“All the people of the village came to see the Lord, and on seeing Him they all became devotees of Krishna. All the brahmanas wanted to invite the Lord, but Vallabha Bhatta held them all back: ‘In the madness of love Gosani throws Himself into the middle of the Yamuna. I will take Him to Prayaga; I will not let Him stay here. Whoever wishes may go to Prayaga and invite Him there.’ Saying this, he took the Lord and set out. Seating Mahaprabhu in a boat, Bhatta brought Gosani to Prayaga by way of the Ganga.” (CC, Madhya 19.109–113) <!-- F34 Q1 -->

Govinda-deva in the “Gaura-krishnodaya” relates that while the Lord was waiting at Prayaga for Rupa to come, Vallabha
Bhatta and Raghupati Upadhyaya invited Him every day to their homes and served Him, and when Rupa and Vallabha (Anupama)
arrived, the Lord introduced the brothers to the Bhatta and the other devotees (14.63, 14.70).[^10] And in his brief
summary of the Lord's pastimes Kaviraja says: “Then Vallabha Bhatta met the Lord, and the Lord explained to him the
meaning of Krishna's name” (CC, Madhya 1.263). <!-- F36 F47 -->

The seat (*baithak*) of Vallabhacharya is shown today about a *krosha* from present-day Arail, at a place called Deorah.
The name, it is said, is a corruption of *deva-rishi*: according to the books of Vallabha's followers, gods and rishis
lived there for the sake of his company. Local tradition holds that Sri Chaitanya came there too. The baithak belongs to
Muralidhara Lalji, the owner of the Gopalji temple in Kashi. Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati and his companions, after
visiting Kashi and Prayaga, also visited Arail, where Mahaprabhu had met Vallabhacharya. <!-- F37 -->

## At Puri: Mercy that Looks like Disregard
The second meeting the “Charitamrita” tells of took place at Puri, in one of the following years, when the Gaudiya
devotees had again come for the Ratha-yatra:

“While the Lord was thus enjoying His pastimes with the devotees, Vallabha Bhatta came to meet Him. Coming before the Lord, the Bhatta bowed at His feet, and the Lord embraced him, regarding him as a *bhagavata*, a devotee. Showing him honour, the Lord seated him near Himself, and the Bhatta humbly began to speak. ‘For many days I have longed to see You. Jagannatha has fulfilled my desire: I have seen You. Whoever obtains Your sight is fortunate. When I see You, it is as if I see Bhagavan Himself.’” (CC, Antya 7.4–8)

Citing a verse of the “Bhagavatam” (1.19.33) — that even remembering saints purifies a home — Vallabha went on:

“The dharma of the age of Kali is the sankirtana of Krishna's name. Without Krishna's power no one can propagate it. You have propagated it — that is the proof. You bear Krishna's power; of this there is no doubt. You have made Krishna's name manifest in the world. Whoever sees You floats in love for Krishna. Without Krishna's power prema is not manifested. Krishna alone is the giver of prema — so the scriptures testify.” (CC, Antya 7.11–14)

And he supported this with a verse of the “Laghu-bhagavatamrita” (1.5.37): who but Krishna gives pure love? These words
of Vallabha's — that only one endowed with Krishna's power can spread the name — one of the biographies also cites in
its account of Gadadhara Dasa. <!-- F48 F49 F41 Q6 -->

The Lord answered him:

“Mahaprabhu said, ‘Listen, wise Bhatta. I am a Mayavadi sannyasi; I do not know devotion to Krishna.’” (CC, Antya 7.16)

There is no confession in these words: thus the Lord hides Himself behind the humility of a sannyasi. And, turning the
praise away from Himself, He began to glorify His associates, as though He owed them all He knew of bhakti. Advaita
Acharya, He said, is Ishvara Himself, and in His company His mind became pure; Nityananda Avadhuta, too, is Ishvara
Himself, an ocean of love for Krishna. Of Sarvabhauma the Lord said:

“Sarvabhauma Bhattacharya knows the six systems of philosophy (*darshanas*). In these six systems he is the guru of the world, and he is the best of bhagavatas. He showed Me the farthest limit of bhakti-yoga; by his grace I came to know that the yoga of devotion to Krishna is the essence.” (CC, Antya 7.21–22)

Ramananda Raya, the Lord continued, had revealed to Him that Krishna is Bhagavan Himself and that love on the path of
spontaneous attraction is the highest of all (7.23–37); Svarupa Damodara is rasa embodied, and in his company the Lord
came to know the madhura-rasa of Vraja (7.38–45); Haridasa Thakura revealed to Him the glory of the holy name
(7.46–47). And at the end the Lord named the Vaishnavas of Bengal:

“Acharyaratna, Acharyanidhi, Gadadhara Pandita, Jagadananda, Damodara, Shankara, Vakreshvara, Kashishvara, Mukunda, Vasudeva, Murari and all the other devotees who descended in Gauda have preached the name of Krishna and prema throughout the world, and whatever devotion to Krishna I have, I have gained in their company.” (CC, Antya 7.48–50)

Why the Lord said all this, Kaviraja explains:

“Mahaprabhu knew how firmly pride was rooted in the Bhatta's heart, and so He spoke all this by way of a hint. ‘I am a Vaishnava,’ the Bhatta thought. ‘I know all the conclusions of bhakti, and no one explains the meaning of the Bhagavata better than I.’ Such was the pride that had long lived in his mind, but the Lord's words humbled it.” (CC, Antya 7.51–53)

One of the biographies says just this: knowing that Vallabha was proud of his learning, the Lord hid His own glory from
him and chose to speak of the virtues of His associates. <!-- F50 F38 F39 F40 F51 Q4 Q5 I3 -->

Vallabha wished to see these Vaishnavas, and the Lord told him that all of them were now in Puri, having come for the
Ratha-yatra. Then Vallabha earnestly invited the Lord, and the next day the Lord introduced him to the Vaishnavas:

“Seeing the radiance of the Vaishnavas, the Bhatta was amazed: before them all he was like a firefly.” (CC, Antya 7.60) <!-- F52 -->

He brought a great quantity of Jagannatha's mahaprasada and fed the Lord and His associates. The sannyasis headed by
Paramananda Puri sat on one side of the Lord, Advaita and Nityananda on either side of Him, and the Bengali Vaishnavas,
too many to count, in rows in the courtyard; in the Lord's presence Vallabha bowed at the feet of all the Vaishnavas.
Svarupa Damodara, Jagadananda, Kashishvara, Shankara, Raghava and Damodara Pandita served the prasada, and after the meal
Vallabha offered everyone garlands, sandal paste and betel (CC, Antya 7.61–69). On the day of the Ratha-yatra he saw
how the Lord divided the Vaishnavas into seven kirtana groups and moved from one to another; seeing this, Vallabha was
amazed to the depths of his heart (7.70–75). <!-- F53 F54 -->

But soon the scholar's pride showed itself again. At another festival Vallabha came to the Lord:

“‘I have written something of a commentary on the Bhagavata. Would You, Mahaprabhu, hear it Yourself?’ The Lord said, ‘I cannot understand the meaning of the Bhagavata. I am not qualified even to hear its meaning. I simply sit and chant Krishna's name, and even so, night and day, I cannot complete My fixed number of names.’ The Bhatta said, ‘I have explained the meaning of Krishna's name at length. Please hear it.’ The Lord said, ‘I do not accept many meanings of Krishna's name. I know only this: Shyamasundara, the dark and beautiful One, and Yashoda-nandana, the son of Yashoda.’” (CC, Antya 7.77–81)

The Lord confirmed this with a verse of the “Nama-kaumudi”: the principal meaning of the name “Krishna” is He whose form
is dark like a *tamala* tree and who is the son of Yashoda (7.82–83).

“The Bhatta's explanations of the name and the rest were all nearly worthless (*phalgu*). Knowing this, the all-knowing Lord disregarded them. Dejected, the Bhatta went home, and his devotion to the Lord partly ebbed away.” (CC, Antya 7.84–85) <!-- F55 -->

Since the Lord showed no interest in his learning, no one in Puri wanted to hear his explanations. Ashamed and hurt,
Vallabha began to visit Gadadhara Pandita often, showing him friendship, and begged him to hear his explanation of the
name. Gadadhara did not know what to do; without waiting for his consent, Vallabha began to read. Gadadhara could not stop
a brahmana pandita of noble standing, and only prayed to Krishna in his mind: the Lord, who dwells in the heart, knows
his thoughts, but the Lord's associates are severe. And indeed, though Gadadhara was in no way at fault, many of the
Lord's associates showed him a loving displeasure (CC, Antya 7.86–95). One of the biographies tells this a little
differently: seeing Vallabha's pride, the Lord withdrew from him and found fault with all he said; then Vallabha began to
visit Gadadhara, and the Lord grew cool towards Gadadhara as well, until he fell at His feet and the Lord embraced
him.[^11] <!-- F56 F42 -->

Every day Vallabha came to the Lord and picked arguments with Advaita Acharya and the other Vaishnavas, but all his
arguments were at once refuted, and among the Vaishnavas he was “like a heron among royal swans” (CC, Antya 7.96–98).
One day he put to Advaita a question that seemed to him unanswerable:

“One day the Bhatta asked the Acharya: ‘The jiva is *prakriti*, the female, and accepts Krishna as her husband (*pati*). A wife devoted to her husband does not utter her husband's name. Yet you utter Krishna's name — what sort of dharma is that?’ The Acharya said, ‘Before you stands dharma embodied. Ask Him; He will resolve the question.’ The Lord said, ‘You do not know what is dharma and what is adharma. A faithful wife's dharma is to follow her husband's order. The husband's order is to utter his name ceaselessly, and a faithful wife cannot transgress her husband's order. Therefore she utters the name and gains the fruit of the name: by that fruit love (prema) for Krishna's feet awakens.’” (CC, Antya 7.99–104) <!-- F57 -->

Vallabha went home dejected and began to think how he might prevail, if only once. The next day he declared in the
assembly:

“‘In the *Bhagavata* I have refuted Shridhara Svami's explanations. I cannot accept the words of his commentary. He explains whatever passage he takes up just as it stands there; there is no consistency in it. Therefore I do not accept Svami.’ The Lord smiled and said, ‘Anyone who does not accept the *svami* — the husband — I count among prostitutes.’” (CC, Antya 7.109–111) <!-- F58 Q9 -->

One of the biographies presents this answer as Gaurasundara's verdict on a view contrary to the Svami: it is “the opinion
of a harlot.” Govinda-deva in the “Gaura-krishnodaya” relates that the Lord, raising objections word by word, said:
“Your commentary, brahmana, transgresses the Svami's words — it is unfaithful, like an unchaste woman,” and when the
brahmana lost his lustre, graciously consoled him: “Do not grieve, brahmana — how can one in Kali distinguish Shridhara
from Shuka?” (17.18–19).[^12] <!-- F45 -->

Kaviraja at once explains what lay behind this severity:

“Gaura descended for the good of the world, and He knew the pride hidden in the Bhatta's heart. By various kinds of disregard Bhagavan purified the Bhatta, just as Krishna broke the pride of Indra.” (CC, Antya 7.113–114)

And that night Vallabha himself understood it:

“‘Earlier, in Prayaga, the Lord showed me great mercy. With His associates He accepted my invitation. Why is the Lord's mind now so changed towards me? He wants my mind to be free of the pride that says “I shall win”: it is the Lord's nature to do good to all. To make a name for myself, I give way to pride; to break that pride, He dishonours me. He is doing me good, yet I take it as sorrow — just as Indra, that great fool, behaved towards Krishna.’” (CC, Antya 7.116–120) <!-- F59 -->

In the morning he came to the Lord and surrendered at His feet:

“‘I am an ignorant jiva, and I acted as the ignorant act. Fool that I am, I displayed my learning before You. You are the Lord, and You showed mercy befitting Yourself: by dishonouring me, You broke all my pride. […] I have committed offences; forgive me. I have taken shelter of You. Be merciful and place Your feet on my head.’” (CC, Antya 7.122–126) <!-- F60 -->

The Lord replied:

“‘You are a pandita and a great devotee (*maha-bhagavata*). Where these two qualities are found, there can be no mountain of pride. Yet you blaspheme Shridhara Svami and write your own commentary! You do not accept Shridhara Svami — such is the pride you hold! By the grace of Shridhara Svami I understand the *Bhagavata*. I accept Shridhara Svami, the guru of the world, as My guru. […] Explain the *Bhagavata* in the footsteps of Shridhara. Give up your pride and worship Krishna, Bhagavan. Give up offences and perform the sankirtana of Krishna. Then you will very soon attain the feet of Krishna.’” (CC, Antya 7.127–133) <!-- F61 -->

One of the biographies stresses this: in Nilachala Vallabha bowed his head, accepted Gaurasundara's merciful correction
and was purified by it; and those later, “over-zealous” would-be followers who belittle the Lord and His associates for
the sake of Vallabha's glory only wound his heart. <!-- I2 -->

## Initiation from Gadadhara Pandita
Vallabha invited the Lord to his home once more, and the Lord, wishing to give him joy, agreed: for even when He punishes,
He only purifies the heart (CC, Antya 7.134–137). Meanwhile, in Gadadhara's company, a change had taken place in
Vallabha's heart:

“Vallabha Bhatta worshipped in the mood of parental love (*vatsalya*); he served with the Bala-gopala mantra. In the company of the Pandita his heart changed, and he gave his mind to the worship of Kishora-gopala. He wanted to receive the mantra and so on from the Pandita, but the Pandita said, ‘This is not for me to do. I am dependent; my Lord is Gaurachandra. Without His order I am not free to act on my own. Just because you come to me, the Lord reproaches me.’” (CC, Antya 7.144–148) <!-- F43 Q7 -->

On the day of Vallabha's invitation the Lord sent Svarupa Damodara, Jagadananda and Govinda to fetch Gadadhara. On the
way Svarupa asked the Pandita why he had not spoken out to the Lord, but had borne it all as if afraid; Gadadhara replied
that he dared not contradict the All-knowing and was only waiting for His mercy. When he came, he fell weeping at the
Lord's feet, and the Lord, smiling, embraced him and said before everyone: “By your firm, simple-hearted love you have
bought Me” (CC, Antya 7.150–160). One of the biographies says plainly that this test of Gadadhara was occasioned by his
fondness for Vallabha. But, as Srila Bhaktisiddhanta explains in the “Anubhashya,” here too the Lord's displeasure was
only outward: Gadadhara is His own internal potency, and Mahaprabhu never was and never can be displeased with him.
<!-- F62 Q8 -->

Kaviraja sums it up:

“Washing away the mire of pride, He purified the Bhatta, and by this He taught everyone else as well. Within is grace; outwardly it looks like disregard. Whoever takes only the outer meaning is ruined.” (CC, Antya 7.163–164)

The next day Gadadhara himself invited the Lord with all the devotees, and there Vallabha's long-held wish was fulfilled:

“There Vallabha Bhatta received the Lord's permission, and from the Pandita he obtained all he had long prayed for.” (CC, Antya 7.167)

— that is, the mantra and initiation he had asked of the Pandita. Kaviraja ends the chapter with a promise: “By hearing of
it, one gains the treasure of love for Gaura” (CC, Antya 7.168); and in his review of the book he recalls that the Lord,
teaching Vallabha many lessons, broke his pride completely (Antya 20.114). <!-- F63 -->

Thus Vallabha became a disciple of Gadadhara, and through him, as one of the biographies reminds us, an heir of the line
of Pundarika Vidyanidhi and Madhavendra Puri. The same biography says that, having received the mercy of Gaurasundara and
Gadadhara Pandita, Vallabhacharya fully restored the temple of Gopala on Govardhana and on the third lunar day of the
bright fortnight of Vaishakha, Shaka 1441 (1519), installed Gopala in it;[^13] and that one way or another — then or
later — Vallabha and his sons received the mercy of Sri Krishna Chaitanya and His associates. <!-- F44 F46 -->

## The Meeting with Jiva Gosvami
In Vrindavana Vallabha met the young Jiva Gosvami; this meeting is told in different ways. According to the
“Bhakti-ratnakara” (fifth taranga), Vallabha came to Sri Rupa while he was writing the “Bhakti-rasamrita-sindhu,” read
the opening verses and proposed some corrections; Jiva followed him to the Yamuna and refuted all his objections, and
Vallabha, returning to Rupa, praised the young man's learning, whereupon Rupa sent Jiva away. According to another
account, which one of the biographies gives after Srila Bhaktivinoda Thakura with the reservation “it is said,”
Vallabha, “an acharya of the Vishnusvami sampradaya,” showed Jiva his book “Tattva-dipa”; Jiva pointed out its faults,
and Vallabha corrected much. There is also an account by Vallabha's followers (the “Nij-varta”).[^14] <!-- F64 -->

## Works and Teaching
After living some years in Varanasi, Vallabha, according to the biography, spent twenty years in the village of Arail
near Allahabad, at the confluence of the Ganga and the Yamuna. While still in Varanasi, the Vedic brahmanas, mostly
advaitins, had opposed his settling there for good as a householder; nor did they approve of the way he celebrated the
completion of his pilgrimage and what he had experienced on it — a festival with a feast for three thousand guests. At
Arail he saw that three kinds of tulasi had sprung up there of themselves, and understood that this place was an eternal
abode of Sri Krishna; there he wrote his commentaries on the “Brahma-sutra” and the “Bhagavatam.” Srila Bhaktisiddhanta
in the “Anubhashya” names his “Shodasha-grantha,” the “Anubhashya” on the “Brahma-sutra,” and the commentary
“Subodhini” on the “Bhagavatam,” and says that he has many other books besides. <!-- F65 F66 F67 -->

One of the biographies sets out his teaching as follows. Karma is inferior to jnana in its power to bear fruit, and both
fall far short of bhakti. The worship of Sri Krishna's Deity is obligatory; the Deity is honoured as the Lord Himself and
not as a symbol; He is served in homes, and everyone gathers there for darshana. To all his disciples Vallabha gave as the
highest mantra “*sri krishnah sharanam mama*” — “Sri Krishna is my shelter” — and he especially exalted the
“Bhagavata-purana.” The foundation of his teaching is reliance on God's mercy: that is why his path is called the path
of *pushti*, of grace. Vallabha's community and his teaching of *shuddhadvaita* spread widely in Rajasthan, Gujarat,
Madhya Pradesh and part of Uttar Pradesh. <!-- F68 F69 F70 F71 F73 F74 -->

The same biography writes that in Vallabha's community *vatsalya-prema* predominates, as the *madhura-bhava* of Radha and
Krishna does in the community of Sri Chaitanya, and that Sri Chaitanya laid more stress on kirtana and gave bhakti the
highest place. According to the “Charitamrita,” however, Vallabha himself had earlier served Bala-Gopala in the mood of
vatsalya, but in the company of Gadadhara Pandita his heart turned to Kishora-Gopala, and with the Lord's permission he
received the mantra from the Pandita (Antya 7.144–145, 167). <!-- F72 -->

Manohara Dasa, searching in the “Anuraga-valli” for the succession of Mahaprabhu, mentions his books:

“In the commentary that Sri Vallabha Acharya established he wrote of the four sampradayas. There too I found this shloka
as proof; I came to know firmly the statement of the *Padma-purana*.” (“Anuraga-valli” 8.31–32)

The verse in question is the “Padma-purana” verse on the four sampradayas — Shri, Brahma, Rudra and Sanaka (8.33–34). Of
the Rudra sampradaya Manohara Dasa writes that it comes from Vishnusvami; Sri Vallabhacharya became the adhikari of
Nathji, and the sampradaya came to be called by the name “Vallabhi,” while his son and disciple is Sri Vitthalanatha
(8.87–91). <!-- F75 -->

## His Sons and His Departure
Vallabha had two sons, Gopinatha and Vitthalanatha. According to one of the biographies, Gopinatha was born in Shaka 1432
(1510) and Vitthala in 1437 (1515); the “Anubhashya” (on CC, Madhya 18.47) also calls Vitthaleshvara the son of Vallabha
Bhatta. Of Vitthalanatha and his service to Gopala — see the article on Madhavendra Puri. <!-- F76 -->

The biography has preserved Vallabha's last instruction. His sons and disciples came to him and asked what their duty
would be when he was gone. Then, on the granite slab on which he was sitting, he wrote: if you stray from the
remembrance of Krishna, time will imperceptibly swallow you and destroy body, mind and life itself. <!-- F77 -->

At the end of his life, according to his followers' books (the “Ghar-varta” and the “Vallabha-digvijaya”) cited by one
of the biographies, Vallabha passed through all the stages of sannyasa — kutichaka, bahudaka, hamsa and paramahamsa; he
took sannyasa thirty-nine days before his departure and left this world in Vikrama 1587 (Shaka 1452, 1530). Srila
Bhaktisiddhanta writes in the “Anubhashya” that at the end of his life he took tridandi-sannyasa and in Shaka 1452 left
this world in Varanasi. The sources do not name the day of his departure, nor is it in the Vaishnava calendar of the Sri
Chaitanya Saraswat Math. Ten years after his father's departure the elder son, Gopinatha, left this world at Puri.
<!-- F78 -->

Some, according to one of the biographies, consider Keshava Puri, one of Madhavendra's followers, to be the elder brother
of Sri Vallabhacharya; but, as the biography itself cautions, this report needs verification, and it does not specify
which Vallabhacharya is meant. <!-- F79 -->

## In the Traditions of His Followers
Vallabha's followers have preserved many stories about him and Mahaprabhu: that at Manasi-Ganga the Lord, by Vallabha's
mercy, saw her “milk form”; that Gopala Bhatta, on the Lord's advice, received Radha-ramana from Vallabha; that
Madhavendra was merely a manager of Shrinathji's service; stories of Vyasatirtha and Bilvamangala, of “three
Vishnusvamis,” of Chaitanya as “a branch of the Vishnusvami sampradaya.” One of the biographies cites and examines these
stories in detail and considers them inventions of later authors, contrary both to history and to the words of Vallabha
and Vitthalanatha themselves, who always honoured Madhavendra (see the article on Madhavendra Puri). <!-- F80 -->

The same biography also speaks of the later Pushti-marga — of its authors, of the sahajiyas and sakhi-bhekis; but it
distinguishes “the original Vallabha,” a follower of Gadadhara, from the teaching as altered by his grandson Gokulanatha.
Its reproaches concern the later tradition, not Vallabha himself, whom in the “Charitamrita” the Lord Himself calls a
great devotee (Antya 7.127). <!-- F81 -->

## Significance
The life of Vallabha Bhatta bears witness to Mahaprabhu's mercy towards a scholar. At Prayaga, a pandita of noble family,
he passed the Lord's test: in the humble Rupa and Anupama, on whose lips the name of Krishna dances, he saw “the best of
all,” and so the Lord accepted his service. At Puri the Lord would not hear his explanations and outwardly disregarded
him until his pride collapsed; and Vallabha himself understood that this disregard was mercy, as the breaking of Indra's
pride was Krishna's mercy. Bowing his head, he accepted the Lord's correction, acknowledged Shridhara Svami as the guru
of the world and Gadadhara Pandita as his own guru. Srila Bhaktisiddhanta counts him a branch of Gadadhara; Kavi
Karnapura reveals in him Shuka, the son of Vyasa. One of the biographies sees the heart of his life in love for Krishna
and reliance on God's mercy; the “Charitamrita” shows how that mercy touched him, and promises: “By hearing of it, one
gains the treasure of love for Gaura” (Antya 7.168). <!-- F2 F3 F63 I1 I2 I3 I4 -->
