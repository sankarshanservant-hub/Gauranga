# Madhavi-devi
*Мадхави-деви · @madhavi-devi*

## The Sister of Shikhi Mahiti
Among Sri Chaitanya's Utkala companions in Puri there was a family the Lord especially favoured: Shikhi Mahiti, the
scribe of Jagannatha's temple, his younger brother Murari Mahiti, and their sister Madhavi-devi. Krishnadasa Kaviraja
calls her Madhavi-devi, simply Madhavi, and “the sister of Shikhi Mahiti”; the biographies call her Sri Madhavi-devi and
“the maha-bhagavata Madhavi-devi.”[^1] She was the youngest of the three. So writes Kavi Karnapura in the
“Chaitanya-charita-mahakavya”: Shikhi has a younger brother, Murari, “and younger still, a sister of pure understanding”
(13.90); so too say Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati in the “Anubhashya” and one of the biographies. The
“Charitamrita” is silent on who was older. Madhavi and her brothers belonged to the Lord's closest circle. The
explanation to the “Chaitanya-bhagavata” (Adi 2.28–30) names Sri Madhavi-devi together with her brothers among the
devotees born in Utkala. <!-- F1 F4 F5 F28 -->

The elder brother served Jagannatha as the temple scribe (*deula-karana*); the “Charitamrita” names him so: “This is
Shikhi Mahiti, the scribe” (CC, Madhya 10.42). All three lived in Purushottama-kshetra — “thus the three of them lived
there,” says Karnapura (13.90). One of the biographies adds that Shikhi Mahiti was the son of a brother of Bhavananda
Raya, Ramananda's father; Madhavi-devi would then have been Bhavananda's niece.[^2] <!-- F6 F7 F9 -->

The sources give neither the year, the day nor the place of her appearance, and it is not known when she left this
world. Her days are not in the Vaishnava calendar of the Sri Chaitanya Saraswat Math either.

## Kalakeli, Radha's Maidservant
Who she is in Krishna's eternal pastimes Kavi Karnapura tells us:

“Ragalekha and Kalakeli, Radha's maidservants, are to be known as Shikhi Mahiti and his sister Madhavi respectively.”
(“Gaura-ganoddesha-dipika” 189)

Kalakeli is one of the maidservants (*dasi*, *kinkari*) of Srimati Radhika. This is not a former birth but her eternal
nature: Radha's eternal maidservant descended into Gaura's pastimes together with the Lord, to serve Him here as well.
Kaviraja says the same without giving her name in Krishna's pastimes:

“Madhavi Devi, the sister of Shikhi Mahiti, is counted among the maidservants of Sri Radha.” (CC, Adi 10.137)

In the “Anubhashya” to this verse she is named the seventeenth of the Utkala branches of Sri Chaitanya's tree.

## Before Her Elder Brother
How Shikhi Mahiti came to Gaura is told by Karnapura in the thirteenth canto of the “Chaitanya-charita-mahakavya”
(13.89–109), and in that story the younger brother and sister come first. Srila Bhaktisiddhanta cites it in the
“Anubhashya” to Adi 10.136, and the biographies retell it briefly after him.[^3] Murari and Madhavi became devoted to
Gaurachandra before their elder brother did:

“But these two younger ones, the dear brother and sister, became devoted to Gaurachandra: a steadfast, innate, auspicious
inclination never admits forgetfulness.” (“Chaitanya-charita-mahakavya” 13.91)

They knew with certainty that the Moon of Vrindavana, Krishna, had now risen on earth as Gaurachandra, the moon born from
the ocean of Shachi's womb; such was the auspicious conviction of these two, who constantly nourished an abundance of love
(13.92–93). Srila Bhaktisiddhanta understands these words to mean that the Lord Himself ever bestowed abundant love upon
the brother and sister. <!-- F10 F11 Q5 -->

Shikhi, however, was a loving servant of Jagannatha, and try as the younger ones might to turn him to Gaura's service,
he did not incline to it (13.94). But one night, after turning over in his mind what he had heard from them,[^4] he was
overwhelmed towards dawn by a vision in a dream. And the younger ones, who delighted in Gaura's lotus feet, came at that
very moment to wake him and saw how shaken he was. He opened his tear-filled eyes, saw them beside him, and in his joy
embraced them both so tightly that they were astonished. Then he told them what he had seen: Gaurasundara, gazing on Jagannatha, entered into Him and came out again;
and when Shikhi approached, the Lord called him by name and embraced him (13.95–103). <!-- F12 F13 -->

Hearing this, the brother and sister rejoiced and told him to go and see the Lord:

“Hearing this, the brother and sister rejoiced and told him to go there and see the Master who had gone to behold the
Lord of the Blue Mountain.” (“Chaitanya-charita-mahakavya” 13.104)

All three hurried to the temple. In the *jagamohana*, the hall before the sanctum, the brother and sister were the first
to see the son of Shachi, and they wept for joy; Shikhi saw Him exactly as he had seen Him in the dream. And the
Lord, saying, “You are Murari's elder brother,” embraced him with both arms (13.105–108).[^5] From that day Shikhi Mahiti
served Gaura as his own worshipful Deity (13.109). Thus the younger sister, together with her brother, brought to Mahaprabhu's
feet the one whom the Lord would later name among His three most intimate companions. <!-- F14 -->

## An Elderly Ascetic
Kaviraja speaks of her in the “Antya-lila”:

“Mahiti's sister was named Madhavi-devi. She was elderly, given to austerity, and a supreme Vaishnavi.” (CC, Antya 2.104)

In the Bengali verse she is *vriddha tapasvini*, “an elderly ascetic,” and *parama vaishnavi*, “a supreme Vaishnavi.”
Srila Bhaktisiddhanta heads this verse in the “Anubhashya”: “Who the maha-bhagavata Madhavi-devi is.” Her austerity was
not dry renunciation, nor severity for its own sake: it was how her pure, wholehearted devotion showed itself. One of the
biographies calls her “an elderly renunciant,” another “a sister of high qualities.” <!-- F15 Q4 -->

## The “Half” of Three and a Half
Next Kaviraja says of her what every Gaudiya Vaishnava remembers her name for:

“The Lord counted her among Radhika's associates. In all the world there are only three and a half such worthy persons:
Svarupa Gosani, Ramananda Raya and Shikhi Mahiti are the three, and his sister is the half.” (CC, Antya 2.105–106)

The closing words in Bengali are *tanra bhagini — arddha-jana*, “his sister — the half”; the Lord counted her among
the circle (*gana*) of Radhika. Srila Bhaktisiddhanta cites these verses in the “Anubhashya” to Adi 10.137 as well,
beside her name among the branches of the tree. One of the biographies retells them thus: among all the devotees of
Mahaprabhu, just three and a half belong to Srimati's group. <!-- F16 F17 Q3 -->

The “half” is no diminishment. The verse gives no reason, and we shall not supply one of our own; one thing is clear: it
does not at all mean that her devotion was somehow half as great. The verse itself says something else: the Lord counted her among Radhika's circle and set her beside Svarupa Damodara and Ramananda
Raya — among the very few worthy ones in all the world. That is why the biographies cite this verse, to show how intimate
her place is: of the Lord's entire circle only four did He name thus — worthy ones of Srimati's group — and she is one of
the four. <!-- I2 -->

## Rice for the Lord's Meal
Madhavi-devi's name is linked as well with one of Mahaprabhu's sternest pastimes in Puri — the story of the Younger
Haridasa. The “Charitamrita” relates:

“One day the Acharya invited the Lord. At home he cooked rice and made various vegetable dishes. The Lord had a singer
named Chota Haridasa. The Acharya himself called him, brought him over and said, ‘Go in my name to Shikhi Mahiti's
sister, ask her for one *mana* of white rice and bring it.’” (CC, Antya 2.101–103)

The *mana* was a measure for grain used in Utkala; another reading, cited in the “Anubhashya,” names the *aroya* variety
of rice. The biographies speak of “fine rice.” One of them adds that from time to time Bhagavan Acharya prepared meals in
his own house and served them to Mahaprabhu with no one else present.[^6] <!-- F18 F19 F21 -->

“Haridasa begged the rice from her and brought it, and when the Acharya saw the rice he was overjoyed.” (CC, Antya 2.107)

With affection the Acharya cooked the rice and the dishes the Lord loved, and at midday Mahaprabhu came to him and,
seeing the rice, asked:

“‘Such excellent rice! Where did you get grain like this?’ The Acharya said, ‘It was begged from Madhavi and brought
here.’ The Lord said, ‘Who went and begged it?’ The Acharya named Chota Haridasa.” (CC, Antya 2.110–111)

What followed — how the Lord barred the Younger Haridasa from His presence because he, a renunciant, had conversed with a
woman — is told in Haridasa's own life. When the devotees pleaded for him, they said that in begging rice from Madhavi he
sought nothing but the Lord's pleasure; so Srila Bhaktivinoda Thakura explains their words in the
“Amrita-pravaha-bhashya.” <!-- F20 F22 -->

On Madhavi-devi there is not a shadow of blame in this story: she simply gave rice for the Lord's meal when His companion
asked for it — and that was her service. Kaviraja says:

“Mahaprabhu is an ocean of mercy — who can understand Him? He punishes His own devotee to make people understand
dharma.” (CC, Antya 2.143)

Srila Bhaktisiddhanta explains in the “Anubhashya” to this verse: Sri Madhavi-devi is a maha-bhagavata of a high order,
and for the Lord's companion to accept rice from her as alms was no forbidden act. But in the future deceivers,
imitating this example, would have used it to cover non-Vaishnava views; to deny them such a prop, the Lord, the teacher
of the world, enacted this pastime of a stern lesson. The lesson is addressed to renunciants and to future imitators,
not to her.[^7] <!-- F23 F24 I1 -->

## The Service of Sri Gopinatha
One of the biographies has preserved facts about Madhavi-devi that are not in the original sources. In the village of
Bentpur (Bentapur), not far from Alalanatha, beside the house of Sri Bhavananda Raya, she opened the service of the Deity Sri
Gopinatha, and, according to this biography, He is served there to this day.[^8] <!-- F8 -->

The same biography passes on two more traditions. There is a report that Sri Madhavi-devi composed a drama,
“Sri Purushottama-deva.” And according to some, she was placed by Maharaja Prataparudra in Jagannatha's temple as
*panjiya* — the writer of the “Madala Panji,” the temple chronicle.[^9] <!-- F26 F27 -->

## After the Lord's Disappearance
After the Lord had withdrawn His pastimes, Srinivasa came to Puri and visited the house of Shikhi Mahiti. The
“Bhakti-ratnakara” relates:

“Shikhi Mahiti with his sister says: ‘Only to see you are we still alive.’” (“Bhakti-ratnakara” 3.185)

Narahari Chakravarti does not give the sister's name, but the sources know of no other sister of Shikhi Mahiti, and in
all likelihood this is Madhavi-devi.[^10] These words are not worldly longing or a complaint against life: this is how
separation from the Lord (*vipralambha*) speaks, and meeting His devotee was a solace to them in that separation. When
and where Madhavi-devi herself left this world is not known. <!-- F25 -->

## Significance
Madhavi-devi is one of the few women companions of Mahaprabhu of whom the “Charitamrita” speaks directly and with such
reverence. In the eternal pastimes she is Kalakeli, Sri Radha's maidservant; in Gaura's pastimes she is an elderly
ascetic and a supreme Vaishnavi whom the Lord Himself counted among Radhika's circle. Together with her brother Murari she
gave herself to Gaura before the others in her family and brought her elder brother to Him. Her rice became service to
the Lord, and Mahaprabhu's sternness in the story of the Younger Haridasa was a lesson to renunciants, not a shadow on
her. And her name remains for ever in the verse of the three and a half worthy ones — the “half” who stands beside
Svarupa Damodara, Ramananda Raya and Shikhi Mahiti. <!-- F2 F16 F23 I1 I2 -->
