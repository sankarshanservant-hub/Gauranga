# Gadadhara Dasa
*Гададхара Дас · @gadadhara-dasa*

## Gadadhara of the Dasa Family
Listing the branches of Sri Chaitanya's tree, Krishnadasa Kaviraja speaks of him briefly and solemnly:

“Sri Gadadhara Dasa is a branch above all others; he made even the Kazis utter the name of Hari.” (CC, Adi 10.53)

Vrindavana Dasa Thakura calls him Gadadhara Dasa and Gadadhara Mahashaya, and says that he “was infinitely dear to the
Lord” and that “his body was filled with the sweetness of loving service” (CB, Antya 5.93). Kavi Karnapura in the
“Gaura-ganoddesha-dipika” calls him “Gadadhara of the Dasa family” (*dasa-vamsha-gadadhara*), and later generations
call him Dasa Gadadhara, Sri Gadadhara Dasa Thakura, Srila Gadadhara Dasa Prabhu. He should not be confused with
Gadadhara Pandita, the Lord's eternal companion and shakti.[^1] <!-- F1 F7 -->

Of his worldly life the sources say almost nothing. They name neither his parents nor the year or day of his
appearance; Karnapura tells us only that he was of the Dasa family. Nor is the day of his appearance found in the
Vaishnava calendar of the Sri Chaitanya Saraswat Math. <!-- F8 -->

His shripat is Ariadaha (Eriyadaha, Endiyadaha) on the bank of the Bhagirathi, eight miles north of Calcutta. Srila
Bhaktivinoda Thakura in the “Amrita-pravaha-bhashya” calls him “Gadadhara Dasa of Endiyadaha,” and Srila
Bhaktisiddhanta in the “Gaudiya-bhashya” speaks of the Kazi who lived “in this village of Endiyadaha.”[^2] <!-- F9 -->

## The Radiance of Radha
Who he is in Krishna's eternal pastimes, Kavi Karnapura tells us:

“Chandrakanti, who was a manifestation of Radha's glory, is now near Gauranga as Gadadhara of the Dasa family.”
(“Gaura-ganoddesha-dipika” 154)

And he adds at once:

“Purnananda, who in Vraja was foremost among the beloveds of Baladeva, also entered into this Gadadhara for the sake
of the work.” (“Gaura-ganoddesha-dipika” 155)

Thus two were united in Gadadhara Dasa: Chandrakanti, the radiance of Radha, and Purnananda, the beloved companion of
Balarama; one of the biographies calls him plainly their union. This entering is not possession in any worldly sense
but the spiritual presence (*avesha*) of Baladeva's eternal companion, who came to serve Nityananda, Baladeva Himself;
and so Gadadhara Dasa, though his whole heart belonged to madhura-rasa, remained at Nityananda's side all his life.
<!-- F2 F3 Q7 -->

Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati, in his “Anubhashya” on this verse of the “Charitamrita,” explains the difference
between the two Gadadharas. Gadadhara Pandita is the very form of the daughter of Vrishabhanu, while Gadadhara Dasa is
the beauty of Her body (*anga-shobha*), Her radiance (*kanti*). Gaura is Krishna endowed with the mood and radiance of
Radha (*radha-bhava-dyuti-suvalita*), and Gadadhara Dasa is that radiance (*dyuti*) of Gaura.[^3] <!-- F4 Q6 -->

Therefore he is counted among the associates of both Gaura and Nityananda. The “Charitamrita” says:

“Sri Ramadasa and Gadadhara Dasa, devotees of Chaitanya Gosani, stayed at Nityananda's side. When Mahaprabhu told
Nityananda to go to Gauda, He sent these two with Him. Therefore they are counted in both groups, and so it is with
Madhava and Vasudeva Ghosha.” (CC, Adi 11.13–15)

Srila Bhaktisiddhanta explains: the followers of Gaura are connoisseurs of the madhura-rasa of Vraja, while the
followers of Nityananda serve in sakhya and the other rasas in which pure devotion predominates. Gadadhara Dasa, though
he belonged to Nityananda's circle, is not a cowherd boy in the mood of friendship but a connoisseur of madhura-rasa. In
the “Gaudiya-bhashya” on the “Chaitanya-bhagavata” Srila Bhaktisiddhanta calls him “an eternally perfect resident of
Vraja” (*nitya-siddha vraja-jana*), in whom the transcendental (*aprakrita*) bhava of Radha is manifest. <!-- F5 F6 -->

## With Mahaprabhu in Navadvipa
Before the Lord sent him with Nityananda to Gauda, Gadadhara Dasa lived with Mahaprabhu Himself; that is why the
“Charitamrita” calls him and Ramadasa “devotees of Sri Chaitanya” (Adi 11.13). <!-- F12 -->

Of the days in Navadvipa sing the padas of his disciple Yadunandana and of Narahari Sarakara, preserved in the
“Bhakti-ratnakara” (12) and the “Gaura-pada-tarangini.” Gaura, weeping “Radha!”, embraces Gadai, while Dasa Gadadhara
softly smiles; Gaura exchanges glances with him, holds his hand in kirtana, weeps on his lap. “Gaura is the life of Dasa
Gadadhara,” sings the poet, and Gadadhara Dasa shines beside Gaura “like a moon beside the moon.” <!-- F49 -->

Lochana Dasa in the “Chaitanya-mangala” names him among those who danced in Advaita's house at Shantipur when the Lord
came there after His sannyasa.[^4] In Karnapura's drama “Chaitanya-chandrodaya” (act 10) he is among the Gaudiya
devotees going to the Lord in Puri whom Sarvabhauma meets. <!-- F14 -->

The “Charitamrita” also tells how in Puri the non-brahmanas — Vasudeva Datta, Gadadhara, Murari Gupta, and the residents
of Kulinagrama and Khanda — invited the Lord and fed Him with Jagannatha's prasada bought in the market (Antya
10.140–141). Whether this is the same Gadadhara is hard to say.[^5] <!-- F15 -->

## With Nityananda to Gauda
When Mahaprabhu resolved to open the treasure of divine love to Gauda, He sent Nityananda there:

“To Nityananda He gave this order: ‘Go to Gauda and reveal loving devotion freely, without restraint. I am sending
Ramadasa, Gadadhara and a few others along to help You. From time to time I will come to You, and, staying unseen, I
will watch You dance.’” (CC, Madhya 15.42–44)

Srila Bhaktivinoda Thakura in the “Amrita-pravaha-bhashya” explains that this Gadadhara is Gadadhara Dasa. Vrindavana
Dasa lists all who set out from Puri with Nityananda:

“With Nityananda went Ramadasa, Gadadhara Dasa, Raghunatha Vaidya, full of the sweetness of loving devotion, Krishnadasa
Pandita, Parameshvari Dasa and the jubilant Purandara Pandita.” (CB, Antya 5.231–233)

Gadadhara Dasa became one of Nityananda's chief helpers in preaching the Holy Name in Gauda.[^6] <!-- F16 F17 Q2 -->

Already on the way Nityananda's companions, overwhelmed by love, forgot the outer world, and in each his eternal mood
became manifest. Ramadasa stood bent in three places like a cowherd boy, and Gadadhara Dasa —

“Gadadhara Dasa was filled with the mood of Radhika. Laughing loudly, he cried, ‘Who will buy yogurt?’” (CB, Antya 5.238)

Like a girl of Vraja carrying milk products to sell, he forgot everything, and Nityananda watched him with joy.[^7]
<!-- F18 F19 Q3 -->

## “Who Will Buy Milk?”
Having come to Gauda, Nityananda went round the villages on both banks of the Ganga, and one day He came to the house of
Gadadhara Dasa:

“One day Nityananda came to the house of Gadadhara Dasa to show him His affection. The great soul Gadadhara Dasa was
absorbed in the mood of a gopi and full of the highest bliss. Carrying a pot of Ganga water on his head, he called out
without cease, ‘Who will buy milk?’” (CB, Antya 5.371–373)

In his temple sat a beautiful Bala-Gopala whom he served. Seeing his host's bhava, Nityananda took up Bala-Gopala,
pressed Him to His chest, roared like a lion and began to dance in the mood of a cowherd boy, while all around loudly
sang the names of the Lord (CB, Antya 5.374–377).[^8] <!-- F20 F21 Q3 -->

Then Madhavananda Ghosha, the brother of Vasudeva Ghosha, sang of the dana-lila, and a wonderful bhava arose in
Nityananda; He danced in a way that cannot be described (CB, Antya 5.378–383). The Dana-keli is a pastime of Krishna
with the gopis: Krishna and His friends bar the way of the girls carrying milk products to market and demand a “toll”
from them; Srila Rupa Gosvami described it in the “Dana-keli-kaumudi.” This lila Nityananda revealed in Gadadhara's
house through kirtana and dance, and since then his temple has been renowned as the place of the dana-lila. The
“Charitamrita” says:

“Gadadhara Dasa was full of bliss in the mood of a gopi, and in his house Nityananda revealed the *dana-keli* pastime.
Sri Madhava Ghosha was foremost among the kirtana singers, and Nityananda Prabhu would dance to his singing.”
(CC, Adi 11.17–18)[^9] <!-- F22 Q2 -->

Nityananda stayed in his house for several days, hearing the dana-khanda from Madhavananda and bestowing mercy on many.
And Gadadhara Dasa himself was unaware of the outer world:

“In the mood of a gopi Gadadhara Dasa had no outward awareness; at every moment he thought of himself as a gopi.”
(CB, Antya 5.381)

Unconscious of his body, he kept urging everyone: “Haribol! Haribol!” (CB, Antya 5.393–394). <!-- F23 Q4 -->

Srila Bhaktisiddhanta in the “Gaudiya-bhashya” on this verse explains: Gadadhara Dasa always abided in his eternal
spiritual form (*svarupa-siddhi*), and yet he did not outwardly put on the dress of a sakhi; ever absorbed in the bhava
of a gopi, he displayed no pretence in dress. His gopi mood is not worldly femininity and not imitation but his eternal
nature, the radiance of Radha; therefore he had no need of anything outward. Failing to understand his divine madness of
love, wilful people later invented the community of “sakhi-bhekis”: conscious of themselves as men, they dress the body
in women's clothes. No acharya following Srila Rupa Gosvami ever approved this path; Srila Bhaktisiddhanta calls it a
degradation, an obstacle to devotion (“Gaudiya-bhashya” on CB, Antya 5.238). <!-- F24 F25 I1 Q4 -->

Murari Gupta relates (4.22.13–15) that when Nityananda came to the house of Gadadhara, who was full of the gopi bhava,
Gadadhara, seeing Him, was overwhelmed with love and went with Him in kirtana to Saptagrama; there, on the bank of the
Triveni, while the qualities of Gauranga were sung, he danced in the highest bliss, revealing the mood of a gopi.
<!-- F26 -->

## The Kazi of Ariadaha
In Ariadaha lived a powerful Kazi, the ruler of the village, an enemy of religion who hated kirtana. Srila
Bhaktisiddhanta adds in the “Gaudiya-bhashya” that whoever failed to show him respect he deprived of caste; people
feared him and fled from him. <!-- F27 -->

One night Gadadhara Dasa, intoxicated with love of God, went straight to his house. Vrindavana Dasa tells:

“The people, in fear of that Kazi, would flee from him; but Gadadhara fearlessly went to his house at night. Ceaselessly
calling the name of Hari, he entered the Kazi's house. The Kazi's men saw him, and not one of them could utter a word.”
(CB, Antya 5.397–399)

Ceaselessly calling out the name of Hari, he summoned the master:

“Gadadhara said: ‘Hey, where is that Kazi? Say “Krishna” at once, or I will cut off your head!’” (CB, Antya 5.400)

This is not a threat of violence but the speech of one intoxicated with prema — so Nityananda too spoke to the godless,
and so the Lord Himself came with kirtana to Chand Kazi. And it was not force that softened the Kazi, but the form and
bhava of the devotee. <!-- F28 Q5 -->

“The Kazi came out blazing with anger like fire, but the moment he saw Gadadhara Dasa he grew still.” (CB, Antya 5.401)

His anger subsided, friendliness appeared on his face, and he asked: “Gadadhara, why are you here?” Gadadhara answered
that he had come to say something important:

“Sri Chaitanya and Nityananda Prabhu have descended and made the whole world say ‘Hari, Hari.’ You alone do not say the
name of Hari; I have come to you to make you say it. Say the name of Hari, the supreme blessing, and I will deliver you
from all your sins.” (CB, Antya 5.403–405)

Cruel by nature though he was, the Kazi stood stunned and could not say a word. Then he smiled and said: “Listen, Dasa
Gadadhara. I will say ‘Hari’ tomorrow; today go home” (CB, Antya 5.406–407).[^10] <!-- F29 F30 F31 -->

But the Name had already sounded from his lips, and Gadadhara Dasa was filled with the happiness of love:

“Gadadhara Dasa said: ‘Why tomorrow? You have just said “Hari” yourself, with your own mouth! Since you have taken the
name of Hari, there is no more misfortune for you, not for a moment.’” (CB, Antya 5.409–410)

With these words, in the highest ecstasy of love, he began to dance, clapping his hands (CB, Antya 5.411). The same
reply — “Why tomorrow? You have just said ‘Hari’!” — Srila Bhaktisiddhanta also cites in his “Anubhashya” on CC, Adi
10.53. <!-- F32 Q5 -->

Thus the dreaded Kazi, from whom the whole countryside fled, received the name of Hari. Whence did Gadadhara have such
power? Vrindavana Dasa answers:

“After a while Gadadhara returned to his own house — he in whose body Nityananda abides.” (CB, Antya 5.412)

Gadadhara's body was the abode of Nityananda: what acted in him was Nityananda's power, not the personal power of a man.
One of the biographies says that he delivered the Kazi “by his transcendental power,” another that “Krishna abided in
his body”; the sense is one: only one whom Krishna has empowered can spread the name of Hari. Vallabha Bhatta says this to
the Lord:

“The dharma of the age of Kali is the sankirtana of Krishna's name. Without Krishna's power no one can propagate it.”
(CC, Antya 7.11)

And further: “Without Krishna's power prema is not manifested” (CC, Antya 7.14).[^11] <!-- F33 F34 I2 Q8 -->

Saintly people would not even come near this Kazi for fear of losing caste, yet Gadadhara Dasa bestowed on him a
merciful glance; and that a cruel man gave up his cruelty Vrindavana Dasa calls the work of Krishna within His devotee
(CB, Antya 5.414–416). As Gaurasundara Himself in Mayapur came at the head of the sankirtana to Chand Kazi and made him
utter the Name, so His servant Gadadhara Dasa made the Kazi of his own village utter the name of Hari: the servant
follows in the footsteps of the Lord. <!-- F35 I4 -->

But is it not an offence to give the Name to the faithless? All the sattvata scriptures and Srila Jiva Gosvami count
instructing the faithless in the Name among the offences against the Name. One of the biographies answers: Gadadhara
Dasa did not encourage this offence. He is the very merciful shakti of Gaura-Nityananda, and he endowed even the hostile
Kazi with a special power of mercy, as Narada once showed mercy to Nalakuvara and Manigriva. The prohibition of the
scriptures remains in force; here is the special mercy of a great soul — and the Kazi, moreover, had already uttered the
name of Hari himself. <!-- F36 I3 -->

## The Lord's Mercy at Panihati
When Mahaprabhu, wishing to go to Vrindavana, set out through Gauda, He met the devotees at Shantipur and Kumarahatta and
came to Panihati, to the house of Raghava Pandita. Hearing of this, Gadadhara Dasa hurried there:

“As soon as Gadadhara heard that Sri Gaurasundara was in Raghava's house, he hurried there at once. Gadadhara Dasa was
infinitely dear to the Lord; his body was filled with the sweetness of loving service. Seeing the fortunate Gadadhara,
the Lord placed His lotus feet on his head.” (CB, Antya 5.92–94)

By the Lord's wish Purandara Pandita, Parameshvari Dasa, Raghunatha Vaidya and others came there as well (CB, Antya
5.95–109).[^12] <!-- F13 Q1 -->

Later, when Raghunatha Dasa, at Nityananda's command, held the festival of flat rice with yogurt (chida-dadhi) at
Panihati, Gadadhara Dasa sat with the other associates on the platform around Nityananda:

“Ramadasa, Sundarananda, Gadadhara Dasa, Murari, Kamalakara, Sadashiva, Purandara.” (CC, Antya 6.61)

According to the “Bhakti-ratnakara” (8.156–158), Nityananda came to Panihati with Ramadasa and Gadadhara Dasa.
<!-- F37 Q9 -->

## After the Lord's Disappearance: Navadvipa and Katwa
According to the “Gaudiya-vaishnava-abhidhana,” Gadadhara Dasa lived in Navadvipa and took care of Shachimata and
Vishnupriya Devi, managing their affairs. When they left this world, he settled in Katwa — where Keshava Bharati gave the
Lord sannyasa — and installed a Deity of Gauranga; according to one of the biographies, Srila Bhaktisiddhanta too, in
the “Anubhashya,” calls him the founder of the temple of Gauranga in Katwa. This temple is known in Katwa as the “house
of Mahaprabhu” (*mahaprabhu-bari*); there, beside the Deity, is now his samadhi. Thus Ariadaha remained his shripat,
while his last years he spent in Katwa.[^13] <!-- F10 F11 -->

According to the “Advaita-prakasha” (22), after Shachi's departure Ishana, together with Gadadhara Dasa and Sri Rama
Pandita, goes in to Vishnupriya. <!-- F52 -->

After the Lord's disappearance the elder associates lived in the grief of separation, and their consolation came to be
the young Srinivasa and Narottama. When Srinivasa came to Navadvipa, all received him:

“Sri Murari, Srivasa Pandita, Damodara, Sanjaya, Vijaya, Shuklambara Brahmachari, Dasa Gadadhara and the other dear ones
of the Lord — all showed mercy to Srinivasa. Although in separation from the Lord all were nearly dead, yet by the Lord's
will they felt joy. For the sake of mercy to Srinivasa — for this [the Lord] kept His associates manifest.”
(“Bhakti-ratnakara” 4.57–60)

Later Srinivasa came to him at Kantakanagara (Katwa). Gadadhara Dasa embraced him, asked about the Vaishnavas of
Vrindavana and the Gosvamis, rejoiced and blessed him: “You will surely taste the nectar of the Lord's sankirtana among
His associates; go and marry — it will bring you good.” When Srinivasa married, Gadadhara Dasa and Narahari Sarakara
rejoiced in it especially. <!-- F39 -->

The “Anuragavalli” (2–3) relates that Gadadhara Pandita in Puri sent a riddle to his namesake through Srinivasa, but
Srinivasa forgot to deliver it. Dasa Gadadhara, who already knew of the Pandita's departure, drove him away in grief, but
at Vishnupriya's intercession forgave and blessed him. His bhava is inconceivable: now laughter, now stupor, now the
madness of separation; and his breath scorched one who doubted him. <!-- F53 -->

In Navadvipa-Mayapur Narottama met the elder associates of the Lord — Shuklambara, Ishana, Murari, Damodara and Dasa
Gadadhara — grieving over His disappearance. He came to Katwa as well, to the house of Gadadhara Dasa; there he saw the
Deity of Gaura whom Gadadhara served: “Gaurachandra is the life of Dasa Gadadhara,” says the “Bhakti-ratnakara” (8.453),
and on seeing Him Narottama could not restrain his feelings. Narottama prostrated himself before Gadadhara Dasa, who
embraced him lovingly and bathed him with tears. Gadadhara Dasa spent his days in the pain of separation from Gaura and
Nityananda; Narottama stayed with him two days. <!-- F40 -->

According to the “Narottama-vilasa” (5), it was Yadunandana Chakravarti who brought Narottama to Dasa Gadadhara, who
after Vishnupriya's departure was living in Katwa in grief, and Gadadhara Dasa foretold that the Lord would dance in
Narottama's kirtana. <!-- F54 -->

Yadunandana Chakravarti of Katwa was his disciple: the “Bhakti-ratnakara” calls him “the learned Yadunandana
Chakravarti, whose guru and worshipful lord was Gadadhara Dasa” (9.332).[^14] Shyamananda too, returning from Kheturi,
stayed with Gadadhara Dasa at Kantakanagara. <!-- F38 F41 -->

His grief in these years is not worldly sorrow but the separation of an eternal associate from the Lord — the very
vipralambha in which Mahaprabhu and His intimates lived.

## Departure and Samadhi
Gadadhara Dasa left this world in Katwa. According to our calendar the day of his departure is observed on
Goshthashtami (Gopashtami) — the shukla-ashtami (Gaura-ashtami) of the month of Damodara, on the same day as Srinivasa
Acharya and Dhananjaya Pandita (in 2026, on 17 November). The year of his departure the sources do not give.[^15]
<!-- F43 -->

According to the “Prema-vilasa” (19), in his grief for Vishnupriya he grew weak and disappeared; after him Narahari
Sarakara also departed. Srinivasa, grieving over the departure of Shuklambara, Gadadhara Dasa, Narahari and Dvija
Haridasa, went again to Vrindavana, and on his return took part in the festivals of separation: for Gadadhara Dasa in
Katwa, for Narahari in Shrikhanda, for Dvija Haridasa in Kanchanagaria. The festival held by Srinivasa on the day of
Gadadhara Dasa's departure was immense and became as renowned as the festival at Kheturi (“Bhakti-ratnakara,” 9).
According to the “Prema-vilasa,” the memorial festival in Katwa was arranged by Yadunandana Chakravarti and Raghunandana,
and the branches of Mahaprabhu, Nityananda, Advaita and Gadadhara gathered for it.[^16] <!-- F44 -->

The biographies mention “the festival of Gadadhara Dasa in Katwa,” attended by Raghunandana and Kamalakara Pippalai; to
all appearances it is the same memorial festival.[^17] <!-- F42 -->

His samadhi is in Katwa, in the “house of Mahaprabhu,” beside the samadhi of Keshava Bharati.[^18] <!-- F45 -->

The shripat at Ariadaha survives to this day. The samadhi there had been in the keeping of samyogi Vaishnavas; with the
permission of Srila Bhagavan Dasa Babaji of Kalna, Madhusudana Mallik of Narikeldanga (Calcutta) established a temple
estate (*patbari*) there and began the service of the Deity of Sri Radhakanta (in the Bengali year 1256, about
1849–1850). His son Balai Mallik in 1312 (about 1905–1906) established the service of Sri Gaura-Nitai. On the altar are
the Deities of Gaura-Nityananda and Radha-Krishna, and beneath it a Sanskrit verse is carved; there too is the
Shiva-linga of Gopeshvara. All this is recorded on a stone at the entrance.[^19] <!-- F46 -->

## The Two Gadadharas
In the Bengali year 1340 (about 1933) a booklet appeared, “Dasa Gadadhara-tattva.” Its author, posing as a zealous
admirer of Dasa Gadadhara, set Gadadhara Pandita against him in a sectarian spirit: supposedly the Pandita, having
studied nyaya, at first did not wish to follow Dasa Gadadhara, the expounder of the “Bhagavatam,” but later, seeing
that he lacked the taste of Gauranga's pastimes, became a disciple of Pundarika Vidyanidhi and adopted the mood of Dasa
Gadadhara; and so the Pandita's followers had better leave him and follow the Dasa. Such a view is the height of
godlessness (*pashandita*). <!-- F47 -->

The two Gadadharas must not be divided and set against each other: both are associates of Gaura, His own people, eternal
associates in both Gaura-lila and Vraja-lila. To honour one and disregard the other is what the “Charitamrita” calls the
logic of half a hen:

“You have faith in one but show the other no respect: your standard is the logic of half a hen.” (CC, Adi 5.176)

The acharyas taught no such logic. The difference of which Karnapura and Srila Bhaktisiddhanta speak (Radha Herself and
the radiance of Her body) is no ground for rivalry, but different services within one lila. <!-- F48 I5 Q8 -->

## In Other Original Sources
Lochana Dasa, in the Sutra-khanda of the “Chaitanya-mangala,” names him among the associates who descended with the
Lord. Jayananda in his “Chaitanya-mangala,” listing the wonders of Nityananda's associates, tells that Gadadhara Dasa
threw himself into fire — as Vrindavana Dasa too says, fire, snakes and wild beasts cannot harm those who have love for
Krishna (CB, Antya 5.417). Devakinandana in the “Vaishnava-vandana” ascribes to him the wonder of a karavi bud that
became fragrant like a lotus.[^20] <!-- F50 F51 F55 -->

## Significance
Vrindavana Dasa, concluding the story of the Kazi, says: “Such is the glory of Gadadhara Dasa, who is counted among the
associates of Lord Chaitanya” (CB, Antya 5.413); and elsewhere:

“Gadadhara Dasa was full of the rasas of divine love; merely by seeing him people were freed from all sins.”
(CB, Antya 5.727) <!-- F7 -->

Gadadhara Dasa is Chandrakanti, the radiance of Radha and the radiance of Gaura Himself; Purnananda too abided in him,
and Nityananda acted in him. Therefore he belongs to both Gaura and Nityananda: in the Avadhuta's circle he carried the
madhura-rasa of Vraja. <!-- F2 F4 F5 F34 -->

His life shows that the mood of a gopi is a matter of the heart, not of dress: genuine bhava springs from one's eternal
spiritual form and needs no outward imitation, while dressing up the body is a distortion that no follower of Srila
Rupa ever approved. His fearlessness before the Kazi teaches that only one empowered by Krishna can spread the name of
Hari, and that the mercy of a great soul can touch even one whom no one dares approach. And as the Lord delivered Chand
Kazi, so His servant delivered the Kazi of his own village: the servant follows in the footsteps of the Lord. Lastly, one
must never speak of him so as to belittle the other Gadadhara: both are eternal associates of Gaura. <!-- I1 I2 I3 I4 I5 -->
