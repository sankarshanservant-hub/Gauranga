# Gopala Guru Gosvami
*Gopala Guru Gosvami · @gopala-guru*

## A Boy at the Lord's Side
Neither the “Chaitanya-charitamrita” nor the “Chaitanya-bhagavata” mentions Gopala Guru Gosvami by name. His memory
has been kept alive by the traditions of the Radhakanta Matha in Puri, the house he once headed, by the writings of
his followers, and by a song that is sung on the day of his passing. From these his life can be pieced together. <!-- F2 -->

He was born in Orissa into a brahmana family. His father was named Murari Pandita; his mother's name has not come
down to us, nor have the years of his life or the day of his appearance. His earlier name was Makaradhvaja Pandita. <!-- F1 F3 -->

While still a child he came to be near Mahaprabhu in Puri and served the Lord under Govinda, His personal servant.
From his earliest years he also lived with Vakreshvara Pandita, who cared for him and guided him. Mahaprabhu was
especially tender towards him, often joked with him, and affectionately called him “Gopala,” for in the boy's
childlike ways He saw Gopala-Krishna Himself. For Gopala, in turn, the whole world was contained in Gauranga. He knew
nothing but service to Him, lived for that joy alone, and could not bear to be parted from the Lord even for a
moment: Gaura was his very life. <!-- F4 F5 F6 F7 -->

## How Gopala Became “Guru”
Tradition tells how the boy came by his second name. There lived in Puri a devotee so attached to the holy name that
his tongue kept repeating it ceaselessly, of its own accord. One day Gopala saw him on his way to the latrine
holding his tongue with his fingers, so that the Name would not sound in an unclean place. The boy began to set him
right: the chanting of the holy name is bound by no rules of time or place; it must be repeated always, in purity
and in impurity alike. For if death should come at such a moment, how could one then attain the auspicious
remembrance of the Lord?[^1] <!-- F8 -->

Mahaprabhu heard this and was delighted. He announced to the devotees that Gopala was doing the work of a guru, and
from that day the Vaishnavas called the young Makaradhvaja Pandita Gopala Guru[^2]. Thus the Lord Himself gave him
the name “Guru” because he taught others to chant the names of Krishna in every circumstance. And he earned that
name by his whole life: what he taught, he himself practised — and that is the mark of a true acharya. <!-- F9 F10 F11 I3 I4 -->

## Abhirama's Obeisance
The boy's fame quickly spread among the Vaishnavas. Around that time, tradition says, Abhirama Thakura came to Puri
to test him. Abhirama's obeisance was a fearsome trial: if the stone he bowed to was not a genuine shalagrama, a
vishnu-shila, it would crack or crumble into dust. Only a pure Vaishnava could withstand his obeisance; for anyone
else it could cost him his life. <!-- F12 -->

The Vaishnavas grew anxious for the boy whom they all loved. Seeing their alarm, Mahaprabhu placed His foot on the
boy's forehead — and in memory of this a distinctive tilaka mark is worn to this day in Gopala Guru's line. Gopala
was still afraid, so the Lord took him onto His lap. In this way he received Abhirama's obeisance and came to no
harm. It is also said that Gopala Guru displayed extraordinary powers. <!-- F13 -->

## Disciple of Vakreshvara
Gopala's guru was Vakreshvara Pandita, an intimate companion of the Lord, who in the last years of His pastimes
settled permanently in Puri in order to dance in His sankirtana and serve Him (see the article on Vakreshvara
Pandita). Gopala was his dear and principal disciple. His devotion to his guru was beyond description, and
Vakreshvara was always pleased with him. According to the “Vakreshvara-charita,” five branches issued from
Vakreshvara — through Chandrashekhara, Shankararanya Acharya, Govindananda, Devananda, and Gopala Guru, whose
qualities surpass all description. <!-- F14 F15 F16 F17 -->

## Heir of Svarupa Damodara
Svarupa Damodara set down the worship of Krishna in rasa, rasopasana, in his diary, his kadacha, in two parts. The
inner path he placed in the mouth of Raghunatha Dasa, the outer path in that of Vakreshvara. From Raghunatha the
current reached Krishnadasa Kaviraja, who revealed the “Chaitanya-charitamrita” and the “Govinda-lilamrita.” From
Vakreshvara it came to Gopala Guru, who in the line of disciplic succession revealed the
“Smarana-krama-paddhati.” Thus Svarupa's method of worship in madhura-rasa spread along two lines: from Raghunatha
to Kaviraja, and from Vakreshvara to Gopala Guru[^3]. <!-- F19 F20 F21 -->

Srila Bhaktivinoda Thakura writes of him more than once in the “Jaiva-dharma” (ch. 26). In Purushottama, in the
house of Kashi Mishra, on the very seat of Mahaprabhu, there now sits Vakreshvara's disciple Gopala Guru Gosvami,
and the teaching of Svarupa Gosvami now lives in his mouth; he knows it all by heart. Bhaktivinoda calls him the
chief guru of the Sri Nimananda-sampradaya in Purushottama, a teacher of the world seated on Svarupa's gadi in
Mahaprabhu's place. The science of rasa that the book's hero Vrajanatha hears from Gopala Guru's lips differs in
nothing from what is set out in the “Bhakti-rasamrita-sindhu” and the “Ujjvala-nilamani.” Bhaktivinoda, then, saw
in him a follower of Svarupa and Rupa. <!-- F18 F22 F23 Q7 -->

When Gopala Guru took Svarupa's gadi in Mahaprabhu's place, the Gaudiya Vaishnavas who followed the path of Svarupa
and Rupa welcomed it gladly, without a trace of envy. The pure Vaishnavas of his day recognised him as the chief
guru of the Sri Nimananda-sampradaya. Some communities, secretly hungry for prestige, hold that a disciple who sits
as acharya on his guru's gadi offends him by usurping his place. But Gopala Guru gave no occasion for such
reproach, and Bhaktivinoda did not share that view. When a loving, worthy, and well-conducted disciple takes the
vyasa-asana, the guru-asana, in order to serve his guru, that is genuine service to the guru: a joy to him and a
blessing to the world. <!-- F24 I1 -->

## Radhakanta and the Gambhira
In Nilachala, Mahaprabhu lived in the house of Kashi Mishra. The tradition of the Radhakanta Matha also tells of the
Deity of that house. King Purushottama-deva, the father of Prataparudra, having defeated the king of Kanchi,
brought back several Deities from there, among them Radhakanta. At first He was housed in a small temple by the
north-western corner of the Chatrabhoga, Jagannatha's kitchen; later Kashi Mishra, the guru of Prataparudra, asked
for Him for his own personal worship. <!-- F27 -->

Kashi Mishra had no children, and he bequeathed all his property to Mahaprabhu. Mahaprabhu in turn entrusted the
service of Radhakanta, together with His temple and gardens, to Gopala Guru and made him the first acharya of the
Radhakanta Matha. Vakreshvara himself never sat on the gadi of that house: he remained in kirtana and dance beside
the Lord. This took place on the shukla-dvadashi of the month of Magha, and ever since, on that day each year, the
abhisheka of the present head of the matha is performed[^4]. <!-- F25 F26 F28 F29 -->

Since Gopala Guru's time the house of Kashi Mishra has been called the Radhakanta Matha. In Kashi Mishra's day
Krishna alone was worshipped here; under Gopala Guru, Radha came to stand at Radhakanta's left and Lalita at His
right, and shri-murtis of Gauranga and Nityananda were installed as well. At the same time the temple buildings
were restored and enlarged — between 1538 and 1548. <!-- F30 -->

In an Orissan temple, as Bhaktivinoda explains, a veranda runs in front of the altar room, a covered paved court lies
before that, and a small room in this part of the house is called a gambhira. The Gambhira in Kashi Mishra's house is
the very room where Mahaprabhu sank into contemplation and where He slept. Gopala Guru kept here His wooden sandals
and His quilt, sewn by Svarupa Damodara or by Jagadananda Pandita — they are worshipped to this day. Here too stands
a clay water-pot from Vraja that the Lord used, and a wooden pot placed there later. On one side of the Radhakanta
Matha is the temple of Radhakanta; on the other, the room where Gopala Guru used to sit, in which his shri-murti is
now installed. <!-- F31 F32 F33 -->

In Utkala most of those who belong to his line of disciples call themselves Gaudiya Vaishnavas. <!-- F34 -->

## Narottama in the House of Kashi Mishra
When Narottama Dasa Thakura came to Puri, Gopala Guru received him in the house of Kashi Mishra with great love.
Narahari Chakravarti describes the meeting in the “Bhakti-ratnakara” (8.382–392). Gopala Guru confides to his
guest that he lives on only by the Lord's will, and that his heart breaks each time he sees these places. He shows
him the grass mat on which the Lord sat alone in deep seclusion, and the place where He slept while Govinda
massaged His feet. Here the Lord revealed a love beyond the reach even of Brahma. <!-- F35 F36 -->

Seeing the Lord's bed and seat, Narottama rolled on the ground and wept. Gopala Guru embraced him in agitation, and
both cried aloud. Then, recovering himself, Gopala Guru consoled his guest, showed him all the places where the Lord
had been overwhelmed by divine emotion, told him of Vakreshvara, and placed Narottama at the lotus feet of
Radhakanta. So Narottama received the darshana of Gopala Guru's feet and of the Deities of the Radhakanta Matha,
and from there went on to the Gundicha. <!-- F37 F38 Q1 -->

## Writings
His principal work is the “Smarana-krama-paddhati,” also known as the “Seva-smarana-paddhati” or simply the
“Smarana-paddhati,” that is, “the method of remembrance” of the Lord. It consists of twenty-six chapters or topics.
It begins with the nature of Krishna; then come the evidence that service to Him in Vraja is rendered in sweetness,
madhurya, a discussion of fear and similar feelings in His humanlike pastimes, and how He differs from the jiva. Next come the manifest and unmanifest
pastimes: parakiya, the gopis' three months of separation, Krishna's return to Vraja after the slaying of
Dantavakra, the eternity of the pastimes in the three dhamas, the unsurpassed pastimes of the young cowherd, and
Vrindavana as Goloka. Then raganuga-bhajana in its two forms, kama-rupa and sambandha-rupa, the qualification for
it, and service in the body of the sadhaka. After this, mantras and meditations: the thirty-two-syllable mantra
composed of sixteen names and meditation on it according to the tantras, the ten-syllable mantra as the basis of
the Gopala-mantra, the kama-gayatri, radha-tattva and the mantra of Radha, remembrance of the guru and the order of
remembering the line of gurus, the gaura-mantra and gaura-gayatri. And finally the service itself: to Gaurasundara
from His waking until His rest; to Radha-Govinda in the spiritual form, the siddha-deha, at the side of one's guru
in the form of a sakhi, together with Lalita and the other sakhis and with Rupa-manjari; meditation on the Divine
Couple, the lotus, the eight sakhis
and the groups, yuthas, with their mantras, remembrance of Radha-Govinda in the thousand-petalled lotus, the
ashtakala-lila, and the order of japa. <!-- F39 F42 -->

Gopala Guru opens the order of worship of Sri Chaitanya thus: rising before sunrise, having bathed and dressed, the
devotee bows in turn to his guru, parama-guru, paratpara-guru, parama-preshtha-guru and the Gosvamis, then to the
chief companions of Chaitanya headed by Svarupa and Rupa, then to Chaitanya Himself, and after this, following the
instructions of the guru and the Gosvamis, serves Him from His waking until His rest. This alone shows how he
revered Svarupa and Rupa. Here he calls his guru the sakhi Tungavidya and the Son of Shachi the Son of Nanda: “I
honour Vakreshvara Pandita — Sri Tungavidya; I honour Sri Chaitanya, the son of Shachi — Sri Nanda-nandana.” And he
adds that in the age of Kali she became renowned as Vakreshvara. <!-- F43 F44 Q2 Q3 -->

The book closes with a verse saying that neither saffron robes, nor strict rules of diet, nor life in the forest, nor
learned commentary, nor a vow of silence can destroy the lust born of the mind. What destroys it is even the slightest
beginning of service to the feet of Govinda, who plays on the banks of the broad Yamuna. <!-- F45 Q4 -->

At Vakreshvara's instruction Gopala Guru drew out the gaura-mantra and the order of worship of Gaura from many
eternal sattvata-shastras and made them the treasure of the devotees. Gaurasundara is not merely a historical figure, a
hero of this world: He is the beginningless, original Govinda, the cause of all causes, and therefore His name, His
mantra, and the order of His worship are eternal. Those communities that oppose the gaura-mantra have only
deprived themselves: Gopala Guru's paddhati is closed to them. <!-- F46 I5 -->

The “Gaura-govindarchana-paddhati” is also his. His “Smarana-paddhati” and the “Dhyanachandra-paddhati” of his
disciple are well known among pure Vaishnavas. Several old manuscripts of both books have been seen, copied in the hand of Srila
Bhaktivinoda Thakura and Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati and gathered in Vraja-mandala. Gopala Guru's paddhati
itself Bhaktivinoda received from his gadi. It was kept in various ancient mathas of Vraja, and that alone shows
how the Gaudiya Vaishnavas revered it. Since it describes the methods of bhajana, the original is not usually
printed, and spreading either paddhati among all is especially forbidden. <!-- F40 F41 -->

Gopala Guru also composed the “Harinamartha-nirnaya,” an explanation of the meaning of the holy name. It contains
the verse “sri-chaitanya-mukhodgirna…” with which the “Sara-sangraha,” ascribed to Srila Jiva Gosvami, opens. Linked
to his explanation of the maha-mantra is the account Manohara Dasa gives in the “Anuraga-valli” (8.15–30). Searching
for a description of Mahaprabhu's succession, Manohara Dasa met in Vrindavana, in the kunja of Sri Jiva Gosvami, an
old Vaishnava named Radhavallabha Dasa, of the family of Gopala Guru's disciples. He gave him an old manuscript of
Gopala Guru's — an explanation of the Hari-nama and a determination of the sampradaya. “His seat is in Nilachala, the
service of Radhakanta,” Manohara Dasa writes of him, and quotes four shlokas from that manuscript: <!-- F47 F48 -->

“Revealing the truth of Bhagavan, whose form is concentrated consciousness and bliss, He removes ignorance and its
effects; therefore He is remembered as Hari. She steals the mind of Sri Krishna, She whose nature is Krishna's
delight; therefore by the word ‘Hara’ (vocative ‘Hare’) Sri Radha is glorified. The master of the sole joy of bliss,
Shyama, lotus-eyed, the delight of Gokula, the son of Nanda — He is called Krishna. The embodiment of the whole
essence of artfulness, the presiding deity of the pastimes, He eternally delights Radhika — therefore He is called
Rama.” (“Anuraga-valli,” 8.26) <!-- Q5 -->

Gopala Guru's writings also record the succession from Madhva — as do the “Gaura-ganoddesha-dipika” and the
“Prameya-ratnavali.” Baladeva Vidyabhushana's list of acharyas from Madhva to Chaitanya seems to have been taken from
the “Gaura-ganoddesha-dipika,” and also from Gopala Guru's sadhana-paddhati and the works of Dhyanachandra,
Chaitanya Dasa, and Manohara Dasa. <!-- F49 -->

## Disciples and Succession
His disciple was Dhyanachandra Gosvami, one of the important acharyas of the Gaudiya sampradaya and author of the
“Dhyanachandra-paddhati.” The “Guru-pranali” — a record of the succession compiled by Sri Adikanda Dasa and found in
Vraja-mandala — traces the line thus: Mahaprabhu — Vakreshvara Pandita — Gopala Guru Gosvami — Dhyanachandra Mahanta
Gosvami (in the manuscript his name appears twice) — Balabhadra Mahanta — Vanamali Dasa — Prabhakara Dasa — Sadananda
Dasa — Kalpataru Dasa — Paramananda Dasa — Dinabandhu Dasa — Adikanda Dasa. <!-- F53 F54 -->

The Utkala poet Govinda-deva, of Vakreshvara's circle, glorifies Vakreshvara in his poem “Sri Gaura-krishnodaya”
(1680 Shaka, 1758 CE; 1.5, 18.60), yet does not mention Gopala Guru, Vakreshvara's principal disciple. Perhaps the
poet was not of his branch; nor does he anywhere name his own guru (see the article on Vakreshvara Pandita). <!-- F55 Q8 -->

## His Passing and the Traditions about It
Growing old, Gopala Guru handed over charge of the Radhakanta Matha and the service of Radhakanta to his disciple
Dhyanachandra. Soon afterwards he left his body. What follows is told by tradition. Dhyanachandra, stricken with
grief, carried his guru's body to the Svarga-dvara on the seashore, where cremations were held in Puri in those
days. While the disciples were there, officers of a government department barred the temple, declaring that
authority had been passed to Dhyanachandra without the minister's sanction. <!-- F56 F57 -->

Hearing this, Dhyanachandra fell weeping at the feet of his guru, whose body already lay upon the funeral pyre. The
prayer of his beloved disciple reached Gopala Guru: he rose from the pyre, chanting the holy names in sankirtana.
The officials took fright and gave up their claim to the matha. Gopala Guru returned to the temple, made over the
house to his disciple by a lawful deed, and some time later departed for good[^5]. <!-- F58 F59 -->

This was on the shukla-navami of the month of Kartika (in our calendar, the month of Damodara), and on that day the
Gaudiya Vaishnavas honour his memory[^6]. <!-- F60 -->

A year after his passing, tradition relates, devotees returning to Vraja from the Ratha-yatra were astonished to see
him in Vrindavana, at Vamshivata: he was sitting beneath a pakur tree chanting the holy name. When Dhyanachandra
learned of it, he hurried to Vrindavana, fell at his feet, and begged to be allowed to stay in Vraja, though Gopala
Guru told him to return to Puri and his duties. <!-- F61 -->

At last Gopala Guru said: if the separation was so unbearable, let Dhyanachandra have his image carved from neem
wood, set it before the altar room of the temple, and serve it. So it was done, and his shri-murti is worshipped in
the Radhakanta Matha to this day. <!-- F62 -->

On the day of his passing a song about him is sung, signed with the name Narahari. The song calls him a wish-fulfilling tree for
the devotees, recalls Makaradhvaja
Pandita, whom the Lord named Gopala; it says that he was always intoxicated with his own songs, incomparable and
captivating every heart; that no one was as merciful as he, that all praised him, and that he made everyone worthy
of divine love. The poet laments that, because of his sins, he alone has been left without prema. <!-- F50 F63 F64 F66 -->

## Manjumedhika
Gopala Guru has always been honoured by the whole Gaudiya Vaishnava community. In calling his guru Tungavidya and
the Son of Shachi the Son of Nanda, he preached nothing contrary to siddhanta — nothing like the later gaura-nagari
teaching that grew up in Shrikhanda: the teaching of Svarupa and Rupa stood firm in his mouth. His paddhati shows
that his worship of Gaura as the Son of Nanda and of the guru as a sakhi remains within the siddhanta of Svarupa and
Rupa. <!-- F64 F65 I2 -->

Who he is in the eternal pastimes is told by the “Guru-pranali” of his line — both Adikanda Dasa's manuscript and
the edition of the Radhakanta Matha. Vakreshvara in his perfected form is Tungavidya. She has eight sakhis:
Manjumedha, Sumadhura, Sumadhya, Madhurekshana, Tanumadhya, Madhusudha, Gunachuda, and Varangana. “The
magnanimous Gopala Guru is her maidservant, her anugopi; by Chaitanya's mercy his siddha name is Manjumedhika.” Thus
in Vraja he is the sakhi Manjumedha, the maidservant of Tungavidya[^7]. <!-- F51 F52 Q6 -->
