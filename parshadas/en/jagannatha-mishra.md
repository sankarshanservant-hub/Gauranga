# Jagannatha Mishra
*Джаганнатха Мишра · @jagannatha-mishra*

## A Family from Shrihatta
Jagannatha Mishra's forebears lived in Shrihatta (Sylhet), in eastern Bengal. The “Prema-vilasa” (ch. 24) preserves
their genealogy. Madhu Mishra had four sons — Upendra, Rangada,[^1] Kirtida and Kirtivasa. The eldest, Upendra Mishra,
and his wife Kalavati[^2] raised seven sons: Kamsari, Paramananda, Padmanabha, Sarveshvara, Jagannatha, Janardana and
Trailokyanatha. Jagannatha was the fifth; he was born in the village of Dhaka-dakshina. Upendra himself is identified
by the “Gaura-ganoddesha-dipika” (35) as the cowherd Parjanya, Krishna's grandfather in Vraja. <!-- F1 F2 F3 F4 F5 F6 -->

From Shrihatta Jagannatha moved to the bank of the Ganga, to Navadvipa, and built his house in Mayapur — on the very
spot where the Lord would later appear. Krishnadasa Kaviraja describes him thus:

“The best of the brothers, Sri Jagannatha Mishra, was also known as Purandara. Like Krishna's fathers — Vasudeva and
Nanda Maharaja — he was a reservoir of every virtue.” (CC, Adi 13.59)

“His wife, Srimati Shachidevi, was a chaste woman, wholly devoted to her husband. Her father was Nilambara
Chakravarti.” (CC, Adi 13.60)

The title “Purandara” is one of the epithets of Indra, king of the gods. <!-- F7 F8 F9 Q1 -->

## Neighbours and Friends
Around Mishra's house settled people who were destined to become the Lord's companions. Even before His appearance
Srivasa built his home close by on the bank of the Ganga. He and his brothers were Jagannatha's close friends;
tradition says they understood in advance that Krishna would come to this very house. Srivasa visited Mishra so often
that he was called his host's shadow, and his wife Malini became Shachi's inseparable friend. Nearby also lived
Madhava Mishra and Ratnavati, the parents of Gadadhara Pandita. <!-- F36 F37 F38 -->

Later Jagadisha Pandita came from Assam with his wife Duhkhini and his brother Hiranya[^3] and also settled next door.
Jagadisha became one of Jagannatha's closest friends. He and Hiranya knew who had been born as their neighbour's son,
and so any request of the little boy was a joy to them. <!-- F67 F68 -->

## The Children and the Lord's Appearance
For a long time grief weighed upon the family: Shachi and Jagannatha had eight daughters, and not one survived.[^4]
Then Jagannatha performed a special rite praying for a son, and soon Vishvarupa was born — a manifestation of
Baladeva, the Sankarshana of the spiritual world, who is also called Nityananda's second self. By the day of
Mahaprabhu's appearance He was the only one of the children left in the house. <!-- F17 F18 F19 F20 -->

Before He comes, the Lord sends to the earth those whom He will honour as His elders:

“Before descending to the earth, Krishna sends here many of His devotees, who take the roles of His respected
forefathers, teachers and elders.” (CC, Adi 3.92)

“Thus first His venerable parents and teachers take birth on the earth.” (CC, Adi 3.93)

“Madhavendra Puri, Ishvara Puri, Srimati Shachimata and Jagannatha Mishra appeared together with Advaita Acharya.”
(CC, Adi 3.94)

Lochana Dasa, in the “Chaitanya-mangala,” tells how Krishna, while still in Vrindavana, revealed to Narada that He
would appear in Navadvipa in a golden form, together with His associates, as the son of Shachi and Jagannatha Mishra.
<!-- F15 Q5 -->

When Shachi was carrying the Lord, in the sixth month Advaita Acharya came to the house. He bowed to the child in her
womb and walked around her, and the couple could not understand what this homage meant. When the time drew near,
Jagannatha, seeing the signs of the coming birth, sent word to Shantipur. On hearing it, Advaita bathed in the Ganga
with Haridasa, sang and danced for a long time, and then sent His wife Sita to Mayapur. <!-- F22 F23 -->

The Lord appeared on the full-moon day of the month of Phalguna in 1486 (1407 of the Shaka era), at sunset, during a
lunar eclipse. The townspeople were calling “Hari!” and “Krishna!”, the women raised the joyful ulu cry, and the music
of the gods sounded from the heavens. So in Sri Mayapur the Lord accepted Jagannatha and Shachi as His father and
mother. When they saw their son's face, both forgot themselves in joy. Jagannatha himself and all the people of Nadia
who came to see the child marvelled at His beauty: a lion's neck, arms like an elephant's trunk, a broad chest, and on
His feet the marks of Vishnu. “This is no ordinary child,” people said. Day and night crowds came to Mishra's house,
and it became like Vaikuntha; Narahari Sarakara, too, sings of this in one of his songs. <!-- F24 F25 F26 F27 -->

The child's maternal grandfather, Nilambara Chakravarti, a master of the science of the stars, cast His horoscope and
announced that the boy would be a king of kings and would hold every virtue in Himself — more than that, He was
Narayana Himself. Jagannatha and the whole household were plunged in an ocean of happiness. According to one account,
a realised soul in the guise of a brahmana was present when the horoscope was cast. He foretold that through this
child the world would receive love for Krishna in its highest, most intimate rasa, such as no one had ever given
before. “How fortunate I am to have cast the Lord's horoscope!” he said, and named the baby Vishvambhara. He also
foresaw the future sannyasa but kept silent, so that the sad news would not cloud the parents' joy.[^5]
<!-- F28 F29 F30 -->

The women, however, called the boy Nimai — after the bitter neem tree, which, it is said, witches and evil spirits
dare not approach. In this way they wished Him a long life. Later, on taking sannyasa, He received the name Sri
Krishna Chaitanya; He is also called Vishvambhara, Navadvipa-chandra, Gaurahari, Gauranga and Mahaprabhu. <!-- F31 -->

Sita Thakurani, Advaita's wife, soon arrived in a palanquin, with maidservants and a wealth of gifts. On the day
mother and child first came out of the birth chamber, she gave them presents and honoured Jagannatha, and the couple
in turn honoured her. Afterwards she often came from Shantipur and taught the young parents how to care for the child.
On His birthday Shachi and Jagannatha gave her the honour of being the first to offer the boy new clothes. Srivasa and
his wife also came with gifts: they brought oil, bananas and fruits and performed the auspicious rites.
<!-- F34 F35 F102 -->

On the day after Phalguni-purnima, on Krishna-pratipada, Vaishnavas still keep the Anandotsava of Jagannatha Mishra,
remembering the festival of joy he held to celebrate his son's birth. The days of Gaura's and Nityananda's appearance are themselves holy:

“Nityananda was born on the thirteenth day of the bright fortnight of Magha, and Gaurachandra appeared on the full
moon of Phalguna.” (CB, Adi 3.45)

Whoever honours these days with fasting and festival has the bonds of ignorance cut and the desire to serve Krishna
awakened. <!-- F32 F33 Q7 I6 -->

## Nimai's Childhood
Only the holy names could console the crying baby: as soon as the adults began to sing, He fell silent. At four
months He would scatter household things about, but on catching sight of His mother He would lie down and cry. Shachi
never thought to blame Him: she supposed it was an evil spirit that her protective prayers had driven off. Every
morning the neighbour women gathered round the boy and sang the names of Hari, while He danced and rolled in the
dust. The sweets and bananas that friends and strangers alike gave Him He handed out to the singers as prasada.
<!-- F39 F40 F47 -->

When the time came to test the child's inclinations, Jagannatha laid before Him rice, puffed rice, gold, silver and a
manuscript of the “Srimad-Bhagavatam.” The boy reached past everything straight for the book, and the learned guests
foretold that He would be a great pandita. Some biographies place this at the name-giving ceremony, others at the
annaprashana, the first feeding of rice; the latter add that He pressed the book to His chest, as if foreshadowing
His future preaching.[^6] At the annaprashana, one biography says, He took neither grain, nor silver, nor gold, the
things that would suit a vaishya, nor puffed rice, which a brahmana fond of food would snatch, but out of the many
scriptures that follow the Veda He chose the “Bhagavatam” alone. The meaning of this pastime is plain: the highest authority in spiritual matters is the
“Bhagavatam.” According to the “Chaitanya-mangala,” the annaprashana was held when He was six months old, and only
afterwards was He named. <!-- F41 F42 F43 F109 I2 -->

Vishvambhara learned to walk holding His father's finger with His tiny hand. When He had begun to crawl, He once saw a
snake in the courtyard — it was Shesha. The golden Narayana played with him and lay down on his coils. Jagannatha and
the whole household cried out and wept in terror, and the snake slid away. Shachi and Jagannatha had seen their son's
wondrous form, and in their hearts they no longer doubted that He was the Lord Himself. <!-- F44 F45 F46 -->

As He grew, Nimai would go into the neighbours' houses for milk and rice, and if He found nothing to eat He would
break their pots. His mischief delights devotees to this day. Once, as He was playing by the gate, two thieves coveted
His ornaments, put Him on their shoulders and carried Him off. But the Lord bewildered them, and without noticing it
they came back to Mishra's house and fled in fear. Jagannatha, who had been searching everywhere, pressed his son to
his heart: it seemed to him that his own life had been given back. <!-- F48 F49 I3 -->

Another time the father asked Nimai to bring a book from the house. While the boy ran to fetch it, the parents heard a
wonderful jingling of ankle bells, and then saw on the floor small footprints bearing the marks of the flag, the
thunderbolt, the elephant goad and the banner. Parental love would not let them believe these were their son's
footprints. They decided that their household Deity, the Damodara shalagrama, was walking about the house, and they
bathed Him, worshipped Him and made Him an offering.[^7] Thus again and again their love for the child hid from them
who He was. <!-- F50 F51 F52 I4 -->

## The Pilgrim with Bala-Gopala
Once a pilgrim brahmana who worshipped Bala-Gopala — Krishna as a child — passed through Navadvipa. Jagannatha took him
in and gave him a place for his worship and everything needed for cooking. But no sooner had the guest sat down to
offer the food to his Deity in meditation than Nimai appeared and began to eat. The brahmana cried out. Jagannatha,
deeply upset, was about to punish his son, but the guest held him back. <!-- F53 F54 -->

The brahmana did not want to cook again, yet his host prevailed upon him and this time took Nimai to a neighbour's
house. It all happened again: the moment the pilgrim began his offering, Gaura-Gopala was there. “Everything is
ruined!” the guest lamented, and when Jagannatha again wanted to punish the boy, he said: “He is only a child and
understands nothing. It is not His fault — it seems I am not meant to eat today.” <!-- F55 F56 -->

The third time it was the elder brother, Vishvarupa, who persuaded the guest. It was already long past midnight;
Nimai was asleep, and the brahmana waited until the whole house was sleeping. Yet even now Gaura-Gopala came to the
offering — and showed the guest an eight-armed form: in four hands the conch, disc, club and lotus of Narayana, in one
a lump of butter, from which He took and ate with another, and with two He played the flute. The pilgrim fainted. On
coming to, he heard: “Tell no one of this. You have been My servant for many lives; the same thing happened in Nanda's
house in Gokula.” From then on the brahmana wandered no more and came every day to Mishra's house to see his
Lord.[^8] <!-- F57 F58 F59 F60 -->

## Schooling
The rites of childhood — the piercing of the ears, the first tonsure and the initiation into letters — Jagannatha
performed himself. In three days the boy learned the whole alphabet and began to read and write the names of Krishna.
On the day his son began school, the father was very happy. That night a brahmana appeared to him in a dream and said:
“Your son is Bhagavan Himself. Who can teach the Lord or punish Him?” Mishra understood this in the dream, but on
waking he gave himself up again to parental love and forgot it. <!-- F61 F62 F63 -->

Sometimes Nimai would demand birds, the moon or the stars from His parents and cry if He did not get them; only the
holy names would quiet Him. One Ekadashi even they did not help. At last the boy confessed that He wanted to taste what
had been offered to Vishnu that day in the house of the brahmanas Jagadisha and Hiranya. Jagannatha was astonished:
how could a child know about the offering? Besides, was it fitting for his son to eat rice on Ekadashi? Still, he went
to his friends and told them of the request. Jagadisha understood at once that it was Gopala Himself, in the form of
Nimai, who was hungry, and without hesitation gave Him the whole offering. As soon as the boy received the prasada His
tears dried, and He joyfully began to eat. <!-- F64 F65 F66 -->

“Other branches are Jagadisha Pandita and Hiranya Mahashaya, to whom Sri Chaitanya showed His greatest mercy in His
childhood.” (CC, Adi 10.70)

“In their houses Sri Chaitanya asked for the food offered to Vishnu on the day of Ekadashi, and ate it Himself.”
(CC, Adi 10.71)

For Krishna the son of Nanda, Gaurasundara the son of Jagannatha Mishra, and Lord Jagannatha are one and the same
Supreme Lord. <!-- Q8 F69 -->

The men complained of Nimai's pranks to His father, the girls and women to Shachi. Jagannatha would grow angry and go
to punish his son, but would find Him so calm and innocent that he could only marvel. “Who is this child?” the parents
wondered. “Has Nandanandana Himself been secretly born to us?” To treat the Lord as a son is granted only to His
eternal associate. <!-- F70 F71 I5 -->

## Vishvarupa Leaves Home
From childhood Vishvarupa was indifferent to worldly things. He studied with Advaita Acharya and understood that all
the scriptures come down to one thing — service to Krishna. When He was twelve and His parents began to speak of
marriage, He left home and took sannyasa with the name Shankararanya. Shachi and Jagannatha were struck down by
separation from their firstborn; little Gaurasundara grieved too. Advaita Acharya and Sita Thakurani consoled them and
helped to bring up Nimai. <!-- F72 F73 F74 F75 F76 -->

Now the father feared that learning would turn the younger son away from the family as well. “Better he stay an
unlettered boy at home than become a pandita and leave,” he decided, and stopped his son's lessons.[^9] Nimai answered
with fresh mischief. Once He sat down on a heap of discarded pots in which food had been cooked for Vishnu. Shachi
called Him away, but He answered like the sage Dattatreya: an unlettered boy cannot tell pure from impure; pots in
which food was cooked for Vishnu cannot be defiled; and where I am there can be no impurity; talk of purity drawn from
the karma-kanda is only the invention of those who know nothing but the material world. Seeing that her son would not
move, Shachi dragged Him away by force, bathed Him and bathed herself. Then she and the neighbours came to Jagannatha
and begged him to let the boy return to His studies, and the father gave way. <!-- F77 F78 F79 F80 F81 -->

## The Last Years
After the upanayana — the sacred-thread ceremony — Nimai, like Vamana, went round the houses with a begging bowl. Then
Jagannatha placed Him under Gangadasa Pandita — he who in Krishna's pastimes had been His teacher Sandipani Muni.
Gangadasa was delighted to have such a pupil, though the boy plagued him and His fellow students with tricky questions
of logic, to everyone's amazement. <!-- F82 F83 -->

The father rejoiced at his son's disciplined life: a bath in the Ganga every day, worship of Vishnu according to all
the rules, water for the tulasi, prasada — and then study in solitude. Mishra prayed to Krishna for his son's welfare, yet fear
would not leave him: what if his son grasped the emptiness of samsara and went away? One night he dreamed of
Mahaprabhu as a sannyasi: surrounded by devotees, He was singing, dancing, laughing and weeping in sankirtana. From
then on Mishra was sure that Nimai would leave home, and Shachi, however much she argued that their son was too
absorbed in His studies, could not dissuade him. Much later, admonishing his pupil, Gangadasa would tell Him: “Your
maternal grandfather is Nilambara Chakravarti, your father Jagannatha Mishra; in neither family has there ever been
an unlettered man.” <!-- F84 F85 F86 F87 F88 -->

## Departure from the World
Jagannatha Mishra did not see his son's sannyasa: he left this world before it. This happened soon after the
upanayana, when Nimai had become wholly absorbed in study; according to the “Chaitanya-mangala,” Mishra fell ill and
entered the unseen, eternal pastimes of the Lord.[^10] Gaurasundara mourned His father as Ramachandra mourned
Dasharatha, but held firm in order to console His mother. He explained to Shachi how fleeting a man's life in this
world is, and revealed other truths to her; after this He took up His studies in earnest. <!-- F89 F90 F91 F92 F93 F94 -->

To perform the shraddha for His father the Lord went to Gaya. There He met Ishvara Puri and, as Vrindavana Dasa Thakura
relates (CB, Adi 17), told him that His pilgrimage had been crowned with success: an offering to the ancestors at a
holy place delivers the one for whom it is made, but the mere sight of Ishvara Puri delivers countless souls in a
moment. <!-- F95 Q9 -->

The care of the widow's household was taken up by Chandrashekhara Acharyaratna, who even in Jagannatha's lifetime had
constantly come with his wife to see the child and help in the house. Soon Shachi married her son to Lakshmipriya,
the daughter of Vallabhacharya. The matchmaker Vanamali Acharya told the bride's father: “Vishvambhara, the son of Jagannatha Mishra,
is a great scholar and an ocean of every virtue.” <!-- F96 F97 -->

## Mishra's House
Ishana Thakura served in the house as Jagannatha's faithful servant. Sannyasis, too, remembered the hospitality of
this home. Once Madhavendra Puri had come here with his disciple Sri Ranga Puri, and Shachi had served them a curry of
banana flowers. Years later, in Pandharpur, Ranga Puri met Mahaprabhu, learned that He came from Navadvipa, and
recalled:

“They had then taken their meal in the house of Jagannatha Mishra, and he still remembered the indescribable taste of
the banana-flower curry.” (CC, Madhya 9.296)

“Jagannatha Mishra's wife remained in his memory as a most devoted and chaste woman. She showed them such care, as if
she were the mother of the whole universe.” (CC, Madhya 9.297)

“One of her worthy sons took sannyasa while still young, receiving the name Shankararanya.” (CC, Madhya 9.299)

“Sri Ranga Puri told Sri Chaitanya that Shankararanya had attained perfection and left this world right in this holy
place, Pandarpur.” (CC, Madhya 9.300)

And the Lord answered that Shankararanya had been His brother, and Jagannatha Mishra and Shachi His father and mother.
Vishvarupa left His body at Pandharpur (Sholapur district, Maharashtra) in 1509 (1431 Shaka). <!-- F98 F99 F100 F101 F21 Q6 -->

The courtyard of Shachi and Jagannatha, where the infant Vishvambhara danced, was sung of by Vasu Ghosha in his padas.
After the Lord's departure the house became a place of pilgrimage. Narottama Dasa Thakura came to its threshold and fell at the door
in tears, and inside he met Shuklambara Brahmachari. Shyamananda (then still Duhkhi Krishnadasa) asked the people of
Navadvipa where the old house of Jagannatha Mishra was, and found there the aged Ishana. Later Ramachandra Kaviraja
came as well and received the blessings of the same Ishana. <!-- F103 F104 F105 F106 -->

When the Lord's birthplace was rediscovered at the end of the nineteenth century, Jagannatha Dasa Babaji proposed that
Deities of Jagannatha and Shachi be installed there in one temple, and Mahaprabhu with Lakshmipriya and Vishnupriya in
another. And on 13 June 1934,[^11] while the foundations of the new temple at the Yogapitha were being dug, a
four-armed Deity of Vishnu — Adhokshaja — was taken from the earth. It was He whom Jagannatha Mishra had worshipped in
his home. <!-- F107 F108 -->

## The Father of the Lord
Who, then, was this brahmana from Shrihatta? Krishnadasa Kaviraja answers:

“The king of Vraja, Nanda Maharaja, has now become the father of Sri Chaitanya, and Yashoda, the queen of Vraja, has
become His mother, Sri Shachidevi.” (CC, Adi 17.294)

“Now the son of Nanda Maharaja is Sri Chaitanya, and Baladeva is Sri Chaitanya's brother, Nityananda.” (CC, Adi 17.295)

Kavi Karnapura says the same:

“Yashoda and Nanda, the king of Vraja, who once in Vrindavana were treasuries of the rasa of love, became Shachi and
Jagannatha Purandara — of this there is no doubt. Into them also entered the gods Aditi and Kashyapa, Kaushalya and
Dasharatha, and Prishni and her husband.” (GGD 37–38)

Thus in the Lord's father were united all who in earlier descents had been the father of God: Kashyapa, Dasharatha,
Sutapa and Vasudeva.[^12] <!-- F10 F11 F12 Q2 Q3 -->

Jagannatha and Shachi are eternal, ever-free associates of Krishna. Their hearts and bodies are made of pure goodness,
shuddha-sattva, which is also called “vasudeva”: in it Krishna manifests Himself, and therefore He is called Vasudeva.
This brahmana couple is the foremost shelter of transcendental parental love. Vrindavana Dasa Thakura says of
Jagannatha:

“Magnanimous is his character; he is the very summit of brahminical virtue; there is nothing to which he could be
compared.” (CB, Adi 2.137)

And Vaishnavas glorify the Lord as Vishvambhara — the son of Jagannatha Mishra and Shachidevi, ever beloved of His
devotees. <!-- F13 F14 F16 Q4 I1 Q10 -->
