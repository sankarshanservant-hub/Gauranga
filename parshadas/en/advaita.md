# Sri Advaita Acharya
*Шри Адвайта Ачарья · @advaita*

## Maha-Vishnu in the Form of a Devotee
The lives of Mahaprabhu's companions almost always tell of parshadas — eternal servants of the Lord who descend into the
world with Him, or at another time, to take part in His lilas. Sri Advaita Acharya has to be read differently. He
conducts Himself as one of the companions, but in truth He is Maha-Vishnu Himself, the purusha-avatara by whose will the
universes arise, come in the form of a devotee. So the Gosvamis of Vrindavana teach, and so says Svarupa Damodara in the
verses with which Kaviraja Gosvami opens the “Chaitanya-charitamrita”:

“Lord Advaitacharya is the incarnation of Maha-Vishnu, who creates this whole world by means of His external energy,
Maha-Maya.” (CC, Adi 1.12)

The next verse explains His name as well: He is called Advaita, “non-different,” because He is non-different from Hari,
the Supreme Lord, and Acharya because He teaches the sacred path of bhakti; He is the Supreme Himself, come as His own
devotee (CC, Adi 1.13). Therefore all that is told below — His “not knowing,” His “tests,” His discipleship, His disputes
and punishments — is not the story of an ascetic rising towards God, but the lila of the Lord Himself, who has taken on
the role of a servant. <!-- F1 F2 Q4 I1 -->

Srila Bhaktivinoda Thakura explains how creation takes place. Maya acts in two ways, and Maha-Vishnu as the creator
appears in two forms: as the efficient cause, nimitta-karana, in the form of Vishnu; and in the pradhana, the primeval
ground of matter, in the form of Rudra — and this second identity of His is Advaita[^1]. And Srila Bhaktisiddhanta
Sarasvati, commenting on the verse of the “Charitamrita” that Advaita bestows good upon the whole world (Adi 6.12),
writes: Advaita is Maha-Vishnu, the supreme teacher of the world and the root of all good; whoever cannot understand
that everything that comes from Him — even what seems tangled and hard in this world — is pure, eternal and complete
good, deprives himself of devotion to Krishna. <!-- F4 F5 -->

In the Pancha-tattva Sri Chaitanya is the bhakta-rupa, the Lord in the form of a devotee; Nityananda is the
bhakta-svarupa, His manifestation as a devotee; and Advaita is the bhakta-avatara, the descent of God as a devotee. All
three are Prabhus, vishnu-tattva, and therefore tulasi is offered at Their feet, as at the feet of Vishnu. Srila
Bhaktisiddhanta explains: the purusha-avatara Maha-Vishnu, accepting the mood of a devotee, appears in the world as
Advaita. Krishnadasa Kaviraja speaks of the Three Prabhus thus:

“One of Them is Mahaprabhu, and the other two are Prabhus. These two Prabhus serve the lotus feet of Mahaprabhu.” (CC,
Adi 7.14)[^2] <!-- F6 Q9 -->

Sri Chaitanya is the body of the movement of bhakti, and Nityananda and Advaita are its limbs; Nityananda, non-different
from Baladeva, Sankarshana-Vishnu, and Advaita, “Acharya Gosvami,” are the two “angas” of Sri Chaitanya, two parts of
His body. In the image of the tree of devotion with which Kaviraja Gosvami describes the Lord's companions, Mahaprabhu
Himself is the main trunk, and Advaita and Nityananda are two secondary trunks:

“Above, the trunk divides into two great branches. One branch is Sri Advaita, and the other branch is Nityananda.” (CC,
Adi 9.21)

“I offer obeisances to Advaitachandra, the second extension of the trunk of the eternal tree of Sri Chaitanya
Mahaprabhu, and to all the branches and twigs — His countless followers.” (CC, Adi 12.3) <!-- F7 Q11 -->

Vrindavana Dasa Thakura, opening his account of Navadvipa on the eve of the Lord's appearance, places Advaita before all
its Vaishnavas:

“In Navadvipa at that time lived Advaita Acharya, the greatest Vaishnava, renowned throughout the world.” (CB, Adi 2.78)

“He was a venerable spiritual teacher, equal to Shiva himself in explaining the science of devotion to Sri Krishna, and
the essence of true knowledge and renunciation.” (CB, Adi 2.79) <!-- F8 Q5 -->

And Mahaprabhu Himself, telling Vallabha Bhatta in His humility that He knows nothing of devotion, names those whose
company purified His heart — and Advaita first:

“But I was fortunate to have the company of Advaitacharya — the Supreme Himself. Because of this My mind was purified.”
(CC, Antya 7.17)

“He has no equal in knowledge of the scriptures on devotion to Krishna. Therefore He is called Advaita Acharya, the
incomparable Teacher.” (CC, Antya 7.18)

“By His mercy even an eater of flesh can attain Krishna-bhakti. Can anyone fully understand or describe the greatness
of His loving service to Krishna?” (CC, Antya 7.19) <!-- F9 Q14 -->

Narottama Dasa Thakura, in a song addressed to Sitapati, “the husband of Sita,” begs Advaita for mercy: without it one
cannot attain service either to Chaitanya or to Nityananda. But the same truth has another side. The
“Chaitanya-bhagavata” says:

“One who glorifies Advaita Acharya as the foremost of Vaishnavas is a true Vaishnava. In every birth he will be bound
to Krishna.” (CB, Madhya 10.162)

And Srila Bhaktisiddhanta explains in the “Gaudiya-bhashya”: whoever sees in Advaita the best of Vaishnavas is a
Vaishnava; but whoever considers Him the vishaya, the object of love — that is, Krishna Himself — and Sri Chaitanya His
ashraya, His loving servant, will not reach the feet of Krishna. Advaita is non-different from Hari, but in His lila He
is the servant of Gaura, and wishes to be known precisely so. <!-- F10 F11 I13 -->

## Sadashiva
Speaking of who Advaita is in the lilas of Krishna, the “Gaura-ganoddesha-dipika” (76–80) calls Him Sadashiva — a
manifestation of the Lord in Vraja, non-different from Chaitanya. Kavi Karnapura also cites a tradition from the
“Shiva-tantra,” told by Bhairava. Once in the month of Karttika, at the festival of lamps, the Dipa-yatra,
Shankara-deva, Bhairava's guru, saw Krishna and Balarama dancing among the cowherd boys, and himself longed to become a
cowherd boy — and whirled into their dance. So by Krishna's mercy Sadashiva in Vraja is twofold: he is both Shiva
himself and a cowherd boy dancing with Krishna. Lochana Dasa in the “Chaitanya-mangala” says the same briefly: Shiva came
with the Lord — as Advaita[^3]. <!-- F3 Q3 -->

## Names
His earlier name was Sri Kamalaksha Veda-panchanana; according to the “Gaudiya-vaishnava-abhidhana,” Kamalaksha, or
Kamalakanta, Veda-panchanana. <!-- F12 -->

The “Charitamrita” explains each of His names. He is Mangala, “auspiciousness,” for He bestows good upon the whole world
(Adi 6.12). He is Advaita:

“Sri Advaita is a manifestation of Maha-Vishnu and the source of all divine qualities. He is called Advaita because He
is in every way equal to the Supreme.” (CC, Adi 6.25)

He is Acharya, for He is wholly engaged in preaching bhakti and has become the teacher of all devotees; the two names
together — Advaita Acharya — make up His full name (Adi 6.28–29). And He is called Kamalaksha, “Lotus-eyed,” because He
is a part of the body of the lotus-eyed Lord:

“Because He is an expansion, or a part of the body, of the lotus-eyed Lord, all also call Him Kamalaksha (Lotus-eyed).”
(CC, Adi 6.30)[^4] <!-- F13 -->

Mahaprabhu affectionately called Him “Nara”[^5]. He is also called “Acharya Gosvami,” and the Lord Himself in the
“Charitamrita” calls Him “Acharya Gosai” (Antya 12.70); Narottama in his song calls Him Sitapati. <!-- F14 F15 -->

## Appearance in Shrihatta
Advaita's father was Kuvera Pandita, a Varendra brahmana — according to the “Gaura-ganoddesha-dipika” (91), Kubera, the
friend of Mahadeva and leader of the guhyakas. His mother was named Nabha Devi[^6]. The “Bhakti-ratnakara” (5.2041–2043)
tells of them: Kuvera, a worshipper of Nrisimha, lived in Navagrama near Sylhet; fortune attended him on the path of
bhakti, and he knew nothing but the feet of Krishna; and his faithful wife Nabha Devi is worthy of the worship of the
whole world — for she became the mother of Advaita. <!-- F16 F17 F18 Q6 -->

Advaita's parents lived for a time in Shantipura on the Ganga, and there they saw a prophetic dream. A wondrous person
was asking another to take birth in the world to relieve the sufferings of the people of the age of Kali, and said that
He Himself would come later. Having received this sign, the couple returned to their home in Navagrama (Navegrama) in
Shrihatta (Sylhet), and there a son was born to them[^7]. <!-- F21 F22 -->

He appeared on the seventh lunar day of the bright fortnight of the month of Magha — on Maghi shukla-saptami, which has
ever since been called Advaita-saptami. In our calendar (Sri Chaitanya Saraswat Math) this day — Gaura-saptami of the
month of Madhava — is marked as “The Appearance of the Avatara of Maha-Vishnu, Sri Advaita Acharya.” A song of
Ghanashyama in the “Bhakti-ratnakara” (12.1759–1762) celebrates the day: the “moon” of Advaita rose from the womb of
Nabha Devi; Kuvera gave gifts to brahmanas and the poor, stole into the birth chamber and rejoiced to see his son's
face; the villagers wondered by what merit such a son was granted to an old man, and the gods, unseen by men, showered
the house with flowers. <!-- F19 F20 -->

According to the “Gaudiya-vaishnava-abhidhana,” He appeared in 1434. He was fifty-two years older than Mahaprabhu — when
the Lord appeared, Advaita was fifty-two — and seventeen years older than Haridasa Thakura (who, according to the
“Advaita-vilasa,” was born in 1451). The same “Abhidhana” says that He remained on earth until 1559 and departed at the
age of one hundred and twenty-five, and the “Advaita-vilasa,” that He stayed in the world “a century and a
quarter”[^8]. From Shrihatta He moved to Shantipura on the Bhagirathi, not far from Navadvipa. <!-- F23 F24 -->

## Study and Pilgrimage
According to the “Prema-vilasa,” Advaita studied the Vedas and other scriptures under Shantacharya in Phullabati — one
of the quarters of Shantipura — and received from him the title “acharya”[^9]. When Kuvera and Nabha Devi left this
world, He went to Gaya — as if to perform the funeral rites for them — and from there set out to wander through the holy
places of India. <!-- F25 F26 -->

In Mithila, the “Advaita-vilasa” relates, He met Vidyapati. The great Maithili poet was by then very old; in a sweet
voice he recited his verses about Krishna, and the young pilgrim and the old poet glorified each other. The songs of
Vidyapati, a mahajana, were later sung before Mahaprabhu in Puri. <!-- F27 -->

In Vraja Advaita stayed for a long time beneath a banyan tree in Vrindavana. The place is called Advaita-vata: whoever
sees it is purified and attains the love of the gopis. There He became absorbed in the worship of Krishna and in
meditation understood that Krishna was about to appear in Navadvipa. From Vrindavana He returned to Shantipura, staying a
few days in Navadvipa on the way, and the people of Shantipura, who had long pined without Him, were overjoyed.
<!-- F28 F29 -->

## Disciple of Madhavendra Puri
Advaita's guru was Sri Madhavendra Puri — he whose disciple Ishvara Puri gave initiation to the Lord Himself, and whom
Mahaprabhu called the original source of ecstatic love for Krishna. How and when they met is told in different
ways[^10]. The “Chaitanya-bhagavata” says that long before the Lord's appearance Madhavendra, in his wanderings, came to
Advaita's dwelling in Shantipura: Advaita saw in him all the signs of a true Vaishnava and bowed down before him, and
Madhavendra embraced Him and bathed Him in tears of love. They became so absorbed in talk of Krishna that they forgot
their bodies, and Advaita accepted instruction from him (CB, Antya 4.433–440). The “Charitamrita” names the occasion as
well: Madhavendra, at the command of Gopala, was going to Puri for sandalwood, and on his way through Bengal stopped at
Advaita's house:

“Reaching Shantipura, he visited the house of Advaitacharya. Seeing the divine love of Madhavendra Puri, He rejoiced.”
(CC, Madhya 4.110)

“He asked Madhavendra Puri for initiation into the mantra. Madhavendra gave Him initiation and went on further south.”
(CC, Madhya 4.111)

From Shantipura Madhavendra went south, to Remuna. <!-- F30 Q2 -->

Advaita is Maha-Vishnu Himself; why should He need a guru? He accepted initiation to show conditioned souls that one
cannot do without a guru — just as Mahaprabhu Himself accepts initiation from Ishvara Puri. Vrindavana Dasa says that
Advaita only played the role of Madhavendra's disciple[^11]. <!-- F31 I11 -->

Madhavendra's disciples in the line of the Madhva-sampradaya are Ishvara Puri, Advaita, Paramananda Puri, Brahmananda
Puri, Ranga Puri, Pundarika Vidyanidhi, Raghupati Upadhyaya and others; Srila Bhaktisiddhanta names Ishvara Puri,
Nityananda and Advaita as the chief among them. According to the “Gaura-ganoddesha-dipika,” Ishvara Puri received from
Madhavendra the fruit of madhurya, love in the conjugal mood, and Advaita the fruits of dasya and sakhya, service and
friendship. It is said that Pundarika Vidyanidhi received initiation from Madhavendra at the same time as Advaita. The
“Chaitanya-bhagavata” sees in this discipleship the highest praise of Madhavendra himself:

“What more can I say of the devotion of one who became the spiritual teacher of Sri Advaita Acharya?” (CB, Adi 9.157)

And Srila Bhaktivinoda Thakura explains why Mahaprabhu always honoured Advaita as an elder and a teacher: Advaita is the
godbrother of Ishvara Puri, the Lord's guru. <!-- F33 F34 F35 F36 -->

## Sita and Shri; Two Houses
Advaita married two daughters of Nrisimha Baduri (Bhaduri), a devoted brahmana — Shri and Sita. Vishnu has three
shaktis — Shri, Bhu and Nila; without His shaktis Advaita too would not have revealed Himself in fullness. According to
the “Gaura-ganoddesha-dipika,” Sita is Yogamaya, and Shri is her prakasha, her manifestation[^12]. The wedding with Sita
took place in Phulia-nagara, and after it Advaita settled in Shantipura, in Nadia[^13]. <!-- F37 F38 F39 -->

Advaita had two houses: in Shantipura and in Navadvipa-Mayapura, not far from the house of Srivasa. Navadvipa in those
days was called “the Oxford of India,” and in the Navadvipa house Advaita kept a tol — a Sanskrit school — and studied
the scriptures deeply. He lived there from time to time and would talk of Krishna until dawn with the devotees headed by
Srivasa. Seeing around Him people bereft of devotion to Vishnu, He taught the “Bhagavad-gita” and the
“Srimad-Bhagavatam” and revealed their essence as devotional service. <!-- F40 F41 -->

## The Call
Having returned to Bengal, Advaita became the head of its Vaishnavas. Around Him gathered Srivasa, Chandrashekhara
Acharya, Gangadasa and others; Srivasa's brothers came to His house to hear the “Bhagavatam” and sing sankirtana. And
Navadvipa all around was far from God. The learned were hostile to bhakti; they took for religion karma-yoga, rites
performed for wealth; they taught the “Gita” and the “Bhagavatam” without a word about devotion; they were proud of
birth and learning — and nowhere was the name of Krishna to be heard. The Vaishnavas headed by Advaita grieved.
<!-- F42 F43 -->

The people of the age of Kali were indifferent to their only good — to the sankirtana of Krishna's name, and Advaita's
heart overflowed with compassion. He wished to awaken the whole people to harinama, and He reasoned: a partial avatara
would not be enough here — the world would be saved only if Hari Himself descended. The “Charitamrita” conveys His
thoughts:

“My name Advaita will gain its true meaning only when I can induce Krishna Himself to perform sankirtana.” (CC, Adi
3.101) <!-- F44 -->

And He began to call the Lord. He worshipped Krishna with Ganga water and tulasi leaves — tulasi-manjaris — often
fasted and gathered the Vaishnavas for common prayer; and He called loudly, with a “roar of love,” prema-hunkara:

“Thinking of the lotus feet of Krishna, He began to offer Him tulasi-manjaris with Ganga water.” (CC, Adi 3.107)

“And as He did so He called Krishna loudly, and this became the cause of Mahaprabhu's descent into this world.” (CC,
Adi 3.108)

“The main cause of Sri Chaitanya's descent is the plea of Advaita. The Lord, the Protector of dharma, comes only by the
desire of His devotee.” (CC, Adi 3.109)

Vrindavana Dasa says that this call was heard in Vaikuntha:

“He called out loudly to Sri Krishna. The sound of Advaita Acharya's voice pierced the coverings of the universe and was
heard in Vaikuntha.” (CB, Adi 2.82)

“Hearing the loving call of Sri Advaita, Lord Krishna appeared in person, for He is always captivated by the love of
His devotees.” (CB, Adi 2.83) <!-- F45 Q5 -->

The “Bhakti-ratnakara” (12.3753–3764) sings of Him: Advaita's body is a treasury of the nectar of bhakti, non-different
from the body of Chaitanya Himself; He brought Chaitanya down by His roar and then glorified Him everywhere; Advaita is
non-different from Gauranga, and His consort Sita is the mother of the universe; whoever takes shelter of His feet will
attain love for Gaura. So the cause of Gaura's descent was Advaita's compassion for the people of Kali, His “audacious”
call and the prayer of Haridasa, of which more below — and the Lord Himself confirmed this more than once.
<!-- F46 I2 -->

The loud sankirtana angered the godless. They objected and threatened; they mocked: “The Lord sleeps through the four
months of the rains — wake Him with your shouting, and there will be a great offence, and famine will come!”; they said
that some brahmanas sing only to fill their bellies. When the devotees lost heart, Advaita encouraged them: the Lord
will hear and will come. <!-- F47 -->

## Advaita and Haridasa
Long before the Lord's appearance Haridasa — by tradition, an avatara of Brahma — settled in Phulia, not far from
Advaita's house in Shantipura. He lived in a cave and from time to time came to Advaita in Shantipura; so it was also
after he had preached in Chandpur, in the house of Govardhana and Hiranya Majumdar. Haridasa was drawn by Advaita's
talks, and Advaita recognised in him a bhajanandi who chants the holy name without cease. He built him a cave-like hut,
explained the “Gita” to him every day in the spirit of steadfast devotion, nishtha-bhakti, and sent him prasada from
His house. <!-- F48 F49 -->

Haridasa was born in a family of yavanas, yet Advaita honoured him as no one else would have dared. At the yearly
shraddha for His father He offered a meal to the Lord, and the first dish, the shraddha-patra, which is given to the
most honoured brahmana, He gave to Haridasa. The mahaprasada of the shraddha is presented to the honoured guest, and the
“Charitamrita” says that Advaita so revered Haridasa that He let him be the first to taste the remnants of the memorial
offerings in honour of His father (Adi 10.44). Haridasa was afraid, and Advaita answered:

“‘Do not fear, dear Haridasa. I shall certainly act in full accordance with the injunctions of the scriptures.’” (CC,
Antya 3.219)

“‘You are so great that to feed you is equal to feeding ten million venerable brahmanas.’ Saying this, Advaita Acharya
offered him to taste the offering He had presented to His ancestors.” (CC, Antya 3.220)[^14]

From then on Haridasa dined at Advaita's house every day. For Advaita faithfulness to scripture stands above caution
about caste: a Vaishnava is worthy of worship whatever family he is born in. <!-- F50 Q10 I3 -->

So the two of them together begged the Lord to come. Advaita worshipped Him with Ganga water and tulasi — in Shantipura
and in Navadvipa-Mayapura — while Haridasa in his hut chanted the holy name and called on the Lord to appear; it is said
that their prayer brought the Lord into the world in the form of Gauranga. The “Charitamrita” says:

“Advaita Acharya constantly pondered how liberation could be given to all the fallen souls of this world. He thought:
‘How can this whole world be saved, in which it is so hard to meet unselfish devotion to the Lord?’” (CC, Antya 3.221)

“Wishing to call into this world Sri Krishna, the Master of all creation, Advaita Acharya took a vow to worship Him
daily with Ganga water and tulasi leaves.” (CC, Antya 3.222)

“In the same way Haridasa Thakura chanted the holy name of God in solitude, wishing to induce Krishna to descend into
this world.” (CC, Antya 3.223)

“The holy devotion of these two Vaishnavas became the cause of the descent of Sri Chaitanya, who gave liberation to the
whole world, calling all to sing the holy name of God and so attain divine love for Krishna.” (CC, Antya 3.224)

Later Haridasa moved to Navadvipa, wishing to live among pure devotees like Advaita. <!-- F51 F52 -->

## The Lord's Appearance
Before coming Himself, Krishna sends into the world those whom He will honour as elders:

“First He sent to Earth all those close to Him and His elders. I shall list them briefly — to describe them all in
detail is simply impossible.” (CC, Adi 13.53)

“Sri Shachidevi and Jagannatha Mishra, Sri Madhavendra Puri, Keshava Bharati, Ishvara Puri, Advaita Acharya, Srivasa
Pandita, Acharyaratna, Vidyanidhi and Thakura Haridasa.” (CC, Adi 13.54)

The Three Prabhus descend together with Their eternal companions — with Srivasa, Haridasa and others. <!-- F53 Q15 -->

When Shachi was in the sixth month of carrying the Lord, Advaita came to Jagannatha Mishra, left him, and, reciting
hymns and bowing, walked around Shachi — He was worshipping the Child in her womb. The astonished couple asked what this
meant, but Advaita only answered that in due time they would know; and they did not understand. <!-- F54 -->

On the evening of the Lord's birth, in Shantipura, Advaita together with Haridasa suddenly broke into a dance of joy,
and no one knew why. It was the day of a lunar eclipse, and on that occasion many gifts were given away; about what had
happened both kept silent[^15]. <!-- F55 -->

Advaita sent Sita in a palanquin to the house of Jagannatha Mishra with a casket of precious cloth and ornaments, and
with her husband's permission she came from Shantipura with gifts:

“Once the universally honoured Sita Thakurani, the wife of Sri Advaita Acharya, came with Advaita's consent to see the
wondrous child, bringing various gifts with her.” (CC, Adi 13.111)

“Touching the baby's head with fresh grass and rice, she blessed Him with the words: ‘May both brothers live long!’ And
to protect Him from witches and evil spirits she gave Him the name Nimai.” (CC, Adi 13.117)[^16] <!-- F56 Q7 -->

Sita often came from Shantipura to teach Shachi how to care for the Infant, and at the festival of His birthday she was
the first allowed to offer the Child clothing. All the devotees together with Advaita and Sita prayed for the long life
of the Child. Sita is worthy of worship equally with Shachi: her motherly love, vatsalya, for Gaura is of the same kind.
