# Sri Advaita Acharya
*Шри Адвайта Ачарья · @advaita*

## Maha-Vishnu in the Form of a Devotee
The lives of Mahaprabhu's companions almost always tell of parshadas — eternal servants of the Lord who descend into the
world with Him, or at another time, to take part in His lilas. Sri Advaita Acharya has to be read differently. He
conducts Himself as one of the companions, but in truth He is Maha-Vishnu Himself, the purusha-avatara by whose will the
universes arise, come in the form of a devotee. So the Gosvamis of Vrindavana teach, and so says Svarupa Damodara in the
verses with which Kaviraja Gosvami opens the “Chaitanya-charitamrita”:

“Lord Advaitacharya is the incarnation of Maha-Vishnu, who creates this whole world by means of His external energy,
Maha-Maya.” (CC, Adi 1.12)

The next verse explains His name as well: He is called Advaita, “non-different,” because He is non-different from Hari,
the Supreme Lord, and Acharya because He teaches the sacred path of bhakti; He is the Supreme Himself, come as His own
devotee (CC, Adi 1.13). Therefore all that is told below — His “not knowing,” His “tests,” His discipleship, His disputes
and punishments — is not the story of an ascetic rising towards God, but the lila of the Lord Himself, who has taken on
the role of a servant. <!-- F1 F2 Q4 I1 -->

Srila Bhaktivinoda Thakura explains how creation takes place. Maya acts in two ways, and Maha-Vishnu as the creator
appears in two forms: as the efficient cause, nimitta-karana, in the form of Vishnu; and in the pradhana, the primeval
ground of matter, in the form of Rudra — and this second identity of His is Advaita[^1]. And Srila Bhaktisiddhanta
Sarasvati, commenting on the verse of the “Charitamrita” that Advaita bestows good upon the whole world (Adi 6.12),
writes: Advaita is Maha-Vishnu, the supreme teacher of the world and the root of all good; whoever cannot understand
that everything that comes from Him — even what seems tangled and hard in this world — is pure, eternal and complete
good, deprives himself of devotion to Krishna. <!-- F4 F5 -->

In the Pancha-tattva Sri Chaitanya is the bhakta-rupa, the Lord in the form of a devotee; Nityananda is the
bhakta-svarupa, His manifestation as a devotee; and Advaita is the bhakta-avatara, the descent of God as a devotee. All
three are Prabhus, vishnu-tattva, and therefore tulasi is offered at Their feet, as at the feet of Vishnu. Srila
Bhaktisiddhanta explains: the purusha-avatara Maha-Vishnu, accepting the mood of a devotee, appears in the world as
Advaita. Krishnadasa Kaviraja speaks of the Three Prabhus thus:

“One of Them is Mahaprabhu, and the other two are Prabhus. These two Prabhus serve the lotus feet of Mahaprabhu.” (CC,
Adi 7.14)[^2] <!-- F6 Q9 -->

Sri Chaitanya is the body of the movement of bhakti, and Nityananda and Advaita are its limbs; Nityananda, non-different
from Baladeva, Sankarshana-Vishnu, and Advaita, “Acharya Gosvami,” are the two “angas” of Sri Chaitanya, two parts of
His body. In the image of the tree of devotion with which Kaviraja Gosvami describes the Lord's companions, Mahaprabhu
Himself is the main trunk, and Advaita and Nityananda are two secondary trunks:

“Above, the trunk divides into two great branches. One branch is Sri Advaita, and the other branch is Nityananda.” (CC,
Adi 9.21)

“I offer obeisances to Advaitachandra, the second extension of the trunk of the eternal tree of Sri Chaitanya
Mahaprabhu, and to all the branches and twigs — His countless followers.” (CC, Adi 12.3) <!-- F7 Q11 -->

Vrindavana Dasa Thakura, opening his account of Navadvipa on the eve of the Lord's appearance, places Advaita before all
its Vaishnavas:

“In Navadvipa at that time lived Advaita Acharya, the greatest Vaishnava, renowned throughout the world.” (CB, Adi 2.78)

“He was a venerable spiritual teacher, equal to Shiva himself in explaining the science of devotion to Sri Krishna, and
the essence of true knowledge and renunciation.” (CB, Adi 2.79) <!-- F8 Q5 -->

And Mahaprabhu Himself, telling Vallabha Bhatta in His humility that He knows nothing of devotion, names those whose
company purified His heart — and Advaita first:

“But I was fortunate to have the company of Advaitacharya — the Supreme Himself. Because of this My mind was purified.”
(CC, Antya 7.17)

“He has no equal in knowledge of the scriptures on devotion to Krishna. Therefore He is called Advaita Acharya, the
incomparable Teacher.” (CC, Antya 7.18)

“By His mercy even an eater of flesh can attain Krishna-bhakti. Can anyone fully understand or describe the greatness
of His loving service to Krishna?” (CC, Antya 7.19) <!-- F9 Q14 -->

Narottama Dasa Thakura, in a song addressed to Sitapati, “the husband of Sita,” begs Advaita for mercy: without it one
cannot attain service either to Chaitanya or to Nityananda. But the same truth has another side. The
“Chaitanya-bhagavata” says:

“One who glorifies Advaita Acharya as the foremost of Vaishnavas is a true Vaishnava. In every birth he will be bound
to Krishna.” (CB, Madhya 10.162)

And Srila Bhaktisiddhanta explains in the “Gaudiya-bhashya”: whoever sees in Advaita the best of Vaishnavas is a
Vaishnava; but whoever considers Him the vishaya, the object of love — that is, Krishna Himself — and Sri Chaitanya His
ashraya, His loving servant, will not reach the feet of Krishna. Advaita is non-different from Hari, but in His lila He
is the servant of Gaura, and wishes to be known precisely so. <!-- F10 F11 I13 -->

## Sadashiva
Speaking of who Advaita is in the lilas of Krishna, the “Gaura-ganoddesha-dipika” (76–80) calls Him Sadashiva — a
manifestation of the Lord in Vraja, non-different from Chaitanya. Kavi Karnapura also cites a tradition from the
“Shiva-tantra,” told by Bhairava. Once in the month of Karttika, at the festival of lamps, the Dipa-yatra,
Shankara-deva, Bhairava's guru, saw Krishna and Balarama dancing among the cowherd boys, and himself longed to become a
cowherd boy — and whirled into their dance. So by Krishna's mercy Sadashiva in Vraja is twofold: he is both Shiva
himself and a cowherd boy dancing with Krishna. Lochana Dasa in the “Chaitanya-mangala” says the same briefly: Shiva came
with the Lord — as Advaita[^3]. <!-- F3 Q3 -->

## Names
His earlier name was Sri Kamalaksha Veda-panchanana; according to the “Gaudiya-vaishnava-abhidhana,” Kamalaksha, or
Kamalakanta, Veda-panchanana. <!-- F12 -->

The “Charitamrita” explains each of His names. He is Mangala, “auspiciousness,” for He bestows good upon the whole world
(Adi 6.12). He is Advaita:

“Sri Advaita is a manifestation of Maha-Vishnu and the source of all divine qualities. He is called Advaita because He
is in every way equal to the Supreme.” (CC, Adi 6.25)

He is Acharya, for He is wholly engaged in preaching bhakti and has become the teacher of all devotees; the two names
together — Advaita Acharya — make up His full name (Adi 6.28–29). And He is called Kamalaksha, “Lotus-eyed,” because He
is a part of the body of the lotus-eyed Lord:

“Because He is an expansion, or a part of the body, of the lotus-eyed Lord, all also call Him Kamalaksha (Lotus-eyed).”
(CC, Adi 6.30)[^4] <!-- F13 -->

Mahaprabhu affectionately called Him “Nara”[^5]. He is also called “Acharya Gosvami,” and the Lord Himself in the
“Charitamrita” calls Him “Acharya Gosai” (Antya 12.70); Narottama in his song calls Him Sitapati. <!-- F14 F15 -->

## Appearance in Shrihatta
Advaita's father was Kuvera Pandita, a Varendra brahmana — according to the “Gaura-ganoddesha-dipika” (91), Kubera, the
friend of Mahadeva and leader of the guhyakas. His mother was named Nabha Devi[^6]. The “Bhakti-ratnakara” (5.2041–2043)
tells of them: Kuvera, a worshipper of Nrisimha, lived in Navagrama near Sylhet; fortune attended him on the path of
bhakti, and he knew nothing but the feet of Krishna; and his faithful wife Nabha Devi is worthy of the worship of the
whole world — for she became the mother of Advaita. <!-- F16 F17 F18 Q6 -->

Advaita's parents lived for a time in Shantipura on the Ganga, and there they saw a prophetic dream. A wondrous person
was asking another to take birth in the world to relieve the sufferings of the people of the age of Kali, and said that
He Himself would come later. Having received this sign, the couple returned to their home in Navagrama (Navegrama) in
Shrihatta (Sylhet), and there a son was born to them[^7]. <!-- F21 F22 -->

He appeared on the seventh lunar day of the bright fortnight of the month of Magha — on Maghi shukla-saptami, which has
ever since been called Advaita-saptami. In our calendar (Sri Chaitanya Saraswat Math) this day — Gaura-saptami of the
month of Madhava — is marked as “The Appearance of the Avatara of Maha-Vishnu, Sri Advaita Acharya.” A song of
Ghanashyama in the “Bhakti-ratnakara” (12.1759–1762) celebrates the day: the “moon” of Advaita rose from the womb of
Nabha Devi; Kuvera gave gifts to brahmanas and the poor, stole into the birth chamber and rejoiced to see his son's
face; the villagers wondered by what merit such a son was granted to an old man, and the gods, unseen by men, showered
the house with flowers. <!-- F19 F20 -->

According to the “Gaudiya-vaishnava-abhidhana,” He appeared in 1434. He was fifty-two years older than Mahaprabhu — when
the Lord appeared, Advaita was fifty-two — and seventeen years older than Haridasa Thakura (who, according to the
“Advaita-vilasa,” was born in 1451). The same “Abhidhana” says that He remained on earth until 1559 and departed at the
age of one hundred and twenty-five, and the “Advaita-vilasa,” that He stayed in the world “a century and a
quarter”[^8]. From Shrihatta He moved to Shantipura on the Bhagirathi, not far from Navadvipa. <!-- F23 F24 -->

## Study and Pilgrimage
According to the “Prema-vilasa,” Advaita studied the Vedas and other scriptures under Shantacharya in Phullabati — one
of the quarters of Shantipura — and received from him the title “acharya”[^9]. When Kuvera and Nabha Devi left this
world, He went to Gaya on the pretext of performing
the funeral rites, and from there set out to wander through the holy
places of India. <!-- F25 F26 -->

In Mithila, the “Advaita-vilasa” relates, He met Vidyapati. The great Maithili poet was by then very old; in a sweet
voice he recited his verses about Krishna, and the young pilgrim and the old poet glorified each other. The songs of
Vidyapati, a mahajana, were later sung before Mahaprabhu in Puri. <!-- F27 -->

In Vraja Advaita stayed for a long time beneath a banyan tree in Vrindavana. The place is called Advaita-vata: whoever
sees it is purified and attains the love of the gopis. There He became absorbed in the worship of Krishna and in
meditation understood that Krishna was about to appear in Navadvipa. From Vrindavana He returned to Shantipura, staying a
few days in Navadvipa on the way, and the people of Shantipura, who had long pined without Him, were overjoyed.
<!-- F28 F29 -->

## Disciple of Madhavendra Puri
Advaita's guru was Sri Madhavendra Puri — he whose disciple Ishvara Puri gave initiation to the Lord Himself, and whom
Mahaprabhu called the original source of ecstatic love for Krishna. How and when they met is told in different
ways[^10]. The “Chaitanya-bhagavata” says that long before the Lord's appearance Madhavendra, in his wanderings, came to
Advaita's dwelling in Shantipura: Advaita saw in him all the signs of a true Vaishnava and bowed down before him, and
Madhavendra embraced Him and bathed Him in tears of love. They became so absorbed in talk of Krishna that they forgot
their bodies, and Advaita accepted instruction from him (CB, Antya 4.433–440). The “Charitamrita” names the occasion as
well: Madhavendra, at the command of Gopala, was going to Puri for sandalwood, and on his way through Bengal stopped at
Advaita's house:

“Reaching Shantipura, he visited the house of Advaitacharya. Seeing the divine love of Madhavendra Puri, He rejoiced.”
(CC, Madhya 4.110)

“He asked Madhavendra Puri for initiation into the mantra. Madhavendra gave Him initiation and went on further south.”
(CC, Madhya 4.111)

From Shantipura Madhavendra went south, to Remuna. <!-- F30 Q2 -->

Advaita is Maha-Vishnu Himself; why should He need a guru? He accepted initiation to show conditioned souls that one
cannot do without a guru — just as Mahaprabhu Himself accepts initiation from Ishvara Puri. Vrindavana Dasa says that
Advaita only played the role of Madhavendra's disciple[^11]. <!-- F31 F32 I11 -->

Madhavendra's disciples in the line of the Madhva-sampradaya are Ishvara Puri, Advaita, Paramananda Puri, Brahmananda
Puri, Ranga Puri, Pundarika Vidyanidhi, Raghupati Upadhyaya and others; Srila Bhaktisiddhanta names Ishvara Puri,
Nityananda and Advaita as the chief among them. According to the “Gaura-ganoddesha-dipika,” Ishvara Puri received from
Madhavendra the fruit of madhurya, love in the conjugal mood, and Advaita the fruits of dasya and sakhya, service and
friendship. It is said that Pundarika Vidyanidhi received initiation from Madhavendra at the same time as Advaita. The
“Chaitanya-bhagavata” sees in this discipleship the highest praise of Madhavendra himself:

“What more can I say of the devotion of one who became the spiritual teacher of Sri Advaita Acharya?” (CB, Adi 9.157)

And Srila Bhaktivinoda Thakura explains why Mahaprabhu always honoured Advaita as an elder and a teacher: Advaita is the
godbrother of Ishvara Puri, the Lord's guru. <!-- F33 F34 F35 F36 -->

## Sita and Shri; Two Houses
Advaita married two daughters of Nrisimha Baduri (Bhaduri), a devoted brahmana — Shri and Sita. Vishnu has three
shaktis — Shri, Bhu and Nila; without His shaktis Advaita too would not have revealed Himself in fullness. According to
the “Gaura-ganoddesha-dipika,” Sita is Yogamaya, and Shri is her prakasha, her manifestation[^12]. The wedding with Sita
took place in Phulia-nagara, and after it Advaita settled in Shantipura, in Nadia[^13]. <!-- F37 F38 F39 -->

Advaita had two houses: in Shantipura and in Navadvipa-Mayapura, not far from the house of Srivasa. Navadvipa in those
days was called “the Oxford of India,” and in the Navadvipa house Advaita kept a tol — a Sanskrit school — and studied
the scriptures deeply. He lived there from time to time and would talk of Krishna until dawn with the devotees headed by
Srivasa. Seeing around Him people bereft of devotion to Vishnu, He taught the “Bhagavad-gita” and the
“Srimad-Bhagavatam” and revealed their essence as devotional service. <!-- F40 F41 -->

## The Call
Having returned to Bengal, Advaita became the head of its Vaishnavas. Around Him gathered Srivasa, Chandrashekhara
Acharya, Gangadasa and others; Srivasa's brothers came to His house to hear the “Bhagavatam” and sing sankirtana. And
Navadvipa all around was far from God. The learned were hostile to bhakti; they took for religion karma-yoga, rites
performed for wealth; they taught the “Gita” and the “Bhagavatam” without a word about devotion; they were proud of
birth and learning — and nowhere was the name of Krishna to be heard. The Vaishnavas headed by Advaita grieved.
<!-- F42 F43 -->

The people of the age of Kali were indifferent to their only good — to the sankirtana of Krishna's name, and Advaita's
heart overflowed with compassion. He wished to awaken the whole people to harinama, and He reasoned: a partial avatara
would not be enough here — the world would be saved only if Hari Himself descended. The “Charitamrita” conveys His
thoughts:

“My name Advaita will gain its true meaning only when I can induce Krishna Himself to perform sankirtana.” (CC, Adi
3.101) <!-- F44 -->

And He began to call the Lord. He worshipped Krishna with Ganga water and tulasi leaves — tulasi-manjaris — often
fasted and gathered the Vaishnavas for common prayer; and He called loudly, with a “roar of love,” prema-hunkara:

“Thinking of the lotus feet of Krishna, He began to offer Him tulasi-manjaris with Ganga water.” (CC, Adi 3.107)

“And as He did so He called Krishna loudly, and this became the cause of Mahaprabhu's descent into this world.” (CC,
Adi 3.108)

“The main cause of Sri Chaitanya's descent is the plea of Advaita. The Lord, the Protector of dharma, comes only by the
desire of His devotee.” (CC, Adi 3.109)

Vrindavana Dasa says that this call was heard in Vaikuntha:

“He called out loudly to Sri Krishna. The sound of Advaita Acharya's voice pierced the coverings of the universe and was
heard in Vaikuntha.” (CB, Adi 2.82)

“Hearing the loving call of Sri Advaita, Lord Krishna appeared in person, for He is always captivated by the love of
His devotees.” (CB, Adi 2.83) <!-- F45 Q5 -->

The “Bhakti-ratnakara” (12.3753–3764) sings of Him: Advaita's body is a treasury of the nectar of bhakti, non-different
from the body of Chaitanya Himself; He brought Chaitanya down by His roar and then glorified Him everywhere; Advaita is
non-different from Gauranga, and His consort Sita is the mother of the universe; whoever takes shelter of His feet will
attain love for Gaura. So the cause of Gaura's descent was Advaita's compassion for the people of Kali, His “audacious”
call and the prayer of Haridasa, of which more below — and the Lord Himself confirmed this more than once.
<!-- F46 I2 -->

The loud sankirtana angered the godless. They objected and threatened; they mocked: “The Lord sleeps through the four
months of the rains — wake Him with your shouting, and there will be a great offence, and famine will come!”; they said
that some brahmanas sing only to fill their bellies. When the devotees lost heart, Advaita encouraged them: the Lord
will hear and will come. <!-- F47 -->

## Advaita and Haridasa
Long before the Lord's appearance Haridasa — by tradition, an avatara of Brahma — settled in Phulia, not far from
Advaita's house in Shantipura. He lived in a cave and from time to time came to Advaita in Shantipura; so it was also
after he had preached in Chandpur, in the house of Govardhana and Hiranya Majumdar. Haridasa was drawn by Advaita's
talks, and Advaita recognised in him a bhajanandi who chants the holy name without cease. He built him a cave-like hut,
explained the “Gita” to him every day in the spirit of steadfast devotion, nishtha-bhakti, and sent him prasada from
His house. <!-- F48 F49 -->

Haridasa was born in a family of yavanas, yet Advaita honoured him as no one else would have dared. At the yearly
shraddha for His father He offered a meal to the Lord, and the first dish, the shraddha-patra, which is given to the
most honoured brahmana, He gave to Haridasa. The mahaprasada of the shraddha is presented to the honoured guest, and the
“Charitamrita” says that Advaita so revered Haridasa that He let him be the first to taste the remnants of the memorial
offerings in honour of His father (Adi 10.44). Haridasa was afraid, and Advaita answered:

“‘Do not fear, dear Haridasa. I shall certainly act in full accordance with the injunctions of the scriptures.’” (CC,
Antya 3.219)

“‘You are so great that to feed you is equal to feeding ten million venerable brahmanas.’ Saying this, Advaita Acharya
offered him to taste the offering He had presented to His ancestors.” (CC, Antya 3.220)[^14]

From then on Haridasa dined at Advaita's house every day. For Advaita faithfulness to scripture stands above caution
about caste: a Vaishnava is worthy of worship whatever family he is born in. <!-- F50 Q10 I3 -->

So the two of them together begged the Lord to come. Advaita worshipped Him with Ganga water and tulasi — in Shantipura
and in Navadvipa-Mayapura — while Haridasa in his hut chanted the holy name and called on the Lord to appear; it is said
that their prayer brought the Lord into the world in the form of Gauranga. The “Charitamrita” says:

“Advaita Acharya constantly pondered how liberation could be given to all the fallen souls of this world. He thought:
‘How can this whole world be saved, in which it is so hard to meet unselfish devotion to the Lord?’” (CC, Antya 3.221)

“Wishing to call into this world Sri Krishna, the Master of all creation, Advaita Acharya took a vow to worship Him
daily with Ganga water and tulasi leaves.” (CC, Antya 3.222)

“In the same way Haridasa Thakura chanted the holy name of God in solitude, wishing to induce Krishna to descend into
this world.” (CC, Antya 3.223)

“The holy devotion of these two Vaishnavas became the cause of the descent of Sri Chaitanya, who gave liberation to the
whole world, calling all to sing the holy name of God and so attain divine love for Krishna.” (CC, Antya 3.224)

Later Haridasa moved to Navadvipa, wishing to live among pure devotees like Advaita. <!-- F51 F52 -->

## The Lord's Appearance
Before coming Himself, Krishna sends into the world those whom He will honour as elders:

“First He sent to Earth all those close to Him and His elders. I shall list them briefly — to describe them all in
detail is simply impossible.” (CC, Adi 13.53)

“Sri Shachidevi and Jagannatha Mishra, Sri Madhavendra Puri, Keshava Bharati, Ishvara Puri, Advaita Acharya, Srivasa
Pandita, Acharyaratna, Vidyanidhi and Thakura Haridasa.” (CC, Adi 13.54)

The Three Prabhus descend together with Their eternal companions — with Srivasa, Haridasa and others. <!-- F53 Q15 -->

When Shachi was in the sixth month of carrying the Lord, Advaita came to Jagannatha Mishra, left him, and, reciting
hymns and bowing, walked around Shachi — He was worshipping the Child in her womb. The astonished couple asked what this
meant, but Advaita only answered that in due time they would know; and they did not understand. <!-- F54 -->

On the evening of the Lord's birth, in Shantipura, Advaita together with Haridasa suddenly broke into a dance of joy,
and no one knew why. It was the day of a lunar eclipse, and on that occasion many gifts were given away; about what had
happened both kept silent[^15]. <!-- F55 -->

Advaita sent Sita in a palanquin to the house of Jagannatha Mishra with a casket of precious cloth and ornaments, and
with her husband's permission she came from Shantipura with gifts:

“Once the universally honoured Sita Thakurani, the wife of Sri Advaita Acharya, came with Advaita's consent to see the
wondrous child, bringing various gifts with her.” (CC, Adi 13.111)

“Touching the baby's head with fresh grass and rice, she blessed Him with the words: ‘May both brothers live long!’ And
to protect Him from witches and evil spirits she gave Him the name Nimai.” (CC, Adi 13.117)[^16] <!-- F56 Q7 -->

Sita often came from Shantipura to teach Shachi how to care for the Infant, and at the festival of His birthday she was
the first allowed to offer the Child clothing. All the devotees together with Advaita and Sita prayed for the long life
of the Child. Sita is worthy of worship equally with Shachi: her motherly love, vatsalya, for Gaura is of the same kind.
<!-- F57 I16 -->

## Nimai and Vishvarupa
The Lord was growing up in Navadvipa, and even the devotees were not sure that Nimai was Bhagavan Himself. Advaita, for
His part, behaved as though He did not know who He was — and wished more than anyone that the learned Nimai would
become a devotee[^17]. Nimai's elder brother, Vishvarupa, studied in Advaita's tol: every day, after bathing in the
Ganga, He listened to Advaita's lectures and then Himself explained to those gathered that the essence of all scripture is
devotion to Krishna, and Advaita, rising from His puja, would embrace Him. Whole days Vishvarupa spent in Advaita's
house in talks on the scriptures of bhakti. Little Nimai used to run over to call His brother to lunch, and Advaita
could not take His eyes off His face. Many years later He recalled:

“His elder brother, Vishvarupa, always used to come to Me before and discuss the ‘Gita’ with Me.” (CB, Madhya 2.21)

“As a child Vishvambhara was exceptionally beautiful. He always came to Me to call His brother, Vishvarupa.” (CB,
Madhya 2.22)

“Enchanted by His beauty, I always blessed Him with the words: ‘May You attain bhakti.’” (CB, Madhya 2.23)
<!-- F58 F59 -->

When Vishvarupa was twelve years old and His wedding was already being prepared at home, He left and took sannyasa
with the name Shankararanya. Advaita grieved, but comforted everyone: soon Krishna Himself would appear; together with
Sita He consoled Jagannatha and Shachi and helped them bring up Nimai. In Advaita's Navadvipa house the “Bhagavatam” was
still read and kirtan sung, and everyone delighted in the voice of Mukunda Datta. <!-- F60 F61 -->

## Ishvara Puri in Advaita's House
Once Ishvara Puri, a disciple of Madhavendra, came to Navadvipa unrecognised by anyone and entered Advaita's house:

“When Ishvara Puri, by the will of providence, arrived at Advaita's house, no one could recognise him in such dress.”
(CB, Adi 11.72)

“He humbly sat down beside Advaita, who was performing puja.” (CB, Adi 11.73)

“The radiance of a Vaishnava is never hidden from another Vaishnava. Advaita looked at the guest and could not take His
eyes off him.” (CB, Adi 11.74)

“Advaita asked: ‘Who are you, dear sir? I think you are a Vaishnava sannyasi.’” (CB, Adi 11.75)

The guest answered that he was lower than a shudra. Then Mukunda sang of Krishna, and Ishvara Puri fell unconscious in
the ecstasy of love; Advaita embraced him and was Himself drenched with his tears, and on Him too the signs of ecstasy
appeared. So Ishvara Puri and Advaita met, both disciples of Madhavendra, though Advaita only played that
role[^18]. <!-- F62 -->

## The Dream about the “Gita”
When Nimai returned from Gaya transformed, with the signs of the highest devotion, Advaita, Srivasa and the other
devotees rejoiced, and Advaita began little by little to reveal to them Who He was. He told the Vaishnavas His dream.
Not having understood a verse of the “Gita,” He had fasted, and at night someone said to Him: “Get up, Acharya, and
eat; the meaning of the verse is — worship Me alone.” And further — about the vow Advaita had made with arms raised:

“Why do You grieve? You have attained all You strove for. Your desire is fulfilled. That for which You kept fasts,
performed worship and, weeping, chanted the names of Krishna has come to pass. You made an unbreakable vow to call down
the Supreme Lord, and that Lord has now appeared.” (CB, Madhya 2.12–14)

By His mercy, the voice said, people would see a bhakti inaccessible even to Brahma. Opening His eyes, Advaita saw
Vishvambhara — and He vanished. The “Chaitanya-bhagavata” speaks of this in another place too:

“Having revealed to Advaita the true meaning of the verse of the ‘Bhagavad-gita,’ Vishvambhara closed that door leading
to the path of bhakti.” (CB, Madhya 10.166) <!-- F63 F64 Q1 Q11 -->

The secrets of Krishna, Advaita added, He could not fathom — but let everyone wish that Nimai would surrender to
Krishna. The devotees bowed to Him and began to sing the name of Hari. <!-- F65 -->

## Gadadhara Sees the Worship
Soon Gauranga came with Gadadhara to Advaita. Advaita cried out loudly, “Hari! Hari!” — and Gauranga fell unconscious.
Then Advaita worshipped His feet with all the articles of puja and recited hymns to Govinda. Gadadhara objected: it is
not fitting for an old man to worship a boy! “In a few days you will know,” Advaita answered. Coming to Himself, the
Lord begged Advaita for mercy, and Advaita begged Him to lead them all in kirtan; and the Lord agreed. <!-- F66 -->

Then Advaita withdrew to Shantipura: if Gauranga is Krishna, He Himself will draw Him to Himself. Advaita's mind is hard
to understand — now He reveals Nimai, now He pretends not to know Him, now He “tests” Him and hides; but all this is so
that the Lord should reveal Himself to the world[^19]. <!-- F67 I4 -->

## “Nara!”
Soon the Lord revealed Himself to Srivasa. He kicked the door of his temple: “Whom are you worshipping? Here I am!” —
and showed His four-armed form. “Because of your loud sankirtana and Advaita's audacious call,” He said, “I have left
Vaikuntha.” <!-- F68 -->

And when Nityananda's Vyasa-puja was being performed in the house of Srivasa, the Lord began to call loudly: “Nara!
Nara!” So He called Advaita, whose cry had brought Him down from Vaikuntha — and now Advaita lived without a care in
Shantipura with Haridasa:

“Only because of Nara, whom you all know as Advaita Acharya, have I now descended into this world.” (CB, Madhya 5.51)

“Nara called Me from Vaikuntha, and now lives without a care together with Haridasa.” (CB, Madhya 5.52)

“I lay in the Ocean of Milk,” the Lord would say another time, “and My sleep was broken by the roar of Nada” (CB, Madhya
22.16)[^20]. <!-- F69 Q8 -->

## In Nandana Acharya's House
Soon after the Vyasa-puja the Lord, filled with the mood of the Supreme, sent Ramai Pandita, Srivasa's younger brother,
to Shantipura: “Tell Advaita: He for whose sake He worshipped with Ganga water and tulasi, wept and fasted, has come.
Nityananda too has come. Let Him come with His wife and with all that is needed for puja.” Ramai could not utter a word
for joy, but to Advaita everything was already revealed: “It seems Mahaprabhu has sent for Me,” He said. Advaita, Sita
and their companions wept with happiness and set out at once[^21]. <!-- F70 F71 -->

Raising His arms, Advaita danced: “It is I who brought the Lord down!” And to Ramai He said: “My faith will grow firmer
if He places His feet on My head. I shall hide — let Him find Me Himself.” Coming to Navadvipa, He concealed Himself in
the house of Nandana Acharya, and told Ramai to keep silent — or to say that Advaita had refused to come. And this
“test” too is a lila: in this way Advaita let the Lord reveal His omniscience to all. <!-- F72 -->

The Lord, meanwhile, ascended the vishnu-khatta, the throne of Vishnu, in Srivasa-angana and announced: “Nara wants
proof. Advaita is testing Me — He is hiding at Nandana's with everything He has prepared for My puja.” Nityananda held
an umbrella over Him, Gadadhara and others served. And the Lord sent Ramai again — to Nandana's; so Advaita learned that
there is no hiding from the Lord. <!-- F73 F74 -->

Advaita and His wife prostrated themselves before the Lord from a distance, and Advaita recited hymns. A vision opened to
Him: the Lord in radiance, in the ornaments of which the dhyana-mantras speak; four-, five- and six-faced gods pray to
Him; Ananta shades Him with His hoods, and the whole sky is full of gods and sages. The Lord said that He had come at His
call from His rest in the Ocean of Milk. Advaita was struck dumb; then He washed His feet and worshipped Him with the
five offerings, panchopachara, with the mantra “namo brahmanya-devaya…”. <!-- F75 -->

He worshipped the Lord with various gifts — and the Lord, fulfilling His desire, placed His feet on His head, while all
around cried, “Jaya!” At the Lord's bidding Advaita danced, forgetting Himself, and the devotees marvelled at such a
dance at so advanced an age. One of the biographies adds that Advaita had brought Sita with Him and was the first of all
to worship the Lord's feet. <!-- F76 -->

The Lord placed His own garland on Advaita and told Him to ask for a blessing. “For Myself I have nothing to ask,”
Advaita answered. “But let bhakti go to people of low station: those proud of learning, wealth, birth and renunciation
only hinder the preaching.” Advaita's desire is not for Himself: devotion is for the low, the unlearned and the fallen,
for pride closes the way to it. <!-- F77 I5 -->

## The Mahaprakasha
From then until the Lord's sannyasa Advaita lived in Navadvipa-Mayapura and every day attended the kirtan in Srivasa's
house. Another time, seated on the vishnu-khatta, the Lord ate all the offerings and the betel and called: “Nara!”;
Nityananda held an umbrella over Him, and Advaita, His palms joined, recited hymns. The Lord told Him to ask for a
blessing, for it was for His sake that He had come, and then embraced each one and wept for Krishna. <!-- F78 F79 -->

In the Mahaprakasha — the great revelation that lasted seven praharas, twenty-one hours — the Lord called each of the
devotees, told him of his past and bade him ask for what he wished. Advaita said that He had nothing to ask. Then the
Lord reminded Him of that dream about the verse of the “Gita” — and Advaita wept, for only He knew of it. “Be merciful
to the unlearned and the fallen,” He asked; and, growing calm, He rejoiced that the Lord would regard Him as His
servant. <!-- F80 -->

## Shantipura and the Bank of the Ganga
Lochana Dasa in the “Chaitanya-mangala” tells of other days as well. The Lord sang kirtan in Advaita's house, and They
embraced each other. Advaita came to Navadvipa, bowed to the Lord and danced, and at Srivasa's question the Lord
expounded the truth about Advaita, advaita-tattva. Nityananda stayed two days as Advaita's guest, and Advaita worshipped
the Lord in Srivasa's house. In Advaita's house the Lord told Him to preach love for Krishna without discriminating who
is worthy of it and who is not; the devotees gathered there, and to all of them, headed by Advaita, the Lord entrusted
the preaching. <!-- F81 Q16 -->

Once, on the bank of the Ganga, Gauranga seated Gadadhara beside Himself, and Advaita performed arati to Them both.
Before His sannyasa Mahaprabhu came more than once with Nityananda to Advaita's house in Shantipura and sang kirtan day
and night. Parameshvari Dasa tells in a pada: the Lord sat in Advaita's temple, Nityananda and Advaita sat beside Him and
playfully discussed the festival; Sita came in, smiling, and the Lord told them to call the Vaishnavas and prepare
everything for kirtan. <!-- F82 F83 -->

## Honour That Wounds
The Lord in His humility conducted Himself as one of the devotees, and honoured Advaita as a guru, as an elder: He fell
at His feet and would not let Him touch His own. Srila Bhaktivinoda Thakura explains this by Advaita's being the
godbrother of Ishvara Puri, the Lord's guru. But such honour gave Advaita pain. <!-- F84 -->

Once, when the Lord lost consciousness in sankirtana, Advaita smeared Himself with the dust of His feet and worshipped
them according to all the rules. Coming to Himself, the Lord in confusion threw Himself into the Ganga; Nityananda and
Haridasa pulled Him out, and afterwards He lived for some time in another place[^22]. <!-- F85 -->

The “Chaitanya-bhagavata” (Madhya 17) tells of the same leap differently. Once the Lord felt no prema in kirtan, and
Advaita, dancing, said to Him: “You made Nityananda the keeper of the storehouse of prema, and deprived Me and Srivasa
of it — and You give it away even to the low-born. So I have dried up the ocean of Your prema!” The Lord decided there
was no point in living without prema and threw Himself into the Ganga; Nityananda and Haridasa pulled Him out, and He
secretly took shelter with Nandana Acharya. <!-- F86 -->

The devotees searched everywhere for the Lord, and Advaita fasted from grief. Then the Lord, at the request of Srivasa,
whom Nandana had brought to Him, came to Advaita. Advaita lay without strength, almost unconscious, and the Lord felt
Himself at fault; but as soon as His voice sounded, Advaita rose and broke His fast. He begged forgiveness for His
audacious words and prayed for a place at His feet — birth after birth. In Nandana's house the Lord, Nityananda and
Advaita all hid at one time or another. <!-- F87 -->

## The “Yoga-vasishtha”
The Lord's honour was a torment to Advaita, and He resolved to provoke His anger. He began to expound the
“Yoga-vasishtha,” placing knowledge, jnana, and liberation above bhakti, and on some pretext went away to Shantipura.
Advaita Himself says in the “Charitamrita”:

“Before, Mahaprabhu showed Me great honour as an elder. This state of affairs was not to My liking, and I devised how
to change it all.” (CC, Adi 12.39)

“I began to explain the ‘Yoga-vasishtha,’ in which liberation is accepted as the highest goal of life. Learning of
this, Sri Chaitanya grew angry and punished Me, forgetting His former respect.” (CC, Adi 12.40)

According to Srila Bhaktivinoda, He explained the path of knowledge to “a few unfortunate people.” Vrindavana Dasa
conveys how it looked:

“Intoxicated with bliss, He constantly swayed from side to side, commenting on the book ‘Yoga-vasishtha’ and glorifying
jnana.” (CB, Madhya 19.20)[^23] <!-- F88 Q11 -->

The Lord understood His design and went to Shantipura with Nityananda. Seeing Them, Advaita began to extol jnana still
more zealously, and the Lord struck Him with His fists: “Why did You bring Me down from Vaikuntha if now You preach
jnanavada?” <!-- F89 -->

And Advaita, having received the punishment, danced with joy: He understood that He had won, and rubbed His head
against the Lord's feet. “My desire is fulfilled,” He said. “Before, the Lord was sparing with Me, honouring Me as an
elder, but now He has dealt with Me as with a servant and saved Me from the heresy of mayavada.” The Lord, seeing
through His ruse, was abashed, but was pleased and merciful to Him:

“Sri Chaitanya highly honoured Sri Advaita as His Guru on the path of bhakti. But Advaita Acharya Himself was greatly
saddened by this.” (CC, Adi 17.66)

“Then Advaita began to explain the path of philosophical speculation, and Sri Chaitanya in feigned anger showed Him His
disrespect.” (CC, Adi 17.67)

“Sri Advaita received this punishment as the greatest bliss. Understanding Advaita's motive, Sri Chaitanya was abashed
and blessed Him.” (CC, Adi 17.68) <!-- F90 -->

The Lord embraced Him: “I will be pleased with anyone, even one who has offended Me, if he takes Your shelter.” Advaita
answered: “Whoever rejects You is nothing to You.” Little Achyutananda too saw his father's punishment. <!-- F91 -->

So Advaita's “path of knowledge” was only a ruse for the sake of punishment, not a conviction. He arranged this lila to
show what a blessing punishment from the Lord, from the guru or from a Vaishnava is. The highest thing for Advaita is to
be a servant; honour from the Lord is a torment to Him, and for the sake of punishment He is ready to “preach” jnana and
rejoice under the blows. And the Lord promises His mercy to anyone who takes Advaita's shelter — even an offender.
<!-- F92 I6 I7 -->

## Shachi and Advaita
Shachi blamed Advaita for Vishvarupa's taking sannyasa. Before, she had kept silent, fearing to offend a Vaishnava — she
knew Advaita to be a Vaishnava. But now Nimai too spent all His time in Advaita's house, leaving Lakshmipriya at home,
and Shachi feared that He too would leave. In her sorrow she said to herself: “Who calls Him Advaita — ‘non-dual’? He is
‘Dvaita’: one son He has already led away from home, and now He will not let me keep the other” (CB, Madhya
22.113–117). Only her Son knew of this. <!-- F93 Q12 -->

During the Mahaprakasha Srivasa asked the Lord to grant Shachi the darshana of His form, but the Lord said: “Mother has
offended Advaita — I will not show Myself to her. Only He can forgive, as Ambarisha forgave Durvasa.” Then Shachi went
herself to beg Advaita's forgiveness. And Advaita began to glorify her who had borne the Lord in her womb — until He lost
consciousness; and then she took the dust of His feet. The Lord revealed His form to her[^24]. <!-- F94 F95 -->

So the Lord showed how grave vaishnava-aparadha is: it bars the way even to Shachi, and only the forgiveness of the
offended Vaishnava saves one from it. And He showed that Vaishnavas are not proud: Shachi went without delay to beg
forgiveness. Shachi herself is the eternal mother of the Lord, Yashoda; Vrindavana Dasa says plainly that in truth it
was not even an offence, but the Lord called it so (CB, Madhya 22.58)[^25]. <!-- F96 I8 -->

## The Universal Form
Once Advaita, sorrowful, was dancing in Srivasa's courtyard and lamenting that His hope had still not been fulfilled;
only after two praharas, some six hours, could He be calmed. The Lord, seeing how He rolled on the ground and wept, led
Him into the room of Vishnu, and Advaita asked: “Let Me see what Arjuna saw.” And before Him appeared the universal
form, the vishvarupa: a dark-blue four-armed Krishna, in whose body were the luminaries, the seas and the mountains,
millions of faces, arms and eyes; Arjuna was reciting hymns to Him, and Advaita, taking a blade of grass between His
teeth as a sign of humility, begged for service (cf. CB, Madhya 24). <!-- F97 F98 -->

## The Drama in Chandrashekhara's House
When in the house of Chandrashekhara Acharyaratna the Lord staged a drama about Krishna, Advaita was given the role of
the vidushaka — the jester. The Lord played Rukmini and then appeared as the Adya-shakti, the original energy, Lakshmi;
Nityananda was an old woman, Gadadhara a gopi, Srivasa Narada, Haridasa a watchman. The Lord announced that only those
who had controlled their senses could watch the drama, and Advaita and Srivasa sadly said that this meant they could
not. “Today all will become yogis,” the Lord answered (CB, Madhya 18). <!-- F99 -->

## Night Kirtans and the Procession to the Kazi
Advaita was beside the Lord in sankirtana — in Srivasa's courtyard, in the streets of Navadvipa and on the bank of the
Ganga — and among those who sang all night long:

“All of them sang in jubilation: ‘Krishna, Rama, Mukunda, Murari, Vanamali!’” (CB, Madhya 23.29)

“Nityananda and Gadadhara whirled, holding hands. Advaita, like a lion, ran about the room in ecstatic bliss.” (CB,
Madhya 23.30)[^26] <!-- F100 -->

When Chand Kazi forbade the sankirtana, the Lord led Navadvipa out into the streets, and Advaita led one part of the
procession, dancing and loudly singing the names:

“The Lord danced as He moved along the bank of the Ganga, and all who walked beside Him sang the names of Lord Hari.”
(CB, Madhya 23.202)

“Advaita Acharya with His party went in front and danced in indescribable bliss.” (CB, Madhya 23.203)

And the Kazi became a friend and vowed never again to hinder the sankirtana[^27]. <!-- F101 Q12 -->

## Nityananda's Wedding
Much later, after the Lord's sannyasa, when Nityananda agreed to marry, Advaita rejoiced: “Let it happen soon!” He was
among the devotees at the wedding in Boroganchhi, and afterwards Nityananda with His wives, Vasudha and Jahnava, at
Shachi's word stayed with Advaita in Shantipura, and Sita embraced them both. <!-- F102 -->

## The Lord's Sannyasa
At the beginning of 1510 Mahaprabhu accepted sannyasa in Katwa. Intoxicated with love for Krishna, He wandered for three
days, thinking He was going to Vrindavana, while Nityananda sent Chandrashekhara Acharya ahead:

“At that time Sri Nityananda ordered Chandrashekhara: ‘Go quickly to the house of Advaita Acharya.’” (CC, Madhya 3.20)

“‘I shall bring Sri Chaitanya to Advaita's house. But let Advaita wait for us with a boat on the bank of the Ganga.’”
(CC, Madhya 3.21)

From there Chandrashekhara was to go on to Navadvipa for Mother Shachi and all the devotees (Madhya 3.22)[^28]. The news
of the Lord's sannyasa broke hearts, and, according to one of the biographies, Advaita wept until He fell down as though
lifeless; then a voice from the sky told Him not to grieve: in a few days the Lord would be with them.
<!-- F103 F104 Q13 -->

## “Wherever You Are, There Is Vrindavana”
Nityananda taught the cowherd boys to point out the road to the Lord so that it led Him not to the Yamuna but to the
Ganga, opposite Shantipura. Taking the Ganga for the Yamuna, the Lord offered her a prayer and entered the water — and
just then a boat came to the bank:

“Just at that time Advaita Acharya arrived by boat with new cloth for Sri Chaitanya.” (CC, Madhya 3.30)

“‘Advaitacharya! Is it You? How did You come here? How did You know that I am in Vraja?’” (CC, Madhya 3.32)

“‘Truly, wherever You are, there is Vrindavana!’ the Acharya replied. ‘I am immensely fortunate that You have come to
the bank of the Ganga.’” (CC, Madhya 3.33)

The Lord exclaimed that Nityananda had deceived Him by calling the Ganga the Yamuna; but Advaita objected that there was
no deception: here the Ganga and the Yamuna flow in one bed, and along the western bank flows the Yamuna, so the Lord had
truly bathed in the Yamuna (Madhya 3.35–37). He dressed the Lord in dry cloth, brought Him by boat to His house and washed
His feet[^29]. <!-- F105 -->

Advaita fell prostrate before the Lord and embraced His feet, and the Lord raised Him up. To Advaita and Sita, says one
of the biographies, it was as though life had been given back. <!-- F106 -->

Advaita's little son Achyutananda came running too. Vrindavana Dasa speaks of him thus:

“Advaita had a little son named Achyutananda, whose naked body seemed to radiate effulgence.” (CB, Antya 1.213)

“Understanding that the Lord had come, this child, covered with dust from head to foot, came laughing to see Him.”
(CB, Antya 1.215)

“Coming, he fell at the lotus feet of Gaurachandra, and the Lord took this dust-covered child onto His lap.” (CB, Antya
1.216)

The Lord called the boy His brother: “If Advaita is My father, then you are My brother.” And he replied: “The scriptures
say that You are the father of all”[^30]. <!-- F107 Q15 -->

## Ten Days in Shantipura
The Lord had eaten nothing for three days, and in Advaita's house a great feast was prepared for Him. Sita cooked, and
Advaita offered everything to Vishnu; the rice lay in mountains on whole banana leaves. The Lord, says one of the
biographies, after His fast accepted prasada from Sita's hands. At the meal Advaita joked with Him and with Nityananda,
and Nityananda and Advaita began a playful quarrel: Advaita called Nityananda a “fallen avadhuta,” and Nityananda, as if
in anger, threw a handful of rice — and Advaita danced with joy that the remnants of Nityananda's meal had touched Him.
Such “disputes” of Advaita with Nityananda are lila: the one who seems to censure Nityananda in truth glorifies Him. On
the following days the Lord ate Shachi's cooking as well. <!-- F108 I15 -->

The next morning Chandrashekhara brought Shachi from Navadvipa:

“On the morning after Mahaprabhu's arrival Chandrashekhara brought Mother Shachi in a palanquin. Many other devotees
came with them as well.” (CC, Madhya 3.137)

The people of Navadvipa came as well; in Shantipura the Lord was visited by Gangadasa, Shridhara, Damodara,
Nandana Acharya and others. For four days Sita did not leave Shachi, who lay as though at the point of death.
<!-- F109 -->

For ten days sankirtana never ceased in Advaita's house:

“All danced in jubilation, crying ‘Hari! Hari!’ Truly, Advaita's house became like Vaikuntha.” (CC, Madhya 3.156)

Then, at His mother's request, the Lord resolved to live in Puri, and in that same year 1510 He went there, having
encouraged the devotees, whom Advaita now led. For Advaita and all Navadvipa a time of separation began. <!-- F110 Q13 -->

## Every Year to Nilachala
From Puri the Lord set out to travel through South India. When Kala Krishnadasa brought to Bengal the news that He had
returned, Advaita and the other devotees, with Shachi's permission, made ready to go to Puri. Devotees from Shrikhanda
came to Advaita's house as well, to travel together:

“Mukunda, Narahari and Raghunandana from Khanda also came to the Acharya's house, to go with Him to Jagannatha Puri.”
(CC, Madhya 10.90) <!-- F111 -->

Kaviraja Gosvami speaks of their first coming thus:

“In the year after Sri Chaitanya accepted sannyasa, all the devotees, headed by Advaita, set out for Jagannatha Puri to
see Mahaprabhu.” (CC, Madhya 1.46)

“Having seen the Ratha-yatra, all the devotees stayed four months more, to relish the supreme bliss of dancing and
singing the holy names in Sri Chaitanya's company.” (CC, Madhya 1.47)

“Taking leave of everyone, the Lord asked the devotees to come every year to the Ratha-yatra festival at the Gundicha
temple.” (CC, Madhya 1.48)

According to one of the biographies, Advaita set out with Shachi's permission even before the Ratha-yatra, and the Lord
sent mahaprasada ahead to meet the travellers at Cuttack[^31]. <!-- F112 -->

Of the twenty-four years after His sannyasa the Lord spent six in travel and eighteen in Puri (Madhya 1.249), and all
that time the Bengalis came to Him every year:

“And every year the Bengali devotees came to see Sri Chaitanya and stayed four months in Puri.” (CC, Madhya 1.250)

“The other devotees, headed by Advaita, Nityananda, Vidyanidhi, Mukunda, Vasudeva, Murari and Srivasa, came to Sri
Chaitanya every year and stayed four months in Nilachala.” (CC, Madhya 1.255)

And it was always Advaita who led them:

“The devotees set out for Nilachala under the leadership of Advaita Acharya.” (CB, Antya 8.6)[^32]

Sita and Achyutananda went with Him. <!-- F113 Q13 -->

## The Meeting at Narendra-sarovara
One of the biographies tells that in Puri the Lord declared before all that He had descended into the world at
Advaita's call — and the people of Puri were filled with devotion to Advaita. Learning that the Bengalis headed by
Advaita had already reached Athara-nala, the bridge at the entrance to Puri, the Lord Himself went out to meet them;
with Him were Kashishvara, Bhagavan Acharya, Pradyumna Mishra and others. At Narendra-sarovara both sides fell prostrate
before one another. “It is I who brought Him!” Advaita cried, and all bowed to Him. Nityananda and Advaita embraced, and
the Lord, first of all, placed Jagannatha's garland on Advaita. <!-- F114 F115 -->

## The Ratha-yatra and Gundicha
At the Ratha-yatra the Lord Himself divided the singers into groups and appointed a dancer for each — Nityananda,
Advaita, Haridasa and Vakreshvara. In the first group the lead singer was Svarupa Damodara and the dancer Advaita. There
was also the Shantipura group, which Advaita Himself had gathered; His son danced in it, and Gopala Mishra sang with them:

“In the group of singers from Shantipura, which Advaita Acharya had gathered, Achyutananda, Advaita's son, was chosen as
the dancer. All the others were to sing along with Advaita.” (CC, Madhya 13.45)

Before the kirtan the Lord with His own hand adorned His senior companions with sandal paste and garlands:

“Advaita Acharya and Nityananda also felt immense happiness when the Lord's hand touched Them.” (CC, Madhya 13.31)
<!-- F116 Q15 -->

In other years too, going around the temple of Jagannatha in the bera-kirtan, the Lord formed seven groups:

“And in each of these seven groups there was a principal dancer, such as Sri Advaita Acharya or Nityananda Prabhu.”
(CC, Antya 10.59)

“While in the other groups the principal dancers were Vakreshvara Pandita, Achyutananda, Srivasa Pandita, Satyaraja Khan
and Narahari Dasa.” (CC, Antya 10.60)

In the days when Jagannatha stayed at Gundicha, the Lord sang with the devotees in the temple courtyard from morning
till evening:

“At times He inspired Advaita Acharya, Nityananda, Thakura Haridasa and Achyutananda to dance.” (CC, Madhya 14.71)

And when the Gundicha temple was washed before the festival, everyone carried water except a few of the elders:

“All except Nityananda, Advaita, Damodara, Bharati and Paramananda Puri filled pots and carried water.” (CC, Madhya
12.109)[^33]

Advaita also played with the Lord and the devotees in the water pastimes at Narendra-sarovara. <!-- F117 Q15 -->

Among the Lord's companions in Puri Svarupa Damodara was especially dear to Advaita:

“He was very dear to Advaita and Sri Nityananda, and the very soul of the whole community of devotees headed by Srivasa
Thakura.” (CC, Madhya 10.117)

Together with the Lord, Advaita also went to the garden of Tota-Gopinatha to hear Gadadhara Pandita read the
“Bhagavatam.” <!-- F118 F119 Q15 Q16 -->

## A Meal in the Storm
Once Advaita began to entreat the Lord to come to Him for a meal. He wanted to feed the Lord alone: when He came with the
sannyasis, out of respect for them He ate very little. Advaita cooked Himself, while His wife helped Him — cutting
vegetables and handing Him the provisions brought from Bengal (CB, Antya 9.20–24)[^34]. When the cooking was done,
Advaita placed a tulasi-manjari on every dish and sat down to meditate: let the Lord come alone. <!-- F120 -->

Suddenly a storm arose — wind, downpour and hail — and the sannyasis, losing their way, did not reach the place, while at
Advaita's it only drizzled a little. The Lord came alone and ate everything Advaita served Him. When He had eaten,
Advaita began to glorify Indra:

“‘O Indra! Today I have understood your greatness. Today I have understood that you are a Vaishnava.’” (CB, Antya 9.61)

But the Lord saw through Him. “Acharya, why do You hide the truth?” the Lord asked. “Is not this wind and rain the work
of Your hands?” (CB, Antya 9.65) And He explained:

“You thought that if I came together with the sannyasis, I would not eat much.” (CB, Antya 9.69)

“In truth Indra carried out Your order, so that Your real greatness would not be revealed to all. The chance to serve
You is the greatest fortune for him.” (CB, Antya 9.72)

Indra, the servant of Krishna, fulfils the desires of His devotees; if Krishna fulfils Advaita's desires, what wonder is
it that Indra obeys Him? One of the biographies places this meal in the third year, when the Bengalis came with their
wives and brought foodstuffs with them. <!-- F121 Q13 -->

## A Kirtan in Glory of Gaura
The Lord would not allow Himself to be glorified: He called Himself only a servant of Krishna, and the devotees feared
His anger. But one day Advaita told them: “Today we will glorify no avatara other than Sri Chaitanya — the source of all
avataras. I will dance, and you sing His glory” (CB, Antya 9.157–162). No one could disobey Advaita, and the devotees
began to sing, while Advaita danced and sang a song He Himself had composed in glory of the Lord:

“‘O Sri Chaitanya! You are Narayana Himself! You are an ocean of mercy and the friend of the distressed. Please be
merciful to Me.’” (CB, Antya 9.168)

Hearing Himself glorified, the Lord was embarrassed and went home. When the devotees came to Him singing, He lay turned
away and reproached them; and then He was answered that the sun cannot be hidden with one's palm — just so the truth
about Him cannot be concealed (CB, Antya 9.204–208)[^35]. <!-- F122 -->

Thus Advaita was the first openly to introduce kirtan in glory of Sri Krishna Chaitanya — honouring Him equally with
Krishna, for He is Krishna. In this one sees Advaita's chief trait — spiritual fearlessness: it sounded in His loud call,
in the shraddha-patra given to Haridasa, and in this kirtan. <!-- I10 -->

## Again in Shantipura: the Journey to Gauda
Five years after His sannyasa the Lord resolved to go to Vrindavana by way of Bengal. According to the
“Charitamrita,” He visited Srivasa in Kumarahatta, then Kulia, where He stayed a week in the house of Madhava Dasa and
granted deliverance to crowds of people — even to sinners and offenders (the “Chaitanya-chandrodaya,” 9.33, also speaks
of those seven days in Kulia), and from there He came to Shantipura, to Advaita's house, and consoled His mother there.
From Shantipura He went to Ramakeli, where He met Rupa and Sanatana, reached Kanai-natashala and returned again to
Shantipura, staying there ten days this time (CC, Madhya 16.205–212)[^36]. It is said that in those days Vamshivadana was
born, and the Lord and Advaita were present at his birth. <!-- F123 Q16 -->

## Achyuta and the Question about Keshava Bharati
Even before the Lord came back from Ramakeli, a certain sannyasi came to Advaita's house and asked what relation Keshava
Bharati bore to Sri Chaitanya. From the spiritual point of view the Lord has not and cannot have a guru; but Advaita
reasoned that one may begin from the worldly side — so Devaki too is called Krishna's mother and Sandipani His guru — and
answered: “Keshava Bharati is Chaitanya's guru” (CB, Antya 4.147–151). Just then five-year-old Achyuta ran in, naked and
all dusty from play. The “Charitamrita” tells:

“Hearing from his father that Keshava Bharati was Sri Chaitanya's teacher, Achyutananda was very upset.” (CC, Adi
12.14)

“Then he said to his father, Advaitacharya: ‘Your words that Keshava Bharati became Sri Chaitanya's guru bring ruin to our
whole country.’” (CC, Adi 12.15)

“‘Sri Chaitanya is the Guru of the whole universe. Yet You say that someone is His teacher. But there is no confirmation
of this in the scriptures.’” (CC, Adi 12.16)

“Advaita felt great joy when He heard the essence of the scriptural conclusions from His five-year-old son.” (CC, Adi
12.17) <!-- F124 Q11 -->

According to the “Chaitanya-bhagavata,” Advaita in bliss embraced His son, bathed him in tears of love and said:

“‘You are My father, and I am your son. You have appeared as My son in order to instruct Me.’” (CB, Antya 4.174)

He begged His son's forgiveness, and the boy, hearing himself praised, lowered his head in embarrassment. The sannyasi
fell at Achyuta's feet and exclaimed: “As the father, so the son!” (CB, Antya 4.177–178)[^37]. Advaita danced together
with His son: in His house an associate of the Lord had been born. Achyuta is an example of what a true son of Advaita is
like: he cannot bear even worldly speech that belittles the Lord. <!-- F125 I9 -->

At that moment the Lord Himself arrived. Advaita fell prostrate before Him, crying “Hari! Hari!”; the Lord embraced Him
and bathed Him in tears, and Advaita lost consciousness, and on coming to Himself seated the Lord. Advaita and Nityananda
embraced. The Lord took Achyuta on His lap and washed the dust off him with His tears; He blessed the boy. <!-- F126 -->

## The Festival at Advaita's; Raghunatha
Advaita sent for Shachi. She came in a palanquin, and the Lord fell prostrate at her feet; she herself cooked for her Son.
The festival in Advaita's house lasted several days, and no one knew where the provisions came from. <!-- F127 -->

At that time Raghunatha Dasa came to Shantipura. His father Govardhana Majumdar respectfully served Advaita with gifts,
and therefore Advaita presented the young man to the Lord:

“Raghunatha Dasa's father always respectfully served Advaita, and Advaita was favourably disposed to his whole family.”
(CC, Madhya 16.225)

“So Raghunatha stayed in Advaita's house, obtaining the chance for about a week to serve Sri Chaitanya and to eat the
remnants of Mahaprabhu's meals.” (CC, Madhya 16.226)[^38]

Raghunatha had seen the Lord already on His first coming to Shantipura, after the sannyasa (Madhya 16.223), and later, in
Puri, he also received Advaita's blessings. Now the Lord told him to go home and wait until He came back from Vrindavana.
<!-- F128 Q13 -->

Taking leave, the Lord told the devotees to come every year to the Ratha-yatra, and consoled Advaita: He hoped to see Him
in Nilachala within that very year. <!-- F129 -->

## Puri: the Following Years
In the third year the Bengalis went to Puri together with their wives:

“All the devotees set out under the leadership of Advaita Acharya, who joyfully anticipated meeting Mahaprabhu.” (CC,
Madhya 16.13)

“That year all the devotees set out together with their wives, including the wife of Sri Advaita Acharya, the mother of
Achyutananda.” (CC, Madhya 16.21)

All gathered at Advaita's house; He first took leave of Shachi and received her permission, and the travellers moved on
to Nilachala, carrying the foods the Lord loved:

“Throughout the whole journey the devotees performed sankirtana, purifying all the places through which they passed.”
(CB, Antya 8.41)

“Taking their wives, children and servants with them, the devotees went in great ecstasy to see Lord Chaitanya.” (CB,
Antya 8.43) <!-- F130 Q13 -->

One of the wonders of Puri concerned Advaita's son Gopala. When, on one of the Bengalis' visits, the Gundicha temple was
being washed, the Lord told him to dance, and the boy fell unconscious in prema and stopped breathing. Advaita laid him on
His lap; together with the devotees He loudly chanted the mantra to Nrisimha and sprinkled His son with water, but the
boy did not come to himself, and all wept. Then:

“Mahaprabhu placed His hand on his chest and loudly said: ‘Gopala, get up!’” (CC, Madhya 12.148)

“Hearing this, Gopala came to himself, and the devotees began to dance and sing the holy name of God.” (CC, Madhya
12.149)

The “Charitamrita” tells of this in the Adi-lila as well (12.19–26). <!-- F131 -->

## Kamalakanta Vishvasa
Kamalakanta Vishvasa, Advaita's servant and disciple, without His knowledge wrote to King Prataparudra that Advaita was
the Supreme Lord Himself, but by the will of fate had run into a debt of three hundred rupees, and asked the king to pay
this debt. The letter reached the Lord. He said that to call Advaita the Supreme Lord was no mistake, but to present the
Supreme Lord as a poor beggar was a disgrace, and He told Govinda no longer to let Kamalakanta in to see Him (CC, Adi
12.29–36). Kamalakanta grieved, but Advaita rejoiced:

“Sri Advaita said to Kamalakanta: ‘You simply do not understand your immense good fortune. For it is the Supreme Lord
Himself — Sri Chaitanya Mahaprabhu — who has punished you.’” (CC, Adi 12.38)

And Advaita went to the Lord to “complain” that He had shown Kamalakanta more mercy than He had shown Him. The Lord
laughed, called Kamalakanta and instructed him:

“An acharya-Vaishnava must never take gifts or money from kings and the wealthy. He must never eat food from the hands of
materialistic people, for this pollutes the mind.” (CC, Adi 12.50)

Srila Bhaktivinoda Thakura explains: although Advaita is God Himself, in this world He plays the role of a man in order
to teach people. It is shameful for an acharya to beg from a king, and a teacher of the holy name takes no payment for
it. <!-- F132 I12 -->

## Mercy to Rupa; the Debt of Love
When Rupa Gosvami came to Puri, the Lord Himself brought him to Advaita and Nityananda and asked Them to bless him:

“Then Sri Chaitanya turned to Advaita Acharya and Nityananda: ‘With all Your heart You must bestow Your mercy on Rupa.’”
(CC, Antya 1.56)

And Advaita with Nityananda and all the devotees bestowed mercy on Rupa and embraced him (Antya 1.207)[^39]. <!-- F133 -->

In the year when Nityananda too came with the Bengalis — against the Lord's order to remain in Bengal — the Lord, at the
end of the four months of the rains, taking leave of the devotees, said of Advaita, “Acharya Gosai”:

“To grant Me His mercy, Advaitacharya too has come here. Because of His love I am in His debt, and this debt I am unable
to repay.” (CC, Antya 12.70)

And then He addressed everyone: for My sake you have left your homes, wives and children and walked a hard road; I am a
penniless sannyasi, and I have nothing to give you but My body — this I give to you (Antya 12.71–74)[^40].
<!-- F134 Q14 -->

## The Riddle
Every year the Lord sent Jagadananda Pandita to Nadia to console Shachi. The last time, having stayed there a month,
Jagadananda came to take leave of Advaita, and Advaita sent with him a message to the Lord — a riddle, a tarja,
understandable to Him alone:

“Tell the madman that all the people have gone mad. Tell the madman that rice is no longer sold in the market.” (CC,
Antya 19.20)

“Tell the madman that the mad one is no longer occupied with worldly affairs. Tell the madman that a madman has said
this.” (CC, Antya 19.21)[^41]

Hearing the riddle, the Lord smiled slightly, said, “Such is His order,” and fell silent. To Svarupa Damodara's question
He answered only that the Acharya is a great knower of worship according to the agamas: for the puja He invokes the
Deity, and when the worship is finished He sends Him away — and the meaning of the riddle, said the Lord, even He could
not understand (Antya 19.23–28). Svarupa grew anxious: from that day the Lord's separation from Krishna became twice as
strong, and His divine madness grew — His pastimes in this world were soon to be concealed. <!-- F135 Q14 -->

Srila Bhaktivinoda Thakura reveals the meaning of the riddle: all are already intoxicated with prema, and the purpose for
which Advaita called the Lord has been achieved; now the Lord is free to act as He pleases[^42]. <!-- I14 -->

## Sons
One of the biographies counts six sons of Advaita. The eldest is Achyutananda, after him Krishna Mishra and Gopala
Mishra: these three grasped the essence of their father's teaching and were devoted to Gaura; according to the
“Advaita-charita,” they were born of Sita, and they are called the “three jewels” of Gaura. The fourth son is Balarama,
the fifth Svarupa, the sixth Jagadisha; they did not accept Sri Chaitanya[^43]. The “Charitamrita,” listing the branches
of Advaita's tree, speaks of this thus:

“Unhusked rice is first measured together with the husk. But afterwards the husk must be winnowed away and the rice
cleaned.” (CC, Adi 12.12) <!-- F136 Q11 -->

Srila Bhaktivinoda Thakura explains in the “Amrita-pravaha-bhashya”: in the course of time some of Advaita's followers
adopted other teachings, but the essence is the teaching of Advaita Himself, and whoever transgresses it will attain
nothing:

“The instructions of the teacher are the basis of spiritual life. One who transgresses his orders cannot make any
progress.” (CC, Adi 12.10)

“But those descendants of Advaita Acharya who accepted the path of Sri Achyutananda were all great devotees of God.”
(CC, Adi 12.73)

“And all those who received the mercy of Sri Advaita easily found shelter at the lotus feet of Sri Chaitanya.” (CC,
Adi 12.74)

Vrindavana Dasa says the same still more sternly:

“If a person worships Advaita but neglects Gaurachandra, he dooms himself to ruin, even if he is Advaita's son.” (CB,
Antya 4.183) <!-- F137 Q11 -->

Achyutananda, according to the “Charitamrita,” is a great branch of Advaita's tree: from his very birth he served the
feet of Sri Chaitanya (Adi 12.13). The “Gaura-ganoddesha-dipika” calls him the beloved disciple of Gadadhara Pandita and
sees in him Karttikeya or the gopi Achyuta (others consider Krishna Mishra to be Karttikeya). One of the biographies gives
the year of his birth as 1428 Shaka (1506), and the place as Shantipura[^44]. Srila Bhaktisiddhanta in the “Anubhashya”
calls Achyutananda the first of Advaita's disciples, while the “Shakha-nirnayamrita” calls him a disciple of Gadadhara. In
the Lord's last years he lived in Puri:

“Achyutananda is the son of Advaita Acharya. He remained living in Jagannatha Puri, having taken shelter at the lotus feet
of Sri Chaitanya.” (CC, Adi 10.150)

According to one of the biographies, Advaita's other sons also lived in Puri at that time. <!-- F138 Q11 -->

Yet another biography mentions “Sri Nrisimha, the son of Advaita Acharya,” who lived in Ramakeli[^45]. <!-- F139 -->

## Disciples
Advaita's mercy extended also to those who were bound to Him as disciples. Yadunandana Acharya of Chandpur, the guru of
Raghunatha Dasa, was a close disciple of Advaita; Srinatha Chakravarti, the guru of Kavi Karnapura, a sub-branch of His
tree; Padmanabha Chakravarti, the father of Lokanatha Gosvami, a dear follower of His. Kamalakanta Vishvasa, of whom we
have told above, was also Advaita's disciple. <!-- F140 -->

## Concealment
When and where Advaita concluded His pastimes in this world, most of the biographies do not say. According to the
“Gaudiya-vaishnava-abhidhana,” it was in 1559, in the hundred and twenty-fifth year of His life (see above, “Appearance in
Shrihatta”); the biographies do not name the lunar day of His departure, and in our calendar there is no day of Sri
Advaita's departure[^46]. <!-- F141 -->

It is known that Advaita remained in the world even after the Lord's concealment, and departed before Sita. Srinivasa,
returning from Puri, where he no longer found Gadadhara Pandita, learned on the way that Advaita and Nityananda too had
concealed Themselves, and in grief resolved to give up his body; but both Prabhus appeared to him in a dream and
consoled him. In Shantipura he saw Sita, barely alive in separation from her husband[^47]. Advaita came to Srinivasa in
dreams later too: He told him to marry — even before Narahari told him the same. <!-- F141 F142 -->

In Shantipura the service was continued by Advaita's sons. Achyutananda, Gopala and Krishna Mishra received Jahnava
there; in Advaita's house Narottama spent two days with Achyutananda and bowed to him, and Krishna Mishra received
Virachandra with honours and sankirtana. At the great festival in Kheturi were Achyutananda, Krishna Mishra and Gopala
from Shantipura; and when kirtan was sung there, the Lord Himself appeared with His departed associates, and the
“Bhakti-ratnakara” (10) exclaims: how Advaita and Nityananda shone in ecstasy! <!-- F143 F144 Q16 -->

## Places of Remembrance and Books
In Ramakeli, in the temple of Madana-mohana, the Deity of Advaita is worshipped together with Nitai-Gauranga. In
Mayapura, on the site of Advaita's Navadvipa house, in 1932, on the day of Gaura-purnima, Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati
laid the foundation stone of a new temple; the Advaita-bhavana in Mayapura became a centre of the Gaudiya Math.
<!-- F145 -->

About Advaita were written the “Advaita-mangala,” the “Advaita-vilasa” and the “Sita-charitra”; Sarvabhauma Bhattacharya
composed in His glory the “Advaita-dvadasha-nama,” the “Advaitashtaka” and the “Advaita-ashtottara-shata-nama.” Most
fully He is spoken of in the “Chaitanya-charitamrita,” the “Chaitanya-bhagavata,” the “Bhakti-ratnakara” and the
“Advaita-vilasa”; there is also the late “Advaita-prakasha”[^48]. <!-- F146 F147 -->

## Advaita in the Memory of the Devotees
Advaita's name sounds wherever Sri Chaitanya is sung of. He is glorified by the benedictions at the beginning of the
chapters of the “Charitamrita” and by the verse “jaya advaitadi…”; the “Chaitanya-bhagavata” glorifies Him too at the
beginning of the ninth chapter of the Adi-khanda; Vrindavana Dasa says that the Lord descended into the world amid
Nityananda, Advaita and Gadadhara. His name is in the Pancha-tattva mantra: “sri-krishna-chaitanya prabhu-nityananda
sri-advaita gadadhara srivasadi-gaura-bhakta-vrinda.” The “Bhakti-ratnakara” recalls Him at the kirtan at Shridhara's
(12) and tells how Narottama prayed to Advaita together with other associates of the Lord (1). He is remembered in small
things too: one evening it was to Advaita that Haridasa confessed he would no longer go preaching with Nityananda — so
unpredictable were the avadhuta's pastimes. And in every list of the companions at the Ratha-yatra Advaita is among the
first. <!-- F147 -->

So the life of Advaita Acharya closes in a circle. His call, full of compassion for the people of Kali, brought down the
Lord; His fearlessness revealed to the world Who Sri Chaitanya is; His humility showed how a servant seeks not honours but
punishment from the Lord; and His riddle announced that the purpose was fulfilled — the world was intoxicated with prema.
Whoever takes shelter of His feet will, in the words of the “Charitamrita,” easily find shelter at the feet of Sri
Chaitanya. <!-- I14 I13 -->
