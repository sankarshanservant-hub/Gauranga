# Vamshivadana Thakura
*Вамшивадана Тхакур · @vamshivadana*

## Krishna's Flute
Among the companions of Sri Chaitanya who stayed on in Navadvipa after His sannyasa there is a devotee whose very name
tells who he was in Krishna's pastimes. Vaishnavas know him by five names: Vamshivadana (“He whose mouth is the flute”),
Vamshidasa, Vamshi, Vadana and Vadananda; the biographies call him Sri Vamshivadanananda Thakura. In the primary sources
he is “Vamshidasa Thakkura” in the “Gaura-ganoddesha-dipika,” “Sri Vamshivadana” and “Sri Thakura Vamshi” in the
“Bhakti-ratnakara,” “Vamshivadana Dasa” in the “Prema-vilasa,” “Sri Vamshivadana” in the “Narottama-vilasa,” and
“Vamshivadana Thakura” and “Vamshivadana Dasa” in the “Vaishnava-vandanas”; his own songs he signed “Vamshi” and
“Vamshivadana.” Here we call him Vamshivadana Thakura, or simply Vamshi.[^1] <!-- F1 -->

Kavi Karnapura in the “Gaura-ganoddesha-dipika” reveals his place in the eternal lila:

“The flute, dear to Krishna, is Vamshi Dasa Thakura.” (“Gaura-ganoddesha-dipika,” 179)

Sri Krishna's flute is no wooden object but His eternal companion, full of consciousness and love (*chinmaya*), as
everything in Vraja is. She alone drinks without cease the nectar of His lips, and the gopis, Krishna's beloved, sang of
her good fortune — yet there is no worldly jealousy in their words: it is the voice of their prema, which longs only to
serve Krishna as closely as the flute serves Him. That is why the biographies say that the spiritual flute of Vraja, which
touched the nectar of His lips, appeared in Gauranga's lila in the form of Sri Vamshivadana. This is not an object turned
into a man but the descent of an eternal associate: the one who sounds on Krishna's lips in Goloka came to Navadvipa as a
devotee and servant of the Lord's family.[^2] <!-- F2 Q1 I1 -->

The “Vaishnava-vandanas” remember the same. Sri Jiva in the Sanskrit “Vandana” praises “Krishna's flute — Vamshivadana
Thakura” (v. 120); the “second” Vrindavana Dasa sings: “I shall praise the feet of Thakura Vamshivadana, who formerly was
the flute at Krishna's lips” (v. 112); the “Sri Vaishnavabhidhana” calls him “Sri Vamshivadana Dasa, who revealed the song
of the flute” (v. 32); and Devakinandana says, enigmatically: “Gadadhara Dasa made [him] a descent of the flute”
(v. 86).[^3] And Narahari Chakravarti, opening the “Bhakti-ratnakara,” praises him too among the Lord's companions: “Glory
to Abhirama, Vamshi, Saranga and Sundara!” (1.29). <!-- F3 F44 -->

Krishnadasa Kaviraja in the “Chaitanya-charitamrita” does not name him; he tells only of the house of Madhava Dasa in
Kuliya, where the Lord stayed for a week — the house of his father. All that we know of Vamshi has been preserved by the
“Gaura-ganoddesha-dipika,” the “Bhakti-ratnakara,” the “Prema-vilasa,” the “Narottama-vilasa,” the “Vaishnava-vandanas,”
his own songs and family traditions. <!-- F15 -->

## The Chattopadhyayas of Kuliya
Vamshi was born in Kuliya — on the far bank of the Ganga, opposite old Navadvipa, in Koladvipa, where the town of
Navadvipa stands today. Kuliya consisted of four quarters — Teghari, Benchi, Yara-Bedrapara and Chinedanga.[^4] According
to the “Shripata-paryatana,” Kuliya and neighbouring Paharpur were two villages where Vamshivadana, Kavidatta and Saranga
Thakura lived; when the villages grew and merged, the town came to be called Kulia-Paharpur. <!-- F8 F9 Q5 -->

He was a brahmana of the Chattopadhyaya family. Its founder is held to be the renowned Srikara Chattopadhyaya, who, by
tradition, came from Bilvagrama and settled in Benchiara, one of the quarters of Kuliya; among his descendants was
Yudhishthira Chattopadhyaya.[^5] Yudhishthira had three sons, and all three bore playful nicknames counted in cowries —
the small shells that served as petty coin: the eldest, Madhava Dasa, was Chhakari (“Six Cowries”), the second, Haridasa,
Tinkari (“Three Cowries”), and the youngest, Krishna-sampatti, Dukari (“Two Cowries”).[^6] Vamshi's father was Chhakari —
Madhava Dasa Chattopadhyaya; his mother was Sri Chandrakala Devi. <!-- F10 F11 F12 -->

He appeared on Madhu-purnima, the full moon of the month of Chaitra — according to some, in 1416 Shaka (1494), according
to others, in 1427 Shaka (1505).[^7] A family work, the “Vamshi-shiksha,” says that on that day, Madhu-purnima of 1416
Shaka, the whole world sings of the festival of Vamshi's appearance. Our calendar, the calendar of the Sri Chaitanya
Saraswat Math, marks “the Appearance Day of Srila Vamshivadananda Thakura” on the Purnima at the end of the month of
Vishnu, at Vasanta-rasa; in 2026 it fell on 2 April. <!-- F5 F6 Q4 -->

It is said that Sriman Mahaprabhu and Advaita Acharya Prabhu were present at his birth. This is a tradition, and the
primary sources are silent about it; one biography links it with the days when the Lord stayed in Madhava Dasa's house,
but the years do not allow it.[^8] <!-- F7 -->

## The Lord's Week in Madhava Dasa's House
Chhakari was devoted to Mahaprabhu with all his heart, and the Lord dearly loved his son, little Vamshi. <!-- F14 -->

Madhava Dasa's house entered the story of the Lord's pastimes. Five years after His sannyasa Mahaprabhu set out from Puri
for Bengal, wishing to see the Ganga and His mother, and visited Navadvipa. Krishnadasa Kaviraja relates:

“Then the Lord stayed in Vachaspati's house, and for fear of the crowds He went on to Kuliya. In Kuliya the son of Shachi
lodged in the house of Madhava Dasa, and countless millions of people had His darshana. He stayed there seven days and
delivered the people; in His own way He saved even all the offenders.” (CC, Madhya 16.207–209)

Kavi Karnapura in the ninth act of the “Chaitanya-chandrodaya” says that the Lord, after visiting Advaita's house, crossed
by boat to the far bank opposite Navadvipa, to the village of Kuliya, “to the house of Madhava Dasa,” and out of mercy to
the people of Navadvipa stayed there seven days. People thronged in such crowds that

“the fence of the strongest, thickest bamboos that Madhava put up at night, the press of the crowds by morning gradually
ground to dust” (“Chaitanya-chandrodaya,” 9.16).

The biographies add that in those days, in Chhakari's house, the Lord bestowed His mercy on the people of Navadvipa,
forgave all who had once offended Him, and instructed and delivered Devananda Pandita; according to one of them, this was
when the Lord passed through Kuliya on His journey through Bengal that ended at Kanai-natashala.[^9] <!-- F13 Q3 -->

## Guardian of Shachi and Vishnupriya
When Mahaprabhu took sannyasa and settled in Puri, Vamshivadana was appointed guardian and servant (*rakshaka-sevaka*) of
the Lord's mother, Shachi, and of His consort, Vishnupriya Devi. Together with him Ishana Thakura, the old servant in the
Lord's house, was blessed with this service.[^10] To serve the Lord's mother and consort in the days when He had left them
for sannyasa is a special fortune, and the biographies speak of it in just those terms: both were “blessed” with this
care. In serving them Vamshi served the Lord Himself, for service to those dear to Him is dearer to the Lord than service
to Himself. <!-- F16 I2 -->

The grief of Navadvipa after the Lord's departure Vamshi poured out in a song — the most famous of his padas. In it sounds
the separation of Shachi and Vishnupriya, mother-in-law and daughter-in-law, and of the poet himself, who was with them:

“No more shall I see on the broad brow the adornment of curls and tilaka, no more see on the golden lotus [of His face]
the dance of the wagtail eyes.

No more will He dance in Srivasa's house with His chataka-bird devotees, no more will He dance in His own house for us to
gaze at Him.

Will the two brothers, Nimai and Nitai, ever dance together again? I keep crying, ‘Nimai!’ — but Nimai is nowhere.

Pitiless Keshava Bharati came and brought down a thunderbolt on our heads. How, not seeing beautiful Gauranga, shall I
stay on in Nadia?

Who is there now to bring back my Gaura Raya? Hearing the weeping of mother-in-law and daughter-in-law, Vamshi rolls in
the dust.” (“Pada-kalpataru” 1855)

“Pitiless” here is no reviling of Keshava Bharati, the Lord's sannyasa-guru, through whom the Lord Himself chose to
manifest sannyasa, but the reproach of loving hearts from whom separation has taken Gaura. For, as the
“Chaitanya-bhagavata” shows (Madhya 28.154–159), the Lord Himself, choosing a pretext, whispered the mantra to Bharati and
so made him His guru: the sannyasa was His own will. This is no worldly despair but the *vipralambha* of eternal
associates, for whom the Lord, hidden from their eyes, becomes all the nearer to the heart. The song shows that Vamshi
lived close to Shachi and Vishnupriya then and heard their weeping.[^11] <!-- F40 Q8 -->

When Mahaprabhu had withdrawn from sight, and then Shachimata too had left this world, Vamshivadana remained in the
Lord's house and served Vishnupriya Devi and Ishana Thakura. He was a disciple (*shishya*) of Vishnupriya and lived in
Mayapur to serve her; he was renowned as her “exclusive vessel of mercy” (*ekanta kripa-patra*). One biography says that
after Mahaprabhu's withdrawal Vishnupriya took him as her servant and that he, as is told, became the first to receive her
mercy.[^12] <!-- F17 F18 -->

## The Meeting with Srinivasa
The fullest account of Vamshi is preserved in the “Bhakti-ratnakara.” The young Srinivasa, the future acharya, came to
Navadvipa to see Vishnupriya's feet, found the Lord's house and stood stock-still at the gate, looking about him like a
wooden doll. Then Vamshi saw him:

“Sri Vamshivadana, seeing him, without any introduction thought to himself, ‘This must be Srinivasa.’ Coming near, he
asked who he was; Srinivasa told him everything from beginning to end. Sri Vamshivadana embraced him and bathed Srinivasa
in his own tears. Srinivasa, falling to the ground, wishes to bow down, but Sri Thakura Vamshi will not release him from
his embrace. To inform Sri Ishvari, Mother Vishnupriya, Sri Vamshivadana went [to her] with care.” (“Bhakti-ratnakara,”
4.20–24)

Vamshi recognised Srinivasa though he had never seen him, and would not let him bow down — so does an eternal associate
greet one whom the Lord has already called His own. According to one biography, he blessed the youth, set him on his lap
and bathed his head with tears of love; through him Srinivasa was blessed with the darshana of Vishnupriya's feet as
well.[^13] <!-- F19 Q2 -->

And Vishnupriya at that very time was telling her maidservant of her dream: the Lord had come to her and told her to show
mercy to a young brahmana, Srinivasa, who would come that day. And then Vamshi announced:

“‘Srinivasa has come here from Nilachala.’ Hearing this, Ishvari wished to see him.” (“Bhakti-ratnakara,” 4.39–40)
<!-- F20 -->

The biographies tell of this meeting in different ways. According to one, Srinivasa found Vamshivadana in Mahaprabhu's
house and bowed at his feet; learning who stood before him, Vamshi was overjoyed, loudly called out the name of Sri
Chaitanya and asked Vishnupriya to grant the youth her darshana. At first she would not agree; only when Vamshi told her
how ardently Srinivasa longed for it, and begged her again and again, did she relent, and at her bidding Vamshi brought him
in. According to another, Vamshivadananda, seeing how Srinivasa was languishing in separation from Mahaprabhu, went to
Vishnupriya of his own accord and asked her to receive and bless him. <!-- F22 -->

The meeting is recalled with reference to the “Prema-vilasa” as well: when Srinivasa came to Navadvipa to see
Vishnupriya's feet, in Sri Mayapur he met her disciple Vamshivadana Dasa and received the blessing both of Vishnupriya and
of her disciple. In the “Prema-vilasa” Srinivasa, wasted by an eight-day fast in separation from Gadadhara Pandita, “met
Vamshivadana Dasa” (4.169), who asked him who he was and where he came from; but further on, in that book, it is Ishana
who looks after the youth.[^14] <!-- F21 Q7 -->

## Service of the Deities
Vamshi served the Lord not only in those dear to Him but in His Deities as well. From of old Sri Gopinatha, the Deity of
his forefathers, had resided in his family, and Vamshi worshipped Him. He himself served Sri Pranavallabha.[^15] And later,
at the wish of Vishnupriya Devi, he established the worship of the Deity of Sri Gauranga. This Deity is the Lord Himself,
for the Lord's name, Deity and svarupa are one; in serving Pranavallabha, Gopinatha and Gauranga, Vamshi served the Lord
Himself.[^16] <!-- F25 F26 F27 -->

After Vishnupriya Devi left this world, the service of the Deities was moved to Kuliya (Kulia-Paharpur).[^17] And when
Vamshi's descendants, taking shelter of the mercy of Sri Jahnava Mata, settled at the shripata of Baghnapara, the service
of the Deities in Kuliya passed to servitors (*sevaits*) from Malhana. <!-- F28 F29 -->

## Family
Vamshi lived as a householder; the biographies call this his *grihastha-lila* — the pastime of an eternal associate in
the role of a householder, not worldly family life. He had two sons — Sri Nityananda Dasa (also called Nitai Dasa) and Sri
Chaitanya Dasa. <!-- F23 F24 -->

Chaitanya Dasa was close to Sri Jahnava. When she set out for the great festival at Kheturi, he came to her with his
people:

“Sri Vamshivadana's son Sri Chaitanya Dasa at that moment came with his people to Prabhu. Seeing the feet of Sri Jahnava
Ishvari, he thought himself blessed; streams flowed from both his eyes.” (“Narottama-vilasa,” 6.221–222)

And at Kheturi, in the circle of her companions, “Sri Vamshivadana's son Sri Chaitanya Dasa ate with his people,
rejoicing” (“Narottama-vilasa,” 8.59). <!-- F38 -->

## Songs
Vamshivadana was a renowned *padakarta*, a composer of Vaishnava songs. His songs are especially sweet and gentle and full
of deep devotional feeling. He sang much of Krishna's pastimes: of the *dana-lila*, when Krishna levies a “toll” on the
gopis, of the *nauka-vilasa*, the pastimes on the boat, and of the *vana-vihara*, the rambles in the forest.
<!-- F4 F41 -->

He sang too of Gauranga's pastimes in Navadvipa. In one of his padas Gora, the son of Shachi, remembering how He herded
the cows, calls them by name; Nityananda sounds a call with His mouth, imitating a horn, Gauridasa and Abhirama come
running, and Gauranga appears in the dress of Natavara — and the poet concludes: “Says Vamshivadana: ‘Come, let us go to
Govardhana!’” (“Pada-kalpataru” 2564). Another pada sings of the eve of a kirtana festival in Advaita's house: Advaita
prepares the consecration of the mridanga, Nitai hands out garlands and sandal, Govinda plays the mridanga, Haridasa sings,
Srivasa holds the tune, Gora dances — “says Vamshi: ‘Cry out “Glory!”’” (“Pada-kalpataru” 26). Padas signed “Vamshidasa”
may not be his at all: that signature was used also by a later poet, a disciple of Srinivasa Acharya.[^18] <!-- F42 -->

Yet the most famous remains the song of separation after the Lord's sannyasa, given above: in it, the biographies say, he
expressed the grief of Mother Shachi — and in it his own sorrow can be heard as well. <!-- I3 -->

## Last Years
At the end of his life Vamshivadana Thakura settled in Bilvagrama, the village of his forefathers.[^19] The Bhattacharyas
of Bilvagrama are said to be his descendants; one biography writes that there he was renowned among scholars,
Bhattacharyas and pious people. <!-- F30 F31 -->

When, where and on what day Vamshivadana Thakura left this world the sources do not say; the day of his departure is not in
our calendar either. <!-- F32 -->

## Descendants
Chaitanya Dasa had two sons — Sri Ramachandra, who was also called Ramai Thakura, and Sri Shachinandana. Ramachandra was
asked for by Jahnava Mata, the shakti of Nityananda: she gave him initiation, kept him with her at Kharadaha and herself
taught him Vaishnava philosophy (the journal “Gaudiya,” 22.30–37).[^20] Thus the whole family of Vamshivadananda received
the mercy of Jahnava Thakurani. <!-- F33 F34 Q6 -->

Ramachandra, Vamshivadananda's grandson, found in Vraja, at Praskandana-tirtha, the Deities of Sri Rama and Sri Krishna and
installed Them at Baghnapara, in the Bardhaman district; They are called Sri Rama-Kanai. Praskandana-tirtha is a ghat on
the Yamuna not far from Dvadashaditya-tila. Tradition tells that when Krishna came out of the Yamuna after His struggle
with Kaliya and felt cold, the twelve Adityas rose all at once to warm Him; He grew hot, and His perspiration flowed into
the Yamuna — hence the name of the place: Praskandana. <!-- F35 F36 -->

Sri Ramachandra Gosvami himself was a brahmachari; the service of Rama and Krishna at Baghnapara he entrusted to his
younger brother, Shachinandana, and the sons and descendants of Sri Shachinandana Gosvami became the line of the Gosvamis
of Baghnapara. <!-- F37 -->

Ramachandra left a pada in which he laments that he was born only after Gauranga's departure and met none of His
companions, and calls himself Vamshi's grandson:

“The Lord's dear associate is Thakura Vamshivadana: I am his son's son.” (pada of Ramachandra, 24.4) <!-- F39 -->

Vamshivadana's life was described, by tradition, by his grandson Sri Vallabha Dasa in the “Vamshi-vilasa”; there are
accounts of him in the “Shripata-paryatana” and the “Bhakti-ratnakara” as well, and a short biography in the
“Gaudiya-vaishnava-abhidhana.”[^21] <!-- F43 -->

## Significance
Vamshivadana Thakura is Krishna's flute, the eternal companion of His lips, who appeared in Navadvipa to serve Gauranga in
those closest to Him. When the Lord took sannyasa, Vamshi remained as guardian of His mother and consort; when Shachi
departed, he stayed with Vishnupriya as her disciple and “the exclusive vessel of her mercy.” He met Srinivasa at the
Lord's house and brought him to Vishnupriya, established at her wish the service of the Deity of Gauranga, and poured out
the separation of Navadvipa in a song that is sung to this day. The flute that in Vraja sang for Krishna began in
Navadvipa to sing of Gauranga. <!-- F2 F16 F18 F40 I1 I2 I3 -->
