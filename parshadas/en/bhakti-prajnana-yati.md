# Srila Bhakti Prajnana Yati Gosvami Maharaja
*Шрила Бхакти Праджняна Яти Госвами Махарадж · @bhakti-prajnana-yati*

## The Beloved Disciple
Srila Bhakti Prajnana Yati Gosvami Maharaja was a disciple of Srila Bhakti Vilasa Tirtha Gosvami Maharaja, the beloved
one (*preshthajana*) of Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati Prabhupada, founder of Sri Chaitanya Math and the Gaudiya Maths.
The biography calls him his guru's "especially dear" and even "most dear" disciple. Before sannyasa he was called
Nandadulal, or Nandadulal Brahmachari; from 1970 his guru addresses him in letters as "Yati Maharaja," and later writers
call him "Srila Yati Gosvami Maharaja."[^1] He served as general secretary of Sri Chaitanya Math, and from 1990 until he
left this world he was its acharya. <!-- F1 F109 -->

His life belongs to the twentieth century and the beginning of the twenty-first, and neither the “Chaitanya-charitamrita”
nor any other primary source speaks of him: all we know comes from one of the biographies of the Gaudiya acharyas, from
his own recollections and from his guru's letters quoted there. The Vaishnava calendar of Sri Chaitanya Saraswat Math
does not list the days of his appearance and departure; the dates below follow the biography. <!-- F110 -->

## Family
He appeared in 1930 on the day of Nandotsava — the day after Janmashtami — in the village of Usha, near the village of
Balgona (Nityanandapur), in the Bardhaman district of West Bengal (Bhatar police station). The family was brahmin: his
grandfather, Sri Biharilal Banerjee, was known as a brahmin of very strict piety; his grandmother was Vindhyavasini Devi.
His father was Sri Kalipada Banerjee, his mother Srimati Vinarani (Binarani) Banerjee. Nandadulal was the youngest of
three sons — the elder two were Prahlada and Subala — and there were also twin daughters, Ekkari and Hara, both older
than he. According to the biography, the name Nandadulal was given him by his father.[^2] <!-- F2 F3 F4 F5 F6 F7 -->

His father was an independent man who bowed his head before no one, yet all his life he served the household Deities,
Narayana and Radha-Gopala: he cooked Gopala's bhoga himself and performed the puja himself. In worldly matters he was
strict, in spiritual life simple, and the children were rather afraid of him. Yet he honored the Holy Name so deeply
that when singers of the Name came to the village he would forget everything external and roll in the dust of the road.
From his mother the boy first heard of Prahlada and of Raghunatha Dasa Gosvami. Learning that fasting on Ekadashi and on
the Lord's appearance days brings *sukriti* — the pious merit that leads to the sight of God — he begged his mother to
let him fast with her, and she gladly agreed: the family kept Vaishnava customs. <!-- F8 F9 F22 -->

## Childhood and Schooling
His childhood passed in simple village surroundings. He studied at the primary school in Usha, then at the high school
in Arui (by other accounts, at the Shushum-dighi school); in 1946 he passed the matriculation examination and lived in
the hostel of Bardhaman Raj College. At college he took Sanskrit with honors, and in grammar he earned the *adya*,
*madhya* and *upadhi* degrees. He then wished to study Vedanta and for a time studied under the Ramakrishna Mission, but
he did not like the joint education of young men and women. <!-- F10 F11 -->

From an early age he loved to serve cows: even in his student years he spent his days overseeing the field work and the
herd, and this love did not fade to the end of his life. Like his father, he was deeply devoted to God: sitting down to
puja in the Deity room, he would say something to Them and weep. He saw in the Deity the Supreme Lord Himself, and
worship by the mere recitation of ordinary mantras did not satisfy him — he served with love and, without knowing why,
was bathed in tears. He composed songs about the Thakura himself and called to Him in them; from the very beginning
prasada was nectar to him. His mother helped him greatly on this path. <!-- F12 F13 F23 -->

Srila Bhaktisiddhanta wrote in the journal “Gaudiya” that one in whom the inclination to serve God lives by nature does
not need the austerity of *vaidhi-bhakti* — devotion by rules alone; the biography sees these words fulfilled in
Nandadulal's childhood. <!-- F24 Q3 I2 -->

## Sannyasis at the Door
When he was eleven or twelve, one or two tridandi sannyasis and several brahmacharis of Sri Chaitanya Math came to the
house. He never forgot how his parents received them. His father — a respected brahmin of a noble family, himself used
to giving others the dust of his feet — prostrated himself before them right in the dust of the road and sought to take
the dust of their feet. His mother, mistress of an orthodox Hindu household, who never went out into the street, left
the bhoga she was cooking for the Deity, came out onto the road and bowed to them with her whole body. And the boy,
without knowing why, began to weep and at once told his mother that he too wanted to become a sannyasi. <!-- F14 -->

The sannyasis had brought two pictures. One showed Sri Sri Gauranga-Gandharva-Giridhari; the other, Srila Prabhupada
Bhaktisiddhanta seated on a chair, with his beloved disciple Kunja-da — Kunjavihari Vidyabhushana, later Srila Bhakti
Vilasa Tirtha Gosvami Maharaja — standing beside him. The boy thought, "What a pity I was not born in that time — I
would have seen him!" — and began to pray: "Shall I be able to give myself to the feet of Sri Kunja-da, whom you love so
much?" <!-- F15 -->

As they left the village, one of the sannyasis gave his father the “Sri Sri Chaitanya-charitamrita.” His father kept it
in the Deity room, allowed no one to touch it and read a portion every day, while Nandadulal, drawn by an unknown force,
sat outside by the door and listened. After his upanayana, spending the customary three days secluded at home, he took
the opportunity and read the book himself. <!-- F16 F17 -->

## The “Charitamrita” and the Holy Name
From then on the “Chaitanya-charitamrita” never let him go. He himself said that from the very beginning of his life
this book shaped his path, above all the lives of Haridasa Thakura and Raghunatha Dasa Gosvami: he wished to build his
own life on the same renunciation. He reread them from childhood and learned the whole book by heart, yet later too he
read it again and again and each time found something new and rejoiced. Sitting for hours over the “Charitamrita,” he
seemed to absorb its heroes; the worship of the household Deity and these readings changed his life to its foundations.
<!-- F18 F19 F23 -->

Reading of Haridasa Thakura, he was especially captivated by his words, spoken to the ruler who threatened him with
punishment and demanded that he recite the Quran:

"Even if my body is cut to pieces and my life departs, I will never give up chanting the Holy Name of the Lord."
(CB, Adi 16.94)

And by another prayer of the same Haridasa, addressed to Mahaprabhu before his departure:

“I shall hold Your lotus feet to my heart and gaze with my eyes upon Your moonlike face. With my tongue I shall utter
Your name, ‘Krishna Chaitanya.’ This is my desire — to give up my life in this way.”
(CC, Antya 11.33–34)

He copied these lines of the “Charitamrita” onto a sheet of paper and hung it in his room. Unshakable faith in the Holy Name ran through his
whole life: with it he accomplished much that seemed impossible, and to his last day no one could turn him from his goal.
<!-- F20 F21 Q1 Q2 -->

From childhood he also followed the instruction of Prahlada: "The wise should practice bhagavata-dharma here from early
childhood" (*kaumara acharet prajno dharman bhagavatan iha*, “Bhagavatam” 7.6.1). He held spiritual practice to be an
inescapable duty and understood early that in hari-bhajana none of one's relatives can help — one must walk alone; not
for nothing do the scriptures sternly call relatives "robbers who go by the name of kin" (*svajanakhya-dasyu*).
<!-- F28 Q4 Q5 -->

His childhood attraction to the “Charitamrita” — to the lives of Haridasa and Raghunatha Dasa — and to the Holy Name,
says the biography, determined everything that came after: his renunciation, his fidelity to the Name and his
fearlessness. <!-- I1 -->

## Longing for the Deity of the Heart
The older he grew, the more he longed for One he did not yet know. "Where is the Deity for whom this temple of the heart
was made?" he would ask, and lie awake at night: "Am I forsaken forever? Will You never take Your seat in my heart?" In
half-sleep a radiant sannyasi would appear to him; he would reach out to touch his feet — and the sannyasi would vanish,
and he, waking, would understand that it had been a dream. This happened many times. Once he saw himself following this
sannyasi across a rice field — and the next moment woke in his bed. <!-- F25 -->

Later he told of these years in his book “Amrite Sandhane” (“In Search of Nectar”), which he called "the lament of my
suffering soul" and published as a story of the search for Krishna. With the blossoming of youth, he writes, "the seed
of the creeper of bhakti" sprouted in the garden of the heart; "For whom have I adorned this throne of the heart? Will
the temple remain without its Deity?" — many such verses he composed then and offered to the Deity like flowers. In this
absorption he saw "a sannyasi-thakura walking with the name of Krishna," went to touch his feet — and the vision broke
off; once, at the sannyasi's sign, he followed him. But he could not yet discern his path and wept for a goal he did not
know. <!-- F26 -->

## What He Was Like
Already in his boyhood there appeared in him traits that later unfolded in full. He received brahmins and Vaishnavas as
dear guests and built a separate room for them — it stands to this day, as does the old cowshed. In his quarter he set up
a *hari-mandapa*, a pavilion for kirtana, and led everyone out in *nagara-sankirtana*, processions singing the Name
through the streets; under his supervision a *shivatala*, a place for the worship of Shiva, was also arranged; he himself
started the village festivals of Janmashtami, Dola and others. <!-- F27 -->

The biography counts ten such traits: love for sadhus and Vaishnavas; love for cows and fine service to them; deep
attachment to the “Charitamrita,” in which, with reverence and devotion, he saw Mahaprabhu Himself; an attraction to
worship — besides the household Deity he faithfully arranged Dola, Janmashtami, Nrisimha-chaturdashi and other festivals;
a gift for organizing — the guest house, the shivatala, the hari-mandapa; an irrepressible urge to compose songs and
kirtanas; love of Sanskrit; strong renunciation after the example of Haridasa Thakura and Raghunatha Dasa Gosvami;
complete dedication to the feet of his guru, for which his gurudeva came to love him especially; and, from childhood, a
philosophical turn of mind. <!-- F29 -->

Later there were added many books and articles in English and Bengali, a gift for poetry and kirtana, a deep knowledge
of the scriptures that let him resolve the hardest questions of people from every walk of life and refute false views,
great fearlessness, a special love for those who relied on him and were devoted to him, and an extraordinary capacity to
forgive. By nature he was above all a *bhajanandi* — one whose joy lies in solitary bhajana — yet in carrying out his
guru's orders he showed such a gift for organization that his guru himself wrote to him that his works "have made
history," that "no one could show such a thing before," that they "will be written in letters of gold." <!-- F30 F31 -->

Srila Bhakti Hridaya Bon Gosvami Maharaja — a godbrother of his guru, an eloquent preacher, a gifted organizer and a
profound scholar — also valued him. On 14 October 1978 he wrote to him: "…I tell you again and again: I love you with all
my heart and sincerely appreciate your many-sided qualities." <!-- F32 -->

## Leaving Home
In 1950, "in the footsteps of Srila Raghunatha Dasa Gosvami," he left home, not knowing where he was going, and ran
across the fields. Suddenly he saw a bright light ahead — the very sannyasi who had appeared to him before. After resting
a little on the green grass of a mango grove, he felt that the unknown place he was heading for was now very near.
<!-- F33 -->

Setting out again, toward evening near a town he saw three or four sannyasis in a horse carriage, and among them was the
one who had led him along this road. "We are coming from Sri Mayapur, the birthplace of Sriman Mahaprabhu," they said.
"Our ashram is there. If you like, go there; take a little money for the journey." He took none. "It is time for our
train; we shall see you in Mayapur" — and they drove away. And he reproached himself: "What a fool I am! The one I am
seeking, who will become my guide, called me, and I did not respond — and he has left me alone." <!-- F34 -->

Then he had to cross the Ganga, but the ferryman would not take him without payment, and he sat anxiously to one side
until a stranger helped him across. Later he recalled that the Ganga had seemed to him then the river Viraja, which
divides this world from the spiritual, and that his future teacher had wished to give him something, but he had been
unable to accept it. Walking a little farther, he came to Sri Yogapitha in Mayapur, and a sadhu there sent him on to the
mission's main seat — Sri Chaitanya Math. <!-- F35 F36 -->

The next morning, before mangala-arati, he saw the sadhus of the Math and recognized in them those who had come to his
home when he was eleven or twelve. He remembered his ardent prayer to Prabhupada and his beloved disciple, and the
wondrous form that had appeared to him near the station. There was only one difference: in the picture Prabhupada's
beloved disciple wore ordinary clothes and had well-kept black hair, whereas now before him stood a tridandi-svami with a
shaven head, in saffron, with a tridanda in his hand. Thus his new life began. <!-- F37 -->

In an interview he described these years as follows: he first came to Mayapur in 1950, but found neither the acharya
nor the president of the Math there and went off to the Ramakrishna Mission; he came to Mayapur a second time at the end
of 1951 — and then his gurudeva spent fully ten months teaching him Gaudiya Vaishnava philosophy, and in 1952 sent him
to Madras.[^3] <!-- F38 -->

Of his guru he said: "I have had occasion to meet many famous people of India — rishis, sannyasis, governors,
presidents — but no one so devoted, practical and disciplined as my gurudeva, Srila Bhakti Vilasa Tirtha Gosvami
Maharaja." Not one of his words ever had to be set aside as out of place; his life ran by the clock: he was never a
second late and never spoke a second longer than needed; until the age of eighty he spent eight to ten hours a day
discussing books. Such inspiration as he received from his gurudeva, he said, he received from no one else. <!-- F39 -->

## Sadhana
Inspired by the ideal of Srila Prabhupada Bhaktisiddhanta, Nandadulal Brahmachari remained a *naishthika-brahmachari* —
a lifelong celibate — all his life. He never departed from the *lakh* enjoined by Mahaprabhu — one hundred thousand
Holy Names a day; chanting the Name from midnight until morning was his ordinary practice, and the darshana of
mangala-arati an unfailing rule. <!-- F40 F41 -->

The biography sees the power of his japa in the way he shielded both himself and the Math through grave trials: there
was no lack of hardship and of lawsuits, yet no trial broke him and no suit ended in his defeat. The Holy Name was his
protection in everything. <!-- F42 I4 -->

## The Guru Revealed by Prabhupada
What his guru was in the eyes of Srila Bhaktisiddhanta himself the biography shows through Prabhupada's letter of 8
January 1921. In it, the biography says, Srila Prabhupada with great love revealed the hidden, eternal spiritual nature
of Kunjavihari Vidyabhushana — the future Srila Bhakti Vilasa Tirtha — and his own. The letter was written in deep
sorrow: on 18 May 1920 Kunjavihari, without a word to his guru, had left for Basra.[^4] <!-- F43 F44 Q6 -->

Yati Maharaja himself touched on this hidden side of service in his book “He Krishna! Vede ki tomar sthan nai?” (“O
Krishna! Is there no place for You in the Vedas?”), where he showed "the art of sadhana and bhajana" through the story of
a maidservant named Madhavi. Madhavi gathers flowers in the garden and gives them to a friend who is her guru; that friend
weaves two garlands and passes them to her own friend and preceptress, who gives them to Sri Rupa-manjari, and
Rupa-manjari, at Lalita-sakhi's direction, places them on Sri Radha-Govinda. After the pastimes in the kunja, Madhavi and
her preceptress are sent to Radha-kunda to prepare a bed of flowers for the midday pastimes; on the way Paurnamasi meets
them, sends the preceptress with Vrinda to tidy the kunja, and keeps Madhavi with her to teach her service — and soon
Madhavi dances and sings before her in the rhythm of *dhruvapada*, so that Paurnamasi is enchanted. <!-- F45 -->

Then Paurnamasi reveals a secret to her: the same Radha and Govinda, Rasaraja and Mahabhava in one, eternally abide as
Sri Krishna Chaitanya, the embodiment of generosity. By the grace of one of His dear companions Madhavi has attained her
eternal existence as a manjari and must serve Him too when He appears; but in Gaura-lila He does not reveal Himself as
the *nagara*, the Beloved, and so she will have to take a male form.[^5] Her parents, though she was born a girl, called
her "Madhu," and in Gaura-lila, by Paurnamasi's blessing, she will be known by that name. Radha-Krishna and Sri Krishna
Chaitanya dwell in different chambers of Goloka, which on earth is manifest as Vraja-dhama and as Shvetadvipa — Sri
Navadvipa-Mayapur. <!-- F46 -->

And the next moment Madhu sees herself in the pastimes of Sri Chaitanya: with the devotees she sweeps and washes the
courtyard of the Gundicha temple; then in the Nrisimha temple she dances and sings in the dhruvapada rhythm together with
Gopala, the son of Advaita, and draws the gaze of Mahaprabhu and Svarupa Damodara; the following day, at the Ratha-yatra,
at Mahaprabhu's bidding she begins a wondrous dance and song, delighting Jagannatha and all around; and a moment later
she is at work in a wondrous flower garden in Sri Mayapur. This is not a story about himself but an example of how he
taught remembrance of the Lord's pastimes in the footsteps of Sri Rupa. <!-- F47 -->

## The Pilgrim
A philosophical outlook is evident in all his books, his talks about Hari and his pilgrimages. The chief aim of
pilgrimage, he said, is the good of the soul, not the wish to gratify the senses. In his travels it seemed to him that
nature herself, in all her forms, had dressed like a devotee going out to worship the Lord. Holy places and nature
revealed the Lord's pastimes to him, and pilgrimage was his sadhana. <!-- F48 I5 -->

Before a journey to Badri-Narayana a storm broke, and it reminded him of the storm on the night when Sri Krishna appeared
in Kamsa's prison. On the way, the murmur of the Alakananda, the cool Himalayan air and the roar of waterfalls awakened
feelings in him that he expressed in a letter to his guru. At dawn, as the sun's rays fell on the snowy peaks, he
thought: perhaps Gandharvika, like molten gold, will reveal Her transcendental form to him — with this longing he, "a
fallen servant," was making his pilgrimage. But Her service can be attained only by the blessing of the best of
devotees — Kedarnatha, that is, Shiva:[^6] it was he who anointed his ignorant eyes with the salve of knowledge and turned
him toward Krishna, and only by causeless grace can the true image of the intimate Gandharvika enter our hearts. "With
this longing we have set out on the great road — to dive into the ocean of nectar." One sees, the biography remarks, how
constantly he was absorbed in the mood of the soul. <!-- F49 -->

The Mandakini seemed to him the stream of Bhakti-devi. In a light doze a wondrous person from an unknown world gave him a
message and disappeared, and he hoped to meet that person again. At Vishnuprayaga, at the confluence of the Dhauli and
the Alakananda, as he sang kirtana alone in time with the waves, it seemed to him that Sri Gauranga, the source of all
avataras, and Nitai, the giver of prema, were dancing and singing. The Alakananda at the feet of Badrinatha, he wrote,
carries a stream of prema even into a dry heart. He imagined himself a maidservant who gathers flowers, weaves a garland
and waits with a joyful smile to offer it to the Lord through one of His intimates: if the Lord accepts it, she will be
blessed. <!-- F50 -->

A question occupied him: is Badrinatha, served by the great devotees Uddhava and Narada, Narayana, in whom majesty
predominates, or a form of sweet pastimes? But out of humility he did not discuss it aloud. He remembered that Uddhava
longed to be born as a bush or creeper in Vraja, but Sri Krishnachandra sent him to Sri Badrinatha, where he eternally
renders confidential service; and nearby, at Samyaprasa, Vyasadeva, the foremost of those who relish rasa, drank the rasa
of the “Bhagavata” from Narada's lips and composed the “Bhagavata.” <!-- F51 -->

Thirst for the rasa of bhakti brought him along the same road to Badri again within the same year: he wished to bring up
a jewel from the deep darkness of solitude, as a diver brings one up from the bottom of the ocean. In May, seeing that the
trees on the slopes of the Vindhya hills had not withered, he reflected: for one who has made the mercy of guru and
Vaishnavas his support the Lord Himself takes care — if only there is a little moist soil where bhakti can live. The road
to Badrinatha begins at Haridvara, "the place of Shiva": only by the grace of Shiva, the Lord's devotee, can one bathe
even once in the Mandakini of bhakti and awaken love for Sri Hari. <!-- F52 -->

On this second pilgrimage to Kedara and Badri, constantly remembering the Lord's pastimes, he realized that the Deity of
Sri Badrinatha manifests the mood of separation (*vipralambha*); that is why it was here, at Samyaprasa, that Vyasadeva
quenched the thirst of his heart by composing the “Srimad-Bhagavatam.” <!-- F53 -->

He also explained why the road from Kedarnatha to Badrinatha is so long. Formerly, he said, they were less than
forty-five kilometers apart, but now Mount Nilakantha has risen between them, and the road has become about two hundred.
In earlier yugas devotees came to Kedarnatha, saw in him their guru and with his blessing went on to Badrinatha. But when
in Kali-yuga people bereft of divine knowledge, under the strong influence of Mayavada, began to proclaim "*so 'ham*" and
"*shivo 'ham*" — "I am He," "I am Shiva" — Kedarnatha, the best of Vaishnavas, remained there in a form that leads them
astray, and so that "shivo 'ham" should not reach the ears of Badrinatha's devotees, he raised Mount Nilakantha as a great
barrier between the material and the transcendental. So he interpreted the mountain: not as geography, but as a lesson
about Shiva's service in turning the Mayavadis away from the Lord. <!-- F54 -->

There are many such examples in his books, says the biography; his descriptions of the world of bhava clearly show that
his mind always dwelt there. <!-- F55 -->

## "This Judgment Is Wrong"
He himself was not drawn to missionary affairs. On 25 July 1970 he wrote frankly to his guru: "I have never had any
interest in missionary work, nor do I have any now." The next day, 26 July, Srila Bhakti Vilasa Tirtha replied: "Yati
Maharaja, this judgment is wrong," and explained much to him. <!-- F56 -->

In the preface to “Amrite Sandhane” he admits that the missionary temperament is in many ways at odds with his own ideal
of inner practice, and that the idea of the book took shape amid the extreme difficulties of the mission, when
missionary life clashed with his personal bhajana: "This book is the *krishnanushilana* of my soul" — the cultivation of
Krishna in the depths of the heart. And yet, taking his gurudeva's word upon his head, he showed extraordinary skill and
many-sided organizing power in the mission's work: his labors, as his guru wrote to him more than once, not only "made
history" but surpassed all previous "records." Obedience to the guru proved higher for him than his own inclination, and
he accepted the mission with reverence as Prabhupada's blessing. <!-- F57 F58 I3 -->

## Madras and the All India Vaishnava Conferences
In 1952 his gurudeva sent him to Madras, and thanks to his abilities he soon became secretary of the Math there. While
Nandadulal Brahmachari was serving in the Madras Math, its silver jubilee was celebrated — from 27 August to 1 September
1956. On 22 November 1957 Srila Bhakti Vilasa Tirtha wrote to him that the way preaching had been organized in the South
was a wonderful event, and that this ideal of service would forever be written in letters of gold in Gaudiya history. <!-- F59 F74 F60 -->

His too was the idea of the All India Vaishnava Conference — a gathering of the heads of all the Vaishnava sampradayas for
the wide preaching of hari-bhakti; the biography calls it "his brainchild." Gurumaharaja gladly approved and blessed the
plan, and Nandadulal held such a conference four times. In preparing the first he was helped by Sri Narayana Dasa
Brahmachari of Sri Chaitanya Math, later Bhakti Sharana Narayana Maharaja, and by the learned pandita K. S. V. Shastri:
together with Shastri he traveled all over India, contacted every sampradaya and secured their willing participation.
The first conference, lasting four days, opened on 23 December 1961 and was an enormous success; Srila Bhakti Vilasa
Tirtha called it "a wondrous miracle."[^7] The second took place on 28–31 August 1964, the third on 1–5 September 1972,
the fourth in Sri Mayapur.[^8] <!-- F61 F62 F63 -->

On 14 August 1960, on Sri Krishna-jayanti, he brought the first President of India, Rajendra Prasad, to the festival at
the Madras Math. He brought Vice-President Sarvepalli Radhakrishnan to Calcutta to lay the foundation stone of the Sri
Chaitanya Research Institute — on 28 December 1958[^9] — and on 29 June 1964 Radhakrishnan, by then President, opened the
institute, again at his invitation. <!-- F64 F65 -->

## The Golden Jubilee and Prabhupada's Centenary
Before the golden jubilee of Sri Chaitanya Math, Srila Bhakti Vilasa Tirtha wrote to Nandadulal on 7 December 1968 that,
for his extraordinary moral strength, enterprise, courage and spiritual strength, Srila Prabhupada himself was leading
him to success. The jubilee lasted from 7 February to the beginning of March 1969,[^10] and on 19 March his guru wrote:
after enormous and long labors the jubilee had been brilliantly completed, his service had made new history in their
society, and his zeal had neither equal nor limit. <!-- F66 F67 -->

In a letter of 13 July 1970 his guru is already writing to "Sri Yati Maharaja" — by then Nandadulal Brahmachari had
apparently accepted sannyasa and the name Bhakti Prajnana Yati: in him there was an astonishing gift for planning and a keen mind able to
carry out plans with the feeling of bhakti; he saw no equal to him, no one in their Math had such perfection; in the
future an enormous responsibility for the mission would fall on him — and he should know that this was Prabhupada's
blessing, accept it with reverence and carry it with great care and caution; for the future of the mission the guru
relied above all on him. The prediction came true to the letter: in time he served with great skill as general
secretary of Sri Chaitanya Math and its Gaudiya Maths, and then as acharya — to the last day of his life.[^11]
<!-- F68 F69 -->

Under his direction the third All India Literary and Cultural Conference was held at the Tridandi Gaudiya Math in
Bhubaneshvara on 19–31 December 1971. <!-- F70 -->

The centenary of the founder, Srila Bhaktisiddhanta, was marked in 1973 by a religious conference in Calcutta and a
year-long program that began on 22 February 1973. From 7 May to 30 June a special train with five hundred pilgrims set
out from Howrah across all India: Bhubaneshvara, Puri, Kovvur, Guntur, Madras, Trivandrum, Kanyakumari, Srirangam,
Kanchipuram, Madras Beach, Bangalore, Tirupati, Bombay, Dvaraka, Porbandar, Prabhasa-tirtha, Nathdvara, Udaipur, Pushkar,
Jaipur, Agra, Vraja-mandala, Mathura, Delhi, Haridvara, Rishikesh, Badrinatha, Naimisharanya, Ayodhya, Prayaga, Varanasi,
Gaya and other places.[^12] On 3 June his guru wrote to him from the road: the great personality for whose service he had
dared this journey was showering blessings on him; his gurudeva was very pleased with his diligence; "divine power is
your motive power." Then, from 23 December 1973 to 4 January 1974, a religious conference was held in Kalighat Park in
Calcutta, attended by Vice-President G. S. Pathak, the Chief Minister of West Bengal Siddhartha Shankar Ray, many judges
of the Calcutta High Court, representatives of the Madhva and Sri sampradayas from the South, and many others.
<!-- F71 F72 F73 -->

His guru told him, "All the service you are doing is completely one with my way of thinking," and blessed him in
English: "You have opened a new chapter in the annals of Gaudiya Vaishnavism. Srila Prabhupad guides you." These words
of praise are a guru's blessings on his disciple, not a measure of worldly success. <!-- F75 F76 -->

## Preaching in Society
More than three decades in the Maths of the South brought him into contact with many eminent people and taught him to
live within society for a good purpose. The inspiration of a simple life, he said, he drew from his association with
S. Radhakrishnan, Rajendra Prasad, C. Rajagopalachari, M. Ananthasayanam Ayyangar, Morarji Desai, Prabhudas Patwari,
Giani Zail Singh, Shankar Dayal Sharma, B. Satyanarayana Reddy, Ram Kishan, B. D. Jatti, Vishvanatha Das, Hanuman Prasad
Poddar and others. He was general secretary of the "All India Vaishnava Samajam" and so knew the heads of the various
Vaishnava sampradayas closely; he was invited to speak at seminars and religious gatherings throughout the South, at
colleges, cultural and research centers, and universities. <!-- F77 F78 F79 -->

On 21–25 April 1982, by invitation, he spoke at the World Hindu Conference in Sri Lanka, and his addresses were
appreciated by the delegates of twenty-nine countries. From then on the press knew him well: his talks "The Scientific
Caste System,"[^13] "The Worship of Sri Murti" and "Hindus Are Not Idol-Worshippers" were widely reported by the
newspapers and radio of Sri Lanka and Tamil Nadu; he was the first to be invited to speak on Sri Lankan radio, and "The
Hindu" and the "Indian Express" in the South printed his Sunday talks. The wide expansion, renovation and improvement of
all the Maths of Sri Chaitanya Math drew general attention. <!-- F80 F81 -->

## After His Guru's Departure
On 10 September 1976 Srila Bhakti Vilasa Tirtha left this world. Until then he had praised all his disciple's further
programs and festivals as well — "this has never been done before," "they have made history," "they will be written in
letters of gold." After his departure the Vishva-vaishnava-raja-sabha ceased to meet, and preaching and building came to
a standstill for almost ten years. From 1986 Yati Maharaja again gave himself to them with tremendous zeal — and labored
so until his own departure. Not everything, says the biography, can be described in a short essay; here are only a few
examples. <!-- F82 F83 -->

The five-hundredth anniversary of Mahaprabhu's appearance was celebrated in various parts of India; at different times
the celebrations were attended by President Giani Zail Singh, Chief Justice Shankar Prasad Mitra, the Governor of West
Bengal B. D. Pande, Prime Minister Indira Gandhi, Karan Singh and others. Festivities were held at Nagarjuna University
(10 September 1984), at the Gita Bhavan in Tenali (15–17 September 1984), at Venkateshvara University (22–24 September
1984) and in many other places, and the concluding ones in Mayapur on 25–27 March 1986. For the anniversary the
Government of India issued a commemorative postage stamp on 12 March 1986; Sri Chaitanya Math held a seven-day fair, and
on 25 March Prime Minister Rajiv Gandhi attended the celebration. On 22 January 1987 President Giani Zail Singh laid the
foundation stone of the natya-mandira at Sri Yogapitha. <!-- F84 F85 F86 -->

From 29 June to 5 July 1990 the silver jubilee of the Sri Chaitanya Research Institute was celebrated. Chief Justice of
India Sabyasachi Mukherjee presided; among the participants were former Chief Minister Prafulla Chandra Sen, thirty-eight
professors of twenty-three universities from twelve states, fourteen judges of the Calcutta High Court, the former Chief
Minister of Punjab Ram Kishan, the former education minister Pratap Chandra Chandra and the vice-chancellors of several
universities. In March of the same year, on 9–11 March, the Vishva-vaishnava-raja-sabha was convened once more,[^14] and
on 15–22 April a large conference was held in Secunderabad and Hyderabad; a report on it — "A Gospel of love extended to
heart land of Deccan" — was printed in the English journal “The Gaudiya” (vol. XXXII, pp. 103–110). <!-- F87 F88 F89 -->

## Acharya of Sri Chaitanya Math
On 12 February 1990, the day of Vyasa-puja, the general secretary, Yati Maharaja, was elected acharya of Sri Chaitanya
Math and its Gaudiya Maths. When he came to Madras as acharya, he was received with a *purna-kumbha* — a full pot, as
saints are welcomed — and the Guntur Bar Association honored him at a special meeting. <!-- F90 -->

He traveled a great deal to preach. On 27–31 March 1991 he spoke at various places in Bangalore; on 17–23 June 1994 he
was there again, with twelve svamis from Madras, Guntur and Kovvur — each day a new place and a new theme; on 24 June he
went on to Hyderabad and Secunderabad, where the program continued until 27 June, and on 28 June a meeting on "The
Contribution of Sri Chaitanya Mahaprabhu" was held in Warangal. <!-- F91 -->

On 25–28 March 1994 the anniversary of the "Sri Navadvipa-dhama-pracharini-sabha" and the centenary of the appearance of
Srila Bhakti Vilasa Tirtha were celebrated. His guru's year-long centenary concluded on 24–28 October 1994 in the
natya-mandiras of Sri Chaitanya Math and Sri Yogapitha with a "National Conference on Religion and Spirituality":
professors, philosophers and expounders of the Vaishnava scriptures read papers. President Shankar Dayal Sharma agreed to
open the conference but could not come; it was opened by the Governor of Orissa, B. Satyanarayana Reddy, and chaired by
Justice Mukul Gopal Mukherjee of the Calcutta High Court. Professors of the universities of Delhi, Madras, Andhra
Pradesh, Osmania, North Bengal, Calcutta, Jadavpur, Kalyani, Bardhaman, Punjab and others responded; eleven professors of
Osmania staged the play "Bharata-Bharati," and the Calcutta theater "Girish Chandra Natya Samsad" — "Sri
Chaitanya-lila." The acharya and general secretary Yati Maharaja himself spoke in particular of what his guru had done
for Mayapur. <!-- F92 F93 -->

So that preaching would not be interrupted, every year he traveled to the annual festivals in Bhubaneshvara, Puri,
Basirhat, Siliguri and Bangladesh; he preached in Dhaka in 2000, 2008, 2009, 2012, 2014 and 2015. At the seventy-fifth
anniversary of Sri Ramananda Gaudiya Math in Kovvur he presided over the opening and spoke in detail about the purpose for
which the Math was founded and about the teachings of Sriman Mahaprabhu. On 28–30 November 2008 a Gaudiya Vaishnava
conference was held in Siliguri, and in 2016 he laid the foundation stone of the temple of Uttar Banga Gaudiya Math — a
branch of Sri Chaitanya Math — in Narayanpalli (Siliguri). <!-- F94 F95 F96 -->

In 2010, for the five-hundredth anniversary of Mahaprabhu's sannyasa, he presided over a meeting of the "People's Forum
for Chaitanya Mahaprabhu" at Sadhabali in Puri, before the Gundicha temple. On 14 July 2015, during the Godavari
Pushkaram, he took part in the Godavari-arati and a ceremonial reception in Kovvur. <!-- F97 F98 -->

## Books
In more than three decades in Madras he published in English, among others, these books: “The Renaissance of Gaudiya
Vaishnav Movement”; “Srimad Bhagabatam” in six volumes; “Twelve Essential Upanishads”; “Three Apostles of Gaudiya
Vaishnav Movement”; “Thus Spake Sri Chaitanya”; “Facets of Truth”; “Shandilya Bhakti Sutra”; “Naradiya Bhakti Sutra”;
“Sanatan Dharma”; “Srimad Bhagabad-Arka-Marichimala”; “Gita Darshan as Bhakti Yoga”; “Jaiva Dharma”; “Ontological &
Morphological Concepts of Lord Sri Chaitanya Mahaprabhu & His Mission”; “Brihat Bhagabatamritam”; “Sri Bhakti Sandarva”
was in preparation. In Bengali: “Upanishader itikatha,” “Amrite Sandhane,” “He Krishna! Vede ki tomar sthan nai?,” “Sri
Chaitanyadev o Sri Jagannathadever Nilachal o Sundarachal lila-lahari” and “Gurupreshther anusandhane” (“In Search of
the Beloved Guru”). Each of his books appeared in both English and Bengali. Many articles he edited in the English “The
Gaudiya” and the Bengali “Gaudiya” have been collected in a separate volume. <!-- F99 F100 F101 -->

He was also the editor of “Srila Bhaktivinoda Thakurer prabandhavali,” a collection of Srila Bhaktivinoda's articles
(Mayapur, Sri Chaitanya Math, first edition 26 November 2004), and the publisher of the second edition of Srila
Bhaktisiddhanta's “Sri Chaitanya-darshane” (Sri Chaitanya Math, 14 February 2009). <!-- F103 -->

By his mercy and inspiration, by his causeless blessing, one of the biographies of Mahaprabhu's associates and the
Gaudiya acharyas on which our work relies was compiled; the compiler dedicated his labor to him as the former acharya of
Sri Chaitanya Math, placing the book in his hands, and among his sources named his book “Lord Sri Chaitanya and His
Mission.” <!-- F102 F104 -->

## Departure
He himself said many times that from childhood he had given more importance to personal bhajana than to the affairs of
the mission. Toward the end of his life the detached (*virakta*) Yati Maharaja gradually withdrew into himself, into
bhajana. The beginning of this was that on 26 May 2013, for reasons of health, he laid down the duties of general
secretary, which he had carried since 16 May 1978. <!-- F105 F106 -->

He left this world on 18 January 2018, a Thursday, at the end of the night, about 3:45. Unnoticed by all, constantly
remembering the eightfold (*ashtakaliya*) pastimes of Sri Sri Radha-Krishna, he entered, in the biography's words, the
*nishanta-lila* — the pastimes of the hour before dawn — and the Gaudiya Vaishnava world was plunged into a deep ocean of
grief.[^15] The next day, 19 January, he was placed in samadhi on the bank of Sri Radha-kunda, very close to the
samadhi-mandira of his guru, Srila Bhakti Vilasa Tirtha; there his own samadhi-mandira has been built, with a spacious
natya-mandira.[^16] <!-- F107 F108 -->

## Significance
The life of Srila Bhakti Prajnana Yati Gosvami Maharaja — from the boy who wept over the “Charitamrita” and prayed to the
picture of Prabhupada's beloved disciple to the acharya of Sri Chaitanya Math — is bound up with his guru from beginning
to end: he sought him even before they met, carried out his will all his life, and his samadhi at Radha-kunda stands beside his guru's
samadhi-mandira.
Inwardly he remained a bhajanandi inspired by Haridasa and Raghunatha Dasa, while outwardly, at his guru's word, he became
an outstanding organizer of the mission — and saw in this not his own achievement but the blessing of Srila Prabhupada.
