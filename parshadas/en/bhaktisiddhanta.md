# Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati Gosvami Thakura Prabhupada
*Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati Gosvami Thakura Prabhupada · @bhaktisiddhanta*

## Srila Prabhupada
Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati Gosvami Thakura was the son of Srila Bhaktivinoda Thakura, the only disciple of Srila
Gaurakishora Dasa Babaji, and the founder of the Sri Chaitanya Math in Mayapur and of the Gaudiya Maths throughout
India and beyond. His disciples called him Srila Prabhupada; his contemporaries called him Sarasvati Thakura and Srila
Siddhanta Sarasvati, and on solemn occasions “Om Vishnupada Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati Gosvami Thakura” and “the
acharya of the Gaudiya Math.” When the Gaudiya Math announced his departure, it named him “Paramahamsa
Parivrajakacharya Om Vishnupada Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati Gosvami Prabhupada.” As a child he was called
Bimalaprasad (or Vimalaprasad). At initiation his guru gave him the name Sri Varshabhanavi-dayita Dasa — “the servant
of one dear to the Daughter of Vrishabhanu”; after taking sannyasa in 1918 he was known as Parivrajakacharya Sri
Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati, yet even then he sometimes signed himself by his earlier name, Varshabhanavi-dayita Dasa.
<!-- F1 F22 -->

## Appearance in Puri
He appeared in Puri, Nilachala-dhama, on Friday, 6 February 1874, at half past three in the afternoon. By the Bengali
reckoning it was the 25th of Magha 1280 (year 1795 of the Shaka era), the krishna-panchami of the month of Magha; by
our calendar (Sri Chaitanya Saraswat Math) it is the krishna-panchami of Govinda-masa, the day on which his appearance
is celebrated. The house where he appeared was called “Narayana-chhata”: Bhaktivinoda Thakura lived there, and it
stood on the Grand Road (Bada-danda) not far from the temple of Jagannatha. The Holy Name resounded in it ceaselessly.
The child’s mother was Sri Bhagavati Devi; he was the fourth child of the family. <!-- F2 F3 -->

The newborn shone with a golden complexion, and his body bore all thirty-two marks of a great personality
(*mahapurusha*). Across his shoulder lay a natural sign like a sacred thread: the umbilical cord was wound over the
shoulder just as a brahmana’s thread lies, and those who saw it marvelled. <!-- F4 F5 -->

One of the biographies sees the meaning of his coming in the very place of his appearance. In Purushottama-kshetra
Mahaprabhu had left the treasure of prema; appearing there, Srila Bhaktisiddhanta was to carry that treasure to the
place where the Lord Himself appeared — Sri Mayapur — to establish it in the principal math, the Sri Chaitanya Math,
and in its branches, and to distribute it to all without distinction of birth or caste. So an eternal associate of the
Lord descends into the world not by the law of karma but for the work of his Master. <!-- F6 I1 -->

The name “Bimalaprasad” means “the grace of Vimala.” Vimala Devi is the internal energy (*para-shakti*) of Lord
Jagannatha, and the child’s first solid food (*anna-prashana*) was prasad[^1]. <!-- F7 -->

Six months later, during the Ratha-yatra, Jagannatha’s chariot stopped on the Grand Road — in front of Bhaktivinoda’s
house. At the Thakura’s word Bhagavati Devi carried the infant to the chariot; he stretched out his hands towards
Jagannatha’s feet, and the garland from the Lord’s neck fell upon his head. The panda priests foretold that the boy
would become a great personality[^2]. <!-- F8 -->

He spent the first ten months of his life in Purushottama-dhama and then travelled overland with his mother, in a
palanquin, to Ranaghat in Bengal. His whole childhood passed amid a ceaseless festival of harinama-sankirtana. <!-- F9 -->

## Childhood in Bhaktivinoda’s Home
From his earliest years he listened to the “Bhagavata” from his father’s lips, attentively and with deep absorption.
Bhaktivinoda also read to him the “Chaitanya-shikshamrita,” which he was then writing. And when the family lived in
Shrirampur (Serampore), the Thakura, returning from Puri, gave his son — then in the seventh class — a tulasi mala
that had been worn by Jagannatha and initiated him into chanting the Holy Name and into the mantra of Nrisimha.
<!-- F10 F13 F11 -->

In 1881 Bhaktivinoda began building his house “Bhakti-bhavan” at Rambagan in Calcutta. While the foundations were being
dug, a small Deity of Kurma was found in the earth. The Thakura taught his seven-year-old son the rules of worship and
the mantra of Kurma, and from then on Bimalaprasad always wore tilaka and performed puja. In 1885 his father founded
the “Vaishnava Depository” press in Bhakti-bhavan, and in the same year his monthly “Sajjana-toshani” began to appear
again. The boy learned the printer’s craft, read proofs, and helped in every other way. <!-- F14 F15 -->

With his father he visited the shripatas of Mahaprabhu’s associates — Kulinagrama, Saptagrama and others — and there
heard the Thakura speak on the doctrine of the Holy Name. In that same year 1885, the 399th year since Mahaprabhu’s
appearance, Bhaktivinoda founded the “Vishva-vaishnava-sabha,” the World Vaishnava Association, in the house of
Ramagopal Basu on Krishna Singh Lane (now Bethune Row); the following year it arranged the celebration of the four
hundredth anniversary of the Lord’s appearance. Its members included Madangopal Gosvami, Nilakantha Gosvami, Bipin
Bihari Gosvami, Radhika Nath Gosvami and Shishir Kumar Ghosh. The boy went with his father to its weekly Sunday
meetings, carried his “Bhakti-rasamrita-sindhu” for him, and listened attentively. <!-- F16 F23 -->

He studied at the Shrirampur school, and while still in the fifth class he invented a simple shorthand, which he called
“Bicanto.” At home he did not go over his lessons but read Narottama Thakura’s “Prarthana” and “Prema-bhakti-chandrika,”
his father’s books and various philosophical shastras; yet he always answered his teachers’ questions at once and
correctly. In the upper classes he gave more time to astronomy (*jyotisha*) and the Vaishnava scriptures than to the
syllabus. His knowledge astonished his teachers of mathematics, astronomy and philosophy, and whoever argued with him
was defeated. He kept away from ill-behaved boys: the wish to avoid unholy company (*asat-sanga*) and to seek only the
company of saints is visible in him from his earliest years. <!-- F12 F17 F18 F19 -->

His gift for mathematics and jyotisha showed itself in the fifth class. He studied under Pandita Maheshachandra
Chudamani of Siyakhala (on the Tarakeshvar line), quickly mastered the casting of horoscopes, and later studied with
Sundar Lal Pandita of Aloya. Chudamani never ceased to wonder at his fifteen-year-old pupil. Thus the youth came to bear
the title “Siddhanta Sarasvati”[^3]. “Our Teachers” adds that by the age of seven he knew the whole “Bhagavad-gita” by
heart. <!-- F20 F21 F268 -->

## College and Tripura
While still a schoolboy he published the “Surya-siddhanta,” the “Bhakti-bhavana-panjika” and other books on astronomy.
In the afternoons he would go to Beadon Square in Calcutta to debate with students on religion, politics and much else;
in 1891 their circle took the name “The August Assembly,” and all its members vowed not to marry. People of every age
attended, including educated and respected men. <!-- F24 F25 -->

Having passed the entrance examination, in 1892 he entered the Sanskrit College in Calcutta. Instead of the syllabus he
systematically read every book in the library, and he studied the Vedas under Pandita Prithvidhara Sharma. At the
college he openly differed with the famous scholar of Kashi, Bapudeva Shastri, and with Professor Panchanana
Sahityacharya. He soon left the college, and later explained why in his autobiographical notes: had he gone on to
university, his relatives would certainly have married him off; but if he showed the world a “useless fool,” no one
would push him into such a life. He looked for an honest living that would demand the least labour, so that he could
live in devotion to the Lord. One of the biographies compares this with Mahaprabhu’s life: first the joys of learning
and debate, then only the chanting and preaching of the Name. <!-- F26 F28 F29 I2 -->

In 1895 he entered the service of the royal house of independent Tripura as assistant editor of the dynastic chronicle
“Raja-ratnakara,” and studied the books of the royal library. Already in July 1896 Bhaktivinoda had taken him to
Agartala to the Raja of Tripura, Birchandra Devavarma Manikya, and in the same year to Kurseong in the Himalayan
foothills. After Maharaja Virachandra’s death (11 December 1896) the new maharaja, Radha Kishor Manikya Bahadur,
entrusted him with teaching Sanskrit and Bengali to the heir, Vrajendra Kishor, and later sent him to Calcutta on
various commissions. When in 1905 he wished to leave service altogether, the maharaja granted him a pension equal to his
salary; he received it until 1908. <!-- F30 F31 F244 -->

## The Sarasvata Chatushpathi
In 1897 a Sanskrit school, the Sarasvata Chatushpathi, opened in Bhakti-bhavan. Many educated men came there to study
astronomy: Lala Hara Gauri Shankar, Dr. Ekendranath Ghosh, Satkori Chattopadhyaya Siddhanta-bhushana, Shyamlal
Gosvami — a scholar of Nityananda’s family — Sharat Chandra Jyotir-vinoda and others. From it appeared the monthlies
“Jyotirvid” and “Brihaspati” and ancient works on astronomy. In 1898, while teaching Sanskrit in this school, he went
through the whole “Siddhanta-kaumudi” with the same Prithvidhara Sharma — quickly, from beginning to end. He was
advised to devote his life to grammar; he replied that human life is meant for the worship of the Supreme, not for
grammars with their roots and conjugations, nor for mundane poetry. <!-- F32 F27 -->

In Bhakti-bhavan Srila Jagannatha Dasa Babaji saw him and showered boundless love upon him. Learning of his knowledge
of astronomy, the Babaji told him to compile a Vaishnava calendar — and so the “Navadvipa-panjika” began[^4]. <!-- F247 -->

From 1897 to the end of his life he kept chaturmasya according to the Vaishnava shastras: he cooked his own *havishyanna*
— food without salt or spices — ate straight from the floor without a plate, and slept on the ground without a pillow.
<!-- F35 -->

## Pilgrimages and the Schools of the Acharyas
In October 1898 he followed his father on pilgrimage to Kashi and Prayaga, and on the way back to Gaya: one of the
biographies remarks that pilgrimage without following a Vaishnava brings only fatigue. In Kashi he discussed the
Ramanuja-sampradaya with Rama Mishra Shastri; at that time his spirit of renunciation clearly showed itself. What he
gathered in these places later went into his “Anubhashya” on the “Sri Chaitanya-charitamrita.” <!-- F33 F34 -->

He had begun studying Ramanuja’s school while still in Bengal. Earlier Sundareshvar Shrauti had brought him books in four
South Indian languages, and from them he learned the teachings of Ramanuja and Madhva; he published the fruits of his
study as books and articles, and in 1898 printed in the “Sajjana-toshani” a series of articles on Shrinatha Muni,
Yamunacharya and other acharyas of the Shri-sampradaya. In 1899 he wrote for the weekly “Nivedana,” and in 1900 he
published the book “Bange Samajikata” (“Social Customs in Bengal”), the fruit of extensive research. <!-- F36 F37 -->

## Gaurakishora’s Only Disciple
In 1897 Bhaktivinoda made himself a place for bhajana in Godrumadvipa of Navadvipa, on the bank of the Sarasvati river
— the Svananda-sukhada-kunja. Here in 1898 Sarasvati Thakura first saw Srila Gaurakishora Dasa Babaji Maharaja — a
paramahamsa indifferent to all worldly conventions — and was drawn to him with all his heart. He was captivated by the
Babaji’s heartfelt kirtana; one of his songs — on Raghunatha Dasa’s separation from Radha, “*kothay go premamayi radhe
radhe*” — he wrote down and later taught to his disciples. “Our Teachers” relates that at their very first meeting the
Babaji gave the young man his beads — knotted cords — a wooden tilaka stamp, and a cap and basket for worship which he
himself had received from his guru, Bhagavata Dasa Babaji. <!-- F38 F240 F269 -->

Gaurakishora had vowed not to accept disciples. Sarasvati Thakura asked for initiation more than once; the first time
the Babaji answered, “If Mahaprabhu tells me to, I will give it.” But seeing the young man’s determination and devotion,
he changed his mind. In the month of Magha 1900, with Bhaktivinoda’s permission — according to one of the biographies,
at his instruction — Sarasvati Thakura received *bhagavati-diksha* from Srila Gaurakishora Dasa Babaji and the name Sri
Varshabhanavi-dayita Dasa. He became Babaji Maharaja’s only disciple. <!-- F239 F39 -->

## Puri: Bhakti-kuti and the “Vaishnava-manjusha”
In March 1900 he accompanied his father to Baleshvar and Remuna, where they had darshana of Kshirachora Gopinatha, then
to Bhubaneshvar, Sakshi-Gopala and Puri; at that time his attachment to Puri grew strong. He wished to found a math by
the samadhi of Haridasa Thakura. The sub-registrar Jagabandhu Pattanayak and other leading citizens asked him to take
charge of the service of one of the seven thrones of the Satasan Math — the Deity Giridhari. And in 1902 Bhaktivinoda
himself began building a house near Haridasa’s samadhi and named it “Bhakti-kuti.” <!-- F40 F41 -->

Here Sarasvati Thakura constantly read and explained the “Chaitanya-charitamrita” in his father’s presence — especially
in 1903. Nearby, in a tent on the seashore, lived the Maharaja of Cossimbazar, Manindra Chandra Nandi Bahadur, grieving
the loss of one dear to him, and he often came to hear Bhaktivinoda and his son. The famous Radharaman Charan Dasa Babaji
also visited the Thakura; Sarasvati Thakura, on the basis of the shastras, showed that this babaji’s conduct and teaching
departed from the genuine tradition. <!-- F42 F244 -->

In Puri he gathered material for the “Vaishnava-manjusha.” He conversed with the abbot of the Govardhana Math,
Madhusudana Tirtha, who held him in high esteem, and discussed the shastras with Vasudeva Ramanuja Dasa and Damodara
Ramanuja Dasa (Samadhi Math), Raghunandana Ramanuja Dasa (Emar Math), Jagannatha Dasa (Papudiya Math, of the Jamayat
Ramanandi sampradaya), Omkara Japi Vriddha Tapas (Svargadvara-chhata), the scholar Sadashiva Mishra, the advocate
Harishchandra Basu, Bihari Dasa Pujari (the math of Gangamata), the abbot of the Radhakanta Math Narottama Dasa,
Ananta Charan Mahanti and many others. <!-- F43 -->

The “Vaishnava-manjusha” was conceived as a Vaishnava encyclopaedia. Bhaktivinoda and Shishir Kumar Ghosh asked him to
undertake it, and for its sake he travelled all over India. It was to contain explanations of Vaishnava terms, short
lives of the mahajanas, descriptions of the acharyas’ works and of Vaishnava tirthas, the doctrines of the various
sampradayas, and the technical, philosophical and difficult words of Vaishnava books — with sources, sense and
significance. The late Sri Haridasa Dasa Babaji of the “Haribol Kutir” in Navadvipa warmly praised the preparation of
this material, and afterwards himself compiled and published the “Gaudiya-vaishnava-abhidhana.” <!-- F44 -->

Preaching from house to house among the leading people of Puri, Sarasvati Thakura met with many difficulties and even
dangers; nor was his service of Giridhari at the Satasan Math easy. Like Prahlada he stood firm and paid no heed to
abuse. Then Bhaktivinoda advised him to go to Mayapur and perform bhajana there in seclusion — as Ramanuja once withdrew
to Tirunarayanapuram. <!-- F45 -->

## Mayapur and the Vow of the Name
In Navadvipa Bhaktivinoda introduced him to Vamshidasa Babaji. Sarasvati Thakura was drawn by this babaji’s Vaishnava
conduct and his indifference to worldly rules; he himself would bow to him from a distance, but he did not allow his
disciples to associate with him: for a beginner, imitating a paramahamsa is dangerous. Soon Charan Dasa Babaji, with
Vishnu Dasa of Kalna and many others, went with Sarasvati Thakura in kirtana to Mayapur, the place of Mahaprabhu’s
appearance, for the festival of Dola-purnima. The following year Charan Dasa Babaji announced to Bhaktivinoda that he
wished to lead a kirtana party around Navadvipa-dhama every year, but in 1906 he left this world before he could do so[^5].
<!-- F46 F249 -->

His learning had not left him. On 2 January 1902 he debated astronomy with a gifted pupil of Bapudeva Shastri, under the
chairmanship of Rai Bahadur Rajendra Chandra Shastri, president of the “Royal Society.” Sarasvati Thakura so routed his
opponent that the opponent’s teacher, overcome by shock, lost control of himself in the very assembly. <!-- F47 -->

In January 1904 he visited Sita-kunda and Chandranatha, in December Puri, and on 23 February 1905 he set out on pilgrimage
to the South: Simhachalam, Rajahmundry, Madras, Perumbudur, Tirupati, Kanchivaram, Kumbakonam, Shrirangam, Madurai and
other places; then he returned to Calcutta and Mayapur. In Perumbudur a tridandi of the Ramanuja-sampradaya explained to
him the rules of Vaishnava tridandi sannyasa. <!-- F48 -->

Having settled in Sri Mayapur and begun to preach, in 1905, following in the footsteps of Haridasa Thakura, he vowed to
chant a hundred crores of Holy Names — no fewer than three lakhs a day, that is, a hundred and ninety-two rounds on his
beads — and he fulfilled the vow[^6]. <!-- F49 F270 -->

In 1906 his first disciple came to him: Rohini Kumar Ghosh, a nephew of Judge Chandra Madhava Ghosh, had a wondrous dream
telling him to go to Sarasvati Thakura for initiation. <!-- F50 -->

In 1909, on the site of Chandrashekhara Acharya’s house, he built a hut called “Vraja-pattana” and continued his vow
there, contemplating himself on the bank of Radha-kunda. <!-- F51 -->

## Vaishnava and Brahmana
Three years before Bhaktivinoda’s departure, Sarasvati Thakura made a vow to his father: unworthy servant though he was,
he would take up the highest good — the overthrow of every teaching opposed to pure bhakti. The Thakura was delighted.
The occasion soon came. Descendants of Bengali acharyas, holding to smarta caste views, were humiliating and oppressing
Vaishnavas; the smarta brahmanas were jointly attacking the Vaishnava faith and its acharyas, and some of those who
called themselves descendants of Nityananda and other associates of Mahaprabhu had taken their side for gain. In 1911,
on 8–11 September, a great assembly of hundreds of scholars was convened at Balighai (Medinipur district); it was
chaired by Pandita Vishvambharananda Deva Gosvami of Gopivallabhapura, and Sri Madhusudana Gosvami Sarvabhauma of the
Radharamana enclosure in Vrindavana was also present. Bhaktivinoda was invited, but he was confined to bed and sent his
son in his place, together with Suresh Chandra Mukhopadhyaya. <!-- F245 F52 -->

First the smarta pandits spoke, each proving the superiority of his own view. Then Madhusudana Gosvami gave the floor to
Sarasvati Thakura. He read a paper and, in a long address that was the fruit of great research, set out the nature of
the brahmana and the Vaishnava and the difference between them, demolishing the arguments of the smarta karmis. The
assembly was enchanted and struck dumb; the smarta descendants of acharyas and the brahmanas were amazed, and all
acknowledged that the glory of the Vaishnava stands above that of the brahmana. The address later appeared as a book,
“Brahmana o Vaishnava.” A year later Madhusudana Gosvami, visiting Bhaktivinoda at Bhakti-bhavan, enthusiastically
declared his son a worthy successor to his father in defending the Gaudiya sampradaya. Sarasvati Thakura was defending
not one caste against another but the truth of the shastras: a devotee of the Lord, whatever his birth, stands above one
who is a brahmana by birth alone. <!-- F52 F245 -->

In that same year, 1911, a dispute arose at Bara-akhra in Navadvipa over the Gaura-mantra. On the basis of the
“Chaitanyopanishad” of the Atharva-veda and other shastras, Sarasvati Thakura proved the eternal validity of a distinct
mantra for the worship of Gauranga. <!-- F53 -->

## Against Imitation Devotion
He stood no less firmly against the *prakrita-sahajiyas* — those who pass off mundane feelings as the highest love of
God. “Our Teachers” relates that once a well-known sahajiya babaji came to Bhaktivinoda; the son came in, saw the guest,
bowed to his father from a distance and left, and the Thakura explained to the puzzled guest that the young man had
vowed never to come within a hundred paces of a sahajiya. Against their errors he wrote the poem
“Prakrita-rasa-shata-dushini.” He expressed his creed in a couplet that is now inscribed over the entrance to the
nat-mandira of the Sri Chaitanya Saraswat Math in Navadvipa:

*matala hari-jana kirttana-range*
*pujala raga-patha gaurava-bhange*

“We shall worship the path of raga-marga, of exclusive and selfless devotion to God, from afar, with the deepest
reverence, always performing kirtana in the company of true Vaishnavas”[^7]. <!-- F273 -->

On 23 March 1912, at a religious conference in Cossimbazar, in the palace of Maharaja Manindra Chandra Nandi, he spoke
for four days on shuddha-bhakti — on the marks of pure devotion and against the worldly pretensions and sycophancy of
the “leaders” of Gaudiya Vaishnavism of the day. Seeing around him so-called prakrita-sahajiyas with their show of
feeling, he ate nothing for all four days, and only on returning to Mayapur did he again take prasad. The maharaja,
learning of this, came to Mayapur, expressed his sorrow and long begged forgiveness. Sarasvati Thakura said: “Having
judged that there was no bhakti there, one should not eat in a common place”[^8]. <!-- F54 -->

On 4 November 1912, with a small party of devotees, he set out for the places of Mahaprabhu’s associates — Shrikhanda,
Yajigrama, Katwa, Jhamatpur, Akai-hat, Chakhandi, Daihat and others — preaching pure devotion everywhere. <!-- F55 -->

## The “Anubhashya”
In 1913 he began writing a commentary on the “Chaitanya-charitamrita,” the “Anubhashya,” conceived as a companion to
Bhaktivinoda’s “Amrita-pravaha-bhashya,” and read passages of it to his father. In April 1913 he founded the
“Bhagavata Yantralaya” press; its first book was the “Chaitanya-charitamrita” with the “Anubhashya,” followed by the
“Bhagavad-gita” with Vishvanatha Chakravarti’s commentary and the mahakavya “Gaura-krishnodaya” of the Oriya poet
Govinda Deva of Vakreshvara’s line[^9]. <!-- F246 F56 F257 -->

Bhaktivinoda Thakura left this world on 23 June 1914, and his son became editor of the monthly “Sajjana-toshani” that
he had founded. In January 1915 the press was moved to Vraja-pattana in Mayapur; in March the journal appeared again;
on 14 June the “Anubhashya” was completed there; and in July the press moved to Krishnanagar, from where the journal and
Bhaktivinoda’s books were issued. <!-- F57 -->

He wrote at that time in the “Sajjana-toshani” that the journal’s aim was unchanged: the Thakura had entered the
eternal pastimes (*nitya-lila*), but the journal would serve as before. Some, he wrote, set aside pure devotion for the
sake of a supposed bhakti; others take as their standard the easy path of mundane “devotees.” And he quoted
Bhaktivinoda’s lines from the “Kalyana-kalpataru” beginning *bhakti-badha jaha haite*: on the head of learning that
obstructs bhakti — a kick without guile; Sarasvati, dear to Krishna, is full of devotion to Krishna — and that is
Vinoda’s wealth. The verse rejects not learning as such but only the knowledge that eclipses bhakti. “Service to Gaura’s
teaching in following the guru is the same as service to Radha-Govinda… following Sri Rupa Manjari,” he wrote; “be a
servant of the followers of Svarupa and Rupa and always sing the Name.” <!-- F238 Q6 -->

## The Guru’s Departure
On 17 November 1915, on utthana-ekadashi, Srila Gaurakishora Dasa Babaji entered the eternal pastimes at Kulia, in the
Rani Dharmashala. Overwhelmed by separation, Sarasvati Thakura hurried there. The mahantas of various akharas were
disputing where and how to give the body samadhi: each wanted the future temple, and the income that would come with
it. He alone stood against them. The police chief of Navadvipa, Jatindranath Singh Rai Bahadur, arrived. The babajis
argued that he was not yet a sannyasi and had no right to perform the rite. Sarasvati Thakura replied that he was
Babaji Maharaja’s only disciple, and said: let him touch the body who among them had been pure with regard to women for
even the last six months, three months, a month or three days — for one who was impure that touch would be ruinous. On
the 1st of Agrahayana he performed the rite according to Vaishnava smriti, the “Samskara-dipika” of Gopala Bhatta
Gosvami, and established his guru’s samadhi at Nutan-chora in old Kulia (now the town of Navadvipa). His sternness
was the protection of his guru’s sanctity, not enmity towards people. <!-- F58 F241 -->

After the departure of his father and his guru, a deep despondency came over him: how could he fulfil their command to
preach when spiritual life around him had fallen so low? “Our Teachers” relates that one night Sri Gaurasundara
appeared to him in a sankirtana procession from the Yogapitha, with the six Gosvamis, Jagannatha Dasa Babaji,
Bhaktivinoda and Gaurakishora; they told him to abandon his doubts and preach, promising their constant support. This
despondency, “Our Teachers” says, was not weakness but part of an inconceivable lila. <!-- F271 -->

## Sannyasa
In 1918, on the day of Mahaprabhu’s appearance, Gaura-purnima, he took tridandi sannyasa at Vraja-pattana in Sri
Mayapur[^10]. “Our Teachers” describes the day: having bathed in the Ganga and put on saffron cloth, he bowed before
the pictures of his two gurus — Bhaktivinoda and Gaurakishora — and took the vows of the tridanda: to dedicate body,
mind and speech to the Lord’s service. He took the principle of tridandi sannyasa from the “Bhagavatam” (11.23.57) and
from the example of the Ramanuja-sampradaya, whose rules he had once learned in Perumbudur. From that day he was known
as Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati. <!-- F59 F272 -->

To one eternally perfect (*nitya-siddha*) renunciation was not needed: its goal he already possessed. But he accepted
sannyasa in order to preach the Lord’s teaching everywhere without hindrance, to establish *daiva-varnashrama* — an
order of life whose centre is service to the Lord — and to show the transcendental nature of the paramahamsa-babaji dress
worn by his preceptors: Jagannatha Dasa, Sachchidananda Bhaktivinoda Thakura and Gaurakishora Dasa Babaji. Having
accepted sannyasa himself, he then introduced it among his disciples, and was the first in the Gaudiya Vaishnava
sampradaya to do so[^11]; and from that day the mission went swiftly forward. <!-- F60 I3 -->

On the same day he installed the Deities of Guru-Gauranga and Radha-Govinda in the house of Chandrashekhara Acharya[^12]
and founded the Sri Chaitanya Math — the principal math of all the Gaudiya Maths in the world and the head office of the
mission; the Sri Chaitanya Math still stands on the site of Chandrashekhara’s house. <!-- F61 F256 -->

## A Sannyasi’s First Journeys
At the end of March 1918 he gave a full lecture on Vaishnava philosophy in the Krishnanagar town hall, and in May he
spoke hari-katha at Daulatpur and other places. On 2 June he set out for Puri with devotees from Calcutta, preaching on
the way — at Sauri, Kuamara and elsewhere. Walking Mahaprabhu’s road, he was overcome by separation from the Lord. At
Remuna he bowed to Gopinatha; at Baleshvar he lectured on the “Shikshashtaka” at the “Hari-bhakti-pradayini Sabha,”
where the sub-divisional magistrate Rai Saheb Gaura Shyama Mahanti and others received him; in Cuttack he was the guest
of Dewan Bahadur Sri Krishna Mahapatra and expounded the “Bhagavata.” In Puri they stayed at Bhakti-kuti and walked
around Sri Kshetra-mandala, and all the while he remained in the mood of separation. <!-- F62 F63 -->

In Puri the former collector and deputy magistrate Atal Bihari Maitra heard his explanations of the
“Chaitanya-charitamrita” and the “Bhagavata.” At a large gathering in the courtyard of the house of Hariballabha Basu Rai
Bahadur (“Shashi-bhavana”) he lectured on the difference between the personal and impersonal understanding of the
Absolute Truth. At that time he also composed several Sanskrit verses on the memorial of Mahaprabhu’s footprints in the
temple of Jagannatha. <!-- F64 -->

In August–September 1918 the spokesman of a circle of atheists sent him twenty-nine questions against the Vaishnava
faith and its acharyas. He answered with reason and shastra; the questions and answers appeared as a booklet,
“Pratipera Prashnera Pratyuttara” (“Reply to an Opponent’s Questions”). <!-- F65 -->

## Bhaktivinoda Asana
So that more people could hear from his lips the incomparably powerful word of Sri Chaitanya, in November 1918 he opened
a centre in Calcutta. At his instruction Kunjavihari Vidyabhushana rented the house at No. 1 Ultadanga Junction Road,
near the Pareshnath temple, for fifty rupees a month, and at Kunja Babu’s wish the centre was named “Sri Bhaktivinoda
Asana.” It soon became a splendid centre of preaching: the foremost scholars of the city and beyond came to hear
Sarasvati Thakura’s hari-katha, and his addresses were printed by Sri Motilal Ghosh in the “Amrita Bazar Patrika” and
by Sri Piyush Kanti Ghosh in the “Bengalee.” From here he travelled to preach in the districts of Jessore and Khulna.
Soon several highly educated young men answered his call and dedicated their lives to the mission. <!-- F66 F265 F67 -->

On 5 February 1919, the appearance day of Srimati Vishnupriya, he revived here with kirtana the World Vaishnava
Association — now under the name “Vishva-vaishnava-raja-sabha,” the name of the assembly once begun by Sri Rupa and
Sanatana. On 27 June 1919 he installed the image of Srila Bhaktivinoda Thakura in the Svananda-sukhada-kunja in
Godrumadvipa, and from 18 August to 18 September the first month-long kirtana festival was held at the Bhaktivinoda
Asana. On 4 October, the appearance day of Madhvacharya, he began preaching in northern and eastern Bengal. In April
1920, together with other leaders of the “Vishva-vaishnava-raja-sabha,” he sent the annual conference of Vaishnava
scholars held by the Maharaja of Cossimbazar a letter with seven questions — a challenge to explain the difference
between pure and mixed devotion. <!-- F68 F248 F69 F70 -->

On 23 June 1920, exactly six years after Bhaktivinoda’s departure, his mother, Bhagavati Devi, departed for the eternal
abode. <!-- F71 -->

On 6 September 1920 the Deities of Guru-Gauranga and Radha-Govinda were installed at the Bhaktivinoda Asana: so arose the
first Sri Gaudiya Math — the first branch of the Sri Chaitanya Math. In October Maharaja Sir Manindra Chandra Nandi
Bahadur invited him to Cossimbazar, and he told him of his plan for a Vaishnava encyclopaedia; the maharaja promised a
monthly grant towards its publication but did not keep his promise. Sarasvati Thakura then preached at Saidabad,
Novallis-para, Kheturi and other places of Mahaprabhu’s associates. <!-- F72 F73 -->

On 1 November 1920 he gave tridandi sannyasa to a disciple of Bhaktivinoda, Jagadisha Bhakti Pradipa, B.A., who bore the
titles “Vaishnava-siddhanta-bhushana” and “Sampradaya-vaibhavacharya.” So the “Vishva-vaishnava-raja-sabha” gained its
first sannyasi — Tridandi Svami Bhakti Pradipa Tirtha Maharaja. On 14 March 1921 Sarasvati Thakura revived the annual
parikrama of Navadvipa-dhama, and at the end of March he again preached in Puri. Bhakti Pradipa Tirtha then published
the book “Achara o Acharya” (“Conduct and the Spiritual Teacher”), which greatly agitated the caste gosvamis — for the
most part professional gurus. <!-- F74 F75 -->

## Maths throughout India
He then preached at Dhanbad, Katrasgarh and Dhaka. In Dhaka for a whole month he explained the first verse of the
“Bhagavata” — *janmady asya yato ’nvayat* — in thirty different ways. On 13 October 1921 he founded the Madhva Gaudiya
Math there, installed the Deities on 31 October with a great festival, and then visited Mymensingh. Returning to
Navadvipa, he restored the worship of Gaura-Gadadhara at Champahati, and at the birthplace of Vrindavana Dasa Thakura,
by the house of Sharngi Murari in Modadrumadvipa, he installed Deities and built a temple; then he preached in Calcutta
and its surroundings. <!-- F76 Q2 F77 F256 -->

Veda-vyasa says: *utkale purushottamat* — from Purushottama in Utkala the pure teaching of the Vaishnavas will spread
through the world. On 9 June 1922 Sarasvati Thakura founded the Sri Purushottama Math at Bhakti-kuti with the Deity of
Gauranga. Then, following Mahaprabhu’s example, he and his followers washed the Gundicha temple, walked around Puri, and
during the *anavasara* went to Alalanatha. At the Purushottama Math he established an annual festival in memory of
Bhaktivinoda and Gadadhara Pandita Gosvami, and sent disciples to preach in Cuttack, Baripada, Kuamara, Udala,
Kaptipada and Nilgiri. <!-- F78 Q1 -->

On 19 August 1922 the first issue of the weekly “Gaudiya,” the organ of the Gaudiya Math, came out from the “Bhagavata
Press”; its editor was Sri Haripada Vidyaratna[^13]. On 28 September of that year Sarasvati Thakura set out with his
disciples for Vrindavana to open a centre of preaching there: he visited Mathura, Vrindavana, Radha-kunda and other
places, and in the Lala Babu temple in Vrindavana he spoke on Mahaprabhu’s teaching at a gathering of learned
Vaishnavas. In the month of Karttika, returning to Bengal, he again spoke in Dhaka on the nature of bhakti, then revealed
at Kulia the place where Mahaprabhu forgave Devananda Pandita and Chapala Gopala their offences, and preached in the
Santal Parganas. <!-- F79 F265 F80 F81 -->

On 2 March 1923, the day of Mahaprabhu’s appearance, construction of the Chaitanya Math temple began according to his
design. On the main altar — Guru-Gauranga and Radha-Govinda; at the four corners — images of the founders of the four
Vaishnava sampradayas (Lakshmi, Brahma, Rudra and the four Kumaras) and of their acharyas — Ramanuja, Madhva,
Vishnusvami and Nimbarka. Then he went to Puri for the annual festival and danced before the chariot at the Ratha-yatra
in the mood of Mahaprabhu separated from Krishna. He preached to many, among them Raja Manindra Chandra Nandi and
Shashimohana Gosvami of Bhadrak, sent preachers to Mayurbhanj and Madras, and himself visited Amlajora (Burdwan) and
Banari-para (Barisal). <!-- F82 F83 -->

In 1923, before the annual festival of the Gaudiya Math in Calcutta, the “Gaudiya Printing Works” was opened. Here began
to appear the parts of the “Srimad-Bhagavatam” with the word-for-word rendering “Gaura-kishoranvaya,” the translation
“Svananda-kunjanuvada,” and the commentaries “Ananta-gopala-tathya” and “Sindhu-vaibhava.” On 24 February 1924, for the
fiftieth anniversary of his appearance, the Vyasa-puja was performed in his honour for the first time at the Calcutta
Gaudiya Math; one of the biographies calls his address that day an immortal gem of Vaishnava literature. Soon after, on
Mahaprabhu’s appearance day, the Madhva Gaudiya Math in Dhaka issued his edition of the “Chaitanya-bhagavata”[^14].
<!-- F84 F85 F86 -->

On 9 July 1924 he founded the Tridandi Math in Bhubaneshvar, then visited the Madras Presidency, where he founded the
“Sarasvata Asana” and taught his disciples in detail from Bhaktivinoda’s books. In early September Raut Rai of
Mayurbhanj, General Jang Bahadur of Nepal, Justice Manmathanath Mukhopadhyaya and others came to hear him. In October
he came to Dhaka for the fifth time and spoke much on the life of Madhvacharya and his sampradaya, explaining with
learning the differences between the teachings of Madhva and of the Gaudiyas, especially in their view of
varnashrama-dharma. <!-- F87 F88 -->

The founder of the Benares Hindu University, Pandita Madan Mohan Malaviya, invited him, and on 16 December 1924 he
lectured to the university assembly on “The Place of Vaishnavism among the Religions of the World”; among his
enthusiastic listeners were the head of the department of Western philosophy, Pramatha Nath Tarkabhushan, and Professor
Phani Bhushan Adhikari. Then he visited Mahaprabhu’s places in Kashi, pointed out in Prayaga the exact place where Sri
Rupa Gosvami received the Lord’s instructions, and went to Arail, where Mahaprabhu met Vallabhacharya. On 29 January
1925, with a large party of devotees, he set out through Gauda-mandala, to the places of Mahaprabhu and His associates,
and everywhere, in divine inspiration, spoke of pure bhakti. <!-- F89 F90 -->

On the Navadvipa parikrama of that same year, 1925, Radha-Govinda were carried on an elephant through Koladvipa. At
Porama-tola envious ringleaders of the “guru business” incited thugs to throw bricks at the Deities and the devotees, and
with them at Sarasvati Thakura. An eyewitness wrote the next day (24 Phalguna 1331) in the “Ananda Bazar Patrika” that he
had seen the same lila as nearly four hundred years before, when Jagai and Madhai tried to wound Nityananda Avadhuta. On
17 April 1925 Madan Mohan Malaviya, head of the “Hindu Mahasabha,” came to the Calcutta Gaudiya Math and heard his
explanation of *daiva-varnashrama* according to the “Agama-pramana.” Preachers were sent to Sylhet and elsewhere.
<!-- F91 F92 -->

In 1926 a three-day *nama-yajna* was held for the appearance of Nityananda Prabhu, and it became annual. In April he
founded the “Bhagavata Janananda Math” at Chirulia (Medinipur). The tridandi sannyasis grew in number, and he sent them
all over India while himself preaching in Medinipur; from this time, according to one of the biographies, the great
expansion of the Gaudiya Math began. <!-- F93 -->

At the beginning of November 1926 he set out on a tour of India: he conversed with scholars, gathered information and
preached at large gatherings. The acharyas of many schools acknowledged him as the leading acharya of Gaudiya
Vaishnavism: the mahanta of Nathdvara, Gokulanatha Gosvami Maharaja of Bombay, the abbot of Udupi and the abbot of the
seat of Salimabad (both of the Madhva-sampradaya) received him with the honour due to the spiritual teacher of a
Vaishnava sampradaya. On this tour he founded the Paramahamsa Math in Naimisharanya, and on his return he opened in
Mayapur the “Paravidya-pitha” school and the completed temple of the Chaitanya Math with its twenty-nine spires, with
the images of the acharyas and Radha-Govinda. <!-- F94 F95 -->

## “The Harmonist,” Kurukshetra, Delhi
In 1927 he began publishing the “Sajjana-toshani” in three languages — English, Sanskrit and Hindi; the English edition
was named “The Harmonist.” On 17 September 1927 he founded the Chaitanya Gaudiya Math at Dumurkonda (Manbhum), and at
the end of September he preached in Northern India. In 1928, at the festival of the Gaudiya Math, he spoke at the
Albert Hall and other public places in Calcutta; the fourth edition of the “Chaitanya-charitamrita” appeared. On 26
September 1928 the foundation of the Gaudiya Math at Bagbazar, on the bank of the Ganga, was laid, and on 7 October he
set out with a large party of devotees for Assam and spoke at large gatherings in Shillong; Prince Sharadindu Narayan
Ray was among his listeners. <!-- F96 F97 F98 -->

On 4 November 1928, during a solar eclipse, he came with devotees to Kurukshetra — to relive there the mood of separation
of the gopis and of Mahaprabhu at the Ratha-yatra. Hundreds of thousands of pilgrims heard the devotees of the Gaudiya
Math chanting the names of Gaura; in the Sri Vyasa Gaudiya Math he installed the Deity of Gauranga and opened the
diorama exhibition “Bhagavata-pradarshini.” On 30 December the great scholar Pramatha Nath Tarkabhushan came to the
Gaudiya Math and heard a detailed explanation of *daiva-varnashrama*. <!-- F99 F100 -->

In January 1929 he founded the Ekayana Gaudiya Math in Krishnanagar and at its opening gave an original exposition: the
one path of exclusive devotion (*ekayana*) is the trunk of the Vedic tree, and all other paths (*bahayana*) are its
branches. On 14 January he met Professor Albert E. Suthers of Ohio University and explained to him how Vaishnavism
broadens and brings to perfection Christianity: bhakti, pure love of God, is the highest goal of every religion. On 16
January he opened the Gaudiya Math in New Delhi. <!-- F101 F102 -->

On 30 March 1929 he lectured on the Holy Name in the Ramagopal town hall in Krishnanagar; in May he began in Puri the
festival of Gaurasundara’s Chandana-yatra and the repair of the Alalanatha temple; on 12 August he spoke on Gaudiya
philosophy at the Albert Hall in Calcutta. On 1 June 1929 a temporary post office opened in Mayapur, and in October a
permanent one. At that time, at his word, the devotees laid out Ishodyan — “the Lord’s garden” Bhaktivinoda had longed
for; he brought electricity to Mayapur, and light shone on the spire of the Chaitanya Math temple. <!-- F103 F106 -->

## Mahaprabhu’s Footprints
He wished to set up throughout India a hundred and eight memorials — *pada-pithas* — at the places sanctified by
Mahaprabhu’s feet, with the footprints of Gaurasundara in marble, so that people everywhere might hear and sing the
Lord’s message. For this, on 13 October 1929 he visited Kanai Natshala, and two days later Mandara; then, preaching, he
went to Rajmahal, Bhagalpur, Nalanda and Rajgir; in Kashi he spoke on Mahaprabhu’s teachings to Sanatana Gosvami[^15].
In Kashi, Faizabad, Ayodhya, Naimisharanya, Karaun, Misrikh, Sitapur and Lucknow educated people received him, and he
led many seekers of truth to pure bhakti; his listeners included the advocate A. P. Sen, Professor Radha Kumud
Mukhopadhyaya, Dr. Radha Kamal Mukhopadhyaya, Dr. A. N. Sen Gupta and others. <!-- F104 F105 -->

On 8 January 1930 the great scholar Dr. Haraprasad Shastri visited him; they spoke of the history of the Vaishnava
sampradayas and of the Gaudiya sampradaya, of the dates of the acharyas, of the Pancharatra and of Sri Chaitanya. In mid-
January, at the Purna Kumbha Mela in Prayaga, the preachers of the Chaitanya Math spoke of Mahaprabhu’s teachings to
Rupa Gosvami, and he installed at the Triveni Radha-Govinda — the life and soul of Sri Rupa. <!-- F107 F108 -->

From 3 February to 17 March 1930 an unprecedented diorama exhibition was held in Navadvipa-Mayapur; it was opened by
the famous chemist Dr. Prafulla Chandra Ray. On 18 February, the day of his Vyasa-puja, a memorial of the acharya’s
footprints was opened at the Chaitanya Math. On 4 May J. H. Napier heard him on Indian spiritual philosophy; on 25 May he
visited Chhatrabhoga, where Mahaprabhu passed on His way to Puri, and blessed many; in July he founded the
Sachchidananda Math in Cuttack; on 22 August he came to Allahabad, stayed with the retired judge Monomohan Sanyal, spoke
with him for several days, drew him to Mahaprabhu’s feet, and resolved the doubts of the townspeople, especially of
Professor P. K. Acharya. <!-- F109 F110 -->

On 5 October 1930 the devotees and the Deities — Radha-Madana-mohana, Radha-Govinda and Radha-Gopinatha — moved from the
math on Ultadanga Junction Road to the new buildings at Bagbazar. The festival was accompanied by a diorama exhibition
and a large spiritual conference. The chief donor for the temple, Jagabandhu Bhakti-ranjan, left this world on 19
November of that year; the day of his departure was thereafter marked every year by memorial gatherings. <!-- F111 -->

From 25 to 31 December 1930 he set up memorials of Mahaprabhu’s footprints and preached at Jajpur (on the 25th),
Kurmakshetra (26th), Simhachalam (27th), Kovvur (29th) and Mangalagiri (31st); his preaching drew Sir P. S. Sivaswami
Iyer, Dr. U. Ramrao, P. N. Subrahmanya Iyer and others. <!-- F112 -->

## The Year 1931
On 3 April 1931 he opened in Mayapur the “Thakur Bhaktivinoda Institute” — an English high school where young men were
educated in the Vaishnava way — and spoke on the difference between mundane and spiritual knowledge; later a junior
school in the Thakura’s name was opened as well. On 3 May he was in Darjeeling, and on 28 June he formally took charge
of the shripata of Mahesha Pandita, Nityananda’s associate, at Chakdaha and spoke at the opening. On 12 July, at the
Brahma Gaudiya Math in Alalanatha, he installed the Deity of Gauranga “Gaudiyanatha”; five days later he laid the
foundation of the Purushottama Math temple on land donated by the Maharaja of Mayurbhanj, then visited Cuttack, the
Sachchidananda Math, and sent preachers to Simla. <!-- F113 F114 F115 -->

On 30 July his hari-katha at Bagbazar was heard by Professor Kalidas Nag of Calcutta University, and on 5 September by
Justice Manmatha Nath Mukhopadhyaya. On 6 September, at the annual festival of the Gaudiya Math, he opened in Calcutta an
exhibition of spiritual education, the “Sat-shiksha-pradarshini,” in the newly built Gaudiya Math at Bagbazar. Hundreds
of thousands saw it, including highly educated people; they said that the acharya of the Gaudiya Math, even in an age of
materialism, had shown an exhibition of faith in God (*astikya-vada*) such as no one could have imagined before. In the
following weeks it was visited by Dr. Dinesh Chandra Sen Rai Bahadur, Biraj Mohan Majumdar, the German scholar Dr.
Magnus Hirschfeld, Dr. Stella Kramrisch and others. He held such exhibitions also in Sri Mayapur, Dhaka, Patna, Cuttack,
Kurukshetra and elsewhere[^16]. <!-- F117 F210 F116 -->

On 29 September 1931 he spoke on the nature of spiritual sound to the principal of the Calcutta Medical College,
Colonel Dvarakaprasad Goel, and to the American A. J. Jacobs; on 11 October in Prayaga he met the Vice-Chancellor of
Allahabad University, Dr. Ganganath Jha, and the divisional commissioner, Vinayak Shankar Mehta. On 16 October in
Benares he stayed and preached in the maharaja’s palace, “Mint Palace”; on 19–20 October he spoke on Vaishnava
philosophy and Krishna’s lila with Vasanta Kumar Chatterji; on 31 October he came to Lucknow and then to Naimisharanya,
where on 9 November, at the Paramahamsa Math, he began publishing the Hindi journal “Bhagavata.” <!-- F118 F119 -->

On 14 November he sent preachers to New Delhi to tell the Viceroy, Lord Willingdon, about the math’s preaching. On 17
November he established the annual festival of the Delhi Gaudiya Math and spoke on bhakti in the hall of the “Gurudwara
Bangla Sahib”; on 29 November he spoke at a gathering in the garden of Rai Bahadur Lala Jagadish Prasad. On 30 November
he came to Shukaratala, where Shuka first narrated the “Bhagavatam,” and himself read the “Bhagavatam” there. On 6
December he installed Radha-Govinda in the Delhi Gaudiya Math, and on 9 December the first anniversary of Jagabandhu
Bhakti-ranjan’s departure was observed; the chair was Justice Manmatha Nath Mukhopadhyaya, who on the 13th also visited
Mayapur. <!-- F120 -->

## The South: Madras, Mysore, Kovvur
On 10 January 1932 he arrived in Madras with twenty devotees. At Basin Bridge station he was met by the president of the
city corporation, T. S. Ramaswami Iyer, by T. Rajan, S. V. Ramaswami Mudaliar, Dewan Bahadur G. Narayanaswami Chettiar,
T. Punurulla Pillai and others, and a huge sankirtana escorted him to the Gaudiya Math, which then stood in North
Gopalpur; Dewan Bahadur Kumaraswami Reddiar welcomed him with an address. In January he was visited by a judge of the
High Court, Dewan Bahadur Sundaram Chettiar; on the 23rd he installed the Deities in the Madras Gaudiya Math and laid the
foundation of a new math at Royapettah; on the 24th he spoke at a large gathering chaired by Sir P. S. Sivaswami Iyer; on
the 27th the Governor of Madras, Sir George Frederick Stanley, laid the foundation of the “Sri Krishna Kirtan Hall”; on
the 29th the city corporation gave him an official reception, and he replied with an address in the “Ripon Building”;
on the 30th at Eluru (West Godavari) the “Janardana Prarthana Samaj” welcomed him, and he initiated many. <!-- F121 F122 -->

For his fifty-eighth appearance day he wrote an address that was read at the Vyasa-puja in Calcutta. In it he spoke of
Kunjavihari Vidyabhushana: on the day of his guru’s departure Kunjavihari had appeared before him as a second form of
the guru, and since then had not let him serve by himself, taking every opportunity of service upon himself.
<!-- F123 F264 -->

Before the Navadvipa parikrama of 1932 he returned to Mayapur. On the day of Mahaprabhu’s appearance he laid the
foundation of a new temple at the house of Advaita Acharya, spoke at the annual meeting of the “Sri Dhama Pracharini
Sabha,” and instituted examinations for the degrees of “Bhakti-shastri” and “Bhakti-vaibhavacharya.” On 3 April, at the
annual prize-giving of the Bhaktivinoda Institute, he gave an address on “Altruism and Extended Altruism.” <!-- F124 -->

On 23 May he was again in Madras, explaining the distinctive features of the Gaudiya sampradaya to the heads and scholars
of the schools of Shankara, Ramanuja and Madhva; on 25 May he resolved the doubts of a group led by Professor K.
Panchapagesan of Pudukkottai; on 29 May he visited Coimbatore, then Mettupalayam on the Bhavani river, and settled in
“Rangavilas Bhavan” at Udagamandalam (Nilgiris). There he corrected Professor Nishikanta Sanyal’s English book “Sree
Krishna Chaitanya,” completed his English translation of the “Brahma-samhita” with Jiva Gosvami’s commentary, his
“Gaudiya-bhashya” on the “Chaitanya-bhagavata,” and a short English book on the life of Ramananda Raya. Through his
principal disciples he was heard there by Sir Kishan Prasad, prime minister to the Nizam of Hyderabad, the zamindar
Dhanraj Girji, Sir P. S. Sivaswami Iyer and Dewan Bahadur P. Muniswami Naidu. <!-- F125 F126 F258 -->

On 17 June 1932 the Maharaja of Mysore, Sir Krishnaraja Wadiyar, invited him and his disciples; they stayed as guests
in the “Rama Mandir” and preached. On the way he visited the Lingayat temple of Kantheshvara and the Madhva Math at
Nanjangud, and on 19 June Krishnaraja Sagar and Shrirangapattanam; on the morning of 20 June he spoke on Krishna-bhakti
at the Mysore Sanskrit College, and in the afternoon, at the maharaja’s palace, he spoke of Mahaprabhu and answered
questions. Then he visited Kovvur on the Godavari — where Mahaprabhu first met Ramananda Raya — and on 5 July installed
the Deities in the Ramananda Gaudiya Math. Hundreds of thousands who had come to bathe at the *pushkara-yoga* heard the
names of Gauranga, and to educated people he spoke of the gradual path of spiritual life and its goal. <!-- F127 F128 -->

## His Guru’s Samadhi in Mayapur
In August–September 1932 in Calcutta he was heard by Sir Deva Prasad Sarvadhikari (16 August, on “Sri Chaitanya’s
Prema”), Professor Suniti Chatterji and the magistrate of Nadia, T. K. Ray; he lectured on “The Relative Worlds” (28
August), “The Highest Goal of Life” (3 September) and on the Vedanta (11 September); three lectures were published. In
early September, at his word, the Assamese monthly “Kirtana” began to appear in Dhubri. <!-- F129 F131 -->

His guru’s samadhi at Kulia had met with misfortune. The land for it had been given by a certain Poddar of Lohagara
(Jessore), who renounced his rights but later settled there himself and behaved badly; and the Ganga was coming ever
closer. On 21 August 1932, learning that the samadhi was about to fall into the river, Srila Bhaktisiddhanta sent his
disciples to carry his guru’s sacred remains to the Sri Chaitanya Math in Mayapur, and raised a new samadhi temple on the
bank of Radha-kunda; in September he opened it[^17]. Later a small temple with an image of Gaurakishora was built beside
it, where service has never ceased. He often told his disciples incidents from his guru’s life full of meaning for
sadhakas. <!-- F130 F242 F243 -->

On 9 October 1932, the appearance day of Madhvacharya, he set out with countless devotees on the eighty-four-krosha
parikrama of Vraja-mandala. At all the places of Krishna’s pastimes his disciples preached in various languages, and he
himself expounded Rupa Gosvami’s “Upadeshamrita” to the Vrajavasis and to scholars. On 4 November he laid the foundation
of the Sri Sarasvata Gaudiya Math in Hardvar; on 21 November in Prayaga, at his invitation and in his presence, the
Governor of the United Provinces, Sir William Malcolm Hailey, laid the foundation of the Sri Rupa Gaudiya Math; on the
24th he installed Radha-Govinda in the Sanatana Gaudiya Math in Benares; on the 27th the second anniversary of
Jagabandhu’s departure was observed (chaired by Sir Manmatha Nath Ray Chaudhuri); on 4 December Professor Sudhendu Kumar
Das of Krishnanagar and Vishvambhar Vyakarana-tirtha of the Radhakanta Math in Puri learned much from him in Mayapur
about the Vaishnava sampradayas of India. <!-- F132 F133 -->

From 12 December 1932 to 30 January 1933 he stayed in Dhaka, and on 6 January opened there a diorama exhibition of
spiritual education such as the city had never seen; his address “The Exhibitor’s Word” changed his listeners’ ideas of
religion. On 2 February 1933 at the Calcutta Gaudiya Math, at the question of the dean of Narasingh College, Satishchandra
De, and of Professor Ranada Charan Chakravarti, he explained the difference between *eka-danda* and *tri-danda*
sannyasa, and on 8 February he left for Mayapur — for Nityananda’s appearance, his own Vyasa-puja and the appearance of
Mahaprabhu. <!-- F134 F135 -->

## The Message Goes to Europe
On Mahaprabhu’s appearance day in 1933 Nishikanta Sanyal’s English book “Sree Krishna Chaitanya” came out, and Srila
Bhaktisiddhanta decided that the time had come to carry the Lord’s message to Europe. He chose three leading disciples —
Bhakti Pradipa Tirtha, Bhakti Hridaya Bon and Sambidananda Dasa, M.A., Bhakti-shastri. On 18 March, at a meeting chaired
by Jatindranath Basu, he gave a farewell address, “My Word” (“Amar Katha”), praising them. <!-- F136 -->

At the beginning of May the first news came from London: a centre had been opened at 39 Drayton Gardens, Kensington. On
31 May he answered spiritual questions sent from London by the Marquess of Lothian and Lord Zetland, former Governor of
Bengal. On 15 June his disciples spoke at a meeting of the “Society for the Study of Religions” in Bedford Square under
Zetland’s chairmanship, and on 23 June a meeting in honour of Bhaktivinoda was held at the London Gaudiya Math. In July
letters of thanks for the Gaudiya Math’s work in Britain came from R. A. Butler (Lord Irwin’s private secretary), the
Marquess of Lothian, Sir Stanley Jackson and the editor of “The Times,” Brown; on 5 July the former Viceroy Lord Irwin
and his wife and members of the Joint Parliamentary Committee heard the missionaries; on 14 July they were received by
the Archbishop of Canterbury, Cosmo Gordon Lang. <!-- F137 F138 -->

On 20 July 1933, at four in the afternoon, the Secretary of State for India, Sir Samuel Hoare, presented the preachers of
the Gaudiya Math to King George V and Queen Mary, and Svami Bon Maharaja told them the purpose of the mission. Many
British governors and the Viceroy of India himself were impressed by his addresses; he spoke at British and German
universities. On 7–8 October the preachers spoke on the Holy Name to a gathering of professors and students at Oxford,
on 24–25 November at the Theosophical Society in Eastbourne, on 10 December at the “Deutsche Akademie” in Munich, on 12
December at the “Humboldt-Haus” in Berlin, then in Königsberg and at the Institute of Indian Civilisation in Paris; on
20 December the London Gaudiya Math moved to Gloucester House, 3 Cornwall Gardens. “Our Teachers” adds that his envoys
also preached in Holland, Austria and Czechoslovakia, and that among the first Western disciples was the German writer
Walther Eidlitz (Vamanadeva). <!-- F139 F140 F141 F273 -->

The tridandi sannyasis of the Gaudiya Math studied the philosophy of the four Vaishnava sampradayas and Gaudiya
philosophy in comparison with the other philosophies of the world, and preached throughout India all the year round:
large meetings, lectures, sankirtana processions, talks from house to house, the distribution of booklets. Bhakti
Hridaya Bon, Bhakti Svarupa Parvata and Bhakti Sambandha Turyashrami became famous as speakers. <!-- F140 -->

## At Home: 1933–1934
Meanwhile he opened the “Sri Krishna Kirtan Hall” in Madras; in Bombay he answered three sincere questions of the
Nepalese Professor Sanjiva Kumar Chaudhuri and opened the Bombay office of the Gaudiya Math (“Jangu Villa,” Babulnath
Road); on 20 May, at the question of a relative of Dadabhai Naoroji, he gave his own solution to the question of the
admission of untouchables to temples. On 16 June in the Krishnanagar town hall, at a memorial meeting for Bhaktivinoda
(chaired by Khitipati Nath Mitra and Dinanath Sanyal Rai Bahadur), he spoke on the uniqueness of the
“Srimad-Bhagavatam,” and on 3 July he installed Gaurasundara and Radha-Govinda in the new temple of the Tridandi Gaudiya
Math in Bhubaneshvar. <!-- F142 F143 -->

In August 1933 a second diorama exhibition was held at Kurukshetra during a solar eclipse, and a kirtana procession went
through Calcutta; he lectured on “The Highest Goal of Human Life” (on the 12th), “The Uniqueness of Sri Chaitanya
Mahaprabhu” (20th) and, in English, “The Vedanta, its morphology and ontology” (27th). On 7 September he went by motor
launch to Navadvipa and visited with sankirtana the places along the Ganga. On 27 October in Patna he was heard by
Amarendranath Das Rai Bahadur, the archaeologist Ganesh Chandra Chand, the barrister P. R. Das, the advocate Navadvip
Chandra Das and Justice Shivepriya Chatterji; on 14 November the Maharaja of Darbhanga, Sir Kameshvar Singh, opened an
exhibition in Patna. <!-- F144 F145 -->

On 19 November the third anniversary of Jagabandhu’s departure was observed (chaired by Sir Vijaya Prasad Singh Ray). At
the end of November Jiva Gosvami’s “Bhakti-sandarbha” appeared complete under his editorship. He spoke hari-katha at
Tetiya-palli near Nrisimha-palli on the 24th and led sankirtana at the Ekayana Math in Krishnanagar on the 26th–27th;
preaching went on at Amarshigrama (Medinipur) and in Karachi; on 24 December the magistrate Pannalal opened an exhibition
in Benares, at Michir-pokhra. On 15 January 1934 the Calcutta Gaudiya Math was visited by the King of Tripura,
Viravikrama Kishor Devavarma Manikya Bahadur: he showed his respect to the acharya, and in the evening praised the work
of the math in a speech; on 2 February his secretary, Kumar Bahadur Radhika Ranjan Chakravarti, came. <!-- F146 F147 -->

On 4 February 1934, his sixtieth appearance day, the Vyasa-puja was held and printing began of his disciples’ book about
his life, the “Sarasvati-jayashri”; on the same day in London, at Grosvenor House, Park Lane, a meeting in his honour was
held under Lord Zetland’s chairmanship. On 25 February a new temple was opened at the birthplace of Vrindavana Dasa in
Modadrumadvipa. At the Navadvipa parikrama and Gaura-purnima of 1934 temples were consecrated at Shrivasa-angana and at
the samadhi of Gaurakishora; three disciples took tridandi sannyasa; he spoke at the “Bhakti-vijaya Bhavan” and at a
meeting of the “Navadvipa Dhama Pracharini Sabha”; on 19 February Rajarshi Kumar Sharadindu Narayan Ray and Rai Bahadur
Rama Prasad Chanda heard him. On 5 March a large preaching party went to Chanchuri-Puruliya (Jessore), the native village
of the temple manager of the Gaudiya Math, Maha-mahopadeshaka Acharyatrika Kunja Bihari Vidyabhushana, and preached
there for five days. <!-- F148 F149 F150 -->

## Adhokshaja of the Yogapitha
On 18 March 1934 the foundations were laid for a temple at the place of Mahaprabhu’s appearance and for a small temple at
the house of Murari Gupta. On 13 June at ten in the morning, while the foundations were being dug — the building was
paid for by Sakhicharan Ray Bhakti-vijaya — the four-armed Deity of Vishnu Adhokshaja appeared from the earth: the very
Deity whom Jagannatha Mishra had worshipped. <!-- F151 -->

On 2 April he opened the pada-pitha at Chhatrabhoga, and the villagers honoured him; on 8 April he gave sannyasa to
several disciples, and on the 20th came to Puri. On 24 April, at Caxton Hall in Westminster, the “Gaudiya Mission
Society” was founded under Lord Zetland’s chairmanship, and on 6 May Rama Prasad Chanda lectured on “Navadvipa in the
Time of Lord Chaitanya.” In May in Puri he spoke of Krishna with the ayurveda lecturer Ananda Mahapatra, the writer
Shachishchandra Chattopadhyaya, the abbot of the Emar Math Gadadhara Ramanuja Dasa, Hanuman Khuntia, Gaura Shyama
Mahanti, Radha Shyama Mahanti, Professor Junakar, Revatinath Chattopadhyaya, Narendranath Datta, the founder of the
“Bodhana Ashrama” Girija Prasanna Mukhopadhyaya and Professor Khagendranath Mitra. <!-- F152 F153 -->

On 27 June he installed Gopinatha in the Brahma Gaudiya Math at Alalanatha; a revised edition of “Brahmana o Vaishnava”
appeared. On 12 July he installed in Mayapur an image of Gaurakishora Dasa Babaji; on 13 August he preached to Jamini
Nath Mukhopadhyaya; on the 14th he installed the Deities in the Patna Gaudiya Math. On 1 September, Janmashtami, the
“Sarasvati-jayashri” came out; on 4 September “The Harmonist” began to appear in a new form, twice a month; on 16
September he spoke on Radhashtami. <!-- F154 -->

## Karttika in Mathura
From 17 October 1934 he kept the Karttika vow with his disciples in Mathura and showed them by example how to discuss
the *ashtakaliya-lila* — the pastimes of Radha and Krishna in the eight periods of the day. On 29 October he found in
Mathura, in the Satghara quarter, the place where Rupa Gosvami used to come for darshana of Gopala; on 1 November he
visited Chandra-sarovara, Parasauli, Gauri-tirtha and Paitha-grama, speaking of the pastimes of these places, and on 17
November he gave tridandi sannyasa to brahmacharis. In the third week of October his representatives preached in German
universities; on 29 November at the “Rajendra Bhavan” in New Delhi he gave addresses on “The Duty of Human Life” and
“Mahaprabhu’s Mercy and His Teaching.” On 6 December the fourth anniversary of Jagabandhu’s departure was observed
(chaired by Raja Bhupendra Narayan Singh). At his word the Andhra disciple Y. Jagannath published Bhaktivinoda’s “Sri
Chaitanya-shikshamrita” in Telugu; an English edition of the “Jaiva-dharma” was in preparation. <!-- F155 F156 F157 -->

## 1935: Puri, Radha-kunda
On 15 January 1935 the Governor of Bengal, Sir John Anderson, visited the place of Mahaprabhu’s appearance in Mayapur
and, having heard Srila Bhaktisiddhanta’s account of the holy place, himself made a speech. On 23 February his
sixty-first Vyasa-puja was celebrated where he had appeared — in Puri, among the dunes by the ocean; the chair was the
Maharaja of Puri, Gajapati Ramachandra Deva Bahadur. The next day he walked around Puri and lectured; on 4 March Sir B. L.
Mitra heard him. By Dola-purnima Sakhicharan Ray Bhakti-vijaya had brought electric light into the Yogapitha temple,
and on 20 March, Dola-purnima, the new temple was opened: its doors were formally opened by the Maharaja of Tripura, Sir
Viravikrama Kishor Devavarma Manikya Bahadur. <!-- F158 F159 F160 -->

In spring he preached in Eastern Bengal: on 24 March — large meetings at Deuli (Khulna); on 31 March in Calcutta the
Maharaja of Burdwan, Sir Vijaya Chand Mahtab, heard him; on 8 April in Dhaka he laid the foundation of a new temple of
the Madhva Gaudiya Math (Narindapalli); on 12 April he installed the Deities in the Jagannatha Gaudiya Math in Mymensingh
and until the 15th was the guest of Maharaja Shashi Kanta Acharya at “Shashi Lodge.” On 19 April he visited all the
places of Mahaprabhu in Gaya and on the 22nd founded the Gaya Gaudiya Math; on 30 April he sent preachers to Eastern
Bengal and Burma; on 31 May he came to Darjeeling, where on 9–10 June his disciples spoke at meetings chaired by Sir
Jadunath Sarkar and Colonel Upendranath Mukhopadhyaya; on 9 June Chaitanya’s message was broadcast by the “Indian
Broadcasting Service.” <!-- F161 F162 -->

On 28 June at the Calcutta Gaudiya Math he was heard on Vaishnava philosophy by the Maharani of Cooch Behar, Indira Devi,
the princesses Ila Devi and Gayatri Devi, Prince Indrajitendra Narayan and the French scholar Dr. Maximilian Poitier.
On 8 July he installed the Deities in the Bombay Gaudiya Math on Proctor Street and spoke on the “Bhagavata” and the
Pancharatra at the “People’s Jinnah Hall.” His disciple Sambidananda Dasa (M.A., Bhakti-shastri,
Pratna-tattva-visharada) received a doctorate from the University of London for work on the history and literature of
Vaishnavism. From the end of July to mid-August Srila Bhaktisiddhanta preached in the Navadvipa district. <!-- F163 F164 -->

From the annual festival of the Gaudiya Math in 1935 there were street sankirtana every Sunday and radio broadcasts — for
Janmashtami, Nandotsava, Radhashtami and Bhaktivinoda’s appearance day. From Balarama’s appearance he expounded the
“Bhagavata” for sixteen days in a row, and at the great meeting of the festival, chaired by the Maharaja of
Cossimbazar, Shachindra Nandi, he gave an address on “Devotion and Indifference to the World.” On 18 September the
citizens of Calcutta welcomed Bhakti Hridaya Bon, returned from London with two German devotees, and honoured him with
an address. On 21 September, the full moon of Bhadra, he lectured on the occasion of the Gaudiya Math’s completing the
publication of all twelve books of the “Bhagavata”[^18]. <!-- F165 F166 -->

From 1 to 7 October he preached in New Delhi, and from 8 October he spent more than a month at Radha-kunda keeping the
vows of the month of Karttika (*niyama-seva*): every day the Upanishads, the “Chaitanya-charitamrita,” the
“Bhagavatam,” circumambulation of the kunda, and hearing and singing the *ashtakaliya-lila*. On 9 October a committee for
the service of Vraja-dhama, the “Vraja-dhama-pracharini Sabha,” was founded there. On 4 November he installed the
Deities in the Kunja-bihari Math at Radha-kunda, and two days later the *pushpa-samadhi* of Bhaktivinoda (a memorial
enshrining flowers from his samadhi) and the service of his *bhava* (*bhava-seva*) in the Vraja-Svananda-sukhada-kunja.
<!-- F167 F168 -->

On 7 November he visited Sheshashayi (Punjab), then Delhi (festival on the 10th); from 11 to 15 November he was in Gaya
and on the 13th installed the Deities in the Gaya Gaudiya Math. The preaching in Burma was going with great success. On
23 December the fifth anniversary of Jagabandhu’s departure was observed (chaired by the Maharaja of Tripura). He spoke
of transcendental knowledge beyond the senses with Dr. Henry Hand and S. V. Rossetto of California, the barrister S. N.
Rudra and Justice Lalita Mohan Basu. On 27 December he came to Patna, on the 30th to Allahabad, where he spoke on
Mahaprabhu’s teachings to Rupa Gosvami, and on 7 January 1936 he opened a diorama exhibition in Prayaga and gave an
English presidential address before scholars. <!-- F169 F170 -->

## 1936: The Nine Islands
On 11 January 1936 he returned to Mayapur and for two months preached every day to the residents of the Chaitanya Math
and of Gauranga’s birthplace. On 12 February, for his sixty-second appearance day, he founded the “Thakur Bhaktivinoda
Research Institute” — the “Krishnanushilana-agara,” a library of Krishna culture — and the “Daiva-varnashrama Sangha”[^19].
The Vyasa-puja was held at Shrivasa-angana; in London his appearance day was celebrated under the chairmanship of the head
of the “London Gaudiya Mission Society,” Sir Shadilal. <!-- F171 F172 -->

From 25 February he installed Deities — *vishaya-* and *ashraya-vigrahas*, the One who is served and those who serve —
on each of the nine islands of Navadvipa: on 1 March at Suvarna-vihara (the Suvarna-bihari Math), on 5 March at the
Sarvabhauma Gaudiya Math in Vidyanagara, on 7 March at the Rudradvipa Gaudiya Math. On 8 March, Mahaprabhu’s appearance
day, at his word the Minister of Education of Burma, Dr. Ba Maw, opened the Gaudiya Math office in Rangoon (29 Brooking
Street), and in London on that day there were addresses on the Lord’s appearance under the chairmanship of Dr. Parikhi. On
15 March he was in Assam and installed the Deities in the Gaudiya Math; the people of Sarbhog honoured him. <!-- F173 F174 -->

On 27 March he came to Cuttack, and from 29 March he lived at Chataka-parvata in Puri: there he founded a residence for
devotees and a temple with Radha-Govinda, and with an address before scholars began a hundred-day festival of
sankirtana. On 4 May he observed Nrisimha-chaturdashi at the Brahma Gaudiya Math in Alalanatha, and on 30 May gave
tridandi sannyasa to several brahmachari preachers. In June he was in Dhaka, stayed with Supati Ranjan Nag and initiated
many (on the 7th); on the 9th the people of Baliati welcomed him; on the 10th he installed Radha-Govinda in the new temple
of the Gadai-Gauranga Math; on the 13th–14th his sannyasis and a German disciple spoke at Dhaka University and the Dhaka
Bar Library. <!-- F175 F176 -->

On 19 June in the Svananda-sukhada-kunja in Godrumadvipa he observed the twenty-second anniversary of Bhaktivinoda’s
departure: he spoke of the need to give up the company of non-devotees and led sankirtana; on the same day at
Kurukshetra, during a solar eclipse, the devotees of the Gaudiya Math showed the pilgrims the exhibition
“Sat-shiksha-pradarshini.” On 27 June he came to Darjeeling and spoke hari-katha at the Gaudiya Math office, on 19 July
installed Radha-Govinda there, and on 24 July at Bogra, at a meeting of the local “Hindu Sabha,” he spoke of the need to
preach Gaudiya Vaishnavism in Northern Bengal, once blessed by Narottama Dasa Thakura. <!-- F177 F178 -->

After Balarama-purnima and Janmashtami in Calcutta he decided to spend the *purushottama-masa* (*mala-masa*, the
intercalary month) in Mathura-mandala, and on 12 August set out: he stayed in Mathura, in the cantonment (“Shivalaya”),
held daily classes on the “Bhagavatam” in Vrindavana (Madhumangala-kunja), opened a place of worship at Govardhana, and
on 9 September returned to Calcutta for the annual festival. On 16 October he spoke for nearly an hour with Dr.
Shivapada Bhattacharya, and on 23 October at the “Sarasvata Shravana Sadana” he entrusted the preaching in Great Britain
and America to Bhakti-saranga Prabhu, blessed him, and instructed him in the worship of the Gomati, the shalagrama and
the govardhana-shila. <!-- F179 F180 -->

## Last Months in Puri
On 24 October 1936 he left for Puri. On 1 November his dear disciple Tridandi Svami Bhakti Shrirupa Puri Maharaja left
this world. At Chataka-parvata, which is not different from Govardhana, Srila Bhaktisiddhanta celebrated the
appearance of Madhvacharya and the Govardhana-puja — with the hymns of Rupa and Raghunatha Gosvamis — and observed the day
of separation from his dear master, Srila Gaurakishora Dasa Gosvami Maharaja. Every day his talks bathed his disciples
and guests. <!-- F181 F182 -->

In Puri he constantly warned everyone: “Worship the Lord without duplicity — little time remains.” So the acharya,
seeing ahead, urged his disciples on. And he ceaselessly repeated two prayers to Govardhana — Rupa Gosvami’s from the
“Govardhanashtaka”: *pratyasham me tvam kuru govardhana purnam* — “O Govardhana, fulfil my hope,” — and Raghunatha
Dasa’s from the “Govardhana-vasa-prarthana-dashaka”: *nija-nikata-nivasam dehi govardhana tvam* — “O Govardhana, grant
me a dwelling near you.” On 9 December he returned to Calcutta, instructing his disciples without pause the whole way.
