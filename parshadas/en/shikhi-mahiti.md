# Shikhi Mahiti
*Шикхи Махити · @shikhi-mahiti*

## Scribe of Jagannatha's Temple
In Purushottama-kshetra, at the temple of Sri Jagannatha, lived Shikhi Mahiti, one of the most
intimate companions of Sri Chaitanya Mahaprabhu in Nilachala. The original sources spell his name in several ways:
“Shikhi Mahiti” (CC, Adi 10.136–137; Antya 2.103, 2.106), “Shikhi Mahati” (CC, Madhya 10.42, 10.44;
“Gaura-ganoddesha-dipika” 189; Karnapura's drama “Chaitanya-chandrodaya”; “Bhakti-ratnakara”), and simply “Shikhi”
(CC, Madhya 16.254; “Chaitanya-charita-mahakavya” 13.89, 13.107; “Gaura-krishnodaya” 11.5). In his “Anubhashya”
Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati adds “Mahanti” to his name in brackets, apparently another form of the same family
name.[^1] <!-- F1 F9 -->

He came from Utkala. The “Anubhashya” calls him and his sister natives of Utkala, intimate and qualified (*adhikari*)
devotees of Mahaprabhu, and the commentary to the “Chaitanya-bhagavata” (Adi 2.28–30) lists Shikhi Mahiti,
Madhavi-devi and Murari Mahiti among the Lord's companions born in Utkala. Kavi Karnapura begins his account of him
thus:

“There, in the land of Purushottama, lived a pure man named Shikhi Mahiti, a great soul; as His servant he had won
the mercy of the tilaka of the Blue Mountain.” (“Chaitanya-charita-mahakavya” 13.89)

The tilaka, the ornament of the Blue Mountain, is Lord Jagannatha Himself, the Lord of Nilachala. Shikhi Mahiti was
His servant, and Karnapura also calls him “the loving servant (*prema-bhritya*) of the Lord of the Blue Mountain”
(13.94). The “Anubhashya” too portrays him as pure, radiant in appearance, a great soul with a compassionate heart.
<!-- F6 F7 F14 F18 F40 -->

At the temple he served as scribe. When Sarvabhauma Bhattacharya was introducing the servants of Jagannatha to the
Lord, he said of him:

“This is Krishnadasa, who bears the golden staff, and this is Shikhi Mahiti, the scribe.” (CC, Madhya 10.42)

In the Bengali verse his office is called *likhanadhikari*, “the one in charge of records,” and Karnapura uses the
same word for him in the drama “Chaitanya-chandrodaya” (act 8). Srila Bhaktivinoda Thakura explains in his
“Amrita-pravaha-bhashya” that this is an official holding the post of *deula-karana*, the temple scribe, who keeps
the chronicle of Jagannatha's temple, the “Madala-panji.” One of the biographies adds that the chronicle was kept
year by year and that this was his chief duty. Govinda-deva in the “Gaura-krishnodaya” speaks of him and his brother
as “two worthy kayasthas, Shikhi and Murari, the chief scribes” (11.5). Here the social class speaks only of a worldly office: the
position of the Lord's eternal companion is not determined by birth, and Ramananda Raya too was a karana by birth.

## Brother and Sister
Shikhi was the eldest in the family. Murari Mahiti was the next after him, and Madhavi-devi the youngest of the three.
Karnapura says:

“He has a younger brother named Murari, and younger still a sister of pure mind, Madhava-devi. So the three of them
lived there.” (“Chaitanya-charita-mahakavya” 13.90)

Kaviraja Gosvami, listing the branches of Sri Chaitanya's tree, names the sister right after the brothers:

“Madhavi Devi, the sister of Shikhi Mahiti, is counted among the maidservants of Sri Radha.” (CC, Adi 10.137)

Madhavi-devi and her brothers are among Mahaprabhu's very closest companions. One of the biographies also reports that
Shikhi Mahiti was the son of a brother of Bhavananda Raya, Ramananda's father.[^2] <!-- F3 F8 F10 F11 Q2 -->

## The Dream
Murari and Madhavi became devotees of Gaura before their elder brother. How Mahaprabhu's mercy came to him as well is
told by Karnapura in the “Chaitanya-charita-mahakavya” (canto 13, verses 89–109), and after him by Srila
Bhaktisiddhanta in the “Anubhashya” to CC, Adi 10.136.[^3] The “Charitamrita” does not tell this story.
<!-- F13 F19 Q11 -->

The younger brother and sister had been unshakably devoted to Gaurasundara from birth: their innate, pure mind never for
a moment allowed them to forget Gaura. “The Moon of Vrindavana has now risen here on earth as Gaurachandra”: such was their
conviction (13.93). And Krishna Himself, appearing as Gaurachandra, says the “Anubhashya,” constantly nourished in the two
younger ones their blessed love for Gaura.[^4] Through them His mercy touched their elder brother as well, who
was wholly absorbed in the service of Jagannatha. <!-- F12 I1 -->

The brother and sister did their utmost to bring him to the worship of Gaurasundara, but for a long time Shikhi did
not turn to Gaura: he was entirely given to the loving service of the Lord of Nilachala. There is nothing here of
worldly unbelief. So the Lord arranged His pastime, to reveal both the power of association with His devotees and His
own mercy. <!-- F20 -->

One day, after his siblings' instructions and long talks with them, he fell asleep, and as the night was ending he
started from a dream: he dreamt that his younger brother and sister, who behold Gaura's lotus feet, were rousing him. This wondrous
dream made the hair of his body stand on end, joy shook him twice over, and his eyes filled with tears. Opening his eyes, he
saw that his brother and sister had indeed come to him, and in great joy he embraced them, and they were astonished.[^5]
Then he told them his dream:

“‘Brother, listen to the dream I have had — it is most wonderful. Only today have I become convinced that the glory of
Sri Shachi's son is immeasurable. I saw Gaurasundara looking at the Lord of Nilachala, entering into Him moment after
moment, coming out again and again and looking at Him once more — such is the pastime He was displaying. How
wonderful! Even now I see the Supreme Lord Gaurasundara in that very state — are my eyes deceiving me? Ah, that ocean of
boundless mercy, Gaurasundara, saw me near Jagannatha-deva, called me by name and, as it were, embraced me with His
long, raised, graceful arms.’” (“Anubhashya” to CC, Adi 10.136)

In Karnapura the Lord's arms are “long, mighty, gracefully reaching to the knees” (13.102). <!-- F21 F22 F23 -->

What Shikhi saw is a pastime (*lila*) of the Lord, not a “dissolving” of one into the other. Gaura, absorbed in
beholding Jagannatha, and Jagannatha Himself are one Lord; in Karnapura's drama Sarvabhauma tells Mahaprabhu that
Jagannatha and He are equally Krishna-Chaitanya, without any difference (“Chaitanya-chandrodaya,”
act 8). Nor was this dream a play of the imagination: it was the Lord's mercy, His revelation.

As he spoke, Shikhi trembled all over, his eyes were full of tears, his voice broke with love, and when he had finished
he went out.[^6] Murari and Madhavi, having heard their elder brother, told him to go to Jagannatha: Mahaprabhu, says
Karnapura, had gone there too, to behold the Lord of the Blue Mountain. All three consented and set out for the Lord of
Nilachala. <!-- F24 F25 -->

In the *jagamohana*, the hall before the sanctum, Murari and Madhavi beheld the Lord and wept for joy. But Shikhi saw
Gaurasundara on every side exactly as He had appeared to him in the dream, in the very same mood, and love flowered in
his heart. Karnapura concludes the story:

“And the Lord, abounding in mercy, saying, ‘You are Murari's elder brother,’ embraced him with both arms. And he, giving
his mind to Him, became as if happiness embodied. From then on, forgetting everything through a drop of the fragrance
of His lotus feet, he serves Him always, day after day, as his own worshipful Lord — so great is his fortune.”
(“Chaitanya-charita-mahakavya” 13.108–109)

The Lord, who knows every heart, recognized him before any words were spoken and Himself took him into His embrace.
From that day, intoxicated with the scent of Gaura's feet, Shikhi Mahiti forgot everything else and served Sri Gaura
as the beloved Lord of his heart (*abhishta-deva*). This was no turning away from Jagannatha: Gaura and Jagannatha are
non-different, and service to the One is service to the Other. <!-- F26 F27 F28 -->

## With the Lord in Nilachala
When Mahaprabhu returned from His journey through South India, Svarupa Damodara came to Him in Puri. Listing the
events of those days, Kaviraja Gosvami says:

“Meeting Svarupa Damodara brought Him supreme joy; He also met Shikhi Mahiti and Bhavananda Raya.” (CC, Madhya 1.130)
<!-- F29 Q6 -->

At that time, in the house of Kashi Mishra, Sarvabhauma introduced the servants of Jagannatha to the Lord. He named
the scribe Shikhi Mahiti right after Krishnadasa with the golden staff, and a little further on pointed out his
brother as well:

“This is Murari Mahiti, Shikhi Mahiti's brother. He has no refuge but Your feet.” (CC, Madhya 10.44)

In Karnapura's drama Sarvabhauma says: “This is Shikhi Mahiti, in charge of the records, and these are his brother
and sister”;[^7] and the “Gaura-krishnodaya” names Shikhi and Murari among those servants of Jagannatha who, introduced
by Sarvabhauma, took shelter of the Lord (11.5). Karnapura in his mahakavya also tells of Shikhi's dream soon after the Lord's
return from the south, before the Bengali devotees arrive; but how this event relates to the introduction in Kashi
Mishra's house, and which came first, the sources do not say. <!-- F30 -->

When the Bengali devotees came from Gauda to Puri, Shikhi Mahiti was among those residents of the Kshetra who joyfully
went out with the Lord to meet them (CB, Antya 8.56–61). Murari Gupta names him here too: “the one known as Shikhi
Mahiti” went with the Lord and the other devotees of the Kshetra to Lake Narendra (Murari, 4.17.22). <!-- F32 Q8 -->

And when the Lord came back from His attempted journey to Vrindavana, from Kanai-natashala, Shikhi Mahiti was again
among those who met Him:

“The delighted devotees came to meet Him, and the Lord embraced them all with love. Kashi Mishra, Ramananda, Pradyumna,
Sarvabhauma, Vaninatha, Shikhi and all the other devotees came.” (CC, Madhya 16.253–254) <!-- F31 Q7 -->

His name is heard once more in the “Charitamrita,” when Bhagavan Acharya, wishing to feed the Lord, sends Junior
Haridasa for fine rice:

“Go in my name to Shikhi Mahiti's sister, ask her for one *mana* of white rice and bring it.” (CC, Antya 2.103)

What came of this request is told in the articles on Madhavi-devi and Junior Haridasa. <!-- F34 -->

## Three and a Half
Immediately afterwards Kaviraja Gosvami reveals who Shikhi's sister and Shikhi himself were in the Lord's eyes:

“The Lord counted her among Radhika's associates. In all the world there are only three and a half such worthy
persons: Svarupa Gosani, Ramananda Raya and Shikhi Mahiti are the three, and his sister is the half.” (CC, Antya
2.105–106)

The Lord Himself “enrols” (*lekha kare*) them in Radhika's company (*gana*): these three and a half are the only ones in all the world
worthy (*patra*) of His most intimate bhava, the mood of Sri Radha. Their closeness to the Lord is not worldly
friendship or kinship but the closeness of service to Radha. It is the “Charitamrita” itself that calls Madhavi the
“half,” and we shall not interpret the word on our own. <!-- F4 Q3 -->

Who they are in Krishna's eternal pastimes, Karnapura tells us:

“Ragalekha and Kalakeli, Radha's maidservants, are to be known as Shikhi Mahiti and his sister Madhavi respectively.”
(“Gaura-ganoddesha-dipika” 189)

Ragalekha is one of two maidservants (*dasi*) of Sri Radha; the other, Kalakeli, is Madhavi. <!-- F2 Q1 -->

Kaviraja Gosvami also names Shikhi Mahiti as a branch of Sri Chaitanya's tree:

“Other branches are King Prataparudra, Krishnananda of Orissa, Paramananda Mahapatra, Shivananda of Orissa, Bhagavan
Acharya, Brahmananda Bharati, Sri Shikhi Mahiti and Murari Mahiti.” (CC, Adi 10.135–136)

One of the biographies calls him one of the Lord's personal branches and one of His closest companions: the
“Charitamrita,” the “Chaitanya-bhagavata” and the “Bhakti-ratnakara” taken together show how dear Shikhi Mahiti was
to Sri Chaitanya. <!-- F5 Q4 F33 I2 -->

## After the Lord's Disappearance
Shikhi Mahiti remained in this world after the Lord had withdrawn from sight. When Srinivasa came to Nilachala,
the Lord's companions, Shikhi Mahiti among them, had in separation from Gaura given up food, sleep and all else and
ceaselessly sang the name, form, qualities and pastimes of Gaurasundara. <!-- F35 -->

Srinivasa received the blessings of all whom he found in Puri: Ramananda, Paramananda Puri, Sarvabhauma, Vakreshvara,
Govinda, Shankara, Gopinatha Acharya, and Shikhi Mahiti. The “Bhakti-ratnakara” tells how he came to his house:

“Srinivasa went to the house of Shikhi Mahiti; there he met many people. When Srinivasa bowed, all embraced him;
Srinivasa was soaked with the tears of them all. <…> Shikhi Mahiti with his sister says: ‘Only to see you are we still
alive.’” (“Bhakti-ratnakara” 3.181–182, 185)

One of the biographies adds that from Srinivasa's unprecedented love for Gaura the Lord's companions understood that he
was Gaura-shakti, the power of Gaura, and that Sri Chaitanya's message and that of the scriptures would be spread by
him, and they gave him important instructions. <!-- F36 Q9 I3 -->

Later Narottama came to Puri. The “Narottama-vilasa” relates that on that day, before any news of his arrival had come, the
Lord's companions came out onto the road to the Lion Gate, and Shikhi Mahiti asked Mangaraja: “Why has joy suddenly
risen in my heart?” (“Narottama-vilasa” 4.41). And when Narottama, having calmed himself, had performed his morning
duties, “Gopinatha Acharya, together with Shikhi Mahiti, quickly sent him to have darshan of Jagannatha” (ibid.,
4.166). The “Bhakti-ratnakara” says the same: “Sri Shikhi Mahati and others say to Gopinatha: the time for Sri
Jagannatha's darshan has come” (8).[^8] Shikhi Mahiti is also among the companions whom Narottama saw in a dream
playing with the Lord in the kirtana before the chariot (“Narottama-vilasa” 4.135). <!-- F37 F38 Q10 -->

Nor is he forgotten in Narahari Chakravarti's “Namamrita-samudra,” a book of prayers to the Lord's associates:

“O Shikhi Mahiti, do me this good: may I have love for Sri Krishna Chaitanya and Jagannatha.” (“Namamrita-samudra” 49)
<!-- F41 -->

When Shikhi Mahiti left this world is not known. The sources name neither the day, nor the year, nor the place of his
appearance and departure, nor are his days found in the Vaishnava calendar of the Sri Chaitanya Saraswat Math.
