# Vasudeva Vipra
*Васудева Випра · @vasudeva-vipra*

## A Brahmana of Kurma-kshetra
Among those whom Sri Chaitanya met on His journey through the South there is one whom almost no one knew before he met the
Lord: the brahmana Vasudeva, Sri Vasudeva Vipra. Krishnadasa Kaviraja calls him a “noble brahmana” (*dvija-mahashaya*).
He lived in the south of India, in utter poverty, lowly and unnoticed. Such a devotee — destitute, sick, in no way
distinguished by worldly measures — ordinary people do not recognise; Mahaprabhu Himself revealed his glory to the world.
In his brief summary of the Lord's pastimes Krishnadasa Kaviraja gives him a whole line:

“Then the Lord set out for the South. At Kurma-kshetra He delivered Vasudeva.” (CC, Madhya 1.102)[^1] <!-- F1 F2 F3 F13 I1 Q1 -->

The meeting took place at Kurma-kshetra (Kurmasthana), at the start of the Lord's journey through the South, in 1510,
when Mahaprabhu was twenty-four.[^2] Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati explains in his “Anubhashya” that this is
Kurmachalam, or Srikurmam, in Ganjam district, eight miles east of Chicacole Road station on the B. N. R. line — the
chief holy place of the Telugu-speaking people, where the Deity of Lord Kurma, the Tortoise avatara, resides. Today the
station is called Srikakulam Road and the line the South Eastern Railway; the station lies in the state of Andhra
Pradesh, while Ganjam district is in Orissa. <!-- F4 F5 F8 -->

According to the same commentary, Sri Ramanuja came to Kurma-kshetra: Jagannatha carried him there, and Ramanuja, at
first taking the Deity for a Shiva-linga, fasted until he recognised in Him Vishnu in the form of the Tortoise; then he
opened His worship. In Mahaprabhu's time the temple was under the supervision of the Sri Madhva Math, in the domain of the
king of Vijayanagara. <!-- F6 F7 -->

Nothing more is known of Vasudeva's worldly life. The sources name neither his parents nor the days of his appearance and
disappearance; nor are they in the Vaishnava calendar of the Sri Chaitanya Saraswat Math. When he left this world is also
unknown.

## Leprosy
Vasudeva's whole body was eaten away by oozing leprosy (*galat-kushtha*). Krishnadasa Kaviraja relates:

“There was a noble brahmana named Vasudeva. His whole body was rotting with leprosy and swarming with worms. Whenever a
worm slipped and fell from his body, he would pick it up and put it back in the same place.” (CC, Madhya 7.136–137)
<!-- F9 F10 F11 Q2 -->

The worms fed on his blood and pus, yet he did not drive them away: when one fell out, he lifted it and carefully put it
back. The biographies marvel at this nature of his, and Kavi Karnapura in his drama “Chaitanya-chandrodaya” adds that he
did it without the least disgust, as if his mind were absorbed in the Lord. There was neither worldly asceticism nor
madness in this: so far did the humility and compassion of a devotee reach — even to the worms feeding on his body.[^3]
People fled from the sight and stench of him while still far off; he himself would tell the Lord that even sinners ran
away from him. <!-- F11 F12 -->

## At Kurma's House
The Lord came to Kurma-kshetra and stayed in the house of a local brahmana named Kurma, whom He blessed.[^4] News of Him
reached Vasudeva, and, sick as he was, he hurried in great impatience to Kurma's house. The “Charitamrita” says:

“At night he heard of the Gosani's arrival, and in the morning he came to Kurma's house to see Him. When he heard from
Kurma that the Lord had gone, he fell to the ground unconscious with grief. He began to lament in many ways, and at that
very moment the Lord came and embraced him.” (CC, Madhya 7.138–140) <!-- F14 F15 F16 F17 -->

The Lord is the Supersoul of all beings; though He had already gone far, He knew His devotee's grief and appeared before
him. One of the biographies says plainly that the Lord came back; another says only that, having gone far, He appeared to
Vasudeva and embraced him. What drew Him was the brahmana's heartfelt devotion.[^5] <!-- F18 -->

## The Embrace
The Lord paid no regard to his leprosy. He embraced Vasudeva as one embraces the dearest — out of love for him as His
devotee. The Lord is conquered by devotion alone; worldly merits and defects He does not take into account, nor does He
look at any outward deformity or wretched condition of one wholly devoted to Him. Whoever longs for Him attains Him.
<!-- F19 I2 -->

“At the Lord's touch his leprosy vanished together with his grief, and his body became beautiful and full of bliss.”
(CC, Madhya 7.141)

He received the body of the most handsome of men.[^6] Amazed at such causeless mercy, the brahmana remembered the words of
Sudama — the poor brahmana, Krishna's childhood friend, whom the Lord had once met and embraced in just the same way in
Dvaraka — and glorified the Lord with a verse of the “Bhagavatam”:

“Who am I, a poor and sinful man, and who is Krishna, the abode of the goddess of fortune? Yet because I was born a
brahmana, He embraced me in His arms!” (CC, Madhya 7.143; “Srimad-Bhagavatam” 10.81.16)[^7] <!-- F20 F21 Q3 -->

The healing was neither the purpose of this meeting nor its chief wonder. The Lord embraced Vasudeva not to display His
power but because He loves His devotee; the leprosy left of itself, at the touch of His body. Vasudeva himself had not
asked to be healed — and, as we shall now see, he met his healing with fear rather than joy.

## “Now Pride Will Arise in Me”
After offering the Lord many prayers, Vasudeva said:

“Hear me, O Merciful One: no jiva has this quality; it is found in You alone. Seeing me and smelling my stench, even the
wretched flee from me; yet You touch someone like me. You are the independent Lord! But I was better off being lowly; now
pride will arise in me.” (CC, Madhya 7.144–146) <!-- F22 F25 Q4 -->

Here is the heart of the whole story. What troubles Vasudeva now is not his illness but its cure: he fears that a
beautiful body will bring honour, honour will bring pride, and he will fall. The proud lose the Lord's mercy and even the
right to chant His names; that is why he prayed not for health but that he might not become proud. <!-- I4 -->

## The Order to Become an Acharya
The Lord answered him:

“You will never become proud. Chant the name ‘Krishna! Krishna!’ constantly. Teach about Krishna and deliver the jivas,
and very soon Krishna will accept you as His own.” (CC, Madhya 7.147–148) <!-- F26 Q5 -->

So the Lord found him fully worthy to be an acharya — a teacher who himself chants the name of Krishna without cease and
instructs others in it. He assured Vasudeva that the beauty of his body would not bring him the conceit that usually
comes with it. One of the biographies recalls this in its account of Kurma as well: the Lord embraced Vasudeva, made him
most handsome, delivered him and, blessing him, assured him that his new body would not give rise to pride; then He
ordered him too to deliver all jivas by instructing them in the name of Krishna. <!-- F26 F27 -->

A similar order the Lord gave on the same road to Kurma himself and to all the householders who received Him in their
homes (Madhya 7.127–130). On these verses Srila Bhaktisiddhanta writes in the “Anubhashya”: to those who wish to give up
everything for His service, Mahaprabhu says “stay at home” — that is, give up the pride of one “wholly given to bhajana,”
chant the pure name with humility and preach it. To think “I am the best Vaishnava; if I accept disciples I shall ruin my
bhajana” is the same pride; and whoever considers the books of Rupa, Sanatana, Jiva and Raghunatha Dasa, and the many
disciples of Narottama, Madhva and Ramanuja, an obstacle to bhakti offends the selfless devotees. One who does the work of
a guru with humility will not be swept away by the craving for fame. The words “stay at home” are not addressed to
Vasudeva; but the thought is the same as in the order given to him: humility and preaching the name are the protection
against pride.[^8] <!-- I6 -->

Another comment of Srila Bhaktisiddhanta (on Madhya 7.152) reveals the meaning of these orders. Sri Krishna Chaitanya
awakens consciousness in jivas who lack it; once awakened and turned to the service of Krishna, they in turn, as acharyas,
awaken others. Thus, increasing the family of Achyuta and spreading the path of *shrauta*, Gaurasundara manifests the
glory of His descent. Vasudeva is one of such acharyas, whom the Lord Himself awakened and sent forth. <!-- I7 -->

## Vasudevamrita-prada
“Having said this, the Lord disappeared, and the two brahmanas wept in each other's arms, remembering the Lord's
qualities.” (CC, Madhya 7.149)

So the two brahmanas of Kurma-kshetra — the host in whose house the Lord had stayed and the leper whom He had embraced —
were left alone together, weeping in separation from Him and remembering His mercy.[^9] <!-- F28 -->

Krishnadasa Kaviraja calls this pastime “Vasudeva-uddhara,” “the Deliverance of Vasudeva”; and from then on the Lord
Himself had a new name:

“Thus I have told the story of Vasudeva's deliverance, through which the Lord received the name Vasudevamrita-prada, ‘the
Giver of nectar to Vasudeva.’” (CC, Madhya 7.150)

Srila Bhaktivinoda Thakura notes in the “Amrita-pravaha-bhashya” that this name is among the “hundred names of Sri
Chaitanya” composed by Sarvabhauma Bhattacharya. It also opens the whole seventh chapter of the Madhya-lila: the heading
of the edition is an obeisance to “Vasudevamrita-prada,” and the first verse of the chapter praises the Lord whose heart
melts with compassion, who “cured Vasudeva of leprosy, filled him with beauty and made him content in devotion” (Madhya
7.1). Concluding the account, Krishnadasa Kaviraja sums up: so began the Lord's journey — with His visit to Kurma and the
deliverance of Vasudeva (7.151). <!-- F29 F37 -->

## How Others Tell It
Other primary sources also preserve the story of Vasudeva, and each tells it in its own way.[^10]

Murari Gupta writes that Vasudeva, “the best of brahmanas,” on seeing the Lord recognised Him as Krishna in rapture and
began to dance; “Bhagavan embraced that brahmana, a leper and the best of great devotees, and he shone like gold”
(“Kadacha” 3.14.17–18). To both His devotees, Vasudeva and Kurma, the Lord gave the order to lead people to devotion to
Krishna — and vanished (3.14.19–20). <!-- F31 -->

Kavi Karnapura in his mahakavya relates that Vasudeva, hearing that the Lord had left Puri for the South, reached Kurma's
house with great difficulty. Kurma told him: “Had you come only a moment earlier, you would have seen the Master right
here.” Vasudeva cried out, “He has gone!” and fell unconscious; and then the Lord came back, embraced him and took away
his swoon together with his leprosy. Bowing to the Lord in joy and sorrow, Vasudeva recited the same verse of Sudama and
said: “Alas, this is not mercy — this is a punishment for me!” The Lord replied: “Do not lose heart: one who is free of
selfishness will come to Me,” — and disappeared. Kurma too fell unconscious and lamented bitterly that he had not
recognised God in his guest (“Chaitanya-charita-mahakavya” 12.106–117). <!-- F32 -->

In the drama “Chaitanya-chandrodaya” the story of Vasudeva is told to Sarvabhauma and King Prataparudra by a brahmana
returning from the South. No one had told the Lord about him — the Lord found him Himself and, as one embraces the
closest of friends after long separation, held him tightly, just as he was. The brahmana uttered Sudama's verse, and at
once his body became radiant and beautiful, “as it was by nature.” Then he said: “Lord! Why have You punished me so? While
I was like that — diseased — I lived without sorrow and without agitation. But now, when honour comes to me, all the
movements of the mind will rise again.” The Lord answered: “Brahmana! From now on your mind will turn to no external
affairs, only to the remembrance of the Lord. So give up your anxiety.” Hearing this, the king said: “Bhattacharya! Truly
He is Ishvara. Otherwise such compassion is impossible for a jiva — whereas even a great yogi can cure leprosy” (act 7,
verse 3). <!-- F33 -->

The words “this is a punishment for me” are no complaint against the Lord. They are the speech of humility, the same as in
the “Charitamrita”: “I was better off being lowly.” What frightens Vasudeva is not health as such but the honour and pride
that might draw him away from the Lord. And the king's words draw the same line as the whole story: a yogi too could remove leprosy, but
to embrace a leper as a friend — only the Lord Himself. <!-- F33 -->

Govinda-deva in the “Gaura-krishnodaya” says that Vasudeva lay helpless on the outskirts of the town; learning that the
Lord had stayed with Kurma, he came there fearlessly, and hearing that the Lord had left at dawn, he rolled on the ground,
weeping: “Lord, protect me!” And the Lord, appearing at the threshold of the house, before Kurma's eyes embraced him
again and again. Vasudeva's body became beautiful as that of Kama regaining his body; he cried: “Where am I, with a heart
full of nothing but filth, and where are You, the sole abode of Kamala!” — and fell at the Lord's feet. The Lord raised
him, instructed him and vanished like lightning; and those two, weeping in separation from Gaura, became honoured together
with the townspeople (9.7–12). <!-- F34 -->

Lochana Dasa in the Shesha-khanda writes that Vasudeva lived in the same village as Kurma, and that the Lord met them both
in one place. By the sight of the Lord they became pure, recognised Gaurachandra as Krishna and fell at His feet; the Lord
raised and embraced them both and said: “In the Kali age the dharma is the sankirtana of Hari's name… Rejoice in singing
the names and qualities [of the Lord]; dance, dance — let people be freed from bondage!” Of leprosy Lochana Dasa says
nothing. Srila Bhaktisiddhanta, in his summary of this chapter, says that at Kurma-kshetra the Lord showed mercy to the
brahmana Vasudeva and instructed him that the dharma of the Kali age is the holy name of Sri Hari alone, and from there
came to Jiyada-Nrisimha. The lists of the journey run the same way: the deliverance of Vasudeva, and next Nrisimha of
Jiyada, Simhachalam; so too in the “Charitamrita” (Madhya 8.3). <!-- F35 F30 -->

Finally, “The Harmonist” — Srila Bhaktisiddhanta's journal, in which a biography of the Lord was printed under his
editorship (1928) — says that the Lord showed mercy to the brahmana Vasudeva, afflicted with malignant leprosy, freed him
at once “from the diseases of body and mortality” and made him an acharya; for this the Lord became known as
“Vasudevamrita-prada,” the One who gave Vasudeva nectar, that is, immortality.[^11] Immortality here is not a long life of
the body but release from the cycle of birth and death — the nectar that the Lord's mercy bestows. <!-- F36 -->

## A Vaishnava's Body
Many years later, in Puri, the Lord was embracing Sanatana Gosvami, whose body was covered with sores, and Haridasa Thakura
recalled Vasudeva:

“Vasudeva was a leper with oozing sores, his body full of worms, yet out of compassion You embraced him. By embracing him
You made his body as beautiful as Kandarpa's. We cannot understand the waves of Your mercy.” (CC, Antya 4.189–190)
<!-- F23 -->

The Lord replied:

“A Vaishnava's body is never material. A devotee's body is transcendental (*aprakrita*), full of spirit and bliss
(*cid-ananda-maya*).” (CC, Antya 4.191) <!-- F24 Q6 -->

A devotee's body is not mundane; it is beyond this world, full of consciousness and bliss. One who lacks remembrance of
Krishna sees it only from outside — sores, sickness, poverty — and does not see its spiritual nature. So the Lord Himself
explained why He did not shrink from the sores of Vasudeva or of Sanatana. <!-- I3 -->

## Significance
Vasudeva Vipra shows how the Lord finds a devotee whom the world does not notice. Destitute, sick and shunned, he was a noble
brahmana and the best of devotees, and Mahaprabhu Himself came to him, Himself embraced him and Himself made his glory
known to all. <!-- I1 I2 -->

His story speaks of the mercy of the Lord and of His devotees, who see the sufferings of conditioned souls. The Lord
embraces one from whom everyone flees; Kurma and Vasudeva weep for Him in each other's arms; and Vasudeva, who fears only
pride, the Lord makes an acharya and sends out to deliver others by the name of Krishna. For this the Lord received the
name Vasudevamrita-prada. <!-- I5 -->
