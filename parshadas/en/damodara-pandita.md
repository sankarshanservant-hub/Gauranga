# Damodara Pandita
*Дамодара Пандит (Дамодара Брахмачари) · @damodara-pandita*

## The Brahmachari of Navadvipa
His name was Damodara Pandita, and he was also called Damodara Brahmachari: all his life he kept the vow of celibacy,
and the Lord Himself, speaking of him, called him a brahmachari (CC, Madhya 7.25). He was a brahmana of Navadvipa.
Murari Gupta, telling his poem about the Lord, addresses him as “the brahmana Damodara” and “the best of brahmanas.”
<!-- F1 -->

Who he was in Vraja is revealed by Kavi Karnapura:

“Shaibya, who in Vraja was known for her fiery temper, is Damodara Pandita; for a certain purpose the goddess Sarasvati
also entered into him.” (GGD 159)

The word Karnapura uses for her temper is *chandi*, “sharp, fiery of speech”[^1]. That one word says the main thing
about Damodara in Sri Chaitanya's pastimes as well: none of the associates spoke to the Lord as directly as he did. In
the same place Karnapura names his younger brother, Shankara Pandita, as Sri Bhadra (GGD 157), and Pitambara, of whom
more below, as Kaveri (GGD 168). <!-- F2 F42 Q1 -->

Krishnadasa Kaviraja, listing the branches of Sri Chaitanya's tree, gives him the tenth place — and at once says what
he is remembered for:

“The tenth branch is Sri Damodara Pandita. He had such great love for Sri Chaitanya that once he even rebuked Him.”
(CC, Adi 10.31)

“I shall tell of this in more detail later. Fully pleased with this reproach, Sri Chaitanya sent Damodara to
Navadvipa.” (CC, Adi 10.32)

“The eleventh branch is Shankara Pandita, Damodara's younger brother. Everyone also called him Prabhu-padopadhana (the
pillow for the Lord's feet).” (CC, Adi 10.33)

The love that dares to reproach the Lord Himself the “Charitamrita” calls *prochanda*, “impetuous.” In the language of
the Vaishnavas such a reproach is *vakya-danda*, “chastisement by word.” It is neither impudence nor self-assurance: the
Lord Himself willed this pastime and was Himself “fully pleased” with it. Krishnadasa recalls it in the brief summaries
of his book too: after the Lord punished Junior Haridasa, “Damodara Pandita tried to correct the Lord's behaviour”
(Madhya 1.259); the third chapter of the Antya-lila tells how “Damodara Pandita boldly tried to rebuke the Lord”
(Antya 20.106). <!-- F3 F43 Q2 -->

Kavi Karnapura in the “Chaitanya-chandrodaya-nataka” names Damodara and Shankara among the beloved associates, “His own
since childhood,” who descended to the earth together with the Lord to deliver the world (1.25). Of his family the
“Charitamrita” knows only one thing: Shankara was his younger brother. Later traditions say more. In the
“Vaishnava-vandana” Devakinandana praises “Damodara Pandita, the most dispassionate, and Pitambara, his elder brother
by birth,” and the Sanskrit vandana names five brothers “free from desires” — Damodara, Pitambara, Jagannatha, Shankara
and Narayana. Lochana Dasa may be speaking of the same: “Together with Damodara Pandita, five brothers took birth on
earth in one place” (“Chaitanya-mangala,” Sutra-khanda, pt. 8, v. 630)[^2]. <!-- F64 F66 -->

## Damodara's Questions
The first biography of Sri Chaitanya, Murari Gupta's Sanskrit poem “Sri Krishna-chaitanya-charitamrita,” owes its
existence to Damodara Pandita. The whole of it is a dialogue: Damodara asks, Murari tells. Murari brings him into the
poem with love:

“Hearing of this wonder, the brahmachari Sri Damodara Pandita, master of his senses and intoxicated with the stories of
Sri Chaitanya, said: ‘Tell the divine and wondrous story that purifies the world… For what reason was He, the
all-powerful Lord of all, born on earth? What did He, the Lord of the worlds, accomplish here?’” (Murari, 1.2.15–18)

With this question — why the Lord came — the narrative begins. And after it Damodara's questions, like milestones, open
one part of the poem after another. “Tell us, then, the story of Nrihari,” he asks, and Murari speaks of the Lord's
descents (1.4.1). He asks to hear of Vishvarupa, the Lord's elder brother (1.7.1). “What pastimes did the Lord, the
treasury of pastimes, perform in Navadvipa?” — so the second part of the poem opens (2.1.1). He wishes to hear of the
wondrous form of the Lord revealed in a dream (2.9.1); of how the Lord accepted sannyasa, wandered and came to
Purushottama (3.1.1); of how He beheld the Lord Purushottama (3.11.1). Murari calls his listener “the best of
brahmanas,” “the great soul,” “a man of great power.” <!-- F58 -->

Damodara does not only listen — he also argues. When Murari told how little Vishvambhara, shining with light, said to
His mother, “I am going; guard the body of your son,” and fell senseless, Damodara could not accept it in silence:

“What is this you have told, good friend? Krishna Himself, the teacher of the world, was born here — how then could He
say to His mother, ‘I am going; guard your son, good lady’? About this I have a great doubt. Is this the yogamaya of the
Lord of the world? Please explain it — for the deeds of Hari are done for the good of the world.” (Murari, 1.7.25–27)

Murari, having reflected and bowed to Hari, replied: when a person meditates on Bhagavan, sings of Him and hears of Him,
Hari enters the heart of that great soul, and he, forgetting his own body, bears within him His splendour and might; so
the Lord taught the world that the devotee's body is truly His own (1.8.1–7). Another time, hearing that at
Bhubaneshvara the Lord had reverently eaten the remnants of offerings to Shiva, Damodara objected: because of Bhrigu's
curse they are not eaten — how then could Bhagavan, knowing this, accept them? Murari explained: Mahadeva joyfully
offered Krishna hospitality; and when people worship Maheshvara, regarding him as the best of Vaishnavas, the Lord
accepts what they offer, and such food purifies (3.9.8–21). <!-- F58 -->

And when the poem was finished, Damodara bowed to Murari:

“I have reached my goal, I have reached my goal, I have reached my goal — of this there is no doubt! Blessed are you,
who fill hearts with the rasa of Krishna Chaitanya!” (Murari, 4.26.24) <!-- F58 -->

Lochana Dasa kept the same frame. Opening the “Chaitanya-mangala,” he praises “Pandita Damodara” together with Rupa,
Sanatana and Raghava Pandita (Sutra-khanda, pt. 1, v. 30) and names his source:

“All that the Lord did from His birth in childhood, how from beginning to end He spread prema — about all this Damodara
Pandita questioned him, and he told him everything from beginning to end in due order. So came about a book of
Gauranga's deeds composed in verse — the dialogue with Damodara, issued from Murari's mouth. Hearing it, I was filled
with love, and I tell of Gaura's deeds in a sung narrative, a panchali.” (“Chaitanya-mangala,” Sutra-khanda, pt. 1,
vv. 53–56)

He repeats it at the end of the Madhya-khanda: Murari told it in verses, “Pandita Sri Damodara asked him”; “what I have
heard is by the grace of them both” (Madhya-khanda, pt. 22, vv. 252–253). <!-- F61 F62 -->

In Lochana too the talks of the two friends sound alive. “I make one request — it is a long-standing pain in my heart:
where did Vishvarupa, the Thakura's brother, go?” asks Damodara, and Murari replies, “Listen, listen, Damodara, foremost
of pandits” (Adi-khanda, pt. 5, vv. 423–426). He asks what Vishvambhara's motionless body meant — “Did the Lord display
His yogamaya, or some power?” — and Murari explains that the Lord regards the devotee's body as His own (Adi-khanda,
pt. 8, vv. 630–652). And having heard the answer about Shiva's prasada, Damodara exclaims, “Now the knot is untied!”
(Madhya-khanda, pt. 21, vv. 96–110). According to one of the biographies, Lochana Dasa says that Damodara himself
passed Murari's verses on to him and told him about the Lord; the “Chaitanya-mangala” says nothing of the kind[^3].

## Navadvipa
In Navadvipa Damodara was beside the Lord in the years of kirtana. When the Lord returned from Gaya and His associates
gathered around Him, Lochana Dasa names among them “Rudra Pandita and Pandita Damodara” (“Chaitanya-mangala,”
Madhya-khanda, pt. 1, v. 60). A pada attributed to Yadunatha sings that Damodara sang in the Lord's night kirtana in
Navadvipa. <!-- F65 F72 -->

When the Lord left Navadvipa and accepted sannyasa, “Sri Chandrashekhara Acharya, Pandita Damodara, Vakreshvara and
others” hastened after Him to Katwa (“Chaitanya-mangala,” Madhya-khanda, pt. 16, v. 43). Karnapura in the
“Chaitanya-chandrodaya-nataka” gives the lament of the devotees for the departed Lord: Jagadananda falls senseless,
and Damodara cries out, “O Lord of my life! Where are You, where are You?” (Act 4). In a pada of Vasu Ghosha mourning
the sannyasa his name is heard too: “How will Gadadhara and Damodara live?” <!-- F41 F66 F72 -->

## The Road to Nilachala
From Katwa Nityananda cleverly brought the Lord to Shantipura, to the house of Advaita Acharya. All the people of
Navadvipa came there, and Damodara with them:

“Srivasa, Ramai, Vidyanidhi, Gadadhara, Gangadasa, Vakreshvara, Murari and Shuklambara were there.” (CC, Madhya 3.153)

“Buddhimanta Khan, Nandana, Shridhara, Vijaya, Vasudeva, Damodara, Mukunda and Sanjaya.” (CC, Madhya 3.154)

The Lord stayed in Shantipura ten days. His mother asked Him to settle in Puri so that she could receive news of Him,
and He agreed. Then Advaita gave Him companions:

“Nityananda, Jagadananda Pandita, Damodara Pandita and Mukunda Datta.” (CC, Madhya 3.209)

“Sri Advaita asked these four to accompany Lord Chaitanya to Puri. Mahaprabhu comforted His mother and offered prayers
at her feet.” (CC, Madhya 3.210)[^4]

The “Charitamrita” says that Nityananda, Jagadananda, Damodara and Mukunda were beside themselves with joy at having
the Lord's company (Madhya 1.100)[^5]. <!-- F4 F44 F5 F55 F65 F71 -->

One of the biographies traces their road: Atisara, Panihati, Baranagar, Vriddha-Mantreshvara on the border of Utkala,
and Remuna, where the companions saw Kshirachora-Gopinatha and heard the Lord tell of Madhavendra Puri; another
mentions the way through Chhatrabhoga. In Puri, on seeing Jagannatha, the Lord lost consciousness, and Sarvabhauma
Bhattacharya carried Him to his own house. The four companions, coming after Him to the Lion Gate, learned where the
Lord was and went there too. <!-- F6 F7 -->

According to one of the biographies, the Lord accepted sannyasa in the bright fortnight of the month of Magha, came to
Puri in Phalguna, granted His mercy to Sarvabhauma in Chaitra, and left for the South in Vaishakha. <!-- F9 -->

## Sarvabhauma's Two Verses
Sarvabhauma was an unrivalled master of logic and an interpreter of Vedanta in the spirit of Mayavada. By the Lord's
mercy he gave up his former arguments and became so pure a devotee that, as one of the biographies says, he could no
longer utter the word “mukti” and said “bhakti” instead. Once he went for darshana of Jagannatha together with
Jagadananda and Damodara, and on returning home gave them maha-prasada for the Lord (CC, Madhya 6.248–249). With them
he also sent a palm leaf bearing two verses about the Lord:

“On a palm leaf Sarvabhauma wrote two verses and gave it to Jagadananda, asking him to deliver it to Sri Chaitanya.
Jagadananda went to Mahaprabhu with the prasada and this palm leaf. Mukunda Datta took the leaf from Jagadananda's
hands. Then Mukunda copied the verses onto the wall at the entrance of the room, and after that gave the leaf to Sri
Chaitanya. As soon as the Lord read these verses, He at once tore up the leaf; but the devotees read them on the wall
before Mahaprabhu's room and kept them in their hearts.” (CC, Madhya 6.250–253)

The first glorifies the Lord who came as Sri Chaitanya to teach renunciation, knowledge and devotion to Himself; the
second, the Lord who revived the path of love for Himself, almost lost over the ages (Madhya 6.254–255). Ever since,
these two verses have been “the strings of pearls on the necks of all Mahaprabhu's devotees” (6.256)[^6]. <!-- F8 F45 -->

Karnapura in the “Chaitanya-chandrodaya-nataka” makes Damodara one of the living participants of those days. He and
Jagadananda are with the Lord when Sarvabhauma first receives Him in his house; at the Lord's word he leads Gopinatha
Acharya away to be fed with maha-prasada; it is he who, coming from Sarvabhauma, tells Gopinatha that the heart of the
proud pandita has melted; and it is the two of them who, “hastily throwing back the curtain,” come in to the Lord with
the words: “Master! The Bhattacharya has sent two verses and alms-food — the remnants of Lord Jagannatha's meal”
(Act 6). <!-- F66 -->

    “fieriness” of Shaibya and of Damodara is not worldly anger but the directness of love, which will not keep silent
    about anything that, to its mind, does not befit the beloved. Karnapura does not name the reason why Sarasvati, the
    goddess of speech, entered into Damodara.
[^2]: The biographies speak only of Shankara. The “Gaura-ganoddesha-dipika” (168) names Pitambara but says nothing of
    his kinship with Damodara; only the “Vaishnava-vandana” considers him Damodara's elder brother. The meaning of
    Lochana Dasa's verse is not entirely clear.
[^3]: One of the biographies, retelling the “Chaitanya-mangala,” writes that Lochana Dasa heard Murari's verses from
    Damodara Pandita, who taught them to him, and that he also heard from Damodara the stories of the
    “Jaimini-bharata” and of Vishvarupa's sannyasa. The “Chaitanya-mangala” itself — both in the edition of Srila
    Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati (1922) and in the edition of 1983 — says only that Damodara questioned Murari and that
    Lochana “heard” their dialogue (Sutra-khanda, pt. 1, vv. 53–56; pt. 2, v. 4; Madhya-khanda, pt. 22, vv. 252–253);
    both stories Lochana gives as a conversation between Murari and Damodara.
[^4]: According to one of the biographies, it was Shachi Devi who told Damodara to accompany the Lord; according to
    another, the mother's request concerned only the Lord's living in Puri. According to the “Charitamrita,” it was
    Advaita Acharya who gave the Lord His companions.
[^5]: The primary sources tell this differently. According to the “Chaitanya-bhagavata,” the Lord was accompanied on
    the road to Puri by Nityananda, Gadadhara, Mukunda, Govinda and Brahmananda, while Damodara Pandita came to
    Nilachala later, together with Shankara Pandita — after Svarupa Damodara and the associates from Orissa: “Also,
    after a couple of days, Damodara Pandita and Sri Shankara Pandita came there” (CB, Antya 3.185). According to the
    “Chaitanya-mangala,” the Lord set out with Srinivasa, Murari, Mukunda and Damodara (Madhya-khanda, pt. 20, v. 83).
    The “Gaura-krishnodaya” and the late “Advaita-prakasha” (15.113) also name him as the Lord's companion on this
    road. We follow the “Charitamrita,” according to which Shankara came to Puri later, with the devotees of Bengal
    (Madhya 11.146).
[^6]: One of the biographies takes these two verses to be the first of the hundred verses of a hymn that Sarvabhauma
    composed on beholding the Lord's six-armed form, and says that Mukunda wrote them on the wall before the Lord's
    dwelling. According to the “Charitamrita,” the hymn of a hundred verses is a separate song (Madhya 6.257–258), and
    the two verses on the leaf are distinct (6.254–255).
