# Jagadananda Pandita
*Джагадананда Пандит · @jagadananda-pandita*

## The Very Life of the Lord
Krishnadasa Kaviraja, listing the branches of the tree of Sri Chaitanya, names Pandita Jagadananda as the sixth branch —
“the very life of Sri Chaitanya,” a manifestation of Queen Satyabhama (CC, Adi 10.21). And at once he speaks of what
was to become the keynote of his whole life:

“Jagadananda always cared for Sri Chaitanya. But Sri Chaitanya would accept nothing that would be an impermissible
comfort for a sannyasi.” (CC, Adi 10.22)

Jagadananda longed to cherish and look after the Lord with love, while the Lord, guarding His renunciation from the
gossip of people, would not accept his care. To an onlooker it seemed that the two argued and quarrelled, but those
quarrels, says Kaviraja, were born only of love — and he promises to tell of it more fully later (Adi 10.23).
<!-- F3 Q2 -->

His name is Sri Jagadananda Pandita. Sanatana Gosvami respectfully calls him “Pandita-mahashaya” (CC, Antya 13.58),
while the Lord Himself, rebuking him, calls him with affectionate reproach simply “Jaga” — “Jaga, a mere boy of
yesterday” (CC, Antya 4.158, 4.167). Vrindavana dasa Thakura, glorifying Gaurachandra, praises Him as Jagadananda's life:

“Glory to Him, the life and soul of Sri Jagadananda and Shrigarbha!” (CB, Madhya 7.3) <!-- F1 F4 Q3 -->

The Lord relished different rasas with different associates. Krishnadasa Kaviraja says:

“Sri Chaitanya relished the feelings of parental love with Paramananda Puri, friendship with Ramananda Raya, service
with Govinda and others, and the rasa of conjugal love with Gadadhara, Jagadananda and Svarupa Damodara. These four
rasas gave Him indescribable joy, and for this He felt Himself forever indebted to these devotees.” (CC, Madhya 2.78)

Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati explains in the “Anubhashya” that Gadadhara, Jagadananda and Svarupa served the Lord
in the chief of the rasas, madhura-rasa, and the Lord felt Himself their debtor. Jagadananda's mood is above all
madhura, especially sweet to the Lord; by it he won His heart. <!-- F5 Q7 Q20 -->

## Origins
Where and when Jagadananda was born, who his parents were, of what family and caste he came — the biographies do not
say. One of them remarks that for one who seeks bhakti the details of a guru's worldly origin do not matter. We can
only conclude that he came from Bengal: from the very beginning he took part in the kirtanas of Navadvipa, and the Lord
Himself names him among the Vaishnavas who appeared on the soil of Bengal (CC, Antya 7.48–49). His days of appearance
and departure are found neither in the biographies nor in our calendar (Sri Chaitanya Saraswat Math). <!-- F7 I6 -->

## In the Kirtanas of Navadvipa
When the Lord returned from Gaya and began the sankirtana, Jagadananda was with Him from its first days.

“Every night the devotees sang kirtana in the house of Srivasa, or sometimes in the house of Chandrashekhara.” (CB,
Madhya 8.111)

Listing those who attended these night kirtanas, Vrindavana dasa Thakura names Jagadananda as well (Madhya 8.113), and
then adds:

“They all danced together with the Lord. Only the Lord's associates were there, and no one else.” (CB, Madhya 8.117)
<!-- F8 Q4 -->

He was also in the great sankirtana procession through Navadvipa on the day the Lord subdued the Kazi: the
“Chaitanya-bhagavata,” among the masters of kirtana who went with the Lord that day, names Govinda, Jagadananda,
Nandana Acharya, Shuklambara and others (Madhya 23.150–152). And when at the end of that day, at Shridhara's house, the
Lord drank water from his leaking iron pot, all the devotees wept:

“Govinda, Govindananda, Shrigarbha, Shriman, Kashishvara, Sri Jagadananda and Rama also wept.” (CB, Madhya 23.451)
<!-- F10 F9 -->

## The Road to Nilachala
Having accepted sannyasa, the Lord set out from Shantipura for Nilachala. Advaita Acharya asked four to accompany Him:

“Nityananda, Jagadananda Pandita, Damodara Pandita and Mukunda Datta. Sri Advaita asked these four to accompany Lord
Chaitanya to Puri.” (CC, Madhya 3.209–210)[^1] <!-- F11 -->

One of the biographies traces their route: Atisara, Chhatrabhoga, northern Orissa, the Suvarnarekha river, Jaleshvara,
Remuna, Jajpur, the Vaitarani river, Cuttack, Sakshi-Gopala, Bhuvaneshvara, Kamalapura and Athara-nala. On the way the
Lord taught His companions detachment, selflessness and complete reliance on the Lord. <!-- F12 -->

Having crossed the border of Orissa, at a place called Ganga-ghat, the Lord bathed, left the devotees in a temple and
Himself went begging alms through the village. Returning with rice and vegetables, He gave them to Jagadananda, and he
cooked the meal:

“Jagadananda cooked the food with a contented heart, and the Lord ate it together with the devotees.” (CB, Antya 2.162)
<!-- F13 -->

On this journey Jagadananda carried the Lord's staff — the sannyasi's danda — and collected alms. Once, going for alms,
he left the danda with Nityananda:

“‘Please take care of the Lord's danda. I shall collect alms and be back in a few minutes.’” (CB, Antya 2.204)

Nityananda broke the danda into three pieces. One of the biographies sees a lesson in this: a Vaishnava, accepting
sannyasa, accepts the tridanda. Jagadananda, on returning, picked up the pieces and brought them to the Lord; the Lord
was saddened and wished to go on alone, and His companions chose to follow Him at a distance (CB, Antya
2.202–222)[^2]. Later, listing the “offences” His intimates had done Him, the Lord would recall this too: “On the way
to Jagannatha Puri You broke My danda” (CC, Madhya 7.20). <!-- F14 F15 Q5 -->

At Athara-nala the Lord saw Krishna with His flute on the summit of the temple and ran ahead alone. Nityananda,
Jagadananda, Mukunda and Damodara, reaching the temple, the Lion Gate, learned that the Lord, who had lost
consciousness, had been carried to the house of Sarvabhauma Bhattacharya, and Gopinatha Acharya took them there. Thus,
according to one of the biographies, Jagadananda first met Sarvabhauma. <!-- F16 -->

## The Two Shlokas of Sarvabhauma
After the Lord had delivered Sarvabhauma, Sarvabhauma once went with Jagadananda and Damodara for darshan of Jagannatha
and, on returning, sent much maha-prasada to the Lord through them. One of the biographies relates that, having beheld
the Lord's six-armed form, Sarvabhauma composed a hymn of a hundred shlokas; two of them he placed in Jagadananda's
hands[^3].

“On a palm leaf Sarvabhauma wrote two shlokas and handed it to Jagadananda, asking him to give it to Sri Chaitanya.
Jagadananda went to Mahaprabhu with the prasada and this palm leaf. Mukunda Datta took the leaf from Jagadananda's
hands. Then Mukunda copied the shlokas onto the wall at the entrance to the room, and only after that handed the leaf
to Sri Chaitanya. As soon as the Lord read these shlokas He at once tore up the leaf, but the bhaktas read them on the
wall in front of Mahaprabhu's room and kept them in their hearts.” (CC, Madhya 6.250–253)[^4] <!-- F17 F18 -->

These are the two shlokas:

“I take shelter of the Supreme Lord, Sri Krishna, who has descended in the form of Sri Chaitanya Mahaprabhu to teach
true knowledge, loving devotion to Himself and renunciation of all that does not further love for Sri Krishna. He has
descended because He is an ocean of supreme mercy. I humbly surrender to His lotus feet.”

“May my mind, like a bee intoxicated with the nectar of a flower, cling to the fragrant feet of the Supreme Lord who has
come as Sri Chaitanya Mahaprabhu. He has revived the path of loving devotion to Sri Krishna — to Himself — for this path
had been almost lost under the relentless influence of time.” (CC, Madhya 6.254–255; “Chaitanya-chandrodaya-nataka”
6.74–75)

“These two shlokas are strands of pearl necklaces on the necks of all Mahaprabhu's devotees; like the roll of a drum,
they proclaim the glory of Sarvabhauma Bhattacharya.” (CC, Madhya 6.256)[^5] <!-- F19 Q8 -->

## “Out of Fear I Do Whatever He Says”
According to one of the biographies, the Lord accepted sannyasa in the bright fortnight of the month of Magha, came to
Nilachala in Phalguna, delivered Sarvabhauma in Chaitra and left for the South in Vaishakha. <!-- F20 -->

Leaving for the South — as He said, in search of Vishvarupa — the Lord wished to go alone. Nityananda objected and
asked Him to take at least one or two companions. And then the Lord, as if accusing them, listed the “transgressions”
of His intimates: Nityananda, when after sannyasa He was going to Vrindavana, had brought Him to Advaita's house, and
on the way to Puri had broken His danda (CC, Madhya 7.19–20). And of Jagadananda the Lord said that he wants Him to
enjoy worldly pleasures, and that out of fear of hurting him He does whatever he asks (Madhya 7.21):

“And if I act against his words, he gets angry with Me and may not speak to Me for three whole days.” (CC, Madhya 7.22)
<!-- F21 Q9 -->

This is a reproach only in appearance. Under the guise of reproaches the Lord was relishing the virtues of His
devotees: every “accusation” is praise of their love. Jagadananda did not wish worldly enjoyments for the Lord: he
simply could not bear that the Lord's body knew neither rest nor care, and he tried with all his might to serve Him.
And the Lord's “fear” of him is the power of a devotee's love over the Lord Himself, who gladly submits to it. In the
end the Lord agreed to take with Him a simple brahmana, Krishnadasa. <!-- I5 -->

While the Lord wandered in the South, the devotees in Puri suffered bitterly in separation from Him. When, on His way
back, He reached Alalanatha and sent Krishnadasa ahead with the news, Nityananda hurried to meet Him, and others with
Him:

“Eager to see Sri Chaitanya, Jagadananda, Damodara Pandita and Mukunda danced with happiness, unable to master their
feelings.” (CC, Madhya 9.340) <!-- F22 Q10 -->

The deeper the love, the sharper the pain of separation; and true love is known by the force of joy at meeting again.
<!-- I2 -->

Soon the same four resolved to tell the Lord's mother and the devotees of Bengal of His return:

“Then a plan ripened in the minds of four devotees — Nityananda, Jagadananda, Mukunda and Damodara: ‘We must send
someone to Bengal to tell Shachimata that Sri Chaitanya has arrived in Jagannatha Puri.’” (CC, Madhya 10.67–68)

The Lord agreed, and Kala Krishnadasa set out for Bengal with maha-prasada for all the Vaishnavas (Madhya 10.73–74).
<!-- F23 -->

## In Nilachala
In Puri Jagadananda gradually grew close to all the Lord's devotees. When Svarupa Damodara came from Varanasi, he,
having bowed to the Lord, met Jagadananda, Mukunda, Shankara and Sarvabhauma and offered them due respect (CC, Madhya
10.127); and Ramananda Raya, returning to Puri, honoured Nityananda, Paramananda Puri, Brahmananda Bharati, Svarupa,
Jagadananda, Mukunda and others. He came to know Haridasa Thakura as well. <!-- F24 -->

He became one of the Lord's constant companions in Nilachala:

“Gadadhara Pandita, Vakreshvara, Damodara, Shankara and Haridasa Thakura remained to live with the Lord in Puri.
Jagadananda, Bhagavan, Govinda, Kashishvara, Paramananda Puri and Svarupa Damodara also moved there.” (CC, Madhya
1.252–253)[^6]

Elsewhere too the “Charitamrita” names him among the old companions who had formerly accompanied the Lord everywhere
and, when He settled in Puri, remained to serve Him there (Adi 10.124–127). Together with Puri Gosvami, Svarupa,
Damodara, Govinda and Kashishvara he went every morning with the Lord for darshan of Jagannatha. <!-- F26 Q6 -->

He especially loved to feed the Lord and the devotees with what he had cooked himself. When the Bengali devotees first
came to Puri and the Lord Himself began serving them prasada, no one would start eating; then Svarupa seated the Lord
at the meal together with Nityananda, and others began to serve:

“In jubilation Svarupa, Damodara Pandita and Jagadananda began to help him.” (CC, Madhya 11.208) <!-- F25 F29 -->

Soon after, on the eve of the Ratha-yatra, after the cleansing of the Gundicha temple, the Lord sat down with the
devotees to a meal in the garden:

“Seven devotees — Damodara Gosvami, Jagadananda, Damodara Pandita, Kashishvara, Gopinatha, Vaninatha and Shankara —
served prasada to all, while the others joyfully cried, ‘Hari! Hari!’” (CC, Madhya 12.163)[^7] <!-- F28 -->

Here Jagadananda's love showed itself in its own way:

“Serving prasada, Jagadananda, as if by chance, placed the tastiest dishes before Sri Chaitanya. Chaitanya made a show
of being angry. And yet, when by such tricks, against His will, such prasada reached Him, at heart He was glad of it.
Having received this prasada, He looked at it for some time, but then, fearing to hurt Jagadananda, tasted a little.
Chaitanya knew that if He did not eat the food Jagadananda offered, he would fast, and so He began to eat it.” (CC,
Madhya 12.169–172) <!-- F27 -->

Many years later, at the festival in honour of the departure of Haridasa Thakura, he tirelessly served prasada together
with Svarupa Damodara, Kashishvara and Shankara (CC, Antya 11.84). One of the biographies sees a lesson in this:
serving prasada to devotees is an important part of devotional service. <!-- F30 I4 -->

When the Lord first tried to go to Vrindavana by way of Gauda — through Panihati, Kumarahatta and Kulia, spending five
days in Vidyanagara and reaching Ramakeli — Jagadananda was with Him. Among the Lord's companions at Ramakeli the
“Charitamrita” names Nityananda, Haridasa, Srivasa, Gadadhara, Mukunda, Jagadananda, Murari and Vakreshvara (Madhya
1.212–224); the Lord told them to bless Rupa and Sanatana. At Kanai-natashala, because of the huge crowds, the Lord
turned back. And when, having at last visited Vrindavana, He returned to Puri through the forests of Jharikhanda,
Jagadananda again knew the pain of separation — and the indescribable joy of meeting again. <!-- F31 F32 -->

## In the House of Shivananda
Part of his time Jagadananda spent in Bengal, in the house of Shivananda Sena. Once the Lord sent word through
Shrikanta Sena, Shivananda's nephew, that the Bengali devotees should not come to Puri that year: He would come to
Bengal Himself.

“Gladden Shivananda Sena with the news that in the winter month of Pausha I shall be an unexpected guest in his house.
Jagadananda also lives with him, and he will cook for Me.” (CC, Antya 2.42–43)

Shivananda and Jagadananda, having prepared everything, waited for the Lord every day until evening throughout the
month of Pausha, but He did not come, and both were deeply saddened. Then Nrisimhananda Brahmachari came to them and
promised that in three days he would bring the Lord before them; both knew his power and believed him (CC, Antya
2.44–48). <!-- F33 -->

## The Nectar of Intimacy
When Sanatana Gosvami came from Vraja to Nilachala through the forests of Jharikhanda, his body was covered with sores.
The Lord, heedless of his pleas, embraced him again and again, and Sanatana was tormented by the thought that the
discharge from his sores was touching the Lord's body. One day, after a talk about Krishna, he asked Jagadananda's
advice, and Jagadananda advised him, after seeing the Ratha-yatra, to go to Vrindavana — the land the Lord had given
him (CC, Antya 4.155–156)[^8]. <!-- F34 -->

Learning of this, the Lord displayed a play of anger at Jagadananda:

“‘Jagadananda is still a boy, but he has already become so proud that he thinks himself entitled to advise even you
and other senior Vaishnavas. In spiritual and worldly matters you always stand in the position of guru to him. And he
tries to instruct you, without any understanding of his own position.’” (CC, Antya 4.158–159)

Everyone respects your authority, the Lord went on, even I value your advice — how then does this boy dare to
instruct you? (Antya 4.160) <!-- F35 -->

But Sanatana heard something quite different in these words. Falling at the Lord's feet, he said, “Now I understand
how great is the good fortune of Jagadananda Pandita” (Antya 4.161), and went on:

“Lord! You let Jagadananda constantly drink the nectar of Your love. But me, while praising me before everyone, You
give only the bitter juice of nimba and nishinda.” (CC, Antya 4.163)

The Lord was a little embarrassed and, wishing to console Sanatana, replied:

“‘Jagadananda Pandita is dear to Me, but you, Sanatana, are far dearer. Yet I cannot bear it when anyone violates
spiritual etiquette.’” (CC, Antya 4.166)

Sanatana is a recognised master of the shastras, while Jagadananda is a young novice; that, said the Lord, is why it
is unbearable for Me to hear that he gives you advice (Antya 4.167–169). And to Sanatana the Lord said that He saw in
him a pure devotee: He is the guardian and the devotees are His children, and it does not befit Him, a sannyasi, to
judge Sanatana's body as mundane (more on this in the life of Sanatana Gosvami). <!-- F36 F37 Q11 -->

This lila is a lesson in Vaishnava etiquette. Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati explains: each has his own position, and
one who oversteps his measure and, thinking himself important, gives advice to one worthy of respect violates
etiquette; such violations in juniors, as in Jagadananda, the Lord checked. Thus He showed Sanatana's seniority and
taught all to honour senior Vaishnavas. But the reproach itself is mercy: the Lord says that Jagadananda is dear to Him,
and Sanatana sees in His scolding not disgrace but the nectar of intimacy. If the Lord scolds a devotee, the devotee
should consider it great good fortune. One of the biographies remarks that even after Sanatana's words the Lord still
regarded Jagadananda's act as presumption. <!-- I3 I7 Q21 -->

## The Love of Satyabhama
When the learned Vallabha Bhatta came to Puri, the Lord told him that He Himself had grasped the essence of bhakti by
the mercy of the Vaishnavas of Bengal — and named among them Acharyaratna, Acharyanidhi, Gadadhara Pandita,
Jagadananda, Damodara, Shankara, Vakreshvara, Kashishvara, Mukunda, Vasudeva, Murari and many others (CC, Antya
7.48–49):

“‘Everywhere they all preach the glory of chanting the holy names of God and the enchanting beauty of love for
Krishna. Thanks to them all I have understood the essence of unalloyed devotion to Krishna.’” (CC, Antya 7.50)
<!-- F77 Q12 -->

Telling of the same time, Krishnadasa Kaviraja reveals the nature of Jagadananda's love:

“The pure and most profound love that Jagadananda Pandita had for Sri Chaitanya Mahaprabhu can be compared only with
the supreme love that Queen Satyabhama had for Krishna, while often quarrelling with Him. Jagadananda Pandita often
started loving quarrels with Sri Chaitanya. Yet these quarrels brought joy to them both.” (CC, Antya
7.138–139)[^9]

Satyabhama's love is vamya: wilful, unyielding, it shows itself in mana, the proud pique of the beloved, and in loving
quarrels — pranaya-kalaha. That is why Jagadananda, being Satyabhama, so often contended with Mahaprabhu. His anger,
silences and fasts are not worldly touchiness but sweet expressions of this prema, which the Lord Himself relishes.
<!-- F6 Q13 -->

## Ramachandra Puri
Mahaprabhu and Paramananda Puri honoured Ramachandra Puri as a disciple of Madhavendra Puri. When he came to Nilachala,
Jagadananda invited him to his place[^10], brought much prasada of Jagannatha from the temple and fed his guest.
Having eaten his fill, Ramachandra Puri himself began to serve out the remnants, piling them again and again on
Jagadananda's plate, and when he had eaten, began to reproach him:

“‘I have often heard that the followers of Chaitanya always overeat. And it really is so! Today I have seen it for
myself!’” (CC, Antya 8.13)

Ramachandra Puri always behaved like this: first he overfed people, then he condemned them (Antya 8.15); according to
one of the biographies, he himself deliberately ate a great deal so as to have something to reproach afterwards.
Kaviraja, in the view of another biography, here contrasts the sincerity of Jagadananda with the hypocrisy of
Ramachandra Puri — a disciple of Madhavendra only in appearance, who had lost the mercy of his guru. <!-- F38 -->

## Messenger to the Mother
Mahaprabhu deeply honoured His mother and, even after accepting sannyasa, continued to serve her.

“Jagadananda Pandita was very dear to Mahaprabhu, and the Lord delighted in his conduct. Every year Mahaprabhu sent him
to Nadia to console His mother, knowing how she suffered in separation from Him.” (CC, Antya 19.4–5)

Jagadananda brought Shachi Devi the Lord's obeisances and words, cloth-prasada of Jagannatha, the finest prasada of His
temple, sweets and more (Antya 19.6–13)[^11], and spoke with her at length about her son, easing her separation.
<!-- F39 -->

Once, in the year when the Lord told Nityananda not to come to Puri, Jagadananda, coming to Shachi, revealed a secret
to her: “Mother, sometimes Chaitanya comes to you and eats your food, and then says, ‘Today Mother has fed Me to the
full.’ He eats everything you offer to God, and you think you dreamed it.” And Shachi admitted that sometimes it seems
to her that Nimai has eaten everything on the tray, but afterwards she takes it for a dream (CC, Antya 12.86–95). So
she came to believe that it had all been real. <!-- F40 -->

Meeting him plunged the devotees of Navadvipa into an ocean of happiness. Murari Gupta and Vasudeva Datta surrounded
him with care and would not let him go, listening to his intimate stories of the Lord (Antya 12.96–100); according to
one of the biographies, after the chaturmasya Vasudeva, Murari and Jagadananda came together to Shachi with news from
Puri. Kaviraja says:

“Pandita Jagadananda, who attained the divine love of Sri Chaitanya, is truly worthy of the highest glory. Anyone who
met him felt that he had met Chaitanya Himself.” (CC, Antya 12.101) <!-- F41 -->

## The Sandalwood Oil
Once, returning from Navadvipa, Jagadananda stayed in the house of Shivananda Sena. There they prepared one matra —
about fifteen kilograms — of the fragrant oil “chandanadi,” made of sandalwood with many aromatic additions (CC, Antya
12.102).

“With this fragrant sandalwood oil they filled a large jar, and carefully Jagadananda carried it to Sri
Jagannatha-Puri.” (CC, Antya 12.103)

In Puri he left the jar with Govinda and asked him to anoint the Lord's head with this oil (Antya 12.104). Govinda
reported to the Lord: Jagadananda has brought this oil from Bengal with great care; it is excellent for soothing
disorders of vayu and pitta. The Lord replied: <!-- F42 -->

“‘A sannyasi has no need whatever of oil. And oil with many aromatic additions he should not even touch. Give this oil
to the temple of Jagannatha, to be burned in the lamps. Then Jagadananda's labours will meet with full success.’” (CC,
Antya 12.108–109)

Thus, in the view of one of the biographies, the Lord set an example for all who have renounced the world. <!-- F43 -->

Jagadananda, hearing this, said nothing. Ten days later Govinda again conveyed his wish to the Lord, and the Lord
indignantly asked whether He should not hire a masseur as well (Antya 12.110–112):

“‘Why then did I become a sannyasi, renouncing worldly joys? It seems you are mocking Me, calling down My ruin by this.
If anyone, passing by Me, catches the scent of this oil, he will at once think that I am a dari-sannyasi (or a
debauched tantrika).’” (CC, Antya 12.113–114) <!-- F44 Q14 -->

This anger is part of the lila of a renounced sannyasi. The Lord needs neither austerity nor comforts, and His body is
not bound by worldly needs; rejecting the oil, He teaches the renunciants, while He Himself relishes His devotee's love
in all its shades.

Next morning the Lord Himself told Jagadananda: “You know that I am a sannyasi and cannot accept this oil; give it to
the temple of Jagannatha, and your labour will be crowned with success” (Antya 12.115–117).

“Then Jagadananda asked: ‘Who has told You all these fables? I brought no oil from Bengal.’ Saying this, Jagadananda
carried the jar of fragrant oil out of the room, and in the courtyard broke it before Chaitanya. Having broken the jar
of fragrant oil, Jagadananda went off to his own house, and lay down, firmly locking the door.” (CC, Antya 12.118–120)
<!-- F45 -->

He lay there without rising or taking food[^12]. And then, to soften his mana, the Lord Himself came to him:
<!-- F46 -->

“Three days later Chaitanya Mahaprabhu came to his door and said: ‘Get up, My dear Jagadananda! I want you today to
cook your lunch for Me. Now I am going to Jagannatha's temple. I shall come to you afterwards, by noon.’” (CC, Antya
12.121–122)

Jagadananda got up, bathed and set about cooking. When the Lord came, he washed His feet, seated Him in the place of
honour, heaped the finest rice on a banana leaf, poured ghee over it, set the vegetable dishes around it in little cups
of banana bark, adorned everything with tulasi leaves and served cakes, sweet rice and much other prasada of Jagannatha
(Antya 12.123–126). <!-- F47 -->

The Lord told him to put rice and vegetables on a second leaf for Jagadananda himself and, raising His hands, would not
touch the food until he sat down with Him. But Jagadananda asked the Lord to eat first, promising to eat afterwards
(Antya 12.127–129). One of the biographies remarks: thus his mana melted away. <!-- F48 -->

Having tasted the meal, the Lord said:

“‘Truly, you have attained the mercy of Krishna! For all that you cook, even in anger, turns out so nectarean. You cook
so deliciously because Krishna, wishing to taste it all, cooks it all Himself through you. You offer Krishna rice that
can be compared with nectar in taste. Who can describe the limit of your good fortune?’” (CC, Antya 12.131–133)

“Jagadananda answered Him thus: ‘Yes, the cook is the one who will eat. I have only gathered the ingredients.’” (CC,
Antya 12.134) <!-- F49 Q15 -->

Jagadananda kept on serving more and more, and the Lord, fearing that otherwise he would fast again, did not dare to
refuse and ate ten times more than usual, until at last He begged the Pandita to stop (Antya 12.135–139). <!-- F50 -->

When the Lord rose and washed His hands, Jagadananda offered Him spices, sandal paste and a garland. The Lord sat down
and said that He wished to see him eat. “Go, Lord, and rest,” Jagadananda replied. “Ramai and Raghunatha helped me
cook; first I shall feed them.” The Lord left Govinda to see whether the Pandita ate; but Jagadananda sent Govinda to
massage the Lord's feet, promising to keep for him the remnants from His plate. He fed the remnants of the Lord's meal
to Ramai, Raghunatha Bhatta, Govinda and Nandai, and honoured them himself; and only when Govinda told the Lord that
Jagadananda was eating did the Lord fall peacefully asleep (Antya 12.140–151). <!-- F51 -->

Kaviraja concludes that Jagadananda's love for the Lord is like Satyabhama's love for Krishna described in the
“Bhagavata” (Antya 12.152), and continues:

“Is anyone able to grasp what good fortune fell to Jagadananda? There is no other example of such good fortune. Anyone
who hears of that love between Chaitanya and Jagadananda, or reads his work ‘Prema-vivarta,’ will be able to understand
the nature of that love. He will surely attain the wealth of divine love for Sri Krishna.” (CC, Antya 12.153–154)
<!-- F52 Q16 -->

The broken jar, the locked door and the fast are not resentment or hot temper but the vamya of Satyabhama, a loving
quarrel in which the devotee is “angry” with the Lord out of love, and the Lord out of love “fears” him, humbles
Himself before him and comes Himself to make peace. The Lord's refusals of comfort, for their part, are a lesson to
renunciants, not coldness towards His devotee. <!-- I1 -->

## “Prema-vivarta”
Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati explains that the word “prema-vivarta” in this verse of the “Charitamrita” means both
the “transformations of love” — Jagadananda's loving quarrels with the Lord — and Jagadananda's book of that name. The
“Prema-vivarta,” written in Bengali, was published in 1905 by Srila Bhaktivinoda Thakura. One of the biographies cites
a thought from it: it is not enough to say “I belong to Gaura” — one must follow Gaura's conduct and teachings.
<!-- F53 Q21 Q23 -->

## The Bed of Banana Bark
In His last years the Lord, tormented by separation from Krishna, became utterly emaciated.

“Separation from Krishna wasted the body and mind of Sri Chaitanya Mahaprabhu. Yet He blossomed again when He
experienced the emotions of divine love. When the emaciated Sri Chaitanya lay down on a mat of dry banana bark, it
caused Him only pain.” (CC, Antya 13.4–5)

The devotees could not bear to see this (Antya 13.6). Jagadananda obtained fine cloth, dyed it[^13], stuffed it with
the cotton of the shimula tree and made a mattress and pillow; he gave them to Govinda, so that he would ask the Lord to
accept the bed, and asked Svarupa Damodara himself to persuade the Lord to lie on it (Antya 13.7–10)[^14].
<!-- F54 F55 F56 -->

Seeing the bed, the Lord grew angry and asked Govinda who had made it. Hearing Jagadananda's name, He became a little
afraid — one of the biographies explains: because Jagadananda is Satyabhama, inclined to jealous mana — and yet He had
the bed removed and lay down, as before, on the dry banana bark (Antya 13.10–12). Svarupa said that by this He would
greatly grieve Jagadananda, and the Lord answered: “Shall I have a bedstead brought too? Jagadananda wants Me to enjoy
comforts like worldly people” (Antya 13.13–14). And He added: <!-- F57 -->

“Having become a monk, I have renounced the world and now sleep only on the floor. I shall cover Myself with great
disgrace if I again begin to use a bed, a mat or a pillow.” (CC, Antya 13.15)

This anger is feigned: the Lord, as one of the biographies says, displayed it in the mood of a renounced sannyasi. His
body is wasted not by worldly want but by separation from Krishna, in the mood of Radha — and sleeping on bark is also
part of His lila and a lesson for renunciants.

Jagadananda was deeply saddened. Then Svarupa found a way: he brought dry banana leaves, tore them with his nails into
fine fibres and stuffed with them two of the Lord's outer cloths; thus a new mat and pillow were made, and with great
difficulty he persuaded the Lord to accept them (Antya 13.16–19). <!-- F58 -->

“Everyone was happy to learn that the Lord was sleeping on the new bed. Only Jagadananda remained dissatisfied and did
not hide it.” (CC, Antya 13.20) <!-- F59 -->

In the Gambhira — in the house of Kashi Mishra, now the Radha-kanta Math — according to one of the biographies, a
quilted blanket made for the Lord by Svarupa Damodara or by Jagadananda Pandita is worshipped together with the Lord's
wooden sandals. <!-- F60 -->

## In Vraja
Jagadananda had long dreamed of seeing Vrindavana, but the Lord would not let him go (Antya 13.21). Now, not showing
his sorrow, he again asked permission to go to Mathura[^15].

“Sri Chaitanya replied: ‘You are angry with Me. And if you go to Mathura now, you will simply become a beggar and go on
scolding Me.’ But falling at the Lord's lotus feet, Jagadananda pleaded: ‘For a long time I have dreamed of reaching
Vrindavana.’” (CC, Antya 13.23–24) <!-- F61 -->

Out of love for him the Lord would not agree, but Jagadananda asked again and again and at last turned to Svarupa
Damodara. Svarupa said to the Lord:

“Just as You earlier allowed him to go to Bengal and see Shachimata, so now let him see Vrindavana and then come back
here.” (CC, Antya 13.32)

Heeding Svarupa, the Lord gave permission (Antya 13.26–33) and gave Jagadananda instructions for the road: <!-- F62 -->

“‘You may go without fear as far as Varanasi, but after that be extremely careful and continue your journey only in the
company of kshatriyas. When bandits see a lone Bengali traveller on the road, they seize him, rob him and make him work
for them. In Mathura stay constantly in the company of Sanatana Gosvami and offer due respect to the feet of all the
senior Vaishnavas. Honour all these holy devotees of Mathura with love, but do not associate with them closely. Nor
should you try to imitate their conduct and worship. Visit all twelve forests of Vrindavana together with Sanatana
Gosvami, not leaving his company for a moment. Do not stay long in Vrindavana, and return to Jagannatha Puri
immediately. Do not climb the sacred hill of Govardhana to see the Deity Gopala.’” (CC, Antya 13.34–39)[^16]
<!-- F63 Q17 -->

And the Lord charged him to tell Sanatana that He would soon come to Vrindavana again — let him prepare a dwelling for
Him, a place for bhajana (Antya 13.40, 13.65). <!-- F64 -->

Jagadananda went by the forest road to Varanasi, where he met Tapana Mishra and Chandrashekhara and joyfully told them
of the Lord, and from there came to Mathura, to Sanatana (Antya 13.42–44).

“Sanatana Gosvami showed Jagadananda the twelve forests of Vrindavana. Having seen Mahavana last, they then settled in
Gokula.” (CC, Antya 13.45) <!-- F65 F66 -->

Jagadananda lived with Sanatana in his cave, but they ate separately, each according to his custom: Sanatana lived on
alms — madhukari — while Jagadananda went to a nearby temple and cooked for himself (Antya 13.46–47). One of the
biographies explains that he was not used to wheat food, and Srila Bhaktivinoda Thakura (“Amrita-pravaha-bhashya”) adds
that in those days rice and dal were not offered to the Deities in the temples of Vraja. <!-- F67 Q22 -->

Once Jagadananda invited Sanatana to lunch at the temple. A certain sannyasi, Mukunda Sarasvati, had given Sanatana his
upper cloth[^17], and Sanatana, tying it round his head, came and sat at the temple door (Antya 13.49–51).

“Taking this red cloth for a gift from Sri Chaitanya, Jagadananda felt a surge of divine love and asked Sanatana
Gosvami: ‘Please tell me, where did you get this red cloth?’ Sanatana Gosvami answered him: ‘Mukunda Sarasvati gave it
to me.’ Jagadananda became angry on hearing these words. Seizing a cooking pot, he was about to strike Sanatana.” (CC,
Antya 13.52–54) <!-- F68 -->

Sanatana was abashed, and Jagadananda, putting the pot back on the hearth, said:

“‘You are one of the chief associates of Sri Krishna-Chaitanya Mahaprabhu. No one is dearer to Him than you. And in
spite of this you have wrapped your head in cloth given you by another sannyasi! Who could bear such a thing?’” (CC,
Antya 13.56–57)

Sanatana replied: you are a great knower of the scriptures, and no one is dearer to Sri Chaitanya than you; such faith
in Him is worthy of you. I tied this cloth on precisely to see the strength of your faith — and now I have seen how
extraordinarily strong your love for the Lord is. And it does not befit a Vaishnava to wear the saffron cloth of a
monk: I shall certainly give it to someone (Antya 13.58–61). Sanatana's deep devotion pacified Jagadananda.
<!-- F69 Q18 -->

His impulse was no outburst of anger in the worldly sense but exclusive devotion to Gaura, ananya-nishtha, which
could not bear to see on the head of His associate the cloth of a sannyasi of another path. Sanatana had deliberately
provoked this impulse to reveal Jagadananda's love to the world, and, according to one of the biographies, was very
pleased to see it.

“Having offered the cooked food to Sri Chaitanya, Jagadananda then honoured this prasada together with Sanatana.” (CC,
Antya 13.62) <!-- F70 -->

“Thus Jagadananda stayed two months in Vrindavana. But he could no longer bear the pain of separation from Sri
Chaitanya.” (CC, Antya 13.64)

He made ready to return to Puri, and Sanatana sent with him gifts of Vraja for the Lord: sand from the place of the
rasa-lila, a stone from Govardhana, dried ripe pilu fruits and a gunja garland (Antya 13.66–67). <!-- F71 -->

Jagadananda's return delighted the Lord and all the devotees. The Lord embraced him tightly; Jagadananda conveyed to
Him Sanatana's obeisances and all the gifts. The pilu fruits the Lord distributed to the devotees: those who knew these
fruits sucked the stones, while the Bengalis, who did not know them, chewed and swallowed the seeds and burned their
tongues with their pungent taste — and this became yet another of the Lord's sweet pastimes (Antya 13.71–77).
<!-- F72 -->

## The Last Years
Once the Lord took the sand dunes by the sea for Govardhana Hill and rushed towards them with the speed of the wind.
Everyone ran after Him:

“Among them were Svarupa Damodara, Jagadananda Pandita, Gadadhara Pandita, Ramai, Nandai and Shankara Pandita.” (CC,
Antya 14.89) <!-- F76 -->

When Jagadananda visited Navadvipa and Shantipura for the last time, Advaita, according to one of the biographies,
having returned from Puri, met him in Navadvipa and rejoiced in his company. On parting, Advaita sent with him for
Mahaprabhu a riddle — a tarja (taraja-prahelika), understood by the Lord alone:

“‘Convey my countless obeisances to Mahaprabhu and say at His feet from me the following: Tell the madman that all the
people have gone mad. Tell the madman that rice is no longer sold in the market. Tell the madman that the mad one is no
longer busy with worldly affairs. Tell the madman that a madman has said this.’” (CC, Antya 19.19–21) <!-- F73 Q19 -->

Srila Bhaktivinoda Thakura reveals its meaning: the task for which Advaita had called the Lord is accomplished. Having
read the riddle, Mahaprabhu became ever more absorbed in His states, and the devotees began to think that He would
soon withdraw. Svarupa was alarmed, and in reply to his question the Lord revealed that the purpose of His descent had
been fulfilled. <!-- F74 F75 Q22 -->

## Satyabhama
Kavi Karnapura says of him in the “Gaura-ganoddesha-dipika”:

“By a certain secondary distinction, the Satvatas hold that Jagadananda Pandita is a manifestation of Satyabhama.”
(GGD 51)[^18]

The “Charitamrita” says the same: Jagadananda is a manifestation of Queen Satyabhama (Adi 10.21), and his love for the
Lord is like Satyabhama's love for Krishna (Antya 7.138, 12.152). Satyabhama is a queen of Dvaraka, wilful and proud in
love; her mana is not a flaw but a special taste of prema. So too in Gaura's lila Jagadananda's love showed itself in
quarrels, silences and fasts, while the Lord, relishing this prema, both gave in to him and “feared” him, and came
Himself to make peace. <!-- F2 Q1 -->

## Significance
The life of Jagadananda Pandita is a story of love that is always seeking how to serve the Lord's body and cannot
reconcile itself to His renunciation. The oil, the bed, the best dishes on His plate — all these are the cares of
Satyabhama, and the anger, the broken jar and the locked door are her mana. The Lord rejected his cares to teach the
renunciants, but loved him so much that He came Himself to make peace and ate ten times more than usual just so that
he would not fast. His scolding is mercy and a lesson in etiquette; his impulse in Vraja is fidelity to Gaura; his
yearly visits to Shachi were consolation for the Lord's mother, and for the devotees of Navadvipa meeting him was
meeting Chaitanya Himself. <!-- I1 I3 -->

When and where Jagadananda Pandita left this world, the sources do not say: no biography names the day or the place of
his departure, nor is the day of his departure found in our calendar. <!-- F78 -->
