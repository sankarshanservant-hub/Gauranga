# Raghava Pandita
*Рагхава Пандит · @raghava-pandita*

## The Lord's Original Follower
In Panihati, a village on the eastern bank of the Ganga, south of Kumarahatta, lived Raghava Pandita — one of Sri
Chaitanya's very first followers, a devotee in whose house, as Krishnadasa Kaviraja says, the Lord always manifested
Himself. Today the place lies in the North 24 Parganas district of Bengal; one of the biographies gives the way there —
about a mile from Sodpur station on the railway out of Sealdah — and Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati describes the route
to Panihati in the same way in his “Anubhashya.” The “Bhakti-ratnakara” (8.158) names Panihati as the place
where this great devotee was born, and says that the glory of a devotee's birthplace knows no end.
<!-- F6 F7 F8 -->

Krishnadasa Kaviraja calls him Raghava Pandita, Raghava Thakura and simply Raghava; Vrindavana Dasa calls him Raghava
Pandita, Raghava and, affectionately, Raghavananda, and elsewhere “the generous (*udara*) Raghava Pandita”; the
vandanas, too, call him Raghavananda. He was a brahmana: Vrindavana Dasa, describing how Raghava cooked for the Lord,
speaks of him as “this brahmana,” and Kaviraja names him among the brahmanas — Acharyaratna, Acharyanidhi, Nandana and
Srivasa — who, when they invited the Lord, cooked for Him themselves. <!-- F1 F5 -->

With him lived his sister Damayanti, the Lord's dear maidservant. Brother and sister made their home famous throughout
Gauda: the verses on the holy places of Gauda, the “Pata-paryatana” of Abhirama Dasa, say that Raghava and Damayanti live
in Panihati and that their house is renowned for “Raghava's bags” (*raghavera jhali*) — the delicacies that every year
went from here to Nilachala, to the Lord. Of the year and day of his appearance, as of the day of his departure, the
sources are silent; nor are these days found in the Vaishnava calendar of the Sri Chaitanya Saraswat Math. It lists
only the festivals of Sripat Panihati: the festival of flat rice and yogurt in honour of Raghunatha Dasa Gosvami, and
the day of Sri Gauranga's arrival in Panihati. <!-- F9 Q14 F4 -->

## Dhanishtha
Who he is in Krishna's eternal pastimes, Kavi Karnapura tells us:

“Sri Kashishvara Gosvami is the former Shashirekha in Vraja. Dhanishtha, who in Vraja gave Krishna countless delicacies,
is now Raghava Pandita, dear to Gauranga.” (“Gaura-ganoddesha-dipika” 166)

And next, of his sister:

“She who in Vraja was Gunamala is Damayanti, his sister.” (“Gaura-ganoddesha-dipika” 167)

The verse about Dhanishtha is cited both by the biographies and by Srila Bhaktisiddhanta in his “Anubhashya.”[^1] In
Vraja Dhanishtha served Krishna with delicacies, and in Gaura's pastimes Raghava's service is again in delicacies for
the Lord: one and the same service continues in both lilas. One of the biographies recalls here Srila Bhaktivinoda
Thakura's song “Bhoga-arati”: at mother Yashoda's bidding Dhanishtha carries the remnants of Krishna's meal to Srimati
Radhika, and She lovingly partakes of them. In the same way, this biography says, Mahaprabhu — Krishna who has taken on
the mood of Radha — joyfully accepted food from the one who in Vraja was Dhanishtha. <!-- F2 F3 Q1 -->

## A Branch of the Tree
Listing the branches of Sri Chaitanya's tree, Krishnadasa Kaviraja names Raghava, his disciple and his sister:

“Raghava Pandita was one of the Lord's first followers, and his chief branch is Makaradhvaja Kara. His sister Damayanti,
a dear maidservant of the Lord, prepared provisions for Him all year round. Filling bags with all those provisions,
Raghava would carry them secretly to the Lord. All year round the Lord accepted them, and they became famous as
‘Raghava's bags.’” (CC, Adi 10.24–27)

“First follower” is in the original *adya-anuchara*, an original follower, one of the Lord's very first companions;
Srila Bhaktisiddhanta in the “Anubhashya” counts him as the seventh branch of the tree, and the biographies call him
one of its principal branches. <!-- F12 Q2 -->

His own chief branch, Makaradhvaja Kara, also lived in Panihati; according to the “Anubhashya” he was a follower of
Raghava Pandita, and in the eternal pastimes, according to Karnapura, he is the actor Chandramukha
(“Gaura-ganoddesha-dipika” 141).[^2] The Lord Himself, when He came to Panihati, told him to serve Raghava and said that
his love for Raghava would show his love for the Lord; of this, more below. <!-- F13 -->

## The Lord in Raghava's House
Vrindavana Dasa tells how Mahaprabhu, having already accepted sannyasa, travelled from Nilachala to Gauda, wishing to go on
from there to Vrindavana. Leaving Advaita's home in Shantipura, He went to Kumarahatta, to Srivasa, and after staying with
him some days, to Panihati, to the house of Raghava Pandita:

“When Sri Gaurasundara came to Raghava Pandita, Raghava was engaged in worshipping Krishna. Seeing the Lord of his life,
Sri Raghava Pandita fell to the ground in obeisance. Raghavananda wept in ecstasy, clasping the Lord's lotus feet, which
are infinitely dear to Rama, the goddess of fortune. Then the Lord embraced Raghava Pandita and drenched his body with
tears of ecstatic love.” (CB, Antya 5.76–79)
<!-- F16 F17 F18 -->

Raghava did not know what to do for joy, and the Lord of Vaikuntha, seeing his sincere devotion, cast a merciful glance
upon him and said:

“‘Coming to Raghava's house and seeing him, I have forgotten all My troubles. In Raghava's house I have found the same
joy and satisfaction as if I had bathed in the sacred Ganga.’” (CB, Antya 5.82–83)

And, smiling, He added: “Listen, Raghava Pandita! Go quickly and cook for Krishna.” Receiving this order, Raghava went
to the kitchen in joyful agitation and, following his heart, cooked a countless variety of dishes. Mahaprabhu sat down
to eat with Nityananda and His close companions and praised every dish. Raghava knew that the Lord loved *shaka* —
dishes of greens — and had made it in many ways; and the Lord said, “How wonderful is Raghava's cooking! Never before
have I eaten such shaka” (CB, Antya 5.84–90). Afterwards, too, the Lord often asked him to cook: He held dear a cooking
in which there was nothing but devotion. Jayananda in his “Chaitanya-mangala” also recalls how the Lord praised
Raghava's shaka, and however much was eaten, it never grew less; Raghava's shaka and rice, he says, are famous in
Panihati to this day. <!-- F19 F20 F21 F22 Q4 -->

As soon as Gadadhara Dasa, infinitely dear to the Lord, heard that the Lord was in Raghava's house, he hurried there,
and the Lord placed His feet on his head; after him came Purandara Pandita, Parameshvari Dasa and the great
Vaishnava Raghunatha Vaidya, and Vaishnavas gathered from everywhere (CB, Antya 5.92–98). All Panihati, says Vrindavana
Dasa, was immersed in bliss, because Gaurachandra Himself was there. <!-- F23 Q5 -->

Then, in a secluded place, the Lord revealed to Raghava a confidential truth:

“‘O Raghava, I will tell you the greatest secret. Nityananda is not different from Me. More than that, I tell you: I do
whatever Nityananda wishes. I tell you without concealment: all My deeds I perform by Nityananda. There is no
difference between Me and Nityananda. Let everyone in your house know this. From Nityananda you will easily obtain what
is rarely attained even by the greatest of mystic yogis.’” (CB, Antya 5.101–105)

Therefore, the Lord concluded, serve Nityananda with care, knowing Him to be the Supreme Lord Himself. And to
Makaradhvaja Kara He said: “You must serve Sri Raghavananda. Know that your love for Me will be seen in how much love you
show to Raghava Pandita” (CB, Antya 5.107–108). So in a single visit the Lord gave two lessons: service to Nityananda is
the same as service to Him, and service to His Vaishnava is also service to Him. <!-- F24 F14 I4 -->

Thus the Lord glorified Panihati; after staying there some days He went on to Varahanagara (CB, Antya 5.109–110).
<!-- F25 -->

Krishnadasa Kaviraja tells of the Lord's coming to Panihati on His way to Gauda (in 1514) differently. Hearing of the
Lord, people gathered in such crowds that those who found no room on the bank stood in the water:

“Raghava Pandita came and took the Lord with him, but because of the crowds on the way they got there only with great
difficulty. The Lord stayed there one day, and in the morning He came to Kumarahatta, where Srinivasa lived.” (CC,
Madhya 16.204–205)

Karnapura's drama “Chaitanya-chandrodaya” also speaks of the crowd on the bank; in his mahakavya the Lord stays in
Raghava's home five or six days, and in the “Gaura-krishnodaya” a week.[^3] <!-- F64 -->

## Nityananda in Panihati
Later, at Mahaprabhu's command, Nityananda travelled from Nilachala to Gauda together with His associates to give people
love of God. Wandering along the banks of the Ganga, He came to Panihati — drawn, one of the biographies says, by
Raghava's pure devotion — went straight to his house and stayed there; Raghava, Makaradhvaja Kara and their households were overjoyed (CB, Antya 5.251–253). According
to the “Bhakti-ratnakara” (8.156–158), Ramadasa and Gadadhara Dasa came with Nityananda, and the first house He visited
was Raghava's. <!-- F26 -->

As soon as the desire to dance arose in Nityananda's heart, the best singers gathered round Him — the three Ghosha
brothers, Madhava, Vasudeva and Govinda; Madhava, the finest of kirtaniyas, was especially dear to Nityananda. To their
singing Nityananda danced so that the earth trembled, and His roar resounded all around (CB, Antya 5.255–260); one of the biographies, in its account of Govinda
Ghosha too, recalls in particular that he took part in the pastimes in Raghava Pandita's house. Thus,
says Vrindavana Dasa, Nityananda began His all-auspicious mission — the deliverance of the world by the gift of divine
love. <!-- F27 Q15 F29 F70 -->

Then He sat on the Deity's throne and ordered that He be bathed (*abhisheka*). Raghava and his companions bathed Him
according to the rules of scripture: thousands of pots of Ganga water mixed with fragrances were poured over
Nityananda's head to the chanting of mantras and holy names; then He was dressed in new cloth, anointed with sandal
paste and adorned with garlands of forest flowers and tulasi leaves (CB, Antya 5.265–271). Murari Gupta in his “Kadacha”
recalls that Nityananda Himself told Raghava to bathe Him with Ganga water and adorn Him, “so that Gaurachandra's wish
might be fulfilled.” In this there is neither presumption nor self-exaltation: Nityananda is Balarama Himself, and in
accepting worship on Vishnu's throne He was only beginning the pastime for which Mahaprabhu had sent Him to Gauda.
<!-- F28 -->

When Nityananda had taken His seat on the golden throne, “Sri Raghavananda raised an umbrella over His head” (CB,
Antya 5.273).[^4] On every side rang out “Jaya! Jaya!”, and in the midst of this jubilation Nityananda suddenly said:

“He commanded: ‘Listen, Raghava Pandita! Bring Me at once a garland of kadamba flowers. I am very fond of kadamba
flowers; I always dwell in kadamba groves.’ With folded hands Raghavananda replied, ‘But this is not the season for
kadamba flowers.’ The Lord replied, ‘Go and look carefully. Perhaps a few blossoms have opened somewhere.’” (CB, Antya
5.277–280)

Raghava went into the garden — and stood amazed: on the lime tree (*jambira*) by his house beautiful kadamba flowers had
blossomed. Seeing them he forgot himself and laughed for joy; with difficulty regaining his composure, he wove a garland
of them and brought it to Nityananda, who put it on and was greatly pleased (CB, Antya 5.281–286). The vandanas that
glorify the Lord's companions also keep the memory of this wonder.[^5] <!-- F30 F31 F69 -->

At once everything was filled with the wonderful scent of damanaka blossoms. Nityananda, smiling, asked where it came
from, and then revealed the secret: it was Mahaprabhu Himself, who had come from Jagannatha Puri to hear their kirtan;
His body was adorned with a garland of damanaka, and as He listened He was leaning against a tree (CB, Antya 5.288–297).
The “Bhakti-ratnakara” (12.3645–3647) and a pada of Narahari also sing of the dancing and kirtan in Raghava's house when
Nityananda came and Raghava, Makaradhvaja and the others rejoiced; and the “Nityananda-vamsha-vistara” recalls that by
day there was feasting in Raghava's house and by night kirtan. For three months Nityananda lived in Panihati, and, in
Vrindavana Dasa's words, those three months seemed to all like a single moment (CB, Antya 5.319, 5.332).
<!-- F32 F33 Q11 F34 -->

## In Puri, at the Ratha-yatra
Every year, as the Ratha-yatra drew near, the devotees of Gauda gathered and went with Advaita to Nilachala, to
the Lord; and Raghava went with them. Vrindavana Dasa, listing those who went, names him thus:

“There came also the magnanimous Raghava Pandita, in whose house Lord Chaitanya secretly performed many pastimes.” (CB,
Antya 8.32)

Krishnadasa Kaviraja names him among those who rejoiced to hear that the Lord had returned from His journey through the
south (Madhya 10.81–84), and on the first visit of the Gaudiyas Gopinatha Acharya, pointing them out from the roof to
King Prataparudra, says: “This is Raghava Pandita, and this is Nandana Acharya” (Madhya 11.89). After the cleansing of
the Gundicha temple, when Mahaprabhu took His seat among His companions to honour prasada, Raghava was among those seated
(Madhya 12.157). <!-- F35 Q6 -->

On the day of the Ratha-yatra the Lord divided the devotees into four kirtan groups. The first was led by Svarupa
Damodara, and Mahaprabhu gave him five to sing the responses — Damodara, Narayana, Govinda Datta, Raghava Pandita and
Sri Govindananda — and Advaita to dance in that group (Madhya 13.36–38).[^6] And when Jagannatha's chariot reached
Sundarachala and Mahaprabhu, remembering the pastimes of Vrindavana, began the water sports in Lake Indradyumna, the
devotees splashed one another in pairs, and Raghava Pandita fought Vakreshvara (Madhya 14.81).
<!-- F36 F37 F38 Q16 F39 -->

In other years too Raghava was among the closest. When Sanatana Gosvami came to Puri, the Lord introduced him to His
companions, Raghava among them (Antya 4.108–110); when Vallabha Bhatta held a feast, Raghava served out prasada together
with Svarupa, Jagadananda, Kashishvara, Shankara and Damodara, and at the Ratha-yatra of that year, when the Lord, as before,
divided the kirtan into seven groups, Raghava Pandita led one of them (Antya 7.66, 7.70–72). Kavi Karnapura in his mahakavya
mentions how in Puri “Raghava comes,” and in the drama “Chaitanya-chandrodaya” the Lord speaks to him affectionately in
the house of Kashi Mishra; Murari Gupta names “Raghava Pandita and Murari” among the Gaudiyas who danced with Gauranga
and went to Nilachala. <!-- F65 F66 -->

## “Your Love Has Conquered Me”
When the Ratha-yatra was over and the Gaudiyas were preparing to go home, Mahaprabhu glorified each one before all. To
Raghava He said: “Your steadfast love has brought Me under your control” (Madhya 15.68), and, turning to the devotees,
He Himself described how Raghava serves Krishna:

“Hear, everyone, how he serves Krishna. His service is supremely pure, the very best of all. Leaving aside his other
offerings, hear about the coconuts. In those parts a coconut sells for five *gandas*. In his garden hundreds of trees
bear fruit by the hundreds of thousands, yet he still asks where the sweetest coconuts are to be had. He pays four
*panas* for every single fruit and takes pains to have them brought from ten *kroshas* away. Every day he has five or
seven fruits husked and keeps them immersed in water to make them very cool. At the time of the offering he trims and
prepares them again, cuts an opening in the top, and offers them to Krishna. Krishna drinks the coconut water: sometimes
He leaves the fruit empty, sometimes still full of water. When the Pandita sees a fruit emptied of its water, he is
delighted. He breaks it open and fills a plate with the pulp. He offers the pulp and meditates outside, and Krishna eats
it and leaves the plate empty. Sometimes, having eaten it, Krishna fills the plate with pulp again. Then the Pandita's
faith grows, and he floats in an ocean of love.” (CC, Madhya 15.69–78)
<!-- F40 F41 F42 Q10 -->

Krishna truly drank the water and ate the pulp: the Deity whom Raghava served is the Lord Himself, and He accepted his
offering not in the devotee's imagination but in fact, sometimes leaving the plate empty, sometimes filling it again, to
show His servant that his service had been accepted. That is why Raghava's faith grew.

Then the Lord told an incident that the devotees especially remembered:

“One day a servant prepared ten coconuts and brought them to be offered. It was not yet time to offer them; there was a
delay, so the servant waited at the door with the plate of fruits in his hand. He touched the wall above the door with
his hand and then touched the fruits with the same hand. The Pandita saw it. The Pandita said, ‘People are always coming
and going through this door. The dust from their feet flies up and settles on the wall above it. You put your hand on
that wall and then touched the fruits. They are no longer fit for Krishna — they have become impure.’ With these words he
threw the fruits over the boundary wall. Such is his pure, loving service: it conquers the world. Then he had other
coconuts prepared and offered them with the utmost purity.” (CC, Madhya 15.79–85)
<!-- F43 -->

Worldly fastidiousness has nothing to do with it. Raghava did not fear dirt for himself: he could not offer Krishna
anything but the flawless, and a fruit touched by the dust of others' feet was no longer fit for his Lord. Srila
Bhaktisiddhanta explains in the “Anubhashya”: Raghava Pandita was neither a karmi obsessed with the worldly “disease of
cleanliness” nor a beginner in devotion who sees holiness in earth and matter; he was an eternally perfect servant of
Krishna, wholly absorbed in transcendental service, without the slightest tinge of a worldly attitude. And one who,
imitating only his outward rules, reasons about worldly purity and impurity does not thereby prove that he has pure
devotion. <!-- I2 Q9 -->

In the same way, the Lord went on, Raghava seeks out and buys for Krishna the best bananas, mangoes, oranges and
jackfruits, wherever they may be — even in distant villages — paying dearly and bringing them with great effort; he
cooks vegetables, greens, sweets, cakes, sweet rice and pickles such as *kashandi*, and offers the best scents, cloth and
ornaments. “Such is the incomparable service he renders in love; it is a balm to the eyes of all who see it,”
Mahaprabhu concluded, and with these words He embraced Raghava (Madhya 15.86–92).[^7] Nothing impure and nothing but
the best did Raghava offer Krishna; and Mahaprabhu joyfully praised his devotion before His companions, especially his
coconuts, and joyfully accepted his offerings. His service is a model of how the Deity is to be served: the best and the
most precious for Krishna, and not a shadow of carelessness. <!-- F44 F45 F46 I1 -->

## “Raghava's Bags”
Raghava is remembered most of all for his *jhalis* — the bags of delicacies for the Lord. All year his sister Damayanti,
at his word, prepared what Mahaprabhu loved: long-keeping *nishkari* pickles, made without water or boiled rice, sweets,
condiments and many other foods — and together with other provisions she gathered she packed them into hampers and
bags for the Lord's daily meals. Raghava, as Kaviraja says, carried them secretly to the Lord in Nilachala (Adi 10.26),
and the Lord ate these foods all year round. <!-- F47 -->

Kaviraja devoted part of the tenth chapter of the “Antya-lila” to these bags and names “the description of Raghava
Pandita's bags” in particular in the summaries of that chapter and of the whole book (Antya 10.159, 20.117). The account
begins thus:

“Raghava Pandita set out with his *jhalis* (hampers), packed with all the things that Damayanti had prepared and given
him.” (CC, Antya 10.13)

Having listed all that Damayanti prepared, Kaviraja speaks of brother and sister together:

“Raghava gave the orders, and Damayanti carried them out. Both had deep affection and supreme devotion for the Lord.”
(CC, Antya 10.34)
<!-- F50 Q7 F48 F71 -->

Brother and sister knew that Mahaprabhu was Bhagavan Himself, and yet love made them care for Him as one cares for
one's very own. This is not ignorance of His greatness but the sweetness (*madhurya*) of the love of Vraja, before which
reverence gives way: the devotees of Vraja have no awe before the Lord, only care for Him. And Mahaprabhu, who accepts
not the thing but the feeling (*bhava*) with which it is offered, accepted this loving service of Raghava and Damayanti.
<!-- I5 F54 -->

The bags were tied tight and sealed, so that no one would open them on the way, and it took three porters to carry
them. So they became famous as “raghavera jhali,” and their steward (*munsib*) was Makaradhvaja Kara, who guarded them
as if they were his very life (Antya 10.38–40). According to one of the biographies, it was he who carried them to Puri every year. <!-- F49 F15 Q8 -->

The biographies recall that in the chaturmasya when Raghava first carried his celebrated bags to Purushottama, Nandana
Acharya too fed the Lord. In Puri Raghava handed the bags over to Govinda, the Lord's servant; Govinda kept them in a corner of the
room where the Lord ate, and emptied the previous year's bags and took them away to be filled again (Antya 10.55–56).
When all the other Gaudiyas' delicacies had been eaten, Govinda said, “Only Raghava's jhalis are left.” The Lord put
them aside for last, and the next day, eating in seclusion, He opened them, looked at everything, tasted a little of
each and praised every item for its taste and fragrance; and all this prasada Svarupa Damodara served Him a little at a
time at His meals for a whole year (Antya 10.128–131). <!-- F53 F52 -->

Kaviraja does not forget the bags when he tells of the Gaudiyas' journeys. In the third year, when Ramananda Raya and
Sarvabhauma were dissuading the Lord from going to Vrindavana and the Gaudiyas came again to Nilachala, the wives of the
Vaishnavas, wishing to invite the Lord, brought from Bengal many of His favourite foods: “Raghava Pandita packed his
bags (*jhali*) for the journey” (Madhya 16.16–17). And in another year Kaviraja pictures Raghava on the road, going
with his bags packed (Antya 12.12). Every year Raghava brought the Lord countless foods dear to Him, prepared by his
sister. <!-- F51 -->

## The Festival in Panihati
In Panihati, on the bank of the Ganga, Raghunatha Dasa at Nityananda's command held a festival of flat rice and yogurt
(*chida-dadhi*) for Nityananda and His companions. In the midst of the festival Raghava Pandita arrived — saw it all,
was astonished and began to laugh. He brought many kinds of prasada cooked in ghee and untouched by boiled rice, placed
it before Nityananda and distributed it among the devotees. “I offered food for You,” he said to Nityananda, “but You
are holding a festival here, and the prasada remains at home.” Nityananda said, “Let Me eat this food by day; at night I
will eat the prasada in your house” (Antya 6.71–74). Nityananda had Raghava sit down too; he was given two pots, and he
himself soaked two kinds of flat rice in them (Antya 6.76). <!-- F55 F56 F67 -->

When everyone had been served, Nityananda became absorbed in meditation, and Mahaprabhu, drawn by His love, came to the
festival Himself and accepted the food. The Lord comes not because He has been summoned: He comes by His own will, when
a devotee's love calls Him. Govinda-deva's “Gaura-krishnodaya” also recalls how at the chida-dadhi festival Gaura showed
mercy to Raghava Pandita. <!-- F57 -->

In the evening Nityananda came to Raghava's house, and there a beautiful kirtan began. At last Nityananda Himself began
to dance, flooding the world with love, and Mahaprabhu, unseen by others, watched His dance; only Nityananda saw Him.
Then Raghava invited Nityananda to eat:

“The Lord sat down to eat with His associates, having a seat spread for Mahaprabhu at His right. Mahaprabhu came and sat
on that seat; seeing this, Raghava's joy grew.” (CC, Antya 6.107–108)

Raghava served the brothers cakes, sweet rice, excellent fine rice and vegetable dishes that put nectar to shame, and
They ate everything to the last morsel. To this account Kaviraja adds a word about the hidden side of Raghava's service:

“The prasada of Raghava Thakura is the very essence of nectar; Mahaprabhu comes again and again to eat it. When Raghava
has cooked and places the offering before the Deity, he serves out a separate offering for Mahaprabhu. Mahaprabhu eats
there every day, and now and then the Lord lets Raghava see Him. […] I cannot say how many dishes he brought. In
Raghava's house Radha Thakurani Herself cooks. She received a boon from Durvasa: Her cooking is sweeter than nectar.”
(CC, Antya 6.111–113, 6.115–116)

This is not praise of good cooking but Kaviraja's word about the hidden nature of Raghava's service: in his house Srimati
Radhika Herself cooked. After the meal Raghava adorned the brothers with garlands and sandal paste, offered Them betel,
bowed at Their feet, and gave garlands, sandal and betel to the devotees. So the Lord, sitting beside Nityananda in
Raghava's house, revealed to the world His servant's good fortune (Antya 6.101–121). <!-- F58 -->

## Mercy to Raghunatha
At that festival was Raghunatha Dasa, who longed with all his heart for the feet of Mahaprabhu. When at supper the
devotees asked him to sit down with them, Raghava replied that Raghunatha would eat later (Antya 6.118) — and showed
him special mercy:

“Raghava's mercy rested on Raghunatha: he gave him the plates with the remnants of the two brothers. He said, ‘Chaitanya
Gosani has eaten from these. If you receive His remnants, your bondage will be cut.’” (CC, Antya 6.122–123)
<!-- F59 F68 -->

In the morning Raghunatha came to Nityananda and, through Raghava, humbly asked Him to show him the way to Mahaprabhu's
feet (Antya 6.127). Nityananda answered with a blessing:

“‘Gaura came Himself to deliver you. All your bonds — obstacles and the rest — have been loosed. He will entrust you to
Svarupa and keep you at His feet, calling you His intimate servant. Go home free from care. Soon, without obstacles, you
will attain the feet of Chaitanya.’” (CC, Antya 6.141–143) <!-- F60 Q13 -->

Then Raghunatha withdrew with Raghava to take counsel and on his advice secretly gave the treasurer of Nityananda a
hundred coins and seven *tolas* of gold. Raghava took the young man home, showed him his Deity, gave him a garland,
sandal paste and plenty of prasada for the road. Raghunatha asked him to make gifts to Nityananda's companions as well —
five coins to one, ten to another, twenty to another; Raghava had a note written for each with the name and the sum,
and Raghunatha humbly placed a hundred coins and two tolas of gold before him, touched his feet and then went home (Antya 6.145–154).
So Raghava became Raghunatha's intercessor before Nityananda. <!-- F61 -->

## Where the Lord Always Is
Krishnadasa Kaviraja says that there are four places where Mahaprabhu always manifested Himself:

“In Shachi's house, at Nityananda's dancing, at Srivasa's kirtana and in Raghava's home — in these four places the Lord
always appears. He is drawn by prema: that is the Lord's innate nature.” (CC, Antya 2.34–35)

And a little further on: “in Raghava's house He is ever present” (Antya 2.80) — in the original *nirantara avirbhava*,
an unceasing appearance. The “Gaura-krishnodaya” says the same. This is not a vision or an image in the devotee's mind:
the Lord is truly present, by His own will, where He is served with love. As Kaviraja says, the independent Lord always
abides in the heart and in the home of His devotee — sometimes hidden, sometimes manifest (Antya 6.124). The Lord yields
to the love of His devotees, and so He constantly manifested Himself in Raghava's house. <!-- F62 Q3 I3 -->

To this same place belongs the story of Nrisimhananda Brahmachari: after meditating for two days, he told Shivananda
Sena that the Lord had “come to Panihati,” and from there would come to Shivananda's house (Antya 2.54–55). In the drama
“Chaitanya-chandrodaya” Nrisimhananda brings the Lord precisely to Raghava's house. <!-- F63 -->

## Samadhi and Deity in Panihati
When and where Raghava Pandita left this world, the sources do not say; his samadhi is in Panihati. Srila
Bhaktisiddhanta wrote in the “Anubhashya” that over the samadhi a high platform had been built, surrounded by a bower of
creepers, and that to the north of it, in a dilapidated house, resided Raghava's Deity, Sri Madana-mohana; His service was
carried on without due care, under the supervision of the then zamindar of Panihati, Shivachandra Raya Chaudhuri.[^8]
It was this Deity that Raghava showed to Raghunatha Dasa (Antya 6.148), and when Mahaprabhu came to him, Raghava was
engaged in serving Krishna. One of the biographies adds that sixty years after these lines were written the situation
had changed: on the site of Raghava's house a new temple and a residential building have been built. <!-- F10 F11 Q12 -->

## Significance
Raghava Pandita is Dhanishtha, who served Krishna with delicacies in Vraja, and in Gaura's pastimes an original follower
of the Lord, one of the principal branches of His tree. Mahaprabhu Himself said to him: “Your steadfast love has brought
Me under your control.” <!-- F2 F12 F40 -->

His life is a model of service to the Deity. The best and most precious for Krishna, without the least carelessness; and
this scrupulousness is born not of fear of impurity but of a love that cannot offer the Lord anything but the flawless.
An eternally perfect servant of Krishna, he showed what purity of service can be — yet, as Srila Bhaktisiddhanta
teaches, to imitate only his outward rules is not yet to have his devotion. <!-- I1 I2 -->

His house became one of the four places where the Lord always manifested Himself, for the Lord yields to love. There,
too, the Lord revealed to him that Nityananda is not different from Him and that service to a Vaishnava is service to
the Lord Himself. And Raghava's bags, which the Lord ate from all year, remain a testimony that the Lord accepts not the
thing but the love with which it is offered. <!-- F62 I3 I4 I5 -->
