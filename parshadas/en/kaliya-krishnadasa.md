# Kaliya Krishnadasa
*Калия Кришнадас · @kaliya-krishnadasa*

## A Friend from Vraja
Kaliya Krishnadasa is one of the twelve gopalas, Krishna's eternal friends who came into Gaura's pastimes as associates
of Nityananda Prabhu. Vrindavana Dasa in the “Chaitanya-bhagavata” and nearly all the Vaishnava vandanas call him Kaliya
Krishnadasa; Krishnadasa Kaviraja in the “Chaitanya-charitamrita” and Lochana Dasa call him Kala Krishnadasa; Kavi
Karnapura calls him Kala Sri Krishnadasa. In later books he is Kala Krishnadasa Thakura, Sri Kalakrishna Dasa, and, by
his name in Vraja, Lavanga-sakha, “the friend Lavanga.” <!-- F1 -->

Who he is in Krishna's eternal pastimes the “Gaura-ganoddesha-dipika” tells us:

“The former friend named Arjuna is Sri Parameshvara Dasa; Lavanga, a friend in Vraja, is Kala Krishnadasa.”
(“Gaura-ganoddesha-dipika” 132)

So too Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati in his “Anubhashya” calls him an eternally perfect friend of Vraja (*nitya-siddha
vraja-sakha*), for whom Nityananda is his very life. Lavanga is a cowherd boy, a friend of Krishna and Balarama; in the
pastimes of Gaura and Nityananda he was not born anew but appeared together with the Lord, keeping his eternal nature and
his mood of friendship (*sakhya*).[^1] <!-- F2 Q2 -->

Of all Nityananda's associates Krishnadasa Kaviraja says:

“All of Nityananda's associates are cowherd friends from Vraja. Each carries a horn and a staff and wears the dress of a
cowherd, with a peacock feather on his head.” (CC, Adi 11.21)

Among them the twelve gopalas are the chief; in the lists of the twelve Kalakrishna Dasa stands now ninth (so too the
“Anubhashya”: “gopala 9”), now fourth.[^2] All twelve hold the seats of “patra-raja” in the Universal Assembly of
Vaishnava Kings (Vishva-vaishnava-raja-sabha). Lochana Dasa in the “Chaitanya-mangala,” listing those who came to earth
with the Lord, closes his list of the gopalas thus:

“Kala Krishnadasa and Uddharana Datta — the twelve cowherd boys (*gopalas*) of Vraja: such is their greatness.”
(“Chaitanya-mangala,” Sutra-khanda 8.627) <!-- F3 -->

## “No One but Nityananda”
In his list of the branches of Nityananda's tree Krishnadasa Kaviraja gives him a single line:

“Kala Krishnadasa was a great Vaishnava, among the foremost; he knew no one but the moonlike Nityananda.”
(CC, Adi 11.37)

The biographies call him the best of Vaishnavas and one of Nityananda's chief devotees. <!-- F5 Q3 -->

And Vrindavana Dasa, listing Nityananda's associates, says of him:

“Kaliya Krishnadasa is famed throughout the three worlds. Remembering him, one attains Gaurachandra.” (CB, Antya 5.740)
<!-- F4 Q1 -->

The vandanas praise him again and again: “I praise Kaliya Krishnadasa, overwhelmed by prema alone”; “with great devotion
I praise Kaliya Krishnadasa, in divine sacred thread and garments, bearing the splendour of Krishna”; “I praise the mad,
playful Kala Krishnadasa: always beside himself with prema, he cannot keep hold of his cloth.” This “madness” is not
worldly lack of restraint but intoxication with divine love and the bhava of a cowherd boy, a friend of Krishna: so are
all Nityananda's associates described. In the list of one more vandana there stands a “Sri Krishnadasa,” but whether
this is he is unclear.[^3] <!-- F33 -->

Jayananda in his “Chaitanya-mangala,” listing the wonderful deeds of Nityananda's associates, writes:

“Kaliya Krishnadasa binds and brings a crocodile; seeing this, Sri Nityananda was filled with delight.”

This is neither sorcery nor bravado: it is the power of prema in an associate of Nityananda serving the joy of
Nityananda Himself — as with Sundarananda, who, by the same Jayananda, catches a crocodile in the water and brings it
before everyone.[^4] <!-- F34 -->

## Not the Other Krishnadasa
He is often confused with another Krishnadasa — the simple brahmana whom Nityananda gave to Mahaprabhu as a companion
when the Lord set out to wander through South India. Nityananda said then:

“Here is a simple brahmana named Krishnadasa. Please take him with You — grant My request. He will go with You and carry
the water pot and the cloth.” (CC, Madhya 7.39–40)

The “Charitamrita” also calls this brahmana Kala Krishnadasa; later the Lord sent him with news to Gauda (Madhya
10.62–74). But Srila Bhaktisiddhanta in the “Anubhashya” to Madhya 7.39 states plainly that these are two different
persons. The brahmana who carried the Lord's water pot and kaupina is one man; Kaliya Krishnadasa, the eternal friend
from Vraja among the twelve gopalas, is another. <!-- F6 Q4 -->

## The Sripat at Akai-hat
His sripat is the village of Akai-hat in Bardhaman district, under the police station and post office of Katwa, on the
main road from Navadvipa to Katwa. One goes there by train from Bandel junction to Katwa and then walks two miles; or one
may get off a station earlier, at Daihat, and then the way is about a mile.[^5] The village is very small, and few
people live there.[^6] <!-- F8 F9 F10 -->

What the sripat was like at the beginning of the twentieth century Srila Bhaktisiddhanta recorded in his “Anubhashya”
to the “Charitamrita” (Adi 11.37). The place lay desolate. The temple over Kala Krishnadasa Thakura's samadhi had
collapsed: the beams had broken and everything lay buried. Approaching from the road by way of a mango grove, one came upon a
ruined little chamber; in it stood an altar from which the Deity had already been taken elsewhere, and in its
south-eastern corner a thatched shelter, under which lay the *samaj*, the burial places of the servitors. The servitor
of the sripat at that time was Harerama Dasa Babaji; a later biography, repeating this account, names his successor as
the servitor. <!-- F11 Q5 -->

South of the sripat lies the pond Nupura-kunda. By tradition, the nupura — the anklet — of Raghunandana Thakura, son
of Mukunda of Shrikhanda, fell into it; some say it was the nupura of Nityananda Prabhu Himself. Tradition holds that this
nupura and the Deities of the Akai-hat sripat — Sri Radhavallabha and Sri Gopala — reside to this day in the house of the
mahantas (*mahanta-bati*) in the village of Kadui (Karui), three kroshas from Akai-hat.[^7] Old manuscripts survive as
well: a “Sri Chaitanya-bhagavata” of the year 1200 of the Bengali era (about 1793) and a “Sri Charitamrita” copied in the
year 1171 of the Bengali era (about 1764); where exactly they are kept — in Kadui or in Akai-hat itself — the account
does not make clear. <!-- F12 F13 F14 -->

On 14 November 1912 Srila Bhaktisiddhanta with a small party of devotees went round the places where the Lord's
associates had lived — Shrikhanda, Jajigram, Katwa, Jhamatpur, Akai-hat, Chakhandi and Daihat — and everywhere
preached pure devotion. <!-- F16 -->

## Pabna and Sonatala: The Family Tradition
In the same “Anubhashya” Srila Bhaktisiddhanta gives the tradition of the gosvamis of the village of Sonatala, Kala
Krishnadasa's descendants. Sonatala lies on the northern bank of the river Ichhamati, some three miles west of the
famous river port of Bera-bandar, in Pabna district. According to these gosvamis, Kala Krishnadasa Thakura belonged to a brahmana
family of the Varendra class, of the Bharadvaja gotra, and the ancestral “village” (*grami*) of his clan was Bhadar;
where he was born, the tradition does not say. <!-- F17 -->

From Akai-hat he came to Pabna, preaching the holy name of Hari, and set up an ashram there; the ruins of its buildings
remain visible in that field. Later his kinsmen moved there too. Akai-hat had no Varendra brahmanas, and so
he took a wife in that region.[^8] While he lived in Sonatala, a son was born to him, Sri Mohana Dasa.
<!-- F18 F19 F20 F21 F23 -->

After some time Kala Krishnadasa set out again for Akai-hat and Vrindavana. He left his son with his mother's brother
— in Sonatala or in the village of Bhaduti-Mathurapur — handed over to him everything he owned, and left with his wife
for Vrindavana. There a second son was born to him, Gauranga Dasa; because he was born in Vrindavana, he bore the second
name Vrindavana Dasa. Later the father sent him too to his elder brother, Mohana Dasa, telling him to take six annas — six
sixteenths — of the property.[^9] <!-- F22 F24 F25 F26 -->

Gauranga Dasa revealed the Deity Sri Kalachand, like Sri Govinda Jiu of Vrindavana, and the brothers served Him
together.[^10] In Sonatala one can still see where the ashram house stood, the bricks of the temple and the ghat of the
pond, and Kala Krishnadasa's descendants serve Sri Kalachand Jiu in turn. Headed by Sri Vijayagovinda Gosvami, they lived
in Srila Bhaktisiddhanta's time in Sonatala and elsewhere in Pabna district; according to one biography, his
descendants now live in various holy places of Bengal. <!-- F27 F28 F29 -->

All this is the tradition of his family, preserved by his descendants. Marriage and sons do not diminish the eternal
position of Nityananda's associate: in Pabna and in Vrindavana alike he remained what the “Gaura-ganoddesha” calls him,
Lavanga, Krishna's friend. And his handing everything he owned to his son and leaving for Vrindavana speaks of his
detachment. <!-- F24 -->

## Departure
When and where Kaliya Krishnadasa left this world the sources do not say, but two traditions about the day of his
departure have come down. In Akai-hat the day is observed in the month of Chaitra, on the krishna-dvadashi — the twelfth
lunar day of the waning fortnight — on the day of Varuni. In Sonatala it is observed in the month of Agrahayana, also on the
krishna-dvadashi.[^11] In our Vaishnava calendar (Sri Chaitanya Saraswat Math) the “Disappearance Day of Srila Kaliya
Krishnadasa” is the krishna-dvadashi of the month of Keshava (≈ Agrahayana), that is, following the Sonatala tradition;
in 2026 it falls on 5 December. The day of his appearance is found neither in the sources nor in the calendar.
<!-- F15 F30 F31 -->

## Krishnadasa of Akai-hat on the Way to Kheturi
After the Lord's disappearance, when Jahnava Mata was going to the great festival at Kheturi, her companions, according
to the “Narottama-vilasa,” stopped at Akai-hat with Krishnadasa:

“Rising at dawn, all set out quickly and came to Akaihat, to Krishnadasa's house. The excellent singer Krishnadasa, in
prema, bringing them to his home, deemed himself blessed. Preparing food very quickly, he made ready to go to Kheturi.”
(“Narottama-vilasa” 6.264–266)

He reached Kantaka-nagara (Katwa) first and told Yadunandana everything; at Kheturi the lodging of “Krishnadasa of
Akaihat” is mentioned too (6.319; 8.66). The “Bhakti-ratnakara” says that “with Krishnadasa of Akaihat and others all
came to Kantaka-nagara” (10.409), and the “Prema-vilasa” that Yadunandana of Katwa came to Kheturi “together with
Krishnadasa of Akaihat” (19.321). One of the biographies sees in him Kala Krishnadasa.[^12] <!-- F32 Q6 -->

## Significance
The sources say little of Kaliya Krishnadasa, but they say what matters most: he is Lavanga, Krishna's eternal friend
in Vraja, one of the twelve gopalas; he knew no one but Nityananda; and remembering him, in Vrindavana
Dasa's words, leads to Gaurachandra Himself. <!-- F2 F3 F4 F5 -->
