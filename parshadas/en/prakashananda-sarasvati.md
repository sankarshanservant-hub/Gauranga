# Prakashananda Sarasvati
*Пракашананда Сарасвати · @prakashananda-sarasvati*

## The Head of the Sannyasis of Kashi
Prakashananda Sarasvati lived in Kashi — Varanasi — at the same time as Sri Chaitanya and stood at the head of the
Mayavadi sannyasis there. The “Chaitanya-charitamrita” calls him “the chief among the sannyasis, Prakashananda” (Adi
7.62), and once, in the Bengali text, Prakashananda Shripada (Madhya 17.104); in the “Chaitanya-bhagavata” his name is
heard only in the wrathful words of the Lord Himself; Govinda-deva in the “Gaura-krishnodaya” honours him as “the best
of the sannyasis of the Sarasvati order” (16.38). The biographies call him Sri Prakashananda Sarasvati,
“sannyasi-pradhana” — the chief of the sannyasis — and “the guru of the sannyasis.” <!-- F1 -->

He was a danda-bearing sannyasi of Shankaracharya's school — an *ekadandi*, one who carries a single staff. Srila
Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati in his “Anubhashya” calls him “an ekadandi sannyasi of Shankara's sampradaya who lived in
Kashi in Mahaprabhu's time,” the then head of the ekadandis of Kashi. Shankaracharya instituted ten names for his
sannyasis (*dashanami*); three of them — Tirtha, Ashrama and Sarasvati — are ranked above the rest for purity of
conduct and honour, and Prakashananda bore the name Sarasvati. By Shankaracharya's rule, only brahmanas who have taken
sannyasa in one of these ten orders count as the “Vedic sannyasis” whom the world reveres. <!-- F2 F3 -->

Kashi was then the chief stronghold of Mayavada, the doctrine of the impersonal Brahman, and Prakashananda led a great
number of sannyasis of Shankara's school — nearly a thousand, says one of the biographies. The “Charitamrita” testifies:

“Prakashananda Shripada sat in his assembly and taught Vedanta to his many disciples.” (CC, Madhya 17.104)

In expounding the Vedas he denied the Lord's eternal form and called His body false, woven of maya — so the Lord Himself
says of him in the “Chaitanya-bhagavata.” Srila Bhaktisiddhanta in his “Gaudiya-bhashya” calls him a teacher of
Mayavada who in his commentaries on the Vedas “dissected” the transcendental body of the Lord. Not without reason does
Krishnadasa Kaviraja, speaking of the mercy that flooded the whole world, make one exception:

“He made His devotees of all — even the mlecchas and others; only the Mayavadis of Kashi escaped.” (CC, Adi 7.39)

Who these Mayavadis were, Srila Bhaktivinoda Thakura explains in his “Amrita-pravaha-bhashya”: the sannyasi
Prakashananda and others. <!-- F4 F5 -->

Of his worldly life the sources say nothing. Neither his birthplace and family nor his sannyasa-guru nor the years and
days of his appearance and departure are known; nor are his days found in the Vaishnava calendar of the Sri Chaitanya
Saraswat Math. Srila Bhaktisiddhanta in his preface to the “Chaitanya-chandramrita” notes only that he was a resident of
Aryavarta, that is, a northerner. Nor has the place where he lived survived: in the “Anubhashya” (on Madhya 17.87)
Srila Bhaktisiddhanta writes that in Kashi today there is no trace left of Chandrashekhara's house, of Tapana Mishra's
house, or of “the place of Prakashananda Sarasvati, the leader of the Mayavadis.” <!-- F6 F7 -->

## The Lord's Wrath in Navadvipa
Prakashananda's name was spoken by the Lord Himself long before He came to Kashi — while still in Navadvipa, before
His sannyasa. Once in the house of Murari Gupta the Lord manifested the form of the Boar, Varaha. Murari offered Him
prayers, and the Lord, pleased with them but angered at the Vedas, said:

“‘The Vedas slander Me, claiming that I have no hands, feet, mouth or eyes. In Kashi lives a rogue named Prakashananda
who hacks My body to pieces. He explains the Vedas, yet rejects My eternal form. Though leprosy has already covered his
whole body, he still cannot understand his mistake. My body is perfectly pure and is the embodiment of all sacrifices.
Therefore even such great personalities as Brahma and Shiva glorify it. By touching My body even the purest thing in
this world is purified. How then does this scoundrel dare to say that My body is made of illusion?’” (CB, Madhya
3.36–40)

One of the biographies explains: there are many who recite the Vedas without grasping such mantras as “Without hands
and feet, He is swift and grasps…” (“Shvetashvatara Upanishad” 3.19), and so picture the Lord as having neither hands nor
feet, whereas these words say only that His body is not made of matter. <!-- F8 Q9 -->

Another time, again in Murari's house, the Lord gave him His betel and joked that by touching His remnants Murari had
lost his caste. And at once He entered the mood of the Supreme Lord:

“Having spoken thus, the Lord took on the mood of the Supreme Lord. Clenching His teeth in anger, He went on with great
resolve: ‘In Kashi lives a sannyasi named Prakashananda. He takes pleasure in cutting Me to pieces. He teaches the
“Vedanta,” but denies My divine form. I have struck him with leprosy, yet he still has understood nothing. Countless
universes are manifest in My body. How then does this fool dare to claim that My body is illusory?’” (CB, Madhya
20.32–35)

“I am eternal, My servants are eternal, and the servants of My servants are eternal too,” the Lord went on. “My
pastimes and deeds are eternal. My abode is eternal. One who says they are illusory cuts Me to pieces” (20.39–40).[^1]
<!-- F9 Q10 -->

The harsh words here are not personal enmity but the wrath of the Lord for the sake of His eternal form. The leprosy He
speaks of twice is no worldly punishment: Srila Bhaktisiddhanta explains in the “Gaudiya-bhashya” that Prakashananda
had become an offender against the Lord, and leprosy struck his body, yet understanding never dawned; whoever tries to
“dissect” the spiritual body of the Lord is struck with leprosy in body and in mind alike. The “Charitamrita” says
nothing of leprosy, and whether he was healed the sources do not say. <!-- F10 -->

According to Srila Bhaktisiddhanta, these words of the Lord belong to the years 1425–1430 of the Shaka era (about
1504–1509); according to one of the biographies, to a time “long before 1431 Shaka.” Already then, therefore,
Prakashananda was a Mayavadi sannyasi in Kashi. <!-- F11 -->

Narahari Chakravarti, too, remembers this story. In the “Bhakti-ratnakara,” taking the reader around Navadvipa, he
brings him to Murari's house and recalls how the Lord, chewing betel, entered ever more deeply into the mood of the
Lord of all and “railed at Prakashananda, who lived in Kashi” (BR 12). And in the “Gaudiya-bhashya,” commenting on the
sannyasa of Vishvarupa, Srila Bhaktisiddhanta contrasts two sannyasis named Sarasvati: Prakashananda spent his time in
arguments built on speculation, while Prabodhananda Sarasvati, Madhvacharya and other tridandipadas served Hari.
<!-- F12 F13 -->

## “The Sentimental Sannyasi from Gauda”
After His sannyasa, on His way to Vrindavana, the Lord stopped briefly in Varanasi. Krishnadasa Kaviraja relates:

“On His way to Vrindavana the Lord stayed in Kashi, and the Mayavadis began to blaspheme Him: ‘Though a sannyasi, He
sings and dances. He does not hear Vedanta, but performs sankirtana. This foolish sannyasi does not know His own
dharma; a sentimentalist Himself, He wanders about with sentimentalists.’” (CC, Adi 7.40–42)

Their reproach rested on a rule of smriti: one who has renounced the world is to abandon *tauryatrika* — song, dance
and instrumental music — and devote himself always to Vedanta. Because the new sannyasi paid no heed to Shankara's
teaching, but sang of Krishna and danced in the rapture of love, the Kashi sannyasis judged Him ignorant of the dharma of
renunciation. But
the Lord's songs, dancing and tears are prema, not worldly *tauryatrika*; the Mayavadi, Srila Bhaktisiddhanta explains
in the “Anubhashya,” confuses the Lord's transcendental emotions with mundane singing and dancing.

“Hearing all this, the Lord smiled within, and, paying no heed, He spoke with none of them. Disregarding them, He went
on to Mathura.” (CC, Adi 7.43–44) <!-- F14 F15 -->

The “Charitamrita” tells of those days in more detail in the Madhya-lila. The Lord was then staying with Chandrashekhara
and Tapana Mishra, declining every invitation so as not to meet the Mayavadi sannyasis. Every day a respected brahmana
from Maharashtra came to Him, charmed by His beauty and His love of God. One day this brahmana went to Prakashananda and
began to tell him of the Lord's greatness: of His golden form and arms reaching to His knees, of His lotus eyes, of how
all the signs of a maha-bhagavata described in the “Bhagavatam” were manifest in Him, how Krishna's name never left His
tongue and tears of love flowed from His eyes. Kaviraja gives Prakashananda's reply thus:

“Hearing this, Prakashananda laughed heartily, and mocking the brahmana, he said: ‘I have heard of Him — the
sentimental sannyasi from Gauda, a disciple of Keshava Bharati and a deceiver of people. His name is Chaitanya. He goes
about with His sentimentalists from land to land and village to village, making them dance. Whoever sees Him declares
Him to be God. Such is His art of enchantment: whoever looks at Him falls under its spell. Sarvabhauma Bhattacharya is a
formidable scholar, yet I hear that in Chaitanya's company he has gone mad. He is a sannyasi in name only; in fact He is
a great conjurer. But here in Kashi His wares of emotion will not sell. Keep hearing Vedanta and do not go near Him. The
company of unruly people ruins both this world and the next.’” (CC, Madhya 17.115–121)

Such were Prakashananda's words while the Lord's mercy had not yet touched him. <!-- F16 -->

Pained at heart, the brahmana left, repeating “Krishna! Krishna!”, and told the Lord everything:

“‘While speaking of Your faults he uttered Your name, saying “Chaitanya, Chaitanya” three times. But not once in those
three times did the name of Krishna come to his lips. He takes Your name with contempt, and hearing it pains me.’” (CC,
Madhya 17.126–127) <!-- F17 -->

The Lord explained to him why this was so:

“The Lord said, ‘The Mayavadis are offenders against Krishna. They incessantly say “Brahman,” “atma,” “chaitanya”
(consciousness). Therefore the name of Krishna does not come to their lips. The name of Krishna and Krishna's own form
are one and the same.’” (CC, Madhya 17.129–130)

And with a smile He added:

“‘I came to Kashi to sell My wares of emotion, but there is no buyer; they do not sell, so I shall have to take them
back home.’” (CC, Madhya 17.144)

The brahmana himself the Lord accepted as His own devotee (17.146), and next morning He left for Mathura. In Varanasi,
says one of the biographies, the sayings of the Mayavadis were heard everywhere — “tat tvam asi,” “aham brahmasmi,” the
words “Brahman,” “atma,” “chaitanya”; and Chandrashekhara complained to the Lord that here one hears nothing but
“maya” and “Brahman” (17.95).[^2] <!-- F18 F19 -->

## The Assembly in the Brahmana's House
After visiting Vrindavana, the Lord came to Varanasi again. This time He stayed in the house of Chandrashekhara — “the
scribe, a shudra,” as the “Charitamrita” says[^3] — and took His meals with Tapana Mishra. Sanatana Gosvami came there
from Bengal, and for two months the Lord instructed him. But the Mayavadi sannyasis went on reviling the Lord, and
Chandrashekhara and Tapana Mishra could not bear it:

“Meanwhile Chandrashekhara and Tapana Mishra, deeply grieved, came to the Lord's feet with a petition: ‘How much longer
must we hear You blasphemed, O Lord? We cannot bear it; we would rather give up our lives. All the sannyasis revile You,
and we cannot listen to it: our hearts and ears are bursting.’” (CC, Adi 7.49–51) <!-- F20 Q1 -->

The Lord heard them with a slight smile, and just then a brahmana came to Him. Touching His feet, he said: “I have
invited all the sannyasis of Varanasi to my house. If You too accept the invitation, all my desires will be fulfilled.
I know You do not attend gatherings of the Mayavadi sannyasis, but be merciful to me — come.” Kaviraja continues:

“Smiling, the Lord accepted the invitation; He made this gesture to show mercy to the sannyasis. The brahmana knew that
the Lord does not go to anyone's house, but, inspired by the Lord Himself, he pressed Him with great insistence.” (CC,
Adi 7.56–57)

The Lord, who until then had answered His revilers with nothing but a smile, accepted the invitation not for the sake of
honour: it was a means to save the sannyasis, and the request itself He had inspired in the brahmana. In the
Adi-lila the brahmana is not named; in the second account (Madhya 25.7–15) he is the same brahmana from Maharashtra who
had earlier heard Prakashananda's mockery.[^4] <!-- F21 I1 -->

Next day the Lord arrived at the brahmana's home and found the sannyasis of Varanasi already seated. He Himself had
taken sannyasa in the Bharati branch, and so He acknowledged the Sarasvati branch, to which Prakashananda belonged, as
the higher, and called Himself a sannyasi of the lower. Thus, as a Vaishnava sannyasi, He showed the dharma of *amani-manada*: to seek no honour
for oneself and to give honour to others.

“Bowing to them all, He went to wash His feet, and when He had washed them He sat down in that very place. As He sat
there, He revealed a little of His majesty: His body shone with great effulgence, with the splendour of millions of
suns. That splendour drew the minds of all the sannyasis, and they all rose from their seats.” (CC, Adi 7.59–61)
<!-- F22 F23 Q2 -->

Prakashananda was the first to speak:

“The chief among the sannyasis, Prakashananda by name, addressed the Lord with respect: ‘Come here, Gosani; listen,
Shripada. You are sitting in an impure place — why are You so downcast?’ The Lord replied, ‘I belong to a lower
sampradaya; it is not fitting for Me to sit in your assembly.’ Then Prakashananda himself took Him by the hand and,
with respect, seated Him in the midst of the assembly.” (CC, Adi 7.62–65) <!-- F24 F25 Q3 -->

And he asked Him directly:

“He asked, ‘Your name is Sri Krishna Chaitanya, is it not? You are a disciple of Keshava Bharati, and so You are worthy
of glory. You are a sannyasi of our own sampradaya and You live in this town; why then do You not come to see us? Though
You are a sannyasi, You dance and sing, and You take all these sentimentalists with You and perform kirtana. The study of
Vedanta and meditation are the dharma of a sannyasi; why do You abandon them and do the work of sentimentalists? From
Your splendour we see that You are Narayana Himself; why then do You behave so lowly? What is the reason?’” (CC, Adi
7.66–70) <!-- F26 -->

The Lord's answer is full of humility. My guru, He said, considered Me a fool and instructed Me:

“‘You are a fool; You are not qualified for Vedanta. Always chant the Krishna-mantra — it is the essence of all mantras.
From the Krishna-mantra will come release from samsara, and by Krishna's name You will attain Krishna's feet. In the age
of Kali there is no dharma other than the name; the name is the essence of all mantras — this is the heart of the
scriptures.’” (CC, Adi 7.72–74)

Chanting the name, the Lord went on, I lost My composure: like a madman I laugh and weep, I dance, I sing. When I
spoke of it to My guru, he replied: this is simply what the maha-mantra does; it is well that You have attained love for Krishna, the
highest goal of life, and by Your love my own life has been crowned with success.

“‘Dance, sing and perform sankirtana with the devotees; teach Krishna's name and deliver all people.’” (CC, Adi 7.92)

The words “I am a fool” and “I am lower” are not an admission of ignorance by the Lord but His play. The very Object of
Vedanta speaks here as a student, to show who is fit for Vedanta: the humble one, who seeks no honour — so Srila
Bhaktisiddhanta explains in the “Anubhashya.” <!-- F27 Q4 I2 -->

His sweet words turned the hearts of the whole assembly:

“‘All that You have said is true: love for Krishna is attained only by one whose good fortune has dawned. You have
devotion for Krishna, and we are all pleased with that. But why do You not hear Vedanta? What fault is there in it?’”
(CC, Adi 7.100–101) <!-- F28 -->

Then the Lord spoke of Vedanta:

“The Lord said, ‘The *Vedanta-sutra* is the word of the Lord: Sri Narayana Himself composed it in the form of Vyasa.
<…> But to hear the commentary that the Acharya wrote in the secondary sense is to lose every spiritual gain. The fault
is not his: he received the Lord's order and so gave the secondary meaning, covering the primary. <…> One who regards
the body of Vishnu as material commits the gravest blasphemy against Vishnu; there is none greater than this.’” (CC, Adi
7.106, 109–110, 115)

Shankara is not to blame — he is a servant who carried out the Lord's will; but whoever listens with faith to his
secondary interpretation meets with ruin (7.114).[^5] The pranava, Om, is the seed of the tree of the Vedas and the true
great saying, the sound-form of God Himself; Shankara covered it and raised “tat tvam asi” to the rank of the great
sayings. The Vedas are self-evident, and by interpreting them indirectly one robs them of their authority; so in every
sutra Shankara cast aside the direct sense and put his own in its place (7.128–133).[^6] <!-- F29 Q5 I3 -->

The sannyasis were astonished. The “Charitamrita” gives their answer with striking frankness:

“All the sannyasis said, ‘Listen, Shripada: the meaning You have refuted is beyond dispute. We all know that the meaning
is the Acharya's own invention; yet out of deference to our sampradaya we accept it as the truth.’” (CC, Adi
7.135–136) <!-- F30 -->

At their request the Lord explained the direct meaning of the sutras. Brahman is Bhagavan Himself, full of spiritual
perfections and untouched by maya; He is *sambandha*, the One with whom everyone is eternally related. The way to Him is
bhakti, beginning with hearing and glorifying; this is *abhidheya*. The goal is love for Krishna, the fifth goal of life,
*prayojana*. “In these three meanings all the sutras find their conclusion” (7.146). <!-- F31 -->

Then a change came over the assembly:

“Hearing all the sutras explained in this way, all the sannyasis humbly said: ‘You are the Vedas embodied — Narayana
Himself. Forgive us for having blasphemed You before.’ From that time the sannyasis' hearts were changed, and they
always took the name ‘Krishna, Krishna’. So He forgave their offences and graced them all with Krishna's name.” (CC, Adi
7.147–150) <!-- F32 F33 Q6 Q7 -->

Then the sannyasis seated the Lord in their midst and took their meal together with Him, and afterwards He returned
home. Chandrashekhara, Tapana Mishra and Sanatana, hearing His arguments and seeing His mercy, rejoiced (7.153). Now all
the Mayavadi sannyasis came of themselves to see the Lord, and all Varanasi glorified Him. Crowds thronged His door,
thousands gathered at the temple of Vishveshvara and at the Ganga when He went there, and the Lord, raising His arms,
called out: “Sing ‘Hari! Hari!’” (7.154–159). Kaviraja promises to tell of this in more detail later (7.162) — and does
so in the Madhya-lila. <!-- F34 F35 -->

## A Speech in Defence of Bhakti
From the time the Lord showed mercy to the sannyasis of Varanasi, the whole town spoke of nothing else. Panditas and
disputants, learned in many shastras, began to come to Him; He shattered their false interpretations and with
irrefutable arguments established bhakti as the highest truth, and they, accepting His teaching, began to sing and
dance in Krishna-sankirtana (CC, Madhya 25.18–21). The Mayavadi sannyasis, giving up their study of Vedanta, discussed
among themselves only the path of bhakti revealed by the Lord (25.22). <!-- F36 -->

At one such gathering a disciple of Prakashananda spoke up:

“One of Prakashananda's disciples, his equal, spoke in the assembly in praise of the Lord: ‘Sri Krishna Chaitanya is
Narayana Himself. He explains the meaning of the *Vyasa-sutra* most charmingly.’” (CC, Madhya 25.23–24)

He praised the Lord's interpretation as revealing the *chich-chhakti-vilasa* — the play of Krishna's spiritual energy —
and censured Shankara's teaching:

“‘I accept the words of Sri Krishna Chaitanya as firmly true: in the age of Kali one does not conquer *samsara* by
sannyasa. <…> The *Bhagavata* says that without bhakti there is no liberation, and in the age of Kali liberation comes
easily by *namabhasa*, the mere semblance of the holy name.’” (CC, Madhya 25.28, 30)

One of the biographies says of this disciple simply that he was devoted to Mahaprabhu. <!-- F37 -->

And then Prakashananda himself took the word. The head of the sannyasis of Kashi, who not long before had laughed at
“the sentimental sannyasi from Gauda,” now supported his disciple with many arguments:

“Hearing him, Prakashananda spoke: ‘The Acharya was determined to establish *advaita-vada*, and so he explained the
sutras in another way. If one accepts that He is Bhagavan (*bhagavatta*), *advaita* cannot be established. Therefore he
refutes all the scriptures. An author who wants to establish his own doctrine cannot give the natural meaning of the
scriptures.’” (CC, Madhya 25.45–48)

He went through the doctrines of the Mimamsakas, of Sankhya, Nyaya, the Mayavadis and Patanjali's yoga — each puts
forward its own and refutes the others — and concluded that Vyasa refuted all six and set forth Vedanta in his sutras.

“‘In the view of Vedanta, Brahman is defined as having form (*sakara*), and the word *nirguna*, a negation, shows that
He is in truth *saguna* — possessed of qualities. <…> So we do not come to know the truth from the six philosophies; we
accept as truth what the *mahajanas* say. <…> The words of Sri Krishna Chaitanya are a stream of nectar. Whatever He
declares is the truth, the very essence.’” (CC, Madhya 25.53, 55, 57)

The last verse one of the biographies cites as the conclusion of the whole speech: the word of Chaitanya is what one
must follow. This is no longer an opponent speaking, but a devotee. Srila Bhaktisiddhanta in the “Anubhashya” so heads
this passage: “the words of Prakashananda, who had now become a devotee of Gaura.” The change in him was genuine: he
himself, before his own disciples, refuted the interpretations of Shankara that he had once taught. <!-- F38 Q8 F39 -->

## At Bindu-madhava
The brahmana from Maharashtra, hearing these words, hurried to the Lord with the good news. Mahaprabhu, as was His
custom in Kashi, had bathed at Panchanada and was on His way to the temple of Bindu-madhava, and on the way the brahmana
told Him all that he had heard from the lips of Prakashananda, now a devotee. The Lord listened with a gentle smile (CC,
Madhya 25.58–60). <!-- F40 -->

“Seeing the beauty of Madhava, He was overwhelmed with feeling, and coming into the courtyard, He began to dance in
love. Chandrashekhara, Paramananda, Tapana and Sanatana — these four together joined in the sankirtana of the holy
name: ‘*Hari haraye namah, krishna yadavaya namah, gopala govinda rama shri-madhusudana*.’ On all sides hundreds of
thousands of people called out, ‘Hari! Hari!’ An auspicious sound arose, filling heaven and earth.” (CC, Madhya
25.61–64) <!-- F41 -->

“Hearing the sound of ‘Hari’ nearby, Prakashananda came out of curiosity to see, bringing his disciples with him.
Seeing the Lord's dance, His love and the sweetness of His form, he too, with his disciples, called out, ‘Hari!
Hari!’” (CC, Madhya 25.65–66)

He was struck, says one of the biographies, by the Lord's wondrous beauty, by His humility, simple as a child's, and by
the largeness and bounty of His heart. And the people of Kashi marvelled to see joy, humility, restlessness and the other *sanchari-bhavas*
succeed one another in the Lord (25.67). <!-- F42 -->

Noticing the crowd around Him and the sannyasis led by Prakashananda, the Lord stopped dancing. And then something
happened that no one expected:

“The Lord honoured Prakashananda's feet, and Prakashananda came forward and clasped the Lord's feet.” (CC, Madhya
25.69)[^7] <!-- F43 -->

A conversation followed in which each tried to give honour to the other:

“The Lord said, ‘You are the guru of the world, most worthy of worship. I am not equal even to a disciple of your
disciple. Why should you, who are superior, honour one who is inferior? You are equal to Brahman, and your obeisance is
My total ruin. Although to you everything appears as Brahman, for the sake of teaching the people you should not act in
this way.’ Prakashananda replied, ‘The blasphemy of You that I committed before has all been destroyed by the touch of
Your feet.’” (CC, Madhya 25.70–73)

“The Lord said, ‘Vishnu! Vishnu! I am a tiny, lowly jiva. To regard a jiva as Vishnu is the mark of an offender.’” (CC,
Madhya 25.76)

“Prakashananda said, ‘You are Bhagavan Himself. Even so, if You think of Yourself as His servant, You are still more
worthy of worship than all of us. Blaspheming You has brought me total ruin. <…> Now devotion to Your lotus feet will
arise in me; for that I bow down at Your feet.’” (CC, Madhya 25.79–80, 84)

By bowing to a sannyasi's feet and exclaiming “Vishnu! Vishnu!”, the Lord teaches the world: a jiva must not be called
God, and sannyasis should be honoured. But Prakashananda is right in calling Him Bhagavan Himself: the Lord's humility
does not make Him a jiva. So the head of the sannyasis of Kashi repentantly acknowledged his former offences and by the
touch of the Lord's feet was cleansed of them. <!-- F44 -->

Then Prakashananda sat down beside the Lord and began to ask:

“‘We all know now that the faults You have shown in Mayavada come from the Acharya's imagined interpretation. You have
explained the primary meaning of the sutras, and hearing it, everyone's mind was filled with wonder. You are Ishvara,
and all potencies are Yours. Please explain it to us briefly; I long to hear it.’” (CC, Madhya 25.86–88) <!-- F45 -->

The Lord, once again in humility calling Himself a tiny jiva, replied: the true meaning of the sutras is known only to their author, Vyasa,
and he himself explained them. The meaning of the pranava is unfolded in the Gayatri, and the Gayatri in the four
principal verses of the “Bhagavatam,” which the Lord revealed to Brahma, Brahma to Narada and Narada to Vyasa;
understanding that this was the meaning of his sutras, Vyasa made the “Bhagavatam” their commentary. In these verses
sambandha, abhidheya and prayojana are all given. Having explained them, the Lord concluded:

“‘Therefore study the *Bhagavatam* carefully: from it you will gain the essential meaning of the sutras and the shruti.
Always chant the name of Krishna in sankirtana: you will easily attain liberation and gain the treasure of love.’” (CC,
Madhya 25.146–147)

Thus the Lord revealed to Prakashananda the bhakti-siddhanta handed down in the Brahma-sampradaya — so it is called both
by Srila Bhaktivinoda Thakura in the “Amrita-pravaha-bhashya” and by the biographies — and showed that the
“Srimad-Bhagavatam” is the natural commentary on the “Brahma-sutra.” True Vedanta is bhakti. <!-- F46 I4 -->

Then the brahmana from Maharashtra told the assembly how the Lord had once explained the
“atmarama” verse in sixty-one ways; everyone wished to hear them, and the Lord explained them again, while the people marvelled and felt
that He was Krishna Himself (25.153–156). <!-- F47 -->

“All the residents of Kashi took up the sankirtana of the Name; in love they laughed, wept, sang and danced. The
sannyasis and panditas began to study the *Bhagavatam*. Thus the Lord delivered the city of Varanasi.” (CC, Madhya
25.158–159)

From that day, the biographies say, the sannyasis of Kashi became devotees of Mahaprabhu: Prakashananda and all the
sannyasis fell at His feet and glorified Him, and the nectar-stream of the holy name swept away the false teaching
of an impersonal Absolute. Varanasi became like Navadvipa, and the Lord, smiling, said to the devotees: no one would
take My wares here, and I thought I would have to carry them back; but you were grieved, and so I have given them to
everyone free (25.160–163). <!-- F48 -->

For five days the Lord gave deliverance to all who came to Him, and on the sixth He left Varanasi (25.170); in all, on
His way back from Vrindavana, He spent two months there instructing Sanatana (Adi 7.47; Madhya 25.3).[^8] In his brief
summary of the pastimes Kaviraja mentions it in a single line:

“He sent him to Mathura, investing him with the strength of bhakti; then, after showing mercy to the sannyasi, He went
to Nilachala.” (CC, Madhya 1.245)

Srila Bhaktisiddhanta writes in the “Anubhashya” (on Madhya 25.1) that the Lord brought the Kashi sannyasis, with
Prakashananda at their head, onto the path of pure bhakti. <!-- F49 F50 -->

According to the “Anubhashya” and one of the biographies, this took place in the year 1435 of the Shaka era
(1513).[^9] <!-- F51 -->

## How Others Tell It
Govinda-deva in the “Gaura-krishnodaya” tells it differently. The sannyasis of Kashi, adherents of another doctrine,
disregarded the Lord; one night, at someone's invitation, He came to them with a few companions, and they, seated on
their seats, said to Him disdainfully, “Stand there.” Then the Lord made the radiance of His own being blaze forth, and
they could neither look at Him nor approach. To each of them He appeared in the form of the god to whom that one was
devoted, while Prakashananda recognised in Him “the one Consciousness itself — Brahman.”[^10]

“‘O Lord, embodiment of the Vedas, You Yourself, king of ascetics, shine in the highest heaven!’ — saying this, he fell
in shame at Hari's feet together with the best of the sannyasis.” (“Gaura-krishnodaya” 16.33)

The Lord embraced him: “Get down from the elephant of pride and say, ‘Hari, Achyuta, Krishna, victory!’” — and he
answered: “O Lord of the world, what You command is precisely what the shrutis establish; what need is there here of
the interpretations of us, the unrighteous?” His throat choked with rapture, he sang the names of Krishna (16.34–37).

“When the Lord had thus revealed His all-pervading power to Prakashananda, best of the sannyasis of the Sarasvati order,
and awakened [in him] devotion to Himself, the other sannyasis gave up their other intentions.” (“Gaura-krishnodaya”
16.38) <!-- F52 -->

Kavi Karnapura in the “Chaitanya-chandrodaya” says of Varanasi on the Lord's return journey only that everyone came to
Him — brahmacharis, householders, mendicants and hermits, priests and those under vows —

“only a few envious leaders of the sannyasis did not go there — and did not see Him.” (“Chaitanya-chandrodaya” 9.33)

Prakashananda is not named here, and the drama is silent about any conversion. Jayananda in his “Chaitanya-mangala”
writes: “Gaurachandra went on pilgrimage to Varanasi and duly put to shame a heretical sannyasi” — no name is given, but
he is probably meant; the same work mentions that the old sannyasis of Varanasi went away to Nilachala. Lochana Dasa
only remarks that in holy Varanasi “many great sannyasis live” (47.36), with neither name nor conversation. Murari
Gupta, Karnapura's mahakavya and the “Gaura-ganoddesha-dipika” say nothing of Prakashananda. We tell the story
according to the “Charitamrita.” <!-- F53 F54 F55 -->

## Not Prabodhananda
Prakashananda is sometimes identified with Prabodhananda Sarasvati — the tridandi of the Sri-sampradaya from Shrirangam,
uncle and teacher of Gopala Bhatta Gosvami, author of the “Chaitanya-chandramrita.” Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati
rejects this identification. In the “Gaudiya-bhashya” (on CB, Madhya 3.37) he writes that inexperienced people take
Prakashananda to be one with Prabodhananda, Venkata Bhatta's younger brother from the Kaveri; the mistake
crept into the sahajiya “Bhakta-mala” of Lal Dasa, and from there, more or less, into the books of many recent
authors. In the “Anubhashya” (on Adi 7.149 and Madhya 17.104) he says the same, and on Madhya 25.45
adds: there is no evidence that after his deliverance Prakashananda received the name “Prabodhananda.” And the
“Charitamrita” itself, which describes the change in him in such detail, says not a word about a new name. The whole
dispute, with all its arguments, is examined in the article on Prabodhananda Sarasvati.[^11] <!-- F56 Q11 Q12 I6 -->

Srila Bhaktisiddhanta's preface to the “Chaitanya-chandramrita” shows how different the two were. Prakashananda was an
ekadandi of Shankara's school, a resident of Aryavarta, a *nirvisheshavadi* — a preacher of a God without attributes —
an opponent of Vishnu and the Vaishnavas, who became a devotee only after his deliverance; Prabodhananda was a
southerner, a tridandi of Ramanuja's school, an eternal associate of the Lord. The stern words about Mayavada in
Prabodhananda's books are praise of the mercy of the Lord who saves the fallen: the conversion of Sarvabhauma and of
Prakashananda, says Srila Bhaktisiddhanta, is a still greater miracle than the deliverance of Jagai and Madhai.
<!-- F57 -->

Prakashananda's conversion, then, is told in the “Charitamrita” — in Adi 7 and Madhya 25;[^12] the
“Chaitanya-bhagavata” only records the Lord's words about him spoken in Navadvipa. <!-- Q13 -->

## After the Conversion
What became of Prakashananda afterwards the sources do not say. It is not known how long he lived after meeting the
Lord, or where and when he left this world; nor are his days found in our calendar. The last we know of him is that he
sang “Hari! Hari!” at the temple of Bindu-madhava, clasped the Lord's feet and heard from Him the essence of the
“Vedanta-sutra,” while the sannyasis of Kashi, his disciples, from then on studied the “Bhagavatam” and chanted
Krishna's name.

## Significance
Prakashananda's story is a story of mercy. He who taught that the Lord's body is created by maya, whose doctrine angered
the Lord already in Navadvipa, who laughed at Him as a “conjurer” and “a sannyasi in name only,” in the end called Him
Bhagavan Himself, clasped His feet and before his own disciples refuted Shankara's interpretations. This was not the
victory of one debater over another. The Lord answered abuse with nothing but a smile; He accepted the invitation to
the assembly in order to save the sannyasis, and He came to them humbly, sat down where feet are washed, called Himself
a fool and one of the lower order. The change in the heart of the leader of the Mayavadis was worked by the touch of
the Lord's feet and by His mercy. That is why Srila Bhaktisiddhanta ranks Prakashananda's conversion above the
deliverance of Jagai and Madhai. <!-- I1 I5 -->

It is also a lesson about Vedanta. Shankara himself is not to blame — he hid the direct meaning by the will of God; what
is ruinous is only to listen to his indirect interpretation. True Vedanta is bhakti: Brahman is Bhagavan with an eternal
spiritual form, the “Srimad-Bhagavatam” is the natural commentary on the “Brahma-sutra,” the way is hearing and singing
the holy name, the goal is love for Krishna. And the right to Vedanta belongs to the humble — to one who seeks no honour
and gives honour to others. <!-- I2 I3 I4 -->
