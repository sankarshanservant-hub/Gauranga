# Prakashananda Sarasvati
*Пракашананда Сарасвати · @prakashananda-sarasvati*

## The Head of the Sannyasis of Kashi
Prakashananda Sarasvati lived in Kashi — Varanasi — at the same time as Sri Chaitanya and stood at the head of the
Mayavadi sannyasis there. The “Chaitanya-charitamrita” calls him “the chief among the sannyasis, Prakashananda” (Adi
7.62), and once, in the Bengali text, Prakashananda Shripada (Madhya 17.104); in the “Chaitanya-bhagavata” his name is
heard only in the wrathful words of the Lord Himself; Govinda-deva in the “Gaura-krishnodaya” honours him as “the best
of the sannyasis of the Sarasvati order” (16.38). The biographies call him Sri Prakashananda Sarasvati,
“sannyasi-pradhana” — the chief of the sannyasis — and “the guru of the sannyasis.” <!-- F1 -->

He was a danda-bearing sannyasi of Shankaracharya's school — an *ekadandi*, one who carries a single staff. Srila
Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati in his “Anubhashya” calls him “an ekadandi sannyasi of Shankara's sampradaya who lived in
Kashi in Mahaprabhu's time,” the then head of the ekadandis of Kashi. Shankaracharya instituted ten names for his
sannyasis (*dashanami*); three of them — Tirtha, Ashrama and Sarasvati — are ranked above the rest for purity of
conduct and honour, and Prakashananda bore the name Sarasvati. By Shankaracharya's rule, only brahmanas who have taken
sannyasa in one of these ten orders count as the “Vedic sannyasis” whom the world reveres. <!-- F2 F3 -->

Kashi was then the chief stronghold of Mayavada, the doctrine of the impersonal Brahman, and Prakashananda led a great
number of sannyasis of Shankara's school — nearly a thousand, says one of the biographies. The “Charitamrita” testifies:

“Prakashananda Shripada sat in his assembly and taught Vedanta to his many disciples.” (CC, Madhya 17.104)

In expounding the Vedas he denied the Lord's eternal form and called His body false, woven of maya — so the Lord Himself
says of him in the “Chaitanya-bhagavata.” Srila Bhaktisiddhanta in his “Gaudiya-bhashya” calls him a teacher of
Mayavada who in his commentaries on the Vedas “dissected” the transcendental body of the Lord. That is why Krishnadasa
Kaviraja, speaking of the mercy that flooded the whole world, says:

“He made His devotees of all — even the mlecchas and others; only the Mayavadis of Kashi escaped.” (CC, Adi 7.39)

Who these Mayavadis were, Srila Bhaktivinoda Thakura explains in his “Amrita-pravaha-bhashya”: the sannyasi
Prakashananda and others. <!-- F4 F5 -->

Of his worldly life the sources say nothing. Neither his birthplace and family nor his sannyasa-guru nor the years and
days of his appearance and departure are known; nor are his days found in the Vaishnava calendar of the Sri Chaitanya
Saraswat Math. Srila Bhaktisiddhanta in his preface to the “Chaitanya-chandramrita” notes only that he was a resident of
Aryavarta, that is, a northerner. Nor has the place where he lived survived: in the “Anubhashya” (on Madhya 17.87)
Srila Bhaktisiddhanta writes that in Kashi today there is no trace left of Chandrashekhara's house, of Tapana Mishra's
house, or of “the place of Prakashananda Sarasvati, the leader of the Mayavadis.” <!-- F6 F7 -->

## The Lord's Wrath in Navadvipa
Prakashananda's name was spoken by the Lord Himself long before He came to Kashi — while still in Navadvipa, before
His sannyasa. Once in the house of Murari Gupta the Lord manifested the form of the Boar, Varaha. Murari offered Him
prayers, and the Lord, pleased with them but angered at the Vedas, said:

“‘The Vedas slander Me, claiming that I have no hands, feet, mouth or eyes. In Kashi lives a rogue named Prakashananda
who hacks My body to pieces. He explains the Vedas, yet rejects My eternal form. Though leprosy has already covered his
whole body, he still cannot understand his mistake. My body is perfectly pure and is the embodiment of all sacrifices.
Therefore even such great personalities as Brahma and Shiva glorify it. By touching My body even the purest thing in
this world is purified. How then does this scoundrel dare to say that My body is made of illusion?’” (CB, Madhya
3.36–40)

One of the biographies explains: many reciters of the Vedas, failing to understand such mantras as “Without hands and
feet, He is swift and grasps…” (“Shvetashvatara Upanishad” 3.19), imagine the Lord to be without hands and feet,
whereas these words say only that His body is not made of matter. <!-- F8 Q9 -->

Another time, again in Murari's house, the Lord gave him His betel and joked that by touching His remnants Murari had
lost his caste. And at once He entered the mood of the Supreme Lord:

“Having spoken thus, the Lord took on the mood of the Supreme Lord. Clenching His teeth in anger, He went on with great
resolve: ‘In Kashi lives a sannyasi named Prakashananda. He takes pleasure in cutting Me to pieces. He teaches the
“Vedanta,” but denies My divine form. I have struck him with leprosy, yet he still has understood nothing. Countless
universes are manifest in My body. How then does this fool dare to claim that My body is illusory?’” (CB, Madhya
20.32–35)

“I am eternal, My servants are eternal, and the servants of My servants are eternal too,” the Lord went on. “My
pastimes and deeds are eternal. My abode is eternal. One who says they are illusory cuts Me to pieces” (20.39–40).[^1]
<!-- F9 Q10 -->

The harsh words here are not personal enmity but the wrath of the Lord for the sake of His eternal form. The leprosy He
speaks of twice is no worldly punishment: Srila Bhaktisiddhanta explains in the “Gaudiya-bhashya” that Prakashananda
had become an offender against the Lord, and leprosy struck his body, yet understanding never dawned; whoever tries to
“dissect” the spiritual body of the Lord is struck with leprosy in body and in mind alike. The “Charitamrita” says
nothing of leprosy, and whether he was healed the sources do not say. <!-- F10 -->

According to Srila Bhaktisiddhanta, these words of the Lord belong to the years 1425–1430 of the Shaka era (about
1504–1509); according to one of the biographies, to a time “long before 1431 Shaka.” Already then, therefore,
Prakashananda was a Mayavadi sannyasi in Kashi. <!-- F11 -->

Narahari Chakravarti, too, remembers this story. In the “Bhakti-ratnakara,” taking the reader around Navadvipa, he
brings him to Murari's house and recalls how the Lord, chewing betel, entered ever more deeply into the mood of the
Lord of all and “railed at Prakashananda, who lived in Kashi” (BR 12). And in the “Gaudiya-bhashya,” commenting on the
sannyasa of Vishvarupa, Srila Bhaktisiddhanta contrasts two sannyasis named Sarasvati: Prakashananda spent his time in
arguments built on speculation, while Prabodhananda Sarasvati, Madhvacharya and other tridandipadas served Hari.

## “The Sentimental Sannyasi from Gauda”
After His sannyasa, on His way to Vrindavana, the Lord stopped briefly in Varanasi. Krishnadasa Kaviraja relates:

“On His way to Vrindavana the Lord stayed in Kashi, and the Mayavadis began to blaspheme Him: ‘Though a sannyasi, He
sings and dances. He does not hear Vedanta, but performs sankirtana. This foolish sannyasi does not know His own
dharma; a sentimentalist Himself, He wanders about with sentimentalists.’” (CC, Adi 7.40–42)

Their reproach rested on a rule of smriti: a sannyasi must give up *tauryatrika* — singing, dancing and playing
instruments — and always study Vedanta. Seeing that the new sannyasi did not listen to Shankara's teaching but sang of
Krishna and danced, intoxicated with love, the sannyasis of Kashi judged Him ignorant of the dharma of renunciation. But
the Lord's songs, dancing and tears are prema, not worldly *tauryatrika*; the Mayavadi, Srila Bhaktisiddhanta explains
in the “Anubhashya,” confuses the Lord's transcendental emotions with mundane singing and dancing.

“Hearing all this, the Lord smiled within, and, paying no heed, He spoke with none of them. Disregarding them, He went
on to Mathura.” (CC, Adi 7.43–44) <!-- F14 F15 -->

The “Charitamrita” tells of those days in more detail in the Madhya-lila. The Lord was then staying with Chandrashekhara
and Tapana Mishra, declining every invitation so as not to meet the Mayavadi sannyasis. Every day a respected brahmana
from Maharashtra came to Him, charmed by His beauty and His love of God. One day this brahmana went to Prakashananda and
began to tell him of the Lord's greatness: of His golden form and arms reaching to His knees, of His lotus eyes, of how
all the signs of a maha-bhagavata described in the “Bhagavatam” were manifest in Him, how Krishna's name never left His
tongue and tears of love flowed from His eyes. Kaviraja gives Prakashananda's reply thus:

“Hearing this, Prakashananda laughed heartily, and mocking the brahmana, he said: ‘I have heard of Him — the
sentimental sannyasi from Gauda, a disciple of Keshava Bharati and a deceiver of people. His name is Chaitanya. He goes
about with His sentimentalists from land to land and village to village, making them dance. Whoever sees Him declares
Him to be God. Such is His art of enchantment: whoever looks at Him falls under its spell. Sarvabhauma Bhattacharya is a
formidable scholar, yet I hear that in Chaitanya's company he has gone mad. He is a sannyasi in name only; in fact He is
a great conjurer. But here in Kashi His wares of emotion will not sell. Keep hearing Vedanta and do not go near Him. The
company of unruly people ruins both this world and the next.’” (CC, Madhya 17.115–121)

Such were Prakashananda's words while the Lord's mercy had not yet touched him. <!-- F16 -->

Pained at heart, the brahmana left, repeating “Krishna! Krishna!”, and told the Lord everything:

“‘While speaking of Your faults he uttered Your name, saying “Chaitanya, Chaitanya” three times. But not once in those
three times did the name of Krishna come to his lips. He takes Your name with contempt, and hearing it pains me.’” (CC,
Madhya 17.126–127) <!-- F17 -->

The Lord explained to him why this was so:

“The Lord said, ‘The Mayavadis are offenders against Krishna. They incessantly say “Brahman,” “atma,” “chaitanya”
(consciousness). Therefore the name of Krishna does not come to their lips. The name of Krishna and Krishna's own form
are one and the same.’” (CC, Madhya 17.129–130)

And with a smile He added:

“‘I came to Kashi to sell My wares of emotion, but there is no buyer; they do not sell, so I shall have to take them
back home.’” (CC, Madhya 17.144)

The brahmana himself the Lord accepted as His own devotee (17.146), and next morning He left for Mathura. In Varanasi,
says one of the biographies, the sayings of the Mayavadis were heard everywhere — “tat tvam asi,” “aham brahmasmi,” the
words “Brahman,” “atma,” “chaitanya”; and Chandrashekhara complained to the Lord that here one hears nothing but
“maya” and “Brahman” (17.95).[^2] <!-- F18 F19 -->

[^1]: One of the biographies refers to this episode as “CB 2.10.31…44”; in fact it is Madhya 20.31–44.
[^2]: One of the biographies retells this chapter freely: the brahmana supposedly named the Lord three times as “Krishna
    Chaitanya,” while Prakashananda said “chaitanya” three times but could not utter “Krishna”; the brahmana begs the
    Lord to deliver the city of Kashi, and the Lord resolves to do so on His way back. Another writes that the Lord
    forbade the brahmana to associate with Mayavadis. None of this is in the “Charitamrita”: Prakashananda, reviling the
    Lord, himself called Him “Chaitanya” three times; the brahmana only asks why this was so (Madhya 17.128); it is
    Prakashananda who tells the brahmana not to go to the Lord (17.121), and the Lord forbids nothing. We follow the
    “Charitamrita.”
