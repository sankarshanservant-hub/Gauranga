# Shivananda Sena
*Шивананда Сена · @shivananda-sena*

## The Closest Servant
Year after year, while Mahaprabhu lived in Puri, hundreds of devotees came to Him from Bengal — and almost always one
man led them. He knew every road and river crossing in Orissa, paid the tolls for everyone, found lodging and food, and
at home, in Kumarahatta, received the Lord Himself and His associates. This is Sri Shivananda Sena. Krishnadasa
Kaviraja, enumerating the branches of the tree of Sri Chaitanya, speaks of him thus:

“Shivananda Sena was an intimate servant of the Lord; all who went to see the Lord went in his company. Every year he
took the Lord’s devotees with him to Nilachala, caring for them all along the way.” (CC, Adi 10.54–55)

His youngest son, Kavi Karnapura, opens the “Gaura-ganoddesha-dipika” with a bow to his father:

“With supreme devotion I worship my father, Sri Shivananda, the lamp of the Sena family, foremost among the associates
of Mahaprabhu.” (“Gaura-ganoddesha-dipika” 4)

Nityananda Prabhu called him simply and affectionately “Shiva,” and one of the biographies, citing the “Charitamrita,”
calls him “a devotee of the devotees.” <!-- F1 F27 -->

## Lineage and Family
Shivananda came of the vaidya caste, a hereditary line of physicians. A Vaishnava, the biographies remind us, may appear
in any family, and it is not his lineage that exalts him but he who exalts his lineage: Shivananda came into the world
among the vaidyas in order to bless and glorify them. The names of his father and mother, like the name of his wife,
the sources have not preserved. <!-- F4 I1 F5 -->

He had three sons — Chaitanya Dasa, Ramadasa, and the youngest, Paramananda, whom the world knows as Kavi Karnapura.
The “Charitamrita” calls them all fearless devotees of the Lord (Adi 10.62), and Kavi Karnapura in the
“Gaura-ganoddesha-dipika” reveals who his elder brothers were in Krishna’s pastimes:

“The two famous parrots of Vrindavana, Daksha and Vichakshana, have now been born as my elder brothers, Chaitanya and
Ramadasa.” (“Gaura-ganoddesha-dipika” 145)

The eldest, Chaitanya Dasa, according to Srila Bhaktisiddhanta in the “Anubhashya,” wrote a commentary on the
“Krishna-karnamrita.” <!-- F6 -->

With him also were two nephews, the sons of his sister — Shrikanta Sena and Shrivallabha Sena. In Kavi Karnapura’s drama
“Chaitanya-chandrodaya” Shrikanta addresses Shivananda as “revered uncle,” and the Lord speaks of him as “Shivananda,
your maternal uncle.” According to the “Gaura-ganoddesha-dipika” (174), Shrikanta in Vraja is Katyayani; and at the
Ratha-yatra, as Srila Bhaktisiddhanta writes, both brothers sang in Mukunda’s kirtana party. <!-- F7 -->

Shivananda’s whole family served the Lord. The “Charitamrita” sees his people as the offshoots of his branch:

“The offshoots of Shivananda are his own people — his sons, his servants and others, all devoted to the service of
Sri Chaitanya.” (CC, Adi 10.61)

His wife and three sons received the boundless mercy of the Lord precisely because they were dear to Shivananda (CC,
Antya 12.44), and the Lord, as Krishnadasa Kaviraja says, called his whole family His own (Antya 12.51). Kavi Karnapura
writes the same in the introduction to the “Ananda-vrindavana-champu”: Mahaprabhu is the deity of our family, who
accepted all of Shivananda’s household as His own. <!-- F8 I6 -->

The family’s hereditary guru and priest was Srinatha Pandita of Kanchrapara, a disciple of Advaita Acharya — the one
whom Kavi Karnapura in the “Gaura-ganoddesha-dipika” calls his guru, “a moon in the line of the brahmanas,” and the one
whose glory is “Krishnadeva, who shines in Kumarahatta” (3, 210–211). Srinatha received Mahaprabhu’s mercy, and of his
service to Krishna the biographies say that it won the three worlds. Although Kavi Karnapura received the mantra of the
Name from the Lord Himself, he took initiation from Srinatha according to custom and bows to him in the introduction to
the “Ananda-vrindavana-champu.” Initiation “according to custom” in no way diminishes the mantra given by the Lord: in
this Karnapura simply kept the established order, honouring the guru of his family. In the “Chaitanya-chandrodaya” there
is a scene in which the young Srinatha, “dear to all people,” goes to Puri not with Shivananda but with Advaita: Advaita
had promised to bring him to the Lord Himself and to win special mercy for him. <!-- F9 -->

Shivananda himself worshipped the Lord with the four-syllable mantra of Gaura-Gopala: this was his chosen mantra, his
secret, and we shall come back to it. <!-- F10 -->

## Kumarahatta and Kanchrapara
Shivananda lived in Kumarahatta, now Halisahar, in the 24 Parganas district, on the bank of the Ganges. His *shripat*,
the holy site of his home, is there. Srila Bhaktisiddhanta in the “Gaudiya-bhashya” on the “Chaitanya-bhagavata” writes
that Kumarahatta is the present Halisahar near Kancharapada station, and that Shrivasa, Shivananda Sena, Vasudeva Datta
and other devotees lived there. <!-- F11 -->

Kumarahatta is the birthplace of Ishvara Puri, Mahaprabhu’s guru. The Lord, coming here, took a handful of earth;
the devotees took it after Him, and so a pond was formed, called Chaitanya-doba. After the Lord accepted sannyasa,
Shrivasa Pandita moved to Kumarahatta with his family: to stay on in Navadvipa without the Lord had become unbearable to
him. Here too lived the lame Bhagavan Acharya and other devotees of Gaura, and Vasudeva Datta, a close friend of Shrivasa
and Shivananda, moved to Kumarahatta, to Kanchanpalli, and lived beside them.[^1] <!-- F13 F14 F15 -->

A mile and a half from Kumarahatta, at Kanchrapara, Shivananda installed a Deity of Gaura-Gopala. He is worshipped to
this day — in the temple of Sri Krishna-raya.[^2] At Kanchanpalli, or Kanchrapara, Shivananda’s youngest son was born;
according to one of the biographies, in 1527. Another biography calls his birthplace Kanchan-puri and places it near
the present railway workshops outside Calcutta. <!-- F12 F16 -->

## Jagadananda’s Teacher
One of the few pieces of news about Shivananda before the Lord’s sannyasa has survived in the “Prema-vivarta” — a poem
that tradition ascribes to Jagadananda Pandita. Jagadananda recalls in it the man who brought him up:

“Blessed is Shivananda, Kavi Karnapura’s father: in my childhood he taught me the ‘Bhagavatam’ and the ‘Gita.’ Taking
me to Nadia, he left me at the Lord’s feet; Shivananda is my protector in good times and bad.” (“Prema-vivarta”
8.17–18)

In Shivananda’s house, cooking offerings, Jagadananda learned to cook — and later served Sri Gauranga with this skill
(8.19). Thus Shivananda brought to the Lord’s feet one of His dearest associates. Neither the “Chaitanya-bhagavata” nor
the “Charitamrita” speaks of this; we know it only from the “Prema-vivarta.” <!-- F26 -->

## Poet and Witness
Shivananda was a poet too. Several Bengali padas about Gaura bear his name, and in the view of the scholar of Bengali
padas B. Majumdar they were composed during the Lord’s lifetime, by an eyewitness of His pastimes.[^3] In one of them,
known from the “Bhakti-ratnakara,” young Gaura plays Holi in Navadvipa, holding in His embrace Gadadhara, filled with
the bhava of Radha; Narahari sings of Vraja, Mukunda, Murari and Vasu dance, and the Lord, lost to Himself, asks: “Where
is My flute? Where is Govardhana, where the bank of the Yamuna?” The pada ends thus:

“Says Shivananda, hearing the Lord’s words full of rasa: where the Lord and Gadadhara are, there is a mine of rasa.”
(“Bhakti-ratnakara,” tar. 12)

In another pada (“Padakalpataru” 2127) the Lord, leaning on Govinda, listens to talk of Vrindavana and falls
unconscious crying “Radha, Radha!”, while Shivananda weeps, “not comprehending the Lord’s bhava.” Yet another
(“Padakalpataru” 2355) glorifies Gadadhara Pandita, who for the Lord’s sake gave up his vow to live in the Kshetra and
his service of Gopinatha: Gaura and Gadadhara are “waves of one prema.” <!-- F22 -->

The most piercing of his padas, known from the “Gaura-pada-tarangini,” was composed, according to Majumdar, just after
the Lord had left Shantipura for Nilachala and told the devotees of Gauda to come to Him once a year:

“What You have commanded I shall surely carry out, but how shall I live alone? My sons and all my family will be like
poison to me: how shall I pass my days without You? You told me to come to Nilachala with the pilgrims of Gauda at the
year’s end, for darshana. How shall I endure, how pass a whole year, when a moment seems a hundred yugas?” (pada
“Dayamaya Gaurahari…,” 2–3)

This is the language of separation, viraha. What sounds here is not dislike of the family — Shivananda’s whole family,
as we have seen, consisted of devotees — but the feeling in which, without the Lord, everything grows hateful, even what
is dearest; and the poet’s threat in the last couplet to “take his own life” is a hyperbole of separation common in
Bengali padas. There is also a pada, “Akhila bhuvana bhari…,” signed “Shivananda-dasa” — on Gaura as a cloud of mercy that
poured a downpour of prema over the whole world; whether it belongs to our Shivananda is unclear. <!-- F22 F23 -->

Kavi Karnapura, too, remembers his father as a poet and a connoisseur of rasa. In the “Gaura-ganoddesha-dipika” he
quotes a verse about Nityananda and adds that his father used to recite it while dancing:

“‘And so the divine Avadhuta, whose nature is Bala, always moving among the Vaishnavas, blazed with the splendour of a
thousand suns’ — so my father said as he danced.” (“Gaura-ganoddesha-dipika” 63)

And where he speaks of Saranga Thakura, the son also passes on his father’s judgement: Saranga was Nandimukhi in Vraja;
“some consider him Prahlada, but my father does not accept this” (“Gaura-ganoddesha-dipika” 172). <!-- F24 F25 Q15 -->

## “From the Very Beginning I Have Known Your Love”
When the news reached Bengal that the Lord had returned to Puri from His tour of the South, Shivananda was among the
devotees of Gauda who rejoiced beyond words (CC, Madhya 10.84). Soon they all set out for Nilachala. Murari Gupta names
among those going “Sri Shivananda Sena with his sons and wife” (4.17.6). And Kavi Karnapura in the
“Chaitanya-charita-mahakavya” relates that his father came together with Murari Gupta and then saw the Lord for the
first time — “like a heap of his own good fortune made visible” (13.127). The Lord, His heart softened with compassion,
again and again touched his head with the great toe of His foot and said: “Why, I know you” (13.128). <!-- F21 F19 -->

King Prataparudra was then watching the Bengali Vaishnavas arrive from the roof of his palace, and Gopinatha Acharya
named them for him one by one: “This is Hari Bhatta, and this is Nrisimhananda. And these are Vasudeva Datta and
Shivananda Sena.” (CC, Madhya 11.87) Meeting the devotees, the Lord had a kind word for each, and for Shivananda a
special one:

“Then Sri Chaitanya said to Shivananda: ‘From the very beginning I have known your deep love for Me.’ Hearing this,
Shivananda Sena was overwhelmed with divine love and fell flat before the Lord. Then he recited this verse: ‘O Lord,
having lost count of time, I was sinking in the fathomless, boundless ocean of material existence, and now at last I
have reached the shore — I have found You, my Deity. And in me You have at last found one who needs Your mercy more than
any other.’” (CC, Madhya 11.149–151)

The verse with which Shivananda answered comes from Yamunacharya’s “Stotra-ratna” (21). Kavi Karnapura describes the
same scene in the “Chaitanya-chandrodaya”: “Shivananda! I know that you are very attached to Me,” says the Lord, and
Shivananda answers with the same words of Yamunacharya (8.40–41). <!-- F19 -->

## Every Year to Nilachala
Taking leave of the Bengali devotees, the Lord wished to see them every year at the Ratha-yatra, and He Himself
entrusted Shivananda with leading them and caring for them on the way:

“Come every year with all My devotees for the festival of cleaning the Gundicha temple. Please, take care of them too
on the way.” (CC, Madhya 15.97)

From then on this became the service of his whole life. Every spring the devotees of Gauda, headed by Advaita, set out
for Puri, and Shivananda took everything upon himself — the costs of the road, food, lodging (CC, Madhya 1.138–139). He
knew that this was what the Lord desired, and he did it not as an organizer of pilgrimages but as a servant carrying
out His will. Vrindavana Dasa writes:

“In accordance with the Lord’s instructions they came every year to celebrate the Ratha-yatra… Mukunda Datta too came,
the singer of Lord Krishna. Shivananda Sena and others brought their families with them.” (CB, Antya 8.5, 15)
<!-- F27 F28 Q2 -->

Krishnadasa Kaviraja describes how Shivananda led the pilgrims:

“Shivananda Sena gladly took upon himself the expenses and the care of all the pilgrims. He led everyone, knowing all
the roads of Orissa perfectly, and at the halts he provided the devotees with shelter and all they needed.” (CC, Madhya
16.19–20)

On the roads and river crossings of Orissa a levy was taken from travellers, and Shivananda settled it himself for
everyone; usually, having paid for each, he let them all go on ahead and crossed the toll-post last (CC, Antya
12.15–17). Kavi Karnapura in the “Chaitanya-chandrodaya” puts these words into the mouth of a wayfarer who wants to join
the pilgrims: they are looked after by one Shivananda, an associate of the Lord; he removes all the obstacles that the
guards of the crossings put in the way, those thorns on the road, and takes everyone along with him, even chandalas
(Act 10). <!-- F29 Q3 F33 -->

With him went the people of Khanda headed by Narahari (CC, Madhya 1.132), Vasu Ghosha with his brothers, and many
others; and in the year when the Younger Haridasa left this world the Bengalis came to Puri, “taking all the devotees
with them,” in Shivananda’s care (CC, Antya 2.162). Rupa Gosvami, delayed in Bengal by the death of his brother Anupama,
no longer found on the road the Vaishnavas of Gauda headed by Shivananda (CC, Antya 1.37–39). <!-- F30 -->

He fixed the day of departure himself, but waited for Advaita’s word. In the “Chaitanya-chandrodaya” Advaita sends His
servant Gandharva to him: “Tell Shivananda to fix the day of departure,” and Shivananda replies that he himself is
awaiting this command (Act 10). In the “Advaita-prakasha” a messenger from Shivananda announces to Advaita that he will
lead Gaura’s associates to the chariot festival (18.15–18). According to the “Chaitanya-charita-mahakavya,” Shrivasa,
Vasudeva and Shivananda fixed the day of departure together; the Lord, meeting them in Puri, spoke joyfully with
Vasudeva and Shivananda, and Shivananda bowed to Him after Advaita and Nityananda. He and Vasudeva brought the Lord two
vessels of water — one for Jagannatha’s bathing festival, the other for Himself (canto 14). <!-- F33 F21 -->

In the third year the wives of the Vaishnavas also set out, carrying from home the dishes the Lord loved:

“With Shrivasa Pandita went his wife Malini. Shivananda Sena’s wife also set out for Nilachala with her husband.
Shivananda Sena’s son, named Chaitanya Dasa, went joyfully with his parents to see Mahaprabhu.” (CC, Madhya 16.22–23)

“Shivananda Sena looked after all the devotees. He paid all the expenses and tolls and found the best places to stay.
Thus Shivananda Sena went in bliss to meet the Lord, caring for all the devotees and providing them with food.” (CC,
Madhya 16.26–27) <!-- F31 Q4 -->

One scene of such an arrival Kavi Karnapura recorded in the “Chaitanya-chandrodaya.” Advaita, seeing the Lord from afar, dances and falls to the ground, and Shivananda tells
his son, so that all can hear: “Look, look, there He is, there!” — and in verses he describes Gauranga, whose arms are
like golden bolts, and the embrace of Advaita and the Lord in which no one can tell which is Advaita and which Chaitanya
(10.7–9). <!-- F21 -->

## Vasudeva Datta’s Sarakhela
Shivananda’s closest friend and neighbour was Vasudeva Datta, generous beyond measure: whatever he earned he gave away
the same day. The Lord, caring for his household, gave Shivananda a special charge:

“Sri Chaitanya respectfully said to Shivananda Sena: ‘Please look after Vasudeva Datta. He is very generous. As soon as
he earns anything, he spends it all at once. He is a family man, and therefore he must keep some money in reserve. It is
hard to maintain a family without savings for unforeseen needs. Take upon yourself the care of his house and his money
affairs. Do whatever is needed for this.’” (CC, Madhya 15.93–96)

In the Bengali text the Lord calls Shivananda Vasudeva’s *sarakhela* — the steward, the guardian of his household. In
this charge the biographies see the Lord’s instruction to all householder Vaishnavas: a family given to the service of
God is also part of their duty, and even the most generous devotion must not leave it without support. And for
Shivananda it was one more service by the direct word of the Lord. <!-- F32 Q5 F15 -->

## The Lord in Kumarahatta
In 1514, going from Puri to Vrindavana by way of Bengal, the Lord visited Kumarahatta too. Vrindavana Dasa writes:

“The Lord stayed a few days in Advaita’s house, and then went to Kumarahatta, to the house of Shrivasa Pandita.” (CB,
Antya 5.5)

“At that moment Vasudeva Datta came, accompanied by Shivananda Sena and other devotees.” (CB, Antya 5.18)

Jayananda tells the same in his “Chaitanya-mangala”: “Shivananda Sena and Vasudeva Datta came there” (Vijaya-khanda,
5.26). And according to the “Charitamrita,” the Lord not only received them at Shrivasa’s but came to them Himself:

“Then He visited Shivananda Sena and Vasudeva Datta.” (CC, Madhya 16.206) <!-- F17 Q14 F18 -->

Kavi Karnapura in the “Chaitanya-chandrodaya” describes this visit in detail, and Jagadananda appears again in the
story. As soon as the Lord stepped ashore, Jagadananda, who had long lived in Shivananda’s house and loved it, slipped
away ahead and with special art decorated the whole way to it with banana trunks, full pitchers, buds and rows of lamps.
At the end of the night Shivananda led the Lord along the river. Vasudeva begged the Lord to visit Shivananda’s house
first; the Lord entered his household shrine, Jagadananda washed His feet, sprinkled the roof with that water and gave
it out to the household, and then the Lord looked in on Vasudeva for a moment (Act 9). In the
“Chaitanya-charita-mahakavya” the Lord at night secretly slips away by boat from the crowd that pressed upon Him and
comes to Vasudeva’s house, and then to Shivananda’s (20.11–19).[^4] <!-- F18 -->

## Held at the Toll-Post
Shivananda’s service was not always easy. The “Charitamrita” mentions briefly that once a toll collector detained him
(Antya 1.20). Kavi Karnapura in the “Chaitanya-chandrodaya” tells of it at length. In the region of Remuna, while the
great king Gajapati was away on campaign in the south, one of his officials, the head of the guards of the crossings, was
doing as he pleased. He counted all the pilgrims head by head, demanded the toll for past years as well, and to extract it
put Shivananda in wooden stocks. At night the official sent for him; Shivananda, offering praise to the Lord’s feet,
went. “Whose man are you?” asked the official. “Sri Krishna Chaitanya’s.” — “You are Chaitanya’s, and I am Jagannatha’s.
Who is greater — Jagannatha or Chaitanya?” — “For me Krishna Chaitanya is greater.” And then the official confessed: in a
dream Sri Krishna Chaitanya had told him to release “His man” at once. He begged forgiveness and let them all go without
any toll (Act 10). So the Lord Himself protected the one who for the sake of His devotees bore hardship and humiliation.
<!-- F34 -->

## The Dog
The best-known story about Shivananda is the story of the dog. On the way to Puri a stray dog attached itself to the
pilgrims, and Shivananda took it under his care and fed it himself (CC, Antya 1.17). When an Oriya boatman at a
crossing would not let it into the boat, Shivananda paid ten panas of cowries for its passage — no small sum. Once, when
he had been detained at a toll-post, the servant forgot to give the dog its rice, and it was lost. Shivananda was
grieved, sent ten men to look for it, and, when they did not find it, did not eat that evening himself (Antya
1.18–24). <!-- F35 F36 F37 -->

Anxious, they all reached Nilachala, met the Lord, saw Jagannatha, took prasada and went to their lodgings (Antya
1.25–27). And next morning, coming to the Lord, they were astonished:

“Coming there, they saw that the very dog that had been lost was sitting right in front of the Lord. Sri Chaitanya was
feeding it the pulp of a green coconut and saying with a smile: ‘Chant the names Rama, Krishna and Hari!’ Seeing the
dog eat the coconut and say ‘Krishna, Krishna’ again and again, everyone was amazed to the depths of their hearts.”
(CC, Antya 1.28–30)[^5] <!-- F38 F39 -->

“Seeing this, Shivananda humbly fell flat before the dog, begging its forgiveness. From that day no one saw the dog
again, for it had obtained a spiritual body and gone to Vaikuntha. Such are the divine deeds of the Son of Mother Shachi.
He delivered even a dog, making it chant the name of Krishna.” (CC, Antya 1.31–33) <!-- F40 F41 Q6 -->

Krishnadasa Kaviraja says the same at the beginning of the “Charitamrita”: “With Shivananda Sena came a supremely
fortunate dog, which attained liberation by seeing the lotus feet of Sri Chaitanya.” (Madhya 1.140) <!-- F41 -->

Shivananda’s bow is not the worship of an animal. It is the humility of a Vaishnava who saw the Lord’s mercy upon a
dumb creature and felt himself at fault before one whom the Lord had so exalted. And this mercy did not come to the dog
by chance: it came through the service of a Vaishnava. The “Advaita-prakasha” relates that the dog lived in Shivananda’s
house and was purified by eating the remnants of the Vaishnavas’ food; with him it went to Purushottama and, chanting
“Hare Krishna,” obtained a perfect body. The author glorifies here the power of the Vaishnavas’ remnants and of the dust
of their feet: eating a particle of their prasada, one obtains devotion to Krishna (18.113–124). In the
“Chaitanya-chandrodaya” Shivananda gave the dog pure food at every halt and paid separately for its passage; it was lost
two thirds of the way, and found on the seashore beside the Lord, who was tossing it bits of coconut — Jagannatha’s
prasada — and saying: “Say: Krishna!” In Karnapura’s words, it seems that in that very body it took on another form and
reached another world (Act 10). And the “Gaura-krishnodaya” says that the dog came to the Lord’s feet, loudly uttered
the name of Hari and shone (16.48). Its liberation is not a “merging,” but departure to Vaikuntha in a spiritual body,
by the mercy of the Lord given to it through His devotee. <!-- F42 -->

## Three Kinds of Mercy
Krishnadasa Kaviraja, naming Shivananda as a branch of the tree of Sri Chaitanya, adds that he tasted the Lord’s mercy
in all its kinds:

“The Lord shows His mercy to the devotees in three ways: by being with them in person (sakshat), by entering someone
with His power (avesha), and by appearing where He is not present in person (avirbhava). In His direct presence all the
devotees see Him alike; in the body of Nakula Brahmachari there was the Lord’s avesha. His earlier name was Pradyumna
Brahmachari; afterwards the Lord gave him the name Nrisimhananda. In him Sri Chaitanya manifested Himself (avirbhava).
The Lord has many such wonderful, unearthly ways. Shivananda Sena tasted all these rasas.” (CC, Adi 10.56–60)

In person, *sakshat*, he saw the Lord year after year in Puri. Of the avesha and the avirbhava Krishnadasa Kaviraja tells
in the second chapter of the Antya-lila.[^6] <!-- F48 -->

## Nakula Brahmachari
In Ambika, a village by the town of Kalna in the present Burdwan district, lived the great Vaishnava Nakula Brahmachari.
The country was then divided into *muluks* — districts where the revenue offices of the Muslim rulers were seated — and
Ambika was such a district: the “Charitamrita” calls it Ambuya (Antya 2.16), and the biographies write that the district
court of Kalna was in the Ambika quarter, now Pyari-ganj. <!-- F43 -->

“And to deliver the people of Bengal, Chaitanya entered the heart of Sri Nakula.” (CC, Antya 2.17)

Nakula laughed and wept, danced and roared as if possessed;[^7] all the signs of ecstasy appeared on his body, his face
shone with the same light as the Lord’s, and he asked all who came to him to sing the names of Krishna. People flocked to
him from all over Bengal (Antya 2.18–21). The word “possessed” here is only a comparison: no alien power had seized
Nakula; the Lord Himself had entered a pure Vaishnava, and this was His *avesha*. <!-- F44 -->

Hearing of this, Shivananda came to Ambika, but did not at once believe the report. To test Nakula, he stopped at a
distance and resolved: if he himself calls me and names the mantra with which I worship the Lord, then Sri Chaitanya is
in him (Antya 2.22–25). Kavi Karnapura in the “Chaitanya-chandrodaya” adds that at first Shivananda thought of not going
at all: “I have seen the Lord Himself face to face — what is this man to me?” (Act 9). The crowd was countless, people
came and went, and Nakula suddenly said: “Shivananda Sena is standing somewhere here; let him be found and called.”
Shivananda was found; he came up, bowed and sat down (Antya 2.26–29). <!-- F45 F46 F47 -->

“Nakula said: ‘You still doubt. But listen to me attentively. I know that you chant the four-syllable Gaura-Gopala
mantra. Cleanse your heart of all doubt.’” (CC, Antya 2.30–31)

Shivananda became convinced that the Lord Himself was present in Nakula, bowed to him, and began to serve him with
heartfelt devotion (Antya 2.32). In the “Chaitanya-chandrodaya” Nakula says: “Your chosen mantra has four syllables; its
deity is Gaura-Gopala.” And the “Gaura-krishnodaya” relates that the brahmachari exposed the pride with which Shivananda
had come to test him, named the Gaura-Gopala mantra, and at his request set out the order of worship of Gaura for
householders — the name, the mantra, the Gaura-gayatri, nyasa and meditation (18.20–27). <!-- F46 Q7 F10 F47 -->

Shivananda’s doubt does not diminish his faith. It is part of the lila: thus the Lord who had entered Nakula revealed
Himself both to Shivananda and to all who hear this story — and Shivananda himself, in Krishnadasa Kaviraja’s words,
thereby tasted yet another kind of His mercy. <!-- F48 -->

## Nrisimhananda and the Three Offerings
Once Shivananda’s nephew Shrikanta came alone to Puri. The Lord in His mercy kept him with Him for two months, and,
sending him back to Bengal, told him to say that no one should come that year: He would come to Gauda Himself and meet
Advaita and all the devotees.

“Gladden Shivananda Sena with the news that in the winter month of Pausha I shall come to his house as an unexpected
guest. Jagadananda also lives with him, and he will cook for Me.” (CC, Antya 2.42–43)[^8] <!-- F49 -->

The devotees rejoiced. Advaita, who was already setting out, stayed at home, while Shivananda and Jagadananda prepared
everything and all through Pausha waited every day for the Lord until evening (Antya 2.44–46). According to the
“Chaitanya-chandrodaya,” Shivananda laid in what the Lord especially loved: vastuka greens, banana pith, ears of grain.
But the month passed, the Lord did not come, and both grew sad. Karnapura explains why: Ramananda Raya had come to Puri
from the Godavari and begged the Lord to stay (Act 9). <!-- F50 -->

Then Pradyumna Brahmachari, to whom the Lord had given the name Nrisimhananda, came to them. Learning the cause of their
sadness, he promised to bring the Lord to them in three days, and they, knowing his power and his love, believed him
without a shadow of doubt (Antya 2.48–53). He spent two days in meditation and said to Shivananda: “I have brought Sri
Chaitanya to Panihati; tomorrow at noon He will come to your house” — and told him to make ready provisions and utensils
(Antya 2.54–58).[^9] <!-- F51 F52 -->

At dawn Nrisimhananda himself cooked many dishes and divided them into three offerings — for Jagannatha, for Sri
Chaitanya and for his own chosen Deity, Nrisimhadeva. Having offered them, he sat outside and became absorbed in
meditation — and saw the Lord come, sit down and eat all three offerings, leaving not a crumb. Nrisimhananda was beside
himself with happiness, but, keeping faith with his own Deity, he lamented on purpose: “You are one with Jagannatha and
may eat His offering, but why did You eat what was meant for Nrisimhadeva? It seems Nrisimha must fast today!” (Antya
2.59–66). <!-- F53 -->

He did not lament in earnest: Sri Chaitanya, Nrisimha and Jagannatha are not different in essence (Antya 2.67), and,
in the words of the biographies, by this lila the Lord showed that He is one with Jagannatha and Nrisimha. Shivananda
asked what grieved him, and heard:

“He alone has eaten the offerings for the three Deities, leaving Lord Jagannatha and Lord Nrisimhadeva hungry.” (CC,
Antya 2.71) <!-- F54 Q8 -->

Shivananda wondered: was it true, or was Nrisimhananda speaking so in a transport of love? But he did not argue — he
brought provisions again, and Nrisimhananda cooked and offered food to Nrisimhadeva once more (Antya 2.72–74). A year
later Shivananda, as usual, brought the Bengali devotees to Puri, and there the Lord praised Nrisimhananda before
everyone:

“‘Last winter Nrisimhananda Brahmachari fed Me such wonderful sweets and vegetable dishes as I had never tasted
before.’” (CC, Antya 2.77)

All were amazed, and Shivananda’s doubt vanished of itself (Antya 2.78). The biographies see in this one more example of
the Lord’s mercy to Shivananda: He Himself, before everyone, answered the question that Shivananda carried in his
heart. <!-- F55 Q9 I5 -->

In the same chapter Krishnadasa Kaviraja says that the Lord constantly manifested Himself (*avirbhava*) in four places
— in Shachi’s household shrine, where Nityananda dances, at the kirtana at Shrivasa’s, and in the house of Raghava
Pandita (Antya 2.34–35, 79–80) — and he concludes the story of Shivananda thus:

“Who can describe the greatness of Shivananda Sena’s love? Captivated by his pure love, Sri Chaitanya came to him again
and again.” (CC, Antya 2.82) <!-- F48 F20 -->

The Lord appearing in Nakula’s body, before Nrisimhananda and in four homes of Bengal is not “possession,” nor is it
the devotee’s equality with God: it is the inconceivable power of the Lord, by which He, conquered by love, comes to His
devotees. <!-- F48 -->

## News of Raghunatha
One year, when Shivananda was already leading the pilgrims to Puri, Raghunatha Dasa, the son of the wealthy landholder
Govardhana Majumdar, ran away from home. His guru, Yadunandana Acharya, had taken him along to persuade another of his
disciples to return to the service of the Deity, and Raghunatha halfway sent the guru home and, having received his
leave, went away (CC, Antya 6.161–169). His father, deciding that his son had gone with the Bengali Vaishnavas, sent ten
men after them with a letter to Shivananda:

“At once he wrote a letter to Shivananda Sena, humbly asking him: ‘Please, return my son to me.’” (CC, Antya 6.180)

The messengers overtook the pilgrims at Jhankra, but Raghunatha was not among them, and they returned empty-handed
(Antya 6.181–183). In Puri Shivananda, meeting Raghunatha with the Lord, told him of this pursuit (Antya 6.246–247).
<!-- F70 F71 Q15 -->

Four months later, when Shivananda had returned to Gauda, Govardhana sent a man to him again, and Shivananda told him
how his son was living: the Lord had placed him in the care of Svarupa Damodara; day and night he sang the names of
Krishna, cared neither for food nor clothing, and in the evening stood at the Lion Gate begging alms (Antya 6.248–256).
The parents were grieved and sent to Shivananda two servants and a brahmana with four hundred coins for their son’s
service; Shivananda told them to come when he went to Puri again, and the next year took them with him (Antya
6.258–261, 266).[^10] Raghunatha took nothing for himself. Only for his father’s good did he begin to use part of this
money to feast the Lord twice a month, and after two years he stopped (Antya 6.267–275). <!-- F71 F72 -->

Kavi Karnapura in the “Chaitanya-chandrodaya” puts Raghunatha’s glory into his father’s mouth: to Govardhana’s messenger
Shivananda replies with verses asking who among those living at Nilachala does not know this disciple of Yadunandana,
follower of Svarupa, sole treasury of renunciation — and promises to take the messenger under his own care and lead him
to Puri (10.3–4). The “Charitamrita” quotes these verses and remarks:

“Thus Kavi Karnapura described the greatness of Sri Raghunatha Dasa, of whom Shivananda Sena had spoken.” (CC, Antya
6.265) <!-- F73 -->

## Chaitanya Dasa’s Feast
Once in Puri Shivananda brought his eldest son to the Lord. Hearing his name — Chaitanya Dasa — the Lord asked: “What
sort of name is this?” Shivananda answered: “He bears the name that came to me from within” (CC, Antya 10.142–145). He
invited the Lord to his lodging and fed Him the finest and costliest prasada of Jagannatha. Out of regard for Shivananda
the Lord ate it all, but ate too much. The next day Chaitanya Dasa invited the Lord: understanding what He wanted, he
served rice with yogurt, lime, ginger and salt. The Lord was very pleased — “this boy has understood what I really
need” — and gave him the remnants of His meal (Antya 10.146–151). <!-- F69 -->

## Nityananda’s Foot
Nityananda Prabhu, the deliverer of the fallen, was boundlessly merciful to Shivananda too, and His dearest mercy came
as a blow of His foot. It happened in the year of Haridasa Thakura’s departure. The devotees, as usual, gathered in
Navadvipa to go to Puri; Nityananda went with them, although the Lord had told Him to stay in Bengal. Shivananda went
with his wife and three sons and, as always, led everyone and settled the tolls at the toll-posts (CC, Antya 12.7–16).
<!-- F56 -->

This time he was long detained by the collectors. The devotees reached a village, but without him no lodging could be
found, and all settled down under a tree. And here Nityananda, in the guise of a wondrous cowherd boy of Vraja, as if
faint with hunger, displayed a feigned anger:

“‘Because Shivananda has not seen to a place to stay, I am now dying of hunger. Since he is not here when he is needed,
let his three sons die!’” (CC, Antya 12.20)

Shivananda’s wife, hearing this “curse,” began to weep. Returning, Shivananda comforted her: why weep? Let our sons die
— for we have caused Nityananda inconvenience (Antya 12.21–23). There is no indifference to his children in these words:
it is complete devotion to Nityananda, for whom he is ready to give up everything. <!-- F57 F58 -->

“Having said these words, Shivananda went without delay to Nityananda. But Nityananda rose and kicked him.” (CC, Antya
12.24)

Under the guise of a blow Nityananda placed His foot upon him — the foot that even Brahma cannot touch.[^11] Shivananda
rejoiced at this good fortune, quickly found lodging in a milkman’s house, led Nityananda there with prayers at His feet,
and glorified Him: <!-- F59 -->

“‘Today You have accepted me as Your servant and rightly punished me for my fault. Under the pretext of punishment You
have given me Your mercy. Who can understand the nature and greatness of Your deeds? Even Brahma cannot touch the dust
of Your beautiful feet, yet You have touched my sinful body with Your foot. Today my birth, my family and my deeds have
become wholly successful. Today I have attained all goals — dharma, wealth, worldly joy and, at last, love for Sri
Krishna.’” (CC, Antya 12.27–30) <!-- F59 Q10 -->

Shivananda himself calls this not a penalty but acceptance as a servant, and says that together with love for Krishna
he has obtained all that people strive for. He begged to be touched by those feet again and again. Pleased with the
praise, Nityananda smiled, rose and lovingly embraced him (Antya 12.31). Krishnadasa Kaviraja explains:

“Extraordinarily contradictory are all the qualities of Sri Nityananda. When He kicks someone in anger, that person
attains good fortune.” (CC, Antya 12.33)

The anger of Nityananda, the guru of the whole world, is only a veil hiding the highest mercy and eternal good; all His
conduct is the opposite of the ordinary. <!-- F61 I3 -->

But not everyone understood this lila. Shivananda’s nephew Shrikanta — then still very young — loved his uncle dearly
and was hurt that Nityananda had kicked an associate of Mahaprabhu known to all. He went ahead alone to the Lord and bowed
to Him without taking off his *petangi*, his upper shirt. Govinda, the Lord’s servant, told him that the shirt must be
taken off before bowing, but the Lord stopped him:[^12]

“But Sri Chaitanya objected: ‘Please, do not bother Shrikanta. Let him do as he likes, for he has come here already
deeply upset about something.’” (CC, Antya 12.38) <!-- F62 Q11 -->

From these words Shrikanta understood that the Lord is all-knowing, and said not a word about what had happened (Antya
12.40–41). So the Lord soothed his childlike attachment to his uncle. One of the biographies recalls here the Lord’s
words in the “Bhagavatam”: “I am wholly dependent on My devotees and, as it were, not free, O twice-born: the saints
have taken possession of My heart, and dear to Me are those who are dear to My devotees” (9.4.63). <!-- F62 I6 Q15 -->

Soon all the Vaishnavas arrived. The Lord met them as before; the women saw Him from a distance. All were lodged where
they had stayed in former years and fed with Jagannatha’s mahaprasada (Antya 12.41–43).[^13] <!-- F63 -->

## Puri Dasa
And now Shivananda brought his three sons to the Lord, and the Lord was especially merciful to them — for their father’s
sake (CC, Antya 12.44). He asked the name of the youngest, and Shivananda answered: “Paramananda Dasa.” That name had its
own story:

“Once Sri Chaitanya had said to Shivananda: ‘When a son is born to you, give him the name Puri Dasa.’” (CC, Antya
12.46–47)

The boy was then still in his mother’s womb, and was born when Shivananda returned home.

“By Sri Chaitanya’s instruction he received the name Paramananda Dasa, but in jest Sri Chaitanya always called him Puri
Dasa.” (CC, Antya 12.49)[^14] <!-- F64 Q12 -->

The “Chaitanya-chandrodaya” gives this conversation its own way: Shrikanta reports to the Lord that coming too are the two
sons of his uncle, whom the Lord has already seen, and the youngest, who has not yet seen His feet; and the Lord, turning
to Paramananda Puri, says: “Svami, this is your servant” (Act 10).[^15] <!-- F64 -->

When Shivananda brought the boy to the Lord, the Lord put the great toe of His foot into his mouth (Antya 12.50);
according to one of the biographies, the boy, having bowed, began of himself to suck the toe of His foot.

“The good fortune of Shivananda Sena is boundless! Who can surpass it? For Sri Chaitanya Mahaprabhu Himself called his
whole family His own.” (CC, Antya 12.51)

After His meal the Lord gave Govinda this instruction:

“‘As long as Shivananda Sena’s wife and children are staying here in the town, give them a plate of the remnants of My
meal every day.’” (CC, Antya 12.53) <!-- F65 Q12 F8 -->

Another time Shivananda came to the Ratha-yatra with his wife and youngest son, still a small child, and told
him to bow at the Lord’s feet. The Lord again and again said to the boy: “Say: Krishna!” — but he was silent. His father
too urged him — in vain (CC, Antya 16.65–68). The Lord wondered:

“Then Mahaprabhu said: ‘I have made the whole world chant the holy name. Even trees and stones I have made utter the name
of Krishna! But him I could not make utter the name of Krishna!’ Hearing this, Svarupa Damodara said: ‘You gave him the
mantra of Krishna’s name. Having received the mantra, he does not wish to reveal it to anyone. He chants it within, but
does not utter it aloud. I think that is his intention.’” (CC, Antya 16.69–72) <!-- F66 -->

The child’s silence is not obstinacy but faithfulness to the mantra received from the Lord Himself. Srila
Bhaktisiddhanta explains in the “Anubhashya”: a mantra received from the guru is not revealed to others, or it loses its
power — and he recalls that this can be seen also in the story of Gadadhara Pandita. Puri Dasa at his age already kept
this rule. <!-- F67 I4 -->

On another day the Lord said: “Puri Dasa, recite!” — and the boy at once composed and recited a verse:

“Glory to Hari, the ornament of all the girls of Vrindavana! He is the blue lotus at their ears, the collyrium of their
eyes and the necklace of wondrous sapphires on their breasts.” (CC, Antya 16.74)

All were amazed: a seven-year-old child who had not yet studied anything had composed such a verse. “Such is the
greatness of the mercy of Sri Chaitanya Mahaprabhu, whose limit even Brahma and the other gods cannot find” (Antya
16.75–76). With this verse Kavi Karnapura’s “Arya-shataka” opens. <!-- F68 Q13 -->

## Oil for the Lord
Shivananda’s house was always open to the Lord’s service. Once Jagadananda Pandita, returning from Navadvipa, stayed
with him:

“When he was staying at Shivananda’s house, about fifteen kilograms of sandalwood oil was prepared there with many
fragrant ingredients. With this fragrant sandal oil they filled a large pot, and Jagadananda carried it with care to Sri
Jagannatha Puri.” (CC, Antya 12.102–103)

The oil was meant for the Lord’s head; how this story ended is told in the article on Jagadananda. <!-- F74 -->

## Everything for Service
Power, wealth and all that this world gives are justified only when they serve Hari, guru and the Vaishnavas. Shivananda
disposed of his possessions — his lands and property — in just this way: all he had he gave to the service of Sri
Gauranga and His devotees. Not only his sons but the servants in his house were devotees of the Lord. <!-- F75 I2 F8 -->

The sources have preserved neither the year, nor the day, nor the place of his appearance and departure; nor do they
name the place of his samadhi. The days of Shivananda Sena’s appearance and departure are not in our calendar (Sri
Chaitanya Saraswat Math) either. <!-- F60 F5 -->

## Vira-duti
Who Shivananda was in Krishna’s pastimes, his son reveals:

“The messenger Vira, who once in Vrindavana led all the gopis to Krishna, is now my father; she who in Vraja was
Bindumati is now my mother.” (“Gaura-ganoddesha-dipika” 176) <!-- F2 Q1 F3 -->

A *duti* is a messenger, a sakhi-friend who serves the meetings of Radha and Krishna and brings the gopis to Krishna.
As Vira nourished the pastimes of Krishna and the gopis, so Shivananda, descending into Gaura’s lila, nourished the
pastimes of Gaurahari: he led His devotees to Him, year after year, across the rivers and toll-posts of Orissa.
<!-- F2 -->

## Significance
The life of Shivananda Sena is the life of a householder given wholly to the Lord. His wealth, house, family and
strength served one purpose — that the devotees of Gauda might see their Lord. He paid for all, fed all, found lodging
for all, and did not leave even a stray dog without care; and the Lord, captivated by his love, came to him again and
again. <!-- I2 I5 -->

The Lord’s mercy came to him in all its kinds — face to face, through Nakula and through Nrisimhananda; and Nityananda’s
mercy came in the form in which only a true servant recognizes it: under the guise of anger. And the Lord so loved him
that He accepted his whole family as His own: dear to Him are also those who are dear to His devotees. <!-- I3 I6 -->
