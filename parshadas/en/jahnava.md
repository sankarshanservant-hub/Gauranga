# Jahnava Devi
*Джахнава-деви · @jahnava*

## The Shakti of Nityananda
Jahnava Devi is the eternal energy, the shakti, of Nityananda Prabhu, and His consort in Gaura-lila. She is called
Jahnava Thakurani, Jahnava Mata — “Mother Jahnava,” Jahnava Ishvari, that is, the Queen, and Nityanandeshvari; in the
“Gaura-ganoddesha-dipika” her name is Jahnavi. Together with her elder sister Vasudha she is a transcendental (aprakrita)
shakti of Nityananda; one of the biographies calls her “Nityananda-svarupini,” His second self, and another says still
more boldly: Jahnava Devi is Nityananda Prabhu Himself.[^1] <!-- F1 F2 F3 -->

She is also called “Bhakta-svarupini” — devotion personified, a treasury of prema-bhakti; in her divine majesty
(aishvarya) and divine sweetness (madhurya) are equally wonderful. Like Nityananda, she is a jagad-guru, a teacher of love
for Krishna to the whole world. When Gaurasundara and Nityananda withdrew Their pastimes, it was she who was honoured as
guru in all three holy lands — in Gauda-mandala, in Kshetra-mandala, that is, in Puri, and in Vraja-mandala. By her
mercy she delivered many sinners and godless people. <!-- F4 F11 F41 F114 -->

## Daughter of Surya Dasa
Jahnava's father was Surya Dasa Sarakhela, also known as Suryadasa Pandita and Surya Pandita. He was dear to
Sri Chaitanya, and Nityananda and Gauranga too regarded him as Their intimate devotee. Her mother's name was Bhadravati;
one of the biographies calls her most fortunate — it fell to her to become the mother of two shaktis of Nityananda. The elder
daughter was Vasudha, the younger Jahnava. <!-- F13 F14 F15 -->

Jahnava's grandfather was Kamsari Mishra, a brahmana of the Vatsa gotra from the Ghoshal family. He and his wife
Kamala Devi had six sons, and Surya Dasa was the third.[^2] His elder brothers were Damodara and Jagannatha, his younger
ones Gauridasa Pandita, Krishnadasa Sarakhela and Nrisimha Chaitanya. Gauridasa, Surya Dasa's younger brother, moved with
his permission from Shaligrama to Ambika on the Ganges (“Bhakti-ratnakara” 7.330–331); his niece would one day weep at
his samadhi in Vrindavana. Another uncle, Krishnadasa Sarakhela, was later with her at the festival in Kheturi.[^3] <!-- F16 F17 F18 F19 F159 Q3 -->

Surya Dasa lived in Shaligrama, not far from Navadvipa, near the present Muragachha station of the Eastern Railway; his
shripat is there.[^4] He held a high post under the ruler of Gauda, from which he received the title “Sarakhela” —
“chief,” “commander” — and great wealth (“Bhakti-ratnakara” 12.3875–3878).[^5] Yet it was not this that made him
renowned: he and his brothers were pure in character and spotless in people's eyes, and the virtues of Surya Dasa himself,
a large-hearted man, were beyond counting. Above all there was his faith in Nityananda. Krishnadasa Kaviraja, listing the
branches of Nityananda's tree, names both brothers together:

“Suryadasa Sarakhela and his brother Krishnadasa had firm faith in Nityananda and were abodes of love”
(CC, Adi 11.25) <!-- F20 F21 F22 F164 Q4 -->

Around 1409 of the Shaka era (≈ 1487), says one of the biographies, Jahnava appeared in the world to Bhadravati, the
wife of Surya Dasa Sarakhela of Ambika-Kalna.[^6] The day of her appearance is the ninth day of the bright
fortnight of Vaishakha, “Gaura-navami”; on that same day the appearance of Sita Devi, the eternal consort of Ramachandra,
is also celebrated. Our calendar marks it in the same way: on the Gaura-navami of the month of Madhusudana it remembers
the appearance of Nityananda's shakti Sri Jahnava Devi and of Rama's shakti Sri Sita Devi.[^7] <!-- F23 F24 F25 -->

## Surya Dasa's Dream
When his daughters came of age, Surya Dasa began to wonder to whom he should give them; he asked brahmanas to find
husbands, but nothing was ever settled (“Bhakti-ratnakara” 12.3880). At that time a certain
Krishnadasa of Borgachhi near Shaligrama, son of Harihara, a kayastha and a devotee of Nityananda, took it upon himself to
find a wife for Nityananda. And an old brahmana of Shaligrama, seeing how anxious Surya Dasa was, revealed to him who
Nityananda was: the son of Harai Pandita and Padmavati of Ekachakra — in Krishna-lila, Vasudeva and Rohini — Balarama
Himself, who had wandered through all the holy places, and the eternal husband of his daughters. <!-- F26 F27 -->

One night, having sat up late with these thoughts, Surya Dasa fell asleep and saw a dream: he was giving both his
daughters to Nityananda. Nityananda appeared to him as Balarama Himself, and Vasudha and Jahnava, shining brighter than
gold and kunkuma, in jewelled ornaments and many-coloured garments, stood at His left and right in the forms of Revati
and Varuni, lighting up all ten directions with their radiance. Surya Dasa forgot himself in rapture
(“Bhakti-ratnakara” 12.3901–3911):[^8] Nityananda granted him this vision of His majesty to delight His devotee. <!-- F26 F28 F31 I7 -->

In the morning Surya Dasa told his dream to a brahmana friend: “Until He accepts them, I shall have no peace.” He sent his
friend to Navadvipa, to Srivasa, in whose house Nityananda was then staying. Nityananda sent word that He would fulfil
Surya Dasa's wish, and Advaita Acharya, hearing of it, said, “Let it happen soon.” The brahmana returned to Shaligrama
with the good news. <!-- F29 -->

Krishnadasa, son of Harihara (one of the biographies calls Harihara a king and the village Boroganchhi), took upon himself
a splendid wedding in his own house and prevailed on Nityananda to come.[^9] Srivasa, Advaita, Chandrashekhara, Murari
Gupta and many others gathered with sankirtana; devotees from Shaligrama came too. Surya Dasa ran out to meet Nityananda,
fell at His feet and could not utter a word for tears. Nityananda, laughing with joy, embraced him, and Gauridasa,
watching his brother, rejoiced as well. <!-- F30 -->

On the eve of the wedding the adhivasa was performed in the house of Krishnadasa Sarakhela in Shaligrama, with the
brahmanas of Borgachhi and Shaligrama present.[^10] Then Surya Dasa, according to rite and custom, gave both his daughters
to Nityananda. “Both by the custom of the world and by scripture Surya Dasa is fortunate,” says the “Bhakti-ratnakara”:
“he gave his two daughters to Nityananda-chandra” (12.3983). <!-- F32 F33 Q2 -->

The “Bhakti-ratnakara” tells of the wedding in more detail. The bridegroom's adhivasa was held in Baragachhi, while Surya
Dasa prepared his daughters for the rite at home, and the brides' mother “cannot keep her feet on the ground.” At the hour
of godhuli, with lamps and music, Nityananda rode from Baragachhi to Shaligrama, to Surya Dasa's house. Vasudha and
Jahnava, “hidden from sight, saw the Lord of [their] life,” and their mother and her friends adorned them for the wedding;
there, in their father's house, the rite itself was performed (12.3935–3983).[^11] The next day Nityananda returned with
His consorts to Baragachhi, and Srivasa's wife and the elder women performed all that was fitting. <!-- F160 -->

One of the biographies stresses that the wedding was performed “according to human custom.” This does not mean that
Nityananda entered into a worldly union. Every vishnu-tattva has three energies — Shri, Bhu and Nila (Lila) — and
Nityananda, being vishnu-tattva, possesses them too; Vasudha and Jahnava are His eternal shaktis, and the marriage only
disclosed to people what exists eternally. <!-- F34 -->

## The Newly-Weds
After the wedding Nityananda stayed several days in Baragachhi and Shaligrama and then came with both wives to
Navadvipa, to Shachi Devi. Seeing Vasudha and Jahnava, Shachi embraced them again and again, sat them on her lap and
pinched their cheeks affectionately — she “showed [them] such affection that it is beyond my power to tell”
(“Bhakti-ratnakara” 12.4010); all the mistresses of the Vaishnava households of Navadvipa surrounded the young wives with
love as well. At Shachi's request Nityananda stayed on in Navadvipa. <!-- F35 F36 F162 -->

On her advice He took His wives to Shantipura, to Advaita. Sita Thakurani received them with delight and embraced them as
tenderly as Shachi had. They stayed with Advaita until Uddharana Datta invited Nityananda to Saptagrama, where a great
sankirtana festival went on for several days; from there the way led to Borograma, and Nityananda wandered, leading
kirtana. At last He settled for good in Khardaha on the Ganges.[^12] <!-- F37 -->

In Khardaha Nityananda had a house where Vasudha and Jahnava lived; Parameshvari Dasa served them. Jahnava had no
children. Vasudha bore a son, Virabhadra (Virachandra), and a daughter, Ganga Devi; Virabhadra is revered as an
incarnation of Kshirodakashayi Vishnu, and Ganga as an incarnation of the holy river Ganges. <!-- F38 F39 -->

## Mother of a New Generation
After Nityananda's departure the work of an acharya fell to Jahnava. Jiva Gosvami and the other Gaudiya acharyas gave her
the place of jagad-guru, and so did Srinivasa Acharya, Narottama Thakura, Shyamananda and the other acharyas of the next
generation. The sankirtana festivals that Srinivasa, Narottama and their companions carried everywhere took place by her
mercy and under her guidance — as also under the guidance of Vishnupriya Devi. When Nityananda, Advaita, Srivasa and
many other associates of Mahaprabhu had left the world, Jahnava wished to flood the world with the nectar of sankirtana
even more abundantly than before. <!-- F40 F42 F43 -->

Before setting out for Vrindavana, Srinivasa came to Khardaha. Parameshvari Dasa led him to Vasudha, Jahnava and
Virachandra; Srinivasa prostrated himself before them in tears, and Jahnava placed her feet on his head, blessing him
with the dust of her feet. He stayed there several days, received the mercy of both mothers and the blessing of
Virabhadra, and Jahnava, together with other associates of the Lord, seeing his devotion, told him to go to Vrindavana
(“Bhakti-ratnakara” 4.86–96); from Khardaha he went on to Khanakula, and on returning to Yajigrama he told his mother of Jahnava's command and received
her blessing. <!-- F44 -->

Narottama too visited Khardaha — after his time in Vrindavana. He was brought into the inner apartments of the house, to
the feet of Jahnava and Vasudha,[^13] who were sitting with Virabhadra, talking of the Lord's pastimes. Understanding that
he had already received the mercy of Lokanatha and Jiva, they bestowed their own generously upon him; here the
“Bhakti-ratnakara” (8.213) calls both queens, Vasudha and Jahnava, knowers of all truth. Narottama spent several days
with them in talks about Krishna, and they said of him: “The name ‘Mahashaya’ befits him” (8.215). And at the parting,
says the author, “Sri Jahnava, very softly — I do not know what she said, with tears in her eyes” (8.217). <!-- F45 F118 -->

Vasudha's son Virabhadra also became her disciple in diksha: Nityananda's younger wife became his mother and guru.
According to the “Prema-vilasa” (24.699–704), he once saw her in a four-armed form — and his heart was changed: he
resolved to take initiation from none other than her. <!-- F93 -->

## The Road to Kheturi
Narottama Thakura planned a great festival in Kheturi on Phalguna-purnima, the appearance day of Mahaprabhu. The host was
Raja Santosha Datta, Narottama's cousin and his disciple; all three acharyas came together — Narottama,
Shyamananda and Srinivasa. The “Bhakti-ratnakara” (1.429–434) tells how the associates of Mahaprabhu in Bengal rejoiced in Narottama.
Jahnava rejoiced too — the daughter of Surya Pandita, the consort of Nityananda, honoured by the whole world, who
generously gives away the jewels of prema: his very name filled her with joy, and his renunciation and extraordinary love
for Krishna gladdened her still more. And she graciously set out for Kheturi so that all might see her. <!-- F46 F50 Q5 -->

This journey, according to the “Bhakti-ratnakara,” had a beginning of its own. When Jahnava in Khardaha fixed the day of
departure, the Lord, remaining invisible, told her not to delay: “Srinivasa and Narottama are My dear servants… With My
dear associates, manifest and unmanifest, I will dance and sing in that wonderful sankirtana” (10.360–361). Having said
this, He showed Himself and vanished, and Jahnava, in tears of bliss, at once began to prepare for the road. The people of
Khardaha and the neighbouring villages gathered to see her off; she gave everyone instructions, spoke in private with
Vasudha and, consoling Ganga and Virabhadra with great care, left the house remembering the Lord (10.357–371). <!-- F119 F120 -->

A large company of Nityananda's associates went with her from Khardaha: Parameshvari Dasa, her uncle Krishnadasa
Sarakhela, Madhava Acharya — Nityananda's son-in-law, Raghupati Vaidya, Minaketana Ramadasa, Murari Chaitanya Dasa,
Jnanadasa, Mahidhara, Sri Shankara, Kamalakara Pippalai, Gauranga Dasa, Nakadi, Krishna Dasa, Damodara, Balarama Dasa,
Sri Mukunda and Vrindavana Dasa Thakura, as well as Srivasa's brothers Shripati and Shrinidhi.[^14] The
“Bhakti-ratnakara” (10.372–384) also names in her company Manohara, Nrisimha Chaitanya, Jiva and Pandita Kanai, and of her
companions, Minaketana Ramadasa among them, it says that the mere sight of such Vaishnavas sanctifies the three worlds.
Part of the way Jahnava went on foot, and then, shyly, took her seat in a palanquin; in every village crowds ran out to see
her. At Halisahar she was joined by the sculptor Nayana Bhaskara and by Raghunatha Acharya, son of the lame Bhagavan. Her disciples Jnanadasa and Manohara Dasa went with her, and, according to the
“Prema-vilasa,” little Kanu Thakura as well. Krishnadasa of Akai-hat, too, came with them all to Kantaka-nagara
(Katwa) (“Bhakti-ratnakara” 10.409); one of the biographies sees in him Kala Krishnadasa, one of the twelve gopalas, but
the original sources do not give the name “Kala,” and the identity is not proven. <!-- F51 F52 F121 Q6 -->

First the travellers came to Ambika-Kalna, to the house of Gauridasa Pandita. His disciple Hridaya Chaitanya welcomed
everyone warmly, and Jahnava cooked an offering for the Deities of Gaura and Nityananda with her own hands. Chaitanya Dasa,
son of Vamshivadana, also came out to meet her, and he, Hridaya Chaitanya and many devotees of Ambika went on with her
(“Bhakti-ratnakara” 10.385–390). After a night of festival they went on to Navadvipa. <!-- F53 F122 -->

In Navadvipa the sorrow of separation awaited her. At the sight of the town all, remembering the Lord's pastimes, wept and
fell senseless. In Mahaprabhu's house she did not find Shachi Devi — she was no longer in this world — and she grieved for
her. Shripati and Shrinidhi, Srivasa's brothers, took her to their home, but neither Srivasa nor Malini was there, and
Jahnava wept. “Why has no one come from Shantipura for so long?” she asked — and just then Advaita's sons, Achyutananda
and Gopala, arrived with the Vaishnavas of Shantipura (“Bhakti-ratnakara” 10.391–404). Advaita and Sita Thakurani too were
no longer in the world, and she grieved again; Advaita's sons received her and all the Vaishnavas with due honour.[^15]
Jahnava spent two or three days in Navadvipa, and the people of Navadvipa went with her to Kheturi. <!-- F54 F55 F123 -->

From Navadvipa all went on to Kantaka-nagara, Katwa. Yadunandana and others led the guests to Gauranga's abode;
Raghunandana came from Shrikhanda, and many mahantas gathered. At the place where the Lord accepted sannyasa all wept.
That day Jahnava cooked herself, fed Gaurachandra and the mahantas, and Yadunandana and others ate last — only after her
(“Bhakti-ratnakara” 10.408–425). <!-- F124 -->

The way then led to Teliya-Budhari: there Govinda Kaviraja, the brother of Ramachandra Kaviraja, met her reverently and
worshipped her.[^16] After a night's rest all set out for Kheturi. On the far bank of the Padma, Raja Santosha Datta had
prepared a huge boat for the crossing and, beyond the river, palanquins, carts, lodgings, prasada and decorations; he
himself went far out to meet Jahnava with flowers, garlands and gifts. Srinivasa and Narottama came out to meet her too;
each guest was given a separate house and servants. <!-- F56 F57 -->

## The Festival at Kheturi
Singing sankirtana, the company led by Jahnava entered Kheturi. Srinivasa, Narottama and Shyamananda prostrated
themselves before her in the very dust of the road. Handsome houses had been built in advance for her companions. <!-- F58 -->

On the eve of Mahaprabhu's appearance day the last preparations went forward at her command. In the evening, before the
sankirtana began, Narottama offered her sandal paste and garlands and honoured her; Srinivasa too offered her
sanctified sandal and garlands. At her word Nrisimha Chaitanya Dasa adorned Srinivasa, Shyamananda and Narottama with
garlands and sandal. Jahnava sat on a raised seat at the front of the hall.[^17] Raghunandana of Shrikhanda led the kirtana
first, and then, at the bidding of Jahnava and Achyutananda, Narottama began to sing; Gauranga Dasa, Gokula Dasa and
Vallabha Dasa responded, and Devi Dasa played the mridanga. In the midst of the assembly Jahnava, the shakti of
Nityananda, shone with beauty, and the very sight of her and of the devotees, together with the kirtana, purified even
the godless and the sinful. They sang until midnight. <!-- F59 F60 F61 -->

Narottama manifested six Deities in Kheturi — Gauranga, Vallavikanta, Vrajamohana, Krishna, Radhakanta and Radharamana.
They were installed at Jahnava's direction: the whole festival took place under her guidance, and at her word Srinivasa
performed the abhisheka and archana of the Deities — the Deities themselves Narottama had manifested at the command of
his guru Lokanatha. Having offered the bhoga to the Deities, Srinivasa bowed at her feet, and she told him: “Give everyone
the prasada flower garlands and sandal paste”; she accepted them herself as well, and at Narottama, says the
“Bhakti-ratnakara,” she looked “with a merciful glance” and “joyfully infused [into him] — I do not know what power”
(10.510–522). The next day Srinivasa bathed all six Deities, and at the request of Jahnava and the devotees
Narottama sang again — and in the kirtana Gauranga Himself appeared with Nityananda and His eternal associates. When the
Lord vanished and everyone around was weeping, Jahnava said gently: “Full mercy [has been shown] to Narottama and
Srinivasa. What the Lord ordered has come true: together with His associates He danced in such a kirtana… This lila of
the Lord is beyond this world, full of prema” (“Bhakti-ratnakara” 10.628–631). Then she herself entered the temple and
sprinkled the Deities with phagu — red powder and flower pollen (10.634–641), and at her word the devotees began to shower
one another, as at Holi. At dusk Srinivasa performed the abhisheka in honour of Gauranga's appearance and sang and danced with the
devotees all night. <!-- F47 F48 F62 F63 F125 F126 F127 -->

Jahnava herself cooked for the Deities. The next morning, after the night of kirtana and the mangala-arati, as the
“Bhakti-ratnakara” relates (10.686–688), Jahnava Ishvari in great joy performed her bath and morning rites, with great
eagerness prepared wonderful dishes — rice, vegetables and much else beyond description — and with wonderful ceremony
herself offered the bhoga to Gauranga, Vallavikanta and the other Deities. Skilled in the art of cooking, she quickly
made sweets, cakes and drinks as well, and it was she who directed the whole kitchen.[^18] Then at her direction Madhava
Acharya invited everyone to the meal, and Jahnava distributed prasada to the mahantas with her own hands (10.701–703), and when they had eaten, she asked Narottama, Srinivasa and Shyamananda to
eat — they dared not disobey, and she fed them with care — and took prasada last of all herself. Outside the hall, meanwhile, Raja Santosha was feeding thousands of people. <!-- F64 F65 F128 -->

The great acharyas — Narottama, Srinivasa and others, themselves teachers of the whole world — felt that they had
attained the goal of life by hearing talks about Hari from her lips. And the presence of Jahnava and of the eternal
associates of the Lord who were there with her — Raghunandana, Achyutananda, Vrindavana Dasa, Shrinidhi and Shripati,
Krishna Mishra, Gokula and many others — made happy all who came to the festival. <!-- F49 F68 F115 -->

A controversy is also connected with the Kheturi festival. The “Prema-vilasa” (19th vilasa) claims that Srinivasa
worshipped Gauranga there with the mantra of Radha-Krishna, that the Gosvamis prescribed the worship of Gaura with the
ten-syllable Gopala mantra, that Jahnava approved of this, and that on Phalguna-purnima, Gaura's appearance day, all
took mahaprasada — that is, broke the fast. One of the biographies regards this as an interpolation by a hand hostile to
the Gaura-mantra and to the observance of Gaura's appearance day, and adds that the 19th and 20th vilasas are not a
genuine part of the “Prema-vilasa” at all: of the four manuscripts collected for its edition, three ended with the 18th
vilasa. Nothing of the kind is found in the
“Bhakti-ratnakara” (10th wave): there each of the six Deities is honoured separately, and Narottama calls each by name
(10.492, 495–496). For the form, name, qualities and pastimes of the Lord are varied, and therefore the worship of each
Deity requires its own mantras — so teach scripture and the mahajanas. <!-- F66 Q8 Q10 I8 -->

When the time came to part, the mahantas came to take leave of Jahnava, and she could not hold back her tears. Raja
Santosha in tears presented everyone with gold, cloth and vessels, and Jahnava with her company set out for Vrindavana. At
Srinivasa's command Govinda Kaviraja and others went with her; she promised to return soon, and Srinivasa asked Narottama
to write to him about her journey and to go with her on her way back (“Bhakti-ratnakara” 10.723–765). <!-- F67 F128 F129 -->

## In Vraja
Her holy life was described in detail by Narahari Chakravarti in the “Bhakti-ratnakara”; the eleventh wave of that book
is devoted to her journey to Vraja. Of the road the author tells only a little — what he had heard “from the lips of the
saints.” In one large village the local pashandas, seeing how in the evening great bhagavatas bowed at her feet,
laughed: “They bow to a human being, but to a deity they do not bow,” — and in the temple of Chandi begged the goddess to
destroy the newcomers. At night Chandi, sword in hand, appeared to them in a dream and said in anger: “She whom you called
a brahmana's wife… — you do not know her truth. She is to be borne on my head, she is worshiped by all: she is the wife of
Nityananda-Balaramachandra. ‘Jahnava Ishvari’ — the name is exceedingly sweet; by taking this name the fear of worldly
existence goes away” (11.47–49). In the morning the scoffers fell in tears at the feet of the mahantas, and Jahnava showed
them great mercy: the whole village was immersed in bhakti.[^19] Another time two Yavana robbers planned to plunder her
camp on a river bank, but all night long they could not reach it — the short road became endless — and at dawn, terrified
of “the wrath of the Gaudiya Gosani,” they came to her repentant and received mercy (11.67–89). <!-- F69 F70 F130 F131 Q7 Q20 -->

Through Prayaga and Kashi, says one of the biographies, she came to Mathura, visited the birthplace of Krishna, honoured
the Mathura brahmanas, bathed at Vishrama-ghata and had darshana of Varaha and Keshava-deva (11.90–97). The Vaishnavas of
Mathura went with her to Vrindavana. <!-- F71 F132 -->

The Gosvamis of Vrindavana came out to meet her; according to the “Bhakti-ratnakara,” they met her at the place of
Akrura.[^20] Parameshvari Dasa quietly named each of them to her: Gopala Bhatta, Bhugarbha, Lokanatha, Krishnadasa
Brahmachari, Krishna Pandita, Madhu Pandita and Jiva (11.102–106). The Gosvamis bowed to the ground before her, and Jahnava,
“prema-bhakti embodied,” considering herself lowly, answered them with such humility that she embarrassed them
(11.107–110). Parameshvari introduced to the Gosvamis Srinivasa's disciples as well — Govinda, the younger brother of
Ramachandra Kaviraja, and Bhagavan Kaviraja. At the place of Akrura Jiva told her that the Lord, escaping the crowds in
Vrindavana, used to come here and accept alms on this very spot — and Jahnava in tears bowed to that place
(11.120–122). <!-- F72 F132 -->

In Vrindavana she stayed in the lodging Jiva had prepared in advance, and together with the Gosvamis went for darshana of
Govinda, Gopinatha, Madana-mohana, Radha-vinoda, Radha-ramana and Radha-Damodara, offering the garments and ornaments she
had brought from Gauda. Through Madhava Acharya she told the Gosvamis about the Kheturi festival, and Lokanatha and the
others wept with love (11.124–143). The Gosvamis saw to it that she was as well provided for as possible. At that time,
at Parameshvari Dasa's request, they listened to Govinda's poems and began to call him Govinda Kaviraja, which greatly
pleased Jahnava (11.145–148). <!-- F74 F133 F134 -->

At Radha-kunda lived Raghunatha Dasa Gosvami. Wasted in separation from Rupa, he could not come to her himself and sent a
humble message through Krishnadasa Kaviraja, Raghava Pandita and others. By way of Bahulavana Jahnava came to the Kunda;
Krishnadasa Kaviraja announced her, and Dasa Gosvami came out to meet her in tears. Seeing his body, wasted by austerity
and shining like the sun — he ceaselessly chanted the holy names — she wept and blessed him. Three or four days she spent
at Radha-kunda: she cooked herself, offered bhoga to Krishna and fed the Vrajavasis of Arit-grama (11.149–175). <!-- F76 F135 -->

Once at midday, sitting on the bank of the Kunda, she heard the flute and saw under a kadamba Shyama, standing in His
threefold-bent pose and playing the flute, and around Him Sri Radhika with Her friends led by Lalita. She fainted, and
when she came to herself she disclosed what she had seen to no one (11.178–185).[^21] The ghata where she bathed and where
Krishna appeared to her is called Jahnava-ghata to this day. <!-- F77 F110 F136 -->

With the leave of the humble Dasa Gosvami she went to Govardhana, Manasi-ganga and Nandagrama, and in every place of the
pastimes the feelings connected with what had happened there awoke in her; Madhava Acharya and others lost their
composure, and Jiva calmed them. Returning to Vrindavana, she cooked herself and fed Govinda, Gopinatha, Madana-mohana,
Radha-Damodara, Radha-ramana and Radha-vinoda. “Her heart was eager to hear the Gosani's books,” and Jiva Gosvami read
her the “Brihad-bhagavatamrita” and other books (11.186–203). Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati, in his “Anubhashya” to the
“Chaitanya-charitamrita” (Adi 10.85), also mentions that Jahnava and other devotees came to Vrindavana during Jiva's
lifetime; Jiva provided prasada and lodging for all who came from Gauda. <!-- F75 F78 F79 F137 Q19 -->

Then Jahnava went round the twelve forests of Vraja. In solitude near Govardhana the spring rasa was revealed to her: the
son of Rohini and Krishna, the son of Yashoda, each with His beloveds, played with phagu — and she fell senseless. And at
Rama-ghata, says the “Bhakti-ratnakara,” “Ishvari beheld the Lord of her own life” — Balarama, dancing the rasa with His
beloveds: for Nityananda, her Lord, is Balarama Himself.[^22] Of this too she told no one (11.204–222). <!-- F138 -->

In a village on the Yamuna an old brahmana's only son, still a boy, died. The mother would not let anyone touch the dead
child, but Jahnava said: “You are a Vrajavasi; I shall become pure by touching your son,” — placed her hand on his head,
and the boy came back to life and bowed at her feet. To the parents who thanked her she replied: “Seeing the grief of you
both, Krishna revived the son — what part is mine in this?” (11.223–233). <!-- F139 -->

Remembering the Lord's command in Khardaha, Jahnava resolved to hurry back to Gauda. One day, standing before Gopinatha,
she thought playfully that Radhika beside Him ought to be a little taller. That night Gopinatha appeared to her in a
dream: “My beloved is not as tall as I am… Going to Gauda, quickly manifest the beloved and send [her here]; she will take
her place on [My] left,” — and Radhika, smiling, added: “Do not be shy: this is my desire too.” In the morning Jahnava told
Nayana Bhaskara to meditate constantly on Gopinatha: he was to sculpt His Beloved (11.235–248). <!-- F140 -->

The Gosvamis fixed the time of departure, and Vaishnavas from all over Vraja gathered in Vrindavana. Jahnava in tears took
leave of Govinda, Gopinatha, Madana-mohana and the other Deities, of Gopishvara and Vrinda; she wept at the samadhis of
Raghunatha Bhatta, Kashishvara, Sanatana and Rupa. At the samadhi of her uncle Gauridasa Pandita she wept uncontrollably
(11.259) and, having seen something wondrous there, was comforted by the words of Bara Gangadasa. Gangadasa was her cousin
— the son of the elder sister of her mother Bhadravati, “the wholly devoted wife” whose bhakti amazed everyone. Gauridasa's
beloved disciple, after his guru's departure he nearly gave up his life, and at his guru's command in a dream came to
Vrindavana, where he lived in solitude serving the Lord at Dhira-samira. Before her departure someone presented Jahnava
with a Deity of Krishna with His Beloved; she entrusted His service to Gangadasa and took him with her (11.250–271). <!-- F73 F141 F142 -->

At an auspicious moment she set out for Gauda. At the place of Akrura she looked at Vrindavana for the last time and in
tears took leave of the Gosvamis; the Vrajavasis, saying farewell, wept and told one another of her virtues; Jiva escorted
her all the way to Mathura (11.287–299). <!-- F143 -->

With her in Vrindavana was the boy Kanu, son of Purushottama Thakura — the future Kanu Thakura, also called Shishu
Krishnadasa. He had left Bengal with her at the age of five (according to one tradition, Jahnava adopted him). Seeing what
deep feelings lived in the child, Jiva and the other Vaishnavas of Vraja named him Kanai Thakura. <!-- F80 -->

## Return to Gauda
Jahnava's journey home, like her journey to Vraja, became a pilgrimage to the places of the Lord's associates.[^23] First
came Kheturi: Narottama, Ramachandra Kaviraja and Raja Santosha received her there, rolling in the dust at her feet, and
she stayed with them three or four days. At Santosha's wish all the guests put on the new white garments he gave them —
Jahnava too put them on, and she herself gave garments to her maidservants. To Narottama and Ramachandra she told of Vraja
“from beginning to end” and asked for news of Gauda. Narottama could not say a word for tears, and Ramachandra answered:
“The Lord's dear ones, in this short time, have almost all gone into concealment.” — “Everywhere the Lord will bring
darkness,” said Jahnava, and wept (11.300–344). From Kheturi Narottama and Ramachandra went with her and escorted her by
way of Budhari and Ekachakra all the way to Yajigrama (“Bhakti-ratnakara,” 11.358–361, 11.397, 11.404, 11.686). <!-- F81 F82 F117 F144 -->

In Budhari, where Ramachandra Kaviraja and his brother Govinda lived, Vamshidasa Chakravarti and his family took charge
of her reception. To Vamshidasa's brother, Shyamadasa Chakravarti, Jahnava said with a smile: “What I shall ask of you,
you will have to give. It is very easy to obtain.” That night Shyamadasa dreamt that at her command he was giving his
daughter Hemalata to Bara Gangadasa, and in the morning he himself told her the dream.[^24] The wedding was held that same
day: Gangadasa, long since renounced, kept silent, but “he could not transgress Ishvari's command.” After the wedding
Jahnava entrusted to him the service of the Deity presented to her in Vrindavana — Shyama-raya; and when Gangadasa worried
how to feed Him, Shyama-raya told him in a dream: “Whatever is obtained at any time — feed Me with that,” and Jahnava
herself set the order of the offerings (11.359–395). <!-- F83 F102 F145 -->

In Ekachakra, Nityananda's birthplace, they met a decrepit brahmana with a staff, wandering among the places of Nitai's
pastimes. He showed them the ashvattha and the banyan where Nitai used to eat with the other children, and cried aloud:
“Where are You, my life, my Nitai?”; Jahnava and everyone wept and took the dust of his feet. He told her about His
parents — her father- and mother-in-law, Harai Pandita and Padmavati — and she wept in separation from them. The brahmana
showed them the Pandita's house — empty and in ruins — and they spent the night there chanting the holy names
(11.397–415, 604–624). <!-- F84 F146 -->

Jahnava could not sleep. “I never got to see my father-in-law and mother-in-law,” she thought, and in a dream Ekachakra
was revealed to her as a golden city where Harai Pandita and Padmavati, worshipped by the gods, tenderly love their son and
two daughters-in-law, Vasudha and Jahnava. And in a second dream she saw a jewelled throne in a flowering garden on a river
bank: on it Nityananda, with Vasudha and Jahnava to His left and right; maidservants hand them tambula, garlands and
sandal, and He exchanges these with His consorts (11.625–656).[^25] In the morning someone's voice said to her: “You are
always present here. Go quickly to Khardaha and fulfil the wish of your heart” (11.660). <!-- F147 -->

On the road from Ekachakra they came upon a drunken brahmana: he danced, laughed and wept, falling to the ground. Learning
who he was, Jahnava said: “May Lord Gaurahari make him intoxicated with prema in this way,” — and, says the
“Bhakti-ratnakara,” by her mercy he became qualified for bhakti (11.661–668). At Maudeshvara she had darshana of the Shiva
whom Nityananda had worshipped, and saw the place where Nityananda subdued Kundali (11.669–673). <!-- F148 F149 -->

At Katwa, in Kanthaka-nagara, she saw the place where the Lord accepted sannyasa, and wept. There too Yadunandana met her,
and he sent word to Yajigrama, and Srinivasa at once came to her with his companions; in the morning he asked her to come
to his house.[^26] In Yajigrama Srinivasa and his devotees received her with great love; Srinivasa put Narottama,
Ramachandra and other intimates in charge of all the arrangements for receiving the mahantas, and lodged Jahnava in his
own house. There the Acharya's wife came to meet her: with great humility she led her into the inner rooms, seated her on
a fine seat and washed her feet with fragrant water, while Jahnava, seeing her good nature, raised and embraced her and
grew fonder of her with every moment (“Bhakti-ratnakara,” 11.686–696). This wife was the first, Draupadi (13.139):
Srinivasa's second marriage, according to the “Bhakti-ratnakara,” came later. According to one of the biographies,
Srinivasa had just then married for the second time — Gauranga-priya, the daughter of Raghava Chakravarti — and, coming
home with his young wife, found Jahnava staying with him, took the dust of her feet, seated her in the place of honour and
told his wife to bow to her. <!-- F85 F86 F150 -->

Raghunandana came to Yajigrama from Shrikhanda, and Parameshvari Dasa told him the whole story of Jahnava's journey. In
the evening, at her command, Srinivasa read the “Srimad-Bhagavatam.” Jahnava said that from Shrikhanda she would hurry on
to Khardaha, and Srinivasa urged her: “Go quickly to Khardaha and manifest the Deity… Sri Gopinatha greatly desires this”
(11.701–717). Consoling Srinivasa's wife at parting, Jahnava went on to Shrikhanda. Raghunandana came out to meet her,
and his young son Kanai, “always overwhelmed by the qualities of Sri Gaurachandra,” asked his father about the mahantas
and bowed before them — and Jahnava showed him “boundless motherly affection.” In Gauranga's courtyard she had darshana of
Madana-gopala, whom Raghunandana as a child had fed a laddu; the night passed in sankirtana (11.726–750). <!-- F150 F151 -->

In Navadvipa hardly any of the Lord's associates remained: Ishana, the servant of Mahaprabhu's household, was by then a
very old man. He and the few others came out to meet her and “seemed to regain their life.” Entering the Lord's house,
Jahnava fainted in ecstasy, and all the devotees wept tears of love. She spent the night in the house of Srivasa, where
Mahaprabhu had once begun the sankirtana. Towards the end of the night Gaurasundara appeared to her in a dream — Gadadhara
on His left, Nityananda on His right, Advaita with Srivasa in front; Murari, Vakreshvara and Haridasa danced, Govinda,
Madhava, Vasu and Mukunda sang, and Brahma, Shiva and Shesha danced in the crowd. On waking she bowed many times to the
dhama of Navadvipa, disclosing the dream to no one, consoled Ishana with the promise that Srinivasa would soon come, and
after two days went on to Ambika-Kalna (11.751–769). <!-- F87 F88 F152 -->

The people of Ambika rejoiced. Jahnava remembered the departed Gauridasa and wept, and prayed at the feet of the Deities
of Gaura and Nityananda — and at Their command, says the “Bhakti-ratnakara,” went on her way (11.770–771).[^27] <!-- F89 F153 -->

She also visited the house of Uddharana Datta, who had already departed from this world, and remembered his life; there a
sankirtana festival went on for another night, and from there she returned home by boat to Khardaha. The Vaishnavas of
the banks of the Ganges came out to meet her, and people crowded to see her. Virachandra and Ganga bowed to her, and she
embraced them and, as mothers caress their children, tenderly breathed the fragrance of their heads: “What Ishvari's
motherly love was like, I do not know how to tell,” writes Narahari (11.784). Jahnava and Vasudha exchanged respectful
greetings. To Vasudha, Virabhadra and all who had gathered, Jahnava told of the holy places of Gauda- and Vraja-mandala,
and she kept Parameshvari Dasa with her as her servant. Then she gave her command to Nayana Bhaskara, and he began to
sculpt the Deity of Sri Radhika (11.788). The story of her pilgrimage is well known among Gaudiya Vaishnavas to this
day. <!-- F90 F91 F92 F153 F154 -->

## Disciples and Family
Of her first disciple, Virabhadra, we have already spoken. Jahnava also took care of his marriage. “Whatever villages
Ishvari went to — the good fortune of all those villages cannot be described,” says the “Bhakti-ratnakara” (13.248).
According to the same book (13.249–255), at her wish Yadunandana Acharya gave Virabhadra his two daughters, Shrimati and
Narayani. This took place near Rajabol-hata, in Jhamatpur, where Jahnava had come to the temple of one of her disciples.
Yadunandana attained great devotion by her mercy, and both daughters-in-law became her disciples and
received the mantra from her hands.[^28] Vasudha rejoiced in her daughters-in-law, and Ganga Devi in her brother's
wedding (13.259–261). <!-- F94 F157 Q9 -->

Her disciple in diksha was Nityananda Dasa, the author of the “Prema-vilasa.” She also asked for Ramachandra, or Ramai —
the grandson of Vamshivadana, son of Chaitanya Dasa: she initiated him, kept him in Khardaha and herself taught him
Vaishnava philosophy (the journal “Gaudiya,” 22.30–37). The whole family of Vamshivadana received her mercy, and his
descendants, sheltering under her protection, made Baghnapara their shripat; the “Pata-nirnaya” too names Baghnapara as the place of “Sri Vamshi [and] Ramai Thakura” (47). Jnanadasa of Kandra in Birbhum was also her
disciple, and with him his friend Manohara Dasa, a compiler of a collection of padas; both went with her to Kheturi. About
Madhavacharya of Jirat — Madhava Chattopadhyaya, husband of Ganga, Nityananda's daughter — opinions differ: some regard
him as a disciple of Nityananda, others of Jahnava, still others of Purushottama Thakura. <!-- F95 F96 F97 F98 F163 Q17 Q21 -->

Back in Khardaha, Jahnava fulfilled the request with which Gopinatha had appeared to her in a dream in Vrindavana. When
Nayana Bhaskara had sculpted the Deity of Sri Radhika, Jahnava, gazing at Her beauty, “became greatly overwhelmed” and sent
Her by boat to Vrindavana, giving seven hundred coins, garments and ornaments, with the words: “Quickly draw Your beloved
to Yourself” (“Bhakti-ratnakara” 13.69–73). Radha was accompanied by her dear servant Parameshvari Dasa (13.66–74;
“Narottama-vilasa” 9.81–84);[^29] at Katwa the boat was met by Srinivasa and King Vira Hambira. In Vrindavana, at
Gopinatha's command, the Radha who had been sent was placed on His left, while the former Radha “joyfully” took her place
on the right, and Parameshvari Dasa saw with his own eyes Gopinatha between His two beloveds. “Radhika, sent by Sri
Jahnava, is at the left side,” the Vrajavasis said, singing her virtues and praying to Gopinatha that they might see her
herself (13.229–236). Returning to Khardaha, Parameshvari told Vasudha and Jahnava everything, and Jahnava, overwhelmed
with love, told him to go without delay to the village of Tada-Atpur (Atpur) and manifest there the service of
Radha-Gopinatha; then she came there herself, and “the festival that took place there the fortunate saw” (13.243–247). <!-- F99 F100 F155 F156 -->

The title “kaviraja” — “king of poets” — was given to Ramachandra and his brother Govinda by Jiva Gosvami.[^30] <!-- F101 -->

## Departure
Having married her son, Jahnava went to Vrindavana again and saw Gopinatha in the middle, with Radha on His right and on
His left. Here the “Bhakti-ratnakara” quotes Vishvanatha Chakravarti's shlokas about Gopinatha “between the two Radhas,”
“the embodied treasure of Sri Jahnava's prema,” and explains: “That embodiment of love is subject to the prema of Sri
Jahnava” (13.270–272). She offered Radha-Gopinatha what she had brought from Gauda, herself cooked rice and many dishes and
fed Gopinatha with Radhika, offered Them prayers and had darshana of Govinda and Madana-mohana with Radhika. How she lived
in Vrindavana and how she went to Vraja again the author does not tell in detail, referring to the “Anuraga-valli” and
other books (13.266–282). <!-- F158 -->

About when and how Jahnava left this world the biographies say little; two of them are entirely silent. One names the
ninth day of the bright fortnight of Vaishakha as the day of her departure — the very day that other sources and our
calendar honour as her appearance day.[^31] The year has not been preserved. <!-- F103 -->

According to tradition, she went to Vraja for the last time together with Parameshvari Dasa, and he saw how, in the temple
of Gopinatha, Jahnava entered into Srimati Radhika — returning to her eternal abode, to Her service.[^32] <!-- F104 -->

## Deities and Places of Remembrance
Gopinatha is the Deity especially close to Jahnava. In Gauda-mandala, Abhirama Thakura manifested the service of
Gopinatha in Khanakula-Krishnanagara; in Kshetra-mandala, in Puri, Gadadhara Pandita serves Gopinatha together with
Radha and Ananga-manjari, that is, Jahnava; in Vrindavana, Madhu Pandita serves Gopinatha, the Deity of life's highest
goal, together with Radha and Jahnava — Ananga-manjari. <!-- F105 -->

In Ekachakra a Deity of Jahnava Mata was later installed to the right of Bankim-raya; the local sevaites explain this by
saying that Nityananda entered into Bankim-raya. <!-- F106 -->

She also had her own “gadi” — seat — at Sukhasagar. When the Ganges swallowed the temple of Sukhasagar, the Deities of
her gadi, together with the Deities of Purushottama (Nagara), which had earlier been brought there from Beledanga, were
moved to Sahebdanga-Berigram, and when that place too was ruined, to Chandure, in Nadia district (Chakdaha police
station), a mile from Simurali station and from Palpara. Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati also writes of this in his
“Anubhashya” (to CC, Adi 11.39). When one of the biographies was written, the Deities had
already been in Chandure for about fifty years. <!-- F107 F108 Q19 -->

The temple in Chandure was then a thatched hut with mud walls. There Jagannatha, Balarama and Subhadra are worshipped,
two pairs of Nitai-Gaura, Gopinatha, Jahnava Mata, Bala-Gopala, five pairs of Radha-Govinda, Revati and Balarama, and a
shalagrama. Some of the Deities were installed by Purushottama; the rest, it is said, came from Jahnava's gadi, which is
why the shripat is also called the “pat of Vasu and Jahnava.” Here the service passed from mahanta to mahanta: Gopala
Dasa, Ramakrishna, Bihari Dasa, Rama Dasa, another Gopala Dasa and at that time the aged Sitanatha Dasa. <!-- F109 -->

In the “Namamrita-samudra,” where Narahari Chakravarti's prayers call upon the Lord's associates, she is invoked
together with Vasudha:

“Vasudha and Jahnava Devi, be merciful to me: may the pastimes of your Nitai be revealed to me.” (“Namamrita-samudra” 7)
<!-- F116 -->

## Revati and Ananga-manjari
Who is Jahnava in the eternal pastimes of Krishna? Kavi Karnapura answers in the “Gaura-ganoddesha-dipika”:

“His two beloveds, Vasudha and Jahnavi — Sri Varuni and Revati, born in the line of Revata — are the daughters of the
great-souled Sri Suryadasa, radiant as the sun, who is Kakudmi.” (GGD 65)

Varuni and Revati are the consorts of Balarama; Kakudmi is Revati's father. Read in the order of the names, the verse makes
Vasudha Varuni and Jahnava Revati. But the next verse adds something more:

“Some even in the age of Kali declare Sri Vasudha-devi [to be Ananga-manjari], and some say Jahnavi is Ananga-manjari.
According to the former rule [of distinct manifestations], the saints hold both to be correct.” (GGD 66)

Ananga-manjari is the younger sister of Srimati Radhika, the daughter of Vrishabhanu. One of the biographies, following
this verse, says that in truth both sisters are manifestations of Ananga-manjari. Another remarks that both opinions —
Revati and Ananga-manjari — are true, for several eternal personalities may combine in one associate of the Lord.[^33] A
third calls Jahnava Ananga-manjari without qualification[^34] and explains: a shakti is non-different from her possessor, the
shaktiman, and so she, the shakti of Nityananda-Balarama, is the younger sister of Radha. <!-- F5 F6 F7 F161 Q1 -->

As Ananga-manjari she eternally abides to the right of Tota-Gopinatha in Puri and to the right of Gopinatha in
Vrindavana.[^35] Radha, Lalita and Vishakha love her boundlessly. Ananga-manjari is happy when she unites Radha with
Krishna, and Radha rejoices in just the same way in bringing her younger sister to Krishna.[^36] Gopinatha is the lord of
the life of both Gadadhara and Jahnava; Bhaktivinoda Thakura calls Nityananda-Gopinatha the treasure of her life. <!-- F8 F9 F10 -->

## A Guru in a Woman's Form
One who regards a spiritual master as an ordinary mortal because he has appeared as a woman, a householder, a child, a
youth or in a low family will not attain the good. The form in which the guru comes into the world does not diminish
his position: Hanuman appeared among monkeys, Prahlada and Bali among asuras, Haridasa among mlecchas, Vasudeva (the
future Madhvacharya) as a child, and Goda Devi, Jahnava, Gangamata, Hemalata Thakurani and others as women; and all of
them are gurus for the whole world. A guru is one who knows the truth about Krishna. So Mahaprabhu teaches in His
conversation with Ramananda Raya:

“Whether one is a *vipra*, a sannyasi or a shudra — whoever knows the truth of Krishna is a guru.”
(CC, Madhya 8.127) <!-- F12 I1 I2 -->

## Shelter at Her Feet
Narottama Dasa Thakura, in one of his songs of prayer, draws a ladder of mercy: without shelter at Jahnava's feet there is
no mercy of Nityananda; without Nityananda's mercy the desires for worldly enjoyment do not go; until they go, there is no
mercy of Rupa and Raghunatha; and without their mercy there is no attaining love for the youthful Couple of Vraja. The
Vaishnavas say the same: without the mercy of Jahnava Devi, the energy of Nityananda, no one crosses the ocean of birth
and death or obtains service to Nityananda and loving service to Those whom He worships — Gaura-Hari and
Radha-Krishna. <!-- I3 I4 -->

The great Vaishnava poet Krishna Dasa, in the song “jaya radhe, jaya krishna, jaya vrindavana,” having glorified the name
of Krishna, His abode and His associates, at the end begs for Jahnava's mercy: remembering her feet, the insignificant
Krishna Dasa sings the holy names. <!-- F112 Q16 -->

The “Bhakti-ratnakara” has also preserved a shloka of homage to her: “I worship Sri Jahnava Ishvari, the beloved of
Nityananda, the bestower of the jewel of prema-bhakti, the remover of the threefold misery” (11.52). Poets of later
centuries remember her too. “I worship Vasudha and Jahnava, the wives of the elder brother,” writes the author of the
“Rasika-mangala” (a late source; Purva-vibhaga, lahari 1, 22); Narahari Chakravarti in the “Gaura-charita-chintamani”
(a late source; 1.13) glorifies Nityananda as “the lord of the life of Vasudha and Jahnavi,” and in the prayer-song with
which the “Bhakti-ratnakara” ends he asks to sing of the qualities of Nitai-chand together with Vasudha and Jahnava. <!-- F130 F165 -->

Closer to our own time Bhaktivinoda Thakura prays to her. Already in the opening lines of the “Kalyana-kalpataru” he
asks all the Vaishnavas to show mercy and bring him to Jahnava's feet. In the “Dainyamayi Prarthana” — the prayer of
humility — he sings of a soul drowning in the ocean of worldly existence who begs for the boat of Jahnava's feet, the
shakti of Nityananda, the teacher of devotion to Krishna; and in the “Lalasamayi Prarthana” — the prayer of longing — he
yearns to serve Krishna in a perfected spiritual body at the feet of a sakhi and cries out: “Jahnava, be merciful to me
now!” In his songs of petition to Gopinatha — “Gopinatha, You are my hope,” “Gopinatha, I am Yours” — there is so much
anguish of separation and loving rasa that if even a particle of it sincerely enters the heart, the soul easily crosses
the ocean of worldly existence and becomes worthy of eternal service to Gopinatha. <!-- F113 Q11 Q12 Q13 Q15 I5 -->

Vrindavana Dasa Thakura, the Vyasa of Chaitanya's pastimes, boldly proclaimed the glory of the Lord of his Lord:

“Sri Gaurasundara is the Lord of my Master. I constantly keep this conviction in my heart.” (CB, Madhya 17.117)

He repeats the same thought elsewhere (CB, Madhya 28.191). And Bhaktivinoda, the Vyasa of our time, prays with humility,
placing his hope in Nityananda-Gopinatha — the treasure of Jahnava's life. <!-- I6 Q14 -->

Jahnava is glorified also in the “Sri Jahnava-ashtaka” — eight shlokas attributed to Jiva Gosvami; it is listed in the
catalogue of the Government Oriental Manuscripts Library in Madras. The ashtaka glorifies the daughter of Surya Dasa
Sarakhela, the shakti of Nityananda; the first shloka addresses her as Ananga-manjari, the younger sister of Radhika in
Vraja, and the last promises that whoever constantly recites these eight verses will become a bee at the lotus feet of
Sri Chaitanya. <!-- F111 Q18 -->
