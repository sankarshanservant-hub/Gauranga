# Jahnava Devi
*Джахнава-деви · @jahnava*

## The Shakti of Nityananda
Jahnava Devi is the eternal energy, the shakti, of Nityananda Prabhu, and His consort in Gaura-lila. She is called
Jahnava Thakurani, Jahnava Mata — “Mother Jahnava,” Jahnava Ishvari, that is, the Queen, and Nityanandeshvari; in the
“Gaura-ganoddesha-dipika” her name is Jahnavi. Together with her elder sister Vasudha she is a transcendental (aprakrita)
shakti of Nityananda; one of the biographies calls her “Nityananda-svarupini,” His second self, and another says still
more boldly: Jahnava Devi is Nityananda Prabhu Himself.[^1] <!-- F1 F2 F3 -->

She is also called “Bhakta-svarupini” — devotion personified, a treasury of prema-bhakti; in her divine majesty
(aishvarya) and divine sweetness (madhurya) are equally wonderful. Like Nityananda, she is a jagad-guru, a teacher of love
for Krishna to the whole world. When Gaurasundara and Nityananda withdrew Their pastimes, it was she who was honoured as
guru in all three holy lands — in Gauda-mandala, in Kshetra-mandala, that is, in Puri, and in Vraja-mandala. <!-- F4 F11 F41 -->

## Daughter of Surya Dasa
Jahnava's father was Surya Dasa Sarakhela, also known as Suryadasa Pandita and Surya Pandita. He was dear to
Sri Chaitanya, and Nityananda and Gauranga too regarded him as Their intimate devotee. Her mother's name was Bhadravati;
the biographies call her most fortunate — it fell to her to become the mother of two shaktis of Nityananda. The elder
daughter was Vasudha, the younger Jahnava. <!-- F13 F14 F15 -->

Jahnava's grandfather was Kamsari Mishra, a brahmana of the Vatsa gotra from the Ghoshal family. He and his wife
Kamala Devi had six sons, and Surya Dasa was the third. His elder brothers were Damodara and Jagannatha, his younger
ones Gauridasa Pandita, Krishnadasa Sarakhela and Nrisimha Chaitanya. Gauridasa, Surya Dasa's younger brother, moved with
his permission from Shaligrama to Ambika on the Ganges (“Bhakti-ratnakara” 7.330–331); his niece would one day weep at
his samadhi in Vrindavana. Another uncle, Krishnadasa Sarakhela, was later with her at the festival in Kheturi.[^2] <!-- F16 F17 F18 F19 Q3 -->

Surya Dasa lived in Shaligrama, not far from Navadvipa, near the present Muragachha station of the Eastern Railway; his
shripat is there.[^3] He held a high post under the ruler of Gauda, from which he received the title “Sarakhela” —
“chief,” “commander” — and great wealth (“Bhakti-ratnakara” 12.3875–3878).[^4] Yet it was not this that made him
renowned: he and his brothers were pure in character and spotless in people's eyes, and the virtues of Surya Dasa himself,
a large-hearted man, were beyond counting. Above all there was his faith in Nityananda. Krishnadasa Kaviraja, listing the
branches of Nityananda's tree, names both brothers together:

“Suryadasa Sarakhela and his brother Krishnadasa had unshakable faith in Nityananda. They were full of love for God.”
(CC, Adi 11.25) <!-- F20 F21 F22 Q4 -->

Jahnava appeared in the world around 1409 of the Shaka era (≈ 1487) in Ambika-Kalna — so says one of the biographies;
the others give neither the year nor the place of her birth.[^5] The day of her appearance is the ninth day of the bright
fortnight of Vaishakha, “Gaura-navami”; on that same day the appearance of Sita Devi, the eternal consort of Ramachandra,
is also celebrated. Our calendar marks it in the same way: on the Gaura-navami of the month of Madhusudana it remembers
the appearance of Nityananda's shakti Sri Jahnava Devi and of Rama's shakti Sri Sita Devi.[^6] <!-- F23 F24 F25 -->

## Surya Dasa's Dream
When his daughters came of age, Surya Dasa began to wonder to whom he should give them. One night, having sat up late with
these thoughts, he fell asleep and saw a dream: he was giving both his daughters to Nityananda. Nityananda appeared to him
as Balarama Himself, and Vasudha and Jahnava stood at His left and right in the forms of Revati and Varuni, wondrously
adorned, lighting up all ten directions with their radiance. <!-- F26 F28 -->

Meanwhile a certain Krishnadasa of Borgachhi near Shaligrama, son of Harihara, a kayastha and a devotee of Nityananda,
took it upon himself to find a wife for Nityananda. And an old brahmana of Shaligrama, seeing how anxious Surya Dasa was,
revealed to him who Nityananda was: the son of Harai Pandita and Padmavati of Ekachakra — in Krishna-lila, Vasudeva and
Rohini — Balarama Himself, who had wandered through all the holy places, and the eternal husband of his daughters. <!-- F27 -->

In the morning Surya Dasa told his dream to a brahmana friend: “Until He accepts them, I shall have no peace.” He sent his
friend to Navadvipa, to Srivasa, in whose house Nityananda was then staying. Nityananda sent word that He would fulfil
Surya Dasa's wish, and Advaita Acharya, hearing of it, said, “Let it happen soon.” The brahmana returned to Shaligrama
with the good news. <!-- F29 -->

Krishnadasa, son of Harihara (one of the biographies calls Harihara a king and the village Boroganchhi), took upon himself
a splendid wedding in his own house and prevailed on Nityananda to come.[^7] Srivasa, Advaita, Chandrashekhara, Murari
Gupta and many others gathered with sankirtana; devotees from Shaligrama came too. Surya Dasa ran out to meet Nityananda,
fell at His feet and could not utter a word for tears. Nityananda, laughing with joy, embraced him, and Gauridasa,
watching his brother, rejoiced as well. <!-- F30 -->

When Surya Dasa offered his daughters to Nityananda, He showed him while awake what he had earlier seen in his dream:
Vasudha and Jahnava shone like gold and kunkuma, in jewelled ornaments and many-coloured garments — Varuni and Revati on
either side of Balarama. Surya Dasa fainted with rapture (“Bhakti-ratnakara” 12.3908–3910).[^8] Nityananda granted him
this vision of His majesty to delight His devotee. <!-- F31 I7 -->

On the eve of the wedding the adhivasa was performed in the house of Krishnadasa Sarakhela in Shaligrama, with the
brahmanas of Borgachhi and Shaligrama present.[^9] Then Surya Dasa, according to rite and custom, gave both his daughters
to Nityananda (“Bhakti-ratnakara” 12.3983). “Fortunate he was both by scripture and in the eyes of the world,” says the
“Bhakti-ratnakara,” “for he gave his daughters to the Supreme Lord.” <!-- F32 F33 Q2 -->

The “Bhakti-ratnakara” stresses that the wedding was performed “according to human custom.” This does not mean that
Nityananda entered into a worldly union. Every vishnu-tattva has three energies — Shri, Bhu and Nila (Lila) — and
Nityananda, being vishnu-tattva, possesses them too; Vasudha and Jahnava are His eternal shaktis, and the marriage only
disclosed to people what exists eternally. <!-- F34 -->

## The Newly-Weds
After the wedding Nityananda stayed for some time in Shaligrama and then came with both wives to Navadvipa, to Shachi
Devi. Seeing Vasudha and Jahnava, Shachi embraced them again and again, sat them on her lap and pinched their cheeks
affectionately; all the mistresses of the Vaishnava households of Navadvipa surrounded the young wives with love as well.
At Shachi's request Nityananda stayed on in Navadvipa. <!-- F35 F36 -->

On her advice He took His wives to Shantipura, to Advaita. Sita Thakurani received them with delight and embraced them as
tenderly as Shachi had. They stayed with Advaita until Uddharana Datta invited Nityananda to Saptagrama, where a great
sankirtana festival went on for several days; from there the way led to Borograma, and Nityananda wandered, leading
kirtana. At last He settled for good in Khardaha on the Ganges.[^10] <!-- F37 -->

In Khardaha Nityananda had a house where Vasudha and Jahnava lived; Parameshvari Dasa served them. Jahnava had no
children. Vasudha bore a son, Virabhadra (Virachandra), and a daughter, Ganga Devi; Virabhadra is revered as an
incarnation of Kshirodakashayi Vishnu, and Ganga as an incarnation of the holy river Ganges. <!-- F38 F39 -->

## Mother of a New Generation
After Nityananda's departure the work of an acharya fell to Jahnava. Jiva Gosvami and the other Gaudiya acharyas gave her
the place of jagad-guru, and so did Srinivasa Acharya, Narottama Thakura, Shyamananda and the other acharyas of the next
generation. The sankirtana festivals that Srinivasa, Narottama and their companions carried everywhere took place by her
mercy and under her guidance — as also under the guidance of Vishnupriya Devi. When Nityananda, Advaita, Srivasa and
many other associates of Mahaprabhu had left the world, Jahnava wished to flood the world with the nectar of sankirtana
even more abundantly than before. <!-- F40 F42 F43 -->

Before setting out for Vrindavana, Srinivasa came to Khardaha. Parameshvari Dasa led him to Vasudha, Jahnava and
Virachandra; Srinivasa prostrated himself before them in tears, and Jahnava placed her feet on his head, blessing him
with the dust of her feet. He stayed there several days, received the mercy of both mothers and the blessing of
Virabhadra, and Jahnava, together with other associates of the Lord, seeing his devotion, told him to go to Vrindavana;
from Khardaha he went on to Khanakula. <!-- F44 -->

Narottama too visited Khardaha — after his time in Vrindavana. Parameshvari Dasa brought him into the inner apartments
of the house, to the feet of Jahnava and Vasudha. Understanding that he had already received the mercy of Lokanatha and
Jiva, they bestowed their own generously upon him; here the “Bhakti-ratnakara” calls Vasudha the knower of all truth and
Jahnava the supreme queen. For four days Narottama spoke with them about Krishna, and then went on to
Khanakula-Krishnanagara. <!-- F45 -->

Her stepson, Vasudha's son Virabhadra, also became her disciple in diksha. According to the “Prema-vilasa,” he once saw
her in a four-armed form — and his heart was changed: he resolved to take initiation from none other than her, his aunt
and stepmother. <!-- F93 -->

## The Road to Kheturi
Narottama Thakura planned a great festival in Kheturi on Phalguna-purnima, the appearance day of Mahaprabhu. The host was
Raja Santosha Datta, the son of Narottama's brother and his disciple; all three acharyas came together — Narottama,
Shyamananda and Srinivasa. The “Bhakti-ratnakara” tells how the associates of Mahaprabhu in Bengal rejoiced in Narottama.
Jahnava rejoiced too — the daughter of Surya Pandita, the consort of Nityananda, honoured by the whole world, who
generously gives away the jewels of prema: his very name filled her with joy, and his renunciation and extraordinary love
for Krishna gladdened her still more. And she graciously set out for Kheturi so that all might see her. <!-- F46 F50 Q5 -->

A large company of Nityananda's associates went with her from Khardaha: Parameshvari Dasa, her uncle Krishnadasa
Sarakhela, Madhava Acharya — Nityananda's son-in-law, Raghupati Vaidya, Minaketana Ramadasa, Murari Chaitanya Dasa,
Jnanadasa, Mahidhara, Sri Shankara, Kamalakara Pippalai, Gauranga Dasa, Nakadi, Krishna Dasa, Damodara, Balarama Dasa,
Sri Mukunda and Vrindavana Dasa Thakura, as well as Srivasa's brothers Shripati and Shrinidhi.[^11] The
“Bhakti-ratnakara” (10.375–382) names separately among her companions Parameshvari Dasa, Kamalakara Pippalai, and
Bhagavan Acharya with his son Raghunatha Acharya; of Minaketana Ramadasa it says that the mere sight of such Vaishnavas
sanctifies the three worlds. Her disciples Jnanadasa and Manohara Dasa went with her, and, according to the
“Prema-vilasa,” little Kanu Thakura as well. At Katwa she was joined by Kala Krishnadasa of Akai-hata
(“Bhakti-ratnakara” 10.409). <!-- F51 F52 Q6 -->

First the travellers came to Ambika-Kalna, to the house of Gauridasa Pandita. His disciple Hridaya Chaitanya welcomed
everyone warmly, and Jahnava cooked an offering for the Deities of Gaura and Nityananda with her own hands. After a night
of festival they went on to Navadvipa. <!-- F53 -->

In Navadvipa the sorrow of separation awaited her. In Mahaprabhu's house she did not find Shachi Devi — she was no
longer in this world — and she grieved for her. Shripati and Shrinidhi took her to their home, but neither Srivasa nor
Malini was there, and Jahnava wept. In the morning they went on to Shantipura. Learning that Advaita and Sita Thakurani
had already departed, she grieved again; Advaita's sons Achyutananda and Gopala received her and all the Vaishnavas with
due honour. <!-- F54 F55 -->

The way then led to Teliya-Budhari: there Govinda Kaviraja, the brother of Ramachandra Kaviraja, met her reverently and
worshipped her.[^12] After a night's rest all set out for Kheturi. On the far bank of the Padma, Raja Santosha Datta had
prepared a huge boat for the crossing and, beyond the river, palanquins, carts, lodgings, prasada and decorations; he
himself went far out to meet Jahnava with flowers, garlands and gifts. Srinivasa and Narottama came out to meet her too;
each guest was given a separate house and servants. <!-- F56 F57 -->

## The Festival at Kheturi
Singing sankirtana, the company led by Jahnava entered Kheturi. Srinivasa, Narottama and Shyamananda prostrated
themselves before her in the very dust of the road. Handsome houses had been built in advance for her companions. <!-- F58 -->

On the eve of Mahaprabhu's appearance day the last preparations went forward at her command. In the evening, before the
sankirtana began, Narottama offered her sandal paste and garlands and honoured her; Srinivasa too offered her
sanctified sandal and garlands. At her word Nrisimha Chaitanya Dasa adorned Srinivasa, Shyamananda and Narottama with
garlands and sandal. Jahnava sat on a raised seat at the front of the hall. Raghunandana of Shrikhanda led the kirtana
first, and then, at the bidding of Jahnava and Achyutananda, Narottama began to sing; Gauranga Dasa, Gokula Dasa and
Vallabha Dasa responded, and Devi Dasa played the mridanga. In the midst of the assembly Jahnava, the shakti of
Nityananda, shone with beauty, and the very sight of her and of the devotees, together with the kirtana, purified even
the godless and the sinful. They sang until midnight. <!-- F59 F60 F61 -->

Narottama manifested six Deities in Kheturi — Gauranga, Vallabhikanta, Vrajamohana, Krishna, Radhakanta and Radharamana.
They were installed at Jahnava's direction: the whole festival took place under her guidance, and at her word Srinivasa
performed the abhisheka and archana of the Deities — the Deities themselves Narottama had manifested at the command of
his guru Lokanatha. The next day Srinivasa bathed all six Deities, and at the request of Jahnava and the devotees
Narottama sang again — and in the kirtana Gauranga Himself appeared with Nityananda and His eternal associates. After the
sankirtana Jahnava offered red abir powder to the Deities, and at her word the devotees began to shower one another with
it, as at Holi. At dusk Srinivasa performed the abhisheka in honour of Gauranga's appearance and sang and danced with the
devotees all night. <!-- F47 F48 F62 F63 -->

Jahnava herself cooked for the Deities. According to the “Bhakti-ratnakara” (10.686–687), early in the morning, full of
joy, she bathed, performed her morning rites and meditation on the mantra, and eagerly prepared countless vegetable
dishes; skilled in the art of cooking, she quickly made sweets, cakes and drinks as well, and it was she who directed the
whole kitchen. Srinivasa offered the bhoga to the Deities.[^13] Then Jahnava distributed prasada to the mahantas with her
own hands, and when they had eaten, she asked Narottama, Srinivasa and Shyamananda to eat — and took prasada last of all
herself. Outside the hall, meanwhile, Raja Santosha was feeding thousands of people. <!-- F64 F65 -->

The great acharyas — Narottama, Srinivasa and others, themselves teachers of the whole world — felt that they had
attained the goal of life by hearing talks about Hari from her lips. And the presence of Jahnava, Raghunandana,
Achyutananda, Vrindavana Dasa and other eternal associates of the Lord made happy all who came to the festival. <!-- F49 F68 -->

A controversy is also connected with the Kheturi festival. The “Prema-vilasa” (19th vilasa) claims that Srinivasa
worshipped Gauranga there with the mantra of Radha-Krishna, that the Gosvamis prescribed the worship of Gaura with the
ten-syllable Gopala mantra, that Jahnava approved of this, and that on Phalguna-purnima, Gaura's appearance day, all
took mahaprasada — that is, broke the fast. One of the biographies regards this as an interpolation by a hand hostile to
the Gaura-mantra and to the observance of Gaura's appearance day. Nothing of the kind is found in the
“Bhakti-ratnakara” (10th wave): there each of the six Deities is honoured separately, and Narottama calls each by name
(10.492, 495–496). For the form, name, qualities and pastimes of the Lord are varied, and therefore the worship of each
Deity requires its own mantras — so teach scripture and the mahajanas. <!-- F66 Q8 Q10 I8 -->

When the time came to part, Raja Santosha in tears presented everyone with gold, cloth and vessels, and Jahnava with her
company set out for Vrindavana. <!-- F67 -->

## In Vraja
Her journey to Vraja is described in the eleventh wave of the “Bhakti-ratnakara.” On the way, in a certain wealthy
village, she freed the local godless men and evildoers from sin and bestowed on them love for Krishna. Through Prayaga and
Kashi she came to Mathura, visited the birthplace of Krishna, bathed at Vishrama-ghata and entered Vrindavana. <!-- F69 F70 F71 Q7 -->

Some of the Vaishnavas of Vraja met her already in Mathura, and in Vrindavana the heads of the community came to welcome
her. Parameshvari Dasa, as the “Bhakti-ratnakara” relates, names them: Gopala Bhatta, Bhugarbha, Lokanatha, Krishnadasa
Brahmachari, Krishna Pandita, Madhu Pandita and Jiva; Jiva introduced each of them to her by name. The Gosvamis fell at
her feet, and she in turn bowed before them. Seeing the samadhi of her uncle Gauridasa Pandita in Vrindavana, Jahnava
wept uncontrollably (“Bhakti-ratnakara” 11.259). <!-- F72 F73 -->

With the Vaishnavas she visited the principal temples — of Madana-mohana, Govinda and Gopinatha — and the holy places of
Vrindavana; the Gosvamis saw to it that she was as well provided for as possible. Then she came to Govardhana, Radha-kunda
and Shyama-kunda, and in every place of the pastimes the feelings connected with what had happened there awoke in her. <!-- F74 F75 -->

At Radha-kunda she met Raghunatha Dasa Gosvami, wasted by austerity and ceaselessly chanting the holy names, and blessed
him. She spent three days at Radha-kunda in bhajana. Once, sitting on the bank, she heard Krishna's flute and saw Him
Himself, and waves of divine love overwhelmed her. The ghata where she bathed and where Krishna appeared to her is called
Jahnava-ghata to this day. <!-- F76 F77 F110 -->

Together with the Vaishnavas she went round Vraja-mandala and listened to Jiva Gosvami explaining the
“Brihad-bhagavatamrita.” Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati, in his “Anubhashya” to the “Chaitanya-charitamrita”
(Adi 10.85), also mentions that Jahnava and other devotees came to Vrindavana during Jiva's lifetime. <!-- F78 F79 Q19 -->

With her in Vrindavana was the boy Kanu, son of Purushottama Thakura — the future Kanu Thakura, also called Shishu
Krishnadasa. He had left Bengal with her at the age of five (according to one tradition, Jahnava adopted him). Seeing what
deep feelings lived in the child, Jiva and the other Vaishnavas of Vraja named him Kanai Thakura. <!-- F80 -->

## Return to Gauda
Jahnava's journey home, like her journey to Vraja, became a pilgrimage to the places of the Lord's associates.[^14] First
came Kheturi: Narottama and Ramachandra Kaviraja received her there, and she stayed with them three or four days. Then
Budhari, the home of Ramachandra Kaviraja in the present district of Murshidabad. <!-- F81 F82 -->

In Budhari lived Shyamadasa Chakravarti, the brother of Vamshidasa. Jahnava proposed that his daughter Hemalata be given
in marriage to Baru Gangadasa, and Shyamadasa, obedient to her word, gave his daughter. After the wedding Jahnava
entrusted Gangadasa with the service of the Deity Shyamasundara. <!-- F83 F102 -->

In Ekachakra, Nityananda's birthplace, she was told about His parents — her father- and mother-in-law, Harai Pandita and
Padmavati — and she wept in separation from them. A local brahmana showed her the places of Nityananda's childhood
pastimes, and she spent the night in the deserted house of His parents, chanting the holy names. <!-- F84 -->

At Katwa, in Kanthaka-nagara, she saw the place where the Lord accepted sannyasa, and wept. There too she met Yadunandana
Acharya, and with Srinivasa she went on to Yajigrama.[^15] In Yajigrama Srinivasa and his devotees received her with
great love; Srinivasa brought her into his house and worshipped her. He had just married for the second time — Gauranga-priya,
the daughter of Raghava Chakravarti — and brought his young wife home just when Jahnava, come from Vrindavana, was
staying with him. Srinivasa took the dust of her feet, seated her in the place of honour and told his wife to bow to
her; Jahnava, seeing the bride's gentle nature and beauty, embraced her affectionately. After staying several days she
went on. <!-- F85 F86 -->

In Navadvipa Ishana, the servant of Mahaprabhu's household, was by then a very old man. Entering the Lord's house,
Jahnava fainted in ecstasy, and all the devotees wept tears of love. She spent the night in the courtyard of Srivasa,
where Mahaprabhu had once begun the sankirtana; maha-sankirtana resounded there until morning, and in a dream
Gaurasundara and His devotees appeared to her in various pastimes. In the morning, having bowed many times to the dhama of
Navadvipa, she went on to Ambika-Kalna. <!-- F87 F88 -->

The people of Ambika rejoiced. Jahnava remembered the departed Gauridasa and wept, and prayed at the feet of the Deities
of Gaura and Nityananda — and during the kirtana Gaura and Nityananda Themselves appeared there. At night, as on the
previous occasion, she cooked many dishes, offered them to the Deities and distributed the prasada; in a dream Gauridasa
appeared to her together with Gaura and Nityananda, and They blessed her. <!-- F89 -->

She also visited the house of Uddharana Datta, who was no longer living; there a sankirtana festival went on for another
night, and from there she returned home by boat to Khardaha. She was received with sankirtana, and people crowded to see
her. Virachandra and Ganga bowed to her, and she embraced them and, as mothers caress their children, tenderly breathed
the fragrance of their heads; Jahnava and Vasudha exchanged respectful greetings. To Vasudha, Virabhadra and all who had
gathered, Jahnava told of the holy places of Gauda- and Vraja-mandala, and she kept Parameshvari Dasa with her as her
servant. The story of her pilgrimage is well known among Gaudiya Vaishnavas to this day. <!-- F90 F91 F92 -->

## Disciples and Family
Of her first disciple, Virabhadra, we have already spoken. Jahnava also took care of his marriage. According to the
“Bhakti-ratnakara” (13.251–253), at her wish Yadunandana Acharya gave Virabhadra his two daughters, Shrimati and
Narayani. This took place near Rajabol-hata, in Jhamatpur, where Jahnava had come to the temple of one of her disciples.
Yadunandana and his wife Lakshmi attained great devotion by her mercy, and both daughters-in-law became her disciples and
received the mantra from her hands.[^16] <!-- F94 Q9 -->

Her disciple in diksha was Nityananda Dasa, the author of the “Prema-vilasa.” She also asked for Ramachandra, or Ramai —
the grandson of Vamshivadana, son of Chaitanya Dasa: she initiated him, kept him in Khardaha and herself taught him
Vaishnava philosophy (the journal “Gaudiya,” 22.30–37). The whole family of Vamshivadana received her mercy, and his
descendants, sheltering under her protection, made Baghnapara their shripat. Jnanadasa of Kandra in Birbhum was also her
disciple, and with him his friend Manohara Dasa, a compiler of a collection of padas; both went with her to Kheturi. About
Madhavacharya of Jirat — Madhava Chattopadhyaya, husband of Ganga, Nityananda's daughter — opinions differ: some regard
him as a disciple of Nityananda, others of Jahnava, still others of Purushottama Thakura. <!-- F95 F96 F97 F98 Q17 -->

Parameshvari Dasa, her dear servant, by her mercy saw Gopinatha together with Radha in Vrindavana. Returning to Khardaha,
he told Jahnava and Vasudha about it, and Jahnava, overwhelmed with love, told him to go without delay to Antpur (Atpur)
and install Radha-Gopinatha there. She also had a Deity of Radha made and sent it to Vrindavana, to Sri Govindadeva; the
same Parameshvari Dasa accompanied Radha.[^17] <!-- F99 F100 -->

The songs of Govinda Kaviraja, Ramachandra's brother, so delighted Jiva Gosvami, Srinivasa Acharya and Jahnava Mata that
together they gave him the title “kaviraja” — “king of poets.”[^18] <!-- F101 -->

## Departure
About when and how Jahnava left this world the biographies say little; two of them are entirely silent. One names the
ninth day of the bright fortnight of Vaishakha as the day of her departure — the very day that other sources and our
calendar honour as her appearance day.[^19] The year has not been preserved. <!-- F103 -->

According to tradition, she went to Vraja for the last time together with Parameshvari Dasa, and he saw how, in the temple
of Gopinatha, Jahnava entered the body of Srimati Radhika — returning to her eternal abode, to Her service.[^20] <!-- F104 -->

## Deities and Places of Remembrance
Gopinatha is the Deity especially close to Jahnava. In Gauda-mandala, Abhirama Thakura manifested the service of
Gopinatha in Khanakula-Krishnanagara; in Kshetra-mandala, in Puri, Gadadhara Pandita serves Gopinatha together with
Radha and Ananga-manjari, that is, Jahnava; in Vrindavana, Madhu Pandita serves Gopinatha, the Deity of life's highest
goal, together with Radha and Jahnava — Ananga-manjari. <!-- F105 -->

In Ekachakra a Deity of Jahnava Mata was later installed to the right of Bankim-raya; the local sevaites explain this by
saying that Nityananda entered into Bankim-raya. <!-- F106 -->

She also had her own “gadi” — seat — at Sukhasagar. When the Ganges swallowed the temple of Sukhasagar, the Deities of
her gadi, together with the Deities of Purushottama (Nagara), which had earlier been brought there from Beledanga, were
moved to Sahebdanga-Berigram, and when that place too was ruined, to Chandure, in Nadia district (Chakdaha police
station), a mile from Simurali station and from Palpara. Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati also writes of this in his
“Anubhashya” (to CC, Adi 11.39). The Deities have now been in Chandure for about fifty years. <!-- F107 F108 Q19 -->

The present temple in Chandure is a thatched hut with mud walls. There Jagannatha, Balarama and Subhadra are worshipped,
two pairs of Nitai-Gaura, Gopinatha, Jahnava Mata, Bala-Gopala, five pairs of Radha-Govinda, Revati and Balarama, and a
shalagrama. Some of the Deities were installed by Purushottama; the rest, it is said, came from Jahnava's gadi, which is
why the shripat is also called the “pat of Vasu and Jahnava.” Here the service passed from mahanta to mahanta: Gopala
Dasa, Ramakrishna, Bihari Dasa, Rama Dasa, another Gopala Dasa and now the aged Sitanatha Dasa. <!-- F109 -->

## Revati and Ananga-manjari
Who is Jahnava in the eternal pastimes of Krishna? Kavi-karnapura answers in the “Gaura-ganoddesha-dipika”:

“His two beloveds, Vasudha and Jahnavi — Sri Varuni and Revati, born in the line of Revata — are the daughters of the
great-souled Sri Suryadasa, radiant as the sun, who is Kakudmi.” (GGD 65)

Varuni and Revati are the consorts of Balarama; Kakudmi is Revati's father. Read in the order of the names, the verse makes
Vasudha Varuni and Jahnava Revati. But the next verse adds something more:

“Some even in the age of Kali declare Sri Vasudha-devi [to be Ananga-manjari], and some say Jahnavi is Ananga-manjari.
According to the former rule [of distinct manifestations], the saints hold both to be correct.” (GGD 66)

Ananga-manjari is the younger sister of Srimati Radhika, the daughter of Vrishabhanu. One of the biographies, following
this verse, says that in truth both sisters are manifestations of Ananga-manjari. Another remarks that both opinions —
Revati and Ananga-manjari — are true, for several eternal personalities may combine in one associate of the Lord.[^21] A
third calls Jahnava Ananga-manjari without qualification and explains: a shakti is non-different from her possessor, the
shaktiman, and so she, the shakti of Nityananda-Balarama, is the younger sister of Radha. <!-- F5 F6 F7 Q1 -->

As Ananga-manjari she eternally abides to the right of Tota-Gopinatha in Puri and to the right of Gopinatha in
Vrindavana.[^22] Radha, Lalita and Vishakha love her boundlessly. Ananga-manjari is happy when she unites Radha with
Krishna, and Radha rejoices in just the same way in bringing her younger sister to Krishna.[^23] Gopinatha is the lord of
the life of both Gadadhara and Jahnava; Bhaktivinoda Thakura calls Nityananda-Gopinatha the treasure of her life. <!-- F8 F9 F10 -->

## A Guru in a Woman's Form
One who regards a spiritual master as an ordinary mortal because he has appeared as a woman, a householder, a child, a
youth or in a low family will not attain the good. The form in which the guru comes into the world does not diminish
his position: Hanuman appeared among monkeys, Prahlada and Bali among asuras, Haridasa among mlecchas, Vasudeva (the
future Madhvacharya) as a child, and Goda Devi, Jahnava, Gangamata, Hemalata Thakurani and others as women; and all of
them are gurus for the whole world. A guru is one who knows the truth about Krishna. So Mahaprabhu teaches in His
conversation with Ramananda Raya:

“If a person has realized the truth about Krishna, he is a true guru, whether he be a brahmana, a sannyasi or a shudra.”
(CC, Madhya 8.127) <!-- F12 I1 I2 -->

## Shelter at Her Feet
Narottama Dasa Thakura, in one of his songs of prayer, draws a ladder of mercy: without shelter at Jahnava's feet there is
no mercy of Nityananda; without Nityananda's mercy the desires for worldly enjoyment do not go; until they go, there is no
mercy of Rupa and Raghunatha; and without their mercy there is no attaining love for the youthful Couple of Vraja. The
Vaishnavas say the same: without the mercy of Jahnava Devi, the energy of Nityananda, no one crosses the ocean of birth
and death or obtains service to Nityananda and loving service to Those whom He worships — Gaura-Hari and
Radha-Krishna. <!-- I3 I4 -->

The great Vaishnava poet Krishna Dasa, in the song “jaya radhe, jaya krishna, jaya vrindavana,” having glorified the name
of Krishna, His abode and His associates, at the end begs for Jahnava's mercy: remembering her feet, the insignificant
Krishna Dasa sings the holy names. <!-- F112 Q16 -->

Closer to our own time Bhaktivinoda Thakura prays to her. Already in the opening lines of the “Kalyana-kalpataru” he
asks all the Vaishnavas to show mercy and bring him to Jahnava's feet. In the “Dainyamayi Prarthana” — the prayer of
humility — he sings of a soul drowning in the ocean of worldly existence who begs for the boat of Jahnava's feet, the
shakti of Nityananda, the teacher of devotion to Krishna; and in the “Lalasamayi Prarthana” — the prayer of longing — he
yearns to serve Krishna in a perfected spiritual body at the feet of a sakhi and cries out: “Jahnava, be merciful to me
now!” In his songs of petition to Gopinatha — “Gopinatha, You are my hope,” “Gopinatha, I am Yours” — there is so much
anguish of separation and loving rasa that if even a particle of it sincerely enters the heart, the soul easily crosses
the ocean of worldly existence and becomes worthy of eternal service to Gopinatha. <!-- F113 Q11 Q12 Q13 Q15 I5 -->

Vrindavana Dasa Thakura, the Vyasa of Chaitanya's pastimes, boldly proclaimed the glory of the Lord of his Lord:

“Sri Gaurasundara is the Lord of my Master. I constantly keep this conviction in my heart.” (CB, Madhya 17.117)

He repeats the same thought elsewhere (CB, Madhya 28.191). And Bhaktivinoda, the Vyasa of our time, prays with humility,
placing his hope in Nityananda-Gopinatha — the treasure of Jahnava's life. <!-- I6 Q14 -->

Jahnava is glorified also in the “Sri Jahnava-ashtaka” — eight shlokas attributed to Jiva Gosvami; it is listed in the
catalogue of the Government Oriental Manuscripts Library in Madras. The ashtaka glorifies the daughter of Surya Dasa
Sarakhela, the shakti of Nityananda; the first shloka addresses her as Ananga-manjari, the younger sister of Radhika in
Vraja, and the last promises that whoever constantly recites these eight verses will become a bee at the lotus feet of
Sri Chaitanya. <!-- F111 Q18 -->
