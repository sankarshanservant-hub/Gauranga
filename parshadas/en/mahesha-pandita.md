# Mahesha Pandita
*Махеша Пандит · @mahesha-pandita*

## Mahabahu, Krishna's Friend
Mahesha Pandita is one of the twelve cowherd boys, the gopalas, who came into Gaura's pastimes together with
Nityananda. The biographies call him Sri Mahesha Pandita; Krishnadasa Kaviraja calls him Mahesha Pandita and simply
Mahesha; Vrindavana Dasa, Mahesha Pandita; Narahari Chakravarti, Pandita Mahesha and Mahesha. Who he is in Krishna's
eternal pastimes, Kavi Karnapura tells us:

“Subahu, a cowherd of Vraja, is Uddharana Datta; Mahabahu, a friend in Vraja, is Sriman Mahesha Pandita.” (GGD 129)

Mahabahu is a friend (*sakha*) of Krishna, a cowherd boy of Vraja. This is not the former birth of an ordinary soul who
in time became a devotee, nor is it imitation of a cowherd boy: an eternal associate, bound to Krishna in friendship
(*sakhya-rasa*), appeared in Gaura-lila together with his Lord Nityananda — the Balarama of Vraja — to serve Him here as
well. <!-- F1 F2 Q1 -->

In the “Anubhashya” Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati calls him the seventh of the twelve gopalas, and the biographies
count him the same way: in the order of the “Gaura-ganoddesha-dipika” he is preceded by Abhirama, Sundarananda,
Dhananjaya, Gauridasa, Kamalakara and Uddharana.[^1] All twelve are given seats of honour in the Vishva-vaishnava-raja-sabha,
the assembly of the Vaishnavas of the whole world. The order of names in the lists of gopalas is not always the same,
however, and in one of them Mahesha stands tenth.[^2] <!-- F3 F4 -->

On the tree of Sri Chaitanya drawn by Krishnadasa Kaviraja, Mahesha Pandita is named twice. Among the branches of
Mahaprabhu Himself:

“Subuddhi Mishra, Hridayananda, Kamala-nayana, Mahesha Pandita, Shrikara, Sri Madhusudana.” (CC, Adi 10.111)

And among the branches of Nityananda:

“Mahesha Pandita is a generous cowherd boy of Vraja; to the beat of the kettledrum he would dance, drunk with love.” (CC,
Adi 11.32)

So the biographies count him as a branch both of Sri Chaitanya and of Sri Nityananda.[^3] <!-- F5 Q2 -->

## Birthplace and Brothers
Little is known of Mahesha's worldly life. One of the biographies takes present-day Chakdaha in Nadia to be his
birthplace, but at once adds that there is no reliable evidence for this. <!-- F17 -->

Some hold that he was the youngest brother of Jagadisha Pandita, whose shripata is at Yashara, not far from those
parts. In this view there were three brothers: Jagadisha the eldest, Hiranya the middle one, and Mahesha the youngest.
But Srila Bhaktisiddhanta, citing this opinion, remarks that the authoritative books say nothing of such a kinship, “and
therefore it is doubtful.”[^4] Yet one of the biographies tells it as settled fact: Jagadisha's youngest brother Mahesha
also wished to live by the Ganges and went with Jagadisha and his wife to Mayapur, where they settled next to the house
of Sri Jagannatha Mishra.[^5] <!-- F18 F19 -->

## Nityananda's Companion
Mahesha Pandita was a companion (*parshada*) of Sri Nityananda and, in the words of one of the biographies, an especially
close friend of His. Like all who surrounded Nityananda, he was generous beyond measure and laboured for the deliverance
of fallen souls. This does not mean that the servant became equal to his Lord: the mercy he gave away was the mercy of
Nityananda Himself, flowing through His companion. <!-- F20 I1 -->

Vrindavana Dasa Thakura, listing Nityananda's servants, says of him:

“Mahesha Pandita was a most exalted great soul.” (CB, Antya 5.744)

In Bengali — *ati parama mahanta*, “the very greatest of great devotees.”[^6] And the vandana ascribed to Vrindavana Dasa
himself adds that he is especially dear to Nityananda. <!-- F22 Q3 -->

How he is remembered can be seen from the verse of the “Charitamrita” quoted above: he danced to the beat of the
kettledrum (*dhaka*), drunk with love. This is the madness of divine love, *prema-unmada*, not worldly frenzy: so the
eternal friendship of a cowherd boy of Vraja with Krishna shows itself in Gaura-lila. Srila Jiva Gosvami too, in his
vandana, praises “Mahesha Pandita, overwhelmed by madness for Krishna.” His name also stands in Devakinandana's list of
Vaishnavas. <!-- F21 I2 F34 -->

In the “Chaitanya-mangala” Lochana Dasa names Mahesha Pandita among the companions gathered around Gauranga in
Navadvipa when the Lord was giving away prema: “Sri Rama Pandita and Mahesha Pandita, Haridasa, Nandana Acharya — the
righteous…” (Madhya 24.59).[^7] <!-- F33 -->

## Panihati and Saptagrama
When Nityananda held the festival of flat rice with yogurt (*chida-dadhi*) at Panihati for Raghunatha Dasa, Mahesha was
among His companions. Krishnadasa Kaviraja lists those seated around Nityananda on the platform:

“Ramadasa, Sundarananda, Gadadhara Dasa, Murari, Kamalakara, Sadashiva, Purandara, Dhananjaya, Jagadisha, Parameshvara
Dasa, Mahesha, Gauridasa, Krishnadasa Hoda.” (CC, Antya 6.61–62)

After the festival Mahesha Pandita followed Nityananda to Saptagrama — so Srila Bhaktisiddhanta says in the
“Anubhashya.”[^8] <!-- F23 F24 F25 -->

## After the Lord's Disappearance
Mahesha Pandita remained in this world even after Mahaprabhu and Nityananda had withdrawn Their pastimes. We meet him in
Khardaha, in Nityananda's house, where Vasudha and Jahnava lived with Virabhadra. When Srinivasa came there and stayed
four or five days, the devotees of Khardaha showered their mercy on him:

“Suryadasa, Gauridasa, Pandita Mahesha — many devotees there showed him boundless mercy.” (“Bhakti-ratnakara” 4.91)

After that Jahnava and Virabhadra told Srinivasa to go to Vrindavana. <!-- F26 -->

Later Narottama came to Khardaha. He was on his way to Nilachala; in Saptagrama he had learned that Uddharana Datta had
already departed from this world, and he went on, visiting the homes of devotees along the road. In the
“Narottama-vilasa” Narahari Chakravarti tells us that the first to notice him was Mahesha Pandita:

“At that moment Mahesha Pandita and others, from afar, seeing Narottama, said softly: ‘In separation from Prabhu we have
become as if dead, yet at the sight of him happiness arises in the heart. Without Prabhu's power this would not be
possible.’” (“Narottama-vilasa” 3.176–178)

Learning who stood before them, they told Narottama that they had long heard of him: how he had left home for
Vrindavana, how on the way back the books had been stolen and recovered, how he had come to Navadvipa — “that is why we
are still alive — to see you with our own eyes” (3.182). Narottama wept and rolled at their feet, and they embraced him,
bathed him in tears and at last, growing calm, brought him into Prabhu's house, to Vasudha, Jahnava and Virabhadra
(3.183–186). <!-- F27 F29 I3 -->

For two to four days Narottama was not allowed to leave Khardaha. Virabhadra spoke with him, Parameshvari Dasa showed him
the road, and when Jahnava gave him leave to go to Nilachala,

“Mahesha Pandita and others dear to Prabhu embraced Narottama again and again. All bade him go to Nilachala, and
Narottama bowed at the feet of all.” (“Narottama-vilasa” 3.200–201)

Having gone with him a little way, the weeping devotees returned home. Narahari speaks of this in the
“Bhakti-ratnakara” as well:

“Mahesha Pandita and the others, having seen Narottama off in great affection, could not regain their calm.”
(“Bhakti-ratnakara” 8.220)[^9]

Srila Bhaktisiddhanta likewise notes, citing the eighth wave of the “Bhakti-ratnakara,” that Mahesha Pandita was in
Khardaha when Narottama came there. <!-- F28 Q4 -->

More than once Narottama saw Mahesha when the Lord revealed His pastimes to him in dreams. Before he had even left home
he dreamt of the sankirtana of Gaura, Nityananda and Advaita on the bank of the Ganges in Navadvipa, and among the
companions were “Mahesha, Shankara, Yadu, Acharya-nandana” (“Narottama-vilasa” 2.123); and in Puri, in a dream before
the chariot, among the Lord's companions were “Gauridasa, Mahesha Pandita, Damodara” (4.129). <!-- F32 -->

And at the great festival in Kheturi, when Narottama began the kirtan, Mahaprabhu by His own will appeared there with
all His companions, and everyone saw Him. Among the dancing companions the “Narottama-vilasa” names Mahesha too:
“Jagadisha, Suryadasa, Acharya-nandana, Shrinatha, Mahesha, Yadu, Sri Madhusudana…” (7.119); so too the
“Bhakti-ratnakara” (10.595). This was no vision or product of imagination, but a real pastime of the Lord, who fulfilled
what He had promised Narottama in a dream. Whether Mahesha was in Kheturi in person or came with the Lord among the
companions who had already departed, the texts do not say outright: “the manifest and the unmanifest came together in
one place” (“Narottama-vilasa” 7.123).[^10] <!-- F30 Q5 -->

## Departure
Mahesha Pandita disappeared — departed from this world — on the thirteenth lunar day of the dark fortnight of the month
of Pausha (*krishna-trayodashi*); on this the biographies agree. In the Vaishnava calendar of the Sri Chaitanya
Saraswat Math it is the Krishna-trayodashi of the month of Narayana; it will next fall on 5 January 2027, and on the
same day the departure of Srila Uddharana Datta Thakura is honoured — the very Subahu whom Karnapura names in the same
verse with him. The sources name neither the year nor the place of his departure, nor the day of his appearance; nor do
they speak of the place of his samadhi, apart from the memorial altar at the shripata described below. <!-- F31 -->

## The Shripata
Mahesha Pandita's first shripata was at Masipur (Srila Bhaktisiddhanta adds, not quite certainly: “or Yashipur”), on the
eastern bank of the Ganges, opposite Jirat. The Ganges swallowed Masipur, and the Deity installed by Mahesha Pandita was
moved for a time to Beledanga near Sukhsagar. When the river ate away Beledanga too, the zamindar of Palpara,
Navakumara Chattopadhyaya, persuaded the servitor of the day, the babaji Ramakrishnadasa, to bring the Deity to Palpara,
and built a temple there. His son Rajani Babu in 1883 deeded the temple lands to Harekrishnadasa Babaji; hence the name
“Palpara-pat.” <!-- F6 F7 F8 -->

Palpara lies in the district of Nadia, in the forest, a mile south of Chakdaha station; the Ganges, as Srila
Bhaktisiddhanta writes, is far from here.[^11] This is the pargana of Panchanagar: it has fourteen villages (*mauzas*) —
Beledanga, Berigram, Sukhsagar, Chandure, Manasapota, Palpara and others — and some call this country “Nagardesh.”
<!-- F9 -->

The shripata remained in Palpara until the first months of the Bengali year 1334 (1927) and was then moved to
Kathalpuli (Kathaldhuli) near Chakdaha; the move was reported in the journal “Gaudiya” (year 6, no. 13, “Letters
received”).[^12] On 28 June 1931 Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati formally took over the management of Mahesha Pandita's
shripata in Chakdaha and spoke at the opening before a large gathering. In the list of maths and preaching centres
founded by Srila Bhaktisiddhanta the shripata appears as number 22: “Shripata of Sri Mahesha Pandita, Kathalpuli,
P.O. Chakdaha, Nadia.” <!-- F10 F11 F12 -->

The temple of the shripata is a modest building, like an ordinary dwelling house, and already in the days of the
“Anubhashya” it was dilapidated. In it reside Sri Gaura-Nityananda, Sri Gopinatha, Sri Madana-mohana, Sri Radha-Govinda
and a shalagrama-shila — the Lord Himself, manifest as the *archa-vigraha* to accept the service of His devotees. Before
the temple stands the altar of the *phul-samaj*, the “flower tomb” of Mahesha Pandita — a memorial place where his
presence is honoured.[^13] <!-- F13 F14 F15 -->

In Srila Bhaktisiddhanta's time the service was kept up only by alms: a local resident, Sri Kalikrishna Chakravarti,
gave twenty-five rupees a year towards it, and the servitor was Sri Sanatana Dasa Babaji. Mahesha Pandita left no
descendants. <!-- F16 -->

## Significance
The original sources say little of Mahesha Pandita, yet it is enough to let us see who he was. In Vraja he is Mahabahu,
Krishna's friend; in Gaura's pastimes, one of the twelve gopalas, a servant of Nityananda and, in Vrindavana Dasa's
words, “the very greatest of great devotees.” His dancing to the kettledrum is the madness of the pure prema of a
cowherd boy of Vraja; his generosity to the fallen is Nityananda's mercy flowing through His companion. And when the
Lord withdrew His pastimes, Mahesha and his fellows, by their own words, lived only in the hope of seeing the Lord's new
devotees — and on seeing Narottama they found their joy again. <!-- F2 F3 F22 I1 I2 I3 -->

Let us pray, then, for the mercy of Mahesha Pandita — Mahabahu of Vraja, Krishna's friend and Nityananda's companion.
