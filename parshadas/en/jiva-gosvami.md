# Jiva Gosvami
*Jiva Gosvami · @jiva-gosvami*

## One of the Six Gosvamis
In the journal “Gaudiya” (year 1, no. 4) Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati explained what three titles of honour mean.
Everyone knows Sri Chaitanya as Mahaprabhu, while Nityananda and Advaita, whom He honoured, are known as Prabhu; and those
are called Gosvamis who are poets that love Him, are dear to Him and left their homes for His sake. There were many
Gosvamis in Vrindavana, but six are glorified everywhere, and Jiva is one of them. All his life he aspired to be nothing
more than a follower (anuga) of Sri Rupa; Sanatana was his parama-guru, and the One he worshipped was Sri Chaitanya-chandra,
who for the pure vision of the Gaudiyas is not different from Vrajendra-nandana Himself. From his boyhood Jiva displayed
the lila of a lifelong celibate (naishthika-brahmachari); he was a brihad-vrati, a keeper of the great vow, and he spent
his whole life in the company of Sarasvati, the goddess of speech, full of spiritual play. That is why he is called the
crown of the Gaudiya Vaishnava acharyas. <!-- F1 F2 F3 -->

Bhaktivinoda Thakura wrote in the “Sajjana-toshani” (year 2) that a Vaishnava has only to hear the name of Jiva Gosvami
for his heart to dance with joy. And Srila Bhaktisiddhanta, in his article “Toshanir katha” in the same “Sajjana-toshani”,
by then published under his editorship (year 18, no. 5), says that it is only by Jiva's boundless mercy that the
Rupanuga-bhakti-dharma — the love of Krishna preached by Mahaprabhu — still brings endless good to all living beings in the
world. Jiva did not write a single book in Bengali, yet Krishnadasa Kaviraja, the best of the Rupanugas, drew a number of
conclusions from his “Sandarbhas” for the “Chaitanya-charitamrita” and so increased the joy of all who learn bhakti.
<!-- F4 F5 F6 -->

In the same place Srila Bhaktisiddhanta names the two original gurus (mula-gurus) of the Rupanugas — Jiva and Raghunatha
Dasa Gosvami. Raghunatha Dasa is the acharya of the path on which living taste (ruchi) decides everything: he leads
fortunate souls along the easy road of bhajana. Jiva, the supreme knower of transcendental rasa, took care of those in
whom ruchi has not yet awakened: he preserved the wealth of the sampradaya through the ordinances of vaidhi-marga, the
path of rules, and left no one any room to doubt the transcendental greatness of his guru. So the Rupanugas stand on two
pillars: one guides those in whom taste has already awakened, the other guards the path of rules for those in whom it
has not yet. <!-- F7 I5 -->

## Years of His Life
It is impossible to say for certain when Jiva appeared: several dates of his appearance and departure have been found in
different records.[^1] Those most often cited come from the note “Determining the Years of the Six Gosvamis”, which
Bhaktivinoda Thakura printed in the “Sajjana-toshani” (year 2, no. 2) from the records of a certain Vaishnava. According
to them Jiva appeared in 1455 of the Shaka era (about 1533 CE) and departed in 1540 Shaka (about 1618); he spent
eighty-five years on earth — twenty at home and sixty-five in Vrindavana. An old manuscript from the collection of the
late Vishvambharananda Deva Gosvami, the pandita of the shripata of Gopivallabhapura, gives the same figures, and other
researches arrive at them too. Yet Bhaktivinoda himself remarked that some dates in his source seem certain and others
doubtful, and that by this record Jiva would have been born in the very year of Mahaprabhu's disappearance, which is
hardly correct.[^2] <!-- F8 F9 F10 F11 F12 F13 F14 F15 F16 -->

According to our Vaishnava calendar, the appearance day of Srila Jiva Gosvami is Gaura-dvadashi of the month of
Hrishikesha — the same day on which the appearance of Sri Vamanadeva is celebrated. His departure day, honoured by
Vaishnavas all over the world as supremely auspicious, is Gaura-tritiya of the month of Narayana.[^3] <!-- F17 F18 -->

## Lineage
Out of deep humility Rupa and Sanatana called themselves men of low birth. Sanatana says to Mahaprabhu:

“I took birth in a truly godless family, in which sin and the breaking of God's commandments were an everyday matter”.
(CC, Antya 4.28)

And in Ramakeli the brothers confessed to the Lord:

“We have defiled our birth by low deeds and by association with base people. We are unworthy even to stand before You”.
(CC, Madhya 1.189) <!-- Q1 Q2 -->

Pretended scholars of coarse understanding failed to grasp this lila of humility played by the teachers of the world. As
they had earlier taken Mahaprabhu for a “Mayavadi sannyasi”, so they took the Lord's eternal associates, Rupa and
Sanatana, for men of low birth — and sank into offence. Had Jiva not mercifully given in his writings true information
about his predecessor gurus and his family, people would have remained in that error. He set out the genealogy at the
end of the “Laghu-toshani”, his commentary on the Tenth Canto of the “Bhagavatam”; Narahari Chakravarti also gives it, seven
generations deep, in the “Bhakti-ratnakara”. <!-- F19 F20 F21 I1 Q3 -->

This genealogy is told in detail in the article on Rupa Gosvami; here it is given briefly, together with what is not found
there.[^4] The founder of the line was Sarvajna “Jagad-guru”, king of Karnata — a renowned sovereign, the best of the
Bharadvaja line, honoured by assemblies of kings. A brahmana of the Yajur-veda and a master of all the shastras, he earned
the name “teacher of the world” by his unrivalled learning: all the brahmanas of Karnata revered him, and students came to
him from many lands. Jiva's verse says that his tongue, rich in beautiful turns of speech, “danced like a bee” without
tiring among the three wish-fulfilling creepers — the three Vedas. His son Aniruddha-deva was handsome, like the sage
Kashyapa; in fame he rivalled the moon, in might Indra; all kings honoured him, and in the Yajur-veda he was the one
refuge of all. <!-- F22 F23 F24 F25 -->

Of his two queens Aniruddha had two sons: Rupeshvara, accomplished in many shastras, and Harihara, skilled in warfare. On
the day of his departure (the verse says, “setting out for the city of Madhuripu”) the father divided the kingdom between
them, but the younger, Harihara, seized his elder brother's share. Rupeshvara and his wife went on eight horses to an
eastern land and lived happily in the domain of his friend Shikhareshvara; there a son, Padmanabha, was born to him. On
Padmanabha's tongue the entire Yajur-veda with its angas and all the Upanishads resounded clearly, and his heart melted
with love for Jagannatha.[^5] Wishing to live on the bank of the Ganga, he settled, with honours shown him by King
Danujamardana, in Navahatta (now Naihati), and there worshipped the Deity of Sri Purushottama with sacrifices and
festivals. He had eighteen daughters and five sons: the eldest Purushottama, the second Jagannatha, steadfast Narayana,
Murari rich in virtues, and the youngest, the renowned Mukunda. <!-- F26 F27 F28 F29 -->

In Navahatta a son, Kumara-deva, the best of brahmanas, was born to Mukunda. When religious strife broke out in Naihati,
the pious Kumara-deva left for Bengal and settled in Bakla-Chandradvipa, and he also made himself a dwelling in
Fatehabad (then in the Jessore district), between Naihati and Bakla.[^6] Of his sons, three were dearest of all to the
best Vaishnavas. Jiva writes of them (the “Laghu-toshani”, in the translation of the verses quoted by Narahari
Chakravarti):

“The first is Srila Sanatana, his younger brother named Sri Rupa, then [the youngest] bearing the name Sriman Vallabha;
turning away from royal power and obtaining the great mercy of Bhagavan Sri Krishna Chaitanya, they truly enjoyed the
kingdom of the wealth of bhakti called prema for Murahara.

He who was the youngest of all — my father — soon attained Sri Rama on the Ganga, while the elder [brothers] then came to
Vrindavana; by them the hidden tirthas of Mathura were revealed, and bhakti for Sri Vrajaraja-nandana was raised high
everywhere”. (“Bhakti-ratnakara”, 1.784–785) <!-- F30 F31 F32 F33 F49 -->

Jiva's father was Vallabha, the youngest of the three brothers, and Jiva was his only son. The genealogical table does not
name Kumara-deva's other children: it is known only that there were some. It is “said” that after Kumara-deva's
departure the three brothers lived and studied in the house of their maternal uncle in the hamlet of Sakurma near the
capital of Gauda, and that later the two elder ones became ministers of Hussain Shah with the titles “Sakara Mallik” and
“Dabir Khas”. <!-- F32 F38 F39 -->

Further on in the “Laghu-toshani” Jiva glorifies Raghunatha Dasa — the friend of Rupa and Sanatana, renowned on earth, who
whirls forever in the waves of the great ocean of love for Radha and Krishna; the best of the worthy, to their own
amazement, honour him as equal to both brothers. He also tells how Gopala Himself, in the guise of a cowherd boy, brought
the brothers milk. Then Jiva lists the books of Rupa[^7] and of Sanatana: the “Sri Bhagavatamrita”, the
“Hari-bhakti-vilasa” with the commentary “Dig-darshini”, the “Lila-stava”, and the commentary on the Tenth Canto itself —
the “Vaishnava-toshani” (“Dashama-tippani”); and to abridge this book, he says, Sanatana ordered “an insignificant jiva”,
that is, Jiva himself. He asks forgiveness for what he wrote in haste, with or without understanding, and for the places
where he left out his teacher's explanations: “And if I have written only what he himself awakened in my heart, I have
nothing to fear from doubters”.[^8] <!-- F34 F35 F36 F37 Q4 Q5 -->

## His Father, Anupama
Jiva appeared in Bakla-Chandradvipa.[^9] His mother's name is not known. <!-- F40 F41 -->

His father was originally called Vallabha; the name “Anupama” was given to him by Mahaprabhu. For his service under the
badshah of Gauda he, like his brothers, bore the title “Mallik”. The “Chaitanya-charitamrita” says:

“Sri Rupa's brother was also a great Vaishnava. His name was Sri Vallabha, but he was better known as Anupama Mallik”.
(CC, Madhya 19.36) <!-- F42 Q7 -->

And in his list of the branches of the tree of bhakti Krishnadasa Kaviraja names father and son together:

“Sri Rupa and Sri Sanatana are a great branch, and Sri Anupama, Sri Jiva Gosvami, Rajendra and other devotees are twigs on
those branches”. (CC, Adi 10.85)

What kind of devotee Anupama was, Sanatana himself tells Mahaprabhu:

“My younger brother Anupama was from his very childhood a great devotee of Ramachandra. He worshipped Him with
determination and devotion in his heart. Day and night he chanted the holy name of Rama and meditated on His eternal
form, and constantly heard and recounted the narratives of the sacred «Ramayana». <…>

After these words he could not sleep all night, thinking: «How can I give up the lotus feet of Lord Ramachandra?» He
wept the whole night through, and in the morning came to us with a humble request: «I have sold my head, offering it to
the lotus feet of Ramachandra. I cannot take it back. The very thought of it brings me unbearable pain. Do not be so
strict with me. Please show your mercy. Allow me, life after life, to serve the lotus feet of Ramachandra. I have no
strength to reject the service of Ramachandra's lotus feet. The very thought of it tears my heart to pieces». Hearing
these words, we embraced him and began to praise him: «You are a true saint, for your devotion to God is firm and
unshakeable»”. (CC, Antya 4.30–31, 38–43)

Anupama lived inseparably with his brothers and with them heard talks about Krishna and the “Bhagavatam”. The brothers
wanted to test him and urged him to worship the all-attractive Krishna together with them; out of respect for them he
agreed to ask for initiation — and then came what Sanatana described. Hearing this story, Mahaprabhu compared Anupama
with Murari Gupta, who was just as firmly devoted to Rama. <!-- F43 F44 Q6 -->

Anupama first saw Mahaprabhu when the Lord came to Ramakeli.[^10] By His will renunciation awoke in Rupa and Sanatana, and
soon the brothers left everything to seek His company. When Rupa, displaying the lila of abandoning worldly affairs, set
out for Vrindavana and the feet of Mahaprabhu, Anupama went with him. In Prayaga both received the Lord's darshana in the
house of a southern brahmana and at His command went on to Vrindavana. Subuddhi Raya, who was then selling dry firewood in
Mathura and feeding himself and other Vaishnavas on the proceeds, went round the twelve forests of Vrindavana with them.
After a month the brothers, looking for Sanatana, went along the Ganga to Prayaga, then to Kashi, heard Mahaprabhu's
teachings there and ten days later left for Gauda. All this is told in detail in the articles on Rupa and Sanatana.
<!-- F45 F46 F47 -->

Having settled their worldly affairs in Gauda, both went to Nilachala at Mahaprabhu's command, and on the way, on the bank
of the Ganga, Anupama attained the abode of Sri Ramachandra.[^11] Jiva himself, as we have seen, writes of this in the
“Laghu-toshani”: “my father soon attained Sri Rama on the Ganga”. <!-- F48 F49 -->

## Childhood
Jiva spent his childhood with his uncles in Ramakeli. The “Bhakti-ratnakara” preserves the tradition that when
Mahaprabhu came there to accept Rupa and Sanatana, little Jiva secretly saw His feet:

“Sri Jiva and others saw the Lord in secret; this I heard from the lips of very old people”. (“Bhakti-ratnakara”,
1.638)[^12]

By the Lord's will the same spirit of renunciation that awoke in his uncles then touched Jiva's heart as well.
<!-- F50 F51 Q8 -->

From early childhood an inclination to service could be seen in him: even when playing with friends he chose only games
connected with Krishna. Narahari Chakravarti relates:

“In his childhood Sri Jiva, with other boys, knew no games but those connected with Sri Krishna. Making figures of Krishna
and Balarama, he would worship Them with flowers, sandal and the like. In various ornaments and garments They were very
beautiful; gazing at Them without blinking, he rejoiced at heart. Falling to the ground like a golden doll, he bowed to
Them and was bathed in tears. Offering Them various sweets with great care, he would eat the prasada together with the
boys”. (“Bhakti-ratnakara”, 1.719–723) <!-- F52 F53 I2 Q10 -->

From childhood Jiva loved the “Bhagavatam”, and very soon he became foremost in grammar, poetry, rhetoric and the other
shastras; his incomparable beauty and transcendental qualities amazed everyone. <!-- F54 -->

When Rupa and Sanatana left for Vraja, Jiva lost all peace:

“From the time the Gosvamis went to Vrindavana, what came over Sri Jiva's heart! Various jewelled ornaments, fine
garments, wondrous beds and luxurious meals — all this he gave up, nothing pleases him, and of kingdoms and worldly
affairs he cannot bear to hear”. (“Bhakti-ratnakara”, 1.686–688)

And when Gaurasundara too disappeared, Jiva's heart ached with separation: day and night, thinking of the feet of Rupa,
Sanatana and Gaurasundara, he swam in an ocean of tears of love.[^13] <!-- F55 Q9 -->

## Two Dreams and Navadvipa
Once, during nama-kirtana, singing the names of Gaurasundara, Jiva wept and lost all composure. Dozing off at the end of
the night, he had a dream: “In the midst of the sankirtana Gaura-raya dances, intoxicating the world with prema
unattainable even for Brahma” (“Bhakti-ratnakara”, 1.714). The vision vanished, Jiva woke in turmoil — and fell asleep
again, and saw a second dream. Krishna and Balarama appeared before him, and then the same Two, golden in hue, Gaura and
Nityananda. They placed Their feet on his head, embraced him with fatherly love and comforted him, and then

“Sri Gaurasundara, overcome with great prema, entrusted him to the feet of Prabhu Nityananda. Nityananda says to Sri Jiva
again and again: «Let this Lord of Mine be everything to you»”. (“Bhakti-ratnakara”, 1.736–737)[^14] <!-- F56 -->

On waking, Jiva soon left home. From Bakla-Chandradvipa he set out by way of Fatehabad for Sri Mayapur-Navadvipa: as far
as Fatehabad companions saw him off, and from there he went on without them. In Navadvipa, in the house of Srivasa
Pandita, Nityananda Prabhu was waiting for him:

“Prabhu Nityananda, overwhelmed with great parental love, placed both His feet on Sri Jiva's head. He showed Sri Jiva the
utmost mercy: raising him from the ground, He embraced him tightly. In the rapture of prema the Lord says: «For your sake
I hastened here from Khardaha». Saying many such things, He calmed Sri Jiva and had Srivasa and the other devotees show
him mercy. Having kept him by His side in great joy, He sends Sri Jiva off to the western country. <…> The Lord says: «Go
quickly to Vraja: the Lord has given that place to your family»”. (“Bhakti-ratnakara”, 1.765–769, 772)
<!-- F57 F58 F59 Q11 -->

Krishnadasa Kaviraja writes of the same:

“Having decided to go from Bengal to Mathura, Sri Jiva Gosvami asked Srila Nityananda Prabhu for permission. Since Sri
Jiva was the nephew of Sanatana and Rupa Gosvami, who were especially dear to Sri Chaitanya Mahaprabhu, Sri Nityananda
bestowed His mercy on him, touching his head with His foot and clasping him in His embrace. Then Nityananda Prabhu gave
Sri Jiva this order: «Go to Vrindavana at once. Sri Chaitanya has made the holy land of Vraja a special place for your
whole family». At His command Sri Jiva went to Vrindavana and there obtained the fruit of fulfilling the will of God: he
composed many spiritual books and successfully preached from there the teaching of bhakti”. (CC, Antya 4.232–235)
<!-- F60 Q12 -->

Before leaving, Jiva, following Nityananda and by His mercy, went round all the holy places of Navadvipa-dhama. In
Bhaktivinoda Thakura's “Navadvipa-dhama-mahatmya” it is Nityananda Himself who leads Jiva through the dhama. At the house
of Sridhara Pandita Nityananda tells him that here Mahaprabhu used to stop the kirtana so that the devotees could rest —
this is the “vishrama-sthana”, the place of rest — and invites the two of them to rest as well. <!-- F61 F62 -->

## Studies in Kashi
From Navadvipa Jiva went to Kashi (Benares) and for some time studied all the shastras under Madhusudana Vachaspati.
Tradition links this teacher with Mahaprabhu Himself: in Nilachala Sarvabhauma Bhattacharya had heard from Sri Krishna
Chaitanya Vedanta conclusions full of spiritual play, and he passed them on to his disciple Madhusudana. So Jiva, coming
to Madhusudana Vachaspati at Nityananda's command and studying nyaya, Vedanta and other shastras with him, heard these
teachings of Mahaprabhu. In the “Jaiva-dharma” (ch. 39) Bhaktivinoda says the same: the burden of preaching the tattva lay
on Sarvabhauma, and he passed this task on to Jiva through one of his disciples. <!-- F63 F64 F65 -->

## At the Feet of Rupa and Sanatana
From Kashi Jiva came to Vrindavana, took full shelter of Rupa and Sanatana, studied with them the whole “Bhagavata” and the
shastras of bhakti, and from then on never left Vraja-mandala. Krishnadasa Kaviraja writes:

“The son of Sri Vallabha (Anupama), the younger brother of Rupa Gosvami, was the greatest knower of the sacred scriptures.
His name was Sri Jiva Gosvami. Sri Jiva Gosvami came to Vrindavana, completely leaving his worldly life. He also wrote many
books on pure devotion to God and helped to spread them. He wrote the book “Bhagavat-sandarbha”, in which he revealed the
essence of all the scriptures. In it we find the deepest explanation of the nature of God and of loving service to Him.
He also wrote the “Gopala-champu”, in which he explained the nature of the loving pastimes of Sri Sri Radha-Krishna in
Vrindavana. In the “Shat-sandarbha” he set out all the highest truths about divine love for Krishna. In his works one can
find more than four hundred thousand Sanskrit verses”. (CC, Antya 4.227–231) <!-- F66 F67 -->

Rupa and Sanatana were so pleased with Jiva's transcendental learning and his inborn grasp of the conclusions of bhakti
that they even gave him their own books to correct:

“Sri Rupa composed the «Sri Hamsa-duta» and other books, Sanatana wrote the «Bhagavatamrita» and others. Having composed
the «Sri Vaishnava-toshani», Sanatana ordered Sri Jiva to edit it”. (“Bhakti-ratnakara”, 1.791–792)

So the “Laghu-toshani” came into being, of which more will be said later. At that time too, according to the
“Bhakti-ratnakara”, Jiva clashed with Vallabha Bhatta — of this, and of how the acharyas view the story, see below, in the
section on slanders. <!-- F68 Q14 -->

## Head of the Vaishnavas of Vraja
When Rupa, Sanatana and the other Gosvamis had disappeared, Jiva took the place of universal acharya of the Gaudiya
Vaishnava sampradaya — in Utkala, Gauda and Mathura-mandala. He proclaimed to all the conclusions of pure bhakti preached
by Gaurasundara and drew everyone into Hari-bhajana. Srila Bhaktisiddhanta in the “Anubhashya” (on CC, Adi 10.85) calls
him the supreme teacher of siddhanta in the sampradaya, the undisputed head of the Gaudiya Vaishnavas of Mathura, Bengal
and Orissa, and the “prime minister” of the universal assembly of Vaishnavas in Vrindavana — the Vishva-vaishnava-raja-sabha.
A newspaper note from Srila Bhaktisiddhanta's time reports that this sabha was founded by Jiva Gosvami himself eleven years
after Mahaprabhu's departure, and that Bhaktivinoda Thakura gave it new life; its revival was marked on the appearance day
of Vishnupriya, and Srila Bhaktisiddhanta set out the history of the sabha in the “Sajjana-toshani” (vol. 21, no. 9).[^15]
<!-- F72 F73 F74 -->

From time to time Jiva went round Vraja with the devotees and went to Mathura — for the darshana of Sri Gopaladeva, who
then resided in the house of Vitthaleshvara (Vitthalanatha), the son of Vallabha Bhatta. Krishnadasa Kaviraja wrote the
“Chaitanya-charitamrita” while Jiva was still living. <!-- F75 F76 -->

All who came from Gauda Jiva received with prasada and lodging. Jahnava-devi, the consort of Nityananda, also came to
Vrindavana with other devotees. The heads of the Vaishnavas of Vraja came out to greet her — Jiva, Gopala Bhatta,
Lokanatha, Bhugarbha and Madhu Pandita; according to Parameshvari Dasa's account, “the renowned Sri Jiva” was among
those who met her in Mathura and told her the names of the other Gosvamis. Going round Vraja-mandala, Jahnava listened to
Jiva expounding the “Brihad-bhagavatamrita”. When she brought the five-year-old Kanu to Vrindavana, Jiva and the other
Gosvamis, seeing the boy's devotion, named him “Kanai Thakura”.[^16] <!-- F77 F78 F79 F80 Q25 -->

When Nityananda's son Virachandra (Virabhadra) came to Vrindavana, the mahajanas of Vraja went out to meet him — Jiva,
Krishnadasa Kaviraja, Ananta Acharya, Haridasa Pandita, Krishnadasa Brahmachari (a disciple of Gadadhara Pandita and
servant of Madana-Gopala), Gopinatha Adhikari, Madhu Pandita and his fellow Madhavacharya. With the permission of Bhugarbha
and Jiva, Virachandra went round Vraja: the twelve forests, Radha- and Shyama-kundas, Govardhana.[^17] <!-- F81 F82 -->

Jiva's name appears in many other accounts of the Vaishnavas of that time as well. He is among those who teach the worship
of Krishna together with the ashraya-vigraha, the shelter of devotion; together with other Gaudiya acharyas he gave
Jahnava the place of jagad-guru; in relation to Bhugarbha Jiva is one of the juniors. Srila Bhaktisiddhanta in the
“Anubhashya” (on CC, Madhya 7.130) calls fools those who consider writing books, such as occupied Rupa, Sanatana, Jiva and
Raghunatha Dasa, an obstacle to bhajana. Baladeva names Jiva in his list of the acharyas of the sampradaya, and the
“Vaishnava-vandana”, attributed to Jiva, glorifies Gopala Bhatta.[^18] Narottama in one of his songs, lamenting that he had
not served the feet of the Gosvamis, names Jiva together with Svarupa Damodara, Sanatana, Raghunatha Bhatta, Raghunatha
Dasa, Gopala Bhatta, Bhugarbha and Lokanatha. <!-- F83 F84 -->

Jiva, following all the satvata-shastras, counts among the offences against the Holy Name instructing a faithless person
(ashraddadhana) in Hari-nama. Therefore, when Gadadhara Dasa gave the Name to the kazi, his biography specially notes that
he did not commit this offence. <!-- F85 -->

## Sri Sri Radha-Damodara
According to the “Bhakti-ratnakara” (4th wave), Rupa Gosvami himself revealed the Deity of Sri Sri Radha-Damodara and gave
Him to Jiva. That Deity now resides in Jaipur, while in Vrindavana, in the Radha-Damodara temple near Shringara-vata,
resides His manifestation (prakasha-murti).[^19] In one of the rooms of the Radha-Damodara temple many old manuscripts
were kept, but because of long temple lawsuits the library was under lock, and where the books went no one can say.
<!-- F86 F87 -->

## The Ganga and the Yamuna
The Hindi book “Bhakta-kalpadruma” tells the following story. Princes subject to Akbar, from the banks of the Ganga and
from Rajputana, fell into a dispute as to which was higher — the Ganga or the Yamuna. Akbar respectfully invited Jiva to
judge between them. Jiva replied that he would not spend a single night outside Vrindavana; so the princes posted relays
of horses along the road, so that he could ride from Vrindavana to Agra and back in a single day. <!-- F88 -->

With arguments from the shastras Jiva showed that the Ganga is the water that washed the feet of Vishnu and a shakti of
Vishnu, whereas the Yamuna is Krishna's beloved and therefore higher than the Ganga on the ladder of rasa. The badshah and
the princes fervently begged him to accept gifts, but he took nothing. At last, yielding to their insistence, he said
that if they were set on giving something, let them send manuscripts of the shastras and Puranas from Varanasi and paper
for writing books from Agra. Akbar and the princes gladly did so. Tradition says that Jiva was the first to have
“tulat” paper brought from Agra and began the copying of manuscripts on it; before that books were written on birch bark,
palm leaves and the like. <!-- F89 F90 F91 -->

## Master of Vedanta
Bhaktivinoda Thakura, in his article “Sri Sri Jiva Gosvami Prabhu” (“Sajjana-toshani”, year 2, pp. 220–221), writes that
in the science of Vedanta Jiva had no equal in his day. It is “said” that Vallabha, the acharya of the Vishnusvami
sampradaya, showed him his book “Tattva-dipa”; Jiva put forward many Vedantic arguments and showed the flaws in his views,
and Vallabhacharya on his advice corrected a good deal in the book. Bhaktivinoda quotes four verses of the “Tattva-dipa”
in which, he says, knowing Vaishnavas will easily recognise Jiva's hand.[^20] Jiva, he says, is a pandita and knower of
Vedanta equal to Ramanuja; his “Shat-sandarbha” is a jewel of the world: whoever understands it well will find no Vedantic
argument unknown to him. <!-- F92 F93 F94 F95 Q15 -->

## Slanders and the Acharyas' Answer
People of superficial vision, not grasping the line of thought of Jiva, the best of the Rupanugas, hold distorted
opinions about him and so deprive themselves of the kingdom of pure bhakti. Srila Bhaktisiddhanta, in the “Anubhashya”
(on CC, Adi 10.85), where Jiva's life is briefly told, warns the inexperienced: among the ignorant prakrita-sahajiyas three
slanders about Jiva are current. Whoever yields to them becomes hostile to Krishna, the guru and the Vaishnavas, sinks ever
deeper into offences and loses his taste for service. Only when by Krishna's mercy such people see themselves as eternal
servants will they receive Jiva's blessing, understand what it means to be “humbler than a blade of grass” and to “honour
all”, and be able to chant the Holy Name properly. <!-- F96 Q13 I11 -->

The first slander concerns a pandita-digvijayi — a “conqueror of the quarters” — who sought worldly fame. He obtained from
Sanatana and Rupa, the crown jewels of the unpretentious (nikinchana-shiromani), a certificate of victory (jaya-patra): the
gurus of Jiva acknowledged defeat without debate, as though they were not learned. Then the proud man began to revile
them as ignoramuses and demanded the same certificate from their disciple. Jiva entered into debate with him, defeated
him, silenced the tongue of the reviler of his gurus, and revealed the glory of the effulgence of his teachers' toenails.
By this he showed what a true disciple is — a “guru-devatma”, who sees in the guru his deity and his very life. <!-- F97 -->

Those averse to Hari, failing to understand this, did not scruple to call Jiva a violator of the verse on humility —
“trinad api sunichena” (“humbler than a blade of grass”) — and invented a story that his guru punished him for it.[^21]
Srila Bhaktisiddhanta calls this story a slander of the sahajiyas: victory over the reviler of one's gurus is not a breach
of humility but a model of a disciple's fidelity. Whoever sees anger and pride in it is like certain present-day worldly
men of letters who, seeing Vrindavana Dasa Thakura's anger at the revilers of Nityananda, took even him, an avatara of
Vyasa, to be subject to passions; a stream of such opinions about Jiva can be seen in the “Bhakta-mala” of Laladasa and
similar books. <!-- F98 I10 F69 F70 F71 -->

The second slander concerns a well. Certain envious people spread a story that Jiva threw the “Chaitanya-charitamrita” into
a well, and that Krishnadasa Kaviraja, hearing of it, gave up his life; the book supposedly survived only because the
Kaviraja's disciple Mukunda had copied it beforehand.[^22] Srila Bhaktisiddhanta calls this a contemptible fabrication,
born of enmity towards the guru and the Vaishnavas and without any foundation. <!-- F99 F100 -->

The falsity of this story is seen also from another tradition, itself apparently interpolated and contradictory. The
“Prema-vilasa” (13th vilasa) says that when the books Jiva had sent to Gauda with Srinivasa, Narottama and Shyamananda
were seized by King Vira Hambira of Vana-Vishnupura and the news reached Vraja, the Kaviraja left his body at Radha-kunda,
calling on the mercy of the Lord's associates:

“Lokanatha, Gopala Bhatta, Sri Jiva Gosani, show me mercy — I have no one”. (“Prema-vilasa”, 13.136)

But if the Kaviraja died at the news of the theft even before Srinivasa, then still unmarried, first brought the books to
Yajigrama, how could Jiva in a later letter, when Srinivasa already had sons and daughters, report: “From here — the
obeisances of Sri Krishnadasa” (“Prema-vilasa”, 25, letter 6)? Such contradictions show that these traditions are
malicious and were inserted later.[^23] <!-- F100 F101 Q16 I12 -->

## Svakiya and Parakiya
The third slander touches the most intimate realm — the rasa of the gopis. Worldly sahajiyas, devoted to gratifying the
senses, say that Jiva did not follow Rupa in accepting the parakiya-rasa of the gopis of Vraja but approved svakiya-rasa —
that in his treatises he rejected parakiya-vada, the teaching that the gopis of Vrindavana were “married to others” — and
that he is therefore not a true Rupanuga and not a rasika-bhakta. This opinion is refuted by the very conduct and teachings
of the disciples Jiva trained — Srinivasa Acharya, Narottama Thakura and Shyamananda. <!-- F102 F103 -->

Srila Bhaktisiddhanta explains why Jiva spoke of svakiya. Already in his lifetime some of his followers inclined towards
svakiya-vada. Seeing their limitations, Jiva, for their sake — and for the sake of those in future who would not grasp
the transcendental nature of parakiya-vada and, imitating Krishna, would fall into adultery — gave room to the teaching of
svakiya. That is just how an acharya should act. To conclude from this that Jiva opposed the transcendental parakiya-vada
is impossible: he is the best of Rupa's followers and one of the shiksha-gurus of Krishnadasa Kaviraja. Bhaktivinoda Thakura
writes the same: the “parakiya”-bhakti of worldly-minded people in many places degenerates into worldly lawlessness, and,
seeing this, “our tattva-acharya” Jiva grew alarmed and spoke his words. To accept their essence is pure Vaishnavism; to try
to establish another view by belittling the acharya is an offence. <!-- F104 F105 F141 I13 I14 -->

That Jiva himself held to Rupa's intention is attested by his followers. Yadunandana Dasa Thakura, a disciple of Hemalata
Thakurani, the eldest daughter of Srinivasa Acharya, tells in the “Karnananda” how Raghunatha Dasa settled at Radha-kunda:

“Having settled all this, Sri Dasa Gosani made rules for himself and settled there, on the bank of the kunda. With him
were Krishnadasa and Lokanatha Gosani; day and night — unceasing talk of Krishna. At that time the book called
«Gopala-champu» they all relished together unceasingly. Relishing it, they rejoiced greatly at heart; but very hard to grasp
is the intent of its verses: by the outward meaning it is understood as svakiya, but the inner meaning is parakiya alone.
Not understanding Sri Jiva's deep heart, outsiders explain it as svakiya; but the inner meaning of the book points to
parakiya. They were all immersed in bliss, relishing this”. (“Karnananda”, 4.431–437) <!-- F106 Q17 -->

What Jiva intended in his commentary “Lochana-rochani” on the “Ujjvala-nilamani” was disclosed by Vishvanatha Chakravarti in
his commentary “Ananda-chandrika”, and the purpose of that intention was explained by Narahari Chakravarti. In the
“Narottama-vilasa” (in the verses about himself as author) he writes: in his commentary on the “Ujjvala-nilamani” Jiva gave
many explanations for the sake of people of a bad disposition; the ill-intentioned do not understand his words and bring
disquisitions on tattva into all the lilas. Jiva is a follower of Rupa; by his mercy the truth reveals itself in the heart
— who, then, can understand the words of such a Jiva? And Vishvanatha is not different from Jiva in outlook: Rupa's intention
was manifest in him, and together with Radhika and her friends he showed the world great mercy. <!-- F107 F108 Q18 -->

The dispute over svakiya and parakiya did not die down later either. Tradition tells that the Jaipur pandita-digvijayi
Krishnadeva Sharma, with a decree from the Delhi padishah Muhammad Shah, at the head of troops and vassals, appeared at the
court of the Murshidabad nawab Jafar Ali and demanded a debate on svakiya and parakiya. Then Radhamohana Thakura, a
descendant of Srinivasa Acharya, conducted the examination for six months, established the siddhanta of parakiya and made
Krishnadeva his disciple. The dispute was decided in the month of vaishakha of the year 1128;[^24] the certificates of
defeat (ajaya-patra) and of renunciation (istafa-patra) issued at that debate survive to this day. <!-- F109 F110 F111 -->

## Goloka and Gokula: Bhaktivinoda's Explanation
That Jiva's siddhanta always remains within the bounds of the instructions of Rupa and Sanatana, Bhaktivinoda Thakura
showed with many arguments from shruti in his commentary “Prakashini” on the “Brahma-samhita” (on verse 5.37,
“ananda-chinmaya-rasa…”). Jiva, he writes, is “our tattva-acharya” and therefore always within the bounds of the
instructions of Rupa and Sanatana; more than that, in Krishna's lila he is a special manjari and knows every truth. Failing
to grasp his intention, some interpret him at their whim and divide everyone into supporters and opponents. What follows
is the course of that argument. <!-- F112 F114 -->

In his explanation of the verse of the “Brahma-samhita”, in his commentary on the “Ujjvala-nilamani”, in the
“Krishna-sandarbha” and elsewhere Jiva says: Krishna's manifest (prakata) lila is arranged by Yogamaya. Because it is in
contact with mundane qualities, actions appear in it that cannot exist in the essential truth (svarupa-tattva) — the
slaying of asuras, “possessing the wives of others”, birth and the like. The gopis belong to Krishna's svarupa-shakti and
are therefore His own (svakiya); how could they be “wives of others”? Their “marriage to others” in the manifest lila is
only an appearance, a notion created by Yogamaya.[^25] If the hidden meaning of these words of Jiva is disclosed, says
Bhaktivinoda, no doubt will remain. <!-- F113 -->

According to Rupa and Sanatana, the unmanifest (aprakata) and the manifest lilas are not different: one is manifest beyond
the world, the other within it. In the transcendental manifestation both the seer and the seen are wholly pure. The full,
pure lila of Goloka can be seen and relished only by one who by great fortune and Krishna's mercy has left all connection
with the world and entered the spiritual world, and who moreover in the time of sadhana attained perfection in relishing
the variety of rasa — and such a one is rare. But one who, while still in this world, has by perfection in bhakti and
Krishna's mercy gained experience of the spiritual sees the lila of Goloka in the earthly lila of Gokula. Even here there is
a difference: until full perfection (vastu-siddhi) is reached, obstacles from maya remain in the vision of Goloka, and the
vision of devotees differs according to how far each has attained his spiritual form (svarupa-siddhi). Only those wholly
bound by maya lack the eyes of bhakti: some are fettered by its variety, others, relying on knowledge turned away from
Bhagavan, hope for “final annihilation”; even looking at the manifest lila, they see in it only the mundane, without any
connection with the unmanifest. <!-- F115 F116 F117 F118 -->

The subtle truth is that as Goloka is pure, so Gokula too is pure, without the slightest taint, though it is manifest in
the world by Yogamaya — the Lord's spiritual (chit) shakti. In neither the manifest nor the unmanifest lila is there any
taint of maya, any lowness or incompleteness; only according to the level of those who see does the lila appear otherwise.
Taint, lowness, limitation, maya, ignorance, impurity, emptiness, insignificance, coarseness — all this lies in the worldly
eyes, mind, reason and ego of the seer, not in what he sees; and the freer he is from these defects, the more he is able to
see the pure truth. The truth revealed in shastra is free from taint; only in those who discuss it, according to their
level, do notions “with taint” or “without it” arise. So too the sixty-four arts (chatuh-shashti-kala), spoken of earlier
in the “Prakashini”, exist originally and in pure form in Goloka, and the impression of lowness and coarseness comes only
from the level of those who discuss them. <!-- F119 F120 -->

According to Rupa and Sanatana, all kinds of lila manifest in Gokula exist in Goloka as well — in integral form, without a
whiff of maya. Therefore parakiya-bhava too must exist in Goloka in some inconceivably pure form. All that Yogamaya manifests
is pure; “marriage to others” is created by Yogamaya and must therefore have its basis in some pure truth — and that is
what must be investigated. Rupa in the “Ujjvala-nilamani” writes of the hero (nayaka): of his four kinds (dhirodatta and
the others) “two divisions are known here — pati and upapati”, the husband and the lover of another's wife. The pati takes
the maiden's hand in marriage; the upapati, out of passion (raga), seeking another's woman and counting her love as
everything, transgresses dharma. But the “lowness” spoken of here belongs to the mundane hero, not to Krishna, who has
descended to taste the essence of rasa; and what is said of heroines — that a married woman is unfit for the principal rasa
of drama — refers to mundane, petty heroines. Having examined these verses many times, Jiva recognised “marriage to others”
as a play of appearance (vibhrama-vilasa) created by Yogamaya, like the lilas of birth and the like; and yet he expressed
his deep intention in the words: “tathapi patih pura-vanitanam dvitiyo vraja-vanitanam” — “and yet for the wives of the
city He is the husband, and for the wives of Vraja the second”, that is, the upapati. The play of appearance created by
Yogamaya is accepted by Rupa and Sanatana too; but since Jiva affirmed the oneness of Goloka and Gokula, it must be admitted
that all the lilas of Gokula have an original, root truth. <!-- F121 F122 F123 F124 Q19 Q20 -->

In Goloka there is no dharma of the marriage bond, and hence no “husbandhood” of Krishna in that sense; and since the
gopis, who rest on His own svarupa, are not married to others, there is no “upapati-hood” of His either: there svakiya and
parakiya cannot be established separately. But in the manifest lila in the world the dharma of marriage exists, and
Krishna is above it; therefore the dharma that forms the circle of madhurya is arranged by Yogamaya, and by transgressing it
Krishna relishes parakiya-rasa. This lila of transgressing dharma is seen in the world only by people whose eyes are veiled
by the world; in truth there is no such lowness in Krishna's lila. Parakiya-rasa is the essence of all rasas; to say that it
is absent in Goloka is to belittle Goloka: it cannot be that in the highest abode the highest rasa is not relished.
Krishna, the source of the avataras, relishes it in one form in Goloka and in another in Gokula. Therefore, although
“marriage to others” looks to worldly eyes like a transgression of dharma, some truth of it exists in Goloka as well.
<!-- F125 F126 -->

The words of the “Bhagavatam” — “atmaramo ’py ariramat” (“though He rejoices in Himself, He enjoyed pastimes”), “atmany
avaruddha-sauratah” (“He held His amorous bliss within Himself”), “reme vraja-sundaribhir yatharbhakah
pratibimba-vibhramah” (“He played with the beauties of Vraja as a child plays with his reflection”) (Srimad-Bhagavatam,
10.29.42; 10.33.25; 10.33.16) — show that contentment in Himself (atmaramata) is Krishna's very nature. In the spiritual
world full of majesty (aishvarya) He manifests His shakti as Lakshmi and enjoys with the awareness “she is Mine”; because
this awareness is strong there, rasa flows only as far as dasya-rasa. But in Goloka He divides His shakti into hundreds and
thousands of gopis and, forgetting that they are His own, eternally enjoys with them. In the awareness “she is one's own”
rasa does not reach its utmost rarity; therefore the gopis have, from beginningless time, the natural awareness of being
“married to another” (parora-abhimana), and Krishna, following it, takes on the awareness of a lover (aupapatya) and, with
the help of His dear friend the flute, performs the rasa-lila and other pastimes. Goloka is the eternally perfect abode of
rasa, beyond worldly notions, and rasa flows there by this awareness (abhimana) alone. <!-- F127 F128 Q21 -->

So too vatsalya directed towards the source of the avataras is absent in Vaikuntha — such is the way of majesty; in Goloka
there is only the original awareness of this rasa. Nanda and Yashoda are present there, but there is no event of birth:
their fatherhood and motherhood are not a mundane fact but an awareness (abhimana), as it is said: “jayati jana-nivaso
devaki-janma-vadah” — “glory to Him who dwells in all, of whom it is said that He was born of Devaki” (Srimad-Bhagavatam,
10.90.48). This awareness is eternal for the sake of the perfection of rasa. If in shringara the awareness of being
“married” and the awareness of being “the lover” are likewise eternal, there is no defect in this and no contradiction of
shastra. When the truth of Goloka reveals itself in Vraja, both these awarenesses appear somewhat coarse to worldly vision:
in vatsalya the awareness of Nanda and Yashoda appears as the lila of Krishna's birth, in shringara the gopis' awareness of
being “married” appears as marriage to Abhimanyu, Govardhana and others. In truth the gopis have no husbands with separate
existence either in Goloka or in Gokula; therefore shastra says: “na jatu vraja-devinam patibhih saha sangamah” — “never do
the goddesses of Vraja have union with husbands”. <!-- F129 F130 Q22 Q23 -->

Therefore Rupa, the acharya of the teaching on rasa, names two kinds of hero in ujjvala-rasa — “patish chopapatish cheti
prabhedav iha vishrutau” — while Jiva, with the words “patih pura-vanitanam dvitiyo vraja-vanitanam”, acknowledges Krishna's
“husbandhood” in Vaikuntha, Dvaraka and other abodes and His eternal upapati-hood in Goloka and Gokula: the marks of the
upapati are fully seen in the Lord of Goloka and in the Lord of Gokula. Krishna, content in Himself, transgresses this
dharma of His out of raga — the longing to meet the “married” ones; and the gopis' “marriage” is their eternal awareness:
though they have no husbands with separate existence, this awareness makes them “others'” there. Therefore
“ragenollanghayan dharmam” (“transgressing dharma out of raga”) and the other marks of the upapati are eternally present in
the abode of madhurya, while in Vraja people with worldly eyes see them in a somewhat coarse form. <!-- F131 F132 -->

In Goloka between parakiya- and svakiya-rasa there is inconceivable difference and non-difference: one may say there is no
difference, and one may say there is no non-difference. The essence of parakiya lies in the cessation of the awareness of
svakiya, the essence of svakiya in the cessation of parakiya — that is, in enjoyment with the svarupa-shakti without the
rite of marriage; in this both are one rasa and the basis of both varieties. So also in Gokula — only those who see from the
world have a different notion. In Govinda, the hero of Goloka, “husbandhood” and upapati-hood, free from dharma and adharma,
abide in purity; in the hero of Gokula likewise, but Yogamaya creates a variety of notions. If it is asked: “Everything
Yogamaya manifests is the highest truth, created by the chit-shakti; then is the notion of «marriage to others» true just as
it is?” — the answer is this: in relishing rasa such an awareness may exist, and there is no defect in it, for it is not
groundless; the defect lies only in the low notion born of the worldly mind, and that cannot exist in the pure world.
<!-- F133 F134 -->

So, Bhaktivinoda concludes, Jiva drew his conclusion rightly, and the conclusion of the other side is also inconceivably
true; false and empty is only the barren worldly quarrel “svakiya-vada versus parakiya-vada”. Whoever impartially studies
the commentaries of Jiva and of the other side will have no doubts left. All that pure Vaishnavas say is true; there are no
parties among them, and in their “war of words” a mystery is hidden. People of worldly mind, devoid of pure Vaishnavism, do
not understand this loving mystery and ascribe partisanship to the Vaishnavas. So too Sanatana's explanation in the
“Vaishnava-toshani” of the verse of the rasa-panchadhyayi “gopinam tat-patinam cha” was accepted by Vishvanatha
Chakravarti without objection, with reverence. <!-- F135 F136 I16 -->

In every discussion of Goloka one must remember the teaching of Mahaprabhu and the Gosvamis: Bhagavat-tattva is always
varied with spiritual particularities beyond the mundane and is never impersonal; Bhagavan's rasa — with vibhava, anubhava,
sattvika- and vyabhichari-bhavas — is beautiful in its variety and abides eternally in Goloka and Vaikuntha. By the power of
Yogamaya the rasa of Goloka is manifest in the world for the good of the devotees and is perceived as vraja-rasa.
Everything seen in the rasa of Gokula exists clearly in Goloka too: the variety of lovers and relishers, land, water,
rivers, hills, houses and doors, kunjas, cows — all the belongings of Gokula exist in Goloka in integral form; only the
worldly notion of them is absent there. <!-- F137 F138 -->

In the manifold lila of Vraja a different revelation (sphurti) of Goloka opens to each according to his level (adhikara);
which parts of it come from maya and which are pure is hard to decide. The more the eyes of bhakti are washed by prema or
lined with its collyrium, the clearer the revelation. There is no point in arguing: argument does not raise one's level,
for the truth of Goloka is an inconceivable feeling (achintya-bhava); to seek it by speculation is like pounding empty
husks. One must give up the efforts of jnana and gain experience by the effort of bhakti, and reject everything that in the
end leads to an impersonal notion. Pure parakiya-rasa, free from the notions of maya, is extremely rare; it is described in
the lila of Gokula. Relying on it, let raganuga-bhaktas practise sadhana — and at the time of perfection they will attain
the still more desirable original truth. Thus the parakiya-bhava of Vraja proves to be no worldly liaison: the gopis'
“marriage” is an appearance created by Yogamaya, they have no husbands with separate existence either in Goloka or in
Gokula, and their awareness of being “others'” and Krishna's awareness of being their “lover” are eternal for the sake of
the highest rarity of rasa; it looks low only to worldly vision. <!-- F139 F140 I15 -->

## Srinivasa, Narottama, Shyamananda
When Srinivasa came to Vraja, Rupa, Sanatana, Kashishvara and Raghunatha Bhatta had already disappeared; Raghunatha Dasa,
Gopala Bhatta and Jiva were still living, and he received the blessing of all three.[^26] Jiva had already heard of him.
When Srinivasa bowed down, Jiva embraced him with great love, asked — himself a Bengali — about the welfare of the Gaudiya
Vaishnavas, and arranged prasada and lodging for him. Srinivasa arrived in the afternoon, on vaishakha-purnima; that day
his prasada was brought by Krishna Pandita, the servant of Govinda, and Srinivasa honoured it together with Jiva himself.
Srinivasa became “one of his own” (nija-jana) for Jiva, and in a letter Jiva calls him “mahattama” — “the greatest”.
<!-- F142 F143 F144 F145 -->

Jiva lovingly gave Srinivasa lodging in a secluded place, entrusted him to the feet of Sri Radha-Damodara, introduced him to
Lokanatha, Bhugarbha, Paramananda and Madhu Pandita, took him to Gopala Bhatta and let him see Radha-ramana. Later, at Gopala
Bhatta's instruction, Jiva bathed Srinivasa in the Yamuna and himself brought him to Gopala Bhatta, who gave him
initiation.[^27] The next day Srinivasa went with Jiva to Radha-kunda and saw Raghunatha Dasa, Raghava Gosvami and
Krishnadasa Kaviraja.[^28] So Srinivasa became the diksha disciple of Gopala Bhatta and the shiksha disciple of Jiva: it was
from him that he received instruction in the Vaishnava scriptures. <!-- F146 F147 F148 -->

Once Jiva was discussing a verse of the “Ujjvala-nilamani” on uddipana-vibhava — on what awakens loving feeling — and asked
Srinivasa its meaning. By Rupa's mercy the explanation was so beautiful that Jiva and the Vaishnavas were amazed and
admitted that without Rupa's mercy such an explanation would be impossible. With the consent of all the Vaishnavas Jiva
joyfully gave Srinivasa the title “Acharya”, and at Jiva's command he from then on taught the Vaishnavas of Vraja the
“Bhagavatam” and other shastras.[^29] <!-- F149 -->

Narottama too came, from Kheturi; Jiva sent him to serve the feet of Lokanatha Gosvami, and later gave him the title
“Thakura Mahashaya”. At Jiva's instruction Srinivasa and Narottama went round Vraja-mandala together with Raghava Gosvami,
the southern brahmana of Govardhana.[^30] Narottama became the favourite of the three Gosvamis then still living — Gopala
Bhatta, Jiva and Raghunatha Dasa; he lived on alms (madhukari) and under Jiva's guidance studied the works of the Gosvamis
deeply. <!-- F150 F151 F152 -->

Then Duhkhi Krishnadasa of Dandeshvara in Utkala, a disciple of Hridaya Chaitanya, came to Jiva.[^31] When he arrived,
Narottama and Srinivasa had just returned from going round the forests; Jiva was delighted and asked him about each of the
devotees of Bengal and Utkala. Hridaya Chaitanya had earlier himself asked Jiva: “I give you my disciple Duhkhi
Krishnadasa; teach him Krishna consciousness well and, when his mind has become steady, after thorough study send him back
to me”.[^32] In Vrindavana Duhkhi found out from local people where Jiva's bhajana-kutira was, came there, bowed down and
told about himself. Jiva gladly took him under his protection, and Duhkhi served him zealously and studied the scriptures
of the Gosvamis together with Srinivasa and Narottama. When he asked for a service that would bring him close to Jiva, Jiva
told him to carry water every day from Kanana-kunja, and this service filled his life with meaning and bliss.
<!-- F153 F154 F155 F156 F157 F158 -->

Jiva taught Duhkhi Krishnadasa the “Bhagavatam”, made him an acharya of the bhakti-shastras and entrusted him to Srinivasa.
He also gave him the name “Shyamananda” — “the joy of Shyama” — because he delighted Radha and Shyamasundara; seeing his
longing to become a maidservant of Radha, Jiva gave him the inner readiness for it. Tradition tells that once, while
sweeping the Rasa-mandala, Shyamananda found Radharani's anklet (nupura) and touched his forehead with it, and the mark
became the tilaka of his line — the “nupura-tilaka”.[^33] So service to the guru became Shyamananda's path to Radha's
mercy. <!-- F159 F160 F161 I8 -->

Although Shyamananda had received initiation from Hridaya Chaitanya — a disciple of Gauridasa Pandita who worshipped Gaura
and Nitai in sakhya-bhava — his guru himself entrusted him to Jiva's care. Through association with Jiva and service to him
Shyamananda acquired a taste for serving Radha and Krishna in madhurya-rasa; that is why he later initiated Rasikananda in
the worship of Radha and Krishna. There is no offence to the guru in this: sakhya is contained in madhurya, and the
disciple's growth only increases the teacher's glory. A few days after Hridaya Chaitanya, rebuked by Radha in a dream, had
been reconciled with his disciple and left Vraja, Jiva told Shyamananda to return to his homeland.[^34] <!-- F162 F163 I9 -->

For some time Srinivasa, Narottama and Shyamananda lived with Jiva as his shiksha disciples, receiving detailed personal
instruction; with his approval they arranged an order of kirtana (kirtana-paddhati). Jiva regaled all three with the rasa
of bhakti from the books of Rupa and Sanatana, his own, and those of Raghunatha Dasa, Gopala Bhatta and others, and soon
Shyamananda became as expert as the other two: training under Jiva made all three scholars in the philosophy and shastras
of Mahaprabhu. They lived on alms and performed bhajana as one heart.[^35] Later, in Navadvipa, Narottama told Shuklambara
Brahmachari that he had lived in Vrindavana under the care of Jiva and Lokanatha, and Shuklambara embraced him and asked
about the welfare and doings of the Gosvamis; and in Khardaha Jahnava and Vasudha, understanding that Narottama had
received the mercy of Lokanatha and Jiva, bestowed their own mercy on him. <!-- F164 F165 F166 -->

Everyone who came from Gauda or Utkala Jiva embraced, asked about all the devotees, and provided with prasada and lodging —
Bengali hospitality remained with him in Vraja too. <!-- I7 -->

## The Books Leave for Gauda
After Mahaprabhu's disappearance confusion set in in Gauda. In Prabhu's family there was no worthy successor; Virachandra,
being independent by nature, did not embrace the whole of Gauda; discord arose among Advaita's descendants; the Lord's
associates were departing one after another; and bauls, sahajiyas, darveshes, sais and others spread their own paths,
taking cover under the names of Chaitanya and Nityananda. Jiva was then the only Vaishnava acharya. Living in Vraja and
grieved by this news, he decided to make Srinivasa, Narottama and Shyamananda acharyas — restorers of pure bhakti in Gauda —
and to send the books of siddhanta there. So Jiva restored pure bhakti in Gauda not by himself but through his disciples,
training them and sending the books with them. The Gosvamis of Vraja, having discussed his proposal, decided to send all
three.[^36] <!-- F167 F168 I6 -->

Srila Bhaktisiddhanta writes in the “Anubhashya”: Jiva gave Srinivasa, Narottama and Duhkhi Krishnadasa, who had come from
Gauda, the names “Acharya”, “Thakura Mahashaya” and “Sri Shyamananda” and sent them to Gauda to preach nama-prema — the Holy
Name and love of Krishna — with all the books, his own and those of the other Gosvamis. <!-- F169 -->

Before the departure Jiva prayed with the Vaishnavas in the temple of Govinda: “O Govindadeva, give Srinivasa the strength
to preach the books”. At once a garland fell from Govinda's neck, and the pujari handed it to Srinivasa. Seeing this mercy,
Jiva and the other acharyas fixed the departure for aghrahayana shukla-panchami, the fifth lunar day of the bright fortnight
of the month of aghrahayana. This was in 1504 Shaka (1582–1583) — the same year in which Jiva completed the
“Laghu-toshani”.[^37] <!-- F170 F171 -->

Before many mahantas Jiva himself packed all the books into a wooden chest. A wealthy merchant of Mathura took upon himself
the cost of the cart, a chest proof against rain, and foot guards. Having bowed to the three principal Deities of
Vrindavana, the three disciples set out, and Jiva with a few companions accompanied the cart as far as Mathura; among those
who blessed them was Uddhava Dasa (“Bhakti-ratnakara”, 6.514). <!-- F172 -->

On the way the books were seized by the men of King Vira Hambira of Vana-Vishnupura. Later the king, hearing Srinivasa
expound the “Bhagavatam”, became a Vaishnava and took initiation from him, and Srinivasa got the books back. The news of
the loss, and then of the recovery, reached Jiva — it was sent with the drivers and the guards — and Jiva and the other
Gosvamis rejoiced greatly and marvelled at Srinivasa's deeds. After this Narottama went to preach in North Bengal and
Shyamananda in Orissa, so that the teaching of the books should spread through Bengal and Orissa; later Shyamananda,
returning to Vrindavana, found Srinivasa already with Jiva. <!-- F171 F173 F174 -->

Why does the “Chaitanya-charitamrita” name neither Srinivasa nor Narottama? Many answer thus: the Kaviraja finished the book
before their preaching began. Jiva sent the “Chaitanya-charitamrita” to Gauda with Srinivasa together with the other books
of the Gosvamis, and shortly before that Srinivasa had received from him the title “Acharya”. <!-- F175 -->

## Srinivasa's Second Visit
Srinivasa came to Vraja a second time when in Gauda Shuklambara, Gadadhara Dasa, Narahari and Dvija Haridasa had departed.
Gopala Bhatta, Jiva, Bhugarbha and Lokanatha encouraged him and softened his grief; Jiva was already waiting for him,
raised him up, embraced him tightly and asked about the Bengali Vaishnavas he had long not seen. At that time Shyamananda
too came from Puri; Jiva embraced him as well and asked about the devotees of Puri, and soon news came of Dvija Haridasa's
departure. <!-- F176 F177 -->

Srinivasa and Shyamananda lived with Jiva, and he taught them the conclusions of the “Shat-sandarbha”, which he had just
completed. At that time, says one of the biographies, Jiva began writing the “Gopala-champu” and read them its
mangalacharana — the opening verses.[^38] <!-- F178 F179 -->

Jiva gave the title “Kaviraja” to Srinivasa's disciple Ramachandra Sena and his younger brother Govinda Sena. Ramachandra
used to visit Vrindavana and associated with Jiva and the other Vaishnavas; Jiva liked his verses very much, and Ramachandra
became one of the eight kavirajas. The “Bhakti-ratnakara” relates:

“Sri Paramananda Bhattacharya, a treasury of prema, Sri Jiva Gosvami and other residents of Vrindavana — all, hearing from
his lips the verses he had composed, with great joy gave him the title «Kaviraja»”. (“Bhakti-ratnakara”, 1.267–268)[^39]
<!-- F180 Q24 -->

Ramachandra had been summoned from Gauda by Srinivasa, who then spent several months with Jiva and the other Gosvamis. He
was presented to Jiva; Jiva raised him up, embraced him and told him to visit Radha-ramana, Govinda, Gopinatha and the feet
of the Gosvamis, and the Gosvamis were very pleased with Ramachandra's humility. With the permission of Srinivasa and Jiva
he saw the Deities and the samadhi of Sanatana, at Jiva's command went round the forests of Vrindavana, and at Radha-kunda
saw Raghunatha Dasa and Krishnadasa Kaviraja. When Ramachandra visited the Radha-Damodara temple together with Jiva and saw
Rupa's samadhi, he went into an ecstasy that amazed everyone (“Bhakti-ratnakara”, 9th wave). After this, at Jiva's command,
Srinivasa and Shyamananda left for Gauda. <!-- F181 F182 F183 Q26 -->

From Vrindavana Jiva wrote letters to Srinivasa; the “Bhakti-ratnakara” (14th wave) and the “Prema-vilasa” preserve them.
In one he reports the departure of Bhugarbha and how the revision of the “Rasamrita-sindhu”, the “Madhava-mahotsava”, the
“Uttara-champu” and the “Hari-namamrita-vyakarana” is progressing, and asks about the welfare of Srinivasa's eldest son,
Vrindavana Dasa, and other disciples; the name “Vrindavana Dasa” had been given to the boy by Jiva himself as soon as news
of his birth reached Vraja. Another letter Jiva opens with the address
“samasta-guna-prashasta-bandhuvara-srinivasacharya-mahantameshu” — “to Srinivasa Acharya, most eminent, best of friends,
praised for every virtue” — and writes:

“He is now carrying there the revised and examined books «Vaishnava-toshani», «Durgama-sangamani» and «Sri Gopala-champu».
Therefore you should engage with him in the revision of the books and of their interpretation <…>. And also: if the
«Harinamamrita» grammar that was earlier sent to you is to be taught, then, consulting the bhashya, the vritti and so on,
the errors and the like should be corrected. <…> The «Sri Uttara-gopala-champu» is now written, but it still has to be
examined — this I report”. (“Prema-vilasa”, 25, letter 3) <!-- F184 -->

## Lord of the Sampradaya, and Achintya-bheda-abheda
At the beginning of the “Krama-sandarbha” and the “Sarva-samvadini” Jiva glorifies Gaurasundara as
“svasampradaya-sahasradhidaiva” — the Lord of thousands in his own sampradaya — and shows in particular that Sri Krishna
Chaitanya is the avatari, the source of the avataras, the purifier of the Age of Kali, and that the intelligent worship Him
by the sacrifice of sankirtana. The teaching that Sri Krishna Chaitanya proclaimed to the world, harmonising the whole
“Brahma-sutra” in its spiritual sense (chit-samanvaya) — achintya-bheda-abheda, “inconceivable oneness and difference” —
Jiva set down clearly in his books, especially in the “Sarva-samvadini”, where he preached it on the basis of an analysis
of Vedanta. <!-- F185 F186 -->

Envious and self-seeking people want to present Jiva as the founder of the teaching of achintya-bheda-abheda and the
teaching itself as new. The cause is their superficiality. Contrary to the sutra “tarka-apratishthanat” (“because reasoning
has no firm ground”, “Brahma-sutra” 2.1.11), they set about applying logic to the self-evident, transcendental,
inconceivable truth, which can be grasped only through the word of shruti, and therefore they do not understand the word
“achintya”. Thus in the month of magha of the year 1347 of the Bengali era[^40] the monthly of a certain “brand-new akhara of
avatara-vada” tried to pass off achintya-bheda-abheda as a new teaching — as a modified “anirvachaniya-vada” (the doctrine
of the indescribable) of the kevala-advaitins, that is, as the same advaita-vada under another name. <!-- F187 F188 -->

What does Jiva himself say? In the “Sarva-samvadini” on the “Bhagavat-sandarbha” he quotes a verse of the “Vishnu-purana”:

“The shakti of Vishnu is called the higher; higher also is the one called kshetrajna; and the other, called ignorance and
karma, is held to be the third shakti”. (“Vishnu-purana”, 6.7.61)

And he explains: “the shakti of Vishnu” is Vishnu's own (svarupa-bhuta) higher spiritual shakti. According to the
“Vishnu-purana” (6.7.53), the svarupa turned towards action is called “shakti”; shakti is the basis of the capacity to
create the world, and it is eternal; and since it cannot be defined as wholly separate from the svarupa, it is the
svarupa-shakti. The objection “there is only the thing itself — why shakti?” is not the view of the Vedantists (it is the
naiyayikas who do not accept shakti); moreover, the power of a thing, while the thing remains, can be checked by a mantra
and the like, so to deny shakti is against reason. Shakti cannot be thought of as identical with the svarupa — hence
difference is seen; nor as separate from it — hence non-difference is seen. So, Jiva concludes, “difference and
non-difference of the shakti and the Possessor of shakti are accepted, and both are inconceivable”. <!-- F189 F190 Q31 Q32 -->

In the “Sarva-samvadini” on the “Paramatma-sandarbha” Jiva examines this siddhanta in detail. Some, like Bhaskara, teach
difference and non-difference according to states — as of cause and effect, genus and individual: so clay and pot, bull
and cow. Others, the naiyayikas, teach difference and ascribe non-difference only to a “qualified” thing, interpreting
“tat tvam asi” otherwise. Still others, mindful of the sutra “tarka-apratishthanat” and seeing the flaws of both sides,
accept inconceivable difference and non-difference. The Puranikas and the Shaivas hold bheda-abheda, as does Bhaskara; for
the Mayavadis difference is only practical (vyavaharika) or apparent (pratitika); Gotama, Kanada, Jaimini, Kapila and
Patanjali stand for difference (bheda), and so do Ramanuja and Madhva.[^41] “But in our view,” says Jiva, “there is only
inconceivable difference and non-difference, for the Supreme Truth is full of inconceivable shakti”. <!-- F191 F192 Q33 -->

This teaching is not new. A brand-new, self-willed community without a sampradaya (asampradayika-bruva), stricken by the
hidden disease of slander, calls even the eternal truths — Krishna-bhakti and Krishna-tattva — “new”. In fact “our former
acharya, the eldest Vaishnava” Anandatirtha (Madhva) in his bhashya cites testimonies of the “Brahma-tarka”, an eternal
shastra composed by Vyasa — and from them achintya-bheda-abheda emerges as a beginningless siddhanta of shruti. The
“Brahma-tarka” says that in Janardana the whole and its parts, the bearer of qualities and the qualities, the Possessor of
shakti and the shakti, the doer and the deed, the whole and His own parts (svarupamsha) are eternally non-different; so also
in the jivas and in the spiritual prakriti; therefore He is spoken of as “without parts, without qualities, without action,
without limbs”. All this is possible through Vishnu's inconceivable shakti: by it are explained the eternity of action, the
difference between the manifest and the unmanifest, talk of being and non-being, the non-difference of a particularity and
its possessor; by the power of His shakti difference and non-difference coexist in the jivas and the spiritual prakriti; so
also the difference and non-difference of effect and cause, except the efficient cause. In the opinion of a modern
researcher, Jiva knew that the basis of his achintya-bheda-abheda lies in Madhva's “Bhagavata-tatparya”: at the beginning
of the “Shat-sandarbha” he says that he followed Madhva's “Tatparya” and accepted the texts quoted there — the
“Brahma-tarka” and others — as authoritative, though he could not study those sources themselves. <!-- F193 F194 F195 Q34 -->

Between “achintya” (inconceivable) and “anirvachaniya” (indescribable) there is a difference as between heaven and the
netherworld. “Anirvachaniya” rests on the impersonal teaching: what is beyond description is devoid of particularities. But
the inconceivable is not impersonal: it is directly grasped by spiritual perception; it is described by senses turned to
service and by a mind and speech obtained in spiritual samadhi; it is described on the levels of Vaikuntha and Goloka — but
it cannot be thought by a limited nature or by a mind seized by maya. That is why the satvata-shastras call Bhagavat-tattva
“achintya” and His svarupa-shakti “avichintya”. This siddhanta has been described from beginningless time in the Vedas, in
other shastras and in the words of Vyasa; Mahaprabhu spread it through His closest associates — Rupa, Sanatana and others —
and the best of the Rupanugas, Jiva, gave it special expression through Vedantic analysis and the arguments of shruti. Thus
achintya-bheda-abheda proves to be neither an innovation of Jiva's nor a variety of the “indescribable” of advaita: it is
eternal; Mahaprabhu completed with it the teachings of the earlier acharyas, and Jiva set it out with the arguments of
Vedanta. <!-- F196 F197 I17 -->

## Bhaktivinoda on Achintya-bheda-abheda
Bhaktivinoda Thakura explains this teaching as follows. Having studied the Vedas and Vedanta, the acharyas arrived at
conclusions of two kinds: Shankara, following the sages Dattatreya, Ashtavakra, Durvasa and others, preached kevala-advaita,
while the Vaishnava acharyas, following Narada, Prahlada, Dhruva, Manu and others, preached the truth of pure bhakti. There
are four kinds of bhakti-siddhanta. For Ramanuja, Ishvara is the one reality, endowed with two attributes, chit and achit;
for Madhva, the jiva is different from Ishvara, but bhakti to Ishvara is its nature; for Nimbaditya, the jiva is at once
different and non-different from Ishvara, and the difference too is eternal; for Vishnusvami, reality is one, but the state
of Brahman and the state of the jiva are eternally distinct. For all their differences, they all accept the eternity of
bhakti, the eternity of Bhagavan, the eternal service of the jiva and prema as the highest goal, and therefore all of them
are fundamentally Vaishnavas. <!-- F198 F199 -->

Vyasa's “Brahma-sutra” teaches parinama-vada — the doctrine of transformation — not vivarta-vada, the doctrine of
appearance. Shankara, thinking that with parinama “Ishvara is changeable”, twisted the meaning of the sutras and established
vivarta-vada. Both notions are defined in the “Vedanta-sara” of Sadananda Yogindra:

“A different cognition when the essence itself has changed is called vikara; a different cognition without a change of
essence is called vivarta”. (“Vedanta-sara”, 59)

Parinama is like milk becoming yogurt; vivarta is like a rope being taken for a snake. The scholars of Shankara's school
say: the world and the jiva cannot be a parinama of Ishvara, otherwise He would be changeable; the world does not exist,
it is only through ignorance taken for reality. <!-- F200 Q35 -->

But in Mahaprabhu's teaching there is no place for vivarta-vada. The example of rope and snake holds only for the jiva's
identification of himself with the body — that is vivarta; but the body itself is not false, and to say that Ishvara
became the body, the world or the svarupa of the jiva through vivarta is an unsound conclusion. Vyasa accepts parinama; to
reject it is to call the omniscient Vyasa mistaken. Ishvara's inconceivable shakti, by His will, transformed into the jivas
and matter, as milk into yogurt; but Ishvara (Brahman) Himself is not transformed, and the transformation of the shakti,
by its wondrous power, does not make Him changeable. A worldly example cannot fully show a transcendental truth, but it
illuminates one side of it: it is said that the chintamani stone produces heaps of jewels and remains unchanged; so too
Parameshvara, having by the mere will of His inconceivable shakti created a world of countless jivas and countless universes,
each with fourteen worlds, remains entirely unchanged. <!-- F201 F202 -->

“Unchanging” does not mean “only impersonal”. Brahman is always the form of Bhagavan, full of the six opulences; to call
Him only impersonal is to deny His chit-shakti. By His inconceivable shakti He is eternally both with particularities and
without them; to accept only the latter is to accept half of His nature. The shrutis describe three roles in Him — the
source (apadana), the instrument (karana) and the substratum (adhikarana):

“That from which these beings are born, by which, once born, they live, into which, departing, they enter — know That: it
is Brahman”. (“Taittiriya Upanishad”, 3.1)

These are His particularities, and therefore Bhagavan is always savishesha — full of particularities — and never merely
formless; His eternal transcendental form is sach-chid-ananda, full of the six opulences. So the “Chaitanya-charitamrita”
teaches as well:

“The direct meaning of the word «brahma» (Brahman) is Bhagavan, the Supreme Lord. The unsurpassed and incomparable Lord is
full of all spiritual perfections”. (CC, Adi 7.111) <!-- F203 Q36 Q38 -->

Jiva says in the “Bhagavat-sandarbha” (16): the Supreme Truth is one, but by Its natural inconceivable shakti It always
abides in four aspects — as the svarupa, as its vaibhava (splendour), as the jivas and as pradhana — like the radiance within
the sun, the solar disc, the rays issuing from it, and their distant reflection. The svarupa is a form made wholly of
sach-chid-ananda; the spiritual abode, the name, the associates and all the paraphernalia of the pastimes are the
svarupa-vaibhava; the countless eternally liberated and eternally conditioned souls are the jivas; maya, pradhana and all
the worlds made of it are “pradhana”. How can what is eternally opposed coexist in one Truth? For the limited mind of the
jiva it is impossible, but for the inconceivable shakti of Parameshvara it is possible. <!-- F204 Q37 -->

In the “Sarva-samvadini” Jiva named this teaching “achintya-bheda-abheda”. The bheda-abheda (dvaita-advaita) of Nimbarka
did not reach completeness; the Vaishnava world attained it on receiving Mahaprabhu's teaching. Mahaprabhu accepted the
sampradaya of Madhva because the eternal sach-chid-ananda form acknowledged by Madhva is the root of
achintya-bheda-abheda. The conclusions of all the earlier Vaishnava acharyas lacked something, and from this lack arose the
differences between the sampradayas. Sri Chaitanya, the Supreme Truth Himself, by His omniscience supplied what was missing
— Madhva's “eternal form of sach-chid-ananda”, Ramanuja's “doctrine of shakti”, Vishnusvami's “shuddhadvaita” and
“belonging wholly to Him” (tadiya-sarvasvatva), Nimbarka's “eternal dvaita-advaita” — making them flawless and complete,
and out of mercy gave the world His own most pure and rigorously grounded teaching of achintya-bheda-abheda. <!-- F205 F206 -->

According to this siddhanta, the difference and non-difference of the jiva from Krishna and of the world from Krishna are
true simultaneously. Limited human reason cannot reconcile them, and so this eternal principle is called “inconceivable”.
Yet it is not contrary to reason: for Bhagavan, who possesses inconceivable shakti, it is reasonable, and what is
established by this shakti is truth, obtained by mercy. The ancients taught not to apply logic to the inconceivable, for
argument about it never becomes proof; for those who forget this there is no end of misfortune. <!-- F207 -->
