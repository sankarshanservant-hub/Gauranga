# Jiva Gosvami
*Jiva Gosvami · @jiva-gosvami*

## One of the Six Gosvamis
In the journal “Gaudiya” (year 1, no. 4) Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati explained what three titles of honour mean.
Everyone knows Sri Chaitanya as Mahaprabhu, while Nityananda and Advaita, whom He honoured, are known as Prabhu; and those
are called Gosvamis who are poets that love Him, are dear to Him and left their homes for His sake. There were many
Gosvamis in Vrindavana, but six are glorified everywhere, and Jiva is one of them. All his life he aspired to be nothing
more than a follower (anuga) of Sri Rupa; Sanatana was his parama-guru, and the One he worshipped was Sri Chaitanya-chandra,
who for the pure vision of the Gaudiyas is not different from Vrajendra-nandana Himself. From his boyhood Jiva displayed
the lila of a lifelong celibate (naishthika-brahmachari); he was a brihad-vrati, a keeper of the great vow, and he spent
his whole life in the company of Sarasvati, the goddess of speech, full of spiritual play. That is why he is called the
crown of the Gaudiya Vaishnava acharyas. <!-- F1 F2 F3 -->

Bhaktivinoda Thakura wrote in the “Sajjana-toshani” (year 2) that a Vaishnava has only to hear the name of Jiva Gosvami
for his heart to dance with joy. And Srila Bhaktisiddhanta, in his article “Toshanir katha” in the same “Sajjana-toshani”,
by then published under his editorship (year 18, no. 5), says that it is only by Jiva's boundless mercy that the
Rupanuga-bhakti-dharma — the love of Krishna preached by Mahaprabhu — still brings endless good to all living beings in the
world. Jiva did not write a single book in Bengali, yet Krishnadasa Kaviraja, the best of the Rupanugas, drew a number of
conclusions from his “Sandarbhas” for the “Chaitanya-charitamrita” and so increased the joy of all who learn bhakti.
<!-- F4 F5 F6 -->

In the same place Srila Bhaktisiddhanta names the two original gurus (mula-gurus) of the Rupanugas — Jiva and Raghunatha
Dasa Gosvami. Raghunatha Dasa is the acharya of the path on which living taste (ruchi) decides everything: he leads
fortunate souls along the easy road of bhajana. Jiva, the supreme knower of transcendental rasa, took care of those in
whom ruchi has not yet awakened: he preserved the wealth of the sampradaya through the ordinances of vaidhi-marga, the
path of rules, and left no one any room to doubt the transcendental greatness of his guru. So the Rupanugas stand on two
pillars: one guides those in whom taste has already awakened, the other guards the path of rules for those in whom it
has not yet. <!-- F7 I5 -->

## Years of His Life
It is impossible to say for certain when Jiva appeared: several dates of his appearance and departure have been found in
different records.[^1] Those most often cited come from the note “Determining the Years of the Six Gosvamis”, which
Bhaktivinoda Thakura printed in the “Sajjana-toshani” (year 2, no. 2) from the records of a certain Vaishnava. According
to them Jiva appeared in 1455 of the Shaka era (about 1533 CE) and departed in 1540 Shaka (about 1618); he spent
eighty-five years on earth — twenty at home and sixty-five in Vrindavana. An old manuscript from the collection of the
late Vishvambharananda Deva Gosvami, the pandita of the shripata of Gopivallabhapura, gives the same figures, and other
researches arrive at them too. Yet Bhaktivinoda himself remarked that some dates in his source seem certain and others
doubtful, and that by this record Jiva would have been born in the very year of Mahaprabhu's disappearance, which is
hardly correct.[^2] <!-- F8 F9 F10 F11 F16 -->

According to our Vaishnava calendar, the appearance day of Srila Jiva Gosvami is Gaura-dvadashi of the month of
Hrishikesha — the same day on which the appearance of Sri Vamanadeva is celebrated. His departure day, honoured by
Vaishnavas all over the world as supremely auspicious, is Gaura-tritiya of the month of Narayana.[^3] <!-- F17 F18 -->

## Lineage
Out of deep humility Rupa and Sanatana called themselves men of low birth. Sanatana says to Mahaprabhu:

“I took birth in a truly godless family, in which sin and the breaking of God's commandments were an everyday matter”.
(CC, Antya 4.28)

And in Ramakeli the brothers confessed to the Lord:

“We have defiled our birth by low deeds and by association with base people. We are unworthy even to stand before You”.
(CC, Madhya 1.189) <!-- Q1 Q2 -->

Pretended scholars of coarse understanding failed to grasp this lila of humility played by the teachers of the world. As
they had earlier taken Mahaprabhu for a “Mayavadi sannyasi”, so they took the Lord's eternal associates, Rupa and
Sanatana, for men of low birth — and sank into offence. Had Jiva not mercifully given in his writings true information
about his predecessor gurus and his family, people would have remained in that error. He set out the genealogy at the
end of the “Laghu-toshani”, his commentary on the Tenth Canto of the “Bhagavatam”; Narahari Chakravarti also gives it, seven
generations deep, in the “Bhakti-ratnakara”. <!-- F19 F20 F21 I1 Q3 -->

This genealogy is told in detail in the article on Rupa Gosvami; here it is given briefly, together with what is not found
there.[^4] The founder of the line was Sarvajna “Jagad-guru”, king of Karnata — a renowned sovereign, the best of the
Bharadvaja line, honoured by assemblies of kings. A brahmana of the Yajur-veda and a master of all the shastras, he earned
the name “teacher of the world” by his unrivalled learning: all the brahmanas of Karnata revered him, and students came to
him from many lands. Jiva's verse says that his tongue, rich in beautiful turns of speech, “danced like a bee” without
tiring among the three wish-fulfilling creepers — the three Vedas. His son Aniruddha-deva was handsome, like the sage
Kashyapa; in fame he rivalled the moon, in might Indra; all kings honoured him, and in the Yajur-veda he was the one
refuge of all. <!-- F22 F23 F24 F25 -->

Of his two queens Aniruddha had two sons: Rupeshvara, accomplished in many shastras, and Harihara, skilled in warfare. On
the day of his departure (the verse says, “setting out for the city of Madhuripu”) the father divided the kingdom between
them, but the younger, Harihara, seized his elder brother's share. Rupeshvara and his wife went on eight horses to an
eastern land and lived happily in the domain of his friend Shikhareshvara; there a son, Padmanabha, was born to him. On
Padmanabha's tongue the entire Yajur-veda with its angas and all the Upanishads resounded clearly, and his heart melted
with love for Jagannatha.[^5] Wishing to live on the bank of the Ganga, he settled, with honours shown him by King
Danujamardana, in Navahatta (now Naihati), and there worshipped the Deity of Sri Purushottama with sacrifices and
festivals. He had eighteen daughters and five sons: the eldest Purushottama, the second Jagannatha, steadfast Narayana,
Murari rich in virtues, and the youngest, the renowned Mukunda. <!-- F26 F27 F28 F29 -->

In Navahatta a son, Kumara-deva, the best of brahmanas, was born to Mukunda. When religious strife broke out in Naihati,
the pious Kumara-deva left for Bengal and settled in Bakla-Chandradvipa, and he also made himself a dwelling in
Fatehabad (then in the Jessore district), between Naihati and Bakla.[^6] Of his sons, three were dearest of all to the
best Vaishnavas. Jiva writes of them (the “Laghu-toshani”, in the translation of the verses quoted by Narahari
Chakravarti):

“The first is Srila Sanatana, his younger brother named Sri Rupa, then [the youngest] bearing the name Sriman Vallabha;
turning away from royal power and obtaining the great mercy of Bhagavan Sri Krishna Chaitanya, they truly enjoyed the
kingdom of the wealth of bhakti called prema for Murahara.

He who was the youngest of all — my father — soon attained Sri Rama on the Ganga, while the elder [brothers] then came to
Vrindavana; by them the hidden tirthas of Mathura were revealed, and bhakti for Sri Vrajaraja-nandana was raised high
everywhere”. (“Bhakti-ratnakara”, 1.784–785) <!-- F30 F31 F32 F33 F49 -->

Jiva's father was Vallabha, the youngest of the three brothers, and Jiva was his only son. The genealogical table does not
name Kumara-deva's other children: it is known only that there were some. It is “said” that after Kumara-deva's
departure the three brothers lived and studied in the house of their maternal uncle in the hamlet of Sakurma near the
capital of Gauda, and that later the two elder ones became ministers of Hussain Shah with the titles “Sakara Mallik” and
“Dabir Khas”. <!-- F32 F38 F39 -->

Further on in the “Laghu-toshani” Jiva glorifies Raghunatha Dasa — the friend of Rupa and Sanatana, renowned on earth, who
whirls forever in the waves of the great ocean of love for Radha and Krishna; the best of the worthy, to their own
amazement, honour him as equal to both brothers. He also tells how Gopala Himself, in the guise of a cowherd boy, brought
the brothers milk. Then Jiva lists the books of Rupa[^7] and of Sanatana: the “Sri Bhagavatamrita”, the
“Hari-bhakti-vilasa” with the commentary “Dig-darshini”, the “Lila-stava”, and the commentary on the Tenth Canto itself —
the “Vaishnava-toshani” (“Dashama-tippani”); and to abridge this book, he says, Sanatana ordered “an insignificant jiva”,
that is, Jiva himself. He asks forgiveness for what he wrote in haste, with or without understanding, and for the places
where he left out his teacher's explanations: “And if I have written only what he himself awakened in my heart, I have
nothing to fear from doubters”.[^8] <!-- F34 F35 F36 F37 Q4 Q5 -->

## His Father, Anupama
Jiva appeared in Bakla-Chandradvipa.[^9] His mother's name is not known. <!-- F40 F41 -->

His father was originally called Vallabha; the name “Anupama” was given to him by Mahaprabhu. For his service under the
badshah of Gauda he, like his brothers, bore the title “Mallik”. The “Chaitanya-charitamrita” says:

“Sri Rupa's brother was also a great Vaishnava. His name was Sri Vallabha, but he was better known as Anupama Mallik”.
(CC, Madhya 19.36) <!-- F42 Q7 -->

And in his list of the branches of the tree of bhakti Krishnadasa Kaviraja names father and son together:

“Sri Rupa and Sri Sanatana are a great branch, and Sri Anupama, Sri Jiva Gosvami, Rajendra and other devotees are twigs on
those branches”. (CC, Adi 10.85)

What kind of devotee Anupama was, Sanatana himself tells Mahaprabhu:

“My younger brother Anupama was from his very childhood a great devotee of Ramachandra. He worshipped Him with
determination and devotion in his heart. Day and night he chanted the holy name of Rama and meditated on His eternal
form, and constantly heard and recounted the narratives of the sacred «Ramayana». <…>

After these words he could not sleep all night, thinking: «How can I give up the lotus feet of Lord Ramachandra?» He
wept the whole night through, and in the morning came to us with a humble request: «I have sold my head, offering it to
the lotus feet of Ramachandra. I cannot take it back. The very thought of it brings me unbearable pain. Do not be so
strict with me. Please show your mercy. Allow me, life after life, to serve the lotus feet of Ramachandra. I have no
strength to reject the service of Ramachandra's lotus feet. The very thought of it tears my heart to pieces». Hearing
these words, we embraced him and began to praise him: «You are a true saint, for your devotion to God is firm and
unshakeable»”. (CC, Antya 4.30–31, 38–43)

Anupama lived inseparably with his brothers and with them heard talks about Krishna and the “Bhagavatam”. The brothers
wanted to test him and urged him to worship the all-attractive Krishna together with them; out of respect for them he
agreed to ask for initiation — and then came what Sanatana described. Hearing this story, Mahaprabhu compared Anupama
with Murari Gupta, who was just as firmly devoted to Rama. <!-- F43 F44 Q6 -->

Anupama first saw Mahaprabhu when the Lord came to Ramakeli.[^10] By His will renunciation awoke in Rupa and Sanatana, and
soon the brothers left everything to seek His company. When Rupa, displaying the lila of abandoning worldly affairs, set
out for Vrindavana and the feet of Mahaprabhu, Anupama went with him. In Prayaga both received the Lord's darshana in the
house of a southern brahmana and at His command went on to Vrindavana. Subuddhi Raya, who was then selling dry firewood in
Mathura and feeding himself and other Vaishnavas on the proceeds, went round the twelve forests of Vrindavana with them.
After a month the brothers, looking for Sanatana, went along the Ganga to Prayaga, then to Kashi, heard Mahaprabhu's
teachings there and ten days later left for Gauda. All this is told in detail in the articles on Rupa and Sanatana.
<!-- F45 F46 F47 -->

Having settled their worldly affairs in Gauda, both went to Nilachala at Mahaprabhu's command, and on the way, on the bank
of the Ganga, Anupama attained the abode of Sri Ramachandra.[^11] Jiva himself, as we have seen, writes of this in the
“Laghu-toshani”: “my father soon attained Sri Rama on the Ganga”. <!-- F48 F49 -->

[^1]: A manuscript from the library of the venerable pandita Banamali Lala Gosvami, who lived outside the outer wall of
    the Radha-ramana temple in Vrindavana, gives 1580 Samvat as the year of appearance and 1665 Samvat as that of “attaining
    the Ishtadeva” (departure) — that is, 1445 and 1530 Shaka, about 1523 and 1608 CE; by it Jiva lived twenty-four years
    at home, studying, and eighty-five years in all, so that at the time of Mahaprabhu's disappearance he was ten years
    old. One of the biographies, citing the “Gaudiya-vaishnava-abhidhana”, gives 1511–1596 (1433–1518 Shaka): the same
    length of life, but 22 years earlier. Our own observation: only this, the earliest dating, agrees with the tradition
    of the “Bhakti-ratnakara” that little Jiva secretly saw Mahaprabhu in Ramakeli (see below); by the other dates Jiva
    would have been born nine or nineteen years after that visit.

[^2]: In the records printed by Bhaktivinoda and in Vishvambharananda's manuscript, pausha shukla-tritiya is called the day
    of “appearance” (in Bhaktivinoda, with a question mark). Probably “appearance” was put in place of “departure” in
    printing or copying: everywhere this day is known as the day of Jiva's departure.

[^3]: One of the biographies gives as the day of appearance the twelfth lunar day of the bright fortnight of the month of
    bhadra (bhadra shukla-dvadashi), and as the day of departure the third lunar day of the bright fortnight of pausha
    (pausha shukla-tritiya); all other testimonies about the departure agree. The month of Hrishikesha in our calendar
    roughly corresponds to bhadra, and the month of Narayana to pausha, so there is no discrepancy.

[^4]: One of the biographies also gives years: Sarvajna was king of Karnata from 1381.

[^5]: The Bengali retelling of the verse calls Padmanabha a king as well.

[^6]: The verse says that Kumara-deva left “having suffered enmity”; the retelling, “because of a rebellion”.

[^7]: The list of Rupa's books according to the “Laghu-toshani” is given in the article on him. In the Bengali retelling of
    one of the biographies the “Stava-mala” stands in place of the “Utkalika-vallari” named in the verse.

[^8]: In the verse itself forgiveness is asked in the plural — “may they forgive”; the Bengali retelling addresses these
    words to Sanatana.

[^9]: According to another biography, following Srila Bhaktisiddhanta's “Anubhashya” (on CC, Adi 10.84), the three
    brothers moved for the sake of their service from Fatehabad to Ramakeli (Malda district), and there Jiva was born. The
    same biography, following the “Bhakti-ratnakara”, says that Jiva left for Navadvipa from his home in
    Bakla-Chandradvipa.

[^10]: One of the biographies gives the year — 1513.

[^11]: One of the biographies gives the year — 1436 Shaka (1514–1515). But by the course of events in the
    “Chaitanya-charitamrita” — Rupa went to Vrindavana after the Lord had returned from there, spent a month in Vraja, then
    came Kashi and Gauda — Anupama's departure apparently falls in 1437 Shaka.
