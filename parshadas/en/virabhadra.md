# Virachandra (Virabhadra) Prabhu
*Вирачандра (Вирабхадра) Прабху · @virabhadra*

## The Great Branch
Sri Virachandra Prabhu is the son of Nityananda Prabhu and Vasudha. Krishnadasa Kaviraja calls him Virabhadra Gosani;
the “Gaura-ganoddesha-dipika” and Narahari Chakravarti call him Virachandra. The “Bhakti-ratnakara” itself says that
some call him Virabhadra and others Virachandra (9.420). The names Virabhadra Goswami, Prabhu Virachandra and
Birachandra are also found, and one Vaishnava song extols him as Mahaprabhu Virachandra. <!-- F1 -->

Listing the branches of Nityananda's tree, the author of the “Charitamrita” names him first, right after the trunk itself:

“Sri Virabhadra Gosani is the great branch of the trunk, and the offshoots that spring from Him are beyond counting.
Though He is the Lord, He calls Himself a great devotee; though He is beyond the dharma of the Vedas, He keeps faithfully
to it. Within, He acts as the Lord; outwardly, He shows no pride. He is the main pillar in the hall of devotion to Sri
Chaitanya. Even today, through the glory of His mercy, the whole world sings of Chaitanya and Nityananda. The feet of
that Virabhadra Gosani are my shelter, for by His grace every desire is fulfilled.” (CC, Adi 11.8–12)[^1]

And closing the list, Kaviraja Goswami says again:

“Virabhadra Gosani is the best of all the branches, and His offshoots are without end.” (CC, Adi 11.56)
<!-- F7 F8 Q1 Q2 -->

These verses hold his whole portrait. He is Ishvara Himself, and yet he conducts himself as a devotee: he honours the
Vedic injunctions though he stands above them, and outwardly shows not a trace of greatness. This is neither pretence
nor the self-effacement of an ordinary man, but the Lord's play in the mood of a servant: in this way He teaches
humility and shows that devotion stands higher than lordship. That is why Kaviraja calls him the main pillar on which
the hall of devotion to Sri Chaitanya rests, and why shelter at his feet fulfils every desire of a devotee: by his
mercy the whole world sings the names of Chaitanya and Nityananda. <!-- I1 I2 -->

## Who He Is
Kavi Karnapura reveals his nature thus:

“The *vyuha* of Sankarshana, called the One who lies on the Ocean of Milk, became Virachandra, whose form is
non-different from Chaitanya; into him, for the sake of the work, entered the brothers Nishatha and Ulmuka.”
(“Gaura-ganoddesha-dipika” 67–68)

Nishatha and Ulmuka are sons of Balarama. Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati cites this verse in his “Anubhashya” on CC,
Adi 11.8, and the “Bhakti-ratnakara” cites it in the account of his marriage (13.258). “Non-different from Chaitanya”
means that he is *vishnu-tattva*, the Lord Himself; so the biographies explain it, too. <!-- F2 F6 Q3 -->

Srila Bhaktivinoda Thakura writes in his “Amrita-pravaha-bhashya” on CC, Adi 11.9 that Virachandra Prabhu is this
*vyuha* of Sankarshana, Payobdhishayi, and that, though the Lord Himself, he regarded himself as a Vaishnava; one of the
biographies says the same in almost the same words.[^2] The biographies unfold the thought: Nityananda is non-different
from Baladeva; a portion of Baladeva is the Mahasankarshana of Vaikuntha; His third *purusha-avatara* is Kshirodakashayi
Vishnu, the maintainer of each individual universe, who is also Aniruddha, the Indweller in the heart of every living
being[^3] — and this Lord appeared as the son of Nityananda and Vasudha. Srila Bhaktisiddhanta teaches that Vasudha's son
is eternally known as Sri Virabhadra Prabhu, Kshirodakashayi Vishnu, the Vishnu of an individual universe
(*vyashti-Vishnu*), eternally manifest in Goloka. <!-- F3 F4 -->

Nityananda's pastimes are eternal, Srila Bhaktisiddhanta continues, and so is Virabhadra's appearance; there is nothing
material in them, and one who relies on worldly judgement will not see Nityananda. He and His eternal associates can be
understood only by the mercy of the guru, not by the worldly intellect. <!-- F5 I5 Q10 -->

Virachandra's divine opulences, one biography says, are non-different from those of Nityananda. And like every
*vishnu-tattva* he has three *shaktis*: according to one biography, Shrimati, Narayani and the *lila-shakti*; according
to another, Shri, Bhu (Bhakti) and Nila (Lila); his Shri-shakti is Shrimati, of whom more will be said below.
<!-- F9 F10 -->

## Appearance and Family
Virachandra is the son of Nityananda and Vasudha; Jahnava had no children. One of the biographies says that he appeared
taking shelter of Vasudha, Nityananda's *shakti*, and Srila Bhaktisiddhanta in the “Anubhashya” calls Vasudha
Nityananda's “other wife.” According to the “Bhakti-ratnakara” (9.413–414), Virabhadra is Nityananda's only son, the
purifier of the world, renowned as His branch.[^4] <!-- F12 -->

His sister is Ganga-devi. Virabhadra and Ganga are honoured as descents of Kshirodakashayi Vishnu and of the holy river
Ganga, and Ganga's husband Madhavacharya is called King Shantanu by the “Gaura-ganoddesha-dipika”: “The Ganga, born from
the foot of Vishnu, was born under her own name as the daughter of Nityananda; her husband Madhava is King Shantanu”
(69). At her brother's wedding, says the “Bhakti-ratnakara” (13.261), Ganga-devi rejoiced. <!-- F13 -->

The day of his appearance is the *krishna-navami* of the month of Kartika; in the calendar of the Sri Chaitanya Saraswat
Math it is the Krishna-navami of the month of Damodara, “the Appearance Day of Srila Virachandra Prabhu” (in 2026,
3 November). On the same *tithi* Srila Bhakti Rakshaka Sridhar Deva Goswami Maharaj appeared in this world. No source
names the year of his appearance.[^5] <!-- F14 -->

## Disciple of Jahnava Mata
Virachandra received initiation from Jahnava, his mother and guru; so say the “Anubhashya” and all the biographies.
According to the “Prema-vilasa,” he once saw Jahnava in a four-armed form; his heart was changed, and he resolved to
accept her as his *diksha-guru*.[^6] <!-- F23 Q7 -->

When Jahnava returned from Vraja, Nityananda's son and daughter welcomed her in Khardaha. “Ganga and Virachandra bowed
with great joy at Jahnava's feet,” says the “Bhakti-ratnakara,” “and looking at their faces she shed tears of
tenderness” (11.782–783); she embraced them and, as a mother does, smelled their heads, and then told Vasudha and
Virabhadra about the journey she had made. <!-- F45 -->

## Shrimati and Narayani
Jahnava also saw to his marriage. The “Bhakti-ratnakara” (13.249–267) tells it so: near Rajbal-hat lies the village of
Jhamatpur (Hooghly district). Jahnava came there, to the temple of one of her servant-disciples, the brahmana
Yadunandana Acharya, who by her mercy had become filled with bhakti. His wife Lakshmi was chaste and devoted to her
husband, and their daughters Shrimati and Narayani were of matchless beauty; at Jahnava's wish this fortunate brahmana
gave both to Virabhadra. At the wedding Virachandra made Yadunandana his disciple, and Jahnava accepted Shrimati and
Narayani as her disciples. Narahari Chakravarti calls the bridegroom here the greatly effulgent son of Nityananda, whose
form is non-different from Chaitanya, the enchanter of the worlds — and at once cites Kavi Karnapura's verse about him
(13.257–258); “who does not sing of Virachandra's deeds?” he exclaims (13.266). “Jahnava Ishvari married her son,” the
“Bhakti-ratnakara” sums up (13.267). <!-- F15 Q5 F64 -->

Srila Bhaktisiddhanta in the “Anubhashya” calls the father-in-law Yadunatha Acharya of Jhamatpur: Virabhadra married his
daughter Shrimati, born of Vidyunmala (Lakshmi), and their adopted daughter Narayani. On Narayani's parents the sources
differ.[^7] But both are his *shaktis*, and this marriage is no worldly event but part of the pastimes in Nityananda's
house, arranged by Jahnava Mata. <!-- Q6 -->

After the wedding Virachandra returned to Khardaha. Vasudha, seeing her daughters-in-law, was filled with joy, and all
Khardaha rejoiced (“Bhakti-ratnakara” 13.259–260). <!-- F16 -->

## Srinivasa and Narottama in Khardaha
When Srinivasa, before leaving for Vrindavana, was visiting the homes of the Lord's associates, Parameshvari Dasa led
him in Khardaha to Vasudha, Jahnava and Virachandra. “Sri Vasu[dha] and Jahnava, together with Virabhadra, were very
pleased to see Srinivasa,” says the “Bhakti-ratnakara” (4.88); Srinivasa received the mercy of both mothers and the
blessing of Nityananda's son, and “Sri Jahnava, the Prabhu [Virabhadra] and the others, their hearts aching, ordered
him to go to Vrindavana” (4.92). <!-- F46 -->

And when Srinivasa came back once more from Vrindavana to Gauda, “Prabhu Virachandra showed him great mercy, and day and night he
was absorbed in the rasa of sankirtana” (“Bhakti-ratnakara” 1.885). <!-- F47 -->

Narottama, too, visited Khardaha. He found Vasudha and Jahnava with Virabhadra speaking of the Lord's pastimes, and
Virabhadra spoke with him very sweetly (“Bhakti-ratnakara” 8.210–218). The “Narottama-vilasa” adds: “He bowed at the
lotus feet of Sri Virabhadra and on seeing him was overwhelmed with prema”; “Sri Vasudha and Jahnava together with
Virachandra could not let Narottama go even for a moment” (3.189, 195). <!-- F48 -->

## Festivals in Kantaka-nagara and Shrikhanda
The ninth *taranga* of the “Bhakti-ratnakara” tells of festivals that Virachandra attended together with Srinivasa and
other associates. In Kantaka-nagara (Katwa), where Mahaprabhu accepted sannyasa, a great festival went on for three days
in the month of Kartika — on the *krishna-saptami*, *ashtami* and *navami* (9.450–456). Here the “Bhakti-ratnakara”
glorifies him: the only son of Nityananda, purifier of the world, the one whom some call Virabhadra and others
Virachandra; Narahari also cites the verses of the “Charitamrita” about him and adds: “whoever sees such a Virachandra
even once will give up everything and make his feet the essence of his life” (9.413–421). The people of
Kantaka-nagara, gazing at “Nitai's son,” said that before such beauty even Madana himself could not stay composed
(9.422–425): the form of Nityananda's son eclipses all the beauty of this world. His dancing was wonderful (9.453).
<!-- F50 Q4 F63 -->

Then came Shrikhanda and the festival of Narahari Sarakara Thakura's departure. Srinivasa performed the rites; he
expounded the “Bhagavatam,” and Virachandra sat on a wonderful seat and listened (9.532). All night Virabhadra danced in
sankirtana, and on the *dvadashi* there was a great festival. Raghunandana anointed him with sandal paste and adorned him
with garlands, and Srinivasa, at Virachandra's sign, brought him garland and sandal, and he himself joyfully
placed them on Raghunandana (9.593–596). When he was dancing in sankirtana, a blind man was there; learning that the
dancer's name was Virabhadra (“*bhadra*, because he destroys evil”), he prayed for mercy — and received his sight
(9.616–626). And at parting Raghunandana took him by both hands and, in tears, spoke with him for a long time (9.731).
<!-- F49 -->

## The Journey to Vrindavana
The thirteenth *taranga* of the “Bhakti-ratnakara” describes his journey to Vrindavana. Having received his mother's
permission, he set out from Khardaha with his companions at an auspicious moment (13.283–284). Some biographies say it
was Vasudha who permitted him, others that he went at his mother's bidding and with her blessing; which mother, the
“Bhakti-ratnakara” does not specify. <!-- F24 -->

The road led through the homes of the Lord's associates. In Saptagrama, at the house of Uddharana Datta, Uddharana's son
Srinivasa Datta Thakura received him respectfully; Virachandra spent two days there absorbed in kirtana — “in the house of
a most virtuous merchant,” as the “Bhakti-ratnakara” says — and bestowed bhakti on the fallen (13.284–286). In
Shantipur, at Advaita's house, Advaita's son Sri Krishna Mishra welcomed him warmly; there was sankirtana, all were
drowned in the sweet waves of the holy name, and Virachandra spoke with Krishna Mishra in private (13.286–288). In
Ambika-Kalna, at the house of Gauridasa Pandita, Hridaya Chaitanya received him with great love (13.288). In Navadvipa,
at the house of Jagannatha Mishra, he spent two days; the followers of Sri Chaitanya, seeing Nityananda's son, rejoiced
and received him joyfully (13.289–290). <!-- F25 F26 F27 F28 -->

In Shrikhanda Kanai Thakura, the son of Raghunandana, came out to meet him, and Virachandra stayed there for some
time.[^8] In Yajigrama Srinivasa Acharya came out with his companions and with great care brought him into the house;
knowing him to be the Supreme Lord, he worshipped him with great devotion. For several days there was a great festival
of sankirtana, all were absorbed in talks of Krishna, and Virachandra spoke with the Acharya in private (13.293–295).
<!-- F29 F30 -->

Srinivasa went on with him. In Kantaka-nagara, near Katwa, where Mahaprabhu accepted sannyasa, they spent a day and a
night in prema; there Virachandra took leave of the Acharya and went on to Kheturi by way of Budhari (13.296–298). In
Budhari, according to one biography and the “Narottama-vilasa,” Govinda Kaviraja received him, worshipped him with great
devotion, and the pleased Virachandra stayed with him two days. <!-- F31 -->

In Kheturi Thakura Mahashaya Narottama came out to meet him with great joy. For several days a great festival of
sankirtana went on in the village: Virachandra, the great branch of Chaitanya's tree, danced in sankirtana in the
courtyard of Gauranga, and countless people came to see the Prabhu; in private he told Narottama something
(“Bhakti-ratnakara” 13.298–301). <!-- F32 -->

The “Narottama-vilasa” (11.10–196) tells of Virachandra's coming to Yajigrama and Kheturi in detail.[^9] When he arrived
unexpectedly in Yajigrama, Srinivasa's wives washed his feet, and the Acharya's sons and daughters bowed to him, and he
blessed them; he seated the Acharya beside himself at the meal and, tasting the food, said: “Today it is just like
Vraja!” With him was the Govardhana-shila given by Nityananda, and he himself served it and offered it food. In Kheturi
Narottama fell at his feet, and Virachandra, embracing him, could not contain himself; before the Deities of Kheturi he
wept and bowed down, and the people said: “The world-enchanting Nityananda-Balarama — his son is Prabhu Virabhadra, abode of virtues.” When at his wish the
kirtana began, he danced in the midst of the circle of Vaishnavas, arms raised, crying “Hari! Hari!”, embraced Narottama,
and said to Govinda Kaviraja, “I would die for your poetry!” — and asked for more singing; then Gokula sang
Govinda's song “Glory to Nityananda…”. In the morning he asked Ramachandra to speak of the rasa-lila and, listening,
embraced him again and again; “will such rare company ever be again?” he said to the Acharya, and wept.
<!-- F67 -->

On the way many sinners and godless people were saved by his power. The “Bhakti-ratnakara” names, for example, a poor
brahmana and a scholar proud of his learning: his pride was crushed, and he became a devotee (13.302–303). <!-- F33 -->

When he came to Mathura, people ran to look at him and said to one another: “Look — the son of Nityananda-Baladeva!”
Some asked, “Can a human being have such beauty?” — and others answered, “Why, he is no human being at all.” They
marvelled at his companions too, and the news of his coming spread everywhere (13.304–309). <!-- F65 -->

In Vrindavana, learning of his coming, the Vaishnavas came out to meet him. According to the “Bhakti-ratnakara”
(13.310–324), they were Sri Jiva, Krishnadasa Kaviraja, Ananta Acharya — the best of Gadadhara Pandita's disciples, the
*adhikari* of Govinda — and his disciple Haridasa Pandita, also an *adhikari* of Govinda; another disciple of Gadadhara,
Gosai Gopala Dasa; the *adhikari* of Gopinatha, Madhu Pandita, also a disciple of Gadadhara, and his godbrother
Bhavananda, the servant of Gopinatha; the disciples of Kashishvara Gosani — Govinda Gosani and Sri Yadavacharya — and
many others.[^10] All the residents of Vrindavana rejoiced at his darshan; there was no one who was not moved by his
ecstatic bhakti, and the Vaishnavas of Vraja sang his virtues. Together with everyone he visited the Deities of Sri
Govinda, Sri Gopinatha and Sri Madana-mohana, and also Radha-vinoda, Radha-ramana and Radha-Damodara (13.325–328).
<!-- F34 F35 -->

Then, with the permission of Bhugarbha Goswami, Sri Jiva and others, he went round Vraja: the twelve forests,
Radha-kunda, Shyama-kunda and Giriraja Govardhana; Yadava Acharya and others went with him. At Radha-kunda, by the tamala
tree where Gaurachandra once sat, he was immersed in prema, and at Radha- and Shyama-kunda and at Govardhana he danced in
sankirtana (13.329–336). The wondrous prema he showed in these places spread his fame everywhere. So the greatness of a
devotee is revealed in love for Krishna and His abode. <!-- F36 I4 -->

At the kundas he fed the Vrajavasis and stayed five or six days. Taking leave of Jiva, Bhugarbha and others, he went from
Govardhana to the hut of Krishnadasa Kaviraja, who went on with him; Vasudeva, Uddhava, Yadava and others were with him
too. He visited Kamyavana, Vrishabhanupura (Varshana), Nandagrama and other places, where his companion Vasudeva told him of
the festivals of Radha's and Krishna's birth. At Rama-ghat he sang of Baladeva and danced, and at Bhandira-vata,
meditating alone on the pastimes of Bhandira, he suddenly saw the two brothers, Rama and Krishna, playing there with the
cowherd boys. After visiting Gokula-Mahavana, Raval and Mathura, he entered the temple of Govinda in Vrindavana on the
day of Janmashtami, when Govinda's *abhisheka* was performed, and on the *bhadra shukla-ashtami* he saw the *abhisheka*
on Sri Radhika's birthday — and could not contain his heart (13.337–424). At parting everyone wept, and he, himself
bathed in tears, comforted each one (13.426–428). <!-- F66 -->

Having stayed some time in Vrindavana, he returned by way of Mathura to Gauda-desha, telling everywhere of
Vrindavana, and in Khardaha bowed to his mother (13.425–431). “The life of Ganga and Virachandra is nectar,” Narahari
Chakravarti concludes; “I cannot describe it in detail” (13.433). <!-- F37 F38 -->

## Shyamasundara of Khardaha
Virachandra himself brought a stone to Khardaha. The place on the Ganga where it was landed is called Shyamasundara-ghat
to this day. From this stone three Deities manifested in the old temple of Khardaha — Sri Shyamasundara, Sri
Radhavallabha and Sri Nandadulal Jiu — and they are worshipped in Khardaha to this day.[^11] The Deity is not a statue:
the Lord Himself appears in the stone to accept the service of His devotees. <!-- F39 -->

In the old temple of Shyamasundara a manuscript of the “Bhagavatam” is kept, copied, by tradition, by Virabhadra, and
in the opinion of some by Nityananda Himself. Virachandra established in Khardaha the festival of Nityananda's
appearance. The service of the Deities in his time was generous: for the offering they cooked rice from one and a half
*maunds* of unhusked grain, with other foodstuffs in proportion. The present managers of the Khardaha temple preserve
many traditions about him. <!-- F40 F41 F42 F43 -->

He is remembered in his father's birthplace, too. Ekachakra — with Garbhavasa and other places near Mallarpur station —
was later named Birachandrapur after Sri Birachandra Prabhu; the servants of the Deities there come from the line of
Gopijana-vallabha, his eldest disciple-“son.” <!-- F44 F21 -->

## With the Acharyas of the New Generation
When Govinda Chakravarti, a disciple of Srinivasa, held a festival in Boraquli, Virachandra came there with his
companions, together with Krishna Mishra. He danced clinging to the Acharya; in the rapture of the dance he embraced
Narottama with tears, and at parting he was very sad (“Bhakti-ratnakara” 14.90–147). <!-- F51 -->

His letter to Srinivasa Acharya has been preserved. “Sri Virachandra-deva, worthy of your remembrance,” it begins, “with
loving embraces informs you: Srila Srinivasacharya, you are the power of Sri Sri Mahaprabhu. Through one power, through
Rupa and others, the books were revealed; through another, you spread them in the assembly of the *mahajanas* of
Gauda-mandala.” (“Bhakti-ratnakara” 14, letter 4) The sense of the letter is this: Rupa Goswami, the hidden power of
Gaurasundara, composed the books of bhakti in Vraja, and Srinivasa, another power of Mahaprabhu's mercy, brought them to
the Vaishnavas of Gauda. <!-- F52 Q8 -->

The same letter carries a stern word as well. In Kandara (Kadra), where Jnanadasa's home was, lived a *kayastha*
named Jayagopala. Proud of his learning, when asked about his guru he named not his own guru, whom he thought
“unlearned,” but his *parama-guru*. Virachandra skilfully exposed him: he had “transgressed the grace” of the guru — and
rejected him; in the
letter he forbade his people to associate with Jayagopala and asked Srinivasa to do the same. In Yajigrama they heard
then that Prabhu Virachandra had upheld dharma (“Bhakti-ratnakara” 14.179–190). So too
writes Srila Bhaktisiddhanta in his “Gaudiya-bhashya” on CB, Adi 14.86–87: “Virachandra tactfully exposed him and then
rejected him”; disregard
for the guru is no small matter, and he guarded the purity of the teaching firmly. <!-- F53 -->

After Mahaprabhu's disappearance there was confusion in the Vaishnava world. In the Prabhu's family there was no
suitable person, various doctrines crept into the community, and Gauda was left without the guidance of an acharya.
Prabhu Sri Virachandra, by his independent nature, did not take on the governance of all Gauda — this independence is
the freedom of Ishvara, not a flaw — and the Lord arranged the matter otherwise: Sri Jiva sent Srinivasa, Narottama and
Shyamananda to Gauda. <!-- F54 -->

Srinivasa's son Gati-Govinda, in the signature (*bhanita*) of his song “Vira-ratnavali,” says of himself: “At the priceless
feet of Mahaprabhu Virachandra speaks Gati-Govinda, son of Srinivasa.” According to the tradition of later books,
Gati-Govinda was even born by Virabhadra's blessing. <!-- F55 Q9 -->

Tradition also tells of Abhirama Thakura: any stone other than a Vishnu-shila or a consecrated Deity cracked from his
obeisance. Nityananda's son Virachandra and His daughter Ganga-devi withstood Abhirama's obeisances — and this revealed
their divinity. Abhirama composed *ashtakas* in their honour — the “Sri Virabhadrashtaka” and the “Ganga-stotra” — which
speak of it. <!-- F56 Q11 -->

According to the “Prema-vilasa,” Virabhadra Prabhu was at the great festival in Kheturi together with Jahnava-devi, and
Kanu Thakura was there as well.[^12] <!-- F57 Q7 -->

According to the family tradition of Srila Bhakti Sundar Govinda Dev-Goswami Maharaj, the Deities of Radha and Krishna of
his family were installed by Virachandra Prabhu. And Jayananda says that the poet spreads his song having received the
garland-prasada of Virabhadra, and that after Nityananda's departure the mahantas found consolation in seeing Virabhadra.
<!-- F58 -->

## Disciples Renowned as Sons
Virachandra had no children of his own by his wives.[^13] Three disciples became renowned as his sons:
Gopijana-vallabha, Ramakrishna and Ramachandra. The eldest, Gopijana-vallabha, lived in the village of Lata near Mankar
(Bardhaman district); the middle one, Ramakrishna, near Maldah, in Gayeshpur;[^14] the youngest, Ramachandra, in
Khardaha. Ramachandra was a pure *shrotriya* brahmana of the Batavyal family, of the Shandilya *gotra*. So writes Srila
Bhaktisiddhanta in the “Anubhashya” on CC, Adi 11.8. <!-- F17 F18 -->

The “Bhakti-ratnakara” calls them his three sons, full of prema (14.187–189). But “sons” here means disciples. The three
have different *gotras*, different families and different villages, Srila Bhaktisiddhanta explains: “If the three had
one gotra and one village, no one would doubt that they were his sons by birth.” <!-- F19 -->

From Ramachandra, according to the “Anubhashya,” the line ran thus: he had four sons; the third son of the eldest,
Radhamadhava, was Yadavendra; then came Nandakishora, Nidhikrishna, Chaitanya-chand, Krishnamohan, Jaganmohan,
Vrajanatha and Shyamalal Goswami, whom the “Anubhashya” speaks of as already departed. The biographies add that
Ramachandra founded in Khardaha his own line, known as the “Khardaha Goswamis.”[^15] <!-- F20 -->

A large community formed around Virabhadra: twelve hundred *neras* and thirteen hundred *neris* — monks and nuns who had
controlled their senses. Later bad customs appeared among them, eating fish became habitual, and many lost people's
respect. The followers' departure from the teaching does not diminish Virabhadra himself. <!-- F22 -->

Srila Bhaktisiddhanta explains more than once what Nityananda's family is. People of worldly mind, he writes in the
“Gaudiya-bhashya” on CB, Adi 9.12, claim that the descendants of Nityananda's son Virabhadra are as powerful as
Nityananda Himself and by blood kinship hold the position of the Supreme Lord; but then how could this tradition have
fallen under the influence of the *smartas*? There is also the view that Virabhadra's three “sons” were his disciples,
since their families came from different villages, and by worldly measure they were not his sons. To seek a material
kinship with Nityananda and count Him among conditioned souls is a grave offence. <!-- F59 -->

Those who follow the path of bhakti, Srila Bhaktisiddhanta continues (on CB, Madhya 12.26), do not define Nityananda's
family by bodily kinship — that is how the *smartas* judge. Nityananda's family is the foundation of the whole line of
disciplic succession; that is why many simple villagers who became disciples of Virabhadra Prabhu regard themselves as
part of Nityananda's family. Of the “Nityananda-vamsha-vistara” he writes that it was compiled at the end of the
nineteenth century by a native of Beniatola in Calcutta, that it is a recent book and lacks historical accuracy.
<!-- F60 -->

The appendix on Vrindavana Dasa in the Russian edition of the “Chaitanya-bhagavata” recalls a sad case as well: three
disciples of Virachandra Prabhu, who were honoured as his sons, yielded to the intimidation of the *smartas* and placed a
murti of Durga on the altar beside Shyamasundara.[^16] <!-- F61 -->

So the line of Nityananda and Virabhadra is above all a spiritual succession, not one of blood; his “sons” are
disciples, and those who serve in their footsteps are Nityananda's family. <!-- I3 Q6 -->

## Traditions of Later Books
Later works — the “Nityananda-vamsha-vistara,” “Prema-vilasa,” “Advaita-prakasha,” “Vaishnava-vandana” and others — tell
much about him that is not in the early sources; we give it only as tradition, mindful of Srila Bhaktisiddhanta's words
on the unreliability of the “Nityananda-vamsha-vistara.” By tradition, already in Nilachala the Lord, in secret counsel
with Nityananda, said that Vira Prabhu would be His descent;[^17] they tell of Virachandra's birth and childhood and of how
Abhirama and Advaita recognised Gauranga in the infant; of a festival and anointing after Nityananda's departure; of a
festival in Khardaha where three offerings were made to the three Lords; of long travels through Nilachala, Bengal, the
north and Rarh, Ekachakra, through Jharikhanda, Gaya, Kashi and Prayaga to Vrindavana, where the rasa-lila was revealed
to him; of Dhaka, Maldah, Vana-Vishnupur and Vira Hambira; of a conversation with Jiva and an arati in Vrindavana; of his
speech on the superiority of a Vaishnava and of Narottama's sacred thread in Kheturi; of Advaita's disappearance. The
author of the “Prema-vilasa” calls himself his *shiksha*-disciple and writes at the bidding of Jahnava and Virachandra.
<!-- F62 -->

## Disappearance
When and where Virachandra Prabhu left this world no source says; the day of his disappearance is not in the calendar of
the Sri Chaitanya Saraswat Math either. Only the day of his appearance is known — the Krishna-navami of the month of
Damodara. <!-- F14 -->

## Significance
Narahari Chakravarti, in the “Namamrita-samudra” — prayers in which he calls upon the Lord's associates one after
another — places him in the row of the kin of the Pancha-tattva, after Vishvarupa, the Lord's brother, and Achyuta, Advaita's son:

“Vishvarupa, Sri Achyuta, Virachandra Prabhu! Grant me service at your feet, so that I may never forget it.”
(“Namamrita-samudra” 18) <!-- F11 -->

And in the song that concludes the “Bhakti-ratnakara” Narahari asks for the same: “Sri Achyutananda — the root of bliss,
merciful Virachandra — may I serve their lotus feet.” <!-- F69 -->

The Vaishnava *vandanas* speak of him as well. The Sanskrit *vandana* ascribed to Sri Jiva calls him “devoted to the Vedic
dharma and yet detached, free from egotism, without pride, self-controlled — the servant of Jahnavi here”
(1.23),[^18] and Devakinandana Dasa calls him the one “by whose conduct the whole world is won” (2.13). <!-- F68 -->

Nityananda's son — according to the “Gaura-ganoddesha-dipika,” the *vyuha* of Sankarshana who lies on the Ocean of Milk,
non-different from Chaitanya — Virachandra Prabhu came into the world in the form of a devotee. He received initiation
from Jahnava Mata, lived in Khardaha, served Shyamasundara and celebrated his father's appearance, danced in sankirtana
at the festivals of Srinivasa and Narottama, went round Vraja in prema and firmly guarded the purity of the teaching.
Krishnadasa Kaviraja calls him the main pillar in the hall of devotion to Sri Chaitanya and the best of Nityananda's
branches: by his mercy the whole world sings the names of Chaitanya and Nityananda, and at his feet the author of the
“Charitamrita” himself seeks shelter. <!-- F2 F7 F8 I1 I2 I4 -->
