# Ramachandra Kaviraja
*Рамачандра Кавираджа · @ramachandra-kaviraja*

## "Kaviraja-Narottama"
Ramachandra Kaviraja was a disciple of Srinivasa Acharya and the inseparable friend of Narottama Thakura. The biographies
call him Sri Ramachandra Kaviraja, Ramachandra Sena, or simply Kaviraja; the "Bhakti-ratnakara" calls him Ramachandra
Sena (1.264) and "the king of poets" (*kavi-nripati*; 1.270, 8.531), and in Srila Jiva Gosvami's letters the bare name
"Kaviraja" also means him ("Bhakti-ratnakara," 14.37). The "Prema-vilasa" (19) calls him Ramachandra Dasa. He was so
completely one with Narottama that their names merged into one: Vaishnavas spoke of "Kaviraja-Narottama" and
"Ramachandra-Narottama" ("Bhakti-ratnakara," 1.272–274).[^1] <!-- F1 -->

Among Srinivasa's disciples eight kavirajas were especially honoured: Ramachandra, his younger brother Govinda,
Karnapura, Nrisimha, Bhagavan, Vallabha Dasa, Gopiramana and Gokula; Ramachandra was the eldest of them.[^2] <!-- F3 Q10 -->

His name also appears in the "Chaitanya-charitamrita." Listing the branches of Sri Nityananda's tree, Krishnadasa
Kaviraja says:

"Kamsari Sena, Ramasena and Ramachandra Kaviraja, and the three physicians — Govinda, Shriranga and Mukunda." (CC, Adi
11.51)

In his "Anubhashya" on this verse Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati takes the name to be his: Ramachandra Kaviraja was the
son of Chiranjiva and Sunanda of Khanda, a disciple of Srinivasa Acharya and the dear friend of Narottama Thakura, who
prayed to be near him birth after birth; Sri Jiva Gosvami gave him the name "Kaviraja" in Vrindavana, seeing his devotion
to Krishna; "from birth he was detached from worldly life," and he was the chief and dearest companion of Thakura
Mahashaya and Srinivasa Acharya in preaching and in bhajana. <!-- F63 Q6 -->

## Chiranjiva's Line
Ramachandra's father, Sri Chiranjiva Sena, was a resident of Shrikhanda and a companion of Mahaprabhu. When the Gaudiya
devotees came to the Lord in Puri, Svarupa Damodara named him to King Prataparudra among the people of Khanda:

"Here are Mukunda dasa, Narahari and Sri Raghunandana, and Chiranjiva and Sulochana — residents of Khanda." (CC, Madhya
11.92)

Chiranjiva's family belonged to the vaidyas, the physicians' community. His home was in Kumaranagara on the bank of the
Bhagirathi, and after his marriage he settled in Khanda ("Bhakti-ratnakara," 1.249–250); the people of Khanda, led by
Narahari Sarakara, loved and honoured this mahabhagavata dearly. Ramachandra's mother was named Sri Sunanda; Ramachandra
was the elder of two sons, the younger being Sri Govinda Kaviraja (ibid., 1.238, 1.445). According to one of the
biographies, Ramachandra was born in Shrikhanda, in the Bardhaman district.[^3] The sources give neither the year nor
the day of his appearance, and that day is not in the calendar of the Sri Chaitanya Saraswat Math. <!-- F4 F5 F6 F7 F8 Q7 -->

His maternal grandfather was Sri Damodara Kaviraja, Damodara Sena of Shrikhanda, a poet whose name was known everywhere
("Bhakti-ratnakara," 1.238–239).[^4] The "Bhakti-ratnakara" tells how the birth of his grandsons was foretold. Damodara
was unbeatable in debate; a digvijayi whom he had easily defeated cursed him in vexation: "May you be sonless!" Damodara
appeased him in every way, and the man said, "You will have a daughter blessed in the world; from her two jewel-like sons
will be born, and by their power all that is inauspicious will be destroyed." This daughter, Sunanda, Damodara gave in
marriage to Chiranjiva (1.241–247). <!-- F9 F10 -->

Of the boy the "Bhakti-ratnakara" says:

"As were the father and mother, so was the son Ramachandra; by his birth Ramachandra brought great joy."
("Bhakti-ratnakara," 1.258)

From infancy he charmed everyone; his form was radiant, in beauty he was like Madana, and he soon became learned. The
wise, looking at him, said, "This is a portion of some god: by his own will he has appeared in a family of vaidyas, and
because of his human form no one recognizes him," while the Vaishnavas felt, "If he becomes a Vaishnava, many will become
Vaishnavas" (1.259–264). He grew up diligent, serious and persevering, active, intelligent and handsome. <!-- F11 F12 -->

His father left this world early — soon after the birth of the younger son — and the brothers grew up in Kumaranagara,
in their grandfather's house. Damodara worshipped Shakti, and under his influence both brothers inclined for a time
toward the path of the shaktas, although their father was a mahabhagavata and a personal companion of Mahaprabhu. One of
the biographies sees in this the law of association: a man becomes like the company he keeps, and only Srinivasa's mercy
turned Chiranjiva's sons to Krishna. But this cannot be measured by worldly standards: Ramachandra is an eternal
associate, and later Sri Gaurasundara Himself would tell him in a dream, "By My will you do not know yourself"
("Bhakti-ratnakara," 10.231). Thus Yogamaya for a time veils their own nature from the Lord's eternal servants for the
sake of His pastimes; this is not a fall but a preparation for meeting the guru. <!-- F13 F14 I2 -->

Ramachandra became a physician and in time grew famous as a most learned poet. People would describe him thus: "This is a
great pandita named Ramachandra, known as the king of poets, a conqueror of all quarters, a physician, foremost among the
famed, born of the Vaidya caste; he lives in Kumaranagara" ("Bhakti-ratnakara," 8.531–532). <!-- F15 -->

One of the biographies counts Ramachandra and his brother among the prominent residents of Shrikhanda who were devotees of
Lord Chaitanya — alongside Chiranjiva and Damodara — and adds that Krishnadasa Kaviraja names "many of them" as branches
of the Lord's tree:[^5]

"Mukunda dasa, Sri Raghunandana, Narahari dasa, Chiranjiva and Sulochana, all of Shrikhanda, are great branches, abodes
of Sri Chaitanya's mercy. Everywhere they give away the fruits and flowers of love." (CC, Adi 10.78–79)
<!-- F62 Q8 -->

## A Meeting on the Road at Yajigrama
Not all agree that Ramachandra was married. Srila Bhaktisiddhanta writes that from birth he was detached from worldly
life; the "Gaudiya-vaishnava-abhidhana" says that he married but never lived as a householder; and the
"Bhakti-ratnakara" relates that he met Srinivasa while returning from his own wedding.[^6] The sources say nothing about
his wife, and we shall not guess. One thing is clear: the detachment the acharya saw in him was not worldly
disillusionment but the nature of an eternal associate of the Lord, awakened at the hour of meeting by the mercy of the
guru. <!-- F17 F18 F19 -->

Srinivasa Acharya was already married at that time, and one of the biographies draws a lesson from this: Ramachandra,
"the soul of Narottama," took shelter at Srinivasa's feet when Srinivasa was a householder. The marriage of a
mahabhagavata is a pastime meant to teach the world: householders should not imitate the guru, nor should the falsely
renounced revile him. <!-- F20 I1 -->

The "Bhakti-ratnakara" describes the meeting thus. One day Srinivasa was sitting with his companions on the bank of a pond
west of his house in Yajigrama, gazing compassionately at the road. Along the road, in a fine palanquin, a newly married
young man of wondrous beauty, splendidly adorned, was travelling home. Srinivasa thought, "This body will find its
purpose if he serves Krishna," and asked who he was. He was told: this is Ramachandra, the king of poets, a physician from
Kumaranagara. Srinivasa went into the house with a quiet smile, and Ramachandra, who had heard the whole exchange, looked
at the Acharya from his palanquin — and gave him his heart (8.519–535).[^7] <!-- F21 -->

He returned home in silence and could barely wait for evening; at night he came to the house of a brahmana next to
Srinivasa's and spent the hours till dawn thinking of the Acharya's feet. At daybreak he came to Srinivasa, wept, and
fell at his feet like a felled tree. The Acharya, who had himself been awaiting this meeting, lifted him, embraced him
tightly and, placing a hand on his head, said through tears:

"Birth after birth you are my dearest friend; today Providence, being kind, has brought [us] together. In the same way it
united [me] with Narottama in Vrindavana — who does not pine without cease for his virtues? He is one eye, you are the
second eye; you both are my eyes, you two are my two arms." ("Bhakti-ratnakara," 8.544–546)[^8]

On hearing Narottama's name, Ramachandra felt a natural prema awaken in his heart. He said nothing, but the Acharya
understood his wish and answered with a smile, "What you said in your mind will soon come to pass" (8.547–550).
<!-- F22 F23 Q1 -->

Seeing his wonderful gifts, Srinivasa had him begin reading the books of the Gosvamis and at an auspicious moment
initiated him into the mantra of Radha and Krishna (8.551–552). Ramachandra remained at his guru's feet, studying these
books; Srinivasa taught them to his disciples in Yajigrama, and Ramachandra was among the most important of them. The
Acharya made him his personal servant (*bhritya*), and Srinivasa, Narottama and Ramachandra became one in heart and mind.
Later traditions ("Prema-vilasa," 13–14; "Karnananda," 1; "Anuraga-valli," 6) tell of his coming to Srinivasa and of his
initiation in their own way.[^9] <!-- F24 F25 F26 -->

## Answering the Shaktas
A few days later Ramachandra returned home from Yajigrama. The shaktas there, learning that he had accepted Vaishnava
initiation, were furious. He paid them no attention: every day he marked twelve places of his body with tilaka and
chanted the name of Hari. <!-- F27 -->

One day, as he was returning home after bathing in the Ganges, the shaktas stopped him: "Why are you going home without
honouring Shiva? Your grandfather Damodara Kaviraja was a great worshipper of Shiva." Ramachandra answered them from the
shastras. Shiva and Brahma are guna-avataras of Sri Krishna, and Krishna is the root of all avataras: whoever worships
Krishna worships everyone, as watering the root nourishes the branches and leaves. Prahlada, Dhruva and Vibhishana were
devotees of Krishna, and therefore Shiva and Brahma are always kind to them and glorify them. Ravana, Kumbhakarna, Bana
and others were hostile to Hari and worshipped Shiva alone — and Shiva himself destroyed them. The shastras say that
Brahma creates the world by the power of his worship of Vishnu, and Shiva blesses the world by bearing on his head the
Ganges, the water from the Lord's feet that purifies the three worlds. The smarta brahmanas and shaktas were struck dumb.
<!-- F28 F29 -->

There is no belittling of Shiva in this answer. Shiva is the greatest of Vaishnavas ("Srimad-Bhagavatam," 12.13.16), and
the destruction of Ravana and the others was his judgment upon the enemies of Hari, not revenge. Ramachandra only reminded
them that whoever serves Krishna serves all, and whoever honours a demigod while hostile to Hari perishes at the hand of
that very demigod. <!-- I4 -->

## In Vrindavana
Meanwhile Srinivasa had left for Vrindavana. Before leaving he said something to Ramachandra in private — what it was, the
author of the "Bhakti-ratnakara" does not know (9.71). Without the Acharya the Vaishnavas of Gauda pined. One day in
Shrikhanda Raghunandana said to Ramachandra, "Everything has become empty — what more is there to say? Go quickly to
Vrindavana — this is your task." In Yajigrama everyone asked the same: "Without Sri Acharya everything has become
darkness. Bring the Acharya back and preserve everyone's life" (9.110–114).[^10] With the permission of Raghunandana and
the other Vaishnavas Ramachandra set out. The Acharya had left at the end of the month of Margashirsha, Ramachandra left
at the end of Pausha (9.132). <!-- F30 F31 -->

He travelled through Gaya, Kashi, Ayodhya, Prayaga and other tirthas (9.194); in Mathura he bathed and rested at Vishrama
Ghata and bowed to Krishna's birthplace and to the Deity of Adikeshava. In Vrindavana, where Srinivasa was then living,
people came running to see him: "This is no man at all — it is some god." Sri Jiva was told that an extraordinary person
had come from Gauda: his body shone brighter than gold, it was all trembling with rapture, and tears flowed from his eyes.
Jiva asked the Acharya, who guessed, "Ramachandra must have come." Ramachandra bowed at the feet of the Acharya and the
Gosvami, and both embraced him again and again (9.179–190). He conveyed Raghunandana's words and the news from the
Vaishnavas of Gauda, and Jiva understood how great the grief there was and resolved to let Srinivasa go home soon
(9.191–196). <!-- F32 F33 -->

Jiva himself took him to Radha-Damodara. At the samadhi of Sri Rupa Ramachandra lost all composure: he rolled on the
ground crying, "Ha, ha, Prabhu Rupa!" and Jiva, holding him in his arms, consoled him (9.197–200). With the permission of
Srinivasa and Jiva he received darshana of Govinda, Gopinatha, Madana-mohana and Radha-ramana, and visited the samadhis of
Sanatana Gosvami, Kashishvara Pandita and Raghunatha Bhatta (9.201–205); according to one of the biographies, he went to
the Deities together with Srinivasa and Shyamananda. Sri Gopala Bhatta, the merciful Lokanatha, Sri Bhugarbha and other
Gosvamis showed him boundless mercy (9.206). <!-- F34 F35 -->

He hurried to Aritagrama, bathed in Radha-kunda and Shyama-kunda and bowed to Raghunatha Dasa Gosvami, who embraced him
tenderly, bathing him in tears, and, on hearing his sweet verses, showed him inexpressible mercy. There he also met
Krishnadasa Kaviraja; he saw Govardhana and walked through the twelve forests of Vraja (9.208–212). <!-- F36 -->

It was in Vrindavana that Ramachandra received his title. The "Bhakti-ratnakara" says:

"Sri Paramananda Bhattacharya, a treasury of prema, Sri Jiva Gosvami and other residents of Vrindavana — all, hearing from
his lips the verses he had composed, with great joy gave him the title 'Kaviraja.' Ramachandra Kaviraja, full of all
virtues, is the one with whom Narottama Mahashaya is one in soul." ("Bhakti-ratnakara," 1.267–269)

In the ninth wave of the same book the title is given by Gopala Bhatta and all the Gosvamis, on hearing his marvellous
verses (9.213–214); according to the "Anubhashya" and nearly all the biographies, Sri Jiva gave it.[^11] <!-- F37 Q4 -->

The Gosvamis of Vrindavana came to love him for his humility and other virtues. Everyone praised his unwavering fidelity
to his guru: together with the Acharya he served Gopala Bhatta Gosvami in every way, and Jiva, watching guru and disciple,
rejoiced more and more (9.215–218). <!-- F38 -->

## Return to Gauda
When Srinivasa prepared to go home, Ramachandra left Vrindavana with him. On the full moon of Vaishakha, the day of Radha-
ramana's simhasana-yatra, Jiva sent Srinivasa on his way, and the next morning entrusted Shyamananda and the books of the
Gosvamis to him. Ramachandra and Shyamananda bowed again and again to all the Gosvamis with longing, and the travellers
went by way of Mathura to Gauda ("Bhakti-ratnakara," 9.219–229).[^12] <!-- F39 -->

In Vana-Vishnupura King Vira Hambira came out to meet Srinivasa. The Acharya presented Ramachandra and Shyamananda to
him, and the king fell to the ground and tearfully bowed at the feet of both: "By the Prabhu's mercy I have attained the
feet of these two" (9.230–236). After initiating the king, Srinivasa entrusted him to "dear Ramachandra Kaviraja" and
said, "You will learn the particulars from him" (9.264). Later, in Yajigrama, the Acharya again entrusted Vira Hambira to
him, and whatever came into the king's mind he asked Ramachandra about; and when Vira Hambira secretly gave Ramachandra a
thousand coins, he told the Acharya, who smiled and took them with him (13.53–58, 13.80–81). Though the king himself was
Srinivasa's disciple, Ramachandra, according to one of the biographies, became his shiksha-guru; the "Karnananda"
(4–6) describes his instructions to the king at length. <!-- F41 -->

Later Srinivasa took Narottama and Ramachandra to Navadvipa. In Mayapur, in the house of Jagannatha Mishra, the aged
Ishana was still living — "of the Lord's people only Ishana remains" ("Bhakti-ratnakara," 11.721). Ishana embraced them
and showed them the places of Gaura's pastimes, by then quite dilapidated; Srinivasa with Narottama and Ramachandra walked
through all nine islands of Navadvipa. And soon after, on the road to Shrikhanda, they heard that Ishana Thakura had
withdrawn from sight (13.21).[^13] <!-- F40 Q9 -->

## Budhari
Even as he was leaving for Vrindavana, Ramachandra was thinking about where he should live. "How many days until I attain
the company of Sri Narottama? I shall settle in a place where everything will be joyful," he reflected, and, revealing his
intention to no one, chose a place in his mind. On the eve of his departure he told his younger brother Govinda in private
that life in Kumaranagara had become unsettled, and advised him to move to Teliya-Budhari — a holy place between the
Ganges and the Padmavati, where their grandfather had once lived. Govinda agreed and moved two to four days after his
brother left ("Bhakti-ratnakara," 9.116–133). Thus the village of Teliya-Budhari, in what is now the Murshidabad
district, became the shripat of both brothers.[^14] <!-- F16 -->

When Srinivasa returned to Gauda, Ramachandra and Govinda received him in Budhari with great pomp. The Acharya and his
companions stayed in Ramachandra's house; kirtana sounded there day and night, "rare even for the gods," and the Acharya
was full of tenderness toward Ramachandra (10.214–218, 10.282). At the request of Govinda, supported by Ramachandra,
Srinivasa initiated the younger brother as well. One of the biographies adds that Ramachandra and Shyamananda accompanied
Srinivasa on his second journey to Vrindavana too. <!-- F42 -->

Here in Budhari Ramachandra learned that Narottama was coming to them. That night he could not sleep: at the mere name
"Narottama" a thrill ran through his body. Toward morning, "by the Lord's will," sleep overcame him for a little while,
and in a dream Sri Gaurasundara appeared to him. Ramachandra fell at His feet, and the Lord embraced him, bathed him in
tears of love and said:

"By My will you do not know yourself. You are dear to Me, and Narottama is dear to Me; seeing each other, you two will
remember what was before. You two will bestow My prema-bhakti and free living beings from the cruel threefold
suffering." ("Bhakti-ratnakara," 10.231–233)

On waking, Ramachandra could think of one thing only: "How soon shall I meet Mahashaya?" (10.219–239). <!-- F43 -->

## Narottama's Friend
Narottama loved Ramachandra more than life, and Srinivasa loved him ardently. In the "Prarthana" Narottama prays:

"Be merciful, Sri Acharya Prabhu Srinivasa: Narottama Dasa begs for the company of Ramachandra."

Again and again he names Ramachandra in the closing lines of his songs. One of the biographies observes that although
Narottama and Srinivasa were one in soul, Narottama longed for the company of Ramachandra, the Acharya's servant — and in
this his love for Srinivasa himself is seen, for Ramachandra was very dear to Srinivasa. <!-- F46 Q2 -->

Ramachandra was Narottama's dearest companion in preaching and bhajana; they were almost inseparable, and the biographies
call him "one in soul" with Narottama, his "soul" and his "second self." Govinda Kaviraja writes of his brother in the drama
"Sangita-madhava":

"…was born the excellent Sri Ramachandra; he, the king of poets, became truly one with the illustrious [Narottama]."
("Bhakti-ratnakara," 1.270)

The "Bhakti-ratnakara" continues: "Body, mind, life and name — the two have but one," and so people say
"Narottama-Kaviraja," as though Nara-Narayana; both are expert in all the shastras, crush unbelievers without effort and
are skilled in bestowing pure bhakti (1.272–276). And in the same drama:

"We bow to these two, who became a pair through great mutual love: ever devoted to the Lord, who have crossed beyond
samsara, foremost in the teaching of the Satvata-tantras, masters of all conclusions, rasikas ever bestowing the rasa of
bhakti, ornaments [of the world] who drive out unbelief." ("Bhakti-ratnakara," 1.277)

And in Govinda's song about Narottama, Ramachandra is "the minister of Narottama, the king of bhakti, one with him in
body" (1.439). <!-- F47 Q3 Q5 -->

As Lokanatha and Bhugarbha had done before them, Narottama and Ramachandra showed the bhajana of like-minded friends,
without a trace of rivalry or envy. Pure devotees choose one another as companions by kinship of heart; this does not mean
they lived in one hut — it was love that united them. <!-- I3 -->

One day Srinivasa said something to Narottama in private and entrusted dear Ramachandra to him; giving him Ramachandra and
a few others as companions, he sent him off to Kheturi: "I shall come soon" ("Bhakti-ratnakara," 10.286–289). Ramachandra,
Narottama and Govinda lived in Kheturi, "ever intoxicated with sankirtana." Once a letter from Jiva came to them,
addressed to all three: "To those named Sri Ramachandra Kaviraja, Sri Narottama Dasa and Sri Govinda Dasa…"; Jiva thanked
them for the songs they had sent and explained to them service "in the form of a sadhaka" and "in the form of a siddha"
(14.30–32). <!-- F48 -->

Meanwhile in Yajigrama Srinivasa pined for Ramachandra and at last called him back. Ramachandra came from Kheturi, fell at
the Acharya's feet and bowed to both his wives, Draupadi and Gauranga-priya; the Acharya was always conquered by his love
and constantly relished the books of the Gosvamis with him, for Ramachandra was a great scholar (14.46–65). One full-moon
night, as the moon was rising, Ramachandra suddenly laughed for joy. Draupadi quietly asked the Acharya why he was
laughing, and Srinivasa replied:

"Sri Radhika and Krishna, both, in a grove of flowers are gathering flowers, surrounded by the sakhis. Rai picks
wondrously blossoming kumuda flowers, while Madhai by mistake picks the moonlight, [taking it] for them. Seeing Krishna's
mistake, the sakhis laugh. Ramachandra, being there beside me, laughs." ("Bhakti-ratnakara," 14.60–62)

This was neither a vision nor a flight of fancy: in his spiritual form as a maidservant of Sri Radha, Ramachandra was
beside his guru in the eternal pastimes of Radha and Krishna. <!-- F44 -->

One day Narottama and Ramachandra were going to bathe in the Padmavati when they met two young brahmanas, Harirama and
Ramakrishna, sons of the zamindar Shivananda Acharya of Goyasa-grama. The youths were driving goats and sheep to be
sacrificed to Durga. After hearing Narottama they let the animals go, came with them to Mahaprabhu's temple, listened to
both, and the next morning accepted initiation: Harirama from Ramachandra, Ramakrishna from Narottama.[^15] <!-- F49 -->

Harirama Acharya, a composer of padas, became Ramachandra's beloved disciple. The "Bhakti-ratnakara" says: "The dearest
disciple of Srinivasa Acharya is Ramachandra Kaviraja, peerless in qualities; Sri Ramachandra's disciple is Harirama
Acharya" (15.113–114). On Srinivasa's instruction, Ramachandra and Harirama preached the conclusions of the Gosvamis
everywhere. <!-- F50 -->

## The Stalls in the Kumarapura Market
The smarta brahmanas, having conspired to humiliate Narottama, set out for Kheturi with Raja Narasimha and the
digvijayi pandita Rupa-Narayana; the party was to halt for a day in Kumarapura. When the news reached Kheturi, Narottama
told Ramachandra that a debate would disturb their bhajana, and Ramachandra promised with a smile that their pride would
fall without effort ("Narottama-vilasa," 10.165–187). Ramachandra and Sri Ganganarayana Chakravarti opened two stalls in
the Kumarapura market: Ramachandra sold pots like a potter, and Ganganarayana sold betel. The panditas' students came to
buy pots and betel, and the sellers addressed them in Sanskrit and drew them into debate. The teachers, coming up, were
amazed at their learning; finally Rupa-Narayana and the raja arrived. The digvijayi entered the debate — and conceded
defeat before the conclusions of the "Bhagavata." The smartas were refuted, and pure bhakti was established. The raja
asked who these merchants were, and was told that they were the most insignificant disciples of Narottama, "servants of
his servants."[^16] "If you cannot overcome a mere disciple of his," said the raja, "how will you overcome him?" — and the
smartas, not daring to go further, turned back. <!-- F51 -->

Thus even a humble disciple of a pure devotee, by the power of the "Bhagavata's" conclusions, defeats proud scholars.
After this Durga in a dream told Raja Narasimha and Rupa-Narayana to seek shelter with Narottama, and he initiated
them.[^17] <!-- I5 F52 -->

## Kheturi and Jahnava
By Ramachandra's mercy many sinners and unbelievers were delivered. At the great festival in Kheturi he was one of the
leaders: together with others he prepared the invitations ("Bhakti-ratnakara," 10.274), and one of the biographies says
that Srinivasa, his disciple Ramachandra and Ramachandra's brother Govinda worked hard to make the festival a success.
<!-- F53 F54 -->

When Jahnava Devi, Nityananda's consort, was returning from Vrindavana, Ramachandra and Narottama went out to meet her
("Bhakti-ratnakara," 11.302–308). Narottama gave him the "Gopala-virudavali" sent by the Gosvamis (11.355); later
Ramachandra handed this book to Srinivasa (11.680). When Jahnava asked for news of Gauda, Narottama could not utter a word
for tears, and Ramachandra answered: "The Lord's dear ones, in this short time, have almost all gone into concealment;
those who still remain are also all but hidden from sight" (11.341–342). Together with Narottama he escorted her from
Kheturi across the Padmavati to Budhari, and from there toward Ekachakra (11.361–404). One of the biographies says that
Jahnava spent some time in Budhari, "the home of Ramachandra Kaviraja"; according to another, in Teliya-Budhari she was
received by Govinda. <!-- F55 -->

## Writings
Ramachandra wrote the "Smarana-chamatkara," the "Smarana-darpana," the "Siddhanta-chandrika" and a life of Srinivasa
Acharya (*Shrinivasacharyera jivana-charita*). One of the biographies also ascribes to him the pada "Dekha dekha are bhai,
Gauranga-chanda parakasha," signed "Ramachandra Dasa": Gaura has risen like the full moon to dispel the darkness of Kali;
in Kali there is no other way than the Hare Krishna mantra; the flood of prema has saved all sinners — only the poet has
not received a drop. So speaks a humble Vaishnava of himself.[^18] <!-- F56 F57 -->

## Departure
With the permission of Narottama and Srinivasa — according to one of the biographies, at their command — Ramachandra went
to Vrindavana once more. Almost all the Gosvamis had already withdrawn from sight, and he lived there with an aching
heart; after a few days (according to another passage, a few months), meditating on the lotus feet of Sri Radha and
Govinda, the Lord and Lady of Vraja, he entered Their eternal pastimes. The sources say nothing of his samadhi.
<!-- F58 F61 -->

The "Narottama-vilasa" tells it this way. Narottama and Ramachandra spent some days in Kheturi in great bliss and one day
took counsel together in private. Then Ramachandra, in great anguish, went to Yajigrama; soon news came that Sri Acharya
had gone to Vrindavana and that Ramachandra was constantly with him. Narottama, unable to find peace, sang of Gauranga's
companions whom he had not been granted to see: "He has gone, leaving me; Ramachandra has not come back," — and as soon as
he said this, all heard that Ramachandra Kaviraja had passed from sight. Narottama went into a lonely forest and wept aloud:
"O Ramachandra, where have you gone, leaving me?" ("Narottama-vilasa," 11.212–221).[^19] In the depth of separation
Narottama composed the song "Ye anila prema-dhana karuna prachura…" — "He who brought the treasure of prema, boundlessly
merciful…" <!-- F60 Q11 -->

The biographies give the day of his departure differently — the third day of the dark fortnight of Pausha, or of Magha; in
the calendar of the Sri Chaitanya Saraswat Math it is Madhava-masa, krishna-tritiya: "Disappearance of Srila Ramachandra
Kaviraja."[^20] No source gives the year. <!-- F59 -->

## Significance
In Krishna's eternal pastimes Ramachandra Kaviraja is, according to later sources, Karuna-manjari, a maidservant of Sri
Radha.[^21] Later traditions ("Prema-vilasa," 19; "Karnananda," 3; "Anuraga-valli," 6) tell of his flawless obedience to
his guru, and of how Srinivasa, absorbed in samadhi, was searching in the form of Mani-manjari for Sri Radha's nose-ring,
lost during the water pastimes, and Ramachandra, entering meditation, found it in the form of Karuna-manjari and brought
his guru back from samadhi. <!-- F2 F45 -->

He came into the world when the Lord had already withdrawn, and lived his whole life among those who carried the legacy
of the Gosvamis to Gauda: he was Srinivasa's disciple and servant, Narottama's second self, the beloved of Jiva and of
Raghunatha Dasa, the instructor of King Vira Hambira and the guru of Harirama. His learning and poetic gift — "the king of
poets" — were wholly given to the service of Krishna: in his answer to the shaktas and at the potter's stall in the
Kumarapura market alike. And the Lord Himself told him: "You are dear to Me, and Narottama is dear to Me… you two will
bestow My prema-bhakti." <!-- F3 F47 F50 F53 F54 -->
