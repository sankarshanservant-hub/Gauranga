# Damayanti
*Дамаянти · @damayanti*

## The Sister of Raghava Pandita
In Panihati, on the bank of the Ganga, lived Raghava Pandita, one of Sri Chaitanya's first followers, and with him his
sister Damayanti. Krishnadasa Kaviraja calls her “a dear maidservant of the Lord” (*prabhura priya dasi*); the
biographies call her Damayanti and Damayanti Devi, and Narahari Chakravarti “loving (*snehavati*) Damayanti Devi.”[^1]
A line of the “Pata-paryatana,” verses on the holy places of Gauda, remembers brother and sister:

“In the village of Panihati is the abode of Raghava and Damayanti; of it the tale is told that goes by the name
‘Raghava's jhalis.’”

So their home entered the memory of Gauda: through the hampers (*jhalis*) that left it every year for Nilachala, for the
Lord. <!-- F1 F2 F5 Q7 -->

Of her life in the world the sources tell almost nothing more. Neither the year and day of her appearance are known,
nor when and where she left this world; nor are her days found in the Vaishnava calendar of the Sri Chaitanya Saraswat
Math — as her brother's days are not found there either. Yet of her service to the Lord Krishnadasa Kaviraja tells in
greater detail than of almost anyone's: a whole part of a chapter of the “Chaitanya-charitamrita” he gave to a single
list of what she prepared for Mahaprabhu.

## Gunamala
Who she is in Krishna's eternal pastimes, Kavi Karnapura tells us. First, of her brother:

“Sri Kashishvara Gosvami is the former Shashirekha in Vraja. Dhanishtha, who in Vraja gave Krishna countless delicacies,
is now Raghava Pandita, dear to Gauranga.” (“Gaura-ganoddesha-dipika” 166)

And next, of her:

“She who in Vraja was Gunamala is Damayanti, his sister.” (“Gaura-ganoddesha-dipika” 167)

So the biographies identify her, and so does Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati in his “Anubhashya” on CC Adi 10.25.
Gunamala is a gopi of Vraja; she who served Krishna in Vraja came into Gaura's pastimes together with the Lord and
together with her brother, whose service in Vraja too was in delicacies for Krishna. What her flavour of service
(*rasa*) is in Vraja, Karnapura does not say, and we shall not name it: her care for the Lord, of which we shall speak
below, is touching and full of tenderness, but the sources give no ground for counting it either as parental or as
friendly love. It is enough that Kavi Karnapura named her among the eternal associates. <!-- F3 F4 Q1 -->

## “A Dear Maidservant of the Lord”
Krishnadasa Kaviraja, listing the branches of Sri Chaitanya's tree, names Raghava, his disciple Makaradhvaja and his
sister:

“Raghava Pandita was one of the Lord's first followers, and his chief branch is Makaradhvaja Kara. His sister Damayanti,
a dear maidservant of the Lord, prepared provisions for Him all year round. Filling bags with all those provisions,
Raghava would carry them secretly to the Lord. All year round the Lord accepted them, and they became famous as
‘Raghava's bags.’” (CC, Adi 10.24–27)

And he adds:

“I shall describe those provisions in detail later. Hearing of them, the devotees shed streams of tears.”
(CC, Adi 10.28) <!-- F6 Q2 F22 -->

All twelve months of the year (*vara-masi*) Damayanti cooked for the Lord, and her offerings were so many that they
lasted Him a whole year. She did not simply cook: she considered which dish would do the Lord good at which season,
and in each season she prepared what was fitting. Long-keeping pickles (*niskri* — made without water or boiled rice),
other foods and gathered provisions for His daily meals she lovingly packed into boxes and bags and sent to Nilachala
with Raghava and other devotees. Every year Raghava brought the Lord countless foods dear to Him, prepared by his
sister, and Krishnadasa Kaviraja described them in the tenth chapter of the “Antya-lila.” <!-- F7 F8 F9 F23 -->

Damayanti and Raghava knew that Mahaprabhu is the Supreme Lord Himself. And yet their love for Him was so strong that
it veiled this knowledge: she cared for His health as one cares for someone near and dear. She cooked at her brother's
bidding (*ajna*), and Kaviraja says of them both:

“Raghava gave the orders, and Damayanti carried them out. Both had deep affection and supreme devotion for the Lord.”
(CC, Antya 10.34)

Her cooking was not housekeeping, nor the art of the kitchen, but the service (*seva*) of an eternal associate. What she
prepared was an offering (*naivedya*) to the Lord; what remained from Him Kaviraja calls *prasada*. <!-- F10 F13 -->

## Raghava's Jhalis
Every year in the month of Ashadha, for the Ratha-yatra, the devotees of Gauda went to Puri to see the Lord, and each
brought something for His service. With them went Raghava Pandita with his hampers. Damayanti prepared what was in them,
and Raghava carried them and secretly brought them to the Lord:

“Raghava Pandita set out with his *jhalis* (hampers), packed with all the things that Damayanti had prepared and given
him. There were many wonderful foods, offerings worthy of the Lord, enough for Him to partake of for a whole year.”
(CC, Antya 10.13–14)

Whether she herself went to Puri, the “Chaitanya-charitamrita” does not say.[^2] <!-- F11 F12 -->

When everything was ready, Damayanti packed the *jhalis* herself, and here her care reached down to the smallest
things:

“She brought clay from the Ganga, strained it through cloth, mixed in fragrant substances and made it into *panch-kudi*
(thin wafers). She filled thin clay pots with sandalwood paste and other things, and packed everything else in little
cloth bags. She made *jhalis* twice the size of ordinary ones and neatly filled them all. She tied up the *jhalis* and
carefully sealed them. Three porters carried them, taking turns.” (CC, Antya 10.35–38)

The seal was set on the *jhalis* so that no stranger would open them on the way. <!-- F17 -->

“I have briefly described these *jhalis*, which became famous as ‘Raghava's *jhalis*.’” (CC, Antya 10.39)

By that name — “Raghava's jhalis” — her service has remained in the memory of the Vaishnavas. On the road they were
guarded by Raghava's disciple Makaradhvaja Kara of Panihati:

“Makaradhvaja Kara was the *munsib* (steward) of the *jhalis*; he guarded them vigilantly, as if they were his very
life.” (CC, Antya 10.40)

As their steward he brought them to Puri every year with great diligence.[^3] <!-- F18 F19 Q5 -->

## What Was in the Jhalis
Krishnadasa Kaviraja gives almost in full the list of what Damayanti prepared for the Lord. It opens with relishes and
tart pickles:

“There were mango *kashandi* and the relishes called ginger and chili *kashandi*, lime and ginger with mango buds, and
other preparations; *amsi*, *ama-khanda*, *tailamra* (mango pickle in mustard oil) and *ama-satta*; and dried *shukta*
(bitter greens), carefully ground into powder.” (CC, Antya 10.15–16)

Then come *ladus* of coriander and anise cooked in sugar syrup; ginger with sugar and remedies for indigestion and
excess bile, each in its own little bag; about a hundred pickles of *kul* berries (*koli-shunthi*, *koli-churna*,
*koli-khanda*); coconut sweets and white *gangajali* balls, like the waters of the Ganga; long-keeping *khirsa*, sweets
of milk and cream, *amrita-karpura*; a whole bag of new cloth full of flat rice (*chida*) from sun-dried *shali* paddy;
*hurum* fried in ghee and cooked in syrup with camphor; *ladus* of ground fried *shali* rice with camphor, black pepper,
cloves and cardamom; *ukhra* of popped rice (*khai*); *ladus* of fried peas (CC, Antya 10.22–32). And Kaviraja ends
humbly:

“There were a thousand kinds of such foods, whose names I could not tell in this lifetime.” (CC, Antya 10.33)
<!-- F16 Q3 -->

Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati remarks in the “Anubhashya” that this description shows how skilled Krishnadasa
Kaviraja himself was in preparing offerings for Krishna, and that the words “whose names I could not tell” are his
humility. One of the biographies repeats this thought.[^4] <!-- I4 -->

## Shukta
Among all these sweets and pickles was one humble thing — *shukta*, dried bitter greens ground to powder. Of it Kaviraja
speaks especially:

“Do not look down on it because it is only *shukta*: the joy found in *shukta* is not found even in *panchamrita*.
Mahaprabhu receives the feeling (*bhava-grahi*): it is the affection alone that He accepts. So the *shukta* leaves and
*kashandi* gave Him great joy. Damayanti, in her affection, regarded the Lord as a human being (*manushya-buddhi*):
‘From heavy food, *ama* (indigestion) may sometimes form in His stomach. If He eats *shukta*, that *ama* will be
cured.’ Thinking of her affection, the Lord was delighted.” (CC, Antya 10.17–20) <!-- Q4 F14 F15 -->

Damayanti feared that rich, heavy food might harm the Lord and cause Him indigestion (*ama*).[^5] Against this she
prepared her *shukta*. Kaviraja says that she saw the Lord “as a human being” (*manushya-buddhi*). This is not worldly
ignorance, nor doubt of His divinity: she knew who He is. Srila Bhaktisiddhanta explains that this is how the people
of Gauda and Vraja love the Lord — with a natural (*svarasiki*) love. Their hearts, made of pure goodness, do not
remember His majesty (*aishvarya*), and they know Gaura-Krishna in His human form as one conquered by their pure love.
That is why Bhaktisiddhanta heads these verses: the evidence of Damayanti's pure, natural and extraordinarily deep love
for Gaura. Love surpasses the knowledge of majesty: even knowing that before her was the Supreme Lord Himself, she
cared for Him as for one dear to her, who needed her care. <!-- I2 -->

And the Lord is *bhava-grahi*, He who receives the feeling. He accepts not the thing but the love with which it is
offered, and so the plain bitter *shukta* pleased Him more than *panchamrita*. Nor was it only the taste that pleased
Him: thinking of Damayanti's love, He rejoiced. The *bhava* spoken of here, Srila Bhaktisiddhanta explains in the
“Anubhashya,” is not a worldly movement of the mind seeking its own pleasure, as with the prakrita-sahajiyas, but a
causeless, transcendental movement of the heart, born of pure goodness (*shuddha-sattva*) and turned only to Krishna's
joy. <!-- I3 -->

## How the Lord Accepted Her Service
Having brought the *jhalis* to Puri, Raghava handed them to the Lord's servant Govinda:

“Raghava handed the *jhalis* over to Govinda, and they were kept in a corner of the dining room. Govinda emptied the
previous year's *jhalis* and took them to another room, keeping them there to be filled with provisions.”
(CC, Antya 10.55–56)

The next day the Lord opened them Himself:

“Another day, when the Lord ate in seclusion, He opened Raghava's *jhalis* and looked at everything. He tasted a little
of every item and, finding them delicious and fragrant, praised them highly. The rest He kept in store for a whole
year, and at mealtimes Svarupa would take out a little and serve it. Sometimes, at night too, Svarupa would have Him
partake of a little; for the Lord surely enjoys what a devotee gives with faith (*shraddha*).” (CC, Antya 10.129–132)

So Govinda kept Damayanti's provisions, and Svarupa Damodara served them at the Lord's meals.[^6] “The Harmonist,”
published under the editorship of Srila Bhaktisiddhanta, writes that Govinda with great care gave the Lord to taste the
foods constantly sent by Raghava Pandita and Damayanti. Thus Mahaprabhu, who receives the feeling, accepted all year
round the loving service of brother and sister. <!-- F20 F21 -->

Here lies the secret of her service. The wish to please Krishna's senses — that is prema:

“The desire to please one's own senses is called lust (*kama*); the wish to please Krishna's senses bears the name of
prema.” (CC, Adi 4.165)

And so a devotee, offering the Lord his gift, seeks not his own joy but His alone.[^7] Damayanti spent months thinking of
what would do the Lord good and what would please Him — and of herself the sources have kept not a word.
<!-- I1 Q6 -->

## Her Memory
Narahari Chakravarti, in the song that concludes the “Bhakti-ratnakara,” prays to serve, birth after birth, the feet of
the Lord's associates, and names among them Madhava Mishra and Ratnavati, Malini, Madhavi and “loving Damayanti Devi.”
And in the opening of his “Namamrita-samudra” he calls to the Lord's women associates:

“O Madhavi, Malini, Damayanti, O Sri Sita! Who but you is Gauranga's protector?” <!-- F24 F25 -->

## Significance
Damayanti did not preach or write books, and the stories about her are few. But Krishnadasa Kaviraja called her “a dear
maidservant of the Lord” and gave a whole part of a chapter to her offerings, and the devotees, he says, weep to hear of
them. Her life was a service of love: all year long she thought of the Lord, of what was good for Him and what would
gladden Him, and she put that thought into every little bag. Bitter *shukta* offered with such love proved dearer to
the Lord than *panchamrita*, for He accepts the feeling, not the thing. Her care for Him, as for one near and dear, is
not ignorance but the natural love of the people of Gauda and Vraja, before which the knowledge of His majesty grows
dim. With this love Damayanti won the Lord: thinking of it, He rejoiced. <!-- F6 F14 F15 I1 I2 I3 -->
