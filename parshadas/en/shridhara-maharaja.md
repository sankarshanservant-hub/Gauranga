# Srila Bhakti Rakshaka Sridhar Deva Goswami Maharaj
*Srila Bhakti Rakshaka Sridhar Deva Goswami Maharaj · @shridhara-maharaja*

## Guardian of Devotion
Srila Bhakti Rakshaka Sridhar Deva Goswami Maharaj was a disciple of Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati Thakura, an
acharya in his line, and the founder of the Sri Chaitanya Saraswat Math in Navadvip. His godbrothers and disciples
called him Srila B. R. Sridhar Maharaj, Sripad Sridhar Maharaj, or simply Sridhar Dev. The name “Bhakti Rakshaka” —
“guardian of devotion” — was given him by his guru: Srila Bhaktisiddhanta saw in him a faithful exponent of his own
conclusions. <!-- F1 I1 -->

On the eve of his departure Srila Bhaktisiddhanta wished to hear from his lips the song of the feet of Sri Rupa
Manjari, and his godbrothers understood that on that day the guru had passed on to him the authority of the Rupanuga
Sampradaya, the line of Sri Rupa’s followers. From then on they honoured him as the sampradaya-acharya, and many of
them accepted sannyasa from him. He was revered by all as an eminent acharya of this line, yet he himself never
thought himself worthy of glory and remained deeply humble all his life. His disciple and successor, Srila Govinda
Maharaj, said that after the great wave of Srila Bhaktivedanta Swami Maharaj’s preaching had swept over the whole
world, “now a new wave has come — the preaching of Srila Sridhar Maharaj”. <!-- I3 F93 F152 -->

## Family and Appearance
He appeared on 12 October 1895 in Sripat Hapania, in the district of Burdwan, West Bengal — on the Krishna Navami of
the month of Damodara. The same lunar day also commemorates the appearance of Virachandra Prabhu, the son of
Nityananda[^1]. <!-- F42 F2 -->

He came of a respected brahmana family. His father’s name was Sri Upendra Chandra Deva Sharma Bhattacharya Vidyaratna,
his mother’s Srimati Gauri Bala Devi; they named their son Sri Ramendra Chandra Deva Sharma Bhattacharya[^2].
<!-- F41 -->

His birth was preceded by a sign. A relative of the family had gone on pilgrimage to receive the darshan of the Deity,
and in a dream the Deity handed her a beautiful, radiant infant. Remembering that her sister-in-law had no children,
she gave the child to her. When Ramendra was born, she recognised in him the very infant of her dream. <!-- F43 -->

Learning came easily to him, and from childhood his heart was drawn to God and to the sacred books. At primary school
he was always first in his class and twice skipped a year; early on, too, he showed an inborn inclination for
Sanskrit. “My memory can go back to the time when I was learning to read — I was five then,” Srila Sridhar Maharaj
recalled. From five to eight he lived in his uncle’s house and received his first schooling there, and his aunt helped
him study the “Ramayana” and the “Mahabharata”. Even then he was drawn to the ancient teachings — the Vedas and
Upanishads — and to the way of life of the orthodox part of society. <!-- F3 F44 F45 -->

At ten he moved on to middle school. In the school debating club he always took the side of the shastras and often
argued with teachers who championed “the Renaissance or the Reformation”. “I fought without fear of defeat,” he said.
At fourteen, by family tradition, he received the sacred thread of a brahmana. <!-- F46 F47 F4 -->

His mother cooked many tasty vegetable dishes for the whole family, but he would eat only rice stewed with bitter nim
leaves, which greatly upset her. While still a child he rose every day before sunrise and walked three miles to the
Ganges to bathe, picking lotuses on the way from the ponds nearby as offerings to the Ganges and to Lord Shiva. Once,
as he was walking to Jamapur, the universal form of the Lord suddenly appeared before his eyes. <!-- F49 -->

## Youth: Rama, Gaura, Krishna
He was born into a revered family of smarta brahmanas, yet from his earliest childhood he was drawn to the sankirtana
of Sri Chaitanya Mahaprabhu. <!-- F53 -->

“In life I value self-sacrifice and magnanimity,” Srila Sridhar Maharaj said. That is why at first he especially loved
Lord Ramachandra, for His flawless deeds. For Krishna he did not at first feel the same attachment: the Vrindavan-lila
was then beyond his understanding. And yet, looking at the murti of Radha-Govinda, he felt the charm that came from
Them. His first attraction to Krishna was awakened by the “Bhagavad-gita”, and later by Mahaprabhu. He admired
Radharani and loved Mahaprabhu with all his heart — and They led him to Krishna: “Mahaprabhu and Radharani showed me
mercy — it is They who helped me attain love for Krishna.” So the young man’s perplexity before the Lord’s most
intimate pastimes was dispelled by the mercy of Those who Themselves bring love for Him to the world. <!-- F48 F5 -->

While still at school he read in a book that japa of the gayatri 432 times a day brings swift progress on the
spiritual path, and for some time he did just that, trying to grasp the meaning of the mantra without a grammar or a
dictionary. “I was convinced: the Gayatri mantra is not lifeless words — it is spiritual and full of consciousness,”
he recalled; and so, if he chanted it, it would itself reveal its meaning and purpose. Many years later he would write
the “Gayatri-nigudhartha”, an explanation of the hidden meaning of the gayatri (of which more below). <!-- F54 -->

## College, a Turning Point, the Search for a Guru
After school he entered Krishnanath College in Berhampore, in the district of Murshidabad. In those years an
astrologer looked at his palm and foretold that the young man would become a perfect, enlightened personality, would
be famed throughout the world as a preacher of Hari-katha, and would leave this world at the age of ninety-three. (He
departed in 1988, in the ninety-third year of his life.) Textbooks held little interest for him: he read the
“Chaitanya-charitamrita” and the “Chaitanya-bhagavata” far more — and still passed his examinations, while many
diligent students failed. Four years later he became a Bachelor of Arts in philosophy and took up the study of law.
<!-- F6 F50 F51 -->

The turning point came unexpectedly. One day a fellow student referred to his own father as “that gentleman” and
explained: where was I in my past lives, and who were my parents then? He is my father only in this life. The words
cut him to the quick. “He is right. We are all almost strangers,” he thought, and the longer he thought, the more
keenly he felt emptiness and loneliness. “It was a great shock and a great upheaval in my life,” Srila Sridhar Maharaj
recalled. Worldly achievements lost all meaning for him, and he neglected his studies. <!-- F55 -->

At that time a friend gave him books on the life and teachings of Mahaprabhu. He read them eagerly, as if drinking
nectar, and found in them what he had longed for: hope, support and shelter. His attachment to Sri Chaitanyadeva, he
thought, had come to him from a past life. His mother’s family revered Mahaprabhu, but his father’s kin — orthodox
smarta brahmanas, worshippers of Shakti and logicians of the Nyaya school — were hostile to His followers. Even so, he
surrendered to Chaitanyadeva not only with his mind but with his heart: “I underwent a complete conversion.” He began
to look for devotees of Gauranga and for a sadhu whom he could accept as his guru, but for a long time he found none.
<!-- F56 F57 F7 -->

Soon afterwards his father died, broken by the change in his eldest son, on whom he had placed the family’s hopes. On
his deathbed he asked his son to promise that he would marry. Sridhar Maharaj had never told a lie and could not give
his word where he was not sure of keeping it; but his father begged him not to grieve him in the hour of death, and he
answered, “I will try to fulfil your request.” He had to keep his word — he married; his family life lasted six years.
<!-- F58 -->

Meanwhile Mahatma Gandhi’s civil disobedience movement was gaining strength, promising independence — swaraj — within
a year. In 1921, before finishing law college, he joined it: he wished to become a sannyasi, but resolved to give that
year to his countrymen. On behalf of the movement he worked as a secretary in Kalna; so two or three years went by.
Then he withdrew from its affairs, but later he was called to Calcutta, where he acquired another profession and held
an executive post. <!-- F52 -->

His search for a guru went on. A college friend introduced him to a man of his own age — Paramananda, the founder of
the Bharat Sevashram, a man of action (a karmi). Paramananda urged him to join his organisation and argued that
Mahaprabhu’s teaching is the highest, but one must first acquire detachment by following the Buddha and then study
Shankara’s Vedanta; otherwise prema may easily be mistaken for ordinary worldly love. He objected: why, then, did
Chaitanyadeva Himself say nothing of the kind? On the contrary, He taught: whoever you are, take the Name and the
company of sadhus — and so you will come to Me. Paramananda had no answer. Then he asked him to say where a guru could
be found; Paramananda gave him an address, but he found no one there. <!-- F59 -->

## Meeting Srila Bhaktisiddhanta
One day he came across a poster: the Gaudiya Math was inviting people to a festival that ran for a whole month at No.
1 Ultadanga, not far from the Pareshnath temple. He went, reasoning that a movement called “Gaudiya” must have to do
with Sri Chaitanyadeva. He found only one man there — the rest had gone out on nagara-sankirtana — and even in a short
conversation he saw that here the scriptures were known more deeply and thoroughly than he knew them. Then the
devotees returned, and for the first time he saw Srila Bhaktisiddhanta — “Guru Maharaj”, as he always called his guru
— with the danda of a wandering monk in his hand. “At first sight I understood that Guru Maharaj was completely
indifferent to the world. Nothing could disturb him — he was completely self-sufficient.” From that day on he began to
visit the Math. <!-- F60 -->

He was drawn by these people’s faith in the scriptures. Worldly authorities counted for nothing here: if anyone began
to speak of the ideas of Aurobindo, Vivekananda, Ramakrishna or Gandhi, he was quickly cut short — the measure of
everything was Mahaprabhu, the “Bhagavatam” and the “Gita”. Gradually the true meaning of the “Gita” and the
“Bhagavatam” began to open to him, and he understood that Sri Chaitanyadeva and the “Chaitanya-charitamrita” are the
highest ideal of spiritual knowledge. <!-- F61 -->

He longed to see and hear Guru Maharaj himself. But once, when he wanted to go upstairs to his room, Kirtanananda
Brahmachari stopped him: this threshold was not to be crossed without permission. It sounded harsh, and he thought,
“Why do they divide people here into their own and outsiders? I shall not come here again.” But at once he remembered
a story from the “Mahabharata”: Vyasadeva sent Shukadeva to study with the royal sage Janaka, and at each of the seven
gates of the capital Shukadeva was kept waiting seven days — forty-nine days he waited for the meeting. And he
reasoned: were there something wrong here, while he was surrounded with flattery and honour, it would be right to
leave; “but if I can attain the true good here, then even if they beat me with a broom — I must not leave!”
<!-- F62 -->

Soon their first conversation took place. After his meal Guru Maharaj was walking on the roof, and he was standing
nearby. Through a companion Srila Bhaktisiddhanta asked whether he wished to say something, whether he had any
questions, and at last what purpose had brought him. “To attain your mercy at any cost,” he answered. “I need nothing
else.” Then the guru came over himself, asked who he was, where he came from and what he did, and said, “You are
undoubtedly fortunate. You were born within Gaura-mandala.” The companion handed him a newly printed booklet — a gift
from Guru Maharaj. He already had one, but he was told: take it — this is something more than a mere gift.
<!-- F63 -->

His decision ripened at one of the Math’s festivals. Srila Bhaktisiddhanta was speaking to a large gathering of
educated guests from Krishnanagar; elsewhere Sripad Bhakti Pradip Tirtha Maharaj was speaking, Sripad Bhakti Svarupa
Parvat Maharaj was receiving donations, and the Math was alive like a beehive. When the festival was over and the
guests, having bowed to the guru, were going home, he suddenly said, “Do not deceive me.” You came to dedicate
yourselves to the service of Krishna, and now you are leaving to plunge again into worldly affairs, thinking: when I
am free, I shall come to the Math. No! Even if a fire is burning somewhere and you hurry to put it out — there is no
need: “Even if fire engulfs the whole world, you will lose nothing.” “Your needs and desires will be fulfilled only in
the service of Krishna’s lotus feet, and in no other way.” Never before had he heard Krishna-bhajana spoken of with
such force and urgency — and he understood that here was where he must bow his head. He resolved to become a disciple
of Srila Bhaktisiddhanta. <!-- F64 -->

He was drawn to the company of the noble and learned men who had given their lives to Mahaprabhu’s service. What kept
him from coming and staying? His father was dead; his mother and three younger brothers would manage without him — but
his wife would be unhappy. That same evening, on returning home, he learned that his wife had suddenly fallen ill;
three days later she was gone. His mother tried to marry him again, but in vain. <!-- F65 -->

In those days he was invited to Mayapur for the festival of Sriman Mahaprabhu’s appearance. His mother, too, was
calling him — to the village of Gumo, where she had stopped with one of her sons before a journey to Haridwar: her
health was failing, and the meeting might be their last. The choice was hard, but he went to Mayapur. Six months after
his wife’s death his mother passed away in Haridwar during the Kumbha Mela; before her death she was anxious about who
would perform her shraddha if her eldest son became a sannyasi. He came, fulfilled a son’s duty, and returned to join
the Math. At first he had thought to do so once his younger brothers had finished their studies and found work, but
Sripad Bharati Maharaj and others persuaded him not to delay: some new obstacle would surely arise later, and this
life would pass in vain. <!-- F66 F67 -->

So he became a member of the Gaudiya Math, and little by little his doubts were dispelled. Soon many, even senior
godbrothers, began to ask him to explain difficult passages in the lectures of Srila Bhaktisiddhanta — “Srila
Prabhupada”, as his disciples called him — and his explanations gave everyone great satisfaction; the educated part of
the mission acknowledged that he presented Mahaprabhu’s teaching very simply and clearly. <!-- F68 -->

In 1926 he left home for good and dedicated himself entirely to the service of the mission. Srila Bhaktisiddhanta was
overjoyed that such a learned man had come to Sriman Mahaprabhu’s mission, and greeted it with a verse of the
“Bhagavad-gita” (3.21):

*yad yad ācarati śreṣṭhas tat tad evetaro janaḥ*  
*sa yat pramāṇaṁ kurute lokas tad anuvartate*

“People follow the example of a great man. Whatever conclusion such a man accepts as truth, the rest agree with it.”
<!-- F69 Q7 -->

So after a long search, by the mercy of the Supreme, he found his guru. According to one of the biographies, he
received initiation from Srila Bhaktisiddhanta in 1927[^3]. <!-- F7 -->

## Preacher of the Gaudiya Math; Sannyasa
At first he lived in the Calcutta Math and served in the printing press, reading proofs. This work, he admitted, did
not particularly inspire him: what drew him most was preaching. This was noticed, and soon he was sent out to preach.
First they stayed at a Math in Bengal, then in Benares, and finally in Vrindavan, where they held nagara-sankirtana;
then he was sent to Delhi to collect donations. When a Math was founded at Kurukshetra, he was put in charge of it;
for two or three years he laboured there alone — Kurukshetra is deserted except on the days of solar eclipses, when
hundreds of thousands of pilgrims flock there. Then a Math was established in Delhi, and he headed it; later he
preached in Shimla and other places. <!-- F70 -->

Then Srila Bhaktisiddhanta sent them to Madras. The party lived in a rented house; it was led by the senior sannyasi,
Sripad Bon Maharaj. He himself was known as “Sri Ramendrasundar Bhattacharya, Bachelor of Arts”: he addressed the
public, introduced the sannyasis, and sometimes collected donations. But Bon Maharaj once remarked that this devotee
could preach well — better that he preach than collect donations. He also encouraged Srila Bhakti Dayita Madhava while
the latter was building the Madras Gaudiya Math: the temple in Madras rose through the labours of them both, of Bon
Maharaj and of others. <!-- F71 F11 -->

Even before sannyasa he was one of the outstanding preachers of the Math, and Srila Bhaktisiddhanta was very pleased
with him. He spoke of him as “a worthy person to continue the spiritual succession”; valuing his deep knowledge of the
scriptures and spiritual insight, he called him shastra-nipuna, “expert in the conclusions of the scriptures”, and
once niveditatma, “a pure soul wholly surrendered to the Supreme”. In October 1930, at the first Gaudiya Math on
Ultadanga Road, Srila Bhaktisiddhanta gave him sannyasa and the name “Srimad Bhakti Rakshaka Sridhar” — “Guardian of
Devotion” — for his supreme devotion and strict fidelity to the ideals of the Rupanuga Sampradaya. The guru saw in him
the strength to guard the line of the Gaudiya Vaishnavas against distortion. His own disciple, the translator of one
of the biographies, also calls him “sarva-shastra-siddhanta-vit” — knower of the conclusions of all scriptures.
<!-- F72 F73 F8 F10 -->

When Bon Maharaj left to preach in England, he took charge of the Math in Madras. Srila Bhaktisiddhanta wanted to send
him to London too, but he replied: “I am not fit for this. I do not understand their pronunciation, and I have no wish
to mix closely with them. You will spend a great deal of money on my journey, but I shall not be able to justify Your
hopes. Of course, if You order me, I will go.” And in his place the guru sent Sriyukta A. B. Goswami Prabhu — later
Srila Bhakti Saranga Goswami Maharaj. <!-- F74 -->

He travelled widely through India, preaching in Bengali, Hindi, Sanskrit and English; he had an excellent command of
English and was uncommonly eloquent. Tirelessly carrying the message of Sri Chaitanyadeva, he lectured in colleges and
universities, held public seminars and arranged sankirtana festivals. Once in Garidi, in Bihar, after his lecture in
the local law library, a listener bitterly admitted: we fancied ourselves civilised because we mix with the English
and thought our ancestors savages — your talk has shown us what a great heritage we possess and how low we have
fallen. Another time, also in Garidi, the chairman of a scientific conference, having heard him, concluded: until now
we regarded Vaishnava-dharma as a branch of Hinduism, but the arguments and the evidence of scripture have convinced
us that it is the original religion — jaiva-dharma, the religion of all souls. <!-- F75 F76 -->

Together with Srila Bhakti Dayita Madhava, Bhakti Pradip Tirtha and Jajabar Maharaj he also went out collecting
donations for the mission. <!-- F15 -->

## Sarbhog and the Hymns to Srila Bhaktivinoda
In March 1936 Srila Bhaktisiddhanta sent him with Srila Bhakti Dayita Madhava and others to Sarbhog in Assam, to
prepare the installation of the Deities of Sri Sri Guru-Gauranga and Gandharvika-Giridhari. By the guru’s order he
directed the ceremony and dressed and adorned the Deities beforehand. Seeing this, Srila Bhaktisiddhanta said, “The
Deities are already installed,” and Sridhar Maharaj grew sorrowful, thinking he had committed an offence. He asked
Srila Bhakti Dayita Madhava to write to the guru and beg forgiveness for him. The reply came: one who is surrendered
commits no offence — the Lord does not see his faults. <!-- F12 F13 F14 -->

Of Srila Bhaktisiddhanta’s disciples, two became especially known for their hymns to Srila Bhaktivinoda Thakura:
Bhakti Vichar Jajabar wrote in Bengali, and Sridhar Maharaj in Sanskrit. In that same year, 1936, for the anniversary
of Srila Bhaktivinoda’s departure, he composed the “Srimad-Bhaktivinoda-viraha-dashakam”, ten verses on separation
from him, glorifying Bhaktivinoda and his place in the succession of Sri Chaitanya Mahaprabhu. The style and depth of
the poem so struck Srila Bhaktisiddhanta that he said to his sannyasi disciples Sripad Bhaktiyadava Goswami Maharaj
and Sripad Shrauti Maharaj: “It is Bhaktivinoda Thakura who speaks through him. I am glad that when I leave this world
there will remain at least one person able to present the siddhanta in all its fullness.” According to one of the
biographies, on reading these Sanskrit verses the guru said that he was now at peace: after him there would remain at
least one person able to present his conclusions — bhakti-siddhanta. <!-- F17 F18 F77 F9 -->

Srila Sridhar Maharaj himself recalled that he composed the poem about a year before his guru’s departure. When he
read it to Srila Bhaktisiddhanta, the latter remarked, “You have chosen the style very well.” Later he heard the guru
tell Sripad Shrauti Maharaj that the poem was so beautiful, and it was not written by him: its author was Srila
Bhaktivinoda Thakura himself, who had revealed his work through him — “to me this is perfectly clear.” And to Sriyukta
Aprakrita Prabhu Srila Bhaktisiddhanta said, “I am glad that my message will survive me — I see the siddhanta in this
verse.” Here is the ninth verse of the poem, on Srila Bhaktivinoda’s most hidden gift: <!-- F78 -->

*śrī-gaurānumataṁ svarūpa-viditaṁ rūpāgrajenādṛtaṁ*  
*rūpādyaiḥ pariveśitaṁ raghu-gaṇair āsvāditaṁ sevitam*  
*jīvādyair abhirakṣitaṁ śuka-śiva-brahmādi-sammānitaṁ*  
*śrī-rādhā-pada-sevanāmṛtam aho! tad dātum īśo bhavān*

“Approved by Sri Gaura, understood by Svarupa, revered by Sanatana, the elder brother of Rupa; offered to the world by
Rupa and his companions, relished and served by Raghunatha and his followers, guarded by Jiva and others, honoured by
Shuka, Shiva, Brahma and the rest — the nectar of service to the feet of Sri Radha! O wonder: and this you have the
power to bestow” (“Srimad-Bhaktivinoda-viraha-dashakam”, 9). <!-- Q4 Q1 -->

Another poem of his, the “Srimad-Bhaktivinoda-dashakam”, was printed in the journal “Gaudiya” in July 1939.
<!-- F19 -->

## The Guru’s Last Day
Srila Bhaktisiddhanta left this world on 1 January 1937[^4]. Just before his departure he wished to hear from the
lips of Srila Sridhar Maharaj Narottama Dasa Thakura’s bhajan “Sri Rupa-manjari-pada” — a hymn of the Gaudiya
Vaishnavas that expresses the whole siddhanta, the highest mood of devotion, and the special position of Srila Rupa
Gosvami. <!-- F79 -->

Srila Govinda Maharaj told the story thus. The disciples took turns watching at the guru’s bedside, each for two hours
a day, and Sridhar Maharaj chose the hardest time — from two to four in the morning, when sleep presses hardest. When
relieved, he did not sleep but only rested: an hour later the arati would begin. On the day before his departure Srila
Bhaktisiddhanta called together his closest disciples and, not seeing Sridhar Maharaj among them, said, “Call Sridhar
Maharaj.” When he came, the guru asked, “Sridhar Maharaj, sing *śrī-rūpa-mañjarī-pada, sei mora sampada*.” Everyone
understood that the guru would soon leave the world, and they hung on his every word. Sridhar Maharaj was not a
skilled singer and hesitated in embarrassment; then Kunjabihari Vidyabhushan — secretary and manager of the Gaudiya
Math, later Sripad Bhakti Vilasa Tirtha Maharaj — asked a brahmachari with a good voice to sing. But Srila
Bhaktisiddhanta said, “I do not want beautiful singing — I want to hear this bhajan from Sridhar Maharaj.” Everyone
was stunned. Deeply moved, Sridhar Maharaj began to sing, and the guru said, “I am happy.” Then he told others to sing
Srila Bhaktivinoda’s song “*tuhu doyā-sāgara tāraite prāṇī*” and others[^5]. <!-- F80 F16 -->

Srila Sridhar Maharaj himself recalled: “When Prabhupada fell ill, I was at his bedside serving him. Exactly one day
before his departure he called me and asked me to sing ‘Sri Rupa-manjari-pada’.” He was not yet used to leading
kirtana and lacked the resolve; Sriyukta Kunja Babu asked a brahmachari to sing, but the guru was displeased: “I do
not need correct intonation or sweet sounds.” “And I had to sing ‘Sri Rupa-manjari-pada’. Someone said, ‘Thus
Prabhupada has accepted you into the world of rasa-seva.’” <!-- F81 -->

His godbrothers said many times afterwards — and Srila Govinda Maharaj heard it from them himself: “Before, we did not
know how the transfer of authority takes place, but that day we witnessed it. Srila Prabhupada passed on the authority
of the Rupanuga Sampradaya to Srila Sridhar Maharaj.” From then on they showed him the greatest reverence as the
sampradaya-acharya of the Rupanuga line. <!-- F80 I3 -->

## Seclusion: Vrindavan, Ekachakra, Navadvip
After Srila Bhaktisiddhanta’s departure the affairs of the mission were to be managed by the trustees he had
appointed: by the guru’s will they were to form a governing body. “But, unfortunately for us... there were many
disagreements, and the Mission could not preserve its unity,” Srila Sridhar Maharaj recalled. As his conscience
bade, he did all he could to keep the movement from falling apart, but he sided with no party: “to take anyone’s side
in a fight against others was not my rule.” A lawsuit began, and some of his godbrothers were imprisoned; in those
days he did not leave: “I could not run away like a coward.” Only when the case was dropped about a year later did he
leave the Math and go to Vrindavan, though his godbrothers did everything to keep him. <!-- F82 -->

For a month he lived near Govardhana, keeping the vow of urja-vrata, and there he accepted a Govardhana-shila. On the
way back he met a relative, Mani Babu, who worked on the railway, and asked him to donate ten rupees a month; he
agreed and sent the money for two or three months. With this “capital” he came to Navadvip and rented a house for two
rupees a month. His godbrothers searched for him a long time, and when they found him they began to visit him — and
never came without gifts. <!-- F83 -->

Some leaders of the Gaudiya Math asked him to become acharya, but he had never wished for such a post. His
godbrothers hoped that he would give initiation to the new people whom the preaching would bring — but he refused,
because he “did not feel the confirmation of the heart.” Yet one anxiety would not leave him: Srila Bhaktisiddhanta
had not approved of nirjana-bhajana, solitary practice, and had always taught that devotees must gather together and
sing kirtana. <!-- F84 -->

He did not mean to stay long in Vrindavan. “Only perfected souls can live in Vrindavan,” he said; fallen souls should
rather seek the shelter of Mahaprabhu in Navadvip, at the aparadha-bhanjan-pat, the place where all offences are
forgiven. But Mahaprabhu’s mercy comes only by the mercy of Nityananda Prabhu. Narottama Dasa Thakura sings of it:
*heno nitāi bine bhāi, rādhā-kṛṣṇa pāite nāi* — “without such a Nitai, O brother, one cannot attain Radha and
Krishna.” Srila Sridhar Maharaj explained: “by the mercy of Nityananda we shall receive the mercy of Gauranga, and by
the mercy of Gauranga we shall attain the mercy of Radha-Govinda.” Such, he said, is the path shown long ago by Lord
Brahma: “A devotee of Krishna can easily correct and wholly change even what destiny has ordained for him.”
<!-- F85 Q5 -->

He resolved to settle for good in Koladvipa, near Gupta-Govardhana. But to take eternal shelter in Navadvip-dham one
needs the blessing of the dhameshvara, the Lord of the dham — Nityananda Prabhu. So he first went to Ekachakra, the
place of Nityananda’s appearance, and prayed: “You are patita-pavana, the saviour of the fallen. I pray for Your
mercy. Allow me to spend the rest of my days in Koladvipa, in Navadvip-dham, where I can find shelter.” And suddenly
he heard a voice within: “You pray for the mercy of Nityananda Prabhu and want to win His favour?” Nityananda saves
the fallen — while he himself did not wish to help others; how could he hope for His compassion? He pondered this
long and at last surrendered to the will of Nityananda Prabhu — and came to Navadvip. <!-- F85 F86 -->

In Navadvip he rented a room for two rupees a month. By day he studied the shastras; in the evening he chanted the
Holy Name and lay down to sleep only after completing a lakh — a hundred thousand Names. For Sri Giridhari, whom he
had brought from Vrindavan, he made a small throne in his room; he cooked once a day, offered the food to the Lord and
himself took a little prasada. “The days flowed by evenly.” <!-- F87 -->

At that time he was studying the books of Srila Jiva Gosvami. One who has wealth but does not give it to the Lord is
guilty, says Jiva, of vitta-shathya, miserliness; likewise one who has understood the science of bhakti but does not
share it with others is guilty of jnana-shathya. He remembered a Puranic story heard in childhood: a great learned
brahmana would share his knowledge with no one, and in his next life became a mango tree laden with beautiful fruit
that no one — neither man nor even bird — would touch; so the Lord answered Anantadeva, who asked about that tree’s
fate. Another thought came too: if a physician has not yet reached perfection, can he not help the sick? If everyone
waits until he is perfect, many will never live to receive help. “With these thoughts I decided that I would no
longer seek solitude, but would begin to help others.” He had no wish to preach widely or to have many disciples, but
he resolved to explain the fundamentals of the knowledge kept by the succession to everyone who came, and to give
initiation — remembering the instruction given him by Nityananda Prabhu, the patita-pavana: “You must do everything
possible to help those whom you consider to be sincere seekers.” So the humility and the love of quiet reflection on
the scriptures that had led him to decline the post of acharya gave way, by Nityananda’s will, to compassion for every
sincere soul. <!-- F88 I4 -->

Helping everyone, he did not, however, overexert himself: the “Bhakti-rasamrita-sindhu”, in listing the sixty-four
limbs of bhakti, warns against superfluous undertakings — not to build too many temples, not to hold large preaching
programmes — and he would quote a verse from it[^7]:

*bahu-granthakalābhyāsa-vyākhyā-vāda-vivarjanam*  
*vyavahāre ’py akārpaṇyaṁ śokādy-avaśa-vartitā*

“Giving up the study of many books and arts, of commentaries and disputes; generosity in worldly dealings; not
yielding to grief and the like.” <!-- Q6 -->

This very rule had once puzzled him. Reading the “Bhakti-rasamrita-sindhu” with his disciples, Guru Maharaj came to
the sixty-four limbs of devotion — yet he himself preached widely, collected huge sums and built temples. What would
he say of this verse? But in two words the guru dispelled all doubt: “Everything depends on capacity. One person can
rule an empire and still have time to rest. Another wears himself out trying to cope with family cares, and still
does not succeed.” Each must do what is within his power. <!-- F89 -->

## Sri Chaitanya Saraswat Math
In 1941 Srila Sridhar Maharaj founded the Sri Chaitanya Saraswat Math[^6]. At first it was only a thatched hut on the
bank of the Ganges, but even then he composed a shloka that foretold the future worldwide glory of the Math. The plot
with its tumbledown hut was given to him by Sriyukta Sakhi Charan Ray, who bought it with his own money. Before that
he had lived for some time in a rented house together with Sripad Keshava Maharaj, and sometimes at the math in
Midnapore, where Sripad Jajabar Maharaj, Sripad Madhava Maharaj and others lived. In 1942, on the day of the
Ratha-yatra, he came to this hut for the first time with his Govardhana-shila. “But here I lived separately,” he
recalled. At first there was only one person with him; then another came and brought several devotees from Orissa;
in time others joined as well, among them some of his godbrothers, and the mission began to grow. <!-- F90 F92 -->

The Math stands in Kuliya, ancient Koladvipa, where Devananda Pandita lived — at the place called aparadha-bhanjana,
“where offences are forgiven”: here Mahaprabhu forgave all who had once offended Him in Navadvip. It was here, to the
feet of the Lord who forgives the fallen, that he had longed to come when he left Vrindavan. The lake beside the Math
was revealed to him as Govinda-kunda — the very one in Vrindavan, manifest in Navadvip as well; under Srila Govinda
Maharaj there rose on its banks the main temple of the Math, a temple of Shiva, the Nat-mandir, the
Chandan-yatra-mandir and other buildings. <!-- F24 F25 F158 F149 -->

He explained the name of the Math thus: “‘Chaitanya Saraswat’ points to the succession that runs from Chaitanya to
Sarasvati, our Gurudeva”: here Srila Bhaktisiddhanta’s teaching is presented in its true light, and “the purity of
his teaching is the object of our care. Our mission is the preservation, preaching, propagation and development of
the teaching of our whole guru-parampara from Chaitanya to Sarasvati.” <!-- F91 -->

Many godbrothers saw in him the acharya of the Rupanuga line and came to him for counsel. At their request he gave
sannyasa to Srila Bhakti Saranga Goswami Maharaj, to Srila Bhakti Prajnana Keshava Maharaj — his first sannyasa
disciple — to Bhakti Aloka Paramahamsa Maharaj, Bhakti Kamal Madhusudan Maharaj, Narasimha Maharaj, Bhagavat Maharaj,
Acharya Maharaj and others; practically all the senior disciples of Srila Bhaktisiddhanta either took sannyasa from
him or asked him from whom to take it. Many regarded him as their shiksha-guru — among them Srila A. C. Bhaktivedanta
Swami, Bhakti Prajnana Keshava, Bhakti Saranga Goswami, Bhakti Dayita Madhava and Bhakti Pramoda Puri. Akinchana
Krishnadasa Babaji, Bhakti Pramoda Puri and other revered Vaishnavas were convinced that Srila Sarasvati Thakura had
passed the authority of acharya of the Rupanuga Sampradaya to him. Bhakti Pramoda Puri, Bhakti Prajnana Keshava and
Akinchana Krishnadasa Babaji remained his close companions all their lives, and Srila Bhakti Ballabha Tirtha Maharaj,
too, received his blessings and close association. <!-- F93 F94 F95 F20 F22 F23 -->

Once, during a parikrama of Vrindavan, on his appearance day, Srila Bhakti Saranga Goswami Maharaj, one of the senior
preachers among Srila Bhaktisiddhanta’s disciples, himself worshipped him — offering incense and a lamp of a hundred
and eight wicks — in the presence of many of his own disciples and godbrothers. Srila Govinda Maharaj recorded his
words on tape: “Going forward, the lion often looks back. In Sanskrit this is called simhavalokanam. We go forward,
putting all our strength into preaching, but sometimes we look back to learn what Srila Sridhar Maharaj thinks of
it.” <!-- F96 -->

## Writings
His first book was the “Sri Sri Prapanna-jivanamrita”, a thorough study of sharanagati, surrender to the Lord, on the
evidence of the scriptures. He wrote many songs, prayers and commentaries in Bengali and Sanskrit; among the most
important are his commentaries on Srila Bhaktivinoda’s “Sharanagati”, his Bengali translations of the
“Bhagavad-gita” and the “Bhakti-rasamrita-sindhu”, and his Sanskrit poem on Sri Chaitanya’s pastimes, the
“Prema-dhama-deva-stotram”. Akinchana Krishnadasa Babaji, a well-known disciple of Srila Sarasvati Thakura, used to
say that he saw no difference between his writings and those of Narottama Dasa Thakura. In English there appeared a
book of his talks, “Golden Volcano of Divine Love”, and his explanation of Vasu Ghosha’s song “yadi gaura na ha’ta”
is quoted in his book by Bhakti Vidhan Mahayogi Swami. <!-- F26 F27 F29 F33 F34 -->

An outstanding gift of his to the heritage of Sri Rupa’s followers is the “Gayatri-nigudhartha”, an explanation of
the gayatri mantra in the spirit of the “Srimad-Bhagavatam”; enlightened souls call it the highest treasure of the
Gaudiya Vaishnavas. The “bharga”, the effulgence on which the gayatri meditates, is Srimati Radharani, Krishna’s
svarupa-shakti, who manifests Herself as Goloka, as effulgence, and as the power of the Lord’s pastimes. The sound of
the gayatri is the song of Krishna’s flute, full of love for Radha, and the hidden meaning of the mantra is
“radha-padam dhimahi”: we meditate on Radha’s feet, we serve Her. This is Mahaprabhu’s highest teaching.
<!-- F28 I2 F31 Q2 -->

Srila Bhakti Pramoda Puri Maharaj wrote that this understanding of the Brahma-gayatri as centred on Srimati Radharani
was revealed to Sridhar Maharaj in the depth of realisation by the mercy of Srila Bhaktisiddhanta, and that it is the
best path to Krishna’s mercy. He recalled the words of the Upanishads, “shabalach chhyamam prapadye” — “through the
Fair One I come to the Dark One”, through Radha to Krishna. For his gift of revealing to sincere souls the hidden
meaning of the Lord’s pastimes of love, “Deva” was added to his name. <!-- F30 Q3 F32 -->

## Godbrothers and Disciples
Many godbrothers honoured him as their senior and regarded him as their shiksha-guru — among them Srila A. C.
Bhaktivedanta Swami, Bhakti Prajnana Keshava, Bhakti Saranga Goswami, Bhakti Dayita Madhava and Bhakti Pramoda Puri.
Srila Bhaktivedanta Swami wrote that he accepted his instructions “on my head”, that Sridhar Maharaj was his constant
well-wisher, that after the departure of Srila Sarasvati Thakura one should heed him, and that from him he had
received the instruction to remain “in this country” for good. <!-- F20 F21 -->

Srila Bhakti Pramoda Puri Maharaj was his close companion throughout life, as were Bhakti Prajnana Keshava and
Akinchana Krishnadasa Babaji. Srila Bhakti Ballabha Tirtha Maharaj, too, received his blessings and close
association. Bhakti Vidhan Mahayogi Swami calls both him and Srila Bhaktivedanta Swami his spiritual masters.
<!-- F22 F23 F40 -->

## Last Years and Departure
When he spoke of the Lord’s pastimes his speech was wondrous: his voice and every movement breathed love, and one
disciple called him “beauty, sweetness and charm in motion”. In his later years devotees came from all over the world
to hear from him the teaching of Srila Bhaktisiddhanta and the previous acharyas, and he spoke with them at length.
These talks were recorded on audio and video, transcribed, and are published by the Sri Chaitanya Saraswat Math and
his followers. <!-- F35 F36 -->

In his last days he was absorbed in the mood of separation and in longing to serve Sri Sri Radha-Govinda. Srila
Bhakti Rakshaka Sridhar Deva Goswami Maharaj entered the eternal pastimes in 1988, on the Amavasya of the month of
Sridhara[^99]. Many devoted followers carry his teaching through the world to this day. <!-- F37 F38 F39 -->
