# Vishnupriya Devi
*Вишнуприя-деви · @vishnupriya*

## Mistress of Gaura's Home
Vishnupriya Devi is the second consort of Sri Chaitanya Mahaprabhu and the mistress of His home in Navadvipa. She came
into Gaura-lila to assist the Lord in His foremost work — the preaching of the holy name — and after His sannyasa she
remained in Navadvipa for many years as a living example of how to worship the name in separation from Him. She has many
names and titles: Vishnupriya Devi, Vishnupriya Thakurani, “Vishnupriya-ma”; in the “Prema-vilasa” and the
“Bhakti-ratnakara” she is Ishvari, “the Queen”; she is called the Mother of the world (jaganmata), Lakshmi and
Mahalakshmi, Narayani, Parameshvari, or simply Priya — “the Beloved.” The “Chaitanya-bhagavata” calls her “Lakshmi, the
Mother of the world,” and in the childhood prayer of Srinivasa quoted in the “Bhakti-ratnakara” (2.176–180) the Lord is
glorified as “the husband of Lakshmi and Vishnupriya.” <!-- F13 F123 -->

The primary sources say little about her. Already in the list of pastimes with which the “Chaitanya-bhagavata” opens,
Vrindavana Dasa mentions her marriage:

“In the Adi-khanda the Lord's consort (Lakshmipriya) leaves the world, and the Lord marries a second time — the daughter
of the Raja Pandita (Vishnupriya).” (CB, Adi 1.110)

The wedding itself he describes in detail in the fifteenth chapter of the Adi-khanda. Krishnadasa Kaviraja in the
“Chaitanya-charitamrita” records it in a single line:

“Then Nimai married Vishnupriya, the goddess of fortune.” (CC, Adi 16.25)

He passes at once to the Lord's victory over the digvijayi; beyond this the “Charitamrita” says not a word about
Vishnupriya. The matchmaking and the wedding are described by Murari Gupta in his “Kadacha” (first prakrama, sargas
13–14), and in particular detail by Lochana Dasa in the “Chaitanya-mangala” (Adi-khanda, chapter 6); in Lochana Dasa's
poem Vishnupriya appears in many other places as well, and at its very beginning, among the invocations, he glorifies
her:

“I glorify Vishnupriya-ma, [the queen] of Navadvipa: her ornament is the Lord's crimson feet.” (CM, Sutra-khanda)

## The Daughter of the Raja Pandita
Vishnupriya's father was Sanatana Mishra, a brahmana of Navadvipa and an exalted devotee of Vishnu. All the virtues that
accompany vishnu-bhakti lived in him: a gentle heart, straightforwardness, truthfulness, mastery of the senses,
readiness to help, generosity and hospitality. Of high birth and great nobility, very wealthy and influential in worldly
and public affairs, he maintained many people, feeding and clothing them and giving them shelter. He was known as the
“raja-pandita” — the court pandita, “king of panditas”; so the “Chaitanya-bhagavata” too calls him, naming Vishnupriya
“the daughter of the Raja Pandita.” In social standing he was above Nimai's family[^1]. Vishnupriya's grandfather was
named Durgadasa Mishra[^2]; according to the “Prema-vilasa,” the descendants of Durgadasa's line through Yadavacharya are
known as “Vishnupriya's parivara” — her family. <!-- F1 F2 F3 F4 F5 -->

For his pure devotion to Vishnu, Sanatana was granted a daughter rich in every good quality — uncommonly gentle and
well-mannered, like Lakshmi in person. From early childhood she was devoted to her parents and to Vishnu and bathed in
the Ganga three times a day[^3]; she kept strictly to the injunctions of scripture, performed puja and archana, served
tulasi and observed many vows — from her very first years her life was service to Vishnu. <!-- F6 F7 F8 -->

She appeared on the fifth lunar day of the bright fortnight of the month of Magha — shukla-panchami, or Sri-panchami. On this day those
who seek perfection in worldly learning honour Sarasvati, the goddess of knowledge. But knowledge is of two kinds: apara,
the worldly, and para, the transcendent; and Vishnupriya is honoured as the goddess and mistress of transcendent
knowledge, para-vidya. The day of her appearance was determined by Jagannatha Dasa Babaji; he revealed it anew to the
world through Bhaktivinoda Thakura, and, as the “Sarasvati-jaya-shri” writes, from Bhaktivinoda the custom of honouring
her on this day spread through the whole Vaishnava world. The year of her birth, like the year of her departure, has not
been preserved; we know only that she was born in Navadvipa, in her father's house. <!-- F11 F117 F12 F14 -->

As a girl, whenever she met Shachi Devi at the bathing ghat on the Ganga, Vishnupriya humbly bowed down to her, and
Shachi blessed her: “By Krishna's mercy may you obtain a worthy husband.” The Lord's mother secretly wished to have her
as her daughter-in-law, and when she learned that this was the daughter of Sanatana Mishra, knowing her good qualities,
she gladly embraced the thought of such a marriage. <!-- F9 F10 -->

## The Matchmaking
The Lord's first consort, Lakshmipriya, left the world while He was travelling in East Bengal. On His return the Lord
consoled His mother with instruction. For a long time pain tormented Shachi's heart, but in time she began to think of
her son's happiness, and, having made sure that Gaurasundara was not unwilling, she took the matter seriously in hand and
resolved to have Him married again. <!-- F15 -->

She sent a servant for Kashinatha Pandita, a well-known matchmaker of Navadvipa — in Krishna-lila, according to the
“Gaura-ganoddesha-dipika” (50), the brahmana whom King Satrajit sent to Krishna to propose the marriage of
Satyabhama; he is called Kulaka[^4]. Shachi herself put the match to him: “Speak to the raja-pandita; if he wishes, let him give his daughter
to my Nimai.” Yet she was anxious whether so eminent a man would give his daughter to the son of poor parents, who was
reputed to be eccentric besides. “Thakurani,” the matchmaker replied, “if not to Nimai Pandita, to whom else should he
give her?” Delighted, he promised to arrange everything quickly and set off at once for Sanatana's house, repeating the
name of Hari. <!-- F16 F17 F18 F19 -->

Sanatana, for his part, had long desired this marriage. Seeing his daughter come of age, he had been looking for a
bridegroom and had decided that none could be found better than Nimai Pandita — in beauty, in qualities and in years; a
strong wish to give his daughter to Nimai grew in his heart, and he prayed to Hari: “If I have any merit from past births,
let my daughter become the wife of Nimai Pandita.” Kashinatha arrived just as Sanatana and his wife were talking in
private about their daughter's marriage. The host seated his guest, offered him sweetened water and, taking the arrival
of the famous matchmaker as a good omen, asked: “Pandita, what news?” <!-- F20 F21 -->

Vrindavana Dasa gives the matchmaker's answer thus:

“Give your daughter in marriage to Vishvambhara Pandita. I consider them a perfect pair. Vishvambhara is a worthy husband
for your daughter, and your daughter is a worthy wife for Vishvambhara. The union of Vishnupriya and Nimai Pandita will
be perfect, like the union of Krishna and Rukmini.” (CB, Adi 15.57–59) <!-- F22 Q2 -->

Sanatana and his wife were almost beside themselves with joy: the Lord, the Supersoul, knew their innermost wish and
fulfilled it Himself. Sanatana took counsel with his wife, relatives and friends, and all agreed that the wedding should
be held as soon as possible. “Of course I will give my daughter to Nimai Pandita,” he told the matchmaker; “it will
increase the good fortune of my whole family. Go and assure them.” Kashinatha returned to Shachi and told her everything,
and she answered: “I have done all I could; the rest is in the hands of Sri Hari.” Until then Nimai had known nothing;
when everything was settled His mother told Him, and He consented, and the people of Navadvipa rejoiced to hear that Nimai
Pandita was to marry. <!-- F23 F24 F25 F26 -->

Murari Gupta places in Sanatana's heart the thought that, receiving such a husband as Vishvambhara, his daughter would
make her name — “dear to Vishnu” — true, as Rukmini obtained Krishna (“Kadacha” 1.13.13–14). Lochana Dasa repeats this
thought:

“Vishnupriya will obtain a husband like Vishvambhara — and the name Vishnupriya (‘beloved of Vishnu’) will become true for
her. As Rukmini obtained Sri Krishna as her husband, so it will be with her.” (CM, Adi 6) <!-- F61 Q4 -->

## The Wedding
The Lord's students rejoiced to hear of the wedding. All the expenses were taken on by Buddhimanta Khan — a neighbour and
devoted follower of the Lord, a wealthy and wise friend of His family, one of the richest men of Navadvipa.
Mukunda-Sanjaya, in whose chandimandapa Nimai kept His school, offered to share the costs, but Buddhimanta refused: this
was to be no modest wedding of a poor brahmana, but one worthy of a prince. Nimai's students too helped to make it
splendid. For this service Buddhimanta received Mahaprabhu's embrace and mercy. <!-- F27 F28 F29 F30 -->

An auspicious day and hour were chosen, and on the eve the adhivasa — the rite of preparation for the wedding — was
performed with great pomp. The house was decorated with banana trees, full pitchers and mango leaves; lamps, unhusked rice
and curd were set out, the ground was painted with alpana designs, and huge canopies were stretched. All the Vaishnavas,
brahmanas and good people of Navadvipa were invited; people came from the neighbouring villages as well, and poets sang
songs of their own composing about the bride and groom. By noon the mridangas, sanais, great drums and karatalas began to
play; Vishnu was worshipped and arati performed. Such an adhivasa had never been seen in Navadvipa even among the rich, and
people said they had never witnessed such a festival or such generosity to brahmanas. <!-- F31 F32 F33 F34 -->

The Lord Himself anointed the forehead of every brahmana and devotee with sandal paste, garlanded him and gave him betel.
Some, greedy for gifts, joined the line again and again, but Gauranga ordered that each be given three times — and still
the sandal, garlands and betel did not diminish; what fell on the ground alone would have sufficed for five weddings (cf.
CB, Adi 15). Sanatana came too — with auspicious gifts, with relatives, friends and brahmanas, to music and singing — and
anointed the Lord with fragrances (gandha-adhivasa). Gaurasundara performed the prescribed rites, worshipping Vishnu and
the ancestors; Shachi with the women worshipped the Ganga and the goddess Shashthi and gave generously to the women;
Vishnupriya's mother too rejoiced with her neighbours. And the Lord bestowed food, cloth and wealth on the brahmanas,
honouring each according to his worth. <!-- F35 F36 F37 -->

The wedding ceremonies fell on the following day. Towards evening the devotees adorned the Lord: sandal paste and fragrant
oils, a crescent of sandal on His forehead, a crown, garlands, garments, collyrium on His eyes, on His wrist a thread with
rice and kusha grass, in His hand a banana leaf and a little mirror, golden earrings, bracelets and chains. Before setting
out Gaurasundara bowed at the feet of His mother, the brahmanas and His teacher. The palanquin for the groom was prepared
by Buddhimanta Khan, and his foot soldiers and servants walked behind it. First the Lord was carried to the Ganga; He
bathed and honoured Ganga Devi, and then, after midday, the groom in His palanquin was borne along the riverbank through
the whole of Navadvipa to the bride's house. From every side rang out “Jaya! Jaya!” and the women's ulu-dhvani. Hundreds
and thousands of torches and lamps lit the procession, fireworks soared and a rain of flowers fell; banners were carried,
jesters and conjurors amused the crowd, dancers danced, countless instruments resounded, and children and old men alike
broke into dance. Those who saw it said that neither Indra nor the Lord of Vaikuntha had such splendour, and brahmanas who
had failed to marry their daughters to Nimai Pandita lamented a good fortune that comes but once in many lives.
<!-- F38 F39 F40 F41 F42 F43 -->

At dusk, in the hour of godhuli, when the herds return home, the groom arrived at the house of Sanatana Mishra. Sanatana
and his wife received their son-in-law with the varana rite and blessed Him; Sanatana helped the Lord down from the
palanquin, led Him by the hand to the seat of honour, offered Him water to wash His feet, fragrance for the mouth,
garments and ornaments. His wife with the women placed on the Lord's head a wreath of fresh ears of grain and kusha,
performed arati with a seven-wicked lamp, sang hymns, showered Him with rice and blew the conch. <!-- F44 F45 F46 -->

Then Vishnupriya, adorned with ornaments, was brought to the place of the wedding. Clad in silks and jewels she seemed a
celestial maiden, while Nimai in His wedding attire outshone Cupid in beauty. Seven times she circled the Lord — her
beloved Gaura-Narayana — and bowed at His feet. By custom bride and groom must look at each other for the first time,
but in her natural modesty she could not raise her eyes to the Lord's face, and they were screened by a cloth so that the
rite might take place in private. With that first glance they affirmed anew their eternal bond: as Lakshmi and Narayana
are eternally inseparable, so Vishnupriya and Gaurasundara are eternally united. <!-- F47 F48 F49 -->

Then they exchanged garlands. Vishnupriya first offered hers at the Lord's feet and placed it on Him, thereby giving
herself to Him; the Lord, smiling, placed a garland on her, and the divine couple joyfully showered each other with
flowers, while the gods headed by Brahma invisibly showered them with flowers from the heavens[^5]:

“The Mother of the universe, Vishnupriya, offered the Lord a garland of flowers and bowed devotedly at His lotus feet.
Smiling gently, the Lord also offered a garland to His bride. After this Lakshmi and Narayana joyfully began to shower each
other with flowers.” (CB, Adi 15.176–178) <!-- F50 Q5 -->

Playful banter broke out between the families of the bride and groom, and the Lord's smile delighted everyone. Then
Sanatana, having washed his feet and rinsed his mouth, performed the kanyadana — gave his daughter to the Lord — and with
many gifts gave her dowry: many cows, land and servants. Vrindavana Dasa speaks of the meaning of this gift:

“Wishing to give pleasure to Lord Vishnu, Sanatana Mishra placed his daughter in the Lord's hands.” (CB, Adi 15.188)

After this the final fire sacrifice was performed, and the newly-weds were led to new chambers in the raja-pandita's
mansion — that night Sanatana's house became Vaikuntha: Lakshmi and Narayana passed an auspicious night in the bridal
chamber, on a bed adorned with flowers. <!-- F51 F52 F53 -->

Sanatana was granted a good fortune known only to the Lord's great fathers-in-law — Janaka, who gave Sita to Rama, and
Bhishmaka, who gave Rukmini to Krishna:

“Such great good fortune was obtained only by the greatest kings of the past: Nagnajit, Janaka, Bhishmaka and Jambavan.
Now this good fortune fell to the lot of Sanatana Mishra and his family by virtue of their unceasing service to Lord
Vishnu.” (CB, Adi 15.195–196) <!-- F54 -->

The next day, after midday, the divine couple set out for Shachi's house to music and singing; the bride rode in the
palanquin together with her husband, and when Lakshmi sat beside the Lord, Shachi's house was filled with light and
rejoicing[^6]. The pious women of Navadvipa, looking at them, said:

“‘How fortunate she is! She must have served Lakshmi and Parvati for many lives.’ ‘They are exactly like Shiva and
Parvati.’ — ‘These are Lakshmi and Lord Hari Themselves.’ ‘They are like Kamadeva and Rati.’ — ‘They are like Indra and
Shachi.’ ‘I think They are Rama and Sita.’” (CB, Adi 15.205–208) <!-- F55 F56 F57 -->

And Vrindavana Dasa concludes:

“Everyone who saw the Lord's wedding was cleansed of all sins and attained Vaikuntha.” (CB, Adi 15.216)

That is why, he adds, the Lord is called the All-merciful, the Lord of the fallen. The Lord's wedding is not a worldly
union that binds a person to the world: it is the union of the Supreme Lord with His own energy. Therefore hearing of it
does not bind but frees — both from maya and from the desire for worldly relations of “enjoyer” and “object of
enjoyment,” for the only enjoyer is Narayana. <!-- F58 I1 -->

## Life with the Lord in Navadvipa
Some time after the wedding the Lord, having completed His studies and now occupied with teaching, set out for Gaya (CM,
Adi 7), and on His return from there He revealed to Navadvipa His love for Krishna. Of the years when kirtana resounded in
Navadvipa the primary sources preserve little about Vishnupriya. When Mahaprabhu, in the house of Chandrashekhara
Acharyaratna, played the role of Lakshmi in a sacred drama (CB, Madhya 18), Shachi came to watch Him together with
Vishnupriya. <!-- F62 F63 -->

It is said that Vishvanatha Chakravarti, in his “remembrance of the eight periods of the day” of Gauranga, described a
conversation of Gaura with Vishnupriya as well. Among the verses on His morning pastimes is this one: at the end of the
night, hearing the cuckoos and the cocks, the Lord rose from His bed, delighted Vishnupriya with talk of rasa, went out,
sat on the ground and washed His face together with good people, and His mother and relatives rejoiced to see Him. But
even in this description the special character of the pastimes of Lakshmi and Gaura-Narayana — the pastimes of the Lord
with His own energy in the home of a pious brahmana — is in no way violated. <!-- F64 F136 -->

## The Night before Sannyasa
The “Chaitanya-bhagavata” does not relate the Lord's conversation with Vishnupriya before His sannyasa; it is described by
Lochana Dasa — in detail, and with the tenderness that fills his whole poem. This account must be read as the account of
the wedding is read: it is not a tale of worldly spouses being parted, but a pastime of Lakshmi and Gaura-Narayana. The
Lord Himself directs it — and Himself prepares His bhu-shakti for the service that lies ahead of her, the service of
separation. <!-- F65 F127 -->

Mother Shachi was the first to learn of her son's decision. She wished to tell her daughter-in-law what was in her heart,
but could not utter a word — her face only grew wan, and tears streamed from her eyes. Vishnupriya herself had already
heard what people were whispering, and from these hints she understood everything: “a thunderbolt fell out of season,”
and she lost consciousness. At the close of day the Lord came home in haste, ate and lay down to rest, and Vishnupriya
came to Him. Sighing, she sat by His lotus feet, laid them on her bosom, bound them with her arms and wept until her
tears ran down His feet. Feeling this, the Lord rose:

“‘You are dear to Me as life — why do you weep? I want to know: answer Me, beloved.’ Setting her on His lap, taking her
chin in His right hand, He asks in sweet words.” (CM, Madhya-khanda, part 15, v. 6)

She said nothing and only wept, clasping His feet. Again and again the Lord questioned her in many ways, wiped her face
with the edge of His cloth, began one tender, playful talk after another — “at such words even a stone would blossom” —
until at last she spoke in a faltering voice: <!-- F150 -->

“‘Listen, listen, Lord of my life, put Your hand on my head [and tell the truth]: are You really going to take sannyasa?
Hearing it from people, I feel my heart will burst — I shall enter fire.’” (CM, Madhya-khanda, part 15, v. 10)

For Him, she said, were her life and wealth, her beauty and youth; if He went away, what use was this wretched life? Her
heart burned as with poison[^7]. But then she spoke no longer of herself, but of Him: <!-- F66 F152 -->

“‘Shame on my body! One thing I ask You: how will You walk the roads on foot? Your feet are tender as shirisha flowers —
my hand is afraid to touch them. When You stand on the ground, my heart quakes with fear and my whole body shudders; in
the forests, among thorns, where will You go, to what lands? How will You walk on Your crimson feet?’” (CM,
Madhya-khanda, part 15, vv. 12–13)

At the slightest effort, she went on, drops of sweat appear on His face, and a sannyasi wanders now in downpour, now in
scorching heat. To whom would He leave her? Shachi is old and half-dead — how could He leave such a mother? How could He
leave Murari and Mukunda, Srivasa and Haridasa, Advaita Acharya? The whole world thinks Him a treasury of prema — would
He cover everyone with disgrace? And at last:

“‘What shall I, worthless one, say? If I am the root of Your household life and You take sannyasa because of me — let me
take Your misfortunes upon myself and die by taking poison, and You live happily in Your own home.’” (CM,
Madhya-khanda, part 15, v. 18) <!-- F151 -->

In this plea there is not a word of her own enjoyment. Vishnupriya fears for His tender feet, for His aged mother, for the
devotees, for His good name — and is ready to die herself, if only He need not suffer hardship. This is what pure
devotees see in her: not a trace of the possessive desire that would stand in the way of the Lord's sannyasa.
<!-- F128 -->

Hearing her, Gauranga smiled, again took her onto His lap, wiped her face with His cloth and began to jest:

“‘Do not dwell on needless grief. That I will leave you and go to take sannyasa — who ever told you that? Whatever I do,
when I do it, I shall tell you then — but now do not die of needless sorrow.’” (CM, Madhya-khanda, part 15, vv. 20–21)

So, embracing her, in sweet and playful pastimes the Lord comforted Vishnupriya in every way[^8]. Yet even when “in
joyful play the night came to an end,” the fire in her heart was not quenched. At daybreak, gazing at the face of her
Lord and pressing His hand to her breast, she asked again: <!-- F153 -->

“‘Do not lie to me for fear of me. This is what I think: all You say is mere playfulness — You will run away without my
knowing. You, Lord, are Your own master, never in another's power, and You do what pleases You. If You will take
sannyasa — what can I say? [Only] tell me the truth.’” (CM, Madhya-khanda, part 15, vv. 24–25)

Then the Lord smiled softly and said: “Listen, My beloved, do not grieve at all in your heart: what I say for your good,
listen to attentively.” And He began to instruct her: <!-- F154 -->

“All that you see in the world count as empty: Bhagavan alone is true, and true are the Vaishnavas; all else besides them
count as empty. Empty are son, husband, wife, father, mother — all that goes by such names: in the end, who belongs to
whom? Apart from Sri Krishna's feet there is no other kin; all that you see is His maya. Whether woman or man — see: the
soul of all is one [in nature]; only in the bonds of maya does one imagine ‘two.’ Sri Krishna is the husband of all, and
all others are prakriti: this no one understands.”[^9] (CM, Madhya-khanda, part 15, vv. 27–29)

A person, He went on, is born of blood and seed in a place of filth, suffers from childhood to old age, priding himself on
body and home; those he raised revile him, his hearing and sight grow dim, he weeps over his woes — and still he does not
serve Govinda. He was given a body in order to serve Krishna, yet, forgetting that service, he paves his own way to hell.
And the Lord concluded:

“Your name is Vishnupriya (‘dear to Vishnu’): make it meaningful, do not keep needless grief in your heart. I have told
you this — put away other thoughts and give your mind to the deeds of Krishna.” (CM, Madhya-khanda, part 15, v. 33)
<!-- F67 F68 -->

By this conversation Gaurasundara was teaching not Vishnupriya alone: His words are addressed to all living beings
intoxicated with the thirst for enjoyment — the true husband of all is one, Krishna. So too was fulfilled what had been
foretold of her name at the matchmaking: the name “Vishnupriya” was to become true. <!-- I5 -->

Then the Lord, Himself the Master, withdrew His maya, which had hidden His divinity from her[^10] — and her heart grew
clear:

“Grief and sorrow went away, her breast filled with joy — and suddenly she sees [Him] four-armed.” (CM, Madhya-khanda,
part 15, v. 34)

Before her stood Narayana, the Supreme Lord, and the very vision told her: give up the grief born of maya. Her sorrow left
her, and her heart filled with joy and wonder. <!-- F69 -->

Yet even seeing the four-armed form, she did not give up thinking of Him as her husband. The form of Narayana, full of
majesty, did not eclipse for her the One whom she had served at home; falling at His feet, she pleaded:

“‘I, the lowest and most worthless, was born in this world, and You are my beloved husband, the Lord of my life; such was
my good fortune — I had become Your maidservant — why then [now] such a fall?’” (CM, Madhya-khanda, part 15, v. 36)

Without serving You, she said, I shall perish — and she wept aloud. Seeing His beloved's anguish, the Lord, His eyes
brimming with tears, took her in His arms, bestowed His grace on her[^11] and said: <!-- F70 F71 F155 -->

“‘Listen, Vishnupriya-devi, this is what I tell you: whenever you think of Me — wherever I may go, I am with you; truly,
truly I tell you firmly.’” (CM, Madhya-khanda, part 15, v. 38) <!-- F72 -->

This promise the Lord kept: in separation He remained with her in His holy name and in the Deity whom she served all her
life. Then she understood that the Lord is wholly independent:

“Hearing the word of Krishna's command, Vishnupriya thought: ‘This Lord is the independent Master, He does what pleases
Him — who can stand in His way?’ — and she made no reply.” (CM, Madhya-khanda, part 15, v. 39)

She lowered her face, her eyes brimming with tears[^12], and the Lord spoke tender words to her. “It is painful to hear of
the Lord's deeds,” sighs Lochana Dasa. In this silence is the whole of Vishnupriya. She does not argue, does not hold Him
back, does not become an obstacle on His path: she gives Him her grief as well. In taking sannyasa the Lord enters the
mood of Radha parted from Krishna, and Vishnupriya's separation from Him is also a service to this vipralambha of His,
His “pastime of separation.” <!-- F73 F156 F128 -->

Then, Lochana writes, days and nights went by in anxious surmise, “as if a fire had flared up in everyone's heart.”
Shachi and Vishnupriya wept day and night — “darkness on every side, all seems empty.” Srivasa begged to be taken along;
in the house of Murari Gupta the Lord privately told Murari to worship Advaita Acharya and, consoling him, said that the
sannyasa was “not yet soon.” <!-- F157 -->

Haridasa Shastri's edition (1983) — and before it the edition of 1903 — continues the story here with a section, “The
Consolation of the Devotees,” which is not in Srila Bhaktisiddhanta's edition[^13]. In those last days, according to this
section, the Lord laid aside His absorption in renunciation and showed His love in every house: He gladdened His mother
with words that set her heart at ease, jested and laughed with townsfolk and household, anointed Himself with sandal and
wore ornaments. Those who had secretly meant to go with Him His yogamaya sent back home, and everyone concluded there
would be no sannyasa. On the last night <!-- F158 F74 -->

“The Lord lay down in His chamber, and Vishnupriya came with a bunch of betel in her hand. Smiling with delight, the Lord
says, ‘Come, come!’ — and with great tenderness seated her on His lap. Vishnupriya anointed the Lord's body with sandal
and drew a tilaka with fragrant aguru and musk, put a divine garland of malati on Gora's body and with tender jests placed
betel in His holy mouth. Then Mahaprabhu, the crown of connoisseurs of rasa, Himself began to adorn Vishnupriya.” (CM,
Madhya-khanda, part 15, vv. 68+9–68+13)

He braided her long hair and decked it with malati flowers, placed on her forehead a dot of sindura ringed with dots of
sandal — “as if the moon had taken the sun onto its lap” — lined her eyes and adorned her. Further on Lochana Dasa speaks
of the tenderness of that night in the images with which poetry sings of divine love, and himself cautions that this is
“beyond the reach of Kama.”[^14] And this is how the night ended: <!-- F159 -->

“At the end of the night the Lord rose quickly; Vishnupriya sleeps a deep sleep. At the time of renunciation His prema
surges higher still; with the thought ‘I shall take sannyasa’ His heart is as if maddened.” (CM, Madhya-khanda, part 15,
vv. 68+27–68+28) <!-- F160 -->

Why at such an hour did the Lord unfold play, rasa and bhava? Lochana Dasa answers himself: the Lord repays each according
to his service, but Krishna is captured only by prema full of anuraga — and Gaura, the ocean of mercy, Himself begs for
such prema and Himself bestows it:

“In mercy He reveals His own anuraga, so that in separation her bhava may grow in her heart. ‘Whoever sees My body with
bhava — with him My bhava is never broken.’ Who else is such a treasury of mercy, who gives prema and anuraga so as to
bind His very Self? This is why [He showed] grace to Vishnupriya — knowing this, let no one go astray in mind.” (CM,
Madhya-khanda, part 15, vv. 68+35–68+38) <!-- F161 -->

So the poet himself understands this night: not as worldly love, but as a grace by which the Lord deepened the prema of
His eternal consort before her long years of separation. Yet Gaudiya Vaishnavas, following Vrindavana Dasa, see in the
Gaura of Navadvipa a model of the strict propriety of a brahmana householder, and Srila Bhaktisiddhanta apparently left
this section out of his edition deliberately: written in the spirit of nagari-bhava, it easily becomes a prop for the
doctrine of gaura-nagari. That is why it is retold here only briefly, and only in the light of Lochana Dasa's own words.
<!-- F162 F135 -->

Rising at dawn and performing His morning duties, Gaurahari settled His resolve: “I shall take sannyasa from Bharati
Gosani in Kantakanagara.” Setting out, He noted that His breath was flowing through the right nostril — an auspicious
sign. The Lord went to the Ganga[^15]:

“Bowing to the Ganga, He leaves Navadvipa — as if a thunderbolt struck everyone on the head. As if the sun had hidden in
the middle of the day, as if the swans had left the lake — where have they gone?” (CM, Madhya-khanda, part 16, vv. 5–6)
<!-- F163 F164 -->

## Separation
When Mahaprabhu went to Katwa to take sannyasa, He was only twenty-four years old. On learning that He had swum across the
Ganga and gone to Keshava Bharati, Shachi and Vishnupriya fell senseless; Nityananda tried to console them. Later
Chandrashekhara Acharya returned from Katwa with the news of the sannyasa, and both wept bitterly again (CM,
Madhya-khanda, parts 16–18). <!-- F75 F76 -->

Lochana Dasa paints their grief thus:

“Shachi Devi weeps, holding Vishnupriya on her lap — and Vishnupriya lies as though dead: the body is there, but life
has left it. Shachi and Vishnupriya weep, rolling on the ground.” (CM, Madhya-khanda, part 16, vv. 10–11)

“Where have you run off to, abandoning Vishnupriya?” laments Shachi. “You have counted the love of all the devotees as
nothing!” And Vishnupriya now rises, now falls, as if mad (CM, Madhya-khanda, part 16, vv. 21–22). <!-- F77 -->

When Chandrashekhara returned, her grief grew still stronger:

“From Vishnupriya's weeping the earth splits; beasts, birds, creepers, trees — even the stones weep.” (CM,
Madhya-khanda, part 18, v. 15)

“‘Life does not leave my sinful body!’ — rolling on the ground, the Devi cries: ‘Alas, alas!’ Her breath bursts forth
unceasingly, burning with the fire of separation; her lips are parched, her body trembles. Lying in the dust, she tends
neither her hair nor her clothes; now her body wastes away, now it seems to swell. Now she loses consciousness,
contemplating His crimson feet, now with great difficulty she comes to herself. Now she cries aloud: ‘Lord, Lord!’ — and
at Vishnupriya's weeping everyone weeps.” (CM, Madhya-khanda, part 18, vv. 26–30) <!-- F78 -->

Those who came to console her burned in their own hearts and said: “‘The deeds of your Lord are not unknown to you:
understand this and comfort your heart’” (CM, Madhya-khanda, part 18, v. 33). Then the devotees remembered His former
word — “Whoever takes My name will attain Me” — and then

“all sat down to chant the name — Shachi, Vishnupriya and all the rest, children and old people, young men and maidens —
sat down to chant the name, making Gauranga their shelter.” (CM, Madhya-khanda, part 18, vv. 39–40) <!-- F79 Q6 -->

So began for her the long years of separation. According to the “Advaita-prakasha,” every morning she went with Shachi
to bathe in the Ganga, and all day she remained in the house, allowing neither the sun nor the moon to see her; even the
devotees saw only her feet and never heard her voice. She ate only the remnants of Shachi's food, grew pale and thin.
All day in seclusion she chanted the holy name before a picture of Gauranga, serving it as the Lord Himself and
surrendering at His feet. Her life in separation is the ideal of a devoted wife and of that forbearance of which the
Lord's own verse speaks: “humbler than a blade of grass, more tolerant than a tree” (“trinad api sunichena”).
<!-- F80 F81 I2 -->

## The Sacrifice of the Holy Name
She counted the holy name with grains of rice. On either side of her stood two clay pots — one with uncooked rice, the
other empty — and after each complete utterance of the mantra of sixteen names and thirty-two syllables she moved one
grain[^16]; thus she chanted the name until three in the afternoon. The rice so gathered she cooked — she prepared no
other food for herself — and, moistening it with her tears, offered it to Mahaprabhu, eating only a small portion herself,
as much as was needed to sustain life. Even while honouring prasada she chanted the name in the mood of separation;
giving up sleep and rest, she chanted it through all eight praharas, day and night, wholly immersed in the transcendent
vipralambha-rasa — the taste of separation. So passed her days and nights. <!-- F82 F83 -->

The “Prema-vilasa” says: as Mahaprabhu chanted the name day and night without counting, so His beloved, “Ishvari
Vishnupriya,” chanted it without pause; and this was within her power, for the Lord had placed in her the seed of His own
potency. <!-- F84 Q11 -->

The “Bhakti-ratnakara” (4.47–51), from the words of Srinivasa, who came to her every day, relates that her deeds are
indescribable. She gave up sleep, and if she slept at all, it was on the bare ground; her body, once brighter than gold,
grew dim, and she became as thin as the moon on the fourteenth night of the dark fortnight; she counted the names with
grains, cooked them and offered them to the Lord, herself eating a morsel, and no one understood how she remained alive.
<!-- F85 Q7 -->

Thus by her separation she teaches the world: in the pastimes of Gaurasundara's separation she is the exemplary acharya,
the teacher of the worship of the holy name. As after Nityananda's disappearance Jahnava carried out the work of an
acharya, so Vishnupriya after Gaurasundara's disappearance lived in His house in Mayapur-Navadvipa and enacted the pastime
of an acharya who teaches the service of Gaura's name, Gaura's abode and Gaura's desire — gaura-nama, gaura-dhama and
gaura-kama. <!-- I3 F138 -->

## The Deity of Mahaprabhu
It is said that Vishnupriya was the first to establish the worship of the form of Gauranga. Murari Gupta writes in the
“Kadacha” (4.14.8) that Krishna in His manifest form came to His beloved, Himself created His murti and abided in it, and
she, the form of Lakshmi, serves Him. While she was near Him, Mahaprabhu manifested Himself in the form of the Deity and
instructed her: “In Him Krishna abides,” — and she served that Deity. Thus she was the first to reveal to the world the
Deity of Vishvambhara and brought good to the world by singing the nectar of Gaura's name. <!-- F86 F87 Q8 -->

The devotees of Mahaprabhu also tell this differently. Sri Rama, who had vowed to have one wife only, performed a
sacrifice in exile together with a golden image of Sita but did not marry again; so too Vishnupriya in the pastimes of
Gaura-Narayana paid her debt to her Lord: she had an image of Mahaprabhu made, so as to offer Him the sacrifice of the holy
name. This Deity abides in Navadvipa to this day, and He is worshipped. At her wish the worship of the Deity of Mahaprabhu
was established by Vamshivadana, and after her departure His service was moved to Kuliya, the present town of Navadvip;
and when the descendants of Vamshi, having accepted Jahnava's mercy, settled in Baghnapara, the service in Kuliya remained
with servitors from Malkhana[^17]. <!-- F88 F89 F90 -->

Consoling His mother on the eve of sannyasa, the Lord promised her:

“Beginning this movement of sankirtana, I shall soon take two more births as your son.” (CB, Madhya 27.47)

Gaudiya commentators see in these words an indication that the Lord soon descends again — as the Deity-avatara (archa)
and as the Name-avatara, for the name, the Deity and the Lord Himself are one, and all three are eternal consciousness and
bliss. Vishnupriya served Him both in the name and in the Deity. <!-- F92 Q9 -->

At almost the same time as her Deity was established, Gauridasa Pandita manifested in his house the Deities of Nityananda
and Gaurachandra (Murari 4.14.12–14). The “Bhakti-ratnakara” names other Deities of Gaura too: the Gaura-Govinda of
Kashishvara in Vrindavana (2.438–443), the Deity of Narahari Sarakara in Shrikhanda (8.431–432) and the Deity of Dasa
Gadadhara in Katwa (8.453). <!-- F91 -->

## Srinivasa, Vamshivadana and Ishana
Soon after Mahaprabhu disappeared in Nilachala, Srinivasa came to Mayapur on the instruction of Gadadhara Pandita — he was
travelling from Shrikhanda to Navadvipa to see Vishnupriya's feet (BR 4; “Prema-vilasa” 4.167). At the Yogapitha, in
Mahaprabhu's house, he saw her and the old Vaishnava Ishana, and there he also met her disciple Vamshivadana Dasa, who
lived in Mayapur to serve her. <!-- F93 F97 -->

Vamshi, according to the “Bhakti-ratnakara” (4.20–24), guessed without any introduction that this was Srinivasa, embraced
him, bathed him in tears, would not let him bow down, and went to inform “Ishvari Vishnupriya-Ma” of him cautiously[^18].
At first she did not grant him darshana; only when Vamshi asked her again and again — seeing Srinivasa's despair — to
receive and bless him, did she agree, and at her bidding Vamshi brought him[^19]. Srinivasa fell to the ground with tears
of love; in motherly affection Vishnupriya placed her feet on his head, blessed him, and told him to stay and take
prasada. Thus he received the blessing of both her and Vamshi. <!-- F94 F95 F96 -->

Srinivasa saw with his own eyes her service in separation and marvelled at her renunciation and faith. Having received her
loving blessing and instructions, he went to Sita in Shantipur and then to Khardaha. Later Srinivasa, Narottama and other
acharyas carried the sankirtana, having received the mercy and guidance not only of Jahnava but also of Vishnupriya —
Gaura's shakti. <!-- F98 F139 -->

After the Lord's sannyasa Ishana Thakura took upon himself the care of Shachi, of Vishnupriya and of the house. Srila
Bhaktisiddhanta, in the “Gaudiya-bhashya” on the “Chaitanya-bhagavata” (Madhya 8.73), says that by serving the Lord's
mother and wife even after His sannyasa, Ishana became “the most blessed of the blessed.” For service to a devotee is higher
than service to the Lord Himself: Ishana served Shachi and Vishnupriya and received their love, and the Lord's mercy
follows the mercy of the devotee. Ishana lived very long — he outlived Vishnupriya and all of Mahaprabhu's devotees in
Navadvipa. <!-- F99 I4 F100 -->

Beside him Vamshivadana Thakura was blessed with service: he became the servant and protector of Vishnupriya and Shachi.
After Mahaprabhu's disappearance Vishnupriya took him as her servant, and, it is said, he was the first to receive her
mercy; he was glorified as her “exclusive vessel of mercy,” and he was her disciple[^20]. According to the
“Gaudiya-vaishnava-abhidhana,” Gadadhara Dasa too looked after Shachi and Vishnupriya in Navadvipa; when they were gone,
he moved to Katwa and established a Deity of Gauranga there. <!-- F101 F102 F103 -->

## Departure
When, where and how Vishnupriya Devi left this world the biographies do not say. We know only that after the Lord's
disappearance she lived in His house in Mayapur and that she outlived many: Gadadhara Dasa moved to Katwa only after the
departure of Shachi and Vishnupriya, Vamshivadana moved her Deity after her departure, and Ishana outlived even her. The
news of her departure in Navadvipa reached Srinivasa in Yajigrama, where he had returned with a chest of books after the
events in Vishnupur; he fell senseless, and the devotees brought him round with difficulty. <!-- F104 F106 F105 -->

## The Bhu-shakti of Gaura-Narayana
Kavi Karnapura in the “Gaura-ganoddesha-dipika” reveals who she and her father were in the eternal lila:

“Sri Sanatana Mishra is the former King Satrajit, whose daughter is Vishnupriya, the Mother of the world, the incarnation
of Bhu.” (GGD 47)

The daughter of Satrajit whom Krishna married is Satyabhama; therefore in Krishna-lila Vishnupriya is called Satyabhama,
the Lord's bhu-shakti. Karnapura finds confirmation in the “Chaitanya-chandrodaya,” where Kali says that the Lord, “taking
as wife another, a partial incarnation of Bhu, named Vishnupriya…” (GGD 48); the same drama calls her “devotion itself
embodied” — sakshat bhakti-svarupini. <!-- F107 Q1 F110 F119 -->

But Gaudiya authors at once note a difference as well. Satyabhama is the lawful (svakiya) wife of Krishna, the Lord of
Dvaraka, who has many wives, and a son was born to Krishna from her; in Vishnupriya's pastimes there is nothing of the
kind, and Gaura-Narayana, the Lord of Mayapur, has no multitude of wives. Therefore on the path of reverence
(maryada-marga) she is worshipped as Sri Lakshmi in the pastimes of Gaura-Narayana. Gaura-Narayana and Gaura-Vishvambhara
are in essence one non-dual Truth, but in the manifestations of lila there is a difference between Them, and Their
pastimes have different abodes. <!-- F108 F109 -->

Vrindavana Dasa compares her with Rukmini: the union of Vishnupriya and Nimai is like the union of Rukmini and Krishna
(CB, Adi 15.59); Murari Gupta and Lochana Dasa say the same, and her father's good fortune is likened to that of Bhishmaka,
Rukmini's father. For comparison: the father of the Lord's first consort, Vallabhacharya, is called by the
“Chaitanya-bhagavata” (Adi 10.48) and the “Gaura-ganoddesha-dipika” (44) both Janaka and Bhishmaka, and his daughter
Lakshmipriya — Lakshmi, the single form of Janaki and Rukmini. And since both Satyabhama and Rukmini are manifestations of
the daughter of Vrishabhanu, Sri Radha, Vishnupriya too may be called her manifestation — but, as will be said below, not
Radha Herself. <!-- F111 F112 F113 -->

Vishnu has three energies — Sri, Bhu and Nila (or Lila). In the pastimes of Gaura-Narayana the sri-shakti is Lakshmipriya,
the bhu-shakti is Vishnupriya, and the third energy is Sri Navadvipa-dhama Herself[^21]; all three are manifestations and
maidservants of the supreme shakti, Sri Radhika. Srila Bhaktisiddhanta in the “Anubhashya” (on CC, Adi 1.37–45) explains:
when Gaura shows His oneness with Narayana, His shaktis are His consorts Lakshmipriya and Vishnupriya; when with Krishna —
Gadadhara Pandita, the Radha of Krishna-lila. Lakshmipriya, Vishnupriya and others are the shaktis of Gaura-Vasudeva, while
Gadadhara Pandita, Svarupa Damodara and Ramananda Raya are the shaktis of madhura-rasa; and all of them assist
Gaurasundara in His vipralambha, His mood of separation. <!-- F114 F115 F116 -->

The change of consorts is explained in the same way. Before Gaurasundara revealed Himself as the incarnation of
prema-bhakti, Lakshmipriya disappeared: while Vishnupriya — prema-bhakti embodied — was growing up, Lakshmipriya served
Gaura-Narayana; but when this prema-bhakti had grown and become worthy of serving Gaurasundara, Lakshmipriya departed. As
Navadvipa is the seat of the nine kinds of devotion, so Vishnupriya is ninefold devotion itself, the mistress of Gaura's
home. <!-- F120 F118 -->

Gaurasundara is Radha and Krishna united in one form. Therefore Vishnupriya, the Mother of the world, who bestows love on
the devotees, may be called a maidservant of Radha-Krishna, a devotee and companion (sahachari) of the daughter of
Vrishabhanu — and Parameshvari. Srila Bhaktisiddhanta in one of his songs calls her the incarnation of prema-bhakti,
Narayani, and says that, following the gopis, she served all three in her mind — Radha, Krishna and Gaura.
<!-- F121 F122 -->

## Gaura-nagari and the Sahajiyas
Vishnupriya's wedding, her conversation with the Lord before His sannyasa and her separation are subjects on which it is
especially easy to stumble. The Gaudiya tradition — following Srila Bhaktisiddhanta — speaks of them directly and
strictly, and without these warnings an account of her would be incomplete. <!-- I6 -->

Into Lochana Dasa's “Chaitanya-mangala” self-interested people inserted the doctrine of gaura-nagari and various invented
descriptions contrary to siddhanta; even some modern secular literary scholars acknowledge this. Into the
“Chaitanya-bhagavata” and the “Chaitanya-charitamrita” nothing contrary to siddhanta has been inserted; therefore any view
of another biography that cannot be reconciled with them is not accepted as true by the followers of Gaura.
<!-- F125 F126 -->

The pastimes of Lakshmi and Narayana are not an attempt at enjoyment such as is natural to ordinary living beings. If an
account of them is made the support of a false doctrine of enjoyment — the doctrine of gaura-nagari or of the
prakrita-sahajiyas — this is a grave offence. In speaking with Vishnupriya before leaving home, Gaurasundara was teaching
precisely those intoxicated with the thirst for enjoyment. And Lochana Dasa himself shows that pure devotees see in
Vishnupriya not a trace of a possessive desire for enjoyment that might hinder the Lord's sannyasa; on the contrary, she
nourished the joy of the independent Gaura-Vishvambhara — the pastime of separation of Vrajendra-nandana, imbued with
Radha's bhava. <!-- F127 F128 -->

Those who distort Lochana Dasa's account and, displaying a seemingly great devotion to Vishnupriya, seek in their
imagination to arrange her meeting with Gaurasundara in the Gambhira, in the house of Kashi Mishra in Nilachala, are great
offenders at the feet of Gaurasundara and Vishnupriya, opponents of the siddhanta of pure devotion, prakrita-sahajiyas
deserving censure. The same people write that Mahaprabhu, even after sannyasa, while living in Nilachala, bought cloth and
other things and sent them to the wife of His former household life. In truth this is only the uncontrollable agitation of
the inner enemies in those who write with a worldly mind and try to load their own worldly attachment onto the shoulders
of Gaurasundara. <!-- F129 F130 -->

Various sects of “grihi-bauls,” householder bauls, paint invented pictures of Nimai's sannyasa, and some blame Sri
Chaitanya for having, by taking sannyasa, abandoned His grown wife, violated dharma and caused a woman grief. They do not
comprehend the words of the Vyasa of Chaitanya's pastimes:

“In truth all these examples of renunciation are not a rejection of one's obligations. At their root lies the attainment
of the highest good, but only great souls can understand this. Even a hard tree melts on hearing of these divine pastimes,
which bring liberation to all living beings.” (CB, Madhya 3.104–105) <!-- F131 Q10 -->

Nor may the two lilas be confused. Those who call Vishnupriya “the original Radha” (amshini), and Gaura and Vishnupriya
“Radha and Krishna,” erase the distinctive features of both lilas and commit a grave offence in both. Gaurasundara, adorned
with Radha's bhava and radiance, is nowhere a “nagara” — a lover-gallant. <!-- F135 -->

Bhaktivinoda Thakura wholly rejected the doctrines of “gaura-bhaja,” “gaura-vadi,” “gaura-nagari” and those mixed with
“theosophy” — doctrines which, abandoning the path of Rupa's followers, merely imitated the worship of Vishnupriya and
Gauranga — and preached the worship of Gaurasundara on the pure path of the rupanugas. Where the worship of Krishna together
with the ashraya-vigraha — taught by Rupa, Raghunatha, Jiva, Krishnadasa Kaviraja and Narottama — is abandoned, and Gaura
and Vishnupriya, under the guise of worshipping them, are honoured as separate from Krishna, there is neither Vishnupriya,
the bhu-shakti, nor her Lord Gaurasundara. The worship of Gaura and Vishnupriya is genuine only on the path of the
rupanugas — as part of the service of Radha and Krishna. <!-- F132 F133 I6 -->

## The Worship of Gaura and Vishnupriya
The true form of this worship was revealed by Narottama Dasa Thakura. He dispelled the misconception that Gaurasundara is
not Vishnu but a living being within varnashrama, a “bhagavad-bhakta,” a “so-called great man” or merely a guru — the
founder of one of the sampradayas — and in Kheturi he revealed the bhu-shakti Vishnupriya together with Gaura-Narayana.
There she assists Gaurasundara's separation, whose form in separation is imbued with the bhava of the gopis; in Their
relationship there is not a shadow of enjoyment. <!-- F134 -->

From his guru Lokanatha Gosvami Narottama had received the order to establish Deities of Radha-Krishna and Gauranga.
Returning to Kheturi, he prepared five pairs of Radha-Krishna Deities, while the Deities of Gaura and Vishnupriya he
obtained miraculously. The night before he received an instruction in a dream, and in the morning he went to the
neighbouring village of Gopalpur and wished to enter the granary of the brahmana Vipradasa, full of rice. People tried to
dissuade him: venomous snakes hissed there, which even snake-charmers had failed to drive out. But when Narottama came and
the granary was opened, the snakes vanished, and the Deities of Gaura and Vishnupriya, before everyone's eyes, came of
Themselves onto his lap[^22]; at once a great kirtana began. The “Bhakti-ratnakara” (10.202) says: “Gaurasundara and His
beloved Vishnupriya came out of the granary and sat on Narottama's lap.” Narottama took these Deities to Kheturi and
installed Them there; today They are served in Gambhila[^23]. <!-- F142 F141 Q12 F143 -->

At the great festival in Kheturi, on Phalguni Purnima, Narottama revealed six Deities: Gauranga with Vishnupriya,
Vallabhikanta, Krishna, Vrajamohana, Radhakanta and Radharamana (BR 10, 12; “Prema-vilasa” 19; “Narottama-vilasa” 6). It
is said that in Shrikhanda too, by the order of Narahari Sarakara, Raghunandana's son Kanai Thakura revealed the service of
Gauranga and Vishnupriya. <!-- F140 F144 -->

Three centuries later this service was revived by Bhaktivinoda Thakura. In 1892 (the 20th of Phalguna, 1299 of the Bengali
era), at a meeting in Mayapur, Jagannatha Dasa Babaji proposed that Jagannatha Mishra and Shachi be worshipped in one
building and Mahaprabhu with Lakshmi and Vishnupriya at His sides in another; the Pancha-tattva in Srivasa-angana. In
January 1894 a meeting convened by Bhaktivinoda at the A. B. school in Krishnanagar resolved to establish Deities at the
Lord's birthplace; the “Navadvipa-pracharini-sabha” was founded, and Dvarakanath-babu, Naphar-babu and others resolved to
build a hut there for the Deities of Gaura and Vishnupriya. On Friday, 21 March 1894 (the 9th of Chaitra, 1300 of the
Bengali era), on Phalguni Purnima, during a lunar eclipse, amid loud kirtana, the Deities of Gaura and Vishnupriya were
installed; for Their service the committee “Sri Mayapur Seva Samiti” was formed, joined by many well-known people. Thus
Bhaktivinoda — following Narottama and with the permission of Jagannatha Dasa Babaji — revealed the service of
Gaurasundara with Vishnupriya in Gaura's home in Mayapur. <!-- F145 F146 F147 -->

The meaning of this service Srila Bhaktisiddhanta expressed in a song appended to his “Anuvritti” on the “Upadeshamrita.”
Some, he says, call for worshipping Vishnupriya all one's life, abandoning the path of Rupa and the service of
Radha-Krishna, following “theosophy” and worldly enjoyment — such people have no association with Raghunatha, the
rupanuga. Narottama, however, dispelling the world's delusion, established Gaura and Vishnupriya, while he himself,
remembering Radha and Krishna, worshipped Them with all his heart in Vraja. The new doctrine of nagari, stricken by Kali,
destroys bhakti; but Bhaktivinoda, the servant of Gaura at the feet of his Prabhupada, remembering the feet of Narottama,
established Priya and Hari in Mayapur as his duty. <!-- F148 -->

Srila Bhaktisiddhanta also chose the day of Vishnupriya's appearance to re-establish the “Vishva-vaishnava-raja-sabha”:
this took place amid kirtana on 5 February 1919 at the Ultadanga Math, the Bhaktivinoda-asana, and the “Amrita Bazar
Patrika” wrote about it[^24]. <!-- F149 -->

## Significance
The life of Vishnupriya Devi is service to the Lord in two forms: at home, as Lakshmi beside Gaura-Narayana, and in
separation, as the acharya of the holy name, serving Him in the name and in the Deity. By her mercy the worldly life ruled
by the relation of “enjoyer and object of enjoyment,” born of the worldly distinction of man and woman, falls away, and
there awakens the longing to enter Gaura's home and to serve, even as a broom, in the marketplace of the holy name
(nama-hatta) of Nityananda and of Sach-chid-ananda Bhaktivinoda. By her mercy the dharma of attachment to home
(grihavrata) is torn out by the root, and one gains entry into Gaura's home in Yoga-Mayapur — and love for it.
