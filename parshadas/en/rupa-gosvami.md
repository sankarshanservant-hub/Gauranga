# Rupa Gosvami
*Rupa Gosvami · @rupa-gosvami*

## The One Who Established the Lord's Desire
Narottama Dasa Thakura opens his “Prema-bhakti-chandrika” with a verse that says in a few words what matters most
about Rupa Gosvami:

“When will Sri Rupa, who established on earth the cherished desire of Sri Chaitanya, himself give me a place at his
feet?” (“Prema-bhakti-chandrika,” 1) <!-- F314 Q62 -->

That desire was this: the Lord descended from Goloka to give living beings what the world had never received
before — devotion to Himself, the exalted, radiant rasa. It was Rupa who revealed to the world this most intimate
prema-bhakti, which belongs to Krishna alone. That is why he is called the one who carried out Sri Chaitanya's
cherished design, and why his name stands first among the names of the six Gosvamis. <!-- F315 F318 I24 -->

In the “Chaitanya-chandrodaya-nataka,” Kavi Karnapura places in the mouth of Sarvabhauma Bhattacharya a verse that
lists Rupa's virtues one after another:

“In Rupa — dear, beloved, whose very nature is prema, naturally beautiful, like Him, one with Him and the image of
His vilasa — the Lord unfolded bhakti-rasa.” (“Chaitanya-chandrodaya-nataka,” Act 9)[^1]

In other words, Rupa is the Lord's dear devotee, the object of His deep love and tenderness, wholly filled with
prema, non-different from Him, like Him, His intimate associate and the image of His vilasa; and in him the Lord
revealed the scripture of bhakti-rasa. In Krishna's eternal pastimes he is Rupa-manjari, of which more will be said
at the end of this article. <!-- F316 F317 Q63 Q1 -->

## Years of His Life
Two testimonies about the time of Rupa's appearance differ by four years. Bhaktivinoda Thakura, going through the
papers (daptar) of a certain Vaishnava, found in them the dates of the six Gosvamis and in 1885 (1292 of the Bengali
era) published them in the “Sajjana-toshani” (2nd year, p. 25), in a note entitled “Determining the Dates of the Six
Gosvamis.” According to these records, Rupa appeared in 1411 of the Shaka era (1546 Samvat, 1489 CE) and remained on
earth for seventy-five years — twenty-two at home and fifty-three in Vraja — and departed in 1486 Shaka (1621 Samvat,
1564 CE), on the twelfth day of the bright fortnight of the month of Shravana. The old manuscripts collected by the
learned pandita of the shripata of Gopivallabhapura, Vishvambharananda Deva Gosvami, agree with this exactly.[^2]
According to our Vaishnava calendar, the day of Rupa Gosvami's departure is the Gaura-dvadashi of the month of
Shridhara, which falls the morning after the Gaura-ekadashi that begins the Jhulana-yatra (in 2026, on 24 August).[^3]

## Lineage
Rupa's family goes back to brahmanas who were kings of Karnata in South India. His genealogy of seven generations was
preserved by Rupa's nephew Jiva Gosvami at the end of the “Laghu-vaishnava-toshani” and by Narahari Chakravarti in the
“Bhakti-ratnakara” (1.540–568); Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati sets it out as well in his “Anubhashya” (on CC, Adi
10.84). <!-- F16 F17 Q4 Q5 -->

The founder of the line was Sarvajna, a Yajurvedi brahmana of the Bharadvaja gotra and king of Karnata; as the most
honoured of the brahmanas of that land he was called “Jagad-guru.” His son Aniruddha-deva, a master of the Vedas, had
two wives and two sons: Rupeshvara, learned in the shastras, and Harihara, skilled in arms. After their father's death
Harihara seized the kingdom from his brother, and Rupeshvara with his wife went east on eight horses and settled
with his friend, King Shikhareshvara.[^4] <!-- F9 F10 F11 -->

Rupeshvara's son, Padmanabha-deva, a pandita and a devotee of Jagannatha, settled on the Ganga at Navahatta
(Naihati); King Danujamardana honoured him, and there he worshipped the Deity of Purushottama. He had eighteen
daughters[^5] and five sons — Purushottama, Jagannatha, Narayana, Murari and the youngest, Mukunda-deva. Mukunda's
son, the brahmana Kumara-deva, moved because of unrest in Naihati to Bakla-Chandradvipa and also set up a residence
at Fatehabad (then in the district of Jessore), between Naihati and Bakla.[^6] Chandradvipa was an ancient kingdom in
the lands of present-day Bangladesh; it included Faridpur, Bakharganj and the southern parts of the Dacca and Pabna
districts. <!-- F12 F13 F14 -->

Kumara-deva was a pious brahmana; the name of his wife, Rupa's mother, has not come down to us. Of his many sons three
became renowned: the eldest, Sanatana, the second, Rupa, and the youngest, Vallabha, or Anupama; the names of the
others are unknown. Vallabha's only son was Jiva. Sanatana himself, in his commentary on the “Brihad-bhagavatamrita”
(1.3), calls Rupa his dear servant and says of him: the son of Kumara, of the line of Jagad-guru, the renowned acharya
of the brahmana families of Karnata. And the “Sanatanashtaka,” attributed to Rupa, calls Sanatana “the ornament of the
line of brahmana kings of the blessed South.” Apart from this genealogy, no reliable information about the
descendants of the family has been found. <!-- F15 F18 Q2 Q3 -->

## Childhood and Names
After Kumara-deva's death, Sanatana, Rupa and Vallabha lived and studied in the house of their maternal uncle in the
small village of Sakurma near the capital of Gauda. The elder brothers became brilliant scholars — in the words of
one biography, “the crown jewels of the Gaudiya Vaishnava scholars”; they were taught philosophy and the shastras by
Sarvabhauma Bhattacharya's brother Vidyavachaspati, who often stayed in Ramakeli.[^7] <!-- F19 F20 -->

From early childhood, the same biography relates, all three were immersed in bhava-bhakti. Remembering Vrindavana,
they played in the groves of tamala, keli-kadamba and tulasi around their home, named the ponds there Radha-kunda and
Shyama-kunda, and always served Sri Madana-mohana. News reached them of Gaurasundara's wonderful pastimes in nearby
Navadvipa, and they longed to see Him, but an inner voice told them to wait: soon they would have the darshana of
the Saviour of the fallen. When word came that Sri Chaitanya had taken sannyasa and gone to Puri, Rupa and Sanatana
fainted: they had never seen Him in Navadvipa, and now He was leaving Bengal for ever. Then a voice from the sky said,
“Do not grieve: the merciful Sri Gauranga will soon come here,” and the brothers were consoled. <!-- F21 F22 F23 -->

In this way the biographers stress that Rupa and Sanatana were not worldly people who came to faith in time, but
eternal participants in Krishna's pastimes. In their earthly pastimes the Lord's associates conceal their
transcendental nature, yet when they are near one another they cannot help revealing it; outwardly, however, the
brothers behaved as sadhakas striving for perfection, to show people how one ought to act. <!-- I1 -->

The names “Rupa” and “Sanatana” were given to the brothers by Sri Chaitanya Himself when they became His disciples;
their earlier names are unknown, though some hold that Sanatana was called Amara and Rupa Santosha. Vallabha too
received the name “Anupama” from the Lord. At court, however, the brothers were known by their titles — Sakara
Mallik and Dabir Khas. Vrindavana Dasa Thakura confirms this as well, anticipating the story at the very beginning
of the “Chaitanya-bhagavata”:

“In the Antya-khanda Sri Gaurasundara reveals Himself to Dabir Khas. The two brothers, recognising the Supreme Lord,
attain liberation and receive new names — Rupa and Sanatana.” (CB, Adi 1.171–172)

And in the story itself he says:

“At that time two fortunate souls came before Sri Chaitanya. The Lord cast His merciful glance on these two brothers,
Sakara Mallik and Rupa.” (CB, Antya 9.238–239) <!-- F24 F25 F26 Q13 Q14 -->

## At the Court of Hussain Shah
The brothers' learning, intelligence and noble character drew the attention of the ruler of Gauda, Hussain Shah, and
they came to hold the two highest posts at his court: Sanatana, “a Brihaspati in intellect,” and Rupa, unequalled in
appearance and virtues, became the “two pillars” of his rule.[^8] Sanatana received the title Sakara Mallik, Rupa that
of Dabir Khas; the Sultan addresses Rupa by this title in the “Chaitanya-charitamrita” as well. Rupa was the
Sultan's personal adviser, his private secretary, and Sanatana his first minister.[^9] <!-- F28 F29 -->

According to the dictionary “Raja-vyavahara-kosha,” the word “dabir” means “one versed in reasoning” (yuktabhijno
dabirah syat), and “khas” means “one's own,” that is, the sovereign's personal adviser. Others derive it from the
Persian “dabir” (secretary, munshi) and the Arabic “khas” (personal): “personal secretary”; “Sakara Mallik” would then
mean “chief secretary.” “Mallik” means “lord”: so the Muslims styled respected wealthy families close to the
government. Such titles were mere ranks conferred by Hussain Shah and in no way mean that the brothers' ancestors had
embraced Islam: Jiva calls their father “the best of the twice-born, the venerable Kumara” (dvija-varah shriman
kumarah), and there is nothing non-Hindu in the names of their forefathers. <!-- F30 F31 F32 -->

According to the “Anubhashya,” all three of Kumara's sons became Vaishnavas; for the sake of their service they left
their home in Fatehabad and settled in Ramakeli, where Jiva was born, and for their service to the nawab all three
received the title “Mallik.” The youngest, Vallabha, was the chief superintendent of the royal mint. The Shah rewarded
the brothers generously, and they became very wealthy. Hindus not infrequently had to serve a Muslim ruler in those
days, and among them were great devotees: Keshava Vasu Khan, the city magistrate and chief of the guard; Gopinatha Vasu
and Purandara Khan, first ministers; the physician Mukunda Kaviraja; and Keshava Chhatri, the royal diplomat and
adviser. <!-- F27 F33 F34 F35 -->
