# Rupa Gosvami
*Rupa Gosvami · @rupa-gosvami*

## The One Who Established the Lord's Desire
Narottama Dasa Thakura opens his “Prema-bhakti-chandrika” with a verse that says in a few words what matters most
about Rupa Gosvami:

“When will Sri Rupa, who established on earth the cherished desire of Sri Chaitanya, himself give me a place at his
feet?” (“Prema-bhakti-chandrika,” 1) <!-- F314 Q62 -->

That desire was this: the Lord descended from Goloka to give living beings what the world had never received
before — devotion to Himself, the exalted, radiant rasa. It was Rupa who revealed to the world this most intimate
prema-bhakti, which belongs to Krishna alone. That is why he is called the one who carried out Sri Chaitanya's
cherished design, and why his name stands first among the names of the six Gosvamis. <!-- F315 F318 I24 -->

In the “Chaitanya-chandrodaya-nataka,” Kavi Karnapura places in the mouth of Sarvabhauma Bhattacharya a verse that
lists Rupa's virtues one after another:

“Into Rupa — dear by his very nature, beloved by his very nature, by his very nature love, beautiful from birth,
like the Lord Himself — the Lord, who is one in His form, placed the form of His own pastimes.”
(“Chaitanya-chandrodaya-nataka,” 9.30)[^1]

The word “rupa” here is both “form” and Rupa's name; the sense of the verse is that the Lord enabled Rupa to understand and describe His lilas.
One biography explains the epithets of the verse thus: Rupa is the Lord's dear devotee, the object of His deep love and tenderness, wholly filled with
prema, non-different from Him, like Him, His intimate associate and the image of His vilasa; and in him the Lord
revealed the scripture of bhakti-rasa. In Krishna's eternal pastimes he is Rupa-manjari, of which more will be said
at the end of this article. <!-- F316 F317 Q63 Q1 -->

## Years of His Life
Two testimonies about the time of Rupa's appearance differ by four years. Bhaktivinoda Thakura, going through the
papers (daptar) of a certain Vaishnava, found in them the dates of the six Gosvamis and in 1885 (1292 of the Bengali
era) published them in the “Sajjana-toshani” (2nd year, p. 25), in a note entitled “Determining the Dates of the Six
Gosvamis.” According to these records, Rupa appeared in 1411 of the Shaka era (1546 Samvat, 1489 CE) and remained on
earth for seventy-five years — twenty-two at home and fifty-three in Vraja — and departed in 1486 Shaka (1621 Samvat,
1564 CE), on the twelfth day of the bright fortnight of the month of Shravana. The old manuscripts collected by the
learned pandita of the shripata of Gopivallabhapura, Vishvambharananda Deva Gosvami, agree with this exactly.[^2]
According to our Vaishnava calendar, the day of Rupa Gosvami's departure is the Gaura-dvadashi of the month of
Shridhara, which falls the morning after the Gaura-ekadashi that begins the Jhulana-yatra.[^3]

## Lineage
Rupa's family goes back to brahmanas who were kings of Karnata in South India. His genealogy of seven generations was
preserved by Rupa's nephew Jiva Gosvami at the end of the “Laghu-vaishnava-toshani” and by Narahari Chakravarti in the
“Bhakti-ratnakara” (1.540–568); Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati sets it out as well in his “Anubhashya” (on CC, Adi
10.84). <!-- F16 F17 Q4 Q5 -->

The founder of the line was Sarvajna, a Yajurvedi brahmana of the Bharadvaja gotra and king of Karnata; as the most
honoured of the brahmanas of that land he was called “Jagad-guru.” His son Aniruddha-deva, a master of the Vedas, had
two wives and two sons: Rupeshvara, learned in the shastras, and Harihara, skilled in arms. After their father's death
Harihara seized the kingdom from his brother, and Rupeshvara with his wife went east on eight horses and settled
with his friend, King Shikhareshvara.[^4] <!-- F9 F10 F11 -->

Rupeshvara's son, Padmanabha-deva, a pandita and a devotee of Jagannatha, settled on the Ganga at Navahatta
(Naihati); King Danujamardana honoured him, and there he worshipped the Deity of Purushottama. He had eighteen
daughters[^5] and five sons — Purushottama, Jagannatha, Narayana, Murari and the youngest, Mukunda-deva. Mukunda's
son, the brahmana Kumara-deva, moved because of unrest in Naihati to Bakla-Chandradvipa and also set up a residence
at Fatehabad (then in the district of Jessore), between Naihati and Bakla.[^6] Chandradvipa was an ancient kingdom in
the lands of present-day Bangladesh; it included Faridpur, Bakharganj and the southern parts of the Dacca and Pabna
districts. <!-- F12 F13 F14 -->

Kumara-deva was a pious brahmana; the name of his wife, Rupa's mother, has not come down to us. Of his many sons three
became renowned: the eldest, Sanatana, the second, Rupa, and the youngest, Vallabha, or Anupama; the names of the
others are unknown. Vallabha's only son was Jiva. Sanatana himself, in his commentary on the “Brihad-bhagavatamrita”
(1.3), calls Rupa his dear servant and says of him: the son of Kumara, of the line of Jagad-guru, the renowned acharya
of the brahmana families of Karnata. And the “Sanatanashtaka,” attributed to Rupa, calls Sanatana “the ornament of the
line of brahmana kings of the blessed South.” Apart from this genealogy, no reliable information about the
descendants of the family has been found. <!-- F15 F18 Q2 Q3 -->

## Childhood and Names
After Kumara-deva's death, Sanatana, Rupa and Vallabha lived and studied in the house of their maternal uncle in the
small village of Sakurma near the capital of Gauda. The elder brothers became brilliant scholars — in the words of
one biography, “the crown jewels of the Gaudiya Vaishnava scholars”; they were taught philosophy and the shastras by
Sarvabhauma Bhattacharya's brother Vidyavachaspati, who often stayed in Ramakeli.[^7] <!-- F19 F20 -->

From early childhood, the same biography relates, all three were immersed in bhava-bhakti. Remembering Vrindavana,
they played in the groves of tamala, keli-kadamba and tulasi around their home, named the ponds there Radha-kunda and
Shyama-kunda, and always served Sri Madana-mohana. News reached them of Gaurasundara's wonderful pastimes in nearby
Navadvipa, and they longed to see Him, but an inner voice told them to wait: soon they would have the darshana of
the Saviour of the fallen. When word came that Sri Chaitanya had taken sannyasa and gone to Puri, Rupa and Sanatana
fainted: they had never seen Him in Navadvipa, and now He was leaving Bengal for ever. Then a voice from the sky said,
“Do not grieve: the merciful Sri Gauranga will soon come here,” and the brothers were consoled. <!-- F21 F22 F23 -->

In this way the biographers stress that Rupa and Sanatana were not worldly people who came to faith in time, but
eternal participants in Krishna's pastimes. In their earthly pastimes the Lord's associates conceal their
transcendental nature, yet when they are near one another they cannot help revealing it; outwardly, however, the
brothers behaved as sadhakas striving for perfection, to show people how one ought to act. <!-- I1 -->

The names “Rupa” and “Sanatana” were given to the brothers by Sri Chaitanya Himself when they became His disciples;
their earlier names are unknown, though some hold that Sanatana was called Amara and Rupa Santosha. Vallabha too
received the name “Anupama” from the Lord; Krishnadasa Kaviraja says of him: “Sri Rupa's brother was also a great
Vaishnava. His name was Sri Vallabha, but he was better known as Anupama Mallik” (CC, Madhya 19.36). At court, however, the brothers were known by their titles — Sakara
Mallik and Dabir Khas. Vrindavana Dasa Thakura confirms this as well, anticipating the story at the very beginning
of the “Chaitanya-bhagavata”:

“In the Antya-khanda Sri Gaurasundara reveals Himself to Dabir Khas. The two brothers, recognising the Supreme Lord,
attain liberation and receive new names — Rupa and Sanatana.” (CB, Adi 1.171–172)

And in the story itself he says:

“At that time two fortunate souls came before Sri Chaitanya. The Lord cast His merciful glance on these two brothers,
Sakara Mallik and Rupa.” (CB, Antya 9.238–239) <!-- F24 F25 F26 Q7 Q13 Q14 -->

## At the Court of Hussain Shah
The brothers' learning, intelligence and noble character drew the attention of the ruler of Gauda, Hussain Shah, and
they came to hold the two highest posts at his court: Sanatana, “a Brihaspati in intellect,” and Rupa, unequalled in
appearance and virtues, became the “two pillars” of his rule.[^8] Sanatana received the title Sakara Mallik, Rupa that
of Dabir Khas; the Sultan addresses Rupa by this title in the “Chaitanya-charitamrita” as well. Rupa was the
Sultan's personal adviser, his private secretary, and Sanatana his first minister.[^9] <!-- F28 F29 -->

According to the dictionary “Raja-vyavahara-kosha,” the word “dabir” means “one versed in reasoning” (yuktabhijno
dabirah syat), and “khas” means “one's own,” that is, the sovereign's personal adviser. Others derive it from the
Persian “dabir” (secretary, munshi) and the Arabic “khas” (personal): “personal secretary”; “Sakara Mallik” would then
mean “chief secretary.” “Mallik” means “lord”: so the Muslims styled respected wealthy families close to the
government. Such titles were mere ranks conferred by Hussain Shah and in no way mean that the brothers' ancestors had
embraced Islam: Jiva calls their father “the best of the twice-born, the venerable Kumara” (dvija-varah shriman
kumarah), and there is nothing non-Hindu in the names of their forefathers. <!-- F30 F31 F32 -->

According to the “Anubhashya,” all three of Kumara's sons became Vaishnavas; for the sake of their service they left
their home in Fatehabad and settled in Ramakeli, where Jiva was born, and for their service to the nawab all three
received the title “Mallik.” The youngest, Vallabha, was the chief superintendent of the royal mint. The Shah rewarded
the brothers generously, and they became very wealthy. Hindus not infrequently had to serve a Muslim ruler in those
days, and among them were great devotees: Keshava Vasu Khan, the city magistrate and chief of the guard; Gopinatha Vasu
and Purandara Khan, first ministers; the physician Mukunda Kaviraja; and Keshava Chhatri, the royal diplomat and
adviser. <!-- F27 F33 F34 F35 -->

## Ramakeli
The shripata of Ramakeli lies on the Ganga in the district of Malda (West Bengal), south of the present town of
Malda.[^10] Here in Ramakeli, which Sultan Barbak made the capital of Bengal in 1486, Rupa and Sanatana had their
headquarters; many well-known devotees lived here as well, among them Sri Nrisimha, the son of Advaita Acharya. Under
the British, Ramakeli was a district centre. The place is also called Gupta-Vrindavana, “hidden Vrindavana.” <!-- F36 -->

Rupa and Sanatana built large houses both in Ramakeli and in Fatehabad. Great scholars and brahmanas came to them from
Bengal and beyond; many brahmanas from Karnata settled near their house, which stood on the Ganga near a small town
that one biography calls Bhatravari; brahmana panditas from Navadvipa also came to serve the brothers. <!-- F43 F44 -->

The memorial places of Rupa and Sanatana are shown here to this day. First of all there is Tamatala: on a high platform
grows a spreading tamala tree, and beside it four keli-kadambas, two on each side. According to tradition, it was under
this tree, at midnight, that Rupa and Sanatana first met Mahaprabhu; it is said that the Lord sat here during the
meeting and distributed love for Krishna, and a shrine with the prints of His feet now stands on the spot. There is also
in Ramakeli the Deity of Madana-mohana, known to have been installed by Rupa and Sanatana; there is the Sanatana-kunda,
and next to it are shown Radha-kunda, Shyama-kunda and eight kundas named after the sakhis headed by Lalita. Nearby is
the Rupa-sagara, a large pond said to have been dug at Rupa's order; it survives to this day. Here too is the
Baraduari, a huge stone audience hall (darbar) with twelve doors; in 1801, in the time of “Krent Sahib” (the reading of
the name is uncertain), its domes were covered with gold leaf, and tradition says that here Dabir Khas held his office.
And finally there is the ghat of Havaskhana: according to tradition, it was from here that Sanatana, having given the
jailer seven thousand coins, left his confinement and crossed the Ganga by night.[^11] <!-- F37 F38 F39 F40 F41 F42 -->

## The Meeting under the Tamala
On the pretext of a journey from Nilachala to Vrindavana, Sri Chaitanya came to Gauda-mandala and spent five days in
the house of Vachaspati in Vidyanagara (in Jahnu-dvipa, near Kola-dvipa); from there He went to Kuliya and then, by way
of Malda, to Ramakeli.[^12] When the rumour spread that from Kuliya the Lord would go to Vrindavana, Nrisimhananda
Brahmachari began in meditation to pave for Him a road of precious stones from Kuliya to Vrindavana and to spread over it
an unbroken bed of flowers. But when the road reached Kanai-natashala near Gauda, his mind was disturbed and the
meditation broke off. “This time,” said Nrisimhananda, “the Lord will go only as far as Kanai-natashala, not to
Vrindavana.” And so it happened. <!-- F45 F46 F47 F216 -->

Countless crowds followed the Lord, and so He came to Ramakeli on the Ganga, near Gauda. Even the badshah Hussain Shah
was amazed at His influence and grew alarmed, suspecting Him of a political aim. At first the king was reassured by the
devotee Keshava Chhatri, his adviser: this was only a mendicant sannyasi on pilgrimage with few people about Him, and
there was nothing to fear; but the king did not believe him, for thousands were following the Lord. At the same time
Keshava secretly sent the Lord advice to leave, since the king could not be trusted.[^13] <!-- F48 F49 -->

Then the Sultan called Dabir Khas in private and asked him about the Lord. Krishnadasa Kaviraja Gosvami records Rupa's
answer:

“‘The person you have taken for a great prophet is the Supreme Lord Himself. How great is your fortune, that the Lord
who granted you your power has Himself descended here, in your country. He always wishes you only good and grants
success to your words. His blessings will make you successful in every undertaking. Why do you ask me? Ask your own
heart! You are a king, a representative of God. Your heart already knows about Sri Chaitanya. The voice of your heart
will itself confirm the truth of this knowledge.’ The king replied, ‘Listen, my heart tells me that Sri Chaitanya is
the Supreme Lord Himself. Of this there is no doubt!’” (CC, Madhya 1.176–180)

So diplomatically did Rupa glorify the Lord that the Shah was reassured. <!-- F50 Q8 Q9 -->

Returning home, Dabir Khas took counsel with his elder brother. Both wished to see the Lord for themselves; concealing
their royal dress, they slipped out of the palace and late at night came first to Nityananda and Haridasa Thakura, who
told the Lord of their arrival.[^14] The brothers took straws between their teeth and tied cloth about their necks as a
sign of humility, fell at the Lord's feet like rods and with tears began to entreat Him. The Lord bade them rise and
blessed them, and they, with folded palms, glorified Sri Krishna Chaitanya, the merciful Saviour of the fallen, and said
of themselves: by birth, occupation and association we are among the lowest of men; You have come to deliver the
fallen — know then that there is no one in the world more sinful than we. You delivered Jagai and Madhai, but they were
brahmanas from holy Navadvipa, they did not serve low people, and they were saved by the Name, uttered even in abuse;

“whereas we are millions of times more sinful and lower than Jagai and Madhai. Our association with meat-eaters has
made us like them, mlechchhas, and our deeds no longer differ from theirs. Our connection with them has become so
strong that we ourselves have become enemies of the cows and of the whole brahminical culture. Our deeds have bound us
hand and foot so tightly that we have been cast into the foul abyss of sense enjoyment. No one in the whole universe
can save us but You, for You are the Saviour of the most fallen.” (CC, Madhya 1.196–199)

If You save us, they said, the whole universe will learn the greatness of Your mercy. We grieve over our unworthiness,
but Your divine qualities irresistibly attract us, and our hearts burn with an unattainable desire — as a dwarf dreams
of reaching the moon (CC, Madhya 1.204–205). <!-- F51 F52 Q11 -->

These words are an expression of great Vaishnava humility (dainya), not an admission of any real fall. Rupa and
Sanatana are the Lord's eternal associates, who stand above varnashrama, and to take their self-abasement literally,
regarding them as men of low birth, is an offence. <!-- I2 -->

The Lord answered:

“‘Listen, Dabir Khas. You and your brother are both My servants from long ago. From now on your names are Rupa and
Sanatana. Give up your humility now, for it breaks My heart. You sent Me several humble letters. From these letters I
gained an understanding of your life. From your letters I understood your hearts. And to instruct you, I constantly
quoted one verse: “A married woman attracted to another man only appears to be diligent in her household duties, while
in her heart she anticipates the sweetness of a new meeting with her beloved.” I came to Bengal only to see the two of
you. I had no other purpose. No one understands why I came to this village, Ramakeli. No one knows My intentions. It
is very good that you both have come to Me. Now go home and do not worry about anything. You are both My servants birth
after birth. Very soon Sri Krishna will certainly set you free.’” (CC, Madhya 1.207–215) <!-- F53 F54 Q10 Q12 -->

The verse about the woman who, while doing her household duties, thinks in her heart of her beloved was the Lord's
instruction to the brothers in reply to their letters: while serving at court, think of Krishna with all your heart.
Then He lovingly placed His hands on their heads, and they placed His feet on their heads and, having received the
mercy of His feet, began to look for a way to leave their service. Wishing to show that a conditioned soul is delivered
by the mercy of devotees, the Lord told His associates to bless the brothers. Nityananda, Haridasa, Srivasa, Gadadhara
Pandita, Mukunda, Jagadananda, Murari, Vakreshvara and others were there; at the Lord's bidding Rupa and Sanatana touched
their feet, and the devotees, joyfully calling “Hari! Hari!”, congratulated them on their great good fortune.
<!-- F55 F56 -->

At that time the youngest brother, Vallabha, also received the Lord's mercy: Mahaprabhu met him for the first time when
He came to Ramakeli and gave him the name “Anupama.” According to one biography, this meeting took place in 1513.
Haridasa Thakura, who accompanied the Lord on this interrupted journey to Vrindavana, also met Rupa and Sanatana here for
the first time, and from then on they knew one another's virtues well. And little Jiva, who was then living with his
uncles in Ramakeli, secretly saw Mahaprabhu's feet, as the “Bhakti-ratnakara” relates. <!-- F57 F59 F60 F61 -->

Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati explains in his “Anubhashya” (on CC, Madhya 1.208): Mahaprabhu showed His mercy to Dabir
Khas and Sakara Mallik by giving them the spiritual names Rupa and Sanatana. The giving of a name (nama-karana) is a
samskara of the lowest stage, yet whoever disregards the mercy shown in a spiritual name will not attain krishna-bhakti
and remains intoxicated with worldly position; the prakrita-sahajiyas, who wear neither the marks of a Vaishnava nor a
name received from the guru, are not Gaudiya Vaishnavas. <!-- F58 -->

Before leaving, the brothers gave the Lord their advice:

“‘O Lord! Although the ruler of Bengal is respectful towards You, You should leave this place as soon as possible. He is
a Muslim and should not be trusted. Nor is it right that such a crowd follows You. You are going to Vrindavana, and
thousands of people are following You. That is not fitting for a pilgrim.’” (CC, Madhya 1.222–224)

Vrindavana is a place of simplicity, not of pomp; the Lord's work here was done, and it was better for Him to leave
before misfortune came. Having blessed the brothers, the Lord left Ramakeli. In the morning He came to Kanai-natashala,
where He saw many pictures of Krishna's pastimes, and at night He pondered Sanatana's advice and decided: with such a
crowd I shall spoil the simplicity of Vrindavana — I shall go there alone or with one companion. And having gone only
as far as Kanai-natashala, He turned back to Puri.[^15] <!-- F62 -->

The “Chaitanya-bhagavata” tells of the brothers' meeting with the Lord differently — as an event in Nilachala, where Rupa
and Sanatana came after giving up their worldly affairs. The Lord told Sakara Mallik and Dabir Khas to beg for
prema-bhakti at the feet of Advaita Acharya, and Advaita blessed them; then He told them to go to Mathura and give the
sweetness of bhakti to the people of the West, and said:

“I shall come to Mathura later. Prepare a secluded place for Me.” Then the Lord changed the name of Sakara Mallik to
Sanatana. (CB, Antya 9.272–273)

Sakara Mallik, in Vrindavana Dasa's words, became “Sanatana the avadhuta”; the note on this passage calls such a
giving of a name the “third samskara.”[^16]
<!-- F63 Q15 -->

Already from the meeting in Ramakeli it is clear that Mahaprabhu intended to teach the world through Rupa and Sanatana.

## Leaving Royal Service
Soon a strong spirit of renunciation awoke in the brothers. To be freed from service the sooner and join the Lord, they
paid two brahmanas handsomely to perform a purashcharana of the Krishna mantra on their behalf.[^17] Rupa left his post
and carried a great deal of wealth from Ramakeli by boat to his house in Fatehabad. There he divided it: half he gave to
brahmanas and Vaishnavas, a quarter for the maintenance of his relatives, and the remaining quarter he entrusted to
reliable brahmanas against future misfortune. For Sanatana he left ten thousand coins in Gauda.[^18] <!-- F64 F65 F66 -->

Meanwhile Sanatana, pleading illness, stopped attending court and studied the “Bhagavatam” with a group of Sanskrit
scholars. The Shah first sent him a physician, then, learning the truth, came himself, and when Sanatana flatly refused
to return to his duties, had him imprisoned and set off on a campaign against Orissa. <!-- F67 -->

Hearing that the Lord had returned from Gauda to Nilachala and would soon go to Vrindavana, Rupa sent two messengers to
Puri to find out when Mahaprabhu would set out by the forest road through Jharikhanda; the messengers returned to Gauda
with news of His journey. Rupa wrote to Sanatana that the Lord had gone to Vrindavana and that he himself was following
Him with his younger brother Anupama, and begged his brother to free himself quickly by any means and come to the Lord's
feet.[^19] Bhaktivinoda Thakura (“Amrita-pravaha-bhashya,” 2.20.3) reports that the letter consisted of a single Sanskrit
verse:

“Where is Mathura, the city of the lord of the Yadavas? Where is Uttara-Koshala of the lord of the Raghus? Remembering
this, steady your mind: this world is not eternal.”

Sanatana understood the hidden meaning — the Lord had gone to Mathura — and rejoiced. He bribed the jailer with seven
thousand coins from the money Rupa had left, escaped from confinement and set out after his brothers.[^20]

## Prayaga
Rupa and Anupama came to Prayaga and learned with great joy that the Lord was there. When He went for darshana of
Bindu-madhava, crowds ran after Him, and the brothers kept their distance; but when the Lord came to take His meal in
the house of a South Indian brahmana, they came there and, holding straws between their teeth, prostrated themselves at
His feet, rising and falling down again and again and reciting verses of praise and humility. <!-- F72 -->

“Sri Chaitanya was overjoyed to see Sri Rupa and asked him to rise and come to Him. ‘It is impossible to describe
Krishna's mercy!’ He said. ‘By His mercy you have both been pulled out of the well of worldly pleasures.’ ‘One who is
brilliantly educated and knows all the Vedas, but has not dedicated himself to unalloyed service to Me, should not be
considered My devotee. On the other hand, a person, even if born in a family of dog-eaters, may be My dear devotee if he
is not attracted by the fruits of karma and jnana. All should show respect to such a person, and whatever he offers
should be accepted with respect. Such a devotee of Mine is worthy of the same worship that is offered to Me.’ Reciting
this verse, He embraced both brothers and then mercifully touched their heads with His foot.” (CC, Madhya 19.48–51)

The verse the Lord recited is from the “Itihasa-samuchchaya.” <!-- F73 F74 Q17 -->

Having received this mercy, Rupa — the best of transcendental poets (aprakrita-kavi-shreshtha) — together with his younger
brother, with folded palms, bowed to the Lord with a verse of his own:

“O Krishna, I offer You my obeisances. Your name is Sri Krishna Chaitanya. The colour of Your body is golden yellow. You
embody divine compassion. You are engaged in bestowing love for Krishna. And at the same time You are Krishna Himself! I
offer You my humble obeisances.” (CC, Madhya 19.53) <!-- F75 Q18 -->

This obeisance from Rupa's lips was accepted by the whole Rupanuga-sampradaya as its ever-revered prayer to Sri Gaura.
In it are named at once the name, form, quality, associates and pastimes of Gaurasundara: the name — “Sri Krishna
Chaitanya”; the form — golden radiance; the quality — supreme magnanimity; the associates — the parshadas who belong to
Krishna Himself (Svarupa, Rupa, Ramananda Raya and others, and with them the sampradaya that follows them); the pastime —
the bestowal of krishna-prema. Thus the whole of gaura-tattva is contained in a single verse. <!-- F76 F77 I6 -->

The Lord seated Rupa beside Him and asked about Sanatana. “He is now the king's prisoner,” Rupa answered, “and will be
freed only if You release him.” “Sanatana's bonds are already broken,” said the Lord; “he will soon come to Me.” That day,
at the brahmana's invitation, the brothers stayed in his house and received the remnants of the Lord's meal. The Lord
took up residence at Triveni, the confluence of the Ganga and the Yamuna, and Rupa and Anupama settled nearby.[^21]

## Vallabha Bhatta
The learned Vallabha Bhatta was living at that time in the village of Arail (Adail), about a mile from the confluence,
on the other bank of the Yamuna. The seat (baithak) of Vallabhacharya is shown today about a krosha from present-day
Arail, at a place called Deorah: it is no longer in the village itself but in the Arail pargana, and according to local
tradition Sri Chaitanya came there. The owner of this baithak is Muralidhara Lalji, the owner of the well-known Gopalji
temple in Kashi. The very name “Deorah” is a corruption of “deva-rishi”: according to the books of the Vallabha
sampradaya, gods and rishis lived there for the sake of Vallabhacharya's company. <!-- F81 F82 F83 F84 -->

Hearing of the Lord's great influence and that He was at Triveni, Vallabha Bhatta hastened to Him and prostrated himself
at His feet; the Lord embraced him and began to speak of Krishna. In this conversation His prema welled up, but,
regarding Vallabha as an outsider (bahiranga), the Lord concealed His feeling, and Vallabha could only marvel at what he
saw.[^22] Rupa and Anupama were at the Lord's feet, and He introduced His most beloved devotees to Vallabha. Out of
humility the brothers bowed to the Bhatta from a distance; and when he moved towards them to embrace them, they —
seeking no honour and honouring others (amani-manada) — drew back, calling themselves unworthy: “We are low and
untouchable; do not defile yourself,” and sat at a distance. <!-- F85 F86 -->

Pleased with their humility, the Lord, after showing Vallabha — a man of high family proud of his learning — outward
worldly respect, decided to test his way of thinking:

“‘Do not touch him, for he belongs to the lowest caste, whereas you are a brahmana of an ancient and noble family, of
profound learning and experienced in performing many Vedic rites.’ Hearing that their lips were constantly repeating the
names of Krishna, Vallabha Bhatta understood Sri Chaitanya's hints and said, ‘The constant chanting of Krishna's names
makes them dance. Therefore they cannot be low; on the contrary, they are most exalted.’” (CC, Madhya 19.69–71)

And Vallabha quoted a verse of the “Srimad-Bhagavatam” (3.33.7): “Glory and praise to those whose tongue repeats Your
holy name! They are to be honoured even if they were born in families of dog-eaters…” (CC, Madhya 19.72)
<!-- F87 F88 Q19 Q20 -->

Vallabha passed the test: there was in him no offence of seeing a Vaishnava as an ordinary mortal; he had faith in the
name of Hari and was not merely a smarta brahmana bound by rituals; the Vaishnava nature had largely awakened in him,
and so his invitation could be accepted. The measure here is not birth or learning but one's attitude towards the
devotee and faith in the holy name. Seeing that an understanding of the devotee's superiority had taken root in
Vallabha's heart, the Lord praised him and, condemning the views of the ritualistic smartas, quoted verses of the
“Hari-bhakti-sudhodaya” (3.11–12):

“In the fire of the sacred practice of loving service to God, all the consequences of past sins, such as low birth, are
burned away. Even one born in a family of dog-eaters may win the approval of great theologians by the power of his
sincere service to God. Whereas even a knower of the Vedas is not recognised as such if he is an atheist.” “If someone
has no loving devotion to the Supreme Lord, all his virtues, such as high birth, knowledge of the scriptures,
repetition of Vedic mantras, as well as austerity and strict observance of vows, are like ornaments on a corpse. Such
ornaments can bring only false joy to the unenlightened.” (CC, Madhya 19.74–75) <!-- F89 F90 Q21 I7 -->

Vallabha invited the Lord and His companions to his home and at the Triveni ghat seated them in a boat; with the Lord
were Rupa, Anupama, Balabhadra Bhattacharya and Krishnadasa the Rajput. As the boat was crossing the Yamuna, love seized
Mahaprabhu at the sight of its dark waters, and He began to dance, frightening everyone:

“With a loud cry He leapt into the waters of the Yamuna. At this sight everyone was seized with fear and trembling. In
great haste the devotees pulled Him back into the boat, but, finding Himself there again, He began to dance.” (CC,
Madhya 19.79–80) <!-- F91 -->

Having brought the Lord to his home, Vallabha washed His feet with his own hands, and the whole family placed that water
on their heads; he dressed the Lord in a new kaupina and upper cloth and performed a “great puja” with incense, flowers,
fragrances and lamps. Then with great care he fed the Lord many dishes and gave the remnants of His meal to Rupa and
Krishnadasa the Rajput;[^23] after the meal he offered Him spices, laid Him down to rest and himself began to massage His
feet, and the Lord told him to go and eat too. <!-- F92 F93 -->

There came the mahabhagavata Raghupati Upadhyaya from Tirhut,[^24] a disciple of Madhavendra Puri, and the Lord spoke
with him at length about rasa. To the Lord's questions about the highest form, the highest abode, the age and the rasa,
he answered thus:

“Shyamasundara is the highest form of God, the city of Mathura is the highest divine abode, the beginning of blossoming
youth is the highest time of life, and nothing can compare with the sweetness of the rasa of conjugal love.”
(“Padyavali,” 82; CC, Madhya 19.106)

The Lord embraced him. All the people of the village, on seeing Mahaprabhu, became devotees of Krishna, and Vallabha took
the Lord back to Prayaga by boat. <!-- F94 F95 Q22 -->

## Ten Days at Dashashvamedha
Wary of the crowds at Triveni, the Lord went to the Dashashvamedha ghat. In all He remained in Prayaga only ten days,
staying there unrecognised. <!-- F96 -->

“Wary of the great gathering of people, Sri Chaitanya went to the Dashashvamedha ghat. There He instructed Sri Rupa
Gosvami, investing him with His special power. There He revealed to Sri Rupa the highest truths about Krishna, teaching
him the highest conclusions of the science of bhakti and rasa in full accord with the ‘Srimad-Bhagavatam.’ He imparted
to him all the confidential conclusions He had earlier heard from Ramananda Raya. By His mercy Sri Rupa was able to
grasp them perfectly.” (CC, Madhya 19.114–116)[^25] <!-- F97 Q155 -->

Having invested His shakti in the heart of this most worthy disciple, the Lord made him mature and awakened all truths
in his heart, so that Rupa became skilled in expounding them. These teachings are called the “Sri-rupa-shiksha.” Kavi
Karnapura says in the “Chaitanya-chandrodaya-nataka”:

“When in the course of time the accounts of the pastimes of Vrindavana had vanished from people's memory, the Lord, to
glorify them again in all their fullness, bathed Rupa and Sanatana in the nectar of His mercy in that same place.”
(“Chaitanya-chandrodaya-nataka,” 9.38)[^26]

And elsewhere in the same drama (9.29): Rupa, long bound fast by Gaura's beloved qualities and free of attachment to
home, revealed his form like the highest rasa embodied, and in Prayaga the Lord bestowed His mercy on him and his younger
brother Anupama with loving talks and tight embraces. As one biography explains, Rupa, attached from the very beginning
to the qualities of his ishtadeva Gaurasundara, only played the role of household life and displayed the lila of
liberation from it: an eternal associate of the Lord is not bound by home. <!-- F98 F99 F100 F101 Q23 Q24 -->

Rupa himself recorded this in the opening verse of the “Bhakti-rasamrita-sindhu” (1.1.2), which Krishnadasa Kaviraja
quotes:

“I am the lowest of men and have no knowledge, yet the Lord has mercifully inspired me to write books on bhakti.
Therefore I humbly bow down at the lotus feet of the Supreme Lord Sri Chaitanya Mahaprabhu, who has given me the ability
to do this.” (CC, Madhya 19.134)

“Lowest,” “wretched” are words of humility; and the inspiration in the heart of which Rupa speaks is the shakti
invested by the Lord: He instructed Rupa not with words alone but revealed all truths within him. This was not the training of a novice: the
Lord was giving shiksha to His eternal associate in order to reveal bhakti-rasa to the world through him. According to one
biography, in Prayaga Mahaprabhu directly told Rupa to write the “Bhakti-rasamrita-sindhu.” <!-- F102 Q25 I8 -->

For ten days in succession, having invested His shakti in Rupa, the Lord set forth to him in concise form, like sutras,
the characteristics of bhakti-rasa. Giving him the plan of a future book on rasa, He described the characteristics of
the various rasas, showed how to taste the bottomless ocean of rasa and explained how difficult it is to attain
devotion to Krishna. Through Rupa, Mahaprabhu revealed to the world Krishna's pastimes in Vrindavana and the path to
Krishna in Vrindavana. Krishnadasa Kaviraja testifies:

“By the mercy of Sanatana Gosvami I learned the truths of bhakti. And by the mercy of Sri Rupa Gosvami I tasted the
ambrosia of loving relationships with Sri Krishna.” (CC, Adi 5.203)

And again:

“Through Haridasa Thakura He revealed the greatness of the holy names of Krishna. And through Sanatana Gosvami He
disclosed the essence of sacred devotion to God. Through Sri Rupa Gosvami He revealed the taste of transcendental loving
relationships with Krishna in Vrindavana. Who is able to describe all the confidential deeds of Sri Chaitanya?” (CC,
Antya 5.86–87)

Kaviraja begins the nineteenth chapter of the Madhya-lila, which tells of the teachings given to Rupa, thus:

“Wishing to revive the memory and knowledge of Sri Krishna's pastimes in Vrindavana, lost over time, Sri Chaitanya
Mahaprabhu invested Sri Rupa with His divine power and empowered him to do so, just as earlier He had given Brahma the
power to create all the worlds of this universe.” (CC, Madhya 19.1) <!-- F103 F104 F105 F106 F107 F108 Q26 Q27 -->

## A Drop of the Ocean
Krishnadasa Kaviraja sets out the teachings of Prayaga in the nineteenth chapter of the Madhya-lila; here we shall only
trace their course. The Lord began thus:

“‘Sri Rupa! I shall explain to you the science of bhakti-rasa briefly, for no one can describe it in its fullness. The
ocean of bhakti-rasa is boundless; it simply cannot be measured. I shall help you taste just one drop of it, so that you
can understand its essence.’” (CC, Madhya 19.136–137) <!-- F109 -->

Countless living beings, said the Lord, wander through eight million four hundred thousand species of life as their
karma dictates. The shrutis compare the soul to a particle of the tip of a hair divided into a hundred parts and then
again into many hundreds (“Mundaka Upanishad” 3.1.9: “this atma is minute”). The prayers of the personified Vedas in the
“Bhagavatam” (10.87.30) say: “If the countless embodied souls were all-pervading, they would not be subject to You, O
Unchanging One; but they are minute, and therefore subject. You are their inseparable cause and ruler; and whoever
fancies You their equal does not know, for his view is corrupt.” Souls come from the Lord like sparks from a fire, and
He is their cause, ruler and inner witness; the doctrine of the identity of the soul and God is the fruit of a false
teaching. <!-- F110 F111 Q28 Q29 -->

Souls are eternally liberated or eternally conditioned. The conditioned are immobile (trees and plants) or mobile; the
mobile are beasts and birds, creatures of the waters and of the land, and among the creatures of the land human beings
are very few. If from among humans one excludes the barbarian mlechchhas, the Pulindas, the Buddhists and the Shabaras,
there remain the followers of the Vedas; some of them follow dharma, others do not; of those who follow dharma many are
devoted to rituals, others to knowledge, and of ten million knowers only one is truly liberated, that is, free of worldly
understanding.[^27] Of all the liberated, one who has taken up the service of Krishna with faith is a krishna-bhakta: he
has no desires other than service. All others, up to the liberated, are bound by the thirst for enjoyment (bhukti) or
liberation (mukti): the pious and the ritualists seek enjoyment, the knowers liberation, others the siddhis of yoga. As
long as these three desires are in the heart, there is no peace; only the selfless devotee of Krishna attains the highest
peace. So Parikshit says to Shukadeva (“Bhagavatam” 6.14.4–5): “Of the pious only a few seek liberation, O best of the
twice-born; of thousands who seek it hardly one is liberated and attains perfection; and among millions of the liberated
and perfected a peaceful devotee of Narayana is the greatest rarity, O great sage.” <!-- F112 F113 F114 F115 F116 Q30 -->

Then the Lord told Rupa about the creeper of bhakti:

“Wandering throughout the universe, only immensely fortunate souls, by the mercy of Guru and Krishna, receive the rare
opportunity to obtain the seed of loving service to God. <…> Such a fortunate soul should, like a gardener, carefully
plant this seed in his heart. And it should be watered by hearing and chanting.” (CC, Madhya 19.151–152)

The creeper grows, pierces the shell of the universe, passes the waters of the Viraja and the effulgence of Brahma-loka,
reaches the spiritual sky and rises to Goloka-Vrindavana, where it climbs the wish-fulfilling tree of Krishna's feet and
there bears the fruit of prema. This creeper blossoms not in this world, nor even in Vaikuntha, but only in Vrindavana,
at Krishna's feet. But watering alone is not enough:

“But if an offence against a Vaishnava is committed, it is like a mad elephant that uproots or tramples this sprout,
and it dies and withers. <…> The devotee, like a careful gardener, must fence this sprout of devotion on all sides,
protecting it from the mad elephant of offences.” (CC, Madhya 19.156–157)

The leaves of the creeper can also be withered by scorching heat; but at the unripe stage the greatest obstacle on the
path of sadhana is offence against a Vaishnava. Therefore the wise gardener puts up a fence — he hears hari-katha in the
company of sadhus, and this destroys the anarthas. The verse about the mad elephant, says one biography, should always
be remembered by one who seeks pure bhakti. <!-- F117 F118 F119 I9 -->

The second danger is the weeds that grow beside the creeper: the thirst for enjoyment and liberation, forbidden acts,
hypocrisy and fault-finding, a tendency to violence against living beings, the thirst for gain, the hope of worldly
honour and position. It is they that are fed by offences in hearing and chanting, while the main stem withers; therefore
they must be cut from the very beginning. Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati (“Anubhashya” on CC, Madhya 19.160–161) lists
what bad association leads to: the thirst for enjoyment, liberation and siddhis, deceit, illicit connection with women,
the sham bhakti of the sahajiyas, the five abodes of Kali — gambling, intoxication, illicit sex, slaughter and the hoarding
of gold — regarding Vaishnavas as a caste, dishonest collection of money, ostentatious seclusion for the sake of fame,
concessions to non-devotees, and “discipleship” under a caste “guru” hostile to Vishnu and the Vaishnavas. Pull up the
weeds at once, and the creeper will reach Goloka; otherwise there is the round of births in higher and lower worlds.
Then the main stem will reach Vrindavana and yield the ripe fruit of prema, and the gardener, holding on to the creeper,
will approach the tree of Krishna's feet and, serving them, will taste in the highest happiness the juice of that fruit.
<!-- F120 F121 I10 -->

Prema is the highest goal of the soul; the four aims of life — dharma, artha, kama and moksha — are like a blade of grass
before it. It is born not from a semblance of bhakti (bhaktyabhasa) but only from pure bhakti. The mark of pure bhakti
is that there is no desire other than the eternal satisfaction of the senses of Krishna, Adhokshaja; no worship of His
other aspects — Brahman, Paramatma — in place of Krishna; no coverings of impersonal knowledge, of the obligatory and
occasional rites of the smritis (these are not service), of the thirst for the siddhis of hatha- and raja-yoga, of bodily
and mental renunciation for its own sake — austerities and vows. Free of all this, accepting in life only what is
favourable to bhakti, to serve Krishna with all the senses — this is shuddha-bhakti. The “Narada-pancharatra,” which
Rupa will quote at the beginning of the “Bhakti-rasamrita-sindhu,” defines it: “Service to Hrishikesha, the Lord of the
senses, with one's senses — free of all designations (upadhis) and pure through devotion to Him alone — is called
bhakti.” Service to Hrishikesha with all the senses is the essential characteristic of bhakti (svarupa-lakshana), and
its two marginal characteristics (tatastha) are freedom from upadhis and orientation towards Krishna alone together with
its own purity. <!-- F122 F123 F124 F125 F126 Q31 -->

Bhakti tinged by the three gunas is saguna; but beyond it there is nirguna-bhakti, pure bhakti. Its mark is given in the
“Bhagavatam” (3.29.11–14): as soon as one hears of the Lord's qualities, the mind flows to Him unceasingly, as the Ganga
flows to the ocean; such bhakti is causeless and unobstructed; the devotees do not accept, even when offered, salokya,
sarshti, samipya, sarupya or oneness with the Lord without service; this is the supreme (atyantika) bhakti-yoga, by which
the soul crosses beyond the three gunas of maya and attains pure prema.[^28] But if desires for enjoyment, liberation and
the like live in the heart, prema will not be born even from hundreds of sadhanas: the thirst for bhukti and mukti is
two witches, and as long as they are in the heart, the happiness of bhakti will not come. <!-- F127 F128 F129 Q32 -->

Bhakti has three stages — sadhana, bhava and prema; its nine forms, beginning with hearing and chanting, are first
performed in sadhana-bhakti. As the anarthas diminish through hearing and chanting with faith, faith rises and is
called nishtha, ruchi, asakti, and then bhava, or rati. Rati is born from sadhana-bhakti; thickening, it is called prema,
and further sneha, mana, pranaya, raga, anuraga, bhava and mahabhava, just as the juice of the sugar cane becomes
molasses (gur), then khanda-sara, sugar, sitopala and the finest candy, mishri. All the stages from rati to mahabhava are
the sthayi-bhava, the abiding sentiment of krishna-bhakti-rasa. Rasa arises when vibhava, anubhava, sattvika and
vyabhichari join the sthayi-bhava. The sthayi-bhava is the chief foundation, the root (mula) of rasa; vibhava is its
cause (hetu), anubhava its effect (karya), the sattvika-bhavas its special effect (karya-vishesha), and the vyabhichari
(sanchari) its helpers (sahaya). Vibhava is alambana and uddipana; alambana is vishaya and ashraya: in krishna-bhakti-rasa the devotee is the
ashraya, Krishna the vishaya, and Krishna's qualities the uddipana. <!-- F130 F131 F132 F133 -->

“One of these five rasas abides constantly in the devotee's heart, whereas the seven additional tastes may appear
suddenly, owing to particular circumstances.” (CC, Madhya 19.188)

The five principal rasas are shanta, dasya, sakhya, vatsalya and madhura; the seven secondary ones are hasya, adbhuta,
vira, karuna, raudra, bhayanaka and bibhatsa. The secondary ones only nourish the principal: they come when occasioned
and, having played their part, depart.[^29] Krishna-rati is twofold: mixed with awareness of majesty, and pure. In
Dvaraka, Mathura and Vaikuntha bhakti is mixed with awareness of majesty, and prema there is constrained; but in Gokula
the cowherds and gopis, in pure love, do not acknowledge Krishna's majesty even when they see it. <!-- F134 F135 I11 -->

In shanta-rasa there is fidelity to Krishna and the giving up of thirst for anything else; as sound, the quality of
ether, is present in all the elements, so the qualities of shanta are present in all the higher rasas. But in shanta
there is no feeling of “He is mine”: its object is Parabrahman, Paramatma, worship in it is imbued with knowledge, and
there is no service in it. In dasya this feeling is added to shanta — “He is my Lord, I am His eternal servant”; awareness
of His lordship and reverence abound, and here service begins. In sakhya confidence (vishrambha) is added to the
qualities of shanta and dasya, there is no reverence, and “mine” grows into “He is my equal.” In vatsalya service becomes
care, and the informality of sakhya and an abundance of the feeling “mine” go as far as punishments and scoldings: the
devotee regards himself as the guardian and Krishna as his ward. And in madhura, to fidelity, diligent service,
informality and care is firmly joined service by the giving of oneself in the mood of the beloved (kanta-bhava); in it
all five qualities meet, and as in earth the properties of all the elements beginning with ether are united, so in
madhura-rasa the qualities of all the rasas are united. Thus the Lord established the supremacy of madhura-rasa.
<!-- F136 F137 F138 F139 F140 -->

In conclusion the Lord said:

“‘Such is My general description of the various rasas of loving service to God. You should consider how this can be
explained in more detail. In one who constantly thinks of Krishna, love for Him gradually manifests. By Krishna's mercy
even an uneducated person can reach the far shores of the ocean of loving relationships with God.’” (CC, Madhya
19.235–236) <!-- F141 -->

## First Visit to Vrindavana
“Having said this, Sri Chaitanya tenderly embraced Sri Rupa. Then He decided to set out for Benares. When the next
morning Sri Chaitanya was ready to set out, Sri Rupa bowed before Him and made a humble request: ‘If You permit, I would
like to go with You. I cannot bear these powerful waves of separation.’ Sri Chaitanya replied, ‘Your duty is to carry out
My instructions. Vrindavana is not so far from here. Therefore go there. From Vrindavana you will come to Bengal, and
from there go to Jagannatha Puri, where we shall meet.’ So Sri Chaitanya embraced Sri Rupa and boarded the boat, and Rupa
fell unconscious on the spot. The brahmana from South India took Sri Rupa into his house, and then both brothers set out
for Vrindavana.” (CC, Madhya 19.237–243) <!-- F142 F143 -->

In Mathura, at Dhruva-ghata, Rupa met Subuddhi Raya. He had formerly been an official in Gauda; when Hussain Shah
deprived him of caste, he came to Kashi and, rejecting the verdicts of the smarta panditas, at the Lord's bidding took
complete shelter of Krishna's name and went to Vrindavana. In Mathura he gathered and sold dry firewood; he himself lived
on a pice worth of roasted chickpeas, and with the rest he fed needy Vaishnavas, and to all the Bengalis who came there he
gave rice with yoghurt and oil for massage. Serving the Vaishnavas, as his example shows, is not for the rich alone:
whoever sincerely wishes to serve will be given the means by the Lord. Rupa had known Subuddhi Raya before and rejoiced greatly
to see his renunciation and his service to the Vaishnavas; Subuddhi served him with great love and himself led him
round the holy places of Vraja-mandala — all the twelve forests of Vrindavana (CC, Madhya 25.200). <!-- F144 F145 F146 F147 I12 -->

At the sight of the places of Krishna's pastimes, the plan of a drama about His pastimes was born in the heart of Rupa,
“the crown of transcendental poets.” He began it right there in Vrindavana and then composed its nandi-shloka, the
opening verse of benediction (and with it several verses of the prologue: obeisances, blessing, an indication of the
subject). That time Rupa stayed in Vrindavana only a month. Looking for Sanatana, the brothers went to Prayaga by the road
along the Ganga, while Sanatana was meanwhile going to Mathura by the great royal road — and they missed each other. In
Mathura Sanatana learned about Rupa from Subuddhi Raya, and later, walking round the twelve forests with Subuddhi and a
Sanoriya brahmana, heard from them that Rupa and Anupama had shortly before made this circuit and gone along the Ganga to
Bengal. <!-- F148 F149 F150 -->

In Kashi Rupa and Anupama met the Maharashtrian brahmana, Chandrashekhara and Tapana Mishra, took their meals in Tapana
Mishra's house and heard from him the whole account of the Lord's teachings to Sanatana. At that time the boy Raghunatha,
Tapana's son, saw Rupa and heard Mahaprabhu's teachings. After staying ten days in Kashi, the brothers went to Gauda; on
the way Rupa pondered the events of the drama and made notes (kadchas). And in Gauda Anupama left his body on the bank of
the Ganga (“attained the Ganga”) and went to the eternal abode of Ramachandra.[^30] <!-- F151 F152 F153 -->

## Satyabhamapura and Puri
After Anupama's passing, Rupa, burning with longing, set out for Nilachala, to the Lord's feet. Because of his
brother's passing he was delayed on the road longer than he expected and no longer found the Gaudiyas led by Shivananda
Sena — they had gone to Nilachala earlier; so Rupa missed their annual journey and reached Puri much later. <!-- F154 -->

“In the province of Orissa there is a village called Satyabhama-pura. On his way to Puri he stopped there for the night.
There in a dream he saw a maiden of unearthly beauty, who showed him her special favour. ‘Dedicate a whole play to Me,’
she commanded him. ‘By My mercy it will turn out extraordinarily beautiful.’ On waking, Sri Rupa thought: ‘Satyabhama
tells me to write a separate play. I intended to describe Krishna's pastimes in Vrindavana and in Dvaraka in one play.
Now I must divide them into two parts.’” (CC, Antya 1.40–44)

So Rupa understood that the maiden who had appeared to him was Satyabhama-devi, and decided to write the vraja-lila
and the dvaraka-lila separately, as two dramas. <!-- F155 F156 -->

“Absorbed in these thoughts, he soon reached Jagannatha Puri. Arriving there, he went to the hut of Haridasa Thakura.
With boundless love Haridasa Thakura received Rupa and said, ‘Mahaprabhu told me that you would come.’ Every day Sri
Chaitanya came to Haridasa after seeing the upala-bhoga ceremony in the temple of Jagannatha. Unexpectedly He appeared
before them. Rupa Gosvami prostrated himself. ‘This is Rupa offering You dandavat,’ said Haridasa. Having greeted
Haridasa, the Lord embraced Rupa.” (CC, Antya 1.45–48)

Drawn by Rupa's humble and pure devotion, the Lord seated them both beside Him, asked about their welfare and talked
with them at length. Asked about Sanatana, Rupa replied, “We did not meet: I went along the Ganga, he by the high road;
in Prayaga I heard that he had gone to Vrindavana.” He also told of Anupama's passing. <!-- F157 F158 F159 -->

Rupa met the Lord's other devotees, and one day the Lord came with all His associates and introduced Rupa to them; Rupa
bowed at the feet of each, and all embraced him. Out of love for Rupa the Lord asked Nityananda and Advaita to pour Their
mercy on him with all Their hearts: “By Your mercy may Rupa obtain the power to spread krishna-rasa-bhakti in the
world.” Thus Mahaprabhu arranged for Rupa, and later Sanatana too, to meet Advaita and Nityananda. Rupa became dear to
all the Lord's devotees — both the Gaudiyas and the people of Utkala. <!-- F160 F161 F162 -->

Every day the Lord visited Haridasa, spoke intimately with Rupa and gave them both mahaprasada received from the temple;
He sent them the remnants of His own meal with Govinda. For Haridasa, Rupa and Sanatana, though they were gurus even for
the best of transcendental brahmanas, did not go into the temple of Jagannatha: out of great humility and for the sake of
propriety, after their long closeness to the Muslim court, they considered themselves no better than mlechchhas.[^31]

“Haridasa Thakura, Sri Rupa and Sri Sanatana never entered the temple of Jagannatha. After seeing the upala-bhoga
ceremony, Mahaprabhu would visit these three saints and only then go home. So the Lord came to these devotees every day,
even if one of them was not there.” (CC, Madhya 1.63–65)

Thus Sri Chaitanya, the “moving Jagannatha,” came to them Himself, and this was His constant rule. Rupa did not even enter
the house of Kashi Mishra to see the Lord, though nothing prevented him, and lived with Haridasa at Siddha-bakula.
Mahaprabhu respected his decision and never told him to go to Jagannatha: Rupa's humility attracted the Lord, and through
his example He wished to teach the world humility. The “Bhakti-ratnakara” says that the Lord “through Ramananda revealed
the crushing of Kandarpa's pride, through Damodara revealed detachment, through Haridasa showed forbearance, through
Sanatana and Rupa revealed humility” (“Bhakti-ratnakara,” 1.630–631).[^32] <!-- F163 F164 F165 F166 Q46 I15 -->

## “Do Not Take Krishna out of Vraja”
“The next day the omniscient Lord Sri Chaitanya came to Rupa Gosvami and said to him: ‘Do not try to take Krishna out of
Vrindavana, for He never wishes to go beyond it. “The Krishna who is called Yadu-kumara is Vasudeva-Krishna. He is
different from Krishna, the son of Nanda Maharaja. Krishna Yadu-kumara performs His pastimes in Mathura and Dvaraka, but
Krishna Nanda-nandana never leaves Vrindavana.”’” (CC, Antya 1.65–67)

The verse the Lord quoted is from the “Yamala.” Krishna, the son of Yadu, is Vasudeva, who plays in Mathura and Dvaraka;
but the son of the king of the cowherds, Gopendra-nandana, does not leave Vrindavana. <!-- F167 F168 Q33 Q34 -->

Hearing this, Rupa was amazed. Now he was firmly convinced that Satyabhama-devi and the Lord were separately telling him
to write two dramas: the “Lalita-madhava,” about the pastimes in Dvaraka, and the “Vidagdha-madhava,” solely about the
pastimes in Vraja. And the dramas, formerly conceived as one, he began to plan and write separately — each with its own
nandi, prologue (prastava) and plot. <!-- F169 -->

## “Priyah So ’yam”
The Ratha-yatra came. In divine madness the Lord danced before the chariot in the mood of separation and repeated a verse
by an ordinary poet, seemingly about quite worldly lovers; no one understood why He loved this verse so much. Only Svarupa
Damodara knew its hidden meaning and delighted the Lord with padas in the same mood. But by Mahaprabhu's mercy Rupa
understood His inner states: the Lord looked at Jagannatha and spoke the verse in the mood of Radha — and Rupa, the only
one besides Svarupa, grasped His intention and at once composed a verse after His heart, revealing its deeper meaning.
This is told in detail in the article on Svarupa Damodara. <!-- F170 F171 -->

The verse the Lord repeated:

“He who in youth stole My heart is again My Lord. Again there are the clear moonlit nights of the month of Chaitra, the
malati flowers smell just the same, and the same gentle breeze blows from the kadamba groves. Nothing has changed in
Our intimate relationship: I am the same beloved, yet I find no happiness here. My heart longs to return to the bank of
the Reva, to the shelter of the vetasi tree.” (CC, Antya 1.78)

And Rupa's verse:

“Dear friend, at last on the field of Kurukshetra I have met My long-dear sweet friend Krishna. I am the same Radhika,
and We are together again. How sweet is the magical moment of Our meeting! Yet I want to be again on the bank of the
Yamuna, beneath the shade of the groves of Vrindavana, and hear again His flute sounding the fifth note and driving Me
mad.” (CC, Antya 1.79)[^33] <!-- F172 F173 Q35 Q36 -->

Rupa wrote his verse on a palm leaf, tucked the leaf into the thatched roof of the hut and went to bathe in the sea.
Meanwhile the Lord came, happened to look up, saw the leaf, read the verse — and was plunged into prema, marvelling at
how clearly Rupa had grasped His innermost feelings. On returning, Rupa saw the Lord in prema and prostrated himself
before Him.[^34] The Lord slapped him: “How did you know the most secret thing in My heart?” — and embraced him tightly.
<!-- F174 F175 -->

Then the Lord, as if not knowing the answer, playfully showed the verse to Svarupa, asking him to check whether it had
any faults, and asked how Rupa had entered His thoughts.

“‘As I understand it,’ replied Svarupa Damodara, ‘You are especially favourable to him. No one is able to understand this
confidential meaning. I think You have fully endowed him with Your causeless mercy.’ Sri Chaitanya said, ‘I met Rupa
Gosvami in Prayaga. I could not help granting him My mercy, seeing that he was supremely worthy of it. I endowed him with
My spiritual power. Now you too must instruct him. In particular, enlighten him in the science of spiritual rasas.’
Svarupa said, ‘As soon as I saw his unique verse, I understood that You had given him Your special mercy. Seeing the
effect, one can understand its cause.’” (CC, Antya 1.86–91)

Rupa's poetry disclosed the Lord's most intimate mood — the mood of Radha longing for Vraja; it can be understood only by
His mercy, and by the effect the cause is known. <!-- F176 F177 I13 -->

## “Tunde Tandavini”
“So the Chaturmasya ended, and all the Vaishnavas of Bengal went home, but Rupa Gosvami remained in Jagannatha Puri in
the shelter of Mahaprabhu's lotus feet. One day, when he was writing his book, Sri Chaitanya suddenly came. Seeing the
Lord, Haridasa and Rupa sprang to their feet and fell before Him in dandavat. Sri Chaitanya embraced them both and sat
down. ‘What book are you writing?’ He asked. Taking one of the palm leaves, He was very pleased to see the beautiful
handwriting. ‘Rupa's handwriting is like strings of pearls,’ He praised him.” (CC, Antya 1.93–97)

Rupa was then writing the “Vidagdha-madhava.” On the leaf the Lord read a newly composed verse in glory of the Name —
and was plunged into prema:

“How much sweetness these two syllables ‘Krish-na’ contain! I cannot even imagine it. As soon as one utters Krishna's
name, it seems to dance on the tongue, and then one wishes to have thousands of mouths. When this name enters the ears,
one wishes to have millions of ears. And when the holy name of Krishna begins to dance in the courtyard of the heart, it
conquers the mind, and all the senses fall still.” (“Vidagdha-madhava,” 1.15; CC, Antya 1.99)[^35]
<!-- F178 F179 F180 F181 Q37 -->

Hearing it, the nama-acharya Haridasa Thakura in rapture began to glorify the meaning of the verse and broke into a
dance (CC, Antya 1.100): for the true greatness of the Name, says Kaviraja, is revealed only by great devotees who
disclose the meaning of the scriptures (1.101). According to one biography, Haridasa said that he had heard of the glory
of the Name from many scriptures and sadhus, but never such a description of its sweetness. <!-- F182 -->

## The Gathering at Rupa's
On another day, after the darshana of Jagannatha, the Lord came to Rupa at Siddha-bakula with all His associates —
Sarvabhauma, Ramananda Raya, Svarupa and others. On the way He praised to them Rupa's verses “priyah so ’yam” and “tunde
tandavini” and glorified his virtues in every way. This gathering is also described in the article on Ramananda Raya;
here we tell of it from Rupa's side. <!-- F183 -->

At Rupa's the Lord told him to read “priyah so ’yam.” Out of respect Rupa was abashed and kept silent, and Svarupa read
the verse; all were amazed, and Ramananda and Sarvabhauma said to the Lord that the power to disclose what is hidden in
His heart can be obtained only by His mercy. Svarupa explained that by Mahaprabhu's special mercy Rupa had understood
what neither Brahma nor the demigods comprehend. Then the Lord told him to read “tunde tandavini.” Rupa was shy of reading
his own verse, but did not dare disobey the repeated commands:

“When the devotees, and especially Ramananda Raya, heard this verse, they felt unprecedented bliss and amazement. All
agreed that although they had heard many descriptions of the glory of God's holy name, it had never been so sweet as with
Rupa Gosvami.” (CC, Antya 1.121–122) <!-- F184 F185 -->

Ramananda asked what book Rupa was writing, that it held such a treasury of unprecedented truths, and Svarupa told of the
two dramas: the “Vidagdha-madhava,” about the pastimes in Vraja, and the “Lalita-madhava,” about the pastimes in the
cities (pura-lila). At Ramananda's request Rupa read the nandi-shloka of the “Vidagdha-madhava”:

“May these pastimes of Krishna lessen all the sufferings of this world and dispel all unworthy desires. These pastimes
are as sweet as shikharini (a mixture of yoghurt and sugar candy). They bring to nothing the pride even of the nectar made
on Chandra-loka, for they spread so strong and enchanting a fragrance of the loving relationships of Srimati Radharani and
the other gopis.” (“Vidagdha-madhava,” 1.1; CC, Antya 1.128) <!-- F186 F187 Q38 -->

Then Ramananda asked him to read the verse of benediction describing Rupa's chosen Deity. Rupa was abashed and silent: the
subject of the verse was the Lord Himself, seated beside him. “In an assembly of Vaishnavas there is nothing to be shy of
in reading the very essence of one's book,” said Ramananda, and at the Lord's insistence Rupa read:

“May the Supreme Lord, who has come as the son of Srimati Shachidevi, abide for ever in the innermost region of your
heart. Shining with the colour of molten gold, He has by His causeless mercy descended in this age of Kali to give what no
Avatara offered before — the most exalted mood of loving service to God, the rasa of conjugal love.”
(“Vidagdha-madhava,” 1.2; CC, Antya 1.132) <!-- F188 F189 Q39 -->

Ramananda asked about the various parts of the “Vidagdha-madhava,” and Rupa answered every question with great humility,
quoting verses from each part. Admiring his transcendental poetry, Ramananda wished to hear the nandi and the obeisance
to the chosen Deity in the second drama, the “Lalita-madhava.” Rupa, humbly speaking of Ramananda's greatness and his own
insignificance, read the nandi and then the prayer for blessing to his ishtadeva, Sri Krishna Chaitanya:

“The moon-faced Supreme Lord has come as the son of mother Shachi to spread loving devotion to Himself. He is the emperor
of the whole community of brahmanas. He drives away the darkness of ignorance and governs the mental faculties of each and
every one. May this waxing moon, Sri Chaitanya, grant us all well-being and happiness.” (CC, Antya 1.177)
<!-- F190 F191 Q40 -->

In His heart the Lord rejoiced, but to teach people He outwardly showed anger:

“‘Your poetic descriptions are full of the sweetness of Krishna's rasas and are therefore like an ocean of nectar. But why
have you inserted unjustified praise of Me? It is like a spoonful of tar in a barrel of honey.’ ‘It is not tar,’ objected
Ramananda Raya. ‘It is a little camphor, with which he has only enhanced the taste of his exalted poetry.’ ‘Ramananda
Raya,’ answered Mahaprabhu, ‘you are glad to hear these verses, but I am ashamed, for people will laugh at them.’ ‘On the
contrary, they will be very happy to hear these verses,’ said Ramananda Raya, ‘for remembering one's beloved Deity at the
beginning of any undertaking attracts good fortune.’” (CC, Antya 1.179–182)

The Lord's anger was only an appearance meant to teach people, and Rupa, by remembering his Deity at the beginning of his
book, had done nothing contrary to the shastras and siddhanta.[^36] <!-- F192 F193 Q41 I14 -->

Ramananda asked about each part of the “Lalita-madhava” too, and Rupa answered, quoting the relevant verses. Having gone
through both dramas, Ramananda turned to Mahaprabhu's feet and began, as if with a thousand mouths, to extol Rupa's
poetry:

“‘This is not verse but an endless stream of nectar. It is the essence of all the highest truths presented in the form of
a play. Rupa Gosvami's wonderful poetry is the highest example of the description of loving relationships. These verses
can draw anyone into a whirlpool of spiritual bliss.’” (CC, Antya 1.193–194)

And he added: what use are a poet's poetry and an archer's arrow if, entering another's heart, they do not make the head
spin? Without the Lord's mercy an ordinary person could not write such verses: in them all the characteristics of drama
are observed and the essence of siddhanta is expressed, and the love of Radha and Krishna is depicted so that mind and ear
reel with delight. <!-- F194 F195 F196 Q42 Q43 -->

“‘I met Rupa Gosvami in Prayaga,’ replied Mahaprabhu. ‘He attracted Me by his extraordinary qualities, which gave Me great
joy. This poetry is endowed with such wonderful and sweet metaphors, and with many other literary ornaments. Without these
poetic virtues the preaching of the spiritual rasas is simply impossible. I ask you all to bless Rupa Gosvami, so that he
may ceaselessly describe these pastimes of Vrindavana, full of the divine rasas of love for Krishna. His elder brother,
Sanatana, is so wise and learned that there is no one who can compare with him. His detachment from everything worldly is
as strong as yours, Ramananda. In him humility, detachment and unsurpassed learning are united. I empower these two
brothers to go to Vrindavana and write literary works on bhakti.’” (CC, Antya 1.197–202)

In one biography's retelling the Lord says here: “Having invested shakti in the hearts of both brothers, I have sent
them to Vrindavana to propagate the scriptures of bhakti.”

“Then Sri Chaitanya embraced Rupa Gosvami and asked him to offer prayers at the lotus feet of all the devotees present.
Advaita Acharya, Nityananda Prabhu and all the other devotees poured their causeless mercy on Rupa Gosvami and embraced
him. All the devotees were immensely astonished at the special mercy Sri Chaitanya had shown Rupa Gosvami, and at the
unsurpassed virtues of Rupa himself.” (CC, Antya 1.206–208) <!-- F197 F198 F199 -->

Sri Gaurasundara is Himself the lord of Sarasvati; Ramananda, the author of the “Jagannatha-vallabha-nataka,” is
Vishakha-devi in the pastimes of Vraja; Svarupa Damodara, Gaurasundara's “second self” and an ocean of transcendental
rasa, is Lalita-devi. Both they and all the devotees who know rasa praised Rupa's poetry: it cannot be compared with the
verses of worldly, “village” poets. The “Vidagdha-madhava,” as one biography says, was heard and praised without
measure by Sri Chaitanya Himself — “the united form of Radha and Madhava” — by Ramananda, Svarupa and others close to
Gaura. Worldly men of letters, for their part, place Rupa on a level with Kalidasa, or below
or above him. But just as neither a piece of glass nor even the Koh-i-noor can be compared with the transcendental
Kaustubha gem, so no worldly poet celebrated by the crowd can be compared even with the radiance of the toenails of
Rupa's feet.[^37] <!-- F200 F201 -->

The “Srimad-Bhagavatam” (1.5.10) says: “Speech, however colourful its words, that does not sing the glory of Hari, which
purifies the world, is a place of pilgrimage for crows; the swans find no joy in it.” Poetry that does not serve with
undeviating devotion the one transcendental Madana, Krishna, is the delight of “crow-like” seekers of pleasure; but the
bhagavata-paramahamsas, like the royal swans of Lake Manasa, reject poetry without the taste of hari-katha, however full
of verbal brilliance. Worldly poets sometimes bow to Hari by custom at the beginning of a book, but their devotion is not
undivided: now they glorify Parvati and Shiva as the mother and father of the world, now they describe their love-play
and the birth of Kumara, as in the “Kumara-sambhava.” But the poetry of Rupa, the crown of transcendental poets, is ever
honoured by the undividedly devoted paramahamsas, for it serves Krishna's pleasure alone. <!-- F202 F203 Q44 I16 -->

Once in the same Nilachala, Svarupa Damodara, the Lord's “second self” and the best judge of the siddhanta of
bhakti-rasa, on hearing words contrary to siddhanta in the nandi-shloka of a village poet from Bengal, held up as an
example the opening verses of Rupa's two dramas:

“Poetry that describes the contradictory relationships of worldly people cannot give anyone joy. But the poetry of one
who is absorbed in love of God and skilled in describing pure loving feelings gives true happiness. The two beautiful
plays of Sri Rupa Gosvami are an example of this. Merely on hearing their introduction all the devotees feel the highest
happiness.” (CC, Antya 5.107–108) <!-- F204 Q45 -->

## The Command to Go to Vrindavana
“So all the devotees spent the four months of the rainy season in Sri Chaitanya's company. Then the Lord bade them
farewell, and they returned to Bengal. But Rupa Gosvami remained at Sri Chaitanya's lotus feet until the festival of
Dola-yatra, which he happily witnessed together with Mahaprabhu. After Dola-yatra Sri Chaitanya took leave of Rupa
Gosvami too. The Lord endowed him with His mercy and spiritual power and gave him this command: ‘Now go to Vrindavana and
stay there. And send your elder brother Sanatana here. Having come to Vrindavana, live there, write books on the treasure
of the spiritual rasas and restore the forgotten holy places. Serve Krishna and preach rasa-bhakti. I too shall soon come
to Vrindavana again.’ With these words Sri Chaitanya embraced Rupa Gosvami, and he then placed the Lord's lotus feet on
his head. Rupa Gosvami took leave of all Sri Chaitanya's devotees and returned to Vrindavana by way of Bengal.” (CC,
Antya 1.214–221) <!-- F205 F206 -->

This command is called fourfold: to write the scriptures of bhakti-rasa, to find the lost holy places, to establish the
service of Deities and temples, and to preach transcendental bhakti-rasa — and so to establish the Lord's innermost
desire. Rupa and Sanatana fulfilled it.[^38] Taking leave of the Lord, Rupa placed His feet on his head and went to
Vrindavana by way of Gauda.[^39] In Gauda he divided his movable and immovable property among his relatives, had the money
kept there brought to him and distributed it to relatives, brahmanas and temples; because of this he was delayed in Gauda
for nearly a year. <!-- F207 F208 F209 I17 -->

## Life in Vraja
Having settled in Vrindavana, the brothers carried out the Lord's command:

“Having met in Vrindavana, the two brothers began to live together, carrying out the will of Sri Chaitanya. They
collected many sacred scriptures and searched out all the forgotten holy places. At these places they built shrines and
began the worship of Krishna there.” (CC, Antya 4.217–218)

Of Rupa's books Kaviraja writes:

“Rupa Gosvami too has many works. The most important of his books is the ‘Bhakti-rasamrita-sindhu.’ In it he described the
essence of the bhakti-rasas, loving service to Krishna in various spiritual relationships with Him. He also wrote the book
‘Ujjvala-nilamani,’ by which we can understand the greatness and essence of the loving relationships between Sri Radha and
Sri Krishna. He also composed two plays: ‘Vidagdha-madhava’ and ‘Lalita-madhava.’ From them we can learn everything about
the rasas with which Krishna's pastimes are filled. Sri Rupa wrote many books, such as the ‘Dana-keli-kaumudi.’ In all he
composed more than a hundred thousand verses in Sanskrit. In all his books he described in detail the nature of
transcendental loving relationships with Krishna in Vrindavana.” (CC, Antya 4.223–226)

Of the books themselves we shall speak below, in the section “Works.” <!-- F210 F211 Q47 -->

Describing the branches of Gaurasundara's tree of prema, Krishnadasa Kaviraja speaks also of the branch of Rupa and
Sanatana:

“By the sweet will of the gardener, Sri Chaitanya, the branches of Sri Rupa and Sanatana grew so much that they covered
all the western lands. Spreading as far as the river Indus and the Himalayas, those branches embraced all of India,
including all the holy places of pilgrimage such as Vrindavana, Mathura and Haridvara. The fruits of divine love that grew
on these branches the devotees distributed generously everywhere. Having tasted the nectar of this love, all people became
intoxicated. Usually the people of the western provinces were unprincipled and unwise. But the preaching of Sri Rupa and
Sanatana Gosvami transformed them, giving them an understanding of how to live properly in devotion to Krishna. Acting in
full accord with the instructions of the scriptures, these two Gosvamis discovered the forgotten holy places and
established proper worship of the Deities in Vrindavana.” (CC, Adi 10.86–90) <!-- F212 Q48 -->

How the brothers lived in Vrindavana, performing bhajana all eight praharas of the day, was told in Puri by Vaishnava
eyewitnesses:

“‘They live in the forest without a home of their own. Every night they sleep under a different tree. Sometimes they come
to the houses of brahmanas and collect a little alms. Having given up all enjoyments, they eat only dry flatbread and
roasted chickpeas. Their only possessions are a water pot, a patchwork blanket and a worn cloth. They constantly chant the
names of Krishna, speak of Him and dance in jubilation. They worship Krishna all day and night and sleep only an hour and
a half. And sometimes for several days without sleep they perform nama-sankirtana in the bliss of divine love. They write
books devoted to bhakti-rasa. Sometimes they hear about Sri Chaitanya or become absorbed in thinking of Him.’ Hearing
such words, Mahaprabhu's personal associates felt great happiness and exclaimed: ‘Can one be surprised at the deeds of one
who has received Sri Chaitanya's mercy? For him nothing is impossible.’” (CC, Madhya 19.127–132)

The alms spoken of here is madhukari: a handful of food from door to door, as a bee gathers honey from flowers.
<!-- F213 Q49 Q50 -->

Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati explains (“Anubhashya” on CC, Madhya 19.131): the Gosvamis now chanted the Name, now
worshipped the Lord by writing books on bhakti-rasa, now remembered and glorified Mahaprabhu. The view of the
prakrita-sahajiyas that sadhana requires giving up the study and writing of the shastras for the sake of “ignorance” is
foreign to the Rupanugas; but whoever studies or writes for money, fame and honour grows weeds and will not obtain the true
fruit, and a genuine Rupanuga does not seek such petty gains. <!-- I19 -->

Of this same renunciation one biography speaks in reply to those who called the Gosvamis of Vrindavana self-seeking —
and it speaks of them all together: Rupa, Raghunatha and the other Gosvamis, desiring only Krishna's pleasure, gave up
ministerial posts under Hussain Shah, wealth like that of Indra, and wives as lovely as apsaras; they spent only one
night under each tree and chewed dry chickpeas. The crown of the great renunciants, they showed themselves to be great
lovers, intoxicated with divine madness, and even those who trust only their own experience unanimously praise their
renunciation; to say that they appropriated the money of worldly people and of the Deities is childish prattle.
<!-- F631 -->

Drawn by the bhajana of Rupa and Sanatana and by the Lord's earlier instructions, Raghunatha Bhatta, Gopala Bhatta and
Raghunatha Dasa Gosvami came to Vrindavana, and Jiva Gosvami at Nityananda's merciful bidding. Following Rupa and Sanatana,
they preached Gaurasundara's innermost desire; the brothers' example drew the other Gosvamis to Vrindavana, and all of them
are followers of Rupa. Soon after Lokanatha had settled in Vrindavana at the Lord's bidding, Rupa, Sanatana, Gopala Bhatta
and others began to come there to live, and their company gladdened Lokanatha; they were all of one mind and heart, and
Lokanatha was the eldest among the Gosvamis.[^40] Sanatana, Rupa and Raghunatha, according to one biography, performed
their bhajana in Vraja as one soul (abhinnatma) — just as Lokanatha and Bhugarbha did, and later Narottama and
Ramachandra. <!-- F214 F215 I18 F634 -->

Jiva recalls in the “Laghu-toshani” that Gopala Himself, in the form of a cowherd boy, appeared to the two brothers,
bringing them milk (kshira-aharana-lila), and that Raghunatha Dasa was their renowned friend, whom the best of the Aryas
regarded as their equal. Rupa and Sanatana were also the first to found the Sri Vishva-vaishnava-raja-sabha, “the assembly
of the king of the Vaishnavas of the whole world”; it was founded anew on 5 February 1919 in Calcutta. <!-- F217 F218 -->

## Sri Govinda-deva
In Vrindavana Rupa established the service of Govindaji, and Sanatana that of Madana-mohana: as Madana-mohana was the
treasure of Sanatana's life, so Govinda was the lord of Rupa's life. Krishnadasa Kaviraja, naming the three Deities of
Vrindavana — Madana-mohana, Govinda and Gopinatha — says:

“These three principal Deities have for ever captured the hearts of all the Gaudiya Vaishnavas. I worship Their beautiful
feet, for They are the Lords of my life.” (CC, Adi 1.19)[^41] <!-- F219 F220 Q50 -->

Among the obeisances to the three lords of Vrindavana at the beginning of the “Chaitanya-charitamrita,” Kaviraja bows to
Govinda as the lord of abhidheya:

“I pray for the mercy that will help me constantly remember the Divine Couple, Sri Sri Gandharva-Govindasundara, seated on
a throne adorned with shining jewels in the forest of Vraja, beneath a mind-enchanting wish-fulfilling tree and surrounded
by Their closest maidservants (sakhis and manjaris).” (CC, Adi 1.16)

Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati explains: Madana-mohana is the Deity of sambandha, and the acharya of sambandha is Sanatana;
Govinda is the Deity of abhidheya, who accepts service, and the acharya of abhidheya is Rupa; Gopinatha is the Deity of
prayojana, and the acharya of prayojana is Raghunatha Dasa. Rupa shows how to serve Radha and Krishna, and therefore Rupa's
feet are the highest goal even for the acharya of prayojana, Raghunatha Dasa. <!-- F221 Q57 I22 -->

How Rupa revealed the service of Govinda is told in the “Bhakti-ratnakara” (2nd taranga, 422–453), following the verses of
the “Sadhana-dipika” of Radhakrishna Gosvami, a disciple of Haridasa Pandita Gosvami, who lived in Vraja-mandala.[^42]
Krishnadasa Kaviraja calls Haridasa Pandita the superintendent of Govinda's service (CC, Adi 8.54). Pandita Banamali Lala
Gosvami of the Radha-ramana-ghera in Vrindavana spoke in 1940 of a “Sadhana-dipika” by Narayana Bhatta, but the
“Sadhana-dipika” quoted in the “Bhakti-ratnakara” is the work of Radhakrishna Gosvami. This book (the verses from it
given below are quoted by the “Bhakti-ratnakara” elsewhere, in the 6th taranga) beautifully glorifies
following Rupa: “With those who stand outside the siddhanta of Rupa, the ocean of mercy, travellers on the path of raga
should not converse about prema-rasa. I bow to the feet of Rupa, by whose mercy even an ignorant person grasps his
teaching.” And again: “Tongue, always utter the name ‘Rupa’! Mind, remember Rupa, the embodiment of compassion! Head, bow
before Rupa of the merciful glance — and before Raghunatha Dasa, his second body, who has no equal.”
<!-- F222 F223 F224 F225 F226 Q51 Q52 -->

According to the “Sadhana-dipika,” having come to Vrindavana at the Lord's bidding, Rupa found no Deity there and grieved
deeply. He searched for his beloved Lord through the forests of Vraja, through the villages, in every house of the
Vrajavasis, and, not finding Him, one day sat under a tree on the bank of the Yamuna and wept, burning in separation from
Krishna. Then a handsome Vrajavasi came up to him and in a sweet voice asked why he was sad. Rupa told him of the Lord's
instructions. The man led him to the hill of Goma-tila and said, “Every morning a cow (kamadhenu) comes here and pours out
her milk of her own accord; understand what this means and do what is needed,” and vanished.[^43] At his words and his
form Rupa fainted in prema, and on coming to himself understood: “Krishna came and spoke with me, and I did not recognise
Him.” He understood the secret and announced to the Vrajavasis that here was the Yoga-pitha and here Govinda-deva lay
hidden. All the Vrajavasis — children, old men, youths — with hearts melted by love began to dig and, by the mercy of
Baladeva, beheld within the Yoga-pitha the face of Govinda, who enchants millions of Kamadevas.[^44] It is said that this
Govinda was originally worshipped by Krishna's great-grandson Vajranabha.[^45] <!-- F227 F228 F229 F230 F231 -->

Rupa informed Gaurasundara in Nilachala by letter, and the Lord, greatly pleased, told “the strong” Kashishvara to go to
Vrindavana to help Rupa and Sanatana; Kashishvara did not want to leave the Lord. Then, understanding that Kashishvara's heart was pained by separation from
Him, the Lord brought the Deity of
Krishna that stood beside Jagannatha and said, “Know that He is non-different from Me.” The Deity and Mahaprabhu took food
together, and Kashishvara, bowing down, carried the Deity of Sri Sri Gaura-Govinda to Vrindavana: this is Mahaprabhu
standing beside Govinda. The “Sadhana-dipika” prays: “May Gaura-Govinda be merciful to me, whose feet by their radiance
surpass Madana, whose face puts to shame the lotus and the jewel, at whose feet Rupa took shelter.”[^46]
<!-- F232 F233 F234 F235 Q53 -->

The “Bhakti-ratnakara” (6th taranga, 92–110, also following the verses of the “Sadhana-dipika”) tells of the Deity of Radha
too. A South Indian brahmana, Brihadbhanu, a great Vaishnava, settled in the village of Radhanagara in Utkala; the Deity of
Srimati Radharani came from Vrindavana to his house, and he served Her as a daughter, in vatsalya-rasa. After Brihadbhanu's
passing, the king of Utkala, Prataparudra, hearing of this, himself came to Radhanagara for darshana, and at night in a
dream Radhika told him to take Her to the temple of Jagannatha; there She was installed with great reverence at a place
called Chakraveda (Chakraberha), and ordinary people honoured Her as Lakshmi. Then She gave a command in a dream to the
prince Purushottama Jana, and the Deity with many people set out from Sri Kshetra for Vrindavana and was installed to the
left of Govinda-deva (“Bhakti-ratnakara,” 6.107); all in Gauda, Utkala and other lands came to know of it.
<!-- F236 F237 F238 Q54 -->

The huge temple of Govinda-deva, with its seemingly broken summits, draws every eye; it stands on the high hill of
Goma-tila, west of the broad road from Mathura to Vrindavana. Its construction is told of in various ways; it is said to
have been built of red Jaipur sandstone by the famous Man Singh, king of Amber, who in 1590 renewed an earlier structure
and added to it. Growse, the authority on Mathura, in “Mathura: A District Memoir,” called the temple of Gobind Deva not
only the finest of its series but the most impressive religious edifice of Hindu art, at least in Northern India. The
enclosure around the temple is called the “Govindera ghera.” On either side of the jagamohana stands a small temple; the
interior of the southern one is called the Yoga-pitha — it is said that Govinda was found there. Going down the steps, one
comes to a narrow dark place where the pujaris show by lamplight the Deity of Yogamaya; there too is a footprint of
Krishna. On the north wall of this small temple there is an inscription in Devanagari: in the thirty-fourth year of
Akbar's reign the maharajadhiraja Man Singh, son of Maharaja Bhagavan Dasa, of the line of Prithviraja, built the temple on
the Yoga-pitha of Vrindavana for Govinda-deva; the work was supervised by Kalyana Dasa, the sculptor was Manikchand Chopang,
the mason Govinda Dasa (Dilvali); at the end is what appears to be a signature — Ganesha Dasa Bimabala. From the date of the
inscription it is clear that the temple was built many years after Govinda-deva's appearance.
<!-- F239 F240 F243 F244 F245 F246 -->

Govindaji, revealed on Goma-tila, was at first served in a simple hut. Krishnadasa Kaviraja writes of Raghunatha Bhatta,
whose life was in Govinda alone:

“Later he instructed his disciples to erect a temple for Lord Govinda. He personally made many different ornaments for the
Deity of Govinda, such as a flute and earrings in the shape of the mythical sea creature.” (CC, Antya 13.131)

The CC names neither the disciple of Raghunatha Bhatta to whom he entrusted this nor the builder of the temple. <!-- F247 F248 Q55 -->

Aurangzeb, it is said, destroyed the main temple and its five upper towers. Tradition relates that the badshah from his
distant palace in Agra saw every day a bright light on the summit of Govinda's temple and out of envy could not bear that
the temple stood higher than his pleasure palace; he sent troops to destroy the temples of Mathura and Vrindavana, but the
priests, learning of it through spies, at once carried their Deities away by forest paths. So Govinda-deva came to Jaipur,
where He is served to this day.[^47] But just as Ravana, having stolen only Sita's shadow, thought he had stolen Sita
herself, the Lord's svarupa-shakti, so it was here: enemies touch only the outward. The almighty Lord plays at being
helpless, the protector of Brahma and Shiva as if in need of protection; this wonderful pastime attracts the service of the
devotees and bewilders those who have turned away from Him. <!-- F240 F241 F242 I20 -->

According to the “Sadhana-dipika” (in the “Bhakti-ratnakara,” 2.449–453), at the Lord's bidding the merciful Rupa also
revealed Vrinda-devi, on the bank of the Brahma-kunda. It is said that She resided in a small temple north of the
Govinda-ghera and has now been moved to Kamyavana. <!-- F249 -->

In the “Bhakti-rasamrita-sindhu” Rupa describes the “ruin” that the darshana of Govinda brings:

“Friend, if you value the company of your kinsfolk, do not look at Govinda standing at Keshi-ghata — smiling, bent in three
places, with sidelong glance, with the flute at His lips tender as young shoots, with a peacock feather in His hair.”
(“Bhakti-rasamrita-sindhu,” 1.2.239)[^48]

Its meaning is that the darshana of Govinda takes away the taste for everything else. <!-- F250 Q56 I21 -->

Baladeva Vidyabhushana, at the beginning of his commentary on Rupa's “Govinda-virudavali,” tells how it came to be. A
certain poet from the South recited a laudatory “deva-virudavali,” and Govinda, pleased with the elegance of its words and
meaning, gave him the garland from His neck. Rupa wondered: how could Govinda, the Lord of all, be pleased on hearing a
“deva-virudavali”? And one day, while Rupa slept, Govinda said to him, “Compose My virudavali with such-and-such
characteristics.” <!-- F251 F252 -->

In Krishnadasa Kaviraja's time Govinda was served by His beloved servants. The “Chaitanya-charitamrita” says:

“In the most intimate temple of Vrindavana, beneath wish-fulfilling trees, there is a golden throne adorned with jewels. On
that golden throne the son of Nanda Maharaja, Sri Govinda, the God of Love Himself, is ever seated. In that temple Sri
Govinda is worshipped, served by the highest standard. His garments, ornaments and paraphernalia are divine and wholly
spiritual. Thousands of servants serve Sri Govinda unceasingly. And even tens of thousands of mouths would not suffice
merely to describe this worship. At that time the superintendent of this temple was Sri Haridasa Pandita. His virtues and
fame were known to the whole world.” (CC, Adi 8.50–54)

“Kashishvara Gosvami had a disciple named Govinda Gosvami, who was a dear servant of the Deity of Govinda in Vrindavana.”
(CC, Adi 8.66)

“And the disciple of Gadadhara Pandita named Bhugarbha Gosvami also spoke only of the deeds of Sri Chaitanya. Other topics
did not interest him at all. The disciples of Bhugarbha Gosvami were Chaitanya Dasa (the servant of the Deity of Govinda),
Mukundananda Chakravarti and Krishnadasa, full of divine love.” (CC, Adi 8.68–69)

Haridasa Pandita was a disciple of Ananta Acharya, a disciple of Gadadhara Pandita; Govinda Gosani, a disciple of “the
strong” Kashishvara, was a great servant of Sri Govinda. <!-- F253 F254 Q58 -->

## Gopala in Mathura
Rupa revered the hill of Govardhana as Krishna Himself and therefore, following Mahaprabhu's command, together with
Sanatana never climbed it. Just as Gopala once on some pretext gave Mahaprabhu darshana at the foot of the hill, so He
showed mercy to Rupa and Sanatana as well. <!-- F255 -->

In his old age Rupa enacted the pastime of no longer being able to reach Govardhana, and longed to see Gopala, the Deity
of Madhavendra Puri. And when Rupa's heart was deeply longing for His beauty, Gopala, wishing to give “His own” Rupa darshana — in fact, to
show him mercy — on the pretext of fear of
iconoclast mlechchhas came Himself from Govardhana to Mathura, to the house of Vitthalanatha (Vitthaleshvara), the younger
son of Vallabhacharya, and accepted service there for a month. So Rupa with his companions gazed his fill on the beauty
of Gopala-deva (CC, Madhya 18):[^new-gopala]

“Sri Rupa with his companions also spent a whole month in Mathura, beholding this Deity. He was accompanied by Gopala
Bhatta Gosvami, Raghunatha Dasa Gosvami, Raghunatha Bhatta Gosvami and Lokanatha Dasa Gosvami, Bhugarbha Gosvami, Sri Jiva
Gosvami, Sri Yadava Acharya and Govinda Gosvami, Sri Uddhava Dasa, Madhava, Sri Gopala Dasa and Narayana Dasa. Also the
great devotee Govinda, Vani Krishnadasa, Pundarikaksha, Ishana and Laghu Haridasa. With great joy Sri Rupa came for the
darshana of Sri Gopala accompanied by all these eminent devotees.” (CC, Madhya 18.48–53)

Ishana, a Vaishnava of Vrindavana, was also among those accompanying Rupa according to the “Bhakti-ratnakara.” The place
in Mathura, in the Satghara quarter, where Rupa came for the darshana of Gopala was found in the mid-1930s by Srila
Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati (on 29 October, in the month of Kartika). <!-- F256 F257 F258 Q59 -->

One biography relates that the brothers continued to care for Gopala's service later as well. When His pujaris, driven
out and beaten by Krishnadasa's men (see the article on Madhavendra Puri), came to Rupa and Sanatana in Vrindavana — the
brothers were then living with hearts deeply wounded by separation from Gaura — and afterwards lodged a complaint in the
royal court of Mathura, whose officials had been bribed beforehand, the brothers, hearing that the service of Gopala,
the treasure of Madhavendra Puri's life, would pass into the hands of the royal officials, handed over the service and
the temple of Gopala to Vitthalanatha, the son of Vallabhacharya, and duly arranged for Gaudiya pujaris. Vitthalanatha
was especially favoured by Rupa and the Rupanugas: because he accepted the authority of Sri Krishna Chaitanya and His
associates, the Gosvamis headed by Rupa and Raghunatha approved his service to Gopala. According to the same biography,
Vitthalanatha in Mathura together with Rupa honoured Gopala Bhatta Gosvami (the words also allow the reading “honoured
both Rupa and Gopala Bhatta”), and Rupa, wandering through Vraja-mandala, came to Gathuli and greatly rejoiced to see
Vitthala's service to the Deity of Krishna Chaitanya.[^new-gathuli] <!-- F627 F628 F629 F630 -->

## The Sweet Rice and Radha's Braid
The “Bhakti-ratnakara” (5.1311–1330) relates that once Rupa wished to cook sweet rice for Sanatana and invite him to eat,
but there was nothing needed in his hut. And Srimati Radharani, who fulfils the innermost desires of the devotees, in the
form of a young cowherd girl brought milk, rice, sugar and ghee.[^49] Rupa opened the door and saw a beautiful cowherd girl
with gifts. “Child, why have you come? Why did you trouble yourself?” “What trouble, baba? I have only come to render a
little service to a saint.” Rupa accepted the gifts and invited her to sit down, but she said she had work to do and
vanished, leaving Rupa perplexed. <!-- F259 F260 -->

He cooked the sweet rice, offered it to his Deity, Govinda-deva, and gave the prasada to Sanatana when he came. Sanatana
felt an extraordinary bliss and asked where the milk and rice had come from. “A cowherd girl came,” said Rupa. “I was just
thinking how to cook sweet rice for Sanatana, and she appeared as if by magic.” Sanatana understood that it had been Sri
Radha Thakurani Herself and with tears of love said, “By accepting service from Her we are ruined — we shall not reach our
cherished goal”; for Radha is their Mistress and they Her servants, and the relationship of service had been reversed. Again
and again he reproached himself for having accepted service from Her whom he longed to serve, and strictly forbade Rupa to
accept such gifts in future.[^50] <!-- F261 F262 -->

According to the “Bhakti-ratnakara,” Sanatana once came to Radha-kunda to Rupa and Raghunatha Dasa; they rose, showed him
honour, seated him, and all three held an ishtagoshthi, a discussion about Krishna. Rupa had then written a hymn to
Radharani, the “Chatu-pushpanjali” (“Offering of Four Flowers”), and one verse had lodged in Sanatana's mind: “You, O
Gaurangi, of the colour of fresh gorochana, clad in garments the colour of the blue lotus, and Your long braid, glittering
with clusters of jewels, is like a black serpent.” Sanatana had his doubts: is it fitting to compare Radharani's braid to a
snake? That same day, bathing in Radha-kunda, he saw cowherd boys and girls playing at a distance; behind the head of one
girl something black was swaying, and it seemed to him a poisonous snake. “Child, beware, there is a snake on your back!”
he cried, but she did not hear. Running up, he saw that it was Sri Radha Herself, and the boys and girls laughed for a long
time. Sanatana was struck dumb — and understood the rightness of Rupa's comparison. <!-- F263 F264 F265 Q60 -->

## The Elder Brother
Even when speaking of Sanatana, the Gaudiya acharyas call him again and again “Rupa's elder brother.” So write Kavi
Karnapura (“Chaitanya-chandrodaya-nataka,” 9.34)[^new-cc934], Raghunatha Dasa Gosvami in the “Stavavali” (“Sva-niyama-dashaka,” 1;
“Manah-shiksha,” 3) and in the “Mukta-charita” (4), Jiva in the closing verse of the “Laghu-toshani,” and Krishnadasa
Kaviraja, in the verse of obeisance to the shastra-gurus at the beginning of chapters of the Antya-lila, bows “before my
shastra-gurus: Srila Rupa Gosvami and his elder brother Srila Sanatana Gosvami” (CC, Antya 2.1; likewise Antya 3.1).
<!-- F266 -->

Sanatana himself, in the opening verse of his commentary “Dig-darshini” on the “Brihad-bhagavatamrita,” bows to Sri
Chaitanya, “having taken shelter of whose Rupa” this low man obtained bhakti: the word “rupa” here means both the Lord's
“form” and Rupa himself. Rupa always revered Sanatana, the acharya of sambandha, as his spiritual teacher — “Sanatana is the
guru of Srila Rupa Gosvami,” says one biography; and Sanatana, as already mentioned, in his commentary on the
“Brihad-bhagavatamrita” calls Rupa his beloved servant. The dedication of Sanatana's “Vaishnava-toshani” (“shrimach-chaitanya-rupasya prityai…”) can be understood in two ways: “for the pleasure of Sri Krishna Chaitanya” or “for the pleasure
of Srila Rupa Gosvami,” at whose bidding this commentary was written. Sanatana's literary contribution to the Gaudiya
sampradaya is comparable only with that of Rupa and Raghunatha Dasa. <!-- F267 F268 F269 F270 F271 Q65 Q76 -->

## Raghunatha Dasa
Raghunatha Dasa lived for sixteen years in Puri with Mahaprabhu, in the care of Svarupa Damodara. When both had departed,
he came in grief to Vrindavana to see the feet of Rupa and Sanatana one last time and then throw himself from
Govardhana. The brothers spoke with him at length and held him back: his body was dedicated to Mahaprabhu's service and
would yet fulfil many of His desires. They accepted him as their “third brother,” kept him always with them, and were
themselves enriched by hearing from him the nectarean accounts of Mahaprabhu's pastimes. So being accepted by Rupa and
Sanatana saved Raghunatha from perishing in separation. Rupa's two best servants were Raghunatha Dasa Gosvami and Jiva
Gosvami. <!-- F272 F274 I23 -->

The songs about Raghunatha Dasa say that he is “non-different from Rupa”; and when Rupa and Sanatana disappeared, “his
eyes went blind,” and, not seeing Rupa and his companions, he wept; after Sanatana's departure he drank only water, and after Rupa's
departure he gave up even water. Raghunatha himself says at the end of the
“Mukta-charita” that he wove this bouquet at Jiva's bidding, following the full instruction of Sri Rupa. There is also a
tradition: reading Rupa's “Lalita-madhava,” a drama of separation, Raghunatha Dasa sank into such an ocean of separation
that people began to fear for his life. Seeing this, Rupa wrote the short, merry “Dana-keli-kaumudi,” full of jests, gave
it to Raghunatha and took back the “Lalita-madhava”; having read the new book, Raghunatha forgot his separation.
<!-- F273 F275 F297 Q78 F635 -->

## Raghunatha Bhatta
Mahaprabhu told Raghunatha Bhatta, the son of Tapana Mishra, not to marry, but after his parents' death to go to Vrindavana
and, under the shelter of Rupa and Sanatana, perform bhajana all eight praharas of the day, following in Rupa's footsteps
and studying the “Bhagavatam” and other scriptures under their guidance; and so he did. When he came to Vrindavana, Rupa and
Sanatana were overjoyed and embraced him lovingly. He gave himself into their care and every day in a sweet voice read
the “Bhagavatam” to them — in Rupa's assembly, “the assembly of the king of the Vaishnavas of the whole world”
(shri-shri-vishva-vaishnava-raja-sabha) — and, overwhelmed by prema (tears, trembling, a faltering voice), sometimes had to
break off the reading:

“Later, at Sri Chaitanya's bidding, he went to Vrindavana and stayed there to live, taking shelter of Sri Rupa Gosvami.
Raghunatha Bhatta read the ‘Srimad-Bhagavatam’ aloud for Sri Rupa Gosvami. And so, by the mercy of Sri Chaitanya, he became
intoxicated with divine love for Sri Krishna.” (CC, Adi 10.157–158) <!-- F276 F277 Q81 -->

## Gopala Bhatta
Gopala Bhatta, at his parents' behest, came straight to Vrindavana and settled with Rupa and Sanatana (“Karnananda,” 5th
niryasa; “Bhakti-ratnakara,” 1.163–165). The brothers already knew that he would come and received him as a brother; from
then on a lifelong friendship bound them. Rupa was just about to send word to Mahaprabhu in Puri with some devotees, and the
brothers informed the Lord of Gopala Bhatta's arrival. The Lord, delighted, replied: “Regard Bhatta Gopala as your younger
brother, look after him with love and from time to time send Me good news”; and with His answer He sent Gopala Bhatta the
signs of renunciation — a dor, a kaupina and an upper garment.[^51] <!-- F278 -->

Gopala Bhatta was very close to Rupa and Sanatana; Rupa held him as dear as his own life and entrusted him with the service
of Radha-ramana. Hearing that Krishna had mercifully appeared to Gopala Bhatta as Radha-ramana from a shalagrama, Rupa and
Sanatana with other devotees at once came for darshana and with tears of love beheld this world-enchanting Deity; Gopala Bhatta invited Rupa, Sanatana and the other Gosvamis
to a great festival of the Deity's abhisheka — on the Vaishakha full moon of 1599 Samvat (1542 CE).[^52]
Together with Rupa and others Gopala Bhatta restored the lost holy places of Vrindavana. And the “Hari-bhakti-vilasa,” as
the book itself says (1.2), was compiled by Gopala Bhatta, a disciple of Prabodhananda, to please Raghunatha Dasa, Rupa
and Sanatana. <!-- F279 F280 F281 F282 F283 Q73 -->

## Jiva
Jiva, having studied the scriptures in Kashi under Madhusudana Vachaspati, came to Vrindavana, took full shelter of Rupa
and Sanatana and studied under them the whole “Bhagavata” and the scriptures on bhakti; both gurus entrusted him with
revising their works. <!-- F284 -->

How Rupa supposedly sent Jiva away for a debate with a scholar and forgave him only at Sanatana's intercession is told in
different ways; Srila Bhaktisiddhanta and one of the biographies regard such stories as a fabrication of the sahajiyas
(see the article on Jiva Gosvami).[^jiva-br] <!-- F285 F286 F287 -->

The incident itself, according to Srila Bhaktisiddhanta, was this. A certain digvijayi, thirsting for worldly fame, obtained from Rupa and Sanatana a written
acknowledgement of victory (jaya-patra): Jiva's gurus yielded to him without debate. The proud man began to revile them as
ignoramuses and demanded the same from their disciple; Jiva engaged him in debate, defeated him and silenced the reviler
of his gurus (Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati, “Anubhashya” on CC, Adi 10.85). Thus Jiva showed what a true disciple should
be. Ignorant sahajiyas say that Rupa reproached Jiva for this and banished him for a time, and only Sanatana's intercession
restored Jiva to Rupa's company; Srila Bhaktisiddhanta rejects this interpretation: Jiva acted rightly in defending the
honour of his gurus. Only by Krishna's mercy, seeing themselves as servants of the guru and the Vaishnavas, will such
interpreters understand what it means to be “humbler than a blade of grass” and to “honour others.”
<!-- F288 F289 I27 I29 -->

After the departure of Rupa and Sanatana, writes Srila Bhaktisiddhanta, Jiva became the chief teacher of siddhanta, the
undisputed head of the Gaudiya sampradaya in Mathura, Bengal and Orissa, and the “prime minister” of the
Vishva-vaishnava-raja-sabha in Vrindavana; he is the best of Rupa's followers and one of the shiksha-gurus of Krishnadasa
Kaviraja. And if Jiva, for the good of certain disciples, allowed svakiya-vada in his teaching, he acted as befits an
acharya and was no opponent of parakiya-vada. <!-- F290 F291 I28 -->

In the opening verses of his works Jiva remembers Rupa. In the “Madhava-mahotsava” — “my Prabhu Sri Rupa Gosvami, at whose
bidding I undertook this work; his mercy is my only support”; in the “Gopala-champu” — “my uncles Rupa and Sanatana, my two
Prabhus, who instructed me — may they be my shelter”; in the commentary on the “Brahma-samhita” — “Sri Rupa Prabhu is my only
shelter”; in the “Durgama-sangamani” his commentary is a boat for crossing the “Bhakti-rasamrita-sindhu,” which came forth
from Rupa's lips. And in several works a verse is repeated: “He whose elder brother is the venerable Sanatana, like the
Eternal, and whose younger brother is Sri Vallabha — that Sri Rupa is the eternal shelter of Jiva.” <!-- F292 Q6 -->

In the “Tattva-sandarbha” and at the beginning of each of the six “Sandarbhas” Jiva writes: “Glory in Mathura-mandala to
Srila Rupa and Sanatana, who inspire me to write this book revealing the truth”; the colophons state that the books were
written “by the instruction of Rupa and Sanatana.” As the basis of the “Sandarbhas” Jiva names the notes of Gopala Bhatta —
a devotee from the South, of a brahmana family, a very close friend of Rupa and Sanatana (according to Baladeva, “the friend
of them both”): he had written the book but not put it in order, and Jiva put it in order to please the two saints, Srila
Rupa and Sanatana; its content, moreover, is what Sanatana heard from the Lord in Kashi and Rupa in Prayaga.
<!-- F293 Q71 Q77 -->

In the epic poem “Madhava-mahotsava” Jiva, at the end of each of its nine ullasas, honours Rupa as the guru's feet, a form
non-different from Krishna. The verse at the end of the first ullasa can be read in two ways: “The Lord, full of compassion,
rescued me, long burning in the forest fire of samsara, accepted me as His own and placed me at His pure feet — Him, the
great Krishnadeva (or: Rupa, whose chosen Lord is Krishna), I worship here in Vrindavana.” The other eight such verses with
a translation were printed in the article “Sri Sri Rupanuga-dhara” in the journal “Gaudiya” (20th year, no. 2). In the
“Krishna-sandarbha” too Jiva calls Rupa his only shelter: in anuchchheda 178, “my revered feet by which I live”
(upajibya-charana) concluded thus the account of the manifest pastimes of Radha and Govinda in the “Lalita-madhava”; and in
anuchchheda 189, the eternal pastimes of Radha and Krishna are as described by the “younger brother” of those feet,
Sanatana's — that is, by Rupa. <!-- F294 F295 F296 Q68 Q69 -->

## Lokanatha, Narottama and Srinivasa
When Mahaprabhu and His intimates headed by Rupa and Sanatana disappeared, Lokanatha could not bear the separation. Coming
again to Vrindavana, he learned that Rupa and Sanatana had already departed, and grieved bitterly; it was then that
Narottama came to him.[^53] Narottama Dasa Thakura prays in the “Prarthana”: “When will my master Lokanatha take me with him
and give me to Rupa's feet?” <!-- F298 F299 F300 Q67 -->

Narottama Thakura, the servant of Rupa, Raghunatha and Jiva, sang Rupa's greatness “with a million mouths” and chose him as
his cherished krishna-prema. He is the first of the later followers of Rupa, the intimate associate of Mahaprabhu; in the
eternal pastimes he is Champaka-manjari, who ever assists Rupa-manjari in the service of Radha and Krishna. In his four songs
about Rupa-manjari (one of them, “shri rupa-manjari pada…” from the “Prarthana,” was much loved by Srila Bhaktisiddhanta
Sarasvati) the feet of Rupa-manjari are Narottama's whole wealth, his bhajana, life, ornament, desired perfection, dharma,
vow and japa; he longs for her darshana; “by Rupa's mercy one attains the feet of the Couple,” he has heard from the sadhus;
he prays to Sanatana and the whole family of Gaura to fulfil his desire, to Lokanatha to give him to Rupa, and to the
narma-sakhis to instruct him. In the third and fourth songs Narottama sees himself as a new maidservant (nava-dasi): Rupa
tells her to prepare everything for the service, and she carries to the Couple on a jewelled tray the paraphernalia and a
golden pitcher of fragrant water, standing timidly behind Rupa. The Couple, smiling, ask, “Rupa, where did you find this new
maidservant?” — and Rupa-manjari answers that Manjunali brought her and gave her, and sets Narottama to service.
<!-- F301 F302 F303 F304 F305 Q70 -->

On the way to Vraja the weary Narottama dreamed of Rupa and Sanatana: they placed their hands on his chest and fed him milk
brought by Mahaprabhu Himself, and his weariness vanished. Reaching Mathura and seeing the Yamuna, Narottama remembered the
names of the six Gosvamis headed by Rupa and Sanatana and wept; and in one of his songs he laments: “Where are my Svarupa
and Rupa, where are Sanatana, Raghunatha Dasa?..” In another song he sings: “Glory to Sanatana and Rupa — bottomless wells of
prema-bhakti-rasa, embodiments of ujjvala-rasa, wish-fulfilling trees! Their books clearly set forth prema-bhakti; they are
the shelter of madhura-rasa. Rupa and Sanatana, pierce my heart with the precious arrows of love for Radha and Krishna!”
<!-- F306 F307 F308 F309 Q72 Q74 -->

Mahaprabhu foretold the birth of Srinivasa: “Through Rupa, Sanatana and others I shall reveal the scriptures of bhakti, and
through Srinivasa they will be distributed” (“Bhakti-ratnakara,” 2.120–123). When Srinivasa came to Vraja, Rupa, Sanatana,
Kashishvara and Raghunatha Bhatta had already disappeared, while Raghunatha Dasa, Gopala Bhatta and Jiva were still on earth.
According to the “Nava-padya” of Nrisimha Kaviraja, Rupa and the others headed by Sanatana said to Srinivasa in a dream:
“This is no time to grieve: Gopala Bhatta is here; receive the mantra from him and our books, go to Gauda and preach.”[^54]
Jiva entrusted Srinivasa, Narottama and Shyamananda with taking to Gauda the books of Rupa and Sanatana, and also of
Raghunatha Dasa, Gopala Bhatta and others. And when the robber-king of Vana-Vishnupura, Vira Hambira, opened the stolen chest
of the Gosvamis' books, he saw on a palm leaf the name “Sri Rupa Gosvami” — and at the mere sight of Rupa's flawlessly
beautiful handwriting the sins of his whole long life flew away, his heart was purified, and krishna-prema awoke in it.

## The Rupanugas
Raghunatha Dasa Gosvami in the “Mukta-charita,” proclaiming the incomparable glory of the dust of Rupa's feet, prays:
“Holding a straw between my teeth, again and again I beg: may I be, birth after birth, the dust of Sri Rupa's lotus feet.” In
the “Sva-niyama-dashaka” and the “Manah-shiksha” he proclaims that to be initiated as a follower of Rupa (a Rupanuga) is the
highest, the only goal of the soul's life, and in the “Manah-shiksha” (3) he instructs the whole Rupanuga-sampradaya that
wishes to live in Vraja: “If you wish, birth after birth, to live with love in the land of Vraja and serve the youthful
Couple, then, O mind, always remember and honour with love Svarupa, Rupa with his companions and his elder brother
Sanatana.” <!-- F319 F320 F321 Q64 Q66 I25 -->

Of the incomparable greatness of following Rupa Raghunatha Dasa speaks many times in other places of the “Stavavali” too: in
the “Vraja-vilasa-stava” (38), “Vilapa-kusumanjali” (1, 14, 72), “Sva-niyama-dashaka” (1, 10),
“Radha-krishna-ujjvala-kusuma-keli” (44), “Prarthanamrita” (opening verse and 20), “Madana-gopala-stotra” (21),
“Vishakhananda-stotra” (134), “Prarthanashraya-chaturdashaka” (4, 10, 11, 14) and “Abhishta-suchana” (1, 2, 13); the
“Dana-keli-chintamani,” he says, he wrote by the power of the dust of Sri Rupa's lovely lotus feet. In the opening verse of
the “Mukta-charita” he says that by the mercy of his guru Yadunandana Acharya he obtained Svarupa, Rupa and his elder brother
Sanatana. Thus the highest acharyas — Raghunatha Dasa, Jiva, Narottama — define themselves by following Rupa.
<!-- F322 F323 Q79 I26 -->

Krishnadasa Kaviraja begins each chapter of the “Chaitanya-charitamrita” with obeisances to Mahaprabhu and His associates and
ends it with a prayer for service at the feet of Rupa and Raghunatha:

“Praying for mercy at the feet of Sri Rupa and Sri Raghunatha, I, Krishnadasa, narrate this ‘Sri Chaitanya-charitamrita.’”
(CC, Adi 1.110)

Kaviraja is honoured as the best of Rupa's followers. Vishvanatha Chakravarti followed Rupa's example, going to live in
Vrindavana for the sake of bhajana, and with detailed commentaries explained Rupa's confidential works on rasa, difficult
for ordinary people: his “Ananda-chandrika” is a commentary on the “Ujjvala-nilamani,” and he also commented on the
“Vidagdha-madhava.” The Vaishnavas speak of three of his works — summaries of Rupa's books, the “Ujjvala-nilamani-kirana,”
the “Bhakti-rasamrita-sindhu-bindu” and the “Bhagavatamrita-kana”: “kirana, bindu, kana — with these three you will become a
Vaishnava.” Govinda Kaviraja composed songs on shanta, dasya, sakhya, vatsalya and madhurya following Rupa's
“Bhakti-rasamrita-sindhu” and “Ujjvala-nilamani.” <!-- F324 F325 F326 F327 F328 -->

The shiksha-guru-parampara from Mahaprabhu to Vishvanatha, according to Srila Bhaktisiddhanta, is this: Mahaprabhu — Svarupa
Damodara — Rupa and Sanatana (with Raghunatha Dasa, Raghunatha Bhatta and Gopala Bhatta) — Jiva — Krishnadasa Kaviraja — and so
on. This Madhva-Gaudiya succession in the spirit of Rupa was not broken even in “periods of darkness”: acharyas may be
solitary (viviktanandi) or preachers (goshthyanandi). <!-- F329 F330 -->

Baladeva Vidyabhushana served Rupa's Govinda in Jaipur and was therefore called “Govinda-dasa.” Troubled by the dispute with
the Ramanandis, at Vishvanatha's bidding he went to the temple of Sri Govinda, Rupa's Deity, told Him everything and in a
dream received from Govinda the command to write a commentary on the “Vedanta-sutra” which He Himself would approve; the
“Govinda-bhashya” is dedicated to Govinda and named after Him. From the date in his commentary on the “Stavamala” it is
clear that he lived after the battle of Plassey (1757). In the “Prameya-ratnavali” he bows to Shyamasundara, Chaitanya,
Vyasa, Rupa and Sanatana, “risen like two moons.” On Rupa's books he wrote the “Stavamala-vibhushana” (a commentary on the
“Stavamala”), a commentary on the “Nataka-chandrika,” the “Saranga-rangada” (a commentary on the “Laghu-bhagavatamrita”)
and a commentary on the “Utkalika-vallari” (1686 Shaka, 1764 CE). <!-- F331 F332 -->

Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati considered the dust of Rupa's feet the chief thing in his life and often said that he
desired nothing else. He taught: many fools think that teaching through books, as Rupa, Sanatana, Jiva and Raghunatha Dasa
did, or accepting many disciples, as Madhva, Ramanuja and Narottama did, is an obstacle to sadhana and a worldly
entanglement; this is a delusion. Some Rupanuga Vaishnavas say that Prabodhananda inclines to the svakiya doctrine and do not
care to read his books, considering Rupa's parakiya-vada higher; one biography, following Narahari Chakravarti, declines to
enter this dispute. <!-- F333 F334 F335 -->

## Acharyas of the Nineteenth and Twentieth Centuries and the Rupanugas
Jagannatha Dasa Babaji is called in a verse of obeisance “the best of Rupa's followers,” and his teaching, according to Srila
Bhaktisiddhanta, accords with Rupa's. Bhaktivinoda Thakura is “the best of the Rupanugas”; his svarupa is Kamala-manjari, a
maidservant of Rupa-manjari, the head of Lalita's retinue. In June 1868 Bhaktivinoda visited the house of Rupa and Sanatana
in Ramakeli, and in Puri he studied the “Bhakti-rasamrita-sindhu” in detail. And Srila B. R. Sridhar Maharaj, in the
“Prabhupada-padma-stavaka,” his hymn to Srila Bhaktisiddhanta, glorifies the service of Radha's feet, approved by
Gauranga, understood by Svarupa, honoured by Sanatana, Rupa's elder brother, distributed by Rupa and others and tasted by
Raghunatha and the other followers of Rupa. <!-- F336 F339 F341 F342 -->

Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati worshipped Govardhana with the hymns of Rupa and Raghunatha and constantly repeated the words
of Rupa's first “Govardhanashtaka” — “Govardhana, fulfil my hope” (pratyasham me tvam kuru govardhana purnam) — and those of
Raghunatha: “Govardhana, grant me a dwelling near you” (nija-nikata-nivasam dehi govardhana tvam). During a journey to
Vrindavana, on 3 October, he had the darshana of Rupa's beloved Deities, Radha-Govinda. He began a commentary on Rupa's
“Upadeshamrita” but stopped after eight verses, and lectured on the “Upadeshamrita” in Vraja. In Prayaga, around 1924–1925,
he pointed out the exact place where Rupa received Mahaprabhu's teachings; at the Purna-kumbha-mela in January 1930 he
installed at Triveni the Deities of Radha-Govinda, “the life and soul of Sri Rupa,” and spoke of the teachings given to Rupa;
on 21 November 1932 the Sri Rupa Gaudiya Math was founded in Prayaga (Allahabad), and on 30 December 1935 he spoke there again
of the teachings given to Rupa; and in the mid-1930s he found in Mathura the place where Rupa came for the darshana of
Gopala. <!-- F337 F338 F343 F344 F348 Q75 -->

The followers of Rupa, he wrote in his letters, do not preach faith in their own strength but attribute everything to the
source of strength: “we do everything for the sake of Sri Krishna Chaitanya, Sri Rupa, Bhaktivinoda and our guru.” His last
instructions: preach the message of Rupa and Raghunatha; the highest goal is to become dust at the feet of Rupa's followers;
our essence is a speck of dust at Sri Rupa's feet birth after birth; may the current of Rupa's ideas flow through the world;
Narottama accepted the understanding of Rupa and Raghunatha. His sayings: the followers of Rupa do not trust in their own
strength; the intimate devotee longs only for service to Rupa's followers; the one desire is the dust of the feet of Rupa,
who embodied on earth the desires of Chaitanya; may this institution of Rupa's followers be ever engaged in service.
Bhaktisiddhanta himself is glorified as “the destroyer of unorthodox teachings contrary to the conclusions of Rupa and his
followers,” “the best of the followers of Svarupa and Rupa,” through whom flows the current of Svarupa, Rupa and
Bhaktivinoda; “be a servant of the followers of Svarupa and Rupa,” say the verses dedicated to him, and service to the gopis
and Gopinatha in them follows Rupa-manjari. <!-- F340 F345 F346 F347 -->

His disciple Bhakti Dayita Madhava Gosvami is called “dear to the followers of Rupa”; Srila Bhaktisiddhanta told his
disciples to preach the message of Rupa and Raghunatha together, and the commentary of Srila B. R. Sridhara Maharaja on the
gayatri is called a contribution to the Rupanuga-sampradaya. <!-- F349 -->

## Works
Rupa's books are listed by three witnesses whom the Gaudiya Vaishnavas trust without reservation. The first is his nephew
and disciple Jiva Gosvami. In the conclusion of the “Laghu-vaishnava-toshani” he names the works of “the younger of the
brothers”: the poems “Hamsa-duta” and “Uddhava-sandesha,” the “Chhando-’shtadashaka,” the “Utkalika-vallari,” the
“Govinda-virudavali,” the “Premendu-sagara” and many other well-known stotras to Krishna; two plays, the
“Vidagdha-madhava” and the “Lalita-madhava”; the bhanika “Dana-keli”; the “pair” of books on rasa, the
“Bhakti-rasamrita-sindhu” and the “Ujjvala-nilamani”; the compilations “Mathura-mahima,” “Padyavali” and
“Nataka-chandrika”; and finally the abridged “Bhagavatamrita.”[^55] <!-- F350 Q82 -->

The second witness is Narahari Chakravarti. He quotes these verses of Jiva in the “Bhakti-ratnakara” (1.796–799) and then,
taking a list from Krishnadasa Adhikari, a disciple of Jiva (his name stands in the guru-parampara kept in the temple of
Radha-Damodara), adds four books that Jiva did not name: the “Krishna-janma-tithi-vidhi,” the greater and lesser
“Ganoddesha-dipika” and the “Prayuktakhyata-chandrika.” In the verse list of the same first wave of the
“Bhakti-ratnakara” Rupa's books number sixteen: the “Hamsa-duta,” “Uddhava-sandesha,” “Krishna-janma-tithi-vidhi,” the
greater and lesser “Ganoddesha-dipika,” the “Stavamala” on Krishna and His intimates, the “Vidagdha-madhava,”
“Lalita-madhava,” “Dana-keli-kaumudi,” “Bhakti-rasamrita-sindhu,” “Ujjvala-nilamani,” “Prayuktakhyata-chandrika,”
“Mathura-mahima,” “Padyavali,” “Nataka-chandrika” and “Laghu-bhagavatamrita.” This count and order are followed in the
biographies of Rupa, and the books are taken in the same order below. In Bengali verses (1.811–821) Narahari adds that
Rupa wrote sixteen books, showing in them the limits of siddhanta together with lila; the “Lalita-madhava” is the summit of
vipralambha, the rasa of separation; the “Dana-keli-kaumudi” (also called “Dana-lila-kaumudi”) is an ocean of bliss; the
“Bhakti-rasamrita-sindhu” is incomparable, and the “Ujjvala-nilamani” is full of rasa. <!-- F351 F352 F353 F354 Q83 Q84 -->

The third witness is Krishnadasa Kaviraja. One of his lists has already been given above, in the account of the
brothers' life in Vraja (Antya 4.223–226); the other stands at the beginning of the “Madhya-lila”:

“He devoted a hundred thousand shlokas to the Lord's pastimes in Vrindavana. The chief of Sri Rupa's books are these:
‘Bhakti-rasamrita-sindhu,’ ‘Vidagdha-madhava,’ ‘Ujjvala-nilamani’ and ‘Lalita-madhava,’ ‘Dana-keli-kaumudi,’ ‘Stavavali,’
the eighteen ‘Lila-chhandas’ and ‘Padyavali,’ ‘Govinda-virudavali,’ ‘Mathura-mahatmya’ and ‘Nataka-varnana.’ One cannot
count all the books of Sri Rupa, such as the ‘Laghu-bhagavatamrita,’ in which he described in detail the pastimes of
Vraja.” (CC, Madhya 1.37–41)

“A hundred thousand” is in Bengali “laksha granthas”; a grantha here means a shloka or a stretch of text of a fixed
number of syllables. Kaviraja's “Nataka-varnana” is the same as the “Nataka-chandrika,” and the “Stavavali” is the
“Stavamala.” <!-- F357 Q85 Q86 -->

Sometimes the “Upadeshamrita,” the “Namashtaka,” the “Siddhanta-ratna,” the “Kavya-kaustubha” and other books are also
attributed to Rupa. The last two were in fact written by Baladeva Vidyabhushana; of the “Upadeshamrita” and of the other
attributed works we shall speak below. <!-- F358 F359 -->

### “Hamsa-duta”
First in Jiva's list stands the “Hamsa-duta” (“The Swan Messenger”). It is a khanda-kavya — a short poem modelled on one
part of a great poem (mahakavya) — and Rupa is held to have composed it before he met Sri Chaitanya. The poem has 142
verses in the shikharini metre (seventeen syllables to the line); so many are in the edition of Jivananda Vidyasagara
(“Kavya-sangraha,” part 1, Calcutta, 1888), while the Bengali “Basumati” edition, with a hundred and one verses, is full
of errors and not to be trusted. Several old manuscripts survive, and the poem has been printed more than once in
Devanagari. Later Rupa himself quoted its verses as examples in the “Bhakti-rasamrita-sindhu” (three verses) and in the
“Ujjvala-nilamani” (more than ten). <!-- F360 F361 F362 -->

The subject of the poem is Radha's separation from Krishna. When at Akrura's request Krishna left Nanda's house and went
to Mathura, Radha, pining, came with Her friends to the bank of the Yamuna. The familiar kunjas reminded Her of Krishna
still more keenly, and She fainted; the sakhis could barely keep life in Her. Seeing Her divine madness (divyonmada),
Lalita notices a white swan floating on the Yamuna and decides to send it to Krishna — to tell Him how Radha is and bring
Him back to Vraja. She describes the way to the swan: the kadamba by the ghata where Krishna stole the gopis' garments, the
pool of Kaliya, Vrinda-devi, the forests full of the cries of peacocks — and the beauty of Mathura, capital of the Yadavas,
the rapture of its women at the sight of Krishna, His chambers and the service rendered to Him, His form from feet to
face. The swan should speak of the maidens of Vraja when the cuckoo sings or the scent of jasmine drifts by: let it remind
Krishna of all that delighted Him in Vrindavana and tell Him that in Mathura, captivated by the service of the
townspeople, He has grown cold to the simple wealth of the forest and to the forest maidens. <!-- F366 F367 F368 F369 -->

Let the swan tell Him also of Radha's burning separation, of Kubja's good fortune, of how Radha worships Parvati and Shiva
for the sake of meeting Krishna, and of how in adhirudha-mahabhava She at times takes Herself to be Krishna. Uddhava,
whom Krishna had sent to console the gopis with knowledge of the Paramatma, only deepened their grief; a disciple of
Brihaspati, a minister absorbed in affairs of state, he will not tell Krishna how the gopis suffer — so there remains only
the guileless white swan. Lalita recounts to it Radha's laments in Her divine madness, speaks of her own ardent wish to see
the Divine Couple together again in Vrindavana and to serve Them, addresses Krishna's forest garland, His makara earrings,
the Kaustubha gem and His conch, praising their good fortune and asking their sympathy, and bids the swan convey the
gopis' loving anger to Krishna in the guise of a tale of the ten avataras — Matsya, Kurma and the rest. In these verses
(128–137) Rupa, through Lalita and the double meanings (shlesha) of loving anger, shows that the son of Nanda is the source
of all avataras and the shelter of all; this is the essence of Gaudiya Vaishnava philosophy and the hidden secret of
vraja-bhajana. The poem ends with an entreaty: the swan can separate milk from water — surely it will feel the grief of
the maidens of Vraja, fly to Mathura and carry out its errand. <!-- F369 F370 F371 F372 F373 -->

The opening verse of the poem is an obeisance to Shyamasundara:

“Clad in garments brighter than powdered orpiment, His feet redder than hibiscus flowers, dark of body like the tamala, a
playful smile on His face — may He appear in my heart.” (“Hamsa-duta,” 1)

In the penultimate verse Rupa praises Sanatana: wholly loving Bhagavan, ever serving the “Bhagavata,” turned away from
worldly rasa from birth, full of the sweetness of bhakti, known as “Sakara,” first among heroes — he is like the son of Vyasa, Shukadeva.
The last verse is a prayer for the poem's fruit: may sensitive rasikas find no flaw in it against rasa, bhava or
ornaments of speech, and may it pour dense waves of bliss into the heart of Krishna, friend of the whole world, hero of
the pastimes of rasa. <!-- F363 F364 F365 Q87 Q88 Q89 -->

That the poem was written before the meeting with Mahaprabhu is seen from two signs: its opening has no obeisance to Sri
Krishna Chaitanya, and Sanatana is called in its conclusion by his court name, “Sakara Mallik.” Some propose to read
instead of “viditah sakarataya” (“known as Sakara”) a supposedly corrupted “viditah sat-kavitaya” (“known for good
poetry”), but the reading “sakarataya” is given by many manuscripts and several printed editions; the commentary in
Jivananda's “Kavya-sangraha” explains it as “by the nobility of his family.” This verse also refutes the inventions of
scholars who trust only their own experience (adhyakshika): that Sakara Mallik was formerly attached to the world and only
circumstances brought him to renunciation, or that Dabir Khas and Sakara Mallik, living among mlechchhas, adopted their
ways and lost caste. These offensive opinions are fabricated from beginning to end. Sanatana here is a stranger to worldly
rasa from birth, wholly surrendered, full of prema and, like Shukadeva, the crown of paramahamsas, first among the heroes
of knowledge, bhakti and renunciation. Thus the “Hamsa-duta” testifies that Rupa and Sanatana are eternally perfect
associates of Bhagavan, not worldly men who came to renunciation later. <!-- F374 F375 F376 F377 F378 F379 I3 -->

More wonderful still: the truth that the rasa of meeting is not nourished without separation, and all the states of
Radha's adhirudha-mahabhava that Shachinandana, having taken Her feeling and radiance, would display — all this
Gaurahari awakened in Rupa's heart even before, in the outward lila, Rupa came to the shelter of His feet, and Rupa
expressed it in this short poem. Already here the hidden secret of krishna-bhajana is revealed: the only desirable service
of a sakhi is service to the meeting of Radha and Govinda; desire for one's own enjoyment is no sign of krishna-prema; the
heart is given to the ashraya-vigraha, the abode of love, and above all to Radha, crown of such abodes; vraja-lila is
higher and more wonderful than the lila of the cities (pura-lila); bhajana is performed in the mood of separation. No
worldly poet, however skilled in the rasa-shastras, could express such conclusions about transcendental separation; there
is nothing like it in Kalidasa and the other poets who sing of the world. This is one more sign that Rupa is an eternal
associate of the Lord. <!-- F380 F381 F382 I4 -->

Two commentaries on the “Hamsa-duta” are known: by Gopala Chakravarti and by Madhu Mishra, son of Ananda (or of
Purushottama). The second is described in the catalogue of the Madras Government Oriental Manuscripts Library, and its
colophon calls it Madhu Mishra's commentary on the “Hamsa-duta” composed by “Rupa and Sanatana.” <!-- F383 F384 Q90 -->

### “Uddhava-sandesha”
The second poem is a mirror of the first. In the “Hamsa-duta” Lalita, on behalf of Radha, crown of heroines, sends a swan
from Vraja to Mathura; in the “Uddhava-sandesha” Krishna Himself, crown of heroes, sends Uddhava, Brihaspati's disciple,
from Mathura to Vraja to console the gopis suffering in separation. It is also called “Uddhava-duta,” but
“Uddhava-sandesha” — “the message Uddhava carries” — is more exact: it is Krishna's own message. The basis of the plot is
this: angered by the arrogant words of Kamsa that Akrura had conveyed, Krishna left Vraja for Mathura, lived there for a
time and sent Uddhava to console the gopis, Nanda, Yashoda and all the Vrajavasis and to tell them about Himself. The title
and subject come from the “Bhagavatam” (10.46.2–3), where Krishna in private takes Uddhava by the hand and sends him to
Vraja to gladden His parents and with His message to ease the gopis' pain of separation. <!-- F385 F386 F387 F388 Q91 -->

The poem opens thus: from the roof of His pleasure palace Krishna gazes at Mathura, beautiful with blossoming trees — and
suddenly, deeply stirred, remembers the forests of Gokula. Overwhelmed by prema at the thought of the love of the beauties
of Vraja, He chooses Uddhava, first among His close friends, as messenger, explains that He went to Mathura out of anger at
Kamsa's words, says that Radha is His dearest and that She, Lalita and the sakhis barely bear the burden of life, holding
on only by His consolations, and bids Uddhava revive Radha, bitten by the serpent of separation, with the mantra of His
message. The forest of Vraja is dearer to Him than anything; the memory of even the shadow of His trouble pains the gopis
more than their own troubles the size of Mount Sumeru. Krishna describes the road and the places of His pastimes,
recalls the past at each and sinks into prema; when Uddhava's chariot reaches the slopes of Nandishvara, He says, Radha's
friends will think Krishna Himself has come. <!-- F389 F390 F391 Q92 -->

Uddhava is given errands: to bless the trees of Vraja, to ask after the welfare of the cows, to bow to the old cows as to
mothers, to embrace the cowherd friends as His representative, to bow to Nanda and Yashoda, and to introduce himself to the
gopis as “Krishna's counsellor in matters of love”; to console Chandravali, Vishakha, Dhanya, Shyamala, Padma, Lalita,
Bhadra, Shaibya and the others, and to give Radha, crown of the maidens of Vraja, wasted by separation, His vaijayanti
garland to bring Her back to consciousness. The two concluding verses are benedictions: may the “Uddhava-sandesha” again
and again pour streams of joy into the hearts of the devotees of the Lord who delights in His pastimes in Vraja, who
dances in Mathura out of guileless love and whose lotus feet are Rupa's shelter; and may the young, merciful Lord, who
played in childhood with Shridama and others, performed the rasa-lila with the young gopis and slew many demons in play,
carry the readers across the ocean of worldly existence. <!-- F392 F393 Q93 Q94 -->

When the poem was written it does not say; neither the opening nor the concluding verses contain Krishna's name or an
obeisance to Him. But in one of the last verses Rupa names himself (“rupashraya-pada,” “the feet that are Rupa's shelter”)
and with the word “svami” his master, Sanatana or Krishna. The name “Sri Rupa” could not have been there before the
meeting with Sri Chaitanya, and so the poem is assigned to the time after the meeting.[^56] It has 131 verses in the
mandakranta metre (seventeen syllables); it was printed in Devanagari in the “Kavya-sangraha” edited by Haeberlin and in
Jivananda Vidyasagara's “Kavya-sangraha” (part 3, Calcutta, 1888). Rupa quotes verses of the “Uddhava-sandesha” as examples
in the “Bhakti-rasamrita-sindhu” (Uttara 5.7) and many times in the “Ujjvala-nilamani.” <!-- F394 F395 F396 F397 F398 -->

### “Krishna-janma-tithi-vidhi”
Jiva calls this book “The Rule for Krishna's Birthday” (“Krishna-janma-tither vidhih”); its full title is
“Krishna-janma-tithi-mahotsava-vidhi,” and it is also known as “Krishnabhisheka.” The compilers of one of the biographies
obtained one manuscript in Vrindavana and saw another, old one with Banamali Lala Gosvami. In the opening verse Rupa bows to
the two Lords of Vrindavana and says that he records the rule for Krishna's birthday according to the shastras and custom
— “at the command of prabhu.” The plural of respect here points to Sanatana: the book was written at his special command.
Rupa speaks of Sanatana in the plural of respect — “prabhupada,” “prabhu” — in other openings as well, and in the
“Padyavali” (verse 233; in some manuscripts, instead of “shrimat-prabhunam,” it says outright “to the feet of Shrimat
Sanatana Gosvami”). <!-- F399 F400 F401 F402 Q95 -->

The rules of the Janmashtami vow are set out in detail in the “Hari-bhakti-vilasa” (15.133–240): the obligation of the
vow, its origin and greatness, the harm of neglecting it, the determination of the day (with the Rohini nakshatra, the
“midnight” Janmashtami, the prohibition of a day touched by Saptami), the breaking of the fast, the morning sankalpa, the
birth chamber and the whole puja — from bathing to the arghya to the moon, the worship of Devaki and Krishna and the
prayer. Why, then, did Sanatana bid Rupa write another book? Either Rupa compiled a brief rule before the detailed rules
entered the “Hari-bhakti-vilasa,” or he wrote the book later to describe specially the mahabhisheka on Krishna's
birthday. It is precisely this that distinguishes Rupa's book: on the eve, on Saptami, the platform for the bathing is
cleansed, a pit is dug in the courtyard, banana posts are set at the corners, with an awning and flags; on the morning of
Janmashtami the Deity is carried with kirtana and dance to the platform adorned with lamps and pots, with umbrella and
chamara; then come the svasti-vachana, the bhuta-shuddhi, the installation of the pot, the sankalpa, the puja, the bathing
and abhisheka in various ways with mantras, the offering of the sacred thread, betel and garlands, the maha-niranjana and a
stotra to Krishna. The day is spent with Hari's intimates, glorifying Govinda with Vedic and Puranic hymns; at midnight
comes the auspicious abhisheka for the birth and a vigil with song and dance; in the morning, the great festival of the
king of Vraja, the Nandotsava, and the honouring of mahaprasada with the Vaishnavas. <!-- F403 F404 F405 F406 Q96 -->

The other rules of the vow Rupa directs to be taken from the “Bhavishyottara-purana” — on which the “Hari-bhakti-vilasa,”
too, bases the origin of the vow (Yudhishthira asks, and Krishna tells) — while he himself describes in detail, with the
applied mantras, only the abhisheka, more fully than the “Hari-bhakti-vilasa”; hence the book's second title. The
concluding verses say that this rule of bathing Krishna on His birthday is set out in the mood of Vraja according to the
Tenth Canto of the “Bhagavatam”; whoever performs it will become dear to Govinda, be filled with deep prema and serve Him
ever directly in Vrindavana. The colophon of the Vrindavana manuscript names Sri Rupa Gosvami as the author. <!-- F407 F408 F409 F410 Q97 -->

### “Ganoddesha-dipika”
The full title of the book is “Sri Sri Radha-Krishna-ganoddesha-dipika,” “The Lamp Pointing Out the Associates of Radha
and Krishna.” Why it is needed is explained by a well-known verse: the object of worship is the son of the king of Vraja
together with His abode Vrindavana, and the highest way of service is the one shown by the maidens of Vraja. Those who
walk the path of raga follow Krishna's ragatmika associates and their manner of service, and for that one must know all
the members of His family. In our own nature (svarupa) we are Krishna's eternal intimates, and our eternal relationship is
with His family; without knowing it one cannot be established in sambandha, abhidheya or prayojana. But this knowledge
must be heard and realized from the lips of a transcendental guru — with an ear turned towards service, a pure heart and
transcendental faith. <!-- F411 F412 F413 Q98 I30 -->

The sadhus of old lovingly mentioned the names of Krishna's associates and facts about them briefly, like sutras; all
this was kept in tradition and in the shastras. Rupa, as he says, gathered these facts from the traditions of
Mathura-mandala, from the shastras, Puranas and Agamas and from the words of saintly devotees, and arranged them — for
the joy of his friends and to lay out the path of raga in order (“Ganoddesha-dipika,” 3–5). The book also contains direct
references, for example to the “Adi-purana,” the “Garuda-purana” and the “Sammohana-tantra.” Krishna's family are the
Vrajavasis, and the book gives the names, appearance, qualities, retinue and service of all its members and their
branches. The family is divided into cowherds (pashupala) of the Yadu line, brahmanas (vipra) and artisans (bahishtha);
the cowherds into vaishyas who live by milk, abhiras who tend calves and buffaloes, and gurjaras who tend goats; and by
the kind of relationship with Krishna into eight classes: revered elders, brothers and sisters, female messengers,
servants, artisans, maidservants, friends of His own age and beloveds. Also described are the groups (yuthas), garments,
ornaments, umbrellas, couches, canopies, kunjas, houses, carts and mounts of Radha-Krishna and Their retinue, the nature of
the eight sakhis, their arts, bhavas, gatherings and much else; from the “Sammohana-tantra” two more groups of eight sakhis
of Radha are given. <!-- F414 F415 F416 F417 F418 Q99 -->

The book has two parts, a greater and a lesser. The greater (“Brihad-ganoddesha-dipika”) opens with obeisances to the feet
of the guru with the devotees, to Chaitanya, who appeared together with Nityananda, to the son of Nanda with the gopis and
Vrajavasis, and to the feet of Radhika. At its end (250–252) it says: the associates of the Lords of Vrindavana are
countless, only a pointer is given here, and the rest — couches, food, drink, betel, swings, tilaka and so on — the wise
will learn from the shastras and from the guru; knowledge of the names and forms of Krishna's associates had almost
vanished, but Rupa's sight, illumined by Bhagavan's mercy like a lamp, beheld these names full of rasa. The last verse gives
the time: the book was “lit” in the house of Nanda, king of Vraja, in 1472 Shaka (1550 CE), in the month of Shravana, on a
Sunday, on the shashthi. The lesser part describes Krishna's form, qualities, sweetness and ages, His friends of every
degree — companions, well-wishers, priya-sakhas and priya-narma-sakhas — Baladeva, servants, messengers male and female,
Paurnamasi, Vrinda, Nandimukhi, His cows, deer, peacocks and parrots, the places of His pastimes — ghatas, hills, lakes,
trees — and then Radhika: the marks on Her palms and soles, Her beauty, Her relatives and friends — the priya-, prana- and
nitya-sakhis and the manjaris — Her worshipful deity, Her cows, heifer, doe, swan, myna, ornaments, flower garden, raga,
dance and birthday. It too ends by pointing to the countlessness of the associates (250); in some manuscripts the closing
verse of the greater part is repeated in the lesser, and the penultimate verse names the same year 1472 Shaka. <!-- F419 F420 F421 F422 F423 F424 F425 Q100 Q101 Q102 -->

Attempts have been made to dispute Rupa's authorship of the book. The first argument: if it was written in 1550, why does
Jiva not name it in the “Laghu-toshani” (1582)? But Krishnadasa Kaviraja in his list does not name the “Hamsa-duta” and
“Uddhava-sandesha” either, nor quote a single verse from them, though he could not have been unaware of them: he quotes
the “Bhakti-rasamrita-sindhu” and “Ujjvala-nilamani” abundantly, where both poems are cited more than once. Nor can one
say that he omitted them as written before Rupa came to the Lord: their siddhanta fully agrees with Rupa's rasa-shastras,
or Jiva would not have named them either, and in the conclusion of the “Uddhava-sandesha” (verse 130) Rupa calls himself by
name — “shri-rupashraya-pada” — so the poem was certainly written after the meeting with the Lord. Nor is there any
“copper-plate decree” obliging every later author to name all the books of his predecessor: the four books of Rupa that
Jiva did not name could well have been mentioned by another, later witness. <!-- F426 F428 F429 F430 -->

The second argument: the opening of the “Brihad-ganoddesha-dipika” names Nityananda, whereas nowhere else, not even in the
three “Chaitanyashtakas,” does Rupa name Him explicitly. This argument is weak. Karnapura in the
“Chaitanya-chandrodaya-nataka” names neither Gopala Bhatta nor Jiva, while in the “Gaura-ganoddesha-dipika” he names both
and speaks of their position in Vraja; and in the openings of Jiva and Raghunatha, too, Nityananda is not named explicitly.
The “Ujjvala-nilamani” has no obeisance to Mahaprabhu, though in the earlier “Vidagdha-madhava,” “Bhakti-rasamrita-sindhu”
and “Lalita-madhava” He is specially glorified: Rupa received the subject of the “Ujjvala-nilamani” in Prayaga, briefly,
“in sutras,” by the power of Gaurasundara's mercy, and in the second opening verse he says that here he unfolds the
confidential truths of madhura-rasa told briefly in the “Bhakti-rasamrita-sindhu”; Jiva says the same in the
“Lochana-rochani.” A mind relying on empirical knowledge alone does not penetrate such matters, and to declare the book
someone else's because of Nityananda's name is to destroy one's own understanding. <!-- F427 F431 F432 F433 F434 -->

The third argument: after verse 246 the names of Radhika's sakhis are given from the “Sammohana-tantra,” whereas Rupa
supposedly never refers to tantras elsewhere. But he does refer to them by name: in the “Laghu-bhagavatamrita” to the
“Sammohana-tantra,” the “Satvata-tantra” and the “Bhargava-tantra,” in the “Bhakti-rasamrita-sindhu” to the
“Vaishnava-tantra” and simply to “the tantra,” and likewise in the “Ujjvala-nilamani.” Such arguments against the
authenticity of Rupa's books are constructions of those to whom the realm of the acharyas is closed; they must be examined
impartially, by hearing through succession (shrauta). <!-- F435 I31 -->

### “Stavamala”
The “many stavavalis” of which Kaviraja speaks (Madhya 1.39) are the “Stavamala,” “The Garland of Hymns,” compiled by Jiva.
In his introduction Jiva says that he, insignificant, wholly dependent on Rupa — his chosen master, the author of the
“Rasamrita” — has strung his stotras into a garland, and gives the order: stotras to Chaitanya, to Krishna, to Radhika and
to the Divine Couple; then the “Virudavali”; the pastimes from the Nandotsava to the slaying of Kamsa in various metres;
figured verses; songs (the “Gitavali”); and finally stotras to Lalita, the Yamuna, Mathura, Govardhana, Vrindavana and the
names of Krishna. <!-- F436 F437 Q103 -->

The “Stavamala” contains forty-nine works. It opens with three “Chaitanyashtakas.” Then come stotras to Krishna: the
“Mahanandakhya-stotra,” “Lilamrita-nama-dashaka,” “Premendusagara” (a hundred and eight names of Krishna),
“Keshavashtaka,” two “Kunjaviharyashtakas,” “Mukundashtaka,” “Vraja-nava-yuvaraj-ashtaka,” “Pranama-pranaya-stava,”
“Hari-kusuma-stavaka,” “Gatha-chhandah-stava,” “Tribhangi-panchaka,” two untitled verses — on self-surrender and on the
bonds of hope — and the “Mukunda-muktavali.” To Radha are dedicated the “Ananda-chandrika” (ten of Her names),
“Premendusudhasava” (a hundred and eight names of Vrindavaneshvari), “Radhashtaka,” “Prarthana-paddhati,”
“Chatu-pushpanjali” and “Gandharva-samprarthana-ashtaka”; to the Divine Couple the “Radha-Krishna-nama-yugala-ashtaka,” the
“Vraja-navina-yuva-dvandva-ashtaka” with a verse of meditation, the “Karpanya-panjika-stotra,” the “Utkalika-vallari” and
two verses on the night's-end (nishanta) pastimes. Next come the “Govinda-virudavali” with many kinds of viruda metres,
the eighteen pastimes in metres (“Ashtadasha-chhandah”), the “Govardhanoddharana,” further accounts of the stealing of the
garments and of the rasa-lila, the “Svayam-utprekshita-lila,” “Khandita” and “Lalitokta-totashtaka,” twelve figured verses
(“Chitra-kavitvani”: of two letters and of one, in the shape of a wheel, a serpent, a lotus and so on), the forty-two songs of
the “Gitavali” and finally the “Lalitashtaka,” “Yamunashtaka,” “Mathurashtaka,” two “Govardhanashtakas,”
“Vrindavanashtaka” and “Krishna-namashtaka.” In different editions (Nirnaya-Sagara, 1903; Baharampur) a number of works
are titled or divided differently. <!-- F438 F439 F440 F441 F443 -->

When the Lord withdrew, Rupa, crushed by separation, constantly remembered and sang of His pastimes; they are preserved in
the “Chaitanyashtakas,” where every verse ends with the same plea: “Will that Chaitanya come again before my eyes?” The
time of one stotra Rupa gave himself: the “Utkalika-vallari” was written while he lived in Gokula, in the month of Pausha,
1471 Shaka (at the turn of 1549 and 1550). How the “Govinda-virudavali” came about has been told above in Baladeva
Vidyabhushana's words; Rupa used many of its virudas as examples in the “Samanya-virudavali-lakshana.” In the table of its
kinds (kalikas) are virudas of short and long syllables named “achyuta,” “utpala,” “tilaka,” “turanga,” “purushottama” and
others, “manjaris” of three kinds, tribhangis, and mixed and prose kalikas. <!-- F444 F445 F454 F456 Q104 -->

The “Chhando-’shtadashaka,” “Eighteen Metres,” is named both by Jiva at the end of the “Laghu-toshani” and by Kaviraja
(“the eighteen lila-chhandas”). The second verse of the “Nandotsavadi-charita” says that the great pastimes of Hari from the
Nandotsava to the slaying of Kamsa are told in eighteen elegant metres: the Nandotsava; the slaying of Shakata,
Trinavarta and other childhood pastimes; the yamala-arjuna trees; the herding of the calves; the stealing of the calves by
Brahma; Talavana; the subduing of Kaliya; the pastimes in Bhandiravana; the pastimes of the rainy season and autumn; the
stealing of the garments; the mercy to the brahmanas' wives; the abduction of Nanda by Varuna; the rasa-lila; the
deliverance of Sudarshana; the song of the gopis; the slaying of Arishtasura and others; the pastimes in Kamsa's arena.
These eighteen stotras Jiva included in the “Stavamala.” Baladeva Vidyabhushana wrote a commentary on them and at its end
asks that Krishna, Rupa and the sadhus be pleased with it. The figured verses of the “Stavamala,” with their definitions,
are quoted by Jiva in the “Bhakti-rasamrita-shesha” and by Baladeva in the “Sahitya-kaumudi.” <!-- F440 F446 F447 F448 F449 F450 Q105 Q106 -->

At the end of every song of the “Gitavali” the name “Sanatana” stands as the signature (bhanita), and some consider it
Sanatana's work, citing the “Dashama-charita” in the list of his books (CC, Madhya 1.35). But Baladeva at the end of his
commentary calls these songs Rupa's work.[^57] The songs are in the gatha metre, with ten anushtubh verses added; their
themes are the Nandotsava, Vasanta-panchami, the Dola-yatra and the rasa-lila, and within it the marks and examples of the
eight kinds of heroine. For each song a raga is given — ashavari, karnata, kalyana, kedara, gaudi, dhanashri, bhairava,
mallara, ramakeli, lalita, vasanta, saurashtri — while the tala (ekatali) is named only twice. <!-- F442 F451 F452 F455 Q107 -->

Into the “Padyavali” Rupa took verses from the “Govinda-virudavali,” from the “Chhando-’shtadashaka” (on the herding of the
cows and calves) and from the “Mathurashtaka.” Of the “Chatu-pushpanjali” and of the first “Govardhanashtaka,” whose verse
Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati constantly repeated, we have spoken above. <!-- F453 F457 -->

### “Vidagdha-madhava”
The “Vidagdha-madhava” is a play in seven acts on Krishna's vraja-lila. The marks of transcendental separation and meeting
of hero and heroine that Rupa would later describe in the “Ujjvala-nilamani” he shows here through the pastimes of his
chosen Deities, Radha and Govinda, and the “Ujjvala-nilamani” draws many examples from the play. The acts are titled:
“The Play of the Flute's Sound,” “The Love Letter,” “Meeting with Radha,” “The Stealing of the Flute,” “Radha's Grace,”
“Autumn Pastimes” and “Pastimes at Gauri's Shrine.” <!-- F458 F459 F460 -->

Of the verses of benediction (nandi) that open the play, and of how Rupa read them in Puri, we have spoken above. The play
itself, as the sutradhara says in the first act, was written at a command received in a dream from Bhagavan Shankaradeva,
the bhakta-avatara; Vishvanatha Chakravarti explains in his commentary that this is Gopishvara, who dwells on the bank of
Brahma-kunda. At Gopishvara's command the play was also presented to an assembly of scholars from many lands. These are his
words: “The life of these devotees, in whom separation from Mukunda has awakened, is ready to leave the body. Save them by
revealing the river of the nectar of His pastimes; My mercy will gather for you all that is needed.” <!-- F461 F462 F464 F465 Q108 Q109 -->

Some, not understanding these words, raise objections: what separation is meant, if the play was being written in the
Lord's presence? But separation from Mukunda is the natural trait of the Gaura-bhaktas who follow Rupa: they are always
steeped in the rasa of separation. Besides, the Gosvamis sometimes revised their books long years afterwards. Jiva writes
in a letter quoted in the “Bhakti-ratnakara” (14.19) that some corrections still remained in the “Rasamrita-sindhu,” the
“Madhava-mahotsava,” the “Uttara-champu” and the “Harinamamrita”; he finished the “Uttara-champu” in 1592, while the
“Madhava-mahotsava” is dated 1555 — so he was still revising his own book thirty-seven years later. Rupa, too, could have
inserted these words during a revision. <!-- F466 F467 Q110 I32 -->

Another objection from those who trust empirical knowledge alone: by the date at its end the play was completed in the year
of Mahaprabhu's departure, while Kaviraja writes that the Lord Himself heard its verses — how can his account be true? The
answer is simple: often most of a book is written at once and the remainder, for particular reasons, long afterwards; so
it was with many mahajanas. Most of the “Vidagdha-madhava” was written in Mahaprabhu's presence, and He Himself relished it
with Ramananda Raya and others; this is also indicated by Svarupa Damodara's words to the Bengali poet about Rupa's two
plays, which refer only to their opening:

“The two beautiful plays of Sri Rupa Gosvami are an example of this. Merely on hearing their opening alone, all the
devotees feel the highest happiness.” (CC, Antya 5.108)

In the year of Mahaprabhu's departure Rupa wrote the remainder, finished the revision and then put the date; to begin so
large a play after the Lord's departure and finish it in the same year would have been impossible. The account that
Kaviraja, Rupa's undivided servant, recorded from Raghunatha Dasa's words must not be declared a “poetic fiction” on the
strength of the conjectures of modern scholars. <!-- F468 F469 F470 F471 F472 -->

The characters of the play: Nanda, king of Vraja; Krishna, the hero; Balarama; the friends Shridama and Subala;
Madhumangala, companion and jester (vidushaka); Abhimanyu, son of Jatila; the sutradhara — Rupa Gosvami himself — and his
assistant (pariparshvika), a disciple of Rupa. The female roles: Yashoda, queen of Vraja; Radhika, the heroine; Her
sakhis Lalita and Vishakha; the messenger Vrinda; Paurnamasi, mother of the muni Sandipani and disciple of Narada;
Nandimukhi, Madhumangala's sister; Jatila; Mukhara, Radhika's maternal grandmother and Yashoda's nurse; Sarangi, a sakhi of
Radhika; the old gopis Karala and Karalika; Chandravali, a group-leader (yutheshvari), and her sakhis Padma and Shaibya. <!-- F473 F474 -->

The “Vidagdha-madhava” reveals one of the secrets of Gaudiya Vaishnava siddhanta. Paurnamasi explains how the group-leaders
Radhika and Chandravali, deceiving Abhimanyu, Jatila's son, Govardhana, the overseer of Kamsa's cows, and others, always
delight Krishna: for the sake of this deception Yogamaya Herself made everyone believe in the appearance of their
marriages, whereas they are Krishna's eternal beloveds (“Vidagdha-madhava,” 1.24–25). <!-- F475 Q111 I33 -->

The play ends with three verses: Rupa calls on the learned to study the “Vidagdha-madhava,” full of Sri Radha's pastimes and
separation and adorned with the sixty-four arts; names the time and place of completion — Sri Gokula, at the close of 1589
Samvat (1532 CE);[^58] and humbly says that as stars of feeble light adorn the night, so the peaceful, exalted bhagavatas
turn even a heap of faults into virtues. The manuscript collection of the shripata of Gopivallabhapura holds a Bengali copy
of the play on sixty-six leaves, made in 1657. Yadu-nandana Thakura, a disciple of Hemalata Thakurani, composed an elegant
Bengali verse rendering of the play, the “Rasa-kadamba.” <!-- F463 F476 F477 F478 Q112 -->

### “Lalita-madhava”
The “Lalita-madhava” is a play in ten acts. The first four depict the sweet pastimes of Vrindavana, but from the fifth to
the tenth the pastimes of Dvaraka are mingled with them, and so the play is considered devoted to dvaraka-lila. Rupa
explains the title in the conclusion: here Hari, who captivates the mind of Kamadeva himself, by His own free will did not
display the lofty (udatta) heroism befitting the heroes of plays, and by His pastimes took on the “lalita”-bhava — a
playful, graceful mood.[^59] The acts are titled: “The Evening Festival,” “The Slaying of Shankhachuda,” “Radhika
Maddened,” “Radha's Tryst,” “Chandravali Found,” “Lalita Found,” “Meeting in the New Vrindavana,” “Pastimes in the New
Vrindavana,” “Gazing at the Picture” and “Desires Fulfilled.” <!-- F479 F480 F481 Q113 -->

Like the “Vidagdha-madhava,” this play Rupa wrote at the command, given in a dream, of Gopishvara Shiva, who dwells on the
bank of Brahma-kunda. At its beginning, in the role of the sutradhara, he relates that at the festival of Dipamalika,
during the worship of Govardhana, he read the play to an assembly of Vaishnavas gathered to the east of the temple of
Madhavi-Madhava on the bank of Radha-kunda, and pleased them; the command he had received in a dream from Gopishvara,
crowned with the crescent moon, great lord of the land by Brahma-kunda adorned with lotuses, knower of the rasa of the vow
to live by the bowers of Vrindavana (1.3). The opening of the play is a wish for the Vaishnavas' joy, an obeisance to
Krishna, a mention of Gopishvara's command, a prayer for Gaura's mercy (“may the son of Shachi grant me good”), a confession
of his own unworthiness, and praise of Sanatana Gosvami: <!-- F482 F483 F484 Q114 -->

“He who once appeared in the world as the third of the perfected ones — Sanaka and the others — to instruct the
paramahamsas, has now descended to place in the hearts of the bhaktas the whole secret of bhakti-rasa with all its parts.
To him, the complete teacher of the world, obeisance.” (“Lalita-madhava,” 1.7)

So Rupa calls Sanatana an avatara of the four Kumaras and the teacher of the world (vishva-guru). <!-- F485 F486 Q115 -->

In the first act, in a conversation between Gargi and Paurnamasi, Rupa reveals a special secret: the semblance of Radha's
marriage to Abhimanyu is only an appearance created by yogamaya;[^60] otherwise how could a lowly man take the hand of
Radha — a vaijayanti garland woven of the flowers of Mount Vindhya's austerities, enriched with the nectar of Brahma's
blessing, a nectar of sweetness that softens Madhava's heart (1.25)? In the plot of the play, two daughters, famed in the
three worlds, were born to Vindhya by Brahma's blessing. Among the eight great shaktis full of sweetness — Radha,
Chandravali, Lalita, Vishakha, Padma, Shaibya, Shyamala and Bhadra — these two are especially renowned as the abode of all
virtues and as group-leaders. At Brahma's request Yogamaya transferred the girls from the wombs of the wives of Vrishabhanu
and Chandrabhanu into the wombs of Vindhya's wives; Putana, the stealer of children, brought Vrishabhanu's daughter to
Gokula, while Vindhya's elder daughter fell into a river flowing into Vidarbha and was found by Bhishmaka, king of
Vidarbha. And the marriages of Chandravali and the others to the cowherds headed by Govardhana are only an appearance: the
cowherds who fancy themselves husbands are left with nothing but the feeling “mine,” for even to see these girls is almost
impossible for them (1.44). <!-- F487 F488 F489 Q116 Q117 -->

In the fifth act, through Narada, Rupa briefly reveals also the secret of how the queens of Dvaraka are related to the
maidens of Vraja: in essence they are one but different in body, and only in the interval, by the power of the same
yogamaya, did they become as if quite other. The beauties of Vraja are still in Vraja, overwhelmed by prema; but Yogamaya,
to give them even in separation the joy of their Beloved's company, gave them the sense of being the queens of the city,
and they experienced this as in a long dream; while the sixteen thousand one hundred and eight queens whose deeds will be
described at the pilgrimage to Kurukshetra are others. “Enough of revealing this secret,” Narada concludes (5.5). <!-- F490 Q118 I34 -->

The penultimate verse of the play gives the time and place: 1459 Shaka (1537 CE), the month of Shukra (Jyeshtha), the
fourth tithi, a Sunday; Rupa, bowing to Hari, completed it in Bhadravana. There are many more characters here: besides
the dwellers of Vraja — Uddhava, Narada, Garuda, Sunanda, Bhishmaka, Shankhachuda, Vishvakarma, Suparna; among the female
roles — Rohini, Kundalata, Madhavi, Nava-vrinda, Sukanthi, Tulasi, Malati, Pingala, Vindhyavasini (Ekanamsha), the
doorkeeper Kanchuki, Bhargavi, Gargi, Bakula and others. There is a Bengali verse rendering of the play, the
“Prema-kadamba,” made in imitation of the “Rasa-kadamba”; it is not known that Yadu-nandana himself translated the
“Lalita-madhava.” <!-- F491 F492 F496 F497 Q119 -->

Both plays, the “Dana-keli-kaumudi” and Rupa's rasa-shastras are beyond the reach not only of the ordinary human mind but
even of the empirical judgement of extraordinary people: one who is in the grip of lust, anger and the other enemies will
not, by learning alone, touch even the shore of these transcendental oceans. Therefore many who fancied themselves
panditas, not grasping the meaning of the plays, fell into confusion — especially over the Lord's words to Rupa: “Krishna
never leaves Vraja” (CC, Antya 1.66; see above). The true resolution was given by Bhaktivinoda Thakura in an article,
“Remarks on the Plays ‘Vidagdha-madhava’ and ‘Lalita-madhava’”; he did not publish it in his lifetime, and it was printed
later in the journal “Gaudiya” (year 20, no. 6). <!-- F493 F494 F495 I35 -->

### “Dana-keli-kaumudi”
The “Dana-keli-kaumudi” is a one-act play of the bhanika type, one of the minor dramas (uparupaka). According to the
“Sahitya-darpana” of Vishvanatha Kaviraja (6.308–313), a bhanika has elegant costumes, two junctures of action (sandhi) —
opening and conclusion — the kaishiki and bharati styles, one act, an exalted heroine and an excellent hero, and seven
parts; Rupa's book comes still closer to the marks of a bhanika in Sharadatanaya's “Bhava-prakashana”: its subject is the
deeds of Hari, its chief rasa is shringara, dance and song are subsidiary, and it is full of witty jests. <!-- F498 F499 F500 Q120 -->

In the introduction Rupa wishes the Vaishnavas good from Radha's glance and victory for Her love for Krishna; the
sutradhara speaks of Vaishnavas overwhelmed by prema in the valley of Mount Nandishvara: the lovers' quarrel of Nanda's son
draws even the atmaramas away from the bliss of brahman. The opening verse of the bhanika has a double meaning: it
glorifies Krishna, whose name attracts the knowers of rasa, whose conduct increases the joy of Nanda and of the sadhus,
whose beauty bestows festivity, whose body is eternal (“sanatana”) — and at the same time “my master,” whose name attracts
people, whose nature gladdens the sadhus, who gives festivity to Rupa and bears a body named “Sanatana,” that is, Sri
Sanatana (11). <!-- F501 F502 Q121 -->

The plot is this. Vasudeva, wishing good to Balarama and to his friend's son Krishna, son of the king of Vraja, began a
sacrifice in the forest, appointing as his representative Bhaguri, Gargi's son-in-law. Radha and Her friends, with the
elders' permission, carried fresh ghee (haiyangavina) to the sacrificial pavilion by Govinda-kunda. Paurnamasi had informed
Krishna beforehand through Nandimukhi, and at Govardhana Krishna, posing as a toll collector (dana), demanded the toll from
Radhika and Her friends; at the end Paurnamasi, acting as mediator, arranged a fitting payment. The characters are
Krishna, Madhumangala, Arjuna, Subala, Ujjvala, the sutradhara and an actor, and among the female roles Radha, Lalita,
Vishakha, Champakalata, Chitra, Nandimukhi, Vrinda and Paurnamasi. The dana-lila, the toll pastime, is described in this
bhanika in detail. <!-- F503 F512 F513 -->

Before the end Paurnamasi prays to Krishna in two verses. In the first she asks Madhava ever to play at the crossing with
Radhika, best in virtues, surrounded by Her friends, and with His jesting companions; in the second she says:

“Madhava, who by Your merciful glance fulfil the desires of all the dwellers of Vrindavana! He who lives in a hut on the
bank of Radha-kunda, having given up all other affairs, and longs for direct service to You Two — quickly make the tree of
his longing bear fruit.” (“Dana-keli-kaumudi,” 114)

So Rupa points to Raghunatha Dasa Gosvami. The two concluding verses say that the bhanika, which gives joy to the sadhus,
was woven at the command of “my dear friend” — may it adorn the bank of his kunda even for a moment — and written when Rupa
lived at Nandishvara; the commentary that in the Baharampur edition is circulated as Jiva Gosvami's explains that the
“dear friend” is Raghunatha Dasa, who lives by Radha-kunda. <!-- F504 F505 F506 F507 Q122 Q123 Q124 -->

The year in the last verse is given in number-words (“manu-shata,” “chandra,” “svara”); by the rule “figures are read from
right to left” this is 1471 Shaka, that is, 1549 CE. Another reading would give years when Rupa was a child and Raghunatha
Dasa had not yet appeared or was an infant; Raghunatha appeared in 1506, and by 1549 his request is quite possible.[^61]
That the “Bhakti-rasamrita-sindhu,” completed in 1541, quotes verses of the bhanika does not stand in the way: Rupa could
have been writing both books at once and quoted verses from the finished part of the bhanika (the printed edition has 414
sections, and the “Bhakti-rasamrita-sindhu” quotes sections up to the 117th), finishing it afterwards. Following Rupa's
bhanika, Raghunatha Dasa wrote the “Dana-keli-chintamani” and says in it that “the blind one” gathered this gem only by the
power of the dust of Rupa's feet. This also answers the question whether the bhanika was written before Raghunatha came to
the Lord, since it has no obeisance to Sri Chaitanya. Aufrecht's catalogue mentions a commentary on the
“Dana-keli-kaumudi” by a certain “Raghunathadasa” (whether this is the same Raghunatha is unknown); the “Padyavali” has
three verses under Raghunatha Dasa's name. Of the tradition that Rupa wrote this merry bhanika to draw Raghunatha out of the
ocean of separation we have spoken above. <!-- F508 F509 F510 F511 F514 F515 Q79 -->

### “Bhakti-rasamrita-sindhu” and “Ujjvala-nilamani”
The “Bhakti-rasamrita-sindhu” (“The Ocean of the Nectar of Bhakti-rasa”) is the finest ripe fruit of the wish-fulfilling
tree of Gaudiya rasa literature, the scientific shastra on bhakti-rasa; of how the Lord in Prayaga told Rupa to write it,
and of its opening verse, we have spoken above. A detailed review of the book was printed in the journal “Gaudiya” (year
12, nos. 47–49). Among its conclusions is that mental service (manasi-seva) is higher than service with physical
articles: Rupa gives the example of a brahmana of Pratishthanapura who by mental service attained the company of Narayana
in Vaikuntha (1.2.182). <!-- F516 F517 -->

The “Ujjvala-nilamani” (“The Radiant Sapphire”) is the scientific shastra on the ujjvala, or madhura, rasa of Krishna, the
embodiment of the nectar of all rasas; it unfolds what is said briefly about madhura-rasa in the “Bhakti-rasamrita-sindhu”
(see above). A review of it was printed in the “Gaudiya” (year 12, nos. 40–41). <!-- F518 -->

### “Prayuktakhyata-chandrika”
This book has been sought, and is sought to this day, in Vrindavana and elsewhere, but there is no reliable information
anywhere about a manuscript of it. The compilers of one of the biographies, with their guru and senior Vaishnavas, searched
for it in the library of the temple at Jaipur (October 1927), in the book collection of the shripata of Gopivallabhapura
together with Vishvambharananda Deva Gosvami (11 February 1925), in the library of the Ranganathaji temple in Vrindavana,
in the Madras Government Oriental Manuscripts Library and in various shripatas of Bengal and Orissa — the search is not
over. Judging by its title, it is, like Jiva's “Dhatu-sangraha,” a special treatise on Sanskrit grammar dealing with the
akhyata — verbal forms and the use of roots. Colebrooke mentions a Vaishnava grammar, the “Sri Chaitanyamrita”; Baladeva
Vidyabhushana's grammar “Vyakarana-kaumudi” is almost lost (its manuscript was with Radhacharana Vidyavagisha in
Vrindavana); and the library of Dacca University holds a manuscript of sixteen leaves attributed to Rupa, the “Sri
Harinamamrita-vyakarana-sankshepa.” <!-- F519 F520 F521 F522 F523 -->

### “Mathura-mahatmya”
Jiva calls this book “Mathura-mahima,” Kaviraja “Mathura-mahatmya,” Narahari Chakravarti again “Mathura-mahima”: it is a
compilation by Rupa of testimonies to the glory of Mathura. It begins with an obeisance to Sri Mathura and the verse: “Hari
usually grants liberation, but not bhakti, even to those who worship Him; to you, Mathura, who have vowed to give this
highest gift — obeisance.” Then Rupa says that for the joy of fortunate hearts he gathers the glory of Mathura, crown of
all tirthas, and cites testimonies to her purifying power — from the “Adi-varaha” (58.1: Mathura-mandala extends twenty
yojanas, and one who bathes anywhere in it is freed from grave sins) and other shastras. <!-- F524 F526 Q125 -->

The compilation then proceeds thus: Mathura destroys sins, contains countless tirthas, gives shelter to the shelterless,
is attainable only by Bhagavan's mercy, grants liberation, Vishnu-loka and everything desired and is beyond the world; the
fruits of residence there at special times — chaturmasya, the Janmashtami of the month of Bhadra, Karttika, Prabodhini,
dvadashi, the Bhishma-panchaka; Madhupuri in Madhuvana, Krishna's birthplace, Keshavadeva, Bhuteshvara, Vishranti-tirtha
and the twenty-four chief tirthas of the Yamuna; the brahmanas and residents of Mathura; the twelve forests and Govinda in
Vrindavana; Brahma-kunda, Keshi-tirtha, the pool of Kaliya; the order of circling the twelve forests, the parikrama of
Govardhana and bathing in the Manasi-ganga; Govardhana with Brahma-kunda and the tirthas of Indra, Varuna, Kubera and Yama
at the four quarters, Govinda-kunda; and finally the great tirthas and Deities of Mathura — Narayana, Keshava, Svayambhu,
Padmanabha, Dirgha-Vishnu, Govinda, Hari and Varaha. Rupa cites the “Adi-purana” most often, but also the
“Gopalottara-tapani,” the “Gautamiya-tantra,” the “Padma,” “Brahma-vaivarta,” “Brahmanda,” “Bhavishya,” “Vamana,” “Vayu,”
“Varaha,” “Vishnu” and “Skanda” Puranas, the “Bhagavatam,” the “Vishnu-dharmottara,” the “Brihan-naradiya” and others. <!-- F525 F536 -->

The end of the compilation in the old Vrindavana manuscript runs thus: in the “Gopalottara-tapani” still more is glorified,
and the Puranas name many tirthas of Mathura, but here only some, known today, are recorded; there follow the colophon, an
obeisance to the Yamuna, a wish that by this compilation, the Yamuna's friend, the Enemy of Madhu may be pleased “with me,”
and an obeisance to Vrindavana. In the manuscript described by Haraprasad Shastri the obeisances to the Yamuna and
Vrindavana and the penultimate verse are absent, and the colophon calls the book compiled by “Shrimad Rupa Gosvami.” These
words apparently belong to the copyist: Rupa, the embodiment of superhuman humility, calls himself in the
“Bhakti-rasamrita-sindhu” and other books “wretched” and “little Rupa” and would never have called himself “Shrimad Rupa
Gosvami”; the author's humility is a sign of authenticity, and grand titles in colophons come from copyists. Manuscripts of
the compilation also exist at Dacca University (a copy of 1766, with the obeisance to the Yamuna, as in the Vrindavana one),
in Jaipur, in the library of the Govindaji temple, and in Gopivallabhapura with Vishvambharananda Deva Gosvami — there they
are kept separately from the “Mathura-mahatmya” of the “Varaha-purana.” <!-- F527 F528 F529 F530 F531 Q126 Q127 I37 -->

Farquhar and some others asserted that Rupa's compilation was later inserted into the “Varaha-purana” as its chapters
152–180; some build conjectures on seeing in the “Hari-bhakti-vilasa” (11.260) a reference to a “Mathura-mahatmya” in the
“Varaha,” and perhaps misunderstand the CC as well:

“He collected books on the greatness of Mathura and uncovered hidden places of the divine pastimes, wandering through the
forests of Vraja.” (CC, Madhya 25.208)

The compilation itself easily refutes this guess. Were it part of the “Varaha-purana,” Jiva and Kaviraja would not have
named it as a separate book of Rupa, nor would separate manuscripts of it be found in the libraries of the old Gosvamis of
Vraja; moreover, in the “Varaha-purana” the glory of Mathura-mandala fills whole chapters, 152–180, whereas Rupa takes from
it only individual verses and cites verses from many other shastras. The wish to make an ancient testimony received
through succession “modern” is the work of maya, which, knowingly or unknowingly to them, beguiles those who have turned
away from the Lord. <!-- F532 F533 F534 F535 Q128 I38 -->

### “Padyavali”
The “Padyavali” (“A String of Verses”) is an anthology of verses on Hari and His pastimes: here are mahajanas,
contemporaries of Rupa, and ancient “ordinary” poets — Amaru, Umapatidhara, Kshemendra, Bana, Bhavabhuti, Mayura and
others. It shows that the worship of Radha-Govinda, the son of Nanda, in various rasas has from time without beginning
adorned the lips of the Vaishnava mahajanas and been a subject of verse even for ordinary poets. The first verse explains
the design: this “Padyavali,” composed by rasikas, beautiful with words about Mukunda, an ocean of waves of joy dispelling
all darkness, is gathered for the pleasure of an assembly of the discerning. After an obeisance to the flute-playing
Vanamali, Radha's beloved, and a blessing on the bhaktas come a hundred and twenty-four sections: Krishna's greatness, the
glory of bhajana, prema, the glory of the holy name, krishna-katha, meditation, the devotees — their humility, entreaties,
longing and disregard of liberation — the glory of Mathura and Vrindavana, Nanda and Yashoda, Krishna's childhood and
boyhood, Radha's purva-raga, trysts and the kinds of heroine, the stealing of the flute, the lifting of Govardhana, the
boat pastime, the rasa, Krishna's disappearance, the water pastimes, the eternal lila, the departure to Mathura, Radha's
lament, messages through Uddhava, Krishna's separation in Dvaraka, Sudama, Kurukshetra. <!-- F537 F538 F539 Q129 -->

In the conclusion Rupa says that besides verses of Jayadeva, Vishvamangala and others, other verses too are gathered here.
Vishvamangala's “Krishna-karnamrita” Gaurasundara brought from the south, and Jayadeva's “Gita-govinda” circulated as a book
anyway; but the verses of poets and mahajanas not collected in books were scattered or passed from mouth to mouth among
devotee-rasikas with good memories, and Rupa set them in order. In some manuscripts and editions one more verse is added at
the end — an entreaty to a wish-fulfilling tree like a young tamala, in blossoms of radiant ujjvala-rasa, surrounded by the
protectresses of Gokula. <!-- F540 F541 F542 Q130 Q131 -->

Some suppose that the “Padyavali” was compiled before Rupa met the Lord, since it has no obeisance to Gaura. But it
includes the “Shikshashtaka” (under the name “Sri Bhagavatah”), a verse of Sanatana (under the name “Shrimat-prabhunam”),
verses of Raghunatha Dasa and Gopala Bhatta — among them a verse on chanting the holy name — verses of Raghupati Upadhyaya
that Rupa heard at Adaila (his complete verse on the highest rasa is no. 82), verses of Ramananda Raya and Karnapura, and two
verses of a certain Manohara (274–275); so it was compiled after the meeting with Mahaprabhu. The CC proves this especially
clearly (Madhya 1; Antya 1): the verse “priyah so ’yam,” which entered the “Padyavali,” was composed already by Gaura's
mercy (on the numbering of the verse see above). <!-- F543 F544 F545 -->

### “Nataka-chandrika”
The “Nataka-chandrika” (“Moonlight of Drama”) is a treatise on dramaturgy: Rupa wrote it to bring together the
definitions, examples and subjects of both his plays. Almost all the marks of drama are present in the “Lalita-madhava,”
so the examples for them are almost always taken from it (some hundred and seventy-five times; from the
“Vidagdha-madhava,” only a few). At the beginning Rupa says that he has studied the shastra of Bharata Muni and the fine
“Rasa-sudhakara” of Shingabhupala and sets out the definition of drama very briefly, while for the most part not
accepting the method of the “Sahitya-darpana,” as little suited to practice and contrary to Bharata. <!-- F546 F547 F551 Q132 -->

The book examines the marks of drama; the three kinds of hero (divine, mixed and earthly); the three kinds of plot
(known, invented and mixed); the three kinds of nandi (benediction, obeisance, indication of the subject); the prologue,
the five elements of the plot, the five stages of action, the five junctures (sandhi) with their subdivisions, the
thirty-six ornaments, the kinds of stage speech (aloud, aside, in confidence), the act and the interpolated act
(garbhanka), the rules for Sanskrit and the Prakrits and the four styles (vritti) — with definitions and examples of which
style suits which rasa. There is no concluding verse; the colophon calls the author “Srila Rupa Gosvamipada, the best of
the teachers of the Gaudiya Vaishnava sampradaya, whose poetry is fragrant in the forest of the hearts of sadhus, first
among the dear associates of Bhagavan Chaitanyadeva.” The compilers of one of the biographies saw an old manuscript of the
book copied in Bhaktivinoda Thakura's own hand. <!-- F548 F549 F550 Q133 -->

Besides his own plays and the “Padyavali,” Rupa quotes in the book verses from the “Dasha-rupaka,” the
“Rasa-sudharnava,” the “Kamsa-vadha,” the “Keshava-charita,” the “Vira-charita” and the “Hari-vilasa,” and opinions of
“others,” “some” and “the teachers”; the “Dasha-rupaka” and the “Rasa-sudhakara” he names also in the
“Bhakti-rasamrita-sindhu” and the “Ujjvala-nilamani.” Verses of both plays and of the “Padyavali” are in the
“Nataka-chandrika,” but not one from the “Dana-keli-kaumudi”; so it was written after the two plays and the “Padyavali”
but before the bhanika, the “Bhakti-rasamrita-sindhu” and the “Ujjvala-nilamani.” In the “Bhakti-rasamrita-sindhu”
(4.9.22) Rupa says that the styles, “the mothers of drama,” are described in “the marks of drama” — and Jiva in the
“Durgama-sangamani” explains: “in his own work, the ‘Nataka-chandrika.’” The definition of pravartaka that Kaviraja gives is
also from the “Nataka-chandrika”:

“When the characters appear in the play separately and at the proper time, this is called pravartaka.” (CC, Antya 1.135)

Many definitions from the “Nataka-chandrika” are also cited by the commentary on the “Lalita-madhava” that some attribute
to Jiva Gosvami. <!-- F551 F552 F553 F554 F555 F556 Q134 -->

### “Laghu-bhagavatamrita”
The conclusions that Sanatana set out at length in narrative form in the “Brihad-bhagavatamrita,” Rupa set out briefly in
the “Sankshepa-bhagavatamrita” and, out of humility and in respect for his elder brother's book, called it “the lesser” —
the “Laghu-bhagavatamrita” (so Kaviraja too calls it). It is a special handbook of the concepts of the whole “Bhagavatam”
and the Puranas, in which every conclusion is established by the testimony of shabda. In the introduction Rupa says that
he sets out briefly what “the lotus feet of Shrimat Prabhu,” that is, Sanatana, unfolded in the “Bhagavatamrita”; that this
amrita is twofold — by relation to Krishna and by relation to His bhaktas — and the “Krishnamrita” is served to the friends
first; and that, giving up attachment to lengthy reasoning, he accepts of all the pramanas the chief one, shabda, for Vyasa
in the “Vedanta-sutra” by the sutra “shastra-yonitvat” (1.1.3) recognized the sole authority of revelation and by the sutra
“tarkapratishthanat” (2.1.11) rejected reasoning as having no foundation. <!-- F557 F558 F559 Q135 I39 -->

Four verses of benediction glorify Sri Krishna Chaitanya — the avatara who purifies the Kali-yuga, who bestows love for
Krishna's name and is worshipped in nama-sankirtana together with His associates (“krishna-varnam tvishakrishnam,”
“Bhagavatam” 11.5.32) — the sound of Krishna's flute and Krishna's name: may the maha-mantra of thirty-two syllables that
came from Sri Chaitanya's lips plunge the world into prema. The book is divided into the “Krishnamrita,” an examination of
Krishna's forms and avataras, and the “Bhaktamrita,” on the supreme value of worshipping His intimates and on the grades of
bhaktas. A description of it was printed in the “Gaudiya” (year 18, no. 46). Rupa relies above all on the “Bhagavatam,” and
also on the “Padma,” “Vishnu,” “Brahmanda” and “Skanda” Puranas, the “Vishnu-dharmottara,” the “Brahma-samhita,” the
“Hari-vamsha,” the “Mahabharata,” the “Brahma-sutras,” the “Gita,” the “Gopala-tapani,” the tantras and pancharatras, the
“Brahma-tarka” and the words of Shridhara Svami. <!-- F560 F561 F562 Q136 -->

### Eleven Verses on the Eightfold Lila
According to the “Bhakti-ratnakara” (1.818–819), Rupa, at the wish of a certain Vaishnava, composed eleven verses on the
eightfold (ashta-kaliya) lila — a treasure for the fortunate — and gave them to Krishnadasa Kaviraja to expand. In some
manuscripts these verses are called “Ashta-kalika-shlokavali” or “Smarana-mangala-ekadasham.” In the Baharampur edition of
the “Govinda-lilamrita” they stand at the beginning of the first sarga and open sargas 2, 5, 8, 19, 20 and 21; individual
words of the last two verses coincide with the beginning of the 22nd sarga. Haraprasad Shastri describes a manuscript of
the “Smarana-mangala” of thirty-five verses with the colophon “thus ends the ‘Smarana-mangala,’ the
‘Ashta-kalika-shlokavali’ on Radha-Krishna, composed by Shrimad Rupa Gosvami”; the Bangiya Sahitya Parishad holds a
manuscript of eleven verses. The report of the “Bhakti-ratnakara” is confirmed by Kaviraja himself at the end of the
“Govinda-lilamrita”: he wrote this sequence of the eightfold pastimes of Radha and Her Lord following the path shown by Sri
Rupa (23.54). <!-- F355 F563 F564 F565 F566 F567 Q137 Q138 Q139 -->

### “Samanya-virudavali-lakshana”
Listing Rupa's books, Kaviraja names the “Govinda-virudavali,” and Narahari Chakravarti adds that Rupa also briefly composed
its “marks” (lakshana) but did not include them in the count of books: the example for them is the “Govinda-virudavali”
itself, and the two form one whole. Baladeva Vidyabhushana in the introduction to his commentary on the
“Govinda-virudavali” likewise says that the author himself composed the “marks of a virudavali” and that, having studied
them, the reader will understand the “Govinda-virudavali” fully; there he also sets out how a general virudavali differs
from the “Govinda-virudavali,” and quotes many verses of the “Lakshana.” <!-- F356 F568 F574 Q140 Q141 -->

Rupa begins the book with an obeisance to Krishna — the highest bliss, lord of the forest of Vrindavana — and promises to
set out briefly the marks of a virudavali: it contains kalikas, verses and virudas of various kinds, shines with the
glorification of the hero's fame, might, valour and beauty, has verses at the beginning and end of the kalikas, is free of
faults and is composed in a sonorous, rich style; its composer must be learned, steady of mind and free of envy, and both
composer and reader must be devotees of Krishna. The main content of the book is the marks, kinds and examples of the
kalika, the shloka and the viruda. Rhythmically ordered words are called “kala,” a group of kalas a “kalika” (no more than
sixty-four kalas and no fewer than twelve); syllables are combined by five rules, each with short and long syllables, ten
kinds in all; at the beginning and end of a kalika stand verses on the hero's virtues, and the description of these virtues
poets call a “viruda.” Of earlier authors are named “some,” Bhujageshvara Pingala and one more whose name is unclear; almost
all the examples Rupa took from his own “Govinda-virudavali.” The conclusion of the book: Vasudeva, praised with a fine
virudavali bearing all the marks, rejoices and bestows mercy, but one who glorifies Him with a virudavali lacking the proper
marks Hari does not accept, however well he may recite. Following this book and the “Govinda-virudavali,” Jiva Gosvami
wrote the “Gopala-virudavali,” and Vishvanatha Chakravarti the “Nikunja-keli-virudavali.” <!-- F569 F570 F571 F572 F573 F575 Q142 Q143 -->

### “Upadeshamrita”
The “Upadeshamrita” (“The Nectar of Instruction”) is a little book of eleven verses in which instructions on bhajana, from
the stage of the sadhaka to perfection, are set out in good order. Its first verse:

“That steady person who can restrain the urge of speech, the urge of the mind, the urge of anger, the urge of the tongue,
the urge of the belly and the urge of the genitals can instruct the whole world.” (“Upadeshamrita,” 1)

A similar verse is found in the “Mahabharata,” in the instruction of Brahma, who had taken the form of a golden swan: one
who withstands the urges of speech, mind, anger, the desire to retaliate, the belly and the genitals I consider a true
brahmana and muni (Shanti-parva 305.14). <!-- F576 F579 F580 Q144 Q145 -->

Then, in order: six tendencies hostile to bhakti — overeating or hoarding, excessive endeavour, idle talk, wrong adherence
to rules, association with those averse to God, and fickleness and greed; six favourable ones — enthusiasm, confidence,
patience, performing the limbs of bhakti (hearing, chanting and the rest), giving up bad company and following the conduct
of the sadhus; six kinds of association with sadhus that nourish bhakti; the proper treatment of Vaishnavas of the
beginning, middle and highest stages; the prohibition of looking at a Vaishnava with a worldly eye; the way of remembering
Krishna's name and the rest; the way of bhajana, the place of residence and the conduct of the practitioner; a comparison
of places of bhajana and the pre-eminence of Sri Radha-kunda, and in the last two verses its glory. To restrain the six
urges is the essence of a true tridandi-gosvami. <!-- F578 -->

Rupa's poetics, plays and teaching on the ornaments of speech are prized and discussed by the prakrita-sahajiyas and
ordinary men of letters for the sake of enjoyment, but the “Upadeshamrita” enjoys no such esteem; some even doubt that Rupa
wrote it. The reason is that it teaches the actual practice of pure bhakti — the sacrifice of the six urges and of all
other desires. Yet it is an indispensable beacon on the path of pure bhakti: where it is unknown, ignorance and weakness
obscure the understanding of the essence and practice of bhakti. <!-- F577 F581 I40 I41 -->

Bhaktivinoda Thakura brought the “Upadeshamrita” from Vrindavana and published it in the ninth year of the
“Sajjana-toshani” (1898) with the Sanskrit commentary “Upadeshamrita-prakashika” by Radharamana Dasa Gosvami of the
Radha-ramana temple and his own Bengali commentary, the “Piyusha-varshini-vritti.” At its end he writes that he composed it
for the joy of Shrimad Gosvami Banamali, the happiness of the devotee Prabhunatha and the prosperity of his own abode of
bhajana, as a resident of Sri Godruma, on the bright ashtami in the year 412 of the Gaura era, and dedicates it to “the moon
of Godruma.” The manuscript was given to him by Pandita Banamali Lala Gosvami, who had it copied for the purpose from the
old book collection of his family. The compilers of one of the biographies met him in that collection in Vrindavana on 28
October 1940; he told them that Bhaktivinoda, on reading the verses of the “Upadeshamrita,” was so amazed and delighted that
he placed the manuscript on his head and, trembling with prema, in a faltering voice, said again and again that it would
bring the world immeasurable good. Bhaktivinoda, who considered the book a priceless treasure for the true servants of Hari,
also made a verse rendering of it (“Sri Upadeshamrita-bhasha”), set it to songs in the “Sharanagati,” and in the 10th–11th
years of the “Sajjana-toshani” (1898–1899) wrote, on the basis of the second and third verses, twelve articles on the six
things favourable and the six unfavourable to bhakti. <!-- F582 F583 F584 F585 F590 Q146 -->

One edition with a translation into Brajabhasha (Vrindavana, 1924) attributes the “Upadeshamrita” to Raghunatha Dasa
Gosvami, but Banamali Lala Gosvami firmly confirmed that it is Rupa's work. It is called Rupa's work also by a manuscript
in the collection of Vishvambharananda Deva Gosvami at Gopivallabhapura and by Rajendralal Mitra. Mitra, it is true,
describes under this title an instruction of Rupa to Jiva Gosvami in forty-three verses, with the colophon “thus ends the

### Obeisances to Gaura and Sanatana
An obeisance to Sri Gaurasundara is found in Rupa's books at the beginning of the greater “Ganoddesha-dipika,” in the three
“Chaitanyashtakas,” in the second nandi verse of the “Vidagdha-madhava,” in the opening of the “Lalita-madhava,” in the
“Bhakti-rasamrita-sindhu” (1.1.2), in the “Padyavali” (the verses of the “Shikshashtaka” and, in some manuscripts, other
verses signed “Sri Bhagavatah”) and in the opening of the “Laghu-bhagavatamrita.” Sanatana Rupa remembers in the
“Hamsa-duta” (“Sakara”), in the “Krishna-janma-tithi-vidhi” (“at the command of prabhu”), in the “Gitavali” (his name at
the end of the songs), in the “Lalita-madhava” (1.7), in the “Bhakti-rasamrita-sindhu” (1.1.3, 5), in the “Padyavali”
(“Shrimat-prabhu”) and in the opening of the “Laghu-bhagavatamrita.” For Rupa, Sanatana is an avatara of the Kumaras and
the teacher of the world; and in the opening verses of his books Rupa remembers Gaura, Sanatana and Raghunatha Dasa. <!-- F591 F592 I36 -->

### Works Attributed to Rupa
Besides the books described, attested by Jiva, Kaviraja, Narahari Chakravarti and Krishnadasa Adhikari, many other works
are attributed to Rupa; in Aufrecht's “Catalogus Catalogorum” and some other books certain hymns of Jiva and Raghunatha Dasa
are also wrongly ascribed to him. The catalogue of the Madras Government Oriental Manuscripts Library (1927) lists under
Rupa's name the “Ekanta-nikunja-vilasa” (a hymn on Radha and Krishnachandra in a secluded bower: one who reads it every
night with concentrated mind will gain friendship among Radha's friends), the “Panchashloki” (its first verse is the fifth
verse of the “Upadeshamrita,” its last an entreaty to Krishna that by the mercy of Lalita and others the author may be
allowed to fan Him), the “Premandha-stava” (“though I am a vessel of offences, I await Your mercy — be merciful to me,
Madhava”), the “Ujjvala-chandrika” (a conversation of Radhika with Lalita on the nature of love for Krishna) and the
“Vaishnava-puja-vidhana” — an order of worship from the remembrance of Radha-Krishna and meditation on the siddha-deha to
arati according to the rule of the “Brahmanda-purana” (four times at the feet, twice at the navel, once at the face, seven
times around the whole body) and sprinkling with water from the conch. The colophons of all these manuscripts name Rupa as
the author. <!-- F593 F594 F595 F596 F597 F598 Q148 Q149 Q150 Q151 -->

Rajendralal Mitra describes a “Gangashtaka” and a “Sadhana-paddhati” attributed to Rupa. The “Gangashtaka,” written in the
same metre as the “Yamunashtaka” of the “Stavamala,” is a hymn to Sri Ganga-devi, the daughter of Nityananda Prabhu; the
“Sadhana-paddhati” is a hundred verses with prose on the method of sadhana to Radha-Krishna: to meditate on the guru, a
follower of Shachinandana, His beloved servant, serving him, with his permission to meditate on the earlier teachers, and by
Sri Chaitanya's mercy, the guru's word and the holy name to go to Vraja; its last verse is about Vilasa-manjari. Manuscripts
of a “Sadhanamrita” (in Kathavate's report, 1904) and a “Shiksha-dashaka” (in Roth's Tübingen catalogue) are also
mentioned. The Bengali journal “Sri Nityananda-dayini” (1870s) printed a “Sahasranama-stotra” of Sri Krishna Chaitanya
Mahaprabhu “spoken by Shrimad Rupa Gosvami” — Raghunatha Dasa asks Rupa about the Lord's names, and Rupa tells of the reason
for Gaurasundara's appearance and of His thousand names — as well as a “Navadvipashtaka” (its fruit is rare prema for
Shachinandana's feet) and a “Vrindavanashtaka” (one who lives in Vrindavana free of attachments will attain rati and play
with Him who is clad in yellow). The “Gadadhara-pandita-gosvami-dashaka,” supposedly composed by Rupa, was taken from a
manuscript of the Madras library and printed in the “Gaudiya”; Gopivallabhapura holds copies of an “Upasana-vidhi” (1853)
and a “Prema-samputa” “that came from the lips of Sri Rupa” (1684). Also attributed to Rupa are a
“Hare-Krishna-maha-mantrartha-nirupana,” “Tulasyashtaka,” “Vrinda-devyashtaka,” “Nanda-nandanashtaka,”
“Vrindavana-dhyana,” a “Sri Sanatanashtaka” (where Sanatana is praised as the ornament of the line of the royal brahmanas
of the south, grandson of Mukunda-deva, son of Kumara-deva, elder brother of Rupa and of Vallabha, Jiva's father) and
others; in some catalogues Rupa's well-known books appear under other titles. <!-- F599 F600 F601 F602 F603 F604 F605 F606 F607 F608 Q152 Q153 Q154 -->

The prakrita-sahajiyas, for their part, in order to connect their invented teachings, contrary to the Rupanugas, with
Rupa's name, vainly ascribe to him several Bengali books of recent date; such books are ascribed also to Jiva and to
Krishnadasa Kaviraja, but to the seeker of truth it is clear that the acharyas of the rasa of pure bhakti could not have
written them. The “Pancha-rasikas” and other sahajiyas also tell various legends about Rupa, the founder of the siddhanta of
pure bhakti; the servants of the true Rupanugas accept them with special caution. <!-- F609 F610 I42 -->

### Songs in Praise of Rupa
Songs too were composed about Rupa. In one of them, signed by Radhavallabha, it is sung: no one proclaimed Gaura's moods as
Rupa did; through him the world learned that Vrindavana is the eternal supreme abode, the son of Nanda the source of all
avataras, Radhika the first and most revered of His beloveds, and the sakhis Her retinue; whoever has realized this on the
path of raga has realized it by his mercy. Having received Sri Chaitanya's command and studied the “Bhagavatam,” Rupa drew
from it the treasures of the siddhanta of bhakti, made them into his books and gave living beings the chintamani of prema;
in plays, songs and verses on the rasa of Radha-Krishna he established the pure view of parakiya, established on earth Sri
Chaitanya's innermost desire and tasted its sweetness; and at the end, in separation from Him, he bore the cruellest
anguish — hence his raving and lament. <!-- F611 F612 F613 -->

Two other songs are signed “Madho.” If Rupa had not taken a body in the Kali-yuga, the first sings, who would have opened the
storehouse of the treasure of vraja-prema, who, like a swan, would have separated milk from water, who, giving up
everything, would have worshipped Vrindavana, who would have told of Mathura and Vrindavana, the customs of Vraja, the deeds
of Radha-Madhava and that love? By the mercy of his feet all sing and make others sing. The second glorifies Rupa, the ocean
of the great rasa: his darshana, touch and words are nectar; he is deep, steadfast and merciful, a treasury of
prema-bhakti, who revealed the great gem of ujjvala-prema; he renounced Gauda, is adorned with virtues, gladdens the
learned, lives in Vrindavana, and his fame is pure. <!-- F614 F615 -->

## Departure and Memorial Places
Rupa left this world in Vraja, on the twelfth day of the bright fortnight of the month of Shravana; by our calendar this is
the Gaura-dvadashi of the month of Shridhara, the morning after the Gaura-ekadashi that begins the Jhulana-yatra (on the
years and the disagreement of sources see above, in the section “Years of His Life”). <!-- F616 -->

Raghunatha Dasa poured out his separation from him in the “Prarthanashraya-chaturdashaka”:

“Sri Rupa, my life, ever sprinkled me with the fragrant foam of an unprecedented ocean of prema; and now, by ill fate, at
every step I burn in the fire of calamities and am without support — with whom but him shall I seek shelter?”

“Without him the great pasture is empty, the king of hills is like a python, and the kunda like the jaws of a tiger.”
(“Prarthanashraya-chaturdashaka”) <!-- F617 Q61 -->

Rupa's samadhi temple stands in the courtyard of the Radha-Damodara temple in Vrindavana; there too he performed bhajana
in the last years of his life. When Ramachandra Kaviraja came with Jiva to the Radha-Damodara temple and saw Rupa's samadhi,
he was plunged into such ecstasy that he amazed everyone (“Bhakti-ratnakara,” 9th wave). Another memorial place of Rupa in
Vraja is the place of his bhajana, Teri-kadamba (Ter-kadamba), near Nandagrama. <!-- F618 F619 -->

## Rupa-manjari
In Krishna's eternal pastimes Rupa is Rupa-manjari. The “Gaura-ganoddesha-dipika” says: “She who was formerly called
Rupa-manjari in Vrindavana has now appeared as Rupa Gosvami” (180). Lalita is first among the sakhis of Radharani's
retinue, and Rupa-manjari is first among those who follow Lalita; therefore in Gaura-lila too Rupa is the head of the six
Gosvamis.[^62] Her closest friend is Rati-manjari, whom the wise also call Lavanga-manjari; according to the
“Gaura-ganoddesha-dipika,” she appeared as Sanatana. <!-- F620 F621 F622 F625 -->

Rupa and Sanatana are eternal associates of Krishna in Vrindavana: they came to enrich Gauranga's pastimes and only played
the role of sadhakas striving for perfection. Therefore the words in which the brothers called themselves men of “low
birth,” “consorting with the low” —

“We have disgraced our birth by low deeds and association with base people. We are unworthy even to stand before You.” (CC,
Madhya 1.189)

— are the humility (dainya) of a Vaishnava, and to take them literally is an offence: Rupa and Sanatana are eternal
associates of the Lord, above varnashrama, gurus even for paramahamsas. So Narottama in his songs prays for service under
Rupa-manjari, and Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati considered the dust of Rupa's feet the one desire of his life (see above).
Information about Rupa is also found in the articles on Madhavendra Puri, Nityananda, Gadadhara Pandita, Svarupa Damodara,
Ramananda Raya, Bhugarbha, Gopala Guru, Sundarananda and Vishnupriya. <!-- F623 F624 F626 -->
