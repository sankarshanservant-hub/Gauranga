# Raghunatha Dasa Gosvami
*Raghunatha Dasa Gosvami · @raghunatha-dasa*

## The Friend of Rupa and Sanatana
In the “Laghu-toshani,” his commentary on the Tenth Canto of the “Bhagavatam,” Srila Jiva Gosvami remembers Raghunatha
Dasa as the friend of Rupa and Sanatana, renowned on earth, who ever sports in the waves of the ocean of love for Radha
and Krishna; the best people of the three worlds honour him with wonder as the equal of them both. Vaishnavas call him
simply Dasa Gosvami or Sri Dasa Gosani, and Mahaprabhu Himself gave him the name “Svarupa's Raghu.” <!-- F1 -->

While the Lord was still on earth, Kavi Karnapura, in his drama “Chaitanya-chandrodaya,” put these words about him into
the mouth of Shivananda Sena:

“The teacher Yadunandana, most sweet, the beloved of Sri Vasudeva — his disciple Raghunatha, rich in virtues, dearer
than life to such as me, ever anointed with the abundant mercy of Sri Chaitanya, a follower of Svarupa, the one
treasury of renunciation — to whom among those who live in Nilachala is he unknown? He is a blessed soil, the one
delight of all hearts, which bears fruit without ploughing; on it the tree of love for that Lord, no sooner planted,
at once bore fruit, unequalled.” (“Chaitanya-chandrodaya,” 10.3–4)

The Yadunandana Acharya spoken of here was the beloved of Vasudeva Datta of Kanchanapalli; and Raghunatha himself was
dear to Svarupa Damodara. <!-- F2 Q1 -->

Raghunatha Dasa is one of the six Gosvamis of Vrindavana. Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati, tracing the line of
instruction (shiksha-parampara), leads it from Mahaprabhu to Svarupa Damodara, from him to Rupa and Sanatana, beside
whom stand Raghunatha Dasa, Raghunatha Bhatta and Gopala Bhatta, and onward to Jiva and Krishnadasa Kaviraja.
Bhaktisiddhanta calls Jiva and Raghunatha Dasa the original gurus of all the Rupanugas; Rupa and Raghunatha Dasa are as
one being in two bodies, and Rupa's two best servants are he and Jiva. Among the three great acharyas of Vrindavana,
Sanatana is called the acharya of sambandha, Rupa the acharya of abhidheya, and Raghunatha Dasa the acharya of
prayojana, the highest goal — and that goal, for him, was the feet of Sri Rupa.[^1] <!-- F132 F136 F129 -->

His life is known to everyone who has read the “Chaitanya-charitamrita”: Krishnadasa Kaviraja devoted almost a whole
chapter to it (Antya 6). Gaudiya Vaishnavas have retold it again and again in their journals — the “Sajjana-toshani,”
the “Gaudiya,” the “Nadiya-prakasha” — and in a separate booklet, “Gosvami Sri Raghunatha Dasa.” <!-- F14 -->

## Years of His Life
Srila Bhaktivinoda Thakura printed in the second volume of the “Sajjana-toshani” an article, “Determining the Years of
the Six Gosvamis” — a record found among the papers of a certain Vaishnava; some of its dates he considered certain,
others doubtful. According to it, Raghunatha Dasa was born in 1428 of the Shaka era and lived on earth seventy-six
years; he departed in 1504 Shaka, on the twelfth day of the bright fortnight (shukla-dvadashi) of the month of Ashvina.
The same is reported by an old manuscript from the library of Pandita Banamali Lala Gosvami in Vrindavana (appearance in
1563 of the Vikrama era, that is, 1428 Shaka; departure, “the attainment of the desired,” in 1639 Vikrama, 1504 Shaka)
and by the manuscripts collected by Pandita Vishvambharananda Deva Gosvami at Gopivallabhapura. All three records agree:
Raghunatha Dasa appeared in 1428 Shaka (1507) and departed in 1504 Shaka (1582).[^2] <!-- F3 F4 F5 F6 F84 -->

By our calendar, the day of Raghunatha Dasa Gosvami's appearance is Gaura-panchami (Vasanta-panchami) of the month of
Madhava, and the day of his departure is Gaura-dvadashi of the month of Padmanabha; on that day Vaishnavas honour three
Gosvamis at once — Raghunatha Dasa, Raghunatha Bhatta and Krishnadasa Kaviraja. <!-- F6 -->

## Family and Childhood
Raghunatha appeared in the house of Govardhana Dasa in the village of Sri Krishnapura in Saptagrama (Hooghly district).
The village lies on the eastern bank of the ancient Sarasvati, a little south of the present Adi-Saptagram station:
about a mile from it and about a mile and a half from Trish-Bigha station. Saptagrama itself means “seven villages”;
those named are Saptagrama, Bamshibati, Shivapura, Basudevapura, Nityanandapura and Shankhanagara. In the fifteenth
and sixteenth centuries it was the chief trading port of Bengal on the Ganga; present-day Triveni grew out of it.
<!-- F7 F84 F121 -->

Govardhana and his elder brother Hiranya belonged to the Kayastha community and were respected and very wealthy
landholders — the foremost in all Saptagrama, which then stretched from the little river Bhairava in Jessore almost to
the Rupnarayan. They bore the royal title “Majumdar,” and the yearly revenue of their estates was twelve lakhs of
coins.[^3] Hiranya had no sons, and Raghunatha, Govardhana's only son, was heir to everything. His mother's name has
not come down to us. <!-- F8 F85 F89 -->

In Gaurasundara's time the brahmana scholars of Navadvipa lived on the generosity of these brothers: with their money,
lands and villages they supported themselves and taught. Hiranya and Govardhana stood towards Nilambara Chakravarti,
the Lord's maternal grandfather, as younger brothers, and so Mahaprabhu called them “aja,” “grandfather.”[^4] In earlier
years they had greatly helped Jagannatha Mishra, and they honoured and served Advaita Acharya. <!-- F9 F10 -->

The family priest was Balarama Acharya, and the guru was Yadunandana Acharya; both lived in Chandapura. Yadunandana was an
intimate disciple of Advaita, a devotee of the Lord, favoured by Vasudeva Datta — and it was he who initiated Raghunatha.
Raghunatha's kinsman and uncle Kalidasa was also a devotee of Mahaprabhu; he lived in Shankhanagara — or, by another
account, in the village of Bheda (Bhaduya), some three miles south of Krishnapura and a mile west of the present Bandel
station. <!-- F13 F86 F87 -->

Balarama Acharya was a recipient of Haridasa Thakura's mercy. After converting the harlot whom Ramachandra Khan had sent
to him, the Namacharya left Benapola for Chandapura and lived for some time with Balarama: in a thatched hut, accepting
alms in his house and ceaselessly chanting the Name. Haridasa glorified the holy name in the Chandapura house of
Govardhana and Hiranya as well. The boy Raghunatha, who came to Balarama for his studies, saw Haridasa every day, kept
his company and won his mercy:

“At that time Raghunatha Dasa, the son of Govardhana, was still a boy and studied at school. Every day he came to see
Haridasa Thakura. Haridasa Thakura showed him great mercy. This blessing of a great Vaishnava became the cause of his
attaining the lotus feet of Sri Chaitanya Mahaprabhu.” (CC, Antya 3.168–169)

From childhood he was indifferent to wealth. <!-- F12 F88 Q24 F89 -->

## At the Lord's Feet in Shantipura
Raghunatha first saw Mahaprabhu in Shantipura, where the Lord came after taking sannyasa, and fell at His feet in prema.
Govardhana faithfully served Advaita, and Advaita saw to it that the young man received the remnants of the Lord's meals
for all the days that He stayed in Shantipura:

“Raghunatha Dasa's father always respectfully served Advaita, and Advaita was well disposed towards his whole family.
So Raghunatha stayed in Advaita's house and had the chance for about a week to serve Sri Chaitanya and to taste the
remnants of Mahaprabhu's meals.” (CC, Madhya 16.225–226)[^5]

Raghunatha begged to be accepted as a servant in Nilachala. Advaita, who knew his father by his generous gifts, presented
the young man to the Lord, and the Lord told him to wait until He returned from Vrindavana. Thus, the biographers say,
the Lord, leaving Shantipura for Nilachala, accepted Raghunatha Dasa, as in Ramakeli He had accepted Rupa and Sanatana.
<!-- F90 Q25 F134 -->

Back in Saptagrama, Raghunatha lived in separation from the Lord, and all his attempts to run away failed. His father set
over him a guard of eleven men — five watchmen, four servants and two brahmanas. <!-- F91 -->

In 1513 Mahaprabhu, having gone as far as Kanai-natashala, turned back and came again to Shantipura. Raghunatha
persuaded his father to let him go to Advaita; his father agreed — with a large retinue and on condition that he return
soon. The Lord said to the young man:

“Go back home and be patient. And do not behave like a madman any more. Only gradually, step by step, can one cross the
boundless ocean of worldly existence. Do not make a show of false renunciation, like a monkey. Accept material
enjoyments properly, but do not become attached to them. Keep faith in your heart, and outwardly act like an ordinary
man. And very soon Krishna will free you.” (CC, Madhya 16.237–239)

Raghunatha returned home and began to carry out his duties without attachment, and his parents relaxed their watch.
<!-- F92 Q25 -->

Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati explains: “monkey renunciation” is renunciation for show, displayed for the eyes of
others. True renunciation is the fruit of pure bhakti. Yet to reject what is needed for Krishna's service, thinking it
worldly (phalgu-vairagya), is also a mistake; the right way is yukta-vairagya — to use everything without attachment,
for the sake of service. <!-- I5 -->

## Arrest in Saptagrama
One day misfortune fell on the family. The taxes due to the Shah were collected by a chaudhuri — a Turkish Muslim, a
middleman who kept a quarter of the collection for himself. Hiranya began paying the Shah directly, and the offended
chaudhuri lodged a complaint. The brothers went into hiding, and the minister arrested Raghunatha. The chaudhuri
threatened him, but, looking at his face and wary of the Kayasthas, did not beat him. Raghunatha said to him meekly:
“My father and uncle are like brothers to you, and I am like your son. You know the scriptures; you are almost a saint
(pir).” The chaudhuri wept, released the young man and made peace with his uncle (CC, Antya 6.16–31). <!-- F93 -->

Meanwhile his father was arranging his marriage to a very beautiful girl. But hearing that the Lord had returned from
Vrindavana to Puri, Raghunatha again began to long for Him. A year passed; again and again he tried to run away, and
each time he was caught. His mother asked that he be tied up, but Govardhana answered:

“‘Our son is as rich as Indra, the king of heaven. His wife is as lovely as a heavenly dancer. Yet none of this could
bind his mind or his heart. How then can we hold him by binding him with mere ropes? A father can give birth to a son,
but he cannot undo the results of past deeds. Sri Chaitanya has lavished His causeless mercy upon him. Who can hold back
a madman gone mad for lotus-eyed Chaitanya?’” (CC, Antya 6.39–41) <!-- F94 Q31 -->

## Panihati: The Chida-dadhi Festival
Learning that Nityananda Prabhu was in Panihati, Raghunatha went there. Nityananda was sitting under a tree on the bank
of the Ganga amid His companions; Raghunatha fell flat from afar, and Nityananda, learning who it was, called him over,
placed His feet on his head and announced the “punishment”:

“‘You do not come close, but keep your distance like a thief. But I have caught you and shall punish you today. I want
you to feed all My companions yogurt and flat rice.’ Hearing these words, Raghunatha became inexpressibly happy.” (CC,
Antya 6.50–51) <!-- F95 Q26 -->

So began the festival that to this day is called the “Panihati chida-dadhi mahotsava” — the great festival of flat rice
and yogurt. Raghava Pandita brought his own foods; Gadadhara Dasa, Kamalakara Pippalai and many other devotees came,
brahmanas and countless people. Nityananda and Mahaprabhu, who Himself came there, ate together on the bank of the
Ganga, like the cowherd boys on the bank of the Yamuna. The remnants of Mahaprabhu's and Nityananda's meal Raghava gave
to Raghunatha. (The festival is described in detail in the article on Nityananda.) By our calendar the Panihati festival
is celebrated on Gaura-trayodashi of the month of Trivikrama. <!-- F95 F96 -->

The next morning Raghunatha asked through Raghava how he might gain his freedom. Nityananda again placed His feet on his
head and said:

“‘Because you arranged this great feast on the bank of the Ganga, Sri Chaitanya Himself came here to show you His favour.
Granting you special mercy, He tasted the flat rice with milk. And in the evening, having delighted in the dancing of the
sankirtana, He partook of wonderful prasada. Gaurachandra Himself came to save you. From now on everything that hindered
and bound you is removed by His sweet will. Sri Chaitanya will entrust you to the care and protection of His closest
servant, Svarupa Damodara. Thus you will gain the shelter of His lotus feet. Go home with this assurance. And very soon
you will gain, without any hindrance, the shelter of Sri Chaitanya Mahaprabhu.’” (CC, Antya 6.139–143)

Through Raghava, Raghunatha offered a generous dakshina to Nityananda and His companions and went home. From then on he
no longer entered the inner apartments and slept in the Durga-mandapa, while the guards still watched him.
<!-- F97 Q27 -->

## The Escape
The chance came one night. An hour before dawn his guru Yadunandana Acharya came to Raghunatha: a disciple of his, a
pujari, had abandoned the Deity's service, and Yadunandana asked Raghunatha to persuade him to return. On the way
Raghunatha let his guru go and, as soon as he was alone, turned east. Avoiding the roads, he went through the jungle and
on the very first day covered thirty miles; he spent the night in the cowshed of a milkman, who gave him milk.
<!-- F98 -->

Govardhana sent ten servants after him with a letter to Shivananda Sena, who was leading the Bengalis to Puri. The
servants caught up with Shivananda at Jhankra, but Raghunatha was not among the pilgrims. <!-- F99 -->

He reached Puri in twelve days and in all that time ate only three times. Mukunda Datta announced his arrival to the
Lord. Mahaprabhu embraced him and said:

“Sri Chaitanya said: ‘The mercy of Krishna is mightier than anything in the world. Now the Lord has freed you from the
cesspit of worldly pleasures.’” (CC, Antya 6.193)

And Raghunatha thought to himself: “Not Krishna — it was You who pulled me out.” <!-- F100 Q28 -->

## “Svarupa's Raghu”
The Lord went on, joking about Govardhana and Hiranya:

“Your father and uncle are like worms burrowing in the refuse pit of materialistic pleasures, taking for happiness the
torments caused by the poison of these worldly delights. Although they give charity and help all the brahmanas, they are
not pure Vaishnavas, but ‘almost’ Vaishnavas. Understand that the craving for enjoyment is the cause of great blindness
and of delusion about our true spiritual self. This ignorance entangles one in karma and in the round of endless births
in this world of death. And Krishna has saved you from this mire of worldly pleasures. Who can describe all the might
and glory of Krishna's mercy?” (CC, Antya 6.197–200)

Seeing their attachment to property, the Lord did not hesitate to speak so even of those whom He called “grandfather.”
Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati explains: Krishna's mercy is stronger than karma; the Lord knew that Raghunatha was free
from worldliness, and He spoke of the “refuse pit” to instruct conditioned souls. So it was that the Lord told one
Raghunatha to leave his parents, and the other, Raghunatha Bhatta, to serve his own (on this, see the article about him).
<!-- F101 Q29 F11 I6 F136 -->

Seeing how thin and dusty the young man had grown on the road, the Lord entrusted him to Svarupa Damodara:

“‘I give Raghunatha Dasa wholly into your care. Please accept him as your son or servant. From now on there are three
Raghunathas in My circle. So let this Raghunatha be known as Svarupa Damodara's Raghu.’” (CC, Antya 6.202–203)

So, to distinguish him from Raghunatha Vaidya and Raghunatha Bhatta, he came to be called “Svarupa's Raghu.” The Lord
told Govinda to look after him. Having bathed in the ocean and had darshan of Jagannatha, Raghunatha for five days ate
the remnants of the Lord's meals that Govinda brought him. On the sixth day he stopped taking them: after the evening
offering of flowers (pushpanjali) he would stand at the Lion Gate and beg mahaprasada from the servitors coming out of
the temple. Hearing of this, the Lord was pleased:

“Hearing this, Sri Chaitanya was very pleased and said: ‘He has done rightly in accepting the dharma of renunciation as
his lot. One who has renounced the world can constantly chant the holy names of God. And he can maintain his life by
humbly begging alms. One who has given up everything worldly must not depend on others in anything. Otherwise he will
not succeed on the spiritual path, and Krishna will not grant him His mercy. If one who has renounced the world seeks
to please his tongue, he gradually forgets his spiritual life and becomes a slave of his worldly cravings. The duty of
a renounced monk is to chant the holy names of God unceasingly. He should eat only what comes to him without effort:
vegetables, greens, fruits and roots. But one who everywhere seeks only the pleasure of the tongue, the belly or the
genitals will never attain Krishna.’” (CC, Antya 6.222–227) <!-- F102 Q30 -->

Srila Bhaktisiddhanta observes that the rites of the “Hari-bhakti-vilasa” are prescribed for wealthy householders,
whereas a renunciant who has taken shelter of the holy name has no duty other than kirtana and remembrance (smarana).
<!-- I7 -->

Raghunatha never addressed the Lord directly — he made his requests through Govinda or Svarupa. When Svarupa asked on
his behalf what his duty was, the Lord told him to learn from Svarupa, and Himself gave only a brief instruction:

“Do not listen to worldly talk and do not discuss worldly affairs and news. Do not eat rich food and do not wear costly
clothes. Treat everyone with respect, but expect no respect from others for yourself. Chant the holy names of Krishna
all the time, and in your mind worship the divine couple Sri Sri Radha-Krishna in Vrindavana.” (CC, Antya 6.236–237)

So the young man who had given up wealth like Indra's and a beautiful wife, against the efforts and pleas of his
parents, came to Nilachala by the mercy of Nityananda. Becoming “Svarupa's Raghu” — the ward of Svarupa, Mahaprabhu's
second self — he attained intimate service to Gaurasundara, in whose form Radha and Madhava are united. How Svarupa
guided him is told more fully in the article on Svarupa Damodara. <!-- F103 Q30 F15 -->

## His Father's Money and Alms at the Lion Gate
At Ratha-yatra the Bengali devotees came to Puri. Raghunatha received Advaita's blessing, and Shivananda Sena, on
returning home, told Govardhana how austerely his son lived. The parents sent to Puri a brahmana, two servants and four
hundred rupees. For his father's good Raghunatha began, with this money, to invite the Lord to his place twice a month.
After two years he stopped. The Lord asked Svarupa why, and Svarupa explained: Raghunatha had understood that food bought
with a worldly man's money pollutes the mind and that the Lord had accepted it only out of mercy to him. The Lord
approved:

“‘The food of worldly people pollutes the mind, and in such a polluted state it is simply impossible to remember Krishna
properly. Worldly feasts are in the mode of passion. They can pollute the mind both of the one who invites and of the
one who accepts the invitation. All this time I accepted only Raghunatha's intense eagerness. And it is very good that,
understanding this, he himself stopped inviting Me.’” (CC, Antya 6.278–280) <!-- F104 Q31 -->

Srila Bhaktisiddhanta and Srila Bhaktivinoda explain: a worldly man's money offered to Hari, guru and the Vaishnavas
only increases his worldly prestige, while food from worldly people and sahajiyas pollutes the mind and robs one of the
remembrance of Krishna. Invitations are of three kinds: sattvic — from a pure devotee; rajasic — from a pious
householder of the world; and tamasic — from a sinner. <!-- I8 -->

Later Raghunatha stopped standing at the Lion Gate as well: at noon he would go to the chatra, where alms were
distributed. The Lord explained this too: to stand at the gate, watching for givers, is like a harlot waiting for a
customer, whereas at the chatra, having received one's share, one can give all one's time to the holy name.
<!-- F105 -->

## The Govardhana Shila and the Gunja Garland
The sannyasi Shankarananda Sarasvati brought Mahaprabhu from Vrindavana a stone from Govardhana and a garland of gunja
seeds. For three years the Lord kept and worshipped them: the garland as Radha, the shila as Krishna Himself; He would
press the shila to His head, His eyes and His heart. Then He gave both sacred treasures to Raghunatha. Raghunatha
understood: the Lord was entrusting him to the feet of Radhika and granting him residence by Govardhana. He worshipped
the shila with water and tulasi leaves as Giridhari, and the garland as Gandharvika, the daughter of Vrishabhanu, and
while worshipping fell into trance. The poet Radhavallabha Dasa sings of it so: the Lord gave him the name of Radha and
Krishna, the Govardhana shila and the gunja garland — and thereby, on Govardhana, in Vrajavana, entrusted him to
Radhika's feet. <!-- F106 F15 F73 -->

Raghunatha himself remembers this gift in the “Gauranga-stava-kalpataru”:

“By His mercy He drew me out when I had fallen into the forest-fire of great wealth, and joyfully entrusted me, a
wretch, to His own Svarupa; He gave me the gunja necklace from His breast and the Govardhana stone so dear to Him.
Gauranga, rising in my heart, intoxicates me.” (“Gauranga-stava-kalpataru,” 11)

And in the “Govardhana-vasa-prarthana-dashaka” he addresses Govardhana himself:

“Sri Shachinandana, mercy without condition, Your beloved, has given me over to You, though I am a deceitful rogue;
therefore, not regarding whether I am worthy or unworthy, grant me to live near You, O Govardhana!”
(“Govardhana-vasa-prarthana-dashaka,” 10) <!-- F109 Q32 F34 Q9 -->

After Raghunatha's departure the Govardhana shila was installed at Radha-kunda, in the temple of Gokulananda built by
Vishvanatha Chakravarti. There it is served every day and can be seen to this day. <!-- F106 -->

## Remnants at the Lion Gate
Raghunatha's renunciation grew ever stricter. At night, by the Lion Gate, he gathered the prasada that the vendors threw
away unsold, after the rice had begun to rot. He washed it with water down to the firm core and ate it with salt. Once
Svarupa asked for a little for himself, and when the Lord learned of it, He came in person:

“‘What delicious treats you eat! But why don't you share them with Me?’ Saying this, He forcibly took a handful of that
rice and ate it. When Sri Chaitanya took another handful, Svarupa Damodara snatched the stale rice from Him, saying:
‘It is not fitting for You to eat this.’” (CC, Antya 6.322–323)

(On this episode and the lesson drawn from it, see the article on Svarupa Damodara.) <!-- F108 Q31 -->

For more than twenty-one hours a day Raghunatha chanted the name and remembered the Lord, leaving an hour and a half for
sleep and food. He never touched anything tasty and wore torn cloth and an old blanket. Krishnadasa Kaviraja concludes:

“Can anyone count the innumerable virtues of Raghunatha Dasa? All the principles of spiritual life that he strictly
followed were as if carved in stone.” (CC, Antya 6.309) <!-- F107 F18 Q3 -->

## Keeper of Svarupa's Treasury
Raghunatha lived in Puri under Svarupa's care for sixteen years[^6] — until the very departure of the Lord and Svarupa.
In Mahaprabhu's last pastimes he was Svarupa's right hand. The Vaishnavas came to know the kadcha — Svarupa's record of
the Lord's intimate pastimes — only through him: Svarupa, as Srila Bhaktivinoda writes, had him learn its verses by
heart, and Krishnadasa Kaviraja spread them throughout the world. Thus Svarupa placed the “inner path” of worship in
rasa in the throat of Dasa Gosvami; he preserved it in his books, and Kaviraja in the “Chaitanya-charitamrita” and the
“Govinda-lilamrita.” One stream of Svarupa's rasopasana flows through Raghunatha Dasa to Kaviraja, another through
Vakreshvara to Gopala Guru; and Gopala Guru, by one account, himself studied rasa under Svarupa and Raghunatha Dasa.
<!-- F110 F122 -->

Kaviraja says plainly how he knows of the Lord's last pastimes:

“The pastimes of Sri Chaitanya are like the most beautiful jewels. Svarupa Damodara keeps them in the treasury of his
heart. He bestowed this knowledge on Raghunatha Dasa, and he retold it to me. All that I heard from him I have described
in this book, which I dedicate to all the devotees.” (CC, Madhya 2.84)

“Sri Raghunatha Dasa Gosvami was always near Sri Chaitanya. I write only what I heard from him. Though all these
pastimes seem incredible, I believe in them with all my heart.” (CC, Antya 14.83)

“I personally heard from the lips of Sri Raghunatha Dasa Gosvami what Svarupa Damodara Gosvami told of the wonderful
pastimes of Sri Chaitanya.” (CC, Antya 3.267–268) <!-- Q7 F28 -->

It is clear, too, that Kaviraja unfolded his book from the verses of Raghunatha's two hymns to the Lord, the
“Shachisunu-ashtaka” and the “Gauranga-stava-kalpataru”; in the “Chaitanya-charitamrita” he himself refers to the
latter, saying that Raghunatha has described these pastimes in detail. Besides the chapter devoted to him (Antya 6),
the “Chaitanya-charitamrita” names him among the six Gosvamis, the author's instructors (Adi 1 and 8, Antya 4.236),
among the branches of the Chaitanya tree (Adi 10.91–103), beside Nityananda (Madhya 1.283), in Shantipura (Madhya 16),
beside Rupa in Mathura (Madhya 18.49), among those who left everything for the Lord (Antya 9.71), in Antya 11.9,
14.7–10, 14.72 and in the book's closing summary (Antya 20.112–113); the author repeats his obeisance to him at the end
of many chapters (Adi 17, Madhya 25 and others). <!-- F28 Q33 -->

Krishnadasa Kaviraja was closely bound to Raghunatha Dasa and received from him the material for his book; according to
the “Prema-vilasa,” he also received initiation from him.[^7] Telling of Raghunatha's childhood, Kaviraja praises the
intelligence and practical skill of the Kayasthas — an argument sometimes used when speaking of Kaviraja's own caste.
