# Sri Ranga Puri
*Шри Ранга Пури · @ranga-puri*

## A Disciple of Madhavendra Puri
Sri Ranga Puri was a sannyasi, a disciple of Sri Madhavendra Puri and a godbrother of Sri Ishvara Puri, the guru of
Mahaprabhu. We know little of him: the primary sources tell of only one meeting between him and the Lord — at
Pandharpur, during the Lord’s journey through the South. Yet it was from him that the Lord then heard the news of His
elder brother Vishvarupa. <!-- F2 -->

The “Charitamrita” calls him Sri Ranga Puri, and once simply “Puri”; Kavi Karnapura, in the
“Gaura-ganoddesha-dipika,” calls him Sriman Rangapuri; Narahari Chakravarti, in the “Bhakti-ratnakara,” Rangapuri;
Govinda-deva, in the “Gaura-krishnodaya,” “the noble Ranga Puri.” The biographies call him Sri Rangapuri. <!-- F1 -->

Among the disciples of Madhavendra the biographies list Ishvara Puri, Advaita Acharya, Nityananda, Paramananda Puri,
Brahmananda Puri, Pundarika Vidyanidhi, Raghupati Upadhyaya and others — and among them Sri Ranga Puri. Narahari
Chakravarti says of them:

“Madhavendra Puri is wholly made of the rasa of loving devotion: by remembering his name every perfection is
attained. Sri Ishvara Puri, Rangapuri and all the other disciples of Madhava are intoxicated with the taste of
devotion. The followers of Madhava in Gauda, Utkala and other lands are all devotees of Krishna, given over to loving
service.” (“Bhakti-ratnakara” 5.2272–2274) <!-- F3 F5 Q3 -->

Kavi Karnapura, in the “Gaura-ganoddesha-dipika,” calls Madhavendra the wish-fulfilling tree that grows in Vraja: it
bears four fruits — service, friendship, parental love and conjugal love. Each of his disciples took his own fruit:

“His disciple was the renunciant Sriman Ishvara Puri, who, being the very fruit of conjugal love, manifested
shringara. Advaita manifested both fruits, service and friendship; and Sriman Rangapuri took shelter in parental
love.” (“Gaura-ganoddesha-dipika” 23–24)

Thus Ranga Puri took from Madhavendra’s tree the fruit of vatsalya — parental love. This is a pure rasa, a fruit of
prema, not worldly attachment. Who he is in Krishna’s eternal pastimes Karnapura does not say: he names only the
rasa in which Ranga Puri took shelter. <!-- F4 Q4 -->

The title “Puri” is the name of one of the ten orders of sannyasis, but Ranga Puri was no Mayavadi renunciant seeking
to merge into the impersonal Brahman. He was a Vaishnava sannyasi in the line of Madhavendra, and all his wealth was
love for Krishna: it is not for nothing that the “Bhakti-ratnakara” says he was intoxicated with the taste of
devotion. <!-- F2 F5 -->

Of his worldly life the sources are silent: we know neither his family, nor his varna, nor the years of his life.
According to the “Charitamrita,” he was only staying at a brahmana’s house in Pandharpur and later went on to
Dvaraka; one of the biographies, however, calls him a resident of Pandharpur.[^1] <!-- F6 -->

## A Guest of Jagannatha Mishra
Once, before the Lord’s appearance, Ranga Puri came with his guru to Navadvipa, to Mayapur, and accepted alms in the
house of Jagannatha Mishra. One of the biographies places this visit at the time when Madhavendra came to Gauda and
initiated Advaita Acharya. The mistress of the house, Shachi, cooked for the sannyasis a ghanta of banana flowers
(mocha-ghanta), and many years later, at Pandharpur, Ranga Puri recalled it in conversation with the Lord:

“Sri Madhavendra Puri’s disciple Sri Ranga Puri had come to the town of Nadia before. He had taken alms in the house
of Jagannatha Mishra and had eaten there a wonderful ghanta of banana flowers. Jagannatha’s brahmani was a great and
chaste wife; in her motherly affection she was like the mother of the world. In cooking she was so skilled that there
was none like her in the three worlds, and she fed the sannyasis with the affection she had for her sons.” (CC, Madhya
9.295–298)

The “Charitamrita” does not call her by name — only “Jagannatha’s brahmani” — but it is, of course, Shachi. “Like
the mother of the world” is a comparison on Ranga Puri’s lips: he speaks of the motherly love with which she fed the
sannyasis as her own sons. Shachi herself is the eternal mother of the Lord, the Yashoda of Krishna’s pastimes.

## The Meeting at Pandharpur
Pandharpur stands on the river Bhima, in the Sholapur district (formerly the Bombay Province, now the state of
Maharashtra), thirty-eight miles west of the town of Sholapur. Here resides Vitthala, or Vithoba, a four-armed form of
Narayana especially dear to the Marathas. Later the celebrated Vaishnava saint Tukaram lived here.[^2] So, according
to one of the biographies, Srila Bhaktisiddhanta describes Pandharpur in his “Anubhashya” on this chapter of the
“Charitamrita.” <!-- F10 Q6 -->

In the brief summary of the pastimes with which the Madhya-lila opens, Krishnadasa Kaviraja mentions this meeting too:

“Mahaprabhu met Sri Ranga Puri, and freed the vipra Ramadasa from his inner grief.” (CC, Madhya 1.113)

Srila Bhaktisiddhanta, in his “Anubhashya,” makes it a subheading: “The meeting with Sri Rangapuri.” <!-- F11 Q1 -->

Travelling through the South, the Lord came to Pandharpur:

“From there Gaurachandra came to Pandharpur, and seeing Vitthala Thakura He was filled with joy. Absorbed in prema,
He sang and danced a great deal. There a brahmana invited Him, and with great affection gave the Lord His meal; and
after the meal the Lord heard there a piece of good news: a disciple of Madhava Puri named Sri Ranga Puri was resting
in a brahmana’s house in that same village. Hearing this, the Lord went to see him, and found him seated in the
brahmana’s house.” (CC, Madhya 9.282–286)

Seeing Ranga Puri, the Lord, overwhelmed with love, fell flat before him like a rod, and the signs of prema appeared
in His body — tears, hair standing on end, trembling and perspiration (CC, Madhya 9.287). The Lord Himself, the Master
of all, is above every guru; but to set an example for the world He, a sannyasi and the disciple of Ishvara Puri,
bowed down before His guru’s godbrother — and so taught us to honour the guru-varga, the circle of one’s elders.
<!-- F12 F13 F14 Q2 I2 -->

“Sri Ranga Puri was astonished at the sight and said: ‘Rise, Sripada! Sripada, You hold a relation with my Gosani;
without that, the fragrance of such prema is found nowhere else.’” (CC, Madhya 9.288–289)

Ranga Puri speaks of fragrance: apart from the connection with his Gosani, there is not even the scent of such prema
anywhere. From the love alone that showed itself in the Lord’s body he understood that the Lord belonged to his
guru’s line. <!-- F15 F16 -->

Why so? One of the biographies explains it — apparently following Srila Bhaktisiddhanta. Before Madhavendra Puri, up
to his guru Lakshmipati Tirtha, Krishna was worshipped in this succession without Sri Radha. With Madhavendra there
descends into the world love for Krishna in separation — vipralambha — rooted in exclusive service to Sri Radha. That
is why the “Charitamrita” calls him the first sprout of the tree:

“Glory to Sri Madhava Puri, brimming with love for Krishna! He is the first sprout of the wish-fulfilling tree of
bhakti.” (CC, Adi 9.10)

Only a devotee in whom a taste for this prema has awakened (jata-ruchi), and who is bound to Madhavendra by dear ties,
has the right to it. There is no reproach to the earlier acharyas in these words: they are Vaishnavas, and their
line, the Brahma-Madhva sampradaya, is our sampradaya. The point is different: such prema was first revealed in this
line through Madhavendra — and it is recognised by its fragrance. <!-- F19 Q5 I1 -->

“Saying this, he raised the Lord and embraced Him, and with their arms around each other’s necks the two wept. After
a moment they put aside their ecstasy and grew composed, and the Lord told him of His relation with Ishvara Puri. A
wondrous flood of prema welled up in them both; each honouring the other, the two sat down in bliss.” (CC, Madhya
9.290–292)

One of the biographies adds that on learning that the Lord was a disciple of Ishvara Puri, his godbrother, Ranga
Puri was filled with great tenderness towards Him. <!-- F17 F18 -->

For five or seven days, day and night, they spoke together of Krishna.[^3] Once Ranga Puri playfully asked the Lord
where He was born, and the Lord, just as playfully, answered: “Navadvipa” (CC, Madhya 9.293–294). Ranga Puri was
delighted: he had been to Navadvipa — and it was then that he recalled the house of Jagannatha Mishra and the cooking
of his wife. <!-- F20 F21 -->

## The News of Vishvarupa
Remembering that house, Ranga Puri added:

“One worthy son of hers had taken sannyasa; his name was Shankararanya, and he was young in years.” (CC, Madhya 9.299)

And here, in this very holy place, he said, Shankararanya had attained siddhi — perfection (9.300). According to one
of the biographies, it was Ranga Puri who first told Mahaprabhu of the siddhi of Shankararanya — Srila Vishvarupa.
<!-- F22 F23 -->

Shankararanya is the sannyasa name of Vishvarupa, the Lord’s elder brother. And he is no ordinary man: according to
the “Gaura-ganoddesha-dipika” (58), Vishvarupa is Baladeva, the vyuha Sankarshana. His “siddhi,” therefore, is not
death but disappearance: he withdrew from the Lord’s manifest pastimes.[^4] So too Srila Bhaktisiddhanta speaks of it.
In his “Anubhashya” he explains that Shankararanya, one of the branches of Mahaprabhu’s tree (CC, Adi 10.106), is
Vishvarupa and that he departed at Pandharpur. In his “Gaudiya-bhashya” on the “Chaitanya-bhagavata,” he says that
Shankararanya, wandering, came to Pandharpur and on the bank of the Bhima entered samadhi, and that many years later,
in 1511, Mahaprabhu learned of this from Sri Ranga Puri. And Srila Bhaktisiddhanta’s journal “The Harmonist” (1928)
writes that at Pandharpur the Lord heard from the lips of Sri Rangapuri of the disappearance of His elder brother
Vishvarupa, known as Shankararanya.[^5] <!-- F27 F28 F29 -->

The Lord replied:

“The Lord said: ‘In My purva-ashrama he was My brother; Jagannatha Mishra, in My purva-ashrama, was My father.’” (CC,
Madhya 9.301)

Purva-ashrama means “the former ashrama”: so a sannyasi speaks of his life before sannyasa. It is no renunciation of
eternal relationships: Jagannatha Mishra and Shachi are the Lord’s eternal parents, the Nanda and Yashoda of Krishna’s
pastimes, and Vishvarupa is Baladeva.[^6] <!-- F24 -->

## The Parting
“Thus the two of them spoke together of what was dear to them, and then Sri Ranga Puri set out to see Dvaraka.” (CC,
Madhya 9.302)

The Lord, at the request of the brahmana who had sheltered Him, stayed on in Pandharpur another four days: He bathed
in the Bhima and went to have darshan of Vitthala, and then went on to the river Krishna-venva (CC, Madhya
9.303–304). <!-- F25 -->

Govinda-deva, in the “Gaura-krishnodaya,” tells of this meeting more briefly and in his own way. He calls Pandharpur
Pandava-grama:

“Seeing there the Supreme Lord named Vitthala, taking alms for a few days in a brahmin’s house, singing ‘Krishna,
Krishna’ with deep longing, He met the noble Ranga Puri. The disciple of Madhavendra, running up with love, embraced
Sri Gauranga tightly; and there, in talks of prema and well-being, he told of how Vishvarupa had attained perfection.
Having spent [the night] with him reverently, in the morning He bowed to him and left.” (“Gaura-krishnodaya”
10.46–48) <!-- F26 -->

## His Later Life
What became of Ranga Puri afterwards the primary sources do not say. The biographies pass on only the opinion of some:
that later he came to the Lord in Jagannatha Puri and spent the rest of his life in Sri Purushottama-kshetra, in His
company.[^7] Neither the year, nor the day, nor the place of his departure is known; nor are the days of his
appearance and disappearance found in the Vaishnava calendar of the Sri Chaitanya Saraswat Math. <!-- F30 F31 -->

## Significance
The meeting at Pandharpur reveals two truths. Pure prema is known by its fragrance: from the signs of love in the
Lord’s body alone Ranga Puri understood that He was connected with Madhavendra — for love in separation, founded on
service to Sri Radha, descends into the world through Madhavendra, and those connected with him have the right to it.
And the Lord Himself, the Master of all, in the guise of a sannyasi fell down before His guru’s godbrother — so He
teaches us to honour the guru-varga. <!-- I1 I2 -->

Ranga Puri, who took from Madhavendra’s tree the fruit of parental love, brought Mahaprabhu the news of His elder
brother: from him the Lord learned that Vishvarupa had disappeared at Pandharpur. <!-- F4 F16 F23 -->
