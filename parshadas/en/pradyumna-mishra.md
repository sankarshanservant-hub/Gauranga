# Pradyumna Mishra
*Прадьюмна Мишра · @pradyumna-mishra*

## A Brahmana of Orissa
Among the devotees of Nilachala whom Sri Chaitanya regarded as His own was the brahmana householder Pradyumna Mishra.
Krishnadasa Kaviraja calls him Pradyumna Mishra, and in the story about him simply Mishra; Vrindavana Dasa calls him
Sri Pradyumna Mishra; Murari Gupta, Srila Pradyumna Mishra; Kavi Karnapura in the “Gaura-ganoddesha-dipika,” “the
Mishra named Pradyumna.” According to one reading of the “Charitamrita” he also bore the name Dasa; of this, more
below. <!-- F1 -->

He was born in a brahmana family in Utkala, that is, in Orissa. So writes Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati, both in the
“Anubhashya” on the “Charitamrita,” where he calls him an Oriya brahmana, a resident of Orissa, and in the
“Gaudiya-bhashya” on the “Chaitanya-bhagavata”; and so too hold the biographies. According to other accounts he came
from Shrihatta (Sylhet) and only later settled in Orissa for good.[^1] He was a householder (*grihastha*) of noble
family and high standing, righteous and exemplary in his life. Yet when he spoke with the Lord he called himself “a
fallen and sinful family man” and “a brahmana who lives on alms”: this is a Vaishnava's humility, not an account of his
worldly affairs. <!-- F2 F3 F7 Q14 -->

## “Foremost among the Vaishnavas”
Krishnadasa Kaviraja counts Pradyumna Mishra among the branches of the tree whose root and trunk is Sri Chaitanya
Himself:

“Then come Kashi Mishra, Pradyumna Mishra and Bhavananda Raya, whose company gave the Lord great joy.” (CC, Adi 10.131)
<!-- F5 Q6 -->

The Lord met him when He returned to Puri from His journey through South India: “He showed mercy to Kashi Mishra and met
Pradyumna Mishra and others” (CC, Madhya 1.129). Kavi Karnapura in his mahakavya paints this first meeting thus: “A
certain fortunate man of abundant merit, an ocean of good fortune, named Pradyumna Mishra, joyfully made the pair of
Gaurachandra's lotus feet the guest of his eyes.” And from that one glance alone the Lord's mercy poured upon him: a
stream of tears burst from his eyes, as from a rain cloud in the month of Shravana (“Chaitanya-charita-mahakavya”
13.70–71). <!-- F13 -->

At that time Sarvabhauma Bhattacharya was presenting the Vaishnavas of Puri to the Lord. In Karnapura's drama
“Chaitanya-chandrodaya” he says: “This is Pradyumna Mishra, this is Vishnudasa” (act 8). And in the “Charitamrita”
Sarvabhauma introduces him thus:

“This is Pradyumna Mishra, foremost among the Vaishnavas. He is Jagannatha's chief cook and is known by the name Dasa.”
(CC, Madhya 10.43)[^2] <!-- F6 Q4 -->

Vrindavana Dasa, listing the Lord's associates who were born in Orissa, puts him first:

“Among them were Pradyumna Mishra, love divine embodied, and the two great steadfast souls, Paramananda and Ramananda.”
(CB, Antya 3.184)

Of the devotees of Nilachala who one after another recognized Sri Chaitanya as the Lord of their life, he writes:

“Sri Pradyumna Mishra was an ocean of love for Krishna; to him Sri Gaurasundara gave His own feet.” (CB, Antya 5.211)[^3]

Thus the Lord gave him His feet: He took him under His shelter as His servant. <!-- F8 Q5 F9 Q2 -->

Every year, when Advaita Acharya and the Gaudiya devotees came from Bengal for the Ratha-yatra, the Lord went out with
His associates to meet them. Among those who went with Him Vrindavana Dasa names “Sri Pradyumna Mishra, foremost in
prema-bhakti” (CB, Antya 8.57);[^4] and Murari Gupta too, in his list of those who went out to meet them, remembers
“Srila Pradyumna Mishra” (Murari Gupta's “Karcha,” 4.17.19). <!-- F10 Q3 -->

Pradyumna Mishra welcomed the Lord also when He returned to Puri after going as far as Kanai Natashala on the way to
Vrindavana:

“The delighted devotees came to meet Him, and the Lord embraced them all with love. Kashi Mishra, Ramananda, Pradyumna,
Sarvabhauma, Vaninatha, Shikhi and all the other devotees came.” (CC, Madhya 16.253–254)[^5]

And when the Lord came back from Vrindavana, at Narendra-sarovara He was again met by Kashi Mishra, Pradyumna Mishra,
Damodara Pandita, Haridasa Thakura, Shankara Pandita and others (CC, Madhya 25.222). <!-- F14 Q13 F15 -->

## The Entering of Gaurahari
Kavi Karnapura writes in the “Gaura-ganoddesha-dipika”:

“The appearance (*avirbhava*) of Gaurahari was in Nakula Brahmachari; and His entering (*avesha*) is likewise to be
known in the Mishra named Pradyumna.” (“Gaura-ganoddesha-dipika” 73–74)

Avesha is a special entering of the Lord's power into His devotee. The devotee does not thereby become God; he remains
His servant, and through him the Lord reveals Himself as He wishes. The biographies take this verse as spoken of
Pradyumna Mishra. But Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati applies it to another Pradyumna — Pradyumna Brahmachari, whom
the Lord named Nrisimhananda.[^6] <!-- F4 Q1 -->

## “The Life of Pradyumna Mishra”
Vrindavana Dasa opens one of the chapters of the “Chaitanya-bhagavata” thus:

“Glory to the Lord, the very life of Sri Pradyumna Mishra! Glory to Him who became the treasure of the heart of Sri
Paramananda Puri!” (CB, Adi 14.2)[^7]

In this chapter the Lord, still living in Navadvipa as a householder, shows the exemplary conduct of a Vaishnava
grihastha. That is why, explains Srila Bhaktisiddhanta in the “Gaudiya-bhashya,” Vrindavana Dasa remembers here
Pradyumna Mishra — the ideal householder, who worshipped the Lord as his Deity. <!-- F11 Q7 -->

Nobility, learning and a householder's righteous life are not yet the goal in themselves. The Lord wished all this to
find its meaning in the service of Hari and His devotees, and so He sent Pradyumna Mishra to Srila Ramananda Raya — not
a brahmana by birth, but the finest teacher of bhakti-rasa, a Vaishnava acharya. Pradyumna Mishra heard him as a
disciple and received the causeless mercy of the Lord. This is the subject of the fifth chapter of the Antya-lila of
the “Charitamrita.” <!-- F12 Q12 I1 -->

## The Story of Pradyumna Mishra
Listing the pastimes of the Lord's last years, Krishnadasa Kaviraja says:

“Telling Pradyumna Mishra of Ramananda's qualities, the Lord had him hear talks about Krishna from Ramananda.” (CC,
Madhya 1.264)

And at the end of the book, recalling the contents of its chapters, he says that in the fifth chapter of the Antya-lila
is “the Lord's mercy to Pradyumna Mishra, whom He sent to hear talks of Krishna from Ramananda Raya” (CC, Antya 20.110).
The chapter itself tells it thus. <!-- F16 -->

One day Pradyumna Mishra came to the Lord, bowed at His feet and humbly asked:

“Hear me, O Lord: I am a wretched, lowly householder. By what fortune have I obtained Your feet, so rarely attained? I
wish to hear talks about Krishna. Be kind to me and tell me about Krishna.” (CC, Antya 5.5–6) <!-- F17 -->

The Lord, Himself the source of all talk of Krishna, answered as though He had no right to speak of the confidential
rasas:

“The Lord said, ‘I know nothing of talks about Krishna. Only Ramananda knows them, and I hear them from his lips. It is
your good fortune that your mind longs to hear about Krishna. Go to Ramananda and hear from him.’” (CC, Antya 5.7–8)

And He added that whoever has awakened a taste for talks of Krishna is truly fortunate, for without that taste, as the
“Bhagavatam” says, even the most flawless performance of duty is only wasted labour (CC, Antya 5.9–10; Bhag. 1.2.8).
Srila Bhaktisiddhanta explains: the Lord only pretends to be ignorant. He sends Pradyumna Mishra, a brahmana by birth
(*shaukra*, “by seed”), as a disciple to Ramananda, who was not a brahmana by birth but was the guru of all varnas and
ashramas. <!-- F18 -->

Mishra went to Ramananda Raya's house. A servant seated him and asked him to wait: the master was busy and would soon
come out. Ramananda was in the garden, in a secluded place.[^8] He had written a drama in Sanskrit, the
“Jagannatha-vallabha,” to be staged before Sri Jagannatha, and was now teaching its songs and dance to two young
devadasis — maidens dedicated to the service of Jagannatha (in Orissa they are still called *mahari*). He taught them
to convey in dance and acting the feelings of the gopis, the deep meaning of every song, so that the dance would become
an offering to Jagannatha. <!-- F19 F20 -->

Srila Bhaktivinoda Thakura explains what this service was. The maidens were to play the principal gopis, and Ramananda
saw in them those who are to be served (*sevya-buddhi*), and himself as a maidservant following them. Knowing himself
in his spiritual form to be a maidservant of Srimati Radhika, he himself bathed and adorned the girl who played Her role
and taught both of them songs and service. His mind and body remained unmoved through it all, “like wood or stone” (CC,
Antya 5.19). This is no worldly affair and no example to imitate: such service is beyond the power even of great sages
who have conquered their senses, and the Lord Himself will say of it, “Ramananda alone has this qualification.” While
Ramananda served in this way, no one dared to interrupt him. <!-- F21 -->

When the servant told him that Pradyumna Mishra had come, Ramananda at once hurried to his guest, bowed respectfully and
humbly said:

“You have been here a long while, and no one told me. I have committed an offence at your feet. Your coming has purified
my home. Command me — what shall I, your servant, do?” (CC, Antya 5.29–30)

Mishra replied that he had come only to see him and now felt that his heart had become purer (CC, Antya 5.31). The hour
was already late, and, saying nothing more, Mishra took his leave and went home (CC, Antya 5.32). <!-- F22 F23 -->

The next day he came to the Lord, and the Lord asked whether he had heard about Krishna from Ramananda. Mishra told Him
what Ramananda Raya had been doing (CC, Antya 5.33–34).[^9] <!-- F24 -->

Then the Lord — the model of humility, who seeks no honour for Himself but honours others — began to glorify His
devotee's love for Krishna as greater than His own:

“I am a sannyasi and consider Myself detached; yet, to say nothing of seeing a woman, if I merely hear a woman's name, My
body and mind are disturbed. Who can remain steady on seeing a woman? Hear, all of you, about Ramananda Raya — an
account so wondrous that it can hardly be told. First, they are devadasis, maidservants of the Deity, and moreover they
are beautiful young women; yet he himself performs every service for them. <…> Yet the mind of Raya Ramananda remains
unmoved as he teaches them to express the stirrings of many different bhavas. His body and mind are unmoved, like wood
or stone. How wonderful — at the touch of a young woman his mind is unmoved! Ramananda alone has this qualification;
from it I know that his body is transcendental.” (CC, Antya 5.35–38, 40–42) <!-- F25 Q8 Q9 -->

The nature of such a mind, the Lord went on, is known to Ramananda alone; but from the effect one may judge the cause.
Whoever hears with faith of Krishna's rasa dance with the maidens of Vraja is freed from lust, the disease of the heart,
and attains pure love (Bhag. 10.33.39). A mind established in pure goodness beyond the gunas is untouched by the
mundane; and one who serves Krishna in love day and night is an eternally perfect soul. Therefore Ramananda's body is not
of matter — it is made of consciousness and bliss, and so this great devotee (*maha-bhagavata*) is fit to speak of
Krishna:

“I know that Raya's worship follows the path of raganuga. His body is like a perfected spiritual body (*siddha-deha*),
and so his mind is not mundane.” (CC, Antya 5.51) <!-- F26 -->

And the Lord sent Mishra to Ramananda once more:

“I too hear about Krishna from Raya. If you wish to hear, go there again. Tell him in My name: ‘He has sent me to hear
about Krishna from you.’ Go quickly, while he is still in the hall.” (CC, Antya 5.52–54) <!-- F27 -->

Mishra went at once. Ramananda received him with obeisances and asked what he could do for him; hearing that the Lord
Himself had sent his guest, he counted it his great fortune: “Where else could I find such great fortune?” (CC, Antya
5.55–58). He took Mishra to a secluded room and asked what he wished to hear. <!-- F28 -->

Mishra asked him to tell, in order, all that Ramananda had once spoken of with the Lord at Vidyanagara in the south —
sambandha, abhidheya and prayojana: the soul's relationship with Krishna, the path to Him, and the goal, divine love. And
he added:

“You are the instructor not only of others but of the Lord Himself! I am a poor mendicant brahmana, and you are my
sustainer. I do not know how to ask anything, good or bad. Seeing how wretched I am, be merciful and tell me of your own
accord.” (CC, Antya 5.61–62)

Ramananda began to speak of Krishna in due order, as he had once spoken to the Lord Himself, and in his words the
nectar-ocean of rasa surged up (CC, Antya 5.63). <!-- F29 -->

He himself raised the questions and himself answered them. The hour of the midday meal passed, and still the talk did
not end; speaker and listener, absorbed in love for Krishna, forgot themselves and did not notice that the whole day had
gone by. Only when the servant reminded them that the sun was setting did Ramananda fall silent (CC, Antya 5.64–66).
<!-- F30 -->

With deep respect he bade his guest farewell, and Mishra, as he left, cried, “My life is fulfilled!” and began to dance.
At home he bathed, took prasada, and in the evening came to the Lord (CC, Antya 5.67–68). <!-- F31 -->

His desire fulfilled, he bowed at the Lord's feet in great joy and told of his good fortune:

“Mishra said, ‘O Lord, You have fulfilled my life! You have plunged me into the nectar-ocean of talks about Krishna. The
words of Ramananda Raya cannot be retold. Raya is not a mere human being; he is made of the rasa of devotion to
Krishna.’” (CC, Antya 5.70–71)

On this verse Srila Bhaktisiddhanta writes that to see a mortal man in a guru who sings of Krishna is forbidden. Mishra
also passed on to the Lord the words of Ramananda himself: “Gaurachandra Himself speaks these words through my mouth. As
He makes me speak, so I speak, like a vina” (CC, Antya 5.73) — and concluded:

“You have made me drink such rasa; birth after birth I have sold myself at Your feet.” (CC, Antya 5.76)

The Lord answered with fresh praise of Ramananda's humility: great souls never glorify themselves and ascribe their own
words to others (CC, Antya 5.77–78). <!-- F32 -->

## Why the Lord Sent Mishra to Ramananda
Kaviraja himself explains the meaning of this pastime:

“Though a householder, Raya is not under the control of the six enemies (*shad-varga*); though seemingly a worldly man
(*vishayi*), he instructs sannyasis. To reveal all these qualities of his, the Lord sent Mishra there to hear. <…> To
destroy the pride of the sannyasis and panditas, He reveals dharma through a lowly shudra. He speaks of bhakti, prema and
the truth (*tattva*), making Raya the speaker, while He Himself, together with Pradyumna Mishra, becomes the listener.”
(CC, Antya 5.80–81, 84–85) <!-- F33 Q10 -->

Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati explains in the “Anubhashya.” To the worldly eye Ramananda seemed a householder on the
path of worldly action, a man absorbed in mundane affairs (*vishayi*). But his mind, established in pure goodness,
always worshipped the pastimes of Krishna: he is “vishayi” only in the sense that his whole world is Krishna, and he is
able to turn to Krishna even sannyasis who have renounced the world and seek the impersonal. In the world a brahmana is
the guru of the three other varnas, and a sannyasi the guru of brahmanas and of the three other ashramas. To crush
their pride in their position, the Lord, through the mouth of Ramananda, whom the world knew as a “shudra” and a
“grihastha,” instructed Pradyumna Mishra, a brahmana by birth, and Himself, a sannyasi, accepted the dharma that
Ramananda preached. <!-- F34 Q11 -->

So the Lord, wishing to reveal to the world Ramananda's spiritual nature and greatness, showed his power to all through
Pradyumna Mishra. And the lesson of this pastime is plain: neither worldly varna, nor ashrama, nor learning gives the
right to teach true dharma; he teaches in whom there is love for Krishna. The pride of brahmana and sannyasi in their
position is crushed when a brahmana by birth and the Lord Himself in the robes of a sannyasi humbly listen to one whom
the world takes for a “shudra” and a householder. <!-- I2 I3 -->

## In the Memory of the Vaishnavas
The “Vaishnava-vandanas” also preserve Pradyumna Mishra's name. Devakinandana Dasa writes: “I glorify Sri Pradyumna
Mishra and Raya Bhavananda” (66);[^10] the “Vaishnavabhidhana” names him too (36). <!-- F35 -->

Nothing more is known of his life. Neither the original sources nor the biographies speak of his years, or of when and
where he left this world; nor are his days of appearance and disappearance found in the Vaishnava calendar of the Sri
Chaitanya Saraswat Math. <!-- F36 -->

## Significance
Pradyumna Mishra entered the “Charitamrita” above all as a listener. He did not judge Ramananda by externals:
Ramananda's service to the devadasis was performed in the spiritual body of a maidservant of Sri Radha, and the Lord,
hearing of it, did not condemn but glorified him, revealing that his body is not mundane. Mishra himself teaches a
disciple's humility: he goes to the one the Lord pointed out, calls himself fallen, does not presume to choose his own
questions and asks to be told “in order,” and having heard, gives his heart to the Lord's feet and returns to Him to
tell of the mercy received. A noble Oriya brahmana, “foremost among the Vaishnavas,” “foremost in prema-bhakti,” he
showed how high position in the world finds its meaning when it bows before a Vaishnava. <!-- I4 -->
