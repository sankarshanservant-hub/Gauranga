# Shyamananda Prabhu
*Shyamananda Prabhu · @shyamananda*

## The Third of the Three Acharyas
When the associates of Sri Chaitanya had one by one left this world, His message was carried on by three disciples of
Srila Jiva Gosvami: Srinivasa Acharya, Narottama Dasa Thakura and Shyamananda Prabhu. They were trained together in
Vrindavana, sent together to Gauda with the books of the Gosvamis, and afterwards honoured together, by householders and
renunciants alike, as acharyas of the whole Gaudiya sampradaya. Srinivasa remained in Bengal, Narottama went north to
Kheturi, and to Shyamananda fell Orissa — Utkala and the lands of Medinipur along the Bengal border. Narahari
Chakravarti, the biographer of all three, calls upon them together as he closes his “Namamrita-samudra”, a garland of
prayers to the Lord's associates:

“O Srinivasa! Narottama! Shyamananda! Be merciful to me together with your followers — I am very wicked.”
(“Namamrita-samudra” 270)
<!-- F85 F86 F96 -->

He bore several names, each marking a season of his life. At home he was called Duhkhi, “the wretched one”; his guru,
Hridaya Chaitanya of Ambika, gave him the name Krishnadasa at initiation, and from then on he was known as Duhkhi
Krishnadasa; and in Vrindavana Sri Jiva named him Shyamananda, “the joy of Shyama”. So he is called in the
“Bhakti-ratnakara” and the “Prema-vilasa”, and the biographies call him Sri Shyamananda Prabhu. The “Shyamananda-shataka”,
a hundred verses composed about him by his disciple, calls him “the beloved disciple of Hridayananda” and “the
crest-jewel of the kings of rasikas” (*rasikendra-mastaka-mani*)[^1]. His life is told in the “Bhakti-ratnakara”, the
“Prema-vilasa”, the “Narottama-vilasa”, the “Shyamananda-prakasha”, the “Abhirama-lilamrita” and other books, and in the
songs of Narahari Chakravarti. According to the biographies, he left no writings of his own. <!-- F1 F84 -->

## Family and Birth
Shyamananda's father was Sri Krishna Mandala and his mother Durika. The “Bhakti-ratnakara” relates:

“He lived in the village of Dandeshvara, prosperous in every way; his mother was Sri Durika, his father Sri Krishna
Mandala. The best of the Sadgopa caste and most virtuous, [the father] saw his all in Krishna and greatly loved the
devotees. The virtues of Sri Krishna Mandala and Durika cannot be described for fear of the bulk of the book. Formerly
[the family] lived at Dharenda-Bahadurpura; respectable people say that Shyamananda was born there.”
(“Bhakti-ratnakara” 1.351–354)

So, by the primary source, the family's home was Dandeshvara, while he was born, as people say, at Dharenda-Bahadurpura,
where the family had lived before. Dharenda-Bahadurpura lies near the present Kharagpur station in Medinipur, and
Dandeshvara on the river Suvarnarekha; in those days the whole region belonged to the kingdom of Orissa. Krishna
Mandala's ancestors, according to the biographies, came from Bengal, from Gauda, and the parents themselves were
deeply devoted[^2].
<!-- F6 F7 Q1 -->

The family were Sadgopas, cultivators and cowherds, of the castes from whose hands a brahmana may accept water
(*jala-chala*)[^3]. But one of the biographies at once adds that a Vaishnava stands above mundane qualities and may be
born in any family, and that to judge him by birth is to ruin oneself. The “Padma Purana” counts among those of hellish
nature the one who sees stone in the Deity of Vishnu, a mere man in the guru, and caste in a Vaishnava. In the
“Itihasa-samuchchaya” the Lord says: “A knower of the four Vedas who is not My devotee is not dear to Me; My devotee,
though born among dog-eaters, is dear to Me.” And Krishnadasa Kaviraja writes:

“One of low birth is not unfit for the worship of Krishna, nor is a brahmana of good family fit for it by that alone.
Whoever worships is great; the non-devotee is low and contemptible. In the worship of Krishna there is no consideration
of caste, family and the like.” (CC, Antya 4.66–67)

Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati in his “Anubhashya” names Shyamananda among the gurus who were in truth pure, genuine
brahmanas — which is why brahmanas by birth accepted them as their gurus (Madhya 24.325). <!-- F5 I2 Q4 Q5 Q6 -->

Before him Krishna Mandala and Durika had had sons and daughters, but all of them died. When another boy was born, the
women of the village, following the custom of giving such a child an unlovely name to ward off misfortune, kept saying:
“For now let his name be Duhkhiya.” His parents raised him with anxiety — and named him Duhkhi (“Bhakti-ratnakara”
1.355–359)[^4]. He appeared at a most auspicious moment: whoever saw the infant even once was filled with joy, and his
father, seeing the radiance of his son, said to his wife: “Take care of him — may Krishna protect him.” One of the
biographies adds that everyone foretold his greatness (*mahapurusha*), and that Lord Jagannatha Himself, whose glory he
was to proclaim, protected him as he grew; the child was so beautiful that no one could take their eyes off him.
<!-- F2 F9 -->

He was born on the purnima of the month of Chaitra. In the Vaishnava calendar of the Sri Chaitanya Saraswat Math this is
the full moon at the end of the month of Vishnu — the very day on which Vasanta-rasa is celebrated. The year, according
to one of the biographies, was 1456 of the Shaka era (1534), soon after Mahaprabhu's disappearance; according to another,
1533[^5]. <!-- F8 -->

## Childhood
At the proper time his parents performed for him the ceremonies of the first feeding with rice (*annaprashana*) and the
first haircut, and the joy in the house, says Narahari, cannot be told. He did not mix with other boys: his time was
given to study and to Vaishnava observances. He mastered Sanskrit grammar ahead of time, and with it poetry, rhetoric
and other subjects, so that the learned marvelled at his intelligence; then he applied himself in earnest to the
scriptures. His parents rejoiced both in his gifts and in his piety. He served his father and mother diligently.
<!-- F10 F11 F12 F15 -->

From the local Vaishnavas he studied the accounts of Sri Chaitanya, Nityananda, Advaita and their associates, and he
loved to retell them to others. The “Bhakti-ratnakara” says that he constantly sang their virtues, “and tears flow from
his eyes like the stream of a river” (1.364), and always drank the nectar of the pastimes of Radha and Krishna. So the
desire awoke in the boy to take shelter at the feet of Gaura and Nityananda. <!-- F13 F14 -->

## At Ambika, with Hridaya Chaitanya
When their son had grown, the parents themselves proposed that he take initiation, though he was still young, so that
he might give himself wholly to the Lord's service. Seeing him always absorbed in thoughts of Gaura and Nityananda, his
father said: “Take initiation in the Krishna mantra from whomever you wish.” Dreams had inspired him even before this,
and he had his answer ready. The “Bhakti-ratnakara” continues:

“Hearing them, he says with joined palms: ‘My master is Hridaya Chaitanya in Ambika. He is a branch of Prabhu Gauridasa
Pandita, who is dear to Sri Krishna Chaitanya and Nityananda; in his house the two brothers dwell in person. If I
receive permission, I shall go there and become his disciple.’” (“Bhakti-ratnakara” 1.367–369)

Hridaya Chaitanya lived at that time in Ambika near Kalna, on the Ganga — in the shripata of his guru Gauridasa Pandita,
the associate of Gaura and Nityananda; Gauridasa's chief disciple, he himself became Shyamananda's guru.
<!-- F16 F17 F18 -->

His father objected: the way was long — how would he get there? But Duhkhi had thought of that too: soon many people
from their region would go to Gauda to bathe in the Ganga — he would go with them and have nothing to fear. After
reflection his parents gladly let him go, and he set out with the pilgrims[^6]. At Ambika, in the house of Gauridasa,
he saw the Deities of Sri Gaura and Nityananda. Tradition says that Mahaprabhu, when about to leave, gave Them to the
Pandita Himself to soothe the pain of separation, and that the Two Brothers abide in Them still[^7]. At the sight of
Them the youth was filled with prema and wept for great joy. <!-- F19 F20 F21 -->

Hridaya Chaitanya, seeing him, was pleased and asked who he was and why he had come. He told everything: that he had
come from far away, from Dharenda-Bahadurpura, that he was born among the Sadgopas, that his father was Krishna Mandala
and his own name Duhkhi, and that he wished only to serve his master's feet. Moved, Hridaya Chaitanya heard the whole
of his past life, waited for an auspicious day and in his great mercy initiated him into the Krishna mantra. <!-- F22 -->

“Mercy arose in Sri Hridaya Chaitanya: his former name was Duhkhi — he gave him the name Krishnadasa. That the name
‘Shyamananda’ would be revealed in Vrindavana he indicated by a hint, and the wise understood. He became known as Duhkhi
Krishnadasa and was appointed to serve his guru's Deity.” (“Bhakti-ratnakara” 1.376–378)[^8]

Having obtained what he longed for, Krishnadasa was very happy and tried with all his heart to please his guru, and
Hridaya Chaitanya, greatly pleased with his service, offered him to the Lord's feet. Of this time the
“Bhakti-ratnakara” quotes a verse of the “Shyamananda-shataka”:

“He whom people on earth glorify as the beloved disciple of Hridayananda and as the dear disciple's disciple of Sri
Subala, the Lord's friend — may that illustrious crest-jewel of the kings of rasikas shine day and night in my heart,
awakening a taste for the intimate sports of Sri Radha's Beloved.” (“Bhakti-ratnakara” 1.381)

Subala is Krishna's eternal friend, who appeared in Gaura's pastimes as Gauridasa Pandita. That is why one of the
biographies calls Shyamananda, in Krishna-lila, “the servant of the servant of Subala”. <!-- F3 F23 F38 Q2 -->

## The Road to Vrindavana
A few days later Hridaya Chaitanya told him to go quickly to Vrindavana — to study the writings of the Gosvamis under
Sri Jiva[^9] and to live there a life of intent bhajana. Krishnadasa pleaded: “Prabhu, order me to stay near you.” His
guru embraced him with tears of love and said again: “Go to Vrindavana.” And he left, weeping bitterly, for separation
from his guru was hard for him. He carried many messages from Hridaya Chaitanya to the residents of Vrindavana and his
obeisances to the six Gosvamis; according to the “Prema-vilasa”, his guru, sending him off, gave him a sanctified cloth
from the Deities. <!-- F24 F25 -->

On the way he went round Navadvipa-Mayapur and the homes of the Lord's associates, and the more he saw, the less he
wished to leave Gaura-mandala. Narahari writes:

“Crying ‘Sri Gauda-mandala!’ he wails aloud; tears pour down his face and chest. The associates of Nityananda, Advaita
and Chaitanya — uttering their names, he weeps without end. Again and again he prays to the Lord: ‘May Sri
Gauda-mandala be merciful to me!’” (“Bhakti-ratnakara” 1.389–391)

Why does he pray to Gauda? Because, the poet answers, everyone calls Gauda-mandala a chintamani, and by its mercy all
desires are fulfilled — and he quotes Narottama's song “Gaurangera duti pada”:

“He who knows Gauranga's associates as eternally perfect goes to the son of the king of Vraja. He who knows the land of
Sri Gauda-mandala as a chintamani obtains residence in the land of Vraja.” (“Bhakti-ratnakara” 1.396)

In Navadvipa, according to one of the biographies, he sought out the old house of Jagannatha Mishra and found there the
aged Ishana Thakura; he bowed to him, received his blessing and spent several days in Navadvipa. <!-- F26 Q7 I5 -->

Then the road led west. At Gaya he bowed to the feet of Vishnu — the place where Mahaprabhu took initiation from Ishvara
Puri — and, remembering it, was overcome with ecstasy. At Kashi he received the darshana and blessings of Tapana Mishra,
Chandrashekhara and other devotees. At Mathura he bathed at Vishrama-ghata and bowed to the Deity of Adikeshava, and at
Krishna's birthplace his breath caught with prema. The “Prema-vilasa” adds that, seeing from afar the pinnacle of
Govinda's temple, he fell unconscious. “Having seen many tirthas, he soon came to Vrindavana,” says the
“Bhakti-ratnakara” (1.399). <!-- F27 -->

## At the Feet of Sri Jiva
In Vrindavana he went round Vraja-mandala and came to Sri Raghunatha Dasa Gosvami. The “Prema-vilasa” relates that at
Radha-kunda Raghunatha Dasa rejoiced to hear that the youth's parama-guru was Gauridasa Pandita and sent him to the aged
Krishnadasa Kaviraja, and Narahari's song says that there, on the bank of Radha-kunda, Sri Dasa Gosani, seeing him
intoxicated with prema, showed him mercy. According to one of the biographies, it was Raghunatha Dasa who directed him
to Sri Jiva Gosvami. Asking the local people where Jiva's bhajana-kutira was, Krishnadasa went there and bowed down.
Jiva asked who he was, and he told the story of his past life: he was a disciple of Hridaya Chaitanya, sent to study the
scriptures. Jiva was delighted — both that Hridaya Chaitanya had sent him and with his devotion — asked about the
Vaishnavas of Bengal and Utkala and took him under his protection. <!-- F29 F32 -->

Hridaya Chaitanya himself entrusted his disciple to Jiva. Narahari writes:

“Hearing of Sri Shyamananda's fine life, Sri Hridaya Chaitanya here [in Gauda] rejoiced at heart. He writes in a letter
to Sri Jiva Gosvami: ‘I have entrusted my disciple Duhkhi Krishnadasa to you. Fulfil all the desires of his heart, and
after some time send him back here.’ And to Shyamananda he constantly sends word: ‘Know that Sri Jiva is my second
self. Be careful in acquiring the jewel of bhakti, and see that you commit no offence against the Vaishnavas.’”
(“Bhakti-ratnakara” 1.405–409)

One of the biographies gives the guru's words thus: “Teach him Krishna consciousness, and when his mind is steady,
after he has studied the scriptures, send him back to me.” So the initiated disciple of Hridaya Chaitanya became a
siksha-disciple of Sri Jiva, and his guru himself told him to see in Jiva an extension of himself. <!-- F30 -->

Here too he met Srinivasa and Narottama. One of the biographies tells that one day Srinivasa Acharya and Narottama
Thakura had a presentiment that a new Vaishnava friend would soon come to them — and that very day Duhkhi Krishnadasa
came to Jiva, and the three met with joy[^10]. So began a lifelong friendship: the three are called “of one life and
one aim, friends at heart”. <!-- F31 -->

Jiva gave all three a taste of the sweetness of bhakti-rasa from the books of Sri Rupa and Sanatana, his own, those of
Raghunatha Dasa, Gopala Bhatta and others. He taught Krishnadasa the “Srimad-Bhagavatam”; according to the
“Prema-vilasa”, grammar and poetry as well, and the “Bhakti-rasamrita-sindhu” and “Ujjvala-nilamani” with commentary.
Receiving detailed personal instruction from Jiva, the three became masters of the teaching and scriptures of Sri
Chaitanya — what is usually called Gaudiya Vaishnavism — and adept in joyful bhakti full of feeling; soon Krishnadasa,
like the other two, became a teacher of the bhakti-shastras himself, and Jiva placed him in Srinivasa's care. With
Jiva's consent they held kirtanas: all three were great connoisseurs of music, skilled in the *kalavati* of the Delhi
school. They lived as humble mendicants, sustained by *madhukari*, and performed their bhajana as one, with a single
mind[^11]. <!-- F33 F35 -->

Krishnadasa begged Jiva for some service that would bring him nearer to his teacher, and Jiva gladly told him to draw
water for him every day at Kanana-kunja. He carried it with love, and this service transformed him: at the loud
kirtana of the names of Radha and Govinda, at the remembrance of Their pastimes, he would stand stunned with wonder, and
his eyes would fill with tears. The “Bhakti-ratnakara” says: “In Vrindavana he performs a wondrous sadhana; at the sight
of it everyone's eyes and hearts are gladdened” (1.400). By his nature and devotion he became dear to all — to the
elder Gosvamis, to the Vaishnavas of Vraja and to Sri Shyamasundara Himself — and all the Lord's associates in
Vrindavana rejoiced to see him. <!-- F28 F34 F37 -->

His guru, Hridaya Chaitanya, like Gauridasa, worshipped Gaura and Nitai in the mood of friendship (*sakhya-bhava*). But
Jiva saw in Krishnadasa an inclination to become a maidservant (*dasi*) of Srimati Radha and gave him the inner
preparation for it; serving Jiva and keeping company with the Vrajavasis headed by him, Krishnadasa acquired a taste for
the service of Radha and Krishna in the conjugal mood (*madhurya-bhava*) and took shelter of it. The “Prema-vilasa”
relates that when he asked about the course of his sadhana, Jiva explained to him the five rasas and madhura-rasa and
gave him the mantra of Radhika and instructions on remembrance — but first of all reminded him:

“Listen, Krishnadasa, to what must and must not be done: Hridaya Chaitanya Dasa is assuredly your guru. He gave you the
Krishna-mantra; from his mercy and together with his mercy all these attainments come. If you commit an offence against
him, all will be destroyed; keep these words of mine in your heart.” (“Prema-vilasa” 12.357–359)

The siksha-guru does not replace the diksha-guru: all that Shyamananda did in Vrindavana, says the “Bhakti-ratnakara”,
was “only at the command of his gurudeva” (1.404). <!-- F36 -->

## The Name “Shyamananda” and Sri Radha's Nupura
It was in Vrindavana that Sri Jiva gave him his new name — Shyamananda. This is stated by the “Bhakti-ratnakara”, the
“Prema-vilasa”, the biographies, and Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati in his “Anubhashya”: “Some time later Sri Jiva gave
Srinivasa, Narottama and Dukhi Krishnadasa, who had come from Gauda, the names ‘Acharya’, ‘Thakura’ and ‘Shyamananda’”
(on CC, Adi 10.85). Why this name? Narahari answers briefly:

“He gave great joy to Shyamasundara — and in Vrindavana he received a new name: ‘Shyamananda’.” (“Bhakti-ratnakara”
1.401)

The biographies say the same: Jiva, knowing that Krishnadasa had pleased Shyamasundara — or, according to another
biography, that he brought joy (*ananda*) to Radha and Shyamasundara — gave him the name, as he gave titles to his other
best disciples too[^12]. <!-- F4 -->

His line preserves a tradition about it. One early morning, absorbed in ecstasy, Shyamananda was sweeping the
Rasa-mandala of Vrindavana — and by Sri Radha's mercy found on the ground a nupura, an ankle-bell from Her foot. In
his excitement he touched it to his forehead, and on that spot a mark of the same shape appeared. This mark, the
*nupura-tilaka*, his followers wear on the forehead to this day in coloured clay. So Sri Radha showed how dear this
servant was to Her. Narahari sings in a pada:

“In Vrindavana, in a new grove, he found the anklet of Rai; there he became known as Shyamananda — who will understand
his life?” (pada of Narahari; “Bhakti-ratnakara” 15)
<!-- F39 F97 -->

The “Prema-vilasa” tells it in more detail. At night in the kunja Radha sang and danced for Krishna's joy, and the
nupura slipped from Her left foot; neither She nor the sakhis noticed. At dawn Duhkhi Krishnadasa went to bow down at
the Rasa-sthali and found the precious anklet under the leaves. He placed it on his head and, barely able to walk for
prema, brought it to Jiva. Jiva understood whose nupura it was and fell unconscious; coming to, he pressed it to his
breast and wept: “The kunkuma from Her feet has touched your head.” He marked Krishnadasa with a tilaka in the shape of
Krishna's foot with Radhika's dot and said: “From today your name is Shyamananda” (12.388) — and then placed his hand
in the hand of Narottama, Thakura Mahashaya[^13]. <!-- F40 -->

One of the biographies, without naming its source, tells this lila at still greater length. From the grove where
Krishnadasa drew water there sometimes came a girl with a pitcher on her head, beautiful as a princess. His service
pleased Radha and Krishna, and They wished to reveal Themselves to him. One day, absorbed in Krishna-prema, he saw in
the water a shining nupura with the edge of a woman's veil attached to it; he touched it to his head, felt a wave of
bliss and resolved to find its owner. That morning the sakhis noticed that Sri Radha's anklet was missing: She had
dropped it in the kunja at night, while dancing with Krishna, and She sent Vishakha for it. Vishakha, calling herself
the daughter of a cowherd of the village, told Krishnadasa that the anklet had been lost by a young daughter-in-law of
their house while picking flowers; and Radha Herself, coming with Vishakha, stood apart behind a tree. Seeing from afar
the radiance of King Vrishabhanu's daughter, he understood that a great secret was being revealed to him, gave the
anklet to Vishakha and fell prostrate in tears. As a reward he asked only for the dust of her feet. Vishakha told him to
bathe in the kunda, and as he immersed himself he attained the form of a young gopi; Vishakha led her to Sri Radha, and
the sakhis seated her among them. Radha put kunkuma on the anklet, marked the forehead of Her new maidservant with it
and said: “May this tilaka remain on your forehead; from now on your name is Shyamananda; now go.” The vision vanished,
and in the reflection in the water he saw the shining tilaka on his forehead. Weeping with joy and offering prayers to
Sri Radha, he returned to Jiva, who heard him with tears of joy and said: “Do not reveal this secret to people; from now
on your name is Shyamananda.” <!-- F40 -->

There is nothing mundane in this lila. Vishakha's words about the “young daughter-in-law” are Radha's concealment within
Her own pastimes, where She hides Her love for Krishna; the gopi form that Krishnadasa attained is not a transformation of
the body but the revelation of his eternal form of service; and Jiva's command to keep silent shows how intimate this
grace is[^14]. <!-- F40 -->

## The Guru's Anger
The same biography tells what followed. The Vaishnavas began to talk of Krishnadasa's new name and new tilaka, and word
reached Ambika. Hridaya Chaitanya, angered, hurried to Vrindavana and, seeing an unfamiliar tilaka on his disciple's
forehead, said: “Your conduct is unworthy; you disgrace me!” — and scolded and even beat him, while the Vaishnavas tried
to calm him. Duhkhi Krishnadasa bore it all with a cheerful face, for he knew he had never for a moment abandoned his
guru's service. That night Sri Radha appeared to Hridaya Chaitanya in a dream and reproached him: “Sri Duhkhi
Krishnadasa has greatly pleased Me, and I gave him this new name and tilaka; why do you instruct him otherwise?” Hridaya
Chaitanya fell at Her feet, begging forgiveness. In the morning he called Shyamananda, embraced him again and again and
said with tears: “You are truly fortunate” — and a few days later he left Vraja[^15]. <!-- F41 I3 -->

This story cannot be measured by worldly standards. The guru's sternness was care for the purity of his line, and it was
resolved by Radha Herself; the disciple accepted his teacher's rebuke as mercy and never left him in thought or deed.
One of the biographies explains the essence too: whoever thinks that Shyamananda offended his diksha-guru by passing from
his mood to another is mistaken. Friendship (*sakhya*) is contained within conjugal love (*madhurya*), and the growth of
the disciple only increases the glory of the teacher. <!-- I1 -->

## The Books of the Gosvamis
The time came when Jiva told Shyamananda to return to Gauda. The Gosvamis of Vraja, having considered Sri Jiva's plan,
resolved to send the three disciples to Bengal with the books on bhakti, to establish and spread the Vaishnava dharma
there: Jiva wished them to become acharyas in Gauda — restorers of pure bhakti — and the Gosvamis agreed that the books
be entrusted to Srinivasa. According to one of the biographies this was in 1504 of the Shaka era (1582–1583), and the
books were to carry the Gosvamis' teaching through Bengal and Orissa. Jiva explained the Gosvamis' will to the three and
handed them the manuscripts[^16]. Srila Bhaktisiddhanta writes in the “Anubhashya” that Jiva sent them to Gauda with all
the books of the Gosvamis “so that they might preach the holy name and prema” (on CC, Adi 10.85). <!-- F42 F92 -->

Lokanatha, Bhugarbha, Gopala Bhatta, Raghunatha Dasa, Krishnadasa Kaviraja and other Gosvamis came to bless them, as did
Ishana of Vrindavana and Uddhava Dasa, who accompanied them a little way out of Mathura (“Bhakti-ratnakara” 6.514).
Before setting out the three bowed to the three principal Deities of Vrindavana — Govinda, Gopinatha and Madana-mohana.
They departed on shukla-panchami, the fifth day of the bright fortnight of the month of Agrahayana. <!-- F43 F44 Q8 -->

Everything needed was arranged by Sri Jiva, the author of the plan. He warmly received the Vaishnavas who had come for
this from other parts of Vraja, and at his bidding a fortunate merchant of Mathura bore the expenses: a cart, a stout
wooden chest that kept out the rain, travel passes for the toll-stations, and foot-guards in front and behind.
According to the “Prema-vilasa”, four oxen were yoked to the cart and ten men went with it; according to the
“Narottama-vilasa”, eleven armed Vrajavasis. Jiva with a few companions escorted the cart as far as Mathura and beyond.
Before parting, according to the “Prema-vilasa”, he entrusted Shyamananda to Narottama — to take him along and later
send him home with an escort — and told him to ask Narottama about the intimate matters of bhajana; there too he
foretold that in Shyamananda's homeland Murari Dasa, son of Achyutananda, would take his shelter. According to the
“Narottama-vilasa”, Jiva told Shyamananda to go on to Utkala by way of Ambika (3.14), and the “Anuragavalli” gives his
words: “Through you in the land of Utkala many will be delivered — know it for certain” (6.204). <!-- F45 F46 -->

They travelled under guard with the locked chest of scriptures, by the road by which Mahaprabhu and Sanatana had once
gone from Nilachala to Vrindavana, through Agra and the forests of Jharikhanda — village by village, halting for the
night, with sankirtana and preaching. But along the way rumours spread that the chest held the treasure of some great
zamindar, or of a rich merchant bound for Puri. When the travellers had crossed the border of Bihar and entered Bengal,
the soothsayer of a band of robbers in the service of Vira Hambira, king of Vishnupura, calculated that the chest held
valuables. In the kingdom of Vana-Vishnupura the Vaishnavas thought the danger past and slept in peace, and at midnight
the robbers carried off the chest, cart and all, to the palace. <!-- F47 F48 F49 -->

Finding only books in the chest, the king felt cheated — and wished to meet their owner, to beg forgiveness and escape
the consequences of offending a saint. The three Vaishnavas, for their part, were as if thunderstruck; in despair they
were ready to give up their lives. At last, after many inquiries, Srinivasa learned that the chest could probably be
recovered from the palace. He stayed in Vishnupura, sending Narottama to Kheturi in North Bengal and Shyamananda to
Orissa, to Utkala — for Medinipur too was then under the king of Orissa[^17]. <!-- F50 F51 F52 -->

Not only were the books recovered: Vira Hambira himself became Srinivasa's disciple and a great patron of the preaching
of Mahaprabhu's bhakti. At the king's request Srinivasa informed Jiva in Vrindavana — through the carter and the guards
— and wrote to Narottama and Shyamananda. According to the “Narottama-vilasa”, the letter about the recovered books threw
both into ecstasy. Srila Bhaktisiddhanta writes that Jiva first received news of the theft of the books, and then that
they had been found. <!-- F53 F91 -->

## In Gauda Again
The first months after the theft Shyamananda spent with Narottama at Kheturi. The “Prema-vilasa” relates that for ten
days Narottama explained to him from the “Ganoddesha-dipika” the order of service in the siddha body, from the
“Rasamrita-sindhu” raga and vaidhi, and how to guard against offences to the holy name and in service, and then, in the
pain of parting, sent him home with two companions and money for the road (13.183–197). After that Shyamananda visited
the places of many devotees in Bengal and by way of Navadvipa came to Ambika, to his guru[^18]. <!-- F54 -->

The “Narottama-vilasa” describes this meeting. Hridaya Chaitanya was telling those around him of his dear disciple —
“he devoted himself ceaselessly to service in the kunja, found happiness, and received the name ‘Shyamananda’” (3.76) —
and he had barely finished when Shyamananda himself came in and fell respectfully at his feet. The guru placed his feet
on his head, embraced him, led him into the temple courtyard and offered him to the feet of Nityananda and Chaitanya,
and then joyfully gave him the remnants of his own meal. According to one of the biographies, Hridaya Chaitanya asked
him about the Gosvamis of Vrindavana and, hearing of the theft of the books, was shaken; the disciple served him again,
and his joy grew from day to day. <!-- F55 -->

But Hridaya Chaitanya was troubled about Utkala. Almost all of Mahaprabhu's personal associates there had already left
this world, and the preaching of His message had nearly died out. So he told his disciple to go to Utkala and preach
the teaching of Sri Chaitanya and the Gosvamis. As he was leaving, Shyamananda suffered at the separation; his guru
called him back and explained his concern, and he, taking the command upon his head, went. According to the
“Narottama-vilasa” and the “Prema-vilasa”, Hridaya Chaitanya, sending him off, gave him a garland-prasada from the
Deities and invested him with his own power: “Much will be accomplished through you” (“Narottama-vilasa” 3.91).
<!-- F55 -->

On the way he passed through his native Dharenda-Bahadurpura. The villagers rejoiced at their Duhkhi; he stayed several
days and preached, and many took shelter at his feet. Then came Dandeshvara: Shyamananda blessed the village and its
devotees, spoke hari-katha for many days and held a great festival, and many became his disciples. And then he entered
Utkala and preached everywhere. <!-- F56 -->

## Narottama at Nrisimhapura; the Second Visit to Vrindavana
Meanwhile Narottama, having visited Navadvipa-Mayapur, the places of Nityananda in Gauda, and Nilachala, came on his
way back to Nrisimhapura, where Shyamananda lived. The “Narottama-vilasa” relates that someone, running up from afar,
told Shyamananda that Thakura Mahashaya had come, and Shyamananda hurried out with his people to meet him. “Seeing each
other, both lost their composure and, bowing to each other, were bathed in tears” (4.196); all around wept to see such
love. Shyamananda showed Narottama a letter from Srinivasa and the mahaprasada sent from Ambika by Hridaya Chaitanya, and
Narottama with his own hand put Jagannatha's prasada into his mouth. Out of love Shyamananda kept his guest for many
days; Narottama flooded Nrisimhapura with sankirtana, and day and night they spoke of Krishna. Narottama also showed
mercy to Shyamananda's disciple Rasika-Murari. And when the time came to part, both were pierced by the pain of
separation. <!-- F57 -->

On Narottama's advice Shyamananda went to Nilachala and saw the Lord's associates there. Later he set out once more for
Vrindavana and found Srinivasa already with Sri Jiva. One of the biographies relates: Jiva embraced him and asked about
the devotees of Puri; Srinivasa and Shyamananda embraced joyfully. News came of the departure of Dvija Haridasa, and all
were saddened. Jiva taught them the conclusions of the just-completed “Shat-sandarbha” and read them the opening verses
of the “Gopala-champu” he had begun, and together with Ramachandra Kaviraja they visited the Deities and saints of
Vrindavana. <!-- F58 F59 -->

At Jiva's bidding Srinivasa and Shyamananda went together to Gauda. In Vana-Vishnupura the king danced for joy, honoured
Srinivasa as his guru and held a great festival, and Shyamananda marvelled at the king's devotion; there Vira Hambira
took initiation and the name Sri Chaitanya Dasa. From there Shyamananda went to Puri and Srinivasa to Yajigrama.
<!-- F60 -->

## The Festival at Kheturi
On his return Shyamananda received an invitation — according to one of the biographies, at the behest of Sri
Lokanatha, Narottama's guru — to the famous festival at Kheturi, in the house of Raja Santosha Datta, where on
Phalguna-purnima the Deities of Sri Radha-Govinda and Sri Gauranga were being installed. He came from Nrisimhapura with
Rasikananda and other disciples, and Narottama introduced him to the assembled Vaishnavas and put Rasikananda in charge
of caring for the guests. There he found his old friends Narottama and Srinivasa, and they embraced warmly; Jahnava-mata,
Raghunandana Thakura, Achyutananda, Vrindavana Dasa Thakura and many others were there[^19]. <!-- F61 -->

The three acharyas, ahead of everyone, received the procession of Sri Jahnava, prostrating themselves in the dust of the
road. Together they distributed sandal paste and garlands to the guests, and at Jahnava's bidding Nrisimha Chaitanya
adorned the three themselves with garlands and sandal; Jahnava asked the three to take prasada after the mahantas. At
this festival Srinivasa, Narottama, Shyamananda and the other acharyas acknowledged Jahnava as *jagad-guru*, the teacher
of the whole world. <!-- F63 F65 -->

The festival was of unprecedented splendour and boundless sanctity: Sri Mahaprabhu Himself, with His associates who had
already disappeared from the world's sight, appeared for a time in Narottama's sankirtana and joined the dancing. This
was no vision of “spirits of the dead”: the eternal associates of the Lord do not die, and the Lord manifests His
pastimes together with them wherever He is glorified with such love. <!-- F62 -->

After the festival Srinivasa and Shyamananda stayed on at Kheturi for a few more days. Then, according to one of the
biographies, Shyamananda visited Vrindavana once more and, encouraged by the elder Gosvamis, returned home by way of
Ambika, the place of his guru. Another says that he and Srinivasa went back to Yajigrama, where Narottama came too, and
that from there Shyamananda went unhurriedly, from one devotee's house to another, to Utkala, calling on the way at
Kantaka-nagara (Katwa) on Gadadhara Dasa Thakura and at Srikhanda on Raghunandana[^20]. Soon afterwards many of
Mahaprabhu's eternal associates left this world. <!-- F64 -->

## Preaching in Utkala
So began Shyamananda's principal labours. In many places of Utkala he carried Sri Chaitanya's religion of love and
turned even robbers into devotees; many Muslims also became his disciples. He preached above all through kirtana. Each
of the three acharyas had his own style of singing: Srinivasa *manohara-shahi*, Narottama *garanhati*, while Shyamananda
introduced a special kind of sankirtana, *reneti*, and captivated his listeners with his heartfelt singing; these
styles have not survived. His fame spread from Utkala in all four directions, and under his influence the regular
worship of Gaura and Nityananda was established everywhere. <!-- F66 F67 -->

The “Prema-vilasa” preserves the story of how, in his native Dharenda, where Shyamananda was spreading
nama-sankirtana, the king's governor, the Pathan Sher Khan, forbade the kirtana and, when Shyamananda did not obey,
smashed and scattered the khols and kartals. Shyamananda raised his voice in a terrible roar — and the yavanas' beards,
says the poet, were burnt, and they vomited blood. That night the Lord appeared to Sher Khan, first in a terrible form,
then in a golden one: “My name is Sri Chaitanya, the refuge of all; Shyamananda is My great devotee. From him you will
take the mantra of Krishna” (19.80–81). In the morning Sher Khan fell at Shyamananda's feet, begging forgiveness, and
received his mercy; later he was known as Chaitanya Dasa (20)[^21]. <!-- F66 -->

One of the biographies tells of other places as well. In Balarampura lived wealthy and pious people; hearing of his
fame, they fervently invited him, and he came with Rasikananda, the yogi Damodara and many disciples; they worshipped
him, the service was conducted according to the shastras, and after a few days a great festival of kirtana and
hari-katha was held, and many surrendered to him. In Nrisimhapura there was a sizeable party of atheists, agnostics and
blasphemers. Shyamananda held a festival of sankirtana there; the godless pandits came, heard his hari-katha, their
hearts softened, and they took shelter at his feet. <!-- F73 -->

His disciples were beyond counting. The “Bhakti-ratnakara” (15.62–66) names among them Radhananda, Purushottama,
Manohara, Chintamani, Balabhadra, Jagadishvara, Uddhava, Akrura, Madhuvana, Govinda, Jagannatha, Gadadhara,
Anandananda and Radha-mohana; with them, it says, he was ever immersed in the joy of kirtana. On his journeys he was
surrounded by disciples such as Kishora Dasa. The “Prema-vilasa” (20.187–199) lists the “branches” of Shyamananda:
Kishoridasa, Dinabandhu and the cowherds of Dharenda, Rasikananda and Murari of Rayani with their wives, the yogi
Damodara, the disciples at Balarampura and Nrisimhapura, Sher Khan. The chief places of his disciples are Dharenda,
Bahadurpur, Rayani (Rohini), Gopivallabhapura and Nrisimhapura. <!-- F68 F69 Q3 -->

News of his preaching greatly gladdened the devotees of Vrindavana and Gauda. The “Prema-vilasa” relates how messengers
of the Gosvamis came to him from Vrindavana with Jiva's charge — to keep his dharma, right conduct and service to the
Vaishnavas. Shyamananda's disciple Murari Dasa washed their feet, while Shyamananda himself wept in humility:
“Forgetting those feet, I remain intoxicated [with the world]; and still he shows mercy, knowing [me] to be low”
(17.132) — and guru and disciple rolled on the ground in tears. Thanks to his labours Orissa can say that the
proportion of Chaitanya's followers among its people is greater than anywhere else; at the end of the sixteenth and the
beginning of the seventeenth century the wide Gaudiya preaching of Shyamananda and Rasikananda-Murari transformed the
whole district of Balasore. And Narahari sings: “In Utkala, where people utterly darkened in mind had not a trace of
bhakti, he flooded everyone with the rasa of Gaura's prema and made the land blessed” (pada of Narahari;
“Bhakti-ratnakara” 15). <!-- F75 F76 I6 -->

## Rasika-Murari
The foremost of his disciples was Rasika-Murari, or Rasikananda, son of Achyutananda, ruler of Rayani on the river
Suvarnarekha. The “Bhakti-ratnakara” (15.27–28) says that Rasikananda and Murari are two names of one person[^22]. His
whole family was devoted, and he himself had long sought a true guru; one day he heard a divine voice: a saint named Sri
Shyamananda would come. From then on he repeated that name, sometimes with tears of longing. At the end of the night
Shyamananda appeared to him in a dream, and in the morning Rasika saw him in person: at Ghatshila, by the Suvarnarekha,
he was walking with a few disciples, absorbed in bhava, tall, radiant as the sun, dancing in love and singing the names
of Sri Krishna Chaitanya and Nityananda. Rasika fell at his feet; Shyamananda raised and embraced him, performed the
preliminary rites and gave him the mantra of Radha-Krishna, offering him to Chaitanya and Nityananda. His wife received
initiation together with him[^23]. This is told in detail in the article on Rasikananda. <!-- F70 Q9 -->

Then Rasika brought his guru to the capital, Rayani, where many became initiated devotees, among them a yogi named
Damodara. According to one of the biographies, Damodara saw from afar a radiance issuing from Shyamananda that dazzled
him, and fell at his feet; Shyamananda said to him with tears: “If you wish to attain divine purity, always chant the
names of Gaura and Nityananda; the Lord is supremely merciful and will give you Krishna-prema.” The
“Bhakti-ratnakara” (15.55–58) says that Shyamananda mercifully flooded the yogi with bhakti-rasa, and he, having become a
disciple, wept, sang the names of Nitai-Chaitanya and danced, crying “Bhakti is above all!” — lamenting the time wasted
in vain attempts to control the mind by yoga. The “Prema-vilasa” (19.93–108) tells it differently: a learned Vedantist
yogi debated with Shyamananda for many days at Gopivallabhapura and was defeated by the teaching of the Gosvamis; at
night it was revealed to him in a dream that a cowherd had become a brahmana by the power of bhajana; after taking
initiation he once saw a dazzling sacred thread on Shyamananda's radiant body, but Shyamananda, as soon as Rasika and
the others came, hid both the thread and the radiance and began nama-sankirtana[^24]. <!-- F71 -->

With Rasika-Murari and Damodara Shyamananda held a great festival at Dharenda that is remembered to this day. He
invested Rasikananda with such power that Rasikananda turned robbers, atheists and people of other faiths to bhakti: a
disciple who gives himself wholly to his guru receives all his guru's power as well. <!-- F77 I4 -->

## Gopivallabhapura
Having turned many more to the path of pure bhakti, Shyamananda came to Gopivallabhapura, on the border of Orissa and
Bengal, on the Bengal side. Many wealthy people lived there; almost all of them took his shelter and asked him to
install a Deity. The devotees built a temple with a hall for kirtana, a storeroom, a special kitchen, an ashrama for
the servants and a guesthouse for Vaishnavas, with a pond and gardens beside it. At a great festival Shyamananda had the
Deity of Sri Govindadeva (Radha-Govinda) installed and appointed Rasikananda to serve Him; the “Bhakti-ratnakara”
(15.81) says that at Gopivallabhapura he “poured down a rain of prema and entrusted the service of Sri Govinda to Sri
Rasika”. His followers serve this Deity in due form to this day[^25]. <!-- F74 -->

According to one of the biographies, after this Shyamananda preached throughout Utkala and, returning to
Ambika-Kalna, bowed to Hridaya Chaitanya and told him of the success of his preaching — in Utkala, in Dandeshvara, in
Rohini, in Balarampura, in Nrisimhapura and in Gopivallabhapura; his guru embraced him with love. It was here, according
to this biography, that the invitation to Kheturi reached him. <!-- F72 -->

At Gopivallabhapura, according to the “Prema-vilasa”, Virachandra, the son of Nityananda, also met him while travelling
to Nilachala[^26]. <!-- F78 -->

## The Last Years and Departure
Once, on returning to Gopivallabhapura, Shyamananda heard that his guru, Hridaya Chaitanya, had left this world. He fell
senseless and, on coming to, wept for a long time, bewildered and anxious. But that night Hridaya Chaitanya appeared to
him in a dream and encouraged him to continue preaching. The “Rasika-mangala” adds that an aradhana was held for the
guru at Shyamasundarapura in the month of Phalguna. <!-- F79 -->

The last part of his life, according to one of the biographies, he spent at Nrisimhapura in Orissa, never ceasing to
preach. The “Rasika-mangala” relates that for his last four months he was ill at Nrisimhapura, in the house of Uddhanda
Raya, and that before his departure he tied a cloth round Rasika's head with his own hands and gave him “all authority”
in the Shyamananda-mandali. According to another biography, having completed his preaching tours, he returned to
Gopivallabhapura, where a great festival was held a few days later. <!-- F80 -->

Shyamananda Prabhu left this world — entered the eternal pastimes of the Lord — on krishna-pratipada, the first day of
the dark fortnight of the month of Ashadha. The “Rasika-mangala” specifies: after the Snana-purnima had passed, at the
onset of Ashadha. In the Vaishnava calendar of the Sri Chaitanya Saraswat Math this is the krishna-pratipada of the month
of Vamana — the day after the Snana-yatra. The year, according to one of the biographies and the “Rasika-mangala”, was
1552 of the Shaka era (1630). His samadhi, according to one of the biographies, is at Gopivallabhapura, where the
service of his Deity continues to this day; the others are silent about the samadhi, and according to the
“Rasika-mangala” he departed at Nrisimhapura[^27]. <!-- F81 F82 -->

In Vrindavana his disciples and their followers worship his Deity, Sri Radha-Shyamasundara, to this day; this temple is
one of the principal places of pilgrimage in Vrindavana. The math “Shyamasundara-kunja” founded by Shyamananda
continued there later: in Baladeva Vidyabhushana's time its head was Radha-Damodara. <!-- F83 -->

## Legacy
Shyamananda's line continued through Rasikananda. Srila Bhaktisiddhanta writes in the “Anubhashya” that Rasikananda
received pancharatrika-diksha from Shyamananda, who was not born in a family of brahmanas by birth — and gives this as
proof that a guru is determined by knowledge of the truth about Krishna, not by varna (on CC, Madhya 8.127). In the
family of Rasikananda, the disciple of Shyamananda Prabhu, the upanayana has been performed for three or four centuries,
and they serve to this day as diksha-gurus for all varnas (on CC, Adi 7.45). Srila Bhaktisiddhanta calls Rasikananda
“the successor of Sri Shyamananda Prabhu” and “the servant of Shyamananda Prabhu”: from his line comes the servitor of
the temple of Kshira-chora Gopinatha at Remuna, where Rasikananda's samadhi is preserved (on CC, Madhya 1.97, 4.12). And
of Shyamananda himself he says: “Sri Dasa Gosvami, and in the following age Sri Thakura Narottama, Srila Shyamananda
Prabhu and others are likewise manifestations of this magnanimity of Sri Mahaprabhu” (on CC, Antya 5.88).
<!-- F93 F94 -->

Through Rasikananda, his grandson Nayanananda and Radha-Damodara, Shyamananda's line led to Baladeva Vidyabhushana:
Gauridasa Pandita — Hridaya Chaitanya — Shyamananda — Rasikananda — Nayanananda — Radha-Damodara — Baladeva. It is a
branch of the line of Nityananda. Baladeva wrote a commentary on Rasikananda's “Shyamananda-shataka”. <!-- F88 -->

The Gosvamis of Vrindavana themselves saw in Shyamananda, Srinivasa and Narottama those who would carry on their work.
Srinivasa returned to Gauda at the wish of the devotees, with Narottama and Shyamananda beside him — and a flood of
hari-kirtana rose across Gauda. After the departure of Mahaprabhu's associates in Vrindavana the three preserved their
traditions and widened the movement. The conduct and instructions of the disciples trained in Jiva's teaching —
Srinivasa, Narottama and Shyamananda — themselves refute the claim of the prakrita-sahajiyas that Jiva approved of
svakiya rather than Rupa's parakiya. One of the biographies, exposing the interpolations in the “Prema-vilasa”, also
examines its account of the theft of the books Jiva sent with the three acharyas. <!-- F86 F87 F90 -->

When Srinivasa, Narottama, Shyamananda and Rasikananda had gone, there began for Bengali Vaishnavism, in the words of
one of the biographies, one of its “dark periods”: the world of the Gaudiya Vaishnavas sank into the darkness of the
apasampradayas. And in the succession of acharyas leading to Vishvanatha Chakravarti, Narottama stands “together with
Shyamananda and Srinivasa”. <!-- F89 -->

His memory lives on in the twentieth century. In 1942 Srila Bhakti Dayita Madhava Gosvami Maharaja, together with Bhakti
Vichara Jajavara and Bhakti Kumuda Santa Maharajas, founded the Shyamananda Gaudiya Math in Medinipur — in honour of
Shyamananda, who first brought Mahaprabhu's message to that land. <!-- F95 -->

## In the Eternal Pastimes
Of who Shyamananda is in the eternal pastimes the primary sources say little. The “Shyamananda-shataka” calls him the
disciple of a disciple of Subala, Krishna's friend — hence one biography's words: “the servant of the servant of
Subala”. The lila of the nupura reveals his service to Sri Radha. The “Prema-vilasa” also gives his siddha name,
Kanaka-manjari (19.111; 20.184), and calls his guru Hridaya Chaitanya the sakhi Sudhira; it also says that Srinivasa is
a manifestation of the power of Chaitanya, Narottama of Nityananda, and Shyamananda of Advaita (20.185,
207–216)[^28]. One of the biographies says of all three that they are eternal associates of Sri Gaurasundara who
appeared in the world to carry His message over all the earth. <!-- F38 F85 -->

His life is the path of a faithful disciple. A Sadgopa's son from a border country, he became an acharya whom brahmanas
by birth accepted as their guru; initiated in the mood of friendship, he accepted by Sri Radha's grace and under
Jiva's guidance service in madhurya-bhava without ever leaving his guru; and that Utkala where, in Narahari's words,
there was “not a trace of bhakti”, he flooded with the prema of Gaura. <!-- I1 I2 I6 -->
