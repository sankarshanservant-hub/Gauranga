# Gadadhara Pandita
*Гададхара Пандит · @gadadhara-pandita*

## The Potency of the Devotee
Gadadhara Pandita Gosvami is one of the five forms in which the Lord appeared in Gaura-lila. Sri Gauranga, Nityananda,
Advaita, Gadadhara and Srivasa make up the Pancha-tattva: in essence They are not different, and They divided only to
relish the various tastes of devotional service. In this row Gadadhara is the energy, the shakti, of the devotee. So
too teaches the “Gaura-ganoddesha-dipika”: “And ‘the potency of the devotee’ is the foremost of brahmanas, Sri Gadadhara
Pandita” (GGD 11). Krishnadasa Kaviraja defines the place of each in the Pancha-tattva:

“The first three truths (Sri Chaitanya, Nityananda Prabhu and Advaita Acharya) are the highest object of worship for all
living beings. The fourth truth (Gadadhara Prabhu) eternally worships and serves Them. <…> No one can count all the pure
devotees of Krishna, headed by Srivasa Pandita. And all of them represent the very truth of bhakti. <…> And the
manifestations of the divine energies, represented by Gadadhara Pandita, are considered the intimate associates of the
Supreme Lord.” (CC, Adi 7.15–17)

Mahaprabhu is one; the two Prabhus, Nityananda and Advaita, serve Him, and these three are worthy of worship; Srivasa and
the others are the devotees who worship; and Gadadhara Pandita and those like him are embodiments of the Lord's
energies, His most confidential associates. All the mahajanas of the past unanimously recognized Gadadhara as one of the
Pancha-tattva. At the very beginning of the “Charitamrita” the author bows down to him:

“With reverence I bow down thousands of times to the internal personal energies of the Lord. Gadadhara Pandita is the
manifestation of one of His most confidential Shaktis.” (CC, Adi 1.41) <!-- F1 F2 F3 F4 Q2 Q4 -->

Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati explains: Gadadhara Pandita is the first among those closest to Sri Chaitanya, the
source of all shakti-tattva, and therefore he is equally present in the lilas of Navadvipa and in the lilas of
Nilachala. When Gaura shows His oneness with Narayana, His shaktis are His consorts Lakshmipriya and Vishnupriya; when He
shows His oneness with Krishna, His shakti is Gadadhara. Together with Svarupa Damodara and Ramananda Raya, Gadadhara is
a shakti of madhura-rasa: they nourish and sustain the vipralambha, the separation, that Gaurasundara relishes.
<!-- F5 F106 F8 -->

Sri Chaitanya is glorified as “the life of Nityananda and Gadadhara”:

“Glory to Him, the very life of Nityananda and Gadadhara! Glory to Him, the treasure of the hearts of Advaita and
Srivasa!” (CB, Madhya 9.3)

Bhaktisiddhanta also unfolds this double title. Pure devotees who wish to worship Radha-Govinda in madhura-rasa take
shelter of Gadadhara, and they are considered Gauranga's confidential devotees; those not so inclined take shelter of
Nityananda. Therefore some call the Lord “the life of Nityananda,” and others “the life of Gadadhara.” Some of
Mahaprabhu's associates — Narahari Sarakara, for example — themselves became followers of Gadadhara: they knew that he
was dearest of all to the Lord, and therefore sought to serve him. <!-- F6 Q18 I1 F7 -->

He has several names. He is called “Gaura-shakti,” “Pandita Gosvami” (in Bengali, Pandita-gosani), “Gadadhara Deva,” and,
affectionately, Gadai. And because of him the Lord is called “Gadadhara-prananatha,” “the Lord of Gadadhara's life,” and
“Gadai-Gauranga,” “the Gauranga who belongs to Gadadhara”; these names were current already in the time of Krishnadasa
Kaviraja, and he himself cites them in the “Charitamrita” (Antya 7.159–160; on this, see below). <!-- F9 F10 -->

## Family and Birth
Gadadhara was born in the village of Beleti in the district of Chittagong (now in Bangladesh)[^1] in the year 1408 of the
Shaka era (1486), on the new moon of the month of Vaishakha; this day, the amavasya of Vaishakha, is honored as the day
of his appearance. Whether he was the same age as the Lord or a year younger, the biographies say differently[^2]. His
father, Madhava Mishra, was a Varendra brahmana of the Kashyapa gotra; his mother's name was Ratnavati Devi, and his
younger brother's, Vaninatha. <!-- F11 F12 F13 F14 F15 -->

Chittagong gave Gaura-lila many associates: Mukunda Datta and Vasudeva Datta also came from there, and Madhava Mishra
was a close friend of Pundarika Vidyanidhi — his son's future guru. The family lived in Beleti until Gadadhara was twelve
years old, and then moved to Navadvipa. Their house stood in Mayapur, next door to the house of Jagannatha Mishra;
Ratnavati revered Shachi Devi as an elder sister, and the two were almost never apart. <!-- F18 F16 F17 -->

## Childhood beside Nimai
So from childhood Gadadhara became Nimai's inseparable companion. Gaurahari often played with him, they went together to
the village school, and Gadadhara loved Him so much that he could not part from Him for a moment. From his earliest
years he was the Lord's closest devotee and His friend. <!-- F19 F21 -->

In temperament they were unlike. Gadadhara grew up serious and restrained, calm, and fond of solitude; from a tender age
he was indifferent to worldly pleasures, strict and detached in daily life, and so — a brahmachari — he remained all his
life. Nimai, in those years, seemed restless and wilful. At school He plagued Gadadhara with tricky questions that threw
him into confusion; this hindered his studies, and Gadadhara tried to keep his distance from Him — yet he could not bear
separation either. Once Nimai told him: “Gadadhara! Soon I shall become such a Vaishnava that Brahma and Shiva will come
to bow down to Me.” <!-- F20 F22 -->

Later, when Nimai had become the foremost scholar of Navadvipa, no one dared to argue with Him: He would first refute any
thesis and then defend it Himself. Mukunda, Srivasa and the other devotees avoided these debates. Once Nimai asked
Gadadhara what liberation is. He answered according to nyaya: the final and complete cessation of suffering
(atyantika-duhkha-nivritti). Nimai showed that this definition was insufficient, and the devotees, listening, thought how
good it would be if such a scholar became a devotee. <!-- F23 -->

In those same years Ishvara Puri came to Navadvipa and stayed for several months in the house of Gopinatha Acharya. He
grew fond of Gadadhara: he saw in the youth an innate detachment, present since childhood, he saw streams of tears of
love for Krishna — and with affection he began to teach him his book “Sri Krishna-lilamrita,” straight from the
manuscript. As the “Bhakti-ratnakara” (12.2205–2207) reports, it was in the house of Gopinatha Acharya that Puri wrote
this book. Vrindavana Dasa relates:

“Ishvara Puri began to teach Gadadhara Pandita from his book ‘Sri Krishna-lilamrita.’” (CB, Adi 11.100)

Every day, while Puri was teaching Gadadhara, Nimai would come and bow to Puri; once Puri asked Him to correct the
mistakes in the book, and Nimai replied that Krishna accepts any glorification composed by His devotee, even an imperfect
one (CB, Adi 11.105–110). <!-- F24 Q10 F25 -->

Once Gauranga took Gadadhara with Him to the house of Advaita Acharya — to see what He was doing. Advaita was preparing
for the puja of His household Deity and loudly singing the name of Hari; hearing this, Gauranga fell unconscious before
Him, and Advaita worshipped His feet with the articles of puja and began to sing a hymn to Govinda. Gadadhara bit his
tongue in astonishment and objected: it is not fitting for a venerable elder to worship the feet of a boy. Advaita
smiled: “Gadadhara, in a few days you will know who this boy is.” And Gadadhara began to wonder: could Gauranga Himself
be the One for whose sake Advaita had performed all His worship? <!-- F26 -->

## The Awakening of Love
When the Lord returned from Gaya, the first to see His transformations of love was Shriman Pandita; he told the devotees
of them, and the Lord appointed a meeting with them in the house of Shuklambara Brahmachari. Hearing of this from
Shriman, Gadadhara hurried after him and hid in the house: the Lord would surely speak of Krishna — and he wanted to
listen, if only in secret. The Lord spoke on the veranda, while Gadadhara sat inside. Seeing the Lord intoxicated with
the name of Krishna, he could not hold back his tears[^3], and the Lord asked who was weeping there. “It is Your
Gadadhara,” Shuklambara replied. Vrindavana Dasa continues:

“Gadadhara lowered his head and wept. Seeing this, Vishvambhara felt deep satisfaction. He said: ‘O Gadadhara, you are
very fortunate. From your very childhood you have been endowed with strong faith in Krishna. But I have wasted My life on
meaningless pursuits. Recently I found a priceless treasure, but by misfortune I have lost it.’” (CB, Madhya 1.97–99)

The Lord embraced him. Witnesses of His transformations in the house of Shuklambara were Gadadhara, Sadashiva, Murari,
Srivasa, Shriman and others. From that time it became hard for Gadadhara to part from the Lord even for an hour; seeing
His tears of love, he too would weep. <!-- F27 F28 Q1 F29 -->

Once Gadadhara brought the Lord betel. The Lord, tormented by separation from Krishna, turned to him with a question:

“Seeing Gadadhara, Vishvambhara asked: ‘Where is that dark-complexioned Krishna in yellow garments?’ <…> In great
agitation Gadadhara Mahashaya answered: ‘Krishna always dwells in Your heart.’ But as soon as Mahaprabhu heard that
Krishna was in His heart, He began to tear at His chest with His nails. Gadadhara at once seized Vishvambhara by the
hands and calmed Him, speaking many consoling words. Gadadhara said: ‘Be patient, Krishna will come now.’ Mother Shachi
saw this. Mother Shachi was very pleased with Gadadhara. She thought: ‘I have never met such a clever youth before. I
cannot approach my son out of fear. Yet this youth was able to calm Him so skilfully.’” (CB, Madhya 2.203, 205–210)

“Let go of My hands!” the Lord pleaded. “If I do not see Krishna, I cannot live.” Shachi asked Gadadhara always to stay by
Nimai, and from then on he calmed the Lord whenever the waves of love overwhelmed Him. <!-- F30 Q8 -->

In the Navadvipa-lila Gadadhara became the Lord's chief helper. Those who in Vrindavana are Radha and Krishna are here, on
the banks of the Ganga, Gaura and Gadadhara; and the cowherd girls and boys of Vrindavana take part in this lila as the
performers of kirtana. Once the Lord, strolling by the Ganga, sat down in a grove and became absorbed in remembering the
pastimes of Vrindavana. Mukunda began to sing of purva-raga — the first awakening of love; Gadadhara gathered flowers in
the forest and placed a garland on the Lord, as Radha adorned Krishna, and some began to sing, some to dance. Then
Gauranga seated Gadadhara beside Him on a raised place at the foot of a tree. Advaita performed arati to Them both,
Nityananda sat on the left, Srivasa decorated Them with flowers, Narahari fanned Them with a chamara, Shuklambara offered
sandalwood paste, Murari sang Their glories, and Madhava, Vasudeva, Purushottama, Vijaya, Mukunda and others sang in
various ragas. <!-- F31 F108 F32 -->

Gadadhara was with the Lord in all His pastimes: in the water pastimes after the deliverance of Jagai and Madhai, at the
mahaprakasha in Srivasa's courtyard, in the procession to the Kazi, and later at the sannyasa; in Puri — at the
cleaning of the Gundicha temple, at the bathing in Narendra lake, and in many other pastimes. When the Vaishnavas spoke
of Nityananda and Gauranga — two Lords who honor each other — and Srivasa compared Them to Madhava and Shankara,
Gadadhara took it up: “Well said; it is like the story of Rama and Lakshmana.” <!-- F33 F35 -->

A special place among these pastimes belongs to the drama in the house of Chandrashekhara Acharya. The Lord Himself
assigned the roles: Haridasa was the village watchman, Srivasa was Narada, He Himself was Rukmini, and Gadadhara too was
Rukmini, with Brahmananda as her old friend Suprabha (CB, Madhya 18.9). In the second prahara of the evening Gadadhara
came out together with Brahmananda, dressed as a maiden of Vraja, his heart overflowing with love, and began to dance:

“Hearing Advaita's words, Gadadhara was very satisfied. Then he began to dance under the influence of ecstatic love.
Gadadhara danced wonderfully in the garments of Rama, the goddess of fortune, and his ‘companion’ sang songs befitting
the occasion. <…> At that moment Gadadhara was like the Ganga personified. In truth, he is a manifestation of Krishna's
internal energy. Lord Chaitanya said again and again: ‘In Vaikuntha Gadadhara is My wife.’” (CB, Madhya 18.111–112,
115–116)

Then the Lord appeared as the original energy, adya-shakti, the Mother of the universe[^4]. <!-- F34 Q11 -->

That Gaura and Gadadhara are inseparable is told also by the “Navadvipa-dhama-mahatmya”: the pious king Suvarna Sena of
Suvarna-vihara in Godrumadvipa, by the mercy of Narada, saw in a dream Gaura and Gadadhara with all Their associates,
and on waking grieved that the vision was gone. <!-- F36 -->

## Pundarika Vidyanidhi
In those days the Lord would weep and call: “Pundarika, My father!” — but none of the devotees knew Pundarika. He had in
fact come to Navadvipa, but he went unrecognized: he hid his deep devotion and carried himself like a pampered lover of
pleasure. Only Mukunda and Vasudeva Datta, his countrymen, knew him. Gadadhara, though he came from the same parts, did
not know him — according to one biography, only in appearance, for the sake of the lila. <!-- F37 F38 -->

Gadadhara was especially attached to Mukunda, and Mukunda loved him dearly: whenever a sadhu or sannyasi came to
Navadvipa, Mukunda would call Gadadhara and they would go to him together. And now Mukunda said that he would introduce
him to an extraordinary Vaishnava, not of this world — “and as a reward, remember me as your servant.” Gadadhara gladly
went at once. Mukunda presented him: “This is Gadadhara. From childhood he has been indifferent to the world; in worldly
terms he is the son of the brahmana Madhava Mishra; from an early age he has kept to renunciation and is dear to all the
Vaishnavas; he has come because he heard your name.” Vidyanidhi was overjoyed and spoke with him warmly[^5].
<!-- F39 F40 F41 -->

But what Gadadhara saw troubled him. Pundarika sat like a prince: a high bed, silk sheets whiter than milk foam, soft
pillows, a canopy, costly clothes, incense and perfumes, five or seven little jars, pan in a brass box, a hookah; two
servants fanned him with peacock-feather fans; on his forehead were the urdhva-pundra and a red dot of abir, his hair was
well groomed, and before him hung an ornate swing. “If he is a Vaishnava,” Gadadhara thought, “why does he look so much
like a worldly man?” — and he was disappointed. <!-- F42 -->

Mukunda understood his doubt and wished to reveal who Pundarika really was. He sweetly sang a verse of the “Bhagavatam”
about Putana — how Krishna granted the position of a mother even to the demoness who came to poison Him[^6]. With a cry
of “Ha Krishna!” Pundarika fell unconscious. He wept, “Krishna! Krishna!” — his tears flowed like the descending Ganga,
he rolled on the floor, overturned the hookah, tore his clothes; all eight sattvika-bhavas appeared on him, without the
slightest artifice. Gadadhara was astounded. Srila Bhaktisiddhanta, commenting on this story, warns: a Vaishnava cannot
be recognized by his outward appearance; devotees of Krishna sometimes hide themselves in the guise of worldly men, and
whoever judges by dress loses faith in the Vaishnava-guru. <!-- F43 F44 I6 -->

Remorse seized Gadadhara: “I took a mahabhagavata for an ordinary lover of pleasure and offended him. I will take mantra
from him — and be forgiven; I see no other way”[^7]. He opened his heart to Mukunda, and Mukunda, waiting until
Vidyanidhi came to himself two praharas later, conveyed it: “Gadadhara had a flaw in his thoughts; in atonement he wishes
to receive mantra from you.” Pundarika was happy: “The Lord has brought me together with a great jewel: such a disciple
is obtained only after many, many births. On the coming shukla-dvadashi, at the most auspicious hour, Gadadhara will
receive diksha.” <!-- F45 F46 -->

Gadadhara and Mukunda came to the Lord, told Him of Vidyanidhi's arrival (and it was then that Pundarika met the Lord),
and Gadadhara said that he had decided to take initiation from him. The Lord was delighted: “Quickly, quickly!” On the
appointed auspicious day Gadadhara received mantra from Pundarika Vidyanidhi. Thus two persons dear to Krishna Chaitanya
became for each other a worthy guru and a worthy disciple; Gadadhara enacted the lila of choosing his guru — and through
Pundarika, a disciple of Madhavendra Puri, entered his line. In Krishna-lila Pundarika is Vrishabhanu, the father of
Radha, and Gadadhara is Radha Herself; that is why the Lord, in the mood of Radha, called Pundarika “father,” and between
guru and disciple the love of their former kinship was revealed. <!-- F47 F48 F49 -->

Many years later, in Puri, this bond of guru and disciple was revealed once more:

“Once Sri Gadadhara Pandita asked Sri Chaitanya about the initiation into mantra that he had received. ‘I passed my
ishta-mantra on to someone, and now I do not feel its power when I chant. Please grant me this mantra again, and then my
mind will find joy.’ <…> The Lord said: ‘You already have a spiritual master, so be careful. You should not commit an
offense. What to speak of a mantra — I can give you even My life. But it would be improper to give you mantra while your
spiritual master is alive.’ Gadadhara replied: ‘But he is not here now, and You can do it on his behalf.’ Then Lord
Chaitanya said: ‘By the will of Providence your spiritual master, Pundarika Vidyanidhi, will soon come here, and you will
be able to meet him.’ <…> ‘You will meet him in ten days, for he will come straight to Me.’” (CB, Antya 10.22–28, 30)

And so it happened. When Vidyanidhi came, the Lord exclaimed in rapture, “Father has come!” — and Gadadhara went to his
guru and asked him to give him the ishta-mantra again, and he lovingly gave it.

“Thus Gadadhara Pandita had the opportunity to receive mantra again from Premanidhi. What more can be said of
Premanidhi's glory? The power of his divine love can be understood simply from the fact that he had such a disciple as
Gadadhara Pandita.” (CB, Antya 10.79–80) <!-- F50 Q7 -->

Srila Bhaktisiddhanta explains: mantra is sound that frees the mind from the pull toward enjoyment; if it is disclosed
to a faithless person, its action in the heart weakens, and then it must be heard again from one's own guru, not from
anyone else. But Gadadhara is an eternal associate of the Lord; no taint could be in him; his words “I do not feel its
power as before” are his humility, a lesson for future gurus and disciples. The “Anubhashya” cites this example too, in
speaking of the boy Puri Dasa (CC, Antya 16): a mantra received from the guru is not disclosed to others, or else it
loses its power. <!-- I2 F51 -->
