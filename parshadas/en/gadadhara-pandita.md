# Gadadhara Pandita
*Гададхара Пандит · @gadadhara-pandita*

## The Potency of the Devotee
Gadadhara Pandita Gosvami is one of the five forms in which the Lord appeared in Gaura-lila. Sri Gauranga, Nityananda,
Advaita, Gadadhara and Srivasa make up the Pancha-tattva: in essence They are not different, and They divided only to
relish the various tastes of devotional service. In this row Gadadhara is the energy, the shakti, of the devotee. So
too teaches the “Gaura-ganoddesha-dipika”: “And ‘the potency of the devotee’ is the foremost of brahmanas, Sri Gadadhara
Pandita” (GGD 11). Krishnadasa Kaviraja defines the place of each in the Pancha-tattva:

“These three truths we accept as worshipable by all; the fourth, the truth of the devotee, we know as the worshipper. The countless millions of devotees headed by Srivasa are all counted among the pure devotees (*shuddha-bhakta*). Gadadhara Pandita and others are incarnations of the Lord’s potency (*shakti*); they are counted as His intimate devotees” (CC, Adi 7.15–17)

Mahaprabhu is one; the two Prabhus, Nityananda and Advaita, serve Him, and these three are worthy of worship; Srivasa and
the others are the devotees who worship; and Gadadhara Pandita and those like him are embodiments of the Lord's
energies, His most confidential associates. All the mahajanas of the past unanimously recognized Gadadhara as one of the
Pancha-tattva. At the very beginning of the “Charitamrita” the author bows down to him:

“Gadadhara Pandita and the others are the Lord’s own potencies; at the feet of them all I offer thousands of obeisances” (CC, Adi 1.41) <!-- F1 F2 F3 F4 Q2 Q4 -->

Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati explains: Gadadhara Pandita is the first among those closest to Sri Chaitanya, the
source of all shakti-tattva, and therefore he is equally present in the lilas of Navadvipa and in the lilas of
Nilachala. When Gaura shows His oneness with Narayana, His shaktis are His consorts Lakshmipriya and Vishnupriya; when He
shows His oneness with Krishna, His shakti is Gadadhara. Together with Svarupa Damodara and Ramananda Raya, Gadadhara is
a shakti of madhura-rasa: they nourish and sustain the vipralambha, the separation, that Gaurasundara relishes.
<!-- F5 F106 F8 -->

The “Charitamrita” too speaks of this:

“Puri's love was chiefly parental; Ramananda's was pure friendship; Govinda and others had the rasa of pure servitude; and Gadadhara, Jagadananda and Svarupa knew the bliss of the chief rasa. By these four moods the Lord was held captive.” (CC, Madhya 2.78)

Srila Bhaktisiddhanta explains: Gadadhara, Jagadananda and Svarupa served the Lord in the principal rasa — madhura-rasa,
and the Lord, accepting their worship, company and service, was indebted to these devotees. <!-- F111 -->

Sri Chaitanya is glorified as “the life of Nityananda and Gadadhara”:

“Glory to Him, the very life of Nityananda and Gadadhara! Glory to Him, the treasure of the hearts of Advaita and
Srivasa!” (CB, Madhya 9.3)

Bhaktisiddhanta also unfolds this double title. Pure devotees who wish to worship Radha-Govinda in madhura-rasa take
shelter of Gadadhara, and they are considered Gauranga's confidential devotees; those not so inclined take shelter of
Nityananda. Therefore some call the Lord “the life of Nityananda,” and others “the life of Gadadhara.” Some of
Mahaprabhu's associates — Narahari Sarakara, for example — themselves became followers of Gadadhara: they knew that he
was dearest of all to the Lord, and therefore sought to serve him. <!-- F6 Q18 I1 F7 -->

He has several names. He is called “Gaura-shakti,” “Pandita Gosvami” (in Bengali, Pandita-gosani), “Gadadhara Deva,” and,
affectionately, Gadai. And because of him the Lord is called “Gadadhara-prananatha,” “the Lord of Gadadhara's life,” and
“Gadai-Gauranga,” “the Gauranga who belongs to Gadadhara”; these names were current already in the time of Krishnadasa
Kaviraja, and he himself cites them in the “Charitamrita” (Antya 7.159–160; on this, see below). <!-- F9 F10 -->

Narahari Chakravarti in the “Bhakti-ratnakara” opens his chapters with glorifications in which Gadadhara stands beside
Nityananda, Advaita and Srivasa: “Glory, glory to Gadadhara Pandita Gosani, the foremost of the Lord's shaktis: there is
no end to your virtues!” (1.20); “Glory to Gadadhara, the son of Ratnavati!” (9.2); “Glory to Gadadhara, whose life is
Gauranga!” (14.2). He teaches the Lord to be invoked as “Lord of Gadadhara's life” (2.177), and of the mercy of Gadadhara
and the other shaktis of the Lord he says: “to whom did they not mercifully give bhakti for Krishna?” (1.521). The late
“Jagadisha-charitra-vijaya” (18th century) bows to him and Srivasa as “the two vessels of Chaitanya's prema, who descended
as [His] devotee and [His] shakti,” and exclaims: “Glory, glory to Gadadhara, the shakti of Chaitanya!” (1.18–19).

## Family and Birth
Gadadhara was born in the village of Beleti in the district of Chittagong (now in Bangladesh)[^1] in the year 1408 of the
Shaka era (1486), on the new moon of the month of Vaishakha; this day, the amavasya of Vaishakha, is honored as the day
of his appearance. Whether he was the same age as the Lord or a year younger, the biographies say differently[^2]. His
father, Madhava Mishra, was a Varendra brahmana of the Kashyapa gotra; his mother's name was Ratnavati Devi, and his
younger brother's, Vaninatha. <!-- F11 F12 F13 F14 F15 -->

Chittagong gave Gaura-lila many associates: Mukunda Datta and Vasudeva Datta also came from there, and Madhava Mishra
was a close friend of Pundarika Vidyanidhi — his son's future guru. The family lived in Beleti until Gadadhara was twelve
years old, and then moved to Navadvipa. Their house stood in Mayapur, next door to the house of Jagannatha Mishra;
Ratnavati revered Shachi Devi as an elder sister, and the two were almost never apart. <!-- F18 F16 F17 -->

## Childhood beside Nimai
So from childhood Gadadhara became Nimai's inseparable companion. Gaurahari often played with him, they went together to
the village school, and Gadadhara loved Him so much that he could not part from Him for a moment. From his earliest
years he was the Lord's closest devotee and His friend. <!-- F19 F21 -->

In temperament they were unlike. Gadadhara grew up serious and restrained, calm, and fond of solitude; from a tender age
he was indifferent to worldly pleasures, strict and detached in daily life, and so — a brahmachari — he remained all his
life. Nimai, in those years, seemed restless and wilful. At school He plagued Gadadhara with tricky questions that threw
him into confusion; this hindered his studies, and Gadadhara tried to keep his distance from Him — yet he could not bear
separation either. Once Nimai told him: “Gadadhara! Soon I shall become such a Vaishnava that Brahma and Shiva will come
to bow down to Me.” <!-- F20 F22 -->

Later, when Nimai had become the foremost scholar of Navadvipa, no one dared to argue with Him: He would first refute any
thesis and then defend it Himself. Mukunda, Srivasa and the other devotees avoided these debates. Once Nimai asked
Gadadhara what liberation is. He answered according to nyaya: the final and complete cessation of suffering
(atyantika-duhkha-nivritti). Nimai showed that this definition was insufficient, and the devotees, listening, thought how
good it would be if such a scholar became a devotee. Nishikanta Sanyal, a disciple of Srila Bhaktisiddhanta, tells it
thus: “Another day in course of His peripatetic wanderings the Lord of Vaikuntha fell in with Gadadhar. The Lord
laughingly caught him by both hands and would not quit His hold of him. ‘You study Nyaya. Tell me something about it.’”
Gadadhara held to the position of the Nyaya Shastra, “according to which liberation (mukti) ensues on the cessation or
destruction of the extreme miseries,” and “the Lord of the goddess of learning found fault with the proposition in many
ways.” <!-- F23 F153 -->

In those same years Ishvara Puri came to Navadvipa and stayed for several months in the house of Gopinatha Acharya (CB,
Adi 11.96)[^3]. He grew fond of Gadadhara: he saw in the youth an innate detachment, present since childhood, he saw
streams of tears of love for Krishna — and with affection he began to teach him his book “Sri Krishna-lilamrita,”
straight from the manuscript. As the “Bhakti-ratnakara” (12.2205–2207) reports, it was in the house of Gopinatha Acharya
that Puri wrote this book; “here, together with Gadadhara Pandita, there was discussion of the scriptures — and great
amusement in it” (12.2210). Vrindavana Dasa relates:

“Ishvara Puri began to teach Gadadhara Pandita from his book ‘Sri Krishna-lilamrita.’” (CB, Adi 11.100)

Every day, while Puri was teaching Gadadhara, Nimai would come and bow to Puri; once Puri asked Him to correct the
mistakes in the book, and Nimai replied that Krishna accepts any glorification composed by His devotee, even an imperfect
one (CB, Adi 11.105–110). <!-- F24 Q10 F25 F125 -->

Once Gauranga took Gadadhara with Him to the house of Advaita Acharya — to see what He was doing. Advaita was preparing
for the puja of His household Deity and loudly singing the name of Hari; hearing this, Gauranga fell unconscious before
Him, and Advaita worshipped His feet with the articles of puja and began to sing a hymn to Govinda. Gadadhara bit his
tongue in astonishment and objected: it is not fitting for a venerable elder to worship the feet of a boy. Advaita
smiled: “Gadadhara, in a few days you will know who this boy is.” And Gadadhara began to wonder: could Gauranga Himself
be the One for whose sake Advaita had performed all His worship? The “Bhakti-ratnakara” (12.2338–2348) shows this place
in Navadvipa: “Seeing Advaita's actions, Gadadhara laughs; biting his tongue with his teeth, he says in a soft voice: ‘You
should bestow blessings that bring good; to treat a boy like this is not proper’”; hearing Advaita's reply, “in his mind
he reflects: ‘Surely this is the Lord.’” <!-- F26 -->

## The Awakening of Love
When the Lord returned from Gaya, the first to see His transformations of love was Shriman Pandita; he told the devotees
of them, and the Lord appointed a meeting with them in the house of Shuklambara Brahmachari. Hearing of this from
Shriman, Gadadhara hurried after him and hid in the house: the Lord would surely speak of Krishna — and he wanted to
listen, if only in secret. The Lord spoke on the veranda, while Gadadhara sat inside. When the Lord, crying
“Where is Krishna?”, fell to the ground, Gadadhara in the next room also lost consciousness (CB, Madhya 1.86–88)[^4]. Coming to Himself, the Lord asked
who was hiding there, and Shuklambara replied: “It is Your Gadadhara” (1.96). Vrindavana Dasa continues:

“Gadadhara lowered his head and wept. Seeing this, Vishvambhara felt deep satisfaction. He said: ‘O Gadadhara, you are
very fortunate. From your very childhood you have been endowed with strong faith in Krishna. But I have wasted My life on
meaningless pursuits. Recently I found a priceless treasure, but by misfortune I have lost it.’” (CB, Madhya 1.97–99)

The Lord embraced him. Witnesses of His transformations in the house of Shuklambara were Gadadhara, Sadashiva, Murari,
Srivasa, Shriman and others. From that time it became hard for Gadadhara to part from the Lord even for an hour; seeing
His tears of love, he too would weep. <!-- F27 F28 Q1 F29 -->

Once Gadadhara brought the Lord betel. The Lord, tormented by separation from Krishna, turned to him with a question:

“Seeing Gadadhara, Vishvambhara asked: ‘Where is that dark-complexioned Krishna in yellow garments?’ <…> In great
agitation Gadadhara Mahashaya answered: ‘Krishna always dwells in Your heart.’ But as soon as Mahaprabhu heard that
Krishna was in His heart, He began to tear at His chest with His nails. Gadadhara at once seized Vishvambhara by the
hands and calmed Him, speaking many consoling words. Gadadhara said: ‘Be patient, Krishna will come now.’ Mother Shachi
saw this. Mother Shachi was very pleased with Gadadhara. She thought: ‘I have never met such a clever youth before. I
cannot approach my son out of fear. Yet this youth was able to calm Him so skilfully.’” (CB, Madhya 2.203, 205–210)

“Let go of My hands!” the Lord pleaded. “If I do not see Krishna, I cannot live.” Shachi asked Gadadhara always to stay by
Nimai, and from then on he calmed the Lord whenever the waves of love overwhelmed Him. <!-- F30 Q8 -->

The “Bhakti-ratnakara” adds one more day to this. Returning from Srivasa's and having bathed, the Lord went to perform
the worship of Vishnu, but tears flooded His face, chest and cloth: “Gaura-raya cannot stop the streams from His eyes; at
the Lord's command Gadadhara worships Vishnu. The Lord is deeply immersed in the pastimes of Vraja; ceaselessly Gadadhara
speaks of them” (12.2107–2108). <!-- F128 -->

In the Navadvipa-lila Gadadhara became the Lord's chief helper. Those who in Vrindavana are Radha and Krishna are here, on
the banks of the Ganga, Gaura and Gadadhara; and the cowherd girls and boys of Vrindavana take part in this lila as the
performers of kirtana. Once the Lord, strolling by the Ganga, sat down in a grove and became absorbed in remembering the
pastimes of Vrindavana. Mukunda began to sing of purva-raga — the first awakening of love; Gadadhara gathered flowers in
the forest and placed a garland on the Lord, as Radha adorned Krishna, and some began to sing, some to dance. Then
Gauranga seated Gadadhara beside Him on a raised place at the foot of a tree. Advaita performed arati to Them both,
Nityananda sat on the left, Srivasa decorated Them with flowers, Narahari fanned Them with a chamara, Shuklambara offered
sandalwood paste, Murari sang Their glories, and Madhava, Vasudeva, Purushottama, Vijaya, Mukunda and others sang in
various ragas. <!-- F31 F108 F32 -->

The “Bhakti-ratnakara” (12.2008–2028) tells of the same arati through a song: after going round the town with
sankirtana, “at the Lord's hint, Gadadhara here creates the Lord's attire with ornaments of flowers,” while Dasa Gadadhara
and Narahari bring all that is needed; Advaita performs the arati, Nityananda on the right, “on the left, Gadadhara, full
of grace.” Late at night, when all had gone, Gadadhara prepared the Lord's bed: “In this room Vishvambhara lay down; near
[Him] lay Gadadhara Pandita. Drinking the nectar of each other's words, both were absorbed [in it]” (12.2033–2034). So it
was every day — as both Sanskrit poets, Murari Gupta and Kavi Karnapura, write, whose verses Narahari Chakravarti quotes
right there. Murari relates: “One night, while with him, the Lord spoke auspicious words: ‘In the morning you will give My
prasada to the Vaishnavas’”; “every day Gadadhara joyfully anointed Him with sandal paste and constantly adorned His body
with garlands” (Murari 2.3.11, 15). In Karnapura the Lord tells Gadadhara, lying near Him: “This nirmalya on My chest is to
be taken off [and given] to these [devotees],” and in the morning Gadadhara gives each exactly what the Lord had assigned
(“Mahakavya” 6.12–14). <!-- F129 F143 -->

In Karnapura's “Mahakavya” Gadadhara is especially close to the Lord in the days of the mahaprakasha. When the Lord wished
to go into the inner rooms of Srivasa's house, “Srivasa said to Gadadhara: ‘Let the couch and everything else be carried
there.’ Thus addressed, Gadadhara, boundlessly melted with love and forgetful of what he himself was doing, carried
everything there”; he wove a multitude of garlands — “as if he were weaving them from the desires of his own heart” — and
anointed the Lord with sandalwood, aguru, camphor and kunkuma “from the toes to the beautiful expanse of the forehead”
(5.55, 62, 66). For eight months, from Jyeshtha to the end of Pausha, the Lord danced every day in Srivasa's house —

“And Gadadhara Pandita, best of the virtuous, always abiding close beside Him, day after day, filled with ardent longing,
serves Him — his own life, his most beloved.” (“Mahakavya” 5.128) <!-- F141 F142 -->

The “Bhakti-ratnakara” quotes this verse too. And in the songs it cites — songs of Narahari Sarakara, Vasudeva Ghosha and
other poets — Gaura in Navadvipa is almost always beside Gadadhara: in the mood of Radha He walks along the Ganga “taking
the hand of His beloved Gadadhara,” calls “Radha!”, “Lalita, Vishakha!” and, “seeing the Suradhuni, the Lord sees in it the
Yamuna”; at the festival of Radhika's birth in Pundarika's house, dressed as a cowherd, He “softly, softly smiles and looks
toward Gadadhara”; in a flower garden He plays dice with him; at Holi He swoons in his embrace and asks: “Where is My
flute? Where is Govardhana, where the bank of the Yamuna?” — and Shivananda answers: “Where the Lord [and] Gadadhara are,
there is the treasury of rasa” (12.2985–3075, 3178–3184, 3230–3234, 3343–3349). One of the songs says: “My Gauranga dances
in Gadadhara's rasa; and Gadadhara dances in Gauranga's pastimes” (12.3069)[^5]. Similar pictures are drawn by the late
“Gaura-charita-chintamani” of the same Narahari Chakravarti: at dawn, hearing Gaura in His sleep speak anxiously about the
waking of Rai in the kunja, Gadadhara awakes and “gazes unblinking, with delight in his eyes” at Him and gently serves His
feet (2.14–2.16); there too Shachi asks Gadadhara to persuade Nimai to bathe — “You know how to persuade Him; He will heed
[your] words” (17.12). <!-- F130 F152 -->

Gadadhara was with the Lord in all His pastimes: at the night kirtanas in the houses of Srivasa and Chandrashekhara (CB,
Madhya 8.111–112), in the water pastimes after the deliverance of Jagai and Madhai, at the mahaprakasha in Srivasa's
courtyard, in the procession to the Kazi, and later at the sannyasa; in Puri — at the cleaning of the Gundicha temple,
at the bathing in Narendra lake, and in many other pastimes. At one of the kirtanas behind closed doors, still before
the procession to the Kazi, Nityananda and Gadadhara whirled about holding hands, while Advaita, like a lion, rushed
about the room (CB, Madhya 23.30)[^6]. When the Vaishnavas spoke of Nityananda and Gauranga — two Lords who honor each
other — and Srivasa compared Them to Madhava and Shankara, Gadadhara took it up: “Well said; it is like the story of
Rama and Lakshmana.” <!-- F33 F35 F109 F122 -->

Going round Navadvipa with Srinivasa, the “Bhakti-ratnakara” names other such moments. At the first meeting with
Nityananda in the house of Nandana Acharya, when the Lord took Nityananda on His lap, “seeing it, Gadadhara smiles softly,
softly” (12.2442); when the Lord sat on the throne of Vishnu awaiting Advaita, “Gadadhara offers tambula to [His] mouth”
(12.2474); after the Lord had consoled Srivasa's family on the death of their son, He “went to His own home, taking
Gadadhara with Him” (12.3462); in the procession to the Kazi the Lord went with His own group — “with [Him] are Nityananda
and Sri Pandita Gadadhara” (12.3094). In Karnapura, in Advaita's house, the Lord stands “placing His right arm on the
shoulder of Srivasa, the ornament of the brahmanas, and the other on the shoulder of Gadadhara” (“Mahakavya” 6.61) — and
here too Gadadhara is at Gaura's left hand. <!-- F126 F144 -->

A special place among these pastimes belongs to the drama in the house of Chandrashekhara Acharya. The Lord Himself
assigned the roles: Haridasa was the village watchman, Srivasa was Narada, He Himself was Rukmini, and Gadadhara too was
Rukmini, with Brahmananda as her old friend Suprabha (CB, Madhya 18.9). In the second prahara of the evening Gadadhara
came out together with Brahmananda, dressed as a maiden of Vraja, his heart overflowing with love, and began to dance:

“Hearing Advaita's words, Gadadhara was very satisfied. Then he began to dance under the influence of ecstatic love.
Gadadhara danced wonderfully in the garments of Rama, the goddess of fortune, and his ‘companion’ sang songs befitting
the occasion. <…> At that moment Gadadhara was like the Ganga personified. In truth, he is a manifestation of Krishna's
internal energy. Lord Chaitanya said again and again: ‘In Vaikuntha Gadadhara is My wife.’” (CB, Madhya 18.111–112,
115–116)

Then the Lord appeared as the original energy, adya-shakti, the Mother of the universe[^7]. <!-- F34 Q11 -->

Kavi Karnapura in the “Mahakavya” describes this entrance thus: Shuklambara, as Narada's disciple, “spoke most sweetly to
the Lord's beloved one named Gadadhara” (11.8), and then “the one named Gadadhara entered with the singers, having assumed
the appearance of a woman's role with all its adornments, his hands shining with bangles of conch-shell. As the mridanga
resounded very swiftly and loudly, he shone there with rhythm, measure and the art of dancing” (11.22). <!-- F145 -->

That Gaura and Gadadhara are inseparable is told also by the “Navadvipa-dhama-mahatmya”: the pious king Suvarna Sena of
Suvarna-vihara in Godrumadvipa, by the mercy of Narada, saw in a dream Gaura and Gadadhara with all Their associates,
and on waking grieved that the vision was gone. <!-- F36 -->

## Pundarika Vidyanidhi
In those days the Lord would weep and call: “Pundarika, My father!” — but none of the devotees knew Pundarika. He had in
fact come to Navadvipa, but he went unrecognized: he hid his deep devotion and carried himself like a pampered lover of
pleasure. Only Mukunda and Vasudeva Datta, his countrymen, knew him. Gadadhara, though he came from the same parts, did
not know him — according to one biography, only in appearance, for the sake of the lila. <!-- F37 F38 -->

Gadadhara was especially attached to Mukunda, and Mukunda loved him dearly: whenever a sadhu or sannyasi came to
Navadvipa, Mukunda would call Gadadhara and they would go to him together. And now Mukunda said[^8] that he would
introduce him to an extraordinary Vaishnava, not of this world — “and as a reward, remember me as your servant.”
Gadadhara gladly went at once. Mukunda presented him: “This is Gadadhara. From childhood he has been indifferent to the
world; in worldly terms he is the son of the brahmana Madhava Mishra; from an early age he has kept to renunciation and
is dear to all the Vaishnavas; he has come because he heard your name.” Vidyanidhi was overjoyed and spoke with him
warmly[^9]. <!-- F39 F40 F41 -->

But what Gadadhara saw troubled him. Pundarika sat like a prince: a high bed, silk sheets whiter than milk foam, soft
pillows, a canopy, costly clothes, incense and perfumes, five or seven little jars, pan in a brass box, a hookah; two
servants fanned him with peacock-feather fans; on his forehead were the urdhva-pundra and a red dot of abir, his hair
was well groomed, and before him stood an ornate palanquin. “If he is a Vaishnava,” Gadadhara thought, “why does he look
so much like a worldly man?” — and he was disappointed. <!-- F42 -->

Mukunda understood his doubt and wished to reveal who Pundarika really was. He sweetly sang a verse of the “Bhagavatam”
about Putana — how Krishna granted the position of a mother even to the demoness who came to poison Him[^10]. With a cry
of “Ha Krishna!” Pundarika fell unconscious. He wept, “Krishna! Krishna!” — his tears flowed like the descending Ganga,
he rolled on the floor, overturned the hookah, tore his clothes; all eight sattvika-bhavas appeared on him, without the
slightest artifice. Gadadhara was astounded. Srila Bhaktisiddhanta, commenting on this story, warns: a Vaishnava cannot
be recognized by his outward appearance; devotees of Krishna sometimes hide themselves in the guise of worldly men, and
whoever judges by dress loses faith in the Vaishnava-guru. So Gadadhara's doubt too is no flaw in an eternal associate,
but a part of the lila through which the Lord gives this lesson. <!-- F43 F44 I6 F115 -->

Remorse seized Gadadhara: “I took a mahabhagavata for an ordinary lover of pleasure and offended him. I will take mantra
from him — and be forgiven; I see no other way”[^11]. He opened his heart to Mukunda, and Mukunda, waiting until
Vidyanidhi came to himself two praharas later, conveyed it: “Gadadhara had a flaw in his thoughts; in atonement he wishes
to receive mantra from you.” Pundarika was happy: “The Lord has brought me together with a great jewel: such a disciple
is obtained only after many, many births. On the coming shukla-dvadashi, at the most auspicious hour, Gadadhara will
receive diksha.” <!-- F45 F46 -->

Gadadhara and Mukunda came to the Lord, told Him of Vidyanidhi's arrival (and it was then that Pundarika met the Lord),
and Gadadhara said that he had decided to take initiation from him. The Lord was delighted: “Quickly, quickly!” On the
appointed auspicious day Gadadhara received mantra from Pundarika Vidyanidhi. Thus two persons dear to Krishna Chaitanya
became for each other a worthy guru and a worthy disciple; Gadadhara enacted the lila of choosing his guru — and through
Pundarika, a disciple of Madhavendra Puri, entered his line. In Krishna-lila Pundarika is Vrishabhanu, the father of
Radha, and Gadadhara is Radha Herself; that is why the Lord, in the mood of Radha, called Pundarika “father,” and between
guru and disciple the love of their former kinship was revealed. <!-- F47 F48 F49 -->

The “Bhakti-ratnakara” shows the place of this initiation in Navadvipa: Pundarika's house, where “Pandita Gosani came
here with Mukunda to see him” — “Gadadhara Pandita became [his] disciple here” (12.2509, 2527). Narahari Chakravarti also
preserves Gadadhara's words of repentance to Mukunda: “I have committed an offence; you have saved me” (12.2521), and sums
up: “A worthy guru, Pundarika; the disciple, Gadadhara; both are the body of Sri Krishna Chaitanya” (12.2526). <!-- F127 -->

Many years later, in Puri, this bond of guru and disciple was revealed once more:

“Once Sri Gadadhara Pandita asked Sri Chaitanya about the initiation into mantra that he had received. ‘I passed my
ishta-mantra on to someone, and now I do not feel its power when I chant. Please grant me this mantra again, and then my
mind will find joy.’ <…> The Lord said: ‘You already have a spiritual master, so be careful. You should not commit an
offense. What to speak of a mantra — I can give you even My life. But it would be improper to give you mantra while your
spiritual master is alive.’ Gadadhara replied: ‘But he is not here now, and You can do it on his behalf.’ Then Lord
Chaitanya said: ‘By the will of Providence your spiritual master, Pundarika Vidyanidhi, will soon come here, and you will
be able to meet him.’ <…> ‘You will meet him in ten days, for he will come straight to Me.’” (CB, Antya 10.22–28, 30)

And so it happened. When Vidyanidhi came, the Lord exclaimed in rapture, “Father has come!” — and Gadadhara went to his
guru and asked him to give him the ishta-mantra again, and he lovingly gave it.

“Thus Gadadhara Pandita had the opportunity to receive mantra again from Premanidhi. What more can be said of
Premanidhi's glory? The power of his divine love can be understood simply from the fact that he had such a disciple as
Gadadhara Pandita.” (CB, Antya 10.79–80) <!-- F50 Q7 -->

Srila Bhaktisiddhanta explains: mantra is sound that frees the mind from the pull toward enjoyment; if it is disclosed
to a faithless person, its action in the heart weakens, and then it must be heard again from one's own guru, not from
anyone else. But Gadadhara is an eternal associate of the Lord; no taint could be in him; his words “I do not feel its
power” are his humility, a lesson for future gurus and disciples. The “Anubhashya” cites this example too, in
speaking of the boy Puri Dasa (CC, Antya 16): a mantra received from the guru is not disclosed to others, or else it
loses its power. <!-- I2 F51 -->

## The Lord's Sannyasa and the Road to Puri
Govinda Ghosha in his song tells how Gadadhara, hearing that the Lord would leave Navadvipa, ran to Mukunda: “Mukunda,
my life! Did I really hear it rightly today? I speak — and die; the words will not leave my lips: Gauranga is leaving
Navadvipa… I ran to you without saying anything more to Him; do what you can — as for us, I think we shall not live.”
And Mukunda, looking into his face, wept. According to the “Bhakti-ratnakara,” when the devotees understood that the
Lord would soon take sannyasa, they went in distress to Mukunda and then “to the house of Gadadhara Pandita” — and, on
seeing him, “lost all composure” (12.3504–3505). <!-- F110 -->

Gadadhara was beside the Lord on His last night at home in Navadvipa as well: according to the “Chaitanya-bhagavata”
(Madhya 28.43–48), he and Haridasa slept near Him, and before dawn, waking, he said: “I will go with You”; he was
present at the sannyasa itself too: to Kantaka-nagara (Katwa) after the Lord went “Nityananda-deva, Sri Pandita
Gadadhara, Sri Mukunda Datta and Sri Chandrashekhara” (“Bhakti-ratnakara” 12.3559). When
the Lord, having accepted sannyasa, set out from Shantipura for Puri, Gadadhara Pandita was among the few who went with
Him — together with Nityananda, Jagadananda and Mukunda Datta; Karnapura writes that the Lord went “placing Nityananda,
joyful at heart, in front,” surrounded by those devoted to His feet — “Gadadhara, the brahmanas and others, together with
Mukunda” (“Mahakavya” 11.76)[^12]. So, when the Nadiya-lila came to its close and the
Lord settled in Puri, Gadadhara followed Him and stayed there as well. In the “Charitamrita” he is named first among
those who lived with the Lord in Puri:

“Gadadhara Pandita Gosani lived in Nilachala, as did Vakreshvara, Damodara, Shankara and Haridasa.” (CC,
Madhya 1.252) <!-- F52 F53 F54 F58 Q14 F146 -->

## Tota-Gopinatha
In Puri Gadadhara manifested the lila of kshetra-sannyasa — he lived having vowed never to leave the holy land of
Purushottama, and Vakreshvara and other associates lived with him in Nilachala. Mahaprabhu
entrusted him with the service of the Deity of Gopinatha and told him to live in Yameshvara-tota, a garden by the sea,
near the temple of Shiva Yameshvara; after this garden (tota) the Deity is called Tota-Gopinatha. The Lord constantly
came to him and became absorbed with him in talks about Krishna. In the hot month of Jyeshtha He stayed with Gadadhara
in Yameshvara-tota and from there called Sanatana Gosvami to Him at midday — and Sanatana came across the burning sand.
As Abhirama manifested the service of Gopinatha in Khanakula, and Madhu Pandita in Vrindavana, so Gadadhara Pandita
manifested in Purushottama-dhama the service of Gopinatha together with the daughter of Vrishabhanu and
Ananga-manjari (Jahnava); a song sings of it: “The Lord of Gadadhara's life, the Lord of Jahnava's life — Sri
Gopinatha.” <!-- F56 F59 F57 F120 -->

Why Gadadhara bound himself by these vows is explained by Mamu Gosvami, his disciple, in an account that the
“Bhakti-ratnakara” preserves: “Service to Gopinatha and residence in Kshetra — these were the Gosvami's two vows; there was
no going anywhere else. Sri Gaurasundara would be staying in Nilachala; that was the reason for the vow: His company is
hard to leave” (8.290–291). The vow was for the Lord's sake — and so, “when Gauranga went from Kshetra elsewhere, the
Gosvami would abandon his vow and go with Him” (8.292; on this, see below). <!-- F131 -->

When Nityananda came to Puri and arrived at the Tota, Gadadhara, learning of it, broke off his reading of the
“Bhagavatam” and went out to meet Him; Nityananda had brought fine rice from Gauda for Gopinatha, Gadadhara cooked it,
and Mahaprabhu too came to the prasada of Gopinatha — the three ate together, and the devotees snatched up the remnants
of the meal of Nitai-Gaura-Gadadhara. <!-- F60 -->

Mamu Gosvami, showing Narottama this place, recalled the details. Meeting Nityananda, Gadadhara embraced Him and both
wept; Vrindavana Dasa says of him: “Such is the resolve of Gadadhara-deva: he does not look at the face of one who reviles
Nityananda” (quoted in the “Bhakti-ratnakara” 8.329). Nityananda had brought the rice from Gauda so that the Pandita
would offer it to Gopinatha and partake of it himself; Gadadhara cooked it, with greens picked in Tota and a sour dish of
tender tamarind leaves. Then Gaura came: “Such a secret affair of you two! Don't you know that I have a share in it?” —
and, dividing the rice into three parts, sat down to eat: “On the right Sri Nityananda, on the left the Pandita”
(8.333–345). <!-- F132 -->

In the house of Gadadhara, “[the servant] of Gopinatha,” the Lord once received news from Vraja: He told the devotees He
had long had no news from there and wished to send a letter — and at that very moment a letter came from Rupa and
Sanatana about the arrival of Gopala Bhatta in Vrindavana. From here the Lord sent them a reply together with His own
waist-cord, kaupina and outer cloth (“Bhakti-ratnakara” 1.167–194). Beginning this account, Narahari Chakravarti says: “The
feelings Gorachand has for Gadadhara are understood only as the fruit of great pious merit”; “Srivasa Pandita loves
Gadadhara Pandita more than life”; “Sri Gaurasundara is Gadadhara's life” (1.168, 171, 174). <!-- F124 -->

Gadadhara's chief service in Puri was the reading of the “Bhagavatam.” By the Lord's order he preached the “Bhagavata”
every day on the shore of Narendra lake, and at the Tota he expounded it, sinking into trance — he loved best of all the
stories of Prahlada and Dhruva. His explanations were like an ocean of nectar. Gaurasundara would sit down to listen,
and at his reading all eight sattvika-bhavas appeared in Him — just as at the kirtana of Svarupa Damodara: the master
of the “Bhagavata” was Gadadhara, the master of kirtana was Svarupa. Vakreshvara, together with the Lord, Advaita and
others, would go to Tota-Gopinatha to hear his discourses. Hence the lesson: the “Bhagavata”-book must be heard from the
“bhagavata”-devotee. Centuries later Bhaktivinoda Thakura likewise heard the “Bhagavata” from Gopinatha Pandita.
Vrindavana Dasa calls Gadadhara the preacher of the “Bhagavata,” boundlessly dear to Gaura and Nityananda and
non-different from Them; and in the lila of Mahaprabhu, Who is Himself the embodiment of separation
(vipralambha-vigraha), Gadadhara nourishes and sustains this rasa of separation and helps Him in preaching kirtana.
Mamu Gosvami also showed Narottama the very place of these readings: “My lord Gadadhara, sitting here, would read the
Srimad-Bhagavatam, his heart overwhelmed… Prabhu Gaurahari, the Lord of Gadadhara's life, sitting here, would listen to
the sweetness of that exposition”; nearby, at the time of the Ratha-yatra, sat Nityananda, Advaita, Svarupa Damodara,
Vakreshvara, Murari, Dasa Gadadhara, Mukunda, Narahari, Ramananda; here too Gadadhara conversed in seclusion with Dasa
Gadadhara, Narahari and Damodara (“Bhakti-ratnakara” 8.275–287). <!-- F61 I5 F62 F99 F100 -->

When Gadadhara Pandita, Bhagavan Acharya or Sarvabhauma invited the Lord, He accepted their invitation even if He had
already been invited by others, and ate at their homes just as they wished (CC, Antya 8.88–89)[^13]. And to Vallabha
Bhatta the Lord named Gadadhara Pandita among the Vaishnavas born in Bengal who carry the holy name to the world:

“‘Acharyaratna, Acharyanidhi, Gadadhara Pandita, Jagadananda, Damodara, Shankara, Vakreshvara, Kashishvara, Mukunda, Vasudeva, Murari and all the other devotees who descended in Gauda have preached the name of Krishna and prema throughout the world, and whatever devotion to Krishna I have, I have gained in their company.’” (CC, Antya 7.48–50) <!-- F63 F64 -->

## The Test: Vallabha Bhatta
With this same Vallabha Bhatta is connected a story in which the nature of Gadadhara's love is seen with special
clarity. Krishnadasa Kaviraja begins it thus:

“Gadadhara Pandita's *bhava* was pure and deep, with a compliant nature (*dakshina*) like that of Rukmini-devi. The Lord wanted to see his loving anger (*pranaya-rosha*), but because Gadadhara was conscious of the Lord's majesty, anger never arose in him. Taking this occasion, the Lord feigned anger, and when the Pandita heard of it, fear arose in his heart. So it was long ago: when Krishna made fun of Rukmini, fear arose in her mind as she heard Him.” (CC, Antya 7.140–143)

Gadadhara was by nature simple and loving. Vallabha, proud of his learning, came to the Lord, and the Lord, to cure him of
pride, withdrew from him and found fault with everything he said. Then Vallabha began to visit Gadadhara, showing him
affection. Gadadhara came to love him, and this love became the occasion for a test: the Lord, as if displeased with
their closeness, outwardly feigned anger and grew cool toward Gadadhara too. Gadadhara was afraid that the Lord would
reject him. <!-- F65 -->

Vallabha had formerly worshipped Bala-Gopala, Krishna as an infant; in the company of Gadadhara his heart changed:

“Vallabha Bhatta worshipped in the mood of parental love (*vatsalya*); he served with the Bala-gopala mantra. In the company of the Pandita his heart changed, and he gave his mind to the worship of Kishora-gopala. He wanted to receive the mantra and so on from the Pandita, but the Pandita said, ‘This is not for me to do. I am dependent; my Lord is Gaurachandra. Without His order I am not free to act on my own.’” (CC, Antya 7.144–147) <!-- Q16 -->

A few days later the Lord showered His mercy on Vallabha and, on the day of his invitation, sent Svarupa Damodara and
others for Gadadhara. On the way Svarupa said: “The Lord was only testing you. Why did you not come and tell Him
everything, instead of enduring it as if in fear?” Gadadhara replied: “The Lord knows everything about everyone; how
could I be insolent to Him? I will bear whatever He says, and I trust that He will soon be merciful to me.” The duty of
one who loves, he understood, is to accept from the beloved Lord any severity, for that too is affection. Arriving, he
fell weeping at the Lord's feet:

“Smiling slightly, the Lord embraced him and spoke sweet words for all to hear: ‘I showed you anger, but you were not moved. You said nothing in anger; you bore it all. My gestures did not move your mind. By your firm, simple-hearted love you have bought Me.’ The marks of the Pandita's *bhava* cannot be described; by them the Lord came to be named ‘Gadadhara-prananatha’ (Lord of Gadadhara's life). The Lord's favour to the Pandita cannot be described. People sing of Him as ‘Gadai-Gauranga’.” (CC, Antya 7.156–160) <!-- F66 Q5 I4 -->

So by simplicity and patience Gadadhara “bought” the Lord. The next day he himself invited the Lord and His associates,
and there Vallabha asked Mahaprabhu's permission to take initiation from Gadadhara; the Lord permitted it (CC, Antya
7.166–167). Thus Vallabha, who had formerly served the Bala-Gopala mantra, by Gadadhara's mercy received from the Pandita
Gosvami initiation into the Kishora-Gopala mantra. <!-- F67 -->

## Cuttack: A Love That Knows No Vows
The time came when the Lord decided to go to Vrindavana. Ramananda Raya and Sarvabhauma long held Him back, but after
Vijaya-dashami He set out after all — by way of Bengal; King Prataparudra saw to His journey, and beyond the river
Chitrotpala Ramananda, Mardaraja and Hari-chandana went with Him. Gadadhara's vow of kshetra-sannyasa did not allow him
to go further, but he could not bear the separation. The Lord reminded him of his vow and forbade him to come — and then
an argument took place between them, which Krishnadasa Kaviraja relates thus: <!-- F68 -->

“When Gadadhara Pandita set out with them, the Lord forbade him: ‘Do not give up your *kshetra-sannyasa*.’ The Pandita said, ‘Wherever You are, that is Nilachala. Let my *kshetra-sannyasa* go to Rasatala!’ The Lord said, ‘Stay here and serve Gopinatha.’ The Pandita said, ‘Seeing Your feet is worth ten million services.’ The Lord said, ‘If you give up the service, the fault will fall on Me. Stay here and serve — that will please Me.’ The Pandita said, ‘All the fault will fall on me. I shall not go with You; I shall go alone. I shall go to see Ai (Mother Shachi), not for Your sake. The fault of giving up my vow and my service — that I shall bear myself.’” (CC, Madhya 16.130–135) <!-- F69 Q6 -->

The Lord went on northward, and Gadadhara followed, alone[^14]. In Cuttack, about a hundred kilometres from Puri, the
Lord called him to Him:

“The Pandita's love for Gauranga is beyond understanding: he gave up his vow and the service of Sri Krishna as though they were a blade of grass. Inwardly the Lord was pleased with his conduct, but He took him by the hand and spoke with loving anger: ‘You meant to give up your vow and your service, and you have succeeded: you have left them behind and come to a distant land. In wanting to stay with Me, you seek your own happiness. But you are failing in both your duties, and that makes Me sad. If you want My happiness, go back to Nilachala. I bind you by My oath — not one word more.’ Saying this, Mahaprabhu got into the boat, and the Pandita fell there unconscious.” (CC, Madhya 16.137–142) <!-- F70 F71 -->

Gadadhara bore the separation from the Lord harder than anyone[^15], and his separation knew no bounds. The Lord
entrusted him to Sarvabhauma — to console him and escort him back — and went on Himself. Sarvabhauma reminded Gadadhara
how Krishna broke His own promise so as not to let Bhishma break his word: so too Mahaprabhu, parting from him against
His own heart, was protecting his vow. And both, overcome by the grief of separation, returned to Puri (CC, Madhya
16.143–147). Such “rebellious” love — the readiness to abandon vows, service, everything for the Lord — is understood
only by the closest: love on the path of spontaneous devotion is not easy to grasp. <!-- F72 F73 I3 -->

The Lord was then going to Gauda; in Ramakeli, where He bestowed His mercy on Rupa and Sanatana, the “Charitamrita”
names Gadadhara too among His intimate associates; the Lord told them all to bless the two brothers (CC, Madhya
1.219–220)[^16]. <!-- F55 -->

Until the Lord returned, Gadadhara, in Mamu's words, “passed his days here in great distress”; and “when Sri Gaurasundara
came here from Vrindavana together with His dear associates, Pandita Gosvami, gazing at the Lord, fainted here in the bliss
of prema” (“Bhakti-ratnakara” 8.311, 316–317). <!-- F131 -->

## Gadadhara's Disciples and Branch
The branch of Gadadhara Pandita is one of the great branches of the Chaitanya tree. Among the first to take shelter of
him were not disciples in the ordinary sense but the Lord's associates themselves: Narahari Sarakara had the good
fortune to enter Gadadhara's company while Gadadhara served constantly at the Lord's side, and he became his follower;
therefore Narahari's disciple Lochana Dasa writes that Gadadhara and Narahari are Mahaprabhu's dearest associates. A stanza on
Narahari cited by one biography says just this: formerly Madhumati in Vrindavana, now in Shrikhanda, “following
Gadadhara” — Narahari Sarakara. King Prataparudra too is called a branch of Gadadhara, and so is Vakreshvara — though
Vakreshvara is also called a branch of Chaitanya Himself. <!-- F74 F119 F84 F85 -->

Bhugarbha was Gadadhara's disciple. When Lokanatha was taking leave of the Lord, weeping at His feet, Gadadhara was
there with Bhugarbha; Bhugarbha, moved to the depths of his heart, wished to go with Lokanatha and asked permission of
his guru. The Lord glanced approvingly at Gadadhara, and he gladly let his disciple go. So the two young men were the
first of the Lord's devotees to go to Vrindavana. Together with Bhugarbha, Bhagavata Dasa, his fellow disciple and close
friend, was a branch of Gadadhara:

“Vaninatha Brahmachari was a great soul, and Vallabha-Chaitanya Dasa was full of love for Krishna” (CC, Adi 12.82)

From Bhugarbha the branch continued through his disciples — Chaitanya Dasa, the pujari of Govinda, Mukundananda
Chakravarti and “Premi” Krishnadasa. <!-- F75 F76 F77 -->

Another disciple was Ananta Acharya:

“Ananta Acharya was the disciple of Pandita Gosani; his very body was made of love for Krishna, and he was generous and noble in every way. Who could recount his endless virtues? And this Haridasa Pandita is his dear disciple” (CC, Adi
8.59–60)

Ananta Acharya and Haridasa Pandita lived in Vrindavana and constantly heard the “Chaitanya-bhagavata” of Vrindavana
Dasa; when Virachandra came to Vrindavana, they received him[^17]. According to the “Anubhashya,” Ananta Acharya is the
sakhi Sudevi (GGD 165), and in the line of the Ganga-mata-matha he is called “Vinoda-manjari”; Haridasa Pandita, also
known as Raghu-gopala, is “Rasa-manjari.” Through Haridasa Pandita, Ganga-mata Gosvamini entered the line of the
Gaura-shakti Gadadhara: Haridasa initiated her, a princess of Putiya; his disciple too was Lakshmipriya, Ganga-mata's
maternal aunt. <!-- F78 F80 F79 -->

Among the principal branches of Gadadhara the “Charitamrita” names Raghunatha Bhagavatacharya (Adi 12.79). He himself,
in his “Sri Krishna-prema-tarangini,” calls himself a disciple of Gadadhara Pandita Gosvami: in the opening stanza he
honors his guru Gadadhara — “the best of brahmanas, ever devoted to bhakti, dear to the devotees, in form a
servant”[^18]. In Vraja he is known as “Shveta-manjari.” <!-- F116 -->

Achyutananda, the son of Advaita Acharya, is “the beloved disciple of Srimat Pandita Gosvami,” as the
“Gaura-ganoddesha-dipika” says (GGD 87); according to the “Shakha-nirnayamrita” of Yadunandana Dasa (in the biographies, Yadunatha Dasa) too he is a disciple
and branch of Gadadhara, while Srila Bhaktisiddhanta in the “Anubhashya” calls him the first of the disciples of
Advaita, Whose son he is. Achyutananda came to be regarded as the chief among Gadadhara's disciples, was present at the
festival in Kheturi, and after the Lord's disappearance and the departure of his guru returned to Shantipura and served
the Lord's mission in obedience to his father. The “Shakha-nirnayamrita” itself (“The Nectar of Determining the
Branches”) is a book about Gadadhara's disciples, written by his disciple Yadunandana Dasa; he also indicated that
Chandrashekhara Acharya appeared in Shrihatta. This book glorifies Achyutananda with the stanza “mahā-rasāmṛtānandam…”
and mentions (35) Uddhava Dasa — whom the “Charitamrita” too calls a branch of Gadadhara (Adi 12.83). <!-- F81 F117 F82
Q13 F83 -->

One of the biographies calls Paramananda Gosvami Gadadhara's beloved disciple. With him is linked the appearance of
Gopinatha at Vamshivata, on the bank of the Yamuna: according to the “Bhakti-ratnakara” (2.473–479), the Lord revealed
Himself through His devotees — Paramananda Bhattacharya and Madhu Pandita, to whose love Krishna is subject — and
Gopinatha mercifully revealed Himself to Madhu Pandita and gave him the right of service.[^19] And Hridayananda, whom Gadadhara loved as his own life, he gave to Gauridasa Pandita.
One morning Gauridasa came to him; Gadadhara seated his guest with honor and asked why he had come. “I need
Hridayananda,” Gauridasa replied. And Gadadhara, painful as it was for him, placed Hridayananda's hand in the hand of
Gauridasa; Gauridasa gave him diksha and later entrusted him with the service of Gaura-Nityananda. The “Bhakti-ratnakara” adds: Gadadhara “had raised him from childhood and in a short time had him study the scriptures. He
was overwhelmed with parental love, yet did not act from the feeling ‘he is mine’: having given [him] to Pandita Thakura,
he rejoiced” (7.402–403); when Gauridasa came, Gadadhara said: “All this is yours; whatever you wish, take it” (7.397).

In Vrindavana, by the time Virachandra came, many of his disciples were serving there. The “Bhakti-ratnakara” lists
them: “the foremost disciple of Gadadhara Pandita Gosani, the adhikari of Govinda, Sri Ananta Acharya” with his disciple
Haridasa Pandita; “the adhikari of the service of Sri Madana-gopala, Gadadhara's disciple Krishnadasa Brahmachari”;
“another disciple of Gadadhara Pandita Gosani, Gosani Gopala Dasa”; “the adhikari of Sri Gopinatha, Sri Madhu Pandita,
known as a disciple of Gadadhara Pandita,” and his fellow disciple Bhavananda (13.312–320). <!-- F137 -->

In Puri too he had disciples. One of them, Madhava Pattanayaka — an Oriya poet of the karana community who moved from
Khandapada to Puri — wrote the “Vaishnava-lilamrita” in Oriya (a late source: known only from late copies and not cited in
the Gaudiya tradition), and says of himself there: “My guru is Gadadhara Pandita; he showed [me] boundless mercy. He gave
[me] initiation in the Gopala mantra and delivered [me], a foolish soul. I am the disciple of Gadadhara; my faith is in the
devotees” (1.58–60). Of his guru he writes: “Guru Gadadhara performs worship; his abode is Tota-Gopinatha. He cannot go
anywhere; he listens to [the narrations of] Krishna's lila” (8.91–92) — that is, he is faithful to his vow of
kshetra-sannyasa. Madhava also passes on traditions of his own circle, unknown to the “Charitamrita” and the
“Chaitanya-bhagavata”: that in a dispute over which words should begin the mahamantra, Gadadhara stood for “Hare Krishna,”
and the Lord ruled that in Nilachala “Hare Rama” should be current and in Gauda “Hare Krishna” (6.12–18); that after the
departure of Svarupa Damodara the Lord sent Gadadhara to Gauda to inform the devotees (9.25–27)[^20].

Of Gadadhara's transcendental lineage Narottama Dasa Thakura sings: “…advaita-ācārya bala, gadādhara mora kula, narahari

Those born into this lineage acknowledge Gaurasundara as their only Lord, Nityananda as their wealth, Advaita as their
strength, the feet of Radha-Govinda as their life, and Narahari as their companion in pastimes. When Narottama came to
Puri, Tota-Gopinatha was served by Mamu Gosvami Thakura (the “Charitamrita” names him in Gadadhara's branch, Adi 12.81).

## After the Lord's Disappearance
Mahaprabhu's manifest pastimes lasted forty-eight years. According to a tradition cited in the “Bhakti-ratnakara,” at
their close the Lord entered the temple of Gadadhara Pandita's Gopinatha and did not come out again — He disappeared
into this Deity[^21]. <!-- F90 -->

Mamu Gosvami told Narottama how it happened: in Tota, by Gadadhara's worn seat, where “Gaurahari, the life of the
Gosvami, used to come alone,” the Lord “softly told the Pandita something — I do not know what,” and tears streamed from
the eyes of both; then He entered the temple of Gopinatha, “vanished from sight, and did not come out again.” Gadadhara
fainted, and the Lord, consoling him, commanded: “Srinivasa, a brahmana's son, has suffered great pain. He is coming along
the road; hearing of My disappearance, he resolved to give up his life. I consoled him; he will come here, and his life
will be saved by your mercy. <…> Remain for some days, as I wish” (“Bhakti-ratnakara” 8.350–364). <!-- F133 -->

After this Gadadhara remained on earth only eleven months. Narahari Chakravarti in the “Bhakti-ratnakara” describes him
as Srinivasa saw him: his beautiful body, which surpassed gold, had grown pale, “like the moon by day”; before him lay the
“Bhagavata,” soaked with tears; his voice broke on the verses —

“Crying ‘Sri Gaurasundara!’, he closes his eyes and heaves long sighs like flames. <…> Only to show mercy to Srinivasa
does life linger in his motionless body.” (“Bhakti-ratnakara” 3.142, 144) <!-- F91 Q9 -->

Srinivasa came to Puri and the next morning went to Gadadhara's house. With tears he fell at his feet; Gadadhara
lovingly raised and embraced him, and Srinivasa wept in separation from Gauranga; both sank into an ocean of divine
feeling. Gadadhara and the other associates of the Lord whom Srinivasa saw lived only by the name, form, qualities and
pastimes of Gaura. Srinivasa spent a day in his house, then went to the other associates, walked round the Kshetra, saw
Gadadhara's Gopinatha, and for some time heard the “Bhagavatam” from the lips of the Pandita Gosvami — only this helped
him hold on to life. At his order Srinivasa returned to Gauda; it was then too that Gadadhara sent him to Mayapur, to
Vishnupriya. <!-- F92 -->

The “Bhakti-ratnakara” also preserves Gadadhara's words. Seeing Srinivasa, he exclaimed, “Come, my son!”, lifted and
embraced him — “as a father and mother cherish a son” (3.150; 8.369). He could no longer truly teach him the “Bhagavata”:
“You had a wish to study the Bhagavata, and I had a wish to teach you. What can one say, and to whom? A contrary obstacle
has arisen” (3.268–269) — and yet from time to time he explained its meaning to him. He showed Srinivasa his book:
“through the tears of Sri Chaitanya Prabhu and Gadadhara letters had been effaced here and there, and reading was
impossible” (3.276); “Go to Vrindavana: there all these desires will be fulfilled” (3.273) — but for now, to Gauda. In
parting he “lamented greatly” for Gadadhara Dasa, “dear to the Pandita as his life,” and sent word to Narahari and the
people of Khanda (3.280–282). <!-- F135 -->

Soon the news came that Gadadhara had departed. Srinivasa, having returned to Gauda and come to Shrikhanda, decided to
go again to Nilachala and stay with Gadadhara, even against his order; but on the way he heard from pilgrims returning
from Nilachala of his departure — and fell unconscious. In a dream Sri Gaura-Gadadhara appeared to him, and at Their
command he resolved to go to Vrindavana by way of Navadvipa[^22]. The “Bhakti-ratnakara” tells the same: the news reached him on the way from Khanda, “before reaching
Jajpur”; “Ha, ha, Prabhu Gadadhara! <…> that is why you sent me, ignorant one, away!” Srinivasa cried; first Gadadhara
alone appeared to him in a dream and consoled him, and then together with Gaura commanded: “Go by way of Navadvipa quickly
to Vrindavana” (1.871; 3.305–322). <!-- F93 -->

Gadadhara himself, having sent Srinivasa away, “sat here, distraught” in Tota: “Day by day that tender body grew thin;
with his tears he watered the earth night and day. Again and again long sighs, like tongues of flame; suddenly he
disappeared here,” says Mamu Gosvami, in whom, by his own words, “only life remained [in me] — by his will”; Gadadhara had told
Mamu about Narottama earlier (“Bhakti-ratnakara” 8.371–375). <!-- F134 -->

Gadadhara departed in Puri[^23] on the new moon of the month of Jyeshtha in the year 1456 of the Shaka era (1534)[^24].
On the same day, almost four centuries later, on 23 June 1914, Bhaktivinoda Thakura departed, “non-different from the
Gaura-shakti Gadadhara.” In 1922 Srila Bhaktisiddhanta instituted at the Purushottama Matha (Bhakti-kuti in Puri) an
annual festival in memory of Bhaktivinoda and Gadadhara Pandita. <!-- F94 F95 I8 F96 -->

But even after his departure, says the “Bhakti-ratnakara,” he remained with his own. When Srinivasa in Shrikhanda read
the “Bhagavata” to the mahantas, some said: “The power of the mercy of Gadadhara Pandita Gosani must be fully manifest
here” (9.548). At the festival in Kheturi, when Gaura appeared in the sankirtana with His associates, departed and still
living, “Gadadhara Pandita dances, losing all composure, having brought Srinivasa near Gaurachandra” (10.583). And to
Jahnava in a deserted Navadvipa Gaurasundara appeared in a dream in kirtana: “On His left is Gadadhara, on His right
Nityananda” (11.759). <!-- F139 -->

## Places of Remembrance
Narottama Dasa Thakura, coming to Puri, on the advice of Gopinatha Acharya visited the samadhi of Haridasa Thakura, the
Gambhira and the temple of Gadadhara Pandita's Gopinatha. He came to Gadadhara's house and cried aloud: “O life of
Gauranga! O Gadadhara!” — and then received darshan of Tota-Gopinatha. He also approached that place of the Deity into
which, according to tradition, the Lord had entered. <!-- F97 -->

Since ancient times the Deity of Gadadhara has been worshipped daily to the left of Sri Gaurasundara. Witness to this
are the ancient Deities of Sri Gaura-Gadadhara served by Dvija Vaninatha; they stand to this day in the village of
Champahatta in Ritudvipa, one of the islands of Navadvipa. Whether this is the Vaninatha who was Gadadhara's younger
brother is not said. <!-- F98 -->

So too Lochana Dasa more than once describes Gadadhara to the left of Gaurasundara, Narahari to the right, and
Raghunandana at His feet, drinking the nectar of His face. In the “Chaitanya-mangala” the Lord “with His left hand
holds Gadadhara's hand” (Madhya-khanda, pt. 3, v. 25), and in Srivasa's house “Gadadhara and Narahari stand on either
side” (pt. 6, v. 41). Lochana sings the glory of Sri Gaura-Gadadhara, and in the drama at Chandrashekhara's house
Srivasa, in the mood of Narada, glorifies Gadadhara: “You are the first of prakritis, you are Radha, Krishna's shakti”
(pt. 11, v. 101). In Saptagrama, beside the six-armed form of Mahaprabhu installed by Uddharana Datta, Nityananda stands
on the right and Gadadhara on the left; and in the royal palace in Puri, among many images, the Deities of
Gaura-Nityananda and Gaura-Gadadhara are worshipped too. <!-- F113 F114 F112 -->

The Gaudiya Maths too preserve the memory of Gaura-Gadadhara. Bhaktivinoda Thakura established the service of
Gaura-Gadadhara, seeing in Them Radha-Madhava; Srila Bhaktisiddhanta restored the service of Gaura-Gadadhara in
Champahatta and founded there the Gaura-Gadadhara Math, and in Baliati (Dhaka) the Gadai-Gauranga Math. At
Prabhupada's wish Bhakti Vilasa Tirtha Maharaja built a temple of Gadadhara beside the Advaita-bhavana in
Srivasa-angana, and Bhakti Pramoda Puri Maharaja in 1989 founded in Ishodyan the Sri Gopinath Gaudiya Math for the
service of Sri Sri Gaura-Gadadhara. <!-- F121 -->

Narahari Chakravarti, in the “Namamrita-samudra,” where his prayers call upon the Lord's associates, names him in the
very second prayer, and then entreats his parents as well:

“Gadadhara, Srinivasa, Svarupa, Narahari! Be merciful and give me to drink the nectar of Gaura's prema.”
(“Namamrita-samudra” 2)

“Madhava and Ratnavati, take pity on this wretch: may my mind rest at the feet of your son Gadadhara.”
(“Namamrita-samudra” 9)

## Radha in Gaura-lila
Who Gadadhara was in Krishna-lila is told by the “Gaura-ganoddesha-dipika” of Kavi Karnapura:

“Sri Radha, love personified, formerly the Queen of Vrindavana, is Sri Gadadhara, dear to Gaura, called the Pandita. As
Sri Svarupa determined, he is the Lakshmi of Vraja: ‘The Lakshmi who was once the beloved of Shyamasundara in Vrindavana
is now the Lakshmi of Gaura's love, Sri Gadadhara Pandita.’ Since Lalita follows Radha, being ‘Anuradhika,’ she too
entered into him, as is said in the ‘Gaura-chandrodaya.’” (GGD 147–150) <!-- F104 Q3 -->

She who in Krishna-lila was the daughter of Vrishabhanu, Srimati Radharani, became Gadadhara in Gaura-lila; this is
confirmed by the notes of Svarupa Damodara and the songs of Vasudeva Ghosha. That is why Pundarika, Vrishabhanu, is his
guru and the Lord's “father” in the mood of Radha; that is why the Lord said that in Vaikuntha Gadadhara is His consort,
and in Navadvipa he and Gaura are what Radha and Krishna are in Vrindavana. One biography calls Gadadhara the embodiment
of hladini, the Lord's pleasure energy. Kavi Karnapura in the “Gaura-ganoddesha” shows the distinction between the two
Gadadharas: Gadadhara Pandita is the form of Sri Radhika (GGD 153), the svarupa of the daughter of Vrishabhanu, while
Gadadhara Dasa is Chandrakanti, “a manifestation of Radha's glory” (GGD 154), the radiance of Her body; so too teaches
Srila Bhaktisiddhanta: Gadadhara Pandita is Radha Herself, Gadadhara Dasa Her bodily effulgence. Svarupa Damodara, according to tradition, recorded this in his “Kadacha,” which the “Bhakti-ratnakara” quotes: “The best
of brahmanas, the lord of ascetics named Sri Pandita — he indeed is Radha in the descent of Srila Gaura. Such too is the
essential view of Narahari Sarakara and of me, Damodara” (8.313). The same thought is expressed by the
“Sarvabhauma-shataka,” which tradition ascribes to Sarvabhauma (its authenticity is not proven): “That Krishna who once
enjoyed pastimes with Radha in the grove now, joined with Gadadhara, dwells on earth as Gaura” (6).
<!-- F105 F107 F103 F131 F151 -->

All this is a transcendental mystery, and it must be understood as Bhaktisiddhanta teaches. Gaura is Krishna Himself,
Who has taken the mood and radiance of Radha, and Gadadhara is Radha serving Him in the form of a devotee-brahmana. Their
intimacy is not the worldly attachment of two people but the eternal play of the Lord with His own internal energy; even
Gadadhara's feminine attire in the drama at Chandrashekhara's house is a revelation of this energy, not worldly
play-acting[^25]. <!-- F49 F108 F34 -->

## Two Gadadharas
In the year 1340 of the Bengali era (1933–1934) a pamphlet appeared, the “Dasa Gadadhara-tattva.” Its author, posing as an
admirer of Gadadhara Dasa, in a sectarian spirit set Gadadhara Pandita against him. Supposedly the Pandita, having
studied nyaya, at first did not wish to submit to Dasa Gadadhara, the expounder of the “Bhagavatam”; and, realizing that
he was being deprived of the taste of Gauranga's pastimes, in submission to Advaita Acharya — a manifestation of
Gopeshvara-Shiva — became a disciple of Pundarika Vidyanidhi, a “bilva tree” in Advaita's sub-branch, and only then took
shelter of the mood of Dasa Gadadhara; and therefore those who follow Pandita Gadadhara would do better to leave him and
follow the Dasa. Such a view is the height of pashanditva, godlessness. <!-- F101 -->

The two Gadadharas must not be separated and set against each other: both are associates of Gaura, His own people,
eternal associates both in Gaura-lila and in Vraja-lila. To honor one and reject the other is exactly what the
“Charitamrita” calls the logic of half a hen:

“You have faith in one but show the other no respect: your standard is the logic of half a hen” (CC, Adi 5.176)

Such logic the gurus have not told us to accept; and the distinction spoken of by Kavi Karnapura and Bhaktisiddhanta
(Radha Herself and Her radiance) is no ground for rivalry, but different services within one lila. So too did Yadunandana Chakravarti, a disciple of Dasa Gadadhara, regard them: according to the “Bhakti-ratnakara,” for
him “Dasa Gadadhara and Sri Pandita Gadadhara are not different” (9.468); quoting the “Gaura-ganoddesha-dipika” at the
same place, Narahari Chakravarti calls Gadadhara Pandita in the words of the “Charitamrita”: “A great branch is Gadadhara
Pandita Gosani; he is an embodiment of Lakshmi; there is none equal to him” (9.480; CC, Adi 10.15). <!-- F102 Q17 I7 F138 -->

## Significance
The life of Gadadhara Pandita — from his childhood games with Nimai to the eleven months of separation in
Yameshvara-tota — was wholly given to Gaura. He is His shakti, Radha serving Him; that is why the Lord is “the life of
Gadadhara,” and whoever wishes to serve Radha-Govinda in madhura-rasa takes shelter of him. His life teaches humility
before guru and Vaishnavas (Pundarika, the mantra in Puri), the simplicity and patience by which the Lord is “bought”
(Vallabha Bhatta), and a love ready to abandon both vow and service only to be with the Lord — and obedience when the
Lord orders him to return (Cuttack). And his “Bhagavata,” which resounded by Narendra lake, reminds us that the
“Bhagavata” must be heard from a bhagavata. <!-- I1 I2 I3 I4 I5 -->

His glory is also sung in the “Sri Gadadhara-pandita-gosvami-dashaka,” verses attributed to Rupa Gosvami; their copy
from a manuscript of the Madras Government Oriental Library was printed in the “Gaudiya.” <!-- F118 -->

Bhaktivinoda Thakura, who departed on the day of his departure, left an instruction in which Gaura and Gadadhara are
one:

“Remember the pastimes of Gaura-Gadadhara, become a servant of Gaura-Gadadhara, hear the wondrous narrations of
Gaura-Gadadhara, and worship Gaura — the moon of the groves of Godruma” (“smara gaura-gadādhara keli-kalām…”).
