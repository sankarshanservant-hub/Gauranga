# Srila Jagannatha Dasa Babaji Maharaja
*Шрила Джаганнатха Дас Бабаджи Махарадж · @jagannatha-dasa-babaji*

## Vaishnava-sarvabhauma
Srila Jagannatha Dasa Babaji Maharaja was a babaji saint of the nineteenth century whose name stands in our line of
teachers between Srila Baladeva Vidyabhushana and Srila Bhaktivinoda Thakura. The Vaishnavas called him
Vaishnava-sarvabhauma, “the sovereign of all Vaishnavas,” and Siddha Babaji Maharaja: in Vraja and in
Navadvipa-mandala alike, the two holiest lands, he was honoured as the foremost sadhu of his time, and the whole
Vaishnava world revered him as one perfect in devotion to Krishna. His full title is Om Vishnupada
Vaishnava-sarvabhauma Sri Srila Jagannatha Dasa Babaji Maharaja; he is also called Jagannatha Dasa Gosvami Maharaja,
and in the songs of the line, Jagannatha Prabhu. In Navadvipa he was spoken of everywhere as the “siddha-baba.” Srila
Bhaktivinoda called him Siddha Babaji Mahashaya and senapati, “the general of the devotees,” and Srila Bhaktisiddhanta
Sarasvati called him Siddha Jagannatha Dasa Babaji and “vaishnava-samrat,” the emperor of the Vaishnavas.
<!-- F1 F2 F3 -->

The pranama-mantra with which he is honoured names the chief gift he gave the world:

“gauravirbhava-bhumes tvam nirdesta saj-jana-priyah /
vaishnava-sarvabhaumah sri-jagannathaya te namah”

“You revealed the land where Gaura appeared, you are dear to the saints; obeisance to you, Sri Jagannatha, sovereign
of the Vaishnavas.”[^1] <!-- Q1 -->

## Homeland and Years
Little is known of his worldly life. He was born in East Bengal, in what is now Bangladesh, into a respectable family —
by one account in a village of Tangail, then part of Mymensingh district; by another, into a Varendra Kayastha family
in the village of Taras in Pabna district, in the north of Bengal. The names of his parents have not come down to
us.[^2] <!-- F6 F7 F8 -->

Nor is the year of his birth known. He appeared, in the words of one biography, “about two centuries ago,” according to
another about 1780, and he lived an extraordinarily long and holy life on earth: in 1892 Srila Bhaktivinoda wrote that
the Babaji was over a hundred, while the biographies speak of a hundred and thirty-five and of more than a hundred and
fifty years.[^3] Nor is his appearance day found in the Vaishnava calendar of the Sri Chaitanya Saraswat Math.
<!-- F9 -->

## The Line of Teachers
He received the dress of a paramahamsa, the babaji-vesha, from the renowned Siddha Madhusudana Dasa Babaji of
Surya-kunda. Madhusudana belonged to the line of Srila Baladeva Vidyabhushana: he had received renunciation from
Uddhava Dasa, a disciple of Baladeva.[^4] Three principal disciples of Madhusudana Dasa Babaji are named — Govinda Dasa,
Harigopala Dasa and Jagannatha Dasa. <!-- F10 F11 -->

In his song on the line of teachers Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati names him immediately after Baladeva:

“vishvanatha bhakta-satha, baladeva jagannatha, tara priya bhaktivinoda” —
“Vishvanatha, the friend of the devotees, Baladeva, Jagannatha, and his dear Bhaktivinoda.”

So too in the Sanskrit verses on the succession: “and likewise Vaishnava-sarvabhauma Sri Jagannatha Prabhu, who
revealed the dhama of Mayapur, dear to the saints.” Every devotee who follows Srila Bhaktisiddhanta remembers him daily
and prays for his mercy. <!-- F13 Q2 Q3 -->

Between Baladeva and Jagannatha Dasa Babaji lies almost a century, and in Srila Bhaktisiddhanta's line Uddhava Dasa and
Madhusudana Dasa are not named. When Srila B. R. Sridhar Maharaja was asked about this gap, he answered that the
spiritual line runs not by flesh but by truth: in this world the current of truth is always being muddied by material
admixtures, and then the Lord or His representative again establishes the truth in its former purity. “The flow of
spiritual truth is a living thing, not a dead thing. The vigilant eye of the Lord is always over our heads, and
whenever it is necessary to keep the current pure and flowing in full force, help descends from above” (“Sri Guru and
His Grace”). So Srila Sridhar Maharaja writes the succession: “(Baladeva Vidyabhushana) — Jagannatha Dasa Babaji —
Bhaktivinoda.” The line of teachers is above all a line of instruction (shiksha), and dark times do not break it.
<!-- F14 I2 -->

Srila Bhaktisiddhanta said that all these Vaishnavas, from Baladeva to Jagannatha Dasa, lived as paramahamsas and
preached pure bhakti by their example, and therefore in the Gaudiya sampradaya they are objects of the highest faith
and veneration. In other words, even the secluded bhajanandis were acharyas and preached — by their very lives.
<!-- F12 Q9 -->

## Bhajana in Vraja
Taking up the life of a renunciant who lives on alms, he gave himself wholly to the service of Sri Krishna and Sri
Gauranga, who in essence (tattva) are one. For long periods he lived at Radha-kunda by Govardhana, a few miles from
Vrindavana, serving Radha and Krishna in the way of the Gaudiya Vaishnavas, by the instructions of Sri Rupa. He knew
Navadvipa-mandala to be nondifferent from Vraja-mandala and at times showed the example of unbroken remembrance in which
the one land is not separate from the other. <!-- F17 F18 F19 -->

He himself was ceaselessly absorbed in bhajana — remembering Radha and Krishna and Sri Gauranga and singing Their
names — and he expected the same of others: he inspired everyone to bhajana, for there is no better use of a human
being's time, and the highest happiness lies in this alone. When he took the paramahamsa's dress, his strong and
strict bhajana in Vrindavana and Navadvipa became a model for all, and the bhajanandi Vaishnavas acknowledged him as
their leader; in Vrindavana he was renowned as one perfect in krishna-bhakti. <!-- F20 F21 -->

## The Reader of the “Bhagavata”
When he was living in Vrindavana among other strict (naishthika) paramahamsas, a learned brahmana came there from
Katwa — the town where Mahaprabhu took sannyasa — a Sanskrit scholar and reciter of the “Bhagavata.” He read
beautifully: his voice and his explanations enchanted his hearers, and among those who went to hear him were some
babajis. But he read for payment, for money and fame. Jagannatha Dasa and his companions never once came to
him.[^5] <!-- F22 F23 -->

The reader asked why, and the Babaji answered plainly. One who reads the “Bhagavata” for money or any other reward is
lower and more sinful than a harlot: the “Bhagavata” is nondifferent from the Lord Himself, and there is no sin more
terrible than earning by it. To seek money and fame by leaning on the Lord and on the scriptures equal to Him is an
unpardonable offence at His feet. The hearers of such readers sin together with them, as accomplices, and their
raptures are pretence, for show and in rivalry. A discourse on the “Bhagavata” by a man with other aims is no longer
true exposition: it does good to no one, not even to himself, and only poisons the minds of the hearers. To trade in
the kirtana of the “Bhagavata” is a harlot's trade; those who trade in it offend the Holy Name, and one must not hear
them, for this leads to offence against the Name (nama-aparadha) and to a fall. The Vaishnavas headed by Jagannatha
Dasa advised him to give up this trade. <!-- F22 F23 F24 -->

The reader proved wise: he did not take the reproaches for abuse, understood that the sadhus cared for his true good,
and that very day gave up selling his reading. By the Vaishnavas' mercy he was utterly changed: he gave up his pride of
learning and high birth, in time settled in Vrindavana and became a devotee so full of humility that he prostrated
himself before every living being of the holy dhama. <!-- F25 -->

Scripture and the Holy Name must not be made into merchandise: by trading in them both reader and hearers sin, and a
true discourse on the “Bhagavata” is possible only for one who seeks nothing else. <!-- I3 -->

## Srila Bhaktivinoda
In 1880, on pilgrimage to Vrindavana, Srila Bhaktivinoda Thakura, in the kunja of Rupa Dasa Babaji where
bhajanandi Vaishnavas lived, for the first time saw the “divine mahatma” — as he himself later wrote — and bowed at
his feet. The Babaji then gave him many instructions on hari-bhakti. From 1885 Bhaktivinoda often received spiritual guidance from him; their mutual love seemed to come “from time immemorial.” Thus the Babaji became Srila
Bhaktivinoda's instructing guru, his shiksha-guru, and Srila Bhaktisiddhanta's song calls Bhaktivinoda his “dear”
disciple. <!-- F26 F27 F16 -->

In Kulia — today the town of Navadvip — the Babaji had his bhajana-kutira on the bank of the Ganga. In May 1890 Bhaktivinoda, then serving in Krishnanagar, came to Kulia on foot for the darshana of this
bhajana-kutira and in the Babaji's service added a solid veranda to it, spending nearly a hundred and fifty rupees on
it.[^6] <!-- F28 Q7 -->

## Amlajora
In the month of Phalguna 1298 of the Bengali era (March 1892) the Babaji came to Burdwan district and stayed in the
village of Amlajora. Srila Bhaktivinoda, then on his way to Vrindavana, also stopped there — in the house of
Kshetramohana Babu — and again received his darshana. A “Prapanna-ashrama” was being prepared there, an abode for
those surrendered to the Lord, where hari-katha would be heard and kirtana sung. The night of Hari-vasara, ekadashi,
the Babaji spent with the devotees without sleep: kirtana and talks of Krishna went on until morning. He was very
pleased with Bhaktivinoda's zeal in preaching the Holy Name and inspired him to preach the name and abode of
Gaura.[^7] <!-- F29 F30 F32 F54 -->

In the morning, at eight o'clock, the whole village went out in nagara-sankirtana and moved towards the
Prapanna-ashrama, and the Babaji himself led it. Bhaktivinoda described that day in the “Sajjana-toshani”: the Babaji,
then over a hundred, danced “like a lion” and sang:

“nitai ki nama enechhe re, nama enechhe namer hate, shraddhara mile nama ditechhe re” —
“What a name Nitai has brought! He has brought it to the market of the Name and gives it for faith alone.”

He wept and fell in ecstasy; everyone's tears flowed, their hair stood on end, and, barely able to sing, all danced.
In the dance the old man leapt upward, drenched the earth with his tears and for a long time lost outward
consciousness. Then the Thakura spoke in his hari-katha of how such abodes are needed, and the Babaji declared the
ashrama open. <!-- F29 F31 Q5 Q6 -->

## Godruma
In the month of Magha 1299 of the Bengali era (February 1893) the Babaji with his companions came from his
bhajana-kutira in Kulia, crossing the Ganga, to Godruma — to Surabhi-kunja, Srila Bhaktivinoda's place of bhajana. His
coming was a wondrous event: the Babaji “took his seat” at Surabhi-kunja, and on Wednesday, 27 Magha, Bhaktivinoda held
a great festival of kirtana at which the Babaji was the guest of honour. Loud hari-sankirtana hardly ceased, and the
whole neighbourhood was reminded of the days of Sri Chaitanya Mahaprabhu.[^8] From then on the Thakura often came to
the Babaji, and the Babaji visited the Thakura both in Godruma and in Calcutta. <!-- F33 F34 F35 Q7 -->

## The Place of the Lord's Appearance
In those years Srila Bhaktivinoda was seeking where the house of Jagannatha Mishra, in which Mahaprabhu appeared, had
stood. He studied the “Bhakti-ratnakara,” old government maps — statistical and topographical — and the records of the
district court, presented his evidence to an assembly of prominent people of the district and well-known pandits of
Navadvipa, received their approval and revealed Sri Mayapur to the world as the Lord's birthplace. But the last word
was spoken by the Babaji. <!-- F36 -->

On Thursday, 20 Phalguna 1299 of the Bengali era (early March 1893),[^9] the Thakura came with kirtana in the morning
to Sri Jagannatha Dasa Babaji, who at once joined them and, despite his frailty, led everyone, inspiring them, to the
place of the Lord's appearance. At eleven o'clock the devotees set out in three boats from the western bank, from the
town of Navadvipa. The great devotee was carried in a palanquin; by another account, his servant Bihari carried him to
the place in a basket. A countless crowd gathered towards Mayapur, and at the Lord's birthplace Dvarika Babu waited
with flags and kirtana. <!-- F37 F39 F40 -->

Reaching the place, the old man stepped down and broke into a wild dance, singing “Jaya Shachinandana Gaura-Hari!” —
and all marvelled to see a man of such great age dance so. By divine vision he pointed out the place of the Lord's
appearance — the Yogapitha — and with the innate intuition of an eternal associate of the Lord struck several times
with his staff, with force, the very spot where Nimai, Sri Chaitanya Mahaprabhu, was born. The last doubts of all were
dispelled.[^10] Then he also pointed out the place where the Kazi broke the mridanga — Khola-bhangar-danga, that is,
Srivasa-angana, where Mahaprabhu's night kirtanas were held. Such dancing on the mound by the place of appearance,
Bhaktivinoda wrote, Navadvip had not seen for almost four hundred years. <!-- F38 F39 F40 Q6 -->

It was then also decided to begin the service of the Deities at the place of appearance and at Srivasa-angana, and the
Babaji proposed how it should be arranged: in one building, Jagannatha Mishra and Shachi Devi; in another, Mahaprabhu
with Lakshmi and Vishnupriya at His sides; and at Srivasa-angana, the Pancha-tattva. The Deities of Gaura and
Vishnupriya Srila Bhaktivinoda installed in Gaura's house on 21 March 1894 — following Sri Narottama and “by the
permission” of Vaishnava-sarvabhauma Jagannatha Dasa. <!-- F40 F47 -->

Thus the Babaji revealed many hidden holy places of Mayapur — the Yogapitha, Srivasa-angana and others. On that day a
great many Vaishnavas came to Mayapur. By the place of Mahaprabhu's appearance grew a huge kadamba tree; under it the
Babaji danced, and under the same tree, absorbed in the Name, sat Srila Gaurakishora Dasa Babaji. In that same year,
1893, after the consecration of the Yogapitha, Jagannatha Dasa Babaji told Srila Gaurakishora to live in Navadvipa.
<!-- F42 F41 F45 -->

Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati writes in the “Gaudiya-bhashya”: “Under the guidance of perfected devotees such as
Srila Jagannatha Dasa Babaji Maharaja it has been finally and unambiguously established that Sri Mayapur-Yogapitha,
situated near the samadhi (tomb) of Chand Kazi, is the exact site of the house of Jagannatha Mishra and Shachi Devi in
which Sri Chaitanya Mahaprabhu appeared” (on CB, Adi 1.7). So too N. Sanyal writes in his book “Sree Krishna
Chaitanya” (1933): the site of Jagannatha Mishra's house was finally determined by Sri Jagannatha Dasa Babaji, and the
neighbouring plot is still called Khola-bhangar-danga. Srila Bhaktivinoda's researches and the testimony of the siddha
do not contend with one another but complete one another: learned arguments confirmed what the saint saw by divine
vision. This is neither a guess nor a legend but the revelation of what was hidden: not for nothing do the verses in
the Babaji's praise say that Gaura-chandra Himself moved him to reveal the house of His birth, that he danced at the
Yogapitha of Mayapur, was wholly attached to Mayapur-dhama, rejoiced in Gaura's appearance and wept as he sang the
Name. <!-- F43 Q14 F44 Q4 -->

One of the biographies adds that, fulfilling the will of Gaurasundara, who once sang kirtana by night in Srivasa's
house, Bhaktivinoda and Jagannatha Dasa Babaji — “Gaura's own associates” — manifested the service of Sri
Pancha-tattva and opened at Srivasa-angana a centre of sankirtana for pure devotees. <!-- F46 -->

## Vishnupriya's Appearance Day and the Vaishnava Calendar
The Babaji also restored to the Vaishnavas the appearance day of Srimati Vishnupriya. It was he who determined that
she appeared on the shukla-panchami of the month of Magha — on Sri-panchami — told Srila Bhaktivinoda of it and had the
day entered in the calendar; through Bhaktivinoda the custom of honouring her on that day spread throughout the
Vaishnava world. So writes the collection “Sri Sarasvati-jayashri” (1948); and when in 1919 Srila Bhaktisiddhanta
revived the “Vishva-vaishnava-raja-sabha,” he did so precisely on the appearance day of Vishnupriya.
<!-- F48 Q8 Q11 -->

Srila Bhaktivinoda's son, the future Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati, founder of Sri Chaitanya Math and sixty-four
Gaudiya Maths, met the Babaji as a boy in his father's Calcutta house, “Bhakti Bhavana” in Ram Bagan, and the old saint
poured out boundless love on him. Learning that the twelve-year-old boy was already versed in jyotisha, the Babaji told
him to compile a Vaishnava calendar — a panjika — according to true siddhanta: to count the years from Sri Chaitanya's
appearance (Chaitanya-abda), to name the months by names of the Lord, and to determine the weekdays, lunar days
(tithis) and festivals by Mahaprabhu's teaching. The dates of appearance and departure of the great Vaishnavas —
Mahaprabhu's associates and followers — the Babaji collected and gave him himself. Sarasvati Thakura joyfully took up
the task and compiled the calendar, and the Babaji was very pleased. Thus began the “Navadvipa-panjika”: by the
Babaji's will Sri Chaitanya Math publishes it every year to this day, and Mahaprabhu's followers keep to it.
<!-- F49 F50 -->

## In Calcutta: the Photograph and Giridhari
In Calcutta the Babaji stayed in Srila Bhaktivinoda's house on Manikatala Street. The Thakura always invited him to
take prasada, but the detached old saint came only rarely, and when others invited him to their homes or to a feast,
he did not agree at all. <!-- F51 -->

He allowed no one to photograph him. But once, when the Babaji came to “Bhakti Bhavana,” Bhaktivinoda nonetheless
arranged for a photograph, and this picture now stands on the altars of the Gaudiya Maths: the Babaji sits on a wooden
seat, holding to his breast the Giridhari he worshipped. Without this photograph we would never have known what he
looked like. <!-- F52 -->

The Babaji loved Bhaktivinoda deeply and, by tradition, gave him this Giridhari; the Thakura installed the shila in his
Calcutta house, and He is served at “Bhakti Bhavana” to this day.[^11] <!-- F53 -->

## The Bhajana-kutira in Kulia
His last long years he spent in Kulia, in Koladvipa — today part of the town of Navadvip, where the temple of Srila
B. R. Sridhar Maharaja now also stands. He performed his bhajana mostly on the bank of the Ganga; for a time he even
lived there in a tent. His ancient bhajana-kutira draws the faithful all year round as a most holy place; his
samadhi-mandira stands there too. <!-- F56 -->

He was carried by his servant, a strong Vrajavasi, Bihari Dasa Babaji. At the end of his earthly life the Babaji could
no longer move by himself, and Bihari would set him in a large wicker basket and carry him on his shoulders from place
to place. The Babaji often warned this servant against the bad company of false babajis. His joined eyebrows covered
the old saint's eyes, so that to look he had to lift them with his hand, and his outward sight had grown weak; but he
had an innate inner sight.[^12] <!-- F65 F66 -->

Those who came to his kutira bowed to him and left offerings; these were gathered in a vessel, and the attendant
sometimes counted them. The Babaji himself would neither keep nor count money, and yet, if the attendant took a
little of it, the Babaji at once asked why there was less money. It is told how he once asked Bihari: “How many rupees
do I have?” Bihari set aside twelve for a service he had in mind and handed over the rest. The almost sightless old
saint said: “Bihari, why have you set aside twelve? Give them all!” — and Bihari, laughing at the joke, gave them. Seeing
that nothing could be hidden from the sadhu's inner sight, the attendant gave up this habit.[^13] <!-- F57 F58 -->

The Babaji spent the offerings wherever service required them. Once the money came to two hundred rupees, and he told
Bihari at once to buy rasagullas with all of it and feed them to the cows of Navadvipa-dhama.[^14] But on false babajis
he would not spend a penny, and he let not one of them into his ashrama. <!-- F59 F60 -->

## Guardian of Purity
He would not tolerate any breach of the rules established by the shastras and kept in the line of teachers. Once an impostor who passed himself off as his disciple obtained through his servant the Babaji's worn
blanket, wrapped it around his loins and set off for the kutira with a troupe of musicians, singing, in place of the
maha-mantra, an empty, unauthorised jingle. As soon as he heard it, the Babaji told those beside him to stop that crowd
and drive it away. So zealously did he guard the purity of the bhakti-dharma established by Mahaprabhu and cut short
every corruption of it. <!-- F61 -->

Srila Bhaktisiddhanta said that the teaching of Siddha Jagannatha Dasa Babaji ran in the current of Sri Rupa: he would
by no means accept “smart” jingles that mix divine sentiments with worldly ones; offensive chanting must not be equated
with the pure Name, and chanting the Name “with faith or without faith” is not the same as chanting having given up
the ten offences. The verses in the Babaji's praise call him a thunderbolt to the wicked and the hypocritical and gentle
to those who have faith. And of the hypocrites who merely bore the name of his disciples, Srila Bhaktisiddhanta said:
they transgressed his order and fell. <!-- F63 Q10 Q4 -->

This strictness was neither temperament nor dislike but care for the purity of Mahaprabhu's teaching: the same sadhu,
as we shall see, shared his meal with puppies and despised no one. <!-- I5 -->

Many came to him for the renunciant's dress (bhek), but he did not give it to everyone: he told the askers first to
serve, and under the weight of service many ran away. One Gaura Haridasa, whom he had refused, lay without food for
three days before his tent; only then did the Babaji tell Bihari to give him a kaupina. Of those who received the
vesha from him, Bhagavata Dasa Babaji is known — he who later gave the babaji-vesha to Srila Gaurakishora Dasa Babaji.
He also had a disciple by diksha, Rasavihari Gosvami of Puruniya in Burdwan district; he initiated the independent Raja
of Tripura, Ishana Chandra Manikya Bahadur, and Rasavihari Gosvami's Deity, Rasaviharijiu, is served in the royal palace
to this day. <!-- F62 F15 -->

Nor did he indulge the delusion that a sadhu-Vaishnava needs no bodily labour and may sit idle, pretending to chant
the Name on his beads. Himself a great bhajanandi, he did not allow disciples with worldly desires to harm the service
of Vishnu and the Vaishnavas on the pretext of serving the Name. Newcomers among the renunciants, not yet ready for the
Name alone, he engaged in work in the garden by his kutira: they grew vegetables for the service of Krishna, of His
Deity and of His true devotees. For one who has not engaged his senses in service will not gain the capacity for the
Name; the renunciant's dress gives no right to idleness. <!-- F64 I4 -->

## Stronger than Infirmity
In extreme old age he became bent and very small and could hardly control his body. But in sankirtana, in ecstasy, he
would raise his arms, his body would straighten to its full height — about six feet — so that his fingers touched the
ceiling, and at times he leapt five or six feet into the air; even sitting on his servant's shoulders he would spring
up three or four cubits in kirtana. Tall, with long arms reaching to his knees, his frame like a nyagrodha tree and
four of his own cubits in height, in kirtana he reminded people of Mahaprabhu Himself. So he showed that in the ecstasy
of devotion a Vaishnava is above bodily weakness: bhakti is not subject to the body. <!-- F67 I6 -->

He was always aflame with kirtana and the service of the Vaishnavas, and to his last days, for the sake of fallen
souls, he preached the message of Gaurasundara. <!-- F68 -->

## The Dogs of the Dhama
His prasada was served to him on a very large platter, and he ate it together with several puppies and dogs. Under the
influence of his love they forgot their enmity and did not fight over the food. Orthodox Hindu society looks down on
dogs and does not let them near the kitchen, but the saint despised no one: love for God had taught him to see all
beings equally. <!-- F69 -->

On the bank of the Ganga, by his tent, lived a dog with five puppies, and when the Babaji took prasada they ate from his
plate. Once Bihari hid one or two of the puppies to drive them away, and the Babaji said to him: “Bihari, if you drive
them away, take the plate too: today I shall not eat.” Bihari brought the puppies back — “Here are your puppies” — and
the Babaji answered: “These are dogs of the dhama!” The dwellers of the holy dhama were for him not mere animals but
residents of Navadvipa — so a devotee sees the dhama. <!-- F70 -->

## Departure
Srila Jagannatha Dasa Babaji withdrew from this world on Monday, 25 February 1895, on the shukla-pratipad of the month
of Phalguna — in the Vaishnava calendar of the Sri Chaitanya Saraswat Math this is Gaura-pratipad of Govinda-masa.[^15]
Srila Bhaktivinoda wrote in the “Sajjana-toshani”: at ten in the morning, in the bhajana-kutira in Koladvipa, in the
town of Navadvipa, the old general of the Vaishnavas departed for the eternal abode; Siddha Babaji Mahashaya has gone
to the spiritual world, and the world is left in darkness; with worldly eyes we shall no longer see his ecstatic
dancing in kirtana — may he bless us from the eternal abode. <!-- F71 F72 Q6 -->

His samadhi is in his bhajana-kutira in Koladvipa (Kulia). On the day of Gaura-dvadashi the pilgrims of the Navadvipa
parikrama come to the samadhi-mandira of Sri Jagannatha Dasa Babaji Maharaja. On his day of departure the Vaishnavas
also remember Sri Rasikananda and, since 1979, Srila Bhakti Dayita Madhava Maharaja, who departed on Tuesday, 27
February 1979, “on the day of the departure of Jagannatha Dasa Babaji and Rasikananda Deva Gosvami.” <!-- F73 F74 -->

## The Current of Bhaktivinoda
The verses in the Babaji's praise say that he placed all his power in the devotee Bhaktivinoda and through him sent the
stream of harinama through the world, and they pray: “I honour the auspicious line of Bhaktivinoda, which grew from
your body and your labours.” The Babaji showed himself a preacher too, a goshthyanandi: his desire to see the
sampradaya grow and pure bhakti preached blossomed and bore fruit in Bhaktivinoda. And Srila B. R. Sridhar Maharaja in
his “Srimad-Bhaktivinoda-dashakam” (the journal “Gaudiya,” July 1939) glorifies Bhaktivinoda as “dear to Sriman
Jagannatha Prabhu.” <!-- F54 Q4 F55 Q15 -->

After the departure of Srila Bhaktivinoda and Srila Gaurakishora, there appeared to Srila Bhaktisiddhanta in a vision
(svapna-samadhi), consoling and encouraging him, Mahaprabhu with the Pancha-tattva, the six Gosvamis and
“Vaishnava-sarvabhauma Jagannatha Dasa Babaji” with Bhaktivinoda, Gaurakishora and others. In 1918 Srila
Bhaktisiddhanta accepted tridanda-sannyasa — among other reasons, to show the transcendental nature of the
paramahamsa's dress worn by his gurus: Jagannatha Dasa Babaji, Bhaktivinoda and Gaurakishora. He accepted sannyasa
neither in place of that dress nor in reproach of it. And in 1922 in Vrindavana, answering Ramakrishna Dasa Babaji, he
relied on the teaching of the Gosvamis carried on by Vishvanatha, Baladeva, Jagannatha Dasa and Gaurakishora, and
cited the teaching of Siddha Jagannatha Dasa Babaji on the Holy Name. <!-- F75 F76 F77 -->

In the “Gaudiya-bhashya,” having spoken of the proud and envious people who put obstacles in the way of preaching pure
devotion, Srila Bhaktisiddhanta continues: “Vaishnava-samrat Srila Jagannatha Dasa and his follower Sri Srimad
Bhaktivinoda Thakura Mahashaya made great efforts to help the Gaudiya Vaishnavas overcome these obstacles. They
cleansed the teaching of Gaudiya Vaishnavism of the influence of illusion that had crept into it. These exalted
Vaishnavas and their faithful followers may rightly be called vishvambhara-priya-vaishnava-samaja — ‘the society of
Vaishnavas infinitely dear to Vishvambhara’” (on CB, Madhya 1.3). Mahaprabhu's teaching is not subject to maya; what they cleansed away was the overlay with which conditioned souls had obscured it in people's eyes. <!-- F4 Q13 -->

And in the “Anubhashya” on the “Chaitanya-charitamrita” Srila Bhaktisiddhanta names him among those who truly live in
Vrindavana: “In a later age Thakura Narottama in the village of Kheturi and Srinivasa Acharya in the village of
Yajigrama, and in the age after them Sri Jagannatha Dasa Babaji Maharaja in Gauda-desha, Sri Bhagavan Dasa in Kalna,
Srimad Gaurakishora Dasa Babaji Maharaja in Navadvipa-dhama, Sri Srimad Bhaktivinoda Thakura in Calcutta and other
devotees wholly devoted to the holy name certainly never lived in any dhama other than Sri Vrindavana” (on CC, Madhya
16.280–281). This is not about a place on the map: only the pure devotee whose eyes are tinged with the salve of love
truly lives in Vrindavana, and for the Babaji Navadvipa was nondifferent from Vraja. <!-- F5 Q12 F19 -->

Srila Bhakti Pramoda Puri Gosvami also wrote of the Babaji's merits — his article was included in the collection “Sri
Sarasvati-jayashri” (Bag Bazar Gaudiya Math, 1948). <!-- F78 Q8 -->

The life of Srila Jagannatha Dasa Babaji shows that an eternal associate of the Lord may come even centuries after Him,
to restore what was forgotten. The place of Mahaprabhu's appearance and Srivasa-angana, the appearance day of Srimati
Vishnupriya, the Vaishnava calendar — all this, through Srila Bhaktivinoda and Srila Bhaktisiddhanta, became the common
treasure of the Vaishnavas. The measure of an acharya is not the number of his disciples but faithfulness to the truth
and the power he passes on to those who will carry it further. <!-- I1 I2 -->
