# Sanatana Gosvami
*Sanatana Gosvami · @sanatana-gosvami*

## The Eldest of the Gosvamis
The six Gosvamis of Vrindavana established in the world the innermost desire of Sri Krishna Chaitanya, the avatara
who purifies the Age of Kali, and the eldest of them was Sanatana. His contribution to the literary heritage of the
Gaudiya sampradaya can be compared only with what Rupa and Raghunatha Dasa left behind. <!-- F1 F2 -->

Krishnadasa Kaviraja Gosvami says in a few words how each of three great associates served the Lord:

“Through Haridasa Thakura He revealed the glory of Krishna's holy names. And through Sanatana Gosvami He disclosed the
essence of sacred devotion to God. Through Sri Rupa Gosvami He revealed the taste of transcendental loving relations
with Krishna in Vrindavana.” (CC, Antya 5.86–87)

And of himself he says:

“By the mercy of Sanatana Gosvami I learned the truths of bhakti. And by the mercy of Sri Rupa Gosvami I tasted the
nectar of loving relations with Sri Krishna.” (CC, Adi 5.203)

That is why Sanatana is called the bhakti-siddhantacharya — the teacher of the conclusions (siddhanta) of bhakti, that
is, of sambandha-jnana: the knowledge of how God, the living being and the world are related to one another.
<!-- F3 F4 -->

Through each of His close devotees Mahaprabhu gave the world one great teaching. The “Bhakti-ratnakara” says:

“Through Ramananda He revealed the crushing of Kandarpa's pride, through Damodara He revealed impartiality, through
Haridasa He showed forbearance, through Sanatana and Rupa He revealed humility.” (“Bhakti-ratnakara,” 1.630–631)

Humility (dainya) became the mark of both brothers, and it will be spoken of more than once in this article.
<!-- F5 Q50 -->

In his song “Ke vaishnava?” (“Who Is a Vaishnava?”), which he gave to his disciples, Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati
rebukes the crafty mind that makes a show of preaching “nirjana-bhajana,” solitary bhajana, like the feats of a yogi
seeking mystic powers, and bids it reflect carefully on everything the Lord so painstakingly taught Sanatana. Srila
Bhaktisiddhanta therefore teaches us to give special attention to the instructions Mahaprabhu gave the world through
Sanatana: they are the remedy for a showy “solitary bhajana.” <!-- F6 F7 Q68 I16 -->

## Years of His Life
Unlike Rupa's dates, Sanatana's are the same in every known testimony. They are given by the note “Determining the
Dates of the Six Gosvamis,” which Bhaktivinoda Thakura printed in the “Sajjana-toshani” (2nd year, 2nd issue); by the
records collected by the late Vishvambharananda Deva Gosvami of the shripata of Gopivallabhapura; and by the
information of Pandita Banamali Lala Gosvami of the Radha-ramana-ghera in Vrindavana. According to them, Sanatana
appeared in 1410 of the Shaka era (1545 Samvat, 1488 AD).[^1] He lived at home for twenty-seven years — until he met
Mahaprabhu and left his family and his post as minister — spent forty-three years in Vraja, and remained on earth
seventy years in all. <!-- F8 F9 F10 -->

Sanatana departed in 1480 Shaka (1615 Samvat, 1558 AD), on the day of ashadha-purnima, displaying this pastime in the
midst of preaching Gaurasundara's cherished desire. According to our Vaishnava calendar, the day of Srila Sanatana
Gosvami's departure is the purnima of the month of Vamana: on that same day Guru- (Vyasa-) purnima is celebrated and
chaturmasya begins.[^2] <!-- F11 -->

## Lineage
Sanatana's genealogy over seven generations was preserved by his nephew Jiva Gosvami at the end of the
“Laghu-vaishnava-toshani”; it is set out in detail in the article on Rupa Gosvami. The founder of the line was Sarvajna
“Jagad-guru,” king of Karnata; after him came Aniruddha-deva and his son Rupeshvara, whose brother Harihara seized his
kingdom, so that Rupeshvara went east with his wife to King Shikhareshvara. Rupeshvara's son Padmanabha settled on the
Ganga at Navahatta (Naihati); his youngest son Mukunda-deva was Sanatana's grandfather, and Mukunda's son Kumara-deva
was his father.[^3] The pious Kumara-deva, because of strife in Naihati, moved to Bakla-Chandradvipa and, for his
journeys, also made himself a residence at Fatehabad (then in the district of Jessore), between Naihati and
Bakla.[^4] Beyond this genealogy no reliable information about the family has been found; one can only hope that
historians will find more. <!-- F12 F13 F14 -->

The “Gaudiya-vaishnava-abhidhana” adds that Sanatana's grandfather Mukunda entered the court in the capital of Gauda as
early as the reign of Barbak Shah (1460–1470). The times were troubled. Barbak Shah kept a great many Abyssinian slaves
and eunuchs (“habshi”) at court and in the harem; after him reigned his son Yusuf, then Yusuf's son Fateh Shah. Under
Fateh Shah the Abyssinians rose in revolt, killed him and ruled themselves for five or six years; Hussain Shah was vizier
to the last of them and afterwards became ruler of Bengal himself. <!-- F15 F16 -->

Of Kumara-deva's sons three became famous: the eldest, Sanatana; the second, Rupa; and the youngest, Vallabha. There
were other sons as well, but nothing is known of them.[^5] Vallabha's only son was Jiva, the servant and nephew of Rupa
and Sanatana — the very one who described the genealogy in the “Laghu-toshani.” <!-- F17 F18 -->

## Youth and the “Bhagavatam”
After Kumara-deva passed away, the brothers lived and studied in the house of their maternal uncle, in a small village
near the capital of Gauda.[^6] The names “Rupa” and “Sanatana” were given to them later by Sri Chaitanya Himself; their
former names are unknown, though some think that Sanatana was called Amara and Rupa Santosha.[^7] <!-- F19 F20 -->

From childhood, one of the biographies relates, all three brothers lived in bhava-bhakti. Around their home in
Ramakeli grew groves of tamala, keli-kadamba and tulasi; there were ponds there which the brothers named Radha-kunda and
Shyama-kunda, and they served Sri Madana-mohana. Hearing of Gaurasundara's pastimes in Navadvipa, they longed to see
Him, but an inner voice told them to wait. And when the news came that Sri Chaitanya had accepted sannyasa and gone to
Puri, the brothers fainted, and they were consoled only by a voice from the sky: the merciful Gauranga would soon come to
them. In this way the authors show that Sanatana and his brothers were not worldly men who came to faith in time, but
eternal associates of the Lord. <!-- F21 F22 F23 I1 -->

Sanatana studied the shastras under Vidyavachaspati, the brother of Sarvabhauma Bhattacharya and the foremost scholar of
the land. Narahari Chakravarti writes:

“Sri Sanatana's guru is Vidya-vachaspati; from time to time he stayed in the village of Ramakeli. Under him [Sanatana]
studied all the shastras; what his devotion to his guru was cannot be told.” (“Bhakti-ratnakara,” 1.598–599)

Even before he outwardly displayed his coming to Mahaprabhu's feet, Sanatana was absorbed in the study of all the
shastras, but he was especially devoted to the “Srimad Bhagavatam.” <!-- F24 F25 -->

This love, Jiva Gosvami tells at the end of the “Laghu-toshani,” was kindled in him by a wondrous dream. The young
Sanatana dreamt of a brahmana who gave him the “Srimad Bhagavatam”; his hair stood on end, and the dream broke off.
Waking and not finding the book, Sanatana was agitated. But the next morning, when he had bathed and sat down to
worship, that very brahmana came with the “Bhagavata” and, according to one biography, said: “Take it and study it
always, and all perfection will be yours.” Having given him the book, he went away, and Sanatana sank into an ocean of
the nectar of love for Krishna. Narahari Chakravarti tells the same story in verse, following it with Jiva's own words:

“The life of Sri Sanatana is truly wondrous; he loves the Srimad Bhagavatam beyond measure. In his early youth, in a
dream, a venerable brahmana with joy in his heart gives him the Srimad Bhagavatam. On waking, Sanatana was agitated —
[and in waking life] that same brahmana lovingly handed him the Srimad Bhagavatam. Having received the Sri Bhagavata,
with great joy at heart my master sank into the ocean of the nectar of prema. How he relished the meaning of the Srimad
Bhagavatam he revealed in the Sri Vaishnava-toshani.” (“Bhakti-ratnakara,” 1.531–535)

“Those who in early youth received the Sri Bhagavata in a dream, and in the morning, awake, from the very brahmana they
had seen in the dream, and sank into the great ocean of the nectar of prema for Bhagavan — this writing is truly theirs,
of those called Sri Sanatana.” (“Laghu-toshani,” conclusion; “Bhakti-ratnakara,” 1.537–538)

From that day on, one biography says, Sanatana set other books aside and studied only the “Bhagavata,” as the essence of
all scripture.[^8] <!-- F26 F27 F28 Q1 Q2 -->

Sanatana honoured his teachers at the beginning of the “Vaishnava-toshani,” his commentary on the Tenth Canto:

“I bow to my gurus — Bhattacharya Sarvabhauma and Vidya-vachaspati — and to Vidya-bhushana, the ornament of the land of
Gauda. I bow to Sri Paramananda Bhattacharya, the lover of rasa, and also to Ramabhadra and Vani-vilasa, my
instructors.” (“Vaishnava-toshani,” opening verses; “Bhakti-ratnakara,” 1.601–602)[^9] <!-- F29 Q3 -->

And in the “Krishna-lila-stava” he addresses the “Bhagavata” itself:

“You are my only friend, my companion, my guru, my great treasure, my deliverer, my good fortune, my bliss: obeisance to
you!” (“Krishna-lila-stava”) <!-- F30 Q8 -->

## In the Service of Hussain Shah
The brothers' learning, intelligence, knowledge and noble character drew the attention of the ruler of Gauda, Hussain
Shah. Sanatana, “a Brihaspati in intellect,” and Rupa, peerless in appearance and virtues, occupied the two highest posts
at his court and received the titles Sakara Mallik and Dabir Khas; they became the Shah's “two hands” in the business of
government. Sanatana was prime minister.[^10] Rupa held another ministerial post — perhaps that of minister of finance,
collector of revenue and treasurer — while Vallabha was chief superintendent of the mint; the Shah rewarded the brothers
generously. Hindus at that time often had to serve a Muslim sovereign, and among such officials there were many
devotees: Keshava Vasu Khan, Gopinatha Vasu, Purandara Khan, the physician Mukunda Kaviraja, Keshava Chhatri.
<!-- F31 F33 F34 F37 -->

The “Gaudiya-vaishnava-abhidhana” tells of Sanatana's rise differently: Mukunda died under Fateh Shah, and his grandson
was taken into the court in his place; Sanatana survived the years of the Abyssinians, and under Hussain Shah rose ever
higher by his talents and at last became prime minister. <!-- F32 -->

Sanatana himself did not want this post. Hussain Shah heard of the brothers' virtues from pious people and wished to give
them office, and out of fear of the yavana king they could not refuse. Narahari Chakravarti writes:

“Sanatana and Rupa were great ministers in every respect: the king heard of them from the lips of respectable people. The
king of Gauda, a yavana ruling many lands, summoned Sanatana and Rupa and gave them the burden of government. For fear of
the mlechchhas they accepted the post, and through their efforts his kingdom grew.” (“Bhakti-ratnakara,” 1.581–583)
<!-- F35 F36 Q4 -->

Sanatana was born into a high brahmana family, yet because he served a Muslim government he considered himself fallen and
always behaved with the utmost humility. In the “Bhakti-ratnakara” the brothers say of themselves:

“‘Our father, on seeing a yavana, performed atonement, and we are in constant association with yavanas. Fawning, we go to
the houses of yavanas’ — and therefore they consider themselves equal to mlechchhas. Their inner disposition is not at
all like that, but in this they consider themselves the lowest of the low. When they sink into the ocean of humility,
they consider themselves lower than mlechchhas and the rest. With the low-born there is always low dealing: that is why
they speak of themselves as low-born. Though kings among brahmanas, with great grief in their hearts they never think of
themselves as brahmanas.” (“Bhakti-ratnakara,” 1.609–614)[^11] <!-- F38 Q5 Q6 -->

This humility is not an admission of any real fall but an expression of great Vaishnava humility; for it Sanatana is
glorified in the “Chaitanya-charitamrita” and was praised by Mahaprabhu Himself. By “lowness” he meant aversion to
Krishna, and scripture confirms that a twice-born man devoid of devotion to Hari is lower than a dog-eater. It is the
nature of devotees to hide themselves behind humility and so mislead those who have turned away from God, that they may
serve Him unhindered: thus Mahaprabhu Himself called Himself “a Mayavadi sannyasi without prema-bhakti,” and Narottama
Thakura called himself “a chandala.” Sanatana is an eternal associate of the Lord and cannot be fallen. His humility is
beyond description, and one can only pray for shelter at his feet. <!-- I2 I3 I4 -->

Even in service Sanatana went on studying the “Bhagavatam” and other books and longed constantly for shelter at the feet
of Sri Krishna Chaitanya. From time to time he wrote humble letters to Mahaprabhu, and once the Lord answered him with a
verse:

“A married woman attracted to another man only appears to be diligent in her household duties, while in her heart she
anticipates the sweetness of a new meeting with her beloved.” (CC, Madhya 1.211)

The meaning of the verse, one biography explains, is this: just as such a woman, however busy with household work,
always tastes in her heart the joy of meeting as she remembers her beloved, so a devotee on the path of raga, while
outwardly carrying on worldly affairs as if absorbed in them, is inwardly united without cease with the remembrance of
Krishna, whom he worships. <!-- F39 F40 F41 Q7 I6 -->

## Ramakeli
The brothers' seat was Ramakeli, a shripata on the Ganga, right by the border, in the district of Maldah: some eight
miles south of English Bazar and five or six miles from Maldah station. Sultan Barbak made Ramakeli the capital of
Bengal in 1486; under the British it was a district headquarters; and many devotees lived there, among them Sri
Nrisimha, the son of Advaita Acharya. The place is also called Gupta-Vrindavana, the “hidden Vrindavana.” <!-- F42 -->

The brothers lived mostly in Ramakeli: there they studied all the shastras and duly honoured the brahmana Vaishnavas.
Narahari Chakravarti describes their life thus:

“They settled in Gauda, in the village of Ramakeli; the very limit of wealth, a truly wondrous splendour! To the
assembly of Sanatana and Rupa, who were like Indra, knowers of the shastras come from various lands. Singers, musicians,
dancers and poets from every country are constantly in their service. They spend a great deal of money without cease,
and no one is ever left without honour. Both always occupy themselves with all the shastras and refute and establish
[doctrines] without effort. An interpretation of the nyaya-sutras that anyone made of his own is established only when
Sanatana and Rupa have heard it. <…> The fame of their virtues spread everywhere; brahmanas came from Karnata and other
lands. To the brahmanas from their native country Sanatana and Rupa gave all of them places to live near the Ganga. The
village where the community of Bhattas lives is called Bhattavati; all there are knowers of the shastras, incomparable
in all doctrines. With those brahmanas in the village of Ramakeli they joyfully carry out all their worldly affairs. How
Rupa and Sanatana honour the communities of Vaishnavas cannot be described. How many brahmanas come from Navadvipa, and
how they honour them all, I cannot tell.” (“Bhakti-ratnakara,” 1.585–597)[^12] <!-- F43 F44 F45 F46 F47 Q9 -->

The brothers' memorial places in Ramakeli are the tamala and kadamba under which they met the Lord, with an altar of His
footprints; the temple of Madana-mohana, where Nitai-Gauranga and Advaita are also worshipped; the ponds Radha-,
Shyama-, Surabhi-, Lalita- and Vishakha-kunda; Rupa-sagara and Sanatana-sagara; and the brothers' large houses in
Ramakeli and in Fatehabad. The Madana-mohana of Ramakeli is still there; He is known as the Deity installed before
Sanatana left home, and Sanatana himself served Him. The memorial places of Ramakeli are described more fully in the
article on Rupa Gosvami. In June 1868 Bhaktivinoda Thakura visited the house of Rupa and Sanatana in Ramakeli.
<!-- F48 F49 -->

## Meeting the Lord
To draw “His servants” Sanatana and Rupa to His feet, Mahaprabhu chose a special way: on the pretext of a journey to
Vrindavana He set out from Nilachala for Gauda and came to Ramakeli. Thus He met Rupa and Sanatana for the first time,
and with them their younger brother Anupama — in 1513, according to one biography; by His will renunciation awoke in the
brothers.[^13] Hearing of the Lord's wondrous prema and seeing its power, even the badshah recognised Him as Ishvara and
ordered that no one disturb the “Hindu prophet.” Keshava Chhatri played down the Lord's importance before the king and
secretly advised the Lord to leave, while Rupa, the Dabir Khas, glorified the Lord to the sultan in private. All this is
told in detail in the article on Rupa Gosvami. <!-- F50 F51 F52 -->

Learning that Mahaprabhu was in Ramakeli, the brothers exchanged the robes of royal officials for inconspicuous clothes
and came to Him secretly — first to Nityananda and Haridasa Thakura, “the crown jewels of the saviours of the fallen,”
who informed the Lord.[^14] With bunches of grass between their teeth, cloth around their necks and joined palms,
sobbing with humility, the brothers fell prostrate at His feet. The Lord told them to rise, and with folded hands they
offered the prayer recorded by Krishnadasa Kaviraja (CC, Madhya 1.188–206): we are lower than Jagai and Madhai, we are
servants of meat-eaters, and no one but You can save us. <!-- F53 F55 Q10 -->

The Lord answered them (CC, Madhya 1.207–215):

“‘Listen, Dabir Khas. You and your brother are both My servants from long ago. From now on your names are Rupa and
Sanatana. Give up your humility now, for it breaks My heart. <…> You are both My servants birth after birth. Very soon
Sri Krishna will certainly set you free.’” (CC, Madhya 1.207–208, 215)

From their letters He knew their hearts and had more than once sent them, for their instruction, the verse about the
woman drawn to her beloved; He had no business in Gauda — He had come to see the two of them, though no one knows why He
came to Ramakeli. Placing His hands on their heads, the Lord sent them home without fear. Thus Gaurasundara took from His
eternal associates the names Dabir Khas and Sakara Mallik given by the badshah and named them Sri Rupa and Sri Sanatana.
Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati explains (“Anubhashya” on CC, Madhya 1.208): by giving the brothers spiritual names the
Lord showed them mercy; name-giving is a samskara of the lowest step, yet whoever neglects it remains in worldly pride,
and the prakrita-sahajiyas, who wear neither the marks of a Vaishnava nor a name received from a guru, are not Gaudiya
Vaishnavas. <!-- F56 F57 F58 Q11 -->

Present at the meeting were Nityananda, Haridasa, Shrivasa, Gadadhara Pandita, Mukunda Datta, Jagadananda, Murari Gupta,
Vakreshvara and many others; at the Lord's bidding the brothers touched their feet, and He told everyone to bless them.
At that same time Vallabha, who became Anupama, also received mercy. <!-- F54 -->

Vrindavana Dasa Thakura in the “Chaitanya-bhagavata” tells of the giving of the names differently — as an event in
Nilachala, where the brothers came later: the Lord told them to beg for prema-bhakti at Advaita's feet and to go to
Mathura to preach bhakti, and changed their names as a “third samskara.” He calls Sanatana “Sanatana-avadhuta”:

“sakara-mallika nama ghuchaiya tana / sanatana avadhuta thuilena nama” — “taking from him the name Sakara Mallik,
[the Lord] named him Sanatana-avadhuta.” (CB, Antya 9.273)[^15] <!-- F59 Q13 -->

Seeing the huge crowds around the Lord, Sanatana, wise in worldly affairs, lovingly dissuaded Him from going to
Vrindavana in this way. The Shah trusts You, he said, but he is a Muslim and cannot be relied upon; Your work here is
done, and one does not go to Vrindavana as a pilgrim leading hundreds of thousands of people (CC, Madhya 1.222–224).
Mahaprabhu acknowledged the advice as sound. Later He Himself recalled it:

“But as he was leaving, Sanatana said one mysterious phrase to Me: ‘One who is setting out for Vrindavana should not
lead a crowd of many thousands behind him.’ At first I paid no attention to these words, and in the morning I set out
for Kanai-natashala. But late at night I pondered Sanatana's words, which he had cast in the form of a profound aphorism.
He spoke rightly: when countless people follow Me, ordinary people may easily take Me for an impostor.” (CC, Madhya
16.265–269)[^16] <!-- F60 F61 Q12 -->

Murari Gupta in his “Karcha” tells of the meeting at Ramakeli in his own way:

“Then Krishna, surrounded by His devotees, came to Ramakeli. Hearing of this, Sanatana came there to see the Lord's feet.
Seeing the Lord, he rejoiced at heart; together with his younger brother he fell to the ground and, holding a straw
between his teeth, said to Keshava: ‘There is no sinner like me, no such offender anywhere. I am ashamed even to excuse
myself — what can I say, O Purushottama?’ Janardana placed His foot on his head and said, ‘You are a dweller of
Vrindavana — truly, truly, there is no doubt. I wish to go at leisure to Mathura together with you. But to reveal the
lost holy places, and Vrindavana as well — that is your task: all of it will come about by My mercy — for My mercy is
devotion itself embodied, and it bestows loving devotion.’ Hearing this, the most wise Sri Sanatana, with his younger
brother, said, ‘Vrindavana is the pleasure-garden of Krishnachandra, lovely and auspicious: there Krishna plays for ever
with Sri Radha <…>. With the sword of Your mercy You have cut my strong chain — my royal ministership and the rest — and
brought me into Your presence. So now infuse Your power into me, O Krishna, and do as You please.’” (Murari Gupta's
“Karcha,” 3.18.1–10) <!-- F62 F63 F64 -->

Having bestowed His mercy on the brothers, Mahaprabhu went as far as Kanai-natashala, where He saw pictures of Krishna's
pastimes, and, pondering Sanatana's word, gave up the intention of going to Vrindavana with a crowd: He decided to go
there alone or with one companion, and now turned back — through Shantipura to Puri.[^17] <!-- F65 -->

## The Prisoner of Hussain Shah
What followed one of the biographies calls “a pastime of renunciation for the instruction of the people”: Sanatana's
giving up his post and his journey in rags are shown as an example, not as a need of the Lord's eternal associate. From
the time the brothers saw Mahaprabhu in Ramakeli, they sought a way to leave worldly life. Generously rewarding some
brahmanas, they commissioned them to perform two purashcharanas of the Krishna mantra, so as to attain shelter at Sri
Chaitanya's feet. Rupa and Sanatana are Krishna's eternal associates in Vrindavana, but in Gauranga's pastimes they
appeared in the role of sadhakas. <!-- F66 F67 I7 -->

Rupa brought a great deal of wealth by boat to his house in Fatehabad and divided it: half he gave to brahmanas and
Vaishnavas, a quarter to his relatives, and a quarter he left with trustworthy brahmanas against misfortune; for
Sanatana he left ten thousand coins in Gauda, and Sanatana deposited them with a merchant-banker (mahajana) and drew on
them as needed.[^18] Hearing that the Lord had returned from Ramakeli to Puri and would go from there to Vrindavana by
the forest road, Rupa sent two messengers to Nilachala to learn the exact day of His departure. <!-- F68 F69 F70 -->

Sanatana stayed in Ramakeli, looking for an opportunity to leave the royal service for ever. He reasoned: the king's love
for me is my fetter, as is the love of any worldly man; only by incurring his displeasure shall I get the king to let
me go. Hussain Shah did indeed love him and treated him like a younger brother. So Sanatana declared himself ill, gave
up his duties and stopped appearing at court. Seeing his detachment, several kayastha officials under him began to show
great zeal in the hope of getting his place; according to tradition, Sanatana's place was in fact taken by his
subordinate, the well-known Purandara Khan. <!-- F71 F72 F73 F74 F75 -->

Sanatana himself gathered twenty or thirty pandita-bhattacharyas at home and discussed the shastras and the
“Bhagavatam” in their assembly. One day the badshah came to him unexpectedly with a single companion and found him in
good health among the scholars; everyone rose, greeted him respectfully and seated him.[^19] <!-- F76 F77 -->

“I sent a physician to you,” said the badshah, “and he reported that you are well. All my affairs depend on you, yet you
sit at home, and everything is in disorder. Tell me plainly what is on your mind.” “I can no longer serve you,” Sanatana
answered. “Let someone else manage the business.” The angry badshah exclaimed: “I am your elder brother! I do not
govern the kingdom: I make war with my army and vassals, march through foreign lands, hunt in the district of Gauda and
kill many animals, and you are my whole support. If I, the elder, am occupied only with plunder and violence to living
beings, and you, the younger, have abandoned your duties — how is the kingdom to stand?” Sanatana replied with a hidden
meaning: “You are the ruler of Gauda, independent and empowered to punish and reward: give each his due according to his
fault.” In other words, the king, who himself behaved like a robber, would reap the fruit of it, and let him dismiss
Sanatana, his negligent servant. <!-- F78 F79 F80 -->

The badshah went back to his palace and, so that Sanatana would not escape, had him put under guard. When setting out on
a campaign against Orissa, he called on Sanatana to go with him, but Sanatana answered: “You are making war on Vishnu,
and I cannot assist you in that.” And the badshah went off to Orissa, leaving him in chains.[^20] <!-- F81 F82 -->

When Mahaprabhu set out from Nilachala for Vrindavana, Rupa's messengers returned with the news, and Rupa, leaving at once
for Vraja with Anupama, wrote to Sanatana: “Anupama and I are going to Vrindavana to Mahaprabhu's feet; break out of
prison by any means and come to Vrindavana; free yourself quickly with the ten thousand coins kept with the mahajana in
Ramakeli.” Bhaktivinoda Thakura (“Amrita-pravaha-bhashya,” 2.20.3) reports that the letter was a Sanskrit verse: “Where is
Mathura, the city of the lord of the Yadavas? Where is Koshala of the lord of the Raghus? Remembering this, make your mind
steady: this world is not eternal.” Sanatana understood its hidden meaning — the Lord had gone to Mathura — and,
rejoicing, began to think of escape.[^21] And in Prayaga the Lord, hearing from Rupa that Sanatana was in prison,
foretold that he would soon be freed and come to Him. <!-- F83 F84 F85 Q14 -->

Having received the letter, Sanatana began to win over with kind words the jailer, whom he himself had once appointed to
that post: “You are a living pir, fortunate and well versed in the Koran; if you free me, an innocent prisoner, Khoda will
free you from the world. I once helped you a great deal — repay me; I will give you five thousand coins — you will have
both dharma and wealth.” The jailer wavered: “I am not unwilling to release you, but I fear the badshah.” “Do not be
afraid,” said Sanatana. “The badshah has gone on campaign to the south. When he returns, tell him: Sakara Mallik went to
the Ganga to relieve himself, saw the water close by and threw himself into it; I searched for a long time, but in his
leg-irons he went under and was carried away — I found no trace. As for me, I shall not stay in this country: I shall
become a dervish and go to Mecca.”[^22] <!-- F86 F87 F88 -->

Krishnadasa Kaviraja gives these words thus:

“You need not be afraid, for I will not remain here. I will become a dervish, an ascetic, and go to Mecca.’”
(CC, Madhya 20.13)

For Sanatana the dervish's guise and the words about Mecca were only a means of concealing himself and getting out of
captivity, not a teaching; therefore the traditions relied on by people with ulterior desires (the name “Amara,” an
initiation he supposedly gave to the Oriya poet Achyuta Dasa, the appeal of various sects and of the aulas and bauls to
his dervish dress) should be accepted with great caution. <!-- Q16 I5 -->

Seeing that the jailer still hesitated, Sanatana laid before him a heap of seven thousand coins from the money Rupa had
left. The man could not resist: he cut the fetters and at night ferried him across the Ganga.[^23] <!-- F89 -->

Flattery, a reminder of past favours, temptation, advice to lie, a bribe — the means Sanatana resorted to are usually
considered unworthy. But he used them in the service of the Lord, and the purity of the means is determined by the
purity of the goal. Hanuman burned Lanka for Rama's sake, and everyone received good from it; a soldier who kills enemies
for his country is rewarded, not executed; all the more justified is an act done for the Lord of countless universes.
The “Padma Purana” says this too: “A sin committed for My sake becomes dharma; and dharma without regard for Me becomes
sin — such is My power.” And Krishna says in the “Gita” (18.17): one who is free from false ego and whose intelligence
is unstained, even if he kills, does not kill and is not bound. On the other hand, outward piety for the sake of harming
others is tamas, and chanting the Name for worldly ends is a sin. <!-- I8 Q19 Q20 -->
