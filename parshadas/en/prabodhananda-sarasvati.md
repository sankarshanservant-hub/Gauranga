# Prabodhananda Sarasvati
*Прабодхананда Сарасвати · @prabodhananda-sarasvati*

## “The Sarasvati Who Sings of Gaura”
Shripada Prabodhananda Sarasvati was a tridandi sannyasi of the Bhatta family of Shrirangam in South India, the paternal
uncle and teacher of Gopala Bhatta Gosvami, one of the six Gosvamis of Vrindavana, and the poet of the “Sri
Chaitanya-chandramrita.” Kavi Karnapura in the “Gaura-ganoddesha-dipika” calls him the renunciant (yati) Prabodhananda,
“the Sarasvati who sings of Gaura” (GGD 163). The colophon of the “Chaitanya-chandramrita” honours him as “Shripada
Shrimat Prabodhananda Sarasvati, crown of the tridandi gosvamis, foremost of wandering teachers (parivraj-acharya),
eminent associate of Gaura”; so too he is named on the title page of the edition published by Srila Bhaktisiddhanta
Sarasvati. Devakinandana Dasa in his “Vaishnava-vandana” praises him as “Prabodhananda Gosani” (2.61), and Dhruvadasa in
the “Bhakta-namavali” calls him simply “Prabodha” (29). Narahari Chakravarti, in the very opening invocations of the
“Bhakti-ratnakara,” exclaims: “Glory, glory to Sri Prabodhananda Sarasvati!” (1.30). <!-- F1 F18 -->

The title “Sarasvati” — the name of the goddess of learning and eloquence — was given him, it is said, by people for
his virtues: “Great are Prabodhananda's virtues; his fame as Sarasvati has spread everywhere,” they said of him,
according to the “Bhakti-ratnakara” (1.149)[^1]. Earlier, it is said, he was called Gopala-guru: so one of the
biographies, and Srila Bhaktisiddhanta's preface to the “Chaitanya-chandramrita,” list the three brothers — Tirumalla,
Venkata and Gopala-guru, later Sri Prabodhananda[^2]. <!-- F1 F2 -->

## Family and School
There were three brothers — Trimalla (Tirumalai), Venkata and Prabodhananda — and of them Narahari Chakravarti says:

“Trimalla, Venkata and Sri Prabodhananda — the treasure of the lives of these three brothers is Gaurachandra. Formerly
they were worshippers of Lakshmi-Narayana; by the Lord's mercy they became intoxicated with the rasa of Radha and
Krishna.” (“Bhakti-ratnakara,” 1.83–84)

Prabodhananda, by most accounts, was the youngest: Srila Bhaktisiddhanta in the “Gaudiya-bhashya” calls him the younger
brother of Venkata Bhatta, who lived on the bank of the Kaveri, and one of the biographies, citing the late
“Anuraga-valli,” makes him the youngest of the three, after Trimalla the eldest and Venkata the middle one[^3]. Venkata's son was Gopala, the future Gopala Bhatta
Gosvami, and Prabodhananda was thus his paternal uncle. “Sri Gopala, the nephew of Prabodhananda,” the
“Bhakti-ratnakara” calls him (1.155), and so too does the “Sadhana-dipika,” which Narahari quotes (4.329).
<!-- F3 F4 Q3 -->

The brothers had moved to Shrirangam — Ranga-kshetra — from the region of Mysore, but they were apparently not Tamils
by birth: Srila Bhaktisiddhanta in his preface to the “Chaitanya-chandramrita” calls them natives of Andhra, or of the
Northern Region[^4]. In his commentary on the “Charitamrita” (Madhya 9.82) he remarks that the names Venkata and Tirumalai are not in
use in Tamil Nadu, so the family had probably settled in Shrirangam shortly before the Lord's coming; and in the
“Anubhashya” on Madhya 1.109 he explains that Tirumalaya and Venkata were Vadagalais — “Vaishnavas of the northern regions, from
Andhra” — whereas the native people of Shrirangam are Tengalais. The “Prema-vilasa” says that the three brothers, great
panditas, lived in Tailanga, the land of the Telugus (20.5), and the “Anuraga-valli” names their homeland Telingana
(1.46). <!-- F5 -->

All of them were Shri Vaishnavas, followers of Ramanuja, and worshipped Lakshmi-Narayana. Srila Bhaktivinoda Thakura, in
his commentary on the “Charitamrita” (Madhya 9.82), writes that Venkata, Trimalla and Prabodhananda had formerly been
acharyas of the Shri Sampradaya, and Srila Bhaktisiddhanta specifies that Venkata's brother was the tridandi sannyasi
Prabodhananda, an acharya of that school, of the Vadagalai branch. In the “Anubhashya” (Adi 7.149) he calls him “a
tridandi of Ramanuja's line, a jiyar svami, who had come from the land of Mysore and lived in Ranga-kshetra.” When Shri
Vaishnavas leave home, they accept not the single staff (eka-danda) of the sannyasis of Shankara's school but the
triple staff — the tridanda — and the title “Ramanujarya-svami.” Thus Prabodhananda was by birth and by firm faith a
Shri Vaishnava and a tridandi sannyasi of the Shri Sampradaya. <!-- F6 F7 Q7 -->

## The Lord in Shrirangam
When Mahaprabhu, travelling through South India and giving out the name of Krishna, came to Shrirangam, where Sri
Ranganatha resides, He spent the four months of the rains — the chaturmasya — in the brothers' house[^5]. This was in
Shaka 1433 (about 1511), in the month of Ashadha[^6]. The “Chaitanya-charitamrita” names the master of the house:

“A Shri Vaishnava named Venkata Bhatta invited Sri Chaitanya to his home with the deepest reverence.” (CC, Madhya 9.82)

And in the brief summary of the southern pastimes it says:

“During the rainy season the Lord spent four months as the guest of the brahmana Trimalla Bhatta.” (CC, Madhya 1.108)

Prabodhananda himself the “Charitamrita” does not name — neither here nor anywhere else; why, will be told below. His
life beside the Lord is described by later books — the “Bhakti-ratnakara,” the “Prema-vilasa,” the “Anuraga-valli.” By
the Lord's mercy the whole family, who had worshipped Lakshmi-Narayana, turned to the service of Radha and Krishna.
<!-- F8 F9 Q7 F44 F47 -->

The “Bhakti-ratnakara” tells how the brothers, knowing that the Lord would stay with them only four months, could find
no peace:

“Trimalla, Venkata and Sri Prabodhananda, all three, reflect: ‘How shall we live without the Lord? Who will joke with
us? Who will take us with Him to bathe in the Kaveri? Who will sing sankirtana before Ranganatha? Who will give us
fallen ones that rare wealth of bhakti?’” (“Bhakti-ratnakara,” 1.128–130)

And when the time was up and the Lord took His leave, the three brothers wept aloud, and He embraced them and consoled
them at length (1.133–135). <!-- F10 Q4 -->

According to the “Prema-vilasa,” on leaving the Lord entrusted the boy Gopala to Prabodhananda. First He told the
father to make his son learned and not to marry him, and then —

“Smiling, Prabhu looked at Prabodhananda: ‘Your disciple will be a treasury of virtues in all the shastras.’”
(“Prema-vilasa,” 18.75)

And having revealed to Gopala that, after parting from his parents, he would go to Vrindavana, He turned again to
Prabodhananda:

“‘Be sure to send him to Vrindavana: I have need of him — this I tell you.’” (“Prema-vilasa,” 18.79)

“That Prabodhananda was dear to Prabhu as life; in His mercy Prabhu made him the best of bhagavatas,” concludes
Nityananda Dasa (18.81)[^7]. <!-- F11 F12 -->

Srila Bhaktisiddhanta, in his preface to the “Chaitanya-chandramrita,” says that the house where the Lord of Goloka
Himself was a guest for four months holds an inexhaustible treasure of prema, and we little ones may hope for at least
its crumbs. <!-- I3 -->

## Gopala Bhatta's Teacher
Through Prabodhananda's care Gopala became a great pandita and as a boy was already defeating the mayavadis in debate.
The “Bhakti-ratnakara” records what people said, marvelling at his learning:

“Some say: ‘Sri Prabodhananda took care of him and taught him from an early age. By his uncle's mercy he came to know
all the shastras; there is no one here as learned as Gopala.’” (“Bhakti-ratnakara,” 1.147–148)

His uncle instructed him in detail in the bhakti-shastras, and, in Srila Bhaktisiddhanta's words, it was Prabodhananda's
pure teaching that made the son of Venkata a great acharya of the Gaudiya school. <!-- F13 -->

Prabodhananda was also his guru. Gopala Bhatta himself, in the second verse of the “Hari-bhakti-vilasa,” calls himself
his disciple, and Narahari Chakravarti quotes the verse:

“Gopala Bhatta, the disciple of Prabodhananda, who is dear to the Lord, gathers [the accounts of] the delights of bhakti,
pleasing Raghunatha Dasa, Rupa and Sanatana.” (“Hari-bhakti-vilasa,” 1.2; “Bhakti-ratnakara,” 1.151)

Srila Bhaktisiddhanta writes in the “Anubhashya” (Adi 10.105) that Gopala received initiation from his paternal uncle,
the tridandi Shrimat Prabodhananda Sarasvati; one of the biographies understands it so as well. Another calls Gopala his
shiksha-disciple — one who received instruction from him. The “Anuraga-valli” calls Prabodhananda Gopala's “former
guru”: learning, upanayana, instruction in right conduct — all he received from his uncle, and to him, his teacher, he
felt a special attachment. Manohara Dasa refers to Sanatana Gosvami's “Dig-darshini” on the same verse of the
“Hari-bhakti-vilasa”: the word “bhagavat-priya” — “dear to the Lord” — points to Prabodhananda's greatness, and his
disciple “is like him”; and since “Bhagavan” here is Sri Krishna Chaitanya, Prabodhananda is a vessel of His mercy and
His associate (“Anuraga-valli,” 1.74–95). <!-- F14 Q2 F15 -->

How Gopala came to Vrindavana the sources tell differently. According to the “Bhakti-ratnakara” and the biographies, he
went there after his parents' death, by their parting counsel and at Mahaprabhu's bidding; Srila Bhaktisiddhanta writes
that the desire to live in Vraja gradually awakened in Gopala, and he followed the path of his uncle and guru[^8]. The
“Prema-vilasa,” however, says that it was Prabodhananda himself who carried out the Lord's command:

“At the end of his days Prabodhananda remembered [this] and, calling the Bhatta, told him Prabhu's words. <…> At the end
of his days Sarasvati said: ‘Go and take shelter of Rupa and Sanatana.’ <…> He wrote a fitting letter to the two
Gosanis: ‘I have sent Gopala Bhatta to you.’” (“Prema-vilasa,” 18.84, 87, 89) <!-- F16 -->

## The Glory of His Virtues
How his contemporaries saw Prabodhananda the “Bhakti-ratnakara” conveys in the words of the noble people who praised
both Gopala and his teacher:

“Others say: ‘Great are Prabodhananda's virtues; his fame as Sarasvati has spread everywhere. Purna-brahman Sri Krishna
Chaitanya Bhagavan — apart from love for Him he knows nothing, even in dreams. <…> Supreme renunciation, a charming
embodiment of affection, a great poet, incomparable in song, music and dance — such is Prabodhananda: hearing his verses
everyone is filled with joy; his greatness is boundless.’” (“Bhakti-ratnakara,” 1.149–153) <!-- F17 Q5 -->

Srila Bhaktisiddhanta writes that Prabodhananda's attachment to Sri Chaitanya was incomparable and that his place among
the Lord's servants is very high. <!-- F18 -->

## In Vraja
Some years after the Lord returned from the south to Puri, Prabodhananda, having deeply realised the confidential
service dear to Mahaprabhu's heart, left Shrirangam and settled in Kamyavana, in the region of Mathura, under the
shelter of Gaura's feet; so writes Srila Bhaktisiddhanta, and in the “Anubhashya” he likewise says that Prabodhananda
“lived in Ranga-kshetra and later in Kamyavana.” When exactly he came to Vraja is not known; Kamyavana is named as the
place of his bhajana, and he lived there long. <!-- F19 -->

Later, according to one of the biographies, he moved to Vrindavana, and his samadhi is marked at Kaliya-daha
(Kaliya-hrada) — the pool where Krishna subdued the serpent Kaliya[^9]. Neither the day, nor the year, nor the place of
his appearance and departure is given by the sources; nor are his days in our calendar. <!-- F20 -->

## His Works
His principal work is the “Sri Chaitanya-chandramrita”: one hundred and forty-three shlokas in glorification of
Mahaprabhu, gathered into twelve sections, declaring with ardour and great power that Sri Chaitanya is Krishna Himself.
The colophon names Prabodhananda as its author, and the Vaishnava “vandanas” remember him above all as its poet. “With
joy I praise the pure Prabodhananda Sarasvati, by whom the ‘Chandramrita’ was composed, whose disciple is Gopala
Bhatta,” says the Sanskrit “Vaishnava-vandana” attributed to Jiva Gosvami (73); “this book is the treasure and life of
the devotees,” echoes the “Vaishnava-vandana” of the “second” Vrindavana Dasa (52); and Prabodhananda's name also stands
in the “Vaishnavabhidhana” of Devakinandana Kaviraja (24). The rasikas especially love this book. Srila Bhaktisiddhanta,
who published it, writes that it spread widely through Bengal, that even the opponents of Mahaprabhu were purified at
heart by reading it, and that His followers, reading it, are immersed in an indescribable ocean of bliss. He quotes its verses in
the “Anubhashya” too (on Adi 7.27 and 13.123). Thus, speaking of how the purpose of the Lord's descent was fulfilled, he
recalls Prabodhananda's words:

“When Chaitanyachandra revealed the supreme path of bhakti-yoga, the worldly gave up talk of wives, sons and the like;
the learned gave up disputes over the shastras; the kings of yogis gave up the hardship of restraining the breath;
ascetics gave up their austerities; and sannyasis gave up the practice of knowledge. No other rasa remained.”
(“Chaitanya-chandramrita,” as quoted in the “Anubhashya” on CC, Adi 7.27) <!-- F22 -->

His second great book is the “Radha-rasa-sudhanidhi.” According to one of the biographies, in it Prabodhananda,
following the instruction he had received from Mahaprabhu Himself in Shrirangam, revealed the highest love and intimacy
with Radha and Krishna; Srila Bhaktisiddhanta calls it truly incomparable in the world and in the “Anubhashya”
(Adi 7.149) lists it among his books. His writings are so beautiful that, according to the same biography, there was an
attempt to pass this book off as the work of another, non-Gaudiya sampradaya: a manuscript with interpolations was
cleverly planted in the library of the Govinda temple in Jaipur and placed beside the genuine one. But true experts,
carefully comparing its language and its way of expressing the love-pastimes with his other books, pointed out the
interpolations exactly, and the internal evidence of his authorship, according to this biography, was fully
confirmed[^10]. <!-- F23 F24 -->

Other books are ascribed to him too. The “Vrindavana-mahimamrita,” or “Vrindavana-shataka,” is an ecstatic praise of
Vrindavana, which devotees read daily even now; the “Navadvipa-shataka,” similar to it, glorifies Navadvipa — Srila
Bhaktisiddhanta published it together with the “Chaitanya-chandramrita.” Theodor Aufrecht's catalogue counts the
“Sangita-madhava” among his works; Srila Bhaktisiddhanta found this book and printed it in the “Sajjana-toshani”
(vol. 18, nos. 5–12). Also named are the “Ashcharya-rasa-prabandha” and the “Viveka-shataka” — the latter Aufrecht too
assigns to him, and Srila Bhaktisiddhanta mentions that the late Ramadasa Sena of Baharampur had seen it. One of the biographies adds
commentaries on the “Shruti-stuti,” the “Gita-govinda” and the “Kama-bija — Kama-gayatri.” And in the library of the
late Madhusudana Gosvami Sarvabhauma at Radha-ramana-ghera in Vrindavana the authors of another biography saw a
manuscript of the commentary “Krishna-vallabha” on the “Gopala-tapani-shruti” with the colophon: “…revealed by Sri
Prabodhananda Sarasvati, the paramahamsa, the wandering acharya”[^11]. <!-- F25 F26 F27 -->

In all his books he kept to single-minded devotion, and his language is equally full of philosophical depth and the
sweetness of love. He is remembered beyond the Gaudiya sampradaya as well. Nabhadasa in his Hindi “Bhaktamal” names
Prabodhananda among the sannyasis who are “crowns,” blessed in the Kali-yuga (chhappay 182);
Priyadasa in his kavittas praises him as a great rasika, the root of bliss, a dear associate of Sri Chaitanya-chandra,
who described the ever-new pastimes of Radha and Krishna in the kunja groves, so that millions, hearing them, settled
in Vrindavana (612); and Dhruvadasa says that Prabodha's mind sank into the ocean of the rasa of the Divine Couple's
love and he sang the sweetness of the rasa of Vrindavana (“Bhakta-namavali,” 29). <!-- F28 F29 -->

It is said that some rupanugas find in his books an inclination towards the doctrine of svakiya and so study them
reluctantly, holding Sri Rupa's parakiya-vada higher[^12]. Srila Bhaktisiddhanta answers: every devotee of Mahaprabhu is
blessed; let us follow the impartial Narahari Chakravarti and relish Sri Prabodhananda's pure devotion to Gaura and the
sweetness of parakiya service to Sri Vrindavaneshvari, of which his books are full. <!-- F30 -->

## Why the Early Books Are Silent About Him
Like his nephew and disciple Gopala Bhatta, Prabodhananda is not named in the “Chaitanya-bhagavata,” or in the
“Chaitanya-charitamrita,” or in Murari Gupta's “Kadacha”; nearly all the literature of his own time is silent about him,
whereas the “Gaura-ganoddesha-dipika” names him and later books — the “Bhakti-ratnakara” and others — praise him
highly[^13]. The
reason is humility: the intimate, renounced associates of Gaura — Prabodhananda, Gopala Bhatta and others — themselves
forbade their names to be mentioned as associates of Mahaprabhu. Of Gopala Bhatta, Narahari Chakravarti writes:

“Sri Gopala Bhatta gladly gave his permission, but forbade him to describe in the book anything concerning himself. Why
he forbade it — who can understand? He always considered himself the lowest of the low.” (“Bhakti-ratnakara,”
1.222–223)

So too Prabodhananda, according to Srila Bhaktisiddhanta, asked Krishnadasa Kaviraja through Gopala Bhatta not to
describe his deeds in the “Charitamrita,” and the Kaviraja could not disobey — hence the present controversies. Had
Prabodhananda known that because of this silence he would be confused with a blasphemer of Vishnu, Srila Bhaktisiddhanta
adds, he would not have given such a prohibition. Yet from his own books it is clear that he is one of Mahaprabhu's
wholly devoted followers. <!-- F34 Q6 I1 -->

It does not follow from the silence of the early books that the Lord's associates left him out from dislike. The
authors of the lilas leave something for the later “Vyasas” — this is a feature of their service, not an omission.
Narahari says of the poets of former times:

“The great rasikas, the poets of former times, though able to describe it, did not describe it: thinking to
themselves, ‘It will be described later,’ they leave it for the delight of all [the poets to come].”
(“Bhakti-ratnakara,” 1.211–212) <!-- F34 I2 -->

Some, however, invent reasons of their own. It is said that Vrindavana dasa Thakura, an opponent of the gaura-nagari
doctrine, did not name Prabodhananda because of the words “gaura-nagara-vara” in the “Chaitanya-chandramrita”[^14]; the
“Vishnupriya-patrika” even wrote that Prabodhananda had sheltered Harivamsha, whom Gopala Bhatta had rejected, and that
this is why the biographers are silent about him. But none of Gaura's devotees accepted such fabrications.

## Prabodhananda and Prakashananda
In those same years there lived in Kashi a sannyasi with a similar name — Prakashananda Sarasvati, a learned mayavadi,
the head of a great many ekadandi sannyasis of Shankara's school. The “Chaitanya-charitamrita” (Adi 7, Madhya 25) tells
how Mahaprabhu defeated him in debate and made him His follower. After that conversation in Kashi —

“From that time the sannyasis' hearts were changed, and they always took the name ‘Krishna, Krishna.’” (CC, Adi 7.149)

It is to this very verse that Srila Bhaktisiddhanta adds in the “Anubhashya”:

“The leader at that time of the ekadandi sannyasis of Shankara's sampradaya living in Kashi was Sri Prakashananda
Sarasvati. Some mistakenly try to identify him with Sri Prabodhananda Sarasvati, who lived in Sri Ranga-kshetra and later
in Kamyavana. <…> Mahaprabhu saw him in Shaka 1433, during the chaturmasya, as a member of Ramanuja's sampradaya; so it is
unreasonable to think that in Shaka 1435 He saw him in Kashi as a member of Shankara's sampradaya.”
<!-- F35 Q15 F21 Q10 -->

The error, however, dies hard. The Bengali “Bhakta-mala” of Lal Dasa (Laladasa Babaji) asserts: “His name was
Prakashananda Sarasvati; the Lord gave him the name Prabodhananda”; the late “Advaita-prakasha” (ch. 17) holds the same
view[^15]. Following them, and relying on a coincidence of incidental circumstances, some modern authors repeat this
opinion[^16]. The compiler of a Bengali dictionary, Ashutosh Deb, writes that Prabodhananda's “real name” was
Prakashananda and that Chaitanya-deva gave him the new name, while Haridasa Dasa's “Gaudiya-vaishnava-abhidhana” cites
the opinion that “Prabodhananda” is Prakashananda's Vaishnava name and looks for confirmation of it in the last verse of
the “Radha-rasa-sudhanidhi.” It is also said that some of his books are so perfect that only a man of Prakashananda's
stature could have written them; and the most learned of those who hold this view search his books for a few words from the
“Vedanta-sutra” and for hints at the difficulties the mayavadis go through; and from the “Chaitanya-chandramrita” they
infer that he had once been a “brahma-sannyasi,” a worshipper of the impersonal Brahman. <!-- F36 Q13 Q14 F37 F38 F39 F32 F44 F46 -->

This identification was refuted as early as the “Sajjana-toshani” (year 18, no. 5) and in Srila Bhaktisiddhanta's
article “Sri Prabodhananda,” and he refutes it himself in the “Anubhashya” and in the “Gaudiya-bhashya” on the
“Chaitanya-bhagavata.” In the “Chaitanya-bhagavata” it is the Lord Himself who speaks of Prakashananda — before His
sannyasa, in Navadvipa, in the house of Murari Gupta, having manifested the form of the Boar:

“‘The Vedas slander Me, claiming that I have no hands, feet, mouth or eyes. In Kashi lives a rogue named Prakashananda,
who cuts My body to pieces. He explains the Vedas, yet rejects My eternal form. Though leprosy has already spread over
his whole body, he still cannot understand his error. My body is perfectly pure, the embodiment of all sacrifices;
therefore even such great personalities as Brahma and Shiva glorify it.’” (CB, Madhya 3.36–39)

And later, again revealing His glory to Murari, the Lord says of the same man: “In Kashi lives a sannyasi named
Prakashananda. <…> He teaches the ‘Vedanta,’ but denies My divine form. I have sent leprosy upon him, but still he has
understood nothing. In My body countless universes are manifest” (CB, Madhya 20.33–35)[^17]. This is the wrath of the
Lord, revealing Himself in the mood of Ishvara, against blasphemy of His eternal form — and these words are spoken of
Prakashananda of Kashi. <!-- Q8 Q9 -->

On these verses Srila Bhaktisiddhanta writes in the “Gaudiya-bhashya”: Prakashananda was a teacher of mayavada and a
sannyasi who in his interpretations of the Vedas “dissected” the transcendental body of the Lord; simple people take him
to be the same person as Prabodhananda, the younger brother of Venkata Bhatta from the banks of the Kaveri, and this
delusion entered the sahajiya “Bhakta-mala,” and from there, to a greater or lesser degree, the works of many modern
scholars. One of the biographies, repeating these words, adds: there is no evidence whatever that Prakashananda, having
received deliverance, came to be called Prabodhananda. <!-- F41 Q11 F36 -->

Srila Bhaktisiddhanta's argument is simple and rigorous: it is chronology. The Lord's words about Prakashananda in
Murari's house belong to Shaka 1425–1430 — he was already a mayavadi in Kashi then. In Shaka 1433 the Lord saw
Prabodhananda in Shrirangam as a Shri Vaishnava, a vishishtadvaitin by birth and faith, and soon as His own follower and
the teacher of Gopala. And in Shaka 1435 the Lord in Kashi converts Prakashananda, the head of nearly a thousand
sannyasis of Shankara's school. How could a mayavadi become a follower of Ramanuja in the south and, two years later,
again be a sannyasi of Shankara in Kashi? This can be explained only if two different people are meant — one from the
north, the other from the south[^18]. And one thing more: the “Charitamrita,” having described the change in
Prakashananda in such detail, does not say a word about his changing his name to “Prabodhananda.” <!-- F39 F42 F43 -->

Srila Bhaktisiddhanta contrasts them in everything. Prakashananda was an ekadandi sannyasi of Shankara's school living in
Kashi, a resident of Aryavarta, a nirvisheshavadi — a preacher of the impersonal Absolute — an opponent of Vishnu and the
Vaishnavas, who became a devotee only after his deliverance. Prabodhananda was a tridandi of Ramanuja's school, who came
from Mysore to Ranga-kshetra and later lived in Kamyavana, a southerner, a savisheshavadi who acknowledges the personal
God, and then a follower of Mahaprabhu's achintya-bhedabheda, an eternally perfect associate of Gaura and the guru of
Gopala Bhatta. To identify them Srila Bhaktisiddhanta calls “sheer madness,” betraying only utter ignorance, and to call
the revered uncle and guru of Gopala Bhatta a mayavadi, a hater of Vishnu and a conditioned soul — a terrible offence
against a Vaishnava, leading to hell. To accept the inference about a “brahma-sannyasi” without firm evidence
is, in his words, to entangle oneself in difficulties. <!-- F42 F21 F32 I4 -->

The argument from the “Radha-rasa-sudhanidhi” is answered as well. In its last verse, as the “Abhidhana” cites it, the poet speaks of
those “scorched by the burning sun of mayavada” (mayavadarka-tapa-santapta); but this is no confession of his own past: all the
followers of Mahaprabhu denounced mayavada, and Prabodhananda simply glorifies the Lord as the deliverer of the fallen —
for the conversion of Sarvabhauma and Prakashananda is an even greater miracle of mercy than the deliverance of Jagai and
Madhai. The stern words about mayavada in his books are praise of the Lord's boundless mercy. And the learned words from
the “Vedanta-sutra” found in his verses cannot stand against plain historical events. One of the biographies
concludes: the theory of Lal Dasa and of those who take his conjecture for history withstands no logic and is wholly

## Tungavidya
Who Prabodhananda is in the eternal pastimes of Krishna Kavi Karnapura reveals in the “Gaura-ganoddesha-dipika,” listing
the eight closest friends of Sri Radha and their manifestations in Gaura-lila:

“Tungavidya, who in Vraja was learned in all the shastras, is the renunciant Prabodhananda, the Sarasvati who sings of
Gaura.” (GGD 163)

Tungavidya is one of the eight principal sakhis of Radha, Her eternal friend, versed in all the shastras; her service in
the pastimes of the Divine Couple is a service of pure love, with nothing worldly in it. And Prabodhananda, the most
learned sannyasi and poet, who gave his learning to the glorification of Gaura and the sweetness of Vrindavana, is, in
the eternal pastimes, she — Tungavidya. The table of associates appended to Govinda Dasa's “Karcha” identifies him in
the same way (no. 41)[^19]. <!-- F33 Q1 -->

## Significance
The sources say little about Prabodhananda, and almost all they say is connected with two things — his books and his
disciple. The silence of the early biographies is not a sign of his smallness but the fruit of the humility of an
intimate associate who forbade others to write about him; and his “Chaitanya-chandramrita” testifies to his complete
devotion to Gaura more clearly than any chronicle. Through Gopala Bhatta, whom he raised and taught, his service entered
the very heart of the Gaudiya sampradaya — the “Hari-bhakti-vilasa” and the service of the Gosvamis of Vrindavana.
<!-- I1 F22 -->

To take this eternal associate for a converted mayavadi is not merely a historical error but an offence against a
Vaishnava and a distortion of tradition; that is why Srila Bhaktisiddhanta defended his name so insistently. And the
house in Shrirangam where the Lord of Goloka Himself was a guest for four months holds, in his words, an inexhaustible
treasure of prema — and in that house Prabodhananda, the eternal Tungavidya, the sakhi of Radha, served Mahaprabhu
together with his brothers. May this saint grant us the ability to understand the wondrous love of Radha and Krishna.
