# Jagai and Madhai
*Джагай и Мадхай · @jagai-madhai*

## Two Brothers of Navadvipa
Of all whom Sri Chaitanya and Nityananda delivered in Navadvipa, no names are better known than those of Jagai and Madhai.
Two brahmana brothers, drunkards and robbers before whom all Nadia trembled, they became branches of the Chaitanya tree
and, in the words of Krishnadasa Kaviraja, living witnesses to His name “Deliverer of the Fallen.” Their full names were
Jagannatha and Madhava: so they are called by Kavi Karnapura in the “Gaura-ganoddesha-dipika” and in the drama
“Chaitanya-chandrodaya,” by Murari Gupta and by the “Prema-vilasa.”[^1] The biographies also call them “the two robbers”
(*dasyu-dvaya*) and “great sinners and drunkards”; Lochana Dasa calls them “the masters (*thakuras*) of Navadvipa”;[^2]
and after his repentance everyone called Madhai “Madhai the Brahmachari.” <!-- F1 -->

The brothers were born in Navadvipa, in a brahmana family of good lineage. Their father was a pious brahmana, strict in
keeping his duty; according to the people of Nadia whose words Vrindavana Dasa records, both father and mother came of a
respectable family, and all their ancestors had lived in Nadia free from even small faults (CB, Madhya 13.47–48,
13.122).[^3] The “Charitamrita” says the same briefly: they were born in a brahmana family and always lived in Navadvipa
(Madhya 1.193). Their home, according to Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati's “Gaudiya-bhashya,” stood on the bank of the
Ganga, not far from Mayapur. Only the late “Prema-vilasa” gives their pedigree: the brothers were grandsons of Shubhananda
Raya, a kulina brahmana and zamindar of Navadvipa and a friend of the padishah, and their fathers were Shubhananda's sons
Raghunatha and Janardana; the brothers themselves it calls “the rajas of Nadia.” Neither the early sources nor the
biographies know of this. Nor are the years of their life known: no one gives the day of their appearance or of their
departure, and their days are not in the Vaishnava calendar of Sri Chaitanya Saraswat Math. <!-- F2 F4 F5 -->

But bad company ruined the sons of a pious father. While still young they abandoned the dignity of their family and made
robbery their trade. Vrindavana Dasa says that the brothers drank wine and ate meat, robbed, set houses on fire and fell
upon passers-by with their fists; their relatives disowned them, and the people of Nadia feared that one day these two
would burn down their houses too (CB, Madhya 13.31–52). Lochana Dasa adds that they dishonoured women and killed, and that,
living on the very bank of the Ganga, they never once bathed in her; Jayananda, that they read the “Masnavi”; the
“Prema-vilasa,” that they did not acknowledge the padishah and that even the qazi feared them. So close were the brothers
that Vrindavana Dasa calls them one soul in two bodies (CB, Madhya 13.200). The “Charitamrita,” through the mouths of
Rupa and Sanatana, says that their only fault was attachment to sin (Madhya 1.194). <!-- F6 F7 -->

And yet one sin was not upon them:

“Every sin was manifest in their bodies except the sin of offending a Vaishnava.” (CB, Madhya 13.39)

Day and night they kept company with drunkards, and so they had no occasion to offend a devotee (13.40). Srila
Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati in the “Gaudiya-bhashya” sees here the key to their whole destiny: for all their many sins,
Jagai and Madhai never offended the Vaishnavas — and therefore the mercy of Gaura and Nityananda awakened the greatest
good fortune in them; offence to a Vaishnava is the gravest of sins, which without the mercy of the Vaishnavas themselves
not even the holy name washes away. The biographies say the same: Nityananda, most merciful of the merciful, delivered
such sinners as Jagai and Madhai and gave them love of God, yet even He is angered by one who offends a Vaishnava.

## Jaya and Vijaya
Who they truly were Kavi Karnapura reveals:

“The two doorkeepers of Vaikuntha, Jaya and Vijaya, have now by their own will been born as Sri Jagannatha and Madhava.”
(“Gaura-ganoddesha-dipika” 115)

Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati cites this verse in the “Anubhashya” to the “Charitamrita” (Adi 10.120): born in
Navadvipa by their own will, they received the holy name and became maha-bhagavatas. The “Prema-vilasa” says the same;
the biographies do not give this identification. The doorkeepers of the Lord came into this world not compelled by
karma but by their own will — to reveal to the world, through their fall and their deliverance, Mahaprabhu's name
“Deliverer of the Fallen.” Yet in the Lord's lila they were real sinners, and the sources tell of their crimes without
softening them; their identity with Jaya and Vijaya does not turn their sins into make-believe, nor does it excuse them —
it only reveals whose hand guided this whole story. <!-- F3 Q12 -->

## “Our Alms Is Service to Krishna”
It began with the command Mahaprabhu gave to Nityananda and Haridasa Thakura:

“Listen, Nityananda! Listen, Haridasa! Go and make My teaching known to the whole world. Go to every house and beg:
‘Chant the names of Krishna, worship Krishna and follow Krishna's teaching.’ Speak of nothing else and ask for nothing
else. Come at the end of the day and tell Me everything.” (CB, Madhya 13.8–10)

So they went from door to door. When householders respectfully invited “the two sannyasis” to accept alms, they begged
for one thing only — service to Krishna, kirtana of Krishna and teaching about Krishna — and went on. <!-- F8 Q10 -->

One day in the street they met two drunken brahmanas — Jagai and Madhai. Seeing the abyss to which they had fallen,
Nityananda, the all-merciful saviour of the fallen, and Haridasa wept at heart. Vrindavana Dasa says:

“Boundless is the glory of Sri Nityananda Prabhu. He descended to deliver the fallen souls.” (CB, Madhya 13.62)

Nityananda was especially merciful to them because He saw their fall. He told Haridasa His intention and asked him to
help:

“O Haridasa, see how wretched they are! Born brahmanas, they have fallen so low. They will never escape the punishment
of Yamaraja… And since you have wished them well, they will surely be delivered. The Lord fulfils all your desires. He
Himself has revealed this truth to us.” (CB, Madhya 13.63–64, 66–67)

Let the whole universe see the Lord's greatness, Nityananda said, when He delivers these two — as the Puranas sing of the
deliverance of Ajamila (13.68–69). Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati explains here in the “Gaudiya-bhashya”: the deliverance
of Jagai and Madhai in Navadvipa-dhama is not merely one more story of the scriptures, like the case of Ajamila, nor only
an event of the distant past; such wonders take place in Sri Chaitanya's lila even today. <!-- F9 F10 Q1 Q2 F73 -->

Haridasa answered, “Whatever You desire, Gaurasundara will support in full.” The very truth of Nityananda was revealed to
him, and he already knew that these two were saved (13.70–71). But the brothers are so drunk, he said, that they will
listen neither to morality nor to worldly good; we, carrying out the command, bring the name to all without distinction.
And yet, as people ordinarily reason (“there is no point preaching to the wicked”), Haridasa tried to dissuade
Nityananda from approaching them. One biography, following the “Gaudiya-bhashya,” calls these objections just that — the
plain reasoning of the uninformed: in truth Haridasa, a great devotee, was only playing his part in Nityananda's lila.
Respectable townsfolk tried to stop them as well: the brothers had no regard for sannyasis, and many killings of brahmanas
and cows lay at their door (13.78–80). Nityananda listened to no one. <!-- F11 -->

Both, chanting “Krishna, Krishna,” came close enough for the brothers to hear and loudly proclaimed the Lord's command:

“Call on Krishna, worship Krishna and chant Krishna's names. Krishna is mother and father; He is the wealth of life
itself. Krishna has descended to grant you the highest good. So give up your sinful life and worship Him alone.”
(CB, Madhya 13.83–84)

One biography, following the “Gaudiya-bhashya” on this verse, says: all worship apart from Krishna is only a departure
from what is due, and therefore Mahaprabhu taught the service of Hari everywhere — through His own manifestation
(*prakasha-vigraha*) and through the creator of the world. <!-- F12 Q3 I1 -->

## The Flight
Hearing the name of Krishna, the brothers raised their heads in anger, their eyes went bloodshot, and with a shout of
“Seize them! Catch them!” they rushed after the preachers. Nityananda and Haridasa ran. The fat drunkards could not run
fast, but they shouted after them, “Where can you escape from the hands of Jagai and Madhai!” — while “the two Prabhus”
ran, crying, “Protect us, Krishna! Protect us, Krishna-Govinda!” Old Haridasa could barely keep up with Nityananda, but
both escaped: the tipsy pursuers stumbled, rolled on the ground and fell behind (CB, Madhya 13.85–109). On the way the
two Prabhus bantered: Haridasa reproached Nityananda for His restlessness, and He replied that the restless one was
Haridasa's own Lord, who had given such an order — and both laughed. <!-- F13 -->

The all-powerful Nityananda, who will soon Himself deliver these brothers, runs from them — this is not fear but a
mysterious lila. The biographies say so: Nityananda's flight is inscrutable, for later He alone delivered them. <!-- I2 -->

Nityananda and Haridasa came to Mahaprabhu and told Him everything. “Who are they?” the Lord asked. “What are their
names? How can brahmanas do such things?” Gangadasa Pandita and Srivasa explained: these are Jagai and Madhai, sons of
a pious brahmana, born here, but bad company has spoiled them, and from childhood they have given themselves to violence
and robbery (CB, Madhya 13.120–125). Mahaprabhu said: it does not befit a brahmana to drink wine; to live by robbery and
to chase Vaishnavas is not the dharma of a brahmana. <!-- F14 F15 -->

“I know them,” the Lord went on. “If they come here, I shall tear them to pieces.” Nityananda answered:

“‘You may tear them to pieces,’ said Nityananda, ‘but while they are there I shall go nowhere. Why do You boast? First try
to make them chant the name of Govinda. The pious chant Krishna's names by themselves, but these two know nothing but
sinful deeds. If You deliver these two and bring them to the service of God, then You will be known as Patita-pavana,
the Deliverer of the Fallen.’” (CB, Madhya 13.127–130)

While the brothers live and run riot, Nityananda says, He will not be able to carry out the Lord's command:[^4] to bring
the pious to the name is easy, but to deliver such as Jagai and Madhai — that is glory truly worthy of the name
“Deliverer of the Fallen”; it will bring more glory than delivering Him (13.131). <!-- F16 -->

“Vishvambhara smiled and answered, ‘They were delivered the moment they saw You. Since You are so concerned for them,
Krishna will arrange everything for their good.’” (CB, Madhya 13.132–133)

Whomever Nityananda wishes well, Krishna has already blessed — there is no doubt of it. The Vaishnavas understood that the
brothers were already saved and loudly chanted the name of Hari (13.134–135). And in the evening Haridasa complained to
Advaita Acharya: he would not go with Nityananda any more — He was reckless. Advaita, smiling, replied that within two or
three days Nityananda would bring these drunkards into the assembly of Vaishnavas (13.135–152), and Haridasa smiled in
return: he himself knew that their lot was decided (13.157). <!-- F17 Q4 F18 -->

## To the Sound of Kirtana
Having roamed and plundered all over Nadia, the brothers, by Providence, settled at the very ghat on the Ganga where the
Lord Himself went to bathe, near His house. People stopped going to the Ganga at night, or went only in groups of ten or
twenty (CB, Madhya 13.161–164). At night the sounds of the Lord's kirtana poured from His house, and the drunken brothers,
never closing their eyes, listened to the mridangas and karatalas and danced to them; meeting the Lord one day, they took
His kirtana for songs to Mangalachandi and, asking whether the rite was complete, offered to procure themselves all that
such singing required (13.165–171). <!-- F19 -->

In their wine-darkened minds the kirtana of Krishna appeared as a “tamasic worship” like their own: so one biography
renders their words. The Lord's kirtana was in no way worldly merrymaking; it was the brothers who, by their own nature,
saw their own in it. Yet the very fact that, drunk as they were, they listened to the holy name all night foretold their
coming good fortune. <!-- F19 -->

For their deliverance Nityananda one night, after going round the town, came there Himself. “Who's there?” the brothers
called out. “I am going to the Lord's house,” He replied. “What is Your name?” “My name is Avadhuta.” He had come with
one thought: “I shall deliver these two” (CB, Madhya 13.173–177). <!-- F20 -->

## Madhai's Potsherd
Hearing the name “Avadhuta,” Madhai flew into a rage, seized a shard of a broken clay pot and struck Nityananda on the
head. The head was cut open and blood flowed, but Nityananda only remembered Govinda (CB, Madhya 13.178–179).[^5] Madhai
raised his hand again, but Jagai, moved to pity, caught his arm: “Why did you do that? You have no heart. What will you
gain by beating a stranger? Leave the Avadhuta alone. What will beating a sannyasi bring you?” (13.180–182). <!-- F21 F22 -->

Someone ran to tell the Lord. Mahaprabhu came at once with His associates, saw Nityananda covered in blood and smiling
between the two drunkards, and in anger called aloud, “Chakra! Chakra! Chakra!” At once the Sudarshana chakra appeared,
and Jagai and Madhai saw it with their own eyes in terror (CB, Madhya 13.183–186). Sudarshana is not a vision or sorcery
but the eternal weapon and associate of the Lord, appearing in person at His call. Lochana Dasa shows this clearly:

“In anger Gaurahari summons Sudarshana, and Sudarshana stood [before Him] with joined palms: ‘Why, O Ishvara, have You
summoned me? Glory, glory to Mahaprabhu, the son of Shachi!’” (“Chaitanya-mangala,” Madhya-khanda, 112–113)

The Lord's anger is His love for His wounded devotee; and yet in the Kali age the Lord does not punish with weapons but
saves by the name and by prema. Sudarshana only appeared — and the very fact that the brothers saw the Lord's eternal
associate was already mercy. <!-- F23 -->

Nityananda interceded for His assailants:

“All the devotees stood as if spellbound, but Nityananda pleaded with the Lord: ‘When Madhai struck Me, Jagai saved Me.
By the will of Providence blood has flowed, but I feel no pain. Lord, give Me these two as alms. Please do not be
troubled. It does not hurt Me at all.’” (CB, Madhya 13.187–189)

Thus from the very first moment the deliverance of the brothers was Nityananda's work: it was He who longed to save them,
and He, wounded, who begged them from the Lord “as alms.” <!-- F24 Q5 -->

## Mercy to Jagai
Hearing that Jagai had held back his brother's hand, the Lord rejoiced and embraced him:

“May Krishna shower His mercy on you. By protecting Nityananda you have won Me over. Ask of Me any blessing you wish. From
this day may you attain Krishna-prema-bhakti.” (CB, Madhya 13.190–192)

Jagai fell senseless with love. “Jagai, rise and look at Me,” said the Lord, and Jagai saw Vishvambhara in His four-armed
form, holding conch, disc, club and lotus. Again he lost consciousness, and Sri Chaitanya placed His foot on his chest;
having gained the Lord's foot — the life and treasure of Lakshmi — fortunate Jagai pressed it to his breast and sobbed
(13.194–199). But even his intercession was the fruit of mercy: Nityananda Himself said “Jagai saved Me,” and so opened
the way for him. <!-- F25 -->

Seeing the reward Jagai had received, Madhai too was changed. He let go of Nityananda's cloth, fell at the Lord's feet and
pleaded: “Lord, we sinned together — why have You not shared Your mercy between us both? Grant me Your mercy, and I shall
chant Your name” (CB, Madhya 13.201–204). His repentance did not come from any merit of his own: as the
“Gaudiya-bhashya” says, the brothers were equally sinful and by turns held each other back from evil, and therefore the
blessing given to Jagai transformed Madhai's heart as well. <!-- F26 F78 -->

## At Nityananda's Feet
But the Lord replied, “I see no way for you to be delivered, for Nityananda is covered in blood.” Madhai did not give way:
did not the demons of old, who fought the Lord and pierced Him with arrows, attain His feet? Why then should such an asura
as he not be saved? The Lord said: “Your crime is far graver than theirs, for you have drawn blood from Nityananda. The
body of Nityananda is dearer to Me than My own” (CB, Madhya 13.205–209).[^6] To wound the body of Nityananda, the servant
of Vishnu, is graver than to be at enmity with Vishnu Himself. <!-- F27 -->

“Tell me, how can I be free of this?” Madhai begged: the Lord is the best of physicians; if He does not heal, who will?
And the Lord answered: “Your offence is very grave. Fall at the lotus feet of Nityananda.” Madhai embraced Nityananda's
feet, and the Lord Himself asked: “It was he who wounded You, and You alone can forgive him” (13.210–217). <!-- F28 -->

There is no severity in this. An offence against a Vaishnava can be removed only by the one offended. One biography
compares this lila with the story of Durvasa: the offence committed at the feet of Ambarisha not even Vishnu Himself could
forgive, and He sent the sage back to the king; so Mahaprabhu sends Madhai to Nityananda's feet — the fault is removed only
by the one at whose feet it was committed. <!-- F48 I7 -->

And Nityananda forgave:

“‘Lord, what shall I say?’ Nityananda replied. ‘You can grant Your mercy even to trees. I declare openly: if in My life
I have gathered even a little piety, I give it all to Madhai. I forgive him all his offences. Do not perplex him — grant
Your mercy openly to Your Madhai.’” (CB, Madhya 13.218–220)

You protect in distress the gods, men and all living beings, even the unconscious plants, Nityananda says; Madhai has not
offended Me; I have served You birth after birth — and if in any birth I have some merit (*sukriti*), let it go to Madhai;
You have already forgiven everything — so set aside the semblance of a double judgement and grant causeless mercy. The
words “Madhai has not offended Me” do not belittle the deed; they are the boundless mercy of Nityananda, who gives His
offender all that He has. <!-- F29 -->

“If You have truly forgiven him,” said Vishvambhara, “embrace him.” Nityananda lovingly embraced His assailant and
invested him with His power; Vrindavana Dasa says that Nityananda “entered Madhai's body and filled him with every
potency” (13.221–223) — this is the bestowal of power (*shakti-sanchara*), not any kind of “possession.” By Nityananda's
power Madhai gained every virtue. The delivered brothers offered prayers to the feet of the two Lords; empowered to attain
the Lord's service, they wholly accepted His command. “Never sin again,” said the Lord. “Yes, Lord. Never again,” replied
Jagai and Madhai (13.224–225). <!-- F30 -->

Then the Lord said to them:

“The Lord said: ‘Listen, both of you. Today I have delivered you. I take upon Myself all the sinful deeds you have
committed in millions of past lives — but only on condition that you never give yourselves to sin again. I shall be
manifest in your bodies and shall eat through your mouths.’” (CB, Madhya 13.226–228)

Sin cannot touch the Lord. “The mighty are not stained” by what would stain others, says the “Bhagavatam” (10.33.29–30):
so fire consumes everything and remains pure. In accepting the brothers' sins the Lord does not take their guilt upon
Himself or suffer from it — He only sets them free. <!-- F59 -->

## In the Lord's House
The brothers swooned with bliss, and the Lord ordered:

“Take these brothers and carry them to My house. Today I shall sing kirtana with them. Today I shall reward them with what
is rarely attained even by Brahma. I shall grant them the highest position in this world.” (CB, Madhya 13.231–232)[^7]

Once the very sight and touch of the robbers stirred sin in people; now, having received God's mercy, they purified like
the touch of the Ganga: whoever bathes in the Ganga after touching them will say that these two are equal to the Ganga
(13.233). “Nothing can stand in the way of Nityananda's decision,” said the Lord. “Know that this was His wish” (13.234).
The Vaishnavas accepted the brothers as their own and carried them to Mahaprabhu's house. Only His intimates entered, with
the two robbers He had accepted, and the door was shut (13.235–236). Mahaprabhu sat on His seat, Nityananda and Gadadhara
on either side of Him, Advaita facing Him, and the Vaishnavas all around:

“Pundarika Vidyanidhi, Haridasa Thakura, Garuda, Ramai, Srinivasa, Gangadasa, Vakreshvara Pandita and Chandrashekhara
Acharya knew well the lilas of Lord Chaitanya.” (CB, Madhya 13.239–240)

The biographies name Chandrashekhara, Vakreshvara, Gangadasa and Pundarika Vidyanidhi as witnesses of that night.
<!-- F31 Q6 F32 Q7 -->

The brothers rolled on the ground in ecstasy: the hair on their bodies stood on end, tears streamed from their eyes,
their bodies trembled (13.242). Vrindavana Dasa exclaims:

“Who can understand the will of Lord Chaitanya, who turned two bandits into maha-bhagavatas?” (CB, Madhya 13.243)

The position of a maha-bhagavata a living being does not reach even in many births of service to Hari, yet these two,
unworthy before, attained it in a single moment; no one has the right to judge such power of mercy. <!-- F33 I3 -->

Seated among the devotees before the Lord, the brothers showed every sign of prema, and Pundarika Vidyanidhi, seeing their
good fortune, was plunged into ecstasy. By the will of Chaitanyachandra, Shuddha Sarasvati, the goddess of transcendent
speech, appeared on the brothers' tongues, and Jagai and Madhai glorified Gaura in every way (CB, Madhya 13.246–293).
Their prayer contains these words:

“And we, after drawing blood from Nityananda, have seen You together with Your associates, servants, weapons and faithful
retinue.” (CB, Madhya 13.269)

Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati unfolds them in the “Gaudiya-bhashya”: having wounded His *anga* — Nityananda — the
brothers were blessed to see His *anga*, *upanga*, *astra* and *parishad*; the *anga* is Nityananda and Advaita, the
*upanga* the devotees headed by Srivasa, the *astra* the name of Hari, the *parishada* Gadadhara, Svarupa and the other
eternal associates. He gives a second reading as well: Krishna's beauty, His ornaments, mahabhava and the eternal
associates. <!-- F34 F75 -->

The brothers clasped the feet of each of the devotees and begged for blessings, and all blessed them (13.293–294). Then
the Lord said:

“‘Jagai and Madhai, rise,’ said the Lord. ‘You have nothing more to fear, for from now on you are My servants.’”
(CB, Madhya 13.295)

All that they had felt, the Lord said, was solely by the mercy of Nityananda; I Myself have taken away the consequences of
your sins (13.297–298). And as a sign that no sin remained on them, the Lord's body turned black. “How do I look?” He asked.
“You are exactly like Sri Gokulachandra,” replied Advaita. The Lord had kirtana sung so that the brothers' sins would pass
to the offenders of the Vaishnavas (13.299–302).[^8] This darkening is the Lord's play, a visible testimony of mercy, and
not a trace of sin upon Him: in His form Advaita saw Krishna Himself. The kirtana began; the Lord danced with Nityananda,
and the two former drunkards too danced with Him whom Lakshmi herself fears to touch; and Shachi and her daughter-in-law,
Vishnupriya, watched it all from the house, immersed in an ocean of nectar (13.303–310). <!-- F59 -->

After the dance the Lord said to the devotees: “Do not regard these two as sinners any longer. I have completely burnt
up all their sins” (13.317). It was He Himself who had directed the brothers' deeds and freed them from the proud “I do,
I speak”; therefore the Vaishnavas must accept them into their circle and never judge them by the past; to feed them is
like offering honey to Krishna's mouth; and whoever mocks them will perish (13.320–326). Hearing this, the Vaishnavas,
weeping with love, bowed to Jagai and Madhai (13.327); so all the Vaishnavas showed them the honour due to Vaishnavas.
<!-- F35 F60 -->

Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati writes of these words of the Lord in the “Gaudiya-bhashya”: Gaurasundara accepted the
bodies of Jagai and Madhai as His own property and made them wholly transcendent, for at initiation Krishna makes the
devotee's body spiritual (CC, Antya 4.192–193); and all that they did, said and ate afterwards was His will and service to
Vishnu. And whoever regards them as sinners, remembering their past (by the *nagna-matrika-nyaya*, the “maxim of the naked
mother”: remembering a person as he was at birth), offends their lotus feet and will perish; the Vaishnavas, for their
part, bow before them with great reverence. Born in a high brahmana family, the acharya says, the brothers had left it
for robbery, and now by the Lord's mercy their position was restored: their foolish thirst for enjoyment vanished, they
realized *sambandha*, *abhidheya* and *prayojana*, and Madana-mohana, Govinda and Gopinatha became the Lords of their
hearts. <!-- F76 F77 F74 -->

Then the Lord led everyone to the Ganga and played long with the devotees in her waters. After the play He gave everyone
garlands, prasada and sandal paste and sent them home, and Jagai and Madhai He entrusted to all the Vaishnavas and gave
them the garland from His own neck. The biographies say: the Lord forgave their offences, cleansed them of sin and gave
them the right to take part in sankirtana with the devotees. In the water play after their deliverance were Gadadhara,
Buddhimanta Khan, Jagadisha, Shuklambara, Sadashiva, Gangadasa and Pundarika, and Vakreshvara was present at the
deliverance itself. <!-- F36 F37 -->

Vrindavana Dasa closes the account with a promise:

“Whoever hears these accounts of the deliverance of the two bandits will be delivered by Sri Gaurachandra.”
(CB, Madhya 13.394)

And the last verse of the chapter glorifies Gauranga as the deliverer of two brahmanas who had become demons (13.395);
Srila Bhaktisiddhanta explains that hence comes His name “Brahma-daitya-tarana,” and that the brothers were called
“demons” because they had turned away from the Lord. <!-- F38 Q8 -->

## Yamaraja's Dance
The next chapter of the “Chaitanya-bhagavata” takes us to the demigods. Every day they came invisibly to serve the Lord
and, returning to their abodes after the deliverance of “the two demonic brahmanas,” rejoiced: if He has saved even them,
there is hope for us too (CB, Madhya 14.1–8). Yamaraja came as well. “How many sins have these two committed?” he asked
Chitragupta. The reply: a hundred thousand scribes could not list them in a whole month; Yama's servants had grown weary
of recording their crimes, a mountain of records had piled up, and now Mahaprabhu in a moment had freed the brothers from
every consequence — and, if ordered, the records could be thrown into the water (14.9–19). Yamaraja is by nature a
Vaishnava who knows the inner meaning of bhagavata-dharma (14.21); hearing this, he fell senseless in his chariot with love
for Krishna. Shiva and Brahma, understanding his state, sang kirtana into his ear, and, coming to himself, Yamaraja broke
into dance, glorifying the magnanimous deliverer of the fallen; with him danced Shiva, Brahma, Narada, Shukadeva, Indra,
Ananta and all the demigods (14.22–51). Yama's swoon and his dance are not the confusion of a god of death but prema.

## Two Hundred Thousand Names
From then on, by Mahaprabhu's mercy, Jagai and Madhai lived in Nadia a strict and righteous life:

“Every day they bathed in the Ganga early in the morning and then sat in a secluded place to chant two hundred thousand
names of Krishna.” (CB, Madhya 15.5)

The “Gaudiya-bhashya” remarks here that Haridasa chanted three hundred thousand names a day, and that a follower of the
Lord should chant no fewer than a hundred thousand. <!-- F39 -->

Remembering their former sins, the brothers repented, wept and fell senseless, crying, “O Gaurachandra! O saviour of the
fallen!” Having tasted the boundless mercy, they saw the whole world as connected with Krishna. The Lord and His associates
comforted them again and again, came Himself and fed them, but they found no peace (CB, Madhya 15.6–12). <!-- F40 -->

Madhai above all was inconsolable. Though Nityananda had forgiven him everything, he could not forget that he had shed His
blood, and he reproached himself without end: “I struck the One in whom Lord Chaitanya enjoys His pastimes” (15.13–17).
One day, finding Nityananda alone, he fell at His feet with a straw between his teeth, bathed them with tears and glorified
Him at length: You are Ananta, who upholds the universes, the bed, throne and umbrella of the Lord; none is dearer to
Chaitanya than You; and I wounded this divine body! (15.20–62). Nityananda smiled:

“Rise, Madhai! You are My servant. From now on I am manifest in your body. Does a father take offence at the slaps of his
little son? So I have taken the blows you dealt Me.” (CB, Madhya 15.64–65)

Nityananda saw no fault in him at all: “My Lord has shown you mercy, and so not a shadow of sin remains on you” (15.67) —
and He embraced him. <!-- F41 F42 -->

## Madhai's Ghat
But Madhai asked again: “You dwell in the hearts of all living beings, and I have caused pain to many of them. I do not
even remember all who suffered from me. How can I receive their blessings?” (15.71–75). Nityananda replied:

“The Lord answered: ‘Listen to what I tell you. You must build a ghat, a bathing place on the bank of the Ganga. Bathing
happily in the waters of the Ganga, people will always bless you.’” (CB, Madhya 15.76–77)

And He told him to bow humbly before everyone (15.79). From then on Madhai every day, in tears, chanting “Krishna,
Krishna,” built the ghat and served it, prostrated himself before all who came to bathe, and begged forgiveness for the
wrongs he had done knowingly or unknowingly.[^9] People marvelled and wept with him, and all Nadia admitted: “Nimai Pandita
has turned Jagai and Madhai into great saints! Nimai Pandita is no ordinary mortal” (15.80–91). <!-- F43 F44 -->

For his severe austerity Madhai was called “the Brahmachari” (15.92). Vrindavana Dasa says of him:

“Living by the ghat on the bank of the Ganga, Madhai always kept his gaze on the sacred river. Spade in hand, he worked
there tirelessly. To this day everyone calls Madhai's ghat a witness to the mercy of Lord Chaitanya. Thus the two brothers
became widely famed as wicked men saved by the mercy of Sri Chaitanya Mahaprabhu.” (CB, Madhya 15.93–95)

Madhai the Brahmachari, who had once wounded Nityananda, became a holy ascetic — and both brothers became famous for the
very mercy that saved them. <!-- F45 F46 Q9 -->

Where this ghat was, Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati explains. The Lord's own ghat lay a little south of His house,
Madhai's ghat next to it, and beyond it the Barakona ghat; in the great procession to the qazi the Lord danced long at His
own ghat and then went on to Madhai's ghat (CB, Madhya 23.299 and the bhashya to 23.299–300). Among the ghats young Nimai
used to visit, the acharya also names the Madhai ghat (bhashya to CB, Adi 8.50). The place is near present-day
Shrinathapur; the ghat itself was long ago swallowed by the Ganga, and the village of Mahatpur near Ekadala, which is passed
off as Madhai's ghat, has nothing to do with it (bhashya to CB, Madhya 15.76; cf. “Chitra-Navadvipa,” p. 52). The
biographies are silent on the site of the ghat. <!-- F47 -->

## The Deliverance in Other Sources
Krishnadasa Kaviraja in the “Charitamrita” does not retell this story — it is described in detail by Vrindavana Dasa —
but mentions it from the very first chapters:

“Such love Sri Chaitanya gave everywhere, even to Jagai and Madhai — what then of others?” (CC, Adi 8.20)

“Jagai and Madhai received the Lord's great mercy; these two brothers bear witness to His name ‘Deliverer of the Fallen.’”
(CC, Adi 10.120)

“Then Shachi saw the two brothers as Rama and Krishna, and after that the Lord delivered Jagai and Madhai.”
(CC, Adi 17.17)

The “Anubhashya” to Adi 8.20 adds: even such sinners, receiving Gaura's mercy, one day attain love for Krishna. And
Vrindavana Dasa himself, at the start of his book, listing the lilas of the Madhya-khanda, names among them “the wonder of
the deliverance of the two greatest sinners” (CB, Adi 1.125), and, closing the account, says that the stories of the
deliverance of “the two godless ones” are like nectar (Madhya 15.96). <!-- F57 F62 -->

Murari Gupta does not describe the deliverance itself but speaks of the Lord's promise. When Mahaprabhu refused to heal a
leper who had offended Srivasa, Srivasa asked Him to deliver all who had offended him:

“‘Deliver also Jagannatha, Madhava and the others, full of sin though they are.’ ‘So be it,’ said Bhagavan, who tears out
the root of all sins.” (Murari Gupta, “Sri Krishna-chaitanya-charita,” 2.13.17) <!-- F63 -->

Lochana Dasa tells this lila in his own way. The devotees complain to the Lord that Jagai and Madhai hinder them from giving
the name from house to house; the Lord, recalling Ajamila, resolves to save them by sankirtana and goes out with kirtana
through the town. Woken, the brothers curse and throw a potsherd;[^10] the Lord binds Nityananda's head with His own cloth so
that His blood should not fall to the ground; Murari Gupta is eager to punish the offenders; Sudarshana appears with joined
palms, but Nityananda holds him back and begs the Lord:

“‘Lord, give these two fallen ones to Me: let the name “Purifier of the fallen” be renowned. In other ages You destroyed
the demons — deliver these two in their very bodies.’” (“Chaitanya-mangala,” Madhya-khanda, 121–122)

Repentant, the brothers come to the Lord's door, and Murari at His bidding brings them in; by the Ganga, before everyone,
the Lord asks them for their sins as a gift, they place a tulasi leaf in His hand, and, embracing them, He dances, having
accepted their sins.[^11] One biography also recalls that the sixth chapter of the Madhya-khanda of the
“Chaitanya-mangala” tells of their deliverance. <!-- F64 F56 -->

In Kavi Karnapura's drama “Chaitanya-chandrodaya,” Kali relates how the Lord Himself called the brahmana brothers
Jagannatha and Madhava, sunk in the five great sins, and asked them to give Him all their sins; taking water from His hand,
they at once shone, with tears of joy, stammering, chanted “Krishna, Krishna,” and rose to the rank of great devotees
(1.38). In Govinda-deva's mahakavya “Gaura-krishnodaya” the Lord saves two lost brahmanas and raises the banner of the
Purifier of the fallen (5.36). In Jayananda, Madhai wounds Nityananda with a potsherd and Jagai saves Him; Gaurachandra
grants Jagai prema-bhakti, and Nityananda gives Madhai the name of Hari; the brothers place their sins in the Lord's hands,
He pours Ganga water on their heads — and His face turns dark and then shines golden again. In the “Prema-vilasa” Madhai
strikes Nitai with a potsherd, Jagai holds him back, the Lord summons the Chakra, Nitai begs the brothers from Him as alms,
Madhai at the Lord's word falls at Nitai's feet, and Nitai, accepting their sins, turns dark in body.[^12] The same work has
Sita relate that at the deliverance of Jagai and Madhai Advaita threatened in anger to “dry up prema.” <!-- F65 F66 F67 -->

In Narahari Chakravarti's “Bhakti-ratnakara” the story is told by Chaitanya Dasa: the Lord saved the brothers not by
weapons but by prema; Madhai wounded Nityananda, by Nityananda's mercy the Lord forgave them, and they swept a ghat on the
Ganga and became the Lord's associates (2.265–295). The “Advaita-prakasha” recalls how the deliverance of Jagai and Madhai
and the conversion of the qazi amazed the people (14.155–158). The poets sang of them too: in Vasu Ghosha, Jagai and
Madhai, saved by the name, dance in the kirtana; in Balarama Dasa they bow at the feet of Gaura as before Vamana; Shivananda
sings of the cloud of Chaitanya that poured upon Jagai and Madhai; Krishnadasa sings how the Lord saved them; and in
Narahari all Nadia runs to see the transformed brothers — they shine, weep, roll in the dust, bathe Nitai's feet with tears
and with straw between their teeth beg for mercy (BR 12). <!-- F68 F69 -->

## “Be Merciful”
Narahari Chakravarti in the “Namamrita-samudra,” his prayers to the Lord's associates, addresses them as well:

“O two brothers, Jagai and Madhai, be merciful: now take away [from me] the sins of many births.” (“Namamrita-samudra” 47)

Those whom the Lord freed from the sins of millions of births have themselves become a shelter for sinners. In another
prayer of the same collection the poet asks Bhagavan Acharya: may I sing how Jagai and Madhai attained prema (131).
<!-- F70 -->

## An Example of Mercy
For Vaishnavas the names of Jagai and Madhai became the measure of the Lord's mercy. Krishnadasa Kaviraja, in humility,
writes of himself:

“I am more sinful than Jagai and Madhai, lower than a worm in excrement.” (CC, Adi 5.205)

And he adds at once: who in all the world but Nityananda would show mercy to one as despicable as I? (5.207). <!-- F51 -->

Rupa and Sanatana, meeting the Lord for the first time in Ramakeli, prayed to Him:

“You delivered the two brothers Jagai and Madhai, but delivering them cost You no effort. They were brahmanas by birth and
lived in Navadvipa. They did not serve low people, nor were they the ‘elbow’ — the ready tool — of low men. Their only fault
was sinful conduct, and even the semblance of Your name (*namabhasa*) burns up heaps of sin. They uttered Your name while
blaspheming You, and that very name became the cause of their liberation. We two are millions of times lower, more fallen
and more sinful than Jagai and Madhai.” (CC, Madhya 1.192–196) <!-- F52 F58 -->

King Prataparudra, whom the Lord long refused to receive, lamented:

“He has descended to deliver the sinful and the fallen; He delivered even Jagai and Madhai. He has descended with this vow:
He will deliver the whole world, leaving out Prataparudra alone.” (CC, Madhya 11.45–46)

He has vowed not to see me, said the king, but I too make a vow: without Him I shall give up my life (11.48–49). One
biography also cites a song-prayer about Sanatana: You easily delivered Jagai and Madhai, Vasudeva and Ajamila — show
Sanatana too even a drop of mercy. <!-- F53 F54 Q11 -->

The biographies place the brothers in a row with other wonders of mercy: as the courtesan became a saint by Haridasa's
mercy, and the robber Ratnakara became Valmiki by Narada's, so did Jagai and Madhai by the mercy of Gaura and Nityananda; a
pure devotee transforms the fallen. The deliverance of Sarvabhauma and Prakashananda, they say, is a still greater wonder of
mercy than the deliverance of such sinners as Jagai and Madhai. And in 1925, when Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati was
attacked at Porama-tola during the parikrama of Navadvipa-dhama, an eyewitness compared the attack to the way “the
constables of Navadvipa, the two thugs Jagai and Madhai,” had once tried to wound Nityananda Avadhuta. <!-- F50 I6 I5 F55 -->

## Departure
When and where Jagai and Madhai left this world neither the sources nor the biographies say. Only the “Prema-vilasa” names
them among Nityananda's branch at a memorial festival in Katwa, after the Lord's disappearance;[^13] neither the
“Chaitanya-bhagavata” nor the “Charitamrita” knows of this. <!-- F49 -->

## Significance
Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati sums up their story in the “Gaudiya-bhashya” to the first chapter of the
“Chaitanya-bhagavata”: having received brahmana birth, Jagai and Madhai did not follow their duty and sank into sin, and
attacked Nityananda and Haridasa as they preached the holy name; but when Nityananda forgave their offence, by the mercy
of Gaurasundara they attained deliverance and became pure devotees. <!-- F71 -->

In these words lies the whole of it. The deliverance of the brothers is Nityananda's mercy: He was the first to long for
their salvation, He, wounded, begged them from the Lord as alms, forgave Madhai and gave him all His merits, embraced him
and invested him with His power; the Lord Himself says that everything came about solely by Nityananda's mercy (CB, Madhya
13.297). Their story teaches, too, that the gravest sin is offence to a Vaishnava, and that only the one offended can remove
it. And after the mercy Jagai and Madhai are maha-bhagavatas, whose bodies the Lord accepted as His own; to see in them the
former robbers is to offend their feet. The doorkeepers of Vaikuntha, born in Navadvipa by their own will, became an
eternal witness that for the mercy of Gaura and Nitai no one is unworthy. <!-- F3 F33 F60 F77 I3 I7 -->
