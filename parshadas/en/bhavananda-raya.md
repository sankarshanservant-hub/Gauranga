# Bhavananda Raya
*Бхавананда Рай · @bhavananda-raya*

## “You Are Pandu”
Sri Bhavananda Raya was an Oriya devotee, the father of Ramananda Raya and of four more sons whom Mahaprabhu called His
eternal servants. In the “Charitamrita” his name appears in several forms: “Raya Bhavananda” (Adi 10.131; Madhya
10.50), “Bhavananda Raya” (Antya 9.15, 9.61, 9.103) and simply “Bhavananda” (Antya 9.120); Kavi Karnapura too calls him
so, both in the “Gaura-ganoddesha-dipika” and in his poem on the Lord. Krishnadasa Kaviraja Gosvami, enumerating the
branches of the tree of Sri Chaitanya, places him beside two brahmanas of Nilachala:

“Then come Kashi Mishra, Pradyumna Mishra and Bhavananda Raya, whose company gave the Lord great joy.” (CC, Adi 10.131)

In the numbering of the branches adopted in our edition of the “Charitamrita,” Bhavananda Raya is the seventh branch,
and his five sons headed by Ramananda are the eighth. And at once Kaviraja recalls the words the Lord spoke to
Bhavananda at their first meeting:

“Embracing him, the Lord said, ‘You are Pandu, and your sons are the five Pandavas: Ramananda Raya, Gopinatha
Pattanayaka, Kalanidhi, Sudhanidhi and Vaninatha Nayaka. These five sons of yours are dear to Me, and between Ramananda
and Me there is a difference of body alone.’” (CC, Adi 10.132–134)[^1] <!-- F1 F2 F27 Q1 F10 F12 Q2 -->

The Bengali verse says it in the plainest words: “You are Pandu.” This is no tale of rebirth in the worldly sense: the
Lord Himself revealed the eternal nature of His associate. He who in the pastimes of Krishna was Pandu, the father of
the five Pandavas, in the pastimes of Gaura became Raya Bhavananda and came with his sons to serve the Lord. Kaviraja
describes this meeting in detail in the Madhya-lila, and there the Lord names his wife too as Kunti. On this word of the
Lord rests the “Gaura-ganoddesha-dipika” as well. It has no verse of its own on Bhavananda: he is mentioned only where
Karnapura discusses the nature of Ramananda and disagrees with those who see Lalita in him:

“Some call him Lalita, but this is accepted by only a few: for Gaura said to Bhavananda: ‘You are Prithapati.’”
(GGD 122)

Prithapati is “the husband of Pritha,” that is, of Kunti: it is Pandu. If the father is Pandu, the son is Arjuna, the
Pandava; thus the Lord's word to the father became for Karnapura the key to the nature of the son (more on this in the
article on Ramananda Raya). <!-- F15 F16 Q11 -->

## Family and Village
Bhavananda belonged to the karanas, an Oriya community of officials similar to the kayasthas of Bengal; his family
itself is called “shaukra-karana.” Oriya society counts the karanas among the shudras, and Srila Bhaktisiddhanta
Sarasvati says so plainly. But a Vaishnava stands above every varna: not for nothing did the Lord make Bhavananda's son
Ramananda, born in this community, a teacher of sannyasis and panditas. <!-- F3 -->

Bhavananda lived in Orissa, by the ancient holy place of Alalanatha, or Brahmagiri, six kroshas — about twelve miles —
from Puri[^2]. More precisely, his house stood in the village of Bentpur, not far from Brahmagiri-Alalanatha, and he was
a landholder there. His descendants live in Bentpur to this day and bear the family title “Chaudhuri Pattanayaka.” When
Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati visited Alalanatha in the year 1337 of the Bengali era (about 1930), the local people
told him that Ramananda Raya had been born in this house in Bentpur and that the Lord used to come to Brahmagiri every
year; more on this tradition will be found in the article on Ramananda Raya. <!-- F4 F5 F6 -->

Near Bhavananda's house in Bentpur, according to one of the biographies, Madhavi-devi, the sister of Shikhi Mahiti,
began the service of the Deity Sri Gopinatha, and He is served there to this day. The same biography calls Shikhi
Mahiti the son of Bhavananda Raya's brother, which would make Madhavi-devi his niece too[^3]. <!-- F7 F8 Q13 -->

The sources have not preserved the name of Bhavananda's wife. We know only what the Lord called her: “Your wife is
Kunti” (CC, Madhya 10.53). They had five sons: Ramananda Raya, Gopinatha Pattanayaka, Kalanidhi, Sudhanidhi and
Vaninatha Pattanayaka. Ramananda was the firstborn (CC, Madhya 10.50); Gopinatha, by the order of the names in the
“Charitamrita,” the biographies consider the second. And all five were dear to Mahaprabhu — He said so to their father
Himself. <!-- F9 F10 F11 F12 -->

What Bhavananda himself was like, his son told best. In the prologue to his drama “Jagannatha-vallabha,” Ramananda Raya,
through the stage-director, calls himself “son of Sri Bhavananda Raya, an ocean of jewel-virtues such as serenity”; the
father's counsels, “steeped in the many maxims of policy laid down by the preceptor of the gods,” Brihaspati, made the
king upright and virtuous. The Sanskrit here is partly corrupt, and the compound can also be read otherwise: then it is
Bhavananda himself who is called “lord of the earth,” a ruler. Relying on this prologue, the biographies say that
Bhavananda was versed in all branches of learning and endowed with every virtue. <!-- F13 Q12 -->

The Utkal poet Govinda-deva in his poem “Sri Gaura-krishnodaya” (1758) portrays him thus: “the learned Bhavananda Raya,
lord of Vidyapura… he who had gladly laid the burden of the kingdom on his firstborn Ramananda and himself lives at
home, knowing not the slightest hardship” (11.6)[^4]. Later hymns of praise remember him too: the “Vaishnava-vandana” of
Devakinandana says, “I praise Sri Pradyumna Mishra and Raya Bhavananda, I praise Kalanidhi, Sudhanidhi and Gopinatha”
(v. 66) — though scholars hold that this couplet was added later, from the “Charitamrita.” And a commentary on the
“Chaitanya-bhagavata” (Adi 2.30) names Bhavananda Raya and his sons headed by Ramananda among the devotees born in
Utkala; in the “Bhagavata” itself his name does not occur. <!-- F14 F49 F50 -->

## The First Meeting
When Mahaprabhu returned to Puri from His long journey through the south, His devotees hastened to see His feet. In
the brief summary of those days Kaviraja says that the Lord then met both Shikhi Mahiti and Bhavananda Raya (CC, Madhya
1.130), and he tells of this meeting in detail in the tenth chapter of the Madhya-lila:

“At that time Bhavananda Raya came with his four sons, and all of them fell at the feet of Sri Chaitanya.” (CC, Madhya
10.49)

The fifth son, Ramananda, was still on the Godavari. Sarvabhauma Bhattacharya, introducing the devotees of Nilachala to
the Lord, said: “This is Bhavananda Raya. Ramananda Raya is his eldest son” (CC, Madhya 10.50). Mahaprabhu embraced
Bhavananda and began to glorify his son:

“He whose son is a jewel like Ramananda — his glory cannot be told in this world. You are Pandu himself, your wife is
Kunti, and your five great-minded sons are the five Pandavas.” (CC, Madhya 10.52–53) <!-- F19 Q4 F20 F21 Q3 -->

Kavi Karnapura shows the same scene in the eighth act of his drama “Chaitanya-chandrodaya.” Sarvabhauma names to the
Lord, one after another, the servants of Jagannatha and the devotees of Puri, and, coming to Ramananda's family, says:
“These are the brothers of Ramananda Raya, and among them — here is Vaninatha Pattanayaka. And this is their father,
Bhavananda Raya.” They all fall prostrate, and Sarvabhauma asks the Lord to accept them as His own. <!-- F28 -->

Bhavananda's reply is a model of Vaishnava humility:

“Bhavananda Raya said: ‘I am a shudra, a worldly man, lowly. Yet You have touched me — this is the mark of the Supreme
Lord.’” (CC, Madhya 10.54)

These are the words not of a worldly man but of a devotee whom the Lord had just called Pandu. He was a shudra only by
social reckoning, and he called himself “worldly” out of humility — the dainya that adorns every Vaishnava. And in the
Lord's touching a “shudra” he sees no breach of propriety, but a sign of His divinity: only the Lord Himself could act
so. <!-- F22 -->

And Bhavananda at once gave the Lord all that he had:

“Together with my house, wealth, servants and five sons, I have surrendered myself at Your feet. This Vaninatha will
remain at Your feet and carry out whatever order You give, whenever You give it. Regard me as Your own, have no
hesitation with me, and give whatever order You wish, whenever You wish.” (CC, Madhya 10.55–57)[^5]

And the Lord replied:

“What hesitation? You are no stranger to Me. Birth after birth you, with your whole family, are My servant.” (CC,
Madhya 10.58) <!-- F23 F24 F25 -->

Having said this, Mahaprabhu embraced Bhavananda again, placed His feet on the heads of his sons and promised that in a
few days Ramananda too would come. Vaninatha He kept with Him as His personal servant, and Bhavananda with the other
sons He sent home (CC, Madhya 10.59–61). So in one short meeting the whole life of Bhavananda was revealed: he
surrendered to the Lord with all his house and family, and heard in reply that he and his family are the Lord's
servants birth after birth. <!-- F26 I3 -->

The poets too remember that day in their own way. Kavi Karnapura in the “Chaitanya-charita-mahakavya” writes that the
Lord was seen by “a noble man of pure mind, virtuous and righteous, named Bhavananda, adorned with streams of joy”;
the Lord embraced him firmly with both arms and spoke sweet words to him: “O, you are fortunate like Pandu!”[^6] His five
sons — Ramananda and the others — stayed near the Lord and, serving Him, became very dear to Him, and the gentle,
large-hearted Vaninatha, “the unfailing friend of all people,” served Him (13.133–136). <!-- F17 -->

Govinda-deva in the “Gaura-krishnodaya” gives the Lord's words thus: “You are Pandu himself with five sons; and you take
shelter of Sri Krishna, who is beyond the world — therefore you rightly bear your name” (11.7). Bhavananda answers:
“This servant is sold for a drop of Your boundless mercy,” asks leave to return to his land, promising that his eldest
son will come in his place, and leaves with the Lord Vaninatha, “the best of counsellors.” After a few days in Puri he
sees Jagannatha, honours His prasada and departs for his city (11.8–10). <!-- F18 -->

## The Sons with the Lord
The word given that day Bhavananda kept. Vaninatha remained with Mahaprabhu and served Him in Nilachala, and once King
Prataparudra himself said of him: “Vaninatha, Bhavananda's son, has gone to bring maha-prasada from the temple of
Jagannatha” (CC, Madhya 11.108). Ramananda became the Lord's intimate associate (he has an article of his own). Of the
other brothers the sources say less, but the Lord regarded all five as His own. <!-- F47 F12 -->

Among those who settled with the Lord in Puri, some editions of the “Charitamrita” also name a certain “Bhavananda”
(Madhya 1.253); whether this is Bhavananda Raya the biographies do not say, and he himself, as we have seen, lived in
his own village[^7]. His family remained connected with Puri even after the Lord: Ramadasa Vishvasa, having settled
there, taught the “Kavya-prakasha” to the Pattanayaka family (CC, Antya 13.111) — our translation explains: “the
descendants of Bhavananda Raya.” <!-- F46 Q5 F48 -->

## Gopinatha on the Changa
The words “with your whole family you are My servant” Bhavananda was to see proved in deed, when trouble came to his
second son. Gopinatha Pattanayaka served the king as collector of revenue in the district of Malajathya-Dandapata (now
Medinipur, according to the biographies) and fell into arrears to the treasury of two lakhs of kahanas — counted in
cowrie shells, more than two hundred and fifty million. The king's eldest son, Prince Purushottama Jana, who was called
Bara-Jana, angered by Gopinatha, ordered him raised onto the changa — a scaffold for execution under which naked swords
were set, so that the condemned could be thrown down onto them (CC, Antya 9.13–30, 9.99). People, hoping for the Lord's
mercy, ran to Him: <!-- F29 F30 -->

“Swords have been laid out below, and they will throw him down onto them from above. Only if the Lord protects him
will he be saved. Bhavananda Raya with his whole family is Your servant; it befits You to protect his son, Your
servant.” (CC, Antya 9.14–15) <!-- F31 -->

But Mahaprabhu, setting the example of a sannyasi's dharma, remained outwardly unconcerned. One who collects revenue in
the king's name, He said, must first give the king his due; the king has the right to take what is his — and what can
a monk who has renounced the world do? (CC, Antya 9.31–35). Then fresh news came: Vaninatha and others had been taken
into custody with the whole family[^8]. Svarupa Damodara and the other devotees fell at the Lord's feet: “The whole
family of Ramananda Raya are Your servants. Such indifference does not become You” (CC, Antya 9.37). The Lord answered
again that He was a beggar and could do nothing, and showed them the only way: <!-- F32 F34 -->

“So if all of you wish to save him, go together to the feet of Jagannatha. Jagannatha is the Lord, in whose hand all
things rest; He is able to do, not to do, and to do otherwise.” (CC, Antya 9.43–44) <!-- F33 -->

This “unconcern” is not indifference to the devotees, but a pastime and a lesson. A sannyasi who has renounced the
world does not meddle in worldly affairs and does not petition kings; and to householders in a ruler's service the
Lord gives an instruction: not to spend what belongs to the king on themselves. His mercy, however, works quietly and
without His visible part. At that very time Harichandana Patra went to the king and told him everything, and the king
replied: <!-- I2 -->

“I knew nothing of this. I have no need to take his life; I want only my money back. Go there and settle it, so that
he soon repays the debt. But I do not wish his death.” (CC, Antya 9.49–50)

Harichandana conveyed the king's will to Bara-Jana, and Gopinatha was at once taken down from the changa (CC, Antya
9.51). The biographies add that Harichandana was moved to this by the Lord Himself, who dwells in everyone's heart as
the Supersoul. <!-- F35 -->

## “I Shall Go to Alalanatha”
Soon Kashi Mishra came to the Lord, and Mahaprabhu, as if troubled, said to him:

“I cannot stay here; I shall go to Alalanatha. There are many disturbances here, and I find no peace.” (CC, Antya
9.60)

The family of Bhavananda Raya, the Lord said, manages the king's affairs and spends the king's wealth in various ways,
and when the king demands his due, they come to Him; four times people had run to Him with the news that Gopinatha had
been raised onto the changa (CC, Antya 9.61–63):

“I am a mendicant sannyasi who lives in solitude. They come and give Me pain by telling Me their own pains.” (CC,
Antya 9.64) <!-- F36 -->

The Lord speaks these words in the role of a sannyasi, for whom it is not fitting to hear of worldly matters; they
teach detachment, but they do not denounce Bhavananda's family. Kashi Mishra long urged Him to stay: “Please remain here
in Jagannatha Puri. Why go to Alalanatha? No one will trouble You any more with talk of worldly matters” (CC, Antya
9.78). And at noon, when King Prataparudra came as usual to Kashi Mishra's house, Mishra told him that the Lord meant to
leave Puri and go to Alalanatha (CC, Antya 9.80–85). <!-- F37 F38 -->

The king grew sad and at once resolved to forgive Gopinatha the whole debt. Kashi Mishra objected that the Lord would be
displeased by this: He had not asked for it. But the king replied:

“I will remit the debt in any case — but do not tell Him that; let Him know that they are naturally dear to me.
Bhavananda Raya is worthy of my reverence, and his sons are naturally dear to me.” (CC, Antya 9.102–103) <!-- F39 Q6 -->

He remitted the debt, doubled Gopinatha's salary, restored him to his former post and put a silk cloth on him (CC,
Antya 9.104–107). And to Kashi Mishra, so that the Lord should not think the debt had been forgiven only for His sake,
the king sent word:

“All the sons of Bhavananda are dearest to me; I regard them as my own self. Therefore, wherever I give them charge,
they eat, drink, plunder and give away — and I do not call them to account.” (CC, Antya 9.120–121)

This is the speech not of a judge about grasping officials, but of a sovereign about his own kin. The king went on:
Ramananda he himself had made governor of Rajamahendri; he had not known that his son meant to destroy Gopinatha, for he
regards all the sons of Bhavananda Raya as his own family, and for this love is ready to forgive them any debt (CC,
Antya 9.122–126). So the king's love for Bhavananda's family became known to all. <!-- F40 Q7 -->

Kaviraja stresses that the Lord did not wish Gopinatha's debt to be forgiven, his salary doubled and his post restored;
He had only been asked to save Gopinatha from death — and that alone bore such fruit. The Lord's intentions are
inconceivable even to Brahma and Shiva (CC, Antya 9.112–115).

“He did not entreat Kashi Mishra, nor did He entreat the king; yet without any effort He gave all these results.” (CC,
Antya 9.150)

The Lord punished no one: the execution was the scheme of an angry prince and had been cancelled even before Kashi
Mishra came, and the whole affair ended not in punishment but in forgiveness of the debt and honours. <!-- F43 -->

## Bhavananda's Gratitude
When Kashi Mishra had conveyed the king's words to the Lord, Bhavananda Raya came to Mahaprabhu. Together with his five
sons he bowed at His feet, and the Lord raised him up and embraced him; thus, says Kaviraja, Ramananda Raya and all his
family met the Lord (CC, Antya 9.127–129). And Bhavananda said: <!-- F41 -->

“All these, my whole family, are Your servants. By keeping us safe in this danger, O Lord, You have taken us once more
as Your own.” (CC, Antya 9.130)

Now You have shown Your love for Your devotees, he said, as long ago You saved the five Pandavas from danger (CC, Antya
9.131). The father of the Pandavas recognized in Gaura's mercy the same care with which Krishna had once surrounded his
sons. <!-- F42 I3 -->

Then Gopinatha bowed at the Lord's feet — with the silk cloth on his head, the king's gift[^9]:

“The arrears have been remitted and my salary doubled; giving me my charge again, the king has put the silk cloth on
me. <…> On the changa I meditated on Your feet, and by the power of remembering Your feet I obtained this result.”
(CC, Antya 9.133, 135) <!-- F43 F44 Q8 -->

But Gopinatha understood that worldly gains are only a shadow of the true good that remembrance of the Lord's feet
bestows, and he prayed for more:

“Ramananda Raya and Vaninatha You have made free from the worldly; that mercy, which would make me so, I do not have.
Give me pure mercy, O Lord, and take the worldly away from me.” (CC, Antya 9.138–139)

This is the meaning of the whole story: the chief fruit of remembering the Lord is love for Him, while worldly
prosperity is a by-product, for everything worldly is impermanent. <!-- Q9 I1 -->

The Lord answered Gopinatha in words that embraced the whole of Bhavananda's family:

“Whether you engage in great affairs or remain detached and indifferent, birth after birth you five are My own
servants. But keep one order of Mine: spend nothing of the king's capital. Whatever profit comes after the king's
capital is paid — spend that on various religious and worldly duties.” (CC, Antya 9.141–143)

And He forbade them to spend money on wrongdoing, for that ruins both this life and the next (CC, Antya 9.144). On
returning home, all the family of Bhavananda Raya praised the Lord's extraordinary mercy, and people marvelled at the
love He had shown this family — for when He had been asked for help, He had said that He could do nothing (CC, Antya
9.145–148). <!-- F45 Q10 -->

## Days of Remembrance
Neither the day, nor the year, nor the place of Bhavananda Raya's appearance and departure is named by the biographies
or by the primary sources; nor are his days found in our calendar. <!-- F51 -->

## Significance
Bhavananda Raya has remained in the memory of Vaishnavas above all through the Lord's word: “You are Pandu.” The father
of the five Pandavas in Krishna's pastimes, he came into Gaura's pastimes together with his sons, and Mahaprabhu,
recognizing him at first sight, made him a branch of the tree of Chaitanya and his sons His eternal servants.
<!-- F15 F27 -->

His life is one of self-surrender. A noble landholder, father of a governor and of royal officials, he called himself a
fallen shudra and gave to the Lord's feet himself, his house, his wealth, his servants and his sons — and one of them
for good. In reply he heard that he was no stranger to the Lord and, with his whole family, served Him birth after
birth. <!-- F25 I3 -->

And when trouble came to his son, that word was fulfilled. The Lord, keeping the dharma of a sannyasi, did not petition
the king or intervene in worldly affairs — yet His mercy, working through the prayer to Jagannatha, through Harichandana
and through the king's love, turned the threat of execution into forgiveness and honour. Bhavananda saw in this what
Pandu had once seen: the Lord does not abandon those who have surrendered to Him. And his son Gopinatha showed why one
should remember the Lord: not for worldly gains, but for love of His feet. <!-- I1 I2 -->
