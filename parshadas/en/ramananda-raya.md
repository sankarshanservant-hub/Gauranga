# Ramananda Raya
*Ramananda Raya · @ramananda-raya*

## “Different Only in Body”
When Sri Chaitanya, back from His pilgrimage through the south, first received Bhavananda Raya in Nilachala, He
embraced him and spoke words that Krishnadasa Kaviraja Gosvami preserved in his list of the branches of the Chaitanya
tree:

“Embracing Bhavananda Raya, Sri Chaitanya said: ‘Formerly you were King Pandu, and your five sons are the Pandavas.
<…> Your five sons are indescribably dear to Me. But Ramananda Raya is so dear to Me that I feel Myself one with him;
we differ only in our bodies.’” (CC, Adi 10.132, 134)

Kaviraja names Bhavananda’s five sons one by one: Ramananda Raya, the eldest, then Gopinatha Pattanayaka, Kalanidhi,
Sudhanidhi and Vaninatha (Nayaka) (CC, Adi 10.133). Of them it was Ramananda who was destined to become the one
through whom the Lord revealed to the world the hidden science of rasa. Statesman and governor, poet and pandit,
skilled in everything, he was also the best of the mahabhagavatas — those who belong wholly to the Lord. <!-- F5 F6 Q1 F8 -->

## The House of Bhavananda
Six kroshas (about twelve miles) west of Puri lies the ancient holy place of Brahmagiri, or Alalanatha. It is said
that already in Satya-yuga Brahma performed bhajana here. Later the southern Alvars — the divyasuris — came and
installed the four-armed Deity of Janardana, whom they served according to the Pancharatra. The very name
“Alalanatha” is a corruption of “Alvar-natha”, “Lord of the Alvars”; to this day the villages around are called
Alvarpattanam, Alarapur and similar names. <!-- F1 F111 -->

Not far from Alalanatha lived a landholder, Bhavananda Raya, a great bhagavata. He belonged to the Oriya service
community of the karanas, akin to the kayasthas of Bengal (his own lineage is called “shaukra-karana”), and lived at
Brahmagiri; his second son was Gopinatha. Oriya society counts the karanas among the shudras, and so Ramananda too
was taken for a shudra. But Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati, retelling in his “Anubhashya” the history of the family
written by Ramananda’s descendant Manohara Raya, remarks that though born in this community, Ramananda was in truth
a brahmana, and as a Vaishnava paramahamsa, the guru of brahmanas. <!-- F2 F108 F109 F110 I1 -->

Of the place of his appearance only a local tradition survives. In the Bengali year 1337 (about 1930), when Srila
Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati visited Alalanatha with his disciples, the local people told him that Ramananda Raya had
been born in Bhavananda’s house in the village of Bentpur, a mile from Alalanatha. Bhavananda’s descendants, bearing
the family title “Chaudhuri Pattanayaka”, live there still. According to the same people, Sri Chaitanya came to
Brahmagiri every year to see Alalanatha and the place where Ramananda had appeared. Neither the day nor the year of
his appearance is given in the biographies. <!-- F3 F4 -->

## Governor on the Godavari
The independent king of Orissa at that time was Gajapati Prataparudra. Ramananda served him as trusted governor of
the southern lands — East and West Godavari, with their capital at Vidyanagara; later he became one of the king’s
ministers. His brothers too were in the king’s service. Gopinatha Pattanayaka once squandered part of the treasury
and, having roused the prince’s anger, was already raised onto the scaffold for execution; but Prataparudra,
remembering how close Gopinatha was to Mahaprabhu and to Ramananda, pardoned him — of which more below. Another
brother, Vaninatha Pattanayaka, later served the Lord in Puri in many ways, among them by bringing Him
maha-prasada. <!-- F7 F112 F9 F10 -->

Prataparudra was a mighty commander, yet a humble and generous Vaishnava and a patron of learning; so he appears in
Ramananda’s own drama as well. Ramananda, Kashi Mishra and Sarvabhauma loved their king. Of Ramananda’s spiritual
lineage only one testimony has come down: the “Bhajana-nirnaya” calls him a disciple of Raghavendra Puri, a disciple
of Madhavendra Puri. <!-- F138 F113 -->

## Friends in Nilachala
In Puri Ramananda had two special friends, bound to him by eternal friendship — both from Navadvipa, both having left
their former ashrama to live by Jagannatha as kshetra-sannyasis. The first was Vasudeva Sarvabhauma, son of Maheshvara
Visharada of the Vidyanagara quarter of Navadvipa. The second was Purushottama Acharya, likewise once a resident of
Navadvipa and later known as Svarupa Damodara; with him, it is said, Ramananda was “one soul”. <!-- F11 F12 F13 -->

Sarvabhauma did not understand his friend at first. Himself a smarta brahmana who had taken renunciation at the holy
place, he saw in Ramananda only a worldly shudra of the karana caste, did not grasp the meaning of his words and deeds,
and mocked him. But when by the Lord’s mercy Sarvabhauma himself came to know pure bhakti, Ramananda’s greatness too
was revealed to him. Even so learned a man failed, without mercy, to recognise either the Lord or His devotee: the
greatness of the Lord and of those who are His is known only by their mercy. <!-- F14 I4 -->

Mahaprabhu took sannyasa in the month of Magha, came to Puri in Phalguna, delivered Sarvabhauma after the Dola-yatra
in Chaitra, and in Vaishakha set out for the south; at Nityananda’s insistence He took a servant, Krishnadasa, with Him.
At parting Sarvabhauma gave Him four kaupinas and outer cloths and earnestly begged:

“In the town of Vidyanagara on the bank of the Godavari lives a man worthy in every respect, named Ramananda Raya.
Please, be sure to meet him, and do not reject him because by birth he is a shudra. He is the most fit of all to be
Your associate, for in the whole world there is no rasika devotee like him. He is at once an eminent pandit and the
greatest knower of bhakti-rasa. If You meet him, You will surely be convinced of it. When I came to know him, I could
not appreciate the extraordinary spiritual depth of his words and deeds, and I mocked him simply because he was a
Vaishnava. By Your mercy I can now understand the truth of what he spoke. Speak with him, and You will understand his
greatness.” (CC, Madhya 7.62–67)

Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati explains on these verses that outwardly Ramananda was not a sannyasi, but he was
learned, renounced in substance, a perfect man; and Sarvabhauma, having become a Vaishnava, recognised in him a true
rasika devotee, worthy of every honour. <!-- F15 F114 F115 Q2 -->

## The Meeting on the Godavari
Passing Kurmasthana, where He healed the leper Vasudeva, and Simhachalam, where He danced before Jiyada-Nrisimha, the
Lord on the following day came to the Godavari in ecstasy, dancing and singing.[^1] The river reminded Him of the
Yamuna, and the forest on its bank of Vrindavana, and He danced there for a while. Having crossed over, He bathed at
Goshpada-tirtha, by present-day Kovvur on the western bank, and sat down to sing the names of Krishna, hoping to meet
Ramananda. Just then, to the sound of music and with a splendid procession, a royal dignitary arrived at the river in
a palanquin, and with him many Vedic brahmanas who had come to bathe and offer tarpana. The Lord understood that
this was Ramananda Raya himself — the governor of Rajamahendri.[^2] <!-- F16 F17 F18 F19 F116 -->

Ramananda stepped down from the palanquin. Seeing an extraordinary sannyasi — radiant as the sun, in saffron cloth, of
mighty build, with eyes like lotuses — he ran up and prostrated himself before Him. The Lord, outwardly restraining
Himself, raised him and asked, “Are you Ramananda Raya?” “I am a wretched shudra, a servant of Your servants,” he
humbly replied. Then the Lord embraced him tightly, and both, losing outer consciousness, fell to the ground. “Sacred
mutual love awoke in them. As soon as they embraced, they fell to the ground” (CC, Madhya 8.23). In both appeared all
eight spiritual transformations, and from their lips “Krishna” broke out in a faltering voice. The brahmanas were
bewildered and stood as if painted:

“In this sannyasi we see the effulgence of Brahman. So why does He weep, embracing a shudra? Ramananda Raya is the wise
governor of Madras and a great pandit. But, having touched this sannyasi, he has lost his composure and begun to
behave like a madman.” (CC, Madhya 8.26–27) <!-- F20 F21 Q34 -->

Seeing outsiders who knew nothing of bhakti, the Lord checked His feelings and said that He had come at
Sarvabhauma’s request. Ramananda called himself a royal servant, a worldly man, the lowest of shudras and an
untouchable, and glorified the Lord as One who, having no purpose of His own, comes to deliver the fallen:

“You granted Sarvabhauma such mercy only because of his love for You. And that You have touched me, an untouchable,
confirms it. You are the Supreme Lord Narayana Himself, while I am only an ordinary official, sunk in worldliness, the
lowest of shudras. Unafraid of the injunctions of the Vedas, without a trace of aversion You have touched me.” (CC,
Madhya 8.34–36) <!-- F22 F23 F117 -->

Meanwhile the hearts of the brahmanas themselves — men of ritual who had never loved bhakti — melted at the sight of
the Lord and His acts: “Krishna” and “Hari” sounded on their lips, and signs of love appeared on their bodies. Ramananda
said that this was possible only by the power of Bhagavan Himself, manifest before their eyes: in form and in nature
alike the Lord is God. Seeing that He had been recognised, the Lord tried to hide behind humble words and began to
praise Ramananda:

“You are the best of the mahabhagavatas, souls wholly devoted to God,” the Lord said. “The hearts of all people simply
melt at the sight of you. What to speak of others, when I, a Mayavadi sannyasi, have plunged into an ocean of love for
Krishna simply by touching you.” (CC, Madhya 8.44–45) <!-- F24 F25 -->

Just then a Vaishnava brahmana invited the Lord to his home for bhiksha. The Lord expressed His wish to meet again, and
Ramananda pleaded:

“Though You have come to correct me, one meeting will not be enough to purify my heart. Please stay at least a week and
cleanse my polluted mind.” (CC, Madhya 8.51–52) <!-- Q3 -->

## Nights of Conversation
In the evening, when the Lord had taken bhiksha, bathed and was resting, Ramananda came to Him with a single servant.
So began the conversation that Krishnadasa Kaviraja relates in the eighth chapter of the Madhya-lila, which he opens
thus:

“Sri Chaitanya Mahaprabhu is like a boundless ocean of knowledge of loving service to God. He filled the heart of
Ramananda Raya with this nectar and then commanded him to become like a cloud and pour the rain of these life-giving
truths over that very ocean, thus filling it with the jewels of the highest conclusions.” (CC, Madhya 8.1)

The Lord asked and Ramananda answered — but he answered by a power that the Lord Himself had placed in him. So the
Lord became the questioner and made His devotee the teacher, in order to preach dharma through him. <!-- F26 F118 Q40 I6 -->

“Recite a shloka about the goal of life,” the Lord asked. Ramananda began with varnashrama-dharma. “This is external;
tell Me further,” the Lord replied. Then Ramananda named the offering of the fruits of one’s acts to God, then the
giving up of duties for the sake of devotion, then bhakti mixed with knowledge — and each time he heard: “This too is
external.” Only when he named pure bhakti, free from speculative knowledge, did the Lord accept it as the true goal.
The order shows that everything outside the Vedic norms is rejected at once, yet even the stages of varnashrama and
the offering of fruits are only external; pure bhakti begins with hearing of Krishna from the lips of a pure devotee,
and “freedom from knowledge” rejects impersonal knowledge, not knowledge of one’s relationship with God. <!-- F27 I5 -->

The Lord asked him to go deeper still, and Ramananda said:

“The essence of all perfection is prema-bhakti, unalloyed nectarean devotion to God.” (CC, Madhya 8.68)

In support he cited a verse of his own composition, later included by Sri Rupa in the “Padyavali”:

“The feeling of hunger and thirst turns eating and drinking into a great pleasure. So too any activity performed as
loving worship of the Lord fills the devotee’s heart with bliss.” (CC, Madhya 8.69)

It is followed by another verse of the “Padyavali” — “krishna-bhakti-rasa-bhavita matih…”, which in some places is also
ascribed to Ramananda:

“The sweetness of bhakti, in which one’s individual relationship with Krishna unfolds, cannot be attained even by
performing pious deeds for millions of births in a row. Only one thing is needed — an ardent desire to obtain such
unalloyed devotion without delay, wherever it may be found.” (CC, Madhya 8.70) <!-- Q25 Q41 F95 F96 -->

Then, step by step, Ramananda described love for Krishna — pure attachment (krishna-rati), servitude (dasya),
friendship (sakhya), parental love (vatsalya) and, as the essence of all goals, the love of lovers (kantabhava); above
it, the love of Sri Radhika; then the nature of Krishna, the nature of Radha, the nature of rasa and of prema. When
the Lord asked for more, Ramananda sang his own song about the highest feeling of separation — adhirudha-bhava — about
“prema-vilasa-vivarta”, the “reversal” of loving play; and the Lord acknowledged it as the limit of the goal (CC,
Madhya 8.193; on the song, see the section “Writings”). <!-- F28 F29 -->

Then the Lord asked about the means, and Ramananda replied that it was the Lord Himself who spoke through his lips and
the Lord Himself who listened. The only means of attaining the service of Radha and Krishna is to follow the sakhis of
Vraja: Radha is the creeper of love for Krishna, and the sakhis are its shoots, flowers and leaves; without following
the gopis, the service of the son of the king of Vraja cannot be attained. <!-- F30 -->

So night after night passed in talks of Krishna. At another time the Lord put short questions, and Ramananda answered
each with a single word of truth:

“‘Which glory is the best?’ ‘To be a devotee of Krishna — that is the highest glory.’ <…> ‘Of all sufferings, which is
the most painful?’ ‘I see no greater misfortune than separation from a devotee of Krishna.’ <…> ‘What brings the soul
the highest good?’ ‘Only the company of a devotee of Krishna brings the soul true good.’ <…> ‘Where should one live,
rejecting all else?’ ‘One should live in the land of Vraja, where the Rasa-lila took place.’” (CC, Madhya 8.245, 247,
250, 253)

And of those who seek liberation or worldly pleasure Ramananda said: not knowing their relationship with God, people
are like crows pecking at the bitter fruits of the nimba tree, while those who have come to know it are like cuckoos
tasting the nectarean buds of the mango (CC, Madhya 8.256–258). Ramananda humbly begged the Lord to stay at least ten
days. “What are ten days!” the Lord answered. “I will not part from you all My life: we shall live together in
Nilachala.” <!-- F31 F32 -->

## The Secret of the Golden Form
Several nights passed, and one day Ramananda saw the Lord differently. Before, a sannyasi had stood before him — now
he saw a dark form, and before it a golden figure whose radiance covered His whole body:

“You resemble a golden deity wrapped in golden radiance. I see a flute at Your lips, and Your lotus eyes are ever in
motion. This form of Yours fills me with wonder. O Lord, reveal to me its cause and hide nothing.” (CC, Madhya
8.268–270)

At first the Lord evaded him: a mahabhagavata, He said, sees his beloved Deity everywhere, and Ramananda simply sees
Radha and Krishna wherever he looks. But Ramananda would not yield:

“O Lord! Leave aside these philosophical words. Be merciful, do not hide Your true form from me. You have descended,
taking the heart and radiance of Radharani, to taste Your own sweetness. <…> You came to save me, and now You are
trying to deceive me. Why do You act so?” (CC, Madhya 8.277–278, 280) <!-- F33 -->

“Then, with a smile on His lips, Sri Chaitanya Mahaprabhu showed him His form as Krishna and Radharani united in one,
Rasaraja and Mahabhava, the abode of all spiritual rasas and the highest embodiment of prema. Seeing this, Ramananda
Raya lost consciousness in bliss and fell to the ground.” (CC, Madhya 8.281–282)

The Lord restored him to consciousness with the touch of His hand, embraced and comforted him. No one but Ramananda
had seen this form: to him, who knew all His pastimes and rasas, the Lord disclosed the hidden reasons why Krishna had
taken the feeling and radiance of Radha — and forbade him to reveal this secret:

“My body is not fair; it only seems so from contact with the body of Radha. But She touches no one but the son of Nanda
Maharaja. So My body and mind have been transformed by Her ecstatic feeling, and now I can taste My own sweetness.
<…> Keep all this secret and tell no one, for people will laugh, taking it all for madness. In truth we are both
madmen, and in this we are very alike.” (CC, Madhya 8.286–287, 289–290) <!-- F34 F119 -->

Ten nights the Lord spent with Ramananda on the Godavari. On leaving, He said to him:

“Give up worldly affairs and come to Jagannatha Puri. When I have finished My pilgrimage, I too shall return there.
We shall live together in Nilachala, delighting in talks of Krishna.” (CC, Madhya 8.296–297)

All these conversations were written down in his diary (kadacha) by Ramananda’s close friend Svarupa Damodara; it was
from this kadacha that Krishnadasa Kaviraja retold them in detail. <!-- F35 Q4 F36 -->

## The Meeting as Told by Murari Gupta and Lochana Dasa
The oldest biographies tell of this meeting differently. In Murari Gupta’s kadacha the Lord, singing of Hari, goes
one clear morning to the city of Kanchi to see Ramananda:

“Ramananda, in his own house, having finished his worship of Krishna, was meditating on the Supreme Brahman, the son
of the king of Vraja — and three times he beheld the great and wondrous sweetness of Gauranga, and was utterly
astonished. Opening his eyes, he saw that very form — the Supreme Brahman in the dress of a sannyasi. Bowing his head
to the ground and joining his palms, he asked, ‘Where have You come from, my Lord?’ The Lord laughed and said, ‘How
can you not remember, O bee at the lotus feet of Sri Radhika?’ And having thus made Himself known, Hari Himself
embraced him with both arms.” (Murari Gupta, “Sri Krishna-chaitanya-charitamrita”, 3.15.2–4)

Having revealed to him the wondrous secret of the pastimes in the forests of Vrindavana, the Lord bade him go quickly
to Kshetra (Puri), comforted him and went His way (ibid., 3.15.5). <!-- F82 Q31 -->

Lochana Dasa took this account from Murari. In his “Chaitanya-mangala” the Lord, after visiting Jiyada-Nrisimha, comes
to Kanchi-nagara, to the gates of the royal palace, and asks about the prince. The gatekeeper replies that the prince
is in the inner apartments, engaged in worship. That prince was Ramananda, Bhavananda’s son. He was chanting the
maha-mantra and meditating on Krishna, but instead of Shyamasundara he saw the unheard-of form of Gaurasundara; and
opening his eyes, he saw Him in person: unnoticed by any of the guards, Gaurasundara had entered his apartments and
given him darshana in his “temple of meditation”. <!-- F83 F84 -->

Astonished, Ramananda asked how a sannyasi had come in to him.

“The Lord says: ‘Why do you not know your own self? You do not recognise Me — and I have come to take you.’ <…> Hearing
this, the Lord laughed aloud; the Lord makes Himself known and reveals Himself. All that was there — dark, white, red —
everything appears as one golden form.” (“Chaitanya-mangala”, Shesha-khanda 104–106)

Then the Lord became dark, like a kanada flower, with a peacock feather on His head and a flute at His lips, in yellow
garments and a forest garland — and golden again, so that beasts, birds, trees and creepers glittered in the radiance
of His body. Understanding who stood before him, Ramananda, overcome with love, fell weeping at the feet of his Lord,
and the Lord, taking him by the hand, led him outside (ibid., 107–113). Elsewhere in the “Chaitanya-mangala” too
Ramananda is named among the eternal associates of the Lord.[^3] <!-- F85 Q23 F155 -->

## Return from the South and Arrival in Puri
Having visited many holy places of the south, the Lord returned to Vidyanagara along the seven branches of the
Godavari. Ramananda came to Him, and their talks began anew. The Lord told him of His pilgrimage and handed him two
manuscripts He had found in the south — the “Krishna-karnamrita” and the “Brahma-samhita”: “Here are the proofs of all
your conclusions about rasa.” Ramananda relished them together with the Lord and made copies of both. This time the
Lord stayed with him for about another week. Ramananda could not follow Him at once: he had to wait for the king’s
permission and set his affairs in order. He promised to come to Puri as soon as he could. <!-- F37 F119 -->

In Puri the Lord settled in the house of Kashi Mishra. There Bhavananda came to Him, and the Lord glorified his son
in his presence:

“Your son Ramananda is like the greatest of jewels. His greatness and glory cannot be described in this mortal world.
You are Maharaja Pandu himself, your wife is Kunti, and your five wise sons are manifestations of the five famous sons
of Pandu.” (CC, Madhya 10.52–53)

Bhavananda humbly called himself a lowly shudra sunk in worldliness, surrendered everything at the Lord’s feet and
gave Him his son Vaninatha for constant service. The Lord kept Vaninatha with Him and said that Ramananda would soon
arrive. <!-- F38 Q5 F39 -->

Meanwhile Ramananda, at the Lord’s bidding, had handed over his duties to a successor and petitioned the king for
retirement, so as to spend the rest of his life with the Lord. A few days later Prataparudra, with Ramananda and other
courtiers, came from his capital Kataka to Puri, and Ramananda hastened first of all to the Lord’s feet.[^4] He told
Him that he had conveyed His will to the king: that he should leave the royal service. The king had gladly agreed,
saying that no one is more fortunate than one who serves the feet of Chaitanya, and had promised to pay him his full
salary even after retirement. “The king will be blessed by serving such a mahabhagavata,” the Lord replied. <!-- F136 F40 F41 F120 -->

In Puri Ramananda met and honoured those gathered around the Lord — Nityananda, Paramananda Puri, Brahmananda
Bharati, Svarupa Damodara, Jagadananda, Mukunda and others. It was here, according to one of the biographies, that
Svarupa and Jagadananda came to know him. “Have you been to see Jagannatha?” the Lord asked him. “No,” Ramananda
replied. “The feet are only the chariot, and the heart is the charioteer: wherever the charioteer drives, there the
chariot goes.” Then at the Lord’s bidding he went to Jagannatha, and afterwards to his family. <!-- F42 F43 F155 -->

## Intercessor for the King
Prataparudra longed with all his heart to see the Lord, but the Lord, a sannyasi, would not agree to meet a king. When
Nityananda sent the king one of the Lord’s kaupinas for consolation, the king began earnestly to beg Ramananda to
intercede. Through him he sent word that he was ready to give up his life if he did not see the Lord. The minister,
skilled in affairs, again and again told the Lord of the king’s Vaishnava qualities and of his love for His feet — and
the Lord’s heart softened. <!-- F44 F121 F136 F137 -->

“What am I, a sannyasi, to do?” the Lord asked. Ramananda called Him the Master who stands above all injunctions and
prohibitions. The Lord replied that He was bound by rules, yet He could not refuse His friend outright, so close were
they:

“A pot may be full of milk, but a drop of wine can spoil it completely, and then no one wants it. No doubt the king
has many virtues, but the royal title spoils everything. Yet if you still wish this meeting to take place, then bring
his son to Me. The scriptures say, ‘The father manifests himself in the son’, and so, meeting the prince, I shall in
effect be meeting the king himself.” (CC, Madhya 12.53–56)

Ramananda brought the young prince to the Lord. The dark-complexioned boy awakened in the Lord the remembrance of
Krishna. And a few days later, in the garden of Balagandi, during the Ratha-yatra, Prataparudra, alone, in the dress
of a simple Vaishnava, reciting shlokas of the “Bhagavatam”, was blessed to massage the feet of the Lord, who was
absorbed in love for Krishna. <!-- F45 Q33 F46 -->

## Seeing the Lord off to Vrindavana
Of the first years the Lord spent in Nilachala after sannyasa, two went to the journey south and back, and for two
more He wished to go to Vrindavana but could not set out: Ramananda and Sarvabhauma, at the king’s special request, did
all they could to keep Him in Puri and put various obstacles in His way. At last they gave in. <!-- F47 F133 F134 -->

After Vijaya-dashami the Lord set out by way of Gauda. Ramananda came with Him to Kataka, where in a garden under a
bakula tree the Lord met Prataparudra; and then, when the Lord crossed the Chitrotpala, he went on after Him together
with Mardaraja and Hari-chandana. Only at Remuna did the Lord send him back.[^5] That parting cannot be described in
words:

“Unable to bear the pain of separation, Ramananda Raya lost consciousness and fell to the ground, and Sri Chaitanya
laid his head on His lap and wept. The Lord Himself could not bear separation from Ramananda Raya. I am simply unable
to describe such intensity of spiritual feeling.” (CC, Madhya 16.154–155) <!-- F135 F48 Q6 -->

That year the Lord did not reach Vrindavana: at Ramakeli He received Sanatana and Rupa and, returning through
Shantipura, where He instructed Raghunatha dasa, came back to Nilachala. Among those who met Him then were Kashi
Mishra, Ramananda, Pradyumna, Sarvabhauma, Vaninatha and Shikhi Mahiti (CC, Madhya 16.254). In the autumn the Lord again
wished to go to Vrindavana and secretly consulted Ramananda and Svarupa. He wanted to go alone, by night, through the
forests, but they persuaded Him to take companions and gave Him Balabhadra Bhattacharya and another brahmana. And when
the Lord, having visited Vrindavana, Prayaga and Kashi, returned through Jharikhanda, Ramananda came from Kataka to
see Him. <!-- F49 F143 F155 F50 -->

## Rupa and Sanatana
At Prayaga the Lord placed in Sri Rupa’s heart all that He Himself had once heard from Ramananda’s lips:

“There He revealed to Sri Rupa the highest truths about Krishna, teaching him the highest conclusions of the science of
bhakti and the rasas in full agreement with the ‘Srimad-Bhagavatam’. He told him all those confidential conclusions
that He had heard before from Ramananda Raya. By His mercy Sri Rupa was able to grasp them perfectly.” (CC, Madhya
19.115–116)

And later in Kashi, instructing Sanatana, the Lord repeated what had happened on the Godavari, only with the roles
changed: then the Lord had asked and Ramananda had answered by His power; now Sanatana asked by the Lord’s power, and
the Lord answered. Ramananda’s conversation and Sanatana’s questions alike draw the line between the means (sadhana)
and the goal (sadhya). <!-- F144 Q36 F145 F155 -->

When Rupa came to Puri and stayed with Haridasa Thakura, he was writing two dramas — the “Vidagdha-madhava” and the
“Lalita-madhava”; the greater part of the first was composed in the Lord’s presence. One day the Lord came to him
together with Svarupa, Ramananda and Sarvabhauma. When Svarupa read out Rupa’s verse “priyah so ’yam”, Ramananda and
Sarvabhauma told the Lord that without His mercy no one could have expressed His innermost feelings. After the verse
“tunde tandavini” Ramananda asked Rupa what book he was writing; Svarupa told him of the two dramas. Ramananda had him
recite the nandi-shloka and the verse of blessing “anarpita-charim chirat…”, then the shloka on his chosen Deity —
“in the company of Vaishnavas there is nothing to be shy of” — and questioned him on every part of both plays. Rupa
spoke humbly of Ramananda’s greatness. The Lord, feigning displeasure, said, “I am ashamed, and people will laugh,” but
Ramananda, unsparing in his praise, replied: “This is not poetry but a stream of nectar, with a drop of camphor in it.
People will only rejoice, for Rupa is simply remembering his Deity.” So they talked of dramaturgy, and Ramananda
repeated that such poetry was possible only by the Lord’s mercy. No wonder Rupa revered him so: according to one
interpretation, in Ramananda he honoured those very sakhis — Lalita and Vishakha — whom Rupa-manjari follows in the
eternal pastimes. <!-- F51 F122 F123 F124 -->

Then the Lord asked everyone to bless Rupa, and to Ramananda He spoke of Rupa’s elder brother:

“I ask all of you to bless Rupa Gosvami, so that he may ceaselessly describe these pastimes of Vrindavana, full of
divine rasas towards Krishna. His elder brother, Sanatana, is so wise and learned that there is no one to compare with
him. His detachment from all worldly things is as strong as yours, Ramananda.” (CC, Antya 1.199–201) <!-- F52 Q7 -->

So the Lord Himself declared equal the renunciation of two very different devotees. Sanatana slept each night under
a new tree and chewed dry cakes of chickpea flour; Ramananda, remaining outwardly a householder and a dignitary, was
the best teacher of yukta-vairagya — the renunciation in which everything is given to the service of Krishna. Both
nourished the rasa of the Lord’s separation. Ramananda’s renunciation, like Sanatana’s, is not a dry rejection of the
world but the summit of thirst for Krishna’s pastimes; in this Pundarika Vidyanidhi, Srila Bhaktivinoda Thakura and
Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati stand beside them. <!-- F53 F148 I2 -->

## Pradyumna Mishra and the Devadasis
To reveal Ramananda’s greatness to the world, the Lord performed a special pastime. One day the brahmana Pradyumna
Mishra from Shrihatta came to Him and asked Him to speak about Krishna. The Lord replied that He Himself heard about
Krishna from Ramananda and that only Ramananda knew such talks: let the Mishra go to him. The “Chaitanya-bhagavata”
names Pradyumna Mishra, Paramananda Mahapatra and Ramananda among the natives of Orissa who came to the Lord: Pradyumna
is “the embodiment of divine love”, while Paramananda and Ramananda are “two very great saints” (CB, Antya 3.184).
<!-- F56 F142 Q39 -->

The Mishra went to Ramananda’s house and learned from a servant that his host had taken two very beautiful young
devadasis — girls dedicated to the service of Jagannatha (in Orissa they are called “mahari”) — into a secluded garden
and was teaching them the songs and dance of his drama, which was staged in the temple for Jagannatha’s pleasure.
Krishnadasa Kaviraja explains who Ramananda was in this service:

“The touch of the young dancers in no way affected his mind and body, as if he were touching wood or stone. He felt
himself a maidservant of the gopis of Vraja and performed his service in natural spiritual feeling (regarding those
maidens as the cowherd girls of Vrindavana). The glory and greatness of the associates of Sri Chaitanya Mahaprabhu are
practically inconceivable. And in Ramananda Raya we find the highest limit of love and devotion to Krishna. <…> Every
day he taught them to dance. Can tiny conditioned souls understand the greatness of Ramananda Raya’s mind and heart?”
(CC, Antya 5.19–21, 26)

Ramananda — a soul satisfied in the self, higher than any renounced sage, victorious over every impulse of the senses —
saw no flesh in the girls. In his spiritual body (siddha-deha), in the mood of a gopi, he performed spontaneous,
ragatmika bhakti and served his mistress, the daughter of Vrishabhanu. He honoured the performers as the chief gopis,
the objects of his service, and himself as Radha’s maidservant, and served them without the slightest worldly
attraction: he himself bathed, dressed and adorned them, and did not only teach them songs and dance. <!-- F57 F125 F126 F58 Q10 -->

Hearing of the guest, Ramananda, having finished the lesson, came out to the reception room, honoured the Mishra and
asked forgiveness for the delay. But the Mishra, having waited long and heard much from the servant about his host’s
conduct, had lost faith in him at heart; seeing the late hour, he scarcely spoke and went home. When the Lord asked
him what he had heard, the Mishra told Him what he had seen and heard. In reply the Lord glorified Ramananda:

“Although Sri Ramananda Raya teaches these girls to express various spiritual emotions, his mind remains calm and
unmoved. And even touching young girls, his mind and body remain unmoved, as though they were made of wood or stone.
Truly, this is the wonder of wonders! Only Ramananda Raya can act so. Now it is clear to Me that his body only seems
material. In reality it is wholly spiritual.” (CC, Antya 5.40–42)

The Lord also cited the words of the “Bhagavatam”: whoever hears with faith of Krishna’s rasa-lila with the girls of
Vraja is freed from lust — the disease of the heart — and attains pure love (CC, Antya 5.45–48; Bhag. 10.33.39). And He
concluded:

“We understand that Sri Ramananda Raya has entered the path of spontaneous love for God, which transcends all rules
and regulations. And since his whole body is wholly spiritual, his mind too is beyond the influence of maya.” (CC,
Antya 5.51) <!-- F59 F60 Q11 -->

Ramananda is an eternally perfect associate of the Lord. By Krishna’s will he abides in a body that is only “almost
perfect”, awaiting the full attainment of his eternal form, and therefore in that transcendental body there is not a
shadow of worldly change. To judge such a Vaishnava by birth and outward conduct, by a worldly measure, is not to
understand him at all: the “Padma Purana” plainly warns against seeing in a Vaishnava his earthly caste. “Go to him
again,” the Lord told the Mishra, “and say that I have sent you. I Myself hear about Krishna from Ramananda.” <!-- F61 I3 -->

The Mishra came again and conveyed the Lord’s words. When Ramananda asked what he wished to hear, the Mishra asked him
to tell in order all that he had spoken to the Lord in Vidyanagara. Both were absorbed in talk of Krishna, and the
Mishra came to know Ramananda’s greatness. In the evening, having bathed and eaten, he returned to the Lord and said:

“No one can retell the talks with Ramananda Raya. He cannot be considered an ordinary man, for he is full of the
transcendental rasas of loving devotion to Krishna. Ramananda Raya also told me: ‘You should not think that it is I
who explain to you the pastimes and qualities of Krishna. Sri Chaitanya Himself speaks through me. I am only like a
musical instrument (a vina) — He draws from me whatever sounds He Himself desires.’” (CC, Antya 5.71–73) <!-- F62 F63 Q12 -->

The Lord answered with fresh praise:

“Know that Ramananda’s great humility has no equal. He often attributes his own words to others. Such is the inner
nature of great souls who constantly feel loving devotion to Krishna in their hearts — they will never glorify
themselves.” (CC, Antya 5.77–78)

“Though Ramananda Raya was a family man, he was not subject to the six vices (lust, anger, greed, bewilderment,
madness and envy). Though he outwardly continued to live in the world, he gave instruction to renounced monks.” (CC,
Antya 5.80) <!-- F64 Q13 -->

Why, then, did the Lord send the Mishra? Kaviraja answers:

“And so that Ramananda Raya might reveal his saintly qualities, Sri Chaitanya sent Pradyumna Mishra to him to hear
about Krishna. <…> Wishing to destroy the arrogance of proud theologians and monks, Lord Chaitanya sometimes teaches
dharma even through the low-born (shudras). Sri Chaitanya came to preach love and devotion to Krishna, as well as the
nature of the Absolute Truth. By His sweet will He made Ramananda Raya the speaker, while He Himself, together with
Pradyumna Mishra, became his humble listener.” (CC, Antya 5.81, 84–85) <!-- F65 Q14 -->

Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati explains that to the worldly eye Ramananda seemed a householder on the path of worldly
action, but he was a Vaishnava householder who had attained the transcendent, free from the six enemies; he was wholly
turned to Krishna and able to turn even renounced impersonalist sannyasis to bhakti. Through a “shudra” and a
“grihastha” the Lord instructed Pradyumna, a brahmana by birth, and Himself accepted the dharma that Ramananda
preached. Kaviraja himself nowhere calls Ramananda “low” in his own voice: Ramananda merely displayed the pastime of
birth in a humble family, to teach that anyone who has the highest love for Krishna is a guru of the world, whatever
his birth. Ramananda is the teacher of brahmanas and sannyasis, a guru even for the eternally perfect teachers of the
world. No wonder the “Bhakti-ratnakara” says that “through Ramananda” the Lord crushed the pride of Kandarpa, the god of
love (“Bhakti-ratnakara”, 1.630–631). <!-- F127 F66 I1 I6 F146 Q37 -->
