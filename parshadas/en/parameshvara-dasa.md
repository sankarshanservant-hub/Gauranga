# Parameshvari Dasa
*Парамешвари Дас · @parameshvara-dasa*

## The Friend Arjuna
Parameshvari Dasa Thakura is one of the twelve gopalas, the closest companions of Nityananda Prabhu. The primary
sources call him by several names: Krishnadasa Kaviraja calls him Parameshvara Dasa; Vrindavana Dasa Thakura, and after
him the “Bhakti-ratnakara” and the “Narottama-vilasa,” call him Parameshvari Dasa, Parameshvari, or Parameshvari Dasa
Thakura; Kavi Karnapura calls him “the servant Sri Parameshvara”; the vandanas, Parameshvara (Dasa) Thakura; and he
himself signed his songs “Parameshvari Dasa.” The biographies also call him Srila Parameshvari Thakura and Parameshvara
Thakura. <!-- F1 -->

Who he is in Krishna's eternal pastimes is told by the “Gaura-ganoddesha-dipika”:

“The former friend named Arjuna is Sri Parameshvara Dasa” (“Gaura-ganoddesha-dipika” 132)

Arjuna is a cowherd friend (*sakha*) of Krishna in Vraja; so too Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati understands this verse
in the “Anubhashya”: one of the twelve gopalas, the friend Arjuna. The same verse names Kala Krishnadasa as Lavanga.[^1]
<!-- F2 Q10 F4 -->

The twelve gopalas are friends from Vraja who came with Nityananda into His pastimes; in the royal assembly of the
Vaishnavas of the world they hold the seats of chief ministers (*patra-raja*). They are Nityananda's principal
companions. In the lists of the gopalas Parameshvari Dasa stands now fifth, now seventh; a subheading in the edition of
the “Anubhashya” to the “Charitamrita” calls him “gopala five.” Lochana Dasa, in his “Chaitanya-mangala,” having listed
the twelve gopalas, at once names him too among the companions who descended with the Lord:

“Parameshvara Dasa and Vrindavana Dasa, Kashishvara, Srila Rupa and Sanatana appeared” (“Chaitanya-mangala,” Sutra
8.628) <!-- F3 F5 -->

He was born into a family of vaidyas, physicians. Of his family the sources say nothing more: neither his parents'
names, nor his native village, nor the day and year of his appearance. <!-- F8 -->

## “Nityananda Is His Life”
Krishnadasa Kaviraja, counting the branches of Nityananda's tree, speaks of him in a single verse:

“Parameshvara dasa took shelter in Nityananda alone; whoever remembers him attains devotion to Krishna” (CC, Adi
11.29)

Such is his glory: Nityananda is his only shelter, and therefore the very remembrance of him bestows devotion to
Krishna. <!-- F6 Q1 I1 -->

Vrindavana Dasa, listing the servants of Nityananda — cowherd boys and girls of Nanda's circle whose former names he
does not disclose — says of Parameshvari Dasa:

“Nityananda was the very life of Parameshvari Dasa; in his body Nityananda enjoys His pastimes” (CB, Antya 5.732)

He is among the chief associates through whom Nityananda's pastimes unfold; he came to enrich those pastimes, and
Nityananda regarded him as His own life. He is not an agent apart from the Lord but His instrument: in him Nityananda
Himself plays. The “First Vandana” repeats the same list, and Devakinandana in the “Vaishnavabhidhana” names “Sri
Parameshvara” among the companions of Nityananda. <!-- F7 Q2 I4 F27 -->

## With the Lord in Panihati
When Mahaprabhu set out from Nilachala to go to Vrindavana by way of Gauda, He stayed at Raghava Pandita's home
in Panihati. There, by the Lord's wish, great Vaishnavas gathered to Him — Gadadhara Dasa, Raghunatha Vaidya, and
others; Purandara Pandita and Parameshvari Dasa hurried there too:

“At that moment Purandara Pandita and Parameshvari Dasa, in whose Deity Gaurachandra Himself is manifest, came running.
Seeing the Lord, the two of them wept in the bliss of divine love” (CB, Antya 5.95–96)

Srila Bhaktisiddhanta explains in the “Gaudiya-bhashya”: Sriman Mahaprabhu became manifest in the Deity of Gauranga
whom Parameshvari Dasa worshipped in the village of Tada-Atpur; he began the service of Sri Gaurasundara. So too the
biographies understand the verse.[^2] <!-- F19 F20 Q3 -->

## With Nityananda to Gauda
After returning from Kanai-natashala to Nilachala, Mahaprabhu told Nityananda to go to Gauda and deliver the lowly, the ignorant, and the
fallen by giving them love of God. Nityananda set out at once with His companions:

“With Nityananda went Ramadasa, Gadadhara Dasa, Raghunatha Vaidya, full of the sweetness of loving devotion,
Krishnadasa Pandita, Parameshvari Dasa, and the jubilant Purandara Pandita” (CB, Antya 5.231–233)

The “Charitamrita” names only two here — Ramadasa and Gadadhara Dasa.[^3] <!-- F21 Q4 -->

On the very road Nityananda filled His companions with divine love:

“Thus Lord Nityananda and all His companions set out, overwhelmed by divine love. They forgot themselves completely,
and there was no end to the signs of ecstasy that appeared in their bodies” (CB, Antya 5.234–235) <!-- F22 -->

Each companion revealed his eternal bhava on the way. Of Parameshvari Dasa and his comrade Vrindavana Dasa writes:

“Krishnadasa and Parameshvari Dasa, the two of them, absorbed in the mood of cowherd boys, cried out ceaselessly:
‘Hai! Hai!’” (CB, Antya 5.240)[^4] <!-- F23 Q5 -->

What this mood is, Srila Bhaktisiddhanta explains in the “Gaudiya-bhashya”: Parameshvari Dasa and Krishnadasa are
servants of Sri Nityananda, and so their gopala-bhava is that of the twelve cowherd boys of Vraja, the friends of
Balarama, rather than Krishna-Gopala's own. Bhava is an inner state of the soul, not an outward imitation;
therefore those who dress up as sakhis or as cowherd boys (*sakhi-bheki*, *gopala-bheki*) are no example for a devotee.
The cowherd mood of Parameshvari Dasa is not a role and not an imitation of Krishna but his eternal nature: he is the
friend of Vraja Arjuna, a servant of Nityananda-Balarama. <!-- F24 Q6 I3 -->

Jayananda, in his own “Chaitanya-mangala,” adds details of his own. According to him, on Nityananda's way to Gauda
Parameshvara Dasa entered a divine mood, and Purandara the mood of Angada; among the wonders of Nityananda's
companions he also tells that Parameshvara Dasa “ate three *adi* of earth,” like the cowherd boy Krishna in His
childhood play.[^5] Earlier still, when the Lord took sannyasa, Nityananda, according to Jayananda, went off rejoicing
together with Ramadasa, Parameshvara, and Sundarananda. <!-- F25 -->

## The Festival in Panihati
Later, when Raghunatha Dasa held the festival of flat rice (*chida-dadhi*) in Panihati, Parameshvari Dasa was among the
companions of Nityananda who came with Him:

“Ramadasa, Sundarananda, Gadadhara dasa, Murari, Kamalakara, Sadashiva, Purandara, Dhananjaya, Jagadisha,
Parameshvara dasa, Mahesha, Gauridasa, Krishnadasa Hoda…” (CC, Antya 6.61–62) <!-- F26 -->

## The Jackal Who Chanted the Name
Parameshvari Dasa's name is bound up with a wonder of which the vandanas sing. Devakinandana writes in the
“Vaishnava-vandana”:

“With attention I shall praise Parameshvara Dasa Thakura: at the place of sankirtana he made the jackals chant the
name” (“Vaishnava-vandana” of Devakinandana, 85)

Srila Bhaktisiddhanta cites this verse in nearly the same words in the “Anubhashya.” The biographies see in it a sign of
his extraordinary spiritual power. <!-- F28 Q7 -->

Other vandanas tell the same in their own ways. The vandana ascribed to Jiva Gosvami praises Parameshvara Thakura, “the
self-luminous,” who, dancing, made the jackals hear the name of Hari. The vandana in tripadi metre says that he revealed
himself in the dance of kirtana: calling forty jackals one by one, he made them all utter the sixteen names. <!-- F29 -->

Tradition tells the story in more detail. Once in Akna-Mahesh, near Shrirampur in the Hugli district, in the shripat of
Sri Kamalakara Pippalai, harinama-sankirtana was going on, and Parameshvari Dasa was dancing, overcome by love. The
loud singing and dancing roused the envy of some godless men (*pashanda*); to defile the place of kirtana and teach the
devotees a lesson, they flung the carcass of a jackal among the singers. But the great devotee did not break off the
sankirtana for a moment, and by the power of the holy name the dead animal revived and joined everyone in chanting the
name. Everyone present marvelled and was flooded with bliss.[^6] <!-- F30 -->

This is not sorcery: it is the working of the holy name's power and of a Vaishnava's mercy. The
insult of the godless did not stop the sankirtana, and even a dead animal came to life and sang the name. <!-- I2 -->

## In Khardaha
After Nityananda's disappearance Parameshvari Dasa stayed a while at Khardaha, in Nityananda's house, where His
energies Vasudha and Jahnava resided. Sri Jahnava Mata held him very dear as her servant. <!-- F31 -->

Here, before setting out for Vrindavana, the young Srinivasa came. The “Bhakti-ratnakara” relates:

“Seeing Srinivasa, Sri Parameshvari Dasa, though in great sorrow, felt joy. He was sure in his mind that this was
Srinivasa, and coming near, he learned who he was” (“Bhakti-ratnakara” 4.82–83)

While the news of Srinivasa's arrival spread through Khardaha and everyone hurried to see him, Parameshvari Dasa quickly
took the guest to the Prabhu's house, and Srinivasa, bathed in tears, fell at the feet of the Ishvari, Jahnava; Vasudha,
Jahnava, and Virabhadra were overjoyed to see him (“Bhakti-ratnakara” 4.86–88). So, himself grieving in separation
from Nityananda, Parameshvari Dasa rejoiced at Srinivasa's coming. <!-- F32 -->

Narottama too came to Khardaha. When he was taking leave to go to Nilachala, Parameshvari Dasa, deeply moved, told him
the whole road in detail (“Bhakti-ratnakara” 8.219).[^7] <!-- F33 -->

## Kheturi
When Nityananda's companions in Khardaha asked Vasudha and Jahnava to attend the great Kheturi festival, it was he who
made everything ready for the journey:

“At the Ishvari's command Sri Parameshvara Dasa joyfully made everything ready for the journey” (“Narottama-vilasa”
6.193) <!-- F34 -->

He himself went in her company. The “Bhakti-ratnakara” lists her companions: “Gauranga, Nakadi, Krishnadasa, Damodara,
Sri Parameshvari, the learned Balarama, Sri Mukunda, Vrindavana Dasa, and others — with all of them the Ishvari went
happily” (10.376–377). The biographies, too, name him among those who came from Khardaha, in Jahnava's retinue. In
Kheturi, at the feast on the second day of the festival, he sat among the mahantas (“Narottama-vilasa” 8.51–53).
<!-- F35 Q8 F36 -->

## With Jahnava in Vrindavana
From Kheturi Jahnava went on to Vrindavana, and Parameshvari Dasa went with her (“Bhakti-ratnakara” 10.735–746;
“Narottama-vilasa” 8.266–270). <!-- F37 -->

At Akrura-ghata the Gosvamis of Vrindavana came out to meet the Ishvari. Seeing them from afar, she asked Parameshvari
Dasa who they were, and at her bidding he quietly pointed to each and named him: “This is Gopala Bhatta, full of love
for Gaura; this is Bhugarbha; this is Lokanatha, the abode of virtues; this is Krishnadasa Brahmachari, this is Krishna
Pandita, this is Sri Madhu Pandita, this is the renowned Jiva” (“Bhakti-ratnakara” 11.104–105; cf. “Narottama-vilasa”
9.15–17).[^8] <!-- F38 -->

There Krishnadasa Sarakhel, Madhava Acharya, and Jahnava's other companions met the Gosvamis, and Srinivasa Acharya's
disciples — Govinda and the others — were joyfully introduced to them by Parameshvari Dasa (“Bhakti-ratnakara”
11.111–116; “Narottama-vilasa” 9.21–24). It was he, too, who asked the Gosvamis to hear Govinda's poetic nectar;
delighted with his verses, the Gosvamis glorified Govinda as “Kaviraja,” “king of poets” (“Bhakti-ratnakara”
11.145–147).[^9] <!-- F39 F40 -->

When Jahnava returned, Parameshvari Dasa was still at her side. All that Raja Santosha sent with the Ishvari was
entrusted to him (“Bhakti-ratnakara” 11.367; “Narottama-vilasa” 9.156–160); in Ekachakra he was among her companions
(11.398–404); in Yajigrama, when Raghunandana asked about the Ishvari's journey, Parameshvari Dasa, collecting himself,
told everything from beginning to end (11.704–705); and when they left Shrikhanda for Khardaha, Raghunandana gave him
many provisions for the road (11.745–749). According to one of the biographies, on returning to Khardaha Jahnava Mata
kept Parameshvari Thakura with her as her servant, and both rejoiced beyond measure.[^10] <!-- F41 F42 -->

## Radha for Gopinatha
Once Gopinatha of Vrindavana, in a dream, asked Jahnava to send Him His beloved. Jahnava had a Deity of Sri Radhika
carved and sent Her to Vrindavana; Parameshvari Dasa accompanied Radha.[^11] <!-- F43 -->

A messenger from Khardaha told Srinivasa about it: the Ishvari, enchanted by Radhika's beauty, had told Sri
Parameshvari Dasa and other knowing Vaishnavas to take Her to Vrindavana, had given seven hundred coins, garments and
ornaments, had seated Sri Radha in a decorated boat and, remembering Gopinatha, had said: “Draw Your beloved to
Yourself quickly” (“Bhakti-ratnakara” 13.66–74). <!-- F44 -->

At Katwa, at the ghat of Keshava Bharati, the boat was met by Srinivasa Acharya and Raja Vira Hambira. With Parameshvari
Dasa were Nrisimha Chaitanya and Thakura Kanai. Parameshvari took Srinivasa into the boat and told him that the Ishvari
would go to Vrindavana again, “and we, having delivered Radhika to Gopinatha, will soon return.” He lifted the covering
and revealed Sri Radha to him, showed the garments and ornaments for Gopinatha and His two beloveds and for Govinda and
Madana-mohana, and lovingly let everyone see Sri Radha in the boat. In the courtyard of Gauranga at Katwa, in kirtana,
he cried out in ecstasy, “Sing, sing, O Narottama, Srinivasa!” — and the next morning sailed on from Kantakanagara
(“Bhakti-ratnakara” 13.82–121). <!-- F45 -->

On the way back he came to Srinivasa in Yajigrama — Srinivasa had foretold his coming — and told him: they had reached
Vrindavana without hindrance; at Gopinatha's command the priest had placed the Radha who had been sent on His left, and
the former Thakurani gladly stood on His right; people said, “Here is the Radhika sent by Jahnava, at His left hand”;
there was a great festival, and the Ishvari would soon go there again (“Bhakti-ratnakara” 13.223–239;
“Narottama-vilasa” 9.84). Thus Parameshvari Dasa saw with his own eyes Sri Radhika together with Sri Gopinatha and was
filled with love.[^12] <!-- F46 F47 -->

## Radha-Gopinatha in Atpur
Returning to Khardaha, he bowed at the feet of Vasudha and Jahnava and told them everything. The Ishvari, overcome with
love, said to him softly: “Make haste to Tada-Atpur and manifest there the seva of Radha-Gopinatha.” And at her command
Sri Parameshvari Dasa manifested that seva of Radha and Gopinatha (“Bhakti-ratnakara” 13.240–246).[^13] So it was that
he installed the Deities of Radha-Gopinatha in Atpur. <!-- F48 Q9 F49 -->

## The Shripat in Atpur
The shripat of Parameshvari Dasa is Atpur, on the Howrah–Amta railway, on the Champadanga branch line, not far from
Atpur station. Close by is the ancient Radha-Govinda temple, founded by the late Krishnakumar Mitra, diwan of
Raja Tej Bahadur of Bardhaman. Formerly the place was called Vishakhala; in the “Bhakti-ratnakara” and the
“Gaudiya-bhashya” the village is called Tada-Atpur.[^14] <!-- F9 F10 -->

Before the temple two bakula trees grow side by side, casting a deep shade, and a little apart stands a kadamba tree;
and between them lies Parameshvari Dasa's samadhi, crowned by a raised platform with tulasi. It is said that today's
bakulas sprang from cuttings of the pair that grew here in Parameshvari Thakura's time. Every year a single flower
blooms on the kadamba, and it is offered at the Deity's feet.[^15] <!-- F11 F12 F13 F54 -->

Srila Bhaktisiddhanta writes in detail about the servitors of the shripat in his “Anubhashya” to CC, Adi 11.29 — as
matters stood in his time. The service here was kept by descendants of Parameshvari Dasa's brother, some of whom lived
in the village of Garalgachha (post office Chanditala, Hugli district). Until recently they had had many disciples from
families brahmana by birth (*shaukra-brahmanas*); but in time, like worldly people, they took to the
vaidya's profession, and one by one the disciples of brahmana lineage drifted away. Their title was “Adhikari,” their
family name “Gupta”; the servitors then were Sri Radhikaprasanna Adhikari Kaviraja and the mother-in-law of Natavara
Adhikari, a widow without sons. Counting themselves mere vaidyas, they left the worship of the Thakura to devala
brahmanas. The servitors were eight families, merged into two; much land had once been endowed for the Deity's service,
but they lost all of it, and the service was barely kept up on a modest debottara. <!-- F14 F15 F16 F17 Q11 -->

In the temple Baladeva shares one throne with Radha-Gopinatha; His Deity was probably installed later. Srila
Bhaktisiddhanta remarks that to seat Baladeva together with Krishna and Srimati on a single throne goes against
tattva. <!-- F18 -->

After returning from Vrindavana, Parameshvari Dasa lived for a while in Khardaha and then in the village of
Garalgachha.[^16] <!-- F50 -->

## The Poet
Parameshvari Dasa was a poet as well. His pada signed “Parameshvari Dasa” tells of a day when the Lord, before His
sannyasa, came to the temple of Advaita and sat down; with Nityananda and Advaita He playfully discussed a great
festival; Sita Thakurani came in, smiling; and the Lord told them to call the Vaishnavas of the neighbourhood, to make
ready mridangas and karatalas, sandal and aguru, everything at the ghat, and to gather in a circle for loud kirtana. It
begins with the words “One day the Lord came smiling to Advaita's temple” (“Pada-kalpataru” 23); the compilers of the
padas regard it as an eyewitness account. <!-- F51 Q12 -->

His second pada begins with the words “Receiving the Lord's command, all the devotees…”: at the Lord's word the
devotees form seven kirtana parties led by Nityananda, Mukunda, Advaita, Gadadhara, Vasu Ghosha, Murari, and Narahari,
and fourteen madalas resound. <!-- F52 -->

## Departure
Parameshvari Dasa left this world on Vaishakhi Purnima, the full moon of the month of Vaishakha; on this day his
tirobhava-utsava is celebrated. In the calendar of the Sri Chaitanya Saraswat Math it is the “Disappearance Day of
Srila Parameshvari Dasa Thakura” — the purnima of the month of Madhusudana (in 2026, May 1); on the same day the
appearance of Srinivasa Acharya is observed. No source names the year or place of his departure, nor the day of his
appearance. His samadhi is in Atpur, before the temple, between the bakulas and the kadamba. <!-- F53 F54 -->

## Significance
Parameshvari Dasa is Krishna's eternal friend Arjuna, who came with Balarama into the pastimes of Nityananda.
“Nityananda is his life; in his body Nityananda plays”: so Vrindavana Dasa sees him, and therefore, in the words of
the “Charitamrita,” the mere remembrance of him bestows devotion to Krishna. His cowherd's “Hai, hai!” on the road to
Gauda is not imitation but the voice of the eternal nature of a friend of Vraja; his sankirtana did not break off at an
insult, and even a dead jackal sang the holy name. After Nityananda's disappearance he became Jahnava's faithful servant:
he led her retinue, introduced Vaishnavas to her, brought Sri Radha to Gopinatha, and at her command manifested the
service of Radha-Gopinatha in Atpur, where his samadhi stands to this day. <!-- F2 F6 F7 F49 I1 I2 I3 I4 -->
