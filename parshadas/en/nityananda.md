# Nityananda Prabhu
*Нитьянанда Прабху · @nityananda*

## The Brother of Gaura
When the Son of Nanda came into the age of Kali as Sri Chaitanya, His elder brother came with Him. Krishnadasa Kaviraja
says it simply:

“Now the son of Nanda Maharaja is Sri Chaitanya, and Baladeva is the brother of Sri Chaitanya, Nityananda.” (CC, Adi
17.295)

As in the Dvapara-yuga Baladeva appeared as the elder brother of Krishna, so in the Kali-yuga, in the form of
Nityananda, He became the elder brother of Sri Gaurahari. Narottama Dasa sings the same: the son of the king of Vraja
became the son of Shachi, and Balarama became Nitai. Lochana Dasa tells in the “Chaitanya-mangala” that even before the
Lord's descent Narada came to Shvetadvipa and beheld there the lilas of Balarama — the Lord in the form of service; and
then His eternal companions descended together with the Lord: Balarama as Nityananda, Shiva as Advaita, and all the
rest. <!-- F6 F7 F25 Q1 -->

The “Charitamrita” opens its account of Him with the same words — and at once confesses that His glory cannot be
exhausted:

“Sri Chaitanya is Krishna Himself, and Nityananda is Balarama. Sri Nityananda fulfils all the desires of Sri
Chaitanya.” (CC, Adi 5.156)

“The ocean of Sri Nityananda's glory has no bounds or limits. By His mercy I have been able to touch only a single drop
of His greatness.” (CC, Adi 5.157)

Even the thousand-mouthed Ananta could not exhaust that ocean in all eternity; and Vrindavana Dasa adds that by
Nityananda's mercy the whole world sings of Chaitanya to this day (CB, Antya 5.220). At the very beginning of the
“Chaitanya-bhagavata” (Adi 1.16–19) He is praised as Haladhara — the bearer of the plough, vast of body, imperturbable
and yet intoxicated by the nectar of Chaitanyachandra's glory; whoever hears of His deeds is taken under the special
protection of Sri Krishna Chaitanya Himself, Shiva and Parvati are pleased with him, and Sarasvati appears upon his
tongue. <!-- F26 Q55 Q44 Q18 -->

Vrindavana Dasa himself does not undertake to settle what his Lord should be called. Some, he says, see in Him Balarama
Himself, others the abode of love for Sri Chaitanya; ascetic, devotee or sage — whatever He may be to Chaitanya, may His
feet be fixed in the hearts of all. For the “Bhagavata,” Nityananda is the first of Sri Chaitanya's devotees, His best
and greatest beloved, and Krishna Chaitanya Himself is His life and soul. Opening the chapter on His childhood and
wanderings, Vrindavana Dasa glorifies together Krishna Chaitanya, Nityananda the friend of the helpless, Advaita, and
Vishvambhara, the refuge of Srivasa and Gadadhara; and elsewhere he says that the Lord descended surrounded by His eternal
companions — Nityananda, Advaita, Gadadhara and all the pure devotees. How Baladeva manifests Himself as Sankarshana and
the purusha-avataras, and what His form as a devotee in the Pancha-tattva means, will be told further on.
<!-- F23 F24 Q8 Q71 -->

His names are past counting. Vrindavana Dasa, “the Vyasa of Sri Chaitanya's lilas,” calls Him Nityananda Avadhuta,
Nityananda-chandra, Nityananda-rama and Nityananda-raya, Nityananda-svarupa and Nityananda-svarupa-gosani, Nityananda
Bhagavan and Nityananda Mahaprabhu, Prabhuvara, Mahavira, Mahabali, Mahamati, Mahashaya, Mahamaheshvara,
Nityananda-simha, Nityananda-mahamalla and Mahamalla-raya, Nitai-chand, Nitai Thakura, Ishvara Nitai, Vishvambhara and Gaudeshvara, “the lord of Gauda”; to
him He is Avadhuta-chandra, Avadhutavara, Avadhutamani, Avadhuta-mahabala, Avadhuta-mahashaya, Avadhuta-raya and
Avadhuta-simha, “the abode of Sri Chaitanya's great love,” “the father of sankirtana,” “the guardian of the dharma of
the age,” “the delight of the world,” “the one most dear to Sri Chaitanya,” “the beloved form of Chaitanya-chandra”[^1].
The name “Mahamalla-raya” is explained as “the commander-in-chief of the army of sankirtana.” He is also called Ananta,
the original Lord, the moonlike Nityananda and the friend of the helpless. Vaishnavas say that particular joy and
blessing come from the very names with which Vrindavana Dasa glorified Him, for in chanting the names of Nityananda one
reveals the glory of Sri Chaitanya. <!-- F1 F2 F3 F4 -->

Most often He is called an avadhuta. Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati, following Shridhara Svami (on the “Bhagavatam”
3.1.19), explains the word thus: one who takes no care of the body (Anubhashya on CC, Adi 5.161); an avadhuta has cast off
worldly feelings and duties and is bound by no conventions, not even the rules of varnashrama. Nityananda lives in the
world of krishna-prema, in unbroken divine ecstasy, and gives no thought to adorning the body — that is why He is the
Avadhuta. In His wanderings, too, He went about in the guise of an avadhuta. <!-- F5 I12 -->

But above all He is mercy. Krishnadasa Kaviraja, speaking of Him, suddenly turns to himself:

“I am more sinful than Jagai and Madhai and lower than a wretched worm in dung.” (CC, Adi 5.205)

“Who in all the world but Sri Nityananda could have poured out His mercy on such a sinner as I am?” (CC, Adi 5.207)

“Sri Nityananda is intoxicated with loving frenzy. He is the embodiment of supreme mercy and compassion, and so He makes
no distinction between good and bad.” (CC, Adi 5.208)

It was He, Kaviraja goes on, who brought him to Vrindavana and gave him shelter at the feet of Sri Rupa (Adi 5.209–210).
For the most fallen, who have no merit at all, hope in Nityananda's feet is the crown of piety; and even the pain of
knowing one's own unworthiness, and the longing for His shelter, are already His mercy. He alone drags the sinner out
“by the hair,” leads him out of the fortress of Mahamaya and gives him the right to enter the market of the holy name,
Vrindavana, and the service of the Divine Couple. Gaura is merciful to those who belong to Nityananda — and so great
devotees pray to the Vaishnavas for Nityananda's mercy. Vrindavana Dasa says the same at the beginning of his book:

“One who wishes to cross the ocean of material existence and plunge into the ocean of devotional service must devote
himself to the worship of Sri Nityananda.” (CB, Adi 1.77)

“At the feet of the Vaishnavas I pray for one desire alone: to serve, birth after birth, the lotus feet of my Gurudeva,
Sri Balarama!” (CB, Adi 1.78) <!-- Q50 Q51 I13 I17 Q16 -->

## Appearance in Ekachakra
Nityananda appeared before Mahaprabhu — on the thirteenth lunar day of the bright fortnight of the month of Magha, Maghi
shukla-trayodashi. The “Chaitanya-bhagavata” sets these two days side by side:

“Gaurachandra appeared on the night of the full moon of the month of Phalguna, and Lord Nityananda appeared in the month
of Magha on the thirteenth day of the waxing moon.” (CB, Adi 3.45)

“If a person honours these two festivals, he will attain devotion to Lord Krishna and cut the bonds of material
slavery.” (CB, Adi 3.47)

Therefore this tithi, like Phalguni-purnima, the day of Gaurasundara's appearance, brings blessing to the whole world
and is worthy of the highest honour. <!-- F27 F28 Q17 -->

The place of His appearance was the village of Ekachakra, or Ekachaka, in Rarh — Radha-desha, in the land of Gauda.
Where it once stood are now Birchandrapur, Garbhavasa and other villages, about four kroshas east of Mallarpur station on
the loop line of the East Indian Railway. The place was later named Birchandrapur in honour of Nityananda's son, Sri
Birachandra (Virabhadra) Prabhu. The “Charitamrita” names Rarh as the birthplace of more than Nityananda alone:

“Whereas Nityananda Prabhu, Gangadasa Pandita, Murari Gupta and Mukunda were born in Radha-desha.” (CC, Adi 13.61)[^2]
<!-- F30 F31 F32 F33 -->

His father was Hadai Pandita (or Harai Pandita); he was also known as Hadai, or Hado, Ojha — Hado Upadhyaya: “ojha,” or
“jha,” is a corruption of “upadhyaya,” the family title of Maithila brahmanas. Hadai came of a Maithila brahmana family
and was a highly renounced Vaishnava. His mother, Padmavati-devi, was wholly devoted to the service of Hari; herself a
Vaishnava shakti, she tirelessly served Vaishnavas and guests. According to the “Bhakti-ratnakara,” an old brahmana of
Shaligrama told Surya Dasa that Harai Pandita and Padmavati of Ekachakra were, in Krishna's lila, Vasudeva and Rohini,
and Balarama appeared as their son Nityananda. Coming into their family, He granted them service to Himself in parental
love — vatsalya-rasa. Vrindavana Dasa says that He rose like the moon from the ocean of the womb of Padmavati, wife of
Hadai Ojha, and dispelled the darkness over Rarh. One of the biographies calls Vishvarupa, Mahaprabhu's elder brother,
Nityananda's “other Self.” <!-- F34 F35 F36 F37 F38 F39 F415 F416 -->

With His coming every kind of good fortune arrived in Radha-desha: famine, poverty and misfortune vanished. From infancy
He was restrained and very intelligent, endowed with every virtue, and His beauty eclipsed millions of Kamadevas.
<!-- F42 F43 -->

Not far from Ekachaka resides Shiva Maudeshvara (Maureshvara). Nityananda worshipped him to show an example of service to
a Vaishnava: without service to the Vaishnava there is no mercy of Vishnu. It is said that Hadai Pandita too worshipped
Shiva — Bhandishvara, the best of Vaishnavas. <!-- F40 F41 I18 -->

On the day Gaurachandra appeared in Navadvipa-Mayapur, Nityananda in Ekachakra gave a thunderous cry of joy, and the
sound filled the universe. People explained it in different ways: some thought lightning had struck, others that the
voice of God had thundered, still others that it was an omen of disaster; many understood that a divine power was at
work, and someone said, “The lord of Gauda has spoken in a voice of thunder.” But Krishna's yogamaya covered everyone,
and no one recognised in the boy Who He was. <!-- F44 F45 -->

## Bankim-raya and the Memorial Places of Ekachakra
In Ekachakra resides the Deity of Krishna — Sri Bankim-raya (Bankima-deva), installed by Nityananda, together with
Srimati Radha. Tradition tells that Bankim-raya was floating on the waters of the Yamuna at Kadambakhandi ghat, east of
the temple, and Nityananda lifted Him from the water and installed Him. Srimati appeared at the hamlet of Bhaddarpur,
about half a mile west of Birchandrapur. The local sevaites say that Nityananda entered into Bankim-raya, and therefore
Sri Jahnava-mata, Nityananda's shakti, was later installed at His right. The sevaites of Birchandrapur themselves are a
collateral branch of the line of Gopijana-vallabha. <!-- F46 F47 F48 F49 F50 -->

There is also a place in Ekachakra called Vishramtala: according to local tradition, here the boy Nityananda played the
lilas of Krishna and Rama with His friends. These places were honoured later as well. According to the
“Bhakti-ratnakara,” when Jahnava visited Ekachakra on her way back from Vraja, she was shown the places of His childhood
lilas; she spent the night in His parents' deserted house and passed the whole night in kirtana. Narottama came here
too; an old brahmana showed him the places of the lilas, and Narottama, remembering the names of Hadai Pandita and
Padmavati-devi, fell senseless. <!-- F51 F52 -->

## Childhood: the Lilas of Krishna, Rama and Vamana
Concealing His divinity, the boy Nityananda played with the children not at ordinary games but at the lilas of Krishna,
Rama and other avataras; and Krishna was always at the centre of every game. His friends never left Him and did not go
home, playing day and night, and the parents did not worry — they loved Him with all their hearts and embraced Him
tenderly. Everyone wondered — Hadai and Padmavati first of all — how the child knew Krishna's deeds and why,
instead of ordinary pastimes, He enacted the lilas of the incarnations of Vishnu. <!-- F53 F54 F55 -->

It all began with an “assembly of the gods.” One boy played the Earth presenting the gods' petition to Vishnu; all went
with the “Earth” to the riverbank and prayed to Kshirodakashayi Vishnu, and a hidden boy announced loudly, “Soon I shall
be born in the cowherd village of Mathura”[^3]. Then they enacted the wedding of Vasudeva and Devaki, Krishna's
appearance at midnight in “Kamsa's prison” — a play-hut — the carrying of the infant to the cowherd settlement, and His
exchange for yogamaya. <!-- F56 F57 -->

Then came the lilas of Vraja. One boy dressed as Putana, another “sucked” her breast; they broke a cart of reeds; they
stole milk and yoghurt from the house of the local milkman; they made snakes of leaves, and a boy lay motionless in the
pond while Nityananda “woke” him — that was how they played Kaliya; in a palm grove they “slew” Dhenuka and ate the tala
fruits; they overcame toy demons — Baka, Agha, Vatsa; at evening they returned from the pasture blowing buffalo horns.
They lifted Govardhana, built a Vrindavana, stole the gopis' garments, went to the wives of the sacrificial brahmanas. A
bearded boy-“Narada” gave secret counsel to Kamsa, and a boy-“Akrura” carried Krishna and Balarama away — and then
Nityananda, overwhelmed by the feelings of the gopis, wept in separation from Krishna, and tears streamed from His eyes
before the children. <!-- F58 F59 -->

They built Mathura too: some were garland-sellers, others those who wore the garlands; Nityananda dressed one boy as
Kubja and accepted fragrant paste from her; with a thunderous crack He broke the bow; He slew the elephant Kuvalayapida
and the wrestlers Chanura and Mushtika, seized “Kamsa” by the hair and, having “killed” him, danced merrily with the
children, while the people of Ekachakra laughed with delight. They played the Vamana-lila as well: a boy-“Bali” gave away
the three worlds, an old boy-“Shukracharya” forbade the gift, and “Vamana,” having accepted the alms, placed His foot on
“Bali's” head. By the will of Vishnu's yogamaya no one understood that these games were no childish play. <!-- F60 F61 F62 -->

He loved the Ramayana no less. Children playing monkeys built a bridge across the ocean: Nityananda cut branches and
threw them into the water as though they were huge trees, to cries of “Jaya Rama! Jaya Raghunatha!” As Lakshmana He
strode in anger, bow in hand, to Sugriva's palace: “Worthless monkey! My Lord Rama grieves on Mount Malyavan, and you make
merry with women. If you want to live, hurry to Rama with help!” As Lakshmana He also threatened Parashurama[^4]. Meeting
five “monkeys” in the forest, “Lakshmana” asked who they were, calling Himself a servant of Raghunatha; they begged Him to
lead them to Rama, for they feared Bali, and He embraced them, led them to “Rama” and fell prostrate. “Lakshmana” was now
defeated by Indrajit, now slew him; a boy-“Vibhishana” He brought to Rama and crowned king of Lanka. <!-- F63 F64 F65 F66 -->

Once a boy-“Ravana” threw a lotus flower at Him as though it were the shakti spear, and “Lakshmana” fell without sign of
life. The children began to weep; the parents ran up and, finding no life in their son, themselves fell senseless; the
whole village gathered. Someone recalled an actor who had played Dasharatha and died on the stage on hearing of Rama's
exile; but another said, “It is only a part: Hanuman will bring the medicine, and He will come round.” Nityananda had
taught the children beforehand: when He fell, they were to surround Him and weep, then send “Hanuman,” and life would
return as soon as the medicine was held to His nose; but in their fright the children forgot the instruction — and
remembered it only after the onlooker's words. <!-- F67 F68 -->

And “Hanuman” set out — every word he spoke had been taught him by Nityananda. A “sage” with fruits and roots invited him
to stay — Hanuman hurried on; a “crocodile” in a pond seized his feet — and was defeated; a “rakshasa” threatened to
swallow him — and after a fight was defeated too; the “gandharvas” on Mount Gandhamadana were also overcome, and Hanuman
brought the mountain on his head. A boy-physician — Sushena, the monkeys' physician — held the herb vishalyakarani to
“Lakshmana's” nose, and “Lakshmana” rose, and the parents came to themselves. Everyone laughed; Hadai Pandita embraced his son,
and people asked, “Child, how do You know all this?” — and He answered, smiling, “These are all My lilas.”[^5] <!-- F69 F70 F71 -->

In early childhood His body was tender and fair; no one wanted to let Him out of their arms, and people loved Him more
than their own sons, while from infancy nothing was dear to Him but Krishna's lila. Vrindavana Dasa sees in Him the
Supreme Lord Himself, though the children and His kin did not understand it; he bows a hundred times at the feet of the
boys who played with Him, for His lilas are revealed only by His own mercy. <!-- F72 F73 F22 -->

To Hadai and Padmavati He was the eldest son[^6] — and the one apple of their eye, their very life: they could not bear
to be without Him even for a little while. Hadai Pandita tilled the land, served as a priest, went away on business — and
thought of Nityananda all the time. The “Chaitanya-bhagavata” says:

“Hadai Pandita would not let Nityananda go a single step from him. One might have thought that Hadai Pandita was the
body and Nityananda his soul.” (CB, Madhya 3.75)

He never let his son out of his sight and embraced Him endlessly, and it seemed as if the child's tender body melted into
his father's (CB, Madhya 3.73–74). So, in play, the twelve years of His life at home went by. <!-- F74 F75 F76 Q19 -->

## The Sannyasi at the Door
When Nityananda turned twelve, a wandering Vaishnava sannyasi came to Ekachakra. Hadai recognised in him a pure
Vaishnava, invited him for bhiksha and talked with him all night about Krishna. In the morning the guest asked for
“alms”: to give him for a time the eldest son, marked with auspicious signs, so that on pilgrimage the boy might cook
offerings for the Lord — for a sannyasi does not kindle a fire. He promised to guard Him more than his own life and to
show Him all the holy places. The biographies have not preserved this sannyasi's name[^7]. <!-- F77 F78 F82 -->

Hadai was shaken — not because he was unwilling to give, but at the very thought that his son would pass out of his
sight. Yet he remembered the mahajanas of former times: so had Dasharatha given Rama to Vishvamitra. Praying to Krishna
for protection in this trial of dharma (dharma-sankata), he told everything to his wife, and Padmavati answered without a
shadow of hesitation, “To follow your will is my dharma.” With his wife's joyful consent Hadai entrusted his son to the
sannyasi, and Nityananda went with him — to display the lila of serving a Vaishnava and purifying the holy places.
<!-- F79 F80 F81 -->

Hadai and Padmavati are the model of a Vaishnava householder: to serve the Vaishnava, giving him one's whole life — for,
as the “Bhagavatam” teaches, one should always do good: with one's life, wealth, mind and words. A gift into which life
is put is the highest gift; a gift without it is only a ritual. We readily praise the sacrifices of others, while we
ourselves draw back or deceive ourselves; millions of sannyasis long for the dust from the feet of such grihasthas. Nor
was Hadai's parental love a worldly attachment to a son: worldly attachment is only its defective reflection, while a
drop of such love makes even the happiness of liberation insignificant. The partings of great souls the
“Chaitanya-bhagavata” explains by the examples of Kapila, who left his mother Devahuti, of Shukadeva, who went away from
Vyasa, and of Gaura Himself, who left Shachi:

“In truth none of these examples of renunciation is a forsaking of one's obligations. At their root lies the attainment of
the highest good, but only great souls can understand this.” (CB, Madhya 3.104) <!-- I19 I20 Q25 Q67 -->

The parting was hard for Hado Upadhyaya: he nearly lost his mind, seemed to people to be stunned, and for three months
neither ate nor drank. He remained alive — but from then on was forever “mad for Nityananda.” <!-- F83 -->

## Twenty Years of Wandering
So at twelve Nityananda set out for the holy places — to purify them of the sins left there by pilgrims. In the guise of
a pilgrim He went through the north and south of India, sanctified places that had never been holy before, and only then
came to Sri Chaitanya in Navadvipa. The “Chaitanya-bhagavata” gives the periods:

“Thus Lord Nityananda spent twelve years in the house of His parents. Then He set out on pilgrimage to the holy places.”
(CB, Adi 9.100)

“For the next twenty years He travelled through the holy places, and then He joined Lord Chaitanya.” (CB, Adi 9.101)[^8]

The old brahmana of Shaligrama, according to the “Bhakti-ratnakara,” told Surya Dasa: Nitai went to all the holy places,
performed many austerities and became a great scholar, and then He came to Navadvipa and became Chaitanya's dearest
companion. <!-- F85 F86 F99 F29 F134 -->

Vrindavana Dasa describes His route in order[^9]. From Vakreshvara He went alone to the forest of Vaidyanatha, from there
to Gaya, then to Kashi, where the Ganga flows northward — there He bathed and drank her water with insatiable thirst; in
the month of Magha He took the morning bath at Prayaga. Then He came to Mathura, the place of His former birth: He bathed
in the Yamuna at Vishrama-ghata and in ecstasy circled Govardhana and all twelve forests of Vrindavana. In Gokula, in the
house of Nanda Maharaja, He sat and wept with overflowing feeling and bowed down to Madana-Gopala. In Hastinapura, the abode
of the Pandavas, He wept at the sight of the place of great devotees — the people there, who knew nothing of devotion,
could not understand it; and seeing the glory of Balarama in Hastinapura, He bowed and cried out, “Save Me,
Haladhara!” <!-- F87 F88 F89 -->

In Dvaraka He bathed joyfully in the sea; He was at Siddhapura, the abode of Kapila; at Matsya-tirtha, at a great festival,
He distributed much cooked rice; at Shiva-Kanchi and Vishnu-Kanchi He laughed to see the bitter enmity of the devotees of
Vishnu and of Shiva. He passed through Kurukshetra, Prithudaka, Bindu-sarovara, Prabhasa, Sudarshana-tirtha, Trita-kupa,
Vishala, Brahma-tirtha, Chakra-tirtha, Pratisrota, the ancient, western Sarasvati and Naimisharanya. In Ayodhya He wept much
at Sri Rama's birthplace; in the kingdom of Guha the chandala, Shringaverapura, remembering his devotion, He lay
unconscious for three days; in all the forests where Rama had stayed He rolled on the ground in the agony of separation.
<!-- F90 F91 F92 -->

He bathed in the Sarayu, was at the Kaushiki and at the hermitage of the sage Pulastya, bathed in the Gomati, the Gandaki
and the Shona; on the summit of Mount Mahendra He bowed down to Parashurama; He was at Haridvara, where the Ganga descends
to earth, at Pampa, Bhimarathi, Sapta-Godavari, and at the tirthas of the Venva and the Vipasha. He saw Karttikeya and came
to Mount Shri (Shri-shaila), where Shiva and Parvati live in the form of a brahmana and his wife. Both recognised in the
avadhuta wayfarer their own Deity; Parvati cooked prasada for Him with her own hands, and they fed Him with reverence,
while He smiled and bowed to them. What they spoke of in secret, only Krishna knows. <!-- F93 F94 -->

In the land of Dravida He visited Venkatanatha, Kamakoshti-puri, Kanchi, the river Kaveri and Srirangam, where He stayed
for some time, Hari-kshetra, Mount Rishabha, Southern Mathura (Madurai), the Kritamala and the Tamraparni, and then
Uttara-Yamuna and the hermitage of Agastya on Mount Malaya; everywhere the local people received Him gladly. At
Badarikashrama He lived for some time in complete solitude, in the hermitage of the rishi Nara-Narayana; and at the
hermitage of Vyasa, Shamyaprasa, Vyasa himself recognised Balarama in Him, appeared before Him and received his guest, and
the Lord bowed down to Srila Vyasadeva. He also entered a monastery of Buddhists: He put questions to them, but no one
could answer; then, angered, He struck them on the head with His foot — the Buddhists ran off laughing, and He calmly went
on His way. <!-- F95 F96 F98 -->

Next came Kanyakumari, where He had darshana of Durga-devi, the Southern Sea, Sri Ananta (Anantapura), the pond of the five
apsaras (Pancha-apsara), the temple of Shiva at Gokarna; He went from door to door through Kerala and Trigarta, saw
Arya-Dvaipayani — the goddess Parvati on an island near Gokarna, was at the Nirvindhya, the Payasvini and the Tapti, at the Reva, in the city of
Mahishmati and at Malla-tirtha, and through Shurparaka went on to the west. In all His wanderings He feared nothing and no
one; constantly absorbed in Krishna, He had no control over His body; He now laughed, now wept — who can fathom His
moods? <!-- F97 F100 F101 -->

## Meeting Madhavendra Puri
In the west of India[^10] Nityananda chanced to meet Madhavendra Puri. He wandered freely with disciples full of
krishna-prema; he knew no food but the taste of Krishna's qualities, and his body was the place of Krishna's lilas; and
the highest praise of his love is that the great Advaita Acharya was his disciple. Mahaprabhu Himself more than once
called Madhavendra the original teacher of pure devotion. With him were Ishvara Puri, Brahmananda Puri and other
disciples. <!-- F106 F107 F108 F110 -->

Seeing each other, both fainted from love and lay motionless, while Ishvara Puri and Madhavendra's other disciples wept.
Coming to, they embraced and wept, rolled on the ground and roared with joy; tears flowed in streams, their bodies
trembled, their hair stood on end — all eight spiritual transformations appeared in them, for Chaitanya Mahaprabhu Himself
was dancing in their hearts. Nityananda said:

“Lord Nityananda said: ‘I have gone round many holy places, but today I have reached the goal of My pilgrimage. Today I
have seen the lotus feet of Madhavendra Puri. Seeing his exalted love, I have attained the perfection of life.’” (CB, Adi
9.166–167) <!-- F109 F111 F112 Q5 -->

And Madhavendra embraced Nityananda, bathed Him in tears, took Him on his lap and pressed Him to his breast; from love he
could not utter a word and would not let Him go. Such love, he said, he had met nowhere[^11], and where it is, there for him
are all the holy places; Krishna was merciful to him, since He had sent such a companion:

“I have met Nityananda, and therefore I know that Krishna has granted me His mercy.” (CB, Adi 9.183)

“The place where one can have the company of Nityananda is the holiest and wholly transcendental.” (CB, Adi 9.184)

“If a person hears the name of Nityananda, he will certainly attain the lotus feet of Lord Krishnachandra.” (CB, Adi
9.185)

For him Nityananda's company contained in itself all the tirthas and Vaikuntha itself; and whoever bears even a little
dislike for Nityananda will not be dear to Krishna, however much of a devotee he may seem. Day and night Madhavendra
glorified Nityananda and served Him with love[^12]. <!-- F113 F114 F115 Q6 F444 -->

Ishvara Puri, Brahmananda Puri and all Madhavendra's disciples felt a natural, irresistible love for Nityananda; Ishvara
Puri understood how infinitely dear He was to his guru. Before, meeting many renunciants — karmis, jnanis, yogis, ascetics
and keepers of vows, each with desires of his own — Madhavendra and his disciples had found in them no trace of love for
Krishna and had gone off sorrowfully into the forests; the meeting with Nityananda dispelled that sorrow. <!-- F116 F117 -->

For a few days[^13] they wandered together — from forest to forest, as if searching for Krishna — and spoke of His lilas,
while the brotherhood of disciples sang the name of Hari without pause. Madhavendra, at the sight of a dark cloud,
remembered Krishna and fell senseless — his love in separation is beyond description; day and night, intoxicated with
love, both laughed, wept and, beside themselves, cried, “Haya! Haya!”[^14] And Nityananda, intoxicated with the nectar of
Govinda's sweetness, reeled, stumbled, fell, laughed aloud and thundered. Their talks of Krishna were confidential, and
only Krishna could understand them; day and night lost all meaning for them:

“Nityananda and Madhavendra Puri noticed neither day nor night — so deep was their absorption in the moods of ecstatic
love. The few days they spent together flew by for them like a single moment.” (CB, Adi 9.179) <!-- F118 F119 F120 F121 -->

At last they parted: Nityananda went to Setubandha, Rama's bridge, and Madhavendra to the Sarayu. Both were so absorbed in
Krishna that they did not remember their bodies — only so could they bear the agony of separation. Whoever hears with
faith of Nityananda and Madhavendra, says Vrindavana Dasa — of His pilgrimage and of their meeting — obtains the treasure
of love for Krishna. <!-- F122 F123 Q7 -->

Whether Nityananda was Madhavendra's disciple is a matter on which opinions differ[^15]. One thing is clear: although He
is the teacher of the world's teachers and the crown of acharyas, He honoured Madhavendra as guru for the sake of example
and never treated him otherwise. According to the “Bhakti-ratnakara,” Madhavendra dealt with Nityananda as a friend and
equal, and Nitai with him as with an elder. Nityananda was pleased to accept Madhavendra as guru for the instruction of
people; and to regard Him as Madhavendra's disciple or as his godbrother is in essence one and the same: honour to a
godbrother is not different from honour to the guru. <!-- F102 F103 F104 F105 I21 -->

## Nilachala, Gangasagara and the Waiting in Vrindavana
Having parted from Madhavendra, Nityananda bathed at Dhanus-tirtha, visited Rameshvaram, Vijayanagara, Mayapuri
(Haridvara), Avanti, the Godavari, the abode of Jiyur-Nrisimhadeva (Simhachala), Tirumala, the shrine of Kurmanatha, and
came to Purushottama. As soon as He entered the city of Sri Jagannatha, the moon of Nilachala, He saw the flag above the
temple and fell senseless[^16]; at darshana He saw Jagannatha in four forms (chaturvyuha), with all His servants, and again
and again lost and regained consciousness: He was shaken with trembling, bathed in perspiration and tears, and gave
thunderous cries. After a few days in Nilachala He visited Gangasagara, where the Ganga flows into the ocean. Vrindavana
Dasa confesses that he has described only a small part of His wanderings — and that only by His mercy. <!-- F124 F125 F126 -->

Having gone round the holy places, Nityananda came again to Mathura and settled in Vrindavana:

“He dwelt in Vrindavana and was so absorbed in thoughts of Krishna that He could not tell day from night.” (CB, Adi
9.205)

“He ate nothing, and only now and then drank a little milk if it was offered to Him.” (CB, Adi 9.206) <!-- F127 Q9 -->

Meanwhile in Navadvipa Gaurasundara was already living[^17] — and grieved, not seeing Nityananda, His “second body.”
Nityananda, for His part, thought: “Sri Gaurachandra is in Navadvipa, but His glory is still hidden; as soon as the Lord
wishes to reveal His divinity, I shall go at once to Navadvipa to serve Him.” So He did not go to Navadvipa and did not
leave Mathura, but kept playing with children in the waters of the Yamuna and in the dust of Vrindavana, wandering through
Vrindavana and the holy places until Sri Chaitanya revealed Himself:

“Lord Nityananda is all-powerful, but at that time He did not bestow devotional service to the Lord on people. Only after
Lord Gaurachandra had revealed His divine greatness and ordered Him to preach did Lord Nityananda begin His preaching. The
servants and companions of the Lord do nothing without receiving the Lord's instruction. Yet this in no way diminishes
their glory.” (CB, Adi 9.211)

Possessed of all power, He concealed His lila of bestowing prema-bhakti and waited for Gaurachandra to reveal the greatness
of sankirtana and give the order. The thought “to do nothing without the will of Sri Chaitanya” does not diminish but
exalts service to the Lord: even Ananta, Brahma, Shiva and all the gods do nothing without His sanction, and the
destroyers, rulers and maintainers of the universe carry out their duties only by His command. And on those who do not like to hear the glory of Nityananda the Vaishnavas
do not even cast a glance.
<!-- F128 F129 F130 F131 F132 F133 Q10 -->

Vrindavana Dasa closes the chapter on Nityananda's childhood and wanderings with praise and prayer. Nityananda delivered
the whole world; He is the incomparable ocean of mercy; only by His mercy has the author himself grasped the truth about
Sri Chaitanya, only through Him has Mahaprabhu's greatness been proclaimed, and only wicked godless men revile Him. All
three worlds bear witness that the treasure of love for God was obtained by Nityananda's mercy. His tongue keeps the
praise of Sri Chaitanya, and day and night He speaks only of Chaitanya; by serving Him one obtains devotion to Chaitanya,
and attachment to Nityananda is itself a gift of Mahaprabhu's mercy. For one who knows Nityananda there is no danger
anywhere; and whoever wishes to cross the darkness of this world and plunge into the ocean of devotion, let him serve Him.
Mahaprabhu, says Vrindavana Dasa, “gave us Nityananda — and took Him away.” <!-- Q11 Q12 F413 -->

He also warns against one mistake. Some followers of Sri Chaitanya speak of Nityananda in a way that seems like blame but
is in fact praise; these Vaishnavas are pure of heart, and their apparent quarrel is only lila (one translation of the
chapter explains that Vaishnavas such as Advaita Acharya are meant). But whoever takes the side of one and reviles the
other will perish; and whoever follows Nityananda without seeking faults in Him will attain the shelter of Sri Gaurachandra. And Vrindavana
Dasa prays: to see Sri Chaitanya and Nityananda among the devotees; that Nityananda be his eternal master; that, keeping
faith with Nityananda, he may serve the feet of Gaurachandra and, birth after birth, study the “Srimad-Bhagavatam” at Nityananda's
feet — for without Mahaprabhu's consent no one attains the feet of Nityananda. <!-- Q14 I10 Q15 -->

## Arrival in Navadvipa
How Nityananda learned that the Lord had revealed Himself the biographies tell differently[^18]. Wandering through India
in the mood of separation and searching for Krishna, He came to Vrindavana — and here it was revealed to His heart that
Krishna had already appeared in Navadvipa-Mayapur as Gaura Hari, the son of Shachi. He hastened from Mathura to
Mayapur-Navadvipa, which is not different from Mathura, and arrived there after the Lord had returned from Gaya. He stayed
in the house of the mahabhagavata Nandana Acharya[^19] and lived there incognito; Nandana, seeing a radiant avadhuta with
arms reaching to His knees, fed Him with reverence and kept Him in his home, and, given the chance to serve Him, considered
his life fulfilled. At different times Mahaprabhu, Nityananda and Advaita all took refuge in Nandana's house.
<!-- F135 F137 F138 F139 -->

The all-knowing Mahaprabhu knew of His coming and hinted to the Vaishnavas that in two or three days a great personage
would arrive. He learned of it in a dream. To His door came the chariot of Baladeva with its palm-tree banner
(tala-dhvaja); in it sat a huge man with an enormous pole — a club or a plough — on his shoulder[^20], in blue garments, with a cane kamandalu in
his left hand. A resident of Vraja, he kept asking in Vraja-buli, “Is this the house of Nimai Pandita?” — and when asked
who he was, answered with a smile, “I am your brother; tomorrow we shall know each other.” And Mahaprabhu felt as though
He too were that very one. Telling the dream, Gaura entered the mood of Baladeva, roared and began to demand “wine”;
Srivasa replied that the wine of Hari-rasa is with Him alone, and only he to whom He gives it will receive it[^21]. Returning
to external consciousness, Gaura Himself explained the meaning of the dream to the Vaishnavas: someone truly great had
come to Navadvipa[^22].
<!-- F140 F141 F142 F143 -->

Mahaprabhu announced the guest's coming to the devotees and sent Srivasa and Haridasa to look for Him. For three praharas
they searched the town — the houses of Vaishnavas, sannyasis, householders, even of the godless; only the villages outside
Navadvipa they did not search — and did not find Him. Gaura smiled and hinted:
only one to whom Sri Chaitanya Himself reveals Nityananda can recognise Him. And the Lord with His devotees went straight to the house
of Nandana Acharya[^23]. <!-- F144 F145 F146 F147 -->

There they saw One who shone like a million suns. According to the “Bhakti-ratnakara,” He was sitting in meditation, and
all gazed on His incomparable beauty; and the Two of Them looked long and steadily at each other. Seeing that Nityananda
remained silent, Mahaprabhu told Srivasa to recite the verse of the “Bhagavatam” about Krishna's entering Vrindavana:

“With an ornament of peacock feathers, in the form of the best of dancers, with karnikara flowers at His ears, in garments
the colour of gold and with a vaijayanti garland, He filled the holes of the flute with the nectar of His lips and entered
Vrindavana, made beautiful by His footprints, while His cowherd friends sang His glory.” (Bhag. 10.21.5)

On hearing the verse Nityananda fell senseless, and all eight spiritual transformations appeared in Him. The verse was
recited again and again; coming to, Nityananda wept for joy, and the madness of love grew ever greater. His roar seemed to
split the universe; He fell to the ground so that His bones seemed to break, and the Vaishnavas cried out in fear,
“Krishna, protect Him!”; He rolled on the ground in tears, never taking His eyes from Gaura's face, danced, fell
prostrate, leapt — and the Vaishnavas could not hold Him. Then Vishvambhara took Him on His lap and pressed Him to His
breast, as brother to brother — and He grew calm, and all made the air ring with the name of Hari.
<!-- F148 F149 F150 F151 Q22 -->

Mahaprabhu glorified Him before the devotees: “Today I have seen with My own eyes prema-bhakti-yoga — the essence of the
four Vedas. You are the embodied wealth of love for Krishna. Whoever stays with You even for a moment, even for half a
sesame seed of time, cannot be called wicked, even with millions of sins; worship of You gives the treasure of
krishna-prema.” When Gaura asked where He had come from, Nityananda, like a child, told Him everything: He had seen all
the places of Krishna's lilas, but had not seen Krishna Himself; great Vaishnavas had told Him that Krishna had gone to
Gauda-desha, had lately been in Gaya and had come to Nadia; hearing that Krishna, the Saviour of the fallen, had appeared
in Nadia with sankirtana, “this sinner” had come here. Gaura replied, “We are all fortunate — a great bhagavata has
appeared here.” <!-- F152 F153 F154 -->

The Vaishnavas each spoke in their own way of the two Lords. Murari: “These are Your affairs; we cannot understand them.”
Srivasa: “What can we understand? They honour each other like Madhava and Shankara.” Gadadhara: “Well said; it is like
Rama and Lakshmana”[^24]. <!-- F155 Q24 F156 -->

## The Vyasa-puja in Srivasa's House
A few days later Mahaprabhu wished Nityananda to perform the Vyasa-puja. “Tomorrow is the full moon, the day of the
Vyasa-puja,” said Gaura. “Where will You perform it?” Nityananda understood His intention, took Srivasa by the hand and led
him forward: “In the house of this brahmana.” Srivasa joyfully took the whole puja upon himself. At Mahaprabhu's bidding
the sankirtana-adhivasa was held: Nitai and Gaura entered Srivasa's house-temple and had the outer door locked, so that only
their own people could enter — and countless waves and signs of love appeared. That night Mahaprabhu revealed that
Nityananda is Baladeva, and made it known that He Himself had descended for the sake of Advaita Acharya[^25].
<!-- F158 F159 F160 -->

Gaura calmed Nityananda and went home for the night, but Nityananda stayed at Srivasa's — and in the night, with a roar,
He broke His staff (danda) and kamandalu. In the morning Mahaprabhu learned of it from Ramai Pandita, Srivasa's brother;
He came, took Nityananda to bathe in the Ganga, laid the danda in the river and told Him to perform the Vyasa-puja without
delay. <!-- F161 F162 -->

The acharya of the Vyasa-puja was Srivasa. He placed in Nityananda's hand a garland of forest flowers of wonderful
fragrance, so that He might offer it to Vyasadeva with a hymn. Nityananda answered aloud, “Yes, yes,” — but then only
murmured something indistinct, holding the garland and looking about Him. Srivasa complained, “Your Shripada is not performing the Vyasa-puja!” Mahaprabhu came up and urged
Him on — and the ever-independent Nityananda, seeing Him before Him, placed the garland around the neck of Mahaprabhu
Himself. At once Mahaprabhu showed Him His six-armed form — with the club and plough of Balarama — and Nityananda fainted
from love. <!-- F163 F164 F165 -->

“Nityananda, be calm,” said Gaura. “The purpose of Your incarnation — the spreading of kirtana — is fulfilled. You are the
keeper of the treasury of prema-bhakti: only if You give will people receive prema.” Later, explaining why the Lord once
did not feel prema, Advaita would say, “You made Nityananda the keeper of the treasury of prema, and You deprived Srivasa
and me of it and gave it away to everyone, even the low-born.” At the end Mahaprabhu declared the Vyasa-puja complete and
told all to sing the name of Hari; Nitai and Gaura danced together, and Shachi secretly watched them both with a mother's
love. Mahaprabhu took prasada from Srivasa and distributed it to all Himself, even to Srivasa's servants. So, together
with Nityananda, Mahaprabhu began the lilas of sankirtana, and their centre became Srivasa's courtyard, Srivasa-angana; the
Lord, Nityananda and Their companions were immersed in the ecstasy of kirtana. <!-- F166 F167 F168 F136 -->

## In Srivasa's House and at Shachi's
Nityananda settled in Srivasa's house in Mayapur, and Malini-devi, Srivasa's wife, served Him like her own son. Once Gaura
tested Srivasa: “It is not good to keep close company with Nityananda — neither His family nor His character is known. If
you want to keep your caste, let Him go.” Srivasa smiled: “Do not test me. Nityananda is Your body, and in God there is no
difference between the body and the One within it.” Even if Nityananda should drink wine and keep company with a Yavana
woman, he said, even if He should ruin my caste, my wealth and my life — my faith will not waver (CB, Madhya 8.15–16)[^26].

“Hearing these words from Srivasa's lips, the Lord sprang up, gave a loud triumphant cry and pressed Srivasa to His
breast.” (CB, Madhya 8.17)

“‘O Srivasa Pandita, what are you saying? Where does such deep faith in Nityananda come from? You have recognised in
Nityananda My closest companion. I am very pleased with you and wish to grant you a blessing.’” (CB, Madhya 8.18–19)

And the Lord blessed him without any request: there will be no want in his house even if Lakshmi Herself goes begging
through the towns; even the cats and dogs in his house will gain firm devotion.

“‘I entrust Nityananda to you. Take care of all His needs.’” (CB, Madhya 8.22)[^27] <!-- F169 F170 F171 Q20 Q21 -->

Much later, when Srivasa's son died during kirtana, Mahaprabhu said to him, “From this day Nityananda and I are your sons;
We shall never leave you; remember this — and you will know no grief for your son.” Chaitanya and Nityananda were in debt
to Srivasa for his devotion and service: so the Lord becomes the debtor of His devotees. <!-- F172 I11 -->

Shachi had a dream: Gaura and Nitai are Kanai and Balai of Vraja Themselves; Nitai called her “mother” and asked for food.
She invited Nitai and fed Him together with Nimai. The Lord would beg His mother to let Him invite Nitai and, having her
consent, would go to call Him Himself. Ishana Thakura served Nityananda too, Chaitanya's “other Self”: before the meal at
Shachi's he brought water to wash Their feet (CB, Madhya 8.59), and, as Srila Bhaktisiddhanta says, he gathered the rice
Nityananda had scattered about the house and put things in order. <!-- F173 F174 -->

Nityananda lived in Navadvipa like a child. Once a crow carried off from Srivasa's house a pot of ghee meant for the
worship of Vishnu; Malini, fearing her husband's anger, wept — and Nityananda told the crow to bring the pot back, and it
returned it at once. Once, while Gaura was accepting Vishnupriya's service, Nityananda, without external consciousness,
entered the courtyard of His house naked and answered questions at random; Mahaprabhu Himself clothed Him, while Shachi
laughed at His childlike mood. Another time, naked and in the same childlike mood, He came to Gaura roaring, “My Lord
Nimai Pandita!” — and Gaura, laughing, wrapped Him in the cloth from His own head, anointed Him with fragrances, put a
garland on Him, seated Him and glorified Him. In the rainy season He swam fearlessly in the Ganga, full of crocodiles, and
at times lay senseless from love for three or four days[^28]. <!-- F175 F176 F177 F178 I23 -->

Gaura asked Nityananda for His kaupina, tore it to pieces and told the devotees to tie them on their heads, and at His
bidding the devotees drank the water that had washed Nityananda's feet — charanamrita, hard to obtain even for Shiva and
Brahma[^29]. <!-- F179 F157 -->

Murari Gupta, coming once to Srivasa's house, bowed first to Mahaprabhu and then to Nityananda; the Lord said that the
order was wrong. That night Murari saw Nityananda in a dream as Haladhara — dressed as a wrestler, with plough and club in His
hands, and the hoods of a great serpent swaying above His head; behind Him stood Vishvambhara, fanning Him. “Do you
understand now, Murari?” the Lord smiled. “You must consider Me the younger.” From then on Murari bowed first to
Nityananda[^30]:

“Lotus-eyed Mahaprabhu sat on His seat, and at His right sat Nityananda, laughing.” (CB, Madhya 20.22)

“Murari first offered obeisances at the lotus-like feet of Nityananda, and then bowed down at the lotus-like feet of
Vishvambhara.” (CB, Madhya 20.23)

So through Murari the Lord taught the truth about Himself and Nityananda, and the etiquette. Murari was a worshipper of
Balarama, and Srila Bhaktisiddhanta explains: to worship the Supreme Lord without first bowing to the guru and
jagad-guru, that is, to Nityananda, is a breach of etiquette. <!-- F185 F186 Q28 F445 -->

Nityananda was among the companions gathered at Srivasa's and at Chandrashekhara's (CB, Madhya 8.110–112); and when
Mahaprabhu drank water from Shridhara's leaky pitcher, Nityananda and Advaita led the kirtana in the courtyard (BR
12.3136–3141). Once, after He had been worshipped in Srivasa's house, Nityananda, the saviour of the fallen, visited
Gangadasa Pandita — at that time He roamed Navadvipa in a childlike mood, playing with the boys. And in the drama at
Chandrashekhara's house He played an old cowherd woman — Radha's maternal grandmother; and when Mahaprabhu danced as
Adya-shakti, Nityananda fell senseless. <!-- F442 F187 F188 -->

## Advaita
Soon after the Vyasa-puja the Lord sent Ramai Pandita for Advaita with the message: the Lord of Goloka has appeared in
Navadvipa, and Nityananda has come with Him. Advaita came to Navadvipa and hid in the house of Nandana Acharya. Mahaprabhu,
understanding his intention, ascended the throne of Vishnu (vishnu-khattu) and revealed Advaita's thoughts to all;
Nityananda, at the Lord's sign, held the royal umbrella over Him, while the Vaishnavas served and sang hymns. At a new
bidding Ramai brought Advaita with his wife; Advaita bowed at Mahaprabhu's feet, and on seeing Nityananda glorified His
greatness in many ways. <!-- F180 F181 F182 F183 -->

When Mahaprabhu threw Himself into the Ganga, Nityananda and Haridasa leapt in after Him and pulled Him out; to these two
alone the Lord disclosed that He would hide in the house of Nandana Acharya. Before His sannyasa, Chaitanya and Nityananda
would from time to time come to Advaita's house in Shantipur, hold a festival and sing kirtana all day and all night.
Parameshvari Dasa sings in a pada (“Pada-kalpataru”): the Lord came to Advaita's temple and sat down; Nityananda and Advaita
sat beside Him and jokingly discussed the festival; Sita Thakurani came in smiling, and the Lord had the Vaishnavas called
and mridangas, karatalas, sandal paste and aguru made ready. <!-- F184 F189 -->

Once Mahaprabhu with Nityananda came unexpectedly to Shantipur, to Advaita, with whom Haridasa was living. The four of Them
bathed in the Ganga; Advaita prostrated himself at Mahaprabhu's feet, and Haridasa at Advaita's, “like three links of the
chain of a bridge,” — and Nityananda rejoiced. For several days They spoke of preaching the holy name, and then Mahaprabhu
with Nityananda and Haridasa returned to Navadvipa. Another time Advaita began to extol knowledge above bhakti, and the
Lord in anger dragged Him into the courtyard and began to punish Him. It was a lila: Advaita rejoiced at receiving
punishment from the Lord (CB, Madhya 19.152). Advaita's wife, Sita, begged Him to spare the old brahmana, while Nityananda
only smiled (CB, Madhya 19.135–138). Afterwards the Lord said to Nityananda, “If I showed agitation, forgive Me,” and They
looked at each other and laughed; the Lord bathed in the Ganga with Nityananda, Advaita and Haridasa, and Sita served Them
a meal. <!-- F450 F451 -->

## Preaching the Name; Jagai and Madhai
Mahaprabhu made Nityananda and Haridasa the first preachers of the holy name:

“‘Listen, Nityananda! Listen, Haridasa! Go and reveal My instructions to the whole world. Go to every house and ask: “Chant
the names of Krishna, worship Krishna and follow Krishna's instructions.” Speak of nothing else and ask for nothing more.
Come at the end of the day and tell Me everything.’” (CB, Madhya 13.8–10)

The people of Navadvipa mocked such preaching, but the two, strong in Chaitanya's command, paid no attention.
<!-- F190 F191 Q26 -->

In Navadvipa lived Jagai and Madhai — drunkards and robbers, great sinners born in a brahmana family; there was no vileness
they had not committed — only offence to Vaishnavas had not touched them. Seeing their fall, Nityananda was especially
merciful to them: His heart wept for them. He asked people about them and was fired with the wish to save them — then the
whole world would know the glory of the Lord. He disclosed His plan to Haridasa, and Haridasa answered, “Whatever You
desire is the Lord's desire.” Respectable people tried to dissuade Them, but They would not listen; They stood where the
brothers could hear Them and loudly delivered Mahaprabhu's command:

“Krishna has descended to grant you the highest good. Therefore give up your sinful life and worship Him alone.” (CB,
Madhya 13.84) <!-- F192 F193 Q27 F446 -->

The brothers, eyes bloodshot, rushed at Them in anger shouting “Seize them!”, and Nityananda and Haridasa ran without
looking back, crying, “Krishna! Govinda! Save us!”; old Haridasa could not keep up with Nityananda, but escaped: the drunken
pursuers stumbled and fell behind. The two came to Gaura and told Him everything, and Gangadasa and Srivasa explained to
the Lord who these brothers were. “If they come here, I shall tear them to pieces,” said the Lord. But Nityananda
answered, “Tear them You may, but while they are there I shall go nowhere any more. What are You so proud of? The pious
chant Krishna's name by themselves; first make these two say ‘Govinda’ — to save such as Jagai and Madhai is greater glory;
then the name ‘Saviour of the fallen’ will be Yours by right.” Vishvambhara smiled: “They were saved the moment they saw
You. To whomever Nityananda wishes good, Krishna has already granted good — there is no doubt of it.” And Haridasa
complained to Advaita that he would not go with Nityananda any more: His behaviour was reckless and unpredictable. But the
flight of the all-powerful Nityananda is a mysterious lila: for later He alone delivered them.
<!-- F194 F195 I2 F196 F446 -->

One night Nityananda came to the brothers on purpose, to save them. “Who are You?” — “I am going to the Lord's house.” —
“What is Your name?” — “My name is Avadhuta.” At that name Madhai in anger struck Him on the head with a shard of an
earthen pot; the head was split, blood flowed, but Nityananda only thought of Govinda. Jagai, taking pity on Him, held back
his brother's hand: “What is the use of beating a sannyasi?”[^31] People ran to the Lord, and Mahaprabhu, seeing the blood,
summoned the Sudarshana-chakra — the brothers saw it in terror; but Nityananda said, “When Madhai struck Me, Jagai saved
Me. Forgive them — give Me their bodies as alms.” Gaura embraced Jagai: “By protecting Nityananda you have bought Me; from
today you will obtain prema-bhakti,” — and showed him His four-armed form. Seeing the change in Jagai, Madhai too was changed
and fell at the Lord's feet; but the Lord said, “Your guilt is heavier — you have shed Nityananda's blood, and Nityananda's
body is higher than Mine; fall at His feet.” Madhai took shelter at Nityananda's feet, and He forgave him all his offences:
“If in all My life I have gathered even a little piety, I give it all to Madhai”; at the Lord's word He embraced Madhai,
entered into him and endowed him with every power. Both vowed to sin no more, took the shelter of Nitai-Gaura and became
mahabhagavatas, and Gaura had the victory of Nityananda's mercy proclaimed. <!-- F197 F198 F199 F200 F201 F203 F447 -->

But Madhai, remembering that he had shed Nityananda's blood, found no peace. Finding Him alone, he held His feet and with
tears begged forgiveness, and Nityananda embraced him: “Does a father take offence at the slaps of his little son?” — and
told him to build a ghat on the Ganga and humbly bow to everyone who came to bathe. From then on Madhai every day, with
tears and pain, repeating “Krishna, Krishna,” served the ghat, prostrated himself before the Vaishnavas who came there and
begged forgiveness for his offences. For his severe austerity he was called Madhai-brahmachari, and the ghat is called
Madhai's ghat to this day (CB, Madhya 15.92–94). <!-- F202 F448 -->

## The “Dari” Sannyasi; the Kazi; the Universal Form
Once, on the way to Advaita's house, Nityananda and Gaura called on a certain sannyasi. Gaura bowed, and he blessed Him:
“May You have wealth, followers, beautiful women, learning.” Gaura objected: a sannyasi should bless otherwise — “may there
be Krishna's mercy, may there be bhakti”; that is the true blessing. The sannyasi ridiculed Nimai as a foolish boy.
Nityananda, seeing that such a seeker of enjoyment would not accept a good word, outwardly honoured him and “deceived”
him: “We must go on business — give us bhiksha.” At the host's request both bathed in the Ganga and there accepted
offerings to Krishna — milk, mangoes, jackfruit. <!-- F204 F205 -->

This sannyasi was a vamachari, a follower of the “left-hand path” — a “dari-sannyasi,” living with a woman — and he drank
wine. Not understanding Who stood before him, he offered, “Shripada, shall I bring You some ‘ananda’?” Nityananda explained
to Gaura that the vamacharis call wine “ananda,” and Gaura, crying “Vishnu! Vishnu!”, leapt into the Ganga and swam to
Advaita's house. To deliver the fallen dari-sannyasi, Nityananda displayed the lila of taking a meal in his house. But
blasphemers of Vaishnavas and Mayavadis, arrogant offenders of Vishnu and the Vaishnavas, are deprived of the mercy even
of this best of saviours: the dari-sannyasi may come to good, if not in this life then in another, the Mayavadi sannyasi —
never. Vrindavana Dasa says:

“Although this sannyasi drank wine and kept close company with women, the Lord visited his house.” (CB, Madhya 19.96)

“Even if this sannyasi does not attain perfection in this life, he will attain it in the next. But blasphemers will never
attain perfection.” (CB, Madhya 19.98)

The witnesses of this, he adds, are the sannyasis of Kashi, who cannot see the Lord (CB, Madhya 19.99)[^32].
<!-- F206 F207 Q23 I22 -->

In Advaita's house he joyfully received Gaura and Nityananda; after a meal cooked by Sita, Nityananda in a childlike mood
scattered rice all over the house, and Advaita, feigning anger, revealed to the devotees the greatness of the avadhuta.
<!-- F208 -->

At Mahaprabhu's bidding Nityananda went from town to town preaching the kirtana of Hari's name. The Kazi, hearing of the
preaching and the noise of the kirtana, broke a mridanga; the hostile Hindus of Navadvipa supported him, called Nimai and
Nitai “cheats” and said, “Soon the Kazi will stop Nityananda's street kirtana.” Then Gaura with Nityananda and all the
Vaishnavas went out in a sankirtana procession through Navadvipa and subdued the Kazi. Nityananda danced in the procession beside the Lord;
the order of the companies one biography gives in different ways: now Haridasa in front, Advaita in the middle and
Gaurachandra Himself behind, now three companies led by Advaita, Haridasa and Srivasa. And before the procession, at the
kirtana behind closed doors, Nityananda and Gadadhara whirled hand in hand, and Advaita, like a lion, rushed about the
room (CB, Madhya 23.29–30)[^33]. <!-- F209 F210 F449 -->

Once, while Nityananda was walking about Nadia, Mahaprabhu revealed His universal form in Srivasa's house. Nityananda
inwardly knew it, came at once to Srivasa's door and roared, and Gaura showed Him His form containing countless universes.
<!-- F211 -->

## The Lord's Sannyasa and Shantipur
The hostile students (paduya) of Navadvipa were piling up offences, reviling Gaura and plotting violence. For their own
good Mahaprabhu resolved to take sannyasa and told the devotees of it in a riddle (hiyali); only Nityananda understood (according to another account
of the same biography, Lokanatha was the first to learn of the coming sannyasa, while Nityananda, the Lord's “second
Self,” learned of it only a day before). He grieved that Gaura's beautiful hair would be gone; Gaura explained to Him in private the reason for sannyasa, and
Nityananda did not stand in the way but told the devotees, and, thinking of Shachi's separation, was stunned. According to
the “Chaitanya-mangala,” when Shachi and Vishnupriya fell senseless, Nityananda tried to console them, and then devotees
led by Chandrashekhara Acharya and Damodara Pandita followed Nityananda to Katwa. <!-- F212 F213 F214 -->

At Katwa with the Lord were Nityananda, Chandrashekhara Acharya (Acharyaratna), Mukunda Datta, Gadadhara and Brahmananda;
they performed all the rites[^34]. When the barber touched Gaura's curls with the razor, Nityananda fell to the ground and
wept. <!-- F215 F216 -->

After sannyasa the Lord wandered for three days through Rarh, intoxicated with love for Krishna, singing the song of the
tridandi-bhikshu of Avanti, and going, as it seemed to Him, to Vrindavana; Nityananda followed Him. He taught the cowherd
boys to point the Lord the way to Navadvipa as the way to Vrindavana — and so, wishing to show Him to the people of
Navadvipa and bring Him to Shantipur, by a ruse He led Him to the Ganga, opposite Shantipur:

“But those children directed Him toward the Ganga. Intoxicated with spiritual bliss, He went there.” (CC, Madhya 3.19)

Meanwhile Nityananda told Chandrashekhara to go quickly to Advaita Acharya's house:

“I shall bring Sri Chaitanya to Advaita's house. But let Advaita wait for us with a boat on the bank of the Ganga.” (CC,
Madhya 3.21)

“Then go to Navadvipa for mother Shachi and all the devotees.” (CC, Madhya 3.22) <!-- F217 F218 F219 Q29 -->

So the Lord came to Shantipur and met there Shachi and the devotees of Navadvipa; the “Chaitanya-mangala” also describes
Nityananda's conversation with Shachi. In Advaita's house Mahaprabhu and Nityananda ate Sita's enormous feast on whole
banana leaves, while Advaita joked and talked with Them. At the kirtana Advaita danced holding Nityananda, and Haridasa behind Them.
After His sannyasa the Lord stayed ten days in Shantipur. <!-- F220 F221 F222 F452 -->

## The Way to Puri; the Broken Staff
At His mother's request that He settle in Puri, the Lord went from Shantipur through Chhatrabhoga to Nilachala. With Him
went Nityananda, Mukunda Datta, Jagadananda, Damodara Pandita, Gadadhara Pandita, Govinda and Brahmananda[^35]. At Remuna,
before Kshira-chora Gopinatha, the Lord told His companions the story of Madhavendra Puri — as Ishvara Puri had told it to
Him — and when, reciting Madhavendra's verse “ayi dīna-dayārdra nātha,” He fainted from love, Nityananda caught
Him and laid Him on His lap (CC, Madhya 4.197–199). At Cuttack Nityananda told the story of Sakshi-Gopala. Mahaprabhu and Nityananda also passed through
Bansdaha (Vamshadaha) near Jaleshvara (CB, Antya 2.263–264). <!-- F223 F224 F225 F226 F227 F228 -->

From Cuttack the Lord went to Bhuvaneshvara, for darshana of Shiva, and then to Kamalapur on the bank of the Bhargi. On
the way the Lord's danda was carried by Jagadananda; going for alms, he left it with Nityananda:

“‘Please look after the Lord's danda. I shall gather alms and be back in a few minutes.’” (CB, Antya 2.204)

And Nityananda broke the danda into three pieces and set them floating down the river[^36]. By this He showed that,
though outwardly accepting the ekadanda, Mahaprabhu had in truth accepted the tridanda — yet Gaura needs no tridanda
either: He is the Lord even of avadhuta-paramahamsas, who bear no signs of varnashrama at all[^37].
<!-- F229 F230 F231 Q30 -->

The Lord was upset at the loss of His only possession and said that from here He would go alone — His companions might go
ahead or behind; they chose behind. Reaching the Lion Gate of the temple, Nityananda, Mukunda, Jagadananda and Damodara
learned that the Lord had been carried unconscious to Sarvabhauma's house. Gopinatha Acharya led them there, and they saw
Gaurahari in a frenzy of love. Sarvabhauma bowed to Nityananda and sent them for darshana of Jagannatha with his son
Chandaneshvara; their loud kirtana brought the Lord back to consciousness, and then Sarvabhauma fed them maha-prasada.
<!-- F232 F233 -->

Nityananda, intoxicated with the rasa of Chaitanya, rushed to embrace Jagannatha. And once He climbed onto the golden
throne, embraced the Deity of Balarama and put His garland on Himself. <!-- F234 F235 -->

## In Puri
When He set out for the South, the Lord took none of His intimates with Him. Nityananda offered to go along — He knew
the roads to the holy places of the South — but Mahaprabhu declined and, as though reproaching Nityananda, Jagadananda,
Mukunda and Damodara, in fact praised their love:

“After accepting sannyasa I set out for Vrindavana, but You brought Me to the house of Advaita Acharya.” (CC, Madhya 7.19)

“On the way to Jagannatha Puri You broke My danda. All of you love Me boundlessly, but such deeds stand in the way of My
plans.” (CC, Madhya 7.20)

Still, at Nityananda's request the Lord took a servant with Him — a simple brahmana named Krishnadasa[^38] — to
carry His kaupina, upper cloth and waterpot; Nityananda Himself arranged it. <!-- F236 F237 F454 -->

Returning from the South, the Lord sent Krishnadasa ahead from Alalanatha to inform Nityananda and the others. Nityananda
could not wait:

“Hearing of Mahaprabhu's imminent arrival, Nityananda Raya at once hurried to meet Him, unable to bear the waiting, so
full was He of the feelings of divine love.” (CC, Madhya 9.339)

Jagadananda, Damodara and Mukunda danced with joy all along the road, and at the meeting the waves of love surged again.
Soon the same Krishnadasa carried to Gauda, to Navadvipa, the news of the Lord's return[^39]. <!-- F238 F239 Q31 -->

Life in Puri took its course. When the Lord was distributing prasada to the devotees Himself and not eating, and they
would not eat without Him, Svarupa Damodara asked Him to sit down to the meal together with Nityananda. Svarupa was very
dear to both Advaita and Nityananda: arriving in Puri and receiving the Lord's embrace, he first of all bowed to
Nityananda and Paramananda Puri. Svarupa Damodara, Raya Ramananda and Haridasa Thakura all honoured Nityananda's feet in
Nilachala. <!-- F240 F241 -->

King Prataparudra longed to see the Lord, but Mahaprabhu, a sannyasi, refused to meet a ruler. Sarvabhauma advised not to
ask Him directly, and Nityananda simply told the Lord of the king's resolve:

“You may think it proper or improper, but the fact is that King Prataparudra has resolved to become a mendicant beggar if
he cannot meet You.” (CC, Madhya 12.19)

“He is ready to put on the ivory earrings of renunciation and give up his kingdom, which has become hateful to him without
Your mercy.” (CC, Madhya 12.20)

The Lord remained unbending, pointing to the strict Damodara Pandita. Then Nityananda said: those rejected in love take
their own lives, — and asked that, to save the king, He at least give a worn upper cloth (bahirvasa). The Lord could not
refuse Him. Nityananda took the cloth from Govinda and sent it to the king through Sarvabhauma; the moment he touched it,
Prataparudra felt love for God and began to worship it as the Lord Himself[^40]. <!-- F242 F243 Q32 -->

In the festivals of Nilachala Nityananda was always beside the Lord. When the Gundicha temple was cleaned, Mahaprabhu did
not give heavy work to His elders:

“All except Nityananda, Advaita, Damodara, Bharati and Paramananda Puri filled pots and carried water.” (CC, Madhya
12.109)

At Gundicha, where kirtana went on three times a day, the Lord made now Advaita dance, now Nityananda, now Haridasa,
Achyutananda, Vakreshvara and others. At the Ratha-yatra He anointed His companions with sandal paste with His own hand
and adorned them with garlands:

“Advaita Acharya and Nityananda, too, felt immense happiness when the Lord's hand touched Them.” (CC, Madhya 13.31)

In the second kirtana party, led in song by Srivasa, the dancer was Nityananda (CC, Madhya 13.38–39); the Lord told
Nityananda, Advaita, Haridasa and Vakreshvara each to dance in one of four parties. The dancing Lord was surrounded by three
rings of guards: the inner one led by Nityananda, the second by Kashishvara, Mukunda and others, the outer by Prataparudra
with his soldiers. And in the bera-kirtana, when the devotees circled the temple in seven parties, each had its own
dancer — Advaita, Nityananda, Vakreshvara, Achyutananda, Srivasa, Satyaraja Khan and Narahari — and the Lord passed from
party to party so that each felt He was with it alone (CC, Antya 10.58–61). <!-- F244 F245 F246 F247 F248 F249 Q33 -->

Though Mahaprabhu now lived in Puri, in Gauda He still manifested Himself wherever He was loved:

“The Lord is always attracted by divine love, for such is His nature. Therefore Sri Chaitanya Mahaprabhu always
manifested Himself in four places: in the home temple of Mother Shachi, wherever Nityananda Prabhu dances, in the kirtana
of Srivasa Thakura and in the house of Raghava Pandita.” (CC, Antya 2.34)

Nityananda Himself came every year with the devotees of Bengal to Puri and spent the four months of the rains with the
Lord. <!-- F250 F251 Q34 -->

On one of these visits Nityananda, having been to Jagannatha and shown there wondrous transformations of love, went to
Tota, to the house of Gadadhara Pandita, for darshana of Gopinatha. Gadadhara, learning that Nityananda was in Puri, left
off reading the “Bhagavata” and came out to meet Him. Nityananda had brought from Gauda fine rice worthy of the Deity and a
beautiful coloured cloth for Gopinatha's service; Gadadhara cooked the rice with greens and vegetables from the garden of
Yameshvara. Mahaprabhu, too, came to the offering: praising Nityananda's rice and Gadadhara's cooking, He honoured
Gopinatha's prasada, and the three ate together in joy, while the devotees afterwards snatched up the remnants of
Nitai-Gaura-Gadadhara's meal (CB, Antya 7). <!-- F252 F253 F254 -->

When Rupa and Sanatana came to Puri, Mahaprabhu arranged for them to meet Advaita and Nityananda and asked Them to bless
the brothers. <!-- F255 -->

## The Command to Preach in Gauda
One day Mahaprabhu, in private, told Nityananda to leave Puri with His companions and tirelessly preach pure devotion —
prema-bhakti — in Gauda, delivering the low-born, the ignorant and the fallen. And He said that He Himself, ever remaining
unseen, would watch Nityananda's dance: Nityananda's dance is one of the places where the Lord eternally manifests
Himself (see above). <!-- F261 F264 -->

With Nityananda the Lord sent His own devotees:

“When Sri Chaitanya sent Nityananda to Bengal, He ordered these two devotees to go with Him.” (CC, Adi 11.14)

These were Ramadasa (Abhirama) and Gadadhara Dasa; Gadadhara Dasa became one of Nityananda's chief helpers in preaching
sankirtana. According to the “Chaitanya-bhagavata” (Antya 5.231–233), there left Puri with Nityananda Ramadasa, Gadadhara
Dasa, Raghunatha Vaidya-Ojha, Krishnadasa Pandita, Parameshvari Dasa and Purandara Pandita[^41]. One of the biographies
dates this command to the year 1434 of the Shaka era[^42]. <!-- F262 F263 F265 -->

Before setting out, Nityananda empowered His companions, filling them with love of God:

“So Lord Nityananda and all His companions set out, overwhelmed by the feeling of divine love.” (CB, Antya 5.234)

“They forgot themselves in it completely. There was no end or limit to the signs of divine ecstasy that appeared in their
bodies.” (CB, Antya 5.235)

On the way each lived in his own bhava. Gadadhara Dasa, forgetting himself like a girl of Vraja selling yogurt, laughed
loudly and cried “Who will buy yogurt!” — and Nityananda noticed it. Abhirama, in the mood of a cowherd boy, stood nine
hours in the tribhanga pose, bent in three places, unaware of the outer world (CB, Antya 5.237). Krishnadasa and
Parameshvari shouted “hoi! hoi!” like the cowherd boys of Vraja, and Purandara climbed a tree crying “I am Angada!” and
leapt down. <!-- F266 F267 Q35 -->

Coming to Gauda, Nityananda went all over it as a wandering parivrajaka, preaching the Name and love of God. Whomever He
met, He took a straw between His teeth and begged: “Buy Me — worship Gaurahari.” The avadhuta wandered for two tasks:
preaching prema and subduing the godless. Lochana Dasa, who in the “Chaitanya-mangala” glorified more his guru Narahari
as the Lord's dearest companion and wrote little of Nityananda's glory, later, fearing to offend Him, composed several
songs about Him that devotees especially love. In one (“nitāi guṇa-maṇi…”) he sings that Nitai brought a flood of prema
to Gauda, passing over neither the downtrodden nor the sinners, and went from house to house giving out a love
unattainable even for Brahma — whoever has not worshipped Nitai ruins himself. In another (“akrodha paramānanda…”) — that
Nityananda, knowing neither anger nor pride, roams the town with a blade of grass between His teeth, asks everyone “buy
Me — worship Gaura Hari” and falls to the ground like a golden mountain in the dust; whoever has not become attached to
such an avatara is a sinner. And in the song “parama karuṇa pahun dui jana…” Lochana glorifies the two Lords, Nitai and
Gaurachandra, boundlessly merciful, the essence of all avataras: “Brother, worship Chaitanya and Nitai with firm faith” —
in the three worlds there is none like Them, and Their mercy makes even birds and beasts weep. <!-- F268 Q36 Q37 Q38 F398 -->

## Ramachandra Khan
Ramachandra Khan, the zamindar of Benapol (Jessore district), hated the Vaishnavas fiercely. Out of envy he sent a young
prostitute to Haridasa Thakura to ruin his dharma; by Haridasa's mercy she became a great devotee, and Khan's envy only
grew (CC, Antya 3). <!-- F269 -->

On His preaching round Nityananda came to him too. As the Inner Controller, He knew of his offences against Haridasa: He
is most merciful, but with the offender of a Vaishnava He is angry. Nityananda sat down in Khan's durga-mandapa and
asked for shelter. The proud man sent word through his people: He could stay with His party in the cowshed — there was
no other place. Nityananda, still more displeased, left at once, saying: “This house is not for Vaishnavas — it is fit
only for mlecchas who kill cows.” Thus Nityananda Raya, “never angry, supreme bliss” (akrodha paramānanda, as Lochana
Dasa sings of Him), showed a lila of anger towards the enemy of the Vaishnavas and left both his house and his village.
<!-- F270 F271 F272 -->

Khan had a servant dig up the earth where Nityananda had sat and sprinkle the place with water mixed with cow dung. The
fruit of the offence was not long in coming: soon a vizier of the Muslim ruler, a tax collector, took up quarters in that
same durga-mandapa; he bound and carried off Khan with his wife and sons, plundered Benapol for three days and for three
days cooked forbidden meat in Khan's house. Khan lost his caste, his wealth and his people, and his house and village were
ruined. Though Nityananda is most merciful, He does not forgive the offender of a Vaishnava, and a place where a great
devotee is offended perishes. <!-- F273 F274 I1 -->

## Panihati: Raghava Pandita
Wandering along the banks of the Ganga, Nityananda, drawn by the pure devotion of Raghava Pandita, came to Panihati.
According to the “Bhakti-ratnakara” (8.156–158), Ramadasa and Gadadhara Dasa were with Him, and first of all He visited
Raghava's house; one of the biographies also names the three Ghosha brothers. The kirtana begun there filled the universe
with joy. Nityananda flooded Raghava's house with love, delighting Raghava, Makaradhvaja Kara and others; when the Ghoshas
began to sing, He danced in trance to the singing of Madhava Ghosha — the finest kirtaniya on earth. <!-- F275 F276 -->

Then He sat on the throne of Vishnu, and Raghava and his companions performed His abhisheka according to the shastras,
dressed Him and adorned Him with a garland; He sat on the throne again, and Raghava held an umbrella over Him (according to one account, Makaradhvaja Kara). In trance
Nityananda told Raghava to bring at once a garland of kadamba flowers. Raghava objected that the kadamba was not yet in
bloom; Nityananda told him to look near the house — and Raghava found kadamba flowers on a lemon (jambira) tree and
garlanded Him[^43]. Soon the house filled with the fragrance of damanaka flowers, and Nityananda explained: Mahaprabhu
Himself had come from Nilachala wearing a garland of damanaka to hear the kirtana and watch the dance (BR 12.3645–3647).
<!-- F277 F278 F279 -->

He told everyone to give up their affairs, forget body and home, and sing Krishna's name without cease:

“Therefore you should give up all your other occupations and immerse yourselves in the unceasing glorification of Lord
Krishna.” (CB, Antya 5.298)

“By constantly glorifying Sri Krishna Chaitanyachandra you will taste the sweetness of divine love of God in all its
fullness.” (CB, Antya 5.299) <!-- F280 Q39 -->

Nityananda stayed in Panihati three months. With His companions He would come to eat in Raghava's house, and all praised
his cooking; and the Lord, taking Raghava aside, explained to him His oneness with Nityananda — “another form of
Balarama”: “Sri Nityananda is My second body; serve Him with care — and you will easily obtain even what is rarely
given by serving the great lord of yoga”; and Makaradhvaja Kara He told to serve Raghava with love — that is service to
Him Himself. It is also said that Nityananda gave Panihati over to Kamalakara Pippalai[^44]. <!-- F281 F282 F283 Q60 F455 -->

## The Chida-dadhi Festival
Hearing that Nityananda was in Panihati, young Raghunatha Dasa decided: by the mercy of the saviour of the fallen he
would gain his freedom. With his father's permission he crossed the Ganga and found Nityananda seated under a tree on a
rise by the river amid His companions, to the sound of kirtana; seeing Him from afar, Raghunatha fell flat. Nityananda
was told who it was. <!-- F284 -->

“Hearing this, Nityananda said: ‘Oh! This thief has come to meet Me again. Come here to Me, then, don't be afraid. Today I
shall punish you.’” (CC, Antya 6.47)

Nityananda drew him to Himself, placed His feet on his head and announced the “punishment”:

“‘You do not come close, but keep your distance like a thief. But I have caught you and shall punish you today.” (CC,
Antya 6.50)

“I want you to feed all My companions yogurt and flat rice.’ Hearing these words, Raghunatha became inexpressibly happy.”
(CC, Antya 6.51)

So, understanding the young man's innermost desires, He assigned him service to the Vaishnavas. Raghava Pandita added
foods that had not touched boiled rice and watched in wonder this “picnic” of Nityananda's by the river. Present at the
festival were Gadadhara Dasa, Mahesha Pandita and Jagadisha Pandita[^45]. <!-- F285 F286 F287 -->

Mahaprabhu Himself came too[^46]. Nityananda and Mahaprabhu ate on the bank of the Ganga as the cowherd boys on the bank
of the Yamuna, and with Them ate Their companions, many brahmanas and countless men and women. In the afternoon
Nityananda went to Raghava's house and spent the evening in kirtana and dance; drawn by His dancing, Mahaprabhu came again,
and to show Raghava's good fortune They sat together and accepted his meal — fine rice, vegetables, cakes, sweet rice.
(According to one account, the prasada Raghava had brought Nityananda set aside for the night.) The remnants of
Mahaprabhu's meal — according to the same account, of both brothers — Raghava gave to Raghunatha. <!-- F288 F289 F455 -->

Next day Raghunatha asked through Raghava how he might attain Mahaprabhu's feet. Nityananda placed His feet on his head
and answered:

“Gaurachandra Himself came to save you. From now on everything that hindered and bound you is removed by His sweet
will.” (CC, Antya 6.141)

“Sri Chaitanya will entrust you to the care and protection of His closest servant, Svarupa Damodara. Thus you will gain
the shelter of His lotus feet.” (CC, Antya 6.142)

“Go home with this assurance. And very soon you will gain, without any hindrance, the shelter of Sri Chaitanya
Mahaprabhu.” (CC, Antya 6.143)

On Raghava's advice Raghunatha gave a generous dakshina to Nityananda and His companions and, having received their
blessing, considered his life fulfilled. This festival is celebrated in Panihati to this day — as the
chida-dadhi-mahotsava, the festival of flat rice with yogurt. <!-- F290 F291 F292 Q40 -->

## Through Gauda: Gadadhara Dasa, Saptagrama, Shantipur
Once Nityananda wished to wear ornaments — and at once countless ornaments appeared; putting them on, He wandered in the
dress of a cowherd (gopa-vesha). So He went round the homes of devotees in the villages on both banks of the Ganga,
spreading kirtana everywhere. <!-- F293 F294 -->

In the house of Gadadhara Dasa He found the master in the bhava of a gopi: with a pot of Ganga water on his head he was
crying “Who will buy milk!” Seeing this, Nityananda took from his temple the Deity of Bala-Gopala whom Gadadhara Dasa
served, and danced, pressing Him to His chest. There, too, the dana-lila was played — the pastime of Krishna levying
“toll” on the gopis:

“Gadadhara Dasa was in bliss, overwhelmed by the feelings of a gopi. In his house Nityananda played in the drama
‘Danakeli.’” (CC, Adi 11.17)

Madhavananda Ghosha sang the song of the dana-khanda-lila, and Nityananda danced and sank into deep trance; that is why
the house of Gadadhara Dasa is called the place of the dana-lila[^47]. <!-- F295 F296 -->

Then Nityananda came to Mayapur to Shachi, spent a few days in Khardaha and set out with His companions for Saptagrama:

“Having spent a few days in Khardaha, Nityananda set out with His companions for Saptagrama.” (CB, Antya 5.443)

“In great bliss Nityananda bathed at that ghata together with His companions.” (CB, Antya 5.448)

“Lord Nityananda stayed on the bank of the Triveni in the house of the fortunate Uddharana Datta.” (CB, Antya 5.449)

“Uddharana Datta sincerely worshipped Nityananda's feet with body, mind and speech.” (CB, Antya 5.450) <!-- F297 Q41 -->

Nityananda accepted Uddharana's service and gladly ate what he cooked, won over by His devotee's love, and all around He
preached love for the Name. Uddharana's presence purified the whole caste of gold merchants; by Nityananda's mercy the
banik traders, though considered low and unlearned, attained prema-bhakti, and even a hostile yavana took shelter at His
feet. Sri Krishna Chaitanya and Nityananda wanted to show that a great devotee can appear in any family and caste —
therefore some of Their devotees were born in such families. By worshipping Uddharana and pleasing him one quickly gains
Nityananda's mercy, and through it love for Krishna. <!-- F298 F299 F300 I4 -->

One biography remarks with sorrow that later some born in the community Nityananda had delivered, calling themselves
devotees, came to oppose Him. <!-- F457 -->

Chaitanya and Nityananda delivered everyone — sinners and offenders alike. Ordinarily offences keep the holy name from
bearing fruit, but with Them it is otherwise:

“But such considerations do not apply to the names of Sri Chaitanya and Nityananda Prabhu. One who chants these holy
names will gradually be purified of all possible offences and, with tears in his eyes, will taste the nectar of divine
love for Krishna.” (CC, Adi 8.31)

Bhaktivinoda Thakura explains: whoever takes shelter of Chaitanya and Nityananda with faith is quickly purified of
offences. <!-- F301 Q48 -->

After the chida-dadhi festival Mahesha Pandita, too, followed Nityananda to Saptagrama. From there Nityananda came to
Shantipur and delighted in sankirtana with Advaita; by His mercy hundreds of villains, thieves and robbers attained the
good. <!-- F302 F303 -->

## The Robber Chief
In Navadvipa Nityananda was staying at that time in the house of Hiranya Pandita. The son of a Navadvipa brahmana had
become the chief of a robber band; coveting Nityananda's ornaments, he dogged His steps. <!-- F304 -->

On the first night the band surrounded with weapons the house where kirtana was going on and waited for everyone to fall
asleep — but by Nityananda's will it was the robbers who fell asleep. They woke to the cawing of crows, hid their weapons,
went to the Ganga and blamed one another. On the second night, after worshipping Chandi with wine and meat, they again
surrounded the house — and saw a host of armed guards singing the name of Hari; in amazement they did not dare go out to
their work for some ten days. On the third night, a very dark one, they entered the house — and all went blind: they fell
into pits of leftovers, onto thorns, into rubbish heaps, and on top of that a storm with hail broke out. <!-- F305 F306 F307 -->

Then the chief remembered Nityananda's inconceivable power, fell at His feet, begged forgiveness and glorified Him.
Nityananda placed His foot on his head; the offences of the chief and of the whole band were removed, and they attained
prema-bhakti. Through the influence of the former chief, thieves and robbers everywhere turned to righteousness and took
shelter of Gaura:

“They stopped stealing, harming others and doing immoral deeds, and their conduct became saintly.” (CB, Antya 5.697)

“Other avataras of the Supreme Lord do not give shelter so easily, but Nityananda always moved each and every one to
surrender to Lord Chaitanya.” (CB, Antya 5.700)

And Vrindavana Dasa ranks a brahmana who does not accept Nityananda no higher than a thief and robber — and such a one
Nityananda teaches through robbers (CB, Antya 5.701). <!-- F308 F309 Q42 -->

## A Brahmana's Doubt; Slander of the Avadhuta
Nityananda preached Gaura's name wearing ornaments and fragrant garlands, His lips red with betel. A brahmana from
Navadvipa, a former schoolfellow of Gaura and His devotee, came to doubt Nityananda and went with this to the Lord. Gaura
answered: the conduct of the highest, the uttama-adhikari, is not subject to the general rules and is not given for
imitation; whoever would imitate Rudra, who drank the kalakuta poison, will surely die; and if the highest adhikari
outwardly does something reprehensible, not only blasphemy of him but even mockery brings ruin[^28]. <!-- F310 F311 -->

“His deeds lie beyond the jurisdiction of any rules and injunctions. Who is able to understand Him?” (CB, Antya 6.118)

“‘Even if Sri Nityananda takes the hand of an outcaste woman or enters a wine shop, His lotus feet still remain an object
of worship even for Brahma.’” (CB, Antya 6.124)

“Strong faith in Nityananda arose in him. Then that brahmana returned home to Navadvipa.” (CB, Antya 6.126) <!-- Q43 -->

Bhaktivinoda Thakura in the “Sajjana-toshani” (8.9) rejects the judgments of the “adhyakshikas,” worldly judges: though
Nityananda was an avadhuta, never once in His life did He show bad conduct, and shame on those who call Him immoral.
People of bad conduct slander the acharya to pass off their vices as virtues. Many pseudo-Vaishnavas say that Nityananda
ate fish and meat, ascribe to Mahaprabhu dealings with women and count Him among the “nava-rasikas,” and slander Rupa
Gosvami and Ramananda. All this is the slander of the age of Kali, meant to justify one's own vices: the avadhuta
Nityananda is spotless. <!-- F312 Q63 I24 -->

## Shivananda Sena and the Last Coming to Puri
Every year it was Shivananda Sena who led the Bengali devotees to Puri: he paid the road tolls for everyone and knew the
way through Orissa well. Once he was delayed at the toll station, leaving everyone at the crossing, and without him no
lodging could be found — all waited under a tree. Nityananda, like a hungry cowherd boy of Vraja, acting out the pangs of
hunger, feigned anger and cursed: “Shiva has not come and found us lodging, and I am dying of hunger — may his three sons
die!” Shivananda's wife wept. But Shivananda, on arriving, told her: let all three die, since we have caused Nityananda
Prabhu inconvenience. <!-- F313 F314 F315 -->

“Having spoken thus, Shivananda went without delay to Nityananda. But Nityananda rose and kicked him.” (CC, Antya 12.24)

Shivananda took this blow as a sign of the greatest good fortune. He found lodging in a milkman's house, brought
Nityananda there and glorified Him:

“Under the pretext of punishment You have granted me Your mercy. Who can understand the nature and greatness of Your
deeds?” (CC, Antya 12.28)

“Even Brahma cannot touch the dust of Your beautiful feet. Yet with Your foot You have touched my body, full of sin.” (CC,
Antya 12.29)

Pleased, Nityananda embraced him. So Shivananda received the touch of His feet, unattainable even for Brahma. But
Shivananda's nephew Shrikanta was upset that Nityananda had kicked a companion of Mahaprabhu honoured by all, and went on
ahead to Puri alone; the Lord knew his thoughts. <!-- F316 F317 Q47 -->

This was Nityananda's last coming to Puri — in the year Haridasa Thakura departed. Mahaprabhu had forbidden Him to come
and told Him to preach in Gauda; that same year He sent Jagadananda to Navadvipa with news for His mother. But
Nityananda, having taken leave of Shachi, could not bear the separation and joined the Bengali pilgrims (CC, Antya 12).
<!-- F256 F257 -->

In Puri Nityananda stayed in a flower garden. Gaura came to Him alone and, circling Him, glorified Him with the words:
let Nityananda take the hand of a yavana woman or enter a wine shop — His feet are worthy of Brahma's worship[^48]. The
ornaments on Nityananda, Gaura said — gold, gems, pearls, diamonds, silver — are the nine kinds of bhakti themselves, and
the ignorant do not grasp it; Nityananda and His companions are the Baladeva of Vraja and His cowherd friends. And again
and again Gaura forbade Him to come to Nilachala: “Stay in Gauda and spread what I desire — there You will have My
eternal company.” <!-- F258 F259 F260 -->

## Other Homes and Places of Gauda
In Gopalpur Nityananda with His retinue stayed in the house of Raja Krishnananda Datta, Narottama's father, and blessed
him generously. In Shrikhanda, when Mahaprabhu and Nityananda, coming to the house of Narahari Sarakara, wished for honey,
Narahari turned the water of the pond by the house into honey; since then the pond has been called Madhu-pushkarini. At
Nupura-kunda near the shripata of Lavanga (Akai-hat, near Katwa), tradition says, an ankle bell fell — according to some,
that of Raghunandana, the son of Mukunda of Shrikhanda, according to others, that of Nityananda Prabhu.
<!-- F318 F319 F320 -->

As far as Ramakeli the Lord was accompanied by Nityananda — according to one account, with seven companions and
Haridasa. There Rupa and Sanatana, coming to the Lord by night, first met Nityananda and Haridasa:

“In the dead of night they came to the house of Sri Chaitanya. First they met Sri Nityananda and Thakura Haridasa.” (CC,
Madhya 1.183)

“And They informed Mahaprabhu that Rupa and Sanatana had come to see Him.” (CC, Madhya 1.184)

To show that a conditioned soul is saved by the mercy of devotees, the Lord told Nityananda, Haridasa, Srivasa, Gadadhara
and others to bless the brothers, and they touched the feet of His companions, among them the feet of Nityananda.
<!-- F321 F442 F458 -->

With the place called Prematali on the bank of the Padmavati is linked the tradition of the love left for Narottama: by one
version, Mahaprabhu, travelling in Bengal and having sent Nityananda to Puri, wept there in ecstasy and kept prema for
Narottama; by another (the “Prema-vilasa”), at Kanai-natshala the Lord in kirtana called out “Narottama!” and to
Nityananda's question replied: “You do not know Your own glory: in Puri You shed tears of love day after day; I have
gathered Your love and shall leave it in the keeping of the Padmavati for Narottama”; by a third, it was Nityananda
Himself who bathed at Prematali and left prema there for Narottama[^49]. <!-- F322 -->

According to the “Bhakti-ratnakara” (1.765–772), young Jiva Gosvami met Nityananda in Navadvipa, in Srivasa's house.
Nityananda with a father's tenderness touched his head with His feet, raised him, embraced him tightly and said in
ecstasy: “I rushed here from Khardaha only for your sake.” He told Srivasa and others to bless Jiva, kept him with Him for
some time, and then sent him west: “Hurry to Vraja — the Lord has given that place to your family.” By Nityananda's mercy
Jiva went round all the holy places of Navadvipa-dhama; in Bhaktivinoda Thakura's “Navadvipa-dhama-mahatmya” it is
Nityananda who guides him through Navadvipa. At Shridhara's house, He says there, the Lord used to stop the kirtana to let
the devotees rest — this is the “vishrama-sthana,” the place of rest — and He invites Jiva to rest there too.
<!-- F323 F409 Q61 -->

## Marriage: Vasudha and Jahnava
Nityananda, the avadhuta bound by no rules, in Gauda took on the role of a householder as well. Not all the biographies
speak of it: one of them, which calls Him the eldest son of Hadai and Padmavati (but does not name their other children),
is silent about the marriage, as it is silent about the date and place of His departure; of the identifications according
to the “Gaura-ganoddesha-dipika” it gives only two — Baladeva, and Jahnava as Ananga-manjari (the “Gaura-ganoddesha-dipika” itself, 66, gives both
opinions — some call Ananga-manjari Vasudha, others Jahnava — and accepts both as true; see below, “Identifications”).
<!-- F84 -->

Nityananda's wives were the two daughters of Surya Dasa Sarakhela (Sarahela), elder brother of Gauridasa Pandita —
Vasudha and Jahnava. By one account, Surya Dasa lived in Shaligrama and served as a secretary to the Muslim authorities;
by another, his house and temple stood in Ambika, a little west of the shripata of Gauridasa[^50]. He and his younger
brother Krishnadasa Sarakhela had firm faith in Nityananda:

“Suryadasa Sarakhela and his brother Krishnadasa had unshakable faith in Nityananda. They were full of love of God.” (CC,
Adi 11.25) <!-- F324 F325 F326 -->

The “Bhakti-ratnakara” (12th wave) tells in detail of this wedding, performed “according to human custom.” Its
arrangement was taken on by Krishnadasa, son of Harihora (by some, a king) of Borgachi (Boroganchhi) near Shaligrama, a
dear devotee of Nityananda[^51]. An old brahmana of Shaligrama, seeing Surya Dasa searching for a bridegroom for his two
daughters, told him that Nityananda was their eternal husband. And Surya Dasa saw Nityananda in a dream as Balarama
Himself — in incomparable radiance filling all ten directions, in wondrous ornaments — and on either side of Him his
daughters in the forms of Revati and Varuni[^52]. “Until He accepts them, I shall have no peace,” he told a brahmana
friend. <!-- F327 F328 F329 F330 F331 -->

Surya Dasa sent the brahmana to Navadvipa to Srivasa, with whom Nityananda was then living. Srivasa rejoiced and passed
the matter on to Nityananda, and the merciful Nityananda had Surya Dasa assured that He would fulfil his wish; Advaita,
on hearing it, said: “Let it happen soon.” Srivasa, Advaita, Chandrashekhara, Murari Gupta and others gathered and sang
sankirtana; Nityananda arrived, and the devotees from Shaligrama arrived too. Surya Dasa ran out to meet Him, fell at
Nityananda's feet and held them, flooding the ground with tears and unable to utter a word; Nityananda smiled, could not
hold back His joyful laughter and, bestowing full mercy on him, embraced him. Gauridasa, seeing his brother, was in
ecstasy too. <!-- F332 F333 F334 -->

When Surya Dasa offered his daughters at Nityananda's feet, He mercifully showed him the same vision in waking: both
shone brighter than mountains of gold and kunkuma, in jewels and many-coloured garments, and Surya Dasa fainted with love.
The adhivasa on the eve of the wedding was held in the house of Krishnadasa Sarakhela in Shaligrama in the presence of all
the brahmanas of Borgachi and Shaligrama (BR 12.3983); Surya Dasa bowed to Nityananda's feet and, according to rite and
custom, gave Him both daughters. He was truly fortunate, says the “Bhakti-ratnakara”: he gave his daughters to the
Supreme Personality of Godhead, Lord Nityananda. <!-- F335 F336 F337 -->

After the wedding Nityananda stayed for a while in Shaligrama, and then with His two wives came to Navadvipa to Shachi Devi
and bowed at her feet; Shachi joyfully embraced Vasudha and Jahnava, sat them on her lap and pinched their cheeks
affectionately. At Shachimata's request Nityananda lived for a time in Navadvipa, then in Saptagrama with Uddharana Datta,
and then settled for good in Khardaha on the Ganga[^53]. In Khardaha He had a house where His two energies, Vasudha and
Jahnava, lived; Parameshvari Dasa served them there. <!-- F338 F339 -->

Nityananda's marriage is no worldly union. The eternal associates of the Lord, descending into the world, act in a human
way, but never touch maya and are never entangled in family life; their unearthly nature is recognised by those who have
surrendered to Vishnu and the Vaishnavas. Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati says: Nityananda had no material body like
ours; He entered into no material intimacy with a material body and did not sink into the material rasa of
separation[^54]. <!-- I7 F431 -->

## Jahnava and Vasudha after Nityananda's Departure
Jahnava is Nityananda's shakti, “Nityananda-svarupini,” His other self. Bhaktivinoda Thakura in the
“Kalyana-kalpataru” prays to her as “Nityananda-shakti” — the energy of Nityananda, devotion to Krishna and to her guru
personified. Without the mercy of Jahnava Devi, the Vaishnavas say, no one will cross the ocean of births or attain the
service of Nityananda and of Those whom He worships — Gaura Hari and Radha-Krishna. The “Bhakti-ratnakara” (10th wave)
glorifies her as the daughter of Surya Pandita and the wife of Nityananda, honoured by the whole world, skilled in giving
out the pearls of prema. <!-- F340 F341 Q62 -->

After Nityananda's departure Jahnava became the centre of His circle. She came to the festival at Kheturi and there
blessed Shrinivasa and Narottama, visited Katwa, Shantipur and Navadvipa, made the journey to Vraja and at Radha-kunda
blessed Raghunatha Dasa (one biography calls her here “Nityananda's widow”). Returning from Vraja to Khardaha, she told
Vasudha and Virabhadra of the journey; Virachandra and Ganga received her there. In Khardaha Shrinivasa and Narottama
visited Jahnava and Vasudha, Virabhadra and Parameshvari Dasa; Shrinivasa was then going round the shripatas of the
companions of Mahaprabhu and Nityananda. <!-- F342 F343 F346 -->

With Jahnava to Kheturi went Nityananda's companions: Minaketana Ramadasa, Abhirama, Murari Chaitanya, Jnanadasa,
Kamalakara Pippalai, and also Krishna Mishra, Shripati, Shrinidhi, Parameshvari Dasa, Balarama Dasa, Vrindavana Dasa
Thakura and others (BR 10.372–375)[^55]; Kala Krishnadasa, too, was with her when she came to Katwa. After the departure of
Uddharana Datta, Jahnava visited his house. <!-- F348 F349 -->

Parameshvari Dasa, a dear companion of Nityananda, by Jahnava's mercy saw Gopinatha together with Radha in Vrindavana;
returning to Khardaha, he told Jahnava and Vasudha of it, and Jahnava told him to install Radha-Gopinatha at Antpur. The
Deities of Purushottama (Nagara) were moved together with the Deity of Jahnava Mata to Sahebdanga-Berigram, and then to
Chanduregram (Anubhashya to CC, Adi 11.39). Nityananda Dasa, author of the “Prema-vilasa,” is a diksha disciple of
Jahnava Mata. Jahnava departed on the shukla-navami of the month of Vaishakha. <!-- F344 F345 F347 F350 -->

Seeing the devotion of Purushottama's son, Kanu Thakura, from his earliest years, Nityananda named him Shishu Krishnadasa.
The boy lost his mother early (she too was called Jahnava). From here the biographies differ: by one, Nityananda's wife
Jahnava, it is said, adopted him and took him with her to Vrindavana; by another, Jahnava-mata took him to Vrindavana at
five, and there Jiva and the other Gosvamis named him Kanai Thakura; by a third, on learning of his mother's departure,
Nityananda Himself came to Purushottama's house and took the “twelve-year-old child” to His own home in Khardaha[^56].
<!-- F351 F453 -->

## Descendants: Virachandra and Ganga
Jahnava had no sons; Vasudha bore Virabhadra (Virachandra, Birchandra) Gosvami and a daughter, Ganga Devi. According to
the “Bhakti-ratnakara” (9.413–414, 420–421), Virabhadra is Nityananda's only son, famed as His branch. Srila
Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati in the “Anubhashya” calls him the son of Vasudha — Nityananda's “other wife” — and a disciple of
Jahnava: according to the “Prema-vilasa,” seeing her four-armed, he resolved to accept her as his guru.
<!-- F352 F353 F354 F355 -->

Krishnadasa Kaviraja devotes special lines to Virabhadra:

“On the branch of Sri Nityananda there grew a great branch — Virabhadra Gosani. There is no counting all the twigs and
shoots to which this branch gave rise.” (CC, Adi 11.8)

“Sri Virabhadra is the Supreme Himself. But He called Himself only a great devotee. And though He is above all the
injunctions of the Vedas, He strictly performed all the rituals and injunctions.” (CC, Adi 11.9)

“He is the main pillar in the hall of loving devotion to Sri Chaitanya. He knew Himself to be the Supreme God, but
outwardly showed no pride.” (CC, Adi 11.10)

“And to this day, by the mercy of the all-glorious Sri Virabhadra Gosani, the holy names of Sri Chaitanya and Nityananda
are chanted everywhere in the world.” (CC, Adi 11.11)

And again: Virabhadra is the best of all Nityananda's branches, and there is no end to the shoots that grew from it (CC,
Adi 11.56). <!-- F356 Q49 -->

Virabhadra's wives were Shrimati, daughter of Yadunandana (Yadunatha) Acharya and his wife Vidyunmala of Jhamatpur (Hooghly
district), and Narayani. By one biography, the marriage was arranged by Jahnava, and both daughters-in-law were her
disciples; by another, Yadunandanacharya was himself a disciple of Virachandra. As vishnu-tattva, says one biography, Virabhadra had three shaktis, and the Shri-shakti is Shrimati[^57]. He had no
children of his own. Three are named as his adopted sons: the eldest, Gopijana-vallabha, who lived in Latagram near
Mankar (Burdwan district); the middle one, Ramakrishna, at Gayespur near Malda; and the youngest, Ramachandra, who lived in
Khardaha, a pure shrotriya brahmana of the Batavyal family, of the Shandilya gotra. In the “Bhakti-ratnakara” all three
are called Virabhadra's sons; but, explains one biography, “sons” here means disciples, for their gotras differ.
<!-- F357 F358 F359 -->

Virabhadra's followers, called “neras” and “neris,” numbered, it is said, twelve hundred and thirteen hundred, and all
lived restrained lives; later laxity, the eating of fish and the like appeared among them, and many of them earned
contempt. <!-- F360 -->

With the permission of his mother Vasudha, Virabhadra went to Vrindavana and went round Vraja with the blessings of
Bhugarbha Gosvami and Jiva (BR 13). In the old temple of Shyamasundara in Khardaha a manuscript of the “Bhagavatam” is kept,
copied by tradition by Virabhadra, and in the opinion of some by Nityananda Himself. Virabhadra brought to Khardaha the
stone from which the Deities of Shyamasundara, Radhavallabha and Nandadulala were carved (hence the name
Shyamasundar-ghat). He also established the custom of celebrating Nityananda's appearance in Khardaha. Virabhadra's own
appearance day is usually reckoned the krishna-navami of the month of Kartika, and by the “Gaudiya-vaishnava-abhidhana”
the shukla-chaturdashi of the month of Agrahayana. <!-- F361 F362 F363 -->

Virachandra was in Shrikhanda at the festival of Narahari Sarakara's departure, where Shrinivasa performed the rites; in
Ambika the followers of Chaitanya joyfully welcomed him as Nityananda's son. Tradition says that only a Vishnu-shila or a
consecrated Deity could bear the obeisance of Abhirama Thakura — anything else cracked or crumbled; Nityananda's son
Virachandra and His daughter Ganga Devi received his obeisances unharmed, which confirmed their divinity, and Abhirama
composed ashtakas in their honour. <!-- F364 F365 F366 -->

Ganga Devi's husband was Madhavacharya (Madhava Chattopadhyaya) of Jirat (Hooghly); in the opinion of some, he was a
disciple of Purushottama (Nagara), in the opinion of others — of Nityananda or of Jahnava. <!-- F367 F453 -->

Descendants of Nityananda were spoken of in later times as well. From His line came the well-known scholar Shyamlal
Gosvami, who studied at the “Sarasvata-chatushpathi” (1897). And in 1911 those who called themselves descendants of
Nityananda and other companions took the side of the smartas against the Vaishnavas at the debate in Balighai
(Midnapore), where Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati spoke. <!-- F368 -->

## Disciples and Companions
Nityananda's companions are without number. They are always in bliss, and they have no other occupation than sankirtana:

“All the companions of Nityananda Prabhu were always filled with transcendental bliss.” (CB, Antya 5.712)

“They devoted themselves to nothing but holy sankirtana and were always intoxicated with the mood of cowherd boys.” (CB,
Antya 5.713)

The chief among them are the twelve gopalas (dvadasha-gopala), Balarama's friends in Vraja; all of them hold the seats of
“patra-raja” in the Universal Assembly of Vaishnava Kings (Vishva-vaishnava-raja-sabha). One biography lists them thus:
Abhirama Thakura, Sundarananda Thakura, Kamalakara Pippalai, Gauridasa Pandita, Parameshvari Dasa, Dhananjaya Pandita,
Mahesha Pandita, Purushottama Pandita, Kalakrishna Dasa, Purushottama (two different Purushottamas, as printed),
Uddharana Thakura and Shridhara. Nityananda's companions are like Him: generous, saving the fallen, fulfilling desires,
and many showed wondrous powers. They are an extension of His mercy: they seek out the still more fallen, and through
them Nityananda distributes His mercy; for the sinners and offenders of this world have no shelter other than the mercy
of Nityananda. <!-- F369 F370 F371 I3 I5 Q45 -->

Abhirama Thakura (Ramadasa, Abhirama Gopala) is Nityananda's “dearest” devotee. By His order he became a great acharya
and preacher and saved many godless people; he was endowed with His power (according to the “Bhakti-ratnakara,” as if
Nityananda had entered him), and at His request married Malini (BR 4.103–108). <!-- F377 -->

Sundarananda (in Vraja, Sudama, GGD 127) is a branch of Nityananda:

“Sundarananda, a branch of Sri Nityananda, is known as one of His closest servants. With him Nityananda performed deeds
repeating the pastimes of Vraja.” (CC, Adi 11.23)

The “Chaitanya-bhagavata” (Antya 5.728) calls him the eldest among Nityananda's companions. Like Nityananda, he is a saviour
of the fallen; one biography ascribes to him the miracle of the kadamba blossoming on a lemon tree (cf. note 43).
<!-- F378 -->

Gauridasa Pandita — in Vraja, Subala (GGD 128) — is one of the twelve gopalas and one of Nityananda's dearest companions. His
body, mind and speech were given to Nityananda (CB, Antya 5.730):

“For the sake of serving Sri Nityananda, Gauridasa sacrificed the support of his class and family. So he accepted Sri
Chaitanya and Nityananda as the Lords of his whole life.” (CC, Adi 11.27)

He is one of the most important sub-branches of Nityananda's branch. As Nityananda gave out Krishna-prema to all, without
distinguishing the worthy from the unworthy, as if intoxicated, so His close companion Gauridasa showed great power in
preaching. <!-- F379 F385 -->

With Gauridasa is linked the tradition of the Gaura-Nitai Deities of Ambika. When the Lord and Nityananda were about to go
from Ambika-Kalna to Puri, Gauridasa begged Them to stay; the Lord promised to leave him His own form and Nityananda's:
“My murti is the same as I.” The two brothers manifested Themselves in two perfect Deity forms, tasted what Gauridasa had
cooked, left him the “two Lords,” and Themselves went to Puri[^58]. <!-- F380 F381 -->

These Deities, it is said, once revealed Themselves to Gauridasa as Krishna and Balarama in the dress of cowherd boys —
with sticks, Krishna with flute and peacock feather, forest garlands round Their necks, anklets on Their feet —
reminding him that he was Subala, and returned to the altar as Gaura and Nityananda. Seeing how old Gauridasa toiled, They
refused to eat and asked him to cook more simply — shak on a banana leaf — and the ornaments he wished to adorn Them with
They put on Themselves. Once the Deities left the altar and sang and danced in the kirtana of Hridaya Chaitanya on the
bank of the Ganga, and seeing the anger of Gauridasa, who came out with a cudgel, became invisible — They entered the
heart of Hridaya Chaitanya. Gaura and Nityananda always abide in Gauridasa's house; later, in Ambika, Gaura and Nityananda
appeared in a kirtana, and Jahnava saw Gauridasa with Them in a dream. In the present temple of Ambika are Deities of
Gaura-Nitai (together with Radha-Krishna, Jagannatha, Balarama and Sita-Rama); there too, by one biography, are kept a
manuscript of the “Gita” — by tradition written in the Lord's own hand (not all accept this) — and the Lord's oar.
<!-- F382 F383 F384 -->

Gauridasa's (Subala's) disciple Hridaya Chaitanya worshipped Gaura-Nitai in the mood of friendship (sakhya). From
Gauridasa runs the succession called the “line of Nityananda”: Gauridasa Pandita — Hridaya Chaitanya — Shyamananda —
Rasikananda — Nayanananda — Radha-Damodara — Baladeva Vidyabhushana. <!-- F386 -->

Uddharana Datta is one of the twelve gopalas (Subahu, GGD 129). He was born in 1481 in Saptagrama to help Nityananda:

“The best of the great devotees of God, Uddharana Datta, served the lotus feet of Sri Nityananda with all reverence.”
(CC, Adi 11.41)

As one of Nityananda's closest companions he is called “parama-parama-patita-pavana,” the saviour of the most fallen;
whoever takes shelter of him attains service at the feet of Nityananda and Gauranga. According to the
“Chaitanya-bhagavata,” he has the right to serve Nityananda; possessing great wealth, he gave all his senses to
Nityananda's service. In his shripata at Saptagrama is the six-armed Mahaprabhu whom he installed and himself served, to His right the Deity
of Nityananda, to His left that of Gadadhara. The Nitai-Gaura Deities that Uddharana installed in Uddharanpur near Katwa
were, it is said, taken to the capital of the Banowari rajas. <!-- F405 F406 F457 -->

Parameshvari Dasa is one of Nityananda's most important companions, who appeared to enrich His pastimes:

“Nityananda was the very life and soul of Parameshvari Dasa. In his body Nityananda enjoyed various pastimes.” (CB,
Antya 5.732)

“Parameshvara Dasa was wholly devoted to the lotus feet of Sri Nityananda. Anyone who simply remembers his name easily
attains devotion to Krishna.” (CC, Adi 11.29) <!-- F403 -->

Purushottama (Nagara) — in Vraja, the cowherd boy Dama (GGD 131). The “Charitamrita” counts him among Nityananda's
devotees:

“From birth Purushottama Dasa was absorbed in service to the lotus feet of Sri Nityananda. All his childhood he plays
games with Krishna.” (CC, Adi 11.39)

According to the “Chaitanya-bhagavata,” he is one of Nityananda's chief companions, unaware of his body, for Nityananda
constantly acted through him:

“Nityananda Chandra Himself abode in the heart of Purushottama Dasa, and therefore in his body there was not a trace of
external consciousness.” (CB, Antya 5.742)

Many of Nityananda's companions showed such powers: Purushottama, according to the “Gaudiya-vaishnava-abhidhana,” ate
snake poison in trance. <!-- F404 -->

Nityananda delivered Dhananjaya Pandita and, according to the “Chaitanya-bhagavata,” came to dwell in his heart forever
(CB, Antya 5.733); in his shripata, Shitala-grama, the Deities of Nitai-Gauranga are served to this day. And on returning from Vrindavana, by one biography,
he installed Deities — Gopinatha, Nitai-Gaura and a Damodara shila — in the village of Jalandi-grama. Kala (Kaliya)
Krishnadasa, also one of the twelve gopalas, is one of Nityananda's chief devotees:

“One of the outstanding great Vaishnavas was Kala Krishnadasa. He wished to know nothing but the feet of Sri
Nityananda.” (CC, Adi 11.37)

He must not be confused with the brahmana Krishnadasa, the Lord's servant in the South (see above): though the
“Charitamrita” calls that one, too, Kala Krishnadasa, they are different persons, as Srila Bhaktisiddhanta distinguishes
them (see note 38). Kamalakara Pippalai,
one of the twelve gopalas (Mahabala, GGD 128; “Vaishnavachara-darpana”), is counted among Nityananda's branches. Mahesha
Pandita is considered a branch of both Nityananda and Chaitanya; like all Nityananda's companions he was generous and
devoted to saving the fallen, and danced in ecstasy like a madman; he was an especially close friend of Nityananda and,
according to the “Chaitanya-bhagavata,” especially dear to Him; he was also in Khardaha when Narottama came there (BR
8.220). Jagadisha Pandita, too, is considered a branch of both Nityananda and Chaitanya: Nityananda, like Mahaprabhu, was
won over by his devotion and considered him one of His intimates, and for Jagadisha Nityananda was life and soul; he who
served Jagannatha and Gaura-Gopala is called “a dear companion of Nityananda.” Drawn by the parental love of Jagadisha
and his wife, Gauranga and Nityananda twice came to his shripata at Yashada, sang sankirtana there and held festivals.

To the singing of the three Ghosha brothers — Govinda, Madhava and Vasudeva — Nityananda danced:

“The three brothers — Madhava, Vasudeva and Govinda — sang while Lord Nityananda danced.” (CB, Antya 5.259)

“Three brothers — Govinda, Madhava and Vasudeva. When they sang kirtana with self-forgetfulness, Sri Chaitanya and
Nityananda danced.” (CC, Adi 10.115)

Madhava Ghosha was especially dear to Nityananda; when the three brothers sang, Nityananda Himself danced in great
ecstasy. <!-- F399 -->

Srila Bhaktisiddhanta counts Gadadhara Dasa among the followers of both Gaura and Nityananda: as a follower of Gaura he is
in the conjugal mood, as a follower of Nityananda in pure service; but his friendship is not the mood of a cowherd boy,
but service in the spirit of madhurya (Anubhashya to CC, Adi 10.53). According to the “Gaura-ganoddesha-dipika” (154–155),
in him are the radiance of Radha (Chandrakanti) and Purnananda, the beloved companion of Balarama; he is not one of the
gopalas who served Nityananda in sakhya-rasa. Speaking with the kazi of Eriyadaha, Gadadhara Dasa declared that Gaura and
Nityananda had descended to save people with the nectar of Hari's name. <!-- F407 F408 -->

Counted in Nityananda's branch are also Nandana — the brother of Ganga Dasa and Vishnu Dasa, who composed verses and songs
(by the “Gaudiya-vaishnava-abhidhana,” a different person from Nandana Acharya), — and those whom the “Charitamrita”
names thus:

“Kamsari Sena, Ramasena, Ramachandra Kaviraja, and also the three physicians — Govinda, Shriranga and Mukunda.” (CC,
Adi 11.51)

And Narottama from childhood meditated on the qualities of Chaitanya and Nityananda and prayed to Them; Shyamananda loved
to hear of the glory of Gauranga and Nityananda. And Advaita's son Achyutananda, in the words of one biography, was “dear
to Nityananda as life.” <!-- F401 F372 F402 Q46 Q59 F458 -->

Vrindavana Dasa Thakura, author of the “Chaitanya-bhagavata,” is called a disciple of Nityananda[^59]; he himself called
himself Nityananda's last servant.

“Having received the greatest mercy of Sri Nityananda, Srila Vrindavana Dasa Thakura beautifully described the pastimes
of Sri Chaitanya, becoming like the original Vyasa.” (CC, Antya 20.82)

Whoever reads his book, says the “Charitamrita,” knows the glory of Chaitanya and Nitai. His Deities of Gaura-Nitai are
worshipped to this day at Mamgachhi. <!-- F373 F374 Q70 -->

More than once Vrindavana Dasa repeats that he is ready to “put his foot on the head” of a blasphemer of Nityananda:

“I shall break the head of the wretched sinner who disregards the glory of Lord Nityananda and dares to criticise Him.”
(CB, Adi 9.225)

Fools blame him for this and themselves fall into vaishnava-aparadha. Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati in the
“Gaudiya-bhashya” to this verse explains: in these words is the compassion of Nityananda's servant, the highest guru, even
for blasphemers; the dust of his feet, falling on their heads, will destroy their sins. This is an example for the
preacher: even in threatening, he wishes good[^60]. <!-- F375 Q13 I9 -->

From the “Chaitanya-bhagavata” (it was also called the “Chaitanya-mangala”) Krishnadasa Kaviraja learned of the wonders
of Chaitanya and Nityananda (CC, Adi 8). Srila Bhaktisiddhanta explains: Vrindavana Dasa, absorbed in describing the
deeds of Nityananda, left out many events of his plan, and the Vaishnavas of Vrindavana asked Krishnadasa to describe
them. <!-- F376 -->

Krishnadasa Kaviraja himself was born in Jhamatpur; there his Deities of Gaura-Nitai are worshipped to this day, and in
the courtyard of his house stands a small temple with the footprints of Nityananda. By local tradition, it was here that
Krishnadasa received diksha from Nityananda[^61]. His brother — by the “Gaudiya-vaishnava-abhidhana,” Shyama Dasa
Kaviraja — believed in Chaitanya but not in Nityananda. When Minaketana Ramadasa came to Jhamatpur, shouting the name of
Nityananda and filling the devotees' hearts with ecstasy, the pujari Gunarnava Mishra, who did not believe in Nityananda,
did not rise to greet His disciple, and Minaketana called him a second Romaharshana:

“‘This is a second Romaharshana-suta, who on seeing Sri Balarama did not rise to offer Him his respects.’” (CC, Adi
5.170)

From this one sees how dear Minaketana is to Nityananda. Krishnadasa's brother quarrelled with Ramadasa; the latter in
anger broke his flute and left — and this brought the brother ruin[^62]. Krishnadasa took Ramadasa's side and rebuked his
brother:

“‘These two Brothers (Sri Chaitanya and Nityananda) form one body. They are one and indivisible. And you will fall,
because you have no faith in Nityananda.” (CC, Adi 5.175)

“If you believe in one and are disrespectful to the other, your logic is just like accepting only one half of a hen (the
one that lays eggs).” (CC, Adi 5.176)

“Better to be an atheist and reject both Brothers than to be a hypocrite — to believe in one and not acknowledge the
other.’” (CC, Adi 5.177) <!-- F391 F392 F393 F394 F395 F396 Q52 -->

Pleased, Nityananda appeared to Krishnadasa in a dream:

“Lord Sri Nityananda-Balarama appeared to me in a dream in the village of Jhamatpur, which lies near Naihati.” (CC, Adi
5.181)

“Krishnadasa, do not fear, go to Vrindavana — there you will attain everything,” said Nitai, and with a wave of His hand
showed him the way; He granted him the vision of Himself, the right to live in Vrindavana and service at the feet of
Radha-Krishna. To accept Chaitanya without Nityananda or Nityananda without Chaitanya is hostile to bhakti; and the Lord
values even a small attachment: Krishnadasa merely took the side of Nityananda's companion — and received His blessing.
Not for nothing does he begin every chapter of the “Charitamrita” by invoking Mahaprabhu and His companions — Nityananda
and Advaita. <!-- Q53 I6 -->

The “Gaura-ganoddesha-dipika” (67–68) says that the brothers Nishatha and Ulmuka entered Virachandra for a purpose, and
that Minaketana Ramadasa is “another Sankarshana, the original vyuha”[^63]. As Nityananda is an avadhuta, so is His follower Minaketana; Srila Bhaktisiddhanta explains: a
disciple of Nityananda is an eternally perfect maha-bhagavata, a paramahamsa beyond varnashrama. <!-- F397 -->

## The Disappearance
Neither the date, nor the place, nor the circumstances of Nityananda's departure are named by the biographies on which this
article rests. All that is known is that when Shrinivasa was returning from Puri to Bengal (Gadadhara Pandita was by then
no longer in this world), Advaita and Nityananda had already withdrawn Themselves; hearing this on the way, he fell down on
the road and again resolved to give up his body, but that same night both Prabhus out of causeless mercy appeared to him
in a dream, consoled him and dissuaded him. <!-- F410 F411 -->

Of the order of the departures one can speak only on the word of two biographies, and even then without dates. By one,
Vrindavana Dasa was born four years after the Lord's sannyasa, at Mahaprabhu's departure he was barely twenty, and soon
after that he received initiation from Nityananda, and later went with Jahnava to Kheturi. The other remarks: since
Vrindavana Dasa called himself Nityananda's last servant, Nityananda remained in His manifest pastimes only a short time
after that initiation. From both accounts it follows that Nityananda withdrew Himself after Mahaprabhu; when exactly, none
of the books says. <!-- F458 F460 -->

A tradition recorded in later works (by Jayananda and in the “Nityananda-vamsha-vistara”) says that Nityananda entered
Bankimdeva in Ekachakra — and the sevaits of Bankim-raya tell the same (see above, “Bankim-raya and the Memorial Places
of Ekachakra”). Be that as it may, His pastimes did not cease: at the festival in Kheturi (BR 10) Gauranga Himself
appeared in the kirtana with Nityananda and all the eternal associates, and the devotees saw Nityananda and Advaita
overwhelmed with ecstasy. And Raghunandana, consoling Shrinivasa before his own
departure, quoted verses from the “Sri Krishna-bhajanamrita” of Narahari Sarakara (3–4): when Krishna Chaitanya and His
companion Nityananda conclude Their appearance, the Vaishnavas of the Kali-yuga will grow ever more anxious and full of
doubts — a hard time is drawing near; and then he blessed Shrinivasa to preach. <!-- F412 F414 -->

## Identifications
According to the “Bhakti-ratnakara,” Hadai Pandita and Padmavati are in Krishna's lila Vasudeva and Rohini (see above).
Balarama's wives, Varuni and Revati, according to the “Gaura-ganoddesha-dipika” (65), became Vasudha and Jahnava; both
are daughters of Surya Dasa, who was formerly Kakudmi, the father of Revati[^52]. Ananga-manjari, the younger sister of
Radha, daughter of Vrishabhanu, the same book (66) connects with both: “Some in the age of Kali declare Ananga-manjari to
be Sri Vasudha Devi, and some call Jahnavi Ananga-manjari,” — and the saints, by the former rule of the distinction of
manifestations, accept both as true. The biographies speak only of Jahnava as Ananga-manjari: the possessor of energy is
not different from His energy. One of them holds this view; another considers acceptable both it and the identification
with Revati, for several personalities can be united in the Lord's associates. <!-- F417 -->

Virachandra, according to the “Gaura-ganoddesha-dipika” (67), is Kshirodakashayi Vishnu, an expansion (vyuha) of
Sankarshana, not different from Chaitanya, and Nishatha and Ulmuka entered him for a purpose (68; see above); by one
biography, he is eternally known by this name and eternally manifest in Goloka; by another view, he is Aniruddha, who appeared as Nityananda's son. Ganga Devi, Nityananda's daughter, is an incarnation of the river
Ganga, and her husband Madhavacharya an incarnation of King Shantanu, Ganga's husband in the “Mahabharata” (GGD 69).

## The Tattva of Nityananda
Krishna is svayam-rupa, the original form of God; Baladeva is svayam-prakasha, the first manifestation of that same form,
the full expansion of Krishna (so explains Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati on CC, Adi 1.14). The “Charitamrita” says:

“Sri Krishna is the original Bhagavan, the source of all manifestations of God, and Sri Balarama is His second body.” (CC,
Adi 5.4)

“Their essence is one; the difference is only in form. Sri Balarama is the first expansion of Krishna's body. He assists
Krishna in all His pastimes.” (CC, Adi 5.5)

Krishna's first expansion is mula-Sankarshana Balarama, the original father of all; He it is who came with Lord Chaitanya
as Nityananda. Nityananda is baladeva-tattva, mula-Sankarshana, the cause of Maha-Sankarshana in Vaikuntha and the source
of the three purusha-avataras: Karanodakashayi, Garbhodakashayi and Kshirodakashayi Vishnu. Mula-Sankarshana is the
eternally worshipped deity of Shiva. Ananta-Shesha, who holds up the earth, is so closely bound to Him that Nityananda is
called Ananta Himself[^64]. <!-- F8 F9 F10 F13 -->

Svarupa Damodara, the “second self” of Sri Gaurasundara, glorified Nityananda in five verses of his “Kadcha,” which
Krishnadasa Kaviraja quotes at the beginning of the “Charitamrita” (CC, Adi 1.7–11):

“May Lord Sri Nityananda-Balarama become my eternal shelter. For Maha-Sankarshana, Shesha-Naga and also the three
Purusha-avataras (Karanodakashayi Vishnu, Garbhodakashayi Vishnu and Kshirodakashayi Vishnu) are His full or partial
divine manifestations.” (CC, Adi 1.7)

The sense of these verses is this. In Vaikuntha, beyond maya, in the quaternity Vasudeva — Sankarshana — Pradyumna —
Aniruddha, He is present as Sankarshana. His portion is Karanabdhishayi, the first purusha-avatara, master of maya and
shelter of the universes; the portion of the portion is Garbhodakashayi, from the lotus of whose navel Brahma is born; the
portion of the portion of the portion is Kshirodakashayi, the Paramatma of all, Vishnu the maintainer; His kala is Ananta,
who holds up the earth. <!-- F11 F12 Q58 -->

Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati unfolds the same in more detail. From Baladeva, Sankarshana — the form of Nityananda —
abides in Mahavaikuntha. By His glance on the waters of the Karana He manifests the lila of Maha-Vishnu; rising in the
waters of the Garbha, the lila of the collective (samashti) Vishnu; by His glance on the Milk Ocean, the lila of the
individual (vyashti) Vishnu, caring for the welfare of living beings. As Shesha He serves Krishna in ten forms — as bed,
seat and so on (cf. CC, Adi 5.10). From His form abiding in the waters of the Karana are manifested Goloka-Vrindavana,
Vaikuntha, the countless anu-chaitanyas, the minute living beings of the jiva-shakti, and the universe with its fourteen
worlds[^65]; from Him, too, Advaita, the inner controller and material cause of the world; from Him the
naimittika-avataras — Rama, Nrisimha and others — eternally appear. And when Govinda, the cause of all causes, manifested
the magnanimous lila of Gaura and began to give out Krishna-prema, Baladeva manifested His form as Nityananda to serve this
lila of the bestowal of love. The living beings eternally dependent on Him are known as Gaura's associates —
gaura-parshadas. <!-- F420 F421 F422 F423 F424 -->

Nityananda is vishnu-tattva and therefore the Lord of all shaktis; and yet He is a faithful servant, showing the way to
Krishna's hladini, the energy of bliss: He is the sandhini-shaktimad-vigraha, the bearer of the energy of being. At His
feet is all vishnu-tattva; though He is not different from it and stands within its hierarchy, He is the servant of
Gauranga. Gaurangasundara is Mahaprabhu, and Nityananda is His vibhu, vaibhava-prakasha; He is shaktiman-tattva, not
shakti-tattva. The “Charitamrita” says it briefly:

“One of Them is Mahaprabhu, and the other two are Prabhus. These two Prabhus serve the lotus feet of Mahaprabhu.” (CC, Adi
7.14) <!-- F425 F426 F427 Q56 -->

Nityananda is the original cause of Maha-Vishnu and vishaya-tattva — the One who is served; He manifested the rasa-lila
with the gopis, who serve Him as ashraya. In the language of the alankara-shastra the one served is called vishaya, the
one serving ashraya[^66]. He is the master of prakriti, no part of Him is material, His body is eternally manifest from a
supernatural substance; He is shaktiman, vishnu-tattva, and Vasudha and Jahnava Thakurani are His transcendental shaktis.
For the aprakrita Vishnu and His associates there is no difference between the body and the possessor of the body — maya
does not divide them; His appearance in the world is not painful and not caused by karma, as ours is, and His pastimes are
eternally manifest in Goloka. Without aprakrita vision this cannot be understood; by the guru's mercy, having gained
transcendental knowledge, one sees it all clearly, while those who judge in a worldly way cannot see Nityananda — the
dance of the material senses never pleases Him. Nityananda's pastimes are eternal, eternal too is the appearance of
Virabhadra; in them there is not even a scent of matter. <!-- F428 F429 F430 F432 F433 -->

Accepting the mood of a devotee, Baladeva appears as the bhakta-svarupa — Nityananda; though He is Bhagavat-tattva, He
behaves as a devotee. When Krishna comes as Sri Krishna Chaitanya, taking the mood and colour of Radha, His eternal
associates come with Him; so too Baladeva, the first expansion (prakasha) of Krishna, appears in Ekachakra as Nityananda
to adorn the pastimes of Mahaprabhu, and He has His own circle of intimates who descend to help Him. In the
Pancha-tattva Nityananda is the bhakta-svarupa, the form of a devotee; Gauranga is the bhakta-rupa, Advaita the
bhakta-avatara; these three are prabhu-tattva, vishnu-tattva, and therefore tulasi leaves are offered at Their feet. The
one Truth, teaches Srila Bhaktisiddhanta, manifests five forms for the tasting of different rasas, but ontologically they
are not different from one another:

“I offer obeisances to the Supreme Lord Krishna in His five aspects, known as the Pancha-tattva: I fall prostrate before
Sri Gauranga, who appeared as His own devotee, wishing to taste His own sweetness. Then I bow to the lotus feet of His
elder brother Sri Nityananda, the original Guru. …” (CC, Adi 1.14)

Every form of vishnu-tattva possesses three energies — Shri, Bhu and Nila (Lila); Nityananda, too, possesses them.
<!-- F14 F15 F16 F17 F18 F19 Q2 -->

Mahaprabhu is the main trunk and the gardener of the tree of devotion, and Advaita and Nityananda are the two trunks into
which it divides:

“Above, the trunk divides into two great branches. One branch is Sri Advaita, and the other branch is Nityananda.” (CC,
Adi 9.21)

Sri Chaitanya is the body of the movement of bhakti, Advaita and Nityananda its limbs; all three descended together with
Srivasa and others to preach hari-bhakti. The eleventh chapter of the Adi-lila, on Nityananda's branches, Krishnadasa
opens with a verse in which Nityananda is the “moon among avadhutas,” the highest branch of the eternal tree of love:

“The Avadhuta Sri Nityananda Prabhu is the continuation of the lofty trunk of the unfading tree of Sri Chaitanya
Mahaprabhu, which embodies eternal love for Sri Krishna. I offer obeisances to all the devotees, who are like the twigs of
this main branch.” (CC, Adi 11.4)

From this branch grow branches and sub-branches, and the greatest of them after Him is Virabhadra. <!-- F20 F21 Q3 -->

Among all the Lord's eternal associates Nityananda is the first:

“Sri Nityananda Avadhuta is the chief among all the eternal associates of Sri Chaitanya Mahaprabhu. In the bliss of prema
He serves Sri Chaitanya as a servant, and this intoxication of love makes Him a madman.” (CC, Adi 6.47) <!-- Q57 -->

Sri Chaitanya is glorified both as “the life of Nityananda” and as “the life of Gadadhara” (“Chaitanya-bhagavata”, Madhya 9.3: “Glory to Him, the very life of Nityananda and Gadadhara!”). Srila Bhaktisiddhanta explains: devotees who wish to worship
Radha-Govinda in madhura-rasa take shelter of Gadadhara; those not so inclined take shelter of Nityananda and serve with
pure devotion in His mood. And without Advaita's mercy, sings Narottama Dasa (“dayā karo sītāpati…”), one attains the
service of neither Chaitanya nor Nityananda. <!-- F434 F435 Q4 -->

## Deities of Gaura-Nitai and Later Worship
Nityananda is worshipped together with Gaura in many temples. In the royal palace of Puri the Deities of Gaura-Nityananda
and Gaura-Gadadhara are honoured; in the house of Kashi Mishra (Radha-kanta-matha), in the time of Gopala Guru
(1538–1548), Deities of Gauranga and Nityananda were installed. Deities of Nitai-Gauranga together with Advaita stand in
the Madana-Mohana temple in Ramakeli, Gaura-Nityananda in the temple at Chakra-tirtha on Govardhana; Narottama brought to
Kheturi the Deities of Sri Gauranga-Nityananda[^67]. In the shripata of Mahesha Pandita the Deities of Gaura-Nityananda
are served, together with Gopinatha, Madana-Mohana, Radha-Govinda and a shalagrama-shila; in Ambika Jahnava-mata, on the
way to Kheturi, cooked with her own hands the offering for Gauridasa's Deities of Gaura-Nityananda.
<!-- F436 F437 F438 F439 F459 -->

The name of Nitai never leaves the lips of His followers. Vamshi sang in longing: “Will Nimai and Nitai ever dance
together again?” Shyamananda's disciples — the yogi Damodara, Rasika Murari — sang the names of Nitai-Chaitanya, and
Shyamananda himself “offered” Rasika to Chaitanya and Nityananda. Narottama, Shrinivasa, Shyamananda, Rasikananda,
Gangamata, Mahidhara Sharma and others preached “the message of Gaura-Nityananda” and sang Their names; Gadadhara Dasa and
Abhirama lived in separation from Gaura-Nityananda, and Lokanatha grieved that he was unable to serve Gaura-Nityananda.
Jagannatha Dasa Babaji sang: “What a Name Nitai has brought! He has brought it to market and gives it for the price of
faith.” Vamshi Dasa Babaji carried Deities of Nitai-Gaura (and of Radha-Govinda) in a little bag and answered seekers:
“Worship Nitai — and you will attain Gaura”; outside the worship of Gaura-Nitai there is no joy in this world. (Tobacco,
by his custom, he offered only to Radha-Govinda, not to Nitai-Gauranga.) <!-- F442 F443 I8 -->

In the Gaudiya Math from 1926 a three-day nama-yajna was held every year in honour of Nityananda's appearance, and in
1933 Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati celebrated His appearance day in Mayapur. When in 1925 Bhaktisiddhanta's parikrama
was attacked, a newspaper compared it with Jagai and Madhai's attempt to wound Nityananda Avadhuta. Srila Bhaktivedanta
Swami is called “nityanandavesha” — empowered by Shripada Nityananda Prabhu. One of the biographies opens with a picture
of “Sri Nityananda Prabhu” and closes with the line “Krishna Chaitanya, Prabhu Nityananda, Advaita, Gadadhara, Srivasa.”

## Significance
Nityananda is a mercy to which anyone may turn. Madhava is pleased by bhakti alone, not by birth, learning or wealth:
“what good conduct had the hunter, what age had Dhruva…” (“Padyavali,” 8). Krishna grants good even to those who remember
Him with hostility: even Putana received the position of a nurse (“Bhagavatam,” 3.2.23). But the living beings of the age
of Kali cannot imitate hostile “service”: Shishupala and Dantavakra were unremitting in enmity, while Vena had not even
such concentration — only sin resulted; therefore the mahajanas teach only favourable service. <!-- I14 I15 Q64 Q65 -->

The fallen one fears even the “Bhakta-vatsala,” the One who loves His devotees: “remembering Your name ‘Friend of the
humble’…” (“Padyavali,” 64). This fear is removed by Gaura's devotees: whoever opens his heart at the feet of Nityananda,
Him He will save — and this is a plain fact, not praise for its own sake (arthavada). <!-- I16 Q66 -->

Krishnadasa Kaviraja sums up all that Nityananda gave him:

“Glory to Sri Nityananda-Balarama, by whose mercy I have attained the shelter of Vrindavana-dhama.” (CC, Adi 5.200)

“May Sri Nityananda be glorified forever! By His causeless mercy I have attained the shelter of Sri Rupa and Sanatana
Gosvami.” (CC, Adi 5.201)

“All glory to the lotus feet of Sri Nityananda! By His mercy I have attained connection with the divine Couple, Sri Sri
Radha-Govinda.” (CC, Adi 5.204) <!-- Q54 -->
