# Abhirama Thakura
*Абхирама Тхакур · @abhirama*

## Ramadasa, Whose Life Was Nityananda
When the companions of Nityananda Prabhu are listed, the first named is usually Abhirama Thakura. Krishnadasa Kaviraja
calls him “Ramadasa Abhirama” and “Sri Ramadasa,” Vrindavana Dasa “Ramadasa” and “Ramadasa Mahashaya,” Kavi Karnapura
“the great Abhirama.” In the “Bhakti-ratnakara” he is “Abhirama Gopala,” “Sri Thakura Abhirama” and “Abhirama Gosvami”;
Lochana Dasa too calls him “Abhirama Gosvami,” and Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati in his “Anubhashya” calls him “Sri
Ramadasa (Abhirama)” and “Thakura Abhirama.” The biographies also know him as Sri Abhirama Gopala Thakura and as
Abhirama Dasa.[^1] <!-- F1 -->

Srila Bhaktisiddhanta says of him that he was one “whose very life was Nityananda” (*nityanandaika-prana*); the
biographies call him Nityananda's dearest devotee. Vrindavana Dasa, listing Nityananda's companions, puts Ramadasa
Mahashaya at their head (CB, Antya 5.722),[^2] and the “Bhakti-ratnakara” tells how from time to time Nityananda came
with His companions to the house of His dear Abhirama (4.120). <!-- F4 -->

Of his worldly life little is known. According to the “Bhakti-ratnakara,” he “descended in a brahmana's house to
deliver living beings” (4.105), and his disciple Vedagarbha Acharya calls him “the crest-jewel of brahmanas” (4.112).
The sources name neither his birthplace nor his parents, nor the year and day of his appearance; nor is the day of his
appearance found in the Vaishnava calendar of the Sri Chaitanya Saraswat Math. <!-- F7 -->

## Shridama
Who he is in Krishna's eternal pastimes, Kavi Karnapura tells us:

“He who was formerly called Shridama is now the great Abhirama; it was he who carried a log that only thirty-two men
could lift.” (“Gaura-ganoddesha-dipika” 126)

This verse is quoted both by Srila Bhaktisiddhanta in the “Anubhashya” and by the biographies. Shridama is a cowherd
friend of Krishna, one of those with whom He tends the calves in Vraja. The “Bhakti-ratnakara” says the same: an old
brahmana of Khanakula tells Srinivasa that Thakura Abhirama “is, in Krishna's pastimes, the famous Shridama,” and that
even now “he has revealed that former nature,” appearing to a certain servant in the form of Shridama (4.109–110). The
same book quotes a verse by his disciple Vedagarbha Acharya:

“I worship the venerable Abhirama, formerly called Shridama, the embodiment of love, the crest-jewel of brahmanas, the
husband of Sri Malini.” (“Bhakti-ratnakara” 4.112)

The “Vaishnava-vandana” also calls him Shridama (1.95, 5.72). Shridama did not “turn into” Abhirama: the eternal friend
of Krishna descended into Gaura's pastimes together with the Lord, and his nature remained what it always was.
<!-- F2 Q4 -->

With the verse about him Karnapura opens his list of the friends of Vraja who descended with Nityananda
(“Gaura-ganoddesha-dipika” 126–133). These companions of Nityananda are the twelve gopalas, and in the biographies'
lists Abhirama always stands first; the “Anubhashya” on CC, Adi 11.13 calls him just so: “Thakura Abhirama (gopala 1).”
<!-- F3 -->

Krishnadasa Kaviraja counts him both as a branch of Sri Chaitanya's tree (Adi 10.116) and as one of the chief branches
of Nityananda:

“Ramadasa, a chief branch, was a treasury of loving friendship. He lifted a log that it took sixteen men to carry and
made a flute of it.” (CC, Adi 11.16) <!-- F6 Q3 -->

## A Treasury of Loving Friendship
What defines Abhirama is his love for Krishna as a friend (*sakhya-prema*). It explains his most famous wonder as well.
Krishnadasa Kaviraja speaks of it twice; in the list of Sri Chaitanya's branches:

“Ramadasa Abhirama was a treasury of loving friendship. He lifted a log that it took sixteen men to carry and made a
flute of it.” (CC, Adi 10.116)

and again in the list of Nityananda's branches, in the verse quoted above (Adi 11.16). <!-- F8 Q2 -->

How it happened the “Bhakti-ratnakara” relates (4.121–124). Once Abhirama, intoxicated with love, was dancing. Absorbed
in sakhya-rasa, he wished to play the flute and looked for it everywhere, but could not find it. Then

“a log that even a hundred men could not move he took in his hand as a flute.” (“Bhakti-ratnakara” 4.123)

All who saw it were amazed; and “such unearthly pastimes are always happening with him,” the narrator adds. The
“Vaishnava-vandana” says that he lifted the sixteen-man log with two fingers.[^3] <!-- F9 F10 Q6 -->

The source of this strength is not bodily might. The cowherd boy Shridama is looking for something to play for his
Friend, and in the fullness of his love the log becomes a flute. <!-- I1 -->

## With Nityananda to Gauda
While Mahaprabhu was living in Puri, He told Nityananda to go to Gauda and give love for Krishna to everyone without
distinction:

“To Nityananda He gave this order: ‘Go to Gauda and reveal loving devotion freely, without restraint. I am sending
Ramadasa, Gadadhara and a few others along to help You.’” (CC, Madhya 15.42–43)

In the list of branches Krishnadasa Kaviraja names three:

“At the Lord's command Nityananda went to Gauda, and by the Lord's command three devotees went with Him: Sri Ramadasa,
Madhava and Vasudeva Ghosha. Govinda stayed with the Lord, and was overjoyed to do so.” (CC, Adi 10.117–118)

According to the “Anubhashya” (on Adi 10.53), this was in the Shaka year 1434, about 1512–1513. <!-- F12 -->

That is why Abhirama belongs to two circles at once, Sri Chaitanya's and Nityananda's:

“Sri Ramadasa and Gadadhara Dasa, devotees of Chaitanya Gosani, stayed at Nityananda's side. When Mahaprabhu told
Nityananda to go to Gauda, He sent these two with Him. Therefore they are counted in both groups, and so it is with
Madhava and Vasudeva Ghosha.” (CC, Adi 11.13–15)

Srila Bhaktivinoda Thakura explains in the “Amrita-pravaha-bhashya”: “Ramadasa is Abhirama dasa.”[^4] <!-- F5 Q1 Q10 -->

Vrindavana Dasa names the other companions on that journey:

“With Nityananda went Ramadasa, Gadadhara Dasa, Raghunatha Vaidya, full of the sweetness of loving devotion, Krishnadasa
Pandita, Parameshvari Dasa and the jubilant Purandara Pandita.” (CB, Antya 5.231–233)

The “Bhakti-ratnakara” remembers it so too: “to Gauda goes Nityananda, and with Him Abhirama, Dasa Gadadhara and others”
(12.3611). <!-- F13 Q11 -->

Already on the road love seized them all, and in each his eternal mood came forth. First of all in Ramadasa:

“Ramadasa, the foremost of devotees, was the first to show the mood of a cowherd boy. On the way he stood bent in three
places, and for nine hours he lost all awareness of the outer world.” (CB, Antya 5.236–237) <!-- F14 -->

Later, listing Nityananda's companions, Vrindavana Dasa speaks of him again in particular:

“His chief companion was Ramadasa Mahashaya, who always spoke in the mood of the Lord. Not everyone could grasp the
meaning of his words. Nityananda always dwelt in his heart. Of all the devotees Ramadasa was the most deeply possessed
by love; Krishna Himself stayed in his body for three months.” (CB, Antya 5.722–724)

This does not mean that the devotee became God. The Lord manifests Himself in the heart and body of His eternal servant,
investing him with His power (*avesha*); the servant remains a servant, and that is why his words spoken “in the mood
of the Lord” are not understood by everyone. <!-- F11 -->

With Nityananda and Gadadhara Dasa, Abhirama came to Panihati, and first of all they visited the house of Raghava
Pandita; the kirtana begun there filled the whole world with bliss. Murari Gupta relates that in Panihati Nityananda's
friends — “Sri Rama, Sundara, Gauridasa” — appeared as cowherd boys, with flutes and horns; “Sri Rama” here is
apparently he. <!-- F15 -->

There too, in Panihati, he was at the festival of flat rice and yogurt (chida-dadhi) that Raghunatha Dasa held for
Nityananda. On the raised platform with Nityananda sat His companions:

“Ramadasa, Sundarananda, Gadadhara Dasa, Murari, Kamalakara, Sadashiva, Purandara.” (CC, Antya 6.61)

“Sri Ramadasa and the other cowherd boys were overwhelmed with love: on the bank of the Ganga they felt they were on
the bank of the Yamuna.” (CC, Antya 6.90)

Srila Bhaktisiddhanta explains in the “Anubhashya” that this Ramadasa is Abhirama Thakura, the cowherd boy. <!-- F18 -->

## The Power of Nityananda
At the command of Nityananda, who crushes godlessness, Abhirama Thakura became an acharya and preacher of bhakti; so
writes Srila Bhaktisiddhanta in the “Anubhashya.” The biographies call him a mighty acharya and a great preacher of
Gaudiya Vaishnavism: preaching bhakti at Nityananda's command, he delivered many godless men and opponents of the
Vaishnava faith. <!-- F16 -->

The old brahmana of Khanakula describes him to Srinivasa thus:

“The power of Abhirama Gosvami is formidable: seeing him, even invincible unbelievers tremble. He is ever mad with
absorption in Nityananda; his charming mercy is known throughout the world.” (“Bhakti-ratnakara” 4.103–104)

The “Anubhashya” quotes these verses too. The biographies retell them so: the blasphemers of Vaishnavas feared him and
fled at the mere sight of him, while he, invested with Nityananda's power, was always in ecstasy and merciful to all
the fallen. <!-- F17 Q5 -->

He is formidable only to godlessness. The power before which the godless tremble is Nityananda's power in him:
Nityananda has entered His servant (*avesha*) and acts through him, and the work of that power is not punishment but the
deliverance of the fallen. <!-- I2 -->
