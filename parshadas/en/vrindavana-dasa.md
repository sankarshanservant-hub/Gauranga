# Vrindavana Dasa Thakura
*Вриндаван Дас Тхакур · @vrindavana-dasa*

## The Vyasa of Sri Chaitanya's Pastimes
Sri Vrindavana Dasa Thakura is the author of the “Sri Chaitanya-bhagavata,” the book in which Mahaprabhu's pastimes
appeared in all their sweetness. Vaishnavas call him Thakura Vrindavana and Thakura Mahashaya, and also the son of
Narayani: so, “the son of Narayani” (*narayanira nandana*), Krishnadasa Kaviraja too calls him. He himself, in the
refrain of every chapter, signs simply “Vrindavana Dasa,” and in the list of Nityananda's companions he places himself
as the last of His servants. The biographies add other praises to his name: “the Vyasa of Sri Chaitanya's pastimes,”
“non-different from Vedavyasa,” “an incarnation of Vyasa,” “the servant of Nityananda,” “the last servant of
Nityananda,” “the biographer of Mahaprabhu.”[^1] <!-- F1 -->

The first of these praises is not a compliment to a skilful writer but a statement of who he is. Kavi Karnapura says in
the “Gaura-ganoddesha-dipika”:

“He who was Vedavyasa is now Vrindavana Dasa; for the sake of the work the friend Kusumapida also entered into him.”
(“Gaura-ganoddesha-dipika” 109)

He who divided the Vedas and gave the world the “Srimad-Bhagavatam” came into Gaura's pastimes to describe these as
well; and together with him, for the same service, Kusumapida, Krishna's friend in Vraja, entered into him. Srila
Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati, too, cites this verse in his “Anubhashya” on the “Chaitanya-charitamrita.” <!-- F2 Q7 -->

Krishnadasa Kaviraja Gosvami, who continued his work, says the same in his own words:

“Veda-vyasa told the pastimes of Krishna in the *Bhagavata*; the Vyasa of Chaitanya's pastimes is Vrindavana Dasa.”
(CC, Adi 8.34)

Kaviraja speaks this verse twice (again in Adi 11.55), and elsewhere he says plainly: “Vrindavana Dasa is the Vyasa of
Sri Chaitanya's pastimes” (Adi 13.48). And at the close of his whole book he calls him the original Vyasa:

“Vrindavana Dasa, the vessel of Nityananda's mercy, is the original Vyasa (*adi-vyasa*) of Chaitanya's pastimes.”
(CC, Antya 20.82)

Srila Bhaktisiddhanta explains in the “Anubhashya”: the pure acharyas who described Gaura's pastimes after him follow
this original Vyasa and, as his parts, are also called “Vyasa.” And Narahari Chakravarti says of him in the
“Bhakti-ratnakara”: “Thakura Vrindavana described the pastimes of the Lord… He is Vyasa himself; who can understand his
intention?” (1.213–214). One of the biographies remarks that it was probably from this comparison with Vyasa that his
book received its name, “Chaitanya-bhagavata.” Another says that by the mercy of the Lord's prasada he was born of
Narayani as an incarnation of Vyasa, that Gauranga and Nityananda were his life and soul, and that he is an eternal
servant of Nityananda. He himself closes his chapters with the words: “Sri Krishna Chaitanya and Sri Nityananda are my
life and soul!” <!-- F3 F4 Q6 -->

## The Son of Narayani
Of his family Vrindavana Dasa says almost nothing, but Kaviraja names his mother with reverence:

“Narayani partook of Chaitanya's remnants, and from her womb Sri Vrindavana Dasa was born.” (CC, Adi 8.41)

And elsewhere: “Vrindavana Dasa, the son of Narayani” (Adi 11.54). Vrindavana Dasa himself, having called himself the
last of Nityananda's servants, says only one thing about himself: “He was born from the womb of Narayani, who received
the remnants of Lord Chaitanya's food” (CB, Antya 5.757). Srila Bhaktisiddhanta writes on this verse in the
“Gaudiya-bhashya”: the author of the book is introduced not through his father's line — he is renowned through his
mother, a devotee of Gaura. And where Vrindavana Dasa tells how little Narayani received the remnants of the Lord's
meal, Srila Bhaktisiddhanta heads the passage: “The mother of the author of the book, Narayani, receives the remnants of
Sri Chaitanya's food,” adding that in speaking of his mother the author is describing a story of the past. <!-- F5 -->

Narayani was the daughter of a brother of Srivasa Pandita. So Vrindavana Dasa himself calls her — “the daughter of
Srivasa's brother” (Madhya 2.321, 10.291) — and so Srila Bhaktisiddhanta writes in the “Anubhashya.” According to
tradition her father was Nalina Pandita, the eldest of Srivasa's brothers.[^2] Srivasa Pandita, then, was Vrindavana
Dasa's great-uncle, and Srivasa's wife, Malini Devi, his great-aunt. Of Srivasa himself one of the biographies reminds
us that he lived first in Shrihatta, then by the Ganga in Navadvipa, and after the Lord's disappearance in Kumarahatta;
his brothers were Shripati, Shrirama and Shrinidhi, and Srivasa's only son left the world very young. <!-- F6 F7 -->

Who Narayani is in the eternal pastimes, Kavi Karnapura tells us:

“Ambika's sister, named Srila Kilimbika, who partook of Krishna's remnants, is considered to be Narayani.”
(“Gaura-ganoddesha-dipika” 43)

Ambika is Krishna's nurse, in Gaura's pastimes Malini; her younger sister Kilimbika ate the remnants of Krishna's meals in
Vraja — and in Navadvipa, as Narayani, ate the remnants of Gaura's meals. The “Anubhashya” gives this identification as
well. <!-- F8 -->

Narayani grew up in Srivasa's house and witnessed its greatest pastimes. At the time of the Mahaprakasha, when the Lord
revealed Himself in Srivasa's house, she was four years old:

“Then the Lord cast His glance on Narayani, Srivasa's little niece, who stood before Him. Even now all the Vaishnavas
honour Narayani, because the special mercy of Lord Chaitanya fell to her. Sri Gauranga, the Supreme Soul of all living
beings, ordered her: ‘Narayani, chant the names of Krishna with tears in your eyes!’ And the four-year-old child
suddenly began to behave like one mad, weeping in complete oblivion and crying out ‘O Krishna!’ Tears poured in streams
from her eyes, falling straight to the ground.” (CB, Madhya 2.321–325)

The Lord gave her the remnants of His meals, and so the Vaishnavas still call her “the partaker of Chaitanya's
remnants” (*chaitanyera avashesha-patra*). Vrindavana Dasa tells of this too: “Fortunate Narayani also received the
remnants of Lord Chaitanya's meal. The little girl Narayani was the daughter of Srivasa's brother. The Lord blessed her
with the remnants of His food” (Madhya 10.290–291); “even today everyone in the Vaishnava community remembers that
Narayani received Gauranga's remnants” (10.296). <!-- F9 Q3 -->

## Father and Family
Vrindavana Dasa never once names his father in his book. Kavi Karnapura, Murari Gupta and Kaviraja Gosvami are silent
about him as well; of his forefathers almost nothing is known beyond his kinship with Narayani. Srila Bhaktisiddhanta sees in this not
a gap but a principle: the Thakura is known through his mother, for to introduce a Vaishnava there is no need to name
his forefathers, and they are left out (“Anubhashya” on CC, Adi 8.41). A Vaishnava is known by his devotion, not by his
pedigree. <!-- F10 -->

The father's name has been kept only by later tradition. According to the “Prema-vilasa,” after the Lord's sannyasa
Srivasa and Shrirama moved to Kumarahatta; Narayani was given in marriage to a brahmana named Vaikuntha Dasa, and he left
this world while she was carrying her son, and Srivasa took her to himself. The “Gaudiya-vaishnava-abhidhana,” relying on
the same book, names him Sri Vaikuntha Dasa Vipra, in another place Vaikunthanatha Vipra, and writes that his forefathers
lived in Shrihatta; one of the biographies adds that Narayani, widowed, left her husband's home in Kumarahatta
(Halisahar) and settled with Srivasa in Navadvipa.[^3] According to other accounts, Narayani was married in
Mamgachhi, to a man of the same family as Malini Devi, whose parental home was in Mamgachhi and to which Malini returned
to live in her old age.[^4] <!-- F10 F11 -->

His family was brahmana: he was born into a family of brahmanas of the *Radhiya* class. Later certain prakrita-sahajiyas of
Radhadesha, under the influence of the smartas, decided that he was not a brahmana; but this is an empty rumour: he was a
true, pure brahmana who simply did not share the convictions of the smartas. <!-- F12 -->

## Appearance
Where Vrindavana Dasa was born is not known for certain. It is said that it was in Mamgachhi, a village on the bank of
the Bhagirathi, on Modadruma-dvipa of Navadvipa, in eastern Bardhaman district, a mile from the railway station of
Purvasthali on the Howrah–Katwa line. In Mamgachhi his house and the place of his childhood play are still shown, and
here too stands the temple of Vasudeva Datta's Madana-Gopala. So the old residents hold, and so does our line: in the
calendar of the Sri Chaitanya Saraswat Math “the place of appearance of Srila Vrindavana Dasa Thakura” is marked on
Modadruma-dvipa, and pilgrims visit it on Gaura-chaturdashi, during the Navadvipa parikrama. According to other
accounts he was born in Kumarahatta (Halisahar); the “Prema-vilasa” says he was born in Srivasa's house.[^5] <!-- F13 -->

The year of his birth is not given either. But one thing is clear from his own words: he did not witness the Lord's
pastimes in Navadvipa. Having told of them, he laments:

“Woe is me, a sinner! I was not fortunate enough to be born at that time! To my great misfortune I did not see these
deeds!” (CB, Adi 12.284)

He repeats almost the same at the beginning of the Madhya-khanda (Madhya 1.399),[^6] and after describing the great
festival in Srivasa's house he says:

“I am surely sinful, for I was not born at that time and could not see this great festival.” (CB, Madhya 8.198)

Srila Bhaktisiddhanta explains in the “Gaudiya-bhashya”: in his humility the author calls himself a sinner because he
was not born when these pastimes were manifest and did not see the Lord's dancing; and the passage at Madhya 1.399 he
heads: “The grief and prayer of the author, who did not see the Lord's pastimes of study.” Nor is there in his book any
hint that he saw the Lord in Nilachala. Vrindavana Dasa, then, came into this world after the Lord had left Navadvipa.
One of the biographies supposes that when Mahaprabhu disappeared (in 1455 Shaka, 1533 CE) he was fifteen at most;
another, that he was born four years after the Lord's sannyasa and was barely twenty in the year of His disappearance;
a third writes that he appeared sometime after 1510.[^7] <!-- F14 F16 Q2 -->

His appearance day is celebrated, according to the calendar of the Sri Chaitanya Saraswat Math, in the month of
Trivikrama, on the twelfth lunar day of the dark fortnight (Krishna-dvadashi); in 2026 it fell on 14 May.[^8] <!-- F15 -->

## Childhood in Mamgachhi
Wherever he was born, his childhood was spent in Mamgachhi, in the family home of his great-aunt Malini Devi. Srila
Bhaktisiddhanta writes in his commentary on the “Chaitanya-bhagavata” that in this house little Vrindavana grew up
surrounded by care, like a precious jewel. According to another account, Narayani raised her “precious son” here until he
was ten. <!-- F17 -->

Another tradition, too, has come down from those years. Narayani lost her husband early and lived very simply; she and
her son were cared for by Vasudeva Datta, the great devotee whose temple of Madana-Gopala stands in Mamgachhi. According
to the “Prema-vilasa,” the five-year-old Vrindavana settled with his mother at this temple; Vasudeva Datta kept them with
great care, and there the boy studied and became a pandita. One of the biographies relates that Narayani served in
Vasudeva Datta's house, and that her son, once born, soon began his studies. Thus service in a Vaishnava's house and the
protection of a great devotee surrounded Vrindavana Dasa from his first years.[^9] <!-- F18 -->

One of the biographies also writes that after the father left this world mother and son moved to Srivasa's house and received
special blessings from Mahaprabhu there. But Vrindavana Dasa himself, as we have seen, says that he did not witness the
Lord's pastimes in Navadvipa — and we follow his words.[^10] <!-- F19 -->

## The Last Servant of Nityananda
Vrindavana Dasa regarded Sri Nityananda Prabhu as his Lord and guru. Having listed His companions, he concludes:

“I have mentioned here only those whom I knew. All the others Vedavyasa will describe in future. The last of His servants
was Vrindavana Dasa. He was born from the womb of Narayani, who received the remnants of Lord Chaitanya's food.” (CB,
Antya 5.756–757)

He was an initiated disciple of Nityananda; one of the biographies writes that he received initiation soon after the
Lord's disappearance, and the “Gaudiya-vaishnava-abhidhana” calls him a mantra-disciple of Nityananda Prabhu. Kaviraja
Gosvami says that he wrote his book as “the vessel of Nityananda's mercy” (CC, Antya 20.82). Nityananda blessed him
generously — and that, one of the biographies writes, is why he is regarded as His initiated disciple. From the words
“the last of His servants” another biography infers that after Vrindavana Dasa's initiation Nityananda remained manifest
in this world only a short time; otherwise He would have had other disciples who came after. <!-- F20 F21 Q1 -->

The same biography reads that verse also as a word of humility: “I have neither virtue nor piety for which Nityananda
should accept me as a servant. Only because I was born of Narayani, glorified by the Vaishnavas and the partaker of
Chaitanya's remnants — out of love and mercy for my mother Sri Nityananda accepted me, worthless as I am, as His
servant.” <!-- F22 -->

Nityananda was everything to him. He calls Him by a multitude of names — Nityananda Avadhuta, Nityananda-chandra,
Nityananda Prabhu — and says of Mahaprabhu: “Sri Gaurasundara is the Lord of my Master” (CB, Madhya 17.117; cf. Madhya
28.191). For him Nityananda is “my Prabhu, my Lord”; Kaviraja, following Svarupa Damodara, reveals His greatness in
the fifth chapter of the Adi-lila as Vibhu. Srila Bhaktisiddhanta writes in the “Gaudiya-bhashya” on Adi 17.154–158: having
received Nityananda's mercy and the right to serve Him, Vrindavana Thakura became the acharya-guru of the whole world.
Nityananda was his spiritual master and the meaning of his whole life. <!-- F23 -->

## The “Sri Chaitanya-bhagavata”
Vrindavana Dasa's only work is the “Sri Chaitanya-bhagavata.” At first the book was called the “Chaitanya-mangala”: so
Kaviraja Gosvami calls it throughout the “Chaitanya-charitamrita” (Adi 8.33, 8.35, 11.54) — and later the name
“Chaitanya-bhagavata” became established. Srila Bhaktisiddhanta confirms that it is one and the same book: Vrindavana
Dasa Thakura too wrote under the title “Chaitanya-mangala,” and it is this book that Kaviraja mentions, while it became
famous as the “Sri Chaitanya-bhagavata.” Who gave it its new name is told differently. According to one biography, it
was renamed when Lochana Dasa called his own poem by the same name; according to another, “some think” that the new name
was given by Lochana Dasa and Krishnadasa Kaviraja, probably following the comparison of its author with Vyasa; according
to the “Prema-vilasa,” by Vrindavana Dasa's own devotees.[^11] The same name, it is thought, is hinted at by Lochana Dasa
when at the beginning of his poem he praises its author:

“With one mind I praise Sri Vrindavana Dasa, by whose song, the *Bhagavata*, the world is enchanted.”
(“Chaitanya-mangala,” Sutra-khanda 35)

<!-- F24 Q10 F47 -->

When the book was written is also told differently: according to one biography, in 1535 (so too the
“Gaudiya-vaishnava-abhidhana” — 1457 Shaka); according to the “Prema-vilasa,” in 1495 Shaka.[^12] Vrindavana Dasa wrote
it, according to tradition, in Denur; there a temple is shown where the “Chaitanya-bhagavata” was composed, and an old
manuscript of the book has been preserved. <!-- F25 F26 -->

Vrindavana Dasa tells most of all of Mahaprabhu's early pastimes — His childhood, His studies, the subduing of the Kazi,
His leaving Navadvipa; the Adi-khanda goes from the Lord's birth to His return from Gaya. The pastimes in Puri are
described only in part: the author was so immersed in the pastimes of Nityananda that he told the Lord's
later pastimes briefly. Kaviraja says so himself:

“First he set down all the pastimes in a brief summary, and afterwards described them at length. The pastimes of
Chaitanya-chandra are endless and boundless, and as he went on describing them the book grew long. Seeing its length, he
grew somewhat hesitant, and some of the pastimes held in the summary he did not describe. He became absorbed in
describing the pastimes of Nityananda, and Chaitanya's final pastimes were left untold. The devotees of Vrindavana longed
to hear the account of all those pastimes.” (CC, Adi 8.45–49)

What Vrindavana Dasa left out, Kaviraja supplied: “Fearing that his book would grow too long, he left out certain parts,
and of these I shall say something” (Adi 13.49). What is described in detail in the “Chaitanya-bhagavata” he only
mentions, and he tells more fully what Vrindavana Dasa gave in brief — and he bows at his feet:

“Therefore I bow down at his feet, praying that I may not offend him.” (CC, Madhya 4.9) <!-- F27 Q6 -->

In Vrindavana the “Chaitanya-bhagavata” was heard constantly. Ananta Acharya, a disciple of Gadadhara Pandita, and his
beloved disciple Haridasa Pandita read it, and, in Kaviraja's words, “by his grace all the Vaishnavas hear it too” (Adi
8.63); there they longed to hear the Lord's last pastimes, and Haridasa Pandita ordered Kaviraja to describe them (Adi
8.65). According to the “Prema-vilasa,” the “Chaitanya-bhagavata,” formerly called the “Chaitanya-mangala,” was read at
the festival in Kheturi too. <!-- F28 -->

How Kaviraja Gosvami valued this book is seen from his praise in the eighth chapter of the Adi-lila:

“Vrindavana Dasa composed the *Chaitanya-mangala*, and by hearing it all that is inauspicious is destroyed. In it one
comes to know the glory of Chaitanya and Nitai, and in it I come to know the conclusions of devotion to Krishna to their
farthest limit. He understood the essence of all the conclusions of devotion in the *Bhagavata*, drew it out and set it
down in his book. If even an unbeliever or a yavana hears the *Chaitanya-mangala*, he becomes a great Vaishnava at that
very moment. No human being could compose so blessed a book: it is Sri Chaitanya who speaks through the mouth of
Vrindavana Dasa. Millions of obeisances at the feet of Vrindavana Dasa! By writing such a book he delivered the world.”
(CC, Adi 8.35–40)

This is preceded by the call: “O foolish people, hear the *Chaitanya-mangala*, and from it you will know all the glory of
Chaitanya!” (Adi 8.33); and further Kaviraja adds: “How wonderful is his description of Chaitanya's deeds! By hearing it
the three worlds were purified” (Adi 8.42). Srila Bhaktisiddhanta explains in the “Anubhashya”: the “Bhagavata” is the
original source of the teaching on bhakti, but it is vast, and its essence Vrindavana Dasa set down in the
“Chaitanya-bhagavata.” This book, therefore, is neither a chronicle nor the fruit of literary talent: the Lord Himself
speaks through the mouth of its author, and that is why hearing it turns even an unbeliever into a Vaishnava.
<!-- F29 Q5 I3 -->

The same glory of Gaura lives in its very style. It is clear and touches the heart; in telling of the life of Haridasa
Thakura, of what society was like when Gauranga was manifest, of the brahmanas of those days who uttered the Lord's
names, such as “Pundarikaksha,” only “on occasion,” and above all of the greatness and glory of Gaurasundara, Vrindavana
Dasa showed such art that lovers of Bengali letters find extraordinary joy in his book. But this beauty is only the
garment in which the Lord's glory is revealed. <!-- F30 -->

Vrindavana Dasa relied on the “kadacha,” the notes of Murari Gupta: following them, as did Lochana Dasa and others, he
described Mahaprabhu's pastimes. He knew, for instance, that in Varanasi Mahaprabhu for a time stayed hidden in the math
of Ramachandra Puri. <!-- F32 -->

He does not tell of all the Lord's companions. In his book there is neither Narahari Sarakara nor Shrikhanda; there are
neither Lokanatha nor Bhugarbha, whom the “Chaitanya-charitamrita” names (perhaps they themselves forbade writing about
them); there are neither Prabodhananda Sarasvati nor Gopala Bhatta, neither the festival at Panihati nor even the name of
Raghunatha Dasa, nor Jiva Gosvami; nor is there the Lord's conversation with Vishnupriya before His sannyasa, which
Lochana Dasa relates. Sahajiyas see ill will in this silence, but pure devotees do not judge so: among Gaura's
companions there is no enmity, and poets deliberately leave part of the pastimes to those who will come later — to those
“Vyasas” whom Vrindavana Dasa himself foretells in his book. Some even say that Narahari's words “he who will write this
book is not yet born” refer to Vrindavana Dasa.[^13] <!-- F31 I4 -->

He stands firmly, too, against the “gaura-nagari” teaching, which presents Gaura as a nagara enjoying the company of young women:

“Therefore great souls, in their prayers to Lord Gauranga, never address Him as Gauranga-nagara. Although the Supreme Lord may be prayed to in many ways, wise people glorify only those
traits of character that belong to this avatara.” (CB, Adi 15.30–32)

It is said that this is why he did not mention Prabodhananda and Gopala Bhatta: in the “Chaitanya-chandramrita” (132)
Gaura is called “gaura-nagara-vara,” and this expression Vrindavana Dasa did not accept. <!-- F33 Q9 -->

## For the Honour of His Lord
More than once in the “Chaitanya-bhagavata,” having told of Nityananda's glory, Vrindavana Dasa turns to those who
blaspheme Him:

“And if, even after hearing of His glory, someone goes on blaspheming Him, I am ready to kick such an incorrigible
sinner on the head.” (CB, Madhya 11.63)

The same words sound in Adi 9.225 and 17.158, in Madhya 18.223 and 23.522, and in Antya 6.137. Foolish and proud people,
not understanding them, censure him for this and thereby fall into offence against a Vaishnava. Some worldly men of
letters, seeing his anger at Nityananda's blasphemers, even decided that the “incarnation of Vyasa” was subject to anger
like an ordinary man. But Srila Bhaktisiddhanta explains in the “Gaudiya-bhashya” on Adi 9.225: in these words of the most
magnanimous of men one sees what a servant's devotion to his Master is. He is ready even for this for the eternal good of the
blasphemers themselves; if but a speck of dust from his foot touches their heads, they will be freed from everything
unwanted in the heart. Outwardly his conduct, and that of the preachers who follow in his footsteps, may look like
punishment of the Lord's enemies, but within it is full of boundless compassion. <!-- F34 Q8 I2 -->

On Adi 17.154–158 Srila Bhaktisiddhanta adds: people hostile to Hari, guru and the Vaishnavas have not gathered enough
piety to receive the “blessed kick” of Thakura Vrindavana's foot; the prakrita-sahajiyas and other apa-sampradayas offend
him, while his true followers are those who strictly follow Nityananda in glorifying Gaura. Thus his severity is not
hot temper but the fidelity of a servant guarding the honour of his Master. <!-- F34 -->

## Humility
For all the greatness of his book, Vrindavana Dasa does not in the least seek to put himself forward in it: his name is
seen only in the refrain with which his chapters close — “so sings Vrindavana Dasa at Their feet.” Of Narayani he writes
that she is the daughter of Srivasa's brother and that “even now all the Vaishnavas honour Narayani, because the special
mercy of Lord Chaitanya fell to her” (Madhya 2.321–322) — but nowhere does he say that she is his mother, and nowhere
does he say that Srivasa is his great-uncle: he does not wish to display his own greatness. That is why Srila
Bhaktisiddhanta too notes in the “Anubhashya” that not a word is said of the Thakura's forefathers. <!-- F35 -->

With the same humility he speaks of his work itself:

“The tip of the tongue of Ananta Shesha (Nityananda) is the treasury of the narrations of Gaura's glory. Only by His mercy
can Sri Chaitanya's pastimes be revealed.” (CB, Adi 1.81)

“I see no beginning and no end to Sri Chaitanya's beautiful pastimes. Therefore I shall describe only what He gives me the
strength and ability to describe. As a wooden puppet dances by the will of the puppeteer, so I write in full obedience to
the wish of Sri Gaurachandra.” (CB, Adi 1.85–86)

He calls himself a sinner because he was not born in time, and a puppet in Gaura's hands. This is not self-abasement in
the worldly sense but the humility of an eternal associate: such unfeigned humility is impossible unless pure bhakti has
appeared in the heart. He hides his kinship with great devotees, does not write about himself, and ascribes all his
service only to Nityananda's mercy towards his mother. <!-- F36 Q4 I1 -->

## Denur
Of Vrindavana Dasa's marriage and family life nothing is known. For long periods he lived in Denur, a village in
Bardhaman district (Matreshvara police station), four kroshas west of Navadvipa; that is why Denur is regarded as his
shripata. According to the “Prema-vilasa,” he settled there after the three Prabhus — Mahaprabhu, Nityananda and, last,
Advaita — had disappeared. Here, according to the “Gaudiya-vaishnava-abhidhana,” the “Chaitanya-bhagavata” was written,
here he installed Nitai-Gaura, and here Narayani lived. Srila Bhaktivinoda Thakura visited Denur as the place of the
shripata of Vrindavana Dasa Thakura. <!-- F37 F38 -->

He had four disciples. One of them, Sri Ramahari, from a family of *uttara-radhiya kayasthas*, he made heir to all his
possessions in Denur; to this day Ramahari's family lives in the Thakura's home at Denur (Denur-pat) and keeps up the
worship there. Ramahari was a pure Vaishnava, initiated according to all the rules, but later his descendants, under the
pressure of the smartas, abandoned sadhana and took to following the smarta code.[^14] According to the
“Gaudiya-vaishnava-abhidhana,” Ramahari Dasa received the service of Nitai-Gaura, and the other disciples were Shachi
Dasa (service of Radhakanta in Chakta), Gopinatha (of Vrindavana-chandra in Vishnugram) and Debidasa (of Shyamasundara in
Santari). <!-- F39 -->

In Mamgachhi Gaura and Nityananda, whom Vrindavana Dasa served, are worshipped every day to this day; Deities of
Nitai-Gaura connected with him are honoured in Denur too.[^15] <!-- F40 -->

Vrindavana Dasa was a profound knower of the scriptures on bhakti, lived by the Vaishnava way of life, and sought only to
glorify guru and the Vaishnavas. He led a brahmana community that was truly pure and never bowed to the
smartas, who were hostile to the highest good — he was the chief guardian of the preaching of the pure bhakti revealed by
Sri Chaitanyachandra. The smartas envied Nityananda and did not honour His servant; they sought grounds for gossip
everywhere — in the story of Radharamana in Advaita's family, in the image of Durga among Virachandra's disciples, in the
marriages of Ganga Thakurani and of Nityananda Himself, in the fact that the initiated Uddharana was honoured as a
brahmana — and all this hindered his wish to glorify the Vaishnavas. But nothing distracted him from the
“Chaitanya-bhagavata”; his slanderers later fell at his feet, and Sri Chaitanyachandra did not allow his honour to be
sullied. <!-- F41 -->

Together with Sri Jahnava, Vrindavana Dasa came to the great festival at Kheturi (“Bhakti-ratnakara” 10.376–377), and the
presence of Jahnava, Raghunandana, Achyutananda, Vrindavana Dasa and other eternal associates gladdened everyone
assembled. The “Prema-vilasa” also names him among the branches of Nityananda at the memorial festivals in Katwa and
Khanda: “Vrindavana Dasa, who revealed the ‘Chaitanya-mangala.’” <!-- F42 Q11 -->

## Departure
When Vrindavana Dasa left this world is not known for certain. The “Gaudiya-vaishnava-abhidhana” writes that, “as some
say,” it was in 1511 Shaka (1589 CE), but this is mere conjecture.[^16] The day of his departure is observed by Vaishnavas,
according to the calendar of the Sri Chaitanya Saraswat Math, in the month of Madhusudana, on the tenth lunar day of the
dark fortnight (Krishna-dashami); in 2026 this day fell on 12 April. Where he left this world and where his samadhi is,
the sources do not say; there is known only a photograph of the samadhi of Srila Vrindavana Dasa Thakura, without the
place being named. According to the “Gaudiya-vaishnava-abhidhana,” having handed over the service to Ramahari, he went
to Vrindavana. <!-- F43 F46 -->

## Places of Remembrance
Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati founded a temple at Vrindavana Dasa's birthplace in Mamgachhi, not far from the house of
Saranga Murari, and installed Deities at the place of his birth on Modadruma-dvipa. A new temple there was opened on 25
February 1934, in the year of Srila Bhaktisiddhanta's sixtieth birthday. On Gaura-chaturdashi, according to the calendar
of the Sri Chaitanya Saraswat Math, pilgrims go round Modadruma-dvipa, “the places of appearance of Srila Vrindavana Dasa
Thakura and of the Deities of Sri Saranga Murari and Vasudeva Datta Thakura.” Next to the house where Vrindavana Dasa
lived (Bamunpara) was born Srila Bhakti Sundar Govinda Dev-Goswami Maharaj. Much about his life, one of the biographies
writes, can be learned by visiting Mamgachhi. <!-- F44 -->

## In the Memory of the Devotees
Narahari Chakravarti, in the “Namamrita-samudra,” his prayers to the Lord's companions, turns to him as well:

“O Vrindavana, son of Narayani! While you are here, why should such be my lot?” (“Namamrita-samudra” 53)

<!-- F45 -->

In the “Bhakti-ratnakara” he exclaims: “Glory to Vrindavana Dasa, the ocean of virtues!” (1.34) — and says that the great
poets of the past, though they could have told everything, deliberately left part of the pastimes to later poets, while
Thakura Vrindavana, Vyasa himself, said: “Vedavyasa will describe it later” (1.211–216). Lochana Dasa in the
“Chaitanya-mangala” names “Parameshvara Dasa and Vrindavana Dasa” among the associates who appeared on earth (8.628).
Srila Bhaktisiddhanta, in his preface to Lochana's “Chaitanya-mangala,” counts Vrindavana Dasa among the trustworthy
biographers of the Lord — together with Kavi Karnapura, Murari Gupta and Kaviraja Gosvami. Jayananda mentions his book in
three khandas. Later tradition ascribed to him many more books — the “Nityananda-vamsha-vistara,” the
“Gauranga-vilasa,” the “Chaitanya-lilamrita,” the “Bhajana-nirnaya,” the “Bhakti-chintamani” and others — but his work is
one: the “Chaitanya-bhagavata.”[^17] The “Gaudiya-vaishnava-abhidhana” also mentions his close friend Gopinatha.

## Significance
Vrindavana Dasa Thakura is the Vedavyasa of Gaura's pastimes: he who gave the world the “Srimad-Bhagavatam” came to
reveal Sri Chaitanya's “Bhagavata” too — and for this service Kusumapida, Krishna's friend, dwelt in him. His book, in
Kaviraja's word, is the essence of the “Bhagavata” and the farthest limit of the conclusions of bhakti; through it Sri
Chaitanya Himself speaks, and hearing it makes even an unbeliever a Vaishnava. The beauty of its style is a testimony to
the glory of Gaura revealed in it. <!-- F2 F3 F29 F30 I3 -->

In his life the nature of a pure servant is seen. He, “the last of Nityananda's servants,” hid his kinship with great
devotees and ascribed everything to the Lord's mercy towards his mother; his stern words to Nityananda's blasphemers are
not the anger of a worldly man but compassion and fidelity to his Master; and his silence about certain associates is not
ill will, for among Gaura's companions there is no enmity, and there are those to describe the Lord's pastimes after
him. <!-- I1 I2 I4 -->
