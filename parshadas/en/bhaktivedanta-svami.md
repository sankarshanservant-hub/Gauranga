# A. C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupada
*His Divine Grace A. C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupada · @bhaktivedanta-svami*

## The Founder-Acharya
His Divine Grace A. C. Bhaktivedanta Swami was a disciple of Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati and the founder-acharya
of the International Society for Krishna Consciousness (ISKCON); his followers call him Srila Prabhupada. One of the
biographies glorifies him as a rasika-acharya — a teacher who knows the taste of pure devotion just as Sri Chaitanya
gave it to the world. Such a gift is rare, and the biographer recalls the words of the “Srimad-Bhagavatam”: “Among
many millions of the liberated and the perfected, O great sage, a peaceful devotee of Narayana is rarely found”.
(Srimad-Bhagavatam, 6.14.5) <!-- F1 F2 Q1 -->

His whole life grew out of a single command. In 1922, at their very first meeting, Srila Bhaktisiddhanta told him
to preach the teaching of Sri Chaitanya throughout the world; forty-three years later, at nearly seventy, the
disciple sailed for America alone, without money or companions, to carry that command out. <!-- F28 F59 -->

## Childhood and Youth
Srila Bhaktivedanta Swami appeared in this world on 1 September 1896 in Calcutta — on Nandotsava, the day after
Janmashtami[^1]. The boy was named Abhay Charan De. His father, Gour Mohan De, and his mother were Vaishnavas who
worshipped Krishna. His father’s house, as Srila Govinda Maharaj recalled, stood on “Hazen Road”, in one of the best
quarters of the city. <!-- F18 F12 F20 F113 -->

Following Bengali custom, the parents called an astrologer to cast the child’s horoscope. Among other things he
foretold that when the boy reached seventy he would cross the ocean, become a great preacher and open a hundred and
eight temples. The book “Our Teachers”, published by our mission, says that this prediction came true in
full. <!-- F19 -->

Abhay grew up in Vedic culture, and from the age of about six he was already serving the Deities of Sri Radha and Sri
Krishna himself. When the time came for study, his father sent him to the Scottish Church College in Calcutta. The
times were troubled: Hindus and Muslims were at odds, the movement for independence from Britain was gaining
strength, and the college schooled young men in Western ways, its professors often disparaging the ancient culture
of India without any ground. Abhay, raised in a Vaishnava family, saw the narrowness of such an education, yet he
went on studying because his family wished it. <!-- F20 F21 -->

Absorbed in his studies, he gave up the worship, and the Deities were put away in a box for a time. Then Krishna
appeared to him in a dream and asked: “Why have you put Me in a box? Why do you not worship Me?” Abhay returned to
his service. Many years later he wrote to one of his disciples: “There has never been a moment in my life when I did
not remember Krishna. I have always known Him, from my very birth.” <!-- F22 F23 -->

While still a student Abhay was married; the match was arranged by his father. Like many of his generation he
answered Mahatma Gandhi’s call: in 1920 he sat the examinations of his fourth year and, in protest, refused his
diploma. What drew him to Gandhi was the conviction that spiritual values must rule the life of society, together
with loyalty to the Vedic tradition. He then entered the service of Dr. Kartik Chandra Bose, a well-known Calcutta
surgeon and pharmaceutical manufacturer; but the work occupied his thoughts far less than his spiritual
search. <!-- F24 F25 F26 -->

## Meeting His Guru
Abhay first heard of Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati in 1922. He had seen many sadhus — his father welcomed them
warmly into his home — but some of those meetings had brought him nothing but disappointment. So when a friend
invited him to a festival of the Gaudiya Math to meet the Math’s acharya, Abhay was sceptical; still, the friend
persuaded him. <!-- F27 -->

The first words they heard from Srila Bhaktisiddhanta were a call: “You are educated young men. You must preach the
teaching of Lord Sri Chaitanya throughout the world.” That meeting turned Abhay’s life around. <!-- F28 -->

He put a pointed question: who would listen to such preaching while India was a dependent country? Should not
independence come first? Srila Bhaktisiddhanta answered: “Krishna consciousness does not depend on who is in power.
Humanity needs it so badly that we have no right to wait until India gains independence.” Every government is
temporary, he explained; Krishna consciousness alone is eternal, and a person’s true self is the soul. Then he quoted
in Sanskrit the verse of the “Bhagavad-gita” in which the Lord calls on us to give up all other duties and surrender
to Him alone (by its sense, 18.66). <!-- F29 Q12 -->

The guru, “Our Teachers” observes, is not an external authority or an office-holder to be obeyed blindly: he gives
convincing answers to spiritual questions and dispels doubts, and by hearing him a sincere disciple is freed from
every obstacle on the path. So it was with Abhay. Later he recalled that on that evening he had, in essence, accepted
Srila Bhaktisiddhanta as his guru: “Not formally,” he would say, “but in my heart. I understood that I had met a
real saint.” <!-- I5 F30 -->

The business of the pharmaceutical firm took Abhay and his family to Allahabad (Prayaga). There he rented a house and
a small shop and sold Dr. Bose’s products; Srila Govinda Maharaj recalled that Jawaharlal Nehru and Indira Gandhi used
to come to him as customers. In 1928 Bhakti Pradipa Tirtha, one of the leading sannyasis of the Gaudiya Math, opened a
branch of the mission in Allahabad and asked Abhay for financial help. He gave it gladly and often invited the
devotees of the Math to his home. <!-- F31 F113 -->

“Our Teachers” calls Gour Mohan De a great Vaishnava. Before his departure in 1930 he asked his son to support the
preaching of the Gaudiya Math in every way and to do all he could for Srila Bhaktisiddhanta’s disciples. Abhay loved
his father dearly and fulfilled his wish. <!-- F32 -->

In 1932 Abhay received initiation from Srila Bhaktisiddhanta — harinama and the gayatri mantra on the same day; he
had been recommended to the guru by Srila B. K. Tapasvi Maharaj. He still remained a family man and travelled on the
business of a pharmaceutical firm. He wished to become rich, but only so as to serve his guru’s mission with money.
He could not travel with Srila Bhaktisiddhanta or see him often, yet he planned his journeys so as to be in Calcutta
whenever his guru was there. The thirty years he was still to spend in India are the story of a single desire that
grew stronger in him year by year: to fulfil his guru’s will and preach Krishna consciousness throughout the
world. <!-- F42 F43 -->

To his followers he was father and friend. The biographer sees in him a shaktyavesha-avatara — an envoy invested with
a special power of the Lord — and calls him “nityanandavesha”: the power of Sripada Nityananda Prabhu worked through
him. Flawless humility and full surrender to the Lord were joined in him with high demands on his disciples; yet his
insistence, too, was for the Lord’s sake — the active humility of one who carries out His will. Perfection and
liberation can be reached even without humility, the biographer observes, but pure devotion stands on humility
alone; Srila Bhaktivedanta Swami had both. <!-- F3 F4 I1 -->

## Preaching in the West
Shortly before setting out across the ocean, he asked Bhakti Ballabha Tirtha to come with him to America; but that
devotee, then secretary of the Sri Chaitanya Gaudiya Math, was serving his guru and humbly declined. In 1965 Srila
Bhaktivedanta Swami came to America — in his own words, “as an aggressor”, to challenge illusion. <!-- F8 F5 -->

For twelve years, until his departure in 1977, he travelled the world, transplanting into foreign soil what many had
thought to be merely an Indian faith. He showed in practice that the teaching of the Gaudiya Vaishnavas belongs to no
single culture: it is the dharma of the soul, the eternal nature of everyone. He named Mahaprabhu’s living teaching
“Krishna consciousness” and carried it farther than the Gaudiya thinkers of his time had thought possible. To attack
illusion, the biographer says, is the highest service to people: its price is humility in the face of ingratitude,
and its reward is the dignity of the soul restored to many. What some sought by the sword and others by wealth he
achieved with a humble heart, flooding the world with love of God — and a day will come when all nations sing his
glory. <!-- F6 F7 I2 I3 -->

In 1967, back from America, he came at the invitation of the temple’s custodians to the temple of Uddharana Datta in
Saptagram, bringing his American disciples. The whole suvarna-vanik community honours this temple, and as a child he
had visited it with his parents; he tells of this himself in his commentary on the “Sri Chaitanya-charitamrita”
(Adi 11.41). <!-- F9 -->

## His Godbrothers
Among the disciples of Srila Bhaktisiddhanta, Srila Bhaktivedanta Swami — like Bhakti Prajnana Keshava, Bhakti
Saranga Goswami, Bhakti Dayita Madhava, Bhakti Pramoda Puri and others — regarded Srila B. R. Sridhar Maharaj as his
shiksha-guru. In a letter he writes that he accepts Sripad Sridhar Maharaj’s instruction “on my head”: he is his
constant well-wisher, and after the departure of Srila Sarasvati Thakura it is fitting to follow his guidance; from
him, too, he received the instruction to live “in this country” for good. <!-- F10 F11 -->

Bhakti Vidhan Mahayogi Swami, translating into English one of the biographies of the Lord’s associates, calls both
Srila Bhaktivedanta Swami and Srila Sridhar Maharaj his beloved spiritual masters, and relies on Srila Bhaktivedanta
Swami’s translation of the “Sri Chaitanya-charitamrita” with commentary. He also writes that Lochana Dasa’s song
“parama karuna pahun dui jana”, on the mercy of Nitai and Gaurachandra, was especially dear to Srila Bhaktivedanta
Swami. He explained it thus: Nitai-Gaura are the essence of all avataras, and the path They give is joyful — sing and
dance. <!-- F15 F16 F17 -->

## Departure
Srila Bhaktivedanta Swami left this world in 1977, on the Gaura Chaturthi of the month of Damodara. His appearance day
is celebrated on the Krishna Navami of the month of Hrishikesha — Nandotsava, the day after Janmashtami[^2].
<!-- F12 -->

## Legacy
To all his followers he left a legacy of love, not of law. He gave to everyone, yet searched for the one who would
truly understand his message. His inner form, the biographer says, is revealed only to those who accept the essence
of his teaching and example: one can see his true nature only by his will. Deep realisation is the ability to apply
spiritual truth to differing circumstances; an acharya does not freeze in time but flows out in love to everyone who
reaches, however slightly, for pure devotion. <!-- F13 F14 I4 -->

[^1]: The date and place of his appearance are given in the book “Our Teachers” (Sri Chaitanya Saraswat Math,
    ch. II.1); another biography does not give them. The day of the lunar calendar — the Krishna Navami of the month
    of Hrishikesha, Nandotsava — follows our calendar (Sri Chaitanya Saraswat Math); it agrees with the date of
    1 September 1896.

[^2]: The biography gives only the year of his departure (1977); the days of departure and appearance follow our
    calendar (Sri Chaitanya Saraswat Math).
