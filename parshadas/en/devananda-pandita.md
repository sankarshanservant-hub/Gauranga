# Devananda Pandita
*Девананда Пандит · @devananda-pandita*

## Reciter of the “Bhagavatam”
In the “Chaitanya-charitamrita,” in the list of the branches of Sri Chaitanya's tree, Krishnadasa Kaviraja speaks of him
in a single line:

“Devananda, the reciter of the *Bhagavata*, is an offshoot of Vakreshvara's branch. By Vakreshvara's mercy he received
from the Lord the devotional meaning of the *Bhagavata*.” (CC, Adi 10.77)

Devananda Pandita was a *bhagavati*, one who reads and expounds the “Bhagavatam.” This one line holds his whole story: a
learned expounder of the holy book did not know its essence until he served a devotee of the Lord, and then the Lord
Himself revealed to him that the essence of the “Bhagavatam” is bhakti.[^1] <!-- F1 Q9 -->

Who he is in Krishna's eternal pastimes, Kavi Karnapura tells us:

“Sri Devananda Pandita, the knower of the meaning of the Puranas, was formerly the sage Bhaguri, the pandit of Nanda's
assembly.” (“Gaura-ganoddesha-dipika” 106)

So too Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati in his “Anubhashya” to CC, Adi 10.77: in Vraja he is Bhaguri Muni, the pandit of
Nanda Maharaja's court.[^2] <!-- F2 Q1 -->

Little is known of his worldly life. He was a brahmana; the sources name neither his parents nor his family, nor the
year of his appearance or departure. <!-- F8 F40 -->

## Navadvipa and Kuliya
Vrindavana Dasa Thakura, beginning his account of Devananda in the “Chaitanya-bhagavata,” tells us where he lived:

“Thus the Lord Vishvambhara came to the embankment by the house of Visharada Maheshvara, father of Sarvabhauma
Bhattacharya. There lived a brahmana named Devananda Pandita — supremely peaceful, and desiring liberation.” (CB, Madhya
21.6–7)

So he lived near the house of Maheshvara Visharada, father of Sarvabhauma Bhattacharya, by the embankment (*jangal*)
along which the Lord would pass. Narahari Chakravarti in the “Bhakti-ratnakara,” leading a pilgrim through the holy
places of Navadvipa, points out this place too: “Here is the embankment of Visharada: here the Lord met Devananda, who
had offended Srivasa; at the Lord's chastisement in words he was grieved” (BR 12.2976–2977). <!-- F3 F4 Q2 Q5 -->

The school (*tol*) where Devananda taught the “Bhagavatam,” according to the biographies, stood in the village of Kuliya;
so also writes Srila Bhaktisiddhanta in the “Gaudiya-bhashya” to CB, Madhya 9.98: “Devananda's school was in Kuliya.”
One biography says that he also lived in Kuliya, near where the Sri Chaitanya Saraswat Math of Srila Sridhara Maharaja
now stands.[^3] And in the “Anubhashya” to CC, Madhya 16.207 Srila Bhaktisiddhanta names Vidyanagara on Jahnu-dvipa,
near Koladvipa — where Vidya-Vachaspati's house stood — as the place where he lived. <!-- F5 Q4 F43 -->

Kuliya, according to the “Gaudiya-bhashya,” was a suburb of Navadvipa on the Ganga's western shore; the town that bears
the name Navadvipa today is the old Kuliya, “the place where offences are destroyed” (*aparadha-bhanjaner pat*). One of the
biographies explains that the heart of Navadvipa then, Mayapur, lay on the eastern bank, and the old names of quarters
of the present town keep the memory of this: Koler-ganj, Koler-daha, Gadkhali-kol. <!-- F6 -->

Navadvipa-dhama consists of nine islands, each corresponding to one of the nine kinds of bhakti. Kuliya is Koladvipa,
the island of *pada-sevana*, service to the Lord's feet; in common speech “Kola” became “Kuliya.” The word “kola”
means a boar: here, in Satya-yuga, the Lord as Varaha showed Himself to the brahmana Vasudeva.[^4] <!-- F7 -->

## A Scholar without Bhakti
Vrindavana Dasa describes Devananda with respect:

“He was intelligent, discerning, austere and detached from birth. He studied the ‘Srimad-Bhagavatam,’ yet there was no
devotion to the Lord in him.” (CB, Madhya 21.8)

“At that time Devananda Pandita was honoured by all as a holy and wise scholar. He always taught the
‘Srimad-Bhagavatam.’ From early childhood he had taken the vow to live as a sannyasi.” (CB, Madhya 21.57–58)

He was endowed with many good qualities, peaceful by nature, and highly respected; he read the “Bhagavatam,” kept a
sannyasi's vows and had been celibate from childhood. Many studied under him, and he was famed for his knowledge of the
sayings of the “Bhagavatam.” <!-- F8 -->

But his renunciation was not warmed by devotion. One of the biographies says that because there was no bhakti in him,
his celibacy turned into an unwillingness to serve the devotees; therefore, though he kept his vows and walked the path
of renunciation, he did not obtain the fruit of these virtues. Another adds that for all his learning he did not
understand that the highest aim of the “Bhagavatam” is bhakti, and, aspiring to liberation, he extolled austerity and
renunciation in his expositions rather than service to the Lord.[^5] <!-- F9 F10 -->

Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati in the “Gaudiya-bhashya” judges him with care: Devananda was a *smarta*, yet a great
scholar and ascetic; he read nothing but the “Bhagavatam,” had faith in God and mastery over his senses — he lacked only
faith in Mahaprabhu. So speaks of him Vrindavana Dasa himself:

“From his very birth he was pious, detached and learned. He taught no scripture other than the ‘Srimad-Bhagavatam.’ He
was peaceful, restrained, master of his senses, free from greed and endowed with many other pious qualities. But he had
no faith in Lord Gaurachandra.” (CB, Antya 3.482–484) <!-- F11 Q13 -->

Not a villain but an ascetic and scholar who lacked one thing — bhakti: so the Vaishnava authors see him. That is what
makes his story instructive.

## The Offence to Srivasa
Once Srivasa Pandita came to Devananda to hear the “Bhagavatam.” Vrindavana Dasa relates:

“Srivasa's heart melted on hearing the ‘Srimad-Bhagavatam,’ for every syllable of it is full of ecstatic love for
Krishna. Srivasa was a maha-bhagavata brahmana, and as he listened he began to weep and sigh deeply. The wicked students
said: ‘What a disturbance! Brothers, we cannot study properly, and our time is being wasted.’” (CB, Madhya 21.60–62)

The biographies add details. Srivasa, a *rasika-bhakta*, fell to the ground at the very first verse; tears streamed from
his eyes, his voice choked, he rolled on the floor. The students shouted: “Madman! He doesn't listen properly — out with
him!” — and dragged him out by force. They came to Devananda only for knowledge of grammar and worldly recognition, and
so they did not understand Srivasa's bhajana. His weeping would not stop, and they never understood that this man, so
dear to Chaitanya, purifies the world, that the *sattvika-bhavas* of his spiritual body bring all purity to the world;
taking him for an ordinary emotional man, they put him out of the classroom so that the lesson could go on.
<!-- F12 F13 F14 -->

“Srivasa could not stop his tears. And though he was a close friend of Sri Chaitanya, able to deliver the whole
universe, the wicked students together pushed him out into the street. Devananda Pandita did not stop them. When the
guru has no desire to serve God, his disciples become the same. Returning to outer consciousness, Srivasa went home sad.”
(CB, Madhya 21.63–66)

Devananda saw this and did not stop his students — and so became guilty of *vaishnava-aparadha*, an offence against a
devotee. One of the biographies remarks that a disciple's sin committed through his teacher's ignorance falls on the
teacher too. Another says it more plainly: had Devananda been even slightly inclined to serve God, he would not have
let his ignorant students do such a thing; since he did not, both he and they proved to be mere disputing reciters,
lost in the sickness of sense objects. Without bhakti, vows and celibacy are fruitless: a teacher not inclined to serve
the Lord will not keep his students from offence and becomes guilty himself. <!-- F15 I3 -->

Srivasa went home saddened and said nothing to anyone. According to one of the biographies, all this happened before
the appearance of Sri Gauranga.[^6] <!-- F16 -->

But the Lord, who dwells in every heart, knew everything. When in Srivasa's house He revealed His glory in the
*mahaprakasha*, He reminded Srivasa of this incident:

“Mahaprabhu said: ‘Srivasa, do you remember how you once heard the “Bhagavatam” from the mouth of Devananda Pandita?
Every line of the “Bhagavatam” is full of prema-rasa, and your heart melted at those narrations. You began to weep loudly
and fell to the ground, overwhelmed by ecstatic feelings. ‹…› Unconscious, you showed the signs of divine love, and the
students put you out into the street. Devananda did not stop them. The guru proved as ignorant as his students.’” (CB,
Madhya 9.90–95)

According to one of the biographies, the Lord then spoke of the book-bhagavata and the devotee-bhagavata, and for a long
time remained indifferent to Devananda, not granting him prema. <!-- F17 -->

## The Rebuke
Vrindavana Dasa tells of two occasions when the Lord spoke about Devananda.[^7] The first time He only heard his lesson.
One day the Lord was walking through Navadvipa with His devotees and passed by Visharada's house:

“By Providence the Lord and His devotees heard on their way how he was expounding the scriptures. Present in the hearts
of all beings, the Lord knows everything. He did not hear the glorification of loving service to God in that
exposition.” (CB, Madhya 21.11–12)

And the Lord, in anger, spoke of the “Bhagavatam”: this reciter would not grasp its meaning in any birth —

“‘The “Srimad-Bhagavatam” is Krishna Himself in the form of a book. In the “Srimad-Bhagavatam” loving service to God is
described as the highest goal of life. The four Vedas proclaim the “Srimad-Bhagavatam” to be the embodiment of ecstatic
love for Krishna. The four Vedas are like curd, and the “Srimad-Bhagavatam” is the butter. Shukadeva churned it, and
Maharaja Parikshit tasted this nectar. My dear Shukadeva knows the conclusion of the “Srimad-Bhagavatam,” in which the
science of My greatness and glory is revealed. Whoever makes a difference between Me, My servant and the book
“Bhagavatam” surely brings ruin upon himself.’” (CB, Madhya 21.14–18)[^8] <!-- F18 Q7 -->

The Lord said that a reciter who, expounding the “Bhagavatam,” says nothing of loving service to God knows nothing — and
set out to tear up his book; but the Vaishnavas stopped Him (CB, Madhya 21.20–22).[^9] Srila Bhaktisiddhanta
Sarasvati explains in the “Gaudiya-bhashya” to these verses why the Lord is angry. Those devoid of bhakti take
indifference to service for the highest goal and thereby irritate the Lord, though by nature He is merciful. Gaurasundara
shows that such a view is not merely needless but faulty, and that both enjoying the fruits of one's actions and
renouncing them are out of place. The Lord's anger gladdens the devotees: it is not like the anger of a conditioned
soul; it is His care for the purity of bhakti. <!-- Q6 I5 -->

Then the Lord said:

“All the scriptures glorify the ‘Srimad-Bhagavatam’ as the highest spiritual work, yet such understanding does not come
through erudition, austerity or fame. If someone thinks, ‘I have understood the “Srimad-Bhagavatam,”’ he in fact does
not know the conclusions of this holy book. But one who accepts the ‘Srimad-Bhagavatam’ as a manifestation of the
inconceivable Lord understands that its meaning is pure loving service to God.” (CB, Madhya 21.23–25)

One of the biographies renders these words thus: the “Bhagavatam” is not understood by learning and austerity, but only
by bhakti; whoever wishes to understand it must serve both the book-bhagavata and the devotee-bhagavata. Having said
this, the Lord went home with His devotees. Devananda heard it all from afar but paid it no heed. <!-- F19 -->

The second time the Lord met him face to face:

“Walking about, Gaurachandra met Devananda Pandita and addressed him in anger. The Lord remembered how Devananda Pandita
had earlier offended Srivasa Pandita.” (CB, Madhya 21.53–54)

It was by that same embankment of Visharada (BR 12.2976–2977). The Lord sternly rebuked Devananda for having no faith in
the Vaishnavas: by indulging his students, hostile to Srivasa, he had lost the right to read the “Bhagavatam.” According
to the “Gaudiya-vaishnava-abhidhana,” at that very time Saranga Thakura met the Lord in a street of Navadvipa.[^10]
<!-- F20 F24 -->

“‘Devananda, I want to tell you something. Now you teach everyone the “Srimad-Bhagavatam.” Once Srivasa, whom the Ganga
herself longs to see, came to you to hear the “Srimad-Bhagavatam.” What was his fault, that you and your students put
him out of your house? Is it right to drive out one who weeps with love for Krishna while hearing the
“Srimad-Bhagavatam”?’” (CB, Madhya 21.67–71)

In the retelling of one of the biographies the Lord said: hearing the “Bhagavatam” sung melts the heart; only those
attached to worldly enjoyment keep it hard; when Srivasa gave himself wholly to devotional feeling, neither you
nor your students understood him, and you drove him away from the hearing; yet even the Ganga, who stands on Hara's head, came down
to earth as a river to see such a devotee; this offence has justly made you averse to God. <!-- F21 -->

“Though you expound the ‘Srimad-Bhagavatam,’ I am convinced that you will not understand its meaning in any of your
future lives.” (CB, Madhya 21.72)

An expounder of the “Bhagavatam,” he had not in any birth gained the *sukriti* — the piety — that would let him grasp its
meaning. <!-- F22 -->

## Mercy in the Form of Punishment
Vrindavana Dasa closes the account thus:

“Hearing these words of the Lord, the exalted brahmana Devananda Pandita felt deep shame and said nothing in reply.
Having spoken such angry words, Vishvambhara went away, and Devananda returned home dejected. That the Lord Himself
rebuked him shows Devananda's great good fortune. Lord Chaitanya punishes only the most fortunate. Even if a person
perishes as a result of such punishment, he attains Vaikuntha.” (CB, Madhya 21.75–78)

Devananda was overcome with shame. But having received this punishment in words from the Lord, he gained the awakening of
*sukriti*, and in time the punishment proved the source of his good fortune: great good fortune is the Lord's mercy in the
form of punishment. The biographies explain Vrindavana Dasa's words: whom Vishnu punishes gains liberation; whoever is
punished by Chaitanya obtains great good, and even one who would perish by His punishment goes to Vaikuntha. This speaks
of what the Lord's punishment is — it always brings good; Devananda the Lord punished only with words, and
it was the beginning of his deliverance. <!-- F23 I1 Q10 -->

“Whoever accepts with faith the punishment of Lord Chaitanya will surely obtain love for Krishna in the future.” (CB,
Madhya 21.79)

And Vrindavana Dasa draws one more truth from this story — why the offence to Srivasa so closed the meaning of the
“Bhagavatam” to Devananda:

“Krishna manifests in this world in four forms: the ‘Srimad-Bhagavatam,’ Tulasi, the Ganga and the devotees of God. The
worship of the Lord's Deity begins after the rite of installation; but the Vedas say that these four manifestations of
the Supreme Lord are fully equal to Him from their very appearance.” (CB, Madhya 21.81–82)

One of the biographies, following the “Gaudiya-bhashya,” explains: by external reckoning, worship of the Deity begins
after the rite of *prana-pratishtha*, while the book “Bhagavatam,” the Vaishnava, Tulasi and the Ganga are worshipped
without it. Though the world sees them as objects for its own use, they are themselves enjoyers, non-different from the
Lord; so say the Vedas. Therefore one who offends a devotee offends the “Bhagavatam” too — and the meaning of the book
closes to him. <!-- I2 -->

## Meeting Vakreshvara
While the Lord lived in Navadvipa, Devananda never came to have faith in Him:

“When Gaurachandra was performing His pastimes as a householder, Devananda Pandita had no faith in Him. Therefore he
could not understand the Lord. Though he was worthy of seeing the Lord, Krishna alone knows why he was unable to do so.”
(CB, Antya 3.465–467) <!-- F25 -->

One of the biographies relates that when the Lord had taken sannyasa and gone to Puri, Devananda remembered His
teachings and began to lament: so great a personality, full of love for Krishna, had come to his very door, and he had
not met Him and had not understood His glory. <!-- F26 -->

And then great good fortune came to him:

“When the Lord left home and took sannyasa, Devananda Pandita had the good fortune to meet Vakreshvara Pandita.” (CB,
Antya 3.468)

The Lord's dear associate Vakreshvara Pandita, by Providence, stayed in his house (CB, Antya 3.474).[^11] Devananda saw
how devotion to Vishnu shone in him, and began to serve him with sincere love:

“While Vakreshvara Pandita danced, Devananda Pandita guarded him with a stick in his hand. Devananda himself kept the
curious crowd away from him. If Vakreshvara fell unconscious, he would place his head on his lap. Lovingly he wiped the
dust from Vakreshvara Pandita's body and smeared it over himself. Constant association with Vakreshvara Pandita revealed
his glory to Devananda Pandita, and thus faith in Lord Chaitanya arose in Devananda.” (CB, Antya 3.477–480)

By serving Vakreshvara, Devananda became a vessel of Mahaprabhu's mercy. From Vakreshvara's lips he heard of the Lord's
glory, his heart was changed, and under the influence of that company he came to love pure bhakti. Srila Bhaktisiddhanta
remarks in the “Gaudiya-bhashya” that what Devananda lacked — faith in Mahaprabhu — was supplied by Vakreshvara's mercy.
And Vrindavana Dasa draws from this a general rule:

“‘Service to the Vaishnavas is higher than service to Krishna’ — all the scriptures headed by the ‘Srimad-Bhagavatam’
confirm this truth. ‘There may be doubt whether the servant of the Supreme reaches perfection, but there is no doubt
that the servant of a Vaishnava reaches it.’ Therefore service to the Vaishnavas is the highest spiritual practice. By
serving the Vaishnavas anyone can attain Krishna.” (CB, Antya 3.485–487)

More about how Devananda served Vakreshvara is told in the article on Vakreshvara Pandita. <!-- F27 -->

## Forgiveness at Kuliya
Some years after His sannyasa the Lord came from Puri to Bengal and stayed at Kuliya, across the Ganga from Nadia.
Thousands flocked there, and all who had once offended Him in Navadvipa came to ask forgiveness. One of the biographies
says that this was five years after His sannyasa and that the Lord then forgave everyone at the place now called
*aparadha-bhanjana*, “the destruction of offences” — where the temple of Srila Sridhara Maharaja stands today — and
taught them the science of bhakti.[^12] <!-- F28 F29 F30 F31 -->

Krishnadasa Kaviraja describes this journey: because of the crowds at Vidya-Vachaspati's house the Lord went away to
Kuliya and stayed in the house of Madhava Dasa; countless people saw Him there, and staying seven days He delivered all
the offenders in a special way (CC, Madhya 16.207–209). Srila Bhaktivinoda Thakura in his “Amrita-pravaha-bhashya” to
these verses says plainly that in those seven days the Lord destroyed the offences of Devananda and others. <!-- F42 -->

Shortly before Devananda came, the Lord gave a remedy to another brahmana who repented of having reviled the Vaishnavas:

“‘O brahmana, listen. One who has taken poison must drink nectar with the same mouth. Then the poison will be
neutralized, and the nectar will make his body immortal.’” (CB, Antya 3.449–450)

With the same mouth that once offended the Vaishnavas one must now glorify them (CB, Antya 3.453).[^13] And right after
this Devananda came:

“Lord Gaurachandra was seated when Devananda Pandita appeared before Him. Humbly offering obeisances, Devananda Pandita
remained standing at a distance, hesitant. The Lord, seeing him, was pleased, and taking him aside to a quiet place,
sat down with him. The Lord forgave all Devananda's former offences and bestowed His mercy on him. The Lord
said: ‘You have served Vakreshvara Pandita, and that has brought you close to Me. Vakreshvara Pandita is full of the spiritual energies of the Supreme Lord. Whoever
serves him attains Krishna. Krishna has made His abode in Vakreshvara's heart. When he dances, Krishna dances too.
Wherever Vakreshvara stays becomes like all the holy places; Vaikuntha is manifest there.’” (CB, Antya 3.489–496)
<!-- F32 Q8 Q11 -->

Devananda prayed with tears:

“O all-merciful Lord! You appeared in Navadvipa to deliver the whole universe. I am sinful and unfortunate, and so I
could not recognize You. Because of this I was deprived of the transcendental happiness of Your company. You are always
compassionate to all living beings. Therefore I pray to You: grant me attachment to You.” (CB, Antya 3.498–500)

And he asked: “I am not all-knowing, yet I teach the book of the All-knowing, the ‘Bhagavatam,’ knowing nothing myself.
Tell me what I should explain and what I should teach” (CB, Antya 3.501–503). One of the biographies renders his prayer
thus: I am a sinner, I did not worship Your feet and deceived myself; but by Your causeless mercy I see Your feet;
teach me what the goal of life is and explain the meaning of the “Bhagavatam.” <!-- F33 -->

## The Essence of the “Bhagavatam”
The Lord replied:

“‘O brahmana, listen. In teaching the “Srimad-Bhagavatam” you must explain nothing but loving service to God. In the
beginning, middle and end of the “Srimad-Bhagavatam” loving service to Vishnu is described as eternally perfect,
inexhaustible and flawless.’” (CB, Antya 3.505–506)

The Lord told him that the “Bhagavatam,” like the Lord's avataras, appears and disappears by its own will; that it
became manifest on Vyasa's tongue by Krishna's mercy, when he was absorbed in bhakti; and that the “Bhagavatam” is not
grasped by pride of learning:

“One who thinks, ‘I understand the “Srimad-Bhagavatam,”’ knows nothing of the glory of the ‘Srimad-Bhagavatam.’ But even
an unlettered person who takes shelter of the ‘Srimad-Bhagavatam’ can easily understand its true meaning. The
‘Srimad-Bhagavatam’ is made of ecstatic divine love. It is the body of Sri Krishna. The confidential pastimes of Krishna
are described there.” (CB, Antya 3.514–516) <!-- Q12 -->

And once more the Lord repeated the main point:

“‘You must explain loving service everywhere in the ‘Srimad-Bhagavatam’ — in its beginning, middle and end. Then you will
never commit any offence, and you will surely find joy in your heart. ‹…› Now go and teach the ‘Srimad-Bhagavatam,’
explaining to everyone the bliss of loving service to Krishna.’” (CB, Antya 3.520–523)

Thus by Vakreshvara's mercy Devananda received from the Lord the devotional meaning of the “Bhagavata,” as Krishnadasa
Kaviraja says (CC, Adi 10.77). <!-- F34 Q9 -->

Murari Gupta tells of this meeting briefly. Devananda, who had received the mercy of Vakreshvara, came to the Lord's
feet, confessed his former ill will and asked what his true good might be; and the Lord said to him: “Know that the
Srimad Bhagavatam is Sri Krishna Himself, directly — the embodiment of being, consciousness and bliss, free from envy
and every taint. Whoever reads it, tasting the rasa of devotion, will attain bliss.” “Hearing this, the brahmana bowed
his head and, covered with the dust of His feet, immersed in the rasa of Gaurachandra, danced most wondrously” (Murari,
3.17.17–20). <!-- F44 -->

The meaning of the “Bhagavatam” is open not to the proud scholar but to one who has surrendered. “Bhagavata” is both the
book and the devotee, and they are one:

“There are two kinds of Bhagavata: the book Bhagavata and the person who has received Krishna's special mercy.” (CB,
Antya 3.532)

One who expounds the “Bhagavatam” knowing this attains the highest good. The Lord also spoke to Devananda of the glory of
Vakreshvara — the devotee-bhagavata through serving whom he came to understand the book-bhagavata. <!-- F35 -->

According to one of the biographies, the Lord told Devananda to go and beg forgiveness at Srivasa's feet, for the
book-bhagavata and the devotee-bhagavata are one; Devananda went at once, Srivasa embraced and forgave him, and the name
of Hari resounded on all sides; from that day Devananda was accepted among Mahaprabhu's followers as a great
devotee.[^14] <!-- F36 -->

Kuliya, Koladvipa, is the place where offences are destroyed, and that is why Devananda's offence too was destroyed
here. Here also the Lord forgave Gopala Chapala, who had offended Srivasa (CC, Adi 17.55–59). Krishnadasa Kaviraja names
both in one verse:

“In Kuliya He showed His grace to Devananda and forgave the brahmana Gopala his offence against Srivasa.” (CC, Madhya
1.153)

One of the biographies says that Devananda received this mercy because he lived in Kuliya. <!-- F37 Q3 -->

## The Branch of Vakreshvara and the Place of Forgiveness
Krishnadasa Kaviraja calls Devananda an offshoot of Vakreshvara Pandita's branch. According to the
“Vakreshvara-charita,” one of the five branches stemming from Vakreshvara comes from Devananda.[^15] <!-- F38 -->

The place where the Lord forgave Devananda and Gopala Chapala was revealed by Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati Thakura:
in the month of Kartika 1922 he came to Kuliya and identified it. On this place of aparadha-bhanjana now stands the Sri
Chaitanya Saraswat Math. <!-- F39 -->

## Departure
Devananda Pandita left this world on the ekadashi of the month of Pausha, in the dark half of the moon. Our calendar, the
calendar of the Sri Chaitanya Saraswat Math, marks the day of his departure on the krishna-ekadashi of the month of
Narayana, which corresponds to Pausha (Saphala Ekadashi; in 2027 it falls on January 3).[^16] The year and place of his
departure, the place of his samadhi and the day of his appearance are not named in the sources. <!-- F40 -->

## Significance
Devananda's story is a lesson about *vaishnava-aparadha*. A scholar and ascetic, celibate from childhood, he expounded
the “Bhagavatam” for years — and did not know its essence, because he did not serve the devotees and allowed an offence
against a devotee. An offence to a Vaishnava closes the meaning of the “Bhagavatam” even to a scholar: “Bhagavata” is
both the book and the devotee, and they are non-different from the Lord. <!-- I2 I3 -->

The Lord's anger here is not worldly anger but mercy in the form of punishment. Whom the Lord punishes, He wishes good
for; the punishment in words awakened *sukriti* in Devananda and became the beginning of his good fortune. Taking
indifference to service for the highest goal irritates the Lord, though by nature He is merciful, and Gaurasundara
shows that such a view is faulty; and the devotees are gladdened by such anger of the Lord. <!-- I1 I5 -->

The “Bhagavatam” is understood not by learning but by one who has surrendered, and to understand the book-bhagavata one
must serve the devotee-bhagavata. As reviling the devotees deprives one of mercy, so glorifying and serving them is
the best path to mercy. An offence to a Vaishnava is removed by the forgiveness of the one offended and by service to a
Vaishnava: the blessing of a devotee quickly gives bhakti. Devananda, having served Vakreshvara, gained faith in
Mahaprabhu, received forgiveness, and heard from the Lord Himself that the whole essence of the “Bhagavatam” is bhakti.
Not without reason does one of the biographies say that through Devananda Sri Chaitanya Mahaprabhu revealed the glory
