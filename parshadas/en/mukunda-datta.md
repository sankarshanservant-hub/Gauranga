# Mukunda Datta
*Мукунда Датта · @mukunda-datta*

## Gauranga's Singer
When Sri Chaitanya first revealed His love for Krishna to the devotees of Navadvipa, one voice was almost always heard
beside Him — the voice of Mukunda Datta. Krishnadasa Kaviraja calls him simply Mukunda, Mukunda Datta and “fortunate Sri
Mukunda” (CC, Adi 12.41); Vrindavana Dasa calls him Mukunda Mahashaya, “the great Vaishnava,” the Lord's “great servant”
(*maha-dasa*), “Sri Mukunda the physician-teacher (*bej-ojha*)” and, finally, “Mukunda Datta, the singer of Krishna” (CB,
Antya 8.15). Murari Gupta calls him “the Ambashtha,” “the vaidya” and “the best of singers,” and Kavi Karnapura “Mukunda
the physician.” One of the biographies once calls him Sri Mukunda Dasa, another Srila Mukunda Datta Thakura.[^1] <!-- F1 -->

Who he is in Krishna's eternal pastimes Kavi Karnapura tells us:

“The two singers of Vraja, Madhukantha and Madhuvrata, are Gauranga's singers Mukunda and Vasudeva Datta.”
(“Gaura-ganoddesha-dipika” 140)

Madhukantha means “honey-throat”: a singer who delights Krishna in Vraja. Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati cites the same
verse in his “Anubhashya” to CC, Adi 10.40, and the later “Prema-vilasa” says likewise that Mukunda Datta's true form is
Madhukantha. The eternal singer of Vraja descended into Gaura's pastimes to sing for the Lord here as well.[^2]

## Family and Birthplace
Mukunda was the younger brother of Srila Vasudeva Datta, himself a great devotee. Vasudeva says of him to the Lord in
Puri: “Though Mukunda is my younger brother…” (CC, Madhya 11.140). The brothers came of a respected Ambashtha family — the
caste of vaidyas, physicians; that is why Vrindavana Dasa calls Mukunda “the physician-teacher,” and the biographies,
following him, “a teacher of the vaidya caste from Chittagong.” <!-- F3 F4 -->

Mukunda's homeland was Chittagong (Chattagrama) in eastern Bengal, now in Bangladesh. Vrindavana Dasa says of Pundarika
Vidyanidhi that Mukunda was born in Chattagrama “together with him” (CB, Madhya 7.40). Srila Bhaktisiddhanta is more
precise: Mukunda Datta was born in the village of Chhanhara, in the Patiya police-station area, ten kroshas from Mekhala,
the village of Pundarika Vidyanidhi.[^3] The “Prema-vilasa” names the ancestral home of the Datta Ambashthas differently
— the village of Chakrashala, in the same land of Chittagong; the biographies cite it after that book. <!-- F5 Q16 -->

Krishnadasa Kaviraja himself writes in one of his lists: “In the land of Radha were born Thakura Nityananda, Gangadasa
Pandita, Murari Gupta and Mukunda” (CC, Adi 13.61).[^4] <!-- Q15 -->

From Chittagong the brothers moved to Navadvipa-Mayapur — then the centre of Sanskrit learning and the place where the
Lord appeared. Many did the same in those days: Vrindavana Dasa writes that a number of Vaishnavas from Chattagrama
settled on the bank of the Ganga and studied in Navadvipa; all were renounced devotees of Krishna who had come into the
world by the Lord's will (CB, Adi 11.19–20). <!-- F6 -->

## Nimai's Classmate
Mukunda studied together with the Lord. The “Charitamrita” calls him Mahaprabhu's fellow student:

“Sri Mukunda Datta, another branch, was the Lord's fellow student; when he sang kirtana, Chaitanya Gosani danced.”
(CC, Adi 10.40)

And years later the Lord Himself would call him a friend “since childhood” (Madhya 11.138). According to one of the
biographies, they sat under the same teacher, Gangadasa Pandita.[^5] Mukunda was a diligent and learned youth, and the
Lord loved him dearly. Seeing Mukunda in the street, Nimai would stop him and put a question; Mukunda answered, Nimai
declared, “Wrong!” — and the debate began. Vrindavana Dasa explains that Mukunda, blessed by the Lord, was so learned that
he always found an answer (CB, Adi 11.28–30). Nimai treated others in the same way: He demolished a fellow student's
argument and then skilfully restored it Himself, showing how it could have been defended; older students, and even the
teacher — who only rejoiced in such a pupil — had their share. All this was a play of learning: until the appointed time
the Lord hid Himself in the guise of a young pandita. <!-- F8 F9 Q1 -->

But from childhood Mukunda belonged to another circle. In the evenings the devotees of Navadvipa gathered in the house of
Advaita Acharya — almost the only place in the town where the scriptures of bhakti were read and the name and glories of
Krishna were sung. Mukunda's sweet voice captivated everyone:

“The Vaishnavas dearly loved Sri Mukunda; when he sang, their hearts melted… As soon as Mukunda began to sing of Krishna,
all who were present fell to the ground, senseless in ecstatic love. Some of the Vaishnavas wept, some laughed, some
danced; some rolled on the ground, their clothes in disarray. Some shouted aloud as if challenging maya, and others caught
hold of Mukunda's feet.” (CB, Adi 11.22, 24–26)

So, says Vrindavana Dasa, the Vaishnavas forgot all their cares. One sorrow remained to them: the learned golden Nimai
was not among them. But the Lord was waiting for the time to reveal Himself. <!-- F10 -->

## “Where Will You Go from Me?”
Mukunda and Srivasa, who had tasted bhakti, avoided idle disputes with Nimai Pandita. One midday Mukunda was going to
bathe in the Ganga and, seeing Nimai with His students, hurried off to one side. The Lord asked His companion Govinda why
he was running away, and answered Himself:

“I know why he avoids Me. He does not wish to talk with a non-devotee. This young man studies only the books of the
Vaishnavas, while I speak only of panji, vritti and tika. I do not speak of Krishna, and so he ran off as soon as he saw
Me.” (CB, Adi 11.41–43)

Outwardly the Lord scolded Mukunda, but inwardly He was pleased with him — and, as Vrindavana Dasa remarks, He thereby
indirectly revealed His divinity (11.44). He called after him: “How long will you keep running from Me? Where will you
go?” — and added with a smile: “When I finish My studies, you will all see what a devotee of Krishna I am. I shall become
such a Vaishnava that even Brahma and Shiva will come to My door” (11.45–47). And those who now ran from Him would sing
His glories. According to one of the biographies these words had a hidden meaning: soon the Lord would reveal Himself,
and Mukunda together with all the devotees would worship Him. <!-- F11 Q3 I1 -->

At about that time Ishvara Puri came to Navadvipa, unrecognised by anyone. He sat humbly in Advaita's house, and Advaita,
seeing in him the radiance of a Vaishnava, asked whether he was a Vaishnava sannyasi. The guest replied that he was lower
than a shudra and had come only to see the Acharya's feet. Mukunda, understanding from these humble words that a great
Vaishnava was before them, wanted to draw him out of his concealment — and began to sing:

“Understanding what was happening, Mukunda began to sing the glories of Krishna with great love. As soon as Mukunda's
voice touched Sri Ishvara Puri's ears, he fell to the ground unconscious. Tears streamed unceasingly from his eyes, and
wave after wave of divine love, ever increasing, swept over him.” (CB, Adi 11.77–79)

Advaita caught him in His arms and was drenched with his tears; Mukunda recited verse after verse in the same mood, and
the surge of love only grew (11.80–81). Ishvara Puri's humility was the humility of a great Vaishnava, and Mukunda's song
only uncovered his prema — for who can remain unmoved when Mukunda sings? <!-- F12 Q4 -->

There was another occasion. One day the Lord met Mukunda, took him by the hand and would not let go: “Why do you run away
whenever you see Me? Today you will not leave until you answer.” He began questioning him on grammar and refuted every
reply. Mukunda thought: “In grammar He cannot be beaten — I shall ask Him about alankara, the ornaments of poetry; there,
surely, He will be at a loss.” “Grammar is a science for children,” he said. “Let us rather discuss alankara.” “As you
wish,” the Lord replied. Mukunda cited several difficult verses and asked Him to find their faults — and the Lord easily
exposed every flaw in their ornaments. Mukunda could not defend what had been refuted, and the Lord let him go with a
smile: “Go home, read carefully, and come early tomorrow — we shall continue” (CB, Adi 12.6–16). Mukunda took the dust of
His feet and on the way thought:

“No one could have such profound learning! There is no scripture He does not know! If so intelligent a person as He
became a devotee of Krishna, I would not leave His company for a moment.” (CB, Adi 12.18–19)

That wish was very soon fulfilled. <!-- F13 -->

[^1]: He should not be confused with his namesakes: Mukunda Dasa of Shrikhanda, the court physician, brother of Narahari
    and father of Raghunandana; Mukunda-Sanjaya, in whose chandi-mandapa Nimai kept His school; the sannyasi Mukunda
    Sarasvati in Vrindavana; Mukundadeva, grandfather of Rupa and Sanatana; Mukundadeva, king of Orissa; Mukunda the
    physician of Nityananda's branch (CC, Adi 11.51); and others. Mukunda is also one of the names of Krishna Himself.
[^2]: Murari Gupta writes that Mukunda, of a family of physicians and a beautiful singer, was born as a portion of a
    gandharva, a celestial singer (“Sri Krishna-chaitanya-charitamrita” 1.4.7). This does not contradict the
    “Gaura-ganoddesha-dipika”: Murari speaks of a celestial singer, Karnapura of the eternal singer of Vraja; we follow
    Karnapura.
[^3]: One of the biographies applies these words of the “Anubhashya” to Vasudeva Datta, the elder brother; in the
    “Anubhashya” they are said of Mukunda. Both villages, Chhanhara and Chakrashala, lie in the Chittagong district;
    according to the “Prema-vilasa,” Chakrashala was also Pundarika's village. We follow Srila Bhaktisiddhanta.
[^4]: The Bengali line also allows another reading: “Nityananda was born in Radha; [and also] Gangadasa, Murari,
    Mukunda” — that is, the verse says nothing of where the last three were born; so one of the biographies understands it
    (“in other places”). According to the “Chaitanya-bhagavata” (Madhya 7.40) and the “Anubhashya,” Mukunda was born in
    Chattagrama, and Murari Gupta in Shrihatta (CB, Adi 2.35), so the literal reading of the translation disagrees with
    them.
[^5]: The primary sources do not name the teacher: the “Charitamrita” says only that Mukunda was the Lord's fellow
    student (*samadhyayi*), and the “Chaitanya-bhagavata” tells of their debates.
