# Mukunda Datta
*Мукунда Датта · @mukunda-datta*

## Gauranga's Singer
When Sri Chaitanya first revealed His love for Krishna to the devotees of Navadvipa, one voice was almost always heard
beside Him — the voice of Mukunda Datta. Krishnadasa Kaviraja calls him simply Mukunda, Mukunda Datta and “fortunate Sri
Mukunda” (CC, Adi 12.41); Vrindavana Dasa calls him Mukunda Mahashaya, “the great Vaishnava,” the Lord's “great servant”
(*maha-dasa*), “Sri Mukunda the physician-teacher (*bej-ojha*)” and, finally, “Mukunda Datta, the singer of Krishna” (CB,
Antya 8.15). Murari Gupta calls him “the Ambashtha,” “the vaidya” and “the best of singers,” and Kavi Karnapura “Mukunda
the physician.” One of the biographies once calls him Sri Mukunda Dasa, another Srila Mukunda Datta Thakura.[^1] <!-- F1 -->

Who he is in Krishna's eternal pastimes Kavi Karnapura tells us:

“The two singers of Vraja, Madhukantha and Madhuvrata, are Gauranga's singers Mukunda and Vasudeva Datta.”
(“Gaura-ganoddesha-dipika” 140)

Madhukantha means “honey-throat”: a singer who delights Krishna in Vraja. Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati cites the same
verse in his “Anubhashya” to CC, Adi 10.40, and the later “Prema-vilasa” says likewise that Mukunda Datta's true form is
Madhukantha. The eternal singer of Vraja descended into Gaura's pastimes to sing for the Lord here as well.[^2]

## Family and Birthplace
Mukunda was the younger brother of Srila Vasudeva Datta, himself a great devotee. Vasudeva says of him to the Lord in
Puri: “Though Mukunda is my younger brother…” (CC, Madhya 11.140). The brothers came of a respected Ambashtha family — the
caste of vaidyas, physicians; that is why Vrindavana Dasa calls Mukunda “the physician-teacher,” and the biographies,
following him, “a teacher of the vaidya caste from Chittagong.” <!-- F3 F4 -->

Mukunda's homeland was Chittagong (Chattagrama) in eastern Bengal, now in Bangladesh. Vrindavana Dasa says of Pundarika
Vidyanidhi that Mukunda was born in Chattagrama “together with him” (CB, Madhya 7.40). Srila Bhaktisiddhanta is more
precise: Mukunda Datta was born in the village of Chhanhara, in the Patiya police-station area, ten kroshas from Mekhala,
the village of Pundarika Vidyanidhi.[^3] The “Prema-vilasa” names the ancestral home of the Datta Ambashthas differently
— the village of Chakrashala, in the same land of Chittagong; the biographies cite it after that book. <!-- F5 Q16 -->

Krishnadasa Kaviraja himself writes in one of his lists: “In the land of Radha were born Thakura Nityananda, Gangadasa
Pandita, Murari Gupta and Mukunda” (CC, Adi 13.61).[^4] <!-- Q15 -->

From Chittagong the brothers moved to Navadvipa-Mayapur — then the centre of Sanskrit learning and the place where the
Lord appeared. Many did the same in those days: Vrindavana Dasa writes that a number of Vaishnavas from Chattagrama
settled on the bank of the Ganga and studied in Navadvipa; all were renounced devotees of Krishna who had come into the
world by the Lord's will (CB, Adi 11.19–20). <!-- F6 -->

## Nimai's Classmate
Mukunda studied together with the Lord. The “Charitamrita” calls him Mahaprabhu's fellow student:

“Sri Mukunda Datta, another branch, was the Lord's fellow student; when he sang kirtana, Chaitanya Gosani danced.”
(CC, Adi 10.40)

And years later the Lord Himself would call him a friend “since childhood” (Madhya 11.138). According to one of the
biographies, they sat under the same teacher, Gangadasa Pandita.[^5] Mukunda was a diligent and learned youth, and the
Lord loved him dearly. Seeing Mukunda in the street, Nimai would stop him and put a question; Mukunda answered, Nimai
declared, “Wrong!” — and the debate began. Vrindavana Dasa explains that Mukunda, blessed by the Lord, was so learned that
he always found an answer (CB, Adi 11.28–30). Nimai treated others in the same way: He demolished a fellow student's
argument and then skilfully restored it Himself, showing how it could have been defended; older students, and even the
teacher — who only rejoiced in such a pupil — had their share. All this was a play of learning: until the appointed time
the Lord hid Himself in the guise of a young pandita. <!-- F8 F9 Q1 -->

But from childhood Mukunda belonged to another circle. In the evenings the devotees of Navadvipa gathered in the house of
Advaita Acharya — almost the only place in the town where the scriptures of bhakti were read and the name and glories of
Krishna were sung. Mukunda's sweet voice captivated everyone:

“The Vaishnavas dearly loved Sri Mukunda; when he sang, their hearts melted… As soon as Mukunda began to sing of Krishna,
all who were present fell to the ground, senseless in ecstatic love. Some of the Vaishnavas wept, some laughed, some
danced; some rolled on the ground, their clothes in disarray. Some shouted aloud as if challenging maya, and others caught
hold of Mukunda's feet.” (CB, Adi 11.22, 24–26)

So, says Vrindavana Dasa, the Vaishnavas forgot all their cares. One sorrow remained to them: the learned golden Nimai
was not among them. But the Lord was waiting for the time to reveal Himself. <!-- F10 -->

## “Where Will You Go from Me?”
Mukunda and Srivasa, who had tasted bhakti, avoided idle disputes with Nimai Pandita. One midday Mukunda was going to
bathe in the Ganga and, seeing Nimai with His students, hurried off to one side. The Lord asked His companion Govinda why
he was running away, and answered Himself:

“I know why he avoids Me. He does not wish to talk with a non-devotee. This young man studies only the books of the
Vaishnavas, while I speak only of panji, vritti and tika. I do not speak of Krishna, and so he ran off as soon as he saw
Me.” (CB, Adi 11.41–43)

Outwardly the Lord scolded Mukunda, but inwardly He was pleased with him — and, as Vrindavana Dasa remarks, He thereby
indirectly revealed His divinity (11.44). He called after him: “How long will you keep running from Me? Where will you
go?” — and added with a smile: “When I finish My studies, you will all see what a devotee of Krishna I am. I shall become
such a Vaishnava that even Brahma and Shiva will come to My door” (11.45–47). And those who now ran from Him would sing
His glories. According to one of the biographies these words had a hidden meaning: soon the Lord would reveal Himself,
and Mukunda together with all the devotees would worship Him. <!-- F11 Q3 I1 -->

At about that time Ishvara Puri came to Navadvipa, unrecognised by anyone. He sat humbly in Advaita's house, and Advaita,
seeing in him the radiance of a Vaishnava, asked whether he was a Vaishnava sannyasi. The guest replied that he was lower
than a shudra and had come only to see the Acharya's feet. Mukunda, understanding from these humble words that a great
Vaishnava was before them, wanted to draw him out of his concealment — and began to sing:

“Understanding what was happening, Mukunda began to sing the glories of Krishna with great love. As soon as Mukunda's
voice touched Sri Ishvara Puri's ears, he fell to the ground unconscious. Tears streamed unceasingly from his eyes, and
wave after wave of divine love, ever increasing, swept over him.” (CB, Adi 11.77–79)

Advaita caught him in His arms and was drenched with his tears; Mukunda recited verse after verse in the same mood, and
the surge of love only grew (11.80–81). Ishvara Puri's humility was the humility of a great Vaishnava, and Mukunda's song
only uncovered his prema — for who can remain unmoved when Mukunda sings? <!-- F12 Q4 -->

There was another occasion. One day the Lord met Mukunda, took him by the hand and would not let go: “Why do you run away
whenever you see Me? Today you will not leave until you answer.” He began questioning him on grammar and refuted every
reply. Mukunda thought: “In grammar He cannot be beaten — I shall ask Him about alankara, the ornaments of poetry; there,
surely, He will be at a loss.” “Grammar is a science for children,” he said. “Let us rather discuss alankara.” “As you
wish,” the Lord replied. Mukunda cited several difficult verses and asked Him to find their faults — and the Lord easily
exposed every flaw in their ornaments. Mukunda could not defend what had been refuted, and the Lord let him go with a
smile: “Go home, read carefully, and come early tomorrow — we shall continue” (CB, Adi 12.6–16). Mukunda took the dust of
His feet and on the way thought:

“No one could have such profound learning! There is no scripture He does not know! If so intelligent a person as He
became a devotee of Krishna, I would not leave His company for a moment.” (CB, Adi 12.18–19)

That wish was very soon fulfilled. <!-- F13 -->

## The Singer of the Kirtanas
The Lord returned from Gaya transformed. Though He is the Lord of all, He had enacted there the pastime of taking
initiation from Ishvara Puri in order to teach the world — and from a pandita seemingly proud of His learning He became a seeker of
Krishna: now weeping in separation, asking, “Where is Krishna?”, now jubilant. The devotees were overjoyed and gathered
round Him every day, and nearly all the verses of bhakti sung before the Lord in those days were sung by Mukunda. His
singing deepened the Lord's feelings, and He asked for more and more:

“In the evening all the devotees gradually gathered in Vishvambhara's house. Then Sri Mukunda Mahashaya began to sing
various verses of scripture glorifying loving service to God. Pious Mukunda had so sweet a voice that as soon as
Mahaprabhu heard it He was enchanted. The Lord began to cry out loudly, ‘Haribol!’ and to fall to the ground, and no one
could hold Him.” (CB, Madhya 2.215–218)

The night passed like a single moment. Srila Bhaktisiddhanta writes that Mukunda delighted the Lord by reciting verses of
the “Bhagavatam.” <!-- F14 -->

From then on Mukunda was the Lord's constant kirtaniya. In the courtyard of Srivasa, where kirtana went on through the
nights, one of the biographies calls him the Lord's “constant singer.” On Hari-vasara, the day of ekadashi, when they sang
day and night, Srivasa led one group and Mukunda the other (CB, Madhya 8.141); so too the “Charitamrita” says that when he
sang, Mahaprabhu danced (Adi 10.40). He was the chief singer also during the “seven praharas” — twenty-one hours in which
the Lord continuously manifested His divinity (the maha-prakasha). Then the devotees performed His great bathing, the
maha-abhisheka, with a thousand pots of water to the hymn “Purusha-sukta” of the Rig Veda, and “led by Mukunda, all sang
the hymns befitting the abhisheka” (CB, Madhya 9.32). Afterwards the Lord quickly ate abundant offerings, called the
devotees to Him one by one, revealed to each a past unknown to him — how He had protected him even before His own
appearance — and gave His blessings. <!-- F19 F20 -->

## Gadadhara's Friend
In those same days the Lord, absorbed in ecstasy and for a moment revealing His divinity, began to call out: “Pundarika
Vidyanidhi, father!” The devotees asked who that was, and He answered: the greatest devotee of Krishna, a brahmana from
Chattagrama who also had a house in Navadvipa; outwardly he looked like a worldly rich man — richly dressed, surrounded by
costly things — and he would soon come. Before long Vidyanidhi did quietly settle in Navadvipa, and Mukunda, his
countryman, told the Lord of it:

“None of the Vaishnavas knew him except Mukunda, who recognised him at once. The physician Sri Mukunda knew him well,
for both of them came from Chattagrama.” (CB, Madhya 7.39–40)

Only Mukunda and Vasudeva Datta knew the glory of his love for Krishna (7.43). Gadadhara Pandita, though from the same
parts, did not know him — for the sake of the lila, of course, as one of the biographies says. <!-- F15 -->

Mukunda loved Gadadhara dearly; they were almost inseparable, and whatever Mukunda learned he at once told him (CB,
Madhya 7.44–45). Whenever a sadhu or sannyasi came to Navadvipa, Mukunda would call Gadadhara and they would go to the
holy man together. And now he said to his friend: “Today I shall introduce you to a Vaishnava not of this world — and in
return consider me your servant” (7.46–47). <!-- F16 -->

Gadadhara himself wished to see the one for whom the Lord so longed. Vidyanidhi received his guest with respect and asked
Mukunda who this youth was whose body shone with devotion to Vishnu — and Mukunda told him about Gadadhara (7.51–56). But
Gadadhara, seeing the rich couch, the perfumes and the servants, began to doubt: could such a dandy be a devotee? Mukunda
understood his thoughts and, wishing to reveal who Vidyanidhi really was, sweetly began to sing verses on the greatness of
bhakti (7.73) — the story of Putana, who came to kill Krishna and received the position of a mother:

“Ah, how wonderful! Wicked Putana, wishing to kill Krishna, gave Him poison from her breast — and still attained a
destination worthy of a mother. To whom but Him, so merciful, shall I take refuge?” (“Srimad-Bhagavatam” 3.2.23)[^6]

Hearing this, Vidyanidhi burst into tears, fell from his couch, displayed every symptom of ecstatic love, kicked his
vessels to pieces and loudly lamented that he had not become a vessel of the mercy of so merciful a Lord. According to one
of the biographies, Mukunda introduced Gadadhara to him only after Pundarika had come back to himself. <!-- F17 Q5 -->

Gadadhara repented of his thought and resolved to take initiation from Vidyanidhi to atone for the offence. He opened his
heart to Mukunda, who rejoiced — “Excellent! Excellent!” — and, when Vidyanidhi returned to outer consciousness, himself
put his friend's request to him (7.106–111). Vidyanidhi gladly agreed, and Gadadhara embraced Mukunda: “You are my true
friend!” The Lord, learning of it, was very pleased; that same night Vidyanidhi came to Him secretly and, seeing Him,
fainted with joy. Srila Bhaktisiddhanta notes that Gadadhara took initiation from Vidyanidhi through Mukunda's efforts.

## Mercy in the Form of Punishment
During the maha-prakasha the Lord called the devotees to Him one by one and bestowed His mercy on each. Only Mukunda He
did not call — and Mukunda sat behind the curtain, not daring to come forward unbidden. The devotees grew sad, and Srivasa
interceded for him:

“Mukunda is very dear to You; he is our very life. Is there a heart that does not melt at Mukunda's singing?… If he has
really committed some fault, then punish him. But why do You neglect Your servant?” (CB, Madhya 10.179, 181) <!-- F21 -->

The Lord's answer sounded stern. There is a saying, He said, “now with straw, now with a stick” (*khada-jathiya*): Mukunda
now holds a straw between his teeth in token of humility, now strikes with a stick.

“When he studies the ‘Yoga-vasishtha’ in Advaita's company, he holds a straw between his teeth and sings and dances like a
devotee of God. But when he keeps company with another sampradaya, he constantly beats Me with a stick, rejecting bhakti.
Whoever says there is anything higher than bhakti beats Me with a stick.” (CB, Madhya 10.189–191)

For this offence against bhakti, the Lord said, Mukunda would not see Him. One of the biographies, telling of this, adds
the words with which the edition of the “Chaitanya-bhagavata” explains the verse: such a person mixes with every
community and approves the doctrines of each — with one hand he clasps the feet, with the other the throat. Srila
Bhaktivinoda Thakura in his “Amrita-pravaha-bhashya” to CC, Adi 17.65 explains it likewise: among the devotees he spoke
of pure bhakti, while among the mayavadis he agreed with the mayavada of the “Yoga-vasishtha.” These are the Lord's words
in His pastime of instruction, not a description of His eternal associate. Through Mukunda the Lord teaches everyone that
association with mayavadis, and the idea that anything is higher than bhakti, is an offence to Bhakti-devi. <!-- F22 -->

Mukunda heard everything from behind the door. He did not defend himself but inwardly accepted the faults laid upon him:
“The Lord knows everything: it is true that earlier, at the urging of [a former] teacher, I did not give bhakti its due”
(CB, Madhya 10.194). And he resolved that without the Lord's mercy he would live no longer. This was not worldly despair:
one of the biographies stresses that Mukunda, an eternally perfect intimate associate of the Lord, showed such resolve to
instruct living beings — the grief of a servant deprived of darshana teaches one to value the Lord's mercy above life
itself. <!-- F23 I2 -->

In tears — and everyone around wept with him — he asked Srivasa to find out whether he would ever see the Lord. The answer
came: “He will certainly see Me — but after millions (*koti*) of births.” And then the unexpected happened:

“As soon as Mukunda heard from the Lord's lips that he would surely see Him, his heart filled with spiritual bliss. He
began to dance, crying, ‘I shall attain Him! I shall attain Him!’” (CB, Madhya 10.200–201)

The Lord smiled and ordered him to be brought at once. <!-- F24 -->

Mukunda heard nothing for joy, and the devotees had to lead him in. The Lord said, “Mukunda, you are free of all
offences; come to Me and receive My mercy.” Unable to utter a word, Mukunda fell unconscious, and the Lord went on:

“Get up! Get up, My dear Mukunda! There is no fault left in you. All the effects of your bad association are destroyed.
You have conquered Me. I said you would see Me after millions of lives, yet you have lived them in a moment. You had deep
faith that My words cannot fail, and so you have bound Me in your heart for ever.” (CB, Madhya 10.207–210)

So, in the words of one of the biographies, the power of firm faith in the infallibility of the Lord's word compressed
millions of births into a single moment and bound the Lord Himself in the devotee's heart: faith in His word conquers Him
Himself. And then the Lord revealed that there had been no punishment at all. Mukunda is His singer and always with Him;
He had only jested with one with whom one can jest; were Mukunda to have millions of faults, they would not count, for he
is infinitely dear to Him: “You are My servant, and your body is full of bhakti. I dwell constantly on your tongue”
(10.211–213). <!-- F25 I3 -->

But Mukunda, sobbing, reproached himself: how could his lips have slighted bhakti? What joy is there in seeing the Lord
without bhakti? Duryodhana saw Him, and Hiranyakashipu, and Kamsa, and the kings saw Him when He carried off Rukmini — and
found no happiness; while Shiva, Narada and Vyasa attained eternal bliss by the power of bhakti (CB, Madhya 10.214–240).
“My head is still on my shoulders — truly this is astonishing,” he said. Vrindavana Dasa responds to these penitent words:

“Mukunda, the great servant of God, raised his arms and wept. His breath came heavily, and his body trembled. By his very
nature Mukunda was an unalloyed devotee. How can I tell all his glory? He is one of Lord Chaitanya's closest
associates.” (CB, Madhya 10.241–242) <!-- F26 -->

And the Lord, as if abashed, said to him: “Your service gives Me great joy. Wherever you sing, I always appear there in
person. You have spoken the truth: without bhakti there is no good even from meeting Me face to face.” As an example He
cited Kamsa's washerman (*rajaka*) in Mathura, who saw Krishna but did not understand Whom he saw. And at the end He gave Mukunda the
blessing to be His companion in every birth:

“The hearts of all Vaishnavas melt when they hear your kirtana. As you are dear to Me, so you will be dear to all
Vaishnavas. Whenever I come into this world, you will always accompany Me as My singer.” (CB, Madhya 10.258–260)

Everyone around began to sing, “Hari! Haribol!” And Vrindavana Dasa promises that whoever hears Mukunda's prayers and the
blessing given to him will sing together with him (10.263). <!-- F27 Q2 -->

The “Charitamrita” recalls this pastime twice. In the list of the Lord's pastimes it says:

“To Mukunda Datta the Lord gave His grace in the form of punishment, and so dispelled all the dejection of his heart.”
(CC, Adi 17.65)

In Bengali this is *danda-parasada*, “mercy-punishment.” And Advaita Acharya, Himself once punished by the Lord, says:

“Being punished brought Me the greatest joy; fortunate Sri Mukunda received the same punishment.” (CC, Adi 12.41)

Srila Bhaktivinoda Thakura explains: seeing how firmly Mukunda had given up the company of mayavadis, the Lord called him
at once and bestowed mercy on him — the fruit of association with pure devotees. <!-- F28 -->

## Instruction on the Two-armed Krishna
Other primary sources tell of the lesson given to Mukunda differently.[^7] In Murari Gupta the Lord, seeing Mukunda
before Him, asks with a smile, “Are you expounding your own knowledge of Brahman?” — and recites a verse of the
“Padma Purana”: “The yogis delight in the Infinite, whose nature is truth, bliss and consciousness; therefore the Supreme
Brahman is called by the word ‘Rama.’” Then He gently reproaches Mukunda for ranking meditation on the four-armed form
above the two-armed one, and tells him to meditate diligently on the two-armed Krishna. This is a playful rebuke and a
lesson on the supremacy of Krishna's original form, of which Narayana too is a manifestation. Mukunda bowed his head:

“I have bathed abundantly in the waters of the river of the gods, and my body is adorned with the dust of the feet of
holy Vaishnavas; now place on my head the wondrous parasol of Your lotus feet — anoint me, raising me to the rank of Your
servant.” (Murari Gupta, “Sri Krishna-chaitanya-charitamrita” 2.4.19)

The Lord placed His foot on his head, and Mukunda trembled with joy, his eyes full of tears. Kavi Karnapura tells the
same in his mahakavya (6.35–41) and in his drama “Chaitanya-chandrodaya” (act 1), where Advaita pleads for Mukunda, as
for Murari, and the Lord places His feet on both; and Lochana Dasa in the “Chaitanya-mangala” says that the Lord
instructed Mukunda Datta on the supremacy of devotion to Krishna. <!-- F29 -->

## Other Pastimes in Navadvipa
Mukunda's kirtana sounded in nearly all the Lord's pastimes in Navadvipa. When Nityananda came to Navadvipa, the Lord sent
Rama Pandita, Murari, Narayana and Mukunda to bring that “great soul” (Murari 2.8.4; mahakavya 6.108). Murari recalls how
the Lord sang and danced in Srivasa's house “with Mukunda and another vaidya,” that is, the author himself (2.2.5). When
the Lord, in the mood of Shiva, sat on the shoulders of a singer of songs about Shiva, Srivasa recited praises of Shiva,
and Mukunda in a most beautiful voice sang the hymn “Mahimna-stava” (Murari 2.11.17; so too Karnapura and Lochana Dasa).
<!-- F47 F48 -->

In Chandrashekhara's house, where the Lord in the form of Lakshmi performed the sacred drama, Mukunda opened the
performance by beginning the kirtana: “Rama Krishna Bala, Hari Gopala Govinda!” (CB, Madhya 18.38). In it Advaita was the
jester, Haridasa the watchman (*kotal*), Srivasa Narada; according to Karnapura's drama, Haridasa directed the
performance and Mukunda was his assistant. In the night procession with kirtana to the Kazi's house, among the singers
and dancers were Murari, Mukunda Datta, Ramai, Govinda, Vakreshvara and Vasudeva (CB, Madhya 23.209), and in the games in
the waters of the Ganga “Haridasa, Srivasa and Mukunda came together” (13.335). <!-- F46 F49 -->

One of the biographies relates that when Lokanatha came to the Lord, the Lord was sitting among Srivasa, Murari, Mukunda,
Haridasa and others. Govinda-deva in the “Gaura-krishnodaya” writes how the devotees, with Mukunda's kirtana, play and
dancing, brought the Lord back to outer consciousness (17.71). Krishnadasa Kaviraja places Mukunda in the lists of the
Lord's closest associates: “Srivasa, Haridasa, Ramadasa, Gadadhara, Murari, Mukunda, Chandrashekhara and Vakreshvara —
all these are learned men of the highest greatness, and service to Chaitanya makes every one of them mad” (CC, Adi
6.48–49); “Glory to Mukunda and Vasudeva, and glory to Haridasa!” (Adi 13.3). <!-- F59 -->

## Before the Sannyasa
Mukunda was so close to the Lord that he was among the five to whom the Lord disclosed in advance His decision to take
sannyasa. The Lord said to Nityananda: “You may tell this to five only: My mother, Gadadhara, Brahmananda Bharati, Chandrashekhara Acharya and
Mukunda” (CB, Madhya 28.11–12). The Lord was leaving home to free a multitude of people from the bonds of worldly life,
but for Mukunda the news was a deep wound. Vrindavana Dasa tells how the Lord came to his house and asked, “Sing of
Krishna's glory.” Mukunda sang; the Lord, overwhelmed with feeling, cried, “Sing! Sing!” — and then, recovering Himself,
said that He would not remain here: He would cut off His shikha, remove His brahmana thread and go away. Mukunda grew sad
and humbly begged: “If You must take sannyasa, then before that lead kirtana for many days; and after that do as You
wish” (CB, Madhya 26.157–165).[^8] <!-- F30 -->

Murari Gupta recalls how Mukunda said to the Vaishnavas: “Look at the Lord while He is still here: soon the teacher of
the world will leave His home and go away” (2.18.17–18); in Karnapura he asks them to gaze at the Lord unblinking. Lochana
Dasa writes that Mukunda understood it from the Lord's hints and, weeping, fell at the devotees' feet, and then spoke
bitter reproaches to the Lord: You are a deceiver, Your heart is harder than diamond, and if You go — what is life to
us?[^9] These are the reproaches of love (*pranaya*) in the grief of separation — the same with which the gopis reproached
Krishna when He left for Mathura. <!-- F31 -->

On the morning after the night the Lord left, Mukunda with the others found His house empty and rolled on the ground in
grief (CB, Madhya 28.85). Karnapura's drama shows Mukunda lamenting, and Advaita telling him to console Shachi with the
news that the Lord had gone not alone but with Nityananda and Acharyaratna. Jayananda writes that Govindananda went to
Shantipura and sent Mukunda to Navadvipa. <!-- F32 F36 -->

## The Sannyasa and the Wandering in Radha-desha
Then Mukunda and the others whom the Lord had told in advance to come went to Katwa (Kantaka-nagara), to Keshava
Bharati: “As the Lord had asked, Nityananda Prabhu, Gadadhara, Mukunda, Chandrashekhara Acharya and Brahmananda Bharati
gradually gathered there” (CB, Madhya 28.103–104). During the sannyasa Mukunda sang while the son of Shachi danced in
waves of bliss (28.112, 149), and the “Charitamrita” names him among those who performed the rites:

“With Him were Nityananda, Chandrashekhara Acharya and Mukunda Datta, and these three performed all the rites.” (CC, Adi
17.273)

As soon as the Lord had accepted sannyasa, He at once told Mukunda to sing kirtana (CB, Antya 1.8). <!-- F33 Q8 -->

Afterwards the Lord wandered through Radha-desha in ecstasy, searching for Krishna, and only a few followed Him:
“Nityananda, Acharyaratna and Mukunda — these three followed close behind the Lord” (CC, Madhya 3.11).[^10] When the Lord
wept, Mukunda would begin to sing (CB, Antya 1.84). <!-- F34 -->

In Shantipura, in Advaita's house, the Lord and Nityananda invited Haridasa and Mukunda to the midday meal, although
Mukunda had in any case been with Him all the way from Katwa. Both, with joined palms, declined. “Mukunda said, ‘Some of
my duties are not yet finished. I shall take prasada later; You go inside’” (CC, Madhya 3.62). Later Advaita ate together
with Mukunda and Haridasa, as He had wished in His heart (3.106–107): the Acharya, crown of the brahmanas, sat in one row
with Mukunda and Haridasa. In the evening there was kirtana, and when the Lord fell in separation from Krishna, Mukunda
understood His heart:

“Mukunda knew the Lord's heart well, and he began to sing a song suited to His mood. The Acharya raised the Lord up to
dance, but on hearing the song the Lord could not hold His body steady… ‘Alas, My friend, dear to Me as life! What has
become of Me! The poison of love for Kanu consumes My body and mind. Night and day My heart burns, and I find no relief.
Wherever I could find Kanu, there I would fly.’” (CC, Madhya 3.121–122, 124–125)

The “Gaura-krishnodaya” tells the same: the crossing of the Ganga with Advaita, Mukunda and Nityananda, and Advaita's meal
with Mukunda (6.36, 54). <!-- F35 -->

## The Road to Puri
From Shantipura the Lord set out for Nilachala. The “Charitamrita” names four who accompanied Him: “Nityananda Gosani,
Jagadananda Pandita, Damodara Pandita and Mukunda Datta” (CC, Madhya 3.209); Govinda-deva in the “Gaura-krishnodaya” and
Ishana Nagara in the “Advaita-prakasha” say the same.[^11] On the way Mukunda sang: in Chhatrabhoga, where the Lord danced
all night (CB, Antya 2.122), in the boat at His command (2.133), and in Jaleshvara, where he arrived soon after the Lord
(2.247). According to the “Anubhashya,” he was nearby also when Nityananda broke the Lord's staff (*danda*). <!-- F37 F38 -->

Angered by the broken staff, the Lord told His companions that either they should go ahead and He would follow, or He
would go ahead and they behind. “Mukunda Datta said, ‘Lord, You go ahead. We shall all follow behind; we shall not go
with You’” (CC, Madhya 5.155); so too in Vrindavana Dasa (CB, Antya 2.235), and at Atharanala Mukunda again said, “You go
ahead” (2.423). <!-- F39 -->

Murari Gupta and Lochana Dasa preserve an amusing incident at a toll post. Mukunda assured the Lord: “There is nothing to
fear from the toll collectors here — I know everyone, however haughty they may be.” The Lord smiled and replied: “So
that is the end of our danger: you have already seen to our protection.” And at the toll post an angry collector bound
Mukunda and held him all day, releasing him in the evening after taking the best cloth (in Lochana, Mukunda's only
blanket). Mukunda, weeping, fell at the Lord's feet: “I did not know Your greatness!” That same night the chief of the
toll collectors himself came to the Lord to beg forgiveness (in Lochana, after seeing the Lord in a dream lying on the
serpent Ananta) and presented a new, precious cloth (Murari 3.7.1–14; “Chaitanya-mangala,” Madhya-khanda).[^12]
<!-- F40 -->

Seeing the temple of Jagannatha from afar, Mukunda pointed it out to the Lord (“Chaitanya-chandrodaya,” act 6). When the
Lord, on reaching Puri, lost consciousness at the sight of Jagannatha and Sarvabhauma Bhattacharya carried Him to his
home, Nityananda, Mukunda, Jagadananda and Damodara came to the Lion Gate and met Gopinatha Acharya:

“He had known Mukunda before, and he was surprised to see him there. On seeing him, Mukunda bowed down, and Gopinatha
embraced him and asked for news of the Lord. Mukunda said, ‘The Lord has come here, and we have all come with
Mahaprabhu’… ‘After taking sannyasa, Mahaprabhu came to Nilachala and brought us all with Him.’” (CC, Madhya 6.19–21, 23)

Gopinatha took them to Sarvabhauma (6.68), and they arrived after the Lord and “all rejoiced” to be with Him again
(Madhya 1.100). In Karnapura's drama Mukunda himself proposes arranging the darshana through Gopinatha and Sarvabhauma,
and the mahakavya says that together with Mukunda Datta and others the Lord beheld Jagannatha for several days (12.9).
<!-- F41 F44 -->

## In Puri
At first Sarvabhauma reasoned about the Lord as a mayavadi would, while Gopinatha argued against him. Mukunda was saddened (CC,
Madhya 6.77); “Mukunda was pleased with the Acharya's conclusions, but the Bhattacharya's words filled him with sorrow and
anger” (6.113). Together with Gopinatha he reported them critically to the Lord, but the Lord stopped them: “Do not speak
like that. The Bhattacharya is kind to Me” (6.115–116). Srila Bhaktisiddhanta remarks that Mukunda's anger at the
Bhattacharya's mayavada words was itself his mercy to him. <!-- F42 -->

When Sarvabhauma had become the Lord's devotee, he wrote two verses about the Lord on a palm leaf and sent them with Jagadananda
together with prasada. Mukunda managed to intercept the leaf:

“The two came to the Lord with the prasada and the letter, and Mukunda Datta, finding the letter in Jagadananda's hand,
took it. He copied the two verses onto the outer wall; then Jagadananda took the letter and gave it to the Lord.” (CC,
Madhya 6.251–252)

The Lord tore up the leaf, but the verses survived on the wall, and the devotees learned them by heart. Here is the
first:

“I take refuge in the one primeval Person, the ocean of mercy, who has assumed the form of Sri Krishna Chaitanya to teach
detachment, knowledge and devotion to Himself.” (CC, Madhya 6.254)

The second says that the Lord came to revive the path of love for Krishna, almost lost through the action of time
(6.255). So, in the words of one of the biographies, these verses became a necklace on the neck of the Gaudiya
Vaishnavas. <!-- F43 Q14 -->

Before leaving for the south, the Lord, explaining to the devotees why He wished to go alone, said of Mukunda:

“Mukunda is unhappy to see the duties of My sannyasa: bathing three times a day even in the cold, and sleeping on the
bare ground. Mukunda grieves within but says nothing; and seeing his grief, I grieve twice as much.” (CC, Madhya
7.23–24)

This is not worldly pity but the love of a servant who cannot calmly watch the austerity the Lord has accepted; and the
Lord Himself treasures that love. <!-- F45 -->

While the Lord was in the south, Mukunda waited for Him in Puri. When news came of His return, “Jagadananda, Damodara
Pandita and Mukunda went along dancing; their bodies could not contain their joy” (CC, Madhya 9.340). Then Nityananda,
Jagadananda, Mukunda and Damodara, taking pity on Kala Krishnadasa, the Lord's servant in the south, took counsel apart
and decided to send him as a messenger to Gauda, and the Lord agreed (Madhya 10.67–70). <!-- F50 Q9 -->

Together with the others Mukunda welcomed the Lord's new companions: Svarupa Damodara (Madhya 10.127), Ramananda Raya,
Haridasa Thakura (11.196). It was he who announced to the Lord the arrival of Brahmananda Bharati:

“The next day Mukunda Datta came to the Lord and said, ‘Brahmananda Bharati has come to see You. If You permit, shall I
bring him here?’… Brahmananda was wearing a deerskin, and when the Lord saw it He was pained at heart. Though He saw him,
He pretended not to, and asked Mukunda, ‘Where is Bharati Gosani?’ Mukunda said, ‘Here he is, right before You.’ The
Lord said, ‘That is not he. You are mistaken.’” (CC, Madhya 10.151–152, 154–156)

In Karnapura's drama the Lord, back from the south, asks Mukunda where Nityananda has gone, and Mukunda answers: “To
Gauda — He promised to return with everyone in time for Your coming.” And when the devotees of Gauda first came to Puri,
Mukunda was with the Lord together with Paramananda Puri, Brahmananda Bharati, Svarupa Damodara, Nityananda and
Jagadananda (Madhya 11.33). <!-- F51 -->

At that time the Lord said to his elder brother:

“Though Mukunda has been with Me since childhood, I feel greater joy in seeing you.” (CC, Madhya 11.138)

And Vasudeva replied:

“Mukunda obtained Your company; he obtained Your feet — that was his second birth. Though he is younger, Mukunda has now
become my elder. He is the recipient of Your mercy, and so he excels in every quality.” (CC, Madhya 11.139–140)

Karnapura writes likewise that Mukunda had long been favoured by the Lord. Seniority among devotees is determined not by
age but by nearness to the Lord: Mukunda had received His mercy earlier — and the elder brother honoured the younger as
his elder. <!-- F52 Q7 I4 -->

At the Ratha-yatra the Lord divided the devotees into seven kirtana groups, and Mukunda led the third:

“He formed another group with Mukunda as the leader, in which Vasudeva, Gopinatha and Murari sang, with two more,
Srikanta and Vallabha Sena; and Haridasa Thakura danced there.” (CC, Madhya 13.40–41)

Srila Bhaktisiddhanta notes that of the five responsive singers the first was Vasudeva Datta — the brother sang in his
brother's chorus.[^13] In the Lord's dance Mukunda was among the nine devotees in Svarupa Damodara's group (13.73), and
when the Lord danced, “Kashishvara, Mukunda and all the other devotees joined hands and formed the second ring” (13.89) to
protect Him. At the Ratha-yatra and in the other festivals of Nilachala Mukunda was the chief singer at the Lord's side.
<!-- F53 Q10 -->

## From Gauda to Nilachala
In time Mukunda, like the other devotees of Gauda, came to Puri every year:

“Advaita, Nityananda, Mukunda, Srivasa, Vidyanidhi, Vasudeva, Murari and all His other servants came together every year
and stayed four months, and with them the Lord enjoyed many kinds of pastimes.” (CC, Madhya 1.255–256)

Vrindavana Dasa writes that the devotees came for the Ratha-yatra at the Lord's instruction (CB, Antya 8.5) and that with
them “came also Mukunda Datta, the singer of Lord Krishna” (8.15); on Lake Narendra Mukunda Datta and Murari Gupta fought
again and again in the water, shouting loudly in bliss (8.123). Karnapura portrays Mukunda on this journey: pure, tender
of heart, sweet, peaceful, with consoling speech, exceedingly gentle — and beside him, “like a second Mukunda,” walked
Murari (mahakavya 14.27–28). Murari Gupta too names Vasudeva Datta and Mukunda Datta among those who came (4.17.5).
According to one of the biographies, after the Lord's sannyasa the brothers moved to Kumarahatta, near Kanchrapara, north
of Calcutta — once the home of Ishvara Puri,[^14] and after the Lord's return from the south Mukunda was sent to preach
bhakti in Bengal.[^15] <!-- F54 F7 Q11 Q12 -->

When in 1514 the Lord went from Puri to Gauda on His way to Vrindavana, Mukunda was among His chief companions (CC,
Madhya 16.127–129). A Muslim governor of the frontier who had become a devotee asked how he could serve, and Mukunda
replied:

“Listen, good sir: Mahaprabhu wishes to go to the bank of the Ganga. Help Him in some way to get there. That is the great
order, and that will be a great service.” (CC, Madhya 16.190–191)

In Ramakeli, where the Lord met Rupa and Sanatana, Mukunda was among the companions whom the Lord told to bless the two
brothers: “Nityananda, Haridasa, Srivasa, Gadadhara, Mukunda, Jagadananda, Murari and Vakreshvara were there” (Madhya
1.219). <!-- F55 -->

Years later, when Raghunatha Dasa came to Puri, it was Mukunda who first noticed him: “Keeping his distance in the
courtyard, he bowed down. Mukunda Datta said, ‘Here is Raghunatha!’” (CC, Antya 6.190). Srila Bhaktisiddhanta explains:
Mukunda names him to the Lord. And once Jagadananda, Svarupa Damodara, Govinda, Kashishvara, Shankara, Damodara and
Mukunda, coming to bathe in the sea, heard from afar the singing of Junior Haridasa, who had already left this world
(Antya 2.153–154). <!-- F56 F57 -->

The Lord Himself named Mukunda when, in His humility, He told Vallabha Bhatta from whom He had learned bhakti:

“Acharyaratna, Acharyanidhi, Gadadhara Pandita, Jagadananda, Damodara, Shankara, Vakreshvara, Kashishvara, Mukunda,
Vasudeva, Murari and all the other devotees who descended in Gauda have preached the name of Krishna and prema
throughout the world, and whatever devotion to Krishna I have, I have gained in their company.” (CC, Antya 7.48–50)
<!-- F58 Q13 -->

## Departure
When and where Mukunda Datta left this world the sources do not say; neither the year nor the day of his appearance is
known. According to the Vaishnava calendar of the Sri Chaitanya Saraswat Math, the day of his departure is the purnima of
the month of Trivikrama, the day of Jagannatha's Snana-yatra (in 2026, 29 June); on the same day the departure of
Shridhara Pandita is observed. <!-- F60 -->

## Significance
Mukunda Datta is Madhukantha, Krishna's eternal singer in Vraja, and in Gaura's pastimes the one whose voice accompanied
the Lord from the first evenings in Advaita's house to the Ratha-yatra in Puri. The Lord Himself said of him: “Wherever you
sing, I always appear there in person,” and “Whenever I come into this world, you will always be My singer.” <!-- F2 F27 -->

His life teaches above all the purity of bhakti. The Lord “rejected” Mukunda only until the appointed time — until He
revealed Himself; and through the “punishment” of His eternal associate He showed the world that any compromise with a
doctrine that places anything above bhakti is an offence, and that seeing the Lord without bhakti bears no fruit. The
punishment itself was mercy: Advaita calls Mukunda fortunate, and Krishnadasa Kaviraja speaks of the Lord's grace in the
form of punishment. <!-- I1 I2 F28 -->

And Mukunda revealed one thing more: the power of faith. Hearing that he would see the Lord only after millions of
births, he did not despair but danced with joy that he would see Him after all — and by this faith in the infallibility
of the Lord's word he compressed millions of births into a moment and bound the Lord in his heart. And the elder
brother's reverence for the younger reminds us that among Vaishnavas seniority is given not by age but by the Lord's
mercy. <!-- I3 I4 -->
