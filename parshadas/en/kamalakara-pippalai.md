# Kamalakara Pippalai
*Камалакара Пиппалаи · @kamalakara-pippalai*

## A Branch of Nityananda
Among the twelve gopalas, the closest companions of Nityananda Prabhu, stands Sri Kamalakara Pippalai — the one to whom
the Lord Himself entrusted the service of Sri Jagannatha at Mahesh on the bank of the Ganga. The “Chaitanya-charitamrita” calls him
Kamalakara Pippalai and simply Kamalakara; the vandanas call him Pippalai, Pippalai Thakura and Sri Kamalakara; in the
“Narottama-vilasa” and the “Prema-vilasa” he is Kamalakara Piplai, and in the “Nityananda-vamsha-vistara,” Pipilai.
<!-- F1 -->

Listing the branches of Nityananda’s tree, Krishnadasa Kaviraja Gosvami first speaks of His whole company:

“All of Nityananda’s associates are cowherd friends from Vraja. Each carries a horn and a staff and wears the dress of a
cowherd, with a peacock feather on his head.” (CC, Adi 11.21)

And of Kamalakara himself he writes a single line:

“Kamalakara Pippalai was extraordinary in his ways, and his extraordinary love is known throughout the world.”
(CC, Adi 11.24) <!-- F3 Q2 -->

In the lists of the twelve gopalas he stands third, after Abhirama and Sundarananda and before Gauridasa. Lochana Dasa,
in the “Chaitanya-mangala,” naming those who came to earth with the Lord, includes “Sri Kamalakara” among the twelve
cowherd boys (gopalas) of Vraja (Sutra-khanda 626–627). <!-- F5 F6 -->

## Family and Birthplace
Kamalakara appeared at Khalijuli, a village of the Sundarbans — according to the “Gaudiya-vaishnava-abhidhana,” in
the Shaka year 1414 (Bengali year 899; 1492 CE).[^1] He was a brahmana by birth, of the Radhiya brahmanas; “Pippalai” is a
family name, counted among the fifty-five “grami” that the Radhiyas bear after the villages of their forefathers. His
father, a wealthy landowner, is not named in the biographies. Kamalakara’s younger brother was Nidhipati
Pippalai. <!-- F8 F9 F10 Q9 -->

Though Khalijuli was his birthplace, he lived at Mahesh, a village on the Ganga in the Hooghly district, some 2½ miles
from Srirampur (Serampore). Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati says the same in the “Anubhashya,” noting that the
temple of Jagannatha at Mahesh is 2½ miles from Srirampur station.[^2] <!-- F11 -->

## Jagannatha at Mahesh
How the service of Jagannatha came into Kamalakara’s hands is told in a tradition that Srila Bhaktisiddhanta records in
the “Anubhashya.” Once a renunciant Vaishnava (*udasina*) named Dhruvananda came to Purushottama-kshetra, Puri, with a
burning desire to cook bhoga for Sri Jagannatha himself and offer it to the Lord with his own hands. At night Jagannatha
came to him in a dream and bade him go to Mahesh, a village on the Ganga, establish His service there and every day
offer Him bhoga with his own hands: thus his desire would be fulfilled.[^3] <!-- F22 Q3 -->

Dhruvananda came to Mahesh — and saw, approaching him on the waters of the Ganga, Sri Jagannatha with Balarama and
Subhadra-devi. The Lord Himself had appeared where He wished to accept service. Dhruvananda reverently lifted the three
Deities out of the river, raised a hut for Them on the shore and took up Their service. <!-- F23 -->

As the time of his own departure drew near, one thought filled Dhruvananda’s heart: who would be a worthy servant of
Jagannatha? Again the Lord answered him in a dream: the next morning a man of Khalijuli near the Sundarbans, named
Kamalakara Pippalai, a great devotee of Jagannatha and the crown of Vaishnavas, would come to Mahesh, and the service
was to be entrusted to him. According to tradition,
Kamalakara too received the Lord’s command.[^4] In the morning Dhruvananda met him and handed over to him the service of
Sri Jagannatha, Baladeva and Subhadra. From him the Lord’s service at Mahesh descends, and therefore it is said
that Kamalakara Pippalai established the Deity of Mahesh; today it is the famous Deity of Jagannatha. Having received the
right to serve the Lord, Kamalakara and his family came to be called Adhikari, and the family bears that name to this
day. <!-- F24 F25 F20 F26 -->

The biographies see in this story a law of bhakti: only a devotee’s service does the Lord accept, and a devotee counts
the chance to serve Vishnu and the Vaishnavas his highest wealth. The Lord Himself shows how to serve Him to one who
longs for it with all his heart; but one attached to sense enjoyment sees service as a burden and looks for ways to
avoid it. <!-- I1 -->

And Kamalakara had come to Mahesh in this way: as soon as he received Sri Jagannatha’s command, he at once left his
home and all his relatives. In this there was neither flight from duty nor harsh austerity. A devotee, even when he plays the role of a
householder, is not like a worldly family man: his mind is ever immersed in viraha, the pain of being apart from the Lord, and at a single
word from Him he gladly leaves worldly ties for the sake of service. Such renunciation comes of itself, naturally, not
forced as with jnanis and yogis; and at times the Lord Himself wishes His devotee to remain in family life.
<!-- F27 I2 -->

Nor was Kamalakara’s family abandoned. A second tradition preserved in the “Anubhashya” tells that his younger brother
Nidhipati searched for him long and found him at Mahesh. Unable to persuade Kamalakara to return home, he at last
settled at Mahesh himself, bringing both his own family and Kamalakara’s. <!-- F12 -->

According to the “Vaishnavachara-darpana,” Kamalakara lived at Mahesh on the western bank of the Ganga, served the Deity
of Jagannatha there and glorified Radha and Krishna in song day and night. One of the biographies adds that before he
came the country around Mahesh was jungle: he cleared the land and founded an exemplary settlement, famed far and wide.
<!-- F4 F21 Q6 -->

## Descendants and Jagannatha’s Land
Kamalakara’s line, according to the “Anubhashya,” is this: his son was Chaturbhuja, who had two sons, Narayana and
Jagannatha; Jagadananda was born to Narayana, and Rajiva-lochana to Jagadananda.[^5] To this day some thirty
brahmana families of his line live at Mahesh, among them Sri Prasadadasa Adhikari.[^6] His descendants, as the
“Amrita-pravaha-bhashya” writes, serve Jagannatha at Mahesh. <!-- F14 F13 F28 Q3 -->

In Rajiva-lochana’s time the means for Jagannatha’s service ran short. Then, in the Bengali year 1060 (1653 CE), the
Nawab of Dhaka — apparently Shah Shuja — made a gift of 1185 bighas to Jagannathadeva, in a village a krosha and a half
west of Mahesh; the mauza was named after the Lord — Jagannathpur.[^7] <!-- F15 -->

A mile north of Mahesh lies Vallabhapura, the shripata of Rudra Pandita (Rudrarama), whose Deity is Radha-vallabha.
Srila Bhaktisiddhanta writes that formerly, at Ratha-yatra, Jagannatha of Mahesh used to visit the temple of
Radha-vallabha at Vallabhapura; but from the Bengali year 1262 (1855–1856), owing to quarrels among the servitors, the
custom ceased. <!-- F28 -->

## Daughter and Granddaughter
The sources speak differently of Kamalakara’s daughter. The “Nityananda-vamsha-vistara” begins its account of
Virachandra’s line thus:

“In Mahesh there lived a brahmana of pure heart; the worship of Vishnu and the Vaishnavas was his daily duty. His name
was Sudhamaya; he was the son-in-law of Pipilai, and his wife was named Vidyunmala.” (“Nityananda-vamsha-vistara” 3.3–4)

By this account Vidyunmala was Kamalakara’s daughter and Sudhamaya of Mahesh his son-in-law.[^8] The couple had no
children, and Narayani became their daughter: according to the “Nityananda-vamsha-vistara,” she was given to them by the
Ocean. Narayani became the wife of Sri Virabhadra Prabhu, Nityananda’s son. The Adhikaris of Mahesh, descendants of the
Pippalai line, call this girl Radharani.[^9] <!-- F16 F17 Q5 -->

Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati writes otherwise: Virabhadra married Shrimati, born to Yadunatha Acharya of the village of
Jhamatpur in the Hooghly district and his wife Vidyunmala (Lakshmi), and also their adopted daughter Narayani. That Vidyunmala was Kamalakara’s daughter Srila Bhaktisiddhanta does not say outright. The
“Vaishnavachara-darpana,” for its part, relates that Vidyunmala was given in marriage to a worthy brahmana, Yadunandana, whose father-in-law
was a great devotee of Jagannatha who knew no desire but to serve Jagannatha. <!-- F18 F19 Q4 -->

## With Nityananda and Jahnava
Kamalakara’s shripata at Akna-Mahesh, near Srirampur, became the scene of one of the wonders of sankirtana. Once, while
harinama-sankirtana was going on there, godless men flung a jackal’s carcass into the circle of singers — and by the power of
Parameshvari Dasa’s kirtana the jackal revived. <!-- F29 -->

According to one tradition, Kamalakara was great in the irrepressibility of his bhava, and Nityananda gave him the
village of Panihati.[^10] Jayananda, telling of the wonders of Nityananda’s companions after
the Lord’s disappearance, calls Kamalakara’s “madness of prema” great: overwhelmed by ecstasy, he did not notice even the
wounds on his own body and did not return to outward consciousness.[^11] This was no worldly derangement, but the
self-forgetfulness of a cowherd boy of Vraja in love for the Lord. <!-- F30 F31 Q7 -->

He was also at the famous chida-dadhi festival at Panihati arranged by Raghunatha Dasa, which the biographies call the
Danda-mahotsava.[^12] Krishnadasa Kaviraja, naming the close companions who sat around Nityananda on the raised platform,
names him as well:

“Ramadasa, Sundarananda, Gadadhara Dasa, Murari, Kamalakara, Sadashiva, Purandara…” (CC, Antya 6.61)

In the “Anubhashya” on this verse Srila Bhaktisiddhanta explains that this is the same Kamalakara spoken of in Adi
11.24. <!-- F32 -->

Kamalakara attended the festival at Katwa given by Gadadhara Dasa as well. And when Narottama Thakura called the
Vaishnavas to the great Kheturi festival, Kamalakara went there from Kharadaha in the retinue of Jahnava-devi. The
“Bhakti-ratnakara” names him among her companions: “Sri Shankara, Sri Kamalakara Pippalai, Nrisimha, Chaitanya, Jiva,
Pandita Kanai” (BR 10.375). His name also appears in the “Narottama-vilasa” — among those whom the news of the gathering
of Vaishnavas reached (6.188), among the mahantas who sat down to the feast in Jahnava’s courtyard on the festival’s second day (8.51) and among those whom Ishvari, Jahnava-devi,
told to return to Kharadaha (8.152–153); the “Prema-vilasa” mentions him too (19.309). <!-- F33 F34 Q8 -->

## Departure
According to the “Vaishnavachara-darpana,” once his daughter was married, Kamalakara went to Vrindavana-dhama and,
living there, disappeared from view. The Adhikaris of Mahesh keep a tradition that he left this world in the Shaka year
1485 (Bengali year 970; 1563 CE), at the age of seventy-one, on the shukla-trayodashi of the month of Chaitra (by the
Vaishnava reckoning, the month of Vishnu).[^13] No primary source for these dates is named, and our calendar of the Sri
Chaitanya Saraswat Math has no days of Kamalakara Pippalai’s appearance or disappearance. <!-- F35 F36 -->

## Mahabala
Who Kamalakara was in Krishna’s eternal pastimes Kavi Karnapura reveals in the “Gaura-ganoddesha-dipika”:

“Subala, the best of the dear friends, is Gauridasa Pandita; the mighty Mahabala is Kamalakara Pippalai.” (GGD 128)

So he is called by Srila Bhaktisiddhanta in the “Anubhashya” — “one of the twelve gopalas, Mahabala” — and by the
“Vaishnavachara-darpana”: the cowherd boy Mahabala of Vrindavana has now come as Kamalakara Pippalai. A cowherd friend
of Vraja, in Gaura’s pastimes too he remained a companion of Nityananda — of Balarama. <!-- F2 Q1 -->

The vandanas remember in him just this cowherd, childlike mood. Devakinandana praises “Kamalakara Pippalai, sporting in
bhava, who said to the Lord: ‘Take the stick, give me the flute’” (Devakinandana’s “Vaishnava-vandana,” 96) — the words of
a cowherd friend of Vraja. The Sanskrit vandana attributed to Srila Jiva Gosvami honours “Pippalai,
overwhelmed by a childlike mood” (100); the tripadi vandana, “Thakura Pippalai, artless in a child’s feeling, all of whose
pastimes and play are like a child’s” (85); and beside him in both stands Kamalakara Dasa — he “whose bliss is
sankirtana” (100), and “whose highest exultation is in the sankirtana of Krishna” (86).[^14] In Devakinandana’s Sanskrit list of the names of Vaishnavas “Sri Kamalakara” is named among the
associates as well (32). <!-- F7 -->

The extraordinary conduct and extraordinary love of which Krishnadasa Kaviraja speaks are the love of Mahabala, the
friend of Krishna and Balarama. Its fruit has remained in the world: at the Lord’s command Kamalakara left everything to
serve Him, and Jagannatha of Mahesh, who chose His servant Himself, accepts service from his line to this day.
