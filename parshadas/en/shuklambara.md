# Shuklambara Brahmachari
*Шукламбара Брахмачари · @shuklambara*

## The Mendicant Brahmachari of Navadvipa
In the Navadvipa of the days when Sri Chaitanya appeared there, a poor brahmana lived right on the bank of the Ganga, and
his neighbours scarcely noticed him. Every morning he slung a bag over his shoulder and went from house to house, and
whatever he was given he offered to Krishna. His name was Shuklambara Brahmachari. Vrindavana Dasa calls him “the pious
(*sukriti*) Shuklambara” and “the brahmana”; Murari Gupta, “the brahmana Shuklambara” and “the brahmachari Shuklambara”;
Kavi Karnapura, “Shuklambara, the best of the twice-born”; Krishnadasa Kaviraja, Shuklambara Brahmachari.[^1] His house
stood by the Ganga: so says Vrindavana Dasa (CB, Madhya 1.78; 26.22), and a century later Narahari Chakravarti, leading
pilgrims around Navadvipa, points to the spot: “This is the house of Shuklambara Brahmachari” (“Bhakti-ratnakara,”
12.2314–2315). <!-- F1 F4 -->

Before telling of the great mercy the Lord showed Shuklambara, Vrindavana Dasa describes him thus:

“Shuklambara steadily kept to his duties and remained wholly at peace. He was a great devotee, though no one knew it. He
carried a bag on his shoulder and with it went begging from house to house throughout Navadvipa. Repeating the names of
Krishna day and night, he often wept. People took him for an ordinary beggar and did not know who he really was. He was
so poor that he had to beg for his food. Everything he collected in a day the brahmana offered to Krishna and then ate as
the remnants of His meal. Rapt in the mercy of Krishna he had received, he did not know what poverty was. He went from
house to house singing the names of the Lord.” (CB, Madhya 16.111–115) <!-- F6 -->

He was a brahmachari and lived on alms, and the biographies add that in this way Shuklambara enacted the pastimes of a
poor brahmana begging for his food. But his poverty was no worldly destitution. He had nothing but Krishna, and so he
felt no want: day and night he sang the names and qualities of the Lord, and the beggar's bag was for him only a means of
service. Only those whose eyes are turned outward saw him as a beggar; a servant of the Lord, says Vrindavana Dasa, can be
recognized only by one who has himself received Sri Chaitanya's mercy (Madhya 16.116). He also compares Shuklambara with
the poor brahmana Damodara, that is, Sudama, Krishna's friend: Shuklambara served Vishnu just as the penniless Damodara
once served Him (16.117). One of the biographies says that to the conditioned soul Shuklambara is a pauper, but to
transcendental sight a rich man, for he is rich in love for Mahaprabhu; and it recalls here words of the
“Charitamrita” that were spoken, it is true, not of him but of the treasure of prema in general:

“Without the wealth of love, life is poor and futile! Make me Your servant and give me the wealth of love as my wages!”
(CC, Antya 20.37) <!-- F5 F6 F7 I3 -->

Nothing more is known of his worldly life. The sources name neither his parents nor his family, neither the year nor the
day of his appearance, nor when and where he left this world; nor are his days found in the Vaishnava calendar of the
Sri Chaitanya Saraswat Math. <!-- F5 -->

## A Priest's Wife and the Brahmana Sudama
Who Shuklambara is in Krishna's pastimes, Kavi Karnapura tells us:

“Shuklambara Brahmachari, whose food the Lord Gauranga asked for and ate, was formerly the wife of a sacrificial priest.
Some say that the Brahmachari was formerly a brahmana performing sacrifices.” (“Gaura-ganoddesha-dipika” 191)

By the first view he is one of the wives of the sacrificing brahmanas (*yajna-patni*) of Vraja who, hearing that Krishna
and His cowherd friends were hungry, carried food to them, transgressing for Krishna's sake the rules of the sacrifice;
their love for Him was pure and knew no calculation. So too, as we shall see, Mahaprabhu for Shuklambara's sake
transgressed the rules for offering food. One of the biographies says explicitly: “one of the wives of the priests in
Vraja.” The second view in the same verse is that Shuklambara was formerly a brahmana priest; the biographies give both
views without choosing between them. And the Lord Himself, in the “Chaitanya-bhagavata,” reminds Shuklambara how in
Dvaraka He snatched broken rice from him — that is, He calls him Sudama (of this below). This is a different
correspondence; we give both, neither merging them nor choosing between them. <!-- F2 Q1 -->

## Named in Advaita's Vow
Even before the Lord appeared, Navadvipa hardly knew what kirtan of Krishna's names was, or dance and music dedicated to
Him, or anything at all that concerns Krishna. People were busy with their pleasures, and the holy name from the lips of
pure devotees only disturbed them; the devotees, fearing the blasphemers of the name (*pashandi*), anxiously told their
grief to Krishna. Then Advaita Prabhu, the incarnation of Mahavishnu, in anger vowed to bring Krishna down. Among those
he addressed was Shuklambara:

“Listen, Srivasa, Gangadasa and Shuklambara! I promise you that Lord Krishna will soon descend to the earth, and all
will be able to see Him. He will deliver all living beings by teaching the science of devotional service, and you will
help Him.” (CB, Adi 2.118–119)

People had fallen into wrong thinking, Advaita said, because they had no faith in Krishna; but Krishna Himself would come,
show Himself to all, and together with devotees such as they were explain to people why one must serve Krishna — and so
deliver them. <!-- F8 F9 -->

## The Lord's First Prema — in His House
When He returned from Gaya, Mahaprabhu was changed. Speaking of the holy place, He for the first time revealed to the
devotees how love in separation from Krishna worked in Him, and on the next day He showed it in all its power — in the
house of Shuklambara. One of the biographies places Nimai's return from Gaya in 1509 and says that Srivasa, Shriman,
Gadadhara, Sadashiva and Shuklambara were amazed at the change in His nature. <!-- F10 F14 -->

The first to see the Lord's transformations was Shriman Pandita. Coming to Himself, the Lord said to him:

“Come early in the morning, the two of you, with Sadashiva, to the house of Shuklambara Brahmachari.” (CB, Madhya 1.40)

And to all His intimates:

“Let us meet tomorrow in the house of Shuklambara. In that secluded place I shall tell you what I feel in My heart.”
(CB, Madhya 1.50)

So the Lord resolved to reveal Himself to the devotees. Shriman passed His words on to the Vaishnavas: the Lord was
calling both Sadashiva and Murari to Shuklambara's house (1.69–70). <!-- F11 Q5 -->

In the morning Shriman went there along the bank of the Ganga. Gadadhara, learning of it, hurried after him and hid in
the house: the Lord would surely speak of Krishna, and he wished to hear it, if only in secret. Vrindavana Dasa relates:

“Shriman Pandita went to the house of Shuklambara Brahmachari, which stood on the bank of the Ganga. Hearing of the
Lord's request, Gadadhara at once set off for the house of Shuklambara Brahmachari. Thinking of how Mahaprabhu would
speak about Krishna, Gadadhara hid in Shuklambara's house. Soon all the Lord's intimate companions gathered there —
Sadashiva, Murari, Shriman and Shuklambara.” (CB, Madhya 1.78–81)

According to one of the biographies, Gadadhara sat inside while the Lord spoke with the devotees on the veranda. Here too,
in Shuklambara's house, the Lord after His return from Gaya saw Murari Gupta: he was among those invited, and with all
the rest watched in amazement how the Lord had changed. <!-- F12 F14 -->

As soon as He came in, Vishvambhara began to recite verses on devotion, and then cried, “I found My Lord — but where has
He gone?!” and clasped a pillar.

“The pillar in Shuklambara's house cracked under Mahaprabhu's weight. He fell to the ground, His hair dishevelled, and
with tears in His eyes He asked, ‘Where is Krishna?’” (CB, Madhya 1.86)

After Him all the devotees fell senseless, and Gadadhara in the next room.

“Singing and weeping merged into one in Shuklambara's house, filling the hearts of all the devotees with ecstatic
feelings of love for Krishna.” (CB, Madhya 1.94)

So in his house the Lord for the first time openly showed the signs of great love (*mahaprema*): swoons, streams of
tears, trembling and gooseflesh, a faltering voice, mad laughter. Murari Gupta adds a touch an eyewitness preserved: the
Lord rolled on the ground, and mucus ran from His nose —

“…when the Lord rolled on the ground in the hermitage of the brahmana Shuklambara, the ceaseless stream of mucus was drawn
away again and again and tirelessly thrown far off by Shuklambara Brahmachari — ever pure, and full of the rasa of love
for Gaurachandra.” (Murari, 2.1.20–21)

Lochana Dasa tells it the same way: in Shuklambara's house the Lord weeps, lost in love, and the brahmana Shuklambara
keeps wiping away the mucus (“Chaitanya-mangala,” Madhya-khanda, pt. 1, vv. 35–37). So in those very first days, when the Lord's
love was only beginning to break through, the master of the house was beside Him and served Him. <!-- F10 F15 -->

When the Lord came to Himself and sat up, tears were still flowing from His eyes.

“The Lord asked, ‘Who is hiding in the next room?’ Shuklambara Brahmachari answered, ‘It is Your Gadadhara.’” (CB,
Madhya 1.96)

So too in the “Bhakti-ratnakara” Narahari Chakravarti, showing pilgrims Shuklambara's house, recalls these words
(12.2319). All that followed — the Lord's words to Gadadhara, his tears, the embrace — belongs to the story of Gadadhara
Pandita. As witnesses of the Lord's transformations in Shuklambara's house the biographies name Gadadhara, Sadashiva,
Murari, Srivasa, Shriman and others. <!-- F13 F10 -->

## “From Now On, Every Night”
Soon the Lord called the devotees to sing together:

“The Lord said: ‘Brothers, hear the essence of all mantras. Why do we waste our nights? From now on let us hold a festival
every night — all singing the holy names of God together.’” (CB, Madhya 8.106–107)[^2]

From then on kirtan resounded every night — in Srivasa's house, and sometimes at Chandrashekhara Acharya's — and
Vrindavana Dasa, listing those who came, names Shuklambara too (Madhya 8.112–116). So from the very beginning of the
sankirtana pastimes he was among Sri Chaitanya's many servants. <!-- F16 Q2 F17 Q7 -->

He was also among those with whom the Lord sported in the waters of the Ganga when, after delivering Jagai and Madhai, He
went with the devotees to bathe (Madhya 13.336–339). And on the great day when the Lord led sankirtana through the town and
subdued the Kazi, Vrindavana Dasa names him among those who “knew well how to perform kirtan rightly” (23.150–152), and
among those who wept with love at Shridhara's house:

“Jagadisha, Gopinatha, Nandana Acharya, Shuklambara, Garuda and many other devotees shed tears.” (CB, Madhya 23.452)
<!-- F18 F19 Q11 -->

Once on the bank of the Ganga Gauranga seated Gadadhara beside Him on a platform at the foot of a tree, and Advaita
performed arati to Them. One of the biographies says that Shuklambara anointed the Lord with sandal paste;[^3] so too sings
Narahari Sarakara in a pada on Gaura's arati: “Shuklambara anoints Him with sandal.” <!-- F20 -->

When the Lord staged a play about Krishna in Chandrashekhara's house, He Himself assigned the roles. Karnapura in the drama
“Chaitanya-chandrodaya” gives His words:

“Srivasa, you will be Narada. Shuklambara will be your snataka (your brahmachari disciple).” (“Chaitanya-chandrodaya,”
act 3)

And in the mahakavya Karnapura paints his entrance: after Narada comes his servant with a water-pot and a seat of kusha
grass, his body white with the clay of the Ganga — “on earth he was called Shuklambara” (“Chaitanya-charita-mahakavya,”
11.8). <!-- F43 -->

When the Lord, in separation, threw Himself into the Ganga and Nityananda carried Him to the bank, Shuklambara and others
wept; but, seeing that the Lord was calm and happy, they began to speak among themselves of Krishna (Murari, 2.11.25;
“Chaitanya-mangala,” Madhya-khanda, pt. 9, v. 77). Lochana Dasa names him also among the companions who gathered together at
the Lord's call (pt. 7, v. 76), and among those whom the Lord took with Him to teach people service by His own example
(pt. 9, v. 117). <!-- F44 -->

## Rice from the Bag
The dearest of the Lord's pastimes with Shuklambara Vrindavana Dasa tells in the sixteenth chapter of the Madhya-khanda.
It was at night, in Srivasa's house, during kirtan. The Lord, in the mood of the Supreme Master (*ishvara-avesha*) —
openly revealing Himself as Bhagavan — was showering mercy on the devotees, on each according to his fortune, when He saw
Shuklambara standing before Him (Madhya 16.108–109). How he came to be there, Vrindavana Dasa tells thus:

“Vishvambhara was so merciful to him that He let him stay inside the house, where he could watch the Lord's dance.
Seeing the brahmana dance in rapture with the bag on his shoulder, the Lord and all the Vaishnavas smiled broadly. When
Vishvambhara, absorbed in the mood of the Supreme Lord, sat on the throne, Shuklambara went on dancing, weeping and
laughing, the bag still on his shoulder.” (CB, Madhya 16.118–120)[^4]

Narahari Chakravarti, showing pilgrims Srivasa's house, points to the platform (*khatta*) on which the Lord then sat: here,
in the rapture of sankirtana, He thrust His hand into the bag of Shuklambara, who had just returned from begging, and with
great love ate his rice, calling him Sudama; and Shuklambara, again and again expressing his humility, danced in the
kirtan with the bag on his shoulder, and seeing his love the Lord and His companions rejoiced; then the Lord went home
through the town (“Bhakti-ratnakara,” 12.2754–2758). <!-- F21 Q10 -->

Seeing Shuklambara, the Lord began to glorify him:

“Looking at Shuklambara, the all-merciful Gauranga called to him: ‘Come! Come here! Life after life you remain My
penniless servant. You give Me everything and yourself live on alms. I always long for your offerings, and even if you do
not offer them to Me, I take them from you and eat them. Have you forgotten how in Dvaraka I snatched broken rice from you
and ate it? Kamala, the goddess of fortune, caught My hand then.’” (CB, Madhya 16.121–124)

So the Lord Himself reveals that Shuklambara is that same poor brahmana Sudama whose broken rice Krishna once snatched
from him in Dvaraka in the eagerness of love, until Kamala — the goddess Lakshmi — held back His hand. <!-- F22 F26 Q3 -->

Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati, in the “Gaudiya-bhashya” on these verses, unfolds their meaning in the Lord's own voice:
“Birth after birth you are My poor devotee. You have no wish to enter worldly life and become a householder: as a
brahmachari you beg from door to door and offer Me all you have collected. You are a naishthika-brahmachari; you are free
of the worldly, verbal pride of householders and vanaprasthas. Abiding in the dharma of the paramahamsa, you have taken
the form of the fourth ashrama — the form of one who owns nothing (*akinchana*) — and so you are a tridandi-bhikshu wholly
surrendered to Me. All the acts of your body, mind and speech you have been able to give wholly to Me. I always ask for
your offering, and I have no taste for anything you have not offered Me. That is why I have taken all you had by force —
that is why you are poor.” <!-- F25 -->

There is no worldly “poverty by the will of God” here, nor any seizing of what belongs to another: it is the loving play
of the Lord, who longs for His devotee's offering. He accepts love, not wealth, and so He Himself makes His servant
possess nothing — so that all he has may belong to Him. <!-- F25 -->

And having spoken so, the Lord put His hand into the bag:

“With these words Vishvambhara took a handful of raw rice from Shuklambara's bag and began to chew it. ‘O Lord, You have
killed me!’ cried Shuklambara. ‘This rice is full of broken grains!’ The Lord replied: ‘I eat your broken rice, but I will
not even look at the nectar of a non-devotee.’” (CB, Madhya 16.125–127)

The rice was dry, unwashed, full of broken grains, of the plainest kind, as one of the biographies stresses. Handful after
handful the Lord took it and chewed, and all the rice Shuklambara had collected in ten houses Gaurasundara, the husband of
Lakshmi, ate up (16.140). Shuklambara's alarm was not worldly anxiety but humility: he feared he had offended the Lord
with unworthy food. But the Lord reassured him: a devotee's food He eats with eagerness, while the most exquisite dishes
of a non-devotee are of no use to Him. <!-- F23 F24 -->

Seeing such compassion of the Lord, the devotees wept, fell wherever they were, took straw between their teeth and
pleaded, “Lord, do not abandon me!” (16.129–132). And Shuklambara himself —

“The virtuous Shuklambara rolled on the ground, while the Lord of Vaikuntha ate his rice with relish.” (CB, Madhya
16.133)[^5]

One of the biographies sees a lesson here: whatever an exclusive (*aikantika*) devotee does comes from Krishna's will.
<!-- F27 I2 -->

Then the Lord blessed him:

“Then the Lord said: ‘Listen, Shuklambara Brahmachari! I always enjoy My pastimes in your heart. Whenever you eat, I eat.
And when you go begging, I go with you. I have descended to the earth to give away prema-bhakti. You are My servant life
after life. And now I give you prema-bhakti. Know for certain that this gift is My very life and soul.’” (CB, Madhya
16.134–137)

Hearing that the Lord had granted Shuklambara prema, the Vaishnavas cried, “Jaya! Jaya! Hari! Hari!” (16.138), rejoicing
to see such mercy. <!-- F28 F29 Q3 -->

Vrindavana Dasa explains what the Lord showed by this. Through the Vedas He Himself laid down the rules by which food is to
be offered to Him, and He does not accept what is not offered by those rules; but for His devotees He may set aside every
injunction.

“The rice accepted from Shuklambara is the proof of this. Devotion, then, is the soul of all the commandments.” (CB, Madhya
16.143)

The brahmana made no mudras of offering and did not even offer Him the rice, yet the Lord ate it with great longing
(16.146). So He showed that the path of love (*anuraga*) is more glorious than the path of archana, and that devotion to
Krishna stands above all. Elsewhere one of the biographies places Shuklambara beside Sudama and the words of the
“Bhagavad-gita” (9.26): the Lord gladly accepts what a devotee brings Him with love, and does not accept the gifts of a
non-devotee. <!-- F30 I1 I5 -->

Krishnadasa Kaviraja in the “Charitamrita” recalls this rice twice. Listing the branches of Sri Chaitanya's tree, he says:

“Shuklambara Brahmachari was most fortunate: the Lord Himself would beg his rice, or snatch it from him, and eat it.”
(CC, Adi 10.38)

Srila Bhaktisiddhanta explains in the “Anubhashya”: in Navadvipa the Lord with great joy would snatch and eat the rice
that Shuklambara had collected by begging — of this the “Chaitanya-bhagavata” tells in Madhya 16 and 26.[^6] And in the list
of the Madhya-lila pastimes Kaviraja writes:

“Then He ate the raw rice that Shuklambara had begged, and He explained the meaning of the verse *harer nama*.” (CC,
Adi 17.20)

Srila Bhaktivinoda Thakura adds in the “Amrita-pravaha-bhashya” that Shuklambara lived on the bank of the Ganga and came
with his bag while the Lord was dancing. And Vrindavana Dasa, at the very beginning of his book, listing the pastimes of
the Madhya-khanda, says:

“In the Madhya-khanda the Lord eats the rice of Shuklambara Brahmachari and performs many other pastimes of Narayana.”
(CB, Adi 1.134)

Srila Bhaktisiddhanta explains on this verse that, coming to Shuklambara's house, the Lord accepts two kinds of rice —
*atapa* (sun-dried) and *siddha* (parboiled). <!-- F31 F3 Q9 -->

The chapter ends with a promise:

“Hearing the account of how the Lord ate Shuklambara's rice grants one devotional service at the lotus feet of Lord
Chaitanya.” (CB, Madhya 16.151)[^7] <!-- F32 Q4 -->

## “Do Not Jackals Go There Too?”
Another scene in which Shuklambara receives prema is told by Murari Gupta, Lochana Dasa and Kavi Karnapura.[^8] The Lord,
having revealed Himself, was granting the devotees the boons they desired.

“The Lord, loving to His devotees, granted them the boons they desired. Then Shuklambara Brahmachari said to Him, the bull
among men: ‘O Bhagavan, I have been to Mathura and to Dvaravati, and still I am very unhappy. Knowing this, give me loving
devotion.’ The Lord answered him: ‘Do not jackals go there too? What is that to Me?’ On hearing this he fell to the ground,
and Janardana said to him: ‘Let there be love in you this very day!’ — and at that very moment, overwhelmed by love, he
fell at the feet of Vishnu and wept.” (Murari, 2.3.5–8)

The Lord does not belittle Mathura and Dvaraka here: He says only that bodily presence in the dhama alone, without love for
Krishna, does not yet bear fruit — for jackals too roam there. Shuklambara asked not for a pilgrim's merit but for love
itself, and the Lord granted it at once. <!-- F33 -->

Lochana Dasa gives his plea more fully. Shuklambara Brahmachari is most pure, his body hallowed by holy places; without
fear he tells the Lord all that is in his heart: he has wandered through many holy places, borne many hardships, travelled
to Madhupuri and Dvaravati — and still he is unhappy: let the Lord give him the treasure of prema. The Lord replies:

“As long as Krishna does not rise in the heart, so long one has no grace from the holy places.” (“Chaitanya-mangala,”
Madhya-khanda, pt. 2, v. 167)

The brahmana fell to the ground and wept, and the Lord, unable to bear His devotee's suffering, cried aloud, “I have given
prema, given prema!” — and Shuklambara's body trembled, thrilled all over, and his eyes filled with tears (vv. 159–174). In
Karnapura's mahakavya Shuklambara pleads: “I have gone again and again to Dvaraka and Mathura — look but for a moment on
me, unhappy as I am, and have mercy, Lord!” — and the Lord promises him: “This very day, here, you will have devotion to
the Lord's lotus feet, full of love” (“Chaitanya-charita-mahakavya,” 6.8–11). And in Karnapura's drama Shuklambara says
humbly:

“I have performed many austerities, O Lord, and visited many holy places, and still my mind has found no peace. Cast a
merciful glance on me, be merciful!” (“Chaitanya-chandrodaya,” 1.65)

— and lays his head on the Lord's feet; and Srivasa, looking at him, tells Gadadhara that this brahmana's heart, hard
from severe austerities, has at the touch of the Lord's feet as if melted, and pours out in tears and thrills (1.66).
<!-- F33 -->

One of the biographies speaks of this briefly: Shuklambara, a singer of kirtan, received Sri Chaitanya's mercy.[^9] Lochana
Dasa, at the very start of his poem, promises to tell “how Shuklambara Brahmachari then obtained prema”
(“Chaitanya-mangala,” Sutra-khanda, pt. 2, v. 99). And after that, says Murari, Shuklambara wept constantly and, falling to
the ground like a stick, said again and again:

“Now You have made Navadvipa into Mathura, O dear one!” (Murari, 2.4.2)

Karnapura gives his words the same way: “By Your manifold pastimes You have now turned Navadvipa into a new Mathura!”
(“Chaitanya-charita-mahakavya,” 6.23). <!-- F34 F33 -->

## The Meal in the House by the Ganga
There was another day when the Lord Himself asked Shuklambara for food — this time not from the bag, but cooked in his
own house. Vrindavana Dasa devotes to it the twenty-sixth chapter of the Madhya-khanda.

“Once the Lord mercifully turned to Shuklambara Brahmachari and asked him to treat Him to rice: ‘I have a strong desire
to taste your rice. Do not be afraid. This is a sincere request.’” (CB, Madhya 26.1–2)

Shuklambara thought the Lord was teasing him (*chhalana*) and long entreated Him: he was a wretched beggar, lower than an
ant — “and You want to trick me” (26.4–5). This is not distrust of God but the humility of a devotee who cannot believe
he is worthy of such mercy; one of the biographies explains that he was abashed, fearing he could not make, from rice
collected as alms, a meal worthy of the Lord. But the Lord asked again and again: “I am not playing a joke on you. Go home
and cook quickly; I shall come to you at noon” (26.6–7). Then Shuklambara went to the devotees for advice. They praised
his good fortune: the Lord Himself always seeks food from those who are wholly devoted to Him — so He asked for food and
ate it at the house of Vidura, the son of a shudra woman, for the sake of his devotion (26.10–12).

“But if you are still afraid, do not touch the food with your hands.” (CB, Madhya 26.13)

So they advised him to cook without touching the food (*alagochhe*) and serve it to the Lord. <!-- F35 -->

“Having bathed, he boiled fragrant water. Then he dropped into it rice and the tender pith of the banana stem without
touching them, and folded his hands in prayer. Then he began to sing joyfully: ‘Jaya Krishna, Govinda, Gopala, Vanamali!’
At that moment Lakshmi, the holy mother of the whole universe, glanced at the rice of this devotee. In the twinkling of an
eye the rice became like nectar.” (CB, Madhya 26.15–19)

The pith of the banana stem (*thor*) and rice — that was the whole of the beggar's meal; but Lakshmi-devi, the Lord's
eternal consort, cast her merciful glance on it, and the devotee's simple food became nectar. This is not magic but the
mercy of the Lord's eternal consort toward His devotee. <!-- F36 -->

At noon, after His bath, the Lord came, and with Him Nityananda and other intimate companions (26.19–20). With His own
hand the Lord took the rice and in Shuklambara's house by the Ganga offered it to Vishnu (26.21–22). So, in the role of a
devotee, He teaches by His own example: He is not other than Vishnu, but He shows how food is to be offered to the Lord.
Then He sat down to eat, smiling, and Vrindavana Dasa marvels: He who enjoys the sacrifices of Brahma and all the gods now
eats Shuklambara's rice (26.23–24). And the Lord praises the meal:

“‘Since My birth I have tasted nothing more delicious than this splendid rice,’ said the Lord. ‘The nectarean sweetness of
the banana is beyond description. How did you manage to cook this without touching it? Surely you are one of My friends
for whose sake I have descended into this world.’” (CB, Madhya 26.25–27) <!-- F37 -->

“Seeing the extraordinary mercy granted to Shuklambara, the devotees began to weep.” (CB, Madhya 26.28)

And Vrindavana Dasa exclaims:

“Let the wicked rich who have no devotion to God see what mercy the penniless Shuklambara received. Lord Chaitanya is not
attained by wealth, followers or learning. All the scriptures declare: ‘Devotional service alone can conquer God.’” (CB,
Madhya 26.30–31) <!-- F38 Q6 -->

Srila Bhaktisiddhanta explains in the “Gaudiya-bhashya” on verse 26.24: Vishnu is the Lord of sacrifice. Rice collected as
alms from many houses is outwardly considered impure — many hands have touched it — and brahmana householders do not
accept such rice. But the Vaishnavas consider it purer than any other, for it has come by the Lord's mercy. On
Mahaprabhu's path what matters most in mahaprasada is the purity of the devotee's heart, and one must not think that only a
rich man can offer food worthy of the Lord.[^10] <!-- F39 I4 Q6 -->

When the Lord had eaten, the devotees took up the vessels of prasada and in ecstasy honoured the remnants of His meal
(26.33). The Lord chewed betel, talked a little with His intimates (*ishtagoshthi*) about Krishna's pastimes and lay down to
rest right there, in the house of the poor brahmana; the devotees too lay down after Him (26.32–36). <!-- F40 -->

And then in Shuklambara's house another wonder took place. Among those resting was the scribe Vijaya, nicknamed
Ankharia — the finest copyist of Navadvipa, who had copied many books for the Lord. The Lord laid His hand on his body, and
Vijaya saw it as it is: long and mighty, like a golden pillar set with jewels, shining like millions of suns and moons. He
nearly cried out, but the Lord covered his mouth: “As long as you are in this world, tell no one what you have seen”
(26.36–45). After that Vijaya wandered for a week beside himself. Srila Bhaktisiddhanta, telling of Vijaya in the
“Anubhashya” on CC, Adi 10, also remembers that this happened “in the house of Shuklambara,” and so says the
“Bhakti-ratnakara” (12.3470–3471). <!-- F41 -->

Narahari Chakravarti, guiding pilgrims through Navadvipa, shows also the lane along which the Lord and His companions went
to Shuklambara's that day: once the Lord, having asked for rice, went this way to his house; what can be said — the Lord ate
what Shuklambara had cooked, and a wondrous pastime took place there; having eaten, He chewed betel and lay down to rest
with His companions. “In Shuklambara's house,” he says, “there were many pastimes” (“Bhakti-ratnakara,” 12.3467–3472).
<!-- F42 Q10 -->

## After the Sannyasa
When the Lord had taken sannyasa and came from Katwa to Shantipura, to Advaita, the devotees of Navadvipa gathered there,
and Shuklambara was among them:

“Srivasa, Ramai, Vidyanidhi, Gadadhara, Gangadasa, Vakreshvara, Murari, Shuklambara…” (CC, Madhya 3.153)

In Jayananda the Lord, on the eve of His sannyasa, calls him among the companions who are to go with Him across the Ganga
to Katwa, and later Shuklambara hurries to the Lord at Kuliya. And Lochana Dasa tells that when the Lord, returning from
Kuliya, at His mother's word secretly came into Navadvipa,

“He took alms in the house of Shuklambara Brahmachari and in the morning, bowing to His mother, departed.”
(“Chaitanya-mangala,” Shesha-khanda, pt. 6, v. 52)[^11] <!-- F45 -->

Every year before the Ratha-yatra the devotees of Gauda went at the Lord's bidding to Him in Nilachala, and Shuklambara
went with them:

“The fortunate Shuklambara Brahmachari also came — he from whom Gaurahari Himself begged rice and ate it.” (CB, Antya 8.23)

Krishnadasa Kaviraja too names him among the countless Gaudiyas going to Puri:

“Vasudeva Datta, Murari Gupta, Gangadasa, Shriman Sena, Shriman Pandita, Akinchana Krishnadasa, Murari, Garuda Pandita,
Buddhimanta Khan, Sanjaya Purushottama, Bhagavan Pandita, Shuklambara, Nrisimhananda and all the others set out. Their
names cannot all be written down.” (CC, Antya 10.9–11) <!-- F46 Q8 -->

When the Gaudiyas first approached Puri, King Prataparudra watched them from the palace roof, and Gopinatha Acharya named
each of them to him. He pointed out Shuklambara too:

“Look, this is Shuklambara; here are Shridhara and Vijaya. This is Vallabha Sena, and here are Purushottama and
Sanjaya.” (CC, Madhya 11.90)[^12] <!-- F47 Q9 -->

## After the Lord's Disappearance
After the Lord's disappearance Shuklambara remained in Navadvipa. When Srinivasa Acharya came there, the word spread
through the town: “Srinivasa, the vessel of Gaura's love, has come,” — and the Lord's elder companions blessed him:

“Sri Murari, Srivasa Pandita, Damodara, Sanjaya, Vijaya, Shuklambara Brahmachari, Dasa Gadadhara and the other dear ones of
the Lord — all showed mercy to Srinivasa.” (“Bhakti-ratnakara,” 4.57–58) <!-- F48 -->

Narottama too came to Navadvipa. The “Narottama-vilasa” relates:

“Along that road came the brahmachari Shuklambara; seeing Narottama, he was stirred at heart. Narottama bowed, falling to
the ground; ‘Tell me who you are,’ he said, and embraced him.” (“Narottama-vilasa,” 3.121–122)

Learning who stood before him, Shuklambara with tears recalled how Gaurachandra at Ramakeli, intoxicated with prema, had
drawn Narottama to Himself and even then proclaimed his name; all the mahantas, he said, longed to see him; in separation
from the Lord no one has peace of mind, and some had soon passed from sight. And he himself led Narottama to the Lord's
devotees (3.123–128).[^13] Among the elder companions grieving in Mayapura over the Lord's disappearance Narottama saw
Shuklambara, Ishana, Murari, Damodara and Dasa Gadadhara. <!-- F49 -->

When and where Shuklambara left this world the primary sources do not say. According to the biographies, Shuklambara
Brahmachari, Dasa Gadadhara and Narahari Sarakara Thakura entered the unmanifest pastimes after these meetings, and
Srinivasa, grieving for them, went again to Vrindavana.[^14] Neither the date nor the place of his departure, nor his
samadhi, do the sources name. <!-- F50 -->

## His Memory
The invocations of the “Bhakti-ratnakara” glorify him (“Glory to Narahari, Gauridasa and Shuklambara!” — 1.23); there too he
is among the associates “ceaselessly absorbed in the Lord's virtues” (2.261). The “Narottama-vilasa” remembers him — in
Narottama's dream of Gaura's kirtan (2.122), among the associates (4.130) and in a litany of prayer (11.280). Devakinandana
Dasa sings in the “Vaishnava-vandana”:

“I shall praise the brahmachari Shuklambara, to whom the Lord granted the boon of His own prema-bhakti.”
(“Vaishnava-vandana,” pt. 2, v. 32) <!-- F51 -->

## Significance
Shuklambara is one of the branches of Sri Chaitanya's tree, and Krishnadasa Kaviraja calls him “most fortunate”: the Lord
Himself would ask him for food, and at times even snatch it from him to eat. In this lies his whole life. Outwardly he was
a beggar, but in the eyes of those to whom the Lord has given sight he was rich, for he was rich in love; and a servant of
the Lord can be recognized only by His mercy. Accepting Shuklambara's rice against the rules of offering, the Lord showed
that the path of love is higher than the path of archana and that bhakti to Krishna stands above all; whatever an exclusive
devotee does comes from Krishna's will. The Lord's mercy cannot be bought by wealth, birth or learning: in mahaprasada what
matters is the purity of the heart, and alms that come by His mercy are purer than any food. The Lord gladly eats what a
devotee brings Him with love — Sudama's broken rice and the dry rice from Shuklambara's bag — and will not even look at the
nectar of a non-devotee. And Vrindavana Dasa promises everyone who hears of this rice devotion to the lotus feet of Sri
Chaitanya. <!-- F3 I1 I2 I3 I4 I5 -->
